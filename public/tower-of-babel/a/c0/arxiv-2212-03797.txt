Monte Carlo convergence rates for k k th moments in Banach spaces
Kristin Kirchner a,∗ and Christoph Schwab b

Abstract
. We formulate standard and multilevel Monte Carlo methods for the k k th moment 𝕄 ε k ​ [ ξ ] \mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi] of a Banach space valued random variable ξ : Ω → E \xi\colon\Omega\to E , interpreted as an element of the k k -fold injective tensor product space ⊗ k ε E \otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E . For the standard Monte Carlo estimator of 𝕄 ε k ​ [ ξ ] \mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi] , we prove the k k -independent convergence rate 1 − 1 p 1-\tfrac{1}{p} in the L q ( Ω ; ⊗ ε k E ) L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E) -norm, provided that (i) ξ ∈ L k ​ q ​ ( Ω , E ) \xi\in L_{kq}(\Omega;E) and (ii) q ∈ [ p , ∞ ) q\in[p,\infty) , where p ∈ [ 1 , 2 ] p\in[1,2] is the Rademacher type of E E . By using the fact that Rademacher averages are dominated by Gaussian sums combined with a version of Slepian’s inequality for Gaussian processes due to Fernique, we moreover derive corresponding results for multilevel Monte Carlo methods, including a rigorous error estimate in the L q ( Ω ; ⊗ ε k E ) L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E) -norm and the optimization of the computational cost for a given accuracy. Whenever the type of the Banach space E E is p = 2 p=2 , our findings coincide with known results for Hilbert space valued random variables. We illustrate the abstract results by three model problems: second-order elliptic PDEs with random forcing or random coefficient, and stochastic evolution equations. In these cases, the solution processes naturally take values in non-Hilbertian Banach spaces. Further applications, where physical modeling constraints impose a setting in Banach spaces of type p < 2 p<2 , are indicated.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and motivation

Many applications in uncertainty quantification
require the estimation of statistical moments.
The first statistical moment,
that is to say the mean,
is often itself the quantity of interest,
whereas higher-order moments
are needed to infer
certain characteristics about the
probability distribution of
the underlying real- or
vector-valued random variable.
In the case that this
distribution is Gaussian,
it is fully determined by
the first two statistical moments.
Third-order and fourth-order
moments, which define the
skewness and kurtosis of the probability distribution,
play for instance an important role
for tests if
the distribution is Gaussian,
see e.g.
[
38
,
49
]
.

In order to estimate statistical moments
one resorts to sampling strategies, i.e., Monte Carlo methods.
It is well-known that
for estimating the mean
the convergence
rate
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
in the number of samples
is achieved as long as
the random variable
\xi
is
square-integrable in Bochner sense
with values in a Hilbert space
H
,
i.e.,
\xi\in L_{2}(\Omega;H)
.
Moreover, this result extends
to statistical moments of an arbitrary order
k\in\mathbb{N}

when interpreted as elements of the
Hilbert tensor product space
H^{(k)}
​,
provided that the random variable
exhibits sufficient integrability
in
L_{2k}(\Omega;H)
.

Vector-valued random variables occur, for instance,
in the context of differential equations
involving randomness.
Here, numerical methods
for generating samples of
approximate solutions often
allow for a hierarchical multilevel structure
corresponding to different degrees
of refinement of the discretization parameters.
The idea of multilevel Monte Carlo (MLMC) methods
is to reduce the computational
cost for achieving a given target accuracy
by optimizing the number of samples
used on each level
to compute the MLMC estimator.
To the best of our knowledge
this approach was first formulated by Giles
[
22
]

for stochastic ordinary differential equations (SDEs)
after having previously been introduced
by Heinrich
[
32
]

in the context of numerical integration.
Since then MLMC methods
have been used to approximate means
of Hilbert space valued random variables
for a variety of problems in uncertainty quantification,
including but not limited to
SDEs
[
13
,
21
,
23
,
25
,
58
]
,
partial differential equations (PDEs) with random
coefficients
[
3
,
10
,
12
,
13
,
26
,
27
,
31
,
56
]
,
stochastic
PDEs
[
2
,
24
]
,
and
hyperbolic PDEs with random fluxes
or uncertainties in the initial
data
[
50
,
51
,
52
]
.

With regard to higher-order moments, MLMC strategies
have been applied to estimate

diagonals of central
statistical moments
in
[
5
,
6
]
,
and combined with sparse tensor techniques
to approximate (
full
) moments
in
[
3
,
51
,
52
]
;
the latter approach has been refined and generalized
by the multi-index Monte Carlo method
in
[
30
]
.

All of the previously mentioned references
have in common that the error analysis of the
(multilevel) Monte Carlo estimators proceeds in Hilbert spaces.
For the first statistical moment, it is known that
estimating means of random variables taking
values in a Banach space
E

via the standard Monte Carlo method

does in general not converge at the rate
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
,
even in the presence of high Bochner integrability.
More specifically, the rate of convergence
depends on geometric properties of the Banach space
E
:
If
E
has
Rademacher type

p\in[1,2]
and the random variable is an
element of the Bochner space
L_{q}(\Omega;E)
,
q\geq 1
,
the standard Monte Carlo method will,
in general, converge only
at the rate
1-\tfrac{1}{\min\{p,q\}}
,
see
[
47
, Proposition 9.11]
.
This behavior necessitates
tailoring of MLMC methods
not only to the discretization
of a particular problem, but also
to the type of the Banach space.
The only two references known to the authors
addressing this issue are
[
42
]
,
where a scalar, degenerate conservation law with random data
is discretized by a MLMC finite difference method,
and
[
14
]
,
where the authors perform a MLMC analysis in
Hölder spaces for solutions to
stochastic evolution equations.

Besides the aforementioned issue
of type-dependent convergence rates,
another difficulty occurs when considering
higher-order moments of Banach space valued
random variables:
As opposed to the Hilbert space case,
there is no canonical choice for
the norm on the tensor product space
E\otimes E
.
Two options which are widely used in the literature
are the
projective
and

injective tensor product norms
,
mostly caused by the fact that any reasonable
cross norm is bounded from above, respectively from below,
by these norms, see
[
54
, Proposition 6.1(a)]
.
Janson and Kaijser
[
37
]

defined and analyzed
statistical moments of order
k\in\mathbb{N}

(in Bochner, Dunford or Pettis sense)
as elements of projective and injective
tensor product spaces.
One of the findings
[
37
, Theorem 3.8]

shows that
both the
projective
and
injective
k
th
moment
of
\xi\colon\Omega\to E
exist in Bochner sense
(and coincide) whenever
\xi\in L_{k}(\Omega;E)
.

Clearly, the choice of the tensor product norm
will play a crucial role in the error analysis
of (multilevel) Monte Carlo estimation
of higher-order statistical moments.
A simple argumentation
(see Example
3.21
)
shows that,
no matter how “good” the (Rademacher) type
of the Banach space
E
is,
Monte Carlo methods for the second moment
will in general not converge
in the projective tensor product
of
E
.

While this work is devoted to the numerical analysis
of Monte Carlo (sampling) methods
to approximate higher-order moments
of vector-valued random variables,
we remark that
certain
linear
(or
linearized
)
stochastic equations allow for
alternative approaches
to
deterministically compute

approximations to
k
-point correlations
of random solutions.
In this context, we mention

[
11
,
40
,
41
,
43
,
46
]

and the references therein.
This methodology does not raise
the mathematical issue of the type of
a Banach space and its impact
on the convergence of
numerical approximations.
In the present paper, we
shall not pursue this direction further.

1.2.
Contributions

We consider the injective
k
th moment
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]

of a Banach space valued random variable

\xi\in L_{k}(\Omega;E)
and, for the first time,
formulate standard and multilevel Monte Carlo methods
for this higher-order moment
in the Banach space setting.
We prove that the standard Monte Carlo estimator
for
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]
converges in the

L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E)
-norm
at the rate
1-\tfrac{1}{p}

provided that
(i)
\xi\in L_{kq}(\Omega;E)

and
(ii)
q\in[p,\infty)
, where
p\in[1,2]
is
the Rademacher type of
E
,
see Theorem
3.16
.
Here,
\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E
denotes the

k
-fold injective tensor product of
E
.
Note, in particular, thatthis convergence rate
is independent of the order
k

of the statistical moment
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]
.
This result readily implies error estimates
and convergence rates for abstract

single-level Monte Carlo
methods
for
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]
, see Corollary
3.20
.

By means of replacing Rademacher sums
by Gaussian averages and exploiting
a version of Slepian’s inequality for Gaussian processes
due to Fernique
[
18
]
,
we are furthermore able to formulate
corresponding abstract results
for
multilevel Monte Carlo
methods.
This includes a rigorous error estimate
in the
L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E)
-norm,
see Theorem
3.24
,
and the optimization of the computational cost
for a given accuracy
(in the MLMC context also known as
“
\alpha\beta\gamma
theorem”),
see Theorem
3.25
.

We apply these abstract results
to several classes of problems,
where the stochastic solution
processes naturally take values in
(non-Hilbertian) Banach spaces:
second-order elliptic PDEs

(a)

with random forcing
taking values in
L_{p}

for some general
p\in(1,\infty)
, or

(b)

with log-Gaussian diffusion coefficient
and right-hand side in
L_{p}
; and

(c)

stochastic evolution equations,
where we extend the MLMC analysis in Hölder
norms, performed in
[
14
, Section 5]

for mean values of solution processes,
to their
k
th moments,
being
k
th order (spatio-)temporal
correlation functions.

Layout

In Section
2

we introduce the necessary notation,

2.1
,
as well as the analytical preliminaries on

E
,

-fold injective tensor product of

.

.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
General notation and setting

Given parameter sets

\mathscr{P},\,\mathscr{Q}
,
and mappings

\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G}\colon\mathscr{P}\times\mathscr{Q}\to\mathbb{R}
,
we use the notation

\mathscr{F}(p,q)\lesssim_{q}\mathscr{G}(p,q)

to indicate that for each
q\in\mathscr{Q}

there exists a constant
{C_{q}\in(0,\infty)}

such that

\mathscr{F}(p,q)\leq C_{q}\,\mathscr{G}(p,q)

holds
for all
{p\in\mathscr{P}}
.
Whenever both relations,

\mathscr{F}(p,q)\lesssim_{q}\mathscr{G}(p,q)

and
\mathscr{G}(p,q)\lesssim_{q}\mathscr{F}(p,q)
,
hold simultaneously,
we write
\mathscr{F}(p,q)\eqsim_{q}\mathscr{G}(p,q)
.

For a Banach space
(F,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{F})
over
\mathbb{R}
,
we write

B_{F}:=\{x\in F:\|x\|_{F}\leq 1\}

for its closed unit ball,
and

\mathcal{B}(F)
for the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
on

(F,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{F})
,
that is the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
the open sets.
The dual space
of all continuous linear functionals

g\colon F\to\mathbb{R}

is denoted by
{F}^{\prime}
​.
We write
g(x)
or
\langle g,x\rangle

for the duality pairing between
g\in{F}^{\prime}

and
x\in F
, and

\|g\|_{{F}^{\prime}}:=\sup_{x\in B_{F}}|g(x)|

for the norm on
{F}^{\prime}
​.

Throughout this article, we assume that

(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
is
a complete probability space
with expectation operator
\mathbb{E}
,
and we mark statements which hold

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely with
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
For a second probability space

(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
with expectation operator
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
,

(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega},\mathcal{A}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\mathbb{P}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

denotes the product probability space, i.e.,

\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega}
is the
set of all tuples
(\omega,\widetilde{\omega})
with

\omega\in\Omega
,
\widetilde{\omega}\in\widetilde{\Omega}
,

\mathcal{A}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
is the product
\sigma
-algebra generated by
all sets of the form
A\times\widetilde{A}
with
A\in\mathcal{A}
,

\widetilde{A}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
,
and
\mathbb{P}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
is the uniquely defined
product measure satisfying

(\mathbb{P}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})(A\times\widetilde{A})=\mathbb{P}(A)\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}(\widetilde{A})

for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
\widetilde{A}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
.
The expectation operator on

(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega},\mathcal{A}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\mathbb{P}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

will be denoted by
\mathbb{E}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
.

In addition, we let

(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})
be a Banach space
over
\mathbb{R}
, the set
\mathbb{N}

contains all
(strictly) positive integers
and, unless otherwise stated,

k\in\mathbb{N}
is a fixed positive integer
which indicates the order of (statistical) moments.

2.2.
k
-fold tensor products
of Banach spaces

In this subsection we define
(full and symmetric)
k
-fold
tensor products
of the Banach space
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})
,
with the aim of
obtaining a new Banach space
satisfying the following:

(i)

It contains the set of all

k
th moments
(in Bochner sense)
of Bochner integrable random
variables
\xi\colon\Omega\to E

satisfying
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\xi\|_{E}^{k}\bigr]<\infty
.

(ii)

The topology on this space
(prescribed by its norm) allows to
quantify the convergence
of Monte Carlo estimation for
statistical moments of order
k
.

For this purpose, symmetry of moments
will be particularly important.

We start by defining the
(full)
k
-fold algebraic tensor product of
E
,

\otimes^{k}E=\underbrace{E\otimes\cdots\otimes E}_{\text{$k$ times}},

that is the vector space consisting of all finite sums of the form

\sum_{j=1}^{M}x_{j,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{j,k}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bigotimes_{n=1}^{k}x_{j,n},\qquad x_{j,n}\in E,\quad 1\leq j\leq M,\quad 1\leq n\leq k,

equipped with the algebraic operations
rendering the tensor product
linear in each of the
k
components,
see
[
19
, Section 1.1]
.
The
injective tensor norm

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\varepsilon}

for an element

U\in\otimes^{k}E
with representation

U=\sum_{j=1}^{M}x_{j,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{j,k}

is defined by

(2.1)

\|U\|_{\varepsilon}:=\sup\Biggl\{\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\prod_{n=1}^{k}f_{n}(x_{j,n})\biggr|\;\Bigg|\;f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}\Biggr\},

cf.
[
54
, Section 3.1]

and
[
37
, Section 2.3.2]
.
Note that
the value of
the supremum in (
2.1
)
is independent of the choice of the representation
of
U\in\otimes^{k}E
, since

\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\prod_{n=1}^{k}f_{n}(x_{j,n})\biggr|=\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\langle(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{k}),(x_{j,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{j,k})\rangle\biggr|=|\langle(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{k}),U\rangle|.

For
k=1
, the Hahn–Banach theorem shows that

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\varepsilon}=\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E}
.
We call the completion of the
k
-fold
algebraic tensor product space
\otimes^{k}E

with respect to
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\varepsilon}
in (
2.1
)
the (full)
k
-fold injective tensor product of
E

and denote it by
\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E
.

In the context of moments of

E
-valued random variables,
we will be interested in subspaces
of
\otimes^{k}E
and
\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E
containing
only their symmetric elements.
To this end, we first introduce for

x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{k}\in\otimes^{k}E

its
symmetrization

(2.2)

s(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{k}):=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(k)},

where

S_{k}
is the group of permutations
of the set
\{1,...,k\}
.
The
k
-fold symmetric algebraic tensor product of
E
,
denoted by
\otimes^{k,s}E
,
is then defined as the linear span
of
\{s(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{k}):x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in E\}

in
\otimes^{k}E
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\otimes^{k,s}E

\displaystyle:=\Biggl\{\sum_{j=1}^{M}s(x_{j,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{j,k})\;\Bigg|\;M\in\mathbb{N},\;x_{j,n}\in E,\;1\leq j\leq M,\;1\leq n\leq k\Biggr\}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}:}=\Biggl\{\sum_{j=1}^{M}\lambda_{j}\,{\otimes^{k}}x_{j}\;\Bigg|\;M\in\mathbb{N},\;\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R},\;x_{j}\in E,\;1\leq j\leq M\Biggr\}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}:}=\Biggl\{\sum_{j=1}^{M}\delta_{j}\,{\otimes^{k}}x_{j}\;\Bigg|\;M\in\mathbb{N},\;\delta_{j}\in\mathscr{E}(k),\;x_{j}\in E,\;1\leq j\leq M\Biggr\},

see
[
19
, Section 1.5]

or
[
20
, Section 1.1]
.
Here, we set

{\otimes^{k}}x:=\underbrace{x\otimes\cdots\otimes x}_{\text{$k$ times}}\quad\forall x\in E,\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{E}(k):=\begin{cases}\{-1,1\}&\text{if $k$ is even},\\
\{1\}&\text{if $k$ is odd}.\end{cases}

The
symmetric injective tensor norm

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}
on
the
k
-fold symmetric algebraic
tensor product space
\otimes^{k,s}E

is given by
(see
[
19
, Section 3.1]
)

(2.3)

\|U\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}:=\sup\Biggl\{\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\lambda_{j}f(x_{j})^{k}\biggr|\;\Bigg|\;f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}\Biggr\},

if
U=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\lambda_{j}\,{\otimes^{k}}x_{j}
,
where
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
x_{j}\in E

for
1\leq j\leq M
.
Note that,
as for the injective tensor norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\varepsilon}
,
this definition does
not depend on the choice of
the representation of
U
.
The
k
-fold symmetric injective tensor product of
E
,
denoted by
\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E
,
is the completion of
\otimes^{k,s}E
with respect to
the norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}
in (
2.3
).

The symmetrization

s
in (
2.2
)
extends linearly
to a projection
s\colon{\otimes^{k}}E\to\otimes^{k,s}E

and, since for

U=\sum_{j=1}^{M}x_{j,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{j,k}\in\otimes^{k}E

we moreover have that

(2.4)

\begin{split}&\|s(U)\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}\leq\|s(U)\|_{\varepsilon}=\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}x_{j,\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{j,\sigma(k)}\biggr\|_{\varepsilon}\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}x_{j,\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{j,\sigma(k)}\biggr\|_{\varepsilon}=\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}x_{j,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{j,k}\biggr\|_{\varepsilon}=\|U\|_{\varepsilon},\end{split}

it also has a unique continuous extension
to a linear projection

s_{\varepsilon}\colon{\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}}E\to\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E
.
Furthermore,
on
\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E

the injective tensor norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\varepsilon}

and the symmetric injective tensor norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}

are equivalent, with

k
-dependent equivalence constants,

(2.5)

\|U\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}\leq\|U\|_{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{k^{k}}{k!}\|U\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}\quad\forall U\in\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E.

see
[
20
, Section 2.3 and 2.7]
.

Remark 2.1
.

There are several meaningful options to define norms
on the
k
-fold algebraic tensor product spaces
\otimes^{k}E

and
\otimes^{k,s}E
.
Besides the injective tensor norm,
a common choice is the

projective tensor norm

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\pi}
,
defined for
U\in\otimes^{k}E
by

(2.6)

\|U\|_{\pi}:=\inf\Biggl\{\sum_{j=1}^{M}\prod_{n=1}^{k}\|x_{j,n}\|_{E}\;\Bigg|\;M\in\mathbb{N},\,U=\sum_{j=1}^{M}x_{j,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{j,k}\Biggr\}.

The
symmetric projective tensor norm

on
\otimes^{k,s}E
is given by
(see
[
19
, Section 2.2]
)

(2.7)

\|U\|_{\pi_{s}}:=\inf\Biggl\{\sum_{j=1}^{M}|\lambda_{j}|\,\|x_{j}\|_{E}^{k}\;\Bigg|\;M\in\mathbb{N},\,U=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\lambda_{j}\,{\otimes^{k}}x_{j}\Biggr\}.

Then, for every
U\in\otimes^{k}E
,

\|U\|_{\pi}\geq\|U\|_{\varepsilon}

and

\|s(U)\|_{\pi_{s}}\geq\max\{\|s(U)\|_{\varepsilon_{s}},\|s(U)\|_{\pi}\}
hold.
The closures of
\otimes^{k}E
and of
\otimes^{k,s}E

with respect to the norms
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\pi}

and
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\pi_{s}}
, respectively,
yield well-defined Banach spaces,
the
full
and
symmetric

k
-fold projective tensor product of
E
,
denoted by
\otimes^{k}_{\pi}E
and
\otimes^{k,s}_{\pi_{s}}E
.
As shown in Example
3.21
,
the projective norm is not suitable
for the error analysis of Monte Carlo methods.

2.3.
Moments of Banach space valued random variables

The purpose of this subsection is to generalize the notion
of the
k
th moment, defined for a real-valued
random variable
X\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
as

\mathbb{M}^{k}[X]:=\mathbb{E}\bigl[X^{k}\bigr]=\int_{\Omega}X(\omega)^{k}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega),

to Banach space valued random variables

\xi\colon\Omega\to E
.
To this end, we first specify
the concept of vector-valued integration
which we imply when taking expected values
of
E
-valued random variables.

2.3.1.
Vector-valued integration

An
E
-valued random variable
\xi
defined on the
probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
is a mapping

\xi\colon\Omega\to E

which is measurable in a certain sense.
Specifically, we consider the
class of Bochner measurable
random variables; these are all mappings

\xi\colon\Omega\to E
which are

(i)

measurable
with respect to the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}(E)

on
E
, and

(ii)

almost surely separably valued, i.e.,

\xi\in E_{0}

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for some separable subspace
E_{0}\subseteq E
.

A Bochner measurable random variable
\xi

is often also called
strongly measurable
,
emphasizing the contrast to the notion
of weak measurability, which only requires
the real-valued random variable

\langle f,\xi\rangle
to be measurable
for every
f\in{E}^{\prime}
​.
Note that
these notions are equivalent
whenever
\xi
is
almost surely separably valued
(e.g., in the case of
a separable Banach space
E
),
see
[
37
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Furthermore, it turns out that

\xi\colon\Omega\to E
is
Bochner measurable if and only if there
exists a sequence of
Borel measurable simple functions

\xi_{n}\colon\Omega\to E
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
such that
\xi_{n}\to\xi
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
This characterization facilitates the definition
of the Bochner integral

\int_{\Omega}\xi(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)=:\mathbb{E}[\xi]\in E,

whenever
\xi
is Bochner measurable
and
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\xi\|_{E}\bigr]<\infty
.

We close this subsection with introducing
the corresponding Bochner
L_{q}
-spaces.
For a real Banach space
(F,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{F})

and
q\in[1,\infty)
,

L_{q}(\Omega;F):=L_{q}(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P};F)

is the space of all
(equivalence classes of)
F
-valued
Bochner measurable random variables

\eta\colon\Omega\to F

such that
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\eta\|_{F}^{q}\bigr]<\infty
,
with norm given by

\|\eta\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;F)}:=\left(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\eta\|_{F}^{q}\bigr]\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}=\biggl(\int_{\Omega}\|\eta(\omega)\|_{F}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)\biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}\!.

2.3.2.
Moments of order
k

For an
E
-valued random variable
\xi
,
its
injective
k
th moment
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]
is defined as
the expectation
(see e.g.
[
37
, Section 3.1]
)

(2.8)

\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]:=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\otimes^{k}\xi\bigr]=\int_{\Omega}\otimes^{k}\xi(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)=\int_{\Omega}\underbrace{\xi(\omega)\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi(\omega)}_{\text{$k$ times}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega),

whenever this integral exists (in Bochner sense)
in the
k
-fold injective tensor product space
\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E
.
In what follows, we will always assume
that
\xi\in L_{q}(\Omega;E)
holds for some
q\in[k,\infty)
.
This guarantees that
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]
exists in Bochner sense:
Firstly,
Bochner measurability of
\xi

implies that also

\otimes^{k}\xi\colon\Omega\to\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E

is Bochner measurable
since the non-linear
mapping
E\ni x\mapsto\otimes^{k}x\in\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}E

is continuous and, secondly,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|{\otimes^{k}}\xi\|_{\varepsilon}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\xi\|_{E}^{k}\bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\xi\|_{E}^{q}\bigr]=\|\xi\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;E)}^{q}<\infty.

Of particular relevance
in the present context is that
the injective
k
th moment
is an element of the

k
-fold
symmetric

injective tensor product
space
\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E
, since

s_{\varepsilon}\bigl(\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]\bigr)=s_{\varepsilon}\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\otimes^{k}\xi\bigr]\bigr)=\mathbb{E}\bigl[s_{\varepsilon}\bigl(\otimes^{k}\xi\bigr)\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\otimes^{k}\xi\bigr]=\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi].

Here, we have used
continuity of the symmetrization

s_{\varepsilon}\colon{\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}}E\to\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E
,
see (
2.2
) and
(
2.4
), to exchange the order
of
s_{\varepsilon}(\,\cdot\,)
and
\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot\,]
.

Remark 2.2
.

The assumption
\xi\in L_{k}(\Omega;E)

does not only guarantee the existence of
the injective
k
th moment
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]

but also that of the
projective
k
th moment
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\pi}[\xi]
,
i.e., the integral in (
2.8
)
converges in Bochner sense
also in the stronger projective tensor norm
defined in (
2.6
),
see Remark

.
This observation follows from the identities

\|{\otimes^{k}}\xi\|_{\pi}=\|\xi\|_{E}^{k}=\|{\otimes^{k}}\xi\|_{\varepsilon}

showing that the above arguments
may be translated verbatim,
see also
[

, Theorem 3.8]
.

3.1.
Auxiliary results on Rademacher and Gaussian averages

Definition 3.1

(Rademacher family)

Let

Definition 3.2

Let

Suppose that

.

Assuming that

\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|e_{j}\otimes e_{j}\|_{\pi}^{p}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}=\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|e_{j}\otimes e_{j}\|_{\varepsilon}^{p}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}=\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|e_{j}\|_{H}^{2p}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}=n^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}.

Moreover, the
calculations in Appendix
A

(see the identities (
A.2
)
and (
A.3
)
of Lemma
A.1
)
imply that
for any Rademacher family
(r_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

on a complete probability space

(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
,
for all
p\in[1,\infty)

and
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}\,e_{j}\otimes e_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{p}(\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{2}_{\pi}H)}=n\qquad\text{and}\qquad\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}\,e_{j}\otimes e_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{p}(\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{2}_{\varepsilon}H)}=1,

and the same statements hold when
replacing
\otimes^{2}_{\pi}H
by the symmetric projective tensor product
space
\otimes^{2,s}_{\pi_{s}}H

and

\otimes^{2}_{\varepsilon}H
by the symmetric injective tensor product
space
\otimes^{2,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}H
, respectively.
This shows that (i) neither

\otimes^{2}_{\pi}H
nor
\otimes^{2,s}_{\pi_{s}}H

have a non-trivial type
p>1
,
and (ii) neither

\otimes^{2}_{\varepsilon}H
nor
\otimes^{2,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}H

have a
non-trivial cotype
q<\infty
.
Thus,

\otimes^{2}_{\varepsilon}H
and
\otimes^{2,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}H

do not have a non-trivial type either,
cf.
[
36
, Theorem 7.1.14]
.

In the next subsections we will see
that the type
p\in[1,2]
of a Banach space
E

determines the rate of convergence
when approximating statistical moments
of
E
-valued random variables
by means of Monte Carlo methods
and, moreover, that this convergence rate
does not depend on the order
k
of the moment.
However, as the above example illustrates,
to derive this finding,
it is not possible to argue
by transferring the type of
a Banach space to its
k
-fold tensor product.

3.2.
Standard and single-level Monte Carlo estimation

The next proposition is the key result
for proving convergence
of Monte Carlo methods for means,
i.e., statistical
moments of order
k=1
.
It can be found, e.g., in
[
47
, Proposition 9.11]

for the case
q=p
and
in this generality in
[
14
, Proposition 5.10]
.

Proposition 3.14
.

Assume that

(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

is of Rademacher type
p\in[1,2]
.
Let
q\in[p,\infty)
,
M\in\mathbb{N}

and
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{M}\in L_{q}(\Omega;E)
be
independent
E
-valued random variables
with vanishing mean,

\mathbb{E}[\eta_{j}]=0
for all
1\leq j\leq M
.
Then,

\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\eta_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;E)}\leq 2K_{q,p}\tau_{p}(E)\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\|\eta_{j}\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;E)}^{p}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}\!.

Corollary 3.15
.

Assume that

(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

is of Rademacher type
p\in[1,2]

and let
\eta\in L_{1}(\Omega;E)
.
In addition, let
q\in[p,\infty)
,
M\in\mathbb{N}

and
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{M}\in L_{q}(\Omega;E)
be
independent and identically distributed

E
-valued random variables.
Then,

\displaystyle\biggl\|\mathbb{E}[\eta]-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\xi_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;E)}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|\mathbb{E}[\eta-\xi_{1}]\bigr\|_{E}

\displaystyle\quad+2K_{q,p}\tau_{p}(E)\,M^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\bigl\|\xi_{1}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]\bigr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;E)}.

Proof.

By applying the triangle inequality on
L_{q}(\Omega;E)

and Proposition
3.14

(noting that
\xi_{j}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]
,
1\leq j\leq M
,
are independent and centered),
we find that

\displaystyle\biggl\|\mathbb{E}[\eta]-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;E)}\leq\bigl\|\mathbb{E}[\eta-\xi_{1}]\bigr\|_{E}+\frac{1}{M}\,\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bigl(\xi_{j}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]\bigr)\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;E)}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|\mathbb{E}[\eta-\xi_{1}]\bigr\|_{E}+2K_{q,p}\tau_{p}(E)\,M^{-1}\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bigl\|\xi_{j}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]\bigr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;E)}^{p}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}\!,

and the claim follows by
the identical distribution
of
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{M}
.
∎

The remainder of this subsection is devoted to generalizing
the approximation result of Monte Carlo estimation
for the first statistical moment in Corollary
3.15

to (injective) statistical moments
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\eta]

of an arbitrary order
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Example
3.13
shows that
it is in general not possible to argue via the type
of the tensor product space.
We therefore prove the convergence rates of Monte Carlo methods
directly by means of the auxiliary results derived in
Subsection
3.1
.

Theorem 3.16
.

Assume that

(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

is of Rademacher type
p\in[1,2]
.
Let
q\in[p,\infty)
,
k,M\in\mathbb{N}

and
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{M}\in L_{kq}(\Omega;E)
be
independent and identically distributed

E
-valued random variables.
Then,

(3.13)

\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\otimes^{k}\xi_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}\leq C^{\,\sf SL}_{q,p,k}\,M^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|\xi_{1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{k}.

Here, we recall
the constant
B_{q}\in(0,\infty)

from the classical
Khintchine inequalities (
3.12
),
as well as the Kahane–Khintchine constant
K_{q,p}

and type
p
constant
\tau_{p}(E)

from Definitions
3.7
and
3.10
,
respectively, and set

(3.14)

C^{\,\sf SL}_{q,p,k}:=2(2kK_{q,p}\tau_{p}(E)+B_{q}).

Proof.

Assume that
(r_{j})_{j=1}^{M}
is
a Rademacher family on a complete probability space

(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

and, for
j\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
,
let
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}\colon\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega}\to E

and
\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\colon\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega}\to\{-1,1\}
denote the mappings
that satisfy
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}(\omega,\widetilde{\omega})=\xi_{j}(\omega)

and
\boldsymbol{r}_{j}(\omega,\widetilde{\omega})=r_{j}(\widetilde{\omega})

for all
(\omega,\widetilde{\omega})\in\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega}
.
Notice that
on
(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega},\mathcal{A}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\mathbb{P}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

the random variables

(\boldsymbol{r}_{j})_{j=1}^{M}
form a Rademacher family and

\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{M},\boldsymbol{r}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{r}_{M}

are independent.
Moreover,

\xi_{j}\in L_{kq}(\Omega;E)
implies that

\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\in\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E
and

\otimes^{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}\in L_{q}(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)

are well-defined.
We further note that by the identical distribution
of
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{M}
,

(\mathbb{E}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{E}})\bigl[\otimes^{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\otimes^{k}\xi_{j}-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\otimes^{k}\xi_{j}\bigr]-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{j}]=0.

This shows that the independent random variables

\otimes^{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\colon\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega}\to\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E
,

1\leq j\leq M
,
are centered.
Therefore,
we can use Lemma
3.6

on the
probability space

(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega},\mathcal{A}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\mathbb{P}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

and for the Banach space
\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E
to deduce that

\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bigl(\otimes^{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr)\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}\leq 2\,\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\bigl(\otimes^{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr)\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}.

By the triangle inequality
on
L_{q}(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)

we then obtain
that

(3.15)

\begin{split}\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}&\bigl(\otimes^{k}\xi_{j}-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr)\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}=\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bigl(\otimes^{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr)\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}\\
&\leq 2\,\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\bigl(\otimes^{k}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr)\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}\\
&\leq 2\,\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\otimes^{k}\!\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}+2\,\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}\\
&=:2\text{(A)}+2\text{(B)}.\end{split}

To bound term (A) from above,
we apply Fubini’s theorem and
the Kahane–Khintchine inequality (
3.9
)
for the Banach space
F:=\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E

and obtain that

(A)

\displaystyle=\Biggl(\int_{\Omega}\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}(\,\cdot\,)\otimes^{k}\!\xi_{j}(\omega)\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}^{q}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}

\displaystyle\leq K_{q,p}\Biggl(\int_{\Omega}\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}(\,\cdot\,)\otimes^{k}\!\xi_{j}(\omega)\biggr\|_{L_{p}(\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}^{q}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}\!.

Upon inserting the definition (
2.3
)
of the symmetric injective tensor norm,
we use Proposition
3.4
(i)

for the convex increasing function
G(t):=t^{p}
,
t\geq 0
,
and
the fact that the Banach space
E
has type
p\in[1,2]

with type constant
\tau_{p}(E)\in(0,\infty)
,
to conclude that

(A)

\displaystyle\leq K_{q,p}\Biggl(\int_{\Omega}\Biggl(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Biggl[\biggl(\sup_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}}\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}(\,\cdot\,)f\bigl(\xi_{j}(\omega)\bigr)^{k}\biggr|\biggr)^{p}\Biggr]\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{q}}{{p}}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2kK_{q,p}\Biggl(\int_{\Omega}\Biggl(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Biggl[\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}(\,\cdot\,)\|\xi_{j}(\omega)\|_{E}^{k-1}\xi_{j}(\omega)\biggr\|_{E}^{p}\Biggr]\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{q}}{{p}}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}

(A)

\displaystyle\leq 2kK_{q,p}\tau_{p}(E)\Biggl(\int_{\Omega}\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\|\xi_{j}(\omega)\|_{E}^{kp}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{q}}{{p}}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}

\displaystyle=2kK_{q,p}\tau_{p}(E)\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\|\xi_{j}\|_{E}^{kp}\biggr\|_{L_{\nicefrac{{q}}{{p}}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}.

Since
q\geq p
,
we can use the triangle inequality
on
L_{\nicefrac{{q}}{{p}}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, yielding

(3.16)

\text{(A)}\leq 2kK_{q,p}\tau_{p}(E)\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\|\xi_{j}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{kp}\biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}=2kK_{q,p}\tau_{p}(E)M^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}\|\xi_{1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{k},

where we also used the
identical distribution of
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{M}
.

For term (B) we use the estimate

\bigl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}=\bigl\|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\otimes^{k}\xi_{1}\bigr]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|{\otimes^{k}}\xi_{1}\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\xi_{1}\|_{E}^{k}\bigr]\leq\|\xi_{1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{k},

as well as the classical
Khintchine inequalities (
3.12
)
so that

\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}\leq B_{q}\,M^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\leq B_{q}\,M^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}\!,

and conclude that

(3.17)

\begin{split}\text{(B)}&=\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\sum_{j=1}^{M}\boldsymbol{r}_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}=\Biggl(\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}(\widetilde{\omega})\biggr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}(\widetilde{\omega})\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}\\
&=\bigl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}\Biggl(\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}(\widetilde{\omega})\biggr|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}(\widetilde{\omega})\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}\leq B_{q}\,M^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}\|\xi_{1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{k}.\end{split}

Finally, combining (
3.15
),
(
3.16
) and
(
3.17
) shows that

\displaystyle\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\otimes^{k}\xi_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}

\displaystyle=M^{-1}\,\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bigl(\otimes^{k}\xi_{j}-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr)\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}

\displaystyle\leq 2(2kK_{q,p}\tau_{p}(E)+B_{q})\,M^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|\xi_{1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{k},

which along with
the definition (
3.14
)
of
C^{\,\sf SL}_{q,p,k}

completes the proof
of (
3.13
).
∎

The estimate (
3.16
)
of term (A) in the proof of Theorem
3.16

reveals the following analogue of
Proposition
3.14

for independent (not necessarily identically distributed) random variables

\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{M}\in L_{kq}(\Omega;E)

with vanishing
k
th moment.

Corollary 3.17
.

Assume that

(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

is of Rademacher type
p\in[1,2]
.
Let
q\in[p,\infty)
,
k,M\in\mathbb{N}

and
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{M}\in L_{kq}(\Omega;E)
be
independent
E
-valued random variables
with vanishing
k
th moment,

\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\eta_{j}]=0
for all
1\leq j\leq M
.
Then,

(3.18)

\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\otimes^{k}\eta_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}\leq 4kK_{q,p}\tau_{p}(E)\,\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\|\eta_{j}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{kp}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}\!.

Remark 3.18
.

Optimality of the convergence rate

1-\frac{1}{p}
in (
3.13
)
is ultimately related to the question
whether it is
necessary
that the Banach space
E

has Rademacher type
p\in[1,2]

for (
3.18
)
to hold for all finite sequences

{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{M}\in L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}

of independent
E
-valued random variables
with vanishing
k
th moment.

For the first moment,
k=1
, it is evident that
the choice
\eta_{j}:=r_{j}x_{j}

in (
3.18
),
where
(r_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Rademacher family
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})

and
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots\in E
,
implies that the Banach space
E

has Rademacher type
p
.
However, for higher-order moments,
this question is
more complex due to the injective
tensor norm on the left-hand side.
For odd orders
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
the space
E:=\ell_{1}

of summable real-valued sequences
(which only has Rademacher
type
p=1
),
the choice
\eta_{j}:=r_{j}e_{j}

shows that (
3.18
)
cannot hold for any
p>1
.
Here,
(e_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
denote the
standard unit vectors in
\ell_{1}
.
In addition,
the classical Khintchine inequalities (
3.12
)
imply that, for any Banach space
E
,
the convergence rate in (
3.13
)
cannot be better that
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}

(one may take, e.g.,
\xi_{j}:=g_{j}\,x
,
where
(g_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is an
orthogaussian family on
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})

and
x\neq 0
is a non-zero vector in
E
).
Sharpness of the rate

1-\frac{1}{p}
in (
3.13
)
and necessity of the Rademacher type
p

for (
3.18
)
for the case
p\in(1,2)

remains an open question.

The next lemma complements Theorem
3.16

when deriving convergence rates of
single-level Monte Carlo methods
for approximating injective
k
th moments.

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
suppose that
\eta,\xi\in L_{k}(\Omega;E)
. Then,

\bigl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\eta]-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}\leq\bigl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\eta]-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon}\leq\|\eta-\xi\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;E)}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Bigl[\|\eta\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;E)}^{i}\|\xi\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;E)}^{k-i-1}\Bigr].

Proof.

The first inequality of the assertion is trivial.
We next note that also the remaining relation
is evident in the case
k=1
, since

\bigl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\eta]-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon}=\bigl\|\mathbb{E}[\eta-\xi]\bigr\|_{E}\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\eta-\xi\|_{E}\bigr]=\|\eta-\xi\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;E)}\quad\text{if}\quad k=1.

We now assume that
k\geq 2
and
observe that

\displaystyle{\otimes^{k}}\eta-{\otimes^{k}}\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Bigl[\bigl(\otimes^{i+1}\eta\bigr)\otimes\bigl(\otimes^{k-(i+1)}\xi\bigr)-\bigl(\otimes^{i}\eta\bigr)\otimes\bigl(\otimes^{k-i}\xi\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Bigl[\bigl(\otimes^{i}\eta\bigr)\otimes(\eta-\xi)\otimes\bigl(\otimes^{k-(i+1)}\xi\bigr)\Bigr].

Therefore, we may estimate as follows,

\displaystyle\bigl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\eta]-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon}=\bigl\|\mathbb{E}\bigl[{\otimes^{k}}\eta-{\otimes^{k}}\xi\bigr]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon}\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\|{\otimes^{k}}\eta-{\otimes^{k}}\xi\bigr\|_{\varepsilon}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\,\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\bigl\|\bigl(\otimes^{i}\eta\bigr)\otimes(\eta-\xi)\otimes\bigl(\otimes^{k-i-1}\xi\bigr)\bigr\|_{\varepsilon}\Biggr]=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\|\eta\|_{E}^{i}\|\eta-\xi\|_{E}\|\xi\|_{E}^{k-i-1}\Bigr].

Combined with the Hölder inequality
this completes the proof,
since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\|\eta-\xi\|_{E}\|\xi\|_{E}^{k-1}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\eta-\xi\|_{E}^{k}\bigr]\bigr)^{\frac{1}{k}}\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\xi\|_{E}^{k}\bigr]\bigr)^{\frac{k-1}{k}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\|\eta\|_{E}^{k-1}\|\eta-\xi\|_{E}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\eta-\xi\|_{E}^{k}\bigr]\bigr)^{\frac{1}{k}}\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\eta\|_{E}^{k}\bigr]\bigr)^{\frac{k-1}{k}},

and, whenever
k\geq 3
,
we obtain for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k-2\}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\|\eta\|_{E}^{i}\|\eta-\xi\|_{E}\|\xi\|_{E}^{k-i-1}\Bigr]\leq\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\eta\|_{E}^{k}\bigr]\bigr)^{\frac{i}{k}}\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\eta-\xi\|_{E}^{k}\bigr]\bigr)^{\frac{1}{k}}\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\xi\|_{E}^{k}\bigr]\bigr)^{\frac{k-i-1}{k}}

by a triple Hölder inequality
with

\bigl(\tfrac{k}{i}\bigr)^{-1}+k^{-1}+\bigl(\tfrac{k}{k-i-1}\bigr)^{-1}=1
.
∎

We are now ready to state the main result of this subsection:
an abstract convergence rate bound in
L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)

for single-level Monte Carlo
estimation of the injective
k
th moment
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\eta]
,
assuming at our disposal
M
independent
samples of an approximation

\xi_{1}\in L_{kq}(\Omega;E)
to
\eta\in L_{k}(\Omega;E)
.

Corollary 3.20
.

Assume that

(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

is of Rademacher type
p\in[1,2]
.
Let
q\in[p,\infty)
,
k,M\in\mathbb{N}

and
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{M}\in L_{kq}(\Omega;E)
be
independent and identically distributed

E
-valued random variables.
Then, for every
U\in\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E
, we have

\biggl\|U-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\otimes^{k}\xi_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}\leq\bigl\|U-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}+C^{\,\sf SL}_{q,p,k}\,M^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|\xi_{1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{k},

where the constant
C^{\,\sf SL}_{q,p,k}
is defined as in (
3.14
).

In particular,
for all
\eta\in L_{k}(\Omega;E)
,
we have

\displaystyle\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\eta]-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\otimes^{k}\xi_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta-\xi_{1}\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;E)}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Bigl[\|\eta\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;E)}^{i}\|\xi_{1}\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;E)}^{k-i-1}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+C^{\,\sf SL}_{q,p,k}\,M^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|\xi_{1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{k}.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality on
L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)
we obtain,
for every
U\in\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E
,

\displaystyle\biggl\|U-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\otimes^{k}\xi_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|U-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}

\displaystyle\quad+\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\otimes^{k}\xi_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)},

and the first claim follows
by applying the estimate (
3.13
)
of Theorem
3.16
.
Subsequently, we derive the second assertion
by combining this result with Lemma
3.19

which we use to bound
the difference of the
k
th moments

\bigl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\eta]-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}
.
∎

We close this subsection with a counterexample
which shows that the convergence results for the standard
and single-level Monte Carlo estimators in
Theorem
3.16
and Corollary
3.20

can, in general, not hold when measuring the error
in the (symmetric or full) projective tensor norm.
More specifically, we discuss
this for
second-order moments
of Hilbert space valued random variables, i.e.,
the random variables take values in a Banach space
of type
p=2
.

Example 3.21
.

Let
(H,(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{H})

be a real separable Hilbert space,
and let
(e_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis for
H
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the
H
-valued random variable

\xi_{n}\colon\Omega\to H
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
,
whose (discrete uniform) distribution
is defined by

\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:\quad\mathbb{P}(\{\omega\in\Omega:\xi_{n}(\omega)=e_{i}\})=n^{-1}\!.

Then, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
both the projective and injective second moments
of
\xi_{n}
exist,

\mathbb{M}^{2}_{\pi}[\xi_{n}]=\mathbb{M}^{2}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{n}]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\xi_{n}\otimes\xi_{n}\bigr]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\otimes e_{i},\quad\bigl\|\mathbb{M}^{2}_{\pi}[\xi_{n}]\bigr\|_{\pi}=\bigl\|\mathbb{M}^{2}_{\pi}[\xi_{n}]\bigr\|_{\pi_{s}}=1,

see (
A.2
) in Lemma
A.1

of Appendix
A
for the
norm identities.

In addition,
for all
q\in[1,\infty)

and every

n\in\mathbb{N}
,
we have that

\|\xi_{n}\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;H)}^{q}=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\xi_{n}\|_{H}^{q}\bigr]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|e_{i}\|_{H}^{q}=1.

We let
q\in[1,\infty)
,

\xi_{n,1},\ldots,\xi_{n,M}
be
M\in\mathbb{N}
independent
copies of
\xi_{n}
and estimate

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}_{q,\pi}^{(n)}:=\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{2}_{\pi}[\xi_{n}]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\otimes^{2}\xi_{n,j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes_{\pi}^{2}H)}^{q}=\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{2}_{\pi}[\xi_{n}]-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\otimes^{2}\xi_{n,j}\biggr\|_{\pi}^{q}\Biggr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu_{1}=1}^{n}\cdots\sum_{\nu_{M}=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n^{M}}\biggl\|\,\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{i}\otimes e_{i})-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bigl(e_{\nu_{j}}\otimes e_{\nu_{j}}\bigr)\biggr\|_{\pi}^{q}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{M}\leq n\\
\text{pairwise distinct}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n^{M}}\biggl\|\,\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{i}\otimes e_{i})-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bigl(e_{\nu_{j}}\otimes e_{\nu_{j}}\bigr)\biggr\|_{\pi}^{q}.

Thus,
assuming that
n\geq M
,
again by (
A.2
)
in Lemma
A.1

we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}_{q,\pi}^{(n)}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{M}\leq n\\
\text{pairwise distinct}\end{subarray}}n^{-M}\bigl[M\bigl(\tfrac{1}{M}-\tfrac{1}{n}\bigr)+(n-M)\tfrac{1}{n}\bigr]^{q}

\displaystyle=2^{q}\bigl(1-\tfrac{M}{n}\bigr)^{q}n^{-M}[n\cdots(n-M+1)]\geq 2^{q}\bigl(1-\tfrac{M}{n}\bigr)^{q+M}\!.

Given
q\in[1,\infty)
and

M\in\mathbb{N}
, we choose an integer

n_{\star}=n_{\star}(q,M)\in\mathbb{N}

such that

n_{\star}\geq M\bigl(1-2^{-\nicefrac{{q}}{{(q+M)}}}\bigr)^{-1}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\bigl(1-\tfrac{M}{n_{\star}}\bigr)^{q+M}\geq 2^{-q}.

This proves that,
for all
q\in[1,\infty)
and
every
M\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists

{n_{\star}=n_{\star}(q,M)\in\mathbb{N}}

such that

\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{2}_{\pi}[\xi_{n_{\star}}]-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\otimes^{2}\xi_{n_{\star},j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes_{\pi_{s}}^{2,s}H)}\geq\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{2}_{\pi}[\xi_{n_{\star}}]-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\otimes^{2}\xi_{n_{\star},j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes_{\pi}^{2}H)}\geq 1.

Since
\|\xi_{n_{\star}}\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;H)}=1

is also true
for all
q\in[1,\infty)
and
since
H
has type
p=2
,
this shows that an analogue
of (
3.13
) cannot hold with respect to
the (full or symmetric)
projective tensor norm.

3.3.
Multilevel Monte Carlo estimation

Assuming that
(X_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{L}
is
a family of
E
-valued random variables
corresponding to
L\in\mathbb{N}

different refinement
levels

of underlying discretization parameters,
translating the idea of multilevel Monte Carlo (MLMC)
estimation, as formulated
e.g. in
[
12
, p. 5]

for means of
Hilbert space valued random variables,
to higher-order moments of Banach space
valued random variables
results in exploiting the telescopic sum
(here:
X_{0}:=0\in E
)

\mathbb{E}\bigl[{\otimes^{k}}X_{L}\bigr]=\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X_{L}]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\bigl(\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X_{\ell}]-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X_{\ell-1}]\bigr)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathbb{E}\bigl[{\otimes^{k}}X_{\ell}-{\otimes^{k}}X_{\ell-1}\bigr],

and estimating
\mathbb{E}[{\otimes^{k}}X_{\ell}-{\otimes^{k}}X_{\ell-1}]

for each
1\leq\ell\leq L

via Monte Carlo sampling
instead of
\mathbb{E}[{\otimes^{k}}X_{L}]
.
As we will see in Theorem
3.25

and Remark
3.27
,
this approach considerably reduces the computational cost.

Evidently, the corresponding error analysis
requires a Monte Carlo convergence result for
estimating differences of injective
k
th moments, i.e.,
for expected values of the form

\mathbb{E}[{\otimes^{k}}\eta-{\otimes^{k}}\xi]=\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\eta]-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]
,
via standard Monte Carlo methods.
This auxiliary result is derived in Proposition
3.23

by means of the next lemma, Lemma
3.22
,
which acts as the analogue of the Rademacher
type estimate (
3.11
) for Rademacher sums
of differences

{\otimes^{k}}x_{j}-{\otimes^{k}}y_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq M
.

Lemma 3.22
.

Let
(r_{j})_{j=1}^{M}
be
a Rademacher family
on a complete probability space

(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

with expectation
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
.
Assume that

(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

has Rademacher type
{p\in[1,2]}

Then,

(3.19)

\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}\bigl(\otimes^{k}x_{j}-\otimes^{k}y_{j}\bigr)\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}\leq C^{\,\sf diff}_{q,p,k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{M}\|x_{j}-y_{j}\|_{E}^{ip}\|y_{j}\|_{E}^{(k-i)p}\Biggr]^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}\!.

Here,

from Definitions
3.7

and
3.10

Proof.

For the proof of (
3.19
), we
assume that
(g_{j})_{j=1}^{M}
,
(\widetilde{g}_{j})_{j=1}^{M}

are two independent orthogaussian families on

(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
.
We first note that
by
[
47
, Lemma 4.5 and (4.8)]
,
applied for the convex function
t\mapsto t^{q}

and the Banach space
\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E
,
and by the definition (
2.3
)
of the symmetric injective

(3.21)

\begin{split}\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}\bigl(\otimes^{k}x_{j}&-\otimes^{k}y_{j}\bigr)\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}g_{j}\bigl(\otimes^{k}x_{j}-\otimes^{k}y_{j}\bigr)\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}\hskip-28.45274pt\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\Biggl\|\sup_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}}\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}g_{j}\bigl(f(x_{j})^{k}-f(y_{j})^{k}\bigr)\biggr|\Biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\Biggl\|\sup_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}}\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}g_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{k}{i}f(x_{j}-y_{j})^{i}f(y_{j})^{k-i}\biggr|\Biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}\\

Here, we also used the binomial expansion
for

and the triangle inequality on
L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})
.
We now claim that, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
,

(3.22)

\begin{split}&\Biggl\|\sup_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}}\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}g_{j}f(x_{j}-y_{j})^{i}f(y_{j})^{k-i}\biggr|\Biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}\\
&\leq 2\sqrt{2}\,\Biggl\|\sup_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}}\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bigl(g_{j}f(x_{j}-y_{j})^{i}\|y_{j}\|_{E}^{k-i}+\widetilde{g}_{j}\|x_{j}-y_{j}\|_{E}^{i}f(y_{j})^{k-i}\bigr)\biggr|\Biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}\!.\hskip-28.45274pt\end{split}

To establish (
3.22
),
we set

for all

consider for a fixed

which are

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{i,1}(f)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{i,2}(f)

For all

and

we then obtain by independence of the
standard Gaussian random variables

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|\mathcal{G}_{i,1}(f)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{G}_{i,1}(h)|^{2}\bigr]=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bigl(f(\delta_{j})^{i}f(y_{j})^{k-i}-h(\delta_{j})^{i}h(y_{j})^{k-i}\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bigl(\bigl[f(\delta_{j})^{i}-h(\delta_{j})^{i}\bigr]f(y_{j})^{k-i}+h(\delta_{j})^{i}\bigl[f(y_{j})^{k-i}-h(y_{j})^{k-i}\bigr]\bigr)^{2}

for all

.

We thus may apply the comparison result
derived in Lemma
B.2

(see Appendix
B
)
for every
{i\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}}
,
which shows that,
for all

,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\bigl(\sup\nolimits_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}\!}|\mathcal{G}_{i,1}(f)|\bigr)^{q}\Bigr]\leq 2^{q}\,\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\bigl(\sup\nolimits_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}\!}|\mathcal{G}_{i,2}(f)|\bigr)^{q}\Bigr].

Next, combining (
3.21
)

\displaystyle\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}\bigl(\otimes^{k}x_{j}-\otimes^{k}y_{j}\bigr)\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\widetilde{C}_{k,i}\,\Biggl\|\sup_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}}\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}g_{j}f(\delta_{j})^{i}\|y_{j}\|_{E}^{k-i}\biggr|\Biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\Biggl\|\sup_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}}\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}g_{j}f(\delta_{j})^{k}\biggr|\Biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}\hskip-3.01598pt+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\widetilde{C}_{k,i}\,\Biggl\|\sup_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}}\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\widetilde{g}_{j}\|\delta_{j}\|_{E}^{i}f(y_{j})^{k-i}\biggr|\Biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}\!,

where we set

\widetilde{C}_{k,i}:=2\sqrt{\pi}\binom{k}{i}
.
By noting that

\sqrt{\pi}/\sqrt{2}\leq\widetilde{C}_{k,k}

and estimating
the

on the right-hand side for all

using
Proposition
3.4
(i)
,
with
k_{j}=i

and the vectors

\|y_{j}\|_{E}^{\frac{k-i}{i}}\delta_{j}

(respectively,
for every

\|\delta_{j}\|_{E}^{\frac{i}{k-i}}y_{j}

for all

\displaystyle\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}\bigl(\otimes^{k}x_{j}-\otimes^{k}y_{j}\bigr)

\displaystyle\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\widetilde{C}_{k,i}\,4i\,\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}g_{j}\|\delta_{j}\|_{E}^{i-1}\|y_{j}\|_{E}^{k-i}\delta_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};E)}

for the independent, centered

Proposition 3.23
.

(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

.

\displaystyle\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\eta_{1}]-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]

\displaystyle\leq C^{\,\sf diff}_{q,p,k}\,\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\|\eta_{j}-\xi_{j}\|_{E}^{ip}\|\xi_{j}\|_{E}^{(k-i)p}\biggr\|_{L_{\nicefrac{{q}}{{p}}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}

\displaystyle\Bigl\|

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\eta_{1}-\xi_{1}\|_{E}^{kq}\bigr]\bigr)^{\frac{i}{k}}\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\xi_{1}\|_{E}^{kq}\bigr]\bigr)^{\frac{k-i}{k}}\Bigr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}=\|\eta_{1}-\xi_{1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{i}\|\xi_{1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{k-i}

\text{(A)}\leq C^{\,\sf diff}_{q,p,k}\,M^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl[{\textstyle\binom{k}{i}}\|\eta_{1}-\xi_{1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{i}\|\xi_{1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{k-i}\Bigr].

For term (B) we obtain

\begin{split}\text{(B)}&=\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}\bigl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\eta_{1}]-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}\leq B_{q}\,M^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\bigl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\eta_{1}]-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi_{1}]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}\\

The claim now follows by

Theorem 3.24
.

Let

(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

be of Rademacher type
p\in[1,2]
,

{q\in[p,\infty)}
and
{k,L\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Suppose further that, for every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
,

{X_{\ell}\in L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}
,

M_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
, and

\xi_{\ell,1},\ldots,\xi_{\ell,M_{\ell}}

are independent

\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E

\xi_{\ell}:=\otimes^{k}X_{\ell}-\otimes^{k}X_{\ell-1}\in L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E),\qquad X_{0}:=0\in E.

Then, for every
U\in\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E
,

\displaystyle\biggl\|U

\displaystyle-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}\leq\bigl\|U-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X_{L}]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}

\displaystyle+C^{\,\sf ML}_{q,p,k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\biggl[M_{\ell}^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|X_{\ell}-X_{\ell-1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}

\displaystyle\hskip 48.36967pt\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Bigl[\Bigl({\textstyle\binom{k}{i+1}}\|X_{\ell}-X_{\ell-1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{i}+\|X_{\ell}\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;E)}^{i}\Bigr)\|X_{\ell-1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{k-i-1}\Bigr]\biggr],

where

C^{\,\sf ML}_{q,p,k}:=2\max\bigl\{C^{\,\sf diff}_{q,p,k},B_{q}\bigr\}

and the constants

B_{q},C^{\,\sf diff}_{q,p,k}\in(0,\infty)

are defined as in (
3.12
)
and (
3.20
).

Proof.

First note that, for every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}

the random variables

\xi_{\ell,1},\ldots,\xi_{\ell,M_{\ell}}

are identically distributed
and we have that

\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\Biggr]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\ell}]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\bigl(\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X_{\ell}]-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X_{\ell-1}]\bigr)=\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X_{L}].

Thus, we find by the triangle inequality
on

that,
for every
U\in\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E

\displaystyle\biggl\|U-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|U-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X_{L}]\bigr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathrm{err}^{\sf\,SL}_{q,\varepsilon_{s}}(\xi_{\ell}),

where, for
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
, we define

\mathrm{err}^{\sf\,SL}_{q,\varepsilon_{s}}(\xi_{\ell}):=\biggl\|\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\ell}]-\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}.

For every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
,
we let the tuples
(X_{\ell-1,1},X_{\ell,1}),\ldots,(X_{\ell-1,M_{\ell}},X_{\ell,M_{\ell}})

be
M_{\ell}
independent
copies of
(X_{\ell-1},X_{\ell})

and observe that

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}^{\sf\,SL}_{q,\varepsilon_{s}}(\xi_{\ell})=\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X_{\ell}]-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X_{\ell-1}]-\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\bigl(\otimes^{k}X_{\ell,j}-\otimes^{k}X_{\ell-1,j}\bigr)\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}E)}.

We are thus in the position to
apply Proposition
3.23

on every level
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}^{\sf\,SL}_{q,\varepsilon_{s}}(\xi_{\ell})

\displaystyle\leq 2C^{\,\sf diff}_{q,p,k}\,M_{\ell}^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl[{\textstyle\binom{k}{i}}\|X_{\ell}-X_{\ell-1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{i}\|X_{\ell-1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{k-i}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+2B_{q}\,M_{\ell}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|X_{\ell}-X_{\ell-1}\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;E)}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Bigl[\|X_{\ell}\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;E)}^{i}\|X_{\ell-1}\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;E)}^{k-i-1}\Bigr],

which after recalling that

p\in[1,2]
and
q\geq p\geq 1

as well as combining the two sums
completes the proof of the assertion.
∎

facilitates optimizing the number

\displaystyle\|X_{\ell}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}\bigr\}\leq C_{\sf stab}.\quad

Proof.

We will show by explicit construction that, for every

)

\displaystyle\quad\hskip 51.21495pt\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Bigl[\Bigl({\textstyle\binom{k}{i+1}}\|X_{\ell}-X_{\ell-1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{i}+\|X_{\ell}\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;E)}^{i}\Bigr)\|X_{\ell-1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;E)}^{k-i-1}\Bigr]\biggr]

4.
Applications

In this section
we illustrate the preceding, abstract theory by several
examples of stochastic equations,
where the need for the presently
developed modifications of the standard
Monte Carlo theory
is entailed either
by problem-specific constraints on the choices of
non-Hilbertian function spaces for well-posedness
or by the interest in error estimates
in norms on Banach spaces (such as Hölder norms).

Specifically, Subsections
4.1

and
4.2

are concerned with
the
k
th moment
MLMC finite element convergence
analysis for explicit, linear,
second-order elliptic PDEs
with random forcing (in dimensions
d\in\{2,3\}
)
or random diffusion coefficient (for
d=1
),
respectively.
Here, the right-hand side is assumed to
be an element of
(or taking values in)

L_{p}(\mathrm{D})
for some
p\in(1,\infty)
,
where
\mathrm{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes the spatial domain.
To obtain well-posed problems,
the case
p\in(1,2)
necessitates
variational formulations on Banach spaces,
whereas for
p\in(2,\infty)

such formulations may be advantageous
to derive error estimates in Hölder norms
via Sobolev embeddings.

In Subsection
4.3

we discuss the MLMC approximation
of higher-order moments
for vector-valued stochastic
processes
{X\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to E}

in tensor norms of
Hölder spaces
C^{\delta}([0,T];E)

for problem-specific
Hölder exponents
\delta\in[0,1)
.
These results
are applicable to many
semi-discrete or fully discrete
numerical schemes
for SDEs and stochastic PDEs
and we give some explicit examples.

4.1.
Linear elliptic PDEs with random forcing

Let
\mathrm{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\in\{2,3\}

be an open, bounded,
polytopal Lipschitz domain (with closure
\overline{\mathrm{D}}
)
and, for
p\in[1,\infty]
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
L_{p}(\mathrm{D})
and
W^{m}_{p}(\mathrm{D})

denote the standard Lebesgue
and Sobolev spaces
of real-valued functions on
\mathrm{D}
.

We write
{\mathring{W}}^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})
for the
closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathrm{D})

(the space of smooth functions
with compact support inside
\mathrm{D}
)
with respect to the norm
on
W^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})
,
and
W^{-1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})

for the dual space of
\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{D})
,
where

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.

4.1.1.
Deterministic model problem

We assume given deterministic,
continuous diffusion coefficients

a_{ij}\in C^{0}(\overline{\mathrm{D}})
,

a_{ij}=a_{ji}
,
1\leq i,j\leq d
,
which are uniformly positive definite.
Thus, there exist constants

0<\underline{a}\leq\overline{a}<\infty

such that, for all
x\in\overline{\mathrm{D}}
,

(4.1)

\forall\phi,\psi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\quad\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x)\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\geq\underline{a}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2},\;\;\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x)\phi_{i}\psi_{j}\leq\overline{a}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\psi\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}},

where
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
denotes
the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
and a given
source term
f
in
L_{p}(\mathrm{D})

(which in the sequel shall be generalized to be random),
we consider the following
variational formulation of a homogeneous
Dirichlet boundary value problem:
Find

(4.2)

u\in\mathring{W}^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D}):\;\;B(u,v)=\langle f,v\rangle\quad\forall v\in\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{D}).

Here,
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
denotes
the
W^{-1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})\times\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{D})
duality pairing,
and
the bilinear form
B

is given by

B\colon\mathring{W}^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})\times\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{D})\to\mathbb{R},\qquad B(w,v):=\int_{\mathrm{D}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x)\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}w(x)\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}v(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Evidently,
Hölder’s inequality implies continuity
of
B
on
\mathring{W}^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})\times\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{D})
.
However, as opposed to the Hilbert space
case
p=p^{\prime}=2
, the
uniform strong ellipticity assumption
in (
4.1
)
is in general not sufficient to guarantee
that the mapping

\mathring{W}^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})\ni u\mapsto B(u,\,\cdot\,)\in W^{-1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})

is an isomorphism.
For the case of the Laplace operator
(i.e.,
a_{ij}(x)=\delta_{ij}
)
and every
p\in(1,\infty)
,
an inf-sup condition and
hence well-posedness of (
4.2
)
have been shown in
[
55
, Theorem 6.1]
,
see also
[
8
, Equation (8.6.5)]
.
Following the arguments
used in
[
8
, Section 8.6]

this can be generalized to
diffusion coefficients
(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d}

satisfying (
4.1
),
provided that
p
is sufficiently close to
2
.
In what follows, we will
require

for an appropriate range of
integrability indices
p\in(1,\infty)
that
(
4.2
) has a unique solution
and, moreover, that this solution
is
W^{2}_{p}(\mathrm{D})
-regular.
This is summarized in the next
assumption.

Assumption 4.1
.

There exists
p_{0}\in(d,\infty)

such that, for all
p\in(1,p_{0})

and every
f\in L_{p}(\mathrm{D})
,
the variational problem
(
4.2
)
admits a unique solution
u\in\mathring{W}^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})
, and

(4.3)

\forall p\in(1,p_{0})\quad\exists C_{p}\in(0,\infty)\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(\mathrm{D}):\quad\|u\|_{W^{2}_{p}(\mathrm{D})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathrm{D})}.

Sufficient conditions for the

W^{2}_{p}(\mathrm{D})
-regularity (
4.3
)
to hold for the Laplace problem
in polygons (i.e.,
d=2
)
can, for instance, be found
in
[
28
, Theorem 4.3.2.4]
.

Since
p_{0}>d
and
d\in\{2,3\}
are assumed,
for a given
q\in[p_{0},\infty)
,
we may choose

{p=\frac{qd}{d+q}\in(1,\min\{d,q\})}

in (
4.3
)
and conclude by continuity of the
Sobolev embedding
W^{2}_{p}(\mathrm{D})\subseteq W^{1}_{q}(\mathrm{D})

and Hölder’s inequality that

\|u\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\mathrm{D})}\lesssim_{(q,\mathrm{D})}\|u\|_{W^{2}_{p}(\mathrm{D})}\lesssim_{p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathrm{D})}\lesssim_{(q,\mathrm{D})}\|f\|_{L_{q}(\mathrm{D})}
.
For
q\in(1,p_{0})
this estimate
trivially holds by (
4.3
)
so that we obtain the
following stability estimate
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
:

(4.4)

\forall p\in(1,\infty):\quad\|u\|_{W^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})}\lesssim_{(p,\mathrm{D})}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathrm{D})}.

4.1.2.
Finite element approximation

For the numerical approximation,
we use a conforming finite element method (FEM)
for (
4.2
)
based on continuous, piecewise
first-order Langrangean basis functions on
\overline{\mathrm{D}}
:
on a regular, simplicial triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of
\overline{\mathrm{D}}

with mesh size
h\in(0,\infty)
,
we consider the finite-dimensional space

S^{1}_{0}(\mathrm{D};\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\bigl\{v\in C^{0}(\overline{\mathrm{D}}):v|_{\partial\mathrm{D}}=0,\;v|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}\;\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\bigr\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{1}
denotes the space of polynomials
of degree at most one.
The corresponding Galerkin discretization
of (
4.2
) reads:
Find

(4.5)

u_{h}\in S^{1}_{0}(\mathrm{D};\mathcal{T}_{h}):\;\;B(u_{h},v_{h})=\langle f,v_{h}\rangle\quad\forall v_{h}\in S^{1}_{0}(\mathrm{D};\mathcal{T}_{h}).

Evidently, this finite-dimensional problem is equivalent to
a linear system of equations, with matrix that is symmetric
and, by (
4.1
), positive definite, so that there
exists a unique solution

u_{h}\in S^{1}_{0}(\mathrm{D};\mathcal{T}_{h})
of (
4.5
).

Under Assumption
4.1
and
the additional condition that

(4.6)

\forall i,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}:\quad a_{ij}\in W^{1}_{q}(\mathrm{D})\quad\text{for some}\quad\begin{cases}q>2&\text{if }d=2,\\
q\geq\frac{12}{5}&\text{if }d=3,\end{cases}

it is shown
in
[
8
, Theorem 8.5.3]

that, for all
p\in(1,\infty)
,
the Galerkin projection
u_{h}
in (
4.5
)
is bounded in
\mathring{W}^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})
:
There exists a mesh size

h_{0}\in(0,\infty)
such that

(4.7)

\forall h\in(0,h_{0}):\quad\|u_{h}\|_{W^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})}\lesssim_{(p,\mathrm{D})}\|u\|_{W^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})}.

Combining (
4.4
) and (
4.7
)
implies stability of both
the exact solution
u
and
its approximation
u_{h}
:
For all
p\in(1,\infty)
, there exist
a mesh width

h_{0}\in(0,\infty)

and a constant

\widetilde{C}_{\sf stab}\in(0,\infty)
such that

(4.8)

\forall h\in(0,h_{0}):\quad\max\bigl\{\|u\|_{W^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})},\|u_{h}\|_{W^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})}\bigr\}\leq\widetilde{C}_{\sf stab}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathrm{D})}.

Moreover, by
[
8
, Equation (8.5.4)]

u_{h}
is quasi-optimal in
\mathring{W}^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})

for all
p\in(1,\infty)
:
There exist
h_{0},C_{\sf opt}\in(0,\infty)

(which may depend on
\mathrm{D}
and
p
)
such that

\forall h\in(0,h_{0}):\quad\|u-u_{h}\|_{W^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})}\leq C_{\sf opt}\inf_{v_{h}\in S^{1}_{0}(\mathrm{D};\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|u-v_{h}\|_{W^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})}.

Therefore,
under Assumption
4.1

and (
4.6
),
for every quasi-uniform family of
triangulations
(\mathcal{T}_{h})_{h\in\mathcal{H}}
,
standard approximation properties
of the corresponding finite element
spaces
S^{1}_{0}(\mathrm{D};\mathcal{T}_{h})
,

h\in\mathcal{H}\subseteq(0,\infty)
,
show that, for all
p\in(0,p_{0})
,

(4.9)

\forall h\in\mathcal{H}\cap(0,h_{0}):\quad\|u-u_{h}\|_{W^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})}\lesssim_{(p,\mathrm{D})}h\,\|u\|_{W^{2}_{p}(\mathrm{D})}\lesssim_{(p,\mathrm{D})}h\,\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathrm{D})},

where we also used the
assumed regularity (
4.3
).

4.1.3.
Random forcing and MLMC-FEM

Suppose the setting of the previous
subsections. In particular,
the coefficients
(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d}
satisfy
(
4.1
) and (
4.6
),
and Assumption
4.1
holds
for some
p_{0}\in(d,\infty)
.
In this subsection we fix
p\in(1,p_{0})
, which
determines the spatial integrability
of a given random forcing.
Random forcing in (
4.2
)
amounts to assuming that
the right-hand side
f
is an element of

L_{r}(\Omega;L_{p}(\mathrm{D}))

for some suitable integrability index
{r\in[1,\infty)}

with respect to the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
, i.e.,
we seek a
\mathring{W}^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D})
-valued random variable

such that

(4.10)

B(u(\omega),v)=\langle f(\omega),v\rangle\quad\forall v\in\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{D}),\;\;\;\text{for almost all }\omega\in\Omega.

Under the above mentioned regularity
requirements, see Assumption
4.1
,
we may argue for almost all
\omega\in\Omega

to establish the existence and uniqueness
of a (stochastic) solution
u\in L_{r}(\Omega;\mathring{W}^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D}))

satisfying (
4.10
),
with

u\in L_{r}(\Omega;W^{2}_{p}(\mathrm{D})),\qquad\|u\|_{L_{r}(\Omega;W^{2}_{p}(\mathrm{D}))}\lesssim_{(p,\mathrm{D})}\|f\|_{L_{r}(\Omega;L_{p}(\mathrm{D}))}.

Multilevel finite element discretizations

of (
4.10
) will be based on the
discrete variational problem (
4.5
),
considered
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
To this end, we denote by
\{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}

a nested sequence of regular, simplicial triangulations

\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
of
\overline{\mathrm{D}}
,
with corresponding sequence of mesh sizes

\{h_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
.
We assume that

\mathcal{T}_{\ell+1}
is obtained from
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}

via uniform red refinement.
Then,
h_{\ell+1}\lesssim h_{\ell}/2

and, without loss of generality,
we may assume that
h_{1}<h_{0}
,
with
h_{0}
as in (
4.7
)–(
4.9
).
The corresponding sequence of
Galerkin solutions

u_{h_{\ell}}\in S^{1}_{0}(\mathrm{D};\mathcal{T}_{\ell})

shall be denoted by
u_{\ell}

(with slight abuse of notation).

In the next corollary we verify
that all assumptions
of the “
\alpha\beta\gamma
theorem”,
see Theorem
3.25
,
are satisfied to bound the computational
costs of the MLMC-FEM estimator for
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[u]

for a given accuracy
and provide an upper bound
for these costs.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let (
4.1
) and (
4.6
)
be satisfied and suppose that
Assumption
4.1

holds for some
p_{0}\in(d,\infty)
.
Assume that
p\in(1,p_{0})
,
q\in[\min\{p,2\},\infty)
,
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
f\in L_{kq}(\Omega;L_{p}(\mathrm{D}))

be the solution to (

Furthermore, let

Then, for

​,

are fulfilled,

(

α

\displaystyle\quad\forall\ell\in\mathbb{N}:

\displaystyle\lesssim_{(k,p,\mathrm{D},f)}N_{\ell}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{d}}},

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt\text{i.e., }\alpha

\displaystyle=d^{-1},

(

β

)

\displaystyle\quad\forall\ell\in\mathbb{N}:

\displaystyle\|u_{\ell}-u_{\ell-1}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;W^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{(k,p,q,\mathrm{D},f)}N_{\ell}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{d}}},

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt\text{i.e., }\beta

\displaystyle=d^{-1},

(

γ

)

\displaystyle\quad\forall\ell\in\mathbb{N}:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{\ell}^{k},

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt\text{i.e., }\gamma

\displaystyle=k,

(

stab

)

\displaystyle\quad\forall\ell\in\mathbb{N}:

\displaystyle\max\bigl\{\|u\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;W^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D}))},\|u_{\ell}

)
for almost all

\forall\ell\in\mathbb{N}:\quad\|u-u_{\ell}\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;W^{1}_{p}(\mathrm{D}))}\lesssim_{(p,\mathrm{D})}h_{\ell}\,\|f\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;L_{p}(\mathrm{D}))}.

Remark 4.3
.

In the Hilbert space case,
it is in general not optimal
to obtain a convergence rate bound for (

α

)
by combining strong convergence with
stability (

stab

), as outlined
in Remark
3.26
.
For instance, the error analysis
of Galerkin approximations for
generalized Whittle–Matérn fields
in
[
15
, Proposition 4]

reveals that the corresponding
approximations of the covariance function
converge more than twice as fast
in the
L_{2}(\mathrm{D}{\times}\mathrm{D})
-norm
as the corresponding Gaussian random field approximations
in the strong
L_{q}(\Omega;L_{2}(\mathrm{D}))
-sense.
However, it is not obvious if and how
this behavior generalizes to random variables
with values in Banach spaces.

4.2.
Linear elliptic PDEs with log-Gaussian coefficient

We next consider a linear, second-order elliptic PDE
with mixed Dirichlet–Neumann boundary conditions
and right-hand side in
L_{p}(\mathrm{D})
.
As opposed to Subsection
4.1
,
we now assume that the
diffusion coefficient
a
is random.
More specifically,
as e.g. in
[
9
,
10
,
56
]
,
we suppose that

a
is log-Gaussian,
i.e.,
a(x)=\exp(g(x))

for almost all
x\in\mathrm{D}
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,
for some Gaussian random field

g\colon\mathrm{D}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
.
The spatial domain is assumed to be a bounded interval

\mathrm{D}=I=(0,b)
of length
b\in(0,\infty)
.
The restriction of the spatial dimension to
d=1

facilitates an explicit expression of the inf-sup constant
of the bilinear form,
appearing in the corresponding
variational formulation,
depending on

\underline{a}:=\operatorname{ess}\inf_{x\in I}a(x)
.
In this setting,
\underline{a}
is a random variable
satisfying
\underline{a}\in L_{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})

for all
q\in[1,\infty)
.
Similarly as in
[
9
]
,
this, in turn, yields well-posedness
of the variational problem
and strong convergence
of finite element approximations.

4.2.1.
Deterministic model problem

For
p\in[1,\infty]
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
we recall the Lebesgue and Sobolev
spaces
L_{p}(I)
and
W^{m}_{p}(I)

from Subsection
4.1
.
We furthermore note that,
since
d=1
, for every
p\in[1,\infty]
,
elements
v
in
W^{1}_{p}(I)
coincide
(upon a modification on a subset of
\overline{I}

of zero Lebesgue measure)
with a unique function which
is continuous on
\overline{I}=[0,b]
,
denoted by
\widetilde{v}\in C^{0}(\overline{I})
,
and we define the subspace

\mathring{W}_{p,\{0\}}^{1}(I):=\bigl\{v\in W^{1}_{p}(I):\widetilde{v}(0)=0\bigr\}.

In virtue of the Poincaré inequality,
on this subspace
the map

v\mapsto|v|_{W^{1}_{p}(I)}
,

|v|_{W^{1}_{p}(I)}:=\|v^{\prime}\|_{L_{p}(I)}

defines a norm,
where
v^{\prime}
denotes the weak derivative of
v
.
In addition,
we write
p^{\prime}\in[1,\infty]
for
the Hölder conjugate of
p\in[1,\infty]
,
and we let

W^{-1}_{p,\{0\}}(I)

be the dual space of

\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}\!,\{0\}}(I)
,
equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{W^{-1}_{p,\{0\}}(I)}:=\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}\!,\{0\}}(I)}\frac{\langle f,v\rangle}{|v|_{W^{1}_{p^{\prime}}(I)}}.

We assume given a
finite partition
\mathcal{P}=\{J_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n_{\mathcal{P}}}

of pairwise disjoint, open subintervals
J_{i}
of
I

such that

\overline{J_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup\overline{J_{n_{\mathcal{P}}}}=\overline{I}=[0,b]
.
Furthermore,
we suppose that the scalar diffusion coefficient
satisfies

a\in W^{1}_{\infty}(I;\mathcal{P}),\quad\text{where}\quad W^{1}_{\infty}(I;\mathcal{P}):=\bigl\{a\in L_{\infty}(I)\,|\,\forall J\in\mathcal{P}:\,a|_{J}\in W^{1}_{\infty}(J)\bigr\},

and it is positive in the sense that there
exist constants
\underline{a}
,
\overline{a}
such that

0<\underline{a}\leq a(x)\leq\overline{a}<\infty\qquad\text{for almost all }x\in I.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
and
f\in L_{p}(I)

we then consider the following boundary value problem,
with mixed (Dirichlet–Neumann) boundary conditions:
Find

(4.12)

u\colon\overline{I}\to\mathbb{R}:\quad\;-(a(x)u^{\prime}(x))^{\prime}=f(x),\quad\text{a.a. }x\in I,\quad\;\;u(0)=a(b)u^{\prime}(b)=0.

The weak formulation of (
4.12
)
reads: Find

(4.13)

u\in\mathring{W}^{1}_{p,\{0\}}(I):\;\;B_{a}(u,v)=\langle f,v\rangle\quad\forall v\in\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}\!,\{0\}}(I),

where the bilinear form
B_{a}

is defined by

B_{a}(w,v):=\int_{0}^{b}a(x)w^{\prime}(x)v^{\prime}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
,
for all
w\in\mathring{W}^{1}_{p,\{0\}}(I)

and
v\in\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}\!,\{0\}}(I)
.
For every
p\in(1,\infty)
,
existence and uniqueness of
a solution
u
to (
4.13
)
follow from continuity of
B_{a}
,

(4.14)

\forall w\in\mathring{W}^{1}_{p,\{0\}}(I)\;\;\;\forall v\in\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}\!,\{0\}}(I):\quad|B_{a}(w,v)|\leq\overline{a}\,|w|_{W^{1}_{p}(I)}|v|_{W^{1}_{p^{\prime}}(I)},

and the following
inf-sup condition
:

(4.15)

\inf_{0\neq w\in\mathring{W}^{1}_{p,\{0\}}(I)}\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}\!,\{0\}}(I)}\frac{B_{a}(w,v)}{|w|_{W^{1}_{p}(I)}|v|_{W^{1}_{p^{\prime}}(I)}}\geq\underline{a}.

For the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary value problem
(
u(0)=u(b)=0
),
a constructive proof for
the inf-sup condition
on
\mathring{W}_{p}^{1}(I)\times\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}}(I)

has been given
in
[
1
, Proof of Theorem 3.1]
.
We adjust the argument
from
[
1
]
,

\displaystyle\lesssim_{(b,p,\mathcal{P})}h_{\ell}\,\Bigl\|\bigl(1+\tfrac{\overline{a}}{\underline{a}}\bigr)\max_{J\in\mathcal{P}}\|u|_{J}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{p}(J)}\Bigr\|_{L_{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq h_{\ell}\,\bigl(1+\|\overline{a}\|_{L_{4r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}\|\underline{a}^{-1}\|_{L_{4r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}\bigr)\,C_{a,p,2r}^{\sf\,reg}\,\|f\|_{L_{p}(I)},

where we also used (
4.28
)
of Proposition
4.5
.
Thus, the conditions
(

α

) and (

β

)
are satisfied with

\alpha=\beta=1

by Remark
3.26

and the triangle inquality, respectively.

Finally, the
complexity of computing the Galerkin approximation

u_{\ell}
in (

) per one realization
of the Gaussian random field

g(\,\cdot\,,\omega)=\log(a(\,\cdot\,,\omega))

(assumed given)
at discretization level
\ell\in\mathbb{N}

scales linearly with

N_{\ell}=\dim\bigl(S^{1}_{0,\{0\}}(I;\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\bigr)
:
Observe that the linear system
of
N_{\ell}

N_{\ell}

4.19
)
for each sample of

is tridiagonal and symmetric, positive definite
when the standard Courant (“hat functions”)

).
The formation of the

(full, algebraic) tensor product
\otimes^{k}u_{\ell}

\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\lesssim\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}N_{\ell}^{k}

for one sample of the random

4.3.
Approximation of stochastic processes in Hölder spaces

In this subsection we let
T\in(0,\infty)

be a time horizon and consider
approximating higher-order moments of
vector-valued stochastic processes

{X\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to E}

by means of multilevel Monte Carlo methods.
In particular, we derive explicit convergence rates
for the error of the corresponding approximation
in injective tensor norms of Hölder spaces

C^{\delta}([0,T];E)
.
We furthermore detail the implications
of this general result
for the Euler–Maruyama
method for stochastic ordinary differential equations,
and give an overview of further possible applications
including approximations of
stochastic partial differential equations.

In order to properly define the relevant Hölder spaces,
we introduce for a Banach space
(F,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{F})

and
\delta\in(0,1)
the mappings

|\,\cdot\,|_{C^{\delta}([0,T];F)},\,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{C^{\delta}([0,T];F)}\colon C([0,T];F)\to[0,\infty]

on the Banach space

\bigl(C([0,T];F),\|\,\cdot\,\|_{C([0,T];F)}\bigr),\qquad\|f\|_{C([0,T];F)}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|f(t)\|_{F},

of continuous
functions from
[0,T]
to

(F,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{F})

via

\displaystyle|f|_{C^{\delta}([0,T];F)}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in[0,T]\\
s\neq t\end{subarray}}\frac{\|f(s)-f(t)\|_{F}}{|s-t|^{\delta}},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{\delta}([0,T];F)}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|f(t)\|_{F}+|f|_{C^{\delta}([0,T];F)}.

We note that the norm

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{C^{\delta}([0,T];F)}

renders the subspace

C^{\delta}([0,T];F)=\left\{f\in C([0,T];F):\|f\|_{C^{\delta}([0,T];F)}<\infty\right\}\subset C([0,T];F)

of
F
-valued,

\delta
-Hölder continuous
functions a Banach space.
For brevity, we also use the
notation
C^{0}([0,T];F):=C([0,T];F)

to include the case
\delta=0
.

We now consider the
setting of
[
14
, Section 5]
,
that is, we are given
a stochastic process

X\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to E

with continuous sample paths
satisfying the following regularity assumption:
There exists a constant
\bar{\beta}\in(0,1]

such that

(4.29)

\forall\beta\in[0,\bar{\beta})\quad\forall q\in[1,\infty):\quad X\in C^{\beta}([0,T];L_{q}(\Omega;E)).

Recall from Subsection
2.1

that
(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})

is assumed to be a real Banach space.
Additionally, we let

(Y^{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}

be a sequence
of approximations

Y^{N}\!\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to E

to the process
X

with continuous sample paths,
which is known to converge at
the nodes of the temporal partitions

\displaystyle\Theta^{N}

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{t_{0}^{N},t_{1}^{N},\ldots,t_{\#(\Theta^{N})-1}^{N}\bigr\},\quad\#(\Theta^{N})<\infty,\qquad N\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}:}=:t_{0}^{N}<t_{1}^{N}<\ldots<t_{\#(\Theta^{N})-2}^{N}<t_{\#(\Theta^{N})-1}^{N}:=T,

in the strong sense
essentially at the rate
\bar{\beta}\in(0,1]
,
i.e.,

(4.30)

\forall\beta\in[0,\bar{\beta})\quad\forall q\in[1,\infty):\quad\sup\limits_{t\in\Theta^{N}}\bigl\|X(t)-Y^{N}\!(t)\bigr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;E)}\lesssim_{(\bar{\beta},q,T)}\bigl|\triangle t^{N}_{\max}\bigr|^{\beta}\!,

where

\triangle t^{N}_{\max}:=\max_{j\in\{0,\ldots,\#(\Theta^{N})-2\}}|t_{j+1}^{N}-t_{j}^{N}|
.
These partitions
do not necessarily
have to be equidistant or nested.
We only require the following
quasi-uniformity:

(4.31)

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\,\frac{\triangle t^{N}_{\max}}{\triangle t^{N}_{\min}}<\infty,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{N\to\infty}\triangle t^{N}_{\max}=0,

where

\triangle t^{N}_{\min}

is defined as

\triangle t^{N}_{\max}

with the maximum replaced by the minimum.
Furthermore, we assume that,
for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
the approximation

Y^{N}
is linearly interpolated
on the partition
\Theta^{N}
​, i.e.,
for all
j\in\{0,\ldots,\#(\Theta^{N})-2\}
,

(4.32)

Y^{N}\!(s)=\frac{\bigl(t_{j+1}^{N}-s\bigr)\,Y^{N}\!\bigl(t_{j}^{N}\bigr)}{t_{j+1}^{N}-t_{j}^{N}}+\frac{\bigl(s-t_{j}^{N}\bigr)\,Y^{N}\!\bigl(t_{j+1}^{N}\bigr)}{t_{j+1}^{N}-t_{j}^{N}},\qquad s\in\bigl[t_{j}^{N},t_{j+1}^{N}\bigr].

This general setting facilitates
combining the abstract
multilevel Monte Carlo results
of Subsection
3.3

with
[
14
, Corollary 2.11]

and, thus, quantifying
the convergence of the MLMC estimator
for
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X]
based on
approximations
Y^{N_{1}},\ldots,Y^{N_{L}}

in the norm on

L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}C^{\delta}([0,T];E))

for
q\in[p,\infty)
and
\delta\in[0,\bar{\beta})
,
see Theorem
4.8
below.
To this end, the following proposition
which readily follows
from
[
14
, Corollary 2.11]

will be crucial.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(\Theta^{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\subset[0,T]

be a sequence of partitions
fulfilling (
4.31
).
Assume that

{X,Y^{N}\!\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to E}
,

N\in\mathbb{N}
,
are stochastic processes
with continuous sample paths,
such that,
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in[0,T]
,
the random variables

X(t),Y^{N}\!(t)\colon\Omega\to E

are Bochner measurable and
there exists
\bar{\beta}\in(0,1]

such that (
4.29
)
and (
4.30
) hold.
In addition, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
Y^{N}
be linearly interpolated on
the partition
\Theta^{N}
​,
see (
4.32
).

Then, we have for every
q\in[1,\infty)
,

\delta\in[0,\bar{\beta})
,
and all
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\|X\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;C^{\delta}([0,T];E))}+\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigl\|Y^{N}\bigr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;C^{\delta}([0,T];E))}<\infty,

\displaystyle\bigl\|X-Y^{N}\bigr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;C^{\delta}([0,T];E))}\lesssim_{(\bar{\beta},\delta,\epsilon,q,T)}\bigl|\triangle t^{N}_{\max}\bigr|^{\bar{\beta}-\delta-\epsilon}\!.

Theorem 4.8
.

Suppose that all assumptions
of Proposition
4.7

are fulfilled.
In addition, let

(E,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{E})
be of
Rademacher type
p\in[1,2]
,

{k,L\in\mathbb{N}}

and
{\{N_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}}
be a
strictly increasing sequence of integers.
Assume further that,
for all
{\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}}
,

M_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
and

\xi_{\ell,1},\ldots,\xi_{\ell,M_{\ell}}

are independent
copies of the
random variable

\otimes^{k}Y^{N_{\ell}}-\otimes^{k}Y^{N_{\ell-1}}\colon\Omega\to\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}C([0,T];E),\qquad Y^{N_{0}}:=0\in C([0,T];E).

Then, for every

{q\in[p,\infty)}
,

\delta\in[0,\bar{\beta})
,
and all
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
,
we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}_{q,\varepsilon_{s}}^{k,\sf ML}(X;\delta)

\displaystyle:=\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X]-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}C^{\delta}([0,T];E))}

\displaystyle\,\lesssim_{(\bar{\beta},\delta,\epsilon,k,p,q,T)}\bigl|\triangle t^{N_{L}}_{\max}\bigr|^{\bar{\beta}-\delta-\epsilon}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}M_{\ell}^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\bigl|\triangle t^{N_{\ell-1}}_{\max}\bigr|^{\bar{\beta}-\delta-\epsilon}\!.

Proof.

We fix
{q\in[p,\infty)}
,

\delta\in[0,\bar{\beta})
,

\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
,
and first observe that by the triangle inequality
on
L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}C^{\delta}([0,T];E))
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}_{q,\varepsilon_{s}}^{k,\sf ML}(X;\delta)

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X]-\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}\bigl[Y^{N_{L}}\bigr]\bigr\|_{\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}C^{\delta}([0,T];E)}

\displaystyle\;\;\;+\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}\bigl[Y^{N_{L}}\bigr]-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}C^{\delta}([0,T];E))}=:\text{(A)}+\text{(B)}.

Term (A)
can be bounded by
combining Lemma
3.19
with
the stability and convergence results
of Proposition
4.7
,
showing that

(A)

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|X-Y^{N_{L}}\bigr\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;C^{\delta}([0,T];E))}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Bigl[\|X\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;C^{\delta}([0,T];E))}^{i}\bigl\|Y^{N_{L}}\bigr\|_{L_{k}(\Omega;C^{\delta}([0,T];E))}^{k-i-1}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{(\bar{\beta},\delta,\epsilon,k,T)}\bigl|\triangle t^{N_{L}}_{\max}\bigr|^{\bar{\beta}-\delta-\epsilon}\!.

To bound term (B), we may without loss of
generality assume that
\epsilon<\bar{\beta}-\delta

and define

\beta:=\delta+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\in(\delta,\bar{\beta})
.
We then exploit continuous embeddings,
similarly as in the proof of

[
14
, Corollary 5.15]
:
There are constants

C_{1},C_{2}\in(0,\infty)
,
depending only on
\beta,\delta,T
,
such that

\|f\|_{C^{\delta}([0,T];E)}\leq C_{1}\|f\|_{W^{\bar{s}}_{\bar{p}}((0,T);E)}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{C^{\beta}([0,T];E)}\quad\forall f\in C^{\beta}([0,T];E),

where
\bar{s}:=\tfrac{\beta+\delta}{2}\in(\delta,\beta)\subset(0,1)

and

\bar{p}:=\tfrac{4}{\beta-\delta}\in(4,\infty)
.
Here, for
s\in(0,1)
and
q\in[1,\infty)
,
the space

W^{s}_{q}((0,T);E)
denotes the vector-valued
fractional Sobolev space,
see
e.g.
[
35
, Definition 2.5.16]
.
Continuous embeddings
are preserved under (full or symmetric)
injective tensor products
and, thus, we also have that

\|u\|_{\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}C^{\delta}([0,T];E)}\lesssim_{(\beta,\delta,k,T)}\|u\|_{\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}W^{\bar{s}}_{\bar{p}}((0,T);E)}\quad\forall u\in\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}W^{\bar{s}}_{\bar{p}}((0,T);E).

In addition,
we note that

\bar{E}:=W^{\bar{s}}_{\bar{p}}((0,T);E)
has type

\min\{p,\bar{p}\}=p
:
This observation follows from
the fact that both

L^{\bar{p}}((0,T);E)

and

W^{1}_{\bar{p}}((0,T);E)

have type
\min\{p,\bar{p}\}

(see
[
36
, Proposition 7.1.4]
)
combined with the property that

\bar{E}=W^{\bar{s}}_{\bar{p}}((0,T);E)=\bigl(L^{\bar{p}}((0,T);E),W^{1}_{\bar{p}}((0,T);E)\bigr)_{\bar{s},\bar{p}}

is the real interpolation space
between
L^{\bar{p}}((0,T);E)

and

W^{1}_{\bar{p}}((0,T);E)

(cf.
[
35
, Theorem 2.5.17]
)
and the specification of the type of
interpolation spaces

[
36
, Proposition 7.1.3]
.
Thus,
we may conclude
with Theorem
3.24

and Proposition
4.7

that

\displaystyle\text{(B)}\lesssim_{(\beta,\delta,k,T)}\biggl\|\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}\bigl[Y^{N_{L}}\bigr]-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{1}{M_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell,j}\biggr\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;\otimes^{k,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}\bar{E})}

\displaystyle\;\lesssim_{(k,p,q)}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\Bigl[M_{\ell}^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\bigl\|Y^{N_{\ell}}-Y^{N_{\ell-1}}\bigr\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;\bar{E})}\max_{1\leq\ell\leq L}\bigl\|Y^{N_{\ell}}\bigr\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;\bar{E})}^{k-1}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\;\lesssim_{(\beta,\delta,k,T)}\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigl\|Y^{N}\bigr\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;C^{\beta}([0,T];E))}^{k-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\Bigl[M_{\ell}^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\bigl\|X-Y^{N_{\ell-1}}\bigr\|_{L_{kq}(\Omega;C^{\beta}([0,T];E))}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\;\lesssim_{(\bar{\beta},\delta,\epsilon,\widetilde{\epsilon},k,q,T)}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}M_{\ell}^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\bigl|\triangle t^{N_{\ell-1}}_{\max}\bigr|^{\bar{\beta}-\beta-\widetilde{\epsilon}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}M_{\ell}^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\bigl|\triangle t^{N_{\ell-1}}_{\max}\bigr|^{\bar{\beta}-\delta-\frac{\epsilon}{2}-\widetilde{\epsilon}}

holds for all
\widetilde{\epsilon}\in(0,\infty)
,
and the claim follows
for the choice

\widetilde{\epsilon}:=\frac{\epsilon}{2}
.

Example 4.9

(Euler–Maruyama method for SDEs)
.

Let
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a normal filtration on

(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
and let

B\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be
an
m
-dimensional

(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
-Brownian motion
(with continuous sample paths).
For Lipschitz continuous functions

\mu\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\sigma\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
​,
consider the
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
-adapted
stochastic process

X\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

with continuous sample paths
that satisfies

X(t)=X(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\mu(X(s))\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(X(s))\,\mathrm{d}B(s),\quad\text{$\mathbb{P}$-a.s.},

as well as the Euler–Maruyama
approximations

(Y^{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}

to
X
, defined
with respect to equidistant partitions
of size

\triangle t^{N}_{\max}=\triangle t^{N}_{\min}=\nicefrac{{T}}{{N}}

as follows:
Y^{N}\!(0):=X(0)
,
and for
j\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}

and
s\in\bigl(\tfrac{jT}{N},\tfrac{(j+1)T}{N}\bigr]
,

Y^{N}\!(s):=Y^{N}\!\bigl(\tfrac{jT}{N}\bigr)+\bigl(s-\tfrac{jT}{N}\bigr)\mu\bigl(Y^{N}\!\bigl(\tfrac{jT}{N}\bigr)\bigr)+\bigl(\tfrac{sN}{T}-j\bigr)\sigma\bigl(Y^{N}\!\bigl(\tfrac{jT}{N}\bigr)\bigr)\bigl(B\bigl(\tfrac{(j+1)T}{N}\bigr)-B\bigl(\tfrac{jT}{N}\bigr)\bigr).

Then, using the notation of
Theorem
4.8

with
E:=\mathbb{R}^{d}
​,
we conclude that
for every
q\in[2,\infty)
,
\delta\in[0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})
and
all
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
,

\mathrm{err}_{q,\varepsilon_{s}}^{k,\sf ML}(X;\delta)\lesssim_{(\delta,\epsilon,k,q,T)}N_{L}^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\delta-\epsilon\right)}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}M_{\ell}^{-\frac{1}{2}}N_{\ell-1}^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\delta-\epsilon\right)}\!.

​,

Since
\otimes^{k}_{\varepsilon}C([0,T];\mathbb{R})=C([0,T]^{k};\mathbb{R})

5.
Conclusions

We have analyzed the convergence of Monte Carlo sampling
for higher-order moments of
Banach space valued random variables.
Specifically, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
we have derived explicit,
k
-independent
strong convergence rates
in the injective tensor norm
for approximating the
k
th moment
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X]

of a random variable
X\colon\Omega\to E
,
taking values in a Banach space
E
,
by means of

I.

standard Monte Carlo
sampling,
involving no further numerical approximation,
see Theorem
3.16
;

II.

the
single-level Monte Carlo
method,
combining Monte Carlo sampling
with an approximation

X_{1}\colon\Omega\to E_{1}
of
X

to generate
samples in a (usually finite-dimensional)
subspace
E_{1}\subseteq E
,
see Corollary
3.20
;

III.

the
multilevel Monte Carlo
method,
combining Monte Carlo sampling
with a hierarchy of approximations

X_{\ell}\colon\Omega\to E_{\ell}
,

\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
,
in (usually nested, finite-dimensional)
subspaces
E_{\ell}\subseteq E
,
see Theorems
3.24

and
3.25
.

These findings
extend the numerical analysis of
Monte Carlo based algorithms in
computational uncertainty quantification
to a broad range of mathematical models
beyond the classical theory in Hilbert spaces,
which relies on assumed
square-integrability
and bias-variance decompositions.
Several examples have illustrated
the wide scope of
the presently developed theory:
linear, second-order elliptic PDEs with data
affording well-posedness
in
W^{1}_{p}
,
and
stochastic evolution equations
with almost sure path regularity
in Hölder spaces.

The results of
Subsections
3.2
and
3.3

are essential for the error analysis of
Monte Carlo approximations of
k
-point correlations for
every operator equation with random input data
which,
due to modeling or physical constraints,
does not admit a
well-posed formulation
in Hilbert spaces.
We indicate some further applications,
where this is of relevance:
In
[
42
]

Monte Carlo finite difference
discretizations for
scalar, degenerate convection-diffusion equations
with random initial data were considered.
In that case, the
particular structure of the degeneracy in the diffusion coefficient,
imposed from physical properties of the underlying model,
mandated a mathematical formulation
in Banach spaces of type
p<2
.
Assuming random initial data,
the corresponding Monte Carlo error analysis
for mean values of
the solution therefore
required a setting in Banach spaces
as in Corollary
3.15
.
With the abstract MLMC results of
Theorems
3.24
and
3.25
,
the MLMC finite difference
convergence analysis
for first-order moments
of
[
42
]

generalizes to spatiotemporal

k
-point correlations with
k\geq 2
.

Another application is related to fluid flows:
For the compressible Navier–Stokes equations
with spatially periodic solutions,
the (isentropic) equation of state
relates the pressure
P

to the fluid density
\varrho
via

P(\varrho)=a\varrho^{\gamma}
​,
where
a>0
and
\gamma>1

are
physical constants
.
In well-posed variational formulations
[
48
]
,
the density
\varrho(t,\,\cdot\,)
and
the corresponding momentum
\boldsymbol{m}(t,\,\cdot\,)

at time
t\in[0,T]

take values in

L_{\gamma}\bigl(\mathbb{T}^{d}\bigr)
and

L_{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\bigl(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}\bigr)
,
respectively,
where
d\in\{2,3\}

and
\mathbb{T}^{d}
denotes
the
d
-dimensional torus.
With random data,
this entails a Banach space setting of type

p=\min\{\gamma,2\}
for the density
and

p=\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}\in(1,2)

for the momentum.
The convergence of
single-level

Monte Carlo finite volume approximations
for higher-order moments
of
\varrho(t,\,\cdot\,)

and
\boldsymbol{m}(t,\,\cdot\,)

has been discussed
in the recent work
[
17
]
,
using the isomorphic identification

(5.1)

\otimes_{\gamma}^{k}L_{\gamma}\bigl(\mathbb{T}^{d}\bigr)\cong L_{\gamma}\bigl(\mathbb{T}^{kd}\bigr)

(and similarly for the
d
components
of the momentum).
Here,
\otimes_{\gamma}^{k}
indicates
the appropriate Chevet–Saphar
tensor product space,
see e.g.
[
54
, Chapter 6]
.
The present, abstract MLMC results
apply directly to the
setting of
[
17
]
,
implying corresponding convergence results for
multilevel Monte Carlo approximations.

An interesting topic for future
research is to investigate
whether the Monte Carlo
convergence results,
derived for injective tensor
product spaces in this work,
hold also with respect to stronger
cross norms.
In particular, the identification of
tensor products of

L_{p}
-spaces
as in (
5.1
)
raises the question if
it is possible to use
one of the
p
th
Chevet–Saphar tensor norms
d_{p}
or
g_{p}

(see
[
54
, p. 135]
)
if the Banach space
E
has type
p
.
The Chevet–Saphar norms
and the Hilbert tensor norm
w_{2}

are unified by the
tensor norms
\{\alpha_{p,q}\}_{1\leq p,q\leq\infty}

due to Lapresté:

g_{p}=\alpha_{p,1}
,

d_{p}=\alpha_{1,p}
,
and

w_{2}=\alpha_{2,2}
,
see
[
16
, Sections 12.5–12.8]

and the references there.
However, in this generality,
there do not seem to be symmetric versions
of these tensor norms available in the literature.
A corresponding (ML)MC convergence analysis
would thus have to be based on
considerably different arguments.

Furthermore, this work
may be extended to

sparse tensor approximations

as considered in the
Hilbert space setting in
[
3
]
.
Specifically,
we analyzed the MLMC approach
for approximating the
k
th moment
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[X]

using samples of the (exact, full) tensor
product
\otimes^{k}X_{\ell}

on levels
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
.
The formation of this
k
-fold
tensor product
\otimes^{k}X_{\ell}

on level
\ell

typically entails costs in work and memory
of the order
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\lesssim N_{\ell}^{\gamma}

with
\gamma=\max\{\gamma_{1},k\}
,
assuming that
\gamma_{1}
is the exponent
in the asymptotic cost bound
for computing one sample of
X_{\ell}
,
and that one computed sample of
X_{\ell}

requires storage of order
N_{\ell}
.
As it is well-known
in the Hilbert space case,
various consistent
sparse tensor product approximations
allow to reduce this complexity considerably.
For example, for the applications discussed
in Subsections
4.1

and
4.2
,
the sparse tensor product approach
for the MLMC approximation
of
k
th moments
proposed in
[
3
]

for Hilbert spaces
can be leveraged to reduce the parameter
\gamma
in
Theorem
3.25

from
\max\{\gamma_{1},k\}

to
\gamma_{1}+\delta
for some
(arbitrarily small)
\delta>0
.
Yet, in this setting
the error analysis of Theorem
3.24

and, consequently,
also of Theorem
3.25

does not readily apply.

Beyond the MLMC estimation
of
k
th moments
\mathbb{M}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\xi]=\mathbb{E}[\otimes^{k}\xi]
,
one may consider

anisotropic

k
-fold
correlations of the form

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{k}]
.
Here, the vector-valued random variables
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}

entering the anisotropic,
injective tensor product formation
may take values in Banach spaces

E_{1},\ldots,E_{k}
of (possibly different) types

p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\in[1,2]
.
This rather general setting has numerous applications,
and can be analyzed with the techniques in the present paper,
in conjunction with the
multi-index Monte Carlo

approach from
[
30
]
.
Details shall be reported elsewhere.

Appendix A
Tensor norms of symmetric elements of Hilbert spaces

In this section we consider a real separable
Hilbert space
(H,(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{H})

and explicitly compute the
projective and injective tensor norms
of Subsection
2.2

for symmetric elements in
\otimes^{2,s}H

of the form
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\,e_{j}\otimes e_{j}
,
where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}

are orthonormal in
H
,
and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
.

For this purpose,
we need the notion of the

k
-fold Hilbert tensor product space

\otimes^{k}_{w_{2}}H
,
which is defined as the closure
of the (full)
k
-fold algebraic tensor product space

\otimes^{k}H
with respect to the norm
which is induced by the inner product

(A.1)

\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigotimes_{\nu=1}^{k}x_{j,\nu},\sum_{i=1}^{\widetilde{n}}\bigotimes_{\nu^{\prime}=1}^{k}y_{i,\nu^{\prime}}\biggr)_{\!w_{2}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{\widetilde{n}}\prod_{\nu=1}^{k}(x_{j,\nu},y_{i,\nu})_{H}.

In particular, the tensor product space

(\otimes^{k}_{w_{2}}H,(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{w_{2}})

is again a Hilbert space.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume that
(H,(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{H})

is a real separable Hilbert space
and
(e_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis for
H
.
Let the injective and projective tensor norms,

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\varepsilon}
and
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\pi}
,
be defined on
\otimes^{2}H

as in (
2.1
) and (
2.6
),
and let the symmetric injective and projective tensor norms,

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}
and
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\pi_{s}}
,
be defined on
\otimes^{2,s}H
as in
(
2.3
) and (
2.7
), respectively.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\,e_{j}\otimes e_{j}\biggr\|_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\,e_{j}\otimes e_{j}\biggr\|_{\pi_{s}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\lambda_{j}|,

(A.3)

\displaystyle\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\,e_{j}\otimes e_{j}\biggr\|_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\biggl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\,e_{j}\otimes e_{j}\biggr\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|\lambda_{j}|.

Proof.

We set

U_{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\,e_{j}\otimes e_{j}\in\otimes^{2,s}H
.
Then, by the definitions of the projective norms
in (
2.6
) and (
2.7
),
see Remark
2.1
,
it follows that

\|U_{n}\|_{\pi}\leq\|U_{n}\|_{\pi_{s}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\lambda_{j}|\,\|e_{j}\|_{H}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\lambda_{j}|.

Furthermore,
for any representation

U_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{\widetilde{n}}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in\otimes^{2}H

of
U_{n}

we find that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\lambda_{j}|=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|(U_{n},e_{j}\otimes e_{j})_{w_{2}}|=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{\widetilde{n}}x_{i}\otimes y_{i},\,e_{j}\otimes e_{j}\biggr)_{\!w_{2}}\right|,

where
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{w_{2}}

is the inner product on the Hilbert tensor
product space
\otimes^{2}_{w_{2}}H
,
defined as in (
A.1
)
for
k=2
.
Thus, by the triangle and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\lambda_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\widetilde{n}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\bigl|(x_{i}\otimes y_{i},e_{j}\otimes e_{j})_{w_{2}}\bigr|=\sum_{i=1}^{\widetilde{n}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\bigl|(x_{i},e_{j})_{H}\bigr|\bigl|(y_{i},e_{j})_{H}\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\widetilde{n}}\Biggl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(x_{i},e_{j})_{H}^{2}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\Biggl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(y_{i},e_{j})_{H}^{2}\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\widetilde{n}}\|x_{i}\|_{H}\|y_{i}\|_{H}.

By taking the infimum over all representations of
U_{n}\in\otimes^{2}H

we obtain the reverse inequality

\|U_{n}\|_{\pi}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\lambda_{j}|
and,
since also
\|U_{n}\|_{\pi_{s}}\geq\|U_{n}\|_{\pi}
,
this proves (
A.2
).

To show (
A.3
),
let
j_{\star}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
be an index
such that

|\lambda_{j_{\star}}|=\max_{1\leq j\leq M}|\lambda_{j}|
,
and recall the definitions of the injective norms from
(
2.1
) and (
2.3
).
Then, we find

\displaystyle\|U_{n}\|_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq\|U_{n}\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}=\sup_{f\in B_{{H}^{\prime}}}\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\langle f,e_{j}\rangle^{2}\biggr|\geq\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}(e_{j_{\star}},e_{j})_{H}^{2}\biggr|=|\lambda_{j_{\star}}|=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|\lambda_{j}|.

The reverse estimates follow again by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality
combined with the Riesz representation theorem,

\displaystyle\|U_{n}\|_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\sup_{f_{1},f_{2}\in B_{{H}^{\prime}}}\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\langle f_{1},e_{j}\rangle\langle f_{2},e_{j}\rangle\biggr|\leq|\lambda_{j_{\star}}|\sup_{f_{1},f_{2}\in B_{{H}^{\prime}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\langle f_{1},e_{j}\rangle|\,|\langle f_{2},e_{j}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|\lambda_{j}|\sup_{v_{1},v_{2}\in B_{H}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|(v_{1},e_{j})_{H}|\,|(v_{2},e_{j})_{H}|\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|\lambda_{j}|.

Thus,

\|U_{n}\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}\leq\|U_{n}\|_{\varepsilon}\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|\lambda_{j}|

completing the proof of (
A.3
).
∎

Remark A.2

(Relation of
\otimes^{2,s}H

to self-adjoint finite-rank linear operators)
.

In the setting of Lemma
A.1
,
we may associate
a self-adjoint linear operator on the Hilbert space
H

with the element

U_{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\,e_{j}\otimes e_{j}

in the symmetric algebraic tensor product space
\otimes^{2,s}H
.
More specifically,
we can define the self-adjoint
finite-rank linear operator
T_{U_{n}}\colon H\to H

associated with
U_{n}
by

T_{U_{n}}x:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}(x,e_{j})_{H}\,e_{j}
,
for every
x\in H
.
Using this definition, the norm identities
in (
A.2
) and (
A.3
)
can be reformulated in terms of
the trace-class (or nuclear) norm,

\|T_{U_{n}}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1}(H)}:=\operatorname{tr}(|T_{U_{n}}|)
,
and the operator norm,

\|T_{U_{n}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H)}:=\sup_{x\in B_{H}}\|T_{U_{n}}x\|_{H}
,
of
T_{U_{n}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\textstyle\|U_{n}\|_{\pi}=\|U_{n}\|_{\pi_{s}}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}|\lambda_{j}|=\|T_{U_{n}}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1}(H)},

\displaystyle\|U_{n}\|_{\varepsilon}=\|U_{n}\|_{\varepsilon_{s}}=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|\lambda_{j}|=\|T_{U_{n}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H)},

see e.g.
[
57
, Theorem 14.15.(1) and Theorem 8.11]

for the operator norm identities.

More generally,
to every element
U\in\otimes^{2,s}H
,
we can associate a self-adjoint linear operator
T_{U}\colon H\to H
,
whose action on
x\in H
is defined by

(T_{U}x,y)_{H}=(U,x\otimes y)_{w_{2}}\quad\forall y\in H.

Here, the Riesz representation theorem
ensures that the linear operator

T_{U}
is well-defined.
The implied linear mapping

{\mathcal{I}:U\mapsto T_{U}}

extends continuously
to an isometric isomorphism
between the
symmetric projective tensor product space
\otimes^{2,s}_{\pi_{s}}H

and the space of self-adjoint trace-class linear operators on
H

(respectively, between the
symmetric injective tensor product space
\otimes^{2,s}_{\varepsilon_{s}}H

and the space of self-adjoint compact linear operators on
H
).

Appendix B
A consequence of Slepian’s inequality

In this section we restate the version of Slepian’s inequality
for finite-dimensional Gaussian processes as
formulated by Fernique
[
18
]
.
We subsequently use it to derive a comparison result for
real-valued Gaussian processes indexed by the closed unit ball

B_{{E}^{\prime}}
of the dual of a real Banach space
E
,
see Lemma
B.2
.
This result is
needed in Subsection
3.3

to prove convergence
of multilevel Monte Carlo methods.

The following theorem is taken from
[
18
, Theorem 2.1.2]
.
We note that a more general version of Slepian’s inequality,
which includes Fernique’s formulation
as a special case, can be found
in
[
33
, Theorem 2.8]
.

Theorem B.1
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and

X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{N})^{\top}
​,

Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{N})^{\top}
be
two centered Gaussian
random vectors
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
​, defined
on a complete probability space

(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

with expectation
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
.
Assume further that

\forall i,j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}:\quad\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|X_{i}-X_{j}|^{2}\bigr]\leq\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|Y_{i}-Y_{j}|^{2}\bigr],

and let
G\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

be convex and increasing. Then,

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}G\Bigl(\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|X_{i}-X_{j}|\Bigr)\leq\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}G\Bigl(\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|Y_{i}-Y_{j}|\Bigr).

Lemma B.2
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
and assume that

(g_{j})_{j=1}^{M}
is
an orthogaussian family
on a complete probability space

(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

(with expectation
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
),
and that
\Psi\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}

is a continuous function
such that
\Psi(0,0)=0
.
Let
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{M},\,(y_{j})_{j=1}^{M}\subset E
,
and define the
centered Gaussian process

\mathcal{G}_{1}\colon B_{{E}^{\prime}\!}\times\widetilde{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}

on
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})

indexed by the closed unit ball
B_{{E}^{\prime}\!}

in the dual space
{E}^{\prime}
by

(B.1)

\mathcal{G}_{1}(f):=\sum_{j=1}^{M}g_{j}\Psi\bigl(f(x_{j}),f(y_{j})\bigr),\quad f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}\!}.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{2}\colon B_{{E}^{\prime}\!}\times\widetilde{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}

be a second centered Gaussian process on

(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
such that

(B.2)

\forall f,h\in B_{{E}^{\prime}\!}:\quad\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f)-\mathcal{G}_{1}(h)|^{2}\bigr]\leq\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|\mathcal{G}_{2}(f)-\mathcal{G}_{2}(h)|^{2}\bigr].

Then, for all
q\in[1,\infty)
we have that

(B.3)

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\bigl(\sup\nolimits_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}\!}|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f)|\bigr)^{q}\Bigr]\leq 2^{q}\,\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\bigl(\sup\nolimits_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}\!}|\mathcal{G}_{2}(f)|\bigr)^{q}\Bigr].

Proof.

Set
N_{0}:=0

and
f_{0}:=0\in{E}^{\prime}
​.
Given
M\in\mathbb{N}
and

(x_{j})_{j=1}^{M},\,(y_{j})_{j=1}^{M}\subset E
,
for
f\in{E}^{\prime}
and
\delta\in(0,\infty)
,
define the subset

U_{\delta}(f):=\Bigl\{h\in{E}^{\prime}:\max_{1\leq j\leq M}|f(x_{j})-h(x_{j})|<\delta,\ \max_{1\leq j\leq M}|f(y_{j})-h(y_{j})|<\delta\Bigr\}\subseteq{E}^{\prime}\!.

Then,
for every
f\in{E}^{\prime}
and all
\delta\in(0,\infty)
,
the set
U_{\delta}(f)
is open
(more precisely, an open neighborhood of
f
)
in
{E}^{\prime}

with respect to the

\text{weak}^{*}
-topology on the dual space
{E}^{\prime}
​.
By the Banach–Alaoglu theorem the closed unit ball

B_{{E}^{\prime}}
is
\text{weak}^{*}
-com- pact.
Hence, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the open cover

\bigcup_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}}U_{\frac{1}{n}}(f)\supseteq B_{{E}^{\prime}}

contains a finite subcover.
Iteratively, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
one can find
an integer
N_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
,
satisfying
N_{n}>N_{n-1}
,
and elements

f_{N_{n-1}+1},\ldots,f_{N_{n}}\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}

such that

B_{{E}^{\prime}}\subseteq\bigcup_{\nu=1}^{N_{n}}U_{\frac{1}{n}}(f_{\nu}).

Note, in particular,
that this definition of

f_{1},\ldots,f_{N_{n}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
implies nestedness,

(f_{1},\ldots,f_{N_{n}})\subseteq(f_{1},\ldots,f_{N_{m}})

for
n<m
.

Next,
we define for every
non-negative integer
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

the real-valued centered Gaussian random variables

X_{\nu}:=\mathcal{G}_{1}(f_{\nu})

and

Y_{\nu}:=\mathcal{G}_{2}(f_{\nu})
.
By assumption (
B.2
) we then have
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\{0,\ldots,N_{n}\}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|X_{\nu}-X_{\nu^{\prime}}|^{2}\bigr]=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f_{\nu})-\mathcal{G}_{1}(f_{\nu^{\prime}})|^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|\mathcal{G}_{2}(f_{\nu})-\mathcal{G}_{2}(f_{\nu^{\prime}})|^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|Y_{\nu}-Y_{\nu^{\prime}}|^{2}\bigr].

By Fernique’s version of Slepian’s inequality,
see Theorem
B.1
,
applied for the convex increasing function

G(t):=t^{q}
,
t\geq 0
,
and by using the fact that
f_{0}=0\in{E}^{\prime}

implies that
\mathcal{G}_{1}(f_{0})=0

holds
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s.,
we find that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
q\in[1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\bigl(

\displaystyle\max\nolimits_{0\leq\nu\leq N_{n}}|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f_{\nu})|\bigr)^{q}\Bigr]\leq\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\bigl(\max\nolimits_{0\leq\nu,\nu^{\prime}\leq N_{n}}|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f_{\nu})-\mathcal{G}_{1}(f_{\nu^{\prime}})|\bigr)^{q}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\bigl(\max\nolimits_{0\leq\nu,\nu^{\prime}\leq N_{n}}|X_{\nu}-X_{\nu^{\prime}}|\bigr)^{q}\Bigr]\leq\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\bigl(\max\nolimits_{0\leq\nu,\nu^{\prime}\leq N_{n}}|Y_{\nu}-Y_{\nu^{\prime}}|\bigr)^{q}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2^{q}\,\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\bigl(\max\nolimits_{0\leq\nu\leq N_{n}}|Y_{\nu}|\bigr)^{q}\Bigr]\leq 2^{q}\,\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\bigl(\sup\nolimits_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}\!}}|\mathcal{G}_{2}(f)|\bigr)^{q}\Bigr].

To derive (
B.3
),
it remains to prove that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|S_{n}\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}=\|S_{*}\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}
,
where

S_{n}:=\max\limits_{0\leq\nu\leq N_{n}}|X_{\nu}|=\max\limits_{0\leq\nu\leq N_{n}}|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f_{\nu})|\qquad\text{and}\qquad S_{*}:=\sup\limits_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}\!}}|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f)|.

By the assumptions on the process
\mathcal{G}_{1}
in (
B.1
),
there exists a set

\widetilde{\Omega}_{0}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
with

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}(\widetilde{\Omega}_{0})=0

such that

\overline{g}(\widetilde{\omega}):=1+\max_{1\leq j\leq M}|g_{j}(\widetilde{\omega})|<\infty

for all

\widetilde{\omega}\in\widetilde{\Omega}\setminus\widetilde{\Omega}_{0}
.
Fix
\widetilde{\omega}\in\widetilde{\Omega}\setminus\widetilde{\Omega}_{0}

and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.
Then, there exists

f^{\epsilon}=f^{\epsilon}(\widetilde{\omega})\in B_{{E}^{\prime}}
such that

\sup_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}\!}}|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f)(\widetilde{\omega})|\leq|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f^{\epsilon})(\widetilde{\omega})|+\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

In addition,
there exists
\delta_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{\omega})\in(0,\infty)
,
such that the implication

(B.4)

h\in U_{\delta_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{\omega})}(f^{\epsilon})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f^{\epsilon})(\widetilde{\omega})-\mathcal{G}_{1}(h)(\widetilde{\omega})|<\frac{\epsilon}{2}

holds.
Indeed, by continuity of
\Psi\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}

we may choose
\delta_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{\omega})\in(0,\infty)

such that

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq M}

\displaystyle\bigl\{\max\{|f^{\epsilon}(x_{j})-h(x_{j})|,|f^{\epsilon}(y_{j})-h(y_{j})|\}\bigr\}<\delta_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{\omega})

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad\max_{1\leq j\leq M}\bigl|\Psi\bigl(f^{\epsilon}(x_{j}),f^{\epsilon}(y_{j})\bigr)-\Psi\bigl(h(x_{j}),h(y_{j})\bigr)\bigr|<\frac{\epsilon}{2}\,M^{-1}\overline{g}(\widetilde{\omega})^{-1}\!.

Furthermore, by definition of
the sequences

(N_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}
and

(f_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq B_{{E}^{\prime}}
,
there exist integers

n_{\epsilon}=n_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{\omega})\in\mathbb{N}

and

\nu_{\star}=\nu_{\star}(\widetilde{\omega})\in\{1,\ldots,N_{n_{\epsilon}}\}

such that

f^{\epsilon}\in U_{\delta_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{\omega})}(f_{\nu_{\star}}).

By combining this observation
with (
B.4
)
we conclude
that

\bigl||\mathcal{G}_{1}(f^{\epsilon})(\widetilde{\omega})|-|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f_{\nu_{\star}})(\widetilde{\omega})|\bigr|\leq|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f^{\epsilon})(\widetilde{\omega})-\mathcal{G}_{1}(f_{\nu_{\star}})(\widetilde{\omega})|<\frac{\epsilon}{2},

and

\displaystyle S_{*}(\widetilde{\omega})

\displaystyle-S_{n_{\epsilon}}(\widetilde{\omega})=\sup\limits_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}\!}}|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f)(\widetilde{\omega})|-\max\limits_{0\leq\nu\leq N_{n_{\epsilon}}}|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f_{\nu})(\widetilde{\omega})|

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}\!}}|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f)(\widetilde{\omega})|-|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f_{\nu_{\star}})(\widetilde{\omega})|\leq|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f^{\epsilon})(\widetilde{\omega})|-|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f_{\nu_{\star}})(\widetilde{\omega})|+\frac{\epsilon}{2}<\epsilon

follows.
This shows that, for almost all
\widetilde{\omega}\in\widetilde{\Omega}
,

S_{*}(\widetilde{\omega})=\sup_{f\in B_{{E}^{\prime}\!}}|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f)(\widetilde{\omega})|=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\max_{0\leq\nu\leq N_{n}}|\mathcal{G}_{1}(f_{\nu})(\widetilde{\omega})|=\lim_{n\to\infty}S_{n}(\widetilde{\omega}).

Since the
non-negative random variables
(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

are non-decreasing in
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
-almost surely,
the
L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})
-convergence

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|S_{n}\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}=\|S_{*}\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})}

follows from the monotone convergence theorem.
∎

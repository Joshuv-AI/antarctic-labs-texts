Prismatic cohomology of rigid analytic spaces over de Rham period ring


Abstract
. Inspired by Bhatt-Scholze [ BS19 ] , in this article, we introduce prismatic cohomology for rigid analytic spaces with l.c.i singularities, with coefficients over Fontaine’s de Rham period ring B dR + {\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}} .



Haoyang Guo

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and main theorems

Let
k
be a complete discretely valued
p
-adic field that has a perfect residue field, and let
K
be its completed algebraic closure.
In the seminal work of Bhatt and Scholze
[
BS19
]
, they introduce the notion of prisms and prismatic cohomology for a
p
-adic smooth formal scheme
X
over
\mathcal{O}_{k}
.
A prism can be defined as a diagram of algebras

(A\longrightarrow A/I\longleftarrow R),

where
\Spf(R)
is an open subset of
X
, and
A
is equipped with an additional structure of a lift of Frobenius operator that satisfies various conditions (
[
BS19
, Section 2, 3]
).
The resulted cohomology theory, namely prismatic cohomology, turns out to specialize to many other important
p
-adic cohomology in the
p
-adic Hodge theory, and thus provides a powerful tool to study the relation of different cohomology associated to the given formal scheme
X
.

Let
X
now be a rigid space over
K
, and let
\xi
be a fixed generator of the kernel ideal for the natural surjection
\theta:{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\rightarrow K
,
where
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
is Fontaine’s de Rham period ring (
[
Fon94
]
).
In this article, we study a rational analogue of prismatic cohomology for rigid spaces over
K
, with coefficients in
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
.
We start by defining the notion of a prism in our setting.

Definition 1.1.1
.

A
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-prism over
X
is defined as a diagram of
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-algebras as below

(B\longrightarrow B/\xi\longleftarrow R),

where
\mathrm{Spa}(R)
is an affinoid open subset of
X
, and
B
is a
\xi
-adic complete,
\xi
-torsionfree algebra over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
satisfies the finite type condition as in
Definition

2.2.1
(i).

One essential difference between our notion of prisms and that of Bhatt-Scholze in
[
BS19
]
is that there is
no
Frobenius structure in our definition.
Nonetheless, the resulted cohomology theory over the opposite category of prisms still enjoys many good properties when
X
has mild singularities.

Now we state our main results.

Theorem 1.1.2
.

There is a
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-linear cohomology theory on the category of
K
-rigid spaces

X\longmapsto R\Gamma_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}):=R\Gamma(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}),

such that for an affinoid rigid space
X
that has l.c.i singularities, it satisfies the following property (as
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebras over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
):

(1)

(Pro-étale comparison) There is a natural map

R\Gamma_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})\longrightarrow R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{B}^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}}),

where the right hand side is pro-étale cohomology of the deRham period sheaf as in
[
Sch13
]
.

Remark 1.1.3

(Cohomology of
{\mathbb{B}^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}}

Let

Remark 1.1.4

(Prismatic

.

In the recent work

Remark 1.1.5

(Globalization)
.

Theorem

1.1.2
naturally extends to proper rigid spaces that has l.c.i singularities (with
L\eta_{\xi}R\Gamma_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})
in
Theorem

1.1.2
(ii) replaced by a sheaf version).

When
X
is proper smooth, by Hodge-Tate filtration and the finiteness of coherent cohomology, each
\mathrm{H}^{i}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})
is finite generated over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
and lives in cohomological degree
0\leq i\leq 2\dim(X)
.
In fact, using the primitive comparison theorem (
[
Sch12
]
) one can show that each
\mathrm{H}^{i}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})
is finite free over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
.

However, if
X
is not smooth, the Hodge-Tate filtration in
Theorem

1.1.2
(iii) has infinite graded pieces.
In the special case when
X=Y_{K}
for a proper rigid space
Y
over a discretely valued subfield
k
, the Hodge-Tate filtration splits into an infinite direct sum
\moplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}R\Gamma(X,L\wedge^{i}\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{X/K}(-i)[-i])
, which is in general infinite dimensional for proper rigid spaces that have l.c.i singularities.
Thus one loses the finiteness if we go beyond the smooth proper assumption.

On the other hand, combine
Theorem

1.1.2
(i) and (ii), one obtain the following composition

R\Gamma_{\inf}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})\longrightarrow R\Gamma_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})\longrightarrow R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{B}^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}}).

It can be shown that the composition is an isomorphism after inverting
\xi
(
[
Guo21
, Theoren 7.3.2]
), and thus
R\Gamma_{\inf}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})[\frac{1}{\xi}]
is a direct summand of
R\Gamma_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})[\frac{1}{\xi}]
, and contains all Galois invariants when
X=Y_{K}
is defined over
k
(notice the latter is finite when
Y
is proper).
One can then ask if the complement of
R\Gamma_{\inf}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})
in
R\Gamma_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})
has bounded torsion, which would imply the following:

Question 1.1.6
.

Are the maps in
Theorem

1.1.2
(i) and (ii) inducing isomorphisms after inverting
\xi
?

In the case when
X=Y_{K}
is defined over the discretely valued subfield
k
, it can be shown that the boundedness of torsion in
R\Gamma_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})
follows from the following question on analytic derived de Rham cohomology of
Y/k
.

Question 1.1.7
.

Let
Y
be a rigid space over
k
that has l.c.i singularities.
Is there a positive integer
N
, such that the following map of cohomology is always zero for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}

\mathrm{H}^{i}(Y,{\mathrm{Fil}}^{n+N}{\widehat{\mathrm{dR}}^{\mathrm{an}}}_{Y/k})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{i}(Y,{\mathrm{Fil}}^{n}{\widehat{\mathrm{dR}}^{\mathrm{an}}}_{Y/k}).

Here
{\mathrm{Fil}}^{\bullet}
is the natural filtration on the analytic derived de Rham complex.

Remark 1.1.8
.

The question has a more classical variant in the complex algebraic geometry.
Namely for a complex algebraic variety
Y
that has l.c.i singularities, can we find a positive integer
N
so that the following map is always zero:

\mathrm{H}^{i}(Y,{\mathrm{Fil}}^{n+N}\widehat{\mathrm{dR}}_{Y/\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{i}(Y,{\mathrm{Fil}}^{n}\widehat{\mathrm{dR}}_{Y/\mathbb{C}}),

where
{\mathrm{Fil}}^{\bullet}
is the natural filtration on the Hodge-completed derived de Rham complex
\widehat{\mathrm{dR}}_{Y/\mathbb{C}}
.

If
Y
is of dimension zero, one can show the above by hand, using explicit calculation for a closed immersion into
\mathbb{A}^{1}
.
On the other hand, it is shown in
[
Bha12
, Corollary 5.4]
that the map of cohomology
{\mathrm{Fil}}^{n}\widehat{\mathrm{dR}}_{Y/\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathrm{dR}}_{Y/\mathbb{C}}
is the zero map for
n>>0
.

.

(Condensed mathematics)

As byproducts, we also compute Galois invariant of infinitesmal cohomology, and give a condensed base extension formula of infinitesimal cohomology, as in

1.2.
Simpson’s functor

Recall from
[
Simp
]
that Simpson introduced an equivalence between the category of
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathrm{op}}
-filtered vector spaces over a field and the category of modules over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
, using a Rees algebra construction.
The functor is defined as follows

(V,{\mathrm{Fil}}^{\bullet})\longmapsto\underset{i\in\mathbb{Z}}{{\mathrm{colim}}}\frac{{\mathrm{Fil}}^{i}V}{t^{i}},

where
t
is the coordinate of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
, and admits a natural action by
\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
The functor can be extended to an equivalence in the derived level (
[
Mou19
]
), and its pullback along
\{t=0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}
is the direct sum of graded pieces for
(V,{\mathrm{Fil}}^{\bullet})
.

Analogous to the above construction, we introduce a variant of Simpson’s functor, sending a
\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{op}}
-filtered
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-complex onto the
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-complex as below

\Psi:(M,{\mathrm{Fil}}^{\bullet})\longmapsto(\underset{i\in\mathbb{N}}{{\mathrm{colim}}}\frac{{\mathrm{Fil}}^{i}M}{\xi^{i}})^{\wedge},

where
(-)^{\wedge}
is the derived
\xi
-completion.
One of our main observations is that prismatic cohomology over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
can be computed by applying Simpson’s functor at infinitesimal cohomology of
X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
with a lifted infinitesimal filtration.

Theorem 1.2.1
.

Let
X
be an affinoid rigid space over
K
that has l.c.i singularities.
Then we have the following formula

\Psi(R\Gamma_{\inf}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}),\widetilde{\mathrm{Fil}}^{\bullet})\cong R\Gamma_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}).

Here
\widetilde{\mathrm{Fil}}^{\bullet}
is a lift of the infinitesimal filtration of
X/K
along
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\rightarrow K
, and is not canonical in general (see discussion around
Definition

4.3.9
).
We use the following two examples to illustrate what the formula looks like.

Example 1.2.2
.

Assume
X=\mathrm{Spa}(R)
is smooth affinoid and admits a compatible system of lifts
\mathrm{Spa}(\widetilde{R}_{e})
as in
Remark

1.1.3
.
Then
R\Gamma_{\inf}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})
is isomorphic to
\widetilde{\Omega}^{\bullet}
, and the lifted infinitesimal filtration is defined as the Hodge filtration

\widetilde{\mathrm{Fil}}^{i}\widetilde{\Omega}^{\bullet}=(\widetilde{\Omega}^{i}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\widetilde{\Omega}^{n})[-i].

In particular, applying Simpson’s functor at this filtered object, we get

\Psi(R\Gamma_{\inf}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}),\widetilde{\mathrm{Fil}}^{\bullet})=\widetilde{\Omega}^{\bullet}\cup\frac{\widetilde{\Omega}^{\geq 1}}{\xi}\cup\cdots\cup\frac{\widetilde{\Omega}^{d}}{\xi^{d}},

which is exactly the
\xi
-divided de Rham complex as in
Remark

1.1.3
.
Here the union is taken inside of
\widetilde{\Omega}^{\bullet}[\frac{1}{\xi}]
.

Example 1.2.3
.

Another special case is when
X=Y_{K}
for an affinoid rigid space
Y
over the discretely valued subfield
k
.
Under the assumption, we can take the completed base extension of the infinitesimal filtration of
Y/k
along a fixed embedding
k\rightarrow{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}

\widetilde{\mathrm{Fil}}^{\bullet}:={\mathrm{Fil}}^{\bullet}_{\inf}(Y/k)\widehat{\otimes}_{k}{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}},

which in particular admits a
G_{k}
-action.
We warn the reader that this is
not
the same as the infinitesimal filtration for
R\Gamma_{\inf}(Y_{K}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})
.

1.3.
Simplicial resolution

Another ingredient of the proof is the simplicial resolution and the left Kan extension for affinoid algebras.
Not as in the algebraic setting, where one can use polynomial rings of infinite variables to resolve any algebra; in the rigid geometry a typical algebraic object is topologically generated by finite variables.
It is then natural to ask if we can resolve an affinoid algebra by convergent power series rings of finite variables, thus within the rigid geometry.
We give a positive answer to this question, and show in
Section

5.2
that an affinoid algebra admits a simplicial resolution of Tate algebras with finite variables.
Moreover, we use this to show that the analytic cotangent complex
\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}
and the analytic derived de Rham complex
{\widehat{\mathrm{dR}}^{\mathrm{an}}}
for an affinoid algebra can be computed using the aforementioned resolution (
Proposition

5.3.1
).

With the help of the simplicial resolution, we can apply the left Kan extension at prismatic cohomology of smooth affinoid algebras.
This allows us to define a notion of
derived prismatic cohomology

L{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}
for an arbitrary affinoid algebra
R
over
K
(see
Definition

5.3.4
), analogous to
[
BS19
, Section 7]
.
As expected, derived prismatic cohomology and usual prismatic cohomology coincide for l.c.i singularities.

Theorem 1.3.1
.

Let
X
be a rigid space over
K
that has l.c.i singularities.
There is a natural isomorphism of
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebras

L{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}\longrightarrow R\Gamma_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}).

This in particular allows us to define the Hodge-Tate filtration on reduced prismatic cohomology
R\Gamma_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})\otimes^{L}_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}K
for rigid spaces with l.c.i singularities, which is
Theorem

1.1.2
(iii).

Remark 1.3.2
.

For our application, we consider a slightly more general situation.
Namely we show the existence of the finite type resolutions for a pair
(B,I)
, consisting of a topologically finite type algebra
B
over
{\mathrm{A_{inf}}}/\xi^{e}
(resp.
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}/\xi^{e}
, or
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
) together with an ideal
I
.
This roughly means that any closed immersion of topologically finite type affine formal schemes (resp. affinoid rigid spaces) can be simplicially resolved by regular closed immersions.
In the special case when
I
is the zero ideal, we get the aforementioned simplicial resolution for a topological algebra itself.

Remark 1.3.3
.

As both derived prismatic cohomology and the usual prismatic cohomology are well-defined for general rigid spaces, it is a natural question if
Theorem

1.3.1
holds without the assumption on
X
, or if the cone of the comparing map always has bounded
\xi
-torsion.
We hope to investigate the question in the future.

1.4.
Other comments

Remark 1.4.1
.

We want to mention that one can define an analogous notion of prismatic cohomology for complex algebraic varieties, replacing

In the smooth case, this was studied independently in an unpublished article by Nils Waßmuth (

In general, similar to what we do in this article, one can use Simpson’s equivalence to study prismatic cohomology for complex varieties that has l.c.i singularities, using derived de Rham cohomology (
[

]
,
[
Bha12
]
) and Hartshorne’s de Rham cohomology over
\mathbb{C}
(
[
Har75
]
).

Remark 1.4.2
.

(Crystal)
As in
[
BS21
]
, our prismatic site
X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
admits a natural notion of
crystals
, which we study in
Section

3
.
Analogous to the crystal-connection translation in the crystalline theory, a prismatic crystal corresponds to a quasi-nilpotent integrable connection that have log poles at
\{\xi=0\}
(Definition
3.0.3
,
Proposition

3.0.12
).
In fact, important resources of prismatic crystals are those coming from the infinitesimal site
X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{\inf}
, whose associated log-connections are integral.
Moreover, our
Theorem

1.2.1
can be extended to prismatic crystals that come from the infinitesimal site.

Remark 1.4.3
.

(Non-noetherian enlargement)
As mentioned in the beginning, our prismatic site (
Definition

1.1.1
) consists only of those
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-algebras that are noetherian.
However, one can still ask if we could allow other interesting but non-noetherian rings.
In fact, to compare prismatic cohomology with pro-étale cohomology as in
Theorem

1.1.2
(i), we consider an enlarged version of the prismatic site (with the same indiscrete topology) in
Section

7.2
, adding algebras coming from perfectoid rings.
There we prove that cohomology of the enlarged site coincides with the smaller one in
Definition

1.1.1
.
Similarly, adding
{\mathbb{B}^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}}(R)
for quasi-regular semiperfectoid rings
R
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
would not change the cohomology.

2.1.
Algebraic preliminary

We start with an algebraic preliminary on power-bounded functions of a finite type scheme over a rigid space.
The result here will be used later to show the weak finality of the prismatic envelope (

).

Proposition 2.1.1
.

Let

be a topologically finite type algebra over
K
, and let
S
be a finite type algebra over
R
.
Assume there exists a
K
-linear homomorphism
\phi:K\langle T\rangle\rightarrow S
.
Then the image
\phi(T)
is finite over
R
.

When
R
is the
p
-adic field

, this is proved in
[

, Lemma 5.5]

Proof.

Step 1

We first reduce to the case when
S
is normal.
Let
S_{\mathrm{red}}
be the reduced quotient of the noetherian ring
S
, and let
S_{\mathrm{nor}}
be the normalization of
S_{\mathrm{red}}
.
We then have the diagram

\textstyle{K\langle{T}\rangle\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{S\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{S_{\mathrm{red}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{S_{\mathrm{nor}}}
\textstyle{R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Denote

, and

to be the image of

in

,
S_{\mathrm{red}}
and

separately.
Assume there exists a monic polynomial

over

As the ring

is noetherian, the normalization

is injective.
In particular,

is zero, which then implies that

Thus by taking

So it suffices to show that by passing to the normalization of

Step 2

Denote by

, and

[

]

Here we note that

For each affinoid open subset

of

, the composition of rings

is automatically continuous by

.
In particular, this induces a map of rigid spaces

, where

is the unit disc over

.
Here we note that the ring

is contained in the global section

of the structure sheaf.
Moreover, to give a map from the rigid space

To see this, it suffices to use the criterion of a power-bounded function over a rigid space: an element

is power-bounded if and only if

for any rigid point

(
[
BGR
, 6.2.3/1]
).

So the image

is a power-bounded elements in

.

As the affine scheme

is finite type over the noetherian scheme

with dense image, where

is a proper

-scheme.
In particular, by taking the relative analytification

, the induced morphism

is an open immersion of rigid spaces over

whose complement is Zariski closed of codimension

in

.
Moreover, notice that since

is normal, it is also an open dense subspace in the normalization of

We apply the first Riemann extension theorem (

\lx@sectionsign

But since

2.2.
Prismatic site

In this subsection, we introduce the basics on the prismatic site over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
.

We start by defining the prismatic site.

Definition 2.2.1
.

Let
X=\mathrm{Spa}(R)
be an affinoid rigid space over
K
.

(1)

An
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-
prism
(or in short a
prism
)
(B,\xi B)
over
X
is defined by the following diagram of maps

\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B/\xi}
\textstyle{R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces,}
\scriptstyle{f}

where
B
is a noetherian
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-algebra satisfying the conditions below:

•

it is
\xi
-torsion free and
\xi
-adic complete;

•

each quotient
B/\xi^{m}
is a finite type algebra over some
{\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,m}}}\langle T_{i}\rangle
.

The map
B\rightarrow B/\xi
is the natural surjection, and
f:R\rightarrow B/\xi
is a map of
K
-algebras.

(2)

A map of prisms
(B,\xi B)\rightarrow(B^{\prime},\xi B^{\prime})
over
R
is defined as a commutative diagram between the data above.

(3)

The
prismatic site of
X=\mathrm{Spa}(R)
over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
, denoted as
X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
, is defined on the opposite category of all
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-prisms over
X
and is equipped with the indiscrete topology.

Remark 2.2.2
.

By definition, given a prism
(B,\xi B)
over
R
, the only covering of the prism
(B,\xi B)
is the identity map from itself.

Remark 2.2.3
.

Except for everything is in the rational level (namely over
\mathbb{Q}
), the most obvious simplification from
[
BS19
]
is that a prism does not come with a delta structure defined integrally.
Namely there is no Frobenius lift structure.

Remark 2.2.4
.

To save us from complicated notations, we will abbreviate notation of a prism
(B,\xi B)
simply into
B
from time to time, when the meaning is clear.

Next we define the notion of the prismatic envelope.

Definition 2.2.5
.

Let
B\rightarrow B^{\prime}
be a surjection of complete noetherian
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-algebras, and let
I
be the kernel ideal.
The
prismatic envelope
of
B^{\prime}
in
B
, is defined as the
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-algebra

B[\frac{I}{\xi}]^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{tf}}:=\left(B[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]/(\xi x_{i}-f_{i},1\leq i\leq m)\right)^{\wedge}/(\xi^{\infty}\text{-}torsion).

where
(f_{i})
is a finite set of generators for the ideal
I
, and
(-)^{\wedge}
is the classical
\xi
-adic completion.

To make sense of the notation that is independent of the choice of generators, we have the following easy property.

Lemma 2.2.6
.

Let
I
be an ideal of a noetherian
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-algebra
B
, and let
(f_{i})\subset(f_{i},g)
be two finite sets of generators of
I
.
Then the natural map of rings below is an isomorphism

\left(B[x_{i}]/(\xi x_{i}-f_{i})\right)^{\wedge}/(\xi^{\infty}\text{-}torsion)\longrightarrow\left(B[x_{i},y]/(\xi x_{i}-f_{i};\xi y-g)\right)^{\wedge}/(\xi^{\infty}\text{-}torsion).

In particular, the ring
B[\frac{I}{\xi}]^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{tf}}/(\xi^{\infty}\text{-}torsion)
in
Definition

2.2.5
is independent of the choice of generators of
I
.

Proof.

As a finite type algebra over
B
is noetherian, its formal completion along
\xi=0
identifies the
\xi^{\infty}
-elements before and after the completion (
[
Sta
, Tag 05E9]
).
In particular, we can switch the order of the
\xi
-completion and the
\xi
-torsionfree quotient, and it suffices to show the isomorphism of the map between incomplete algebras

\left(B[x_{i}]/(\xi x_{i}-f_{i})\right)/(\xi^{\infty}\text{-}torsion)\longrightarrow\left(B[x_{i},y]/(\xi x_{i}-f_{i};\xi y-g)\right)/(\xi^{\infty}\text{-}torsion).

By assumption, since
(f_{i})
generates the ideal, we can write
g=\sum a_{j}f_{j}
.
So we get the formula in the second ring above

\xi\cdot(y-\sum a_{j}x_{j})=0.

Thus by the
\xi
-torsion freeness of the target ring, the map above is surjective.
Moreover, we can define a section of the above map by mapping
y
onto
\sum a_{j}x_{j}
, which is possible by the
\xi
-torsion freeness of the quotient.
Hence we get the isomorphism.
∎

P[\frac{I}{\xi}]
.
Hence the ring
P[\frac{I}{\xi}]^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{tf}}
together with the
R
-algebra structure of its quotient forms a prism
D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
, and we get a map of prisms
(D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\xi D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\rightarrow(B,\xi B)
.

∎

3.
Prismatic crystal and log connection

There are two natural
structure sheaves
over
X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
, defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}

\displaystyle:(B,\xi B)\longmapsto B;

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}

\displaystyle:(B,\xi B)\longmapsto B/\xi.

This allows us to define the notion of the crystal over the prismatic site, analogous to the infinitesimal story.

Definition 3.0.1
.

A (coherent)
prismatic crystal
over
X
is defined as a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
-modules over
X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
, satisfying

(1)

each
\mathcal{F}(B,\xi B)
is a finite module over
B
, for every prism
(B,\xi B)\in X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
;

(2)

for every map of prisms
(B,\xi B)\rightarrow(B^{\prime},\xi B^{\prime})
, the natural base change map below is an isomorphism of
B^{\prime}
-modules

\mathcal{F}(B,\xi B)\otimes_{B}B^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(B^{\prime},\xi B^{\prime}).

Notice that given any prismatic crystal
\mathcal{F}
, there is a way to associate a
reduction

\overline{\mathcal{F}}
to it, by assigning

(B,\xi B)\longmapsto\mathcal{F}(B,\xi B)\otimes_{B}B/\xi.

This in particular gives a prismatic crystal over the reduced structure sheaf
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}=\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}\otimes_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}K
.

Definition 3.0.2
.

A prismatic crystal
\mathcal{F}
is called
flat
if each
B
-module
\mathcal{F}(B,\xi B)
is locally free over
B
.
It is called
reduced
if each
B
-module
\mathcal{F}(B,\xi B)
is locally free over
B/\xi
.

Analogous to the infinitesimal theory as in
[
Guo21
, Theorem 3.3.1]
, a crystal can be described as an
integrable log connection
over the envelope.

To start, let
B=\varprojlim_{m}B_{m}
be an inverse limit of flat noetherian
{\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,m}}}
-algebras
B_{m}
, such that the transition maps induce isomorphisms
B_{m}\otimes_{{\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,m}}}}\mathrm{B_{dR,m-1}^{+}}\rightarrow B_{m-1}
, and
B_{1}
is isomorphic to a formal completion of some Tate algebra
K\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{l}\rangle
.
Let
I
be an ideal of
B
.
Denote
D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
to be
\xi
-torsion free quotient of the classical
\xi
-completion of the noetherian ring
B[\frac{I}{\xi}]
.

Fix a map
P:={\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T_{i}\rangle\rightarrow B
lifting
K\langle T_{i}\rangle\rightarrow B_{1}
as above.
We first notice that there is a natural diagram of
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-linear continuous differentials

\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d~~~~~~~~~}
\textstyle{\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~}
\textstyle{D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}\otimes_{P}\frac{\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{1}}{\xi}.}

Here
\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{1}
is the inverse limit of the
p
-adic continuous Kähler differential of
P_{m}/{\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,m}}}
, and horizontal maps are continuous with respect to
\xi
-adic topology, such that their mod
\xi
-reduction are continuous differentials with respect to
K\langle T_{i}\rangle
and
B/\xi
.
On the other hand, the vertical maps are natural maps induced from the inclusions

\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{1}\subset\frac{\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{1}}{\xi}\subset\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{1}\otimes_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\mathrm{B_{dR}}.

In particular, for an element
\frac{f_{1}\cdots f_{i}}{\xi^{i}}
in
D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}=P[\frac{I}{\xi}]^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{tf}}
with
f_{1},\ldots,f_{i}\in I
, we have

d(\frac{f_{1}\cdots f_{i}}{\xi^{i}})=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{i}f_{1}\cdots\hat{f_{j}}\cdots f_{i}\cdot df_{j}}{\xi^{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\frac{f_{1}\cdots\hat{f_{j}}\cdots f_{i}}{\xi^{i-1}}\cdot\frac{df_{j}}{\xi}\in D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}\otimes_{P}\frac{\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{1}}{\xi}.

Now we can consider a generalization to the coefficient theory.

Definition 3.0.3
.

For a finite generated module
M
over
D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
, a
log connection
of
M
is defined as an
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-linear morphism

\nabla:M\longrightarrow M\otimes_{D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\frac{\Omega^{1}_{D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}{\xi}:=M\otimes_{P}\frac{\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{1}}{\xi},

such that for
x\in M
and
f\in D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
we have

\nabla(fx)=f\nabla(x)+x\otimes df,

where
df
is the continuous relative differential of
f\in D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
constructed above.

The connection is called
integrable
if the induced composition below is zero

\nabla^{1}\circ\nabla:M\longrightarrow M\otimes_{D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\frac{\Omega^{2}_{D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}{\xi^{2}}:=M\otimes_{P}\frac{\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{2}}{\xi^{2}}.

Here the map
\nabla^{1}:M\otimes_{D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\frac{\Omega^{1}_{D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}{\xi}\longrightarrow M\otimes_{D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\frac{\Omega^{2}_{D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}{\xi^{2}}
is given by the formula

x\otimes\frac{\omega}{\xi}\longmapsto\nabla(x)\wedge\frac{\omega}{\xi}+x\otimes\frac{d\omega}{\xi}.

Remark 3.0.4
.

When
B
is the
J
-adic completion of
P={\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T_{i}\rangle
for some ideal
J
in
P
, and
I
is the zero ideal, the above definition extends the notion of connections in the infinitesimal theory defined in
[
Guo21
, Definition 3.1.8]
, allowing a log pole at
\{\xi=0\}
.

Remark 3.0.5
.

In the special case when
\nabla:M\rightarrow M\otimes_{D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\frac{\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{1}}{\xi}
factors through
M\otimes_{D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{1}
, we call the connection
integral
.
We will see soon that log connections coming from the infinitesimal site are all integral.

Different from the infinitesimal theory, not all of the integrable log connections come from crystal.
This is more similar to the schematic theory in mixed characteristic, where
quasi-nilpotence
condition is needed in order to make the transition power series associated to a connection to be convergent (c.f.
[
BO78
]
).
We now define the
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-analogue of the quasi-nilpotence as below.

Definition 3.0.6
.

Let
B,~I,~P={\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{l}\rangle
be as above, and
(M,\nabla)
be a log connection over
D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}=B[\frac{I}{\xi}]^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{tf}}
.
We say
(M,\nabla)
is
quasi-nilpotent
if for any element
x\in M
, the following power series are convergent in the module
M\otimes_{D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}B[\frac{I}{\xi},\widetilde{\delta}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\delta}_{l}]^{\wedge}

\sum_{E=(e_{i})}\prod_{i=1}^{l}\widetilde{\nabla}_{i}^{e_{i}}(x)\otimes\frac{\widetilde{\delta}_{i}^{e_{i}}}{e_{i}!}.

Here
\widetilde{\nabla}_{i}:M\rightarrow M
is defined as the composition

\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nabla~~~~~~~~}
\textstyle{M\otimes_{P}\frac{\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{1}}{\xi}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{~~~~~~~x\otimes\frac{dT_{i}}{\xi}\mapsto x}
\scriptstyle{~~~~~~~~x\otimes\frac{dT_{j}}{\xi}\mapsto 0}
\textstyle{M,}

where
j
above is not equals to
i
.

Remark 3.0.7
.

As an exercise of Taylor expansion in calculus, it can be shown that in order to show the quasi-nilpotence, it is enough to check the convergence for a set of generators in
Definition

3.0.6
.

Remark 3.0.8
.

The map
\widetilde{\nabla}_{i}
should be thought as the multiplication of
\xi
with the classically defined map
\nabla_{i}
, where the latter is the composition of
\nabla
and the paring with
\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{i}}
.
More explicitly the pairing map sends
\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{1}
to
P
by the formula

dT_{i}\longmapsto 1;~dT_{j}\longmapsto 0,~\forall j\neq i.

This could be made precise either by inverting
\xi
or when
M
has no
\xi
-torsion.

Example 3.0.9
.

As a simple example of a quasi-nilpotent log connection, let
R=K\langle T\rangle,

P={\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T\rangle
and
I=(0)
, so that
D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}=P
.
Let
(M,\nabla)=({\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T\rangle,d)
be the natural continuous differential operator on the structure sheaf with
df(T)=f^{\prime}(T)dT
.
Then for an element
f(T)\in{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T\rangle
, the power series in Definition
3.0.6
is

\sum_{e}f^{(e)}(T)\cdot\xi^{e}\otimes\frac{\widetilde{\delta}^{e}}{e!}=f(T+\xi\widetilde{\delta}),

which is convergent in the ring
P[{\widetilde{\delta}}]^{\wedge}={\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T\rangle[\widetilde{\delta}]^{\wedge}
, by checking mod
\xi^{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus the canonical continuous differential on
P
is quasi-nilpotent.

In fact, as we will see, this is the (log) connection corresponds to the prismatic structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
over
R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
.

Example 3.0.10
.

If a log connection is integral, then it is quasi-nilpotent.
To show this, it suffices to notice that by assumption in
Remark

3.0.5
when
(M,\nabla)
is integral, each
\widetilde{\nabla}_{i}
(x) is divisible by
\xi
.

Example 3.0.11
.

To give an example that is not quasi-nilpotent, still consider the special setting as above when
R=K\langle T\rangle
,
P={\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T\rangle
and
I
is the zero ideal, in which case the envelope
D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
is equal to the ring
P
.
Let
M
be
D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
itself, and let
\nabla
be the twisted differential map

\nabla=\frac{d}{\xi}:{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T\rangle\longrightarrow{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T\rangle\otimes\frac{dT}{\xi};~f(T)\longmapsto f^{\prime}(T)\otimes\frac{dT}{\xi}.

Then this is an integrable log connection of
M
over
D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
but is not nilpotent.
To see the latter, it suffices to notice that given
f(T)\in{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T\rangle
, the formal power series in Definition
3.0.6
is equal to

\sum_{e}f^{(e)}(T)\otimes\frac{\widetilde{\delta}^{e}}{e!}=f(T+\widetilde{\delta}),

which after mod
\xi
is not convergent in
K\langle T\rangle[\widetilde{\delta}]
whenever
f
mod
\xi
is an infinite series.

Proposition 3.0.12
.

Let
P={\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T_{i}\rangle
, let
I
be an ideal of
P
, let
R
be the topologically finite type algebra
P/(I,\xi)
and let
D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}=P[\frac{I}{\xi}]^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{tf}}
be the associated envelope.
Then the following two categories are equivalent:

\displaystyle\{coherent~prismatic~crystals~over~R\}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\{(M,\nabla)~|~M\in{\mathrm{Coh}}(D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}),~\nabla~integrable~quasi\text{-}nilpotent~log~connection\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\mathcal{F}(D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}),\nabla_{D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}).

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[
Guo21
, Theorem 3.3.1]
.
We first notice that by
Proposition

2.2.11
the prism
D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
associated to the prismatic envelope for the surjection
P\rightarrow P/I
covers the final object of the topos.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent prismatic crystal over
R
.
By Taylor expansion, the formula in
Definition

3.0.6
is exactly the transition isomorphism of the pullbacks along the two projections

\phi:\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}\mathcal{F}(D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\rightarrow\mathrm{pr}_{0}^{*}\mathcal{F}(D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}),

where
\mathrm{pr}_{i}
are two canonical maps of rings
D(1)_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}=P[\frac{I,\Delta(1)}{\xi}]^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{tf}}\leftleftarrows P[\frac{I}{\xi}]
induced by
P\langle\Delta(1)\rangle\leftleftarrows P
.
Here
\Delta(1)
is the kernel ideal for the diagonal surjection
P^{\widehat{\otimes}_{{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}2}\rightarrow P
.
To define the log connection on
D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
, we note that the following map has the image in
\mathcal{F}(D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\otimes_{P}\frac{\Delta(1)/\Delta(1)^{2}}{\xi}
(which is isomorphic to
\cong\mathcal{F}(D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\otimes_{P}\frac{\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{1}}{\xi}
)

\id\otimes 1-\phi\circ(1\otimes\id):\mathcal{F}(D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{pr}_{0}^{*}\mathcal{F}(D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})/(\frac{\Delta(1)^{2}}{\xi^{2}}).

This allows us to define the log connection as

\nabla:\mathcal{F}(D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\otimes_{P}\frac{\Omega_{P/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}^{1}}{\xi}.

Then as a consequence of the crystal condition, both
\mathrm{pr}_{i}^{*}\mathcal{F}(D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})
are isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}(D(1)_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})
, and the existence of the isomorphism implies the convergence of the power series.
Moreover, integrability for the log connection coming from the crystal follows from the crystal conditions again, and can be proved as in
[
Sta
]
[07J6].

Conversely, given an integrable, quasi-nilpotent log connection
(M,\nabla)
over an envelope, Lemma
2.2.11
allows us to define a finite module over any prism
(B,\xi B)
via the pullback along a map
D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}\rightarrow B
.
Then the rest is to show the independence of the choice of maps, which follows from the quasi-nilpotence and the integrability, and can be checked as in the proof of
[
Guo21
, Theorem 3.3.1]
(especially
[
Guo21
, Claim 3.3.4]
).
Here we note that the quasi-nilpotence assumption is used to show the formal power series associated to the transition morphism converges, thus exists.
∎

We also compute the Čech-Alexander complex under the prismatic setting.
As a preparation, we introduce the following notations.
As before, let
P={\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}\rangle\rightarrow P/I\rightarrow R=P/(I,\xi)
be surjections, and let
D_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
be the associated envelope.
Denote
P(l)

P(l):=\varprojlim_{n}\left({\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,n}}}\langle T_{i}\rangle^{\widehat{\otimes}_{{\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,n}}}}(l+1)}\right)\cong\varprojlim_{n}{\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,n}}}\langle T_{i},\delta_{j,i}\rangle,

where

for the diagonal surjection

,
and the complete tensor product
\widehat{\otimes}_{{\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,n}}}}

Proposition 3.0.13
.

Let
P,R
and

The

Let

\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

This implies that a map from

We then consider the integral level.

.

Let

This follows from the general category theory and Proposition

As an application, we obtain a tensor product formula relating the cohomology of a crystal and its reduction.

Corollary 3.0.15
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a flat prismatic crystal over
X
, and let
\overline{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}K
be its reduction.
Then the natural map below is an isomorphism

R\Gamma(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\mathcal{F})\otimes^{L}_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}K\longrightarrow R\Gamma(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\overline{\mathcal{F}}).

Proof.

First notice the map of cohomology above is induced by the reduction map of crystals
\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{F}}
.
So it suffices to check that the map of cohomology is an isomorphism, assuming
R
admits a surjection as in
Corollary

3.0.14
.
Then we notice that by the local freeness of each
\mathcal{F}(D(l)_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\xi D(l)_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})
over

and the

-torsionfreeness of
D(l)
over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
, the derived tensor product of the cosimplicial diagram

with

is equal to

[l]\longmapsto\overline{\mathcal{F}}(D(l)_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\xi D(l)_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})=\mathcal{F}(D(l)_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\xi D(l)_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})/\xi.

Thus the rest follows from
Corollary

3.0.14
.
∎

4.
From infinitesimal to prismatic

In this section, we show how to compute cohomology of a prismatic crystal locally by using infinitesimal cohomology, for rigid spaces that has l.c.i. singularity.

4.1.
A variant of Simpson’s functor

We start this subsection with introducing a variant of Simpson’s functor in
[
Simp
, Lemma 19]
, over the (
\infty
) category of filtered objects (rings, modules, or complexes) over a
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-algebra.
This will be used to connect the infinitesimal theory to the prismatic theory.

Construction 4.1.1
.

Let

\textstyle{{\mathrm{Fil}}^{0}M}
\textstyle{{\mathrm{Fil}}^{1}M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cdot\xi}
\textstyle{{\mathrm{Fil}}^{1}M}
\textstyle{{\mathrm{Fil}}^{2}M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{\cdot\xi}

The idea of the diagram in Construction

Remark 4.1.3

When

Intuitively, using the notation in the above remark, this is to say the following multiplicative structure in

\frac{{\mathrm{Fil}}^{i}M}{\xi^{i}}\otimes\frac{{\mathrm{Fil}}^{j}M}{\xi^{j}}\longrightarrow\frac{{\mathrm{Fil}}^{i+j}M}{\xi^{i+j}}.

Remark 4.1.4
.

Moreover, when

In the following, for a

This would not change the underlying

Remark 4.1.5
.

Given a filtered

4.2.
Prisms associated to infinitesimal thickenings

We now construct a functor from the infinitesimal site to the prismatic site, over the de Rham period ring
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
.

To start, we consider the following pro-version analogue of the infinitesimal site considered in
[
Guo21
]
.

Definition 4.2.1
.

Let
X
be a rigid space over
K
.
The
pro-infinitesimal site
of
X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
, denoted as
X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{\mathrm{pinf}}
, is defined as the category of
(U,T_{\ast}):=(U,T_{e})
for
e\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

•

each
(U,T_{e})
is an infinitesimal thickening of affinoid rigid spaces over
{\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,e}}}
(in the sense of
[
Guo21
, Section 2.1]
, with
T_{e}
flat over
{\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,e}}}
and
U
an affinoid open subspace of
X
;

•

transition maps
T_{e}\rightarrow T_{e+1}
are also infiniteisimal thickenings that are compatible with maps from
U
, and there is a compatible system of closed immersions
T_{e}\rightarrow\mathrm{Spa}({\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,e}}}\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{l}\rangle)
for some fixed
l\in\mathbb{N}
.

The category is equipped with indiscrete topology.

Remark 4.2.2
.

Let
R
be a topologically of finite type
K
-algebra, and let
P\rightarrow R
be a surjection for a smooth
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-algebra
P
(in the sense that each
P/\xi^{e}
is smooth over
{\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,e}}}
as an adic space).
Then the induced pro-infinitesimal thickening
(U=\mathrm{Spa}(R),T_{e}=\mathrm{Spa}(P/I^{e}))
is weakly final over
\mathrm{Spa}(R)/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{\mathrm{inf}}
, where
I=\ker(P\rightarrow R)
.

Remark 4.2.3
.

Analogous to the classical infinitesimal theory, we can define the infinitesimal structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}
and the notion of crystals over
X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{\mathrm{pinf}}
, as in
[
Guo21
]
.
Here for a pro-infinitesimal thickening
(U,T_{e}=\mathrm{Spa}(B_{e}))
we have

\mathcal{O}_{X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}(U,T_{e})=\varprojlim_{e}B_{e}.

Moreover, by
[
Guo21
, Theorem 7.2.3, Theorem 3.3.1]
, the category of crystals over
X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{\mathrm{inf}}
in the sense of
[
Guo21
]
is naturally equivalent to that over
X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{\mathrm{pinf}}
, inducing natural isomorphisms between their cohomology.

Next we define a natural functor from the infinitesimal site to the prismatic site.
Let
R
be a fixed topologically finite type algebra over
K
, and let
X=\mathrm{Spa}(R)
.

Definition 4.2.4
.

Let
(U=\mathrm{Spa}(A),T_{e}=\mathrm{Spa}(B_{e}))\in X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{\mathrm{pinf}}
be a pro-infinitesimal thickening over the rigid space
X=\mathrm{Spa}(R)
over
K
, and let
B=\varprojlim_{e}B_{e}
, with the ideal
I=\ker(B\rightarrow A)
.
The
associated prism
of
(U,T_{e})
is defined as

\left(B^{\prime}\rightarrow B^{\prime}/\xi\leftarrow R\right),

where
B^{\prime}=B[\frac{I}{\xi}]^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{tf}}
, the symbol
(-)^{\wedge}
is the classical
\xi
-adic completion, and the arrow on the right is the natural composition
R\rightarrow A\rightarrow B[\frac{I}{\xi}]^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{tf}}/\xi
.

Remark 4.2.5
.

When
(U,T_{e})
is a pro-infinitesimal thickening as in
Remark

4.2.2
, by the construction above together with
Proposition

2.2.11
, the associated prism covers the final object of the prismatic site.

By construction, this defines a map of ringed sites

\pi:(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\longrightarrow(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{\mathrm{pinf}},\mathcal{O}_{X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}}).

Moreover, by taking the coherent pullback, we can produce a prismatic crystal from an infinitesimal crystal.

Definition/Lemma 4.2.6
.

There is a natural functor between categories of crystals

\pi^{*}:\mathrm{Cry}_{X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{\mathrm{pinf}}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Cry}_{X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}},

such that for an infinitesimal thickening
(U=\mathrm{Spa}(A),T_{e}=\mathrm{Spa}(B_{e}))
with
(B^{\prime}\rightarrow B^{\prime}/\xi\leftarrow R)
being the associated prism as in
Definition

4.2.4
, we have

\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}(B^{\prime})=\mathcal{F}(U,T_{e})\otimes_{B}B^{\prime}.

Proof.

For each
(U=\mathrm{Spa}(A),T_{e}=\mathrm{Spa}(B_{e}))
, we construct a module over
B^{\prime}=B[\frac{I}{\xi}]^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{tf}}
by taking the tensor product as in the formula above.
Then by the crystal condition of
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{Cry}_{X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{\mathrm{inf}}}
, this satisfies the crystal condition with respect to prisms coming from infinitesimal thickenings.
Then it suffices to notice that by
Remark

4.2.5
the category of associated prisms covers the final objects of the prismatic topos.
∎

4.3.
Local calculation for l.c.i singularities

In this subsection, we give some concrete local formulae computing prismatic cohomology of crystals using Simpson’s functor, for affinoid rigid space that has l.c.i singularities (in the sense of
[
GL20
, Appendix]
).
Precisely, we assume the following throughout the subsection.

Assumption 4.3.1
.

We let
R
be a fixed topologically finite type
K
-algebra, and let
P
be the ring
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T_{i}\rangle
.
Assume
I
is a Koszul-regular ideal of
P
(in the sense of
[
Sta
, Tag 07CU]
), such that
P/I
is flat over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
, and
P/(I,\xi)
is isomorphic to
R
.

Slightly abuse the language, we call any such surjection
P\rightarrow P/I
as above a
regular closed immersion
.

Example 4.3.2
.

Assume
R
is a topologically finite type algebra over
K
that has l.c.i singularities.
By definition in
[
GL20
, Proposition 5.3]
, the kernel ideal
\overline{I}
of any surjection from
K\langle T_{i}\rangle
onto
R
is a Koszul-regular ideal in
K\langle T_{i}\rangle
.
In particular, by
[
Sta
, Tag 0669]
, as the union of
\xi
with a lift of any (Zariski) local Koszul-regular sequence of
\overline{I}
is a Koszul-regular sequence, the ideal
\ker(P\rightarrow R)
is also a Koszul-regular ideal of
P
.
So by taking any lift of Koszul-regular sequence of
\overline{I}
, we see Zariski (thus analytic) locally the affinoid algebra
R
always admits a setup as in
4.3.1
.

Remark 4.3.3
.

It is worth mentioning the category of (regular) closed immersions are sifted.
This is because given any two surjections
P_{i}\rightarrow P_{i}/I_{i}\rightarrow R
for
i=1,2
and
P_{i}
being of the form
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle T_{j}\rangle
, we can form the product

P:=\varprojlim_{e}\left((P_{1}\otimes_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}{\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,e}}})\otimes_{\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,e}}}(P_{2}\otimes_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}{\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,e}}})\right),

which naturally admits a surjection to
R
together with maps from
P_{i}
.
Moreover, by taking the ideal
I\subset P
generated by the image of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, the quotient ring
P/I
is a flat
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-lift of
R
along
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\rightarrow K
.
At last, when both surjections
P_{i}\rightarrow R
are locally complete intersection, we can use the analytic cotangent complex to check that
P\rightarrow R
is also regular (
[
GL20
, Appendix]
).

We start by computing the prismatic envelope using Simpson’s functor.

Lemma 4.3.4
.

Let
P
,
I
, and
R
be as in
4.3.1
.
Then the prismatic envelope
P[\frac{I}{\xi}]^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{tf}}
for
P\rightarrow P/I
is naturally isomorphic to
\Psi(P,I^{\bullet})
.
In particular,
\Psi(P,I^{\bullet})
lives in cohomological degree zero and is a
\xi
-adically complete,
\xi
-torsionfree noetherian algebra over
P
.

Proof.

We first assume that
I
admits a finite set of generators
(f_{i})
such that
(\xi,f_{i})
is a Koszul-regular sequence, which is always true Zariski locally around
\Spec(R)
in
\Spec(P)
(c.f.
Example

4.3.2
).
Then there exists a natural map from the polynomial algebra
P[x_{i}]
to
\Psi(P,I^{\bullet})
, sending
x_{i}
onto
\frac{f_{i}}{\xi}\in\frac{I}{\xi}
.
This induces a natural homomorphism from the noetherian ring
P[\frac{f_{i}}{\xi}]=P[x_{i}]/(\xi\cdot x_{i}-f_{i})
to
\Psi(P,I^{\bullet})
, and factors through the derived
\xi
-completion of
P[\frac{f_{i}}{\xi}]
.
Note by
[
Sta
, Tag 0A06]
the classical
\xi
-completion and the derived
\xi
-completion of a noetherian
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-algebra are the same.
Thus the above leads to a natural map from the actual noetherian algebra
P[\frac{f_{i}}{\xi}]^{\wedge}
to
\Psi(P,I^{\bullet})
, where the former is classically
\xi
-completed.
Moreover, as
(\xi,f_{i})
forms a regular sequence of the ring
P
, the map of rings
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}[y_{i}]\rightarrow P
,
y_{i}\mapsto f_{i}
is flat after localizing at the ideal
(\xi)
(c.f.
[
Har66
, Proposition 1]
).
As a consequence, the ring
P[\frac{f_{i}}{\xi}]
has no
\xi
-torsion, and we get a map from
P[\frac{I}{\xi}]^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{tf}}
to
\Psi(P,I^{\bullet})
.

To show the isomorphism, by the derived Nakayama lemma (
[
Sta
, Tag 0G1U]
) for
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
, it suffices to check the above is an isomorphism after a derived tensor product with
K
.
On the one hand, by
Remark

4.1.5
we have

\Psi(P,I^{\bullet})\otimes^{L}_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}K\cong\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathrm{gr}}^{n}(P\otimes^{L}_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}K).

By the assumption of
P
, each
I^{n}/I^{n+1}
is Tor-independent of
K
over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
.
So the above tensor product is equal to the discrete module as below

\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\overline{I})^{n}/(\overline{I})^{n+1},

where
\overline{I}
is the tensor product
I\otimes_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}K
.
On the other hand, the assumption of
P
and
I=(f_{i})
implies the ring
P[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]/(\xi x_{i}-f_{i},1\leq i\leq m)
is flat over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
.
In particular, we have

\displaystyle P[\frac{f_{i}}{\xi}]\otimes^{L}_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}K

\displaystyle\cong P[\frac{f_{i}}{\xi}]\otimes_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}K

\displaystyle\cong P/(\xi,f_{i})[x_{i}],

which by assumption is exactly the direct sum
\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\overline{I})^{n}/(\overline{I})^{n+1}
.
In this way, as the above two reductions are abstractly isomorphic to each other, to finish the proof, it suffices to notice that the map we constructed identifies the generators of each direct summand.

In general, one can still form the map from

to
\Psi(P,I^{\bullet})
, replacing
(f_{i})
above by any finite set of generators of

As the ring

So to show the isomorphism, it suffices to take the derived reduction mod

But notice that the map commutes with any Zariski localization of

, and for each

in

such that

admits a set of Koszul-regular generators and the above is an isomorphism.
Thus a Čech complex argument for an open covering finishes the proof.

∎

5.
Hodge-Tate filtration

Using the local computation from the last section and the simplicial resolutions, we are able to prove the Hodge-Tate filtration theorem.

5.1.
Smooth case

In the smooth case, we first construct a natural map from Hodge-Tate cohomology to prismatic cohomology, using the Bockstein operator as in
[
BS19
]
.
Let
R
be a topological finite type algebra over
K
.
Consider the short exact sequence of
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
-modules

\xi^{i+1}/\xi^{i+2}\longrightarrow\xi^{i}/\xi^{i+2}\longrightarrow\xi^{i}/\xi^{i+1}.

We take the derived tensor product of the sequence with

over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}

R\Gamma(R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\otimes^{L}_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}K\cdot\xi^{i}\longrightarrow R\Gamma(R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\otimes^{L}_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}K\cdot\xi^{i+1}[1].

By the reduction isomorphism in
Corollary

3.0.15
we have

R\Gamma(R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\otimes^{L}_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}K\cong R\Gamma(R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\overline{\mathcal{O}}).

Thus we can rewrite the connecting morphism as below

\beta_{\xi}:R\Gamma(R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})(i)\longrightarrow R\Gamma(R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})(i+1)[1].

As in
[
BS19
, Section 4]

-th cohomology group, the above induces a

-linear cdga over

(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})(\bullet),\beta_{\xi}).

So by the universal property of the algebraic de Rham complex
\Omega_{R/K}^{\bullet,alg}
of

, we get a natural map of cdga

(\Omega_{R/K}^{\bullet,alg},d)\longrightarrow(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})(\bullet),\beta_{\xi}).

Theorem 5.1.1
.

Assume

is a smooth affinoid algebra over

.
Then there is a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{i}(R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\cong\Omega_{R/K}^{i}(-i),

where

is the sheaf of
i
-th analytic differentials.

Assume the above case when

As we have constructed a natural map from algebraic de Rham complex of

(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})(\bullet),\beta_{\xi})

, and induces a termwise isomorphism with the target.

Let us assume

admits a surjection from

, together with an ideal

satisfying
4.3.1
.
By

, prismatic cohomology is isomorphic to

5.2.
Simplicial resolution for affinoid algebras

To extend the Hodge-Tate filtration to non-smooth rigid spaces, we will need the simplicial resolution of affinoid algebras by smooth ones.
Here we mention that different from the algebraic setting, we cannot use the standard polynomial resolution where variables are infinite.
To resolve topologically finite type algebras over a
p
-adic field, we will need a version of simplicial resolution such that each term satisfies the finite generatedness condition, thus within the category of topologically finite type affinoid algebras.

We start by recalling the following general construction resolving a given class of a given simplicial algebra using finite type polynomials.
As a convention, for a simplicial abelian group

and a co-degeneracy map

to denote the corresponding map

for

.
Similarly for (co-) face maps.

Construction 5.2.1

Let

be a commutative ring, and
A
be a simplicial

-algebra.
Let

be an integer, and
\omega\in A_{d-1}
be a cycle of degree
d-1
.

For each

, let

be the finite set

X_{n}:=\{x_{t}|t:[n]\rightarrow[d]\text{ is surjective and monotone}\}.

Then by [
[
Iy07
]
, 4.10], there is a simplicial

Now we are able to extend the Hodge-Tate filtration to general l.c.i rigid spaces, using simplicial resolution developed last subsection.

5.3.2

K
-resolution
K\langle X_{\bullet}\rangle\rightarrow R
to represent derived prismatic cohomology.
It then suffices to show the following isomorphism

\underset{\Delta}{{\mathrm{colim}}}~R\Gamma(K\langle X_{\bullet}\rangle/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\longrightarrow R\Gamma(R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}).

Notice that the pairs
({\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle X_{n},Y_{n}\rangle,(Y_{n}))
and
(P,I)
all satisfy
4.3.1
.
In particular, we can apply Simpson’s functor as in
Theorem

4.3.12
(and in particular
Corollary

4.3.16
) at the pair
({\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}\langle X_{n},Y_{n}\rangle,(Y_{n}))
to compute

, which is functorial with respect to
[n]\in\Delta
.
In particular, by taking the colimit at their reduced prismatic cohomology, we get the following graded morphism

\displaystyle\underset{\Delta}{{\mathrm{colim}}}\moplus_{i}\Omega_{K\langle X_{\bullet}\rangle/K}^{i}(-i)[-i]\longrightarrow\moplus_{i}L\wedge^{i}\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R/K}(-i)[-i].

In this way, thanks to the simplicial descent for the analytic cotangent complex in

5.3.1

∎

As a consequence, we obtain a naturally defined Hodge-Tate filtration on reduced prismatic cohomology.

.

Let

be a rigid space over

There exists an ascending exhaustive
\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{op}}
-filtration

on

6.

In this section, we prove the infinitesimal comparison theorem, using the decalage functor

Theorem 6.0.1
.

Let
X

K

\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{L\eta_{\xi}R\Gamma(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{R\Gamma(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}).}

To simplify the notation, we use

Proof.

To start, we first note that map from infinitesimal cohomology to prismatic cohomology can be constructed without the l.c.i assumption on

Recall there is a natural morphism of ringed sites in

On the other hand, the decalage functor naturally induces a map

In this case, we consider the natural triangle induced by multiplication by

Notice that everything above has no negative cohomology.

\phi

At last, let us further assume

By the derived

Finally, to see the map

is indeed an isomorphism, it suffices to do this locally, assuming

admits a smooth lift
\widetilde{R}
over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
, where we use the

∎

It is well-known to experts that the decalage functor does not commute with taking derived global sections (c.f.
[

).
We give a simple example illustrating the discrepancy.

Example 6.0.2
.

Let
X
be a projective curve over
K
, defined over a discretely valued subfield
k
that has a perfect residue field.
By
[
BMS
, Proposition 6.12]
and the Hodge-Tate comparison
Theorem

5.1.1
, the mod
\xi
-reduction
(L\eta_{\xi}C)\otimes_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}^{L}K
for

is isomorphic to the following explicit complex

\left(\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\longrightarrow(\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})(1)\oplus\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}))\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})(1)\right).

By the Galois equivariance, the complex is isomorphic to the derived sum of
\left(\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})\right)
and

On the other hand, the reduction

in general.

Remark 6.0.3
.

The above example indicates that certain components of infinitesimal cohomology and decalaged-prismatic cohomology still coincide.
In fact, for smooth rigid spaces in general, one may form a map between them, but in the other direction.
To see this, let
X
be a smooth rigid space and let
\{U_{j}\rightarrow X\}
be an affinoid open covering.
Then we have

\displaystyle R\Gamma_{\mathrm{inf}}(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})\cong R\varprojlim_{j}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{inf}}(U_{j}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})\cong R\varprojlim_{j}L\eta_{\xi}R\Gamma_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(U_{j}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})

\displaystyle\leftarrow L\eta_{\xi}R\varprojlim_{j}R\Gamma_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(U_{j}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})

\displaystyle\cong L\eta_{\xi}R\Gamma(X/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}),

where we use the natural isomorphism in
Theorem

6.0.1
for smooth affinoid

in the second isomorphism above.
When

is smooth and proper, the above identifies the first row of Hodge-Tate spectral sequence with the weight
0
part of the decalaged-prismatic cohomology after mod
\xi
.

We also give an example illustrating the discrepancy for l.c.i rigid spaces that are not smooth.

Example 6.0.4
.

Let

be an affinoid rigid space that has l.c.i singularities and is defined over a discretely valued subfield

7.
Pro-étale comparison

In this section, we prove the comparison theorem between prismatic cohomology over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
and pro-étale cohomology of
{\mathbb{B}^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}}
, studied in
[
Sch13
]
and
[
BMS
, Section 13]
.

7.1.
Künneth formula and étale localization

As a preparation for the pro-étale comparison, we first prove the Künneth formula and the étale localization for reduced prismatic cohomology.

We start by observing that the analytic cotangent complex admits a natural product formula.

Lemma 7.1.1
.

Let
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
be two topologically finite type
K
-algebras, and let
p_{i}
be the two natural maps from
R_{i}
to
R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}R_{2}
.
Then the following natural map is an isomorphism after an integral derived
p
-completion.

Lp_{1}^{*}\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R_{1}/K}\moplus Lp_{2}^{*}\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R_{2}/K}\longrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}R_{2}/K}.

Proof.

Let
R_{1,0}
and
R_{2,0}
be two topologically finite type
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-algebras whose generic fibers are
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
separately.
By
[
GR
, Remark 7.2.45]
, the analytic cotangent complex
\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R/K}
is obtained by inverting
p
at the derived
p
-completion of the algebraic cotangent complex
\mathbb{L}_{R_{0}/\mathcal{O}_{K}}
, where
R
is any of
R_{1},R_{2}
or
R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}R_{2}
(respectively for
R_{0}
).
Denote by
\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R_{0}/\mathcal{O}_{K}}
the derived
p
-completion of the algebraic cotangent complex
\mathbb{L}_{R_{0}/\mathcal{O}_{K}}
.
Then we notice that by
[
GR
, Proposition 7.1.31]
, each of
\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R_{0}/\mathcal{O}_{K}}
for
R_{0}=R_{1,0},R_{2,0}
or
R_{1,0}\widehat{\otimes}R_{2,0}
are pseudo-coherent.
On the other hand, recall the natural isomorphism for the algebraic cotangent complex (
[
Sta
, Tag 09DL]
) as below

Lp_{1}^{*}\mathbb{L}_{R_{1,0}/\mathcal{O}_{K}}\moplus Lp_{2}^{*}\mathbb{L}_{R_{2,0}/\mathcal{O}_{K}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{L}_{R_{1,0}{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}R_{2,0}/\mathcal{O}_{K}}.

In this way, by applying the derived
p
-completion and using
[
GR
, Lemma 7.1.25]
, we get a natural isomorphism

Lp_{1}^{*}\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R_{1,0}/\mathcal{O}_{K}}\moplus Lp_{2}^{*}\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R_{2,0}/\mathcal{O}_{K}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R_{1,0}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}R_{2,0}/\mathcal{O}_{K}}.

Thus the result follows after inverting
p
.
∎

In the following, to simplify the notation we denote
R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R)
to be reduced prismatic cohomology
R\Gamma(R/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})
.

Theorem 7.1.2
.

Let
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
be two topologically finite type algebras over
K
that have l.c.i singularities, and let
p_{i}
be the two natural maps
R_{i}\rightarrow R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}R_{2}
.
There is a natural isomorphism of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings over
R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}R_{2}
:

\left(Lp_{1}^{*}R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R_{1})\right)\otimes^{L}_{R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}R_{2}}\left(Lp_{2}^{*}R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R_{2})\right)\longrightarrow R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}R_{2}).

Proof.

Let
R
be a topologically finite type algebra over
K
that has l.c.i singularities.
Recall the Hodge-Tate filtration from
Corollary

5.3.6
that there is an ascending exhaustive
\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{op}}
-filtration
{\mathrm{Fil}}^{\mathrm{HT}}_{\bullet}
on
R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R)
, such that the
i
-th graded piece is equal to

L\wedge^{i}\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R/K}(-i)[-i].

Moreover, it is compatible with the
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring structure on
R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R)
, and is induced from the infinitesimal filtration on the derived de Rham cohomology
{\widehat{\mathrm{dR}}^{\mathrm{an}}}_{R/K}
.
In particular, this implies that the graded algebra
{\mathrm{gr}}^{\mathrm{HT}}_{\bullet}R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R)
obtained by taking the graded pieces of the Hodge-Tate filtration on
R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R)
is generated by the first graded pieces
\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R/K}(-1)[-1]
.
Namely, we have

{\mathrm{gr}}^{\mathrm{HT}}_{\bullet}R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R)\cong\mathrm{Sym}_{R}(\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R/K}(-1)[-1]).

Back to the Künneth formula.
By the functoriality and lax-monoidal structure, the maps
p_{i}:R_{i}\rightarrow R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}R_{2}
induces a natural map of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebras

R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R_{1})\otimes_{K}R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R_{2})\longrightarrow R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}R_{2}).

By the
R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}R_{2}
-linearity of the target, we can improve the above to an
R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}R_{2}
-linear homomorphism

\left(Lp_{1}^{*}R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R_{1})\right)\otimes^{L}_{R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}R_{2}}\left(Lp_{2}^{*}R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R_{2})\right)\longrightarrow R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}R_{2}).

Notice that the Hodge-Tate filtration on both sides are compatible under the map.
Moreover, by applying the Day convolution (
[
GP18
]
) on the Hodge-Tate filtration of
R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R_{i})
, we can define a product filtration on the left side.
As the colimit commutes with the tensor product, the product filtration is again ascending and exhaustive.
The product filtration is further compatible with the Hodge-Tate filtration on
R\Gamma_{\overline{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}(R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}R_{2})
under the map above.
Thus to show the Künneth formula, it amounts to check the isomorphism after applying the graded pieces.

Now by the observation on the Hodeg-Tate graded algebra, the induced map of graded algebras looks like

Lp_{1}^{*}\mathrm{Sym}_{R_{1}}(\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R_{1}/K}(-1)[-1])\underset{R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}R_{2}}{\motimes}Lp_{2}^{*}\mathrm{Sym}_{R_{2}}(\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R_{2}/K}(-1)[-1])\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}_{R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}R_{2}}(\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}R_{2}/K}(-1)[-1]).

Moreover, taking the pullback functors inside of the symmetric functor, the left hand side above can be rewritten as

\mathrm{Sym}_{R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}R_{2}}\left(Lp_{1}^{*}\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R_{1}/K}(-1)[-1]\moplus Lp_{2}^{*}\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}_{R_{2}/K}(-1)[-1]\right).

Thus by the compatibility of the Hodge-Tate filtration and the infinitesimal filtration, the isomorphism follows from
Lemma

7.1.1
.
So we are done. ∎

Remark 7.1.3
.

The above uses crucially the pseudo-coherence of the analytic cotangent complex to free us from a
p
-adic completion “rational”.
Using the condensed mathematics, one might ask if the above can be improved to a Künneth formula of non-reduced prismatic cohomology, namely an isomorphism of solid
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebras over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}
as below

R\Gamma(R_{1}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\otimes_{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}^{L}R\Gamma(R_{2}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\longrightarrow R\Gamma(R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}R_{2}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}).

Notice that the above is taking the tensor product over
{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}

\mathrm{H}^{0}(\underline{G}_{k},\underline{\mathrm{H}}^{i}_{\inf}(Y_{K}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{i}(Y/k_{\inf},\mathcal{O}_{Y/k}).

Proof.

Thanks to
Lemma

8.2.1
and
Lemma

8.2.1
, we have

\mathrm{H}^{i}(\underline{G}_{k},\underline{D}_{\inf}(n))=D_{0,{\inf}}(n),~i=0,1;

and vanishes for other
i
.
Thus the statement follows by inductively applying the equality at the cosimplicial diagram
\underline{D}_{\inf}(n)
and Mayer-Vietoris sequences for Čech covering by affinoid open subsets.
∎

Remark 8.2.8
.

When
Y
is affinoid, by choosing a closed immersion into a polydisc
k\langle T_{i}\rangle
, one can compute its infinitesimal cohomology using the completed de Rham complex of
k\langle T_{i}\rangle^{\wedge}_{I_{0}}
over
k
, where
I_{0}
is the defining ideal of
Y
.
In the special case when
Y
is smooth, we can even use the continuous de Rham complex of
Y/k
.
Pointed out as in
[
Bos21
, Remark 5.14, 5.22]
, the underlying topological structures of the condensed infinitesimal cohomology and the usual infinitesimal cohomology are not the same.
Thus the underlying topological group of condensed Galois invariant in
Theorem

8.2.3
is different from the usual continuous Galois invariant of infinitesimal cohomology.
However, one may still use criteria in
[
BS15
, Lemma 4.3.9]
to check that the underlying group structures, forgetting their topology, would coincide.

We are now ready to compute Galois invariant of prismatic cohomology.

Theorem 8.2.9

Let
Y

k

(1)

There is a natural complex of condensed groups
\underline{R\Gamma}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(Y_{K}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})
underlying prismatic cohomology
R\Gamma_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(Y_{K}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})
;

(2)

Galois invariant of prismatic cohomology is equal to infiniteismal cohomology of
Y/k_{\inf}

R\Gamma(\underline{G}_{k},{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})\otimes_{k}R\Gamma(Y/k_{\inf},\mathcal{O}_{Y/k})\longrightarrow R\Gamma(\underline{G}_{k},\underline{R\Gamma}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(Y_{K}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})).

Similar to
Theorem

4.3.12
, we apply Simpson’s functor at the condensed complex
\underline{R\Gamma}_{\inf}(Y_{K}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})
and its lifted filtration (associated to the canonical lifts
\{Y_{\mathrm{B^{+}_{dR,e}}}\}
).
Namely, we consider the condensed complex below

\displaystyle\underline{R\Gamma}(Y_{K}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})

\displaystyle=\Psi(\underline{R\Gamma}_{\inf}(Y_{K}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}),\underline{{\mathrm{Fil}}}^{\bullet}_{\inf})

(
\ast

\displaystyle:=\left(\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{\mathrm{colim}}}~\xi^{-n}\cdot\underline{{\mathrm{Fil}}}^{n}_{\inf}(Y_{K}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})\right)^{\wedge},

\underline{R\Gamma}(Y_{K}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})
underlies the usual prismatic cohomology, we apply
R\Gamma(\ast,-)
at
\underline{R\Gamma}(Y_{K}/{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})
, and use a global version of
Theorem

4.3.12
and
Corollary

8.2.5
.
Here we notice that thanks to the compactness of the final object
\ast
, the functor
R\Gamma(\ast,-)
commutes with the colimit in the formula above.

Corollary 8.2.10
.

Let
Y

Then Galois invariant of condensed prismatic cohomology

k

Proof.

This follows from
Corollary

8.2.7
, the finiteness of
R\Gamma(\underline{G}_{k},{\mathrm{B_{dR}^{+}}})

8.2.1

R\Gamma(Y/k_{\inf},\mathcal{O}_{Y/k})

[

∎

Abstract
. We construct p p -adic Asai L L -functions for cuspidal automorphic representations Π \Pi of Res F / ℚ ​ GL 2 {\rm Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}{\rm GL}_{2} , where F F is a real quadratic field in which p p splits. Our method relies on higher Hida theory for Hilbert modular surfaces with Iwahori level at one prime above p p .

1.
Introduction

The study of critical values of
L
-functions is central in many areas of modern number theory. The Bloch-–Kato and Beilinson conjectures relate critical values of complex
L
-functions to certain arithmetic data; and much progress towards such conjectures has been made by
p
-adic methods, in which a fundamental role is played by
p
-adic
L
-functions, interpolating
p
-adically the algebraic parts of critical values of a given complex
L
-function or family of
L
-functions. In this paper, we construct such a
p
-adic counterpart for the
Asai
, or
twisted tensor
,
L
-function attached to a Hilbert modular form over a real quadratic field
F
.

1.1.
Higher Hida theory for
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
-ordinary cohomology

The main tool we shall use is a version of
higher Hida theory
for Hilbert modular forms at primes
p=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\mathfrak{p}_{2}
split in
F
. Such a theory was initially introduced in the first author’s paper
[
Gro21
]
; in this paper we develop a variant assuming only ordinarity at a single prime above
p
, and we study functoriality properties of this theory for the embedding
\GL_{2}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\GL_{2}
, defining pullback and pushforward maps between
p
-adic cohomology spaces for these two groups.

For our first result, let
E
be a (sufficiently large) finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with ring of integers
R
, and write
X_{G,0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})
for the compactified Hilbert modular surface with Iwahori level at
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and spherical level at
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
(and some fixed prime-to-
p
level structure
K_{G}^{(p)}
). We also fix
(k_{1},k_{2},w)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
with
k_{1}=k_{2}=w\bmod 2
.

Theorem A
.

The exists a perfect complex
M_{G}^{\bullet}(k_{1}+2\kappa,k_{2},w)
of modules over
\Lambda=R[[\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}]]
, with an action of prime-to-
p
Hecke operators, such that for all integers
a\in\mathbb{Z}
with
k_{1}+2a\geq 2
, we have a Hecke-equivariant isomorphism

M_{G}^{\bullet}(k_{1}+2\kappa,k_{2},w)\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\Lambda,a}E\simeq e({U}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}})\RG\left(X_{G,0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})_{E},\myom^{(k_{1}+2a,k_{2},w)}\right).

There is also an analogous complex
M_{G}^{\bullet}(k_{1}+2\kappa,k_{2},w;-D)
interpolating the cohomology of the cuspidal sheaves
\myom^{(k_{1}+2a,k_{2},w)}(-D)
.

See
3.2.3
below. Here
e({U}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}})
is the ordinary projector associated to the Hecke operator at
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
; we do not impose any ordinarity conditions at
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
. For our second result, let
M_{H}(\tau)
denote the space of
\Lambda
-adic elliptic modular forms of weight
\tau:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\to\Lambda^{\times}
and tame level
K_{H}^{(p)}=K_{G}^{(p)}\cap H
.

Theorem B
.

There exists a pushforward map of
\Lambda
-modules

\iota_{\star}:M_{H}(k_{1}-k_{2}+2\kappa)\to H^{1}(M_{G}^{\bullet}(k_{1}+2\kappa,2-k_{2},w))

which interpolates, via the isomorphism of
A
, the projection to the
U_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
-ordinary parts of the pushforward maps in coherent cohomology in all weights
(k_{1}+2a,2-k_{2},w)
with
k_{1}+2a\geq 2
.

Note that the integer-weight specialisations of forms in the space
M_{H}(\tau)
can be classical holomorphic modular forms of Iwahori level at
p
, but may also include holomorphic forms of higher
p
-power levels, or nearly-holomorphic modular forms (via the
p
-adic unit root splitting); and we show that the specialisations of our
p
-adic pushforward map are compatible with algebraic pushforward maps on these larger spaces. This is crucial for our applications to
p
-adic
L
-functions, since the
\GL_{2}
Eisenstein series we consider are only nearly-holomorphic in general.

Remark 1
.

In this paper we shall apply Theorem B to pushforwards of Eisenstein families, but the case of pushforwards of cuspidal families is also interesting, since these pushforwards are related to the square roots of twisted triple-product
L
-functions. We hope to investigate this case in future work.

1.2.
One-variable
p
-adic
L
-functions

Our first application of the above theory is to construct a one-variable
p
-adic Asai
L
-function attached to a single cuspidal automorphic representation
\Pi
of
G\coloneqq\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\GL_{2}
, interpolating critical values of the Asai
L
-function of
\Pi
and its twists by
p
-power-conductor Dirichlet characters.

Our conventions are such that
\Pi
is unitary, and its central character
\chi_{\Pi}
is a finite-order Grössencharacter of
F
. The weight of
\Pi
is given by a pair of positive integers
(k_{1},k_{2})
; we shall only consider “paritious” weights (i.e. with
k_{1}=k_{2}\bmod 2
), and in order to have critical values for the Asai
L
-function, we need to assume
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
. Without loss of generality we suppose
k_{1}>k_{2}\geq 1
. We suppose
p
is a prime split in
F
and coprime to the level of
\Pi
; we fix an isomorphism
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\cong\mathbb{C}
, and label the primes above
p
as
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2}
so that the prime
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
corresponds to the infinite place
\sigma_{i}
.

We write
L_{\mathrm{As}}(\Pi,s)
for the Asai
L
-function (normalised in the analytic fashion, so the centre of the functional equation is at
s=\tfrac{1}{2}
), and
L_{\As}(\Pi,\chi,s)
for the
L
-function twisted by a Dirichlet character
\chi
. Here we define the local
L
-factors at bad primes via the local Langlands correspondence, or equivalently as the common denominator of the Asai zeta integral defined in §
8.2.1
(for the equivalence of these definitions see
[
LW20
, Remark 2.14]
). The critical points of
L_{\mathrm{As}}(\Pi,\chi,s)
, for any
\chi
, are given by

(crit)

s\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad\tfrac{2-k_{1}+k_{2}}{2}\leq s\leq\tfrac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{2}.

The general conjectures of Coates–Perrin-Riou
[
CPR89
]
and Panchishkin
[
Pan94
]
on the expected form of
p
-adic
L
-functions associated to motives over
\mathbb{Q}
, in this case, predict the following: if a suitable “ordinarity” condition (the Panchishkin condition) holds, the critical
L
-values of
L_{\As}(\Pi,\chi,s)
, for Dirichlet characters
\chi
of
p
-power conductor, can be interpolated by a
p
-adic measure after multiplying by a certain Euler factor
\Phi_{p}(\As(\Pi),\chi,s)
. See
[
Pan94
, Definition 5.5]
for the definition of Panchishkin’s condition; in our setting (with
k_{1}>k_{2}
) the Panchishkin condition amounts to ordinarity of
\Pi
at
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
, while the stronger ordinarity condition assumed in
[
CPR89
]
amounts to ordinarity at both
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
.

Notation 1.2.1
.

For
i=1,2
, we write
\alpha_{i}^{\circ},\beta_{i}^{\circ}
for the Satake parameters of
\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
(unitarily normalised, so that
|\alpha_{i}^{\circ}|=|\beta_{i}^{\circ}|=1
), and define

\alpha_{i}=p^{(k_{i}-1)/2}\alpha_{i}^{\circ},\quad\beta_{i}=p^{(k_{i}-1)/2}\beta_{i}^{\circ}.

We will see below that
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}
are
p
-adically integral (for our chosen embedding into
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
), and the ordinarity condition at
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
is equivalent to precisely one of
\alpha_{1}
and
\beta_{1}
being a
p
-adic unit, which we may take to be
\alpha_{1}
.

Definition 1.2.2
.

Suppose
\Pi
is ordinary at
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
. For
s\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\nu
a Dirichlet character of
p
-power conductor, define

\mathcal{E}_{p}(\As(\Pi),\nu,s)=\begin{cases}\left(1-\frac{p^{s-1}}{\alpha_{1}^{\circ}\alpha_{2}^{\circ}}\right)\left(1-\frac{p^{s-1}}{\alpha_{1}^{\circ}\beta_{2}^{\circ}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\beta_{1}^{\circ}\alpha_{2}^{\circ}}{p^{s}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\beta_{1}^{\circ}\beta_{2}^{\circ}}{p^{s}}\right)&\text{if $\nu=1$},\\[5.69054pt]
\tfrac{1}{G(\nu)^{2}}\cdot\left(\tfrac{p^{2s}}{(\alpha_{1}^{\circ})^{2}\alpha_{2}^{\circ}\beta_{2}^{\circ}}\right)^{r}&\text{if $\nu$ has conductor $p^{r}$, $r\geq 1$}.\end{cases}

where
G(\nu)=\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r})^{\times}}\nu(a)e^{2\pi ia/p^{r}}
is the Gauss sum. This is exactly the modified Euler factor predicted by the conjectures of
[
CPR89
,
Pan94
]
.

Theorem C
.

Suppose
\Pi
is ordinary at
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and its level is coprime to
p
. Then there exists a
p
-adic measure
\mathcal{L}_{p,\As}(\Pi)\in\Lambda_{E}
, for
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
a sufficiently large finite extension, and a period
\Omega(\Pi)\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, such that for every
s
satisfying (
crit
) and Dirichlet character
\nu
of conductor
p^{r}
, for
r\geq 0
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}\nu^{-1}(x)x^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}_{p,\As}(\Pi)=\mathcal{E}_{p}(\As(\Pi),\nu,s)\cdot\frac{\Gamma(s+\tfrac{k_{1}+k_{2}-2}{2})\Gamma(s+\tfrac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{2})}{2^{(k_{1}-2)}i^{(1-k_{2})}(-2\pi i)^{(2s+k_{1}-1)}}\cdot\frac{L_{\As}\left(\Pi,\nu,s\right)}{\Omega(\Pi)}.

Note that both sides of the equality of
C
are algebraic (and lie in
L(\nu)
for a number field
L
depending only on
\Pi
), which is an extension of existing algebraicity results due to Shimura
[
Shi81
]
.

1.3.
Two-variable
p
-adic
L
-functions

Our second main result involves a slight modification of the Asai
L
-function, denoted
L_{\As}^{\rm imp}(\Pi,\chi,s)
, which we call the
imprimitive
Asai
L
-function. Unlike the primitive Asai
L
-function, it is given by a straightforward formula in terms of Hecke eigenvalues: if
a_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\circ}(\Pi)
is the normalised Hecke eigenvalue of the new vector of
\Pi
at an ideal
\mathfrak{m}\trianglelefteqslant\mathcal{O}_{F}
, then the imprimitive Asai
L
-function is given by
(\star)\cdot\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a^{\circ}_{n\mathcal{O}_{F}}(\Pi)n^{-s}
where
(\star)
is a Dirichlet
L
-function. (See
\lx@sectionsign
8.3
for a more precise, representation-theoretic definition). This differs from
L_{\As}(\Pi,\chi,s)
by a finite product of Euler factors, namely

L_{\As}^{\rm imp}(\Pi,\chi,s)=L_{\As}(\Pi,\chi,s)\cdot\prod_{\ell\mid{\rm Nm}(\mathfrak{N})}C_{\ell}(\ell^{-s}\chi(\ell)),

where
\mathfrak{N}\trianglelefteqslant\mathcal{O}_{F}
is the conductor of
\Pi
, and
C_{\ell}(X)
is a polynomial dividing the Euler factor of
L_{\As}(\Pi,\chi,s)
at
\ell
. Since we can clearly define measures interpolating the factors
C_{\ell}(-)
, it follows
a fortiori
from Theorem B that there exists a measure
\mathcal{L}_{p,\As}^{\mathrm{imp}}(\Pi)
interpolating critical values of
L^{\mathrm{imp}}_{\As}(\Pi,\chi,s)
. We shall show that these “imprimitive
p
-adic
L
-functions” vary analytically when
\Pi
itself is allowed to vary in a family.

Theorem D
.

Let
\Pi
be ordinary at
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
, with level coprime to
p
, and with weight
(k_{1},k_{2})
such that
k_{1}=k_{2}\bmod 2
and
k_{2}\geq 2
. (We no longer assume that
k_{1}>k_{2}
).

(1)

There exists a
p
-adic family
\underline{\Pi}
of automorphic representations parametrised by some affinoid neighbourhood
\mathcal{U}
of 0 in the
p
-adic weight space
\mathcal{W}
, with weight
(k_{1}+2\kappa_{\mathcal{U}},k_{2})
where
\kappa_{U}
is the universal character of
\mathcal{U}
, whose specialisation at
0\in\mathcal{U}
is
\Pi
.

(2)

There exists an element
\mathcal{L}_{p,\As}^{\mathrm{imp}}(\underline{\Pi})\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})\mathop{\hat{\otimes}}\Lambda_{E}
, whose specialisation at any integer
a\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathbb{Z}
with
k_{1}+2a>k_{2}
is a non-zero multiple of
\mathcal{L}_{p,\As}^{\rm imp}(\Pi[a])
, where
\Pi[a]
is the specialisation of
\underline{\Pi}
in weight
(k_{1}+2a,k_{2})
.

In particular, this allows us to define the one-variable
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p,\As}^{\rm imp}(\Pi)
without assuming
k_{1}>k_{2}
, since the interpolating property of the two-variable measure uniquely determines the fibre at
a=0
, even when the Asai
L
-function has no critical values. This includes the important case of base-changes of elliptic modular forms.

Remark 1.3.1
.

It should be possible to interpolate the “primitive”
p
-adic
L
-functions
\mathcal{L}_{p,\As}(\Pi[a])
as
a
varies, not just their imprimitive versions, but this would require a careful study of local test vectors at primes
\ell\neq p
in
p
-adic families (analogous to the analysis carried out in
[
CH20
]
for Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions), and we shall not undertake this here for reasons of space.

1.4.
Relation to other work

The results described above are in many ways parallel to the results of
[
LPSZ21
]
in the case of spin
L
-functions of genus 2 Siegel modular forms. In particular, both here and in
op.cit.
, the underlying
L
-value formula requires us to work with coherent cohomology classes in intermediate degrees (not corresponding to either holomorphic or anti-holomorphic forms) and hence the use of higher Hida theory is essential. This contrasts with earlier works in which the theory of ordinary
p
-adic families of modular forms has been used to construct
p
-adic
L
-functions, such as for
\GL_{2}\times\GL_{2}

[
Hid85
,
Pan82
]
, and the
\GL_{2}
triple product
[
HT01
]
. Classical Hida theory is sufficient for those cases since one works with products of the modular curve and it suffices to vary
p
-adically the degree zero cohomology group. Another case where higher Hida theory plays a role in constructing
p
-adic
L
-functions is
U(2,1)
, which has been treated by Oh
[
Oh23
]
.

In a companion paper
[
GLZ23
]
(in preparation), we shall relate the values of the
p
-adic Asai
L
-functions constructed here, at points
outside
the range of interpolation, to the Euler system classes in Galois cohomology constructed in
[
LLZ18
]
; this will be used in subsequent work to prove an explicit reciprocity law for these Euler system classes, and hence obtain new cases of the Bloch–Kato conjecture and Iwasawa main conjecture for Asai motives.

The main result of
[
LW20
]
is a counterpart of
C
for automorphic representations of
\GL_{2}
over
imaginary
quadratic fields. However, the two constructions use very different techniques; indeed, the construction of the
p
-adic
L
-function in the imaginary case is more strongly related to the construction of the Euler system of
[
LLZ18
]
in the real-quadratic case, rather than that of the
p
-adic
L
-function carried out here.

2.
Higher Hida theory for
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
-ordinary cohomology

We adapt the constructions of
[
Gro21
]
to the case Hilbert modular varieties of Iwahori level at one of the primes above
p
. Assume that the prime
p
splits in
F
. Let us write
p=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cdot\mathfrak{p}_{2}
, and let
\sigma_{i}
be the complex embedding corresponding to
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
for our fixed isomorphism
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\cong\mathbb{C}
.

Remark 2.0.1
.

More generally, we could choose an arbitrary totally real field
F
with
p
completely split, and choose an arbitrary subset of primes above
p
; we stick to the case
[F:\mathbb{Q}]=2
for simplicity, and leave the generalisation to the interested reader.

2.1.
Preliminaries

2.1.1.
Hilbert modular surfaces

For
n\geq 1
, we consider the level group
K_{n}\subset\GL_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F,\mathrm{f}})
defined by

K_{n}=K^{(p)}\cdot K_{\mathfrak{p}_{1},n}\cdot\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{p}_{2}}).

where
K_{\mathfrak{p}_{1},n}=\{g\in\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{p}_{1}}):g\equiv\left(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
0&*\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mod p^{n}\}
and
K^{(p)}
is a sufficiently small open compact subgroup of
G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{(p)})
. We let
X_{\mathbb{Q}}=X_{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{n})_{\mathbb{Q}}
denote a toroidal compactification of the Hilbert modular variety of level
K_{n}
, as a an algebraic surface over
\mathbb{Q}
. The toroidal compactification depends on a choice of polyhedral cone decomposition; we can, and do, choose this so that
X_{\mathbb{Q}}
is smooth and projective.

Its connected components are obtained as quotient by a finite group
\Delta_{K}
of totally positive units of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
of the connected components of the compactified moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Q}}
parametrising tuples
(A,\iota,\lambda,\alpha_{K^{(\mathfrak{p}_{1})}},H)
, where
(A,\iota,\lambda,\alpha_{K^{(\mathfrak{p}_{1})}})
is a (suitably) polarised abelian surface with real multiplication by
F
and
K^{(\mathfrak{p}_{1})}
-level structure
\alpha_{K^{(\mathfrak{p}_{1})}}
and
H
is a cyclic subgroup of
A[\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{n}]
of order
p^{n}
, compatible with the other data (see
[
Gro21
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.1]
for more details).

For
n=1
, which is the main cases of interest for us, there are canonical
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-models
\mathcal{M}
and
X
of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
X_{\mathbb{Q}}
, with
\mathcal{M}
being a compactified moduli space for tuples
(A,\iota,\lambda,\alpha_{K^{(\mathfrak{p}_{1})}},H)
over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-algebras (see
[
Pap95
]
). Moreover,
\mathcal{M}
and
X
are both semistable over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, although not smooth; we shall recall the geometry of their special fibres in more detail below.

2.1.2.
Automorphic sheaves and integral models

Let
L
denote the field
F(\sqrt{\epsilon}:\epsilon\in\mathcal{O}_{F,+}^{\times})
(which is Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
) and
\mathcal{O}_{L}
be its ring of integers. Fix a noetherian
\mathcal{O}_{L,(p)}
-algebra
R
, and suppose that either
n=1
or
p
is invertible in
R
, so we have natural
R
-models of
\mathcal{M}
and
X
. Let
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{M}
be the semi-abelian scheme extending the universal abelian surface with real multiplication by
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Let
e:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{A}
be the unit section and

\myom_{\mathcal{A}}\coloneqq\mathop{e^{\star}}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{M}};

this is a
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{F})
-module locally free of rank 1. We can write

(2.1)

\myom_{\mathcal{A}}=\omega_{\mathcal{A},1}\oplus\omega_{\mathcal{A},2},

where
\omega_{\mathcal{A},i}
is the direct summand on which the
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-action is given by the composition of the embedding
\sigma_{i}
with the structure morphism
\mathcal{O}_{L,(p)}\to R
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}
be the canonical extension of the relative de Rham cohomology
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\dr}(\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{M})
. It is a
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{F})
-module locally free of rank 2 and we can decompose it with respect to the
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-action as above.

For
(\underline{k},w)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}
such that
k_{i}\equiv w\mod 2
. Let

(2.2)

\myom^{(\underline{k},w)}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{1\leq i\leq 2}\left(\left(\wedge^{2}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A},i}^{1}\right)^{\frac{w-k_{i}}{2}}\otimes{\omega}_{\mathcal{A},i}^{k_{i}}\right)

Using the descent datum of
[
Gro21
, Definition 2.3.3]
for the map
\mathcal{M}\to X
, we can descend
\myom^{(\underline{k},w)}
to a sheaf over
X
. The cohomology of this sheaf has an action of the prime-to-
p
Hecke algebra of level
K^{(p)}
, and the double coset of
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&x\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for

acts as multiplication by
x\mapsto\nm_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(x)^{w}
.

We also have the semi-abelian surface
\mathcal{A}/H\to\mathcal{M}
extending the universal abelian surface isogenous to
\mathcal{A}
. They also come with unit sections (which we still denote by

by abuse of notation) and give integral sheaves

(2.3)

\myom_{\mathcal{A}/H}\coloneqq e^{\star}\Omega^{1}_{(\mathcal{A}/H)/\mathcal{M}}=\omega_{\mathcal{A}/H,1}\oplus\omega_{\mathcal{A}/H,2}.

(Note that the natural pullback map
\myom_{\mathcal{A}/H}\to\myom_{\mathcal{A}}
is an isomorphism after inverting

, but is not an isomorphism in characteristic

.)

2.1.3.
Geometry of the special fibre for

From now on, we assume

is a local ring with maximal ideal
\wp\ni p
. Denote by

be the formal completion of

\mathfrak{p}_{1}

of

where

\overline{X_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{1-\text{{\'{e}t}}}}

2.2.
The
U_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
-operator at level
K_{\mathfrak{p}_{1},1}

In this section we work at level
K_{\mathfrak{p}_{1},1}
and we define a cohomological correspondence on
X
.
Let
\mathcal{M}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})
be the (toroidal compactification of the) moduli space parametrising tuples

, where
(A,\iota,\lambda,\alpha_{K^{(\mathfrak{p}_{1})}})

through a given

Remark 3.3.4
.

In more geometric language, for each

-adic families of weight

6.3.

We now recall a very general construction of adelic Eisenstein series (as for example in

We now make a choice of the Schwartz function at

a Whittaker function
W_{\mathrm{f}}^{(p)}\in W(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}^{(p)})
which is defined over

;

•

a Schwartz function
\Phi^{(p)}
defined over
L
;

•

a level group
K_{G}^{(p)}
such that

fixes
W_{\mathrm{f}}^{(p)}
and
K^{(p)}_{H}\coloneqq K_{G}^{(p)}\cap H
fixes
\Phi^{(p)}
.

We let
W_{\mathrm{f},\alpha_{1}}=W_{\mathrm{f}}^{(p)}\cdot W_{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\alpha_{1}}\cdot W_{\mathfrak{p}_{2},\sph}\in\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}})
, and we let
\breve{\nu}_{\Pi,\alpha}
be its image under the map (
6.2
).

Definition 7.3.1
.

We define

\mathcal{Z}_{\Lambda}\left(W_{\mathrm{f}}^{(p)},\Phi^{(p)};\kappa\right)=\operatorname{Vol}(K^{(p)}_{H})\cdot\left\langle\breve{\nu}_{\Pi,\alpha},\iota^{p}_{\star}\left(\mathcal{E}_{k_{1}-k_{2}}^{\Phi^{(p)}}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{X}_{G,0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})^{1-\mathrm{m}}}\in\Lambda_{E}.

Here the cup-product is taken in the cohomology of the Shimura variety of prime-to-
p
level
K_{G}^{(p)}
; the volume factor makes the product independent of the choice of level group. Since the cup-product for coherent cohomology classes is given by integration over the Shimura variety (see e.g.
[
Har90
]
, we see that for any locally-algebraic character
s+\nu
, with

in the critical range, we have

(7.4)

\mathcal{Z}_{\Lambda}\left(W_{\mathrm{f}}^{(p)},\Phi^{(p)};s+\nu\right)=\frac{1}{\Omega_{\infty}(\Pi)}Z\left(W_{\mathrm{f},\alpha_{1}}W_{\infty},\Phi^{(p)}\Phi_{p,\nu^{-1},\nu}\Phi_{\infty}^{(k_{1}-k_{2})};\nu,s\right).

From the results of the previous section, we can write this as

\frac{L_{\mathrm{As}}(\Pi,\nu^{-1},s)}{\Omega_{\infty}(\Pi)}\cdot\frac{Z(W_{p,\alpha_{1}},\Phi_{p,\nu^{-1},\nu};\hat{\nu}_{p},s)}{L_{\As}(\Pi_{p},\nu_{p}^{-1},s)}\cdot Z(W_{\infty};\Phi_{\infty}^{k_{1}-k_{2}},s)\cdot\prod_{\ell\neq p}C_{\ell}(W_{\ell},\Phi_{\ell};\ell^{-s}\nu(\ell)^{-1}).

(Note that the local integral at
\infty
is independent of
\nu
, and all but finitely many of the local terms

are 1.)

We shall consider the following two inputs to
\mathcal{Z}_{\Lambda}
:

7.3.1.
Primitive test data

From part (i) of
6.4.2
we know that for each
\ell
, the ideal of
\mathbb{C}[X,X^{-1}]
generated by the local terms
C_{\ell}(\dots)
is 1. Mapping this into
\Lambda_{E}
via the map

, we obtain a
\Lambda_{E}
-linear combination of values
\mathcal{Z}_{\Lambda}(\dots)
whose value at

and finite-order

\frac{L_{\mathrm{As}}(\Pi\times\nu^{-1},s)}{\Omega_{\infty}(\Pi)}\cdot\frac{Z(W_{p,\alpha_{1}},\Phi_{p,\nu^{-1},\nu};\nu_{p},s)}{L_{\As}(\Pi_{p}\times\nu_{p}^{-1},s)}\cdot Z(W_{\infty};\Phi_{\infty}^{(k_{1}-k_{2})},s).

Definition 7.3.2
.

We define
\mathcal{L}_{p,\mathrm{As}}(\Pi)
to be the above element of
\Lambda_{E}
, multiplied by the factor

(7.5)

\tfrac{p+1}{p}\cdot(\sqrt{D})^{1-(k_{1}+k_{2})/2-\sigma}(-1)^{\sigma}\in\Lambda_{E}^{\times},

where
\sigma:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\to\Lambda_{E}^{\times}
is the universal character of
\Lambda_{E}
.

We shall evaluate the local factors at
p
and
\infty
below, and this will give the interpolation property of

. The factor (

) will serve to cancel out various unwanted terms arising from the local zeta-integrals.

7.3.2.
Imprimitive test data

Since we do not know how the primitive test data behave as we vary
\Pi
over the specialisations of a
p
-adic family, we will also work with a less optimal, but explicit, alternative set of data. These “imprimitive test data” are the inputs
W_{\mathrm{f}}^{(p)}
and
\Phi^{(p)}
given by the following recipe, where
N
is the integer generating
\mathfrak{N}\cap\mathbb{Z}
.

•

W_{\mathrm{f}}^{(p)}
is the normalised new-vector,

•

\Phi^{(p)}=\prod_{\ell\neq p}\Phi_{\ell}
, where
\Phi_{\ell}=\ch(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{2})
if
\ell\nmid N
, and if
v_{\ell}(N)=r>0
, we define

\Phi_{\ell}=(\ell^{2}-1)\ell^{2r-2}\cdot\ch(\ell^{r}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell},1+\ell^{r}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}).

Thus we may take
K^{(p)}_{G}=K_{G,1}(\mathfrak{N})^{(p)}
. The imprimitive (one-variable)

-function
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{imp}}_{p,\As}(\Pi)
is defined as the zeta-integral
\mathcal{Z}_{\Lambda}\left(W_{\mathrm{f}}^{(p)},\Phi^{(p)};\kappa\right)
for this choice of data, multiplied by the correction factor
\tfrac{p+1}{p}\cdot\sigma(\sqrt{D})^{-1}
as before.

7.4.
Two-variable interpolation

We now consider the case of families. Given a
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
-ordinary family
\Pi
of level
\mathfrak{N}
over some affinoid
\mathcal{U}
, as above, we choose an arbitrary isomorphism

\nu_{\underline{\Pi}}:H^{1}_{\mathrm{m},\varnothing}(k_{1}+2\kappa_{\mathcal{U}},2-k_{2},w)[\underline{\Pi}^{\vee}]\cong\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U}).

The specialisation of

at an integer weight

is a basis of the
\Pi[a]
-eigenspace in cohomology over our
p
-adic field

, but it may not necessarily descend to a number field. So we choose an algebraic basis
\nu_{\Pi[a]}
defined over the coefficient field

of
\Pi[a]
(which is a subfield of
E
). This defines a
pair
of periods

\Omega_{p}(\Pi[a])\in E^{\times}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Omega_{\infty}(\Pi[a])\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}

with

(\nu_{\underline{\Pi}})_{|a}=\Omega_{p}(\Pi[a])\cdot\nu_{\Pi[a],\mathfrak{p}_{1}},\qquad\phi^{\mathrm{ah},1}_{\mathrm{new},\Pi[a]}=\Omega_{\infty}(\Pi[a])\cdot\nu_{\Pi[a]}.

Definition 7.4.1
.

We let the two-variable measure
\mathcal{L}_{p,\mathrm{As}}^{\mathrm{imp}}(\underline{\Pi})\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})\mathop{\hat{\otimes}}\Lambda_{E}
be defined by

\mathcal{L}_{p,\mathrm{As}}^{\mathrm{imp}}(\kappa,\sigma)=(\star)\cdot\left\langle\nu_{\underline{\Pi}},\iota_{p,\star}^{k_{1}}\left(\mathcal{E}^{\Phi^{(p)}}(\tfrac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{2}+\kappa_{U}-\sigma,\sigma+\kappa_{U}+\tfrac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{2}-1;\chi^{(p)})\right)\right\rangle,

where

is the factor from (
7.5
) (with

in place of

),
\chi^{(p)}
is the common central character of all the
\Pi[a]
, and
\Phi^{(p)}
is determined by

as above.

By construction we have the specialisation formula

\mathcal{L}_{p,\As}^{\mathrm{imp}}(\underline{\Pi})_{|\kappa_{\mathcal{U}}=a}=\Omega_{p}(\Pi[a])\cdot\mathcal{L}_{p,\As}^{\mathrm{imp}}(\Pi[a]).

Combining this result with
3.3.3
(which gives the existence of a family through any given
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
-ordinary

) gives
D
.

8.
Evaluating local zeta-integrals

We now compute the classical specialisations of the measure defined above and prove the interpolation property of Theorem
C
.

8.1.
Non-archimedean case: setup

Let
K
be a nonarchimedean local field, of reside characteristic
\ell
, and
L
a finite étale
K
-algebra of degree 2. Thus
L
is either
K\times K
, or a quadratic field extension. (In our applications,
K
will be
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, and
L
will be
F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
for a real quadratic field
F
.) We write
q_{K}
, resp.
q_{L}
, for the orders of the residue fields of
K
and
L
(understood as
q_{K}=q_{L}
if
L=K\times K
).

We fix a choice of nontrivial character
\psi:L\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
; we can and do assume that
\psi
is trivial on
K\subset L
, and that
\psi
is unramified (i.e. it is trivial on
\mathcal{O}_{L}
but not on any larger fractional ideal).

We set
H=\GL_{2}(K)
, and
G=\GL_{2}(L)
, so
H\subset G
. Write
B_{H},N_{H},Z_{H}
etc for the usual subgroups of
H
, and similarly for
G
.

8.1.1.
Godement–Siegel sections

For
\chi
a smooth character of
K^{\times}
, and
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(K^{2},\mathbb{C})
and
h\in H
, recall

f^{\Phi}(h;\chi,s)=|\det h|_{K}^{s}\int_{K^{\times}}\Phi((0,a)h)\chi(a)|a|_{K}^{2s}\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}a.

This defines a meromorphic section of the family of principal series representations
I(|\cdot|_{K}^{s-1/2},|\cdot|_{K}^{1/2-s}\chi^{-1})
of
H
.

8.1.2.
Whittaker models, new vector theory

Let
\pi
be a generic irreducible smooth representation of
G
, which we suppose to be unitary and tempered. Then we have a Whittaker model
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
(we shall omit
\psi
henceforth).

Theorem 8.1.1

(Casselman)
.

There exists an ideal
\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{c}(\pi)
of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
with the following property: for
\mathfrak{a}
any ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
, the invariants of
\pi
under the subgroup
K_{1}(\mathfrak{a})\coloneqq\{g\in\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{L}):g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\bmod\mathfrak{a}\}
are nonzero iff
\mathfrak{c}\mid\mathfrak{a}
. The invariants at level
K_{1}(\mathfrak{c})
are one-dimensional, and in the Whittaker model
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
, there is a unique generator
W^{\mathrm{new}}
of this subspace satisfying
W^{\mathrm{new}}(1)=1
.

The values of
W^{\mathrm{new}}
along the maximal torus can be extracted from the
\GL_{2}
standard zeta integral:

Proposition 8.1.2
.

(1)

Suppose
L
is a field. Then we have

\sum_{n\geq 0}W^{\mathrm{new}}\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\varpi_{L}^{n}&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)q_{L}^{(1/2-s)n}=L^{\mathrm{std}}(\pi,s)

where
L^{\mathrm{std}}(-,s)
denotes the standard
L
-factor.

(2)

Suppose
L=K\times K
, and let
\varpi_{1}=(\varpi_{K},1)
and
\varpi_{2}=(1,\varpi_{K})
be uniformizers of the two maximal ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. Then

\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0}W^{\mathrm{new}}\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\varpi_{1}^{n_{1}}\varpi_{2}^{n_{2}}&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)q_{K}^{(1/2-s)(n_{1}+n_{2})}=L^{\mathrm{std}}(\pi_{1},s)L^{\mathrm{std}}(\pi_{2},s).

8.2.
The Asai zeta integral

8.2.1.
Definitions

For
W\in\mathcal{W}(\pi)
and
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(K^{2})
, we define

Z(W,\Phi;s)\coloneqq\int_{Z_{H}N_{H}\backslash H}W(h)f^{\Phi}(h;\chi,s)\,\mathrm{d}h

where
\chi=\omega_{\pi}|_{K^{\times}}
. This is well-defined for
\Re(s)\gg 0
and has analytic continuation as a meromorphic function of
q_{K}^{s}
; as usual, we define
L^{\As}(\pi,s)
as the unique
L
-factor which generates the fractional ideal of
\mathbb{C}[q_{K}^{\pm s}]
given by the values of this integral as
W
and
\Phi
vary.

8.2.2.
Torus integral

We define for
W\in\mathcal{W}(\pi)
a “partial zeta integral”

Z(W;s)=\int_{K^{\times}}W\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)|x|_{K}^{s-1}\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x.

Cf.
[
Gro21
, Definition 3.3.1]
. Again, the integral is convergent for
\Re(s)\gg 0
and has analytic continuation as a meromorphic function of
q_{K}^{s}
.

Proposition 8.2.1
.

The function
Z(hW;\chi,s)
transforms as an element of the induced representation
I(|\cdot|^{1/2-s},|\cdot|^{s-1/2}\chi)
, i.e. the dual of the representation in which the Siegel section lies; and we have

\int_{B_{H}\backslash H}Z(hW;s)f^{\Phi}(h;\chi,s)\,\mathrm{d}h=Z(W,\Phi,s).

Proof.

This follows by splitting up the integral over
Z_{H}N_{H}\backslash H
into an integral over
Z_{H}N_{H}\backslash B_{H}
followed by an integral over
B_{H}\backslash H
.
∎

Note that it suffices to take the integral over
K_{H}=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
, since
B_{H}K_{H}=H
.

8.2.3.
A special Schwartz function

Let
r\geq 1
, and consider the Schwartz function

\Phi_{r}=(q_{K}^{2}-1)q_{K}^{2r-2}\cdot\ch(\mathfrak{p}_{K}^{r},1+\mathfrak{p}_{K}^{r}).

From
[
LSZ22
, Lemma 3.2.5]
, if
\chi
is trivial on
1+\mathfrak{p}^{r}
, then
f^{\Phi_{r}}(-;\chi,s)
vanishes outside the coset
B_{H}\backslash B_{H}K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{K}^{r})
(the “small cell” modulo
\mathfrak{p}^{r}
), and we have

f^{\Phi_{r}}(1;\chi,s)=(q_{K}+1)q_{K}^{r-1},

which is also the volume of the small cell for the unramified Haar measure on
B_{H}\backslash H
. (On the other hand, if
\chi
is nontrivial on
1+\mathfrak{p}^{r}
then
f^{\Phi_{r}}(-;\chi,s)
is identically zero.)

Since the small cell modulo
\mathfrak{p}^{r}
is the orbit of the identity under the subgroup
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
\mathfrak{p}^{r}&1+\mathfrak{p}^{r}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, we have the following: if
W\in\mathcal{W}(\pi)
is invariant under this subgroup, the integrand
h\mapsto Z(hW;s)f^{\Phi}(h;\chi,s)
is equal to
f^{\Phi_{r}}(1)Z(W,s)
on the small cell in
(B_{H}\cap K_{H})\backslash K_{H}
, and vanishes everywhere else on
K_{H}
. So for any
W
invariant under this subgroup we have

Z(W,\Phi_{r},s)=Z(W;s).

8.3.
Imprimitive Asai
L
-factors

Definition 8.3.1
.

We define the
imprimitive Asai
L
-factor
by

L^{\mathrm{imp}}_{\As}(\pi,s)=\begin{cases}Z(W^{\mathrm{new}},s)&\text{if $\pi$ is ramified}\\
L(\chi,2s)\cdot Z(W^{\mathrm{new}},s)&\text{if $\pi$ is unramified}.\end{cases}

(Note that “unramified” here means “has invariants under
\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
”, even when
L
is a ramified extension.)

Proposition 8.3.2
.

Let
r_{0}
be such that
\varpi_{K}^{r_{0}}=\mathfrak{c}\cap\mathcal{O}_{K}
, where
\mathfrak{c}\trianglelefteqslant\mathcal{O}_{L}
is the conductor of
\pi
. Then we have

L^{\mathrm{imp}}_{\As}(\pi,s)=Z(W^{\mathrm{new}},\Phi_{r_{0}},s).

If
r_{0}\geq 1
then we have in fact
L^{\mathrm{imp}}_{\As}(\pi,s)=Z(W^{\mathrm{new}},\Phi_{r},s)
for any
r\geq r_{0}
.

8.3.1.
Unramified case

Suppose
\pi
has trivial conductor (but we allow
L
to be a ramified extension). Then the new vector is simply the normalised spherical Whittaker function
W^{\sph}
; and the function
h\mapsto Z(hW^{\sph},s)
is invariant under
K_{H}
, so we want to take
r=0
. In this case
f^{\Phi_{r}}(1;\chi,s)=L(\chi,s)
, so by a well-known explicit computation we find

L_{\mathrm{As}}^{\mathrm{imp}}(\pi,s)Z(W^{\sph},\Phi_{0},s)=L(\chi,s)Z(W^{\sph},s)=L_{\mathrm{As}}(\pi,s).

Remark 8.3.3
.

This computation is well-known if
L=K\times K
or
L
is an unramified quadratic extension, in which case
L_{\mathrm{As}}(\pi,s)
is a degree 4
L
-factor. However the computation is also valid for
L
a ramified field extension, in which case
L_{\mathrm{As}}
is a degree 3
L
-factor (and coincidentally agrees with the symmetric square
L
-factor of
\pi
).

8.3.2.
Explicit formulae

We give explicit formulae for the imprimitive Asai
L
-factor defined above.

Proposition 8.3.4
.

Suppose
L
is a field extension (ramified or unramified), and
\pi
has non-trivial conductor. Let
C
be the multiset of nonzero complex numbers such that

L^{\mathrm{std}}(\pi,s)=\prod_{\alpha\in C}(1-\alpha q_{L}^{-s})^{-1}.

Then

L^{\mathrm{imp}}_{\mathrm{As}}(\pi,s)=\prod_{\alpha\in C}(1-\alpha^{e_{L/K}}q_{K}^{-s})^{-1}

where
e_{L/K}
is the ramification index (1 or 2).∎

Proposition 8.3.5
.

Suppose
L=K\times K
(so again
q_{K}=q_{L}\eqqcolon q
), and
\pi=\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}
is arbitrary (ramified or unramified). For
i=1,2
let
C_{i}
be the multiset of complex numbers such that
L^{\mathrm{std}}(\pi_{i},s)=\prod_{\alpha_{i}\in C_{i}}(1-\alpha_{i}q^{-s})^{-1}
. Then we have

L^{\mathrm{imp}}_{\mathrm{As}}(\pi,s)=\prod_{\alpha_{1}\in C_{1}}\prod_{\alpha_{2}\in C_{2}}(1-\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}q^{-s})^{-1}.

8.3.3.
Relation to the primitive Asai
L
-factor

In all cases there exists an
L
-factor
P(s)
such that

L_{\mathrm{As}}(\pi,s)=P(s)\cdot L^{\mathrm{imp}}_{\mathrm{As}}(\pi,s).

We have seen that if
\pi
is unramified, then
P(s)=1
. One checks similarly that if
L=K\times K
, then we have
P(s)=1
in any of the following cases:

•

at least one of
\pi_{1}
,
\pi_{2}
is unramified;

•

one of the
\pi_{i}
is supercuspidal, but the other is not.

8.4.
Zeta integrals with
U^{t}
-eigenvectors

We now consider the local zeta integral at the split finite place
v=p
. Since we want to consider twists by ramified characters, we no longer assume the character
\nu
is trivial.

We can identify
\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{1},\psi_{p})\otimes\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{2},\psi_{p})
with
\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{p},\psi_{F,p})
via mapping
W_{1}\otimes W_{2}
to the function
g\mapsto W_{1}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1/\sqrt{D}&\\
&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)g_{1})W_{2}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1/\sqrt{D}&\\
&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)g_{2})
(so normalised new vectors go to normalised new vectors). A straightforward substitution gives

Z(W,\Phi;\nu,s)=\hat{\nu}_{p}^{-1}(-\sqrt{D})\cdot\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}}W_{1}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&\\
&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)g)W_{2}(g)\hat{\nu}_{p}^{-1}(\det g)f^{\Phi}(g;\chi\nu^{-2},s)\,\mathrm{d}g,

and the integral on the right-hand side is a Rankin–Selberg zeta integral. We are interested in the following situation:

•

\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are unramified;

•

\nu
is a Dirichlet character of
p
-power conductor (possibly trivial), so
\hat{\nu}_{p}^{-1}(-\sqrt{D})=\nu(-\sqrt{D})
;

•

\Phi=\Phi_{p,\nu^{-1},\nu}
as defined above;

•

W_{2}
is the normalised spherical vector;

•

W_{1}
is the
U_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{t}
-eigenvector given by
(1-\tfrac{\beta_{1}}{U_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{t}})W_{1}^{\sph}
, where
W_{1}^{\sph}
is the normalised spherical vector.

Proposition 8.4.1
.

With the above choices, we have

Z(W,\Phi;\nu,s)=\tfrac{p}{p+1}\nu(-\sqrt{D})\mathcal{E}_{p}(\As\Pi,\nu^{-1},s)L_{\As}(\Pi_{p}\times\hat{\nu}_{p}^{-1},s).

This is a rather standard computation (compare e.g.
[
CH20
]
) so we shall not give the proof in detail here.

8.5.
Archimedean case

It remains to compute the Archimedean zeta integral, for which we use the formula of
6.1.2
for
W_{\infty}
. Since
\hat{\nu}_{\infty}
is trivial on the support of
W_{\infty}
, we need to compute

Z(W_{\infty},{\Phi_{\infty}}^{(k_{1}-k_{2})},s)\coloneqq\int_{Z(\mathbb{R})N(\mathbb{R})\backslash\GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})}W_{\infty}(h)f^{\Phi_{\infty}}(h;1,s)\,\mathrm{d}h.

As shown in
[
Jac72
, p.85]
, since the integrand is invariant under
\mathbb{R}^{\times}\times{\rm SO}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we can write this as

f^{\Phi_{\infty}^{(k_{1}-k_{2})}}(1;1,s)\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}}y^{(k_{1}+k_{2})/2}e^{-4\pi y/\sqrt{D}}y^{s-1}\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}y.

We have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}}y^{(k_{1}+k_{2})/2}e^{-4\pi y/\sqrt{D}}y^{s-1}\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}y=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^{s-1+\tfrac{k_{1}+k_{2}}{2}}\cdot\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}\left(s-1+\tfrac{k_{1}+k_{2}}{2}\right),

where
\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)\coloneqq 2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)
, and similarly

f^{\Phi_{\infty}^{(k_{1}-k_{2})}}(1;1,s)=i^{k_{1}-k_{2}}2^{s-\tfrac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{2}}\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}\left(s+\tfrac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{2}\right),

so we conclude that

Z(W_{\infty},{\Phi_{\infty}}^{(k_{1}-k_{2})},s)=i^{k_{1}-k_{2}}2^{-k_{1}}(\sqrt{D})^{s-1+(k_{1}+k_{2})/2}\cdot\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s-1+\tfrac{k_{1}+k_{2}}{2})\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s+\tfrac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{2}).

8.6.
The global zeta integral

We can finally put together the zeta integrals of the above sections to prove our main result.

Theorem 8.6.1
.

Let
\Pi
be an automorphic representation as above, with central character
\chi_{\Pi}
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{\Pi}
the one-variable measure defined in Definition
7.3.2
. Then for any locally-algebraic
s+\nu
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, with
s
an integer such that
\tfrac{1-k_{1}+k_{2}}{2}\leq s\leq\tfrac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{2}
and
\nu
of finite order, we have

\mathcal{L}_{p,\As}(\Pi)(s+\nu)=\mathcal{E}_{p}(\As(\Pi),\nu^{-1},s)\cdot\frac{\Gamma(s+\tfrac{k_{1}+k_{2}-2}{2})\Gamma(s+\tfrac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{2})}{2^{(k_{1}-2)}i^{(1-k_{2})}(-2\pi i)^{(2s+k_{1}-1)}}\cdot\frac{L_{\As}\left(\Pi,\nu^{-1},s\right)}{\Omega(\Pi)}.

The same holds with
\mathcal{L}_{p,\As}^{\mathrm{imp}}(\Pi)
and
L_{\As}^{\mathrm{imp}}(\Pi,\nu^{-1},s)
in place of the primitive
L
-functions.

Proof.

We first consider the primitive
L
-function. By definition and the discussion in
section

7.3
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{p,\As}(\Pi)(s+\nu)=(\star)\cdot\prod_{v}\int_{Z_{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})N_{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})\backslash H(\mathbb{Q}_{v})}\nu^{-1}(\det(h_{v}))W_{v}(h_{v})f^{\Phi_{v}}(h_{v};\chi_{v}\nu^{-2},s)\,\mathrm{d}h_{v}.

where
(\star)
is the term from (
7.5
). We have chosen our test data so that the terms in this product for all places
v\notin\{p,\infty\}
are the local
L
-factors
L_{\As}(\Pi_{v}\times\nu_{v}^{-1},s)
. From
8.4.1
, the local term at

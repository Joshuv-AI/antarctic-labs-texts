Scattering for the non-radial focusing inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger-Choquard equation
Chengbin Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the long-time behavior of global solutions to the Schrödinger-Choquard equation i ∂ t u + Δ u = − ( I α ∗ | ⋅ | b | u | p ) | ⋅ | b | u | p − 2 u . i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=-(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p-2}u. Inspired by Murphy, who gave a simple proof of scattering for the non-radial inhomogeneous NLS, we prove scattering theory below the ground state for the intercritical case in energy space without radial assumption.



Key Words:
Schrödinger-Choquard equation; Scattering theory.

1.
Introduction

Considering the initial value problem (IVP), also called the Cauchy problem for the inhomogenous nonlinear Schrödinger-Choquard equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=-(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p-2}u,\ \ \ \ t\in\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits,\ x\in\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N})\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
u:\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
N\geq 3
. The inhomogeneous term is
|\cdot|^{b}
for some
b<0
. The Riesz-potential is defined on
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}
by

I_{\alpha}:=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}2^{\alpha}|\cdot|^{N-\alpha}}:=\frac{\mathcal{K}}{|\cdot|^{N-\alpha}},\ \ 0<\alpha<N.

And

1+\frac{2+\alpha+2b}{N}<p<1+\frac{2+\alpha+2b}{N-2}.

We also assume that

\displaystyle\min\{2+\alpha+2b,N+b,N+4b+2\alpha,4+\alpha+2b-N\}>0.

(1.2)

The class of solutions to (
1.1
) is left invariant by the scaling

u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{\frac{2+2b+\alpha}{2(p-1)}}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),

(1.3)

which is also invariant in

\dot{H}^{s_{c}}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N})
norm with
s_{c}=\frac{N}{2}-\frac{2+2b+\alpha}{2(p-1)},
. Thus, we call that the equation (
1.1
) is
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N})
critical.
Moreover, equation (
1.1
) conserves the mass, defined by

M(u):=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}}|u|^{2}dx,

and the energy, defined as the sum of the kinetic and potential energies:

E(u):=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}}\Big[\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{p}(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{b})|x|^{b}|u(x)|^{p}\Big]dx.

The particular case
b=0
, Eq.(
1.1
) becomes the nonlinear generalized Hartree equation. In
[
6
]
, Feng-Yuan have studied the Cauchy problem for the generalized Hartree equation in the energy subcritical. Miao-Xu-Zhao
[
10
,
11
]
have studied the well-posedness issues of the Hartree equation which corresponded to the particular case
p=2
. Saanouni
[
13
]
proved the scattering theory for the radial case via concentration-compactness roadmap.

For the Eq.(
1.1
), Alharbi-Saanouni
[
1
]
studied the local theory and finite time blow-up. In
[
14
]
, Saanouni proved the scattering theory with the radial setting. In this work, we extend Saanouni’s work to non-radial case via a new approach established by Murphy
[
12
]
.

As we known, the equation (
1.1
) admits a global but non-scattering solution

u(t,x)=e^{it}Q(x),

where
Q
is the ground state, i.e., the solution to elliptic equation

-\Delta Q+Q-(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|Q|^{p})|x|^{b}|Q|^{p-2}Q=0.

We refer to Alharbi-Saanouni
[
1
]
about the existence of the ground state for the above elliptic equation. The uniqueness is still open.

Now, we state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
b,\alpha
satisfy (
1.2
). Suppose that
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N})
satisfies

M(u_{0})^{1-s_{c}}E(u_{0})^{s_{c}}<M(Q)^{1-s_{c}}E(Q)^{s_{c}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{s_{c}}<\|Q\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla Q\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{s_{c}}.

Then, there exists a unique global solution
u
to (
1.1
). Moreover, the global solution
u
scatters in the sense that there exists
u_{\pm}\in H^{1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N})
such that

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\|u(t,x)-e^{it\Delta}u_{\pm}\|_{H^{1}_{x}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N})}=0.

(1.4)

Theorem
1.1
was established first in the radial case by Saanouni
[
14
]
. For the non-radial case, the non-radial approach by Dodson-Murphy
[
5
]
fails since the lack of Galilean invariant. However, we have a key observation that the nonlinearity has a decay factor
|x|^{b}
at infinity. Hence, this allow us treat the non-radial setting as the radial case (for more details, see
[
12
]
).

This paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we recall some basic estimates to prove some main tools and establish a new scattering criterion. In Section
3
, we prove a Morawetz estimate, which in turn implies ‘energy evacuation’ as
t\to\infty
. Thus, these two ingredients quickly complete Theorem
1.1
. Finally, a Morawetz identity is proved in Appendix.

We conclude the introduction by giving some notations which
will be used throughout this paper. We always use
X\lesssim Y
to denote
X\leq CY
for some constant
C>0
.
Similarly,
X\lesssim_{u}Y
indicates there exists a constant
C(u)
depending on
u
such that
X\leq C(u)Y
.
We also use the notation
\mathcal{O}
: e.g.
A=\mathcal{O}(B)
indicates
A\leq CB
for constant
C>0
.
The derivative operator
\nabla
refers to the spatial variable only.
We use
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to denote the Banach space of functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
whose norm

\|f\|_{r}:=\|f\|_{L^{r}}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f(x)|^{r}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}

is finite, with the usual modifications when
r=\infty
. For any non-negative integer
k
,
we denote by
H^{k,r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the Sobolev space defined as the closure of smooth compactly supported functions in the norm
\|f\|_{H^{k,r}}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|\frac{\partial^{\alpha}f}{\partial x^{\alpha}}\|_{r}
, and we denote it by
H^{k}
when
r=2
.
For a time slab
I
, we use
L_{t}^{q}(I;L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
to denote the space-time norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{t}^{q}L^{r}_{x}(I\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N})}=\bigg(\int_{I}\|f(t,x)\|_{L^{r}_{x}}^{q}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

with the usual modifications when
q
or
r
is infinite, sometimes we use
\|f\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{r})}
or
\|f\|_{L^{q}L^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}
for short.

2.
Preliminaries

Let us start this section by introducing the notation used throughout the paper. We recall some Strichartz estimates associated to the linear Schrödinger propagator.

We say the pair
(q,r)
is
\dot{H}^{s}
-admissible, if it satisfies the condition

\frac{2}{q}=\frac{N}{2}-\frac{N}{r}-s,\ \ 2\leq q,r\leq\infty,\text{and}\ \ N\geq 3.

For
s\in(0,1)
, We define the sets
\Lambda_{s}
:

\Lambda_{s}:=\left\{(q,r)\ \text{is}\ \dot{H}^{s}\text{-admissible};\Big(\frac{2N}{N-2s}\Big)^{+}\leq r\leq\Big(\frac{2N}{N-2}\Big)^{-}\right\},

and

\Lambda_{-s}:=\left\{(q,r)\ \text{is}\ \dot{H}^{-s}\text{-admissible};\Big(\frac{2N}{N-2s}\Big)^{+}\leq r\leq\Big(\frac{2N}{N-2}\Big)^{-}\right\},

and let
\Lambda_{0}
denote the
L^{2}
-admissible. Here,
a^{-}
is a fixed number slightly smaller than
a
(
a^{-}=a-\epsilon
, where
\epsilon
is small enough).
a^{+}
can be defined by the same way.

Next, we define the following Strichartz norm

\|u\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s},I)}=\sup_{(q,r)\in\Lambda_{s}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I)}

and dual Strichartz norm

\|u\|_{S^{{}^{\prime}}(\dot{H}^{-s},I)}=\inf_{(q,r)\in\Lambda_{-s}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{q^{{}^{\prime}}}L_{x}^{r^{{}^{\prime}}}(I)},

where
q^{\prime}
denotes the dual exponent to
q
, i.e. the solution to
\tfrac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1.

If
I=\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
,
I
is omitted usually.

Now, let us recall some results about Strichartz estimates
[
3
,
7
,
8
]
and Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev’s inequality
[
9
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0\in I\subset\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
, the following statement hold

(i)

(
linear estimate
)

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s})}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}};

(ii)

(
nonlinear estimate I
)

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}g(\cdot,s)ds\right\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\leq C\|g\|_{S^{{}^{\prime}}(L^{2},I)};

(iii)

(
nonlinear estimate II
)

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}g(\cdot,s)ds\right\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s},I)}\leq C\|g\|_{S^{{}^{\prime}}(\dot{H}^{-s},I)}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
N\geq 3
,
0<\lambda<N
and
1<r,s<\infty<\infty
and
f\in L^{r},g\in L^{s}
. If
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{s}+\frac{\lambda}{N}=2
, then

\iint_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}}\frac{f(x)g(y)}{|x-y|^{\lambda}}dxdy\leq C(N,s,\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{r}_{x}}\|g\|_{L^{s}_{x}}.

Next, we prove some interpolation estimates for nonlinearities, which plays an important role in proving scattering theory.

Lemma 2.3

(Nonlinear estimate)
.

Let
N\geq 3
,
b,\alpha
satisfy (
1.2
) and
p\geq 2
. Then there exists
\theta\in(0,2(p-1))
sufficiently small such that

(i)

\|(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p-2}u\|_{S^{\prime}(\dot{H}^{s_{c}})}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s_{c}})}^{2p-1-\theta}
;

(ii)

\|(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p-2}u\|_{S^{\prime}(L^{2})}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{2}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s_{c}})}^{2(p-1)-\theta}\|u\|_{S(L^{2})}
;

(iii)

\|\nabla(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p-2}u\|_{S^{\prime}(L^{2})}\lesssim\|u\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s_{c}})}^{2(p-1)-\theta}(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}_{x}}^{\theta}\|\nabla u\|_{S(L^{2})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}_{x}}^{1+\theta})
.

Proof.

In view of the singular factor
|x|^{b}
in the nonlinearity, we frequently divide our analysis in two region. Let
B_{1}(0)
denote the unit ball of radius
1
and center in origin, and
B_{1}^{c}(0)
be
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0)
.

We introduce the parameters

\displaystyle\hat{r}=\frac{2(p-1)N(2p-\theta)}{2(p-1)(N+2b+\alpha)-\theta(\alpha+2b+2)}.

(2.5)

Choosing
\hat{q},\hat{a},\tilde{a}
and
\theta
such that
(\hat{q},\hat{r})\in\Lambda_{0}
,
(\hat{a},\hat{r})\in\Lambda_{s_{c}}
and
(\tilde{a},\hat{r})\in\Lambda_{-s_{c}}
. These exponents obey the relations

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{a}^{\prime}}=\frac{2p-1-\theta}{\hat{a}}\ \ and\ \ \frac{1}{\hat{q}^{\prime}}=\frac{2(p-1)-\theta}{\hat{a}}+\frac{1}{\hat{q}}.

(2.6)

We first prove
(i)
, using the pair
L_{t}^{\tilde{a}^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}^{\prime}}
. Let
r_{1}
be chosen later and define
\mu
so that

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\alpha}{N}=\frac{2}{\mu}+\frac{2p-\theta}{\hat{r}}+\frac{1}{r_{1}}.

(2.7)

Then,
A\in\{B_{1}(0),B_{1}^{c}(0)\}
, using the Hardy-Littlewood-Paley inequality to estimate

\displaystyle\|(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p-2}u\|_{L^{\hat{r}^{\prime}}}\lesssim\||x|^{b}\|_{L^{\mu}(A)}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{\theta r_{1}}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{\hat{r}}}^{2p-1-\theta}.

(2.8)

Using the scaling relation above, we derive

\frac{2N}{\mu}+2b=\frac{\theta(2+\alpha+2b)}{2(p-1)}-\frac{N}{r_{1}}.

Thus, if
A=B_{1}(0)
we choose
r_{1}
so that
\theta r_{1}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
. On the other hand, if
A=B_{1}^{c}(0)
, we choose
\theta r_{1}=2
. In both case, we have Sobolev embedding that
H^{1}\subset L^{\theta r_{1}}
. Thus, we take the
L_{t}^{\tilde{a}^{\prime}}
-norm, apply Hölder’s inequality, and use the above scaling relation (
2.6
) to obtain

\|(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p-2}u\|_{L_{t}^{\tilde{a}^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\hat{a}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}}}^{2p-1-\theta}.

The estimates of
(ii)
is similar. In this case, we use the second relation of (
2.6
) to get

\|(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p-2}u\|_{L_{t}^{\hat{q}^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1}_{x}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\hat{a}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}}}^{2(p-1)-\theta}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\hat{q}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}}}.

Consider now
(iii)
. We choose the exponents
\bar{q},\bar{r},\bar{a}

\bar{r}=\frac{4(p-1)N(2p-1-\theta)}{2(p-1)(N+2+4b+2\alpha)-\theta(2+\alpha+2b)},

such that
(\bar{q},\bar{r})\in\Lambda_{0},(\bar{a},\bar{r})_{\Lambda_{s_{c}}}
. Since

\displaystyle|\nabla(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|x|^{b}|u|^{p-2}u|\lesssim

\displaystyle(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b-1}|u|^{p})|x|^{b}|u|^{p-1}

\displaystyle+(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p-1}|\nabla u|)|x|^{b}|u|^{p-1}

\displaystyle+(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|x|^{b-1}|u|^{p-1}

\displaystyle+(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|x|^{b}|u|^{p-2}|\nabla u|,

by using Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|\nabla(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|x|^{b}|u|^{p-2}u\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2N}{N+2}}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\||x|^{b}\|_{L_{x}^{r_{1}}(A)}^{2}(\||x|^{-1}|u|^{p}\|_{L_{x}^{r_{2}}}+\||u|^{p-1}\nabla u\|_{L_{x}^{r_{2}}})\||u|^{p-1}\|_{L_{x}^{r_{3}}}

\displaystyle+\||x|^{b}\|_{L_{x}^{r_{1}}(A)}^{2}\||u|^{p}\|_{L_{x}^{r_{2}}}(\||x|^{-1}|u|^{p-1}\|_{L_{x}^{r_{3}}}+\||u|^{p-2}\nabla u\|_{L_{x}^{r_{3}}}),

with the following scaling relation:

1+\frac{\alpha}{N}=\frac{N-2}{2N}+\frac{2}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}+\frac{1}{r_{3}}.

And, we choose
l
such that

\displaystyle\frac{2}{r_{1}}+\frac{2b}{N}=l\ \ \text{and}\ \ l:=\begin{cases}&\frac{\theta(1-s_{c})}{N},\ \ \text{if}\ A=B,\\
&-\frac{\theta s_{c}}{N},\ \ \ \ \text{if}\ A=B^{c}.\end{cases}

Since
1<\frac{2N}{N+2+4b+2\alpha}<N
, if we choose
\theta
small enough, we conclude that
\||x|^{b}\|_{L_{x}^{L^{r_{1}}}(A)}<\infty
and that
1<r_{2},r_{3}<N
. Hence, by Hardy’s inequality, we have

\||x|^{-1}f\|_{L_{x}^{r_{2}}}\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L_{x}^{r_{2}}}.

Now, by splitting

\frac{1}{r_{2}}+\frac{1}{r_{3}}=\left(\theta\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{s_{c}}{N}\right)-l\right)\left(\frac{1}{r_{4}}\right)+\frac{2(p-1)-\theta}{\bar{r}}+\frac{1}{\bar{r}},

\frac{1}{r_{3}}=\frac{p-1}{\bar{r}},

we can get
2\leq\theta r_{4}\leq 2N/(N-2)
. Thus, using Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\|\nabla(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|x|^{b}|u|^{p-2}u\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{2N}{N+2}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\bar{a}}L_{x}^{\bar{r}}}^{2(p-1)-\theta}\|\nabla u\|_{L_{t}^{\bar{a}}L_{x}^{\bar{r}}},

(2.9)

which completes lemma.

2.1.
Scattering criterion

To show Theorem
1.1
, we first establish a scattering criterion by following that in
[
12
]
.

Lemma 2.4

(Scattering Criterion)
.

Let
b,\alpha,p
satisfy the condition of Theorem
1.1
. Suppose
u
is the global solution to (
1.1
) satisfying

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}}\leq E.

There exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(E)>0
and
R=R(E)>0
such that if

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\int_{|x|\leq R}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx\leq\epsilon^{2},

(2.10)

then
u
scatters forward in time.

Proof.

By Duhamel’s formula, we have

u=e^{i(t-T_{0})\Delta}u(T_{0})+i\int_{T_{0}}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}F(u(s))ds

where
F(u)=(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|x|^{b}|u|^{p-2}u.

By Lemma
2.3
and continuity argument, we need to show

\|e^{i(t-T_{0})\Delta}u(T_{0})\|_{L_{t}^{\hat{a}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}}([T_{0},\infty))}\ll 1.

where the exponent
(\hat{a},\hat{r})
is as in Lemma
2.3
.

Next, we use the Duhamel formula to write

\displaystyle e^{i(t-T_{0})\Delta}u(T_{0})=e^{it\Delta}u_{0}-iG_{1}(t)-iG_{2}(t),

where

\displaystyle G_{j}(t):=\int_{I_{j}}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}F(u(s))ds,~j=1,2,

here
I_{1}=[0,T_{0}-\epsilon^{-\eta}],I_{2}=[T_{0}-\epsilon^{-\eta},T_{0}].

Choosing
T_{0}
large enough, we have

\|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{\hat{a}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}}([T_{0},\infty))}\ll 1.

It remains to show

\displaystyle\|G_{j}(t)\|_{L_{t}^{\hat{a}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}}([T_{0},\infty))}\ll 1,\quad\text{~ for ~}j=1,2.

Estimation of
G_{1}(t)
: We may use the dispersive estimate, Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev’s inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|G_{1}(t)\right\|_{L_{t}^{q_{0}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{T_{0}}^{\infty}\left(\int_{I_{1}}|t-s|^{-\frac{2}{q_{0}}-1}\|u\|_{H_{x}^{1}}^{2p-1}ds\right)^{q_{0}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{E}\left(\int_{T_{0}}^{\infty}|t-T_{0}+\epsilon^{-\theta}|^{-2}dsdt\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{E}\epsilon^{\frac{\eta}{q_{0}}},

where let
q_{0}
satisfy
(q_{0},\hat{r})\in\Lambda_{1}
.

On the other hand, we may rewrite
G_{1}
as

G_{1}(t)=e^{i(t-T_{0}+\epsilon^{-\eta})\Delta}u(T_{0}-\epsilon^{-\eta})-e^{it\Delta}u_{0}.

(2.11)

By
(\hat{q},\hat{r})\in\Lambda_{0}
, then

\frac{1}{\hat{a}}=\frac{1-s_{c}}{\hat{q}}+\frac{s_{c}}{q_{0}}.

Using Strichartz estimates and (
2.11
),
we have

\|G_{1}(t)\|_{L_{t}^{q_{0}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}}}\lesssim 1.

Thus, by interpolation, we get

\|G_{1}(t)\|_{L_{t}^{\hat{a}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}}([T_{0},\infty))}\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{\eta s_{c}}{q_{0}}}.

Estimation of
G_{2}(t)
: By Strichartz’s estimates, we have

\displaystyle\|G_{2}(t)\|_{L_{t}^{\hat{q}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}}([T_{0},\infty))}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|F(u)\|_{L_{t}^{\tilde{a}^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}^{\prime}}(I_{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\chi_{R}F(u)\|_{L_{t}^{\tilde{a}^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}^{\prime}}(I_{2})}+\|(1-\chi_{R})F(u)\|_{L_{t}^{\tilde{a}^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}^{\prime}}(I_{2})},

where
\chi_{R}
is a smooth cutoff to
\{x:\;|x|\leq R\}.

Using Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev’s inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we get

\|\chi_{R}F(u)\|_{L^{\hat{r}^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2(p-1)}\|\chi_{R}u\|_{L^{\hat{r}}}\lesssim_{E}\|\chi_{R}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\beta},

where
\beta
satisfies
1/\hat{r}=\beta/2+(1-\beta)/2^{*}
with
2^{\ast}=\tfrac{2N}{N-2}.
On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\|(1-\chi_{R})F(u)\|_{L^{\hat{r}^{\prime}}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\||x|^{b}\|_{L^{\mu_{1}}(A)}\||x|^{b}\|_{L^{\mu_{2}}(|x|\geq R)}\|u\|_{L^{\theta r_{1}}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{\hat{r}}}^{2p-1-\theta},

where the exponents have the scaling relation

\frac{N}{\mu_{1}}+\frac{N}{\mu_{2}}+2b=\frac{\theta(2+\alpha+2b)}{2(p-1)}-\frac{N}{r_{1}}.

Since
\frac{\theta(N-2)}{2}<\frac{\theta(2+\alpha+2b)}{2(p-1)}<\frac{\theta N}{2}
, there exists
\mu_{2}>\frac{N}{-b}
, if
A=B_{1}(0)
we can choose
\theta r_{1}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
, or else
A=B_{1}^{c}(0)
we can choose
\theta r_{1}=2
. In both case, we have
H^{1}\subset L^{\theta r_{1}}
and
|x|^{b}\in L^{\mu_{1}}(A).

Thus, we have

\|(1-\chi_{R})F(u)\|_{L^{\hat{r}^{\prime}}}\lesssim R^{\mu_{2}b+N}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2p-1}.

Let
T_{0}
be large enough. By the assumption (
2.10
) and identity
\partial_{t}|u|^{2}=-2\nabla\cdot Im(\bar{u}\nabla u)
, together with integration by parts and Cauchy-Schwartz, we deduce

\left|\partial_{t}\int_{I_{2}}\chi_{R}|u|^{2}ds\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{R}.

Choosing
R\geq\max\{\epsilon^{-(2+\theta)},\epsilon^{\frac{1}{N+\mu_{2}b}}\}
, we find

\|\chi_{R}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(I_{2}\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N})}\lesssim\epsilon,

and

\|F(u)\|_{L^{\hat{r}^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{E}\epsilon^{\beta}.

Then, let
\eta=\frac{\hat{a}^{\prime}\beta}{2}
, we bound

\displaystyle\|G_{2}(t)\|_{L_{t}^{\hat{q}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}}([T_{0},\infty))}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|F(u)\|_{L_{t}^{\tilde{a}^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\hat{r}^{\prime}}(I_{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|I_{2}|^{1/\hat{a}^{\prime}}\epsilon^{\beta}

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\frac{\beta}{2}},

which completes the proof of lemma.

2.2.
Local theory and Variational analysis

The local theory for (
1.1
) is standard via Strichartz estimates and the fixed point argument. For any
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N})
, there exists a unique maximal-lifespan solution
u
. This solution belongs to
C_{t}H_{x}^{1}(I_{\max}\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N})
and conserves the mass and energy. Because the nonlinear term is
H^{1}
-subcritical, we have an
H^{1}
blow-up criterion. In particular, if
u
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}
, then it is global. For more details, we refer the reader to
[
1
,
13
]
.

We briefly review some of the variational analysis related to the ground state
Q
. For more details, see
[
1
,
13
]
.

The ground state
Q
optimizes the sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality:

\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|x|^{b}|u|^{p}dx\leq C_{0}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{A}\|\nabla u\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{B},

where
B:=Np-N-\alpha-2b=2(p-1)s_{c}+2
,
A:=2p-B=2(p-1)s_{c}
. And
Q
satisfies
\|\nabla Q\|^{2}=\frac{B}{A}\|Q\|^{2}
,

\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|Q|^{p})|x|^{b}|Q|^{p}dx=\frac{2p}{B}\|\nabla Q\|^{2},

thus

\frac{2p}{B}\leq C_{0}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2(p-1)(1-s_{c})}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2(p-1)s_{c}}.

In the spirit of Dodson-Murphy
[
4
,
5
]
, we can obtain the following coercivity, which can be founded in
[
14
]
.

Lemma 2.5

(Coercivity I)
.

Suppose that
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N})
satisfies

M(u_{0})^{1-s_{c}}E(u_{0})^{s_{c}}<(1-\delta)M(Q)^{1-s_{c}}E(Q)^{s_{c}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{s_{c}}<\|Q\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla Q\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{s_{c}}.

Then, there exists
\delta^{\prime}=\delta^{\prime}(\delta)>0
so that

\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}<(1-\delta^{\prime})\|Q\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla Q\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}

for all
t\in I
, where
u:I\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the maximal-lifespan solution to (
1.1
). In particular,
I=\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
and
u
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}
.

Lemma 2.6

(Coercivity II)
.

Suppose
\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{s_{c}}\leq(1-\delta)\|Q\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla Q\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{s_{c}}
. Then there exists
\delta^{\prime}>0
so that

\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}}|\nabla f|^{2}dx-\frac{B}{2p}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|x|^{b}|u|^{p}dx\geq\delta^{\prime}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}}|\nabla f|^{2}dx.

Proof.

Using the identity

\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}}|\nabla f|^{2}dx-\frac{B}{2p}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|x|^{b}|u|^{p}dx=\frac{B}{2}E(f)-\frac{B-2}{2}\|\nabla f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}.

By the sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\displaystyle E(f)

\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\left[1-\frac{C_{0}}{p}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{A}\|\nabla f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{B-2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\left[1-\frac{(1-\delta)C_{0}}{p}\|Q\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{A}\|\nabla Q\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{B-2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{B-2(1-\delta)^{2(p-1)}}{B}\|\nabla f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}.

Thus

\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}}|\nabla f|^{2}dx-\frac{B}{2p}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|\cdot|^{b}|u|^{p})|x|^{b}|u|^{p}dx\geq\delta^{\prime}\|\nabla f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}.

MM

\square

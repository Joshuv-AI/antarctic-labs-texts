Particular existence of positive solutions for elliptic equations involving regional fractional Laplacian of order ( 0 , 1 2 ] (0,\frac{1}{2}]


Abstract
Our purpose of this paper is to investigate positive solutions of the elliptic equation with regional fractional Laplacian ( − Δ ) B 1 s ​ u + u = h ⁡ ( x , u ) in ​ B 1 , u ∈ C 0 ​ ( B 1 ) , (-\Delta)_{B_{1}}^{s}u+u=h(x,u)\quad{\rm in}\ \,B_{1},\qquad u\in C_{0}(B_{1}), where ( − Δ ) B 1 s (-\Delta)_{B_{1}}^{s} with s ∈ ( 0 , 1 2 ] s\in(0,\frac{1}{2}] is the regional fractional Laplacian and h h is the nonlinearity. Ordinarily, positive solutions vanishing at the boundary are not anticipated to be derived for the equations with regional fractional Laplacian of order s ∈ ( 0 , 1 2 ] s\in(0,\frac{1}{2}] . Introduction

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
\Omega
is
C^{2}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
,

(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}
be the regional fractional Laplacian defined by

(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u(x)=c_{N,s}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(z)}{|z-x|^{N+2s}}\,dz,

where
B_{r}(x)

B_{r}=B_{r}(0)
,
here

is the normalized constant of fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
(simply we use the notation
(-\Delta)^{s}
), see
[
17
]
. Our interest of this paper is to study the existence of positive solutions to the following
Schrödinger equation with regional fractional Laplacian

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(-\Delta)_{B_{1}}^{s}u+u=h(x,u)&{\rm in}\ \,B_{1},\\[5.69054pt]
\qquad\ \,u\in C_{0}(B_{1}),\end{array}\right.

is a measurable function,

is the set of function continuous in
\bar{B}_{1}
,
which vanishes at the boundary
\partial B_{1}
.

In the last few years, nonlocal problems have emerged extensively in physics models, operation research, queuing theory, mathematical finance, risk estimation, and others domains (see
[
7
]
). The regional fractional Laplacian serves as a typical operator as the generator of the censored stable process. In fact, from the perspective of Probability, a symmetric
2s
-stable process in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
killed upon leaving the set
\Omega
is called a symmetric
2s
-stable process in
\Omega
. Bogdan, Burdzy and Chen
[
5
]
(also see Guan and Ma
[
14
,
22
]
) extended this type of processes to obtain a version of the strong Markov process, named censored symmetric stable process. A censored stable process in an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is derived from the symmetric stable process by suppressing its jumps from
\Omega
to
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega
. We’d like to mention that the censored stable process
has some interesting properties, which indicate certain differences between
(\frac{1}{2},\,1)
and
(0,\,\frac{1}{2}]
(see
[
5
, Theorem 1]
):

(i) for
s\in(\frac{1}{2},\,1)
, the censored symmetric
2s
-stable process in
\Omega
has a finite lifetime and will approach
\partial\Omega
;

(ii) for
s\in(0,\,\frac{1}{2}]
, the censored symmetric
2s
-stable process in
\Omega
is conservative and will never approach
\partial\Omega
.

On the analysis side, interesting new phenomena occur in relation to
elliptic problems involving the regional fractional Laplacian.
Let

{\bf H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
be the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
under the semi-norm that

\|u\|_{s,\Omega}=\sqrt{\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy}.

The authors in
[
24
]
showed that

for
s\in(\frac{1}{2},\,1)
, Hilbert space

has zero trace;

for
s\in(0,\,\frac{1}{2}]
, Hilbert space
{\bf H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
has no zero trace.

Let

be the closure of

under the norm that

It is worth noting that

,

with zero boundary condition.

2.1
Connections of regional fractional Laplacian and fractional Laplacian

Note that by the zero extension of the function in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
, we can build the connection between
regional fractional Laplacian and fractional Laplacian.

Given
u\in C_{0}(\Omega)
, we denote

\tilde{u}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u(x),&x\in\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
0,&x\notin\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

Then for

, it holds that

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}\tilde{u}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm p.v.}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy+\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{u(x)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u(x)+u(x)\varphi_{{}_{\Omega}}(x),

where

\varphi_{{}_{\Omega}}(x)=\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}.

(2.2)

Proposition 2.1

3
Radial symmetry and decreasing monotonicity

3.1
Maximum Principle for small domain

The essential tool for the moving planes in a ball is the Maximum Principle for small domains:

Proposition 3.1

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an open set in
{B_{1}}
such that
|\mathcal{O}|\leq 2^{-N}|B_{1}|

Suppose that
\phi:\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}<+\infty,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(-\Delta)_{B_{1}}^{s}w\geq\phi w&{\rm in}\ \mathcal{O},\\[5.69054pt]
\qquad\quad\,w\geq 0&{\rm in}\ {B_{1}}\setminus\mathcal{O}.\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

Then there exists
\delta>0
such that for

,

in
\mathcal{O}
.

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus{B_{\rho(x_{1})}(x_{1})}}\frac{1}{|z-x_{1}|^{N+2s+1}}dz

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus{B_{\rho(x_{2})}(x_{2})}}\frac{1}{|z-x_{2}|^{N+2s+1}}dz

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}(\rho(x_{1})^{-1-2s}+\rho(x_{2})^{-1-2s})

and similar to obtain that

\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{1}{|z-x_{1}|^{N+2s}|z-x_{2}|}dz\leq c_{4}(\rho(x_{1})^{-1-2s}+\rho(x_{2})^{-1-2s}),

where
c_{3},c_{4}>0
are independent of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
.
Then

|\varphi_{{}_{\Omega}}(x_{1})-\varphi_{{}_{\Omega}}(x_{2})|\leq c_{5}(\rho(x_{1})^{-1-2s}+\rho(x_{2})^{-1-2s})|x_{1}-x_{2}|,

where
c_{5}=c_{2}(c_{3}+c_{4})
,
it implies that
\varphi_{{}_{\Omega}}
is locally Lipschitz continuous.

(ii) Firstly, we claim that
\varphi_{B_{1}}(x)=\varphi_{B_{1}}(z)
if
|x|=|z|
. In fact,
denote
{\bf A}
a matrix with
|{\bf A}|=1
and
z={\bf A}x
, we have that

\displaystyle\varphi_{B_{1}}(z)=\varphi_{B_{1}}({\bf A}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{dy}{|{\bf A}x-y|^{N+2s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{d\tilde{y}}{|x-\tilde{y}|^{N+2s}}=\varphi_{B_{1}}(x),

where
\tilde{y}={\bf A}^{-1}y
.

Now we show the monotonicity. By the radial symmetry of
\varphi
, we let

\varphi(r)=\varphi_{B_{1}}(x),\quad\ r=|x|\in(0,1).

Fixed
x_{1}=t_{1}e_{1},

x_{2}=t_{2}e_{1}
,

e_{1}=(1,0,\cdots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<1
, by direct computation, it yields that

\displaystyle\varphi(t_{1})-\varphi(t_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}^{c}}(\frac{1}{|t_{1}e_{1}-y|^{N+2s}}-\frac{1}{|t_{2}e_{1}-y|^{N+2s}})dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}}(\frac{1}{|t_{1}e_{1}-y|^{N+2s}}-\frac{1}{|t_{2}e_{1}-y|^{N+2s}})dy

\displaystyle\quad\ +\int_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}(\frac{1}{|t_{1}e_{1}-y|^{N+2s}}-\frac{1}{|t_{2}e_{1}-y|^{N+2s}})dy,

where
\mathcal{A}_{0}=B_{1}\big((t_{1}+t_{2})e_{1}\big)\setminus B_{1},

\mathcal{A}_{1}=\left\{(x^{1},x^{\prime})\ |\ (x^{1},x^{\prime})\in\big(-\infty,\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}\big)\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\setminus B_{1}\right\}

and

\mathcal{A}_{2}=\left\{(x^{1},x^{\prime})\ |\ (x^{1},x^{\prime})\in\big(\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2},+\infty\big)\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\setminus B_{1}\big((t_{1}+t_{2})e_{1}\big)\right\}.

Observe that

\int_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}}(\frac{1}{|t_{1}e_{1}-y|^{N+2s}}-\frac{1}{|t_{2}e_{1}-y|^{N+2s}})dy=0.

Since
|t_{1}e_{1}-y|>|t_{2}e_{1}-y|
for any
y\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
, then it deduces that

\varphi(t_{1})-\varphi(t_{2})=\int_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}(\frac{1}{|t_{1}e_{1}-y|^{N+2s}}-\frac{1}{|t_{2}e_{1}-y|^{N+2s}})dy<0.

and then

\displaystyle\varphi_{B_{1}}(x)=\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}^{c}(x)}\frac{dz}{|z|^{N+2s}}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{1-|x|}}^{c}(\frac{x}{1-|x|})}\frac{(1-|x|)^{N}d\tilde{z}}{(1-|x|)^{N+2s}|\tilde{z}|^{N+2s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-|x|)^{2s}}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{1-|x|}}^{c}(\frac{x}{1-|x|})}\frac{d\tilde{z}}{|\tilde{z}|^{N+2s}}.

Combining with
\bigcap{B_{\frac{1}{1-|x|}}^{c}(\frac{x}{1-|x|})}=(-\infty,-1)\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}

and

\displaystyle\int_{(-\infty,-1)\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{d\tilde{z}}{|\tilde{z}|^{N+2s}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{-1}{d\tilde{z}_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{d\tilde{z}^{\prime}}{(|\tilde{z}_{1}|^{2}+|\tilde{z}^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{-1}{d\tilde{z}_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{{\tilde{z}_{1}}^{N-1}dt^{\prime}}{|\tilde{z}_{1}|^{N+2s}{(1+|t^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{dt^{\prime}}{{(1+|t^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}}\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\frac{d\tilde{z}_{1}}{{\tilde{z}_{1}}^{2s+1}}\,=:c_{1},

it deduces (
2.3
).

\Box

A.2
Potential inequalities

For
r_{0}>0
, denote
\Phi_{r_{0}}
the Green kernel of
(-\Delta)^{s}
in
B_{r_{0}}
with the zero Dirichlet boundary condition in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus(B_{r_{0}}\times B_{r_{0}})
, observe that

\Phi_{r_{0}}(x,y)\leq c_{27}|x-y|^{2s-N}

(A.1)

for some
c_{27}>0
independent of
r_{0}
.

Lemma A.1

Assume that
s\in(0,1)
and integer
N\geq 2
.

(i) if

\frac{1}{q}<\frac{2s}{N},

then there exists some
c_{28}>0

such that

\|\Phi_{r_{0}}\ast h\|_{\infty}\leq c_{28}\|h\|_{q};

(A.2)

(ii) if

\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{r}+\frac{2s}{N},\quad\quad q>1,

then
there exists some
c_{29}>0
such that

\|\Phi_{r_{0}}\ast h\|_{r}\leq c_{29}\|h\|_{q}.

(A.3)

(iii) if

1<\frac{1}{r}+\frac{2s}{N},

then
there exists some
c_{30}>0
such that

\|\Phi_{r_{0}}\ast h\|_{r}\leq c_{30}\|h\|_{1}.

(A.4)

Proof.
Together with (
A.1
), we apply Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev theorem for the fractional integration
[
28
, Chapter 5, section 1]
. For the convenience of the readers, we provides the details of the proof.

Proof of (
A.2
)
.
For any
x\in\Omega
and
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}
, by
Hölder inequality and (
A.1
), it holds that

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{r_{0}}\ast h\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\Phi_{r_{0}}^{q^{\prime}}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\Big(\int_{B_{r_{0}}(x)}|h(y)|^{q}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{31}\|h\|_{q}\Big(\int_{B_{r_{0}}(x)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{(N-2s)q^{\prime}}}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{32}\|h\|_{q},

by the fact that

\frac{1}{q}<\frac{2s}{N},\qquad(N-2s)q^{\prime}<N

and

\int_{B_{r_{0}}(x)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{(N-2s)q^{\prime}}}dy<+\infty.

Proof of (
A.3
) and (
A.4
) with
r\leq q
.
By Minkowski inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\|(\Phi_{r_{0}}\ast h\|_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|h\ast\Phi_{r_{0}}\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{33}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\int_{B_{r_{0}}}\frac{|h|(x-y)\chi_{{}_{B_{2}(0)}}(y)}{|y|^{N-2s}}dx\Big)^{r}dy\Big]^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{34}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|h(x-y)|^{r}\chi_{{}_{B_{2}(0)}}(y)}{|y|^{(N-2s)r}}dxdy\Big]^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{35}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h(x-y)|^{r}dx\frac{\chi_{{}_{B_{2}(0)}}(y)}{|y|^{N-2s}}dy\Big]^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{36}\|h\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Proof of (
A.3
) and (
A.4
) with

r>q\geq 1
and
\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{r}+\frac{2s}{N}
. We claim that
if
r>s
and
\frac{1}{r^{*}}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{2s}{N}
, the mapping

h\to(\Phi_{r_{0}}\eta_{0})\ast h
is weak-type
(q,r^{*})
in the sense that

|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|(\Phi_{r_{0}}\eta_{0})\ast h(x)|>t\}|\leq\Big(A_{q,r^{*}}\frac{\|h\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}}{t}\Big)^{r^{*}},\quad h\in L^{q}(B_{r_{0}}),

(A.5)

for all
t>0
, where
A_{q,r^{*}}>0
.

For
\nu>0
, we denote

G_{1}=\Phi_{r_{0}}\eta_{0}\chi_{{}_{B_{\nu}}},\quad\quad G_{2}=\Phi_{r_{0}}\eta_{0}\chi_{{}_{B_{\nu}^{c}}}.

it deduces that

\displaystyle\Big|\{x\in B_{r_{0}}:|(\Phi_{r_{0}}\eta_{0})\ast h(x)|>2t\}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|G_{1}\ast h(x)|>t\}\Big|+\Big|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|G_{2}\ast h(x)|>t\}\Big|.

One hand, by Minkowski inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\Big|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|G_{1}\ast h(x)|>t\}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|G_{1}\ast h\|^{s}_{s}}{t^{s}}=\frac{\|h\ast G_{1}\|^{s}_{s}}{t^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h(x-y)|^{s}dx)^{\frac{1}{s}}|y|^{2\alpha-N}\chi_{{}_{B_{\nu}}}(y)dy]^{s}}{t^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|h\|^{s}_{s}}{t^{s}}\int_{B_{\nu}}|y|^{2\alpha-N}dy=c_{37}\nu^{2\alpha}\frac{\|h\|^{s}_{s}}{t^{s}}.

On the other hand, direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\|G_{2}\ast h\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{38}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\chi_{{}_{B_{\nu}^{c}}}(x-y)(\Phi_{r_{0}}\eta_{0})|h(y)|dy\Big\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h(y)|^{q}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|\Big(\int_{B_{2}(x)}\chi_{{}_{B_{\nu}^{c}}}(\Phi_{r_{0}}\eta_{0})^{q^{\prime}}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\Big\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|h\|_{q}\|\Phi_{r_{0}}\eta_{0}\chi_{{}_{B_{\nu}^{c}}}\|_{q^{\prime}},

where
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}
if
q>1
, if not,
q^{\prime}=\infty
.

Since

\|\Phi_{r_{0}}\eta_{0}\chi_{{}_{B_{\nu}^{c}}}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\Big(\int_{B_{2}\setminus B_{\nu}}|x|^{(2s-N)q^{\prime}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}=c_{39}\nu^{2s-\frac{N}{q}},

letting
\nu=(\frac{t}{c_{39}\|h\|_{q}})^{\frac{1}{2s-\frac{N}{q}}}
, we have that

\|G_{2}\ast h\|_{\infty}\leq t,

that is,

\Big|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|G_{2}\ast h(x)|>t\}\Big|=0.

Then

\Big|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|(\Phi_{r_{0}}\eta_{0})\ast h|>2t\}\Big|\leq\frac{c_{40}\|h\|^{q}_{q}\,\nu^{2sq}}{t^{q}}\leq\frac{c_{41}{\|h\|_{q}^{r^{*}}}}{t^{r^{*}}}.

The case
(ii)
and
(iii)
with
r>s
follows by Marcinkiewicz Interpolation Theorem.

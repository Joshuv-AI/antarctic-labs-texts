Euler scheme for SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motions: Malliavin differentiability and uniform upper-bound estimates
Jorge A. León, Yanghui Liu, Samy Tindel

Abstract
. The Malliavin differentiability of a SDE plays a crucial role in the study of density smoothness and ergodicity among others. For Gaussian driven SDEs the differentiability issue is solved essentially in [ 3 ] . In this paper, we consider the Malliavin differentiability for the Euler scheme of such SDEs. We will focus on SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motions (fBm), which is a very natural class of Gaussian processes. We derive a uniform (in step size n n ) path-wise upper-bound estimate for the Euler scheme for stochastic differential equations driven by fBm with Hurst parameter H > 1 / 3 H>1/3 and its Malliavin derivatives.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we are interested in the following stochastic differential equation driven by a
d
-dimensional fractional Brownian motion (fBm in the sequel)
x
with Hurst parameter
\frac{1}{3}<H<\frac{1}{2}
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle dy_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{0}(y_{t})dt+{V}(y_{t})dx_{t}\,,\quad t\in[0,T],

\displaystyle y_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Throughout the paper we assume that the collection of vector fields
V_{0}=(V_{0}^{i},{1\leq i\leq m})\in C^{3}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
{V}=(V^{i}_{j},{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq d})
all sit in the class
C^{3}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m},{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{m}))
.
The existence and uniqueness of path-wise solution of equation (
1.1
)
is guaranteed by the theory of rough paths; see e.g.
[
9
]
. In addition, the unique solution
y
in the sense of
[
9
]
has
{\gamma}
-Hölder continuity for all
{\gamma}<H
.

The aim of this paper is to consider the numerical approximation of equation (
1.1
).
It is well-known (see the introduction in
[
6
]
for more details about this issue) that the classical Euler scheme is divergent under this setting. The simplest possible solution to this problem is to use a second-order Euler (that is a Milstein type) scheme, which however involves iterated integrals of the fBm
x
and is not implementable directly.
Several contributions are made to tackle the implementation issue
[
6
,
8
,
13
,
19
]
; see also
[
14
,
15
]
.

In this paper we will focus our attention on the (implementable) Euler scheme introduced in
[
13
,
19
]
.
Take the uniform partition
\pi:0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=T
on
[0,T]
, where for
k=0,\ldots,n
we have
t_{k}=k\Delta
with
\Delta=\frac{T}{n}
. The Euler scheme
is recursively defined as follows:

(1.2)

y^{n}_{t_{k+1}}=y^{n}_{t_{k}}+V_{0}(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\Delta+V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\delta x_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial V_{j}V_{j}(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\,\Delta^{2H}

and

y^{n}_{0}=y_{0}
,
where we have used the notation

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial V_{i}V_{j}=\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\partial_{\ell}V_{i}^{k}V_{j}^{\ell};\,k=1,\dots,m\right)

and
\partial_{i}
stands for the partial derivative in the
y^{(i)}
direction:
\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial{y^{(i)}}}
.
The exact rate of convergence of
y^{n}
to
y
is shown to be of order
1/n^{2H-1/2}
in
[
19
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in proving that the approximation
y^{n}
defined by (
1.2
) is Malliavin differentiable under sufficient smoothness assumption on the coefficients. More importantly, we will establish pathwise upper bounds estimates of the Malliavin derivative which will be uniform in
n
. Our motivation for this endeavor is twofold:

(i)

The integrability of Malliavin derivatives for rough differential equations has been an important open problem a decade ago. This is mostly due to the prominent role played by Malliavin calculus techniques in obtaining results about the density of random variables like
y_{t}
in (
1.1
). The integrability issue for the Malliavin derivatives
Dy_{t}
has been solved
completely in
[
3
]
. Subsequent applications to the smoothness of densities of
y_{t}
are contained in
[
1
,
2
,
10
]
. The corresponding question for numerical approximations of
y
is thus in order. We propose to start a detailed answer to this natural problem in the current paper.

(ii)

Upper bounds on Malliavin derivatives open the way to important results for numerical schemes. Among others, one can quote weak convergence as well as convergence of densities. In our companion paper
[
18
]
we prove the weak convergence of
y^{n}
defined by (
1.2
) towards the solution to (
1.1
). The uniform bounds on Malliavin derivatives obtained in the current contribution are a crucial ingredient in
[
18
]
.

With those motivations in mind, our main result can be informally spelled out as follows. Please refer to Theorem
4.10
, Remark
4.11
and Theorem
4.13
for a more precise statement.

Theorem 1.1
.

{thm:main-intro}

Let
y
and
y^{n}
be the solution of (
1.1
) and the corresponding Euler scheme
(
1.2
)
, respectively. Take an integer
L\geq 1
. Let
\bar{D}^{L}y^{n}_{t}
be the
L
th Malliavin derivative of
y^{n}_{t}
in the Cameron-Martin space
\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}
corresponding to the fBm
x
.
Suppose that
V\in C^{L+2}_{b}
.
Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is a functional
{\mathcal{G}}_{L}^{n}
of the fBm
x
which is almost surely finite and such that the following pathwise bound holds true:

(1.4)

\|\bar{D}^{L}y^{n}_{t}\|_{\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\otimes L}}\leq{\mathcal{G}}_{L}^{n},\quad\text{for all }t=t_{k}\text{ and }k=1,\dots,n.

The explicit expression of
{\mathcal{G}}_{L}
is given in Theorem
4.10
. Furthermore, we have the uniform integrability of
{\mathcal{G}}^{n}_{L}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}[|{\mathcal{G}}^{n}_{L}|^{p}]<\infty.

As mentioned above, Theorem
1.1
is a crucial step in the analysis of weak convergence for the Euler type scheme (
1.2
). In addition, the proof of our main estimate (
1.4
) relies on techniques which are interesting in their own right. Specifically, we will first resort to rough paths type estimates (recalled in Section
2
), and our Malliavin calculus setting will follow Inahama’s approach
[
16
]
for all computations in the Cameron-Martin space. On top of those classical ingredients, our main technical tool will be a representation of higher order Malliavin derivatives of
y^{n}
in terms of a tree expansion (see Lemma
3.8
and Lemma
3.16
below). This kind of expression has to be contrasted with the standard form of higher order Malliavin derivatives, based on sums over partitions of the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
(see
[
21
, Proposition 5]
). We note that the advantage of
our directed-tree notations is that it allows us to distinguish all terms in the chain differentiations. We will benefit from this feature while proving an identities (
3.3
)-(
3.26
) in Lemma
3.16
. We also believe that the tree-based computations presented here can be usefully applied to other numerical schemes. We plan on developping this line of research in subsequent publications.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall some basic material on rough paths and Malliavin calculus. We also review results on the Euler scheme which will be used throughout the paper.
In Section
3
we derive a representation of Malliavin derivatives of the Euler scheme via tree notations. Finally, in Section
4
we prove the uniform upper-bound estimate for the Euler scheme and its Malliavin derivatives.

.

{general-notation}

In what follows, we take
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Delta=T/n
, and consider the uniform partition:

2.
Preliminary results

{section.pre}

In this section we recall some basic notions of rough paths theory and their application to fractional Brownian motion, which allows a proper definition of equation (
1.1
). We also give the necessary elements of Malliavin calculus in order to estimate densities of random variables.

2.1.
Elements of rough paths and fractional Brownian motion

{section2}

This subsection is devoted to introduce some basic concepts of rough paths theory. We are going to restrict our analysis to a generic Hölder regularity of the driving path of order
\frac{1}{3}<{\gamma}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, in order to keep expansions to a reasonable size. We also fix a finite time horizon
T>0
. The following notation will prevail until the end of the paper: for a Banach space
{\mathcal{V}}
(which can be either finite or infinite dimensional) and two functions
f\in C([0,T],{\mathcal{V}})
and
g\in C({\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,T]),{\mathcal{V}})
we set

(2.1)

\displaystyle\delta f_{st}=f_{t}-f_{s},\quad\text{and}\quad\delta g_{sut}=g_{st}-g_{su}-g_{ut},\qquad 0\leq s\leq u\leq t\leq T.

Let us introduce the analytic requirements in terms of Hölder regularity which will be used in the sequel. Namely consider two paths
x\in C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
x^{2}\in C({\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,T]),(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes 2})
. Then we denote

(2.2)

\|x\|_{[s,t],{\gamma}}:=\sup_{(u,v)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([s,t]):u\neq v}\frac{|\delta x_{uv}|}{|v-u|^{{\gamma}}},\qquad\|x^{2}\|_{[s,t],2{\gamma}}:=\sup_{(u,v)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([s,t]):u\neq v}\frac{|x^{2}_{uv}|}{|v-u|^{2{\gamma}}}.

When the semi-norms in (
2.2
) are finite we say that
x
and
x^{2}
are respectively in
C^{{\gamma}}([s,t],\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
C^{2{\gamma}}({\mathcal{S}}_{2}([s,t]),(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes 2})
.
For convenience, we denote
\|x\|_{{\gamma}}:=\|x\|_{[0,T],{\gamma}}
and
\|x^{2}\|_{2{\gamma}}:=\|x^{2}\|_{[0,T],2{\gamma}}
.
With this preliminary notation in hand, we can now turn to the definition of rough path.

Definition 2.1
.

{def:rough-path}

Let
x\in C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
x^{2}\in C({\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,T]),(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes 2})
, and
\frac{1}{3}<{\gamma}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. For
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}([0,T])
we denote
x^{1}_{st}=\delta x_{st}
.
We call
{\bf x}:=S_{2}(x):=(x^{1},x^{2})
a (second-order)
{\gamma}
-rough path if
\|x^{1}\|_{{\gamma}}<\infty
and
\|x^{2}\|_{2{\gamma}}<\infty
, and if the following algebraic relation holds true:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\delta x^{2}_{sut}=x^{2}_{st}-x^{2}_{su}-x^{2}_{ut}=x_{su}^{1}\otimes x_{ut}^{1}\qquad s\leq u\leq t,

where we have invoked (
2.1
) for the definition of
\delta x^{2}
. For a
{\gamma}
-rough path
S_{2}(x)
, we define a
{\gamma}
-Hölder semi-norm as follows:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|S_{2}(x)\|_{{\gamma}}:=\|x^{1}\|_{{\gamma}}+\|x^{2}\|_{2{\gamma}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

An important subclass of rough paths are the so-called
geometric
{\gamma}
-Hölder rough paths
. A geometric
{\gamma}
-Hölder rough path is a
{\gamma}
-rough path
(x,x^{2})
such that there exists a sequence of smooth
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued paths
(x^{n},x^{2,n})
verifying:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|x-x^{n}\|_{{\gamma}}+\|x^{2}-x^{2,n}\|_{2{\gamma}}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

We will mainly consider geometric rough paths in the remainder of the article.

Let
x
be a rough path as given in Definition
2.1
. We shall interpret equation (
1.1
) in a way introduced by Davie in
[
5
]
, which is conveniently compatible with numerical approximations.

Definition 2.2
.

{def:diff-eq-davie}

Let
(x,x^{2})
be a
{\gamma}
-rough path with
{\gamma}>1/3
. We say that
y
is a solution of
(
1.1
)
on
[0,T]
if
y_{0}=a
and there exists a constant
K>0
and
\mu>1
such that

(2.6)

\Big|\delta y_{st}-\int_{s}^{t}V_{0}(y_{u})\,du-V(y_{s})x^{1}_{st}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial V_{i}V_{j}(y_{s})x^{2,ij}_{st}\Big|\leq K|t-s|^{\mu}

for all
(s,t)\in\mathcal{S}_{2}([0,T])
, where we recall that
\delta y
is defined by
(
2.1
)
and the notation
\partial V_{i}V_{j}
is introduced in (
1.3
).

According to
[
5
]
there exists a unique RDE solution to equation
(
1.1
)
, understood as in Definition
2.2
.

In the following we recall a sewing map lemma with respect to discrete control functions. It is an elaboration of
[
19
, Lemma 2.5]
and proves to be useful in the analysis of the numerical scheme. Let
\pi:0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n-1}<t_{n}=T
be a generic partition of the interval
[0,T]
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We denote by
\llbracket s,t\rrbracket
the discrete interval
\{t_{k}:s\leq t_{k}\leq t\}
for
0\leq s<t\leq T
. In this paper, a two variable function
\omega:{\mathcal{S}}_{2}(\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket)\to[0,\infty)
is called a control on
\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket
if it satisfies the super-additivity condition. That is,
\omega(s,u)+\omega(u,t)\leq\omega(s,t)
for
s,u,t\in\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket
such that
s\leq u\leq t
.

Lemma 2.3
.

{lem2.4}

Suppose that
\omega
is a control on
\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket
. Consider a Banach space
{\mathcal{B}}
and an increment
R:{\mathcal{S}}_{2}(\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket)\to{\mathcal{B}}
.
Suppose that
|R_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}|\leq\omega(t_{k},t_{k+1})^{\mu}
for all
t_{k}\in\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket
and that
|\delta R_{sut}|\leq\omega(s,t)^{\mu}
with an exponent
\mu>1
, where recall that
\delta R_{sut}=R_{st}-R_{su}-R_{ut}
. Then the following relation holds:

(2.7)

\displaystyle|R_{st}|\leq K_{\mu}\omega(s,t)^{\mu}\,,\quad\text{where}\quad K_{\mu}=2^{\mu}\,\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l^{-\mu}.

We now specialize our setting to a path
x=(x^{1},\dots,x^{d})
defined as a standard
m
-dimensional fBm on
[0,T]
with Hurst parameter
H\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2})
.
This fBm is defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{P})
, and we assume that the
\sigma
-algebra
{\mathcal{F}}
is generated by
x
.
In this situation, recall that the covariance function of each coordinate of
x
is defined on
\mathcal{S}_{2}([0,T])
by:

(2.8)

\displaystyle R(s,t)=\frac{1}{2}\left[|s|^{2H}+|t|^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\right].

It is established in
[
9
, Chapter 15]
that the geometric rough path
S_{2}(x)
of
x
via the piecewise linear approximation is well defined for
\frac{1}{3}<{\gamma}<H
in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

2.2.
Malliavin calculus for
\mathbf{x}

{subsection.d}

In this subsection we recall some concepts of Malliavin calculus which will be used later in the paper. Recall that
R
is the covariance function of the fBm
x
defined in (
2.8
).
Denote by
\mathcal{E}
the set of step functions on the interval
[0,T]
. We define the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
as the closure of
\mathcal{E}

with respect to the scalar product

(2.9)

\langle\mathbf{1}_{[u,v]},\mathbf{1}_{[s,t]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=R([u,v],[s,t])\equiv R(v,t)-R(v,s)-R(u,t)+R(u,s).

The space
{\mathcal{H}}
is very useful in order to define Wiener integrals with respect to
x
. However, in the current paper we also need to introduce the Cameron-Martin space
\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}
related to our driving process. The latter space is the one allowing to identify pathwise derivatives with respect to
x
and Malliavin derivatives. In order to construct
\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}
,
let first
\mathcal{R}
be the linear operator on
{\mathcal{E}}
such that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]})=R(t,\cdot)\,,

and we also set
\bar{{\mathcal{E}}}=\mathcal{R}({\mathcal{E}})
. Then
we can define the Cameron-Martin space
\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}
as the closure of
\bar{{\mathcal{E}}}
with respect to the inner product

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}),\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{1}_{[0,s]})\rangle_{\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}}=\langle\mathbf{1}_{[0,s]},\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

It is clear that
\mathcal{R}
is an isometry between the two Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{H}}
and
\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}
. Note that according to (
2.10
) we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(h)(t)=\langle h,\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}

for
h\in{\mathcal{E}}
. By the isometry property of
\mathcal{R}
we see that (
2.11
) holds for all
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
. We refer to
[
11
,
21
]
for more details about the spaces
{\mathcal{H}},\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}
.

For the sake of conciseness, we refer to
[
20
]
for a proper definition of Malliavin derivatives in the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
and related Sobolev spaces in Gaussian analysis. Let us just mention that for a functional
F
of
x
we will denote its Malliavin derivative by
{D}F
, the Sobolev spaces by
\mathbb{D}^{k,p}
and the corresponding norms by
\|F\|_{k,p}
.

As mentioned above, in this paper we will mainly focus on a more pathwise Malliavin derivative taking values in
\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}
. Namely we define the Malliavin derivative in the Cameron-Martin
\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}
space via the isometry
\mathcal{R}
. Precisely, we define
\bar{D}
such that
\bar{D}F=\mathcal{R}(DF)
. In other words, for
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
and a functional
F
of
x
we have

\displaystyle\bar{D}_{\mathcal{R}(h)}F:=\langle\bar{D}F,\mathcal{R}(h)\rangle_{\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}}=\langle DF,h\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}=:D_{h}F.

This Malliavin derivative can be expressed easily for cylindrical functionals of
x
. Namely suppose that
F=f(x_{t_{1}},\dots,x_{t_{\ell}})
for
f\in C_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
. According to the definition of
\bar{D}
, for
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
we have

\displaystyle\langle\bar{D}F,\mathcal{R}(h)\rangle_{\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}}=\langle DF,h\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\partial_{i}f(x_{t_{1}},\dots,x_{t_{\ell}})\langle\mathbf{1}_{[0,t_{i}]},h\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}

(2.12)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\partial_{i}f(x_{t_{1}},\dots,x_{t_{\ell}})\mathcal{R}(h)(t_{i}).

Notice that the computation in (
2.2
) shows that
\langle\bar{D}F,\mathcal{R}(h)\rangle_{\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}}
can be interpreted as an extension in the Fréchet derivative of
F
of the
\mathcal{R}(h)
direction. Indeed, for the quantity in the right-hand side of (
2.2
) we have

(2.13)

\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}f(x_{t_{1}}+\varepsilon\mathcal{R}(h)(t_{1}),\dots,x_{t_{\ell}}+\varepsilon\mathcal{R}(h)(t_{\ell}))\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\partial_{i}f(x_{t_{1}},\dots,x_{t_{\ell}})\mathcal{R}(h)(t_{i}).

This pathwise interpretation of Malliavin derivatives is also the one adopted in
[
9
]
.

In this paper, we denote by
\bar{D}^{k}F
the
k
th iteration of the Malliavin derivative
\bar{D}
applied on
F
.
Also notice that we are considering a
d
-dimensional fBm
x=(x^{1},\dots,x^{d})
. Therefore, we shall consider partial Malliavin derivatives with respect to each coordinate
x^{i}
in the sequel.
Those partial derivatives will be denoed by
\bar{D}^{(i)}
. Then for
\bar{h}=(\bar{h}^{1},\dots,\bar{h}^{d})\in\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}^{d}
we write
\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}F=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\langle\bar{D}^{(i)}F,\bar{h}^{i}\rangle_{\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}}
.
For
L\geq 2
we denote by
\bar{D}^{L}_{\bar{h}}
the iterated versions of
\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}
. Namely we set

(2.14)

\displaystyle\bar{D}^{L}_{\bar{h}}F=\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}\circ\cdots\circ\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}F.

The Sobolev spaces related to the Malliavin derivatives in the Cameron Martin space are denoted by
\bar{\mathbb{D}}^{k,p}
and the corresponding norms are written
\|\cdot\|_{\bar{\mathbb{D}}^{k,p}}
.

Let us now review some results on the Malliavin differentiability of equation (
1.1
). In the following we assume that the vector fields
V_{0},\ldots,V_{d}
are at least in
C_{b}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
(bounded together with their derivatives up to order
3
), although later on we’ll have to introduce further smoothness conditions in order to estimate higher order Malliavin derivatives.
We shall express the first order Malliavin derivative of
y_{t}
in terms of the Jacobian
\Phi
of the equation, which is defined by the relation
\Phi_{t}^{ij}=\partial_{a_{j}}y_{t}^{(i)}
. Setting
\partial V_{j}
for the Jacobian of
V_{j}
seen as a function from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let us recall that
\Phi
is the unique solution to the linear equation

(2.15)

\Phi_{t}=\text{Id}_{n}+\int_{0}^{t}\partial V_{0}(y_{s})\,\Phi_{s}\,ds+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}\partial V_{j}(y_{s})\,\Phi_{s}\,dx^{j}_{s},

The following results hold true:

Proposition 2.4
.

{prop:deriv-sde}

Let
y
be the solution to equation (
1.1
). Then
for every
i=1,\ldots,m
,
t>0
, and
a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we have
y_{t}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{D}({\mathcal{H}})
and

{D}_{s}y_{t}=\left(\Phi_{s,t}V_{j}(y_{s}),j=1,\ldots,d\right),\quad 0\leq s\leq t,

where
\Phi_{t}=\partial_{a}y_{t}
solves equation (
2.15
) and
\Phi_{s,t}=\Phi_{t}\Phi_{s}^{-1}
.

Let us now quote the result
[
3
]
, which gives a useful estimate for moments of the Jacobian of rough differential equations driven by Gaussian processes. Note that this result is expressed in terms of
p
-variations, for which we refer to
[
9
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

{prop:moments-jacobian}

Consider a fractional Brownian motion
x
with Hurst parameter
H\in(1/4,1/2]
and
p>1/H
. Then for any
\eta\geq 1
, there exists a finite constant
c_{\eta}
such that the Jacobian
\Phi
defined by (
2.15
) satisfies:

(2.16)

\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Phi\|^{\eta}_{p-{\rm var};[0,1]}\right]=c_{\eta}.

3.
Malliavin derivatives of the Euler scheme

{section.der}

The estimates for the derivatives of the Euler scheme approximation
y^{n}
require a substantial amount of algebraic and analytic efforts. In this section we focus on the algebraic aspect of the problem. Precisely, we apply a tree argument to derive a representation for Malliavin derivatives of
y^{n}
.
This will be useful for our main bound of the derivatives of
y^{n}
in the next section (see Theorem
4.10
).

3.1.
A directed rooted tree

{section.tree}

The higher order Malliavin derivatives of the Euler scheme
y^{n}
are better understood thanks to a tree type encoding. We introduce the necessary notation in this section. Let us start with the definition of rooted trees which will be used in the sequel.

Definition 3.1
.

{def.ca}

In the remainder of the paper we consider rooted trees

{\mathcal{A}}_{N}
of height
N
defined recursively as follows:

(i)

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
contains one branch with length 1 and with root labeled
1
. Namely,
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}=\{(1)\}
.

(ii)

For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
we define
{\mathcal{A}}_{N+1}
such that its first
N
generations coincides with
{\mathcal{A}}_{N}
. Its
(N+1)
-th generation is defined as follows:
Take a branch
i
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{N}
. We call
\ell^{i}_{1}
the number of
1
’s in
i
and we also set
{\alpha}_{i}=\ell^{i}_{1}+1
. Then
{\mathcal{A}}_{N+1}
is constructed by adding the branches
(i,1)
, …,
(i,{\alpha}_{i})
to
{\mathcal{A}}_{N}
. Specifically, one can also define
{\mathcal{A}}_{N+1}
recursively as

\big\{(i,r):i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{N},r=1,\dots,{\alpha}_{i}\big\}.

Notation 3.3
.

{notation.i}

With a slight abuse of notation, we will write
{\mathcal{A}}_{N}
for both the tree
{\mathcal{A}}_{N}
and the collection of its branches.
For each branch
i
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{N}
we denote
|i|
the number of vertices in
i
and denote
i_{\tau}
the
\tau
th label in the branch
i
. We set

\displaystyle\ell_{1}^{i}=\#\{i_{\tau}=1:\tau=1,\dots,|i|\}\quad\text{and}\quad\ell_{r}^{i}=\#\{i_{\tau}=r:\tau=1,\dots,|i|\}+1\quad\text{for }r=2,\dots,|i|.

Also recall that we denote
{\alpha}_{i}=\ell_{1}^{i}+1
.

Remark 3.4
.

{remark.suml}

In the sequel we shall use the relation

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{r=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}\ell^{i}_{r}=N,

valid for every tree
{\mathcal{A}}_{N}
from Definition
3.1
. Let us give a brief proof of this fact. According to the definition of
{\mathcal{A}}_{N}
, for each branch
i
the vertices of
i
are labeled by the numbers
1,2,\ldots,{\alpha}_{i}
. No vertex of
i
is labeled a number
{\alpha}_{i}+1
or larger. Therefore, for all
r>{\alpha}_{i}
we have

\displaystyle\#\{i_{\tau}=r:\tau=1,\dots,|i|\}=0,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ell_{r}^{i}=1.

Moreover, by construction every branch
i
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{N}
has length
|i|=N
. Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{{\alpha}_{i}}\#\{i_{\tau}=r:\tau=1,\dots,|i|\}=|i|=N.

Because
{\alpha}_{i}=\ell^{i}_{1}+1=\#\{i_{\tau}=1:\tau=1,\dots,|i|\}+1
, the above becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{r=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}\left(\#\{i_{\tau}=r:\tau=1,\dots,|i|\}\right)+\left({\alpha}_{i}-1\right)=\sum_{r=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}\left(\#\{i_{\tau}=r:\tau=1,\dots,|i|\}+1\right)=N.

Otherwise stated, according to Notation
3.3
we obtain relation (
3.1
).

In order to state our differentiation rule for Malliavin derivatives, let us also label some notation about partial differentiation in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Notation 3.6
.

{notationA}

For a constant

A=(A^{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}};\,\,p_{1},\dots,p_{k}=1\dots,m)\in(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{\otimes k}=\mathbb{R}^{m^{k}},

and for
a^{(1)}
, …,
a^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

we set

\displaystyle\langle A,a^{(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes a^{(k)}\rangle

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}=1}^{m}a^{(k)}_{p_{k}}\cdots a^{(1)}_{p_{1}}A^{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}}.

Notation 3.7
.

{notation.df}

Let
f:y\to f(y)
be a continuous function from
\mathbb{R}^{m}
to
\mathbb{R}
. We denote by
\partial
the differential operator from
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
to
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R})={\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
. That is
for
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we define
\langle\partial f,a\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial y^{(i)}}
.
Note that the space
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
can also be identified with
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Namely, we can write

\displaystyle\partial f:=(\partial_{1}f,\dots,\partial_{m}f):=\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial y^{(1)}},\dots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial y^{(m)}}\right).

One can generalize this notation to higher order derivatives. Specifically,
we denote by
\partial^{k}
the differential operator from
C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
to
{\mathcal{L}}((\mathbb{R}^{m})^{\otimes k},\mathbb{R})
. Otherwise stated for
a^{(1)}
, …,
a^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
we define a vector

\partial^{k}f(y)=\left\{\frac{\partial^{k}f(y)}{\partial y^{(p_{k})}\cdots\partial y^{(p_{1})}};\,p_{1},\dots,p_{k}=1\dots,m\right\}\,,

so that one can write

(3.2)

\displaystyle\langle\partial^{k}f(y),a^{(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes a^{(k)}\rangle=\sum_{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}=1}^{m}a^{(k)}_{p_{k}}\cdots a^{(1)}_{p_{1}}\frac{\partial^{k}f(y)}{\partial y^{(p_{k})}\cdots\partial y^{(p_{1})}}.

Notice that for
k=2
,
\partial^{2}f
can also be identified with the matrix
(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial y^{(i)}\partial y^{(j)}})_{i,j=1,\dots,m}
.

3.2.
A differentiation rule

With the preparation in the previous subsection,
we are now ready to state a lemma allowing to compute iterated Malliavin derivatives for a functional of the form
f(F)
, with a smooth enough function
f
and random variable
F
.
Recall that the Malliavin derivative operator
\bar{D}
in the Cameron-Martin space
\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}
is introduced in Section
2.2
.

{section.drule}

Lemma 3.8
.

{lem.dfg}

Let
f,g
be continuous functions in
C^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
and let
F\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}^{N,2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, where the space
\bar{\mathbb{D}}^{N,2}
is introduced in Section
2.2
. Then we have the following identity:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{N}f(F)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{N}}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}^{i}}f(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}^{i}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{i}}F\rangle

for
\bar{h}\in\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}^{m}
,
where the sum in the right side of (
3.3
) runs over the branches of
{\mathcal{A}}_{N}
as specified in Notation
3.3
-
3.7
. As far as the product
f(F)g(F)
is concerned, we get the following differentiation rule:

(3.4)

\displaystyle\bar{D}^{N}_{\bar{h}}(f\cdot g)(F)=

\displaystyle M_{1}(N)+M_{2}(N),

where

\displaystyle M_{1}(N)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{N}}\left\langle(\partial^{\ell_{1}^{i}}f\cdot g)(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}^{i}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{i}}F\right\rangle

\displaystyle M_{2}(N)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{N}}\sum_{r=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}\left\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}^{i}-1}f(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}^{i}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{r}^{i}}g(F)\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{i}}F\right\rangle.

Remark 3.9
.

Let us check the dimension compatibilities in the right side of (
3.3
). Since
F
is
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued, according to Notation
3.7
we have
\partial^{\ell^{i}_{1}}f(F)\in{\mathcal{L}}((\mathbb{R}^{m})^{\otimes\ell^{i}_{1}};\mathbb{R})
. Next each term
\bar{D}^{\ell^{i}_{r}}_{\bar{h}}F
sits in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Therefore,
\bar{D}^{\ell^{i}_{2}}_{\bar{h}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}^{\ell^{i}_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{\bar{h}}F\in{\mathcal{L}}((\mathbb{R}^{m})^{\otimes({\alpha}_{i}-1)})
. The compatibility of dimensions in (
3.3
) thus stems from the relation
{\alpha}_{i}=\ell^{i}_{1}+1
(see Notation
3.3
). Similar considerations are also valid for equation (
3.4
), taking into account the fact that
\bar{D}^{\ell^{i}_{r}}_{\bar{h}}F\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\bar{D}^{\ell^{i}_{r}}_{\bar{h}}g(F)\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 3.10
.

In order to get a formula for
\bar{D}^{N}_{\bar{h}}f(F)
, we could have invoked some multivariate elaboration of Faà Di Bruno’s formula; see
[
4
,
17
]
. However, our tree type formulation is required in order to handle the computations in Lemma
3.16
below.

Remark 3.11
.

Lemma
3.8
can easily be generalized in three directions:

(i)
We have stated (
3.3
) using the directional derivative
\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}
. The same formula holds true for the function-valued derivative
D_{r}f(F)
.

(ii)
Instead of the Malliavin derivative, we could have obtained (
3.3
) as a chain rule for any operator
A
satisfying a Leibniz type rule of the form
A(f(F))=f^{\prime}(F)AF
.

(iii)
Instead of considering iterations of the same operation
A
, that is a formula for
A^{N}(f(F))
, one can obtain a formula like (
3.3
) for quantities of the form
A_{1}\circ\cdots\circ A_{N}(f(F))
, where each of the operators
A_{j}
satisfies
A_{j}(f(F))=f^{\prime}(F)A_{j}F
.

Proof of Lemma
3.8
.

We first show by induction that
(
3.3
)
is true. To this aim, note that
\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}f(F)=\langle\partial f(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}F\rangle
. On the other hand, by Definition
3.1
we have
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}=\{(1)\}
and according to Notation
3.3
we have
\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}=1
and
{\alpha}_{(1)}=2
. This concludes
(
3.3
)
for
N=1
.

Now suppose that
(
3.3
)
is true for
N=L
. This means that
\bar{D}^{L}_{\bar{h}}f(F)
is equal to the summation of quantities of the form
\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}f(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}F\rangle
which are one-to-one corresponding to the branches in
{\mathcal{A}}_{L}
.
Consider a generic term in this summation and differentiate it in a direction
h\in\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}
. Dropping the superscript
i
in
\ell^{i}_{r}
for notational sake, we get

\displaystyle\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}\left(\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}f(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}F\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}+1}f(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}F\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}F\rangle

(3.5)

\displaystyle\qquad+\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}f(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}+1}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}F\rangle+\cdots+\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}f(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}+1}F\rangle.

One can relate relation (
3.2
) to our tree Definition
3.1
in the following way.
Namely in the recursive step (ii) in Definition
3.1
,
from
{\mathcal{A}}_{L}

we have
created a new tree by adding labeled offsprings to the branch
i
. Specifically we add the branches
(i,1)
,
(i,2)
, …,
(i,{\alpha}_{i})
, and we set
{\alpha}_{(i,1)}={\alpha}_{i}+1
and
{\alpha}_{(i,r)}={\alpha}_{i}
for
r=2,\dots,{\alpha}_{i}
. This shows that after differentiation, the term corresponding to the branch
i
is replaced by
{\alpha}_{i}
terms corresponding to the branches:
(i,1)
,
(i,2)
,…,
(i,{\alpha}_{i})
. These are exactly the branches in
{\mathcal{A}}_{L+1}
which overlap with
i
in the first
|i|
vertices. Here the sum (
3.2
) can also be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in K_{i}}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}^{j}}f(F),\bar{D}^{\ell_{2}^{j}}_{\bar{h}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{j}}^{j}}_{\bar{h}}F\rangle,

where the branches
j
sit in a set
K_{i}
defined by

\displaystyle K_{i}=\{(i,r):1\leq r\leq{\alpha}_{i}\}.

Summing all those contributions we get

(3.6)

\displaystyle\bar{D}^{L+1}_{\bar{h}}f(F)=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}\sum_{j\in K_{i}}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}^{j}}f(F),\bar{D}^{\ell_{2}^{j}}_{\bar{h}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{j}}^{j}}_{\bar{h}}F\rangle,

from which it is easily seen that (
3.3
) holds up to order
L+1
. This finishes our induction procedure for (
3.3
).

Let us now consider
(
3.4
)
.
First, it is easy to verify that
(
3.4
)
holds for
N=1
. Next suppose that
(
3.4
)
is true for
N=L
. Let us now differentiate the terms in
M_{1}(L)
on the right-hand side of
(
3.4
)
. Still writing
\ell_{r}
instead of
\ell_{r}^{i}
for notational sake, we get:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}\left(\langle(\partial^{\ell_{1}}f\cdot g)(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}F\rangle\right)=H_{1}^{i}+H_{2}^{i},

where
the term
H^{i}_{1}
takes care of the differentiation of
g(F)
in the left side of (
3.7
), while
H_{2}^{i}
corresponds to the differentiation of
\partial^{\ell_{1}}f(F)
and
\bar{D}^{\ell_{r}}_{\bar{h}}F
. Specifically, we get

\displaystyle H_{1}^{i}

\displaystyle=\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}f(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}F\rangle\cdot\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}g(F),

while
H^{i}_{2}
is obtained similarly to (
3.2
) as

\displaystyle H^{i}_{2}

\displaystyle=\langle(\partial^{\ell_{1}+1}f\cdot g)(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}F\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\langle(\partial^{\ell_{1}}f\cdot g)(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}+1}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}F\rangle+\cdots+\langle(\partial^{\ell_{1}}f\cdot g)(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}+1}F\rangle.

Now we follow the same argument as the one leading to (
3.6
) to get

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}H^{i}_{2}=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L+1}}\langle(\partial^{\ell_{1}}f\cdot g)(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}F\rangle=M_{1}(L+1).

In order to complete the induction proof it remains to show that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}H^{i}_{1}+\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}M_{2}(L)=M_{2}(L+1).

For this purpose we consider the map from
i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}
to
(i,1)
. It is clear that this is a one-to-one mapping. We conclude from this one-to-one correspondence that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}H^{i}_{1}=\sum_{j=(i,1):\,i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}^{j}-1}f(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}^{j}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{j}-1}^{j}}F\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{j}}^{j}}g(F)\rangle,

where we recall that according to Notation
3.3
we have
\ell_{{\alpha}_{j}}^{j}=1
for
j=(i,1)
.

We turn to the second summation
M_{2}(L)
in
(
3.4
)
.
Note that each term in (
3.4
) corresponds to a couple
(i;r)
where
i
is a branch in
{\mathcal{A}}_{L}
and
r\in\{2,\dots,{\alpha}_{i}\}
denotes the position for which a term of the form
\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell^{i}_{r}}g(F)
shows up.
By differentiating
M_{2}(L)
we see that the term corresponding to the couple
(i;r)
is replaced by the terms corresponding to
((i,1);r)
,
((i,2);r)
, …,
((i,{\alpha}_{i});r)
.
Precisely, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}M_{2}(L)=\sum_{(j;r)\in{\mathcal{B}}_{2,L}}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}-1}f(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{r}}g(F)\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{j}}}F\rangle,

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{2,L}=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}\bigcup_{r=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}\{((i,1);r),((i,2);r),\dots,((i,{\alpha}_{i});r)\}.

Observe that
in a similar way we can also write (
3.10
) as

(3.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}H^{i}_{1}=\sum_{(j;r)\in{\mathcal{B}}_{1,L}}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}^{j}-1}f(F),\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}^{j}}F\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{j}-1}^{j}}F\otimes\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{j}}^{j}}g(F)\rangle,

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{1,L}=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}\{((i,1);{\alpha}_{i}+1)\}=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}\{((i,1);{\alpha}_{(i,1)})\}.

On the other hand, note that
{\alpha}_{(i,2)}=\cdots={\alpha}_{(i,{\alpha}_{i})}={\alpha}_{i}
, and
{\alpha}_{(i,1)}={\alpha}_{i}+1
. Therefore, we can express the tree
{\mathcal{A}}_{L+1}
as follows:

(3.13)

\bigcup_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L+1}}\bigcup_{r=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}\{(i;r)\}={\mathcal{B}}_{2,L}\cup{\mathcal{B}}_{1,L}.

Observe that
{\mathcal{B}}_{1,L}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{2,L}
corresponds to the terms in
H_{2}^{i}
and
\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}M_{2}(L)
thanks to (
3.12
) and (
3.11
), while the set
\bigcup_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L+1}}\bigcup_{r=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}\{(i;r)\}
corresponds to the terms in
M_{2}(L+1)
. We conclude from identity (
3.13
) that relation (
3.9
) holds.
The proof is now complete.

3.3.
An expression for the Malliavin derivatives of the Euler scheme

{section.xi}

In this subsection we come back to the solution
y
of our rough differential equation (
1.1
). However, for notational sake, we shall omit from now the drift term
V_{0}
in (
1.1
). Therefore we are reduced to an equation of the form

(3.14)

\displaystyle y_{t}=a+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(y_{s})dx^{i}_{s}\,.

The corresponding Euler scheme

y^{n}
(given by (
1.2
)) can now be expressed as:

(3.15)

\displaystyle\delta y^{n}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}=

\displaystyle V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})x^{1}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial V_{j}V_{j}(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\Delta^{2H},

where we recall that
\Delta=T/n
and
t_{k}=k\Delta
, where the notation
x^{1}=\delta x
is introduced in Definition
2.1
, and where we have used the notation in (
1.3
) for the quantities
\partial V_{j}V_{j}(y)
. Notice that for
t\in\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket
, the approximation
y^{n}_{t}
can also be written as

(3.16)

\displaystyle y^{n}_{t}=

\displaystyle y_{0}+\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})x^{1}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial V_{j}V_{j}(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\Delta^{2H}.

The aim of this section is to find a proper expression for the Malliavin derivatives of
y^{n}
.

Remark 3.12
.

{remark.inamaha}

In order to show upper bounds on Malliavin derivatives
\|\bar{D}^{L}y^{n}_{t}\|_{\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\otimes L}}
of the Euler scheme we borrow Inamaha’s approach in
[
16
]
.
However, a very special attention to combinatoric issues will have to be paid, due to the fact that we are considering a discrete equation. We have prepared the ground for this in Section
3.1
and Section
3.2
. Furthermore, note that the uniform continuity in
n
of Lyons-Itô’s map fails in our discrete context. Hence the upper-bound estimates for equation (
3.15
) have to be treated differently for small and large
step sizes of the Euler scheme; see Section
4.2
.

One of the basic ideas in
[
16
]
is to use an independent copy
b
of the fBm
x
in order to obtain norms in the Cameron-Martin space
\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}
. With this consideration in mind, we now define a family of processes which will be at the heart of our computations of Malliavin derivatives.
We start by introducing a family of operators which will be useful for our future definitions.

Notation 3.13
.

{notation.l}

Let
y^{n}
be the numerical scheme defined in (
3.15
). Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function.
For each
l=1,\dots,L
, we let
\xi^{l}_{t}
,
t\in\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket
be a process with values in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We denote the process

\xi_{t}=(\xi^{1}_{t},\dots,\xi^{L}_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{Lm}
,
t\in\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket
.

Recall that the trees
{\mathcal{A}}_{L}
are introduced in Definition
3.1
.
For each
i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}
we denote by
c_{L,i}
and
\tilde{c}_{L,i}
some constant depending on
L
and

.
Also recall our Notation
3.6
-
3.7
. For
\ell_{1}=\ell_{1}^{i}
corresponding to a branch

, we have to consider

{\mathcal{L}}((\mathbb{R}^{m})^{\otimes\ell_{1}};\mathbb{R})

.
With these elementary algebra considerations in mind,
we define the following notation

(3.17)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{L}_{\xi,c}f(y^{n}_{t_{k}})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}c_{L,i}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}f(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\xi^{\ell_{2}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{t_{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}c_{L,i}\sum_{p_{1},\dots,p_{\ell_{1}}=1}^{m}\xi_{t_{k}}^{\ell_{2},p_{1}}\cdots\xi_{t_{k}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}},p_{\ell_{1}}}\frac{\partial^{\ell_{1}}f(y^{n}_{t_{k}})}{\partial y^{(p_{\ell_{1}})}\cdots\partial y^{(p_{1})}}.

Moreover, for smooth functions

and

we set

\displaystyle\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\xi,c}^{L}(\partial f\cdot g)(y^{n}_{t_{k}}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}c_{L,i}\langle(\partial^{\ell_{1}+1}f\cdot g)(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\xi_{t_{k}}^{\ell_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{t_{k}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}\rangle

(3.18)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}\sum_{r=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}c_{L,i}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}f(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\xi_{t_{k}}^{\ell_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\mathcal{L}}^{\ell_{r}}_{\xi,c}g(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{t_{k}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}\rangle,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}^{L}_{\xi,c,\tilde{c}}(\partial f\cdot g)(y^{n}_{t_{k}})=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}\sum_{r=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}c_{L,i}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}f(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\xi^{\ell_{2}}_{{}_{t_{k}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\mathcal{L}}^{\ell_{r}-1}_{\xi,\tilde{c}}g(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{{}_{t_{k}}}\rangle,

where

\partial^{\ell_{1}+1}f\cdot g(y)=\left(\sum_{p_{\ell_{1}+1}=1}^{m}\frac{\partial^{\ell_{1}+1}f(y)}{\partial y^{(p_{\ell_{1}+1})}\cdots\partial y^{(p_{1})}}g^{p_{\ell_{1}+1}}(y),\quad p_{1},\dots,p_{\ell_{1}}=1,\dots,m\right)

and

\displaystyle\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}+1}f\cdot g(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\xi^{\ell_{2}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{t_{k}}\rangle=\sum_{p_{1},\dots,p_{\ell_{1}+1}=1}^{m}\xi_{t_{k}}^{\ell_{2},p_{1}}\cdots\xi_{t_{k}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}},p_{\ell_{1}}}\frac{\partial^{\ell_{1}+1}f(y^{n}_{t_{k}})}{\partial y^{(p_{\ell_{1}+1})}\cdots\partial y^{(p_{1})}}g^{p_{\ell_{1}+1}}(y^{n}_{t_{k}}).

We also set
{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi,c}^{0}=\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\xi,c}^{0}=\text{Id}
and
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\xi,c,\tilde{c}}^{0}=\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\xi,c,\tilde{c}}^{-1}={\mathcal{L}}_{\xi,c}^{-1}=\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\xi,c}^{-1}=0

In order to be able to differentiate our processes of interest in the Malliavin calculus sense, let us label the following regularity assumption on the vector fields

.

Hypothesis 3.14
.

{hyp:regularity-V}

The vector fields

are

(bounded together with all their derivatives up to order

We can now define a family of paths which will encode the expressions for the Malliavin derivatives of the Euler scheme.

Definition 3.15
.

{def.xi}

For
n\geq 1
we consider the Euler scheme
y^{n}
given by (
3.15
), and recall that
b
designates a fBm independent of
x
. Let
{\mathcal{L}}^{L}
,
\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}^{L}
,
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}^{L}
be the operators introduced in Notation
3.13
. Then for
L\geq 0
we define a discrete process
\Xi^{L}

defined for

and taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
given similarly to (
3.16
) by the iterative equation

(3.20)

\delta\Xi^{L}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}={\mathcal{L}}_{\Xi,c}^{L}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\delta x_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}+{\mathcal{L}}^{L-1}_{\Xi,\tilde{c}}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\delta b_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\\
+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\Xi,c}^{L}\left(\partial V_{j}\cdot V_{j}\right)(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\Delta^{2H}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\Xi,c,\tilde{c}}^{L-1}\left(\partial V_{j}\cdot V_{j}\right)(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\Delta^{2H},

or in the integral form

\Xi^{L}_{t}=\Xi^{L}_{t_{0}}+\sum_{t_{0}\leq t_{k}<t}{\mathcal{L}}_{\Xi,c}^{L}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\delta x_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}+\sum_{t_{0}\leq t_{k}<t}{\mathcal{L}}^{L-1}_{\Xi,\tilde{c}}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\delta b_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\\
+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t_{0}\leq t_{k}<t}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\Xi,c}^{L}\left(\partial V_{j}\cdot V_{j}\right)(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\Delta^{2H}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t_{0}\leq t_{k}<t}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\Xi,c,\tilde{c}}^{L-1}\left(\partial V_{j}\cdot V_{j}\right)(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\Delta^{2H},

where
t_{0}\in\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket
is the initial time of the iteration equation and
c=(c_{L,i},i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L})
and
c=(\tilde{c}_{L,i},i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L})
are some constants.

Note that we apply
{\mathcal{L}}^{L}_{\Xi,c}
to every component of

(i.e.
f=V^{i}_{j}
for each
i
and

) in order to get a
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued element

in the right-hand side of (

). Precisely, we have
{\mathcal{L}}^{L}_{\Xi,c}V(y)=({\mathcal{L}}^{L}_{\Xi,c}V^{i}_{j},i=1\dots,m,j=1,\dots,d)
.
\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}^{L}_{\Xi,c}(\partial V_{j}\cdot V_{j})(y)
and

should be interpreted in the same way.

We now state our general expression for the Malliavin derivatives of the Euler scheme. In the following, for conciseness we drop the subscript
(\Xi,c)
of

and simply write

. This simplification is also applied to
\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}^{L}_{\Xi,c}

and
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}^{L}_{\Xi,c,\tilde{c}}
.

Lemma 3.16
.

{lem.dhd}

For
n\geq 1
let
y^{n}
be the Euler scheme defined by (
3.16
). Assume that Hypothesis
3.14
holds for
L\geq 1
. Recall that a notation
\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}
has been introduced in Section
2.2
for the Malliavin derivative with respect to the fBm
x
. We also write
\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}
for the directional derivative with respect to the independent fBm
b
. Let
\Xi^{L}
be the process introduced in Definition
3.15
with
c_{L,i}=\tilde{c}_{L,i}=\frac{\ell_{2}!\cdots\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}!}{L!}
for all
i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}
.
Then
for all
t\in\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket

the iterated derivative (
2.14
) of
y^{n}_{t}
can be expressed as

(3.21)

\displaystyle\bar{D}^{L}_{\bar{h}}y^{n}_{t}=\hat{{D}}^{L}_{\bar{h}}\Xi^{L}_{t}.

Proof.

According to Definition
3.15
and recalling our convention
{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}=\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}^{-1}=0
, it is straightforward to see that
\Xi^{0}=y^{n}
.

For
L\geq 1
, consider the process
\Xi^{L}
defined by (
3.20
). Our next endeavor is to find a difference equation satisfied by
\hat{{D}}^{L}\Xi^{L}
. To this aim we differentiate the terms in the right-hand side of (
3.20
). Note that
y^{n}
does not depend on
b
and therefore
\hat{{D}}\left[\partial^{\ell_{1}}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\right]=0
.
We now prove that
\Xi^{\ell}
belongs to the
\ell
-th Wiener chaos of
b
, which will be denoted by
\mathcal{K}^{b}_{\ell}
(similarly to that of
x
in Section
2.2
). This can be done recursively on
L
using relation (
3.20
). Namely we assume that
\Xi^{p}_{t_{k}}\in\mathcal{K}^{b}_{p}
for all
p\leq L-1
. It can be checked that the terms on the right-hand side of (
3.20
) belongs to
{\mathcal{K}}^{b}_{L}
. For sake of conciseness, let us focus on the following term of (
3.20
) (the other terms being left to the patient reader):

(3.22)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{L-1}_{\Xi,\tilde{c}}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\delta b_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L-1}}\tilde{c}_{L-1,i}\left\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\,\Xi^{\ell_{2}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{t_{k}}\right\rangle\delta b_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}.

By the induction assumption the generic term on the right-hand side of (
3.22
) is in the chaos

(3.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{b}_{\sum_{p=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}\ell_{p}+1}=\mathcal{K}^{b}_{(L-1)+1}=\mathcal{K}^{b}_{L},

where we have invoked Remark
3.4
for the second equation. Relation (
3.23
) thus proves that
\Xi^{\ell}_{t_{k}}\in\mathcal{K}^{b}_{\ell}
by induction. In particular,
\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell^{\prime}}\Xi^{\ell}=0
if
\ell^{\prime}>\ell
.

In order to differentiate the right-hand side of (
3.20
), we need to differentiate generic terms of the form
\Xi^{\ell_{2}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{t_{k}}
for
i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}
. It is easily seen that

\displaystyle\hat{{D}}^{L}_{\bar{h}}\left(\Xi^{\ell_{2}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{t_{k}}\right)=\sum_{(p_{2},\dots,p_{{\alpha}_{i}}):p_{2}+\dots+p_{{\alpha}_{i}}=L}\frac{L!}{p_{2}!\cdots p_{{\alpha}_{i}}!}\hat{D}^{p_{2}}_{\bar{h}}\Xi^{\ell_{2}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{D}^{p_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{\bar{h}}\Xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{t_{k}}.

However, if
(p_{2},\dots,p_{{\alpha}_{i}})\neq(\ell^{i}_{2},\dots,\ell^{i}_{{\alpha}_{i}})
, at least one of the
\ell^{i}_{j}
will be larger than
p_{j}
, yielding a null contribution. Therefore the only surviving term in the sum above is

(3.24)

\displaystyle\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{L}[\Xi^{\ell_{2}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{t_{k}}]=\frac{L!}{\ell_{2}!\cdots\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}!}\cdot\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}\Xi^{\ell_{2}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}\Xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{t_{k}}.

The reader is referred to
[
16
]
for more details about the above computation.
Note that the number of ways to assign the
L
operators
\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}
,…,
\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}
into groups of sizes
\ell_{2}
,…,
\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}
is
\frac{L!}{\ell_{2}!\cdots\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}!}
, which explains the multiplicative constant in the equation.

With (
3.24
) in hand, we are now ready to differentiate the right-hand side of (
3.20
). For the first term, using definition (
3.17
) of
{\mathcal{L}}^{L}

we get

\displaystyle\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{L}{\mathcal{L}}^{L}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}\Xi^{\ell_{2}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}\Xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{t_{k}}\rangle.

Along the same lines and resorting to (
3.18
) for the definition of
\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}^{L}
, it is easily checked that

\displaystyle\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{L}\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}^{L}(\partial V\cdot V)(y^{n}_{t_{k}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}\langle(\partial^{\ell_{1}+1}V\cdot V)(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}\Xi^{\ell_{2}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}\Xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{t_{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}\sum_{r=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}\Xi_{t_{k}}^{\ell_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{r}}{\mathcal{L}}^{\ell_{r}}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}\Xi_{t_{k}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}\rangle.

Similarly, for the second term on the right-hand side of (
3.20
), we end up with

\displaystyle\hat{{D}}^{L}_{\bar{h}}{\mathcal{L}}^{L-1}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\delta b_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}=L\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L-1}}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\hat{{D}}^{\ell_{2}}_{\bar{h}}\Xi^{\ell_{2}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{{D}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{\bar{h}}\Xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\delta\bar{h}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\rangle.

Note also that the fourth term on the right-hand side of
(
3.20
)
is in the
(L-2)
th chaos of
b
and thus has zero
\hat{{D}}^{L}
derivative.
Differentiating both sides of (
3.20
) and taking into account the above computations, we have thus obtained

\displaystyle\hat{{D}}^{L}_{\bar{h}}\Xi^{L}_{t}=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}\Xi^{\ell_{2}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}\Xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{t_{k}}\rangle\delta x_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}

(3.25)

\displaystyle+L\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L-1}}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\hat{{D}}^{\ell_{2}}_{\bar{h}}\Xi^{\ell_{2}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{{D}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{\bar{h}}\Xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\delta\bar{h}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}\Big(\langle(\partial^{\ell_{1}+1}V_{j}\cdot V_{j})(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}\Xi^{\ell_{2}}_{t_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}\Xi^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}_{t_{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{r=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}\langle\partial^{\ell_{1}}V_{j}(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{2}}\Xi_{t_{k}}^{\ell_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{r}}{\mathcal{L}}^{\ell_{r}}V_{j}(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{{D}}_{\bar{h}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}\Xi_{t_{k}}^{\ell_{{\alpha}_{i}}}\rangle\Big)\Delta^{2H}.

Let us now differentiate
y^{n}_{t}
according to its definition (
3.16
). To this aim resorting to the fact that
\delta x_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}
is in the first chaos of
x
, we get

(3.26)

\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{L}y^{n}_{t}=\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}\bar{D}_{\bar{h}}^{L}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\delta x_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}+L\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}\bar{D}^{L-1}_{\bar{h}}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\delta\bar{h}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\\
+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{0\leq t_{k}<t}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bar{D}^{L}_{\bar{h}}\left[\left(\partial V_{j}\cdot V_{j}\right)(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\right]\Delta^{2H}.

Next we differentiate the terms
V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})
and
(\partial V_{j}\cdot V_{j})(y^{n}_{t_{k}})
thanks to
Lemma
3.8
. It is readily checked that we get exactly the same expression as (

). This shows our claim (
3.21
) and finishes our proof.

4.1.
Some auxiliary results

This subsection is dedicated to some necessary auxiliary results.
Throughout the subsection we fix an integer

Recall that
\Xi^{L}
is defined by the iterative equation in Definition
3.15
.
We start by introducing a process related to
\Xi^{L}

Definition 4.1
.

{def.p}

For each

we define

to be the maximum among the quantities of the form

with

such that

and

. We also define

and

.

The following result follows immediately from the definition of

:

Lemma 4.2
.

{lem.pn2}

The following three inequalities hold for
s\in\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket
:

\displaystyle P^{L}\geq P^{L^{\prime}},\quad\quad P^{L}_{s}\times P^{L^{\prime}}_{s}\leq P^{L+L^{\prime}}_{s}\quad\text{ for }L\geq L^{\prime}\geq 0,\quad\text{and}\quad|\Xi^{L}_{s}|\leq P^{L}_{s}\quad\text{ for }L\geq 1.

Let us now fix some constants which we will make extensive use of:
For
f\in C^{N+2}_{b}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
we set

(4.1)

\displaystyle C^{0}_{f}=\sup_{\tau\leq N+2}\|\partial_{\tau}f\|_{\infty},

where
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
denotes the sup norm for continuous functions.
Also recall that for
i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}
the constants
{\alpha}_{i}
and
c_{L,i}
are defined respectively in Notation
3.3
and Lemma
3.16
, and
C^{0}_{V}
is defined in
(
4.1
)
.
Then for
L=1,2,\dots,N
we define

(4.2)

\displaystyle C^{1}_{L,V}=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}c_{L,i}C^{0}_{V},\quad C^{1}_{0,V}=C^{0}_{V},\quad C^{1}_{-1,V}=0,

(4.3)

\displaystyle C^{2}_{L,V}=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}c_{L,i}C^{0}_{V}\left(C^{0}_{V}+\sum_{r=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}C^{1}_{\ell_{r},V}\right),\quad C^{2}_{0,V}=2(C^{0}_{V})^{2},\quad C^{2}_{-1,V}=0,

(4.4)

\displaystyle C^{3}_{L,V}=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}\sum_{r=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}c_{L,i}C^{0}_{V}C^{1}_{\ell_{r}-1,V},\quad C^{3}_{0,V}=0,\quad C^{3}_{-1,V}=0.

We will also resort to the following constants:

(4.5)

\displaystyle K_{1}^{L}=C^{1}_{L,V}+C^{1}_{L-1,V}+1,\qquad K_{2}^{L}=C^{2}_{L,V}+C^{2}_{L-1,V}+C^{3}_{L,V}+C^{3}_{L-1,V}+1.

Next we introduce a family of sets in the following way, for
L\geq 1
:

\displaystyle S_{L}=\{(L^{\prime},\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{L^{\prime}}):L^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{+},\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{L^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{N}_{+},\,\ell_{1}+\dots+\ell_{L^{\prime}}=L\}.

Related to this definition, we define another family of constants:

(4.6)

\displaystyle C_{L}^{4}=\max_{(L^{\prime},\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{L^{\prime}})\in S_{L}}2^{L+1}\times K_{1}^{\ell_{1}}\times\cdots\times K_{1}^{\ell_{L^{\prime}}},

and

(4.7)

\displaystyle C_{f,L}^{5}=2\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{A}}_{L}}c_{L,i}C^{0}_{f}\left(K^{1}_{1}+\sum_{r=2}^{{\alpha}_{i}}K^{\ell_{r}}_{1}\right)(1+C_{L}^{4}),\qquad C_{f,0}^{5}=C^{0}_{f}K^{0}_{1}.

Remark 4.3
.

The constants introduced above will appear in our proof for the upper bound of
\Xi^{L}
. We will see that because our proof is an induction argument, it is important to keep track of these constants.

With this additional notation,
in the following we derive an upper-bound estimate for the product of
\Xi^{L}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

{lem.dXi}

Let
\omega
be a control function on
\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket
.
Recalling our definition (
2.1
) for the operator
\delta
,
let
(s,u)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}(\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket)
be such that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\omega(s,u)^{1/p}\leq 1/2,\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\delta\Xi^{L}_{su}|\leq K^{L}_{1}P^{L}_{s}\omega(s,u)^{1/p},\quad L=1,\dots,N.

Let
L_{0}\leq N
and
(N^{\prime},\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{N^{\prime}})\in S_{L_{0}}
. Then we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle|\delta\left(\Xi^{\ell_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Xi^{\ell_{N^{\prime}}}\right)_{su}|\leq C_{L_{0}}^{4}\cdot P^{L_{0}}_{s}\cdot\omega(s,u)^{1/p}.

Proof.

We first note that a straightforward computation shows that
\delta\left(\Xi^{\ell_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Xi^{\ell_{N^{\prime}}}\right)_{su}
is equal to the summation of products of the quantities of the forms
\Xi^{\ell_{r}}_{s}
and
\delta\Xi^{\ell_{r}}_{su}
with
r=1,\dots,N^{\prime}
.
Apply Lemma
4.2
to
\Xi^{\ell_{r}}
and condition
(
4.8
)
to
|\delta\Xi^{\ell_{r}}_{st}|
.
Also take into account that
\omega(s,u)^{1/p}\leq 1/2
, according to (
4.8

4.3.
Point-wise upper-bound estimate

{section.bound2}

Theorem
4.10
and Corollary
4.12
provide estimates in
p
-variation for the Malliavin derivatives of the Euler scheme
y^{n}
.
In this subsection we use similar arguments in order to derive a pointwise estimate for the Malliavin derivatives of the form
Dy_{t}
(recall that those
{\mathcal{H}}
-valued derivatives have been defined in Section
2.2
).
Notice that we have stated and proved the theorem below for the first two derivatives of
y^{n}
. However, the extension of this result to higher order derivatives is just a matter of cumbersome notation.

Theorem 4.13
.

{thm.xirr}

Let the notations in Theorem
4.10
prevail. Suppose that
V\in C_{b}^{4}
.
Take
r,r^{\prime}\geq 0
and let
k_{0},k_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
r\in(t_{k_{0}},t_{k_{0}+1}]
and
r^{\prime}\in(t_{k_{0}^{\prime}},t_{k_{0}^{\prime}+1}]
.
We define a Malliavin derivative vector
\xi^{n}
as

(4.53)

\xi^{n}_{t}=(D_{r}y^{n}_{t},D_{r}D_{r^{\prime}}y^{n}_{t}):=(\xi_{t}^{n,1},\xi_{t}^{n,2})

Then
\xi^{n,L}
,
L=1,2
satisfies the iterative equation (
3.20
) with
c_{L,i}=1
and
\tilde{c}_{L,i}=0
. Furthermore, we have the estimate

(4.54)

\displaystyle\|\xi^{n,1}\|_{p\text{-var},\llbracket s,t\rrbracket}+\|\xi^{n,2}\|_{p\text{-var},\llbracket s,t\rrbracket}\leq K\cdot\omega(s,t)^{1/p}|S_{0}\cup S_{1}\cup S_{2}|\cdot({\mathcal{M}}_{0}\cdot{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\cdot{\mathcal{M}}_{2})^{L}\,,

for all
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket
,
where
S_{i}
,

{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
are respectively defined for
i=0,1,2
in (
4.34
)-(
4.35
) and (
(a)
). Moreover, for

(s,t)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}(\llbracket s_{j},s_{j+1}\rrbracket)
such that
s_{j}\in S_{0}\cup S_{1}
and
L=1,2
we have

(4.55)

\displaystyle|\delta\xi^{n,L}_{st}-{\mathcal{L}}^{L}V(y^{n}_{s})\delta x_{st}|\leq K\omega(s,t)^{2/p}\cdot P^{L}_{s}.

In both estimates
(
4.54
) and (
4.55
),
K
is a constant independent of
r
,
r^{\prime}
,
j
and
n
.

Proof.

Recall that
y^{n}
is defined in
(
3.16
)
. Let us first derive the iterative equation for the derivatives of
y^{n}
.
Recall that
r\in(t_{k_{0}},t_{k_{0}+1}]
.
We can divide the differentiation of
\delta y^{n}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}
in three cases.

(i)

If
k_{0}>k
, then
r>t_{k+1}
. Therefore, since
y_{t_{k}}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{k}}
we get

D_{r}[\delta y^{n}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}]=0
.

(ii)

If
k_{0}=k
,
then
t_{k}<r\leq t_{k+1}
. Moreover it is readily checked from equation (
2.2
) that
D_{r}[\delta x_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}]=\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}(r)
. Hence we have
\mathbf{1}_{(t_{k},t_{k+1}]}(r)=1
almost everywhere and differentiating
(
3.16
)
on both sides we obtain

\displaystyle D_{r}[\delta y^{n}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}]=\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}(r)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}(y^{n}_{t_{k_{0}}})\equiv a_{1}.

(iii)

If
k_{0}<k
, then we can differentiate both sides of
(
3.16
)
. We obtain the equation:

\displaystyle\delta D_{r}y^{n}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}=\langle\partial V(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),D_{r}y^{n}_{t_{k}}\rangle\delta x_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle\partial(\partial V_{j}V_{j})(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),D_{r}y^{n}_{t_{k}}\rangle\Delta^{2H}.

Gathering item (i), (ii) and (iii) above, and recalling that we have set
a_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}(y^{n}_{t_{k_{0}}})
, we get the following expression for
t\in\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket
,
r\leq t_{k}
and
\xi^{n,1}_{t}=D_{r}y^{n}_{t}
:

(4.56)

\displaystyle\delta\xi_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{n,1}=\langle\partial V(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\xi_{t_{k}}^{n,1}\rangle\delta x_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle\partial(\partial V_{j}V_{j})(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\xi_{t_{k}}^{n,1}\rangle\Delta^{2H}.

Also notice that we have obtained
\xi_{t}^{n,1}=0
if
r>t
. In particular, it is clear that
\xi^{n,1}
satisfies the iteration equation in Definition
3.15
with
c_{L,i}=1
and
\tilde{c}_{L,i}=0
, with
L=1
and initial time

. Precisely,
we have

(4.57)

\displaystyle\delta\xi^{n,1}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}={\mathcal{L}}^{1}_{\xi}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\delta x_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}^{1}_{\xi}(\partial V_{j}V_{j})(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\Delta^{2H}.

\displaystyle D_{r^{\prime}}\xi^{n,1}_{t}=\xi^{n,2}_{t},\qquad D_{r^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{1}_{\xi}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})={\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\xi}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}}),\qquad D_{r^{\prime}}\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}^{1}_{\xi}(\partial V_{j}V_{j})(y^{n}_{t_{k}})=\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}^{2}_{\xi}(\partial V_{j}V_{j})(y^{n}_{t_{k}}).

Let
r\in[t_{k_{0}},t_{k_{0}+1})

4.57

\displaystyle\xi^{n,2}_{t}=a_{2}+\sum_{r\vee r^{\prime}\leq t_{k}<t}{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\xi}V(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\delta x_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\vee r^{\prime}\leq t_{k}<t}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}^{2}_{\xi}(\partial V_{j}V_{j})(y^{n}_{t_{k}})\Delta^{2H},

where

A combination theorem for relatively hyperbolic groups and finite relative height of splitting
Ravi Tomar

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove a combination theorem for a relatively acylindrical graph of relatively hyperbolic groups (Theorem 1.1 ). Here, we are extending the technique of [ Tom21 ] and constructing Bowditch boundary of the fundamental group of graph of groups. Suppose G ⁡ ( 𝒴 ) G({\mathcal{Y}}) is a graph of relatively hyperbolic groups such that edge groups are relatively quasi-convex in adjacent vertex groups. Also, assume that the fundamental group of G ⁡ ( 𝒴 ) G({\mathcal{Y}}) is relatively hyperbolic. Then we show that the edge groups of G ⁡ ( 𝒴 ) G({\mathcal{Y}}) have finite relative height (Definition 1.5 ) if and only if they are relatively quasi-convex (Theorem 1.6 ). In the last section, we give an application.

1.
Introduction

In his seminal work
[
Gro85
]
, Gromov introduced the notion of hyperbolic and relatively hyperbolic groups. In
[
BF92
]
, Bestvina-Feighn proved a celebrated combination theorem for a graph of hyperbolic groups. Using the theorem of Bestvina and Feighn, Mj and Reeves
[
MR08
]
proved a combination theorem for a graph of relatively hyperbolic groups. In
[
Dah03
]
, with a completely different technique, Dahmani also proved a combination theorem for a graph of relatively hyperbolic groups. Dahmani used Yaman’s theorem
[
Yam04
]
to prove his theorem and gave an explicit construction of Bowditch boundary, see
[
Bow12
]
. In Dahmani’s theorem, one additionally requires that the action of the fundamental group of the graph of groups on its Bass-Serre tree is acylindrical and edge groups are fully quasi-convex in adjacent vertex groups. Our paper is also in the same spirit as
[
Dah03
]
and
[
Tom21
]
. Here, we are removing the hypothesis from the theorem of Dahmani that edge groups are fully quasi-convex. Also, we replace acylindrical action with a more general notion, namely relatively acylindrical action (Definition
2.5
). In section
4
, we prove the following combination theorem for relatively hyperbolic groups.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a finite graph of groups satisfying the following:

(1)

vertex groups are relatively hyperbolic, and edge groups are relatively quasi-convex in adjacent vertex groups,

(2)

the induced parabolic structures on each edge group from adjacent vertex groups are the same,

(3)

action of
G=\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
on Bass-Serre tree of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is relatively acylindrical,

(4)

For
n\geq k
,
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda(G_{e_{i}})=\emptyset
if
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}G_{e_{i}}
is finite and
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda(G_{e_{i}})
is a singleton if
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}G_{e_{i}}
is infinite, where
k
is some fixed natural number.

Then
G
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to subgroups corresponding to natural cone-locus. Moreover, the vertex groups are relatively quasi-convex in
G
.

In the above theorem, for an edge
e\in{\mathcal{Y}}
,
\Lambda(G_{e})
denotes the limit set (see Definition
2.4
) of edge group
G_{e}
in adjacent vertex groups. Also, the natural number
k
is the constant of relative acylindricity.
\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
denotes the fundamental group of the graph of groups
G({\mathcal{Y}})
.

In Theorem
1.1
, if we assume that edge groups are fully quasi-convex, then condition(4) is immediate as fully quasi-convex subgroups of a relatively hyperbolic group satisfy limit set intersection property (see
[
Dah03
, Proposition 1.10]
). Thus, as a corollary of Theorem
1.1
, we have the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a finite relatively acylindrical graph of relatively hyperbolic groups such that edge groups are fully quasi-convex in adjacent vertex groups. Also, assume that
G({\mathcal{Y}})
satisfies condition (2) of Theorem
1.1
. Then the fundamental group of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to subgroups corresponding to natural cone-locus.

Another special case of Theorem
1.1
is the following:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be an acylindrical finite graph of relatively hyperbolic groups with relatively quasi-convex edge groups. Assume that, for
n\geq k
, if
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}G_{e_{i}}
is finite then
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda(G_{e_{i}})=\emptyset
. Then the fundamental group
G
of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to the collection of images of parabolic subgroups of vertex groups in
G
.

In
[
Kap01
]
, I. Kapovich proved that the fundamental group of an acylindrical graph of hyperbolic groups with quasi-convex edge groups is hyperbolic, and all vertex groups are quasi-convex in the fundamental group of the graph of groups. An analogue of this result in relatively hyperbolic group setting was proved by Dahmani
[
Dah03
]
with the additional hypothesis that edge groups are fully quasi-convex. In this article, we are removing this additional hypothesis by introducing the notion of relatively acylindrical action. Thus, Theorem
1.1
is an analogous version of I.Kapovich’s result for relatively hyperbolic groups.

In
[
Tom21
]
, the author proved a combination theorem for a graph of relatively hyperbolic groups with parabolic edge groups and constructed the Bowditch boundary. There, the author generalizes the technique of
[
Dah03
]
to construct the Bowditch boundary. In this paper, we are considering the general case when edge groups are relatively quasi-convex. Here, we are extending the technique of
[
Tom21
]
to construct the Bowditch boundary of the fundamental group of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
(as in Theorem
1.1
). In
[
Tom21
, Theorem 1.6]
, edge groups are parabolic, so their limit sets are singletons. Thus, only one point in the edge boundary can have an infinite domain, see Definition
3.1
. However, in our situation, there can be more than one parabolic point in an edge boundary with infinite domains. Note that only parabolic points can have infinite domains by the third condition in Theorem
1.1
. In the construction of Bowditch boundary, we also require that the intersection of infinite domains is uniformly finite. It can be achieved by the last two conditions in Theorem
1.1
. To prove the above theorem, we introduced the notion of relatively acylindrical action, see Definition
2.5
, which generalizes the notion of acylindrical action.

Remark 1.4
.

In Theorem
1.1
, we are not assuming that the monomorphisms from edge groups to vertex groups are quasi-isometric embeddings. Thus, condition (1) of
[
MR08
, Theorem 4.5]
is not satisfied. Therefore, Theorem
1.1
does not follow from the combination theorem of Mj-Reeves
[
MR08
]
.

Let
(G,{\mathcal{P}})
be a relatively hyperbolic group. An element
f\in G
is
elliptic
if the order of
f
is finite. If
f
has infinite order, then
f
is
parabolic
if it is contained in a conjugate of some parabolic subgroup
P\in{\mathcal{P}}
and
loxodromic
otherwise. The notion of the height of a subgroup was introduced in
[
GMRS98
]
. There, they proved that quasi-convex subgroups of a hyperbolic group have finite height. This notion was generalized by Hruska and Wise
[
HW09
]
in the setting of relatively hyperbolic groups. In
[
HW09
]
, the authors proved that relatively quasi-convex subgroups of a relatively hyperbolic group have finite relative height. Now, we recall the definition of the relative height of a subgroup of a relatively hyperbolic group.

Definition 1.5

(Relative height
[
HW09
]
)
.

Let
(G,{\mathcal{P}})
be a relatively hyperbolic group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Relative height of
H
is
n
, if
n
is the maximum number of distinct cosets
g_{i}H
such that
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}Hg_{i}^{-1}
contains a loxodromic element.

It is well known that quasi-convex subgroups of a hyperbolic group have finite height
[
GMRS98
]
. The converse of this fact is an open question. This question is open even when the subgroup is malnormal (subgroup with height
1
). In
[
Mit04b
]
, Mitra proved that when a hyperbolic group
G
split as an amalgamated free product or HNN-extension of hyperbolic groups with quasi-convex edge groups, edge groups have finite height in
G
if and only if they are quasi-convex in
G
. Recently, Pal
[
Pal21
]
generalized the work of Mitra
[
Mit04a
]
in the setting of relatively hyperbolic groups. The following theorem generalizes the work of
[
Pal21
]
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a finite graph of groups satisfying the following:

(1)

vertex groups are relatively hyperbolic, and edge groups are relatively quasi-convex in adjacent vertex groups,

(2)

the induced parabolic structures on each edge group from adjacent vertex groups are the same,

(3)

G=\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to subgroups corresponding to natural cone-locus,

(4)

For
n\geq k
,
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda(G_{e_{i}})=\emptyset
if
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}G_{e_{i}}
is finite and
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda(G_{e_{i}})
is a singleton if
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}G_{e_{i}}
is infinite, where
k
is some fixed natural number.

Then, the edge groups have finite relative height in
G
if and only if they are relatively quasi-convex in
G
.

In Theorem
1.6
, if we assume that the edge groups are fully quasi-convex, then condition(4) is immediate. Thus, we immediately have the following:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a finite graph of relatively hyperbolic groups with fully quasi-convex edge groups. Also, assume that
G({\mathcal{Y}})
satisfies conditions (2) and (3) of Theorem
1.6
. Then edge groups have finite relative height in
G
if and only if they are relatively quasi-convex in
G
.

In
[
Pal21
]
, Pal assumed that the monomorphisms from edge groups to vertex groups are quasi-isometric embeddings. Also, he took a more restrictive parabolic structure (like in
[
Dah03
]
) on
\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
. In Theorem
1.6
, we are relaxing these assumptions and taking a more general parabolic structure (like in
[
MR08
]
) on
\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
.

The following corollary of Theorem
1.6
shows that, under a mild extra assumption, we can obtain
[
Pal21
, Theorem 1.2]
. Note that we are not assuming that the monomorphisms from edge groups to vertex groups are qi embeddings.

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a finite graph of relatively hyperbolic groups with relatively quasi-convex edge groups. Assume that the fundamental group
G
of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to the collection of images of parabolic subgroups of vertex groups in
G
. Also, assume that, for
n\geq k
, if
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}G_{e_{i}}
is finite then
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda(G_{e_{i}})=\emptyset
where
k
is a fixed natural number. Then, the edge groups have finite relative height in
G
if and only if they are relatively quasi-convex in
G
.

Preliminaries

We collect here some necessary definitions from
[
Bow12
]
,
[
Bow99
]
,
[
Dah03
]
.

A group
G
is said to be a
convergence group
if there exists a compact metrizable space
X
and
G
acts on
X
such that: given any sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
G
, there exists a subsequence
(g_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and two points
x
,
y
in
X
such that for all compact subset
K
of
X\setminus\{y\}
,
g_{n_{k}}K
converges uniformly to
x
.

Let
G
be a group acting as a convergence group on a compact metrizable space
X
. An infinite order element
g\in G
is said to be
loxodromic
if it has exactly two distinct fixed points in
X
. A point
x\in X
is said to be a
conical limit point
if there exists a sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
G
and two distinct points
z
,
y
such that
g_{n}x
converges to
z
and for all
x^{\prime}\in X\setminus\{x\}
,
g_{n}x^{\prime}
converges to
y
. A subgroup
P
of
G
is said to be
parabolic
if it is infinite, does not have any loxodromic element, and fixes a point
p
. Such a point is unique and called a
parabolic point
. The stabilizer of a unique parabolic point is called a
maximal parabolic subgroup
. A parabolic point
p
is called a bounded parabolic point if
Stab_{G}(p)
acts co-compactly on
X\setminus\{p\}
. Group
G
is said to be
geometrically finite
if every point of
X
is either a conical limit point or a bounded parabolic point.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
, and
G
acts as a convergence group on a compact space
X
. Then, every conical limit point for
H
action on
\Lambda(H)\subset X
(see Definition
2.4
(1) below) is a conical limit point for
H
acting in
X
and hence for
G
in
X
. Each parabolic point for
H
in
\Lambda(H)
is a parabolic point for
G
in
X
and its maximal parabolic subgroup in
H
is precisely the intersection of maximal parabolic subgroup in
G
with
H
.

Next, we define relatively hyperbolic groups introduced by Gromov
[
Gro85
]
. Several other definitions of relative hyperbolicity are known, see
[
Hru10
]
,
[
Osi06
]
,
[
Bow12
]
. In
[
Hru10
]
, Hruska shows that all these definitions are equivalent when group is countable, and the collection of peripheral subgroups is finite. Here, we use the dynamical definition of Bowditch
[

.

Definition 2.2

(Relatively hyperbolic group)
.

A group
G
is said to relatively hyperbolic with respect to a collection of subgroups
{\mathcal{P}}
if
G
admits a properly discontinuous action by isometries on a proper hyperbolic space
X
such that the induced action on
\partial X
(Gromov boundary of
X
) is convergence, every point of
\partial X
is either conical limit point or bounded parabolic point, and the subgroups in
\mathbb{H}
are precisely the maximal parabolic subgroups.

In short, we say that the pair
(G,{\mathcal{P}})
is a relatively hyperbolic group. In this case, the boundary of
X
is canonical and called the Bowditch boundary of
G
. We shall denote it by
\partial G
. Now, we have the following topological characterization of relatively hyperbolic groups given by Yaman
[
Yam04
]
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
Yam04
]
)
.

Let
G
be a geometrically finite group acting on a non-empty perfect metrizable compactum
X
. Assume that the quotient of bounded parabolic points is finite under the action of
G
, and the corresponding maximal parabolic subgroups are finitely generated. Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be the family of maximal parabolic subgroups. Then
(G,{\mathcal{P}})
is a relatively hyperbolic group and
X
is equivariantly homeomorphic to Bowditch boundary of
G
.

Definition 2.4
.

We collect some more definitions:

(1)

(Limit set
[
Dah03
]
)
Let
G
be a convergence group on
X
. The limit set
\Lambda(H)
of an infinite subgroup
H
is the unique minimal non-empty closed
H
-invariant subset of
X
. The limit set of a finite set is empty.

(2)

(Relatively quasi-convex subgroup
[
Dah03
]
)
Let
G
be a relatively hyperbolic group with Bowditch boundary
\partial G
. Let
H
be a group acting as a geometrically finite convergence group on a compact metrizable space
\partial H
. We assume that
H
is embedded in
G
as a subgroup.
H
is
relatively quasi-convex
if
\Lambda(H)\subset\partial G
is equivariantly homeomorphic to
\partial H
.

Let
(G,{\mathcal{P}})
be a relatively hyperbolic group and let
H
be a relatively quasi-convex subgroup of
G
. Then, by
[
Hru10
, Theorem 9.1]
, we have induced peripheral structure
{\mathcal{P}}_{H}
on
H
.

{\mathcal{P}}_{H}=\{H\cap gPg^{-1}:g\in G,P\in{\mathcal{P}}
,
and

H\cap gPg^{-1}

is infinite
}

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a graph of groups as in Theorem
1.1
. Since each edge group is relatively quasi-convex in adjacent vertex groups, we have induced parabolic structures on the edge group. We assume that, as a set, these induced parabolic structures on the edge groups are the same. Then, every parabolic subgroup in an edge group sits inside corresponding maximal parabolic subgroups in adjacent vertex groups. In this way, we obtain a subgraph of groups of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
where each vertex group is maximal parabolic in the corresponding vertex group of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
, and each edge group is maximal parabolic in the corresponding edge group of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
. Note that the fundamental groups of these subgraphs of groups are subgroups of the fundamental group of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
. We say that these subgroups correspond to the natural cone locus. The notion of cone locus comes from Mj-Reeves
[
MR08
]
, where one has a tree of relatively hyperbolic spaces with qi embedding condition such that the qi embeddings from edge spaces to vertex spaces are strictly type preserving. Then, we have cone locus corresponding to parabolics of vertex and edge spaces.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a graph of groups and let
T
be its Bass-Serre tree. The action of
G=\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
is said to be
acylindrical
if the stabilizer of a geodesic segment in
T
of length greater than
k
is finite for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. When
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is a graph of relatively hyperbolic groups with relatively quasi-convex edge groups (more generally for a graph of convergence groups with dynamically quasi-convex edge groups
[
Tom21
]
), we generalize acylindrical action to relative acylindrical action.

Definition 2.5

(Relatively acylindrical action)
.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a graph of relatively hyperbolic groups with relatively quasi-convex edge groups. The action of
G=\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
on Bass-Serre tree
T
of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is said to be
k
-relatively acylindrical if the stabilizer of any geodesic
\alpha
in
T
of length at least
k
is a parabolic subgroup in the stabilizer of each edge in
\alpha
. If there is no parabolic in edge groups, then we say that the action of
G
on
T
is
k
-relatively acylindrical if it is
k
-acylindircal. The action of
G
on
T
is relatively acylindrical if it is
k
-relatively acylindrical for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Example 2.6
.

Let
G=G_{1}\ast_{P}G_{2}
, where
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are relatively hyperbolic and
P
is a parabolic subgroup sitting properly in corresponding maximal parabolic subgroups of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. Then the action of
G
on its Bass-Serre tree is
2
-relatively acylindrical.

3.
Construction of Bowditch boundary

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be the graph of groups as in Theorem
1.1
, and let
G
be the fundamental group of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
. In this section, we construct a compact metrizable space
X
on which there is a natural action of
G
. In the next section, we show that this action is geometrically finite. Then, by invoking Theorem
2.3
,
G
is a relatively hyperbolic group.

We fix some notation: Let
T
be the Bass-Serre tree of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
. For a vertex
v
of
T
, we write the vertex stabilizer as
G_{v}
. Similarly, for an edge
e
of
T
, we write the edge stabilizer as
G_{e}
. We write
X_{v}
and
X_{e}
as compact metrizable space on which
G_{v}
and
G_{e}
act as convergence groups.

3.1.
Definition of
X
as a set

Contribution of the vertices of
T

Let
\tau
be a subtree of
T
such that
G.\tau=T
. Let
{\mathcal{V}}(\tau)
be the set of vertices of
\tau
. For each vertex
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\tau)
, the group
G_{v}
acts geometrically finitely on compact metrizable space
X_{v}
(Bowditch boundary of
G_{v}
). Set
\Omega
to be
G\times(\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\tau)}X_{v})
divided by the natural relation:

(g_{1},x_{1})=(g_{2},x_{2})\text{ if }\exists v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\tau),x_{i}\in X_{v},g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}\in G_{v},(g_{2}^{-1}g_{1})x_{1}=x_{2}\text{ for }i=1,2

In this way,
\Omega
is the disjoint union of compactums corresponding to the stabilizers of the vertices of
T
. Also, for each
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\tau)
, the space
X_{v}
naturally embeds in
\Omega
as the image of
\{1\}\times X_{v}
. We identify it with its image. The group
G
naturally acts on the left on
\Omega
. For
g\in G
,
gX_{v}
is the compactum for the vertex stabilizer
G_{gv}
.

Contribution of the edges of
T

Each edge allows us to glue together compactums corresponding to the vertex stabilizers along with the limit set of the stabilizer of the edge. Each edge group embeds as a relatively quasi-convex subgroup in adjacent vertex groups. Thus, for each edge
e\in T
, group
G_{e}
is relatively hyperbolic with Bowditch boundary
X_{e}
. Let
e
=
(v_{1},v_{2})
be the edge in
\tau
. Then, by our assumption, there exist equivariant embeddings
\Lambda_{e,v_{i}}:X_{e}\hookrightarrow X_{v_{i}}
for
i=1,2
. Similar maps are defined by translation for edges in
T\setminus\tau
.

The equivalence relation
\sim
on
\Omega
is the transitive closure of the following: Let
v
and
v^{\prime}
be vertices of
T
. The points
x\in X_{v}
and
x^{\prime}\in X_{v^{\prime}}
are equivalent in
\Omega
if there is an edge
e
between
v
and
v^{\prime}
and a point
x_{e}\in X_{e}
satisfying
x=\Lambda_{e,v}(x_{e})
and
x^{\prime}=\Lambda_{e,v^{\prime}}(x_{e})
simultaneously. Let
\Omega/_{\sim}
be the quotient under this relation and let
\pi^{\prime}:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega/_{\sim}
be the corresponding projection. An equivalence class
[x]
of an element
x\in\Omega
is denoted by
x
itself.

Let
\partial T
be the (visual) boundary of the tree. We define
X^{\prime}
as a set:
X^{\prime}=\partial T\sqcup(\Omega/_{\sim})
.

Definition 3.1
.

\bf{(Domains)}
For all
x\in\Omega/_{\sim}
, we define the domain of
x
to be
D(x)=\{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(T)|x\in\pi^{\prime}(X_{v})\}
. We also say that the domain of a point
\xi\in\partial T
is
\{\xi\}
itself.

3.2.
Final constrution of
X

It turns out that
X^{\prime}
with the topology defined in
[
Dah03
]
is not a Hausdorff space. Therefore we need to modify
X^{\prime}
further. For getting the desired space, we further define an equivalence relation on the set
X^{\prime}
. Note that points coming from edge boundaries can have infinite domains. Then, by our hypotheses, the points in
X^{\prime}
whose domains are infinite are parabolics. Also, if
x_{1},x_{2}\in X_{e}
are parabolics for some edge
e\in T
and
D(x_{1})
and
D(x_{2})
are infinite, then
D(x_{1})\cap D(x_{2})
is uniformly finite. So, we identify the boundary points of the infinite domain
D(x)
in
\partial T
to
x
itself. By considering the equivalence relation generated by these relations, we denote the quotient of
X^{\prime}
by
X
. Again,
X
can be written as a disjoint union of two sets of equivalence classes :

X=\Omega^{\prime}\sqcup(\partial T)^{\prime}

where
\Omega^{\prime}
(as a set, it is the same as
\Omega
) is the set of equivalence classes of elements in
\Omega
, and,
(\partial T)^{\prime}
are the equivalence classes of the remaining elements in
\partial T
as some elements of
\partial T
are identified with parabolic points of edge groups. The equivalence class of each remaining element in
\partial T
is a singleton.

Remark 3.2
.

The elements of
\Omega^{\prime}
will be denoted by
x,y,z...
and the elements of
(\partial T)^{\prime}
will be denoted by
\xi,\eta,\zeta....
. In
X
, we define the domain of each element as previously defined. Additionally, for those points
\eta
of
\partial T
, which are identified with parabolic points
x
of edge groups, we define the domain of
\eta
same as the domain of
x
.

It is clear that for each
v
in
T
, the restriction of projection map
\pi^{\prime}
from
X_{v}
to
\Omega
is injective. Let
\pi^{\prime\prime}
be the projection map from
X^{\prime}
to
X
. Let
\pi
be the composition of the restriction of
\pi^{\prime\prime}
to
\Omega
and
\pi^{\prime}
. Again the restriction of
\pi
to
X_{v}
is injective for all
v
in
T
.

3.3.
Definition of neighborhoods in
X

We define a family
(W_{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N},x\in X}
of subsets of
X
, which generates a topology on
X
. For a vertex
v
and an open subset
U
of
X_{v}
, we define the subtree
T_{v,U}
of

as
\{w\in{\mathcal{V}}(T):X_{e}\cap U\neq\emptyset\}
, where e is the first edge of

is maximal parabolic in edge group
G_{e}
. Then, the domain of
p
,
D(p)
is nothing but the Bass-Serre tree of the amalgam
Q=P_{1}\ast_{P}P_{2}
, which is the stabilizer of
p
in
G
. Under the action of
Q
, the quotient of
D(p)
is the edge
e
. Since
P_{1},P_{2}
acts co-compactly on
X_{v_{1}}\setminus\{p\},X_{v_{2}}\setminus\{p\}
respectively, there exists a compact subset
K_{i}
of
X_{v_{i}}\setminus\{p\}
such that
P_{i}K_{i}=X_{v_{i}}
for
i=1,2
. Consider
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
, the edges starting at
v_{i}
whose boundary intersects
K_{i}
but does not contain
p
. Let
e
be an edge with only one vertex in
D(p)
and
v_{i}
be this vertex. Then there exists
h\in P_{i}
such that
X_{e}\cap hK_{i}\neq\emptyset
for
i=1,2
. Therefore the set of edges
\cup_{i=1,2}Q{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
contains every edge with one and only one vertex in
D(p)
. Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{i}
be the set of vertices
w
such that the first edge of
[v_{i},w]
is in
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
. Let
K_{i}^{\prime}
be the subset of
X
consisting of the points whose domain is in
{\mathcal{V}}_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Since a sequence of points in the spaces corresponding to the stabilizers of distinct edges in
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
have all accumulation points in
K_{i}
, the set
K_{i}^{\prime\prime}=K_{i}\cup K_{i}^{\prime}
is a compact for
i=1,2
. Hence
K=\cup_{i=1,2}K_{i}^{\prime\prime}
is a compact set of

not containing

and

. Therefore
p
is a bounded parabolic point for

in

.

HNN extension:
Case(1) Let

where

is isomorphic to a subgroup of

. Let

and

are maximal parabolic subgroups in

, and

, and maximal parabolic subgroups corresponding to parabolic points which are not in any edge spaces are maximal parabolic for
G
in

.

Case(2) Let

). Then, we can write

. Thus, by applying the amalgam and case(1) of HNN extension case respectively, we are done.
∎

Hence, we have completed the proof of Theorem

. Also, from the construction of Bowditch boundary of

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

In this section, we give proof of Theorem
1.6
. First of all, we observe the following:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G
be the group satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
1.6
. Assume that all edge groups have finite relative height in
G
. Then, the action of
G
on the Bass-Serre tree of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is relatively acylindrical.

Proof.

Since
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is a finite graph of groups, the relative height of all edge groups is some fixed number
l
(say). Then, for sufficiently large
k
, the stabilizer of length
k
geodesic segment contains more than
l
conjugates of some fixed edge stabilizer. Hence, by definition of relative height, we see that the action of
G
on the Bass-Serre tree of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is relatively acylindrical.
∎

For the convenience of the reader, we recall Theorem
1.6
here.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a finite graph of groups satisfying the following:

(1)

vertex groups are relatively hyperbolic, and edge groups are relatively quasi-convex in adjacent vertex groups,

(2)

the induced parabolic structures on each edge group from adjacent vertex groups are the same,

(3)

G=\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to subgroups corresponding to natural cone-locus,

(4)

For
n\geq k
,
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda(G_{e_{i}})=\emptyset
if
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}G_{e_{i}}
is finite and
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda(G_{e_{i}})
is a singleton if
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}G_{e_{i}}
is infinite, where
k
is some fixed natural number.

Then, edge groups have finite relative height in
G
if and only if they are relatively quasi-convex in
G
.

Proof.

If edge groups are quasi-convex, then, by
[
HW09
]
, they have finite relative height.
Conversely, assume that edge groups have a finite relative height. Then, by Lemma
5.1
, we see that the action of
G
on the Bass-Serre tree of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is relatively acylindrical. In condition(4), we take
k
as relative acylindricity constant. Now,
G({\mathcal{Y}})
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
. Thus,
G
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to the same parabolic structure as in our assumption. Also, we have explicit construction of Bowditch boundary
X
of
G
. From the construction of
X
, we see that all vertex groups are relatively quasi-convex. Thus, all edge groups are relatively quasi-convex by
[
BW13
, Lemma 2.3]
. Hence the theorem.
∎

6.
Application

In
[
Swa00
]
, Swarup proved that if a hyperbolic group
G
splits as
A\ast_{C}B
or
A\ast_{C}
, where
A,B,C
are quasi-convex in
G
then a finitely generated subgroup
H
of
G
is quasi-convex if and only if the intersection of
H
with conjugates of
A,B,C
in
G
is quasi-convex in
G

[
Swa00
, Theorem 2]
. In
[
Pal21
, Theorem 5.4]
, Pal generalized the theorem of Swarup in the setting of relatively hyperbolic groups with some restrictions on parabolics. This section provides a proof same result in a more general setting.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a graph of groups satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
and let
H
be a finitely generated subgroup of
G
. Then,
H
is relatively quasi-convex in
G
if and only if the intersection of
H
with conjugates of vertex groups in
G
is relatively quasi-convex in vertex groups.

Proof.

If
H
is relatively quasi-convex in
G
, then the conclusion follows as vertex groups of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
are relatively quasi-convex in
G
(see
[
BW13
, Lemma 2.3]
). Conversely, assume that the intersection of
H
with conjugates of vertex groups in
G
is relatively quasi-convex in vertex groups. Since
H
is a finitely generated subgroup,
H
admits a finite subgraph of groups
G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})
structure whose vertex groups are the intersection of
H
with conjugates of vertex groups of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
and edges groups are the intersection of
H
with conjugates of edge groups of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
. Then, by our assumption,
G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})
is a graph of relatively hyperbolic groups with relatively quasi-convex edge groups. Note that the action of
H=\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1}))
on Bass-Serre tree
T_{1}
of
G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})
is relatively acylindrical. Thus,
G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})
satisfies all the conditions of Theorem
1.1
. Therefore,
H
is a relatively hyperbolic group with explicit construction of Bowditch boundary, say
X_{1}
. Also, from the construction of boundaries,
X_{1}
is a minimal closed
H
-invariant subset of
X
. Thus,
X_{1}
is the limit set of
H
in
G
. Since
H
acts geometrically finitely on
X_{1}
,
H
is a relatively quasi-convex subgroup of
G
.
∎

A relatively hyperbolic group is said to be locally quasi-convex if every finitely generated subgroup is relatively quasi-convex. Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a graph of groups as in Theorem
1.6
. Additionally, assume that each vertex group of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is locally quasi-convex. Let
H
be any finitely generated subgroup of
G
. Assume that the intersection of
H
with conjugates of edge groups is finitely generated in edge groups. Then, we have the following:

Proposition 6.2
.

G
is a locally quasi-convex relatively hyperbolic group.

Proof.

Let
H
be a finitely generated subgroup of
G
. Then,
H
admits a finite subgraph of groups
G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})
structure with finitely generated edge groups. Then, by
[
BW13
, Lemma 2.5]
, vertex groups are also finitely generated. As vertex groups of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
are locally quasi-convex, all vertex groups of
G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})
are relatively quasi-convex. Then, edge groups of
G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})
are also relatively quasi-convex in corresponding edge groups of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
. Applying Theorem
6.1
, we see that
H
is relatively quasi-convex in
G
.
∎

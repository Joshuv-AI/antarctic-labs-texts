1.
Introduction

Let
\varGamma^{(n)}=Sp_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
be the Siegel modular group of genus
n
, and
S_{k}(\varGamma^{(n)})
the space of cusp forms of weight
k
for
\varGamma^{(n)}
. Then we have the following Fourier expansion:

F(Z)=\sum_{B}c_{F}(B)\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}\mathrm{tr}(BZ)),

where
B
runs over all positive definite half-integral matrices of size
n
.
It is an interesting problem to estimate
c_{F}(B)
. By the standard method we obtain the following estimate, called the Hecke bound, of
c_{F}(B)
for
B\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})_{>0}
:

c_{F}(B)\ll_{F}(\det(B))^{k/2}.

However it is weak in general. In the case
n=1
, we have Deligne’s estimate (cf.
[
7
]
), which is best possible, and from now on we consider the case
n\geq 2
. Then there are several improvements of the Hecke bound (cf.
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
16
]
). Among others, Böcherer and Kohnen
[
1
]
proved that

(
*
)

\displaystyle c_{F}(B)\ll_{\varepsilon,F}\det(B)^{{k\over 2}-{1\over 2n}-(1-{1\over n})\alpha_{n}+\varepsilon}\ (\varepsilon>0)

if
k>n+1
. Here

\alpha_{n}=\Big(4(n-1)+4\Big[{n-1\over 2}\Big]+{2\over n+2}\Big)^{-1}.

In this paper, we improve this bound for the Duke-Imamoglu-Ikeda lift
I_{n}(h)
of a cuspidal Hecke eigenform
h
in
S_{k-n/2+1/2}^{+}(\varGamma_{0}(4))
to
S_{k}(\varGamma^{(n)})
. (As for a precise definition of the Duke-Imamoglu-Ikeda lift, see Section 3.)
That is, we prove the following estimate:

(Theorem
3.1
) We have

(**)

\displaystyle c_{I_{n}(h)}(B)\ll_{\varepsilon,I_{n}(h)}{\mathfrak{d}}_{B}^{-n/4+5/12}(\det(2B))^{(k-1)/2+\varepsilon}\ (\varepsilon>0)

for any
B\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})_{>0}
,
where
{\mathfrak{d}}_{B}
is the discriminant of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{(-1)^{n/2}\det B})/{\mathbb{Q}}
.

From the above result, we have

(***)

\displaystyle c_{I_{n}(h)}(B)|\ll_{\varepsilon,I_{n}(h)}(\det(2B))^{(k-1)/2+\varepsilon}\ (\varepsilon>0)

for any
B\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})_{>0}
.
We note that our estimate is slightly stronger than (*). We explain how to obtain the estimate (**). By definition,
c_{I_{n}(h)}(B)
is expressed in terms of the
|{\mathfrak{d}}_{B}|
-th Fourier coefficient
c_{h}(|{\mathfrak{d}}_{B}|)
of
h
, and
\prod_{p}\widetilde{F}_{p}(B,\alpha_{p})
, where for a prime number
p
,
\widetilde{F}_{p}(B,X)
is the polynomial in
X
and
X^{-1}
defined in
[
10
]
, and
\alpha_{p}
is a certain complex number such that
|\alpha_{p}|=1
(cf. Section 3). In view of Corollary
5.9
, Theorem
5.10
, we can estimate
\widetilde{F}_{p}(B,a)
for any
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
in purely combinatorial method (cf. Theorem
5.11
),
and therefore we obtain the following estimate (cf. Theorem
6.4
(1)):

\displaystyle|c_{I_{n}(h)}(B)|\leq|c_{h}(|{\mathfrak{d}}_{B}|)|{\mathfrak{f}}_{B}^{k-1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{p|{\mathfrak{f}}_{B}}(1+{\mathfrak{e}}_{i,B}^{(p)})),

where
{\mathfrak{f}}_{B}=\sqrt{\det(2B)/|{\mathfrak{d}}_{B}|}
, and
{\mathfrak{e}}_{i,B}^{(p)}
is that defined after Remark
6.2
.
On the other hand, by
[
5
]
, we obtain a reasonable estimate of
c_{h}(|{\mathfrak{d}}_{B}|)
. Combining these two estimates, we obtain the estimate (**). We also obtain another estimate for
c_{I_{n}(h)}(B)
(cf. Theorem
3.2
).

It is expected that we can obtain a similar estimate for the Fourier coefficient of the lift constructed in
[
11
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review the Siegel series. In Section 3, we state our main result. In Section 4, we review the Gross-Keating invariant. In Section 5, we give an estimate of
\widetilde{F}_{p}(B,\alpha_{p})
, and in Section 6, we prove our main result.

We thank Valentin Blomer for many valuable discussions, by which this paper is motivated. We also thank him for many useful comments, by which our main result has been improved greatly.

2.
Siegel series

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field of characteristic
0
, and
{\mathfrak{o}}={\mathfrak{o}}_{F}
its ring of integers.
The maximal ideal and the residue field of
{\mathfrak{o}}
is denoted by
{\mathfrak{p}}
and
{\mathfrak{k}}
, respectively.
We fix a prime element
\varpi
of
{\mathfrak{o}}
once and for all.
The cardinality of
{\mathfrak{k}}
is denoted by
q
.
Let
\mathrm{ord}=\mathrm{ord}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
denote additive valuation on
F
normalized so that
\mathrm{ord}(\varpi)=1
. If
a=0
, We write
\mathrm{ord}(0)=\infty

and we make the convention that
\mathrm{ord}(0)>\mathrm{ord}(b)
for any

.
We also denote by
|*|_{{\mathfrak{p}}}

F

|\varpi|_{{\mathfrak{p}}}=q^{-1}
.
We put
e_{0}=\mathrm{ord}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(2)
.

For a non-degenerate element

.
If
n
is even, we denote the discriminant ideal of

.
We also put

\xi_{B}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $D_{B}\in F^{\times 2}$,}\\
-1&\text{ if $F(\sqrt{D_{B}})/F$ is unramified quadratic,}\\
0&\text{ if $F(\sqrt{D_{B}})/F$ is ramified quadratic.}\end{cases}

Put

{\mathfrak{e}}_{B}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{ord}(D_{B})-\mathrm{ord}({\mathfrak{D}}_{B})&\text{ if $n$ is even}\\
\mathrm{ord}(D_{B})&\text{ if $n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

We make the convention that
\xi_{B}=1,{\mathfrak{e}}_{B}=0
if
B
is the empty matrix.
Once for all, we fix an additive character
\psi
of
F
of order zero, that is, a character such that

{\mathfrak{o}}=\{a\in F\ |\ \psi(ax)=1\ \text{ for any}\ x\in{\mathfrak{o}}\}.

For a half-integral matrix
B
of degree
n
over
{\mathfrak{o}}
define the local Siegel series
b_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(B,s)
by

b_{{{\mathfrak{p}}}}(B,s)=\sum_{R}\psi(\mathrm{tr}(BR))\mu(R)^{-s},

where

runs over a complete set of representatives of

and
\mu(R)=[R{\mathfrak{o}}^{n}+{\mathfrak{o}}^{n}:{\mathfrak{o}}^{n}]

The series

-plane.
Now for a non-degenerate half-integral matrix
B
of degree
n
over
{\mathfrak{o}}
define a polynomial

in

\gamma_{q}(B,X)=\begin{cases}(1-X)\prod_{i=1}^{n/2}(1-q^{2i}X^{2})(1-q^{n/2}\xi_{B}X)^{-1}&\text{ if $n$ is even}\\
(1-X)\prod_{i=1}^{(n-1)/2}(1-q^{2i}X^{2})&\text{ if $n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

Then it is shown by
[
19
]
that there exists a polynomial

in

F_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(B,q^{-s})={b_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(B,s)\over\gamma_{q}(B,q^{-s})}.

We define a symbol

so that

.

We note that

3.
The Duke-Imamoglu-Ikeda lift and main result

Put
J_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}O_{n}&-1_{n}\\
1_{n}&O_{n}\end{pmatrix}
.
Furthermore, put

\varGamma^{(n)}=Sp_{n}({{\mathbb{Z}}})=\{M\in GL_{2n}({{\mathbb{Z}}})\ |\ J_{n}[M]=J_{n}\}.

Let
{\mathbb{H}}_{n}
be Siegel’s
upper half-space of degree
n
. We define
j(\gamma,Z)=\det(CZ+D)
for
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}
and

. We note that
\varGamma^{(1)}=SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
. Let
l
be an integer or half-integer. For a congruence subgroup
\varGamma
of
\varGamma^{(n)}
, we denote by
M_{l}(\varGamma)
the space of Siegel modular forms of weight
l
with respect to
\varGamma
, and by

Let
T
be an element of
{{\mathcal{H}}_{n}}({\mathbb{Z}})_{>0}
with

{\mathfrak{d}}_{T}
be the discriminant of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{(-1)^{n/2}\det(T)})/{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then

with
{\mathfrak{f}}_{T}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
Now let

be a positive even integer, and
\varGamma_{0}(4)=\Bigl\{\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\

. Let

and

corresponding to

]
).
We define a Fourier series

where
c_{I_{n}(h)}(T)=c_{h}(|{\textfrak{d}}_{T}|){\textfrak{f}}_{T}^{k-(n+1)/2}\prod_{p}\widetilde{F}_{p}(T,\alpha_{f}(p)).

Then the first named author
[

showed that

is a Hecke eigenform in

We call

Let the notation be as above. Then we have

\displaystyle c_{I_{n}(h)}(B)\ll_{\varepsilon,I_{n}(h)}{\mathfrak{d}}_{B}^{-n/4+5/12}(\det(2B))^{(k-1)/2+\varepsilon}\ (\varepsilon>0)

for any
B\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})_{>0}
.
In particular, we have

\displaystyle c_{I_{n}(h)}(B)\ll_{\varepsilon,I_{n}(h)}(\det(2B))^{(k-1)/2+\varepsilon}\ (\varepsilon>0)

for any
B\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})_{>0}
.

We give another estimate of
c_{I_{n}(h)}(B)
in terms of minors of
B
.
We denote by
{\mathcal{I}}_{r}
the set of sequences
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r})
of integers such that
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n
.
Let
R
be an integral domain of characteristic different from

.
For an element

{\bf j}\end{pmatrix},

For

4.
The Gross-Keating invariant

We first recall the definition of the Gross-Keating invariant of a quadratic form over
{\mathfrak{o}}
following
[
9
]
.

For two matrices
B,B^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}})
, we sometimes write
B\sim B^{\prime}
if
B
and
B^{\prime}
are
GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}})
-equivalent.
The
GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}})
-equivalence class of
B
is denoted by
\{B\}
.
Let
B=(b_{ij})\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}})^{\rm nd}
.
Let
S(B)
be the set of all non-decreasing sequences
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}(b_{i})

\displaystyle\geq a_{i},

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}(2b_{ij})

\displaystyle\geq(a_{i}+a_{j})/2\qquad(1\leq i,j\leq n).

Set

S(\{B\})=\bigcup_{B^{\prime}\in\{B\}}S(B^{\prime})=\bigcup_{U\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}})}S(B[U]).

The Gross-Keating invariant (or the GK-invariant for short)
{\underline{a}}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})
of
B
is the greatest element of
S(\{B\})
with respect to the lexicographic order
\succ
on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
.
Here, the lexicographic order
\succ
is, as usual, defined as follows.
For
(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}),(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, let
j
be the largest integer such that
y_{i}=z_{i}
for
i<j
.
Then
(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})\succ(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})
if
y_{j}>z_{j}
.
The Gross-Keating invariant is denoted by
\mathrm{GK}(B)
.
A sequence of length
0
is denoted by
\emptyset
.
When
B
is a matrix of degree
0
, we understand
\mathrm{GK}(B)=\emptyset
.
By definition, the Gross-Keating invariant
\mathrm{GK}(B)
is determined only by the
GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}})
-equivalence class of
B
.
We say that
B\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}})
is an optimal form if
\mathrm{GK}(B)\in S(B)
.
Let
B\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}})
. Then
B
is
GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}})
-equivalent to an optimal form
B^{\prime}
.
Then we say that
B
has an optimal decomposition
B^{\prime}
.
We say that
B\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}})
is a diagonal Jordan form if
B
is expressed as

B=\varpi^{a_{1}}u_{1}\bot\cdots\bot\varpi^{a_{n}}u_{n}

with
a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n}
and
u_{1},\cdots,u_{n}\in{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}
.
Then, in the non-dyadic case, the diagonal Jordan form
B
above is optimal, and

\mathrm{GK}(B)=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.
Therefore, the diagonal Jordan decomposition is an optimal decomposition. However, in the dyadic case, not all half-integral symmetric matrices have a diagonal Jordan decomposition, and the Jordan decomposition is not necessarily an optimal decomposition.

B\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}})^{\rm nd}
, and let
\mathrm{GK}(B)=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.
For
1\leq i\leq n

Theorem 4.4

.

.

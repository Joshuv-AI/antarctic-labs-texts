The Wavefront Sets of Unipotent Supercuspidal Representations
Dan Ciubotaru, Lucas Mason-Brown, Emile Okada

Abstract
Let 𝐆 ⁡ ( 𝗄 ) \mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}) be a semisimple p p -adic group, inner to split. In this article, we compute the algebraic and canonical unramified wavefront sets of the irreducible supercuspidal representations of 𝐆 ⁡ ( 𝗄 ) \mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}) in Lusztig’s category of unipotent representations.



1
Introduction

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected semisimple algebraic group defined over a
p
-adic field
{\mathsf{k}}
with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})
be the group of
{\mathsf{k}}
-rational points. Let
X
be an irreducible admissible representation of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})
with distribution character
\Theta_{X}
. For each nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}
in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}({\mathsf{k}})
of
\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})
, let
\hat{\mu}_{\mathbb{O}}
denote the Fourier transform of the associated orbit integral. In
[
Har99
]
, Harish-Chandra proved that there are complex numbers
c_{\mathbb{O}}(X)\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\Theta_{X}(\mathrm{exp}(\xi))=\sum_{\mathbb{O}}c_{\mathbb{O}}(X)\hat{\mu}_{\mathbb{O}}(\xi)

(1.0.1)

for
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}({\mathsf{k}})
a regular element in a small neighborhood of
0
. The formula (
2.3.1
) is called the
local character expansion
of
X
.

One of the most fundamental invariants which can be extracted from the local character expansion is the so-called
wavefront set
. This is the set of nilpotent orbits

\mathrm{WF}(X)=\max\{\mathbb{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}({\mathsf{k}})\mid c_{\mathbb{O}}(X)\neq 0\}

It is common in the literature to consider a slightly coarser invariant called the
geometric wavefront set
. This is the set of nilpotent orbits over an
algebraic closure

\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}
of
{\mathsf{k}}
which meet some orbit in
\mathrm{WF}(X)
. This set is denoted by
{}^{\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X)
. A longstanding conjecture of Moeglin and Waldspurger
[
MW87
, Page 429]
asserts that
{}^{\bar{k}}\mathrm{WF}(X)
is a singleton, for all
X
.
Another closely related invariant is the
canonical unramified wavefront set
, which was recently introduced by the third-named author in
[
Oka21
, Section 2.2.3]
. This final invariant is a refinement of
{}^{\bar{k}}\mathrm{WF}(X)
.

Lusztig in
[
Lus95
, Section 0.3]
defined the notion of a
unipotent representation
of
\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})
(see Definition
2.6.1
below) and completed a Langlands classification for this class of representations, under the assumption that
{\mathbf{G}}
is simple and adjoint. These restrictions on
{\mathbf{G}}
have since been removed as a result of various works, culminating with
[
Sol18
]
.

Let
G^{\vee}
denote the complex Langlands dual group. The (enhanced) Langlands parameter of an irreducible unipotent representation
X
is the
G^{\vee}
-orbit of a triple
(\tau,n,\rho)
, where
\tau
is a semisimple element in
G^{\vee}
,
n
is a nilpotent element in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
(the Lie algebra of
G^{\vee}
) such that
\Ad(\tau)n=qn
, and
\rho
is an irreducible representation of a certain finite group
A^{1}_{\varphi}
, see section
2.7
. When
{\mathbf{G}}
is adjoint,
A^{1}_{\varphi}
is the component group of the centralizer of
\tau
and
n
in
G^{\vee}
. Let
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{X}
denote the nilpotent
G^{\vee}
-orbit of
n
.

It is natural to ask if and how the local character expansion is related to the Langlands parameter
(\tau,n,\rho)
of
X
. At one extreme, we have the coefficient
c_{0}(X)
. It has long been known that when
X
is tempered,
c_{0}(X)\neq 0
if and only if
X
is square integrable, and in this case,
c_{0}(X)
equals, up to a sign, the ratio between the formal degrees of
X
and the Steinberg representation. An interpretation of the formal degree in terms of the Langlands parameters was conjectured first by Reeder
[
Ree00
]
. This interpretation was verified in the case of split exceptional groups by Reeder (
[
Ree00
]
) and in the remaining cases by Opdam
[
Opd16
]
.

At the other extreme, we have the wavefront set of
X
.
In this article, we compute the geometric and canonical unramified wavefront sets of all
supercuspidal
unipotent representations of
\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})
when
{\mathbf{G}}
is an inner twist of a split group. In all such cases, we find that
{}^{\bar{k}}\mathrm{WF}(X)
is a singleton, and is uniquely determined by the nilpotent part
n
of the Langlands parameter. More precisely, we prove in Theorem
3.0.2

^{\bar{k}}\mathrm{WF}(X)=d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{X}),\quad^{K}\mathrm{WF}(X)=d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{X},1).

(1.0.2)

Here
d
and
d_{A}
are the duality maps defined by Spaltenstein, also Lusztig and Barbasch-Vogan, and Achar, respectively, see section
2.2
. In particular, the geometric wavefront set
{}^{\bar{k}}\mathrm{WF}(X)
determines the nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{X}
via the duality map
d
. We emphasize that the simplicity of the formulas (
1.0.2
) is due to the fact that
X
is supercuspidal and therefore equal to its Aubert-Zelevinsky dual (
[
Aub95
]
). In general, one expects that the wavefront set of
X
is obtained by duality from the nilpotent parameter associated to the
AZ dual
of
X
, see
[
CMO21
]
. This expression for the wavefront set is closely related to Lusztig’s formula for the Kawanaka wavefront set of an irreducible unipotent representation of a finite reductive group
[
Lus92
, Theorem 11.2]
. In fact, the finite reductive group results from
loc.cit.
play an important role in the construction and analysis of test functions in the local character expansion
[
BM97
,
Oka21
]
.

We remark that the methods in this paper show that the canonical unramified wavefront set (and so the geometric wavefront set as well) is in fact a singleton for all depth-zero supercuspidal representations, see Proposition
2.5.4
. The irreducibility of the geometric wavefront set for depth-zero supercuspidal representations was established independently in
[
AGS22
]
, but also as a consequence of the results in
[
BM97
,
Oka21
]
.

2
Preliminaries

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic
0
with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of sufficiently large characteristic, ring of integers
\mathfrak{o}\subset\mathsf{k}
, and valuation
\mathsf{val}_{\mathsf{k}}
. Fix an algebraic closure
\bar{\mathsf{k}}
of
\mathsf{k}
with Galois group
\Gamma
, and let
K\subset\bar{\mathsf{k}}
be the maximal unramified extension of
\mathsf{k}
in
\bar{\mathsf{k}}
.
Let
\mathfrak{O}
be the ring of integers of
K
.
Let
\mathrm{Frob}
be the geometric Frobenius element of
\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathsf{k})
, the topological generator which induces the inverse of the automorphism
x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}x^{q}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a connected semisimple algebraic group defined over
\mathbb{Z}
, and let
{\mathbf{T}}\subset\mathbf{G}
be a maximal torus. For any field
F
, we write
\mathbf{G}(F)
,
\mathbf{T}(F)
, etc. for the groups of
F
-rational points. The
\mathbb{C}
-points are denoted by
G
,
T
, etc. Let
{\mathbf{G}}_{\ad}={\mathbf{G}}/Z({\mathbf{G}})
denote the adjoint group of
{\mathbf{G}}
.

Write
X^{*}(\mathbf{T},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}})
(resp.
X_{*}(\mathbf{T},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}})
) for the lattice of algebraic characters (resp. co-characters) of
\mathbf{T}({\bar{\mathsf{k}}})
, and write
\Phi(\mathbf{T},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}})
(resp.
\Phi^{\vee}(\mathbf{T},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}})
) for the set of roots (resp. co-roots). Let

\mathcal{R}=(X^{*}(\mathbf{T},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}),\ \Phi(\mathbf{T},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}),X_{*}(\mathbf{T},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}),\ \Phi^{\vee}(\mathbf{T},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}),\ \langle\ ,\ \rangle)

be the root datum corresponding to
\mathbf{G}
, and let
W
the associated (finite) Weyl group. Let
\mathbf{G}^{\vee}
be the Langlands dual group of
{\mathbf{G}}
, i.e. the connected reductive algebraic group corresponding to the dual root datum

\mathcal{R}^{\vee}=(X_{*}(\mathbf{T},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}),\ \Phi^{\vee}(\mathbf{T},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}),X^{*}(\mathbf{T},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}),\ \Phi(\mathbf{T},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}),\ \langle\ ,\ \rangle).

Set
\Omega=X_{*}(\mathbf{T},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}})/\mathbb{Z}\Phi^{\vee}(\mathbf{T},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}})
. The center
Z({\mathbf{G}}^{\vee})
can be naturally identified with the irreducible characters
\mathsf{Irr}\Omega
, and dually,
\Omega\cong X^{*}(Z({\mathbf{G}}^{\vee}))
. For
\omega\in\Omega
, let
\zeta_{\omega}
denote the corresponding irreducible character of
Z({\mathbf{G}}^{\vee})
.

For details regarding the parametrization of inner twists of a group
{\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k})
, see
[
Vog93
, §2]
,
[
Kot84
]
,
[
Kal16
, §2]
, or
[
ABPS17
, §1.3]
and
[
FOS19
, §1]
. We only record here that the set of equivalence classes of inner twists of the split form of
\mathbf{G}
are parametrized by the Galois cohomology group

H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbf{G}_{\ad})\cong H^{1}(F,\mathbf{G}_{\ad}(K))\cong\Omega_{\ad}\cong\Irr Z({\mathbf{G}}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{sc}}),

where
{\mathbf{G}}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{sc}}
is the Langlands dual group of
{\mathbf{G}}_{\ad}
, i.e., the simply connected cover of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\vee}
, and
F
denotes the action of
\mathrm{Frob}
on
{\mathbf{G}}(K)
. We identify
\Omega_{\ad}
with the fundamental group of
{\mathbf{G}}_{\ad}
. The isomorphism above is determined as follows: for a cohomology class
h
in
H^{1}(F,\mathbf{G}_{\ad}(K))
, let
z
be a representative cocycle. Let
u\in{\mathbf{G}}_{\ad}(K)
be the image of
F
under
z
, and let
\omega
denote the image of
u
in
\Omega_{\ad}
. Set
F_{\omega}=\Ad(u)\circ F
. The corresponding rational structure of
{\mathbf{G}}
is given by
F_{\omega}
.
Let
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}
be the connected semisimple group defined over
{\mathsf{k}}
such that
{\mathbf{G}}(K)^{F_{\omega}}={\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}(\mathsf{k})
.
Note that
{\mathbf{G}}^{1}={\mathbf{G}}
(where we view
{\mathbf{G}}
as an algebraic group over
{\mathsf{k}}
for this equality).

If
H
is a complex reductive group and
x
is an element of
H
or
\mathfrak{h}
, we write
H(x)
for the centralizer of
x
in
H
, and
A_{H}(x)
for the group of connected components of
H(x)
. If
S
is a subset of
H
or
\mathfrak{h}
(or indeed, of
H\cup\mathfrak{h}
), we can similarly define
H(S)
and
A_{H}(S)
. We will sometimes write
A(x)
,
A(S)
when the group
H
is implicit.
The subgroups of
H
of the form
H(x)
where
x
is a semisimple element of
H
are called
pseudo-Levi
subgroups of
H
.

Let
\mathcal{C}({\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k}))
be the category of smooth complex
{\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k})
-representations and let
\Pi(\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k}))\subset\mathcal{C}({\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k}))
be the set of irreducible objects. Let
R({\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k}))
denote the Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{C}({\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k}))
.

2.1
The Bruhat-Tits Building

In this section we will recall some standard facts about the Bruhat-Tits building (all of which can be found in
[
Tit79
]
).

Fix a
\omega\in\Omega
and let
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}
be the inner twist of
{\mathbf{G}}
corresponding
\omega
as defined in the previous section.
Let
\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega},{\mathsf{k}})
denote the (enlarged) Bruhat-Tits building for
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})
.
Let
\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}},K)
denote the (enlarged) Bruhat-Tits for
{\mathbf{G}}(K)
.
For an apartment
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}},K)
and
\Omega\subseteq\mathcal{A}
we write
\mathcal{A}(\Omega,\mathcal{A})
for the smallest affine subspace of
\mathcal{A}
containing
\Omega
.
The inner twist
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}
of
{\mathbf{G}}
gives rise to an action of the Galois group
{\mathrm{Gal}}(K/k)
on
\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}},K)
and we can (and will) identify
\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega},{\mathsf{k}})
with the fixed points of this action.
We use the notation
c\subseteq\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega},{\mathsf{k}})
to indicate that
c
is a face of
\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega},{\mathsf{k}})
.
Given a maximal
{\mathsf{k}}
-split torus
{\mathbf{T}}
of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}
, write
\mathcal{A}({\mathbf{T}},{\mathsf{k}})
for the corresponding apartment in
\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega},{\mathsf{k}})
.
Write
\Phi({\mathbf{T}},{\mathsf{k}})
(resp.
\Psi({\mathbf{T}},{\mathsf{k}})
) for the set of roots of
{\mathbf{G}}({\mathsf{k}})
(resp. affine roots) of
{\mathbf{T}}({\mathsf{k}})
on
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})
.
For
\psi\in\Psi({\mathbf{T}},{\mathsf{k}})
write
\dot{\psi}\in\Phi({\mathbf{T}},{\mathsf{k}})
for the gradient of
\psi
, and

W=W({\mathbf{T}},{\mathsf{k}})
for the Weyl group of
{\mathbf{G}}({\mathsf{k}})
with respect to
{\mathbf{T}}({\mathsf{k}})
.
For a face
c\subseteq\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega},{\mathsf{k}})
there is a group
{\mathbf{P}}_{c}^{\dagger}
defined over
\mathfrak{o}
such that
{\mathbf{P}}_{c}^{\dagger}(\mathfrak{o})
identifies with the stabiliser of
c
in
{\mathbf{G}}(k)
. There is an exact sequence

1\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{\mathbf{U}}_{c}(\mathfrak{o})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{\mathbf{P}}_{c}^{\dagger}(\mathfrak{o})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{\mathbf{L}}_{c}^{\dagger}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}1,

(2.1.1)

where
{\mathbf{U}}_{c}(\mathfrak{o})
is the pro-unipotent radical of
{\mathbf{P}}_{c}^{\dagger}(\mathfrak{o})
and
{\mathbf{L}}_{c}^{\dagger}
is the reductive quotient of the special fibre of
{\mathbf{P}}_{c}^{\dagger}
.
Let
{\mathbf{L}}_{c}
denote the identity component of
{\mathbf{L}}_{c}^{\dagger}
, and let
{\mathbf{P}}_{c}
be the subgroup of
\bf P_{c}^{\dagger}
defined over
\mathfrak{o}
such that
{\mathbf{P}}_{c}(\mathfrak{o})
is the inverse image of
{\mathbf{L}}_{c}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in
{\mathbf{P}}_{c}^{\dagger}(\mathfrak{o})
.
We also write
{\mathbf{T}}
for the well defined split torus scheme over
\mathfrak{o}
with generic fibre
{\mathbf{T}}
.
This scheme
{\mathbf{T}}
defined over
\mathfrak{o}
is a subgroup of
{\mathbf{P}}_{c}
and the special fibre of
{\mathbf{T}}
, denoted
\bar{\mathbf{T}}
, is a maximal torus of
{\mathbf{L}}_{c}
.
For
c
viewed as a face of
\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}},K)
, the stabiliser of
c
in
{\mathbf{G}}(K)
identifies with
{\mathbf{P}}_{c}^{\dagger}(\mathfrak{O})
.
It has pro-unipotent radical
{\mathbf{U}}_{c}(\mathfrak{O})
and
{\mathbf{P}}_{c}^{\dagger}(\mathfrak{O})/{\mathbf{U}}_{c}(\mathfrak{O})={\mathbf{L}}_{c}^{\dagger}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
For
c
a face lying in
\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega},{\mathsf{k}})\subseteq\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}},K)
,
F_{\omega}
stabilises
{\mathbf{P}}_{c}(\mathfrak{O})
and induces a Frobenius on
{\mathbf{L}}_{c}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
The group
{\mathbf{L}}_{c}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
consists of the fixed points of this Frobenius.
The groups
{\mathbf{P}}_{c}(\mathfrak{o})
obtained in this manner are called (
{\mathsf{k}}
-)
parahoric subgroups
of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}
.
When
c
is a chamber, then we call
{\mathbf{P}}_{c}(\mathfrak{o})
an
Iwahori subgroup
of
\mathbf{G}
.

For this paper it will be convenient to fix a maximal
{\mathsf{k}}
-split torus
{\mathbf{T}}
of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}
and a maximal
K
-split torus
{\mathbf{T}}_{1}
of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}
containing
{\mathbf{T}}
and defined over
{\mathsf{k}}
.
We have that
\mathcal{A}({\mathbf{T}},{\mathsf{k}})=\mathcal{A}({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)^{{\mathrm{Gal}}(K/k)}
.
We will also fix a
{\mathrm{Gal}}(K/{\mathsf{k}})
-stable chamber
c_{0}
of
\mathcal{A}({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)
and a special point
x_{0}\in c_{0}
.
Let
\widetilde{W}=W\ltimes X_{*}(\mathbf{T}_{1},K)
be the (extended) affine Weyl group.
The choice of special point
x_{0}
of
\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}},K)
fixes an inclusion
\Phi({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\Psi({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)
and an isomorphism between
\widetilde{W}
and
N_{{\mathbf{G}}(K)}({\mathbf{T}}_{1}(K))/{\mathbf{T}}_{1}(\mathfrak{O}^{\times})
.
Write

\widetilde{W}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}W,\qquad w\mapsto\dot{w}

(2.1.2)

for the natural projection map.
For a face
c\subseteq\mathcal{A}
let
W_{c}
be the subgroup of
\widetilde{W}
generated by reflections in the hyperplanes through
c
.
The special fibre of
{\mathbf{T}}_{1}
(as a scheme over
\mathfrak{O}
) which we denote by
\overline{{\mathbf{T}}}_{1}
, is a split maximal torus of
{\mathbf{L}}_{c}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
Write
\Phi_{c}(\bar{\mathbf{T}}_{1},\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
for the root system of
{\mathbf{L}}_{c}
with respect to
\bar{\mathbf{T}}_{1}
.
Then
\Phi_{c}(\bar{\mathbf{T}}_{1},\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
naturally identifies with the set of
\psi\in\Psi({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)
that vanish on
c
, and the Weyl group of
\bar{\mathbf{T}}_{1}
in
{\mathbf{L}}_{c}
is isomorphic to
W_{c}
.

Recall that a choice of
x_{0}
fixes an embedding
\Phi({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\Psi({\mathbf{T}}_{1},L)
.
If we fix a set of simple roots
\Delta\subset\Phi({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)
, this embedding determines a set of extended simple roots
\tilde{\Delta}\subseteq\Psi({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)
.
When
\Phi({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)
is irreducible,
\tilde{\Delta}
is just the set
\Delta\cup\{1-\alpha_{0}\}
where
\alpha_{0}
is the highest root of
\Phi({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)
with respect to
\Delta
.
When
\Phi({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)
is reducible, say
\Phi({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)=\cup_{i}\Phi_{i}
where each
\Phi_{i}
is irreducible, then
\tilde{\Delta}=\cup_{i}\tilde{\Delta}_{i}
where
\Delta_{i}=\Phi_{i}\cap\Delta
.
Fix
\Delta
so that the chamber cut out by
\tilde{\Delta}
is
c_{0}
.
Let

{\mathbf{P}}(\tilde{\Delta}):=\{J\subsetneq\tilde{\Delta}:J\cap\tilde{\Delta}_{i}\subsetneq\tilde{\Delta}_{i},\forall i\}.

Each
J\in{\mathbf{P}}(\tilde{\Delta})
cuts out a face of
c_{0}
which we denote by
c(J)
.
In particular
c(\Delta)=x_{0}
.
Note that since
\Omega\simeq\widetilde{W}/W\ltimes\mathbb{Z}\Phi({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)
(recall
{\mathbf{G}}
is semisimple), and
W\ltimes\mathbb{Z}\Phi({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)
acts simply transitively on the chambers of
\mathcal{A}({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)
, the action of
\widetilde{W}
on
\mathcal{A}({\mathbf{T}}_{1},K)
induces an action of
\Omega
on the faces of
c_{0}
and hence on
\tilde{\Delta}
(and
{\mathbf{P}}(\tilde{\Delta})
).
For
\omega\in\Omega
let
\sigma_{\omega}
denote the corresponding permutation of
\tilde{\Delta}
.
Let

{\mathbf{P}}^{\omega}(\tilde{\Delta}):=\{J\in{\mathbf{P}}(\tilde{\Delta})\mid\sigma_{\omega}(J)=J\}

and let
c_{0}^{\omega}
be the chamber of
\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega},{\mathsf{k}})
lying in
c_{0}
.
The set
{\mathbf{P}}^{\omega}(\tilde{\Delta})
is an indexing set for the faces of
c_{0}^{\omega}
.
For
J\in{\mathbf{P}}^{\omega}(\tilde{\Delta})
write
c^{\omega}(J)
for the face of
c_{0}^{\omega}
corresponding to
J
.
The face
c^{\omega}(J)
lies in
c(J)
.
Moreover for
J,J^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{P}}^{\omega}(\tilde{\Delta})
(resp.
{\mathbf{P}}(\tilde{\Delta})
) we have
J\subseteq J^{\prime}
if and only if
\overline{c^{\omega}(J)}\supseteq c^{\omega}(J^{\prime})
(resp.
\overline{c(J)}\supseteq c(J^{\prime})
).

2.2
Nilpotent orbits

Let
\mathcal{N}
be the functor which takes a field
F
to the set of nilpotent elements in
\mathfrak{g}(F)
, and let
\mathcal{N}_{o}
be the functor which takes
F
to the set of adjoint
{\mathbf{G}}(F)
-orbits on
\mathcal{N}(F)
. When
F
is
{\mathsf{k}}
or
K
, we view
\mathcal{N}_{o}(F)
as a partially ordered set with respect to the closure ordering in the topology induced by the topology on
F
.
When
F
is algebraically closed, we view
\mathcal{N}_{o}(F)
as a partially ordered set with respect to the closure ordering in the Zariski topology.
For brevity we will write
\mathcal{N}(F^{\prime}/F)
(resp.
\mathcal{N}_{o}(F^{\prime}/F)
) for
\mathcal{N}(F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}F^{\prime})
(resp.
\mathcal{N}_{o}(F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}F^{\prime})
) where
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}F^{\prime}
is a morphism of fields.
For
(F,F^{\prime})=({\mathsf{k}},K)
(resp.
({\mathsf{k}},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}})
,
(K,{\bar{\mathsf{k}}})
), the map
\mathcal{N}_{o}(F^{\prime}/F)
is strictly increasing (resp. strictly increasing, non-decreasing).
We will simply write
\mathcal{N}
for
\mathcal{N}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathcal{N}_{o}
for
\mathcal{N}_{o}(\mathbb{C})
.
In this case we also define
\mathcal{N}_{o,c}
(resp.
\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}
) to be the set of all pairs
(\mathbb{O},C)
such that
\mathbb{O}\in\mathcal{N}_{o}
and
C
is a conjugacy class in the fundamental group
A(\mathbb{O})
of
\mathbb{O}
(resp. Lusztig’s canonical quotient
\bar{A}(\mathbb{O})
of
A(\mathbb{O})
, see
[
Som01
, Section 5]
). There is a natural map

\mathfrak{Q}:\mathcal{N}_{o,c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}},\qquad(\mathbb{O},C)\mapsto(\mathbb{O},\bar{C})

(2.2.1)

where
\bar{C}
is the image of
C
in
\bar{A}(\mathbb{O})
under the natural homomorphism
A(\mathbb{O})\twoheadrightarrow\bar{A}(\mathbb{O})
. There are also projection maps
\mathrm{pr}_{1}:\mathcal{N}_{o,c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}_{o}
,
\mathrm{pr}_{1}:\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}_{o}
. We will typically write
\mathcal{N}^{\vee}

Remark 2.5.2

For an Iwahori-spherical representation

We now proceed to prove this.

Lemma 2.5.3

Let
X

Then

and in particular
[(c^{\prime},\tau)]=\mathrm{supp}(X)

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{supp}(X)=[(c,\sigma)]
and
c^{\prime},\tau
be as in the statement of the lemma.
Let
[(M,\tau^{\prime})]
be the cuspidal data for
\tau
(i.e. a conjugacy class of Levi of
{\mathbf{L}}_{c^{\prime}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and cuspidal representation of said Levi).
In particular, if
M
is included into any parabolic
P
so that
P
has Levi decomposition
P=MU

\tau^{\prime}
is a subrepresentation of
\tau^{U}
.
Now, all of the parabolics of

are conjugate to a parabolic of the form

where
c^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{c^{\prime\prime}}
.
Thus (conjugating
M
appropriately) we can find a
c^{\prime\prime}
such that
M
is a Levi factor of
{\mathbf{P}}_{c^{\prime\prime}}(\mathfrak{o})/{\mathbf{U}}_{c^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{o})
and so
{\mathbf{L}}_{c^{\prime\prime}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\simeq M
.
We thus have that

\tau^{\prime}\subseteq\tau^{U}\subseteq(X^{{\mathbf{U}}_{c^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{o})})^{{\mathbf{U}}_{c^{\prime\prime}}(\mathfrak{o})/{\mathbf{U}}_{c^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{o})}=X^{{\mathbf{U}}_{c^{\prime\prime}}(\mathfrak{o})}.

In particular
(c^{\prime\prime},\tau^{\prime})
is an unrefined minimal
K
-type for

Thus by

, Theorem 5.2]

is also a minimal face and so

and
\tau=\tau^{\prime}
.
Thus

is a cuspdial representation of

∎

Proposition 2.5.4
.

Let
X
be a depth-0 supercuspidal representation of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})
.
Then

\hphantom{}{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{supp}}(X)=\hphantom{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}(X).

Proof.

Recall that

{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}(X)=\max\{\hphantom{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}_{c}(X):c\subseteq\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega},{\mathsf{k}})\}.

Suppose
c\subseteq\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega},{\mathsf{k}})
is such that
X^{{\mathbf{U}}_{c}(\mathfrak{o})}\neq 0
.
Then for any
\tau
an irreducible constituent of
X^{{\mathbf{U}}_{c}(\mathfrak{o})}
we have by Lemma
2.5.3
that
[(c,\tau)]=\mathrm{supp}(X)
.
Thus we must have that
{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}_{c}(X)=\hphantom{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{supp}}(X)
.
If
c\subseteq\mathcal{B}({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega},{\mathsf{k}})
is such that
X^{{\mathbf{U}}_{c}(\mathfrak{o})}=0
then
{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}_{c}(X,\mathbb{C})=(\{0\},1)
.
Thus

{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}(X)=\hphantom{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{supp}}(X).

∎

Remark 2.5.5
.

Although we have only stated the results for inner twists of the split group, the proposition in fact holds for all connected reductive groups (with identical proof).

Note that since

is a singleton by construction, this establishes Moeglin and Waldspurger’s conjecture for all depth-0 supercuspidal representations for connected reductive groups.

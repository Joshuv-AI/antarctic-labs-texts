Supersonic flows of the Euler-Poisson system in three-dimensional cylinders
Myoungjean Bae, Hyangdong Park

Abstract
. We prove the unique existence of three-dimensional supersonic solutions to the steady Euler-Poisson system in cylindrical nozzles when prescribing the velocity, entropy, and the strength of electric field at the entrance. We first establish the unique existence of irrotational solutions in a cylindrical nozzle with an arbitrary cross section by extending the results of [ 5 ] with an aid of weighted Sobolev norms. Then, we establish the unique existence of axisymmetric solutions with nonzero vorticity in a circular cylinder. In particular, we construct a solution with a nonzero angular momentum density. Therefore this is truly a three-dimensional flow in the sense that the system cannot be reduced to a two-dimensional system via a stream function formulation. The main idea is to reformulate the system via the Helmholtz decomposition method, and to employ the iteration method. Several technical issues, including the issue of corner singularities in a Lipschitz domain, are carefully addressed.

1.
Introduction

Given a positive function
b:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the steady Euler-Poisson system

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{split}&\mbox{div}(\rho{\bf u})=0,\\
&\mbox{div}(\rho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla p=\rho\nabla\Phi,\\
&\mbox{div}(\rho\mathcal{E}{\bf u}+p{\bf u})=\rho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\Phi,\\
&\Delta\Phi=\rho-b,\end{split}\right.

describes a hydrodynamic model of semiconductor devices or plasmas. The function
b
represents the density of fixed, positively charged background ions.
And, the functions
\rho
,
{\bf u}
,
p
, and
\mathcal{E}
represent the macroscopic particle electron density, velocity, pressure, and the total energy, respectively.
The function
\Phi
represents the electric potential generated by the Coulomb force of particles.

In this paper, we assume that
p
and
\mathcal{E}
are given by

(1.2)

p(\rho,S)=S\rho^{\gamma}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{E}(\rho,\lvert{\bf u}\rvert,S)=\frac{\lvert{\bf u}\rvert^{2}}{2}+\frac{S\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}

for a positive function
S
called the
entropy
, and a constant
\gamma>1
called the
adiabatic exponent
.
Then the system (
1.1
) can be rewritten as follows:

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{split}&\mbox{div}(\rho{\bf u})=0,\\
&\mbox{div}(\rho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla p=\rho\nabla\Phi,\\
&\mbox{div}(\rho{\bf u}B)=\rho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\Phi,\\
&\Delta\Phi=\rho-b\end{split}\right.

for the
Bernoulli function

B
defined by

B(\rho,\lvert{\bf u}\rvert,S):=\frac{\lvert{\bf u}\rvert^{2}}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}S\rho^{\gamma-1}.

If
(\rho,{\bf u},p)\in C^{1}
and
\Phi\in C^{2}
solve (
1.3
), then

\rho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla S=\rho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{K}=0

for

(1.4)

\mathcal{K}:=B-\Phi.

We call the function
\mathcal{K}
the
pseudo-Bernoulli invariant
.
For simplicity, we assume that

\mathcal{K}=0.

For an irrotational flow (
\nabla\times{\bf u}={\bf 0}
),
the system (
1.3
) can be simplified as

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{split}&{\rm div}\left(\rho(\Phi,\nabla\varphi)\nabla\varphi\right)=0,\\
&\Delta\Phi=\rho(\Phi,\nabla\varphi)-b\end{split}\right.

with
\rho(\Phi,\nabla\varphi):=\left[\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma S_{0}}(\Phi-\frac{1}{2}\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2})\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}
for a constant
S_{0}>0
.
In
[
3
]
and
[
5
]
, this potential flow system in three dimensional domains has been studied. Especially, the very first result on the existence of a three-dimensional supersonic (
\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert>\sqrt{\gamma S_{0}\rho^{\gamma-1}(\Phi,\nabla\varphi)}
) solution to the system in a rectangular nozzle is proved in
[
5
]
.

The main purpose of this paper is to generalize the result of
[
5
]
. More precisely, the first part of this paper is devoted to prove the existence of a supersonic solution to the potential flow system in a three dimensional cylinder with an arbitrary cross-section. For that purpose, we shall newly define weighted Sobolev spaces.

The second part of this paper is devoted to extend the above result to the full Euler-Poisson system (
1.3
).
Namely, we construct three-dimensional axisymmetric supersonic (
\lvert{\bf u}\rvert>\sqrt{\gamma S\rho^{\gamma-1}}
) solutions to (
1.3
) with
\nabla\times{\bf u}\neq{\bf 0}
. In particular, we shall consider a flow with nonzero angular momentum density(or equivalently, nonzero swirl). Clearly, this feature indicates that the flow is truly three dimensional in the sense that the system (
1.3
) cannot be simplified as a two dimensional system via a stream function formulation. In the spirit of a Helmholtz decomposition, we express the velocity vector field as
{\bf u}=\nabla\varphi+\nabla\times{\bf V}
for a scalar function
\varphi
and a vector field
{\bf V}
. And, we attempt to construct a three-dimensional supersonic solution to the system (
1.3
) as a small perturbation from the case of
{\bf V}={\bf 0}
, which is studied in the first part of the paper. The main idea is to reformulate the system into a second order hyperbolic-elliptic coupled system and two transport equations via the method of Helmholtz decomposition, and to employ the results obtained in the study of the potential flows.

In
[
1
,
2
,
6
]
, the method of Helmholtz decomposition is used to construct various types of multi-dimensional solutions to the system (
1.3
). In particular, an explicit representation for a velocity field
{\bf u}
of an axisymmetric flow with nonzero angular momentum density is introduced in
[
6
]
for the first time to prove the existence of a three-dimensional subsonic (
\lvert{\bf u}\rvert<\sqrt{\gamma S\rho^{\gamma-1}}
) solution to (
1.3
). The representation is as follows:

(1.6)

{\bf u}=\nabla\varphi+\nabla\times(h{\bf e}_{r}+\phi{\bf e}_{\theta})

for axisymmetric functions
\varphi
,
h
and
\phi
.

The main difference of our work from
[
6
]
is that we seek for a solution that satisfies the inequality
\lvert{\bf u}\rvert>\sqrt{\gamma S\rho^{\gamma-1}}
. In this case, the main challenge is to solve a quasi-linear system consisting of a second order hyperbolic differential equation and a second order elliptic differential equations weakly coupled in a three dimensional domain. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first result on the three-dimensional supersonic flows for the steady Euler-Poisson system with nonzero vorticity.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, the main problems and the main theorems are addressed.
In Section
3
, we prove the existence of a supersonic solution to the system (
1.5
) in a three dimensional cylindrical domain with an arbitrary smooth cross-section.
In Section
4
, we prove the
existence of a supersonic solution to the system (
1.3
) in a three dimensional cylindrical domain with a circular cross-section by using the Helmholtz decomposition and applying the result obtained from Section
3
.

2.
Main Theorems

Fix a constant
\gamma>1
.
Suppose that
(\bar{\rho},\bar{u},\bar{p},\bar{\Phi})(x_{1})
solves the Euler-Poisson system (
1.3
) with the function
b
being given by

b=b_{0}

for some constant
b_{0}>0
.
And, let us set
\bar{E}(x_{1}):=\bar{\Phi}^{\prime}(x_{1})
. If the inequalities
\bar{\rho}>0
,
\bar{u}>0
and
\bar{p}>0
hold, then we can rewrite the system (
1.3
) for
(\bar{\rho},\bar{u},\bar{p},\bar{E})
as

(2.1)

\bar{\rho}^{\prime}=\frac{\bar{E}\bar{\rho}}{\gamma S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma-1}-\frac{J_{0}^{2}}{\bar{\rho}^{2}}},\quad\bar{E}^{\prime}=\bar{\rho}-b_{0},\quad\bar{u}=\frac{J_{0}}{\bar{\rho}},\quad\bar{p}=\bar{\rho}^{\gamma}S_{0}

for some constants
J_{0}>0
and
S_{0}>0
. Let us set

\rho_{s}:=\left(\frac{J_{0}^{2}}{\gamma S_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma+1}}.

Then it can be directly checked that the flow governed by the solution
(\bar{\rho},\bar{u},\bar{p},\bar{\Phi})
is
supersonic
(
\bar{u}>\sqrt{\gamma S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma-1}}
) if and only if
\bar{\rho}<\rho_{s}
, and
subsonic
(
\bar{u}<\sqrt{\gamma S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma-1}}
) if and only if
\bar{\rho}>\rho_{s}
. In this paper, we assume that

(2.2)

0<b_{0}<\rho_{s}.

See Figure
1
for a phase plane of solution curves under the assumption of (
2.2
) in Appendix
C
.

For the rest of the paper, we fix the constants
\gamma>1
,
J_{0}>0
and
S_{0}>0
.
Under the condition of (
2.2
), let us consider the initial value problem

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{split}&\bar{\rho}^{\prime}=\frac{\bar{E}\bar{\rho}}{\gamma S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma-1}-\frac{J_{0}^{2}}{\bar{\rho}^{2}}}\\
&\bar{E}^{\prime}=\bar{\rho}-b_{0}\end{split}\right.\quad\mbox{with}\quad(\bar{\rho},\bar{E})(0)=(\rho_{0},E_{0}).

Since we are interested in supersonic flows, we shall fix the initial data
(\rho_{0},E_{0})
in the set
(0,\rho_{s})\times\mathbb{R}
. The unique solvability of the initial value problem (
2.3
) is already well known so we state the following lemma without a proof.

Lemma 2.1

(One-dimensional supersonic solutions (
[
1
, Lemma 1.1]
,
[
9
, Section 3.1]
))
.

For any given constant
\bar{\delta}>0
sufficiently small, there exists a constant
\bar{L}>0
depending on
(\gamma,J_{0},S_{0},b_{0},\rho_{0},E_{0},\bar{\delta})
so that the initial value problem (
2.3
) has a unique smooth solution
(\bar{\rho},\bar{E})(x_{1})
on
[0,\bar{L}]
with satisfying that

\bar{\delta}\leq\bar{\rho}(x_{1})\leq\rho_{s}-\bar{\delta}\quad\text{for $0\leq x_{1}\leq\bar{L}$}.

Note that the above inequality is equivalent to

1+\hat{\delta}\leq\frac{\bar{u}(x_{1})}{\sqrt{\gamma S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma-1}(x_{1})}}<\infty\quad\text{for $0\leq x_{1}\leq\bar{L}$}

for some constant
\hat{\delta}>0
.

Hereafter, we shall fix constants
\gamma>1
,
J_{0}>0
,
S_{0}>0
,
\rho_{0}\in(0,\rho_{s})
and
E_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
(\bar{\rho},\bar{E})
be the solution to the initial value problem (
2.3
), and let
\bar{u}
be given by

\bar{u}=\frac{J_{0}}{\bar{\rho}}.

And, let us define a function
\bar{\Phi}
by

(2.4)

\bar{\Phi}({\rm x})=\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\bar{E}(t)\,dt+B_{0}\quad\text{with $B_{0}=\frac{J_{0}^{2}}{2\rho_{0}^{2}}+\frac{\gamma S_{0}\rho_{0}^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}$}

for
{\rm x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Definition 2.2

(Background solutions)
.

Given constants
\gamma>1
,
J_{0}>0
and
S_{0}>0
, we shall call
(\bar{\rho},\bar{u},S_{0},\bar{\Phi})
the background solution to the steady Euler-Poisson system (
1.3
) associated with
(\rho_{0},E_{0})\in(0,\rho_{s})\times\mathbb{R}
.

Here, the pressure
p
is given by
p=S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma}
. Since we reformulate the system (
1.3
) later so that the new system contains a transport equation for the entropy function
S=\frac{p}{\rho^{\gamma}}
, we include
S_{0}
in Definition
2.2
as a part of a background solution.

The main purpose of this work is to investigate the structural stability of the background solution in three dimensional nozzles by the following regimes:

(i)

First of all, we shall consider the structural stability of the background solution in a three dimensional cylindrical nozzle with an arbitrary cross-section for the potential flow model.

(ii)

Secondly, we shall consider the structural stability in a three dimensional circular cylinder with respect to axisymmetric perturbations for the full Euler-Poisson system.

2.1.
Potential flows

In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, fix an open, connected and bounded domain
\mathcal{D}
with a smooth boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}
.
For a constant
L\in(0,\bar{L}]
, define a three dimensional cylinder
\Omega_{L}
by

(2.5)

\Omega_{L}:=\left\{{\rm x}=(x_{1},{\rm x}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\mbox{ }0<x_{1}<L,\,{\rm x}^{\prime}=(x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{D}\right\}.

We shall denote the entrance, wall, and the exit of
\Omega_{L}
by

\Gamma_{0}:=\partial\Omega_{L}\cap\{x_{1}=0\},\quad\Gamma_{\rm w}:=(0,L)\times\partial\mathcal{D},\quad\Gamma_{L}:=\partial\Omega_{L}\cap\{x_{1}=L\}.

Finally, let
{\bf n}_{w}
represent the inward unit normal vector field on
\Gamma_{{\rm w}}
.

If the velocity field
{\bf u}
is given as

{\bf u}=\nabla\varphi\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega_{L}

for a scalar function
\varphi=\varphi({\rm x})
, and if
(\rho,{\bf u},p,\Phi)
is a classical solution to the system (
1.3
) in
\Omega_{L}
, then it is well known that the entropy
S=\frac{p}{\rho^{\gamma}}
is globally a constant so that we can set as

S\equiv S_{0}\quad\text{in $\Omega_{L}$}

for some constant
S_{0}>0
. Moreover, the solution can be given by solving the following system, which is called
the potential flow model
of the Euler-Poisson system:

(2.6)

\left\{\begin{split}&\mbox{div}(\rho(\Phi,\nabla\varphi)\nabla\varphi)=0,\\
&\Delta\Phi=\rho(\Phi,\nabla\varphi)-b\end{split}\right.

for
\rho
defined by

(2.7)

\rho(z,{\bf q}):=\left[\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma S_{0}}\left(z-\frac{1}{2}\lvert{\bf q}\rvert^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\quad\mbox{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{R},\,{\bf q}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Given constants
\gamma>1
,
J_{0}>0
,
S_{0}>0
and
(\rho_{0},E_{0})\in(0,\rho_{s})\times\mathbb{R}
, let
(\bar{\rho},\bar{u},S_{0},\bar{\Phi})
be the background solution associated with
(\rho_{0},E_{0})
in the sense of Definition
2.2
. And, let us define

(2.8)

\begin{split}&\bar{\varphi}({\rm x}):=\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\bar{u}(t)dt\\
\end{split}

for
{\rm x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\Omega_{L}.

Then
(\varphi,\Phi)=(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\Phi})
solves the system (
2.6
), and satisfies the following boundary conditions:

(2.9)

\begin{split}\varphi=0,\quad\partial_{x_{1}}\varphi=\frac{J_{0}}{\rho_{0}}(=:u_{0}),\quad\partial_{x_{1}}\Phi=E_{0}\quad\mbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{0},\\
\partial_{{\bf n}_{w}}\varphi=0,\quad\partial_{{\bf n}_{w}}\Phi=0\quad\mbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{\rm w}.\end{split}

In addition, it satisfies the inequality

(2.10)

\lvert\nabla\bar{\varphi}\rvert^{2}>\gamma S_{0}\rho^{\gamma-1}(\bar{\Phi},\nabla\bar{\varphi})\quad\text{in $\overline{\Omega_{L}}$}.

First, we set up a problem to find a solution
(\varphi,\Phi)
to the system (
2.6
) as a small perturbation of
(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\Phi})
in
\Omega_{L}
.

Problem 2.3
.

Fix functions
b\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega_{L}})
,
u_{\rm en}\in C^{3}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})
,
E_{\rm en}\in C^{4}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})
, and a function
E_{\rm ex}\in C^{4}(\overline{\Gamma_{L}})
.
In addition, given a small constant
\bar{\epsilon}\in(0,\frac{1}{4}]
, let us set

(2.11)

\Gamma_{0}^{\bar{\epsilon}}:=\{(0,{\rm x}^{\prime})\in\overline{\Gamma_{0}}:\mbox{dist}({\rm x}^{\prime},\partial\mathcal{D})\leq\bar{\epsilon}\}.

Let us assume that the functions
(b,E_{\rm en},E_{\rm ex})
satisfy the compatibility conditions:

(2.12)

\begin{split}&\partial_{x_{1}}b=0,\quad E_{\rm en}-E_{0}=0\quad\mbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{0}^{\bar{\epsilon}},\\
&\partial_{{\bf n}_{w}}(b,u_{\rm en},E_{\rm en},E_{\rm ex})={\bf 0}\quad\mbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{\rm w}.\end{split}

Under this assumption, find a solution
(\varphi,\Phi)
to the Euler-Poisson system in
\Omega_{L}
with satisfying the following properties:

(i)

(The boundary conditions)

(2.13)

\begin{split}\varphi=0,\quad\partial_{x_{1}}\varphi=u_{\rm en},\quad\partial_{x_{1}}\Phi=E_{\rm en}\quad&\mbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{0},\\
\partial_{{\bf n}_{w}}\varphi=0,\quad\partial_{{\bf n}_{w}}\Phi=0\quad&\mbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{\rm w},\\
\partial_{x_{1}}\Phi=E_{\rm ex}\quad&\mbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{L}.\end{split}

(ii)

(Positivity of the density and the velocity along
x_{1}
-direction)

(2.14)

\rho(\Phi,\nabla\varphi)>0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\partial_{x_{1}}\varphi>0\quad\mbox{ in}\quad\overline{\Omega_{L}}.

(iii)

(Supersonic speed)
{\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert}>c(\Phi,\nabla\varphi)
in
\overline{\Omega_{L}}
for the sound speed
c(z,{\bf q})
defined by

(2.15)

c(z,{\bf q}):=\sqrt{(\gamma-1)\left(z-\frac{1}{2}\lvert{\bf q}\rvert^{2}\right)}

for
(z,{\bf q})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
z-\frac{1}{2}\lvert{\bf q}\rvert^{2}>0
.

Before we state our main theorem, we first introduce two
weighted Sobolev norms
.

Definition 2.4

(A weighted Sobolev norm)
.

For each
t\in(0,L)
, let us define
\Omega_{t}
by

\Omega_{t}:=\left\{{\rm x}=(x_{1},{\rm x}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\mbox{ }0<x_{1}<t,\,{\rm x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}\right\}(=\Omega_{L}\cap\{x_{1}<t\}).

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define a weighted
H^{k}
-norm by

\|\phi\|_{H^{k}_{*}(\Omega_{L})}:=\|\phi\|_{H^{k-1}(\Omega_{L})}+\sup_{0<d<L}d^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{L-d})}.

And, we define
H^{k}_{*}(\Omega_{L})
to be

H^{k}_{*}(\Omega_{L}):=\{\phi\in H^{k-1}(\Omega_{L})\cap H^{k}_{\rm loc}(\Omega_{L}):\|\phi\|_{H^{k}_{*}(\Omega_{L})}<\infty\}.

Definition 2.5

(A weighted Sobolev norm with involving a time-like variable )
.

(1) For a function
\phi:\overline{\Omega_{L}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we define two weighted norms involving a time-like variable:

(i)

Regarding the function
\phi
as a map
x_{1}\in(0,L)\mapsto\phi(x_{1},\cdot)\in L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
, we define

\|\phi\|_{L_{*}^{\infty}((0,L);L^{2}(\mathcal{D}))}:=\sup_{0<d<L}d^{\frac{1}{2}}\esssup_{0<s<L-d}\|\phi(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}.

(ii)

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\|\phi\|_{L_{*}^{\infty}((0,L);H^{k}(\mathcal{D}))}:=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\esssup_{0<s<L}\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}^{j}\phi(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}+\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}^{k}\phi\|_{L_{*}^{\infty}((0,L);L^{2}(\mathcal{D}))}.

(2) For a fixed constant
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\begin{split}&\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}^{m,\infty}_{\mathcal{D}}(0,L)}:=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\|\partial_{1}^{j}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,L);H^{m-j}(\mathcal{D}))};\\
&\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}^{m,\infty}_{*,\mathcal{D}}(0,L)}:=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\|\partial_{1}^{j}\phi\|_{L_{*}^{\infty}((0,L);H^{m-j}(\mathcal{D}))}.\end{split}

Finally, we define two vector spaces
\mathcal{W}^{m,\infty}_{\mathcal{D}}(0,L)
and
\mathcal{W}^{m,\infty}_{*,\mathcal{D}}(0,L)
by

\begin{split}&\mathcal{W}^{m,\infty}_{\mathcal{D}}(0,L):=\left\{\phi:\Omega_{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\middle|\;\begin{split}&D^{j}\phi\in L^{\infty}((0,L);L^{2}(\mathcal{D}))\,\,\text{for $j=0,1,\cdots,m$},\\
&\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}^{m,\infty}_{\mathcal{D}}(0,L)}<\infty\end{split}\right\},\\
&\mathcal{W}^{m,\infty}_{*,\mathcal{D}}(0,L):=\left\{\phi:\Omega_{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\middle|\;\begin{split}&D^{j}\phi\in L^{\infty}((0,L);L^{2}(\mathcal{D}))\,\,\text{for $j=0,1,\cdots,m-1$},\\
&D^{m}\phi\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}((0,L);L^{2}(\mathcal{D})),\\
&\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}^{m,\infty}_{*,\mathcal{D}}(0,L)}<\infty\end{split}\right\}.\end{split}

Clearly,
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{W}^{m,\infty}_{\mathcal{D}}(0,L)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{W}^{m,\infty}_{*,\mathcal{D}}(0,L)}
are norms, thus
\mathcal{W}^{m,\infty}_{\mathcal{D}}(0,L)
and
\mathcal{W}^{m,\infty}_{*,\mathcal{D}}(0,L)
are normed vector spaces.

Theorem 2.6

(Potential flows)
.

Fix constants
\gamma>1
,
J_{0}>0
,
S_{0}>0
. And, fix

as

E_{0}=0.

For fixed constants
b_{0}
and
\rho_{0}
satisfying the condition (
2.2
) and the inequality

, respectively, let
(\bar{\rho},\bar{E},\bar{u},\bar{p})
be the solution to (

) with the initial condition

. And, let

be given by (

) and (

For a fixed small constant

, let functions
(b,u_{\rm en},E_{\rm en},E_{\rm ex})

let us set

\begin{split}\sigma(b,u_{\rm en},E_{\rm en},E_{\rm ex})&:=\|b-b_{0}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Omega_{L}})}+\|u_{\rm en}-u_{0}\|_{C^{3}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\\
&\quad+\|E_{\rm en}\|_{C^{4}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}+\|E_{\rm ex}-\bar{E}(L)\|_{C^{4}(\overline{\Gamma_{L}})}.\end{split}

For a fixed constant

.

\begin{split}&\|\Phi-{\bar{\Phi}}\|_{H^{4}_{*}(\Omega_{L})}+\|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}\|_{H^{4}_{*}(\Omega_{L})}+\|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{W}^{4,\infty}_{*,\mathcal{D}}(0,L)}\\
&\leq C\sigma(b,u_{\rm en},E_{\rm en},E_{\rm ex})\end{split}

for a constant

, fixed depending only on

.

Remark 2.7
.

The main difference of this work from
[
5
]
is that the domain that we consider is a three dimensional nozzle with an arbitrary cross-section while a rectangular nozzle is considered in
[
5
]
. But instead, we add the condition (
2.16
) for the following reason.
As we shall see later, a priori
H^{3}
estimates of
(\varphi,\Phi)
can be given by the arguments given in
[
1
,
5
]
. But we need more careful approach to establish a global
H^{4}
estimate of
(\varphi,\Phi)
in this work.

In
[
5
]
, we establish an a priori
H^{4}
-estimate of
\Phi
by applying the method of reflection to the elliptic equation
\Delta\Phi=\rho(\Phi,\nabla\varphi)-b
. To put it simply,
one can achieve a global
H^{4}
-estimate of
\Phi
by using its local even reflection about the wall boundary
\Gamma_{{\rm w}}
, which is a rectangular shell in the Euclidean coordinates. Then, a global

estimate of

is given by a bootstrap argument and the standard hyperbolic estimate method. This is a direct extension of the result from
[
1
]
. In this paper, however, we consider the case where the wall boundary

is non-flat thus the reflection method used in [5] is no longer applicable. To overcome this difficulty, we add the condition (

) and the compatibility condition for

on
\Gamma_{0}
as stated in (

), which enables us to use a local even reflection of
\Phi
about
\Gamma_{0}
so that we can establish an a priori

-estimate of
\Phi
up to
\Gamma_{0}
. And, a global

-estimate of
\Phi
up to the exit boundary

is achieved by using the weighted Sobolev norm

given by Definition

yields an estimate of
\|\varphi\|_{H^{4}_{*}(\Omega_{L})}+\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{W}^{4,\infty}_{*,\mathcal{D}}(0,L)}

2.2.

Definition 2.8
.

Let

denote the cylindrical coordinates of

(i)

A function

\theta

f({\rm x})=g(x_{1},r)

.

(ii)

A vector-valued function

and

. In the above, the vectors
{\bf e}_{x_{1}}
,
{\bf e}_{r}
and
{\bf e}_{\theta}

{\bf e}_{x_{1}}=(1,0,0),\quad{\bf e}_{r}=(0,\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\quad{\bf e}_{\theta}=(0,-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)

Lemma 3.3

This lemma is established based on the weak-
*
compactness of the space
\mathcal{W}^{4,\infty}_{*,\mathcal{D}}(0,L)

Suppose that a sequence of functions

that converges in

. For every small constant

\{\psi_{n}\}

. Therefore, we can apply the generalized Sobolev inequality and the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem again to extract a subsequence of

that converges in

away from

Let
\psi_{\infty}
be the limit function of the convergent subsequence of

.

Given a Hilbert space

is weak-
*
compact. Then the estimate (

*
limit and a diagonal argument.

∎

Next, we set up a linear boundary value problem associated with

|\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(t)|\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)X(s)\,ds\quad\text{for any $t\in[0,L]$}.

Next, we rewrite the term
(\partial_{1}\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h})(V)
as

(\partial_{1}\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h})(V)=2\sum_{j=2}^{3}\partial_{1}a_{1j}\partial_{j}q+\sum_{i,j=2}^{3}\partial_{1}a_{ij}\partial_{ij}V=:I_{1}+I_{2}\quad\text{in $\Omega_{L}$}.

By applying the estimate (
3.27
), we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{t}}|I_{1}\partial_{1}q|\,d{\rm x}\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)X(s)\,ds.\end{split}

In order to estimate the term
\int_{\Omega_{t}}|I_{2}\partial_{1}q|\,d{\rm x}
, we rewrite the equation
\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h}(V)=F_{1}
as

(3.38)

\sum_{i,j=2}^{3}\bar{a}_{ij}\partial_{ij}V+\bar{a}_{1}\partial_{1}V=F_{1}-a_{11}\partial_{11}V-2\sum_{j=2}^{3}a_{1j}\partial_{j}q+\sum_{i,j=2}^{3}(\bar{a}_{ij}-a_{ij})\partial_{ij}V\quad\text{in $\Omega_{L}$}.

Given a constant
s\in(0,L)
, we regard
V(s,\cdot)
as a solution to the two dimensional elliptic equation (
3.38
) in
\mathcal{D}
with the Neumann boundary condition
\partial_{\bf n}V=0
on
\partial\mathcal{D}
. Then, it follows from
[
8
, Theorem 8.12]
and Lemma
3.4
(b) that

\begin{split}&\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}^{2}V(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\\
&\leq C\left(\|V(s,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{D})}+\|F_{1}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}+\|\nabla q(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}+\delta\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}^{2}V(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\right).\end{split}

This directly yields the following lemma:

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose that the constants
L
and
\delta
satisfy all the assumptions stated in Lemma
3.7
. Then one can fix a small constant
\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}]
depending only on the data so that if
\delta
satisfies the inequality
\delta\leq\varepsilon_{2}
, then
V
satisfies the estimate

\begin{split}&\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}^{2}V(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\leq C\left(\|V(s,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{D})}+\|F_{1}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}+\|\nabla q(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\right)\end{split}

for all
0<s<L
.

By applying this lemma and the estimate (
3.27
), we get

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{t}}|I_{2}\partial_{1}q|\,d{\rm x}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)(\|V(s,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{D})}+\|F_{1}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}+\|\nabla q(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})})^{2}\,ds.\end{split}

Then it is derived from (
3.37
) that

(3.39)

X(t)\leq X(0)+C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)\left(X(s)+\|V(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{D})}\right)\,ds+\|F_{1}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}^{2}\right)

provided that the constant
\delta
satisfies the condition
\delta\leq\varepsilon_{2}
for the constant
\varepsilon_{2}
from Lemma
3.10
.

In order to get an estimate of the term
\|V(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{D})}
, let us set
Y(t):=\int_{\Gamma_{t}}|DV|^{2}\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}
. By adjusting the argument given in the above, we can directly derive from the integral expression
\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega_{t}}\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h}(V)\partial_{1}V\,d{\rm x}=\int_{\Omega_{t}}F_{1}\partial_{1}V\,d{\rm x}}
that

\begin{split}Y(t)&\leq Y(0)+C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)Y(s)\,ds+\|F_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{L})}^{2}\right)\\
&=\int_{\mathcal{D}}|g_{1}|^{2}\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}+C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)Y(s)\,ds+\|F_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{L})}^{2}\right).\end{split}

By applying the Grönwall’s inequality with using (
3.32
), we obtain the estimate

\esssup_{0<x_{1}<L}\|DV(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\leq C(\|g_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}+\|F_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{L})}).

Next, we apply the trace inequality to get

\esssup_{0<x_{1}<L}\|V(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\leq C\|V\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}.

The previous two estimates combined with (
3.39
) yield that

X(t)\leq X(0)+C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)X(s)\,ds+(\|V\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}+\|F_{1}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})})^{2}\right).

By rewriting the equation
\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h}(V)=F_{1}
as
\partial_{11}V=F_{1}-\sum_{(i,j)\neq(1,1)}a_{ij}\partial_{ij}V-\bar{a}_{1}\partial_{1}V
on
\Gamma_{0}
, we directly estimate the term
X(0)
as

|X(0)|\leq C\left(\|F_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})}+\sum_{k=0}^{1}\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}^{k}g_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})}+\|W\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}\right)^{2}

so we finally have

X(t)\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)X(s)\,ds+\left(\|V\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\sum_{k=0}^{1}\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}^{k}g_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})}+\|F_{1}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}\right)^{2}\right)

for all
0<t<L
. Then the Grönwall’s inequality yields that

\esssup_{0<t<L}X(t)\leq C\left(\|V\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\sum_{k=0}^{1}\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}^{k}g_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})}+\|F_{1}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}\right)^{2}.

By combining this estimate with (
3.30
), (
3.36
), Proposition
3.9
and Lemma
3.10
, we obtain the following estimate:

(3.40)

\begin{split}&\esssup_{0<x_{1}<L}\|D^{2}V(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}+\|V\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}\leq C\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\right).\end{split}

So the following lemma is obtained:

Lemma 3.11
.

One can fix a constant
\varepsilon_{3}\in(0,\varepsilon_{2}]
sufficiently small depending only on the data so that if the constant
\delta>0
from (
3.10
) satisfies the inequality

0<\delta\leq\varepsilon_{3},

then we have the estimate

\|V\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{\mathcal{D}}(0,L)}+\|(V,W)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}\leq C\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\right).

3.2.3.
A priori estimates of
D^{3}(V,W)

Back to the boundary value problem (
3.35
), we can easily establish the estimate

(3.41)

\|W\|_{H^{3}(\Omega_{L})}\leq C\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\right)

by using a local extension argument combined with Lemma
3.8
and (
3.40
).

Next, we estimate the term
\displaystyle{\esssup_{0<x_{1}<L}\|D^{3}V(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}}
by adjusting the argument given in §
3.2.2
. Since it contains many tedious computations which can be easily derived by minor adjustments in the argument given in §
3.2.2
, we shall explain the whole process very briefly, and provide details only for the parts that needs a careful approach.

1.
Let us set

\zeta:=\partial_{11}V\quad\text{in $\Omega_{L}$},

and define a function
X:[0,L]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

X(t):=\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\nabla\zeta(t,{\rm x}^{\prime})|^{2}\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}.

By differentiating the equation
\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h}(V)=F_{1}
with respect to
x_{1}
twice, we get the following equation for
\zeta
:

\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h}(\zeta)=\partial_{11}F_{1}-(\partial_{11}\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h})(V)\quad\text{in $\Omega_{L}$}

for
\displaystyle{\partial_{11}\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h}:=\sum_{(i,j)\neq(1,1)}\partial_{11}(a_{ij}\partial_{ij}+\bar{a}_{1}\partial_{1})}
. Then, we can show that

(3.42)

\begin{split}X(t)\leq X(0)&+C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)X(s)\,ds+\int_{\Omega_{L}\cap\{x_{1}<t\}}|\partial_{11}F_{1}|^{2}\,d{\rm x}\right)\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{11}\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h}(V)\partial_{1}\zeta|\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}ds.\end{split}

2.
Let us set
\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}:=\sum_{(i,j)\neq(1,1)}\partial_{1}a_{ij}\partial_{1ij}V\partial_{1}\zeta}
. We rewrite
\mathcal{S}
as

\mathcal{S}=\sum_{{\text{$i$ or $j$=1}\atop{(i,j)\neq(1,1)}}}\partial_{1}a_{ij}\partial_{ij1}V\partial_{1}\zeta+\sum_{i,j=2}^{3}\partial_{1}a_{ij}\partial_{1}(\partial_{ij1}V)\partial_{1}\zeta=:\mathcal{S}_{1}+\mathcal{S}_{2}.

By (
3.25
), we have

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\mathcal{S}_{1}|\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}ds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)X(s)\,ds.\end{split}

Next, we differentiate the equation (
3.38
) with respect to
x_{1}
, and apply
[
8
, Theorem 8.12]
, Lemma
3.4
(b), (
3.40
), (
3.41
) and the trace inequality to get the following result:

Lemma 3.12
.

For the constant
\varepsilon_{3}>0
from Lemma
3.11
, one can fix a small constant
\varepsilon_{4}\in(0,\varepsilon_{3}]
depending only on the data so that if the constant
\delta>0
from (
3.10
) satisfies the inequality

0<\delta\leq\varepsilon_{4},

then we have the estimate

\begin{split}&\|D^{2}_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}\partial_{1}V(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\\
&\leq C\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}+\delta\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}^{3}V(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\right)\end{split}

for all
0<s<L
.

Then, by a lengthy computation with using (
3.25
), (
3.40
) and Lemma
3.12
, we can show that

(3.43)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\mathcal{S}_{2}|\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}ds\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)\left(\|DF_{1}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Gamma_{0})}}+\delta\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}^{3}V(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\right)\|\partial_{1}\zeta(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\,ds.\end{split}

Next, we apply
[
8
, Theorem 8.13]
to the equation (
3.38
) to estimate the term
\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}^{3}V(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}
as

\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}^{3}V(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\leq C\left(\|V(s,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{D})}+\|\text{RHS of \eqref{gronwall-7} at $x_{1}=s$}\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{D})}\right).

Then we apply Lemma
3.4
(b), (
3.40
), (
3.41
) and the trace inequality to get

\begin{split}&\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}^{3}V\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{s})}\\
&\leq C\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}+\delta\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}^{3}V(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\right),\end{split}

which leads to the following lemma:

Lemma 3.13
.

One can reduce the constant
\varepsilon_{4}
further from the one given in Lemma
3.12
with depending only on the data so that if the constant
\delta>0
from (
3.10
) satisfies the inequality

0<\delta\leq\varepsilon_{4},

then we have the estimate

(3.44)

\|DD^{2}_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}V(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\leq C\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\right)

for all
0<s<L
.

Then we can finally estimate the term
\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\mathcal{S}|\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}ds}
as

(3.45)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\mathcal{S}|\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}ds\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)X(s)\,ds+\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\right)^{2}\right).\end{split}

3.
Let us set

\mathcal{R}:=\partial_{11}\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h}(V)\partial_{1}q-\sum_{(i,j)\neq(1,1)}\partial_{1}a_{ij}\partial_{1ij}V\partial_{1}q.

A direct computation with applying the generalized Hölder inequality and Sobolev inequality yields the estimate

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\mathcal{R}|\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}ds\\
\leq&C\Bigl(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}(\sum_{k=0}^{2}|D^{k}V|^{2}+|\partial_{1}q|^{2})\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}ds\\
&\phantom{\leq}+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{(i,j)\neq(1,1)}\|\partial_{11}a_{ij}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{D})}\left(\|D^{2}V(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}+\|D_{{\rm x}^{\prime}}\partial_{ij}V(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\right)\|\partial_{1}q(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\,ds\Bigr).\end{split}

By Lemma
3.4
(b), Sobolev inequality and the definition of the function
\kappa(s)
given by (
3.26
), we can estimate the term
\|\partial_{11}a_{ij}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{D})}
as

\|\partial_{11}a_{ij}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{D})}\leq C\kappa(s).

By combining this estimate with (
3.40
) and (
3.44
), we can directly check that

(3.46)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\mathcal{R}|\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}ds\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)X(s)\,ds+\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\right)^{2}\right).\end{split}

4.
Now, we collect all the estimates of (
3.42
), (
3.45
) and (
3.46
) together to show that

\esssup_{0<x_{1}<L}\|D\partial_{11}V(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\leq C\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\right).

Next, we combine the above estimate with (
3.44
) to obtain the estimate

\esssup_{0<x_{1}<L}\|D^{3}V(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\leq C\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\right).

Finally, we can easily prove the following lemma by using (
3.40
) and the above estimate:

Lemma 3.14
.

One can fix a constant
\varepsilon_{4}>0
sufficiently small depending only on the data so that if the constant
\delta>0
from (
3.10
) satisfies the inequality

0<\delta\leq\varepsilon_{4},

then we have the estimate

\begin{split}&\|V\|_{\mathcal{W}^{3,\infty}_{\mathcal{D}}(0,L)}+\|(V,W)\|_{H^{3}(\Omega_{L})}\\
&\leq C\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\right).\end{split}

3.2.4.
A priori estimates of the fourth order derivatives

By extending the argument given in §
3.2.3
to the fourth order derivatives of
(V,W)
, we shall prove the following lemma:

Lemma 3.15
.

For the constant
\varepsilon_{4}>0
given in Lemma
3.14
, one can fix a constant
\varepsilon_{5}\in(0,\varepsilon_{4}]
sufficiently small depending only on the data so that if the constant
\delta>0
from (
3.10
) satisfies the inequality

0<\delta\leq\varepsilon_{5},

then we have the estimate

\begin{split}&\|(V,W)\|_{*}\leq C\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{3}_{*}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{3}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\right).\end{split}

Proof.

1.
The first part of the proof is devoted to estimate the norm
\|W\|_{H^{4}_{*}(\Omega_{L})}
.

(1-1)

Back to the boundary value problem (
3.35
) for
W
, it follows from the compatibility condition (
3.20
) and Lemma
3.4
(f) that the function
F_{2}
, defined by (
3.34
), satisfies the compatibility condition

\partial_{1}F_{2}=\bar{h}_{1}^{\prime}W+\bar{h}_{2}^{\prime}\partial_{1}V\quad\text{on $\Gamma_{0}^{\bar{\epsilon}}$.}

Note that (
2.3
) and (
3.5
) imply that

(\bar{h}_{1}^{\prime},\bar{h}_{2}^{\prime})=\frac{\bar{\rho}^{3-\gamma}\bar{E}}{\gamma S_{0}\left(\gamma S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma-1}-\frac{J_{0}^{2}}{\bar{\rho}^{2}}\right)}\left(2-\gamma,\,\frac{J_{0}}{\bar{\rho}^{2}}-(2-\gamma)\frac{1}{\bar{\rho}}\right)\quad\text{in $\overline{\Omega_{L}}$}.

Then the condition (
2.16
) yields that

(3.47)

\partial_{1}F_{2}=0\quad\text{on $\Gamma_{0}^{\bar{\epsilon}}$.}

Then we can apply a reflection argument (with using a local even extension of
W
about
\Gamma_{0}
) and Proposition
3.14
to obtain the estimate

(3.48)

\|W\|_{H^{4}(\Omega_{L}\cap\{x<\frac{4}{5}L\})}\leq C(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}).

(1-2)

Fix a function
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
that satisfies the following properties:

\chi(x_{1})=\begin{cases}1\quad\mbox{for $x_{1}\leq 0$}\\
0\quad\mbox{for $x_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2}$}\end{cases},\quad\chi^{\prime}(x_{1})\leq 0\quad\text{for all $x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Next, we fix another function
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
that satisfies the following properties:

\eta(x_{1})=\begin{cases}0\quad\mbox{for $x_{1}<\frac{L}{8}$}\\
1\quad\mbox{for $x_{1}\geq\frac{L}{4}$}\end{cases},\quad\eta^{\prime}(x_{1})\geq 0\quad\text{for all $x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}$}.

For each constant
d\in(0,\frac{L}{2})
, we define a scaled cut-off function
\chi_{d}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
by

\chi_{d}(x_{1}):=\eta(x_{1})\chi\left(\frac{x_{1}-L+d}{d}\right).

For each
d>0
, the scaled function
\chi_{d}
satisfies the following properties:

-

\displaystyle{{\rm spt}\,\chi_{d}\subset\left[\frac{L}{8},L-\frac{d}{2}\right]}
;

-

\displaystyle{\chi_{d}(x_{1})=1\,\,\text{for $\frac{L}{4}\leq x_{1}\leq L-d$}}
;

-

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C_{k}>0
depending only on
k
so that the estimate
\displaystyle{\left\|\frac{d^{k}\chi_{d}}{dx_{1}^{k}}\right\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{C_{k}}{d^{k}}}
holds.

For each
d\in(0,\frac{L}{2})
, let us set
\xi_{d}({\rm x}):=\partial_{1}^{3}W({\rm x})\chi_{d}(x_{1})
. Then, by a straight forward computation with using the estimate (
3.48
) and the integral expression

(3.49)

\int_{\Omega_{L}}\nabla(\partial_{1}^{3}W)\cdot\nabla\xi_{d}\,d{\rm x}=\int_{\Omega_{L}}\partial_{11}F_{2}\partial_{1}\xi_{d}\,d{\rm x},

we can show that

\|D\partial_{1}^{3}W\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{L}\cap\{\frac{L}{4}<x_{1}<L-d\})}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\right).

More generally, by locally flattening the wall boundary
\Gamma_{\rm w}
and using the partition of unity, one can similarly show that

\|D^{4}_{{\rm x}}W\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{L}\cap\{\frac{L}{4}<x_{1}<L-d\})}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\right).

By combining this estimate with (
3.48
) and Proposition
3.14
, we obtain the estimate

(3.50)

\|W\|_{H^{4}_{*}(\Omega_{L})}\leq C\left(\|f_{1}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|f_{2}^{P}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|g_{1}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\right).

2.

To complete the proof, it remains to estimate the term

\displaystyle{\sup_{d\in(0,L)}d^{1/2}\esssup_{0<x_{1}<L-d}\|D^{4}V(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}}.

Overall, the estimate is given by adjusting the argument given in §
3.2.3
so we shall provide an outline for the estimate, and add more details only for the parts that need a careful computation.

(2-1)

Let us set

\xi:=\partial_{1}^{3}V\quad\text{in $\Omega_{L}$},

and define a function
Z:[0,L]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

Z(t):=\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\nabla\xi(t,{\rm x}^{\prime})|^{2}\,d{\rm x}.

As an analogy of (
3.42
), we can directly derive from the integral expression

(3.51)

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h}(\partial_{1}^{3}V)\partial_{1}\xi\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}ds=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}(\partial_{1}^{3}F_{1}-(\partial_{1}^{3}\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h})(V))\partial_{1}\xi\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}ds

that, for any
0<t<L
,

(3.52)

\begin{split}Z(t)\leq Z(0)&+C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)Z(s)\,ds+\int_{\Omega_{L}\cap\{x_{1}<t\}}|\partial_{1}^{3}F_{1}|^{2}\,d{\rm x}\right)\\
&+\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}(\partial_{1}^{3}\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h})(V)\partial_{1}\xi\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}ds\right|\end{split}

for
\displaystyle{\partial_{1}^{3}\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h}:=\sum_{(i,j)\neq(1,1)}\partial_{1}^{3}(a_{ij}\partial_{ij}+\bar{a}_{1}\partial_{1})}
.

(2-2)
For each
k=1,2,3
, let us set

T_{k}:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\left(\sum_{(i,j)\neq(1,1)}\partial_{1}^{k}a_{ij}\partial_{1}^{3-k}\partial_{ij}V+\partial_{1}^{k}\bar{a}_{1}\partial_{1}^{3-k}\partial_{1}V\right)\partial_{1}\xi\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}ds

so that we have

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}(\partial_{1}^{3}\mathfrak{L}^{P}_{\rm h})(V)\partial_{1}\xi\,d{\rm x}^{\prime}ds=T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}.

One can adjust the argument in Steps 2–4 in §
3.2.3
, and apply the generalized Hölder inequality, Sobolev inequality, Lemma
3.4
(b) and the estimate (
3.50

\begin{split}|D^{2}_{{\rm x}}S(x_{1},{\rm x}^{\prime})|&=|D^{2}_{{\rm x}}(\mathcal{S}_{\rm{en}}\circ\mathcal{T})(x_{1},|{\rm x}^{\prime}|)|\\
&\leq|D_{{\rm x}}\mathcal{T}(x_{1},|{\rm x}^{\prime}|)|^{2}|\mathcal{S}_{\rm{en}}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\circ\mathcal{T}(x_{1},|{\rm x}^{\prime}|)|+|D^{2}_{{\rm x}}\mathcal{T}(x_{1},|{\rm x}^{\prime}|)||\mathcal{S}_{\rm en}^{{}^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{T}(x_{1},|{\rm x}^{\prime}|)|\\
&\leq\|S_{\rm en}-S_{0}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\left(|D_{{\rm x}}\mathcal{T}(x_{1},|{\rm x}^{\prime}|)|^{2}+\mathcal{T}(x_{1},{\rm x}^{\prime})|D^{2}_{{\rm x}}\mathcal{T}(x_{1},|{\rm x}^{\prime}|)|\right)\\
&\leq C\|S_{\rm en}-S_{0}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}\left(1+\underset{(\leq 2\,\,\text{by \eqref{r vs T}})}{\underbrace{\frac{\mathcal{T}(x_{1},|{\rm x}^{\prime}|)}{|{\rm x}^{\prime}|}}}\sum_{j=0,1}|D^{j}_{{\rm x}}({\bf e}_{1}\cdot{\bf m}_{*})(x_{1},{\rm x}^{\prime})|\right),\end{split}

where we use the compatibility condition
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\rm en}(0)=\partial_{r}S_{\rm en}({\bf 0})=0
stated in Problem
2.9
to obtain the last inequality.
This estimate naturally yields

\|S-S_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{L})}+\|S-S_{0}\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{\mathcal{D}}(0,L)}\leq C\|S_{\rm en}-S_{0}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Gamma_{0}})}.

The rest of the part for the estimate
S-S_{0}
stated in (
4.44
) can be checked similarly with more tedious computations.

4.
Suppose that
S^{(1)}
and
S^{(2)}
are two (not necessarily axisymmetric) solutions to

(4.62)

\begin{cases}{\bf m}_{*}\cdot\nabla S=0\quad\mbox{in $\Omega_{L}$}\\
S=S_{\rm en}\quad\mbox{on $\Gamma_{0}$}\end{cases}.

And, suppose that both solutions satisfy the first estimate stated in (
4.44

Owing to (

given by

\tilde{\bf m}_{*}:=\frac{-{\bf m}_{*}}{{\bf m}_{*}\cdot{\bf e}_{x_{1}}}\quad\text{in $\overline{\Omega_{L}}$}

is well defined. Furthermore, it follows from Remark
2.12
that
Z\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega_{L}})
and
\tilde{\bf m}_{*}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega_{L}})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega_{L}\cap\{x_{1}<L-d\}})
for any
d\in(0,L)
.
Also,
Z
clearly solves the problem

\begin{cases}\tilde{\bf m}_{*}\cdot\nabla Z=0\quad\mbox{in $\Omega_{L}$}\\
Z=0\quad\mbox{on $\Gamma_{0}$}\end{cases}.

For a fixed point
{\rm x}_{0}=(a,{{\rm x}}^{\prime}_{a})\in\overline{\Omega_{L}}\cap\{x_{1}\leq L-d\}
for some

, let us consider the initial value problem for

Since

-solution

thus we obtain that

due to the boundary condition

on
\Gamma_{0}
. This combined with the continuity of

up to
\Gamma_{L}
implies that

in
\overline{\Omega_{L}}

satisfies

(C.6)

\eta\geq\frac{(\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}})^{\eta}(x_{1})-\partial_{1}\bar{a}_{22}{(\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}})}^{\eta}(x_{1})}{\bar{a}_{22}{(\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}})}^{\eta-1}(x_{1})\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}(x_{1})}\quad\text{for $\leq x_{1}\leq L$}.

So we shall choose
\mu\in(0,1]
and
\eta
satisfying

(C.7)

0<\mu\leq\min_{x_{1}\in[0,L]}({\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}})^{\eta}(x_{1})=({\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}})^{\eta}(L)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta\geq\mathfrak{l}_{0}:=\max_{x_{1}\in[0,L]}\left(\frac{(1-\partial_{1}\bar{a}_{22}(x_{1}))\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}(x_{1})}{\bar{a}_{22}(x_{1})\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}(x_{1})}\right)

so that
\partial_{1}(\bar{a}_{22}\mathfrak{M})(x_{1})\geq\mu
holds in
\overline{\Omega_{L}}
.

By a direct computation,
\alpha
can be expressed as

\begin{split}\alpha(x_{1})&=\sum_{l=1}^{2}P_{l}(x_{1})+\sum_{k=1}^{4}N_{k}(x_{1})\end{split}

for
N_{k}
(
k=1,2,3,4)
and
P_{l}
(
l=1,2
) defined by

(C.8)

\begin{split}&N_{1}:=-\frac{(-\bar{E})(\gamma\bar{u}^{2}+\gamma S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma-1})}{(\gamma S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma-1}-\bar{u}^{2})^{2}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\eta}\\
&N_{2}:=-\frac{2\bar{u}^{2}}{(\bar{u}^{2}-\gamma S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma-1})^{2}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2\eta}\\
&N_{3}:=-2\frac{\gamma S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma-1}}{\bar{\rho}}\left(\frac{-(\gamma-1)\bar{u}}{\gamma S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma-1}-\bar{u}^{2}}\frac{\bar{\rho}^{\prime}}{\bar{\rho}}\right)^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2\eta}\\
&N_{4}:=-2\frac{\bar{u}J_{0}}{\gamma S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma-1}}\\
&P_{1}:=-\frac{1}{2}\eta\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\eta-1}\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}\\
&P_{2}:=4\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\eta}\frac{(\gamma-1)\bar{u}^{2}}{\bar{u}^{2}-\gamma S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma-1}}\frac{\bar{\rho}^{\prime}}{\bar{\rho}}.\end{split}

Since
\mathfrak{a}>0
, it directly follows from the conditions stated in Lemma
C.1
that

N_{k}(x_{1})<0\quad\text{and}\quad P_{l}(x_{1})>0\quad\text{for $0<x_{1}\leq L$}.

Next, we shall rewrite (
C.8
) in terms of (
J_{0}
,
h_{0}
,
\kappa
) for
J_{0}
given in (
2.1
),

h_{0}:=(\gamma S_{0})^{\frac{1}{\gamma+1}}\mbox{ and }\kappa(x_{1}):=\frac{\bar{u}(x_{1})}{u_{s}}\mbox{ for }{u}_{s}:=h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}}.

For that purpose, we first rewrite essential functions by a direct computation as follows:

(C.9)

\left.\begin{split}&\bar{u}=u_{s}\kappa=h_{0}J^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}}_{0}\kappa,\quad\bar{\rho}=\frac{J_{0}}{\bar{u}}=h_{0}^{-1}J_{0}^{\frac{2}{\gamma+1}}\kappa^{-1},\\
&\gamma S_{0}\bar{\rho}^{\gamma-1}=h_{0}^{2}J_{0}^{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}}\kappa^{-\gamma+1},\quad\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}=\frac{1}{K}\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2}}\quad\mbox{for }\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}(x_{1}):=\kappa(x_{1}+\mathfrak{a}).\end{split}\right.

It is well known that the solution
(\bar{\rho},\bar{E})
to (
2.3
) satisfies
\frac{1}{2}\bar{E}^{2}-H(\bar{\rho})=\frac{1}{2}E_{0}^{2}-H(\rho_{0})(=:k_{0})
, where
H(\bar{\rho})
is defined by

H(\bar{\rho}):=\int_{\rho_{s}}^{\bar{\rho}}\frac{(t-b_{0})}{t}\left(\gamma S_{0}t^{\gamma-1}-\frac{J_{0}^{2}}{t^{2}}\right)dt\quad\text{for $\rho_{s}:=\frac{J_{0}}{u_{s}}$}.

Owing to the assumption of
E_{0}=0
as given in (
2.16
) and the condition
\bar{E}(x_{1})<0
for
x_{1}\in(0,L^{*}]
, we can express
-\bar{E}
as

(C.10)

-\bar{E}=\sqrt{2h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{F}(\kappa)+2k_{0}}

for
\mathcal{F}
defined by

\mathcal{F}(\kappa):=\int_{1}^{\kappa}\left(1-\frac{t}{\zeta_{0}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{t^{\gamma+1}}\right)dt,\quad\zeta_{0}:=\frac{J_{0}}{b_{0}u_{s}}.

By using (
C.9
) and (
C.10
), we have

(C.11)

\frac{\bar{\rho}^{\prime}}{\bar{\rho}}=\frac{\sqrt{2h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{F}(\kappa)+2k_{0}}}{h_{0}^{2}J_{0}^{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}}\left(\kappa^{2}-\kappa^{-\gamma+1}\right)}.

And, for
\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{a}}(x_{1}):=\bar{\rho}(x_{1}+\mathfrak{a})
, we have

(C.12)

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}=-\frac{\gamma+1}{2}\frac{\sqrt{2h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{F}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}})+2k_{0}}}{h_{0}^{2}J_{0}^{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}}\left(\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}-\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{-\gamma+1}\right)}\frac{1}{K}\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2}}.\end{split}

Then, by using (
C.9
)-(
C.12
), we get

\displaystyle N_{1}=-\frac{\sqrt{2h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{F}(\kappa)+2k_{0}}\left(\gamma\kappa^{2}+\kappa^{-\gamma+1}\right)}{h_{0}^{2}J_{0}^{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}}\left(\kappa^{2}-\kappa^{-\gamma+1}\right)^{2}}\frac{1}{K^{\eta}}\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\frac{(\gamma+1)\eta}{2}}

\displaystyle N_{2}=-\frac{2\kappa^{2}}{h_{0}^{2}J_{0}^{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}}\left(\kappa^{2}-\kappa^{-\gamma+1}\right)^{2}}\frac{1}{K^{2\eta}}\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\gamma+1)\eta}

\displaystyle N_{3}=-2{\kappa^{-\gamma+2}}\frac{\left[(\gamma-1)\kappa\sqrt{2h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{F}(\kappa)+2k_{0}}\right]^{2}}{h_{0}^{3}J_{0}^{\frac{4\gamma-2}{\gamma+1}}\left(\kappa^{2}-\kappa^{-\gamma+1}\right)^{4}}\frac{1}{K^{2\eta}}\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\gamma+1)\eta}

\displaystyle N_{4}=-2h_{0}^{-1}J_{0}^{\frac{2}{\gamma+1}}\kappa^{\gamma}

\displaystyle P_{1}=\eta\frac{\gamma+1}{4}\frac{\sqrt{2h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{F}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}})+2k_{0}}}{h_{0}^{2}J_{0}^{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}}\left(\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}-\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{-\gamma+1}\right)}\frac{1}{K^{\eta}}\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\frac{(\gamma+1)\eta}{2}}

\displaystyle P_{2}=\frac{4(\gamma-1)\kappa^{2}\sqrt{2h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{F}(\kappa)+2k_{0}}}{h_{0}^{2}J_{0}^{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}}\left(\kappa^{2}-\kappa^{-\gamma+1}\right)^{2}}\frac{1}{K^{\eta}}\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\frac{(\gamma+1)\eta}{2}}.

By using the fact that
P_{1}
is strictly positive, we shall fix positive constants
\mu
,
\eta
,
d
and
\mathfrak{a}
to satisfy

\frac{P_{1}}{4}+N_{k}\geq\frac{\mu}{4}\quad\text{for $k=1,2,3,4$},

from which it directly follows that

\alpha(x_{1})\geq P_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{4}N_{k}\geq\mu\quad\text{for $0\leq x_{1}\leq L$}.

For each
k=1,2,3,4
, we rewrite the term
\frac{P_{1}}{4}+N_{k}
as

\left\{\begin{split}\frac{P_{1}(x_{1})}{4}+N_{k}(x_{1})=&\mathfrak{r}(x_{1})(\eta\mathfrak{b}(x_{1})-\beta_{k}(x_{1}))\quad\mbox{for }k=1,2,3,\\
\frac{P_{1}(x_{1})}{4}+N_{4}(x_{1})=&\eta\mathfrak{c}(x_{1})-\beta_{4}(x_{1})\end{split}\right.

for

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}(x_{1}):=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{h_{0}^{2}J_{0}^{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}}}\frac{1}{K^{\eta}}\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\frac{(\gamma+1)\eta}{2}}(x_{1}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}(x_{1}):=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\gamma+1}{16}\frac{\sqrt{2h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{F}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}(x_{1}))+2k_{0}}}{\left(\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}(x_{1})-\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{-\gamma+1}(x_{1})\right)}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}(x_{1}):=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\gamma+1}{16}\frac{\sqrt{2h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{F}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}(x_{1}))+2k_{0}}}{h_{0}^{2}J_{0}^{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}}\left(\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}(x_{1})-\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{-\gamma+1}(x_{1})\right)}\frac{1}{K^{\eta}}\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\frac{(\gamma+1)\eta}{2}}(x_{1}),

\displaystyle\beta_{1}(x_{1}):=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\sqrt{2h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{F}(\kappa(x_{1}))+2k_{0}}\left(\gamma\kappa^{2}(x_{1})+\kappa^{-\gamma+1}(x_{1})\right)}{\left(\kappa^{2}(x_{1})-\kappa^{-\gamma+1}(x_{1})\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\beta_{2}(x_{1}):=

\displaystyle\,\frac{2\kappa^{2}(x_{1})}{\left(\kappa^{2}(x_{1})-\kappa^{-\gamma+1}(x_{1})\right)^{2}}\frac{1}{K^{\eta}}\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\frac{(\gamma+1)\eta}{2}}(x_{1})

\displaystyle\beta_{3}(x_{1}):=

\displaystyle\,2{\kappa^{-\gamma+2}}\frac{\left[(\gamma-1)\kappa(x_{1})\sqrt{2h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{F}(\kappa(x_{1}))+2k_{0}}\right]^{2}}{h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\left(\kappa^{2}(x_{1})-\kappa^{-\gamma+1}(x_{1})\right)^{4}}\frac{1}{K^{\eta}}\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\frac{(\gamma+1)\eta}{2}}(x_{1})

\displaystyle\beta_{4}(x_{1}):=

\displaystyle\,2h_{0}^{-1}J_{0}^{\frac{2}{\gamma+1}}\kappa^{\gamma}(x_{1}).

It follows from the monotonicity of
\bar{u}(x_{1})
and the fact of
L^{*}\leq\bar{L}
that there exists a constant
\delta_{0}>0
satisfying that

1+\delta_{0}\leq\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}(x_{1})<\kappa(x_{1})\leq\frac{u_{0}}{u_{s}}\quad\text{for $x_{1}\in[0,L]$}.

For the constant
K
given by (
C.2
), it holds that
K=\kappa^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2}}(\mathfrak{a})=\kappa^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2}}_{\mathfrak{a}}(0)
so we have

(C.13)

0<\frac{\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2}}(L)}{K}\leq\frac{\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2}}(x_{1})}{K}\leq 1\mbox{ for }x_{1}\in[0,L].

For simplicity, let us set

\lambda_{0}:=h_{0}^{2}J_{0}^{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}}.

Obviously, for each
k=1,2,3
, we have

(C.14)

\mathfrak{r}(\eta\mathfrak{b}-\beta_{k})(x_{1})\geq\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\eta}(x_{1})\quad\text{for $0\leq x_{1}\leq L$}

if and only if

\eta\geq\max_{x_{1}\in[0,1]}\frac{1}{\mathfrak{b}}\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{4}+\beta_{k}\right)\quad\text{for each $k=1,2,3$}.

Due to (
C.13
), we can achieve (
C.14
) if
\eta
and
L
are fixed to satisfy

\eta\geq\max_{x_{1}\in[0,L]}\left\{\frac{1}{\mathfrak{b}}\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{4}+\beta_{1}\right),\frac{1}{\mathfrak{b}}\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{4}+\beta_{2}^{*}\right),\frac{1}{\mathfrak{b}}\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{4}+\beta_{3}^{*}\right)\right\}

for
\beta_{2}^{*}
and
\beta_{3}^{*}
given by

\begin{split}&{\beta_{2}}^{\ast}:=\frac{2\kappa^{2}}{\left(\kappa^{2}-\kappa^{-\gamma+1}\right)^{2}}\\
&{\beta_{3}}^{\ast}:=\frac{2(\gamma-1)^{2}\kappa^{-\gamma+4}\left({2h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{F}(\kappa)+2k_{0}}\right)}{h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\left(\kappa^{2}-\kappa^{-\gamma+1}\right)^{4}}.\end{split}

Next, let us set

{\beta}_{4}^{*}:=2h_{0}^{-1}J_{0}^{\frac{2}{\gamma+1}}\kappa^{\gamma}(0).

It follows from the monotonicity of
\bar{u}
for
0<x_{1}<L
that

(C.15)

\eta\mathfrak{c}(x_{1})-\beta_{4}(x_{1})\geq\eta\mathfrak{c}(x_{1})-{\beta_{4}}^{*}\quad\text{for $0\leq x_{1}\leq L$}.

Note that the value of the function
\mathfrak{c}(x_{1})
solely depends on the value of
\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}(x_{1})
. Furthermore,
\mathfrak{c}
has a continuous dependence on the value of
\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}(x_{1})
for all
x_{1}\in[0,L]
. At
x_{1}=0
where
\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}(0)=\frac{\bar{u}(\mathfrak{a})}{u_{s}}
, we have

\mathfrak{c}(0)=\frac{\gamma+1}{16}\frac{\sqrt{2h_{0}J_{0}^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{F}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}(0))+2k_{0}}}{h_{0}^{2}J_{0}^{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}}\left(\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}(0)-\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}^{-\gamma+1}(0)\right)}=:\mathfrak{c}_{0}>0.

Now we fix a constant
\eta
to satisfy

\eta\geq\max_{x_{1}\in[0,L]}\left\{\mathfrak{l}_{0},\frac{1}{\mathfrak{b}}\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{4}+\beta_{1}\right),\frac{1}{\mathfrak{b}}\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{4}+\beta_{2}^{*}\right),\frac{1}{\mathfrak{b}}\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{4}+\beta_{3}^{*}\right),\frac{1}{\mathfrak{c}_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{4}+\beta_{4}^{\ast}\right)\right\}

for
\mathfrak{l}_{0}
defined by (
C.7
). Note that the choice of
\eta
depends only on the data and
\mathfrak{a}
. By choosing the constant
\eta
as in the above, we have achieved the estimate (
C.14
),

\partial_{1}(\bar{a}_{22}\mathfrak{M})\geq\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\eta}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta\mathfrak{c}(0)-\beta_{4}^{*}\geq\frac{1}{4}.

Finally, we can fix a constant
d\in(0,1)
sufficiently small depending only on the data,
\mathfrak{a}
and
\eta
so that if the condition (
C.1
) holds, then we derive from (
C.15
) and the continuous dependence of
\mathfrak{c}
on
\kappa_{\mathfrak{a}}
that

\eta\mathfrak{c}(x_{1})-\beta_{4}(x_{1})\geq\frac{1}{8}\quad\text{for $0\leq x_{1}\leq L$}.

Note that the choice of
\eta
depends only on the data and
\mathfrak{a}
, therefore we conclude that the choice of the small constant
d
depends only on the data and
\mathfrak{a}
eventually.

We have chosen the constant
d
so that if all the conditions stated in Lemma
C.1
are satisfied, then we establish the estimate (
C.5
) for the constant
\mu
given by

\mu=\min_{x_{1}\in[0,L]}\left\{\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\eta}(x_{1}),\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\eta}(x_{1})+\frac{1}{8}\right\}=\min\left\{\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\eta}(L),\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\eta}(L)+\frac{1}{8}\right\}>0.

∎

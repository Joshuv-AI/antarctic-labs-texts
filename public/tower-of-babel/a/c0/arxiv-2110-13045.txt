Bending of solitonic beams modelled by coupled KMN equation in optical birefringent fibers
Abhik Mukherjee

Abstract
The dynamics of ( 2 + 1 ) (2+1) dimensional optical solitonic beams in birefringent fibers without four wave mixing terms are considered using the recently introduced coupled Kundu Mukherjee Naskar (KMN) equation. The arbitrary bending of solitonic beams in this coupled optical fiber system has been discussed with various two and three dimensional plots. Such analytical results on the bending of optical pulse may pave new directions of research in this field.

II
Coupled KMN equation

The Kundu Mukherjee Naskar (KMN) equation which was first derived in modelling oceanic rogue waves
ourpaper
is given as

iu_{t}+u_{xy}+2iu(uu_{x}^{*}-u^{*}u_{x})=0,

(1)

where the wave profile is given by
u(x,y,t)
and the subscripts denote partial derivatives w. r. to space (x, y) and time (t) coordinates. The asterisk denotes complex conjugation. The coupled Kundu Mukherjee Naskar (KMN) equation was first introduced by Yildirim
Optik-2KMN-2019
;
Optik-2KMN-2019a
in modelling the dynamics of (2+1) dimensional optical solitonic beams in birefringent fibers without four wave mixing terms. In this work, we take the same system of equations taken in
Optik-2KMN-2019
;
Optik-2KMN-2019a
with
a_{i}=1,b_{i}=c_{i}=2
,
i=1,2
for simplicity which can be written as :

\displaystyle{}iu_{t}+u_{xy}+2iu\{(uu_{x}^{*}-u^{*}u_{x})+(vv_{x}^{*}-v^{*}v_{x})\}=0,

\displaystyle{}iv_{t}+v_{xy}+2iv\{(vv_{x}^{*}-v^{*}v_{x})+(uu_{x}^{*}-u^{*}u_{x})\}=0,

(2)

where
u(x,y,t),v(x,y,t)
are two wave profiles. Due to the presence of the current like nonlinearities in Eq. (
2
), it shows few interesting features which will be discussed below.

III
Galilean co-variance (GC)

The Galilean Co-variance (GC) property of KMN equation has been derived in
MyOptik
. Generalizing the same mathematical procedure to (
2
), we can also find the GC of coupled KMN equation.
The equations (
2
) remains co-variant as

\displaystyle{}iU_{T}+U_{XY}+2iU\{(UU_{X}^{*}-U^{*}U_{X})+(VV_{X}^{*}-V^{*}V_{X})\}=0,

\displaystyle{}iV_{T}+V_{XY}+2iV\{(VV_{X}^{*}-V^{*}V_{X})+(UU_{X}^{*}-U^{*}U_{X})\}=0,

(3)

under the Galilean transformation:

\displaystyle X=x-at,\ \ Y=y,\ \ T=t,\ \ u(x,y,t)=U(X,Y,T)\ e^{iaY},v(x,y,t)=V(X,Y,T)\ e^{iaY},

(4)

where
a
is the constant frame velocity. This co-variance property is one of the crucial feature for the bending of optical solitonic beams which will be discussed below.

The Hirota bilinear transformation

\displaystyle{}(iD_{t}+D_{x}D_{y})(g.f)=0,

\displaystyle{}(iD_{t}+D_{x}D_{y})(h.f)=0,

\displaystyle{}D_{x}(f.f_{y})+iD_{x}(g^{*}.g)+iD_{x}(h^{*}.h)=0,

(6)

where the Hirota bilinear operator

\displaystyle{}D_{x}^{m}D_{t}^{n}\ g(x,t)\ .\ f(x,t)=(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\prime}})^{m}\ (\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\prime}})^{n}\ g(x,t)\ f(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})|_{x=x^{\prime},t=t^{\prime}}.

Now we will proceed in the standard way to find the bright and dark soliton solutions. In order to find one bright soliton, we assume

\displaystyle{}g=\epsilon g_{1},\ \ h=\epsilon h_{1},\ \ f=1+\epsilon^{2}f_{2},

\displaystyle{}O(\epsilon)\ :\ (iD_{t}+D_{x}D_{y})(g_{1}.1)=0,\ (iD_{t}+D_{x}D_{y})(h_{1}.1)=0,

\displaystyle{}O(\epsilon^{2})\ :\ -f_{2xy}+iD_{x}(g_{1}^{*}.g_{1})+iD_{x}(h_{1}^{*}.h_{1})=0,\

\displaystyle{}O(\epsilon^{3})\ :\ (iD_{t}+D_{x}D_{y})(g_{1}.f_{2})=0,\ (iD_{t}+D_{x}D_{y})(h_{1}.f_{2})=0,

\displaystyle{}O(\epsilon^{4})\ :\ D_{x}(f_{2}.f_{2y})=0.

(9)

Solving the systems of equations (

VII
Conclusive remarks

In this work, we have considered the coupled Kundu Mukherjee Naskar system which was introduced by Yildirim in

Optik-2KMN-2019
;
Optik-2KMN-2019a
to model (2+1) dimensional optical solitons in birefringence fiber without four wave mixing terms. The Galilean co-variance property of the system has been derived. The dark and bright one solitons of the system has been calculated using Hirota bilinearization method. It has been shown that the dark solitons of the two modes
u
and
v
can
bend arbitrarily due to the presence of an arbitrary function of space (x) and time (t) which is shown by the contour plots. Such arbitrary curvature originates from the current like nonlinearity and Galilean co-variance present in the system (
2
). The condition of larger bending has been derived and explained. Such analytical solutions may pave new directions of research in the field of Nonlinear Optics.

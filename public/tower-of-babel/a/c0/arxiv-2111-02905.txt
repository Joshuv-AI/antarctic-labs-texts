Non-equilibrium Photoionization and Hydrodynamic Simulations of Starburst-driven Outflows
A Danehkar, M S Oey and W J Gray

Abstract
Starburst-driven galactic outflows in star-forming galaxies have been observed to contain complex thermal structures and emission line features that are difficult to explain by adiabatic fluid models and plasmas in photoionization equilibrium (PIE) and collisional ionization equilibrium (CIE). We previously performed hydrodynamic simulations of starburst-driven outflows using the maihem module for non-equilibrium atomic chemistry and radiative cooling functions in the hydrodynamics code flash , and calculated emission lines in combined CIE and PIE conditions. In the present study, we consider time-dependent non-equilibrium ionization (NEI) states produced by our maihem simulations. Through extensive cloudy calculations made with the NEI states from our hydrodynamic simulations, we predict the UV and optical emission line profiles for starburst-driven outflows in time-evolving non-equilibrium photoionization conditions. Our hydrodynamic results demonstrate applications of non-equilibrium radiative cooling for H ii regions in starburst galaxies hosting cool outflows.

Numerical Methods

We consider a galactic-scale outflow driven by starburst feedback from a spherically symmetric super star cluster (SSC) characterized with the SSC radius
R_{\rm sc}
, mass deposition rate
\dot{M}
, wind terminal velocity
V_{\infty}
, and stellar ionizing fields parameterized by ionizing luminosity
L_{\rm ion}
and spectral energy distribution (SED) surrounded by ambient medium with density
\rho_{\rm amb}
and temperature
T_{\rm amb}
. To numerically simulate starburst-driven outflows with various parameters, we model the fluid equations with appropriate initial and boundary conditions (§
2.1
), ionizing radiation fields produced by SSCs (§
2.2
), radiative thermal processes within outflows (§
2.3
), and non-equilibrium photoionization processes (§
2.4
), as illustrated in Figure
2

2.1
Hydrodynamic Simulations

We use the MUSCL method
[
31
]
and hybrid Riemann solver
[
32
]
in the hydrodynamics code
flash

[
33
]
to solve the fluid equations for mass conservation, momentum conservation, and energy conservation coupled with radiative cooling and heating functions of the NEI chemistry package
maihem

[
27
,
28
,
22
]
that are as follows:

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\vec{\nabla}\cdot\left(\rho\vec{u}\right)=0,

(1)

\frac{\partial\vec{u}}{\partial t}+(\vec{u}\cdot\vec{\nabla})\vec{u}+\frac{1}{\rho}\vec{\nabla}P=0,

(2)

\frac{\partial{E}}{\partial t}+\vec{u}\cdot\vec{\nabla}E+\frac{P}{\rho}\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{u}=\frac{\Gamma-\Lambda}{\rho},

(3)

where
\rho
,
u=|\vec{u}|
,
P=\left(\gamma-1\right)\rho\epsilon
, and
E=\epsilon+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}
is the fluid density, velocity, and thermal pressure, and total energy per mass, respectively,
\epsilon
the internal energy per mass,
\gamma=5/3
the heat capacity ratio, and
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
the radiative cooling and heating rates per volume, respectively.

To produce a starburst-driven outflow defined by
\dot{M}
and
V_{\infty}
at the cluster boundary (
r=R_{\rm sc}
) surrounded by the ambient medium, we use the analytic solutions of the steady-state outflow model in one-dimensional spherical coordinates

[
14
,
15
,
20
]
, and set density
\rho
, velocity
u
, and temperature
T
at
r=R_{\rm sc}

with user-defined non-penetrating boundary conditions as follows:

\begin{array}[c]{ccc}\rho\Big|_{r=R_{\rm sc}}=\dfrac{\dot{M}}{4\pi R_{\rm sc}^{2}u},&u\Big|_{r=R_{\rm sc}}=\dfrac{1}{2}V_{\infty},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ T\Big|_{r=R_{\rm sc}}=\dfrac{\mu}{\gamma k_{\rm B}}u^{2},\end{array}

(4)

where
\mu
is the mean mass per particle and
k_{\rm B}
the Boltzmann constant.

We also configure the initial values of the density, temperature, and velocity at
t=0
for the stationary ambient medium (
r>R_{\rm sc}
) with
\rho_{\rm amb}
and
T_{\rm amb}
surrounding the SSC:

\begin{array}[c]{ccc}\rho\Big|_{r>R_{\rm sc},\,t=0}=\rho_{\rm amb},&u\Big|_{r>R_{\rm sc},\,t=0}=0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ T\Big|_{r>R_{\rm sc},\,t=0}=T_{\rm amb}.\end{array}

(5)

The outer boundary at
r=250
pc sets zero-gradient boundary conditions that allow the outflow to leave the simulation domain. We note for an ideal gas
P=\rho k_{\rm B}T/\mu
.

We solve Eqs. (
1
)–(
3
) using the unsplit hydrodynamic solver
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
included in
flash
with the 2nd-order MUSCL–Hancock scheme
[
31
]
. To have stable solutions in regions with strong shocks, we utilize a hybrid Riemann solver
[
32
]
that includes Roe
[
37
]
and HLLE solvers
[
38
,
39
]
. Simulations are performed on a grid with
N_{x}=512
blocks and maximum refinement levels of two, while the refinement criterion
[
40
]
is set to follow the fluid variables
\rho
,
T
,
P
, and
u
. This provides a maximum resolution of 0.244 pc.

2.2
Ionizing Radiation Source Modeling

Radiation fields produced by stars within the SSC result in photoionization and radiative processes.
To model the ionizing radiation source for our hydrodynamic simulations and non-equilibrium photoionization calculations, we generate a SED using the stellar population synthesis program Starburst99

42

43

M
⊙

at solar metallicity and age of 1 Myr. For our Starburst99 model, we employ an IMF with slope

in the mass range 0.5–150 M

, the
Geneva rotational stellar evolution models
[
44
,
45
,
46
]
, and the extended Pauldrach/Hillier stellar atmosphere
[
47
,
48

. The ionizing luminosity and SED spectrum calculated by Starburst99 are supplied to photo-heating functions of

2.3
Radiative Cooling and Heating Processes

To have radiative thermal effects in hydrodynamic simulations, it is necessary to calculate radiative cooling and photo-heating rates. The cooling routine in the
maihem
package implemented by Ref.
[
27
]
computes the radiative cooling rate
\Lambda
using the ion-by-ion cooling efficiencies
\Lambda_{i}
for different species
[
49
]
that depend on the metallicity
Z
and temperature
T
produced by hydrodynamic simulations. The heating routine in
maihem
added by Ref.
[
22
]
calculates the photo-heating cooling rate
\Gamma
using the photoionization cross section
[
50
,
51
]
.

The radiative cooling
\Lambda
and heating rates
\Gamma
are calculated using the ion number density
n_{i}
, and the electron number density
n_{e}
:

\begin{array}[c]{cc}\Lambda=\displaystyle\sum_{i}n_{i}n_{e}\Lambda_{i},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \Gamma=\displaystyle\sum_{i}n_{i}\Gamma_{i},\end{array}

(6)

where
\Lambda_{i}
are the cooling efficiencies from
[
49
]
,
\Gamma_{i}
the heating efficiencies estimated using the SED spectrum from Starburst99 and photoionization cross sections from
[
50
,
51
]
.

Moreover, the
maihem
package as implemented by
[
27
]
also contains the atomic chemistry functions that perform non-equilibrium ionization, collisional ionization and recombination processes using collisional ionization rates
[
52
]
, recombination rates
[
53
]
, and dielectronic recombination rates
[
27
]
. Thus,
maihem
supplies
flash
hydrodynamic simulations with radiative thermal functions and non-equilibrium ionization that allow us to study time-dependent radiative effects in starburst-driven outflows (see Figures
3
and
4
; described in §
3
).

Non-equilibrium photoionization models are built by the program

4
Conclusion

We have conducted hydrodynamic simulations of starburst-driven outflows using the
maihem
module in
flash
to study the formation of radiatively cooling, expanding winds, hot bubbles, and narrow dense shells (see Figure
1
, right) for various wind terminal velocity, ambient density, and mass-loading rate (see Figure
4
, also see
[
23
]
). Our modeling is implemented using the same computational methods developed by
[
29
]
with boundary conditions based on the semianalytic results found by
[
20
]
. Our findings confirm that radiative cooling is increased by raising mass-loading rate and reducing wind terminal velocity
[
23
]
.

In the previous work
[
23
]
, we predicted emissivities for the density and temperature profiles generated by our hydrodynamic simulations in PIE and CIE+PIE conditions, while we additionally calculate emissivities in NEI+PIE conditions in this work (see Figure
5
). Ionization states in PIE and CIE+PIE conditions were calculated by
cloudy
(fully described in
[
23
]
), whereas in NEI+PIE conditions we also provide
cloudy
with time-dependent NEI states created by
maihem

[
30
]
. As found by
[
29
]
, C
iv
emissivity predicted by NEI+PIE model is stronger than those made by PIE and CIE+PIE models (see Figure
5
, right), especially for strong cooling.

Our non-equilibrium photoionization models are conducted using a fixed typical cluster radius (1 pc) and an ionizing source associated with a fixed total stellar mass (
M_{\star}=2\times 10^{6}
M
⊙
) and a final age of 1 Myr. We are carrying out further hydrodynamic simulations to explore other parameters contributing to radiative cooling in starburst-driven outflows such as cluster radius, age, and time-dependent ionizing source.

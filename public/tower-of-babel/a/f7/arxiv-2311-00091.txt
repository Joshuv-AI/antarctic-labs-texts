The derivation problem for one type of bimodules over group algebras
Andronick Arutyunov, Alexey Naianzin

Abstract
The derivation problem is a familiar one concerning group algebras, particularly L 1 ​ ( G ) L_{1}(G) and von Neumann algebras. In this paper, we study the Banach bimodule ℓ p ​ ( G ) \ell_{p}(G) , which is generated by the ℓ p \ell_{p} norm over a specific class of groups with well-organized conjugacy classes. For this case, we will demonstrate that all ℓ p ​ ( G ) \ell_{p}(G) derivations are inner.



1
Introduction

This article is devoted to the study of derivations in bimodules over group rings equipped with various types of norms. The research mainly focuses on classes of groups that act in some way nicely on their conjugacy graph. By employing an approach proposed in earlier works, it is demonstrated that for this class of groups all derivations are inner.

Before providing the historical background and formulating the problem, let us introduce some notation.

3
Bounded Conjugacy Condition

In contrast to the case of Cayley graphs and the translation action, in the case of the conjugation action we are interested in, the distance between vertices can change uncontrollably. For example, in a free group
F_{2}=\langle a\mathchar 59\relax b|\emptyset\rangle
we have
\rho(a\mathchar 59\relax bab^{-1})=1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}
but
\rho(a^{n}aa^{-n}\mathchar 59\relax a^{n}bab^{-1}a^{-n})=n+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}
i.e. tends to infinity. For our construction we will introduce a class of groups in which conjugations act in a controllable manner.

Definition 12
.

A group
G
is called a group with bounded conjugations (a
BC-group
for short) if the following is satisfied:

\forall h_{1}\mathchar 59\relax h_{2}:\rho(h_{1}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}h_{2})=1\,\,\exists\,C>0:\forall g\in G\,\hookrightarrow\rho(gh_{1}g^{-1}\mathchar 59\relax gh_{2}g^{-1})<C.

(18)

It is easy to show that
G
is a BC-group iff for any finite
K\subset G
holds

\sup_{g\in G}\operatorname{diam}(g^{-1}Kg)<\infty.

Theorem 1
.

The property of a group to be a BC-group is well defined, and invariant under the choice of finite generating set.

Proof.

Consider two generating sets
\mathscr{X}=\{x_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\dots\mathchar 59\relax x_{n}\}
and
\mathscr{Y}=\{y_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\dots\mathchar 59\relax y_{m}\}.
Suppose the BC condition holds with respect to
\mathscr{X}
, i.e., for any
h\in G\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}
there exists a constant
C=C(B_{1}(h))
such that for every
g\in G
and
x_{i}\in\mathscr{X}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}
we have
\rho_{\mathscr{X}}(gxhx^{-1}g^{-1}\mathchar 59\relax ghg^{-1})<C.

Now express all generators
\mathscr{Y}
through generators
\mathscr{X}
:

\begin{split}&y_{1}=w_{1}(x_{1}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\dots\mathchar 59\relax x_{n});\\
&\dots\\
&y_{m}=w_{m}(x_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\dots\mathchar 59\relax x_{n}).\end{split}

(19)

Let
L_{y}
be the maximum length of words
w_{1}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\dots\mathchar 59\relax w_{m}.
Similarly, by expressing
x_{i}
through
\mathscr{Y}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}
we define
L_{x}.
Then, for any
h\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}g\in G\mathchar 59\relax\,y_{i}\in\mathscr{Y}
we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathscr{Y}}(gyhy^{-1}g^{-1}\mathchar 59\relax ghg^{-1})\leqslant L_{x}\rho_{\mathscr{X}}(gyhy^{-1}g^{-1}\mathchar 59\relax ghg^{-1})=

\displaystyle=L_{x}\rho_{\mathscr{X}}(gw_{i}(x)hw_{i}(x)^{-1}g^{-1}\mathchar 59\relax ghg^{-1})\leqslant L_{x}C\left(B_{L_{y}}(h)\right)\mathchar 59\relax

(20)

which completes the proof.
∎

be a BC-group, and let
{d\colon f_{p}(G)\to\ell_{q}(G)}
be a bounded derivation with support in
\Gamma_{\text{inf}}
. Then

has a potential from
\ell_{q}.

Proof.

Any potential stabilises by lemma
1
. Choose such
\varphi
that stabilises to
0
. Consider a sequence

from lemma

. By lemma

we get

\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{q}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\left\lVert d(a_{i})\right\rVert\leqslant\left\lVert d\right\rVert_{el}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}

where
\left\lVert d\right\rVert_{el}=\sup_{g\in G}\left\lVert d(g)\right\rVert.

∎

These results allow us to understand what happens on infinite components. It turns out that if the sizes of finite components are uniformly bounded, then a similar result holds for them.

Proposition 6
.

Let the diameters of all finite connected components of
\operatorname{sk}_{G}
be uniformly bounded by some constant
N
. Then a
G
-bounded derivation
d\colon f_{p}(G)\to\ell_{q}(G)
, whose support is contained in
\Gamma_{\operatorname{f}}
, has a potential from
\ell_{q}.

Proof.

In each finite component, pick a vertex
u_{i}
and set the potential at that vertex to zero. Consider a new generating system for the group
G
, containing all words of length at most
N
. Denote this system by
\mathcal{A}
.

Then for each
g\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{f}}
, there exists a vertex
u_{i}
(a vertex of the component containing
g
) and a generating element
a
such that
g=a^{-1}u_{i}a.

Therefore,

\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{f}}}|\varphi(g)|^{q}\leqslant\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{u_{i}\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{f}}}|\varphi(a^{-1}u_{i}a)|^{q}\leqslant\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left\lVert d(a)\right\rVert\leqslant|\mathcal{A}|\left\lVert d\right\rVert_{el}.

So the potential belongs to
\ell_{q}.

∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
be a BC-group with uniformly bounded finite components. Then every derivation
G
-bounded derivation
d\colon f_{p}(G)\to\ell_{q}(G)
is inner.

Proof.

Let’s show that for such
d
, we can find a potential from
\ell_{q}.
The derivation
d
can be represented as the sum
d=d_{\text{inf}}+d_{\operatorname{f}}.
The support of
d_{\text{inf}}
is contained in the infinite components, and the support of
d_{\operatorname{f}}
is contained in the finite components. Note that for every
g\in G
, it holds
\left\lVert d_{\text{inf}}(g)\right\rVert\leqslant\left\lVert d(g)\right\rVert\leqslant\left\lVert d\right\rVert_{el}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}
similarly for the second term. This means that
d_{\operatorname{f}}
and
d_{\text{inf}}
are
G
-bounded, and therefore, by Proposition
6
and Corollary
1
their potentials lie in
\ell_{q}.
Thus, we obtain

\sum_{g\in G}|\varphi(g)|^{q}=\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{\text{inf}}}|\varphi(g)|^{q}+\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{f}}}|\varphi(g)|^{q}=\sum_{g\in G}|\varphi_{\text{inf}}(g)|^{q}+\sum_{g\in G}|\varphi_{\operatorname{f}}(g)|^{q}<\infty.

(30)

Thus, we have shown
\varphi
lies in
\ell_{q}.

∎

Example 8
.

Nilpotent groups of rank 2 are BC-groups with uniformly bounded finite components.

Recall that a group
G
is called a nilpotent group of rank 2 if its factor group modulo the center
Z
is commutative.

Choose a presentation of
G
of the following form
\langle a_{1}\mathchar 59\relax a_{2}\mathchar 59\relax\dots a_{m}\mathchar 59\relax b_{ij}\dots|R\rangle\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}
where
b_{ij}=[a_{i}^{-1}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}a_{j}^{-1}]
. Additionally, assume that if

is a generator, then
a_{i}^{-1}
is also a generator, i.e., there exists

a_{j}={a_{i}}^{-1}.
We consider the group to be finitely generated, as always. We denote the center of the group by

and the subgroup generated by the commutators of generators by

Proposition 7
.

In a nilpotent group of rank 2, the bounded connected components are uniformly bounded by some constant
N.

Proof.

The group
G^{\prime}=[G\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}G]
is finitely generated. Indeed, for a nilpotent group of rank 2, it holds that
a_{i}a_{j}=a_{j}a_{i}a_{i}^{-1}a_{j}^{-1}a_{i}a_{j}=a_{j}a_{i}[a_{i}^{-1}\mathchar 59\relax a_{j}^{-1}]=a_{j}a_{i}b_{ij}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}
where

Consequently, the commutator of any two elements is equal to the product of elements
b_{ij}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}
and there is a finite number of

.

In a finitely generated abelian group, the torsion subgroup is finite. Consider a finite component
\operatorname{sk}_{u_{0}}
. Notice that

where

Therefore, the number of elements in each component is bounded by

∎

Corollary 2
.

In a nilpotent group of rank 2, every

-bounded

Appendix A
Unbounded inner derivation

Consider the following matrices

0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\quad A_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\

(31)

A matrix with elements

. Taking into account the relations, we have

Evidently
a
and

and

The distance between elements is determined only by the difference in

The property of derivation

to be

Indeed, consider the Heisenberg group

We will examine derivations of the form

Let’s take

and define a derivation in such a way that there is a non-zero coefficient before

. To achieve this, define the potential as
\frac{1}{k}
on the vertices

for

and set it to zero on the remaining vertices.

Derivation

defined by
\varphi
is obviously inner and
G
-bounded since
\varphi
lies in
\ell_{2}.
Now let us calculate the images of some elements.

\displaystyle d(A_{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in G}\left(\varphi(A_{x}tA_{x}^{-1})-\varphi(t)\right)A_{x}t=\sum_{t\in G}\varphi(t)tA_{x}-\sum_{t\in G}\varphi(t)A_{x}t=

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\left(A_{p}A_{x}^{-k+1}-A_{x}A_{p}A_{x}^{-k}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\left(A_{x}^{-k+1}A_{p}A_{1}^{-k+1}-A_{x}^{-k+1}A_{p}A_{1}^{-k}\right)

\displaystyle d(A_{x}^{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\left(A_{x}^{-k+m}A_{p}A_{1}^{-k+m}-A_{x}^{-k+m}A_{p}A_{1}^{-k}\right))

Consider the elements

Mittag-Leffler Dichotomy and Roughness of Conformable Fractional Differential Equations Thanks: Supported by NSFC #11701400 and #12071317.
Baishun Wang, Jun Zhou

Abstract
The solutions of traditional fractional differential equations neither satisfy group property nor generate dynamical systems, so the study on hyperbolicity is in blank. Relying on the new proposed conformable fractional derivative, we investigate dichotomy of conformable fractional equations, including structure of solutions of linear systems, Mittag-Leffler dichotomy and stability, roughness and nonuniform dichotomy. Keywords: Conformable fractional differential equations; Mittag-Leffler dichotomy; Roughness; Nonuniform dichotomy; Stability AMS (2020) subject classification: 34D09; 34A08; 37D25

Introduction

The well-known dichotomy concept on various of hyperbolic systems, e.g. ODEs

\displaystyle\dot{x}=A(t)x,~~~t\in J,

(1.1)

where interval
J\subset\mathbb{R}
, is said that there exist a projection
matrix
P
and a fundamental matrix
X(t)
of (
1.1
), and positive constants

K_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
(
i=1,2
) such that for all
t,s\in J
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|X(t)PX^{-1}(s)\|\leq K_{1}e^{-\beta_{1}(t-s)},~~~t\geq s,\\
\|X(t)(I-P)X^{-1}(s)\|\leq K_{2}e^{-\beta_{2}(s-t)},~~~s\geq t.\end{split}

(1.2)

Correspondingly, the roughness of dichotomy is regarded as the persistence of dichotomy undergoing
small linear perturbation, i.e., the perturbed system

\displaystyle\dot{x}=(A(t)+B(t))x,~~~t\in J

still admits dichotomy behaviour in the form of (
1.2
)
along with small variations of
P
,
X(t)
,
K_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
(
i=1,2
).
According to the difference of asymptotic rate,
there are diverse dichotomies, e.g.,
the classical exponential dichotomy (
1.2
) (
[
16
]
),

(h,k)
-dichotomy (
[
30
]
), polynomial dichotomy (
[
8
]
), etc.
The dichotomies and their corresponding properties are core issues
in the field of dynamical systems,
which can be traced back to the papers of Perron (
[

[

And they are gradually formalized, developed and summarized in literatures

The roughness referred before are also widely focused on,

Schäffer (

Fractional derivative started from a letter from L’Hospital to Leibniz about discussing the meaning of a half derivative.

Until 2014, Khalil et al. (

which can almost satisfy all corresponding characteristics analogous to integer derivative.
Thus, the solutions of CFDEs (abbreviation of conformable fractional differential equations) also can generate dynamical systems,
which makes it possible to consider the hyperbolic behaviors of FDEs.
Later, Abdeljawad (

In 2019 Khan et al. (

During the same year, Balci et al. (

In 2020 Xie et al. (

Recently, Wu et al. (

In this paper we attempt to establish the theory of dichotomy for CFDEs.
In order to generalize the hyperbolicity of ODEs to CFDEs,
we first modify the definitions of conformable fractional derivative and integral
and a Mittag-Leffler-type function originated from
[
24
,
1
]
.
Subsequently, we derive the well-posedness of solutions,
conformable integral inequality and variation of constants formula for CFDEs
and structure of solutions and operator semigroups for linear CFDEs.
These fundamental theories are achieved in section 2.
In section 3 we provide the definitions of so-called
Mittag-Leffler stability and dichotomy

with respect to CFDEs, whose asymptotic rate is a Mittag-Leffler-type function.
These stability and dichotomy include the classical exponential stability and dichotomy
(
[

Meanwhile, we develop the conformable fractional integral versions
of projected inequalities to prove the existence of Mittag-Leffler dichotomy
and corresponding invariant manifolds.
In section 4 we discuss the roughness of Mittag-Leffler dichotomy in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
In section 5 we additionally study nonuniform Mittag-Leffler stability and dichotomy and their roughness in
\mathbb{R}_{+}

Our results extend the works of Hale (

2
Linear CFDEs

Throughout this paper, we define the following functions sets:

\displaystyle I(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}):=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\{\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\,|\,\varphi\mbox{ is a nondecreasing function}\},

\displaystyle C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}):=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\{\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\,|\,\varphi\mbox{ is a continuous function}\},

\displaystyle C_{b}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}):=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\{\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\,|\,\varphi\mbox{ is a continuous and bounded function}\},

\displaystyle C_{I}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}):=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\{\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\,|\,\varphi\mbox{ is a continuous and nondecreasing function}\}.

Further, set constants
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
t_{*},t_{0},t^{*}
satisfying
t_{*}<t_{0}<t^{*}
,
function
f:(t_{*},t^{*})\to\mathbb{R}
, and norms

\displaystyle\|x(t)\|\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle:=\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}|x_{i}(t)|,~~~x:[t_{0},+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle\|A(t)\|\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle:=\max\{\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}|a_{i1}(t)|,\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}|a_{i2}(t)|,...,\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}|a_{in}(t)|\},~~~A:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}.

In this section, we focus on the qualitative properties of linear
CFDEs and their perturbations.
Analogously to the linear ODEs, there also exists fundamental solutions
for linear CFDEs.
Consider the nonautonomous linear CFDE

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{\alpha}x=A(t)x,~~~(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},

(2.1)

where matrix function
A\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
.
Our primary purpose is to establish its well-posedness, e.g. existence and uniqueness,
continuous dependence on initial data of solutions and continuation of solutions.
Before this, as a preliminary, we modify the definition and
some properties of conformable fractional derivative and integral
raised by Khalil, Horani, Yousef and Sababheh(
[
24
]
)
and Abdeljawad(
[
1
]
), to make them make more sense.

Definition 2.1

The
\alpha
-conformable fractional derivative of
f
is defined as

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{\alpha}f(t):=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{f(t+\varepsilon|t|^{1-\alpha})-f(t)}{\varepsilon},~~~t\in(t_{*},t^{*}).

(2.2)

In particular, if
\lim\limits_{t\to 0}{\cal T}^{\alpha}f(t)
exists, then

{\cal T}^{\alpha}f(0):=\lim_{t\to 0}{\cal T}^{\alpha}f(t).

Here function
f
is called as
\alpha
-conformable differentiable,
if
{\cal T}^{\alpha}f(t)
exists.

Our Definition
2.1
extends the one in
[
24
]
to the case of
t\leq 0
.
And different from the definition in
[
1
]
,
there are same formulae in (
2.2
) for both
t\leq 0
and
t\geq 0
.
Further, we can deduce the following relations between conformable fractional derivative
and Newton-Leibniz derivative and between conformable fractional integral
and Riemann integral.

Proposition 2.1

The
\alpha
-conformable fractional derivative of
f

can be represented as

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{\alpha}f(t)=|t|^{1-\alpha}f^{\prime}(t),~~~t\in(t_{*},t^{*}).

This proposition implies that there are same sign between
conformable fractional derivative and Newton-Leibniz derivative.

Proposition 2.2

The
\alpha
-conformable fractional integral of
f
is given by

\displaystyle{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}f(t)=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}|s|^{\alpha-1}f(s)ds,~~~t\in(t_{*},t^{*}).

Next, we present some properties of conformable fractional derivative and integral.

Proposition 2.3

Let real functions
f
and
g
be
\alpha
-conformable differentiable in
(t_{*},t^{*})
,
then the following properties hold:

(A1)

{\cal T}^{\alpha}{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}f(t)=f(t)
,
{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}{\cal T}^{\alpha}f(t)=f(t)-f(t_{0})
;

(A2)

{\cal T}^{\alpha}(af+bg)=a{\cal T}^{\alpha}f+b{\cal T}^{\alpha}g
, for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
;

(A3)

{\cal T}^{\alpha}(fg)=f{\cal T}^{\alpha}g+g{\cal T}^{\alpha}f
,

{\cal T}^{\alpha}\big(\cfrac{f}{g}\big)=\cfrac{g{\cal T}^{\alpha}f-f{\cal T}^{\alpha}g}{g^{2}}
;

(A4)

{\cal T}^{\alpha}f\circ g=|g|^{\alpha-1}{\cal T}^{\alpha}f(g){\cal T}^{\alpha}g
, if
g:(t_{*},t^{*})\to(t_{*},t^{*})
.

Specially, the conformable fractional derivatives of some elementary functions
are stated as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}&(1)~{\cal T}^{\alpha}1=0,&(2)~{\cal T}^{\alpha}e^{ct}=c|t|^{1-\alpha}e^{ct},~\forall c\in\mathbb{R},\\
&(3)~{\cal T}^{\alpha}t^{p}=pt^{p-\alpha},~\forall t\geq 0,p\in\mathbb{R}&(4)~{\cal T}^{\alpha}\cfrac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}=1,~\forall t\geq 0,\\
&(5)~{\cal T}^{\alpha}\sin(bt)=b|t|^{1-\alpha}\cos(bt),~\forall b\in\mathbb{R},&(6)~{\cal T}^{\alpha}\cos(bt)=-b|t|^{1-\alpha}\sin(bt),~\forall b\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}

Proposition 2.4

If function
f(t)
is
\alpha
-conformable differentiable at
t=\hat{t}
,
then
f
is continuous at
\hat{t}
.

Proof.

For any
\hat{t}\in(t_{*},t^{*})
,
since
{\cal T}^{\alpha}f(\hat{t})
exists,
we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}[f(\hat{t}+\varepsilon|\hat{t}|^{1-\alpha})-f(\hat{t})]\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{f(\hat{t}+\varepsilon|\hat{t}|^{1-\alpha})-f(\hat{t})}{\varepsilon}\cdot\varepsilon

\displaystyle={\cal T}^{\alpha}f(\hat{t})\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon=0,

where
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon|\hat{t}|^{1-\alpha}=0
.
It leads to the continuity of
f
at
\hat{t}
,
and the proposition is proved.

\qquad\Box

The following special function and fractional integral inequality
both will be useful throughout this paper.

Definition 2.2

The following special function is called as a Mittag-Leffler-type function:

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(\lambda,t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\exp\Big(\lambda\cfrac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\Big)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}\cfrac{\lambda^{k}t^{\alpha k}}{\alpha^{k}k!},~~~\lambda\in\mathbb{R},~~~t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
\exp\Big(-\lambda\cfrac{(-t)^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\Big)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}\cfrac{(-\lambda)^{k}(-t)^{\alpha k}}{\alpha^{k}k!},~~~\lambda\in\mathbb{R},~~~t\in\mathbb{R}_{-}.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 2.1

Let functions
a\in I([t_{0},t^{*}),\mathbb{R}_{+})
and

f\in C([t_{0},t^{*}),\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
Assume that
u:[t_{0},t^{*})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfies fractional
integral inequality

\displaystyle u(t)\leq a(t)+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}f(t)u(t),~~~t\in[t_{0},t^{*}).

(2.5)

Then
u
can be estimated by

\displaystyle u(t)\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle a(t)e^{{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}f(t)}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle a(t)E_{\alpha}\Big(\sup\limits_{s\in[t_{0},t]}f(s),|t|\Big)E_{\alpha}\Big(\sup\limits_{s\in[t_{0},t]}f(s),|t_{0}|\Big),~~~t\in[t_{0},t^{*}).

Proof.

Dividing
a(t)
on the both hands sides of (
2.5
),
by the monotonicity of
a
, we derive

\displaystyle\frac{u(t)}{a(t)}\leq 1+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}f(t)\frac{u(t)}{a(t)},~~~t\in[t_{0},t^{*}).

(2.7)

Let
v(t):=1+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}f(t)\cfrac{u(t)}{a(t)}
,
then

\displaystyle v^{\prime}(t)\leq|t|^{\alpha-1}f(t)v(t),~~~t\in[t_{0},t^{*}).

Integrating both hands sides of equation above from
t_{0}
to
t
,
we get

\displaystyle\ln{v(t)}\leq{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}f(t),~~~t\in[t_{0},t^{*}),

which implies

\displaystyle v(t)\leq e^{{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}f(t)},~~~t\in[t_{0},t^{*}).

Subsequently, from (
2.7
) we prove
the first inequality of estimate (
2.6
).
Because of the continuity of
f
in
[t_{0},t^{*})
,

f
is bounded in
[t_{0},t]
for each
t\in[t_{0},t^{*})
.
Simplifying
a(t)e^{{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}f(t)}
,
we gain

\displaystyle a(t)e^{{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}f(t)}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle a(t)\exp(\sup\limits_{s\in[t_{0},t]}f(s){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}1)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle a(t)\exp(\sup\limits_{s\in[t_{0},t]}f(s)\frac{|t|^{\alpha}+|t_{0}|^{\alpha}}{\alpha}),~~~t\in[t_{0},t^{*}),

following from Definition
2.2
that
the second inequality of estimate (
2.6
) is true.
Therefore, Lemma
2.1
is proved.

\qquad\Box

2.1
Well-posedness of solutions

Subsequently, we study the existence and uniqueness,
continuous dependence on initial data
of solutions and continuation of solutions
for the general CFDEs. Consider the initial value problem (IVP) as follows

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\cal T}^{\alpha}x(t)=f(t,x(t)),~~~(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\\
x(t_{0})=x_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Given constants
a,b>0
and domains

\displaystyle D_{+}=\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:t\in[t_{0}-a,t_{0}+a]\cap\mathbb{R}_{+},\|x-x_{0}\|\leq b\},~~~t_{0}\geq 0,

\displaystyle D_{-}=\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:t\in[t_{0}-a,t_{0}+a]\cap\mathbb{R}_{-},\|x-x_{0}\|\leq b\},~~~t_{0}\leq 0,

assume that the function
f
satisfies:

(B1)

f\in C(D_{+},\mathbb{R}^{n})
(resp.
f\in C(D_{-},\mathbb{R}^{n})
);

(B2)

f(t,x)
satisfies Lipschitz condition with respect to
x
in
D_{+}
(resp.
D_{-}
),
i.e., there is a positive constant
L
such that

\displaystyle\|f(t,x_{1})-f(t,x_{2})\|\leq L\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|,~~~(t,x_{1}),(t,x_{2})\in D_{+}~({\rm resp.}~D_{-}).

Theorem 2.1

Suppose that
(B1)
and
(B2)
hold.
Then IVP (
2.1
) has a unique continuous solution in
I_{+}:=[t_{0}-\delta_{+},t_{0}+\delta_{+}]\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}

for
t_{0}\geq 0

(resp.
I_{-}:=[t_{0}-\delta_{-},t_{0}+\delta_{-}]\cap\mathbb{R}_{-}
for
t_{0}\leq 0
),
where

\displaystyle\delta_{+}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle:=\min\Big\{a,\frac{b}{M_{+}}t_{0}^{1-\alpha}\Big\},~~~M_{+}:=\max_{(t,x)\in D_{+}}\|f(t,x)\|,

\displaystyle\delta_{-}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle:=\min\Big\{a,\frac{b}{M_{-}}(-t_{0})^{1-\alpha}\Big\},~~~M_{-}:=\max_{(t,x)\in D_{-}}\|f(t,x)\|.

Proof.

For convenience, we only discuss the case of
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta_{+}]

for
t_{0}\geq 0
,
all the others can be proved analogously.

Step 1.
By proposition
2.2
,
the equivalent integral equation of IVP (
2.1
) is

\displaystyle x(t)=x_{0}+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}f(t,x(t)),~~~\forall t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta].

(2.11)

Construct the Picard iteration sequence
\{\varphi_{n}(t)\}
successively as follows:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \varphi_{0}(t)&:=x_{0},\\
\varphi_{n}(t)&:=x_{0}+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}f(t,\varphi_{n-1}(t)),~~~n\in\mathbb{N},~t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta].\end{aligned}\right.

(2.12)

We claim that
\varphi_{n}\in C([t_{0},t_{0}+\delta],\mathbb{R}^{n})
is well-defined
and satisfies

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{n}(t)-x_{0}\|\leq b.

(2.13)

It is obvious that the assertion is true for
n=0
.
Assume that it is also true for
n=k
, then
\varphi_{k+1}\in C([t_{0},t_{0}+\delta],\mathbb{R}^{n})

is also well-defined from the inductive hypothesis
and formula (
2.12
). By
(B1)
we compute

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{k+1}(t)-x_{0}\|\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}f(t,\varphi_{k}(t))\Big\|

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq M{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}1=\frac{M}{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}-t_{0}^{\alpha}),~~~t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta].

By the Lagrange’s Mean Value Theorem (
[
37
]
),
there exists a constant
\xi\in(t_{0},t)
such that

\displaystyle|t^{\alpha}-t_{0}^{\alpha}|=|\alpha\xi^{\alpha-1}(t-t_{0})|\leq\alpha t_{0}^{\alpha-1}\delta.

Substituting the inequality above into (
2.14
),
we realize that (
2.13
) holds for
n=k+1
.
Then the assertion is true by induction.

Step 2.
We need explain the uniform convergence of sequence
\{\varphi_{n}(t)\}
with respect to
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
,
that is the uniform convergence of series

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(t)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{+\infty}[\varphi_{k}(t)-\varphi_{k-1}(t)],~~~\forall t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta],

(2.15)

since
\varphi_{0}(t)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}[\varphi_{k}(t)-\varphi_{k-1}(t)]=\varphi_{n}(t)
.
We claim that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{n}(t)-\varphi_{n-1}(t)\|\leq\frac{L^{n-1}b^{n}}{M^{n-1}},~~~\forall t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta].

(2.16)

By
(B2)
and induction, one can verify that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{n+1}(t)-\varphi_{n}(t)\|\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}[f(t,\varphi_{n}(t))-f(t,\varphi_{n-1}(t))]\Big\|

\displaystyle\leq L{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}\|\varphi_{n}(t)-\varphi_{n-1}(t)\|\leq L\frac{L^{n-1}b^{n}}{M^{n-1}}{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}1

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L^{n}b^{n}}{M^{n-1}}\frac{b}{M}=\frac{L^{n}b^{n+1}}{M^{n}},

which yields assertion (
2.16
) is true for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
By the ratio test, we know that the series
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{+\infty}\cfrac{L^{k-1}b^{k}}{M^{k-1}}

is convergent if
b<M/L
, which directly implies that series (
2.15
)
and sequence
\{\varphi_{n}(t)\}
are both uniformly convergent in
[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
.

Step 3.
Let
\varphi(t):=\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\varphi_{n}(t)
,
then we can easily know that
\varphi\in C([t_{0},t_{0}+\delta],\mathbb{R}^{n})
and fulfills
\|\varphi(t)-x_{0}\|\leq b
.
By
(B2)
and the continuity of
f
, we obtain
f(t,\varphi(t))=\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}f(t,\varphi_{n}(t))
.
Then (
2.12
) implies

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\varphi_{n}(t)=x_{0}+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}f(t,\varphi_{n-1}(t)),

that is
x(t)=\varphi(t)
fulfills (
2.11
).
Thus,
\varphi(t)
is a solution of IVP (
2.1
).

Step 4.
Suppose that other solution
x(t)=\psi(t)
also satisfies (
2.11
)
associated with initial condition
\psi(t_{0})=y_{0}
,
then by
(B2)
we have

\displaystyle\|\varphi(t)-\psi(t)\|\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi(t_{0})-\psi(t_{0})\|+\Big\|{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}[f(t,\varphi(t))-f(t,\psi(t))]\Big\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{0}-y_{0}\|+L{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}\|\varphi(t)-\psi(t)\|,~~~\forall t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta].

We employ Lemma
2.1
to receive

\displaystyle\|\varphi(t)-\psi(t)\|\leq\|x_{0}-y_{0}\|E_{\alpha}(L,t)E_{\alpha}(L,t_{0}),~~~\forall t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta].

(2.17)

Since
E_{\alpha}(L,t)E_{\alpha}(L,t_{0})
is bounded for any
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
,
i.e., there exits a constant
M>0
such that
E_{\alpha}(L,t)E_{\alpha}(L,t_{0})\leq M
.
For any constant
\varepsilon>0
, if
\|x_{0}-y_{0}\|<\varepsilon/M
,
it follows from (
2.17
) that
\|\varphi(t)-\psi(t)\|<\varepsilon

for
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
.
This proves the continuous dependence on initial data of solutions for IVP (
2.1
).
Especially, if
x_{0}=y_{0}
then
\varphi(t)\equiv\psi(t)
for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
.
Thus, the uniqueness of solutions is proved.

In conclusion, the proof of Theorem
2.1
is completed.

\qquad\Box

Lemma 2.2

All solutions of (
2.1
) have maximal interval
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we only discuss the case of
t\geq t_{0}
,
the other case can be proved similarly.
Associated with initial time

, the equivalent integral equation of (

) is

\displaystyle x(t)=x(t_{0})+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}A(t)x(t),~~~\forall t\in[t_{0},+\infty),

which implies

\displaystyle\|x(t)\|\leq\|x(t_{0})\|+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}\|A(t)\|\|x(t)\|,~~~\forall t\in[t_{0},+\infty).

It follows from Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle\|x(t)\|\leq\|x(t_{0})\|E_{\alpha}\Big(\sup\limits_{s\in[t_{0},t]}\|A(s)\|,|t|\Big)E_{\alpha}\Big(\sup\limits_{s\in[t_{0},t]}\|A(s)\|,|t_{0}|\Big),~~~\forall t\in[t_{0},+\infty),

where note that the matrix function

.
Therefore, the solution

[t_{0},+\infty)
.

\qquad\Box

Proposition 2.7

2.3
Autonomous systems

As a special case of (
2.1
), here we consider the linear autonomous system

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{\alpha}x=Ax,~~~(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},

(2.19)

where
A
is an
n\times n
real constant matrix.
To solving (
2.19
), we first provide the concept of the Mittag-Leffler-type of a matrix.

Definition 2.3

The Mittag-Leffler-type of an
n\times n
real constant matrix
A
is defined as

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(A,1):=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{A^{k}}{\alpha^{k}k!},

(2.20)

and denote
E_{\alpha}(0,1)=I
for convention.

Proposition 2.8

The power series in (
2.20
) is convergent for any matrix
A
.

Proof.

In deed, it is obvious that
\|A^{k}\|\leq\|A\|^{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, so

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\|A^{k}\|}{\alpha^{k}k!}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\|A\|^{k}}{\alpha^{k}k!}=E_{\alpha}(\|A\|,1)<+\infty.

It yields that the series in (
2.20
) is convergent.

\qquad\Box

Recall the Jordan canonical form in ODEs as follows

\displaystyle P^{-1}AP:=\left[{\begin{array}[]{ccc}J_{1}&\cdots&0\\
0&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&J_{l}\\
\end{array}}\right],~~~J_{i}=\left[{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\lambda_{i}&1&0\\
0&\ddots&1\\
0&0&\lambda_{i}\\
\end{array}}\right],~~~i=1,2,...,l,

where
P
is an
n\times n
nonsingular complex matrix
and
\lambda_{i}
is an eigenvalue of
A
.

Thus, the Mittag-Leffler-type of a matrix can be easily computed as follows.

Proposition 2.9

Let
A
is an
n\times n
real matrix with Jordan canonical form in (
2.3
),
then

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(A,1)=PE_{\alpha}(P^{-1}AP,1)P^{-1}=P\left[{\begin{array}[]{ccc}E_{\alpha}(J_{1},1)&\cdots&0\\
0&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&E_{\alpha}(J_{l},1)\\
\end{array}}\right]P^{-1}.

Proof.

Since
(P^{-1}AP)^{k}=P^{-1}A^{k}P
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, one can compute

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(P^{-1}AP,1)=P^{-1}\Big(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{A^{k}}{\alpha^{k}k!}\Big)P=P^{-1}E_{\alpha}(A,1)P.

Subsequently, one can easily verify that

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(P^{-1}AP,1)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{k}k!}\left[{\begin{array}[]{ccc}J_{1}^{k}&\cdots&0\\
0&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&J_{l}^{k}\\
\end{array}}\right]=\left[{\begin{array}[]{ccc}E_{\alpha}(J_{1},1)&\cdots&0\\
0&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&E_{\alpha}(J_{l},1)\\
\end{array}}\right],

which implies the required conclusion.

\qquad\Box

Further, one can verify the following formula.

Proposition 2.10

If
A=\lambda I+N
, where the nilpotent matrix
N
is

\displaystyle N=\left[{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&\ddots&1\\
0&0&0\\
\end{array}}\right],

then the following expression fulfills:

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(A,1)=E_{\alpha}(\lambda,1)\Big(I+\frac{N}{\alpha}+\frac{N^{2}}{\alpha^{2}2!}+\cdots+\frac{N^{n-1}}{\alpha^{(n-1)}(n-1)!}\Big).

Relying on the preliminaries above, one can solve (
2.19
) as follows.

Lemma 2.3

The matrix
E_{\alpha}(A,t)
is a fundamental solution of (
2.19
)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(A,t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}\cfrac{A^{k}t^{\alpha k}}{\alpha^{k}k!},~~~t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}\cfrac{(-A)^{k}(-t)^{\alpha k}}{\alpha^{k}k!},~~~t\in\mathbb{R}_{-}.\end{array}\right.

The following calculus implies that power series
E_{\alpha}(A,t)
is convergent for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{|t|^{\alpha k}\|A^{k}\|}{\alpha^{k}k!}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{|t|^{\alpha k}\|A\|^{k}}{\alpha^{k}k!}=E_{\alpha}(\|A\|,|t|)<+\infty.

And one can compute that

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{\alpha}E_{\alpha}(A,t)\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{t^{1-\alpha}t^{\alpha k-1}A^{k}}{\alpha^{k-1}(k-1)!}

\displaystyle=A\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha(k-1)}A^{k-1}}{\alpha^{k-1}(k-1)!}=AE_{\alpha}(A,t),~~~t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{\alpha}E_{\alpha}(A,t)\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{-(-t)^{1-\alpha}(-t)^{\alpha k-1}(-A)^{k}}{\alpha^{k-1}(k-1)!}

\displaystyle=A\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{(-t)^{\alpha(k-1)}(-A)^{k-1}}{\alpha^{k-1}(k-1)!}=AE_{\alpha}(A,t),~~~t\in\mathbb{R}_{-},

which yields the wanted result.

\qquad\Box

Both Proposition
2.6
and Lemma
2.3
lead to
the following result on general solutions of (
2.19
).

Proposition 2.11

The general solution of (
2.19
) associated with initial data

can be expressed as

\displaystyle x(t)=\frac{E_{\alpha}(A,t)}{E_{\alpha}(A,t_{0})}x_{0},~~~t\in\mathbb{R}.

3
Stability and Mittag-Leffler dichotomy

In this section, we study the concepts of stability and Mittag-Leffler dichotomy of CFDEs.
Before this, Souahi, Makhlouf and Hammami(
[
42
]
) combined Lyapunov stability and properties of conformable fractional
derivative given by Abdeljawad(
[
1
]
)
to raise the concepts of stability, asymptotic stability and
fractional exponential stability for the nonlinear system (
2.1
).
For the nonautonomous linear CFDE (
2.1
),
the definitions of uniform stability and uniformly asymptotic stability are more essential.

Based on the definition of stability for CFDEs described in
[
42
]
,
we introduce the following definition of uniformly stability
analogous to the corresponding concept of ODEs in e.g.
[
16
, p.1]
.

Definition 3.1

The solution
\hat{x}(t)
of system (
2.1
) is said to be

(C1)

uniformly stable, if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta:=\delta(\varepsilon)>0

such that any solution
x(t)
of (
2.1
) satisfies for some
s\geq 0
,
the inequality
\|x(s)-\hat{x}(s)\|<\delta
implies
\|x(t)-\hat{x}(t)\|<\varepsilon
for all
t\geq s
;

(C2)

attractive, if there exists
\delta_{0}>0
and
T:=T(\varepsilon)>0
for any
\varepsilon>0

such that for some
s\geq 0
, the inequality
\|x(s)-\hat{x}(s)\|<\delta_{0}
implies
\|x(t)-\hat{x}(t)\|<\varepsilon
for all
t\geq s+T
;

(C3)

uniformly asymptotically stable, if it is uniformly stable and attractive.

The following definition is on the
Mittag-Leffler stability
.

Definition 3.2

The solution
x_{*}=0
of system (
2.1
) is Mittag-Leffler stable if

\displaystyle\|x(t)\|\leq K\frac{E_{\alpha}(\lambda,t_{0})}{E_{\alpha}(\lambda,t)}\|x_{0}\|,~~~t\geq t_{0},

where constants
K,\lambda>0
.

More generally, we focus on the significant application of Definition
3.1
to linear equation (
2.1
).

Proposition 3.1

Suppose
X(t)
is a fundamental matrix of (
2.1
) and
c
is a real constant,
then solution
x_{*}=0
of (
2.1
) is said to be

(D1)

stable for any
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if there exists
K:=K(t_{0})>0
such that

\displaystyle\|X(t)\|\leq K,~~~t_{0}\leq t<+\infty;

(D2)

uniformly stable for
t_{0}\geq c
if and only if there exists
K:=K(c)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|X(t)X^{-1}(s)\|\leq K,~~~t_{0}\leq s\leq t<+\infty;

(D3)

asymptotically stable for any
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|X(t)\|=0
;

(D4)

uniformly asymptotically stable for
t_{0}\geq c
if and only if there exist
K:=K(c)>0

and
\lambda:=\lambda(c)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|X(t)X^{-1}(s)\|\leq K\frac{E_{\alpha}(\lambda,s)}{E_{\alpha}(\lambda,t)},~~~t_{0}\leq s\leq t<+\infty.

(3.1)

Particularly,
(D1)-(D4)
all hold for autonomous system (
2.19
),
if fundamental matrix
X(t)
is replaced by
E_{\alpha}(A,t)
.

The proof of
(D1)
-
(D4)
can refer to the Theorem 2.1 in
[
22
, p.84]
.
In particular, since the Mittag-Leffler stability implies the uniformly asymptotic stability,
one can simply verify conclusion
(D4)
.
The definitions of the corresponding stabilities above for Caputo FDEs
had been proposed in references e.g.
[
17
, p.140]
.

Next, we shall propose the concept of
Mittag-Leffler dichotomy
for linear CFDE (
2.1
).

Definition 3.3

Suppose that
X(t)
is a fundamental matrix of (
2.1
).
The equation (
2.1
) possesses a Mittag-Leffler dichotomy
if there exists a projection matrix
P
, i.e.
P^{2}=P
, and positive constants
N_{i}
,
\beta_{i}

(i=1,2)
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|X(t)PX^{-1}(s)\|\leq N_{1}\frac{E_{\alpha}(\beta_{1},s)}{E_{\alpha}(\beta_{1},t)},~~~t\geq s,\\
\|X(t)(I-P)X^{-1}(s)\|\leq N_{2}\frac{E_{\alpha}(\beta_{2},t)}{E_{\alpha}(\beta_{2},s)},~~~s\geq t.\end{split}

(3.2)

In particular, (
2.1
) possesses an ordinary dichotomy if (
3.2
) hold with
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=0
.

Finally, we concern perturbation of nonautonomous linear CFDE (
2.1
).
Consider the perturbed equation

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{\alpha}x=A(t)x+f(t,x),~~~(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},

(3.3)

where

and matrix function

.

The following conclusion give out the projection form of equivalent integral equation
and the existence of bounded solutions for equation (

).

Lemma 3.1

Suppose that function
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

P
is a projection matrix given in Definition
3.3
and

If

for constant

\displaystyle\begin{split}x(t)=&X(t)PX^{-1}(t_{0})x_{0}+X(t)P{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}X^{-1}(t)f(t,x(t))\\
&+X(t)(I-P){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}X^{-1}(t)f(t,x(t)),~~~t\geq t_{0}.\end{split}

(3.4)

If
x\in C_{b}((-\infty,t_{0}],\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a solution of (
3.3
) with
x(t_{0})=x_{0}

for constant
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{-}
, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}x(t)=&X(t)(I-P)X^{-1}(t_{0})x_{0}+X(t)(I-P){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{t_{0}}X^{-1}(t)f(t,x(t))\\
&+X(t)P{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{-\infty}X^{-1}(t)f(t,x(t)),~~~t\leq t_{0}.\end{split}

(3.5)

Conversely, any bounded solution of (
3.4
) or (
3.5
) is a solution of (
3.3
).

Proof.

For convenience, we only prove (
3.4
),
formula (
3.5
) can be proved in an analogous manner.
Assume
x(t)
is a bounded solution of (
3.3
)
and
M:=\sup\limits_{t\in[t_{0},+\infty)}\|x(t)\|
for

4
Roughness of dichotomy

Our focus of this section is the roughness of the Mittag-Leffler dichotomy.
That is the preservation of dichotomy for hyperbolic linear systems
undergoing small linear perturbation.
Consider the perturbed equation of linear CFDE (
2.1
) as follows

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{\alpha}y=[A(t)+B(t)]y,~~~(t,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},

(4.1)

where matrix functions
A\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})

and
B\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
.
The following is one of our main results of this paper.

Theorem 4.1

Assume that
X(t)
is a fundamental matrix of (
2.1
) such that
X(0)=I
,
and equation (
2.1
) possesses a Mittag-Leffler dichotomy,
i.e., estimates (
3.2
) hold in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
If
\varepsilon:=\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}\|B(t)\|
is sufficiently small,
then perturbed equation (
4.1
) also possesses a Mittag-Leffler dichotomy in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Proof.

We divide the proof of Theorem
4.1
into the following three steps.

Step 1: Finding bounded solutions of equation (
4.1
).
Let matrix function
Y\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
equipped with norm

\displaystyle\|Y\|_{\infty}:=\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}\|Y(t)\|.

Define mapping
L:C_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})\to C_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})

as

\displaystyle LY(t)=X(t)P+X(t)P{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y(t)+X(t)(I-P){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y(t).

It follows from (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle\|LY(t)\|\leq\frac{N_{1}}{E_{\alpha}(\beta_{1},t)}+\frac{\varepsilon N_{1}\|Y\|_{\infty}}{E_{\alpha}(\beta_{1},t)}{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}E_{\alpha}(\beta_{1},t)-E_{\alpha}(\beta_{2},t){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}\frac{\varepsilon N_{2}\|Y\|_{\infty}}{E_{\alpha}(\beta_{2},t)}.

Observing that
LY(t)
is bounded and continuous for
t\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|LY\|_{\infty}\leq N_{1}+\varepsilon\Big(\frac{N_{1}}{\beta_{1}}+\frac{N_{2}}{\beta_{2}}\Big)\|Y\|_{\infty}.

Given another
\hat{Y}\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
,
analogously we get

\displaystyle\|LY-L\hat{Y}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon\Big(\frac{N_{1}}{\beta_{1}}+\frac{N_{2}}{\beta_{2}}\Big)\|Y-\hat{Y}\|_{\infty}.

This yields that the mapping
L
has a unique
Y_{1}\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})

such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}Y_{1}(t)=&X(t)P+X(t)P{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{1}(t)\\
&+X(t)(I-P){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{1}(t),\end{split}

(4.2)

if

\displaystyle\theta:=\varepsilon\Big(\frac{N_{1}}{\beta_{1}}+\frac{N_{2}}{\beta_{2}}\Big)<1.

Obviously,
Y_{1}(t)
is also a matrix solution of (
4.1
) and differentiable.
Post-projecting
P
on both hands sides of (
4.2
),
we also know that
Y_{1}(t)P
is the unique fixed point of
L
,
and
Y_{1}(t)P=Y_{1}(t)
.

Step 2: Constructing projection matrix.
Let
Q:=Y_{1}(0)
, then
QP=Q
.
Combining (
4.2
) with the property
P(I-P)=0
, and replacing
t
with
s
, we attain

\displaystyle X(t)PX^{-1}(s)Y_{1}(s)=X(t)P+X(t)P{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(s)B(s)Y_{1}(s).

(4.3)

It follows from (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}Y_{1}(t)=&X(t)PX^{-1}(s)Y_{1}(s)+X(t)P{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{s}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{1}(t)\\
&+X(t)(I-P){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{1}(t),~~~t\geq s\geq 0.\end{split}

(4.4)

Noting (
4.3
) with
t=s=0
, we gain
PQ=P
.
Post-projecting
Q
on both hands sides of (
4.2
) again,
we acquire

\displaystyle Y_{1}(t)Q=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle X(t)P+X(t)P{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{1}(t)Q

\displaystyle+X(t)(I-P){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{1}(t)Q,

implying
Y_{1}(t)Q
is also a fixed point of
L
. In conclusion,

\displaystyle Y_{1}(t)Q=Y_{1}(t)=Y_{1}(t)P.

Obviously,
Q
is a projection when
t=0
.

Provided
Y(t)
is a fundamental matrix of (
4.1
) fulfilling
Y(0)=I
, we derive

\displaystyle Y_{1}(t)=Y(t)Q.

(4.5)

Set

\displaystyle Y_{2}(t):=Y(t)(I-Q),

(4.6)

then
Y(t)=Y_{1}(t)+Y_{2}(t)
.
Relying on the variation of constants formula (
2.33
), we calculate that

\displaystyle Y_{2}(t)\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=X(t)X^{-1}(0)Y_{2}(0)+X(t){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{2}(t)

(4.7)

\displaystyle=X(t)Y(0)(I-Q)+X(t){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=X(t)(I-Q)+X(t){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{2}(t).

Combining (
4.7
) with the fact
(I-P)(I-Q)=I-Q
, and replacing
t
with
s
, we acquire

\displaystyle X(t)(I-P)X^{-1}(s)Y_{2}(s)=X(t)(I-Q)+X(t)(I-P){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(s)B(s)Y_{2}(s).

(4.8)

Subsequently, by (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we receive

\displaystyle Y_{2}(t)=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle X(t)(I-P)X^{-1}(s)Y_{2}(s)+X(t){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{2}(t)

\displaystyle-X(t)(I-P){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(s)B(s)Y_{2}(s)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle X(t)(I-P)X^{-1}(s)Y_{2}(s)+X(t)P{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{2}(t)

\displaystyle+X(t)(I-P){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{2}(t)-X(t)(I-P){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(s)B(s)Y_{2}(s)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle X(t)(I-P)X^{-1}(s)Y_{2}(s)+X(t)P{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{2}(t)

(4.9)

\displaystyle+X(t)(I-P){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{s}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{2}(t),~~~s\geq t\geq 0.

From (
4.4
) and (
4.9
) it follows that for any vector
\xi

\displaystyle\|Y_{1}(t)\xi\|\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle N_{1}\frac{E_{\alpha}(\beta_{1},s)}{E_{\alpha}(\beta_{1},t)}\|Y_{1}(s)\xi\|+\frac{\varepsilon N_{1}}{E_{\alpha}(\beta_{1},t)}|{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{s}E_{\alpha}(\beta_{1},t)Y_{1}(t)\xi|

\displaystyle-\varepsilon N_{2}E_{\alpha}(\beta_{2},t)\Big|{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}\frac{Y_{1}(t)\xi}{E_{\alpha}(\beta_{2},t)}\Big|,~~~t\geq s\geq 0,

Thus, by Lemma

where

,

and

Step 3: Estimation of fundamental solutions.
To prove from (

4.1
) also possesses a Mittag-Leffler dichotomy,

\displaystyle X(t)(I-P)X^{-1}(t)Y_{1}(t)=X(t)(I-P){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{1}(t).

By (
4.10

\displaystyle\leq-\varepsilon N_{2}E_{\alpha}(\beta_{2},t){\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}\frac{\|Y_{1}(t)\xi\|}{E_{\alpha}(\beta_{2},t)}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq-E_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1}+\beta_{2},t)\|Y_{1}(t)\xi\|{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}\frac{\varepsilon K_{1}N_{2}}{E_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1}+\beta_{2},t)}

Analogously, pre-multiplying

\displaystyle X(t)PX^{-1}(t)Y_{2}(t)=X(t)P{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{0}X^{-1}(t)B(t)Y_{2}(t).

4.6

\displaystyle Y(t)QY^{-1}(t)-X(t)PX^{-1}(t)=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle-X(t)PX^{-1}(t)Y(t)[Q+(I-Q)]Y^{-1}(t)

(4.15)

\displaystyle-X(t)PX^{-1}(t)Y(t)(I-Q)Y^{-1}(t).

4.13

4.14

5
Nonuniform dichotomy

This section is a continuation of studies for the Mittag-Leffler dichotomy.
More precisely, we concern
nonuniform Mittag-Leffler dichotomy
.
Let
\mathcal{B}(Z)
consist of all bounded linear operators in Banach space
Z
.
Consider nonautonomous linear CFDE on
Z

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{\alpha}x=A(t)x,~~~(t,x)\in J\times Z,

(5.1)

where linear operator
A\in C(J,\mathcal{B}(Z))
for some interval
J\subset\mathbb{R}

and
\mathcal{B}(Z)
is also a Banach space with the norm

\|A\|:=\sup\limits_{x\in Z,\|x\|=1}\|Ax\|
for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(Z)
.
Let
T(t,s)
be a family of evolution operators satisfying
x(t)=T(t,s)x(s)
for
t\geq s
and
t,s\in J
,
where
x(t)
is any solution of (
5.1
).
T(t,s)
further satisfies:

(F1)

T(t,t)=\mathrm{Id}
(abbreviation of identity) for
t\in J
;

(F2)

T(t,s)T(s,\tau)=T(t,\tau)
for
t,s,\tau\in J
;

(F3)

the evolution operator
T(t,s)
is invertible and
T^{-1}(t,s)=T(s,t)
for
t,s\in J
.

First, we introduce the notions of nonuniform asymptotical stability
and nonuniform Mittag-Leffler dichotomy.

Definition 5.1

Equation (
5.1
) is said to be nonuniform asymptotically stable in
J

if there exist constants
\hat{N},\hat{\beta}>0
and
\epsilon\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\|T(t,s)\|\leq\hat{N}\frac{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta},s)}{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta},t)}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,|s|),~~~t\geq s,~~~t,s\in J.

(5.2)

In particular, (
5.1
) is uniformly asymptotically stable like (
3.1
) if (
5.2
) hold with
\epsilon=0
.

Definition 5.2

Equation (
5.1
) is said to admit a nonuniform Mittag-Leffler dichotomy in
J

if there exist projections
P:J\to\mathcal{B}(Z)
such that

\displaystyle T(t,s)P(s)=P(t)T(t,s),~~~t\geq s,~~~t,s\in J,

(5.3)

and constants
\hat{N}_{i},\hat{\beta}_{i}>0

(i=1,2)
and
\epsilon\geq 0
such that for
t,s\in J
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|T(t,s)P(s)\|\leq\hat{N}_{1}\frac{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta}_{1},s)}{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta}_{1},t)}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,|s|),~~~t\geq s,\\
\|T(t,s)(\mathrm{Id}-P(s))\|\leq\hat{N}_{2}\frac{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta}_{2},t)}{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta}_{2},s)}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,|s|),~~~s\geq t.\end{split}

(5.4)

In particular, (
5.1
) admits a uniform Mittag-Leffler dichotomy like (
3.2
) if (
5.4
) hold with
\epsilon=0
.

All results in this section are presented in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
and denote

\displaystyle{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{s}f(t,\cdot):=\int_{s}^{t}\tau^{\alpha-1}f(t,\tau)d\tau.

Consider the linear perturbation of (
5.1
) as follows

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{\alpha}x=[A(t)+B(t)]x,~~~(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times Z,

(5.5)

where linear operators
A\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{B}(Z))

and
B\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{B}(Z))
.
The following theorem gives out the roughness of nonuniform asymptotical stability.

Theorem 5.1

Assume that equation (
5.1
) admits nonuniform asymptotical stability in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
and there exists constant
\delta
such that
\|B(t)\|\leq\delta/E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,t)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
If
\theta:=\delta\hat{N}/\hat{\beta}<1
, then equation (
5.5
) also admits nonuniform asymptotical stability in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
that is,

\displaystyle\|U(t,s)\|\leq\frac{\hat{N}}{1-\theta}\frac{E_{\alpha}(\gamma,s)}{E_{\alpha}(\gamma,t)}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,s),~~~t\geq s,~~~t,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

(5.6)

where
\gamma=\hat{\beta}-\cfrac{\delta\hat{N}}{1-\theta}
and
U(t,s)
denotes the evolution operator associated to (
5.5
).

Proof.

Consider the space

W:=\{U(t,s)_{t\geq s}\in\mathcal{B}(Z):U
is continuous and
\|U\|_{\alpha}<\infty
,
(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
\}
,

equipped with
\alpha
-weighted norm

\displaystyle\|U\|_{\alpha}:=\sup\left\{\frac{\|U(t,s)\|}{E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,s)}:t\geq s,(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\right\}.

(5.7)

It is easy to verify that
W
is a Banach space.
In
W
define an operator
{\cal J}
by

\displaystyle({\cal J}U)(t,s)=T(t,s)+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{s}T(t,\cdot)B(\cdot)U(\cdot,s).

It follows from (
5.2
) that

\displaystyle\|({\cal J}U)(t,s)\|\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\|T(t,s)\|+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{s}\|T(t,\cdot)\|\|B(\cdot)\|\|U(\cdot,s)\|

\displaystyle\leq\hat{N}\frac{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta},s)}{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta},t)}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,s)+\frac{\delta\hat{N}\|U\|_{\alpha}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,s)}{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta},t)}{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{s}E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta},t)

\displaystyle\leq\hat{N}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,s)+\frac{\delta\hat{N}}{\hat{\beta}}\|U\|_{\alpha}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,s).

And by (
5.7
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|{\cal J}U\|_{\alpha}\leq\hat{N}+\frac{\delta\hat{N}}{\hat{\beta}}\|U\|_{\alpha}<\infty,

which yields that the operator
{\cal J}:W\to W
is well defined.
Analogously to the computation above, we have

\displaystyle\|{\cal J}U_{1}-{\cal J}U_{2}\|_{\alpha}\leq\frac{\delta\hat{N}}{\hat{\beta}}\|U_{1}-U_{2}\|_{\alpha},~~~U_{1},U_{2}\in W,

which implies that
{\cal J}
is a contraction since
\delta<\hat{\beta}/\hat{N}
.
So there exists a unique
U\in W
satisfying
{\cal J}U=U
,
and one can verify that it is a solution of (
5.5
).
We apply Lemma
3.2
with condition
\theta:=\delta\hat{N}/\hat{\beta}<1

to the estimation of
\|U(t,s)\|
.
And inequality (
5.6
) is true.

\qquad\Box

Subsequently,
our purpose is to establish roughness of nonuniform Mittag-Leffler dichotomy in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
A preliminary theorem and the main theorem of roughness are both stated as follows.

Theorem 5.2

Assume that equation (

Step 1: Construction of bounded solutions for (

Lemma 5.1

For each

\displaystyle\begin{split}U(t,s)=&T(t,s)P(s)+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{s}T(t,\cdot)P(\cdot)B(\cdot)U(\cdot,s)\\

Proof.
Clearly, if the function
U(t,s)_{t\geq s}
satisfies (
5.11
),
then it is a solution of (
5.5
). We must demonstrate that the operator
L

\displaystyle(LU)(t,s)=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle T(t,s)P(s)+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{s}T(t,\cdot)P(\cdot)B(\cdot)U(\cdot,s)

\displaystyle+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}T(t,\cdot)(\mathrm{Id}-P(\cdot))B(\cdot)U(\cdot,s),~~~t\geq s,

\displaystyle\|(LU)(t,s)\|\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\|T(t,s)P(s)\|+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{s}\|T(t,\cdot)P(\cdot)\|\|B(\cdot)\|\|U(\cdot,s)\|

\displaystyle-{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}\|T(t,\cdot)(\mathrm{Id}-P(\cdot))\|\|B(\cdot)\|\|U(\cdot,s)\|

\displaystyle\hat{N}_{1}\frac{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta}_{1},s)}{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta}_{1},t)}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,s)+\delta\left(\frac{\hat{N}_{1}}{\hat{\beta}_{1}}+\frac{\hat{N}_{2}}{\hat{\beta}_{2}}\right)\|U\|_{\alpha}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,s).

Combining (
5.7
) with (
5.8

\displaystyle\|LU\|_{\alpha}\leq\hat{N}_{1}+\theta\|U\|_{\alpha}<\infty,

Let

\displaystyle H(t,\tau)={\cal I}^{\alpha}_{\tau}T(t,\cdot)P(\cdot)B(\cdot)H(\cdot,s)+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}T(t,\cdot)(\mathrm{Id}-P(\cdot))B(\cdot)H(\cdot,s).

\displaystyle{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{\tau}\|T(t,\cdot)P(\cdot)\|\|B(\cdot)\|\|\hat{H}(\cdot,s)\|

\displaystyle-{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}\|T(t,\cdot)(\mathrm{Id}-P(\cdot))\|\|B(\cdot)\|\|\hat{H}(\cdot,s)\|

\displaystyle\delta\left(\frac{\hat{N}_{1}}{\hat{\beta}_{1}}+\frac{\hat{N}_{2}}{\hat{\beta}_{2}}\right)\|\hat{H}\|_{\alpha}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,s).

By (

5.19
),
this implies

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t))\hat{T}(t,s)=&T(t,s)(\mathrm{Id}-P(s))(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(s))\\
&+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{\iota}T(t,\cdot)P(\cdot)B(\cdot)(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(\cdot))\hat{T}(\cdot,s)\\
&+{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{s}T(t,\cdot)(\mathrm{Id}-P(\cdot))B(\cdot)(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(\cdot))\hat{T}(\cdot,s).\end{split}

(5.21)

Fixed
\xi\in Z
, we consider
v(t):=\|\hat{T}(t,s)(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(s))\xi\|
for
s\geq t\geq 0

and
v_{s}=\|(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(s))\xi\|
.
According to (
5.19
) and (
5.20
),
it is well known that the function
v(t)
satisfies the inequality (
5.16
).
Employing Lemma
5.6
and the similar proof to Lemma
5.7
,
we easily acquire desired inequality and complete the proof.

\qquad\Box

In conclusion, Lemmas
5.3
,
5.7
and
5.8
all derive Theorem
5.2
together.

The following Lemma will help to prove Theorem
5.3
.

Lemma 5.9

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, if constant
\delta
described as in Theorem
5.3
is small enough, then

\displaystyle\|\hat{P}(t)\|\leq 4\hat{N}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,t),~~~\|\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t)\|\leq 4\hat{N}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,t).

(5.22)

Proof.
Replacing
s
by
t
and pre-multiplying
(\mathrm{Id}-P(t))
on both hands sides of (
5.14
), we have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Id}-P(t))\hat{P}(t)={\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}T(t,\cdot)(\mathrm{Id}-P(\cdot))B(\cdot)\hat{P}(\cdot)\hat{T}(\cdot,t).

(5.23)

It follows from Lemmas
5.7
and
5.3
that for
\tau\geq t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|\hat{P}(\tau)\hat{T}(\tau,t)\|=\|\hat{T}(\tau,t)\hat{P}(t)\hat{P}(t)\|\leq K_{1}\frac{E_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1},t)}{E_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1},\tau)}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,t)\|\hat{P}(t)\|.

(5.24)

By (
5.23
) and (
5.4
) we calculate that

\displaystyle\|(\mathrm{Id}-P(t))\hat{P}(t)\|\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle-{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}\|T(t,\cdot)(\mathrm{Id}-P(\cdot))\|\|B(\cdot)\|\|\hat{P}(\cdot)\hat{T}(\cdot,t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle-E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta}_{2}+\lambda_{1}+\epsilon,t)\|\hat{P}(t)\|{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}\frac{\delta K_{1}\hat{N}_{2}}{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta}_{2}+\lambda_{1}+\epsilon,t)}

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\delta K_{1}\hat{N}_{2}}{\hat{\beta}_{2}+\lambda_{1}-\epsilon}\|\hat{P}(t)\|,

(5.25)

where constant
\epsilon
was chosen as satisfying
\epsilon<\min\{\hat{\beta}_{1},\hat{\beta}_{2}\}

in order to guarantee the above denominator
\hat{\beta}_{2}+\lambda_{1}-\epsilon>0
.
Analogously to (
5.23
), replacing
t
with
s
and pre-multiplying
P(t)
on both hands sides of (
5.21
),
we attain

\displaystyle P(t)(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t))={\cal I}^{\alpha}_{\iota}T(t,\cdot)P(\cdot)B(\cdot)(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(\cdot))\hat{T}(\cdot,t).

(5.26)

Using Lemma
5.8
, for
t\geq\tau\geq 0
this implies

\displaystyle\|(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(\tau))\hat{T}(\tau,t)\|\leq K_{2}\frac{E_{\alpha}(\lambda_{2},\tau)}{E_{\alpha}(\lambda_{2},t)}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,t)\|\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t)\|.

(5.27)

From (
5.26
) and (
5.4
) one can compute that

\displaystyle\|P(t)(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t))\|\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{\iota}\|T(t,\cdot)P(\cdot)\|\|B(\cdot)\|\|(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(\cdot))\hat{T}(\cdot,t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\delta K_{2}\hat{N}_{1}}{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta}_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\epsilon,t)}\|\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t)\|{\cal I}^{\alpha}_{\iota}E_{\alpha}(\hat{\beta}_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\epsilon,t)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\delta K_{2}\hat{N}_{1}}{\hat{\beta}_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\epsilon}\|\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t)\|,

(5.28)

where the chosen constant
\epsilon<\min\{\hat{\beta}_{1},\hat{\beta}_{2}\}
similarly.
Obviously,

\displaystyle\hat{P}(t)-P(t)=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Id}-P(t))\hat{P}(t)-P(t)(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t)).

Taking
\hat{N}:=\max\{\hat{N}_{1},\hat{N}_{2}\}
and
\hat{\beta}:=\min\{\hat{\beta}_{1},\hat{\beta}_{2}\}

and combining (
5.25
) with (
5.28
), we gain

\displaystyle\|\hat{P}(t)-P(t)\|\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\delta K_{1}\hat{N}_{2}}{\hat{\beta}_{2}+\lambda_{1}-\epsilon}\|\hat{P}(t)\|+\frac{\delta K_{2}\hat{N}_{1}}{\hat{\beta}_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\epsilon}\|\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}(\|\hat{P}(t)\|+\|\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t)\|),

(5.29)

where

\hat{\eta}=\cfrac{\delta\hat{N}^{2}\hat{\beta}}{2\hat{\beta}^{2}-5\delta\hat{N}\hat{\beta}-\epsilon(\hat{\beta}-2\delta\hat{N})}.

Moreover, by (
5.4
) with
t=s
, it is easy to obtain that

\displaystyle\|P(t)\|\leq\hat{N}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,t),~~~\|Q(t)\|\leq\hat{N}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,t).

From (
5.29
), this yields

\displaystyle\|\hat{P}(t)\|\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\|\hat{P}(t)-P(t)\|+\|P(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}(\|\hat{P}(t)\|+\|\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t)\|)+\hat{N}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,t).

Since
\|(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t))-(\mathrm{Id}-P(t))\|=\|\hat{P}(t)-P(t)\|
, we also derive

\displaystyle\|(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t))\|\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\|\hat{P}(t)-P(t)\|+\|\mathrm{Id}-P(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}(\|\hat{P}(t)\|+\|\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t)\|)+\hat{N}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,t).

They together imply that

\displaystyle\|\hat{P}(t)\|+\|\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t)\|\leq 2\hat{\eta}(\|\hat{P}(t)\|+\|\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t)\|)+2\hat{N}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,t),

and

\displaystyle\|\hat{P}(t)\|+\|\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t)\|\leq\frac{2\hat{N}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,t)}{1-2\hat{\eta}}.

Choose
\hat{\eta}<1/4
, then

\displaystyle\|\hat{P}(t)\|+\|\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(t)\|\leq 4\hat{N}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,t),

yielding Lemma
5.9
.

\qquad\Box

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

From (
5.24
) and (
5.22
), we show that

\displaystyle\|\hat{P}(\tau)\hat{T}(\tau,t)\|\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{N}\hat{\beta}}{\hat{\beta}-2\delta\hat{N}}\frac{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\lambda},t)}{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\lambda},\tau)}E_{\alpha}(\epsilon,t)\|\hat{P}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{4\hat{N}^{2}\hat{\beta}}{\hat{\beta}-2\delta\hat{N}}\frac{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\lambda},t)}{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\lambda},\tau)}E_{\alpha}(2\epsilon,t),~~~\tau\geq t\geq 0,

where

.
Analogously, it follows from (
5.27

5.22

\displaystyle\|(\mathrm{Id}-\hat{P}(\tau))\hat{T}(\tau,t)\|\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{4\hat{N}^{2}\hat{\beta}}{\hat{\beta}-2\delta\hat{N}}\frac{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\lambda},\tau)}{E_{\alpha}(\hat{\lambda},t)}E_{\alpha}(2\epsilon,t),~~~t\geq\tau\geq 0.

Therefore, we can acquire the desired inequalities like (
5.4
),
and the proof is completed.

Irreducible components of sets of points in the plane that satisfy distance conditions


Abstract
For a given graph whose edges are labeled with general real numbers, we consider the set of functions from the vertex set into the Euclidean plane such that the distance between the images of neighbouring vertices is equal to the corresponding edge label. This set of functions can be expressed as the zero set of quadratic polynomials and our main result characterizes the number of complex irreducible components of this zero set in terms of combinatorial properties of the graph. In case the complex components are three-dimensional, then the graph is minimally rigid and the component number is a well-known invariant from rigidity theory. If the components are four-dimensional, then they correspond to one-dimensional coupler curves of flexible planar mechanisms. As an application, we characterize the degree of irreducible components of such coupler curves combinatorially. Keywords: sets of points in the plane, minimally rigid graphs, rigidity theory, number of realizations, general edge length assignment, counting irreducible components, coupler curves, mechanical linkages, calligraphs MSC Class: 52C25, 70B15, 51K05, 51F99

Terminology needed for main results

Suppose that
P\colon X\to\{\text{True},~\text{False}\}
is a predicate about some variety
X

We say that

holds for a

point if
the set
\left\{x\in X:P(x)=\text{False}\right\}
is contained in a lower dimensional subvariety of
X
.

The
squared distance function

d\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is defined as

d(x,y)=(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}.

The
transformation group

T
is defined as the set of maps

{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2},\quad(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto(c\,x_{1}-s\,x_{2}+u,~s\,x_{1}+c\,x_{2}+v)

such that
c,s,u,v\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
c^{2}+s^{2}=1
.
We refer to elements of
T
as

Notice that transformations such that

define
the subgroup
\mathrm{SE}(2)\subset T
of directed isometries of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Indeed, for all
t\in T
,

d(x,y)=d(t(x),t(y)).

In this article a
graph

G=(V,E)
with
vertex set

V
and
edge set

E

is a simple undirected finite graph.
We call
G^{\prime}\subset G
a subgraph if
V^{\prime}\subset V
and
E^{\prime}\subset E
,
where the subset operators
\subset
and
\subseteq
are considered the same.

If
X
and
Y
are sets, then
Y^{X}
denotes the set of functions
X\to Y
and
for all
f\in Y^{X}
and
x\in X
, we denote the evaluation
f(x)
by
f_{x}
.
By abuse of notation, we denote

{\mathbb{C}}^{2V}:=({\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{V}.

We shall refer to elements of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2V}
and
{\mathbb{C}}^{E}
as
realizations
and
edge length assignments
, respectively.
The
edge map
of a graph
G=(V,E)
is defined as

\mathcal{E}_{G}\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{2V}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{E},\quad r\mapsto\bigl(\{u,v\}\mapsto d(r_{u},r_{v})\bigr).

The
set of
\lambda
-compatible realizations
of
G
for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{E}
is defined as the fiber

\mathcal{E}_{G}^{-1}(\lambda)=\left\{r\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2V}:d(r_{u},r_{v})=\lambda(\{u,v\})\text{ for all }\{u,v\}\in E\right\}.

If

, then
\mathcal{E}_{G}^{-1}({\mathbb{C}}^{\varnothing})={\mathbb{C}}^{2V}
.
The

for

is defined as the number of irreducible components of

.

We denote by

the orbit set, where

acts on
the set of realizations
{\mathbb{C}}^{2V}
as follows:

T\times{\mathbb{C}}^{2V}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2V},\quad(t,r)\mapsto t\circ r.

We call a graph
G

if there exists nonzero

such that for general
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{E}
,

We refer to

as the

of
G
.
We remark that if
G
is minimally rigid, then for general
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{E}

each irreducible component of

is a

-orbit and

Following the dictionary in
[
20
]
, we call a graph
G

sparse

if all its subgraphs
(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
such that
|V^{\prime}|\geq 2
satisfy

|E^{\prime}|\leq 2|V^{\prime}|-3.

We call a graph
G=(V,E)

if it is sparse and either

or

.

The following theorem of Pollaczek-Geiringer
[
23
]
and independently Laman
[

]

characterizes minimally rigid graphs in combinatorial terms.

Theorem A

(Geiringer, Laman)
.

A graph
G
is minimally rigid if and only if

is tight.

We call a subgraph
G^{\prime}\subset G

max-tight
if it is tight and there exists
no tight subgraph
G^{\prime\prime}
such that
G^{\prime}\subsetneq G^{\prime\prime}\subset G
.

We call
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}=\{(V_{i},E_{i})\}_{i\in I}
a
max-tight decomposition
for a graph
G=(V,E)

if
G_{i}\subset G
is a max-tight subgraph for all
i\in I
,

V=\cup_{i\in I}V_{i}
,
E=\cup_{i\in I}E_{i}

and
E_{i}\cap E_{j}=\varnothing
for all different
i,j\in I
.

See
[
9
]
for a

software implementation

of an algorithm that for a given graph outputs its max-tight decomposition.

Main results and examples

Our main result is that the component number of a sparse graph
with respect to a general edge length assignment
is equal to the product of the numbers of realizations of its max-tight subgraphs.

Theorem I
.

If
G=(V,E)
is a sparse graph and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{E}
a general edge length assignment,
then there exists a unique max-tight decomposition
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
for
G
and

c(G,\lambda)=\prod_{i\in I}c(G_{i}).

We remark that if
G
is not sparse and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{E}
a general edge length assignment,
then
c(G,\lambda)=0
as a straightforward consequence of
Theorem

A
.

If
\lambda
is a general edge length assignment of a graph
G
,
then any pair of
\lambda
-compatible realizations of
G

belong to the same component of the set of
\lambda
-compatible realizations
if and only if
for each max-tight
G^{\prime}\subset G
the induced pair of realizations of
G^{\prime}

are equal up to composition with transformations.

Corollary I
.

If
G=(V,E)
is a sparse graph,
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}=\{(V_{i},E_{i})\}_{i\in I}
its max-tight decomposition
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{E}
a general edge length assignment,
then the following are equivalent for all
\lambda
-compatible realizations
r
and
s
in
\mathcal{E}_{G}^{-1}(\lambda)
:

(i)

The realizations
r
and
s
belong to the same irreducible component of
\mathcal{E}_{G}^{-1}(\lambda)
.

(ii)

For all
i\in I
there exists a transformation
t\in T
such that
t\circ r|_{V_{i}}=s|_{V_{i}}
.

(iii)

For all
i\in I
, the restricted realizations
r|_{V_{i}}
and
s|_{V_{i}}

belong to the same irreducible component of
\mathcal{E}_{G_{i}}^{-1}(\lambda|_{E_{i}})
.

1
and
4

about the plane
H_{567}
, it is also possible to either

•

reflect vertex
2
about the plane
H_{134}
, or reflect vertex
3
about the plane
H_{124}
.

The latter two reflections result in two realizations
of

that are related via translations and rotations.

Notice that if we replace the subgraph

The generalization of

Appendix A
The fiber product lemma

The goal of this appendix is to prove
Lemma

10
.
For this we require scheme theoretic language, but
we provide precise references for the non-expert.

We assume that varieties are affine, but not necessarily irreducible.
If
X
is a variety,
then
{\mathbb{C}}[X]
and
{\mathbb{C}}(X)
denote the
function ring

and
function field
of
Y
, respectively
(see
[
15
, pages 4 and 16]
where function ring is called “coordinate ring”).
The
generic fiber
of a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
between irreducible varieties
X
and
Y

is defined as

\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{C}}[X]\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[Y]}{\mathbb{C}}(Y).

We refer to
[
15
, pages xvi, 16, 87 and 89, and Exercise II.2.7 at page 80]

or
[
21
, Definition 1.5 in Chapter 3]

for more details about the fiber at a “generic point”,
which is closely related to our notion of general point.

Lemma 27
.

Suppose that
f\colon X\to Y
is a dominant morphism between irreducible varieties.

(a)

The fiber product
X\times_{f}X
is irreducible
if and only if
the tensor product

{\mathbb{C}}[X]\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[Y]}{\mathbb{C}}[X]

is an integral domain.

(b)

The fiber
f^{-1}(y)
is irreducible for general
y\in Y

if and only if

{\mathbb{C}}[X]\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[Y]}{\mathbb{C}}(Y)

is an integral domain.

Proof.

Assertions
(a)
and
(b)
are a direct consequence of the
definitions.
∎

Lemma 28
.

If
R
is an
A
-module and
S\subset A
a multiplicatively closed subset,
then

R\otimes_{A}S^{-1}A\cong S^{-1}R.

Proof.

See for example
[
1
, Proposition 3.5]
for this well-known result.
∎

Lemma 29
.

If
{\mathbb{C}}[X]\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[Y]}{\mathbb{C}}[X]
is an integral domain for varieties
X
and
Y
,
then
{\mathbb{C}}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}(Y)}{\mathbb{C}}(X)

is an integral domain as well.

Proof.

If
R:={\mathbb{C}}[X]\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[Y]}{\mathbb{C}}[X]
, then its localization at the multiplicatively closed set
S=R\setminus\{0\}

is as a direct consequence of the definitions at
[
1
, Ch. 3]
equal to

S^{-1}R={\mathbb{C}}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}(Y)}{\mathbb{C}}(X).

Since
R
is an integral domain,
S^{-1}R
is an integral domain as well.
∎

Lemma 30
.

If
{\mathbb{E}}/{\mathbb{F}}
is a field extension such that
{\mathbb{E}}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{E}}
an integral domain,
and
e\in{\mathbb{E}}
is algebraic over
{\mathbb{F}}
, then
e\in{\mathbb{F}}
.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that
e\notin{\mathbb{F}}
.
Let
p(t)=\sum^{d}_{i=1}p_{i}\cdot t^{i}

be the minimal polynomial of
e

so that
d\geq 2
and
p_{d}=1
.
Since
p(e)=0
, we have

0=p(e)\otimes 1-1\otimes p(e)=\sum^{d}_{i=1}p_{i}\cdot\left(e^{i}\otimes 1-1\otimes e^{i}\right).

We observe that for all
1\leq i\leq d
,

x^{i}-y^{i}=(x-y)\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}x^{j}y^{i-1-j},

and thus

e^{i}\otimes 1-1\otimes e^{i}=(e\otimes 1-1\otimes e)\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}e^{j}\otimes e^{i-1-j}.

We establised that
\alpha\cdot\beta=0
, where

\alpha:=e\otimes 1-1\otimes e\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta:=\sum^{d}_{i=1}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}p_{i}\cdot e^{j}\otimes e^{i-1-j}.

Since the minimal polynomial
p(t)
is of degree
d
, we find that
the following set is linear independent:

\left\{e^{i}\otimes e^{j}:1\leq i,j\leq d-1\right\}.

This implies that
\beta\neq 0
,
and thus
\alpha
and
\beta
are zero divisors in
{\mathbb{E}}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{E}}
.
We arrived at a contradiction as
{\mathbb{E}}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{E}}
is an integral domain.
∎

Lemma 31
.

Suppose that
f\colon X\to Y
is a dominant morphism between varieties
and let
f^{\star}\colon{\mathbb{C}}(Y)\to{\mathbb{C}}(X)
be the homomorphism between function fields
that sends the function
\alpha
to the composition
\alpha\circ f
.
Let
{\mathbb{E}}:={\mathbb{C}}(X)
and
{\mathbb{F}}:=f^{\star}({\mathbb{C}}(Y))
so that
{\mathbb{E}}/{\mathbb{F}}
is a field extension.
If
{\mathbb{F}}
is algebraically closed in
{\mathbb{E}}
,
then
{\mathbb{C}}[X]\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[Y]}{\mathbb{C}}(Y)
is an integral domain.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that

R:={\mathbb{C}}[X]\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[Y]}{\mathbb{C}}(Y)
is not an integral domain.
In this case, the generic fiber of
f
decomposes into
k\geq 2
irreducible components:

\operatorname{Spec}R=C_{1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{k}.

By Sard’s theorem the generic fiber is smooth and thus its irreducible components coincide
with the connected components.
Let
r\in R
define the function
\operatorname{Spec}R\to{\mathbb{C}}

that sends
c
to
i
if
c\in C_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq k

and consider the polynomial

\tilde{p}:=(t-1)\cdots(t-k).

Notice that the composition
\tilde{p}\circ r
vanishes identically.
Moreover, we observe that
\tilde{p}\in{\mathbb{C}}(Y)[t]
is the minimal polynomial of
r
.
With the multiplicative set
S:={\mathbb{C}}[Y]\setminus\{0\}
, we find that

R={\mathbb{C}}[X]\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[Y]}S^{-1}{\mathbb{C}}[Y].

We know from
Lemma

28
that
R\cong S^{-1}{\mathbb{C}}[X]
.
Since
S^{-1}{\mathbb{C}}[X]\subset{\mathbb{C}}(X)={\mathbb{E}}
, there exists an injective ring homomorphism

\varphi\colon R\to{\mathbb{E}},

such that
\varphi({\mathbb{C}}(Y))={\mathbb{F}}
.
It follows that
\varphi(r)\in{\mathbb{E}}
has a nonzero minimal polynomial in
{\mathbb{F}}[t]
.
As
{\mathbb{F}}
is algebraically closed in
{\mathbb{E}}
by assumption, we established that
\varphi(r)\in{\mathbb{F}}
.
This implies that
\varphi(r)
defines a rational function
X\to{\mathbb{C}}

that is the composition of
f\colon X\to Y
with some function
\alpha\colon Y\to{\mathbb{C}}
.
We arrived at a contradiction as
(\alpha\circ f)(C_{i})=(\alpha\circ f)(C_{j})

although
r(C_{i})\neq r(C_{j})

for all
1\leq i<j\leq k
.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

10
.

It follows from
[
15
, Proposition III.10.4]

that
f\colon X\to Y
is a smooth morphism between irreducible affine varieties.

(a)
This assertion follows from
[
15
, Proposition III.10.1(d)]
.

(b)
Since
X\times_{f}X
is irreducible,
we know from
Lemma

(a)
that
{\mathbb{C}}[X]\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[Y]}{\mathbb{C}}[X]
is an integral domain.
Hence, it follows from
Lemma

29
that
{\mathbb{C}}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}(Y)}{\mathbb{C}}(X)
is an integral domain as well.
We apply
Lemma

30
with
{\mathbb{E}}={\mathbb{C}}(X)
and
{\mathbb{F}}=f^{\star}({\mathbb{C}}(Y))
,
and find that
{\mathbb{F}}
is algebraically closed in
{\mathbb{E}}
.
Therefore,
{\mathbb{C}}[X]\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[Y]}{\mathbb{C}}(Y)

∎

Appendix B
Invariants of components of fibers

The goal of this appendix is to prove
Propositions

33
and
III
, which concerns
algebro geometric invariants of irreducible components of sets
of realizations that are compatible with some general edge length assignment.
We assume the notation of Appendix
A
.

If
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is a variety, then its
dimension
,

degree
and
arithmetic genus
are denoted by
\dim X
,
\deg X
and
p_{a}(X)
,
respectively (see
[
15
, §I.7 and Exercise I.7.2]
).
If
X
is a curve, then we denote by
g(X)
its
geometric genus

[
15
, Remark II.8.18.2]
.

Lemma 32
.

If
f\colon X\to Y
is a dominant morphism between irreducible varieties,
then
{\mathbb{C}}[X]\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[Y]}{\mathbb{C}}(Y)
is an integral domain.

Proof.

Notice that
{\mathbb{C}}[X]
is a
{\mathbb{C}}[Y]
-module
via the pullback
f^{*}\colon{\mathbb{C}}[Y]\to{\mathbb{C}}[X]
.
By
Lemma

28
,
{\mathbb{C}}[X]\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[Y]}{\mathbb{C}}(Y)\cong S^{-1}{\mathbb{C}}[X]
,
where
S:={\mathbb{C}}[Y]\setminus\{0\}
is a multiplicatively closed set.
Since
{\mathbb{C}}[X]
is an integral domain, its localization is integral as well.
∎

Proposition 33
.

Suppose that
f\colon X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\to Y
is a dominant morphism between smooth and irreducible varieties
X
and
Y
.
Let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be different components of the fiber
f^{-1}(y)

for some general
y\in Y
.

(a)

C
is smooth,
C\cap C^{\prime}=\varnothing
,
\dim C=\dim C^{\prime}
,
\deg C=\deg C^{\prime}
and
p_{a}(C)=p_{a}(C^{\prime})
.

(b)

If
\dim C=1
,
\rho\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
is the projection to the first
m\leq n
coordinates and its restriction
\rho|_{f^{-1}(y)}

is birational, then

\deg\rho(C)=\deg\rho(C^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad g(\rho(C))=g(\rho(C^{\prime})).

Proof.

(a)

Since
f
is a morphism between differentiable manifolds,
it follows from Sard’s theorem that the general fiber of
f
is smooth,
which implies that
C
is smooth and
C\cap C^{\prime}=\varnothing
.
It follows from
Lemma

32
that the generic fiber

Z:=\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{C}}[X]\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[Y]}{\mathbb{C}}(Y)

is irreducible over the function field
{\mathbb{C}}(Y)
.
Suppose that
{\mathbb{E}}/{\mathbb{C}}(Y)
is an extension field such that
Z=Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}\cup\cdots\cup Z_{k}

decomposes into
k\geq 2
irreducible components,
where
Z_{i}
is defined by polynomials with coefficients in
{\mathbb{E}}
for all
1\leq i\leq k
.
By
[
15
, Proposition I.7.6 at page 52 and Exercise I.7.2 at page 54]

there exists for all
1\leq i\leq k
a unique
Hilbert polynomial

P_{Z_{i}}\in{\mathbb{C}}(Y)[t]

with
leading coefficient

\operatorname{lc}(P_{Z_{i}})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}

such that
\dim Z_{i}=\deg P_{Z_{i}}
,

\deg Z_{i}=(\deg P_{Z_{i}})!\cdot\operatorname{lc}(P_{Z_{i}})

and

p_{a}(Z_{i})=(-1)^{\deg P_{Z_{i}}}\cdot(P_{Z_{i}}(0)-1).

The Galois group of the field extension
{\mathbb{E}}/{\mathbb{C}}(Z)

induces a field isomorphism
{\mathbb{C}}(Z_{i})\to{\mathbb{C}}(Z_{j})
for all
1\leq i<j\leq k
.
It follows from the procedure for computing Hilbert polynomials
as described at
[
7
, §9.3]

that this field isomorphism identifies
P_{Z_{i}}
with
P_{Z_{j}}

so that

\deg P_{Z_{i}}=\deg P_{Z_{j}}
,
\operatorname{lc}(P_{Z_{i}})=\operatorname{lc}(P_{Z_{j}})

and
P_{Z_{i}}(0)=P_{Z_{j}}(0)
.
The proof is now concluded as a direct consequence of the definitions
of generic and general fibers.

(b)

The ideal of
\rho(Z)
is obtained from the ideal of
Z
by
elimination of variables (see
[
7
, §3.2,Theorem 3]
).
Since
Z
is irreducible over the function field
{\mathbb{C}}(Y)
,
we deduce that
\rho(Z)
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{C}}(Y)
as well.
It follows from
[
15
, Theorem II.8.19, Remark III.7.12.2 and Exercise IV.1.8]

that
g(\rho(C))=p_{a}(C)
.
The same arguments as before conclude the proof.
∎

The following simple example is to clarify

Proposition

33
and its proof.

Example 34
.

Suppose that
f\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
maps
(x,y,z)
to
(x^{2},z)
.
The generic fiber is defined as
\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{F}}[x,y,z]/\langle x^{2}-s,z-t\rangle
,
where
{\mathbb{F}}:=\left\{p/q:p,q\in{\mathbb{C}}[s,t],q\neq 0\right\}
denotes the
function field of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.
Although
x^{2}-s
is irreducible over the function field
{\mathbb{F}}
, it factors as
(x-u)(x+u)

over the extension field
{\mathbb{E}}:={\mathbb{F}}(u)/\langle u^{2}-s\rangle
.
The Galois group of this field extension
{\mathbb{E}}/{\mathbb{F}}
is generated by a field automorphism
that sends
u
to
-u
and leaves
{\mathbb{F}}
elementwise invariant.
If the projection
\rho\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
maps
(x,y,z)
to
(x,y)
,
then the image of the generic fiber of
f
is
\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{F}}[x,y]/\langle x^{2}-s\rangle
.
Hence, the image of the generic fiber is irreducible over the field
{\mathbb{F}}
.
This implies that the irreducible components
\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{E}}[x,y]/\langle x-u\rangle

and
\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{E}}[x,y]/\langle x+u\rangle

have the same dimension, degree and arithmetic genus.

Corollary III
.

Suppose that
G=(V,E)
is a sparse graph,

a general edge length assignment,

AS is grateful for support to the Russian Foundation for Basic Research, project Nr. 21-51-12006.
Anton Savin, Elmar Schrohe

Abstract
. We consider the operator algebra 𝒜 \mathscr{A} on 𝒮 ⁡ ( ℝ n ) \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) generated by the Shubin type pseudodifferential operators, the Heisenberg-Weyl operators and the lifts of the unitary operators on ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} to metaplectic operators. With the help of an auxiliary operator in the Shubin calculus, we find trace expansions for these operators in the spirit of Grubb and Seeley. Moreover, we can define a noncommutative residue generalizing that for the Shubin pseudodifferential operators and obtain a class of localized equivariant traces on the algebra.

58J40, 58J42

1.
Introduction

On the space
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
of Schwartz functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we study the algebra

\mathscr{A}
of all operators

(1.1)

\displaystyle D=\sum R_{g}T_{w}A,

where the sum is finite, the operators
A
are classical Shubin type pseudodifferential operators of integer order, the operators
T_{w}
,
w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, are Heisenberg-Weyl operators, acting on
u\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
by

T_{w}u(x)=e^{ikx-iak/2}u(x-a),\quad w=a-ik,

and finally the operators
R_{g}
,
g\in\Sp(n)\cap\mathrm{O}(2n)\cong\U(n)
are lifts of unitary operators on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\cong T^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to elements of the complex metaplectic group on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. More details will be given, below.
We have denoted by
\Sp(n)
and
\mathrm{O}(2n)
the groups of symplectic and orthogonal matrices, respectively, on
T^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and identified
T^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
via
(x,p)\mapsto p-ix
.

The group
\Mp(n)
of metaplectic operators on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is generated by the operators
S_{q}=e^{i\op^{w}q}
, where
\op^{w}q
is the Weyl quantization of a homogeneous quadratic form
q=q(x,p)
on
\mathbb{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
The operator
S_{q}
defines a symplectic map on
T^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by evaluating the Hamiltonian flow generated by the function
q
at time
1
. In fact, this yields a surjection

\pi:\Mp(n)\to\Sp(n)
.
It is well-known that this is a double covering.
For more details on the metaplectic group we refer to Leray
[
15
]
and de Gosson
[
6
]
as well as to
[
19
]
.

The complex metaplectic group
\Mp^{c}(n)
is generated by the operators
S_{q,\phi}=e^{i(\op^{w}q+\phi)}
with
q
as before and
\phi\in{\mathbb{R}}
. As shown in
[
19
, Proposition 1]
, the map

R:\U(n)\to\Mp^{c}(n),g\mapsto R_{g}=\pi^{-1}(g)\sqrt{\det g},

first defined for
g
close to the identity
g=I\in\U(n)
with the choices
\pi^{-1}(I)=I
and
\sqrt{\det I}=1
, extends continuously to all of
\U(n)
. These are the operators used above. The metaplectic operators form a special class of Fourier integral operators, compatible with the Shubin pseudodifferential operators in the sense that, for a Shubin symbol
a
and a metaplectic operator
S
,

S^{-1}\op^{w}(a)S=\op^{w}(a\circ\pi(S)).

This algebra has been studied before in
[
20
]
, where explicit formulae for the Chern-Connes character in the local index formula of Connes and Moscovici
[
4
]
were derived and applications to noncommutative tori and toric orbifolds were given.
The subalgebra without the Heisenberg-Weyl operators has been used in
[
19
]
in the context of index theory.
The main point of considering these algebras lies in the chance of being able to perform concrete computations, in particular in connection with index theory for operator algebras associated with groups of quantized canonical transformations as they were considered in
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
by the authors and B. Sternin or in
[
9
]
by Gorokhovsky, de Kleijn and Nest.

In the present article, we are interested in trace expansions.
We choose an elliptic auxiliary operator
H
of positive order in the Shubin calculus (a good choice would be the harmonic oscillator
H_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(|x|^{2}-\Delta)
), for which the resolvent
(H-\lambda)^{-1}
exists for large
\lambda
in a sector about the negative real axis.
Given an operator
D=R_{g}T_{w}A
as above, the operator
D(H-\lambda)^{-K}
will be of trace class for sufficiently large
K
. Moreover, the trace will be a holomorphic function of
\lambda
. We will show that, as
\lambda\to\infty
in the sector, we have an expansion

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Tr(R_{g}T_{w}A(H-\lambda)^{-K})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{j}(-\lambda)^{(2m+\ord A-j)/\ord H-K}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(c_{j}^{\prime}\ln(-\lambda)+c_{j}^{\prime\prime})(-\lambda)^{-j-K}

with suitable coefficients
c_{j},c_{j}^{\prime},c_{j}^{\prime\prime}
,
j=0,1,\ldots
. Here,
\ord A
and
\ord H
denote the orders of
A
and
H
, respectively, and
m
is the complex dimension of the fixed point set of
g
.
In this expansion, the coefficients
c_{j}
and
c_{j}^{\prime}
are ‘local’ in the sense that they can be determined from the terms in the asymptotic expansion of the symbols of
D
and
H
, respectively. They can therefore – at least in principle – be computed explicitly, while the
c_{j}^{\prime\prime}
are ’global’; they depend also on the residual part of the symbol and hence cannot be determined in general.
This can be seen as a generalization of the corresponding result by Grubb and Seeley
[
11
, Theorem 2.7]
.

The derivation of this expansion is the heart of the analysis. We obtain from it two other results. The first concerns the operator zeta function
\zeta_{R_{g}T_{w}A}(z)=\Tr(R_{g}T_{w}AH^{-z})
. Here, we additionally assume

(H-\lambda)^{-1}
to exist in the closed sector, including
\lambda=0
.
The zeta function then is defined and holomorphic for
\re z>(2n+\ord A)/\ord H
. Moreover, it has a meromorphic extension to the whole complex plane with at most simple poles. In fact, we have the pole structure

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Gamma(z)\zeta_{R_{g}T_{w}A}(z)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{c}_{j}}{z-(2m+\ord A-j)/\ord H}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\tilde{c}_{j}^{\prime}}{(z+j)^{2}}+\frac{\tilde{c}_{j}^{\prime\prime}}{z+j}\Big)

with suitable coefficients
\tilde{c}_{j},\tilde{c}_{j}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{c}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
related to those above by universal constants.
The analysis of such operator zeta functions goes back to Seeley’s classical article on complex powers of an elliptic operator
[
21
]
, where
\Tr(H^{-z})
was studied for an elliptic pseudodifferential operator
H
.
Grubb and Seeley in
[
12
]
also showed how this expansion can be derived from the expansion of the resolvent trace under rather mild conditions.

Assuming, moreover, that the sector in which the resolvent is holomorphic is larger than the left half plane, we can study the ‘heat trace’
\Tr\big(R_{g}T_{w}Ae^{-tH}\big)
. It exists for all
t>0
and has an expansion

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mbox{\;\;\;}\Tr(R_{g}T_{w}Ae^{-tH})\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\tilde{c}_{j}t^{(j-2m-\ord A)/\ord H}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-\tilde{c}^{\prime}_{j}\ln t+c_{j}^{\prime\prime})t^{j}.

Note that the coefficients coincide with those in (
1.3
).
Just as the result on operator zeta functions, this statement is a consequence of the resolvent expansion together with abstract arguments.

The coefficients
c_{0}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{c}_{0}^{\prime}
play a remarkable role in these expansions.
For one thing, they are the same; moreover, after multiplication by
\ord H
they are completely independent of the auxiliary operator
H
.
In fact they allow us to define an analog of the noncommutative residue for our algebra.
Restricted to the Shubin pseudodifferential operators it coincides with the residue introduced by
Boggiatto and Nicola
[
1
]
for a more general class of anisotropic pseudodifferential operators on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

We recall that the noncommutative residue for the algebra of classical pseudodifferential operators on a closed manifold, discovered independently by Wodzicki
[
23
]
and Guillemin
[
8
]
defines the unique trace – up to multiples – on this algebra (see e.g.
[
7
]
for a simple proof). The same is true for the residue of Boggiatto and Nicola.

Our algebra
\mathscr{A}
is the algebraic twisted crossed product of the algebra
\Psi
of all Shubin type pseudodifferential operators with the group
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\rtimes\U(n)
via the representations of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
by the operators
T_{w}
and of
\U(n)
by the operators
R_{g}
. Given a discrete subgroup

G
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\rtimes\U(n)
and an element
(w_{0},g_{0})
of
G
, we define the residue trace
of the operator
D=\sum R_{g}T_{w}A
localized at the conjugacy class
\langle(w_{0},g_{0})\rangle
by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\res_{\langle(w_{0},g_{0})\rangle}D=\ord H\sum_{(w,g)\in\langle(w_{0},g_{0})\rangle}c_{0}^{\prime}(R_{g}T_{w}A)

and show that these are actually traces on the algebra
\mathscr{A}
.

The present article in this sense continues the work of Dave
[
5
]
. He defined equivariant noncommutative residues for the algebra
\Psi_{\rm cl}(M)\rtimes\Gamma
of operators on a closed manifold
M
, generated by the classical pseudodifferential operators and a finite group
\Gamma
of diffeomorphisms of
M
and computed the cyclic homology in terms of the de Rham cohomology of the fixed point manifolds
S^{*}M^{g}
.
In the same vein, Perrot
[
16
]
studied the local index theory for shift operators associated to non-proper and non-isometric actions of Lie groupoids; he also computed the residue of the corresponding operator zeta functions in zero.

2.
The Operators

Shubin type pseudodifferential operators

A smooth function
a
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is called a (Shubin type) pseudodifferential
symbol of order
{\mathfrak{a}}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, provided that, for all multi-indices
\alpha
,
\beta
, it
satisfies the estimates

D^{\alpha}_{p}D^{\beta}_{x}a(x,p)\lesssim(1+|x|^{2}+|p|^{2})^{({\mathfrak{a}}-|\alpha|-|\beta|)/2},\quad(x,p)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

From such a symbol, we can define the associated pseudodifferential operators
\op a
and

\op^{w}a
, respectively, by standard and Weyl quantization.

By
\Psi^{\mathfrak{a}}
we denote the space of all Shubin type pseudodifferential operators of order
{\mathfrak{a}}
and by
\Psi
the union over all
{\mathfrak{a}}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
An important example is the operator

(2.1)

\displaystyle H_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(|x|^{2}-\Delta)\in\Psi^{2},

the so-called harmonic oscillator, which is selfadjoint and positive on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

These operators act on the Sobolev spaces
\mathcal{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, consisting of all tempered distributions
u
such that
\op((1+|x|^{2}+|p|^{2})^{s/2})u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
: In fact,
\Psi^{\mathfrak{a}}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{B}({\mathcal{H}}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),{\mathcal{H}}^{s-{\mathfrak{a}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
for all
s,{\mathfrak{a}}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. In particular, zero order operators
are bounded on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and those of order
<-2n
are of trace class.

We will work here mostly with classical symbols, i.e. symbols
a
of order
{\mathfrak{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
that have an asymptotic expansion

a\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{{\mathfrak{a}}-j}

with
a_{{\mathfrak{a}}-j}
smooth and positively homogeneous in
(x,p)
of degree
{\mathfrak{a}}-j
for

|(x,p)|\geq 1
. We call
a_{\mathfrak{a}}
the principal symbol and write
\Psi^{\mathfrak{a}}_{\rm cl}

and
\Psi_{\rm cl}
for the classes of operators with classical symbols, respectively.

A Egorov type theorem holds:
Given an element
S
in the metaplectic group
\Mp(n)
and

A={\rm op}^{w}a
a Weyl-quantized classical Shubin pseudodifferential operator with symbol
a
, then
S^{-1}AS

is the Weyl-quantized Shubin pseudodifferential operator with symbol
a\circ\pi(S)
, where
\pi(S)\in\Sp(n)
is the canonical transformation associated with
S
, see

[
6
, Theorem 7.13]
. As the principal symbol of
{\rm op}^{w}(a)
coincides with that of
{\rm op}(a)
, we find the following relation for principal symbols:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sigma(S^{-1}AS)=\sigma(A)\circ\pi(S).

The above operators need not be scalar, it is often important to also consider systems of operators over
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, see e.g.
[
20
]
.

Heisenberg-Weyl operators.

For
w=a-ik\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
a,k\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define

(2.3)

\displaystyle T_{w}u(x)=e^{ikx-iak/2}u(x-a).

We note that for
v,w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

(2.4)

T_{v}T_{w}=e^{-i\im(v,w)/2}T_{v+w},\quad\text{ where }(v,w)=v\overline{w}.

Metaplectic operators.

Since
\U(n)
is generated by
{\rm O}(n)
and
\U(1)
, see
[
19
, Lemma 1]
, we can define a
unitary representation
g\mapsto R_{g}
of
\U(n)
by complex metaplectic operators

R_{g}\in\U(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
by the following two assignments:

(i)

R_{g}u(x)=u(g^{-1}x)
, if
g\in{\rm O}(n)\subset\U(n)
. Then
R_{g}
is a so-called shift operator;

(ii)

R_{g}u(x)=e^{i\varphi(1/2-H_{1})}u(x)
, where
g={\rm diag}(e^{i\varphi},1,\ldots,1),
and
H_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(x_{1}^{2}-\partial_{x_{1}}^{2})
is the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator.

R_{g}
is a so-called fractional Fourier transform with respect to
x_{1}
. According to Mehler’s formula
[
13
, Section 4]
it is given by

\displaystyle R_{g}u(x)=\sqrt{\frac{1-i\ctg\varphi}{2\pi}}

\displaystyle\times\int\exp\left(i\left((x_{1}^{2}+y_{1}^{2})\frac{\ctg\varphi}{2}-\frac{x_{1}y_{1}}{\sin\varphi}\right)\right)u(y_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})dy_{1}.

Here we choose the square root so that the real part is positive.

In (ii) we may assume that
0<\varphi<2\pi
,
\varphi\not=\pi
, as the cases
\varphi=0,\pi
are covered by (i): For
\varphi=0
,
R_{g}
is the identity, and for
\varphi=\pi
, it is the reflection in the first component. Moreover, for
g=(\exp(i\pi/2),1,\ldots,1)
,
R_{g}
coincides with the Fourier transform in the first variable, see the discussion on p. 427 in
[
13
]
.
By Theorem 7.13 in
[
6
]

(2.5)

R_{g}T_{w}R_{g}^{-1}=T_{gw}.

3.
Main Results

Let
E
be a (trivial) vector bundle over
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
h
a classical Shubin symbol of order
{\mathfrak{m}}>0
whose principal symbol is positive and scalar, i.e. a multiple of
id_{E}
, positively homogeneous of degree
{\mathfrak{m}}
for
|x|^{2}+|p|^{2}\geq 1
. Denote by
H
the associated pseudodifferential operator acting on sections of
E
.

For
0<\delta\leq\pi
write
S_{\delta}
for the sector

(3.1)

\displaystyle S_{\delta}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\mid|\arg\lambda-\pi|<\delta\}

and
U_{r}(0)
for the disk of radius
r>0
about
0\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
Standard pseudodifferential techniques show:

Proposition 3.1
.

For every
\delta<\pi
,

H-\lambda
is invertible for all
\lambda\in S_{\delta}
with
|\lambda|
sufficiently large.
The function
\lambda\mapsto(H-\lambda)^{-1}
is meromorphic in
S_{\delta}\cup U_{r}(0)
for suitably small

r>0
.
In particular, we may assume that
H-\lambda
is invertible for all

\lambda\in S_{\delta}
after replacing
H
by
H+c
for suitable
c>0
.

We shall establish the following results:

Theorem 3.2
.

Fix
g\in\U(n)
,
w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, and a pseudodifferential operator
A\in\Psi
of order
\ord A
.
Choose
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
so large that
-K\ord H+\ord A<-2n
and fix
\delta<\pi
. Then

(a)

The operator
R_{g}T_{w}A(H-\lambda)^{-K}
is of trace class for all
\lambda
in
S_{\delta}
,
|\lambda|
sufficiently large.

(b)

The function
\lambda\mapsto\Tr(R_{g}T_{w}A(H-\lambda)^{-K})
is meromorphic in
S_{\delta}
and near
\lambda=0
. It is
O(|\lambda|^{-\varepsilon})
with suitable
\varepsilon>0
for
\lambda\to\infty
in
S_{\delta}
.

(c)

As
\lambda\to\infty
in the sector
S_{\delta}
we have the expansion (
1.2
).

(d)

If the fixed point set of the affine mapping
\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n},v\mapsto gv+w
is empty, then the trace is
O(\lambda^{-\infty})
.

(e)

The coefficient
c_{0}^{\prime}
is independent of the choice of
H
(subject to the properties stated above) when multiplied by the factor
\ord H
.

A sketch of the proof of Theorem
3.2
will be given in Section
4
, below.
Statements (a) and (b) follow immediately from Proposition
3.1
and the fact that the embedding
{\mathcal{H}}^{2n+\varepsilon}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

is trace class for every
\varepsilon>0
. Deriving the expansion (
1.2
) in (c) is the crucial task.
Statements (d) and (e) will be proven along the way of showing (c).

As a consequence, we obtain two more results. Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
follow from Theorem
3.2
with the help of
Propositions 2.9 and 5.1 in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

With the notation of Theorem
3.2

assume, moreover, that
H-\lambda
is invertible for all
\lambda\in S_{\delta}\cup U_{r}(0)
for some
r>0
. Then

(a)

The function

z\mapsto\zeta_{R_{g}T_{w}A}(z):=\Tr(R_{g}T_{w}AH^{-z})
is defined and holomorphic in the half-plane
\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\ord H\re z>2n+\ord A\}
.

(b)

\zeta_{R_{g}T_{w}A}
has a meromorphic continuation to
{\mathbb{C}}
with at most simple poles
in the points
(2m+\ord A-j)/\ord H
, where
m
is the complex dimension of the
fixed point set of
g
.
The function
\Gamma\zeta_{R_{g}T_{w}A}
has the pole structure (
1.3
).

(c)

The coefficients
\tilde{c}_{j},\tilde{c}_{j}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{c}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
in (
1.3
) are related to the coefficients
c_{j},c_{j}^{\prime}
and
c_{j}^{\prime\prime}
in (
1.2
) by universal constants. In particular, we have

\mathrm{Res}_{z=0}\zeta_{R_{g}T_{w}A}=c_{0}^{\prime}=\tilde{c}_{0}^{\prime}.

(d)

If the fixed point set of the affine mapping
\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n},v\mapsto gv+w
is empty, then
\zeta_{R_{g}T_{w}A}
has no poles.

(e)

\zeta_{R_{g}T_{w}A}
has rapid decay along vertical lines
z=c+it
,
t\in\mathbb{R}:

(3.2)

|\zeta_{R_{g}T_{w}A}(z)\Gamma(z)|\leq C_{N}(1+|z|)^{-N},\quad\text{for all }N\geq 0,\quad|\im z|\geq 1,

uniformly for
c
in compact intervals.

Theorem 3.4
.

We use the notation in Theorem
3.2
and assume additionally that
H-\lambda
is invertible for
\lambda\in\overline{S}_{\delta}
for some
\delta\in{]\pi/2,\pi]}
. Then:

(a)

The function
t\mapsto\Tr(R_{g}T_{w}A\exp(-tH))
is defined for all
t>0
.

(b)

As
t\to 0^{+}
it has the asymptotic expansion (
1.4
)
with the same coefficients as for the zeta function in Theorem
3.3
.

The noncommutative residue

Let
G
be a subgroup of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\rtimes\U(n)
.

Definition 3.5
.

For
w_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
,
g_{0}\in\U(n)
such that
(w_{0},g_{0})\in G
we define the noncommutative residue

\res_{\langle(w_{0},g_{0})\rangle}

localized at the conjugacy class
\langle(w_{0},g_{0})\rangle

in
G
by

\displaystyle\res_{\langle(w_{0},g_{0})\rangle}D=\ord H\sum_{(w,g)\in\langle(w_{0},g_{0})\rangle}\mathrm{Res}_{z=0}\zeta_{A,g,w}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ord H\sum_{(w,g)\in\langle(w_{0},g_{0})\rangle}c_{0}^{\prime}(R_{g}T_{w}A)=\ord H\sum_{(w,g)\in\langle(w_{0},g_{0})\rangle}\tilde{c}_{0}^{\prime}(R_{g}T_{w}A)

with the coefficients
c_{0}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{c}_{0}^{\prime}
introduced in Theorem

3.2
and Theorem
3.3
, respectively,
and the order
\ord H
of
H
. The expression is independent of
H
by Theorem
3.2
(e).

While the definition in terms of the zeta residue or the coefficient in the heat trace expansion is more common, that in terms of the coefficient in the resolvent expansion is slightly more general, since we need less assumptions on the auxiliary operator to define it.

One can compute the residue explicitly. Consider the case of diagonal
g
:

(3.3)

g={\rm diag}\Bigl(\underbrace{e^{i\varphi_{1}},...,e^{i\varphi_{m_{1}}}}_{m_{1}},\underbrace{i,...,i}_{m_{2}},\underbrace{-i,...,-i}_{m_{3}},\underbrace{-1,...,-1}_{m_{4}},\underbrace{1,...,1}_{m_{5}}\Bigr)\in\U(n),

where
\varphi_{j}\in{]-\pi,\pi[}\setminus\pi\mathbb{Z}/2
and
m_{5}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{g}
.
For notational convenience we will also write
\varphi_{j}=\pi/2
for

j=m_{1}+1,\ldots,m_{1}+m_{2}
and
\varphi_{j}=-\pi/2
for
j=m_{1}+m_{2}+1,\ldots,m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

For
D=R_{g}T_{w}A
with
g
as above,

w=a-ik\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
A\in\Psi^{-2m_{5}}
we find the explicit formula:

(3.4)

q=q(y,p;\mu)

of
A(H+\mu^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K}
can be written as a finite sum of terms of the form

(4.10)

\displaystyle d(y,p)(h_{\mathfrak{m}}(y,p)+\mu^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K-\ell},

where
d
is homogenous in
(y,p)
of degree
{\mathfrak{d}}={\mathfrak{a}}+\ell{\mathfrak{m}}-j
for
j=0,\ldots,N-1
,
plus a remainder in
S^{-N,-K{\mathfrak{m}}}\cap S^{-N-K{\mathfrak{m}},0}
.

(b)

Each
d
is a polynomial in derivatives of the homogeneous components in the symbol expansions of
a
and
h
. If
\ell=0
in
(a)
, then
d
can only be one of the homogeneous components of
a
.

(c)
The statement corresponding to
(a)
is also true for the symbol
\tilde{q}
. Here,

d
is additionally scalar, since we take the fiber trace in
E
.

(d)

In the expansion of
\tilde{q}(Bw-\tilde{b}_{0};\mu)
into homogeneous components, the only possible coefficient functions of
(h_{\mathfrak{m}}(\tilde{B}w)+\mu^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K}
are of the form

\frac{(-\tilde{b}_{0})^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\partial_{w}^{\alpha}\tr_{E}a_{{\mathfrak{a}}-j}(\tilde{B}w)

for suitable
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and a multi-index
\alpha
.

So we are led to considering integrals of the form

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n^{\prime}}}e^{i\phi(w)}d(w)(h_{\mathfrak{m}}(w)+\mu^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K-\ell}\,dw

with
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
d
homogeneous of
degree
{\mathfrak{d}}
for
|w|\geq 1
.

Similarly as Grubb and Seeley in their analysis of weakly parametric symbols

[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
, we split the integral over
{\mathbb{R}}^{n^{\prime}}
into the integrals
over
\{|w|\leq 1\}
,
\{1\leq|w|\leq|\mu|\}
, and
\{|w|\geq|\mu|\}
.
It is not difficult to see that the integral over
|w|\leq 1

produces powers
\mu^{-(K+\ell+j){\mathfrak{m}}}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, using a binomial series expansion of the integrand. These terms also contribute to the terms
c_{j}^{\prime\prime}(-\lambda)^{-K-j}
in (
1.2
), even though they are ’local’.

For the other two integrals we introduce polar coordinates and use the fact that

\phi
is 2-homogeneous and

d
is homogeneous of degree
{\mathfrak{d}}
.

Step 4. The integral over
|w|\geq|\mu|

Letting
w/|\mu|=v=r\theta
we obtain

(4.12)

\displaystyle\int_{|w|\geq|\mu|}e^{i\phi(w)}d(w)(h_{\mathfrak{m}}(w)+\mu^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K-\ell}\,dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\mu|^{{\mathfrak{d}}-{\mathfrak{m}}(K+\ell)+n^{\prime}}\int_{|v|\geq 1}e^{i\phi(v)|\mu|^{2}}d(v)(h_{\mathfrak{m}}(v)+(\mu/|\mu|)^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K-\ell}\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\mu|^{{\mathfrak{d}}-{\mathfrak{m}}(K+\ell)+n^{\prime}}\int_{1}^{\infty}I(\hbar,r,\omega)_{|\hbar=(|\mu|^{2}r^{2})^{-1}}r^{{\mathfrak{d}}+n^{\prime}-1}\,dr

with

\displaystyle I(\hbar,r,\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n^{\prime}-1}}e^{i\phi(\theta)/\hbar}d(\theta)(h_{\mathfrak{m}}(r\theta)+\omega^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K-\ell}\,dS(\theta).

The stationary phase approximation, see e.g.
[
24
, Theorem 3.16]
, yields an expansion
for this integral:
Since
\phi(\theta)=\sum_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\theta^{2}_{j}
, the stationary
points on the sphere are the unit length eigenvectors of the matrix

\text{\rm diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n^{\prime}})
. We denote by
\{\mu_{l}\}
the set of
different

\lambda_{j}
and by
\varkappa_{l}
the multiplicity of
\mu_{l}
.
To fix the notation let us assume that

\mu_{1}=0
so that, by (
3.3
),
\varkappa_{1}=2\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}^{n}_{g}
is the real dimension of the fixed point set of
g\in\U(n)
.
The set of stationary points therefore
is the disjoint union of the spheres
\mathbb{S}^{\varkappa_{l}-1}
in the eigenspaces for
\mu_{l}
.
Hence we find:

For
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(4.13)

\displaystyle I(\hbar,r,\omega)

\displaystyle\mbox{\;\;\;\;}=

\displaystyle\sum_{J=0}^{N-1}\hbar^{J+\frac{n^{\prime}-\varkappa_{l}}{2}}\sum_{l}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\mu_{l}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{\varkappa_{l}-1}}A_{2J}(\theta^{\prime},\partial_{\theta^{\prime}})\left(d(\theta)(h_{\mathfrak{m}}(r\theta)+\omega^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K-\ell}\right)\,dS

\displaystyle+O\Big(\sum_{l}\hbar^{N+\frac{n^{\prime}-\varkappa_{l}}{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2N+n^{\prime}-\varkappa_{l}+1}\sup|\partial^{\alpha}_{\theta^{\prime}}\big(d(\theta)(h_{\mathfrak{m}}(r\theta)+\omega^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K-\ell}\big)|\Big)

with differential operators
A_{2J}
of order
2J
in the variables
\theta^{\prime}
transversal to the fixed point set.

We insert this expression for
I(\hbar,r,\omega)
with
\hbar=r^{-2}|\mu|^{-2}
into the integral over
r
.
The contribution of the remainder term turns out to be a low power of
|\mu|
as desired.
The terms in the expansion, however, give us expressions of the form

(4.14)

\displaystyle e^{i\mu_{l}|\mu|^{2}}|\mu|^{-(K+\ell){\mathfrak{m}}-j}g_{j}(\mu/|\mu|)

for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and smooth functions
g_{j}
.
This is not quite what we would like to obtain:
There should be no exponential terms with
\mu_{l}\not=0
and, moreover, we would like to have expressions in
\mu
(and its powers), not in
|\mu|
and
\mu/|\mu|
. We shall address this problem in Step 6.

Step 5. The integral over
1\leq|w|\leq|\mu|

This is the part with the most delicate analysis.
We first expand
(h_{\mathfrak{m}}+\mu^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K-\ell}

into a finite number of powers of
h_{\mathfrak{m}}\mu^{-{\mathfrak{m}}}
and a remainder term.
Proceeding similarly as in Step 4, we have to treat terms of the form

(4.15)

\displaystyle\mu^{-(K+\ell){\mathfrak{m}}-k{\mathfrak{m}}}\int_{1}^{|\mu|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n^{\prime}-1}}e^{ir^{2}\phi(\theta)}d(\theta)h_{\mathfrak{m}}(\theta)^{k}\,dS\,r^{{\mathfrak{d}}+{\mathfrak{m}}k+n^{\prime}-1}\,dr

and the corresponding integrals over the remainder terms.
An application of the stationary phase approximation with
\hbar=r^{-2}

for the integral over the sphere results in terms

(4.16)

\displaystyle\mu^{-(K+\ell){\mathfrak{m}}-k{\mathfrak{m}}}c_{l,J}\sum_{l}\sum_{J=0}^{N-1}\int_{1}^{|\mu|}e^{ir^{2}\mu_{l}}\,r^{{\mathfrak{d}}+{\mathfrak{m}}k-2J+\varkappa_{l}-1}\,dr

with

(4.17)

\displaystyle c_{l,J}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{\varkappa_{l}-1}}A_{2J,l}(\theta^{\prime},\partial_{\theta^{\prime}})(d(\theta)h_{\mathfrak{m}}(\theta)^{k})\,dS

plus a remainder term that is easy to handle.

For
\mu_{l}\not=0
integration by parts in (
4.16
) yields, as in Step 4,
terms of the form (
4.14
) above, but
here – and only here – we also encounter logarithmic terms, namely
for the eigenvalue
\mu_{1}=0
, when

(4.18)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{d}}+{\mathfrak{m}}k-2J+\varkappa_{1}=0.

Note from (
4.16
) that the
\mu
-powers associated with log-terms are always integer multiples of
{\mathfrak{m}}
as stated in (
1.2
).

Of particular interest is the coefficient of
\mu^{-K{\mathfrak{m}}}\ln|\mu|
, since this yields the noncommutative residue.
It is immediate from (
4.16
) that it can only arise for
\ell=k=0
.
Together with Proposition
4.4
, this gives us a wealth of information:
By
4.4
(b), the only terms with
\ell=0
in the parametrix construction yielding the symbol of
q
are of there form
a_{{\mathfrak{a}}-j}(h_{\mathfrak{m}}+\mu^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K}
for some term
a_{{\mathfrak{a}}-j}
in the asymptotic expansion of the symbol of
A
.
For
\tr_{E}q(\tilde{B}w-\tilde{b}_{0};\mu)
,
4.4
(d) shows that there may be contributions from the Taylor expansion, leading to terms
(-\tilde{b}_{0})^{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}_{w}\tr_{E}(a_{{\mathfrak{a}}-j}(\tilde{B}w)(h_{\mathfrak{m}}(\tilde{B}w)+\mu^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K})/\alpha!
.
However, none of the derivatives must fall on
(h_{\mathfrak{m}}(\tilde{B}w)+\mu^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K}
, for then we would have
\ell\not=0
.
Hence
d
does not contain factors involving terms
h_{{\mathfrak{m}}-j}
from the asymptotic expansion of
h
or their derivatives.
The fact that
k
equals
0
moreover implies that the integral (
4.15
), and hence the coefficient of
\mu^{-K}\ln|\mu|
, is independent of the choice of the auxiliary operator
H
.
This proves statement (e) in
Theorem
3.2
: The fact that

\ln(-\lambda)=\ln(\mu^{\mathfrak{m}})={\mathfrak{m}}\ln\mu

causes a factor
1/{\mathfrak{m}}=1/\ord H
in the expansion with respect to
-\lambda
.

Finally, one has to treat the remainder terms in the expansion of
(h_{\mathfrak{m}}+\mu^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K-\ell}
. The analysis is slightly more involved, but similar to the above, and also produces terms of the form (
4.14
).

Step 6. Correcting the expansion

We have an expansion of
\Tr(R_{g}T_{w}A(H+\mu^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K})

not only into powers of
\mu
and
\ln\mu
, but also in powers of
|\mu|
times factors
of the form
e^{i|\mu|^{2}\mu_{l}}g_{l}(\mu/|\mu|)
,
\mu_{l}\not=0
, or
h(\mu/|\mu|)
that are not of the form stated in Theorem
3.2
.
This is analogous to a phenomenon already observed by Grubb and Seeley. They excluded this possibility in
[
11
, Lemma 2.3]
.
An argument along similar lines shows that the expansion only contains the terms listed in (
1.2
). In particular, the terms involving factors
e^{i|\mu|^{2}\mu_{l}}
for
\mu_{l}\not=0
necessarily add up to zero.

This last fact has also been established before in the context of the analysis of the zeta function

\Tr(R_{g}T_{w}AH^{-z})
in
[
20
, Theorem 5]
.

Step 7. Conclusion.

As the terms involving the eigenvalues
\mu_{l}\not=0
all cancel and, moreover, only powers of
\mu
and
\ln\mu
occur, we see that we obtain the expansion (
1.2
):
In fact, we saw already how the terms
(c_{j}^{\prime}\ln(-\lambda)+c_{j}^{\prime\prime})^{-K-j}
arise.
As for the terms
c_{j}(-\lambda)^{(2m+{\mathfrak{a}}-j)/{\mathfrak{m}}-K}
: they result from inserting the terms for
\mu_{l}=0
in the stationary phase expansion (e.g. in (
4.13
) with
\hbar=r^{-2}|\mu|^{-2}
) into the corresponding integral (
4.12
), noting that
\varkappa_{1}=2m
.
This completes our sketch.
\Box

5.
The Noncommutative Residue

In Section
4
we saw that there is only one point, where logarithmic terms appear, namely in the analysis of the integral (
4.15
).
We also observed that, as a consequence of Proposition
4.4
, the only possible coefficient functions of
(h_{\mathfrak{m}}(\tilde{B}w)+\mu^{\mathfrak{m}})^{-K}
in the expansion of
\tilde{q}(\tilde{B}w-\tilde{b}_{0})
are the functions

(5.1)

\displaystyle\frac{(-\tilde{b}_{0})^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\partial_{w}^{\alpha}\tr_{E}a_{{\mathfrak{a}}-j}(\tilde{B}w)

for some choice of
j
and
\alpha
. In addition, the application of the stationary phase expansion may add further derivatives
A_{2J,1}(\theta^{\prime},\partial_{\theta^{\prime}})
of order
2J
on
a_{{\mathfrak{a}}-j}
as we saw in (
4.16
) and (
4.17
).

Next, Equation (
4.18
) implies that a nonzero contribution to
\mu^{-K}\ln|\mu|
will only arise if the homogeneity degree satisfies

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{a}}-j-2J+\varkappa_{1}=0,

with
\varkappa_{1}=2m_{5}
.

In order to understand the contribution to the noncommutative residue, we distinguish the cases where
{\mathfrak{a}}=\ord A=-2m_{5}
,
{\mathfrak{a}}<-2m_{5}
or
{\mathfrak{a}}>-2m_{5}
.

(i) Let
{\mathfrak{a}}=-2m_{5}
. According to (
5.2
), the only possibility for obtaining a nontrivial contribution to the
\mu^{-K}\ln|\mu|
-term then is when
j=0
and
\alpha=0
in (
5.1
) and, moreover,
J=0
.
Setting
k=\ell=J=0
and
l=1
in (
4.16
) and (
4.17
) shows that the coefficient is

(5.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2m_{5}-1}}\tr_{E}a_{-2m_{5}}(\tilde{B}\theta)dS.

On the other hand, we know that
\tilde{B}
is the identity on
(x_{n-m_{5}+1},p_{n-m_{5}+1},\ldots x_{n},p_{n})
and that, on
\mathbb{S}^{2m_{5}-1}
the other variables are zero. Hence
(
5.3
) reduces to

(5.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2m_{5}-1}}\tr_{E}a_{-2m_{5}}(\theta)\,dS

as asserted.

(ii) The same argument shows that the residue term vanishes if
\ord A<-2m_{5}
.

(iii) If
\ord A>-2m_{5}
, then we reconsider Equation (
4.15
).
The standard application of the stationary phase expansion does not yield explicit expressions; therefore we take another approach.
We know that there will be no contribution to the
\mu^{-K}\ln|\mu|
-term unless
k=\ell=0
in (
4.15
).

For
\varkappa_{1}=2m_{5}
we have

\mathbb{S}^{2m_{5}-1}=\{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n^{\prime}-1}:\theta^{\prime}=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n^{\prime}-2m_{5}})=0\}.

We introduce a parametrization of a tubular neighborhood of
\mathbb{S}^{2m_{5}-1}
in

\mathbb{S}^{n^{\prime}-1}
as follows: We first choose a parametrization

\Phi:D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2m_{5}-1}\to\mathbb{S}^{2m_{5}-1}
(e.g. the usual spherical coordinates)
and then define, for small
\varepsilon>0
and
W_{\varepsilon}=[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]^{n^{\prime}-2m_{5}}
, the parametrization

F:W_{\varepsilon}\times D\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n^{\prime}},\quad F(\theta^{\prime},\omega)=(\theta^{\prime},\Phi(\omega)\sqrt{1-|\theta^{\prime}|^{2}}).

The associated measure is
(1-|\theta^{\prime}|^{2})^{m_{5}-1}d\theta^{\prime}dS
with the surface measure
dS
of

\mathbb{S}^{2m_{5}-1}
.

Up to terms that furnish
O(\mu^{-\infty})
contributions, the integral in (
4.15
) with
k=0
,
l=1
and
d
in (
5.1
) of homogeneity degree
{\mathfrak{d}}={\mathfrak{a}}-j-|\alpha|
is

(5.5)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{|\mu|}\int_{D}\int_{W_{\varepsilon}}e^{ir^{2}\langle Q\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle}d(\theta^{\prime},\Phi(\omega)\sqrt{1-|\theta^{\prime}|^{2}})\,d\theta^{\prime}dS\ r^{{\mathfrak{d}}+n^{\prime}-1}\,dr,

where
Q={\diag}(\tilde{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\lambda}_{n^{\prime}-2m_{5}})
with
\tilde{\lambda}_{j}
introduced after (
4.7
).

The advantage of this representation is that we may apply to the inner integral the explicit version of the stationary phase expansion for quadratic exponentials with
\hbar=r^{-2}
, see
[
24
, Theorem 3.13]
:

\displaystyle\int e^{\frac{i}{h}\langle Q\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle}d(\theta^{\prime},\Phi(\omega)\sqrt{1-|\theta^{\prime}|^{2}})\,d\theta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{h}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{n^{\prime}-2m_{5}}{2}}\frac{e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\sgn Q}}{|\det Q|^{1/2}}\left(\sum_{J=0}^{N-1}\frac{h^{J}}{J!}\left(\frac{\langle Q^{-1}D,D\rangle}{2i}\right)^{J}d(0,\Phi(\omega))+O(h^{N})\right).

Since
\hbar=r^{-2}
, this furnishes an expansion into decreasing powers of
r
, which we then insert into (
5.5
).
When evaluating the integral over
[1,|\mu|]
, we will only get a nontrivial contribution to the
\mu^{-K}\ln|\mu|
-term, if the total
r
-power is
-1
.
In the case at hand, the possible powers are
({\mathfrak{d}}+n^{\prime}-1)+(-n^{\prime}+2m_{5})-2J={\mathfrak{a}}-j-|\alpha|+2m_{5}-2J-1
. Hence only those (finitely many) terms, where

{\mathfrak{a}}-j-|\alpha|-2J=-2m_{5}

can contribute. For these we obtain an explicit formula.

A further simplification comes from the fact that for
f
positively homogeneous of degree
-2m_{5}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2m_{5}-1}}f(\theta)\,dS=0

whenever
f
is a derivative of a positively homogeneous function of degree
-2m_{5}+1
, see
[
7
, Lemma 1.2]
.
When considering the derivatives
\partial_{w}^{\alpha}\tr_{E}a_{{\mathfrak{a}}-j}(\tilde{B}w)
, we can therefore focus on those that are in directions normal to
\mathbb{S}^{2m_{5}-1}
.

The parameter-dependent calculus of Grubb and Seeley

Theorem 6.2
.

(a)
For

\displaystyle p_{(d,k)}(x,\xi)=\frac{1}{k!}\partial^{k}_{t}(t^{d}p(x,\xi,\frac{1}{t}))_{|t=0}.

\displaystyle p(x,\xi,\mu)-\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mu^{d-k}p_{(d,k)}(x,\xi)\in S^{m+N,d-N}.

Theorem 6.3
.

,

In fact, the parametrix is constructed by letting

\displaystyle p_{-m}(x,\xi,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{m}(x,\xi)+\mu^{m})^{-1}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle p_{-m-j}(x,\xi,\mu)

\displaystyle-\sum_{l,k,\alpha}\frac{1}{\alpha!}(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}p_{-m-l})(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}a_{m-k})p_{-m},

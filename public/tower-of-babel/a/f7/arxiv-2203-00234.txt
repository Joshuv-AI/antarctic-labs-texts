Small infinite partitions and other features of the Nowhere Centered ideal
Mario Jardon Santos

Abstract
The nowhere dense ideal 𝒩 ​ 𝒞 \mathcal{NC} is introduced. It is a coanalytic ideal of ω × ω \omega\times\omega whose defining characteristic is that the sets of the form X × Y X\times Y , where X , Y X,Y are infinite subsets of ω \omega , are dense in the quotient 𝒫 ⁡ ( ω × ω ) / 𝒩 ​ 𝒞 \mathcal{P}(\omega\times\omega)/\penalty\mathcal{NC} . This quotient has countable partitions and consistently has partitions of size ω 1 \omega_{1} while 𝔞 > ω 1 \mathfrak{a}>\omega_{1} . This represents a huge contrast with other definable quotients. Other combinatorial features of this ideal are presented, as well as some results on a family of similar, higher dimensional ideals.

The Nowhere Centered Ideal and its quotient

Recall that a family
\mathcal{X}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}
is said to be
centered
, if
\bigcap F
is infinite for all
F\in[\mathcal{X}]^{<\omega}
. From this almost natural concept in infinite combinatorics an ideal on
\omega\times\omega
is defined.

Definition 2.1
.

The Nowhere Centered ideal is defined on
\omega\times\omega
as follows

\mathcal{NC}:=\{A\subseteq\omega\times\omega\mid\forall X\in[\omega]^{\omega}~the~set~\{A(n):n\in X\}~is~not~centered\}.

Obviously
\omega\times\omega\notin\mathcal{NC}
and
\mathcal{NC}
is closed under subsets. Take
A_{0},A_{1}\subseteq\omega\times\omega
and suppose that
A_{0}\cup A_{1}\notin\mathcal{NC}
. There exists
X\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that
\{A_{0}(n)\cup A_{1}(n)\mid n\in X\}
is a centered family. If
\mathcal{U}
is an ultrafilter extending this family, there exist
i<2
and
X^{\prime}\in[X]^{\omega}
such that
\{A_{i}(n)\mid n\in X^{\prime}\}
is subset of
\mathcal{U}
, and hence is a centered family. Therefore
A_{i}\notin\mathcal{NC}
, and we conclude that
\mathcal{NC}
is closed under finite unions, and hence an ideal.

Observation 2.2
.

If
X\in[\omega]^{\omega}
and
A\in\mathcal{NC}
, then there exist
n<\omega
and
Y\in[X]^{\omega}
such that
|A(m)\cap Y|<\omega
, for all
m\geq n
.

Indeed we claim that there exists
n<\omega
such that
|X\setminus\bigcup_{m\in F}A(m)|=\omega
, for all
F\in[\omega\setminus n]^{<\omega}
. Otherwise for all
n<\omega
there exists
F\in[\omega\setminus n]^{<\omega}
such that
X\subseteq^{*}\bigcup_{m\in F}A(m)
. But hence any ultrafilter containing
X
would give us
B\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that
\{A(m)\mid m\in B\}
is a centered family, which is a contradiction. Given such
n<\omega
, getting such
Y\in[X]^{\omega}
is an easy task.

One could see Observation
2.2
as a
literary justification
for the naming of the ideal
\mathcal{NC}
, since it has a similar flavour as the observation that whenever we have
\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}
and a subtree
T\subseteq\omega^{<\omega}
whose branches form a nowhere dense set of
\omega^{\omega}
, then there exists
\tau\in\omega^{<\omega}
, an extension of
\sigma
, such that
\langle\tau\rangle\cap T=\emptyset
. In this case given the rectangle
\omega\times X
and
A\in\mathcal{NC}
we get an “extension” of the rectangle which “avoids”
A
. A measure of this avoidance as well as a known face to compare
\mathcal{NC}
to is given by the following ideal.

Definition 2.3
.

fin\times fin:=\{A\subseteq\omega\times\omega\mid\exists n<\omega~\forall m\geq n~|A(m)|<\omega\}
.

The ideal
fin\times fin
is often thought of as the ideal on
\omega\times\omega
generated by the columns
\{n\}\times\omega
, for
n<\omega
, and the sets below a function
\{(n,m)\in\omega\times\omega\mid m\leq f(n)\}
, for
f\in\omega^{\omega}
. Since for
A\in fin\times fin
and

there exists
n

\bigcap_{i\in X\cap n}A(i)<\omega,

it easily follows that

\{Y_{n}\mid n<\omega\}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}
is an almost disjoint family, the set

Y:=\coprod_{n<\omega}Y_{n}

is an element of
\mathcal{NC}
which does not lie in
fin\times fin
.

For getting to know the quotient
\mathcal{P}(\omega\times\omega)/\penalty\mathcal{NC}
take

. By definition there exists
X\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that

\{A(n)\mid n\in X\}

is a centered family. If
Y\in[\omega]^{\omega}
is a pseudointersection of said family, it follows that

X\times Y\subseteq_{fin\times fin}A,

which means that

\qquad\qquad\qquad X\times Y\subseteq_{\mathcal{NC}}A.\qquad\qquad\qquad(\star)

Observe that

is as much a key in determining

as

is. Therefore

is the second coordinate projection of the subset of

\forall n<\omega\exists m\geq n\exists k<\omega\forall l\geq k(l\in Y\Rightarrow l\in A(m)),

which means there exist infinite

\mathcal{NC}
is coanalytic.

Theorem 2.4
.

\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{s}(2)=\mathfrak{s}(\mathcal{NC})

Proof.

The first equality holds because

. For proving
\mathfrak{s}\leq\mathfrak{s}(\mathcal{NC})
first take
\kappa<\mathfrak{s}
and
\{A_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\kappa\}\subseteq\mathcal{NC}^{+}

\{A_{\alpha}(n)\mid\alpha<\kappa,~n<\omega\}

is not splitting in
\mathcal{P}(\omega)/\penalty fin
, take
Y\in[\omega]^{\omega}
witnessing this fact. Define

K:=\{\alpha<\kappa\mid\exists^{\infty}n<\omega~(Y\subseteq^{*}A_{\alpha}(n))\}.

For each
\alpha\in K
consider the set
X_{\alpha}:=\{n<\omega\mid Y\subseteq^{*}A_{\alpha}(n)\}
. The family
\{X_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in K\}
is not splitting in
\mathcal{P}(\omega)/\penalty fin
. Take
X\in[\omega]^{\omega}
witnessing this fact. The set

witnesses that
\{A_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\kappa\}\subseteq\mathcal{NC}^{+}
is not splitting in
\mathcal{P}(\omega\times\omega)/\penalty\mathcal{NC}
. Indeed, take
\alpha<\kappa
. If
\alpha\in K
and
X\subseteq^{*}X_{\alpha}
, then
X\times Y\subseteq_{\mathcal{NC}}A_{\alpha}
. If
\alpha\in K
and
|X\cap X_{\alpha}|<\omega
, then
|Y\cap A_{\alpha}(n)|<\omega
for almost all

. On the other hand if
\alpha\notin K
, then
|Y\cap A_{\alpha}(n)|<\omega
for almost all
n<\omega
. From both last cases it follows that
(X\times Y)\cap A_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{NC}
. We conclude that
\mathfrak{s}\leq\mathfrak{s}(\mathcal{NC})
.

Now take family
\{X_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\mathfrak{s}\}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}
splitting in
\mathcal{P}(\omega)/\penalty fin
. The family

\mathcal{S}:=\{X_{\alpha}\times X_{\beta}\mid\alpha,~\beta<\mathfrak{s}\}

is splitting in
\mathcal{P}(\omega\times\omega)/\penalty\mathcal{NC}
. Take

, which will be proved to be split by some element of

. Since the set
\circledast
is dense, w.l.o.g

for some

. Take
\alpha<\mathfrak{s}
and
\beta<\mathfrak{s}
such that
X_{\alpha}
splits
X
and
X_{\beta}

Y
. It follows that both
(X\times Y)\cap(X_{\alpha}\times X_{\beta})\in\mathcal{NC}^{+}
and
(X\times Y)\setminus(X_{\alpha}\times X_{\beta})\in\mathcal{NC}^{+}
, i.e.

. We conclude that
\mathfrak{s}(\mathcal{NC})\leq\mathfrak{s}

.

\mathfrak{r}\leq\mathfrak{r}(2)\leq\mathfrak{r}(\mathcal{NC})\leq\mathfrak{r}_{\sigma}

Proof.

Take
\kappa<\mathfrak{r}
and
\{A_{\alpha},B_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\kappa\}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}

\kappa<\mathfrak{r}(2)

Take
\kappa<\mathfrak{r}(2)
and
\{A_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\kappa\}\subseteq\mathcal{NC}^{+}

Notwithstanding these results, considering towers and infinite partitions huge differences between the corresponding cardinal invariants of
\mathcal{P}(\omega)/\penalty fin
and
\mathcal{P}(\omega\times\omega)/\penalty\mathcal{NC}

\mathcal{P}(\omega)/\penalty fin

\mathcal{P}(\omega)/\penalty fin\oplus\mathcal{P}(\omega)/\penalty fin

. It follows that

\mathfrak{a}(\mathcal{NC})\leq\mathfrak{a}(2)\leq\mathfrak{a}.

Nevertheless the left inequality is strict. This stems from the fact that the all countable subsets of

\{[\omega\times X]_{\mathcal{NC}}\mid X\in[\omega]^{\omega}\}

have a supremum in

.

Lemma 2.6
.

If
\{Y_{n}:n<\omega\}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega},
then

\coprod_{n<\omega}\bigcup_{i\leq n}Y_{i}=\bigvee_{n<\omega}\omega\times Y_{n}.

Proof.

It is immediate that

\omega\times Y_{n}\subseteq_{\mathcal{NC}}\coprod_{n<\omega}\bigcup_{i\leq n}Y_{i}

for all
n<\omega
. Take
A\times B\subseteq_{\mathcal{NC}}\coprod_{n<\omega}\bigcup_{i\leq n}Y_{i}
, with
A,B\in[\omega]^{\omega}
. If
B\nsubseteq^{*}\bigcup_{i\leq n}Y_{i}
for all
n<\omega
, then there exists
B^{\prime}\in[B]^{\omega}
such that
|B^{\prime}\cap Y_{n}|<\omega
for all
n<\omega
, but this is clearly a contradiction. Hence there exists
n<\omega
such that
B\subseteq^{*}\bigcup_{i\leq n}Y_{i}
. It follows that

A\times B\subseteq_{\mathcal{NC}}\bigcup_{i\leq n}\omega\times Y_{i}.

Since every lower bound of
\coprod_{n<\omega}\bigcup_{i\leq n}Y_{i}
is met by
\omega\times Y_{n}
for some
n<\omega
, the lemma follows.

Definition 3.1

Lemma 3.2

Take

Definition 4.1
.

The Katětov-Blass order
\leq_{KB}
on ideals is defined as follows: if
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
be two ideals on
\omega

\mathcal{I}\leq_{KB}\mathcal{J}

for all
I\in\mathcal{I}
.

If the function

.

Definition 4.2
.

•

\Delta:=\{(n,m)\in\omega\times\omega\mid m\leq n\}

•

\mathcal{ED}_{fin}:=\{X\subseteq\Delta\mid\exists n<\omega~\forall m<\omega~|X(m)|\leq n\}

•

A graph is a pair

, where

. We say that

induces a complete subgraph of

if

•

\mathcal{G}_{c}:=\{E\subseteq[\omega]^{2}\mid\forall Y\in[\omega]^{\omega}Y~does~not~induce~a~complete~subgraph\}.

The ideal
\mathcal{ED}_{fin}
can also be defined as the ideal on

generated by the functions from
\omega
to
\omega

Proposition 4.3
.

The following relations hold:

•

•

\mathcal{ED}_{fin}\leq_{KB}\mathcal{NC}

•

.

Proof.

Since
fin\times fin\subseteq\mathcal{NC}
, the Katětov-Blass inequality follows.

For the second relation take the function

for all

. Let

be a function. The set

is clearly the union

The set

is an element of

Since

. We conclude that

is a finite-to-one Katětov function.

For the last inequality consider the function

Take

and

. Since

, it follows that there exists non-empty

In order to construct a fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space setting for problem (

(H_{G})

g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is an odd, continuous and non-decreasing function and

such that

(g_{1})

g(t)>0
, for all

(g_{2})

There exist

(g_{3})

\displaystyle{g^{-}-1\leq\frac{g^{{}^{\prime}}(t)t}{g(t)}\leq g^{+}-1,\ \ \text{for all}\ t>0.}

(g_{4})

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{G^{-1}(t)}{t^{\frac{d+\alpha}{d}}}dt<\infty

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{G^{-1}(t)}{t^{\frac{d+\alpha}{d}}}dt=\infty

G
is an N-function:

is even, positive, continuous and convex function, Moreover
\frac{G(t)}{t}\rightarrow 0
as

\frac{G(t)}{t}\rightarrow+\infty

The conjugate N-function of G denoted

is defined by

\tilde{G}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{g}(s)\ ds,

where
\tilde{g}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by

\tilde{g}(t)=\sup\{s:\ g(s)\leq t\}
. Since

is a continuous function, it comes that
\tilde{g}(\cdot)=g^{-1}(\cdot)
.

Involving the functions
G
and
\tilde{G}
, we have the Young’s inequality

st\leq G(s)+\tilde{G}(t).

(2.2)

We say that an N-function
G
satisfies the
\triangle_{2}
-condition, if there exists

such that

G(2t)\leq CG(t),\ \text{for all}\ t>0.

(2.3)

The assumption
(g_{2})
implies that
G
and
\tilde{G}
satisfy the
\triangle_{2}
-condition (see
[
33
]
).

Let

is essentially
stronger than

(
B\prec\prec A
in
symbols), if for every positive constant

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{B(kt)}{A(t)}=0.

Another important function related to the N-function

is the Sobolev conjugate N-function denoted

and defined by

G_{*}^{-1}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{G^{-1}(s)}{s^{\frac{d+\alpha}{d}}}\ ds,\ t>0.

\displaystyle{G_{*}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g_{*}(s)\ ds}
and according to assumption
(g_{2})

g^{-}_{*}\leq\frac{g_{*}(t)t}{G_{*}(t)}\leq g^{+}_{*},\ \text{for all}\ t>0,\ \text{where}\ g^{+}_{*}=\frac{dg^{+}}{d-g^{+}}\ \text{and}\ g^{-}_{*}=\frac{dg^{-}}{d-g^{-}}.

(2.4)

Since

satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, the Orlicz space

is the vectorial space of all measurable function

\tilde{\rho}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(u)\ dx<\infty\ (\text{see}\ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{22}{}{}]}).

Next, we introduce the fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space. We denote by
W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space defined by

W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\bigg\{u\in L^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\ \overline{\rho}(\alpha;u)<\infty\bigg\},

(2.5)

where
\displaystyle{\overline{\rho}(\alpha;u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G\bigg(\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\bigg)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{d}}}.

The space

\|u\|_{\alpha,G}=\|u\|_{(G)}+[u]_{(\alpha,G)},

(2.6)

is the Gagliardo semi-norm defined by

(2.7)

Since

\tilde{G}
satisfy the

-condition, so the fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space

[
13

).

Let

(2.8)

Evidently,

\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{\alpha,G}\leq\|u\|\leq 2\|u\|_{\alpha,G},\ \text{for all}\ u\in W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

In the sequel we will use

As we mentioned in Section 1, the fractional g-Laplace operator is defined by

2.2
The ”generalized subdifferential”

A main tool used in the present paper is the subdifferential theory of Clark
[
18
,
19
]
for locally Lipschitz functionals. Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space,
\mathbb{X}^{*}
its topological dual, and let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}
denote the duality brackets for the pair
(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X}^{*})
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let the functional
\phi:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. We say that
\phi
is locally Lipschitz if, for every
x\in\mathbb{X}
, there exists an open neighborhood
U(x)
of
x
and
k_{x}>0
such that

|\phi(u)-\phi(v)|\leq k_{x}\|u-v\|_{\mathbb{X}}\ \ \text{for all}\ u,v\in U(x).

Definition 2.7
.

Given a locally Lipschitz function
\phi:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the ”generalized directional derivative” of
\phi
at
u\in\mathbb{X}
in the direction
v\in\mathbb{X}
, denoted by
\tilde{\phi}(u;v)
, is defined by

\tilde{\phi}(u;v)=\mathop{\limsup_{x\rightarrow u}}_{t\searrow 0}\frac{\phi(x+tv)-\phi(x)}{t}.

Definition 2.8
.

The ”generalized subdifferential” of
\phi
at
u\in\mathbb{X}
is the set
\partial\phi(u)\subseteq\mathbb{X}^{*}
given by

\partial\phi(u)=\{\phi^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:\langle\phi^{*},v\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}\leq\tilde{\phi}(u;v)\ \ \text{for all}\ v\in\mathbb{X}\}.

The Hahn-Banach theorem implies that
\partial\phi(u)\neq\emptyset
for all
u\in\mathbb{X}
, it is convex and
w^{*}
-compact (in weak topology sense). If
\phi
is also convex, then it coincides with the subdifferential in the sense of convex functionals (see
[
26
]
). If
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{R})
, then
\partial\phi(u)=\{\phi^{{}^{\prime}}(u)\}
. Note that the generalized subdifferential has a remarkable calculus, similar to that in the classical derivative (see
[
18
,
19
,
26
]
).

J(tw^{+}+sw^{-})\leq J(w),\ \text{for all}\ t,s>0.

Proof.

Let
w\in\mathcal{M}
and consider the fibering map
\mu_{w}:(0,+\infty)\times(0,+\infty)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\mu_{w}(t,s)=J(tw^{+}+sw^{-})\ \ \text{for all}\ t,s>0.

In light of Proposition
6.1
,

\mu_{w}(0,0)=J(0)=0<m_{1}\leq\mu_{w}(1,1)=J(w).

(6.9)

Let
t,s>0
large enough, using Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{w}(t,s)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\|tw^{+}+sw^{-}\|^{g^{-}},\|tw^{+}+sw^{-}\|^{g^{+}}\right\}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)F(x,tw^{+}+sw^{-})dx

\displaystyle\leq 2^{g^{+}-1}\max\left\{|t|^{g^{-}}\|w^{+}\|^{g^{-}}+|s|^{g^{-}}\|w^{-}\|^{g^{-}},|t|^{g^{+}}\|w^{+}\|^{g^{+}}+|s|^{g^{+}}\|w^{-}\|^{g^{+}}\right\}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)F(x,tw^{+}+sw^{-})dx

\displaystyle\leq 2^{g^{+}-1}\max\left\{\max\{|t|^{g^{-}},|s|^{g^{-}}\}\left(\|w^{+}\|^{g^{-}}+\|w^{-}\|^{g^{-}}\right),\max\{|t|^{g^{+}},|s|^{g^{+}}\}\left(\|w^{+}\|^{g^{+}}+\|w^{-}\|^{g^{+}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)F(x,tw^{+}+sw^{-})dx

\displaystyle\leq 2^{g^{+}-1}\max\{|t|^{g^{+}},|s|^{g^{+}}\}\max\left\{\|w^{+}\|^{g^{-}}+\|w^{-}\|^{g^{-}},\|w^{+}\|^{g^{+}}+\|w^{-}\|^{g^{+}}\right\}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)F(x,tw^{+}+sw^{-})dx.

(6.10)

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{w}(t,s)}{\max\{|t|^{g^{+}},|s|^{g^{+}}\}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{g^{+}-1}\max\left\{\|w^{+}\|^{g^{-}}+\|w^{-}\|^{g^{-}},\|w^{+}\|^{g^{+}}+\|w^{-}\|^{g^{+}}\right\}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)\frac{F(x,tw^{+}+sw^{-})}{\max\{|t|^{g^{+}},|s|^{g^{+}}\}}dx.

(6.11)

By assumption
(f_{3})
and the fact that
\text{supp}(w^{+})\cap\text{supp}(w^{-})=\emptyset
, we infer that

\lim\limits_{|(t,s)|\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{F(x,tw^{+}+sw^{-})}{\max\{|t|^{g^{+}},|s|^{g^{+}}\}}=+\infty,\ \ \text{for a.a.}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(6.12)

Applying (
6
) and (
6.12
), we deduce that

\limsup\limits_{|(t,s)|\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{w}(t,s)\leq-\infty.

According to (
6.9
), the map
\mu_{w}(\cdot,\cdot)
has a global maximum
(t_{w^{+}},s_{w^{-}})\in(0,+\infty)\times(0,+\infty)
.
(t_{w^{+}},s_{w^{-}})
is a critical point for
\mu_{w}(\cdot,\cdot)
, that is,

\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(t_{w^{+}}w^{+}+s_{w^{-}}w^{-}),w^{+}\rangle=0

and

\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(t_{w^{+}}w^{+}+s_{w^{-}}w^{-}),w^{-}\rangle=0.

By Proposition
6.3
and the fact that
w\in\mathcal{M}
,

(t_{w^{+}},s_{w^{-}})=(1,1).

Hence,

J(tw^{+}+sw^{-})\leq J(t_{w^{+}}w^{+}+s_{w^{-}}w^{-})=J(w),\ \text{for all}\ t,s>0.

This ends the proof.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Assume that hypotheses
(H_{f})
,
(H_{G})
and
(H_{K})
hold.
Let
\{w_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathcal{M}
such that
w_{n}\rightharpoonup w
in
W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
w^{\pm}\neq 0.

Proof.

We claim that there is
\varrho>0
such that

\varrho\leq\|v^{\pm}\|,\ \ \text{for all}\ v\in\mathcal{M}.

(6.13)

Indeed, by using Lemma
4.4
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\left(\frac{v^{\pm}(x)-v^{\pm}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{v^{\pm}(x)-v^{\pm}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha+d}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(v^{\pm})v^{\pm}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)f(x,v^{\pm})v^{\pm}dx.

2.4

5.1

(g_{2})
and
(K_{1})
, for all
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle[g^{-}-g^{+}\varepsilon\|K\|_{\infty}]\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G\left(\frac{v^{\pm}(x)-v^{\pm}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{d}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(v^{\pm})dx\right]

\displaystyle\leq g^{+}_{*}C_{\varepsilon}\|K\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{*}(v^{\pm})dx.

Without lose of generality, we may assume that

0\neq\|v\|<1
. By Lemma
2.5
and Theorem
2.1
, we deduce that

[g^{-}-g^{+}\varepsilon\|K\|_{\infty}]\|v^{\pm}\|^{g^{+}}\leq g^{+}_{*}\tilde{C}C_{\varepsilon}\|K\|_{\infty}\|v^{\pm}\|^{g^{+}_{*}}.

(6.14)

Choosing

small enough, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{C_{1}}{C_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{g^{+}_{*}-g^{+}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|v^{\pm}\|,

Consequently, there exists a positive radius
\varrho>0

\|v^{\pm}\|\geq\varrho,
with
\varrho=\left(\frac{C_{1}}{C_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{g^{+}_{*}-g^{+}}}.
Thus, the claim.

So, by (
6.13
),

\|w_{n}^{\pm}\|\geq\varrho,\ \ \text{for all}\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

(6.15)

According to Lemma
4.4
,

\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(w_{n}^{\pm}),w_{n}^{\pm}\rangle\leq\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(w_{n}),w_{n}^{\pm}\rangle=0.

By
(g_{2})
and Lemma
2.5
, we get

g^{-}\min\{\|w_{n}^{\pm}\|^{g^{-}},\|w_{n}^{\pm}\|^{g^{+}}\}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)f(x,w_{n}^{\pm})w_{n}^{\pm}\ dx.

(6.16)

Putting together
(\ref{3.7})
and
(\ref{3.6}),
we find

g^{-}\min\{\varrho^{g^{-}},\varrho^{g^{+}}\}\leq g^{-}\min\{\|w_{n}^{\pm}\|^{g^{-}},\|w_{n}^{\pm}\|^{g^{+}}\}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)f(x,w_{n}^{\pm})w_{n}^{\pm}\ dx.

(6.17)

On the other hand, in light of Lemma
4.3
, one has

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)f(x,w_{n}^{\pm})w_{n}^{\pm}\ dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)f(x,w^{\pm})w^{\pm}\ dx.

(6.18)

Combining
(\ref{3.8})
with
(\ref{3.9}),
we get

0<g^{-}\min\{\varrho^{g^{-}},\varrho^{g^{+}}\}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)f(x,w^{\pm})w^{\pm}\ dx,

thus,
w^{\pm}\neq 0
. This ends the proof.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

Assume that the hypotheses
(H_{f})
,
(H_{G})
and
(H_{K})

\widehat{w}\in\mathcal{M}

J(\widehat{w})=m_{1}

Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
5.4

\{w_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

By passing to a subsequence if necessary,

w_{n}\rightharpoonup\widehat{w}\ \text{in}\ W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

w_{n}(x)\rightarrow\widehat{w}(x)\ \text{as}\ n\rightarrow+\infty,\ \text{for a.a.}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(6.19)

and

w_{n}^{\pm}(x)\rightarrow\widehat{w}^{\pm}(x)\ \text{as}\ n\rightarrow+\infty,\ \text{for a.a.}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(6.20)

Applying Proposition
6.4

\widehat{w}^{\pm}\neq 0.
So, according to Proposition
6.1
, there is a unique pair
t_{\widehat{w}^{+}},s_{\widehat{w}^{-}}>0

6.20

6.3

4.3

\displaystyle m_{1}=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}J(w_{n})

\displaystyle\geq J(t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+}+s_{\widehat{w}^{-}}\widehat{w}^{-})

We show that

6.20

4.3

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\left(\frac{\widehat{w}(x)-\widehat{w}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{\widehat{w}^{\pm}(x)-\widehat{w}^{\pm}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha+d}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(\widehat{w}^{\pm})\widehat{w}^{\pm}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)f(x,\widehat{w}^{\pm})\widehat{w}^{\pm}dx.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)f(x,t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+})t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+}\ dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\text{supp}(\widehat{w}^{+})}\int_{\text{supp}(\widehat{w}^{+})}g\left(\frac{t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+}(x)-t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+}(x)-t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha+d}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\text{supp}(\widehat{w}^{-})}\int_{\text{supp}(\widehat{w}^{+})}g\left(\frac{t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+}(x)-s_{\widehat{w}^{-}}\widehat{w}^{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+}(x)}{|x-y|^{\alpha+d}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\text{supp}(\widehat{w}^{+})}\int_{\text{supp}(\widehat{w}^{-})}g\left(\frac{s_{\widehat{w}^{-}}\widehat{w}^{-}(x)-t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{-t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha+d}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+})t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+}\ dx.

(6.25)

Without loss of generality, we suppose that
t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\geq s_{\widehat{w}^{-}}
. By (
6
), Lemma
2.4
and the fact that the map
s\mapsto g(s)
is non-decreasing function on
\mathbb{R}
, it yields that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)f(x,t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+})t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+}\ dx\leq\max\{t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}^{g^{-}},t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}^{g^{+}}\}\sigma,

where

\displaystyle 0\leq\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\text{supp}(\widehat{w}^{+})}\int_{\text{supp}(\widehat{w}^{+})}g\left(\frac{\widehat{w}^{+}(x)-\widehat{w}^{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{\widehat{w}^{+}(x)-\widehat{w}^{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha+d}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\text{supp}(\widehat{w}^{-})}\int_{\text{supp}(\widehat{w}^{+})}g\left(\frac{\widehat{w}^{+}(x)-\widehat{w}^{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{\widehat{w}^{+}(x)}{|x-y|^{\alpha+d}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\text{supp}(\widehat{w}^{+})}\int_{\text{supp}(\widehat{w}^{-})}g\left(\frac{\widehat{w}^{-}(x)-\widehat{w}^{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{-\widehat{w}^{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha+d}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(\widehat{w}^{+})\widehat{w}^{+}\ dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\left(\frac{\widehat{w}(x)-\widehat{w}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{\widehat{w}^{+}(x)-\widehat{w}^{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha+d}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(\widehat{w}^{+})\widehat{w}^{+}\ dx.

Arguing by contradiction, and suppose that
t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}>1,
then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)\frac{f(x,t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+})t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+}}{t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}^{g^{+}}}\leq\sigma.

(6.26)

Putting together (
6.24
) and (
6.26
), using
(K_{1})
and (

), we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)(\widehat{w}^{+})^{g^{+}}\left[\frac{f(x,\widehat{w}^{+})}{(\widehat{w}^{+})^{g^{+}-1}}-\frac{f(x,t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+})}{(t_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\widehat{w}^{+})^{g^{+}-1}}\right]dx<0

0<t_{\widehat{w}^{+}},s_{\widehat{w}^{-}}\leq 1.

Step 2:

Proposition 6.6
.

Under the hypotheses
(H_{f})
,
(H_{G})
and
(H_{K})
,
\widehat{w}
is a critical point for
J
, that is,
\widehat{w}
is a least energy weak nodal solution of problem (
P
).

Proof.

We consider the functionals
\varphi_{\pm}:W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\pm}(w)

\displaystyle=\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(w),w^{\pm}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\left(\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{w^{\pm}(x)-w^{\pm}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha+d}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(w)w^{\pm}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)f(x,w^{\pm})w^{\pm}dx.

\varphi_{\pm}
is locally Lipschitz (see
[
19
, Theorem 2.7.2, p. 221]
).

Let
w\in W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For all
\varphi^{*}_{w^{\pm}}\in\partial\varphi_{\pm}(w)

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{w^{\pm}}^{*},v\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(w^{\pm})v^{\pm}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)f(x,w^{\pm})v^{\pm}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{{}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{[v(x)-v(y)][w^{\pm}(x)-w^{\pm}(y)]}{|x-y|^{2\alpha+d}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{{}^{\prime}}(w^{\pm})(v^{\pm})^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)f^{*}(x,w^{\pm})(v^{\pm})^{2}dx,

for all

6.5
,

for all
\varphi^{*}_{\widehat{w}^{+}}\in\partial\varphi_{+}(\widehat{w})

\varphi^{*}_{\widehat{w}^{-}}\in\partial\varphi_{-}(\widehat{w}).

6

6.30

(g_{3})

\widehat{w}\in\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\langle\varphi^{*}_{\widehat{w}^{\pm}},\widehat{w}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{{}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\widehat{w}(x)-\widehat{w}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{[\widehat{w}(x)-\widehat{w}(y)][\widehat{w}^{\pm}(x)-\widehat{w}^{\pm}(y)]}{|x-y|^{2\alpha+d}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{{}^{\prime}}(\widehat{w}^{\pm})(\widehat{w}^{\pm})^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x)f^{{}^{\prime}}(x,\widehat{w}^{\pm})(\widehat{w}^{\pm})^{2}dx

By (

(f_{4})

(6.32)

According to (
6.29

Proposition 6.7
.

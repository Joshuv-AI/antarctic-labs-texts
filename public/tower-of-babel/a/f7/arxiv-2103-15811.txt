On eigenvalues of symmetric matrices with PSD principal submatrices
Khazhgali Kozhasov

Abstract




2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
14P10, 15A18, 15B57, 90C22

Abstract.

We investigate convexity properties of the set of eigenvalue tuples of
n\times n
real symmetric matrices, whose all
k\times k
(where
k\leq n
is fixed) minors are positive semidefinite.
It is proven that the set
\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,k})
of eigenvalue vectors of all such matrices is star-shaped with respect to the nonnegative orthant
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
and not convex already when
(n,k)=(4,2)
.

1.
Introduction and statement of results

Let
\Sym_{n}
denote the space of
n\times n
real symmetric matrices. A matrix
X\in\Sym_{n}
is called
positive semidefinite
(or, simply, PSD), if

\bm{v}^{T}X\bm{v}\geq 0

holds for all column vectors
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Equivalently,
X\in\Sym_{n}
is PSD if all its eigenvalues are nonnegative.
For
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
let
{\mathcal{S}}^{n,k}
denote the set of those matrices in
\Sym_{n}
, whose all
k\times k
principal submatrices are positive semidefinite.
Elements of
{\mathcal{S}}^{n,k}
are known as
k
-locally positive semidefinite matrices
, they have been systematically studied in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
.
Note that
{\mathcal{S}}^{n,n}
consists of all positive semidefinite matrices in
\Sym_{n}
.
The set
{\mathcal{S}}^{n,k}
is a closed convex cone containing
{\mathcal{S}}^{n,n}
and, moreover,
{\mathcal{S}}^{n,k}\supsetneq{\mathcal{S}}^{n,k+1}
for any
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
.
The dual cone to
{\mathcal{S}}^{n,k}\subset\Sym_{n}
consists of PSD matrices of factor width
k
, which were introduced and investigated in
[
3
,
7
,
5
]
. Matrices of factor width
2
are also known as scaled diagonally dominant matrices, see
[
3
]
.
One of the motivations to study properties of locally PSD matrices came from empirical observations that some optimization problems (see
[
8
,
6
]
), where constraints are given by PSD matrices, have close optimal values to those of their relaxations, where constraints are relaxed to be
k
-locally PSD.

Given a matrix
X\in\Sym_{n}
, let
\lambda(X)=(\lambda_{1}(X),\dots,\lambda_{n}(X))
,
\lambda_{1}(X)\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}(X)
, denote the vector of its ordered eigenvalues and for any permutation on
n
elements
\sigma\in S_{n}
let
\lambda(X)^{\sigma}=(\lambda_{\sigma_{1}}(X),\dots,\lambda_{\sigma_{n}}(X))
be the vector of permuted eigenvalues.
In this work we are interested in the set

\displaystyle\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,k})\ =\ \left\{\lambda(X)^{\sigma}\,:\,X\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n,k},\ \sigma\in S_{n}\right\}

of all possible (permuted) eigenvalue vectors of
k
-locally PSD matrices.
As observed in
[
2
]
, the set
\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,k})
is contained in
the closed hyperbolicity cone

\displaystyle H(e^{n}_{k})\ =\ \{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,e^{n}_{k}(\lambda+t(1,\dots,1))=0\,\Rightarrow t\leq 0\}

of the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial
e^{n}_{k}(\lambda)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot\lambda_{i_{k}}
.
Moreover, the inclusion
\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,k})\subseteq H(e^{n}_{k})
is strict for
2<k<n-1
, for
(n,k)=(4,2)

[
2
, Cor. 3]
and, surprisingly, one has equality
\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,n-1})=H(e^{n}_{n-1})
for
k=n-1
(see
[
2
, Thm.
2.2
]
).
The authors of
[
2
]
pointed out that it is unknown whether the set
\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,k})
of eigenvalue vectors of matrices in
{\mathcal{S}}^{n,k}
is convex for any
k
and
n
.
We give a negative answer to this question, showing that
\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{4,2})\subsetneq H(e^{4}_{2})
is not convex.

Theorem 1.1
.

The set

\displaystyle\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{4,2})\ =\ \{(\lambda_{\sigma_{1}}(X),\lambda_{\sigma_{2}}(X),\lambda_{\sigma_{3}}(X),\lambda_{\sigma_{4}}(X))\,:\,X\in{\mathcal{S}}^{4,2},\ \sigma\in S_{4}\}

of eigenvalue vectors of
2
-locally PSD matrices in
\Sym_{4}
is not convex.

Remark 1.2
.

Specifically, we prove that
(4,4,-1,-1)
is not in
\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{4,2})
, though it lies on the segment joining the vector
(4,4,0,-2)\in\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{4,2})
with its permuted copy.

Even though the set
\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,k})
is not convex in general, it is star-shaped with respect to the nonnegative orthant
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}=\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,n})
.

Proposition 1.3
.

For all
\lambda\in\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,k})
,
\lambda^{+}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
the sum
\lambda+\lambda^{+}
is in
\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,k})
.

We now discuss one application of this result.
It is shown in
[
2
]
that every point on the boundary of
H(e^{4}_{2})
with exactly one negative entry is an eigenvalue vector of some matrix in
{\mathcal{S}}^{4,2}
.
We observe that the same holds for all points in
H(e^{4}_{2})
with this property.

Corollary 1.4
.

Any
\lambda\in H(e^{4}_{2})
with at most
1
negative entry is an eigenvalue vector of some matrix in
{\mathcal{S}}^{4,2}
.

In the rather trivial case
k=1
the set
\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,1})=H(e^{n}_{1})
is a closed half-space.

Theorem 1.5
.

For any
n\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,1})\ =\ \left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,e^{n}_{1}(\lambda)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\geq 0\right\}

2.
Some open questions

By its definition, the set
\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,k})
consists of permuted eigenvalue vectors of
k
-locally PSD matrices.
It is also natural to consider the subset

\displaystyle\lambda_{\leq}({\mathcal{S}}^{n,k})\ =\ \{\lambda(X)\,:\,X\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n,k}\}

of ordered eigenvalue vectors, that is, the intersection of
\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,k})
with half-spaces cut out by inequalities
\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{i+1}
,
i=1,\dots,n-1
.
Our proof of Theorem
1.1
does not imply that
\lambda_{\leq}({\mathcal{S}}^{4,2})
is non-convex, see Remark
1.2
.
It would be interesting to understand whether
\lambda_{\leq}({\mathcal{S}}^{n,k})
is non-convex for some
(n,k)
.
When
(n,k)=(n,n-1)
the set
\lambda_{\leq}({\mathcal{S}}^{n,k}))
is a convex cone by
[
2
, Thm.
2.2
]
and when
(n,k)=(n,1)
it is the convex polyhedral cone

\displaystyle\lambda_{\leq}({\mathcal{S}}^{n,1})\ =\ \left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n},\ \sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j}\geq 0\ \textrm{for all}\ i=1,\dots,n\right\},

see the proof of Theorem
1.5
below.

Nonzero vectors in the hyperbolicity cone
H(e^{n}_{k})
can have at most
n-k
negative entries. Generalizing
[
2
, Lemma
2
]
one can show that if a vector
\lambda\in H(e^{n}_{k})
has
n-k
negative entries, then the other
k
entries must be positive.
One interesting question is to understand whether for any
(n,k)
there exist
k
-locally PSD matrices with
n-k
negative eigenvalues.
In the cases
(n,n-1)
and
(n,1)

[
2
, Thm.
2.2
]
and Theorem
1.5
imply a positive answer to this question. Also, one can see that the real symmetric matrix

\displaystyle X\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}346&84&-16&-98\\
84&240&77&156\\
-16&77&30&70\\
-98&156&70&170\end{pmatrix}

is in
{\mathcal{S}}^{4,2}
and has two negative eigenvalues.

See also Remark
4.2
for an interesting polynomial optimization problem.

Our proof of Theorem
1.5
is based on the following classical result.

Theorem 4.3

(Schur-Horn theorem)
.

Let
x,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be two vectors with
x_{1}\geq\dots\geq x_{n}
and
\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
.
There exists a symmetric matrix
X\in\Sym_{n}
with diagonal entries
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
if and only if for all
i=1,\dots,n-1

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{i}x_{j}\ \leq\ \sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j}\quad\textrm{{and}}\quad\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\ =\ \sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}.

Note that the statement is equivalent to

(4.2)

\displaystyle\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,1})\ =\ \left\{\lambda^{\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\sigma\in S_{n},\ \lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n},\ \sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j}\geq 0\ \textrm{{for all}}\ i=1,\dots,n\right\}.

Indeed, if
\sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j}<0
for some
i<n
, then
\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{i+1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
must be negative. Therefore,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j}<0
and hence no permuted vector
\lambda^{\sigma}
,
\sigma\in S_{n}
, can satisfy
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}^{\sigma}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\geq 0
.
The opposite direction is trivial. Thus, we need to prove that ordered vectors in
\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,1})
are exactly those that satisfy inequalities in (
4.2
).

Let
\lambda=\lambda(X)
, where the matrix
X\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n,1}
has ordered diagonal entries
x_{1}\geq\dots\geq x_{n}
.
By Theorem
4.3
we have that
\sum^{i}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}\geq\sum^{i}_{j=1}x_{j}\geq 0
holds for
i=1,\dots,n
.

Let now
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an ordered nonzero vector that satisfies inequalities from (
4.2
).
There exists an index
r\in\{1,\dots,n\}
so that
\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{r}>0\geq\lambda_{r+1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
.
Let us consider a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by
x_{1}=\dots=x_{r}=\frac{1}{r}\sum^{n}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}\geq 0
and
x_{r+1}=\dots=x_{n}=0
.
For
i=1,\dots,r-1
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{i}x_{j}\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{i}{r}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\ \leq\ \frac{i}{r}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}\ =\ \sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j}+\frac{i-r}{r}\sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j}+\frac{i}{r}\sum_{j=i+1}^{r}\lambda_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\ \sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j}+\frac{i(i-r)}{r}\lambda_{i}+\frac{i}{r}\sum_{j=i+1}^{r}\lambda_{j}\ \leq\ \sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j}+\frac{i}{r}\sum_{j=i+1}^{r}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})\ \leq\sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j},

where ordering
\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{r}>0\geq\lambda_{r+1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
is used in several places. For
i=r,\dots,n
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{i}x_{j}\ =\ \sum^{n}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}\ \leq\ \sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j},

where we use negativity of
\lambda_{r+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}<0
.
These inequalities, the equality
\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}
and Theorem
4.3
imply that
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
are eigenvalues of some matrix
X\in\Sym_{n}
with diagonal entries
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\geq 0
, that is, the vector
\lambda=\lambda(X)
is in
\lambda({\mathcal{S}}^{n,1})
.
∎

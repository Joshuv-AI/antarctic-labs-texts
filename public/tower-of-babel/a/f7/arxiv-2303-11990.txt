Blow-ups and normal bundles in connective and nonconnective derived geometries Thanks: The second author was supported by the Göran Gustafsson foundation. The paper is a result of a research visit partially supported by the CMSC of the University of Haifa and the Signeul foundation.
Oren Ben-Bassat, Jeroen Hekking

Abstract
. This work presents a generalization of derived blow-ups and of the derived deformation to the normal bundle from derived algebraic geometry to any geometric context. The latter is our proposed globalization of a derived algebraic context, itself a generalization of the theory of simplicial commutative rings. One key difference between a geometric context and ordinary derived algebraic geometry is that the coordinate ring of an affine object in the former is not necessarily connective. When constructing generalized blow-ups, this not only turns out to be remarkably convenient, but also leads to a wider existence result. Indeed, we show that the derived Rees algebra and the derived blow-up exist for any affine morphism of stacks in a given geometric context. However, in general the derived Rees algebra will no longer be connective, hence in general the derived blow-up will not live in the connective part of the theory. Unsurprisingly, this can be solved by restricting the input to closed immersions. The proof of the latter statement uses a derived deformation to the normal bundle in any given geometric context, which is also of independent interest. Besides the geometric context which extends algebraic geometry, the second main example of a geometric context will be an extension of analytic geometry. The latter is a recent construction, and includes many different flavors of analytic geometry, such as complex analytic geometry, non-archimedean rigid analytic geometry and analytic geometry over the integers. The present work thus provides derived blow-ups and a derived deformation to the normal bundle in all of these, which is expected to have many applications.



Introduction

The theory of derived blow-ups has recently been developed in
[
KR18
]
,
[
Hek21
]
,
[
HKR22
]
. It gives an analogue in derived algebraic geometry to blow-ups in classical algebraic geometry, and has found applications in (virtual) intersection theory, in algebraic
K
-theory, and in stabilizer reduction of derived Artin stacks.

The key constructions in
[
HKR22
]
are to a large extent formal, since they ultimately depend on the theory of derived Weil restrictions (as first studied in
[
SAG
]
in the context of spectral algebraic geometry), which is essentially an
\infty
-categorical idea. Since the applications of derived blow-ups have been quite satisfying so far, this leads to the natural question whether the same techniques can be used to define derived blow-ups in contexts other than algebraic geometry.

The answer presented in this paper takes as starting point a derived algebraic context, the theory of which is due to Bhatt–Mathew (since their work has not yet appeared, we will use the exposition in
[
Rak20
]
). Such a context
\mathscr{C}
gives rise to derived rings in
\mathscr{C}
, which are generalizations of simplicial commutative rings but which need not be connective. We then use spectra of derived rings in a given algebraic context as affine building blocks to define geometric contexts.

We will first present our main results in this introduction, after which we explain the central definitions in more detail. A recent history of derived blow-ups and its applications in derived algebraic geometry is given in §
1
.

Synopsis

§
2
is a review of the theory of derived algebraic contexts found in
[
Rak20
]
that is relevant to the present work. This story is globalized in §
3
, which leads to the geometric framework used for generalized blow-ups and normal bundles in §
4
. The paper ends in §
5
with an exposition of our constructions in the two main examples of a geometric context: derived algebraic geometry and derived analytic geometry. The remainder of this introduction provides more details on the key definitions used in the paper, a short overview of derived analytic geometry, and an explanation of the benefits of nonconnective geometry in the construction of derived blow-ups.

Derived algebraic contexts and graded modules

A derived algebraic context, simply called an
algebraic context
, is a symmetric monoidal, stable
\infty
-category
\mathscr{C}
endowed with a t-structure, together with a symmetric monoidal subcategory
\mathscr{C}^{0}\subset\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit}
, satisfying certain compatibility conditions. The objects of
\mathscr{C}
—called
\mathscr{C}
-modules
—play the role of modules (in the derived sense) in the context under consideration. For example, taking
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{\mathbb{Z}}
leads to derived algebraic geometry, where
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is the derived
\infty
-category of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules (unbounded and with homological indexing notation).

To produce the algebras for
\mathscr{C}
, one first takes the left derived functor
\LSym_{\mathscr{C}_{\geq 0}}
of the functor
\Sym_{\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit}}:\mathscr{C}^{0}\to\mathscr{C}_{\geq 0}
, where
\Sym_{\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit}}
is the classical symmetric algebra functor with respect to the symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit}
. A key result in the theory of derived algebraic contexts is that
\LSym_{\mathscr{C}_{\geq 0}}
then extends to a monad
\LSym_{\mathscr{C}}:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
. This produces the
\infty
-category
{\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{\mathscr{C}}}
of algebra objects for
\LSym_{\mathscr{C}}
, simply called
\mathscr{C}
-algebras
. Observe that the underlying module of a given
\mathscr{C}
-algebra is typically not connective. We write
{\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{\mathscr{C}_{\geq 0}}}
for the subcategory of algebras that do have connective underlying module.

In §
2
, we also develop the
M
-graded version
\mathscr{C}^{M}
of an algebraic context
\mathscr{C}
, for any discrete commutative monoid
M
. We think of the objects in
\mathscr{C}^{M}
as
M
-graded
\mathscr{C}
-modules. Such an object is not hard to define, since it is just a set of objects
M_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{C}
, indexed by
\alpha\in M
. However, as in classical algebra, the commutative monoid structure on
M
comes into play when defining the appropriate tensor product in
\mathscr{C}^{M}
. We do this through Day convolution, since an explicit description is in general infeasible in the derived setting. The prime examples that we are interested in are
M=\mathbb{N}
and
M=\mathbb{Z}
, since this leads to
\mathbb{N}
-graded and
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathscr{C}
-algebras respectively, which we use to construct the blow-up from the Rees algebra.

Geometric contexts

In §
3
we propose a perspective on geometry relative to a fixed algebraic context
\mathscr{C}
, meaning that
\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{op}}
will be the category of (nonconnective) affines. The datum of a
geometric context
will thus be an algebraic context
\mathscr{C}
, together with a Grothendieck topology
J
on
{\mathscr{A}\mathrm{ff}_{\mathscr{C}}}\coloneqq\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{op}}
. Since we are ultimately interested in the connective part of the theory, we impose certain conditions on
J
such that it restricts to a topology on
{\mathscr{A}\mathrm{ff}_{\mathscr{C}_{\geq 0}}}\coloneqq\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{\mathscr{C}_{\geq 0}}^{\mathrm{op}}
, and such that the induced functor

G\colon{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{t}_{\mathscr{C}_{\geq 0}}}\to{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{t}_{\mathscr{C}}}

from stacks on
{\mathscr{A}\mathrm{ff}_{\mathscr{C}_{\geq 0}}}
to stacks on
{\mathscr{A}\mathrm{ff}_{\mathscr{C}}}
is fully faithful and preserves limits, colimits and affine objects.

After this, we introduce some basic notions for a given geometric context, such as quasi-coherent modules. We also globalize graded algebraic contexts, which leads to a comparison between quasi-coherent,
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebras over a given base
X
, and affine morphisms
Y\to X
endowed with a (fibre-wise)
\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathscr{C}}
-action on
Y
, where
\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathscr{C}}\coloneqq\Spec(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathscr{C}}[\mathbb{Z}])
. This enables us to define the projective spectrum of a given
\mathbb{N}
-graded, quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
as

\Proj\mathcal{B}\coloneqq[((\Spec^{\mathrm{nc}}\mathcal{B})\setminus V(\mathcal{B}_{+}))/\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathscr{C}}]

where
V(\mathcal{B}_{+})=\Spec^{\mathrm{nc}}(\mathcal{B}_{0})
, with
\mathcal{B}_{0}
the part of
\mathcal{B}
of weight 0.

Benefits of the nonconnective setting

Besides derived analytic geometry, the theory of generalized derived blow-ups is also of value to derived algebraic geometry itself. Indeed, the constructions and arguments are significantly simpler than the ones used in
[
Hek21
]
.
The key difference with the previous approach is that we are now able to pass through nonconnective algebras, even though connective algebras are our main interest. Since simplicial commutative
k
-algebras are generally different from connective
\mathbf{E}_{\infty}
-
k
-algebras, the latter are not suitable for this. In contrast, connective derived
k
-algebras do coincide with simplicial
k
-algebras.

In ordinary derived algebraic geometry the derived Rees algebra only makes sense for closed immersions of derived schemes. For example, it can be shown that the deformation space
D_{\mathbb{A}^{1}/\{0\}}
is equivalent to
B\mathbb{G}_{a}
, which is not connectively affine. The
\infty
-category of
A
-algebras
B
with
A\to B
surjective on
\pi_{0}
is not generated under sifted colimits of compact projective objects. This means it is generally hard to define a functor out of this
\infty
-category, since it has no good theory of left derived functors.

On the other hand, the
\infty
-category of
A
-algebras
B
is generated under sifted colimits by finitely generated, polynomial
A
-algebras, which means that it is much easier to define a Rees algebra functor on this category. In order to do this, we need to relax the notion of affine objects, as the example
D_{\mathbb{A}^{1}/\{0\}}\simeq B\mathbb{G}_{a}
shows. This example also suggests how to relax this notion: since
\mathbb{G}_{a}\simeq*\times_{B\mathbb{G}_{a}}*
, we expect
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{G}_{a}}\simeq k\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{B\mathbb{G}_{a}}}k
, and thus
\mathcal{O}_{B\mathbb{G}_{a}}
to be a symmetric algebra on
k[-1]
. This leads to our solution of taking nonconnective derived rings as the target category of the Rees algebra functor.

A by-product of this approach is a derived Rees algebra for any
A
-algebra
B
, and thus a derived blow-up of any derived stack
X
in a relatively affine centre
Z\to X
. On first encounter, this seems like a somewhat bizarre idea. Of course, since the derived Rees algebra of an
A
-algebra
B
will in general be nonconnective unless
A\to B
is surjective on
\pi_{0}
, the derived blow-up of
X
in
Z
will in general not be an ordinary derived algebro-geometric object, unless
Z\to X
is a closed immersion. It is expected that the negative connectivity of the derived Rees algebra is related to the stackiness of the corresponding derived blow-up. It will be interesting to explore this direction further, and perhaps derived blow-ups in centres other than closed immersions are more natural in a context other than derived algebraic geometry.

Future work

There are various ways to expand the present work in order to facilitate applications. We expect that the definition of an algebraic context can be relaxed without changing our main results. An immediate advantage of this should be a streamlined approach to algebraic contexts relative to a base, which is desirable because in the current setup the category
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{R}
of
R
-modules for a given
\mathscr{C}
-algebra
R
is in general not itself an algebraic context.

In order to encompass even more, it is likely one also needs to make some data part of the definition, such as a functor that plays the role of the symmetric algebra functor. We expect that also derived logarithmic geometry will then fall under the scope of geometric contexts.

Finally, any given geometric context should support a notion of étaleness and smoothness, since it includes a well-behaved definition of the cotangent complex. With these definitions in place, it is straightforward to define (higher) algebraic stacks in the context at hand. This could lead to a more refined version of Theorem
4.6
, similar to
[
HKR22
]
. More generally, we expect that there will be a strong link between nonconnectivity on the algebra side and algebraicity on the geometry side.

The development and applications of derived blow-ups

History of derived blow-ups

The first appearance of derived blow-ups is in the proof of Weibel’s conjecture in
[
KST18
]
, which says that a Noetherian scheme of dimension
d
has no non-trivial
K
-groups below degree
-d
. The authors define the derived blow-up of a classical scheme
X
in a centre
Z=V(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
as the derived pullback of the classical blow-up of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
in
\{0\}
along the map
X\to\mathbb{A}^{n}
induced by
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
. With further applications in
K
-theory and in virtual intersection theory in mind (see, e.g.,
[
Kha19
]
,
[
Kha20
]
,
[
Ann21a
]
), this construction is generalized to blow-ups of derived schemes in quasi-smooth centres in
[
KR18
]
. This suggests a generalization to allow arbitrary derived centrers, which is carried out by the second author in
[
Hek21
]
, and further developed and generalized to derived algebraic stacks by Khan, Rydh and the second author in
[
HKR22
]
.

Derived reduction of stabilizers

One of the mayor goals to allow derived blow-ups in arbitrary centres is a derived version of the reduction of stabilizers of classical Artin stacks. Two independent paths have led to this. First, a reduction of stabilizers algorithm for classical Artin stacks with good moduli spaces is given in
[
ER21
]
. Since (saturated) blow-ups are a key ingredient, the main application mentioned in
[
HKR22
]
, as formulated by Rydh, is a derived version of this algorithm.

Second, Kiem–Li–Savvas developed an independent approach to the reduction of stabilizers for classical DM-stacks in
[
KLS17
]
, generalized to classical Artin stack with good moduli spaces in
[
Sav20
]
by Savvas, with applications in generalized Donaldson–Thomas theory and virtual intersection theory in mind. The authors use a construction they call the intrinsic blow-up, which they expected to be the classical shadow of a derived construction.

The goal of a derived stabilizer reduction is partially met by Rydh, Savvas and the second author in
[
HRS22
]
, where a derived reduction of stabilizers algorithm is given under certain finiteness assumptions and over the base scheme
\Spec\mathbb{C}
, using a comparison between intrinsic and derived blow-ups. The work in
[
HRS22
]
has applications in (derived) shifted symplectic geometry and enumerative invariants, such as generalized Donaldson–Thomas theory, and generalized Vafa–Witten invariants.

Deformation to the normal bundle

The deformation to the normal bundle plays a prominent role in classical intersection theory, as in
[
Ful98
]
. A derived version was first defined for quasi-smooth maps of derived schemes in
[
KR18
]
, then generalized to allow arbitrary morphisms of derived algebraic stacks in
[
HKR22
]
. The derived version has found applications in algebraic
K
-theory and virtual intersection theory in
[
Kha22
]
, in derived algebraic cobordism in
[
Ann21b
]
, and in microlocal sheaf theory in
[
Sch22
]
.

Independently of the present work there are (derived) deformation to the normal bundle results in various parts of (derived) analytic geometry. For example, in
[
Ant20
]
such a result for derived analytic spaces is obtained and then used in spreading out Hodge filtrations. The construction is similar to the one found
[
GR17b
]
for the algebraic setting, which is independent from
[
HKR22
]
and involves gluing. A non-derived example can be found in
[
Ni22b
]
, where the author defines a deformation to the normal cone in Arakelov geometry, later used in
[
Ni22a
]
to prove an arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel type theorem.

The advantages of the approach in the present work are as follows: it proposes a unification of all previous constructions, it is robust in the sense that it does not use any glueing, and it is general in the sense that it provides a deformation to the normal bundle for any morphism which allows a cotangent complex (in a given geometric context). Of course, in each example one still has to check whether it coincides with the known formalism for a deformation to the normal bundle. Although interesting, these questions are not pursued in the work at hand.

Assumption 2.1

Definition 2.2

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a stable category with t-structure such that

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
A\in{\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{\mathscr{C}_{\geq 0}}}
, and write
\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{A^{0}}
for the full subcategory of
\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{A}

Proof.

By
[
HTT

\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{A_{\geq 0}}\to\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{A_{\geq 0}}

are compact projective in
\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{A_{\geq 0}}

Consider the functor

H:\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{A_{\geq 0}}\to\mathscr{P}_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{A^{0}})

induced by restricted Yoneda. Recall that
F
is the left Kan extension of
\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{A^{0}}\to\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{A_{\geq 0}}

\displaystyle G:\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{A_{\geq 0}}

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{A_{\geq 0}}

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\mapsto\colim_{(R\to B)\in\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{A^{0}/B}}R

is the full subcategory of

spanned by objects of the form

in the arrow-category

with

Let

Warning 3.30

At the moment, there is no good notion of the underlying topological space of a given
\mathscr{C}
-stack (nor
\mathscr{C}_{\geq 0}
-stack) in general, hence also no good notion of an open substack. Even so, a subobject

In extension, the pseudocomplement should not be thought of as the open complement in general. On the other hand, it seems reasonable to expect that a good definition of underlying topological spaces entails that the pseudocomplement of a closed immersion between connective stacks is an open immersion.

Let

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{\alpha}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{t}_{T}\left(U_{\alpha},\bigsqcup_{\alpha^{\prime}}T_{\alpha^{\prime}}[\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\prime}}]\right)

be a closed immersion of

-stacks. Then the extended Rees algebra

Proof.

corresponds to

. By the deformation to the normal bundle, we have

is connective. From the long exact sequence associated to the fiber sequence

it now follows that the maps

For

, which is homogeneous of weight

\bigsqcup_{f\in R_{1}}\Spec R_{f}\to\Spec R\setminus\Spec R_{0}

is an effective epimorphism for any (possibly nonconnective)
\mathbb{N}
-graded
R
, then Theorem
4.26
holds for any nonconnectively affine morphism
Z\to X
.

5.
Examples

We give our two main examples of a geometric context. Since this involves passing between model 1-categories and
\infty
-categories, we drop our convention that everything is implicitly
\infty
-categorical. We will use the script
\mathsf{C}
for 1-categories, and
\Hom(-,-)
for the mapping sets in 1-categories.

Derived algebraic geometry

The primary example of a geometric context is given by derived algebraic geometry. We spell out some well-known details as an illustration.

Let
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}^{\fgf}
be the 1-category of finitely generated, free
\mathbb{Z}
-modules, put
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{\geq 0}\coloneqq\mathscr{P}_{\Sigma}(\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}^{\fgf})
, and let
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}
be the stabilization of
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{\geq 0}
. The inclusion
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{\geq 0}\to\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}
has a right adjoint
\tau_{\geq 0}
, and this determines a t-structure by
[
HA
, Prop. 1.2.1.16]
, which is both left and right complete by
[
HA
, Prop. 1.2.1.19]
.

The tensor product on
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}^{\fgf}
induces a symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}
through Day-convolution and stabilization. The natural functor
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}^{\fgf}
to the
\infty
-category
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{pc}
of spectra extends to
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{\geq 0}\to\mathscr{S}\mathrm{pc}
by taking the left derived functor, and to
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}\to\mathscr{S}\mathrm{pc}
by completeness. Using
[
SAG
, §25]
, one shows that
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}
(with the t-structure and symmetric monoidal structure just mentioned) is an algebraic context.

We have that
\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{\geq 0}
is the familiar
\infty
-category of simplicial commutative rings, and that
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}
is equivalent to the
\infty
-category of
\mathbb{Z}^{\circ}
-modules in
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{pc}
, where
(-)^{\circ}:\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{\geq 0}\to\mathrm{C}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{\geq 0}
it the unique colimit-preserving functor which sends
\mathbb{Z}[x]
to
\mathbb{Z}[x]\in\mathrm{C}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{\geq 0}
.

We use Example
3.8
to enhance the étale topology on
\mathscr{A}\mathrm{ff}_{\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{\geq 0}}
to a topology on
\mathscr{A}\mathrm{ff}_{\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}}
, also called the
étale topology
. Clearly, this gives a geometric context for which the
\infty
-category of
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{\geq 0}
-stacks recovers ordinary derived algebraic geometry.

Proposition
4.1
shows that, in the connective case, our definition of deformation spaces, hence of derived blow-ups, coincides with the one from
[
HKR22
]
.

Derived analytic geometry

Let
R
be a Banach ring. Recall, this mean that
R
is a ring endowed with a complete norm
\lvert-\rvert
such that the underlying group is a normed group (with respect to
\lvert-\rvert
), and such that there is a constant
C
for which

\lvert xy\rvert\leq C\lvert x\rvert\cdot\lvert y\rvert

holds for all
x,y\in R
. We call
R

non-Archimedean
if

\lvert x+y\rvert\leq\max\{\lvert x\rvert,\lvert y\rvert\}

holds for all
x,y\in R
. Else,
R
is called
Archimedean
, in which case
\lvert x+y\rvert\leq\lvert x\rvert+\lvert y\rvert
holds by definition.

A
Banach
R
-module
is a complete normed abelian group
V
endowed with an
R
-module structure, such that there is a constant
C^{\prime}
for which

\lVert\lambda v\rVert\leq C^{\prime}\lvert\lambda\rvert\cdot\lVert v\rVert

holds for all
\lambda\in R,v\in V
. This gives us the
1
-category of Banach
R
-modules
\mathsf{Ban}_{R}
, with morphisms bounded
R
-linear maps.

Definition 5.1
.

A Banach
R
-module
P
is called
projective
if for any strict epimorphism
f\colon V\to W
of Banach
R
-modules the induced map
\Hom(P,V)\to\Hom(P,W)
of sets is surjective. In this setting, we can define
f
to be a
strict epimorphism
if
V/\ker(f)\to W
is an isomorphism
[
KS06
]
.

The
separated completion
of a semi-normed space
N
is the set of Cauchy-sequences in
N
modulo the sequences which converge to
0
.

The category of Banach modules over
R
carries a symmetric monoidal structure with respect to the
projective tensor product
—written
(-)\widehat{\otimes}_{R}(-)
, and defined as the separated completion of the algebraic tensor product
V\otimes_{R}W
with respect to the
projective semi-norm

\lVert x\rVert\coloneqq\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert v_{i}\rVert\lVert w_{i}\rVert\Bigm|x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\otimes_{R}w_{i}\right\}

for all
x\in V\otimes_{R}W
.

For
R,V,W
non-Archimedean, we can use either the above, or the
non-Archimedean projective tensor product

(-)\widehat{\otimes}^{\mathrm{nA}}_{R}(-)
, which is the separated completion of
V\otimes_{R}W
with respect to the
non-Archimedean projective semi-norm

\lVert x\rVert\coloneqq\inf\left\{\max_{i=1,\dots,n}\{\lVert v_{i}\rVert\lVert w_{i}\rVert\}\Bigm|x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\otimes_{R}w_{i}\right\}

for all
x\in V\otimes_{R}W
.

Remark 5.2
.

Any projective object of
\mathsf{Ban}_{R}
is flat with respect to
\widehat{\otimes}_{R}
. The category of projective Banach
R
-modules is a symmetric monoidal subcategory of the category of Banach
R
-modules.

The category
\mathsf{Ban}_{R}
does not have countable products or coproducts. If we consider the category
\mathsf{Ban}^{\leq 1}_{R}
with the same objects as
\mathsf{Ban}_{R}
, but with morphisms those maps in
\mathsf{Ban}_{R}
which are of norm less than or equal to
1
, then arbitrary products and coproducts do exist. This can be used to show that
\mathsf{Ban}_{R}
has enough projectives, meaning that for any
V\in\mathsf{Ban}_{R}
there is a projective
P
and a strict epimorphism
P\to V
. To see this, denote the coproduct in
\mathsf{Ban}^{\leq 1}_{R}
by
\coprod^{\leq 1}
. Then

\sideset{}{{}^{\leq 1}}{\coprod}_{v\in V\setminus\{0\}}R_{\lVert v\rVert}\longrightarrow V

is a strict epimorphism, and
\coprod_{v\in V-\{0\}}^{\leq 1}R_{\lVert v\rVert}
is projective. We are using the notation
R_{s}
for the module
R
equipped with the norm
|r|_{s}=s|r|
.

The 1-categorical ind-completion
\Ind(\mathsf{Ban}_{R})
is a good setting for merging functional analysis and homological algebra, since it is cocomplete, quasi-abelian, and closed symmetric monoidal, where the symmetric monoidal structure is induced by the projective tensor product. The theory of derived categories of quasi-abelian categories from
[
Sch99
]
is used to produce an
\infty
-categorical version of
\Ind(\mathsf{Ban}_{R})
, which will be the algebraic context of derived analytic geometry over
R
. We review the main steps.

One can use formal colimits of projectives in
\mathsf{Ban}_{R}
as a strictly generating set of projective objects in
\Ind(\mathsf{Ban}_{R})
as follows. The functor
\mathsf{Ban}_{R}\to\Ind(\mathsf{Ban}_{R})
preserves projectives and has as essential image the full subcategory of compact objects of
\Ind(\mathsf{Ban}_{R})
. Let then
\mathsf{P}
be the essential image of the functor
\mathsf{Ban}_{R}\to\Ind(\mathsf{Ban}_{R})
applied to the compact projectives of
\mathsf{Ban}_{R}
. Then for any monomorphism
S\to E
in
\Ind(\mathsf{Ban}_{R})
which is not an isomorphism, there is some
P\to E
with
P\in\mathsf{P}
which does not factor through
E
.

The category of simplicial objects in
\Ind(\mathsf{Ban}_{R})
has a nice (projective) monoidal model category structure, first discussed in
[
BK17
]
. In particular, the weak equivalences are morphisms
X_{\bullet}\to Y_{\bullet}
such that for all
P
projective in
\mathsf{Ban}_{R}
,
\Hom(P,X_{\bullet})\to\Hom(P,Y_{\bullet})
is a weak equivalence of simplicial sets. Now
\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{R_{\geq 0}}
is defined as the
\infty
-category we get from the localization at these morphisms.

Notice that
[
Sch99
, Prop. 2.1.14]
tells us that the left heart of
\Ind(\mathsf{Ban}_{R})
(an abelian category) is equivalent to the category of additive functors from the opposite category of
\mathsf{P}
to the category of abelian groups. Here, the
left heart
is the category of monomorphisms
V\to W
in
\Ind(\mathsf{Ban}_{R})
, localized at the biCartesian squares
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{\prime}}

This category is in turn equivalent to the category of finite product preserving functors from the opposite category of
\mathsf{P}
to the category of sets. It can be shown that
\mathscr{P}_{\Sigma}(\mathsf{P})
is then equivalent to
\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{R_{\geq 0}}
.

Definition 5.3
.

The category
\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{R}
is defined as the stabilization of
\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{R_{\geq 0}}
. There is a t-structure on
\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{R}
for which
\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{R_{\geq 0}}
are the connective objects, and the symmetric monoidal structure with respect
\widehat{\otimes}_{R}
extends to
\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{R}
. Then
\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{R}
is an algebraic context, called the context of
derived analytic geometry over
R
.

If
R
is non-Archimedean, then the same construction but with
\widehat{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathrm{nA}}
instead of
\widehat{\otimes}_{R}
gives us the algebraic context
\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{R}^{\mathrm{nA}}
of
derived non-Archimedean analytic geometry over
R
.

Observe that
\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{R}^{\heartsuit}
is the left heart of
\Ind(\mathsf{Ban}_{R})
by Proposition
2.10
. Now
\mathsf{Ban}_{R}
is not abelian in general, essentially because the image of
V\to W
is the closure of the set-theoretic image. One can thus not hope for an algebraic context which has
\mathsf{Ban}_{R}
at its heart. Since the left heart of
\Ind(\mathsf{Ban}_{R})
is the abelian envelope of
\mathsf{Ban}_{R}
, this construction is in some sense the most economical solution to this problem
[
Sch99
, §1.2.4]
.

Example 5.4
.

The ring of integers endowed with trivial norm is a non-Archimedean Banach ring, written
\mathbb{Z}_{\triv}
, and every abelian group is a Banach
\mathbb{Z}_{\triv}
-module (endowed with trivial norm). For two Banach
\mathbb{Z}_{\triv}
-modules
V,W
with trivial norm, the non-Archimedean projective semi-norm is again trivial, hence the completed tensor product
V\widehat{\otimes}^{\mathrm{nA}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\triv}}W
is just the algebraic tensor product.

One might be tempted to think that the canonical morphism
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}^{\mathrm{nA}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\triv}}
of algebraic contexts is an equivalence. This is however not the case. Indeed, for any prime
p
, the
p
-adic numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(with
p
-adic norm, written
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{p}
for consistency) is a Banach
\mathbb{Z}_{\triv}
-module, since for any nonzero
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\triv}
it holds

\frac{\lVert nx\rVert_{p}}{\lVert x\rVert_{p}}=\lVert n\rVert_{p}=p^{-v_{p}(n)}\leq 1

so we can take
C=1
in the definition of a Banach
{\mathbb{Z}_{\triv}}
-module. Now a quasi-abelian category is always a full subcategory of its left heart
[
Sch99
, Prop. 1.2.27]
, hence
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\in\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\triv}}^{\mathrm{nA},\heartsuit}
, meanwhile any object in
\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\triv}}^{\mathrm{nA},\heartsuit}
which is in the image of
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}^{\mathrm{nA}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\triv}}
has trivial norm.

Remark 5.5
.

By Example
3.8
, any subcanonical topology
J_{\geq 0}
on
\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{R_{\geq 0}}
for which
\mathscr{M}\mathrm{od}_{(-)}\colon\mathscr{A}\mathrm{ff}^{\mathrm{op}}_{\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{R_{\geq 0}}}\to\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}
satisfies descent, induces a geometric context. This part of the theory of analytic geometry is currently still under development, and will appear in
[
BKK
]
.

The primary example of such a topology—which works for any Banach ring
R
, and which has been investigated in
[
BM22
]
—will be the
homotopy monomorphism topology
. Roughly speaking, a covering family for a connective affine
T\in\mathrm{D}\mathscr{A}\mathrm{lg}_{{\mathscr{B}\mathrm{an}_{R}}}^{\mathrm{op}}
is here a finite family of monomorphisms (in the
\infty
-categorical sense)
\{T_{\alpha}\to T\}_{\alpha}
, where each
T_{\alpha}
is affine and connective, such that
\bigsqcup_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}\to T
is an effective epimorphism. Other topologies discussed in
[
BKK
]
include the flat topology, the étale topology, and the Zariski topology.

Appendix A
Set-theoretic background

We will briefly indicate how we deal with size-issues. Fix Grothendieck universes
\mathfrak{U}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{U}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{U}_{2}\subset\cdots
such that
\mathfrak{U}_{i}\in\mathfrak{U}_{i+1}
for all
i
. For each
i
, let
\mathscr{S}_{i}
be the category of
\mathfrak{U}_{i}
-small spaces, and
\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}_{i}
the category of
\mathfrak{U}_{i}
-small categories. This gives us inclusions
\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}_{i}\subset\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}_{i+1}
and
\mathscr{S}_{i}\subset\mathscr{S}_{i+1}
, and moreover
\mathscr{S}_{i},\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}_{i+1}
.

An
i
-small (co)limit
is a (co)limit indexed by some
K\in\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}_{i}
.

Proposition A.1
.

For
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the inclusion
j:\mathscr{S}_{i}\to\mathscr{S}_{i+1}
preserves
i
-small limits and colimits.

Proof.

Write
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{i}
for the category of
i
-small sets. Then
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{i}\subset\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{i+1}
, and this inclusion preserves
i
-small limits and colimits in the 1-categorical sense.

Write now
\mathrm{s}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{i}
for the 1-category
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{i}^{\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}}}
, and let
F:\mathrm{s}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{i}\to\mathrm{s}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{i+1}
be the inclusion induced by
\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{i}\subset\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{i+1}
. We will show that
F
preserves and reflects the model structure of
\mathrm{s}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{i}
, in the sense that
g:K\to L
in
\mathrm{s}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{i}
is a (trivial) fibration, resp. a (trivial) cofibration, resp. a weak equivalence in
\mathrm{s}\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{i}
if and only if
F(g)
is.

Recall that Grothendieck universes are transitive by definition, which means that for
X\in\mathfrak{U}_{i}
and
Y\in X
, it holds
Y\in\mathfrak{U}_{i}
. By the characterization of Kan fibrations as those maps that have the right lifting property with respect to all horn inclusions
\Lambda^{k}[n]\to\Delta[n]
, it then follows that
g
is fibration if and only if
F(g)
is. A similar argument shows the claim on trivial fibrations.

Since cofibrations are those maps with the left lifting property against all trivial fibrations, it follows that
F(g)
being a cofibration implies that
g
is a cofibration as well. Conversely, if
g
is a cofibration, then it can be obtained as a retract of a relative
I
-cell complex, where
I
is the set of horn inclusions. Since both
I
-cell complexes and retracts are preserved by
F
, also
F(g)
is a cofibration. Likewise for the claim on trivial cofibrations.

Now we show that
F
also preserves weak equivalences. Let
g:K\to L
be given. Since the standard bifibrant replacements of
K,L
are preserved by
F
by what we just saw, we may assume without loss of generality that
K,L
are both Kan complexes. Hence, the homotopy groups of
K,L
can be computed purely combinatorially, i.e.,
\pi_{n}(K,x)
is the set of equivalences classes of pointed maps
(S^{n},*)\to(K,x)
modulo simplicial homotopy (relative to the base point), where
S^{n}
is the simplicial
n
-sphere. It follows that
F
preserves the homotopy groups, and hence
g
is a weak equivalence if and only if
F(g)
is.

Using that
F
preserves the model structure, together with the formula for homotopy limits and homotopy colimits in simplicial model categories as given in
[
Hir03
, §18.1]
, one shows that
F
preserves homotopy limits and homotopy colimits. Moreover, since
F
is homotopical, it is both its own left and right derived functor. In other words, localizing
F
at the weak equivalences produces the functor
j
. Since
F
preserves homotopy limits and homotopy colimits,
j
thus preserves limits and colimits in the
\infty
-categorical sense.
∎

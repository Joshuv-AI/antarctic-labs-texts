Representations and cohomologies of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras and applications
Jun Jiang, Yunhe Sheng

Abstract
. In this paper, first we give the notion of a representation of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra and introduce the cohomologies of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra with coefficients in a representation. Then we classify abelian extensions of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras using the second cohomology group, and classify skeletal relative Rota-Baxter Lie 2-algebras using the third cohomology group as applications. At last, using the established general framework of representations and cohomologies of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras, we give the notion of representations of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras, which is consistent with representations of Rota-Baxter associative algebras in the literature, and introduce the cohomologies of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras with coefficients in a representation. Applications are also given to classify abelian extensions of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras and skeletal Rota-Baxter Lie 2-algebras.



1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to introduce the cohomology theory of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra with coefficients in a representation and give applications.

G. Baxter introduced the concept of Rota-Baxter operators on associative algebras
in his study of
fluctuation theory in probability
[
3
]
. Recently it has found many
applications, including Connes-Kreimer’s
[
6
]
algebraic
approach to the renormalization in perturbative quantum field
theory. There are close connections between Rota-Baxter operators and noncommutative
symmetric functions and Hopf algebras
[
10
,
14
,
29
]
. Recently the relationship between Rota-Baxter operators and double Poisson algebras were studied in
[
13
]
. For further details on
Rota-Baxter operators, see
[
15
,
16
]
.
In the Lie algebra context, a Rota-Baxter operator was introduced independently in the 1980s as the
operator form of the classical Yang-Baxter equation that plays important roles in both mathematics and mathematical physics such as integrable
systems and quantum groups
[
4
,
25
]
. B. A.
Kupershmidt introduced a more general notion, an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator on a Lie algebra (later also called
a relative Rota-Baxter operator or a generalized Rota-Baxter operator) in his study of the classical Yang-Baxter equation and
related integrable systems
[
18
]
. Relative Rota-Baxter operators provide solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter equation in the semidirect product Lie algebra and give rise to pre-Lie algebras
[
2
]
.

Recently the theories of deformations and homotopies of relative Rota-Baxter operators and relative Rota-Baxter algebras (both Lie and associative) are well developed in
[
7
,
8
,
19
,
27
]
. Among these studies, a cohomology theory was given to classify infinitesimal deformations and characterize the extendability of order
n
deformations to order
n+1
deformations. Let us briefly recall the approach taken in these studies. The first step is to construct the ‘controlling algebra’ of the algebraic structure under consideration. It is well known that the controlling algebras of associative algebras and Lie algebras are graded Lie algebras given by the Gerstenhaber bracket and the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket respectively
[
11
,
12
,
21
,
22
]
. In
[
19
]
, using higher derived brackets
[
28
]
, the authors constructed the controlling algebra of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras, which turns out to be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. More precisely, an
L_{\infty}
-algebra was given whose Maurer-Cartan elements are relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra structures. Then using the controlling algebra, one can give a cohomology theory that can be applied to study infinitesimal deformations and formal deformations.

In general a cohomology theory is related to the representation theory. However, the cohomology theory developed in the above literatures was not based on representations of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras. Thus it is desirable to give the notion of representations of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras and develop the corresponding cohomology theory that reduces to the cohomology given in
[
19
]
when the representation is the adjoint representation. This is the purpose of this paper. It is well known that a relative Rota-Baxter operator induces a pre-Lie algebra. We generalize this correspondence to the context of representations and cohomologies. We also give applications of the cohomologies of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra with coefficients in a representation. More precisely, we use the second cohomology group to classify abelian extensions of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras, and use the third cohomology group to classify relative Rota-Baxter Lie 2-algebras, which was introduced in
[
26
]
under the terminology of
{\mathcal{O}}
-operators on Lie 2-algebras in the study of solutions of 2-graded Yang-Baxter equations. Note that in the associative algebra context, the notion of a representation (module) over a Rota-Baxter associative algebra was given in
[
17
]
, and further studied in
[
20
,
23
,
24
,
30
]
. The representation of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra introduced in this paper is consistent with the representation of Rota-Baxter associative algebras, namely a representation of a Rota-Baxter associative algebra naturally gives rise to a representation of the corresponding Rota-Baxter Lie algebra.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, first we give the notion of a representation of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra and show that a representation can be characterized by the semidirect product relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra. We also construct the dual representation. We also show that a representation of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra induces a representation of the underlying pre-Lie algebra. In Section
3
, we introduce the cohomology theory of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra with coefficients in a representation. 1-cocycles can be used to characterize derivations on a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra. We establish the relation between the cohomology of a relative Rota-Baxter operator and the cohomology of the underlying pre-Lie algebra. In Section
4
, we introduce the notion of abelian extensions of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras and show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between equivalent classes of abelian extensions and the second cohomology group. In Section
5
, first we recall the notion of relative Rota-Baxter Lie 2-algebras, and then we show that skeletal relative Rota-Baxter Lie 2-algebras are classified by the third cohomology group. Finally in Section
6
, we introduce representations and cohomologies of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras using the above general framework of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras and give various applications.

2.
Representations of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras

In this section, we introduce the notion of a representation of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra. We give the semidirect products characterization of representations of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras. The dual representation is given. A representation of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra also induces a representation of the underlying pre-Lie algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

(i)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie algebra.
A linear operator
T:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is called a
Rota-Baxter operator
if

[T(x),T(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T\big([T(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,T(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}\big),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Moreover, a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with a Rota-Baxter operator
T
is
called a
Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
. We denote it by
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},T)
.

(ii)

A
relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
is a triple
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V,\rho),T)
, where
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a Lie algebra,
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on a vector space
V
and
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a
relative Rota-Baxter operator
, i.e.

[Tu,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T(\rho(Tu)(v)-\rho(Tv)(u)),\quad\forall u,v\in V.

Note that a Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebra is a relative Rota-Baxter operator with respect to the adjoint representation.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V,\rho),T)
and
((\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}),(V^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}),T^{\prime})
be two relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras. A homomorphism from
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V,\rho),T)
to
((\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}),(V^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}),T^{\prime})
consists of a Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
and a linear map
\varphi:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle T^{\prime}\circ\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi\circ T,

\displaystyle\varphi\rho(x)(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}(\phi(x))(\varphi(u)),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V.

It is well known that a classical skew-symmetric
r
-matrix
r\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
gives rise to a relative Rota-Baxter operator
r^{\sharp}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the coadjoint representation. We give another interesting example.

Example 2.3
.

Let
W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{T}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{h}
be a complex of vector spaces. We define

\mathrm{End}(W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{T}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{h})=\{(A_{0},A_{1})|A_{0}\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h}),A_{1}\in\mathfrak{gl}(W),A_{0}\circ\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}\circ A_{1}\}.

Then

((\mathrm{End}(W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{T}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{h}),[\cdot,\cdot]),(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\ker\mathcal{T}),\varsigma),\Omega)

is a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra, where the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
on
\mathrm{End}(W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{T}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{h})
is given by

[(A_{0},A_{1}),(B_{0},B_{1})]=([A_{0},B_{0}],[A_{1},B_{1}]),\quad\forall(A_{0},A_{1}),(B_{0},B_{1})\in\mathrm{End}(W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{T}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{h}),

the representation
\varsigma
of the Lie algebra
(\mathrm{End}(W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{T}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{h}),[\cdot,\cdot])
on
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\ker\mathcal{T})
is given by

\displaystyle\varsigma(A_{0},A_{1})(\Phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{1}\circ\Phi-\Phi\circ A_{0},\quad\forall\Phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\ker\mathcal{T}),

and the relative Rota-Baxter operator
\Omega:\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\ker\mathcal{T})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{End}(W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{T}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{h})
is given by

\displaystyle\Omega(\Phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(0,\Phi\circ\mathcal{T}).

Now we introduce the notion of a representation of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra.

Definition 2.4
.

A
representation of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra

((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V,\rho),T)
on a 2-term complex of vector spaces
W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{T}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{h}
is a triple
(\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\rho_{W},\mu)
, where
\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
and
\rho_{W}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(W)
are representations of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on vector spaces
\mathfrak{h}
and
W
respectively, and
\mu:V\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},W)
is a linear map such that
the following equations are satisfied:

(1)

\displaystyle\mu(\rho(x)u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{W}(x)\mu(u)-\mu(u)\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(x),

(2)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(T(u))\circ\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\circ\rho_{W}(T(u))+\mathcal{T}\circ\mu(u)\circ\mathcal{T},

for all
x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V.

We denote a representation by
[W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{T}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{h},\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\rho_{W},\mu]
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V,\rho),T)
be a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra. Define
\bar{\rho}:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},V)
by

\bar{\rho}(u)(x)=-\rho(x)u.

Then it is straightforward to see that
[V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{ad},\rho,\bar{\rho}]
is a representation, which is called the
adjoint representation of the relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra

((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V,\rho),T)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
[W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{T}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{h},\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\rho_{W},\mu]
be a representation of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V,\rho),T)
. Then
((\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\ltimes}),(V\oplus W,\varrho),\mathfrak{T})
is a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra, where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\ltimes}
is the semidirect product Lie bracket given by

[x+\alpha,y+\beta]_{\ltimes}=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)\beta-\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(y)\alpha,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{h},

the representation
\varrho:\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V\oplus W)
is given by

\displaystyle\varrho(x+\alpha)(u+\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)u+\rho_{W}(x)\xi-\mu(u)\alpha,\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},\alpha\in\mathfrak{h},u\in V,\xi\in W,

and the relative Rota-Baxter operator
\mathfrak{T}:V\oplus W\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}(u+\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(u)+\mathcal{T}(\xi).

This relative Rota Baxter Lie algebra is called the
semidirect product
of
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V,\rho),T)
and the representation
[W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{T}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{h},\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\rho_{W},\mu]
.

Proof.

Since
\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
, it is obvious that
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\ltimes})
is a Lie algebra.

By (
1
), we have

\displaystyle[\varrho(x+\alpha),\varrho(y+\beta)](u+\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varrho(x+\alpha)\varrho(y+\beta)(u+\xi)-\varrho(y+\beta)\varrho(x+\alpha)(u+\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varrho(x+\alpha)(\rho(y)u+\rho_{W}(y)\xi-\mu(u)\beta)

\displaystyle-\varrho(y+\beta)(\rho(x)u+\rho_{W}(x)\xi-\mu(u)\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)\rho(y)u+\rho_{W}(x)\rho_{W}(y)\xi-\rho_{W}(x)\mu(u)\beta-\mu(\rho(y)u)\alpha

\displaystyle-\rho(y)\rho(x)u-\rho_{W}(y)\rho_{W}(x)\xi+\rho_{W}(y)\mu(u)\alpha+\mu(\rho(x)u)\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\rho(x),\rho(y)]u+[\rho_{W}(x),\rho_{W}(y)]\xi

\displaystyle+\rho_{W}(y)\mu(u)\alpha-\mu(\rho(y)u)\alpha+\mu(\rho(x)u)\beta-\rho_{W}(x)\mu(u)\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\rho(x),\rho(y)]u+[\rho_{W}(x),\rho_{W}(y)]\xi+\mu(u)\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(y)\alpha-\mu(u)\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)\beta.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\varrho([x+\alpha,y+\beta]_{\ltimes})(u+\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varrho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)\beta-\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(y)\alpha)(u+\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})u+\rho_{W}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})\xi+\mu(u)\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(y)\alpha-\mu(u)\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)\beta.

Since
\rho
and
\rho_{W}
are representations of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V
and
W
respectively, we have

\varrho([x+\alpha,y+\beta]_{\ltimes})(u+\xi)=[\varrho(x+\alpha),\varrho(y+\beta)](u+\xi),

which implies that
\varrho
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\ltimes})
on
V\oplus W
.

By (
2
), we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}(\varrho(\mathfrak{T}(u_{1}+\xi_{1}))(u_{2}+\xi_{2}))-\mathfrak{T}(\varrho(\mathfrak{T}(u_{2}+\xi_{2}))(u_{1}+\xi_{1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(T+\mathcal{T})(\varrho(T(u_{1})+\mathcal{T}(\xi_{1}))(u_{2}+\xi_{2}))-(T+\mathcal{T})(\varrho(T(u_{2})+\mathcal{T}(\xi_{2}))(u_{1}+\xi_{1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(T+\mathcal{T})(\rho(T(u_{1}))u_{2}+\rho_{W}(T(u_{1}))\xi_{2}-\mu(u_{2})\mathcal{T}(\xi_{1}))

\displaystyle-(T+\mathcal{T})(\rho(T(u_{2}))u_{1}+\rho_{W}(T(u_{2}))\xi_{1}-\mu(u_{1})\mathcal{T}(\xi_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(\rho(T(u_{1}))u_{2})+\mathcal{T}(\rho_{W}(T(u_{1}))\xi_{2}-\mu(u_{2})\mathcal{T}(\xi_{1}))

\displaystyle-T(\rho(T(u_{2}))u_{1})-\mathcal{T}(\rho_{W}(T(u_{2}))\xi_{1}-\mu(u_{1})\mathcal{T}(\xi_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(\rho(T(u_{1}))u_{2})-T(\rho(T(u_{2}))u_{1})+\mathcal{T}(\rho_{W}(T(u_{1}))\xi_{2})+\mathcal{T}(\mu(u_{1})\mathcal{T}(\xi_{2}))

\displaystyle-\mathcal{T}(\mu(u_{2})\mathcal{T}(\xi_{1}))-\mathcal{T}(\rho_{W}(T(u_{2}))\xi_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(\rho(T(u_{1}))u_{2})-T(\rho(T(u_{2}))u_{1})+\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(T(u_{1}))\mathcal{T}(\xi_{2})-\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(T(u_{2}))\mathcal{T}(\xi_{1}).

Moreover,

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{T}(u_{1}+\xi_{1}),\mathfrak{T}(u_{2}+\xi_{2})]_{\ltimes}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[T(u_{1})+\mathcal{T}(\xi_{1}),T(u_{2})+\mathcal{T}(\xi_{2})]_{\ltimes}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[T(u_{1}),T(u_{2})]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(T(u_{1}))\mathcal{T}(\xi_{2})-\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(T(u_{2}))\mathcal{T}(\xi_{1}).

Since
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator, we have

[\mathfrak{T}(u_{1}+\xi_{1}),\mathfrak{T}(u_{2}+\xi_{2})]_{\ltimes}=\mathfrak{T}(\varrho(\mathfrak{T}(u_{1}+\xi_{1}))(u_{2}+\xi_{2}))-\mathfrak{T}(\varrho(\mathfrak{T}(u_{2}+\xi_{2}))(u_{1}+\xi_{1})),

which implies that
\mathfrak{T}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\ltimes})
with respect the representation
(V\oplus W,\varrho)
.
Thus
((\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\ltimes}),(V\oplus W,\varrho),\mathfrak{T})
is a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra.
∎

Next we consider the
dual representation
of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra. Let
[W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{T}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{h},\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\rho_{W},\mu]
be a representation of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V,\rho),T)
. Define
\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
and
\rho_{W}^{*}
by

\langle\rho^{*}_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)\zeta,\alpha\rangle=-\langle\zeta,\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)\alpha\rangle,\quad\langle\rho^{*}_{W}(x)\epsilon,\xi\rangle=-\langle\epsilon,\rho_{W}(x)\xi\rangle,

for all
x\in\mathfrak{g},\alpha\in\mathfrak{h},\xi\in W,\zeta\in\mathfrak{h}^{*},\epsilon\in W^{*}
. Then it is well known that
\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
and
\rho_{W}^{*}
are representations of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and
W^{*}
respectively. We consider the complex
\mathfrak{h}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-\mathcal{T}^{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}W^{*}
, where
\mathcal{T}^{*}
is the usual dual of
\mathcal{T}
, i.e.
\langle\mathcal{T}^{*}(\zeta),\xi\rangle=\langle\zeta,\mathcal{T}(\xi)\rangle
. Define
\mu^{*}:V\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(W^{*},\mathfrak{h}^{*})
by

\langle\mu^{*}(u)\epsilon,\alpha\rangle=\langle\epsilon,-\mu(u)\alpha\rangle,\quad\forall u\in V,\epsilon\in W^{*},\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
[W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{T}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{h},\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\rho_{W},\mu]
be a representation of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V,\rho),T)
. Then
[\mathfrak{h}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-\mathcal{T}^{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}W^{*},\rho_{W}^{*},\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\mu^{*}]
is a representation of
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V,\rho),T)
, called the
dual representation
.

Proof.

Since
\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
and
\rho_{W}^{*}
are representations of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and
W^{*}
, we only need to prove the following two equalities:

(3)

\displaystyle\mu^{*}(\rho(x)u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(x)\mu^{*}(u)-\mu^{*}(u)\rho_{W}^{*}(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V,

(4)

\displaystyle\rho_{W}^{*}(T(u))\circ(-\mathcal{T}^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-\mathcal{T}^{*})\circ\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(T(u))+(-\mathcal{T}^{*})\circ\mu^{*}(u)\circ(-\mathcal{T}^{*}).

By (
1
), for all
\epsilon\in W^{*},\alpha\in\mathfrak{h},
we have

\displaystyle\langle\mu^{*}(\rho(x)u)\epsilon,~\alpha\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\epsilon,~\mu(\rho(x)u)\alpha\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\epsilon,~\rho_{W}(x)\mu(u)\alpha-\mu(u)\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)\alpha\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{W}^{*}(x)\epsilon,~\mu(u)\alpha\rangle-\langle\mu^{*}(u)\epsilon,~\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)\alpha\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\mu^{*}(u)\rho_{W}^{*}(x)\epsilon,~\alpha\rangle+\langle\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(x)\mu^{*}(u)\epsilon,~\alpha\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(x)\mu^{*}(u)\epsilon-\mu^{*}(u)\rho_{W}^{*}(x)\epsilon,~\alpha\rangle,

which implies that (
3
) holds.

By (
2
), for all
\xi\in W,\zeta\in\mathfrak{h}^{*},
we have

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{W}^{*}(T(u))(-\mathcal{T}^{*})(\zeta),~\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}^{*}(\zeta),~\rho_{W}(T(u))\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\zeta,~\mathcal{T}(\rho_{W}(T(u))\xi)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\zeta,~\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(T(u))\mathcal{T}(\xi)-\mathcal{T}(\mu(u)\mathcal{T}(\xi))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(T(u))\zeta,~\mathcal{T}(\xi)\rangle-\langle\mathcal{T}^{*}(\zeta),~\mu(u)(\mathcal{T}(\xi))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(-\mathcal{T}^{*})(\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(T(u))(\zeta)),~\xi\rangle+\langle(-\mathcal{T}^{*})(\mu^{*}(u)(-\mathcal{T}^{*})(\zeta)),~\xi\rangle,

which implies that (
4
) holds.

Therefore,
[\mathfrak{h}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-\mathcal{T}^{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}W^{*},\rho_{W}^{*},\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\mu^{*}]
is a representation of
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V,\rho),T)
.
∎

Example 2.8
.

Let
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V,\rho),T)
be a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra.
Then by Example
2.5
and Proposition
2.7
,
[\mathfrak{g}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-T^{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}V^{*},\rho^{*},\mathrm{ad}^{*},\bar{\rho}^{*}]
is a representation, which is called the
coadjoint representation of the relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra

((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(V,\rho),T)
.

At the end of this section, we establish the relationship between representations of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras and representations of the underlying pre-Lie algebras. Recall that a
pre-Lie algebra
is a pair
(A,\cdot_{A})
, where
A
is a vector space and
\cdot_{A}:A\otimes A\longrightarrow A
is a bilinear multiplication
satisfying that for all
x,y,z\in A
,

(x\cdot_{A}y)\cdot_{A}z-x\cdot_{A}(y\cdot_{A}z)=(y\cdot_{A}x)\cdot_{A}z-y\cdot_{A}(x\cdot_{A}z).

A pre-Lie algebra
(A,\cdot_{A})
gives rise to a Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{C})
, where the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{C}
is the commutator of the pre-Lie multiplication. A
representation
of a pre-Lie algebra
(A,\cdot_{A})
on a vector
space
W
consists of a pair

(\theta,\vartheta)
, where
\theta:A\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(W)
is a representation
of the Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{C})
on
W
and
\vartheta:A\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(W)
is a linear
map satisfying

(5)

\displaystyle\theta(x)\vartheta(y)u-\vartheta(y)\theta(x)u=\vartheta(x\cdot_{A}y)u-\vartheta(y)\vartheta(x)u,\quad\forall x,y\in A,~u\in W.

It is well known that a relative Rota-Baxter operator

Proof.

Since

(6)

\theta([u,v]_{C})=\rho_{W}(T(\rho(T(u))v-\rho(T(v)u)))=\rho_{W}([T(u),T(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}})=[\theta(u),\theta(v)],

By (

\displaystyle\theta(u)\vartheta(v)\xi-\vartheta(v)\theta(u)\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\theta(u)\mu(v)\mathcal{T}(\xi)-\vartheta(v)\rho_{W}(T(u))\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\rho_{W}(T(u))\mu(v)\mathcal{T}(\xi)+\mu(v)\mathcal{T}\rho_{W}(T(u))\xi

6.
Representations and cohomologies of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras

In this section, we adapt the previous general framework of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras to give representations and cohomologies of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras. Applications of the second and the third cohomology groups will also be given to classify abelian extensions of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras and skeletal Rota-Baxter Lie 2-algebras.

Definition 6.1
.

A
representation of a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},T)
on a vector space
W
with respect to a linear transformation
\mathcal{T}\in\mathfrak{gl}(W)
is a representation
\rho_{W}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on
W
, satisfying

\rho_{W}(T(x))\circ\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}\circ\rho_{W}(T(x))+\mathcal{T}\circ\rho_{W}(x)\circ\mathcal{T},\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Denote a representation by
[W;\mathcal{T},\rho_{W}]
.

Example 6.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},T)
be a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra.
Then it is straightforward to see that
[\mathfrak{g};T,\mathrm{ad}]
is a representation, which is called the
adjoint representation
of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},T)
.

Similar to Proposition
2.7
, we have the following result.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
[W;\mathcal{T},\rho_{W}]
be a representation of a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},T)
. Then
[W^{*};-\mathcal{T}^{*},\rho_{W}^{*}]
is also a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},T)
, which is called the
dual representation
.

Example 6.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},T)
be a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra. Then
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};-T^{*},\mathrm{ad}^{*})
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},T)
, which is called the
coadjoint representation
.

Similar to Proposition
2.6
, we also have the following semidirect product characterization of representations of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
[W;\mathcal{T},\rho_{W}]
be a representation of a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},T)
. Then
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus W,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\ltimes},\mathfrak{T})
is a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra, where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\ltimes}
is the semidirect product Lie bracket given by

[x+u,y+v]_{\ltimes}=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho_{W}(x)v-\rho_{W}(y)u,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in W,

and
\mathfrak{T}:\mathfrak{g}\oplus W\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\oplus W
is a linear map given by

\mathfrak{T}(x+u)=T(x)+\mathcal{T}(u),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in W.

Remark 6.6
.

Let
(A,T)
be a Rota-Baxter associative algebra, i.e.
A
is an associative algebra and
T:A\,\rightarrow\,A
is a linear map satisfying

T(x)T(y)=T(T(x)y+xT(y)),\quad\forall x,y\in A.

In
[
17
]
, the authors defined a left Rota-Baxter module over
(A,T)
to be a pair
(V,\mathcal{T})
with a left
A
-module
V
and a linear map
\mathcal{T}:V\rightarrow V
satisfying

T(x)\mathcal{T}(u)=\mathcal{T}\big(x\mathcal{T}(u)+T(x)u\big),\quad\forall x\in A,u\in V.

It is obvious that a Rota-Baxter associative algebra gives rise to a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],T)
, where the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
is the commutator Lie bracket. Furthermore, it is straightforward to deduce that a left Rota-Baxter module
(V,\mathcal{T})
gives rise to a representation
\rho
of the Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],T)
on
V
with respect to
\mathcal{T}:V\rightarrow V
, where
\rho(x)u=xu
, for all
x\in A,u\in V
. Thus the definition of representations of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras is consistent with representations of Rota-Baxter associative algebras given in
[
17
]
.

Let
[W;\mathcal{T},\rho_{W}]
be a representation of a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},T)
. Define the set of 0-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},T;\rho_{W})
to be 0, and define the set of
1
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},T;\rho_{W})
to be
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},W)
. For
n\geq 2
, we define the set of
n
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},T;\rho_{W})
by

\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},T;\rho_{W})=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},W)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g},W).

Define the coboundary operator

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{RB}}:\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},T;\rho_{W})\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{C}^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g},T;\rho_{W})

by

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{RB}}(f,\theta)=(\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}f,\partial\theta+h_{T}(f)),\quad\forall f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},W),\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g},W),

where

•

\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}:\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},W)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},W)
is the Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operator of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in the representation
(W,\rho_{W})
.

•

h_{T}:\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},W)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},W)
is defined by

\displaystyle h_{T}(f)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}f(T(x_{1}),T(x_{2}),\cdots,T(x_{n}))

\displaystyle-(-1)^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{T}(f(T(x_{1}),\cdots,T(x_{i-1}),x_{i},T(x_{i+1}),\cdots,T(x_{n}))).

•

\partial:\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g},W)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},W)
is defined by

\displaystyle\partial\theta(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}\rho_{W}(T(x_{i}))\theta(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}\mathcal{T}\big(\rho_{W}(x_{i})\theta(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n})\big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(-1)^{i+j}\theta([T(x_{i}),x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[T(x_{j}),x_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,\widehat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n}).

Theorem 6.7
.

With the above notations,
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},T;\rho_{W}),\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{RB}})
is a cochain complex, i.e.

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{RB}}\circ\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{RB}}=0.

Proof.

We only give a sketch of the proof and leave details to readers. Let
[W;\mathcal{T},\rho_{W}]
be a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},T)
. Then
[W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{T}}}{{\rightarrow}}W;\rho_{W},\rho_{W},\rho_{W}]
is a representation of the corresponding relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{ad}),T)
. Consider the cochain complex

(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{ad},T;\rho_{W},\rho_{W},\rho_{W}),\mathcal{D}_{R}),

given in Theorem
3.2
. It is straightforward to deduce that

(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},T;\rho_{W}),\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{RB}})
is its subcomplex. Thus
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{RB}}\circ\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{RB}}=0.

∎

Definition 6.8
.

The cohomology of the cochain complex
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},T;\rho_{W}),\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{RB}})
is called
the cohomology of the Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
with coefficients in the representation
[W;\mathcal{T},\rho_{W}]
. The corresponding
n
-th cohomology group is denoted by
\mathcal{H}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},T;\rho_{W})
.

We consider the first cohomology group
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},T;\rho_{W})
. A linear map
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},W)=\mathfrak{C}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},T;\rho_{W})
is a
1
-cocycle if and only if

\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}f=0,\quad f(T(x))=\mathcal{T}(f(x)),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Since
\mathfrak{C}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},T;\rho_{W})=0
, there is no
1
-coboundaries. Thus we have

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},T;\rho_{W})=\{f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},W)|f\circ T=\mathcal{T}\circ f,~\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}f=0\}.

Recall derivations on a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra from Definition
3.5
. Similar to Proposition
3.6
, we have the following characterization of derivations on a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra.

Proposition 6.9
.

A derivation on a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},T)
is a
1
-cocycle on
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},T)
with coefficients in the adjoint representation.

Now we introduce the notion of abelian extensions of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras and briefly show that they are classified by the second cohomology group.

.

is an abelian Lie algebra.

Definition 6.14
.

A
Rota-Baxter Lie
2
-algebra
consists of a Lie
2
-algebra
\mathcal{G}=(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathrm{d},\mathfrak{l}_{2},\mathfrak{l}_{3})
and a relative Rota-Baxter operator
\Theta=(T_{0},T_{1},T_{2})
on
\mathcal{G}
with respect to the adjoint representation
(\mathrm{ad}_{0},\mathrm{ad}_{1},\mathrm{ad}_{2})
. More precisely, the following equalities hold:

(i)

T_{0}\big(\mathfrak{l}_{2}(T_{0}(x),y)-\mathfrak{l}_{2}(T_{0}(y),x)\big)-\mathfrak{l}_{2}(T_{0}(x),T_{0}(y))=\mathrm{d}T_{2}(x,y);

(ii)

(iii)

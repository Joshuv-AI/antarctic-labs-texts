Invariance of velocity angles and flocking in the Inertial Spin model
Ioannis Markou

Abstract
We study the invariance of velocity angles and flocking properties of the Inertial Spin model introduced by Cavagna et al. [J. Stat. Phys., 158, (2015), 601–627]. We present a novel approach, based on a second order nonlinear Gronwall inequality for the velocity diameter, to identify invariant regions and study the flocking behavior of the model. This is an approach inspired by the work of Choi-Ha-Yun in the study of synchronization for kuramoto oscillators with finite inertia [Physica D, 240, (2011), 32–44]. We give sufficient conditions in terms of the parameters of the model and the initial data, so that invariant regions and asymptotic alignment of velocities is possible for coupling interactions that satisfy general assumptions.



Keywords
: Inertial Spin model, Vicsek model, unit speed constraint.

2010 MR Subject Classification
: 82C22, 34D05, 34C15.

[

etc. The research in the above otherwise simple models has followed so many different paths, that it would be futile to mention the entire list. For instance, we may direct the reader to following literature e.g. in Vicsek type models

In this paper we study emergence behavior in a relatively new model called
the Inertial Spin (IS) model that was introduced to
describe the mechanism in which collective turns occur in groups of birds.
The IS model is a model for the collective
dynamics of particles that takes into account, not only position and velocity of particles, but also
the inertial angular momentum (spin) of each agent. The model was introduced in
[
1
,
6
]
and
was later studied in
[
5
,
26
,
39
]
. Its introduction and justification came to treat one major flaw
of the classical Vicsek model
[
40
]
. Indeed, the classical Vicsek model describes the
evolution of self-propelled particles in 2-d having a constant speed, where the direction of the
velocity is updated by computing the average of neighboring directions.
One of the drawbacks of the Vicsek model is the inadequacy to describe how collective turns occur. In experiments
that have been performed in real flocks it has been observed that when a bird starts to turn, then the
turning information is propagated along the group and the whole group performs a collective turn. This has been
addressed with the introduction of an extra (spin) variable that accounts for the turning information. In the
new system each particle is described by vectors
x_{i},v_{i},s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
that stand for the position, velocity and spin of the
i^{\prime}
th particle.

We describe the Inertial Spin model by a system of ODEs for the positions, velocities, and spins, i.e.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dot{x}_{i}(t)&=v_{i}(t),\quad i=1\ldots,N,\quad t>0,\\
\\
\dot{v}_{i}(t)&=\frac{1}{\chi}s_{i}(t)\times v_{i}(t),\\
\\
\dot{s}_{i}(t)&=v_{i}(t)\times\left[\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{ij}(v_{j}(t)-v_{i}(t))-\gamma\dot{v}_{i}(t)\right],\end{array}\right.

with prescribed initial conditions that satisfy:

(x_{i},v_{i},s_{i})\Big|_{t=0}=(x_{i0},v_{i0},s_{i0}),\quad s_{i0}\cdot v_{i0}=1,\,\text{and normalized initial speed}\quad|v_{i0}|=1,\quad 1\leq i\leq N.

(6)

Here the constant
\chi>0
is the moment of inertia,
\gamma>0
is the
friction (damping) parameter, and
k>0
is the coupling strength of interactions. The
matrix
(\psi_{ij})_{i,j}
is the matrix of communication weights between particles
i
and
j

which measures the strength of the social interaction between the two agents.
Since all the vectors are in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
we denote by
\cdot
and
\times
the
usual inner (dot) and outer (cross) product between two vectors. Similarly, by
|\cdot|
we denote
the induced euclidian distance in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We also consider the vectors of positions, velocities and spins defined by

x:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{3N},\qquad v:=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{3N},\qquad s:=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{3N}.

(7)

The derivation of the IS system is beyond the scope of this paper and can be found in e.g.

[
6
,
26
]
.

The final component of system (
1
) that we address is the communication matrix
(\psi_{ij})_{ij}
.
The
\psi_{ij}
element of the communication matrix describes the social interaction
between the
i
and
j
agents. The minimum of assumptions that we make for
\psi_{ij}
is that the
(\psi_{ij})_{ij}
matrix
is nonnegative, symmetric i.e.
\psi_{ij}=\psi_{ji}
for all pairs
i,j
, and its elements are bounded by some
\psi_{M}>0
.
There are two main frameworks: The first one is a general framework that considers variable in time weights, with only basic assumption a lower bound on the

\psi_{ij}
values, i.e that

\psi_{m}\leq\psi_{ij}(t)\leq\psi_{M},\qquad\text{for some}\quad\psi_{M}>\psi_{m}>0,\quad\forall i,j\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

(8)

This assumption is considerably more general than the case of constant communication weights, and is very useful in systems with variable social interactions.
Another realistic framework is the case where
\psi_{ij}:=\psi(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)
is
a function of the metric distance between the
i,j
particles. The function
\psi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
in this framework is bounded, decreasing, and Lipschitz continuous,

\displaystyle 0<\psi(r)\leq\psi(0),\qquad[\psi]_{Lip}:=\sup\limits_{x\neq y}\frac{|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|}{|x-y|}<\infty,

(9)

\displaystyle(\psi(r_{2})-\psi(r_{1}))(r_{2}-r_{1})\leq 0,\quad\forall r,r_{1},r_{2}>0.

A classical
example in this category is the communication rate in the
classical Cucker-Smale model
[
18
,
19
]
, where
\psi(r)

takes the form
\psi(r)=(1+r^{2})^{-\beta/2}
for some
\beta>0
. In this article we will work mainly with the
first framework, where communication weights are allowed to vary with time but have a strong positive bound from below.

2

3

4

5
, we give important lemmas that allow the treatment of the inequalities we derived in Section
4
. Finally, in Section
6
we prove the main results.

Preliminaries and Main results

2.1
Preliminaries and Definitions

The Inertial Spin model enjoys some important properties (e.g. conservations, invariances etc) which
we try to include in this section, starting with the following Proposition from

[
26
]
(see Propositions 2.1 & 2.2).

Proposition 1
.

[
26
]

Assume that
(x(t),v(t),s(t))
is the solution to system (
1
), with initial
conditions
(x_{0},v_{0},s_{0})
that satisfy (
6
). Then, the following assertions hold:

(i) The speed of each particle is conserved,

\frac{d}{dt}|v_{i}(t)|=0,\quad t>0,\quad i=1,\ldots,N.

(ii) The inner product of the spin and velocity is conserved,

\frac{d}{dt}(s_{i}(t)\cdot v_{i}(t))=0,\quad t>0,\quad i=1,\ldots,N.

(iii) The average spin defined by
s_{c}(t):=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}s_{i}(t)
dissipates with rate
\frac{\gamma}{\chi}
, i.e.

s_{c}(t)=s_{c}(0)e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}t}.

(iv) System (
1
) has rotational symmetry, i.e. for an orthogonal
3\times 3
matrix
O\in O(3)
and
the new dynamical variables
\widetilde{x}_{i},\widetilde{v}_{i},\widetilde{s}_{i}
defined by

\widetilde{x}_{i}:=Ox_{i},\qquad\widetilde{v}_{i}:=Ov_{i},\qquad\widetilde{s}_{i}:=Os_{i},

then

(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{v},\widetilde{s})
is also a solution of (
1
).

Simple geometric identities offer some further insights on the system (
1
). For a start, the
identity
v_{i}\times(s_{i}\times v_{i})=(v_{i}\cdot v_{i})s_{i}-(s_{i}\cdot v_{i})v_{i}=s_{i}
means that we can rewrite the spin equation
(1)_{3}
as

\dot{s}_{i}(t)=\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{ij}v_{i}(t)\times v_{j}(t)-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}s_{i}(t).

(10)

We may derive a second order equation for
\ddot{v}_{i}
using the system (
1
). Indeed, differentiating
(1)_{2}
and then using
(1)_{3}
we get

\displaystyle\chi\ddot{v}_{i}

\displaystyle=\dot{s}_{i}\times v_{i}+s_{i}\times\dot{v}_{i}

\displaystyle=\left[v_{i}\times\left(\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{ij}v_{j}-\gamma\dot{v}_{i}\right)\right]\times v_{i}+\frac{1}{\chi}s_{i}\times(s_{i}\times v_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{ij}(v_{i}\times v_{j})\times v_{i}-\gamma(v_{i}\times\dot{v}_{i})\times v_{i}+\frac{1}{\chi}s_{i}\times(s_{i}\times v_{i}).

(11)

We now use the following identities in (
11
) (all of which follow from the triple product formula

a\times(b\times c)=b(a\cdot c)-c(a\cdot b)
for
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
)

\displaystyle(v_{i}\times v_{j})\times v_{i}

\displaystyle=v_{j}-(v_{i}\cdot v_{j})v_{i},\qquad(v_{i}\times\dot{v}_{i})\times v_{i}=(v_{i}\cdot v_{i})\dot{v}_{i}-(v_{i}\cdot\dot{v}_{i})v_{i}=\dot{v}_{i},

\displaystyle s_{i}\times(s_{i}\times v_{i})

\displaystyle=(s_{i}\cdot v_{i})s_{i}-(s_{i}\cdot s_{i})v_{i}=-|s_{i}|^{2}v_{i}=-\chi^{2}|\dot{v}_{i}|^{2}v_{i},

and we end up with the following second order equation for velocities

\chi\ddot{v}_{i}+\gamma\dot{v}_{i}+\chi|\dot{v}_{i}|^{2}v_{i}=\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{ik}(v_{k}-(v_{i}\cdot v_{k})v_{i}).

(12)

The formulation of (
12
) allows us to
have a first glimpse of the mechanism that drives the velocity alignment in certain configurations. Indeed, in this
formulation the RHS can be written as

\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{ij}\Gamma(v_{i},v_{j}),\qquad\text{where}\quad\Gamma(v_{i},v_{j})=v_{j}-(v_{i}\cdot v_{j})v_{i}

is the orthogonal projection of vector
v_{j}
on
v_{i}
. The term
\Gamma(v_{i},v_{j})
is a nonlinear and non-symmetric
coupling term that plays a role on preserving the speed of the agents due to the property

\Gamma(v_{i},v_{j})\cdot v_{i}=0
. This equation reduces to the Cucker-Smale model with constant speed when we take
\chi\to 0
, i.e.

\dot{x}_{i}=v_{i},\qquad\dot{v}_{i}=\frac{\bar{k}}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{ij}\Gamma(v_{i},v_{j}),\qquad\text{where}\quad\bar{k}=\frac{k}{\gamma}.

(13)

This version of CS model with constant speed has been studied in
[
8
,
9
,
28
]
.
The idea of taking a coupling term
\Gamma(v_{i},v_{j})
which takes the orthogonal projection
of
v_{j}
on
v_{i}
instead of
v_{j}-v_{i}
goes back to a model of quantum synchronization
due to Lohe
[
34
]
. We should also note that other instances of C-S dynamics with a
non linear coupling
\Gamma(v_{i},v_{j})
have been studied in the literature.
For instance, in
[
25
,
35
]
the authors studied a velocity coupling of the
type
\Gamma(v_{i},v_{j})=(v_{j}-v_{i})|v_{j}-v_{i}|^{2(\gamma-1)}
for a
constant
\gamma\in\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}\right)
, and gave conditions for flocking as well as collision avoidance.

For a start we define the diameters of positions, velocities, and spins in the usual way

D(x(t)):=\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)|,\quad D(v(t)):=\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|v_{i}(t)-v_{j}(t)|,\quad D(s(t)):=\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|s_{i}(t)-s_{j}(t)|.

Our point of view is to study the evolution of velocity diameters by means of studying
the maximal angles between velocities since these two are geometrically connected.
For this, if we assume that all velocities
v_{i}
lie in a cone with opening (apex) angle
\pi/2
, then we
can study the evolution of angle between
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
by studying the inner product of these two velocities.
In this situation, by taking the minimum of the inner product of all velocity pairs, we can control the velocity diameter of the system.

Thus, given the considerations above, we introduce the
geometric factor of motion
\pazocal{A}(v)
just as in
[
8
]
, which was defined as

\pazocal{A}(v(t)):=\min\limits_{i\neq j}v_{i}(t)\cdot v_{j}(t).

(14)

When all the velocities lie inside a cone with angle in
\pi/2
, then
\pazocal{A}(v)>0
and we can have some control of the velocity diameter. As a result, the geometric factor
plays a crucial role in identifying invariance velocity regions. Furthermore,
the pair of velocities that is the minimizer in the factor

\pazocal{A}(v)
is the same pair that maximizes the velocity diameter with
the exact relationship between these two quantities
being
\pazocal{A}(v(t))=1-\frac{1}{2}D(v(t))^{2}
when
\pazocal{A}(v(t))>0
.
We now proceed with some definitions:

Definition 1
.

Let
(x(t),v(t),s(t))
be a smooth solution to
system (
1
), with initial data
(x_{0},v_{0},s_{0})
that satisfy conditions (
6
). The following
definitions hold:

•

(Spin vanishing) The solution exhibits asymptotic vanishing of spins if and only if

D(s(t))\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty,

which due to average spin dissipation implies
s_{i}(t)\to 0
for all
1\leq i\leq N
.

•

(Orientation invariance) We say that the solution exhibits orientational invariance if and only if
all velocity angles remain inside a cone with slant angle less than
\pi/2

for all times. In other words, if there exists some
0<\beta<1
such that

\pazocal{A}(v(t))>\beta
for all
t\geq 0
.

•

(Velocity alignment) The solution exhibits velocity alignment
if and only if
D(v(t))\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

•

(Asymptotic flocking) We say that the solution exhibits asymptotic flocking if
and only if we have velocity alignment and group coherence i.e.

D(v(t))\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty,\qquad\exists\quad 0<D^{\infty}<\infty\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}D(x(t))<D^{\infty}.

If this holds true for all initial data
(x_{0},v_{0},s_{0})
we speak
of
unconditional flocking
, otherwise if flocking is possible only for a
specific subset of initial conditions, then we speak of
conditional flocking
.

In this article we want to address the following question:

•

Q1
:
Under what assumptions in
terms of the parameters
\chi,\gamma,k
of the problem, the communication matrix
\psi_{ij}
, and in terms
of the initial conditions, we can establish invariant

A more specific question we ask is the following:

•

Does the smallness of initial diameters of the state variables suffice for the proof of orientation invariance for the velocities and/or flocking, or do we need further assumptions on the parameters

in the model?

2.2
State of the art and main Theorems

We should start with the state of art results in the study of asymptotics for the IS model. So far,
the only known result is the one presented in
[
26
]
for weights that are multiplicative, i.e.
for weights that satisfy
\psi_{ij}=p_{i}p_{j}
for any
1\leq i,j\leq N
, and a given set
\{p_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
of positive elements
p_{i}>0
. This result
was given in two important steps. In the first step, it is shown that for constant
weights (which is a more general assumption than that of multiplicative weights) we can deduce
asymptotic spin vanishing as is shown in the following:

Proposition 2
.

[
26
]
Suppose
that parameters
k,\gamma,\chi
are all positive. If
(x(t),v(t),s(t))
is a solution to the
IS model with communication weights that are nonnegative constant and symmetric, then asymptotic spin vanishing occurs. In particular, we have that

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}|s_{i}(t)|=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}|\dot{s}_{i}(t)|=0.

Proof.

A sketch of the main steps in the proof of Proposition
2
is possible in one paragraph. First, we introduce the following
functionals

\pazocal{E}(t):=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum\limits_{i,j}\psi_{ij}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2},\qquad\pazocal{S}(t):=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i}|s_{i}|^{2}.

We can show with a direct computation that
\frac{\chi}{2}\pazocal{E}(t)+\frac{1}{k}\pazocal{S}(t)
is dissipative for symmetric, and constant
communication weights by showing that

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\chi}{2}\pazocal{E}(t)+\frac{1}{k}\pazocal{S}(t)\right)+\frac{2\gamma}{\chi k}\pazocal{S}(t)=0,\qquad t>0.

This in turn implies the a priori energy estimate

\frac{\chi}{2}\pazocal{E}(t)+\frac{1}{k}\pazocal{S}(t)+\frac{2\gamma}{\chi k}\int_{0}^{t}\pazocal{S}(s)\,ds\leq\frac{\chi}{2}\pazocal{E}(0)+\frac{1}{k}\pazocal{S}(0),

or equivalently

\int_{0}^{\infty}\pazocal{S}(t)\,dt\leq\frac{\chi k}{2\gamma}\left(\frac{\chi}{2}\pazocal{E}(0)+\frac{1}{k}\pazocal{S}(0)\right).

(15)

The next step is
to show that
\dot{\pazocal{S}}(t)
is uniformly bounded, and as a result
\pazocal{S}(t)
is uniformly continuous. Then,
with the help of Barbalat’s lemma
[
2
]
the integrability of
\pazocal{S}(t)
together with its uniform continuity implies that
\pazocal{S}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. Similarly, it can be shown that
\dot{\pazocal{S}}(t)
is also uniformly continuous and since
\pazocal{S}(t)\to 0
it also follows that
\dot{\pazocal{S}}(t)\to 0
. ∎

We now impose the additional assumption of multiplicativity in the communication weights.
Then, with the help of Proposition
2
and assumptions on the smallness of initial data
\pazocal{E}(0),\pazocal{S}(0)
,
it was proven in
[
26
]
that the IS system reaches a 2-flock or 1-flock formation asymptotically in time.
For this it helps to introduce the following averaging quantities

p_{c}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}\qquad\bar{v}_{c}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}v_{i}.

The following main result was proven in
[
26
]
(see Theorem 4.1):

Proposition 3
.

[
26
]

In addition to the assumptions made in Proposition
2
we assume that
the communication weights are also multiplicative, i.e. that
\psi_{ij}=p_{i}p_{j}
where
p_{i}>0
for any

1\leq i,j\leq N
. Then the following statements hold :

(i) If the initial data
(x_{0},v_{0},s_{0})
satisfies the condition

\pazocal{E}(0)+\frac{2}{\chi k}\pazocal{S}(0)<2p_{c}^{2},

then each velocity
v_{i}
converges to either
\bar{v}_{c}/|\bar{v}_{c}|
or
-\bar{v}_{c}/|\bar{v}_{c}|
.

(ii) If the initial data
(x_{0},v_{0},s_{0})
satisfies the more restrictive condition

\pazocal{E}(0)+\frac{2}{\chi k}\pazocal{S}(0)<\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\left\{\frac{8p_{i}(Np_{c}-p_{i})}{N^{2}}\right\},

then we have velocity alignment and all velocities converge to
\bar{v}_{c}/|\bar{v}_{c}|
.

Proof.

The proof is presented in
[
26
]
.
∎

There are some remarks regarding Proposition
3
that we have to address before we go on with the presentation of the results of this work :

Remark 1
.

It is important to note that Proposition
3
applies only to a very restrictive class of
weights that adhere to the multiplicativity property. As it was pointed in
[
26
]
, this property appears to be relevant in fragmentation-coagulation processes of polymers, but it is still a very restrictive framework to work with in the general problem. In fact, even the class of constant communication weights is not wide enough to use in many modelling examples e.g. in cases where the interaction weights depend on the metric distance between agents, or
when the network topology changes in time etc.

Remark 2
.

The convergence that is proven in Proposition
3
does not possess any explicit rate. Hence it is impossible to deduce coherence in group formation from this result alone, i.e. we don’t have sufficient information to show flocking in the traditional way that was defined earlier. In many physically realistic cases we would like to have a way to show that when alignment in velocities happens, the group of particles remains bounded uniformly in time, or
at least, we have the vanishing of the velocity diameter in some explicit rate.

Remark 3
.

The IS model has a natural tendency to induce orientation alignment in the particle group when the velocities and spins are not that far apart. This property of the
IS system becomes evident when we write the system as a second order equation for the velocities of agents. Unfortunately, the functional approach that is the cornerstone of Proposition
3
does not make any use of the structure that is “build in” the IS system. In this paper, our approach is to make full use of this structure.

Having these considerations in mind, we are in position to state the main two results of this work.
First, we state a theorem that works for positive, constant and symmetric communication weights (not necessarily multiplicative). This theorem states that in such a case we have invariant regions for the velocity vectors and we can achieve velocity alignment in these regions but with no explicit rate.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(x(t),v(t),s(t))
be a global smooth solution to
(
1
), with a symmetric and constant communication matrix
\{\psi_{ij}\}_{i,j=1}^{N}
,
and initial data
(x_{0},v_{0},s_{0})
that satisfy conditions (
6
). Suppose also that

\psi_{m}=\min\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\psi_{ij}
, and
\psi_{M}=\max\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\psi_{ij}
. Finally, we assume some
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
s.t.
the following two conditions hold:

\pazocal{A}(v_{0})>\delta_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\pazocal{C}_{0}<1-\delta_{0},

where
the exact form of
\pazocal{C}_{0}:=\pazocal{C}(D(v_{0}),\dot{D}(v_{0}),D(s_{0}),s_{0},\gamma,k,\chi)
is given by the following formulas :

(i) If
\gamma>\sqrt{8k\chi\psi_{m}\delta_{0}}:

\pazocal{C}_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}D(v_{0})^{2}+\frac{\chi}{\gamma\sqrt{1-8k\chi\psi_{m}\delta_{0}/\gamma^{2}}}\left(D(v_{0})|\dot{D}(v_{0})|+\frac{1}{2}\nu_{1}D(v_{0})^{2}\right)+\frac{2kN}{\gamma\sqrt{2k\chi\psi_{m}\delta_{0}}}\left(\frac{\chi}{2}\pazocal{E}(0)+\frac{1}{k}\pazocal{S}(0)\right)

(ii) If
\gamma\leq\sqrt{8k\chi\psi_{m}\delta_{0}}:

\displaystyle\pazocal{C}_{0}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&\frac{1}{2}D(v_{0})^{2}\left(1+\frac{2\chi}{\gamma}\right)+\frac{4kN}{\gamma^{2}}\left(\frac{\chi}{2}\pazocal{E}(0)+\frac{1}{k}\pazocal{S}(0)\right),\qquad\text{for}\quad\dot{D}(v_{0})\geq 0\\
\\
&\frac{1}{2}D(v_{0})^{2}+\frac{4kN}{\gamma^{2}}\left(\frac{\chi}{2}\pazocal{E}(0)+\frac{1}{k}\pazocal{S}(0)\right),\qquad\text{for}\quad\dot{D}(v_{0})<0.\end{array}\right.

Then the particle system has the property
\pazocal{A}(v(t))>\delta_{0}

for all
t\geq 0
, and the velocity diameters vanish asymptotically and
D(v)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

The second result that we prove is a stronger result that can be achieved in the “Small Inertia” regime, and
gives exponentially fast alignment of velocities under certain assumptions on the initial configurations.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(x(t),v(t),s(t))
be a global smooth solution to the IS system (
1
),
with initial data
(x_{0},v_{0},s_{0})
that satisfy (
6
). We have also assumed that the communication matrix in (
1
)
satisfies
\psi_{m}\leq\psi_{ij}(t)\leq\psi_{M}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq N
and
t\geq 0
,
and that
\pazocal{A}(v_{0})>\delta_{0}
for some
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
.
Finally, we assume that the initial configurations and parameters satisfy the conditions

(H1)\quad\gamma>\sqrt{\frac{6k\chi\psi_{M}}{\delta_{0}\psi_{m}}},\qquad\qquad(H2)\quad\pazocal{C}_{0}<1-\delta_{0},

where the exact form of
\pazocal{C}_{0}
is given by

\pazocal{C}_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}D(v_{0})^{2}+\frac{\chi D(v_{0})|\dot{D}(v_{0})|}{\gamma}+\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}\left(D(s_{0})^{2}+2\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|s_{i0}|^{2}\right).

Then the particle flow exhibits orientation invariance
with
\pazocal{A}(v(t))>\delta_{0}
for
t\geq 0
. Furthermore, we have the estimates

\displaystyle D(v(t))\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}O(e^{-\frac{\mu_{*}}{2}t}),\qquad\text{for}\quad\mu_{*}<\frac{\gamma}{2\chi},\\
\\
O(\sqrt{t}e^{-\frac{\gamma}{4\chi}t}),\qquad\text{for}\quad\mu_{*}\geq\frac{\gamma}{2\chi},\end{array}\right.

where
\mu_{*}=\sup\pazocal{D}
for the non-empty set

\pazocal{D}=\left\{0<\mu<\frac{\gamma}{\chi}\quad:\quad\mu^{3}-\frac{2\gamma}{\chi}\mu^{2}+\frac{2k\chi\psi_{m}\delta_{0}+\gamma^{2}}{\chi^{2}}\mu\leq\frac{2k}{\chi}\left(\frac{\gamma}{\chi}\psi_{m}\delta_{0}-\frac{6k}{\gamma}\psi_{M}\right)\right\}.

(19)

Remark 4
.

The way we have defined
\pazocal{C}_{0}
in both theorems ensures that the

\pazocal{C}_{0}<1-\delta_{0}
assumption is stronger than
\pazocal{A}(v_{0})>0
, and hence
\pazocal{A}(v_{0})>\delta_{0}
could have been omitted. We have kept it because it signifies the importance of the geometric factor in the analysis, and it is also helpful, since we work with a continuity argument on
\pazocal{A}(v(t))
to show that
\pazocal{A}(v(t))>\delta_{0}
for all
t\geq 0
in both proofs.

3
Some special cases of IS models

In this brief Section we discuss some results for two
simplified instances of the IS model: Namely, the IS model on 2-d plane where it reduces to the inertial Kuramoto model, and the overdamped regime
\chi\to 0
, where the IS reduces to the Cucker-Smale with constant speed (for weights that are functions of the metric distance of particles).

3.1
Inertial Spin on the plane and connection to the inertial Kuramoto model

If we assume a motion restricted on a plane then the velocity vector
v_{i}
can be expressed in polar form as

v_{i}=(\cos{\theta_{i}},\sin{\theta_{i}}),

for
\theta_{i}\in[0,2\pi)
. We Differentiate the velocity vector to get the first and second derivatives

\dot{v}_{i}=(-\sin{\theta_{i}},\cos{\theta_{i}})\dot{\theta}_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ddot{v}_{i}=(-\cos{\theta_{i}},-\sin{\theta_{i}})|\dot{\theta}_{i}|^{2}+(-\sin{\theta_{i}},\cos{\theta_{i}})\ddot{\theta}_{i}.

Substituting the derivatives of
v_{i}
into equation (
12
), then using
the identity
\chi|\dot{v}_{i}|^{2}v_{i}=\chi(\cos{\theta_{i}},\sin{\theta_{i}})|\dot{\theta}_{i}|^{2}

and the fact that

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{ik}(v_{k}-(v_{i}\cdot v_{k})v_{i})=(-\sin{\theta_{i}},\cos{\theta})\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{ik}\sin{(\theta_{k}-\theta_{i})},

we get the equation for the evolution of
\theta_{i}
, i.e.

\chi\ddot{\theta_{i}}+\gamma\dot{\theta_{i}}=\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{ik}\sin{(\theta_{k}-\theta_{i})},\quad 1\leq i\leq N.

(20)

This is the Kuramoto model equation when inertial effects are involved, for a network topology where
\psi_{ij}:=a_{ij}
is the coupling strength between oscillators
i
and
j
, and with zero natural frequencies
\Omega_{i}=0
. This model is coupled with the set of initial conditions

\theta_{i}(0)=\theta_{i0},\quad\dot{\theta}_{i}(0)=\dot{\theta}_{i0},\qquad 1\leq i\leq N.

(21)

The inertial Kuramoto model has been studied extensively in many works e.g.
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
31
]
. In particular, we may consider the more general model

m_{i}\ddot{\theta}_{i}+\gamma_{i}\dot{\theta}_{i}=\Omega_{i}+k\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\sin(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i}),\quad t\geq 0,\quad i=1,\ldots,N,

(22)

with prescribed initial data given by (
21
). Here
m_{i},\gamma_{i}
are the inertia and damping parameters of the i’th oscillator respectively,
k
is the coupling strength, and the
\Omega_{i}
’s are the natural frequencies of the oscillators which are independent to one another and each
one follows a common distribution
g(\Omega)
. The unknown function
\theta_{i}(t)
corresponds to
the phase of the i’th oscillator, and its derivative
\omega_{i}(t):=\dot{\theta}_{i}(t)
to its frequency.
We use the shorthand vector notation
(\theta(t),\omega(t)):=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N},\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{N})
and the initial data are represented by the vector
(\theta_{0},\omega_{0})
. We also define the diameters for the phases and frequencies by

D_{\theta}(t):=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|\theta_{j}-\theta_{i}|,\qquad D_{\omega}(t):=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|\omega_{j}-\omega_{i}|.

We say that the system exhibits
complete phase synchronization
if
D_{\theta}(t)\to 0
, and
complete frequency synchronization
if
D_{\omega}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

The following result regarding the case of identical natural
frequencies, and homogeneous inertia and friction, was the
first result of its kind for (
22
) that was proven in
[
14
]
. Instead of using the formulation
of the theorem from
[
14
]
(where the aggregate result is presented in two Theorems 4.1 & 4.2), we choose the
more synoptic formulation in
[
12
]
:

Proposition 4
.

[
12
]

Suppose that the parameters
m_{i},\gamma_{i},\Omega_{i},k
and initial data satisfy the following relations:

\displaystyle m_{i}=m,\quad\gamma_{i}=1,\quad a_{ij}=\frac{1}{N},\quad\Omega_{i}=\Omega,\quad m,k>0

\displaystyle 0<\pazocal{C}_{1}(0)<\pi\qquad\text{and}\qquad mk\in\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\right)\cup\left(\frac{\pazocal{C}_{1}(0)}{4\sin{\pazocal{C}_{1}(0)}},\infty\right),

where
\pazocal{C}_{1}(m,\theta(t),\dot{\theta}(t)):=\max\{D_{\theta}(t),\,D_{\theta}(t)+m\dot{D}_{\theta}(t)\}
. Then,
for any solution
(\theta(t),\omega(t))
to (
22
), there exists some positive constant
\Lambda_{1}>0
s.t.

D_{\theta}(t)+D_{\omega}(t)\leq O(1)e^{-\Lambda_{1}t}\quad\text{for}\quad t\to\infty.

Remark 5
.

The observant reader might have already noticed the similarity between the above result and Theorem
2
of this article. It is important for instance to see that the initial configurations in Proposition
4
(as manifested by the assumption
0<\pazocal{C}_{1}(m,\theta_{0},\omega_{0})<\pi
) are chosen so that all initial phases are contained on the semi-circle. This implies a certain orientation that is assumed in initial conditions, and it is a common assumption in both Proposition
4
and Theorem
2
(in Theorem
2
this assumption is
\pazocal{C}_{0}<1-\delta_{0}
). On the other hand, there is also a major difference between the two results. Although Theorem
2
makes the assumption of a “small inertia” regime with the condition
\gamma>\sqrt{\frac{6k\chi\psi_{M}}{\delta_{0}\psi_{m}}}
, Proposition
4
applies in both small and large inertia regions, as manifested by
mk\in\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\right)\cup\left(\frac{\pazocal{C}_{1}(0)}{4\sin{\pazocal{C}_{1}(0)}},\infty\right)
. This shouldn’t come as a surprise, as the 2-d setting of the IS system allows for a much sharper inequality for the phase diameter, than the one we derive in Section
4
for the velocity diameter of the IS in 3-d. The problem of showing invariant regions for the velocities in the large inertia regime, when the communication matrix is not constant, remains open.

3.2
Formal zero inertial limit
\chi\to 0

In the overdamped regime, when the inertia becomes
negligible compared to the spin dissipation and
\psi_{ij}=\psi(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)
, the IS
model reduces to the CS model with constant speed. By taking the formal limit

\chi\to 0
(derivation could be made rigorous!) we get system (
13
), which we rewrite as

\dot{x}_{i}=v_{i},\qquad\dot{v}_{i}=\frac{\bar{k}}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{ij}(v_{j}-(v_{i}\cdot v_{j})v_{i}),\qquad\text{with}\quad\bar{k}=\frac{k}{\gamma}.

(23)

This model has been studied in
[
8
]
and later in
[
9
,
28
]
.
In general, it is easy to construct initial configurations for a simple three particle system where
a global flock is not achieved (see
Appendix. A
in
[
8
]
). Furthermore, despite the simplicity of this system, the asymptotic behavior is not yet fully understood for all initial configurations. That being said,
for the restricted class of initial conditions s.t.
\pazocal{A}(v_{0})>0
it is possible to employ a Lyapunov functional
approach to prove asymptotic flocking. Indeed, if we consider initial data with
\pazocal{A}(v_{0})>0
then we can prove the following system of dissipating differential inequalities (SDDI) holds

\Big|\frac{d}{dt}D(x)\Big|\leq D(v),\qquad\frac{d}{dt}D(v)\leq-\bar{k}\psi(D(x))D(v)\pazocal{A}(v_{0}),

(24)

which directly implies that
\pazocal{A}(v(t))\geq\pazocal{A}(v_{0})
for all
t\geq 0
.
From this point, the standard treatment is to introduce the Lyapunov functional

\pazocal{H}_{\pm}(t):=D(v)\pm\bar{k}\pazocal{A}(v_{0})\int_{0}^{D(x)}\psi(s)\,ds,

(25)

and show with the help of (
24
) that this functional dissipates,
i.e.
\pazocal{H}_{\pm}(t)\leq\pazocal{H}_{\pm}(0)
for all
t\geq 0
. Then the following flocking estimate for (
23
) was
proven.

Proposition 5
.

[
8
]
Suppose that the communication weight
\psi(\cdot)
and
initial configurations
(x_{0},v_{0})
satisfy the following conditions:

\pazocal{A}(v_{0})>0,\qquad 0<D(v_{0})<\bar{k}\pazocal{A}(v_{0})\min{\left\{\int_{D(x_{0})}^{\infty}\psi(s)\,ds,\int_{0}^{D(x_{0})}\psi(s)\,ds\right\}}.

Then, there exists a unique solution
(x(t),v(t))
to system (
13
) satisfying the
asymptotic flocking conditions:

\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}D(x)<D^{\infty}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\,D(v)\leq D(v_{0})e^{-\bar{k}\pazocal{A}(v_{0})\psi(D^{\infty})t},\quad t\geq 0,

where
D^{\infty}
is defined implicitly by
D(v_{0}):=\bar{k}\pazocal{A}(v_{0})\int_{D(x_{0})}^{D^{\infty}}\psi(s)\,ds
.

We should also note here that invariant regions of velocities and the flocking property have
also been studied in a “cone-vision” variant of the C-S model in
[
33
]
. In the
“cone-vision” model the agents update their velocities by only considering other agents that belong in a certain vision-cone of their velocity vector. In general,
such a model can give birth to multi-cluster formations, but conditions for the emergence of a unique flock in the 2-d and 3-d models were presented in
[
33
]
.

4
A second order differential inequality for the velocity diameter

We now prove the main inequality for velocity diameters. In our proof we assume the more general communication

\psi:=\psi(|x_{j}-x_{i}|)
where
\psi(\cdot)
is a decreasing weight function s.t.
0<\psi(\cdot)<\psi_{M}
. We
make no assumption of a lower bound for
\psi(\cdot)\geq\psi_{m}>0
in this.

Proposition 6
.

Let
(x,v,s)
be a solution to (
1
)
with initial data
(x_{0},v_{0},s_{0})
that satisfy (
6
). We also assume
that this solution has all flight directions (orientations of velocities)
chosen s.t.
\pazocal{A}(v(t))>0
for all
t\geq 0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}D(v)^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{\chi}\frac{d}{dt}D(v)^{2}+\frac{2k}{\chi}\psi(D(x))D(v)^{2}\pazocal{A}(v)\leq\frac{4}{\chi^{2}}\left(D(s)^{2}+\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|s_{i}|^{2}D(v)^{2}\right).

(26)

Moreover, the inequality for
D(v)
is

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}D(v)^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{\chi}\frac{d}{dt}D(v)^{2}+\frac{2k}{\chi}\psi(D(x))D(v)^{2}\pazocal{A}(v)\leq\pazocal{C}_{1}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}(t-s)}D(v(s))^{2}+\pazocal{C}_{2}e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}t},

(27)

where

\pazocal{C}_{1}=\frac{4k^{2}\psi_{M}}{\gamma\chi}(1+D(v)^{2})<\frac{12k^{2}\psi_{M}}{\gamma\chi}\quad\text{and}\quad\pazocal{C}_{2}=\frac{4}{\chi^{2}}\left(D(s_{0})^{2}+2\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|s_{i}(0)|^{2}\right)

Proof.

We write down eq. (
12
) for two particles
i,j
and take the inner product
with
v_{j}
and
v_{i}
respectively. The idea is that by obtaining an equation for the inner product

v_{i}\cdot v_{j}
we can then derive an equation for
|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2}
.

\chi\ddot{v}_{i}\cdot v_{j}+\gamma\dot{v}_{i}\cdot v_{j}+\chi|\dot{v}_{i}|^{2}v_{i}\cdot v_{j}=\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{ik}(v_{k}\cdot v_{j}-(v_{i}\cdot v_{k})v_{i}\cdot v_{j}),

\chi\ddot{v}_{j}\cdot v_{i}+\gamma\dot{v}_{j}\cdot v_{i}+\chi|\dot{v}_{j}|^{2}v_{i}\cdot v_{j}=\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{jk}(v_{k}\cdot v_{i}-(v_{j}\cdot v_{k})v_{j}\cdot v_{i}).

Now if we sum both equations we get

\displaystyle\chi(\ddot{v}_{i}\cdot v_{j}+\ddot{v}_{i}\cdot v_{j})+\gamma\frac{d}{dt}(v_{i}\cdot v_{j})+\chi(|\dot{v}_{i}|^{2}+|\dot{v}_{j}|^{2})v_{i}\cdot v_{j}=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{ik}(v_{k}\cdot v_{j}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{i})v_{i}\cdot v_{j})+\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{jk}(v_{k}\cdot v_{i}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{j})v_{i}\cdot v_{j}).

(28)

Note that we have
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}(v_{i}\cdot v_{j})=\ddot{v}_{i}\cdot v_{j}+2\dot{v}_{i}\cdot\dot{v}_{j}+\ddot{v}_{j}\cdot v_{i}
, so eq. (
28
) yields

\displaystyle\chi\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}(v_{i}\cdot v_{j})+\gamma\frac{d}{dt}(v_{i}\cdot v_{j})-2\chi\dot{v}_{i}\cdot\dot{v}_{j}+\chi(|\dot{v}_{i}|^{2}+|\dot{v}_{j}|^{2})v_{i}\cdot v_{j}=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{ik}(v_{k}\cdot v_{j}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{i})v_{i}\cdot v_{j})+\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{jk}(v_{k}\cdot v_{i}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{j})v_{i}\cdot v_{j})

(29)

We now use the identity
v_{i}\cdot v_{j}=1-\frac{1}{2}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2}
in (
29
) to get

\displaystyle\frac{\chi}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\frac{d}{dt}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2}=-2\chi\dot{v}_{i}\cdot\dot{v}_{j}+\chi(|\dot{v}_{i}|^{2}+|\dot{v}_{j}|^{2})v_{i}\cdot v_{j}

\displaystyle-\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{ik}(v_{k}\cdot v_{j}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{i})v_{i}\cdot v_{j})-\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{jk}(v_{k}\cdot v_{i}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{j})v_{i}\cdot v_{j}).

(30)

Note also that since
-2\chi\dot{v}_{i}\cdot\dot{v}_{j}+\chi(|\dot{v}_{i}|^{2}+|\dot{v}_{j}|^{2})v_{i}\cdot v_{j}\leq\chi|\dot{v}_{i}-\dot{v}_{j}|^{2}
inequality (
30
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{\chi}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\frac{d}{dt}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2}\leq\pazocal{I}_{1}+\pazocal{I}_{2},

(31)

where
\pazocal{I}_{1}:=\chi|\dot{v}_{i}-\dot{v}_{j}|^{2}
and

\displaystyle\pazocal{I}_{2}:=\pazocal{I}_{21}+\pazocal{I}_{22}:=-\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{ik}(v_{k}\cdot v_{j}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{i})v_{i}\cdot v_{j})-\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{jk}(v_{k}\cdot v_{i}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{j})v_{i}\cdot v_{j}).

To control the term
\pazocal{I}_{1}
we first write
\dot{v}_{i}-\dot{v}_{j}
as

\dot{v}_{i}-\dot{v}_{j}=\frac{1}{\chi}(s_{i}-s_{j})\times v_{i}+\frac{1}{\chi}s_{j}\times(v_{i}-v_{j}),

by virtue of (
1
). We therefore have that

\pazocal{I}_{1}\leq\frac{2}{\chi}|s_{i}-s_{j}|^{2}+\frac{2}{\chi}|s_{j}|^{2}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2},

which yields

\pazocal{I}_{1}\leq\frac{2}{\chi}D(s)^{2}+\frac{2}{\chi}\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|s_{i}|^{2}D(v)^{2}.

(32)

We proceed with the treatment of
\pazocal{I}_{2}
. In this treatment we make the
assumption that the
i,j
indices are chosen so that
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
are the
extremal velocities that minimize
v_{i}\cdot v_{j}
, i.e
v_{j}\cdot v_{k}\geq v_{i}\cdot v_{j}
for
all
1\leq k\leq N
. Indeed, with this choice for the
i,j
pair we have that

\displaystyle\pazocal{I}_{21}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{ik}(v_{k}\cdot v_{j}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{i})v_{i}\cdot v_{j})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{k}{N}\min\limits_{i,j}\psi_{ij}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}(v_{k}\cdot v_{j}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{i})v_{i}\cdot v_{j})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{k}{N}\psi(D(x))\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}(v_{k}\cdot v_{j}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{i})v_{i}\cdot v_{j}).

Note that we used the fact that
v_{k}\cdot v_{j}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{i})v_{i}\cdot v_{j}\geq 0

(since
v_{k}\cdot v_{j}\geq v_{i}\cdot v_{j}
and
|v_{k}\cdot v_{i}|\leq 1
). In similar manner we show for
\pazocal{I}_{22}
that

\displaystyle\pazocal{I}_{22}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\psi_{jk}(v_{k}\cdot v_{i}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{j})v_{i}\cdot v_{j})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{k}{N}\min\limits_{i,j}\psi_{ij}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}(v_{k}\cdot v_{i}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{j})v_{i}\cdot v_{j})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{k}{N}\psi(D(x))\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}(v_{k}\cdot v_{i}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{j})v_{i}\cdot v_{j}).

If we add the two expressions after we re-arrange terms we have

\displaystyle\pazocal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{k}{N}\psi(D(x))\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}(v_{k}\cdot v_{j}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{j})v_{i}\cdot v_{j})-\frac{k}{N}\psi(D(x))\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}(v_{k}\cdot v_{i}-(v_{k}\cdot v_{i})v_{i}\cdot v_{j})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{k}{N}\psi(D(x))\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}(1-v_{i}\cdot v_{j})v_{k}\cdot v_{j}-\frac{k}{N}\psi(D(x))\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}(1-v_{i}\cdot v_{j})v_{k}\cdot v_{i}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{k}{2N}\psi(D(x))D(v)^{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}v_{k}\cdot v_{j}-\frac{k}{2N}\psi(D(x))D(v)^{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}v_{k}\cdot v_{i}\leq-k\psi(D(x))D(v)^{2}v_{i}\cdot v_{j}.

This yields the following bound for
\pazocal{I}_{2}

\pazocal{I}_{2}\leq-k\psi(D(x))D(v)^{2}\pazocal{A}(v).

(33)

Inserting the estimates (
32
)-(
33
) in (
31
), and keeping in mind that we have chosen
i,j
s.t.
D(v)=|v_{i}-v_{j}|
, we get the first inequality (
26
).

Finally, we turn to the term
\pazocal{I}_{1}
which controls the RHS of (
26
).
To control
|s_{i}-s_{j}|^{2}
we use the spin equation (
10
)
for
s_{i}-s_{j}
, and after we multiply by
s_{i}-s_{j}
we get

\frac{d}{dt}|s_{i}-s_{j}|^{2}+\frac{2\gamma}{\chi}|s_{i}-s_{j}|^{2}=\frac{2k}{N}\sum\limits_{k}\left((\psi_{ik}v_{i}-\psi_{jk}v_{j})\times v_{k}\right)\cdot(s_{i}-s_{j}).

We note that when
0\leq\psi(\cdot)\leq\psi_{M}
then
|\psi_{ik}v_{i}-\psi_{jk}v_{j}|\leq\psi_{M}|v_{i}-v_{j}|
. Now using
the Young inequality we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|s_{i}-s_{j}|^{2}+\frac{2\gamma}{\chi}|s_{i}-s_{j}|^{2}\leq\frac{k^{2}\chi\psi_{M}}{\gamma}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{\chi}|s_{i}-s_{j}|^{2}.

With the help of Duhamel’s principle we get

\displaystyle|s_{i}-s_{j}|^{2}\leq\frac{k^{2}\chi\psi_{M}}{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}(t-s)}|v_{i}(s)-v_{j}(s)|^{2}+e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}t}|s_{i0}-s_{j0}|^{2}.

Thus, picking
i,j
s.t.
D(s)=|s_{i}-s_{j}|
we have

D(s)^{2}\leq\frac{k^{2}\chi\psi_{M}}{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}(t-s)}D(v(s))^{2}+e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}t}D(s_{0})^{2}.

A similar bound can be obtained for
|s_{i}|^{2}
, i.e.

\frac{d}{dt}|s_{i}|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{\chi}|s_{i}|^{2}\leq\frac{k^{2}\chi\psi_{M}}{\gamma N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}|v_{i}\times v_{k}|^{2}.

Now using the fact that
|v_{i}\times v_{j}|^{2}=|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{4}
,
which implies
|v_{i}\times v_{j}|^{2}\leq|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2}
, we get the estimate

|s_{i}|^{2}\leq e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}t}|s_{i0}|^{2}+\frac{k^{2}\chi\psi_{M}}{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}(t-s)}D(v(s))^{2}.

In this case, collecting the terms for
\pazocal{I}_{1}
we get

\displaystyle\pazocal{I}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2k^{2}\psi_{M}}{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}(t-s)}D(v(s))^{2}+\frac{2}{\chi}e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}t}D(s_{0})^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2k^{2}\psi_{M}}{\gamma}D(v)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}(t-s)}D(v(s))^{2}+\frac{2}{\chi}e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}t}D(v)^{2}\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|s_{i0}|^{2}.

∎

Remark 7

In addition to identifying invariant regions with the help of Proposition

5
Second order Gronwall inequalities.

We begin with a Lemma that is a generalization of a result presented in
[
14
]
. In
[
14
]
, a second order Gronwall inequality for the differential inequality
a\ddot{y}+b\dot{y}+cy+d\leq 0
with

a>0
,

g(t)
in the place of the constant

.

Lemma 1
.

Let
y=y(t)
be a nonnegative
C^{2}
-function satisfying the second order differential inequality

a\ddot{y}+b\dot{y}+cy\leq g(t),\quad y(0)=y_{0},\quad\dot{y}(0)=y_{1},\quad t>0,

(34)

where
a>0
,
b,c
constants, and
g(t)
is a positive continuous function. Then, we have the estimates for

(i)
b^{2}-4ac>0:

\displaystyle y(t)\leq e^{-\nu_{1}t}y_{0}+a\frac{e^{-\nu_{2}t}-e^{-\nu_{1}t}}{\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}\left(y_{1}+\nu_{1}y_{0}\right)+\frac{1}{a}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{-\nu_{1}(t-s)}\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}e^{-\nu_{2}(s-s^{\prime})}g(s^{\prime}),

(35)

where
\nu_{1}=\frac{b+\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}
and
\nu_{2}=\frac{b-\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}
.

(ii)
b^{2}-4ac\leq 0:

\displaystyle y(t)\leq e^{-\frac{b}{2a}t}\Bigg[y_{0}+\left(\frac{b}{2a}y_{0}+y_{1}\right)t\Bigg]+\frac{1}{a}\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}e^{-\frac{b}{2a}(t-s^{\prime})}g(s^{\prime}).

(36)

Moreover, if
g(t)
vanishes asymptotically as
t\to\infty
, i.e.
g(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. Then, it follows that
y(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

The proof follows the ideas in the proof of Lemma 3.1 pg. 34 in
[
14
]
with modifications.

(i) For
b^{2}-4ac>0
we consider the functional

\displaystyle L(t):=\dot{y}+Ay-\frac{1}{a}\int_{0}^{t}\,ds\,e^{-\frac{h}{a}(t-s)}g(s),

where
A=\frac{b-h}{a}
and
h
any of the two positive solutions of polynomial
h^{2}-bh+ac=0
. For this Lyapunov functional
we find easily that
\dot{L}(t)\leq-\frac{h}{a}L(t)
. Then, we have

L(t)\leq e^{-\frac{h}{a}t}L(0)=e^{-\frac{h}{a}t}(y_{1}+Ay_{0}),

which in turn yields

\dot{y}+Ay\leq\frac{1}{a}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{-\frac{h}{a}(t-s)}g(s)+e^{-\frac{h}{a}t}(y_{1}+Ay_{0}).

Now solving in
y(t)
by integrating factor and taking
h=\frac{b-\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2}
as the solution that optimizes the exponents we derive the bound in (
35
).

(ii) For
b^{2}-4ac\leq 0
we make the substitution
Y(t):=e^{\frac{b}{2a}t}y(t)
and derive the following equation for
Y(t)
, i.e.

\ddot{Y}(t)-\frac{b^{2}-4ac}{4a}Y(t)\leq\frac{1}{a}e^{\frac{b}{2a}t}g(t).

Now since
y(t)
is nonegative we have
\ddot{Y}(t)\leq\frac{1}{a}e^{\frac{b}{2a}t}g(t)
which yields after two integrations

Y(t)\leq y_{0}+\left(\frac{b}{2a}y_{0}+y_{1}\right)t+\frac{1}{a}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}e^{\frac{b}{2a}s^{\prime}}g(s^{\prime}).

The desired inequality (
36
) for
y(t)
follows trivially after we multiply both sides by
e^{-\frac{b}{2a}t}
.
We leave the last part of our proof, i.e. that when the continuous
g(t)\to 0
, as
t\to\infty
, then it follows
that
y(t)\to 0
for both (
35
)-(
36
), in the following autonomous lemma.

Lemma 2
.

Assume that we have a continuous function
g(t)
on
[0,\infty)
s.t.
g(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. Then the following double integrals (for
a,b,\nu_{1},\nu_{2}>0
and
\nu_{1}>\nu_{2}

\pazocal{T}_{1}(t):=e^{-\nu_{1}t}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})s}\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}\,e^{\nu_{2}s^{\prime}}g(s^{\prime}),\qquad\pazocal{T}_{2}(t):=e^{-\frac{b}{2a}t}\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}\,e^{\frac{b}{2a}s^{\prime}}g(s^{\prime}),

also vanish as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

We fix some
\epsilon>0
, and due to the continuity of
g(t)
we know there
exists some
T_{\epsilon}
s.t.
g(t)<\epsilon
for
t\geq T_{\epsilon}
. We can break the double integral

\pazocal{T}_{1}(t)
into 3 terms as follows

\displaystyle\pazocal{T}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-\nu_{1}t}\int_{0}^{t}ds\ldots\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}\ldots=\pazocal{T}_{11}(t)+\pazocal{T}_{12}(t)+\pazocal{T}_{13}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-\nu_{1}t}\int_{0}^{T_{\epsilon}}ds\ldots\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}\ldots+e^{-\nu_{1}t}\int_{T_{\epsilon}}^{t}ds\ldots\int_{0}^{T_{\epsilon}}ds^{\prime}\ldots+e^{-\nu_{1}t}\int_{T_{\epsilon}}^{t}ds\ldots\int_{T_{\epsilon}}^{s}ds^{\prime}\ldots.

We now compute and integral term separately using the bounds
g(t)\leq\|g\|_{\infty}:=\max\limits_{t\geq 0}g(t)

on the interval
[0,T_{\epsilon}]
, and
g(t)<\epsilon
on
[T_{\epsilon},\infty)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\pazocal{T}_{11}(t)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\nu_{1}t}\frac{\|g\|_{\infty}}{\nu_{2}}\int_{0}^{T_{\epsilon}}ds\,e^{(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})s}(e^{\nu_{2}s}-1)\leq e^{-\nu_{1}t}\frac{\|g\|_{\infty}}{\nu_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{1}}(e^{\nu_{1}T_{\epsilon}}-1)-\frac{1}{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}\left(e^{(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})T_{\epsilon}}-1\right)\right),

\displaystyle\pazocal{T}_{12}(t)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\nu_{1}t}\frac{\|g\|_{\infty}}{\nu_{2}}\int_{T_{\epsilon}}^{t}ds\,e^{(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})s}(e^{\nu_{2}T_{\epsilon}}-1)\leq e^{-\nu_{1}t}\frac{\|g\|_{\infty}}{\nu_{2}(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})}\left(e^{(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})T_{\epsilon}}-1\right)\left(e^{\nu_{2}T_{\epsilon}}-1\right),

\displaystyle\pazocal{T}_{13}(t)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\nu_{1}t}\frac{\epsilon}{\nu_{2}}\int_{T_{\epsilon}}^{t}ds\,e^{(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})s}(e^{\nu_{2}s}-e^{\nu_{2}T_{\epsilon}})

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\nu_{1}t}\frac{\epsilon}{\nu_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{1}}\left(e^{\nu_{1}t}-e^{\nu_{1}T_{\epsilon}}\right)-\frac{1}{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}\left(e^{(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})t}-e^{(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})T_{\epsilon}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{\nu_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{1}}\left(1-e^{-\nu_{1}(t-T_{\epsilon})}\right)-\frac{1}{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}\left(e^{-\nu_{2}t}-e^{-\nu_{1}(t-T_{\epsilon})}e^{-\nu_{2}T_{\epsilon}}\right)\right).

It becomes clear from the
\pazocal{T}_{11}(t),\pazocal{T}_{12}(t),\pazocal{T}_{13}(t)
estimates that we can choose

T_{\epsilon}^{\prime}=T_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(\epsilon,T_{\epsilon},\|g\|_{\infty})>0
s.t
\pazocal{T}_{11}(t),\pazocal{T}_{12}(t)<\epsilon
and also

\pazocal{T}_{13}(t)<\frac{2}{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}\epsilon
for

, i.e.

\pazocal{T}_{1}<\left(2+\frac{2}{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}\right)\epsilon
for

. The proof for the term
\pazocal{T}_{2}(t)

is similar and therefore omitted.

It is useful to derive uniform (in time) bounds based on the above

in time, i.e.

We show the uniform bounds in the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Let
y=y(t)
be a nonnegative
C^{2}
-function satisfying the second order
differential inequality (
34
), and
g(t)
is an integrable function. Then we have the
following uniform bounds for
y(t)
:

(i)
b^{2}-4ac>0
:

\displaystyle y(t)\leq y_{0}+\frac{a}{\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}(|y_{1}|+\nu_{1}y_{0})+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{ac}}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,g(s)

(ii)
b^{2}-4ac\leq 0
:

if
y_{1}\geq 0
:

\displaystyle y(t)\leq e^{-\frac{y_{0}}{\frac{b}{2a}y_{0}+y_{1}}}\left(1+\frac{2a}{b}\right)y_{0}+\frac{1}{b}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,g(s),

if
y_{1}<0
:

\displaystyle y(t)\leq y_{0}+\frac{1}{b}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,g(s).

Proof.

It is an elementary exercise in calculus to give a uniform bound for the non integral terms that appear in (
35
) and (
36
) in Lemma 1.
The most important terms to bound are the integral terms

\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{-\nu_{1}(t-s)}\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}e^{-\nu_{2}(s-s^{\prime})}g(s^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}e^{-\frac{b}{2a}(t-s^{\prime})}g(s^{\prime}).

We have the following Hölder inequality for the first integral

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{-\nu_{1}(t-s)}\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}e^{-\nu_{2}(s-s^{\prime})}g(s^{\prime})\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{-2\nu_{1}(t-s)}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\Big|\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}\,e^{-\nu_{2}(s-s^{\prime})}g(s^{\prime})\Big|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The first term computed by direct calculation is
\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\nu_{1}}(1-e^{-2\nu_{1}t})}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\nu_{1}}}
. The second term is bounded
by the integral

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,\Big|\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}\,e^{-\nu_{2}(s-s^{\prime})}g(s^{\prime})\Big|^{2}\right)^{1/2}
which is non other than the
L^{2}
norm of the convolution
of the function
f(t)=e^{-\nu_{2}t}
with
g(t)
, i.e.
\|f\ast g\|_{2}
. Now according to Young’s inequality for convolutions
we have
\|f\ast g\|_{r}\leq\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{q}
for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1+\frac{1}{r}
and
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
, and if we take
p=2
,
q=1
, and
r=2

then we have that the last term is bounded by
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\nu_{2}}}\|g\|_{1}
so we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{-\nu_{1}(t-s)}\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}e^{-\nu_{2}(s-s^{\prime})}g(s^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}}\|g\|_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{a}{c}}\|g\|_{1}.

In similar fashion we can show that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}e^{-\frac{b}{2a}(t-s^{\prime})}g(s^{\prime})\leq\frac{a}{b}\|g\|_{1}.

∎

6
Proof of Main Theorems

We are now in position to prove the main theorems of this paper.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

The proof essentially uses Proposition
6
, the Gronwall lemmas proved in Section
5
, and a continuity argument.

Step A
: Let
T>0
be an arbitrary number so that
\pazocal{A}(v(t))>\delta_{0}
for all

(we know that such a

\pazocal{A}(v)

on that interval
[0,T)

1
with

In order to control

we have

D(s)^{2}=\max\limits_{i,j}|s_{i}-s_{j}|^{2}\leq(|s_{l}|+|s_{m}|)^{2}\leq 2(|s_{l}|^{2}+|s_{m}|^{2})\leq 2N\pazocal{S}(t),

where

are the indices that realize the

Now if we integrate

and use Proposition

\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t)\,dt\leq\frac{16N}{\chi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\pazocal{S}(t)\,dt\leq\frac{8Nk}{\chi\gamma}\left(\frac{\chi}{2}\pazocal{E}(0)+\frac{1}{k}\pazocal{S}(0)\right).

We have that for all

the estimates (

)–(

) in Lemma

hold.

Step B
: We now define the set

\pazocal{S}:=\{T>0:\pazocal{A}(v(t))>\delta_{0},\quad\forall t\in[0,T)\},\qquad T_{*}:=\sup\pazocal{S}.

We have shown in Step A due to the continuity property that the set

is non-empty. We claim that

so that the estimates above hold true for all

\lim\limits_{t\to T_{*}^{-}}D(v(t))^{2}=2-2\delta_{0}
. Now from

we have that

D(v(t))^{2}\leq 2\pazocal{C}_{0}<2-2\delta_{0},\qquad\forall t\in[0,T_{*}).

Taking the limit
\lim\limits_{t\to T_{*}^{-}}D(v(t))^{2}
leads to the contradiction
2-2\delta_{0}<2-2\delta_{0}
. Therefore
T_{*}=\infty
, and the estimates from Step A for
D(v)
hold.
Finally, the vanishing of velocity diameters follows from Lemma
2
.
∎

Abstract
. Motivated by Smith’s work [ 47 , 48 ] on maps between non-commutative projective spaces of the form Proj n ​ c ​ A {\rm Proj}_{nc}A in the setting of non-commutative projective geometry developed by Rosenberg and Van den Bergh, and the notion of schematicness introduced by Van Oystaeyen and Willaert [ 56 ] to ℕ \mathbb{N} -graded rings with the aim of formulating a non-commutative scheme theory à la Grothendieck [ 17 ] , in this paper we consider a first approach to maps in the Smith’s sense in the more general setting of non-commutative projective spaces over semi-graded rings defined by Lezama and Latorre [ 28 ] . We extend Smith’s key result [ 47 , Theorem 3.2] , [ 48 , Theorem 1.2] from the category of schematic ℕ \mathbb{N} -graded rings to the category of schematic semi-graded rings.

1.
Introduction

It is well-known that Serre
[
44

proved a theorem that describes the coherent sheaves on a projective scheme in terms of graded modules. Following Artin and Zhang
[
4

\Bbbk

denotes the category of graded modules and

denotes its subcategory of torsion modules. For

\Bbbk
-algebra and

\Gamma_{*}(\mathcal{F})=\bigoplus_{d=-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(d)),

asserts that if

is generated over
\Bbbk
by elements of degree one, then
\Gamma_{*}
defines an equivalence of categories

[
44
, Section 59, Proposition. 7.8, p. 252]
,
[
17

and
[

, Proposition. II. 5.15]
.

Artin and Zhang
[

]
extended Serre’s theorem. For

an
\mathbb{N}
-graded algebra over a commutative Noetherian ring, they defined the associated projective scheme to be the pair

is the quotient category above and

,

Van Oystaeyen and Willaert

]

]
. In a series of papers

,

Definition 2.1

(

)

Let

(i)

(ii)

Note that

Having in mind the algebraic properties mentioned above, let

3.
Maps between schematic semi-graded rings

This section contains the original results of the paper. We start with the following fact on morphisms in the category
\mathsf{SGR}-R
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f:N\rightarrow M
be a morphism of
\mathsf{SGR}-R
and
X\subseteq N
. Then
f(\langle X\rangle^{\mathsf{SG}})=\langle f(X)\rangle^{\mathsf{SG}}
.

Proof.

Let
y\in\langle f(X)\rangle^{\mathsf{SG}}
. Then
y\in M^{\prime}
for every
\mathsf{SG}
-submodule
M^{\prime}
of
M
with
f(X)\subseteq M^{\prime}
. Since
\langle X\rangle^{\mathsf{SG}}
is an
\mathsf{SG}
-submodule of
N
and
X\subseteq\langle X\rangle^{\mathsf{SG}}
, then
f(\langle X\rangle^{\mathsf{SG}})
is an
\mathsf{SG}
-submodule of
M
such that
f(X)\subseteq f(\langle X\rangle^{\mathsf{SG}})
, whence
y\in f(\langle X\rangle^{\mathsf{SG}})
. This shows that
\langle f(X)\rangle^{\mathsf{SG}}\subseteq f(\langle X\rangle^{\mathsf{SG}})
.

On the other hand, if
y\in f(\langle X\rangle^{\mathsf{SG}})
then there exists
x\in\langle X\rangle^{\mathsf{SG}}
with
f(x)=y
. Let
M^{\prime}
be an
\mathsf{SG}
-submodule of
M
such that
M^{\prime}\supseteq f(X)
. Then
f^{-1}(M^{\prime})
is an
\mathsf{SG}
-submodule of
N
with
X\subseteq f^{-1}(M^{\prime})
, which implies that
x\in f^{-1}(M^{\prime})
, and hence
y=f(x)\in M^{\prime}
and

Linearization of finite elasticity with surface tension
Martin Kružík, Edoardo Mainini

Abstract
. We propose models in nonlinear elasticity for nonsimple materials that include surface energy terms. Additionally, we also discuss living surface loads on the boundary. We establish corresponding linearized models and show their relationship to the original ones by means of Γ \Gamma -convergence.

1.
Introduction

Surfaces of elastic bodies exhibit properties that are different from those associated with the
bulk. This behavior is caused either by the fact that the boundary
of the material is exposed to fatigue, chemical processes, coating, etc., thus
obviously resulting in very different properties in comparatively thin boundary layers, or due to the fact that the atomic bonds
are broken at the surface of the body. These effects can be phenomenologically modeled in terms of boundaries equipped with
their own stored energy density and they have been well studied in the literature. Rational continuum mechanics approach to elastic surfaces
has started by the work of Gurtin and Murdoch
[
6
,
7
]
and of Steigmann and Ogden
[
25
]
. More recently,
Šilhavý introduced a concept of interfacial quasiconvexity and polyconvexity
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
extending
[
5
]
to establish the existence of minimizers for problems with multi-well bulk energy density for simple materials, i.e., depending only on the first deformation gradient. Javili and Steinmann
[
9
,
10
]
designed finite-element methods to model surface elasticity. Thermomechanical approach to interfacial and surface energetics of materials is reviewed in
[
8
,
26
]
. Surface-substrate interactions for shells is described in
[
24
]
and a phase-field modeling approach to the problem can be found e.g. in
[
11
]
.

Rigorous derivation of linear models from nonlinear elasticity by
\Gamma
-convergence
[
2
]
has started by a pioneering work
[
3
]
and then various finer results appeared
[
1
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]

that derive linear elasticity under various constraints as, e.g., incompressibility, or no Dirichlet boundary conditions, i.e., pure traction problems. We also refer to
[
19
]
where the authors performed the linearization procedure for a pressure load, i.e., living load (a follower force) that depends on the deformation.

Our contribution focuses on the linearization of nonlinear elasticity models with surface energy terms.
In order to fix ideas, we perform the analysis on the model functional
\mathcal{G}
below that penalizes local changes of the surface area in the deformed configuration in analogy with the incompressibility constraint in the bulk. The main results are given in Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
, stating the convergence of minimizers of nonlinear models to the unique minimizer of the associated linearized models, in case of Dirichlet boundary conditions and in case of only Neumann boundary conditions, respectively.
In the remainder of this section, we introduce the necessary notation and the functionals. The main results are then stated in Section
2
and Sections
3
and
4
are devoted to proofs.

Model elastic energy with surface tension

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, be a bounded open connected Lipschitz set, representing the reference configuration of a hyperelastic body.
Let us introduce the energy functional depending on the deformation field
y:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathcal{G}(y):=\int_{\Omega}\left(W(\nabla y(x))+H(\nabla^{2}y(x))\right)dx-\overline{\mathcal{L}}(y(x)-x)+\gamma\left\|\,|\mathrm{cof\,}\nabla y\,\mathbf{n}|-1\,\right\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega)}.

The global energy includes the stored elastic energy of a nonsimple material, as it depends on second gradient
[
27
,
28
]
. Here,
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
is a dead loading functional, accounting for the work of given external force fields. It is a linear functional acting on the displacement field
y(x)-x
.
Moreover, local changes in the surface measure of the boundary of
\Omega
are penalized, as
|\mathrm{cof\,}\nabla y\,\mathbf{n}|
represents the density of the surface area element of the deformed configuration: the energy includes the
L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
distance to its reference value corresponding to
y(x)=x
, being
\gamma>0
a surface tension coefficient.

In the linearization process, we view
y
as a perturbation of the identity map so that we write it as
y(x)=x+\varepsilon v(x)
for a suitable rescaled displacement
v
and we set
\mathcal{L}=\overline{\mathcal{L}}/\varepsilon
, which reflects the scaling of external forces. Assuming that
H
and
W
are frame indifferent, that
H
is convex positively
p
-homogeneous for some suitable
p>1
and that
W
is minimized at the identity with
W(\mathbf{I})=DW(\mathbf{I})=0
, i.e., that identity is the natural state of the body
\Omega
, we consider the rescaled nonlinear global energy

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(v)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\nabla v)\,dx+\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{p}}\int_{\Omega}H(\varepsilon\nabla^{2}v)-\mathcal{L}(v)\color[rgb]{0,0,0}

(1.1)

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon}\,\|\,|\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\nabla v)\,\mathbf{n}|-1\,\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega)}.

We notice that as
\varepsilon\to 0

W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\nabla v)=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\,\mathbb{E}(v)\,\nabla^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v)+o(\varepsilon^{2}),

so that the second order term in the Taylor expansion of
W
produces the standard quadratic potential of linear elasticity (being
\nabla^{2}W(\mathbf{I})
the fourth order elastic tensor), acting by frame indifference only on the symmetric part of the gradient

\mathbb{E}(v):=\frac{\nabla v+(\nabla v)^{T}}{2}.

On the other hand, by the formula
\mathrm{cof\,}\mathbf{F}=(\det\mathbf{F})\,\mathbf{F}^{-T}
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\nabla v)

\displaystyle=\det(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\nabla v)\,(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon(\nabla v)^{T})^{-1}

(1.2)

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon\,\mathrm{div}\,v+o(\varepsilon))\,(\mathbf{I}-\varepsilon(\nabla v)^{T}+o(\varepsilon))=\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\mathbb{A}(v)+o(\varepsilon),

where we have introduced the divergence free tensor

\mathbb{A}(v)=\mathbf{I}\,\mathrm{div}\,v-(\nabla v)^{T},

corresponding to the linearization of the cofactor matrix. As a consequence,

|\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\nabla v)\mathbf{n}|=\sqrt{1+2\varepsilon\,\mathbb{A}(v)\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}+o(\varepsilon)}=1+\varepsilon\,\mathbb{A}(v)\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}+o(\varepsilon).

(1.3)

Therefore in the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
we obtain the geometrically linearized functional

\mathcal{G}_{*}(v):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{E}(v)\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla^{2}v)\,dx-\mathcal{L}(v)+\gamma\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathbb{A}(v)\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}|\,dS

(1.4)

as the pointwise limit of functionals
\mathcal{G}_{h}
for every smooth enough
v
. We notice that the stored elastic energy and the surface tension term of functional
\mathcal{G}
are frame indifferent, and indeed their counterparts in functional
\mathcal{G}_{*}
depend on
\nabla v
only through its symmetric part
\mathbb{E}(v)
, so that as expected they are invariant by addition of an infinitesimal rigid displacement. We have indeed
\nabla^{2}(c+\mathbf{W}x)=0
and
\mathbb{A}(c+\mathbf{W}x)\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
for every
c\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and every
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{skew}}^{d\times d}
. We stress that the natural choice
H(\nabla^{2}v)=|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}
, yielding a quadratic stored elastic energy in (
1.4
), is possible within our theory in the physical cases
d=2,3
(as our main results will be given under the restriction
p>d/2
).

Alternative model energies

Let us next introduce possible alternative models, for which we will prove the validity of the same convergence results. We can consider a surface energy term that penalizes differences in surface areas between the reference and deformed configurations, e.g.,

\mathcal{F}(y):=\int_{\Omega}(W(\nabla y)+H(\nabla^{2}y))\,dx-\overline{\mathcal{L}}(y(x)-x)+\gamma\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathrm{cof\,}\nabla y(\sigma)\,\mathbf{n}(\sigma)|\,dS(\sigma)-|\partial\Omega|\right|.

The change-of-variables formula for surface integrals indicates that the last term is equal to
\gamma\,||\partial\Omega^{y}|-|\partial\Omega||
, where
\partial\Omega^{y}
denotes the boundary of the deformed configuration
y(\Omega)
. Here
\gamma>0
is again a physical parameter.
The associated rescaled energies are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(v):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\nabla v)\,dx+\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{p}}\int_{\Omega}H(\varepsilon\nabla^{2}v)\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\mathcal{L}(v)

(1.5)

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon}\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\nabla v)\,\mathbf{n}|\,dS-|\partial\Omega|\right|.

Taking into account (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) we see that the as
\varepsilon\to 0

\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\nabla v)\,\mathbf{n}|\,dS-|\partial\Omega|\right|=\varepsilon\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathbb{A}(v)\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS\right|+o(\varepsilon),

so that the formal linearized functional obtained as
\varepsilon\to 0
is

\mathcal{F}_{*}(v):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{E}(v)\,\nabla^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla^{2}v)\,dx-\mathcal{L}(v)+\gamma\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathbb{A}(v)\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS\right|.

(1.6)

We notice that the divergence theorem implies, assuming enough smoothness of
\partial\Omega
and introducing a suitable extension of the normal
\mathbf{n}
to the whole of
\Omega
,

\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathbb{A}(v)\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS=\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathbb{A}(v)^{T}\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\,(\mathbb{A}(v)^{T}\mathbf{n})\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{A}(v):\nabla\mathbf{n}\,dx,

having used the fact that
\mathbb{A}(v)
is divergence free. Therefore functional
\mathcal{F}_{*}
can also be rewritten as

\mathcal{F}_{*}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{E}(v)\,\nabla^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla^{2}v)\,dx-\mathcal{L}(v)+\gamma\left|\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{A}(v):\nabla\mathbf{n}\,dx\right|.

We can also consider a model featuring surface loading

\mathcal{I}(y):=\int_{\Omega}(W(\nabla y)+H(\nabla^{2}y))\,dx-\overline{\mathcal{L}}(y(x)-x)+\gamma\int_{\partial\Omega}|(\mathrm{cof\,}\nabla y(\sigma)-\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{n}(\sigma)|\,dS(\sigma).

The last term is the
L^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
distance between the normal vector
\mathbf{n}^{y}=\mathrm{cof\,}\nabla y\,\mathbf{n}
in the deformed configuration and the undeformed one corresponding to
y(x)=x
. This restricts energetically favorable deformations in a stronger way as it penalizes local changes not only in the area of the boundary but also in the orientation of the boundary, and indeed the last term pays energy also for rigid motions of the reference configuration. Again we shall introduce the rescaled energies

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(v):

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\nabla v)\,dx+\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{p}}\int_{\Omega}H(\varepsilon\nabla^{2}v)\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\mathcal{L}(v)

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon}\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|(\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\nabla v)-\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{n}\right|\,dS.

Thanks to (
1.2
) we see that the formal limiting functional as
\varepsilon\to 0
is

\mathcal{I}_{*}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{E}(v)\,\nabla^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla^{2}v)\,dx-\mathcal{L}(v)+\gamma\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathbb{A}(v)\mathbf{n}|\,dS.

The main convergence results about functionals
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
, that we shall state in the next section, carry over for functionals
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
(we refer to Corollary
2.3
below).

2.
Main results

Let
d\geq 2
.
The strain energy density
W:\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\to[0,+\infty]
appearing in (
1.1
) is a frame indifferent function that is assumed to be minimized at rotations, and sufficiently smooth around rotations. Summarizing, the basic assumptions satisfied by
W
are the following

\displaystyle\mbox{$W:\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\to[0,+\infty]$ \color[rgb]{0,0,0}is continuous \color[rgb]{0,0,0}},

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mbox{$W(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{F})=W(\mathbf{F})$ for every $\mathbf{R}\in SO(d)$ and every $\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$},

\displaystyle\mbox{$W(\mathbf{F})\geq W(\mathbf{I})=0$ for every $\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$},

\displaystyle\mbox{$W\in C^{2}(\mathcal{U})$ for some suitable open neighborhood $\mathcal{U}$ of $SO(d)$ in $\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$},

where
SO(d)
denotes the special orthogonal group. A consequence of the smoothness of
W
around rotations is therefore
DW(\mathbf{R})=0
for every
\mathbf{R}\in SO(d)
. Continuity of
W
means that whenever
F_{k}\to F
in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
then
W(F_{k})\to W(F)
as
k\to\infty
. Moreover,
W
is assumed to satisfy the following convexity property at the identity:

\mbox{there exists $C>0$ such that }\;\;\mathbf{F}^{T}\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{F}\geq C|\mathrm{sym}\,\mathbf{F}|^{2}\;\;\mbox{ for every $\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$},

(2.2)

where
\mathrm{sym}\,\mathbf{F}
denotes the symmetric part of
\mathbf{F}
and
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, i.e.,
|\mathbf{G}|^{2}=\mathrm{tr}\,(\mathbf{G}^{T}\mathbf{G})
. Here,
D^{2}W
denotes the Hessian of
W
, and
D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})
is the fourth order elasticity tensor, appearing in the quadratic potential acting on the infinitesimal strain tensor in the linearized energy
\mathcal{G}_{*}
from (
1.4
).
Another standard coercivity condition that we shall use is the following:

\mbox{there exists $\bar{C}>0$ such that }\;\;W(\mathbf{F})\geq\bar{C}\,\mathrm{dist}^{2}(\mathbf{F},SO(d))\;\;\mbox{ for every $\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$},

(2.3)

where
\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{F},SO(d)):=\inf\{|\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{R}|:\mathbf{R}\in SO(d)\}
. We remark that if
W
satisfies (
2.1
), then (
2.3
) is stronger than (
2.2
). Indeed, since for every
\mathbf{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with positive determinant there holds
\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{G},SO(d))=|\sqrt{\mathbf{G}^{T}\mathbf{G}}-\mathbf{I}|
, we see that if (
2.3
) holds then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{F}^{T}\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{F}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\mathbf{F})\geq\bar{C}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\mathrm{dist}^{2}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\mathbf{F},SO(d))

\displaystyle={\bar{C}}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,|\sqrt{(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\mathbf{F}^{T})(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon\mathbf{F})}-\mathbf{I}|^{2}=\bar{C}|\mathrm{sym}\,\mathbf{F}|^{2}

for every
\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.
We also remark that the natural conditions
\det\mathbf{F}\leq 0\Rightarrow W(\mathbf{F})=+\infty
and
W(\mathbf{F})\to+\infty
if
\det\mathbf{F}\to 0^{+}
are compatible with all the above assumptions, but not necessary for the theory.

We will further assume that, for suitable
p>1
, the function
H
appearing in the second gradient term of (
1.1
) satisfies the following:

\displaystyle H:\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d}\to\mathbb{R}{\textrm{ is a convex positively $p$-homogeneous function}},

(2.4)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\textrm{there exist $C_{0}>0$, $C_{1}>0\;\;$ s.t. $\;\forall\,\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d}$ }}\;\;C_{0}|\mathbf{B}|^{p}\leq H(\mathbf{B})\leq C_{1}(1+|\mathbf{B}|^{p}),

\displaystyle H(\mathbf{RB})=H(\mathbf{B})\textrm{ for every }\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d}\textrm{ and every }\mathbf{R}\in SO(d).

Here the product between
\mathbf{R}
and
\mathbf{B}
is defined as
(\mathbf{RB})_{imn}=\mathbf{R}_{ik}\mathbf{B}_{kmn}
for all
i,m,n\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.

The load functional
\mathcal{L}
appearing in
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
and in
\mathcal{G}_{*}
is assumed to be a linear continuous functional on
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
p>1
is the exponent that appears in (
2.4
), so that there exists a constant
C_{\mathcal{L}}>0
such that

|\mathcal{L}(v)|\leq C_{\mathcal{L}}\,\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad\mbox{$\forall\;v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})$}.

(2.5)

Let us start by considering the Dirichlet problem. Let
\Gamma
denote a closed subset of
\partial\Omega
such that
S(\Gamma)=\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Gamma)>0
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
p>1
, let
W^{k,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the
W^{k,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
completion of the space of restrictions to
\Omega
of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Gamma;\mathbb{R}^{d})
functions. Taking advantage of the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition on
\Gamma
, for
p>1
we have the following Poincaré inequality: there is
K>0
such that

\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq K\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d})}\quad\mbox{$\forall\;v\in W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})$}.

(2.6)

The following is the main result about convergence of minimizers of the Dirichlet problem for functionals
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
. For
p>d/2
we let
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}:W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be defined by (
1.1
) and
\mathcal{G}_{*}:W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by (
1.4
). Existence of minimizers over
W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
for functional
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
(for fixed
\varepsilon
) and functional
\mathcal{G}_{*}
will be preliminarily proved in Section
3
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
p>d/2
. Suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is a bounded linear functional on
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, that
W
satisfies (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), and that (
2.4
) holds. Let
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
be a vanishing sequence and let
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a sequence of minimizers for functionals
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}
over
W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then the sequence
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is weakly converging in
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
to the unique solution to the problem

\min\left\{\mathcal{G}_{*}(v):v\in W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}.

When considering the pure traction problem, it is natural to assume that external loads are equilibrated, i.e., with null resultant and momentum:

\mathcal{L}(a+\mathbf{W}x)=0\quad\mbox{for every $a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and every skew-symmetric matrix $\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$}.

(2.7)

A consequence of (
2.7
) is that, by invoking Korn and Poincaré inequalities, if
\mathcal{L}
is a bounded linear functional over
W^{2,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
r>1
, thus satisfying (
2.5
) with
p=r
, and if
u\in W^{2,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have for some suitable
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and some suitable skew-symmetric
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}(u)|

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{L}(u-\mathbf{W}x-a)|

(2.8)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathcal{L}}\left(\|u-\mathbf{W}x-a\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\nabla u-\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}+\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathcal{L}}\left((1+\mathfrak{c})\mathfrak{K}\,\|\mathbb{E}(u)\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}+\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d})}\right),

where
\mathfrak{c}
is the constant in Poincaré inequality and
\mathfrak{K}
is the constant in second Korn inequality
[
20
]
, both depending on
r
and
\Omega
only.

A further condition that proves to be necessary for the theory is

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}x-x)\leq 0\quad\mbox{ for every $\mathbf{R}\in SO(d)$}

(2.9)

expressing the fact that external loads have an overall effect of traction (and not of compression) on
\Omega
. This condition appears in
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
17
]
.
Following the same references, for load functionals that satisfy (
2.9
) we introduce the rotation kernel

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{0}:=\{\mathbf{R}\in SO(d):\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}x-x)=0\},

which plays a crucial role in the linearization process. Indeed, if
\mathbf{R}_{0}\in SO(d)
exists such that
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{0}x-x)>0
, if
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
is any vanishing sequence, letting
\tilde{v}_{j}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}(\mathbf{R}_{0}x-x)
, since
\mathrm{cof\,}\mathbf{R}_{0}=\mathbf{R}_{0}
,
W(\mathbf{R}_{0})=0
and
\nabla^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{0}x-x)=0
, we see that

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\tilde{v}_{j})=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{0}x-x),

thus

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\inf_{C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}=-\infty.

Therefore (
2.9
) is a necessary condition for avoiding the nonlinear energies being unbounded from below as the parameter
\varepsilon
goes to zero. Moreover, under the conditions (
2.7
) and (
2.9
), it turns out (see Theorem
2.2
below) that the actual limit functional is

\overline{\mathcal{G}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{E}(u)\,\nabla^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(u)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla^{2}v)\,dx-\max_{\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}v)+\gamma\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathbb{A}(v)\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}|\,dS.

We may notice that under a stronger condition on external loads, i.e. under the additional assumption
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}\equiv\{\mathbf{I}\}
(meaning that external loads do not have any axis of equilibrium), then
\overline{\mathcal{G}}\equiv\mathcal{G}_{*}
. See
[
15
,
17
]
for a thorough discussion on this topic in linearized models with no surface tension effect.

We have the following second main result.
It will be proven in Section
4
, after having proven the existence of minimizers over
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, in the pure traction problem, for functional
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
(for fixed
\varepsilon
) and for functional
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
p>d/2
. Let
W
satisfy (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) and let
H
satisfy (
2.4
). Let
\mathcal{L}
be a bounded linear functional over
W^{2,2\wedge p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
that satisfies (
2.7
) and (
2.9
).
Let
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
be a vanishing sequence.
If
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a sequence of minimizers of
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}
over
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then there exists a sequence

(\mathbf{R}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset SO(d)
such that, by defining

u_{j}(x):=\mathbf{R}_{j}^{T}v_{j}(x)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}(\mathbf{R}^{T}_{j}x-x),

in the limit as
j\to+\infty
, along a suitable (not relabeled) subsequence, there holds

\nabla u_{j}\to\nabla u_{*}\;\mbox{ weakly in $W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})$},\quad

where
u_{*}\in W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a minimizer of
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
over
W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
and

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(v_{j})\to\overline{\mathcal{G}}(u_{*}),\quad\qquad\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\min_{W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}\to\min_{W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\overline{\mathcal{G}}.

(2.10)

In order to obtain compactness in Theorem
2.2
, we shall make use of the Firesecke-James-Müller rigidity inequality
[
4
]
, stating that there exists a constant
C_{\Omega}>0
(only depending on
\Omega
) such that for every
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
there is
\mathbf{R}\in SO(d)
such that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi-\mathbf{R}|^{2}\leq C_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(\nabla\varphi,SO(d)).

(2.11)

In fact, as we shall see from the proof the sequence
(\mathbf{R}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in Theorem
2.2
can be chosen as a sequence of rotations for which (
2.11
) holds with
\varphi=v_{j}
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

We conclude by remarking that
our main converge results hold true for the other model energies that we have introduced in Section

. Indeed, defining
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
(reps.
\overline{\mathcal{I}}

with the term
\max_{\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}v)
in functional
\mathcal{F}_{*}
(resp.
\mathcal{I}_{*}
) we have

.

The same statement of
Theorem
2.1
hold true if we replace functionals
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
and functional
\mathcal{G}_{*}
therein with functionals
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
(resp.
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
) and functional
\mathcal{F}_{*}
(resp.
\mathcal{I}_{*}
).
Moreover, the same statement of
Theorem
2.2
hold true if we replace functionals
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
and functional
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
therein with functionals
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
(resp.

) and functional
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
(resp.
\overline{\mathcal{I}}

3.
The Dirichlet problem: proof of Theorem
2.1

We first prove existence of minimizers for functional
\mathcal{G}_{*}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p\geq 2d/(d+2)
. If
d=2
, let
p>1
. Let (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) hold. Let
\mathcal{L}
be a bounded linear functional over
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. There exists a unique solution to the problem

\min\{\mathcal{G}_{*}(v):v\in W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\}.

Proof.

The conditions
d>p\geq 2d/(d+2)
and
p>1
imply that
{dp}/{(d-p)}\geq 2
and
(d-1)p/(d-p)>1
, therefore we have the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the Sobolev trace embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
, the latter being compact. These embeddings also hold true if
p\geq d
. We deduce that
\mathbb{E}(v)\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and that
\mathbb{A}(v)\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
for every
v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Thus
\mathcal{G}_{*}(v)
is finite for every
v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

By (
2.1
), (
2.4
), (
2.5
), and (
2.6
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{*}(v)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla^{2}v)-C_{\mathcal{L}}\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\geq C_{0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}v|^{p}-C_{\mathcal{L}}\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{0}}{K^{p}}\|v\|^{p}_{W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}-C_{\mathcal{L}}\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}

and the right hand side is uniformly bounded from below on
W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
because
p>1
.

Let
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a minimizing sequence. The above estimate, along with (
2.6
) and
\mathcal{G}_{*}(0)=0
, shows that such a sequence is bounded in
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, hence admitting a (not relabeled) weakly converging subsequence, the limit point being denoted by
v
. However, the term involving
H
in functional
\mathcal{G}_{*}
is weakly lower semicontinuous over
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
thanks to (
2.4
), while
\mathcal{L}
is weakly continuous over
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, as well as the interfacial term (due to the fact that the Sobolev trace embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact, which also implies that
v\in W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
). On the other hand, we have

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}(v_{n})\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v_{n})\geq\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{E}(v)\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v)

by the embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and by the weak
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
lower semicontinuity of the map
\mathbf{G}\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{G}^{T}\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{G}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{G}_{*}
is lower semicontinuous with respect to the weak
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
convergence. The result follows by the direct method of the calculus of variations.
Uniqueness of the minimizer follows from strict convexity of
\mathcal{G}_{*}
.
∎

The following is a key lemma, providing the rigorous linearization of the interfacial term.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p>d/2
. Let
v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
be a vanishing sequence and let
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a sequence such that
\nabla v_{j}
weakly converge to
\nabla v
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
as
j\to+\infty
. Then

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\left||\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|-1\right|\,dS=\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathbb{A}(v)\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}|\,dS

(3.2)

Proof.

We have the Sobolev trace embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{(d-1)p/(d-p)}(\partial\Omega)
if
d>p
.
Else if
p\geq d
, we have
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\partial\Omega)
for every
r\in[1,+\infty)
.
Since
p>d/2
, we have
(d-1)p/(d-p)>d-1
. Since the entries of
\mathrm{cof\,}\mathbf{F}
are polynomials of degree
d-1
in the entries of
\mathbf{F}
, we deduce that
\mathrm{cof\,}\nabla\psi\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
for every
\psi\in W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This shows that all the integrands appearing in (
3.2
) are in
L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
.

By the Cayley-Hamilton formula,
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})=\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\mathbb{A}(v_{j})+\sum_{k=2}^{d-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{k}\,\mathbb{B}_{k}(v_{j})

where
\mathbb{B}_{k}(v_{j})
is a matrix whose entries are polynomials of degree
k
in the entries of
\nabla v_{j}
, and the sum is understood to be zero if
d=2
. Letting

\mathbb{B}(v_{j}):=\sum_{k=2}^{d-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{k-2}\mathbb{B}_{k}(v_{j}),

we get therefore

\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})=\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\mathbb{A}(v_{j})+\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,\mathbb{B}(v_{j}).

(3.3)

We notice that

\displaystyle|\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,\mathbb{B}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|^{2}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=1+2\varepsilon_{j}\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,\mathbb{D}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n},

where

\mathbb{D}(v_{j}):=\mathbb{A}(v_{j})^{T}\mathbb{A}(v_{j})+2\mathbb{B}(v_{j})+2\varepsilon_{j}\,\mathbb{B}(v_{j})^{T}\mathbb{A}(v_{j})+\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,\mathbb{B}(v_{j})^{T}\,\mathbb{B}(v_{j}).

(3.5)

We observe that the following properties hold:

\mbox{$\nabla v_{j}\to\nabla v$ strongly in $L^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})$ as $j\to+\infty$},

(3.6)

\mbox{$\mathbb{A}(\nabla v_{j})\to\mathbb{A}(\nabla v)$ strongly in $L^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})$ as $j\to+\infty$},

(3.7)

\mbox{the sequence $(\mathbb{B}(v_{j}))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}$ is bounded in $L^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})$}.

(3.8)

Indeed, (
3.8
) follows from the Sobolev trace embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{(d-1)p/(d-p)}(\partial\Omega)
, since
d>p>d/2
implies
(d-1)p/(d-p)>d-1
, and since
\mathbb{B}(v_{j})
is polynomial of degree
d-1
in the entries of
\nabla v_{j}
(and
\varepsilon_{j}<1
). Else if
p\geq d
we have the embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\partial\Omega)
for every
r\in[1,+\infty)
. On the other hand, (
3.6
) and its direct consequence (
3.7
) are due to the compactness of the
Sobolev trace embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\partial\Omega)
for every
q\in[1,{(d-1)p}/(d-p))
. In particular,
\nabla v
has a
L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
trace on
\partial\Omega
.

We define

Q_{j}:=\{x\in\partial\Omega:|\mathbb{A}(v_{j}(x))|+\varepsilon_{j}|\mathbb{B}(v_{j}(x))|<2^{-4}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1/4}\}

(3.9)

and we notice that

S(\partial\Omega\setminus Q_{j})\leq\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus Q_{j}}16\varepsilon_{j}^{1/4}(|\mathbb{A}(v_{j})|+\varepsilon_{j}|\mathbb{B}(v_{j})|)\,dS\leq 16\varepsilon_{j}^{1/4}\int_{\partial\Omega}(|\mathbb{A}(v_{j})|+\varepsilon_{j}|\mathbb{B}(v_{j})|)\,dS

so that (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) imply that
S(\partial\Omega\setminus Q_{j})\to 0
as
j\to+\infty
.
By using (
3.9
), (
3.5
) and
\varepsilon_{j}<1
it is not difficult to see that on
Q_{j}
there hold

|2\varepsilon_{j}\,\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,\mathbb{D}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}|\leq 2\varepsilon_{j}\,|\mathbb{A}(v_{j})|+\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,|\mathbb{D}(v_{j})|<\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{j}^{3/4}<\frac{1}{2},

(3.10)

\varepsilon_{j}|\mathbb{D}(v_{j})|\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon_{j}}+2\varepsilon_{j}|\mathbb{B}(v_{j})|.

(3.11)

Thanks to (
3.10
), starting from (
3.4
) and using
Taylor series, on
Q_{j}
we find that

\displaystyle|\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|-1=\sqrt{1+2\varepsilon_{j}\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,\mathbb{D}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}}-1

(3.12)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{j}\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\frac{\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}{2}\mathbb{D}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\sum_{k=2}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}(2\varepsilon_{j}\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,\mathbb{D}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n})^{k},

where

\alpha_{k}:=\dfrac{(-1)^{k-1}(2k)!}{4^{k}(k!)^{2}(2k-1)}
(in particular we have
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k+2}\,2^{-k}<+\infty
)
and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}\,(2\varepsilon_{j}\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,\mathbb{D}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n})^{k}

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,(2\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}\,\mathbb{D}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n})^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k+2}(2\varepsilon_{j}\,\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,\mathbb{D}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n})^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,(2\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}\,\mathbb{D}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n})^{2}\,\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k+2}\,2^{-k}\leq\varepsilon_{j}^{3/2}\,\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k+2}\,2^{-k}.

From (
3.12
) we have

\displaystyle{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{j}}\,\frac{|\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|-1}{\varepsilon_{j}}

\displaystyle={\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{j}}\,\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}+{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{j}}\,\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{2}\mathbb{D}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle+{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{j}}\,\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\sum_{k=2}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}(2\varepsilon_{j}\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,\mathbb{D}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n})^{k},

where we may notice that the last two terms on the right hand side converge strongly to
0
in
L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
, thanks to (
3.8
), (
3.11
) and (
3.13
).
Since
S(\partial\Omega\setminus Q_{j})\to 0
and since (
3.7
) holds, we deduce that

{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{j}}\,\frac{|\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|-1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\to\mathbb{A}(v)\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}\quad\mbox{strongly in $L^{1}(\partial\Omega)$ as $j\to+\infty$}

(3.14)

so that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\int_{Q_{j}}||\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|-1|\,dS=\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathbb{A}(v)\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}|\,dS.

In order to conclude, we are left to prove that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus Q_{j}}||\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|-1|\,dS=0.

(3.15)

But (
3.4
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon j}\,||\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|-1|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\,||\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,\mathbb{B}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|-|\mathbf{n}||

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\,|\varepsilon_{j}\,\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\,\mathbb{B}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|\leq|\mathbb{A}(v_{j})|+\varepsilon_{j}|\mathbb{B}(v_{j})|,

and since
S(\partial\Omega\setminus Q_{j})\to 0
, (
3.15
) follows from (
3.7
) and (
3.8
).
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
p>d/2
. Let
v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
be a vanishing sequence and let
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a sequence such that
\nabla v_{j}
weakly converge to
\nabla v
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
as
j\to+\infty
. Then

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|dS-|\partial\Omega|\right|=\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathbb{A}(v)\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS\right|

(3.16)

and

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\int_{\partial\Omega}|(\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})-\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{n}|\,dS=\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathbb{A}(v)\,\mathbf{n}|\,dS

(3.17)

Proof.

By following the proof of Lemma
3.2
, with
Q_{j}
still defined by (
3.9
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|dS-|\partial\Omega|\right|=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\left|\int_{Q_{j}}(|\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|-1)\,dS\right|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\left|\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus Q_{j}}(|\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|-1)\,dS\right|

so that (
3.16
) follows from (
3.14
) and (
3.15
). On the other hand, (
3.3
) implies that

\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}(\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})-\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{n}=\mathbb{A}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon_{j}\mathbb{B}(v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}

so that (
3.17
) follows from (
3.7
) and (
3.8
).
∎

We next prove existence of minimizers
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
, for fixed
\varepsilon>0
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p>d/2
. Suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is a bounded linear functional on
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, that
W
satisfies (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), and that (
2.4
) hold.
Then the functional
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
attains a minimum on
W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

We assume wlog that
\varepsilon=1
.
For every
v\in W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, by (
2.4
), (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) there holds

\mathcal{G}_{1}(v)\geq C_{0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}v|^{p}-\mathcal{L}(v)\geq C_{0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}v|^{p}-KC_{\mathcal{L}}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d})}.

Similarly to the proof of Lemma
3.1
, this estimate implies boundedness from below of functional
\mathcal{G}_{1}
on
W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
and since
\mathcal{G}_{1}(0)=0
, it implies that any minimizing sequence
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is bounded in
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, thus weakly converging up to subsequences to some
v\in W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This yields up to subsequences pointwise a.e. convergence of
\nabla v_{n}
to
\nabla v
, so that since
W
is continuous and nonnegative, Fatou Lemma implies lower semicontinuity of the term involving
W
in functional
\mathcal{G}_{1}
along the sequence
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
On the other hand,
\mathcal{L}
is obviously continuous along such sequence, while

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla^{2}v_{n})\geq\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla^{2}v)

is a consequence of the convexity of
H
from (
2.4
).
Finally, the interfacial term of functional
\mathcal{G}_{1}
is continuous along the sequence
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Indeed, as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we may notice that if
p\geq d
then the embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact for every
r\in[1,+\infty)
, else if
d/2<p<d
it is compact for every
r\in[1,(d-1)p/(d-p))
, including
r=d-1
since
p>d/2
, so that
\mathrm{cof\,}\nabla v_{n}\to\mathrm{cof\,}\nabla v
strongly in
L^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
.
We conclude that
v
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{G}_{1}
over
W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Convexity of
H
and continuity of the remaining terms imply the result by means of sequential weak lower semicontinuity.
∎

By means of the next two lemmas, we prove the
\Gamma
-convergence

Lemma 3.5

(
\Gamma
-limsup
)
.

Let
p>d/2
. Suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is a bounded linear functional on
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, that
W
satisfies (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), and that (
2.4
) holds. Let
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
be a vanishing sequence.
Let
v\in W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. There exists a sequence
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

v_{j}\to v\quad\mbox{strongly in $W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})$ as $j\to+\infty$}

and

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(v_{j})=\mathcal{G}_{*}(v).

Proof.

By the density of
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
in
W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with respect to the
W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
convergence, there exists a sequence
(\tilde{v}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\tilde{v}_{j}\to v
strongly in
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
j\to+\infty
.
We suppose wlog that the sequence
(\|\nabla\tilde{v}_{j}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is nondecreasing. We notice that it is converging if
p>d
, thanks to the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow C^{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
holding in such a case. In any case, if it is converging we let
v_{j}=\tilde{v}_{j}
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Else if it is diverging, we define a new sequence sequence
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{2,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
as follows:

\{\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{v}_{1},\,\ldots,\tilde{v}_{1},\;\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{v}_{2},\,\ldots,\tilde{v}_{2},\;\tilde{v}_{3},\tilde{v}_{3},\,\ldots,\tilde{v}_{3},\;\tilde{v}_{4},\tilde{v}_{4}\ldots\},

where each of the
\tilde{v}_{k}
’s is repeated
t_{k}
times, where
t_{1}
is defined as the smallest positive integer such that

\varepsilon_{i}\|\nabla\tilde{v}_{2}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}<\frac{1}{2}\quad\mbox{for every integer $i\geq 1+t_{1}$},

whose existence is ensured by the fact that the sequence
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is vanishing, and where

t_{k}
is then recursively defined as the smallest positive integer such that

\varepsilon_{i}\|\nabla\tilde{v}_{k+1}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}<\frac{1}{k}\quad\mbox{ for every integer $i\geq 1+t_{1}+\ldots+t_{k}$}.

It is clear that we have
v_{j}\to v
strongly in
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
j\to+\infty
. Moreover, by construction we also have

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\varepsilon_{j}\|\nabla v_{j}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}=0.

(3.18)

We notice that the assumptions on the strain energy density
W
imply that

\left|W(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{F})-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{F}^{T}\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{F}\right|\leq\omega(|\mathbf{F}|)\,|\mathbf{F}|^{2}

for every
\mathbf{F}\in\mathcal{\tilde{U}}\subset\subset\mathcal{U}
, where
\omega:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
denotes the modulus of uniform continuity of
D^{2}W
on
\mathcal{\tilde{U}}
, which is a continuous nondecreasing function that vanishes at
0
. But (
3.18
) implies that, for every large enough
j
, we have
\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j}(x)\in\mathcal{\tilde{U}}
for every
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. Therefore, if
j
is large enough, we get

\left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j}(x))-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}(v_{j}(x))\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v_{j}(x))\right|\leq\omega(\varepsilon_{j}|\nabla v_{j}(x)|)\,|\nabla v_{j}(x)|^{2}

(3.19)

for every
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, having used the fact that by frame indifference
D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})
acts as a quadratic form only on the symmetric part of
\nabla v_{j}
.

We notice that, since
p>d/2
, if
d>p
we have
p_{*}:=dp/(d-p)>2
, therefore we have the embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
, still holding if
p\geq d
. Therefore
\mathbb{E}(v_{j})\to\mathbb{E}(v)
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}(v)\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v)\right|\leq\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}(v_{j})\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v_{j})\right|

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}(v_{j})\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v_{j})-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}(v)\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v)\right|

and the second term in the right hand side goes to
0
as
j\to+\infty
. On the other hand, the first term in the right hand side gets estimated by means of (
3.19
) as

\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}(v_{j})\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v_{j})\right|\leq\omega(\varepsilon_{j}\|\nabla v_{j}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})})\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{j}|^{2}

and so it vanishes as well as
j\to+\infty
, thanks to (
3.18
) and to the boundedness of
(\nabla v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d,\times d})
, since
\omega(t)\to 0
as
t\to 0
.
We conclude that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{E}(v)\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v).

With respect to the strong
W^{2,p}
convergence, the interfacial term is continuous by Lemma
3.2
, while the load term and the second gradient term are obviously continuous. The proof is concluded.
∎

Lemma 3.6

(
\Gamma
-liminf
)
.

Let
p>d/2
. Suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is a bounded linear functional on

) and (

), and that (

Similarly to the proof of Lemma

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{H_{j}}\mathbb{E}(v_{j})\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(v_{j})-\int_{H_{j}}\omega(\varepsilon_{j}|\nabla v_{j}|)\,|\nabla v_{j}|^{2}\right)

in

With respect to the weak

, the load term is continuous, and the second gradient term is lower semicontinuous thanks to the assumptions (

∎

and
\Omega
) such that

c\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}=\frac{c}{\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{R}_{j}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j}),

and then we deduce, since
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(v_{j})\leq M
and since (
2.9
) holds, that

\displaystyle c\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla^{2}u_{j})

\displaystyle\leq M+\mathcal{L}(v_{j})

(4.5)

\displaystyle=M+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\,\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{j}x-x)+\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{j}u_{j})\leq M+\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{j}u_{j}).

By including (
2.8
) with
r=2\wedge p
and Hölder inequality we obtain

c\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla^{2}u_{j})\leq M+Q\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}+Q\|\nabla^{2}u_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d})}.

where
Q>0
is a suitable constant, only depending on
\Omega,p
and on
C_{\mathcal{L}}
from (
2.5
), and then by Young inequality we get

c\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla^{2}u_{j})\leq M+\frac{Q^{2}}{2\delta^{2}}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}+\frac{p-1}{p}\left(\frac{Q}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}+\frac{\delta^{p}}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u_{j}|^{p}

for every
\delta>0
. Choosing small enough
\delta
we see that the sequence
(\nabla u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and that the sequence
(\nabla^{2}u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d})
.
By Poincaré inequality, there exists a sequence
(a_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a positive constant
\mathfrak{c}
(only depending on
\Omega
) such that
\|u_{j}-a_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\mathfrak{c}\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, by passing to a suitable not relabeled subsequence, we have the existence of
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
u_{j}-a_{j}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\nabla u_{j}\to\nabla u
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})

as
j\to+\infty
; the uniform bound on the sequence
(\nabla^{2}u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d})

and Poincaré inequality again allow to conclude that
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and that
\nabla u_{j}\rightharpoonup\nabla u
weakly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
.

On the other hand, by (
4.5
) and (
2.9
) we have

0\leq-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{j}x-x)\leq M+\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{j}u_{j}).

But then (
2.5
) and
the already established uniform bounds of the sequence
(\nabla u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and of the sequence
(\nabla^{2}u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d})
yield

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{j}x-x)=0.

This shows that any limit point of the sequence
(\mathbf{R}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
belongs to
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
p>d/2
. Let
W
satisfy (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). Let
\mathcal{L}
be a bounded linear functional over
W^{2,2\wedge p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
that satisfies (
2.7
) and (
2.9
). Let
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
be a vanishing sequence. Let
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
(\mathbf{R}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset SO(d)
be sequences such that

\nabla u_{j}\rightharpoonup\nabla u\quad\mbox{weakly in $W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})$}\quad\mbox{as $j\to+\infty$},

where
u_{j}:=\mathbf{R}_{j}^{T}v_{j}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}(\mathbf{R}_{j}^{T}x-x)
, and
\mathbf{R}_{j}\to\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
as
j\to+\infty
. Then

\overline{\mathcal{G}}(u)\leq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(v_{j}).

Proof.

Let

T_{j}:=\{x\in\Omega:\sqrt{\varepsilon_{j}}|\nabla u_{j}(x)|<1\},

so that

|\Omega\setminus T_{j}|\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus T_{j}}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{j}}|\nabla u_{j}|\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon_{j}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{j}|,

thus
|\Omega\setminus T_{j}|\to 0
as
j\to+\infty
. By repeating the argument in the proof of Lemma
3.6
, for every
x\in T_{j}
we have
\varepsilon_{j}|\nabla u_{j}(x)|<\sqrt{\varepsilon_{j}}
and thus (
3.19
) holds for every large enough
j
, yielding

\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})=\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla u_{j})\geq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}\int_{T_{j}}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla u_{j})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{T_{j}}\mathbb{E}(u_{j})\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(u_{j})-\int_{T_{j}}\omega(\varepsilon_{j}|\nabla u_{j}|)\,|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}({\mathbbm{1}}_{T_{j}}\mathbb{E}(u_{j}))\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,({\mathbbm{1}}_{T_{j}}\mathbb{E}(u_{j}))-\int_{T_{j}}\omega(\sqrt{\varepsilon_{j}})|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}\right),

where the first equality is due to the frame indifference of
W
.
But
\omega(\sqrt{\varepsilon_{j}})\to 0
as
j\to+\infty
and
(\nabla u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. Moreover,
for every
\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
we have

\left|\int_{\Omega}\eta:{\mathbbm{1}}_{\Omega\setminus T_{j}}\mathbb{E}(u_{j})\right|\leq\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times{\rm d}})}\left(\int_{\Omega\setminus T_{j}}|\eta|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where the right hand side goes to zero as
j\to+\infty
since
|\Omega\setminus T_{j}|\to 0
, so that
{\mathbbm{1}}_{\Omega\setminus T_{j}}\mathbb{E}(u_{j})\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
and by writing

{\mathbbm{1}}_{T_{j}}\mathbb{E}(u_{j})=\mathbb{E}(u_{j})-{\mathbbm{1}}_{\Omega\setminus T_{j}}\mathbb{E}(u_{j})

we see that
{\mathbbm{1}}_{T_{j}}\mathbb{E}(u_{j})\rightharpoonup\mathbb{E}(u)

weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
.
We conclude that

\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{E}(u)\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(u),

(4.6)

thanks to the weak
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
semicontinuity of the map
\mathbf{F}\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{F}^{T}\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{F}
.

By the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, holding since
p>d/2
, we get
\nabla u_{j}\rightharpoonup\nabla u
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
.
By Poincaré inequality we deduce the existence of a sequence
(a_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and of
\bar{u}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
u_{j}-a_{j}\rightharpoonup\bar{u}
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and such that
\nabla\bar{u}=\nabla u
on
\Omega
. Since
\nabla^{2}u_{j}\rightharpoonup\nabla^{2}u
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d})
, we deduce that
u_{j}-a_{j}\rightharpoonup\bar{u}
weakly in
W^{2,2\wedge p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore since (
2.7
) holds and since
\mathcal{L}
is a bounded linear functional over
W^{2,2\wedge p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we get

\mathcal{L}(u_{j})=\mathcal{L}(u_{j}-a_{j})\to\mathcal{L}(\bar{u})=\mathcal{L}(u)
as
j\to+\infty
,
and since
\mathbf{R}_{j}\to\mathbf{R}
, we obtain

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{j}u_{j})=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{j}(u_{j}-a_{j}))=\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}\bar{u})=\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}u).

By taking (
2.9
) into account we have

-\mathcal{L}(v_{j})=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{j}x-x)-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{j}u_{j})\geq-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{j}u_{j})

and therefore

\liminf_{j\to+\infty}-\mathcal{L}(v_{j})\geq-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}u)\geq-\max_{\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}u).

(4.7)

Since
x+\varepsilon_{j}v_{j}=\mathbf{R}_{j}(x+\varepsilon_{j}u_{j})
, and since
\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{F})=\mathbf{R}\,\mathrm{cof\,}\mathbf{F}
for every
\mathbf{R}\in SO(d)
and every
\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, we have

\int_{\partial\Omega}||\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|-1|\,dS=\int_{\partial\Omega}||\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla u_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|-1|\,dS,

therefore Lemma
3.2
implies

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\partial\Omega}||\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla v_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|-1|\,dS=\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathbb{A}(u)\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}|.

(4.8)

The weak lower semicontinuity of the
L^{p}
norm for the second gradient term, along with (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), entails the desired result.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
p>d/2
. Let
W
satisfy (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). Let
\mathcal{L}
be a bounded linear functional over
W^{2,2\wedge p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
that satisfies (
2.7
) and (
2.9
). Let
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
be a vanishing sequence. For every
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exist a sequence
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathbf{R}_{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
such that

u_{j}\to u\;\mbox{ strongly in $W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})$}\quad

(4.9)

and such that

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}(v_{j})\leq\overline{\mathcal{G}}(u),

where
v_{j}:=\mathbf{R}_{u}u_{j}+\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}(\mathbf{R}_{u}x-x)
.

Proof.

Let
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a sequence that strongly converges to
u
in
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If
p<2
, we also have
\nabla u_{j}\to\nabla u
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
as
j\to+\infty
by Sobolev embedding, since
p>d/2
. By the same argument of the proof of Lemma
3.5
, it is possible to assume that
\varepsilon_{j}\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}\to 0
as
j\to+\infty
, therefore by repeating the arguments therein we get

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}W(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla u_{j})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{E}(u)\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(u).

We have
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u_{j}|^{p}\to\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u|^{p}
as
j\to+\infty
, and moreover by Lemma
3.2
we have

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\int_{\partial\Omega}||\mathrm{cof\,}(\mathbf{I}+\varepsilon_{j}\nabla u_{j})\,\mathbf{n}|-1|\,dS=\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathbb{A}(u)\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}|\,dS.

Let now
\mathbf{R}_{u}
be a minimizer of the function

\mathbf{R}\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{E}(u)\,D^{2}W(\mathbf{I})\,\mathbb{E}(u)+\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla^{2}u)+\gamma\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathbb{A}(u)\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}|-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}u)

over
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
,
and let
v_{j}:=\mathbf{R}_{u}u_{j}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}(\mathbf{R}_{u}x-x)
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
. We notice that by frame indifference all the terms in
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}
, excluding the load term, are the same if evaluated at
u_{j}
or
v_{j}
. In particular, we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{j\to+\infty}|\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(v_{j})-\overline{\mathcal{G}}(u)|=\limsup_{j\to+\infty}|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{u}x-x)+\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{u}u_{j})-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{u}u)|

\displaystyle=\limsup_{j\to+\infty}|\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{u}u_{j})-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}_{u}u)|

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}C_{\mathcal{L}}\left((1+\mathfrak{c})\mathfrak{K}\,\|\nabla u_{j}-\nabla u\|_{L^{2\wedge p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}+\|\nabla^{2}u_{j}-\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2\wedge p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d})}\right)=0,

having used
\mathbf{R}_{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
, (
2.8
) with
r=2\wedge p
, and (
4.9
). The proof is concluded.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{2,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a sequence of minimizers of
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}
over
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(0)=0
, we may assume wlog that
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(v_{j})\leq 1
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
, there exist
u_{*}\in W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
and a sequence
(\mathbf{R}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset SO(d)
such that, by possibly passing to a not relabeled subsequence, there hold
\mathbf{R}_{j}\to\mathbf{R}
and
\nabla u_{j}\to\nabla u_{*}
weakly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
as
j\to+\infty
, where
u_{j}:=\mathbf{R}_{j}^{T}v_{j}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}(\mathbf{R}_{j}^{T}x-x),

and

\overline{\mathcal{G}}(u_{*})\leq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(v_{j}).

(4.10)

Let now
\tilde{u}\in W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By Lemma
4.6
, there exist
\mathbf{R}_{\tilde{u}}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
and a sequence
(\tilde{u}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that, letting
\tilde{v}_{j}:=\mathbf{R}_{\tilde{u}}\tilde{u}_{j}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}(\mathbf{R}_{\tilde{u}}x-x)
, there holds

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\tilde{v}_{j})\leq\overline{\mathcal{G}}(\tilde{u}).

(4.11)

By combining (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), since
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of minimizers, we deduce

\overline{\mathcal{G}}(u_{*})\leq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(v_{j})\leq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\tilde{v}_{j})\leq\overline{\mathcal{G}}(\tilde{u}).

(4.12)

Then, the arbitrariness of
\tilde{u}
shows that
u_{*}
minimizes
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
over
W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and choosing
\tilde{u}=u_{*}
in (
4.12
) we obtain (
2.10
).

\square

Weight decomposition of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 d ​ ( ℝ ) \mathfrak{sl}_{d}({\mathbb{R}}) with respect to the adjoint representation of 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 ​ ( p , q ) \mathfrak{so}(p,q)
Jiyoung Han

Abstract
. In this concise article, we compute the weight decomposition of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 d ​ ( ℝ ) \mathfrak{sl}_{d}({\mathbb{R}}) with respect to the adjoint representation of 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 ​ ( p , q ) \mathfrak{so}(p,q) , where d = p + q d=p+q and demonstrate in detail that 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 d ​ ( ℝ ) \mathfrak{sl}_{d}({\mathbb{R}}) comprises two irreducible 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 ​ ( p , q ) \mathfrak{so}(p,q) -invariant subspaces. This can be employed to establish the well-known fact that the identity component of SO ⁡ ( p , q ) {\mathrm{SO}}(p,q) is a maximal connected subgroup of SL d ​ ( ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{R}}) .



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and consider a Lie algebra representation
(\Phi,V)
of
\mathfrak{g}
. A subspace
W\subseteq V
is called
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant
if
\Phi(X)W\subseteq W
for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, and we say that a
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant subspace
W
is
\mathfrak{g}
-irreducible
if there is no proper nontrivial subspace
W^{\prime}\subset W
which is
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant.
When
\mathfrak{g}
is a semisimple real Lie algebra, it possesses a restricted root system, thus exhibiting the property that any representation
V
for
\mathfrak{g}
decomposes into
\mathfrak{g}
-irreducible sub-representations.

In this article, our focus lies on the adjoint representation for the semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(p,q)
on
\mathfrak{sl}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
, where
d=p+q
.
The theorem below is likely familiar to experts; however, given the callenge in locating references, we will present a detailed proof.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d=p+q\geq 2
with
p,q\geq 0
. The subalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
consists of two
\mathfrak{so}(p,q)
-irreducible subspaces.

As a corollay, we can re-obtain the well-known result regarding the maximality of
{\mathrm{SO}}(p,q)
on
\mathrm{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Corollary 1.2
.

The group
{\mathrm{SO}}(p,q)^{\circ}
is maximal among connected subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
, where
d=p+q\geq 2

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we revisit the restricted root system of
\mathfrak{so}(p,q)
. Based on this root system, we obtain all of weights and corresponding weight vectors of the compliment of
\mathfrak{so}(p,q)
in
\mathfrak{sl}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
in Section 3. For readers seeking a quick overview, we display root and weight vectors for a concrete example,
\mathfrak{so}(4,2)
, in Appendix. Finally, we present the proof of Theorem
1.1
in Section 4.

2.
Restricted Root Space Decomposition of
\mathfrak{so}(p,q)

Let us briefly recall the restricted root system for
\mathfrak{so}(p,q)
, as introduced in
[
1
, Page 372-373]
.
Denote elements of
\mathfrak{sl}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
by two by two block matrix

X=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\end{array}\right)

with
A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{p}({\mathbb{R}})
,
B\in\mathrm{Mat}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}})
,
C\in\mathrm{Mat}_{q,p}({\mathbb{R}})
, and
D\in\mathrm{Mat}_{q}({\mathbb{R}})
, where
p,q\geq 0
and
p+q=d
.
For the sake of convenience, we will take basis of
\mathrm{Mat}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
as
A_{i,j}
,
B_{i,j}
,
C_{i,j}
and
D_{i,j}
as follows: if we let
\{E_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
be a standard basis of
\mathrm{Mat}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{i,j}=E_{p+1-i,p+1-j},&B_{i,j}=E_{p+1-i,p+j}\;\text{and}\\
C_{i,j}=E_{p+i,p+1-j},&D_{i,j}=E_{p+i,p+j}.\end{array}

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(p,q)
consists of matrices
X
in
\mathfrak{sl}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
for which

A
,
D
are skew-symmetric and
B^{t}=C
, so that
\dim\mathfrak{so}(p,q)=\frac{1}{2}(p(p-1)+q(q-1))+pq
.
There is a natural involution
\theta
in
\mathfrak{so}(p,q)
defined as the matrix transpose, leading the Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{so}(p,q)=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
, where
\mathfrak{k}
denotes the set of
X\in\mathfrak{so}(p,q)
for which
B
(and consequently
D
) is a zero matrix, and
\mathfrak{p}
represents the set of those
X
whose
A
- and
D
-components are zero matrices.
Then one can take a maximal abelian subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathfrak{p}
whose elements are of the form

(2.1)

F=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}&\hskip-5.78172pt\begin{array}[]{ccc}&&\\
&&a_{q}\\
&\reflectbox{$\ddots$}&\\
a_{1}&&\end{array}\\
\hline\cr\hskip-5.78172pt\begin{array}[]{cccc}&&&a_{1}\\
&&\reflectbox{$\ddots$}&\\
&a_{q}&&\end{array}&\end{array}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}a_{i}(B_{i,i}+C_{i,i}).

Here, withought loss of generality, we assume that
p\geq q
. Denote by

\{f_{i}:i=1,\ldots,q\}

the dual basis of
\mathfrak{a}
given by
f_{i}(B_{j,j}+C_{j,j})=\delta_{ij}
.
We say that a nonzero
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
is a restricted root for
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
if there is a nonzero
X\in\mathfrak{g}
such that for all

,

[F,X]=\lambda(F)X.

\mathfrak{sl}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})=\mathfrak{so}(p,q)\oplus\mathfrak{s}

&=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{q}a_{k}(B_{k,k}+C_{k,k})\right)\left(A_{q+\ell,i}-B_{q+\ell,i}+A_{i,q+\ell}+C_{i,q+\ell}\right)\\
&\hskip 57.81621pt-\left(A_{q+\ell,i}-B_{q+\ell,i}+A_{i,q+\ell}+C_{i,q+\ell}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{q}a_{k}(B_{k,k}+C_{k,k})\right)\\

&=a_{i}(E_{p+1-\ell^{\prime},p+1-i}-E_{p+1-\ell^{\prime},p+i}+E_{p+1-i,p+1-\ell^{\prime}}+E_{p+i,p+1-\ell^{\prime}})\\

The remaining cases can be verified in a similar manner. Given that the total number of linearly independent weight vectors is equal to
\dim\mathfrak{s}
, we can thus derive the weight decomposition of
\mathfrak{s}
.

4.
Proof of Main Theorem

Theorem
1.1
follows directly from the proposition below.

Proposition 4.1
.

The subspace
\mathfrak{s}
defined in (
3.1
) is
\mathfrak{so}(p,q)
-irreducible.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that any weight vectors of
\mathfrak{s}
provided in Proposition
3.1
is mapped to another weight vector by the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{so}(p,q)
. Let
[H(\lambda)]
and
[S(\omega)]
are the class of root vectors for
\lambda
and weight vectors for
\omega
, respectively, if the root or weight space is one-dimensional, and
H(\lambda)_{\ell}
and
S(\omega)_{\ell}
are as in Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
3.1
, respectively.
The following, for instance, demonstrates the transitivity of the set of all nonzero weights.

•

[S(2f_{1})]{\mathrel{\to}^{H(f_{1}\mp f_{j})}_{H(-f_{1}\mp f_{j})}}[S(f_{1}\pm f_{j})]{\mathrel{\to}^{H(f_{1}\pm f_{j})}_{H(-f_{1}\mp f_{j})}}[S(\pm 2f_{j})]
;

•

[S(\mp f_{i}\pm f_{j})]{\mathrel{\to}^{H(\mp f_{i}-f_{1})}_{H(\pm f_{j}+f_{1})}}[S(f_{1}\pm f_{j})]{\mathrel{\to}^{H(\mp f_{i}+f_{1})}_{H(\pm f_{i}-f_{1})}}[S(\pm f_{i}\pm f_{j})]{\mathrel{\to}^{H(f_{1}\pm f_{i})}_{H(-f_{1}\mp f_{i})}}[S(-f_{1}\pm f_{j})]
;

•

[S(\pm 2f_{i})]{\mathrel{\to}^{H(\pm f_{i})_{\ell}}_{H(\mp f_{i})_{\ell}}}[S(\pm f_{i})_{\ell}]

It can be verified that each mapping sends a nonzero weight vectors to another nonzero weight vector.
As for the connection to weight vectors of zero weight, we have the following observaton:

•

[S(-f_{1})_{j}]{\mathrel{\to}^{H(-f_{1})_{i}}_{H(f_{1})_{i}}}[A_{q+i,q+j}+A_{q+j,q+i}]

•

[S(-f_{1})_{i}]{\mathrel{\to}^{H(-f_{1})_{i}}_{H(f_{1})_{i}}}[2A_{q+i,q+i}+(A_{1,1}+D_{1,1})]

•

[S(-f_{i}-f_{i+1})]{\mathrel{\to}^{H(-f_{i}-f_{i+1})}_{H(f_{i}+f_{i+1})}}[A_{i,i}+D_{i,i}-(A_{i+1,i+1}+D_{i+1,i+1})]
.

∎

3.
Reduction to cohomology

We will begin by explaining how to reduce the computation of
\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{D^{\times}}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})
to extensions between characters, after which we can reduce that problem to computation of certain cohomology groups. There are two main facts used in this reduction: first, that
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are induced from characters. Secondly, the inductions are from
finite index
subgroups, so Frobenius reciprocity is a two-sided adjunction.

Lemma
5
.

For a locally profinite group
G
, let
H\leq G
be a closed subgroup such that
[G:H]<\infty
, and let
V
and
W
be smooth representations of the groups

G

\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{G}(\Ind_{H}^{G}V,W)\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{H}(V,\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}W).

This also holds in the other direction.

Proof.

This is a well-known fact from category theory: Frobenius reciprocity carries on to the derived functors of
\mathrm{Hom}
because we have an adjunction both ways, since for finite index subgroups compact induction agrees with induction.
∎

Now we can use this to reduce our problem to computing extensions of characters. Consider irreducible representations

.

We now pull out the direct sum and apply Frobenius reciprocity on the other side. We then obtain

\bigoplus_{\overline{s}\in D_{a^{\prime}}^{\times}\setminus D^{\times}/D_{a}^{\times}}\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{D_{a^{\prime}}^{\times}}(\Ind_{D_{\mathrm{lcm}(a,a^{\prime})}^{\times}}^{D_{a^{\prime}}^{\times}}\chi^{s},\chi^{\prime})\simeq\bigoplus_{\overline{s}\in D_{a^{\prime}}^{\times}\setminus D^{\times}/D_{a}^{\times}}\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{D_{\mathrm{lcm}(a,a^{\prime})}^{\times}}(\chi^{s},\mathrm{Res}^{D_{a^{\prime}}^{\times}}_{D_{\mathrm{lcm}(a,a^{\prime})}^{\times}}\chi^{\prime}).

The result then follows after tensoring with
(\chi^{s})^{\ast}
on each individual extension group.
∎

Thus, it suffices to be able to compute
\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{D_{a}^{\times}}(\mathbf{1},\chi)
for any character
\chi
of
D_{a}^{\times}
.

We can now employ all the tools of group cohomology to solve our problem. It is tempting to try to use the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence and identify
\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{D_{a}^{\times}}(\mathbf{1},\chi)
with
\mathrm{H}^{n}(D_{a}^{\times},\chi)
, but this does not quite work. Since we must work with continuous cohomology and smooth representations, and
D_{a}^{\times}
is not a profinite group, we cannot use the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence in this case. However, we can recover a similar spectral sequence.

Proposition
7
.

There is a first quadrant spectral sequence

E_{2}^{i,j}=\mathrm{H}^{i}(D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1},\mathrm{H}^{j}(I_{1},\chi))\implies\mathrm{Ext}^{i+j}_{D_{a}^{\times}}(\mathbf{1},\chi).

Proof.

From equation (33) in §9 of
[
Paš10
]
, letting
\mathcal{H}=\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}[D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}]
be the Hecke algebra of
I_{1}
, we have a spectral sequence

E_{2}^{i,j}=\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{H}^{j}(I_{1},\chi))\implies\mathrm{Ext}^{i+j}_{D_{a}^{\times}}(\mathbf{1},\chi).

We may ignore the central character in our setting. The extension group is computed in the category of all
\mathcal{H}
-modules or equivalently
D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}
-representations, which allows us to identify
\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1},\mathrm{all}}(\mathbf{1},-)\simeq\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{all}}(D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1},-)
. Because the quotient
D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}\simeq\varpi_{D}^{a\mathbf{Z}}\ltimes k_{D}^{\times}
is a discrete group, it follows that this is the same as the continuous cohomology. We then obtain a spectral sequence

E_{2}^{i,j}=\mathrm{H}^{i}(D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1},\mathrm{H}^{j}(I_{1},\chi))\implies\mathrm{Ext}^{i+j}_{D_{a}^{\times}}(\mathbf{1},\chi)

as desired.
∎

As the spectral sequence of Proposition
7
is a first quadrant spectral sequence, we may obtain the following five-term exact sequence of low degree terms:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{1}(D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1},\chi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{D_{a}^{\times}}(\mathbf{1},\chi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathrm{Hom}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\otimes\chi)^{D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{2}(D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1},\chi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{D_{a}^{\times}}(\mathbf{1},\chi)}

Here, we have expanded the definition of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\chi)^{D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}}
. Thus, we obtain an exact sequence similar to the inflation-restriction exact sequence we would obtain via the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence if
D_{a}^{\times}
were a profinite group.

In Corollary
18
, we use the same spectral sequence to obtain a description of
\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{D_{a}^{\times}}(\mathbf{1},\chi)
in terms of the cohomology of
I_{1}
, so this step in our method works for all degrees of extensions.

4.
Cohomology of
I_{1}

Recall that we use
I_{1}
to denote the subgroup
1+\varpi_{D}\mathcal{O}_{D}
of
D^{\times}
. A key step in computing all extensions of irreducible representations of
D^{\times}
is to understand the space
\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\pi)
as a representation of
D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}
, where
\pi
is a smooth irreducible representation of
D_{a}^{\times}
. Note that at the end of the previous section we had reduced to the case where
\pi
was a character, but the computation is the same if
\pi
is an irreducible of any dimension which is what we will assume here.

Since the action of
I_{1}
is trivial on
\pi
, we have
\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\pi)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\otimes\pi
, where the action of
g\in D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}
on
\mathrm{Hom}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
sends a homomorphism
\varphi(x)
to
\varphi(g^{-1}xg)
and we view
\pi
as a representation of
D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}
. Note that we use the normality of
I_{1}
here - in general, this cohomology group is only a module over the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{I_{1}}
. Thus, we seek to understand the homomorphism space
\mathrm{Hom}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
.

Because the additive group
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
is abelian and every element is
p
-torsion, any homomorphism
\varphi:I_{1}\to\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
will factor through the following diagram:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\frac{I_{1}}{[I_{1},I_{1}]I_{1}^{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{\prime}}

The homomorphism
\varphi^{\prime}
is unique given
\varphi
. The closed subgroup
[I_{1},I_{1}]I_{1}^{p}\triangleleft I_{1}
generated by commutators and
p
th powers is called the Frattini subgroup of
I_{1}
.

Hence, we can reduce our problem of computing
\mathrm{Hom}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
to that of computing
\mathrm{Hom}(I_{1}/[I_{1},I_{1}]I_{1}^{p},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
. We do this by computing the Frattini subgroup itself.

4.1.
The Frattini subgroup

To compute
[I_{1},I_{1}]I_{1}^{p}
, we will begin with computing
I_{1}^{p}
. In the
p
-adic case, this has a simple description.

Proposition
8
.

If
F
is an extension of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
, then
I_{1}^{p}=1+\varpi_{D}^{de+1}\mathcal{O}_{D}
.

Proof.

First we show that
I_{1}^{p}\subset 1+\varpi_{D}^{de+1}\mathcal{O}_{D}
. Let
1+x\in I_{1}
for
x\in\varpi_{D}\mathcal{O}_{D}
. Then we have
(1+x)^{p}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq p}\binom{p}{i}x^{i}=1+px\sum_{1\leq i\leq p-1}\left(\binom{p}{i}/p\right)x^{i-1}+x^{p}
. Because
F
is a
p
-adic field, we have
\varpi_{D}^{de}=\pi_{F}^{e}=pu
for some unit
u\in\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
. Thus, because
x\in\varpi_{D}\mathcal{O}_{D}
,
px
is in
\varpi_{D}^{de+1}\mathcal{O}_{D}
. Moreover, because
p>de+1
, we have that
x^{p}
is in
\varpi_{D}^{de+1}\mathcal{O}_{D}
, so
(1+x)^{p}
is in
1+\varpi_{D}^{de+1}\mathcal{O}_{D}
as desired.

Now we show that
I_{1}^{p}\supset 1+\varpi_{D}^{de+1}\mathcal{O}_{D}
. Let
1+y\in 1+\varpi_{D}^{de+1}\mathcal{O}_{D}
for some
y\in\varpi_{D}^{de+1}\mathcal{O}_{D}
. As a formal power series, we know that

\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{1/p}{n}Y^{n}\right)^{p}=1+Y,

so to prove that
1+y\in I_{1}^{p}
it suffices to show that
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{1/p}{n}y^{n}
converges in
I_{1}
.

Because the
n=0
term in this series is
1
and
\varpi_{D}\mathcal{O}_{D}
is closed in
D
, it suffices to show that
\nu_{D}\left(\binom{1/p}{n}y^{n}\right)>0
for all
n>0
and that
\nu_{D}\left(\binom{1/p}{n}y^{n}\right)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. We have the identity

\binom{1/p}{n}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\prod_{0\leq i\leq n-1}\left(\frac{ip-1}{p}\right).

Thus, because
\nu_{D}=\nu_{F}=e\nu_{p}
on
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
(where
\nu_{p}
is the
p
-adic valuation), we compute
\nu_{D}\left(\binom{1/p}{n}y^{n}\right)=\nu_{D}(y^{n})-(e\nu_{p}(n!)+e\nu_{p}(p^{n}))=n\nu_{D}(y)-e(\nu_{p}(n!)+n)
. We also have
\nu_{D}(\varpi_{D})=\frac{1}{d}
, so because
y\in\varpi_{D}^{de+1}\mathcal{O}_{D}
, we have
\nu_{D}(y)\geq\nu_{D}(\varpi_{D}^{de+1})=e\left(1+\frac{1}{de}\right)
. By Legendre’s formula,
\nu_{p}(n!)
is bounded above by
\frac{n}{p-1}
. Therefore,

\nu_{D}\left(\binom{1/p}{n}y^{n}\right)\geq ne\left(1+\frac{1}{de}\right)-ne\left(\frac{1}{p-1}+1\right),

and because
p-1>de
this will be greater than
0
for
n>0
and approach
\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

When
F
is an extension of
\mathbf{F}_{p}\left(\mkern-3.0mu\left(t\right)\mkern-3.0mu\right)
, we work in characteristic
p
, so we have
(1+x)^{p}=1+x^{p}
. This implies that

I_{1}^{p}=1+(\varpi_{D}\mathcal{O}_{D})^{p}.

We now turn to computing
[I_{1},I_{1}]
. The following decomposition will be useful here, as well as later on.

Lemma
9
.

Suppose
p>de+1
if
F/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
or
\gcd(d,p)=1
in the local function field case. We have a decomposition

I_{1}=(1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F})\times I_{1,\Nrd=1},

where
I_{1,\Nrd=1}
is the kernel of the reduced norm restricted to
I_{1}
.

Proof.

Because there is no
p
-torsion,
1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F}
is a direct product of copies of
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
as a multiplicative group. It follows that every element has a unique
d
th root, which means that
\Nrd|_{1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F}}:x\mapsto x^{d}
is surjective. Any element
x\in I_{1}
can then be divided by an element of
1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F}
to land in
I_{1,\Nrd=1}
, which shows
I_{1}=(1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F})I_{1,\Nrd=1}
. These subgroups have trivial intersection, since the only
d
th root of
1
in
1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F}
is
1
. Finally, both of these subgroups are normal because
1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F}
is central, so we have
I_{1}=(1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F})\times I_{1,\Nrd=1}
as desired.
∎

As
1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F}
is central in
I_{1}
, we then have
[I_{1},I_{1}]=[I_{1,\Nrd=1},I_{1,\Nrd=1}]
. We compute this using §1.4 of
[
PR94
]
. The following lemma is useful when considering the lowest nonzero coefficients in a commutator in
[I_{1,\Nrd=1},I_{1,\Nrd=1}\cap I_{i}]
. This will be used in the theorem that follows.

Lemma
10
.

Let
i\geq 0
, and for
y\in k_{D}
let
\varphi_{i,y}\in\mathrm{End}_{k_{F}}(k_{D})
denote the map

\varphi_{i,y}:x\mapsto\sigma(x)y-x\sigma^{i}(y).

The image of
\varphi_{i,y}
is the subspace

\ker(\Tr_{k_{D}/k_{F}})\cdot\prod_{0\leq j\leq i}\sigma^{j}(y),

which has codimension one for
y\in k_{D}^{\times}
.

Proof.

Since
\sigma
fixes
k_{F}
, the
k_{F}
-linearity of this map follows immediately. When
y=0
, the result is clear, so suppose now that
y\in k_{D}^{\times}
.

We first compute the kernel of this map. We have
\varphi_{i,y}(x)=0
if and only if
\sigma(x)y=x\sigma^{i}(y)
. For
x\neq 0
, this is equivalent to
\frac{x}{\sigma(x)}=\frac{y}{\sigma^{i}(y)}
, which always has a solution
x
by the multiplicative version of Hilbert’s theorem 90 as

\Nm_{k_{D}/k_{F}}(y/\sigma^{i}(y))=1

and
\sigma
is a generator of the Galois group. Again since
\sigma
is a generator, we have
k_{D}^{\langle\sigma\rangle}=k_{F}
, so the kernel is one-dimensional: supposing for nonzero
x^{\prime}
we have
\frac{x^{\prime}}{\sigma(x^{\prime})}=\frac{x}{\sigma(x)}
, then
\sigma(\frac{x^{\prime}}{x})=\frac{x^{\prime}}{x}
so the ratio lies in
k_{F}^{\times}
. Thus,
\im\varphi_{i,y}
is a codimension one subspace. Noting that

\frac{\varphi_{i,y}(x)}{\prod_{0\leq j\leq i}\sigma^{j}(y)}=\frac{\sigma(x)}{\prod_{0<j\leq i}\sigma^{j}(y)}-\frac{x}{\prod_{0\leq j<i}\sigma^{j}(y)},

applying
\sigma
to the second term on the right hand side yields the first term, so the right hand side is contained in
\ker(\Tr_{k_{D}/k_{F}})
and

\im\varphi_{i,y}\subset\ker(\Tr_{k_{D}/k_{F}})\cdot\prod_{0\leq j\leq i}\sigma^{j}(y).

The additive version of Hilbert’s theorem 90 says
\ker\Tr_{k_{D}/k_{F}}=\im(\sigma(x)-x)=\im\varphi_{i,1}
, which was shown to also have codimension one. Hence, we have equality.
∎

Theorem
11
.

We have

[I_{1},I_{1}]I_{1}^{p}=I_{1,\Nrd=1}I_{1}^{p}\cap I_{2}.

When
F/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
, we may describe
I_{1}^{p}
as in Proposition
8
, and when
F/\mathbf{F}_{p}\left(\mkern-3.0mu\left(t\right)\mkern-3.0mu\right)
we have
I_{1}^{p}=1+(\varpi_{D}\mathcal{O}_{D})^{p}
. Here, we make the same assumptions on
p
as Lemma
9
.

Proof.

There is a filtration
I_{i,\Nrd=1}:=I_{1,\Nrd=1}\cap I_{i}
. As previously discussed, by Lemma
9
we have

[I_{1},I_{1}]=[I_{1,\Nrd=1},I_{1,\Nrd=1}].

By Theorem 1.9 in
[
PR94
]
, for
d>2
this commutator subgroup equals
I_{2,\Nrd=1}
. This can be extended to the case of
d=2
here because of the restrictions on
p
. Because
I_{1}^{p}\subset I_{2}
, the claim of the theorem follows immediately.

The argument in
[
PR94
]
is short, so we summarize it here. We show first that under the quotient map
q_{i}:I_{i}\to I_{i}/I_{i+1}\simeq k_{D}
we have
q_{i+1}([I_{1,\Nrd=1},I_{i,\Nrd=1}])=q_{i+1}(I_{i+1,\Nrd=1})
. Indeed, a computation shows that the image
q_{i+1}([I_{1,\Nrd=1},I_{i,\Nrd=1}])
contains the elements
\varphi_{i,y}(x)
where
y\in k_{D}
and
x\in q_{i}(I_{i,\Nrd=1})
, where we have
q_{i}(I_{i,\Nrd=1})=k_{D}
if
d\nmid i
and
q_{i}(I_{i,\Nrd=1})=\ker(\Tr_{k_{D}/k_{F}})
if
d|i
. Then Lemma
10
can be used to show that these generate all of
q_{i+1}(I_{i+1,\Nrd=1})
in either case: when
d|(i+1)
, we have
\im(\varphi_{i,y})=\ker(\Tr_{k_{D}/k_{F}})
for any
y
, so we indeed get all of
q_{i+1}(I_{i+1,\Nrd=1})
; when
d\nmid(i+1)
, we can show that we generate all of
q_{i+1}(I_{i+1,\Nrd=1})=k_{D}
by first finding
y,y^{\prime}\in k_{D}
so that
\im(\varphi_{i,y})\neq\im(\varphi_{i,y^{\prime}})
. Choosing
y=1
and
y^{\prime}
such that
\prod_{0\leq j\leq i}\sigma^{j}(y^{\prime})
does not lie in
k_{F}
, we can always do this. If, in addition,
d|i
, the values
x
are restricted to
\ker(\Tr_{k_{D}/k_{F}})
so we need to justify why this still suffices. The image of
\varphi_{i,y}|_{\ker(\Tr_{k_{D}/k_{F}})}
still has codimension one since
k_{F}=\ker(\varphi_{i,y})
has trivial intersection with
\ker(\Tr_{k_{D}/k_{F}})
(because
p
and
d
are coprime) and so restricted to
\ker(\Tr_{k_{D}/k_{F}})
the map
\varphi_{i,y}
is an isomorphism. Thus, we have
q_{i+1}([I_{1,\Nrd=1},I_{i,\Nrd=1}])=q_{i+1}(I_{i+1,\Nrd=1})
.

It then follows that

[I_{1,\Nrd=1},I_{i,\Nrd=1}]I_{i+2,\Nrd=1}=I_{i+1,\Nrd=1}.

Now
[I_{1,\Nrd=1},I_{1,\Nrd=1}]
is a non-central normal subgroup of
D^{\times}_{\Nrd=1}
, and so by Theorem 3.3 in
[
PR94
]
it is open and contains
I_{j,\Nrd=1}
for some
j
. Supposing that the minimal such
j
satisfies

, then we have

[I_{1,\Nrd=1},I_{1,\Nrd=1}]\supset[I_{1,\Nrd=1},I_{j-2,\Nrd=1}]I_{j,\Nrd=1}=I_{j-1,\Nrd=1},

which contradicts the fact that
j
is minimal. Thus, we must have

. But we also have
[I_{1,\Nrd=1},I_{1,\Nrd=1}]\subset I_{2,\Nrd=1}
, so we have equality.
∎

Remark
.

In the case of

, as shown in
[

, §5, the same result about the commutator subgroup of

being

will still hold so long as

in any case, so this assumption is important to include.

4.2.
A commutator construction

While not needed for computing
\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\pi)
, we can actually show that nearly all elements of
[I_{1},I_{1}]I_{1}^{p}
are products of a single commutator and a
p
th power, rather than products of many commutators and a
p
th power as done above. Namely, every element of
[I_{1},I_{1}]I_{1}^{p}
that does not lie in
I_{3}
is a product of a commutator and
p
th power when
d>4
.

We first study
q_{2}([I_{1},I_{1}])
in detail in Proposition
12
to determine the exact number of ways a given value in
k_{D}
can be produced. We use this in the corollary that follows to show there are enough ways to do this that we can inductively choose
x,y\in I_{1}
so that
[x,y]
produces the desired commutator by studying the coefficient of
\varpi_{D}^{i}
in the expansion of
[x,y]
using Tiechmuller representatives.

Proposition
12
.

Suppose
d>4
and fix
\alpha\in k_{D}^{\times}
. There exist
1+[x]\varpi_{D}\neq 1
and
1+[y]\varpi_{D}\neq 1
such that

q_{2}([1+[x]\varpi_{D},1+[y]\varpi_{D}])=\alpha,

and
x/y
does not lie in any proper subfield of
k_{D}
.

Proof.

If we show that there are more than
\frac{q^{d/2+1}-1}{q-1}
ratios
x/y
achieve any given value of
\alpha
, then the claim follows. This is because
\frac{q^{d/2+1}-1}{q-1}\geq\sum_{a\leq d/2}q^{a}\geq|\bigcup_{a|d,a<d}\mathbf{F}_{q^{a}}|
, the number of elements in the union of all proper subfields of
k_{D}
. We first show that the number of ratios can be deduced from the number of pairs
(x,y)
.

To have
q_{2}(1+[x]\varpi_{D},1+[y]\varpi_{D}])=\alpha
requires
x\sigma(y)-\sigma(x)y=\alpha
. Viewed as a curve
C_{\alpha}
over
k_{D}
, this has many symmetries. The
(q+1)
th roots of unity
\langle\zeta\rangle=\mu_{q+1}(k_{D})
act on
C_{\alpha}
. In particular, given
(x,y)\in C_{\alpha}(k_{D})
we have
(\zeta x,\zeta y)
as another solution. If we fix the ratio
x/y
, then the system

x\sigma(y)-\sigma(x)y=\alpha,x/y=\beta

gives
x=y\beta
, so we solve
y\beta\sigma(y)-\sigma(y)\sigma(\beta)y=\alpha
. Note that
\beta-\sigma(\beta)\neq 0
as
\alpha\neq 0
, so if solutions exist there are
|\mu_{q+1}(k_{D})|
of them. Thus, over nonzero values
\alpha
the number of ratios
x/y
from
(x,y)\in C_{\alpha}(k_{D})
is precisely
|C_{\alpha}(k_{D})|/|\mu_{q+1}(k_{D})|
, where
|C_{\alpha}(k_{D})|
denotes the number of points over
k_{D}
.

Thus, we now want to show
|C_{\alpha}(k_{D})|
exceeds
|\mu_{q+1}(k_{D})|\frac{q^{d/2+1}-1}{q-1}
. If we are given
x,y\in k_{D}^{\times}
, then
(x,y)
lies on some
C_{\alpha}(k_{D})
for
\alpha\in k_{D}
. Via Lemma
10
, we may show exactly
|k_{D}^{\times}|\cdot|k_{F}^{\times}|
of these yield
\alpha=0
. Then we conclude

\sum_{\alpha\in k_{D}^{\times}}|C_{\alpha}(k_{D})|=(|k_{D}^{\times}|)^{2}-|k_{D}^{\times}|\cdot|k_{F}^{\times}|.

There are two relevant actions on the family of curves
C_{\alpha}
:

·

For
z\in k_{D}^{\times}
, if
(x,y)\in C_{\alpha}(k_{D})
then
(zx,zy)\in C_{z\sigma(z)\alpha}(k_{D})
.

·

For
A\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(k_{F})
, if
(x,y)\in C_{\alpha}(k_{D})
then
A\cdot(x,y)
lies in
C_{\det A\cdot\alpha}(k_{D})
.

Let
d
be odd. The first point shows the cosets
k_{D}^{\times}/k_{D}^{\times(q+1)}
take on common values as the image of
z\sigma(z)
is identical to
z^{q+1}
. As
d
is odd, the image of
z^{q+1}
consists of all the squares. The second item shows that these two common values are the same. Thus,
|C_{\alpha}(k_{D})|=|k_{D}|-|k_{F}|=q^{d}-q
when
\alpha\in k_{D}^{\times}
because we have produced bijections between
C_{\alpha}(k_{D})
over all
\alpha\in k_{D}^{\times}
.

Now let
d
be even, and let
\alpha\in k_{D}
. We claim the curve
C_{\alpha}:x\sigma(y)-\sigma(x)y=\alpha
has
k_{D}
points in bijection with
X_{\alpha}:\sigma(x)x+\sigma(y)y=\zeta\alpha
. Here, we choose
\zeta\neq 0
so that
\sigma(\zeta)=-\zeta
.

Explicitly, given
(x,y)\in X_{\alpha}
we first choose
\omega
such that
\sigma(\omega)\omega=-1
. Then
(x+\omega y,\omega x+y)
is a solution to
x\sigma(y)+\sigma(x)y=(\sigma(\omega)+\omega)\zeta\alpha
. We have
(\sigma(\omega)+\omega)\in k_{F}^{\times}
. Dividing the
x
coordinate by
\zeta
, we get a solution to
x\sigma(y)-\sigma(x)y=(\sigma(\omega)+\omega)\alpha
. Using the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(k_{F})
action, the solutions are in bijection.

Finally, we observe that points in
X_{\alpha}(k_{D})
are in bijection with solutions to
x^{q+1}+y^{q+1}=\zeta\alpha
, simply because
x^{q+1}
and
\sigma(x)x
take on the same values and have equal numbers of preimages for each value. The explicit number of solutions to this general type of equation is computed in
[
Wei49
]
, and will satisfy the desired bound so long as
d>4
. In particular, the values
|C_{\alpha}(k_{D})|
take on constant values for each coset of
k_{D}^{\times}/k_{D}^{\times(q+1)}
, with one coset for
\zeta\alpha=-1
taking the value
q^{d}+1+(-q)^{d/2}\frac{q-q^{3}}{q+1}-(q+1)
and the others a common value so that the average is
q^{d}-q
. The coset for
\zeta\alpha=-1
counts points on
x^{q+1}+y^{q+1}+1=0
, which when projectivized (to add
q+1
points) is the Fermat curve
x^{q+1}+y^{q+1}+z^{q+1}=0
. The zeta function of this curve is well-known over
\mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}
- see
[
SK79
]
. That the remaining cosets take the same value can be seen from the explicit formula of
[
Wei49
]
. Thus, we have shown the desired bound on
|C_{\alpha}(k_{D})|
and the proposition follows.
∎

The following result is analogous to the result at the end of §1.4.3 of
[
PR94
]
, which states that in
[D^{\times},D^{\times}]
every element is a product of at most two commutators (this was originally shown in
[
Wan50
]
).

Corollary
13
.

Suppose
d>4
. Any element
\alpha\in[I_{1},I_{1}]I_{1}^{p}\setminus I_{3}
can be constructed as a product of a commutator and a
p
th power.

Proof.

We wish to write
\alpha=[x,y]z^{p}
for
x,y,z\in I_{1}
. Suppose we have
x=1+\sum_{i\geq 1}[x_{i}]\varpi_{D}^{i}
, and similarly for
y
. We fix
x_{1}
and
y_{1}
to be as in Proposition
12
, so
q_{2}([x,y])=q_{2}(\alpha)\in k_{D}^{\times}
and
x_{1}/y_{1}
does not lie in a proper subfield of
k_{D}
. Let
i>1
and assume
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}
and
y_{1},\ldots,y_{i-1}
are fixed. A calculation shows that the
\varpi_{D}^{i+1}
coefficient of
[x,y]
is a Tiechmuller lift of

C+\varphi_{i,x_{1}}(y_{i})+\varphi_{i,y_{1}}(x_{i}).

Here,
C\in k_{D}
is a constant only depending on
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}
and
y_{1},\ldots,y_{i-1}
. To do this calculation, we recall that we have
[x]+[y]=[x+y]+O(p)
. If
F/\mathbf{F}_{p}\left(\mkern-3.0mu\left(t\right)\mkern-3.0mu\right)
, this error is zero, while in the
p
-adic case by Proposition
8
we can absorb it into the
I_{1}^{p}
component
z^{p}
. We use this expression to construct commutators inductively.

By Lemma
10
, we know
\{C+\varphi_{i,x_{1}}(y_{i})+\varphi_{i,y_{1}}(x_{i}):x_{i},y_{i}\in k_{D}\}\subset k_{D}
is a sum of two codimension one subspaces, shifted by

. This is all of
k_{D}
if and only if the two subspaces are distinct, which will depend on the choice of
x_{1}
and
y_{1}
. By Lemma

,
\im(\varphi_{i,x_{1}})=\im(\varphi_{i,y_{1}})
if and only if

\prod_{0\leq j\leq i}\sigma^{j}(x_{1}/y_{1})\in k_{F},

since

if and only if

due to the non-degeneracy of the trace pairing. A calculation shows that this condition on

holds if and only if
x_{1}/y_{1}
is in the degree

extension of
k_{F}
. Because
x_{1}/y_{1}
does not lie in a proper subfield of
k_{D}
, we may obtain any Tiechmuller lift of
k_{D}
as the coefficient of
\varpi_{D}^{i+1}
of

when

. When

, the values obtained for this coefficient are in the coset
C+\im(\varphi_{i,x_{1}})=C+\im(\varphi_{i,y_{1}})
, which has codimension one in
k_{D}
.

Using Lemma
9
, we can count the number of elements of
I_{1,\Nrd=1}
modulo
I_{i}
for any
i
and use this to compute the number of elements of
[I_{1},I_{1}]I_{1}^{p}\setminus I_{3}=I_{1,\Nrd=1}I_{1}^{p}\cap(I_{2}\setminus I_{3})
modulo
I_{i}
. Doing this gives exactly the number of elements of
[I_{1},I_{1}]I_{1}^{p}
modulo
I_{i}
that we can produce by multiplying a commutator and a

th power as above, thus proving the claim.
∎

Following through the same arguments by inspecting the values from Proposition
12
and comparing to
|\mu_{q+1}(k_{D})|\frac{q^{d/2+1}-1}{q-1}
, we can get results for

as well. For

we can get the same final result, but for

Proposition

need not exceed the bound
|\mu_{q+1}(k_{D})|\frac{q^{d/2+1}-1}{q-1}
. However, it does hold for
\frac{q}{q+1}|k_{D}^{\times}|
values in

.

4.3.
Computing cohomology

With our understanding of the Frattini subgroup, we can now compute the structure of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\pi)
as a representation of
D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}
. For most of this section, we focus on computing the
D^{\times}/I_{1}
-representation structure where
\pi
is a smooth irreducible representation of
D^{\times}
because, as shown after the proof of Proposition
16
, we can obtain the
D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}
-representation structure from this simply by restricting.

First we review why we have a
D^{\times}/I_{1}
representation structure on
\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\pi)
for an irreducible smooth representation
\pi
. Being the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup of
D^{\times}
, there is the structure of a module over the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{I_{1}}\simeq\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}[I_{1}\setminus D^{\times}/I_{1}]
on
\pi^{I_{1}}
. Since
I_{1}
is normal, this has the structure of a representation of
D^{\times}/I_{1}
. The derived functors of this are the continuous cohomology groups
\mathrm{H}^{i}(I_{1},\pi)
, hence there is a structure of a representation of
D^{\times}/I_{1}
on
\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\pi)
. As
\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\pi)\simeq\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\otimes\pi
, we will only need to compute this structure for the trivial representation. For
\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
, the structure as a representation can be described very explicitly as the conjugation action of
D^{\times}/I_{1}
on homomorphisms. Namely,
g\in D^{\times}/I_{1}
sends a homomorphism
\varphi(x)
to
\varphi(g^{-1}xg)
.

We now compute the homomorphisms in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
using Theorem
11
.

Theorem
14
.

We have a decomposition

\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\simeq\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1,\Nrd=1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\oplus\mathrm{H}^{1}(1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}),

where
\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1,\Nrd=1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(k_{D},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\simeq\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}^{df}
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}(1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
is isomorphic to
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}^{ef}
in the
p
-adic case and
\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbf{N}}\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
in the local function field case.

Proof.

Due to Lemma
9
, the decomposition into these cohomology groups follows immediately. This is for the first cohomology group so we do not need more advanced methods, but it is worth noting this is a subcase of the Künneth theorem for profinite groups.

By the proof of Theorem
11
, we know that the Frattini subgroup of
I_{1,\Nrd=1}
is
I_{1,\Nrd=1}\cap I_{2}=I_{2,\Nrd=1}
. We then have

\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1,\Nrd=1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(I_{1,\Nrd=1}/I_{2,\Nrd=1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}).

The quotient
I_{1,\Nrd=1}/I_{2,\Nrd=1}
is isomorphic to
k_{D}
: we know it is a finite dimensional
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-vector space since it is finite, abelian, and
p
-torsion. In the decomposition of Lemma
9
, upon taking the elements
1+[x]\varpi_{D}
over
x\in k_{D}
, the
I_{1,\Nrd=1}
components yield coset representatives of
I_{1,\Nrd=1}/I_{2,\Nrd=1}\subset k_{D}
for any element of
k_{D}
. This is because
d\geq 2
, so division by an element of
1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F}
cannot affect the coefficient of
\varpi_{D}
. Thus, we have

\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1,\Nrd=1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(k_{D},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}),

where this last space is isomorphic to
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}^{df}
because the additive group of
k_{D}
is
\mathbf{F}_{p}^{df}
. Now we turn to
1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F}
. In the
p
-adic case, the assumption
p>de+1
ensures this group has no
p
-torsion, while in the local function field case this fact is a given. This implies that as a topological group we have
1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F}
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{[F:\mathbf{Q}_{p}]}
in the
p
-adic case, and
\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\mathbf{N}}
in the local function field case. As
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbf{Z}_{p},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\simeq\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
, in the
p
-adic case the claim follows. In the local function field case

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\mathbf{N}},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbf{N}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbf{Z}_{p},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}),

which is what we wanted.
∎

∎

We may now compute the structure of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
as a representation of
D^{\times}/I_{1}
.

Proposition
16
.

As a representation of
D^{\times}/I_{1}
via the conjugation action, we have

\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\simeq\mathrm{H}^{1}(1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\oplus\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbf{Z}/f\mathbf{Z}}\Ind_{D_{d}^{\times}/I_{1}}^{D^{\times}/I_{1}}\chi_{\eta_{i}}

where the action on
\mathrm{H}^{1}(1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
is trivial and
\chi_{\eta_{i}}
is extended trivially from the order
d
character
k_{D}^{\times}\to\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}^{\times}
given by
x\mapsto\left(\frac{\sigma(x)}{x}\right)^{p^{i}}
. Up to isomorphism, the choice of a coset representative of
i
does not matter.

Proof.

By Corollary
15
, we see that every map in the component
\mathrm{H}^{1}(1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\subset\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
factors through
\Nrd
. As a result, the conjugation action of
D^{\times}/I_{1}
is trivial on this component and it is a direct sum of copies of the trivial representation.

We now turn to the action on
\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1,\Nrd=1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
. To do this, we will use the basis
\phi^{\eta_{i}}
provided by Corollary
15
. As
D^{\times}/I_{1}\simeq\varpi_{D}^{\mathbf{Z}}\ltimes k_{D}^{\times}
, it suffices to study the
k_{D}^{\times}
and
\varpi_{D}^{\mathbf{Z}}
actions. Supposing
\varpi_{D}[x]\varpi_{D}^{-1}=[\sigma(x)]
and
\sigma
sends
x\mapsto x^{q^{r}}
, the conjugation action of
\varpi_{D}
is by

\varpi_{D}\cdot\phi^{\eta_{i}}=\phi^{\eta_{i-rf}}.

Similarly,
[x]\cdot\phi^{\eta_{i}}=\left(\frac{\sigma(x)}{x}\right)^{p^{i}}\phi^{\eta_{i}}
for all
x\in k_{D}^{\times}
. Defining
V_{i}
to be the span of the
\phi^{\eta_{i^{\prime}}}
where
i^{\prime}\equiv i\pmod{f}
, each is a subrepresentation so we obtain

\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1,\Nrd=1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbf{Z}/f\mathbf{Z}}V_{i}.

Each
V_{i}
is a dimension
d
representation. We have
\mathrm{Res}^{D^{\times}/I_{1}}_{D_{d}^{\times}/I_{1}}V_{i}\simeq\bigoplus_{i^{\prime}\equiv i\pmod{f}}\chi_{\eta_{i^{\prime}}}
where we define
\chi_{\eta_{i^{\prime}}}
for

as sending
x\in k_{D}^{\times}
to
\left(\frac{\sigma(x)}{x}\right)^{p^{i^{\prime}}}
and extend trivially to
D_{d}^{\times}/I_{1}
. It follows that

\mathrm{Hom}_{D_{d}^{\times}/I_{1}}(\chi_{\eta_{i}},\mathrm{Res}^{D^{\times}/I_{1}}_{D_{d}^{\times}/I_{1}}V_{i})\neq 0,

which by Frobenius reciprocity means
\mathrm{Hom}_{D^{\times}/I_{1}}(\Ind_{D_{d}^{\times}/I_{1}}^{D^{\times}/I_{1}}\chi_{\eta_{i}},V_{i})\neq 0
. However, by the classification of irreducible representations, the induced representation is irreducible of dimension
d
. As
\dim V_{i}=d
, it follows that
V_{i}
is isomorphic to
\Ind_{D_{d}^{\times}/I_{1}}^{D^{\times}/I_{1}}\chi_{\eta_{i}}
.

∎

For any smooth irreducible representation
\pi
of
D^{\times}
, this immediately gives us

\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\pi)\simeq(\mathrm{H}^{1}(1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\otimes\pi)\oplus\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbf{Z}/f\mathbf{Z}}\Ind_{D_{d}^{\times}/I_{1}}^{D^{\times}/I_{1}}(\chi_{\eta_{i}}\otimes\mathrm{Res}_{D_{d}^{\times}/I_{1}}^{D^{\times}/I_{1}}\pi).

We have already determined how to compute the restriction of a character of
D^{\times}
in general in Corollary

, so when
\pi
is a character it is easy to write this down explicitly.

We can now derive the
D_{a}^{\times}
-representation structure for other values of

. For the trivial representation, this is

\mathrm{Res}^{D^{\times}/I_{1}}_{D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\simeq\mathrm{H}^{1}(1+\pi_{F}\mathcal{O}_{F},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\oplus\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbf{Z}/f\mathbf{Z}}\mathrm{Res}^{D^{\times}/I_{1}}_{D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}}\Ind_{D_{d}^{\times}/I_{1}}^{D^{\times}/I_{1}}\chi_{\eta_{i}}.

The Mackey formula decomposes each term in the direct sum as

\mathrm{Res}^{D^{\times}/I_{1}}_{D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}}\Ind_{D_{d}^{\times}/I_{1}}^{D^{\times}/I_{1}}\chi_{\eta_{i}}\simeq\bigoplus_{\overline{s}\in D_{a}^{\times}\setminus D^{\times}/D_{d}^{\times}}\Ind_{D_{d}^{\times}/I_{1}}^{D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}}\chi_{\eta_{i}}^{s}.

We can similarly compute the tensor product with a smooth irreducible representation
\pi
of
D_{a}^{\times}
.

Remark
.

In
[
Koz18
]
, for a split reductive

and for a pro-

of

, the group

is computed as a module over the Hecke algebra

. Our result is somewhat analogous to Theorem 6.4 in that paper in how

splits into two components, but the fact that

is anisotropic modulo its center makes its structure far more interesting than that of
\mathrm{GL}_{1}(F)

similarly does not involve supersingular modules, but computing the same cohomology group for

using Theorem

.

Having computed the

Proof.

Theorem

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{1}(D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1},\chi)}

When we tensor with

Example
21
.

Let
D
be a quaternion algebra over a
p
-adic field

5.2.
Higher degree extensions in the
p
-adic case

In
[
Laz65
]
, many fundamental results about
p
-adic analytic pro-
p
groups are shown which will be helpful in understanding the structure of the higher cohomology groups in the case where
F
is a
p
-adic field. Here, by a
p
-adic analytic group we mean a topological group with the structure of a
p
-adic analytic manifold over
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
such that the group multiplication and inversion operations are analytic.

Theorem

(
[
Laz65
]
§2.5.8)
.

Let
G
be a
p
-adic analytic pro-
p
group and with no
p
-torsion, and let
r=\dim_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}G
. Then
G
is a Poincaré duality group of dimension
r
over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
. That is,

·

\mathrm{H}^{n}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})
is finite dimensional for all
n\in\mathbf{N}
.

·

\dim_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{r}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})=1
.

·

The cup product

\mathrm{H}^{n}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})\times\mathrm{H}^{r-n}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})\to\mathrm{H}^{r}(G,\mathbf{F}_{p})

is a non-degenerate bilinear form.

Here, as always, all cohomology taken is continuous.

We will be interested in the case of
I_{1}=G
, and we will also want to understand how the cup product interacts with the
D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}
-representation structure on the cohomology group. We note that
I_{1}
is a
p
-adic analytic group as it is an open subgroup of
D^{\times}
, and since
p>de+1
it has no
p
-torsion. Thus,
I_{1}
is a Poincaré duality group.

The most difficult part of the following result is that the
D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}
-representation structure on the top cohomology is trivial. This is the main result of
[
KS21
]
for general connected reductive groups. Using the method of Theorem 7.2 in
[
Koz18
]
, we can alternatively show this by finding the uniform pro-
p
subgroup
I_{de+1}
and computing explicitly the action on its first cohomology, which determines the action on its top cohomology as the cohomology ring is an exterior algebra. We can then show that this action is trivial, and that it must agree with the action on the top cohomology of
I_{1}
using the corestriction map.

Proposition
22

(
[
KS21
]
)
.

Let
r=d^{2}ef
. As
D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}
-representations, we have that
\mathrm{H}^{n}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})^{\ast}\simeq\mathrm{H}^{r-n}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
. Here,

denotes the dual representation.

Proof.

As representations of
D^{\times}/I_{1}
are equivalent to modules over
\mathcal{H}_{I_{1}}
and
\dim_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}I_{1}=d^{2}ef
, the structure on
\mathrm{H}^{r}(I_{1},\mathbf{F}_{p})
as a

-representation, and hence as a

-representation, is trivial by
[

]
. The cup product

is a non-degenerate bilinear form, which behaves well with respect to the

-action. Let
d
be an element of
D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}
. Then we have for
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{n}(I_{1},\mathbf{F}_{p})
and
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{r-n}(I_{1},\mathbf{F}_{p})
that

It follows from the triviality of the top cohomology that Poincaré duality yields an isomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{n}(I_{1},\mathbf{F}_{p})^{\ast}\simeq\mathrm{H}^{r-n}(I_{1},\mathbf{F}_{p})
. Tensoring with
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
gives the desired isomorphism.
∎

We will now again use the spectral sequence

from Proposition

. The work has already been done in Corollary

and the previous proposition.

Lemma

.

Let

and let

be a smooth character of

This follows from Corollary

∎

For

, which is trivial unless

We can make use of this lemma when

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{1}(D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1},\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\chi)^{\ast})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Ext}_{D_{a}^{\times}}^{r}(\mathbf{1},\chi^{\ast})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\chi^{\ast})^{D_{a}^{\times}/I_{1}}}

We may determine the first group via Proposition

- the dimension is the multiplicity of the trivial representation. This is precisely what was determined in Theorem

.

In the case of

and

, this is enough to determine all extension groups because

. For a smooth character

of

, we can compute all
\mathrm{H}^{i}(I_{1},\chi)
to do this. It is worth noting that this cohomology computation is known and of independent interest in stable homotopy theory, for example see
[

]
Proposition 7. The group

appears as the Morava stabilizer group attached to a formal group law, and
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(I_{1},\mathbf{F}_{p})
controls certain localization functors

in Morava

We have
\mathrm{H}^{0}(I_{1},\chi)\simeq\chi
, so
\mathrm{H}^{4}(I_{1},\chi)\simeq\chi
. As for
\mathrm{H}^{1}
, it becomes

\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\chi)\simeq(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}\oplus\Ind_{D_{2}^{\times}}^{D^{\times}}\chi_{\eta})\otimes\chi,

where
\chi_{\eta}
is extended from the character of
\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}
sending

. The representation
\mathrm{H}^{3}(I_{1},\chi)
is the dual of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})

, so it is given by

\mathrm{H}^{3}(I_{1},\chi)\simeq(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}\oplus\Ind_{D_{2}^{\times}}^{D^{\times}}\chi_{\eta}^{\ast})\otimes\chi.

What remains is to compute
\mathrm{H}^{2}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})

I_{1}
will be

using the main result of
[

\mathrm{H}^{2}(I_{1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\simeq\bigoplus_{i+j=2}\mathrm{H}^{i}(1+p\mathbf{Z}_{p},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\otimes\mathrm{H}^{j}(I_{1,\Nrd=1},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}).

after noting that the elements in

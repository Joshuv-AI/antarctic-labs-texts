Logarithmic submajorization, uniform majorization and Hölder type inequalities for τ \tau -measurable operators The work of the third and fourth authors was supported by the Australian Research Council.
PG Dodds, TK Dodds, FA Sukochev, D.Zanin

Abstract
. We extend the notion of the determinant function Λ \Lambda , originally introduced by T.Fack for τ \tau -compact operators, to a natural algebra of τ \tau -measurable operators affiliated with a semifinite von Neumann algebra which coincides with that defined by Haagerup and Schultz in the finite case and on which the determinant function is shown to be submultiplicative. Application is given to Hölder type inequalities via general Araki-Lieb-Thirring inequalities due to Kosaki and Han and to a Weyl-type theorem for uniform majorization.

Primary 46L52; 47A63 Secondary 46E30,47A30

1.
Introduction

In their study of Brown measures of unbounded operators affiliated with a finite von Neumann algebra
({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
, Haagerup and Schultz extended the classical
Fuglede-Kadison determinant

\Delta(x)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\tau({\bf 1})}\log t\ d(\tau e^{|x|})(s)\right),\quad x\in{\mathcal{M}}

to a multiplicative functional
\Delta
on the subalgebra
{\mathcal{M}}^{\Delta}
consisting of those
\tau
-measurable operators
x
affiliated with
{\mathcal{M}}
for which

\tau(\log_{+}|x|)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\log_{+}td(\tau e^{|x|})(t)<\infty

Here
e^{|x|}(\cdot)=\chi_{(\cdot)}(|x|)
is the spectral measure of
|x|
and
\tau e^{|x|}(\cdot)=\tau(e^{|x|}(\cdot))
is the corresponding Borel measure supported on the spectrum
\sigma(|x|)
of
|x|
.

A principal aim of this paper is to extend the Haagerup-Schultz approach from the setting of finite von Neumann algebras to the semifinite case. Our approach is via the determinant function
\Lambda(\cdot)
given by setting

\Lambda(t;x)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\log\mu(s;x)\,ds\right),\quad t>0,\quad x\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau).

Here
\mu(\cdot;x)
is the generalised singular value function (see below)
and
L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
is the natural extension of the algebra
{\mathcal{M}}^{\Delta}
of Haagerup-Schults to the general semifinite setting. The determinant function
\Lambda
goes back to A.Grothendieck
[
20
]
and was studied by Fack
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
in the case of
\tau
-compact operators and subsequently by Fack and Kosaki
[
17
]

for operators satisfying a ”Lorentz space”-type condition(see the definition of this condition in the domments following Proposition 3.1 below).

Basic properties of the class
L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
are studied in the third section where it is noted that this class is an algebra properly containing the space of
\tau
-compact operators as well as those
\tau
-measurable operators
satisfying a ”Lorentz space”-type condition. One of the principal results of the paper is given in Theorem
4.2
which establishes the submultiplicativity of the determinant
\Delta
on the algebra
L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
. A principal ingredient of the proof is the multiplicativity of the Haagerup-Schultz
functional
\Delta
on the algebra
{\mathcal{M}}^{\Delta}
.

In subsequent sections, attention is directed to Hölder type
inequalities for
\tau
-measurable operators. Principal results in Section 5 include a submajorization
inequality of Hölder type given in Proposition
5.5
and
corresponding symmetric norm inequalities given in
Proposition
5.7
, which are established using techniques based on logarithmic
submajorization. See, for example, Hiai
[
24
]
in the
finite-dimensional setting. While these results extend inequalities for
unitarily invariant norms given in
[
29
]

Theorem 3, the techniques given there do
not extend to the more general setting and a
crucial tool is a strengthened version
[
22
]
given in
Proposition
5.1
of a
submajorization inequality of Araki-Lieb-Thirring type due to
Kosaki
[
30
]
in the setting of semi-finite von Neumann algebras and
based on properties of the Fuglede-Kadison determinant. This section then
concludes with a convexity inequality (Proposition
5.11
) which is
due to Hiai and Zhan [HZ] in the
matrix setting for unitarily invariant norms.

We
show in Section 6 (Theorem
6.3
) that Weyl’s submajorization
inequality for the singular
values of a product (
[
17
]
Theorem 4.2(iii)) continues to hold for
uniform majorization, a notion introduced by Kalton
and Sukochev
[
28
]

which is a strengthening of the classical notion of
submajorization in the sense of Hardy, Littlewood and Polya. This permits us to give a simple proof of generalized
Hölder inequalities for general symmetric spaces proved recently by
Bekjan and Ospanov
[
2
]
and Albadawi
[
1
]
.

The identity in

The closed and densely defined operator

\tau\left(e^{\left|x\right|}(s,\infty)\right)<\infty.

With the sum and product defined as the respective closures of the algebraic sum and

with this topology,

is a complete topological

*
-algebra. These facts and their proofs can be found in the

[
32
]
,
[
37
]
. See also
[
12
]

The collection of all closed, densely defined operators
x
in

H

\mathcal{M}
and

will be denoted by

. The elements of

\tau
-compact operators
. Evidently, each

\tau
-measurable, that is,

For
x\in S(\tau)
, the
(generalised)
singular value function

\mu(x)=\mu(\cdot;x)=\mu(\cdot;|x|)
is
defined by

\mu\left(t;x\right):=\inf\left\{s\geq 0:d(s;|x|)\leq t\right\},\quad t\geq 0,

where

d(s;|x|):=\tau\left(e^{\left|x\right|}\left(s,\infty\right)\right),\quad s\geq 0.

It follows directly that the singular value function
\mu(x)
is a
decreasing, right-continuous function on the positive half-line

[0,\infty)
. Moreover,
\mu(uxv)\leq\|u\|_{\infty}\|v\|_{\infty}\mu(x)
for all
u,v\in\mathcal{M}
and
x\in S(\tau)
and

\mu(f(x))=f(\mu(x))

whenever
0\leq x\in S(\tau)
and
f
is an
increasing continuous function on
[0,\infty)
which satisfies
f(0)=0
.

It should be observed that a sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
S\left(\tau\right)
converges to zero for the measure topology if
and only if
\mu\left(t;x_{n}\right)\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
for all
t>0
.

If
m
denotes Lebesgue measure on the semiaxis
[0,\infty)
, and
if we consider
L^{\infty}(m)
as an Abelian von Neumann algebra
acting via multiplication on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}(m)
, with the trace given by integration with respect to

m
, then
S(m)
consists of all measurable functions on

[0,\infty)
which are bounded except on a set of finite measure.
In this case for
f\in S(m)
, the generalized singular value
function
\mu(f)
is precisely the classical decreasing
rearrangement of the function
|f|
, which is usually
denoted by
f^{*}
. In this setting, convergence for the measure
topology coincides with the usual notion of convergence in

If
\mathcal{M}={\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
and
\tau
is the standard
trace, then
S(\tau)=\mathcal{M}
, the measure topology coincides with the
operator norm topology. If
x\in S(\tau)
, then
x
is compact if and only if

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mu(t;x)=0
; in this case,

\mu_{n}(x)=\mu\left(t;x\right),\quad t\in[n,n+1),\quad n=0,1,2,\dots,

and the sequence
\{\mu_{n}(x)\}_{{}_{n=0}}^{\infty}
is just
the sequence of eigenvalues of
|x|
in non-increasing order
and counted according to multiplicity.

The real vector space
S_{h}\left(\tau\right)=\left\{x\in S\left(\tau\right):x=x^{\ast}\right\}
is a partially ordered vector space with the
ordering defined by setting
x\geq 0
if and only if
\left\langle x\xi,\xi\right\rangle\geq 0
for all

\sup_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}

S\left(\tau\right)^{+}

\tau

\tau\left(x\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu\left(t;x\right)dt,\ \ \ x\in S\left(\tau\right)^{+},

and satisfies
\tau\left(x^{\ast}x\right)=\tau\left(xx^{\ast}\right)

for all
x\in S\left(\tau\right)
. It should be observed that if
f
is
an increasing continuous function on
\left[0,\infty\right)
satisfying

f\left(0\right)\geq 0
, then

f(\mu(x))=\mu(f(|x|))

(2.1)

and if

\tau\left(f\left(\left|x\right|\right)\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu\left(t;f\left(\left|x\right|\right)\right)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}f\left(\mu\left(t;x\right)\right)dt

(2.2)

for all
x\in S\left(\tau\right)
.

If
x,y\in S(\tau)

x

A linear subspace

. If

A wide class of non-commutative symmetrically normed

3.
The algebra
L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)

Throughout, we will write
L_{1},\ L_{\infty}
rather than
L_{1}(m),L_{\infty}(m)
for the usual Lebesgue spaces on the semiaxis
[0,\infty)
equipped with Lebesgue measure
m
. We set

L_{\log_{+}}(\tau):=\{x\in S(\tau):\log_{+}|x|\in L_{1}(\tau)+\mathcal{M}\}

(3.1)

Here,

\log_{+}t=\max\{\log t,0\},\quad t>0.

Observing that

\mu(\log_{+}|x|)=\log_{+}\mu(x),\quad x\in S(\tau),

it follows that, if
x\in S(\tau)
, then

\displaystyle x\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)\quad

\displaystyle\iff\quad\int_{0}^{t}\log_{+}\mu(x)dm<\infty,\quad\forall t>0

(3.2)

\displaystyle\iff\quad\log_{+}\mu(x)\in L_{1}+L_{\infty}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\iff\quad\int_{0}^{1}\log_{+}\mu(x)dm<\infty.

(3.4)

It is clear that if
x\in L_{\log}(\tau)
, then
x^{*},|x|,uxv\in L_{\log}(\tau)
for all unitary
u,v\in{\mathcal{M}}
; moreover, if
x\geq 0
and
\alpha>0
then the equality
\mu(x^{\alpha})=\mu(x)^{\alpha}
readily implies that
x^{\alpha}\in L_{\log}(\tau)
.

If
\tau({\bf 1})<\infty
, then it follows from
[
12
]
Chapter III , remarks preceding Lemma 3.6, that

\tau(\log_{+}|x|)=\int_{0}^{\tau({\bf 1})}\log_{+}\mu(x)dm=\int_{0}^{\tau({\bf 1})}\log_{+}sd(\tau e^{|x|})(s).

Consequently, if
\tau({\bf 1})<\infty
, then the class
L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
coincides with the class
{\mathcal{M}}^{\Delta}
introduced by Haagerup and Schultz
[
21
]
.

We now observe that the class

L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
is an algebra. First recall that, if
s>0
then the dilation operator
\sigma
is defined on
S(m)
by setting

(\sigma_{s}x)(t)=x(t/s),\quad t>0.

If
x,y\in S(\tau)
, then

\mu(x+y)\leq\sigma_{2}(\mu(x))+\sigma_{2}(\mu(y)),\quad\mu(xy)\leq\sigma_{2}(\mu(x))\sigma_{2}(\mu(y))

(3.5)

See, for example,
[
17
]
Lemma 2.5. See also
[
31
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
x,y\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
, then
x+y,xy\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
.

Proof.

If
x,y\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
then, using (
3.5
),

\mu(x+y)\leq\sigma_{2}(\mu(x))+\sigma_{2}(\mu(y))\leq\max\{2\sigma_{2}(\mu(x)),2\sigma_{2}(\mu(y))\}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\log_{+}(\mu(x+y))

\displaystyle\leq\log_{+}\left(\max\{2\sigma_{2}(\mu(x)),2\sigma_{2}(\mu(y))\}\right)

\displaystyle=\max\{\log_{+}(2\sigma_{2}(\mu(x)),\log_{+}(2\sigma_{2}(\mu(y))\}

\displaystyle\leq\log_{+}(2\sigma_{2}(\mu(x)))+\log_{+}(2\sigma_{2}(\mu(y)))

\displaystyle=\sigma_{2}(\log_{+}(2\mu(x))+\log_{+}(2\mu(y)))

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{2}(\log_{+}\mu(x)+\log_{+}\mu(y)+2\log 2)

Since

\log_{+}\mu(x),\log_{+}\mu(y)\in L_{1}+L_{\infty},

as follows from (
3.2
), it follows that

\sigma_{2}(\log_{+}\mu(x)+\log_{+}\mu(y)+2\log 2)\in L_{1}+L_{\infty}.

This implies that

\log_{+}\mu(x+y)\in L_{1}+L_{\infty},

and accordingly,
x+y\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
.

To see that
xy\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
, observe first, again using (
3.5
), that

\mu(xy)\leq\sigma_{2}(\mu(x)\mu(y))=\sigma_{2}(\mu(x))\sigma_{2}(\mu(y)).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\log_{+}\mu(xy)

\displaystyle\leq\log_{+}\left(\sigma_{2}(\mu(x))\sigma_{2}(\mu(y))\right)=\sigma_{2}(\log_{+}\mu(x)+\log_{+}\mu(y))

\displaystyle\in L_{1}+L_{\infty},

and this implies that
xy\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
.

Finally, since
\mu(x)=\mu(x^{*})
, the final assertion of the proposition is trivial.

It should be noted that if
x\in S(\tau)
satisfies a (so-called) ”Lorentz space”-type condition (see
[
17
]
)

x\in{\mathcal{M}}\quad{\rm or}\quad\mu(s;x)\leq Cs^{-\alpha}\quad(C,\alpha>0),\ s>0

(3.6)

then
x\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
. Indeed, this assertion is clear if
x\in{\mathcal{M}}
. Assume then that
x\in S(\tau)
and that
\mu(s;x)\leq Cs^{-\alpha}
for some
C,\alpha>0
and all
s>0
and for simplicity, suppose that
C=1
. Observe that

\int_{0}^{1}\log_{+}\mu(s;x)ds\leq\int_{0}^{1}\log_{+}s^{-\alpha}ds=-\alpha\int_{0}^{1}\log sds<\infty

and this suffices to show that
x\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
. In particular, it follows that if
x\in L^{p}(\tau)
for some

, then
x\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
.
Indeed, as noted in
[
17
]
, in this case

\mu(s;x)^{p}\leq s^{-1}\int_{0}^{s}\mu(x)^{p}dm\leq s^{-1}\|x\|_{p}^{p}

for all
s>0
.

On the other hand, if
x\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
, then it need not be the case that
x
satisfies any Lorentz space condition. By way of example, if

f(t)=\exp(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}),t>0
, then it is evident that

. However, since

\lim_{t\to 0}t^{-\alpha}\exp(-t^{-{\frac{1}{2}}})=0,\quad\forall\alpha>0,

it follows that

fails to satisfy any condition of the type (

).

One immediate consequence of the preceding is that

L_{1}(\tau)+{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)\subseteq S(\tau).

Note that the inclusion on the left hand side is, in general, proper by observing that, if
f(t)=1/t,t>0
, then
f\in L_{\log_{+}}
but

\mu(f)\not\in L_{1}+L_{\infty}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
f,g:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be non-increasing.
If
f,g\in L_{log_{+}}
then the following statements are equivalent:

\displaystyle(i)\quad\int_{0}^{t}\log f(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\log g(s)ds,\quad\forall t>0.

\displaystyle(ii)\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}\log_{+}(rf(s))ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\log_{+}(rg(s))ds,\quad\forall r>0,\quad\forall t>0.

\displaystyle(iii)\quad\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(f(s))ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(g(s))ds,\quad\forall t>0,

for all continuous increasing functions
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
\varphi(0)=0
and
\varphi\circ\exp
is convex.

Remark 3.3
.

It should be observed that a simple consequence of Proposition
3.2
preceding is that if
x,y\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
and if

then

\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s;x)ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s;y)ds\quad\forall t>0.

Indeed, one need only take
\varphi(t)=t,t\geq 0
.

4.
The determinant function

If
x\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)

then the
determinant function

\Lambda(x)
is defined by setting, for all
t>0
,

\Lambda(t;x)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\log\mu(s;x)\,ds\right).

Since
x\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
, it follows that the integral is well-defined in the sense that it is either finite or takes the value
-\infty
. Indeed,

\Lambda(t;x)=0\Leftrightarrow\int_{0}^{t}\log\mu(s;x)ds=-\infty

In the case that
x
is
\tau
-compact, this function, which goes back to A.Grothendieck
[
20
]
, was studied by T.Fack
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
and extended further in
[
17
]
to the class of operators satisfying a ”Lorentz space”-type condition.

We remark that, if
\tau({\bf 1})<\infty
, then
\Delta(x):=\Lambda(\tau({\bf 1});x)
is precisely the extension of the classical Fuglede-Kadison determinant
[
16
]
to
S(\tau)
given by Haagerup and Schultz
[
21

\displaystyle=\mu(|a^{p}x|^{\frac{1}{p}})^{rp^{\prime}}\in E

\displaystyle\mu(|xb^{q}|^{\frac{rq^{\prime}}{q}})

\displaystyle=\mu(|xb^{q}|^{\frac{1}{q}})^{rq^{\prime}}\in E

that

\displaystyle\leq\||a^{p}x|^{\frac{rp^{\prime}}{p}}\|_{E(\tau)}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|\ |xb^{q}|^{\frac{rq^{\prime}}{q}}\|_{E(\tau)}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}},

A Weyl-type inequality for uniform majorisation and Hölder inequalities

The following direct consequence of the Weyl inequality given in Theorem
4.2
and the implication (i)
\Longrightarrow
(iii) of Proposition
3.2

[

Theorem 6.1
.

If
x,y\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
, and if
f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is any continuous
increasing function
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
for which
f(0)=0
and

f\circ\exp
is convex, then

f(|xy|)\prec\prec f(\mu(x)\mu(y)).

We strengthen this as follows, using the notion of
uniform Hardy-Littlewood
majorization
introduced in
[
28
]
;see also
[
31
]
Chapter 3.4. If
x,y\in S(\tau)
then

is said to be
strongly majorized by

, written
x\lhd y
, if there exists
\lambda>0
such that

\int_{\lambda a}^{b}\mu(x)dm\leq\int_{a}^{b}\mu(y)dm,\quad 0<\lambda a\leq b.

It is the case that
x\prec\prec y
if
x\lhd y
, but the converse is not valid. We shall
need the following fundamental result, which is obtained by combining
[
28
]

Proposition 4.3 with
[
28
]

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
E\subseteq S(m)
be a symmetric Banach space on
[0,\infty)
. If
y\in E(\tau)
and if

satisfies

then
x\in E(\tau)
and

\|x\|_{E(\tau)}\leq\|y\|_{E(\tau)}

Our proof of the following theorem uses the approach from the proof of

[
34
]
Theorem 7.

Theorem 6.3
.

If
x,y\in L_{\log_{+}}(\tau)
and
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is an increasing and continuous function for which
f(0)=0
and

f\circ\exp
is convex, then

f(|xy|)\lhd f(\mu(x)\mu(y)).

Proof.

Let
a,b
be positive scalars with
0\leq 2a<b
. It will suffice
to show that

\int_{2a}^{b}f(\mu(xy)dm\leq\int_{a}^{b}f(\mu(x)\mu(y))dm.

Without loss of generality, it may be assumed that
{\mathcal{M}}

is non-atomic. By
[
9
]
Lemma 2.4), there exist projections
p,q\in P({\mathcal{M}})

with
\tau(p)=\tau(q)=a
such that

e^{|x|}\left((\mu(a;x),\infty)\right)\leq p\leq e^{|x|}\left([\mu(a;x),\infty)\right),

e^{|y|}\left((\mu(a;y),\infty)\right)\leq q\leq e^{|y|}\left([\mu(a;y),\infty)\right),

\mu(|x|p)=\mu(x)\chi_{[0,\tau(p))}=\mu(x)\chi_{[0,a)},\quad\mu(|y|q)=\mu(x)\chi_{[0,\tau(q))}=\mu(y)\chi_{[0,a)},

and

\mu(|x|({\bf 1}-p))=\mu(\cdot+a,x),\quad\mu(|y|({\bf 1}-q))=\mu(\cdot+a,y)

Observe that, for all
t>2a
,

\mu(t;xy)\leq\mu(a;xpy)+\mu(a;x({\bf 1}-p)yq)+\mu(t-2a;x({\bf 1}-p)y({\bf 1}-q)).

Using
[
17
]
Lemma 2.5 (vii), observe that, for all
\epsilon>0
,

\mu(a+\epsilon;xpy)\leq\mu(a+\epsilon;|x|py)\leq\mu(a;|x|p)\mu(\epsilon;y)=0

and

\mu(a+\epsilon;x({\bf 1}-p)yq)\leq\mu(\epsilon;x({\bf 1}-p))\mu(a;|y|q)=0.

By right-continuity of the singular value function, it follows that

\mu(a;xpy)=\mu(a;x({\bf 1}-p)yq)=0

and so

\mu(t;xy)\leq\mu(t-2a;x({\bf 1}-p)y({\bf 1}-q))\quad t>2a.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{2a}^{b}f(\mu(xy))dm

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2a}^{b}f(\mu(\cdot-2a;x({\bf 1}-p)y({\bf 1}-q)dm\leq\int_{0}^{b-2a}f(\mu(x({\bf 1}-p)y({\bf 1}-q))dm

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{b-2a}f(\mu(x({\bf 1}-p))\mu(y({\bf 1}-q))dm\quad\quad{\rm using\ Theorem~\ref{WIp1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{b-2a}f(\mu(\cdot+a;x)\mu(\cdot+a;y))dm

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b-a}f(\mu(x)\mu(y))dm\leq\int_{a}^{b}f(\mu(x)\mu(y))dm

This concludes the proof.

Concordance invariants of null-homologous knots in thickened surfaces
Hans U. Boden, Homayun Karimi

Abstract
. Using the Gordon-Litherland pairing, one can define invariants (signature, nullity, determinant) for ℤ / 2 {\mathbb{Z}}/2 null-homologous links in thickened surfaces. In this paper, we study the concordance properties of these invariants. For example, if K ⊂ Σ × I K\subset\Sigma\times I is ℤ / 2 {\mathbb{Z}}/2 null-homologous and slice, we show that its signatures vanish and its determinants are perfect squares. These statements are derived from a cobordism result for closed unoriented surfaces in certain 4-manifolds. The Brown invariants are defined for ℤ / 2 {\mathbb{Z}}/2 null-homologous links in thickened surfaces. They take values in ℤ / 8 ∪ { ∞ } {\mathbb{Z}}/8\cup\{\infty\} and depend on a choice of spanning surface. We present two equivalent methods to defining and computing them, and we prove a chromatic duality result relating the two. We study their concordance properties, and we show how to interpret them as Arf invariants for null-homologous links. The Brown invariants and knot signatures are shown to be invariant under concordance of spanning surfaces.

Introduction

In the previous paper
[
BCK21
]
, we introduced invariants (signature, determinant, nullity) for
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
null-homologous links in thickened surfaces. The invariants are defined in terms of the Gordon-Litherland pairing, and they depend on a choice of spanning surface up to
S^{*}
-equivalence.

In the present paper, we use the invariants to define slice obstructions for knots in thickened surfaces. Specifically, given a
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
null-homologous knot
K
in a thickened surface, we show that if
K
is slice, then its knot signatures vanish and its knot determinants are perfect squares.
(See Theorems
3.2
and
3.4
.) We also show that the knot signatures are invariant under a new notion of concordance, namely concordance of spanning surfaces (see
Definition

5.1
).

These results are deduced from our main result,
Theorem

2.1
.
Suppose
W
is a compact, oriented 3-manifold with
\partial W=\Sigma
, and
E\subset W\times I
is a closed unoriented surface with
[E]=0
in
H_{2}(W\times I;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
and trivial Euler class. Then
Theorem

2.1
asserts that there exists a 3-manifold
V
embedded in
W\times I
with
\partial V=E.
It is used to prove Theorems
3.2
and
3.4
, and it is also used to prove a vanishing result for the Brown invariants for slice knots (
Theorem

4.12
).

The Brown invariants are defined for
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
null-homologous links in thickened surfaces and take values in
{\mathbb{Z}}/8\cup\{\infty\}
. We provide two definitions, one in terms of
{\mathbb{Z}}/4
quadratic enhancements of the Gordon-Litherland pairing, and another in terms of Goeritz matrices associated to checkerboard colorings of link diagrams. We relate the two approaches by proving a chromatic duality result (
Theorem

4.10
).

The first approach shows that the Brown invariants depend on a choice of spanning surface up to
S^{*}
-equivalence. (A proof that they are invariant under
S^{*}
-equivalence can be found in the recent paper of Klug
[
Klu20
]
.) Every non-split,
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
null-homologous link
L\subset\Sigma\times I
has two distinct
S^{*}
-equivalence classes of spanning surfaces, thus each such link admits two distinct Brown invariants. In this way, the Brown invariants are similar to the other invariants (signature, determinant, nullity) defined in terms of the Gordon-Litherland pairing.

The second approach shows that the Brown invariants can always be computed in terms of Goeritz matrices. This leads to a fast and efficient algorithm for computing them. We also study the behavior of the invariants under horizontal and vertical mirror symmetry, and we relate them to Arf invariants in the special case when
L\subset\Sigma\times I
is a null-homologous link.

The Goeritz matrices were first introduced in
[
Goe33
]
, which gave the first applications of quadratic forms to knot theory, see
[
Prz11
]
and
[
Tra17a
]
. Goeritz matrices continue to inspire new and important results, such as
[
Bon21
]
and
[
Tra17b
]
. In
[
Bon21
]
, Boninger shows that the Jones polynomial of a link can be computed from its Goeritz matrix. In
[
Tra17b
]
, Traldi shows that a link is determined up to mutation by the Goeritz matrices of its diagrams.

Our results touch upon another invariant with a long and distinguished history, namely the Arf invariant
[
Arf41
]
. As a knot invariant, it was first studied by Robertello
[
Rob65
]
, who showed it to be invariant under concordance. It is also invariant under the band pass move
[
Kau87
]
, and it is equal to the mod 2 reduction of Casson’s knot invariant
[
PV01
]
. It is also equal to the value
V_{K}(t)|_{t=i}
of the Jones polynomial
[
Lic97
]
. In particular, the Arf invariant is an invariant of finite-type, in fact, it is the only finite-type knot invariant which is also invariant under concordance
[
Ng98
]
.

One motivation for studying concordance of knots in thickened surfaces comes from applications to concordance of virtual knots. For example, the Brown invariants extend to invariants of checkerboard colorable virtual knots and links. In fact, using parity projection, they can be extended to invariants for all virtual knots.
As well, since parity projection preserves concordance (Theorem 5.9
[
BCG20
]
), it follows that the extended Brown invariants are slice obstructions for arbitrary virtual knots.

For a null-homologous knot, the Brown invariant specializes to its Arf invariant, so it is natural to wonder whether the Brown invariants are finite-type invariants. Specifically, can they be computed on subdiagrams? For classical knots, the Casson invariant is an integral lift of the Arf invariant, and so it is natural to wonder whether the Brown invariants also have integral lifts.

For a classical link, it is well-known that the Arf invariant is only defined when the link is
proper
. (Recall that a classical link
L=K_{1}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n}
is proper if
\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(K_{i},L\smallsetminus K_{i})
is even for all
i=1,\ldots,n.
) This is precisely the condition needed to ensure that the quadratic form associated to a Seifert surface for
L
is proper, see
[
KM04
, p.226]
. It would be interesting to determine conditions on a link with spanning surface
F\subset\Sigma\times I
so that its
{\mathbb{Z}}/4
quadratic form
\varphi_{F}
is
proper
(see
Section

4.1
).

In
Section

1
, we review concordance for links
L\subset\Sigma\times I
and discuss the Gordon-Litherland pairing and the resulting link invariants.
In
Section

2
, we state and prove the main result,
Theorem

2.1
. We also provide a discussion focused on the hypotheses of
Theorem

2.1
.
In
Section

3
, we establish the slice criteria on the signature and determinant derived from
Theorem

2.1
.
In
Section

4
, we review
{\mathbb{Z}}/4
enhanced forms and their Brown invariants. We then discuss the associated link invariants, showing they provide slice obstructions and proving a chromatic duality result. In
Section

5
, we introduce the notion of concordance of spanning surfaces, and we study the behavior of the signature and Brown invariants under concordance of spanning surfaces.

Conventions.

Throughout this paper, spanning surfaces are assumed to be compact and connected, but they are not assumed to be oriented or even orientable.

1.
Preliminaries

We begin this section by introducing some basic notions. We then review Turaev’s definition of concordance for links in thickened surfaces, and recall the construction of the Gordon-Litherland pairing and describe link invariants such as the signature, nullity, determinant which are defined in terms of the Gordon-Litherland pairing.

1.1.
Basic notions

Throughout this paper,
\Sigma
will denote a compact, connected, oriented surface and
I=[0,1]
, the unit interval. A link in
\Sigma\times I
is an embedding of
S^{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup S^{1}
into the interior of
\Sigma\times I
, considered up to orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of the pair
(\Sigma\times I,\Sigma\times\{0\})
. The link
L\subset\Sigma\times I
is said to be
R

null-homologous
if
[L]=0
in
H_{1}(\Sigma\times I;R)
. When
R={\mathbb{Z}}
is understood by context, we use null-homologous to mean
{\mathbb{Z}}
null-homologous without any confusion.

Given a link
L\subset\Sigma\times I
, a
spanning surface
for
L
is a compact, connected, unoriented surface
F
with boundary
\partial F=L
. If
F
is oriented, then it is called a
Seifert surface
for
L
. In that case,
L
inherits an orientation as the oriented boundary of
F
.

Note that a link
L\subset\Sigma\times I
is
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
null-homologous if and only if it admits a spanning surface, and it is null-homologous if and only if it admits a Seifert surface.

Any spanning surface
F\subset\Sigma\times I
for
L
determines a symmetric bilinear pairing on
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})
called the
Gordon-Litherland
pairing. Using this pairing, one can define signature, determinant and nullity invariants for
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
null-homologous links
L\subset\Sigma\times I
,
[
BCK21
]
. When
L
is checkerboard colorable, the invariants of
[
BCK21
]
agree with the invariants defined by Im, Lee, and Lee in terms of Goeritz matrices
[
ILL10
]
.

The invariants (signature, determinant, nullity) derived from the Gordon-Litherland pairing depend on the choice of spanning surface, but they are invariant under
S^{*}
-equivalence (see
Definition

1.2
and
[
BCK21
, §2.3]
).

When
\Sigma
has genus
g(\Sigma)\geq 1
, every non-split
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
null-homologous link
L\subset\Sigma\times I
has exactly two
S^{*}
-equivalence classes of spanning surfaces, see
[
BCK21
, Proposition 1.6]
. Thus, links in thickened surfaces typically have two signatures, two determinants, and two nullities.

1.2.
Concordance and slice knots

We recall the notions of cobordism and concordance for knots in thickened surfaces,
originally introduced by Turaev
[
Tur08
]
.

Definition 1.1

(Turaev)
.

Two knots
K_{0}\subset\Sigma_{0}\times I
and
K_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}\times I
are said to be
concordant
if there exists a compact, oriented 3-manifold
W
with
\partial W=-\Sigma_{0}\cup\Sigma_{1}
and an annulus
A
properly embedded in
W\times I
such that
\partial A=K_{0}\sqcup K_{1}
.

More generally, a
cobordism
is an oriented surface
Z
properly embedded in
W\times I
such that
\partial Z=K_{0}\sqcup K_{1}
.

Note that there exists a cobordism between any two knots in thickened surfaces. For a proof, see
[
Kau15
]
. The
slice genus
of a knot
K\subset\Sigma\times I
is defined by setting

g_{s}(K)=\min\{g(Z)\mid Z\text{ is a cobordism from $K$ to the unknot}\},

where
g(Z)
denotes the genus of cobordism surface
Z
. A knot
K\subset\Sigma\times I
is said to be
slice
if it is concordant to the unknot. In particular, a knot
K\subset\Sigma\times I
is slice if and only if
g_{s}(K)=0
.

1.3.
Gordon-Litherland pairing

Associated to a link
L
in
\Sigma\times I
with spanning surface
F
, there is a symmetric bilinear pairing on
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})
called the
Gordon-Litherland pairing
. In this section, we review the definition of the Gordon-Litherland pairing, following
[
BCK21
]
and
[
BK21
]
.

To begin, we recall the asymmetric linking for simple closed curves in
\Sigma\times I
. Given two disjoint oriented simple closed curves
J,K
in the interior of
\Sigma\times I
, define
\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(J,K)=J\cdot B,
where
B
is a 2-chain in
\Sigma\times I
such that
\partial B=K-v
for some 1-cycle
v
in
\Sigma\times\{1\}
and
\cdot
denotes the intersection number.

Let
F\subset\Sigma\times I
be a compact, connected, unoriented surface. Its normal bundle
N(F)
has boundary a
\{\pm 1\}
-bundle
\widetilde{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}F
, a double cover with
\widetilde{F}
oriented. Define the transfer map
\tau\colon H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{1}(\widetilde{F};{\mathbb{Z}})
by setting
\tau([a])=[\pi^{-1}(a)].

For
a,b\in H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})
, define
\mathcal{G}_{F}(a,b)=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(\tau a,b)+\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(\tau b,a)\big).
This pairing is well-defined, takes values in the integers, and is symmetric (for proofs of these and other statements, see
[
BCK21
]
and
[
BK21
]
). The map
\mathcal{G}_{F}\colon H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})\times H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is called the
Gordon-Litherland pairing
.

Let
L\subset\Sigma\times I
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
null-homologous link and
F\subset\Sigma\times I
a spanning surface for
L
.
Assuming that
L
has
m
components, we can write
L=K_{1}\cup\dots\cup K_{m}
. Let
L^{\prime}=K_{1}^{\prime}\cup\dots\cup K_{m}^{\prime}
be the push-off of
L
in
\Sigma\times I
that misses
F
. Fix an orientation on
L
, which gives an orientation for each component
K_{i}
, and choose the compatible orientation on
K_{i}^{\prime}
. Then define

e(F)=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(K_{i},K_{i}^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad e(F,L)=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(K_{i},K_{j}^{\prime}).

Here,
e(F,L)
will depend on the choice of orientation of
L
, but
e(F)
is independent of that choice. The two are related by the formula
e(F,L)=e(F)-\lambda(L)
, where
\lambda(L)=\sum_{i\neq j}\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(K_{i},K_{j})
is the total linking number of
L
. In the case of knots,
e(F,K)=e(F).

In
[
BCK21
]
, the Gordon-Litherland pairing is used to define signature, determinant, and nullity invariants for links in thickened surfaces.
Let
L\subset\Sigma\times I
be a link and
F\subset\Sigma\times I
a spanning surface. Then the determinant is
\det(L,F)=|\det(\mathcal{G}_{F})|
, the nullity is
n(L,F)=\text{nullity}(\mathcal{G}_{F})
, and the signature is
\sigma(L,F)=\operatorname{sig}(\mathcal{G}_{F})+\frac{1}{2}e(F,L)
. Each of
\det(L,F),n(L,F)
and
\sigma(L,F)
depend only on the
S^{*}
-equivalence of the spanning surface
F
.

Definition 1.2
.

Two spanning surfaces are
S^{*}
-equivalent
if one can be obtained from the other by (i) ambient isotopy, (ii) attachment (or removal) of a tube, and (iii) attachment (or removal) of a half-twisted band.

A link in
\Sigma\times I
is said to be
split
if it can be represented by a disconnected diagram on
\Sigma
. A link is said to be
checkerboard colorable
if it can be represented by a checkerboard colorable diagram on
\Sigma
(see
Definition

3.8
).

As previously mentioned, for every link
L\subset\Sigma\times I
that is non-split and
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
null-homologous, there are two
S^{*}
-equivalence classes of spanning surfaces. In fact, a link
L\subset\Sigma\times I
is
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
null-homologous if and only if it is checkerboard colorable (see
[
BCK21
, Proposition 1.1]
), and every spanning surface is
S^{*}
-equivalent to one of checkerboard surfaces (see
[
BCK21
, Proposition 1.6]
). Thus, for such links, there are two sets of invariants arising from the Gordon-Litherland pairing.

2.
Main theorem

In this section, we state and prove the main theorem, which gives a cobordism result for closed unoriented surfaces. The proof involves obstruction theory, and we are grateful to Danny Ruberman, who sent us a sketch of a key step in the following proof. Following the proof, we discuss applications with an eye toward ensuring that the hypotheses of
Theorem

2.1
are satisfied.

Applications of
Theorem

2.1
to questions about concordance of knots will be given in
Section

3
. This is where we show that the signature and determinant provide obstructions to knots being slice.

For additional background information about nonorientable surfaces in 3- and 4-manifolds, we refer readers to
[
BW69
]
and
[
LRS15
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
W
be a compact oriented 3-manifold with
\partial W=\Sigma
. Assume that
E\subset W\times I
is a closed nonorientable surface with
[E]=0
in
H_{2}(W\times I;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
and with normal Euler number
e(E)=0
. Then there exists a compact 3-manifold
V\subset W\times I
with
\partial V=E
.

Proof.

Every nonorientable surface
E
can be written as a connected sum of
{\mathbb{R}}{\rm P}^{2}
, and in this context, a surface with
E=\#_{i=1}^{g}{\mathbb{R}}{\rm P}^{2}
is said to have nonorientable genus
g
. A routine calculation shows that

H_{i}(E;{\mathbb{Z}})=\begin{cases}{\mathbb{Z}}&\text{if $i=0,$}\\
{\mathbb{Z}}/2\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{g-1}&\text{if $i=1,$}\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad H_{i}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)=\begin{cases}{\mathbb{Z}}/2&\text{if $i=0,2,$}\\
({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{g}&\text{if $i=1.$}\end{cases}

In this case, the assumption that
e(E)=0
implies that
E
has nonorientable genus
g=2k
. Thus
E
abstractly bounds a 3-manifold. This fact, together with the assumption that
[E]=0
in
H_{2}(W\times I;{\mathbb{Z}}/2),
implies that
E
is trivial in
{\mathfrak{N}}_{2}(W\times I)
, the unoriented bordism group, by Theorem 17.1 of
[
CF64
]
. It remains to show that
E
bounds a 3-manifold embedded in
W\times I
.

Let
N_{E}
be a tubular neighborhood of
E
in
W\times I
and let
S_{E}
be its boundary. Then
N_{E}
is a
D^{2}
bundle over
E
isomorphic to the normal bundle
\nu(E)
to the embedding
j\colon E\to W\times I
, and
S_{E}
is the associated
S^{1}
-bundle. Since
\nu(E)\oplus TE\cong j^{*}(T(W\times I))
and
W\times I
is orientable, it follows that
w_{1}(\nu(E))=w_{1}(E).

Although the normal bundle
\nu(E)
is not orientable, it has an Euler class in
H^{2}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}_{w}),
cohomology with coefficients twisted by the representation
w\colon\pi_{1}(E)\to\{\pm 1\}
associated to the first Stiefel-Whitney class
w_{1}(E)

[
Mas69
]
. Poincaré duality for twisted coefficients (cf. Theorem 5.7 in
[
DK01
]
) implies that
H^{2}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}_{w})\cong H_{0}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}).
Thus the Euler class is determined by an integer, namely the normal Euler number
e(E).
Since
e(E)=0
, the bundle
\nu(E)
admits a nowhere zero section, which we use to split
\nu(E)=L\oplus\varepsilon^{1}
. Here
\varepsilon^{1}
is trivial and
L
is the line bundle over
E
with
w_{1}(L)=w_{1}(E)
.

We use obstruction theory to describe the sections of
S_{E}
up to homotopy. Indeed, since the higher homotopy groups of
S^{1}
are all trivial, there is a single obstruction to the existence of a section of
S_{E}
lying in
H^{2}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}_{w}),
and the obstruction to finding a homotopy of sections lies in
H^{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}_{w}).
Thus homotopy classes of sections of
S_{E}
are in one-to-one correspondence with elements in
H^{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}_{w}),
which is isomorphic to
H_{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}})
by Poincaré duality for twisted coefficients.

Let
X=W\times I\smallsetminus\text{int}(N_{E})
and consider the Mayer-Vietoris sequence associated with the decomposition
W\times I=X\cup N_{E}
:

(1)

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H_{2}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi\hskip 16.38895pt\hskip 8.19447pt}
\textstyle{H_{2}(N_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\oplus H_{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 8.19447pt\;\;\psi}
\textstyle{H_{2}(W\times I;{\mathbb{Z}}/2).}

Since
[E]=0
in
H_{2}(W\times I;{\mathbb{Z}}/2),
we have
\psi([E],0)=0
. By exactness, there must exist an element
\alpha\in H_{2}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
with
\varphi(\alpha)=([E],0).
We will show that
\alpha
can be represented as the image of a section of
S_{E}.

The Gysin sequence for the bundle
p\colon S_{E}\to E
gives that

(2)

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p^{*}}
\textstyle{H^{1}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{H^{0}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\smallsmile\,w_{2}}
\textstyle{H^{2}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}/2).}

Here the second map
f
is given by evaluating a cohomology class on the circle fiber, and the third map is trivial since
w_{2}=w_{2}(E)=0
. By the sequence (
2
) and our previous calculations, we have that
H^{1}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\cong({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{2k+1}
, thus
H_{2}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\cong({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{2k+1}
by Poincaré duality.

Given a section of
S_{E}
, its image determines an element of
H_{2}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
, which we denote
[E^{\prime}]
. We can write
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
for the first map of the Mayer-Vietoris sequence (
1
), and note that
\varphi_{1}(\alpha)=p_{*}(\alpha)
under the natural identification
H_{2}(N_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\cong H_{2}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
. For any section, we have
\varphi([E^{\prime}])=([E],\beta)
for some
\beta\in H_{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}}/2).
We claim that the section can be chosen so that
\beta=0,
namely so that
\varphi([E^{\prime}])=([E],0)
.

Let
\gamma\in H^{1}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
be the Poincaré dual of
[E^{\prime}]\in H_{2}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2).
According to the Gysin sequence (
2
),
f(\gamma)=1
in
H^{0}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2
. This holds since the intersection number
[E^{\prime}]\cdot[F]=1
in
S_{E}
, where
[F]\in H_{1}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
is the homology class of the circle fiber.

As previously observed, homotopy classes of sections of
S_{E}
are in one-to-one correspondence with elements in
H^{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}_{w})
. Let
r\colon H^{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}_{w})\to H^{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
be the map obtained by reducing coefficients mod 2, and note that this map is surjective. The group
H^{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}_{w})
acts on
H^{1}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
by
\xi\cdot\gamma=\gamma+p^{*}(r(\xi))
, where
\xi\in H^{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}_{w})
and
\gamma\in H^{1}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
. Note that the action is transitive on the fiber
f^{-1}(1)
of (
2
). Thus every element
\gamma\in H^{1}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
with
f(\gamma)=1
is the Poincaré dual of the image
[E^{\prime}]
of some section of
S_{E}.
Correspondingly, every element in
\varphi_{1}^{-1}([E])\subset H_{2}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
is the image of some section. In particular, applied to the element
\alpha\in H_{2}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
with
\varphi(\alpha)=([E],0)
, this shows that
\alpha
can be represented by a section. This proves the claim.

Let
[E^{\prime}]
be the image of a section of
S_{E}
with
\varphi([E^{\prime}])=([E],0)
. Then
E^{\prime}
is a compact surface embedded in
S_{E}
homeomorphic to
E
. Let
\varphi\colon S_{E}\to BO
be the classifying map for the normal bundle of the embedding of
E^{\prime}
in
S_{E}
.

Since
BO={\mathbb{R}}{\rm P}^{\infty}=K({\mathbb{Z}}/2,1)
, the set of homotopy classes
[S_{E},{\mathbb{R}}{\rm P}^{\infty}]
is isomorphic to
H^{1}(S_{E};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
.
Since
S_{E}
is compact, the image of
\varphi
must lie in
{\mathbb{R}}{\rm P}^{N}\subset{\mathbb{R}}{\rm P}^{\infty}
for some
N.

By the claim,
\varphi
extends to a map
\varphi\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}{\rm P}^{N}
,
which we can choose to be transverse to the codimension one submanifold

The manifold

by attaching a cylinder in

and

.
∎

Let

be a knot with spanning surface

and suppose that

is a compact oriented 3-manifold with boundary

. Let

be an oriented surface with boundary

and set

.
It is a closed, unoriented surface in

The hypotheses of

in

and that it has normal Euler number

.

The next proposition shows that if

is a handlebody, then up to

always bounds.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let

with spanning surface

. Let

be a handlebody with
\partial W=\Sigma
and

. Then there exists a spanning surface

which is

-equivalent to

.

By adding half-twisted bands, we can find a spanning surface

, and it follows that

.
∎

We now show that if

is slice, then we can always find a slice disk

in

for some 3-manifold

.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let

be slice, then there is a slice disk

for some 3-manifold

such that

bounds a 3-manifold

.

Proof.

As before, by adding half-twisted bands, we can arrange that

.

Consider the long exact sequence in homology for the triple

(3)

\textstyle{H_{2}(\Sigma\times I,K;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i_{*}}
\textstyle{H_{2}(W\times I,K;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j_{*}\hskip 8.19447pt}
\textstyle{H_{2}(W\times I,\Sigma\times I;{\mathbb{Z}}/2).}

The spanning surface

gives an element

Let

be the associated two-fold cover of W. Then since

.
∎

If

3.
Slice obstructions

Throughout this section,
K\subset\Sigma\times I
will be a
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
null-homologous knot in a thickened surface. Recall that a knot in a thickened surface is
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
null-homologous if and only if it admits a spanning surface.

Our goal in this section is to show that, for a knot
K\subset\Sigma\times I
with spanning surface
F
,
if
K
is slice and
\det(K,F)\neq 0
, then

\sigma(K,F)=0
and
\det(K,F)
is a perfect square.

If
K
is slice, then
Theorem

2.3
implies that there is a 3-manifold
W
with slice disk
D\subset W\times I
such that the closed surface
E=F\cup D
bounds a compact 3-manifold
V\subset W\times I
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There is a generating set
\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{2g}\}
for
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})
such that
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{g}
lie in the kernel of the map
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{1}(V;{\mathbb{Q}}).

Proof.

Let
i\colon E\to V
be the inclusion map. First assume
V
is orientable. Then by
[
Bre93
, VI, Theorem 10.4]
and/or
[
Hat07
, Lemma 3.5]
,
\,{\rm dim}\,(\,{\rm Ker}\,i_{*})=\frac{1}{2}\,{\rm dim}\,(H_{1}(E;{\mathbb{Q}}))
. By a Mayer-Vietoris argument,
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}})
, and by the Universal Coefficient Theorem,
H_{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\cong H_{1}(E;{\mathbb{Q}})
, and the conclusion follows in this case.

Now assume
V
is not orientable.
If
i_{*}\colon H_{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\to H_{1}(V;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
, then
\,{\rm dim}\,(\,{\rm Ker}\,i_{*})=\frac{1}{2}\,{\rm dim}\,(H_{1}(E;{\mathbb{Z}}/2))
. We can choose a generating set
\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{2g}\}
for
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})
, such that the mod 2 reduction of
\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{g}\}
generates
\,{\rm Ker}\,i_{*}
, so they must all be mapped to torsion elements in
H_{1}(V;{\mathbb{Z}})
, and therefore, they all lie in the kernel of the map
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{1}(V;{\mathbb{Q}}).

∎

Let
Q
be a symmetric bilinear form on a vector space
V
over
{\mathbb{R}}
. A subspace
U\subset V
is called
totally isotropic
if
Q
vanishes on
U
. If
U
is a totally isotropic subspace of maximal dimension, then

(4)

2\dim U=r-|\sigma|+n,

where
r,\sigma
and
n
are the rank, signature, and nullity of
Q
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
K\subset\Sigma\times I
be a knot with spanning surface
F
such that
e(F)=0
. Suppose further that
K
is slice. Then

|\sigma(K,F)|\leq n(K,F).

In particular, if
n(K,F)=0
, then
\sigma(K,F)=0
.

Proof.

By
Theorem

2.3
there is a slice disk
D\subset W\times I
for some 3-manifold
W
such that
E=F\cup D
bounds a 3-manifold
V\subset W\times I
.
Now by
Lemma

3.1
, we have a set
\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{2g}\}
of generators for
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})
such that
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{g}
lie in the kernel of the map
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{1}(V;{\mathbb{Q}}).
Let
U
be the subgroup of
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})
generated by
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{g}
. For any
a\in U
, there is a surface
A
with boundary a multiple of
a
. Now suppose
b\in U
, and
B
is a surface with boundary a multiple of
b
. We have

\mathcal{G}_{F}(a,b)=\tfrac{1}{2}(\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(\tau a,b)+\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(\tau b,a)).

Let
N(V)
be a tubular neighborhood of
V
in
W\times I
. Then
N(V)
is a (possibly twisted)
I
-bundle over
V
with boundary
\partial N(V)
a
\{\pm 1\}
-bundle over
V
. If
p\colon N(V)\to V
is the projection map, then
p^{-1}(A)\cap\partial N(V)
gives a surface
\widetilde{A}
in
W\times I
with boundary
\tau a
. (In fact,
p|_{\widetilde{A}}\colon\widetilde{A}\to A
is a two-fold covering.) Notice that
\widetilde{A}
and
B
are disjoint, hence the intersection
\widetilde{A}\cdot B=0.
Thus,
\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(\tau a,b)=\widetilde{A}\cdot B=0
. A similar argument shows that
\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(\tau b,a)=0
. Thus
\mathcal{G}_{F}(a,b)=0
for

Hence

is a totally isotropic subspace for the Gordon-Litherland pairing. Equation (
4
) gives the inequality:

2\dim U\leq\dim H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})-|\operatorname{sig}(\mathcal{G}_{F})|+n(K,F),

but since
\dim U=\tfrac{1}{2}\dim H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})
in our case, it follows that
|\operatorname{sig}(\mathcal{G}_{F})|\leq n(K,F).

Now the self-intersection number of
E
, which is the normal Euler number of
E
, vanishes. To calculate
the self-intersection number of
E
, we push a copy of
E
off itself.
Since the slice disk
D
is orientable, we can push it off so there are no self-intersection points over the slice disk. Therefore, the intersection number is

-\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(K,K^{\prime})=e(F,K)=0
. It follows that
|\operatorname{sig}\mathcal{G}_{F}|=|\sigma(K,F)|\leq n(K,F).

∎

Remark 3.3
.

If
L\subset\Sigma\times I
is a link with spanning surface
F\subset\Sigma\times I
, we can construct a new surface
F^{\prime}=F\#_{\tau}\Sigma
by connecting

to a parallel copy of
\Sigma
near

by a small thin tube
\tau
.
Notice that if

, then

and
[F^{\prime}\cup D]=[F\cup D]
in

. In particular, if

satisfies the hypothesis of

3.2
, then

does as well.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
K\subset\Sigma\times I
be a knot with spanning surface
F
such that
e(F)=0
. Suppose further that
K
is slice. Then
\det(K,F)
is
a perfect square.

Proof.

Choose a slice disk
D\subset W\times I
as in the proof of
Theorem

3.2
.
Let
\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{2g}\}
be a basis for
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})
, obtained by

. Then by
Theorem

3.2
, the matrix of
\mathcal{G}_{F}
with respect to this basis has the form

\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
A^{\mathsf{T}}&B\end{bmatrix}
, where

are

\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
-A^{\mathsf{T}}&xI\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}xI&A\\
A^{\mathsf{T}}&B\end{bmatrix}&=\begin{bmatrix}xI&A\\
0&-A^{\mathsf{T}}A+xB\end{bmatrix},\\
x^{g}\det\begin{bmatrix}xI&A\\
A^{\mathsf{T}}&B\end{bmatrix}&=x^{g}\det(-A^{\mathsf{T}}A+xB).\end{split}

We divide both sides by

, and in the remaining equation put

, it follows that

\det\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
A^{\mathsf{T}}&B\end{bmatrix}=(-1)^{g}(\det A)^{2},

∎

Remark 3.5
.

For a null-homologous knot

Theorem 3.6
.

Let

be a knot with spanning surface

Let

. Since

. Furthermore, since

We can repeat the proof of

, to show that there is a generating set

∎

Remark 3.7
.

4.
Brown Invariants

In this section, we recall the Brown invariant of a proper quadratic enhancement of a finite dimensional
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
inner product space, following
[
Bro72
,
KV88
]
. Using it, one can define invariants for
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
null-homologous links
L\subset\Sigma\times I
together with a choice of spanning surface
F\subset\Sigma\times I
. The invariants are called Brown invariants; they are denoted
{\upbeta}_{F}(L)
and take values in
{\mathbb{Z}}/8\cup\{\infty\}
. They have been studied previously by several different authors
[
Mat86
,
KT90
,
Gil93
,
KM04
,
Klu20
]
.

We outline two methods for defining and computing the Brown invariants of links in thickened surfaces. The first involves the Gordon-Litherland pairing and the second Goeritz matrices. We prove a duality theorem that relates the two approaches. We also prove a vanishing result for
{\upbeta}_{F}(K)
for
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
null-homologous knots
K\subset\Sigma\times I
which are slice. The Brown invariants are seen to change sign under taking vertical or horizontal mirror image,
and they specialize to the Arf invariants when the link is
{\mathbb{Z}}
null-homologous.

4.1.
Quadratic enhancements and the Brown invariant

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space over
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
and with a possibly singular symmetric bilinear form
\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}.
Even though it may be singular, we refer to
\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}

A

a

Equation

is even for all

By (

The

(V,\,\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\,)
is the subspace

R=\{u\in V\mid u\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}v=0,\ \text{for\ every}\ v\in V\}.

The form
\varphi
is said to be
non-singular
if

, and

if
\varphi|_{R}=0.

Given a non-singular enhanced space
(V,\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}},\varphi)
, consider the
Monsky sum

(6)

\lambda(\varphi)=\sum_{v\in V}i^{\varphi(v)}.

By
[
Bro72
, Lemma 3.2]
, it follows that
\lambda
is multiplicative under orthogonal sum (i.e.,
\lambda(\varphi_{1}\oplus\varphi_{2})=\lambda(\varphi_{1})\lambda(\varphi_{2})
), and by
[
Bro72
, Lemma 3.3]
, we see that
\lambda(\varphi)^{8}
is positive and real provided that
\varphi
is non-singular. Therefore, it follows that

(7)

\lambda(\varphi)=(\sqrt{2})^{\dim V}e^{{\upbeta}(\varphi){\pi i}/4}

for some integer
{\upbeta}(\varphi)
well-defined modulo 8. The element
{\upbeta}(\varphi)\in{\mathbb{Z}}/8
is called the
Brown invariant
.

If
\varphi
is singular and proper, then it induces a non-singular quadratic enhancement

\varphi

on
V/R
, and we define
{\upbeta}(\varphi)={\upbeta}(\hbox{ \kern-1.99997pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.07639pt\hbox{\kern-1.00006pt$\varphi$\kern-0.50003pt}}\kern 0.50003pt}).
If
\varphi
is not proper, then we set
{\upbeta}(\varphi)=\infty
and say that the Brown invariant is undefined.

Observe that the Monsky sum (
6
) is well-defined even when
\varphi
is singular. In fact, if
\varphi
is not proper, then
\lambda(\varphi)=0
. If
\varphi
is singular and proper, then

(8)

\lambda(\varphi)=(\sqrt{2})^{(\dim V+\dim R)}e^{{\upbeta}(\varphi){\pi i}/4}.

In particular,
\varphi
is proper if and only if
\lambda(\varphi)\neq 0.

For a fixed inner product space
(V,\,\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\,),
the map

{\upbeta}\colon\{\text{enhanced\ spaces}\}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}/8\cup\{\infty\},

provides a complete isomorphism invariant of quadratic enhancements of
(V,\,\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\,)
, (see Section 3.1 in
[
KM04
]
).

If
\varphi\colon V\to{\mathbb{Z}}/4
is even, then the Monsky sum lies on the real line. If
\varphi
is even and proper, then
\lambda(\varphi)=\pm(\sqrt{2})^{(\dim V+\dim R)}
and
{\upbeta}(\varphi)\in\{0,4\}
. In particular,
{\upbeta}(\varphi)=4\operatorname{Arf}(q),
the (usual) Arf invariant of the ordinary quadratic form
q
associated to
\varphi
. Thus, the Brown invariant specializes to the Arf invariant when
\varphi
is even.

4.3.
Brown invariants of links via the Gordon-Litherland pairing

In this section, we give the first definition of the Brown invariants for oriented links in thickened surfaces.

We start with some basic results about quadratic enhancements in terms of their special matrix representatives.

Let
U
be a free abelian group, and let
B\colon U\times U\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a symmetric bilinear pairing.
Let
V
be the mod 2 reduction of
U
. For
[u],[v]\in V
, define
[u]\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}[v]\equiv B(u,v)
(mod 2), where the
u,v\in U
have mod 2 reductions
[u],[v],
respectively. Define
\varphi\colon V\to{\mathbb{Z}}/4
by setting
\varphi([u])\equiv B(u,u)
(mod 4).

Lemma 4.1
.

The map
\varphi
is well-defined and
(V,\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\,,\varphi)
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}/4
enhanced space.

Proof.

If
u^{\prime}\in U
is another element with
[u^{\prime}]=[u]
, then
u^{\prime}=u+2v
for some
v\in U
. It follows that

\begin{split}\varphi([u^{\prime}])&\equiv B(u+2v,u+2v)\text{ (mod $4$)},\\
&=B(u,u)+B(u,2v)+B(2v,u)+B(2v,2v),\\
&=B(u,u)+4B(u,v)+4B(v,v),\\
&\equiv\varphi([u])\text{ (mod $4$)}.\end{split}

This shows that
\varphi
is well-defined.

Next, we show that
\varphi
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}/4
quadratic form
(V,\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\,)
.
If
[u],[v]\in V
, let
u,v\in U
be chosen so that they map to
[u],[v]
under reduction mod 2. Then

\begin{split}\varphi([u]+[v])&\equiv B(u+v,u+v)\text{ (mod $4$),}\\
&=B(u,u)+B(v,v)+2B(u,v),\\
&\equiv\varphi([u])+\varphi([v])+2(u\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.55}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}v)\text{ (mod $4$)}.\end{split}

The claim now follows.
∎

.

In other words,

\xi

\mu_{\xi}(D)=-\frac{1}{2}e(F,L)

The Brown invariant of the oriented checkerboard colorable link diagram

4.5.
Duality for Brown invariants

In this section, we prove the chromatic duality theorems, which are the analogues for Brown invariants of Theorems 4.1 and 5.4 of
[
BCK21
]
. We apply them to relate the two families of Brown invariants.

In
[
BCK21
]
, a similar approach is used to relate the invariants (signature, determinant, and nullity) defined in terms of the Gordon-Litherland pairing to the invariants defined by Im, Lee and Lee in terms of Goeritz matrices
[
ILL10
]
. The correspondence requires switching the checkerboard coloring, which is an aspect which is unique to this setting and still somewhat curious.

Recall from
Remark

3.3
that for a link
L\subset\Sigma\times I
with spanning surfac
F\subset\Sigma\times I
, we can construct a new surface
F^{\prime}=F\#_{\tau}\Sigma
by attaching a parallel copy of
\Sigma
to
F
using by a small tube
\tau
. From Section 4 in
[
BCK21
]
, we know
F^{\prime}
and
F
are not
S^{*}
equivalent.
Furthermore,
e(F^{\prime},L)=e(F,L)
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
F\subset\Sigma\times I
be a connected spanning surface such that the map
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\to H_{1}(\Sigma\times I;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
is surjective. Let
F^{\prime}=F\#_{\tau}\Sigma
be as above, and set
\mathcal{K}_{F}=\operatorname{Ker}(H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\to H_{1}(\Sigma\times I;{\mathbb{Z}}/2))
. Then the Brown invariant of
\varphi_{F^{\prime}}
is equal to the Brown invariant of the restriction of
\varphi_{F}
to
\mathcal{K}_{F},
i.e.,
{\upbeta}(\varphi_{F^{\prime}})={\upbeta}(\varphi|_{\mathcal{K}_{F}})
.

Proof.

Let
g=g(\Sigma)
be the genus of
\Sigma
. As in the proof of
[
BCK21
, Theorem 4.1]
, we can choose a basis for
H_{1}(F^{\prime};{\mathbb{Z}})
such that the Gordon-Litherland matrix has block decomposition

(10)

M=\begin{bmatrix}A&*&0\\
*&B&J_{g}\\
0&J_{g}^{\mathsf{T}}&0\end{bmatrix},

where
A
is the
n\times n
matrix for the restriction of
\mathcal{G}_{F}
to
\operatorname{Ker}(H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{1}(\Sigma\times I;{\mathbb{Z}}))
, and

J_{g}=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_{g}\\
-I_{g}&0\end{bmatrix}

is the standard
2g\times 2g
symplectic matrix representing the intersection form on
\Sigma
. (Here
I_{g}
denotes the
g\times g
identity matrix.)

The matrix in (
10
) is unimodular congruent to one of the form:

M^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}A&0&0\\
0&B&J_{g}\\
0&J_{g}^{\mathsf{T}}&0\end{bmatrix}.

Therefore, by
Lemma

4.2
, it follows that
{\upbeta}(\varphi_{F^{\prime}})={\upbeta}(\varphi_{M})={\upbeta}(\varphi_{M^{\prime}})
.

A non-singular
{\mathbb{Z}}/4
quadratic form
\phi\colon V\to{\mathbb{Z}}/4
is said to be
metabolic
if there exists a half-dimensional subspace
H\subset V
such that
\phi
vanishes on
H
. (In
[
Mat86
, §4]
, this is defined as
split
.) By
[
Mat86
, Lemma 4.1]
, if
\phi
is metabolic, then

{\upbeta}(\phi)=0
. Since the Brown invariant is additive under orthogonal sum,
and since the quadratic form associated to

\begin{bmatrix}B&J_{g}\\
J_{g}^{\mathsf{T}}&0\end{bmatrix}

is clearly metabolic,
it follows that
{\upbeta}(\varphi_{M^{\prime}})={\upbeta}(\varphi_{A}).
However,
\varphi_{A}
is equal to the restricted
{\mathbb{Z}}/4
quadratic form
\varphi|_{\mathcal{K}_{F}},
and the result now follows.
∎

Now suppose
D\subset\Sigma
is a cellularly embedded, checkerboard colorable link diagram. Then the inclusion map
i\colon D\to\Sigma
induces a surjection
i_{*}\colon H_{1}(D;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{1}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{Z}}).
If
F
is a checkerboard surface, then the map
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{1}(\Sigma\times I;{\mathbb{Z}})
is surjective.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
D\subset\Sigma
be a cellularly embedded, checkerboard colorable link diagram with coloring
\xi
,
and let
F
be its black checkerboard surface. Let
\xi^{*}
be the opposite coloring and
F^{*}
its black checkerboard surface. (Thus
F^{*}
is the white surface for
\xi.
)
The two sets of Brown invariants are related by chromatic duality:

{\upbeta}_{F}(D)={\upbeta}_{\xi^{*}}(D)
and
{\upbeta}_{F^{*}}(D)={\upbeta}_{\xi}(D)
.

Proof.

The proof follows from
Theorem

4.9
and
[
BCK21
, Lemma 5.3]
.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

At first glance, it may seem that the switch of colorings in
Theorem

4.10
is the result of an incompatibility in the choice of conventions. However, it is unavoidable, and in fact it is an intrinsic aspect, and a curious one at that. This aspect is not readily apparent for classical links since the two sets of invariants are always equal in that case.

Given a non-split checkerboard colorable link
L\subset\Sigma\times I
with checkerboard surfaces
F
and
F^{*}
, then any spanning surface for
L
has Brown invariant equal to
{\upbeta}_{F}(L)
or
{\upbeta}_{F^{*}}(L)
.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
K\subset\Sigma\times I
be a knot with spanning surface
F\subset\Sigma\times I
. Assume
e(F,K)=0
and
\det(\mathcal{G}_{F})\not\equiv 0\text{ (mod $2$)}
. If
K
is slice, then
{\upbeta}_{F}(K)=0
.

Proof.

If
K
is slice, then
Theorem

3.2
implies that the Gordon-Litherland pairing is metabolic,
namely that
\mathcal{G}_{F}
vanishes on a half-dimensional subspace
U
of
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})
. It follows that
\varphi_{F}
must vanish on the image of
U
under the mod 2 projection

H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
. The image is
again a half-dimensional subspace of

H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
.

Theorem 1.20 (ix) in
[
Bro72
]
implies that the Brown invariant vanishes on any non-singular, metabolic quadratic space (see also
[
Mat86
, Lemma 4.1]
). Therefore,
{\upbeta}(\varphi_{F})=0
. Since
e(F,K)=0
, it follows that
{\upbeta}_{F}(K)=0
.
∎

4.6.
Mirror Images

In this section, we relate the
Brown invariants of a checkerboard colorable link in a thickened surface to those of its vertical and horizontal mirror images. We begin by considering orientation reversal on
L
.

If
L\subset\Sigma\times I
is an oriented link with spanning surface
F\subset\Sigma\times I
, then
{\upbeta}_{F}(-L)={\upbeta}_{F}(L),
where
-L
denotes the link with opposite orientation. Although
{\upbeta}_{F}(L)
is insensitive to orientation change, it is sensitive to a change of orientation on a single component. In fact, writing
{\upbeta}_{F}(L)={\upbeta}(\varphi_{F})+\frac{1}{2}e(F,L)
, this is evident from the formulas
e(F,L)=e(F)-\lambda(L)
and
\lambda(L)=\sum_{i\neq j}\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(K_{i},K_{j})
for

L=K_{1}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n}
.

Let
L\subset\Sigma\times I
be an oriented link. The image of
L
under the map
\phi\colon\Sigma\times I\to\Sigma\times I
given by
\phi(x,t)=(x,1-t)
is called the
vertical
mirror image of
L
and is denoted
L^{*}
.
Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma
be an orientation reversing homeomorphism and set

\psi\colon\Sigma\times I\to\Sigma\times I
to be the map given by
\psi(x,t)=(f(x),t)
.
The image of
L
under
\psi
is called the
horizontal
mirror image of
L
and is denoted
L^{\dagger}
.

If
F\subset\Sigma\times I
is a spanning surface for
L
, then

F^{*}=\phi(F)
is a spanning surface for
L^{*}
and

F^{\dagger}=\psi(F)
is a spanning surface for
L^{\dagger}
.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
L\subset\Sigma\times I
be a link with spanning surface
F\subset\Sigma\times I
.
Then the Brown invariant of the vertical and horizontal mirror images of
L
satisfy

\displaystyle{\upbeta}_{F^{*}}(L^{*})=-{\upbeta}_{F}(L),\;

and

\displaystyle\;{\upbeta}_{F^{\dagger}}(L^{\dagger})=-{\upbeta}_{F}(L).

Proof.

By
[
BCK21
, Proposition 5.7]
,
\mathcal{G}_{L^{*},F^{*}}=-\mathcal{G}_{L,F},
and
e(F^{*},L^{*})=-e(F,L).
The result now follows from
Definition

4.3
and
[
Bro72
, Theorem 1.20, (iii)]
. A similar argument shows that

\mathcal{G}_{L^{\dagger},F^{\dagger}}=-\mathcal{G}_{L,F},
and
e(F^{\dagger},L^{\dagger})=-e(F,L),
and the
formula for
{\upbeta}_{F^{\dagger}}(L^{\dagger})
follows.
∎

4.7.
Arf invariants of links

Let
L\subset\Sigma\times I
be an oriented, null-homologous link and
F\subset\Sigma\times I
a Seifert surface for
L
. Then the Gordon-Litherland pairing
\mathcal{G}_{F}
coincides with the symmetrized Seifert pairing. In particular,
\mathcal{G}_{F}(a,a)\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}
,
and its associated
{\mathbb{Z}}/4
-valued form
\varphi_{F}
is even. Therefore, the Brown invariant of
\varphi_{F}
is related to the Arf invariant of the ordinary quadratic form
q_{F}
by the formula
{\upbeta}(\varphi_{F})=4\operatorname{Arf}(q_{F})
.

Further, we have
e(F)=0
and
e(F,L)=-\lambda(L)=-\sum_{i\neq j}\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(K_{i},K_{j})
, the total linking number. (Here we write
L=K_{1}\cup\dots\cup K_{m}
as a union of its components.) Therefore, the link invariants are related by the formula

{\upbeta}_{F}(L)=4\operatorname{Arf}(q_{F})+\lambda(L).

In
[
CM21
]
, Chrisman and Mukherjee define Arf invariants for null-homologous knots in thickened surfaces. Indeed, for a knot
K\subset\Sigma\times I
with Seifert surface
F
, they define
q_{K,F}(x)\equiv\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(x^{+},x)\text{ (mod $2$)}
and show that it gives an ordinary quadratic form on
H_{1}(F;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
. Assuming that
q_{K,F}
is non-singular, they
use it to define the Arf invariant of
K
. Note that
q_{K,F}
is non-singular precisely when
\det(K,F)\not\equiv 0\text{ (mod $2$)}
.

We remark that, like the Brown invariants, the Arf invariant can be defined for singular, proper forms, i.e., the assumption that
q_{K,F}
is non-singular is not necessary. This is illustrated in
Example

4.15
below.
In general, the Brown invariant provides a way to define the Arf invariant for any oriented null-homologous link
L\subset\Sigma\times I
and Seifert surface
F\subset\Sigma\times I
.
Thus, the Brown invariants in
Section

4.3
recover and extend the Arf invariants introduced in
[
CM21
]
.

Proposition 4.14
.

If
K\subset\Sigma\times I
is a null-homologous knot with Seifert surface
F
,
and
\det(K,F)\not\equiv 0\text{ (mod $2$)}
, then

{\upbeta}_{F}(K)=4\operatorname{Arf}(q_{K,F}).

Proof.

By the definitions of
q_{K,F}
and
\varphi_{F}
, we see that
\varphi_{F}=2q_{K,F}
. The result then follows from
[
Bro72
, Theorem 1.20, (vii)]
.
∎

5.
Concordance of spanning surfaces

In this section, we introduce a notion of concordance for spanning surfaces of knots in thickened surfaces. This is based on
[
CM21
, Definition 4.1.1]
, which is the corresponding notion of concordance for Seifert surfaces.
We show that the knot signature and Brown invariant are invariant under concordance of spanning surfaces.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
be knots in
\Sigma_{0}\times I
and
\Sigma_{1}\times I
, respectively, with spanning surfaces
F_{0}\subset\Sigma_{0}\times I
and
F_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}\times I
.
So
F_{0},F_{1},
are unoriented surfaces with boundary
\partial F_{0}=K_{0}
and
\partial F_{1}=K_{1}
.

The spanning surfaces
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
are said to be
concordant
if there exist:

(i)

a compact oriented 3-manifold
W
with boundary
\partial W=-\Sigma_{0}\cup\Sigma_{1}
,

(ii)

a properly embedded annulus
A\subset W\times I
with boundary
\partial A=K_{0}\cup K_{1}
, and

(iii)

a compact unoriented 3-manifold
V\subset W\times I
with boundary
\partial V=F_{0}\cup A\cup F_{1}
.

If
K_{0}\subset\Sigma_{0}\times I
and
K_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}\times I
are knots with concordant spanning surfaces
F_{0}\subset\Sigma_{0}\times I
and
F_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}\times I
,
then the knots
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
are concordant. However, the converse is not true. In general, for a checkerboard colorable knot in a thickened surface
\Sigma\times I
with genus
g(\Sigma)\geq 1
, the spanning surfaces given by the black and white regions will not be concordant in the above sense.

The proof of the following lemma is similar to that of
Lemma

3.1
, and we omit it.

Lemma 5.2
.

There are generating sets
\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{g_{0}}\}
for
H_{1}(F_{0};{\mathbb{Z}})
and
\{\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\gamma^{\prime}_{g_{1}}\}
for
H_{1}(F_{1};{\mathbb{Z}})
,
such that
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m_{0}},\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\gamma^{\prime}_{m_{1}}
lie in the kernel of the map
H_{1}(F_{0};{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus H_{1}(F_{1};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{1}(V;{\mathbb{Q}}),
where
m_{0}+m_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(g_{0}+g_{1})
.

We use
Lemma

5.2
to prove the following result, which is an analogue of
Theorem

3.2
for two knots with concordant spanning surfaces.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
K_{0}\subset\Sigma_{0}\times I
and
K_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}\times I
be knots with spanning surfaces
F_{0}\subset\Sigma_{0}\times I
and
F_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}\times I
, respectively. If
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
are concordant and
n(K_{0},F_{0})=n(K_{1},F_{1})=0
, then

\sigma(K_{0},F_{0})=\sigma(K_{1},F_{1}).

Proof.

Define
\Theta
on
H_{1}(F_{0};{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus H_{1}(F_{1};{\mathbb{Z}})
as follows

\Theta((x_{0},x_{1}),(y_{0},y_{1}))=-\mathcal{G}_{F_{0}}(x_{0},y_{0})+\mathcal{G}_{F_{1}}(x_{1},y_{1}).

By
Lemma

5.2
, and similar to the proof of
Theorem

3.2
,
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m_{0}},\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\gamma^{\prime}_{m_{1}}

generate a totally isotropic subspace for
\Theta
. Since the nullity of
\Theta
is zero, it follows that

2(m_{0}+m_{1})=g_{0}+g_{1}\leq g_{0}+g_{1}-|\operatorname{sig}\Theta|,

so
0=\operatorname{sig}\Theta=-\operatorname{sig}\mathcal{G}_{F_{0}}+\operatorname{sig}\mathcal{G}_{F_{1}}
, which implies
\operatorname{sig}\mathcal{G}_{F_{0}}=\operatorname{sig}\mathcal{G}_{F_{1}}
.

Again, the self-intersection number of
E
vanishes. The self-intersection number of
E
equals

-\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(K_{0},K_{0}^{\prime})+\operatorname{\ell{\it k}}(K_{1},K_{1}^{\prime})=-e(F_{0},K_{0})+e(F_{1},K_{1})=0.

It follows that
e(F_{0},K_{0})=e(F_{1},K_{1})
, and
\sigma(K_{0},F_{0})=\sigma(K_{1},F_{1}).

∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
K_{0}\subset\Sigma_{0}\times I
and
K_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}\times I
be knots with spanning surfaces
F_{0}\subset\Sigma_{0}\times I
and
F_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}\times I
, respectively. If
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
are concordant and
\det(\mathcal{G}_{F_{0}}),\det(\mathcal{G}_{F_{1}})\not\equiv 0\text{ (mod $2$)}
, then

{\upbeta}_{F_{0}}(K_{0})={\upbeta}_{F_{1}}(K_{1}).

Proof.

Consider the mod 2 reduction of the form
\Theta
defined in the proof of
Theorem

5.3
. Now define
\Phi:H_{1}(F_{0};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\oplus H_{1}(F_{1};{\mathbb{Z}}/2)\to{\mathbb{Z}}/4
, as
\Phi([a],[b])\equiv-\mathcal{G}_{F_{0}}(a,a)+\mathcal{G}_{F_{1}}(b,b)\text{ (mod $4$)}
. We can check that
\Phi
is a quadratic enhancement, and we can define
{\upbeta}(\Phi)
.

Similar to
Theorem

4.12
, we deduce that
{\upbeta}(\Phi)=0
. Notice that the Brown invariant is additive, and that
{\upbeta}(-\varphi)\equiv-{\upbeta}(\varphi)\text{ (mod $8$)}
. The result now follows.
∎

The next result shows if two knots in thickened surfaces are concordant, then under some mild hypotheses, their spanning surfaces are concordant. As a result, Theorems
5.3
and
5.4
hold more generally for some concordant knots.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
K_{0}\subset\Sigma_{0}\times I
and
K_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}\times I
be knots with spanning surfaces
F_{0}\subset\Sigma_{0}\times I
and
F_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}\times I
, respectively.
If
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
are concordant, then there exists an oriented 3-manifold
W
with
\partial W=\Sigma_{1}\sqcup-\Sigma_{0}
and a properly embedded annulus
A
in
W\times I
with
\partial A=K_{0}\sqcup K_{1}
. Let
E=F_{0}\cup A\cup F_{1}
. If

[E]=0
in
H_{2}(W\times I;{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
, then
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
are concordant.

Proof.

We can arrange that
e(F_{0},K_{0})=e(F_{1},K_{1})
, so
e(E)=0
. Since
[E]=0,
then
Theorem

2.1
implies that there exists a compact 3-manifold
V\subset W\times I
with
\partial V=E
.
The result now follows.
∎

In closing we mention a few open problems and questions for future research.

It would be interesting to develop a theory of algebraic concordance for checkerboard colorable knots in thickened surfaces. Here, we expect that the notion of concordance of spanning surfaces could be useful, cf.
[
CM21
]
. What kind of torsion does this new algebraic concordance group contain?

It would also be interesting to prove more general results about concordance invariants of the signature and Brown invariants for links in thickened surfaces.
A promising approach would be to develop an interpretation of the Brown invariants in terms of Pin-structures and show they are invariants of Pin bordism, cf.
[
KT90
]
.

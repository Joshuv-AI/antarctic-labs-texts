1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,|\,x_{1}>0\}
be the half-space domain,
N\geq 5
and
0\leq s<4
. In this work, we establish the
existence of nontrivial weak solutions for the following doubly critical problem:

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta^{2}u-\frac{\gamma}{|x|^{4}}u=|u|^{2^{\star}_{0}-2}u+\frac{|u|^{2_{s}^{\star}-2}u}{|x|^{s}}&\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N},\\
u=\Delta u=0&\hbox{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N},\end{array}\right.

where
\Delta=\hbox{div}\left(\nabla\right)
is the Laplacian,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
2_{s}^{\star}:=\frac{2(N-s)}{N-4}
is the critical Hardy–Sobolev exponent. In order to write a variational formulation, the relevant space is the following: given
\Omega
be a smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, define

\tilde{H}_{0}(\Omega)=\hbox{ completion of }\{u\in C^{2}_{c}(\overline{\Omega})\hbox{ s.t. }u_{|\partial\Omega}\equiv 0\}\hbox{ for the norm }u\mapsto\|\Delta u\|_{2}.

Note that
\tilde{H}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the usual Beppo-Levi space
D^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We say that
u\in\tilde{H}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})
is a weak solution of (
1
), if

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}}\Delta u\Delta\varphi\,dx-\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}}\frac{u\varphi}{|x|^{4}}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}}\left|u\right|^{2_{0}^{\star}-2}u\varphi\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}}\frac{\left|u\right|^{2_{s}^{\star}-2}u\varphi}{|x|^{s}}\,dx,

for all
\varphi\in\tilde{H}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})
. For any
u\in\tilde{H}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})
, Sobolev’s embedding yields
u\in L^{2_{0}^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})\cap L^{2_{s}^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N},|x|^{-s})
(see (
4
) below), and therefore the definition of weak solution makes sense.

The existence of weak solution to (
1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
has been studied by Filippucci-Pucci-Robert
[
FPR
]
for the p–Laplacian. Equations like (
1
) have been studied for the Fractional Laplacian (see Ghoussoub-Shakerian
[
GS
]
) and the bi-laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(see Bhakta
[
B1
,
B2
]
and Bhakta-Musina
[
BM
]
).

In the present paper, we tackle this type of nonlinear singular problems on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
when
0\in\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
. When
0\in\partial\Omega
where
\Omega
is a smooth domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
(N\geq 3)
, the existence of solutions for the corresponding 2nd order with only nonlinearity have been studied by Ghoussoub–Kang
[
GK
]
and studied by Chern-Lin
[
CL
]
and Ghoussoub-Robert
[
GRGAFA
,
GR
]
. For non-smooth domains modeled on cones, we refer to the more recent works of Cheikh-Ali
[
HCA1
,
HCA2
]
.

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 8
,
0<s<4
and
0<\gamma<\frac{(N^{2}-4)^{2}}{16}
. Then, there exists a nontrivial weak solution of (
1
).

Let us discuss the hypothesis of the theorem. Our problem depends of the value of the constant
\gamma
. From here, given an arbitrary domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 5
, we define the Hardy-Rellich constant

(2)

\gamma_{H}(\Omega,X):=\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}\left|\Delta u\right|^{2}\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{4}}\,dx};u\in X\backslash\{0\}\right\},

for a suitable space
X
. There are several references around this constant. A first version of this Hardy inequality has been introduced by Rellich in 1953
[
R1
,
R2
]
(see also Mitidieri
[
EM
]
), and reads

\gamma_{H}(\mathbb{R}^{N},C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))=\frac{N^{2}(N-4)^{2}}{16}.

When
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
0\in\Omega
, Perez-Llamos-Primo
[
PP
]
proved that

\gamma_{H}(\Omega,H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))=\frac{N^{2}(N-4)^{2}}{16}.

In particular, the value of the constant is independent of the domain as long as
0
is an interior point.

The situation is different when
0\in\partial\Omega
. Let us consider cones. For any regular domain
\Sigma
in the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, we define the cone

C_{\Sigma}:=\{r\sigma\,|\,r>0,\sigma\in\Sigma\}.

Caldiroli-Musina
[
CM
]
proved that

\gamma_{H}(C_{\Sigma},X_{0}(C_{\Sigma}))=\hbox{dist}\left(-\frac{N(N-4)}{4},\Lambda(\Sigma)\right)^{2},

where,

X_{0}(\Omega):=\{u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})\hbox{ s.t. }u_{|\partial\Omega}\equiv 0\},

and
\Lambda(\Sigma)
is the spectrum of the Laplace–Beltrami operator on
\Sigma
. We denote that, for instance:

—

If
\Sigma=\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, then
C_{\Sigma}=\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{H}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\},X_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\}))

\displaystyle=\min_{k\in\{0,1,...\}}\left|\frac{N(N-4)}{4}+k(N-2+k)\right|^{2}=\frac{N^{2}(N-4)^{2}}{16}.

—

If
\Sigma
is the half-sphere
S^{N-1}_{+}
, then
C_{\Sigma}=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
. Therefore, we have

(3)

\gamma_{H}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N},X_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}))=\min_{k\in\{1,2,...\}}\left|\frac{N(N-4)}{4}+k(N-2+k)\right|^{2}=\frac{(N^{2}-4)^{2}}{16}.

In the sequel, we write for convenience

\gamma_{H,+}:=\gamma_{H}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N},\tilde{H}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})).

We will prove in Section
2
that

\gamma_{H,+}=\gamma_{H}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N},X_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}))=\frac{(N^{2}-4)^{2}}{16}.

In order to tackle the nonlinear problem (
1
), let us consider the Sobolev inequality

(4)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2_{s}^{\star}}}{|x|^{s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2_{s}^{\star}}}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}\,dx\hbox{ for all }u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Let us fix a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Interpolating the Hardy inequality (
2
) and the Sobolev inequality (
4
) and using that
X_{0}(\Omega)\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, given
s\in[0,4]
, we get the existence of
C(N,s,\Omega)>0
such that

(5)

\left(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{-s}|u|^{2_{s}^{\star}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2_{s}^{\star}}}\leq C(N,s,\Omega)\int_{\Omega}\left|\Delta u\right|^{2}\,dx\hbox{ for any }u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).

Using again (
2
), for any
s\in[0,4]
and any
\gamma<\gamma_{H}(\Omega,\tilde{H}_{0}(\Omega))
, we get the Hardy-Sobolev inequality

\left(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{-s}|u|^{2_{s}^{\star}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2_{s}^{\star}}}\leq C\,\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\Delta u\right|^{2}-\gamma\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{4}}\right)\,dx\hbox{ for all }u\in\tilde{H}_{0}(\Omega).

Let us define

(6)

Q_{\gamma,s}(\Omega)=\inf\Big\{I_{\gamma,s}^{\Omega}(u)/u\in\tilde{H}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}\Big\},\hbox{ with }I_{\gamma,s}^{\Omega}(u):=\frac{\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\Delta u\right|^{2}-\gamma\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{4}}\right)\,dx}{\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2_{s}^{\star}}}{|x|^{s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2_{s}^{\star}}}}.

In order to prove the existence of weak solutions for (
1
), we need extremals for the best constant
Q_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})
in (
6
), that is
u\in\tilde{H}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})\setminus\{0\}
such that
I_{\gamma,s}^{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}}(u)=Q_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})
. If
u\in\tilde{H}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})\backslash\{0\}
is an extremal for
Q_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})>0
, then, up to a constant factor,
u
is a solution to the following Euler-Lagrange equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\Delta^{2}u-\frac{\gamma}{|x|^{4}}u=\frac{|u|^{2_{s}^{\star}-2}u}{|x|^{s}}&\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N},\\
u=\Delta u=0&\hbox{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}.\end{array}\right.

The question of the existence of extremals is answered in the following theorem that we prove in Section
3
:

Theorem 1.2
.

For
s\in[0,4)
and
\gamma<\gamma_{H,+}
, we have that

(a)

If
\{s>0\}
or
\{s=0,\gamma>0,N\geq 8\}
, then there are extremals for
Q_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})
.

(b)

If
\{s=0\hbox{ and }\gamma\leq 0\}
, there are no extremals for
Q_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})
.

(c)

If there are no extremals for
Q_{\gamma,0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})
, then
Q_{\gamma,0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})=S_{N}
,

where

(7)

S_{N}:=\inf_{u\in\tilde{H}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\Delta u\right|^{2}\,dx}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{0}^{\star}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2_{0}^{\star}}}}.

The remaining case, that is
\{s=0\,,\,\gamma>0\hbox{, and }N=5,6,7\}
, is not clear. This limitation is due to the lack of localization of the
L^{2}-
norm in the computation (
3
).

The study of fourth-order Hardy-Sobolev problem with a singularity on the boundary of a smooth domain is the object of the work
[
HCA3
]
.

With all these elements, we will construct weak solutions for the doubly critical problem (
1
) by finding critical points of corresponding functional on
\tilde{H}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})
. The method to obtain this critical points is via the Mountain-Pass theorem of Ambrosetti and Rabinowitz. Since (
1
) is invariant under the conformal one parameter transformation group:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cccc}T_{r}:&\tilde{H}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})&\to&\tilde{H}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N})\\
&u&\mapsto&T_{r}[u]:=\{x\mapsto r^{\frac{N-4}{2}}u(rx)\}\end{array}\right\},\hbox{ where }r>0,

then the Mountain-Pass theorem will not yield critical points, but only
Palais-Smale sequences. We will use the strategy of Filippucci-Pucci-Robert
[
FPR
]
. As in
[
FPR
]
, the main difficulty will be the asymptotic competition between the energy carried by the two critical nonlinearities. Hence, the crucial point here is to balance the competition to avoid the domination of one term over the other. Otherwise, there is vanishing of the weakest one, and we get solution for the same equation but with only one critical nonlinearity. To deal with this problem, we will choose a Palais-Smale sequence at a minimax energy level. In such a way, after a careful analysis of concentration, we will show that there is a balance between the energies of the two nonlinearities mentioned above, and therefore none can dominate the other. This will yield a solution to (
1
).

Profile of solutions and study the value of Hardy Rellich Constant
\gamma_{H,+}

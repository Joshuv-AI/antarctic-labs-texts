Solution of the Optimal Control Problem for the Cahn-Hilliard Equation Using Finite Difference Approximation Thanks: Submitted.
Gobinda Garai & Bankim C. Mandal

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the designing, analyzing and implementing linear and nonlinear discretization scheme for the distributed optimal control problem (OCP) with the Cahn-Hilliard (CH) equation as constrained. We propose three difference schemes to approximate and investigate the solution behaviour of the OCP for the CH equation. We present the convergence analysis of the proposed discretization. We verify our findings by presenting numerical experiments.

1.
Introduction

In this work, we consider the following distributed optimal control problem (OCP)

\min\limits_{u(x,t)\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}J(y,u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(y(x,t)-\widehat{y}(x,t)\right)^{2}dxdt+\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)^{2}dxdt,

(1.1)

subject to the Cahn-Hilliard (CH) equation

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}=\Delta f(y)-\epsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}y+u,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
\partial_{\nu}y=0=\partial_{\nu}(\Delta y),&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T],\\
u(x,0)=u_{0},\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}(d=1,2)
is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
\nu
is the unit outward normal,
u\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
is the control variable,
\widehat{y}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
is the target state,
\lambda>0
is a regularization parameter,
0<\epsilon\ll 1
and
f(y)=y^{3}-y
. The purpose of the optimization is to determine the control function
u(x,t)
in such a way that the state
y(x,t)
gets as closely as possible to a given desired state
\widehat{y}(x,t)
. Here we follow optimize-then-discretize approach to discretize the OCP (
1.1
) - (
1.2
).

The existence and uniqueness of OCP (
1.1
) - (
1.2
) follows from the standard variational arguments
[
14
,
18
]
, which can be described by its first order optimality system, see [Troltzsch]. The first order optimality system yields the state equation (
1.2
), the adjoint equation

\begin{cases}-\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=f^{\prime}(y)\Delta p-\epsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}p+\widehat{y}-y,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
\partial_{\nu}p=0=\partial_{\nu}(\Delta p),&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T],\\
p(x,T)=0,\end{cases}

(1.3)

and the optimality condition

p(x,t)=\lambda u(x,t),(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T].

(1.4)

The CH equation, represented by Equation (1) without the control term
u
, is a mathematical model commonly used to describe the evolution of a binary melted alloy below its critical temperature as referenced in
[
1
,
2
]
. Numerous research studies have been devoted to developing numerical schemes for approximating the solution of the CH equation. These schemes often employ finite difference or finite element methods, with either Dirichlet boundary conditions
[
5
,
10
]
or Neumann boundary conditions
[
8
,
11
,
16
,
9
,
7
,
17
,
4
,
3
]
, among others. For further insights into the numerical approaches used for solving the CH equation can be found in
[
15
]
.

The solution of the CH equation encompasses two separate dynamics: phase separation, characterized by rapid changes over time, and phase coarsening, which occurs at a slower pace. During the initial stages, the formation of fine-scale phase regions takes place, with interfaces of width
\epsilon
separating them. On the other hand, during phase coarsening, the solution tends to converge towards an equilibrium state. The OCP associated with the CH equation becomes crucial in situations where there is a desire to exert influence over the phase separation or coarsening behaviour, or to attain specific concentration profiles. Within this context, the OCP plays an important role by providing a framework to actively control and shape the dynamics of phase separation or coarsening in order to achieve desired outcomes.
The following assumptions are made regarding the nonlinear terms:

\max_{y}|f^{\prime}(y)|\leq M,\;\max_{y}|\widetilde{f}^{\prime}(y)|\leq\widetilde{M}

(1.5)

where
M,\widetilde{M}
are non-negative constant and
\widetilde{f}(y)=y^{3}
.

We now talk about a few studies that relate OCP to the CH equation. The authors of
[
19
,
13
]
took into account the OCP for the CH equation and presented the existence results. The studies in
[
20
,
6
]
are concerned with OCP for the viscous CH equation and their existence results. You may see a study for OCP with convective CH as constraints in
[
21
]
. The OCP for the CH equation with state constraint has been studied in
[
22
]
. However, the above stated body of works do not have any numerical scheme to solve the OCP. This is, as far as we are aware, the first attempt at constructing a numerical framework for the OCP of the CH equation. In order to discretize continuous spatial and temporal variables, we employ the finite difference approach.

2.
Notation and Difference Schemes

We propose the finite difference scheme for (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) in one spatial dimension, while an extension to higher spatial dimension is straight forward. To discretize the problem (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) in the spatial domain
\Omega=(a,b)
, we partitioned the domain uniformly by taking mesh size
h=(b-a)/(N-1)
, where
N
is the number of spatial grid point. Then the discrete domain is
\Omega_{h}=\{(x_{i-1},x_{i}):x_{i}=a+(i-1)h,i=1,2,...,N\}.
Let
\delta_{t}=T/N_{t}
be the uniform time step corresponding to discrete temporal variable
t_{n}=n\delta_{t}
for
n=0,1,...,N_{t}
, where
N_{t}
is any positive integer. For any function
z
define on
\Omega_{h}
, we denote
z_{i}=z(x_{i})
and define the following difference operators

\nabla_{h}^{+}z_{i}=\frac{z_{i+1}-z_{i}}{h},\;\nabla_{h}^{-}z_{i}=\frac{z_{i}-z_{i-1}}{h},\;\Delta_{h}=\nabla_{h}^{+}\nabla_{h}^{-}=\nabla_{h}^{-}\nabla_{h}^{+},\;\Delta_{h}^{2}=\Delta_{h}\Delta_{h}.

Now we define the discrete
L^{2}
-inner product as

(y,z)_{h}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}y_{i}z_{i}
for any function
y,z
, defined on
\Omega_{h}
. Then the corresponding discrete
L^{2}
-norm is given by
\|z\|_{h}=\sqrt{(z,z)_{h}}
. We also define discrete
H^{1}
-seminorm,
H^{2}
-seminorm and maximum-norm for any grid function
z
in
\Omega_{h}
as

|z|_{1,h}^{2}=h\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}(\nabla_{h}^{-}z_{i})^{2},\;|z|_{2,h}^{2}=h\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}(\Delta_{h}z_{i})^{2},\;\|z\|_{\infty,h}=\sup\limits_{i}|z_{i}|.

Let
Y_{i}^{n}=Y(x_{i},t_{n}),P_{i}^{n}=P(x_{i},t_{n})
and
U_{i}^{n}=U(x_{i},t_{n})
for
i=1,2,...,N
and
n=0,1,...,N_{t}
. Then to get the approximate solution of (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) we discretize the state equation (
1.2
) as

\frac{Y_{i}^{n+1}-Y_{i}^{n}}{\delta_{t}}=\Delta_{h}f(Y_{i}^{n+1})-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}Y_{i}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{\lambda}P_{i}^{n},

(2.1)

and the adjoint equation (
1.3
) as

-\frac{P_{i}^{n+1}-P_{i}^{n}}{\delta_{t}}=f^{\prime}(Y_{i}^{n})\Delta_{h}P_{i}^{n}-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}P_{i}^{n}+\widehat{Y}_{i}^{n+1}-Y_{i}^{n+1}.

(2.2)

Observe that we made a substitution of control variable
U_{i}^{n}
by
\frac{1}{\lambda}P_{i}^{n}
in (
2.1
) using the relation in (
1.4
). The described scheme (
2.1
) - (
2.2
) is undoubtedly nonlinear, with the nonlinearity originating from (
2.1
). We call the scheme (
2.1
) - (
2.2
) as
\textbf{S}1
. Next we display two possible linear approximation to the state equation (
1.2
). First we consider the following linear scheme for the state equation

\frac{Y_{i}^{n+1}-Y_{i}^{n}}{\delta_{t}}=\Delta_{h}((Y_{i}^{n})^{2}Y_{i}^{n+1})-\Delta_{h}Y_{i}^{n}-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}Y_{i}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{\lambda}P_{i}^{n}.

(2.3)

We call the state approximation scheme (
2.3
), along with adjoint approximation scheme (
2.2
) as
\textbf{S}2
. Another way to discretize the state equation is

\frac{Y_{i}^{n+1}-Y_{i}^{n}}{\delta_{t}}=\Delta_{h}(Y_{i}^{n})^{3}-3\Delta_{h}Y_{i}^{n}+2\Delta_{h}Y_{i}^{n+1}-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}Y_{i}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{\lambda}P_{i}^{n}.

(2.4)

We call the state approximation scheme (
2.4
), along with adjoint approximation scheme (
2.2
) as
\textbf{S}3
.

3.
Convergence of the Schemes

We examine the convergence of the finite difference scheme (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) in this section. Discrete Gronwall’s inequality, which will be used extensively in our convergence estimations, was previously discussed in
[
12
]

Lemma 3.1

(Discrete Gronwall inequality)
.

Let
\tau,Q
and
a_{n},b_{n},c_{n},d_{n}
be non-negative numbers for integers
n\geq 0
such that

a_{n}+\tau\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}b_{k}\leq\tau\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}d_{k}a_{k}+\tau\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}+Q.

Suppose that
\tau d_{k}<1,\forall k
and set
\sigma_{k}=1/(1-\tau d_{k})
. Then the following holds for
n\geq 0

a_{n}+\tau\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}b_{k}\leq\exp\left(\tau\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\sigma_{k}d_{k}\right)\left[\tau\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}+Q\right].

Theorem 3.2

(Convergence of the scheme
\textbf{S}1
)
.

Let
y(x,t)
and
p(x,t)
be sufficiently smooth functions. For
\delta_{t}
sufficiently small, the finite difference scheme (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) is first order in time and second order in space convergent, i.e.,

\max\limits_{n}\left\{\parallel y^{n}-Y^{n}\parallel_{\infty,h}+\parallel p^{n}-P^{n}\parallel_{\infty,h}\right\}\leq C(\delta_{t}+h^{2}).

Proof.

Using Taylor expansion we observe that the exact solution
y_{i}^{n}=y(x_{i},t_{n})
and

satisfy the following equations

\frac{y_{i}^{n+1}-y_{i}^{n}}{\delta_{t}}=\Delta_{h}f(y_{i}^{n+1})-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}y_{i}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{\lambda}p_{i}^{n}+F_{i}^{n},

(3.1)

-\frac{p_{i}^{n+1}-p_{i}^{n}}{\delta_{t}}=f^{\prime}(y_{i}^{n})\Delta_{h}p_{i}^{n}-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}p_{i}^{n}+\widehat{y}_{i}^{n+1}-y_{i}^{n+1}+G_{i}^{n},

(3.2)

where

and

denote the truncation error, which satisfy the following for some positive constants

\max\limits_{i,n}|F_{i}^{n}|\leq c_{1}(\delta_{t}+h^{2}),\;\;\max\limits_{i,n}|G_{i}^{n}|\leq c_{2}(\delta_{t}+h^{2}).

(3.3)

Let us define the error

and
\mathfrak{e}_{i}^{n}=p_{i}^{n}-P_{i}^{n}
. Then taking difference between (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) and (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) yields

\frac{e_{i}^{n+1}-e_{i}^{n}}{\delta_{t}}=\Delta_{h}\left(f(y_{i}^{n+1})-f(Y_{i}^{n+1})\right)-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}e_{i}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathfrak{e}_{i}^{n}+F_{i}^{n},

(3.4)

-\frac{\mathfrak{e}_{i}^{n+1}-\mathfrak{e}_{i}^{n}}{\delta_{t}}=f^{\prime}(y_{i}^{n})\Delta_{h}p_{i}^{n}-f^{\prime}(Y_{i}^{n})\Delta_{h}P_{i}^{n}-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}\mathfrak{e}_{i}^{n}-e_{i}^{n+1}+G_{i}^{n}.

(3.5)

Taking the inner product of (
3.4
) and
e^{n+1}
yields

\left(\frac{e^{n+1}-e^{n}}{\delta_{t}},e^{n+1}\right)_{h}=\left(\Delta_{h}\left(f(y^{n+1})-f(Y^{n+1})\right),e^{n+1}\right)_{h}-\epsilon^{2}|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\mathfrak{e}^{n},e^{n+1}\right)_{h}+\left(F^{n},e^{n+1}\right)_{h}.

(3.6)

Using the differentiability of

\left(\frac{e^{n+1}-e^{n}}{\delta_{t}},e^{n+1}\right)_{h}\geq\frac{\|e^{n+1}\|_{h}^{2}-\|e^{n}\|_{h}^{2}}{2\delta_{t}}
on (

\frac{\|e^{n+1}\|_{h}^{2}-\|e^{n}\|_{h}^{2}}{2\delta_{t}}\leq M\|e^{n+1}\|_{h}|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}-\epsilon^{2}|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\mathfrak{e}^{n}\|_{h}\|e^{n+1}\|_{h}+\|F^{n}\|_{h}\|e^{n+1}\|_{h}.

(3.7)

Using Young’s inequality and (
3.3
) on (
3.7

\frac{\|e^{n+1}\|_{h}^{2}-\|e^{n}\|_{h}^{2}}{2\delta_{t}}\leq\underbrace{\left(\frac{M^{2}}{4\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}_{\alpha}\|e^{n+1}\|_{h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\mathfrak{e}^{n}\|_{h}^{2}+\frac{c_{1}}{2}(\delta_{t}+h^{2})^{2}.

(3.8)

From (

\left(1-2\delta_{t}\alpha\right)\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}^{2}\leq\parallel e^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{\lambda}\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}+c_{1}\delta_{t}(\delta_{t}+h^{2})^{2}.

(3.9)

For

An application of Gronwall’s inequality for sufficiently small

Taking now the inner product of (
3.5

) and (
3.20
) we have

|\mathfrak{e}^{n}|_{1,h}\leq C(\delta_{t}+h^{2}).

(3.35)

Then using (
3.20
), (
3.28
) and (
3.35
) in discrete Sobolev’s embedding theorem, we derive that

\parallel{e}^{n}\parallel_{\infty,h}+\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{n}\parallel_{\infty,h}\leq C(\delta_{t}+h^{2}).

Hence the theorem.

Theorem 3.3

(Convergence of the scheme
\textbf{S}2
)
.

Let
y(x,t)
and
p(x,t)
be sufficiently smooth functions. For
\delta_{t}
sufficiently small, the finite difference scheme (
2.3
)-(
2.2
) is first order in time and second order in space convergent, i.e.,

\max\limits_{n}\left\{\parallel y^{n}-Y^{n}\parallel_{\infty,h}+\parallel p^{n}-P^{n}\parallel_{\infty,h}\right\}\leq C(\delta_{t}+h^{2}).

Proof.

We provide an estimate that is consistent with the linear state approximation scheme (
2.3
), and for an estimate that is consistent with discrete adjoint, we turn to Theorem
3.2
. Using Taylor expansion we can see that the exact solution
y_{i}^{n}=y(x_{i},t_{n})
and
p_{i}^{n}=p(x_{i},t_{n})
satisfy the following equations

\frac{y_{i}^{n+1}-y_{i}^{n}}{\delta_{t}}=\Delta_{h}(y_{i}^{n})^{2}y_{i}^{n+1}-\Delta_{h}y_{i}^{n}-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}y_{i}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{\lambda}p_{i}^{n}+\widetilde{F}_{i}^{n},

(3.36)

where
\widetilde{F}_{i}^{n}
denotes the truncation error, which satisfies the following for some positive constant
c_{3}
,

\max\limits_{i,n}|\widetilde{F}_{i}^{n}|\leq c_{3}(\delta_{t}+h^{2}).

(3.37)

Let us define the error
e_{i}^{n}=y_{i}^{n}-Y_{i}^{n}
and
\mathfrak{e}_{i}^{n}=p_{i}^{n}-P_{i}^{n}
. Then taking the difference between (
3.36
) and (
2.3
) yields

\frac{e_{i}^{n+1}-e_{i}^{n}}{\delta_{t}}=\Delta_{h}\left((y_{i}^{n})^{2}y_{i}^{n+1}-(Y_{i}^{n})^{2}Y_{i}^{n+1}\right)-\Delta_{h}e_{i}^{n}-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}e_{i}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathfrak{e}_{i}^{n}+\widetilde{F}_{i}^{n}.

(3.38)

Taking the inner product of (
3.38
) and
e^{n+1}
, we get

\left(\frac{e^{n+1}-e^{n}}{\delta_{t}},e^{n+1}\right)_{h}=\left(\Delta_{h}\left((y^{n})^{2}y^{n+1}-(Y^{n})^{2}Y^{n+1}\right),e^{n+1}\right)-\left(\Delta_{h}e^{n},e^{n+1}\right)_{h}\\
-\epsilon^{2}|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\mathfrak{e}^{n},e^{n+1}\right)_{h}+\left(\widetilde{F}^{n},e^{n+1}\right)_{h}.

(3.39)

Observe that

\left(\Delta_{h}\left((y^{n})^{2}y^{n+1}-(Y^{n})^{2}Y^{n+1}\right),e^{n+1}\right)\leq\left(|\widetilde{f}^{\prime}(y^{n})+\widetilde{f}^{\prime}(Y^{n})||e^{n+1}|,\Delta_{h}e^{n+1}\right)\leq 2\widetilde{M}\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}.

Using the above estimate, Young’s inequality and (
3.37
) on (
3.39
) we obtain

\frac{\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}^{2}-\parallel e^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}}{2\delta_{t}}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}^{2}\leq\underbrace{\left(\frac{4\widetilde{M}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}_{\widetilde{\alpha}}\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}^{2}\\
+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}|e^{n}|_{2,h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{c_{3}}{2}(\delta_{t}+h^{2})^{2}.

(3.40)

From (
3.40
) we have

\left(1-2\delta_{t}\widetilde{\alpha}\right)\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\delta_{t}\left(|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}^{2}-|e^{n}|_{2,h}^{2}\right)\leq\parallel e^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{\lambda}\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}+c_{3}\delta_{t}(\delta_{t}+h^{2})^{2}.

(3.41)

For
\delta_{t}<\frac{1}{2\widetilde{\alpha}}
in (
3.41
) and taking the sum over
n
we get

\parallel e^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\left(1-2\delta_{t}\widetilde{\alpha}\right)}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\parallel e^{i}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{\lambda\left(1-2\delta_{t}\widetilde{\alpha}\right)}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{i}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{c_{3}T}{\left(1-2\delta_{t}\widetilde{\alpha}\right)}(\delta_{t}+h^{2})^{2}.

(3.42)

Applying Gronwall’s inequality on (
3.42
) for sufficiently small
\delta_{t}
yields

\parallel e^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}\leq C\delta_{t}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{i}\parallel_{h}^{2}+C(\delta_{t}+h^{2})^{2}.

(3.43)

Adding (
3.18
) (the error estimates in Theorem
3.2
for adjoint equation) and (
3.43
), and employing Gronwall’s inequality for sufficiently small
\delta_{t}
we have

\parallel e^{n}\parallel_{h}+\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{n}\parallel_{h}\leq C(\delta_{t}+h^{2}).

(3.44)

Next we take the inner product of (
3.38
) and
\partial_{t}e^{n}:=\frac{e^{n+1}-e^{n}}{\delta_{t}}
, that yields

\displaystyle\parallel\partial_{t}e^{n}\parallel^{2}_{h}=

\displaystyle\left(\Delta_{h}\left((y^{n})^{2}y^{n+1}-(Y^{n})^{2}Y^{n+1}\right),\partial_{t}e^{n}\right)-\left(\Delta_{h}e^{n},\partial_{t}e^{n}\right)_{h}

(3.45)

\displaystyle-\epsilon^{2}\left(\Delta_{h}e^{n+1},\partial_{t}(\Delta_{h}e^{n})\right)_{h}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\mathfrak{e}^{n},\partial_{t}e^{n}\right)_{h}+\left(\widetilde{F}^{n},\partial_{t}e^{n}\right)_{h},

\displaystyle\leq 2\widetilde{M}\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}\parallel\partial_{t}(\Delta_{h}e^{n})\parallel_{h}+|e^{n}|_{2,h}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\parallel\partial_{t}e^{n}\parallel^{2}_{h}

\displaystyle-\epsilon^{2}\left(\Delta_{h}e^{n+1},\partial_{t}(\Delta_{h}e^{n})\right)_{h}+\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}}\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\parallel\partial_{t}e^{n}\parallel^{2}_{h}+\parallel\widetilde{F}^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\parallel\partial_{t}e^{n}\parallel^{2}_{h}.

Using (
3.37
) and (
3.20
) on (
3.45
) we get

\epsilon^{2}\frac{|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}^{2}-|e^{n}|_{2,h}^{2}}{2\delta_{t}}\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}\frac{|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}^{2}+|e^{n}|_{2,h}^{2}}{\delta_{t}}+|e^{n}|_{2,h}^{2}+C(\delta_{t}+h^{2})^{2}.

(3.46)

From (
3.46
) we have

\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{3\epsilon^{2}}{2}+2\delta_{t}\right)|e^{n}|_{2,h}^{2}+2\delta_{t}C(\delta_{t}+h^{2})^{2}.

(3.47)

Taking the sum over
n
on (
3.47
) we get

\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}|e^{n}|_{2,h}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{3\epsilon^{2}}{2}+2\delta_{t}\right)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|e^{i}|_{2,h}^{2}+2TC(\delta_{t}+h^{2})^{2}.

(3.48)

Applying Gronwall’s inequality on (
3.48
) we have

|e^{n}|_{2,h}\leq C(\delta_{t}+h^{2}).

(3.49)

Using (
3.44
) and (
3.49
) we obtain

|e^{n}|_{1,h}\leq C(\delta_{t}+h^{2}).

(3.50)

Now using (
3.44
), (
3.50
) and (
3.35
) in discrete Sobolev’s embedding theorem we get our result.
∎

Theorem 3.4

(Convergence of the scheme
\textbf{S}3
)
.

Let
y(x,t)
and
p(x,t)
be sufficiently smooth functions. For
\delta_{t}
sufficiently small, the finite difference scheme (
2.4
)-(
2.2
) is first order in time and second order in space convergent, i.e.,

\max\limits_{n}\left\{\parallel y^{n}-Y^{n}\parallel_{\infty,h}+\parallel p^{n}-P^{n}\parallel_{\infty,h}\right\}\leq C(\delta_{t}+h^{2}).

Proof.

We provide an estimate that is consistent with the linear state approximation scheme (
2.3
), and for an estimate that is consistent with discrete adjoint, we turn to Theorem
3.2
. Using Taylor expansion we can see that the exact solution
y_{i}^{n}=y(x_{i},t_{n})
and
p_{i}^{n}=p(x_{i},t_{n})
satisfy the following equation

\frac{y_{i}^{n+1}-y_{i}^{n}}{\delta_{t}}=\Delta_{h}(y_{i}^{n})^{3}-3\Delta_{h}y_{i}^{n}+2\Delta_{h}y_{i}^{n+1}-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}y_{i}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{\lambda}p_{i}^{n}+\widehat{F}_{i}^{n},

(3.51)

where
\widehat{F}_{i}^{n}
denotes the truncation error, which satisfies the following for some positive constant
c_{4}

\max\limits_{i,n}|\widehat{F}_{i}^{n}|\leq c_{4}(\delta_{t}+h^{2}).

(3.52)

Let us define the error
e_{i}^{n}=y_{i}^{n}-Y_{i}^{n}
and
\mathfrak{e}_{i}^{n}=p_{i}^{n}-P_{i}^{n}
. Then taking the difference between (
3.51
) and (
2.4

\frac{e_{i}^{n+1}-e_{i}^{n}}{\delta_{t}}=\Delta_{h}\left((y_{i}^{n})^{3}-(Y_{i}^{n})^{3}\right)-3\Delta_{h}e_{i}^{n}+2\Delta_{h}e_{i}^{n+1}-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{h}^{2}e_{i}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathfrak{e}_{i}^{n}+\widehat{F}_{i}^{n}.

(3.53)

Taking the inner product of (
3.53
) and
e^{n+1}
yields

\displaystyle\left(\frac{e^{n+1}-e^{n}}{\delta_{t}},e^{n+1}\right)_{h}=

(3.54)

\displaystyle+2\left(\Delta_{h}e^{n+1},e^{n+1}\right)_{h}-\epsilon^{2}|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\mathfrak{e}^{n},e^{n+1}\right)_{h}+\left(\widehat{F}^{n},e^{n+1}\right)_{h}.

Using the differentiability of
f
and Cauchy-Schwarz on (
3.54

\displaystyle\frac{\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}^{2}-\parallel e^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}}{2\delta_{t}}

\displaystyle-{\epsilon^{2}}|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{n}\parallel_{h}\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}+\parallel\widehat{F}^{n}\parallel_{h}\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\parallel e^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}+\frac{9}{2\epsilon^{2}}\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}|e^{n}|_{2,h}+\frac{4}{\epsilon^{2}}\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}

\displaystyle-{\epsilon^{2}}|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}}\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\parallel\widehat{F}^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}^{2},

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\parallel e^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{17}{2\epsilon^{2}}+1\right)}_{\alpha_{0}}\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}^{2}-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}|e^{n}|_{2,h}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}}\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{c_{4}}{2}(\delta_{t}+h^{2})^{2},

where on the second inequality we use Young’s inequality and in the last inequality we use (
3.52

3.55

\left(1-2\delta_{t}\alpha_{0}\right)\parallel e^{n+1}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\delta_{t}\left(|e^{n+1}|_{2,h}^{2}-|e^{n}|_{2,h}^{2}\right)\leq\left(1+\frac{2\delta_{t}M^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)\parallel e^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{\lambda^{2}}\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}+c_{4}\delta_{t}(\delta_{t}+h^{2})^{2}.

For
\delta_{t}<\frac{1}{2{\alpha_{0}}}

n
to obtain

\parallel e^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}\leq\frac{\left(1+\frac{2\delta_{t}M^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)}{\left(1-2\delta_{t}{\alpha_{0}}\right)}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\parallel e^{i}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{\lambda^{2}\left(1-2\delta_{t}{\alpha_{0}}\right)}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{i}\parallel_{h}^{2}+\frac{c_{4}T}{\left(1-2\delta_{t}{\alpha_{0}}\right)}(\delta_{t}+h^{2})^{2}.

(3.57)

Applying Gronwall’s inequality on (
3.57
) for sufficiently small
\delta_{t}
, we get

\parallel e^{n}\parallel_{h}^{2}\leq C\delta_{t}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{i}\parallel_{h}^{2}+C(\delta_{t}+h^{2})^{2}.

(3.58)

Adding (
3.18
) (error estimates in Theorem
3.2
for adjoint equation) and (
3.58

\parallel e^{n}\parallel_{h}+\parallel\mathfrak{e}^{n}\parallel_{h}\leq C(\delta_{t}+h^{2}).

Next we take the inner product of (

Remark 3.5

5.
Conclusions

In this work, we formulate linear and nonlinear discretization schemes for the OCP with the CH equation as constrain. We present rigorous convergence analysis for all the proposed schemes. Lastly we verify the numerical accuracy and present numerical solution for various desired state in 1D and 2D.

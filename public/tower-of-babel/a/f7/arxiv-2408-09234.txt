1.
Introduction and statement of the results

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth compact Riemannian manifold without boundary, of dimension
n\geq 3
. Let also
k\geq 1
be an integer such that
n>2k
. We define the Sobolev space
H^{k}(M)
as the closure of
C^{\infty}(M)
with respect to the norm

(1.1)

\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}(M)}^{2}:=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\int_{M}\left\lvert\Delta_{g}^{l/2}u\right\rvert^{2}\,dv_{g},

where

\left\lvert\Delta_{g}^{l/2}u\right\rvert:=\begin{cases}\left\lvert\Delta_{g}^{m}u\right\rvert&\text{if $l=2m$ is even}\\
\left\lvert\nabla\Delta_{g}^{m}u\right\rvert_{g}&\text{if $l=2m+1$ is odd}\end{cases}.

Here
\Delta_{g}:=-\operatorname{div}_{g}(\nabla\penalty\ \cdot)
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
M
.

Thanks to a Bochner-Lichnerowicz-Weitzenböck-type formula, as showed in
[
27
]
, the norm (
1.1
) is equivalent to the usual Sobolev norm

\left(\sum_{\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leq k}\int_{M}\left\lvert\nabla^{\beta}u\right\rvert_{g}^{2}\,dv_{g}\right)^{1/2},

where
\beta
is now a multi-index. On a compact manifold, the Sobolev space
H^{k}(M)
embeds continuously into
L^{2^{\sharp}}(M)
, where
{2^{\sharp}}:=\frac{2n}{n-2k}
is the critical exponent. That is, there exist
A,\,B>0
such that

(1.2)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{{2^{\sharp}}}(M)}^{2}\leq A\left\|\Delta_{g}^{k/2}u\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}+B\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k-1}(M)}^{2}\qquad\forall\penalty\ u\in H^{k}(M).

This paper is concerned with the optimization of the constant
A
. We are motivated by the following question: What is the smallest possible
A>0
for which there exists
B>0
such that (
1.2
) holds?

In the Euclidean setting, we define
K_{0}>0
to be the optimal constant for the Sobolev inequality

(1.3)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{{2^{\sharp}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq K_{0}^{2}\left\|\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\qquad\forall\penalty\ u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Here
\xi
is the flat metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\Delta_{\xi}=-\textstyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}^{2}
.
This constant has an explicit expression depending only on
n
and
k
, which was computed in
[
29
]
, and a variational characterization:

\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}}:=\inf_{u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\lvert\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}u\right\rvert^{2}dy}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2^{\sharp}}dy\right)^{2/{2^{\sharp}}}}.

We introduce the following norm, for
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\left\|u\right\|_{D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\lvert\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}u\right\rvert^{2}dy.

We also define the homogeneous Sobolev space
D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. The optimal Sobolev inequality (
1.3
) holds for all
u\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by density. The extremals for this inequality were studied in
[
29
]
, and are all rescalings and translations of the so-called
standard bubble
.

Definition 1.1

(the Euclidean bubble)
.

We let
\operatorname{B}\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be defined as

(1.4)

\operatorname{B}(y):=\big(1+\rho_{n,k}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}\big)^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\rho_{n,k}:=\left(\prod\limits_{l=-k}^{k-1}(n+2l)\right)^{-1/k}
is chosen such that
\operatorname{B}
satisfies

(1.5)

\Delta_{\xi}^{k}\operatorname{B}=\operatorname{B}^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We have
\left\|\operatorname{B}\right\|_{D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=K_{0}^{-\frac{n}{2k}}
and

\left\|\operatorname{B}\right\|_{L^{{2^{\sharp}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=K_{0}\left\|\operatorname{B}\right\|_{D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

As showed in
[
32
]
, all the positive solutions of (
1.5
) are given by the transformations of
\operatorname{B}
, for
\mu>0
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(1.6)

\mu^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}}\operatorname{B}\Big(\frac{y-z}{\mu}\Big)=\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu^{2}+\rho_{n,k}\left\lvert y-z\right\rvert^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}.

In this article we show that, in the inhomogeneous context of a closed Riemannian manifold, we can choose
A=K_{0}^{2}
in (
1.2
). Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth compact Riemannian manifold without boundary of dimension
n
, and let
k\geq 1
be an integer such that
n>2k
. There exists
B_{0}>0
such that for all
u\in H^{k}(M)
,

(1.7)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{{2^{\sharp}}}(M)}^{2}\leq K_{0}^{2}\left\|\Delta_{g}^{k/2}u\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}+B_{0}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k-1}(M)}^{2}.

Hebey and Vaugon (1996) proved the case
k=1
,
they considered the case of a compact manifold
[
19
]
, and also proved the result on a complete Riemannian manifold
[
18
]
. Subsequently, Hebey considered the case
k=2
on a compact manifold
[
15
]
. Theorem
1.1
extends these results for an arbitrary order
k\geq 1
. Note that for the case
k=1
, and for
p\geq 1
, the analog embedding of the Sobolev space
H^{1,p}(M)
in the Lebesgue space
L^{\frac{np}{n-p}}(M)
has been largely studied, see for instance
[
9
]
. We also refer to
[
2
,
30
]
for the expression of the optimal constant
K_{0}
depending on
n
and
p
in this case, and the corresponding extremals on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. See also
[
14
,
10
]
for a broader discussion about the optimal constants for Sobolev inequalities on manifolds.
Finally, we refer to
[
8
]
where similar optimal inequalities with a norm involving the celebrated Paneitz-Branson operator were considered, for
k=2
.

In the case of
k\geq 1
, Mazumdar
[
20
]
proved, with a covering argument, the following two facts:

(1)

Any pair of constants
A,B>0
for which (
1.2
) holds satisfies
A\geq K_{0}^{2}
;

(2)

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
B_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(1.8)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{{2^{\sharp}}}(M)}^{2}\leq\big(K_{0}^{2}+\varepsilon\big)\left\|\Delta_{g}^{k/2}u\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}+B_{\varepsilon}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k-1}(M)}^{2}.

In particular, (
1.8
) shows that
\inf\{A>0\,:\,\exists\penalty\ B>0\text{ for which \eqref{eq:sobineq} holds}\}=K_{0}^{2}
. We show in this paper that the optimal constant is in fact attained, that is that one can choose
\varepsilon=0
in (
1.8
).

We briefly describe the strategy of the proof of Theorem
1.1
, which is detailed in section
5
. As in
[
19
,
15
]
, the proof will proceed by contradiction. Assuming that
A=K_{0}^{2}
is not reached is equivalent to considering that for all
\alpha>0
,

(1.9)

\inf_{u\in H^{k}(M)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}u\penalty\ u\,dv_{g}}{\left(\int_{M}\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2^{\sharp}}\,dv_{g}\right)^{2/{2^{\sharp}}}}<\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}}.

Assumption (
1.9
) implies that for each
\alpha>0
, there exists a positive function
u_{\alpha}\in H^{k}(M)
solution to the critical equation

(1.10)

(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}u=u^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}\qquad\text{in }M,

satisfying
\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|_{H^{k}(M)}\leq K_{0}^{-\frac{n}{2k}}
. The main part of this article is devoted to the study of such sequences of solutions: we obtain a precise pointwise description of the blow-up behavior of
u_{\alpha}
as
\alpha\to\infty
, that we use to reach a contradiction.

As we will detail in section
2
, for a sequence
\nu_{\alpha}=(z_{\alpha},\mu_{\alpha})\in M\times(0,+\infty)
such that
\alpha\mu_{\alpha}^{2}\to 0
as
\alpha\to\infty
, we let

V_{\alpha,\nu_{\alpha}}(x)=\Theta_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha},x)\left(\frac{\mu_{\alpha}}{\mu_{\alpha}^{2}+\rho_{n,k}d_{g}{(z_{\alpha},x)}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\qquad x\in M.

Here
\Theta_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha},\cdot)
is a smooth function (see definition
2.4
) which is equal to 1 in a ball of radius
1/\sqrt{\alpha}
around the center
z_{\alpha}
, and then decays as
e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(z_{\alpha},\cdot)}/2}
, with support contained inside a ball of radius
i_{g}
, the injectivity radius of the compact manifold
M
.
The function
V_{\alpha,\nu_{\alpha}}
is then an almost solution to (
1.10
), it satisfies

(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}V_{\alpha,\nu_{\alpha}}=V_{\alpha,\nu_{\alpha}}^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}+o(1),

where
o(1)\to 0
in
H^{-k}(M)
and comes with explicit pointwise estimates (see Proposition
2.6
).

We will obtain the required contradiction, and thus the proof of Theorem
1.1
, as a consequence of the following result. Define

(1.11)

\eta(t):=\begin{cases}t\big(1+\left\lvert\log t\right\rvert\big)&\text{if }n=2k+1\\
t^{3/2}&\begin{aligned} &\text{if }k=1,\,n\geq 4\\
&\penalty\ \text{or }k\geq 2,\,n=2k+2\end{aligned}\\
t^{2}&\text{if }k\geq 2,\,n\geq 2k+3\end{cases}\qquad t\geq 0.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a sequence of positive functions in
H^{k}(M)
, such that
u_{\alpha}
is solution to (
1.10
) for each
\alpha
. Assume that there exist a sequence
(\varepsilon_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
such that
\varepsilon_{\alpha}\to 0
as
\alpha\to\infty
, and

(1.12)

\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|_{H^{k}(M)}\leq K_{0}^{-\frac{n}{2k}}+\varepsilon_{\alpha}\qquad\forall\penalty\ \alpha.

Then, there exist a sequence
(z_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of points in
M
, a sequence
(\mu_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of positive numbers such that
\alpha\mu_{\alpha}^{2}\to 0
as
\alpha\to\infty
, and a constant
C>0
, such that the following holds. Up to a subsequence, writing
\nu_{\alpha}=(z_{\alpha},\mu_{\alpha})
, we have for all
x\in M
and
l=0,\ldots 2k-1
that

(\mu_{\alpha}+d_{g}{(z_{\alpha},x)})^{l}\left\lvert\nabla^{l}\left(u_{\alpha}-V_{\alpha,\nu_{\alpha}}\right)(x)\right\rvert_{g}\leq C\left(\frac{\mu_{\alpha}}{\mu_{\alpha}^{2}+\rho_{n,k}d_{g}{(z_{\alpha},x)}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\\
\times\begin{cases}\eta\big(\sqrt{\alpha}(\mu_{\alpha}+d_{g}{(z_{\alpha},x)})\big)&\text{when }\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(z_{\alpha},x)}\leq 1\\
\alpha^{k}d_{g}{(z_{\alpha},x)}^{2k}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(z_{\alpha},x)}/2}&\text{when }\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(z_{\alpha},x)}\geq 1\end{cases},

where
V_{\alpha,\nu_{\alpha}}
is as defined in (
2.5
).

As a consequence of this theorem, we have immediately

u_{\alpha}-V_{\alpha,\nu_{\alpha}}\to 0\qquad\text{in }H^{k}(M)\quad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty.

Energy decomposition results of this type for sequences of solutions to polyharmonic critical equations are known to hold for the case of bounded coefficients, see
[
17
,
21
]
.
The main novelty of Theorem
1.2
is twofold. First, we obtain strong pointwise estimates on
u_{\alpha}-V_{\alpha,\nu_{\alpha}}
and its derivatives, and not just an energy decomposition. Second, we obtain an explicit dependence in
\alpha
, as
\alpha\to\infty
, in the a priori estimates. This part is shown in section
4
, following the strategy of
[
25
]
.
In the context of Theorem
1.2
, the low energy of the sequence
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
only allows the presence of a single bubble. While this simplifies some arguments compared to the bubble-tree case of
[
25
]
, the arbitrary order
k
of the polyharmonic operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
, and more importantly the presence of the diverging coefficient
\alpha
in the lower-order terms

(1.13)

\alpha^{k-l}\Delta_{g}^{l}u\qquad l=0,\ldots k-1

required us to perform significant and novel modifications to the theory. In the course of the proof, a precise pointwise description of the Green’s function for the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
on
M
is required to obtain the estimates of Theorem
1.2
, which was studied by the author in a previous work
[
6
]
.

With the use of this pointwise decomposition, the proof of Theorem
1.1
reduces to explicit computations, which are carried out in section
5
.
Our approach is therefore completely different from the approach in
[
15
]
, since we see Theorem
1.1
as a direct consequence of Theorem
1.2
. We believe however that Theorem
1.2
has a strong interest in itself, as it serves as a toy-model for general critical polyharmonic equations with diverging coefficients on manifolds. In this case, the proof provides a very precise understanding of the obstructions that do not allow such low-energy solutions to exist as
\alpha\to\infty
.
The analytical machinery developed here is quite flexible and will be adapted in a second step to study more general sequences of solutions to polyharmonic equations of this type. Other examples where equations of the form (
1.10
) are considered are found for instance in
[
31
,
1
,
26
]
for the case
k=1
, where the possible concentration phenomena of sequences of solutions to
-\Delta u+\lambda u=u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}
are investigated on an open set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Neumann boundary conditions, as
\lambda\to\infty
. In the case
k=2
, we refer to
[
12
]
, where the authors studied the energy of solutions to (
1.10
) as
\alpha\to\infty
. As this article was being reviewed, we have learned that Zeitler
[
33
]
has independently obtained Theorem
1.1
, and was able to adapt the method of
[
15
]
to the higher-order case.

This article is structured as follows: Sections
2
-
4
focus on the proof of Theorem
1.2
, while Theorem
1.1
is proved in section
5
. In section
2
, we provide the necessary definitions and computations, and prove technical results concerning the function
V_{\alpha,\nu}
. Sections
3
and
4
are devoted to the construction of solutions to a more general version of (
1.10
), as perturbations around the modified bubble
V_{\alpha,\nu}
. We first develop in section
3
the linear and non-linear theory leading to existence and uniqueness of such solutions in
H^{k}(M)
. In a second step, we extend the theory to obtain pointwise estimates in section
4
, proving Theorem
1.2
. This is the core of the analysis of the paper.
Finally, in section
5
, we use the strong decomposition of Theorem
1.2
to prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminary results

In this section we gather some basic definitions and results which will be needed to develop the full description of the blow-up for sequences of solutions to (
1.10
). Remark first that the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
satisfies the maximum principle since it is in factorized form. Moreover, it is coercive, and

(2.1)

\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}(M)}^{2}\leq\left\langle(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}u,u\right\rangle_{H^{-k},H^{k}},\qquad u\in H^{k}(M)

for all
\alpha\geq 1
. Here and in the following, unless specified otherwise, all constants depend only on
(M,g),\,n,k
. They are denoted
C
, and their explicit value can vary from line to line, sometimes even in the same line.

2.1.
Riemannian bubbles

This subsection is devoted to the definition of Riemannian versions of the minimizing functions
\operatorname{B}
for the critical Sobolev inequality defined in (
1.4
).

Definition 2.1
.

We define the concentrated version of the bubble, of center
z\in M
and with weight
\mu>0
, as

(2.2)

\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}(x):=\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu^{2}+\rho_{n,k}d_{g}{(z,x)}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2k}{2}},

where
\rho_{n,k}
is as in (
1.4
).

In general, for any function
\psi\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we can define a corresponding concentrated version on
M
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\psi\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
z\in M
and
\mu>0
, let also
i_{g}/2<\varrho<i_{g}
and
\chi_{\varrho}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
be a cut-off function such that
0\leq\chi_{\varrho}\leq 1
,
\chi_{\varrho}\equiv 1
on
[0,i_{g}/2)
and
\chi_{\varrho}\equiv 0
on
(\varrho,+\infty)
. We define

(2.3)

\psi_{z,\mu}(x)=\chi_{\varrho}(d_{g}{(z,x)})\mu^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}}\psi\big(\tfrac{1}{\mu}\exp_{z}^{-1}(x)\big)\qquad\forall\penalty\ x\in M.

Let us fix once and for all
i_{g}/2<\varrho<i_{g}
, so that when
\psi\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have
\psi_{z,\mu}\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Note also that if
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists
\mu_{0}>0
such that for all
\mu\leq\mu_{0}
,
\mu\left\lvert y\right\rvert<i_{g}/2
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the support of
\psi
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\psi\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists
\mu_{0}>0
and a constant
C>0
such that, for all
\mu\leq\mu_{0}
,
z\in M
, we have
\psi_{z,\mu}\in H^{k}(M)
with

\left\|\psi_{z,\mu}\right\|_{H^{k}(M)}\leq C\left\|\psi\right\|_{D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We refer to Appendix
A.1
for a proof of this Lemma.

Remark 2.1
.

This still holds when the norm is only taken in a subset of
M
. For instance, for a fixed
R>0
we obtain

\left\|\psi_{z,\mu}\right\|_{H^{k}(M\setminus B_{z}(R\mu))}\leq C\left\|\psi\right\|_{D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{0}(R))},

where
C>0
is independent of
R
.

Comparing (
2.2
) with (
1.6
),
\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}
can be seen as a good candidate to solve

\Delta_{g}^{k}\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}\simeq\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}\qquad\text{in }M

up to lower-order terms coming from the geometry of the manifold. In equation (
1.10
), because of the lower-order terms (
1.13
) with
\alpha\to\infty
, we will need to work with a modified version of the bubble in
M
.

Definition 2.3
.

Fix
\alpha\geq 1
, and let
\chi_{\varrho}
be a cut-off function chosen as in definition
2.2
. Let also
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a
C^{2k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function such that

h(y)\equiv 1\qquad\text{when }\left\lvert y\right\rvert\leq 1,

and such that there exists
C_{l}>0
for
l=0,\ldots 2k+1
and

\left\lvert\nabla^{l}h(y)\right\rvert\leq C_{l}e^{-\left\lvert y\right\rvert/2}\qquad\text{when }\left\lvert y\right\rvert\geq 1.

Let
z\in M
, we define

\Theta_{\alpha}(z,x):=\chi_{\varrho}(d_{g}{(z,x)})\,h(\sqrt{\alpha}\exp_{z}^{-1}(x))\qquad\forall\penalty\ x\in M.

It is then immediate to obtain that, for
l=1,\ldots 2k+1
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\nabla_{g}^{l}\Theta_{\alpha}(z,x)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{when }\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(z,x)}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l}\Theta_{\alpha}(z,x)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{l/2}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(z,x)}/2}

\displaystyle\text{when }\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(z,x)}\geq 1.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\alpha\geq 1
be such that
1/\sqrt{\alpha}<i_{g}/2
, and let
z\in M
and
\mu>0
, we write
\nu=(z,\mu)
. We define, for
x\in M
,

(2.5)

V_{\alpha,\nu}(x):=\Theta_{\alpha}(z,x)\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}(x).

Remark 2.2
.

This new rescaled version of
\operatorname{B}
is modelled after the function

(2.6)

\chi_{\varrho}(d_{g}{(z,x)})c_{n,k}^{-1}d_{g}{(z,x)}^{n-2k}\operatorname{G}_{g,\alpha}(z,x)\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}(x),

where
\operatorname{G}_{g,\alpha}
is the Green’s function for the operator
(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}
in
M
, and
\frac{c_{n,k}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n-2k}}
is the Green’s function for the poly-Laplacian
\Delta_{\xi}^{k}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We refer to
[
6
]
for the construction and pointwise estimates of
\operatorname{G}_{g,\alpha}
and its derivatives, that are similar to (
2.4
). Of course any compactly supported function
h
such that
h\equiv 1
in
B_{0}(1)
would satisfy the requirements of definition
2.3
. We choose here to work with a more general function, with only the minimum requirements to make the proof work. This is done to cover the cases where the bubble needs to be modified precisely as in the expression (
2.6
) (when
\alpha
is fixed this was done for instance in
[
16
,
11
]
). In these cases, which will be treated in future work, the best possible decay would indeed be exponential, as showed by the pointwise estimates of
\operatorname{G}_{g,\alpha}
in
[
6
]
.

As we will see,
V_{\alpha,\nu}
is a better candidate to solve the critical equation (
1.10
), when
\alpha\to\infty
and
\alpha\mu^{2}\to 0
, in a precise sense given by Proposition
2.6
below.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\alpha\geq 1
and
\tau\leq 1
, we define the parameter set

(2.7)

\mathcal{P}(\tau,\alpha):=\{\nu=(z,\mu)\in M\times(0,+\infty)\,:\,\alpha\mu^{2}<\tau\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(\tau_{i})_{i}
,
(\alpha_{i})_{i}
be sequences of positive numbers such that
\tau_{i}\to 0
and
1/\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}<i_{g}/2
for all
i
, and let
\nu_{i}=(z_{i},\mu_{i})\in\mathcal{P}(\tau_{i},\alpha_{i})
for all
i
. Then

\Theta_{\alpha_{i}}(z_{i},\exp_{z_{i}}(\mu_{i}\cdot))\to 1\qquad\text{in }C^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\text{ as }i\to\infty.

Proof.

Fix
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a compact subset. Then, since
\alpha_{i}\mu_{i}^{2}<\tau_{i}
for all
i
, there is
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all

\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}\mu_{i}\left\lvert y\right\rvert\leq 1

\Theta_{\alpha_{i}}(z_{i},\exp_{z_{i}}(\mu_{i}y))=\chi_{\varrho}(\mu_{i}\left\lvert y\right\rvert)h(\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}\mu_{i}y)\equiv 1

.
∎

2.2.
Almost solutions to the linearized equation

The purpose of the next sections is to find approximated solutions to (
1.10

u_{\alpha}=V_{\alpha,\nu}+\varphi_{\alpha}
, where
\varphi_{\alpha}
is small in comparison with
V_{\alpha,\nu}
in a specific sense that will be explained below (see Proposition

V_{\alpha,\nu}
of the critical equation (

(2.8)

(\Delta_{g}+\alpha)^{k}u=({2^{\sharp}}-1)V_{\alpha,\nu}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}u\qquad\text{in }M.

In this subsection, we define functions of the same form as (
2.5

\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 2.6
.

In the Euclidean setting, we consider the equation

(2.9)

\Delta_{\xi}^{k}u=({2^{\sharp}}-1)\operatorname{B}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}u\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

for
n>2k
. We then define

(2.10)

\mathcal{K}_{\operatorname{B}}:=\{v\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,:\,\Delta_{\xi}^{k}v=({2^{\sharp}}-1)\operatorname{B}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}v\quad\text{in the weak sense}\},

the set of solution to (
1.5
) linearized around
\operatorname{B}
as given by (
1.4
).

Remark 2.3
.

If
v\in\mathcal{K}_{\operatorname{B}}
, then

\langle\operatorname{B},v\rangle_{D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}\operatorname{B},\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}v\rangle_{\xi}dy=({2^{\sharp}}-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{B}^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}v\,dy

by definition of
\mathcal{K}_{\operatorname{B}}
. Since
\operatorname{B}
solves (
1.5
), we also have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{B}^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}v\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta_{\xi}^{k}\operatorname{B}v\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}\operatorname{B},\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}v\rangle_{\xi}dy.

We conclude, for all
n>2k
, that
\langle\operatorname{B},v\rangle_{D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0
, so that
\operatorname{B}\in\mathcal{K}_{\operatorname{B}}^{\perp}
the orthogonal to
\mathcal{K}_{\operatorname{B}}
in
D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

It has been shown in
[
5
]
that Equation (

) possesses

D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. These correspond to the partial derivatives of
\operatorname{B}
with respect to the parameters

of the transformation (
1.6
) under which (
1.5
) is invariant.

Definition 2.7
.

We define

(2.11)

\displaystyle Z^{0}(y)

\displaystyle:=y\cdot\nabla\operatorname{B}(y)+\frac{n-2k}{2}\operatorname{B}(y),

\displaystyle Z^{j}(y)

\mathcal{K}_{\operatorname{B}}
in

2.9

\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We now define the rescaled versions of

on
M

as defined in (

Definition 2.8

Let

\displaystyle Z^{j}_{\alpha,\nu_{0}}

.

The multiplication by
\mu_{0}
ensures that

that
V_{\alpha,\nu}
is for
\operatorname{B}
. Indeed, for all

\displaystyle Z^{0}_{\alpha,\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\Theta_{\alpha}(z,x)\big(Z^{0}\big)_{z,\mu}(x)

\displaystyle Z^{j}_{\alpha,\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\Theta_{\alpha}(z,x)\big(Z^{j}\big)_{z,\mu}(x)+\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\Theta_{\alpha}(z,x)\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}(x)

), for

,

(2.13)

\big(Z^{j}\big)_{z,\mu}(x):=\mu^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}}Z^{j}\big(\tfrac{1}{\mu}\exp_{z}^{-1}(x)\big).

Remark 2.5
.

The map
(z,\mu)\mapsto V_{\alpha,\nu}
is differentiable in its variables
z\in M
,
\mu>0
, and the partial derivative along the variable

, near a center

, can be defined through the local charts. For

, we let

be the

, then we define

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow M

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\longmapsto\exp_{z_{0}}(tv_{j})

for a small enough
\varepsilon>0
. The partial derivative along
z_{j}
is defined as

(2.14)

\frac{\partial V_{\alpha,\nu}}{\partial z_{j}}\Big\lvert_{\nu_{0}}:=\frac{d}{dt}\big(V_{\alpha,(\gamma_{j}(t),\mu)}\big)\Big\lvert_{t=0}

By analogy with
\mathcal{K}_{\operatorname{B}}
, we also define the following.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\alpha\geq 1
be such that
1/\sqrt{\alpha}<i_{g}/2
,
\tau\leq 1
, and
\nu=(z,\mu)\in\mathcal{P}(\tau,\alpha)
. We define the set

(2.15)

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\nu}:=\operatorname{span}\left\{Z^{j}_{\alpha,\nu}\,:\,0\leq j\leq n\right\}\subset H^{k}(M).

.

2.3.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\geq 1
be such that
1/\sqrt{\alpha}<i_{g}/2
,
\tau\leq 1
, and let
\nu=(z,\mu)\in\mathcal{P}(\tau,\alpha)
. For
l=0,\ldots 2k
, there is a constant
C_{l}>0
independent of
\alpha,\tau,\nu
such that the following holds.

•

For all
x\in M
with
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(z,x)}\leq 1
,

(2.16)

\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l}X_{\alpha,\nu}(x)-\mu^{-\frac{n-2k+2l}{2}}\big(\nabla_{\xi}^{l}X\big)\big(\tfrac{1}{\mu}\exp_{z}^{-1}(x)\big)\right\rvert_{\xi}\leq C_{l}(\mu+d_{g}{(z,x)})^{2-l}\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}(x).

•

For all
x\in M
with
\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(z,x)}\geq 1
,

(2.17)

\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l}X_{\alpha,\nu}(x)\right\rvert_{g}\leq C_{l}\alpha^{l/2}\mu^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}d_{g}{(z,x)}^{2k-n}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(z,x)}/2}.

Proof.

Let
i_{g}/2<\varrho<i_{g}
be as defined in definition
2.2
. Computing the quantities in local coordinates at
z\in M
, for a smooth
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we have for all
y\in B_{0}(\varrho)
,
l=2,\ldots 2k
,

(2.18)

\big(\nabla_{g}^{l}f\big)_{i_{1},\ldots i_{l}}(\exp_{z}(y))=\big(\nabla_{\xi}^{l}(f\circ\exp_{z})\big)_{i_{1},\ldots i_{l}}(y)\\
+\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lvert y\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{l-1}(f\circ\exp_{z})(y)\right\rvert\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\textstyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{l-2}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{m}(f\circ\exp_{z})(y)\right\rvert\right).

We first consider the case
d_{g}{(z,x)}\leq 1/\sqrt{\alpha}
. Then, since
\Theta_{\alpha}(z,x)\equiv 1
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{g}^{l}V_{\alpha,\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{g}^{l}\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}(x)

\displaystyle\nabla_{g}^{l}Z^{j}_{\alpha,\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{g}^{l}\big(Z^{j}\big)_{z,\mu}(x),\qquad j=0,\ldots n

for
l=0,\ldots 2k
.
For all
y\in B_{0}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
l=2,\ldots 2k
, we have

\nabla_{g}^{l}X_{\alpha,\nu}(\exp_{z}(y))=\mu^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}-l}\big(\nabla_{\xi}^{l}X\big)\left(\frac{y}{\mu}\right)\\
+\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lvert y\right\rvert\mu^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}-l+1}\left\lvert\big(\nabla_{\xi}^{l-1}X\big)\left(\frac{y}{\mu}\right)\right\rvert\right)\\
+\mathcal{O}\left(\textstyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{l-2}\mu^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}-m}\left\lvert\big(\nabla_{\xi}^{m}X\big)\left(\displaystyle\frac{y}{\mu}\right)\right\rvert\right).

Note that the derivatives of
\operatorname{B}
and
Z^{j}
have a known behavior : Using their explicit expression, we obtain for all
y\in M
and
l=0,\ldots 2k
,

(2.19)

\displaystyle(1+\left\lvert y\right\rvert)^{l}\left\lvert\nabla^{l}\operatorname{B}(y)\right\rvert\leq C(1+\left\lvert y\right\rvert)^{2k-n}

(2.20)

\displaystyle(1+\left\lvert y\right\rvert)^{l}\left\lvert\nabla^{l}Z^{j}(y)\right\rvert\leq C(1+\left\lvert y\right\rvert)^{2k-n}

for some
C>0
.
We then obtain, for
y\in B_{0}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{g}^{l}X_{\alpha,\nu}(\exp_{z}(y))

\displaystyle=\mu^{-\frac{n-2k+2l}{2}}\big(\nabla_{\xi}^{l}X\big)\left(\frac{y}{\mu}\right)+\mathcal{O}\Big(\left\lvert y\right\rvert(\mu+\left\lvert y\right\rvert)^{-l+1}\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}(\exp_{z}(y))\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\mathcal{O}\left(\textstyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{l-2}(\mu+\left\lvert y\right\rvert)^{-m}\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}(\exp_{z}(y))\right)

\displaystyle=\mu^{-\frac{n-2k+2l}{2}}\big(\nabla_{\xi}^{l}X\big)\left(\frac{y}{\mu}\right)+\mathcal{O}\Big((\mu+\left\lvert y\right\rvert)^{2-l}\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}(\exp_{z}(y))\Big),

where we used that
\left\lvert y\right\rvert\leq(\mu+\left\lvert y\right\rvert)\leq\frac{1+\tau}{\sqrt{\alpha}}<i_{g}
for all
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\tau,\alpha)
. This proves (
2.16
).

Let now
x\in M
be such that
1/\sqrt{\alpha}\leq d_{g}{(z,x)}\leq\varrho
, where
\varrho
was chosen in definition
2.2
, and write
y=\exp_{z}^{-1}(x)
, we compute

(2.21)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l}V_{\alpha,\nu}(x)\right\rvert_{g}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l-m}\Theta_{\alpha}(z,x)\right\rvert_{g}\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{m}\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}(x)\right\rvert_{g}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg[\mu^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}}\operatorname{B}\big(\tfrac{y}{\mu}\big)\alpha^{l/2}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert u\right\rvert/2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{l}\sum_{p=1}^{m}\mu^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}-p}\left\lvert\big(\nabla_{\xi}^{p}\operatorname{B}\big)\big(\tfrac{y}{\mu}\big)\right\rvert\alpha^{\frac{l-m}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert y\right\rvert/2}\Bigg]

using (
2.4
). As before, we use (
2.19
) and (
2.20
), together with the fact that
\left\lvert y\right\rvert>\mu
for all
y\in B_{0}(\varrho)\setminus B_{0}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
, and obtain

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l}V_{\alpha,\nu}(\exp_{z}(y))\right\rvert_{g}

\displaystyle\leq C\mu^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2k-n}\alpha^{l/2}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert y\right\rvert/2}\left[1+\sum_{m=1}^{l}\sum_{p=1}^{m}\alpha^{-m/2}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{-p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\mu^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2k-n}\alpha^{l/2}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}\left\lvert y\right\rvert/2}

for all
y\in B_{0}(\varrho)\setminus B_{0}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
. Now for
Z^{j}_{\alpha,\nu}
, we have

\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l}Z^{j}_{\alpha,\nu}(x)\right\rvert_{g}\leq C\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l-m}\Theta_{\alpha}(z,x)\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{m}\big(Z^{j}\big)_{z,\mu}(x)\right\rvert\\
+\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l}\left(\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\Theta_{\alpha}(z,\cdot)\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}\right)(x)\right\rvert.

With the same arguments as in (
2.21
), using (
2.20
), we obtain

\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l-m}\Theta_{\alpha}(z,x)\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{m}\big(Z^{j}\big)_{z,\mu}(x)\right\rvert\leq C\mu^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}d_{g}{(z,x)}^{2k-n}\alpha^{l/2}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(z,x)}/2}

for all
x\in B_{z}(\varrho)\setminus B_{z}(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
. Now using (
A.6
) in Lemma
A.2
below, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l}\left(\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\Theta_{\alpha}(z,\cdot)\operatorname{B}_{z,\mu}\right)(x)\right\rvert\leq C\sqrt{\alpha}\mu\mu^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}d_{g}{(z,x)}^{2k-n}\alpha^{l/2}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}d_{g}{(z,x)}/2}.

Realizing that when

,
\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l}X_{\alpha,\nu}\right\rvert=0
, we obtain (
2.17
) which concludes the proof of Proposition

.
∎

2.4.
Finding the best bubble

The purpose of this subsection is to show that when
u\in H^{k}(M)
is close to the set of all possible bubbles
\{V_{\alpha,\nu}\,:\,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\tau,\alpha)\}
, there is some choice of parameters
\bar{\nu}
which is optimal, and the corresponding bubble satisfies
u-V_{\alpha,\bar{\nu}}\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\bar{\nu}}^{\perp}
. The following are standard considerations, we refer to
[
4
]
for analogous results.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(\alpha_{i})_{i}
,
(\tau_{i})_{i}
be sequences of positive numbers such that
\alpha_{i}\to\infty
,
\tau_{i}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
, and let
\nu_{i}=(z_{i},\mu_{i}),\tilde{\nu}_{i}=(\tilde{z}_{i},\tilde{\mu}_{i})\in\mathcal{P}(\tau_{i},\alpha_{i})
, for all
i
, be two sequences such that

\left\|V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}-V_{\alpha_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\right\|_{H^{k}(M)}\xrightarrow{i\to\infty}0.

Then, we have

\begin{aligned} &\frac{\mu_{i}}{\tilde{\mu}_{i}}\to 1\\
&\frac{d_{g}{(z_{i},\tilde{z}_{i})}^{2}}{\mu_{i}\tilde{\mu}_{i}}\to 0\end{aligned}\qquad\text{as }i\to\infty.

Proof.

We first claim that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.34)

\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{i}}{\mu_{i}}+\frac{\mu_{i}}{\tilde{\mu}_{i}}+\frac{d_{g}{(\tilde{z}_{i},z_{i})}^{2}}{\mu_{i}\tilde{\mu}_{i}}\leq C.

By contradiction, suppose that
\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{i}}{\mu_{i}}+\frac{\mu_{i}}{\tilde{\mu}_{i}}+\frac{d_{g}{(\tilde{z}_{i},z_{i})}^{2}}{\mu_{i}\tilde{\mu}_{i}}\to\infty
, then observe that

\int_{M}\langle\Delta_{g}^{k/2}V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}},\Delta_{g}^{k/2}V_{\alpha_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\rangle_{g}\,dv_{g}\\
=\int_{B_{z_{i}}(1/\sqrt{\alpha_{i}})\cap B_{\tilde{z}_{i}}(1/\sqrt{\alpha_{i}})}\langle\Delta_{g}^{k/2}V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}},\Delta_{g}^{k/2}V_{\alpha_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\rangle_{g}\,dv_{g}+o(1)

using (
2.28
). Moreover,

\int_{B_{z_{i}}(1/\sqrt{\alpha_{i}})\cap B_{\tilde{z}_{i}}(1/\sqrt{\alpha_{i}})}\langle\Delta_{g}^{k/2}V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}},\Delta_{g}^{k/2}V_{\alpha_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\rangle_{g}\,dv_{g}\\
=\int_{B_{z_{i}}(1/\sqrt{\alpha_{i}})\cap B_{\tilde{z}_{i}}(1/\sqrt{\alpha_{i}})}\langle\Delta_{g}^{k/2}\operatorname{B}_{z_{i},\mu_{i}},\Delta_{g}^{k/2}\operatorname{B}_{\tilde{z}_{i},\tilde{\mu}_{i}}\rangle_{g}\,dv_{g}=o(1)

as
i\to\infty
, by straightforward computations and with the contradiction assumption. With (
2.23
), we then have

\langle V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}},V_{\alpha_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\rangle_{H^{k}(M)}=\int_{M}\langle\Delta_{g}^{k/2}V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}},\Delta_{g}^{k/2}V_{\alpha_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\rangle_{g}\,dv_{g}+o(1)=o(1),

and thus we compute

\displaystyle\langle V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}},V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}-V_{\alpha_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\rangle_{H^{k}(M)}

\displaystyle=\left\|V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}\right\|_{H^{k}(M)}^{2}-\langle V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}},V_{\alpha_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\rangle_{H^{k}(M)}

\displaystyle=K_{0}^{-\frac{n}{k}}+o(1)

using (
2.22
) and since
\left\|\operatorname{B}\right\|_{D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=K_{0}^{-\frac{n}{2k}}
. We also obtain

\langle V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}},V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}-V_{\alpha_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\rangle_{H^{k}(M)}\leq\left\|V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}\right\|_{H^{k}(M)}\left\|V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}-V_{\alpha_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\right\|_{H^{k}(M)}=o(1)

by assumption, and we reach a contradiction. This proves (
2.34
), and thus as a direct consequence there exists
\lambda>0
and

such that
\frac{\mu_{i}}{\tilde{\mu}_{i}}\to\lambda
and
\tfrac{1}{\mu_{i}}\exp_{z_{i}}^{-1}(\tilde{z}_{i})\to y_{\infty}
, up to a subsequence.

By Sobolev’s inequality, we have

\left\|V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}-V_{\alpha_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\right\|_{L^{{2^{\sharp}}}(M)}=o(1)\qquad\text{as }i\to\infty.

Let

, we compute

o(1)=\int_{B_{z_{i}}(R\mu_{i})}\left\lvert V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}-V_{\alpha_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\right\rvert^{2^{\sharp}}\,dv_{g}\\
\geq\int_{B_{0}(R)}\left\lvert\operatorname{B}(y)-\lambda^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\operatorname{B}(\lambda(y-y_{\infty}))\right\rvert^{{2^{\sharp}}}dy+o(1).

This can only hold if

and

.
∎

We now prove that the optimal bubble can be attained, following the proof of

Definition 2.10
.

\alpha\geq 1

Lemma 2.9

such that

\sum_{l=0}^{k}\tbinom{k}{l}\alpha_{i}^{k-l}\int_{M}\left\lvert\Delta_{g}^{l/2}\varphi_{i}\right\rvert^{2}\,dv_{g}-({2^{\sharp}}-1)\int_{M}V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\varphi_{i}^{2}\,dv_{g}=o(1)

as

. Observe that, as a consequence,

\left\|\varphi_{i}\right\|_{H^{k}(M)}^{2}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\int_{M}\left\lvert\Delta_{g}^{l/2}\varphi_{i}\right\rvert^{2}\,dv_{g}\leq C\int_{M}V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\varphi_{i}^{2}\,dv_{g}+o(1)

as
i\to\infty
, and we estimate this last quantity. Fix some
R>0
, reasoning as before,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{z_{i}}(R\mu_{i})}V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\varphi_{i}^{2}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{0}(R)}\operatorname{B}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\check{\varphi}_{i}^{2}\,dy+o(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{B}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\check{\varphi}_{i}^{2}\,dy+\mathcal{O}\big(\left\|\operatorname{B}\right\|_{L^{{2^{\sharp}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{0}(R))}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\big)+o(1).

Now since we have
\check{\varphi}_{i}\rightharpoonup 0
in
D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
up to a subsequence, using again standard integration theory we get
\check{\varphi}_{i}\rightharpoonup 0
in
L^{2^{\sharp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since
\operatorname{B}\in L^{{2^{\sharp}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, this implies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{B}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\check{\varphi}_{i}^{2}\,dy\to 0\qquad\text{as $i\to\infty$}.

We also obtain by Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding

\displaystyle\int_{M\setminus B_{z_{i}}(R\mu_{i})}V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\varphi_{i}^{2}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{M\setminus B_{z_{i}}(R\mu_{i})}V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}^{2^{\sharp}}\,dv_{g}\right)^{\frac{2k}{n}}\left\|\varphi_{i}\right\|_{H^{k}(M)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\operatorname{B}\right\|_{L^{{2^{\sharp}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{0}(R))}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}.

Letting
R\to\infty
, we have showed that

\left\|\varphi_{i}\right\|_{H^{k}(M)}^{2}\to 0\qquad\text{as $i\to\infty$}

up to a subsequence, which contradicts (
3.4
).
∎

+C\left\|\varphi_{i}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\Psi\big(\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}d_{g}{(z_{i},x)}\big)\begin{cases}\mu_{i}^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\operatorname{B}_{z_{i},\mu_{i}}(x)&\text{if }n<4k\\
\left(\frac{\mu_{i}+d_{g}{(z_{i},x)}}{\mu_{i}}\right)^{-2k+1}&\text{if }n=4k\\
\left(\frac{\mu_{i}+d_{g}{(z_{i},x)}}{\mu_{i}}\right)^{-2k}&\text{if }n>4k\end{cases},

where

(4.8)

\Psi(t):=\begin{cases}1&\text{when }t<1\\
e^{-t/2}&\text{when }t\geq 1\end{cases}

for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

First, note that for
\alpha_{0},\tau_{0}
given by Proposition
3.1
, there exists
i_{0}>0
such that
\alpha_{i}\geq\alpha_{0}
,
\tau_{i}\leq\tau_{0}
for all
i\geq i_{0}
.
Since

and
R_{i}\in C^{0}(M)
, by a standard bootstrap argument, we have
\varphi_{i}\in C^{2k-1,\beta}(M)
for
\beta\in(0,1)
. Observe that equation (
4.4
) can be re-written as

(\Delta_{g}+\alpha_{i})^{k}\left[\varphi_{i}-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{j}Z^{j}_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}\right]=({2^{\sharp}}-1)V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}\varphi_{i}+R_{i}\qquad\text{in }M.

We then have the following representation formula: For all
x\in M
,

(4.9)

\varphi_{i}(x)-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{j}Z^{j}_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}(x)=({2^{\sharp}}-1)\int_{M}V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}(y)\varphi_{i}(y)\operatorname{G}_{g,\alpha_{i}}(x,y)\,dv_{g}(y)\\
+\int_{M}R_{i}(y)\operatorname{G}_{g,\alpha_{i}}(x,y)\,dv_{g}(y).

We now use the estimates on
\operatorname{G}_{g,\alpha_{i}}
in
[

, Theorem 1.1]
, namely that up to increasing
i_{0}>0
, for all

we have

\operatorname{G}_{g,\alpha_{i}}(x,y)\leq Cd_{g}{(x,y)}^{2k-n}\Psi(\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}d_{g}{(x,y)}).

With the definition of
V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}
in (
2.5
) and Giraud’s Lemma
A.5
below, we obtain the following:

•

When
n<4k
,

\left\lvert\int_{M}\operatorname{G}_{g,\alpha_{i}}(x,y)V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}(y)\varphi_{i}(y)\,dv_{g}(y)\right\rvert\\
\leq C\left\|\varphi_{i}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\Psi\big(\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}d_{g}{(z_{i},x)}\big)\mu_{i}^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\operatorname{B}_{z_{i},\mu_{i}}(x).

•

When
n=4k
, for all
\theta>0
, there exists

such that

\left\lvert\int_{M}\operatorname{G}_{g,\alpha_{i}}(x,y)V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}(y)\varphi_{i}(y)\,dv_{g}(y)\right\rvert\\
\leq C_{\theta}\left\|\varphi_{i}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\Psi\big(\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}d_{g}{(z_{i},x)}\big)\left(\frac{\mu_{i}+d_{g}{(z_{i},x)}}{\mu_{i}}\right)^{-2k+\theta}

using the properties of the function
t\mapsto\log t
in
(0,+\infty)
.

•

When
n>4k
,

\left\lvert\int_{M}\operatorname{G}_{g,\alpha_{i}}(x,y)V_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}(y)\varphi_{i}(y)\,dv_{g}(y)\right\rvert\\
\leq C\left\|\varphi_{i}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\Psi\big(\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}d_{g}{(z_{i},x)}\big)\left(\frac{\mu_{i}+d_{g}{(z_{i},x)}}{\mu_{i}}\right)^{-2k}.

We also compute, using Lemma
A.4
with (
4.2
), when
\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}d_{g}{(z_{i},x)}\leq 1
,

(4.10)

\left\lvert\int_{M}R_{i}(y)\operatorname{G}_{g,\alpha_{i}}(x,y)\,dv_{g}(y)\right\rvert\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq C\begin{cases}\alpha_{i}^{1/2}\mu_{i}^{1/2}\big(1+\left\lvert\log\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}(\mu_{i}+d_{g}{(z_{i},x)})\right\rvert\big)&\text{if }n=2k+1\\
\alpha_{i}^{\sigma_{n,k}}\mu_{i}^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}(\mu_{i}+d_{g}{(z_{i},x)})^{2k-n+2\sigma_{n,k}}&\text{if }n\geq 2k+2\end{cases}\\
&\leq Cr_{\alpha_{i},\nu_{i}}(x)\operatorname{B}_{z_{i},\mu_{i}}(x),\end{aligned}

where

is as in (

). Finally, when

(4.11)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{M}R_{i}(y)\operatorname{G}_{g,\alpha_{i}}(x,y)\,dv_{g}(y)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha_{i}^{k}\mu_{i}^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}d_{g}{(z_{i},x)}^{4k-n}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}d_{g}{(z_{i},x)}/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha_{i}^{k}d_{g}{(z_{i},x)}^{2k}\operatorname{B}_{z_{i},\mu_{i}}(x)e^{-\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}d_{g}{(z_{i},x)}/2},

which concludes the proof.

\displaystyle\int_{B_{0}(1/\sqrt{\alpha_{i}})}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{k}U_{i}(y)\right\rvert^{2}dy\leq C\mu_{i}^{2}\int_{B_{0}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}\mu_{i}})}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}(1+\left\lvert y\right\rvert)^{-2(n-k)}dy\leq\frac{C}{\alpha_{i}}\gamma_{i}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)\setminus B_{0}(1/\sqrt{\alpha_{i}})}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{k}U_{i}(y)\right\rvert^{2}dy\leq\frac{C}{\alpha_{i}}(\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}\mu_{i})^{n-2k}=o(\gamma_{i}).

When
k\geq 2
, we also need to compute the following integrals: for
m=1,\ldots k-1
,

(5.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{B_{0}(1/\sqrt{\alpha_{i}})}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{m}U_{i}\right\rvert^{2}dy&\leq C\mu_{i}^{2(k-m)}\int_{B_{0}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}\mu_{i}})}(1+\left\lvert y\right\rvert)^{-2(n-2k+m)}\\
&\leq C\begin{cases}(\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}\mu_{i})^{n-2k}\alpha_{i}^{-k+m}&\text{if }k-m>\frac{n-2k}{2}\\
\mu_{i}^{n-2k}\big(1+\left\lvert\log\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}\mu_{i}\right\rvert\big)&\text{if }k-m=\frac{n-2k}{2}\\
\mu_{i}^{2}&\text{if }k-m<\frac{n-2k}{2}\end{cases}\\
&=o(\gamma_{i}),\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)\setminus B_{0}(1/\sqrt{\alpha_{i}})}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{m}U_{i}\right\rvert^{2}dy\leq C\alpha_{i}^{m-l}(\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}\mu_{i})^{n-2k}=o(\gamma_{i}).

Putting everything together, we have finally

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\lvert\Delta_{g}^{k/2}u_{i}\right\rvert^{2}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{z_{i}}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\Delta_{g}^{k/2}u_{i}\right\rvert^{2}\,dv_{g}+o(\gamma_{i})

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}U_{i}\right\rvert^{2}dy+o(\gamma_{i})

as
i\to\infty
, which proves (
5.12
).

To prove (
5.13
), we compute as before using (
5.14
),

\displaystyle\int_{B_{0}(1/\sqrt{\alpha_{i}})}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}U_{i}^{2^{\sharp}}(y)dy\leq C\mu_{i}^{2}\int_{B_{0}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}\mu_{i}})}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}(1+\left\lvert y\right\rvert)^{-2n}=o(\gamma_{i})

\displaystyle\int_{B_{0}(\varrho_{0})\setminus B_{0}(1/\sqrt{\alpha_{i}})}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}U_{i}^{2^{\sharp}}(y)dy\leq\frac{C}{\alpha_{i}}(\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}\mu_{i})^{n}=o(\gamma_{i}),

and we conclude with the same arguments.
∎

We have now everything we need to conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

End of the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Integrate (
5.3
) against
u_{i}\in H^{k}(M)
, we get

(5.16)

\sum_{l=0}^{k}\tbinom{k}{l}\alpha_{i}^{k-l}\int_{M}\left\lvert\Delta_{g}^{l/2}u_{i}\right\rvert^{2}\,dv_{g}=\lambda_{i}\int_{M}u_{i}^{2^{\sharp}}\,dv_{g}.

Thanks to (
5.7
), there exists
C>0
such that (
5.16
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\lvert\Delta_{g}^{k/2}u_{i}\right\rvert^{2}\,dv_{g}+C\gamma_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{i}\int_{M}u_{i}^{2^{\sharp}}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}}\int_{M}u_{i}^{2^{\sharp}}\,dv_{g}.

It then follows from (
5.12
) and (
5.13
) that

(5.17)

\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}U_{i}\right\rvert^{2}dy+C\gamma_{i}\leq\frac{1}{K_{0}^{2}}\left(\int_{B_{0}(\varrho_{0})}U_{i}^{2^{\sharp}}dy\right)^{2/{2^{\sharp}}}+o(\gamma_{i}),

since
\int_{B_{0}(\varrho_{0})}U_{i}^{2^{\sharp}}dy\leq\left\|u_{i}\right\|_{L^{{2^{\sharp}}}(M)}^{2^{\sharp}}=1
. Recall that
\varrho_{0}<i_{g}/2<\varrho
, and let
\tilde{\chi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a cut-off function such that
\tilde{\chi}\equiv 1
in
B_{0}(\varrho_{0})
and
\tilde{\chi}\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{0}(\varrho)
. We have

\left(\int_{B_{0}(\varrho_{0})}U_{i}^{2^{\sharp}}dy\right)^{2/{2^{\sharp}}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(\tilde{\chi}U_{i}\big)^{2^{\sharp}}dy\right)^{2/{2^{\sharp}}},

and
\tilde{\chi}U_{i}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We use the optimal Sobolev’s inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and obtain

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(\tilde{\chi}U_{i}\big)^{2^{\sharp}}dy\right)^{2/{2^{\sharp}}}\leq K_{0}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\lvert\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}\big(\tilde{\chi}U_{i}\big)\right\rvert^{2}dy=K_{0}^{2}\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}\big(\tilde{\chi}U_{i}\big)\right\rvert^{2}dy.

Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}\big(\tilde{\chi}U_{i}\big)\right\rvert^{2}dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\tilde{\chi}^{2}\left\lvert\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}U_{i}\right\rvert^{2}dy+\mathcal{O}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{m}U_{i}\right\rvert^{2}dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}U_{i}\right\rvert^{2}dy+o(\gamma_{i})

by (
5.15
), so that finally

(5.18)

\left(\int_{B_{0}(\varrho_{0})}U_{i}^{2^{\sharp}}dy\right)^{2/{2^{\sharp}}}\leq K_{0}^{2}\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\Delta_{\xi}^{k/2}U_{i}\right\rvert^{2}dy+o(\gamma_{i})

as
i\to\infty
.
Putting (
5.17
) and (
5.18
) together, we have

C\gamma_{i}\leq o(\gamma_{i})

for some
C>0
which is independent of
i
. This is a contradiction and concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

Since we have proved that (
1.7
) is optimal with respect with the first constant, we can now optimize the constant corresponding to the lower-order terms. We set

B_{0}(g):=\inf\{B>0\text{ such that \eqref{eq:SoboptHk} holds with $B$}\},

then we have for all
u\in H^{k}(M)
,

(5.19)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{{2^{\sharp}}}(M)}^{2}\leq K_{0}^{2}\left\|\Delta_{g}^{k/2}u\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}+B_{0}(g)\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k-1}(M)}^{2}.

This inequality is optimal with respect to both constants, none of them can be improved. Testing (
5.19
) on the constant functions, we have immediately

We refer to
[

]
, and to
[

]
, for a more precise characterization of the optimal second constant
B_{0}(g)
and the corresponding extremals for (
5.19
) on a compact manifold, for the case

The contradiction argument developed here to prove Theorem
1.1
shows the following result along the way.

There exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that for all

, any positive solution

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth compact manifold of dimension
n
, and let
l
be a positive integer such that
n>2l
. For all
0<\varrho<i_{g}
, there exists
C>0
such that for all
z\in M
and
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{0}(\varrho))
, we have

(A.1)

\int_{M}\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l}\big(f\circ\exp_{z}^{-1}\big)\right\rvert_{g}^{2}\,dv_{g}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{l}f\right\rvert^{2}dy.

Moreover, there exists
0<\varrho_{0}<i_{g}
and
C_{0}>0
such that for all
z\in M
and
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{0}(\varrho_{0}))
,

(A.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{l}f\right\rvert^{2}dy\leq C_{0}\int_{M}\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l}\big(f\circ\exp_{z}^{-1}\big)\right\rvert_{g}^{2}\,dv_{g}.

Proof.

We use the properties of the local coordinates: writing
\tilde{g}:=\exp_{z}^{*}g
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\tilde{g}_{ij}(y)-\delta_{ij}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla_{\tilde{g}}^{l}f(y)\right\rvert_{\tilde{g}}

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{l}f(y)\right\rvert+\mathcal{O}\Big(\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}\char 12\relax{\nabla_{\xi}^{l}f(y)}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathcal{O}\Big(\left\lvert y\right\rvert\char 12\relax{\nabla_{\xi}^{l-1}f(y)}\Big)+\mathcal{O}\Big(\textstyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{l-2}\char 12\relax{\nabla_{\xi}^{m}f(y)}\Big)\qquad l\geq 2

for all
y\in B_{0}(\varrho)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We also have, for all
s>0
,

(A.3)

\left\lvert\int_{B_{z}(\varrho)}\left\lvert f\circ\exp_{z}^{-1}\right\rvert^{s}\,dv_{g}-\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert f\right\rvert^{s}dy\right\rvert\leq C\varrho^{2}\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert f\right\rvert^{s}dy.

Then, we compute

(A.4)

\left\lvert\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\nabla_{\tilde{g}}^{l}f\right\rvert_{\tilde{g}}^{2}dy-\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{l}f\right\rvert^{2}dy\right\rvert\\
\leq C\varrho^{2}\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{l}f\right\rvert^{2}dy+C\sum_{m=1}^{l-1}\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{m}f\right\rvert^{2}dy.

For
m\leq l-1
, we use the continuous embedding
D^{l,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow D^{m,2_{l-m}^{\sharp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
2_{l}^{\sharp}:=\frac{2n}{n-2l}
, and get with Hölder’s inequality

(A.5)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{m}f\right\rvert^{2}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\varrho^{n}\big)^{\frac{2(l-m)}{n}}\left(\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{m}f\right\rvert^{2_{l-m}^{\sharp}}\right)^{2/2_{l-m}^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varrho^{2}\int_{B_{0}(\varrho)}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{l}f\right\rvert^{2}dy.

Putting (
A.3
), (
A.4
), (
A.5
) together, this concludes the proof of (
A.1
) for all
\varrho<i_{g}
. Finally, taking a small enough
0<\varrho_{0}<i_{g}
, there is
C_{0}>0
such that

\int_{M}\left\lvert\nabla_{g}^{l}\big(f\circ\exp_{z}^{-1}\big)\right\rvert_{g}^{2}\,dv_{g}\geq(1-C\varrho_{0}^{2})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{l}f\right\rvert^{2}dy\geq\frac{1}{C_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}^{l}f\right\rvert^{2}dy.

∎

We are now in position to prove Lemma

Proof of Lemma

.

Let
\mu_{0}<\varrho

,
z\in M

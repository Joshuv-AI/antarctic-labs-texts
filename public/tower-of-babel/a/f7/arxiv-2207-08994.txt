Higher localization and higher branching laws
Wen-Wei Li

Abstract
For a connected reductive group G G and an affine smooth G G -variety X X over the complex numbers, the localization functor takes 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -modules to D X D_{X} -modules. We extend this construction to an equivariant and derived setting using the formalism of h-complexes due to Beilinson–Ginzburg, and show that the localizations of Harish-Chandra ( 𝔤 , K ) (\mathfrak{g},K) -modules onto X = H \ G X=H\backslash G have regular holonomic cohomologies when H , K ⊂ G H,K\subset G are both spherical reductive subgroups. The relative Lie algebra homologies and Ext \operatorname{Ext} -branching spaces for ( 𝔤 , K ) (\mathfrak{g},K) -modules are interpreted geometrically in terms of equivariant derived localizations. As direct consequences, we show that they are finite-dimensional under the same assumptions, and relate Euler–Poincaré characteristics to local index theorem; this recovers parts of the recent results of M. Kitagawa. Examples and discussions on the relation to Schwartz homologies are also included.

Introduction

1.1
Backgrounds

We work over the complex numbers. Let
G
be a connected reductive group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The main theme of this work begins with the functor

\mathscr{D}_{X}\underset{U(\mathfrak{g})}{\otimes}(\cdot):\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to\mathscr{D}_{X}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}

where
X
is a smooth variety with right
G
-action (we say that
X
is a
G
-variety), with sheaf of algebraic differential operators
\mathscr{D}_{X}
, and
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
(resp.
\mathscr{D}_{X}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
) is the abelian category of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules (resp. left
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-modules that are
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-quasi-coherent). The tensor product is taken using the homomorphism
j:U(\mathfrak{g})\to D_{X}:=\Gamma(X,\mathscr{D}_{X})
induced by
G
-action.

This functor is called
localization
. Its significance can be partly explained by the fact that when
X
is a homogeneous
G
-space, the fiber of
\mathscr{D}_{X}\underset{U(\mathfrak{g})}{\otimes}V
at
x\in X
is isomorphic to
V/\mathfrak{h}V
where
H:=\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(x)
; therefore, localization organizes
various
spaces of co-invariants into a geometric object over
X
.

The celebrated Beilinson–Bernstein localization is the special case when
X
is the flag variety of
G
, but allowing some twists on
\mathscr{D}_{X}
. It has tremendous consequences in the study of
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
and related structures. In this work we try to use localization in another way. Namely, we start with a homogeneous
G
-space
X
and study aspects of representation theory of
G
or its real form that are “relative” to
X
, by means of the localization functors.

Localization has been utilized in a similar flavor in the “group case”
G=H\times H
and
X=H
by D. Ben-Zvi and I. Ganev
[
6
]
, who applied it to study the asymptotics of matrix coefficients of admissible representations. They also related this construction to Beilinson–Bernstein localization by specialization at infinity. We remark that V. Ginzburg’s work
[
13
]
played an important role there; specifically, it ensures that the localized
D_{H}
-modules are regular holonomic and puts some control on its characteristic variety — in fact, their irreducible constituents are character
D_{H}
-modules.

A similar result of regularity is contained in
[
25
]
for localizations of Harish-Chandra
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules to
X:=H\backslash G
, where
H,K\subset G
are reductive spherical subgroups. By a
Harish-Chandra
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module
, we mean a
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module that is finitely generated over
\mathfrak{g}
and locally
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
-finite, where
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
is the center of
U(\mathfrak{g})
. By sphericity of
H
(ditto for
K
), we mean that the homogeneous
G
-space
H\backslash G
has an open Borel orbit. The proof is based on a variant of Ginzburg’s criterion of regularity in
[
13
]
. Note that reductivity implies
X
is affine, so we may work with
D_{X}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
instead of
\mathscr{D}_{X}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
.

Nonetheless, the usage of localization is made complicated by the following two facts, at least.

•

Given an equivariant morphism
f:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
between smooth
G
-varieties, there is no obvious way to relate the corresponding localizations via inverse or direct images;

•

Localization is a right exact functor, but it is non-exact in many cases of interest. This will be shown in Remark
5.5.9
.

1.2
Higher localization

In the first part of this work, we generalize these ideas to the equivariant and derived setting, i.e. to higher localizations.

First off, take a reductive subgroup
K\subset G
and let
(\mathfrak{g},K)\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
be the abelian category of
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules. In representation theory, one is especially interested in its subcategory of Harish-Chandra
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules.

Take an affine homogeneous space
X=H\backslash G
and let
(D_{X},K)\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
be the abelian category of
K
-equivariant
D_{X}
-modules. Since
j:U(\mathfrak{g})\to D_{X}
is equivariant, it is easily seen that the localization lifts to the equivariant level:

D_{X}\underset{U(\mathfrak{g})}{\otimes}(\cdot):(\mathfrak{g},K)\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to(D_{X},K)\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}.

We wish to left-derive this right exact functor in order to obtain “higher localizations”, and apply them to Harish-Chandra modules. Two issues arise immediately.

1.

Homological algebra for
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules is done in the classical way in
[
20
]
. On the other hand, the correct
K
-equivariant derived category of
D_{X}
-modules requires less naive techniques when
K\neq\{1\}
, such as that of Bernstein–Lunts
[
8
]
which replaces
X
by various resolutions
P\to X
, on which
K
acts freely (see also
[
1
]
).

This kind of “type mismatch” makes it non-trivial to derive equivariant localization. Nor is there any obvious way to define it as a compatible family on resolutions.

2.

Another option is to consider
(\mathfrak{g},K)\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to D_{X}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
instead, in order to avoid equivariant derived categories. This discards too many structures, thus impedes applications to higher branching laws. Moreover, since nonzero projectives in
(\mathfrak{g},K)\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
are never Harish-Chandra, it seems difficult to prove regularity or holonomicity in cohomologies, unless one addresses the first issue simultaneously.

For similar reasons, it seems unfeasible to define the derived localization through realization functor
[
1
, Theorem A.7.16]
.

Our approach to these problems is based on the formalism of
h-complexes
, reviewed in §
2
. This theory is presented in
[
9
]
, attributed to Beilinson–Ginzburg and the earlier work of Duflo–Vergne
[
12
]
; for subsequent developments, see
[

27

28

, so there is a natural homomorphism

of Lie algebras (the Lie brackets in

are commutators). A weak
(A,K)
-module is a vector space
M
with

•

a left

-module structure, say through

•

an algebraic

but the

-module. An h-complex over

is a complex

Higher branching

We hope that the results on
\mathbf{Loc}_{X}

In the setting of
p

\operatorname{Hom}_{H}(V|_{H},W)
where
V

.

The finiteness of

, who considers a broader setting of branching laws and obtains uniform bounds. His approach is to interpret relative Lie algebra cohomologies in terms of Zuckerman functors, and then pass to homologies by Poincaré duality. The approach in this work relies on standard results about constructible or regular holonomic equivariant derived categories; although this is conceptually straightforward, the resulting bound is less effective and involves more machinery.

In the special case

\mathrm{EP}_{\mathfrak{h}}(V|_{H},\mathbb{C}):=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}\dim\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathfrak{h}}(V|_{H},\mathbb{C})

.

1.4
Twist by characters

In arithmetic applications, one often has to study branching laws in which the trivial module
\mathbb{C}
of
H
is replaced by some character
\chi
, say for
p
-adic groups. In the setting of
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules, one should consider a
1
-dimensional
(\mathfrak{h},K^{H})
-module; by abusing notation, we identify it with the underlying character
\chi:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathbb{C}
. Denote by
\chi^{\vee}
its contragredient
(\mathfrak{h},K^{H})
-module
2
2

2

In the study of branching laws of real groups, many authors allow
\chi
to be a finite-dimensional module. It seems hard to incorporate this case into our formalism.
.

Since
H
is assumed to be reductive,
\chi
factors through the Lie algebra of some torus
S=H/\underline{H}
and can be identified with an element of
\mathfrak{s}^{*}
. The spaces

\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathfrak{h},K^{H}}(V|_{H},\chi),\quad\operatorname{H}_{n}(\mathfrak{h},K^{H};V|_{H}\otimes\chi^{\vee})

can still be interpreted via localization to
X=H\backslash G
, provided that we work with twisted differential operators (TDO’s) on
X
.

Specifically, we have an
S
-torsor (on the left)

\pi:\tilde{X}:=\underline{H}\backslash G\to H\backslash G=X

that is also
G
-equivariant (on the right). This situation is already considered by
[
9
]
, where it is called an
S
-monodromic
G
-variety.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{\chi}\subset\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{s})
be the maximal ideal corresponding to
\chi
. The sheaf of
\chi
-twisted differential operators on
X
is

\mathscr{D}_{X,\chi}:=(\pi_{*}\mathscr{D}_{\tilde{X}})^{S}\big/\mathfrak{m}_{\chi}(\pi_{*}\mathscr{D}_{\tilde{X}})^{S}.

In the affine setting,
D_{X,\chi}=\Gamma(X,\mathscr{D}_{X,\chi})
equals
D_{\tilde{X}}^{S}/\mathfrak{m}_{\chi}D_{\tilde{X}}^{S}
. There is again a
G
-equivariant homomorphism

j:U(\mathfrak{g})\to D_{X,\chi},

by which we define the
\chi
-twisted localization

\mathbf{Loc}_{X,\chi}=\mathbf{Loc}_{X,K,\chi}:{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathfrak{g},K)\to{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(D_{X,\chi},K).

All the earlier results have monodromic counterparts in this context recorded in §
6.4

6.4.7

\eta\in\mathfrak{h}
and

The key ingredient here is a monodromic version of the regularity Theorem

We remark that the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence also holds in the twisted (i.e. monodromic) and equivariant setting; one has to consider twisted constructible sheaves, though. We refer to

1.5
On the analytic picture

The discussions thus far focus on the algebraic setting. In the representation theory of real groups, one often takes
G
and
H
to be real, and take
K
(resp.
K^{H}
) corresponding to a maximal compact subgroup of
G
(resp.
H
). In studying
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{h},K^{H}}(V|_{H},\mathbb{C})
, one is more interested in those element which extend continuously to the Casselman–Wallach globalization
E
of
V
. This is the same as the continuous linear functionals on the space of co-invariants
E_{H}:=E\big/\sum_{h}(h-1)E
endowed with the quotient topology.

For higher branching laws in the analytic setting, it is thus natural to replace relative Lie algebra homologies by the
Schwartz homologies

\operatorname{H}^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(H;E|_{H}),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}

defined by Y. Chen and B. Sun
[
3
]
; they are locally convex topological spaces, not necessarily Hausdorff, and equal to
E_{H}
when
n=0
. In fact, they agree with the
smooth homologies
constructed earlier by P. Blanc and D. Wigner
[
10
]
.

In §
7.2
we will define the comparison maps

c_{n}(E):\operatorname{H}_{n}\left(\mathfrak{h},K^{H};E^{K\text{-fini}}\right)\to\operatorname{H}^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(H;E|_{H}),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

from the algebraic to the analytic picture, for every Casselman–Wallach representation
E
of
G
, where
E^{K\text{-fini}}
is the
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module of
K
-finite vectors in
E
.

It is natural to ask if
c_{n}(E)
is an isomorphism, at least when
H
is a reductive spherical subgroup of
G
after extension of scalars to
\mathbb{C}
. If it is indeed the case,
\operatorname{H}^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(H;E|_{H})
will be finite-dimensional since
\operatorname{H}_{n}(\mathfrak{h},K^{H};E^{K\text{-fini}})
is; in turn, this will imply
\operatorname{H}^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(H;E|_{H})
is Hausdorff.

It seems too early to pass verdict on the question of comparison. Its validity for
n=0
is equivalent to the automatic continuity theorem, which is open except when
H
is a symmetric subgroup
[
32
]
or
H=G
; another (non-reductive) example is when
H
is maximal unipotent
[
14
,
24
]
. Nonetheless, we will settle two cases in affirmative.

•

In Example
7.3.2
, we show that
c_{n}(E)
is always an isomorphism when
E
is
K^{H}
-admissible in the sense of T. Kobayashi. This includes the case when
H
is symmetric,
H/K^{H}\to G/K
is a holomorphic embedding of Hermitian symmetric domains and
E
is a unitary highest weight module. We refer to
[
23
]
for an overview of admissible restrictions.

•

In Examples
7.3.5
,
7.3.6
, we prove that
c_{n}(E)
is always an isomorphism when
G=\operatorname{SL}(2)
and
H
is a reductive spherical subgroup. This is done by an explicit computation with principal series, which suffices by a general argument of Hecht–Taylor (Proposition
7.3.4
). In fact, these computations show that
\operatorname{H}_{1}\left(\mathfrak{h},K^{H};E^{K\text{-fini}}\right)
can indeed be nonzero in this case.

In general, neither the finiteness nor Hausdorffness of
\operatorname{H}^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(H;E|_{H})
is known for
n>0
, even when
H
is reductive and spherical. See
[
3
]
for related discussions.

2.1
Review of dg-algebras and dg-modules

Below is a short review about dg-modules over dg-algebras. These materials are standard, and detailed expositions can be found in
[
8
, Part II]
or
[
34
]
.

First of all, we make
\mathsf{C}(\mathbb{C})
into a symmetric monoidal category by using Koszul’s sign rule, namely: the tensor product of complexes
M
and
N
are given by
(M\otimes N)^{n}=\bigoplus_{i+j=n}M^{i}\otimes N^{j}
, with
d(m\otimes n)=dm\otimes n+(-1)^{i}m\otimes dn
, and the braiding
M\otimes N\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}N\otimes M
is
m\otimes n\mapsto(-1)^{ij}n\otimes m
on
M^{i}\otimes N^{j}
.

Definition 2.1.1
.

If a complex
A
carries the structure of an algebra
3
3

3

Also known as a
monoid
in some textbooks.
in the symmetric monoidal category
\mathsf{C}(\mathbb{C})
, then
A
is said to be a
dg-algebra
.

Multiplication in a dg-algebra is given by a morphism
A\otimes A\to A
in
\mathsf{C}(\mathbb{C})
. In concrete terms, this means that there are linear maps
A^{i}\otimes A^{j}\to A^{i+j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
, satisfying

d(xy)=dx\cdot y+(-1)^{i}x\cdot dy

and are associative and unital with respect to some element in
A^{0}
.

It is customary to identify the complex
A
with
\bigoplus_{n}A^{n}
and view
d
as an endomorphism of that vector space. Note that dg-algebras in degree zero are simply algebras.

Definition 2.1.2
.

By the abstract formalism of algebras in symmetric monoidal categories, it makes sense to define left (resp. right)
A
-modules
M
, with scalar multiplication given by morphisms
A\otimes M\to M
(resp.
M\otimes A\to M
), which we call left (resp. right)
dg-modules
over
A
. Homomorphisms between dg-modules are defined as morphisms between complexes that respect scalar multiplications.

In concrete terms, a left dg-module over
A
is a complex
M
together with linear maps
A^{i}\otimes M^{j}\to M^{i+j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
, satisfying

d(am)=da\cdot m+(-1)^{i}a\cdot dm

and are associative and unital. Again, it is customary to identify
M
with the vector space
\bigoplus_{n}M^{n}
, so that
M
is also a left
A
-module in the non-dg sense. Right dg-modules over
A
are described similarly, and one can also define bimodules.

When
A=\mathbb{C}
(in degree zero), left and right dg-modules are nothing but complexes.

We now review the tensor product of dg-modules; see
[
34
, Definition 3.3.23]
for details.

Definition 2.1.3
.

Let
M
be a right dg-module and
N
be a left dg-module over a dg-algebra
A
. Let us form the vector space
M\underset{A}{\otimes}N
in the non-dg sense; the kernel of the natural linear map

\bigoplus_{n}(M\otimes N)^{n}\simeq(\bigoplus_{i}M^{i})\otimes(\bigoplus_{j}M^{j})\to M\underset{A}{\otimes}N

is readily seen to be a subcomplex of
M\otimes N
. In this way, we see that
M\underset{A}{\otimes}N
comes from a complex, which will also be denoted as
M\underset{A}{\otimes}N
.

If
M
(resp.
N
) is a dg-bimodule over
(R,A)
(resp. over
(A,S)
), where
R
and
S
are dg-algebras, then
M\underset{A}{\otimes}N
inherits a natural
(R,S)
-bimodule structure.

The
opposite dg-algebra

A^{\text{op}}
of
A
is the same complex with the multiplication
x\overset{\mathrm{op}}{\cdot}y:=(-1)^{ij}yx
for all
x\in A^{i}
and
y\in A^{j}
.
One passes between left dg-modules over
A
and right dg-modules over
A^{\mathrm{op}}
by the rule
ma:=(-1)^{ij}am
, for
a\in A^{i}
and
m\in M^{j}
. Henceforth, we will mainly consider left dg-modules.

Definition 2.1.4
.

A
dg-category
is a category
\mathcal{C}
enriched over the symmetric monoidal category
\mathsf{C}(\mathbb{C})
. In other words, to each pair
(X,Y)
of objects is assigned the
\operatorname{Hom}
-complex
\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\in\mathsf{C}(\mathbb{C})
, and there are morphisms of composition in
\mathsf{C}(\mathbb{C})
of the form

\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,Z)\otimes\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\to\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Z),

subject to strict associativty and unit laws. The non-dg version
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
can be recovered by taking
0
-cocycles in
\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
. We also write
\operatorname{End}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{C}}(X):=\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,X)
.

A
dg-functor

\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
between dg-categories is a functor whose action on
\operatorname{Hom}
sets upgrades to
\operatorname{Hom}
-complexes.

For example, for any abelian category
\mathcal{A}
, the category
\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{A})
is naturally a dg-category. In the case of
\mathsf{C}(\mathbb{C})
, the
\operatorname{Hom}
-complexes will be denoted as
\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}
. Let
M
and
N
be complexes, typical elements in
\operatorname{Hom}^{n}(M,N)
take the form
(f^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
with

.

Definition 2.1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a dg-category. Its opposite
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}
is the dg-category with the same objects, and
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}^{\bullet}(X,Y):=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\bullet}(Y,X)
, with morphisms of composition

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}^{p}(Y,Z)\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}^{q}(X,Y)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}^{p+q}(X,Z)

defined as
(-1)^{pq}
times the reversed composition in
\mathcal{C}
.

We now extend the definition of
\operatorname{Hom}
-complexes to dg-modules.

Definition 2.1.6
.

Let
M
and
N
be left dg-modules over a dg-algebra
A
. Define
\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{A}(M,N)
as the subcomplex of
\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}(M,N)
given by

\operatorname{Hom}^{n}_{A}(M,N):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{r|l}(f^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\operatorname{Hom}^{n}(M,N)&\forall i,j,\;\forall a\in A^{i},\;m\in M^{j}\\
&f^{i+j}(am)=(-1)^{ni}af^{j}(m)\end{array}\right\}.

If
M,N,L
are left dg-modules over
A
, we have the morphism of composition

\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{A}(N,L)\otimes\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{A}(M,N)\to\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{A}(M,L)

in
\mathsf{C}(\mathbb{C})
. We write
\operatorname{End}^{\bullet}_{A}(M):=\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{A}(M,M)
.

Remark 2.1.7
.

For every complex
M
, composition makes
\operatorname{End}^{\bullet}(M)
into a dg-algebra. It is routine to check (see eg.

) that given a dg-algebra

into a left dg-module over

Definition 2.1.8
.

For every dg-algebra

in order to emphasize that its objects are complexes.

.

The construction of

Proof.

The

For any complex

.

Let

2.2
Harish-Chandra dg-algebras

To begin with, let
K
be an affine algebraic group. Set
O_{K}:=\Gamma(K,\mathscr{O}_{K})
. An abstract representation
V
of
K
, possibly infinite-dimensional, is said to be
algebraic
if it arises from a
O_{K}
-comodule structure on
V
; in other words,
V
is the union of finite-dimensional subrepresentations
V_{i}
on which
K
acts through a homomorphism
\sigma_{i}:K\to\operatorname{GL}(V_{i})
of algebraic groups. In general, we say
v\in V
is
K
-algebraic if it belongs to some algebraic subrepresentation, and define

V^{K\text{-alg}}:=\{v\in V:\text{algebraic}\}.

This is the maximal
K
-algebraic subrepresentation of
V
. The assignment
V\mapsto V^{K\text{-alg}}
is functorial, and one readily checks that

V^{K\text{-alg}}=V^{K^{\circ}\text{-alg}}.

The class of algebraic representations is closed under subquotients, direct sums and tensor products. If
\sigma:K\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(V)
gives rise to an algebraic representation, then its derivative at identity

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma:\mathfrak{k}\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V)

makes sense: all reduces to the finite-dimensional case.

Definition 2.2.1
.

We say that
K
acts algebraically on a complex
M
, if
M^{n}
is an algebraic representation of
K
and
d^{n}:M^{n}\to M^{n+1}
is
K
-equivariant, for each
n
. In particular, this induces a homomorphism
K\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{C}(\mathbb{C})}(M)
of abstract groups.

Definition 2.2.2

(
[
9
, 1.9.1]
)
.

By a
Harish-Chandra dg-algebra
we mean a quadruplet

(A,K,\sigma,j),

or more succinctly a pair
(A,K)
, where:

•

A
is a dg-algebra and
K
is as above,

•

K
acts algebraically on
A
, and induces a homomorphism
\sigma:K\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{dga}}(A)
, where
\mathsf{dga}
is the category of dg-algebras;

•

j:\mathfrak{k}\to A^{0}\subset A
is a
K
-equivariant linear map satisfying

\displaystyle d\circ j=0,

\displaystyle j([\xi_{1},\xi_{2}])=[j(\xi_{1}),j(\xi_{2})]:=j(\xi_{1})j(\xi_{2})-j(\xi_{2})j(\xi_{1})

for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathfrak{k}
;

•

furthermore, we impose the condition that for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{k}
,

(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma)(\xi)=[j(\xi),\cdot]\;\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(A).

A morphism
(A,K)\to(A^{\prime},K^{\prime})
between Harish-Chandra dg-algebras consists of a homomorphism
\varphi:K\to K^{\prime}
between algebraic groups together with a
\varphi
-equivariant homomorphism
\psi:A\to A^{\prime}
between dg-algebras, such that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{k}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\prime}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Lie}\varphi}

commutes.

Definition 2.2.3
.

Let
(A,K)
be a Harish-Chandra dg-algebra. A (left) dg-module over
(A,K)
is a left dg-module

over

When

Translation functor

is a coboundary in
\operatorname{Hom}^{0}_{A,K}(M,N)
. The homotopy category
\mathsf{K}(A,K)
of
(A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}
has the same objects as
(A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}
, whilst

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{K}(A,K)}(M,N):=\operatorname{H}^{0}\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{A,K}(M,N)

for all objects

and

. The translation functor

passes to
\mathsf{K}(A,K)
, and we have
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{K}(A,K)}(M,N[n])\simeq\operatorname{H}^{n}\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{A,K}(M,N)
.

Acyclic objects

We say
M
is acyclic if it is acyclic as an object of
\mathsf{C}(\mathbb{C})
.

Quasi-isomorphisms

If

induces isomorphisms
\operatorname{H}^{n}(M)\to\operatorname{H}^{n}(N)
for every

, we say

is a quasi-isomorphism. This property depends only on the image of

in the homotopy category.

Mapping cones

Let
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A,K}(M,N)
. The mapping cone
\mathrm{Cone}(f)
taken in
\mathsf{C}(\mathbb{C})
is made into a dg-module over
(A,K)
by recalling that
\mathrm{Cone}(f)
is

as graded vector spaces, and we let
A
and
K
act on
M[1]
and
N
in the way prescribed before. The natural morphisms

are morphisms in
(A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}
. Note that this is a
K
-equivariant version of
[

, Definition 4.2.1]
.

The constructions above make
\mathsf{K}(A,K)
into a triangulated category: the distinguished triangles are the ones isomorphic to

M\xrightarrow{f}N\to\mathrm{Cone}(f)\xrightarrow{+1}

given before, where
f:M\to N
is any morphism in
(A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}
.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
\mathsf{D}(A,K)
be the Verdier quotient of
\mathsf{K}(A,K)
by acyclic objects; equivalently, it is obtained from
\mathsf{K}(A,K)
by inverting quasi-isomorphisms. We call
\mathsf{D}(A,K)
the

of
(A,K)
. It inherits the triangulated structure from
\mathsf{K}(A,K)
.

When

[

or
[

, Chapter 7]

In order to better understand

[

Definition 2.3.2
.

Let
M
be a dg-module over
(A,K)
.

•

We say

Q\;\text{is acyclic}\implies\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{A,K}(Q,M)\;\text{is acyclic};

equivalently,

Q\;\text{is acyclic}\implies\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{K}(A,K)}(Q,M)=0.

•

We say

Q\;\text{is acyclic}\implies\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{A,K}(M,Q)\;\text{is acyclic};

equivalently,

Q\;\text{is acyclic}\implies\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{K}(A,K)}(M,Q)=0.

Being K-injective (resp. K-projective) is thus a property within

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{K}(A,K)}(X,M)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{D}(A,K)}(X,M)

\displaystyle(\text{resp.}\;\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{K}(A,K)}(M,X)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{D}(A,K)}(M,X))

for all dg-module

(A,K)
. Cf.
[

, Theorems 10.1.13 and 10.2.9]
.

We now move to
t
-structure. We say

for all

.

Routine, see

Using the fact above, we equip

Now comes the definition of derived functors in an abstract context. More general formulations exist and can be found in

Definition 2.3.4
.

Let
(A,K)
(resp.
(A^{\prime},K^{\prime})

(resp.

(resp.
\operatorname{R}\!F
) from
\mathsf{D}

Whenever they exist,
\operatorname{L}\!F
and
\operatorname{R}\!F
fit into 2-cells:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{K}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{D}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{L}\!F}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{K}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{R}\!F}

The morphisms

(resp.

).

Example 2.3.5
.

The easiest example is the case when

is

The pull-back functors

from (

For general

Definition 2.3.6
.

Assume that

Proposition 2.3.8
.

Consider the situation of Definition

.

•

(2.3.2)

Given functors

provided that all these derived functors exist. We record the following standard result (see eg.

Corollary 2.3.9

Keep the notations of Definition

.

•

Suppose

and

.

•

and

.

The standard tool to ensure the preservation of K-injectives or K-projectives is adjunction.

Proposition 2.3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be dg-categories, each is of the form
(A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}
for some

. Suppose that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}:G}

is a pair of adjoint functors, and both

and

upgrade to dg-functors (see the discussions after (

)). Then

•

preserves K-projectives if
G
is exact;

•

G
preserves K-injectives if

is exact.

Proof.

Adjunction is described by morphisms
\eta:\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}}\to GF
and
\epsilon:FG\to\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
satisfying the triangular equalities. As

and

induces not only functorial bijections

i.e. a dg-adjunction. The assertions follow at once.

2.4
h-construction

Throughout this subsection, we consider a Harish-Chandra dg-algebra
(A,K)
with
A
concentrated at degree zero, i.e.
A
is an algebra coming with compatible homomorphisms

j:\mathfrak{k}\to A,\quad\sigma:K\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{alg}}(A).

Recall from Remark
2.2.4
that the
(A,K)
-modules are simply left
A
-modules equipped with compatible
K
-actions. The dg-category of complexes of
(A,K)
-modules is denoted by
\mathsf{C}(A,K)
.

Definition 2.4.1
.

With the conventions above, a
weak
(A,K)
-module
is a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
M
equipped with homomorphisms

\alpha:A\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(M),\quad\rho:K\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(M),

making
M
into a left
A
-module and an algebraic representation of
K
respectively, such that

\alpha(\sigma(k)a)=\rho(k)\alpha(a)\rho(k)^{-1}

holds in
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(M)
for all
k\in K
and
a\in A
, i.e.
\alpha
is
K
-equivariant.

Weak
(A,K)
-modules form an abelian category. Given a weak
(A,K)
-module
M
, define

w:=\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho-\alpha\circ j:\mathfrak{k}\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(M).

(2.4.1)

This map is clearly
K
-equivariant. It is also
A
-linear: indeed,

\displaystyle[\alpha(j(\xi)),\alpha(a)]

\displaystyle=\alpha\left([j(\xi),a]\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha\left(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi)\cdot a\right)=[\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\xi),\alpha(a)]

for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{k}
and
a\in A
; the last equality follows from the
K
-equivariance of
\alpha
. This entails
[w(\xi),\alpha(a)]=0
.

Therefore, a weak
(A,K)
-module is an
(A,K)
-module if and only if
w=0
.

Definition 2.4.2
.

Denote by
{}^{\mathrm{w}}\mathsf{C}(A,K)
the category of complexes of weak
(A,K)
-modules. The
\operatorname{Hom}
-complex in this dg-category is denoted by
{}^{\mathrm{w}}\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}
.

Our main reference for the h-construction below is
[
9
]
; the idea is attributed to Duflo–Vergne
[
12
]
and Beilinson–Ginzburg in
loc. cit.

Definition 2.4.3

(
[
9
, 1.5]
)
.

An
h-complex
over
(A,K)
is a complex
C=(C,d)
of weak
(A,K)
-modules together with a linear map

i:\mathfrak{k}\to\operatorname{End}^{-1}(C):=\operatorname{End}^{-1}_{\mathbb{C}}(C),\quad\xi\mapsto i_{\xi},

such that

(i)

ki_{\xi}k^{-1}=i_{\operatorname{Ad}(k)\xi}
for all
k\in K
;

(ii)

i_{\xi}
is
A
-linear;

(iii)

i_{\xi}i_{\eta}+i_{\eta}i_{\xi}=0
for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{k}
;

(iv)

di_{\xi}+i_{\xi}d=w(\xi)
(recall (
2.4.1
)).

The morphisms between h-complexes are morphisms between complexes of weak
(A,K)
-modules that commute with all
i_{\xi}
.

The homotopy condition (iv) implies that the cohomologies of an h-complex are actually
(A,K)
-modules.

We remark that h-complexes are called
equivariant complexes
in
[
29
,
27
,
28
,
19
]
.

Definition 2.4.4
.

Denote by
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(A,K)
the category of h-complexes over
(A,K)
.

Every h-complex is a complex of weak
(A,K)
-modules, whence the functor
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(A,K)\to{}^{\mathrm{w}}\mathsf{C}(A,K)
. We upgrade
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(A,K)
into a dg-category by defining
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}(C_{1},C_{2})
as the subcomplex of
{}^{\mathrm{w}}\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}(C_{1},C_{2})
:

{}^{\mathrm{h}}\operatorname{Hom}^{n}(C_{1},C_{2}):=\left\{(f^{l})_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\in{}^{\mathrm{w}}\operatorname{Hom}^{n}(C_{1},C_{2}):\forall l,\xi,\;f^{l-1}i_{\xi}=(-1)^{n}i_{\xi}f^{l}\right\}.

As usual, we say a morphism (resp. an object) of
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(A,K)
is a quasi-isomorphism (resp. acyclic) if it is so as a complex. The following definition thus makes sense.

Definition 2.4.5
.

Using
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}
, we define the homotopy category
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{K}(A,K)
of
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(A,K)
, and the

{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}(A,K)
is defined as its Verdier quotient by acyclic complexes, or equivalently by inverting quasi-isomorphisms.

The K-injective and K-projective h-complexes are also defined in this way. The derived functors in this setting are also called
h-derived functors
.

For an h-complex
C
, we make

into an h-complex by setting

i^{\prime}:\mathfrak{k}\to\operatorname{End}^{-1}(C[1]),\quad\xi\mapsto i^{\prime}_{\xi}:=-i_{\xi}.

Using this, mapping cones can be defined for any morphism

in
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(A,K)
. In this way,
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{K}(A,K)
and
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}(A,K)
become triangulated categories.

For every h-complex
C
, the “smart truncation”
\tau^{<0}C
as a subcomplex of weak
(A,K)
-modules is an h-complex, and so is
\tilde{\tau}^{\geq 0}C:=C/\tau^{<0}C
. Consequently,
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}(A,K)
is endowed with a
t
-structure, whose heart is exactly the category
(A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
of
(A,K)
-modules.

We wish to compare the h-derived category with the naive one. There is an evident dg-functor

\mathsf{C}((A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{Mod})\to{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(A,K)

(2.4.2)

by turning complexes over
(A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
into h-complexes with
i_{\xi}=0
. It induces a functor between homotopy categories and preserves acyclicity.

Recall that the derived category
\mathsf{D}((A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{Mod})
of
(A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
is a triangulated category with
t
-structure whose heart is
(A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
. For
\star\in\{\;,+,-,\mathrm{b}\}
, from (
2.4.2
) we obtain a functor

\alpha:\mathsf{D}^{\star}((A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{Mod})\to{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}^{\star}(A,K).

(2.4.3)

It is obviously
t
-exact.

All the assertion above can be checked by hand. Nonetheless, the formalism of Harish-Chandra dg-algebras provides a conceptually more satisfactory approach to the h-construction. We present a summary below after some preparations.

First, suppose that
A_{i}
are dg-algebras for
i=1,2
. By the general theory of algebras in a symmetric monoidal category, the tensor product
A_{1}\otimes A_{2}
of complexes underlies a dg-algebra.

Secondly, consider the dg Lie-algebra

\overline{\mathfrak{k}}:=\left[\mathfrak{k}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}\mathfrak{k}\right],\quad\text{degrees:}\;-1,0,

whose Lie bracket on
\overline{\mathfrak{k}}^{0}\otimes\overline{\mathfrak{k}}^{0}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{k}}^{0}\otimes\overline{\mathfrak{k}}^{-1}
equals the
[\cdot,\cdot]
for
\mathfrak{k}
, and is zero otherwise. Its universal enveloping dg-algebra is

U(\overline{\mathfrak{k}})=\underbracket{\bigwedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{k}}_{\deg\leq 0}\otimes\underbracket{U(\mathfrak{k})}_{\deg=0}.

The differential
d
is induced from the complex
\overline{\mathfrak{k}}
; explicit formulas will be given in §
3.2
. We let
K
act on
U(\overline{\mathfrak{k}})
by adjoint actions on both
\otimes
-slots.

Theorem 2.4.6
.

We have the following equivalences of dg-categories.

(i)

Take
B=A
. Then

(B,K)\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}\simeq\mathsf{C}((A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}).

(ii)

Take
B=U(\mathfrak{k})\otimes A
with diagonal
K
-action, where
U(\mathfrak{k})
is viewed as a dg-algebra concentrated at degree zero. Define
j_{B}:\mathfrak{k}\to B
by

j_{B}(\xi)=\xi\otimes 1+1\otimes j(\xi).

This makes
(B,K)
into a Harish-Chandra dg-algebra, and

(B,K)\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}\simeq{}^{\mathrm{w}}\mathsf{C}(A,K).

Specifically, the action of
\xi\in\mathfrak{k}\subset U(\mathfrak{k})
corresponds to
w(\xi)
; see (

).

(iii)

Take
B=U(\overline{\mathfrak{k}})\otimes A
with diagonal
K
-action. Define
j_{B}:\mathfrak{k}\to B
by

j_{B}(\xi)=j^{\natural}(\xi)\otimes 1+1\otimes j(\xi)

where

is the embedding
\mathfrak{k}\hookrightarrow 1\otimes U(\mathfrak{k})\subset U(\overline{\mathfrak{k}})
. This makes
(B,K)
into a Harish-Chandra dg-algebra, and

(B,K)\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}\simeq{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(A,K).

Specifically, the action of
\xi\in\mathfrak{k}\subset\bigwedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{k}
corresponds to
i_{\xi}
.

All these equivalences are identity on the underlying complexes; they preserve acyclicity, quasi-isomorphisms and homotopies.

Proof.

The case (i) is trivial. Case (iii) is
[

, 1.11.1]
, and (ii) is explained in
[

, p.2201]
.

2.4.2
) corresponds to the pullback induced by the
K
-equivariant homomorphism

\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}:U(\overline{\mathfrak{k}})\otimes A\to\mathbb{C}\otimes A\simeq A

of dg-algebras, where
\epsilon:U(\overline{\mathfrak{k}})\to\mathbb{C}
is the augmentation homomorphism. On the other hand, pull-back along the inclusion
U(\mathfrak{k})\otimes A\to U(\overline{\mathfrak{k}})\otimes A
amounts to forgetting the datum
i
in h-complexes.

2.5
Adjoint functors of oblivion

Consider Harish-Chandra dg-algebras
(A,K,\sigma,j)
,
(A^{\prime},K^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime},j^{\prime})
and a morphism
(A,K)\to(A^{\prime},K^{\prime})
. In most of the scenarios, the maps
A\to A^{\prime}
and
K\to K^{\prime}
will be inclusions. For this reason, the corresponding exact functor from (
2.2.1
) will be named as
oblivion
instead of pullback. Denote it by

\mathrm{oblv}:(A^{\prime},K^{\prime})\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}\to(A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}.

We begin with the change of dg-algebras, i.e. the case
K^{\prime}=K
. Consider a
K
-equivariant homomorphism
\varphi:A\to A^{\prime}
between dg-algebras such that
\varphi j=j^{\prime}
. Two constructions on a dg-module
M
over
(A,K)
will be needed.

•

Definition
2.1.3
affords the dg-module
A^{\prime}\underset{A}{\otimes}M
over
A^{\prime}
. Let
K
acts diagonally on it; this action is algebraic.

•

Since
A^{\prime}
is a dg-bimodule over
(A,A^{\prime})
, the
\operatorname{Hom}
-complex
\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{A}(A^{\prime},M)
is actually a left dg-module over
A^{\prime}
. It carries the standard
K
-action

f\xmapsto{k\in K}\underbracket{k}_{M}\circ f\circ\underbracket{k^{-1}}_{A^{\prime}},

(2.5.1)

which respects differentials and
A^{\prime}
-action. Now take
\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{A}(A^{\prime},M)^{K\text{-alg}}
.

We contend that they yield dg-modules over
(A^{\prime},K)
.

Lemma 2.5.1
.

Both constructions above yield dg-functors

(A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}\to(A^{\prime},K)\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}.

Proof.

We first check that
A^{\prime}\underset{A}{\otimes}M
is a dg-module over
(A^{\prime},K)
. Writing the actions by
K
,
A
and
A^{\prime}
as left multiplication, we have

\displaystyle(ka^{\prime}_{1})\cdot(a^{\prime}_{2}\otimes m)

\displaystyle=(ka^{\prime}_{1})a^{\prime}_{2}\otimes m

\displaystyle=k\cdot\left((a^{\prime}_{1}\cdot k^{-1}a^{\prime}_{2})\otimes k^{-1}m\right)

\displaystyle=k\cdot a^{\prime}_{1}\cdot k^{-1}\cdot(a^{\prime}_{2}\otimes m)

for all
a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2}\in A^{\prime}
,
k\in K
and
m\in M
. This is the first condition in Definition
2.2.3
.

As for the second condition, taking derivative of the diagonal
K
-action yields

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\xi)(a^{\prime}\otimes m)

\displaystyle=(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\prime}(\xi)a^{\prime})\otimes m+a^{\prime}\otimes(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\xi)m)

\displaystyle=[j^{\prime}(\xi),a^{\prime}]\otimes m+a^{\prime}\otimes(j(\xi)m)

\displaystyle=\left([j^{\prime}(\xi),a^{\prime}]+a^{\prime}j^{\prime}(\xi)\right)\otimes m

\displaystyle=(j^{\prime}(\xi)a^{\prime})\otimes m=j^{\prime}(\xi)\cdot(a^{\prime}\otimes m)

for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{k}
since
j^{\prime}=\varphi j
, and we are done.

Similarly, one readily checks that
\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{A}(A^{\prime},M)^{K\text{-alg}}
satisfies these two conditions.

Standard facts from the theory of dg-modules (see eg.
[
34
, §9.1]
) show that they are dg-functors if we forget
K
-actions. Our case follows by imposing
K
-equivariance.
∎

2.6
Invariants and co-invariants

Consider a Harish-Chandra dg-algebra
(A,K)
together with

•

\mathfrak{a}\subsetneq A
: a
K
-invariant dg-ideal;

•

N\lhd K
: a subgroup acting trivially on
A/\mathfrak{a}
.

From this we obtain a morphism
(A,K)\to(A/\mathfrak{a},K/N)
between Harish-Chandra dg-algebras. The pull-back dg-functor in this case is called
inflation

\mathrm{Infl}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}:(A/\mathfrak{a},K/N)\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}\to(A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}.

Proposition 2.6.1
.

There exists a left (resp. right) adjoint dg-functor
\mathrm{coInv}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}
(resp.
\mathrm{Inv}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}
) of
\mathrm{Infl}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}
.

Proof.

The construction is standard:
\mathrm{Inv}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}
(resp.
\mathrm{coInv}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}
) takes the maximal quotient dg-module (resp. dg-submodule) on which
\mathfrak{a}
and
N
act trivially.
∎

The transitivity
\mathrm{Infl}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}=\mathrm{Infl}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K}\mathrm{Infl}^{A/\mathfrak{a},K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}
implies

\displaystyle\mathrm{Inv}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{Inv}^{A/\mathfrak{a},K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}\mathrm{Inv}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K},

(2.6.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{coInv}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{coInv}^{A/\mathfrak{a},K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}\mathrm{coInv}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K}.

Note that the left and right adjoint of
\mathrm{Infl}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K}
have been given in §
2.5
. The right adjoint of
\mathrm{Infl}^{A,K}_{A,K/N}
is given in Theorem
2.5.6
when
K
is reductive.

Proposition 2.6.2
.

The functors
\mathrm{coInv}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}
(resp.
\mathrm{Inv}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}
) preserves K-projectives (resp. K-injectives).

Proof.

This follows from the exactness of inflation and Proposition
2.3.10
.
∎

In general,
\mathrm{Inv}
(resp.
\mathrm{coInv}
) is not exact, and one has to consider its right (resp. left) h-derived functor, provided that
(A,K)\text{-}\mathsf{dgMod}
or some suitable subcategory has enough K-injectives (resp. K-projectives). For these h-derived functors, the adjunction to
\mathrm{Infl}^{A,K}_{A/\mathfrak{a},K/N}
still holds on the level of derived categories.

Example 2.6.3
.

The constructions below for tensor products and internal

’s are taken from

. Let

\displaystyle\eta(x\otimes y)

\displaystyle k(x\otimes y)

\displaystyle=i^{M}_{\xi}(x)\otimes y+(-1)^{p}x\otimes i^{N}_{\xi}(y),\quad\xi\in\mathfrak{k},

Let

and

\displaystyle(kf)(m)=k(f(k^{-1}m)),\quad k\in K,

.

3.2
Standard resolutions

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra. The standard complex

is given by

\cdots\to U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\bigwedge^{1}\mathfrak{g}\to U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\bigwedge^{0}\mathfrak{g}

in degrees
\ldots,-2,-1,0
and zero elsewhere. The differentials

\partial_{n}:U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}\to U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\bigwedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle u\otimes(\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}u\xi_{i}\otimes(\xi_{1}\wedge\cdots\widehat{\xi_{i}}\cdots\wedge\xi_{n})

\displaystyle+\sum_{p<q}(-1)^{p+q}u\otimes([\xi_{p},\xi_{q}]\wedge\cdots\widehat{\xi_{p}}\cdots\widehat{\xi_{q}}\cdots)

where
\widehat{\cdots}
means terms to be neglected.

Let
U(\mathfrak{g})
act on each

by left multiplication, and take the augmentation homomorphism

\epsilon:U(\mathfrak{g})=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\bigwedge^{0}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{C}.

It is well known
[
20
, Theorem 2.122]
that

furnishes a K-projective resolution of the trivial
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\mathbb{C}
.

We now consider the data
(\mathfrak{g},K)
of §
3.1
. View
\mathbb{C}
as the trivial
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module.

Theorem 3.2.1

(P. Pandžić
[
28
, Theorem 3.2.6]
)
.

Let
K
act diagonally on each term of
N\mathfrak{g}
, and define

\displaystyle i_{\xi}:U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\to U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\bigwedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle u\otimes\lambda

\displaystyle\mapsto-u\otimes(\xi\wedge\lambda)

for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{k}
,
u\in U(\mathfrak{g})
,
\lambda\in\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}
and
n\geq 0
. Then

is an h-complex over
(\mathfrak{g},K)
.

If

is reductive, then
\epsilon:N\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{C}
is a K-projective resolution in the sense of h-complexes.

The following result is essentially a formal consequence of Theorem
3.2.1
.

Theorem 3.2.2

(P. Pandžić
[
27
, Theorem 3.5]
)
.

Assume that

is reductive. For every h-complex

over
(\mathfrak{g},K)
, the morphism

Therefore, the homotopy category

of h-complexes have enough K-projectives for

as long as

The upshot is that there are canonical K-projective resolutions

for all

in

when

(take

).

(3.3.1)

3.4
A lemma on restriction

Here we consider two pairs
(\mathfrak{g},K)
and
(\mathfrak{h},T)
as in §
3.1
, assuming:

•

\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
;

•

T
is a subgroup of
K
;

•

the maps
K\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
,
T\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{h})
,
\mathfrak{k}\to\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{t}\to\mathfrak{h}
are compatible with the inclusions above.

Therefore we obtain a morphism of pairs
(U(\mathfrak{h}),T)\to(U(\mathfrak{g}),K)
in the sense of §
2.2
, and similarly for the Harish-Chandra dg-algebras obtained by h-construction.

Below is the h-counterpart of a standard result
[
20
, Proposition 2.57 (c)]
about restriction (or oblivion); the proof is also similar to the one in
loc. cit.

Lemma 3.4.1
.

Suppose that
T
is reductive. Then the functor
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{g},K)\to{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{h},T)
is exact and preserves K-projectives.

Proof.

Exactness is known. As for the preservation of K-projectives, we break the restriction into

{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{g},K)\to{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{g},T)\to{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{h},T).

The first step preserves K-projectives by Corollary
2.5.7
. In view of Proposition
2.3.10
, it suffices to show that
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{g},T)\to{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{h},T)
has a right adjoint which is exact and upgrades to a dg-functor.

The desired right adjoint dg-functor is given by Proposition
2.5.4
(ii), namely

\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{U(\mathfrak{h})}(U(\mathfrak{g}),\cdot)^{T\text{-alg}}:{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{h},T)\to{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{g},T).

It remains to show that
\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{U(\mathfrak{h})}(U(\mathfrak{g}),M)^{T\text{-alg}}
is acyclic for every acyclic h-complex
M
over
(\mathfrak{h},T)
.

By reductivity, we may choose an
T^{\circ}
-invariant subspace
\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathfrak{g}
under
\operatorname{Ad}
, such that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{q}
. Next, apply
[
20
, Lemma 2.56]
(with
L=\{1\}
and swapping left and right) to obtain an isomorphism

U(\mathfrak{h})\otimes\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{q})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}U(\mathfrak{g})\quad\text{as $\mathfrak{h}$-modules.}

In
loc. cit.
the map is
u\otimes v\mapsto u\sigma(v)
where
\sigma:\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{q})\to U(\mathfrak{g})
is symmetrization. Hence it is also
T^{\circ}
-equivariant.

Hence there is a
T^{\circ}
-equivariant (relative to (
2.5.1
)) isomorphism in
\mathsf{C}(\mathbb{C})
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{U(\mathfrak{h})}(U(\mathfrak{g}),M)^{T^{\circ}\text{-alg}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C}}(\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{q}),M)^{T^{\circ}\text{-alg}}

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\mapsto\varphi|_{1\otimes\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{q})}.

Consider any algebraic representation
W
of
T^{\circ}
and a short exact sequence
0\to V^{\prime}\to V\to V^{\prime\prime}\to 0
of algebraic representations of
T^{\circ}
. Taking a compact real form of
T^{\circ}
and passing from algebraic to locally
T^{\circ}(\mathbb{R})
-finite modules, by
[
20
, Proposition 1.18b]
the short exact sequence splits. As a consequence,

0\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(W,V^{\prime})^{T^{\circ}\text{-alg}}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(W,V)^{T^{\circ}\text{-alg}}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(W,V^{\prime\prime})^{T^{\circ}\text{-alg}}\to 0

is acyclic in
\mathsf{C}(\mathbb{C})
.

From this fact,
\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C}}(\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{q}),M)^{T^{\circ}\text{-alg}}
and
\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{U(\mathfrak{h})}(U(\mathfrak{g}),M)^{T^{\circ}\text{-alg}}
are seen to be acyclic. It remains to recall that
(\cdots)^{T\text{-alg}}\simeq(\cdots)^{T^{\circ}\text{-alg}}
.
∎

Basic definitions

4.2
Inverse images

There are two basic operations on derived categories of
D
-modules: inverse image (suitably shifted) and de Rham push-forward. The goal here is to define inverse images for h-complexes on smooth affine
K
-varieties. We do not consider de Rham push-forward in this work.

For every morphism
f:X\to Y
between smooth varieties, we set

\mathrm{rd}(f):=\dim X-\dim Y.

Assuming that there are enough flat
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-modules (eg. when
Y
is quasi-projective), the basic theory of
D
-modules provides a functor

f^{\bullet}=f^{!}[-\mathrm{rd}(f)]:\mathsf{D}^{-}(Y)\to\mathsf{D}^{-}(X).

•

The
f^{\bullet}
above is the inverse image functor denoted by
\operatorname{L}\!f^{*}
in
[
15
, p.33]
, the left derived functor of
\mathscr{D}_{X\to Y}\underset{f^{-1}\mathscr{D}_{Y}}{\otimes}f^{-1}(\cdot)
;

•

The
f^{!}
is denoted by
f^{\dagger}
in
loc. cit.
, and
f^{!}
corresponds to the
!
-pullback under Riemann–Hilbert correspondence when applied to regular holonomic complexes.

The effect of
\mathscr{D}_{X\to Y}\underset{f^{-1}\mathscr{D}_{Y}}{\otimes}f^{-1}(\cdot)
is to take the pullback of a
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-module as a quasi-coherent sheaf, and then equip it with a natural
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module structure.

If
f
is a morphism between
K
-varieties, then
\mathscr{D}_{X\to Y}
is
K
-equivariant, and this functor can be lifted to the level of h-complexes, cf. Proposition
2.5.4
(i). The point is to take its left h-derived functor. This splits into two cases.

Smoooth inverse image

Suppose
f
is smooth, then
\mathscr{D}_{X\to Y}\underset{f^{-1}\mathscr{D}_{Y}}{\otimes}f^{-1}(\cdot)
preserves acyclic objects, hence it induces a
t
-exact functor

f^{\bullet}:{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}(D_{Y},K)\to{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}(D_{X},K)

as well as
f^{!}:=f^{\bullet}[\mathrm{rd}(f)]
.

General inverse image

For general
f
, we shall assume
K
is reductive. Theorem
2.3.7
ensures that
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(D_{Y},K)
has enough K-projectives. Hence we may define the left h-derived functor

f^{\bullet}:{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}^{-}(D_{Y},K)\to{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}^{-}(D_{X},K)

and
f^{!}:=f^{\bullet}[\mathrm{rd}(f)]
. Note that
f^{\bullet}
can be computed from K-flat resolutions, and K-projective implies K-flat (cf.
[
34
, Proposition 10.3.4]
).

Let

From this we obtain a pair

.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}(D_{X},K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{D}(\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{D}(X).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Loc}_{X,K}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{X}\underset{U(\mathfrak{g})}{\overset{\mathrm{L}}{\otimes}}(\cdot)}

Proof.

The starting point is the commutativity of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{g},K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(D_{X},K)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(D_{X},T).}

The vertical arrows are exact and preserve K-projectives by Corollary

Proposition 5.1.6
.

Let
V
be a
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module. There is a canonical isomorphism of

-equivariant
D_{X}
-modules

\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathbf{Loc}_{X}(V)\simeq D_{X}\underset{U(\mathfrak{g})}{\otimes}V;

acts by left multiplication and

Proof.

As

. Now take the h-complex

. Its

∎

In view of this result,

or its cohomologies can be seen as “higher localization”.

The localization functor has the following symmetries. Let

be the algebra of

-invariant differential operators on

. For every

and every h-complex

over
(D_{X},K)

of the h-complex

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{z}^{n}:D_{X}\underset{U(\mathfrak{g})}{\otimes}P^{n}

\displaystyle\to D_{X}\underset{U(\mathfrak{g})}{\otimes}P^{n}

\displaystyle\mapsto Dz\otimes p.

This is functorial in

(5.1.1)

In fact, the construction works over any

5.3
Statement of higher regularity

The assumptions on
(\mathfrak{g},K)
and

from §

remain in force.

Definition 5.3.1
.

An object of
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}(D_{X},K)
is said to be

(resp.

) if all its cohomologies are holonomic (resp. regular holonomic) as

-modules. This gives rise to a full triangulated subcategory
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}_{\mathrm{h}}(D_{X},K)
(resp.
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}_{\mathrm{rh}}(D_{X},K)
) of
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}(D_{X},K)
, and one may impose boundedness conditions as well.

Inside the equivariant derived category
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{K}(X)
, we also have
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{K,\mathrm{h}}(X)
(resp.
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{K,\mathrm{rh}}(X)
), the full triangulated subcategory of objects with holonomic (resp. regular holonomic) cohomologies. It matches
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{h}}(D_{X},K)
(resp.
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{h}}(D_{X},K)
) under Beilinson’s equivalence (Theorem
4.3.4
).

For every
D_{X}
-module
L
, denote by
\mathrm{Ch}(L)\subset T^{*}X
its characteristic variety. We have the

\bm{\mu}:T^{*}X\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

By choosing a base point, every homogeneous
G
-spaces take the form

, and

. Then
\bm{\mu}
maps
[\lambda,g]
to
\operatorname{Ad}^{*}(g^{-1})\lambda
(co-adjoint action) for all
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
and

.

Let
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
denote the nilpotent cone.

Definition 5.3.2
.

Let

be the full subcategory of
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{rh}}(D_{X},K)

for all

, where
\operatorname{H}^{n}(L)
is viewed merely as a
D_{X}
-module.

This is a full triangulated subcategory by standard properties of characteristic varieties
[
15
, §2.2]
. Again, it has the counterpart
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{K,\mathrm{rh}+}(X)
inside
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{K,\mathrm{rh}}(X)
.

Definition 5.3.3
.

A normal
G
-variety is said to be
spherical
if there is an open

-orbit in
X
for some (equivalently, any) Borel subgroup
B\subset G
. A subgroup

is said to be

if the homogeneous

-space

is spherical.

We are ready to state the higher regularity of localizations.

Theorem 5.3.4

Let
G
be a connected reductive group. Suppose that

•

is an affine spherical homogeneous

•

{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{rh}+}(D_{X},K)

The proof will be split into three chunks in §

, the hardest one among which will be settled in §

.

Corollary 5.3.5

Under the assumptions of Theorem

, let
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{HC}}(\mathfrak{g},K)

consisting of objects whose cohomologies are Harish-Chandra

restricts to

Since

is triangulated, one can truncate and shift to reduce to the case of Harish-Chandra

∎

We remark that in view of Theorems

and

However, the construction of

passes through h-derived categories.

Hereafter,

We say a left module

Lemma 5.5.1

Suppose

6.1
General setting of branching laws

Suppose we are given a pair
(\mathfrak{i},K^{I})
(see §
3.1
) and its subpair
(\mathfrak{h},K^{H})
, where
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{i}
and
K^{H}\subset K^{I}
; see §
3.4
for the precise conditions on subpairs. This induces the diagonal embedding of pairs

(\mathfrak{h},K^{H})\to(\mathfrak{i}\times\mathfrak{h},K^{I}\times K^{H}).

On the first component it is
U(\mathfrak{h})\to U(\mathfrak{i})\otimes U(\mathfrak{h})
, given by
\theta\mapsto\theta\otimes 1+1\otimes\theta
for all
\theta\in\mathfrak{h}
. On the second component it is
k\mapsto(k,k)
for all

.

Consider h-complexes
M
over
(\mathfrak{i},K^{I})
and
N
over
(\mathfrak{h},K^{H})
. Taking tensor product yields an h-complex over
(\mathfrak{i}\times\mathfrak{h},K^{I}\times K^{H})
, which we denote as
M\boxtimes N
.

Definition 6.1.1
.

Denote by
M|_{H}
the restriction of
M
to an h-complex over
(\mathfrak{h},K^{H})
. Define
(M\boxtimes N)|_{H}
similarly via the diagonal embedding. Ditto for the functors induced on derived categories.

By the general formalism of §
2.3
, we can define
\operatorname{RHom}
between h-complexes over these pairs; they will be denoted as
\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathfrak{h},K^{H}}
and so on.

Proposition 6.1.2
.

Assume
K^{H}
and
K^{I}
are reductive. Let
M
(resp.
N
) be an h-complex over
(\mathfrak{i},K^{I})
(resp.
(\mathfrak{h},K^{H})
). Denote by
N^{\vee}
the contragredient of
N
, as in Definition
3.1.2
. There is a canonical isomorphism in
\mathsf{D}(\mathbb{C})

\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathfrak{h},K^{H}}\left(M|_{H},N^{\vee}\right)\simeq\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathfrak{h},K^{H}}\left((M\boxtimes N)|_{H},\mathbb{C}\right).

The same isomorphism also holds for complexes of
(\mathfrak{i},K^{I})
-modules and
(\mathfrak{h},K^{H})
-modules, with the corresponding
\operatorname{RHom}
.

Proof.

We will only sketch the case of h-complexes; the other case is similar and well-known.

Take a K-projective resolution
P\to M
in
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{i},K^{I})
. Then so is
P|_{H}\to M|_{H}
in
{}^{\mathrm{h}}\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{h},K^{H})
by Lemma
3.4.1
. We claim that
(P\boxtimes N)|_{H}\to(M\boxtimes N)|_{H}
is a K-projective resolution as well.

Since
((\cdot)\boxtimes N)|_{H}\simeq(\cdot)|_{H}\otimes N
is exact, the above is indeed a quasi-isomorphism. By
[
27
, Lemma 3.2]
,
(\cdot)|_{H}\otimes N
has a right adjoint dg-functor given by internal
\operatorname{Hom}
(see §
3.1
)

\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C}}(N,(\cdot)|_{H})^{K_{H}\text{-alg}}.

As seen in the proof of Lemma
3.4.1
, this right adjoint is exact, thus the claim follows.

All in all,
\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathfrak{h},K^{H}}\left(M|_{H},N^{\vee}\right)
is represented by the complex

{}^{\mathrm{h}}\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak{h},K^{H}}\left(P|_{H},N^{\vee}\right)\simeq{}^{\mathrm{h}}\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak{h},K^{H}}\left(P|_{H}\otimes N,\mathbb{C}\right)

where the aforementioned dg-adjunction is applied once again. By the claim, the right-hand side represents
\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathfrak{h},K^{H}}\left((M\boxtimes N)|_{H},\mathbb{C}\right)
.
∎

Cf.
[
30
, Proposition 2.6]
for the case of
p
-adic groups.

Under the assumptions of reductivity, the study of general

-branching

{}^{\mathrm{h}}\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathfrak{h},K^{H}}(M|_{H},N^{\vee})

for various

thus reduces to the special case

(resp.

) by

(resp.

-modules instead of h-complexes.

We record another easy fact about

)

7.1

In this subsection, we work on the analytic side of representation theory. Therefore, we take

•

•

They are both Lie groups, and we will consider continuous representations of them.

Let

We refer to
[
11
]
for all the details and terminologies.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the complexified Lie algebra of
G
. Every smooth representation
E
of
G
gives rise to an
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module in a generalized sense: it is a vector space equipped with compatible structures of
\mathfrak{g}
-module and
K
-module; unlike §
3.1
, the
K
-action here is not assumed to be algebraic or locally finite, but only smooth.

Let
\mathfrak{p}:=\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k}
, on which
K
acts; we may and do choose a decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
as
K
-modules, and denote by
\mathcal{P}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{p}
the corresponding projection.

Definition 7.1.1
.

For an
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module
E
, the relative Lie algebra homologies of
E
are

\operatorname{H}_{n}(\mathfrak{g},K;E):=\operatorname{H}_{n}\left(C(\mathfrak{g},K;E)\right)

where the
standard complex

C(\mathfrak{g},K;E)
for
E
is defined by

C_{n}(\mathfrak{g},K;E):=(\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p})\underset{K}{\otimes}E,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Here we make the left
K
-module
\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}
into a right one by
uk:=k^{-1}u
, so
\otimes_{K}
is simply the tensor product over the abstract group algebra
\mathbb{C}[K]
. Alternatively,
\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}E
is also the space of co-invariants (algebraically) of
\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\otimes E
under the diagonal
K
-action.

The boundary maps in the complex

\partial_{n}:C_{n}(\mathfrak{g},K;E)\to C_{n-1}(\mathfrak{g},K;E)

for
n\geq 1
are the usual ones (cf.
[
20
, (2.126b)]
):

\partial_{n}((\xi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\xi_{n})\otimes v)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}(\cdots\wedge\widehat{\xi_{i}}\wedge\cdots)\otimes\xi_{i}v\\
+\sum_{p<q}(-1)^{p+q}(\mathcal{P}[\xi_{p},\xi_{q}]\wedge\cdots\widehat{\xi_{p}}\cdots\widehat{\xi_{q}}\cdots)\otimes v.

Now let
E
be a smooth Fréchet representation of
G
of moderate growth. Then each
C_{n}(\mathfrak{g},K;E)
gets topologized, each
\partial_{n}
is continuous, and the homologies are endowed with quotient topologies. In
[
11
, Theorem 7.7]
it is proved that

\operatorname{H}^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(G;E)\simeq\operatorname{H}_{n}(\mathfrak{g},K;E),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

(7.1.1)

canonically and topologically.

Definition 7.1.2
.

Let
E
be a smooth Fréchet representation of
G
of moderate growth. Denote by
E^{K\text{-fini}}
the subspace of
K
-finite vectors in
E
.

Therefore
E^{K\text{-fini}}
is an
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module in the usual algebraic sense. By functoriality and (
7.1.1
), we obtain canonical linear maps

\operatorname{H}_{n}\left(\mathfrak{g},K;E^{K\text{-fini}}\right)\to\operatorname{H}^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(G;E),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Proposition 7.1.3
.

Let
E
be a smooth Fréchet representation of
G
of moderate growth. For every
n
, we have

\operatorname{H}_{n}\left(\mathfrak{g},K;E^{K\text{-fini}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\operatorname{H}^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(G;E)

as vector spaces.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{Irr}(K)
be the set of isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
K
. Let
\mathcal{M}(K)
be the unital algebra of bounded complex Borel measures on
K
under convolution. Let
R(K)
be the non-unital subalgebra comprised of left
K
-finite (equivalently, right
K
-finite) elements, which is also spanned by the matrix coefficients from
\mathrm{Irr}(K)
once we fix a Haar measure. See
[
20
, I.2]
.

By continuity and completeness,
\mathcal{M}(K)
acts on
E
, thus so does
R(K)
.

For each
\gamma\in\mathrm{Irr}(K)
, define

\chi_{\gamma}:=\dim\gamma\cdot\Theta_{\gamma^{\vee}}\cdot\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k}{\mathrm{mes}(K)}\;\in R(K),

where
\Theta_{\gamma^{\vee}}
is the character of the contragredient
\gamma^{\vee}
; see
[
20
, (1.23)]
. This is an idempotent in
R(K)
which is
K
-invariant under conjugation.

For every finite subset
\Gamma
of
\mathrm{Irr}(K)
, we define
\chi_{\Gamma}:=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\chi_{\gamma}
. This is still a
K
-invariant idempotent. When applied to locally
K
-finite representations, its effect is to project to the
\Gamma
-isotypic part.

The evident maps
C_{n}(\mathfrak{g},K;E^{K\text{-fini}})\to C_{n}(\mathfrak{g},K;E)
give a morphism between complexes. It suffices to show it is an isomorphism in each degree
n
.

First, we prove that
C_{n}(\mathfrak{g},K;E^{K\text{-fini}})\to C_{n}(\mathfrak{g},K;E)
is surjective. Take
\Gamma
that contains all irreducible constituents of
\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}
. Let
r\mapsto r^{\prime}
be the anti-involution of
R(K)
induced by
k\mapsto k^{-1}
. For every
\omega\otimes v\in\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}E
, we claim that

\omega\otimes v=\chi_{\Gamma}\omega\otimes v=\omega\otimes\chi^{\prime}_{\Gamma}v.

Indeed, the first equality follows from the choice of
\Gamma
. As for the second one, in the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 7.7]
, a “strong projective resolution” (45) of
E
is shown to be topologically isomorphic to
C(\mathfrak{g},K;E)
after taking
(\cdot)_{G}
. It is part of the theory of Schwartz homologies, especially
[
11
, Theorem 5.9]
, that taking
(\cdot)_{G}
of such a resolution produces Fréchet spaces in each degree. Hence the algebraic co-invariants in the formation of
\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}v
are actually topological, i.e.

\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}E=(\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\otimes E)\bigg/\overline{\langle k\omega\otimes kv=\omega\otimes v:k\in K,\ldots\rangle}.

This implies
\chi_{\Gamma}\omega\otimes v=\omega\otimes\chi^{\prime}_{\Gamma}v
in
\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}E
, by approximating
\chi_{\Gamma}
by Dirac measures. Surjectivity follows since
\chi_{\Gamma}E=E^{\Gamma}
, the
\Gamma
-isotypic part of
E^{K\text{-fini}}
.

Let us show injectivity. By general properties of
\otimes
over
\mathbb{C}[K]
, we have

\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}(E^{K\text{-fini}})=\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}\varinjlim_{\Gamma}E^{\Gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\leftarrow}}\varinjlim_{\Gamma}\left(\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}E^{\Gamma}\right)

where the
\varinjlim
over finite subsets
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{Irr}(K)
is filtered. The map from the leftmost term to
\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}E
is determined by the compatible family of evident maps

\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}E^{\Gamma}\to\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}E.

Fixing
\Gamma
, the map above is a split injection since
\chi_{\Gamma}
is a
K
-invariant idempotent, so

E=\chi_{\Gamma}E\oplus(\mathrm{id}-\chi_{\Gamma})E=E_{\Gamma}\oplus(\mathrm{id}-\chi_{\Gamma})E

as
\mathbb{C}[K]
-modules. As the filtered
\varinjlim
of injections is still injective, this shows the injectivity of
\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}(E^{K\text{-fini}})\to\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}E
.

Note that the same method shows that
\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}E^{\Gamma}\to\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{p}\underset{K}{\otimes}E^{\Gamma^{\prime}}
is split injective whenever
\Gamma\subset\Gamma^{\prime}
.
∎

7.2
Comparison map

We now consider almost linear Nash groups

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\subset}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\subset}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\subset}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\subset}

(7.2.1)

analogously to the algebraic setting of (

), the assumptions now being

•

G
is reductive,

•

K
(resp.
K^{H}
) is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
(resp.
H
); this implies

.

We consider
Casselman–Wallach representations
of
G
, i.e. smooth admissible Fréchet representation of moderate growth; taking
K
-finite vectors establishes an equivalence between this category and the category of Harish-Chandra

-modules. See
[

.

For a smooth Fréchet representation

of

of moderate growth, its restriction

to

is still smooth Fréchet of moderate growth, by unraveling definitions. However, admissibility is usually lost.

In view of (
7.1.1
) applied to
H
and
K^{H}
, the inclusion
E^{K\text{-fini}}\subset E
induces natural linear maps

c_{n}(E):\operatorname{H}_{n}\left(\mathfrak{h},K^{H};E^{K\text{-fini}}\right)\to\operatorname{H}^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(H;E|_{H}),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

(7.2.2)

between vector spaces.

Definition 7.2.1
.

We call (
7.2.2
) the
comparison maps
for the Casselman–Wallach representation
E
relative to the data (
7.2.1
).

The following question is thus natural.

For what data (
7.2.1
), representations
E
and
n
is
c_{n}(E)
an isomorphism?

Remark 7.2.2
.

In view of Proposition
7.1.3
, the map
c_{n}(E)
is the same as the

\operatorname{H}_{n}\left(\mathfrak{h},K^{H};E^{K\text{-fini}}\right)\to\operatorname{H}_{n}\left(\mathfrak{h},K^{H};E^{K^{H}\text{-fini}}\right)

induced by
E^{K\text{-fini}}\subset E^{K^{H}\text{-fini}}
.

For such questions, it is usual to focus on the case when
H
is a real spherical subgroup of
G
. An affirmative answer for all

will have strong consequences. For example, in degree
n=0
it amounts to
automatic continuity
for invariant linear functionals (cf.
[
2
, Theorem A]
), which are hard-core results available only in sporadic cases, for example when (i)
n=0
and
H\subset G
is a symmetric subgroup
[
32
]
, or (ii) when
n
is arbitrary and
H\subset G
is a maximal unipotent subgroup
[
14
,
24
]
.

In order to apply the earlier results, let us assume further that

•

all the groups
G
,
K
,
H
,
K^{H}
arise from the algebraic setting (
6.2.1
), namely they arise from
\mathbb{R}
-points of affine groups;

•

H
is a reductive spherical subgroup of
G
, when viewed as complex groups.

Since

is a Harish-Chandra
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module, by Corollary

or results of
[
18
]
, the source of

is finite-dimensional. Under these assumptions, whether

is an isomorphism or not is still wide open. In §

, we will consider some cases in which

turn out to be isomorphisms for all

.

Remark 7.2.3
.

A smooth Fréchet representation
F
of
H
of moderate growth
is said to be
homologically finite
(resp.
homologically separated
) if
\operatorname{H}^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(H;F)
is finite-dimensional (resp. Hausdorff) for all
n
; homological finiteness implies homological separation. See
[
3
]
for an overview. With all the previous assumptions (algebraicity, sphericity), it is still unknown whether

is homologically finite or not, except for specific choices of

or

, or for

. If

is surjective, then

will be homologically finite. In particular, homological finiteness will hold in all the examples of §

.

Remark 7.2.4
.

In parallel with the monodromic setting considered in §

, one should also consider
\operatorname{H}^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(H;E|_{H}\otimes\chi^{-1})
when

is a smooth character of

factoring through the unipotent radical of

, and modify (

) accordingly.

7.3
Examples of comparison isomorphisms

Consider the groups in (
7.2.1
); we assume that they are all affine groups in what follows.

Proposition 7.3.1
.

Let
E
be a Casselman–Wallach representation of
G
. If
E^{K^{H}\text{-fini}}=E^{K\text{-fini}}
, then the comparison map
c_{n}(E)
is an isomorphisms for all
n
.

Proof.

Immediate from Remark
7.2.2
.
∎

Example 7.3.2
.

Suppose
E
is of the form
E=B^{\infty}
, where
B
is an irreducible unitary representation of
G
and
B^{\infty}
denotes its smooth part. We say
B
is
K^{H}
-admissible
if
B|_{K^{H}}
decomposes discretely into a Hilbert direct sum of irreducibles with finite multiplicities. By
[
22
, Proposition 1.6]
, this implies that
B^{K\text{-fini}}
is
discretely decomposable
as an
(\mathfrak{h},K^{H})
-module. The notions of admissibility and discrete decomposability under restriction have been extensively studied by T. Kobayashi and his collaborators; see
[
22
]
or
[
23
, §4.1]
for an overview.

We claim that when
B
is
K^{H}
-admissible,
c_{n}(E)
is an isomorphism for all
n
.

Indeed, by
[
22
, Proposition 1.6]
, the
K^{H}
-admissibility implies

B^{K\text{-fini}}=(B|_{H})^{\infty,K^{H}\text{-fini}};

the left-hand side is
E^{K\text{-fini}}
whilst the right-hand side contains
B^{\infty,K^{H\text{-fini}}}=E^{K^{H}\text{-fini}}
. Hence Proposition
7.3.1
can be applied.

Discrete decomposability is a rare phenomenon, so the scope of the condition above is limited. Nonetheless, it covers some interesting families: suppose
H\subset G
is a symmetric subgroup, and
H/K^{H}\to G/K
is a holomorphic embedding of Hermitian symmetric domains, then every unitary highest weight module (eg. holomorphic discrete series) is
K^{H}
-admissible, hence discretely decomposable over
(\mathfrak{h},K^{H})
. See
[
22
, Fact 5.4]
.

Example 7.3.3
.

The
K^{H}
-admissibility mentioned above always holds if
H=K
.

Before stating the next example, we record a standard reduction due to Hecht–Taylor.

Proposition 7.3.4
.

If
c_{n}(E)
is an isomorphism for all
n
and all principal series representations
E
, then the same is true for all Casselman–Wallach representations
E
.

Proof.

In view of the isomorphisms of abstract vector spaces

\operatorname{H}_{n}\left(\mathfrak{h},K^{H};E^{K^{H}\text{-fini}}\right)\simeq\operatorname{H}_{n}\left(\mathfrak{h},K^{H};E\right)\simeq\operatorname{H}^{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(H;E|_{H})

of Proposition
7.1.3
, one can reiterate the proof of
[
14
, Proposition 3]
, which proceeds by resolving the
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module
E^{K\text{-fini}}
into a complex of principal series via Casselman’s Subrepresentation Theorem, and analyzing the corresponding map between two double complexes, each computing
(\mathfrak{h},K^{H})
-homologies in the vertical direction.

The point here is that
(\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{p})\underset{K^{H}}{\otimes}(\cdot)
is an exact functor from locally
K^{H}
-finite representations to vector spaces, for every
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. Indeed, taking
K^{H}
-co-invariants is an exact functor on such representations, as follows from
[
20
, Proposition 1.18]
. In
[
14
]
one took
\otimes
instead of
\otimes_{K^{H}}
, but the exactness is all one needs to analyze the spectral sequence
E_{2}^{q,-p}
therein.
∎

Example 7.3.5
.

Take
G=\operatorname{SL}(2)
,
K=\operatorname{SO}(2)
,
H\simeq\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
being the diagonal torus, and
K^{H}=K\cap H
; note that
H
is a symmetric subgroup of
G
, and
K^{H}=\mu_{2}:=\{\pm 1\}
. We also take
U
to be the upper-triangular unipotent subgroup, and identify all these groups with their
\mathbb{R}
-points. By identifying
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with row vectors and
U\backslash G
with
\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, the principal series can be described as follows, on the level of Casselman–Wallach representations.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}
. Define

V^{\epsilon}_{\lambda}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{r|l}f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{C}&\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{\times},\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\{0\}\\
\text{smooth}&f(tx)=\mathrm{sgn}(t)^{\epsilon}|t|^{\lambda}f(x)\end{array}\right\}.

It is topologized in the standard way, and
G
acts by
(gf)(x)=f(xg)
.

In particular,
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&a^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
maps
f
to
(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto f(ax_{1},a^{-1}x_{2})
, and
-1\in K^{H}
maps
f
to
(-1)^{\epsilon}f
. We also remark that
V^{\epsilon}_{\lambda}
is the normalized parabolic induction of

\begin{pmatrix}a&*\\
&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\mathrm{sgn}(a)^{\epsilon}|a|^{-\lambda-1},\quad a\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}.

Let
\theta:=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
&-1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
, which is
K^{H}
-invariant and generates
\mathfrak{h}
. The
\theta
-invariants in
V^{\epsilon}_{\lambda}
are precisely the functions
f
which are locally constant on the real curve
x_{1}x_{2}=c
, for each
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

Clearly,
(V^{\epsilon}_{\lambda})^{K^{H}\text{-fini}}=V^{\epsilon}_{\lambda}
is strictly larger than
(V^{\epsilon}_{\lambda})^{K\text{-fini}}
.

For
L\in\{K^{H},K\}
, the complex computing
\operatorname{H}_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{h},K^{H};(V^{\epsilon}_{\lambda})^{L\text{-fini}})
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\theta\underset{K^{H}}{\otimes}(V^{\epsilon}_{\lambda})^{L\text{-fini}}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{C}\underset{K^{H}}{\otimes}(V^{\epsilon}_{\lambda})^{L\text{-fini}},\quad\deg=-1,0,

(7.3.1)

\displaystyle\theta\otimes v

\displaystyle\mapsto 1\otimes\theta v.

When
\epsilon=0
, the
\operatorname{H}_{1}
is
\{f\in(V^{0}_{\lambda})^{L\text{-fini}}:\theta f=0\}
. On each open quadrant,
f
takes the form
c|x_{1}x_{2}|^{\lambda/2}
for some
c\in\mathbb{C}
. In order that they glue to a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, the constants
c
must coincide and we must have
\lambda\in 4\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, in which case
f(x_{1}x_{2})=c(x_{1}x_{2})^{\lambda/2}
is polynomial, hence
K
-finite. Therefore

\operatorname{H}_{1}\left(\mathfrak{h},K^{H};(V^{0}_{\lambda})^{L\text{-fini}}\right)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if}\;\lambda\notin 4\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\\
\mathbb{C}(x_{1}x_{2})^{\lambda/2},&\text{if}\;\lambda\in 4\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.\end{cases}

When
\epsilon=1
, we have
\mathbb{C}\theta\underset{K^{H}}{\otimes}(V^{1}_{\lambda})^{L\text{-fini}}=\mathbb{C}\underset{K^{H}}{\otimes}(V^{1}_{\lambda})^{L\text{-fini}}=0
, thus

\operatorname{H}_{n}\left(\mathfrak{h},K^{H};(V^{1}_{\lambda})^{L\text{-fini}}\right)=0,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

These computations show that
c_{1}(V_{\lambda}^{\epsilon})
is always an isomorphism. The same also holds for
c_{0}(V_{\lambda}^{\epsilon})
, by either invoking the automatic continuity theorem for
H\subset G
, or a direct computation as before. By Proposition
7.3.4
, it follows that
c_{n}(E)
is an isomorphism for all Casselman–Wallach representations
E
and all
n
.

The example above shows that, even in the simplest case, it may happen simultaneously that

•

E^{K^{H}\text{-fini}}\supset E^{K\text{-fini}}
strictly;

•

c_{n}(E)
is an isomorphism for all
n
;

•

some higher homologies of
E|_{H}
are nonzero.

In particular, the condition in Proposition
7.3.1
is sufficient but not necessary in general.

Example 7.3.6
.

The proper reductive spherical subgroups
H
of
G=\operatorname{SL}(2)
, identified with their

\begin{pmatrix}*&\\
&*\end{pmatrix},\quad\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}*&\\

In Example
7.3.5

For the second case (the normalizer of the diagonal torus), retain the notations from Example

and consider the principal series

, which suffices. One has

whose generators act on

.

Finally, the third case

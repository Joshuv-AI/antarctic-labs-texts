Weak solvability of elliptic variational inequalities coupled with a nonlinear differential equation
Nadia Skoglund Taki

Abstract
. In this paper we establish existence, uniqueness, and boundedness results for an elliptic variational inequality coupled with a nonlinear ordinary differential equation. Under the general framework, we present a new application modelling the antiplane shear deformation of a static frictional adhesive contact problem. The adhesion process has been extensively studied, but it is usual to assume a priori that the intensity of adhesion is bounded by introducing truncation operators. The aim of this article is to remove this restriction. The proof is based on an iterative approximation scheme showing that the problem has a unique solution. A key ingredient is finding uniform a priori bounds for each iterate. These are obtained by adapting versions of the Moser iteration to our system of equations.

Introduction

Let

\displaystyle+\int_{\Gamma_{C}}[\varphi(\beta(t),u(t),v)-\varphi(\beta(t),u(t),u(t))]\>\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle+\int_{\Gamma_{C}}[\phi(\beta(t),u(t),v)-\phi(\beta(t),u(t),u(t))]\>\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\beta(t)

\displaystyle=\beta_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}H(\beta(s),u(s))\>\mathrm{d}s

1.1.
Former existence, uniqueness and boundedness results

There are former existence and uniqueness results for special cases of (

, the system is reduced to the problem studied in
[

. In comparison to these results, we also establish boundedness. A fundamental problem to prove existence and uniqueness of (

In, e.g.,

]
, they simplify the problem by using cut-off functions (see Remark

]
and
[
24
]
, but their applications are, respectively, to the usual frictional elastic and viscoelastic problems (see Remark

1.2.
Contributions and outline

The novelties of this paper are:

•

Existence and uniqueness of (
1.1
), where the proof is based on an iterative decoupling approach that directly gives rise to a numerical method.

•

Boundedness of the solution
(u,\beta)
to (
1.1
).

•

A new application of (
1.1
) for

modelling elastic antiplane shear deformation with frictional forces and adhesion.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

. Section

is devoted to the proof of the main result. The strategy consists of constructing an approximated solution to (

) and (

2.3.
Preliminary results

We include some preliminary results needed to find uniform a priori estimates for the approximated solutions, two results usually used in the Moser iteration, and lastly, a result used to prove that the approximated problem converges to (

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

be as in Definition

\displaystyle\lVert v^{n}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}X}\leq c\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{X}+L\lVert v^{n-1}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}X}

\displaystyle\lVert v^{n}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}X}\leq\tilde{c}\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{X},

where
\tilde{c}
is a positive constant independent of

Proof.

We observe that

\displaystyle\lVert v^{n}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}X}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{X}+L\lVert v^{n-1}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}X}\leq c(1+L)\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{X}+L^{2}\lVert v^{n-2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}X}.

By induction on
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lVert v^{n}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}X}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{X}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}L^{k}+L^{n}\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{X}\leq c\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{X}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}L^{k}.

Since
0<L<1
,

\displaystyle\lVert v^{n}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}X}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{1-L}\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{X}.

Denoting
\tilde{c}:=\frac{c}{1-L}
concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.8

Let

,

, and a.e.

.

Proof.

We observe by

3.1.
Strategy of the proof of Theorem

The proof of the theorem is divided into six steps. In the first step, we introduce an auxiliary problem to (

) (Problem

In the auxiliary problem, we fix two of the functions in (

Proof.

. Choosing

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mu|\nabla u(t)|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}x

Remark 4.7
.

The scheme of our proof can be adapted to (but not limited to) other coupled systems of equations.

Proof.

To simplify the notation, we denote
u=u_{\xi\beta}
. We may write
u=u_{+}-u_{-}
with
u_{+}=\max\{u,0\}
and
u_{-}=\max\{-u,0\}
. As a consequence, if we prove that
\lVert u_{+}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})}\leq c_{0}K
and
\lVert u_{-}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})}\leq c_{0}K
, then

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})}\leq\lVert u_{+}-u_{-}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})}\leq\lVert u_{+}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})}+\lVert u_{-}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})}\leq 2c_{0}K.

Case 1: Estimating
\lVert u_{+}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})}\leq c_{0}K

For convenience, we divided the proof into four main parts. First, we define

(4.2)

\displaystyle w(t):=\log\Big(\frac{M(t)+K}{M(t)+K-u_{+}(t)}\Big)=\log(M(t)+K)-\log(M(t)+K-u_{+}(t))

where
M(t):=\lVert u_{+}(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})}
for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
. We will use
w
in (
4.2
) to obtain the desired estimate. The proof will be as follows; first, we show that
\lVert w(t)\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c_{0}
. Secondly, we verify that
w(t)
is nonnegative. Thirdly, we will obtain the estimate
\lVert w(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})}\leq c_{0}
, and lastly, we conclude by using the three previous steps.

Part I:
\lVert w(t)\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c_{0}

It is important in this step that the constant is independent of
K
. It will be made clear in Part IV, but in short, we will use the exponential in (
4.2
) to obtain the desired estimate. To show the estimate
\lVert w(t)\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c_{0}
, we will use Problem
2
. First, we have to find a convenient choice of
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. That is, choosing a test function
v
such that we can rewrite Problem
2
in terms of
w
. From Proposition
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla w(t)=\frac{\nabla u_{+}(t)}{M(t)+K-u_{+}(t)}.

Therefore, a suitable choice for the test function
v
is
v=u(t)-\frac{1}{M(t)+K-u_{+}(t)}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
in Problem
2
.
Indeed, since
u_{+}(t)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
(by Proposition
2.6
) and
K\leq(M(t)-u_{+}(t))+K
(from
u_{+}(t)\leq M(t)
), we have

\displaystyle\nabla v=\nabla u(t)-\frac{\nabla u_{+}(t)}{(M(t)+K-u_{+}(t))^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M(t)+K-u_{+}(t)}\leq\frac{1}{K}.

This yields
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then choosing
v=u(t)-\frac{1}{M(t)+K-u_{+}(t)}
, Problem
2
becomes

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mu

\displaystyle\frac{\nabla u_{+}(t)\cdot\nabla u_{+}(t)}{(M(t)+K-u_{+}(t))^{2}}\>\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f_{0}(t)|}{M(t)+K-u_{+}(t)}\>\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\frac{|f_{N}(t)|}{M(t)+K-u_{+}(t)}\>\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle+\int_{\Gamma_{C}}[\varphi(\beta(t),u(t),u(t)-\frac{1}{M(t)+K-u_{+}(t)})-\varphi(\beta(t),u(t),u(t))]\>\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle+\int_{\Gamma_{C}}[\phi(\beta(t),u(t),u(t)-\frac{1}{M(t)+K-u_{+}(t)})-\phi(\beta(t),u(t),u(t))]\>\mathrm{d}\sigma

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
. From Lemma
3.2
and the lower bound of
\mu
(
3.1a
), we deduce

\displaystyle\mu_{\ast}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u_{+}(t)|^{2}}{(M(t)+K-u_{+}(t))^{2}}\>\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f_{0}(t)|}{M(t)+K-u_{+}(t)}\>\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\frac{|f_{N}(t)|}{M(t)+K-u_{+}(t)}\>\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle+\int_{\Gamma_{C}}\frac{c_{0\varphi}+c_{1\varphi}|\beta(t)|+c_{2\varphi}|\xi(t)|}{M(t)+K-u_{+}(t)}\>\mathrm{d}\sigma+\int_{\Gamma_{C}}\frac{c_{1\phi}|\beta(t)|^{2}|\xi(t)|}{M(t)+K-u_{+}(t)}\>\mathrm{d}\sigma

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
. With

(

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w(t)|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{\ast}K}\int_{\Omega}|f_{0}(t)|\>\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{\mu_{\ast}K}\int_{\Gamma_{N}}|f_{N}(t)|\>\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\mu_{\ast}K}\int_{\Gamma_{C}}(c_{0\varphi}+c_{1\varphi}|\beta(t)|+c_{2\varphi}|\xi(t)|)\>\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\mu_{\ast}K}\int_{\Gamma_{C}}c_{1\phi}|\beta(t)|^{2}|\xi(t)|\>\mathrm{d}\sigma

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
. Observe, by Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w(t)|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{0}}{\mu_{\ast}K}\Big(c_{0\varphi}+\lVert f_{0}(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\lVert f_{N}(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{N})}+c_{1\varphi}\lVert\beta(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{C})}

\displaystyle+c_{2\varphi}c_{0}\lVert\xi(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+c_{1\phi}c_{0}\lVert\beta(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{C})}^{2}\lVert\xi(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big)

In the first step, we will show the estimate

\displaystyle\leq c_{0},

and as a consequence of the first step, we obtain

\displaystyle\leq\lVert w(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{N})}+\lVert w(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{C})}\leq c_{0}

Part III.1:
\lVert w(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c_{0}

The proof of this part relies on the Moser iteration. The goal is to obtain an estimate where the constant is independent of

. To this extent, we first go back to Problem

(

in terms of

instead of

.

For the sake of simplicity, the dependence on

in
w
will be omitted for the rest of this part. We define
w_{h}=\min\{w,h\}
for any

and choose
v=u(t)-\frac{w_{h}^{2\kappa}w}{M(t)+K-u_{+}(t)}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for any

, where it follows by Proposition

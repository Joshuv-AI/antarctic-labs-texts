We will also develop the theory only for the general linear group

1.1.1
The first fundamental theorem

The group

by the dual action. By convention we will write

\langle g\varphi\mid v\rangle:=\langle\varphi\mid g^{-1}v\rangle,\ \forall g\in GL(V),\ v\in V,\ \varphi\in V^{*}.

(5)

In other words
\langle g\varphi\mid gv\rangle=\langle\varphi\mid v\rangle,\ \forall g\in GL(V)
that is the function
\langle\varphi\mid v\rangle
of

and
\varphi

states that the previous functions generate all invariants of several copies of

and

(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{h};\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{k})\in V^{\oplus h}\oplus(V^{*})^{\oplus k}.

Theorem 1.2
.

The polynomial functions on

basic functions
\langle\varphi_{i}\mid v_{j}\rangle,\ i=1,\ldots,k;

One of the remarkable features of the Theory as presented by H. Weyl is the fact that this Theorem is equivalent to a second Theorem, with

Theorem 1.3
.

The algebra of linear operators on

which commute with the diagonal action of

is generated by the elements of the symmetric group

. The two actions are

g\cdot(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{h})=gv_{1}\otimes gv_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes gv_{h},\qquad\qquad g\in GL(V),\qquad

(6)

\!\sigma\cdot(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{h})=v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(2)}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(h)};\qquad\sigma\in S_{h}.

(7)

1.3.1
The second fundamental theorem

Together with a first fundamental theorem one has a
second fundamental theorem, SFT for short
. In this theorem one describes the relations among the invariants as generated by basic ones.

For the setting of Theorem
1.2
one should think of

V^{\oplus h}=\hom(F^{h},V),\ (V^{*})^{\oplus k}=\hom(V,F^{k}).

Then the invariants

The image of this map is the variety of matrices of rank
\leq d:=\dim V

The polynomial functions on

are in the variables

which can be viewed as entries of a

Theorem 1.4
.

The ideal of functions on the space of

matrices vanishing on the matrices of rank

In this case it is generated by the determinants of all the

This is just the first of a long list of ideas and theorems of geometric nature on special singularities.

Instead for the setting of Theorem
1.3

Theorem 1.5
.

Consider the mapping

The kernel of

Here

This is just the first of a long list of ideas and theorems in representation Theory.

2.1
Algebras with trace

Let us quickly recall this formalism which will be used throughout this paper.

Definition 2.2
.

An algebra with trace is an associative algebra
A
over some ring
F
together with a linear map
tr:A\to A
satisfying the following axioms, see
[
2
]
Chapter 2.3 or
[
19
]
.

tr(a)\cdot b=b\cdot tr(a),\ tr(a\cdot b)=tr(b\cdot a),\ tr(tr(a)\cdot b)=tr(a)\cdot tr(b),\ \forall a,b\in A.

Then the image
tr(A)
of
tr
is a central subalgebra, called the
trace algebra
and
tr
is
tr(A)
linear.

Remark that in the definition, in the approach of
universal algebra
, we do not assume that trace takes values in
F
.

Algebras with trace form a category where maps are trace compatible homomorphisms.

Algebras with trace admit free algebras.

Definition 2.3
.

The free algebra with trace in some list of variables
X
is
T\langle X\rangle:=F\langle X\rangle[tr(M)]
. That is the polynomial algebra in the elements
tr(M)
over the usual free algebra
F\langle X\rangle
.

Its trace algebra is the polynomial algebra
F[tr(M)]
in the elements
tr(M)
over
F
. By
tr(M)
we denote the class of a monomial
M
up to cyclic equivalence, (cf.
[
19
]
for a detailed definition).

Definition 2.4
.

An
n
–fold tensor trace identity of
A
is an element of
T\langle X\rangle^{\otimes n}
vanishing for all evaluations in
A^{\otimes n}
.

In this case by

T\langle X\rangle^{\otimes n}:=T\langle X\rangle\otimes_{F[tr(M)]}T\langle X\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes_{F[tr(M)]}T\langle X\rangle^{\otimes n}

(15)

we mean the tensor product over the central trace subalgebra
F[tr(M)]
.

Remark 2.5
.

Therefore
T\langle X\rangle^{\otimes n}
is a free
F[tr(M)]
module with basis the
tensor monomials

M_{1}\otimes M_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes M_{n}
.

Similarly
A^{\otimes n}
is the tensor product over its trace algebra. Furthermore
A^{\otimes n}
is an algebra with trace where

tr(a_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes a_{n}):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}tr(a_{i})

and by evaluation we mean a homomorphism compatible with trace.

2.6
Tensor Polynomials

This paper can be also used as an introduction to the study of polynomial maps
f:M_{d}^{k}\to M_{d}^{\otimes n}
which are equivariant under the conjugation action of
GL(\mathbb{Q},d).
Or with intrinsic notations
f:End(V)^{k}\to End(V)^{\otimes n}=End(V^{\otimes n}).
These maps form a non commutative algebra using the algebra structure of
M_{d}^{\otimes n}.
Among these are the non commutative polynomials in the tensor variables
x_{j}^{(i)}:=1^{\otimes i-1}\otimes x_{j}\otimes 1^{\otimes n-i}
, where
x_{j}
is a matrix variable.

Definition 2.7
.

We call the
x_{j}^{(i)}

tensor variables
and the (non commutative) polynomials they generate

.

The concept of
tensor polynomial or of tensor trace identities
as far as I know was not considered by algebraists. I wish to thank Felix Huber for pointing out this notion which seems to play some role in Quantum Information Theory, see
[
12
]
and
[

]

Given a free algebra
F\langle X\rangle
and an associative algebra
A
over a field
F
, a map

induces a homomorphism
f:F\langle X\rangle\to A
and conversely a homomorphism

is determined by its values on
X
.

The polynomial identities of
A
are the elements of
F\langle X\rangle
vanishing under all evaluations.

Now though, such a map
f
defines also, for all integers

, a homomorphism of the corresponding
n
–fold tensor products:

f^{\otimes n}:F\langle X\rangle^{\otimes n}\to A^{\otimes n}.

One can thus define as
n
–fold tensor identity for
A
an element
G\in F\langle X\rangle^{\otimes n}
vanishing for all evaluations in
A
, i.e.
f^{\otimes n}(G)=0,\ \forall f:X\to A
.

A similar notion holds for
A
an algebra with trace.

4.1
The structure of
\mathcal{T}_{X}^{n}(V)

What can we say about the algebra
\mathcal{T}_{X}^{n}(V)

X
has at least 2 elements, the case of just one variable being special and left to the reader. Let us first recall the Theory for

The algebra

is called the

and it is the free algebra modulo the polynomial identities of
d\times d

\mathfrak{Z}_{d}(X)\subset T^{(0)}_{d}\langle\Xi\rangle
.

An element

. Moreover the fields of fractions of

and

,

is an Azumaya algebra of rank

over its center

Take the tensor product

Proof.

We have

4.6
The spectrum

Since the algebra
\mathcal{T}_{X}^{n}(V)\subset M_{d^{n}}[\mathbb{Q}[\xi^{(i)}_{a,b}]
, Formula (
2
), is closed under trace then it is a
d^{n}
–Cayley–Hamilton algebra, according to the Theory developed in
[
19

. Its trace algebra equals its center
T^{(0)}_{d}\langle\Xi\rangle
, the same for all
n

Therefore, when we extend the scalars from
\mathbb{Q}
to
\mathbb{C}
the variety with coordinate ring
T^{(0)}_{d}\langle\Xi\rangle\otimes\mathbb{C}
parametrizes isomorphism classes of semisimple representations of dimension
d^{n}
of the algebra
\mathcal{T}_{X}^{n}(V)\otimes\mathbb{C}
.

Now by the same reason, when
n=1
, the variety with coordinate ring
T^{(0)}_{d}\langle\Xi\rangle\otimes\mathbb{C}
parametrizes the isomorphism classes of semisimple representations of dimension
d
of the free algebra
\mathbb{C}\langle\Xi\rangle
or of the free
d
–Cayley–Hamilton algebra. That is, if
X
has
k
elements, conjugacy classes of
k
–tuples of
d\times d
matrices
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})^{k}
generating a semisimple algebra.

To this
k
–tuple is then associated a map
\mathcal{T}_{X}^{n}(V)\to M_{d}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes n}
and the subalgebra of
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})^{k}
generated by the elements
1^{\otimes i}\otimes a_{j}\otimes 1^{\otimes n-i-1}
and
S_{n}
is semisimple. This matrix algebra is the entire algebra
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes n}
if and only if the representation is irreducible which means it is a point of the spectrum of one of the Azumaya algebras
\mathbb{Q}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}][c^{-1}]
.

When the representation is semisimple and not irreducible we have a decomposition
V=\oplus_{j=1}^{a}V_{j}
into irreducibles which induces a decomposition
V^{\otimes n}=\oplus_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}V_{i_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes V_{i_{n}}
whose terms are permuted by the group
S_{n}
. In each orbit there is a term

W:=V_{1}^{\otimes h_{1}}\otimes V_{2}^{\otimes h_{2}}\otimes\ldots\otimes V_{k}^{\otimes h_{a}},\ \sum h_{i}=n
which is stabilized by a Young subgroup
H=S_{h_{1}}\times S_{h_{1}}\times\ldots\times S_{h_{a}}
giving the semisimple representation
Ind_{H}^{S_{n}}W.

The identities belonging to the first subalgebra are the

Although it is true that these can be deduced from the antisymmetrizer their structure is far from being understood.
A start in the study of tensor polynomial identities appears in the paper with F. Huber
[
13

As for tensor trace identities we know that for

they are generated by the

For higher

[

. Let us explain what happens in general.

We have a map

By simple localization arguments we then see that if

then there is an invariant
b
so that

. In fact if

is any central polynomial we have

for some

.

We can also take any nonzero discriminant

of
d^{2}
elements

over the localized trace algebra

So we may say that up to multiplication by some power of this discriminant a tensor trace identity can be deduced from the

On the other hand the situation is similar to that of functional identities, so, as in the paper

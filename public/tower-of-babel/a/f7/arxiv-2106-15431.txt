Non-degeneracy and existence of new solutions for the Schrödinger equations
Yuxia Guo, Monica Musso, Shuangjie Peng and Shusen Yan

Abstract
. We consider the following nonlinear problem − Δ u + V ( | y | ) u = u p , u > 0 in ℝ N , u ∈ H 1 ( ℝ N ) , -\Delta u+V(|y|)u=u^{p},\quad u>0\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\ \ \ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}), (0.1) where V ⁡ ( r ) V(r) is a positive function, 1 < p < N + 2 N − 2 1<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2} . We show that the multi-bump solutions constructed in [ 20 ] is non-degenerate in a suitable symmetric space. We also use this non-degenerate result to construct new solutions for ( 0.1 ).

The functional corresponding to (

It is

U

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+u=u^{p},\;\;u>0,&\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u(y)\to 0,&\text{as}\;|y|\to\infty.\end{cases}

For any integer

D_{k,R}=\bigl\{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}):\;x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\;|x_{j}|\geq R,j=1,\cdots,k,\,|x_{i}-x_{j}|\geq R,i\neq j\bigr\}.

Then for any

\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{x_{j}}

Direct computations give

I\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{x_{j}}\bigr)\approx kA+B_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bigl(V(x_{j})-1\bigr)-B_{2}\sum_{i\neq j}U(|x_{i}-x_{j}|),

To find a stable critical point for the function on the right hand side of (

that the terms in this function may be of different order, depending on the location of each
x_{j}
. To avoid this difficulty, in

[
20
]

V(y)
is radial, and the following problem is studied

-\Delta u+V(|y|)u=u^{p},u>0\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\ \ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(1.5)

We can see easily that if the function on the right hand side of (

V(y)-1
can not be

[
20

(V): There are constants
a>0
,
\alpha>1
, and
\gamma>0
,
such that

V(r)=1+\frac{a}{r^{\alpha}}+O\bigl(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha+\gamma}}\bigr),

(1.6)

as
r\to+\infty
.

Let

x_{j}=\bigl(r\cos\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k},r\sin\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k},0\bigr),\quad j=1,\cdots,k,

where
0
is the zero vector in
\mathbb{R}^{N-2}

I\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{x_{j}}\bigr)\approx kA+\frac{aB_{1}k}{r^{\alpha}}-B_{2}kU(|x_{2}-x_{1}|).

For
k>0
large, the function
\frac{aB_{1}}{r^{\alpha}}-B_{2}U(|x_{2}-x_{1}|)

bumps near infinity provided

be more precisely, we set

y=(y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime})
,
y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
y^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-2}
. Define

\begin{split}H_{s}=\bigl\{u:&u\;\text{is even in}\;y_{2},\cdots,y_{N},\\
&u(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,y^{\prime\prime})=u(r\cos(\theta+\frac{2\pi j}{k}),r\sin(\theta+\frac{2\pi j}{k}),y^{\prime\prime})\bigr\}.\end{split}

Let

W_{r}(y)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{x_{j}}(y).

The following result is proved in

Theorem A.

Suppose that

satisfies (
1.6

integer

, (
1.5
)

If
N\geq 4
, for any large integer

1.5
)
whose shape is, at main order,

We calculate

\begin{split}&I\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{x_{j}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j}}\Bigr)\\
\approx&kA+\frac{aB_{1}k}{r^{\alpha}}-B_{2}kU(|x_{2}-x_{1}|)\\

Following the idea in

(1.11)

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that

.

Similar problem is studied for the following prescribed scalar curvature equation

-\Delta u=K(y)u^{2^{*}-1},\quad u>0\ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\ \ u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

For the nonlinear Schrödinger equations, we can regard condition (

2.
revisit the existence problem

In this section, we will briefly revisit the existence problem for (
1.5
) and give a different proof of Theorem A, so that
we can obtained extra estimates which are needed in the proof of the non-degeneracy result.

We denote

Z_{j}=\frac{\partial U_{x_{j}}}{\partial r},\quad j=1,\cdots,k,

where
x_{j}=\bigl(r\cos\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k},r\sin\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k},0\bigr)
.

Let

\begin{split}\tilde{H}_{s}=\bigl\{u:&u\;\text{is even in}\;y_{2};\;u(y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime})=u(y^{\prime},|y^{\prime\prime}|)\\
&u(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,y^{\prime\prime})=u(r\cos(\theta+\frac{2\pi j}{k}),r\sin(\theta+\frac{2\pi j}{k}),y^{\prime\prime})\bigr\},\end{split}

\tilde{E}_{k}=\bigl\{v:v\in\tilde{H}_{s}\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\;\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U_{x_{j}}^{p-1}Z_{j}v=0,\;j=1,\cdots,k\bigr\},

and

S_{k}=\bigl[(\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}-\beta)k\ln k,(\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}+\beta)k\ln k\bigr],

(2.1)

where
\alpha
is the constant in the expansion for
V
, and
\beta>0

is a small constant.

In this paper, we always assume that
r\in S_{k}
.

Take a small constant
\tau>0
. Define the norm

\|u\|_{*}=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(y)|}{\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-\tau|y-x_{j}|}}.

Let

Lv=-\Delta v+V(|y|)v-pW_{r}^{p-1}v.

For
f\in\tilde{H}_{s}\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we consider the following problem

Lv=f+b_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{x_{j}}^{p-1}Z_{j}

(2.2)

for some
v\in\tilde{E}_{k}
and
a_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
.
We have

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
(a_{k},v_{k})
solves (
2.2
) for
f=f_{k}
. If
\|f_{k}\|_{*}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
,
then
\|v_{k}\|_{*}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that there are
k_{m}\to+\infty
,

r_{k_{m}}\in S_{k_{m}}
, and
(b_{k_{m}},v_{k_{m}})
solving (
2.2
), such that

\|f_{k_{m}}\|_{*}\to 0
,
\|v_{k_{m}}\|_{*}=1
. For simplicity of the notation, we drop the subscript
k_{m}
.

We have

v(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(y,x)\bigl(pW_{r}^{p-1}v+f+b\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{x_{j}}^{p-1}Z_{j}\bigr),

(2.3)

where
G(y,x)
is the Green’s function of
-\Delta+V(|y|)
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

It is easy to prove

\bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(y,x)f\bigr|\leq C\|f\|_{*}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(y,x)e^{-\tau|y-x_{j}|}\,dy\leq C\|f\|_{*}\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-\tau|x-x_{j}|},

and for
\tau>0
small,

\begin{split}\bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(y,x)W_{r}^{p-1}v\bigr|\leq&C\|v\|_{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(y,x)\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-|y-x_{j}|}\bigr)^{p-1}\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-\tau|y-x_{j}|}\bigr)\,dy\\
\leq&C\|v\|_{*}\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-2\tau|x-x_{j}|}.\end{split}

Moreover,

\bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(y,x)\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{x_{j}}^{p-1}Z_{j}\bigr|\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-|x-x_{j}|}.

On the other hand, from

b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{x_{j}}^{p-1}Z_{j}Z_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigl(Lv-f\bigr)Z_{1},

we can prove that
b\to 0
.
So, it holds

|v(x)|\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-\tau|y-x_{j}|}\bigr)^{-1}\leq o(1)+C\|v\|_{*}\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-\tau|y-x_{j}|}\bigr)^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-2\tau|x-x_{j}|}.

(2.4)

Since
v\in\tilde{E}
, we can prove
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x_{j}))}\to 0
for any
R>0
. So we find from (
2.4
) that
\|v\|_{*}\to 0
. This is a contradiction.

∎

Now we want to solve the following problem

L\omega=l_{k}+R(\omega)+b_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{x_{j}}^{p-1}Z_{j},

(2.5)

for
\omega\in E_{k}
and
b_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
, where

l_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(V(|y|)-1)U_{x_{i}}+\bigl(W_{r}^{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}U_{x_{i}}^{p}\bigr),

and

R(\omega)=(W_{r}+\omega)^{p}-W_{r}^{p}-pW_{p}^{p-1}\omega.

We have

Lemma 2.2
.

There is a small
\sigma>0
, such that

\|l_{k}\|_{*}\leq\frac{C}{r^{\alpha}}+Ce^{-\min[\frac{p}{2}-\tau,1]|x_{2}-x_{1}|},

where
r=|x_{i}|
.

Proof.

First, it holds

\bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{k}(V(|y|)-1)U_{x_{i}}\bigr|\leq\frac{C}{1+|y|^{\alpha}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}e^{-|y-x_{i}|}\leq\frac{C}{r^{\alpha}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}e^{-\tau|y-x_{i}|}.

We define

\Omega_{j}=\Bigl\{y=(y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2}:\Bigl\langle\frac{(y^{\prime},0)}{|y^{\prime}|},\frac{x_{k,j}}{|x_{k,j}|}\Bigr\rangle\geq\cos\frac{\pi}{k}\Bigr\}.

For any
y\in\Omega_{1}
, we have
|y-x_{j}|\geq|y-x_{1}|
,
j=2,\cdots,k
.
Thus, for any
y\in\Omega_{1}
, if
p>2
, it holds

\begin{split}\bigl|W^{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}U_{x_{i}}^{p}\bigr|\leq&C\sum_{i=2}^{k}U_{x_{1}}^{p-1}U_{x_{i}}\leq Ce^{-(p-2)|y-x_{1}|}\sum_{i=2}^{k}e^{-|x_{j}-x_{1}|}\\
\leq&Ce^{-\tau|y-x_{1}|}\sum_{i=2}^{k}e^{-|x_{j}-x_{1}|},\end{split}

while if
p\in(1,2]
,

\begin{split}&\bigl|W^{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}U_{x_{i}}^{p}\bigr|\leq C\sum_{i=2}^{k}U_{x_{1}}^{\frac{p}{2}}U_{x_{i}}^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
\leq&Ce^{-\tau|y-x_{1}|}\sum_{i=2}^{k}e^{-(\frac{p}{2}-\tau)|x_{j}-x_{1}|}.\end{split}

∎

Using Lemmas

and the contraction mapping theorem, we can prove that

, such that for any integer

\|\omega_{k}\|_{*}\leq C\|l_{k}\|_{*}\leq\frac{C}{r^{\alpha}}+Ce^{-\min[\frac{p}{2}-\tau,1]|x_{2}-x_{1}|}.

(2.6)

Now we can calculate

I\bigl(W_{r}+\omega_{k}\bigr)=kA+\frac{aB_{1}k}{r^{\alpha}}-k\bigl(B_{2}+o(1)\bigr)U(|x_{2}-x_{1}|)+kO\bigl(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha+\gamma}}+U^{1+\sigma}(|x_{2}-x_{1}|)\bigr),

(2.7)

where

is a small constant. Noting that the function

has a maximum point in

. Therefore,
we can prove that (

) has a solution

of the form

where

,
r_{k}\in S_{k}
and as

,

\|\omega_{k}\|_{*}\leq\frac{C}{r_{k}^{\alpha}}+Ce^{-\min[\frac{p}{2}-\tau,1]|x_{2}-x_{1}|}.

(2.8)

Remark 2.3
.

In
[
20
]
, the reduction argument is carried out in

. Thus, we only obtain the estimate for
the error term
\omega_{k}
in the norm of

Next, assuming that
V
satisfies (
1.12
), we derive a relation for
r_{k}
, which is needed in the proof of the non-degeneracy result.
From

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigl(\nabla(W_{r_{k}}+\omega_{k})\nabla\frac{\partial U_{x_{1}}}{\partial y_{1}}+V(y)(W_{r_{k}}+\omega_{k})\frac{\partial U_{x_{1}}}{\partial y_{1}}-(W_{r_{k}}+\omega_{k})^{p}\frac{\partial U_{x_{1}}}{\partial y_{1}}\bigr)=0,

we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigl((V(y)-1)(W_{r_{k}}+\omega_{k})\frac{\partial U_{x_{1}}}{\partial y_{1}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Bigl((W_{r_{k}}+\omega_{k})^{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}U^{p}_{x_{j}}\Bigr)\frac{\partial U_{x_{1}}}{\partial y_{1}}\bigr)=0.

It is easy to check that

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigl((V(y)-1)(W_{r_{k}}+\omega_{k})\frac{\partial U_{x_{1}}}{\partial y_{1}}\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V(y)-1)U_{x_{1}}\frac{\partial U_{x_{1}}}{\partial y_{1}}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{r_{k}^{2\alpha}}+e^{-\min[p-2\tau,2]|x_{2}-x_{1}|}\Bigr)\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\partial V(y)}{\partial y_{1}}U_{x_{1}}^{2}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{r_{k}^{2\alpha}}+e^{-\min[p-2\tau,2]|x_{2}-x_{1}|}\Bigr)\\
=&\frac{a(\alpha+1)x_{1}}{2r_{k}^{\alpha+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{2}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha+2}}+\frac{1}{r_{k}^{2\alpha}}+e^{-\min[p-2\tau,2]|x_{2}-x_{1}|}\Bigr).\end{split}

Moreover

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Bigl((W_{r_{k}}+\omega_{k})^{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}U^{p}_{x_{j}}\Bigr)\frac{\partial U_{x_{1}}}{\partial y_{1}}\bigr)\\
=&-p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U_{x_{1}}^{p-1}\sum_{j=2}^{k}U_{x_{j}}\frac{\partial U_{x_{1}}}{\partial y_{1}}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{r_{k}^{2\alpha}}+e^{-\min(p-\tau,2)|x_{2}-x_{1}|}\Bigr)\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U_{x_{1}}^{p}\sum_{j=2}^{k}\frac{\partial U_{x_{j}}}{\partial y_{1}}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{r_{k}^{2\alpha}}+e^{-\min(p-\tau,2)|x_{2}-x_{1}|}\Bigr)\\
=&\bigl(B+o(1)\bigr)U(|x_{2}-x_{1}|)\Bigl(\frac{(x_{1}-x_{2})_{1}}{|x_{1}-x_{2}|}+\frac{(x_{1}-x_{k})_{1}}{|x_{1}-x_{k}|}\Bigr)+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{r_{k}^{2\alpha}}+e^{-\min(p-\tau,2)|x_{2}-x_{1}|}\Bigr).\end{split}

Therefore, we have

\begin{split}&\frac{a(\alpha+1)x_{1}}{2r_{k}^{\alpha+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{2}\\
=&\bigl(B+o(1)\bigr)U(|x_{2}-x_{1}|)\Bigl(\frac{(x_{1}-x_{2})_{1}}{|x_{1}-x_{2}|}+\frac{(x_{1}-x_{k})_{1}}{|x_{1}-x_{k}|}\Bigr)\\
&+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{r_{k}^{2\alpha}}+\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha+2}}+e^{-\min(p-2\tau,2)|x_{2}-x_{1}|}\Bigr).\end{split}

(2.9)

Note that

(x_{1}-x_{2})_{1}=(x_{1}-x_{k})_{1}=r(1-\cos\frac{2\pi}{k})=r\bigl(\frac{2\pi^{2}}{k^{2}}+O\bigl(\frac{1}{k^{4}}\bigr)\bigr).

Thus, (
2.9
) can be written as

\begin{split}&\frac{a(\alpha+1)}{2r_{k}^{\alpha+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{2}\\
=&\bigl(B+o(1)\bigr)U(|x_{2}-x_{1}|)\frac{1}{k}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{r_{k}^{2\alpha}}+\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha+2}}+e^{-\min(p-2\tau,2)|x_{2}-x_{1}|}\Bigr).\end{split}

(2.10)

Noting that
e^{-|x_{1}-x_{2}|}\sim\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}}
and

as
k\to+\infty
. This is a contradiction to
\|\xi_{k}\|_{*}=1
.

∎

4.
Existence of new solutions

Let
u_{k}
be the solutions constructed in section 2, where
k>0
is a large even integer. Since
k
is even,
u_{k}
is even in each

y_{j}
,
j=1,\cdots,N
. Moreover,
u_{k}
is radial in
y^{\prime\prime}=(y_{3},\cdots,y_{N})
.

Let
n\geq k
be a large even integer.
Set

p_{j}=\Bigl(0,0,t\cos\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{n},t\sin\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{n},0\Bigr),\quad j=1,\cdots,n,

where
t\in\bigl[t_{0}n\ln n,\;t_{1}n\ln n\bigr]
.

Define

\begin{split}X_{s}=\Bigl\{u:&u\in H_{s},u\;\text{is even in}\;y_{h},h=1,\cdots,N,\\
&u(y_{1},y_{2},t\cos\theta,t\sin\theta,y^{*})=u(y_{1},y_{2},t\cos(\theta+\frac{2\pi j}{n}),t\sin(\theta+\frac{2\pi j}{n}),y^{*})\Bigr\}.\end{split}

Here
y^{*}=(y_{5},\cdots,y_{N})
.

Note that both
u_{k}
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j}}
belong to
X_{s}
, while
u_{k}
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j},\lambda}
are separated from each other. We aim to construct a solution for (
1.5
) of the form

u=u_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j},\lambda}+\xi,

where
\xi\in X_{s}
is a small perturbed term. Since the procedure to construct such solutions are similar to that in
[
20
]
,
we just sketch it.

We define the linear operator

Q_{n}\xi=-\Delta\xi+V(|y|)\xi-p\Bigl(u_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j}}\Bigr)^{p-1}\xi,\quad\xi\in X_{s}.

(4.1)

We can regard
Q_{n}\xi
as a function in
X_{s}
, such that

\bigl\langle Q_{n}\xi,\phi\bigr\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Bigl(\nabla\xi\nabla\phi+V(|y|)\xi\phi-p\bigl(u_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j}}\bigr)^{p-1}\xi\phi\Bigr),\quad\xi,\,\phi\in X_{s}.

(4.2)

Let

\tilde{Z}_{j}=\frac{\partial U_{p_{j}}}{\partial t},\;\;j=1,\cdots,k.

Let
h_{n}\in X_{s}
. Consider

\begin{cases}Q_{n}\xi_{n}=h_{n}+c_{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{Z}_{j},\\
\xi_{n}\in X_{s},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U_{p_{j}}^{p-1}\tilde{Z}_{j}\xi_{n}=0,\end{cases}

(4.3)

for some constants
c_{n}
, depending on
\xi_{n}
.

Let

D_{j}=\Bigl\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{3},y_{4},y^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-4}:\Bigl\langle\frac{(0,0,y_{3},y_{4},0,\cdots,0)}{|(y_{3},y_{4})|},\frac{p_{j}}{|p_{j}|}\Bigr\rangle\geq\cos\frac{\pi}{n}\Bigr\}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
\xi_{n}
solve (
4.3
). If
\|h_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0
, then
\|\xi_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we will use
\|u\|
to denote
\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that there are
p_{n,j}
,
h_{n}
and
\xi_{n}
, satisfying (
4.3
),
\|h_{n}\|\to 0
and
\|\xi_{n}\|\geq c>0
.
We may assume
\|\xi_{n}\|^{2}=n
. Then
\|h_{n}\|^{2}=o(n)
.

First, we estimate
c_{n}
. We have

c_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U_{p_{n,j}}^{p-1}\tilde{Z}_{j}\tilde{Z}_{1}=\bigl\langle Q_{n}\xi_{n}-h_{n},\tilde{Z}_{1}\bigr\rangle,

from which we can proved that
c_{n}=o(1)
.

On the other hand, from
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U_{p_{n,j},\lambda_{n}}^{2^{*}-2}Z_{j}\xi_{n}=0
, it is standard to prove that

\xi_{n}(y+p_{n,j})\to 0,\quad\text{in}\;H^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Moreover, since
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\xi_{n}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we can assume that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\xi_{n}\rightharpoonup\xi,\quad\text{weakly in }\;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

and

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\xi_{n}\to\xi,\quad\text{strongly in }\;L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Thus,
\xi
satisfies

-\Delta\xi+V(|y|)\xi-pu_{k}^{p-1}\xi=0,\quad\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{N}.

By Theorem
1.1
,
\xi=0
. Therefore,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigl(u_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{n,j},\lambda_{n}}\bigr)^{p-1}\xi_{n}^{2}=o(n).

We also have

\langle h_{n},\xi_{n}\bigr\rangle=o(n),

and

\Bigl\langle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{Z}_{j},\xi_{n}\Bigr\rangle=O(n).

So we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi_{n}|^{2}\\
=&O\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Bigl(u_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{n,j},\lambda_{n}}\Bigr)^{p-1}\xi_{n}^{2}\Big)+\bigl\langle h_{n},\xi_{n}\bigr\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}c_{n}\Bigl\langle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{Z}_{j},\xi_{n}\Bigr\rangle\\
=&o(n).\end{split}

This is a contradiction.

∎

From now on, we assume that
N\geq 4
.
We want to construct a solution
u
for (
1.5
) with

u=u_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j},\lambda}+\xi,

where
\xi\in X_{s}
is a small perturbed term, satisfying

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U_{p_{j}}^{p-1}\tilde{Z}_{j}\xi=0.

Then
\xi
satisfies

Q_{n}\xi=l_{n}+R(\xi),

(4.4)

where

l_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigl(V(|y|)-1\bigr)U_{p_{j}}+\Bigl(u_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j}}\Bigr)^{p}-u_{k}^{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j}}^{p},

(4.5)

and

\begin{split}R_{n}(\xi)=&\Bigl(u_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j}}+\xi\Bigr)^{p}-\Bigl(u_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j}}\Bigr)^{p}\\
&-p\Bigl(u_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j}}\Bigr)^{p-1}\xi.\end{split}

(4.6)

We have the following estimate for
\|l_{n}\|
.

Lemma 4.2
.

There is a small
\sigma>0
, such that

\|l_{n}\|\leq\frac{C}{|p_{1}|^{\alpha}}+Ce^{-\min[\frac{p}{2}-\sigma,1]|p_{2}-p_{1}|},

where
\sigma>0
is any fixed small constant.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is quite standard. We thus omit it.

∎

It is also standard to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

\|R_{n}(\xi)\|\leq C\|\xi\|^{\min(p,2)}.

Moreover,

\|R_{n}(\xi_{1})-R_{n}(\xi_{2})\|\leq C\Bigl(\|\xi_{1}\|^{\min(p-1,1)}+\|\xi_{2}\|^{\min(p-1,1)}\Bigr)\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|.

We consider the following problem:

\begin{cases}Q_{n}\xi_{n}=l_{n}+R_{n}(\xi_{n})+C_{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{Z}_{j},\\
\xi_{n}\in X_{s},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U_{p_{j}}^{p-1}\tilde{Z}_{j}\xi_{n}=0.\end{cases}

(4.7)

Using Lemmas
4.1
,
4.2
and
4.3
, we can prove the following proposition in a standard way.

Proposition 4.4
.

There is an integer
n_{0}>0
, such that for each
n\geq n_{0}
and
t\in[t_{0}n\ln n,t_{1}n\ln n]
, (
4.7
) has a solution
\xi_{n}
for some constants

c_{n}
. Moreover,
\xi_{n}

is a
C^{1}
map from
[t_{0}n\ln n,t_{1}n\ln n]
to
X_{s}
,
and

\|\xi_{n}\|\leq\frac{C}{|p_{1}|^{\alpha}}+Ce^{-\min[\frac{p}{2}-\sigma,1]|p_{2}-p_{1}|}

for any fixed small constant
\sigma>0
.

Let

F(t)=I\Bigl(u_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j}}+\xi_{n}\Bigr).

To obtain a solution of the form
u_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j},\lambda}+\xi_{n}
, we just need to find a critical point for
F(t)
in
[t_{0}n\ln n,t_{1}n\ln n]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We have

\begin{split}F(t,\lambda)=&I\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{p_{j},\lambda}\Bigr)+I(u_{k})+nO\bigl(\frac{1}{t^{2\alpha}}+e^{-\min[p-2\sigma,2]|p_{2}-p_{1}|}\bigr)\\
=&I(u_{k})+nA+n\Bigl(\frac{B_{1}}{t^{\alpha}}-(B_{2}+o(1))U(|p_{2}-p_{1}|)\Bigr)\\
&+nO\bigl(\frac{1}{t^{\alpha+1}}+e^{-\min[p-2\sigma,2]|p_{2}-p_{1}|}\bigr),\end{split}

(4.8)

where
A=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigl(|\nabla U|^{2}+U^{2}\bigr)-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{p+1}
,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are some positive constants,
and
\sigma>0
is any fixed small constant.

Now to find a critical point for
F(t)
, we just need to proceed exactly as in
[
20
]
.
∎

Hadronic decay of exotic mesons consisting of four charm quarks
Hong-Fei Zhang, Xue-Mei Mo, Yu-Peng Yan

Abstract
In 2020, a di- J / ψ J/\psi resonance centered at around 6.9 GeV \mathrm{GeV} was discovered by the LHCb Collaboration, and was later confirmed by the ATLAS and CMS Collaborations. In addition to the 6.9- GeV \mathrm{GeV} resonance, its low energy counterpart centered at around 6552 MeV \mathrm{MeV} was also observed by the CMS Collaboration. In this paper, we calculate the inclusive decay width of such exotic mesons into light hadrons and into open charms, respectively, assuming them as either genuine tetraquarks or molecules composed of two J / ψ J/\psi ’s. Since the spins of these resonances have not yet been experimentally measured, regarding them as S-wave bound states, we consider all possible spin configurations, namely spin-0 and spin-2, that decay exclusively into double J / ψ J/\psi , and find that the ratio ( ℛ \mathcal{R} ) of the decay width for the light-hadron final states to that for the charmed final states differs significantly among different spin configurations, which can help to explore the nature of the observed di- J / ψ J/\psi resonances and fix the mixing angle for the J P ​ C = 0 + ⁣ + J^{PC}=0^{++} tetraquark.

Theoretical Framework

2.1
Nonrelativistic QCD factorization for fully-charm tetraquark decay

Within the nonrelativistic QCD framework
Bodwin:1994jh
,
the decay width of a fully-charm multiquark state in principle can be factorized as

\displaystyle\Gamma(H\rightarrow X)=\sum_{n}\langle\mathcal{O}^{H}(n)\rangle f(n\rightarrow X),

(1)

where
X
denotes the final states,

n
stands for the intermediate state of the constituent charm quarks with specific quantum numbers,

\langle\mathcal{O}^{H}(n)\rangle
is the long-distance matrix element describing the possibility of finding the intermediate state
n
inside the hadron
H
,
and
f(n\rightarrow X)
is the short-distance coefficient, a quantity that can be evaluated perturbatively,
describing the decay of the intermediate state
n
into the final states
X
.

n
runs over all possible quantum numbers which contribute to the decay width,
given the restrictions required by the conservation rules.
Each long-distance matrix element is assigned an order in
v
,
the typical relative velocity of the constituent quarks inside the hadron
H
.
For charmonia,
v^{2}\approx 0.3
, while for bottomonia,
v^{2}\approx 0.1
.
However, for a multiquark state consisting of more than two constituent quarks,
more than one velocity scales might arise, which adds to the complexity of the long-distance matrix elements.

In this paper, we are concerned only with colour-singlet contributions,
i.e. when the quantum numbers of
n
and
H
coincide, for the following reasons.
First of all, the contributions from colour-multiplet states are suppressed, especially for decay processes.
Moreover, the long-distance matrix elements for colour-multiplet states have not yet been determined due to lack of data.

Within the colour-singlet model, the decay width of an S-wave fully-charm tetraquark (
T
) can be written as
Zhang:2020hoh

\displaystyle\Gamma(T\rightarrow X)=|\Psi(0)|^{2}\hat{\Gamma}(T\rightarrow X),

(2)

where
\Psi
is the spacial wave function of
T
,
and
\hat{\Gamma}
is the appropriately normalized short-distance coefficient.
Note that in the short-distance coefficient, we denote the
cc\bar{c}\bar{c}
intermediate state by the same letter (
T
) as that to denote the tetraquark.

|\Psi(0)|^{2}
stands for the probability density of finding all the four constituent quarks at the same position.

Although nonrelativistic QCD provides complete framework to separate the long-distance and short-distance effects,
it does not tell how the colour-singlet matrix elements are related to the corresponding wave functions,
which in any case depends on the simplified model of how the tetraquark state is built in terms of those of its constituents.

In the rest of this section, we present the formalism of calculating the decay widths of molecule-like states and compact tetraquarks in terms of tetraquark wavefunctions,
which are constructed conventionally as superpositions of products of the constituent quarks.

2.1.2
Compact tetraquarks

The primary difference between a compact tetraquark and a molecule-like tetraquark in nonrelativistic QCD framework
lies in the fact that a compact tetraquark is composed of a pair of identical quarks and a pair of identical antiquarks,
while a molecule-like tetraquark is composed of two quarkonia.
Under this hypothesis, the wave function of a compact tetraquark should be asymmetric while exchanging the two constituent quarks (antiquarks).
Another difference between the two sorts of tetraquarks is that the wave function of a compact tetraquark has colour degree of freedom
while it is not the case for a molecule-like state.

J/\psi

Now the decay width of tetraquarks can be obtained.
The decay width of a molecule-like tetraquark to light hadrons can be written as

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{M}(J)\rightarrow\mathrm{L.H.})=\Gamma(T_{M}(J)\rightarrow gg)+\Gamma(T_{M}(J)\rightarrow q\bar{q})

\displaystyle~~~~=\frac{1}{2M}\frac{1}{8\pi}\frac{1}{2J+1}\frac{1}{M}\left|\psi_{M}(\bm{0})\right|^{2}\sum_{J_{z}}\epsilon_{JJ_{z}}^{\mu\nu}(\epsilon_{JJ_{z}}^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}})^{*}

\displaystyle~~~~\times\left(\langle gg|J/\psi J/\psi\rangle_{\mu\nu}\langle J\psi J/\psi|gg\rangle_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}+\sum_{q}\langle q\bar{q}|J/\psi J/\psi\rangle_{\mu\nu}\langle J\psi J/\psi|q\bar{q}\rangle_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}\right),

(32)

where the factor
1/(8\pi)
is the phase space.
Similarly, its charmed decay width reads

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{M}(J)\rightarrow\mathrm{C.H.})=\Gamma(T_{M}(J)\rightarrow c\bar{c})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2M}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{16\pi}\frac{1}{2J+1}\frac{1}{M}\left|\psi_{M}(\bm{0})\right|^{2}\sum_{J_{z}}\epsilon_{JJ_{z}}^{\mu\nu}(\epsilon_{JJ_{z}}^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}})^{*}\langle c\bar{c}|J/\psi J/\psi\rangle_{\mu\nu}\langle J\psi J/\psi|c\bar{c}\rangle_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}.

(33)

The decay widths of compact tetraquarks are presented as follows,

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{C}^{+}(0)\rightarrow\mathrm{L.H.})=\Gamma(T_{C}^{+}(0)\rightarrow gg)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~=\frac{1}{2M}\frac{1}{8\pi}(\frac{2}{M})^{3}\left|\Psi_{C}^{+}(\bm{0})\right|^{2}\langle gg|(cc)_{\lambda=0}(\bar{c}\bar{c})_{\lambda=0}\rangle^{+}\langle(cc)_{\lambda=0}(\bar{c}\bar{c})_{\lambda=0}|gg\rangle^{+},

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{C}^{+}(0)\rightarrow\mathrm{C.H.})=\Gamma(T_{C}^{+}(0)\rightarrow c\bar{c})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~=\frac{1}{2M}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{16\pi}(\frac{2}{M})^{3}\left|\Psi_{C}^{+}(\bm{0})\right|^{2}\langle c\bar{c}|(cc)_{\lambda=0}(\bar{c}\bar{c})_{\lambda=0}\rangle^{+}\langle(cc)_{\lambda=0}(\bar{c}\bar{c})_{\lambda=0}|c\bar{c}\rangle^{+},

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{C}^{-}(J)\rightarrow\mathrm{L.H.})=\Gamma(T_{C}^{-}(J)\rightarrow gg)+\Gamma(T_{C}^{-}(J)\rightarrow q\bar{q})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~=\frac{1}{2M}\frac{1}{8\pi}\frac{1}{2J+1}(\frac{2}{M})^{3}\left|\Psi_{C}^{-}(\bm{0})\right|^{2}\sum_{J_{z}}\epsilon_{JJ_{z}}^{\mu\nu}(\epsilon_{JJ_{z}}^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}})^{*}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~\times\Big(\langle gg|(cc)_{\lambda=1}(\bar{c}\bar{c})_{\lambda=1}\rangle^{+}_{\mu\nu}\langle(cc)_{\lambda=1}(\bar{c}\bar{c})_{\lambda=1}|gg\rangle^{+}_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+\sum_{q}\langle q\bar{q}|(cc)_{\lambda=1}(\bar{c}\bar{c})_{\lambda=1}\rangle^{+}_{\mu\nu}\langle(cc)_{\lambda=1}(\bar{c}\bar{c})_{\lambda=1}|q\bar{q}\rangle^{+}_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}\Big),

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{C}^{-}(J)\rightarrow\mathrm{C.H.})=\Gamma(T_{C}^{-}(J)\rightarrow c\bar{c})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~=\frac{1}{2M}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{16\pi}\frac{1}{2J+1}(\frac{2}{M})^{3}\left|\Psi_{C}^{-}(\bm{0})\right|^{2}\sum_{J_{z}}\epsilon_{JJ_{z}}^{\mu\nu}(\epsilon_{JJ_{z}}^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}})^{*}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~\times\langle c\bar{c}|(cc)_{\lambda=1}(\bar{c}\bar{c})_{\lambda=1}\rangle^{+}_{\mu\nu}\langle(cc)_{\lambda=1}(\bar{c}\bar{c})_{\lambda=1}|c\bar{c}\rangle^{+}_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}},

(34)

where the superscripts,
+
and
-
, are to indicate the associated colour functions.
Here we do not distinguish wave functions for different total angular momentum in the spirit of the heavy quark spin symmetry.

3
Phenomenological Results

The analytical results for the decay widths are presented below.

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{M}(0)\rightarrow gg)=\frac{32,768\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{27M^{8}}\left|\psi_{M}(\bm{0})\right|^{2}\left|\psi_{J/\psi}(\bm{0})\right|^{4},

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{M}(0)\rightarrow c\bar{c})=\frac{11,075,584\sqrt{3}\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{243M^{8}}\left|\psi_{M}(\bm{0})\right|^{2}\left|\psi_{J/\psi}(\bm{0})\right|^{4},

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{M}(2)\rightarrow gg)=\frac{25,690,112\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{405M^{8}}\left|\psi_{M}(\bm{0})\right|^{2}\left|\psi_{J/\psi}(\bm{0})\right|^{4},

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{M}(2)\rightarrow q\bar{q})=\frac{8,388,608\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{135M^{8}}n_{l}\left|\psi_{M}(\bm{0})\right|^{2}\left|\psi_{J/\psi}(\bm{0})\right|^{4},

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{M}(2)\rightarrow c\bar{c})=\frac{719,323,136\sqrt{3}\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{1,215M^{8}}\left|\psi_{M}(\bm{0})\right|^{2}\left|\psi_{J/\psi}(\bm{0})\right|^{4},

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{C}^{+}(0)\rightarrow gg)=\frac{991,232\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{27M^{8}}\left|\Psi_{C}^{+}(\bm{0})\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{C}^{+}(0)\rightarrow c\bar{c})=\frac{16,384\sqrt{3}\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{27M^{8}}\left|\Psi_{C}^{+}(\bm{0})\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{C}^{-}(0)\rightarrow gg)=\frac{147,456\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{M^{8}}\left|\Psi_{C}^{-}(\bm{0})\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{C}^{-}(0)\rightarrow c\bar{c})=\frac{3,964,928\sqrt{3}\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{81M^{8}}\left|\Psi_{C}^{-}(\bm{0})\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{C}^{-}(2)\rightarrow gg)=\frac{12,845,056\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{135M^{8}}\left|\Psi_{C}^{-}(\bm{0})\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{C}^{-}(2)\rightarrow q\bar{q})=\frac{4,194,304\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{45M^{8}}n_{l}\left|\Psi_{C}^{-}(\bm{0})\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{C}^{-}(2)\rightarrow c\bar{c})=\frac{359,661,568\sqrt{3}\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{405M^{8}}\left|\Psi_{C}^{-}(\bm{0})\right|^{2},

(35)

where
n_{l}
is the number of light flavours.
For fully-charm tetraquark decay,
n_{l}=3
,
while for fully-bottom tetraquark decay,
n_{l}=4
.
Note that our results for
T_{C}\rightarrow\mathrm{L.H.}
agree with those in Reference
Sang:2023ncm
.

Although there have been numerous works solving the tetraquark systems,
their results on the values of the wave functions at the origin diverge,
which is due to lack of data.
To this end, we are only concerned with the normalized short-distance coefficients (
\hat{\Gamma}
) in this paper.

Before we present further results, it is important to note that
T_{C}^{+}(0)
and
T_{C}^{-}(0)
have completely identical quantum numbers,
thus, they are physically indistinguishable.
The physical states should be two orthogonal mixtures of them.
Assuming the mixing angle for one of the physical states is
\vartheta
,
the Feynman amplitude for its decay is

\displaystyle\langle X|T_{C}(0)\rangle=\langle X|T_{C}^{+}(0)\rangle\mathrm{cos}\vartheta+\langle X|T_{C}^{-}(0)\rangle\mathrm{sin}\vartheta.

(36)

Up to QCD leading order, the short-distance part for either of the amplitudes on the right-hand side of the above equation is real,
therefore the interference contribution to the decay width is twice of the geometric mean of the decay widths for colour symmetric and asymmetric states, namely

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{C}^{inter}(0)\rightarrow X)=2\sqrt{\Gamma(T_{C}^{+}(0)\rightarrow X)\Gamma(T_{C}^{-}(0)\rightarrow X)},

(37)

and the decay widths of the spin-0 state with mixing angle
\vartheta
is

\displaystyle\Gamma(T_{C}(0)\rightarrow X)

\displaystyle~~=\Gamma(T_{C}^{+}(0)\rightarrow X)\mathrm{cos}^{2}\vartheta+\Gamma(T_{C}^{inter}(0)\rightarrow X)\mathrm{cos}\vartheta\mathrm{sin}\vartheta+\Gamma(T_{C}^{-}(0)\rightarrow X)\mathrm{sin}^{2}\vartheta.

(38)

However, the two decay widths on the right-hand side of Equation (
37
) involve different wave functions, of which the values are still unknown,
we do not concern ourselves with the interference contribution and present results only for the unphysical states with specific colour configurations.
Once the wave functions are solved, one can employ Equation (
37
) and Equation (
38
) to obtain the interference contributions.

With the results given above, it is easy to obtain the following analytical results for the decay widths,
which is presented in the form of normalized short-distance coefficient.
One can obtain the decay width by multiplying the corresponding squared wave function at the origin.

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}(T_{M}(0)\rightarrow\mathrm{L.H.})=\frac{32,768\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{27M^{8}},

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}(T_{M}(0)\rightarrow\mathrm{C.H.})=\frac{11,075,584\sqrt{3}\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{243M^{8}},

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}(T_{M}(2)\rightarrow\mathrm{L.H.})=\frac{\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{M^{8}}(\frac{25,690,112}{405}+\frac{8,388,608}{135}n_{l}),

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}(T_{M}(2)\rightarrow\mathrm{C.H.})=\frac{719,323,136\sqrt{3}\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{1,215M^{8}},

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}(T_{C}^{+}(0)\rightarrow\mathrm{L.H.})=\frac{\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{M^{8}}\frac{991,232}{27},

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}(T_{C}^{+}(0)\rightarrow\mathrm{C.H.})=\frac{\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{M^{8}}\frac{16,384\sqrt{3}}{27},

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}(T_{C}^{-}(0)\rightarrow\mathrm{L.H.})=\frac{\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{M^{8}}147,456,

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}(T_{C}^{-}(0)\rightarrow\mathrm{C.H.})=\frac{\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{M^{8}}\frac{3,964,928\sqrt{3}}{81},

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}(T_{C}^{-}(2)\rightarrow\mathrm{L.H.})=\frac{\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{M^{8}}(\frac{12,845,056}{135}+\frac{4,194,304}{45}n_{l}),

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}(T_{C}^{-}(2)\rightarrow\mathrm{C.H.})=\frac{359,661,568\sqrt{3}\pi^{3}\alpha_{s}^{4}}{405M^{8}}.

(39)

For each tetraquark state, it is interesting to investigate the ratio of the decay width for the light-hadron final states to that for the charmed final states, namely

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)\equiv\frac{\Gamma(T\rightarrow\mathrm{L.H.})}{\Gamma(T\rightarrow\mathrm{C.H.})}.

(40)

On the theory side, this ratio is a number independent of physical quantities such as hadron mass and coupling constants,
and is thus free of uncertainties coming from parameter choices.
On the experiment side, compared to the measurement of the decay width,
it is much easier to measure the ratio thanks to the cancellation of the systematic uncertainties.

The values of
\mathcal{R}
are obtained as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T_{M}(0))=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{338}\approx 0.0154,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T_{M}(2))=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{28}+\frac{12\sqrt{3}}{343}n_{l}\approx 0.0619+0.0606n_{l},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T_{C}^{+}(0))=\frac{121\sqrt{3}}{6}\approx 34.9,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T_{C}^{-}(0))=\frac{243\sqrt{3}}{242}\approx 1.74,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T_{C}^{-}(2))=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{28}+\frac{12\sqrt{3}}{343}n_{l}\approx 0.0619+0.0606n_{l}.

(41)

For fully-charm tetraquarks,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T_{M}(2))=\mathcal{R}(T_{C}^{-}(2))=\frac{193\sqrt{3}}{1372}\approx 0.244,

(42)

while for fully-bottom tetraquarks,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T_{M}(2))=\mathcal{R}(T_{C}^{-}(2))=\frac{241\sqrt{3}}{1372}\approx 0.304.

(43)

For both of the cases, the ratios
\mathcal{R}
for the spin-2 tetraquarks are much different from those for the spin-0 ones,
which can serve as an alternative way of determining the spin of a fully-heavy tetraquark candidate,
in addition to measuring the angular distribution of the final-state lepton pairs in the decay process

T\rightarrow J/\psi J/\psi\rightarrow l^{+}l^{-}l^{+}l^{-}
.
For spin-0 tetraquarks,
\mathcal{R}
is different for different colour configurations or binding mechanisms.
Note that such information is crucial for understanding the binding mechanism of quarks,
however is not accessible by most of the observations.

4
Summary

In this paper, we presented the theoretical framework of calculating the hadronic decay width of fully-heavy tetraquarks.
Both molecule-like states and genuine tetraquarks were taken into consideration.
The phenomenological results at QCD leading order were presented,
and a crucial physical quantities, say the ratio
\mathcal{R}
of the decay width for the light-hadron final states to that for the charmed final states were also evaluated.
This value is a number independent of all the physical parameters,
and exhibits good properties for exploring the nature of the observed double charmonia resonances:
it can not only help to determine the spin of the resonances, but also distinguish different colour configurations and binding mechanisms of the spin-0 tetraquarks.
It is expected that our results may help the experiment to acquire useful information that is not accessible by any other tools.

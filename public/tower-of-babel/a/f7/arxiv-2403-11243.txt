On the refined Herglotz-Zagier function
Ziyi Huang

Abstract
. We give a functional equation for the refined Herglotz-Zagier function. It is analogous to a result in the theory of modular forms.

1.
Introduction

Consider the Herglotz-Zagier function

HZ\left(x\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left(\psi\left(nx\right)-\log\left(nx\right)\right)\;(x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\left(-\infty,0\right]),

where
\psi
is the digamma function. It was introduced by Zagier to obtain a Kronecker limit formula for real quadratic fields.
A modification given by the definition

\widetilde{HZ}\left(x\right)=HZ\left(x\right)-HZ\left(1\right)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\left(x-2+x^{-1}\right)-\frac{\log^{2}x}{4}

was introduced to obtain a relation to the Dedekind eta function and the Rogers dilogarithm
[
1
, (11)]
. Recall that the dilogarithm
\Li_{2}
is the function

\Li_{2}\left(x\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{x^{n}}{n^{2}}.

The Rogers dilogarithm is the function

L\left(x\right)=\Li_{2}\left(x\right)+\frac{1}{2}\log\left(x\right)\log\left(1-x\right)\;(0<x<1).

It is extended to
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\setminus\left\{1\right\}
by

L\left(x\right)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-L\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\;(x>1).

To better express the Kronecker limit formula for real quadratic fields, a refinement of the modified function
\widetilde{HZ}
was introduced as follows

\mathcal{HZ}\left(x,y\right)=\widetilde{HZ}\left(x\right)-\widetilde{HZ}\left(y\right)+L\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)\;(x>0,y>0,x\neq y).

2.
Statement of the result

We need some preparation before stating the result.
For an integer
l
, we define a set

S_{l}=\left\{M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\colon M\in\M_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right),0\leq b<a,0\leq c<d,ad-bc=l\right\}.

Let
\theta_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}\left[S_{l}\right]
be the formal sum

\theta_{l}=\sum_{M\in S_{l}}M.

Let
\M_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)_{l}\subset\M_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)
be the set of matrices of determinant
l
. Then
\SL_{2}
acts on
\M_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)_{l}
by right multiplication.
We make an important definition.

Definition 1
.

Suppose
n
is an integer. Let
T=\sum_{M}u_{M}\left(M\right)\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\M_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)_{n}\right]
be a formal sum. We say that
T
satisfies the
\mathscr{C}_{n}
condition
if we have in
\mathbb{Z}\left[\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}}^{1}\right]

\sum_{M\in\alpha}u_{M}\left(\left(M\infty\right)-\left(M0\right)\right)=\left(\infty\right)-\left(0\right),

where for a matrix
M\in\M_{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)_{n}
, we denote

M\infty=M\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},M0=M\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

Example 1
.

If
l=3
, then

\theta_{l}=\begin{pmatrix}3&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&3\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\
1&2\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
2&3\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&3\end{pmatrix}

satisfies
\mathscr{C}_{3}
.

Definition 2
.

For a function
f\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)
with values in
\mathbb{C}
, we define

f|_{\theta_{l}}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)=\sum_{M\in S_{l}}f|_{M}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)

where if
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
, then we define

f|_{M}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)=f\left(\frac{ax_{1}+c}{bx_{1}+d},\cdots,\frac{ax_{n}+c}{bx_{n}+d}\right).

Note that our definitions are essentially the same as those in
[
1
]
, up to transposition of matrices.
Now we can state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1
.

Let
l
be a prime number. If
\theta_{l}
satisfies
\mathscr{C}_{l}
, then we have the functional equation

\mathcal{HZ}|_{\theta_{l}}\left(x,y\right)=\left(l+1\right)\mathcal{HZ}\left(x,y\right)+\frac{l-1}{2}\log\left(l\right)\log\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)\mod\zeta\left(2\right).

Merel proved in
[
2
, Proposition 20]
that for all positive integers
n
,
\theta_{n}
satisfies
\mathscr{C}_{n}
, hence Theorem
1
holds for all prime number
l
.

Corollary 1
.

For all prime numbers
l
, we have

\mathcal{HZ}|_{\theta_{l}}\left(x,y\right)=\left(l+1\right)\mathcal{HZ}\left(x,y\right)+\frac{l-1}{2}\log\left(l\right)\log\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)\mod\zeta\left(2\right).

3.
Properties

We will often use the 5-term relation for the Rogers dilogarithm.

Proposition 1

(The 5-term relation)
.

For
x\neq y\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\setminus\left\{1\right\}
, we have the 5-term relation

(1)

L\left(x\right)-L\left(y\right)+L\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)-L\left(\frac{y-1}{x-1}\right)+L\left(\frac{1-1/y}{1-1/x}\right)\equiv 0\mod\zeta\left(2\right).

Proof.

This is equation (36) in
[
1
]
.

\Box

Since
\zeta\left(2\right)=\pi^{2}/6
, we have another useful equation following directly from the definition

(2)

L\left(x\right)+L\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\equiv 0\mod\zeta\left(2\right).

Let
S_{l}^{+}=\left\{M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\colon M\in S_{l},b>0\right\}
.

Theorem 2

(Radchenko and Zagier)
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
x>0
we have the functional equation

\widetilde{HZ}|_{\theta_{n}}\left(x\right)-\sigma\left(n\right)\widetilde{HZ}\left(x\right)=\sum_{M\in S_{n}^{+}}\left(L\left(\frac{x+c/a}{x+d/b}\right)-L\left(\frac{1+c/a}{1+d/b}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\mid n}r\log\left(\frac{r^{2}}{n}\right)\log\left(x\right).

Proof.

In the sum,
a,b,c,d
are such that
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
, we will use this convention in the sequel. This is equation (21) in
[
1
]
.

\Box

Corollary 2
.

If
l
is a prime number, then we have

(3)

\widetilde{HZ}|_{\theta_{l}}\left(x\right)-\left(l+1\right)\widetilde{HZ}\left(x\right)=\sum_{M\in S_{l}^{+}}\left(L\left(\frac{x+c/a}{x+d/b}\right)-L\left(\frac{1+c/a}{1+d/b}\right)\right)+\frac{l-1}{2}\log\left(l\right)\log\left(x\right).

Now we study the structure of
S_{l}
for prime numbers
l
such that
\theta_{l}
satisfies
\mathscr{C}_{l}
.
A subset
C\subset S_{l}
is called a chain if
C=\left\{M_{0},\cdots,M_{n}\right\}
such that

M_{i}0=M_{i+1}\infty

for all
0\leq i\leq n-1
. It is easy to see that different chains are disjoint. For
1\leq n\leq l-1
, let
C_{n}
be the chain such that

\begin{pmatrix}l&n\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in C_{n}.

Then it is easy to see that there exists a unique
1\leq x_{n}\leq l-1
such that

\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
x_{n}&l\end{pmatrix}\in C_{n}.

Note also that
x_{m}\neq x_{n}
if
m\neq n
.
Since
\theta_{l}
satisfies
\mathscr{C}_{l}
, we have the following structure.

Proposition 2
.

If
l
is prime and
\theta_{l}
satisfies
\mathscr{C}_{l}
, then
S_{l}
is a disjoint union

S_{l}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}l&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right\}\bigsqcup\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&l\end{pmatrix}\right\}\bigsqcup C_{1}\bigsqcup\cdots\bigsqcup C_{l-1}.

Proof.

If
T=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in S_{l}
such that
bc\neq 0
, then there must exist matrices
M,N\in S_{l}
such that

M\infty=T0,N0=T\infty
. If
M=\begin{pmatrix}b&b_{M}\\
d&d_{M}\end{pmatrix}
and
N=\begin{pmatrix}a_{N}&a\\
c_{N}&c\end{pmatrix}
, then we see that
c_{N}<c,b_{M}<b
. We continue by induction and obtain the result.

\Box

4.
Proof of Theorem 1

Proof.

By (
3
), we have

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{HZ}|_{\theta_{l}}\left(x,y\right)-\left(l+1\right)\mathcal{HZ}\left(x,y\right)\\
=&\left(\widetilde{HZ}|_{\theta_{l}}\left(x\right)-\left(l+1\right)\widetilde{HZ}\left(x\right)\right)-\left(\widetilde{HZ}|_{\theta_{l}}\left(y\right)-\left(l+1\right)\widetilde{HZ}\left(y\right)\right)\\
&+\sum_{M\in S_{l}}L\left(\frac{ay+c}{by+d}\frac{bx+d}{ax+c}\right)-\left(l+1\right)L\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)\\
=&\sum_{M\in S_{l}^{+}}\left(L\left(\frac{x+c/a}{x+d/b}\right)-L\left(\frac{y+c/a}{y+d/b}\right)+L\left(\frac{ay+c}{by+d}\frac{bx+d}{ax+c}\right)\right)+\frac{l-1}{2}\log\left(l\right)\log\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)\\
&+\sum_{M\in S_{l}\setminus S_{l}^{+}}L\left(\frac{ay+c}{by+d}\frac{bx+d}{ax+c}\right)-\left(l+1\right)L\left(\frac{y}{x}\right).\end{split}

Now for any
M\in S_{l}^{+}
, we calculate the sum using the 5-term relation (
1
). Let
u=bax+bc,v=bay+bc
, we have

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&L\left(\frac{x+c/a}{x+d/b}\right)-L\left(\frac{y+c/a}{1+d/b}\right)+L\left(\frac{ay+c}{by+d}\frac{bx+d}{ax+c}\right)\\
=&L\left(\frac{u}{u+l}\right)-L\left(\frac{v}{v+l}\right)+L\left(\frac{v\left(u+l\right)}{u\left(v+l\right)}\right)\\
=&L\left(\frac{u+l}{v+l}\right)-L\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)\\
=&L\left(\frac{bx+d}{by+d}\right)-L\left(\frac{ax+c}{ay+c}\right).\end{split}

A key observation is that, by the
\mathscr{C}_{l}
property, many terms will cancel by summing (
5
) over a chain
C_{n}
. In fact, we obtain a very simple result

(6)

\sum_{M\in C_{n}\cap S_{l}^{+}}\left(L\left(\frac{bx+d}{by+d}\right)-L\left(\frac{ax+c}{ay+c}\right)\right)=L\left(\frac{x+x_{n}}{y+x_{n}}\right)-L\left(\frac{x}{y}\right).

Then summing over
S_{l}^{+}
, we obtain

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{M\in S_{l}^{+}}\left(L\left(\frac{bx+d}{by+d}\right)-L\left(\frac{ax+c}{ay+c}\right)\right)&=\sum_{n=1}^{l-1}\sum_{M\in C_{n}\cap S_{l}^{+}}\left(L\left(\frac{bx+d}{by+d}\right)-L\left(\frac{ax+c}{ay+c}\right)\right)\\
&=\sum_{n=1}^{l-1}\left(L\left(\frac{x+x_{n}}{y+x_{n}}\right)-L\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)\right)\\
&=\sum_{n=1}^{l-1}L\left(\frac{x+n}{y+n}\right)-\left(l-1\right)L\left(\frac{x}{y}\right).\end{split}

We now calculate the other sum in (
4
). By Proposition
2
, we see that

S_{l}\setminus S_{l}^{+}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}l&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&l\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&l\end{pmatrix},\cdots,\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
l-1&l\end{pmatrix}\right\}.

Hence we have

(8)

\sum_{M\in S_{l}\setminus S_{l}^{+}}L\left(\frac{ay+c}{by+d}\frac{bx+d}{ax+c}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{l-1}L\left(\frac{y+n}{x+n}\right)+2L\left(\frac{y}{x}\right).

Finally, we combine (
1
) and equations (
4
), (
7
), (
8
) to obtain

\mathcal{HZ}|_{\theta_{l}}\left(x,y\right)=\left(l+1\right)\mathcal{HZ}\left(x,y\right)+\frac{l-1}{2}\log\left(l\right)\log\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)\mod\zeta\left(2\right).

\Box

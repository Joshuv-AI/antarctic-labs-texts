Preliminaries

We define the

.

The
\Phi
-anisotropic gradient of a function

is given by

(13)

\bar{\nabla}\phi=D^{2}\Phi(v)\nabla\phi,

and

(14)

|\bar{\nabla}\phi|^{2}=(\bar{\nabla}\phi,\bar{\nabla}\phi)_{\Phi}=g(D^{2}\Phi(v)\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi).

More generally, for tensor field

on

whose local coordinates representation is

, we can define the
\Phi

\Phi
-anisotropic norm

|T|^{2}=\bar{g}_{i_{1}k_{1}}...\bar{g}_{i_{r}k_{r}}\bar{g}^{j_{1}l_{1}}...\bar{g}^{j_{s}l_{s}}T_{j_{1}...j_{s}}^{i_{1}...i_{r}}T_{l_{1}...l_{s}}^{k_{1}...k_{r}}.

Let

\Phi

The second variation formula in

3.
Generalized Simons’ inequality

C^{3}

\Phi

(Generalized Simons’ inequality)
.

There is constant
\delta_{0}(n)>0

\left(\int_{\Sigma}|f|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq C(n,\Phi)\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2},

Then, by Proposition

for

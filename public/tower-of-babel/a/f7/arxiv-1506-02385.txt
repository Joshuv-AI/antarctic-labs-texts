Uniform convergence of conditional distributions for absorbed one-dimensional diffusions
Nicolas Champagnat, Denis Villemonais

Abstract
This article studies the quasi-stationary behaviour of absorbed one-dimensional diffusions. We obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for the exponential convergence to a unique quasi-stationary distribution in total variation, uniformly with respect to the initial distribution. An important tool is provided by one dimensional strict local martingale diffusions coming down from infinity. We prove under mild assumptions that their expectation at any positive time is uniformly bounded with respect to the initial position. We provide several examples and extensions, including the sticky Brownian motion and some one-dimensional processes with jumps.

Introduction

This article studies the quasi-stationary behaviour of general one-dimensional diffusion processes in an interval
E
of
\mathbb{R}
,
absorbed at its finite boundaries. When the process is absorbed, it is sent to some cemetary point
\partial
. We recall that a

quasi-stationary distribution
for a continuous-time Markov process
(X_{t},t\geq 0)
on the state space
E\cup\{\partial\}
absorbed
in
\partial
, is a probability measure
\alpha
on
E
such that

\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}(X_{t}\in\cdot\mid t<\tau_{\partial})=\alpha(\cdot),\quad\forall t\geq 0,

where
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
denotes the distribution of the process
X
given that
X_{0}
has distribution
\alpha
, and

\tau_{\partial}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}=\partial\}.

We refer to
[
15
,
22
,
19
]
for general introductions to the topic.

We consider a general diffusion
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
[a,b)
with
a<b\leq+\infty
absorbed at
\partial=a
in a.s. finite time
\tau_{\partial}

and such that
b
is an entrance or natural boundary. We are interested in the existence of a quasi-stationary distribution
\alpha

on
(a,b)
such that, for all probability measures
\mu
on
(a,b)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(X_{t}\in\cdot\mid t<\tau_{\partial})-\alpha\right\|_{TV}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t},\ \forall t\geq 0

(1.1)

where
\|\cdot\|_{TV}
is the total variation norm and
C
and
\gamma
are positive constants. We prove that this property is

equivalent
to the following condition:

(B)

The boundary
b
is entrance for
X
and there exists
t,A>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\partial}>t)\leq As(x),\quad\forall x>0,

where
s
is the scale function of the diffusion
X
.

In the sequel, without loss of generality, we will focus on the case of a diffusion on natural scale on
E\cup\{\partial\}=[0,+\infty)
and modify
(B) accordingly.

The exponential convergence (
1.1
) entails number of additional properties. First, it implies the uniqueness of the
quasi-stationary distribution
\alpha
which attracts any initial distribution. Second, (
1.1
) implies

[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
that the following convergence is uniform in
x\in(a,b)
:

\displaystyle\eta(x)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}(t<\tau_{\partial})}{\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}(t<\tau_{\partial})}=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}e^{\lambda_{0}t}\mathbb{P}_{x}(t<\tau_{\partial}).

(1.2)

Note that we used the existence of a constant
\lambda_{0}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}(t<\tau_{\partial})=e^{-\lambda_{0}t},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

which is a well-known property of quasi-stationnary distributions. Moreover
[
3
, Prop. 2.3]
,
\eta

belongs to the domain of the infinitesimal generator
L
of
X
on the Banach space of bounded measurable functions on
[a,b)

equipped with the
L^{\infty}
norm and

\displaystyle L\eta=-\lambda_{0}\eta.

Third, it implies the existence and the exponential ergodicity of the associated
Q
-process, defined as the process
X
conditionned
to never be extinct (see
[
3
, Thm. 3.1]
for a precise definition). The convergence of the conditional
laws of
X
to the law of the
Q
-process holds also uniformly in total variation
norm
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
, which also entails conditional ergodic
properties
[
5
, Corollary 2.2]
.

The second main result of this paper is the fact that Condition (B) is closely related to properties of strict local martingale
diffusions
(Z_{t},t\geq 0)
on
[0,\infty)
. We recall that
(Z_{t},t\geq 0)
is called a strict local martingale if
\mathbb{E}_{x}(Z_{t})<x
for all
x,t>0
. We say that
(Z_{t},t\geq 0)
is a
strict martingale in the strong sense
if, for all

t>0
,

\sup_{x>0}\mathbb{E}_{x}(Z_{t})<\infty.

(1.3)

We prove, under a mild control on the oscillations of the speed measure near
+\infty
, that the property (
1.3
) is true for all
t>0
. This new result has its own interest in
the theory of strict local martingales
[
20
]
and applies to nearly all strict local martingale diffusions on

[0,\infty)
. For instance, it is well-known that the solution on
(0,+\infty)
to the
SDE

\displaystyle dZ_{t}=Z_{t}^{\alpha}dB_{t}

is a strict local martingale if and only if
\alpha>1
. Our result implies that, for
all
\alpha>1
and all
t>0
, (
1.3
) holds true, and hence that
\mathbb{E}_{x}(Z_{t})<x
for all
x>0
is actually
equivalent to
\sup_{x>0}\mathbb{E}_{x}(Z_{t})<\infty
. This equivalence is also true for example for the SDE

\displaystyle dZ_{t}=Z_{t}\ln(Z_{t})^{\beta}dB_{t}

which is a (strong) strict local martingale iff
\beta>1/2
.

We deduce from these results an explicit criterion ensuring Condition (B), which shows that it is satisfied for nearly all diffusions
coming down from infinity and hitting 0 in a.s. finite time. However, in several cases, Condition (B) is simple enough to be checked using elementary
probability tools, and to apply to many other settings (processes with jumps, piecewise deterministic
paths
[
3
]
or time-inhomogeneous processes
[
4
]
). Hence we also provide a
simple probabilistic criterion and show how it applies in an example of one-dimensional process with jumps.

We also provide an explicit formula relating the speed measure of a diffusion on natural scale and its quasi-stationary distribution.
Conversely, we show that, given a probability measure
\alpha
on
(0,\infty)
satisfying suitable conditions and a positive constant

\lambda_{0}
, there exists a unique diffusion on natural scale, with explicit speed measure, admitting
\alpha
as unique
quasi-stationary distribution with associated absorbing rate
\lambda_{0}
. We also use this explicit formula to compute the
quasi-stationary distribution of the sticky Brownian motion on
(-1,1)
, absorbed at
\{-1,1\}
.

The usual tools to prove convergence as in (
1.1
) for conservative Markov processes involve coupling arguments (Doeblin’s
condition, Dobrushin coupling constant, see e.g.
[
16
]
). However, a diffusion conditioned not to hit 0 before a given time

t>0
is a time-inhomogeneous diffusion process with a singular, non-explicit drift for which these methods fail. For instance, the
solution
(X_{s})_{s\geq 0}
to the SDE

\displaystyle dX_{s}=\sigma(X_{s})dB_{s}+b(X_{s})ds

with smooth coefficients and conditioned not to hit 0 up to a time
t>0
has the law of the solution
(X^{(t)}_{s})_{s\in[0,t]}
to the
SDE

\displaystyle dX^{(t)}_{s}=b(X^{(t)}_{s})ds+\sigma(X^{(t)}_{s})dB_{s}+\sigma(X^{(t)}_{s})\,\left[\nabla\ln\mathbb{P}_{\cdot}(t-s<\tau_{\partial})\right](X^{(t)}_{s})ds.

Since
\mathbb{P}_{x}(t-s<\tau_{\partial})
vanishes when
x
converges to 0, the drift term in the above SDE is singular and cannot in general be
quantified.

Hence, convergence of conditioned diffusion processes have been obtained up to now using (sometimes involved) spectral theoretic
arguments, which most often require regularity of the coefficients (see for
instance
[
2
,
13
,
10
,
7
,
17
]
) and are specific to time homogeneous
one-dimensional diffusion processes. None of the above references provide uniform convergence with respect to the initial
distribution. In particular, all the consequences of (
1.1
) listed above are original results of this paper, even for
solutions to SDEs with regular coefficients. Moreover, the uniformity of the convergence with respect to the initial distribution is
important in applications, as discussed in
[
15
, Ex. 2]
. Concerning the sole existence and uniqueness of a
quasi-stationnary distribution, in the few cases where our criterion is not satisfied, we refer the reader
to
[
13
,
10
,
7
]
. An interesting open problem is to prove that (
1.1
), and hence
Condition (B), actually holds true in these cases.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we precisely define the absorbed diffusion processes and recall
their construction as time-changed Brownian motions. Section
3
contains several results on strict local
martingales, including the criterion ensuring (
1.3
). We give in Section
4
the statements of
all our main results on quasi-stationnary distributions (Subsections
4.1
and
4.4
) and on
criteria ensuring Condition (B) (Subsections
4.2
and
4.3
). We also study in
Subsection
4.5
several examples including sticky Brownian motion and diffusions with jumps. Finally, we give in
Section
5
the proofs of the main results of Section
4
.

2
Some reminders on diffusion processes

In this section, we recall the construction of general diffusion processes
(X_{t},t\geq 0)
on
[0,+\infty)
, absorbed at

\partial=0
. The typical situation corresponds to stochastic population dynamics of continuous densities with possible extinction.

The distribution of
X
given
X_{0}=x\in[0,\infty)
will be denoted
\mathbb{P}_{x}
, and the semigroup of the process is given by

P_{t}f(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[f(X_{t})]
for all bounded measurable
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and all
x\geq 0
.

A stochastic process
(X_{t},t\geq 0)
on
[0,+\infty)
is called a diffusion if it has a.s. continuous paths in
[0,\infty)
,
satisfies the strong Markov property and is
regular
. By regular, we mean that for all
x\in(0,\infty)
and
y\in[0,\infty)
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{y}<\infty)>0
, where
T_{y}
is the first hitting time of
y
by the process
X
. Examples of such processes are given by (weak)
solutions of stochastic differential equations (see Section
4.5
).

Given such a process, there exists a continuous and strictly increasing function
s
on
[0,\infty)
, called the
scale
function
, such that
(s(X_{t\wedge T_{0}}),t\geq 0)
is a local martingale
[
6
]
. The stochastic process
(s(X_{t}),t\geq 0)
is itself a diffusion process with identity scale function. Since we shall assume that 0 is regular or exit and that
T_{0}<\infty

a.s., we necessarily have
s(0)>-\infty
and
s(\infty)=\infty
, and we can assume
s(0)=0
. Hence, replacing
(X_{t},t\geq 0)
by

(s(X_{t}),t\geq 0)
, we can assume without loss of generality that
s(x)=x
. Such a process is said to be on
natural scale
and
satisfies for all
0<a<b<\infty
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{a}<T_{b})=\frac{b-x}{b-a}.

To such a process
X
, one can associate a unique locally finite positive measure
m(dx)
on
(0,+\infty)
, called the
speed
measure
of
X
, which gives positive mass to any open subset of
(0,+\infty)
and such that
X_{t}=B_{\sigma_{t}}
for all
t\geq 0

for some standard Brownian motion
B
, where

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}=\inf\left\{s>0:A_{s}>t\right\},\text{ with }A_{s}=\int_{0}^{\infty}L^{x}_{s}\,m(dx).

(2.1)

and
L^{x}
is the local time of
B
at level
x
. Conversely, any such time change of a Brownian motion defines a regular diffusion on

[0,\infty)

[
9
, Thm. 23.9]
.

We will use in the sequel the assumption that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}y\,m(dy)<\infty.

(2.2)

Since the measure
m
is locally finite on
(0,\infty)
, this assumption reduces to local integrability of
y\,m(dy)
near 0 and

+\infty
. These two conditions are respectively equivalent to
\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\partial}<\infty)=1
for all

x\in(0,\infty)

[
9
, Thm. 23.12]
, where
\tau_{\partial}:=T_{0}
is the first hitting time of
\partial=0
by the process
X
, and to
the existence of
t>0
and
y>0
such that

\inf_{x>y}\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{y}<t)>0.

This means that
\infty
is an
entrance boundary
for
X

[
9
, Thm. 23.12]
, or equivalently that the process

X

comes down from infinity

[
2
]
.

We recall that
0
is absorbing for the process
X
iff
\int_{0}^{1}\,m(dx)=\infty

[
9
, Thm. 23.12]
, which is not
necessarily the case under the assumption that
\int_{0}^{1}y\,m(dy)<\infty
. Therefore, we modify the definition of
X
so that
\partial

becomes an absorbing point as follows:

X_{t}=\begin{cases}B_{\sigma_{t}}&\text{if\ }0\leq t<\tau_{\partial}\\
\partial&\text{if\ }t\geq\tau_{\partial}.\end{cases}

(2.3)

Note that this could be done equivalently by replacing
m
by
m+\infty\delta_{0}
.

With this definition,
X
is a local martingale.
Note that
A_{s}
is strictly increasing since
m
gives positive mass to any open interval, and hence
\sigma_{t}
is continuous and

\sigma_{\tau_{\partial}}=T^{B}_{0},\quad\text{or, equivalently,}\quad\tau_{\partial}=A_{T^{B}_{0}},

(2.4)

where
T^{B}_{x}
is the first hitting time of
x\in\mathbb{R}
by the process
(B_{t},t\geq 0)
. In particular,
X_{t}=B_{\sigma_{t}\wedge T_{0}^{B}}
,
\forall t\geq 0
.

3
Strict local martingales

This section is devoted to the study of one-dimensional strict local martingale diffusions on
[0,\infty)
coming down from infinity
and their link with absorbed one-dimensional diffusion processes. We recall that a nonnegative, real local martingale
(Z_{t},t\geq 0)

with
Z_{0}=z\geq 0
is called strict if it is not a martingale, i.e. if
\mathbb{E}(Z_{t})<z
for at least one
z\in(0,\infty)
. If

(Z_{t},t\geq 0)
is a diffusion on
[0,\infty)
on natural scale with speed measure
\widetilde{m}
, then

(Z_{t},t\geq 0)\mbox{ is a strict local martingale }\Longleftrightarrow\ \int_{1}^{\infty}y\,\widetilde{m}(dy)<\infty,

(3.1)

i.e. if
+\infty
is an entrance boundary
[
11
]
. The notion of strict local martingales is of great importance in the
theory of financial bubbles. We refer the interested reader to Protter
[
20
]
and references therein.

The goal of this section is to study a stronger notion of strict martingale. We will say that a nonnegative, real local martingale

(Z_{t},t\geq 0)
is a
strict martingale in the strong sense
if, for all
t>0
, there exists
A_{t}<\infty
such that

\mathbb{E}_{z}(Z_{t})\leq A_{t},\ \forall z>0.

3.1
Strict local martingales in a strong sense

The next result gives a sufficient criterion for a strict local martingale diffusion process
Z
on
[0,\infty)
to be a strictly
local martingale in the strong sense.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\widetilde{m}
be a locally finite measure on
(0,\infty)
giving positive mass to any open subset of
(0,\infty)
, and let

Z
be a diffusion process on
[0,\infty)
stopped at 0 on natural scale with speed measure
\widetilde{m}
. Assume

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x}\,\sup_{y\geq x}\left(y\int_{y}^{\infty}\widetilde{m}(dz)\right)\,dx<\infty.

(3.2)

Then,
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a strict local martingale in the strong sense.

Note that Fubini’s theorem implies that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}(z-1)\,\widetilde{m}(dz)

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{y}^{\infty}\widetilde{m}(dz)\,dy\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x}\left(\sup_{y\geq x}y\int_{y}^{\infty}\widetilde{m}(dz)\right)dx.

Hence (
3.2
) and the fact that
\widetilde{m}
is locally finite implies that

\int_{1}^{\infty}y\,\widetilde{m}(dy)<\infty.

(3.3)

Remark 1
.

Although we do not know if all strict local martingale diffusions are strict in the strong sense, the last result shows that it is
true for most strict local martingale diffusions. Indeed, when
\widetilde{m}
has a density with respect to Lebesgue’s measure

\Lambda
on
(0,\infty)
, we recall that
(Z_{t},t\geq 0)
is (weak) solution to the SDE

dZ_{t}=\sigma(Z_{t})dB_{t},

where
(B_{t},t\geq 0)
is a standard Brownian motion and
\widetilde{m}(dx)=\sigma^{-2}(x)\Lambda(dx)
. In this case, the
condition (
3.2
) covers almost all the practical situations where (
3.3
) is satisfied. For
example, it is true if

\displaystyle\frac{d\widetilde{m}}{d\Lambda}(x)\leq\frac{C}{x^{2}\log x\cdots\log_{k-1}x\left(\log_{k}x\right)^{1+\epsilon}},

or equivalently

\displaystyle\sigma(x)\geq C^{\prime}x\sqrt{\log x\cdots\log_{k-1}x\left(\log_{k}x\right)^{1+\epsilon}},

for all
x
large enough and for some
\epsilon>0
and
k\geq 1
, where
\log_{k}(x)=\log_{k-1}(\log x)

(see
[
14
, Ex. 2]
). In other words, (
3.2
) could fail only when
\widetilde{m}
has strong
oscillations close to
+\infty
and
y\,\widetilde{m}(dy)
is nearly non-integrable at infinity.

Proof.

Since
(Z_{t},t\geq 0)
is a positive local martingale, hence a supermartingale, and a strong Markov process, we have for all
z\geq 1
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z}(Z_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{z}(Z_{t}\mathbbm{1}_{t<T^{Z}_{1}})+\mathbb{E}_{z}(Z_{t}\mathbbm{1}_{T^{Z}_{1}<t})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{z}(Z_{t\wedge T^{Z}_{1}}\mathbbm{1}_{t<T^{Z}_{1}})+\mathbb{E}_{z}[\mathbbm{1}_{T^{Z}_{1}<t}\mathbb{E}_{1}(Z_{t-T^{Z}_{1}})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{z}(Z_{t\wedge T^{Z}_{1}})+1,

where
T^{Z}_{1}
is the first hitting time of 1 by
Z
. Hence, we only need to prove that
\sup_{z\geq 1}\mathbb{E}_{z}(Z^{T_{1}}_{t})<\infty
,
where
Z^{T_{1}}
is the diffusion process
Z
stopped at time
T^{Z}_{1}
.

Since
Z^{T_{1}}
is a diffusion process on
[1,\infty)
, stopped at 1, on natural scale and with speed measure

\nu(\cdot)=\widetilde{m}(\cdot\cap(1,\infty))
satisfying

\nu([1,2])<\infty,

we can apply the result of
[
14
, Thm. 4.1, Ex. 2]
. This result ensures that, under
conditions (
3.3
) and (
3.2
), the probability density function of
Z^{T_{1}}_{t}
with respect to

\nu
, denoted by
p(x,y,t)
, is well defined for all
t>0
and there exists a constant
A^{\prime}_{t}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq x,y<+\infty}p(x,y,t)\leq A^{\prime}_{t},\quad\forall t>0.

As a consequence, for all
t>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z}(Z^{T_{1}}_{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}y\,p(z,y,t)\,d\nu(y)\leq A^{\prime}_{t}\int_{1}^{\infty}y\,d\widetilde{m}(y).\qed

3.2
Links between strict local martingales and absorbed diffusions

The next result gives a change of measure linking the behavior near 0 and near
+\infty
of diffusion processes on
[0,\infty)
on
natural scale. Note that it does not require that
X
comes down from infinity nor that it hits 0 in finite time.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X
be a diffusion process on
[0,+\infty)
on natural scale stopped at 0 with speed measure
m
. We denote by
\mathbb{P}_{x}
its law
when
X_{0}=x
, defined on the canonical space of continuous functions from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to itself, equipped with its canonical filtration

(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. For all
x>0
, we define the following
h
-transform of
\mathbb{P}_{x}
:

\mathchoice{{d\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{x}\,\smash{\vrule height=5.7778pt,depth=2.12502pt}}_{\,\mathcal{F}_{\tau}}}{{d\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{x}\,\smash{\vrule height=5.7778pt,depth=2.12502pt}}_{\,\mathcal{F}_{\tau}}}{{d\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{x}\,\smash{\vrule height=5.7778pt,depth=1.4875pt}}_{\,\mathcal{F}_{\tau}}}{{d\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{x}\,\smash{\vrule height=5.7778pt,depth=2.06259pt}}_{\,\mathcal{F}_{\tau}}}=\frac{X_{\tau}}{x}\mathchoice{{d\mathbb{P}_{x}\,\smash{\vrule height=5.55557pt,depth=2.12502pt}}_{\,\mathcal{F}_{\tau}}}{{d\mathbb{P}_{x}\,\smash{\vrule height=5.55557pt,depth=2.12502pt}}_{\,\mathcal{F}_{\tau}}}{{d\mathbb{P}_{x}\,\smash{\vrule height=3.88889pt,depth=1.4875pt}}_{\,\mathcal{F}_{\tau}}}{{d\mathbb{P}_{x}\,\smash{\vrule height=2.77777pt,depth=2.06259pt}}_{\,\mathcal{F}_{\tau}}},

for all
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-stopping time
\tau
such that
(X_{t\wedge\tau},t\geq 0)
is a uniformly integrable martingale. Then,
under
(\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{x})_{x>0}
, the process
Z_{t}:=1/X_{t}
is a diffusion process stopped at 0 on natural scale with speed
measure

\widetilde{m}(dz)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}(f*m)(dz),

(3.4)

where
f*m
is the pushforward measure of
m
by the application
f(x)=1/x
.

In particular, if the measure
m(dx)
is absolutely continuous w.r.t. Lebesgue’s measure
\Lambda
on
(0,+\infty)
, then

\widetilde{m}(dz)
is absolutely continuous w.r.t.
\Lambda
and

\frac{d\widetilde{m}}{d\Lambda}(z)=\frac{1}{z^{4}}\frac{dm}{d\Lambda}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right),\quad\forall z>0.

(3.5)

Proof.

The fact that
\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{x}
,
x>0
, are probability measures and that the process
Z=1/X
is a diffusion on
[0,\infty)

stopped at 0 on natural scale under
(\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{x})_{x>0}
are proved in
[
18
]
(see Lemma 2.5 for the last
point). Hence, we only have to check that
\widetilde{m}
defined in (
3.4
) is its speed measure. By Green’s
formula
[
9
, Lemma 23.10]
, this means that, for all
0<a<z<b<+\infty
and for all bounded measurable function

g:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{E}^{\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{1/z}}\left[\int_{0}^{T^{Z}_{a,b}}g(Z_{t})\,dt\right]=2\int_{a}^{b}\frac{(z\wedge y-a)(b-z\vee y)}{b-a}g(y)\,\widetilde{m}(dy),

(3.6)

where
T^{Z}_{a,b}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:Z_{t}\in\{a,b\}\}
.

On the one hand, the definition of
\widetilde{m}
and the change of variable formula entails that the right hand term of (
3.6
) is equal to

2\int_{a}^{b}\frac{(z\wedge y-a)(b-z\vee y)}{b-a}\frac{g(y)}{y^{2}}\,(f*m)(dy)\\
\begin{aligned} &=2\int_{1/b}^{1/a}\frac{(z\wedge\frac{1}{x}-a)(b-z\vee\frac{1}{x})}{b-a}x^{2}g(1/x)\,m(dx)\\
&=2z\int_{1/b}^{1/a}\frac{(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{z}\vee x)(x\wedge\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{b})}{\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}}xg(1/x)\,m(dx)\\
&=z\mathbb{E}_{1/z}\left[\int_{0}^{T_{1/b,1/a}}X_{t}g(1/X_{t})\,dt\right],\end{aligned}

where the last equality comes from the fact that
X
is a diffusion on natural scale with speed measure
m
under
(\mathbb{P}_{x})_{x\geq 0}
.

On the other hand, since
(X_{t\wedge T_{1/b,1/a}},t\geq 0)
is a bounded martingale under
\mathbb{P}_{1/z}
, we obtain by definition of
(\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{x})_{x>0}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{1/z}}\left[\int_{0}^{T^{Z}_{a,b}}g(Z_{t})\,dt\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_{1/z}}\left[\int_{0}^{T_{1/b,1/a}}g(1/X_{t})\,dt\right]

\displaystyle=z\mathbb{E}_{1/z}\left[X_{T_{1/b,1/a}}\int_{0}^{T_{1/b,1/a}}g(1/X_{t})\,dt\right].

Now, Itô’s formula implies that, a.s. for all
t\geq 0
,

X_{t}\int_{0}^{t}g(1/X_{s})\,ds=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{s}g(1/X_{u})\,du\right)\,dX_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}g(1/X_{s})\,ds.

Since
(X_{t\wedge T_{1/b,1/a}},t\geq 0)
is a bounded martingale under
\mathbb{P}_{1/z}
, for all
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}_{1/z}\left[X_{n\wedge T_{1/b,1/a}}\int_{0}^{n\wedge T_{1/b,1/a}}g(1/X_{s})\,ds\right]=\mathbb{E}_{1/z}\left[\int_{0}^{n\wedge T_{1/b,1/a}}X_{s}g(1/X_{s})\,ds\right].

Since
\mathbb{E}_{1/z}[T_{1/b,1/a}]<\infty
(this is a general property of diffusions), Lebesgue’s theorem implies (
3.6
), which
ends the proof of Theorem
3.2
.
∎

The nature (regular/exit or entrance) of the boundary points 0 and
\infty
for

and
Z
are related.

Corollary 3.3
.

With the previous notation,
X
hits 0 in a.s. finite time iff
Z
comes down form infinity, and
X
comes down from infinity iff

Z
hits 0 in a.s. finite time. In particular,

\displaystyle(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}\text{ is a strict local martingale}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (X_{t})_{t\geq 0}\mbox{ hits 0 in a.s.\ finite time}.

Proof.

We need to check that

\int_{0}^{1}y\,m(dy)<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\int_{1}^{\infty}y\,\widetilde{m}(dy)<\infty

and

\int_{1}^{\infty}y\,m(dy)<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\int_{0}^{1}y\,\widetilde{m}(dy)<\infty.

This follows from the definition (
3.4
) of
\widetilde{m}
by a simple change of variable.
∎

. We obtain in particular a condition on the diffusion

.

With the previous notation, for all

where

(3.7)

4
Quasi-stationary distribution for one dimensional diffusions

We consider in this section a diffusion process
X
on
[0,\infty)
on natural scale hitting 0 in a.s. finite time
\tau_{\partial}
. Recall
that this is equivalent to assuming

\int_{0}^{1}y\,m(dy)<\infty

where
m
is the speed measure of
X
. We also set
X_{t}=0
for all
t>\tau_{\partial}
.

4.1
Exponential convergence to quasi-stationary distribution

The aim of this section is to establish that the following Condition (B) is equivalent to the exponential convergence to a unique
quasi-stationary distribution of conditional distributions of
X
.

(B)

The process
X
comes down from infinity and there exist two constants
t_{1},A>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\partial}>t_{1})\leq Ax,\quad\forall x>0.

(4.1)

Let us recall that
X
comes down from infinity iff
\int_{1}^{\infty}y\,m(dy)<\infty
.

Remark 2
.

Note that it is well known that, for diffusion processes on natural scale on
[0,\infty)
,
\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\partial}<\infty)>0
for some
x>0

implies that
\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\partial}<\infty)=1
for all
x>0
(it can be also deduced from (
3.1
) and the results of
Section
3.2
). Hence (
4.1
) implies that
\int_{0}^{1}y\,m(dy)<\infty
.

Remark 3
.

The converse inequality in (
4.1
) is true for all diffusion process
X
on natural scale on
(0,+\infty)
: for
all
t>0
, there exists a constant
a>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{x}(t<\tau_{\partial})\geq ax,\quad\forall x\in(0,1).

Indeed, by the strong Markov property at time
T_{1}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(t<\tau_{\partial})\geq\mathbb{E}_{x}[\mathbbm{1}_{T_{1}<\tau_{\partial}}\mathbb{P}_{1}(t<\tau_{\partial})]=\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{1}<\tau_{\partial})\mathbb{P}_{1}(t<\tau_{\partial})=x\mathbb{P}_{1}(t<\tau_{\partial}).

The following result is proved in Section
5
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
X
is a one-dimensional diffusion on natural scale on
[0,\infty)
with speed measure
m(dx)
such that

\tau_{\partial}<\infty
a.s. Then we have equivalence between

(i)

Assumption
(B)
.

(ii)

There exist a probability measure
\alpha
on
(0,\infty)
and two constants
C,\gamma>0
such that, for all
initial distribution
\mu
on
(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(X_{t}\in\cdot\mid t<\tau_{\partial})-\alpha(\cdot)\right\|_{TV}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t},\ \forall t\geq 0.

(4.2)

In this case,
\alpha
is the unique quasi-stationary distribution for the process and there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}(t<\tau_{\partial})=e^{-\lambda_{0}t}
. Moreover,
\alpha
is absolutely continuous with respect to
m
and

\displaystyle\frac{d\alpha}{dm}(x)=2\lambda_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}(x\wedge y)\,\alpha(dy).

(4.3)

In addition,

\int_{0}^{\infty}y\,\alpha(dy)<\infty.

In the next result, we provide additional information on the function
\eta
defined in (
1.2
).

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that
X
is a one-dimensional diffusion on natural scale with speed measure
m(dx)
such that
\tau_{\partial}<\infty
a.s. and such
that (B) is satisfied. Then the limit
\eta
in (
1.2
) is given by the normalized right hand side
of (
4.3
):

\displaystyle\eta(x)=\frac{\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\infty}(x\wedge y)\,\alpha(dy)}}{\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}(y\wedge z)\,\alpha(dy)\,\alpha(dz)}}.

(4.4)

Moreover, the function
\eta
is non-decreasing, bounded,
\alpha(\eta)=1
and
\eta(x)\leq Cx
for all
x\geq 0
and some constant

C>0
.

Remark 4
.

We deduce from the last results that the density of
\alpha
with respect to
m
is proportional to
\eta
. Hence it is positive,
bounded, (strictly) increasing on
(0,+\infty)
, and differentiable at each point of
(0,+\infty)
.

4.2
Conditions ensuring (B) using elementary probabilistic tools

The next result gives a condition implying (B). Its proof is particularly simple. We give in the next section a slightly more general
criterion which makes use of the results on strict local martingales of Section
3
.

Theorem 4.3
.

With the previous notation, assume that
X
comes down from infinity, hits 0 a.s. in finite time and for all
x\in(0,1)
,

I(x):=\int_{0}^{x}y\,m(dy)\leq Cx^{\rho}

(4.5)

for some constants
C>0
and
\rho>0
.
Then, for all
t>0
, there exists
A_{t}<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(t<\tau_{\partial})\leq A_{t}\,x,\ \forall x>0.

We immediately deduce the next corollary.

Corollary 4.4
.

Assume that
X
comes down from infinity and that
0
is regular, i.e.
\int_{0}^{1}m(dx)<\infty
. Then Condition (B) is
satisfied.

Remark 5
.

Equation (
4.5
) is satisfied if
m
is absolutely continuous w.r.t. Lebesgue’s measure
\Lambda
on
(0,\infty)

and if

\frac{dm}{d\Lambda}(x)\leq\frac{C}{x^{\alpha}}

in the neighborhood of
0
, where
\alpha<2
. This corresponds to a diffusion process solution to the SDE

dX_{t}=\sigma(X_{t})dB_{t}

with
\sigma(x)\geq cx^{\alpha/2}
in the neighborhood of
0
.

Proof.

The proof is based on the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

For all
k\geq 1
and
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the function
M_{k}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\tau_{\partial}^{k}\right)
is bounded and satisfies

\displaystyle M_{k}(x)=2k\int_{0}^{\infty}(x\wedge y)\,M_{k-1}(y)\,m(dy)\,<\infty.

(4.6)

We admit this lemma for the moment and give its proof at the end of the subsection.

First, notice that we can assume without loss of generality that
\rho\not\in\mathbb{Q}
. We prove by induction that there exists a
constant
C_{k}>0
such that for all
x\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle M_{k}(x)\leq C_{k}x^{(k\rho)\wedge 1}.

(4.7)

This is enough to conclude since one can choose
k
such that
k\rho>1
and use Markov’s inequality to obtain

\mathbb{P}_{x}(t<\tau_{\partial})\leq\frac{M_{k}(x)}{t^{k}}\leq C_{t}x

where the constant
C_{t}
depends on
t
but not on
x
.

Equation (
4.7
) is trivial for
k=0
. Let us assume it is true for
k\geq 0
and let us denote by
\alpha_{k}
the
constant
(k\rho)\wedge 1
. For all
x\in(0,1)
, by (
4.6
),

\displaystyle M_{k+1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{x}yM_{k}(y)m(dy)+x\int_{x}^{1}M_{k}(y)m(dy)+x\int_{1}^{\infty}M_{k}(y)m(dy)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{x}y^{1+\alpha_{k}}\,m(dy)+x\int_{x}^{1}y^{\alpha_{k}-1}\,y\,m(dy)+x\int_{1}^{\infty}m(dy)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left[x^{\alpha_{k}}I(x)+x\left(I(1)+(1-\alpha_{k})\int_{x}^{1}y^{\alpha_{k}-2}I(y)\,dy\right)+x\int_{1}^{\infty}m(dy)\right]

\displaystyle\leq Cx^{\alpha_{k}+\rho}+Cx+Cx\int_{x}^{1}y^{\alpha_{k}+\rho-2}\,dy,

where the constant
C>0
depends on
k
and may change from line to line, and the third inequality follows from integration by parts.
Since
\rho\not\in\mathbb{Q}
, we have

\int_{x}^{1}y^{\alpha_{k}+\rho-2}\,dy\leq\begin{cases}1/(\alpha_{k}+\rho-1)&\text{if }\alpha_{k}+\rho-1>0,\\
x^{\alpha_{k}+\rho-1}/(1-\alpha_{k}-\rho)&\text{if }\alpha_{k}+\rho-1<0.\end{cases}

This concludes the induction.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

Let us define the functions
M_{k}(x)
recursively from the formula (
4.6
) and
M_{0}(x)=1
. Our aim is hence to prove that, for all
k\geq 0
,
M_{k}
is uniformly bounded and that, for all
x\in(0,+\infty)
, that
M_{k}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{\partial}^{k})
.

An immediate induction procedure
and (
2.2
) proves that, for all
k\geq 0
, the function
M_{k}
is bounded.

The Green function for diffusions on natural scale
[
9
, Lemma 23.10]
implies that, for any
0<a<b<\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\int_{0}^{T_{a,b}}M_{k-1}(X_{t})\,dt\right)=2\int_{a}^{b}\frac{(x\wedge y-a)(b-x\vee y)}{b-a}M_{k-1}(y)\,m(dy).

Now, letting
a\rightarrow 0
(by monotone convergence) and
b\rightarrow\infty
(by monotone convergence in the l.h.s and by Lebesgue’s Theorem in the right hand side - remember that
M_{k-1}
is bounded), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{\partial}}M_{k-1}(X_{t})\,dt\right)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}(x\wedge y)\,M_{k-1}(y)\,m(dy)

and hence

\displaystyle M_{k}(x)=k\,\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{\partial}}M_{k-1}(X_{t})\,dt\right).

Let us now prove by induction that
M_{k}(x)=\mathbb{E}(\tau_{\partial}^{k})
for all
k\geq 0
. The property is true for
k=0
. Assume that
M_{k-1}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{\partial}^{k-1})
, then

\displaystyle M_{k}(x)=k\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{\partial}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{t}}(\tau_{\partial}^{k-1})\,dt\right)=k\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{\partial}}\left(\tau_{\partial}-t\right)^{k-1}\,dt\right)=\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{\partial}^{k}),

where we used the Markov property.
∎

4.3
Conditions ensuring (B) in relation with strict local martingales

The next result gives a sufficient condition for (B), which is a weaker requirement than the one of Theorem
4.3
. It is a direct consequence of
Theorem
3.1
, the definition (
3.4
) of
\widetilde{m}
and the equivalence (
3.7
).

Theorem 4.6
.

Assume that
X
comes down from infinity and

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{x}\,\sup_{y\leq x}\left(\frac{1}{y}\int_{(0,y)}z^{2}\,m(dz)\right)\,dx<\infty.

(4.8)

Then, for all

t>0
, there exists
A_{t}<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(t<\tau_{\partial})\leq A_{t}\,x,\ \forall x>0.

Note that Fubini’s theorem implies that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1/2}z\,m(dz)\leq\int_{0}^{1}z(1-z)\,m(dz)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{x^{2}}\int_{0}^{x}z^{2}\,m(dz)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{x}\left(\sup_{y\leq x}\frac{1}{y}\int_{0}^{y}z^{2}\,m(dz)\right)dx.

Hence (
4.8
) implies that
\int_{0}^{1}z\,m(dz)<\infty
, i.e.
X
hits 0 in a.s. finite time.

The last theorem is based (via Theorem
3.1
) on abstract but powerful general analytical results on the density
of diffusion processes, which are hard to extend to more general settings. But Condition (B) is simple enough to be checked for other
classes of models using elementary probability tools, as we did in Theorem
4.3
. We give in Subsection
4.5.3
a
simple example of a one-dimensional process with jumps where exponential convergence to the quasi-stationary distribution can be
proved by an easy extension of Theorem
4.3
.

Remark 6
.

As in Remark
1
, in the case of a (weak) solution to the SDE
dX_{t}=\sigma(X_{t})dB_{t}
, the
condition (
4.8
) covers almost all the practical situations where
\int_{0}^{\infty}y\sigma^{-2}(y)\,dy<\infty
. For
example, it is true if
X
comes down from infinity and

\displaystyle\sigma(x)\geq Cx\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{x}\cdots\log_{k-1}\frac{1}{x}\left(\log_{k}\frac{1}{x}\right)^{1+\epsilon}}

for all
x
in a neighborhood of
0
and for some
\epsilon,C>0
and
k\geq 1
.

4.4
Consequences of the expression of
\alpha
as a function of
m

In Theorem
4.1
, we proved that, under Assmuption (B),
\alpha
is absolutely continuous with respect to
m
and that

\displaystyle\frac{d\alpha}{dm}(x)=2\lambda_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}(x\wedge y)\alpha(dy),\ \forall x\in(0,+\infty).

(4.9)

Our goal here is to provide a converse property, i.e. to give a necessary and sufficient condition on a given positive measure

\alpha
, such that it is the unique quasi-stationary distribution of a diffusion process on natural scale on
[0,+\infty)
with
exponential convergence of the conditional laws.

We will say (with a slight abuse of notation) that a positive measure
m
on
(0,\infty)
satisfies (B) if: it is locally finite,
gives positive mass on each open subset of
(0,\infty)
and the diffusion process on
[0,\infty)
on natural scale with speed measure

m
and stopped at 0 satisfies Condition (B).

Theorem 4.7
.

Fix
\lambda_{0}>0
and a probability measure
\alpha
on
(0,+\infty)
such that the measure
(\frac{1}{x}\vee 1)\alpha(dx)

satisfies (B). Then, there exists a unique (in law) diffusion process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
[0,+\infty)
on natural scale a.s. absorbed at
0
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t}\in\cdot\mid t<\tau_{\partial})-\alpha\right\|_{TV}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t},\quad\forall x\in(0,+\infty),

for some positive constants
C,\gamma
and such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}(t<\tau_{\partial})=e^{-\lambda_{0}t},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

In addition, the speed measure of the process
X
is given by

\displaystyle m(dx)=\frac{\alpha(dx)}{2\lambda_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}(x\wedge y)\alpha(dy)},\quad\forall x\in(0,\infty).

Conversely, if a speed measure
m
satisfies (B), then the corresponding quasi-stationary distribution
\alpha
is such that

(\frac{1}{x}\vee 1)\alpha(dx)
satisfies (B).

Because of the relation (
4.9
) and the equivalence between (B) and uniform exponential convergence to a quasi-stationary distribution, this result follows from the next lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\alpha
be a probability measure on
(0,+\infty)
. Define the measure
m
on
(0,+\infty)
as

\displaystyle m(dx)=\frac{\alpha(dx)}{\int_{0}^{\infty}(x\wedge y)\alpha(dy)},\ \forall x\in(0,\infty).

(4.10)

Then the measure
(\frac{1}{x}\vee 1)\alpha(dx)
satisfies (B) if and only if the measure
m
satisfies (B).

Proof.

Note that, if
m_{1},m_{2}
are two locally finite measures on
(0,+\infty)
giving positive mass to each open subset of
(0,\infty)
,
such that
m_{1}\leq Cm_{2}
for some positive constant
C>0
and such that
m_{2}
satisfies (B), then
m_{1}
also satisfies (B). This is a direct consequence of the
construction of diffusion processes given in Section
2
and of (
2.1
).

Assume first that
(\frac{1}{x}\vee 1)\alpha(dx)
satisfies (B). Then, using Remark
2
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(1\vee y)\,\alpha(dy)<\infty.

(4.11)

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(x\wedge y)\alpha(dy)\leq\left(x\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha(dy)\right)\wedge\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}y\,\alpha(dy)\right)<\infty.

(4.12)

Hence
m\neq 0
, is locally finite and gives positive mass to each open subset of
(0,\infty)
.
Since

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(x\wedge y)\alpha(dy)\geq\begin{cases}x\int_{1}^{\infty}\alpha(dy),&\text{ if }x\leq 1,\\
\int_{1}^{\infty}\alpha(dy),&\text{ if }x>1,\end{cases}

we have

\frac{dm}{d\alpha}(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}(x\wedge y)\alpha(dy)\right)^{-1}\leq\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\alpha(dy)\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\vee 1\right).

Therefore, if
(\frac{1}{x}\vee 1)\alpha(dx)
satisfies (B),
m
satisfies (B).

Conversely, if
m
satisfies (B), Theorem
4.1
implies that
\alpha
is proportional to the unique quasi-stationary
distribution of
X
with speed measure
m
and satisfies

\int_{0}^{\infty}y\,\alpha(dy)<\infty.

Since
\alpha
is a finite measure, we deduce from (
4.12
) that

\int_{0}^{\infty}(x\wedge y)\alpha(dy)\leq C(x\wedge 1)

for some constant
C<\infty
. Hence

\frac{dm}{d\alpha}(x)\geq C^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\vee 1\right),

i.e.
(\frac{1}{x}\vee 1)\alpha(dx)
satisfies (B).
∎

4.5
Examples

4.5.1
On general diffusions

Let us first recall that our results also cover the case of general (i.e. not necessarily on natural scale) diffusion processes

(Y_{t},t\geq 0)
on
[a,b)
such that
-\infty<a<b\leq+\infty
with speed measure
m_{Y}
, which hits
a
in a.s. finite time
\tau_{\partial}
,
absorbed at
a
and such that
b
is a natural or entrance boundary. Under these assumptions, there exists a continuous and strictly
increasing scale function
s:[a,b)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
of the process
Y
such that
s(a)=0
and
s(b-)=+\infty
. Our results then
apply to the process
X_{t}=s(Y_{t})
which is a diffusion on natural scale on
[0,\infty)
, whose speed measure is given by

\displaystyle m_{X}(dx)=s*m_{Y}(dx),

(4.13)

where
s*m_{Y}
is the pushforward measure of
m_{Y}
through the function
s
(this follows for instance
from
[
21
, Thm. VII.3.6]
).

Hence
X
satisfies (B) if and only if
Y
satisfies the following Condition (B’).

Condition (B’)
Assume that
\int_{c}^{b}s(y)\,m_{Y}(dy)<\infty
for some
c\in(a,b)
and there exist two
constants
t_{1},A>0
such that

\mathbb{P}(t_{1}<\tau_{\partial}\mid Y_{0}=y)\leq As(y),\quad\forall y>a.

Under this assumption, the process
Y
satisfies (
4.2
) for a unique quasi-stationary distribution
\alpha_{Y}
on
(a,b)
and
\alpha_{Y}
satisfies

\int_{a}^{b}s(y)\alpha_{Y}(dy)<\infty.

Moreover

\frac{d\alpha_{Y}}{dm_{Y}}(y)=2\lambda_{0}\int_{a}^{b}(s(y)\wedge s(z))\alpha_{Y}(dz),

where
\lambda_{0}
is such that
\mathbb{P}(t<\tau_{\partial}\mid Y_{0}\sim\alpha_{Y})=e^{-\lambda_{0}t}
. Theorem
4.6
entails the
following result.

Proposition 4.9
.

With the previous notation, if
\int_{a}^{b}s(y)\,m_{Y}(dy)<\infty
and

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{x}\left(\sup_{y\in(a,s^{-1}(x)]}\frac{1}{s(y)}\int_{a}^{y}s(z)^{2}\,m_{Y}(dz)\right)dx<\infty,

(4.14)

then Condition (B’) is satisfied.

Let us also mention that our methods can be easily extended to diffusion processes on a bounded interval
[a,b]
where both boundary
points are either exit or regular (reflecting or absorbing). In the case where
b
is exit or regular absorbing (i.e.
\tau_{\partial}
is
the first hitting time of
\{a,b\}
), we obtain two conditions of the form (
4.14
) in the neighborhood of
a
and
b
.

To illustrate the generality of the processes that are covered by our criteria, we give two simple examples where the speed measure

m
is singular with respect to Lebesgue’s measure
\Lambda
. The case of speed measures absolutely continuous w.r.t. Lebesgue’s
measure (i.e. of SDEs) will be discussed in the next subsection.

Example 1

We recall that a diffusion process on
\mathbb{R}
with speed measure
\Lambda+\delta_{0}
is called a
sticky Brownian
motion

[
8
,
1
]
. We consider a diffusion process on
[0,\infty)
which comes down from infinity (
\int_{1}^{\infty}y\,m(dy)<\infty
) and which is a sticky Brownian motion on
[0,1]
, “sticked” at the points
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots
, where
(a_{i})_{i\geq 1}

is decreasing, converges to 0 and
a_{1}<1
, i.e.

\mathchoice{{m\,\smash{\vrule height=3.44444pt,depth=3.02223pt}}_{\,(0,1)}}{{m\,\smash{\vrule height=3.44444pt,depth=3.02223pt}}_{\,(0,1)}}{{m\,\smash{\vrule height=2.41112pt,depth=2.20056pt}}_{\,(0,1)}}{{m\,\smash{\vrule height=1.72221pt,depth=2.78612pt}}_{\,(0,1)}}=\mathchoice{{\Lambda\,\smash{\vrule height=5.46666pt,depth=3.02223pt}}_{\,(0,1)}}{{\Lambda\,\smash{\vrule height=5.46666pt,depth=3.02223pt}}_{\,(0,1)}}{{\Lambda\,\smash{\vrule height=3.82668pt,depth=2.20056pt}}_{\,(0,1)}}{{\Lambda\,\smash{\vrule height=2.71904pt,depth=2.78612pt}}_{\,(0,1)}}+\sum_{i\geq 1}\delta_{a_{i}}.

Assuming that there exist constants
C,\rho>0
such that for all

,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq j}a_{i}\leq Ca^{\rho}_{j},

(4.15)

then for all
x\in(0,1)
, defining
i_{0}:=\inf\{j\geq 1:a_{j}<x\}
,

\int_{(0,x)}y\,m(dy)=\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\sum_{i\geq i_{0}}a_{i}\leq\frac{x^{2}}{2}+Ca^{\rho}_{i_{0}}\leq\frac{x^{2}}{2}+Cx^{\rho},

and we can apply Theorem
4.3
.

For example, the choice
a_{i}=i^{-\frac{1}{1-\rho}}
, for all

).

Example 2

Let
X
be a sticky Brownian motion stopped at
-1
and
1
. This means that
X
is a diffusion on natural
scale with speed measure
m(dx)=\Lambda(dx)+\delta_{0}(dx)
on
(-1,1)
, absorbed at
-1
and
1
. As mentionned above, it is
straightforward to adapt our results to processes on
[-1,1]
. For the process of this example, the corresponding Assumption (B) is
clearly fulfilled since both boundaries
-1
and
1
are regular for
X
(see Corollary
4.4
). Then the unique
quasi-stationary distribution
\alpha
of
X
is

\displaystyle\alpha(dx)=\frac{\gamma^{*}}{2}\sin\left(\gamma^{*}(1+x)\wedge(1-x)\right)m(dx),

where
\gamma^{*}
is the unique solution in
(0,\pi]
of
\text{cotan }\gamma=\gamma/2
.

Indeed, it satisfies the following adaptation of formula (
4.3
)

\displaystyle\frac{d\alpha}{dm}(x)=\lambda_{0}\int_{-1}^{1}(x\wedge y+1)(1-x\vee y)\,\alpha(dy),\ \forall x\in(-1,1).

(4.16)

In particular, the measure
\alpha
is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue’s measure
\Lambda
on
(-1,1)\setminus\{0\}
and satisfies

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d\alpha}{d\Lambda}\right)^{\prime\prime}(x)=-2\lambda_{0}\frac{d\alpha}{d\Lambda}(x),\ \forall x\neq 0.

Using the symmetry of the problem and the
0
boundary conditions, we deduce that there exist constants
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\alpha(dx)=a\delta_{0}(dx)+b\sin\left(\gamma(1+x)\wedge(1-x)\right)\Lambda(dx),

where
\gamma=\sqrt{2\lambda_{0}}
. Note that the fact that
\alpha
is a positive measure implies that
\gamma\in(0,\pi]
. In
addition, equality (
4.16
) at
x=0
entails

\displaystyle a=\lambda_{0}\left(2b\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)\sin(\gamma(1-y))dy+a\right)

and hence

\displaystyle(1-\gamma^{2}/2)a=b\left(\sin\gamma-\gamma\cos\gamma\right).

(4.17)

In addition,
d\alpha/dm(x)
is continuous at
0
by Remark
4
, hence

\displaystyle a=b\sin\gamma.

(4.18)

Finally,
\alpha
is a probability measure, so that

\displaystyle a+\frac{2b}{\gamma}(1-\cos\gamma)=1.

(4.19)

Now, dividing (
4.17
) by (
4.18
), we obtain
\gamma=\gamma^{*}
. Equality (
4.19
) becomes

\displaystyle 1=a-b\sin\gamma+\frac{2b}{\gamma}.

By (
4.18
), we deduce that
b=\gamma/2
and finally that
a=\frac{\gamma\sin\gamma}{2}
.

4.5.2
On processes solutions of stochastic differential equations

Let
Y
be the solution of the general SDE on
[a,b)
with
a<b\leq+\infty

dY_{t}=\sigma(Y_{t})dB_{t}+\mu(Y_{t})dt,

(4.20)

absorbed at
a
, where
\mu/\sigma^{2}\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}((a,b))
(which ensures the existence of a weak
solution
[
9
, Ch. 23]
, possibly exposive if
b=+\infty
, or which can possibly hit
b
if
b<\infty
). In this case,
a scale function of
Y
is given by

s(x)=\int_{c}^{x}\exp\left(-\int_{c}^{y}2\mu(z)\sigma^{-2}(z)dz\right)dy,\ \forall x\in(a,b)

(4.21)

for any
c\in(a,b)
, and the speed measure by
m_{Y}(dy)=2/(s^{\prime}(y)\sigma^{2}(y))\Lambda(dy)
. In the case where
s(a+)>-\infty
, one can
take
c=a
in Formula (
4.21
), so that
s(a)=0
. If in addition
s(b-)=\infty
(which implies in particular
that (
4.20
) is non-explosive if
b=\infty
or cannot hit
b
in finite time if
b<\infty
), the
formula (
4.21
) can be used to check the condition (
4.14
) in Proposition
4.9
.

In particular, our criteria apply to cases of speed measures with discontinuous densities with respect to Lebesgue’s measure, i.e. to cases of discontinuous
\sigma
. Such diffusions arise as rescaled solutions of SDEs driven by their local times, including skew
Brownian motions (see for instance
[
12
]
). Hence our criteria also extend to one-dimensional SDEs driven by their local
time in the sense of
[
12
]
.

Note that a major part of the existing literature
[
2
,
13
,
10
]
on the subject only considers solutions to SDEs with absolutely
continuous coefficient
\sigma
. The reason behind this is that all the previously cited works are based on Sturm-Liouville theory, which is particularly well suited for the study of diffusion processes
(Y_{t},t\geq 0)
on
[0,\infty)
solution to a SDE of the form

dY_{t}=dB_{t}-q(Y_{t})dt.

After a change of scale, this class of processes can only provide solutions to SDEs of the form (
4.20
) with absolutely
continuous
\sigma
.

4.5.3
A simple example with jumps

The following simple example shows how our method can be easily extended to processes with jumps.

We consider a diffusion process
(X_{t},t\geq 0)
on
[0,\infty)
with speed measure
m
satisfying Assumption (B).
Let us denote by
\mathcal{L}
the infinitesimal generator of
X
. We consider the Markov process
(\widetilde{X}_{t},t\geq 0)
with
infinitesimal generator

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}f(x)=\mathcal{L}f(x)+(f(x+1)-f(x))\mathbbm{1}_{x\geq 1},

for all
f
in the domain of
\mathcal{L}
. In other words, we consider a càdlàg process following a diffusion process with speed
measure
m
between jump times, which occur at the jump times of an independent Poisson process
(N_{t},t\geq 0)
of rate
1
, with
jump size
+1
if the process is above 1, and
0
otherwise. We denote by
\widetilde{\tau}_{\partial}
its first hitting time of
0
.

The proof of the following proposition is postponed to Subsection
5.4
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Under the previous assumptions, there exist a unique probability measure
\alpha
on
(0,\infty)
and two constants
C,\gamma>0

such that, for all initial distribution
\mu
on
(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(\widetilde{X}_{t}\in\cdot\mid t<\widetilde{\tau}_{\partial})-\alpha(\cdot)\right\|_{TV}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t},\ \forall t\geq 0.

(4.22)

In particular, the probability measure

is the unique quasi-stationary distribution of the process.

5.1
Proof of the equivalence between (A) and (B)

Note that

We actually prove below that (A1) and (B) are equivalent and that these properties imply (A2). In other words, for one-dimensional

We first prove that (A1) implies (B). If (A1) holds true, there exist

such that

\inf_{x>0}\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t_{1}}\in[a,b]\mid t_{1}<\tau_{\partial})=\underline{c}>0.

Now, for all

\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{b}<t_{1})}{\mathbb{P}_{b}(t_{1}<\tau_{\partial})}\geq\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t_{1}}\in[a,b])}{\mathbb{P}_{b}(t_{1}<\tau_{\partial})}\geq\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t_{1}}\in[a,b]\mid t_{1}<\tau_{\partial}).

Since we proved above that
\mathbb{P}_{b}(t_{1}<\tau_{\partial})>0
, we deduce that
\inf_{x>b}\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{b}<t_{1})>0
, i.e. that

is an entrance

X
. Equation (

x<a

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(t_{1}<\tau_{\partial})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t_{1}}\in[a,b])}{\underline{c}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{x}(X_{t_{1}\wedge T_{a}})}{a\underline{c}}=\frac{x}{a\underline{c}}.

Hence (i) is proved.

The difficult part of the proof is the implication (B)
\Rightarrow
(A).

Step 1: the conditioned process escapes a neighborhood of 0 in finite time.

The goal of this step is to prove that there exists
\varepsilon,c>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t_{1}}\geq\varepsilon\mid t_{1}<\tau_{\partial})\geq c,\quad\forall x>0.

To prove this, we first observe that, since
X
is a local martingale, for all
x\in(0,1)
,

x=\mathbb{E}_{x}(X_{t_{1}\wedge T_{1}})=\mathbb{P}_{x}(t_{1}<\tau_{\partial})\mathbb{E}_{x}(X_{t_{1}\wedge T_{1}}\mid t_{1}<\tau_{\partial})+\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{1}<\tau_{\partial}\leq t_{1}).

By the Markov property,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{1}<\tau_{\partial}\leq t_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{T_{1}<\tau_{\partial}\wedge t_{1}}\mathbb{P}_{1}(\tau_{\partial}\leq t_{1})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{1}<\tau_{\partial})\mathbb{P}_{1}(\tau_{\partial}\leq t_{1})

\displaystyle=x\mathbb{P}_{1}(\tau_{\partial}\leq t_{1}).

Hence (
4.1

\mathbb{E}_{x}(1-X_{t_{1}\wedge T_{1}}\mid t_{1}<\tau_{\partial})\leq 1-\frac{1}{A^{\prime}},

where

\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t_{1}\wedge T_{1}}\leq\frac{1}{2A^{\prime}-1}\mid t_{1}<\tau_{\partial})\leq\frac{1-1/A^{\prime}}{1-1/(2A^{\prime}-1)}=1-\frac{1}{2A^{\prime}}.

Since
\mathbb{P}_{1/(2A^{\prime}-1)}(t_{1}<\tau_{\partial})>0

\varepsilon\in(0,1/(2A^{\prime}-1))

\mathbb{P}_{1/(2A^{\prime}-1)}(t_{1}<T_{\varepsilon})>0.

(5.5)

Hence, for all
x\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t_{1}}\geq\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{1/(2A^{\prime}-1)}<t_{1})\mathbb{P}_{1/(2A^{\prime}-1)}(T_{\varepsilon}>t_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t_{1}\wedge T_{1}}\geq 1/(2A^{\prime}-1))\mathbb{P}_{1/(2A^{\prime}-1)}(T_{\varepsilon}>t_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}(t_{1}<\tau_{\partial})}{2A^{\prime}}\mathbb{P}_{1/(2A^{\prime}-1)}(T_{\varepsilon}>t_{1})

\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t_{1}}>\varepsilon\mid t_{1}<\tau_{\partial})\geq\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t_{1}}>\varepsilon)\geq\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{\varepsilon}>t_{1})\geq\mathbb{P}_{1/(2A^{\prime}-1)}(T_{\varepsilon}>t_{1})>0.

Hence (

Step 2: Construction of coupling measures for the unconditioned process.

Our goal is to prove that there exist two constants

\inf_{y>0}\mathbb{P}_{y}(\tau_{\partial}<t_{2})=c^{\prime}_{1}>0.

Therefore, (

Using successively the Markov property, Step 2 and Step 1, we have for all

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t_{1}+t_{2}}\in\cdot\mid t_{1}<\tau_{\partial})

The general idea of the proof is close to the case of birth and death processes in

The following reversibility result for diffusions on natural scale is more or less classical but we need a version with precise
bounds on the test functions in the case of a diffusion coming down from infinity and hitting 0 a.s. in finite time. The proof is
given at the end of the subsection for sake of completeness.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be a diffusion on
[0,\infty)
in natural scale with speed measure
m
satisfying (
2.2
). Then it is
reversible with respect to
m
in the sense that, for all bounded non-negative measurable functions
f
on
(0,+\infty)
and all
nonnegative measurable function
g
on
(0,+\infty)
such that
g(x)\leq Cx
for some
C>0
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)P_{t}g(x)m(dx)=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(x)P_{t}f(x)m(dx),\quad\forall t\geq 0,

(5.13)

where both sides are finite.

Applying this lemma for
g=\eta
, we obtain that, for all bounded measurable non-negative
f
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\eta(x)m(dx)=e^{\lambda_{0}t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(x)P_{t}f(x)m(dx)

Now, it follows from (
4.2
) and (
1.2
) that

e^{\lambda_{0}t}P_{t}f(x)=e^{\lambda_{0}t}\mathbb{P}_{x}(t<\tau_{\partial})\mathbb{E}_{x}(f(X_{t})\mid t<\tau_{\partial})\rightarrow\eta(x)\alpha(f)

when

. Since

, Lebesgue’s theorem entails that

\alpha(f)\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta^{2}(x)m(dx)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\eta(x)m(dx)

\alpha\propto\eta\,dm
. Note also that
f=\eta
gives

.

It only remains to prove that
\int y\,\alpha(dy)<\infty
. Since
\eta
is bounded and

\eta(x)\propto\int_{0}^{\infty}(x\wedge y)\,\alpha(dy),

this follows from the monotone convergence theorem.

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

We start by proving that both sides of (

) are finite. This is obvious for the r.h.s. because
of (
2.2
). For the l.h.s., since the positive local martingale
(X_{t},t\geq 0)

By Lebesgue’s theorem, it is enough to prove (

We use the characterization of the infinitesimal generator of diffusion processes of

\mathcal{D}

f
bounded continuous on

f^{+}

So let

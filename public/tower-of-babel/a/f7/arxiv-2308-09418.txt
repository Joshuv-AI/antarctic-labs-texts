Geometric Monodromies, Mixed Hodge Numbers of Motivic Milnor Fibers and Newton Polyhedra 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 14F05, 14M25, 32C38, 32S35, 32S40
Kiyoshi TAKEUCHI

Abstract
We introduce the theory of local and global monodromies of polynomials in cohomology groups in various geometric situations, focusing on its relations with toric geometry and motivic Milnor fibers, and moreover in the modern languages of nearby and vanishing cycle functors. Equivariant mixed Hodge numbers of motivic Milnor fibers will be described in terms of Newton polyhedra of polynomials.

Introduction

Since Milnor’s discovery of Milnor fibers of
complex hypersufaces in
[

mathematicians in different fields, such as

Milnor fibers and nearby cycle sheaves

In this section, we recall the classical definitions of
Milnor fibers and Milnor monodromies and explain their relations with
the nearby and vanishing cycle functors introduced by
Deligne
[
15
]
.
In this note, we essentially follow the terminology of
[
20
]
,
[
45
]
,

[
50
]
,
[
51
]
and
[
101
]
.
For example, for a topological space
X
we denote by
{\bf D}^{b}(X)
the derived category whose
objects are bounded complexes of sheaves of
{\mathbb{C}}_{X}
-modules on
X
. For a complex
analytic space
X
, let
{\bf D}_{c}^{b}(X)
be the full subcategory of
{\bf D}^{b}(X)
consisting
of constructible complexes of sheaves. In what follows, let

X
be a complex analytic space and
f\colon X\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}

a non-constant holomorphic function on it. The problem being local,
we may assume that
X
is a complex analytic subset of
a complex manifold
M
and for a non-constant holomorphic
function
\widetilde{f}\colon M\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
on it
we have
\widetilde{f}\big|_{X}\equiv f
. Set

X_{0}:=f^{-1}(0)\subsetneqq X
. First of all, the following fundamental result was proved by
Milnor
[
75
]
and Lê
[
61
]
. See also Massey

[
67
, Proposition 1.3]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
be a complex analytic subset
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
containing the origin
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
and

f\colon X\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
a non-constant holomorphic
function on it satisfying the condition
f(0)=0
.
Then for
0<\eta\ll\varepsilon\ll 1
the
restriction

X\cap B(0;\varepsilon)\cap f^{-1}(B(0;\eta)^{*})\longrightarrow B(0;\eta)^{*}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{*}

(2.1)

of
f
is a fiber bundle, where we set
B(0;\eta)^{*}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\mid 0<|z|<\eta\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
and
B(0;\varepsilon):=\{x=({x}_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}\mid||x||=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}|x_{i}|^{2}}<\varepsilon\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
.

Definition 2.2
.

In the situation of Theorem
2.1
,
for a point
z\in B(0;\eta)^{*}
we call

F_{0}:=X\cap B(0;\varepsilon)\cap f^{-1}(z)

(2.2)

the Milnor-Lê fiber (or simply the Milnor fiber)
of
f
at the origin

0\in X_{0}=f^{-1}(0)\subset X
.

In the situation of Theorem
2.1
,
for any
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
the
j
-th higher direct image
sheaf of the constant sheaf on

X\cap B(0;\varepsilon)\cap f^{-1}(B(0;\eta)^{*})
by
f

is a local system on the punctured disk

B(0;\eta)^{*}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. Then
by turning around the origin
0\in{\mathbb{C}}
in

B(0;\eta)^{*}
we obtain
automorphisms of their stalks, i.e.

{\mathbb{C}}
-linear isomorphisms

\Phi_{j,0}\colon H^{j}(F_{0};{\mathbb{C}})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}H^{j}(F_{0};{\mathbb{C}})\qquad(j\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

(2.3)

We call them the Milnor-Lê monodromies (or simply
the Milnor monodromies) of
f
at the origin

0\in X_{0}=f^{-1}(0)\subset X
. It is clear
that the Milnor fiber and the Milnor monodromies
are defined similarly
also at any point
x\in X_{0}=f^{-1}(0)\subset X
of

X_{0}
. We denote them
F_{x}
and

\Phi_{j,x}
(
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
) respectively. If

X={\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and the complex hypersurface
X_{0}=f^{-1}(0)\subset X

of it has an isolated singular point at the
origin
0\in X_{0}=f^{-1}(0)\subset X
, we have
the following beautiful theorem due to Milnor
[
75
]

Theorem 2.3
.

(Milnor
[
75
]
)
Let

f\colon X={\mathbb{C}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}\ (n\geq 2)

be a non-constant holomorphic function on

X={\mathbb{C}}^{n}
satisfying the condition
f(0)=0
and
assume that the complex hypersurface
X_{0}=f^{-1}(0)\subset X

has an isolated singular point at the
origin
0\in X_{0}=f^{-1}(0)\subset X
.
Then the Milnor fiber
F_{0}
of
f
at the origin
is homotopy equivalent to the bouquet of some

(n-1)
-dimensional spheres
S^{n-1}
:

F_{0}\sim S^{n-1}\vee\cdots\vee S^{n-1}.

(2.4)

In particular, for its cohomology groups

H^{j}(F_{0};{\mathbb{C}})
(
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
) we have

H^{j}(F_{0};{\mathbb{C}})\simeq\begin{cases}\ {\mathbb{C}}&(j=0)\\
&\\
\ {\mathbb{C}}^{\mu}&(j=n-1)\\
&\\
\ 0&(j\neq 0,n-1),\end{cases}

(2.5)

where
\mu>0
is the number of spheres
S^{n-1}
in
the above bouquet decomposition.

We call the number
\mu>0
in Theorem
2.3

the Milnor number of
f
(or of
X_{0}=f^{-1}(0)
)
at the origin
0\in X_{0}=f^{-1}(0)\subset X
.
Note that the Milnor fiber
F_{0}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
in
Theorem
2.3
is an

(n-1)
-dimensional complex manifold i.e. a

(2n-2)
-dimensional real analytic manifold.
Theorem
2.3
asserts that it
has the homotopy type of the real

(n-1)
-dimensional topological space

S^{n-1}\vee\cdots\vee S^{n-1}
.
If
X_{0}=f^{-1}(0)\subset X={\mathbb{C}}^{n}
for
f:X={\mathbb{C}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}

does not have an isolated singular point at the
origin
0\in X_{0}=f^{-1}(0)

F_{0}
.
Nevertheless, making use of Morse theory

[
60
]
and Massey
[
66
]
obtained
the handle decompositions of
F_{0}
. Namely
we can know at least the homotopy type of it.

3
Theorems of Kouchnirenko, Varchenko and Oka

In this section, we introduce the beautiful theorems of
Kouchnirenko, Varchenko and Oka which express
the Milnor numbers and the monodromy
zeta functions of holomorphic functions in terms of their
Newton polyhedra. Here, for simplicity, we first consider
non-constant polynomials

f(x)\in{\mathbb{C}}[x]={\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

such that
f(0)=0
. We set
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}:=({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})^{n}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and

{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}:=({\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})^{n}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
We shall freely use standard notions in toric geomety,
for which we refer to Fulton
[
32
]
and
Oda
[
83
]
. First of all, we recall
the simplest case of the
Bernstein-Khovanskii-Kouchnirenko
theorem
[
54
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
g(x)=\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}c_{v}x^{v}\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm}]
(
c_{v}\in{\mathbb{C}}
) be a
Laurent polynomial on the algebraic torus

T=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
.

(i)

We call the convex hull of

{\rm supp}(g):=\{v\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\ |\ c_{v}\neq 0\}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
the
Newton polytope of
g
and denote it by
NP(g)
.

(ii)

For a face
\gamma\prec NP(g)
of
NP(g)
,
we define the
\gamma
-part

g^{\gamma}
of
g
by

g^{\gamma}(x):=\sum_{v\in\gamma\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}c_{v}x^{v}
.

Definition 3.2
.

(see
[
85
]
)
Let
g(x)\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm}]
be a
Laurent polynomial on

T=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
and set
\Delta:=NP(g)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Then we say that
g
is
non-degenerate
if for any face
\gamma\prec\Delta
of

\Delta
the
1

dg^{\gamma}
does not vanish
on the complex hypersurface

\{x\in T=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}\ |\ g^{\gamma}(x)=0\}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

(Bernstein-Khovanskii-Kouchnirenko
theorem
[
54
]
, see also
Oka
[
85
, Chapter IV, Theorem (3.1)]
)

Let
g(x)\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm}]
be a
Laurent polynomial on

T=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
and set
\Delta:=NP(g)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Assume that
g
is
non-degenerate. Then for the complex hypersurface

Z^{*}=\{x\in T=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}\ |\ g(x)=0\}
of

we have

\chi(Z^{*})=(-1)^{n-1}{\rm Vol}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(\Delta),

(3.1)

where
{\rm Vol}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(\Delta)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}

is the normalized

-dimensional volume
(i.e. the
n!
times the usual volume) of

\Delta
with respect to the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let

(

{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
such that

.

(i)

4
Singularities and
monodromies at infinity of polynomial maps

In this section, we introduce some basic definitions
and results on
singularities and monodromies at infinity of polynomial maps

f\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
. In particular, here we focus on
Némethi-Zaharia’s theorem in
[
76
]
and
Libgober-Sperber’s one in
[
64
]
.
After the two fundamental papers Broughton

[
6
]
and Siersma-Tibăr
[
102
]
, many authors
studied the global behavior of polynomial maps

f\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
.
First of all, for a
polynomial map
f\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
,
it is well-known that there
exists a finite subset
B\subset{\mathbb{C}}
such that the restriction

{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus f^{-1}(B)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}\setminus B

(4.1)

of
f
is a
C^{\infty}
locally trivial fibration.
We denote by
B_{f}
the smallest
subset
B\subset{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying this
condition. Let
{\rm Sing}f\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

be the set of the critical points of

f\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
. Then by
the definition of
B_{f}
, obviously we have

f({\rm Sing}f)\subset B_{f}
.
We call the elements of
B_{f}
the
bifurcation values of
f
.
The determination of the bifurcation set

B_{f}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is a fundamental problem and has been
studied by a lot of mathematicians such as
[
6
]
,

[
42
]
,
[
46
]
,
[
47
]
,

[
76
]
,
[
77
]
,
[
86
]
,
[
102
]
,
[
107
]
,

[
109
]
,
[
113
]
,
[
119
]
and from
various points of view.
For the generalizations of some of
these results to more general polynomial maps

f\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{k}

for
1\leq k\leq n
,
see also
[
12
]
,
[
43
]
,
[
58
]
,
[
80
]

and
[
88
]
.
The essential difficulty in determining
B_{f}\subset{\mathbb{C}}

lies in the fact that
f\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}

is not proper and hence
we may have
f({\rm Sing}f)\not=B_{f}
i.e.

f
has some extra singularities at infinity.

Definition 4.1
.

(Broughton
[
6
]
)
Let
\partial f\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

be the map defined by
\partial f(x)=(\partial_{1}f(x),\ldots,\partial_{n}f(x))

(
x\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
).
Then we say that
f
is tame at infinity if the restriction
(\partial f)^{-1}(B(0;\varepsilon))\longrightarrow B(0;\varepsilon)
of
\partial f
to a sufficiently
small ball
B(0;\varepsilon)\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
centered at the origin
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is proper.

In
[
6
]
Broughton showed that if

f\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is tame at infinity
then we have
f({\rm Sing}f)=B_{f}
.
In the tame case, the following result is also fundamental.

Theorem 4.2
.

(Broughton
[
6
]
)

Assume that
f
is tame at infinity.
Then the generic fiber
f^{-1}(c)
(
c\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus B_{f}
)
has the homotopy type of the bouquet of some
(n-1)
-spheres. In particular, we have

H^{j}(f^{-1}(c);{\mathbb{C}})=0\quad(j\neq 0,n-1).

(4.2)

By Siersma and Tibăr
[
102
]

this concentration result
was later extended to polynomial maps

f\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}

with isolated singularities with respect
to some fiber-compactifying extensions of
f
. From now on,
we shall introduce the theorem of
Némethi and Zaharia
[
76
]
which
gives an upper bound for
B_{f}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
described in terms of
a Newton polyhedron of
f
.

(Kouchnirenko
[
56
]

\Gamma_{\infty}(f)

For a face
\gamma\prec\Gamma_{\infty}(f)
of
\Gamma_{\infty}(f)

we say that

is at infinity if
0\notin\gamma
.

(Kouchnirenko

First, let

In this section, assuming

10
Milnor fibers and
monodromies of meromorphic functions

In
[
39
]
Gusein-Zade, Luengo and Melle-Hernández
generalized Milnor’s fibration theorem
to meromorphic functions
and defined their Milnor fibers. Moreover they obtained
a formula for their monodromy zeta functions.
Since then many mathematicians studied Milnor fibers of
meromorphic functions (see e.g.
[
4
]
,

[
5
]
,
[
35
]
,
[
41
]
,

[
62
]
,
[
79

,
[
81

,

[
82
]
,
[
87

,

[
92
]
,
[
103
]
,

[
114
]
). However, in contrast to
Milnor fibers of holomorphic functions,
the geometric structures of those
of meromorphic functions are not fully understood yet.
For example, as we saw in Theorem
2.3
,
if a holomorphic function
has an isolated singular point then the Milnor
fiber at it has the homotopy type of a bouquet of
some spheres. This implies that its reduced
cohomology groups are concentrated in the
middle dimension. Unfortunately, so far
we do not know such a nice structure theorem for
Milnor fibers of meromorphic functions.
For this reason, we cannot know any property
of each Milnor monodromy operator of
a meromorphic function even if we
have the formula of
[
39
]
for its monodromy zeta function.
Recently in
[
82
]
,
by developing a theory of
nearby and vanishing cycle functors for
meromorphic functions, Nguyen and the author
overcame this problem partially. In this section,
we introduce some of the above-mentioned results for Milnor fibers
and monodromies of meromorphic functions and explain
some related problems.

Let
X
be a complex manifold and
P(x),Q(x)

holomorphic functions on it. Assume that

is not identically zero on each connected component
of

on

Assume that the rational function

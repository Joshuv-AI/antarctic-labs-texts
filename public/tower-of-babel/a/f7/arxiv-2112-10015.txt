Lagrangian Cobordisms between Enriched Knot Diagrams
Ipsita Datta

Abstract
In this paper, we present new obstructions to the existence of Lagrangian cobordisms in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} that depend only on the enriched knot diagrams of the boundary knots or links, using holomorphic curve techniques. We define enriched knot diagrams for generic smooth links. The existence of Lagrangian cobordisms gives a well-defined transitive relation on equivalence classes of enriched knot diagrams that is a strict partial order when restricted to exact enriched knot diagrams To establish obstructions we study 1 1 -dimensional moduli spaces of holomorphic disks with corners that have boundary on Lagrangian tangles - an appropriate immersed Lagrangian closely related to embedded Lagrangian cobordisms. We adapt existing techniques to prove compactness and transversality, and compute dimensions of these moduli spaces. We produce obstructions as a consequence of characterizing all boundary points of such moduli spaces. We use these obstructions to recover and extend results about “growing” and “shrinking” Lagrangian slices. We hope that this investigation will open up new directions in studying Lagrangian surfaces in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} .

Introduction

Lagrangians are interesting and important objects in symplectic geometry. It is known that they display both flexible and rigid properties.
A generic Lagrangian surface in

Biran and Cornea show that monotone Lagrangians cobordisms in

Setup

Remark 1
.

In some cases, we can conclude that a Lagrangian cobordism is relatively exact purely from topological constraints. Suppose

to

such that the writhes (with respect to the blackboard framing in

\displaystyle 0=\chi(L)=2-2g-b=-2g,

A Lagrangian tangle (Definition

2.2

In this section we describe how we can add small Lagrangian collars to the ends of a given Lagrangian to get desired boundary conditions. We first restate some lemmas from

Lemma 2.3

Suppose we have a Lagrangian cobordism

into

Let

\omega_{g}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=g^{*}(dx_{1}\wedge dy_{1})
on

.

The height of points on
\partial_{+}L
or the value of the

-coordinate gives us a Hamiltonian

\partial_{+}L

to a Hamiltonian

Consider a smooth function cut off function

such that

•

\beta(1)=0

•

\beta(t)=1

for
0<\mu\ll 1

•

\beta(t)\neq 0
for

.

Now, consider the time-dependent Hamiltonian

,

So, we have

On

, we get a Hamiltonian vector field

that is the symplectic dual to

\displaystyle(df_{t})_{p}(v)=\omega_{g}(X_{t},v).

Let

be the flow of the vector field

, that is,

((\theta,s),t)\mapsto\phi_{t}(\theta,s)

Consider the map

\displaystyle F:S^{1}\times[0,1]

\displaystyle\to A\times\mathbb{R}^{2}

\displaystyle(\theta,t)

\displaystyle\mapsto(\phi_{t}(\theta),f_{t}(\phi_{t}(\theta)),t).

If we put the product symplectic form on

is a Lagrangian embedding. Indeed, we can check

\displaystyle F^{*}(\omega_{g}\oplus dx\wedge dy)

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}^{*}\omega_{g}+\frac{\partial(f_{t}\circ\phi_{t})}{\partial\theta}d\theta\wedge dt

\displaystyle=\omega_{g}\left(\frac{\partial\phi_{t}}{\partial\theta},\frac{\partial\phi_{t}}{\partial t}\right)d\theta\wedge dt+df\frac{\partial\phi_{t}}{\partial\theta}d\theta\wedge dt

\displaystyle=-\omega_{g}\left(X_{t},\frac{\partial\phi_{t}}{\partial\theta}\right)d\theta\wedge dt+\omega_{g}\left(X_{t},\frac{\partial\phi_{t}}{\partial\theta}\right)d\theta\wedge dt

\displaystyle=0.

is a diffeomorphism on

for each

\displaystyle dF=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial\phi^{1}}{\partial\theta}&\frac{\partial\phi^{1}}{\partial t}\\

is not injective. By taking the support of

A

Proposition 2.6
.

A generic Lagrangian surface
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}

parametrized by

\displaystyle r(\theta,t)=(x_{1}(\theta,t),y_{1}(\theta,t),x_{2}(\theta,t),t),

(1)

where
\theta\in S^{1}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial x_{2}}{\partial\theta}(\theta,t)=\omega\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial t},\frac{\partial z}{\partial\theta}\right)\text{ for }z(\theta,t)=(x_{1}(\theta,t),y_{1}(\theta,t)).

(2)

Conversely, if there is a family of knots parametrized by

\displaystyle(z(\theta,t),x_{2}(\theta,t))=(x_{1}(\theta,t),y_{1}(\theta,t),x_{2}(\theta,t))

(with parameter
t
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying Condition
2
above, then

\displaystyle r(\theta,t)=(x_{1}(\theta,t),y_{1}(\theta,t),x_{2}(\theta,t),t)

gives a Lagrangian surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

Remark 4
.

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, a tangent to
\pi_{1}(L\cap\mathbb{R}^{3}_{t})
is given by
(\frac{\partial x_{1}}{\partial\theta},\frac{\partial y_{1}}{\partial\theta})
and so, the normal
\eta=\mathrm{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}(z)
is given by
(\frac{\partial y_{1}}{\partial\theta},-\frac{\partial x_{1}}{\partial\theta})
.
Then, the right hand side of (
2
) is equal to

\langle\frac{\partial z}{\partial t},\eta\rangle.

Proof.

If we pull back the form
\omega
on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
via
r
, we get

\displaystyle r^{*}\omega

\displaystyle=r^{*}(dx_{1}\wedge dy_{1}+dx_{2}\wedge dy_{2})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial x_{1}}{\partial\theta}d\theta+\frac{\partial x_{1}}{\partial t}dt\right)\wedge\left(\frac{\partial y_{1}}{\partial\theta}d\theta+\frac{\partial y_{1}}{\partial t}dt\right)+\left(\frac{\partial x_{2}}{\partial\theta}d\theta+\frac{\partial x_{2}}{\partial t}dt\right)\wedge dt

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial x_{1}}{\partial\theta}\frac{\partial y_{1}}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial y_{1}}{\partial\theta}\frac{\partial x_{1}}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial x_{2}}{\partial\theta}\right)d\theta\wedge dt

\displaystyle=\left(\omega\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial\theta},\frac{\partial z}{\partial t}\right)+\frac{\partial x_{2}}{\partial\theta}(\theta,t)\right)d\theta\wedge dt.

Therefore,
r^{*}\omega\equiv 0
if and only if Condition (
2
) holds.
∎

For a Lagrangian,
L
, parametrized by
r(\theta,t)
as above,
fix a
\theta_{0}\in S^{1}
and define

\Delta x_{2}(\theta,t)=x_{2}(\theta,t)-x_{2}(\theta_{0},t),

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{area}(\theta,t)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\theta}\left(x\frac{\partial y}{\partial\theta}-y\frac{\partial x}{\partial\theta}\right)d\theta=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\theta}\omega\left(z,\frac{\partial z}{\partial\theta}\right)d\theta=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\theta}\langle z(\theta,t),\eta(\theta,t)\rangle d\theta.

We call this quantity “area” even though the arc over which we integrate, namely,
\pi_{1}(L\cap\mathbb{R}^{3}_{t})
from

to

does not bound a bounded region unless

Corollary 2.7
.

Given a Lagrangian in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
parametrized by

\displaystyle r(\theta,t)=(x_{1}(\theta,t),y_{1}(\theta,t),x_{2}(\theta,t),t),\quad(\theta,t)\in S^{1}\times\mathbb{R},

suppose that for
\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\in S^{1}
and

\displaystyle(x_{1}(\theta_{1},t),y_{1}(\theta_{1},t))=(x_{1}(\theta_{0},t),y_{1}(\theta_{0},t)).

(3)

Then, for
\Delta x_{2}(\theta,t)
and area
(\theta,t)
defined with reference point
\theta_{0}
as above,

\displaystyle\Delta x_{2}(\theta_{1},t)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathrm{area}(\theta_{1},t).

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(2\,\mathrm{area}(\theta,t))

\displaystyle=\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\theta_{1}}\bigg\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}z(\theta,t),\eta(\theta,t)\bigg\rangle d\theta+\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\theta_{1}}\bigg\langle z(\theta,t),\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\eta(\theta,t)\bigg\rangle d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\theta_{1}}\bigg\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}z(\theta,t),\eta(\theta,t)\bigg\rangle d\theta+\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\theta_{1}}\left(x\frac{\partial^{2}y}{\partial t\partial\theta}-y\frac{\partial^{2}x}{\partial t\partial\theta}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\theta_{1}}\bigg\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}z(\theta,t),\eta(\theta,t)\bigg\rangle d\theta+\left[x\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}-y\frac{\partial x}{\partial t}\right]_{\theta_{0}}^{\theta_{1}}+\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\theta_{1}}\bigg\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}z(\theta,t),\eta(\theta,t)\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=2\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\theta_{1}}\frac{\partial x_{2}}{\partial\theta}(\theta,t)d\theta+0=2\Delta x_{2}(\theta_{1},t),

where the middle term is zero using the assumption, and we use integration by parts in the earlier step.

Enriched Knot Diagrams

In this section, we define precisely what we mean by an enriched knot diagram of a knot or link. We describe equivalence classes of enriched knot diagrams, and describe and discuss the undercut relation between these equivalence classes. In particular, we show that the undercut relation defines a strict partial order on equivalence classes of exact enriched knot diagrams.
Lastly, we describe enriched knot diagram moves, which are adapted from
[
Lin13
]
. Compare enriched knot diagrams with Lagrangian diagrams in
[
Lin13
, Definition 1]
. In particular, the crossings appear reversed as Lin looks at slices
x_{2}=
constant, whereas we consider
y_{2}=
constant.

Consider an immersed curve
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
whose only singularities are transverse double points. Denote by
X(\mathcal{D})
the set of double points of
\mathcal{D}
, which we call
crossings of
\bf\mathcal{D}
.

Definition 3.1
.

For
p\in X(\mathcal{D})
and
\epsilon>0
small enough,
B_{\epsilon}(p)\setminus\mathcal{D}
has four connected components that we call
corners
at
p
. We call two corners at
p

adjacent corners
if they have a common boundary arc and otherwise
opposite corners
. Note that the corners are defined only up to a choice of
\epsilon
and we may also replace the ball
B_{\epsilon}(p)
by a small open neighbourhood of
p
. We call two corners
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
at
p

equal
if there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
C_{1}\cap B_{\epsilon}(p)=C_{2}\cap B_{\epsilon}(p)
.

Definition 3.2
.

The complement
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{D}
consists of disjoint union of connected sets
\cup_{j=1}^{k}D_{j}
. All but one
D_{j}
are a bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
that are diffeomorphic to the open disk,
\mathbb{D}^{\circ}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}|x^{2}+y^{2}<1\}
. In fact, these are images of holomorphic disks with corners
u_{j}:\mathbb{D}\to D_{j}
(see Definition
4.1
).
We refer to these holomorphic disks with corners as
disks cut out by curve
\mathcal{D}
.

Definition 3.3
.

An
enriched knot diagram
is a tuple
(\mathcal{D},\sigma,\mathcal{A})
where (refer Figure
7
):

•

\mathcal{D}
is an immersed, closed curve (possibly disconnected) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
whose only singularities are transverse double points;

•

(SIGNS)
\sigma
is a function assigning sign
\pm 1
to each corner of
\mathcal{D}
such that at each double point of
\mathcal{D}
, adjacent corners have opposite signs and opposite corners have the same sign;

•

(AREA)

\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\sigma_{K}(C)=1\text{ if }

\displaystyle\lim_{\partial C\to q^{-}}x_{2}>\lim_{\partial C\to q^{+}}x_{2}.

For any diagram

Definition 3.5

Definition 3.6

Lemma 3.7
.

For an enriched knot diagram,
(\mathcal{D}_{0},\sigma_{0},\mathcal{A}_{0})

.

and
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\sigma_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})

, and
\psi_{1}^{*}\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{1}

Proof.

The existence of a Hamiltonian isotopy
\psi_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\psi_{1}(\mathcal{D}_{0})=\mathcal{D}_{1}
, and
\psi_{1}^{*}\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{1}

(\mathcal{D}_{0},\sigma_{0},\mathcal{A}_{0})
and
\mathcal{D}_{1},\sigma_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
via the diffeomorphism
\psi_{1}
. As

is the time-
1

For the converse, suppose there exists a diffeomorphism
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
making
(\mathcal{D}_{0},\sigma_{0},\mathcal{A}_{0})\sim(\mathcal{D}_{1},\sigma_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
as in Definition
3.6
. Note that by using appropriate cut off functions outside a compact set
K
that contains both
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, we may assume that the diffeomorphism
\psi
is equal to identity outside

.
We show that the existence of such a diffeomorphism,
\psi
, implies existence of a symplectomorphism,
\phi
, such that
\phi(\mathcal{D}_{0})=\mathcal{D}_{1}
, and for all corners,
C
, of
\mathcal{D}_{0}

\sigma_{2}(\phi(C))=\sigma_{1}(C)

1

2

\psi

1

and outside

3.1
Undercut relation

In this section we define undercut relation and discuss some of its properties.

Define a
diagram pair
to be an ordered pair of two diagrams
([\mathcal{D}_{1},\sigma_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}],[\mathcal{D}_{2},\sigma_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2}])
. We refer to the first diagram,
[\mathcal{D}_{1},\sigma_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}]
, as the
lower diagram
and
[\mathcal{D}_{2},\sigma_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2}]
as the
upper diagram
of the pair. Given a diagram pair
([\mathcal{D}_{1},\sigma_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}],[\mathcal{D}_{2},\sigma_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2}])
, we say a disk
A
is
cut out by the pair
if it is either cut out by
\mathcal{D}_{1}
or by
\mathcal{D}_{2}
. Recall we defined area of an oriented knot as
a(K)=|\int_{K}\lambda|
, which does not depend on the chosen orientation of the knot. So, we may talk about relatively exact cobordisms without mentioning an orientation.

To show that the undercut relation is well-defined on equivalence classes of enriched knot diagrams we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are two links in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with equivalent enriched knot diagrams
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\sigma_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
and
(\mathcal{D}_{2},\sigma_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})

K_{3}

K_{3}\prec K_{1}

K_{3}\prec K_{2}

K_{1}\prec K_{3}

K_{2}\prec K_{3}

and

\epsilon>0

\displaystyle S^{1}\times[b-2\epsilon,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad(\theta,t)\mapsto(z(\theta,t),x_{2}(\theta,t),t).

More conditions on the choice of
\epsilon>0
will be clear as the proof progresses. Label
K_{1}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=L_{b-\epsilon}
and
K_{2}^{\prime\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=L_{b-2\epsilon}

Assume that we have a time dependent Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[b-2\epsilon,b]\to\mathbb{R},\quad(z,\tau)\mapsto H(z,\tau)

such that for Hamiltonian vector field

defined by
\omega(X_{\tau},\cdot)=dH_{\tau}

\displaystyle\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[b-2\epsilon,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

\phi_{1}(\mathcal{D}_{1})=\mathcal{D}_{2}

\frac{d}{d\tau}\phi_{x}(\tau)=X_{\phi_{x}(\tau)}

Then, as

\displaystyle S^{1}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad\theta\mapsto(z(\theta,b),x_{2}(\theta,b))

\displaystyle S^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad\theta\mapsto(\phi_{b}\circ z(\theta,b),h(\theta))

Consider the map

\displaystyle z^{\prime}:S^{1}\times[b-2\epsilon,b-\epsilon]\to\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad(\theta,t)\mapsto\phi_{t}\circ z(\theta,t).

We would like to use
z^{\prime}
as the
(x_{1},y_{1})
-part of a Lagrangian parametrization. Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial z^{\prime}}{\partial\theta}(\theta,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\phi_{t}}{\partial z}(z(\theta,t))\frac{\partial z}{\partial\theta}(\theta,t)=d\phi_{t}\circ\frac{\partial z}{\partial\theta};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial z^{\prime}}{\partial t}(\theta,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\phi_{t}}{\partial z}(z(\theta,t))\frac{\partial z}{\partial t}(\theta,t)+\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\tau}(z(\theta,t),{t-b+2\epsilon});

\displaystyle\omega\left(\frac{\partial z^{\prime}}{\partial\theta},\frac{\partial z^{\prime}}{\partial t}\right)

\displaystyle=\omega\left(d\phi_{t}\circ\frac{\partial z}{\partial\theta},d\phi_{t}\circ\frac{\partial z}{\partial t}\right)+\omega\left(d\phi_{t}\circ\frac{\partial z}{\partial\theta},\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=\omega\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial\theta},\frac{\partial z}{\partial t}\right)+\omega\left(d\phi_{t}\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial\theta}\right),\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial x_{2}}{\partial\theta}+\omega\left(d\phi_{t}\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial\theta}\right),\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\tau}\right)

\displaystyle x_{2}^{\prime}(\theta,t)=x_{2}(\theta,t)+H_{t}\circ\phi_{t}\circ z(\theta,t).

We are now ready to prove Lemma

Proof of Lemma

.

1.

Well-definedness follows directly from Lemma
3.8

2.

Transitivity follows by using Lemma

. Suppose

,
\partial_{+}L_{1}

\mathcal{D}_{2}
,
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, and

implies we can assume

and

Now we prove the additional properties that make

1.

Recall that if we restrict to exact diagrams, that is, if the total signed area bound by the diagram is zero, the relatively exact condition of the Lagrangian cobordism means that it is weakly exact.

Suppose, if possible,

. Then, by using Lemma

Moduli Spaces of Holomorphic Disks

Our main tool for studying Lagrangian cobordisms is holomorphic disks that have boundary on Lagrangian tangles. In this section we define the holomorphic functions we work with, and then review some standard notions about moduli spaces of holomorphic curves See
[
MS12
]
(Chapter 5) and
[
AJ10
]

We consider the complex structure
(\mathbb{R}^{4},\mathrm{i})
obtained by identifying
\mathbb{R}^{4}
with
(\mathbb{C}^{2},\mathrm{i})
. Let
\mathbb{D}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}||z|\leq 1\}
be the closed unit disk. For any subset of
\mathbb{C}
, like the unit disk

, we get a complex structure on it as a subset of
\mathbb{C}
. We denote this sructure also by
\mathrm{i}

Fix a Lagrangian tangle,

, in

.

4.1
Holomorphic Disks

Definition 4.1
.

A
holomorphic disk with corners
is a function

such that the following conditions hold:

•

u
is holomorphic on the interior of
\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle du(p)\circ\mathrm{i}=\mathrm{i}\circ du(u(p))

for all

•

There are finitely many distinct points

that are mapped to double points of
L
and where

jumps from one leaf of
L
to another. The images of these points are called

of

.

•

In the complement of the corner points

is
C^{2}
-regular.

•

u
is an immersion.

We denote by
\partial u
the restriction of

to the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
of the disk
\mathbb{D}
. We can orient
\partial u
with the anti-clockwise orientation on

We make the assumption that at each marked point the disk jumps between leaves of

because we do not allow marked points where

is smooth. Note that unless

jumps leaves at a marked point, it would be a smooth point. See the discussion about the trace operator in the proof of Lemma

Definition 4.2
.

As in Definition
4.1

\mathbb{R}^{2}
that have boundaries on any smooth curve. Given a diagram
(\mathcal{D},\sigma,\mathcal{A})

we say a holomorphic disk with corners

such that each corner point of

is a disk

by the diagram. We say it is a disk
bound by the pair

if it is a disk bound by
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\sigma_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
or by

Note that the area of a disk

Definition 4.3
.

Suppose

is a Lagrangian tangle in

(Definition

).
A

is a holomorphic disk with corners whose image lies completely in a complex plane

for some

.

For this paper, we only consider horizontal holomorphic disks with convex corners.

A holomorphic disk with corners will be horizontal if

or

, see Lemma

for a proof.

We get signs at the corners of a horizontal disk just by assigning the sign of the corner as per the diagram of
\partial_{+}L
or
\partial_{-}L
. We would like to extend this notion of corner sign to all holomorphic disks in a way that is useful in describing broken disks in moduli spaces.
Recall that at each double point

, the Lagrangian has an higher leaf

, and a lower leaf

.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
u:(\mathbb{D},\partial\mathbb{D})\to(\mathbb{R}^{4},L)
be a holomorphic disk with corner

. If

traverses the leaf

first and then

has

at

has

at
q
.
More explicitly, just as in the definition of enriched knot diagram coming from a knot

let us denote by

-values as we approach

\lim_{\partial C\to q^{+}}x_{2}
denote the limit in the reverse direction. Then we assign

\displaystyle\mathrm{sign}_{q}(u)=-1\text{ if }

\displaystyle\lim_{\partial u\to q^{-}}x_{2}<\lim_{\partial u\to q^{+}}x_{2},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathrm{sign}_{q}(u)=1\text{ if }

\displaystyle\lim_{\partial u\to q^{-}}x_{2}>\lim_{\partial u\to q^{+}}x_{2}.

If
u
is an immersed disk, it may pass through

multiple times, but always in the same direction. So, this is well-defined.

If

is a horizontal disk, the signs are part of the information of the enriched knot diagram of

or

Definition 4.5

(Stable Disks)

•

. The domain of the stable holomorphic map

can be represented as the quotient

In the case the Lagrangian is embedded we can take appropriate isomorphism classes of

For

Definition 4.7

4.7
Gromov Compactness

Theorem 4.10

(Gromov compactness)
.

Let
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{[a,b]}
be a Lagrangian tangle. Fix
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
A\in H_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4},L)
, and a map
\lambda:\{1,\dots,m\}\to\Delta(L)
. Then, the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{m}(A,\lambda;L)
with the
C^{\infty}
-topology is compact.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show sequential compactness. Let
u^{\nu}:(\mathbb{D},\partial\mathbb{D})\to(\mathbb{R}^{4},L)
be a sequence of holomorphic disks with marked points
z^{\nu}=(z^{\nu}_{1},\dots,z^{\nu}_{m})
, where
(z^{\nu}_{1},\dots,z^{\nu}_{m})
is a sequence of
m
-tuples of pairwise distinct points on
\partial\mathbb{D}
, with
u^{\nu}(z_{i}^{\nu})
to be double points of
L
. Then,
(u^{\nu},z^{\nu})
has a Gromov convergent subsequence.

This is essentially the same compactness as in
[
AJ10
, Section
4.1
]
, but our Lagrangian has boundary (with transverse double points) and the ambient manifold is not compact. However, we can adapt their argument to our situation as follows.

To apply the compactness results by M. Akaho and D. Joyce in
[
AJ10
, Section 4.1]
, we first extend our Lagrangian by attaching small collars to
\partial_{+}L
and
\partial_{-}L
as in Lemma
3
to get a slightly larger Lagrangian,
L^{\prime}\supset L
, with the same set of double points. In particular, we have
L^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{R}^{4}_{[a,b]}=L
. We denote by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(A,\lambda;L^{\prime})
the moduli space of stable disks with corners that have boundary on
L^{\prime}
. Note that
\{[u,z]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}(A,\lambda;L^{\prime})|\rm{Im}(u)\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{[a,b]}\}=\overline{\mathcal{M}}(A,\lambda;L)
. Now, we argue that if we restrict our attention to those elements of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(A,\lambda;L^{\prime})
that have image in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{[a,b]}
, we get a sequentially compact space.

Consider sequence
[(u^{\nu},z^{\nu})]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}(A,\lambda;L)
.
Note that

\rm{Im}(\partial u^{\nu})\subset L\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{[a,b]}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{a\leq y_{2}\leq b\}

implies, by the maximum principle for harmonic functions,
\rm{Im}(u^{\nu})\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{[a,b]}
and therefore the entire sequence of holomorphic disks have image outside an
\epsilon
-neighbourhood of
\partial L^{\prime}
for some
\epsilon\geq 0
. As,
L
is the image of a compact surface with boundary, it is contained in a compact set. In particular, there exists
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
for
i=1,\dots,4
such that
L\subset K=[a_{1},b_{1}]\times\dots\times[a_{4},b_{4}]
and all of the
u^{\nu}
have image inside
K
. These two observations say that arguments for compactness carried out in
[
AJ10
]
or
[
Liu02
]
will carry through and
[u^{\nu},z^{\nu}]
has a convergent subsequence with limit in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(A,\lambda;L^{\prime})
. As, convergence in the Gromov sense also gives convergence of the images, the limit will also have image within
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{[a,b]}
and therefore be contained in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(A,\lambda;L)
.
∎

5.2
Automatic Transversality

In this section we extend the arguments in
[
HLS97
]
to the case of holomorphic disks with corners, to get transversality. For this section, we do not really use the Lagrangian tangle structure of
L
. In fact, all results in this section hold if
L
is an immersed Lagrangian with only transverse double point singularities.

First, we formalize the idea that nearby holomorphic disks are holomorphic sections of the normal bundle. This is similar to
[
HLS97
, Section 1]
, with changes to account for the presence of corners in the boundary in our case. Second, in Section
5.2.1
, we discuss how the normal bundle we defined has a complex structure. Then we state the main theorem of this section, Theorem
5.2
, which is automatic transverality in the presence of corners. Then, in Section
5.2.2
, we discuss how we can obtain a smooth bundle pair relevant to the problem by adjusting the bundle over the corners. Finally, We prove Theorem
5.2
in Section
5.2.3
.

An unparametrized regular holomorphic disk with corners in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
that has boundary on
L
is a real immersed surface of genus zero and one boundary component,
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
of class
C^{0}
that is
C^{2}
on
\widetilde{\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Sigma\setminus\{q_{1},\dots,q_{m}\},
with
\partial\Sigma\subset L
, and
q_{i}\in\partial\Sigma\cap\Delta(L)
, such that the tangent space at each point
z\in\widetilde{\Sigma}
is
J
-invariant. To simplify notation, we assume
\Sigma
is embedded. Let us denote by
\partial\widetilde{\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\partial\Sigma\cap\widetilde{\Sigma}
. Note that Definition
4.1
gives us a parametrized holomorphic disk with corners.

If
\Sigma
is such a disk, then
J
induces on
T\widetilde{\Sigma}
an integrable almost complex structure
j
, that is, a Riemann surface structure on
\widetilde{\Sigma}
such that the identity map from
\widetilde{\Sigma}
to
\mathbb{R}^{4}
defines a parametrization of the holomorphic curve with Lagrangian boundary and corners, that is, it satisfies
df\circ j=J(f)\circ df
on points of
\widetilde{\Sigma}
and
\partial\Sigma\subset L
.

Let us now fix such a curve
\Sigma_{0}
with corners
q_{1},\dots,q_{m}\in\Delta(L)
and denote

\widetilde{\Sigma}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Sigma_{0}\setminus\{q_{1},\dots,q_{m}\}.

By the tubular neighbourhood theorem, we can identify
\mathbb{R}^{4}
near
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{0}
(as a differential manifold) with the normal bundle
N=T_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{0}}\mathbb{R}^{4}/T\widetilde{\Sigma}_{0}
. Note that here we are defining
T\widetilde{\Sigma}_{0}
on the boundary points by taking the vector space spanned by the half-space of tangent vectors to
\Sigma_{0}
.
Then,
N
is a complex line bundle over
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{0}
. Here the tubular neighbourhood of
\Sigma_{0}
is not really an open neighbourhood of
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{0}
, as
\Sigma_{0}
has boundary. For points of the boundary the tubular neighbourhood does not include a ball around it, instead only half-balls are included.

We get a totally real subbundle of
N|_{\partial\widetilde{\Sigma}}
given by

F\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=T_{\partial\widetilde{\Sigma}_{0}}L/T(\partial\widetilde{\Sigma}_{0})=TL\cap N_{\partial\widetilde{\Sigma}_{0}}.

Here intersection means the image of
TL
under the projection
TV\to N
coming from the identification
N=TV/T\widetilde{\Sigma}
. This is a real line bundle along
\partial\widetilde{\Sigma}_{0}
that cannot be extended to
\partial\Sigma_{0}
as the boundary jumps from one leaf of the Lagrangian to another at the corners.

Every (real) surface that is

6.3
Constraints on Sign at Corner Nodes

In this section we prove Part 4 of Theorem
6.5
as the following lemma. As we already showed that the only relevant nodes are corner nodes, the following lemma suffices.

Lemma 6.13

(
Adjacent disks have opposite signs
)
.

Suppose a stable disk

\displaystyle(\{u_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in T},\{z_{\alpha\beta}\}_{\alpha E\beta},\{z_{i},\alpha_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m})

is the Gromov limit of a sequence,
(u_{n},\{z^{n}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

is a nodal point mapped to a double point of

\displaystyle u_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha\beta})=u_{\beta}(z_{\beta\alpha})=q\in\Delta(L)\subset\partial L,

and it is a corner node.
Then,

\displaystyle\mathrm{sign}_{q}(u_{\alpha})=-\mathrm{sign}_{q}(u_{\beta}).

Proof.

It is enough to show this for the case when
u_{\alpha}
and
u_{\beta}
are embedded. Indeed, if they are only immersed, we can restrict ourselves to a small enough neighbourhoods of
z_{\alpha\beta}
and
z_{\beta\alpha}
, and repeat the same argument in those neighbourhoods. The notation and argument gets more cumbersome.

The main idea is that if we restrict our vision to a small enough neighbourhood of

, the situation looks exactly like that of converging strips in Lagrangian Floer homology setup.

Choose a neighbourhood
q\in U\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
such that
(U\cap L)\setminus\{q\}
has two connected components and
((U\cap L)\setminus\{q\})\cap\Delta(L)=\emptyset

\{q\}
as
L_{1}
and
L_{2}

and

to be small enough so that

and
u_{\beta}^{-1}(U)

z_{\alpha\beta}
and
z_{\beta\alpha}
respectively, and
u_{\alpha}^{-1}(U)\setminus\{z_{\alpha\beta}\}
and
u_{\beta}^{-1}(U)\setminus\{z_{\beta\alpha}\}
are biholomorphic to the strip
\mathbb{R}\times[-1,1]\subset\mathbb{C}
.
As
u_{n}
converge to
(u_{\alpha},u_{\beta})
in the Gromov sense, their images converge in
C^{\infty}
to the union of the images,
\mathrm{Im}(u_{\alpha})\cup\mathrm{Im}(u_{\beta})
. So, by choosing
U
to be small enough, we may assume

contains no marked points and are biholomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times[-1,1]\subset\mathbb{C}
as well. Take coordinates
t,s
on the strip with

and

.

Let us define compact sets

and
K_{\beta}

\partial U

. Note that we can assume sufficient regularity on the neighbourhood

and
K_{\beta}=\overline{\partial U\cap\mathcal{O}p(\mathrm{Im}(u_{\beta}))}
where
\mathcal{O}p(\mathrm{Im}(u_{\alpha}))
and
\mathcal{O}p(\mathrm{Im}(u_{\alpha}))
are arbitrary, small neighbourhoods of
\mathrm{Im}(u_{\alpha})
and
\mathrm{Im}(u_{\beta})
, respectively. We choose the open sets
\mathcal{O}p(\mathrm{Im}(u_{\alpha}))
and
\mathcal{O}p(\mathrm{Im}(u_{\beta}))

and

.

By restricting to large enough

and pre-composing by appropriate biholomorphisms to the strip

, we can assume we have

Of course, the

Additionally,
\tilde{u}_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}\times[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
and
\tilde{u}_{\beta}:\mathbb{R}\times[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
with

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\tilde{u}_{\alpha}(t,s)=q,\lim_{t\to-\infty}\tilde{u}_{\alpha}(t,s)\in K_{\alpha};

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\tilde{u}_{\beta}(t,s)\in K_{\beta},\lim_{t\to-\infty}\tilde{u}_{\beta}(t,s)=q.

In this section, we show that corner signs for non-horizontal holomorphic disks are determined by which boundary of

Lemma 6.14

Let

Suppose

is a non-horizontal holomorphic disk with corner at

. Then
\mathrm{sign}_{q}(u)=+1
.
Similarly,
q\in\partial_{-}L
implies
\mathrm{sign}_{q}(u)=-1
.

Before we prove this lemma, let us discuss how we conclude Part 4 of Theorem
6.5
. Lemma
6.13
and the above Lemma
6.14
together give us that we cannot have two non-horizontal disks attached at a node. We claim that the case of two horizontal disks attached at a node also do not appear in our considered moduli space,
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(u)
. Suppose
\mathrm{Im}(\partial u)\subset\partial_{+}L=L_{b}
. In Section
5.1
, we defined the real line bundle
T^{1}L
along
\partial\mathbb{D}
as a subbundle of
\mathbb{C}\times\{0\}\subset\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}\cong u^{*}(T\mathbb{R}^{4})
for the horizontal disk
u
. The Maslov index
\mu_{1}(u)
of the bundle pair
(\mathbb{C},T^{1}L)
will always be
2
(for convex
u
) and
\mu_{1}(u)
actually gets suppressed in the dimension calculations and we only see
\mu_{2}(u)
in
\dim\mathcal{M}(u)
. Maslov index
\mu_{1}(u)=2
implies the expected dimension of the moduli space of variations of
u
within the plane is

\displaystyle\dim(\mu(u,L_{b}))=\mu_{1}(u)+2-3-1=0.

If we had two horizontal disks attached at a node
q\in\partial_{+}L
, we could glue within
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{b}
to obtain a
1
-dimensional moduli space contained in
\mathcal{M}(u,L_{b})
, which contradicts the dimension calculation. Thus, we can only have the situation where each node has exactly one horizontal disk and one non-horizontal disk attached, which is Part 4 of Theorem
6.5
. Now we prove the lemma.

Proof.

First, we build a local model for
L
near a double point
q
.
Suppose
q\in\partial_{+}L
. Let us assume that
\partial_{+}L\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{0}
and
q=0\in\partial_{+}L
. Fix a small neighbourhood
U
of

in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. In
U
, the Lagrangian has linear approximation given by
(T_{0}L^{h}\cup T_{0}L^{l})\cap\mathbb{R}^{4}_{-\infty,0]}
, where
L^{h}
and
L^{l}
are the two leaves of
L
near
q
. Upto a Hamiltonian isotopy, we may assume the Lagrangian is equal to this linear approximation in a small neighbourhood of
0
. We want to analyze what the Lagrangian
L
looks like near
0
, so we analyze what two Lagrangian subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
intersecting at one point can look like. Consider
L^{h}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\{0\})
. It is a line passing through
0
, let us call it
l_{11}
. It is given by an equation
a_{1}x_{1}+b_{1}y_{1}=0
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Any Lagrangian plane containing this line lies in the symplectic complement of
\mathrm{i}(l_{11})

L_{1}
must be a line in
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, say
a_{2}x_{2}+b_{2}x_{2}=0

a_{2},b_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Similarly, the subspace
T_{0}L^{l}=\{c_{1}x_{1}+d_{1}y_{1}=0,c_{2}x_{2}+d_{2}y_{2}=0\}

c_{1},d_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
. To get a local model for the Lagrangian tangle

\{y_{2}\leq 0\}

Recall the notation
L^{h}
is the higher leaf and
L^{l}
is the lower leaf near

with respect to the

-coordinate.
For ease of notation we can fix coefficients without loss of generality as follows:
Locally,
L\cap U=L^{h}\cup L^{l}
, where

\displaystyle L^{h}=\{(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2})\in U|y_{1}=0,x_{2}+y_{2}=0,y_{2}\leq 0\},

\displaystyle L^{l}=\{(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2})\in U|x_{1}=0,x_{2}-y_{2}=0,y_{2}\leq 0\}.

Now, we prove the lemma.
If
u:(\mathbb{D},\partial\mathbb{D})\to(\mathbb{R}^{4},L)

\displaystyle\pi_{2}(L^{h})=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}|x+y=0,y\leq 0\},

The case for

must bound the same signed area and have the same rotation number. Further, let us denote by
\mathrm{wr}(K_{1})
the writhe of the diagram
\pi(K_{1})=\mathcal{D}_{K_{1}}
with respect to the framing
\ker\omega|_{\mathbb{R}^{3}_{a}}
also known as black board framing. That is, it is the sum of the number of positive crossings minus the number of negative crossings as shown in Figure

, when viewed from positive infinity on the
x_{2}
-axis. Then, for

\displaystyle\mathrm{wr}(K_{1})-\mathrm{wr}(K_{2})=\chi(L)

(6)

where
\chi
denotes the Euler characteristic (see

]
). An interesting observation is that Gromov’s theorem stating that there are no closed weakly exact Lagrangians in
\mathbb{R}^{4}

We want to study those situations where these topological constraints are inconclusive. For example, when

Proof.

Suppose
[\mathcal{D}_{1},\sigma_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}]\prec[\mathcal{D}_{2},\sigma_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2}]
. This means that there exists a Lagrangian cobordism
L^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{[a,b]}
with
\partial_{+}L^{\prime}=K_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}
and
\partial_{-}L^{\prime}=K_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
. Now, by using Lemma
3.9
, we get a Lagrangian tangle
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{[a,b]}
with diagram of
\partial_{+}L=[\mathcal{D}_{2}]
and diagram of
\partial_{+}L=[\mathcal{D}_{1}]
. This also means
\partial_{+}L=[\mathcal{D}_{2}]
and
\partial_{+}L=[\mathcal{D}_{1}]
as immersed curves.

Suppose we have a big disk bound by the diagram pair. This means there exists a big disk bound by
\partial_{+}L
or
\partial_{-}L
in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{a}
or
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{b}
. In either case, we get
(u,{\bf z})
, a big, horizontal, holomorphic disk with corners, that has boundary on
L
. As
u
is a big disk,
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(u)
is a compact
1
-dimensional manifold (Theorem
5.2
, Theorem
4.10
) with
[u]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}(u)
representing a boundary point of the moduli space (Part 1 of Theorem
6.5
).

By Theorem
6.5
, Part 2, we must have even number of type 2 holomorphic disks as boundary points for
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(u)
. So there must exist a stable, holomorphic, Type 2 disk,
[{\bf u^{\prime},z^{\prime}}]
, distinct from
[u,{\bf z}]
.

If
{\bf u^{\prime}}
does not have any nodes, then it is a horizontal disk by Part 1 of Theorem
6.5
. In this case, from the definition of
\mathcal{M}(u)
, all the corners of
u^{\prime}
are the same as those of
u
, with the same signs.

If
({\bf u^{\prime},z})
is a stable disk with nodes, by Theorem
6.5
, the stable disk of type 2 would have at least one horizontal disk, say
v:(\mathbb{D},\partial\mathbb{D})\to(\mathbb{R}^{4},L)
. We claim that
v
gives us a little disk to
u
for the considered diagram pair. To prove the claim, take the case when
\mathrm{Im}(u)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{b}
. Then,
u
has all positive corners.

•

If
\mathrm{Im}(v)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{b}
, then each corner
q
of
v
is either a corner point shared with
u
or it is a node of the stable holomorphic disk that
v
is a component of. If
q
is a node, then it is a node of a stable holomorphic disk of type 2. So, it has to be a node between
v
and a non-horizontal disk
v^{\prime}
. As
v^{\prime}
is not horizontal, and
q\in\partial_{+}L
, by Lemma
6.14
,
\mathrm{sign}_{q}(v^{\prime})=+1
. So, by Lemma
6.13
,
\mathrm{sign}_{q}(v)=-\mathrm{sign}_{q}(v^{\prime})=-1
. If
q
is shared by
v
and
u
, then it is a corner point of the stable holomorphic disk and by virtue of the definition of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(u)
, has the same sign as
\mathrm{sign}_{q}(u)
. So,
\mathrm{sign}_{q}(v)=+1
.

•

In the case
\mathrm{Im}(v)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{a}
, all its corners are nodes of a stable holomorphic disk of type 2. Each node has an attached non-horizontal disk that must have sign equal to
-1
at the node. So, all of
v
’s corners are positive by Part 3 of Theorem
6.5
. For the case when
im(u)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{b}
, the argument is analogous.

The area inequality comes from the fact that every holomorphic disk must have strictly positive area. Suppose,
v
is the little disk of
u
that appears as a horizontal disk of
({\bf u^{\prime},z^{\prime}})
, the stable holomorphic disk of Type 2 that represents a boundary point of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(A)
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in I}\omega([u^{\prime}_{\alpha}])=\omega\left(\sum_{\alpha\in I}[u^{\prime}_{\alpha}]\right)=\omega(A)=\omega([u]).

As each

is a holomorphic disk,
\omega([u^{\prime}_{\alpha}])>0
. So, we get that

\displaystyle\omega([v])=\omega([u])-\sum_{\alpha\in I,u^{\prime}_{\alpha}\neq v}\omega([u^{\prime}_{\alpha}])<\omega([u]).

The only exception to the above inequality happens when
[v]=[u]\in H_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4},L)
.
∎

Moments of quantum purity and biorthogonal polynomial recurrence
Shi-Hao Li and Lu Wei

Abstract
. The Bures-Hall ensemble is a unique measure of density matrices that satisfies various distinguished properties in quantum information processing. In this work, we study the statistical behavior of entanglement over the Bures-Hall ensemble as measured by the simplest form of an entanglement entropy - the quantum purity. The main results of this work are the exact second and third moment expressions of quantum purity valid for any subsystem dimensions, where the corresponding results in the literature are limited to the scenario of equal subsystem dimensions. In obtaining the results, we have derived recurrence relations of the underlying integrals over the Cauchy-Laguerre biorthogonal polynomials that may be of independent interest.



1.
Introduction and main results

Quantum information theory aims at studying the theoretical foundations of quantum technologies including quantum computing and quantum communications. Crucial to successful exploitation of the quantum revolutionary advances is the understanding of the phenomenon of quantum entanglement. Entanglement is the most fundamental characteristic trait of quantum mechanics, which is also the resource that enables quantum technologies.

In this work, we study the statistical behavior of entanglement of quantum bipartite systems over the Bures-Hall ensemble

Quantum purity measures how far a state is from a pure state and is the simplest form of an entanglement entropy. We focus on finding the exact moments of purity, which in practice can be utilized to construct finite-size approximations to the distribution

The considered bipartite model is useful in describing the entanglement between the two subsystems of various real-world quantum systems, in which one subsystem represents a physical object (such as a set of spins) and the other subsystem is the environment (such as a heat bath). Existing results on the moments of purity are limited to the special case of equal subsystem dimensions, where the exact first three moments are known

Entanglement serves as a measure of the non-classical correlation between the subsystems

Statistical information of an entanglement entropy such as the considered quantum purity is encoded through its moments: the first moment (average value) implies the typical behavior of entanglement, the second moment (variance) specifies the fluctuation around the typical value, and the higher order moments (such as skewness and kurtosis) describe the tails of the distribution. Moreover, the moments can be also utilized to construct non-asymptotic approximations to the distribution of purity. Due to the compact support of purity, the resulting approximations become exact as the number of moments involved increases as promised by the Weierstrass approximation theorem. In the literature, the first moment of purity has been recently obtained in

\displaystyle-y^{\alpha+1}e^{-y}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{h_{k}}P_{k}(-y)q_{k}(x)

(2.22c)

\displaystyle K_{11}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{\alpha}y^{\alpha+1}e^{-x-y}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{h_{k}}P_{k}(-y)Q_{k}(-x)-W(x,y),

(2.22d)

where the weight function

of the (monic) Cauchy-Laguerre biorthogonal polynomials

and

,

(2.24)

(2.25a)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{w^{\alpha+1}{\rm e}^{-w}}{y-w}q_{k}(w)\,\mathrm{d}w

(2.31b)

With the results introduced above, the moments (
2.12
) can now be computed by integrating over the appropriate form of the correlation kernels. However, the direct integration may not be an efficient method for the moment computation. We now discuss how the recurrence coefficients of the Cauchy-Laguerre biorthogonal polynomials are useful in obtaining the moments of purity. For positive integers
\beta
and
\gamma
, the idea is to successively reduce the power of the average of
x^{\beta}y^{\gamma}
over the ensemble to averages of lower order powers. The reduction is substantially simplified by the recurrence relations of the biorthogonal polynomials. Instead of performing integrations, the only task now is to collect non-zero contributions as identified by the biorthogonality condition. As a result, the desired integrals boil down to summations involving the recurrence coefficients of the biorthogonal polynomials, which are known explicitly. Besides the considered purity, the approach in fact works for any polynomial linear spectral statistics. To some extend, the idea is similar to that in Aomoto’s proof of the Selberg integral
[
21
, chapter 17.3-17.4]
, which concerns the computation of averages over classical random matrix ensembles
[
21
,
22
]
. The proposed method also has independent interest in the field of combinatorics
[
23
]
as briefly outlined here. Consider an oriented graph
G=(V,E)
with vertices
V=\mathbb{N}^{2}
and edges
E=\left\{(n,k)\to(n+1,m),\,n,k\in\mathbb{N},\,0\leq m\leq k+1\right\}
, where the weight on the path is given by
\omega((n,k)\to(n+1,m))=\langle xp_{k},q_{m}\rangle
. We then have

\displaystyle xp_{k}(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+1}\langle xp_{k},q_{\ell}\rangle p_{\ell}(x),

and this leads, by induction, to

\displaystyle x^{n}p_{k}(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+n}\left(\sum_{\gamma:(0,k)\to(n,\ell)}\prod_{e\in\gamma}\omega(e)\right)p_{\ell}(x).

The summation over the oriented paths
\gamma
on
G
starts from
(0,k)
and ends at
(n,\ell)
, where each path picks the product of the weights along the edges it crosses.

2.2.
Computation of the first moment

To illustrate the proposed method, here we rederive the first moment of purity recently reported in
[
8
,
9
]
. By the definition (
2.12a
), one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{h}\!\left[\text{T}\right]=m\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{2}h_{1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{2}K_{01}(x,x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{2}K_{10}(x,x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(2.33)

We now focus on the first integral

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{2}K_{01}(x,x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

(2.34)

where the second one can be similarly computed. By the summation form of the kernels (
2.22
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{2}K_{01}(x,x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{h_{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{2}p_{k}(x)q_{k}(y)W(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

(2.35)

To compute the integral (
2.35
), it is more convenient to consider a general form

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{\beta}p_{k}(x)q_{k}(y)W(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

(2.36)

with
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. To this end, we first state the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\{a_{k,i}\}_{i\leq k}
denote the coefficients of polynomials
p_{k}(x)
in (
2.22a
) for an arbitrary
k\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle p_{k}(x)=x^{k}+a_{k,k-1}x^{k-1}+\cdots+a_{k,0}x^{0}

with the convention
a_{k,k}=1
. For the Cauchy-Laguerre biorthogonal polynomials defined by (
2.23
)-(
2.24
), one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{\beta}q_{k}(y)W(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=b_{\beta,\beta-k}h_{k},\hskip 10.00002pt\beta\geq k,

(2.37)

where
h_{k}
is the normalization constant in (
2.23
) and
b_{\beta,k}
is recursively given by

\displaystyle b_{\beta,i}=-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}b_{\beta,j}a_{\beta-j,\beta-i},\hskip 10.00002ptb_{\beta,0}=1.

(2.38)

Proof.

The idea is to write
x^{\beta}
in terms of the orthogonal basis
\{p_{k}(x)\}_{k\leq\beta}
before applying the orthogonality condition. When
\beta=k
, it is obvious that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{k}q_{k}(y)W(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=h_{k}

by orthogonality. When
\beta=k+1
, since

\displaystyle x^{k+1}=p_{k+1}(x)-a_{k+1,k}p_{k}(x)+\text{l.o.t.},

the lower order terms (
l.o.t.
) do not contribute to the result when taking the inner product with
q_{k}(y)
on both sides, one finds that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{k+1}q_{k}(y)W(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=-a_{k+1,k}h_{k}.

Continuing this procedure establishes the result of this proposition.
∎

Moreover, if we expand

\displaystyle x^{\beta}p_{k}(x)=x^{\beta+k}+a_{k,k-1}x^{\beta+k-1}+\cdots+a_{k,0}x^{\beta}

and employ the result of above proposition, we then have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\beta}p_{k}(x)q_{k}(y)W(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=\sum_{j=0}^{\beta}a_{k,k-j}b_{\beta+k-j,\beta-j}.

(2.39)

Similarly, if we expand
q_{k}(y)
as

\displaystyle q_{k}(y)=y^{k}+\hat{a}_{k,k-1}y^{k-1}+\cdots+\hat{a}_{k,0}y^{0},

then by the recursion

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{\beta,i}=-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\hat{b}_{\beta,j}\hat{a}_{\beta-j,\beta-i},\hskip 10.00002pt\hat{b}_{\beta,0}=1,

(2.40)

one has

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\beta}p_{k}(x)q_{k}(y)W(x,y)dxdy=\sum_{j=0}^{\beta}\hat{a}_{k,k-j}\hat{b}_{\beta+k-j,\beta-j}.

Putting the results together, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{h}[\text{T}]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j=0}^{2}\left(a_{k,k-j}b_{k+2-j,2-j}+\hat{a}_{k,k-j}\hat{b}_{k+2-j,2-j}\right),

(2.41)

where the explicit formulas for
\{a_{k,j},\,\hat{a}_{k,j}\}_{j\leq k}
are given by
[
17
, equation (2.6)]

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} a_{k,j}&=(-1)^{k-j}{k\choose j}\frac{\Gamma(2a+k+j+2)\Gamma(2a+k+2)\Gamma(a+k+1)}{\Gamma(2a+2k+2)\Gamma(2a+j+2)\Gamma(a+j+1)}\\
\hat{a}_{k,j}&=(-1)^{k-j}{k\choose j}\frac{\Gamma(2a+k+j+2)\Gamma(2a+k+2)\Gamma(a+k+2)}{\Gamma(2a+2k+2)\Gamma(2a+j+2)\Gamma(a+j+2)}.\end{aligned}

(2.42)

Inserting the above
a_{k,j}
and
\hat{a}_{k,j}
into (
2.38
) and (
2.40
), respectively, we claim that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} b_{k,j}&={k\choose j}\frac{\Gamma(2a+k+2)\Gamma(a+k+1)\Gamma(2a+2k+3-2j)}{\Gamma(2a+k+2-j)\Gamma(a+k+1-j)\Gamma(2a+2k+3-j)}\\
\hat{b}_{k,j}&={k\choose j}\frac{\Gamma(2a+k+2)\Gamma(a+k+2)\Gamma(2a+2k+3-2j)}{\Gamma(2a+k+2-j)\Gamma(a+k+2-j)\Gamma(2a+2k+3-j)},\end{aligned}

(2.43)

which is equivalent of stating that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(-1)^{j}{i\choose j}\frac{(2{\alpha}+2k+2-2j)\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k-i-j+2)}{\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k+3-j)}=(-1)^{i-1}\frac{\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k+3-2i)}{\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k+3-i)}.

(2.44)

The left hand side of the above identity is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\frac{(-1)^{j}\Gamma(i+1)}{\Gamma(j+1)\Gamma(i+1-j)}\frac{(2{\alpha}+2k+2-2j)\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k-i-j+2)}{\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k+3-j)}

(2.45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{i-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{i-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}\Gamma(i+1)}{\Gamma(\ell+2)\Gamma(i-\ell)}\frac{(2{\alpha}+2k+4-2i+2\ell)\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k+3-2i+\ell)}{\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k+4-i+\ell)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{i}}{\Gamma(-i)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{i-1}\frac{(2{\alpha}+2k+4-2i+2\ell)\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k+3-2i+\ell)\Gamma(1-i+\ell)}{\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k+4-i+\ell)\Gamma(2+\ell)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{i}}{\Gamma(-i)}\frac{1}{-i}\left(\frac{\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k+3-i)}{\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k+3)\Gamma(i+1)}-\frac{\Gamma(1-i)\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k-2i+3)}{\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k-i+3)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{i-1}\frac{\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k-2i+3)}{\Gamma(2{\alpha}+2k-i+3)},

where the second to last equality is a direct application of
[
19
, lemma 4.1]
.

Finally, inserting (
2.42
) and (
2.43
) into (
2.41
), one obtains

\mathbb{E}_{h}\!\left[\text{T}\right]=\frac{m(2\alpha+m+1)\left(4\alpha^{2}+4\alpha+5m^{2}+10\alpha m+5m+2\right)}{4(2\alpha+2m+1)},

(2.46)

which upon applying the moment relation (
2.11
) for

gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}\!\left[\text{P}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{m(m+2\alpha+1)\left(m^{2}+2\alpha m+m+2\right)}\mathbb{E}_{h}\!\left[\text{T}\right]

(2.47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{5m^{2}+10\alpha m+5m+4\alpha^{2}+4\alpha+2}{(2m+2\alpha+1)\left(m^{2}+2\alpha m+m+2\right)}.

This verifies the mean purity formula (

]

2.3.
Computation of the second moment

We now proceed to the computation of the second moment
\mathbb{E}_{h}\!\left[\text{T}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{h}\!\left[\text{T}^{2}\right]=m\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{4}h_{1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+m(m-1)\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{2}y^{2}~h_{2}\left(x,y\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{4}(K_{01}(x,x)+K_{10}(x,x))\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j=0}^{4}\left(a_{k,k-j}b_{k+4-j,4-j}+\hat{a}_{k,k-j}\hat{b}_{k+4-j,4-j}\right).

The second term becomes
\mathbb{E}_{h}\!\left[\text{T}^{2}\right]-\mathcal{J}
if we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{2}y^{2}\left(K_{01}(x,y)K_{01}(y,x)+K_{10}(x,y)K_{10}(y,x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y-

(2.48)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{2}y^{2}K_{00}(x,y)K_{11}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

The identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{2}y^{2}K_{01}(x,y)K_{01}(y,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}\left(\frac{1}{h_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{2}p_{k}(x)q_{j}(y)W(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\left(\frac{1}{h_{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{2}p_{j}(x)q_{k}(y)W(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{\beta}p_{k}(x)q_{j}(y)W(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,\hskip 10.00002pt\beta\in\mathbb{N}

Corollary 2.2
.

For arbitrary
\beta,\,k,\,j\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{\beta}p_{k}(x)q_{j}(y)W(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}a_{k,\ell}b_{\beta+\ell,\beta+\ell-j}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{2}y^{2}K_{01}(x,y)K_{01}(y,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}a_{k,\ell}b_{\ell+2,\ell+2-j}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{j}a_{j,\ell}b_{\ell+2,\ell+2-k}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\hat{a}_{k,\ell}\hat{b}_{\ell+2,\ell+2-j}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{j}\hat{a}_{j,\ell}\hat{b}_{\ell+2,\ell+2-k}\right).

3.
Conclusion and outlook

In this work, we derived the exact second and third moments of quantum purity of arbitrary subsystem dimensions over the Bures-Hall ensemble, generalizing the results of equal subsystem dimensions in the literature. The derivations are obtained based on the direct integrations over the correlation kernels and the established recurrence relations of integrals involving biorthogonal polynomials. Future work includes devising an integrable system approach to systematically produce moments of any order as well as the study of asymptotic distribution of purity of large subsystem dimensions.

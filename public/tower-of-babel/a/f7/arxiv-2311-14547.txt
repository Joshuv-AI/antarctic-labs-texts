Smooth and Gevrey Microlocal Hypoellipticity for a Class of Hypocomplex Tube Structures
Nicholas Braun Rodrigues

Abstract
. We prove a smooth and Gevrey- s s microlocal hypoellipticity result for a system of complex vector fields associated with a real-analytic locally integrable structure of tube type, that is also microlocal hypocomplex. In order to so, we employ the use of a certain partial F.B.I. transform adapted to the locally integrable structure, first introduced by M. S. Baouendi, C. H. Chang and F. Treves, and we prove a microlocal characterization of the smooth and Gevrey- s s wave front set in terms of the decay of this partial F.B.I. transform.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{C}\mathrm{T}\Omega
be a real-analytic locally integrable structure of corank
m
on a
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
-smooth manifold
\Omega
of dimension
n+m
,
i.e.
it’s orthogonal bundle
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\Omega
, defined by the one forms that annihilate the sections of
\mathcal{V}
, is locally generated by the differentials
m
of real-analytic functions. Locally, the bundle
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}
is generated a small open neighborhood of the origin
U
by the differential of maps of the form

[formula]

Z_{k}(x,t)=x_{k}+i\phi_{k}(x,t),

where the map
\phi:U\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is real-analytic and satisfies
\phi(0)=0
and
\mathrm{d}_{x}\phi(0)=0
. Assuming
U
small enough, the sections of
\mathcal{V}
on
U
are spanned by the following family of complex vector fields,

[formula]

\mathrm{L}_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{j}}-i\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{m}\frac{\partial\phi_{k}}{\partial t_{j}}\big(Z_{x}^{-1}\big)_{\ell,k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\ell}},

A long standing question in the theory of locally integrable structures, also in CR geometry (
\mathcal{V}
is said to be CR if
\mathcal{V}\cap\overline{V}=\{0\}
), is to characterize when the system of complex vector fields
\mathrm{L}_{1},\dots,\mathrm{L}_{n}
is (microlocal) hypoelliptic (analytic hypoelliptic, Gevrey-
s
hypoelliptic) at the origin. We recall that
\mathcal{V}
is said to be hypoelliptic at the origin, if every distributional solution
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U)
of the system

[formula]

\mathrm{L}_{j}u=f_{j},

for
f_{j}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(U)
,
j=1,\dots,n
, is actually
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-smooth in some open neighborhood of the origin. Here we point out that in the CR case (embedded), the analytic hypoellipticity of
\mathcal{V}
is equivalent to holomorphic extension of CR functions (or CR distributions).

In 1981, F. Treves conjectured that, when
m=1
, the hypoellipticity of
\mathcal{V}
should be equivalent to the openness of the map
Z
near the origin. It is a well-known fact that the openness of
Z
near the origin is equivalent to the analytic hypoelipticity of
\mathcal{V}
(it is actually equivalent to the hypocomplexity of
\mathcal{V}
, see
[
30
]
). In 1980, H. M. Maire proved in
[
27
]
that this is true for tube structures,
i.e.
when you can choose the map
\phi
to be independent of the variable
x
. In
[
20
]
, J. E. Castellanos, P. D. Cordaro and G. Petronilho proved, for tube structures, that the openness of
Z
also implies the Gevrey-
s
hypoellipticity of
\mathcal{V}
at the origin. The conjecture is still open, but recently, N. Braun Rodrigues, P. D. Cordaro and G. Petronilho proved in
[
17
]
, that under the additional assumption that
|\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Im}\,Z|\leq C|\mathrm{d}Z\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{Z}|
(which implies a certain Lojasiewicz inequality), the openness of
Z
implies the hypoellipticity of
\mathcal{V}
at the origin.

For
m>1
, the picture changes drastically for hypoellipticity, but not for analytic hypoellipticity. In 1982, M. S. Baouendi and F. Treves proved in
[
10
]
that for tube structures, if
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 0
, then the following are equivalent:

1.

the origin is not a local minimum of the function
t\mapsto\phi(t)\cdot\xi_{0}
;

2.

(0,\xi_{0})\notin\text{WF}_{a}(u|_{t=0})
, for every
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{L}_{j}u=0
, for
j=1,\dots,n
.

Condition
2.
is called microlocal hypocomplexity. The structure is said hypocomplex at the origin if every distributional solution
u
of the homogeneous system
\mathrm{L}_{j}u=0
,
j=1,\dots,n
, is the composition of a holomorphic function
F
with the first integrals
Z
,
i,e,

u=F\circ Z
in an open neighborhood of the origin, or in other words, every distributional solution is hypo-analytic at the origin. Furthermore, a distributional solution
u
is hypo-analytic at the origin if and only if its trace
u|_{t=0}
is the composition of a holomorphic function with
Z|_{t=0}
. It is worth noticing that
\mathcal{V}
is analytic hypoelliptic at the origin if and only if every solution (of the homogeneous system) is real-analytic near the origin. Both properties are in general false for smooth and Gevrey-
s
regularity.

For
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
regularity, even for tube structures, it is a whole different story. Consider in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
the following complex vector fields introduced by H. M. Maire in
[
27
]
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}_{1}=\partial_{t_{1}}+i\left(3\partial_{x_{1}}-(4t_{1}t_{2}+3)t_{1}^{2}\partial_{x_{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}_{2}=\partial_{t_{2}}-it_{1}^{4}\partial_{x_{2}}.

In 1981, M. S. Baouendi and F. Treves proved in
[
9
]
that the system
\{\mathrm{L}_{1},\mathrm{L}_{2}\}
is analytic hypoelliptic but not hypoelliptic. Then, in 2013, J. E. Castellanos, P. D. Cordaro and G. Petronilho proved in
[
20
]
that the system
\{\mathrm{L}_{1},\mathrm{L}_{2}\}
is not Gevrey-
s
hypoelliptic for every
s\geq 4
.

Now let us describe the results contained in the present paper. Suppose
\mathcal{V}
is of tube type,
i.e.

\phi(x,t)=\phi(t)
, and let
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
. In section
4
we introduce the property
(\star)
at
\xi_{0}
(see definition
4.1
), which, in a nutshell, means that the origin is not a local minimum of
t\mapsto\phi(t)\cdot\xi_{0}
, and there exist constants
C>0
,
1/2\leq\theta<1
, such that
|\phi(t)\cdot\xi|^{\theta}\leq C|{}^{t}\mathrm{d}\phi(t)\xi|
, for
t
in an open neighborhood of the origin and
\xi
in an open neighborhood of
\xi_{0}
. Let
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be two sufficiently small open neighborhoods of the origin, as described in section
2
. Under this condition we were able to prove the following Theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
be such that the map
\phi
satisfies condition
(\star)
at
\xi_{0}
. Let
s>1
, and let
u\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(W;\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(V))
be such that
(0,0,\xi_{0},0)\notin\mathrm{WF}_{s}(\mathrm{L}_{j}u)
, for
j=1,\dots,n
. Then
(0,0,\xi_{0},0)\notin\mathrm{WF}_{s}(u)
. Furthermore, if we assume
\theta=1/2
on (
LC
), then for every
u\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(W;\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(V))
such that
(0,0,\xi_{0},0)\notin\mathrm{WF}(\mathrm{L}_{j}u)
, for
j=1,\dots,n
, we have
(0,0,\xi_{0},0)\notin\mathrm{WF}(u)
.

At the end of the paper we discuss a class of examples given by homogeneous polynomials of a fix degree satisfying condition
(\star)
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we gave a partial characterization of the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
and Gevrey-
s
wave front sets in terms of the decay of a partial adapted F.B.I. transform (see section
3
):

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
s>1
,
u\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(W;\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(V))
and
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 0
. Then the following are equivalent:

1.

(0,0,\xi_{0},0)\notin\mathrm{WF}_{s}(u)
;

2.

For every
\chi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(V)
, with
0\leq\chi\leq 1
and
\chi\equiv 1
in some open neighborhood of the origin, there exist
\widetilde{V}\subset V
,
\widetilde{W}\subset W
, open balls centered at the origin, an open cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 0
containing
\xi_{0}
, and constants
C,\varepsilon>0
such that

(1)

|\mathfrak{F}[\chi u](t;Z(x,t),\zeta)|\leq Ce^{-\varepsilon|\zeta|^{\frac{1}{s}}},\qquad\forall t\in\widetilde{W},x\in\widetilde{V},\zeta\in\mathbb{R}\mathrm{T}^{\prime}_{\Sigma_{t}}\big|_{Z(x,t)}(\mathcal{C}).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(W;\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(V))
and
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 0
. Then the following are equivalent:

1.

(0,0,\xi_{0},0)\notin\mathrm{WF}(u)
;

2.

For every
\chi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(V)
, with
0\leq\chi\leq 1
and
\chi\equiv 1
in some open neighborhood of the origin, there exist
\widetilde{V}\subset V
,
\widetilde{W}\subset W
, open balls centered at the origin, an open cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 0
containing
\xi_{0}
, such that for every
N>0
exists
C_{N}>0
satisfying

|\mathfrak{F}[\chi u](t;Z(x,t),\zeta)|\leq\frac{C_{N}}{(1+|\zeta|)^{N}},\qquad\forall t\in\widetilde{W},x\in\widetilde{V},\zeta\in\mathbb{R}\mathrm{T}^{\prime}_{\Sigma_{t}}\big|_{Z(x,t)}(\mathcal{C}).

Theorem
1.2
can be seen as a microlocal version of Theorem 3.4 of
[
16
]
.
This partial F.B.I. was first introduced by M. S. Baouendi, C.H. Chang and F. Treves in
[
10
]
, and it was widely used in the study of microlocal analytic hypoellipticity for CR structures (or extensions of CR functions on wedges), see for instance
[
7
,
8
,
10
,
11
]
. It was also used as a technical tool in order to obtain microlocal regularity of traces, see for instance
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
19
,
18
,
25
,
26
]
. In 2022, the author of the present paper algo gave in
[
16
]
a local characterization of Gevrey-
s
regularity for distributions
u
such that
\mathrm{L}_{j}u\in G^{s}
, for
j=1,\dots,n
, in terms of the decay of this partial F.B.I. transform. This partial F.B.I. was also studied by R. D. Medrado in his Ph.D. thesis
[
28
]
, where he introduced the notion of hypo-DC (here DC stands for Denjoy-Carlman) structures.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we shall recall some of the main definitions and results to make the reading of the present paper more accessible, and we refer to
[
30
]
and for a more detailed discussion on locally integrable structures and hypo-analytic structures. Let
\Omega
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-smooth manifold, by a locally integrable structure
\mathcal{V}
of rank
n
on
\Omega
we mean an
n
-dimensional
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-smooth subbundle of
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{T}\Omega
such that its orthogonal bundle (in the dual sense),
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\Omega
, is locally generated by the differentials of
m
,
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-smooth, functions. In the case that
\Omega
is
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
-smooth manifold, we say that
\mathcal{V}
is a real-analytic locally integrable structure if
\mathcal{V}
is
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
-smooth. In the present paper we shall deal only with real-analytic manifolds endowed with a real-analytic locally integrable structures.

A crucial object in the study of smooth and Gevrey regularity via the F.B.I. transform is the so-called real structure bundle of maximally real submanifolds of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
, so we shall briefly recall it’s definition. Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-smooth submanifold. We say that
\Sigma
is maximally real if, for every
p\in\Sigma
, one of the following equivalent conditions holds true:

•

\mathbb{C}\mathrm{T}_{p}\mathbb{C}^{m}\simeq\mathrm{T}_{p}^{(0,1)}\mathbb{C}^{m}\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathrm{T}_{p}\Sigma
;

•

The pullback map
j^{\ast}:\mathbb{C}\mathrm{T}_{p}^{\ast}\mathbb{C}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\mathrm{T}_{p}^{\ast}\Sigma
, where
j
is the inclusion map
\Sigma\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m}
, induces an isomorphism
j^{\ast}_{1,0}:{\mathrm{T}_{(1,0)}}_{p}\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}\mathrm{T}_{p}^{\ast}\Sigma
,

•

The one forms
\mathrm{d}(z_{1}|_{\Sigma}),\dots,\mathrm{d}(z_{m}|_{\Sigma})
are linearly independent at
p
.

Here we are using the notation
\mathrm{T}_{p}^{(0,1)}\mathbb{C}^{m}
for the set of the
(1,0)
-complex vector fields at
p
,
i.e.
the set of all holomorphic vector fields at
p
, and
{\mathrm{T}_{(1,0)}}_{p}\mathbb{C}^{m}
for the
(1,0)
-forms at
p
. The real structure bundle of
\Sigma
is then defined by the image of
\mathrm{T}^{\ast}\Sigma
under the isomorphism
(j_{1,0}^{\ast})^{-1}
, and it is denoted by
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{T}^{\prime}_{\Sigma}
.

Let
\Omega
be an
n+m
-dimensional
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
-smooth manifold endowed with a real-analytic locally integrable structure
\mathcal{V}
of rank
n
,
i.e.

\text{dim}\mathcal{V}_{p}=n
, for all
p\in\Omega
, and let
p_{0}
be an arbitrary point in
\Omega
. There is a local coordinate system
(U,x_{1},\dots,x_{m},t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
, vanishing at
p_{0}
, and a real-analytic map
\phi:U\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfying
\phi(0)=0
, and
\mathrm{d}_{x}\phi(0)=0
, such that the differential of the functions

(2)

Z_{k}(x,t)=x_{k}+i\phi_{k}(x,t),

for
k=1,\dots,m
, span
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}
on
U
. Shrinking
U
if necessary, we shall assume that
Z_{x}(x,t)
is invertible in
U
. Thus
\mathcal{V}
is spanned on
U
by

(3)

\mathrm{L}_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{j}}-i\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\partial\phi_{k}}{\partial t_{j}}(x,t)\mathrm{M}_{k},

for
j=1,\dots,n
, where

(4)

\mathrm{M}_{k}=\sum_{l=1}^{m}\left({}^{t}Z_{x}(x,t)^{-1}\right)_{kl}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}},

for
k=1,\dots,m
. These vector fields satisfies the equations:

\begin{array}[]{l l}\mathrm{L}_{j}Z_{k}=0&\mathrm{L}_{j}t_{r}=\delta_{jr}\\
\mathrm{M}_{l}Z_{k}=\delta_{lk}&\mathrm{M}_{l}t_{r}=0,\end{array}

so in particular if
h\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(U)
, then

\mathrm{d}h=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{L}_{j}h\mathrm{d}t_{j}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathrm{M}_{k}\mathrm{d}Z_{k}.

Since we are in a local coordinate system, we can identify the open set
U\subset\Omega
with an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
, and then we take two open neighborhoods of the origin
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that
V\times W\subset U
. Since the complex vector fields
\mathrm{L}_{j}
s are elliptic on the
t
variables, if
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(V\times W)
is such that
\mathrm{L}_{j}u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(V\times W)
, then
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(W;\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(V))
. For every
t\in W
, the set

\Sigma_{t}=\{Z(x,t)\;:\;x\in V\}

is a maximally real submanifold of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. The real structure bundle of
\Sigma_{t}
is then given by, see
[
30
]
,

\mathbb{R}\mathrm{T}^{\prime}_{\Sigma_{t}}=\{(Z(x,t),{}^{t}Z_{x}(x,t)^{-1}\xi)\;:\;x\in V,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\}.

Shrinking once again if necessary, we shall assume that for every
t\in W
, every
z,z^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{t}
, and every
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}\mathrm{T}^{\prime}_{\Sigma_{t}}|_{z}\cup\mathbb{R}\mathrm{T}^{\prime}_{\Sigma_{t}}|_{z^{\prime}}
the following holds true:

\begin{cases}|\mathrm{Im}\,\zeta|<\kappa|\mathrm{Re}\,\zeta|;\\
\mathrm{Re}\,\{i\zeta\cdot(z-z^{\prime})-\langle\zeta\rangle\langle z-z^{\prime}\rangle^{2}\}<-\kappa^{\prime}|\zeta||z-z^{\prime}|^{2},\end{cases}

for some
\kappa^{\prime}>0
and
0<\kappa<1
, where
\langle w\rangle^{2}=w\cdot w
, for
w\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
(this condition is called
well-positionedness
). For every
u\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(W;\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(V))
we define the adapted partial F.B.I transform of
u
as

(5)

\mathfrak{F}[u](t;z,\zeta)=\left\langle u(x,t),e^{i\zeta\cdot(z-Z(x,t))-\langle\zeta\rangle\langle z-Z(x,t)\rangle^{2}}\Delta(z-Z(x,t),\zeta)\det Z_{x}(x,t))\right\rangle_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{x}},

for every
t\in W
,
z\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
, and
\zeta\in\mathfrak{C}_{1}=\{\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\;:\;|\mathrm{Im}\,\eta|<|\mathrm{Re}\,\eta|\}
, where
\Delta(z,\zeta)
is the Jacobian of the transformation

\zeta\mapsto\zeta+iz\langle\zeta\rangle.

The adapted partial F.B.I. transform is holomorphic with respect to
z
and
\zeta
,
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-smooth with respect to
t
, and it satisfies

(6)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{j}}\mathfrak{F}[u](t,z,\zeta)=\mathfrak{F}[\mathrm{L}_{j}u](t,z,\zeta)\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{k}}\mathfrak{F}[u](t,z,\zeta)=\mathfrak{F}[\mathrm{M}_{k}u](t,z,\zeta)\end{cases}

for
j=1,\dots,n
, and
k=1,\dots,m
. We can extend
Z(x,t)
in the
x
variable to the whole
\mathbb{R}^{m}
by multiplying
\phi
by a
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}
function, with support contained in
V
and identically one in some open ball centered in the origin, in such a way that we can change
V
by
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in above discussion. It is clear that the new structure generated by the extended
Z
is not real-analytic any more, but it agrees with the original one in some open neighbourhood of the origin, that we are still going to denote by
V\times W
. The advantage of doing this extension is the following inversion formula (see
[
16
]
, and for an inversion formula modulo hypo-analytic functions see
[
30
]
):

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
k\in[0,\infty]
, and
u\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(W;\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(V))
. Then

(7)

u(x,t)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{(2\pi^{3})^{\frac{m}{2}}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}\mathrm{T}^{\prime}_{\Sigma_{t}}}e^{i\zeta\cdot(Z(x,t)-z^{\prime})-\langle\zeta\rangle\langle Z(x,t)-z^{\prime}\rangle^{2}-\varepsilon\langle\zeta\rangle^{2}}\mathfrak{F}[u](t;z^{\prime},\zeta)\langle\zeta\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\wedge\mathrm{d}\zeta,

where the convergence takes place in
\mathcal{C}^{k}(W;\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(V))
.

To emphasize the important role of the real structure bundle on the study of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
and Gevrey-smoothness and using these adapted F.B.I. transform we shall make a small digression. Suppose that
n=0
,
i.e.
we have only the
x
variables, and let
u
be a compacty supported distribution on
V
. Denoting by
F_{u}(x,\xi)
the usual F.B.I. transform of
u
, namely,

F_{u}(x,\xi)=\left\langle u(y),e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi-|\xi|^{2}|x-y|^{2}}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{y}},

we have that there exist constants
C,N>0
such that

|F_{u}(x,\xi)|\leq C_{N}(1+|\xi|)^{N},

for every
(x,\xi)\in V\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. To obtain a similar bound on the adapted F.B.I. transform one must ”stay on the real structure bundle”, as can be seen in the following two estimates (see Proposition IX.1.1 and Proposition IX.2.1 of
[
30
]
):

(1)

For every
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
compact set, and for every
0<\tilde{\kappa}<1
, there are constants
C,R>0
such that

|\mathfrak{F}[u](z,\zeta)|\leq Ce^{R|\zeta|},\quad\forall z\in K,\,\zeta\in\mathfrak{C}_{\tilde{\kappa}},

where
\mathfrak{C}_{\tilde{\kappa}}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\;:\;|\mathrm{Im}\,\zeta|<\tilde{\kappa}|\mathrm{Re}\,\zeta|\}
.

(2)

There exist an integer
k>0
and a constant
C>0
such that

|\mathfrak{F}[u](z,\zeta)|\leq C(1+|\zeta|)^{k},\quad\forall(z,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}\mathrm{T}^{\prime}_{\Sigma}.

It also worth noticing that if an estimate holds ”outside the real structure bundle”, then one obtain holomorphic extendability, as seen in the following Theorem (for a proof see the proof of Theorem IX.3.1 of
[
30
]
):

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
be a maximally real submanifolds, and assume that
\Sigma
is well positioned near the origin. Then there exists
U\subset\Sigma
an open neighborhood of the origin such that, for every
u\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(U)
, the following are equivalent:

1.

There exist
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
, an open neighborhood of the origin,
F
a holomorphic function on
\mathcal{O}
, such that
u|_{\mathcal{O}\cap\Sigma}=F|_{\mathcal{O}\cap\Sigma}
;

2.

There exist
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
, an open neighborhood of the origin,
0<\tilde{\kappa}<1
, and
C,\varepsilon>0
such that

\left|\mathfrak{F}[u](z,\zeta)\right|\leq Ce^{-\varepsilon|\zeta|},\quad\forall(z,\zeta)\in\mathcal{O}\times\mathfrak{C}_{\tilde{\kappa}}

3.

There exists
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
, an open neighborhood of the origin, such that
\mathfrak{F}[u](z,\xi)
is dominated by an integrable function with respect to
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, uniformly in
z\in\mathcal{O}
.

As mentioned before, this adapted partial F.B.I. transform was widely used on the study holomorphic extendability (or hypo-analyticity) for solutions of locally-integrable structures,
i.e.
distributions
u
such that,
\mathbb{L}u=0
, and to the study of the regularity of traces. Here we want to point out one more time, that when dealing with holomorphic extendability of solutions, it is enough to prove the holomorphic extendability of its trace, for if
u(x,0)=F(Z(x,0))
, for some holomorphic function
F
, then the function
F(Z(x,t))
is a solution, because
\bar{\partial}F=0
, and it agrees with
u
when
t=0
. Then by an application of the Baouendi-Treves approximation formula (see
[
10
]
and
[
30
]
), one can show that
u=F\circ Z
in some open neighborhood of the origin. This is not true in general for other types of regularity.

In the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we shall use the following result proved in
[
19
]
, in the case of real-analytic maximally real submanifolds of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
,
i.e.
the case
n=0
:

Theorem 2.3

(N. Braun Rodrigues and A. V. da Silva Jr.)
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(V)
, let
s>1
, and
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}
. The following are equivalent:

1.

(0,\xi_{0})\notin WF_{s}(u)
;

\left|e^{Q(z,Z(x^{\prime},t_{\ast}),\xi)}\right|\leq e^{-|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}|\xi|^{\kappa}-|\xi|^{\kappa}\left(c\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}}|\xi|^{1-\kappa}-4C_{\phi}^{2}\delta_{0}^{2}\right)}.

Here we recall that
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\xi,t)
. By Proposition
4.2
,
\delta_{0}>\delta
, so

\displaystyle c\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}}|\xi|^{1-\kappa}-4C_{\phi}^{2}\delta_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=\delta_{0}^{2}\left[c|\xi|^{1-\kappa}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}-2}-4C_{\phi}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\delta_{0}^{2}\left[c|\xi|^{1-\kappa}\delta^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}-2}-4C_{\phi}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\delta^{2}4C_{\phi}^{2},

if
|\xi|\geq\left[\frac{8C_{\phi}^{2}}{c\delta^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}-2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-\kappa}}
. Therefore, assuming
|\xi|\geq\left[\frac{8C_{\phi}^{2}}{c\delta^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}-2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-\kappa}}
,

|\mathfrak{F}_{\kappa}[\chi u](t_{\ast};z,\xi)|\leq\text{Const}\cdot e^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^{\kappa}},

for some
\varepsilon>0
. Summing up, we have obtained the following: There exist positive constants
C,\varepsilon>0
such that for every
x\in V_{2}
,
\frac{1}{s}<\kappa<\frac{2\theta-1}{s}+2-2\theta
,
\xi\in\Gamma
with
|\xi|\geq\left[\frac{8C_{\phi}^{2}}{c\delta^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}-2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-\kappa}}
and
t\in W_{1}\setminus\Sigma_{\dot{\xi}}
the following estimates hold true:

\begin{cases}|\mathfrak{F}_{\kappa}[\chi\mathrm{L}_{j}u](t^{\prime};z,\xi)|\leq Ce^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^{\frac{1}{s}}};\\
|\mathfrak{F}_{\kappa}[u\mathrm{L}_{j}\chi](t^{\prime};z,\xi)|\leq Ce^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^{\frac{1}{s}}},\\
|\mathfrak{F}_{\kappa}[\chi u](t_{\ast};z,\xi)|\leq Ce^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^{\frac{1}{s}}}.\end{cases}

Since the length of the curves
\gamma_{\dot{\xi},t}
are uniformly bounded for
\xi\in\Gamma
and
t\in W_{2}\setminus\Sigma_{\dot{\xi}}
, we have that, for every
x\in V_{2}
,
\frac{1}{s}<\kappa<\frac{2\theta-1}{s}+2-2\theta
,
\xi\in\Gamma
with
|\xi|\geq\left[\frac{8C_{\phi}^{2}}{c\delta^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}-2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-\kappa}}
and
t\in W_{1}\setminus\Sigma_{\dot{\xi}}

|\mathfrak{F}_{\kappa}[\chi u](t;Z(x,t),\xi)|\leq\text{Const}\cdot e^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^{\frac{1}{s}}}.

In view of
\Sigma_{\dot{\xi}}
being an analytic variety for every
\xi\in\Gamma
, and the set
|\xi|\leq\left[\frac{8C_{\phi}^{2}}{c\delta^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}-2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-\kappa}}
being compact, if
\frac{1}{s}<\kappa<\frac{2\theta-1}{s}+2-2\theta
, there exist
C,\varepsilon>0
such that

|\mathfrak{F}_{\kappa}[\chi u](t;Z(x,t),\xi)|\leq Ce^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^{\frac{1}{s}}},

for every
x\in V_{2}
,
t\in W_{1}
, and
\xi\in\Gamma
. Therefore, by Theorem
3.3
, we have that
(0,0,\xi_{0},0)\notin\mathrm{WF}_{s}(u)
. Now if
\theta=1/2
, and
(0,0,\xi_{0},0)\notin\text{WF}(\mathbb{L}u)
, all estimates but one remains the same, and the one that changes is
|\mathfrak{F}_{\kappa}[\chi\mathrm{L}_{j}u](t^{\prime};z,\xi)|
. As we already pointed out, when
\theta=1/2
, the term
e^{-|\xi|^{\kappa}\left[c\tau^{2}\xi|^{1-\kappa}-4C_{\phi}^{2}\tau^{2}\right]}
is ”harmless” when compared with polynomial decay, which is exactly the type of decay that will replace
e^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^{\frac{1}{s}}}
.
∎

4.1.
Maire’s example

In
\mathbb{R}^{4}
consider the following complex vector fields:

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}_{1}=\partial_{t_{1}}+i\left(3\partial_{x_{1}}-(4t_{1}t_{2}+3)t_{1}^{2}\partial_{x_{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}_{2}=\partial_{t_{2}}-it_{1}^{4}\partial_{x_{2}}.

We already discussed that the system
\{\mathrm{L}_{1},\mathrm{L}_{2}\}
is not Gevrey-
s
hypoelliptic for every
s\geq 4
, therefore it cannot satisfy property
(\star)
at all
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. In this section we shall prove this fact explicitly, to be more precise, we shall prove that the Lojasiewicz inequality with parameters with the exponent
1/2\leq\theta<1
fails. The first integrals associated with these complex vector fields are

\displaystyle Z_{1}(x,t)=x_{1}-i3t_{1},

\displaystyle Z_{2}(x,t)=x_{2}+i(t_{1}t_{2}+1)t_{1}^{3}.

The characteristic set of the system
\{\mathrm{L}_{1},\mathrm{L}_{2}\}
is given by
\eta_{1}=\eta_{2}=t_{1}=\xi_{1}=0
, where
(\xi,\eta)
are the dual variables of
(x,t)
. So we only have to check property
(\star)
at
(0,\pm 1)
. Now note that

\displaystyle\|{}^{\mathrm{t}}\mathrm{d}\Phi(t)\xi\|^{2}=t_{1}^{8}\xi_{2}^{2}+\left(-3\xi_{1}+t_{1}^{2}\xi_{2}(3+4t_{1}t_{2})\right)^{2},

\displaystyle|\Phi(t)\cdot\xi|^{2}=9t_{1}^{2}\xi_{1}^{2}+t_{1}^{6}(1+t_{1}t_{2})^{2}\xi_{2}^{2}.

So let
\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}=1
. If we impose
-3\xi_{1}+t_{1}^{2}\xi_{2}(3+4t_{1}t_{2})=0
, we obtain that

\xi_{1}^{2}=\frac{t_{1}^{4}(3+4t_{1}t_{2})^{2}}{9+t_{1}^{4}(3+4t_{1}t_{2})^{2}}.

If we now set
t_{2}=0
,
\xi_{1}=\frac{t_{1}^{2}}{\sqrt{1+t_{1}^{4}}}
, and
\xi_{2}=\sqrt{1-\frac{t_{1}^{4}}{1+t_{1}^{4}}}
, then

\displaystyle\|{}^{\mathrm{t}}\mathrm{d}\Phi(t)\xi\|^{2}=t_{1}^{8}\left(1-\frac{t_{1}^{4}}{1+t_{1}^{4}}\right)+9t_{1}^{4}\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t_{1}^{4}}}+\sqrt{1-\frac{t_{1}^{4}}{1+t_{1}^{4}}}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle|\Phi(t)\cdot\xi|^{2}=9\frac{t_{1}^{6}}{1+t_{1}^{4}}+t_{1}^{6}\left(1-\frac{t_{1}^{4}}{1+t_{1}^{4}}\right).

Now as
t_{1}
goes to
0
we have that
\|{}^{\mathrm{t}}\mathrm{d}\Phi(t)\xi\|\approx t_{1}^{4}
and
|\Phi(t)\cdot\xi|\approx t_{1}^{3}
, therefore the exponent
\theta
must be at least
4/3
, which is greater than
1
. Thus the system
\{\mathrm{L}_{1},\mathrm{L}_{2}\}
does not satisfy property
(\star)
at
(0,1)
. Analogously we have that the same is valid at
(0,-1)
.

4.2.
A class of examples

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
P\in\mathbb{R}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\doteq\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;:\;|\nabla P(t)|\leq 1\}
. Suppose that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\sup_{t\in\mathcal{A}}|P(t)|\leq C.

Then, the following inequatilly holds true:

(29)

|P(t)|^{1-\frac{1}{k}}\leq C^{1-\frac{1}{k}}|\nabla P(t)|,\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Before proving this Lemma, we point out that this is actually an equivalence, and it is related to the problem of finding the explicit Lojasiewicz exponent for polynomials at infinity, see for instance
[
23
,
24
]
.

Proof.

We only need to check (
29
) for
t\notin\mathcal{A}
. So let
t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be such that
|\nabla P(t)|>1
, and set
\lambda=|\nabla P(t)|^{\frac{-1}{k-1}}
. Since
\nabla P
is homogeneous with degree
k-1
, we have that

\displaystyle|\nabla P(\lambda t)|

\displaystyle=\lambda^{k-1}|\nabla P(t)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\nabla P(t)|}|\nabla P(t)|

\displaystyle=1,

i.e.

\lambda t\in\mathcal{A}
. Thus,
|P(\lambda t)|\leq C
, and in view of the homogeneity of
P
, we have that

\displaystyle|P(t)|^{1-\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{k-1}}|P(\lambda t)|^{1-\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{1-\frac{1}{k}}|\nabla P(t)|.

∎

We shall use Lemma
4.7
to construct a class of examples satisfying condition
(\star)
. So let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\phi(t)=(\phi_{1}(t),\dots,\phi_{m}(t))
be such that each
\phi_{j}\in\mathbb{R}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]
is a homogeneous of degree
k
. For each
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
we define
P_{\xi}(t)\doteq\phi(t)\cdot\xi
. Then
P_{\xi}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
. So
\phi
satisfies condition
(\star)
with
\theta=(k-1)/k
at a point
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
if the origin is not a local minimum of
P_{\xi_{0}}
and
P_{\xi}
is uniformly bounded in
\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;:\;|\nabla P_{\xi}(t)|\leq 1\}
, for every
\xi
in some open neighborhood of
\xi_{0}
. This boundedness condition is valid if, for instance, the set
\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;:\;|\nabla P_{\xi}(t)|\leq 1\}
is bounded for every
\xi
in some open neighborhood of
\xi_{0}
.

Example 4.8
.

Consider in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
two polynomials
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
, homogeneous of degree
k
. Suppose that, for some
0<\rho<1
,

\rho\|\nabla\phi_{2}(t)\|_{1}\leq\|\nabla\phi_{1}(t)\|_{1},\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Then, if
0<\xi_{2}<\rho^{\prime}\xi_{1}
, for any
0<\rho^{\prime}<\rho
, we have that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{t}P_{\xi}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\xi_{1}\partial_{t_{1}}\phi_{1}+\xi_{2}\partial_{t_{1}}\phi_{2}|^{2}+|\xi_{1}\partial_{t_{2}}\phi_{1}+\xi_{2}\partial_{t_{2}}\phi_{2}|^{2},

and

\displaystyle|\xi_{1}\partial_{t_{1}}\phi_{1}+\xi_{2}\partial_{t_{1}}\phi_{2}|

\displaystyle\geq\xi_{1}|\partial_{t_{1}}\phi_{1}|-\xi_{2}|\partial_{t_{1}}\phi_{2}|

\displaystyle\geq\xi_{1}\big(|\partial_{t_{1}}\phi_{1}|-\rho^{\prime}|\partial_{t_{1}}\phi_{2}|\big).

Thus,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{t}P_{\xi}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\xi_{1}^{2}\Big(\big(|\partial_{t_{1}}\phi_{1}|-\rho^{\prime}|\partial_{t_{1}}\phi_{2}|\big)^{2}+\big(|\partial_{t_{2}}\phi_{1}|-\rho^{\prime}|\partial_{t_{2}}\phi_{2}|\big)^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\xi_{1}^{2}\Big(|\partial_{t_{1}}\phi_{1}|+|\partial_{t_{2}}\phi_{1}|-\rho^{\prime}\big(|\partial_{t_{1}}\phi_{2}|+|\partial_{t_{2}}\phi_{2}|\big)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\xi_{1}^{2}\Big(\|\nabla\phi_{1}(t)\|_{1}-\rho^{\prime}\|\nabla\phi_{2}(t)\|_{1}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\xi_{1}^{2}(1-\rho^{\prime}/\rho)\|\nabla\phi_{1}(t)\|_{1}^{2}.

Therefore, the set
\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;:\;|\nabla P_{\xi}(t)|\leq 1\}
is contained in the set

\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\;:\;\|\nabla\phi_{1}(t)\|_{1}^{2}\leq\frac{2\rho}{\xi_{1}^{2}(\rho-\rho^{\prime})}\right\},

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that
0<\xi_{2}<\rho^{\prime}\xi_{1}
. So assuming that for every constant
C>0
, the set
\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\;:\;\|\nabla\phi_{1}(t)\|_{1}\leq C\}
is bounded, then the map
\phi
satisfies condition
(\star)
with
\theta=1-\frac{1}{k}
at
(1,0)
.

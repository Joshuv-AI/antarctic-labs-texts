Changhao Chen, Zhengyan Miao

Abstract
. We study the arithmetic structure of the exceptional set of projections. For any bounded subset E ⊂ ℝ d \displaystyle E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} , let Ω = { ξ ∈ ℝ : dim B ( E + ξ ​ E ) = dim B E } . \Omega=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\dim_{B}(E+\xi E)=\dim_{B}E\}. 1.
Introduction

A fundamental problem in fractal geometry is to determine how the projections affect dimension
[
3
,
14
]
. For
\displaystyle r\in\mathbb{R}
define the projection
\displaystyle\pi_{r}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as
\displaystyle\pi_{r}(x,y)=x+ry
. A classical result is the following Marstrand projection theorem. Suppose that
\displaystyle E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a Borel set.

(1) If
\displaystyle\dim_{H}E\leq 1
, then
\displaystyle\dim_{H}\pi_{r}(E)=\dim_{H}E
for almost all
\displaystyle r\in\mathbb{R}
.

(2) If
\displaystyle\dim_{H}E>1
, then
\displaystyle\pi_{r}(E)
has positive Lebesgue measure for almost all
\displaystyle r\in\mathbb{R}
.

Marstrand
[
10
]
proved this projection theorem in the plane.
Mattila
[
12
]
extended the projection theorem to higher dimensions via Kaufman’s
[
9
]
potential
theoretic methods. There is abundant literature about various extensions of Marstrand projection theorem, see Falconer,
Fraser, and Jin
[
4
]
(2015) for more details.

Let
\displaystyle E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a Borel set and
\displaystyle 0\leq u\leq\dim_{H}E
. The problem of exceptional projections asks for the best dimension upper bound for the set

\{r\in\mathbb{R}:\dim_{H}\pi_{r}(E)<u\}.

The investigation of this problem was initiated by Kaufman
[
9
]
and later by Kaufman and Mattila
[
12
]
.

A major step in the methodology was obtained by Bourgain
[
1
]
who introduced the discretized sum-product technique to investigate the problem.
Suppose
\displaystyle E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a Borel set. Then Bourgain
[
1
]
proved that

\dim_{H}\{r\in\mathbb{R}:\dim_{H}\pi_{r}(E)\leq\dim_{H}E\,/2\}=0.

In general the exceptional set
\displaystyle\{r\in\mathbb{R}:\dim_{H}\pi_{r}(E)\leq\dim_{H}E/2\}
may not be empty. Orponen
[
15
, Theorem 1.5]
showed an Ahlfors
\displaystyle 1
-regular set
\displaystyle E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the set

E_{H}(0)=\{r\in\mathbb{R}:\dim_{H}\pi_{r}(E)=0\}

is a dense
\displaystyle G_{\delta}
set, in particular
\displaystyle E_{H}(0)
is uncountable.

For the dimension bound of the exceptional set, building on a series of works by Orponen and Shmerkin
[
16
,
17
]
, and applying tools from harmonic analysis, Ren and Wang
[
19
]
recently obtained the following sharp bound: for any Borel set
\displaystyle E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\displaystyle u\leq\min\{\dim_{H}E,1\}
,

\dim_{H}\{r\in\mathbb{R}:\dim_{H}\pi_{r}(E)\leq\dim_{H}E/2\}\leq\max\{2u-\dim_{H}E,0\}.

Besides the size, it is of interest to determine also the geometry of the possible exceptional sets.
Mattila
[
14
, p86]
asked the following general question about the structure of exceptional sets of projections.
“What more in addition to dimension estimates could be said about the exceptional sets? …. Can something be said about their structure, for example, could smooth sets or simple self-similar sets appear as exceptional sets…
”

We intend to study the structure of the exceptional set from the arithmetic viewpoint. Let
\displaystyle M_{d\times d}(\mathbb{R})
denote the
\displaystyle d\times d
real matrix ring under general addition and multiplication. For
\displaystyle E,F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the sum sets
\displaystyle E+F
is defined as

E+F=\{x+y:x\in E,y\in F\}.

For
\displaystyle E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\displaystyle a\in M_{d\times d}(\mathbb{R})
, denote
\displaystyle aE=\{ax:x\in E\}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\displaystyle E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and its box dimension exists. Then the set

\{a\in M_{d\times d}(\mathbb{R}):\overline{\dim}_{B}(E+aE)=\dim_{B}E\}

is a subring of the matrix ring
\displaystyle M_{d\times d}(\mathbb{R})
. In particular, let

\Omega=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\overline{\dim}_{B}(E+\xi E)=\dim_{B}E\}.

Then either
\displaystyle\Omega=\{0\}
or
\displaystyle\Omega
is a subfield of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
under the normal addition and multiplication.

The following Theorem
1.2
shows that the set
\displaystyle\Omega
of Theorem
1.1
can be a real subfield of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, the statement does not hold for Hausdorff dimension and lower box dimension.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any
\displaystyle s\in(0,d)
, there exits a compact subset
\displaystyle E\subseteq[0,1]^{d}
such that
\displaystyle\dim_{H}E=\dim_{B}E=s
and the set
\displaystyle E
has the following properties:

(i) the rational number field
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\dim_{B}(E+\xi E)=\dim_{B}E\}\subsetneq\mathbb{R}
;

(ii) the set
\displaystyle\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\dim(E+\xi E)=\dim E\}
is not a subfield of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
when
\displaystyle\dim
denotes Hausdorff dimension or lower box dimension.

For the
\displaystyle\Omega
in the Theorem
1.1
, it should be already known that there exists
\displaystyle E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the corresponding
\displaystyle\Omega
contains
\displaystyle 0
only. Since we could not find an explicit reference, we include it for the sake of completeness.

Example 1.3
.

For any
\displaystyle s\in(0,d)
, there exists a subset
\displaystyle E\subset[0,1]^{d}
with
\displaystyle\dim_{H}E=\dim_{B}E=s
such that
\displaystyle\dim_{H}(E+\xi E)>s
holds for all
\displaystyle\xi\neq 0
.

Proof.

For
\displaystyle s\in(0,d)
, there exits a subset
\displaystyle E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\displaystyle\dim_{H}E=\dim_{B}E=s
and
\displaystyle E
has positive Fourier dimension, i.e., there is a measure
\displaystyle\eta
on
\displaystyle E
such that

\widehat{\eta}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\pi ixy}d\mu(y)\lesssim(1+|x|)^{-\tau/2},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

for some positive
\displaystyle\tau>0
. Here and hereafter
\displaystyle X\lesssim Y
means that there exits a constant
\displaystyle C
such that
\displaystyle|X|\leq C|Y|
. Indeed, let
\displaystyle E
be a typical random fractal percolation with prefixed parameters, then by Falconer
[
3
, Theorem 15.2 and Proposition 15.4]
, we obtain
\displaystyle\dim_{H}E=\dim_{B}E=s
. Moreover, Suomala and Shmerkin
[
21
, Theorem 14.1]
shows that
\displaystyle E
has positive Fourier dimension.

Since
\displaystyle\dim_{H}E=s
, for any
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
by Mattila
[
14
, Theorem 2.8 and Theorem 3.10]
, there exits a Radon measure
\displaystyle\mu
on
\displaystyle E
(i.e.,
\displaystyle\text{spt\,}\mu\subseteq E
) such that

I_{s-\varepsilon}(\mu)=\int_{R^{d}}|x|^{s-\varepsilon-d}|\widehat{\mu}(x)|^{2}dx<\infty.

Let
\displaystyle\xi\neq 0
, then
\displaystyle\nu=\eta\circ S_{\xi}^{-1}
is a measure on
\displaystyle\xi E
where
\displaystyle S_{\xi}(x)=\xi x
. Note that
\displaystyle\widehat{\nu}(x)\lesssim(1+|x|)^{-\tau/2}
holds for all
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It follows that

I_{s-\varepsilon+\tau}(\mu*\nu)=\int_{R^{d}}|x|^{s-\varepsilon+\tau-d}|\widehat{\mu}(x)|^{2}|\widehat{\nu}(x)|^{2}dx\lesssim I_{s-\varepsilon}(\mu)<\infty.

Observe that
\displaystyle\text{spt\,}(\mu*\nu)\subseteq E+\xi E
, by
[
14
, Theorem 2.8]
, we derive that

\dim_{H}(E+\xi E)\geq s+\tau-\varepsilon>s.

The last inequality holds for small
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
, and this finishes the proof.
∎

For Marstrand’s projection theorem, if the initial set
\displaystyle E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
has additional structure, then stronger version of Marstrand’s theorem is known, see Falconer, Fraser, and Ji
[
4
]
for more details. Recently, Orponen
[
18
]
proved the following result for the projections of Ahlfors regular sets:
let
\displaystyle E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be Ahlfors
\displaystyle s
-regular for some
\displaystyle s\in[0,2]
. Then
\displaystyle\dim_{H}E_{B}=0
, where

E_{B}=\{r\in\mathbb{R}:\overline{\dim}_{B}\pi_{r}(E)<\min\{s,1\}\}.

We remark that Wu
[
23
]
has announced that
\displaystyle E_{B}
is countable when
\displaystyle E
is Ahlfors regular. The following result shows that Wu’s result is sharp.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\displaystyle s\in(0,1]
and
\displaystyle(r_{k})\subset\mathbb{R}
be a sequence of real numbers. Then there exists a Ahlfors
\displaystyle s
-regular set
\displaystyle E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

\overline{\dim}_{B}\pi_{r_{k}}(E)<s,\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Recall that a closed subset
\displaystyle E
of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called Ahlfors
\displaystyle s
-regular for some positive
\displaystyle s
, if there is a Radon measure
\displaystyle\mu
on
\displaystyle E
with
\displaystyle\text{spt\,}\mu=E
, such that for any
\displaystyle x\in E
and
\displaystyle r>0
, we have

r^{s}/C\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{s}

for some positive constant
\displaystyle C
.

2.
Preliminary and proof of Theorem
1.1

2.1.
Preliminary

Firstly we recall the definition of box dimension and Hausdorff dimension, see Falconer
[
3
]
for more details.

For any
\displaystyle\delta>0
and
\displaystyle E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\displaystyle N_{\delta}(E)
be the
\displaystyle\delta
-covering number of
\displaystyle E
, that is the smallest number of balls of diameter
\displaystyle\delta
that cover
\displaystyle E
. The lower and upper box dimensions
are defined respectively as

\underline{\dim}_{B}E=\liminf_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{\log N_{\delta}(E)}{-\log\delta}\text{ \quad and \quad}\overline{\dim}_{B}E=\limsup_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{\log N_{\delta}(E)}{-\log\delta}.

If
\displaystyle\underline{\dim}_{B}E=\overline{\dim}_{B}E
, then we denote this common value by
\displaystyle\dim_{B}E
and call it the box dimension of
\displaystyle E
.

For
\displaystyle E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the Hausdorff dimension of
\displaystyle F
is defined as

\dim_{H}E=\inf\left\{s>0:\forall\varepsilon>0,\exists\{U_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}},U_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d},s.t.\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\text{diam}(U_{i})^{s}<\varepsilon\right\}

The relationship between box dimension and Hausdorff dimension is shown in the following inequality, see
[
3
, Equation (3.17)]
,

\dim_{H}E\leq\underline{\dim}_{B}E\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}E.

Moreover, for
\displaystyle F\subseteq\mathbb{R}
let
\displaystyle F^{d}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}):x_{i}\in F,1\leq i\leq d\}
be the Cartesian product set of
\displaystyle F
. If
\displaystyle\dim_{H}F=\dim_{B}F=t
for some
\displaystyle t\in[0,1]
, then by
[
3
, Chapter 7]
,

(1)

\dim_{H}F^{d}=\dim_{B}F^{d}=d\,t.

We will need tools from additive combinatorics, in particular the following standard covering number version of Ruzsa triangle inequality and Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality, see Guth, Katz and Zahl
[
7
, Corollary 3.4 and Proposition 3.5]
, and Orponen
[
17
, Remark 4.40]
. For more details, see the thesis of He
[
8
, Chapter 2]
.

Lemma 2.1

(Ruzsa triangle inequality)
.

Let
\displaystyle A,B,C
be subsets of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

N_{\delta}(A\pm B)\lesssim\frac{N_{\delta}(A\pm C)N_{\delta}(C\pm B)}{N_{\delta}(C)}.

Here and hereafter,
\displaystyle X\lesssim Y
means that
\displaystyle X\leq cY
for some absolutely constant
\displaystyle c
.

Lemma 2.2

(Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality)
.

Let
\displaystyle A,B_{i},1\leq i\leq n
be subsets of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose that
\displaystyle N_{\delta}(A+B_{i})\leq K_{i}N_{\delta}(A)
for each
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq n
. Then

N_{\delta}(B_{1}+\ldots+B_{n})\lesssim N_{\delta}(A)\prod_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}.

For more details on additive combinatorics, see Tao and Vu
[
20
]
, and Zhao
[
24
]
.

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We adapt the discrete argument of Ben Green
[
6
, Lemma 3.4]
to our setting.

Suppose that the matrices
\displaystyle a,b\in\Omega
and
\displaystyle a
is invertible. It is sufficient to show (i)
\displaystyle a^{-1},-a\in\Omega
; (ii)
\displaystyle ab\in\Omega
; and (iii)
\displaystyle a+b\in\Omega
. Note that the definition of box dimension implies that

(2)

N_{\delta}(E+xE)\leqslant\delta^{-s+o(1)},\quad\delta\rightarrow 0,

when
\displaystyle x\in\{a,b\}
.

Since box dimension is invariant under invertible linear transform, and we obtain

\dim_{B}(a^{-1}E+E)=\dim_{B}(E+aE)=s.

By Ruzsa triangle inequality, we obtain

(3)

N_{\delta}(E+E)\leq\frac{N_{\delta}(E+aE)N_{\delta}(aE+E)}{N_{\delta}(aE)}\leq\delta^{-s+o(1)},

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle N_{\delta}(E-aE)\leq\frac{N_{\delta}(E-(-E))N_{\delta}((-E)-aE)}{N_{\delta}(-E)}\leq\delta^{-s+o(1)},\quad\delta\rightarrow 0.

Thus
\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{B}(E-aE)\leq s
. Moreover, since

s=\underline{\dim}_{B}E\leqslant\underline{\dim}_{B}(E-aE)\leqslant\overline{\dim}_{B}(E-aE)\leqslant s,

we have
\displaystyle-a\in\Omega
which proves the claim (i).

For the claim (ii), by Ruzsa triangle inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle N_{\delta}(E+abE)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{N_{\delta}(E+aE)N_{\delta}(aE+abE)}{N_{\delta}(aE)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\delta^{-s+o(1)},\quad\delta\rightarrow 0.

Then
\displaystyle\dim_{B}(E+abE)=s
which establishes (ii).

Finally, to prove the claim (iii), applying (
2
), (
3
) and Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality (taking
\displaystyle A=E,B_{1}=E,B_{2}=aE,B_{3}=bE
), we derive

\displaystyle\displaystyle N_{\delta}(E+(a+b)E)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq N_{\delta}(E+aE+bE)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{N_{\delta}(E+E)N_{\delta}(E+aE)N_{\delta}(E+bE)}{N_{\delta}(E)^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\delta^{-s+o(1)},\quad\delta\rightarrow 0,

and hence
\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{B}(E+(a+b)E)\leq s
. Since
\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{B}(E+(a+b)E)\geq\dim_{B}E=s
, we obtain
\displaystyle\dim_{B}(E+(a+b)E)=s
which finishes the proof the claim (iii).

We will need the following partial homogeneous Cantor sets on
\displaystyle[0,1]
, which can be considered as an infinite generalized arithmetic progression.

Partial homogeneous Cantor sets:
Let
\displaystyle(m_{k})\subset\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle(n_{k})\subset\mathbb{N}
be sequence of integers with
\displaystyle 1\leq m_{k}<n_{k}
for all
\displaystyle k
. Following Feng, Wen and Wu
[
5
]
, we say that the set

(4)

F=F(m_{k},n_{k})=\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{n_{1}n_{2}\dots n_{k}}:0\leqslant a_{k}<m_{k}\right\}

is a partial homogeneous Cantor set. We remark that Feng, Wen and Wu
[
5
]
used the geometric construction of the partial homogeneous which is equivalent to the above definition. Let

t_{1}=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log m_{1}m_{2}\dots m_{k}}{-\log m_{k+1}+\log n_{1}n_{2}\dots n_{k+1}},\quad t_{2}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log m_{1}m_{2}\ldots m_{k}}{-\log n_{1}n_{2}\dots n_{k}}.

Feng, Wen and Wu
[

, Lemma 2.2, Theorem 3.1]
showed that

(5)

\dim_{H}F=\underline{\dim}_{B}F=t_{1},\quad\overline{\dim}_{B}F=t_{2}.

Notation:
In the following let
\displaystyle F=F(\lceil 2^{ks/d}\rceil,2^{k})
for some fixed
\displaystyle s\in(0,d)
. We intend to show that the Cartesian product set
\displaystyle E=F^{d}

will be the desired set for Theorem
1.2
. Firstly, by (

) and (
5
), we obtain

\dim_{H}E=\dim_{B}E=s.

, it is sufficient to prove that

Recall that a subset of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
is of the first Baire category if it is a countable union of nowhere dense sets; otherwise it is called of the second Baire category. For the basic properties and various applications of Baire categories we refer to
[
22
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle E
be given by (
6
). Denote
\displaystyle E_{b}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\underline{\dim}_{B}(E+\xi E)=\underline{\dim}_{B}E\}
, and
\displaystyle E_{h}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\dim_{H}(E+\xi E)=\dim_{H}E\}
.
Then both
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\setminus E_{b}
and
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\setminus E_{h}
are of the first Baire category sets.

Proof.

By (
5
) we have
\displaystyle\dim_{H}E=\dim_{B}E=s
. Suppose that
\displaystyle\xi\in E_{b}
, then

s=\dim_{H}E\leq\dim_{H}(E+\xi E)\leq\underline{\dim}_{B}(F+\xi F)\leq s,

thus
\displaystyle\xi\in E_{h}
, and hence
\displaystyle E_{b}\subseteq E_{h}
. Therefore, it is sufficient to prove that
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\setminus E_{b}
is of first category.

For
\displaystyle m,k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\displaystyle\varepsilon_{m}=1/m,\delta_{k}=1/2^{k},m,k\in\mathbb{N}
, and

X_{m,k}=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:N_{\delta_{k}}(E+\xi E)\leq\delta_{k}^{-s-\varepsilon_{m}}\right\}.

Then

E_{b}=\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{k=n}^{\infty}X_{m,k}.

For any
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{Q}
, by (i) we have
\displaystyle\dim_{B}(E+\xi E)=s
, thus
\displaystyle N_{\delta}(E+\xi E)\leq\delta^{-s+o(1)}
, and hence
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\subseteq\bigcup_{k\geq n_{0}}X_{m,k}
holds for any
\displaystyle n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. Let

X_{m,k}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{r\in X_{m,k}}(r-\delta_{k},r+\delta_{k}).

Then for any
\displaystyle n_{0}
the set
\displaystyle\bigcup_{k\geq n_{0}}X_{m,k}^{\prime}
is a dense open set. For any
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{N}
denote

H_{m}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{k=n}^{\infty}X_{m,k}\text{ \quad and \quad}H_{m}^{\prime}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{k=n}^{\infty}X_{m,k}^{\prime}.

Then
\displaystyle H_{m}\subseteq H_{m}^{\prime}
and
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\setminus H_{m}^{\prime}
is of the first Baire category. For any
\displaystyle m\geq 2
we claim that
\displaystyle H_{m}^{\prime}\subseteq H_{m-1}
. Indeed, if
\displaystyle\xi\in H_{m}^{\prime}
then there exist infinitely many
\displaystyle k
, such that
\displaystyle\xi\in X_{m,k}^{\prime}
. Write
\displaystyle\xi=r_{k}+\theta_{k}
where
\displaystyle r_{k}\in X_{m,k}
and
\displaystyle|\theta_{k}|\leq\delta_{k}
. Observe that

\displaystyle\displaystyle N_{\delta_{k}}(E+\xi E)\leq N_{\delta_{k}}(E+r_{k}E+\theta_{k}E)\leq C\delta_{k}^{-s-\varepsilon_{m}},

where
\displaystyle C
is a constant which depends on
\displaystyle d
only. It follows that
\displaystyle N_{\delta_{k}}(E+\xi E)\leq\delta_{k}^{-s-\varepsilon_{m-1}}
holds for all large enough
\displaystyle k
. Thus
\displaystyle\xi\in H_{m}^{\prime}
,
and hence

\displaystyle H_{m}^{\prime}\subseteq H_{m-1}
. We conclude that for any
\displaystyle m\geq 2
the set
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\setminus H_{m-1}
is of first category. Since
\displaystyle E_{b}=\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{N}}H_{m}
, we obtain that
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\setminus E_{b}
is of fist category.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle E
be given by (
6
). Denote
\displaystyle E_{h}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\dim_{H}(E+\xi E)=\dim_{H}E\}
. Then
\displaystyle E_{h}
is not a subfield of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, we have
\displaystyle\dim_{H}(E+\xi E)>s
for some
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

For
\displaystyle S\subset\mathbb{R}
let
\displaystyle S^{c}=\mathbb{R}\setminus S
. For any
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}
, by Lemma
3.1
the set
\displaystyle E_{h}^{c}
is of the first category, and thus
\displaystyle(x-E_{h})^{c}
is of the first category as well. Then by the fact that
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
is of the second category, we conclude that the set

E_{h}\cap(x-E_{h})=(E_{h}^{c}\cup(x-E_{h})^{c})^{c}\neq\emptyset,

and hence
\displaystyle x\in(E_{h}+E_{h})
. By the arbitrary choice of
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain
\displaystyle E_{h}+E_{h}=\mathbb{R}
.

Suppose to the contrary that
\displaystyle E_{h}
is a subfield of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
, then

(7)

\dim_{H}(E+\xi E)=\dim_{H}E=s,\quad\forall\,\xi\in(E_{h}+E_{h})=\mathbb{R}.

On the other hand, observe that
\displaystyle E+\xi E=(F+\xi F)^{d}
holds for any
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{R}
. Applying
[
14
, Theorem 2.10]
, we derive

(8)

\dim_{H}(E+\xi E)\geq d\dim_{H}(F+\xi F).

Note that
\displaystyle\dim_{H}F^{2}=2s/d
. Recalling that Marstrand projection theorem claims that for almost all

which is contradict to the identity (

).
∎

•

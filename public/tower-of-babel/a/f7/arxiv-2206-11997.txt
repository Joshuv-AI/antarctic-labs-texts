Prescribed graphon symmetries and flavors of rigidity
Alexandru Chirvasitu

Abstract
We prove that an arbitrary compact metrizable group can be realized as the automorphism group of a graphing; this is a continuous analogue to Frucht’s theorem recovering arbitrary finite groups are automorphism groups of finite graphs. The paper also contains a number of results the persistence of transitivity of a compact-group action upon passing to a limit of graphons. Call a compact group 𝔾 \mathbb{G} graphon-rigid if, whenever it acts transitively on each member Γ n \Gamma_{n} of a convergent sequence of graphons, it also acts transitively on the limit lim n Γ \lim_{n}\Gamma . We show that for a compact Lie group 𝔾 \mathbb{G} graphon rigidity is equivalent to the identity component 𝔾 0 \mathbb{G}_{0} being semisimple; as a partial converse to a result of Lovász and Szegedy, this is also equivalent to weak randomness: the property that the group have only finitely many irreducible representations in each dimension. Similarly, call a compact group 𝔾 \mathbb{G} image-rigid if for every compact Lie group ℍ \mathbb{H} the images of morphisms 𝔾 → ℍ \mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{H} form a closed set (of closed subgroups, in the natural topology). We prove that graphon rigidity implies image rigidity for compact groups that are either connected or profinite, and the two conditions are equivalent (and also equivalent to being torsion) for profinite abelian groups.



Key words: graphon; graphing; compact group; metrizable; Borel space; standard; kernel; Lie group

A

Aspects of the rich, multi-faceted ensuing theory are discussed in

One direction the discussion below takes in the general spirit of an “inverse” symmetry problem: given a group (enjoying various properties, as appropriate), realize it as the automorphism group of a structure (here, graphons). The pattern recurs frequently:

•

Every finite group is the automorphism group of some finite graph by a classical result of Frucht’s

•

That result can be improved by imposing various constraints on the graphs realizing the given group (connectivity, chromatic number, etc.)

•

And in fact

•

Adjacently to graphs, (convex)

•

There are variations on this theme: the group might have a distinguished involution which is required to act as the central symmetry in a

•

And then there is of course the celebrated

The graphon analogue of all of this is

Every compact metrizable group is the automorphism group of some graphon.

Or in other words, since

Another point of interest is the limiting behavior of graphon automorphism groups. The issue comes up in

]

By contrast,

To simplify the awkward language, we refer to compact groups

A compact Lie group is graphon-rigid precisely when it is weakly random or, equivalently, when its identity connected component is semisimple.

Being Lie is essential:

The various (counter)examples suggest a related notion of rigidity (

{\mathbb{G}}

This property, in some cases, appears to be more pertinent to graphon rigidity than weak randomness. Aggregating

Let

be a compact group.

•

If

•

If furthermore

Prescribed graphon symmetries

First, recall from

Definition 2.1
.

The

\blacklozenge

We work with metric probability spaces

Proposition 2.2
.

A graphon
\Gamma=(X,W,\mu)
with

•

X
compact Hausdorff second-countable;

•

W
continuous and

X\ni x\mapsto W(x,-)\in C(X)\ :=\ \text{continuous functions on $X$}

(2-1)

one-to-one.

•

and
\mu

Proof
.

For compact Hausdorff spaces being second-countable is equivalent to metrizability

30

, and the continuity of

30
, Theorem 27.6]
. It follows that

2-1
is continuous when equipping the right-hand side with the uniform topology, so the identity map is continuous from the original (compact Hausdorff) topology on

Note also that

is complete (being compact

\blacksquare

In particular, taking for the kernel

as in

The following observation will be implicit in some of the discussion below.

.

Under the hypotheses of

acts continuously on

Proof

Indeed, it acts continuously on the metric space

\blacksquare

On the one hand,

Conversely, according to

2.1
Revisiting a definition

The approach taken in
[
26
, Definition 7]
to defining the automorphism group of a graphon is different from that suggested by

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(X,W,\mu)
be a pure graphon. Its
automorphism group

\Aut_{LS}(X,W,\mu)
consists of those measure-preserving bijections

\forall x\in X,\ W(\varphi x,\ \varphi\ \cdot)=\varphi(x,\cdot)\text{ almost everywhere}.

\blacklozenge

The ‘LS’ subscript (for Lovász-Szegedy) is temporary; we will see that the definition specializes to something more akin to
[

, §1]
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\Gamma:=(X,W,\mu)
be a graphon (pure or not). Its
automorphism group

\Aut(\Gamma)=\Aut(V,{\mathcal{B}},E,\mu)

is defined as follows.

•

Consider the group

of mod-0 equivalence classes of measure-preserving automorphisms of the standard probability space
(X,\mu)
.

•

Set

\widetilde{\Aut}(\Gamma):=\{g\in\Aut(X,\mu)\ |\ W(g\ \cdot,\ g\ \cdot)=W\text{ almost everywhere}\}.

We equip
\widetilde{\Aut}(\Gamma)
with the
weak topology
(e.g.
[
12
, Weak Topology]
).

•

Consider, next, the closed normal subgroup

\widetilde{\Aut}(\Gamma)\trianglerighteq\widetilde{\Aut}_{triv}(\Gamma):=\left\{g\in\widetilde{\Aut}(\Gamma)\ |\ (g\ \cdot,\ \cdot)=\text{ almost everywhere}\right\}.

Finally, set

\Aut(\Gamma):=\widetilde{\Aut}(\Gamma)/\widetilde{\Aut}_{triv}(\Gamma)

with the quotient topology.

\blacklozenge

For pure graphons much of the quotienting (by trivial automorphisms, etc.) is not necessary:

Lemma 2.8
.

If
\Gamma=(X,W,\mu)
is a pure graphon then
\widetilde{\Aut}_{triv}(\Gamma)
is trivial.

Proof
.

Let
\varphi:X\to X
be a measure-preserving and a.e.
W
-preserving bijection with

W(-,-)=W(\varphi-,-)\text{ almost everywhere}.

(2-2)

We then have

\displaystyle\int_{X}r_{W}(x,\varphi x)\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\int_{X}|W(x,y)-W(\varphi x,y)|\ \mathrm{d}\mu(y)\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)\quad\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{def:nbhd}}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:wxwx}}.

Since

is assumed to be a metric and

we identify (by definition) automorphisms modulo measure zero, so we are done:

.

\blacksquare

Furthermore, the reason why

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\Gamma=(X,W,\mu)
be a pure graphon and
\varphi:X\to X
a measure-preserving bijection with

\varphi

.

Proof

We are making two claims: existence and uniqueness. We will drop

\Aut_{LS}=\Aut_{LS}(\Gamma)

(1)

Uniqueness.
The assertion to prove is that if two elements of

For

(2-3)

because

, and the assumption is that

\varphi=\id_{X}\ a.e.

(2-4)

We then have

\displaystyle r_{W}(x,\varphi x)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}|W(x,y)-W(\varphi x,y)|\ \mathrm{d}\mu(y)\quad\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{def:nbhd}}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}|W(x,y)-W(\varphi x,\varphi y)|\ \mathrm{d}\mu(y)\quad\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:phi1}}

3.1

By contrast,

Remark 3.3

actually uses the functions

And additionally, the kernels are meant to take values in

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be a compact metrizable space and

a symmetric continuous function. If a sequence

\Gamma_{n}:=(X,W,\mu_{n})\xrightarrow[\hskip 8.19447ptn\hskip 8.19447pt]{}(X,W,\mu)=:\Gamma.

Proof
.

This is obvious from the characterization of convergence via morphism densities
[

:

for every finite simple graph

.

\blacksquare

Let

A

Following

In light of

\blacklozenge

By contrast to the automorphism group of a graphon, which is compact by

If the edge-set

\blacklozenge

A

Proposition 4.5

For a full subgraphing

As explained in

The graph(ing) consisting of a single vertex and no edges is locally equivalent to the two-vertex discrete graph assigning probability

to each of the vertices (by

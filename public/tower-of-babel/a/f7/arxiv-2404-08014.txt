Lower bounds on
\mathbb{Q}
-gonality via
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-gonality

For a curve
C
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and
p
a prime of good reduction of
C
, it is known that

\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C)\leq\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{Q}}(C).

This is an important tool for determining the
\mathbb{Q}
-gonality because it is generally much easier to find the gonality over finite fields. When working with the modular curve
X_{0}(N)
and its quotients, a very helpful result is Ogg’s inequality
[
19
, Theorem 3.1]
, stated in simpler form in
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p
be a prime not dividing
N
. Then the number of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
-points on the curve
X_{0}(N)
is at least

L_{p}(N):=\frac{p-1}{12}\psi(N)+2^{\omega(N)}.

Here
\psi(N)
is the index of the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, equal to
N\prod_{q\mid N}(1+\frac{1}{q})
, and
\omega(N)
is the number of different prime divisors of
N
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
17
, Lemma 3.5]

Let
C
be a curve over
\mathbb{Q}
,
p
a prime of good reduction for
C
, and
q
a power of
p
. Suppose
\#C(\mathbb{F}_{q})>d(q+1)
. Then
\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{Q}}(C)>d
.

If the quotient curve
X_{0}^{+d}(N)
is tetragonal, then we have a rational composition map
X_{0}(N)\to X_{0}^{+d}(N)\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
8
. Therefore, by
Lemma

2.2
we must have

(1)

\displaystyle L_{p}(N)

\displaystyle\leq 8(p^{2}+1)

for all primes
p\nmid N
. Also, notice that we can exclude all levels
N
which are prime powers since in that case the only quotient of
X_{0}(N)
is the curve
X_{0}^{+}(N)
which has been solved in
[
20
]
. From now on, we will suppose that
N
is not a prime power.

Proposition 2.3
.

For every
N>432
that is not a prime power there exists a prime
p
for which the inequality
1
does not hold.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
. Since
N
is not a prime power, we have
\omega(N)\geq 2
. Now there are several cases:

•

2\nmid N,N>432
: take
p=2
;
\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{12}\psi(N)+2^{\omega(N)}\geq\frac{N}{12}+4>40=8(p^{2}+1)
.

•

2\mid N,3\nmid N,N>304
: take
p=2
;
\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{12}\psi(N)+2^{\omega(N)}\geq\frac{N}{4}+4>80=8(p^{2}+1)
.

•

(2\cdot 3)\mid N,5\nmid N,N>306
: take
p=5
;
\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{12}\psi(N)+2^{\omega(N)}\geq\frac{2N}{3}+4>208=8(p^{2}+1)
.

•

(2\cdot 3\cdot 5)\mid N,7\nmid N,N>326
: take
p=7
;
\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{12}\psi(N)+2^{\omega(N)}\geq\frac{6N}{5}+8>400=8(p^{2}+1)
.

•

(2\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdot 7)\mid N,11\nmid N,N>420
: take
p=11
;
\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{12}\psi(N)+2^{\omega(N)}\geq\frac{16N}{7}+16>976=8(p^{2}+1)
.

•

(2\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdot 7\cdot 11)\mid N
: take
p
to be the smallest prime not dividing
N
and let
q
be the largest prime dividing
N
. Now we have

\frac{p-1}{12}\psi(N)+2^{\omega(N)}\geq\frac{p-1}{12}\cdot\frac{3\cdot 4\cdot 6\cdot 8\cdot 12}{2\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdot 7\cdot 11}N+32\geq\frac{576(p-1)q}{11}+32.

In the last inequality we used that
N\geq 2\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdot 7\cdot q
. Since
p<2q
by Bertrand’s postulate, it is now elementary to prove that this is greater than
8(p^{2}+1)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
X
be a curve of genus
g
over a field
k
.

(i)

If
L
is a field extension of

, then
\textup{gon}_{L}(X)\leq\textup{gon}_{k}(X)
.

(ii)

If
k
is algebraically closed and
L
is a field extension of
k
, then
\textup{gon}_{L}(X)=\textup{gon}_{k}(X)
.

(iii)

If
g\geq 2
, then
\textup{gon}_{k}(X)\leq 2g-2
.

(iv)

If
g\geq 2
and
X(k)\neq\emptyset
, then
\textup{gon}_{k}(X)\leq g
.

(v)

If
k
is algebraically closed, then
\textup{gon}_{k}(X)\leq\frac{g+3}{2}
.

(vi)

If

is a dominant
k
-rational map, then
\textup{gon}_{k}(X)\leq\deg\pi\cdot\textup{gon}_{k}(Y)
.

(vii)

If
\pi:X\to Y
is a dominant
k
-rational map, then
\textup{gon}_{k}(X)\geq\textup{gon}_{k}(Y)
.

Simple Methods for Finding Actual Congruent Number Triangles
Allan J. MacLeod

Abstract
We present a, hopefully, elementary mathematical treatment of the computational aspects of congruent numbers, such that an amateur could understand the problem and perform their own calculations.



1
Introduction

A
congruent number
is a positive integer which is the area of a right-angled triangle with rational sides.
Consider the right-angled triangle

By Pythagoras,
a^{2}+b^{2}=h^{2}
and the area
\Delta=ab/2
. The most well-known such triangle is probably the
(3,4,5)
triangle,
introduced at school. It has area
6
. A natural question is
what other integers can occur as the area of a right-angled
triangle with integer sides?
A follow-up question would be
for such integers, can we give explicit values for the sides?

If we scale the
(3,4,5)
sides by
2
, we get
(6,8,10)
with
6^{2}+8^{2}=10^{2}
and area
24
. Scale by
3
and the area goes up by
factor of
9
to
54
. Thus, we can get any square multiple of
6
by scaling the
(3,4,5)
triangle by an integer factor.

The standard parametric form of Pythagorean triples, with no common factor, is
a=p^{2}-q^{2},b=2p\,q,h=p^{2}+q^{2}
with

p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\gcd(p,q)=1
and
p,q
of opposite parities, which allows us to set up the simple Table

The basic problem can also be phrased differently.

Lemma
There is a right-angled triangle with rational sides and area
N
if and only if there are
3
squares forming an arithmetic
progression (AP) with common difference
N
.

Proof

Let
(a,b,h)
be the sides of such a triangle. Then
(a+b)^{2}=h^{2}+4N
and
(a-b)^{2}=h^{2}-4N
, so, if we define
p=(a-b)/2
,
q=h/2
and

r=(a+b)/2
, we have
p^{2},q^{2},r^{2}
is an arithmetic progression with common difference
N
.

Conversely, let
p^{2},q^{2},r^{2}
be such an AP. Thus
r^{2}=q^{2}+N
and
p^{2}=q^{2}-N
, and
2N=r^{2}-p^{2}=(r+p)(r-p)
. Define
a=r+p,b=r-p
,
giving
a\,b/2=N
and
a^{2}+b^{2}=4q^{2}=(2q)^{2}
, so define
h=2q
.
\,\Box

It is the AP property that gives this problem its name, which is derived from the Latin word for such a progression
congruum
.
For information on the history of the problem until the early 20th century, look at Chapter XVI of Dickson
[
7
]
.
This question has been around for over
1000
years and is still not completely solved. There a several extremely advanced arguments which can
suggest which
N
are congruent, but no totally conclusive theorem. Computationally, we can prove a value
N
is congruent by
computing a specimen triangle, and this is our goal - to compute an actual triangle.

There is, thus, a dual problem.

For
\Delta=N
, does a solution exist? If it does, what is it?

Practically all work has been done on the first question.
For example, the following are congruent numbers - primes of the form
8K+5
or
8K+7
, whilst
N
is not
a congruent number if
N
is a prime of the form
8K+3
or of the form
2p
with
p
prime and
p=16K+9
. There are a plethora of other
conditions, usually involving primes, see Chandrasekar
[
4
]
for some further examples.

We concentrate on the second problem, which is certainly not as trivial as some think. Very advanced methods can be used, such as Elkies’
wonderful Heegner-point method
[
8
]
, but we will concentrate on as simple methods as possible.

If you are interested in pursuing this subject, you will need
a computer and some software. Any modern computer should do, and the software should be able to handle very large integers. I use the
package Pari-gp
[
13
]
which is excellent, easy-to-use and free!

2
Hartley’s method

This method is over
200
years old but provides a simple introduction to some of the ideas which appear in the rest of this report.
It is described on page
464
of Dickson for the specific case of
N=13
. The original appeared in
1803
in a supplement to the Ladies Diary
magazine printed in London. It is easy to generalize to
N
, but, as we will see,
N
is restricted to a subset of possible values.

The method involves looking for an AP of squares
(x^{2}-N,x^{2},x^{2}+N)
. Set
x^{2}+N=(x+y)^{2}
and
x^{2}-N=(x-y\,z)^{2}
. Then simple algebra gives

x=\frac{N-y^{2}}{2y}=\frac{N+y^{2}z^{2}}{2yz}

so that
y^{2}=N(z-1)/(z(z+1))
.

Hartley then looks for a value of
z
of the form
(r^{2}+s^{2})/(2rs)
with
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
. This would give

y^{2}=\frac{2Nrs(r-s)^{2}}{(r^{2}+s^{2})(r+s)^{2}}

To satisfy this equation, Hartley sets
r^{2}+s^{2}=N
and
2rs=\Box
. This is done without any background theory, just trying to see if it works.
The first relation, thus, restricts possible
N
to those integers which can be
written as the sum of
2
squares, such as
5,13,29
. Let
r_{0},s_{0}
be such a representation.

Hartley defines
r=r_{0}-g\,t
and
s=s_{0}-t
, where
g,t
are rational. Thus,
t=2(g\,r_{0}+s_{0})/(g^{2}+1)
, and substituting into
2\,r\,s=\Box
, gives
that
g
must satisfy the quartic

w^{2}=-2(r_{0}g^{2}+2s_{0}g-r_{0})(s_{0}g^{2}-2r_{0}g-s_{0})

(2.1)

The requirement that a quartic be made square is another common feature of most methods.
It is easy to write a simple search for a quartic to be a square. Unfortunately, there are two major problems.
Firstly, the quartic might not give
any
square values, and secondly, it might take a long time to find one, even if it exists.

The test for whether a solution exists is quite advanced. It is possible to avoid it at the risk of considering quartics with no solutions. Searching
can be done very efficiently, up to a reasonable level, with the Pari subroutine
hyperellratpoints
which is an implementation of Michael Stoll’s
excellent ratpoints code.

As an example, consider
N=37
with
r_{0}=6,s_{0}=1
. The quartic is

w^{2}=-4(g^{2}-12g-1)(3g^{2}+g-3)

and it is straightforward evaluation to show that
(12,\pm 42)
are solutions.

This gives
t=146/145
,
r=-882/145
and
s=-1/145
. Thus
z=777925/1764
and
y=37002/128035
. Finally
x=605170417321/9475102140
. All this,
using Lemma
1
, gives a triangle with
a=777923/6090
and
b=450660/777923
.

The method can also be applied to
N=101=10^{2}+1^{2}
and
N=157=11^{2}+6^{2}
.
N=101
was only solved by Bastien
[
3
]
in
1915
, whilst
N=157
was solved
by Don Zagier in the
1970s
. We find
g=149/141
gives a solution for
N=101
,
but
g=-186067/136146
is needed for
N=157
. This would take a long time for a simple search.

We can speed up such a simple search as follows.
The first thing to note is that if
g=u/v
gives a solution, then
g=-v/u
also gives a solution, so we need only search over
|g|\leq 1
.
Secondly, the quartic has
4
real roots, namely

\frac{-s_{0}\pm\sqrt{N}}{r_{0}}\hskip 56.9055pt\frac{r_{0}\pm\sqrt{N}}{s_{0}}

so we have simple bounds for the quartic to be positive.

We can perform a further operation, known as a descent.
The quartic (
2.1
) is of the form

(a\,x^{2}+b\,x+c)(d\,x^{2}+e\,x+f)=w^{2}

(2.2)

which we consider as

a\,x^{2}+b\,x+c=k\,y^{2}\hskip 56.9055ptd\,x^{2}+e\,x+f=k\,z^{2}

(2.3)

where
k
is squarefree.

Suppose
(x_{0},y_{0})
is a rational solution of the first quadric (as such functions are called). Then, the line
y=y_{0}+t(x-x_{0})
will meet the quadric
at one further point, with

x=\frac{a\,x_{0}+b+k\,t(t\,x_{0}-2y_{0})}{k\,t^{2}-a}

(2.4)

Substitute this into the second quadric. Let
x_{0}=p_{0}/q_{0}
and
y_{0}=w_{0}/q_{0}
with
p_{0},q_{0},w_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then the following
integer coefficient quartic, in
t
, must be made square, if possible.

k^{3}(dp_{0}^{2}+ep_{0}q_{0}+f\,q_{0}^{2})t^{4}-2k^{3}w_{0}(2d\,p_{0}+e\,q_{0})t^{3}+

k^{2}(2a(d\,p_{0}^{2}-f\,q_{0}^{2})+b\,q_{0}(2d\,p_{0}+e\,q_{0})+4d\,k\,w_{0}^{2})t^{2}-

(2.5)

2k^{2}w_{0}(a(2d\,p_{0}-e\,q_{0})+2b\,d\,q_{0})t+

k(a^{2}(d\,p_{0}^{2}-e\,p_{0}q_{0}+f\,q_{0}^{2})+a\,b\,q_{0}(2d\,p_{0}-e\,q_{0})+b^{2}d\,q_{0}^{2})

Of course, we have not yet said what values to use for
k
. These come from the resultant of the two quadratics, which is the determinant
of the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{rrrr}r_{0}&2s_{0}&-r_{0}&0\\
0&r_{0}&2s_{0}&-r_{0}\\
-2s_{0}&4r_{0}&2s_{0}&0\\
0&-2s_{0}&4r_{0}&2s_{0}\end{array}\right)

(2.6)

which can be computed to be
-16(r_{0}^{2}+s_{0}^{2})^{2}=-16N^{2}
. Thus
k
is a squarefree divisor of
2N
, positive or negative.

This extra descent finds a solution of the
N=157
triangle with
t=-52/131
.
We find a solution for
N=1093
with the numerator and denominator
of both altitude and base of the triangle having roughly
50
decimal digits.

3
Elliptic Curve Formulation

We now show the link between the triangle problem and elliptic curves. There are many approaches, and I particularly like this one, from
Keith Conrad
[
5
]
.

Let
a,b,h>0
with
a^{2}+b^{2}=h^{2}
and
ab=2N
, with
N\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Then
t=h-a>0
, and

2\,a\,t=b^{2}-t^{2}\hskip 56.9055pt\frac{4Nt}{b}=b^{2}-t^{2}

Multiply by
N^{3}b/t^{3}
giving

\frac{4N^{4}}{t^{2}}=\frac{N^{3}b^{3}}{t^{3}}-N^{2}\frac{Nb}{t}

Now, define
Y=2N^{2}/t
and
X=Nb/t
so that

Y^{2}=X^{3}-N^{2}X=X(X-N)(X+N)

(3.1)

and we have
3
finite rational points on the curve, namely
(0,0)
,
(N,0)
, and
(-N,0)
.

Conversely, let
(u,\pm v)
be rational points on the elliptic curve with
|u\,v|>0
. Define
a=v/u
choosing the sign
of
v
to make
a>0
. Define
b=2N/a
, so
a,b
are strictly positive with
N=a\,b/2
and

a^{2}+b^{2}=\frac{v^{2}}{u^{2}}+\frac{4N^{2}u^{2}}{v^{2}}=\frac{v^{4}+4N^{2}u^{4}}{u^{2}v^{2}}

Using
v^{2}=u^{3}-N^{2}u
, we have

a^{2}+b^{2}=\frac{(u^{3}-Nu)^{2}+4N^{2}u^{4}}{u^{2}v^{2}}=\frac{(u^{3}+N^{2}u)^{2}}{u^{2}v^{2}}=\left(\frac{u^{2}+N^{2}}{v}\right)^{2}

so we can set
h=|(u^{2}+N^{2})/v|
.

As an example, the graph of
y^{2}=x^{3}-4x
is shown. All congruent number elliptic curves have this basic shape.
There are
2
components, a closed convex egg-shape for
-N\leq x\leq 0
and an infinite component for
x\geq N
.

Other transformations are possible. We have

(a+b)^{2}=h^{2}+4N\hskip 56.9055pt(a-b)^{2}=h^{2}-4N

and thus

\left(\frac{h^{2}}{4}-N\right)\frac{h^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{h^{2}}{4}+N\right)=\frac{h^{2}}{4}\frac{(a^{2}-b^{2})^{2}}{16}=\Box

so
h^{2}/4
gives an x-coordinate of a point on
Y^{2}=X^{3}-N^{2}X
.

The problem is that this x-coordinate is a square, whereas many curves have rational points which are non-squares. For example,
the
(3,4,5)
triangle gives the point
(12,\pm 36)
.

4
Geometry of the elliptic curve

For
N=6
, we know some points on
Y^{2}=X^{3}-36X
, namely
(0,0)
,
(-6,0)
,
(6,0)
. The
(3,4,5)
triangle gives
(12,\pm 36)
.
We can generate new points by simple geometrical calculations.

Suppose we join
(0,0)
by a straight line to
(12,36)
. The line has gradient
3
and can be expressed as
Y=36+3(X-12)
, and will meet the curve where

X^{3}-36X-(36+3(X-12))^{2}=0\Rightarrow X^{3}-9X^{2}-36X=0=X(X-12)(X+3)

so the line meets the curve also at
X=-3
where
Y=-9
. By symmetry,
(-3,9)
also lies on the curve. These points both give the
(3,4,5)
triangle.

Joining
(12,36)
to
(-6,0)
gives the points
(-2,\pm 8)
, whilst joining to
(6,0)
gives
(18,\pm 72)
, and these lead to a
(4,3,5)
triangle.

We can also look at the tangent to the curve at a point. We have

\frac{dY}{dX}=\frac{3X^{2}-36}{2Y}

so, at
(12,36)
the tangent will have equation
Y=36+11(X-12)/2
and meets the curve where
(X-12)^{2}(4X-25)=0
. The double root comes from being
a tangent, so there is only one further point of intersection at
(25/4,35/8)
. This point gives a right-angled triangle with
a=7/10
,
b=120/7

and
h=1201/70
but still with area
6
.

These chord and tangent methods can be continued forever to produce an infinite sequence of right-angled triangles all of area
6
, but where
the numbers involved get larger and larger.

5
2-isogeny Curve

There is another related elliptic curve in this problem. Suppose
a^{2}+b^{2}=h^{2}
and
ab/2=N
, then

a^{2}+\frac{4N^{2}}{a^{2}}=h^{2}\Rightarrow a^{6}+4N^{2}a^{2}=h^{2}a^{4}

so if we define
U=a^{2}
and
V=ha^{2}
we have the elliptic curve

V^{2}=U^{3}+4N^{2}U

(5.1)

Now, let
(P,Q)
be a point on (
3.1
) with
P\neq 0
. Then, if

U=\frac{Q^{2}}{P^{2}}\hskip 56.9055ptV=\frac{Q(P^{2}+N^{2})}{P^{2}}

(5.2)

we have a rational point on (
5.1
).

In fact, we can go in the other direction. If
(U,V),U\neq 0
is a rational point on (
5.1
), defining

X=\frac{V^{2}}{4U^{2}}\hskip 56.9055ptY=\frac{V(U^{2}-4N^{2})}{8U^{2}}

(5.3)

gives a rational point on (
3.1
).
The transformations show that
X
will have roughly double the number of decimal digits as
U
.

For example, if
N=53
, it is easy to find a point
U=4335188/14161

V=9552116860/1685159
on
V^{2}=U^{3}+4*53^{2}U
.
This gives

X=99278557225/1158313156\hskip 14.22636ptY=24583549770420915/39422029951304

on the congruent number curve
Y^{2}=X^{3}-53^{2}X
. The increase in the size of the numbers involved is clear.

It is, occasionally, possible to find a point with smaller size numbers by using the tangent to a point method discussed in the previous section.

Let
(P,Q)
be a rational point on (
3.1
). The tangent to this point has gradient
(3P^{2}-N^{2})/(2Q)
so the line

Y=Q+\frac{3P^{2}-N^{2}}{2Q}\,(X-P)

meets (
3.1
) at only one further point, which standard algebra gives as

X=\frac{(P^{2}+N^{2})^{2}}{4P(P+N)(P-N)}=\frac{(P^{2}+N^{2})^{2}}{4Q^{2}}

(5.4)

which is a square, and, sometimes, we can solve the quartic

(P^{2}+N^{2})^{2}-4XP(P-N)(P+N)

(5.5)

For example,
N=62
gives the following data. The point

(7150393600/32798529,695599219282240/187837175583)

lies on
V^{2}=U^{3}+4*62^{2}U
so that

X=4229297547568411201/58630597962753600

gives a rational point on
Y^{2}=X^{3}-62^{2}X
.

The quartic (
5.5
) factors into
4
linear terns giving possible
P
values

\frac{124002}{529}\hskip 28.45274pt\frac{-706831}{19600}\hskip 28.45274pt\frac{2430400}{22801}\hskip 28.45274pt\frac{-1016738}{62001}

all of which are small.

The two curves are said (in the parlance of elliptic curves) to be 2-isogenous to each other.

6
Basic Methods to Find Triangles

All the methods are based on the fact that rational points on (
3.1
) are such that

X=\frac{d\,u^{2}}{v^{2}}\hskip 56.9055ptY=\frac{d\,u\,w}{v^{3}}

with
d,u,v,w\in\mathbb{Z}
,
d
is squarefree, and
\gcd(u,v)=\gcd(w,v)=\gcd(d,v)=1
.

Putting these into (
3.1
) gives (after cancellation)

d^{2}w^{2}=d^{3}u^{4}-N^{2}d\,v^{4}

(6.1)

Dividing by
d^{2}
shows that
d|N
, so we have a (usually) small set of possible
d
values, including
\pm 1
and
\pm N
. If
N

is prime these are the only possible values.

First try
d=1
, so we look for solutions of

w^{2}=u^{4}-N^{2}v^{4}

and we just search with
u,v
positive and
\gcd(u,v)=1
, with
u+v\leq L
, looking for a square.
We can also search for
d=N
using

w^{2}=N(u^{4}-v^{4})

Using
L=9999
, we found the following extra congruent numbers up to
N=97
.

13,23,29,31,37,47,55,61,71,87,95

though we often get the triangles with
a,b
reversed and, for
N=41
, we get two completely different triangles.

If we look closely at the results (always a good idea), we find the first
12
congruent numbers found are

5,6,7,13,14,15,21,22,23,29,30,31

and these are all of the form
8K+5
,
8K+6
or
8K+7
, and it is now a famous conjecture that this is true, though the proof is
taking some time.

It would be nice at this point to have a better idea of how many congruent numbers there are in
[0,99]
. Tunnell’s results
provide a simple computational criterion, though based on the truth of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture. You don’t
need to understand what is going on, just be able to apply it.

For a given square-free integer
N
, define

A_{N}=\#\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:N=2x^{2}+y^{2}+32z^{2}\}

B_{N}=\#\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:N=2x^{2}+y^{2}+8z^{2}\}

C_{N}=\#\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:N=8x^{2}+2y^{2}+64z^{2}\}

D_{N}=\#\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:N=8x^{2}+2y^{2}+16z^{2}\}

where
\#S
denotes the number of elements in the set
S
. You just have to be careful about what happens if one of
x,y,z
is zero.

Tunnell’s theorem states that supposing
N
is a congruent number,
2A_{N}=B_{N}
if
N
is odd, and
2C_{N}=D_{N}
if
N
is even. In the opposite
direction, if the BSD Conjecture is true these set-size equalities are enough to prove
N
is congruent or not.

We thus find that we still need the following values

38,53,62,69,77,79,86,93,94

Hartley’s method disposes of
N=53
giving the triangle

a=\frac{1472112483}{202332130}\hskip 56.9055ptb=\frac{21447205780}{1472112483}

All but
53
and
79
in these values are composite, so we should include information on possible factors. In (
6.1
), set

N=d*e
giving

w^{2}=d(u^{4}-e^{2}v^{4})

so that
d|w
. Set
w=dz
, so we look for solutions of
dz^{2}=u^{4}-e^{2}v^{4}
, just by looping round factors of
N
. This finds
solutions for these values quickly, except for
N=79
.

7
Descent Methods

In this section, we give a further example of a descent - trying to reduce the problem to one with
smaller numbers in the solution. The congruent number problem has a large number of possible descents, with which the reader
is encouraged to experiment.

Given the basic identity,

d^{2}w^{2}=d^{3}u^{4}-N^{2}dv^{4}

we use the fact that
d|N
to write
N=de
with
e
also an integer, leading to

w^{2}=d(u^{4}-e^{2}v^{4})

Thus,
d|w
so we can write
w=dz
with

dz^{2}=u^{4}-e^{2}v^{4}=(u+ev^{2})(u-ev^{2})

(7.1)

Set
u^{2}+ev^{2}=dp^{2}
and
u^{2}-ev^{2}=q^{2}
, so
z=p\,q
. Thus
ev^{2}=u^{2}-q^{2}=(u+q)(u-q)
, so set
u+q=e\,r^{2}
,
u-q=s^{2}
. We have

v=r\,s
and
u=(er^{2}+s^{2})/2
, with

\frac{(er^{2}+s^{2})^{2}}{4}+er^{2}s^{2}=d\,\Box

and finally the quartic

e^{2}r^{4}+6e\,r^{2}s^{2}+s^{4}=4\,d\,t^{2}

(7.2)

8
A Special Descent

The result that is almost the ”poster-child” for congruent numbers is Don Zagier’s solution for
N=157
, which is always given to show the sizes
of the numbers involved. Nobody describes how it was computed and, in fact, some get it wrong, ascribing the solution to the use of Heegner points - a
subject on which Zagier was one of the first experts, see Dalawat
[
6
]
. In a reply, on 10 Nov. 2015, to a question on the web-site MathOverflow,
Noam Elkies stated that it was actually a descent argument which Zagier had used. Carlo Beenakker gave the reference
[
17
]
and a translation from
the original German to English.

This description was extremely sparse, so I set out to try to work out a possible simple descent, which I now present. In (
6.1
), set
d=1

giving

w^{2}=u^{4}-N^{2}v^{4}=(u^{2}+Nv^{2})(u^{2}-Nv^{2})

(8.1)

Now set
u^{2}+Nv^{2}=r\,s^{2}
and
u^{2}-Nv^{2}=r\,t^{2}
, with
r
squarefree. Thus,
2u^{2}=r(s^{2}+t^{2})
,
2Nv^{2}=r(s^{2}-t^{2})
and
w=s\,t
.
Assume
N
is prime and let
r=N
. So,
N|(2u^{2})
, so, since
N>2
, we have
N|u
. Set
u=Nz
, with
2Nz^{2}=(s^{2}+t^{2})
.

Also,
2v^{2}=s^{2}-t^{2}
which we write
t^{2}+2v^{2}=s^{2}
.
We can derive a parametric solution to this last identity as follows. Let
X=v/t,Y=s/t
so
Y^{2}=1+2X^{2}
, which has a trivial solution

X=0,Y=1
. The line
Y=1+kX
passes through this point and meets this curve again at one point. This gives
t=2p^{2}-q^{2}
,
v=2pq
and
s=2p^{2}+q^{2}
.

We end up with
Nz^{2}=4p^{4}+q^{4}=(2p^{2})^{2}+(q^{2})^{2}
.

We now assume
N
is prime of the form
8K+5
, so
N
can be expressed as the sum of two integer squares, eg.
157=11^{2}+6^{2}=6^{2}+11^{2}
. Thus
N=c^{2}+d^{2}
,
and assume
z=x^{2}+y^{2}
. Thus

(c^{2}+d^{2})(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}=(2p^{2})^{2}+(q^{2})^{2}

(8.2)

which can be written, in terms of complex numbers

(c+di)(c-di)(x+yi)^{2}(x-yi)^{2}=((2p^{2})+(q^{2})i)((2p^{2})-(q^{2})i)

(8.3)

We now have

(c+di)(x+yi)^{2}=2p^{2}+q^{2}i

(8.4)

which gives, by equating the real and imaginary parts

c(x^{2}-y^{2})-2dxy=2p^{2}\hskip 56.9055ptd(x^{2}-y^{2})+2cxy=q^{2}

(8.5)

If we find a simple solution
(x_{0},y_{0},p_{0})
to the first equation, we can parameterize, as before, giving

\frac{x}{y}=\frac{cx_{0}-2(dy_{0}-k(kx_{0}-2p_{0}))}{y_{0}(2k^{2}-c)}

where
k
is a rational parameter.

Substituting into the second equations, and simplifying the result, gives the quartic

j^{2}=4(2cx_{0}y_{0}+d(x_{0}^{2}-y_{0}^{2}))k^{4}-16p_{0}(cy_{0}+dx_{0})k^{3}+

4d(c(x_{0}^{2}-y_{0}^{2})-2dx_{0}y_{0}+4p_{0}^{2})k^{2}+8p_{0}(c^{2}y_{0}-cdx_{0}+2d^{2}y_{0})k-

(8.6)

2c^{3}x_{0}y_{0}+c^{2}d(x_{0}^{2}+3y_{0}^{2})-4cd^{2}x_{0}y_{0}+4d^{3}y_{0}^{2}

Now, consider
N=157
. There is no solution
(x_{0},y_{0},p_{0})
for
c=11,d=6
, but
(4,1,1)
is a solution when
c=6,d=11
.
This gives the quartic

j^{2}=4(213k^{4}-200k^{3}+66k^{2}+28k-124)

and a simple search finds a solution when
k=-262/79
. This gives
x/y=322213/49921
,
p=356441
and
q=1143522
.

From this, we have
v=815196250404
, together with
s=1561742937446
and
t=-1053542191522
.
Finally
u=16691191806770
, and we have the point

\left(\,\frac{16691191806770^{2}}{815196250404^{2}}\,\,,\,\,\frac{538962435089604615078004307258785218335}{815196250404^{3}}\,\right)

on the curve
Y^{2}=X^{3}-157^{2}X
.

As a measure of the size of the numbers found, we use the
height
of a rational point on an elliptic curve. Very, very roughly,
the height gives an indication of the number of decimal digits in the numerator and denominator of the X-coordinate of a point.
For example, the rational point for the

Experiments find the largest height point found so far, with this method, to be for

In the section, we describe a descent on the 2-isogenous curve

of a rational point can often be of the form
2u^{2}/v^{2}

X=2u^{2}/v^{2}
and
Y=2uw/v^{3}

w^{2}=2(u^{4}+n^{2}v^{4})

2|w
, giving
w=2t
and

2t^{2}=u^{4}+N^{2}v^{4}

This shows much fewer factorisation possibilities than previous sections. By employing complex numbers, we can proceed. First note
that
2=(1+i)(1-i)
and assume
t=a^{2}+b^{2}=(a+bi)(a-bi)
. Thus

(1+i)(1-i)(a+bi)^{2}(a-bi)^{2}=(u^{2}+Nv^{2}i)(u^{2}-Nv^{2}i)

Define

u^{2}+Nv^{2}i=(1+i)(a+bi)^{2}\hskip 56.9055ptu^{2}-Nv^{2}i=(1-i)(a-bi)^{2}

giving

u^{2}=a^{2}-2ab-b^{2}\hskip 56.9055ptNv^{2}=a^{2}+2ab-b^{2}

(9.1)

The first quadric
u^{2}=a^{2}-2ab-b^{2}
can be parameterized in the standard way to give

\frac{b}{a}=\frac{-2(k+1)}{k^{2}+1}

with
k\in\mathbb{Q}
. This means that

\frac{k^{4}-4k^{3}-6k^{2}-12k-7}{N}=\Box

As an example, consider
N=103
. Note that the algebra is exactly the same if
N
is negative, and we find

gives a solution to the last equation for
N=-103
. This gives

.

Using the numerator and denominator,

and

and the point

\left(\frac{361213442}{66049},\frac{6869952561580}{16974593}\right)

on

This then gives the point

\left(\frac{16332534559428025}{11928893315329},\frac{2081363685506152106939880}{41200286097029552767}\right)

We now return to solving equation (
7.2
), based on the paper by Komoto, Watanabe and Wada
[
9
]
.
I generalized this in a recent arXiv preprint
[

]
.

Define
r^{2}=f
and
s^{2}=g
, so the basic equation is

e^{2}f^{2}+6e\,f\,g+g^{2}=4\,d\,t^{2}

which we write as

(f,g,t)\left(\begin{array}[]{lrr}e^{2}&3e&0\\
3e&1&0\\
0&0&-4\,d\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{r}f\\
g\\
t\end{array}\right)=0

(10.1)

Let
(1,g_{0},t_{0})
be an initial solution of this quadratic. Setting
f=1
makes things a lot easier later on, though we cannot always find such a solution.
Define

\left(\begin{array}[]{l}f\\
g\\
t\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{lrr}1&0&0\\
g_{0}&1&0\\
t_{0}&0&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{l}h\\

(10.2)

so that the basic equation is now

(h,j,k)\left(\begin{array}[]{lrr}0&3e+g_{0}&-4dt_{0}\\
3e+g_{0}&1&0\\
-4dt_{0}&0&-4\,d\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{r}h\\
j\\
k\end{array}\right)=0

(10.3)

or

(6e+2g_{0})hj-8dt_{0}hk+j^{2}-4dk^{2}=0

so that

and

2\gamma\,h\,m-2t_{0}\,d\,h\,k+m^{2}-d\,k^{2}=0

(h,m,k)\left(\begin{array}[]{lrr}0&\gamma&-d\,t_{0}\\
\gamma&1&0\\
-d\,t_{0}&0&-d\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{r}h\\
m\\
k\end{array}\right)=0

\gcd(\gamma,-d\,t_{0})=1
, so there exist integers

Define

\left(\begin{array}[]{l}h\\
m\\
k\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{lrr}1&0&0\\

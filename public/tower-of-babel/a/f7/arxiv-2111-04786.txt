{\mathbb{C}}

Moduli spaces of stable sheaves of rank

In particular, by analysing the chamber structure on the ample cone,
topological invariants of the moduli spaces are extensively studied if
X
is a surface
of Kodaira dimension
-\infty
and
r>0
(cf.
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,

[

[

For the moduli spaces of stable 1-dimensional sheaves on surfaces,
topological properties are also studied for

{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, a quadratic surface and an elliptic surface.
In this note, we shall compute the Hodge numbers of some
moduli spaces on an elliptic ruled surface, that is,
a
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle over an elliptic curve
C
.

Let
\varpi:X\to C
be the structure morphism of the
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-fibration.
Let
g
be a fiber of
\varpi
and
C_{0}
a minimal section.
Then
\operatorname{NS}(X)={\mathbb{Z}}C_{0}+{\mathbb{Z}}g
with

(C_{0}\cdot g)=1
,
(g^{2})=0
and

(C_{0}^{2})=-e
.
The canonical divisor

K_{X}=-2C_{0}-eg
satisfies
(K_{X}^{2})=0
and
K_{X}

is nef if and only if
e=0,-1
.
In each cases, the nef cone is generated by
g
and
-K_{X}

[
6
, Prop. 2.20, 2.21]
.
Let
M_{H}(0,\xi,\chi)
be the moduli space of stable 1-dimensional sheaves
E

such that
c_{1}(E)=\xi
and
\chi(E)=\chi
.

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
\varpi:X\to C
be an elliptic ruled surface with
e=-1
.
Let
(\xi,\chi)
be a pair of
\xi\in\operatorname{NS}(X)
with
(\xi\cdot K_{X})<0

and
\chi\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
\chi\neq 0
.

(1)

Assume that
H
is a general polarization. Then

M_{H}(0,\xi,\chi)
is a smooth projective manifold of dimension

(\xi^{2})+1
.

(2)

The Hodge numbers of
M_{H}(0,\xi,\chi)
is independent
of a general choice of
H
.
If
(\xi\cdot K_{X})=-1
, then the generating function is given by

(0.1)

\begin{split}&\sum_{-(\xi\cdot K_{X})=1}\left(\sum_{p,q}(-1)^{p+q}h^{p,q}(M_{H}(0,\xi,\chi))x^{p}y^{q}\right)q^{\frac{(\xi^{2})}{4}}\\
=&(x-1)^{2}(y-1)^{2}q^{\frac{1}{4}}\prod_{n>0}\frac{(1-x^{-1}(x^{2}y^{2}q)^{n})^{2}(1-y^{-1}(x^{2}y^{2}q)^{n})^{2}(1-x(x^{2}y^{2}q)^{n})^{2}(1-y(x^{2}y^{2}q)^{n})^{2}}{(1-(xy)^{-1}(x^{2}y^{2}q)^{\frac{n}{2}})(1-(x^{2}y^{2}q)^{\frac{n}{2}})^{2}(1-(xy)(x^{2}y^{2}q)^{\frac{n}{2}})}.\end{split}

The smoothness of
M_{H}(0,\xi,\chi)
is an easy consequence of
the deformation theory of a coherent sheaf.
The computation of the Hodge numbers is our main result.
We note that there is an elliptic fibration
\pi:X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
with three multiple fibers
of multiplicity 2.
Then the assumption
(\xi\cdot K_{X})=-1
means that
the support
D
of
E\in M_{H}(0,\xi,\chi)
is a double cover of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.
We shall also treat the case where
e=0
(Theorem
2.8
).

For the proof, we shall use Fourier-Mukai transform assocaited to the elliptic fibration

[
1
]
.
Since
D
is a double cover of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
,
the computation is reduced to the computation of Hodge numbers of the moduli spaces
of stable sheaves of rank two, which is computed in

[
2
]
or
[
15
]
.
We also use indefinite theta function in
[
5
]
to get the product expression.
Since the Betti numbers of moduli spaces for higher rank cases are computed in
[
11
]
,
it is possible to get the Betti numbers of
M_{H}(0,\xi,a)
for a general
\xi
in principle.

We would like to remark that
the same method works for a 9 points blow-ups
X
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.
Thus if
-K_{X}
is nef, then
by using Fourier-Mukai transforms on a rational elliptic surface
and the deformation invariance of the Hodge numbers,
we can compute the Hodge numbers from the computations
for positive rank cases. In particular we can derive an explicit form
of Euler charactersitics of
M_{H}(0,\xi,\chi)

from the computations in
[
16

(\xi\cdot K_{X})=-2

1.
Preliminaries

Notation.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface.
For two divisors
D_{1},D_{2}
on
X
,

D_{1}\equiv D_{2}
means
D_{1}
is algebraically equivalent to
D_{2}
.

(D_{1}\cdot D_{2})
denotes the intersection number of
D_{1},D_{2}
. We set

(D_{1}^{2}):=(D_{1}\cdot D_{1})
.

For a smooth projective variety
X
,

{\bf D}(X):={\bf D}(\operatorname{Coh}(X))
denotes the bounded derived category of
the category
\operatorname{Coh}(X)
of
coherent sheaves on
X
.
For
E\in{\bf D}(X)
,
E^{\vee}:={\bf R}{\mathcal{H}}om_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}(E,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})

denotes the derived dual of
E
.
For the Grothendieck group
K(X)
of
X
,
we set
K(X)_{\operatorname{top}}:=K(X)/\ker\operatorname{ch}
, where

\operatorname{ch}:K(X)\to H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
is the Chern character map.

For an algebraic set
Y
,
e(Y):=\sum_{p,q}\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}h^{p,q}(H^{k}_{c}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}}))x^{p}y^{q}

denotes the virtual Hodge polynomial of
Y
.
If
Y
is a smooth projective manifold, then

e(Y)
is the Hodge polynomial of
Y
.

Assume that
\varpi:X\to C
is an elliptic ruled surface. Thus
C
is an elliptic curve and

\varpi
is a
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle morphism.

C_{0}
denotes a minimal section of
\varpi
and
g
a fiber of
\varpi
.
We set
e:=-(C_{0}^{2})
.
Then we have
(g^{2})=0,(g\cdot C_{0})=1
and
(C_{0}^{2})=-e
.

1.1.
Basic facts.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface.
Let
H
be an ample divisor on
X
and
\alpha
a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor
on
X
.
For a coherent sheaf
E
on
X
,
an
\alpha
-twisted Euler characteristic
\chi_{\alpha}(E)
of
E

is defined by

\chi(E(-\alpha))=\int_{X}\operatorname{ch}(E)e^{-\alpha}\operatorname{td}_{X}
.
Matsuki and Wentworth
[
12
]

defined the
\alpha
-twisted stability
of a torsion free sheaf
E
by using twisted Hilbert polynomial

\chi_{\alpha}(E(nH))
. It is generalized to 1-dimensional sheaf in
[
18
]
.
For
(r,\xi,\chi)\in{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus\operatorname{NS}(X)\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}
,

M_{H}^{\alpha}(r,\xi,\chi)
denotes the moduli space of
\alpha
-twisted stable sheaves
E
on
X

with
(\operatorname{rk}E,c_{1}(E),\chi(E))=(r,\xi,\chi)
and

\overline{M}_{H}^{\alpha}(r,\xi,\chi)
the projective
compactification by adding

S
-equivalence classes of
\alpha
-twisted semi-stable sheaves
(see
[
12
]
for
r>0
and
[
18
, Thm. 4.7]
for
r=0
).

For a torsion free sheaf
E
on
X
, we set

\Delta(E)=c_{2}(E)-\frac{\operatorname{rk}E-1}{2\operatorname{rk}E}(c_{1}(E)^{2})\in{\mathbb{Q}},

where
\operatorname{rk}E
is the rank of
E
.
Then
\chi(E,E)=-2\operatorname{rk}E\Delta(E)
and
we have the following relations.

(1.1)

\begin{split}\chi(E)=&\operatorname{ch}_{2}(E)-\frac{1}{2}(c_{1}(E)\cdot K_{X})=\frac{1}{2}(c_{1}(E)\cdot(c_{1}(E)-K_{X}))-c_{2}(E),\\
2\operatorname{rk}E\Delta(E)=&-2\operatorname{rk}E\chi(E)-\operatorname{rk}E(c_{1}\cdot K_{X})+(c_{1}(E)^{2}).\end{split}

Let
X
be an elliptic ruled surface.
Then
h^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})=h^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})=1
and
h^{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})=0
. In particular

\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})=0
.
We have
e(X)=(1+xy)(1-x)(1-y)
.
We set

(1.2)

Z_{x,y}(X,u):=\frac{(1-xu)(1-yu)(1-x^{2}yu)(1-xy^{2}u)}{(1-u)(1-xyu)^{2}(1-x^{2}y^{2}u)}.

Then the Hodge polynomials of the Hilbert schemes
\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}^{n}
of
n
points on
X

are give by

(1.3)

\sum_{n}e(\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}^{n})q^{n}=\prod_{a>0}Z_{x,y}(X,(xy)^{-1}(xyq)^{a})

(see
[
4
]
).

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\varpi:X\to C
be an elliptic ruled surface
such that

-K_{X}
is nef.
Assume that
\gcd(r,\xi,\chi)=1
and

H
is general with respect to
(r,\xi,\chi)
.

(1)

M_{H}(r,\xi,\chi)
is a smooth projective manifold with

\dim M_{H}(r,\xi,\chi)=-2r\chi-r(\xi\cdot K_{X})+(\xi^{2})+1

unless
r=(\xi\cdot K_{X})=0
.

(2)

Assume that
r\neq 0
or
(\xi\cdot K_{X})\neq 0
. Then
the deformation class of
M_{H}(r,\xi,\chi)
is independent of
X
.
In particular
e(M_{H}(r,\xi,\chi))
is independent of the choice of
X
.

Proof.

(1)
It is sufficiently to prove

\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E)=0
for
E\in M_{H}(r,\xi,\chi)
.
If
r>0
, then
(K_{X}\cdot H)<0
implies
the claim.
If
r=0
, the the claim will be proved in Proposition
1.7
.

(2)

Let
T
be a smooth curve and

\phi:{\mathcal{E}}
a family of semi-stable vector bundles
{\mathcal{E}}_{t}

(t\in T)

of rank 2 and degree 0 or
-1
on
C

Then we have a family of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundles
{\mathcal{X}}:={\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathcal{E}})\to C\times T\to T
.
Let
{\mathcal{L}}:={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}})}(1)
be the tautological line bundle of the projective
bundle. Then

\operatorname{NS}({\mathcal{X}}_{t})={\mathbb{Z}}c_{1}({\mathcal{L}})_{t}+{\mathbb{Z}}g
,

(c_{1}({\mathcal{L}})_{t}\cdot g)=1
and
(c_{1}({\mathcal{L}})^{2}_{t})=-e
.
For a family of ample divisors
{\mathcal{H}}=xc_{1}({\mathcal{L}})+yg
,
the nefness of
K_{{\mathcal{X}}_{t}}
implies that
we have a smooth family of moduli spaces

M_{({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{H}})/T}(r,\xi,\chi)\to T
.
In particular
e(M_{{\mathcal{H}}_{t}}(r,\xi,\chi))
is
independent of
t\in T
.
∎

1.2.
Moduli of stable 1-dimensional sheaves.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface.

Lemma 1.4
.

Assume that
-K_{X}
is nef.
For a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor
\alpha
, let
E
and
F
be

\alpha
-twisted semi-stable sheaves of dimension 1
such that

(1.4)

\frac{\chi_{\alpha}(E)}{(c_{1}(E)\cdot H)}\geq\frac{\chi_{\alpha}(F)}{(c_{1}(F)\cdot H)}.

Then
\operatorname{Hom}(E,F(K_{X}))=0
if one of the following conditions hold:

(1)

(1.5)

\frac{\chi_{\alpha}(E)}{(c_{1}(E)\cdot H)}>\frac{\chi_{\alpha}(F)}{(c_{1}(F)\cdot H)}.

(2)

-K_{X}
is ample.

(3)

E
is
\alpha
-twisted stable and
(c_{1}(E)\cdot K_{X})\neq 0
.

(4)

F
is
\alpha
-twisted stable and
(c_{1}(F)\cdot K_{X})\neq 0
.

Proof.

Assume that
there is a non-trivial homomorphism

\varphi:E\to F(K_{X})
.
We set
G:=\varphi(E)
.
Then

(1.6)

\frac{\chi_{\alpha}(E)}{(c_{1}(E)\cdot H)}\leq\frac{\chi_{\alpha}(G)}{(c_{1}(G)\cdot H)}.

Since
G(-K_{X})
is a subsheaf of
F
,
we also have

(1.7)

\frac{\chi_{\alpha}(G)-(c_{1}(G)\cdot K_{X})}{(c_{1}(G)\cdot H)}=\frac{\chi_{\alpha}(G(-K_{X}))}{(c_{1}(G)\cdot H)}\leq\frac{\chi_{\alpha}(F)}{(c_{1}(F)\cdot H)}.

Since
(c_{1}(G)\cdot K_{X})\leq 0
, we get
(c_{1}(G)\cdot K_{X})=0
and

(1.8)

\frac{\chi_{\alpha}(E)}{(c_{1}(E)\cdot H)}=\frac{\chi_{\alpha}(G)}{(c_{1}(G)\cdot H)}=\frac{\chi_{\alpha}(F)}{(c_{1}(F)\cdot H)}.

In particular cases (1) and (2) do not occur.
If
E
is
\alpha
-twisted stable, then

E\cong G
, which implies
(c_{1}(E)\cdot K_{X})=0
.
If
F
is
\alpha
-twisted stable, then

G\cong F
, which implies
(c_{1}(F)\cdot K_{X})=0
.
Thus cases (3) and (4) do not occur.
Therefore
\operatorname{Hom}(E,F(K_{X}))=0
.
∎

Proposition 1.7
.

Assume that
-K_{X}
is nef and
(\xi\cdot K_{X})\neq 0
.
Then
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E)=0

For an

\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E)=\operatorname{Hom}(E,E(K_{X}))^{\vee}=0

∎

Remark 1.8
.

Assume that
-K_{X}
is nef and
(\xi\cdot K_{X})=0
.
Then
E\in M_{H}^{\alpha}(0,\xi,\chi)
satisfies

\chi(E(K_{X}))=\chi
. In this case,

\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E)=0
if and only if
E\not\cong E(K_{X})
.

For a purely 1-dimensional sheaf
E
,
\operatorname{Div}(E)
denotes the scheme-theoretic support of
E
.
We have a morphism

(1.9)

\begin{matrix}M_{H}^{\alpha}(0,\xi,\chi)&\to&\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}^{\xi}\\
E&\mapsto&\operatorname{Div}(E).\end{matrix}

Assume that
h^{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})=0
and
\xi-K_{X}
is ample.
Then Kodaira vanishing theorem implies
h^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D))=0

if
c_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D))=\xi
.
Hence we see that
\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}^{\xi}
is smooth, by

h^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{D}(D))=0
(
D\in\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}^{\xi}
).

Remark 1.9
.

Let
E
be a stable 1-dimensional sheaf with
(K_{X}\cdot\operatorname{Div}(E))<0
.
If
(K_{X}\cdot C)\leq 0
for all irreducible components
C
of
\operatorname{Div}(E)
,
then the proof of Proposition
1.7
implies
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E)=0
.
On the other hand we have an example of

such that
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E)\neq 0
.
Thus the nefness is a reasonable condition to ensure the smoothness of the moduli space.

Example 1.10
.

Assume that
X
is a ruled surface with a minimal section
C_{0}
.
Assume that

.
Let

(see also the proof of
[

[

]
and

Feynman–Kac perturbation of C ∗ C^{*} quantum stochastic flows


Abstract
The method of Feynman–Kac perturbation of quantum stochastic processes has a long pedigree, with the theory usually developed within the framework of processes on von Neumann algebras. In this work, the theory of operator spaces is exploited to enable a broadening of the scope to flows on C ∗ C^{*} algebras. Although the hypotheses that need to be verified in this general setting may seem numerous, we provide auxiliary results that enable this to be simplified in many of the cases which arise in practice. A wide variety of examples is provided by way of illustration.

Introduction

Very early in the study of quantum stochastic processes on operator
algebras, it was realised in pioneering work of Accardi
[
1
]

that analogues of the method of Feynman–Kac perturbation in
classical probability could be used to perturb Markov semigroups with
cocycles to give new semigroups, giving meaning to a
formal sum of generators.

The creation of quantum stochastic calculus in the early 1980s gave a
means of constructing such a cocycle
j
on a
*
-algebra

\mathsf{A}\subseteq B(\mathsf{h})
by solving the quantum stochastic
differential equation

(1.1)

j_{0}(x)=x\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{d}j_{t}(x)=\widetilde{\jmath}_{t}\bigl(\phi(x)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\Lambda_{t}\qquad(x\in\mathsf{A}_{0}\subseteq\mathsf{A}),

the cocycle in this case being used to perturb the ampliated CCR flow

\sigma
on Boson Fock space over
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathsf{k})
. Here
\mathsf{h}
and
\mathsf{k}

are known as the initial space and multiplicity space, respectively,
and
\mathsf{A}_{0}
is a dense, unital
*
-subalgebra of
\mathsf{A}
. Solving this QSDE
is possible for any completely bounded generator
\phi

[
19
]
, and
this gives a cocycle in a generalised sense, but for many purposes it
is desirable that
j
be
*
-homomorphic. Conversely, a completely
positive and contractive cocycle
j
on a
C^{*}
algebra
\mathsf{A}
that
satisfies the additional continuity requirement of being

Markov regular
or
elementary
necessarily
satisfies (
1.1
), in which case the stochastic generator

\phi
must be completely bounded
[
17
,
18
]
; see also

[
21
, Theorem 6.4]
.

Consequently it has become a folklore result that solutions
of the QSDE (
1.1
) and cocycles should be essentially the same
thing. However, as soon as one loosens the restrictive continuity or
boundedness assumptions, the correspondence between solutions
of (
1.1
) and cocycles is a lot less clear, especially
because of the many difficulties inherent in solving (
1.1
)
for an unbounded generator
\phi
. Our previous work
[
7
]
gave a method for
solving (
1.1
) on a
C^{*}
algebra for certain unbounded stochastic
generators, with the solution being
*
-homomorphic and a cocycle. This
involved two assumptions: a type of domain invariance for

\phi
and growth estimates for the iterates of
\phi
whose
existence follows from the first assumption. In that paper we gave a
range of interesting examples where these assumptions held, but these
assumptions are obviously still somewhat restrictive.

In this paper we now follow the work of Evans and Hudson
[
12
]

for perturbing a cocycle
j
, called the free flow, of the CCR flow

\sigma
to give a new cocycle
k
for
\sigma
. This involves finding
bounded solutions to the multiplier equation, which is the following
operator-valued QSDE with time-dependent coefficients:

(1.2)

X_{0}=I_{\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathcal{F}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\widetilde{\jmath}_{t}(F)\widetilde{X}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\Lambda_{t}.

The multiplier generator
F\in\mathsf{A}\otimes B({\widehat{\mathsf{k}}})
, where

{\widehat{\mathsf{k}}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathsf{k}
. The cocycle
k
is then given by setting

k_{t}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=X^{*}_{t}j_{t}(x)X_{t}
. The process obtained by
conjugation with
X
is, at least formally, a cocycle of the
semigroup
J=(\widehat{\jmath}_{t}\circ\sigma_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
; see
Section
3
for full details.

Evans and Hudson worked with bounded
\phi
for an arbitrary algebra

\mathsf{A}
and with finite-dimensional multiplicity space
\mathsf{k}
. Their work was extended to
the case where
\mathsf{k}
is separable and infinite dimensional,
\mathsf{A}
is
a von Neumann algebra and
\phi
is completely bounded in
[
10
]

and
[
14
]
. A more thorough analysis was later given in

[
5
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
, where
\mathsf{A}
is still a
von Neumann algebra and either
\phi
is completely bounded or
\mathsf{h}

and
\mathsf{k}
are separable. This included an algebraic characterisation
of solutions to (
1.2
).

Here, we move to the broader setting of
C^{*}
algebras. The technical
difficulties faced are much greater than before. Above, we were vague
about the nature of the tensor-product symbol. For those prior works
where
\mathsf{A}
is a von Neumann algebra it will be the von Neumann tensor
product and all bounded maps will be required to be normal. For processes on

C^{*}
algebras this must be modified; we have to use matrix-space
tensor products such as
\mathsf{A}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B({\widehat{\mathsf{k}}})
, and these need
not produce algebras
[
19
]
. Moreover, the matrix-space liftings of

*
-homomorphisms such as
j_{t}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{id}_{B({\widehat{\mathsf{k}}})}
need not
preserve products. Thus much greater care is needed as, for example,
checking equalities for simple tensors and extending by linearity and
continuity will no longer suffice.

This paper is structured as follows. Section
2

contains the necessary definitions and results concerning matrix
spaces. Section
3
introduces and gives general results
about quantum stochastic processes, cocycles and the
QSDE (
1.1
). Here we work with processes on a general
operator space
\mathsf{V}
; this point of view was used to unify several
results about various forms of cocycles in
[
20
]
and

[
21
]
. The key result is Theorem
3.15
which
gives conditions under which weak solutions of (
1.1
) are
actually cocycles. Section
4
contains our main results:
after introducing the notion of a free flow
j
, now on a
C^{*}

algebra, and solving the corresponding multiplier equation, we
establish the effects of Feynman–Kac perturbation. We have given the
result in a very general form, necessitating a long list of hypotheses
regarding multiplicativity of liftings and measurability of processes,
but also provide a number of auxiliary results that can be used to
simplify matters greatly under conditions that frequently arise in
practice. In Section
5
, these results are applied to a
wide variety of examples. Our previous work on the quantum exclusion
process from
[
7
]
is extended; there we obtained the growth
estimates by assuming a symmetry condition on the amplitudes, which
can now be relaxed by means of the perturbation techniques of this
paper. (An alternative construction of exclusion processes
has been given in
[
21
]
by making use of the semigroup
characterisation of completely positive and contractive cocycles given
in
[
20
]
that generalised earlier work of Accardi and Kozyrev

[
2
]
.) Similarly our previous work on flows on universal
C^{*}

algebras from
[
7
]
is extended; Feynman–Kac perturbations
had already been used in this context for flows on the non-commutative
torus in
[
8
]
. Here we show that those techniques apply more
generally for other algebras such as the Cuntz algebras and the
non-commutative spheres of Banica
[
3
]
.

Matrix spaces and liftings

Definition 2.1
.

Given Hilbert spaces
\mathsf{h}
and
\mathsf{H}
and a vector
z\in\mathsf{H}
, let
E_{z}\in B(\mathsf{h};\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathsf{H})
be such that
E_{z}u=u\otimes z
for
all
u\in\mathsf{h}
and let

E^{z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=E_{z}^{*}\in B(\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathsf{H};\mathsf{h})
be its adjoint. Using
Dirac notation, we may write
E_{z}=I_{\mathsf{h}}\otimes|z\rangle
and

E^{z}=I_{\mathsf{h}}\otimes\langle z|
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathsf{V}\subseteq B(\mathsf{h})
be a concrete operator space, that
is, a norm-closed linear subspace of
B(\mathsf{h})
. Given any Hilbert
space
\mathsf{H}
, the
matrix space over
\mathsf{V}
,

\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathsf{H})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{T\in B(\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathsf{H})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{z}TE_{w}\in\mathsf{V}\text{ for all }z,w\in\mathsf{H}\},

is an operator space such that

\mathsf{V}\otimes B(\mathsf{H})\subseteq\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathsf{H})\subseteq\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}B(\mathsf{H})
. The first inclusion is an
equality if
\mathsf{H}
is finite dimensional; the latter inclusion is an
equality if and only if
\mathsf{V}
is ultraweakly closed.
If
\mathsf{K}
is another Hilbert space then

\bigl(\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathsf{H})\bigr)\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathsf{K})=\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathsf{H}\otimes\mathsf{K})
with the standard
identifications.

Rectangular matrix spaces of the form
\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathsf{H};\mathsf{K})
are defined in the same way. Two important
examples are the
column space

\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}|\mathsf{H}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{T\in B(\mathsf{h};\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathsf{H})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{z}T\in\mathsf{V}\text{ for all }z\in\mathsf{H}\}=\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathbb{C};\mathsf{H})

and the
row space

\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}\langle\mathsf{H}|\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{T\in B(\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathsf{H};\mathsf{h})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}TE_{w}\in\mathsf{V}\text{ for all }w\in\mathsf{H}\}=\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathsf{H};\mathbb{C}).

Definition 2.3
.

If
\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{V}\to\mathsf{W}
is a completely bounded map between
operator spaces and
\mathsf{H}
is a Hilbert space then the

matrix-space lifting
of
\Phi
is the unique completely bounded
map

\Phi\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{id}_{B(\mathsf{H})}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathsf{H})\to\mathsf{W}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathsf{H})

such that

E^{z}(\Phi\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{id}_{B(\mathsf{H})})(T)E_{w}=\Phi(E^{z}TE_{w})

for all
T\in\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathsf{H})
and
z
,

w\in\mathsf{H}
. It then holds that

\|\Phi\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{id}_{B(\mathsf{H})}\|_{\text{cb}}=\|\Phi\|_{\text{cb}}
.
For the existence of such a map, see
[
19
]
or

[
4
, Theorem 2.5]
.

If
\mathsf{K}
is another Hilbert space then, with the standard
identifications,

(2.1)

(\Phi\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{id}_{B(\mathsf{H})})\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{id}_{B(\mathsf{K})}=\Phi\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{id}_{B(\mathsf{H}\otimes\mathsf{K})}.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{B}
is a completely positive map between unital

C^{*}
algebras and
\mathsf{K}
is a Hilbert space then the matrix-space
lifting
\Phi\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{id}_{B(\mathsf{K})}
is a completely positive
map between operator systems.

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality that

\mathsf{B}\subseteq B(\mathsf{H})
and let

T\in\mathsf{A}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathsf{K})
be positive. To prove that

(\Phi\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{id}_{B(\mathsf{K})})(T)
is positive, it
suffices to show that

\langle(I_{\mathsf{H}}\otimes P)\xi,(\Phi\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{id}_{B(\mathsf{K})})(T)(I_{\mathsf{H}}\otimes P)\xi\rangle\geqslant 0\qquad(\xi\in\mathsf{H}\otimes\mathsf{K})

for any finite-rank orthogonal projection
P\in B(\mathsf{K})
. If

P
has rank
n
then
(I_{\mathsf{H}}\otimes P)\xi
and
PTP
may be
considered to be elements of
\mathsf{H}^{n}
and
M_{n}(\mathsf{A})
, respectively, with the
latter being positive, so that

\langle(I_{\mathsf{H}}\otimes P)\xi,(\Phi\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{id}_{B(\mathsf{K})})(T)(I_{\mathsf{H}}\otimes P)\xi\rangle=\langle(I_{\mathsf{H}}\otimes P)\xi,(\Phi\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}})(PTP)(I_{\mathsf{H}}\otimes P)\xi\rangle\geqslant 0,

as required. Since
\mathsf{K}
is arbitrary, complete positivity now
follows by (
2.1
).
∎

Stochastic processes

Notation 3.1

Fix a Hilbert space

Definition 3.2

An

Definition 3.3
.

Fix a Hilbert space
\mathsf{h}
, the

. For a fixed

, an

is a collection

(i)

\mathsf{h}\mathbin{\underline{\otimes}}\mathcal{E}(\mathsf{T})\subseteq\mathop{\mathrm{dom}}X_{t}\subseteq\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathcal{F}

\mathop{\mathrm{im}}X_{t}\subseteq\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathcal{F}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

(ii)

E^{\varpi(f)}X_{t}E_{\varpi(g)}=\langle\varpi(1_{[t,\infty)}f),\varpi(1_{[t,\infty)}g)\rangle E^{\varpi(1_{[0,t)}f)}X_{t}E_{\varpi(1_{[0,t)}g)}

,

for all

.

is

,

,

,

with its continuous

Remark 3.4
.

(i)

A bounded operator process which is strongly continuous has
locally bounded norm if and only if it is strongly continuous on

, by the Banach–Steinhaus Theorem.

(ii)

If

(iii)

If

.

A

.

. If

.

3.2
Cocycles

Definition 3.6
.

Given a completely bounded mapping process

on
\mathsf{V}
, let

k_{t}[f,g]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{V}\to B(\mathsf{h});\ x\mapsto E^{\varpi(1_{[0,t)}f)}k_{t}(x)E_{\varpi(1_{[0,t)}g)}=E^{\varpi(f|_{[0,t)})}k_{t)}(x)E_{\varpi(g|_{[0,t)})}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
f
,
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathsf{k})
. A

Markovian cocycle on
\mathsf{V}
is a completely bounded mapping
process
k
such that, for some admissible set
\mathsf{T}
,

(i)

k_{t}[f,g](x)\in\mathsf{V}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
f
,

g\in L^{\mathrm{step}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathsf{T})
and

(ii)

k_{0}[0,0]=\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{V}}
and

(iii)

k_{s+t}[f,g]=k_{s}[f,g]\mathbin{\circ}k_{t}[f(\cdot+s),g(\cdot+s)]
for all
s
,

t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and

,

.

Since
k
is adapted, if (ii) holds then
k_{0}[f,g]=\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{V}}
for
all
f
,
g
in
L^{\mathrm{step}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathsf{T})
.

Remark 3.7
.

Totality of the set
\{\varpi(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f\in L^{\mathrm{step}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathsf{T})\}
, linearity and norm
continuity of the map

, and adaptedness of

that condition (i) of Definition

the requirement that

(i)
′

k_{t}(\mathsf{V})\subseteq\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathcal{F})
.

Furthermore, in this formulation, the following equivalent
versions of conditions (ii) and (iii) appear as well:

(ii)
′

k_{0}(x)=x\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}}
for all
x\in\mathsf{V}
and

(iii)
′

k_{s+t}=\widehat{k}_{s}\mathbin{\circ}\sigma_{s}\mathbin{\circ}k_{t}
for all

,

where
\sigma_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B(\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathcal{F})\to B(\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{[s})

from the natural unitary identification

\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathcal{F}\cong\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{[s}

T\in B(\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathcal{F})
then

E^{\varpi(f)}\sigma_{s}(T)E_{\varpi(g)}=E^{\varpi(f(\cdot+s))}TE_{\varpi(g(\cdot+s))}\qquad\text{for all }f,g\in L^{2}\bigl([s,\infty);\mathsf{k}\big),

and
\widehat{k}_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=k_{s)}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{id}_{B(\mathcal{F}_{[s})}

in

The following proposition is the matrix-space version of the
corresponding result for normal mapping processes on von Neumann
algebras

Proposition 3.8
.

Let

be a completely bounded mapping process on
\mathsf{V}
. Then

is a Markovian cocycle if and only if

(K_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\widehat{k}_{t}\mathbin{\circ}\sigma_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is a semigroup
on
\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathcal{F})
, that is,

K_{t}\bigl(\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathcal{F})\bigr)\subseteq\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathcal{F}),\quad K_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathcal{F})}\quad\text{and}\quad K_{s+t}=K_{s}\mathbin{\circ}K_{t}\quad\text{for all }s,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Proof.

Note first that
\mathop{\mathrm{dom}}K_{t}=\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathcal{F})
, and that

k_{t}(x)=K_{t}(x\otimes I)\qquad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\text{ and }x\in\mathsf{V};

it follows immediately that if

is a semigroup on

\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathcal{F})
then

is a Markovian cocycle.

For the converse, let

be a completely bounded mapping process on

\mathsf{V}
. The identity

E^{\varpi(f)}K_{s}(T)E_{\varpi(g)}=k_{s}[f,g]\bigl(E^{\varpi(f(\cdot+s))}TE_{\varpi(g(\cdot+s))}\bigr)

holds for all

,

and

cocycle then

K_{s}\bigl(\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathcal{F})\bigr)\subseteq\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathcal{F})

for all

as required.

Remark 3.13

Definition 4.8

Proposition 4.9
.

Let

\mathsf{A}

Proof.

Note first that

Lemma 4.13
.

Let

and let

be a bounded integrable process with locally bounded norm. There is a unique strong solution to the

(4.4)

X_{0}=R\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{d}X_{t}=L_{t}\widetilde{X}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\Lambda_{t},

is a
strongly continuous operator process and
\widetilde{X}
is an
integrable process such that

bounded norm for every
f\in L^{\mathrm{step}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathsf{T})

Proof.

This is essentially Proposition 3.1 of
[
14
]

notation and language of
[
15
]
. In particular

is found by
solving the iteration scheme

X^{(0)}_{t}=R\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}},\qquad X^{(n+1)}_{t}=R\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}}+\int^{t}_{0}L_{s}\widetilde{X}^{(n)}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}\Lambda_{s},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

where
\widetilde{X}^{(n)}_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=X^{(n)}_{t}\mathbin{\underline{\otimes}}I_{\widehat{\mathsf{k}}}

. We fix

it follows by induction that

for all

with

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{(n)}_{t}u\|^{2}\leqslant K_{f,T}\int^{t}_{0}\|L_{s}\|^{2}\|\widehat{f}(s)\|^{2}\|Y^{(n-1)}_{s}u\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,

gives a process

∎

Lemma 4.16

Let

(i)

The process

For part (iii), it is enough to show that if

,

and

of

and let

4.3
Perturbation of the free flow

Remark 4.18
.

If
\mathsf{A}\subseteq B(\mathsf{h})
is a unital
C^{*}
algebra and
\mathsf{H}

is a Hilbert space then, in general, the operator space

\mathsf{A}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathsf{H})
is not an algebra, although it is always
an operator system in
B(\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathsf{H})
: see

[
19
, pp. 615–6]
. The reason is already apparent at the level
of row and column spaces, since
\mathsf{A}\otimes|\mathsf{H}\rangle
and

\mathsf{A}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}|\mathsf{H}\rangle
typically differ, with the former having a
natural Hilbert
C^{*}
-bimodule structure not shared with the
latter. Moreover, if
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
is any orthonormal
basis of
\mathsf{H}
then for each
T\in\mathsf{A}\otimes|\mathsf{H}\rangle
it is
the case that

T=\sum_{\alpha\in A}E^{e_{\alpha}}T\otimes|e_{\alpha}\rangle=\sum_{\alpha\in A}E_{e_{\alpha}}E^{e_{\alpha}}T

with the series being norm convergent, whereas if

T\in\mathsf{A}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}|\mathsf{H}\rangle
then the series above converges to

T
in the strong operator topology, but not necessarily in norm.

The following construction provides a means of avoiding some of the
problems caused by this inconvenient difference.

Definition 4.19
.

Let
\mathsf{V}\subseteq B(\mathsf{h})
be an operator space, and let

\mathsf{H}
be a Hilbert space. Let

\displaystyle R(\mathsf{V};\mathsf{H})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{T\in B(\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathsf{H})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{z}T\in\mathsf{V}\otimes\langle\mathsf{H}|\text{ for all }z\in\mathsf{H}\}

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad C(\mathsf{V};\mathsf{H})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{T\in B(\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathsf{H})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}TE_{w}\in\mathsf{V}\otimes|\mathsf{H}\rangle\text{ for all }w\in\mathsf{H}\}.

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
T_{R}
and
T_{C}
denote the topologies on
B(\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathsf{H})

generated by the families of seminorms
\{{}^{z}p\}_{z\in\mathsf{H}}

and
\{p_{w}\}_{w\in\mathsf{H}}
, respectively, where

{}^{z}p(T)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\|E^{z}T\|\quad\text{and}\quad p_{w}(T)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\|TE_{w}\|\qquad\text{for all }T\in B(\mathsf{h}\otimes\mathsf{H}).

Then
R(\mathsf{V};\mathsf{H})=\overline{\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\underline{\otimes}}B(\mathsf{H})}^{R}

and
C(\mathsf{V};\mathsf{H})=\overline{\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\underline{\otimes}}B(\mathsf{H})}^{C}
,
the closures with respect to
T_{R}
and
T_{C}
, respectively. Moreover

\mathsf{V}\otimes B(\mathsf{H})\subseteq R(\mathsf{V};\mathsf{H})\cap C(\mathsf{V};\mathsf{H})\subseteq R(\mathsf{V};\mathsf{H})\cup C(\mathsf{V};\mathsf{H})\subseteq\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathsf{H}).

Proof.

Any
T\in\overline{\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\underline{\otimes}}B(\mathsf{H})}^{R}
is the limit of a

T_{R}
-convergent net
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\underline{\otimes}}B(\mathsf{H})
.
We have that

E^{z}T_{i}\in\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\underline{\otimes}}\langle\mathsf{H}|\subseteq\mathsf{V}\otimes\langle\mathsf{H}|
,
with the latter space being norm closed. Thus
T\in R(\mathsf{V};\mathsf{H})
.

Next, let
S\in R(\mathsf{V};\mathsf{H})
. Given an orthonormal basis

\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
of
\mathsf{H}
, for each finite set

B\subset\subset A
we let

p_{B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\beta\in B}|e_{\beta}\rangle\langle e_{\beta}|\quad\text{and}\quad P_{B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=I_{\mathsf{h}}\otimes p_{B}=\sum_{\beta\in B}E_{e_{\beta}}E^{e_{\beta}}.

For any finite set
C\subset\subset A
, Remark
4.18
with
T=(P_{C}S)^{*}

gives that

\lim_{B\subset\subset A}\|P_{C}SP_{B}-P_{C}S\|=0.

We now consider the net

\{P_{B}SP_{B}\}_{B\subset\subset A}\subseteq\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\underline{\otimes}}B(\mathsf{H})
.
Given any
z\in\mathsf{H}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is a finite set

B_{0}\subset\subset A
such that
\|z-p_{B_{0}}z\|<\varepsilon
.
Then
P_{B}P_{B_{0}}=P_{B_{0}}
for any finite set
B\supseteq B_{0}
and so

{}^{z}p(P_{B}SP_{B}-S)\leqslant 2\varepsilon\|S\|+\|z\|\,\|P_{B_{0}}SP_{B}-P_{B_{0}}S\|.

Hence
R(\mathsf{V};\mathsf{H})\subseteq\overline{\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\underline{\otimes}}B(\mathsf{H})}^{R}
,
as required. That
C(\mathsf{V};\mathsf{H})=\overline{\mathsf{V}\mathbin{\underline{\otimes}}B(\mathsf{H})}^{C}

is proved in the same way. The inclusions are readily verified, in
particular as it is clear that
T_{R}
and
T_{C}
are weaker than the
norm topology.
∎

Lemma 4.21
.

Let
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{B}
be a

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{A}_{0}\to\mathsf{A}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}B(\mathsf{k});\ x\mapsto\sum_{i,j\in I}\delta^{\alpha}_{i,j}(x)\otimes|f_{i,j}\rangle=t_{\alpha}x-(x\otimes I_{\mathsf{k}})t_{\alpha}.

If the series in (

) converges to give a bounded
operator from
\mathsf{h}
to

then

; moreover,
\delta_{\alpha}
is a well defined

\iota
-derivation, and the domain of
\delta_{\alpha}
can be extended
to all of
\mathsf{A}
. However, the standing assumption
in
[
7
]
does not necessarily give such convergence. Instead,
the following
finite-valence
and
symmetry
assumptions
were made:

(5.4)

\{j\in I\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\alpha_{i,j}\neq 0\}\text{ is finite for all }i\in I\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\alpha_{i,j}|=|\alpha_{j,i}|\text{ for all }i,j\in I.

Under these assumptions it turns out that
\delta_{\alpha}(x)
is
well defined for each
x\in\mathsf{A}_{0}
since only finitely many of the
terms in the series (
5.3
) are non-zero, even if

t_{\alpha}
is not a bounded operator.

The generator of the semingroup is then given by

\displaystyle\tau_{\alpha,\eta}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{A}_{0}\to\mathsf{A}_{0};\ x

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathrm{i}\sum_{i\in I}\eta_{i}[b^{*}_{i}b_{i},x]-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in I\times I}\bigl((t^{\alpha}_{i,j})^{*}[t^{\alpha}_{i,j},x]+[x,(t^{\alpha}_{i,j})^{*}]t^{\alpha}_{i,j}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}[H,x]-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}t^{*}_{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha}(x)-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\delta^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)t_{\alpha},

where
H\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i\in I}\eta_{i}b^{*}_{i}b_{i}
. Again, the series for

H
may not converge, but the commutator
[H,x]
is well defined, as
are the products
t^{*}_{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha}(x)
and

\delta^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)t_{\alpha}
, courtesy
of (
5.4
).

We can now write

\phi=\begin{bmatrix}\tau_{\alpha,\eta}&\delta^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\\[4.30554pt]
\delta_{\alpha}&0\end{bmatrix}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{A}_{0}\to\mathsf{A}_{0}\mathbin{\underline{\otimes}}B({\widehat{\mathsf{k}}}).

This map has standard form, and under a variety of
hypotheses
[
7
, Examples 5.11–13]
it was shown that there
exists a quantum stochastic flow
j
with
\phi
as its generator.

For the remainder of this section we will assume that such a flow
j

exists, and show how the methods of this paper can allow us to go
beyond the assumptions (
5.4
).

Let
\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}I\times I\to\mathbb{C}
, and suppose that
t_{\beta}
defined
through (
5.2
) is a well defined element of
\mathsf{A}\mathbin{\otimes_{\mathrm{m}}}|\mathsf{k}\rangle

such that
t^{*}_{\beta}t_{\beta}\in\mathsf{A}
. Choose

such that

and set

F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{i}h-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}t^{*}_{\beta}t_{\beta}&t^{*}_{\beta}\\[4.30554pt]
-t_{\beta}&0\end{bmatrix}.

The strongly continuous operator process
X=X^{F}
given by
Proposition
4.14
is isometric. Furthermore, since

\Delta F=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\[2.15277pt]
-t_{\beta}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in C(\mathsf{A};{\widehat{\mathsf{k}}})
,
Remark
4.29
makes it clear that Theorem
4.26

may be applied, when

is countable, to obtain a completely positive
unital mapping process
k
with generator

(5.5)

\psi=\begin{bmatrix}\tau^{\prime}&(\delta^{\prime})^{\dagger}\\
\delta^{\prime}&0\end{bmatrix},

where

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\tau_{\alpha,\eta}(x)-t^{*}_{\beta}\delta_{\alpha}(x)+t^{*}_{\beta}(x\otimes I_{\mathsf{k}})t_{\beta}-\delta^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)t_{\beta}-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\{t^{*}_{\beta}t_{\beta},x\}-\mathrm{i}[h,x]

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\delta^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\alpha}(x)-(x\otimes I_{\mathsf{k}})t_{\beta}+t_{\beta}x.

for all

. In particular for any choice of

we have

. Furthermore, if the series

is convergent in the weak operator topology for all

then the expression

(ii)

The cost, currently, for circumventing these restrictions imposed in

As an example of conditions that ensure that the various series above

. Since

5.3

algebras

Definition 5.4
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a unital
C^{*}
algebra
\mathsf{A}
and let
\{a_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in I\}
be a subset of
\mathsf{A}
.
We let
\mathsf{W}
denote the set of all words in
the elements
\{a_{i},a_{i}^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in I\}
, so that
\mathsf{A}_{0}=\lin\mathsf{W}
is
the
*
-subalgebra generated by this subset. The set

\mathsf{W}
is said to
generate

\mathsf{A}
if
\mathsf{A}_{0}
is norm dense in
\mathsf{A}
.

These generators
satisfy the relations

\{p_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k\in K\}
if
each
p_{k}
is a complex polynomial in the non-commuting indeterminates

\langle X_{i},X_{i}^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in I\rangle
and the
algebra element
p_{k}(a_{i},a_{i}^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in I)
obtained from

by
replacing each
X_{i}
by
a_{i}
and
X_{i}^{*}
by
a_{i}^{*}
is
equal to
0
for all
k\in K
.

A generator
a_{i}
is called

if in each relation
p_{k}

the difference between the number of instances of
a_{i}
and the number
of instances of
a^{*}_{i}
in each monomial making up
p_{k}
is constant.

Let the unital
C^{*}
algebra
\mathsf{A}
have generators
\{a_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in I\}
that satisfy
the relations
\{p_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k\in K\}
. Then
\mathsf{A}
is
universal
if, given any unital

C^{*}
algebra
\mathsf{B}
containing a set of elements

\{b_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in I\}
which satisfies the relations

\{p_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k\in K\}
, that is,
p_{k}(b_{i},b_{i}^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in I)=0
for
all
k\in K
, there exists a unique
*
-homomorphism

\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{B}
such that
\pi(a_{i})=b_{i}
for all
i\in I
.

Example 5.5
.

In
[
7
]
we considered flows on the universal rotation algebra,
which is the universal algebra generated by the unitaries
U
,
V
and

Z
such that

UV=ZVU,\quad UZ=ZU\quad\text{and}\quad VZ=ZV.

If we label the generators as
a_{1}=U
,
a_{2}=V
and
a_{3}=Z
then
the full list of relations are

\displaystyle p_{1}=X_{1}X^{*}_{1}-1,\quad p_{2}=X_{1}^{*}X_{1}-1,\quad p_{3}=X_{2}X^{*}_{2}-1,\quad p_{4}=X_{2}^{*}X_{2}-1,\quad p_{5}=X_{3}X^{*}_{3}-1,

\displaystyle p_{6}=X_{3}^{*}X_{3}-1,\quad p_{7}=X_{1}X_{2}-X_{3}X_{2}X_{1},\quad p_{8}=X_{1}X_{3}-X_{3}X_{1},\quad p_{9}=X_{2}X_{3}-X_{3}X_{2}.

The relations

, …,

and

are both balanced, but

is not
courtesy of relation

.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a universal
C^{*}
algebra generated by

\{a_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in I\}
and let
\mathsf{W}
and
\mathsf{A}_{0}
be as in

Definition
5.4
. Suppose that the generating
element
a_{j}
is balanced. There is a
C_{0}
group of
automorphisms
\alpha^{j}
with generator
\mathrm{i}d_{j}
such that

(i)

the domain
\mathop{\mathrm{dom}}d_{j}
is a
*
-subalgebra containing
\mathsf{A}_{0}
,

(ii)

the derivation
d_{j}
is skew symmetric, that is,
d_{j}^{\dagger}=-d_{j}
, and

(iii)

we have
d_{j}(b)=n_{j}(b)b
for each
b\in\mathsf{W}
, where

n_{j}(b)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\text{number of copies of $a_{j}$ in $b$}-\text{number of copies of $a^{*}_{j}$ in $b$}.

Proof.

For each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let

(5.7)

b_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}a_{i}&\text{if }i\neq j,\\
e^{\mathrm{i}t}a_{j}&\text{if }i=j.\end{cases}

Since
a_{j}
is balanced it follows that
\{b_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in I\}
is a set
of elements in
\mathsf{A}
that satisfy the same relations as the original
set of generators, and moreover this new set also generates

\mathsf{A}
. Universality implies that is an automorphism
\alpha^{j}_{t}
of

\mathsf{A}
such that
\alpha^{j}_{t}(a_{i})=b_{i}
for all
i
. It is easy to
see that
\alpha^{j}_{t}(b)=e^{\mathrm{i}tn_{j}(b)}b
for all

b\in\mathsf{W}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\|\alpha^{j}_{t}\|=1
for all

t
, it follows by linearity and continuity that

(\alpha^{j}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a
C_{0}
group of automorphisms.

That
\mathop{\mathrm{dom}}d_{j}
is a
*
-subalgebra of
\mathsf{A}
and
d_{j}
is skew symmetric
follows from the fact that
\alpha^{j}
is a group of automorphisms. Moreover,
it is immediate that
\mathsf{W}\subseteq\mathop{\mathrm{dom}}d_{j}
, with
d_{j}(b)=n_{j}(b)b
for
each
b\in\mathsf{W}
, and so
\mathsf{A}_{0}\subseteq\mathop{\mathrm{dom}}d_{j}
.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a universal
C^{*}
algebra generated by the set

\{a_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in I\}
, let
\{a_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}j\in J\}
be a subset of balanced generators, where

,
and let

\mathsf{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\ell^{2}(J)
with the standard orthonormal basis
\{e_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}j\in J\}
.
For any choices of constants
\{c_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}j\in J\}
, the following defines a generator

\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{A}_{0}\to\mathsf{A}_{0}\otimes B(\mathsf{k})
in standard form
according to (

)
:

(5.8)

\delta(x)=\sum_{j\in J}c_{j}d_{j}(x)\otimes|e_{j}\rangle,\qquad\tau=-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\sum_{j\in J}|c_{j}|^{2}d_{j}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\pi=\iota,

The series for

Furthermore, if

. It follows from

that we can
solve (

) to find a weakly multiplicative

. If the generators are all isometries

[
7
, Theorem 3.12]
allows us to complete the proof.
∎

Example 5.10
.

More recent examples of universal

\mathsf{k}

is a natural identification of the Fock space

with the

for
\mathbf{w}

Example 5.13

Let

\delta(x)+(x\otimes I_{\mathsf{k}})l-lx&0\end{bmatrix}.

If the set

\mathsf{k}

\mathsf{h}

k

Letting

\delta(x)+(x\otimes I_{\mathsf{k}})l-lx=\sum_{j\in J}\bigl(c_{j}d_{j}(x)+d_{l_{j}}(x)\bigr),

Feynman–Kac perturbation techniques similar to those developed here
were employed in

Under the assumption that

\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}

\tau^{\prime}(x)=\tau(x)+l^{*}\delta(x)+l^{*}(x\otimes I_{\mathsf{k}})l+\delta^{\dagger}(x)l+k^{*}x+xk\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad d_{j}^{\prime}=\mathrm{i}(\beta_{j}d_{j}+d_{n_{j}})

If

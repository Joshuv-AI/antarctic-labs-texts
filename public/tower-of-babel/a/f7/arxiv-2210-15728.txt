OPEs of rank two 𝒲 \mathcal{W} -algebras
Justine Fasquel

Abstract
. In this short note, we provide OPEs for several affine 𝒲 \mathcal{W} -algebras associated with Lie algebras of rank two and give some direct applications.



1.
Introduction

The
\mathcal{W}
-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is obtained from an Hamiltonian reduction of the affine vertex algebra
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})

[
14
,
17
]
. Hence, both structure are closely related.
However, if the description of
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
in term of strong generators and relations (OPEs) is well-known (see for instance
[
15
,
16
]
), it is no longer the case for the
\mathcal{W}
-algebras.
Indeed, the latter do not directly come from Lie algebras and we need the formalism of vertex algebras to describe them.
The computation of OPEs is difficult in general and only few examples were computed explicitely
[
6
,
10
,
21
]
.

In this short note, we provide the OPEs for
\mathcal{W}
-algebras associated with Lie algebras of rank two (i.e.
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
,
\mathfrak{sp}_{4}
and
G_{2}
).

The OPEs consist in a powerful tool to prove important and precise results. For instance, they can be used to prove rationality of
\mathcal{W}
-algebras
[
6
,
13
]
, compute associated varieties
[
12
]
, or find isomorphisms between vertex algebras
[
2
,
3
,
19
]
.

The present paper is organized as follows. In Sect.
2
we recall the construction of the
\mathcal{W}
-algebra due to Kac, Roan, Wakimoto
[
17
]
.
Sect.
3
presents the OPEs of
\mathcal{W}
-algebras associated to a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of rank two.
We use these OPEs to emphasize isomorphisms of
\mathcal{W}
-algebras for particular values of the level
k
. These levels are listed in Sect.
4
.

2.
Structure of affine
\mathcal{W}
-algebras

We assume the lector familiar with the formalism of vertex algebras
and we refer to
[
17
]
and
[
4
,
12
,
18
]
for details on the construction of
\mathcal{W}
-algebras.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex simple finite dimensional Lie algebra.
Consider
f
a nilpotent element of
\mathfrak{g}
and
k
a complex number. The (affine)
\mathcal{W}
-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is the vertex algebra obtained as the zero-th cohomology of a complex
\mathscr{C}(\mathfrak{g},f,k)
constructed from the affine vertex algebra
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
. It is a particular case of BRST reduction usually referred as the Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction of
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
.
We write

\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)=H_{f}^{0}(V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})).

Its simple graded quotient is denoted by
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
.
Provided that
k
is non-critical, i.e.
k\neq-h^{\vee}
,
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
inherits a structure of conformal vertex algebra from those of
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Denote
L(z)
the usual conformal field defined in
[
17
]
.

Let embed
f
into an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(e,h,f)
given by the Jacobson-Morosov theorem. We have

[h,e]=2e,\qquad[h,f]=-2f,\qquad\text{and}\qquad[e,f]=h.

The semisimple element
x_{0}:=h/2
induces an
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
-gradation on
\mathfrak{g}
,

(1)

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{j\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{j},

where
\mathfrak{g}_{j}=\{y\in\mathfrak{g}\mid[x_{0},y]=jy\}
.
To any
a\in\mathfrak{g}_{-j}
,
j\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, we can associate a field
J^{a}(z)
of
\mathscr{C}(\mathfrak{g},f,k)
. This field has conformal weight
1+j
with respect to
L
.
The fields
\{J^{a}\}_{a\in\mathfrak{g}}
play an important role in the structure of the
\mathcal{W}
-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}^{f}
be the centralizer of
f
in
\mathfrak{g}
, and set
\mathfrak{g}^{f}_{j}:=\mathfrak{g}^{f}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{j}
for all
j\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. By the theory of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, we have

(2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{f}=\bigoplus_{j\leqslant 0}\mathfrak{g}^{f}_{j}.

The strong generators of the
\mathcal{W}
-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
are in one to one correspondance with the elements of a basis of
\mathfrak{g}^{f}
with respect to the grading (
2
).

Theorem 2.1

(
[
18
]
)
.

For each
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{f}_{-j}
,
j\geqslant 0
, there exists a field
J^{\{a\}}(z)
of
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
of conformal weight
1+j

with respect to
L

such that
J^{\{a\}}(z)-J^{a}(z)
is a linear combination of normally order products of the fields

J^{b}(z)
, where
b\in\mathfrak{g}_{-s}
,
0\leqslant s<j
and their derivatives.

Let
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}^{f}
compatible with the graduation (
2
).
Then
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is freely strongly generated by the fields
\{J^{\{a_{i}\}}\}_{i\in I}
.

In practice, to construct
J^{\{a\}}(z)
from
J^{a}(z)
,
we write a linear combination of fields as in the theorem and find coefficients so that the field
J^{\{a\}}(z)
is
d_{(0)}
-closed.
When
f
is an even nilpotent element there exists an algorithm to construct the strong generators of the
\mathcal{W}
-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
(see
[
12
,
18
]
).
In
[
18
]
, Kac and Wakimoto give formulas to construct strong generators with conformal weights
1
,
1/2
and
0
, and they compute OPEs between them.
This provides in particular an explicit description of all
\mathcal{W}
-algebras associated with minimal nilpotent elements.
Indeed, if
f
belongs to the minimal nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}_{\min}
then the grading (
1
) induced by
x
is
minimal
:

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-\frac{1}{2}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\frac{1}{2}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1},

with
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}=\mathbb{C}f
and
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathbb{C}e
.

3.
OPEs of
\mathcal{W}
-algebras associated with rank two Lie algebras

In this section, we present OPEs of the
\mathcal{W}
-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
where
\mathfrak{g}
is a simple Lie algebra of rank two and
k\neq-h^{\vee}
is a non critical level.
For simplicity,
L
will always denote the conformal vector.
In the cases we consider, we were able to find strong generators which are all – except
L
–
primary
with respect to the conformal structure induced by
L
. It means that for any strong generator
J\neq L
of conformal weight
\Delta
,

L(z)J(w)\sim\frac{\Delta}{(z-w)^{2}}J(w)+\frac{1}{(z-w)}\partial J(w).

We take these primary generators in the following. With this choice, the OPEs involving
L
are uniquely determined. We omit them in the computations.

3.1.
\mathcal{W}
-algebras associated with nilpotent elements of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}

The simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
of type
A_{2}
admits two non-trivial nilpotent orbits: the principal and the minimal one.

The corresponding
\mathcal{W}
-algebras are among the first examples of
\mathcal{W}
-algebras which have been described explicitly. They are respectively called the Bershadsky-Polyakov vertex algebra
[
8
,
20
]
and the Zamolodchikov vertex algebra
[
21
]
.

In
[
6
]
, Arakawa gives an explicit description of the Bershadsky-Polyakov vertex algebra which corresponds to the
\mathcal{W}
-algebra associated with a minimum nilpotent element of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
.
The
\mathcal{W}
-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},f_{\mathrm{min}})
is strongly generated by the fields
J(z)
,
G^{\pm}(z)
and
L(z)
of conformal weights
1
,
\frac{3}{2}
and
2
respectively.
These fields satisfy the following relations:

\displaystyle J(z)J(w)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{(3+2k)}{(z-w)^{2}},

\displaystyle J(z)G^{\pm}(w)

\displaystyle\sim\pm\frac{1}{(z-w)}G^{\pm}(w),

\displaystyle G^{\pm}(z)G^{\pm}(w)

\displaystyle\sim 0,

\displaystyle G^{+}(z)G^{-}(w)

\displaystyle\sim-\frac{(1+k)(3+2k)}{(z-w)^{3}}+\frac{3(1+k)}{(z-w)^{2}}J(w)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(z-w)}

\displaystyle\left(-(3+k)L(w)+3:J(w)^{2}:-\frac{3(1+k)}{2}\partial J(w)\right),

where

c_{k}=-\frac{(1+3k)(3+2k)}{3+k}.

On another side, the
\mathcal{W}
-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
is strongly generated by the fields
L(z)
and
W(z)
of conformal weights
2
and
4
respectively satisfying the OPE:

\displaystyle W(z)W(w)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{w_{k}c_{k}}{3(z-w)^{6}}+\frac{2w_{k}}{(z-w)^{4}}L(w)+\frac{w_{k}}{2(z-w)^{3}}\partial L(w)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2(3+k)^{2}}{3}:L(w)^{2}:-\frac{3(3+k)^{2}(2+k)^{2}}{4}\partial^{2}L(w)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(z-w)}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2(3+k)^{3}}{3}:L(w)\partial L(w):-\frac{(3+k)^{2}(18+14k+3k^{2})}{18}\partial^{3}L(w)\right),

where

c_{k}=-\frac{2(5+3k)(9+4k)}{3+k},

and

w_{k}=-\frac{(3+k)^{2}(4+3k)(12+5k)}{6}.

There are three non-trivial nilpotent orbits in

\displaystyle G^{\pm}(z)F^{\pm}(w)

4.1.
Trivial simple
\mathcal{W}
-algebras

Consider the
\mathcal{W}
-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
associated with a nilpotent element
f\in\mathfrak{g}
. Denote
\mathcal{I}_{k}
its maximal ideal – which depends on the level
k
. We have
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)=\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)/\mathcal{I}_{k}
.
At non-critical level, it follows from the conformal structure of
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
that the vertex algebra
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is trivial, i.e. isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}|0\rangle
, if and only if the conformal vector generates
\mathcal{I}_{k}
.
This happens only if the central charge
c_{k}
is zero, but this necessary condition is not sufficient.
For instance, the conformal vector
L
of
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{sp}_{4},f_{\mathrm{min}})
does not belong to the maximal ideal when
k=-1
whereas
c_{-1}=0
.
Indeed, according to the OPEs described in Sect.
3.2
, if
L
were in
\mathcal{I}_{-1}
then
J
would be in
\mathcal{I}_{-1}
as well as the vacuum.

Adding a supplementary condition, we obtain a complete criterion. We enjoy this opportunity to mention that the proofs of Propositions 2.2.2 and 2.2.7 in
[
12
]
are incomplete. We correct them in this paper. It does not impact the results presented in the thesis.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
J^{(1)},\ldots,J^{(n)}
be a set of strong generators of
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
with conformal weights
w_{1}\ldots w_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
respectively.
The simple quotient
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is trivial if and only if for any
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n
the pole of degree
w_{i}+w_{j}
of the OPE
J^{(j)}(z)J^{(i)}(w)
vanishes, that is
(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}}J^{(i)}=0
.

Proof.

Since the
\mathcal{W}
-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is positively graded by the conformal vector, its simple quotient is trivial if and only if all the strong generators are in the maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}_{k}
.
For all
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n
, the vector
(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}}J^{(i)}
has conformal weight zero. Since
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is canonical, i.e.
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)_{0}=\mathbb{C}|0\rangle
, it vanishes.

Conversely, let
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n
. For
m>0
,
(J^{(j)})_{m}J^{(i)}
has conformal weight
w_{i}-m
and it is a linear combination of normally ordered products and derivatives of strong generators
J^{(\ell)}
of conformal weight
w_{\ell}
lower than
w_{i}-m<w_{i}
. In particular,
(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}}J^{(i)}
is a multiple of the vacuum: it exists
c_{i,j}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}}J^{(i)}=c_{i,j}|0\rangle.

It follows that
J^{(i)}
is in
\mathcal{I}_{k}
if all strong generators
J^{(\ell)}
of conformal weight lower than
w_{i}
are in
\mathcal{I}_{k}
and if
c_{i,j}=0
for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
.
Note that if
J^{(i)}
has the lowest conformal weight,
(J^{(j)})_{m}J^{(i)}=0
for all
m\neq w_{i}
and so
J^{(i)}
is in
\mathcal{I}_{k}
if and only if
(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}}J^{(i)}=0
.

Therefore, if
(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}}J^{(i)}=0
for all
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n
, then all the strong generators are in
\mathcal{I}_{k}
and thus
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)\simeq\mathbb{C}|0\rangle
.

∎

4.2.
Simple quotients isomorphic to Virasoro vertex algebras

From now on, we consider non-trivial simple
\mathcal{W}
-algebras. In particular, the conformal vector does not belong to the maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}_{k}
of
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)

, then
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is isomorphic to the Virasoro vertex algebra

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
J^{(1)},\ldots,J^{(n)}:=L
be a set of strong generators of
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
with conformal weights
w_{1}\ldots w_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
respectively.
The simple quotient
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is isomorphic to the simple Virasoro vertex algebra
\Vir_{c_{k}}
if and only if for any
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
and
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
,
(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}}J^{(i)}=0
and
(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}-2}J^{(i)}
does not depend on
L
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one of Proposition
4.1
.
The simple quotient
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)
is isomorphic to
\Vir_{c_{k}}
if and only if it is strongly generated only by the conformal vector
L
.
Hence,
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)\simeq\Vir_{c_{k}}
if and only if
J^{(1)},\ldots,J^{(n-1)}\in\mathcal{I}_{k}
and
L\notin\mathcal{I}_{k}
.
Since
J^{(1)},\ldots,J^{{(n-1)}}\in\mathcal{I}_{k}
, then
(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}}J^{(i)}=0
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
and
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
. Moreover,

(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}-2}J^{(i)}
is a linear combination of normally ordered products and derivatives of strong generators
J^{(\ell)}
of conformal weight lower than
2
. There exist
c_{i,j}\in\mathbb{C}
and a linear combination
R_{i,j}
of normally ordered products and derivatives of the strong generators
J^{(\ell)}
,
1\leqslant\ell\leqslant n-1
, such that

(3)

(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}-2}J^{(i)}=c_{i,j}L+R_{i,j}.

Since
(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}-2}J^{(i)}
and
R_{i,j}
belongs to
\mathcal{I}_{k}
, we deduce that
c_{i,j}=0
. Thus,
(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}-2}J^{(i)}
does not depend on
L
.

Conversely, let
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
and
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
. For
m>0
,
(J^{(j)})_{m}J^{(i)}
is a sum of normally ordered products and derivatives of strong generators
J^{(\ell)}
of conformal weight lower than
w_{i}-m<w_{i}
.
In particular,
(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}}J^{(i)}
is a multiple of the vacuum. In addition,
L
can appear only as a summand in the decomposition of
(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}-2}J^{(i)}
. We have a decomposition (
3
).
Since
L\notin\mathcal{I}_{k}
,
J^{(i)}
is in the maximal ideal if
(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}}J^{(i)}=0
and
(J^{(j)})_{w_{i}-2}J^{(i)}
does not depend on
L
for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
.
Therefore, if this condition is satisfied for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
, then all the strong generators except
L
are in
\mathcal{I}_{k}
and
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\mathfrak{g},f)\simeq\Vir_{c_{k}}
.
∎

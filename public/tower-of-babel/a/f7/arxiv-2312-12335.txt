Nondefectivity of invariant secant varieties
Alexander Taveira Blomenhofer, Alex Casarotti

Abstract
. We show that a large class of secant varieties is nondefective. In particular, we positively resolve most cases of the Baur-Draisma-de Graaf conjecture on Grassmannian secants in large dimensions. Our result improves the known bounds on nondefectivity for various other secant varieties, including Chow varieties, Segre-Veronese varieties and Gaussian moment varieties. We also give bounds for identifiability and the generic ranks.

1.
Introduction

Our starting point is the empirical observation that many secant varieties behave highly “regular” with respect to dimension. If one picks a “reasonable” base variety
V
of small dimension
N
in a space
S^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
of polynomials, then very often, the first secant varieties
\sigma_{m}(V)
will have the
expected dimension
for small
m
, which, in affine notation, equals
mN
.

Conversely, for large
m
, the secants of a nondegenerate variety
V
will fill up the entire space
S^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. The smallest
m
such that the
m
-th secant fills the whole space is called the
generic
V
-rank
, denoted
m^{\circ}
. Frequently, the lower bound

(1)

\displaystyle m^{\circ}\geq\frac{\dim S^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}{\dim V},

obtained from counting parameters, is a very close estimate for the true value of
m^{\circ}
. One of the first proven results of this type was the Alexander-Hirschowitz theorem
[
15
]
, which concerned decompositions

(2)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell_{i}^{d}

of a
d
-form
f
in
n
variables as a sum of
d
-th powers of linear forms
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}
. Decompositions like
Equation

2
are called
1
-Waring decompositions. The value
m
is called the
1
-Waring
rank
of the decomposition. In this example,
V=\nu_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is the degree-
d
Veronese embedding of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Any form
f
with a decomposition like
Equation

2
lies in the
m
-th secant variety
\sigma_{m}(V)
. The Alexander-Hirschowitz theorem states that for all but finitely many
(n,d)
with
n\geq 2,d\geq 3
, the generic
1
-Waring rank is the rounded-up value of
\frac{\dim S^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}{\dim V}
, and that most secants of rank smaller than
m^{\circ}
have the expected dimension.

If the linear forms
\ell_{i}
are replaced by forms
q_{i}
of higher degree
k
, then one obtains the notion of a
k
-Waring decomposition

(3)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}^{d}

of a form
f
of degree
kd
. Those higher-order Waring decompositions can be traced back a long time. E.g., Ramanujan
[
22
]
posed a famous problem about sums of cubes of quadratics in 1913. In recent times, the study of Waring decomposition was fueled by connections to Machine Learning
[
4
]
and circuit complexity
[
14
]
. Defectivity and identifiability properties with respect to
k
-Waring problem have been treated in
[
8
]
.

Yet another type of decompositions within
S^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
are Chow decompositions. Here, one aims to write a
d
-form

(4)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell_{i,1}\cdot\ldots\cdot\ell_{i,d}

as a sum of
d
-fold products of linear forms
\ell_{i,j}
. All of these decompositions have one thing in common: They start with an irreducible, affine cone
V\subseteq S^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
that is closed under the action of
\GL(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Subsequently, they try to write an element
f
of
S^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
as a sum of finitely many elements of
V
.

Therefore, one may produce more problems of this type, by continuing the list of
\GL
-invariant subvarieties of
S^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Machine Learning motivates to look at
Gaussian moment varieties
, since their secants are important to Gaussian mixture models.

A very similar behaviour is encountered, if instead of the symmetric powers, one looks at canonical subvarieties of the alternating powers
\bigwedge^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. The most natural choice is the Grassmannian variety
\grass(n,d)
of
d
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. It may be embedded into
\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))
via the
Plücker map

(5)

\displaystyle\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}\rangle\mapsto[u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{d}],

which sends a basis
u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}
of the space to the projective equivalence class of its wedge product. Secant varieties of Grassmannians are of significance to coding theory, as outlined by Baur, Draisma and de Graaf
[
16
]
.

Contributions

We give a unified treatment and bounds for nondefectivity and the generic rank in all the cases mentioned above. For Grassmannians, Chow varieties, Gaussian moment and Segre-Veronese varieties, our new bounds improve significantly on the best known ones and are asymptotically optimal for large
n
. The common structure in the examples above is the presence of a group action, e.g.,
G=\GL(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, and of an irreducible
G
-module
\mathcal{L}
, e.g.,
\mathcal{L}=S^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
or
\mathcal{L}=\bigwedge^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. We leverage invariant-theoretic techniques to show that all of the above-mentioned varieties, and many more, will have most of their secant dimensions as expected.
Our main result is
Theorem

3.4
, but the applications all follow from its Corollary,
Theorem

1.1
, which we will state here and prove in
3.5
.

1.1 Theorem
.

Let
G
be a group and
V
an
N
-dimensional, irreducible affine cone in an irreducible
G
-module
\mathcal{L}
. If
V
is closed under the
G
-action, then it holds that

(1)

V
is
m
-nondefective for all
m\leq\frac{\dim\mathcal{L}}{N}-N
.

(2)

The generic rank (with respect to
V
) is between
\frac{\dim\mathcal{L}}{N}
and
\frac{\dim\mathcal{L}}{N}+N
.

Despite its generality,
Theorem

1.1
gives better bounds than quite a few specialized results for specific varieties.
In
Section

4
, we list cases where our new result improves on the currently best-known bounds for nondefectivity, identifiability and/or the generic rank. In many of those cases, our bounds are asymptotically optimal for large
n
. In particular, we prove most cases of the Baur-Draisma-de Graaf conjecture, stated in
4.2
.

Organization

We prove our main result in
Section

3
. In
Section

4
, we apply it to a plethora of different varieties, where we can obtain improved bounds on nondefectivity and the generic rank.

2.
Preliminaries

Let us briefly recall the main notions used in this paper. Most of our varieties will be affine cones
V
in a linear space
\mathcal{L}
, i.e.
\mathbb{C}\cdot V\subseteq V
. The corresponding projective varieties we denote by
\mathbb{P}(V)\subseteq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L})
. Frequently, we will also have a group
G
acting on the space
\mathcal{L}
, giving it the structure of a
G
-module.
We say that a variety
V
is
G
-
invariant
, if for all
v\in V
and
g\in G
, it holds
gv\in V
. We use two different notions of irreducibility, that are not to be confused: A variety is called irreducible, if it cannot be covered by the union of two proper subvarieties. A
G
-module
\mathcal{L}
is called irreducible, if
\mathcal{L}
is not the zero module and
\mathcal{L}
does not contain any proper nonzero
G
-submodules. The set of smooth points of a variety
V
is denoted
V_{\smooth}
. For every smooth point
x\in V
we denote by
T_{x}V
the tangent space to
V
at
x
. If
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
are smooth points of
V
we denote by
\langle T_{x_{1}}V_{1},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}V_{m}\rangle
the linear span of the corresponding tangent spaces. The space of
d
-forms on a vector space
\mathcal{L}
is denoted
S^{d}(\mathcal{L})
. A special case is the dual space
\mathcal{L}^{\vee}=S^{1}(\mathcal{L})
.

Definition 1
.

The
m
-th secant variety

\sigma_{m}V
of the affine cone
V
is the Zariski closure of

(6)

\displaystyle\{x_{1}+\ldots+x_{m}\mid x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in V\}.

The
m
-th secant variety has the
expected dimension

e_{m}(V):=\min\{mN,\dim\mathcal{L}\}
. If the dimension of
\sigma_{m}V
is smaller than expected, we say that
V
is
m
-
defective
. Otherwise,
V
is called
m
-
nondefective
. We say that
V
is
m
-
identifiable
, if the general point
p\in\sigma_{m}V
has, up to permutation, a unique representation
p=x_{1}+\ldots+x_{m}
, where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in V
.
Secant varieties are a special case of joins: The
join
of affine cones
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
is defined as the closure of
\{x_{1}+\ldots+x_{m}\mid x_{1}\in V_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in V_{m}\}
. We denote it by
J(V_{1},\ldots,V_{m})
.

The main tool in order to compute the dimension of joins and secant varieties dates back to Terracini
[
24
]
and can be summarized as follows:

2.1 Lemma

(Terracini, see
[
24
]
)
.

For general
x_{1}\in V_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in V_{m}
and general
z\in\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\rangle
, the tangent space to the join
J(V_{1},\ldots,V_{m})
at
z
equals
\langle T_{x_{1}}V_{1},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}V_{m}\rangle
.

Moreover, for smooth points
x_{1}\in V_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in V_{m}
, and any
z\in\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\rangle
,
\langle T_{x_{1}}V,\ldots,T_{x_{m}}V\rangle
is still contained in the tangent to
J(V_{1},\ldots,V_{m})
at
z
.

2.2 Proposition
.

The
apex space
of an (irreducible) affine cone
V
is defined as

(7)

\displaystyle\apex(V)=\{x\in V\mid V+x=V\}.

For brevity, we also denote
\apex_{m}(V):=\apex(\sigma_{m}V)
. It holds that

(8)

\displaystyle\apex_{m}(V)=\bigcap_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathcal{U}_{m,V}}\langle T_{x_{1}}V,\ldots,T_{x_{m}}V\rangle,

where
\mathcal{U}_{V,m}
denotes the set

(9)

\displaystyle\{x\in V_{\mathrm{reg}}^{m}\mid\langle T_{x_{1}}V,\ldots,T_{x_{m}}V\rangle=T_{x_{1}+\ldots+x_{m}}\sigma_{m}V\text{ has generic dimension}\}.

Proof.

For the inclusion from left to right, let
p\in\apex_{m}(V)
and let
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathcal{U}_{V,m}
. By definition of the
m
-apex, it holds
J(\sigma_{m}V,\langle p\rangle)=\sigma_{m}V
. Terracini’s Lemma applied to the join yields
\langle T_{x_{1}}V,\ldots,T_{x_{m}}V,p\rangle\subseteq\langle T_{x_{1}}V,\ldots,T_{x_{m}}V\rangle
. Thus,
p
lies in
\langle T_{x_{1}}V,\ldots,T_{x_{m}}V\rangle
.

Conversely, let
p
in the right hand side. Choose a general point
x\in\mathcal{U}_{V,m}
. Again by Terracini’s Lemma, equality
\langle T_{x_{1}}V,\ldots,T_{x_{m}}V,p\rangle=\langle T_{x_{1}}V,\ldots,T_{x_{m}}V\rangle
holds. Since
x
is general, thus the dimensions of
J(\sigma_{m}V,\langle p\rangle)
and of
\sigma_{m}V
are equal. By irreducibility, the varieties are equal. Thus
p\in\apex_{m}(V)
.
∎

.

The apex space is a linear space satisfying

If

\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle T\colon\mathbb{P}(V)\to\mathrm{Gr}(N,\mathcal{L}),[x]\mapsto T_{x},

(11)

\displaystyle U\colon\mathbb{P}(V^{\prime})\to\mathrm{Gr}(N^{\prime},\mathcal{L}),[y]\mapsto U_{y},

By

, we denote the Grassmannian variety of
N

\mathcal{L}

Let
\mathcal{U}_{T,m}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\mid\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle\text{ is of generic dimension}\}

\displaystyle\apex_{m}(T):=\bigcap_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathcal{U}_{T,m}}\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle

3.1 Lemma
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
a dense open subset of
\mathcal{U}_{T,m}
. Then

(13)

\displaystyle\apex_{m}(T)=\bigcap_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathcal{U}}\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle

Proof.

The inclusion from left to right is clear. Now, let
p
be contained in the right hand side. Then,

lies in
\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle
for all

\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle

, by the Plücker embedding, we can represent

is the zero tensor for all

. By continuity, it is constantly zero. But for

.

3.2 Lemma

(Stationarity Lemma)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
an irreducible
G
-module and
V
,
V^{\prime}
,
T
,
U
,
(a_{m})_{m}
as introduced above. If
m\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
a_{m+1}=a_{m}\neq 0
, then
\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle=\mathcal{L}
and
a_{m}=N^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Consider generic points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m}^{\prime}\in V
, and generic
y\in V^{\prime}
. We know that

\displaystyle\dim\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle\cap U_{y}=a_{m}=a_{m+1}=\dim\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}},T_{x_{m+1}}\rangle\cap U_{y}.

Therefore, it also holds

(16)

\displaystyle\dim\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle\cap U_{y}=\dim\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}},T_{x_{m}^{\prime}}\rangle\cap U_{y}.

The space on the left-hand side is a subspace of the space on the right-hand side, of same dimension. Hence, they are equal:

(17)

\displaystyle\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle\cap U_{y}=\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}},T_{x_{m}^{\prime}}\rangle\cap U_{y}.

We may apply the same argument with exchanged roles of
x_{m}
and
x_{m}^{\prime}
to obtain

\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m-1}},T_{x_{m}^{\prime}}\rangle\cap U_{y}=\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}},T_{x_{m}^{\prime}}\rangle\cap U_{y}.

Together, this implies

(18)

\displaystyle\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m-1}},T_{x_{m}^{\prime}}\rangle\cap U_{y}=\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle\cap U_{y}

We thus showed that swapping
x_{m}
with
x_{m}^{\prime}
does not change the space from
Equation

18
.
Repeating the procedure with all pairs of
x_{i},x_{i}^{\prime}
, we obtain

(19)

\displaystyle\langle T_{x_{1}^{\prime}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}^{\prime}}\rangle\cap U_{y}=\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle\cap U_{y}

In other words, any point

in the set from
Equation

19
lies in
\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle
for all general choices of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
.
Thus,

(20)

\displaystyle\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle\cap U_{y}=\apex_{m}(T)\cap U_{y},

by

.
In particular, the

\apex_{m}(T)
is

is an irreducible

∎

3.3 Remark
.

Similar arguments were present in the work of Ådlandsvik
[
1
]
and Nenashev
[

]
. Ådlandsvik considered the case where

is the tangent map of a variety

Independently, Nenashev proved in
[

to be any

and

, which is an irreducible

are equal and given by

. In other words, a form

is mapped to some graded component of the principal ideal it generates.

3.4 Theorem
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an irreducible
G
-module,
V,V^{\prime}
irreducible
G
-invariant affine cones of dimensions
N
and
N^{\prime}
, respectively. Let
T\colon\mathbb{P}(V)\to\grass(N,\mathcal{L})
and
U\colon\mathbb{P}(V^{\prime})\to\grass(N^{\prime},\mathcal{L})
be
G
-equivariant maps.
Then, there exists an interval
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
of length at most
N^{\prime}
, such that for all
m\notin\mathcal{I}
, and generic
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in V
, generic
y\in V^{\prime}
, either one of the following holds true:

(1)

\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle\cap U_{y}=\{0\}
or

(2)

\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m}}\rangle=\mathcal{L}.

Proof.

There exists a smallest
m_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m_{1}}}\rangle=\mathcal{L}
. There also exists a largest
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
\langle T_{x_{1}},\ldots,T_{x_{m_{0}}}\rangle\cap U_{y}=\{0\}
. The claim holds true for all
m\notin\mathcal{I}:=\{m_{0}+1,\ldots,m_{1}\}
. The length of this interval is
m_{1}-m_{0}
. By
Lemma

3.2
, we know that the dimension of the intersection is strictly increasing between
m_{0}
and
m_{1}
, and for
m_{1}
, the intersection equals
U_{y}
. Therefore,

(21)

\displaystyle m_{1}-m_{0}\leq a_{m_{1}}-a_{m_{0}}=\dim U_{y}=N^{\prime}.

This asserts the claim.
∎

In the special case
T=U
, it is possible to deduce explicit bounds for the quantities
m_{0}
and
m_{1}
, which occur in the proof of
Theorem

3.4
. In the following, we show this for the even more special case, where

is the tangent map of a variety. In this way, we obtain a very general criterion for secant nondefectivity and estimates for the generic rank with respect to a large class of base varieties.

3.5 Corollary
.

Let
V
be an
N
-dimensional, irreducible subvariety of the irreducible
G
-module
\mathcal{L}
. Assume
V
is
G
-invariant. Then,

(1)

V
is
m
-nondefective for all
m\leq\frac{\dim\mathcal{L}}{N}-N
.

(2)

The generic rank of
\mathcal{L}
with respect to
V
is at most
m^{\circ}\leq\frac{\dim\mathcal{L}}{N}+N
.

Proof.

In
Theorem

3.4
, take
T=U
as the tangent map

(22)

\displaystyle T\colon V_{\smooth}\to\grass(\mathcal{L},N),x\mapsto T_{x}V,

which takes a point
x\in V
to its tangent space. We have
N=N^{\prime}=\dim V
in the notation of
Theorem

3.4
. Let
m_{0}
and
m_{1}
be as in
Theorem

3.4
. From counting parameters, we have the obvious bounds
N(m_{0}+1)\leq\dim\mathcal{L}
and
Nm_{1}\geq\dim\mathcal{L}
. Using these together with
Equation

21
, we obtain

(23)

\displaystyle m_{0}\geq m_{1}-N\geq\frac{\dim\mathcal{L}}{N}-N,

and

(24)

\displaystyle m_{1}\leq m_{0}+N\leq\frac{\dim\mathcal{L}-N}{N}+N=\frac{\dim\mathcal{L}}{N}+N-1.

and we obtain that a sum of
m
tangent spaces is direct, as long as
m\leq\frac{\dim\mathcal{L}}{N}-N
. Similarly, we also obtain from
Theorem

3.4
that if
m\geq\frac{\dim\mathcal{L}}{N}+N
, then the
m
-th secant variety
\sigma_{m}V
must fill everything.
∎

We now state an important result by Massarenti and Mella

3.6 Theorem

(Massarenti, Mella,
[
18

)
.

Let
V\subseteq\mathcal{L}
be an irreducible and non-degenerate variety of dimension
N
,
m\geq 1

(1)

mN\leq\dim\mathcal{L}
,

(2)

The fibers of the tangent map

(3)

Then,
V
is

In recent work by Torrance and Vannieuwenhoven, see

4.7 Theorem
.

The Chow variety
\chow_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

-nondefective (and
(m-1)
-identifiable), if
m
is at most
\frac{1}{d(n-1)+1}\binom{n+d-1}{d}-d(n-1)-1
.

1.1
, after plugging in the dimension of

. Oeding computed the dimension of the dual of the Chow variety in
[

implies

-identifiability.
∎

Segre-Veronese varieties

Given
t\in\mathbb{N}
, vector spaces
\underline{L}=(L_{1},\ldots,L_{t})
of dimensions
\underline{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})
and nonnegative integers
\underline{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{t})
, the Segre-Veronese variety on
\underline{L}
of multidegree
\underline{d}
is defined as

(32)

\displaystyle\segreveronese_{\underline{d}}(\underline{L}):=\{\ell_{1}^{d_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\ell_{t}^{d_{t}}\mid\ell_{1}\in L_{1}^{\vee},\ldots,\ell_{t}\in L_{t}^{\vee}\}.

It lives in
S^{d_{1}}(L_{1})\otimes\ldots\otimes S^{d_{t}}(L_{t})
. This space is naturally endowed with the action of
G:=\GL(L_{1})\times\ldots\times\GL(L_{t})
.

4.8 Theorem
.

The Segre-Veronese variety of multidegree
\underline{d}
and dimensions
\underline{n}
is
m
-nondefective (and
(m-1)
-identifiable), if
m
is bounded by

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-t+1}\binom{n_{1}+d_{1}-1}{d_{1}}\cdots\binom{n_{t}+d_{t}-1}{d_{t}}-(n-t+1),

where
n:=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}
. Furthermore, the generic rank
m^{\circ}
is at most

(34)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-t+1}\binom{n_{1}+d_{1}-1}{d_{1}}\cdots\binom{n_{t}+d_{t}-1}{d_{t}}+(n-t+1).

Proof.

As outlined before,
\mathcal{L}:=S^{d_{1}}(L_{1})\otimes\ldots\otimes S^{d_{t}}(L_{t})
has the structure of a
G
-module, where
G:=\GL(L_{1})\times\ldots\times\GL(L_{t})
. But
\mathcal{L}
is in fact an irreducible
G
-module, since it is the tensor product of the irreducible
\GL(L_{i})
-modules
S^{d_{i}}(L_{i})
, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
.
Note that
\segreveronese_{\underline{d}}(\underline{L})
is invariant with respect of the action of
G
, and of dimension
n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}-t+1
. Therefore,
Theorem

1.1
is applicable. Calculating the dimensions of
\mathcal{L}
and the Segre-Veronese variety explicitly yields the result on nondefectivity. Since Segre-Veronese varieties are smooth,
Theorem

3.6
yields
(m-1)
-identifiability.
∎

To the best of our knowledge the actual best bound in the literature is due to Laface-Massarenti-Rischter in

. They give an asymptotical sharp bound on

of the form:

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{d_{j}}{(n_{j}+d_{j}-1)\cdot(n-t+1)}\binom{n_{1}+d_{1}-1}{d_{1}}\cdots\binom{n_{t}+d_{t}-1}{d_{t}}

where

\frac{n_{j}-1}{d_{j}}=\max_{1\leq i\leq t}\{\frac{n_{i}-1}{d_{i}}\}.

Our bound from Theorem

however covers asymptotically more cases then Laface-Massarenti-Rischter one. In Figure

\underline{d}=(1,1,2)
and fixed dimension

The Gaussian moment variety

Gaussian mixtures are an ubiquitous model in Machine Learning. Their identifiability is a long-standing open problem, with the univariate case dating back to Pearson, see
[
21
]
. We refer to
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
for some historical remarks. They are closely connected to secants of the
Gaussian moment variety
, defined as follows.

4.9 Definition
.

Let
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
. We call the closure of the image of

(36)

\displaystyle s_{d}\colon S^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\times S^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\to S^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),(\ell,q)\mapsto\exp\left(\ell+\frac{q}{2}\right)_{=d},

the degree-
d

Gaussian moment variety

\GM_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Here,
\exp
is to be understood as a formal power series on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and the subscript denotes its
d
-homogeneous part.

The parameter
\ell
is a linear form, which corresponds to the mean of the Gaussian, while the parameter
q
is a quadratic form, corresponding to the covariance matrix.
In
[
7
]
and
[
6
]
, the authors gave thorough motivations of the Gaussian moment variety and explained the connection between the statistical estimation problem for Gaussian mixtures and the algebraic identifiability problem for the Gaussian moment variety. In particular, they also showed why for generic identifiability, the constrains of realness and positive-definiteness of
\ell
and
q
, respectively, may be ignored. We settle most cases of Gaussian identifiability for high-dimensional models in the degrees
d=5,\ldots,9
. Note that in applications, the lower degrees are the most relevant ones. Degree
5
is minimal for any nontrivial identifiability result.

4.10 Theorem
.

For
d\geq 5
and
n\geq 2
, the following hold:

(1)

The Gaussian moment varieties
\mathrm{GM}_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
are
m
-nondefective, as long as

\displaystyle m\leq\frac{\dim S^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}{\dim\mathrm{GM}_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}-\dim\mathrm{GM}_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\frac{2{n+d-1\choose d}}{n(n+3)}-\frac{1}{2}n(n+3).

(2)

In addition, the generic rank with respect to the Gaussian moment variety is at most

\displaystyle\frac{{n+d-1\choose d}}{\dim\mathrm{GM}_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}+\dim\mathrm{GM}_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

(3)

For
d=5,\ldots,9
,
m
-nondefectivity of
\mathrm{GM}_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
implies
(m-1)
-identifiability.

Proof.

The first two claims are a direct consequence of
Theorem

1.1
. Note that the dimension of
\GM_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is
n+\binom{n+1}{2}
, since it is parameterized by
S^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\oplus S^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. This value can also be written as
\frac{1}{2}n(n+3)
. For the third claim, we use
Theorem

3.6
, which shows that
m
-nondefectivity implies
(m-1)
-identifiability, if the tangent map is nondegenerate. The tangent map condition was verified for
d=5,\ldots,9
with a partially computer-assisted proof in
[
6
, Proposition 2.13]
.
∎

4.11 Remark
.

For
d\leq 4
, the Gaussian moment varieties are already
2
-defective, cf. for instance
[
6
]
. On the other hand, for
d\geq 5
, the dimension of
\mathrm{GM}_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
grows of smaller order in
n
than the parameter counting rank. Therefore,
Theorem

4.10
asymptotically covers almost all interesting cases of nondefectivity, cf.
Remark

4.17
. We conjecture that
\mathrm{GM}_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
has nondegenerate tangent map for all
d\geq 5
and
n\geq 2
. A proof of this would extend
Theorem

4.10
(3) to all degrees
d\geq 5
.

Low-rank Gaussians

We can prove a similar result for rank-
r
Gaussians.

4.12 Corollary
.

Let
n,m,d,r\in\mathbb{N}
and denote

(37)

\displaystyle\GM_{d,r}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\{s_{d}(\ell,q)\mid\rk q\leq r\}.

Then,
\GM_{d,r}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is
m
-nondefective, as long as

(38)

\displaystyle m\leq\frac{\dim S^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}{M_{r}}-M_{r},

where
M_{r}:=(r+1)n-\binom{r}{2}
. The generic rank is at most
\frac{\dim S^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}{M_{r}}+M_{r}
.

Proof.

Counting parameters, we have that for
d\geq 3
,
\GM_{d}(\mathcal{G}_{r})
has dimension equal to
n
(for the linear form) plus
r(n-r)+\binom{r+1}{2}=rn-\binom{r}{2}
for the rank-
r
covariance form. This sum equals
M_{r}=(r+1)n-\binom{r}{2}
. Clearly,
\GM_{d}(\mathcal{G}_{r})
is a
\GL(\mathbb{C}^{n})
-invariant, irreducible variety. The claim thus follows by
3.5
.
∎

4.13 Remark
.

4.12
treats one example in the large category of
Gaussian models
. These models are very common in applications and they arise from considering some subvariety
\mathcal{G}\subseteq S^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. The major motivation for a restriction to subvarieties is to reduce the number of parameters. This parameter reduction can have a significant impact on defectivity: Indeed,
4.12
implies that, if the rank of the covariance matrices is an absolute constant, then a mixture of
m=\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
general Gaussians is finite-to-one identifiable from its degree
3
moments; a sharp contrast to the case of full Gaussians, where moments of degree
5
are necessary. We believe that nondefectivity results for the rank-
r
Gaussian mixture model were not known before.

Asymptotics and critical degree

4.17 Remark
.

The examples from
Section

1
will be discussed thoroughly in
Section

. However, note that the examples from the introduction have slightly more in common than just

\varphi_{d}(L_{d})
(under equivariant maps) of a linear space
L_{d}=L_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
into spaces
\mathcal{L}_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
of
d
-tensors on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
1
1

1

In fact,
L_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
has the structure of a bounded-degree polynomial functor, for fixed
d
.
In all of the above cases, the dimension of
L_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is a polynomial
p_{d}(n)
in
n
, whose degree
d_{0}:=\deg p_{d}
does not depend on
d
.

Concretely, we have
d_{0}=1
for the Veronese, Chow and Grassmannian varieties. We have
d_{0}=2
for the Gaussian moment variety and
d_{0}=k
for the variety of
d
-th powers of
k
-forms. We may now look at the bounds in
Theorem

1.1
and understand their asymptotical behaviour for

, just in terms of

. Indeed, if
d>2d_{0}
, then for large
n
, almost all secants of
\varphi_{d}(L_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))
will be identifiable. The number of cases not covered by our theorem will be asymptotically negligible. The generic rank will be very close to the parameter count
\frac{\dim\mathcal{L}_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}{\dim\varphi_{d}(L_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))}
.

On the other hand, for
d<2d_{0}

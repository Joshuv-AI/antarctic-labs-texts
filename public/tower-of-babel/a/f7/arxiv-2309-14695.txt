Strong Szegő Limit Theorems for Multi-Bordered, Framed, and Multi-Framed Toeplitz Determinants This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Evolution Equations, Exactly Solvable Models and Random Matrices in honor of Alexander Its’ 70th birthday. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/Its.html
Roozbeh GHARAKHLOO

Abstract
This work provides the general framework for obtaining strong Szegő limit theorems for multi-bordered, semi-framed, framed, and multi-framed Toeplitz determinants, extending the results of Basor et al. (2022) beyond the (single) bordered Toeplitz case. For the two-bordered and also the semi-framed Toeplitz determinants, we compute the strong Szegő limit theorems associated with certain classes of symbols, and for the k k -bordered ( k ≥ 3 {k\geq 3} ), framed, and multi-framed Toeplitz determinants we demonstrate the recursive fashion offered by the Dodgson condensation identities via which strong Szegő limit theorems can be obtained. One instance of appearance of semi-framed Toeplitz determinants is in calculations related to the entanglement entropy for disjoint subsystems in the XX spin chain (Brightmore et al. (2020) and Jin–Korepin (2011)). In addition, in the recent work Gharakhloo and Liechty (2024) and in an unpublished work of Professor Nicholas Witte, such determinants have found relevance respectively in the study of ensembles of nonintersecting paths and in the study of off-diagonal correlations of the anisotropic square-lattice Ising model. Besides the intrinsic mathematical interest in these structured determinants, the aforementioned applications have further motivated the study of the present work.

0&\alpha_{-}(z)\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\alpha^{-1}_{+}(z)&0\\
0&\alpha_{+}(z)\end{bmatrix}.

So, the function

P^{(\infty)}(z):=\begin{cases}\begin{bmatrix}0&\alpha(z)\\
-\alpha^{-1}(z)&0\end{bmatrix},&|z|<1,\\[14.0pt]
\begin{bmatrix}\alpha(z)&0\\
0&\alpha^{-1}(z)\end{bmatrix},&|z|>1,\end{cases}

(A.4)

satisfies (
A.1
). Also, by the properties of the Cauchy integral,
P^{(\infty)}(z)
is holomorphic in
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{T}}
. Moreover,
\alpha(z)=1+O\big(z^{-1}\big)
, as
z\to\infty
, and hence

\displaystyle P^{(\infty)}(z)=I+O(1/z),\qquad z\to\infty.

Therefore,
P^{(\infty)}
given by (
A.4
) is the unique solution of the global parametrix Riemann–Hilbert problem. Let us now consider the ratio

R(z;n):=S(z;n)\big[P^{(\infty)}(z)\big]^{-1}.

We have the following Riemann–Hilbert problem for
R(z;n)
:

•

RH-R1:
R
is holomorphic in
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(\Gamma_{0}\cup\Gamma_{1})
,

•

RH-R2:
R_{+}(z;n)=R_{-}(z;n)J_{R}(z;n)
,
z\in\Gamma_{0}\cup\Gamma_{1}=:\Sigma_{R}
,

•

RH-R3:
R(z;n)=I+O(1/z)
as
z\to\infty
.

This Riemann–Hilbert problem is solvable for large
n
(see
[
21
,
22
]
), and
R(z;n)
can be written as

\displaystyle R(z;n)=I+R_{1}(z;n)+R_{2}(z;n)+R_{3}(z;n)+\cdots,\qquad n\geq n_{0},

where
R_{k}
can be found recursively. Indeed,

\displaystyle R_{k}(z;n)=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\Sigma_{R}}\frac{\left[R_{k-1}(\mu;n)\right]_{-}\left(J_{R}(\mu;n)-I\right)}{\mu-z}{\rm d}\mu,\qquad z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma_{R},\qquad k\geq 1.

It is easy to check that
R_{2\ell}(z;n)
is diagonal and
R_{2\ell+1}(z;n)
is off-diagonal;
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
, and that

\displaystyle R_{k,ij}(z;n)=O\left(\frac{\rho^{-kn}}{1+|z|}\right),\qquad n\to\infty,\qquad k\geq 1,

(A.5)

uniformly in
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma_{R}
, where
\rho

(
resp.
\rho^{-1}
)
is the radius of
\Gamma_{1}
(resp.
\Gamma_{0}
). Let us compute
{R_{1}(z;n)}
; we have

\displaystyle J_{R}(z)-I=\begin{cases}P^{(\infty)}(z)\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
z^{n}\phi^{-1}(z)&0\end{bmatrix}\left[P^{(\infty)}(z)\right]^{-1},&z\in\Gamma_{0},\\[14.0pt]
P^{(\infty)}(z)\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
z^{-n}\phi^{-1}(z)&0\end{bmatrix}\left[P^{(\infty)}(z)\right]^{-1},&z\in\Gamma_{1},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\phantom{J_{R}(z)-I}{}=\begin{cases}\begin{bmatrix}0&-z^{n}\phi^{-1}(z)\alpha^{2}(z)\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},&z\in\Gamma_{0},\\[14.0pt]
\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
z^{-n}\phi^{-1}(z)\alpha^{-2}(z)&0\end{bmatrix},&z\in\Gamma_{1}.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\displaystyle R_{1}(z;n)=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\tfrac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\tfrac{\tau^{n}\phi^{-1}(\tau)\alpha^{2}(\tau)}{\tau-z}{\rm d}\tau\\
\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\tfrac{\tau^{-n}\phi^{-1}(\tau)\alpha^{-2}(\tau)}{\tau-z}{\rm d}\tau&0\end{bmatrix}.

(A.6)

If we trace back the Riemann–Hilbert problems
R\mapsto S\mapsto T\mapsto X
, we will obtain

\displaystyle X(z;n)=R(z;n)\begin{cases}\begin{bmatrix}\alpha(z)&0\\
0&\alpha^{-1}(z)\end{bmatrix}z^{n\sigma_{3}},&z\in\Omega_{\infty},\\[14.0pt]
\begin{bmatrix}\alpha(z)&0\\
-z^{-n}\alpha^{-1}(z)\phi^{-1}(z)&\alpha^{-1}(z)\end{bmatrix}z^{n\sigma_{3}},&z\in\Omega_{2},\\[14.0pt]
\begin{bmatrix}z^{n}\alpha(z)\phi^{-1}(z)&\alpha(z)\\
-\alpha^{-1}(z)&0\end{bmatrix},&z\in\Omega_{1},\\[14.0pt]
\begin{bmatrix}0&\alpha(z)\\
-\alpha^{-1}(z)&0\end{bmatrix},&z\in\Omega_{0},\end{cases}

(A.7)

where for
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma_{R}
, as
n\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle R(z;n)=\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1+O\left(\frac{\rho^{-2n}}{1+|z|}\right)&R_{1,12}(z;n)+O\left(\frac{\rho^{-3n}}{1+|z|}\right)\\
R_{1,21}(z;n)+O\left(\frac{\rho^{-3n}}{1+|z|}\right)&1+O\left(\frac{\rho^{-2n}}{1+|z|}\right)\end{bmatrix}.

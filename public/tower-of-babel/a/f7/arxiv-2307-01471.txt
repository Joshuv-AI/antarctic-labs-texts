On Hofstadter’s G-sequence


Abstract
We characterize the entries of Hofstadter’s G-sequence in terms of the lower and upper Wythoff sequences. This can be used to give a short and comprehensive proof of the equality of Hofstadter’s G-sequence and the sequence of averages of the swapped Wythoff sequences. In a second part we give some results that hold when one replaces the golden mean by other quadratic algebraic numbers. In a third part we prove a close relationship between Hofstadter’s G-sequence and a sequence studied by Avdivpahić and Zejnulahi.

Theorem 2

Suppose that

(0,1)
is irrational, and let
s(n+1)=\lfloor n\gamma\rfloor

n\geq 0

Let

\frac{1}{\gamma}n=L(n)+\varepsilon_{n}

s(L(n))=\big\lfloor(L(n)+1)\gamma\big\rfloor=\big\lfloor n+\gamma(1-\varepsilon_{n})\big\rfloor=n,

since obviously
0\leq\gamma(1-\varepsilon_{n})<1
.

By definition, the sequence
U

\frac{1}{1-\gamma}n=U(n)+\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime},

for some
\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}

s(U(n))=\Big\lfloor(U(n)+1)\gamma\Big\rfloor=\Big\lfloor\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}n+\gamma(1-\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime})\Big\rfloor=\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}n,

since obviously
0\leq\gamma(1-\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime})<1

The following theorem is a conjecture by Murthy in

Theorem 3
.

The averaged Wythoff swap sequence
\overline{W}
is equal to Hofstadter’s G-sequence.

Proof.

The result holds for
n=0,1
. It suffices therefore to consider the sequence of differences.
Subtracting
G(n-1)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}W(i)/n
from
G(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}W(i)/(n+1)
, we see that we have to prove for all
n\geq 2

(n+1)G(n)-nG(n-1)=W(n).

(3)

But we know that there are only two possibilities for the recursion from
G(n-1)
to
G(n)
. Therefore Equation (
3
) turns into the following two equations.

\displaystyle G(n)=G(n-1)

\displaystyle\;\Rightarrow\;G(n)=W(n),

(4)

\displaystyle G(n)=G(n-1)+1

\displaystyle\;\Rightarrow\;G(n)=W(n)-n.

(5)

It is not clear how to prove these equalities directly. However, we can exploit Theorem
1
.
According to this theorem with
s=G
, and
\gamma=(\sqrt{5}-1)/2
, and so
1/\gamma=\varphi
,
1/(1-\gamma)=\varphi^{2}
,

\displaystyle G(n)=G(n-1)

\displaystyle\;\Leftrightarrow\;\exists M\text{ such that }n=U(M),

(6)

\displaystyle G(n)=G(n-1)+1

\displaystyle\;\Leftrightarrow\;\exists M\text{ such that }n=L(M).

(7)

So we first have to prove that
n=U(M)
implies
G(n)=W(n)
. This holds indeed by an application of Theorem
2
and Equation (
2
):

G(n)=G(U(M)=L(M)=W(U(M))=W(n).

Similarly, for the second case
n=L(M)
:

G(n)=G(L(M))=M=U(M)-L(M)=W(L(M))-L(M)=W(n)-n.

Here we applied
U(M)=L(M)+M
for
M\geq 1
, a direct consequence of
\varphi^{2}M=(\varphi+1)M
.
∎

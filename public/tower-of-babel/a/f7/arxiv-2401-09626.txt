Introduction

Currently, a large amount of research is being done on studying elliptic curves and their twists. Many new discoveries and progress have been made in this area. However, despite the large amount of progress there are still a lot of open questions.

One such question, concerning quadratic twists of elliptic curve, is the Goldfeld’s conjecture
[
Gol79
]
.

Conjecture

(
Goldfeld’s conjecture
)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
. Denote with
\rank{(E)}
the rank of the elliptic curve
E
, with
E^{d}
the quadratic twist of
E
by a square-free integer
d
and with

S(x)=\{\text{square-free }d\in\mathbb{Z}:\,\lvert d\rvert\leq x\}

the set of all square-free integers between
-x
and
x
, for a real number
x
. Then the following holds

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{d\in S(x)}\rank{(E^{d})}}{\#S(x)}=\frac{1}{2}.

Smith, in
[
Smi17
]
(see Corollary
1.3
), has proven (assuming the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for a certain set of elliptic curves) that the Goldfeld’s conjecture is true for elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
that have full rational
2
-torsion and don’t have a rational cyclic subgroup of order
4
.

The above conjecture has also a very interesting consequence about the rank of quadratic twists of elliptic curves. By assuming that the Goldfeld’s conjecture and the parity conjecture are true it follows that for
100\%
of square-free integers
d
the rank of
E^{d}/\mathbb{Q}
is ether
0
or
1
.

Another interesting thing to study about quadratic twists of elliptic curve are their
2
-Selmer groups. A lot of progress is made in studying the dimension and the distribution of
2
-Selmer groups of the quadratic twists of elliptic curve and following articles only illustrate some of the results.

Barrera Salazar, Pacetti and Tornaría in
[
BSPT21
]
have obtained a lower and an upper bound for the
2
-Selmer rank of an elliptic curve
E
over a number field
K
without a
K
-rational point of order
2
(under certain hypotheses on the reduction of
E
modulo primes of
K
). As an application, they proved that under certain mild hypotheses a positive portion of the quadratic twists of
E
have the same
2
-Selmer group.

Morgan and Paterson in
[
MP22
]
studied the distribution of the group
\operatorname{Sel}^{2}(E^{d}/K)
, for a fixed elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
and a quadratic extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
, as
d
varies over square-free integers. They have shown that, when considering square-free integers
\lvert d\rvert<X
, the random variable

\frac{\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\operatorname{Sel}^{2}(E^{d}/K)-\log{\log{\lvert d\rvert}}}{\sqrt{2\log{\log{\lvert d\rvert}}}}

converges to the standard normal distribution as
X\to\infty
. It follows that for any fixed real number
z
,
0\%
of quadratic twists satisfy
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\operatorname{Sel}^{2}(E^{d}/K)\leq z
. In particular, the average size of
\operatorname{Sel}^{2}(E^{d}/K)
is infinite. Additionally, if
E
has no rational cyclic
4
-isogeny, then the average size of
\operatorname{Sel}^{2}(E^{d}/\mathbb{Q})
is finite and the probability that
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\operatorname{Sel}^{2}(E^{d}/\mathbb{Q})=n
is positive for every integer
n\geq 2
.

Yu in
[
Yu17
]
has proven that for an arbitrary elliptic curve
E
over an arbitrary number field
K
, if the set
A_{E}
of
2
-Selmer ranks of quadratic twists of
E
contains an integer
c
, then it contains all integers larger than
c
having the same parity as
c
. In addition, Yu also finds sufficient conditions on the set
A_{E}
such that
A_{E}
is equal to
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq t_{E}}
for some number
t_{E}
and gives an upper bound for
t_{E}
in case all points in
E[2]
are rational.

Instead of elliptic curves we can also consider quartics of genus one. One interesting question for genus one quartics over
\mathbb{Q}
is whether they have a rational point. Namely, if a genus one quartic
C/\mathbb{Q}
has a rational point then the curve
C
is birationally isomorphic to an elliptic curve
E
. In this case studying rational points on the curve
C
reduces to studying rational points on the elliptic curve
E
.

Surprisingly, for genus one quartics not much is known about the existence of a rational point. The following articles deal with the question whether quadratic twists of a genus one quartic have a rational point or not.

Kazalicki in his recent work
[
Kaz22
]
connects rational Diophantine
D(d)
-quintuples with quadratic twists of a genus one quartic
H
and their
2
-Selmer groups. In his work he also partially answers the question for which primes
p
the quadratic twist of
H
by
p
has a rational point.

Çiperiani and Ozman in
[
cO15
]
have found necessary and sufficient conditions for a point
P
of an elliptic curve to lie in the image of the global trace map. These conditions can be used to determine whether a quadratic twist of a genus one curve has a rational points.

Motivated by those works, we are inspired to study the quadratic twists of genus one quartics. A natural first step in answering the question whether such twists have a rational point is examining the local properties of those twists. Thus, we try to answer the following question.

4.
Some results about frobenian multiplicative functions

We will start by recalling some useful facts about frobenian multiplicative functions following
[
LM22
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\rho:\operatorname{Val}(\mathbb{Q})\to\mathbb{C}
be a function. We say that
\rho
is
frobenian
if there exist

(a)

A finite Galois extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
, with Galois group
\Gamma
,

(b)

finite set of primes
S
containing all the primes ramifying in
K
,

(c)

A class function
\varphi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
,

such that for all primes
p\notin S
we have

\rho(p)=\varphi(\operatorname{Frob}_{p}),

where
\operatorname{Frob}_{p}\in\Gamma
is the Frobenius element of
p
. We define the
mean
of
\rho
to be

m(\rho)=\frac{1}{\lvert\Gamma\rvert}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\varphi(\gamma).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
be frobenian functions. Then
\rho_{1}\cdot\rho_{2}
is also frobenian.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\rho
be a frobenian function with
m(\rho)\neq 0
. The following holds as
x\to\infty
:

(1)

\sum_{p\leq x}\rho(p)=m(\rho)\cdot\operatorname{Li}(x)+O\left(x\exp{(-c\sqrt{\ln{x}})}\right),\text{ for some constant }c>0.

(2)

\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\rho(p)}{p}=m(\rho)\ln{\ln{x}}+C_{\rho}+O\left(\frac{1}{\ln{x}}\right),\text{ for some constant }C_{\rho}.

(3)

\sum_{p\leq x}\rho(p)\ln{p}=m(\rho)\cdot x+O\left(x\exp{(-c\sqrt{\ln{x}})}\right),\text{ for some constant }c>0.

(4)

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\lvert\rho(p)\rvert<p\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{\rho(p)}{p}\right)\sim C^{\prime}_{\rho}(\ln{x})^{m(\rho)},

for some constant
C^{\prime}_{\rho}\neq 0
, where
C^{\prime}_{\rho}
is real and positive when
\rho
is real-valued.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\rho
be a frobenian function.

(1)

Only finitely many primitive Dirichlet characters
\chi
satisfy
m(\rho\chi)\neq 0
.

Assume that
\rho
is real valued and non-negative and let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character.

(2)

We have
\lvert m(\rho\chi)\rvert\leq m(\rho)
.

(3)

The following are equivalent:

(a)

\lvert m(\rho\chi)\rvert=m(\rho)
,

(b)

m(\rho\chi)=m(\rho)
,

(c)

(\rho\chi)(p)=\rho(p)
for all but finitely many primes
p
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\varepsilon\in\langle 0,1\rangle
and let
\rho:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be a multiplicative function. We say that
\rho
is an
\varepsilon
-weak frobenian multiplicative function
if

(1)

The restriction of
\rho
to the set of primes is a frobenian function, in the sense of Definition
4.1
,

(2)

\lvert\rho(n)\rvert=O\left(n^{\varepsilon}\right)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(3)

There exists a constant
H\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\lvert\rho(p^{k})\rvert\leq H^{k}
for all primes
p
and all
k\geq 1
.

We define the
mean
of
\rho
to be the mean of the corresponding frobenian function.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be a multiplicative function. We say that
\rho
is a
frobenian multiplicative function
if it is
\varepsilon
-weak frobenian for all
\varepsilon\in\langle 0,1\rangle
.

Remark 4.7
.

Note that if
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are (
\varepsilon
-weak) frobenian multiplicative functions, then by Lemma
4.2
and Definition
4.5

\rho_{1}\rho_{2}
is also (
\varepsilon
-weak) frobenian multiplicative functions. In particular
\rho\chi
is a frobenian multiplicative function for a Dirichlet character
\chi
and a frobenian multiplicative function
\rho
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\varepsilon\in\langle 0,1\rangle
and
\rho
be an
\varepsilon
-weak frobenian multiplicative function. Then,

\sum_{n\leq x}\rho(n)=c_{\rho}x(\ln{x})^{m(\rho)-1}+O\left(x(\ln{x})^{m(\rho)-2}\right),

where

c_{\rho}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(m(\rho))}\prod_{p}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\rho(p^{k})}{p^{k}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{m(\rho)}.

If
\rho
is real-valued and non-negative with
m(\rho)\neq 0
, then
c_{\rho}
is real and positive.

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x_{(r,i,j_{1},\dotsc,j_{k})}}(\rho\chi_{i}\psi_{r_{j_{1}}}\dotsm\psi_{r_{j_{k}}})(n)=

For a positive real number

as

1_{\{q\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ n)\}}=\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\sum_{\chi\in X(n)}\chi.

Thus we have that

(5.2)

N(x)=\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\sum_{\chi\in X(n)}\sum_{q\leq x}a(q)\chi(q).

For a Dirichlet character
\chi\in X(n)
, we consider now the frobenian multiplicative function
q\mapsto a(q)\chi(q)
. We have the following two cases:

1^{\circ}

a(p)\chi(p)=a(p)
holds for all primes
p
coprime to
n
.

By multiplicativity we can conclude that
a(q)\chi(q)=a(q)
for all positive integers
q
. Hence

\sum_{q\leq x}a(q)\chi(q)=\sum_{q\leq x}a(q)=c\frac{x}{(\ln{x})^{m}}+O\left(\frac{x}{(\ln{x})^{m+1}}\right)

where the constant
c>0
is as above.

2^{\circ}

a(p)\chi(p)\neq a(p)
for some prime
p
coprime to
n
.

Let
L=K(\zeta_{n})
where
K
is the splitting filed of the polynomial
f(x)
and
\zeta_{n}
a primitive root of unity modulo
n
. Observe that both
a(p)
and
\chi(p)
are determined by the Frobenius value at
p
in
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})
. By the Chebotarev density theorem, there are infinitely many primes
l
coprime to
n
such that
\Frob_{l}\in\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})
is equal to
\Frob_{p}
up to conjugacy. For such primes
l
we also have that
a(l)\chi(l)\neq a(l)
. Thus there are infinitely many primes
l
such that
a(l)\chi(l)\neq a(l)
. By Lemma
4.4
this forces
\lvert m(a\chi)\rvert<m(a)
and thus by Lemma
4.8
we have

\sum_{q\leq x}a(q)\chi(q)=o\left(\frac{x}{(\ln{x})^{m}}\right).

By plugging all this back in (
5.2
) we get

N(x)=rc\frac{x}{(\ln{x})^{m}}+O\left(\frac{x}{(\ln{x})^{\beta}}\right)

for some constant
\beta>m
, where
r
is the number of Dirichlet characters
\chi\in X(n)
such that
a(p)\chi(p)=a(p)
for all primes
p
coprime to
n
. Since the trivial character is one such character, we have that
r\geq 1
. Thus, we have that
rc>0
is the required lower bound for
c_{f}
.

∎

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{K}(s)}{\zeta_{K}(2s)}=

In order to express
F(s)
via Dedekind zeta functions of certain number fields we will need the following Lemma about Dirichlet series and twists with Dirichlet characters.

Lemma 6.1
.

For Dirichlet series
F(s)
and
G(s)
and Dirichlet characters
\chi
and
\psi
the following holds :

i)

(F+G)^{\chi}=F^{\chi}+G^{\chi}
.

ii)

\left(F^{\chi}\right)^{\psi}=F^{\chi\cdot\psi}
.

iii)

(F\cdot G)^{\chi}=F^{\chi}\cdot G^{\chi}
.

iv)

\left(\frac{1}{F}\right)^{\chi}=\frac{1}{F^{\chi}}
for
F\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
F(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{s}}
and
G(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{n^{s}}
. We have the following :

i)

(F+G)^{\chi}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{n}+b_{n})\chi(n)}{n^{s}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}\chi(n)}{n^{s}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}\chi(n)}{n^{s}}=F^{\chi}(s)+G^{\chi}(s).

ii)

\left(F^{\chi}\right)^{\psi}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}\chi(n)\psi(n)}{n^{s}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}(\chi\cdot\psi)(n)}{n^{s}}=F^{\chi\cdot\psi}(s).

iii)

Let
(F\cdot G)(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}}{n^{s}}
where
c_{n}=\sum_{k\mid n}a_{k}b_{\frac{n}{k}}
. Then
(F\cdot G)^{\chi}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}\chi(n)}{n^{s}}
and
(F^{\chi}\cdot G^{\chi})(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{n}}{n^{s}}
where
d_{n}=\sum_{k\mid n}a_{k}\chi(k)\cdot b_{\frac{n}{k}}\chi\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)=c_{n}\chi(n)
. Hence,
(F\cdot G)^{\chi}=F^{\chi}\cdot G^{\chi}
.

iv)

Let
\left(\frac{1}{F}\right)(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a^{\prime}_{n}}{n^{s}}
where
a^{\prime}_{i}
’s satisfy the following conditions:
a^{\prime}_{1}a_{1}=1
and
\sum_{k\mid n}a^{\prime}_{k}a_{\frac{n}{k}}=0
for
n\geq 2
. Now we have that
\left(\frac{1}{F}\right)^{\chi}(s)\cdot F\chi(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}}{n^{s}}
where
c_{1}=a^{\prime}_{1}\chi(1)\cdot a_{1}\chi(1)=1
and
c_{n}=\sum_{k\mid n}a^{\prime}_{k}\chi(k)\cdot a_{\frac{n}{k}}\chi\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)=\chi(n)\sum_{k\mid n}a^{\prime}_{k}a_{\frac{n}{k}}=0
for
n\geq 2
. Hence,
\left(\frac{1}{F}\right)^{\chi}=\frac{1}{F^{\chi}}
.

∎

Finally, by combining Theorem
3.3
and Proposition
1.6
using the above Lemma we are able to express the generating Dirichlet series
F(s)
in terms of Dedekind zeta functions of certain number fields and its twists by certain Dirichlet characters.

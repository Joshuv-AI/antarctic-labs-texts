On a dynamical system linked to the BKL scenario
Robert Conte

Abstract
We consider the six-dimensional dynamical system in three components introduced by Ryan to describe the scenario of Belinskii, Khalatnikov and Lifshitz to the cosmological singularity when the spatial metric tensor is not diagonal. Despite its nonintegrability, recently proven by Goldstein and Piechocki, the three four-dimensional systems defined by canceling one of the three components happen to be integrable. We express their general solution as a rational function of, respectively, two exponential functions, a third Painlevé function, two exponential functions.



Keywords:

third Painlevé equation,
truncation method,
cosmological singularity,
Bianchi IX.

The dynamical system

The approach to the cosmological singularity
in the scenario of Belinskii, Khalatnikov and Lifshitz
[
3

1

is best described in the synchronous frame by the metric of Bianchi type IX,

\displaystyle\hbox{d}s^{2}=\hbox{d}t^{2}-\gamma_{ab}(t)e_{\alpha}^{a}e_{\beta}^{b}\hbox{d}x^{\alpha}\hbox{d}x^{\beta},

(1)

the spatial coordinates,

the three frame vectors,

independent of the spatial coordinates.

Indeed, the dynamics then reduces to a six-dimensional dynamical system
only involving the six components of

(a real, symmetric, third order matrix).
However, this system is quite complicated
[
12

when written in the three eigenvalues
\Gamma_{a}
of
\gamma

which diagonalizes

At this stage, Ryan

\displaystyle\frac{\hbox{d}^{2}\log a}{\hbox{d}t^{2}}=-a^{2}+\frac{b}{a}\raise 2.0pt\hbox{,}\ \frac{\hbox{d}^{2}\log b}{\hbox{d}t^{2}}=+a^{2}-\frac{b}{a}+\frac{c}{b}\raise 2.0pt\hbox{,}\ \frac{\hbox{d}^{2}\log c}{\hbox{d}t^{2}}=+a^{2}-\frac{c}{b}\cdot

We rewrite (

\displaystyle{\hskip-42.67912pt}\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\frac{\hbox{d}^{2}\log(a^{2})}{\hbox{d}t^{2}}=b/a-2a^{2},\frac{\hbox{d}^{2}\log(b/a)}{\hbox{d}t^{2}}=2a^{2}-2b/a+c/b,\frac{\hbox{d}^{2}\log(c/b)}{\hbox{d}t^{2}}=b/a-2c/b.}\end{array}

This system admits a first integral

\displaystyle K=\frac{\hbox{d}\log b}{\hbox{d}t}\frac{\hbox{d}\log c}{\hbox{d}t}+\frac{\hbox{d}\log c}{\hbox{d}t}\frac{\hbox{d}\log a}{\hbox{d}t}+\frac{\hbox{d}\log a}{\hbox{d}t}\frac{\hbox{d}\log b}{\hbox{d}t}-a^{2}-\frac{b}{a}-\frac{c}{b}

\displaystyle\phantom{K=}-a^{2}-\frac{b}{a}-\frac{c}{b}.

(6)

Although this first integral (a component of the Ricci tensor) must be set to zero in cosmology,
we keep it arbitrary here.

The invariance of (

under the scaling transformation

\displaystyle(t,a,b,c,a^{2},b/a,c/b)\to(\lambda^{-1}t,\lambda a,\lambda^{3}b,\lambda^{5}c,\lambda^{2}a^{2},\lambda^{2}b/a,\lambda^{2}c/b),

\displaystyle a=\pm\frac{3}{t},b=\pm\frac{30}{t^{3}},c=\pm\frac{120}{t^{5}},\frac{a^{2}}{9}=\frac{b/a}{10}=\frac{c/b}{4}=\frac{1}{t^{2}},

(8)

easily extrapolated to one more parameter when one allows

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{9}=\frac{b/a}{10}=\frac{c/b}{4}=\frac{k^{2}}{\sinh^{2}(kt)},k^{2}=\frac{K}{23}\cdot

(9)

When computing the Fuchs indices of the linearized system near the above
one-parameter solution,
the same authors found the six values defined by

\displaystyle(j+1)(j-2)\left[(j-1/2)^{4}+95/2(j-1/2)^{2}+5569/16\right]=0,

(10)

The values

and the four others are not real.
This proves that the solution (

and this is
a strong indication of the chaotic nature of the approach to the
cosmological singularity.

Our interest here is different,
and mainly motivated by mathematical similarities between the approximate system (
3
)
and another six-dimensional Bianchi IX system,
defined by the assumption of a diagonal spatial metric tensor
\gamma(t)

In this so-called mixmaster model

[
9
]

[
3

(
(A,B,C)
denote the components of the diagonal tensor
\gamma

(\log A)^{\prime\prime}=A^{2}-(B-C)^{2},(\log B)^{\prime\prime}=B^{2}-(C-A)^{2},(\log C)^{\prime\prime}=C^{2}-(A-B)^{2},

(11)

all singlevalued particular solutions but one

10

have been obtained explicitly.
Since this unknown missing solution
should depend on four arbitrary constants
and be an extrapolation of a three-parameter elliptic solution,
we think it useful to examine the nature of the solutions of
the three four-dimensional systems defined
by canceling one of the three components in (

Since such a cancellation is unphysical
(because the volume
\det\gamma
has been assumed nonzero when rescaling the coefficient of

the obtained solutions will have no physical significance,
but their mathematical structure might be of interest.

The results are the following.
First, the general solutions of these three four-dimensional systems
are indeed singlevalued.
Second, two of them are mathematically identical to two of
the four-parameter explicit solutions of the mixmaster.
However, as explained later,
the third one is of no help to tackle the missing mixmaster solution
because their analytic nature is different.

3
Conclusion

About the search for the missing four-parameter solution
[
10
]
of the mixmaster model,
since none of the above three cases of (
1
)
includes an elliptic solution,
the present study unfortunately does not provide a hint to this missing solution.

On the other hand,
since the two six-dimensional Bianchi IX dynamical systems
(

share two particular cases with the same kinds of particular singlevalued solutions,
we conjecture that the mixmaster system (

admits a third particular case whose solution would be similar to that of the case
a^{2}=0

of (

Remark
.
One might wonder whether there exists some connection between the four-parameter solutions here found
and the general solution of interest to general relativity.
The answer is negative.
Indeed, let us consider the sixth order ODE
E_{6}(a^{2})=0
for
a^{2}(t)

resulting from the elimination of the two other components,
and, for instance, the fourth order ODE

Infinitesimal Operators and the Distribution of Anticommutators and Commutators
James A. Mingo, Pei-Lun Tseng

Abstract
. In an infinitesimal probability space we consider operators which are infinitesimally free and one of which is infinitesimal , in that all its moments vanish. Many previously analysed random matrix models are captured by this framework. We show that there is a simple way of finding non-commutative distributions involving infinitesimal operators and apply this to the commutator and anticommutator. We show the joint infinitesimal distribution of an operator and an infinitesimal idempotent gives us the Boolean cumulants of the given operator. We also show that Boolean cumulants can be expressed as infinitesimal moments thus giving matrix models which exhibit asymptotic Boolean independence and monotone independence.

1.
Introduction

The largest eigenvalues of a random covariance matrix has been much studied in recent years, motivated in many cases by problems in principle component analysis. An important question in principal component analysis is to decide how many eigenvalues can be considered significant. To analyse this problem a spike or signal-plus-noise model is often used where we have a source of noise represented by a random matrix
X_{1}
and a signal represented by a non-random matrix
X_{2}
. Moreover it is usually assumed that the rank of the signal is much much smaller than the rank of the noise, which increases with the sample size. Even though the rank of
X_{2}
is small, it still affects the largest eigenvalue of
X_{1}+X_{2}
. In this paper we idealize this by saying that the signal,
X_{2}
, is
infinitesimal
.

The tools of free probability give explicit rules (see
[
41
,
32
,
34
]
) for computing the distributions of the sum or product of freely independent random variables. The method of linearization extends these rules to the case of polynomials (see
[
9
]
and
[
32
, Ch. 10]
) or rational functions (see
[
29
]
) of freely independent random variables, but explicit solutions are often infeasible.

An example where explicit calculations are feasible is the case of a commutator:
i(x_{1}x_{2}-x_{2}x_{1})
and an anti-commutator:
x_{1}x_{2}+x_{2}x_{1}
. In
[
34
, Lect. 19]
the distribution of the commutator of freely independent operators is given; the case of the anticommutator is more involved (see
[
34
, Rem. 15.21]
); however a graph theoretic description was given by Perales in
[
35
]
. A related case is the recent work of Campbell
[
12
]
on the finite free distribution of a commutator an anticommutator. Also relevant are the papers of Ejsmont, Lehner
[
18
,
19
]
, and Hęćka
[
17
]
on the tangent law.

In
[
13
]
Collins, Hasebe, and Sakuma investigated the spectrum of the commutator
i(X_{1}X_{2}-X_{2}X_{1})
and the anticommutator
X_{1}X_{2}+X_{2}X_{1}
when the rank
X_{1}
tended to infinity and the rank of
X_{2}
was fixed. The regime of
[
13
]
can be modelled by the infinitesimal free independence of Belinschi and Shlyakhtenko
[
9
]
and its application to study asymptotic independence given by Shlyakhtenko
[
38
]
. Expanding on this Cébron and Dahlqvist, and Gabriel
[
15
]
, used infinitesimal freeness to give a rigorous prove the BBP phase transition phenomena of outliers found by Baik, Ben Arous, and Péché
[
6
]
. Finally in
[
40
]
it was shown that infinitesimal free independence was just a special case of freeness with amalgamation over a commutative algebra. This was the inspiration for our work. As we shall see in this paper, many of the algebraic and combinatorial tools of
[
34
]
transfer directly to the infinitesimal case. Moreover this transfer also extends to the Boolean independence of Speicher and Wourodi
[
39
]
, as was shown by Perales and Tseng in
[
36
]
. An important point to notice here is that our methods give the full distribution of the commutator and anticommutator, not just the support of the distribution as was done in
[
13
]
.

From the point of view of free probability, the key property of
X_{2}
used in
[
13
]
was that its limit distribution was
\delta_{0}
, the Dirac mass at
0
. An operator with this property we call
infinitesimal
.

There has been much recent work connecting free and Boolean cumulants (see for example the papers of Février, Mitja, Nica, and Szpojankowski
[
23
]
and
[
22
]
). We show that if one further assumes that the infinitesimal operator is an idempotent. Then there are new and simple formulas relating infinitesimal moments to Boolean cumulants (see Prop.
5.3
). This enables us to produce matrix models exhibiting asymptotic Boolean independence and asymptotic monotone independence, which until now has been difficult to do, see
[
27
]
and
[
30
]
. Also in Remark
5.4
we show how infinitesimal operators give an abstraction for the Markov-Krein transform of Kerov
[
25
]
.

In §
2
we collect the basic notions from
[
34
]
,
[
24
]
, and
[
32
]
that we shall need for our results. In §
3
we consider the distribution of the commutator and the anticommutator when
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are infinitesimally free and
x_{2}
is infinitesimal. In §
4
we consider infinitesimal
R
-diagonal operators. In §
5
we show how to write a Boolean cumulant as an infinitesimal moment. In §
6
we revisit the problem in §
3
with infinitesimal free independence replaced by Boolean independence.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Free Independence

A
non-commutative probability space

(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
consists of a unital algebra over
\mathbb{C}
and a linear functional
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
with
\varphi(1)=1
. We let
\mathbb{C}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{k}\rangle
be the algebra on polynomials in the non-commuting variables
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
.

For
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
be elements in
\mathcal{A}
, the mapping
\mathbb{C}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{k}\rangle\ni p\mapsto\varphi(p(a_{1},\dots,a_{k}))\in\mathbb{C}
is the
joint distribution
of
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}
. We denote this linear functional by
\mu_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}}
.

For a given non-commutative probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
, unital subalgebras
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
of
\mathcal{A}
are
freely independent
(or
free
for short) if for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
are such that
a_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{j}}
where
i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in I
,
i_{1}\neq i_{2}\neq\cdots\neq i_{n}
and
\varphi(a_{j})=0
for all
j
, then

\varphi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=0.

A set
\{a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}\mid i\in I\}
is free if the unital subalgebras
\mathrm{alg}(1,a_{i})
generated by
a_{i}\ (i\in I)
form a free family.

For
n\geq 1
, a partition of
[n]
is a set
\pi=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{r}\}
of pairwise disjoint non-empty subsets of
[n]:=\{1,2,\dots,n-1,n\}
such that

V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{r}=[n]
. The set of all partitions of
[n]
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}(n)
, and
NC(n)
denotes all non-crossing partitions of
[n]
in the sense that we cannot find distinct blocks
V_{r}
and
V_{s}
with
a,c\in V_{r}
and
b,d\in V_{s}
such that
a<b<c<d
.

Notation 2.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
be a non-commutative probability space and
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Let
m_{n}(a)=\varphi(a^{n})
be the
moments
of
a
.
Suppose
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
are elements in
\mathcal{A}
and
V=\{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{s}\}
is a block of some partition of
[n]
, we set
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})|_{V}=(a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{s}}).
Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a sequence of multilinear functionals
f_{n}:\mathcal{A}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
, then for each partition
\pi
, we set

f_{\pi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=\prod_{V\in\pi}f_{|V|}((a_{1},\dots,a_{n})|_{V}).

The
free cumulants

\{\kappa_{n}:\mathcal{A}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{n\geq 1}
are defined inductively in terms of moments via

\varphi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\kappa_{\pi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}).

Note that the free cumulants can also be defined via the following formula

\kappa_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\mu(\pi,1_{n})\varphi_{\pi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}),

where
\mu
is the Möbius function of
NC(n)
(see
[
34
, Lect. 11]
).

Free cumulants are crucial to characterizing freeness. More precisely, unital subalgebras
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
are freely independent if and only if for all
n\geq 2
, and for all
a_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{j}}
for all
j=1,\dots,n
with
i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in I
we have
\kappa_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=0
whenever there exist
1\leq l,k\leq n
with
i_{l}\neq i_{k}
(see
[
34
, Thm. 11.16]
). This characterization of freeness is called the
vanishing of mixed cumulants
.

For a given
a\in\mathcal{A}
, the
Cauchy transform
of
a
is defined by

G_{a}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{m_{n}(a)}{z^{n+1}},

and the
R
-transform
of
a
is defined as

R_{a}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\kappa_{n+1}(a)z^{n}\text{ where }\kappa_{n}(a)=\kappa_{n}(a,\dots,a).

Note that following the vanishing of mixed cumulants property, we have if
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
are free, then

R_{a+b}=R_{a}+R_{b}
. We shall always work with formal power series, however if our distributions arise from Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}
with compact support, then all of our series converge to an analytic function on an appropriate domain.

2.2.
\bm{R}
-diagonal Operators

A non-commutative probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
is called a
*
-probability space
if
\mathcal{A}
is a
*
-algebra and
\varphi
is positive in the sense that
\varphi(a^{*}a)\geq 0
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
.
For
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}
and
a\in\mathcal{A}
we let
a^{(\varepsilon)}=a
if
\varepsilon=1
and
a^{(\varepsilon)}=a^{*}
if
\varepsilon=-1
. Recall from
[
34
, Lect. 15]
that
a\in\mathcal{A}
is
R
-
diagonal
if for all
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}\in\{-1,1\}
we have

\kappa_{n}(a^{(\varepsilon_{1})},\dots,a^{(\varepsilon_{n})})=0

unless
n
is even and
\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{i+1}=0
for
1\leq i<n
. In addition, we recall that
a\in\mathcal{A}
is
even
if
a=a^{*}
and
\varphi(a^{2n+1})=0
for all
n\geq 0
.

Note that the
R
-diagonal element is uniquely determined by its determining sequences (see
[
34
, Cor. 15.7]
). To be precise, if
a
is an
R
-diagonal element in a
*
-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
, then the
*
-distribution of
a
can be uniquely determined by
(\alpha_{n})_{n}
and
(\beta_{n})_{n}
where

\alpha_{n}=\kappa_{2n}(a,a^{*},a,a^{*},\dots,a,a^{*})\text{ and }\beta_{n}=\kappa_{2n}(a^{*},a,a^{*},a\cdots,a^{*},a).

Moreover, the distribution of
aa^{*}
and
a^{*}a
can be described by its determining sequences
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n}
and
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n}
in the following relation

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}(aa^{*},\dots,aa^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in NC(n)\\
\pi=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{r}\}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{|V_{1}|}\beta_{|V_{2}|}\cdots\beta_{|V_{r}|}

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}(a^{*}a,\dots,a^{*}a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in NC(n)\\
\pi=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{r}\}\end{subarray}}\beta_{|V_{1}|}\alpha_{|V_{2}|}\cdots\alpha_{|V_{r}|}.

where
V_{1}
is the block of
\pi
which contains the first element
1
.

In addition, if
a
and
b
are
*
-free elements in a
*
-probability space such that
a
is
R
-diagonal, then
ab
is also
R
-diagonal (see
[
34
, Prop. 15.8]
).

2.3.
Infinitesimal Free Independence

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
be a non-commutative probability space. Let
\varphi^{\prime}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a linear functional such that
\varphi^{\prime}(1)=0
. Then we call the triple
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})
an
infinitesimal probability space
.

For
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
, the pair of linear functionals
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
which both map
\mathbb{C}\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{k}\rangle
into
\mathbb{C}
are the
joint infinitesimal distribution
of
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}
if
\mu=\mu_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}}
and

\mu^{\prime}(p)=\varphi^{\prime}(p(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})).

Similarly, we denote the linear functional
\mu^{\prime}
by
\mu^{\prime}_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})
be an infinitesimal probability space, unital subalgebras
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
of
\mathcal{A}
are
infinitesimally freely independent
(or
infinitesimally free
for short) with respect to
(\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})
if whenever
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
are such that

a_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{k}},i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in I,i_{1}\neq\cdots\neq i_{n}
and
\varphi(a_{j})=0
for all
j
, then we have

\displaystyle\varphi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varphi^{\prime}(a_{k})\varphi(a_{1}\cdots a_{k-1}a_{k+1}\cdots a_{n}).

Notation 2.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})
be an infinitesimal probability space and
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Let
m_{n}^{\prime}(a)=\varphi^{\prime}(a^{n})
be the infinitesimal moments of
a
.
Let
\{f_{n}:\mathcal{A}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{f_{n}^{\prime}:\mathcal{A}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{n\geq 1}
be two sequences of multi-linear functionals. Given a partition
\pi\in NC(n)
, then for a fixed
V\in\pi
, we define
\partial f_{\pi,V}
be the map that is equal to
f_{\pi}
except for the block
V
, where we replace
f_{|V|}
by
f^{\prime}_{|V|}
. Then we define
\partial f_{\pi}
by

\partial f_{\pi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=\sum_{V\in\pi}\partial f_{\pi,V}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}).

Definition 2.5
.

Suppose
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})
is an infinitesimal probability space, the
infinitesimal free cumulants

\{\kappa_{n}^{\prime}:\mathcal{A}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}\}
are defined inductively in terms of moments and infinitesimal moments via

\varphi^{\prime}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\partial\kappa_{\pi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}).

Note that the infinitesimal free cumulants can also be described via

\kappa_{n}^{\prime}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\mu(\pi,1_{n})\partial\varphi_{\pi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})

(see
[
24
,
31
]
).
The infinitesimal free cumulants can be used to characterize infinitesimal freeness.

Theorem 2.6
.

[
24
, Thm. 1.2]

Suppose that
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})
is an infinitesimal probability space, and
\mathcal{A}_{i}
is a unital subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}
for each
i\in I
. Then
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
are infinitesimally free if and only if for each
s\geq 2
and
i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\in I
which are not all equal, and for
a_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{s}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{s}}
, we have
\kappa_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})=\kappa_{n}^{\prime}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})=0.

For a given
a\in\mathcal{A}
, we define the
infinitesimal Cauchy transform
of
a
by

g_{a}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{m_{n}^{\prime}(a)}{z^{n+1}},

and the
infinitesimal
r
-transform
of
a
is defined by

r_{a}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\kappa^{\prime}_{n+1}(a)z^{n}\text{ where }\kappa^{\prime}_{n}(a)=\kappa^{\prime}_{n}(a,\dots,a).

Following
[
31
]
, the infinitesimal Cauchy and
R
-transforms are related by the equations

(2.1)

r_{a}(z)=-g_{a}\left(G^{\langle-1\rangle}_{a}(z)\right)(G^{\langle-1\rangle}_{a})^{\prime}(z)\text{ and }g_{a}(z)=-r_{a}(G_{a}(z))G_{a}^{\prime}(z)

where
G^{\langle-1\rangle}_{a}
is the composition inverse of
G_{a}
.

2.4.
Applications to Random Matrices

In
[
13
, §4]
Collins, Hasebe and Sakuma proved asymptotic infinitesimal freeness for some random matrix ensembles, using the language of cyclic monotone independence. In particular they considered two ensembles of
N\times N
matrices
\{A_{1,N},\dots,A_{k,N}\}
and
\{B_{1,N},\dots,B_{l,N}\}
. Let
\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle N}=\mathrm{E}\circ\mathrm{tr}
and
\varphi^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}=\mathrm{E}\circ\mathrm{Tr}
where
\mathrm{Tr}
is the un-normalized trace and
\mathrm{tr}=N^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}
is the normalized trace. They assumed that there was a limit joint distribution for the
B_{i,N}
’s with respect to
\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle N}
and a limit joint distribution of the
A_{j,N}
’s with respect to
\varphi^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}
. More precisely that for all
i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\in[l]
the limit
\lim_{N}\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle N}(B_{i_{1},N}\cdots B_{i_{m},N})
exists, and for all
j_{1},\dots,j_{n}\in[k]
the limit
\lim_{N}\varphi^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}(A_{j_{1},N}\cdots A_{j_{n},N})
exists.

Then in
[
13
, Thm. 4.1]
, they showed that when
\{U_{N}\}_{N}
is a Haar unitary independent from
\{A_{1,N},\dots,\allowbreak A_{k,N},\allowbreak B_{1,N},\dots,B_{l,N}\}
then the ensembles
\{U_{N}B_{1,N}U_{N}^{*},\dots,\allowbreak U_{N}B_{l,N}U_{N}^{*}\}
and
\{A_{1,N},\dots,\allowbreak A_{k,N}\}
are asymptotically infinitesimally free with respect to
(\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})
. The special case when the
A_{i,N}
’s are stationary was already presented by Shlyakhtenko in
[
38
]
. In both these models the limit operators of
\{A_{1,N},\dots,A_{k,N}\}
are infinitesimal operators in the sense of Definition
3.1
, so the limit distributions of this model are covered by our theory. In addition, the results of
[
13
, Thm. 5.1]
are spectral results, i.e. about the support of the distribution, and do not give information about the distributions themselves.

2.5.
Boolean Independence

Here we first review some basic concepts of Boolean independence, introduced in
[
39
]
.

For a given non-commutative probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
, (non-unital) subalgebras
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
are
Boolean independent
if for all
n\geq 0
,
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
are such that
a_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{j}}
where
i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in I
,
i_{1}\neq\cdots\neq i_{n}
, then

(2.2)

\varphi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=\varphi(a_{1})\cdots\varphi(a_{n}).

The elements
\{a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}\mid i\in I\}
are Boolean independent if the subalgebras
\mathrm{alg}(a_{i})
generated by
a_{i}\ (i\in I)
are Boolean independent.

For
n\geq 1
, a partition
\pi
is an
interval partition
if each block of
\pi
is an interval of the form
\{i,i+1,\dots,i+s\}
for some
1\leq i\leq n
and
0\leq s\leq n-i
.
I(n)
denotes the set of all interval partitions. Then the
Boolean cumulants

\{\beta_{n}:\mathcal{A}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{n\geq 1}
are defined inductively in terms of moments via

(2.3)

\varphi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=\sum_{\pi\in I(n)}\beta_{\pi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}).

As in the free case, Boolean cumulants characterize Boolean independence. To be precise,
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
are Boolean independent if and only if for all
n\geq 2
and for all
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\cup_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
, we have
\beta_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=0
, whenever there are at least two elements from different subalgebras; i.e. there exists
1\leq k,l\leq n
with
i_{k}\neq i_{l}
and
a_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{k}}
and
a_{l}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{l}}
.

The relation between moments and Boolean cumulants can also be understood via the moment-generating function and
\eta
-transform. To be precise, for a given
a\in\mathcal{A}
, the
moment generating function

\psi_{a}
and the
\eta
-transform

\eta_{a}
are defined by

\psi_{a}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}m_{n}(a)z^{n}\text{ and }\eta_{a}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}(a)z^{n}

where
\beta_{n}(a)=\beta_{n}(a,\dots,a)
. Then we have

(2.4)

\eta_{a}(z)(1+\psi_{a}(z))=\psi_{a}(z).

2.6.
Infinitesimal Boolean Independence

The notion of infinitesimal Boolean independence was introduced in
[
36
]
. We recall the definition of it as follows.

Definition 2.7
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})
is an infinitesimal probability space. Non-unital subalgebras
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
are infinitesimally Boolean independent if for all
n\geq 0,a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
are such that
a_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{j}}
where
i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in I
,
i_{1}\neq\cdots\neq i_{n}
, then

\displaystyle\varphi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(a_{1})\cdots\varphi(a_{n});

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varphi(a_{1})\cdots\varphi(a_{j-1})\varphi^{\prime}(a_{j})\varphi(a_{j+1})\cdots\varphi(a_{n}).

Note that the infinitesimal Boolean independence can be understood as Boolean independence with amalgamation.
To be precise, for a given infinitesimal probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})
, we define

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}=\Big\{\begin{bmatrix}a&a^{\prime}\\
0&a\end{bmatrix}\Big|a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}\Big\},\ \widetilde{\mathbb{C}}=\Big\{\begin{bmatrix}c&c^{\prime}\\
0&c\end{bmatrix}\Big|c,c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}\Big\}

and then
\widetilde{\varphi}:\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\to\widetilde{\mathbb{C}}
is defined by

\widetilde{\varphi}\Big(\begin{bmatrix}a&a^{\prime}\\
0&a\end{bmatrix}\Big)=\begin{bmatrix}\varphi(a)&\varphi^{\prime}(a)+\varphi(a^{\prime})\\
0&\varphi(a)\end{bmatrix}.

Note that
(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\widetilde{\mathbb{C}},\widetilde{\varphi})
forms an operator-valued probability space (see
[
40
]
).
Moreover, in
[
36
]
, the authors showed the following result.

Theorem 2.8
.

Non-unital subalgebras
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
are infinitesimally Boolean independent with respect to
(\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})
if and only if
(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i})_{i\in I}

2.7.

[

]

Suppose that

be a (non-unital) subalgebra of

for all

,

, and

\displaystyle\varphi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=

3.
The Infinitesimal Distribution of Some Polynomials

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
be a non-commutative probability space. If
a\in\mathcal{A}
is such that
\varphi(a^{n})=0
for all
n\geq 1
, we say that
a
is an
infinitesimal operator
.

We observe that if
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are free elements and
a_{1}
is infinitesimal, then

\displaystyle\varphi(a_{1}a_{2}a_{1}a_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(a_{1}^{2})\varphi(a_{2})^{2}+\varphi(a_{2}^{2})\varphi(a_{1})^{2}-\varphi(a_{1})^{2}\varphi(a_{2})^{2}=0

since
\varphi(a_{1})=\varphi(a_{1})^{2}=0
. In fact, we can obtain general result as the following Lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are free elements in a ncps
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
that
a_{1}
is infinitesimal. Then
any polynomial in
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
is infinitesimal, provided each term contains at least one
a_{1}
. In particular,
a_{1}a_{2}+a_{2}a_{1}
and
i(a_{1}a_{2}-a_{2}a_{1})
will be infinitesimal.

Proof.

By linearity, it suffices to show that
any word
a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{n}}
is also infinitesimal where there is at least one
j
with
i_{j}=1
. Note that
\varphi((a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{n}})^{m})
may be expanded, by freeness, into a sum of products of moments of
a_{1}
and moments of
a_{2}
. Then every term in this sum will be
0
because
a_{1}
is infinitesimal.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

With this freeness assumption, infinitesimal elements form a weak kind of ideal in
\mathcal{A}
. In
[
13
]
, the infinitesimal elements were trace class operators, which form an ideal in the usual sense.

Going a little further, if we assume that
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are infinitesimally free and both infinitesimal then all mixed moments
\varphi^{\prime}(a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{n}})
vanish whenever
i_{1},\dots,i_{n}
are not all equal. We get this by writing
\varphi^{\prime}(a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{n}})
as a sum of cumulants
\partial\kappa_{\pi}(a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{n}})
with
\pi\in NC(n)
, and then observing that there can be only one
\kappa^{\prime}
in
\partial\kappa_{\pi}(a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{n}})
and all other
\kappa
’s vanish.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
a
be an infinitesimal operator, then we have
r_{a}(z)=z^{-2}g_{a}(z^{-1})
. Furthermore,
m_{n}^{\prime}(a)=\kappa_{n}^{\prime}(a)
for all
n\geq 1.

Proof.

We observe that
\varphi(a^{n})=0
implies that
G_{a}(z)=z^{-1}
and then
G_{a}^{\langle-1\rangle}(z)=z^{-1}.
Then we have

r_{a}(z)=-g_{a}\left(G^{\langle-1\rangle}_{a}(z)\right)(G^{\langle-1\rangle}_{a})^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}g_{a}(\frac{1}{z}),

which implies that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\kappa^{\prime}_{n}(a)z^{n-1}=\frac{1}{z^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{m_{n}^{\prime}(a)}{(z^{-1})^{n+1}}.

Thus, we obtain
\kappa_{n}^{\prime}(a)=m_{n}^{\prime}(a)
for all
n\geq 1
.
∎

Lemma 3.8

For

\mathop{\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1}}_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}=n}(-1)^{k_{2}+k_{4}+\cdots+k_{2[r/2]}}=(-1)^{[r/2]}\binom{l-1}{[\frac{r-1}{2}]}

To find the sum (

and condition on

is even.

\displaystyle\binom{n-u-1}{s-1}\binom{u-1}{s-1}
is the cardinality of

\{(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\mid k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1,k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r-1}=n-u,k_{2}+\cdots+k_{r}=u\}.

Observe that

(1-x)^{-s}(1+x)^{-s}=\Big[\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\tbinom{s+k-1}{s-1}x^{k}\Big]\cdot\Big[\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\tbinom{s+m-1}{s-1}(-x)^{m}\Big]

If we let

because
\displaystyle\binom{n-u-1}{(s+1)-1}\binom{u-1}{s-1}
is the cardinality of

\{(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\mid k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1,k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}=n-u,k_{2}+\cdots+k_{r-1}=u\}.

Observe that

(1-x)^{-s}(1+x)^{-(s+1)}=\Big[\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\tbinom{s+k-1}{s-1}x^{k}\Big]\cdot\Big[\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\tbinom{s+m}{s}(-x)^{l}\Big]

On the other hand,

Remark 3.10

In the anticommutator case with

Remark 3.12
.

Note that the infinitesimal law of anti-commutators and commutators can also be computed via the linearization trick. Recall that for a given a polynomial

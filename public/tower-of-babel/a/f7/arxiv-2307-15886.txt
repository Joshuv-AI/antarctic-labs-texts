The modified scattering of 2 dimensional semi-relativistic Hartree equations
Soonsik Kwon, Kiyeon Lee, Changhun Yang

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the asymptotic behaviors of small solutions to the semi-relativistic Hartree equations in two dimension. The nonlinear term is convolved with the Coulomb potential | x | − 1 |x|^{-1} , and it produces the long-range interaction in the sense of scattering phenomenon. From this observation, one anticipates that small solutions converge to a modified scattering states, although they decay as linear solutions. We show the global well-posedness and the modified scattering for small solutions in weighted Sobolev spaces. Our proof follows a road map of exploiting the space-time resonance by [ 12 , 30 ] . Compared to the result in three dimensional case [ 30 ] , weaker time decay in two dimension is one of the main obstacles.

1.2.
Main results and ideas

We now state our main theorem for the two dimensional semi-relativistic Hartree equations (
1.1
):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 1000
and
k=\frac{n}{100}
.
There exists
\overline{\varepsilon_{0}}>0
satisfying the following:

Suppose that the initial data
u_{0}
is sufficiently small in a weighted space.
In other words, for any
\varepsilon_{0}\leq\overline{\varepsilon_{0}}
,
u_{0}

satisfies

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{H^{n}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+\|\langle x\rangle^{2}u_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+\|\langle\xi\rangle^{k}\widehat{u_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

Then the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) with the initial data
u_{0}

has a unique global solution
u
to (
1.1
) decaying
as

(1.7)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}{\langle t\rangle}^{-1}.

Moreover, there exists a scattering profile
u_{\infty}
such that

(1.8)

\displaystyle\left\|{\langle\xi\rangle}^{k}\mathcal{F}\left[u(t)-e^{iB(t,D)}e^{-it{\langle D\rangle}}u_{\infty}\right]\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}{\langle t\rangle}^{-{\delta}},

for some
0<{\delta}<\frac{1}{100}
.
Here, the phase modification is defined by

(1.9)

\displaystyle B(t,\xi)=\frac{{\lambda}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left|\frac{\xi}{{\langle\xi\rangle}}-\frac{\sigma}{{\langle\sigma\rangle}}\right|^{-1}|\widehat{u}(\sigma)|^{2}d\sigma\right)\frac{\rho(s^{-\frac{2}{n}}\xi)}{{\langle s\rangle}}ds,

where
\rho
is a smooth compactly supported function.

Remark 1.2
.

We do not pursue to optimize the regularity
indices
n
and
k
and time decay
\delta>0
in Theorem
1.1
.

Remark 1.3
.

We prefer to express the formula of phase modification (
1.9
) in the fourier space because it can be seen not only from some heuristic consideration (see (
1.15
) below) but also in our rigorous proof.

Furthermore, we observe that convergence in the weighted

norm in (

L^{2}
spaces.

Remark 1.4
.

The time decay rate of solutions in (
1.7
) is optimal in the sense that
the nonlinear solutions decay as the same rate with the linear ones (
1.4
).

Theorem
1.1
contains the global existence and asymptotic
behaviors of small solutions to (
1.1
). Our proof to obtain the global existence of solutions is basically based on the bootstrap argument in a weighted Sobolev space, and then the next crucial part is to perform a suitable phase correction and find a modified scattering state.
Briefly, the proof of Theorem
1.1
consists of threefold.
First, we find the time decay of solutions to (
1.1
), from which we construct a function space which consists of the weighted energy norm and scattering norm. Then, the second step is to show that the small solutions stay small as long as they exist by performing the weighted energy estimates. Our strategy is based on the method of space-time resonance which was introduced in
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
30
]
.
The final step is to obtain the bound for the scattering norm in the function space. It is in this step that a suitable correction of the phase based on the Taylor expansion is required to close the bootstrap argument.
Collecting all from the three steps, we can finally obtain the modified scattering results for (
1.1
).

Let us explain in detail the ideas of proof in each step.
In the first step, we use the standard stationary phase method on oscillatory integral to derive the time decay of linear solutions,
t^{-1}
.
By a direct proof, without resorting to well-known
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimates (e.g
[
25
, Lemma 3]
), we manage to obtain the time decay of solutions up to higher (
k
th) order derivative, which is essential in the course of weighted energy estimates to overcome the lack of time decay compared to higher dimensional cases.
To fully utilize the time decay of solutions, we construct our solution space based on the weighted
L^{2}
-norms.

In the second step, we show that the small nonlinear solutions stay small during the existing time by performing the weighted energy estimates. We introduce the interaction representation of solutions
u(t)
so as to track the scattering states

(1.10)

\displaystyle f(t,x):=e^{it\langle D\rangle}u(t,x).

Then we can express
f
via Duhamel’s representation

(1.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \widehat{f}(t,\xi)&=\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)+i{\lambda}\mathcal{I}(t,\xi),\\
\mathcal{I}(t,\xi)&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{is\phi(\xi,\eta)}|\eta|^{-1}\widehat{|u|^{2}}(\eta)\widehat{f}(s,\xi-\eta)d\eta ds\end{aligned}

with the resonance function

(1.12)

\displaystyle\phi(\xi,\eta)=\langle\xi\rangle-\langle\xi-\eta\rangle.

In the course of weighted energy estimates, we should bound the
xf
and
x^{2}f
in
L^{2}
which are converted to
\nabla\widehat{f}
and
\nabla^{2}\widehat{f}
in the fourier space, respectively.
The main task is to not only bound the singularity
|\eta|^{-1}
, but also recover the time growth resulting from the derivative
\nabla_{\xi}
taken to exponential term.
Indeed, the most delicate term occurs from
\nabla^{2}\widehat{f}
when two derivatives both fall on
e^{is\phi(\xi,\eta)}

(1.13)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}s^{2}\big(\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi,\eta)\big)^{2}e^{is\phi(\xi,\eta)}|\eta|^{-1}\widehat{|u(s)|^{2}}(\eta)\widehat{f}(s,\xi-\eta)d\eta ds,

where we have to compensate the time growth
s^{2}
. Here, we encounter the main difficulty from two dimensional nature, i.e., the weaker time decay
|s|^{-1}
of solutions in contrast to three or higher dimensional problem,
because
L^{2}
-norm of cubic nonlinearity in (
1.13
) enjoys at most
s^{-2}
decay which is not sufficient to compensate the time growth for (
1.13
) being integrable in time.
Nevertheless, since the singularity
|\eta|^{-1}
near the origin is weaker compared to three or higher dimensional case where
\mathcal{F}(|x|^{-1})(\eta)=C_{d}|\eta|^{-d+1}
, we anticipate that this advantage leads us to obtaining an extra time decay. One of key observations, as already observed in
[
30

, is the null structure from the phase function

(1.14)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi,\eta)\Big|_{\eta=0}=\nabla_{\xi}\Big(\langle\xi\rangle-\langle\xi-\eta\rangle\Big)\Big|_{\eta=0}=0.

The null structure removes the singularity near the origin, more precisely, the multiplier in (
1.13
) behaves as

|\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi,\eta)|^{2}|\eta|^{-1}\sim|\eta|,\;\;\text{ if }\;|\eta|,|\xi|\lesssim 1.

Using this, we can heuristically regard
|\eta|\approx|s|^{-1}
in the analysis respect. Indeed, for
|\eta|\geq|s|^{-1}
, we can exploit the space resonance, in other words, we can apply an integration by parts to the following quadratic term

\displaystyle\widehat{|u(s)|^{2}}(\eta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{is(\langle\sigma+\eta\rangle-\langle\sigma\rangle)}\widehat{f}(s,\sigma)\overline{\widehat{f}(s,\eta+\sigma})d\sigma,

by using
|\nabla_{\sigma}(\langle\sigma+\eta\rangle-\langle\sigma\rangle)|\sim|\eta|
for
|\eta|,|\sigma|\lesssim 1
to derive an additional time decay at the cost of
|\eta|^{-1}
.
In the rigorous proof below, we then can control the
xf
and
x^{2}f
in
L^{2}
, allowing a small growth in
t
.

As mentioned above, since our main equation has the
long-range

nonlinearity, the nonlinearity occurs the logarithm divergence
in terms of time integration. To overcome this difficulty, we employ
a phase modification (
1.8
) from the singular potential
|x|^{-1}
and obtain an extra logarithm time decay by following the argument in
[
30

21
]
. We begin with writing the nonlinear term as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(t,\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{isp(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}|\eta|^{-1}\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\eta)\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\sigma)\overline{\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma)}d\eta d\sigma ds,

where

p(\xi,\eta,\sigma)=\langle\xi\rangle-\langle\xi+\eta\rangle-\langle\xi+\sigma\rangle+{\langle\xi+\eta+\sigma\rangle}.

Let us assume that
|\xi|\lesssim 1
. By Taylor expansion, the phase function is approximated by

\displaystyle p(\xi,\eta,\sigma)=\eta\cdot\left(\frac{\xi}{\langle\xi\rangle}-\frac{\xi+\sigma}{{\langle\xi+\sigma\rangle}}\right)+O\left(|\eta|^{2}\right).

Then, neglecting all contributions that decay faster than
|s|^{-1}
, we can approximate the above integration as

(1.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\iint_{|\eta|\lesssim|s|^{-1+}}e^{is\eta\cdot\left(\frac{\xi}{\langle\xi\rangle}-\frac{\xi+\sigma}{{\langle\xi+\sigma\rangle}}\right)}|\eta|^{-1}\widehat{f}(s,\xi)\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\sigma)\overline{\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\sigma)}d\eta d\sigma\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\widehat{f}(s,\xi)\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{is\eta\cdot\left(\frac{\xi}{\langle\xi\rangle}-\frac{\xi+\sigma}{{\langle\xi+\sigma\rangle}}\right)}|\eta|^{-1}d\eta\big|\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\sigma)\big|^{2}d\sigma+O(s^{-1-})\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\widehat{f}(s,\xi)\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\eta|^{-1})\left(s\big(\tfrac{\xi}{\langle\xi\rangle}-\tfrac{\sigma}{{\langle\sigma\rangle}}\big)\right)\big|\widehat{f}(s,\sigma)\big|^{2}d\sigma+O(s^{-1-}),\end{aligned}

under the suitable assumption on
f

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)=it^{-1}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left|\frac{\xi}{\langle\xi\rangle}-\frac{\sigma}{{\langle\sigma\rangle}}\right|^{-1}\big|\widehat{f}(t,\sigma)\big|^{2}d\sigma+O(t^{-1-})

which implies the modified scattering property (
1.8
) and (
1.9
). The rigorous analysis for error terms will be achieved by identifying suitable scale in
\eta
with respect to time, say
s^{-1+}
, and then, by exploiting the space resonance for
|\eta|\gtrsim s^{-1+}
.

P_{N_{1}\leq\cdot\leq N_{2}}:=\sum_{N_{1}\leq N\leq N_{2}}P_{N}
and

P_{\sim N_{0}}:=\sum_{N\sim N_{0}}P_{N}
. For
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}

we denote
\widetilde{\rho_{N}}=\rho_{N/2}+\rho_{N}+\rho_{2N}
. In
particular,
\widetilde{P_{N}}P_{N}=P_{N}\widetilde{P_{N}}=P_{N}

where
\widetilde{P_{N}}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\widetilde{\rho_{N}}\mathcal{F}
.
Especially, we denote
P_{N}f
by
f_{N}
for any measurable function

f
.

\bullet
Let
\textbf{v}=(v_{i}),\textbf{w}=(w_{i})\in\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Then
\textbf{v}\otimes\textbf{w}
denotes the usual tensor product
such that
(\textbf{v}\otimes\textbf{w})_{ij}=v_{i}w_{j}
for
i,j=1,2
. We also
denote a tensor product of
\textbf{v}\in\mathbf{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
\textbf{w}\in\mathbf{\mathbb{C}}^{m}

by a matrix
\textbf{v}\otimes\textbf{w}=(v_{i}w_{j})_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\cdots,n\\
i=1,\cdots,m\end{subarray}}
. For simplicity, we use the simplified notation

\mathbf{v}^{k}=\overbrace{\mathbf{v}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{v}}^{k\;\text{times}},\qquad\nabla^{k}=\overbrace{\nabla\otimes\cdots\otimes\nabla}^{k\;\text{times}}.

The product of
\mathbf{v}
and
f\in\mathbf{\mathbb{C}}
is given by
\mathbf{v}f=\mathbf{v}\otimes f
.

\bullet
For the distinction between a vector and a scalar,
we use the bold letter for a vector-valued function and the normal
letter for a scalar-valued function.

2.
Time decay estimates

In this section, we find sharp time decay estimates of solutions to linear equation. We define an a priori assumption incorporating time decay to get global solutions to (
1.1
).
Define

(2.1)

\displaystyle f(t,x):=e^{it\langle D\rangle}u(t,x),

where
u(t)
is a solution to (
1.1
). Let us set

(2.2)

\displaystyle n\geq 1000,\;\;k=\frac{n}{100},\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;\delta_{0}=\frac{1}{100}.

For
\varepsilon_{1}>0

to be chosen later, we assume a priori smallness of solutions: for
a large time
T>0,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\Sigma_{T}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1},

with

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|u\|_{\Sigma_{T}}&:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big[{\langle t\rangle}^{-{\delta}_{0}}\|u(t)\|_{E_{1}}+{\langle t\rangle}^{-2{\delta}_{0}}\|u(t)\|_{E_{2}}+\|u(t)\|_{S}\Big],\end{aligned}

where

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{E_{1}}

\displaystyle:=\|u(t)\|_{H^{n}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+\|xe^{it\langle D\rangle}u(t)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})},

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{E_{2}}

\displaystyle:=\|x^{2}e^{it\langle D\rangle}u(t)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})},

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{S_{\ }}

\displaystyle:=\left\|\langle\xi\rangle^{k}\widehat{u}(t)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

We compute the pointwise time decay of the semi-relativistic equation by assuming the a priori assumption (
2.3
). We refer to
[
30
, Proposition 3.1]
for three dimensional case where the sharp time decay
|t|^{-\frac{3}{2}}
was obtained. Here, we obtain sharp time decay estimates for two dimensional case by following the similar strategy in
[
30
, Proposition 3.1]
,
but we have to bound the linear solution with the differential order up to
k
.

Proposition 2.1

(Time decay)
.

Assume that
u
satisfies the a priori assumption (
2.3
) for
\varepsilon_{1}
and
T
with the index conditions (
2.2
). Then for small
\varepsilon_{1}
, There exists
C

satisfying that for
0\leq t\leq T
and
0\leq\ell\leq k

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{\ell,\infty}}\leq C\langle t\rangle^{-1}\varepsilon_{1},

where the index
k
is in the a priori assumption (
2.3
).

Proof.

We prove that

\displaystyle\|{\langle D\rangle}^{\ell}e^{-it\langle D\rangle}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1,

whenever
f
satisfies

(2.5)

\displaystyle\langle t\rangle^{-1}\|\langle\xi\rangle^{k}\widehat{f}\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}+\langle t\rangle^{-1-{\delta}_{0}}\Big[\|xf\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|f\|_{H^{n}}\Big]+{\langle t\rangle}^{-1-2{\delta}_{0}}\|x^{2}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq\varepsilon_{1}.

We begin with writing

{\langle D\rangle}^{\ell}e^{-it\langle D\rangle}f(t,x)=\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}I_{N}(t,x)

where

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\langle\xi\rangle}^{\ell}e^{it\phi(\xi)}\widehat{f}(\xi)\rho_{N}(\xi)d\xi,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\phi(\xi)

\displaystyle:=-\langle\xi\rangle+\xi\cdot\frac{x}{t}.

We decompose

\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}I_{N}(t,x)=\left(\sum_{N\leq\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}}+\sum_{N\geq\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2}{n}}}+\sum_{\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq N\leq\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2}{n}}}\right)I_{N}(t,x).

The low-frequency part can be estimated as

\sum_{N\leq\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}}I_{N}(t,x)\lesssim\sum_{N\leq\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\|\rho_{N}\|_{L^{1}}\left\|{\langle\xi\rangle}^{\ell}\widehat{f}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1}\|{\langle\xi\rangle}^{\ell}\widehat{f}\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}.

For the high-frequency, we exploit the high Sobolev norm bound as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{N\geq\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2}{n}}}I_{N}(t,x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N\geq\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2}{n}}}{\langle N\rangle}^{\ell+1}\|\rho_{N}\widehat{f}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{N\geq\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2}{n}}}N^{\ell+1-n}\|f\|_{H^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1-{\delta}_{0}}\|f\|_{H^{n}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}.

For the remaining mid-frequency part, we apply the non-stationary phase method. One verifies that when
|x|>t
, the phase
\phi
is non-stationary, i.e.

(2.7)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi)|\geq\left|\frac{|x|}{t}-\frac{|\xi|}{\langle\xi\rangle}\right|\geq 1-\frac{|\xi|}{\langle\xi\rangle}\gtrsim\langle\xi\rangle^{-2}.

On the other hand, when
|x|<t
, the phase
\phi
could be stationary around
\xi_{0}
:

\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi_{0})=0\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;\xi_{0}=-\frac{x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}.

We now set
N_{0}\sim|\xi_{0}|
.
First, we consider the non-stationary case
N\nsim N_{0}
. Then one can find that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\Big|\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi)\Big|\gtrsim\max\left(\frac{|\xi-\xi_{0}|}{{\langle N\rangle}^{3}},\frac{|\xi-\xi_{0}|}{{\langle N_{0}\rangle}^{3}}\right),\text{ for }|\xi|\sim N.

We perform an integration by parts twice to write
I_{N}
as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\langle\xi\rangle}^{\ell}e^{it\phi(\xi)}\widehat{f}(\xi)\rho_{N}(\xi)d\xi=I_{N}^{1}(t,x)+I_{N}^{2}(t,x)+I_{N}^{3}(t,x)

where

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{N}^{1}(t,x)&=-t^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\langle\xi\rangle}^{\ell}e^{it\phi(\xi)}\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{4}}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\phi\nabla_{\xi}^{2}\left(\widehat{f_{N}}(\xi)\right)d\xi,\\
I_{N}^{2}(t,x)&=-2t^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\langle\xi\rangle}^{\ell}e^{it\phi(\xi)}\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\left(\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{2}}\right)\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{2}}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\widehat{f_{N}}(\xi)d\xi\\
I_{N}^{3}(t,x)&=-t^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\langle\xi\rangle}^{\ell}e^{it\phi(\xi)}\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\left[\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\left(\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{2}}\right)\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{2}}\right]\widehat{f_{N}}(\xi)d\xi.\end{aligned}

By (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we obtain the following bounds (independent
of
N_{0}
): for
|\xi|\sim N

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\frac{1}{\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi)}\right|&\lesssim N^{-1}{\langle N\rangle}^{3},\\
\left|\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\left(\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{2}}\right)\right|&\lesssim N^{-2}{\langle N\rangle}^{5},\\
\left|\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\left[\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\left(\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{2}}\right)\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{2}}\right]\right|&\lesssim N^{-4}{\langle N\rangle}^{10}.\end{aligned}

Using (
2.10
) and the Sobolev embedding, we see that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Big|I_{N}^{1}(t,x)\Big|&\lesssim t^{-2}N^{-2}{\langle N\rangle}^{\ell+6}\left\|\nabla_{\xi}^{2}\left(\widehat{f_{N}}\;\right)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}\\
&\lesssim t^{-2}{\langle N\rangle}^{\ell+6}\Big(N^{-1}\|x^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}+N^{-2}\left\|\nabla\rho_{N}\right\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}}\|\langle x\rangle^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+N^{-2}\min\big(\langle N\rangle^{-n}N^{-1}\|f\|_{H^{n}},\langle N\rangle^{-k}\|\widehat{f}\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\big)\Big)\\
&\lesssim t^{-2}{\langle N\rangle}^{\ell+6}\left((N^{-1}+N^{-\frac{3}{2}})t^{1+2{\delta}_{0}}+N^{-2}\min\big({\langle N\rangle}^{-n+1}t^{1+\delta_{0}},t\big)\right),\end{aligned}

which implies that

\sum_{\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq N\leq\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2}{n}}}\Big|I_{N}^{1}(t,x)\Big|\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}.

I_{N}^{2}
can be estimated similarly. Indeed, one has

\displaystyle|I_{N}^{2}(t,x)|\lesssim t^{-2}N^{-3}{\langle N\rangle}^{\ell+8}\left\|\nabla_{\xi}\left(\rho_{N}\widehat{f}\;\right)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}

\displaystyle\;\lesssim t^{-2}{\langle N\rangle}^{\ell+8}\left(N^{-2}\left\|\nabla\rho_{N}\right\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}}\|\langle x\rangle^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}+N^{-2}\min\big(\langle N\rangle^{-n}N^{-1}\|f\|_{H^{n}},\langle N\rangle^{-k}\|\widehat{f}\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\big)\right),

which leads us that

\sum_{\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq N\leq\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2}{n}}}|I_{N}^{2}(t,x)|\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}.

Lastly, we estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq N\leq\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2}{n}}}|I_{N}^{3}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq N\leq\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2}{n}}}t^{-2}N^{-4}\langle N\rangle^{\ell+10}\|\rho_{N}\widehat{f}\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq N\leq\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2}{n}}}t^{-2}\langle N\rangle^{\ell+10}N^{-2}\min\big(\langle N\rangle^{-n}N^{-1}\|f\|_{H^{n}},\langle N\rangle^{-k}\|\widehat{f}\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon_{1}.

We remain to consider the stationary phase case
N\sim N_{0}
. We
further decompose dyadically the frequency space around
\xi_{0}
. Let
L_{0}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}

such that
\frac{L_{0}}{2}<t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq L_{0}
. We write

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\langle\xi\rangle}^{\ell}e^{it\phi(\xi)}\rho_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi\right|\leq\sum_{L=L_{0}}^{2^{10}N}|J_{L}|

where

\displaystyle J_{L}(t,x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\langle\xi\rangle}^{\ell}e^{it\phi(\xi)}\rho_{\leq L_{0}}(\xi-\xi_{0})\rho_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi\qquad\mbox{when }L=L_{0},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\langle\xi\rangle}^{\ell}e^{it\phi(\xi)}\rho_{L}(\xi-\xi_{0})\rho_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi\quad\qquad\mbox{when }L>L_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

We bound
J_{L_{0}}
the support of which contains the stationary point by just the measure

|J_{L_{0}}|\lesssim L_{0}^{2}{\langle N\rangle}^{l}\|\rho_{N}\widehat{f}\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\lesssim t^{-1}\|\langle\xi\rangle^{k}\widehat{f}\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}.

When
L>L_{0}
, we return to the non-stationary phase cases. By
integrating by parts twice, we decompose
J_{L}(t,x)
into

J_{L}(t,x)=J_{L}^{1}(t,x)+J_{L}^{2}(t,x)+J_{L}^{3}(t,x),

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J_{L}^{1}(t,x)&=-t^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\langle\xi\rangle}^{l}e^{it\phi(\xi)}\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{4}}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\phi\nabla_{\xi}^{2}\left(\widehat{f}(\xi)\rho_{N}(\xi)\rho_{L}(\xi-\xi_{0})\right)d\xi,\\
J_{L}^{2}(t,x)&=-2t^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\langle\xi\rangle}^{l}e^{it\phi(\xi)}\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\left(\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{2}}\right)\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{2}}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\left(\widehat{f}(\xi)\rho_{N}(\xi)\rho_{L}(\xi-\xi_{0})\right)d\xi,\\
J_{L}^{3}(t,x)&=-t^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\langle\xi\rangle}^{l}e^{it\phi(\xi)}\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\left[\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\left(\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{2}}\right)\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{2}}\right]\widehat{f}(\xi)\rho_{N}(\xi)\rho_{L}(\xi-\xi_{0})d\xi.\end{aligned}

We estimate
J_{L}^{i}
for
i=1,2,3
similarly to the above, but the following bounds are employed instead of (
2.10
): for
|\xi|\sim N
and
|\xi-\xi_{0}|\sim L
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi)\right|^{-1}&\lesssim L^{-1}{\langle N\rangle}^{3},\\
\left|\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\left(\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{2}}\right)\right|&\lesssim L^{-2}{\langle N\rangle}^{5},\\
\left|\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\left[\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\left(\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{2}}\right)\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi|^{2}}\right]\right|&\lesssim L^{-4}{\langle N\rangle}^{10}.\end{aligned}

The computation as in (
2.11
) gives the desired results.
We omit the details and complete the proof of (
2.4
)
∎

Since we have to handle the multipliers to exploit the time decay
(
2.4
) in our main proof, we introduce some useful
estimates in the rest of this section.

Lemma 2.2

(Coifman-Meyer operator estimates)
.

Assume that a multiplier
\textbf{m}(\xi,\eta)
satisfies that

(2.12)

\displaystyle C_{\mathbf{m}}:=\left\|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathbf{m}(\xi,\eta)e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{iy\cdot\eta}\,d\eta d\xi\right\|_{L_{x,y}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}<\infty.

Then, for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}
,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{m}(\xi,\eta)\widehat{u}(\xi\pm\eta)\widehat{v}(\eta)\,d\eta\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim C_{\mathbf{m}}\|u\|_{L^{p}}\|v\|_{L^{q}},

and for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=1
,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\left|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathbf{m}(\eta,\sigma)\widehat{u}(\eta\pm\sigma)\widehat{v}(\eta)\widehat{w}(\sigma)\,d\sigma d\eta\right|\lesssim C_{\mathbf{m}}\|u\|_{L^{p}}\|v\|_{L^{q}}\|w\|_{L^{r}}.

Weighted Energy estimate

In this section, we prove the energy estimate
which plays an important role in the bootstrap argument. In the following Proposition

, we bound the weighted norms
\|u\|_{E_{1}},\|u\|_{E_{2}}
in the a priori assumption (
2.3
).

Proposition 3.1

(Weighted energy estimate)
.

Assume
that
u\in C([0,T],H^{n})
satisfies the a priori assumption (
2.3
)
for some
\varepsilon_{1}>0
with initial data condition (
1.6
)
for
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. Suppose the index conditions (
2.2
). Then, we have the following estimates

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\langle t\rangle^{-{\delta}_{0}}\|u(t)\|_{E_{1}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}+C\varepsilon_{1}^{3},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\langle t\rangle^{-2{\delta}_{0}}\|u(t)\|_{E_{2}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}+C\varepsilon_{1}^{3},

with
\delta_{0}=\frac{1}{100}
.

3.1.
Useful inequalities

For the purpose of proving weighted energy estimates, we introduce some useful inequalities.

Lemma 3.2

(Lemma 3.2 in
[
4
]
)
.

For any
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions
u\in L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
we get

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left\||x|^{-1}*(|u|^{2})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Under the a priori assumption, we find the bounds for the frequency localized terms.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

u
satisfy the a priori assumption (
2.3
) for some
\varepsilon_{1}>0
. Suppose the index conditions (
2.2
). Then, for
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
and a dyadic number
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|P_{N}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim N^{\gamma}\langle N\rangle^{-k(1-\gamma)}\langle t\rangle^{-(1-\gamma)}\epsilon_{1}.

We also have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|P_{N}f(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min(N^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle t\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}\delta_{0}},\langle N\rangle^{-n})\epsilon_{1},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|P_{N}xf(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min(N^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle t\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}\delta_{0}},\langle N\rangle^{-2}\langle t\rangle^{\delta_{0}})\epsilon_{1}.

Proof.

By Young’s inequality, we have from (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\|P_{N}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\rho_{N}\langle\xi\rangle^{-k}\right)\right\|_{L^{1}}\|u(t)\|_{W^{k,\infty}}\lesssim\langle N\rangle^{-k}\langle t\rangle^{-1}\epsilon_{1}.

On the other hand, interpolating time decay estimates (
2.4
) and the mass conservation law (
1.2
), we get for
2\leq p\leq\infty
,

\|P_{N}u(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\|P_{N}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{p}}\lesssim\langle N\rangle^{-k(1-\frac{2}{p})}{\langle t\rangle}^{-\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)}\varepsilon_{1}^{2}.

Then, by Bernstein’s inequality, we obtain for
2\leq p<\infty

\displaystyle\|P_{N}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim N^{\frac{2}{p}}\|P_{N}u(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim N^{\frac{2}{p}}\langle N\rangle^{-k(1-\frac{2}{p})}\langle t\rangle^{-(1-\frac{2}{p})}\epsilon_{1}.

Next, by using Bernstein’s inequality and interpolating weighted norms, one can obtain

\displaystyle\|P_{N}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|xf\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle t\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}\delta_{0}}\epsilon_{1}^{2},

or, one has

\displaystyle\|P_{N}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim\langle N\rangle^{-n}\|u\|_{H^{n}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

Then, (
3.5
) follows by interpolating above two estimates.
The last inequality (
3.6
) can be obtained similarly.
∎

Next, we consider the quadratic terms.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

u
satisfy the a priori assumption (
2.3
) for some

\varepsilon_{1}>0
with the index conditions (
2.2
). For a dyadic number
N\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{Z}}}
, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left\|P_{N}\Big(|u(t)|^{2}\Big)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min(\langle N\rangle^{-k}\langle t\rangle^{-2},N^{2})\varepsilon_{1}^{2},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left\|P_{N}\Big(|u(t)|^{2}\Big)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N\langle N\rangle^{-\frac{k}{2}}\varepsilon_{1}^{2}.

Proof.

We refer to
[
3
, Lemma 2.4]
where the three dimensional case is proved.
∎

We observe that no time decay was obtained in (

). In the following lemma,
we find the time decay in (

) at the cost of derivative.
Especially, the space resonance is employed to obtain the almost second order time decay
|t|^{-2+}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

u
satisfy the a priori assumption (
2.3
) for some

\varepsilon_{1}>0
with the index conditions (
2.2
). Then, for a dyadic number
L\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{Z}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{N}|u(t)|^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1+\delta_{0}}\langle N\rangle^{-k}\epsilon^{2},

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|P_{N}|u(t)|^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-2+\frac{3}{2}\delta_{0}}N^{-1}\langle N\rangle^{-1}\epsilon^{2}.

Proof.

The Plancherel’s identity yields that

\displaystyle\|P_{N}|u(t)|^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=\|\rho_{N}\mathcal{F}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

We write

\displaystyle\rho_{N}(\eta)\mathcal{F}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)(\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\rho_{N}(\eta)\widehat{u}(\sigma)\overline{\widehat{u}(\sigma+\eta)}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\rho_{N}(\eta)}{\langle\sigma\rangle^{k}\langle\sigma+\eta\rangle^{k}}\widehat{\langle D\rangle^{k}u}(\sigma)\overline{\widehat{\langle D\rangle^{k}u}(\sigma+\eta)}d\sigma.

If we let

\mathbf{m}(\eta,\sigma):=\frac{\rho_{N}(\eta)}{\langle\sigma\rangle^{k}\langle\sigma+\eta\rangle^{k}},

one can verify that

\displaystyle C_{\mathbf{m}}\lesssim\langle N\rangle^{-k},

where the constant
C_{\mathbf{m}}
is defined in (
2.12
). Thus, by the Coifman-Meyer estimates (
2.13
), we have

\displaystyle\|\rho_{N}\mathcal{F}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim\langle N\rangle^{-k}\|u\|_{H^{k}}\|\langle D\rangle^{k}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\langle N\rangle^{-k}\langle t\rangle^{-1+\delta_{0}}\epsilon_{1}^{2}.

Next, we consider (
3.9
). we write

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)(\eta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it(\langle\sigma+\eta\rangle-\langle\sigma\rangle)}\widehat{f}(t,\sigma)\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\eta+\sigma})d\sigma,

where
f(t,x)=e^{it\langle D\rangle}u(t,x)
.
We perform an integration by parts to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\big(|u(t)|^{2}\big)(\eta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\Big(I_{1}(t,\eta)+I_{2}(t,\eta)+I_{3}(t,\eta)\Big),

Proof of Proposition

.

We will proceed as in

for

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left\|\langle\xi\rangle^{k}\Big(\mathsf{v}(t_{2},\xi)-\mathsf{v}(t_{1},\xi)\Big)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-{\delta}},

for some
0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{100}
.
We begin with writing
\mathsf{v}
as

where, after change of variables, the nonlinear term

where a resonant function

Let
L_{0}\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{Z}}}
such that

(4.4)

\displaystyle L_{0}\sim M^{-\frac{9}{10}}.

We write

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{I}(t,\xi)=\int_{0}^{t}\bigg(\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}(s,\xi)+\sum_{L\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{Z}}},L>L_{0}}\mathcal{K}_{L}(s,\xi)\bigg)ds,\end{aligned}

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}(s,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{isq{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}\rho_{\leq L_{0}}(\eta)|\eta|^{-1}\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\eta)\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\sigma)\overline{\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma)}d\eta d\sigma,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{L}(s,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{isq{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}\rho_{L}(\eta)|\eta|^{-1}\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\eta)\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\sigma)\overline{\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma)}d\eta d\sigma.

is remainder term. The profile
\mathsf{v}
verifies

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\left[e^{-iB(t,\xi)}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\right]

\displaystyle=i{\lambda}e^{-iB(t,\xi)}\left[\left(\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}(t,\xi)+\sum_{L>L_{0}}\mathcal{K}_{L}(t,\xi)\right)-\frac{1}{{\lambda}}\left[\partial_{t}B(t,\xi)\right]\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\right].

Thus,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathsf{v}(t_{2},\xi)-\mathsf{v}(t_{1},\xi)&=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\partial_{s}\mathsf{v}(s,\xi)ds\\
&=i{\lambda}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}e^{-iB(s,\xi)}\left[\left(\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}(s,\xi)+\sum_{L>L_{0}}\mathcal{K}_{L}(s,\xi)\right)-\frac{1}{{\lambda}}\left[\partial_{s}B(s,\xi)\right]\widehat{f}(s,\xi)\right]ds.\end{aligned}

In order to prove (
4.3
), we use the cancellation effect
between
\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}
and
\partial_{s}B(s,\xi)
, specifically, we show that for each
\xi
with

,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}e^{-iB(s,\xi)}\left(\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}(s,\xi)-\frac{1}{{\lambda}}\left[\partial_{s}B(s,\xi)\right]\widehat{f}(s,\xi)\right)ds\right|\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-{\delta}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-k}\end{aligned}

and

(4.8)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}e^{-iB(s,\xi)}\sum_{L>L_{0}}\mathcal{K}_{L}(s,\xi)ds\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-{\delta}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-k},

for some
0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{100}
.

Proof of (

).

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-{\delta}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-k},

where

.
We remark that the phase correction will be derived in the proof of (

).

We first consider (
4.9
). It suffices to show the integrand bound

\displaystyle\Big|\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}(s,\xi)\Big|\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-(1+{\delta})}{\langle N\rangle}^{-k}.

We further split

dyadically as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}(s,\xi)=\sum_{L_{1}\leq L_{0}+10}\mathcal{K}_{L_{0},L_{1}}(s,\xi),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{L_{0},L_{1}}(s,\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\!\!\!\!\int e^{isq{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}\rho_{L_{1}}(\eta)\rho_{\leq L_{0}}(\eta)|\eta|^{-1}\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\eta)\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\sigma)\overline{\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma)}d\eta d\sigma.

Next, consider (

.
It suffices to show that the integrand satisfies

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}(s,\xi)-\frac{1}{{\lambda}}\left[\partial_{s}B(s,\xi)\right]\widehat{f}(s,\xi)\right|

Step1: Phase approximation.

We approximate the phase function by a simpler one in the support of the integrand in (

\displaystyle q{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\Big(\langle\xi\rangle-{\langle\xi+\eta\rangle}\Big)-\Big({\langle\xi+\sigma\rangle}-{\langle\xi+\eta+\sigma\rangle}\Big)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{-|\eta|^{2}-2\eta\cdot\xi}{\langle\xi\rangle+{\langle\xi+\eta\rangle}}-\frac{-|\eta|^{2}-2\eta\cdot(\xi+\sigma)}{{\langle\xi+\sigma\rangle}+{\langle\xi+\eta+\sigma\rangle}}\right)

\displaystyle=\eta\cdot\left(\frac{\xi}{\langle\xi\rangle}-\frac{\xi+\sigma}{{\langle\xi+\sigma\rangle}}\right)+O\left(|\eta|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=:r(\xi,\eta,\sigma)+O\left(|\eta|^{2}\right).

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}^{\prime}(s,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{isr{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}\rho_{\leq L_{0}}(\eta)|\eta|^{-1}\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\eta)\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\sigma)\overline{\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma)}d\eta d\sigma.

Then we estimate

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}(s,\xi)-\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}^{\prime}(s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|s|\left|q(\xi,\eta,\sigma)-r(\xi,\eta,\sigma)\right||\eta|^{-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 62.59596pt\times\left|\rho_{\leq L_{0}}(\eta)\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\eta)\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\sigma)\overline{\widehat{f}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma)}\right|d\eta d\sigma

where we used (
4.4
) and

in the last inequality.

Step2: Profiles approximation.

We now approximate

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}\;}(s,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{isr{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}\rho_{\leq L_{0}}(\eta)|\eta|^{-1}\widehat{f}(\xi)\left|\widehat{f}(\xi+\sigma)\right|^{2}d\eta d\sigma.

By setting

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{f}(\zeta+\eta)-\widehat{f}(\zeta)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\widehat{\rho_{>R}f}(\zeta+\eta)-\widehat{\rho_{>R}f}(\zeta)\right|+\left|\widehat{\rho_{\leq R}f}(\zeta+\eta)-\widehat{\rho_{\leq R}f}(\zeta)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\widehat{\rho_{>R}f}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}+L_{0}\left\|\nabla_{\xi}\widehat{\rho_{\leq R}f}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-1}\left\|{\langle x\rangle}^{2}f\right\|_{L^{2}}+L_{0}\|\rho_{\leq R}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\left\|xf\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim L_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}M^{2{\delta}_{0}}.

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}^{\prime}(s,\xi)-\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}\;}(s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\rho_{\leq L_{0}}(\eta)|\eta|^{-1}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi+\eta)\widehat{f}(\xi+\sigma)\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi+\eta+\sigma)}-\widehat{f}(\xi)\left|\widehat{f}(\xi+\sigma)\right|^{2}\right|d\eta d\sigma

We conclude the proof of (

by the change of variables, we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{L_{0}}\;}(s,\xi)

Then, (
4.12

(4.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left|\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{is\eta\cdot\mathbf{z}}\rho_{\leq L_{0}}(\eta)|\eta|^{-1}\left|\widehat{f}(\sigma)\right|^{2}d\eta d\sigma-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}|s|}\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\mathbf{z}|^{-1}\left|\widehat{f}(\sigma)\right|^{2}d\sigma\right|\\

)
whenever the summation runs over those indexes in
\mathcal{A}
satisfying
L(L^{\prime})^{2}\leq M^{-(1+2{\delta})}
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{A}\cap\left\{L(L^{\prime})^{2}\leq M^{-(1+2{\delta})}\right\}}|\mathcal{K}_{{\textbf{L},\textbf{N}}}(s,\xi)|\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-(1+{\delta})}{\langle N\rangle}^{-k}.

We are left with a summation over

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\cap\left\{L(L^{\prime})^{2}\geq M^{-(1+2{\delta})}\right\}.

We perform an integration by parts in
\eta
twice, with a slight abuse of notation, to obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{{\textbf{L},\textbf{N}}}(s,\xi)|\leq|\mathcal{K}_{1}(s,\xi)|+|\mathcal{K}_{2}(s,\xi)|+|\mathcal{K}_{3}(s,\xi)|,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}(s,\xi):=\frac{1}{s^{2}}\int\!\!\!\!\int e^{isq{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}\textbf{m}_{1}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)\nabla_{\eta}^{2}\left(\widehat{f_{N_{1}}}(s,\xi+\eta)\overline{\widehat{f_{N_{3}}}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma)}\right)\widehat{f_{N_{2}}}(s,\xi+\sigma)d\eta d\sigma,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{2}(s,\xi):=\frac{1}{s^{2}}\int\!\!\!\!\int e^{isq{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}\textbf{m}_{2}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)\nabla_{\eta}\left(\widehat{f_{N_{1}}}(s,\xi+\eta)\overline{\widehat{f_{N_{3}}}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma)}\right)\widehat{f_{N_{2}}}(s,\xi+\sigma)d\eta d\sigma,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{3}(s,\xi):=\frac{1}{s^{2}}\int\!\!\!\!\int e^{isq{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}\textbf{m}_{3}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)\widehat{f_{N_{1}}}(s,\xi+\eta)\overline{\widehat{f_{N_{3}}}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma)}\widehat{f_{N_{2}}}(s,\xi+\sigma)d\eta d\sigma,

where

\displaystyle\textbf{m}_{1}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\left(\frac{\nabla_{\eta}q{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}{|\nabla_{\eta}q{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}|^{2}}\right)^{2}|\eta|^{-1}\rho_{L}(\eta)\rho_{L^{\prime}}(\sigma)\rho_{N_{1}}(\xi+\eta)\rho_{N_{3}}(\xi+\eta+\sigma),

\displaystyle\textbf{m}_{2}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\nabla_{\eta}q{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}{|\nabla_{\eta}q{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}|^{2}}\nabla_{\eta}\left(\frac{\nabla_{\eta}q{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}{|\nabla_{\eta}q{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}|^{2}}|\eta|^{-1}\rho_{L}(\eta)\rho_{N_{1}}(\xi+\eta)\rho_{N_{3}}(\xi+\eta+\sigma)\right)\rho_{L^{\prime}}(\sigma),

\displaystyle\textbf{m}_{3}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\nabla_{\eta}\textbf{m}_{2}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}.

A direct computation yields that

(4.21)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\nabla_{\eta}q(\xi,\eta,\sigma)\right|&=\left|\frac{\xi+\eta+\sigma}{{\langle\xi+\eta+\sigma\rangle}}-\frac{\xi+\eta}{{\langle\xi+\eta\rangle}}\right|\\
&\gtrsim\frac{|\sigma|}{\max(\langle\xi+\eta+\sigma\rangle,\langle\xi+\eta\rangle)\min(\langle\xi+\eta+\sigma\rangle,\langle\xi+\eta\rangle)^{2}}.\end{aligned}

With the help of (
4.21
), one can verify that the symbols satisfy the following bounds:

(4.22)

\displaystyle\left\|\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\textbf{m}_{1}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}e^{iy\cdot\eta}e^{iz\cdot\sigma}d\eta d\sigma\right\|_{L_{y,z}^{1}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{-1}(L^{\prime})^{-2}\langle\max(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle\langle\min(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{10},

(4.23)

\displaystyle\left\|\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\textbf{m}_{2}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)e^{iy\cdot\eta}e^{iz\cdot\sigma}\,d\sigma d\eta\right\|_{L_{y,z}^{1}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{-2}(L^{\prime})^{-2}\langle\max(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{2}\langle\min(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{12},

and

(4.24)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\textbf{m}_{3}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}\right|&\lesssim L^{-3}(L^{\prime})^{-2}\langle\max(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{2}\langle\min(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{4}.\end{aligned}

By applying the operator inequality (
2.14
) with (
4.22
), we estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{({\textbf{L},\textbf{N}})\in\mathcal{B}}\left|\mathcal{K}_{1}(s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{-2}\sum_{({\textbf{L},\textbf{N}})\in\mathcal{B}}L^{-1}(L^{\prime})^{-2}\langle\max(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle\langle\min(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{10}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left\|P_{N_{2}}u\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\left(\left\|x^{2}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|f_{N_{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|xf\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|x^{2}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|f_{N_{1}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-2+2\delta+2\delta_{0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M^{-(1+{\delta})}\leq N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\leq M^{\frac{2}{n}}\\
L_{0}\leq L\leq\max(N_{2},N_{3})\end{subarray}}\langle\max(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle\langle\min(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{10}\langle N_{2}\rangle^{-k}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-2+3\delta+2\delta_{0}}{\langle N\rangle}^{12}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-(1+{\delta})}{\langle N\rangle}^{-k}.

Similarly, (
4.23
) implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{({\textbf{L},\textbf{N}})\in\mathcal{B}}\left|\mathcal{K}_{2}(s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{-2}\sum_{({\textbf{L},\textbf{N}})\in\mathcal{B}}L^{-2}(L^{\prime})^{-2}\langle\max(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{2}\langle\min(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{12}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left\|P_{N_{2}}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|xf\right\|_{L^{2}}\left(\left\|f_{N_{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|f_{N_{1}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-2+\delta_{0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M^{-(1+{\delta})}\leq N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\leq M^{\frac{2}{n}}\\
L_{0}\leq L\leq\max(N_{2},N_{3})\end{subarray}}L^{-1}\langle\max(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{2}\langle\min(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{12}\langle N_{2}\rangle^{-k}

\displaystyle\hskip 256.0748pt\times\Big(N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle N_{1}\rangle^{-k}+N_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle N_{3}\rangle^{-k}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-\frac{11}{10}+\delta_{0}}\sum_{M^{-(1+{\delta})}\leq N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\leq M^{\frac{2}{n}}}\langle\max(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{2}\langle\min(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{12}\langle N_{2}\rangle^{-k}

\displaystyle\hskip 256.0748pt\times\Big(N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle N_{1}\rangle^{-k}+N_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle N_{3}\rangle^{-k}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-\frac{11}{10}+\delta_{0}}\langle N\rangle^{14}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-(1+{\delta})}{\langle N\rangle}^{-k}.

Finally, using the pointwise bound in (
4.24
), we estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{({\textbf{L},\textbf{N}})\in\mathcal{B}}\left|\mathcal{K}_{3}(s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{-2}\sum_{({\textbf{L},\textbf{N}})\in\mathcal{B}}L^{-3}(L^{\prime})^{-2}\langle\max(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{2}\langle\min(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{4}\|\rho_{L}\|_{L^{1}}\|\rho_{L^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}}\left\|\widehat{f_{N_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\widehat{f_{N_{2}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\widehat{f_{N_{3}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-2}\sum_{({\textbf{L},\textbf{N}})\in\mathcal{B}}L^{-1}\langle\max(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{2}\langle\min(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{4}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{-k}{\langle N_{2}\rangle}^{-k}{\langle N_{3}\rangle}^{-k}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-\frac{11}{10}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M^{-(1+{\delta})}\leq N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\leq M^{\frac{2}{n}}\\
L^{\prime}\sim\max(N_{1},N_{3})\end{subarray}}\langle\max(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{2}\langle\min(N_{1},N_{3})\rangle^{4}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{-k}{\langle N_{2}\rangle}^{-k}{\langle N_{3}\rangle}^{-k}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-(1+{\delta})}{\langle N\rangle}^{-k}.

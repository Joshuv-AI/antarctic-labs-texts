Uniformity of geodesic flow in non-integrable 3-manifolds
J. Beck, W.W.L. Chen, Y. Yang

Abstract




1.
Introduction and the splitting method

Very little is known about non-integrable dynamical systems in dimensions greater than
2
.
A perfect illustration of this sad fact is the following open problem which is the
3
-dimensional non-integrable analogue
of the Kronecker–Weyl equidistribution theorem concerning geodesic flow on the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
.

A vector
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is called a
Kronecker direction
in
3
-space if the coordinates
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}

are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Open Problem
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a finite polycube translation
3
-manifold.
Is it true that any half-infinite geodesic in
\mathcal{M}
with a Kronecker direction and which does not hit a singular point of
\mathcal{M}
is uniformly distributed?

The answer to the corresponding
2
-dimensional problem is given by the Gutkin–Veech theorem,
but the method does not seem to work in higher dimensions.
In
3
dimensions, even the simpler problem of density is wide open.

Here we introduce a new geometric method which enables us to solve the Open Problem
for infinitely many finite polycube translation
3
-manifolds which, in the first instance,
satisfy a very special condition which makes them

essentially integrable
in one of the
3
directions.
We then extend our argument in Sections
8
–
9

to include more finite polycube translation
3
-manifolds.

We call our new approach the
splitting method
.
It uses the Birkhoff ergodic theorem, and leads to time-qualitative uniformity.
It is an open problem whether time-quantitative uniformity can be established in these situations.

To illustrate our method and ideas, we consider in the first instance the L-solid translation
3
-manifold,
the simplest non-integrable example in
3
-dimensions.
This
3
-manifold consists of
3
atomic cubes arranged in the shape of the letter L, with a
top cube
, a
middle cube

and a
right cube
, together with face identification given by perpendicular translation.
The picture on the right in Figure 1.1 shows the
Y
-faces which are perpendicular to the
y
-axis.
The two faces
Y_{2}
are identified with each other, and the two faces
Y_{3}
are identified with each other.

\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{figure-1-1.pdf}\\
\mbox{Figure 1.1: the $X$-faces and $Y$-faces of the L-solid translation $3$-manifold}\end{array}

Theorem 1
.

Any half-infinite geodesic in the L-solid translation
3
-manifold with a Kronecker direction
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

is uniformly distributed unless it hits a singularity.

Suppose that
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a Kronecker direction in
3
-space.
Linear independence guarantees that the coordinates
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
are non-zero.
It is convenient to denote the Kronecker direction instead by the parallel vector
\mathbf{v}=(\alpha,1,\beta)
,
where
\alpha=v_{1}/v_{2}
,
\beta=v_{3}/v_{2}
and
\alpha/\beta
are all irrational.
For simplicity, we assume that
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
.
We can further assume that
\alpha<1
.
If
\alpha>1
, then we simply interchange the roles of
x
and
y
.

We consider the
discretization
of the
\mathbf{v}
-flow in the L-solid translation
3
-manifold relative to the
Y
-faces,
where the flow hits the faces
Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{3}
.
Let

T_{\alpha,\beta}:\mathcal{Y}=Y_{1}\cup Y_{2}\cup Y_{3}\to\mathcal{Y}

(1.1)

denote the relevant discrete transformation defined by consecutive hitting points.

Under our assumptions on the parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
, the
\mathbf{v}
-flow encounters split singularities in the form
of edges of the L-solid translation
3
-manifold, highlighted in Figure 1.1 in bold.
For instance, consider a geodesic segment in the
\mathbf{v}
-direction that hits the common edge of the faces
Y_{1}
and
Y_{3}
.
If we move this geodesic segment marginally to the left, then it crosses the face
Y_{1}
, quickly hits the right face
X_{1}

and then jumps to the left face
X_{1}
.
If we move this geodesic segment marginally to the right, then it crosses the face
X_{3}
, quickly hits the top face
Y_{3}

and then jumps to the bottom face
Y_{3}
.

Consider now the image under
T_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}
of the singular edge of the L-solid translation
3
-manifold which is the
common edge of the faces
Y_{1}
and
Y_{3}
.
It is a line segment in the
z
-direction on the face
Y_{2}
, as shown in Figure 1.2.

\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{figure-1-2.pdf}\\
\mbox{Figure 1.2: a strip round the inverse image of a singular edge}\end{array}

Consider a narrow strip neighbourhood
S_{2}
of this line segment, shown in Figure 1.2 as the gray strip on the face
Y_{2}
.
The image of this strip under
T_{\alpha,\beta}
splits along the singular edge.
Our splitting method concerns making use of such splits.

Remark
.

Note that the front
Z
-faces on the plane
z=0
and the corresponding back
Z
-faces on the plane
z=1
are pairwise identified,
and none of the edges of these faces is a singular edge, so the L-solid translation
3
-manifold is essentially integrable in the

z
-direction.

The discretization
T_{\alpha,\beta}
on
\mathcal{Y}
defined by (
1.1
) is area preserving.
Our first goal is to prove that it is also
ergodic
on
\mathcal{Y}
.
Suppose, on the contrary, that it is not ergodic.
Then there exist two disjoint
T_{\alpha,\beta}
-invariant subsets
\mathcal{W}
(white) and
\mathcal{S}
(silver) of
\mathcal{Y}

such that
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{W}\cup\mathcal{S}
and

xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

0<\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{W})\leqslant\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{S})<3,

(1.2)

where
\lambda_{2}
denotes the
2
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
We show that this leads to a contradiction.

Consider the projection of
\mathcal{Y}
to the unit torus
[0,1)^{2}
, given by

\mathcal{Y}\to[0,1)^{2}:(x,y,z)\mapsto(\{x\},\{z\}),

(1.3)

where we take the fractional parts of the first and the third coordinates and omit reference to the second coordinate.
Then for every point
P\in[0,1)^{2}
, there are precisely
3
distinct points
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}\in\mathcal{Y}
which have projection image
P
.
Let

f_{\mathcal{W}}(P)=|\{P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}\}\cap\mathcal{W}|\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{\mathcal{S}}(P)=|\{P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}\}\cap\mathcal{S}|

(1.4)

denote the number of these
3
points that fall into
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{S}
respectively.
We refer to
f_{\mathcal{W}}
and
f_{\mathcal{S}}
as the multiplicity functions of the invariant sets
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{S}
respectively,
and they are non-negative integer valued functions defined on the unit torus
[0,1)^{2}
that satisfy the condition

f_{\mathcal{W}}(P)+f_{\mathcal{S}}(P)=3\quad\mbox{for almost all $P\in[0,1)^{2}$}.

(1.5)

We also consider the corresponding projection of

T_{\alpha,\beta}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Y}\quad\mbox{to}\quad T_{0}:[0,1)^{2}\to[0,1)^{2},

which is simply the
(\alpha,\beta)
-shift on the unit torus
[0,1)^{2}
.

Remark
.

Strictly speaking,
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{S}
are subsets of
\mathcal{Y}
.
However, it is convenient to view the pair as a
2
-colouring of the set
\mathcal{Y}
with colours
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{S}
,
and not make any distinction between subset and colour.

Since
\mathbf{v}
is a Kronecker direction, it is not difficult to show that
T_{0}
is ergodic.
Indeed, one way is to derive it from the Kronecker–Weyl equidistribution theorem on the unit torus.
Here we give a simpler proof by using the more elementary Kronecker density theorem which is valid in every dimension
d\geqslant 1
.
However, we restrict our discussion to the case
d=2
.

Let
\mathbf{w}=(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
\alpha,\beta
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
,
and let
T_{0}
be the
\mathbf{w}
-shift on the unit torus
[0,1)^{2}
.
If
T_{0}
is not ergodic, then there is a
\mathbf{w}
-shift-invariant measurable subset
A\subset[0,1)^{2}
such that

0<\lambda_{2}(A)<1\quad\mbox{and}\quad A+\mathbf{w}=A,

(1.6)

where
A+\mathbf{w}
denotes the image of
A
under
T_{0}
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Suppose that the set
A\subset[0,1)^{2}
is measurable.
Then

\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\lambda_{2}(A\cap(A+\mathbf{t}))\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}=(\lambda_{2}(A))^{2}.

(1.7)

Proof.

For any set
B\subset[0,1)^{2}
, let
\chi_{B}
denote the characteristic function of
B
.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\lambda_{2}(A\cap(A+\mathbf{t}))\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}=\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\left(\int_{A}\chi_{A+\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\left(\int_{A}\chi_{A-\mathbf{x}}(-\mathbf{t})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}=\int_{A}\left(\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\chi_{A-\mathbf{x}}(-\mathbf{t})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{A}\lambda_{2}(A-\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=\int_{A}\lambda_{2}(A)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=(\lambda_{2}(A))^{2}.

The change in the order of integration is justified by Fubini’s theorem, and this completes the proof.
∎

The identity (
1.7
) represents an average.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be chosen to be sufficiently small.
Then there exists
\mathbf{t}_{0}\in[0,1)^{2}
such that

\lambda_{2}(A\cap(A+\mathbf{t}_{0}))<(\lambda_{2}(A))^{2}+\varepsilon,

and the Kronecker density theorem implies that there exists a positive integer
n_{0}
such that the multiple
n_{0}\mathbf{w}

gets so close to
\mathbf{t}_{0}
modulo
[0,1)^{2}
that

\lambda_{2}(A\cap(A+n_{0}\mathbf{w}))<\lambda_{2}(A\cap(A+\mathbf{t}_{0}))+\varepsilon.

Then it follows that

xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

\lambda_{2}(A\cap(A+n_{0}\mathbf{w}))<(\lambda_{2}(A))^{2}+2\varepsilon.

(1.8)

On the other hand, the identity on the right in (
1.6
) clearly gives
A+n_{0}\mathbf{w}=A
, so (
1.8
) becomes

\lambda_{2}(A)<(\lambda_{2}(A))^{2}+2\varepsilon
.
However, this inequality cannot possibly hold when
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small, since
0<\lambda_{2}(A)<1
.
The contradiction establishes the ergodicity of
T_{0}
.

The Birkhoff ergodic theorem now guarantees that both
f_{\mathcal{W}}
and
f_{\mathcal{S}}
are constant integer valued functions.
It then follows from (
1.2
) and (
1.5
) that

f_{\mathcal{W}}(P)=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{\mathcal{S}}(P)=2\quad\mbox{for almost all $P\in[0,1)^{2}$}.

(1.9)

Let us return to the narrow strip
S_{2}
on the face
Y_{2}
, as shown in Figure 1.2.
We denote by

and
S_{3}
narrow strips on the lower faces

respectively, where the images on

) coincide.

are in the same relative positions on the faces

of the decomposition of the strips

.

.

Indeed, the classical Kronecker density theorem implies that even the

to the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
is not dense.

does not intersect the boundary of a rectangle of the set
\mathcal{W}_{1}
and also satisfies the inequality

xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

\frac{\lambda_{2}(R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;1+i_{0};\beta;j_{0}+i_{0})\cap(\mathcal{W}\bigtriangleup\mathcal{W}_{1}))}{\lambda_{2}(R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;1+i_{0};\beta;j_{0}+i_{0}))}<\delta.

(3.23)

The former implies that

R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;1+i_{0};\beta;j_{0}+i_{0})\subset\mathcal{W}_{1}\quad\mbox{or}\quad R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;1+i_{0};\beta;j_{0}+i_{0})\subset\mathcal{W}_{1}^{c}.

(3.24)

If the first condition in (
3.24
) holds, then it follows from (
3.23
) that

\frac{\lambda_{2}(R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;1+i_{0};\beta;j_{0}+i_{0})\cap\mathcal{W})}{\lambda_{2}(R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;1+i_{0};\beta;j_{0}+i_{0}))}\geqslant 1-\delta.

(3.25)

If the second condition in (
3.24
) holds, then it follows from (
3.12
) and (
3.23
) that

\frac{\lambda_{2}(R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;1+i_{0};\beta;j_{0}+i_{0})\cap\mathcal{S})}{\lambda_{2}(R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;1+i_{0};\beta;j_{0}+i_{0}))}\geqslant 1-\delta.

(3.26)

Since
T_{\alpha,\beta}
is measure-preserving and the subsets
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{S}
are
T_{\alpha,\beta}
-invariant,
it follows that each condition (
3.25
) or (
3.26
), if true, extends to the whole
T_{\alpha,\beta}
-power chain.
Thus in particular, one of the inequalities

\frac{\lambda_{2}(R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;1;\beta;j_{0})\cap\mathcal{W})}{\lambda_{2}(R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;1;\beta;j_{0}))}\geqslant 1-\delta\quad\mbox{or}\quad\frac{\lambda_{2}(R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;1;\beta;j_{0})\cap\mathcal{S})}{\lambda_{2}(R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;1;\beta;j_{0}))}\geqslant 1-\delta

holds.
Similarly, it follows from (ii) that one of the inequalities

\frac{\lambda_{2}(R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;-1;\beta;j_{0}-2)\cap\mathcal{W})}{\lambda_{2}(R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;-1;\beta;j_{0}-2))}\geqslant 1-\delta\quad\!\mbox{or}\quad\!\frac{\lambda_{2}(R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;-1;\beta;j_{0}-2)\cap\mathcal{S})}{\lambda_{2}(R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;-1;\beta;j_{0}-2))}\geqslant 1-\delta

holds.
For
\sigma=1,2,3
, we now take

R_{\sigma}=R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;-1;\beta;j_{0}-2)=R_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;-1;\beta;j_{0}-2)

on the face
Y_{\sigma}
, and take

R^{*}_{\sigma}=R^{\dagger}_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;1;\beta;j_{0})=R_{\sigma,k,h}(\alpha;1;\beta;j_{0})

on the face
Y_{\sigma}
.

To complete the proof of Lemma
1.2
, it remains to specify the parameters.

To satisfy the condition (
1.11
) and its analogues, we need
1-\delta>99/100
, so that
\delta<1/100
.
In view of (
3.20
), we need the parameter
\varepsilon>0
to satisfy

\varepsilon<\frac{1}{810000}.

The parameter
\varepsilon>0
also needs to be sufficiently small to ensure that the proportion (
3.21
)
of defective power chains is small.
Note that that we can guarantee that

\frac{q_{k+1}+q^{\prime}_{h+1}}{3(q^{\prime}_{h+1}-q_{k+1})}\leqslant 1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{q^{\prime}_{h+1}-1}{3(q^{\prime}_{h+1}-q_{k+1})}\leqslant 1

by ensuring that the two continued fraction denominators

and
q^{\prime}_{h+1}
satisfy the condition

q^{\prime}_{h+1}\geqslant c_{2}q_{k+1}
for some suitably large absolute constant

.
Recall next that the constant

depends only on
\varepsilon
and
\mathcal{W}
.
It follows that we can choose the continued fraction denominator

to be sufficiently large
to ensure that the factor

\frac{c_{1}}{q_{k+1}-1}

is as small as we please.
This completes the proof of Lemma
1.2
.

4.
Extending ergodicity to unique ergodicity

The term
unique ergodicity
refers to the extension from uniformity for half-infinite geodesics with almost every starting point
to uniformity for half-infinite geodesics with every starting point and that do not hit any singularity and become undefined.
The basic ideas and pioneering results are due to Bogoliuboff and Krylov
[
1
]
in 1937;
see also Furstenberg
[
2
, Sections 3.2–3.3]
.

The proof of Theorem
1
is by contradiction and consists of two parts.
Part 1 is a general argument based on Krylov–Bogoliuboff theory, and the key idea here is to reformulate the problem
in terms of invariant Borel measures and to use functional analysis.
Part 2 is an
ad hoc
argument based on the special property that the
\mathbf{v}
-flow in the L-solid translation

3
-manifold, when reduced
modulo one
to geodesic flow in the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
, exhibits uniformity.

For convenience, we denote by
\mathcal{M}
the L-solid translation
3
-manifold.

Part 1
.

Suppose, on the contrary, that there exist a half-infinite geodesic
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p}_{0};t)
,
t\geqslant 0
, with arc-length parametization,
where
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a Kronecker direction and
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{M}
is the starting point, and a non-zero continuous function
f_{0}

on
\mathcal{M}
for which uniformity fails.
Then the infinite sequence of integrals

\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}f_{0}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p}_{0};t))\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad n=1,2,3,\ldots,

does not converge to

xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda_{3},

(4.1)

where
\frac{1}{3}\lambda_{3}
is a probability measure on
\mathcal{M}
,
so that there exists an infinite strictly increasing sequence
h_{0}<h_{1}<h_{2}<h_{3}<\ldots
of positive integers such that the limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{h_{n}}\int_{0}^{h_{n}}f_{0}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p}_{0};t))\,\mathrm{d}t

exists but is not equal to (
4.1
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Under these assumptions, there exists an ergodic measure-preserving system
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{B},\nu,\mbox{$\mathbf{v}$-flow})
,
where
\mathcal{B}
denotes the Borel
\sigma
-algebra in
\mathcal{M}
, and
\nu
denotes a new
\mathbf{v}
-flow-invariant Borel probability measure,
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{h_{n}}\int_{0}^{h_{n}}f_{0}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p}_{0};t))\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\neq\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda_{3}.

(4.2)

Proof.

We introduce, for every integer
n=1,2,3,\ldots,
the particular normalized

length-counting measure

\nu_{n}
on Borel sets
B
in
\mathcal{M}
defined by

\nu_{n}(B)=\frac{1}{h_{n}}\int_{0}^{h_{n}}\chi_{B}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p}_{0};t))\,\mathrm{d}t,

where the characteristic function
\chi_{B}
satisfies

\chi_{B}(\mathbf{p})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\mbox{if $\mathbf{p}\in B$},\\
0,&\mbox{if $\mathbf{p}\not\in B$}.\end{array}\right.

We then apply a general theorem in functional analysis which says that the space of Borel probability measures
on any compact set is compact in the weak-star topology, which means that for every continuous function
f

on the compact space, as
n\to\infty
,

\mu_{n}\to\mu\quad\mbox{if and only if}\quad\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}\to\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

(4.3)

Remark
.

This compactness theorem is a non-trivial result.
The standard proof is based on the Riesz representation theorem
and can be found in most textbooks on functional analysis.

Thus the space
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{M})
of Borel probability measures on
\mathcal{M}
is compact,
so there exists a subsequence
\nu_{n_{m}}
of the sequence
\nu_{n}
and a Borel probability measure
\nu_{\infty}

such that
\nu_{n_{m}}\to\nu_{\infty}
as
m\to\infty
, and it follows from (
4.3
) that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{n_{m}}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty}\quad\mbox{for every continuous function $f$ on $\mathcal{M}$}.

(4.4)

Let
t_{1}>0
be arbitrary but fixed.
For any continuous function
f
defined on
\mathcal{M}
, let
f_{t_{1}}
denote the function obtained from
f
writing
f(t+t_{1})=f_{t_{1}}(t)
.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{t_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{n_{m}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h_{n_{m}}}\int_{0}^{h_{n_{m}}}f_{t_{1}}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p}_{0};t))\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{h_{n_{m}}}\int_{0}^{h_{n_{m}}}f(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p}_{0};t+t_{1}))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h_{n_{m}}}\int_{0}^{h_{n_{m}}}f(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p}_{0};t))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{h_{n_{m}}}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}(f(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p}_{0};t+h_{n_{m}}))-f(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p}_{0};t)))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{M}}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{n_{m}}+E(n_{m}),

where

xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

|E(n_{m})|\leqslant\frac{2t_{1}\sup|f|}{h_{n_{m}}}.

Since
f
is continuous on the compact set
\mathcal{M}
, the factor
\sup|f|
exists and is finite.
Thus
E(n_{m})\to 0
as
m\to\infty
, so that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{t_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{n_{m}}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{n_{m}}.

(4.5)

Combining (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we conclude that

\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{t_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty},

and this establishes the
\mathbf{v}
-flow-invariance of the limit measure
\nu_{\infty}
.
Furthermore, combining (
4.2
) and (
4.4
) with
f=f_{0}
, we conclude that

\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty}\neq\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda_{3}.

(4.6)

This completes the proof.
∎

Let
\mathfrak{M}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{M}
denote the set of Borel probability measures
\mu
on
\mathcal{M}
that are
\mathbf{v}
-flow-invariant
and satisfy the requirement

\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty}.

Then
\nu_{\infty}\in\mathfrak{M}_{1}
, so that
\mathfrak{M}_{1}
is a non-empty closed subset of
\mathfrak{M}
, and so
\mathfrak{M}_{1}
is compact.

With some appropriate
\nu^{*}\in\mathfrak{M}_{1}
, we can extend the above to a measure-preserving system

(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{B},\nu^{*},\mbox{$\mathbf{v}$-flow})
which is ergodic.
To prove this, we use the almost trivial fact that
\mathfrak{M}_{1}
is convex.
The general Krein–Millman theorem in functional analysis implies that the non-empty compact and convex set
\mathfrak{M}_{1}

is spanned by its extremal points.
It is a well known result in ergodic theory that the extremal points in the space of measures are the ergodic measures;
see Furstenberg
[
2
, Proposition 3.4]
.
Thus we can choose the measure
\nu^{*}\in\mathfrak{M}_{1}
above to be such an extremal point.

Part 2
.

Consider the measure-preserving system
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{B},\nu^{*},\mbox{$\mathbf{v}$-flow})
which is ergodic.
We now apply the flow version of the Birkhoff ergodic theorem.
For a continuous function
f
on
\mathcal{M}
and
\nu^{*}
-almost every starting point
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{M}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}f(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p};t))\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathcal{M}}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu^{*}.

(4.7)

We return to the continuous function
f_{0}
at the beginning of Part 1 for which uniformity fails, and consider the Borel set

Y=\left\{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{M}:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}f_{0}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p};t))\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\nu^{*}\right\}.

(4.8)

Then it follows from (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) that

\nu^{*}(Y)=1.

(4.9)

Meanwhile, the weaker form of Theorem
1
gives uniformity for
\lambda_{3}
-almost every
starting point
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{M}
, and so

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}f_{0}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p};t))\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda_{3}.

(4.10)

Combining (
4.6
) with
\nu^{*}
replacing
\nu_{\infty}
, (
4.7
) in the special case
f=f_{0}
and (
4.10
),
we conclude that

xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

\lambda_{3}(Y)=0.

This implies that for every
\delta>0
, there exists an infinite collection of disjoint axis-parallel rectangular boxes

\mathfrak{R}_{i}
,
i=1,2,3,\ldots,
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{3}(\mathfrak{R}_{i})<\delta\quad\mbox{and}\quad Y\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{R}_{i},

(4.11)

and it follows from (
4.9
) that

xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

\nu^{*}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{R}_{i}\right)=1.

(4.12)

Comparing (
4.11
) and (
4.12
), we see that there exists a positive integer
i_{0}
such that

\frac{\lambda_{3}(\mathfrak{R}_{i_{0}})}{\nu^{*}(\mathfrak{R}_{i_{0}})}<\delta.

(4.13)

Clearly, for
\nu^{*}
-almost every starting point
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{M}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}\chi_{\mathfrak{R}_{i_{0}}}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p};t))\,\mathrm{d}t=\nu^{*}(\mathfrak{R}_{i_{0}}),

(4.14)

where
\chi_{\mathfrak{R}_{i_{0}}}
is the characteristic function of
\mathfrak{R}_{i_{0}}
.

We next make use of the crucial fact that the
\mathbf{v}
-flow in
\mathcal{M}
reduces modulo one to
\mathbf{v}
-flow in the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
.
Since
\mathcal{M}
consists of
3
cubes, every point in the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
has
3
pre-images in
\mathcal{M}
.
We can clearly assume that
\mathfrak{R}_{i_{0}}
is contained in one of these
3
cubes.
Write
\mathfrak{R}_{i_{0}}=\mathfrak{R}(1)
, and then let
\mathfrak{R}(2)
and
\mathfrak{R}(3)
denote axis-parallel rectangular boxes
in the other
2
cubes with the property that
\mathfrak{R}(1),\mathfrak{R}(2),\mathfrak{R}(3)
reduce modulo one to the same
axis-parallel rectangular box in the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
.

Since
\mathbf{v}
is a Kronecker direction, the continuous form of the Kronecker–Weyl equidistribution theorem applies for
geodesic flow in direction
\mathbf{v}
in the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
.
It follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\int_{0}^{n}\chi_{\mathfrak{R}(i)}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p};t))\,\mathrm{d}t=\lambda_{3}(\mathfrak{R}(1)\cup\mathfrak{R}(2)\cup\mathfrak{R}(3))=3\lambda_{3}(\mathfrak{R}_{i_{0}})

(4.15)

holds for every starting point
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{M}
where the half-infinite geodesic does not hit a singularity.

Recall that (
4.14
) holds for
\nu^{*}
-almost every starting point
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{M}
.
Let
\mathbf{p}^{*}
be such a starting point.
Combining (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) gives

\frac{\lambda_{3}(\mathfrak{R}_{i_{0}})}{\delta}<\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}\chi_{\mathfrak{R}_{i_{0}}}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p}^{*};t))\,\mathrm{d}t.

(4.16)

On the other hand, the right hand side of (
4.16
) can be bounded from above by using (
4.15
)
with
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{p}^{*}
, and we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}\chi_{\mathfrak{R}_{i_{0}}}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{p}^{*};t))\,\mathrm{d}t\leqslant 3\lambda_{3}(\mathfrak{R}_{i_{0}}).

Choosing
\delta=1/3
clearly leads to a contradiction, and this completes the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem

The geodesic flow in
\mathcal{P}\times[0,1)

\mathbf{v}
-flow, with

\alpha
,
\beta
and

and

For the
\sigma

f_{\mathcal{W}}(P)=|\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{d}\}\cap\mathcal{W}|\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{\mathcal{S}}(P)=|\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{d}\}\cap\mathcal{S}|

\sigma=1,\ldots,d

The small special rectangles

), and let

denote small special rectangles of the decomposition of the strips

These special polycube translation

We are currently unable to answer in full generality the question raised in the Open Problem in Section

However, we are nevertheless able to answer this question in the affirmative for infinitely many finite polycube translation
3
-manifolds
that do not have any direction which can be considered integrable.
The construction of such examples of finite polycube translation

9.
Proof of Theorem
4

The proof of Theorem
4
goes very much along the lines of that of Theorem
1
.
However, extra ideas are needed at various stages of the argument.

Suppose that the period polycube translation
3
-manifold
\mathcal{M}_{0}
contains precisely
d
distinct atomic cubes.
Let
Y^{(1)},\ldots,Y^{(d)}
denote the bottom
Y
-faces of these atomic cubes, and let

xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

\mathcal{Y}_{0}=Y^{(1)}\cup\ldots\cup Y^{(d)}.

Then the
s
-fold split-covering
\mathcal{M}
contains precisely
sd
distinct atomic cubes, where each
Y
-face
Y^{(\gamma)}
,
\gamma=1,\ldots,d
,
of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
leads to
s
distinct
Y
-faces
Y^{(\gamma)}_{\sigma}
,
\sigma=1,\ldots,s
.
Let

xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

\mathcal{Y}=\bigcup_{\gamma=1}^{d}\bigcup_{\sigma=1}^{s}Y^{(\gamma)}_{\sigma}.

As before, we consider the discretization of the
\mathbf{v}
-flow in
\mathcal{M}
relative to the
Y
-faces,
where
\mathbf{v}=(\alpha,1,\beta)
is a Kronecker direction, and let

T_{\alpha,\beta}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Y}

denote the relevant discrete transformation defined by consecutive hitting points.
We need to show that this area-preserving map is ergodic on
\mathcal{Y}
.
Suppose, on the contrary, that it is not ergodic.
Then there exist two disjoint
T_{\alpha,\beta}
-invariant subsets
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{Y}

such that
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{W}\cup\mathcal{S}
and

0<\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{W})\leqslant\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{S})<sd,

(9.1)

where
\lambda_{2}
denotes the
2
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
We show that this leads to a contradiction.

The natural projection of
\mathcal{Y}
to
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
can be represented in the form

\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Y}_{0}:(x,y,z)\mapsto(\{x\},y,z),

(9.2)

indicating that, for every
\gamma=1,\ldots,d
, the images of the
Y
-faces
Y^{(\gamma)}_{\sigma}
,
\sigma=1,\ldots,s
,
coincide and are all equal to
Y^{(\gamma)}
,
and the image of each point in
Y^{(\gamma)}_{\sigma}
is in the same relative position in
Y^{(\gamma)}
.
Thus for every point
P\in\mathcal{Y}_{0}
, there are precisely
s
distinct points
P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}\in\mathcal{Y}

which have projection image
P
.
Let

f_{\mathcal{W}}(P)=|\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}\}\cap\mathcal{W}|\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{\mathcal{S}}(P)=|\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}\}\cap\mathcal{S}|

denote the number of these
s
points that fall into
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{S}
respectively.
They are non-negative integer valued functions defined on
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
that satisfy the condition

f_{\mathcal{W}}(P)+f_{\mathcal{S}}(P)=s\quad\mbox{for almost all $P\in\mathcal{Y}_{0}$}.

(9.3)

We also consider the corresponding projection of

T_{\alpha,\beta}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Y}\quad\mbox{to}\quad T_{0}=T_{0,\alpha,\beta}:\mathcal{Y}_{0}\to\mathcal{Y}_{0},

(9.4)

which is simply the discretization of the
\mathbf{v}
-flow in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
relative to the
Y
-faces.

Theorem
3
establishes the ergodicity of
T_{0}
.
The Birkhoff ergodic theorem then guarantees that both
f_{\mathcal{W}}
and
f_{\mathcal{S}}
are constant integer valued functions.
It then follows from (
9.1
) and (
9.3
) that there exist two integers
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
satisfying

1\leqslant s_{1},s_{2}\leqslant s-1\quad\mbox{and}\quad s_{1}+s_{2}=s

(9.5)

such that

xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

f_{\mathcal{W}}(P)=s_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{\mathcal{S}}(P)=s_{2}\quad\mbox{for almost all $P\in\mathcal{Y}_{0}$}.

(9.6)

We then derive a contradiction by establishing a suitable analogue of Lemma
1.2
.

For a Kronecker direction
\mathbf{v}=(\alpha,1,\beta)

\alpha

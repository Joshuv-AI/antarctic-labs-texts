Diffeomorphism groups of Morse-Bott foliation on the solid Klein bottle by Klein bottles parallel to the boundary
Sergiy Maksymenko

Abstract
. Let 𝒢 \mathcal{G} be a Morse-Bott foliation on the solid Klein bottle 𝐊 \mathbf{K} into 2 2 -dimensional Klein bottles parallel to the boundary and one singular circle S 1 S^{1} . Let also S 1 ​ × ~ ​ S 2 S^{1}\widetilde{\times}S^{2} be the twisted bundle over S 1 S^{1} which is a union of two solid Klein bottles 𝐊 0 \mathbf{K}_{0} and 𝐊 1 \mathbf{K}_{1} with common boundary K K . Then the above foliations 𝒢 \mathcal{G} on both 𝐊 0 \mathbf{K}_{0} and 𝐊 1 \mathbf{K}_{1} gives a foliation 𝒢 ′ \mathcal{G}^{\prime} on S 1 ​ × ~ ​ S 2 S^{1}\widetilde{\times}S^{2} into parallel Klein bottles and two singluar circles. The paper computes the homotopy types of groups of foliated (sending leaves to leaves) and leaf preserving diffeomorphisms for foliations 𝒢 \mathcal{G} and 𝒢 ′ \mathcal{G}^{\prime} .

Evidently,

We are also aimed here to illustrate usefulness of the developed methods.

First we recall the following statement:

Lemma 1.3
.

\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{K},\partial\mathbf{K})

W. B. R. Likorish
[

shown that

\mathbf{K}

The latter can be rephrased so that the restriction map

Evidently, its kernel is the group
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{K},\partial\mathbf{K})
of diffeomorphisms of
\mathbf{K}
fixed on the boundary.
Notice that the map

[
2

, R. Palais
[

, and E. Lima
[

]

Also, N. Ivanov

obtianed a general result on Waldhausen manifold which includes the statement that

This implies that

Moreover, it is also proved by C. Earle and J. Eeels

2.3.
Liftings of diffemorphisms

We will also use the following simple statement concerning covering maps:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\beta:X\to Y
be a covering map between path connected topological spaces, and
\xi:X\to X
be any covering transformation, i.e. a homeomorphism satisfying
\beta\circ\xi=\beta
.
Let also
h:Y\to Y
be a continuous map having a lifting
h^{\prime}:X\to X
, so
\beta\circ h^{\prime}=h\circ\beta
.
Then each of the following conditions implies that
h^{\prime}
commutes with
\xi
, i.e.
\xi\circ h^{\prime}=h^{\prime}\circ\xi
.

(1)

There exists a point
x\in X
such that
\xi\circ h^{\prime}(x)=h^{\prime}\circ\xi(x)
.

(2)

There exists a subset
A\subset Y
being invariant under
\xi
, i.e.
\xi(A)=A
, and such that
h^{\prime}
is fixed on
A
.

Proof.

(1)

Notice that
\xi\circ h^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime}\circ\xi
are also liftings of
h
, since

\displaystyle\xi\circ h^{\prime}\circ\beta=\xi\circ\beta\circ h=\beta\circ h,

\displaystyle h^{\prime}\circ\xi\circ\beta=h^{\prime}\circ\beta=\beta\circ h.

Moreover, by assumption, they coincide at
x
.
Then by uniqueness of liftings with one prescribed value, they should identically coincide on all of
X
.

(2)

Let
x\in A
be any point.
Then, by assumption,
\xi(x)\in A
.
As
h^{\prime}
is fixed on
A
we have that
h^{\prime}\circ\xi(x)=\xi(x)=\xi\circ h^{\prime}(x)
.
Hence by
(1)
,
h^{\prime}\circ\xi\equiv\xi\circ h^{\prime}
on all of
X
.
∎

Notice that each
h\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{K},\partial\mathbf{K})
lifts to a unique diffeomorphism
h^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}\times D^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\times D^{2}
fixed on
\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}
, so
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\beta}\circ h^{\prime}=h\circ{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\beta}
.
Since
\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}
is invariant under
\xi
, it follows from Lemma
2.4
that
h^{\prime}
commutes with
\xi
.

Moreover, suppose in addition that
h(K_{0})=K_{0}
,
h^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times 0)=\mathbb{R}\times 0
, then the restriction
h^{\prime}|_{\mathbb{R}\times(D^{2}\setminus\{0\})}
lifts in turn to a unique diffeomorphism
h^{\prime\prime}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0;1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0;1]
fixed on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times 1
, so

{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\alpha}\circ h^{\prime\prime}=h^{\prime}\circ{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0;1]\to\mathbb{R}\times(D^{2}\setminus\{0\}).

Again, since
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times 1
is invariant under
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
, we get from Lemma
2.4
that
h^{\prime\prime}
commutes with
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
.
These liftings
h^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime\prime}
of

will play an important role for our proofs.

Let us also mention that the group
\mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{G},\partial\mathbf{K})

\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{K},\partial\mathbf{K})

(2)

(1)

The proof of contractibility of the group

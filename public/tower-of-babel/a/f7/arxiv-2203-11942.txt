Abstract.

In this paper, we consider a class of quasilinear stationary Kirchhoff type potential systems with Neumann Boundary conditions, which involves a general variable exponent elliptic operator with critical growth. Under some suitable conditions on the nonlinearities, we establish existence and multiplicity of solutions for the problem by using the concentration-compactness principle of Lions for variable exponents found in
[
5
,
7
]
and the Mountain Pass Theorem without the Palais-Smale condition given in
[
43
]
.

Throughout this paper we assume

, and

. Write

C_{+}(\overline{\Omega})=\{p;p\in C(\overline{\Omega}),p(x)>1\quad\text{ for a.e. }x\in\overline{\Omega}\}.

L_{+}^{\infty}(\Omega)=\{p;p\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),p(x)>1\quad\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega\}.

For each

\displaystyle p^{+}=\sup_{x\in\Omega}p(x)\quad\text{ and }p^{-}=\inf_{x\in\Omega}p(x).

For any

\displaystyle L^{p(x)}(\Omega)=\{u:\quad\text{ u is a measurable real-valued function and }\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p(x)}dx<\infty\},

endowed with the Luxemburg norm

|u|_{p(x)}:=|u|_{L^{p(x)}}=\inf\left\{\mu>0;\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{u(x)}{\mu}\right|^{p(x)}dx\leq 1\right\}.

Remark 1

Variable exponent Lebesgue spaces resemble to classical Lebesgue spaces in many respects, the inclusions between Lebesgue spaces are naturally generalized, that is, if
0<~mes(\Omega)<\infty
and
p,q
are variable exponents such that
p(x)<q(x)
a. e. in
\Omega

L^{q(x)}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p(x)}(\Omega).

On the other hand, the variable exponent Sobolev space

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}|b_{ij}|_{\alpha_{ij}(x)}||u_{j}|^{\ell_{ij}-1}|_{\gamma_{j}^{\star}(x)}|u_{i}|_{\gamma_{i}^{\star}(x)}\Big)\Big]

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}|b_{ij}|_{\alpha_{ij}(x)}|u_{j}|_{(\ell_{ij}-1)\gamma_{j}^{\star}(x)}^{\ell_{ij}-1}|u_{i}|_{\gamma_{i}^{\star}(x)}\Big)\Big]

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}|b_{ij}|_{\alpha_{ij}(x)}\|u_{j}\|_{\gamma_{j}(x)}^{\ell_{ij}-1}\|u_{i}\|_{\gamma_{i}(x)}\Big)\Big]<\infty.

(3.6)

Hence,
\Psi_{\lambda}
is well defined. Moreover, one can easily see that
\Psi_{\lambda}{{}^{\prime}}
is also well defined on
X
. Indeed, using
(F2)
for all
h=(h_{1},...,h_{n})\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}{{}^{\prime}}(u)h

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\lambda(x)\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{i}}(x,u_{1},...,u_{n})h_{i}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c_{4}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{ij}(x)|u_{j}(x)|^{\ell_{ij}-1}\Big)|h_{i}(x)|dx.

Following Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}{{}^{\prime}}(u)h

\displaystyle\leq c_{5}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}|b_{ij}|_{\alpha_{ij}(x)}||u_{j}|^{\ell_{ij}-1}|_{\gamma_{j}^{\star}(x)}|h_{i}|_{{\gamma_{i}^{\star}(x)}}\Big)\Big].

The above propositions yield

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}{{}^{\prime}}(u)h

\displaystyle\leq c\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}|b_{ij}|_{\beta_{ij}(x)}\|u_{j}\|_{\gamma_{j}(x)}^{\ell_{ij}-1}\|h_{i}\|_{\gamma_{i}(x)}\Big)\Big]<\infty.

Now let us show that
\Psi_{\lambda}
is differentiable in the sense of Frechet, that is, for fixed
u=(u_{1},...,u_{n})\in X
and given
\varepsilon>0
, there must be a
\delta=\delta_{\varepsilon,u_{1},...,u_{n}}>0
such that

|\Psi_{\lambda}(u_{1}+h_{1},...,u_{n}+h_{n})-\Psi_{\lambda}(u_{1},...,u_{n})-\Psi_{\lambda}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{1},...,u_{n})(h_{1},...,h_{n})|\leq\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\|h_{i}\|_{\gamma_{i}(x)})

for all
h=(h_{1},...,h_{n})\in X
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\|h_{i}\|_{\gamma_{i}(x)})\leq\delta
.

Let
B_{R}
be the ball of radius
R
which is centered at the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{N}

and denote
B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}=\mathbb{R}^{N}-B_{R}
. Moreover, let us define the functional
\Psi_{R}
on

\prod_{i=1}^{n}W^{1,p_{i}(x)}(B_{R})\cap W^{1,\gamma_{i}(x)}(B_{R})
as follows:

\Psi_{R}(u)=\int_{B_{R}}\lambda(x)F(x,u_{1}(x),...,u_{n}(x))\ dx.

If we consider
(\mathcal{F}1)
and
(\mathcal{F}2)
, it is easy to see that
\Psi_{R}\in C^{1}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}W^{1,p_{i}(x)}(B_{R})\cap W^{1,\gamma_{i}(x)}(B_{R})\Big)
, and in addition for all
h=(h_{1},...,h_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}W^{1,p_{i}(x)}(B_{R})\cap W^{1,\gamma_{i}(x)}(B_{R})
, we have

\Psi_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}(u)h=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{B_{R}}\lambda(x)\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{i}}(x,u_{1}(x),...,u_{n}(x))h_{i}(x)\,dx.

Also as we know, the operator
\Psi_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}:X\rightarrow X^{\star}
is compact
[
21
]
. Then, for all
u=(u_{1},...,u_{n}),h=(h_{1},...,h_{n})\in X
, we can write

|\Psi_{\lambda}(u_{1}+h_{1},...,u_{n}+h_{n})-\Psi_{\lambda}(u_{1},...,u_{n})-\Psi_{\lambda}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{1},...,u_{n})(h_{1},...,h_{n})|

\leq|\Psi_{R}(u_{1}+h_{1},...,u_{n}+h_{n})-\Psi_{R}(u_{1},...,u_{n})-\Psi_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{1},...,u_{n})(h_{1},...,h_{n})|

+\left|\int_{B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}}\lambda(x)\Big(F(x,u_{1}+h_{1},...,u_{n}+h_{n})-F(x,u_{1},...,u_{n})\Big)-\right.

\left.-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}}\lambda(x)\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{i}}(x,u_{1},...,u_{n})h_{i})\,dx\right|.

According to a classical theorem, there exist
\xi_{1},...,\xi_{n}\in]0,1[
such that

\left|\int_{B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}}\lambda(x)\Big(F(x,u_{1}+h_{1},...,u_{n}+h_{n})-F(x,u_{1},...,u_{n})\Big)-\right.

\left.-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}}\lambda(x)\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{i}}(x,u_{1},...,u_{n})h_{i}\,dx\right|

=\left|\int_{B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}}\lambda(x)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{i}}(x,u_{1},...,u_{i}+\xi_{i}h_{i},...,u_{n})h_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{i}}(x,u_{1},...,u_{n})\right)\,dx\right|.

Using the condition
(\mathcal{F}2)
, we have

\Big|\int_{B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}}\lambda(x)\Big(F(x,u_{1}+h_{1},...,u_{n}+h_{n})-F(x,u_{1},...,u_{n})\Big)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}}\lambda(x)\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{i}}(x,u_{1},...,u_{n})h_{i}\,dx\Big|

\leq\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}}\lambda(x)b_{ij}(x)(|u_{j}+\xi_{j}h_{j}|^{\ell_{ij}-1}-|u_{j}|^{\ell_{ij}-1})h_{i}dx\Big)\Big|.

Using the elementary inequality
|a+b|^{s}\leq 2^{s-1}(|a|^{s}+|b|^{s})
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we can write

\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big((2^{\ell_{ij}-1}-1)\int_{B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}}b_{ij}(x)|u_{j}|^{\ell_{ij}-1}|h_{i}|dx+

+(\xi_{j}2)^{\ell_{ij}-1}\int_{B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}}b_{ij}(x)|h_{j}|^{\ell_{ij}-1}|h_{i}|dx\Big)\Big).

Then, applying Propositions
2.1
,
2.3
, and
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}c\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big(|b_{ij}(x)|_{\alpha_{ij}}\|u_{j}\|_{\gamma_{1}^{\star}(x)}^{\ell_{ij}-1}+|b_{ij}(x)|_{\alpha_{ij}}\|h_{j}\|_{\gamma_{j}^{\star}(x)}^{\ell_{ij}-1}\Big)\Big)\|h_{i}\|_{\gamma_{i}(x)},

and by the fact that

|b_{ii}(x)|_{L^{\alpha_{i}}(B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}})}\longrightarrow 0,

|b_{ij}(x)|_{L^{\alpha_{ij}}(B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}})}\longrightarrow 0

for
1\leq i,j\leq n
, as
R\rightarrow\infty
, and for
R
sufficiently large, we obtain the estimate

\left|\int_{B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}}\lambda(x)\Big(F(x,u_{1}+h_{1},...,u_{n}+h_{n})-F(x,u_{1},...,u_{n})-\right.

\left.-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{i}}(x,u_{1},...,u_{n})h_{i}\Big)\,dx\right|\leq\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\|h_{i}\|_{\gamma_{i}(x)}).

It remains only to show that
\Psi^{{}^{\prime}}
is continuous on
X
. Let
u^{m}=(u_{1}^{m},...,u_{n}^{m})
be such that
u^{m}\rightarrow u
as
m\rightarrow\infty
. Then, for
h=(h_{1},...,h_{n})\in X
, we have

\displaystyle|\Psi_{\lambda}^{{}^{\prime}}(u^{m})h-\Psi_{\lambda}^{{}^{\prime}}(u)h|

\displaystyle\leq|\Psi_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}(u^{m})h-\Psi_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}(u)h|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{B_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}}\Big|\lambda(x)\Big(\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{i}}(x,u_{1}^{m},...,u_{n}^{m})h_{i}-\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{i}}(x,u_{1},...,u_{n})h_{i}\Big)\,dx\Big|.

Since
\Psi_{R}^{\prime}
is continuous on
\prod_{i=1}^{n}W^{1,p_{i}(x)}(B_{R})\cap W^{1,\gamma_{i}(x)}(B_{R})
(see
[
21
]
), we have

|\Psi_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}(u^{m})h-\Psi_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}(u)h|\longrightarrow 0,

as
m\rightarrow\infty
. Now, using
(\mathcal{F}2)
once again and taking into account that the other terms on the right-hand side of the above inequality tend to zero, we conclude that
\Psi_{\lambda}{{}^{\prime}}
is continuous on
X
.
\blacktriangleleft

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\left\{u_{m}=(u_{1m},u_{2m},...,u_{nm})\right\}
be a Palais-Smale sequence for the Euler-Lagrange functional
E_{\lambda}
. If
(\mathcal{F}3)
is satisfied, then
\left\{u_{m}\right\}
is bounded.

Proof.

Let
\left\{u_{m}=(u_{1m},u_{2m},...,u_{nm})\right\}
be a Palais-Smale sequence for the Euler-Lagrange functional
E_{\lambda}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{\lambda}(u_{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{M_{i}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{i}(u_{im}(x))\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{1}{s_{i}(x)}|u_{im}(x)|^{s_{i}(x)}dx

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\dfrac{1}{t_{i}(x)}|u_{im}(x)|^{t_{i}(x)}d\sigma_{x}-\int_{\Omega}\lambda(x)F(x,u_{1m}(x),u_{2m}(x)...,u_{nm}(x))dx

\displaystyle=C+o_{m}(1).

On the other hand for all
v=(v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{n})\in X
, we have

E_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{m})v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\left(\mathcal{A}_{i}(u_{im})\right)\int_{\Omega}a_{i}(|\nabla u_{im}|^{p_{i}(x)})|\nabla u_{im}|^{p_{i}(x)-2}\nabla u_{im}\nabla v_{i}\,dx-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}|u_{im}|^{s_{i}(x)-2}u_{im}v_{i}\,dx\\
\quad-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}|u_{im}|^{s_{i}(x)-2}u_{im}v_{i}\,d\sigma_{x}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lambda(x)F_{u_{i}}(x,u_{1m},u_{2m},...,u_{nm})v_{i}\,dx=o_{m}(1).

(3.7)

Then

\displaystyle E_{\lambda}(u_{m})-E_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{m})\Big(\frac{u_{m}}{\theta}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\widehat{M_{i}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{i}(u_{im})\right)-\frac{1}{\theta_{i}}M_{i}\left(\mathcal{A}_{i}(u_{im})\right)\int_{\Omega}a_{i}(|\nabla u_{im}|^{p_{i}(x)})|\nabla u_{im}|^{p_{i}(x)}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{\theta_{i}}-\frac{1}{s_{i}^{-}}\Big)\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u_{im}|^{s_{i}(x)}dx+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{\theta_{i}}-\frac{1}{t_{i}^{-}}\Big)\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}|u_{im}|^{t_{i}(x)}d\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle\quad+\inf_{x\in\Omega}\lambda(x)\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{u_{im}}{\theta_{i}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{i}}(x,u_{1m},u_{2m},...,u_{nm})-F(x,u_{1m},u_{2m},...,u_{nm})\right]dx.

Next, using
(\textbf{{H}}_{4}),(\mathcal{M}_{1})-(\mathcal{M}_{2})
and
(\mathcal{F}3)
, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{\lambda}(u_{m})-E_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{m})\Big(\frac{u_{m}}{\theta}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{M}_{i}^{0}\Big(\frac{\sigma_{i}}{p_{i}^{+}\beta}\int_{\Omega}A_{i}(|\nabla u_{im}|^{p_{i}(x)})dx-\frac{1}{\theta_{i}}\int_{\Omega}a_{i}(|\nabla u_{im}|^{p_{i}(x)})|\nabla u_{im}|^{p_{i}(x)}dx\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{\sigma_{i}\mathfrak{M}_{i}^{0}}{p_{i}^{+}\beta}-\frac{\mathfrak{M}_{i}^{0}}{\theta_{i}}\Big)\int_{\Omega}a_{i}(|\nabla u_{im}|^{p_{i}(x)})|\nabla u_{im}|^{p_{i}(x)}dx.

Therefore, by using
(\textbf{{H}}_{2})
, there are positive constants
C_{i1}
and
C_{i2}
such that

\displaystyle E_{\lambda}(u_{m})-E_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{m})\Big(\frac{u_{m}}{\theta}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[C_{i1}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{im}|^{p_{i}(x)}\Big)+C_{i2}\mathcal{H}(k_{i}^{3})\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{im}|^{q_{i}(x)}\Big)\Big].

(3.8)

Suppose, by contradiction, that there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
\left\{u_{im}\right\}
, such that

\left\|u_{im}\right\|_{i}\to+\infty
.

If
k_{i}^{3}=0
, proposition
2.2
gives us

\displaystyle E_{\lambda}(u_{m})-E_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{m})\Big(\frac{u_{m}}{\theta}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}\|u_{im}\|_{i}^{p_{i}^{-}},

thus

\displaystyle C+o_{m}(1)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}\|u_{im}\|_{i}^{p_{i}^{-}},

which is a contradiction because
p_{i}^{-}>1
. Thus, we conclude that
\left\{u_{m}\right\}
is bounded in
X
.

On the other hand, if
k_{i}^{3}>0
, we will need to analyze the following cases :

(i)\left\|u_{im}\right\|_{p_{i}(x)}\to+\infty
and
\left\|u_{im}\right\|_{q_{i}(x)}\to+\infty
as
m\to+\infty
;

(ii)\left\|u_{im}\right\|_{p_{i}(x)}\to+\infty
and
\left\|u_{im}\right\|_{q_{i}(x)}
is bounded;

(iii)\left\|u_{im}\right\|_{p_{i}(x)}
is bounded and
\left\|u_{im}\right\|_{q_{i}(x)}\to+\infty
.

In the case
(i)
, for m large enough,
\|u_{im}\|_{q_{i}(x)}^{q^{-}}\geq\|u_{im}\|_{q_{i}(x)}^{p^{-}}
Hence, by (
3.8
), we get

\displaystyle C+o_{m}(1)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[C_{i}\|u_{im}\|_{p_{i}(x)}^{p_{i}^{-}}+C_{i}\mathcal{H}(k_{i}^{3})\|u_{im}\|_{q_{i}(x)}^{q_{i}^{-}}\Big],

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[C_{i}\|u_{im}\|_{p_{i}(x)}^{p_{i}^{-}}+C_{i}\mathcal{H}(k_{i}^{3})\|u_{im}\|_{q_{i}(x)}^{p_{i}^{-}}\Big],

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}\|u_{im}\|_{{}_{i}}^{p_{i}^{-}}

which is absurd.

In the case (ii), by (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle C+o_{m}(1)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}\|u_{im}\|_{p_{i}(x)}^{p_{i}^{-}},

Thence, since
p_{i}^{-}>1
, taking limit as
m\to+\infty
, we obtain a contradiction.

The case
(iii)
is similar to case
(ii)
.

Therefore, we conclude that
\left\{u_{m}\right\}
is bounded in
X
.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\left\{u_{m}=(u_{1m},u_{2m},...,u_{nm})\right\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X
be a Palais-Smale sequence with energy level

C_{\lambda}
, if

C_{\lambda(x)}<\min\left\{\inf_{1\leq i\leq n}\Big\{\Big(\frac{1}{\theta_{i}}-\frac{1}{t_{i}^{-}}\Big)\overline{T_{x_{j}}}^{N}\Big(D_{i}\Big)^{N/\gamma_{i}(x_{j})}\Big\},\inf_{1\leq i\leq n}\Big\{\Big(\frac{1}{\theta_{i}}-\frac{1}{s_{i}^{-}}\Big)S_{i}^{N}\Big(D_{i}\Big)^{N/\gamma_{i}(x_{j})}\Big\}\right\},

wehre

. Then there exists a subsequence strongly convergent in

.
\overline{T_{x_{j}}}
and

are the best positive constants of the Sobolev trace and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev embedding, see
2.4
and
2.8
.

\left\{u_{m}\right\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}

. By Lemma
3.1
, there is a subsequence still denoted by
\left\{u_{m}\right\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}

. So there exists positive and bounded measures
\mu_{i}
,

and

Hence by Theorem
2.7
and Theorem
2.8
, if
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}(J_{i}^{1}\cup J_{i}^{2})=\emptyset
then
u_{im}\rightharpoonup u_{i}
in
L^{s_{i}(x)}(\Omega)
and
u_{im}\rightharpoonup u_{i}
in
L^{t_{i}(x)}(\partial\Omega)
with

. Let us show that if

C_{\lambda}<\min\left\{\inf_{1\leq i\leq n}\Big\{\Big(\frac{1}{\theta_{i}}-\frac{1}{t_{i}^{-}}\Big)\overline{T_{x_{j}}}^{N}\Big(D_{i}\Big)^{N/\gamma_{i}(x_{j})}\Big\},\inf_{1\leq i\leq n}\Big\{\Big(\frac{1}{\theta_{i}}-\frac{1}{s_{i}^{-}}\Big)S_{i}^{N}\Big(D_{i}\Big)^{N/\gamma_{i}(x_{j})}\Big\}\right\}

and
\left\{u_{m}\right\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Palais-Smale sequence with energy level
C_{\lambda}
then
J_{i}^{1}\cup J_{i}^{2}=\emptyset
for all
i\in\{1,2,...,n\}
. Suppose there exists
i\in\{1,2,3,...,n\}
such that
J_{i}^{1}\cup J_{i}^{2}
is nonempty, then
J_{i}^{1}\neq\emptyset
or
J_{i}^{2}\neq\emptyset
.

.

.

0,&\quad\text{if }\left|x\right|\geq 2.\\

Now, we will prove that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\{\limsup_{m\to+\infty}M_{i}\left(\mathcal{A}_{i}(u_{im})\right)\int_{\Omega}a_{i}(|\nabla u_{im}|^{p_{i}(x)})|\nabla u_{im}|^{p_{i}(x)-2}\nabla u_{im}\nabla\phi_{j,\varepsilon}u_{im}\,dx\right\}

\displaystyle=0.

We remark that, due to the hypotheses

enough to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\{\limsup_{m\to+\infty}M_{i}\left(\mathcal{A}_{i}(u_{im})\right)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{im}|^{p_{i}(x)-2}\nabla u_{im}\nabla\phi_{j,\varepsilon}u_{im}\,dx\right\}

\displaystyle=0.

Abstract
. We generalize linear superalgebra to higher gradings and commutation factors, given by arbitrary abelian groups and bicharacters. Our central tool is an extension, to monoidal categories of modules, of the Nekludova-Scheunert faithful functor between the categories of graded-commutative and supercommutative algebras. As a result we generalize (super-)trace, determinant and Berezinian to graded matrices over graded-commutative algebras. For instance, on homogeneous quaternionic matrices, we obtain a lift of the Dieudonné determinant to the skew-field of quaternions.

Introduction

Superalgebra theory, which relies on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading and Koszul sign rule, admits many applications and turns out to be a non-trivial generalization of the non-graded case, regarding e.g. linear superalgebra, with the notion of Berezinian, or the classification of simple Lie superalgebras. Its success, both in mathematics and physics, prompted from the outset mathematicians to look for generalizations.
Mirroring the superalgebras, were thence introduced first
(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{n}
-graded analogues
[
29
,
30
]
, originally called

, and then more general
\mathchar 28672\relax
-graded versions, for an arbitrary abelian group
\mathchar 28672\relax
.
These latter were introduced independently by Scheunert
[
31
]
in the Lie algebra case, and by Nekludova in the commutative algebra case (see
[
22
]
). The “color” character of the notions considered, is encoded in a pair
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
consisting of a
grading group

\mathchar 28672\relax
and a
commutation factor
(or
bicharacter
), i.e., a biadditive skew-symmetric map
\mathchar 28949\relax:\mathchar 28672\relax\times\mathchar 28672\relax\to\mathbb{K}^{\times}
, valued in the multiplicative group of the base field
\mathbb{K}
.
Then, a
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebra over
\mathbb{K}
is an associative unital
\mathbb{K}
-algebra, which is
\mathchar 28672\relax
-graded, i.e.
\mathcal{A}=\oplus_{\mathchar 28941\relax\in\mathchar 28672\relax}\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28941\relax}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28939\relax}\cdot\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28940\relax}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28939\relax+\mathchar 28940\relax}
for all
\mathchar 28939\relax,\mathchar 28940\relax\in\mathchar 28672\relax

for any homogeneous elements

,

The attention to higher gradings is not just a mere question of generalization. Besides the original motives of a possible application to particle physics along the lines of the coupling of superalgebra and SUSY (see
[

,
31
]
),
higher gradings appear naturally in different branches of mathematics. In geometry, they play a role in the theory of higher vector bundles
[
15
]
.
For instance, the algebra of differential forms over a supermanifold happens to be a

-commutative algebra.
Moreover, many classical non-commutative algebras, such as the algebra of quaternions

(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)

\mathbb{H}\simeq\!\!\mathop{\rm\penalty\ Cl}\nolimits(0,2)

We are interested in graded linear algebra over a graded commutative algebra.
While a basic topic, this is the starting point for many developments. Thus, a well-suited notion of tensor product lies at the heart
of a putative quaternionic algebraic geometry
[
18
]
, while Moore determinant plays a central role in quaternionic analysis
[
3
]
.
In the general graded-commutative setting, the color Lie algebra of traceless elements, with respect to a graded trace, is basic in the derivation based differential calculus
[
14
]
.
Our aim is precisely to generalize trace, determinant and Berezinian to matrices with entries in a graded commutative algebra.
For quaternionic matrices, this is an historical and tough problem, pursued by eminent mathematicians including Cayley, Moore and Dieudonné. We mention the Dieudonné determinant, which is the unique group morphism
\mathrm{Ddet}:\GL(n,\mathbb{H})\to\mathbb{H}^{\times}/[\mathbb{H}^{\times},\mathbb{H}^{\times}]\simeq\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}
, satisfying some normalization condition.

The notion of graded trace, for matrices with entries in a graded commutative algebra
\mathcal{A}
, was introduced in
[
32
]
. The introduction of graded determinant and Berezinian was done slightly after in
[
21
]
.
These objects have been rediscovered in
[
8
]
, where they received a unique characterization in terms of their properties.
In particular, the graded determinant is constructed by means of quasi-determinants and UDL decomposition of matrices. Right after, a cohomological interpretation of the graded Berezinian has been given in
[
7
]
, in a close spirit to the construction in
[
21
]
. The main motivations of the works
[
8
,
7
]
was to lay the ground for a geometry based on quaternions, or more generally on Clifford algebras, and thus restrict to
((\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{n},\mathchar 28949\relax)
-graded commutative algebras.
Note that, so far, the graded determinant is only defined for matrices of degree zero, which is a strong restriction as we will see.

In the present paper, we follow another approach to the problem of defining trace, determinant and Berezinian in the graded setting.
We use their formulation as natural transformations and the Nekludova-Scheunert functor between the categories of graded-commutative and supercommutative algebras.
Its generalization to monoidal categories of graded and supermodules allows us to pull-back the wanted natural transformations
to the graded setting. The morphisms properties of trace, determinant and Berezinian are then preserved if restricted to
categorical endomorphisms

\mathcal{A}
-module

-linear maps

\mathcal{A}

Besides, contrary to the previous works

Categories of Graded Commutative Algebras

In this section, we review the basic notions of graded-commutative algebra and present the Nekludova-Scheunert Theorem, following
[

,
22
]
. For general notions on graded rings and algebras, we refer to
[
6
,
9
,
28
]
.

1.1.
Definitions and examples

A
\mathchar 28672\relax
-algebra
is an associative unital algebra
\mathcal{A}
over
\mathbb{K}
, with the structure of a
\mathchar 28672\relax
-graded vector space
\mathcal{A}=\oplus_{\mathchar 28939\relax\in\mathchar 28672\relax}\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28939\relax}
, in which the multiplication respects the grading,

(1.1)

\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28939\relax}\cdot\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28940\relax}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28939\relax+\mathchar 28940\relax}\;,\quad\forall\mathchar 28939\relax,\mathchar 28940\relax\in\mathchar 28672\relax\,.

If
\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28939\relax}\cdot\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28940\relax}=\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28939\relax+\mathchar 28940\relax}
for all
\mathchar 28939\relax,\mathchar 28940\relax\in\mathchar 28672\relax
, we say that
\mathcal{A}
is
strongly graded
.
The elements in
\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28941\relax}
are called
homogeneous elements
of degree
\mathchar 28941\relax
.
The
\mathchar 28672\relax
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
is called a
crossed product
if there exists invertible elements of each degree
\mathchar 28941\relax\in\mathchar 28672\relax
(see Remark
1.11
for an alternative, more usual, definition). It is a
graded division algebra
if all non-zero homogeneous elements are invertible.
Clearly, if a
\mathchar 28672\relax
-algebra is a crossed product, then it is strongly graded.
The converse holds if
\mathcal{A}^{0}
is a local ring
[
9
]
.

Morphisms of
\mathchar 28672\relax
-algebras
are morphisms of algebras
f:\,\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
that preserve the degree,
f(\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28941\relax})\subset\mathcal{B}^{\mathchar 28941\relax}
, for all
\mathchar 28941\relax\in\mathchar 28672\relax
.
The kernel of such morphism is an
homogeneous ideal
of
\mathcal{A}
, i.e., an ideal
I
such that

I=\bigoplus_{\mathchar 28941\relax\in\mathchar 28672\relax}\left(I\cap\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28941\relax}\right)\;.

The quotient of a
\mathchar 28672\relax
-algebra by a homogeneous ideal is again a
\mathchar 28672\relax
-algebra.
The class of
\mathchar 28672\relax
-algebras (over a fixed field
\mathbb{K}
) and corresponding morphisms form the category
\mathchar 28672\relax\mbox{-}\mathtt{Alg}
.

Let
\mathchar 28949\relax
be a map
\mathchar 28949\relax:\mathchar 28672\relax\times\mathchar 28672\relax\to\mathbb{K}^{\times}
. A
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebra
is a
\mathchar 28672\relax
-algebra in which the multiplication is
\mathchar 28949\relax
-commutative, namely

(1.2)

a\cdot b=\mathchar 28949\relax\big(\,\widetilde{a},\widetilde{b}\,)\,b\cdot a\;,

for all homogeneous elements
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
of degrees
\widetilde{a},\widetilde{b}\in\mathchar 28672\relax
.

Morphisms of
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebras
are morphisms of
\mathchar 28672\relax
-algebras.
The class of
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebras (over a fixed field
\mathbb{K}
) and corresponding morphisms form a full subcategory
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)\mbox{-}\mathtt{Alg}
of
\mathchar 28672\relax\mbox{-}\mathtt{Alg}
.

Example 1.1
.

If
\mathcal{A}^{0}
is a unital associative and commutative algebra, then the group algebra
\mathcal{A}^{0}[\mathchar 28672\relax]
is a
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathbf{1})
-commutative algebra, with
\mathbf{1}:\mathchar 28672\relax\times\mathchar 28672\relax\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
the constant map equal to
1\in\mathbb{K}
. This is a crossed product in general and a graded division algebra if and only if
\mathcal{A}^{0}
is a field.

Example 1.2
.

Supercommutative algebras
are
(\mathbb{Z}_{2},\mathchar 28949\relax^{\!\!\mathop{\rm\penalty\ super}\nolimits})
-commutative algebras, with

\mathchar 28949\relax^{\!\!\mathop{\rm\penalty\ super}\nolimits}(x,y)=(-1)^{xy}\;,

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. The category
(\mathbb{Z}_{2},\mathchar 28949\relax^{\!\!\mathop{\rm\penalty\ super}\nolimits})
-
\mathtt{Alg}
is denoted for short by
\mathtt{SAlg}
.

Example 1.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The local commutative algebra

\mathbb{R}[\mathchar 28962\relax_{1},\ldots,\mathchar 28962\relax_{n}]/\!\raisebox{-2.79857pt}{${(\mathchar 28962\relax_{1}^{2},\ldots,\mathchar 28962\relax_{n}^{2})}$}\;,

which generalizes dual numbers, receives a
(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{n}
-grading by setting
\tilde{\mathchar 28962\relax}_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0),\;\ldots\;,\tilde{\mathchar 28962\relax}_{n}=(0,\ldots,0,1)
. This is a
((\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{n},\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebra with commutation factor

(1.3)

\mathchar 28949\relax(x,y):=(-1)^{\langle\;x,\;y\,\rangle}\;,

where
\langle-,-\rangle
denotes the standard scalar product of binary
n
-vectors. This algebra is not strongly graded if
n\geq 1
.

Example 1.4
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
and
n=p+q
. The real
Clifford algebra

\!\!\mathop{\rm\penalty\ Cl}\nolimits(p,q)
is the real algebra with
n
generators
(e_{i})_{i=1,\ldots,n}
, satisfying the relations
e_{i}e_{j}+e_{j}e_{i}=\pm 2\delta_{ij}
, with plus for
i\leq p
and minus otherwise. Setting
\tilde{e}_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0,1),\;\ldots\;,\tilde{e}_{n}=(0,\ldots,0,1,1)
, it turns into a
\left((\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{n+1},\mathchar 28949\relax\right)
-commutative algebra with commutation factor of the form (
1.3
).
For such a grading, the real Clifford algebras are graded division algebras.

Example 1.5
.

The algebra of
n\times n
matrices
\Mat(n;\mathbb{C})
turns into a graded division algebra with respect to the grading group
\mathchar 28672\relax=\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and is graded commutative for a commutation factor
\mathchar 28949\relax:\mathchar 28672\relax\times\mathchar 28672\relax\to\mathbb{U}_{n}
, with
\mathbb{U}_{n}
the group of
n
-th roots of unity (see
[
5
]
).

1.2.
Basic properties

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebra. Clearly, the unit of
\mathcal{A}
, denoted by
1_{\mathcal{A}}
or simply
1
, is necessarily of degree
0
. The commutation relation (
1.2
), together with the associativity of
\mathcal{A}
, implies that the
commutation factor

\mathchar 28949\relax
on
\mathchar 28672\relax
satisfies the three following conditions (see
[
31
]
):

(C1)

\mathchar 28949\relax(x,y)\mathchar 28949\relax(y,x)=1\;,

(C2)

\mathchar 28949\relax(x+y,z)=\mathchar 28949\relax(x,z)\mathchar 28949\relax(y,z)\;,

(C3)

\mathchar 28949\relax(z,x+y)=\mathchar 28949\relax(z,x)\mathchar 28949\relax(z,y)\;,

for all
x,y,z\in\mathchar 28672\relax
. Conversely, for any commutation factor
\mathchar 28949\relax
, there exists a
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebra.
An easy consequence of the conditions (
C2
) and (
C3
) is that

\mathchar 28949\relax(0,x)=\mathchar 28949\relax(x,0)=1\;,

hence
\mathcal{A}^{0}\subseteq Z(\mathcal{A})
, the center of
\mathcal{A}\;
.
As for the condition (
C1
), it implies
\left(\mathchar 28949\relax(x,x)\right)^{2}=1
, for all
x\in\mathchar 28672\relax
. Therefore the commutation factor induces a splitting of the grading group into an “even” and an “odd” part,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathchar 28672\relax=\mathchar 28672\relax_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{\overline{0\!}}}\cup\mathchar 28672\relax_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{\overline{1\!}}}

\displaystyle\mbox{ with }\;\;\;\mathchar 28672\relax_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{\overline{0\!}}}:=\{x\in\mathchar 28672\relax\,:\,\mathchar 28949\relax(x,x)=1\}\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;\mathchar 28672\relax_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{\overline{1\!}}}:=\{x\in\mathchar 28672\relax\,:\,\mathchar 28949\relax(x,x)=-1\}\;.

This is equivalently encoded into a morphism of additive groups

\begin{array}[]{lccc}\mathchar 28958\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax}:&\mathchar 28672\relax&\to&\mathbb{Z}_{2}\;,\end{array}

with kernel
\mathchar 28672\relax_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{\overline{0\!}}}\;
. The map
\mathchar 28958\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax}
provides a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading on every
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebra
\mathcal{A}
, also called
parity
and denoted by
\bar{\mathchar 28941\relax}:=\mathchar 28958\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax}(\mathchar 28941\relax)\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\;
. Homogeneous elements of
\mathcal{A}
are named
even
or
odd
depending on the parity of their degree. Notice that odd elements of
\mathcal{A}
are nilpotent.
Hence, if
\mathcal{A}
is strongly graded, we have
\mathchar 28672\relax_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{\overline{1\!}}}=\emptyset
.
The group morphism
\mathchar 28958\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax}
induces a
regrading functor

(1.5)

\begin{array}[]{lccc}\mathchar 28680\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax}:&(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)\mbox{-}\mathtt{Alg}&\to&\mathbb{Z}_{2}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Alg}\;,\end{array}

equal to the identity on arrows and such that
\mathchar 28680\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax}(\mathcal{A})
is the algebra
\mathcal{A}
with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\left(\mathchar 28680\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax}(\mathcal{A})\right)_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{\overline{0\!}}}=\bigoplus_{\mathchar 28941\relax\in\mathchar 28672\relax_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{\overline{0\!}}}}\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28941\relax}&\mbox{ and }&\left(\mathchar 28680\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax}(\mathcal{A})\right)_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{\overline{1\!}}}=\bigoplus_{\mathchar 28941\relax\in\mathchar 28672\relax_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{\overline{1\!}}}}\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28941\relax}\;.\end{array}

The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded algebra
\mathchar 28680\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax}(\mathcal{A})
is supercommutative if and only if
\mathchar 28949\relax
can be factorized through the parity, as stated below.

Proposition 1.6
.

[
22
]

Let
\mathchar 28949\relax
a commutation factor over
\mathchar 28672\relax
. Then, the two following statements are equivalent:

(i)

for all
x,y\in\mathchar 28672\relax
,
\mathchar 28949\relax(x,y)=\mathchar 28949\relax^{\!\!\mathop{\rm\penalty\ super}\nolimits}(\mathchar 28958\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax}(x),\mathchar 28958\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax}(y))\;;

(ii)

1.3.
Change of commutation factor

Let
\mathchar 28966\relax:\mathchar 28672\relax\times\mathchar 28672\relax\to\mathbb{K}^{\times}
be a map. One can twist the multiplication of a
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebra
\mathcal{A}=(A,\cdot)
by the map
\mathchar 28966\relax
in the following way:

(1.6)

a\star b:=\mathchar 28966\relax(\widetilde{a},\widetilde{b})\,a\cdot b\;.

The resulting graded algebra
\underline{\mathcal{A}}:=(A,\star)
is an associative deformation of
\mathcal{A}
if and only if
\mathchar 28966\relax
satisfies the cocycle condition

\mathchar 28966\relax(x,y+z)\mathchar 28966\relax(y,z)=\mathchar 28966\relax(x,y)\mathchar 28966\relax(x+y,z)\;,

for all
x,y,z\in\mathchar 28672\relax
. If in addition
\mathchar 28966\relax(0,0)=1
, then
\underline{\mathcal{A}}^{0}=\mathcal{A}^{0}
as algebras and

\mathchar 28966\relax
is called
multiplier
(see
[
31
]
).
Note that biadditive maps are multipliers.

If
\mathchar 28966\relax
is a multiplier, the deformed algebra
\underline{\mathcal{A}}
is a
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax^{\mathchar 28966\relax})
-commutative algebra,
the commutation factor
\mathchar 28949\relax^{\mathchar 28966\relax}:\mathchar 28672\relax\times\mathchar 28672\relax\to\mathbb{K}^{\times}
being given by

\mathchar 28949\relax^{\mathchar 28966\relax}(a,b):=\mathchar 28949\relax(a,b)\mathchar 28966\relax(a,b)(\mathchar 28966\relax(b,a))^{-1}\;.

Several multipliers can lead to the same change of commutation factor. More precisely,

Lemma 1.7
.

[
31
]

Let
\mathchar 28966\relax
,
\mathchar 28966\relax^{\prime}
be two multipliers on
\mathchar 28672\relax
. Then,
\mathchar 28949\relax^{\mathchar 28966\relax}=\mathchar 28949\relax^{\mathchar 28966\relax^{\prime}}
if and only if there exists a biadditive symmetric map
\mathfrak{b}:\mathchar 28672\relax\times\mathchar 28672\relax\to\mathbb{K}^{\times}
such that
\mathchar 28966\relax^{\prime}(x,y)=\mathchar 28966\relax(x,y)\,\mathfrak{b}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\mathchar 28672\relax
.

For a classification of multipliers and commutation factors, we refer to
[
14
]
.

1.4.
The Nekludova-Scheunert Theorem

Let
\mathchar 28966\relax
be a multiplier on
\mathchar 28672\relax
. In general, the
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebra
\mathcal{A}=(A,\cdot)
is not isomorphic to the
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax^{\mathchar 28966\relax})
-commutative algebra
\underline{\mathcal{A}}:=(A,\star)
obtained by change of commutation factor.
The link between these two types of algebra is encompassed by a functor, defined below.

Proposition 1.8
.

[
22
]

Let
\mathchar 28966\relax:\mathchar 28672\relax\times\mathchar 28672\relax\to\mathbb{K}^{\times}
be a multiplier.
There exists an isomorphism of categories

\displaystyle I_{\mathchar 28966\relax}:(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)\mbox{-}\mathtt{Alg}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax^{\mathchar 28966\relax})\mbox{-}\mathtt{Alg}

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=(A,\cdot)

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\underline{\mathcal{A}}=(A,\star)

equal to the identity on morphisms.

The proof is obvious, the inverse functor is given by
I_{\mathchar 28942\relax}
with
\mathchar 28942\relax(x,y):=(\mathchar 28966\relax(x,y))^{-1}\,
.

According to Scheunert
[
31
]
, for any commutation factor
\mathchar 28949\relax
, there exists a multiplier
\mathchar 28966\relax
such that
\mathchar 28949\relax^{\mathchar 28966\relax}
factorizes through the parity. This uses the fact that
\mathchar 28672\relax
is a finitely generated abelian group. By Lemma
1.7
, the set of such
NS-multipliers
,

\mathfrak{S}(\mathchar 28949\relax):=\{\mathchar 28966\relax:\mathchar 28672\relax\times\mathchar 28672\relax\to\mathbb{K}^{\times}\,|\,\mathchar 28949\relax^{\mathchar 28966\relax}=\mathchar 28949\relax^{\!\!\mathop{\rm\penalty\ super}\nolimits}\circ(\mathchar 28958\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax}\times\mathchar 28958\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax})\}\;,

is parameterized by symmetric biadditive maps from
\mathchar 28672\relax\times\mathchar 28672\relax
to
\mathbb{K}^{\times}
. It turns out that all
\mathchar 28966\relax\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathchar 28949\relax)
are biadditive
[
31
]
. This result, combined with Propositions
1.6
and
1.8
, yields the following Theorem, due to Nekludova
[
22
, p. 280]
. Scheunert has proved a similar theorem, in the Lie algebra setting
[
31
]
.

Theorem 1.9

(The Nekludova-Scheunert Theorem)
.

Let
\mathchar 28949\relax
be a commutation factor on
\mathchar 28672\relax
.
There exists a biadditive map
\mathchar 28966\relax
such that the functor
\mathchar 28680\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax^{\mathchar 28966\relax}}
(defined in (
1.5
)) takes values in
\mathtt{SAlg}
, and the composite

\begin{array}[]{rcccl}(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)\mbox{-}\mathtt{Alg}&\xrightarrow[I_{\mathchar 28966\relax}]{\;\sim\;}&(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax^{\mathchar 28966\relax})\mbox{-}\mathtt{Alg}&\xrightarrow[\mathchar 28680\relax_{\mathchar 28949\relax^{\mathchar 28966\relax}}]{}&\mathtt{SAlg}\end{array}

is a faithful functor.

If
\mathchar 28966\relax
is a NS-multiplier, the functors
I_{\mathchar 28966\relax}
satisfy automatically Theorem
1.9
and we call them
NS-functors
.

The
\mathchar 28672\relax
-algebras which are crossed product are characterized in
[
28
]
. This specifies as follows for
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebras.

Corollary 1.10
.

An algebra
\mathcal{A}
is
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative and a crossed product if and only if
\mathcal{A}\simeq I_{\mathchar 28966\relax}^{-1}(\mathcal{A}^{0}[\mathchar 28672\relax])
for some
\mathchar 28966\relax\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathchar 28949\relax)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebra. Since odd elements are nilpotent,
\mathcal{A}
is a crossed product implies
\mathchar 28672\relax=\mathchar 28672\relax_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{\overline{0\!}}}
.
For such
\mathchar 28672\relax
, the NS-functor
I_{\mathchar 28966\relax}
, with
\mathchar 28966\relax\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathchar 28949\relax)
, takes values in the category
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathbf{1})
-
Alg
of commutative
\mathchar 28672\relax
-graded algebras.
Moreover, it clearly sends crossed products to crossed products.
Thence, from the crossed product
\mathcal{A}^{0}[\mathchar 28672\relax]
, we get a
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebra
I_{\mathchar 28966\relax}(\mathcal{A}^{0}[\mathchar 28672\relax])
which is also a crossed product.
Conversely, if
\mathcal{A}
is
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative and a crossed product, it is easy to prove that
I_{\mathchar 28966\relax}^{-1}(\mathcal{A})
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}^{0}[\mathchar 28672\relax]
, and the result follows.
∎

Remark 1.11
.

By definition, the algebra
I_{\mathchar 28966\relax}^{-1}(\mathcal{A}^{0}[\mathchar 28672\relax])
is the
\mathcal{A}^{0}
-module generated by the group elements
(e_{\mathchar 28939\relax})_{\mathchar 28939\relax\in\mathchar 28672\relax}\,
, with product law

e_{\mathchar 28939\relax}e_{\mathchar 28940\relax}=\mathchar 28966\relax(\mathchar 28939\relax,\mathchar 28940\relax)e_{\mathchar 28939\relax+\mathchar 28940\relax}
for all
\mathchar 28939\relax,\mathchar 28940\relax\in\mathchar 28672\relax
.
This algebra
is usually denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{0}\rtimes_{\mathchar 28966\relax}\mathchar 28672\relax
and called
crossed product of
\mathcal{A}
by
\mathchar 28672\relax
relatively to
\mathchar 28966\relax
.

We provide now some examples of NS-functors.

Example 1.12
.

The algebra of differential forms
\mathchar 28682\relax
over a smooth supermanifold is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-algebra, where the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-degree of a homogeneous differential form
\mathchar 28939\relax
is provided by

with

the “super” degree and

the cohomological degree modulo

. There exist two conventions for the commutation relation of homogeneous differential forms

2.
Categories of Graded Modules

From now on,
\mathcal{A}
denotes a
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebra.We prove an analogue of Nekludova-Scheunert Theorem for the monoidal category of graded

-modules. For graded modules over
\mathchar 28672\relax
-algebras we refer to
[
28

, as for basic notions of category theory, we refer to the appendix based on

2.1.
Definitions

A
left
(resp.
right
)
graded
\mathcal{A}
-module

is a
\mathchar 28672\relax
-graded vector space over
\mathbb{K}
,
M=\oplus_{\mathchar 28940\relax\in\mathchar 28672\relax}M^{\mathchar 28940\relax}
, endowed with a compatible
\mathcal{A}
-module structure,
\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28939\relax}M^{\mathchar 28940\relax}\subset M^{\mathchar 28939\relax+\mathchar 28940\relax}
(resp.
M^{\mathchar 28940\relax}\mathcal{A}^{\mathchar 28939\relax}\subset M^{\mathchar 28939\relax+\mathchar 28940\relax}
), for all
\mathchar 28939\relax,\mathchar 28940\relax\in\mathchar 28672\relax
.
As
\mathcal{A}
is a
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebra, a left graded
\mathcal{A}
-module structure on
M
also defines a right graded
\mathcal{A}
-module structure on
M
, e.g., by setting

(2.1)

m\cdot a=\mathchar 28949\relax(\widetilde{m},\widetilde{a})\,a\cdot m\;,

for any
a\in\mathcal{A}
,
m\in M\,
.
A graded
\mathcal{A}
-module is a graded module over
\mathcal{A}
with compatible left and right structures, in the sense of (
2.1
).

A
morphism of graded
\mathcal{A}
-modules
is a map
\ell:M\to N
which is
\mathcal{A}
-linear and of degree
0\,
,

\displaystyle\ell(m+m^{\prime}a)=\ell(m)+\ell(m^{\prime})a

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\ell(m)}=\widetilde{m},

for all homogeneous
m,m^{\prime}\in M
and
a\in\mathcal{A}
.
Notice that “
\mathcal{A}
-linear”, here and thereafter, means right
\mathcal{A}
-linear, as it is of common use in superalgebra theory.
We denote the set of such morphisms by
\Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)
.
Graded
\mathcal{A}
-modules and corresponding morphisms form a category denoted
\mathchar 28672\relax\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}
.

Example 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a supercommutative algebra (see Example
1.2
), the graded
\mathcal{A}
-modules are usually called supermodules (see, e.g.,
[
10
]
).
Accordingly, the category
(\mathbb{Z}_{2})
-
\mathtt{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}
is denoted by
\mathtt{SMod}_{\mathcal{A}}\,
.

2.2.
Closed Symmetric Monoidal Structure

The tensor product of graded bimodules over a
\mathchar 28672\relax
-algebra and the internal
\iHom
functor (see
[
28
]
) particularizes in
\mathchar 28672\relax\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}
as follows.

2.2.1.
Tensor product

Let
M,N
be two graded
\mathcal{A}
-modules. Their tensor product is defined as the quotient
\mathbb{Z}
-module

M\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}N:=(M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N)/\!\raisebox{-2.79857pt}{${I}$}

where
I=\!\!\mathop{\rm\penalty\ span}\nolimits\{ma\otimes n-m\otimes an\;|\;m\in M,n\in N,a\in\mathcal{A}\}
. The
\mathchar 28672\relax
-grading

(2.2)

(M\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}N)^{\mathchar 28941\relax}:=\bigoplus\nolimits_{\mathchar 28939\relax+\mathchar 28940\relax=\mathchar 28941\relax}\left\{\sum m\otimes n\;\Big|\;m\in M^{\mathchar 28939\relax},n\in N^{\mathchar 28940\relax}\right\}

together with the following
\mathcal{A}
-module structures,

\displaystyle a(m\otimes n):=(am)\otimes n

and

\displaystyle(m\otimes n)\,a:=m\otimes(na)\;,

turns
M\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}N
into a graded
\mathcal{A}
-module.
The tensor product
\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}
endows the category
\mathchar 28672\relax\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}
with a monoidal structure.
The isomorphism

\begin{array}[]{lccc}\mathchar 28940\relax_{M,N}:&M\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}N&\rightarrow&N\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}M\\
&m\otimes n&\mapsto&\mathchar 28949\relax(\widetilde{m},\widetilde{n})\,n\otimes m\end{array}

defines a braiding, which satisfies

\mathchar 28940\relax_{N,M}\circ\mathchar 28940\relax_{M,N}=\id_{M\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}N}
thanks to the properties of the commutation factor
\mathchar 28949\relax
.
Hence, the category
\mathchar 28672\relax\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}
is a symmetric monoidal category.

Remark 2.2
.

If
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are two
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebras over
\mathbb{K}
, their tensor product
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{B}
is
\mathchar 28672\relax
-graded according to (
2.2
). The natural product

(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})=\mathchar 28949\relax(\widetilde{b_{1}},\widetilde{a_{2}})\,(a_{1}a_{2})\otimes(b_{1}b_{2})

turns
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{B}
into a
(\mathchar 28672\relax,\mathchar 28949\relax)
-commutative algebra.

2.2.2.
Internal
\iHom

A monoidal category is
closed
if the binary operation
\otimes
defining the monoidal structure admits an adjoint operation, the so-called
internal
\iHom
functor
. By definition, this latter satisfies the natural isomorphism

\Hom(N\otimes M,P)\simeq\Hom(N,\iHom(M,P)),

for all objects

,
N
and

. The next result is an easy adaptation of the ungraded case and appears, e.g., in
[

Proposition 2.3
.

The category
\mathchar 28672\relax\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}
of graded
\mathcal{A}
-modules is a closed symmetric monoidal category. The internal
\iHom

The
\mathcal{A}
-module structure of
\iHom_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)
reads as

and

where

,

are homogeneous.

By abuse of notation, we will refer to elements in

as

A.1.
Categories

A

(respectively
small

consists of

•

;

•

for every pair of objects

, a set of

(if no confusion is possible the subscript is usually dropped);

•

for any triple

, a

which satisfy the following axioms

Associativity:

For any given morphisms
f\in\Hom(Z,W)\,
,
g\in\Hom(Y,Z)
and
h\in\Hom(X,Y)\,
, the equality
f\circ(g\circ h)=(f\circ g)\circ h
holds.

Identity:

For every
X\in\Obj(\mathtt{C})\,
, there exists a morphism
\id_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{X}}\in\Hom(X,X)
such that, for any morphisms
f\in\Hom(X,Z)
and
g\in\Hom(Y,X)\,
, the following equalities hold

\displaystyle f\circ\id_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{X}}=f

and

\displaystyle\id_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{X}}\circ\,g=g\;.

All the categories encountered in this paper are
concrete categories
, i.e., the objects are sets with additional structure, and morphisms are functions. This translates into the existence of a forgetful functor to the category of sets, denoted by "forget".

A.2.
Functors

A
functor

F:\,\mathtt{C}\to\mathtt{D}
between categories consist of

•

an
object function

F:\,\Obj(\mathtt{C})\rightarrow\Obj(\mathtt{D})\,
,

•

for each pair

, an
arrow function

F:\,\Hom_{\mathtt{C}}(X,Y)\to\Hom_{\mathtt{D}}(F(X),F(Y))\,
,

such that
F(\id_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{X}})=\id_{\hskip-2.84544pt\phantom{.}{}_{F(X)}}
for all
X\in\Obj(\mathtt{C})\,
, and, when the composition is meaningful,
F(f\circ\,g)=F(f)\circ\,F(g)\,
.

The

and
G:\,\mathtt{A}\to\mathtt{B}
is a functor

given by usual composition of the corresponding object functions and arrow functions. Composition of functors is associative.

A particular example of functor is the

which assigns to every object, respectively to every morphism of
\mathtt{C}\,
, itself. It acts as the identity element for the composition of functors.

A functor
F:\,\mathtt{A}\to\mathtt{B}
is said

if there exists a second functor

such that

\displaystyle F\circ G=\mathbb{I}_{\mathtt{B}}

and

\displaystyle G\circ F=\mathbb{I}_{\mathtt{A}}\,.

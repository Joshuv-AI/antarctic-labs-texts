1.
Introduction and Main Results

We consider the harmonic map flow (HMF) from
\mathbb{R}^{2}
into
S^{2}

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u+|\nabla u|^{2}u~&\mbox{ in }~\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,+\infty),\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}~&\mbox{ in }~\mathbb{R}^{2}.\\
\end{cases}

HMF formally corresponds to the negative
L^{2}
-gradient flow for the Dirichlet energy

\mathcal{E}[u]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2},

which is decreasing along smooth solutions. A special class of solutions are given by the
k
-equivariant ansatz

u(re^{i\theta},t)=\Big(\cos(k\theta)\sin v,\sin(k\theta)\sin v,\cos v\Big),

and thus HMF gets reduced to a scalar equation for the polar angle

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}=v_{rr}+\frac{1}{r}v_{r}-\frac{k^{2}\sin(2v)}{2r^{2}},\quad(r,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\\
&v(r,0)=v_{0},~\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

In the energy critical dimension
n=2
, the scaling invariance of the Dirichlet energy
\mathcal{E}[u]
gives rise to the energy concentration and a natural question of singularity formation versus global regularity. Asymptotic profile decomposition has been studied in seminal works by Struwe
[
31
]
, Qing
[
24
]
, Ding-Tian
[
5
]
, Wang
[
35
]
, Qing-Tian
[
25
]
and Topping
[
33
]
. In a recent work
[
14
]
, Jendrej and Lawrie proved that the bubble decomposition in the
k
-equivariant class can be in fact taken continuously in time. Finite time blow-up for the two-dimensional HMF has also received much attention since the work by Chang, Ding and Ye
[
2
]
. Formal prediction of singularity with quantized blow-up rates was made by van den Berg, Hulshof and King
[
34
]
, and this was later rigorously proved by Raphaël and Schweyer
[
28
,
29
]
. Beyond the equivariant class, multi-bubble blow-up at finite time was constructed recently by Dávila, del Pino and Wei
[

HMF is a borderline case of the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation (LLG)

u_{t}=a(\Delta u+|\nabla u|^{2}u)+bu\wedge\Delta u,\quad a^{2}+b^{2}=1,~a\geq 0,~b\in\mathbb{R},

which models the evolution of isotropic ferromagnetic spin fields. A series of works by Gustafson, Kang, Nakanishi and Tsai (in various combinations)
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
aimed at investigating the behavior of the solutions to LLG near
k
-equivariant harmonic maps. They found, among other things,
that there is no finite time singularity for LLG with
k\geq 3
and for HMF with
k=2
near
k
-equivariant harmonic maps. Intriguingly, in the
2
-equivariant case, they classified the dynamics of scaling parameter
\mu(t)
of the map as

(1.2)

\log\mu(t)\sim\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{1}^{\sqrt{t}}\frac{v_{1}(s)}{s}ds,

yielding trichotomy dynamics:asymptotical stability, infinite time blow-up and eternal oscillation, see
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
. Here,
v_{1}(r)
is the first entry of the initial degree 2 map. However, the 1-equivariant case is left open, due to the very slow spatial decay of the harmonic map components. The goal of this paper is to fill this gap.

In this paper, we consider the two-dimensional HMF into
S^{2}
in the 1-equivariant class

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}=v_{rr}+\frac{v_{r}}{r}-\frac{\sin(2v)}{2r^{2}},~\quad(r,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(t_{0},+\infty),\\
&v(r,t_{0})=v_{0},\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad r\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\end{aligned}\right.

where
t_{0}>0
is some large initial time. The aim of the paper is to understand possible long-term behavior of (

Theorem 1.1
.

For
t_{0}
sufficiently large, there exist initial data
v_{0}(r)
with
v_{0}(0)=\pi
and
v_{0}(r)\sim r^{1-\gamma}
as

such that the global solutions to (

\|v_{r}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty))}\sim\begin{cases}t^{\frac{\gamma-2}{2}}\ln t~&\mbox{ if }~1<\gamma<2,\\
1~&\mbox{ if }~\gamma=2,\\
\ln t~&\mbox{ if }~\gamma>2,\\
\end{cases}~\mbox{ as }~t\to+\infty.

More precisely, the polar angle

takes the form

v(r,t)\sim\eta\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\left[\pi-2\arctan\left(\frac{r}{\mu(t)}\right)\right]

\mu(t)\sim\begin{cases}t^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}}(\ln t)^{-1},\quad&1<\gamma<2,\\
1,\quad&\gamma=2,\\
(\ln t)^{-1},\quad&\gamma>2.\\
\end{cases}

Here

The method of our construction is different from those used in aforementioned references, and this seems to be the first gluing construction of decaying solutions. In contrast to the local dynamics (
1.2
) when
k=2
, the slow spatial decay for the 1-equivariant case in fact triggers a subtle non-local dynamics of the dilation. The heart of the construction is a non-local dynamics, analogous to (
1.2
), governing the scaling parameter
\mu(t)
in a unified way:

(1.6)

\underbracket{\int_{t/2}^{t-\mu^{2}(t)}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}ds}_{:=I_{{\rm nl}}}+\underbracket{\frac{\mu(t)}{t}\vphantom{\int_{t/2}^{t-\mu^{2}(t)}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}ds}}_{:=I_{{\rm ss}}}\sim\underbracket{2C_{\gamma}v_{\gamma}(t)\vphantom{\int_{t/2}^{t-\mu^{2}(t)}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}ds}}_{:=I_{{\rm ic}}},\quad\forall\gamma>1.

Here,
I_{{\rm nl}}
is in fact from a non-local correction dealing with the slow spatial decay.
Such non-local/global feature usually appears in lower dimensional problems and was first observed in
[
3
,
4
]
. The second term
I_{{\rm ss}}
comes from a self-similar correction improving the error in the intermediate region, and the last term
I_{{\rm ic}}
is the contribution from the initial condition
v_{0}
whose expression depends only on
\gamma
(cf. (
2.4
)). The trichotomy in Theorem
1.1
is captured by approximating the non-local problem by a leading ODE, but the solvability of the full non-local problem is rather involved.

The rest of the paper is devoted to the construction of Theorem
1.1

Approximation and corrections

The first approximation is built on the one parameter family of steady states to the scalar equation (

Then we have

\sin(2Q_{\mu})=\frac{4\rho(\rho^{2}-1)}{(\rho^{2}+1)^{2}},\quad\cos(2Q_{\mu})-1=-\frac{8\rho^{2}}{(\rho^{2}+1)^{2}}.

Let us write

Then we have

t^{-1}(\ln t)^{-2},&\gamma>2\end{cases}\quad\sim|\dot{\mu}_{0}|.

\displaystyle|\varphi[\mu]|\lesssim\begin{cases}\begin{cases}t^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}},&r\leq\mu_{0}\\
t^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\ln t)^{-1}\langle\ln(r^{-1}t^{\frac{1}{2}})\rangle,&\mu_{0}<r\leq t^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
t^{3-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\ln t)^{-1}r^{-6},&r>t^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{cases}&\mbox{ \ if \ }1<\gamma<2\\
\begin{cases}t^{-1},&r\leq t^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
t^{2}r^{-6},&r>t^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{cases}&\mbox{ \ if \ }\gamma=2\\
\begin{cases}(t\ln t)^{-1},&r\leq t^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
t^{2}(\ln t)^{-1}r^{-6},&r>t^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{cases}&\mbox{ \ if \ }\gamma>2.\end{cases}

3.
Further elliptic improvement and the leading dynamics of the
\mu(t)

In order to improve the time decay of the error, we will introduce
\Phi_{e}
by solving the linearized elliptic equation. Let us first denote

v_{1}(r,t):=\eta(z)Q_{\mu}+\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}+\Psi_{*}+\eta(4z)\Phi_{e}

where
\eta(4z)
is used to restrict the influence of
\Phi_{e}
in the self-similar region.
Then we compute

(3.1)

\displaystyle E[v_{1}]=

\displaystyle-{\partial}_{t}\left(\eta(4z)\Phi_{e}\right)+{\partial}_{rr}\left(\eta(4z)\Phi_{e}\right)+\frac{1}{r}{\partial}_{r}\left(\eta(4z)\Phi_{e}\right)+\frac{1}{r^{2}}(\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}+\Psi_{*})

\displaystyle-\frac{\sin[2(\eta(z)Q_{\mu}+\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}+\Psi_{*}+\eta(4z)\Phi_{e})]}{2r^{2}}+\eta(z)\frac{\sin(2Q_{\mu})}{2r^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\partial}_{t}\left(\eta(4z)\Phi_{e}\right)+{\partial}_{rr}\left(\eta(4z)\Phi_{e}\right)+\frac{1}{r}{\partial}_{r}\left(\eta(4z)\Phi_{e}\right)-\eta(4z)\frac{\cos(2Q_{\mu})}{r^{2}}\Phi_{e}

\displaystyle-\eta(z)\frac{\cos(2Q_{\mu})-1}{r^{2}}\left(\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}+\Psi_{*}\right)+E_{e}

where

(3.2)

\displaystyle E_{e}:=

\displaystyle-\eta(z)\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\Big[\sin[2(Q_{\mu}+\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}+\Psi_{*}+\eta(4z)\Phi_{e})]-\sin(2Q_{\mu})-\cos(2Q_{\mu})2\left(\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}+\Psi_{*}+\eta(4z)\Phi_{e}\right)\Big]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\Big[-\sin[2(\eta(z)Q_{\mu}+\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}+\Psi_{*}+\eta(4z)\Phi_{e})]+\eta(z)\sin[2(Q_{\mu}+\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}+\Psi_{*}+\eta(4z)\Phi_{e})]

\displaystyle+2\left(1-\eta(z)\right)\left(\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}+\Psi_{*}\right)\Big].

Roughly speaking, we will choose
\Phi_{e}(\rho,t)
which solves

{\partial}_{rr}\Phi_{e}+\frac{1}{r}{\partial}_{r}\Phi_{e}-\frac{\cos(2Q_{\mu})}{r^{2}}\Phi_{e}\approx\eta(z)\frac{\cos(2Q_{\mu})-1}{r^{2}}(\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}+\Psi_{*}),

namely

{\partial}_{\rho\rho}\Phi_{e}+\frac{1}{\rho}{\partial}_{\rho}\Phi_{e}-\frac{\rho^{4}-6\rho^{2}+1}{\rho^{2}(\rho^{2}+1)^{2}}\Phi_{e}\approx\eta(\frac{\mu\rho}{\sqrt{t}})\mu\frac{-8\rho}{(\rho^{2}+1)^{2}}\left(\varphi[\mu](\mu\rho,t)+\psi_{*}(\mu\rho,t)\right).

The linearly independent kernels
\mathcal{Z},\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}
of
the homogeneous part satisfying the Wronskian
W[\mathcal{Z},\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}]=\rho^{-1}
are given as follows:

\mathcal{Z}(\rho)=\frac{\rho}{\rho^{2}+1},\quad\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}(\rho)=\frac{\rho^{4}+4\rho^{2}\ln(\rho)-1}{2\rho(\rho^{2}+1)}.

Let us write the orthogonality

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}[\mu]=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(\frac{\mu\rho}{\sqrt{t}})\frac{8\rho}{(\rho^{2}+1)^{2}}\left(\varphi[\mu](\mu\rho,t)+\psi_{*}(\mu\rho,t)\right)\mathcal{Z}(\rho)\rho d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(\frac{\mu\rho}{\sqrt{t}})\frac{8\rho^{3}}{(\rho^{2}+1)^{3}}\left(\varphi[\mu](\mu\rho,t)+\psi_{*}(\mu\rho,t)\right)d\rho.

By (
2.5
) and Proposition
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}[\mu]=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(\frac{\mu\rho}{\sqrt{t}})\frac{8\rho^{3}}{(\rho^{2}+1)^{3}}\bigg[-2^{-1}\Big(\mu t^{-1}+\int_{t/2}^{t-\mu_{0}^{2}}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}ds\Big)

\displaystyle+O\big(\mu^{3}t^{-2}\rho^{2}+|\dot{\mu}|\min\{\rho,\ln t\}\big)+g[\mu]\bigg]d\rho

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(\frac{\mu\rho}{\sqrt{t}})\frac{8\rho^{3}}{(\rho^{2}+1)^{3}}\left(v_{\gamma}(t)(C_{\gamma}+g_{\gamma}(t))+O(\mu\rho t^{-\frac{1}{2}}v_{\gamma}(t))\right)d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mu t^{-1}+\int_{t/2}^{t-\mu_{0}^{2}}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}ds\Big)\left(-1+O((t^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{-1})^{-2})\right)+O\big(\mu^{3}t^{-2}\ln(t^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{-1})+|\dot{\mu}|\big)+g[\mu]

\displaystyle+v_{\gamma}(t)(C_{\gamma}+g_{\gamma}(t))\left(2+O((t^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{-1})^{-2})\right)+O(\mu t^{-\frac{1}{2}}v_{\gamma}(t)).

Singling out the leading terms, we then have

(3.3)

\mu t^{-1}+\int_{t/2}^{t-\mu_{0}^{2}}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}ds\approx 2C_{\gamma}v_{\gamma}(t).

Based on this, we now derive the leading term
\mu_{0}
of the scaling parameter
\mu
. For
\mu_{0}(t)
with the form
\mu_{0}(t)=c_{1}t^{1-p_{0}}(\ln t)^{-1}
with
p_{0}<1
, we have

\dot{\mu}_{0}(t)=c_{1}(1-p_{0})t^{-p_{0}}(\ln t)^{-1}\left[1-(1-p_{0})^{-1}(\ln t)^{-1}\right].

For
t_{1}\leq\frac{t}{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t-\mu_{0}^{2}(t)}\frac{\dot{\mu}_{0}(s)}{t-s}ds=\int_{\frac{t_{1}}{t}}^{1-\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}(t)}{t}}\frac{\dot{\mu}_{0}(tz)}{1-z}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}(1-p_{0})t^{-p_{0}}\int_{\frac{t_{1}}{t}}^{1-\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}(t)}{t}}(1-z)^{-1}z^{-p_{0}}(\ln(tz))^{-1}\left[1-(1-p_{0})^{-1}(\ln(tz))^{-1}\right]dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}(1-p_{0})(2p_{0}-1)t^{-p_{0}}+O(t^{-p_{0}}(\ln t)^{-1}\ln(\ln t)),

where we have used the following estimates in the last step

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{t_{1}}{t}}^{1-\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}(t)}{t}}(1-z)^{-1}z^{-p_{0}}(\ln(tz))^{-1}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\ln t)^{-1}\int_{\frac{t_{1}}{t}}^{1-\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}(t)}{t}}(1-z)^{-1}z^{-p_{0}}dz+\int_{\frac{t_{1}}{t}}^{1-\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}(t)}{t}}(1-z)^{-1}z^{-p_{0}}\left((\ln(tz))^{-1}-(\ln t)^{-1}\right)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\ln t)^{-1}\int_{\frac{t_{1}}{t}}^{1-\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}(t)}{t}}(1-z)^{-1}dz+(\ln t)^{-1}\int_{\frac{t_{1}}{t}}^{1-\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}(t)}{t}}(1-z)^{-1}\left(z^{-p_{0}}-1\right)dz

\displaystyle+\int_{\frac{t_{1}}{t}}^{1-\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}(t)}{t}}(1-z)^{-1}z^{-p_{0}}\frac{-\ln z}{(\ln t+\ln z)\ln t}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\ln t)^{-1}\left(-\ln(t^{-1}\mu_{0}^{2}(t))+\ln(1-\frac{t_{1}}{t})\right)+O((\ln t)^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\ln t)^{-1}\left(-\ln(c_{1}^{2}t^{1-2p_{0}}(\ln t)^{-2})+\ln(1-\frac{t_{1}}{t})\right)+O((\ln t)^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\ln t)^{-1}\left(-\ln(c_{1}^{2})-(1-2p_{0})\ln t+2\ln(\ln t)+\ln(1-t_{1}t^{-1})\right)+O((\ln t)^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p_{0}-1+O((\ln t)^{-1}\ln(\ln t));

and

\int_{\frac{t_{1}}{t}}^{1-\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}(t)}{t}}(1-z)^{-1}z^{-p_{0}}(\ln(tz))^{-2}dz=O((\ln t)^{-1})

since

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1-\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}(t)}{t}}(1-z)^{-1}z^{-p_{0}}(\ln(tz))^{-2}dz=O((\ln t)^{-2})\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1-\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}(t)}{t}}(1-z)^{-1}dz=O((\ln t)^{-1}),

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{t_{1}}{t}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-z)^{-1}z^{-p_{0}}(\ln(tz))^{-2}dz\sim\int_{\frac{t_{1}}{t}}^{\frac{1}{2}}z^{-p_{0}}(\ln(tz))^{-2}dz=t^{p_{0}-1}\int_{t_{1}}^{\frac{t}{2}}a^{-p_{0}}(\ln a)^{-2}da=O((\ln t)^{-2}).

\bullet
For
1<\gamma<2
, in order to balance out

c_{1}t^{-p_{0}}(\ln t)^{-1}+c_{1}(1-p_{0})(2p_{0}-1)t^{-p_{0}}+O\left(t^{-p_{0}}(\ln t)^{-1}\ln(\ln t)\right)\approx 2C_{\gamma}v_{\gamma}(t),

we take

p_{0}=\frac{\gamma}{2},\quad c_{1}=(1-\frac{\gamma}{2})^{-1}(\gamma-1)^{-1}2C_{\gamma}.

This then implies

\mu_{0}(t)=(1-\frac{\gamma}{2})^{-1}(\gamma-1)^{-1}2C_{\gamma}t^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\ln t)^{-1},

and

-\Big(\mu_{0}t^{-1}+\int_{t/2}^{t-\mu_{0}^{2}}\frac{\dot{\mu}_{0}(s)}{t-s}ds\Big)+2v_{\gamma}(t)(C_{\gamma}+g_{\gamma}(t))=O\left(t^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\ln t)^{-1}\ln\ln t\right)=O\left(\ln\ln t|\dot{\mu}_{0}|\right).

\bullet
For
\gamma=2
, in order to balance out

we choose

\mu_{0}=2C_{\gamma}+(\ln t)^{-1}.

Then

-\Big(\mu_{0}t^{-1}+\int_{t/2}^{t-\mu_{0}^{2}}\frac{\dot{\mu}_{0}(s)}{t-s}ds\Big)+2v_{\gamma}(t)(C_{\gamma}+g_{\gamma}(t))=O\left(t^{-1}(\ln t)^{-2}\ln\ln t\right)=O\left(\ln\ln t|\dot{\mu}_{0}|\right).

\bullet
For

[
37

, a good approximation is

\mu_{0}=(\ln t)^{-1}

and

-\Big(\mu_{0}t^{-1}+\int_{t/2}^{t-\mu_{0}^{2}}\frac{\dot{\mu}_{0}(s)}{t-s}ds\Big)+2v_{\gamma}(t)(C_{\gamma}+g_{\gamma}(t))=O\left(t^{-1}(\ln t)^{-2}\ln\ln t\right)=O\left(\ln\ln t|\dot{\mu}_{0}|\right).

In conclusion, the non-local problem (

\mu_{0}(t)=\begin{cases}(1-\frac{\gamma}{2})^{-1}(\gamma-1)^{-1}2C_{\gamma}t^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\ln t)^{-1},&1<\gamma<2\\
2C_{\gamma}+(\ln t)^{-1},&\gamma=2\\
(\ln t)^{-1},&\gamma>2,\end{cases}

where the constant

By the same argument in

37

to get

(B.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\partial}_{\tau}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}+Q_{R}(\phi,\phi)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}f_{1}\phi^{2}\rho d\rho-\int_{0}^{R}\rho f_{2}\phi^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{2}\phi^{2}{\partial}_{\rho}f_{2}+\int_{0}^{R}h\phi\rho d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{5}{2}C_{f}\tau^{-d}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}\|h\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}.

By a coercive estimate in
[
36
, Lemma 9.2]

Q_{R}(\phi,\phi)\gtrsim\frac{1}{R^{2}\ln R}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2},

one has

{\partial}_{\tau}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}+\frac{1}{R^{2}\ln R}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}\lesssim R^{2}\ln R\|h\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}

provided

\tau^{d}\gg R^{2}\ln R.

Then Grönwall’s inequality yields

\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}\lesssim R^{2}\ln R\|h\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}\lesssim R^{2}\ln Rv(\tau)\|h\|_{v,\ell}

To get the pointwise control, we introduce the energy norm

\|f\|_{X(B_{R})}^{2}\int_{0}^{R}\left(({\partial}_{\rho}f)^{2}+\frac{f^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\right)\rho d\rho,

and the following embedding holds (cf.
[
13
, page 216]
)

(B.8)

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\leq\|f\|_{X(B_{R})}^{2}.

Integrating both sides of (
B.7
) implies

\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}Q_{R}(\phi,\phi)\lesssim R^{4}(\ln R)^{2}v^{2}(\tau)\|h\|^{2}_{v,\ell},

and thus

(B.9)

Q_{R}(\phi,\phi)(\tilde{\tau})\lesssim R^{4}(\ln R)^{2}v^{2}(\tau)\|h\|^{2}_{v,\ell}

for some
\tilde{\tau}\in(\tau,\tau+1)
. Next we multiply equation (
B.6
) by
\rho\mathcal{L}\phi
and integrate by parts

-\frac{1}{2}{\partial}_{\tau}Q_{R}(\phi,\phi)\lesssim\|\mathcal{L}\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{R})}+\tau^{-2d}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{R}h\mathcal{L}\phi\rho d\rho.

Then using Young’s inequality we get

{\partial}_{\tau}Q_{R}(\phi,\phi)\lesssim v^{2}(\tau)\|h\|^{2}_{v,\ell}

since
\tau^{d}\gg R^{2}\ln R
. By above inequality and (
B.9
), we obtain

Q_{R}(\phi,\phi)(\tau+1)\lesssim R^{4}(\ln R)^{2}v^{2}(\tau)\|h\|^{2}_{v,\ell}.

By the arbitrariness of
\tau
here and the initial condition
\phi(\cdot,\tau_{0})=0
as well as the embedding (
B.8
), we have

(B.10)

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{X(B_{R})}\lesssim[Q_{R}(\phi,\phi)(\tau)]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}\lesssim R^{2}\ln Rv(\tau)\|h\|_{v,\ell}.

Now we upgrade above pointwise control to estimate with spatial decay. We write equation (
B.6
) as

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{\tau}\phi=\left({\partial}_{\rho\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}{\partial}_{\rho}\phi-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\phi\right)+\tilde{h}\mbox{ \ in \ }\mathcal{D}_{R},\\
\phi=0\mbox{ \ on \ }{\partial}\mathcal{D}_{R},\quad\phi(\cdot,\tau_{0})=0\mbox{ \ in \ }[0,R(\tau_{0})],\end{cases}

where

\tilde{h}:=\frac{8\phi}{(\rho^{2}+1)^{2}}+f_{1}\phi+f_{2}\rho{\partial}_{\rho}\phi+h(\rho,\tau).

So we have

\displaystyle|\phi|\lesssim

\displaystyle~\rho\left|\Gamma_{4}\bullet(\rho^{-1}|\tilde{h}|\mathbf{1}_{\rho\leq R(\tau)})\right|\lesssim R^{2}\ln Rv(\tau)\langle\rho\rangle^{-1}\|h\|_{v,\ell}.

where
\Gamma_{4}
is the heat kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, and we have used the fact
\tau^{d}\gg R^{2}\ln R
and the convolution estimates in
[
36
, Lemma A.2]
. The proof of (
B.4
) is complete.

Next, we handle the case with orthogonality condition. We first consider an elliptic problem

\mathcal{L}H=h.

By expressing

H(\rho,\tau)=\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}(\rho)\int_{0}^{\rho}h(s,\tau)\mathcal{Z}(s)sds-\mathcal{Z}(\rho)\int_{0}^{\rho}h(s,\tau)\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}(s)sds

and using orthogonality (
B.5
), we get

(B.11)

\|H\|_{v,\ell-2}\lesssim\|h\|_{v,\ell}

since
1<\ell<3
. We now consider

(B.12)

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{\tau}\Phi=\mathcal{L}\Phi+H(\rho,\tau)\mbox{ \ in \ }\mathcal{D}_{2R},\\
\Phi=0\mbox{ \ on \ }{\partial}\mathcal{D}_{2R},\quad\Phi(\cdot,\tau_{0})=0\mbox{ \ in \ }[0,2R(\tau_{0})].\end{cases}

Similar to above process of getting non-orthogonal linear theory, we have

|\Phi(\rho,\tau)|\lesssim v(\tau)R^{5-\ell}\ln R\langle\rho\rangle^{-1}\|H\|_{v,\ell-2}.

Above pointwise estimate together with a scaling argument yield

|\Phi|+\langle\rho\rangle|{\partial}_{\rho}\Phi|+\langle\rho\rangle^{2}|{\partial}_{\rho\rho}\Phi|\lesssim v(\tau)R^{5-\ell}\ln R\langle\rho\rangle^{-1}\|H\|_{v,\ell-2}.

So we have

{\partial}_{\tau}(\mathcal{L}\Phi)=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{L}\Phi)+h

with

|\mathcal{L}\Phi|+\langle\rho\rangle|{\partial}_{\rho}(\mathcal{L}\Phi)|\lesssim v(\tau)R^{5-\ell}\ln R\langle\rho\rangle^{-3}\|H\|_{v,\ell-2}.

We want to find a desired solution
\phi
and consider the remainder

\tilde{\phi}=\phi-\mathcal{L}\Phi

which solves

{\partial}_{\tau}\tilde{\phi}=\mathcal{L}\tilde{\phi}+f_{1}\tilde{\phi}+f_{2}\rho{\partial}_{\rho}\tilde{\phi}+f_{1}\mathcal{L}\Phi+f_{2}\rho{\partial}_{\rho}(\mathcal{L}\Phi).

By above non-orthogonal linear theory, we have the following control for
\tilde{\phi}

|\tilde{\phi}|\lesssim\tau^{-d}v(\tau)R^{7-\ell}(\ln R)^{2}\langle\rho\rangle^{-1}\|h\|_{v,\ell},

and thus

|\phi|\lesssim v(\tau)\|h\|_{v,\ell}\Big[R^{5-\ell}\ln R\langle\rho\rangle^{-3}+\tau^{-d}v(\tau)R^{7-\ell}(\ln R)^{2}\langle\rho\rangle^{-1}\Big]

as desired.
∎

Next we perform another re-gluing procedure to further improve the linear theory with orthogonality. We have

Proposition B.2
.

Consider

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{\tau}\phi=\mathcal{L}\phi+f_{1}\phi+f_{2}\rho{\partial}_{\rho}\phi+h(\rho,\tau)+c(\tau)\eta(\rho)\mathcal{Z}(\rho)&\mbox{ in }\mathcal{D}_{R},\\
\phi(\rho,\tau_{0})=0&\mbox{ in }[0,R(\tau_{0})],\end{cases}

where
\|h\|_{v,\ell}<\infty
with
1<\ell<3
. Assume
\tau^{d}\gg\max\{R^{2},~R_{0}^{6}\}
,
R_{0}=c_{1}\tau^{\delta}
for some
\delta\geq 0
and
c_{1}>0
, then for
\tau_{0}
sufficiently large, there exists
(\phi,c(\tau))
solving above equation, and
(\phi,c)=(\mathcal{T}_{3i}[h],c[h])
defines a linear mapping of
h
with the estimates

\langle\rho\rangle|{\partial}_{\rho}\phi|+|\phi|\lesssim R_{0}^{6-\ell}\ln R_{0}v(\tau)\langle\rho\rangle^{-a}\|h\|_{v,\ell},\quad a<\ell-2,

\displaystyle c[h](\tau)=

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{0}^{2}\eta(\rho)\mathcal{Z}^{2}(\rho)\rho d\rho\right)^{-1}\left(\int_{0}^{2R_{0}}h(\rho,\tau)\mathcal{Z}(\rho)\rho d\rho+R_{0}^{-\epsilon_{0}}O(v\|h\|_{v,\ell})\right)

for some
\epsilon_{0}>0
, and
O(v\|h\|_{v,\ell})
depends linearly on
h
.

Proof.

We decompose

\phi(\rho,\tau)=\eta_{R_{0}}\phi_{i}(\rho,\tau)+\phi_{o}(\rho,\tau),

where
\eta_{R_{0}}=\eta(\frac{\rho}{R_{0}})
. In order to find a solution
\phi
, it suffices to find
(\phi_{i},\phi_{o})
such that

(B.13)

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{\tau}\phi_{o}={\partial}_{\rho\rho}\phi_{o}+\frac{1}{\rho}{\partial}_{\rho}\phi_{o}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\phi_{o}+J[\phi_{o},\phi_{i}]\mbox{ \ in \ }{\mathcal{D}}_{R},\\
\phi_{o}=0\mbox{ \ on \ }{\partial}{\mathcal{D}}_{R},\quad\phi_{o}=0\mbox{ \ in \ }[0,R(\tau_{0})],\end{cases}

(B.14)

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{\tau}\phi_{i}=\mathcal{L}\phi_{i}+f_{1}\phi_{i}+f_{2}\rho{\partial}_{\rho}\phi_{i}+V\phi_{o}+h+c(\tau)\eta(\rho)\mathcal{Z}(\rho)&\mbox{ \ in \ }{\mathcal{D}}_{2R_{0}},\\
\phi_{i}=0&\mbox{ \ in \ }B_{2R(\tau_{0})},\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle J[\phi_{o},\phi_{i}]=

\displaystyle f_{1}\phi_{o}+f_{2}\rho{\partial}_{\rho}\phi_{o}+(1-\eta_{R_{0}})V\phi_{o}+A[\phi_{i}]+h(1-\eta_{R_{0}}),

\displaystyle A[\phi_{i}]=

\displaystyle\phi_{i}({\partial}_{\rho\rho}\eta_{R_{0}}+\frac{1}{\rho}{\partial}_{\rho}\eta_{R_{0}})+2{\partial}_{\rho}\eta_{R_{0}}{\partial}_{\rho}\phi_{i}+f_{2}\rho{\partial}_{\rho}\eta_{R_{0}}\phi_{i}-{\partial}_{\tau}\eta_{R_{0}}\phi_{i},

and

c(\tau)=c[\phi_{o}](\tau)=C\int_{0}^{2R_{0}}[V(\rho)\phi_{o}(\rho,\tau)+h(\rho,\tau)]\mathcal{Z}(\rho)\rho d\rho,\quad C=-(\int_{B_{2}}\eta(\rho)\mathcal{Z}^{2}(\rho)\rho d\rho)^{-1}.

We reformulate (
B.13
) and (
B.14
) into the following operators

(B.15)

\displaystyle\phi_{o}(y,\tau)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{o}[J[\phi_{o},\phi_{i}]],\quad\phi_{i}(y,\tau)=\mathcal{T}_{2i}\left[V\phi_{o}+h+c(\tau)\eta(\rho)\mathcal{Z}(\rho)\right],

where
\mathcal{T}_{o}
is a linear mapping given by the standard parabolic theory, and
\mathcal{T}_{2i}
is given by Proposition
B.1
.
We now solve the system (
B.15
) by the contraction mapping theorem.
The leading part of the RHS in (
B.14
) is

H_{1}:=h+C\eta(\rho)\mathcal{Z}(\rho)\int_{0}^{2R_{0}}h(\rho,\tau)\mathcal{Z}(\rho)\rho d\rho.

Clearly,
\|H_{1}\|_{v,\ell}\lesssim\|h\|_{v,\ell}
. If
H_{1}
satisfies the orthogonality condition in
\mathcal{D}_{2R_{0}}
, then Proposition
B.1
gives the a priori estimate

\langle\rho\rangle|{\partial}_{\rho}\mathcal{T}_{2i}[H_{1}]|+|\mathcal{T}_{2i}[H_{1}]|\leq D_{i}w_{i}(\rho,\tau)

provided
\tau^{d}\gg R_{0}^{6}
,
where
D_{i}\geq 1
is a constant and

w_{i}(\rho,\tau)=v(\tau)\|h\|_{v,\ell}\left(R_{0}^{5-\ell}\ln R_{0}\langle\rho\rangle^{-3}+v(\tau)R_{0}^{1-\ell}(\ln R_{0})^{2}\langle\rho\rangle^{-1}\right)

So we will choose the space for the inner solution as

\mathcal{B}_{i}=\left\{g(\rho,\tau)\ :\ \langle\rho\rangle|{\partial}_{\rho}g(\rho,\tau)|+|g(\rho,\tau)|\leq 2D_{i}w_{i}(\rho,\tau)\right\}.

For any
\tilde{\phi}_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
, we will find a solution
\phi_{o}=\phi_{o}[\tilde{\phi}_{i}]
of (
B.13
) by the fixed point argument. Let us estimate
J[0,\tilde{\phi}_{i}]
term by term

|A[\tilde{\phi}_{i}]|\lesssim D_{i}vR_{0}^{-\epsilon_{0}}\langle y\rangle^{-\ell_{1}}\|h\|_{v,\ell}

for some
\epsilon_{0}>0
and
\ell_{1}<\ell
. Also we have

|h(1-\eta_{R_{0}})|\lesssim vR_{0}^{-\epsilon_{0}}\langle y\rangle^{-\ell_{1}}\|h\|_{v,\ell}.

Consider (
B.13
) with the right hand side
J[0,\tilde{\phi}_{i}]
. Using
Cv\rho(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{4}})^{-1}[\langle\rho\rangle^{-\ell_{1}-1}]R_{0}^{-\epsilon_{0}}\|h\|_{v,\ell}
as the barrier function with a large constant
C
and then scaling argument, we have

\langle\rho\rangle|{\partial}_{\rho}\mathcal{T}_{o}[J[0,\tilde{\phi}_{i}]](\rho,\tau)|+|\mathcal{T}_{o}[J[0,\tilde{\phi}_{i}]](\rho,\tau)|\leq w_{o}(\rho,\tau)=D_{o}D_{i}vR_{0}^{-\epsilon_{0}}\langle\rho\rangle^{2-\ell_{1}}\|h\|_{v,\ell}

with a large constant
D_{o}\geq 1
.
This suggests that we solve
\phi_{o}
in the following space:

\mathcal{B}_{o}=\left\{f(\rho,\tau)\ :\ \langle\rho\rangle|{\partial}_{\rho}f(\rho,\tau)|+|f(\rho,\tau)|\leq 2w_{o}(\rho,\tau)\right\}.

For any
\tilde{\phi}_{o}\in\mathcal{B}_{o}
, since
\rho\leq 2R(\tau)
, we have

|V\tilde{\phi}_{o}(1-\eta_{R_{0}})|\lesssim R_{0}^{-2}D_{o}D_{i}vR_{0}^{-\epsilon_{0}}\langle\rho\rangle^{-\ell_{1}}\|h\|_{v,\ell},

|f_{1}\phi_{o}+f_{2}\rho{\partial}_{\rho}\phi_{o}|\lesssim\tau^{-d}R^{2}(\tau)D_{o}D_{i}vR_{0}^{-\epsilon_{0}}\langle\rho\rangle^{-\ell_{1}}\|h\|_{v,\ell}.

Since
\tau^{-d}R^{2}
,
R_{0}^{-2}\ll 1
, by comparison principle, we have

\mathcal{T}_{o}[J[\tilde{\phi}_{o},\tilde{\phi}_{i}]]\in\mathcal{B}_{o},

and the mapping is a contraction.

Now we have found a solution
\phi_{o}=\phi_{o}[\tilde{\phi}_{i}]\in\mathcal{B}_{o}
. It follows that

\left\|V\phi_{o}[\tilde{\phi}_{i}]+C\left[\int_{0}^{2R_{0}}V(\rho)\phi_{o}[\tilde{\phi}_{i}](\rho,\tau)\mathcal{Z}(\rho)\rho d\rho\right]\eta(\rho)\mathcal{Z}(\rho)\right\|_{v,\ell}\lesssim D_{o}D_{i}R_{0}^{-\epsilon_{0}}\|h\|_{v,\ell}.

Thanks to the choice of
c(\tau)
,
H_{2}:=V(\rho)\phi_{o}[\tilde{\phi}_{i}]+h+c[\phi_{o}[\tilde{\phi}_{i}]](\tau)\eta(\rho)\mathcal{Z}(\rho)
satisfies the orthogonality condition in
{\mathcal{D}}_{2R_{0}}
.
By Proposition
B.1
, we get

\mathcal{T}_{2i}[h_{2}]\in\mathcal{B}_{i}

since
R_{0}^{-\epsilon_{0}}\ll 1
, and similarly it is a contraction mapping. Thus we find a solution

(B.16)

\phi_{i}=\phi_{i}[h]\in\mathcal{B}_{i},

and we obtain a solution

(\phi_{o},\phi_{i})
for (
B.13
) and (
B.14
) in the chosen spaces.

Since
\phi_{o}[h]\in\mathcal{B}_{o}
, one has

c[h](\tau)=C\int_{0}^{2R_{0}}h(\rho,\tau)\mathcal{Z}(\rho)\rho d\rho+R_{0}^{-\epsilon_{0}}O(v)\|h\|_{v,\ell}.

We also have

|J[0,\phi_{i}]|\lesssim R_{0}v\langle\rho\rangle^{-\ell}\|h\|_{v,\ell}.

Using comparison principle to (
B.13
) repeatedly, we have a refined bound

(B.17)

|\phi_{o}|\lesssim R_{0}v\langle\rho\rangle^{2-\ell}\|h\|_{v,\ell}.

Combining (
B.16
), (
B.17
) and then using scaling argument, we conclude

\langle\rho\rangle|{\partial}_{\rho}\phi|+|\phi|\lesssim R_{0}^{6-\ell}\ln R_{0}v\langle\rho\rangle^{-a}\|h\|_{v,\ell}

with
a<\ell-2
.
∎

Strichartz estimates for equations with structured Lipschitz coefficients
Dorothee Frey and Robert Schippa

Abstract
. Sharp Strichartz estimates are proved for Schrödinger and wave equations with Lipschitz coefficients satisfying additional structural assumptions. We use Phillips functional calculus as a substitute for Fourier inversion, which shows how dispersive properties are inherited from the constant coefficient case. Global Strichartz estimates follow provided that the derivatives of the coefficients are integrable. The estimates extend to structured coefficients of bounded variations. As applications we derive Strichartz estimates with additional derivative loss for wave equations with Hölder-continuous coefficients and solve nonlinear Schrödinger equations. Finally, we record spectral multiplier estimates, which follow from the Strichartz estimates by well-known means.

1.
Introduction and Main results

In the following we show Strichartz estimates for Schrödinger and (half-)wave equations with time-independent Lipschitz coefficients under additional structural assumptions. Let
d\geq 1
, and
a_{i}\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy an ellipticity condition for
i=1,\ldots,2d
:

(1)

\exists\lambda,\Lambda>0:\,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}:\;\lambda\leq a_{i}(x)\leq\Lambda,\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,2d\}.

We consider the Dirac operators

iD_{a_{j}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-ia_{j+d}(x_{j})\partial_{j}(\cdot)\\
ia_{j}(x_{j})\partial_{j}&0\end{pmatrix}.

We further write

L=D_{a}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}D_{a_{j}}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j+d}(x_{j})\partial_{j}(a_{j}(x_{j})\partial_{j})&0\\
0&-\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}(x_{j})\partial_{j}(a_{j+d}(x_{j})\partial_{j})\end{pmatrix},

|D_{L}|=L^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and
|D|=|\nabla|
. We consider the homogeneous Schrödinger

(2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+Lu&=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{2})\end{array}\right.

and half-wave equation

(3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+L^{\frac{1}{2}}u&=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{2}).\end{array}\right.

The homogeneous Strichartz estimates quantify dispersive effects by estimates

(4)

\|u\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim_{T,d,p,q,s}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for solutions to (
2
) or (
3
). In the constant-coefficient case, i.e.,
a_{i}=1
for
i=1,\ldots,2d
global Strichartz estimates

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim_{d,p,q}\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

hold true with
s
determined by scaling, see below.
Our first result are local-in-time Strichartz estimates for
L
and
L^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
2\leq p\leq\infty
,
2\leq q<\infty
,
s_{\ell}=d\big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\big)-\frac{\ell}{p}
,
\ell\in\{1,2\}
. Suppose that
a_{i}\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})
,
i=1,\ldots,2d
satisfy (
1
). Then, we find the half-wave Strichartz estimate to hold

(5)

\||D|^{-s_{1}}e^{itL^{\frac{1}{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

with
\mu=T\max_{i}\|a_{i}\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}\geq 1
provided that
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{d-1}{q}=\frac{d-1}{2}
.

Furthermore, the Schrödinger Strichartz estimate holds true

(6)

\||D|^{-s_{2}}e^{itL}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

with
\mu=T\max_{i}\|a_{i}\|^{2}_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}\geq 1
provided that
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{d}{q}=\frac{d}{2}
.

Remark 1.2
.

For
q=\infty
, the above estimates remain true after changing to Besov norms
\dot{B}^{-s_{\ell},q}_{2}
, except at the double endpoints
(p,q,d)\neq(2,\infty,2)
for the Schrödinger equation or
(p,q,d)\neq(2,\infty,3)
for the wave equation.

If the coefficients have integrable derivatives with small
L^{1}
-norm, we can show global estimates:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
2\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
s_{\ell}=d\big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\big)-\frac{\ell}{p}
,
\ell\in\{1,2\}
, and
a_{i}\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})
,
i=1,\ldots,d
satisfy (
1
). Let
a_{i}=1
for
i=d+1,\ldots,2d
. Suppose that
Var(\log((a_{i}))<2\pi
. Then, we find the following estimate to hold

(7)

\||D|^{-s_{1}}e^{itL^{\frac{1}{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

provided that
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{d-1}{q}=\frac{d-1}{2}
and
(p,q,d)\neq(2,\infty,3)
.

Furthermore, the Schrödinger Strichartz estimate holds true

(8)

\||D|^{-s_{2}}e^{itL}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\frac{2}{p}+\frac{d}{q}=\frac{d}{2}

(p,q,d)\neq(2,\infty,2)

Recall that
p\geq 2
and

are necessary due to convolution structure. The Knapp counterexample for constant coefficients gives the necessary conditions for the integrability indices. For the Schrödinger equation, this reads

(9)

\frac{2}{p}+\frac{d}{q}\leq\frac{d}{2},

and for the half-wave equation, this is

(10)

\frac{2}{p}+\frac{d-1}{q}\leq\frac{d-1}{2}.

Estimates (

.
The double endpoints
(p,q)=(2,\infty)
in two dimensions for the Schrödinger equation and three dimensions for the wave equation are ruled out by more sophisticated counterexamples due to Montgomery–Smith
[
29
]
and E. Stein (cf.
[

Clearly, on a finite time interval we can use Hölder in time and Bernstein’s inequality to estimate low frequencies. Hence, on a finite time interval we can as well consider inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces. The estimates are named after Strichartz’s pioneering work
[
38
]
on constant coefficients, where the relation with
L^{2}
-Fourier restriction was established (cf.
[
43
]
). Ginibre–Velo
[
17
]
covered a wider range of integrability indices, and finally Keel–Tao
[
23
]
covered the time-integrability
p=2

It was clarified in
[
23
]
that Strichartz estimates follow from a dispersive estimate and an energy estimate, see Theorem
3.1
. In the constant-coefficient case the required dispersive estimate reads as

\|P_{1}e^{it(-\Delta)^{k/2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-\sigma(k)}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad(t\neq 0),

where
P_{1}
denotes a smooth frequency projection to unit frequencies,
\sigma(k)
denotes a decay parameter, and the energy estimate is given by

\|P_{1}e^{it(-\Delta)^{k/2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

By the interpolation arguments due to Keel–Tao
[
23
]
these yield Strichartz estimates for unit frequencies. The claim follows by rescaling for any dyadic frequency range and the dyadic frequency pieces are assembled by Littlewood-Paley theory.

We follow this strategy also in the current setup of Lipschitz coefficients as square function estimates and scaling symmetries are still available. The key step remains the proof of the dispersive estimate at unit frequencies. In the constant-coefficient case the crucial kernel estimate is a consequence of stationary phase estimates. Here we can use Phillips functional calculus as substitute for Fourier inversion.

Preliminaries

We recall the basic setup of
[
16
]
with an emphasis on the Phillips functional calculus. We also collect basic resolvent estimates and the square function estimate.
Recall that for
i\in\{1,\ldots,2d\}
,
a_{i}\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})
, which satisfy the ellipticity condition

(15)

\exists\lambda,\Lambda>0:\,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}:\;\lambda\leq a_{i}(x)\leq\Lambda.

The ellipticity constants will be fixed for the rest of the paper.

Definition 2.1
.

For
j\in\{1,\ldots,2d\}
, let
a_{j}\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy (
15
). For
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define

\begin{split}\xi.D_{a}&=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\xi_{j}\begin{pmatrix}0&-a_{j+d}(x_{j})\partial_{j}(\cdot)\\
a_{j}(x_{j})\partial_{j}&0\end{pmatrix},\\
\xi.\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}}&=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\xi_{j}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{-a_{j+d}(x_{j})\partial_{j}(a_{j}(x_{j})\partial_{j})}&0\\
0&\sqrt{-a_{j}(x_{j})\partial_{j}(a_{j+d}(x_{j})\partial_{j})}\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

which we view as unbounded operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{2})
with domain
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

The Dirac operators
D_{a_{i}}
are not commuting. The remedy is to use the cosine group, which is as well generated by
\sqrt{D_{a_{i}}^{2}}
, and the operators
\sqrt{D_{a_{i}}^{2}}
and
\sqrt{D_{a_{j}}^{2}}
are commuting. Recall the following about the generator of the transport group:

Proposition 2.2

(
[
16
, Proposition 3.1]
)
.

The operator
a\frac{d}{dx}
generates a bounded
C_{0}
-group in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
. Moreover,
\phi(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{a(y)}dy
is a global

-diffeomorphism, and we have

(e^{ta\partial_{x}}f)(x)=f(\chi(t,x))\text{ for }f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})

with
\chi(t,x)=\phi^{-1}(t+\phi(x))
,
\chi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
.

We can now prove the following basic property of the Dirac operator:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
a
,
b\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy (
15
). Then, the operators

A:W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2}),\quad A=\begin{pmatrix}0&b\partial_{x}(\cdot)\\
a\partial_{x}&0\end{pmatrix},\;i\tilde{D}_{a}=i\begin{pmatrix}0&-b\partial_{x}(\cdot)\\

with the same domain generate
C_{0}
-groups in

.

Proof.

It will be enough to show the claim for
A
as we shall see that the proof extends to
i\tilde{D}_{a}
. For the proof we diagonalize
A
up to an
L^{p}
bounded operator. The principal symbol is given by

i\xi\begin{pmatrix}0&b(x)\\
a(x)&0\end{pmatrix}.

The eigenvalues are given by
\pm i\xi(ab)^{1/2}(x)
, and it suffices to note a matrix
M
of eigenvectors is given by

M=\begin{pmatrix}b^{1/2}&b^{1/2}\\
a^{1/2}&-a^{1/2}\end{pmatrix},\quad M^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}b^{-1/2}&a^{-1/2}\\
b^{-1/2}&-a^{-1/2}\end{pmatrix}.

With this it is straightforward that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}b^{-1/2}(x)&a^{-1/2}(x)\\
b^{-1/2}(x)&-a^{-1/2}(x)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&\partial_{x}(b(x)\cdot)\\
a(x)\partial_{x}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}b^{1/2}(x)&b^{1/2}(x)\\
a^{1/2}(x)&-a^{1/2}(x)\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}(ab)^{1/2}(x)\partial_{x}&0\\
0&-(ab)^{1/2}(x)\partial_{x}\end{pmatrix}+E(x),\end{split}

where

\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&b\partial_{x}(\cdot)\\

u_{2}\end{pmatrix}

v_{2}\end{pmatrix}

with

\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}b^{-1/2}&a^{-1/2}\\
b^{-1/2}&-a^{-1/2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad\|E\|_{L^{p}\to L^{p}}\leq C(\lambda,\Lambda)\max_{c=a,b}(\|c\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}).

with

-transport group generated by

.

for

We find a representation in terms of solutions to wave equations by squaring the transport group. Let

\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}(t)=e^{itD_{a}}\begin{pmatrix}u_{0}\\
v_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

By taking two time derivatives, we find

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}^{2}u&=b\partial_{x}(a\partial_{x})u,\\
\partial_{t}^{2}v&=a\partial_{x}(b\partial_{x}v).\end{array}\right.

Let
L_{1}=-b\partial_{x}(a\partial_{x})
,
L_{2}=-a\partial_{x}(b\partial_{x})
. Then we can write the solution as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}u(t)&=\cos(tL_{1})u_{0}-i\frac{\sin(tL_{1})}{L_{1}}b\partial_{x}v_{0},\\
v(t)&=\cos(tL_{2})v_{0}+i\frac{\sin(tL_{2})}{L_{2}}a\partial_{x}u_{0},\end{array}\right.

or, concisely,

e^{itD_{a}}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(tL_{1})&-i\frac{\sin(tL_{1})}{L_{1}}(b\partial_{x})\\
i\frac{\sin(tL_{2})}{L_{2}}a\partial_{x}&\cos(tL_{2})\end{pmatrix}.

A similar computation yields for

\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}(t)=e^{it\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}u_{0}\\
v_{0}\end{pmatrix},

the representation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}u(t)&=\cos(tL_{1})u_{0}+i\sin(tL_{1})u_{0},\\
v(t)&=\cos(tL_{2})v_{0}+i\sin(tL_{2})v_{0},\end{array}\right.

or, concisely,

\begin{pmatrix}u(t)\\
v(t)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(tL_{1})+i\sin(tL_{1})&0\\
0&\cos(tL_{2})+i\sin(tL_{2})\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u_{0}\\
v_{0}\end{pmatrix}=e^{it\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}u_{0}\\
v_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

The Dirac operators
(D_{a})
are bisectorial operators (cf.

e^{it\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}}}+e^{-it\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}}}=e^{itD_{a}}+e^{-itD_{a}}.

This allows us to recover a Phillips functional calculus, for which we follow the argument in
[

]
:

Definition 2.4
.

For
\Psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we define
\Psi(\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}})
using Phillips functional calculus associated with the commutative group
\big(\exp(i\xi.\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}})\big)_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
:

\Psi(\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{\Psi}(\xi)\exp(i\xi.\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}})d\xi.

We consider functions
\Psi
which are even in every coordinate, i.e.,
\Psi=\Psi^{s}
with

\Psi^{s}(x)=2^{-d}\sum_{(\delta_{j})^{d}_{j=1}\in\{-1,1\}^{d}}\Psi(\delta_{1}x_{1},\ldots,\delta_{d}x_{d}).

For such functions, we have that

\begin{split}&\quad\Psi(\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}})\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\Psi}^{s}(\xi)\frac{1}{2}\big(\exp(i\xi_{1}e_{1}\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}})+\exp(-i\xi_{1}e_{1}\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}})\big)\exp(i(\xi-\xi_{1}e_{1})\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}})\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{\Psi}^{s}(\xi)\prod_{j=1}^{d}\exp(i\xi_{j}D_{a_{j}})d\xi,\end{split}

since
D_{a}
and
\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}}
generate the same cosine family.
For the sake of brevity, we write

\Psi^{s}(\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}})=\Psi^{s}(D_{a})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{\Psi}^{s}(\xi)\exp(i\xi.D_{a})d\xi.

Moreover, we have

L=D_{a}^{2}=\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j+d}(x_{j})\partial_{x_{j}}(a_{j}(x_{j})\partial_{x_{j}})&0\\
0&-\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}(x_{j})\partial_{x_{j}}(a_{j+d}(x_{j})\partial_{x_{j}})\end{pmatrix}.

As another consequence of the close relation with the transport group, the group generated by

satisfies a strong form of finite speed of propagation (cf.

Lemma 2.5

Let

Moreover, after introducing the scalar product in

\langle\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\\

we find

Proposition 2.6

It turns out that the Besov norms for structured

(a_{i})_{i=1,\ldots,d}\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

Proof.

In the present case, the heat kernel factors as a consequence of

Since the operators

Moreover,

3.
Strichartz estimates for Lipschitz coefficients

This section is devoted to the proof of Strichartz estimates for structured Lipschitz coefficients. The key ingredient in our proof are as in the constant-coefficient case dispersive estimates. Recall how dispersive estimates imply Strichartz estimates by the following abstract result due to Keel–Tao
[
23
]
:

Theorem 3.1

(Keel–Tao)
.

Let
(X,dx)
be a measure space and
H
a Hilbert space. Suppose that for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have an
operator
U(t):H\to L^{2}(X)
which satisfies the following assumptions for
\sigma>0
:

(i)

For all
t
and
f\in H
we have the energy estimate:

\|U(t)f\|_{L^{2}(X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{H}.

(ii)

For all
t\neq s
and
g\in L^{1}(X)
we have the decay estimate

\|U(s)(U(t))^{*}g\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\lesssim(1+|t-s|)^{-\sigma}\|g\|_{L^{1}(X)}.

Then, for
\sigma
-admissible exponents

(p,q)
, which satisfy
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\sigma}{q}\leq\frac{\sigma}{2}
and
(p,q,\sigma)\neq(2,\infty,1)
, the estimate

\|U(t)f\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{H}

holds. Furthermore, for two
\sigma
-admissible pairs
(p,q)
and
(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})
, the estimate

(24)

\|\int_{s<t}U(t)(U(s))^{*}F(s)ds\|_{L^{p}_{t}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}\times X)}\lesssim\|F\|_{L_{t}^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}}

holds true.

We shall see by Littlewood-Paley decomposition and rescaling that it will be enough to show estimates for unit frequencies. The energy estimate required by Theorem
3.1
(i) is Proposition
2.6
. We have to show the dispersive estimate:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
a_{i}\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy (
15
). Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

(25)

\|e^{itL^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}\psi(\sqrt{D_{a}^{2}})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-\sigma(\ell)}

for
\ell\in\{1,2\}
,
0<t\leq C(\lambda,\Lambda)\max(\|a_{i}\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}})
, and

\sigma(\ell)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\frac{d-1}{2},\quad&&\ell=1,\\
&\frac{d}{2},\quad&&\ell=2.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Proof.

By Phillips functional calculus, we have to prove

(26)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|\int K_{t}(\xi)e^{i\xi.D_{a}}u_{0}(x)d\xi\big|\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-\sigma(\ell)}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where

K_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{it|y|^{\ell}}\psi(y)e^{iy.\xi}dy

denotes the kernel of the half-wave equation for
\ell=1
or the Schrödinger equation for
\ell=2
at unit frequencies. Note that
K_{t}(\xi)=K_{t}^{s}(\xi)
and Definition
2.4
applies. Recall that

(27)

|K_{t}(\xi)|\lesssim\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &(1+|t|+|\xi|)^{-N},\quad&&|t|\not\sim|\xi|,\\
&(1+|t|)^{-\sigma(\ell)},\quad&&|t|\sim|\xi|\end{aligned}\end{cases}

by (non-)stationary phase (cf.
[
36
, Chapter 1]
). Hence, to show the dispersive estimate (
25
), it suffices to prove

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|e^{i\xi.D_{a}}f(x)\big|d\xi\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|dx.

We show this after suitable localization.

By the
L^{2}
-estimate, Phillips functional calculus, and the rapid decay of the kernel (
27
) for
|\xi|\gg|t|
, we can localize the integral in (
26
) to
|\xi|\leq T
: Let
\chi_{T}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(0,2T))
be a radially decreasing function with
\chi_{T}(\xi)=1
for
\xi\in B(0,T)
. Let
\eta_{T}=1-\chi_{T}
. We estimate by integration by parts, the
L^{2}
-estimate from Proposition
2.6
, and the resolvent estimate from Proposition
2.7
:

\begin{split}\big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta_{T}(\xi)K_{t}(\xi)e^{i\xi.D_{a}}u(x)d\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\lesssim\big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta_{T}(\xi)K_{t}(\xi)(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{2N}(1+L)^{-2N}e^{i\xi.D_{a}}u(x)d\xi\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\lesssim\big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{N}(\eta_{T}(\xi)K_{t}(\xi))e^{i\xi.D_{a}}(1+L)^{-N}u(x)d\xi\big\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\int_{|\xi|\geq T}(1+|\xi|)^{-N}d\xi\|(1+L)^{-N}u\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim(1+T)^{-M}\|u\|_{L^{1}}.\end{split}

Thus, it is enough to show the estimate

(28)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|\int_{|\xi|\leq T}K_{t}(\xi)e^{i\xi.D_{a}}u_{0}(x)d\xi\big|\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-\sigma(\ell)}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

which follows from

(29)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|\xi|\leq T}\big|e^{i\xi.D_{a}}u_{0}(x)\big|d\xi\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

By Lemma
2.5
, we can suppose that
\text{supp}(u_{0})\subseteq B(x,CT)
to show (
29
). Furthermore, by commutativity of the Dirac operators under the radial frequency constraint, we can write

(30)

\int_{B(0,T)}\big|e^{i\xi.D_{a}}u_{0}(x)d\xi\big|\leq\int_{-T}^{T}d\xi_{2}\ldots\int_{-T}^{T}d\xi_{d}\big(\int_{-T}^{T}d\xi_{1}\big|e^{i\xi_{1}.D_{a_{1}}}\big(e^{i\xi^{\prime}.D_{a}^{\prime}}u_{0}(x_{1},x^{\prime})\big)\big|\big)

with
\xi^{\prime}=(\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{d})
and
D_{a}^{\prime}=(D_{a_{2}},\ldots,D_{a_{d}})
.
It suffices to prove the estimate for a one-parameter group as the estimate for the above expression then follows by iteration. Thus, the proof will be complete once we show the following estimate:

(31)

\int_{-T}^{T}|e^{it\tilde{D}_{a}}u_{0}(x)|dt\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

for
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
\text{supp}(u_{0})\subseteq B(x,CT)
. We write
u(t,x)=e^{itD_{a}}u_{0}(x)
and with the notations from the proof of Proposition
2.3
, we find by change of basis

(32)

\int_{-T}^{T}|u(t,x)|dt\lesssim\int_{-T}^{T}|v(t,x)|dt

with

v(t,x)=T_{t}v_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}T_{t-s}Ev(s,x)ds,

where
T_{t}=\exp\text{diag}(t(ab)^{1/2}(x)\partial_{x},-t(ab)^{1/2}(x)\partial_{x})
. We can write
T_{t}v_{0}(x)=v_{0}(\chi(t,x))
with
\chi(x,\cdot)
a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|T_{t}v_{0}(x)|dt\leq C(\lambda,\Lambda)\|v_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})}.

Hence, integration in time of
v(t,x)
yields

(33)

\int_{-T}^{T}|v(t,x)|dt\leq C(\lambda,\Lambda)\|v_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2})}+\int_{-T}^{T}dt\int_{-T}^{T}ds|Ev(s,\chi(t-s,x))|.

We estimate the second term by the diffeomorphism property of
\chi(x,\cdot-s)
, Fubini’s theorem, and
L^{1}
-boundedness of
E
:

(34)

\begin{split}\int_{-T}^{T}dt\int_{-T}^{T}ds|Ev(s,\chi(x,t-s))|&\leq C(\lambda,\Lambda)\int_{-T}^{T}ds\|Ev(s)\|_{L^{1}}\\
&\leq C(\lambda,\Lambda)\int_{-T}^{T}ds\|E\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1}}\|v(s)\|_{L^{1}_{x}}\\
&\leq C(\lambda,\Lambda)\|E\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1}}\int_{-T}^{T}ds\int_{-CT}^{CT}dx^{\prime}|v(s,x+x^{\prime})|\\
&\leq C(\lambda,\Lambda)\|E\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1}}CT\sup_{x}\|v(t,x)\|_{L_{t}^{1}([-T,T])}.\end{split}

We use the bound for
\|E\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1}}
from (
16
): Let
T=\frac{1}{2\|E\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1}}\cdot C\cdot C(\lambda,\Lambda)}
such that we can absorb (
34
) into the right-hand side of (
33
) to find

\int_{-T}^{T}|v(t,x)|dt\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C})}.

Conclusively, (
31
) follows from (
32
), the previous estimate, and a final change of basis. The proof is complete.
∎

In the following we show the dispersive estimate for arbitrary finite times. We start with the following growth bound as consequence of Grønwall’s lemma:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u(t,x)=e^{itD_{a}}u_{0}
. Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

\|u(t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq e^{C(\|a_{i}\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}},\lambda,\Lambda)t}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}.

Proof.

Starting with the representation by Duhamel’s formula after change of basis

v(t,x)=T_{t}v_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}T_{t-s}Ev(s,\chi(t-s,x))ds,

we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|v(t,x)|dx&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|v_{0}(\chi(t,x))|dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(Ev)(s,\chi(t-s,x))|dxds\\
&\leq C(\lambda,\Lambda)\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\int_{0}^{t}C(\lambda,\Lambda)\|E\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1}}\|v(s)\|_{L^{1}}ds.\end{split}

By
\|E\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1}}\leq C(\lambda,\Lambda,\|a_{i}\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}})
, the proof is concluded by applying Grønwall’s lemma and inverting the change of basis.
∎

In the following we show that the dispersive estimate remains true on arbitrary time intervals, but with a bad constant.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\ell\in\{1,2\}
and
A=\max_{i}\|a_{i}\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}
. Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

\|P_{1}e^{itL^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{\infty}}\leq C_{1}e^{C_{2}TA}(1+|t|)^{-\sigma(\ell)}\quad(0<|t|\leq T).

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
3.2
by the localization and decay of the half-wave or Schrödinger kernel at unit frequencies (
27
), it suffices to prove

|\int_{|\xi|\leq T}e^{i\xi.D_{a}}u_{0}|\leq C_{1}e^{C_{2}TA}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

As above, it suffices to prove the estimate for one Dirac operator by their commutativity and iteration.
Let
u(t,x)=e^{it\tilde{D}_{a}}u_{0}(x)
. We show

\int_{-T}^{T}|u(t,x)|dt\leq C_{1}e^{C_{2}TA}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Proposition
3.2
yields
T^{\prime}(A,\lambda,\Lambda)
such that

\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}|u(t,x)|dt\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

We partition
[0,T]
into
N=\lfloor T/T^{\prime}\rfloor+1
intervals
I_{k}=[a_{k},b_{k}]
of length
T^{\prime}
such that
\int_{I^{\prime}}|u(t,x)|dt\lesssim\|u(a_{k})\|_{L^{1}}
. Hence, by Lemma

we find

\int_{0}^{T}|u(t,x)|dt\leq\sum_{k=0}^{N+1}\int_{I_{k}}|u(t,x)|dt\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{N+1}e^{kT^{\prime}C}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim e^{TC}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

∎

(

We note that Beli

Let
a_{i}\in BV_{loc}(\mathbb{R})

By the reductions from above, it is enough to prove

in earnest:

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We have the scaling symmetry

x\to\|a_{i}\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}x,\qquad t\to\|a_{i}\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}^{\ell}t,

with
\ell=1
for the half-wave equation and
\ell=2
for the Schrödinger equation, which reduces the estimate to

\||D|^{-s_{\ell}}e^{itL^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q})}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

with
\|a\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}\leq 1
. For a sharp Strichartz pair
(s,p,q,d)
we always have
0\leq s<1
. Hence, for Lipschitz coefficients the homogeneous Sobolev spaces
\dot{W}^{s,p}
and
\dot{W}^{s,p}_{L}
are equivalent for
-1<s<1
, and it suffices to estimate

\||D_{L}|^{-s_{\ell}}e^{itL^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q})}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

The estimate for the low frequencies

\begin{split}\||D_{L}|^{-s_{\ell}}e^{itL^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}\phi(|D_{L}|)u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}&\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}\||D_{L}|^{\frac{\ell}{p}}e^{itL^{\frac{1}{2}}}\phi(|D_{L}|)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\\
&\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\end{split}

follows by Hölder’s and Bernstein’s inequality and boundedness of the group on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We turn to the estimate for the high frequencies: The square function estimate (
21
) and Minkowski’s inequality, recall that
p,q\geq 2
, yield

(36)

\begin{split}\||D_{L}|^{-s_{\ell}}e^{itL^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}&\sim\|\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}||D_{L}|^{-s_{\ell}}\psi_{k}(|D_{L}|)e^{itL^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}u_{0}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\\
&\lesssim\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}2^{-2ks_{\ell}}\|\psi_{k}(|D_{L}|)e^{itL^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

Hence, it suffices to show the frequency localized estimate

(37)

\|\psi_{k}(|D_{L}|)e^{itL^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}2^{ks_{\ell}}2^{\frac{k(\ell-1)}{p}}\|\psi_{k}(|D_{L}|)u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

We use the scaling
x^{\prime}=2^{k}x
,
t^{\prime}=2^{\ell k}t
, which yields
\partial_{x}=2^{k}\partial_{x^{\prime}}
and
\frac{D_{a}}{2^{k}}=D_{\tilde{a}}
,
\tilde{a}_{i}(x)=a_{i}(2^{-k}x_{i})
. Note that

(38)

\|a_{i}(2^{-k}\cdot)\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}\lesssim 2^{-k}\|a_{i}\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}\lesssim 2^{-k}.

This reduces (
37
) to unit frequencies:

(39)

\|\psi(|D_{\tilde{L}}|)e^{it\tilde{L}^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,T2^{\ell k}],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\big(T2^{(\ell-1)k}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We note that as a consequence of (
38
) by Proposition
3.2
there is
C
independent of
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that the dispersive estimate

(40)

\|\psi(|D_{\tilde{L}}|)e^{it\tilde{L}^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-\sigma(\ell)}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

holds for
0\leq|t|\leq C2^{k}
with implicit constant independent of
k
and
t
. Thus, by the energy estimate from Proposition
2.6
, the dispersive estimate (
40
), and Theorem
3.1
, we find

(41)

\|\psi(|D_{\tilde{L}}|)e^{it\tilde{L}^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,C2^{k}],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Hence, by partitioning
[0,T2^{\ell k}]
into
O(T2^{(\ell-1)k})
intervals
(I_{m})
of length
C2^{k}
, we conclude

\begin{split}&\quad\|\psi(|D_{\tilde{L}}|)e^{it\tilde{L}^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,T2^{\ell k}],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\\
&=\big(\sum_{m}\|\psi(|D_{\tilde{L}}|)e^{it\tilde{L}^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}u_{0}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(I_{m},L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\big(\#I_{m}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

We have additionally used that
e^{it\tilde{L}^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}
conserves the
L^{2}
-norm independently of
k
.

Thus, by change of variables, we can conclude the Strichartz estimates for high frequencies of the original operators from estimates for unit frequencies of the rescaled operator:

\begin{split}\|e^{itL^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}\psi_{k}(|D_{L}|)u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q})}&\lesssim 2^{-\frac{\ell k}{p}}2^{-\frac{dk}{q}}\|e^{it\tilde{L}^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}\psi(|D_{\tilde{L}}|)\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,T2^{\ell k}],L^{q})}\\
&\lesssim(T2^{(\ell-1)k})^{\frac{1}{p}}2^{-\frac{\ell k}{p}}2^{-\frac{dk}{q}}\|\psi(|D_{\tilde{L}}|)\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim(T2^{(\ell-1)k})^{\frac{1}{p}}2^{-\frac{\ell k}{p}}2^{-\frac{dk}{q}}2^{\frac{dk}{2}}\|\psi_{k}(|D_{L}|)u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&=(T2^{(\ell-1)k})^{\frac{1}{p}}2^{ks_{\ell}}\|\psi_{k}(|D_{L}|)u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

The proof is concluded by (
36
) and square summing the above display.
∎

Next, we turn to the proof of global-in-time Strichartz estimates. The additional hypothesis on small variation allows for dispersive estimates with uniform constant for arbitrary times, from which global Strichartz estimates follow.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
a_{i}\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy (
15
) and suppose that
Var(\log(a_{i}))<2\pi
for
i=1,\ldots,d
and
a_{i}=1
for
i=d+1,\ldots,2d
. Then, we find the following estimates to hold:

(42)

\displaystyle\|e^{itL^{\frac{1}{2}}}\psi(|D_{L}|)\|_{L^{1}\to L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}},

(43)

\displaystyle\|e^{itL}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\quad(t\neq 0)

with implicit constant only depending on the ellipticity constants and
Var(\log(a_{i}a_{i+d}))
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
3.2
, by the decay of the half-wave kernel at unit frequencies, to show (
42
), it suffices that

(44)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|e^{i\xi.D_{a}}f(x)\big|d\xi\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|dx.

For the proof of (
43
), we write by Phillips functional calculus without frequency localization

e^{itL}u_{0}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t}(\xi)e^{i\xi.D_{a}}u_{0}(x)d\xi

with

G_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{iy.\xi}e^{it|y|^{2}}dy=\frac{1}{(4\pi it)^{\frac{d}{2}}}e^{it\xi^{2}}.

Thus,

|e^{itL}u_{0}(x)|\lesssim\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|G_{t}(\xi)|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|e^{i\xi.D_{a}}u_{0}(x)|d\xi\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|e^{i\xi.D_{a}}u_{0}(x)|d\xi,

and (
43
) follows likewise from (
44
).

As in the proof of Proposition
3.2
, by commutativity of the generators and iteration, it suffices to show

(45)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|e^{it\tilde{D}_{a}}u(x)\big|dt\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x)|dx

for
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
. We write
u(t,x)=e^{it\tilde{D}_{a}}u(x)
. Since we can change to the cosine group by radial frequency constraint, (
45
) is immediate from Theorem
3.5
.
∎

With global dispersive estimates at hand, we turn to the proof of Theorem

:

Proof of Theorem

.

We begin with the proof of (

for

due to the global energy and dispersive estimates from Proposition

and

For the proof of (

\begin{split}\||D|^{-s_{1}}e^{itL^{\frac{1}{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}&\sim\|\big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}||D_{L}|^{-s_{1}}\psi_{k}(|D_{L}|)e^{itL^{\frac{1}{2}}}u_{0}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\\

We rescale similarly as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
to reduce to unit frequencies. It suffices to prove that

4.
Applications

In this section we give applications of the preceding analysis. First, we show Strichartz estimates for coefficients of bounded variation by limiting arguments. Next, we prove wave Strichartz estimates for Hölder coefficients under the additional structural assumptions. In this case, they improve the general estimates due to Tataru
[
41
]
. Finally, we indicate applications to the well-posedness theory of nonlinear Schrödinger equations.

4.1.
Strichartz estimates for BV coefficients

In the following we see that by approximation arguments due to Burq–Planchon
[
9
, Proposition 1.3]
, we can drop the hypothesis that the coefficients are Lipschitz and allow for
BV_{loc}(T)
-coefficients, which norm is defined by

\|a\|_{BV_{loc}(T)}=\sup\{\int_{I}d|a^{\prime}|(y)\,:\,I\subseteq\mathbb{R}\text{ interval, }|I|=T\}

for
a^{\prime}
being a locally finite measure. The backbone is the dispersive estimate only depending on the ellipticity constants and
\|a\|_{BV_{loc}(T)}
.
We have the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(a_{i})_{i=1,\ldots,d}\subseteq L^{\infty}
satisfy (
15
) and let
T>0
such that
\|\log(a_{i})\|_{BV_{loc}(T)}<2\pi
and
L=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}(a_{i}(x_{i})\partial_{x_{i}})
. Then, we find the following estimates to hold:

\|e^{itL^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,T],\dot{B}^{-s_{\ell},q}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\ell-1}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for
\ell\in\{1,2\}
,
s_{\ell}=d\big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\big)-\frac{\ell}{p}
,
(p,q,d)
being wave
(\ell=1)
or Schrödinger
(\ell=2)
admissible.

A straight-forward modification of the proof of
[
9
, Proposition 1.3]
yields the following:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(a_{i})^{d}_{i=1}\subseteq C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
satisfy (
15
) and
\|a_{i}\|_{BV_{loc}(T)}<\infty
.

Denote
L=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}(a(x_{i})\partial_{x_{i}})
. Suppose that the
C_{0}
-group
S_{a}(t)
generated by
iL
satisfies

\|S_{a}(t)u_{0}\|_{B}\leq C(\lambda,\Lambda,\|a_{i}\|_{BV_{loc}(T)})\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

with
B
a Banach space (weakly) continuously embedded in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, whose unit ball is weakly compact. Then the same result holds for
(a_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying (
15
) and
\|a_{i}\|_{BV_{loc}(T)}<\infty
.

This yields the Schrödinger Strichartz estimates of Theorem
4.1
.
We turn to Strichartz estimates for wave equations locally-in-time with coefficients
\|a_{i}\|_{BV_{loc}(T)}<\infty
:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
(a_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\subseteq C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
satisfy (
15
). Denote
L=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}(a_{i}(x_{i})\partial_{x_{i}})
. Suppose that the
C_{0}
-group
S_{a}(t)
generated by

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
L&0\end{pmatrix}\text{ on }H^{1}\times L^{2}

satisfies

\|(S_{a}(t)(u_{0},v_{0}))_{1}\|_{B}\leq C(\lambda,\Lambda,\|a_{i}\|_{BV_{loc}(T)})\|(u_{0},\partial_{t}u_{0})\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}

with
B
a Banach space (weakly) continuously embedded in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, whose unit ball is weakly compact. Then the same result holds for
(a_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\subseteq BV_{loc}(T)
satisfying (
15
).

Proof.

Let
(a_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\subseteq BV_{loc}(T)
satisfy (
15
). Let
(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
be a family of positive mollifiers and consider
a_{i}^{\varepsilon}=\rho_{\varepsilon}*a_{i}
. We have
(a_{i}^{\varepsilon})\subseteq C^{\infty}
and
\|\partial_{x}(a_{i}^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}_{loc}(T)}\leq\|a_{i}\|_{BV_{loc}(T)}
. Hence, by assumption

\|(S_{a_{\varepsilon}}(t)(u_{0},v_{0}))_{1}\|_{B}\leq C(\lambda,\Lambda,\|a_{i}\|_{BV_{loc}(T)})\|(u_{0},v_{0})\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}

and
(S_{a_{\varepsilon}}(t)(u_{0},v_{0}))_{1}
converges weakly in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. As in
[
9
]
, it suffices to prove that
S_{a_{\varepsilon}}(t)(u_{0},v_{0})\to S_{a}(t)(u_{0},v_{0})
in
H^{1}\times L^{2}
because by energy estimates this yields convergence to
S_{a}(\cdot)(u_{0},v_{0})
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For this purpose it is enough to show the strong convergence of

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}i&1\\
L_{\varepsilon}&i\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\text{ to }\mathcal{L}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}i&1\\
L&i\end{pmatrix}^{-1}

by
[
30
, Theorem VIII.21]
. Clearly,
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}:L^{2}\times H^{-1}\to H^{1}\times L^{2}
uniformly in
\varepsilon
as well as
\mathcal{L}^{-1}:L^{2}\times H^{-1}\to H^{1}\times L^{2}
. By the resolvent formula,

\mathcal{L}^{-1}-\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}=\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{L})\mathcal{L}^{-1}.

Hence,
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
converges strongly to
\mathcal{L}^{-1}
as operators from
L^{2}\times H^{-1}
to
H^{1}\times L^{2}
. Hence,
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
converges to
\mathcal{L}^{-1}
strongly as operators from
H^{1}\times L^{2}
to
H^{1}\times L^{2}
. The proof is complete.
∎

Now we can prove the Strichartz estimates for half-wave equations stated in Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
, Half-wave Strichartz estimates.

The half-wave Strichartz estimates for Lipschitz coefficients read

\|e^{itL^{\frac{1}{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L_{T}^{p}\dot{B}^{-s_{1},q}_{2}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

These yield wave Strichartz estimates for
C^{0,1}
-coefficients:

\||D|^{1-s}(S_{a}(t)(u_{0},v_{0}))_{1}\|_{L^{p}_{T}L^{q}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

We have
(S_{a}(t)(u_{0},v_{0}))_{1}=\cos(tL)u_{0}+\frac{\sin(tL)v_{0}}{L^{\frac{1}{2}}}
. Since it is enough to consider sharp Strichartz pairs with

, we have
0<1-s<1
and have equivalence of Besov norms by Proposition
2.9
. We estimate

\begin{split}\|(S_{a}(t)(u_{0},v_{0}))_{1}\|_{L^{p}_{T}\dot{B}^{1-s,q}_{2}}&\lesssim\|\cos(tL)u_{0}\|_{L_{T}^{p}\dot{B}^{1-s,q}_{2,L}}+\|\sin(tL)v_{0}\|_{L_{T}^{p}\dot{B}^{-s,q}_{2,L}}\\

At this point, we invoke Proposition
4.3
, which yields estimates

\|(S_{a}(t)(u_{0},v_{0}))_{1}\|_{L_{T}^{p}\dot{B}^{1-s,q}_{2}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

for

-coefficients. We apply this estimate to

, which gives

\|e^{itL^{\frac{1}{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L_{T}^{p}\dot{B}^{1-s,q}_{2,L}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

The half-wave Strichartz estimate now follows from trading derivatives and the substitution
v_{0}=|D_{L}|u_{0}
.
∎

As an example for new local-in-time Strichartz estimates for Schrödinger equations with
BV_{loc}
-coefficients, we consider the Kronig–Penney model: Let
x_{0}\in(0,1)
, and
b_{0}\neq b_{1}>0
with
b_{0}x_{0}=b_{1}(1-x_{0})
. Consider the
1
-periodic function
a:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

a(x)=\begin{cases}b_{0}^{-2}\text{ for }x\in[0,x_{0}),\\
b_{1}^{-2}\text{ for }x\in[x_{0},1).\end{cases}

Banica
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
showed that the dispersive estimate

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\quad(t\neq 0)

fails for solutions

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}(a(x)\partial_{x})u&=0\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Still we find the Strichartz estimates with derivative loss

(48)

\|u\|_{L^{p}([0,1],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}))}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{p}}(\mathbb{R})}

for
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{2}
,

to hold, which indicates dispersive properties on frequency dependent time scales

\|S^{A}_{N}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|S^{A}_{N}u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

for
0<|t|\lesssim N^{-1}
with
S^{A}_{N}
denoting the spectral projection to
[N,2N)
of
-\partial_{x}(a(\cdot)\partial_{x})
. This is reminiscent of the short-time dispersive estimates on smooth compact manifolds due to Burq–Gérard–Tzvetkov
[
8
]
.

4.2.
Strichartz estimates for Hölder coefficients

To show Strichartz estimates for Hölder coefficients, we firstly derive Strichartz estimates with inhomogeneity in
L^{1}L^{2}
and with precise dependence on the Lipschitz norm and time interval. We show the following refinement of Corollary
1.4
:

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(\rho,p,q,d)
a wave admissible Strichartz pair. Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

(49)

\||D|^{-\rho}u\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q})}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\mu^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|P_{1}u\|_{L^{1}([0,T],L^{2})}

with
\mu=T\|a\|^{\ell}_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}\geq 1
and
P_{1}=i\partial_{t}+L^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

We find by Minkowski’s inequality and the homogeneous Strichartz estimate for admissible exponents

\begin{split}\||D|^{-\rho}u\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q})}&\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{T}\mu^{\frac{1}{p}}\|P_{1}u(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds\\
&\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\mu^{\frac{1}{p}}\|P_{1}u\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}.\end{split}

We shall see that we can improve the constants: Suppose that
\|a\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}\lesssim 1
(by rescaling). Divide
[0,T]
into intervals
I_{j}=[t_{j},t_{j+1}]
such that

\|P_{1}u\|_{L^{1}([t_{j},t_{j+1}],L^{2})}\leq T^{-1}\|P_{1}u\|_{L^{1}([0,T],L^{2})}

and
t_{j+1}-t_{j}\leq 1
. Then, there are roughly
T
intervals
I_{j}
and for each interval
I_{j}
, we find by the above argument

\||D|^{-\rho}u\|_{L^{p}(I_{j},L^{q})}\lesssim\|u(t_{j})\|_{L^{2}}+T^{-1}\|P_{1}u\|_{L^{1}([0,T],L^{2})}.

Taking the
\ell^{p}
-sum over intervals
I_{j}
, we find

\||D|^{-\rho}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+T^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|P_{1}u\|_{L^{1}([0,T],L^{2})}.

For arbitrary
\|a\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}
-norm, we find

\||D|^{-\rho}u\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q})}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\mu^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|P_{1}u\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}.

∎

In the following we derive Strichartz estimates for coefficients with lower regularity. The estimates are supposed to be understood as a priori estimates for smooth solutions to equations with smooth coefficients (but only depending on the rough norms). Later it becomes useful that for smooth solutions to wave equations

\partial_{t}^{2}u=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}(a_{i}(x_{i})\partial_{x_{i}}u),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

the energy

(50)

E_{a}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}(x_{i})(\partial_{x_{i}}u)^{2}

is conserved. The arguments are adapted from Tataru’s works
[
41
,
42
]
to our setting. Dealing with time-independent coefficients simplifies matters. The idea remains the same: After truncating the coefficients in frequency, we arrive at Lipschitz coefficients with large Lipschitz norm. The argument only works for operators in divergence form. Let
P=\partial_{t}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(a_{i}(x_{i})\partial_{i})
and let
(S_{N})_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}
denote inhomogeneous Littlewood-Paley projectors. We record the following consequence of Proposition
4.4
:

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
T>0
, and
a_{i}\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})
,
i=1,\ldots,d
satisfy (
15
). Suppose that

T\|a_{i}\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}\leq\mu
for some
\mu\geq 1
. Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

(51)

\||D|^{1-\rho}u\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\mu^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|Pu\|_{L^{1}([0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

provided that
(\rho,p,q,d)
is a sharp wave Strichartz pair.

We turn to the proof of Theorem
1.7
:

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

By scaling invariance, we can suppose that
T=1
and
\|a_{i}\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\infty,1}}\leq\mu
. Let
a_{i,\leq N}
denote the coefficients with Fourier transform smoothly truncated at frequencies
\frac{N}{10}
, and

P_{N}=\partial_{t}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(a_{i,\leq N}\partial_{i})
. We first argue that it is enough to show

(52)

N^{1-\rho-\frac{\sigma}{p}}\|S_{N}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\nabla S_{N}u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\mu^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\||D|^{-\sigma}P_{N}S_{N}u\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}.

Since the spatial frequencies of
P_{N}S_{N}u
are comparable to
N
, (
52
) is equivalent to

(53)

N^{1-\rho}\|S_{N}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim(N^{\sigma}\mu)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\nabla S_{N}u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+(\mu N^{\sigma})^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|P_{N}S_{N}u\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}.

After observing that
\|a_{i,\leq N}\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}\lesssim\mu N^{\sigma}
, (
53
) follows from Corollary
4.5
. To see that (
52
) implies (
14
), it suffices to see that

\||D|^{-\sigma}(S_{N}P-P_{N}S_{N})u\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}\lesssim\mu\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}.

Let
v=\nabla_{x}u
, which is compactly supported in time, and since
P
is in divergence form, it suffices to show the fixed-time estimate

\||D|^{1-\sigma}(S_{N}a_{i}-a_{i,\leq N}S_{N})v(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mu\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}

or

\|(S_{N}a_{i}-a_{i,\leq N}S_{N})v(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim N^{-s}\mu\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}.

We prove this by considering dyadic blocks
S_{K}a_{i}=a_{i,K}
. We have
K^{s}\|a_{i,K}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\mu
. For
K\gg\frac{N}{10}
, the estimate reads

\|S_{N}(a_{i,K}v(t))\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim N^{-s}K^{s}\|a_{i,K}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}},

which is immediate. For
K\lesssim\frac{N}{10}
, we have to prove

\|(S_{N}a_{i,K}-a_{i,K}S_{N})v(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim N^{-s}K^{s}\|a_{i,K}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}.

By rescaling
K\to 1
,
N\to\frac{N}{K}
, it suffices to prove

\|[S_{N},a_{i,1}]v(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim N^{-s}\|a_{i,1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}},

or, equivalently,

\|[S_{N},a_{i,1}]v(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim N^{-s}\|\nabla_{x}a_{i,1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}.

This is a well-known commutator estimate as the kernel is given by

|K(x,y)|=|k_{N}(x,y)(a_{i,1}(x)-a_{i,1}(y))|\lesssim_{M}(1+N|x-y|)^{-M}|x-y|\|\nabla_{x}a_{i,1}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Note that there is slack in the proof as the commutator estimate gives

\|[S_{N},a_{i,1}]v(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim N^{-1}\|\nabla_{x}a_{i,1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}.

Moreover, Strichartz estimates for
P
are suitable to handle lower order perturbations as
\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{i}(t,x)\partial_{i}+c(t,x)
. However, the above argument cannot deal with Schrödinger equations with rougher coefficients as the free part is estimated in
L^{2}
and not in
H^{1}
. The Duhamel integral does not gain one derivative.

To obtain more inhomogeneous estimates for the wave equation, we record the following consequence of Theorem
1.7
for solutions to

(54)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}^{2}u&=Pu,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0)&=f,\quad\partial_{t}u(0)=g.\end{array}\right.

combined with conservation of energy (
50
).

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
s\in(0,1)
,
\sigma=1-s
, and
a_{i}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
i=1,\ldots,d
satisfy (
15
), and let
(\rho,p,q,d)
be a wave Strichartz pair. Suppose that
u
is a smooth solution to (

). Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

\sup_{N}N^{1-\rho-\frac{\sigma}{p}}\|S_{N}u\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

with implicit constant depending only on
T
,
\|a_{i}\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\infty,1}}
,
(\rho,p,q,d)
, and the ellipticity constants.

As a consequence of trading derivatives, we find the following homogeneous Strichartz estimate for the half-wave equation:

(55)

\||D|^{-\rho-\frac{\sigma}{p}}e^{itL^{\frac{1}{2}}}f\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

In fact, for the sharp Strichartz estimates, it is easy to see that it suffices to trade less than one derivative. For non-sharp pairs one reduces to the sharp case first by Sobolev embedding. We prove inhomogeneous estimates by using again the taylored version of the Christ–Kiselev Lemma
3.10
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
s\in(0,1)
,
\sigma=1-s
, and
(\rho,p,q,d)
,
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{p},\tilde{q},d)
be two wave Strichartz pairs with
p>\tilde{p}^{\prime}
. Suppose that
a_{i}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies (
15
) and
u
solves

(56)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}(a(x_{i})\partial_{x_{i}}u)&=F,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
u(0)&=f,\quad\partial_{t}u(0)=g.\end{array}\right.

Then, we find the following estimate to hold for
\rho_{1}>\rho+\frac{\sigma}{p}
and
\rho_{2}>\rho+\frac{\sigma}{p}
:

(57)

\|\langle D\rangle^{1-\rho_{1}}u\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\langle D\rangle^{\rho_{2}}F\|_{L^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}([0,T],L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

with implicit constant only depending on
T
,
\|a_{i}\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}
, and the ellipticity constants.

Proof.

We write

(58)

u(t)=\cos(tL^{\frac{1}{2}})f+\frac{\sin(tL^{\frac{1}{2}})}{L^{\frac{1}{2}}}g+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-s)L^{\frac{1}{2}})}{L^{\frac{1}{2}}}F(s)ds.

The homogeneous components are estimated by (
55
). We turn to the forcing term: Consider

T:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{p}([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\quad f\mapsto\langle D\rangle^{-\rho_{1}}e^{itL^{\frac{1}{2}}}f

whose boundedness follows from the homogeneous estimates (
55
). The dual operator with respect to the
L^{2}
-scalar product is given by

T^{*}:L^{p^{\prime}}([0,T],L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad F\mapsto\int_{0}^{T}e^{-isL^{\frac{1}{2}}}\langle D\rangle^{-\rho_{1}}F(s)ds.

We consider the composition with different exponents

\begin{split}TT^{*}:L^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}([0,T],L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))&\to L^{p}([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\\
F&\mapsto\langle D\rangle^{-\rho_{1}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{i(t-s)L^{\frac{1}{2}}}(\langle D\rangle^{-\rho_{2}}F)(s)ds,\end{split}

which is also bounded. Since by assumption
\tilde{p}^{\prime}<p
, we can invoke Lemma
3.10
to find the bound

\|\langle D\rangle^{-\rho_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)L^{\frac{1}{2}}}F(s)ds\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|\langle D\rangle^{\tilde{\rho}+\frac{\sigma}{\tilde{p}}}F\|_{L^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}([0,T],L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}.

This yields the claim after trading
|D|
to
|D_{L}|
.
∎

4.3.
Applications to nonlinear equations

With the usual Strichartz estimates at disposal, it is straightforward to prove well-posedness for a large class of equations with power-type nonlinearity. Here we consider as an example Schrödinger equations with power type nonlinearity in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
:

(59)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}(a_{i}(x_{i})\partial_{x_{i}})u&=\mu|u|^{p-1}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\end{array}\right.

with
d\geq 1
and
a_{i}\in BV(\mathbb{R})
satisfying (
15
) and
Var(\log(a_{i}))<2\pi
. The results in the constant-coefficient case are due to Tsutsumi
[
44
]
. For further explanation, we refer to
[
40
, Section 3.3]
. The following holds by substituting the free Strichartz estimates for the Schrödinger equation with the estimates from Theorems
1.3
and
1.6
:

Theorem 4.8

(
L^{2}
-well-posedness)
.

Let
1<p<1+\frac{4}{d}
and
\mu\in\{-1;1\}
. Then, (
59
) is analytically globally well-posed in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the subcritical sense. If
p=1+\frac{4}{d}
, then (
59
) is analytically locally well-posed in the critical sense.

We remark that also the
H^{s}
-theory extends as long as it is valid to trade derivatives
\||D|^{s}f\|_{L^{q}}\sim\||D_{L}|^{s}f\|_{L^{q}}
.

5.
Spectral multiplier estimates and Bochner–Riesz means

In the following we consider the non-negative self-adjoint operator

(60)

L=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}(a_{i}(x_{i})\partial_{x_{i}})

in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
a_{i}\in BV(\mathbb{R})
satisfying (
15
) and
Var(\log(a_{i}))<2\pi
. We derive consequences of the dispersive properties worked out in Proposition
3.9
for spectral restriction, multiplier estimates, and Bochner-Riesz means. The results follow from the analysis of Chen
et al.

[
12
]
for
L
being a self-adjoint operator on a doubling metric space. Since
L
is self-adjoint, it admits a spectral resolution
E_{L}(\lambda)
, and for
F:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
a bounded Borel function, the operator

F(L)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(\lambda)dE_{L}(\lambda)

defines an
L^{2}
-bounded operator by the spectral theorem. A special case are the Bochner-Riesz means: We let

S^{\delta}_{R}(\lambda)=(1-\lambda/R^{2})^{\delta}_{+}

for
\delta>0
and
R>0
.
S_{R}^{\delta}(L)
is referred to as Bochner–Riesz mean of order
\delta
corresponding to
L
. In the classical case
L=-\Delta
, the Bochner–Riesz conjecture states that
S^{\delta}_{R}(L):L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is bounded provided that
\delta>\max\big(d\big|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\big|-\frac{1}{2},0\big)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
d\geq 2
. Note that for
p=2
,
\delta=0
is trivially admissible; see also
[
14
]
. The conjecture was verified for
d=2
by Carleson–Sjölin
[
10
]
and Hörmander
[
20
]
. By using the Stein–Tomas restriction theorem and finite speed of propagation, Fefferman verified the conjecture for
d\geq 3
and
\max(p,p^{\prime})\geq\frac{2d+2}{d-1}
(note the self-duality). Further progress was closely tied to work on the restriction conjecture
[
25
,
7
,
18
]
. In terms of the spectral measure
dE_{\sqrt{-\Delta}}(\lambda)
the classical Stein–Tomas restriction theorem is equivalent to

(61)

\|dE_{\sqrt{-\Delta}}(\lambda)\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}=\|\frac{\lambda^{d-1}}{(2\pi)^{d}}R_{\lambda}^{*}R_{\lambda}\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}\leq C\lambda^{d\big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\big)-1}

for all
p\in[1,(2d+2)/(d+3)]
. In the first part of Chen
et al.

[
12
]
spectral multiplier estimates, which are sharp in general, were derived based on the following Stein–Tomas restriction condition:

\big(\text{ST}\big)^{q}_{p,s}\quad\|F(\sqrt{L})\|_{p\to s}\leq CR^{d\big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{s}\big)}\|\delta_{R}F\|_{L^{q}}.

We denote dilations by
\delta_{R}F(x)=F(Rx)
. These estimates in turn can be derived from (
61
). We have the following as an instance of
[
12
, Proposition II.4]
. Note that the required smoothing estimates

(62)

\|\exp(-tL)\|_{p\to\frac{2d}{d+2}}\leq Kt^{-\frac{d}{2}\big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{d+2}{2d}\big)}

for all
t>0
and
1\leq p\leq\frac{2d}{d+2}
follow from the pointwise heat kernel estimate (
22
).

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
, and
L
be defined as in (
60
). Then, for all
1\leq p\leq\frac{2d}{d+2}
and
\lambda\geq 0
, the following estimate holds:

(63)

\|dE_{\sqrt{L}}(\lambda)\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}\leq C\lambda^{d\big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\big)-1}.

As a consequence of (
63
) we derive the Stein–Tomas restriction (cf.
[
12
, p. 267]
)

\begin{split}\|F(\sqrt{L})\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}&=\big\|\int_{0}^{R}F(\lambda)dE_{\sqrt{L}}(\lambda)\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}\leq C\int_{0}^{R}|F(\lambda)|\lambda^{d\big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\big)-1}d\lambda\\
&\leq CR^{d\big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\big)}\|\delta_{R}F\|_{1}.\end{split}

By a
TT^{*}
-argument this is
(\text{ST})^{2}_{p,2}
. This yields the following spectral multiplier estimates and endpoint estimates for Bochner–Riesz means by
[
12
, Theorem II.6]
and
[

, Theorem I.24]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let

be as in (

),

, and

such that

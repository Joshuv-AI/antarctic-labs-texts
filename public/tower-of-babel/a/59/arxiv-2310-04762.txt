Robust Low-Rank Matrix Completion via a New Sparsity-Inducing Regularizer
Zhi-Yong Wang, Hing Cheung So, Abdelhak M. Zoubir

Abstract
This paper presents a novel loss function referred to as hybrid ordinary-Welsch (HOW) and a new sparsity-inducing regularizer associated with HOW. We theoretically show that the regularizer is quasiconvex and that the corresponding Moreau envelope is convex. Moreover, the closed-form solution to its Moreau envelope, namely, the proximity operator, is derived. Compared with nonconvex regularizers like the ℓ p \ell_{p} -norm with 0 < p < 1 0<p<1 that requires iterations to find the corresponding proximity operator, the developed regularizer has a closed-form proximity operator. We apply our regularizer to the robust matrix completion problem, and develop an efficient algorithm based on the alternating direction method of multipliers. The convergence of the suggested method is analyzed and we prove that any generated accumulation point is a stationary point. Finally, experimental results based on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our algorithm is superior to the state-of-the-art methods in terms of restoration performance.

In fact, nonconvex loss functions such as Welsch and Cauchy, are widely utilized to achieve robust performance

Huber function attains robustness via dividing the data into two categories, namely, normal data and outlier-contaminated data. Here, normal data refer to observations without outliers but possibly contain Gaussian noise. The Huber function assigns equal weights for all normal data via the quadratic function, while assigning smaller weights to outlier-corrupted data using the

In this paper, a novel loss function named

-norm. The new function only changes the weights of outlier-corrupted data and the IR generated by HOW is able to make the solution sparse. To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to propose a sparsity-inducing regularizer associated with the Welsch function, and a closed-form expression of its proximity operator, which avoids iterations to finding the corresponding solution. In addition, it is proved that the generated IR is quasiconvex and its
Moreau~envelope
is convex. We apply the generated IR to the RMC problem, and develop an algorithm based on the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM).
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

(i)

We devise the HOW function, which alleviates the two limitations of Welsch function, whereby Welsch function is a special case of HOW.

(ii)

The IR generated by HOW can achieve sparseness, and the closed-form solution to its
Moreau~envelope
is derived. Besides, the properties of the IR are theoretically analyzed.

(iii)

The proposed sparsity-inducing regularizer is utilized to solve the RMC problem, and an ADMM based algorithm is suggested. All subproblems have closed-form solutions and we prove that any accumulation point is a stationary point that satisfies Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions.

(iv)

Extensive experiments are conducted to compare the proposed algorithm with competing methods using synthetic and real-life data. It is demonstrated that our approach achieves better recovery performance.

II-B
1
Low-Rank Matrix Completion

Given the observed matrix
\boldsymbol{X}_{\Omega}
, matrix completion can be written as a rank minimization problem:

\begin{split}&\mathop{\min}\limits_{\boldsymbol{M}}~\text{rank}(\boldsymbol{M}),~\text{s.t.}~\boldsymbol{M}_{\Omega}=\boldsymbol{X}_{\Omega}\end{split}

(1)

where
\boldsymbol{M}
is the recovered/estimated matrix.
However, (

[
14

, leading to

\begin{split}&\mathop{\min}\limits_{\boldsymbol{M}}~\|{\boldsymbol{M}}\|_{*},~\text{s.t.}~\boldsymbol{M}_{\Omega}=\boldsymbol{X}_{\Omega}\end{split}

(2)

\|\boldsymbol{M}\|_{*}
denotes the nuclear norm, which is the sum of singular values of
\boldsymbol{M}
. Nevertheless, nuclear norm is equal to applying the
\ell_{1}
-norm to the singular value of a matrix, which underestimates all nonzero singular values and results in a biased solution. To alleviate such an issue, WNNM is suggested
[
25

\begin{split}&\mathop{\min}\limits_{\boldsymbol{M}}~\|{\boldsymbol{M}}\|_{\boldsymbol{w},*},~\text{s.t.}~\boldsymbol{M}+\boldsymbol{S}=\boldsymbol{X},~\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega}=\boldsymbol{0}\end{split}

(3)

where
\|{\boldsymbol{M}}\|_{\boldsymbol{w},*}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\boldsymbol{w}_{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}
is the weighted nuclear norm,
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}
is the

\boldsymbol{M}
and
\boldsymbol{w}_{i}\geq 0
is a weight assigned to
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}
. However, the above algorithms are vulnerable to outliers. Then, an RMC approach based on the
\ell_{p}
-norm with
0<p<1
is developed
[
38
]
:

\begin{split}&\mathop{\min}\limits_{\boldsymbol{M}}~\|\boldsymbol{X}_{\Omega}-\boldsymbol{M}_{\Omega}\|_{p}^{p}+\gamma\|\boldsymbol{M}\|_{S_{q}}^{q}\end{split}

(4)

where
\|\boldsymbol{X}_{\Omega}-\boldsymbol{M}_{\Omega}\|_{p}^{p}=\sum_{i,j\in\Omega}(\boldsymbol{X}_{ij}-\boldsymbol{M}_{ij})^{p}
and
\|\boldsymbol{M}\|_{S_{q}}^{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{m,n\}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{q}
. Nevertheless, the proximity operator for the
\ell_{p}
-norm does not have a closed-form expression, except for some special cases.

III
Hybrid ordinary-Welsch function and its implicit regularizer

In this section, we first devise a novel loss function, and propose a new regularizer. We prove that the regularizer is a quasiconvex function and its
Moreau~envelope
is convex. In addition, a closed-form expression for its proximity operator is derived.

The expression of our designed HOW is:

l_{\sigma,\lambda}(x)=\begin{cases}x^{2}/2,&|x|\leq\lambda\\
\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\left(1-e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}-x^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}}\right)+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2},&|x|\textgreater\lambda\end{cases}

(11)

where
\lambda\geq 0
is a constant, and
\sigma
is the kernel size.
It is seen that the Welsch function is a special case of (
11
) when
\lambda=0
.
Besides, the Legendre-Fenchel transform is utilized to study the nonconvex HOW function. Given a function
f(x)
, its conjugate

is
[
50
]
:

f^{*}(y)=\mathop{\sup}\limits_{x}~xy-f(x)

(12)

If

is a convex function, we have:

f(x)=\left(f^{*}(x)\right)^{*}=\mathop{\max}\limits_{y}~xy-f^{*}(y)

(13)

where the
\sup
amounts to the
\max
when
f(x)
is a convex function.
Moreover, we define a new convex function
f(x)
:

\begin{split}f(x)&=\frac{x^{2}}{2}-l_{\sigma,\lambda}(x)\\
&={\begin{cases}0,&|x|\leq\lambda\\
\frac{x^{2}}{2}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\left(1-e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}-x^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}}\right)-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2},&|x|\textgreater\lambda\end{cases}}\end{split}

(14)

whose convexity property is proved in Appendix
A
.
By (
12
), it is easy to obtain:

\begin{split}f^{*}(y)&=\mathop{\max}\limits_{x}~xy-\frac{x^{2}}{2}+l_{\sigma,\lambda}(x)\\
&=\mathop{\max}\limits_{x}~-\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{2}+l_{\sigma,\lambda}(x)+\frac{y^{2}}{2}\\
&=\lambda\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda}(y)+\frac{y^{2}}{2}\end{split}

(15)

where

\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda}(y)=\mathop{\max}\limits_{x}~-\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{2\lambda}+\frac{l_{\sigma,\lambda}(x)}{\lambda}

(16)

Since
f(x)
is convex, applying (
13
) yields:

\begin{split}f(x)&=\mathop{\max}\limits_{y}~y\cdot x-f^{*}(y)\\
&=\mathop{\max}\limits_{y}~y\cdot x-\lambda\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda}(y)-\frac{y^{2}}{2}\\
&=\mathop{\max}\limits_{y}~-\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{2}-\lambda\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda}(y)+\frac{x^{2}}{2}\end{split}

(17)

Combining (
14
) and (
17

\begin{split}l_{\sigma,\lambda}(x)=\mathop{\min}\limits_{y}~\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{2}+\lambda\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda}(y)\end{split}

The solution to (

Lemma 1

Definition 1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
. Since the regularizer

\displaystyle\mathop{\min}\limits_{\boldsymbol{y}}~\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y})

(21a)

\displaystyle\mathop{\min}\limits_{\boldsymbol{Y}}~\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{Y}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{Y})

(21b)

are

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{y}_{i}=P_{\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}),~i=1,\cdots,m

(22a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Y}_{ij}=P_{\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{X}_{ij}),~i=1,\cdots,m,~j=1,\cdots,n

(22b)

respectively.
Defining
P_{\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda}}(\cdot)
is an element-wise operator, (
22a
) and (
22b
) are denoted as:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{y}=P_{\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{x})

(23a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Y}=P_{\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{X})

(23b)

Definition 2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{X}=\boldsymbol{U}~{\rm diag}(\boldsymbol{s})~\boldsymbol{V}^{T}
be the singular value decomposition (SVD) of a rank-
r
matrix
\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, where
\boldsymbol{s}=[s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{r}]^{T}
is the vector of singular values. The nuclear norm

is defined as:

\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{*}=\|\boldsymbol{s}\|_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}

(24)

which is the
\ell_{1}
-norm of

Using the nuclear norm to find the low-rank components will underestimate all nonzero singular values because the nuclear norm is equivalent to applying the
\ell_{1}
-norm to the singular values. To address this issue, we replace the
\ell_{1}
-norm with our sparsity-promoting regularizer.

Definition 3

IV-B

Algorithm Development

In this section, we apply the proposed sparsity-inducing regularizer to RMC. The corresponding optimization problem is written as:

\begin{split}&\mathop{\min}\limits_{\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{S}}~\|\boldsymbol{M}\|_{\varphi_{\sigma,1/\rho}}+\lambda\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda/\rho}(\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega})\\
&~\text{s.t.}~\boldsymbol{X}_{\Omega}=\boldsymbol{M}_{\Omega}+\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega}\end{split}

(27)

which is equal to:

\begin{split}&\mathop{\min}\limits_{\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{S}}~\|\boldsymbol{M}\|_{\varphi_{\sigma,1/\rho}}+\lambda\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda/\rho}(\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega})\\
&~\text{s.t.}~\boldsymbol{X}=\boldsymbol{M}+\boldsymbol{S}\end{split}

(28)

where
\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega^{c}}\neq 0
if
\boldsymbol{M}_{\Omega^{c}}\neq 0
. Problem (
28
) can be efficiently solved by ADMM, and its augmented Lagrangian function is:

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\rho}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{S},\boldsymbol{\Lambda})&:=\|\boldsymbol{M}\|_{\varphi_{\sigma,1/\rho}}+\lambda\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda/\rho}(\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega})\\
&+\left<\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{M}-\boldsymbol{S}\right>+\frac{\rho}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{M}-\boldsymbol{S}\right\|_{F}^{2}\end{split}

(29)

which amounts to:

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{S},\boldsymbol{\Lambda})&:={1}/{\rho}\cdot\|\boldsymbol{M}\|_{\varphi_{\sigma,1/\rho}}+\lambda/\rho\cdot\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda/\rho}(\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega})\\
&+\left<\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{M}-\boldsymbol{S}\right>/\rho+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{M}-\boldsymbol{S}\right\|_{F}^{2}\end{split}

(30)

where
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
is the Lagrange multiplier vector, the last term is the augmented term and
\rho>0
is the penalty parameter.

The details of the parameter updates at the
(k+1)
th iteration, i.e.,
\left(\boldsymbol{M}^{k+1},\boldsymbol{S}^{k+1},\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{k+1}\right)
, are derived as follows.

Update~of

\boldsymbol{M}
: Given
\boldsymbol{S}^{k}
,
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{k}
and
\rho^{k}
, the low-rank matrix
\boldsymbol{M}
is updated by:

\boldsymbol{M}^{k+1}={\rm arg}\mathop{\min}\limits_{\boldsymbol{M}}1/\rho^{k}\cdot\|\boldsymbol{M}\|_{\varphi_{\sigma,1/\rho^{k}}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{S}^{k}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{k}}{\rho^{k}}-\boldsymbol{M}\right\|_{F}^{2}

(31)

Invoking Lemma
2
, we have:

\boldsymbol{M}^{k+1}=P_{\|\cdot\|_{\varphi_{\sigma,1/\rho^{k}}}}\left(\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{S}^{k}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{k}}{\rho^{k}}\right)

(32)

Update~of

\boldsymbol{S}
: Given
\boldsymbol{M}^{k+1}
,
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{k}
and
\rho^{k}
,
\boldsymbol{S}^{k+1}
is updated by two steps, i.e., the updates of
\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega}^{k+1}
and
\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega^{c}}^{k+1}
.
\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega}^{k+1}
is obtained from:

{\rm arg}\mathop{\min}\limits_{\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega}}\lambda/\rho^{k}\cdot\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda/\rho^{k}}(\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega})+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{X}_{\Omega}-\boldsymbol{M}_{\Omega}^{k+1}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Omega}^{k}}{\rho^{k}}-\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega}\right\|_{F}^{2}

(33)

whose closed-form solution is:

\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega}^{k+1}=P_{\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda/\rho^{k}}}\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{\Omega}-\boldsymbol{M}^{k+1}_{\Omega}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Omega}^{k}}{\rho^{k}}\right)

(34)

While
\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega^{c}}^{k+1}
is updated by:

{\rm arg}\mathop{\min}\limits_{\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega^{c}}}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{X}_{\Omega^{c}}-\boldsymbol{M}_{\Omega^{c}}^{k+1}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Omega^{c}}^{k}}{\rho^{k}}-\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega^{c}}\right\|_{F}^{2}

(35)

with the optimal solution:

\boldsymbol{S}_{\Omega^{c}}^{k+1}=\frac{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Omega^{c}}^{k}}{\rho^{k}}-\boldsymbol{M}_{\Omega^{c}}^{k+1}

(36)

Combining (
34
) and (
36
) yields:

\boldsymbol{S}_{ij}^{k+1}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &P_{\varphi_{\sigma,\lambda/\rho^{k}}}\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{ij}-\boldsymbol{M}^{k+1}_{ij}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{ij}^{k}}{\rho^{k}}\right),\quad{\rm if}~(i,j)\in\Omega\\
&\frac{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{ij}^{k}}{\rho^{k}}-\boldsymbol{M}_{ij}^{k+1},\quad\quad{\rm if}~(i,j)\in{\Omega^{c}}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(37)

Update~of

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
: Given
\boldsymbol{M}^{k+1}
,
\boldsymbol{S}^{k+1}
and
\rho^{k}
,
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{k+1}
is updated according to

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{k+1}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{k}+\rho^{k}\left(\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{M}^{k+1}-\boldsymbol{S}^{k+1}\right)

(38)

The penalty parameter
\rho^{k}
is determined by
\rho^{k+1}=\mu\rho^{k}
, where
\mu>1
is a constant.

.

Convergence Analysis

The convergence of the proposed algorithm is analyzed in this section and we show that any generated accumulation point satisfies the KKT conditions.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\{(\boldsymbol{M}^{k},\boldsymbol{S}^{k},\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{k})\}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
1
. Given a bounded initialization
(\boldsymbol{S}^{0},\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{0})
,
\{(\boldsymbol{M}^{k},\boldsymbol{S}^{k},\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{k})\}
has the following properties:

(i)

The sequence
\{(\boldsymbol{M}^{k},\boldsymbol{S}^{k})\}
satisfies:

1.

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|\boldsymbol{M}^{k+1}-\boldsymbol{M}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}=0

2.

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|\boldsymbol{S}^{k+1}-\boldsymbol{S}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}=0

3.

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{M}^{k+1}-\boldsymbol{S}^{k+1}\right\|_{F}^{2}=0

(ii)

The sequences
\{(\boldsymbol{M}^{k},\boldsymbol{S}^{k},\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{k})\}
generated are all bounded.

(iii)

Any accumulation point of the iteration sequence is a stationary point that satisfies the KKT conditions for (
28
).

IV-D

Stopping Criteria and Computational Complexity

The algorithm is terminated when it converges or the iteration number reaches the maximum allowable number
I_{m}
. Defining the relative error
rel_{E}^{k}=\|\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{M}^{k}-\boldsymbol{S}^{k}\|_{F}/\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}
, if
rel_{E}^{k}\leq\xi
, where
\xi
is a constant, we assert that the solution satisfies the convergence condition.

Similar to principal component pursuit (PCP)
[
13
]
, the proposed algorithm involves the SVD computation per iteration, whose complexity is
\mathcal{O}(\min(m,n)mn)

[
41
]
, where
m
and
n
are the row and column lengths of the incomplete matrix, respectively. Thus, the total complexity of Algorithm
1
is
\mathcal{O}(K\min(m,n)mn)
, where
K
is the required iteration number.

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we devise a novel loss function, referred to as HOW, and propose a new sparsity-promoting regularizer, which is able to make the solution sparse.
Besides, the solution generated by our regularizer has less bias than that by the
\ell_{1}
-norm. Compared with the
\ell_{p}
norm with
0<p<1
, the closed-form expression for the proximity operator of the developed regularizer is derived. Moreover, the properties of our regularizer are theoretically analyzed. We apply it to RMC, and an ADMM based algorithm with convergence guarantees is suggested. We prove that any generated accumulation point satisfies the KKT conditions.
Extensive numerical examples using synthetic and real-world datasets show that our algorithm is superior to the state-of-the-art robust methods in terms of recovery performance.

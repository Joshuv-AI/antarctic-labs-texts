Asymptotic behavior of a diffused interface volume-preserving mean curvature flow
Matteo Bonforte, Francesco Maggi, Daniel Restrepo

Abstract
. We consider a diffused interface version of the volume-preserving mean curvature flow in the Euclidean space, and prove, in every dimension and under natural assumptions on the initial datum, exponential convergence towards single “diffused balls”.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

In this paper we introduce a PDE reformulation of the classical volume-preserving mean curvature flow (VPMCF) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where the role of the perimeter functional is played by the Allen–Cahn energy. From the physical viewpoint this reformulation seems well justified, since it consists in replacing the classical
sharp
interface model for surface tension based on perimeter minimization with the equally interesting and important
diffused
interface model based on the minimization of the Allen–Cahn energy. From the mathematical viewpoint working in the diffused setting clears up the analysis from the ambiguities brought in by the formation of singularities characteristic of geometric flows, which are directly reflected into the abundance of non-equivalent weak formulations of the VPMCF.

Our main result proves, in every dimension and under a variety of natural assumptions on the initial datum, exponential convergence of the diffused VPMCF towards single “diffused balls”. This result is indeed stronger than the presently known analogous results for the classical VPMCF, see Remark
1.1
below.

The diffused interface model we adopt is based on the volume-constrained minimization of the classical Allen–Cahn functional

(1.2)

Remark 1.1

(Asymptotic analysis of the VPMCF)
.

It is convenient to briefly review the state of the art concerning convergence to equilibrium for the classical VPMCF (with the disclaimer that, due to singularities formation, these various results may pertain to different weak formulations of the VPMCF). First, convergence to a single ball has been proved under a variety of suitable geometric restrictions on the initial datum that can be shown to be preserved along the flow, and that
exclude
singularities formation: these are uniform convexity
[
Hui87
]
,
C^{1}
-proximity to a sphere
[
ES98
]
, star-shapedeness
[
KK20
]
, or integral pinching conditions
[
Li09
]
. In general, singularity formation may lead to convergence to multiple balls, a phenomenon called
bubbling
(see
[
FJM22
, Theorem 1.4]
for an example) and it is thus unclear for which class of initial data one should expect convergence towards a single ball (of the same volume as the initial datum) or towards multiple balls (all with a same volume equal to a fraction of the initial one). Since perimeter decreases along the VPMCF, a natural
conjecture
is that, if a unit volume initial datum has perimeter
strictly
less than
twice
the perimeter of a ball of volume
1/2
, then convergence to a single ball should follow (with exponential rates of convergence). This conjecture has been proved for “flat flow” solutions of the VPMCF, and in dimensions
n=2
and
n=3
respectively, in the recent papers
[
JMPS23
,
JMOS24
]
. Moreover, again when
n=2,3
, in
[
JN23
]
it is proved that flat flow solutions always resolve, as
t\to\infty
, as finite union of balls with possibly moving centers. All these results are based on powerful quantitative bubbling results for sets with
L^{2}
-small mean curvature oscillation. The restriction to dimensions
n=2,3
is strongly correlated, on the one hand, to the fact that the
L^{2}
-oscillation of the mean curvature is the dissipation of the VPMCF, and is thus the quantity to work with in this setting; and that, on the other hand, the critical
L^{p}
-space for the regularity theory of the mean curvature of a boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
p=n-1
. For these reasons, the further extension of these methods to dimensions
n\geq 4
seems delicate.

1.2.
Open problems and metastability

Before presenting the proof of Theorem

1.3.
Analysis of the diffused VPMCF

We now provide a detailed breakdown of a series of results leading to Theorem

The starting point of our analysis is to establish the existence of solutions of the diffused VPMCF and their basic regularity properties. This is a nontrivial task due to the presence of the Lagrange multiplier functional

W^{1,1}(t_{0},\infty)
for every

\frac{d}{dt}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial_{t}u(t))^{2}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{4\,|\nabla(\partial_{t}u)|^{2}+\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\,\Big(\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\varepsilon}-\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]\,V^{\prime\prime}(u)\Big)\,(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\Big\}(t)\,.

(1.18)

In particular,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial_{t}u(t))^{2}=0\,.

It is well-know that the asymptotic behavior of a semilinear parabolic PDE can be “subsequentially resolved” into a bubbling of mutually drifting stationary states: a good exemplification of this kind of result, whose fundamental idea is rooted into Lions’ concentration-compactness principle itself, is found, for example, in
[
Fei97
, Theorem 1.1]
. Another powerful idea found in theory of semilinear parabolic PDE is that when the initial datum is compactly supported, then a sort of star-shapedness of the flow (see (
5.45
)) can be established by means of the parabolic maximum principle, thus excluding bubbling phenomena; for a general exemplification of this idea, see
[
Fei97
, Theorem 1.2]
. In Section
5
we adapt these general methods to our specific problem, which, again, does not follow in the range of application of the general theory because of the non-autonomy of (
DF
) and because of the presence of the non-local Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
. Notice that we are not considering (yet) the physical regime when
\varepsilon
is small: in particular, there is no way to attribute any geometric meaning to the stationary states

appearing in the statement, e.g. by relating them to the minimizers
\zeta_{\varepsilon,m}
of
\Psi(\varepsilon,m)
.

2.2.
The diffused isoperimetric problem

Let us recall that for every
\varepsilon,m>0
we set

\Psi(\varepsilon,m)=\inf\Big\{\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u):\mathcal{V}(u)=m\,,u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])\Big\}\,.

Among the basic properties of
\Psi(\varepsilon,m)
we have the scaling law

\Psi(\varepsilon,m)=m^{(n-1)/n}\,\Psi\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{m^{1/n}},1\Big)\,,\qquad\forall\varepsilon,m>0\,,

(2.1)

and the isoperimetric lower bound and
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
limit

\displaystyle\Psi(\varepsilon,m)\!\!

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\!\!2\,c_{\rm iso}(n)\,m^{(n-1)/n}\,,\qquad\forall\varepsilon,m>0\,,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\Psi(\varepsilon,m)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!2\,c_{\rm iso}(n)\,m^{(n-1)/n}\,,\qquad\forall m>0\,,

(2.3)

where we have set
c_{\rm iso}(n)=n\,\omega_{n}^{1/n}
. A simple concentration compactness argument (see
[
MNR23
, Proof of Theorem A.1, steps one and two]
) shows that
\Psi(\varepsilon,m)
admits radially symmetric decreasing minimizers for each
\varepsilon
and

. Stronger properties are proved in
[

, Theorem 1.1, Theorem 6.1]
under the geometric regime

{\bf(i):}

2.3.
Technical properties of
W
and related potentials

In the linearization of (
DF
) we shall tacitly use the fact that, being
W\in C^{2,1}[0,1]
, we have

\Big|W(r)-W(s)-W^{\prime}(s)(r-s)-W^{\prime\prime}(s)\frac{(r-s)^{2}}{2}\Big|\leq C\,|r-s|^{3}\,,\qquad\forall r,s\in[0,1]\,.

We also observe that
1/C\leq W^{\prime\prime}\leq C
on
[0,1]
, and that there is a universal constant
\delta_{0}<1/2
such that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{W(r)}{r^{2}}\,,\frac{W^{\prime}(r)}{r}\leq C\quad\hskip 42.67912pt\forall r\in(0,\delta_{0}]\,,\\
&\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{W(r)}{(1-r)^{2}}\,,\frac{-W^{\prime}(r)}{1-r}\leq C\quad\hskip 11.38092pt\forall r\in[1-\delta_{0},1)\,.\end{split}

(2.7)

Recalling that
\Phi(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\sqrt{W}
and
V(r)=\Phi(r)^{n/(n-1)}
for
r\in[0,1]
, we use (
2.7
) to quantify the behaviors of
\Phi
and
V
near
r=0
and
r=1
. By (
1.2
),
\Phi\in C^{3}_{{\rm loc}}(0,1)
, with

\Phi^{\prime}=\sqrt{W}\,,\quad\Phi^{\prime\prime}=\frac{W^{\prime}}{2\,\sqrt{W}}\,,\quad\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}=\frac{W^{\prime\prime}}{2\,\sqrt{W}}-\frac{(W^{\prime})^{2}}{4\,W^{3/2}}\,,\qquad\mbox{on $(0,1)$}\,.

By (
2.7
) and (
1.3
) we thus see that
\Phi
satisfies

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{\Phi(r)}{r^{2}}\,,\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(r)}{r}\,,\Phi^{\prime\prime}(r)\leq C\,,\qquad\forall r\in(0,\delta_{0}]\,,\\
&\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{1-\Phi(r)}{(1-r)^{2}}\,,\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(r)}{1-r}\,,-\Phi^{\prime\prime}(r)\leq C\,,\qquad\forall r\in[1-\delta_{0},1)\,.\end{split}

(2.8)

By exploiting (
2.8
) and setting for brevity
a=W^{\prime\prime}(0)
, we see that, as

,

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\,W^{\prime\prime}\,W-(W^{\prime})^{2}}{4\,W^{3/2}}=\frac{2\,(a+{\rm O}(r))\,(a\,(r^{2}/2)+{\rm O}(r^{3}))-(a\,r+{\rm O}(r^{2}))^{2}}{4\,(a\,(r^{2}/2)+{\rm O}(r^{3}))^{3/2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm O}(r^{3})}{4\,a^{3/2}\,r^{3}+{\rm o}(r^{3})}\,,

and by a similar computation for
r\to 1^{-}
, we find

|\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}|\leq C\quad\mbox{on $(0,\delta_{0})\cup(1-\delta_{0},1)$}\,.

(2.9)

By (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) we see that
\Phi\in C^{2,1}[0,1]
with a universal estimate on its

-norm: in particular,

\Big|\Phi(r)-\Phi(s)-\Phi^{\prime}(s)(r-s)-\Phi^{\prime\prime}(s)\frac{(r-s)^{2}}{2}\Big|\leq C\,|r-s|^{3}\,,\qquad\forall r,s\in(0,1)\,.

Since

for

(recall that

) and

if and only if

, with

\displaystyle V^{\prime}=(1+\alpha)\,\Phi^{\alpha}\,\Phi^{\prime}\,,\qquad V^{\prime\prime}=(1+\alpha)\Big\{\alpha\,\frac{(\Phi^{\prime})^{2}}{\Phi^{1-\alpha}}+\Phi^{\alpha}\,\Phi^{\prime\prime}\Big\}\,,

\displaystyle|V^{\prime\prime\prime}|\leq C(\alpha)\,\Big\{\frac{(\Phi^{\prime})^{3}}{\Phi^{2-\alpha}}+\frac{\Phi^{\prime}\,|\Phi^{\prime\prime}|}{\Phi^{1-\alpha}}+\Phi^{\alpha}\,|\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}|\Big\}\,.

and of the fact that negative powers of

are large only near

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{V(r)}{r^{2+2\alpha}}\,,\frac{V^{\prime}(r)}{r^{1+2\alpha}}\,,\frac{V^{\prime\prime}(r)}{r^{2\alpha}}\leq C\,,\hskip 19.91684pt\quad|V^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)|\leq\frac{C}{r^{1-2\alpha}}\qquad\forall r\in(0,\delta_{0}]\,,\\
&\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{1-V(r)}{(1-r)^{2}}\,,\frac{V^{\prime}(r)}{1-r}\leq C\,,\quad|V^{\prime\prime}(r)|\,,|V^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)|\leq C\,,\qquad\forall r\in[1-\delta_{0},1)\,.\end{split}

\varepsilon>0
, and
\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{2}\leq C\,\big\{\varepsilon\,\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)+\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)\big\}\,,

(3.3)

and, with an
\varepsilon
-dependent universal constant
C(\varepsilon)
,

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\Big\{1,\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)}{\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)}\Big\}^{2\,n}\,,

(3.4)

\displaystyle|\lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)\,\frac{\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)^{2n+1}}{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)^{2n}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,.

(3.5)

Furthermore, if
\delta=0
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, then (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) hold with
C
in place of
C(\varepsilon)
.

(ii):
if
u,v\in X\setminus\{0\}
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
\max\{\|u\|_{X}\,,\|v\|_{X}\}\leq C(\varepsilon)
, then

\displaystyle|\lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]-\lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}[v]|\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!C(\varepsilon,\delta)\,\frac{\|u-v\|_{X}}{\min\{1,\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)^{2n}\}\,\min\{1,\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(v)^{2n}\}}\,,\quad\forall\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]\,,\,\,\,

(3.6)

\displaystyle|\lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]-\lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}[v]|\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!C(\varepsilon)\,\frac{\|u-v\|_{X}^{\gamma(n)}}{\min\{1,\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)^{2n}\}\,\min\{1,\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(v)^{2n}\}}\,,\qquad\forall\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]\,,

(3.7)

\displaystyle|\lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]-\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u]|\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!C(\varepsilon)\,\frac{\|V_{\delta}-V\|_{C^{2}[0,1]}}{\min\{1,\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)^{2n}\}\,\min\{1,\mathcal{V}(u)^{2n}\}}\,,\qquad\forall\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]\,,

(3.8)

where
\gamma(n)=\min\{1,2/(n-1)\}
; and if
u,v\in W^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])\setminus\{0\}
, then

|\lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]-\lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}[v]|\leq C(\varepsilon)\frac{\max\{\|u\|_{W^{2,2}},\|v\|_{W^{2,2}}\}\,\|u-v\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\min\{1,\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)^{2n}\}\,\min\{1,\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(v)^{2n}\}}\,,\qquad\forall\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]\,.

(3.9)

Proof.

Step one, diffused relative isoperimetry and nucleation
: We prove two relative isoperimetric inequalities in balls in the diffused setting and a consequent nucleation type lemma modeled after
[
MPS22
, Lemma 2.1]
. This kind of result is in turn inspired by a tool introduced by Almgren
[
Alm76
]
in the study of isoperimetric clusters, see
[
Mag12
, Lemma 29.10]
. More precisely, we prove the existence of universal constants
\eta_{0}
,
\sigma_{0}
and
C
with the following properties:

(a):
if
\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]
,
\varepsilon,r>0
, and if
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])
satisfies

\fint_{B_{r}}V_{\delta}(u)\leq\eta_{0}\,\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\Big)^{2\,n},

(3.10)

then

C\,\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u;B_{r})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\,\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u;B_{r})^{(n-1)/n}\,;

(3.11)

(b):
if
\varepsilon,r>0
are such that
\varepsilon/r<\sigma_{0}
, and if
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])
satisfies

\fint_{B_{r}}V(u)\leq\frac{1}{2}\,,

(3.12)

then

C\,\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u;B_{r})\geq\mathcal{V}(u;B_{r})^{(n-1)/n}\,.

(3.13)

(c):
if
\varepsilon>0
and

\mathcal{B}=\Big\{B_{\sqrt{n+1}\,R/2}(R\,z):z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\Big\}\,,\qquad R=\frac{\max\{1,\varepsilon\}}{\sigma_{0}\,(\sqrt{n+1}/2)}\,,

then for every
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])
we have

\displaystyle C\,\frac{\max\{1,\varepsilon\}}{\min\{1,\varepsilon^{2\,n}\}}\,\sup_{B\in\,\mathcal{B}}\fint_{B}V_{\delta}(u)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\Big\{1,\Big(\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)}{\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)}\Big)^{n}\Big\}\,,\qquad\forall\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]\,,

(3.14)

\displaystyle C\,\max\{1,\varepsilon\}\,\sup_{B\in\,\mathcal{B}}\fint_{B}V(u)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\Big\{1,\Big(\frac{\mathcal{V}(u)}{\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)}\Big)^{n}\Big\}\,.

(3.15)

We first derive conclusion (c) from conclusions (a) and (b)
: Let
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])
. In proving (
3.15
) we can assume without loss of generality that

\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\fint_{B}V(u)\leq\frac{1}{2}\,.

In particular, since the choice of
R
is such that
\varepsilon/(\sqrt{n+1}R/2)<\sigma_{0}
for every
\varepsilon>0
, we deduce from conclusion (b) that (
3.13
) holds for every
B\in\mathcal{B}
. The corresponding bounds can be used together with the fact that
\mathcal{B}
is a covering of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with finite overlapping (depending only on the dimension
n
) to conclude that

\displaystyle C(n)\,\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u;B)\geq\frac{1}{C}\,\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{V}(u;B)^{(n-1)/n}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\,\frac{\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{V}(u;B)}{\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{V}(u;B)^{1/n}}\geq\frac{1}{C}\,\frac{\mathcal{V}(u)}{\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\Big(\int_{B}V(u)\Big)^{1/n}}\,.

Thanks to
|B|^{1/n}\leq C\,\max\{1,\varepsilon\}
for every
B\in\mathcal{B}
, (
3.16
) implies (
3.15
). To prove (
3.14
), let
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
, and let us assume without loss of generality that

\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\fint_{B}V_{\delta}(u)\leq\eta_{0}\,\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n+1}R/2}\Big)^{2\,n}\,,

(3.17)

then we can apply (
3.11
) to each
B\in\mathcal{B}
, and conclude as in (
3.16
) that

C(n)\,\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{C\,R}\,\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)}{\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u;B)^{1/n}}\geq\frac{\min\{1,\varepsilon\}}{C}\,\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)}{\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u;B)^{1/n}}\,.

In particular, (
3.14
) follows by taking again into account that
\varepsilon/R=C\,\max\{1,\varepsilon\}
.

We now prove conclusions (a) and (b)
: Up to a rescaling, we can take
r=1
in both conclusions. To prove conclusion (a), we notice that,
by (
3.10
) and (
2.13
),

\eta_{0}\,\varepsilon^{2\,n}\geq\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}\,\int_{B_{1}\cap\{u\geq 1/2\}}V_{\delta}(u)\geq\frac{|B_{1}\cap\{u\geq 1/2\}|}{C}\,.

(3.18)

Since
W(r)\geq r^{2}/C
for
r\in[0,1/2]
, by (
2.13
), (
3.18
), and the Hölder inequality, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}u^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\cap\{u\leq 1/2\}}u^{2}+\int_{B_{1}\cap\{u\geq 1/2\}}u^{2}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{1}}W(u)+C\,\big|B_{1}\cap\{u\geq 1/2\}\big|^{1/n}\,\Big(\int_{B_{1}}u^{2n/(n-1)}\Big)^{(n-1)/n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{1}}W(u)+C\,\eta_{0}^{1/n}\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\Big(\int_{B_{1}}u^{2n/(n-1)}\Big)^{(n-1)/n}\,.

By combining (
3.19
) with the embedding of
L^{2n/(n-1)}(B_{1})
into
W^{1,2}(B_{1})
, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\varepsilon\,|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{W(u)}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\varepsilon\,|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{u^{2}}{C\varepsilon}-C\,\eta_{0}^{1/n}\,\varepsilon\,\Big(\int_{B_{1}}u^{2n/(n-1)}\Big)^{(n-1)/n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{C}\,\Big(\int_{B_{1}}u^{2n/(n-1)}\Big)^{(n-1)/n}-C\,\eta_{0}^{1/n}\,\varepsilon\,\Big(\int_{B_{1}}u^{2n/(n-1)}\Big)^{(n-1)/n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{C}\,\Big(\int_{B_{1}}u^{2n/(n-1)}\Big)^{(n-1)/n}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{C}\,\Big(\int_{B_{1}}V_{\delta}(u)\Big)^{(n-1)/n}\,,

provided
\eta_{0}
is a sufficiently small universal constant, and where we have used that
V_{\delta}(r)\leq C\,r^{2n/(n-1)}
for every
r\in[0,1]
and
\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]
. Having proved conclusion (a), we now prove conclusion (b). Arguing by contradiction, we can assume the existence of
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0^{+}
and
\{u_{k}\}_{k}
in
W^{1,2}(B_{1};[0,1])
such that, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and setting for brevity

M_{k}=\int_{B_{1}}V(u_{k})=\int_{B_{1}}\Phi(u_{k})^{p}\,,\qquad p=\frac{n}{n-1}\,,

we have
M_{k}\leq\omega_{n}/2
and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\varepsilon_{k}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}+\frac{W(u_{k})}{\varepsilon_{k}}\leq\frac{M_{k}^{1/p}}{k}\,,

(3.20)

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Combining
|\nabla(\Phi\circ u)|=|\nabla u|\,\sqrt{W(u)}
with Young’s inequality as in (
1.5
), we deduce from (
3.20
) and the
BV
-Poincaré inequality
[
AFP00
, (3.41)]
that

\frac{1}{C}\Big(\int_{B_{1}}|\Phi(u_{k})-t_{k}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leq\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla(\Phi\circ u_{k})|\leq\frac{C}{k}\,M_{k}^{1/p}\,,

(3.21)

where
t_{k}=\omega_{n}^{-1}\int_{B_{1}}\Phi(u_{k})
. In particular, there is
c\in[0,1]
such that, up to extracting a subsequence,
u_{k}\to c
in
L^{1}(B_{1})
and a.e. in
B_{1}
as
k\to\infty
. Since (
3.20
) implies
\int_{B_{1}}W(u_{k})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, by Fatou’s lemma we find

\int_{B_{1}}W(c)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{B_{1}}W(u_{k})=0\,.

In particular,
c\in\{0,1\}
. Since
c=1
would contradict
M_{k}\leq\omega_{n}/2
for every
k
, we conclude that
c=0
, and hence, thanks also to
0\leq u_{k}\leq 1
, that
\Phi(u_{k})\to 0
in
L^{p}(B_{1})
(i.e.,
M_{k}\to 0
) as
k\to\infty
. On noticing that
0\leq t_{k}\leq C\,M_{k}^{1/p}
, we deduce by (
3.21
) that

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u_{k})-(M_{k}/\omega_{n})^{1/p}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\leq\|t_{k}-(M_{k}/\omega_{n})^{1/p}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}+\frac{C}{k}\,M_{k}^{1/p}\leq C\,M_{k}^{1/p}\,,

(3.22)

and, in particular, that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\Big|B_{1}\cap\Big\{\Phi(u_{k})\leq(M_{k}/2\omega_{n})^{1/p}\Big\}\Big|=0\,.

Since
\Phi(u_{k})\to 0
in
L^{p}(B_{1})
implies
|B_{1}\cap\{\Phi(u_{k})>1/2\}|\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, we conclude that

\Big|B_{1}\cap\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\geq\Phi(u_{k})\geq(M_{k}/2\omega_{n})^{1/p}\Big\}\Big|\geq\frac{|B_{1}|}{2}\,,

(3.23)

for
k
large enough. In particular, thanks to
\Phi\leq C\,W
on
[0,1/2]
and to (
3.20
), for
k
large enough we have

\frac{M_{k}^{1/p}}{C\,\varepsilon_{k}}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}}\int_{B_{1}\cap\{1/2\geq\Phi(u_{k})\geq(M_{k}/2\omega_{n})^{1/p}\}}\Phi(u_{k})\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}}\int_{B_{1}}W(u_{k})\leq\frac{M_{k}^{1/p}}{k}\,,

(3.24)

that leads to a contradiction as
k\to\infty
.

Step two:
We prove statement (a). To prove (
3.3
) it suffices to recall that
W(r)\geq r^{2}/C
for
r\in[0,1/2]
and
V_{\delta}(r)\geq r^{2}/C
for
r\in[1/2,1]
, so that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{2}=\int_{\{u\leq 1/2\}}u^{2}+\int_{\{u>1/2\}}u^{2}\leq C\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}W(u)+V_{\delta}(u)\,.

To prove (
3.5
) we first notice that
|V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(r)|\leq C
and
|W^{\prime}(r)\,V^{\prime}_{\delta}(r)|\leq C\,W(r)
for every
r\in[0,1]
(and every
\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}])
, so that

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}2\varepsilon\,|\nabla u|^{2}\,V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(u)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\,W^{\prime}(u)\,V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)\Big|\leq C\,\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)\,.

(3.25)

Since
V_{\delta}(r)\leq C\,r^{2}
for
r\in[0,1]
and
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we can find a sequence
R_{j}\to\infty
such that
\int_{\partial B_{R_{j}}}V_{\delta}(u)\to 0
as
j\to\infty
, and thus apply the divergence theorem to deduce that, for every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle(n-1)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{V_{\delta}(u)}{|x-x_{0}|}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{\delta}(u)\,{\rm div}\,\Big(\frac{x-x_{0}}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)\,dx

(3.26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V^{\prime}_{\delta}(u)\,\nabla u\cdot\frac{x-x_{0}}{|x-x_{0}|}\,dx\leq\|V^{\prime}_{\delta}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,.

Setting
R=2\,\max\{1,\varepsilon\}/\sigma_{0}\,\sqrt{n+1}
, we can apply (
3.14
) to find
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

C(\varepsilon)\,\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}V_{\delta}(u)\geq\min\Big\{1,\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)}{\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)}\Big\}^{n}

which combined with (
3.26
) gives

\|V^{\prime}_{\delta}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq\frac{(n-1)}{R}\,\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}V_{\delta}(u)\geq\frac{1}{C(\varepsilon)}\,\min\Big\{1,\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)}{\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)}\Big\}^{n}\,,

(3.27)

that is (
3.4
). In summary, by combining (
3.25
) and (
3.27
) we find

\displaystyle|\lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,\frac{\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)^{2}}\leq C(\varepsilon)\,\frac{\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)}{\min\Big\{1,\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)}{\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)}\Big\}^{2n}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}

(3.28)

that is (
3.5
). When
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
and
\delta=0
we can replace the constant
C(\varepsilon)
in (
3.5
) with a plain universal constant
C
by exploiting the fact that (
3.15
) can be used in place of (
3.14
) (notice indeed that
\max\{1,\varepsilon\}\leq C
when
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
).

Step three
: We prove statement (b). Setting for brevity,

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}2\,\varepsilon\,|\nabla u|^{2}\,V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(u)+\frac{W^{\prime}(u)\,V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)}{\varepsilon}\,,\qquad\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]\,,

we notice that for every
\delta,\delta_{*}\in[0,\delta_{0}]
and
u,v\in X\setminus\{0\}
,

\lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]-\lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta_{*}}[v]=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]-\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta_{*}}[v]}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)^{2}}+\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta_{*}}[v]\,\big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{\delta_{*}}^{\prime}(v)^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)^{2}\big\}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{\delta_{*}}^{\prime}(v)^{2}}\,.

(3.29)

We first work on (
3.29
) with
\delta=\delta_{*}
, with the goal of proving (
3.6
), (
3.7
), and (
3.9
). Since
\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq\|u\|_{W^{1,2}}^{2}/\varepsilon
, we deduce from (
3.4
) that, if
\|u\|_{W^{1,2}}\leq C(\varepsilon)
, then

\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)^{2}}\leq\frac{C(\varepsilon)}{\min\{1,\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)^{2n}\}}\,.

(3.30)

Recalling from (
3.25

|\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]|\leq C\,\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq C(\varepsilon)\,\|u\|_{W^{1,2}}
, and using again first
{\rm Lip}(V_{\delta}^{\prime},[0,1])\leq C
, and then
|V_{\delta}^{\prime}(r)|\leq C\,t
for
r\in[0,1]
, we find that, if
\|u\|_{W^{1,2}}\leq C(\varepsilon)
, then for every
v\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])

\displaystyle\Big|\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[v]\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(V_{\delta}^{\prime}(v)^{2}-V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)^{2}\big)\Big|\leq C(\varepsilon)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)-V_{\delta}^{\prime}(v)|\,\big(|V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)|+|V_{\delta}^{\prime}(v)|\big)

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon)\,\|u-v\|_{L^{2}}\,\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)|^{2}+|V_{\delta}^{\prime}(v)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon)\,\max\{\|u\|_{L^{2}},\|v\|_{L^{2}}\}\,\|u-v\|_{L^{2}}\,.

(3.31)

In summary, by (
3.29
), (
3.30
) and (
3.31
) for every
\varepsilon>0
and
u,v\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])\setminus\{0\}
with
\max\{\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\|v\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\}\leq C(\varepsilon)
we have proved that

\big|\lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]-\lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}[v]\big|\leq C(\varepsilon)\,\frac{|\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]-\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[v]|}{\min\{1,\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)^{2n}\}}+\frac{C(\varepsilon)\,\|u-v\|_{L^{2}}}{\min\{1,\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(u)^{2n}\}\,\min\{1,\mathcal{V}_{\delta}(v)^{2n}\}}\,.

(3.32)

We first estimate that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]-\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[v]|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,\varepsilon\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla(u-v)|\,\big(|\nabla u|+|\nabla v|)\,|V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(u)|

(3.33)

\displaystyle+2\,\varepsilon\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,|V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(u)-V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(v)|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|W^{\prime}(u)-W^{\prime}(v)|\,|V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)|+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|W^{\prime}(v)|\,|V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)-V_{\delta}^{\prime}(v)|\,.

By recalling that
{\rm Lip}(W^{\prime};[0,1])\leq C
,
{\rm Lip}(V_{\delta}^{\prime};[0,1])\leq C
,
{\rm Lip}(V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime};[0,1])\leq C(\delta)
(when
\delta>0

\max\{|V_{\delta}^{\prime}(r)|,|W^{\prime}(r)|\}\leq C\,r
for

\displaystyle|\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]-\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[v]|\leq C\,\max\big\{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}},\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\big\}\,\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}

(3.34)

\displaystyle+C(\varepsilon,\delta)\,\|u-v\|_{C^{0}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\,\|u-v\|_{L^{2}}\,\max\{\|u\|_{L^{2}},\|v\|_{L^{2}}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)\,\max\big\{\|u\|_{W^{1,2}},\|v\|_{W^{1,2}}\big\}\,\|u-v\|_{W^{1,2}}+C(\delta)\,\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\|u-v\|_{C^{0}}\,;

while, using
[V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}]_{C^{0,\gamma(n)}[0,1]}\leq C
(which also holds when
\delta=0
) in place of
{\rm Lip}(V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime};[0,1])\leq C(\delta)
, we find instead

\displaystyle|\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]-\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[v]|

(3.35)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)\,\max\big\{\|u\|_{W^{1,2}},\|v\|_{W^{1,2}}\big\}\,\|u-v\|_{W^{1,2}}+C\,\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\|u-v\|_{C^{0}}^{\gamma(n)}\,;

thanks to
\max\{\|u\|_{X},\|v\|_{X}\}\leq C(\varepsilon)
; with the convention that
C(\varepsilon,\delta)=+\infty
if
\delta=0
, we thus conclude from (
3.33
), (
3.34
) and (
3.35
) that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]-\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[v]|\leq\min\Big\{C(\varepsilon,\delta)\,\|u-v\|_{X},C(\varepsilon)\|u-v\|_{X}^{\gamma(n)}\Big\}\,.

(3.36)

By means of (
3.32
) and (
3.36
) we find that, if
\max\{\|u\|_{X},\|v\|_{X}\}\leq C(\varepsilon)
, then (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) hold. We now assume that
u,v\in W^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])\setminus\{0\}
and prove (
3.9

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(u)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla u-\nabla v)\cdot\nabla v\,\,V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(v)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\,\,V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(u)-\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,\,V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla u-\nabla v)\cdot\nabla v\,V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(v)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta u)\,\big(V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)-V_{\delta}^{\prime}(v)\big)\,,

which can be used to replace (
3.33
) with

\displaystyle|\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[u]-\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon,\delta}[v]|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,\varepsilon\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta u|\,|V_{\delta}^{\prime}(u)-V_{\delta}^{\prime}(v)|+|\nabla(u-v)|\,|\nabla v|\,|V_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(v)|

\displaystyle|F[u](s)-F[v](s)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)\,\Big\{{\rm Lip}(W^{\prime})+|\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(s)]|\,{\rm Lip}(V^{\prime})\Big\}\,|w(s)|

\displaystyle+V^{\prime}(v(s))\,|\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(s)]-\lambda_{\varepsilon}[v(s)]|

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,\big\{|w(s)|+\|w(s)\|_{W^{1,2}}\,|v(s)|\big\}\,,

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,t\,a(t)\,,

\displaystyle\|\nabla w(s)\|_{L^{2}}

: Statement (i) was proved in (

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{2\,\varepsilon\,\nabla u\cdot\partial_{t}(\nabla u)+\frac{W^{\prime}(u)}{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{t}u\Big\}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}u(t)\,\Big\{2\,\varepsilon\,\Delta u-\frac{W^{\prime}(u)}{\varepsilon}\Big\}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}u(t)\,\Big\{\varepsilon\,\partial_{t}u+\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]\,V^{\prime}(u)\Big\}(t)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial_{t}u(t))^{2}+\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}u(t)\,V^{\prime}(u(t))=-\varepsilon\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial_{t}u(t))^{2}\,,

We prove that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(\partial_{t}u\,\partial_{tt}u\big)(t)\!\!\!

\displaystyle\!\!\!-2\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla(\partial_{t}u)|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{W^{\prime\prime}(u)-\varepsilon\,\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]\,V^{\prime\prime}(u)\Big\}(t)\,(\partial_{t}u(t))^{2}

Proof of Theorem

Let

for all

, with

. By Theorem

, there is a unique solution

to the diffused VPMCF (

,

(5.1)

with the property that

(5.12)

In particular, given
t_{j}\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
, then, thanks to (
5.9
), (
5.10
) and (
5.12
), and up to extracting subsequences, we can find
\ell_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\xi_{1}\in\cap_{p\geq 2}W^{3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])\setminus\{0\}
, and a sequence
(x_{j}^{1})_{j}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that, as
j\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t_{j})]=\ell_{\varepsilon}\,,

(5.13)

\displaystyle\mbox{$\tau_{(-x_{j}^{1})}[u(t_{j})]\rightharpoonup\xi_{1}$ weakly in $W^{3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$ as $j\to\infty$}\,,

(5.14)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\big\|\tau_{(-x_{j}^{1})}[u(t_{j})]-\xi_{1}\big\|_{W^{2,p}(B_{R})}=0\,,\qquad\forall p\geq 2\,,\forall R>0\,.

(5.15)

By Theorem
1.2
-(vi), if we first take
t=t_{j}
in

\partial_{t}u(t)=2\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\Delta u(t)-W^{\prime}(u(t))+\varepsilon\,\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]\,V^{\prime}(u(t))\quad\mbox{on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$}\,,

(5.16)

and then let
j\to\infty
, we deduce that

2\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\Delta\xi_{1}=W^{\prime}(\xi_{1})-\varepsilon\,\ell_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime}(\xi_{1})\quad\mbox{on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$}\,.

By step one, we conclude that
\ell_{\varepsilon}>0
, that
\xi_{1}
is strictly radially decreasing, and that

\mathcal{V}(\xi_{1})\geq\frac{1}{C\,\ell_{\varepsilon}^{n}}\,.

(5.17)

Step three
: We now iterate the construction of step two. Let us consider the following statement, depending on
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

(S)_{k}
: There are
\{(x^{i}_{j})_{j}\}_{i=1}^{k}
sequences in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|x^{i}_{j}-x_{j}^{\ell}|\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
(
i\neq\ell
), and there are
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
radially symmetric decreasing functions solving (
5.2
), such that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i})\leq 1\,,

(5.18)

and, for each
i=1,...,k
,

\displaystyle\mbox{$\tau_{(-x_{j}^{i})}[u(t_{j})]\rightharpoonup\xi_{i}$ weakly in $W^{3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$ as $j\to\infty$}\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\big\|\tau_{(-x_{j}^{i})}[u(t_{j})]-\xi_{i}\big\|_{W^{2,p}(B_{R})}=0\,,\qquad\forall R>0\,,\forall p\geq 2\,.

(5.19)

Notice that if
(S)_{k}
holds with equality in (
5.18
) for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then the theorem is proved (up to showing the validity of (
5.4
)) with
M=k
. We also notice that, in step two, we have proved that
(S)_{1}
holds. In this step we prove that if
(S)_{k}
holds for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
with strict sign in (
5.18
), that is, with

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i})<1\,,

then, up to extracting a subsequence in
j
, we can find
(x_{j}^{k+1})_{j}
and
\xi_{k+1}
such that
(S)_{k+1}
holds. Since, by step one,
\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i})\geq(1/C\,\ell_{\varepsilon})^{n}
for each
i=1,...,k+1
, we conclude that, for some
M\in\mathbb{N}
,
(S)_{M}
must hold with equality in (
5.18
).

So let us consider
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(S)_{k}
holds with strict sign in (
5.18
), and let
m=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i})
. Given
h\in\mathbb{N}
, let

A_{j}^{h}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}B_{h}(x_{j}^{i})\,.

By (
5.19
), for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
there is
j(h)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mathcal{V}(u(t_{j});B_{h}(x_{j}^{i}))\geq\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i};B_{h})-\frac{1-m}{2k}\,,\qquad\forall i=1,...,k,\forall j\geq j(h)\,,

so that if,
j\geq j(h)
,

\displaystyle 1-m

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i};B_{h})\leq\frac{1-m}{2}+1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{V}\big(u(t_{j});B_{h}(x_{j}^{i})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-m}{2}+\mathcal{V}(u(t_{j});\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus A_{j}^{h})\,,

that is

\frac{1-m}{2}\leq\mathcal{V}(u(t_{j});\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus A_{j}^{h})\,,\qquad\forall h\in\mathbb{N}\,,\forall j\geq j(h)\,,

If we now set

\beta_{j}^{h}=\max_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus A_{j}^{h}}u(t_{j})\,,

then, either
\beta_{j}^{h}\geq 1/2
or, by arguing as in step two, for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
and
j\geq j(h)
,

\displaystyle\frac{1-m}{2}\leq\mathcal{V}(u(t_{j});\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus A_{j}^{h})\leq C\,(\beta_{j}^{h})^{2/(n-1)}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus A_{j}^{h}}W(u(t_{j}))\leq C\,(\beta_{j}^{h})^{2/(n-1)}\,\varepsilon\,\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0})\,,

that is, for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
and
j\geq j(h)
,

\beta_{j}^{h}\geq\min\Big\{\frac{1}{2},\Big(\frac{1-m}{C\,\varepsilon\,\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0})}\Big)^{(n-1)/2}\Big\}=:\beta_{*}>0\,.

In particular, up to extracting a subsequence in
j
, for each
j
we can find
x_{j}^{k+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\inf_{i=1,...,k}\,|x_{j}^{k+1}-x_{j}^{i}|\geq j\,,\qquad u_{j}(x_{j}^{k+1},t_{j})\geq\beta_{*}\,,\qquad\forall j\,;

and then, thanks to (
5.9
), we can find
r_{*}>0
such that

u(t_{j})\geq\frac{\beta_{*}}{2}\quad\mbox{on $B_{r_{*}}(x_{j}^{k+1})$}\,,\qquad\forall j\,.

Hence, by (
5.10
), there exists
\xi_{k+1}\in\cap_{p\geq 2}W^{3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])\setminus\{0\}
such that

\displaystyle\mbox{$\tau_{(-x_{j}^{k+1})}[u(t_{j})]\rightharpoonup\xi_{k+1}$ weakly in $W^{3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$ as $j\to\infty$}\,,

(5.20)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\big\|\tau_{(-x_{j}^{k+1})}[u(t_{j})]-\xi_{k+1}\big\|_{W^{2,p}(B_{R})}=0\,,\qquad\forall R>0\,,\forall p\geq 2\,,

(5.21)

and by the same argument of step two we see that
\xi_{k+1}
satisfies

2\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\Delta\xi_{k+1}=W^{\prime}(\xi_{k+1})-\varepsilon\,\ell_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime}(\xi_{k+1})\quad\mbox{on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$}\,,

and thus

\mathcal{V}(\xi_{k+1})\geq\frac{1}{C\,\ell_{\varepsilon}^{n}}\,.

(5.22)

We have thus proved the desired induction step.

Step four
: We have so far proved the existence of
M\in\mathbb{N}
,
\ell_{\varepsilon}>0
,
\{(x_{j}^{i})_{j}\}_{i=1}^{M}
sequences in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
radially symmetric decreasing functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that, up to extracting a subsequence, (
5.1
), and (
5.2
) hold, together with part of (
5.3
) (i.e.,
1=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i})
), and

\displaystyle\mbox{$\tau_{(-x_{j}^{i})}[u(t_{j})]\rightharpoonup\xi_{i}$ weakly in $W^{3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$ as $j\to\infty$}\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\big\|\tau_{(-x_{j}^{i})}[u(t_{j})]-\xi_{i}\big\|_{W^{2,p}(B_{R})}=0\,,\qquad\forall R>0\,,p\geq 2\,,i=1,...,M\,.

(5.23)

To complete the proof of (
5.3
) we just need to notice that, by (
5.23
), for every
R>0

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{i};B_{R})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}\big(\tau_{-x_{j}^{i}}[u(t_{j})];B_{R}\big)=\liminf_{j\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}\big(u(t_{j});B_{R}(x_{j}^{i})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}\Big(u(t_{j});\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}B_{R}(x_{j}^{i})\Big)\leq\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0})\,,

where in the last equality we have used
|x_{j}^{i}-x_{j}^{\ell}|\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
if
i\neq\ell
, and in the last inequality we have used the monotonicity of
t\mapsto\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u(t))
. By arbitrariness of
R
, we have completed the proof of (
5.3
). We are thus left to prove (
5.4
). In this step, we shall prove a slightly weaker statement, namely

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\Big\|u(t_{j})-\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tau_{x_{i}^{j}}[\xi_{i}]\Big\|_{W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0\,,\qquad\forall p>2\,.

(5.24)

We will then improve (
5.24
) to (
5.4
) in step five. (This improvement is of crucial importance in discussing convergence to equilibrium of the flow.) We begin the proof of (
5.24
) by showing that, with
n^{\prime}=n/(n-1)
,

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|u(t_{j})-v_{j}\|_{L^{2n^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0\,,\qquad\mbox{where}\,\,v_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}]\,.

(5.25)

Indeed, for every
\sigma>0
we can find
R>0
such that

\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i};B_{R})\geq(1-\sigma)\,\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i})\,,\qquad\forall i=1,...,M\,.

(5.26)

Setting
A_{j}^{R}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{M}B_{R}(x_{j}^{i})
, for
j
large enough to have
B_{R}(x_{j}^{i})\cap B_{R}(x_{j}^{\ell})=\emptyset
(
i\neq\ell
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C(M)}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t_{j})-v_{j}|^{2n^{\prime}}\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\int_{A_{j}^{R}}|u(t_{j})|^{2n^{\prime}}+|v_{j}|^{2n^{\prime}}+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{B_{R}(x_{j}^{i})}|u(t_{j})-\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}]|^{2n^{\prime}}

(5.27)

\displaystyle\!\!+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{B_{R}(x_{j}^{i})}\Big|\sum_{\ell\neq i}\tau_{x_{j}^{\ell}}[\xi_{\ell}]\Big|^{2n^{\prime}}\,.

We first notice that, since
r^{2n^{\prime}}\leq C\,V(r)
for all
r\in[0,1]
,,

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j}^{R}}|v_{j}|^{2n^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M)\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{A_{j}^{R}}|\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}]|^{2n^{\prime}}\leq C(M)\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{j}^{i})}|\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}]|^{2n^{\prime}}

(5.28)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M)\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}V(\xi_{i})\leq C(M)\,\sigma\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i})=C(M)\,\sigma;

similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\int_{A_{j}^{R}}|u(t_{j})|^{2n^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j}^{R}}V(u(t_{j}))=1-\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{V}(u(t_{j});B_{R}(x_{j}^{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i})-\mathcal{V}(u(t_{j});B_{R}(x_{j}^{i}))=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i})-\mathcal{V}(\tau_{-x_{j}^{i}}[u(t_{j})];B_{R})

so that, by (
5.23
),

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{A_{j}^{R}}|u(t_{j})|^{2n^{\prime}}\leq C\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i};\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R})\leq C\,M\,\sigma\,.

(5.29)

Coming to the last term in (
5.27
) we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{B_{R}(x_{j}^{i})}\Big|\sum_{\ell\neq i}\tau_{x_{j}^{\ell}}[\xi_{\ell}]\Big|^{2n^{\prime}}\leq C(M)\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\sum_{\ell\neq i}\int_{B_{R}(x_{j}^{i})}\big|\tau_{x_{j}^{\ell}}[\xi_{\ell}]\big|^{2n^{\prime}}

(5.30)

\displaystyle\leq C(M)\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\sum_{\ell\neq i}\int_{B_{R}(x_{j}^{i}-x_{j}^{\ell})}\big|\xi_{\ell}\big|^{2n^{\prime}}\,,

where the last integral converges to zero since
|x_{j}^{i}-x_{j}^{\ell}|\to 0
as
j\to\infty
by
i\neq\ell
, and since
\xi_{\ell}\in L^{2n^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Finally, since
2n^{\prime}\geq 2
we can use (
5.23
) to address the second integral in (
5.27
), and conclude from (
5.27
), (
5.28
), (
5.29
) and (
5.30
) that

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\|u(t_{j})-v_{j}\|_{L^{2n^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(M)\,\sigma\,,\qquad\forall\sigma>0\,,

that is (
5.25
). Now, by (
3.3
) we have that

\|u(t_{j})-v_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(\varepsilon)\,\Big\{\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u(t_{j}))+\mathcal{V}(u(t_{j}))+\sum_{i=1}M\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{i})+\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i})\Big\}\leq C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,,

so that (
5.25
) implies

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|u(t_{j})-v_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0\,,\qquad\forall p>2\,.

(5.31)

We finally recall that given
k,k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and
p,p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty]
related by
k=\theta\,k_{1}+(1-\theta)\,k_{2}
and
(1/p)=(\theta/p_{1})+(1-\theta)/p_{2}
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
, then we have

\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\,\|f\|_{W^{k_{1},p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\,\|f\|_{W^{k_{2},p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\theta}

for every
f\in W^{k_{1},p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap W^{k_{2},p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and with a constant
C
depending only on
n
,
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
. By combining this interpolation inequality (with
k=1,2
,
k_{1}=0
,
k_{2}=3
, any
p_{1}>2
and
p_{2}\geq 2
) with (
5.31
) and the uniform
W^{3,p}
-bounds satisfied by
u(t_{j})
(recall (
5.10
)) and each of the
\xi_{i}
’s, we conclude the proof of (
5.24
).

Step five
: To complete the proof of (
5.4
) we need to prove the
W^{1,2}
-convergence stated in (
5.24
), that is

\lim_{j\to\infty}\Big\|u(t_{j})-\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}]\Big\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0\,.

(5.32)

To this end we set, for the sake of brevity,

u_{j}=u(t_{j})\,,\qquad v_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}]\,,\qquad w_{j}=u_{j}-v_{j}=u(t_{j})-\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}]\,,

as well as

Z(r)=W^{\prime}(r)-\varepsilon\,\ell_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime}(r)\,,\qquad\forall r\in[0,1]\,.

If we multiply by
w_{j}
in
\varepsilon^{2}(\partial_{t}u_{j}-2\,\Delta u_{j})=-Z(u_{j})+\varepsilon\,(\ell_{\varepsilon}-\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u_{j}])\,V^{\prime}(u_{j})
and in
2\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\Delta v_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}Z(\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}])
, and we then add the resulting identities, we obtain

\displaystyle-2\,\varepsilon^{2}\,w_{j}\,\Delta w_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{2}\,w_{j}\,\partial_{t}u_{j}+\varepsilon\,(\ell_{\varepsilon}-\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u_{j}])\,w_{j}\,V^{\prime}(u_{j})

(5.33)

\displaystyle+w_{j}\Big\{-Z(u_{j})+\sum_{i=1}^{M}Z(\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}])\Big\}

By (
3.3
),
\mathcal{V}(u_{j})=1
,
\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u_{j})\leq\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0})
,
\mathcal{V}(\xi_{i})\leq 1
, and
\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{i})\leq\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0})
we have
\|w_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(\varepsilon,u_{0})
, so that
|w_{j}|\leq M+1
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and (
5.31
) imply

\|w_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(\varepsilon,u_{0},M)\,,\qquad\lim_{j\to\infty}\|w_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0\,,\qquad\forall p>2\,.

(5.34)

Combining (
5.34
) and
0\leq V^{\prime}(u_{j})\leq C\,u_{j}^{2n^{\prime}-1}
with (
5.33
) and the Hölder inequality, we thus find

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w_{j}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon,u_{0},M)\,\|\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,\big|\ell_{\varepsilon}-\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u_{j}]\big|\,\|w_{j}\|_{L^{2n^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(5.35)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w_{j}\,\Big\{-Z(u_{j})+\sum_{i=1}^{M}Z(\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}])\Big\}\,.

where we have also used
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2n^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(\varepsilon,u_{0})
for all
t>0
. The first two terms on the right-hand side of (
5.35
) converge to zero as
j\to\infty
, respectively, thanks to Theorem
1.2
-(vi) and to (
5.1
) and (
5.34
). To deal with the third term on the right hand side of (
5.35
), we consider
\sigma>0
and pick
R>0
as in (
5.26
), so that, as proved in (
5.28
) and (
5.29
), we have

\int_{A_{j}^{R}}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}]^{2n^{\prime}}\leq C(M)\,\sigma\,,\qquad\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{A_{j}^{R}}u_{j}^{2n^{\prime}}\leq C(M)\,\sigma\,,

(5.36)

where
A_{j}^{R}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{M}B_{R}(x_{j}^{i})
. Moreover, up to further increase the value of
R
we can ensure that

\max_{i=1,...,M}\|\xi_{i}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(p)\,\sigma\,,\qquad\forall p\geq 2\,,

(5.37)

as well as

\mbox{$|\xi_{i}|\leq\kappa(\varepsilon)$ on $\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}$, where $\kappa(\varepsilon)>0$ is s.t. $Z^{\prime}(r)\geq\kappa(\varepsilon)$ for all $r\in[0,\kappa]$}\,.

(5.38)

(Notice that the existence of
\kappa(\varepsilon)
is guaranteed by
W^{\prime\prime}(0)>0
,
V^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
, and
|\ell_{\varepsilon}|\leq C(u_{0})
, which in turn follows from
\sup_{j}|\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u_{j}]|\leq C(u_{0})
(recall Theorem
1.2
-(ii)) and (
5.1
).

Now, provided
j
is large enough depending on
R
(thus on
\sigma
),
\{A_{j}^{R},B_{R}(x_{i}^{j})\}_{i=1}^{M}
is a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that we can use to decompose the third term on the right hand side of (
5.35
). We begin by working with the integration over
A_{j}^{R}
, that we decompose by assigning a privileged role to
\xi_{1}
, and writing

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j}^{R}}w_{j}\,\Big\{-Z(u_{j})+\sum_{i=1}^{M}Z(\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}])\Big\}=\int_{A_{j}^{R}}\big(u_{j}-\tau_{x_{j}^{1}}[\xi_{1}]\big)\,\big(Z(\tau_{x_{j}^{1}}[\xi_{1}])-Z(u_{j})\big)

\displaystyle-\int_{A_{j}^{R}}\sum_{\ell=2}^{M}\,\tau_{x_{j}^{\ell}}[\xi_{\ell}]\,\Big\{-Z(u_{j})+\sum_{i=1}^{M}Z(\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}])\Big\}-\int_{A_{j}^{R}}\big(u_{j}-\tau_{x_{j}^{1}}[\xi_{1}]\big)\sum_{\ell=2}^{M}\,Z(\tau_{x_{j}^{\ell}}[\xi_{\ell}])

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A_{j}^{R}}\big(u_{j}-\tau_{x_{j}^{1}}[\xi_{1}]\big)\,\big(Z(\tau_{x_{j}^{1}}[\xi_{1}])-Z(u_{j})\big)

(5.39)

\displaystyle+C(M)\,\max_{1\leq i\leq M}\|\xi_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R})}\,\max_{1\leq i\leq M}\big\{\|u_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\|\xi_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big\}\,,

where we have used
|Z(r)|\leq C\,r
for all
r\in[0,1]
. Now by the properties of
W
and
V
we have

Z(r)-Z(s)=Z^{\prime}(s)\,(r-s)+{\rm O}(r-s)^{2n^{\prime}-1}\,,\qquad\forall r,s\in[0,1]\,,

(5.40)

so that by plugging
s=\xi_{1}
and
r=u_{j}
in (
5.40
), and by recalling that, thanks to (
5.38
),
Z^{\prime}(\tau_{x_{j}^{1}}[\xi_{1}])\geq\kappa(\varepsilon)
on
A_{j}^{R}
,

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j}^{R}}\big(u_{j}-\tau_{x_{j}^{1}}[\xi_{1}]\big)\,\big(Z(\tau_{x_{j}^{1}}[\xi_{1}])-Z(u_{j})\big)

(5.41)

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{A_{j}^{R}}\big(u_{j}-\tau_{x_{j}^{1}}[\xi_{1}]\big)^{2}\,Z^{\prime}(\tau_{x_{j}^{1}}[\xi_{1}])+C\,\int_{A_{j}^{R}}|u_{j}-\tau_{x_{j}^{1}}[\xi_{1}]|^{2n^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq-\kappa(\varepsilon)\,\int_{A_{j}^{R}}\big(u_{j}-\tau_{x_{j}^{1}}[\xi_{1}]\big)^{2}+C\,\int_{A_{j}^{R}}|u_{j}-\tau_{x_{j}^{1}}[\xi_{1}]|^{2n^{\prime}}\,.

Now, for every
p\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{A_{j}^{R}}|u_{j}-\tau_{x_{j}^{1}}[\xi_{1}]|^{p}-\int_{A_{j}^{R}}|w_{j}|^{p}\Big|\leq C(p)\,\sum_{i=2}^{M}\int_{A_{j}^{R}}\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}]^{p}\leq C(p)\,\sum_{i=2}^{M}\|\xi_{i}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R})}\,,

(5.42)

so that, combining (
5.41
) and (
5.42
) with (
5.39
), we finally conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j}^{R}}w_{j}\,\Big\{-Z(u_{j})+\sum_{i=1}^{M}Z(\tau_{x_{j}^{i}}[\xi_{i}])\Big\}\leq-\kappa(\varepsilon)\,\int_{A_{j}^{R}}w_{j}^{2}+C(p,M)\,\sigma\,.

Going back to (

7.9
) to obtain

\displaystyle 2\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\,\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}\,\psi\,(\hat{x}\cdot\nabla\psi)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi^{2}\,(\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime})^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi^{2}\,(\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\,\Big\{\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})-\varepsilon\,\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})}{2\,\varepsilon^{2}}+\frac{n-1}{|x|^{2}}\Big\}\,.

On combining this last identity with (
7.8
) and the definition of
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]
we thus find

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,\varepsilon\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})}{\varepsilon}-\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})\Big\}\,\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,\varepsilon\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\,|\nabla\psi|^{2}+(\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime})^{2}\,\psi^{2}

\displaystyle-2\,\varepsilon\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi^{2}\,(\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime})^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi^{2}\,(\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\,\Big\{\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})}{\varepsilon}-\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})+2\,\varepsilon\,\frac{n-1}{|x|^{2}}\Big\}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})}{\varepsilon}-\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})\Big\}\,\varphi^{2}

which boils down to (
7.7
) thanks to
\varphi=\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\,\psi
.

Step two
: We prove that conclusion (
1.26
) follows from conclusion (
1.25
). Indeed, arguing by contradiction, should (
1.24
) imply (
1.25
) but not (
1.26
), then we could find a sequence
(\varphi_{j})_{j}
in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi_{j}\,V^{\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})=0\,,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi_{j}\,\nabla\zeta_{\varepsilon}=0\,,\qquad\forall j\,,

(7.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\varphi_{j}|^{2}+\varphi_{j}^{2}=1\,,\qquad\forall j\,,

(7.12)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi_{j})=0\,.

(7.13)

Having assumed that (
1.24
) implies (
1.25
), we could deduce from (
7.11
) that

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi_{j})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{C}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi_{j}^{2}\,,\qquad\forall j\,.

(7.14)

By combining (
7.14
) with (
7.13
) we would then find

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi_{j}^{2}=0\,,\quad\mbox{and, hence, by \eqref{p1},}\,\,\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\varphi_{j}|^{2}=1\,.

(7.15)

But then, taking into account that
(W^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})/\varepsilon-\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon}))\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and combining (
7.5
) and (
7.15
), we could conclude that
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi_{j})\to 2\,\varepsilon
as
j\to\infty
, in contradiction with (
7.13
).

Step three
: By step two, we are left to prove that (
1.24
) implies (
1.25
). In this step, we present an additional reduction. Let
L^{2}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the set of radial functions with respect to the origin belonging to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and let

\displaystyle W^{1,2}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!L^{2}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,,

\displaystyle Z\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!L^{2}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\perp}\cap W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,,

\displaystyle Z^{*}\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\Big\{\varphi\in Z:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi\,\nabla\zeta_{\varepsilon}=0\Big\}\,.

We prove that if

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi)\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{C}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi^{2}\,,\qquad\forall\varphi\in Z^{*}\,,

(7.16)

then (
1.24
) implies (
1.25
) (and the theorem is proved).

To begin with, denoting by
\varphi_{\rm rad}
and
\varphi_{\rm rad}^{\perp}
the
L^{2}
-projections of
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
on, respectively,
W^{1,2}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
Z
, so that
\varphi=\varphi_{\rm rad}+\varphi_{\rm rad}^{\perp}
, we notice that

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi)=\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi_{\rm rad})+\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi_{\rm rad}^{\perp})\,,\qquad\forall\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,.

(7.17)

Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\big(\varphi_{\rm rad},\varphi_{\rm rad}^{\perp}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}2\,\varepsilon\,\nabla\varphi_{\rm rad}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{\rm rad}^{\perp}+\Big(\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})}{\varepsilon}-\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})\Big)\varphi_{\rm rad}\,\varphi_{\rm rad}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{-2\,\varepsilon\,\Delta\varphi_{\rm rad}+\Big(\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})}{\varepsilon}-\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})\Big)\varphi_{\rm rad}\Big\}\,\varphi_{\rm rad}^{\perp}=0\,,

where in the last identity we have used that
\varphi_{\rm rad}^{\perp}\in Z
and the fact that the function in the curly bracket is radial.

Next, we observe that the orthogonality relations in (
1.24
) can be equivalently reformulated as follows for any
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V^{\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})\,\varphi=0\qquad\mbox{if and only if}\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V^{\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})\,\varphi_{\rm rad}=0\,,

(7.18)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi\,\nabla\zeta_{\varepsilon}=0\qquad\mbox{if and only if}\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi_{\rm rad}^{\perp}\,\nabla\zeta_{\varepsilon}=0\,,

(7.19)

since
V^{\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})\in L^{2}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\nabla\zeta_{\varepsilon}=\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\,\hat{x}
, and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{x}\,\psi=0
for all
\psi\in L^{2}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In summary, by combining in the order (
7.17
), (
7.1
) applied to
\varphi_{\rm rad}
(as we can do thanks to (
7.18
)) and (
7.16
) to
\varphi_{\rm rad}^{\perp}
(as we can do since
\varphi_{\rm rad}^{\perp}\in Z^{*}
by (
8.11
)) we deduce that

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi)=\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi_{\rm rad})+\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi_{\rm rad}^{\perp})\geq\frac{1}{C\,\varepsilon}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\varphi_{\rm rad})^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{C}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\varphi_{\rm rad}^{\perp})^{2}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{C}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi^{2}\,,

that is (
1.25
).

Step four
: We now begin the proof of (
7.16
) by introducing a Fourier decomposition of
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that is particularly convenient when
\varphi\in Z^{*}
. Since the radial Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e. the map
B(r)\mapsto B^{\prime\prime}(r)+(n-1)\,B^{\prime}(r)/r
, defines a self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}((0,\infty),r^{n-1}\,dr)
, we can consider an orthonormal basis
\{B_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
of
L^{2}((0,\infty),r^{n-1}\,dr)
made up of its eigenfunctions. Since
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and
L^{2}((0,\infty),r^{n-1}\,dr)
are separable Hilbert spaces, it follows that

\{A_{i}\otimes B_{j}\}_{i,j=0}^{\infty}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(K;\kappa)
, where
K=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times(0,\infty)
and
\kappa=(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\times(r^{n-1}\,dr)
. In particular, since
\Phi:K\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\Phi(\theta,r)=\theta\,r
, naturally induces an isometry between
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
L^{2}(K,\kappa)
, we conclude that
\{(A_{i}\otimes B_{j})\circ\Phi\}_{i,j=0}^{\infty}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, each
a_{ij}=(A_{i}\otimes B_{j})\circ\Phi
is an eigenfunction of the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, so that the orthogonality of
a_{ij}
and
a_{hk}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(which holds true for
i\neq h
or
j\neq k
) implies the orthogonality of
a_{ij}
and
a_{hk}
in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In summary, whenever
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have

\displaystyle\varphi=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{ij}\,a_{ij}\qquad\mbox{in $W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$}\,,

\displaystyle\mbox{where}\,\,\varphi_{ij}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a_{ij}\,\varphi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{i}(\hat{x})\,B_{j}(|x|)\,\varphi(x)\,dx\,.

If we define
\varphi_{i}^{*}\in W^{1,2}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\varphi_{i}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

\varphi_{i}^{*}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{ij}\,B_{j}(|x|)\,,\qquad\varphi_{i}(x)=A_{i}(\hat{x})\,\varphi_{i}^{*}(x)\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,

then we have

\varphi=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{i}\qquad\mbox{in $W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$}\,,

(7.20)

as well as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\varphi_{i}|^{2}\,,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\,\varphi^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\,\varphi_{i}^{2}\,,

(7.21)

for every
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that is either radial or angular (i.e.,
f\circ\Phi
depends either on
r
or on
\theta
only). We can also notice that

\varphi_{0}=\varphi_{0}^{*}=0\qquad\forall\varphi\in Z\,,

(7.22)

and then, since
A_{0}
is a constant, if
\varphi\in Z
, then for every
j\in\mathbb{N}

\varphi_{0j}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{0}(|\hat{x}|)\,B_{j}(|x|)\,\varphi(x)\,dx=A_{0}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}B_{j}(|x|)\,\varphi(x)\,dx=0\,.

In particular, by applying (
7.21
) with
f=(W^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})/\varepsilon)-\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})
and (
7.22
) we find

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi_{i})\,,\qquad\forall\varphi\in Z\,.

(7.23)

We make two claims:

Claim one
: if
\varphi\in Z^{*}
and
i\geq n+1
, then

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi_{i})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{C}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varphi_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,.

(7.24)

Claim two
: if
\varphi\in Z^{*}
and
i=1,...,n
, then

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi_{i})\geq\frac{1}{C\,\varepsilon}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi_{i}^{2}\,.

(7.25)

We first show how to complete the proof of the theorem starting from these two claims, and then we prove the claims themselves.

Conclusion of the theorem from the claims
: Since
W^{\prime\prime}(0)>0
,
\zeta_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
(uniformly on
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
), and
\varepsilon\,\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we see that there are universal constants
\kappa
and
R_{1}
such that

W^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})-\varepsilon\,\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})\geq\kappa\,,\qquad\mbox{on $\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R_{1}}$}\,,

(7.26)

for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
. By (
7.24
),

\int_{B_{R_{1}}}\varphi_{i}^{2}\leq\frac{C\,R_{1}^{2}}{\varepsilon}\,\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi_{i})\,,\qquad\forall i\geq n+1\,,

(7.27)

so that, combining (
7.22
), (
7.27
) and (
7.25
) we conclude that

\int_{B_{R_{1}}}\varphi^{2}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\,\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi)\,,\qquad\forall\varphi\in Z^{*}\,.

(7.28)

Now let
L=\max_{B_{R_{1}}}|W^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})-\varepsilon\,\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})|
. If

\frac{\kappa}{2\,L}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R_{1}}}\varphi^{2}\leq\int_{B_{R_{1}}}\varphi^{2}\,,

then we deduce
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi^{2}\leq(C/\varepsilon)\,\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi)
by (
7.28
); if, instead,

\frac{\kappa}{2\,L}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R_{1}}}\varphi^{2}>\int_{B_{R_{1}}}\varphi^{2}\,,

(7.29)

then by (
7.26
) and (
7.29
) we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})}{\varepsilon}-\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon})\Big\}\,\varphi^{2}\geq\frac{\kappa}{\varepsilon}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R_{1}}}\varphi^{2}-\frac{L}{\varepsilon}\,\int_{B_{R_{1}}}\,\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{2\,\varepsilon}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R_{1}}}\varphi^{2}\,,

which combined with (
7.28
) gives again
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi^{2}\leq(C/\varepsilon)\,\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi)
, as desired. We are thus left to prove the two claims.

Proof of claim one
: Setting
\psi_{i}=\varphi_{i}/\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we see that
\psi_{i}(r\,\theta)=A_{i}(\theta)\,\xi_{i}(r)
(where
\xi_{i}=\varphi_{i}^{*}/\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
), with

|\nabla\psi_{i}|^{2}(r\,\theta)=|\nabla^{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}A_{i}(\theta)|^{2}\,\frac{\xi_{i}(r)^{2}}{r^{2}}+|A_{i}(\theta)|^{2}\,\xi_{i}^{\prime}(r)^{2}\,.

Since
i\geq n+1
we can exploit (
7.3
) which, combined with (
7.7
), gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi_{i})}{2\,\varepsilon}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\,\Big\{|\nabla\psi_{i}|^{2}-\frac{(n-1)}{|x|^{2}}\psi_{i}^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(r)^{2}\,\Big\{\Big(\frac{\xi_{i}(r)^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\nabla^{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}A_{i}|^{2}-(n-1)\,A_{i}^{2}\Big)+\xi_{i}^{\prime}(r)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}A_{i}^{2}\Big\}\,r^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c(n)\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(r)^{2}\,\Big\{\Big(\frac{\xi_{i}(r)^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\nabla^{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}A_{i}|^{2}+\,A_{i}^{2}\Big)+\xi_{i}^{\prime}(r)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}A_{i}^{2}\Big\}\,r^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c(n)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\,\Big\{|\nabla\psi_{i}|^{2}+\frac{\psi_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\Big\}\geq c(n)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\,\frac{\psi_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}=c(n)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varphi_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,,

that is (
7.24
).

Proof of claim two
: Since
\varphi\in Z^{*}
we have, for each
i=1,...,n
,

0=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{x}_{i}\,\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\,\varphi=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{x}_{i}\,\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\,\varphi_{k}\,,

which, combined with
\varphi_{k}(x)=A_{k}(\hat{x})\,\varphi_{k}^{*}(|x|)=c(n)\,\hat{x}_{k}\,\varphi_{k}^{*}(|x|)
, gives

\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\varphi_{i}^{*}(r)\,\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(r)\,r^{n-1}\,dr=0\,,\qquad\forall i=1,...,n\,.

(7.30)

We now prove, if
\varphi\in Z^{*}
, then

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}](\varphi_{i})\geq\frac{1}{C\,\varepsilon}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\varphi_{i}^{*})^{2}\,,\qquad\forall i=1,...,n\,.

(7.31)

Notice that (
7.31
) implies (
7.25
) since
\varphi_{i}\leq c(n)\,\varphi_{i}^{*}
for
i=1,...,n
.

We prove (
7.31
) by contradiction, following closely the proof of
[
MR24
, Lemma 4.4]
. Indeed, should (
7.31
) fail, then there would be sequences
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0^{+}
as
j\to\infty
and
(\varphi_{j})_{j}
in
Z^{*}
such that (up to rotations taking
i_{j}\in\{1,...,n\}
to
i_{j}=1
for all
j
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}((\varphi_{j})_{1}^{*})^{2}=1\,,\qquad\forall j\,,

(7.32)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\,(\varphi_{j})_{1}^{*}(r)\,\zeta_{\varepsilon_{j}}^{\prime}(r)\,r^{n-1}\,dr=0\,,\qquad\forall j\,,

(7.33)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon_{j}}[\zeta_{\varepsilon_{j}}]\big((\varphi_{j})_{1}\big)=0\,.

(7.34)

Setting
R_{0}=1/\omega_{n}^{1/n}
and

\beta_{j}(s)=(\varphi_{j}^{*})_{1}(R_{0}+\varepsilon_{j}\,s)\,,\qquad\eta_{j}(s)=\zeta_{\varepsilon_{j}}(R_{0}+\varepsilon_{j}\,s)\,,\qquad s\in\mathbb{R}\,,

we can rewrite (
7.32
) and (
7.33
) as

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}\beta_{j}(s)^{2}\,(R_{0}+\varepsilon_{j}\,s)^{n-1}\,ds=1\,,\qquad\forall j\,,

(7.35)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}\beta_{j}(s)\,\eta_{j}^{\prime}(s)\,(R_{0}+\varepsilon_{j}\,s)^{n-1}\,ds=0\,,\qquad\forall j\,,

(7.36)

where
I_{j}=(-R_{0}/\varepsilon_{j},\infty)
. Concerning (
7.34
) we notice that by
(\varphi_{j})_{1}(x)=A_{1}(\hat{x})\,(\varphi_{j})_{1}^{*}(|x|)
it follows that

|\nabla(\varphi_{j})_{1}|^{2}=|\nabla^{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,A_{1}|^{2}\,\big((\varphi_{j})_{1}^{*}\big)^{2}+A_{1}^{2}\,|\nabla(\varphi_{j})_{1}^{*}|^{2}

so that, as
\|A_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}=1
,

\varepsilon_{j}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla(\varphi_{j})_{1}|^{2}\geq\varepsilon_{j}\,\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}A_{1}^{2}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\,|\nabla(\varphi_{j})_{1}^{*}|^{2}(r)\,r^{n-1}\,dr=\int_{I_{j}}\beta_{j}^{\prime}(s)^{2}\,(R_{0}+\varepsilon_{j}\,s)^{n-1}\,ds\,.

Again by Fubini’s theorem and thanks to
\|A_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}=1
we find

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon_{j}})}{\varepsilon_{j}}-\Lambda_{\varepsilon_{j}}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\zeta_{\varepsilon_{j}})\Big\}\,(\varphi_{j})_{1}^{2}=\int_{I_{j}}\big\{W^{\prime\prime}(\eta_{j})-\varepsilon_{j}\,\Lambda_{\varepsilon_{j}}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\eta_{j})\big\}\,\beta_{j}^{2}\,(R_{0}+\varepsilon_{j}\,s)^{n-1}\,ds

We can thus deduce from (
7.34
) that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{I_{j}}\Big((\beta_{j}^{\prime})^{2}+\big\{W^{\prime\prime}(\eta_{j})-\varepsilon_{j}\,\Lambda_{\varepsilon_{j}}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\eta_{j})\big\}\,\beta_{j}^{2}\Big)\,(R_{0}+\varepsilon_{j}\,s)^{n-1}\,ds=0\,;

(7.37)

and, in fact, by taking into account that
\varepsilon_{j}\,\Lambda_{\varepsilon_{j}}\to 0^{+}
as
j\to\infty
and that
|V^{\prime\prime}|\leq C
on
[0,1]
, we see from (
7.35
) that (
7.37
) is equivalent to

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{I_{j}}\Big((\beta_{j}^{\prime})^{2}+W^{\prime\prime}(\eta_{j})\,\beta_{j}^{2}\Big)\,(R_{0}+\varepsilon_{j}\,s)^{n-1}\,ds=0\,.

(7.38)

In turn, since
|W^{\prime\prime}|\leq C
on
[0,1]
, by combining (
7.35
) with (
7.38
) we see that
(\beta_{j})_{j}
is bounded in
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})
. Hence, up to extracting a subsequence, we can find
\beta\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\beta_{j}\rightharpoonup\beta
weakly in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R})
.

In this position, we can repeat
verbatim
two arguments contained in the proof of
[
MR24
, Lemma 4.4]
. The first argument shows that the sequence of probability measures
(\mu_{j})_{j}
defined by
\mu_{j}=\beta_{j}^{2}\,(R_{0}+\varepsilon_{j}\,s)^{n-1}\,ds
is in the compactness case of the concentration-compactness principle, and thus satisfies

\lim_{s\to\infty}\,\sup_{j}\,\mu_{j}\big(\mathbb{R}\setminus(-s,s)\big)=0\,.

(7.39)

The second argument shows that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{I_{j}}W^{\prime\prime}(\eta_{j})\,\beta_{j}^{2}\,(R_{0}+\varepsilon_{j}\,s)^{n-1}\,ds=R_{0}^{n-1}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,W^{\prime\prime}(\eta_{0})\,\beta^{2}\,,

(7.40)

where
\eta_{0}(s)=\eta(s-\tau_{0})
,
\eta
is the unique solution of
-\eta^{\prime}=\sqrt{W(\eta)}
on
\mathbb{R}
with
\eta(0)=1/2
, and
\tau_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,V^{\prime}(\eta(s))\,\eta(s)\,s\,ds
.

By (
7.39
), (
7.35
), (
7.40
) and (
7.38
) we thus find

\displaystyle R_{0}^{n-1}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,\beta^{2}=1\,,

(7.41)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,2\,(\beta^{\prime})^{2}+W^{\prime\prime}(\eta_{0})\,\beta^{2}\leq 0\,.

(7.42)

By
[
MR24
, Lemma 4.3]
, (
7.42
) implies that
\beta(s+\tau_{0})=t\,\eta^{\prime}(s)
for some
t\neq 0
(the case
t=0
is ruled out by (
7.41
)). In other words,
\beta=t\,\eta_{0}^{\prime}
.

We now claim that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{I_{j}}\beta_{j}\,\eta_{j}^{\prime}\,(R_{0}+\varepsilon_{j}\,s)^{n-1}\,ds=R_{0}^{n-1}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,\beta\,\eta_{0}^{\prime}\,.

(7.43)

Indeed, by
|\eta_{j}^{\prime}(s)|\leq C\,e^{-|s|/C}
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and by (
7.39
) we see that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{I_{j}\setminus(-s_{0},s_{0})}\beta_{j}\,\eta_{j}^{\prime}\,(R_{0}+\varepsilon_{j}\,s)^{n-1}\,ds\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{I_{j}}(\eta_{j}^{\prime})^{2}\,(R_{0}+\varepsilon_{j}\,s)^{n-1}\,ds\Big)^{1/2}\,\mu_{j}\big(I_{j}\setminus(-s_{0},s_{0})\big)^{1/2}\leq\omega(s_{0})\,,

for some function
\omega
, independent of
j
, such that
\omega(s)\to 0^{+}
as
s\to\infty
. Similarly

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-s_{0},s_{0})}\,\beta\,\eta_{0}^{\prime}\Big|\leq\omega(s_{0})\,,

and therefore (
7.43
) follows since, as
j\to\infty
,
\beta_{j}\to\beta
in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})
and by
\eta_{j}^{\prime}\to\eta^{\prime}_{0}
locally uniformly on
\mathbb{R}
. On combining
\beta=t\,\eta_{0}^{\prime}
with (
7.36
) and (
7.43
) we conclude that

0=t\,R_{0}^{n-1}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,(\eta_{0}^{\prime})^{2}\,,

and thus, that
\eta\equiv{\rm constant}
, reaching a contradiction.
∎

8.
Exponential convergence to a single diffused bubble (Proof of Theorem
1.1
)

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We are proving the theorem by showing the existence if
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
u_{0}
is as in the statement, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u(t))-\Psi(\varepsilon,1)\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,e^{-t/C_{*}(\varepsilon)}\,,

(8.1)

\displaystyle\big\|u(t)-\tau_{x_{0}}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,e^{-t/C(\varepsilon)}\,,\qquad\forall t>1/C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,.

(8.2)

By the assumptions on
u_{0}
, Theorem
1.4
holds with
M=1
. In particular, the only accumulation points for the sequences
(\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u(t_{j})))_{j}
and
(\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t_{j})])_{j}
corresponding to any
t_{j}\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
are, respectively,
\Psi(\varepsilon,1)
and
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}
, so that we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u(t))=\Psi(\varepsilon,1)\,,\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]=\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\,.

(8.3)

Step one
: We prove that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\big\|u(t)-\tau_{x(t)}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\big\|_{(W^{1,2}\cap C^{0})(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0\,.

(8.4)

where, for each
t>0
, we have defined
x(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
so that

\big\|u(t)-\tau_{x(t)}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\big\|u(t)-\tau_{x}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,.

Indeed, by (
8.3
), if
(t_{j})_{j}
is an arbitrary sequence with
t_{j}\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
, then
((u(t_{j}))_{j}
is a minimizing sequence of
\Psi(\varepsilon,1)
. Now, in
[
MR24
, step two, proof of Theorem 2.1]
it is proved that if
u_{j}
is a minimizing sequence of
\Psi(\varepsilon,1)
, then, up to extracting subsequences, there is
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\big\|u_{j}-\tau_{x}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\big\|_{(W^{1,2}\cap C^{0})(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0\,.

By combining this fact with the definition of
x(t)
we conclude the proof of (
8.4
).

Step two
: We introduce the Fisher information of the flow

\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)=\varepsilon\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(\partial_{t}u(t)\big)^{2}\,,\qquad\forall t>0\,,

(8.5)

and prove that

\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,e^{-t/C(\varepsilon)}\,,\qquad\forall t>1/C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,.

(8.6)

Indeed, by Theorem
1.2
-(vi) (see, in particular, (
1.18
)) we have that
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,1}(a,\infty)
for every
a>0
, with

\frac{d}{dt}\,\frac{\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)}{2}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{2\,\varepsilon\,|\nabla(\partial_{t}u)|^{2}+\Big(\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\varepsilon}-\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]\,V^{\prime\prime}(u)\Big)\,(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\Big\}\,.

(8.7)

By
W^{\prime\prime}\in{\rm Lip}[0,1]
,
V^{\prime\prime}\in C^{0,\alpha(n)}[0,1]
, and
0<\Lambda_{\varepsilon}<C
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
(recall (
2.6
)) we find that, pointwise on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\Big|\Big(\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(u(t))}{\varepsilon}-\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]\,V^{\prime\prime}(u(t))\Big)-\Big(\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(\tau_{x(t)}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}])}{\varepsilon}-\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\tau_{x(t)}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}])\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\,\Big\{\frac{|u(t)-\tau_{x(t)}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]|}{\varepsilon}+\big|\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]-\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\big|+\big|u(t)-\tau_{x(t)}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\big|^{\alpha(n)}\Big\}\,.

Combining this inequality with (
8.7
) we find that

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dt}\,\frac{\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)}{2}\geq\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\tau_{x(t)}\big[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\big]\big(\partial_{t}u(t)\big)

\displaystyle-C\,\Big\{\frac{\|u-\tau_{x(t)}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\varepsilon}+\big|\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]-\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\big|+\big\|u-\tau_{x(t)}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\big\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\alpha(n)}\Big\}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial_{t}u(t))^{2}\,,

so that (
8.3
) and (
8.4
) imply the existence, for every
\eta>0
, of a positive constant
t_{*}=t_{*}(\varepsilon,u_{0},\eta)
, such that

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dt}\,\frac{\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)}{2}\geq\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\tau_{x(t)}\big[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\big]\big(\partial_{t}u(t)\big)-\eta\,\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)\,,\qquad\forall t>t_{*}\,.

(8.8)

Now let
P_{t}
denote the projection operator of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
onto its closed subspace

Y_{t}=\Big\{\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V^{\prime}\big(\tau_{x(t)}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\big)\,\varphi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{i}\big(\tau_{x(t)}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\big)\,\varphi=0\Big\}\,.

By Theorem
1.5
we have

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}[\tau_{x(t)}\big[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\big]\big(\partial_{t}u(t)\big)\geq\frac{1}{C(\varepsilon)}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(P_{t}[\partial_{t}u(t)]\big)^{2}\,.

(8.9)

To get a control on
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)
we thus need to estimate
\|\partial_{t}u(t)-P_{t}[\partial_{t}u(t)]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

To this end, let us begin by noticing that, since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V^{\prime}(u(t))\,\partial_{t}u(t)=0
for all
t>0
, for every
\delta>0
there is
t_{1}=t_{1}(\delta,u_{0})>0
such that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V^{\prime}(\tau_{x(t)}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}])\,\partial_{t}u(t)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,\|u(t)-\tau_{x(t)}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(8.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta\,\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,\qquad\forall t>t_{1}\,.

On the other hand, testing (
DF
) with
\partial_{i}u(t)\in W^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we find that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}u(t)\,\nabla_{e_{i}}u(t)=2\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta u(t)\,\partial_{i}u(t)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{i}\big(W(u(t))-\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]\,V(u(t))\big)\,,

(notice that
\lambda_{\varepsilon}[u(t)]
is a function of
t
alone, and is not affected by differentiation along
e_{i}
here), and since
W(u(t)),V(u(t))\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
t>0
, we conclude that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}u(t)\,\partial_{i}u(t)=2\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta u(t)\,\partial_{i}u(t)=-2\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla u(t)\cdot\partial_{i}(\nabla u(t))=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{i}|\nabla u(t)|^{2}=0\,,

for all
t>0
. Combining this identity with (
8.4
) we thus conclude that, up to further increase the value of
t_{1}=t_{1}(\delta,u_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{i}\big(\tau_{x(t)}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\big)\,\partial_{t}u(t)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,\|\partial_{i}u(t)-\partial_{i}\tau_{x(t)}[\zeta_{\varepsilon}]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(8.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta\,\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,\qquad\forall t>t_{1}\,,i=1,...,n\,.

Therefore, by choosing
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
small enough in terms of the constant
C(\varepsilon)
appearing in (
8.9
), we conclude from (
8.10
) and (
8.11
) that, if
t>t_{1}=t_{1}(\delta(\varepsilon),u_{0})=t_{1}(\varepsilon,u_{0})
, then, for a positive constant

depending only on
\varepsilon
,

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}\big[\tau_{x(t)}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\big](\partial_{t}u(t))\geq\frac{\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{t}u(t))}{C_{*}(\varepsilon)}\,,\qquad\forall t>t_{1}\,.

(8.12)

If we now choose
\eta=1/(2\,C_{*}(\varepsilon))
in (

), and correspondingly set

, then, we deduce from (

) that

from which (

) immediately follows.

Conclusion
: By Theorem
1.2
-(iv),

\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u(T))-\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u(t))=-\varepsilon\,\int_{t}^{T}\,ds\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial_{t}u(s))^{2}\,,\qquad\forall T>t>0\,.

Combining this identity with (
8.3
) and (

) we find that, if

, then

\mathcal{AC}_{\varepsilon}(u(t))-\Psi(\varepsilon,1)=\int_{t}^{\infty}\,\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds\leq C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-s/C(\varepsilon)}\,ds\leq C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,e^{-t/C(\varepsilon)}\,,

thus proving (
8.1
). Next we notice that if
1/C(\varepsilon,u_{0})<t<T
, then by combining the fundamental theorem of Calculus with the Minkowski inequality, the Hölder inequality and then with (
8.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|u(T)-u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}\partial_{t}u(s)ds\Big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}u(s)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{t+k}^{t+k+1}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}u(s)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,ds\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{t+k}^{t+k+1}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}u(s)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{t+k}^{\infty}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}u(s)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-(t+k)/C(\varepsilon)}\leq C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,e^{-t/C(\varepsilon)}\,,

that is

\|u(T)-u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,e^{-t/C(\varepsilon)}\,,\qquad\forall T>t>1/C(\varepsilon,u_{0})\,.

(8.13)

Now let

as

is a minimizing sequence of

in (

∎

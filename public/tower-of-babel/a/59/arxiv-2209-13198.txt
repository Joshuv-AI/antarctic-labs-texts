Regular covariant representations and their Wold-type decomposition Thanks: *corresponding author
Azad Rohilla, Harsh Trivedi, Shankar Veerabathiran *

Abstract
. Olofsson introduced a growth condition regarding elements of an orbit for an expansive operator and generalized Richter’s wandering subspace theorem. Later on, using the Moore-Penrose inverse, Ezzahraoui, Mbekhta, and Zerouali extended the growth condition and obtained a Shimorin-Wold-type decomposition. Shimorin-Wold-type decomposition for completely bounded covariant representations, which are close to isometric representations, is obtained in [ 26 ] . This paper extends this decomposition for regular, completely bounded covariant representation having reduced minimum modulus ≥ 1 \geq 1 that satisfies the growth condition. To prove the decomposition, we introduce the terms regular, algebraic core, and reduced minimum modulus in the completely bounded covariant representation setting and work out several fundamental results. Consequently, we shall analyze the weighted unilateral shift introduced by Muhly and Solel and introduce and explore a non-commutative weighted bilateral shift.

1.
Introduction

The classical result of Wold
[
27
]
asserts that given isometry on a Hilbert space is either a unitary, a shift, or uniquely breaks as a direct summand of them. Beurling
[
3
]
proved that every
M_{z}
-invariant closed subspace of the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is a copy of an inner function. One of the well-known implications of the Wold decomposition is that when the unitary part is zero, the Wold-decomposition gives uniqueness of the wandering subspace for a shift. Halmos
[
10
]
proved a wandering subspace theorem, which is an abstraction of the Beurling’s theorem, that characterized all the invariant subspaces of a shift. Richter
[
21
]
proved a wandering subspace theorem for an analytic concave operator which satisfies the growth condition that was explicitly mentioned and generalized by Olofsson in
[
17
]
. After that, Shimorin
[
24
]
provided an elementary proof of Richter’s theorem by giving a Wold-type decomposition for concave operators that can be considered close to an isometry. Olofsson
[
17
]
extended the Richter’s wandering subspace theorem as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Olofsson)
Suppose
V
is an analytic bounded linear map defined on Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
such that

(i)

V
is an expansive operator,

(ii)

there exist some positive numbers
d_{m},d
such that
\sum_{m\geq 2}\frac{1}{d_{k}}=\infty
and

\|V^{m}h\|^{2}\leq d\|h\|^{2}+d_{m}(\|Vh\|^{2}-\|h\|^{2}),\quad h\in\mathcal{H}.

Then
V
has the wandering subspace property.

Ezzahraoui, Mbekhta, and Zerouali in
[
7
]
using the reduced minimum modulus
\geq 1,
and a general condition of the expansive operator, extended Theorem
1.1
for the broader class of regular operators
[
13
]
and proved the following Wold-type decomposition:

Theorem 1.2
.

(Ezzahraoui-Mbekhta-Zerouali)
If
V
is a regular bounded linear operator defined on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
such that the reduced minimum modulus of
V
is greater than or equal to 1, and satisfies the growth condition

\displaystyle\|V^{m}h\|^{2}\leq\|V^{\dagger}Vh\|^{2}+d_{m}(\|Vh\|^{2}-\|V^{\dagger}Vh\|^{2}),\>\>\>h\in\mathcal{H}

such that
\sum_{m\geq 2}\frac{1}{d_{m}},
then

\mathcal{H}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}V^{n}(\mathcal{H})+[\mathcal{H}\ominus V(\mathcal{H})]_{V}.

The study of Wold decomposition has begun in Non-commutative Multivariable Operator Theory by Frazho
[
9
]
and by Popescu
[
20
]
. They explored Wold-decomposition for row-isometries considered by Cuntz
[
5
]
. Pimsner
[
19
]
introduced the notion of Cuntz-Pimsner algebra for faithful
C^{*}
-correspondence. Muhly and Solel
[
15
]
provided the Wold-type decomposition for representations of tensor algebras of
C^{*}
-correspondences, and they explored the invariant subspaces of particular subalgebras of Cuntz-Krieger algebras.

There is a map

Consider a CB-representation

3.
Regularity condition of the generalized inverse of a covariant representation

Definitions of the generalized inverse and bi-regularity of the covariant representation are provided in this section and derive some basic properties. Further, we prove the generalized range of a covariant representation is invariant under the generalized inverse.

Definition 3.1
.

Consider a CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}.
A bounded operator
S:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow E\otimes\mathcal{H}
is said to be a generalized inverse of
\widetilde{V}
if
\widetilde{V}S\widetilde{V}=\widetilde{V}
and
S\widetilde{V}S=S.

Assume
(\sigma,V)
to be a CB-representation of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
and let
S
be a generalized inverse of
\widetilde{V}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

S^{(n)}:H\rightarrow E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{H}
by

(3.1)

\displaystyle S^{(n)}:=(I_{E^{\otimes n-1}}\otimes S)(I_{E^{\otimes n-2}}\otimes S)\cdots(I_{E}\otimes S)S.

Observe that

\displaystyle(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S^{(n)})S^{(m)}=S^{(m+n)},\>\>\>m,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Note that if
(\sigma,V)
is isometric, then
S=\widetilde{V}^{*}
.

The next lemma will take a critical role in defining the bi-regularity condition of the CB-representation.

Lemma 3.2
.

Consider a CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}.
Suppose
S
is a generalized inverse of
\widetilde{V}
. Then for every
m,n\in\mathbb{N},

S^{(m)}N(S^{(m+n)})=N(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S^{(n)})\cap S^{(m)}(\mathcal{H}).

Proof.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
and
h\in N(S^{(m+n)}),
then
(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S^{(n)})S^{(m)}h=S^{(m+n)}h=0
and thus
S^{(m)}h\in N(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S^{(n)}).
Therefore, we obtain

S^{(m)}N(S^{(m+n)})\subseteq N(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S^{(n)})\cap S^{(m)}(\mathcal{H}).

On the other hand, let
\xi\in N(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S^{(n)})\cap S^{(m)}(\mathcal{H})
, then
(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S^{(n)})\xi=0
and
\xi=S^{(m)}h
for some
h\in\mathcal{H}.
Consequently

S^{(m+n)}h=(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S^{(n)})S^{(m)}h=(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S^{(n)})\xi=0

which implies
h\in N(S^{(m+n)})
. Hence
\xi=S^{(m)}h\in S^{(m)}(N(S^{(m+n)})),
so the opposite inclusion is verified.∎

The following result presents some valuable connections between the kernel of generalized inverse and the generalized range, which leads to defining the bi-regularity condition.

Theorem 3.3
.

Consider a CB-representation
(\sigma,V)

E
on
\mathcal{H}
and let
S
be a generalized inverse of
\widetilde{V}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

N(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S)\subseteq R(S^{(m)})
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
;

(2)

N(I_{E}\otimes S^{(n)})\subseteq R(S)
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

(3)

N(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S^{(n)})\subseteq R(S^{(m)})
for each
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
;

(4)

N(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S^{(n)})=S^{(m)}(N(S^{(m+n)}))
for each
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

(1)\implies(2):
We will prove it by Mathematical induction. For
n=1
, we need to show that

N(I_{E}\otimes S)\subseteq R(S),

which is true when we substitute
m=1
in (1). Suppose that (2) is valid for
n=k
. We have to prove that (2) holds for
n=k+1
, i.e.,

N(I_{E}\otimes S^{(k+1)})\subseteq R(S).

Let
\xi\in N(I_{E}\otimes S^{(k+1)}),
then

\displaystyle(I_{E^{\otimes k+1}}\otimes S)(I_{E}\otimes S^{(k)})(\xi)=(I_{E}\otimes(I_{E^{\otimes k}}\otimes S)S^{(k)})(\xi)=(I_{E}\otimes S^{(k+1)})(\xi)=0.

Therefore
(I_{E}\otimes S^{(k)})(\xi)\in N(I_{E^{\otimes k+1}}\otimes S)\subseteq R(S^{(k+1)}),
here last inequality follows from (1). Therefore, there is
h\in N(S^{(k+2)})
so that

(I_{E}\otimes S^{(k)})(\xi)=S^{(k+1)}(h)=(I_{E}\otimes S^{(k)})S(h).

Thus we get
\xi-S(h)\in N(I_{E}\otimes S^{(k)})\subseteq R(S).
Hence
\xi\in R(S)
.

(2)\implies(3):
We will prove inequality (3) by Mathematical induction. For
m=1
, nothing to prove. Suppose (3) is valid for
m=k.
Now we need to show that it is valid for
m=k+1
, that is,

N(I_{E^{\otimes k+1}}\otimes S^{(n)})\subseteq R(S^{(k+1)}).

Let
\xi\in N(I_{E^{\otimes k+1}}\otimes S^{(n)}),
then by (2)
N(I_{E^{\otimes k+1}}\otimes S^{(n)})\subseteq(I_{E^{\otimes k}}\otimes S)(E^{\otimes k+1}\otimes\mathcal{H}),
therefore there exists
\eta\in N(I_{E^{\otimes k}}\otimes S^{(n+1)})
such that
\xi=(I_{E^{\otimes k}}\otimes S)(\eta)
and by induction hypothesis
\eta\in R({S^{(k)}}).
It follows that
\xi=(I_{E^{\otimes k}}\otimes S)(\eta)\in R({S^{(k+1)}})
and hence
N(I_{E^{\otimes k+1}}\otimes S^{(n)})\subseteq R(S^{(k+1)}).

(3)\implies(4):
Suppose
N(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S^{(n)})\subseteq R(S^{(m)})
, for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}.
By Lemma
3.2
and (3), we get

\displaystyle S^{(m)}N(S^{(m+n)})=N(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S^{(n)})\cap S^{(m)}(\mathcal{H})=N(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes S^{(n)}),

which proves (4).

(4)\implies(1):
Trivial.
∎

8
]
.

Definition 3.4
.

Consider a regular CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
and let
S
be a generalized inverse of
\widetilde{V}.
We say that
(\sigma,V)
is
bi-regular
if its generalized inverse
S
satisfies any one of conditions of Theorem
3.3
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Consider a regular CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}.
If
S
is the generalized inverse of
\widetilde{V},

then
\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}\widetilde{V}_{n}=\widetilde{V}_{n},

n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

For every
k\geq 1
, we begin by demonstrating that
(I_{E}\otimes S^{(k)})N(\widetilde{V})\subseteq N(\widetilde{V}_{k+1})
.
For that, we need to prove the following inequality

(3.2)

(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes S)N(\widetilde{V}_{n})\subseteq N(\widetilde{V}_{n+1}),\quad\mbox{where}\>\>\>n\in\mathbb{N}.

Let

\xi\in N(\widetilde{V}_{n})\subseteq(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{V})(E^{\otimes{n+1}}\otimes\mathcal{H})

(using by Theorem
2.2
), there is
\eta\in E^{\otimes{n+1}}\otimes\mathcal{H}
such that
\xi=(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{V})(\eta)
. Observe that

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{n+1}(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes S)(\xi)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{V}_{n}(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{V}S\widetilde{V})(\eta)=\widetilde{V}_{n}(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{V})(\eta)=0

and hence the Inequality (
3.2
).
Using the Inequality (
3.2
), it is easy to see that

(3.3)

(I_{E}\otimes S^{(k)})N(\widetilde{V})\subseteq N(\widetilde{V}_{k+1}).

Now, we show that

\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}\widetilde{V}_{n}=\widetilde{V}_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Consider

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}\widetilde{V}_{n}-\widetilde{V}_{n}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{V}_{n}\big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}(I_{E^{\otimes k-1}}\otimes S^{(n-k+1)}\widetilde{V}_{n-k+1})-(I_{E^{\otimes k}}\otimes S^{(n-k)}\widetilde{V}_{n-k})\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\widetilde{V}_{n}(I_{E^{\otimes k}}\otimes S^{(n-k)})((I_{E^{\otimes k-1}}\otimes S\widetilde{V})-I_{E^{\otimes k}\otimes\mathcal{H}})(I_{E^{\otimes k}}\otimes\widetilde{V}_{n-k}).

Note that
((I_{E^{\otimes k-1}}\otimes S\widetilde{V})-I_{E^{\otimes k}\otimes\mathcal{H}})\xi_{k}\in N(\widetilde{V}_{k})
for every
\xi_{k}\in{E^{\otimes k}\otimes\mathcal{H}}
and Equations (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) follows that

(I_{E^{\otimes k}}\otimes S^{(n-k)})((I_{E^{\otimes k-1}}\otimes S\widetilde{V})-I_{E^{\otimes k}\otimes\mathcal{H}})\xi_{k}\in N(\widetilde{V}_{n}),

for
1\leq k\leq n
. Therefore,

\widetilde{V}_{n}(I_{E^{\otimes k}}\otimes S^{(n-k)})((I_{E^{\otimes k-1}}\otimes S\widetilde{V})-I_{E^{\otimes k}\otimes\mathcal{H}})=0.

So, we get
\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}\widetilde{V}_{n}=\widetilde{V}_{n},
for
n\in\mathbb{N}.

∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Consider a bi-regular CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
and let
S
be a generalized inverse of
\widetilde{V}.
Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N},

S^{(n)}
is a generalized inverse of
\widetilde{V}_{n},
that is,

\displaystyle S^{(n)}\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}=S^{(n)}\quad and\quad\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}\widetilde{V}_{n}=\widetilde{V}_{n}.

Proof.

Since
S
is a generalized inverse of
\widetilde{V},

\widetilde{V}S\widetilde{V}=\widetilde{V}
and
S\widetilde{V}S=S.

Now
(\sigma,V)
is bi-regular therefore by Theorem
3.5
, we can see that
\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}\widetilde{V}_{n}=\widetilde{V}_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}.
So it is sufficient to prove
S^{(n)}\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}=S^{(n)}
. For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we begin by demonstrating that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{k}(N(I_{E^{\otimes k}}\otimes S))\subseteq N(S^{(k+1)}).

Let
\xi\in N(I_{E}\otimes S^{(n)})\subseteq R(S)
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
(using the fact that
(\sigma,V)
is bi-regular), there is
h\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\xi=Sh
. Note that

\displaystyle S^{(n+1)}\widetilde{V}\xi=S^{(n+1)}\widetilde{V}Sh=(I_{E}\otimes S^{(n)})S\widetilde{V}Sh=(I_{E}\otimes S^{(n)})Sh=(I_{E}\otimes S^{(n)})\xi=0.

It implies

(3.5)

\widetilde{V}(N(I_{E}\otimes S^{(n)}))\subseteq N({S^{(n+1)}}),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Using Equation (
3.5
) and by Mathematical induction, we get
(
3.4
).

Now we show that

S^{(n)}\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}=S^{(n)}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Consider

\displaystyle S^{(n)}\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}-S^{(n)}

\displaystyle=S^{(n)}(\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}-I)=S^{(n)}\big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\widetilde{V}_{n-k+1}S^{(n-k+1)}-\widetilde{V}_{n-k}S^{(n-k)}\big)

\displaystyle=S^{(n)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\widetilde{V}_{n-k}(I_{E^{\otimes n-k}}\otimes\widetilde{V})(I_{E^{\otimes n-k}}\otimes S)S^{(n-k)}-\widetilde{V}_{n-k}S^{(n-k)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}S^{(n)}\widetilde{V}_{n-k}((I_{E^{\otimes n-k}}\otimes\widetilde{V}S)-I_{E^{\otimes n-k}\otimes\mathcal{H}})S^{(n-k)}.

Note that
((I_{E^{\otimes n-k}}\otimes\widetilde{V}S)-I_{E^{\otimes n-k}\otimes\mathcal{H}})\xi_{n-k}=(I_{E^{\otimes n-k}}\otimes(\widetilde{V}S-I_{\mathcal{H}}))\xi_{n-k}\in N(I_{E^{\otimes n-k}}\otimes S)
for every
\xi_{n-k}\in{E^{\otimes n-k}\otimes\mathcal{H}}
,
1\leq k\leq n
and Equations (
3.5
) and (
3.4
) follows that

\widetilde{V}_{n-k}((I_{E^{\otimes n-k}}\otimes\widetilde{V}S)-I_{E^{\otimes n-k}\otimes\mathcal{H}})\xi_{n-k}\in N(S^{(n-k+1)})\subseteq N(S^{(n)}),

for every
1\leq k\leq n
. Therefore,

S^{(n)}\widetilde{V}_{n-k}((I_{E^{\otimes n-k}}\otimes\widetilde{V}S)-I_{E^{\otimes n-k}\otimes\mathcal{H}})=0.

So we deduce from the above fact that
S^{(n)}\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}=S^{(n)},

n\in\mathbb{N}.

∎

Remark 3.7
.

Consider a regular CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
and let
S
be a generalized inverse of
\widetilde{V}.
Then

\displaystyle R^{\infty}({V})=\{h\in\mathcal{H}:\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}h=h,\quad for\quad all\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Indeed, let
h\in R^{\infty}({V})
, then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is
\xi_{n}\in{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\mathcal{H}

such that
\widetilde{V}_{n}(\xi_{n})=h
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Now, apply
\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}
in both sides, we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}\widetilde{V}_{n}\xi_{n}=\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}h.

Now by using Theorem
3.5
, we have

\widetilde{V}_{n}\xi_{n}=\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}h.
This implies
h=\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}h
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.

On the other hand, let
h\in\mathcal{H}
be such that
\widetilde{V}_{n}S^{(n)}h=h
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
h\in R(\widetilde{V}_{n})
and hence
h\in R^{\infty}({V}).

Now we recall the definition of invariant subspace (cf.
[
25
]
) for the covariant representation
(\sigma,V).

Definition 3.8
.

(1)

Consider a CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and suppose
\mathcal{K}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}.
Then we say
\mathcal{K}
is
(\sigma,V)
-invariant if it is
\sigma(\mathcal{B})
-invariant
and, is invariant by each operator
V(\xi),\>\xi\in E.
In addition, if
\mathcal{K}^{\bot}
is invariant by
V(\xi)
for
\xi\in E,
then we say
\mathcal{K}
is
(\sigma,V)
-reducing. Restricting naturally this representation we get another representation of
E
on
\mathcal{K}
which will be denoted as
(\sigma,V)|_{\mathcal{K}}.

(2)

A closed subspace
\mathcal{W}
of
\mathcal{H}
is called wandering subspace for
(\sigma,V)
, if it is
\sigma(\mathcal{B})
-invariant and
\mathcal{W}\perp\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}.
The representation
(\sigma,V)
has generating wandering subspace property (GWS-property) if there is a wandering subspace
\mathcal{W}
of
\mathcal{H}
satisfying

\mathcal{H}=\displaystyle\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W})

and the corresponding wandering subspace
\mathcal{W}

is called generating wandering subspace (GWS).

Corollary 3.9
.

Consider a regular CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
and let
S
be a generalized inverse of
\widetilde{V}
. Then

\displaystyle S(R^{\infty}({{V}}))\subseteq E\otimes R^{\infty}({{V}}).

Proof.

Let
h\in R^{\infty}({{V}}),
then by Remark
3.7
,
\widetilde{V}Sh=h
and thus there exist
\xi_{n}\in E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{H},n\in\mathbb{N},
such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}Sh=\widetilde{V}_{n+1}(\xi_{n+1})=\widetilde{V}(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{V}_{n})(\xi_{n+1}),

which implies that

\displaystyle Sh-(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{V}_{n})(\xi_{n+1})\in N(\widetilde{V})\subseteq(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{V}_{n})(E^{\otimes(n+1)}\otimes\mathcal{H}),

here the last inequality follows from Theorem
2.2
. Let
\eta:=Sh-(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{V}_{n})(\xi_{n+1}).
Therefore,

\displaystyle Sh=\eta+(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{V}_{n})(\xi_{n+1})\in(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{V}_{n})(E^{\otimes(n+1)}\otimes\mathcal{H}),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence
Sh\in E\otimes R(\widetilde{V}_{n})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
Sh\in E\otimes R^{\infty}({{V}})
. Thus
S(R^{\infty}({{V}}))\subseteq E\otimes R^{\infty}({{V}}).
∎

4.
The Moore-Penrose inverse and the reduced minimum modulus

We begin this section by defining the Moore-Penrose inverse for
\widetilde{V}
and brief details of the Moore-Penrose inverse (for more details see
[
6
]
).

Consider a CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\widetilde{V}
has closed range. If there is a unique operator
\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H},E\otimes\mathcal{H})
so that

(1)

N({\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}})=R({\widetilde{V}})^{\perp}=N({\widetilde{V}^{*}})
and

(2)

\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}\widetilde{V}\xi=\xi
,  for
\xi\in N({\widetilde{V}})^{\perp},

then the operator
\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}
is called the
Moore-Penrose inverse
(MPI) of
\widetilde{V}
and it is satisfies the following:

\widetilde{V}=\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}\widetilde{V},\quad\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}=\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger},\quad\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}=(\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger})^{*},\quad\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}\widetilde{V}=(\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}\widetilde{V})^{*}.

Remark 4.1
.

Observe that

\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}=({\widetilde{V}^{*}\widetilde{V}})^{-1}\widetilde{V}^{*}
when
\widetilde{V}
has left inverse and if
\widetilde{V}
has right inverse, then
\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}
will be right inverse and equal to
\widetilde{V}^{*}({\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{*}})^{-1}.

In the following definition, we introduce a notion of reduced minimum modulus (cf.
[
1
,
2
]
) for the CB-representations of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
:

Definition 4.2
.

Consider a CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}.
The reduced minimum modulus
\gamma({\widetilde{V}})
of
(\sigma,V)
is defined by

\gamma({\widetilde{V}}):=\begin{cases}\inf_{\xi\notin N({\widetilde{V}})}\frac{\|\widetilde{V}\xi\|}{\dis(\xi,N({\widetilde{V}}))}&\text{if }\widetilde{V}\neq 0\\
\infty&\text{if }\widetilde{V}=0.\end{cases}

It is easy to see that
\widetilde{V}
has closed range if and only if
\gamma({\widetilde{V}})\textgreater 0.

Proposition 4.3
.

Consider a regular CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}.
Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\gamma({\widetilde{V}}_{n})\geq\gamma({\widetilde{V}})\gamma(I_{E}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})\cdots\gamma(I_{E^{\otimes n-1}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}}).

Proof.

We prove it by Mathematical induction. The case
n=1
is trivial. Assume that

\gamma({\widetilde{V}}_{n})\geq\gamma({\widetilde{V}})\gamma(I_{E}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})\cdots\gamma(I_{E^{\otimes n-1}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}}).

For
\xi\in E^{\otimes(n+1)}\otimes\mathcal{H}
and
\eta\in N({\widetilde{V}}_{n+1}),
we have

\displaystyle\dis(\xi,N({\widetilde{V}}_{n+1}))

\displaystyle=\dis(\xi-\eta,N({\widetilde{V}}_{n+1}))\leq\dis(\xi-\eta,N(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})),

as
N(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})\subseteq N({\widetilde{V}}_{n+1}),
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By assumption
(\sigma,V)
is regular and by (4) of Theorem
2.2
,

N({\widetilde{V}_{n}})=(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})(N(\widetilde{V}_{n+1}))

and therefore

\displaystyle\dis((I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})(\xi),N({\widetilde{V}_{n}}))

\displaystyle=\dis((I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})(\xi),(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})(N(\widetilde{V}_{n+1})))

\displaystyle=\inf_{\eta\in N({\widetilde{V}}_{n+1})}\|(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})(\xi)-(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})(\eta)\|

\displaystyle=\inf_{\eta\in N({\widetilde{V}}_{n+1})}\|(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})(\xi-\eta)\|

\displaystyle\geq\gamma(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})\inf_{\eta\in N({\widetilde{V}}_{n+1})}\dis((\xi-\eta),N(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}}))

\displaystyle\geq\gamma(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})\dis(\xi,N({\widetilde{V}}_{n+1})).

From the above observation, we get

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{V}_{n+1}(\xi)\|=\|\widetilde{V}_{n}(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})(\xi)\|

\displaystyle\geq\gamma({\widetilde{V}_{n}})\dis((I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})(\xi),N({\widetilde{V}_{n}}))

\displaystyle\geq\gamma({\widetilde{V}_{n}})\gamma(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})\dis(\xi,N({\widetilde{V}}_{n+1})).

Consequently, from our induction hypothesis

\gamma({\widetilde{V}}_{n+1})\geq\gamma({\widetilde{V}_{n}})\gamma(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})\geq\gamma({\widetilde{V}})\gamma(I_{E}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})\gamma(I_{E^{\otimes 2}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}})\cdots\gamma(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes{\widetilde{V}}),

which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Consider a regular CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}.
Then

(1)

R^{\infty}({V})
is closed.

(2)

\widetilde{V}
is left invertible if
R^{\infty}({V})=0.

Proof.

(1) Since
(\sigma,V)
is a regular CB-representation of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
,
R(\widetilde{V})
is closed. Thus for each
n\in\mathbb{N},
we have
R(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{V})
is closed and hence using Proposition
4.3
,
\gamma({{\widetilde{V}}_{n}})\textgreater 0
. From this, we conclude that
R({\widetilde{V}}_{n})
is closed for every
n\in\mathbb{N},
and therefore
R^{\infty}({V})
is closed.

(2) Since
R^{\infty}({V})=0
and
(\sigma,V)
is regular,
\widetilde{V}
is injective and
R(\widetilde{V})
is closed. Therefore
\widetilde{V}
is left invertible.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose
(\sigma,V)

(1)

\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}=P_{R({\widetilde{V}})}
and

R({\widetilde{V}}^{\dagger})=R({\widetilde{V}}^{*})=N({\widetilde{V})}^{\perp}

Theorem 5.8
.

Consider a regular CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
so that
\gamma({\widetilde{V}})\geq 1
and satisfy the following inequality

(5.6)

\|\widetilde{V}_{m}(\xi_{m})\|^{2}\leq d_{m}\big(\|(I_{E^{\otimes{m-1}}}\otimes\widetilde{V})(\xi_{m})\|^{2}-\|(I_{E^{\otimes{m-1}}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}\widetilde{V})(\xi_{m})\|^{2}\big)+\|(I_{E^{\otimes{m-1}}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}\widetilde{V})(\xi_{m})\|^{2},

\xi_{m}\in E^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{H},m\in\mathbb{N}

\sum_{m\geq 2}

\frac{1}{d_{m}}=\infty.
Then

where
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{H}\ominus\widetilde{V}(E\otimes{\mathcal{H}}).

Proof.

For
m\in\mathbb{N},
Inequality (
5.6
) can be rewrite as

\|\widetilde{V}_{m}(\xi_{m})\|^{2}-\|(I_{E^{\otimes{m-1}}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}\widetilde{V})(\xi_{m})\|^{2}\leq d_{m}\|(I_{E^{\otimes{m-1}}}\otimes D_{\widetilde{V}})(\xi_{m})\|^{2},\quad\xi_{m}\in E^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{H}.

Now replace
\xi_{m}
by
\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}h,h\in\mathcal{H},
in the above inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{V}_{m}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}-

\displaystyle\|(I_{E^{\otimes{m-1}}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}\widetilde{V})\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}\leq d_{m}\|(I_{E^{\otimes{m-1}}}\otimes D_{\widetilde{V}})\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}

and by using the identity
\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}=\widetilde{V}^{\dagger},

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{V}_{m}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}-

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}\leq d_{m}\|(I_{E^{\otimes{m-1}}}\otimes D_{\widetilde{V}})\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}.

Therefore

(5.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 2}\frac{\|\widetilde{V}_{m}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}-\|\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}}{d_{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\geq 2}\|(I_{E^{\otimes{m-1}}}\otimes D_{\widetilde{V}})\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|h\|^{2},

since
(d_{m})_{m\geq 2}
is positive sequence and the last inequality holds due to Lemma
5.3
. Moreover, from the hypothesis
\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 2}

\frac{1}{d_{m}}=\infty,
we conclude

\liminf_{m}\big\{\|\widetilde{V}_{m}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}-\|\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}\big\}\leq 0.

Indeed, if
\liminf_{m}\big\{\|\widetilde{V}_{m}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}-\|\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}\big\}\geq\beta\textgreater 0,
then there is a
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that
\|\widetilde{V}_{m}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}-\|\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}\geq\frac{\beta}{2}\textgreater 0
for
m\geq m_{0}.
Thus

\sum_{m\geq 2}\frac{\|\widetilde{V}_{m}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}-\|\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}{h}\|^{2}}{d_{m}}\geq\sum_{m\geq 2}\frac{\beta}{2d_{m}}=\infty,

which contradicts the Inequality (
5.7
). Since
\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}
is contraction, there exists a weakly convergent subsequence
\{\widetilde{V}_{m_{j}}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m_{j})}h\}
of
\{\widetilde{V}_{m}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}h\}
which converges weakly to
y
for some
y\in\mathcal{H}.
In symbols :
\widetilde{V}_{m_{j}}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m_{j})}h\rightharpoonup y.
By the identity

\widetilde{V}_{m}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}\widetilde{V}_{m_{j}}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger({m_{j}})}h=\widetilde{V}_{m_{j}}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger({m_{j}})}h,\quad where\quad m_{j}\geq m.

Apply
m_{j}\to\infty
to the above equation,
we get
\widetilde{V}_{m}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m)}y=y
and hence
y\in R(\widetilde{V}_{m}),
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}.
Now by Lemma
5.4
,
h-\widetilde{V}_{m_{j}}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m_{j})}h\in[\mathcal{W}]_{\widetilde{V}}.
Since
[\mathcal{W}]_{\widetilde{V}}
is weakly closed and
h-\widetilde{V}_{m_{j}}\widetilde{V}^{\dagger(m_{j})}h\rightharpoonup h-y,

h-y\in[\mathcal{W}]_{\widetilde{V}}.
Consequently
\mathcal{H}=[\mathcal{W}]_{\widetilde{V}}+R^{\infty}({V}).

\mathcal{W}.
In this case
\mathcal{W}=N(\widetilde{V}^{*}|_{\mathcal{K}}).
Indeed,

\displaystyle N(\widetilde{V}^{*}|_{\mathcal{K}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}\ominus\widetilde{V}(E\otimes\mathcal{K})=\displaystyle\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W})\ominus\widetilde{V}(E\otimes\displaystyle\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W}))

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W})\ominus\displaystyle\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W})=\mathcal{W}.

Hence, an invariant subspace

determines the wandering subspace

in a unique way. This leads to the conclusion that there are one-to-one correspondences between the set of all

-invariant subspaces of

and the set of all wandering subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
. This conclusion is the refinement of
[
26
, Theorem 2.4]
and
[
17
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Definition 5.14
.

of

Consider an analytic, regular CB-representation

be of

Consider a CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of

on
\mathcal{H}
and
\widetilde{V}

Then

is a CB-representation of

(cf.
[
26
]
) of

Notation 6.1
.

R^{\infty}(V^{\prime}):=\displaystyle\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}^{\prime}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{H})\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\mathcal{W}^{\prime}:=\mbox{N}(\widetilde{V}^{\prime*}).

Observe that

Definition 6.2
.

Consider a CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
A bounded linear operator

is said to

\displaystyle AV(\xi)h=V(\xi)Ah\hskip 19.91684pt\mbox{and}\hskip 19.91684ptA\sigma(a)h=\sigma(a)Ah,

To prove the primary conclusion of this section, we start with the lemma, which is quite helpful.

Lemma 6.3
.

Consider a CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
and
\widetilde{V}
has left inverse. Assume that
(\sigma,V)
and its Cauchy dual
(\sigma,V^{\prime})
have GWS-property. Suppose
\mathcal{K}
is a non-trivial proper
(\sigma,V)
-invariant subspace of
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{K}^{\perp},
where
\mathcal{W}=N(\widetilde{V}^{*}).
Then there is a non-zero
h_{1}\in\mathcal{K}
so that
\widetilde{V}^{*}h_{1}\in E\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\perp}.

Proof.

The subspace
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\mathcal{K}
is not
(\sigma,V)
-reducing. Indeed, if
\mathcal{K}
is
(\sigma,V)
-reducing, then
\mathcal{K}\perp[\mathcal{W}]_{\widetilde{V}}=\mathcal{H},
which contradicts to the assumption that
\mathcal{K}
is proper.

(2)

For any non-zero
h\in\mathcal{K},

\widetilde{V}^{*}h\neq 0,
otherwise
h\in\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{K}^{\perp}.

Note that
N(\widetilde{V}^{*})=N(\widetilde{V}^{\prime*}),
it follows from our assumptions that
\mathcal{H}=[\mathcal{W}]_{\widetilde{V}^{\prime}}.
Since
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
and
\mathcal{K}
is proper, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W})
is not orthogonal to
\mathcal{K}.
Then there exists a non-zero
\eta_{n}\in E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W}
such that
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}_{n}(\eta_{n})
is not orthogonal to
\mathcal{K}.
Let
N
be the smallest positive integer so that
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}_{N}(E^{\otimes N}\otimes\mathcal{W})
is not orthogonal to
\mathcal{K}
(such a
N
always exists because
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
), that is,
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}_{l}(E^{\otimes l}\otimes\mathcal{W})
is orthogonal to
\mathcal{K}
for all
0\leq l\leq N-1.
Choose the non-zero elements
h_{1}\in\mathcal{K}
and
h_{2}\in\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
such that
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}_{N}(\eta_{N})=h_{1}+h_{2}.
Then by the observation
(2)
and the minimality on
N,
we obtain

\widetilde{V}^{*}h_{1}=\widetilde{V}^{*}\widetilde{V}^{\prime}_{N}(\eta_{N})-\widetilde{V}^{*}h_{2}=\widetilde{V}^{*}\widetilde{V}^{\prime}(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{\prime}_{N-1})(\eta_{N})-\widetilde{V}^{*}h_{2}=(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{\prime}_{N-1})(\eta_{N})-\widetilde{V}^{*}h_{2}\in E\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\perp}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 6.4
.

Consider a CB-representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
and
\widetilde{V}
has left inverse. Suppose that
A
is a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{H}
which intertwine
(\sigma,V).
Assume that
(\sigma,V)
and its Cauchy dual
(\sigma,V^{\prime})
have the GWS-property. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

A
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{H}.

(2)

P_{\mathcal{W}}A|_{\mathcal{W}}
is a pure contraction, where
\mathcal{W}=N(\widetilde{V}^{*}).

Proof.

(1)\implies(2):

Note that
\mathcal{W}
is a
A^{*}
-invariant subspace of
\mathcal{H},
therefore for each
n\in\mathbb{N},

{(P_{\mathcal{W}}A|_{\mathcal{W}})^{*}}^{n}={A^{*}}^{n}|_{\mathcal{W}}.
Hence if
A
is pure contraction, then
P_{\mathcal{W}}A|_{\mathcal{W}}
is also pure contraction.

(2)\implies(1):
Suppose
P_{\mathcal{W}}A|_{\mathcal{W}}
is pure contraction. Since
A
is contraction,
\{A^{n}{A^{*}}^{n}\}_{n}
is a decreasing sequence of non-negative operators. Therefore there is a positive operator, denoted by
D_{A},
such that the sequence
\{A^{n}{A^{*}}^{n}\}_{n}
converges to
D_{A}^{2}
in the strong operator topology.
To prove
A
is a pure contraction, only we need to show that
N(D_{A})=\mathcal{H}.
Since
P_{\mathcal{W}}A|_{\mathcal{W}}
is a pure contraction,
\mathcal{W}\subseteq N(D_{A}).
Indeed, for
h\in\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle\|D_{A}h\|=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|A^{*n}h\|=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|{(P_{\mathcal{W}}A|_{\mathcal{W}})^{*}}^{n}h\|=0.

Case 1: If
N(D_{A})
is
(\sigma,V)
-reducing then

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=[\mathcal{W}]_{\widetilde{V}}\subseteq N(D_{A})\subseteq\mathcal{H},

that is,
N(D_{A})=\mathcal{H}.

Case 2: If
N(D_{A})
is not
(\sigma,V)
-reducing then
N(D_{A})
is a proper subspace of
\mathcal{H}.
Note that
N(D_{A})^{\perp}
is
(\sigma,V)
-invariant. Indeed, for
g\in N(D_{A})
and using intertwine property of
A,
we obtain

\displaystyle\|(I_{E}\otimes D_{A})\widetilde{V}^{*}g\|^{2}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle\widetilde{V}(I_{E}\otimes A^{n}{A^{*}}^{n})\widetilde{V}^{*}g,g\rangle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle A^{n}\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{*}{A^{*}}^{n}g,g\rangle\leq\|\widetilde{V}\|^{2}\|D_{A}g\|^{2}=0,

thus
\widetilde{V}^{*}N(D_{A})\subseteq E\otimes N(D_{A})
and hence
N(D_{A})^{\perp}
is
(\sigma,V)
-invariant. Therefore by Lemma
6.3
, there is a
h_{1}\in N(D_{A})^{\perp}
such that
\widetilde{V}^{*}h_{1}\in E\otimes N(D_{A})
and
h_{1}\neq 0.
For each
m\in\mathbb{N},
there exist
\xi^{1}_{m}\in\mathcal{W}
and
\xi^{2}_{m}\in\mathcal{W}^{\perp}
such that

A^{*m}h_{1}=\xi^{1}_{m}+\xi^{2}_{m}.

Since
A
is contraction,
\|\xi_{m}^{2}\|\leq\|h_{1}\|
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}.
Now apply
\widetilde{V}^{*}
both the sides of the previous equation, we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{*}A^{*m}h_{1}=\widetilde{V}^{*}\xi^{2}_{m}.

But
\widetilde{V}^{*}h_{1}\in E\otimes N(D_{A}),

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\widetilde{V}^{*}\xi^{2}_{m}\|=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\widetilde{V}^{*}A^{*m}h_{1}\|=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|(I_{E}\otimes A^{*m})\widetilde{V}^{*}h_{1}\|=0.

Note that
\xi^{2}_{m}\in\mathcal{W}^{\perp}=\widetilde{V}(E\otimes\mathcal{H}),
there is
\eta^{2}_{m}\in E\otimes\mathcal{H}
such that
\widetilde{V}(\eta^{2}_{m})=\xi^{2}_{m}
and also
\widetilde{V}
has left inverse, hence
\widetilde{V}
becomes bounded below. Then there exists
\alpha\textgreater 0
such that

\displaystyle\|\eta^{2}_{m}\|\leq\alpha\|\widetilde{V}(\eta^{2}_{m})\|=\alpha\|\xi^{2}_{m}\|.

From the above inequality, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|\xi^{2}_{m}\|^{2}=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|\widetilde{V}(\eta^{2}_{m})\|^{2}\leq\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|\widetilde{V}^{*}\widetilde{V}(\eta^{2}_{m})\|\|(\eta^{2}_{m})\|\leq\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|\widetilde{V}^{*}(\xi^{2}_{m})\|\|\alpha\xi^{2}_{m}\|=0,

since the sequence
\{\xi_{m}^{2}\}
bounded by
h_{1}.
Thus for each
n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\|D_{A}h_{1}\|=\|D_{A}{A^{*}}^{n}h_{1}\|=\|D_{A}(\xi^{1}_{n}+\xi^{2}_{n})\|\leq\|D_{A}\|\|\xi^{2}_{n}\|.

If we let
n\rightarrow\infty
form the above inequality,
D_{A}h_{1}=0,
i.e.,
h_{1}\in N(D_{A}).
However, by assumptions
h_{1}\in N(D_{A})^{\perp}
and hence
h_{1}=0.
This contradicts to our assumptions
N(D_{A})
is a proper subspace of
\mathcal{H}.
Since
\{0\}\neq\mathcal{W}\subseteq N(D_{A}),
the only possibility for
N(D_{A})
is
\mathcal{H}
which is equivalent to showing that
A
is pure.
∎

is regular. The Moore-Penrose inverse of

is

\widetilde{S}^{\dagger}(\eta)=\bigoplus_{{n\in\mathbb{N}}}({Z}_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})^{{}^{\dagger}}(\eta_{n}\otimes h_{n}),\>\>\>\eta=\displaystyle\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\eta_{n}\otimes h_{n}\in\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes\mathcal{H}.

Suppose if

for

then by Proposition
4.5
we get
\|\widetilde{S}^{\dagger}(\eta)\|^{2}=\bigoplus_{{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\|({Z}_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})^{{}^{\dagger}})(\eta_{n}\otimes h_{n})\|^{2}\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|\eta_{n}\otimes h_{n}\|^{2}.
This demonstrates that

is contraction and hence

Also, it is easy to see that

Now, we only need to show that

are in

\displaystyle=({W}_{k}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})(\bigotimes_{q=1}^{n}\xi_{q}\otimes\eta_{n}\otimes h_{n})=(DT_{\xi_{1}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})\cdots(DT_{\xi_{k}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})(\eta_{n}\otimes h_{n})

\displaystyle=((Z_{k+n}(I_{E}\otimes Z_{k+n-1})\dots(I_{E^{\otimes k-1}}\otimes Z_{n+1}))\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})(\bigotimes_{q=1}^{n}\xi_{q}\otimes\eta_{n}\otimes h_{n}).

and

Therefore

satisfies the growth condition (

(2)

(3)

For

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq{i_{1}},{i_{2}},\dots,{i_{k}}\leq n,\>\>m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}|{\alpha_{{i_{1}},{i_{2}},\dots,{i_{k}},m}|^{2}w_{i_{k},m}^{2}}\prod_{q=0}^{k-1}w_{i_{k-q},n_{k-q+1,k}}^{2}.

\displaystyle(I_{E^{\otimes{k-1}}}\otimes\widetilde{S}^{w})(\eta_{k})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq{i_{1}},{i_{2}},\dots,{i_{k}}\leq n,\>\>m\in\mathbb{Z}}\alpha_{{i_{1}},{i_{2}},\dots,{i_{k}},m}\bigotimes_{q=1}^{k-1}\delta_{i_{q}}\otimes\widetilde{S}^{w}(\delta_{i_{k}}\otimes e_{m})

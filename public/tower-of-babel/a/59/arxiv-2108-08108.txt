Exact and approximate solutions to the Helmholtz, Schrödinger and wave equation in 𝐑 3 \mathbf{R}^{3} with radial data

Abstract

Introduction

The Helmholtz, Schrödinger and wave equation are well known, fundamental partial differential equations. The Helmholtz equation models the propagation of monochromatic waves, i.e., waves with a fixed temporal frequency, and can be applied to the study of acoustic and electromagnetic wave propagation. The Cauchy problem for the wave equation model the time-dependent propagation of waves due to initial disturbances. The Schrödinger equation governs the probabilistic evolution of particles in quantum mechanics. These equations have been thoroughly analyzed many times; see for example
[

,

,

on the wave equation and

]
on the Schrödinger equation.

Closed form solutions to wave equations are useful for multiple reasons, for example in resolution and uncertainty analysis in scattering problems
[

[

]
, regularity estimates
[

[

and references therein. As a consequence, closed form solutions are valuable for convergence testing and analysis of numerical methods.

In this paper we use a method that relies on the spatial symmetry of fundamental solutions to construct closed form solutions to these equations in
\mathbf{R}^{3}
, when the data, i.e., the initial conditions or the source term, is a characteristic function on a ball with arbitrary location and radius. The main results are found in Propositions

Since all equations are linear, the results imply the construction of solutions to equations when the data is any finite sum of such characteristic functions. Although the literature on these equations is vast, we believe the results to be novel.

The idea behind this paper originated while trying to generate non-trivial, high-frequency solutions for an inverse problem for the Helmholtz equation in

2.1
Helmholtz equation

We consider the inhomogeneous Helmholtz equation,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}(\Delta+k^{2})u_{k}&=&\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}},\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{3},\\
\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|\to\infty\end{subarray}}|x|(\partial_{|x|}-ik)u_{k}&=&0,\quad\text{uniformly for }x/|x|\in S^{2}.\end{array}\right.

(1)

Here,
k=\frac{\omega}{c}>0
is the wavenumber
1
1

1

For the case of complex valued
k
, see below.
, where
c
is the wave speed of the medium and
\omega
is the temporal frequency of the wave.
S^{2}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbf{R}^{3}
, and the Sommerfeld radiation condition guarantees a unique, radiating solution
u_{k}
(cf.
[
7
]
, p. 91).
The source term is the characteristic functions
\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(x)
, defined as

\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(x)=\begin{cases}&1,\text{ for }x\in B_{x_{0},r},\\
&0,\text{ for }x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}\setminus B_{x_{0},r},\end{cases}

(2)

where
B_{x_{0},r}=\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}:|x_{0}-x|\leq r\}
is a closed ball of radius
r
centered at
x_{0}
.

We now present the first result.

Proposition 1
.

Let
d=|x-x_{0}|
. The solution to (
1
) is given by

u_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}&\dfrac{(i-kr)\mathrm{e}^{ik(d-r)}-(i+kr)\mathrm{e}^{ik(d+r)}}{2dk^{3}},\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}\setminus B_{x_{0},r},\\
&\dfrac{(i+kr)(\mathrm{e}^{ik(r-d)}-\mathrm{e}^{ik(r+d)})-2dk}{2dk^{3}},\quad\text{for }x\in B_{x_{0},r}.\end{cases}

(3)

Proof.

The solution to (
1
) is given by the convolution

u_{k}(x)=\int_{B_{0}}G_{k}(x-y)\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(y)\mathrm{d}y,\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}.

(4)

Here
G_{k}
is the outgoing fundamental solution of the Helmholtz equation in
\mathbf{R}^{3}
(cf.
[
3
]
),

G_{k}(x)=\frac{\mathrm{exp}(ik|x|)}{4\pi|x|},\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}.

(5)

We now evaluate the integral (
4
).
Assume first that
x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}\setminus B_{x_{0},r}
and let
d=|x-x_{0}|
. Let
B_{x,z}
be a ball centered at
x
with radius
z
, where
d-r\leq z\leq d+r
. Let
S(z)=\partial B_{x,z}\cap B_{x_{0},r}
, i.e., the part of the surface
\partial B_{x,z}
contained in
B_{x_{0},r}
. The surface area of
S(z)
is given by
A(z)=2\pi zh(z)
, where
h(z)
is the height of the spherical cap (cf.
[
19
]
, p. 224). Figure
1
depicts the situation. Computing the length
z_{i}
from
x
to the intersection of the spheres, we find that
h(z)=z-z_{i}=z\left(1-\frac{z^{2}+d^{2}-r^{2}}{2dz}\right)
. Next, we note that for
y\in\partial B_{x,z}
the integrand is constant;
\frac{\exp(ik|x-y|)}{4\pi|x-y|}=\frac{\exp(ikz)}{4\pi z}
. We now write
\mathrm{d}y=\mathrm{d}V(z)=A(z)\mathrm{d}z
. Hence we have reduced the integral dimension from 3 to 1, and we get

\displaystyle u_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}G_{k}(x-y)\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(y)\mathrm{d}y=\int_{B_{x_{0},r}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{ik|x-y|}}{4\pi|x-y|}\mathrm{d}y=\int_{d-r}^{d+r}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{ikz}}{4\pi z}A(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{d-r}^{d+r}\mathrm{e}^{ikz}z\left(1-\frac{z^{2}+d^{2}-r^{2}}{2zd}\right)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{(i-kr)\mathrm{e}^{ik(d-r)}-(i+kr)\mathrm{e}^{ik(d+r)}}{2dk^{3}}.

Figure 1:
2-dimensional sketch of the intersecting spheres. The spherical cap
S(z)
is the part of
\partial B_{x,z}
contained in
B_{x_{0},r}
.

Next, assume that
x\in B_{x_{0},r}\setminus\{x_{0}\}
. We split the integral into two parts: a spherical integral when
0\leq z\leq r-d
, and a spherical cap integral when
r-d\leq z\leq r+d
.

\displaystyle u_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}G_{k}(x-y)\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{x_{0},r}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{ik|x-y|}}{4\pi|x-y|}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r-d}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{ikz}}{4\pi z}4\pi z^{2}\mathrm{d}z+\frac{1}{2}\int_{r-d}^{r+d}\mathrm{e}^{ikz}z\left(1-\frac{z^{2}+d^{2}-r^{2}}{2zd}\right)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{ik(r-d)}(1-ik(r-d))-1}{k^{2}}+\frac{(i-2idk^{2}(d-r)+(r-2d))\mathrm{e}^{ik(r-d)}-(i+kr)\mathrm{e}^{ik(r+d)}}{2dk^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(i+kr)(\mathrm{e}^{ik(r-d)}-\mathrm{e}^{ik(r+d)})-2dk}{2dk^{3}}.

Last, we have

\displaystyle u_{k}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}G_{k}(x_{0}-y)\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{ikz}}{4\pi z}4\pi z^{2}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{ikr}(1-ikr)-1}{k^{2}},

and it is straight forward to check that

u_{k}(x_{0})=\lim_{d\to 0}\frac{(i+kr)(\mathrm{e}^{ik(r-d)}-\mathrm{e}^{ik(r+d)})-2dk}{2dk^{3}}=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{ikr}(1-ikr)-1}{k^{2}}.

∎

Complex wavenumber:
The above calculation also holds for complex wave numbers. If one considers instead the operator
(\Delta+\kappa^{2})
with
\kappa^{2}=k^{2}+ik\sigma
, where
\sigma
is an attenuation parameter (cf.
[
13
]
), the solution is again given by

u_{\kappa}(x)=\int_{B_{0}}G_{\kappa}(x-y)\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(y)\mathrm{d}y,\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{3},

where

G_{\kappa}(x)=\frac{\mathrm{exp}(i\kappa|x|)}{4\pi|x|},\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\},

and

\text{Re}(\kappa)=\left(\frac{\sqrt{k^{4}+k^{2}\sigma^{2}}+k^{2}}{2}\right)^{1/2},\qquad\text{Im}(\kappa)=\left(\frac{\sqrt{k^{4}+k^{2}\sigma^{2}}-k^{2}}{2}\right)^{1/2}.

As one can readily check, the solution
u_{\kappa}(x)
is identical to the one in Proposition
1
, but with
k
replaced by
\kappa
.

2.2
Schrödinger equation

We consider the Schrödinger equation without potential,

\begin{cases}&i\hbar\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\Delta u=0,\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{3},t>0,\\
&u(x,0)=\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(x),\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}.\end{cases}

(6)

Here
m
is the mass of the particle and
\hbar
is the Planck constant. The solution
u
is called the wave function, and
|u(x,t)|^{2}
is interpreted as the probability density function of the particle; the probability that the particle is contained in some region
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{3}
at the time
t
is given by

P(p\in\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Moreover, we have that
\|u(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{3})}=\|u(x,0)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{3})}
, i.e., conservation of probability. We require the total probability to sum to one at all times. Hence, for the solution to be physically meaningful, the initial condition should be multiplied by
(4\pi r^{3}/3)^{-1/2}
, and
(4\pi r^{3}/3)^{-1}\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(x)
represents a uniform probability distribution on
B_{x_{0},r}
with the corresponding solution given by
(4\pi r^{3}/3)^{-1/2}u(x,t)
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
d=|x-x_{0}|
and
\mathcal{M}_{t}=m/2\hbar t
. For
u(x,0)=\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(x)
, the solution to (
6
) is given by

\displaystyle u(x,t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{erf}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i3\pi/4}(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}(d-r)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{erf}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i3\pi/4}(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}(d+r)\right)

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\mathrm{e}^{-i3\pi/4}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}(d-r)^{2}}-\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}(d+r)^{2}}\right)}{(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}d\sqrt{\pi}},\quad\text{for }t>0,x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}.

Above,
\operatorname{erf}(z)=2\pi^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{e}^{-t^{2}}\mathrm{d}t
is the error function.

Proof.

The fundamental solution for the Schrödinger equation in
\mathbf{R}^{3}
is given by (cf.
[
7
,
4
]
)

G(x,t)=\left(\frac{m\mathrm{e}^{-i\pi/2}}{2\pi\hbar t}\right)^{3/2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{im|x|^{2}}{2\hbar t}},\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{3},t>0,

(7)

and the solution to (
6
) is given by

u(x,t)=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}G(x-y,t)\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(y)\mathrm{d}y.

(8)

Proceeding as in the proof of proposition 1, we compute the above integral. We write
\mathcal{M}_{t}=m/2\hbar t
. For fixed
t>0
, the observation that
G(x-y,t)
is constant on the sphere
\partial B_{x,|x-y|}
still holds, and for
x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}\setminus B_{x_{0},r}
and
t>0
we have

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-i3\pi/4}}{\pi^{3/2}}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{3/2}\int_{B_{x_{0},r}}\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}|x-y|^{2}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-i3\pi/4}}{\pi^{3/2}}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{3/2}\int_{d-r}^{d+r}\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}z^{2}}2\pi z^{2}\left(1-\frac{z^{2}+d^{2}-r^{2}}{2zd}\right)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-i3\pi/4}}{\pi^{3/2}}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{3/2}\Bigg(-\frac{(-1)^{1/4}\pi^{3/2}i\operatorname{erf}\left(i(-1)^{1/4}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1/2}z\right)}{2\mathcal{M}_{t}^{3/2}}\Bigg|_{d-r}^{d+r}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{i\pi\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}z^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{t}(d^{2}-2dz-r^{2}+z^{2})+i)}{2d\mathcal{M}_{t}^{2}}\Bigg|_{d-r}^{d+r}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(\operatorname{erf}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i3\pi/4}(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}(d-r)\right)-\operatorname{erf}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i3\pi/4}(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}(d+r)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\mathrm{e}^{-i3\pi/4}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}(d-r)^{2}}-\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}(d+r)^{2}}\right)}{(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}d\sqrt{\pi}}\Bigg).

For
x\in B_{x_{0},r}\setminus\{x_{0}\}
and
t>0
we compute

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-i3\pi/4}}{\pi^{3/2}}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{3/2}\int_{B_{x_{0},r}}\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}|x-y|^{2}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-i3\pi/4}}{\pi^{3/2}}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{3/2}\left(\int_{0}^{r-d}\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}z^{2}}4\pi z^{2}\mathrm{d}z+\int_{r-d}^{r+d}\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}z^{2}}2\pi z^{2}\left(1-\frac{z^{2}+d^{2}-r^{2}}{2zd}\right)\mathrm{d}z\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-i3\pi/4}}{\pi^{3/2}}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{3/2}\Bigg(-\frac{(-1)^{1/4}\pi^{3/2}\operatorname{erf}\left(i(-1)^{1/4}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1/2}z\right)}{\mathcal{M}_{t}^{3/2}}\Bigg|_{0}^{r-d}-\frac{2\pi iz\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}z^{2}}}{\mathcal{M}_{t}}\Bigg|_{0}^{r-d}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(-1)^{1/4}\pi^{3/2}i\operatorname{erf}\left(i(-1)^{1/4}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1/2}z\right)}{2\mathcal{M}_{t}^{3/2}}\Bigg|_{r-d}^{r+d}+\frac{i\pi\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}z^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{t}(d^{2}-2dz-r^{2}+z^{2})+i)}{2d\mathcal{M}_{t}^{2}}\Bigg|_{r-d}^{r+d}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{erf}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i3\pi/4}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1/2}(r-d)\right)-\frac{2i\mathrm{e}^{-i\pi 3/4}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1/2}(r-d)\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}(r-d)^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}-\frac{\operatorname{erf}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-i3\pi/4}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1/2}(r+d)\right)}{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\operatorname{erf}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i3\pi/4}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1/2}(r-d)\right)}{2}+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-i3\pi/4}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}(r-d)^{2}}-\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}(r+d)^{2}}\right)}{\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1/2}d\sqrt{\pi}}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{i\mathrm{e}^{-i\pi 3/4}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}(r-d)^{2}}(4d(d-r))}{2d\sqrt{\pi}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(\operatorname{erf}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i3\pi/4}(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}(d-r)\right)-\operatorname{erf}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i3\pi/4}(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}(d+r)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\mathrm{e}^{-i3\pi/4}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}(d-r)^{2}}-\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}(d+r)^{2}}\right)}{(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}d\sqrt{\pi}}\Bigg),

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\text{erf}(-z)=-\text{erf}(z)
.
Last, we have

\displaystyle u(x_{0},t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-i3\pi/4}}{\pi^{3/2}}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{3/2}\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}z^{2}}4\pi z^{2}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-i3\pi/4}\left(-(-1)^{1/4}i\operatorname{erf}\left(i(-1)^{1/4}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1/2}r\right)-2i\pi^{-1/2}r\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}r^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{erf}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i3\pi/4}(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}r\right)+\frac{2\mathrm{e}^{i3\pi/4}r(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\mathrm{e}^{i(\mathcal{M}_{t})r^{2}}.

We check that

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{d\to 0}-\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(\operatorname{erf}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i3\pi/4}(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}(r-d)\right)+\operatorname{erf}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i3\pi/4}(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}(r+d)\right)

(9)

\displaystyle-\frac{2\mathrm{e}^{-i3\pi/4}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}(r-d)^{2}}-\mathrm{e}^{i\mathcal{M}_{t}(r+d)^{2}}\right)}{(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}d\sqrt{\pi}}\Bigg)

(10)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{erf}\left(\mathrm{e}^{i3\pi/4}(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}r\right)+\frac{2\mathrm{e}^{i3\pi/4}r(\mathcal{M}_{t})^{1/2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\mathrm{e}^{i(\mathcal{M}_{t})r^{2}}.

(11)

∎

2.3
Wave equation

The linear Cauchy problem for the wave equation is

\begin{cases}&\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}-c^{2}\Delta u=0,\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{3},t>0,\\
&u(x,0)=f(x),\quad\frac{\partial u(x,0)}{\partial t}=g(x),\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}.\end{cases}

(12)

Here
c
is the wave speed,
u(x,t)
the wave amplitude and
f(x)
and
g(x)
is the initial configuration and velocity of the wave.

Proposition 3
.

Let
d=|x-x_{0}|
. The solution to (
12
) with
(f(x),g(x))=(\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(x),0)
is given by

u(x,t)=\begin{cases}&\dfrac{d-ct}{2d}\chi_{[d-r,d+r]}(ct),\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}\setminus B_{x_{0},r},t>0,\\
&\chi_{[0,r-d]}(ct)+\dfrac{d-ct}{2d}\chi_{[r-d,r+d]}(ct),\quad\text{for }x\in B_{x_{0},r}\setminus\{x_{0}\},t>0,\\
&\chi_{[0,r]}(ct)-t\delta(r-ct),\quad\text{for }x=x_{0},t>0.\end{cases}

(13)

The solution to (
12
) with
(f(x),g(x))=(0,\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(x))
is given by

u(x,t)=\begin{cases}&\dfrac{t}{2}\left(1-\frac{(ct)^{2}+d^{2}-r^{2}}{2dct}\right)\chi_{[d-r,d+r]}(ct),\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}\setminus B_{x_{0},r},t>0,\\
&t\chi_{[0,r-d]}(ct)+\dfrac{t}{2}\left(1-\frac{(ct)^{2}+d^{2}-r^{2}}{2dct}\right)\chi_{[r-d,r+d]}(ct),\quad\text{for }x\in B_{x_{0},r}\setminus\{x_{0}\},t>0\\
&t\chi_{[0,r]}(ct),\quad\text{for }x=x_{0},t>0.\end{cases}

(14)

Proof.

The fundamental solution to (
12
) with
f(x)=0,g(x)=\delta(x)
is

G(x,t)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{4\pi c^{2}t}\delta(|x|-ct),\quad&\text{for }x\in\mathbf{R}^{3},t\geq 0,\\
\quad 0,\quad&\text{for }x\in\mathbf{R}^{3},t<0.\end{cases}

where
\delta(x)
is the Dirac delta distribution (cf.
[
7
]
).
Hence, the solution to (
12
) is given by

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\dfrac{1}{4\pi c^{2}t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\delta(|x-y|-ct)f(y)\mathrm{d}y\right)+\dfrac{1}{4\pi c^{2}t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\delta(|x-y|-ct)g(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\dfrac{1}{4\pi c^{2}t}\int_{|y|=1}f(x+cty)\mathrm{d}S(y)\right)+\dfrac{1}{4\pi c^{2}t}\int_{|y|=1}g(x+cty)\mathrm{d}S(y),

where
\mathrm{d}S(y)
is the surface measure on
S^{2}
. For
x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}\setminus B_{x_{0},r}
and
d=|x_{0}-x|
we compute

\displaystyle v(x,t)=\int_{|y|=1}\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(x+cty)\mathrm{d}S(y)=2\pi(ct)^{2}\left(1-\frac{(ct)^{2}+d^{2}-r^{2}}{2dct}\right)\chi_{[d-r,d+r]}(ct).

Here
\chi_{[d-r,d+r]}
is the characteristic function on the interval
[d-r,d+r]
. For
x\in B_{x_{0},r}\setminus\{x_{0}\}
we have

\displaystyle v(x,t)=\int_{|y|=1}\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(x+cty)\mathrm{d}S(y)=4\pi(ct)^{2}\chi_{[0,r-d]}(ct)+2\pi(ct)^{2}\left(1-\frac{(ct)^{2}+d^{2}-r^{2}}{2dct}\right)\chi_{[r-d,r+d]}(ct).

Last,

\displaystyle v(x_{0},t)=\int_{|y|=1}\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}}(x_{0}+cty)\mathrm{d}S(y)=4\pi(ct)^{2}\chi_{[0,r]}(ct).

For
(f(x),g(x))=(0,\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}})
and
x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}\setminus B_{x_{0},r},t>0
, we get

u(x,t)=\frac{v(x,t)}{4\pi c^{2}t}=\frac{t}{2}\left(1-\frac{(ct)^{2}+d^{2}-r^{2}}{2dct}\right)\chi_{[d-r,d+r]}(ct).

(15)

Similar expressions are easily found for
x\in B_{x_{0},r}
.

Next, we compute the solution for
(u(x,0),\partial_{t}u(x,0))=(\chi_{B_{x_{0},r}},0)
. For
x\in\mathbf{R}^{3}\setminus B_{x_{0},r},t>0
we
have

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{t}{2}\left(1-\frac{(ct)^{2}+d^{2}-r^{2}}{2dct}\right)\chi_{[d-r,d+r]}(ct)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{2}\left(1-\frac{(ct)^{2}+d^{2}-r^{2}}{2dct}\right)c\left(\delta(d-r-ct)-\delta(d+r-ct)\right)+\frac{d-ct}{2d}\chi_{[d-r,d+r]}(ct)

\displaystyle=\frac{d-ct}{2d}\chi_{[d-r,d+r]}(ct),

where the first term in the second line vanish due to
1-\frac{(z)^{2}+d^{2}-r^{2}}{2dz}
having zeros at
d\pm r
.
Solutions for
x\in B_{x_{0},r}
are obtained in the same way.

∎

3
Approximate solutions for radial data

We want to use the solutions above to approximate solutions when the data are radial functions supported on a ball.
For a ball
B_{x_{0},R}
, let
f(r)\in H^{1}(B_{x_{0},R})
be a radial function, i.e., a function of the radial coordinate
r=|x_{0}-x|
only
2
2

2

Recall that
f
is in
H^{1}(B_{x_{0},R})
if
\|f\|^{2}_{H^{1}(B_{x_{0},R})}=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{x_{0},R})}+\|\nabla f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{x_{0},R})}<\infty.
.
We want to approximate
f(r)
by constant functions on spherical annulus regions. Define an annulus
S_{r_{i},\Delta r}
by
S_{r_{i},\Delta r}=B_{x_{0},r_{i}+\Delta r}\setminus B_{x_{0},r_{i}}
. We define the approximation
f_{N}
of

by

f_{N}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\bar{f}_{i}\chi_{S_{r_{i},\Delta r}}(x),\qquad\Delta r=\frac{R}{N},\quad r_{i}=i\Delta r,\quad\bar{f}_{i}=\frac{1}{\mu(S_{r_{i},\Delta r})}\int_{S_{r_{i},\Delta r}}f(x)\mathrm{d}x.

(16)

From the calculation

\displaystyle\|f_{N}-f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{x_{0},R})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\int_{S_{r_{i},\Delta r}}|\bar{f}_{i}-f|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}(\Delta r)^{2}\int_{S_{r_{i},\Delta r}}|f^{\prime}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R^{2}}{N^{2}}\|f\|^{2}_{H^{1}(B_{x_{0},R})},

where we have used the Poincaré inequality (cf.
[
8
]
), we have the following approximation estimate

\|f_{N}-f\|_{L^{2}(B_{x_{0},R})}\leq\frac{R}{N}\|f\|_{H^{1}(B_{x_{0},R})}.

(17)

Now, let
u_{k}^{r_{i}}
be the solution to the Helmholtz equation (
1
) with data
\chi_{S_{r_{i},\Delta r}}
, i.e., a characteristic function on the annulus
S_{r_{i},\Delta r}

\chi_{S_{r_{i},\Delta r}}=\chi_{x_{0},r_{i}+\Delta r}-\chi_{x_{0},r_{i}}

\displaystyle u_{k}^{r_{i}}(x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(i-k(r_{i}+\Delta r))\mathrm{e}^{ik(|x_{0}-x|-(r_{i}+\Delta r))}-(i+k(r_{i}+\Delta r))\mathrm{e}^{ik(|x_{0}-x|+(r_{i}+\Delta r))}}{2|x_{0}-x|k^{3}}

\displaystyle-\dfrac{(i-kr_{i})\mathrm{e}^{ik(|x_{0}-x|-r_{i})}-(i+kr_{i})\mathrm{e}^{ik(|x_{0}-x|+r_{i})}}{2|x_{0}-x|k^{3}}.

For
N>0
and
f\in H^{1}(B_{x_{0},R})
, let
f_{N}
be the approximation. Inserting
f_{N}
as data in (

u_{k}^{N}=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\bar{f}_{i}u_{k}^{r_{i}}

Taking

{u_{k}^{N}-u_{k}=\int_{B_{x_{0},R}}G_{k}(x-y)(f_{N}(y)-f(y))\mathrm{d}y}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\|u_{k}^{N}-u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{3})}

4
Discussion

Due to the linearity of the above equations, all results can be extended to obtain solutions when the data is any finite linear combination of characteristic functions on balls. As seen in Section
3
, this includes characteristic functions on spherical shells, but any function that can be described by a sum
g=\sum w_{i}\chi_{B_{x_{i},r_{i}}}
will have a similar solution. Since many shapes in nature are spherical, this should have interesting applications. Moreover, it can possibly be used for approximation of more complicated functions than radial ones.
Last, the method used to find solutions in this paper can, in principle, be generalized to any PDE with spherically symmetric fundamental solutions. However, the explicit and simple form of the surface measure on spherical caps that makes the calculation work out is, as far as we know, only available in
\mathbf{R}^{3}
.

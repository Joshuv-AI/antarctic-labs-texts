On the expressive power of mod- p p linear forms on the Boolean cube
Thomas Karam

Abstract
Let ( 𝒜 i ) i ∈ [ s ] (\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in[s]} be a sequence of dense subsets of the Boolean cube { 0 , 1 } n \{0,1\}^{n} and let p p be a prime. We show that if s s is assumed to be superpolynomial in n n then we can find distinct i , j i,j such that the two distributions of every mod- p p linear form on 𝒜 i \mathcal{A}_{i} and 𝒜 j \mathcal{A}_{j} are almost positively correlated. We also prove that if s s is merely assumed to be sufficiently large independently of n n then we may require the two distributions to have overlap bounded below by a positive quantity depending on p p only.



1
Introduction

For
p
a prime and
n
a positive integer, we will throughout say that a
mod-
p
linear form
, or
mod-
p
form
for short, is a linear form
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Such a form can be written as

\phi(x)=a_{1}x_{1}+\dots+a_{n}x_{n}

for some
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and we will say that the
support
of
\phi
is the set

\{z\in[n]:x_{z}\neq 0\}.

If
\phi,\psi
are two mod-
p
forms then we will say that the
support distance
between them is the size of the support of the difference
\phi-\psi
.

1.1
Restrictions of mod-
p
forms to the cube

One central tool in the analysis of Boolean functions and in theoretical computer science more broadly is discrete Fourier analysis. There, the characters involved in the definition of the Fourier coefficients are functions
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
of the type
x\to(-1)^{\phi(x)}
, for some linear form
\phi:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
, where
\{0,1\}
is identified with
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Restrictions to
\{0,1\}^{n}
of mod-
p
forms for some general prime
p
appear to be variants of these objects that are natural to study. Because
\{0,1\}
can be identified to the whole of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, whereas
\{0,1\}
can only be embedded into
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we can expect behaviour arising from linear forms with
p\geq 3
on the cube to be at least a little more complex, and in some ways this is indeed the case.

A basic observation which illustrates some of the difficulties that arise is the following. A given family
(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k})
of linear forms
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
is linearly independent if and only if it is independent in the probabilistic sense that when
x
is an element of
\{0,1\}^{n}
chosen uniformly at random, the
k
events

\phi_{1}(x)=y_{1},\dots,\phi_{k}(x)=y_{k}

are jointly independent, for any given
(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
. On the other hand we do not have such an equivalence for restrictions to
\{0,1\}^{n}
of mod-
p
forms. For instance, the mod-
p
forms
x_{1}
and
x_{1}+x_{2}
are linearly independent, but when
(x_{1},x_{2})
is chosen uniformly at random in
\{0,1\}^{2}
, conditioning on the event
x_{1}=0
decreases (from
1/4
to
0
) the probability of the event
x_{1}+x_{2}=2
. Returning to the topic of Fourier analysis, likewise the character-like functions defined by
\omega_{p}^{\phi(x)}
with
\phi
some mod-
p
form do not have their inner products of the type

\mathbb{E}_{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\omega_{p}^{\phi_{1}(x)}\omega_{p}^{-\phi_{2}(x)}

equal to
0
in general, even if the mod-
p
forms
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are linearly independent, although these inner products become close to
0
if the difference
\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}
has large support.

It is nonetheless not the case that these restrictions to
\{0,1\}^{n}
of mod-
p
forms are so unstructured as to not lead themselves to basic statements analogous to those that would arise from linear forms
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
, or more generally from unrestricted linear forms over finite fields. For instance, a special case of the fact above - the fact that

\mathbb{E}_{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\omega_{p}^{\phi(x)}

is close to
0
if
\phi
has large support -
was used by Gowers and the author in
[
6
]
to show that subsets of
\{0,1\}^{n}
satisfying systems of conditions of the type

\phi_{1}(x)\in E_{1},\dots,\phi_{k}(x)\in E_{k}

with
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k}
some mod-
p
forms and
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
can be arbitrarily well approximated from the inside by sets defined in a similar way but using only a bounded number of conditions.

In the setting of polynomials, it was established
[
5
]
by the same authors that a low-degree polynomial
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
which is not approximately equidistributed on
\{0,1\}^{n}
can be expressed (up to a polynomial vanishing on
\{0,1\}^{n}
) in a simple way in terms of a bounded number of polynomials with strictly smaller degree, a property that had previously been shown by Green and Tao
[
8
]
to be true when the assumption held for the distribution of this polynomial on the whole of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, and then studied further both qualitatively and quantitatively in that unrestricted setting by various authors such as Lovett, Kaufman, Bhowmick, Janzer, Milicevic, Moshkovitz, Cohen, Zhu, Ziegler, Kazhdan, and Adiprasito. Later, it was also shown
[
10
]
that under assumptions on the behaviour of the polynomial on
\{0,1\}^{n}
involving its range rather than its distribution, we can furthermore require that the polynomial be expressed in a simple way in terms of polynomials of yet smaller degree.

What the three previous results have in common is that a property defined using linear forms or polynomials
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
was shown to also hold or to have a simple weakening after restriction to
\{0,1\}^{n}
, so it is reasonable to hope that this will be the case for more properties.

Besides the fact that some properties extend nicely to mod-
p
forms, one concrete motivation for considering their behaviour on sets of the type
S^{n}
for some strict subset
S
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
are their applications to Ramsey theory, as several obstructions can be naturally formulated in terms of these mod-
p
forms. For instance, one special case of the density Hales-Jewett theorem, a strengthening of the Hales-Jewett theorem
[
9
]
originally proved by Furstenberg and Katznelson
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
and then proved again by the Polymath1 project
[
11
]
, states that for any fixed
\delta>0
, there exists a positive integer
n
such that every subset of
[3]^{n}
with density at least
\delta
inside
[3]^{n}
must contain a combinatorial line, that is, some triple
(x,y,z)
of elements of
[3]^{n}
such that for some partition
\{X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},W\}
of
[n]
we have
x_{i}=y_{i}=z_{i}=j
for every
j\in[3]
and every
i\in X_{j}
, as well as
x_{i}=0,y_{i}=1,z_{i}=2
for every
i\in W
. If
A,B,C
are subsets of
[3]^{n}
such that
A\times B\times C
contains a combinatorial line
(x,y,z)
, then for every mod-
p
form
\phi
, linearity shows that the triple
(\phi(x),\phi(y),\phi(z))
must be an arithmetic progression in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. In other words, if for some mod-
p
form
\phi
the triple of images
(\phi(A),\phi(B),\phi(C))
does not contain an arithmetic progression, then
A\times B\times C
contains no combinatorial line. Similar obstructions defined using mod-
p
forms, some of which involving
\{0,1\}^{n}
rather than
\{0,1,2\}^{n}
, will be discussed and ruled out in the upcoming paper
[
7
]
in the context of a conjectured polynomial generalisation of the density Hales-Jewett conjecture and of its simplest unsolved case discussed in
[
4
]
.

In the present paper, rather than focus on obstructions we will use mod-
p
forms to address a more basic kind of question which arises in numerous contexts as a way of measuring the expressive power of a set of functions or of data. Given a class
\mathcal{C}
of objects and a class
\mathcal{F}
of functions defined on
\mathcal{C}
, how many objects in
\mathcal{C}
can we select such that for any two of them we can find a function in
\mathcal{F}
that clearly separates them ? In our case, the class
\mathcal{C}
of objects will be that of sufficiently dense subsets of the cube
\{0,1\}^{n}
and the class
\mathcal{F}
of functions will be the functions sending a set in
\mathcal{C}
to the distribution of a mod-
p
form on that set. Answering this question happens to furthermore be of independent Ramsey-theoretic interest, since it can be equivalently formulated as follows: how many dense subsets of the cube can we choose until we necessarily can find a pair of them for which the pair of distributions of every mod-
p
form are not too far apart ?

1.2
Notions of separation on the distributions of mod-
p
forms

We begin by defining a number of ways to measure proximity between two probability distributions.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
m\geq 2
be an integer. For any
m
-tuple
(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
of distributions each taking values in the same finite set
D
, we define the following quantities.

(i)

The
diameter

\Diam(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
is defined to be
\max\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\TV(X_{i},X_{j})
, where

\TV(X_{i},X_{j})=\sum\limits_{d\in D}|\mathbb{P}(X_{i}=d)-\mathbb{P}(X_{j}=d)|

is the total variation distance between
X_{i}
and
X_{j}
for all
i,j\in[m]
.

(ii)

The
correlation

\cor(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
is defined to be

\big(\sum\limits_{d\in D}\mathbb{P}[X_{1}=d]\dots\mathbb{P}[X_{m}=d]\big)-1/|D|^{m-1}.

(iii)

The
overlap

\omega(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
is defined to be

\sum\limits_{d\in D}\min(\mathbb{P}[X_{1}=d],\dots,\mathbb{P}[X_{m}=d]).

We note in particular that if all but at most one of the variables
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
are uniformly distributed (resp. approximately uniformly distributed), then
\cor(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
is zero (resp. small in absolute value), and that if
\omega(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})>0
, then the intersection of the ranges of
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
is not empty.

These definitions allow us to establish a qualitative hierarchy in the extent to which distributions of random variables
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
are not too far apart. Our definition for the correlation may be slightly surprising, but the centered expression

\sum_{d\in D}(\mathbb{P}[X_{1}=d]-|D|^{-1})\dots(\mathbb{P}[X_{m}=d]-|D|^{-1}),

which might appear at first to be more natural to consider, does not quantify the extent to which the
m
distributions are similar: if for instance
m=3
,
D=\mathbb{F}_{3}
and
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}
satisfy
\mathbb{P}[X_{i}=d]=\delta_{i,d}
for all
i,d\in[3]
then that quantity is equal to
2/9
, which is positive, even though the ranges of
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}
are pairwise disjoint. Each of the following properties is qualitatively stronger than the next, in the sense that whenever
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, for every choice of the parameters in the
i+1
th property which is sufficiently close to
0
(in a manner that depends on
|D|
and
m
), there is a choice of parameters (which may further depend on
|D|
and
m
) in the
i
th property which implies it.

1.

Close distributions for some
\eta>0
:

\Diam(X_{1},\dots X_{m})\leq\eta.

2.

Almost positive correlation for some
\nu>0
:

\cor(X_{1},\dots X_{m})\geq-\nu.

3.

Overlap bounded away from
0
for some
A>0
:

\omega(X_{1},\dots X_{m})\geq A.

4.

Overlapping distributions:

\omega(X_{1},\dots X_{m})>0.

Proof.

The first implication follows from Hölder’s inequality: if
\Diam(X_{1},\dots X_{m})\leq\eta
, then
\TV(X_{1},X_{i})\leq\eta
for each
i\in[m]
, so

\sum_{d\in D}\mathbb{P}[X_{1}=d]\dots\mathbb{P}[X_{m}=d]\geq\sum_{d\in D}(\mathbb{P}[X_{1}=d]-\eta)^{m}\geq|D|^{-(m-1)}(1-|D|\eta)

and hence

\cor(X_{1},\dots X_{m})\geq-|D|^{m-2}\eta.

The second implication follows from a short calculation: if
\cor(X_{1},\dots X_{m})\geq-\nu
then

\sum_{d\in D}\mathbb{P}[X_{1}=d]\dots\mathbb{P}[X_{m}=d]\geq 1/|D|^{m-1}-\nu,

so there exists
d\in D
satisfying

\mathbb{P}[X_{1}=d]\dots\mathbb{P}[X_{m}=d]\geq 1/|D|^{m}-\nu/|D|,

and hence

\mathbb{P}[X_{i}=d]\geq 1/|D|^{m}-\nu/|D|

for each
i\in[m]
, from which

\omega(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})\geq 1/|D|^{m}-\nu/|D|

follows. The third implication is immediate. ∎

If
E
is an event and
\mathcal{A}
is a non-empty subset of
\{0,1\}^{n}
then we shall write
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}[E]
for the probability that
x
satisfies
E
when
x
is chosen uniformly at random inside
\mathcal{A}
. If
F
is a function defined on
\{0,1\}^{n}
and
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
are non-empty subsets of
\{0,1\}^{n}
, then we write
\TV_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}(F)
for the total variation distance between the distributions of
F(x)
when
x
is chosen uniformly at random from
\mathcal{A}
and when it is chosen uniformly at random from
\mathcal{B}
. If
m\geq 2
is a positive integer and
\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{m}
are non-empty subsets of
\{0,1\}^{n}
, then we write
\Diam_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{m}}(F)
for
\Diam(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
, where for each
i\in[m]
the distribution of the variable
X_{i}
is the distribution of
F(x)
for
x
chosen uniformly at random in
\mathcal{A}_{i}
. We define
\cor_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{m}}(F)
and
\omega_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{m}}(F)
in a similar way.

1.3
Main results

Starting with a number
s
of non-empty subsets
\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{s}
of
\{0,1\}^{n}
, we can ask whether it is possible to obtain a pair
(i,j)
of distinct elements of
[s]
such that all mod-
p
forms have close distributions on the pair
(\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{A}_{j})
. As we will now illustrate, this is not possible in general, even if the number
s
grows exponentially with
n
.

In the case
p=2
, that can be seen from the fact that the
2^{n}-1
non-zero linear forms
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{2^{n}-1}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
each have a different linear hyperplane of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
as their kernels; since these hyperplanes have pairwise intersections of size
2^{n-2}
, the hyperplanes
\mathcal{A}_{i}=\ker\phi_{i}
with
i\in[2^{n}-1]
are such that
\phi_{i}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
only takes the value
0
on
\mathcal{A}_{i}
but takes the values
0
and
1
with equal probability on every
\mathcal{A}_{j}
with
j\neq i
.

For
p\geq 3
, it is no longer true in general that two different mod-
p
forms have the same preimage of
0
inside
\{0,1\}^{n}
, even if they are proportional: for instance the mod-
5
forms
x_{1}+x_{2}
and
2x_{1}+x_{2}
both have the same preimage
\{x_{1}=x_{2}=0\}
of
0
inside
\{0,1\}^{n}
. However, we can find a set of mod-
p
forms with size exponential in
n
and within which this is the case. We begin by partitioning
[n]
into pairwise disjoint sets
I_{1},\dots,I_{2d}
each of size
p
, with
d=\lfloor n/2p\rfloor
and a remainder set
I_{0}
with size at most
2p-1
. We then restrict our attention to the mod-
p
forms
\phi
of the type

\phi(x)=a_{1}x_{1}+\dots+a_{n}x_{n}

where for every
u\in[d]
, all coefficients
a_{z}
with
z\in I_{2u-1}\cup I_{2u}
are the same. We can then find a maximal set of such mod-
p
forms with size at least
p^{d-1}=p^{\lfloor n/2p\rfloor-1}
such that no two of them are proportional. We then index the forms in this set as
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{t}
.

We then take
x
to be the element of
\{0,1\}^{n}
such that
x_{i}=0
whenever
i\in I_{0}
or i in
I_{u}
for some odd
u
, and
x_{i}=1
whenever
i\in I_{u}
for some non-zero even
u
. We have
\phi_{i}(x)=0
for every
i\in[t]
, since the contribution of each set
I_{u}
of coordinates is zero. Let now
i,j
be distinct indices in
[t]
. Because
\phi_{i}
and
\phi_{j}
are not proportional, there exist distinct
u_{1},u_{2}\in[d]
such that the pair
(A_{1,i},A_{2,i})
of coefficients of
\phi_{i}
on
I_{2u_{1}-1}\cup I_{2u_{1}}
and on
I_{2u_{2}-1}\cup I_{2u_{2}}
is not proportional to the pair
(A_{1,j},A_{2,j})
of coefficients of
\phi_{j}
on
I_{2u_{1}-1}\cup I_{2u_{1}}
and on
I_{2u_{2}-1}\cup I_{2u_{2}}
. Viewing
A_{1,i},A_{2,i},A_{1,j},A_{2,j}
as integers in
[0,p-1]
, we use
x
to define a new element
y
of
\{0,1\}^{n}
by changing
A_{2,i}
of the coordinates of
x
in
I_{2u_{1}-1}
to
1
and changing
A_{1,i}
of the coordinates of
x
in
I_{2u_{2}}
to
0
. We then have

\displaystyle\phi_{i}(y)-\phi_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=A_{2,i}A_{1,i}-A_{1,i}A_{2,i}=0

\displaystyle\phi_{j}(y)-\phi_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=A_{2,i}A_{1,j}-A_{1,i}A_{2,j}\neq 0,

so
\phi_{i}(y)=0
but
\phi_{j}(y)\neq 0
. We have shown that for every pair
(i,j)
of distinct elements of
[t]
, the set
\phi_{i}^{-1}(0)
is not contained in the set
\phi_{j}^{-1}(0)
.

One of the tools that we will introduce later, Proposition
2.2
, shows that any event on
\{0,1\}^{n}
defined by a bounded number
k
of mod-
p
forms has probability either equal to 0 or bounded below in a way that depends on
p
and
k
only. This implies in particular a positive lower bound that depends only on
p
on the probability, for
x
chosen uniformly in
\{0,1\}^{n}
, of the event

\phi_{j}(x)=0,\phi_{i}(x)\neq 0,

and this now allows us to conclude as we did for
p=2
.

Example 1.2
.

Let
p\geq 2
be a prime. The subsets
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\phi_{1}^{-1}(0),\dots,\mathcal{A}_{t}=\phi_{t}^{-1}(0)
of
\{0,1\}^{n}
each have density at least
2^{-(p-1)}
inside
\{0,1\}^{n}
and satisfy

\TV_{\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{A}_{j}}\phi_{i}\geq\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}[\phi_{i}=0]-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{j}}[\phi_{i}=0]\geq 2^{-2(p-1)}

for any pair
(i,j)
of distinct elements of
[t]
.

Proof.

The lower bound on the densities of the subsets
\mathcal{A}_{i}
follows from Proposition
2.2
. The first inequality follows from the definition. The second follows from the calculation

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}[\phi_{i}=0]-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{j}}[\phi_{i}=0]

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}[\phi_{i}=0,\phi_{j}=0]/\mathbb{P}[\phi_{j}=0]

\displaystyle=1-(\mathbb{P}[\phi_{j}=0]-\mathbb{P}[\phi_{j}=0,\phi_{i}\neq 0])/\mathbb{P}[\phi_{j}=0]

\displaystyle\geq 1-(\mathbb{P}[\phi_{j}=0]-2^{-2(p-1)})/\mathbb{P}[\phi_{j}=0]

\displaystyle\geq 1-(1-2^{-2(p-1)})

where all probabilities on the right-hand side are taken with respect to
x
chosen uniformly in
\{0,1\}^{n}
, and where in the third line we apply Proposition
2.2
.∎

We will nonetheless begin by showing that close distributions can be obtained if we only consider mod-
p
forms with bounded support size and assume the number of dense subsets of
\{0,1\}^{n}
to grow superpolynomially in
n
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\delta>0
, let
k\geq 1
be an integer, and let
\nu>0
. Then there exists
c_{\TV}(\delta,\nu,k)>0
such that that the following holds. If
s
is a positive integer and
\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{s}
are non-empty subsets of
\{0,1\}^{n}
with density at least
\delta
inside
\{0,1\}^{n}
, there exists a positive integer
N(\nu,k)
such that for all
n\geq N(\nu,k)
, there exists a subset
S\subset[s]
with size at least
s/(n+1)^{c_{\TV}(\delta,\nu,k)}
such that

\Diam_{\mathcal{A}_{i}:i\in S}g\leq\nu

for every function
g
defined on
\{0,1\}^{n}
and determined by at most
k
coordinates.

Proposition
1.3
does not involve the structure of the function
g
, and in particular does not require
g
to be a mod-
p
form. However, we will use some of the lemmas and ideas involved in the proof of Proposition
1.3
in our later proofs, and in the case where
g
is a mod-
p
form it seems worthwhile to compare its conclusion to those that we can or cannot obtain when
g
is not assumed to have bounded support size, as we will do in the final summarising table of the present section.

We note that the bound that we get in Proposition
1.3
cannot be replaced by a sufficiently low power of
n
, even for a fixed value of
k
and even if we are only aiming for a conclusion on all mod-
p
forms with bounded support rather than on more general functions.

Example 1.4
.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer. For every subset
I
of
[n]
with size
k
let
\mathcal{A}_{I}
be the set of
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
such that
x_{i}=0
for every
i\in I
, and let
\phi_{I}
be the mod-
p
form such that
\phi(x)
is defined to be the sum of the coordinates
x_{i}
with
i\in I
. Then the sets
\mathcal{A}_{I}
each have density at least
2^{-(p-1)}
, and whenever
I,J
are distinct subsets of [n] with size
k
we have

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{I}}(\phi_{I}=0)-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{J}}(\phi_{I}=0)\geq 1/2.

Proof.

The lower bound on the densities of the sets
\mathcal{A}_{I}
comes from Proposition
2.2
. The inequality follows from pairing elements of
\{0,1\}^{n}
according to one of the coordinates in
J\setminus I
. For any fixed choice of any of the other coordinates, either zero or both elements of the pair belong to
\mathcal{A}_{I}
but at most one element of each pair belongs to
\mathcal{A}_{J}
. ∎

Let us now examine what we can or cannot hope for in that case, where we ask for a result on all mod-
p
forms rather than merely those with bounded support size. We may first aim for a weaker conclusion and ask for a pair
(i,j)
of distinct elements of
[s]
such that all mod-
p
forms have almost positive correlation on the pair
(\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{A}_{j})
. If the number
s
of sets
\mathcal{A}_{i}
is only known to tend to
\infty
with
n
then the following example shows that for
p\geq 3
it is still not possible in general to obtain such a pair. To state it, it will be convenient to identify elements
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
with respective subsets
A\subset[n]
defined by
A=\{i\in[n]:x_{i}=1\}
. We note that this example does not apply to
p=2
, as it then leads to a correlation of zero, rather than to a negative correlation.

Example 1.5
.

Let
p\geq 3

2.1
Basic results on mod-

We begin this section by recalling properties on the distribution of mod-

\{0,1\}^{n}

[

]
, Proposition 2.4 and
[
10

, Lemma 2.3. The first property informally states that a set of several mod-

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
p
be a prime, and let
k\geq 1
,

be integers. If
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k}:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

2.2
Information-theoretic tools

In this subsection we recall tools from information theory and prove some statements that will be repeatedly useful to us throughout this paper.

The
Shannon entropy

\mathbb{H}(X)
of a random variable
X
taking values in a finite set
D
is defined by the formula

\mathbb{H}(X)=-\sum_{x\in D}\mathbb{P}[X=x]\log\mathbb{P}[X=x].

It is a standard inequality that
\mathbb{H}(X)\leq\log|D|
and that
\mathbb{H}(X)=\log|D|
if and only if
X
is uniformly distributed on
D
. We will quantify how far
X
is from the uniform distribution by the difference

\mathbb{J}(X)=\log|D|-\mathbb{H}(X),

which is often called the
negentropy
of
X
, between the largest possible entropy
\log|D|
and the entropy of
X
. An expansion around the uniform distribution provides the following estimate which will be convenient for us to use in our arguments, although a more qualitative statement would suffice for them to work.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a random variable taking values in a finite set
D
. Then there exists
\eta_{0}(D)>0
such that for every
\eta\in[0,\eta_{0})
, if
\mathbb{J}(X)\leq\eta
then

\sum_{x\in D}(\mathbb{P}[X=x]-1/|D|)^{2}\leq(8/|D|)\mathbb{J}(X)

and in particular

|\mathbb{P}[X=x]-1/|D||\leq(8\eta/|D|)^{1/2}

for every
x\in D
.

For
X,Y
random variables taking values respectively in finite domains
D_{X}
and
D_{Y}
, the conditional entropy defined by

\mathbb{H}(X|Y)=\mathbb{H}((X,Y))-\mathbb{H}(Y)

satisfies

\mathbb{H}(X|Y)\leq\mathbb{H}(X)\text{ and }\mathbb{H}((X,Y))\leq\mathbb{H}(X)+\mathbb{H}(Y).

We similarly define

\mathbb{J}(X|Y)=\mathbb{J}((X,Y))-\mathbb{J}(Y)

and the inequalities

\mathbb{J}(X|Y)\geq\mathbb{J}(X)\text{ and }\mathbb{J}((X,Y))\geq\mathbb{J}(X)+\mathbb{J}(Y)

follow from the corresponding reverse inequalities for the entropy. Just as the conditional entropy
\mathbb{H}(X|Y)
can be expressed as

-\sum\limits_{y\in D_{Y}}\mathbb{P}[Y=y]\sum\limits_{x\in D_{X}}(\mathbb{P}[X=x|Y=y]\log\mathbb{P}[X=x|Y=y])=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim Y}\mathbb{H}(X|Y=y),

the quantity
\mathbb{J}(X|Y)
can in turn be written and interpreted as
\mathbb{E}_{y\sim Y}\mathbb{J}(X|Y=y)
. Therefore, one technical aspect to be aware of while using the quantity
\mathbb{J}(X|Y)
, and which will come up in our proofs, is that
\mathbb{J}(X|Y)
being low does not by itself guarantee that for every
y\in D_{Y}
,
X
is close to uniformly distributed conditionally on the event
Y=y
; this is however necessarily the case if the probability of this event is known to be bounded away from zero, since

\mathbb{J}(X)\geq\mathbb{P}[Y=y]\mathbb{J}(X|Y=y).

(1)

Whenever
F:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow D
is a function with finite codomain
D
and
\mathcal{A}
is a non-empty subset of
\{0,1\}^{n}
, we shall write
\mathbb{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(F)
and
\mathbb{J}_{\mathcal{A}}(F)
respectively for the entropy and negentropy of the variable
F(x)
, where
x
is chosen uniformly at random inside
\mathcal{A}
.

Throughout we will consider families of functions which on the whole of
\{0,1\}^{n}
are either uniformly distributed (in the case of coordinate forms of the type
x_{z}
for some
z\in[n]
, which have codomain
\{0,1\}
) or approximately uniformly distributed (in the case of mod-
p
forms with high support) on their respective codomains
\{0,1\}
and
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. As these functions have zero or low negentropy on the whole of
\{0,1\}^{n}
, the negentropy of these functions on a non-empty subset
\mathcal{A}
of
\{0,1\}^{n}
is a useful measure of how different their distribution on
\mathcal{A}
is from their distribution on
\{0,1\}^{n}
.

Let us consider a family
\mathcal{F}
of functions defined on
\{0,1\}^{n}
. We can for instance take the family

\mathcal{F}_{0}:=\{x_{z}:z\in[n]\}

of coordinate forms
\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
, which will be of particular relevance to us. We emphasize that the codomain of these coordinate forms will always be taken to be
\{0,1\}
, even when this is not explicitly mentioned.

For
\mathcal{A}
a non-empty subset of
\{0,1\}^{n}
, a first task is to attribute to
\mathcal{A}
a family
\mathcal{I}
of sets of elements of
[n]
which “represents” the anomalous behaviour of
\mathcal{A}
. For instance, if
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{0}
and

\mathcal{A}=\{A\in\{0,1\}^{n}:A(x_{1})+A(x_{2})=1\}

then we will want in particular

(i)

\{1\}
not to belong to
\mathcal{I}
: on its own, the coordinate is uniformly distributed.

(ii)

\{1,2\}
to belong to
\mathcal{I}
: the pair
(x_{1},x_{2})
is far from uniformly distributed on
\mathcal{A}
, even if
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
individually are.

(iii)

\{1,2,3\}
not to belong to
\mathcal{I}
: while it is true that the distribution of
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
is far from uniform, the coordinate
x_{3}
contributes to this lack of uniformity neither on its own nor through its interactions with the coordinates
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
.

We now give the formal definition that we will use.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a non-empty subset of
\{0,1\}^{n}
, let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of functions defined on
\{0,1\}^{n}
and let
\eta>0
. For each positive integer
k\geq 1
let
\mathcal{I}_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F},\eta)
be the family of non-empty subsets
T
of
\mathcal{F}
with size at most
k
that satisfy one of the following two conditions.

(i)

|T|=\{f\}
for some
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
\mathbb{J}(f)\geq\eta
.

(ii)

|T|>1
and furthermore

\mathbb{J}(T)-\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq t}\mathbb{J}(T_{i})\geq\eta.

(2)

for every
t\geq 2
and every partition
T=\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq t}T_{i}
of
T
(into non-empty sets).

Whenever a non-empty subset
T
of
\mathcal{F}
with size
2\leq|T|\leq k
and a partition
\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq t}T_{i}
of
T
with
t\geq 2
do not satisfy (
2
), we say that
T

reduces to

\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq t}T_{i}
. If
f\in\mathcal{F}
satisfies
\mathbb{J}(f)<\eta
, then we also say that
\{f\}

reduces
to the empty set. For each positive integer
k\geq 1
, we define

I_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F},\eta)=\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{I}_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F},\eta)}T.

We call a set
irreducible
if it has size at most
k
and belongs to
\mathcal{I}_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F},\eta)
, and
reducible
if it is a subset of
\mathcal{F}
of size at most
k
that is not irreducible.

It follows in particular from Definition
2.4
that if
k\geq 1
is a positive integer,
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are disjoint and non-empty subsets of
\mathcal{F}
with
|F_{1}|+|F_{2}|\leq k
, and
F_{1}\cup F_{2}\in\mathcal{I}_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F},\eta)
, then
\mathbb{J}(F_{2}|F_{1})
, which is equal to
\mathbb{J}(F_{1}\cup F_{2})-\mathbb{J}(F_{1})
, is at least
\mathbb{J}(F_{2})+\eta
, and hence is in particular at least
\eta
.

Also, if a set
T\subset\mathcal{F}
with
|T|\leq k
has empty intersection with
I_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F},\eta)
, then it follows from an immediate induction on
|T|
and the (negation of the) definition of an irreducible set that

\mathbb{J}(T)\leq(2|T|-1)\eta\leq(2k-1)\eta.

In particular, the joint distribution of
(f:f\in T)
is therefore approximately uniform on the codomain of
(f:f\in T)
. We now show that this inequality holds more generally after conditioning on some subset
U
of
\mathcal{F}
of small size.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
T,U
be two disjoint subsets of
\mathcal{F}
such that
1\leq|T|+|U|\leq k
and
T\cap I_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F},\eta)
is empty. Then

\mathbb{J}(T|U)\leq(2k-1)\eta.

Proof.

By definition,
I_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F},\eta)
contains all irreducible sets, so any set that is not contained in
I_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F},\eta)
is reducible. We select a family of sets as follows. We first select the family
\{\{T\cup U\}\}
, and then iterate the following inductive step: if a set
F
in the family reduces to a family
F_{1},\dots,F_{t}
, then we deselect
F
and select all sets
F_{1},\dots,F_{t}
, stopping the iterations when we obtain a family where we can no longer reduce any set (including singletons to the empty set).

At the end of the process we obtain a partition
T\cup U=\bigcup_{i}A_{i}
where the sets
A_{i}
are all contained in

(T\cup U)\cap I_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F},\eta)=U\cap I_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F},\eta).

We have reduced at most
2|T\cup U|-1
times, and at each reduction the sum of the negentropies of the selected sets of functions has decreased by at most
\eta
, so

\mathbb{J}(T\cup U)\leq\sum\limits_{i}\mathbb{J}(A_{i})+(2|T\cup U|-1)\eta.

Using furthermore that

\sum\limits_{i}\mathbb{J}(A_{i})\leq\mathbb{J}(U\cap I_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F},\eta))\leq\mathbb{J}(U),

and writing
\mathbb{J}(T|U)=\mathbb{J}(T\cup U)-\mathbb{J}(U)
we conclude that

\mathbb{J}(T|U)\leq(2|T\cup U|-1)\eta.

The result follows by the assumption on
|T|+|U|
. ∎

We now show an upper bound on the size of
\mathcal{I}_{\leq k}
that depends on how far
\mathcal{F}
is from a jointly uniform distribution on
\{0,1\}^{n}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
D
be a finite set, let
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow D
be a function, and let
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{0}\cup\{f\}
. Let
\delta,\eta>0
. Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\{0,1\}^{n}
be a subset of density at least
\delta
. Then the following facts hold.

(i)

The negentropy
J_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{F})
satisfies the upper bound
J_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{F})\leq\log\delta^{-1}+\log|D|
.

(ii)

The size
K=|\mathcal{I}_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F},\eta)|
satisfies the inequality
J_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{F})\geq\eta((K/k)/k!)^{1/k}
.

(iii)

The size
K
satisfies the upper bound
K\leq(k+1)!(\eta^{-1}\log(\delta^{-1}|D|))^{k}
.

Proof.

We first prove (i). We start with the inequality

\mathbb{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},f)\geq\mathbb{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),

which implies that

\mathbb{J}_{\mathcal{A}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},f)\leq\mathbb{J}_{\mathcal{A}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})+\log|D|.

(3)

Since
\mathcal{A}
has density at least
\delta
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}[(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=u]\leq\delta^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{\{0,1\}^{n}}[(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=u]

for every
u\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, and therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\geq-\sum_{u\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}[(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=u]\log\mathbb{P}_{\{0,1\}^{n}}[(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=u]-\log\delta^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{H}_{\{0,1\}^{n}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})-\log\delta^{-1},

since
\mathbb{P}_{\{0,1\}^{n}}[(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=u]
is the same for all
u\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. We deduce the upper bound

\mathbb{J}_{\mathcal{A}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\leq\mathbb{J}_{\{0,1\}^{n}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})+\log\delta^{-1}=\log\delta^{-1}.

Combining this with the inequality (
3
) finishes the proof of (i).

We next prove (ii). By the pigeonhole principle, at least
K/k
of the sets have common size
l\leq k
. By the Erdős-Rado sunflower theorem (proved in
[
1
]
) one can extract from them a sunflower of
r
such sets as long as

k!(r-1)^{k}\leq K/k,

or equivalently

r\leq 1+((K/k)/k!)^{1/k}.

Writing the centre as
T_{0}
and the petals as
T_{1},\dots,T_{r}
we have the lower bound

\mathbb{J}(\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq r}T_{i})\geq\mathbb{J}(\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq r}T_{i}|T_{0})\geq\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq r}\mathbb{J}(T_{i}|T_{0})\geq r\eta

which proves (ii). It is then immediate to deduce (iii) from (i) and (ii). ∎

The following statement will be essential to our proofs of Theorem

.

Let

(4)

\epsilon
of the pairs
(i,j)\in L^{2}
, there exist
x\in A_{i}
and
y\in A_{j}
such that

B(x,r)\cap B(y,r)\neq\emptyset.

(5)

Then there exist a subset
L_{1}
of
L
such that
|L_{1}|\geq(\epsilon/C)|L|
, an element
i\in L_{1}
and an element
x\in A_{i}
such that for each
j\in L_{1}
, there exists
y\in A_{j}
such that

B(y,r)\subset B(x,3r).

Proof.

The set version is the special case
r=0
of the metric entropy version, so it suffices to prove that latter version. By the assumption and an averaging argument there exists
i\in L
such that for a proportion at least
\epsilon
of the indices
j\in L
, there exist
x\in A_{i}
and
y\in A_{j}
satisfying (
5
). Let
L^{\prime}
be the family of these indices
j
. Since
|A_{i}|\leq C
, by the pigeonhole principle there exists
x\in A_{i}
such that for a proportion at least
1/C
of the indices
j\in L^{\prime}
there exists
y\in A_{j}
satisfying (
5
), so in particular
B(y,r)\subset B(x,3r)
. We then take
L_{1}
to be the set of these indices
j
. ∎

As we will explain later in our proofs in Section

, iterating Lemma

several times in its set version shows that we can find a dense subset
\Lambda
of

and a set

with size at most
C
such that

for a proportion at least
1-\epsilon
of the pairs
(i,j)\in\Lambda^{2}
. Iterating the metric entropy version several times gives an analogous consequence: we can find a dense subset
\Lambda
of
[s]
and a set
A
of at most
C
elements such that the intersection
A_{i}\cap A_{j}
is contained in the union of balls with radius
3r
centered at the elements of

for a proportion at least

of the pairs
(i,j)\in\Lambda^{2}
.

3
Proofs of the main results

Whenever

is a function defined on

.

3.1
Obtaining close distributions

We first set out to prove Proposition
1.3
. To do this, we first use irreducible sets to give a condition which entails close distributions. We prove it in slightly greater generality than is needed in our proof of Proposition
1.3
as we shall use the more general version later on.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
k\geq 1
,
D_{0}\geq 1
be positive integers, and let
\eta>0
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of functions defined on
\{0,1\}^{n}
each with finite codomain of size at most
D_{0}
. Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
be non-empty subsets of
\{0,1\}^{n}
, and let
I_{\mathcal{A}}
and
I_{\mathcal{B}}
be the respective unions of all sets in
\mathcal{I}_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F},\eta)
and in
\mathcal{I}_{\leq k}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{F},\eta)
. Assume that
I_{\mathcal{A}}=I_{\mathcal{B}}
and that
\TV_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}(I_{\mathcal{A}})\leq\eta
. Then

\TV_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}(T)\leq 10D_{0}^{k}k^{1/2}\eta^{1/4}=o_{\eta\rightarrow 0,k,D_{0}}(1)

for every
T\subset\mathcal{F}
with
|T|\leq k
.

Proof.

We write
I
for the set
I_{\mathcal{A}}=I_{\mathcal{B}}
. Let
T\subset\mathcal{F}
with
|T|\leq k
, and let
x
be a value in the codomain of
(f:f\in T)
. We can write

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}[T=x]=\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}[I=x_{|I}]\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}[T\setminus I=x_{|T\setminus I}|I=x_{|I}]

(6)

and similarly for the probability
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}}[T=x]
.

Assume that
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}[I=x_{|I}]\leq\eta^{1/2}/2
or
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}}[I=x_{|I}]\leq\eta^{1/2}/2
. Without loss of generality we can assume that the first bound holds. Then by the assumption on
\TV_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}
we have
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}}[I=x_{|I}]\leq\eta^{1/2}/2+\eta
. The decomposition (
6
) and its analogue for
\mathcal{B}
then show that
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}[T=x]
and
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}}[T=x]
are both at most
\eta^{1/2}/2+\eta
, so in particular

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}[T=x]-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}}[T=x]|\leq\eta^{1/2}+\eta.

Assume now instead that
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}[I=x_{|I}]
and
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}[I=x_{|I|}]
are both at least
\eta^{1/2}/2
. Then by (
1
) we have

\mathbb{J}_{\mathcal{A}}(T\setminus I|I=x_{|I})\leq 2\eta^{-1/2}\mathbb{J}_{\mathcal{A}}(T\setminus I),

so by Lemma
2.5
we get

\mathbb{J}_{\mathcal{A}}(T\setminus I|I=x_{|I})\leq 4\eta^{-1/2}k\eta,

and then by Lemma
2.3
we obtain

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}[T\setminus I=x_{|T\setminus I}|I=x_{|I}]-1/(|\cod(T\setminus I)||\leq((32k\eta^{1/2}/|\cod(T\setminus I)|)^{1/2}.

By our assumption we have

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}[I=x_{|I}]-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}}[I=x_{|I}]|\leq\eta,

so by the decomposition (
6
) and its analogue for
\mathcal{B}
we obtain

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}[T=x]-\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{B}}[T=x]|\leq 2\eta+(32k\eta^{1/2}/|\cod(T\setminus I)|)^{1/2}\leq 2\eta+8k^{1/2}\eta^{1/4}.

Summing over all
x\in\cod(f:f\in T)
we therefore conclude

\TV_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}(T)\leq D_{0}^{k}(2\eta+8k^{1/2}\eta^{1/4}).\qed

We now deduce a statement which implies Proposition
1.3
as a special case.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
k,K,D_{0}\geq 1
be integers. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of functions defined on
\{0,1\}^{n}
each with finite codomain of size at most
D_{0}
. Let
\eta\in(0,1)
. Let
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in L}
be a finite family of non-empty subsets of
\{0,1\}^{n}
. We assume

|\mathcal{I}_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{F},\eta)|\leq K

(7)

for each
i\in L
. Then there exists
N(k,K)
such that if
|\mathcal{F}|\geq N
, then there exists
L^{\prime}\subset L
with
|L^{\prime}|\geq\frac{(\eta/Kk)^{D_{0}^{Kk}}}{2\binom{|\mathcal{F}|}{kK}}|L|
satisfying

\Diam_{\mathcal{A}_{i}:i\in L^{\prime}}(g)\leq 10D_{0}^{k}k^{1/2}\eta^{1/4}

(8)

for every function
g
of the type
F\circ(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})
where
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in\mathcal{F}
and
F
is some arbitrary function defined on
\cod(f_{1})\times\dots\times\cod(f_{k})
.

Proof.

For each
i\in L
let
I_{i}=I_{\leq k}(\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{F},\eta)
. By our assumption (
7
) and a union bound we obtain
|I_{i}|\leq kK
for each
i\in L
. There are therefore at most

\sum_{0\leq t\leq kK}\binom{|\mathcal{F}|}{t}\leq 2\binom{|\mathcal{F}|}{kK}

possibilities for each set
I_{i}
, provided that
|\mathcal{F}|
is large enough. By the pigeonhole principle there exists a subset
L_{1}
of
L
of size at least
|L|/(2\binom{|\mathcal{F}|}{kK})
and a set
I\subset[n]
with
|I|\leq kK
such that
I_{i}=I
for every
i\in L_{1}
. Applying the pigeonhole principle again, there exists a subset
L^{\prime}
of
L_{1}
with size at least
(\eta/Kk)^{D_{0}^{Kk}}|L_{1}|
and a probability distribution
\Delta
on
\prod_{i\in I}\cod(f_{i})
such that
I_{i}=I
for every
i\in L^{\prime}
, and the distribution on
\mathcal{A}_{i}
of the map
(f:f\in I)
is within total variation distance at most
\eta
of
\Delta
. We conclude by Proposition
3.1
. ∎

Using Proposition
2.6
we obtain Proposition
1.3
as a special case of Proposition
3.2
by taking
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{0}
,
D_{0}=2
and
K=(k+1)!(\eta^{-1}\log(2\delta^{-1}))^{k}
.

3.2
Obtaining almost positive correlations

We next set out to prove Theorem
1.6
. If we now consider a family of superpolynomially many dense subsets
\mathcal{A}_{i}\subset\{0,1\}^{n}
rather than just one, and assume that the centres
\phi_{1},\dots\phi_{b}
with
b\leq B
of the balls involved in Proposition
2.7
are the same as
i
varies, then applying Proposition
3.2
to the family
\mathcal{F}_{0}\cup\{\phi_{1},\dots\phi_{b}\}
and to
k=b+r
shows that there exist distinct
i,j
such that every mod-
p
form which is within support distance at most
r
of one of the mod-
p
forms
\phi_{1},\dots\phi_{b}
has close distributions on
\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
\mathcal{A}_{j}
. As this is still the case for any other mod-
p
form (since it is approximately uniformly distributed on both
\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
\mathcal{A}_{j}
), we conclude (by taking
\alpha
to be sufficiently small and then
n
to be sufficiently large) that every mod-
p
form has close distributions on
\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
\mathcal{A}_{j}
.

However, in general the centres of the balls can vary from one index
i
to another, which is why the proof we have just suggested will not work in general (and a conclusion as strong as that of Proposition
1.3
cannot hold for all mod-
p
forms, as we have already discussed in Example
1.2
). Instead we shall resort to Lemma
2.8
to obtain a situation which is more similar to the one that we just described.

The next proposition provides us with a structure that will allow us to conclude almost positive correlations.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
p
be a prime, let
m\geq 2
be an integer, and let
\delta>0,\eta>0,\alpha>0,\epsilon>0
. Then there exist positive integers
r,B
depending on
\delta,\alpha
(and
p
) only and positive integers

depending on
\delta,\eta,\alpha,\epsilon,m
(and
p
) only, such that the following holds. For all integers

and all non-empty subsets
\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{s}
of
\{0,1\}^{n}
each with density at least
\delta
, there exist a subset
R\subset[s]
with
|R|\geq s/(A^{\prime}n^{A})
, a set
\Phi
of mod-
p
forms with
|\Phi|\leq B
, and a subset
I\subset\mathcal{F}_{0}\cup\Phi
with
|I|\leq C
such that the three following properties are satisfied.

(i)

For a proportion at least
(1-\epsilon)
of the
m

(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})\in R^{m}

\phi
is a mod-
p
form such that
\TV_{\mathcal{A}}(\phi,U_{p})\geq\alpha
for at least two distinct indices

, then
\phi
is contained in some ball
B(\phi,3r)
for some linear combination
\phi
of elements of
\Phi
.

(ii)

We have
I_{\leq 3r+B}(\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{F}_{0}\cup\Phi,\eta)=I
for every
i\in R
.

(iii)

The diameter
\Diam_{\mathcal{A}_{i}:i\in R}(I)
is at most
\eta
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.7
there exist positive integers

and
B
(depending on
\delta
and
\alpha
) such that for each
i\in[s]
we can associate with
\mathcal{A}_{i}
a family
\Phi_{i}
of size at most
B
of mod-
p
forms such that every mod-
p
form

satisfying
\TV_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}(\phi,U_{p})\leq\alpha
is contained in the union of balls
\bigcup_{\psi\in\Phi_{i}}B(\psi,r)
.

We then iterate the metric entropy version of Lemma
2.8
on the sets
\Phi_{i}
as follows to obtain a set
\Phi
of “common” mod-
p
forms. Let
\epsilon^{\prime}
be a quantity that will depend on
\epsilon
and
n
and which we shall fix later.

If a proportion at least
\epsilon^{\prime}
of the pairs
(i,j)
of indices in

are such that
\mathcal{A}_{i}
and

satisfy (
5
) for some

and

2.8
we obtain a subset

with

and a mod-

form

. We then define
\Phi_{j}^{1}:=\Phi_{j}\setminus\{\phi\}
for each

. Otherwise, we stop the process.

More generally, at step

of the process, if a proportion at least
\epsilon^{\prime}
of the pairs
(i,j)
of elements of
L_{a-1}
satisfies (
5
) for some

and

, then applying Lemma
2.8
we obtain a subset
L_{a}\subset L_{a-1}
with
|L_{a}|\geq(\epsilon^{\prime}/B)|L_{a-1}|
and a mod-
p
form
\phi_{a}
such that for every

. We then define

for each

. Otherwise, we stop the process.

Let

be the number of steps that we take before the process stops. We necessarily have

and for each

). We then let

and obtain a subset

with size at least

such that for a proportion at least

of the pairs

We then assign to each

and the union bound has size at most some

depending on

There are therefore at most

. For

By the pigeonhole principle there therefore exists a subset

of

and a subset

such that

for all

.

The family

, considered jointly as one function, has a codomain with size at most

. Applying the pigeonhole principle on the distribution of

we obtain a subset

with

such that

. Choosing

\epsilon^{\prime}=((\eta/p^{C})^{p^{C}}/(4n^{C}))^{2}\epsilon

then ensures that (i) is satisfied in the case

, and

\epsilon^{\prime}=\binom{m}{2}^{-1}((\eta/p^{C})^{p^{C}}/(4n^{C}))^{2}\epsilon

suffices for general

by a union bound. ∎

We can now conclude the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
\nu>0
be fixed. We shall choose
\alpha
depending on
\nu
, then
\eta
depending on
\alpha
and
\nu
, and then apply Proposition
3.3
. Let
\Phi
and

be as in Proposition
3.3
. We fix
(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})
such that (i) from Proposition
3.3
is satisfied, and consider a mod-
p
form

.

Let us first assume that
\phi

3.3
Obtaining overlap bounded below

We finally set out to prove Theorem
1.7
. We begin with the following lemma, which provides a condition that suffices to guarantee lower bounds on the overlaps of distributions of mod-
p
linear forms on dense subsets of the cube.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
k\geq 1,m\geq 2
,
B\geq 1
,

be integers such that

. Let
\eta>0
. Let
\Phi
be a set of linear forms
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}

of

\omega_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{m}}(\phi)\geq 2^{-(m+1)(p-1)}-o_{\eta\rightarrow 0,k,m}(1).

By the assumption on

\phi=\psi+\sum_{z\in Z}a_{z}x_{z}

(9)

for each

be the set

\{\psi\}\cup\{x_{z}:z\in Z\},

.

We partition
T
into the sets

,
T_{1}=T\cap I_{1}
,…,
T_{m}=T\cap I_{m}
, and

. Writing
\phi_{0},\phi_{i},\phi_{\mathrm{out}}
for the respective contributions of

to the expression (

) of
\phi
, we obtain

\phi=\phi_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\phi_{i}+\phi_{\mathrm{out}}

(10)

as the resulting decomposition.

By hypothesis,
\TV_{\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(T_{0})\leq\eta
for each
i\in[m]
, so since
\phi_{0}
is a function of
T_{0}
, we have

\TV_{\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(\phi_{0})\leq\eta

(11)

for each
i\in[m]
. Letting

be the set of elements

of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}[\phi_{0}=u]\geq\eta^{1/20}+\eta
, by (

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}[\phi_{0}=u]\geq\eta^{1/20}
for every

and every

.

Let

be fixed. We then choose
v(u)=(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{m}
by selecting the
v_{i}
successively for each
i=1,\dots,m
. At the
i
th iteration we choose
v_{i}
such that

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}[\phi_{i}=v_{i}|\phi_{0}=u,\phi_{1}=v_{1},\dots,\phi_{i-1}=v_{i-1}]\geq p^{-1}

(12)

(such a

always exists provided that
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}[\phi_{0}=u,\phi_{1}=v_{1},\dots,\phi_{i-1}=v_{i-1}]\neq 0
, which we guarantee by the previous iterations). We now show that (
12
) then allows us to deduce

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{j}}[\phi_{i}=v_{i}|\phi_{0}=u,\phi_{1}=v_{1},\dots,\phi_{i-1}=v_{i-1}]\geq 2^{-(p-1)}-o_{\eta\rightarrow 0,k,m}(1)

(13)

for each

and hence for each
j\in[m]
.

Let

be fixed. Using the disjointness of the sets

(of mod-

\mathbb{J}_{\mathcal{A}_{j}}(T_{i}|T_{0},T_{1},\dots,T_{i-1})\leq 2k\eta

so by estimates analogous to those in the proof of Proposition

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{j}}[T_{i}=y_{i}|T_{0}=x,T_{1}=y_{1},\dots,T_{i-1}=y_{i-1}]=|\cod(T_{i})|^{-1}+o_{\eta\rightarrow 0,k}(1)

(14)

for all
(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{i-1})\in\cod(T_{0})\times\cod(T_{1})\times\dots\times\cod(T_{i-1})
satisfying

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{j}}[T_{0}=x,T_{1}=y_{1},\dots,T_{i-1}=y_{i-1}]\geq\eta^{1/2},

and for all

. Because

has size at most

and because by the previous choices

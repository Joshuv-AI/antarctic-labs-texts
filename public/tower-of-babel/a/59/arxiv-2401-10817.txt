A proof of the pentagon relation for skeins
Mingyuan Hu Department of Mathematics, Northwestern University

Abstract
. In [ HSZ23 ] , with Gus Schrader and Eric Zaslow we developed a skein-theoretic version of cluster theory, and made a conjecture on the pentagon relation for the skein dilogarithm. Here we give a topological proof of this conjecture. Combining [ MS21 ] and [ BCMN23 ] , we get a surjection from the skein algebra Sk + ​ ( T − D ) \mathrm{Sk}^{+}(T-D) to the positive part of the elliptic Hall algebra ℰ q , t + \mathcal{E}_{q,t}^{+} . Hence our pentagon relation generalizes the ones in [ Zen23 ] and [ GM19 ] .



1.
introduction

The pentagon relation arises in many areas of mathematics and physics.
For a family of operators
\{Q\}
, it takes the form:

Q\cdot Q=Q\cdot Q\cdot Q

Morally, it reflects some coherence in the system. A prominent example is the quantum dilogarithms
[
FK94
]
:

(1.0.1)

\Phi(\hat{V})\cdot\Phi(\hat{U})=\Phi(\hat{U})\cdot\Phi(q^{-1/2}\hat{V}\hat{U})\cdot\Phi(\hat{U}),

where
\hat{V}\hat{U}=q\hat{U}\hat{V}
, and
\Phi(X)=\mathrm{exp}(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}X^{k}}{k(q^{k/2}-q^{-k/2})})=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1+q^{\frac{1}{2}+n}X)
. One way to understand (
1.0.1
) is that it reflects where we land if we want to commute
\Phi(\hat{U})
and
\Phi(\hat{V})
.

In
[
GM19
]
, the authors obtained a pentagon relation in terms of Macdonald polynomials, generalizing (
1.0.1
). This pentagon relation is shown in
[
Zen23
]
to be equivalent to a pentagon relation in the elliptic Hall (or the Ding-Iohara-Miki) algebra
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
, utilizing the result
[
SV11
, Corollary 1.5]
.

The connection between skein modules and representation theory has been studied for years.
In
[
MS21
]
, a surjection from the skein algebra of a punctured torus
\mathrm{Sk}(T-D)
to
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
was conjectured:

(1.0.2)

\mathrm{Sk}(T-D)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{q,t}.

Combining
[
MS21
, Theorem 5.7]
and
[
BCMN23
, Theorem 5.10]
, we obtain such a map defined on a subalgebra
Sk^{+}(T-D)
. (See also
[
GL23
]
, where this map is defined on a larger subalgebra.)
As is pointed out in
[
MS21
, Remark 5.8]
,
\mathrm{Sk}(T-D)
is much “larger” than
\mathcal{E}_{q,t}
. Thus, it is natural to hope for a pentagon relation in
\mathrm{Sk}(T-D)
.

The pentagon relation also plays an important role in cluster theory.
In a recent paper
[
HSZ23
]
with Gus Schrader and Eric Zaslow, we developed a skein-theoretic version of cluster theory, and used it to compute the generating functions of Open Gromov-Witten invariants defined in
[
ES21
]
(also
[
SS23
]
). One of the key constructions is to define a “skein dilogarithm”
Q_{\mathbf{x}}
(or the “Baxter operator” in
[
HSZ23
]
, see Definition
2.8
), whose rank
1
reduction recovers the usual quantum dilogarithm. In this perspective, a pentagon relation for skeins is also desirable.

Main Theorem

(Theorem
2.10
)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
be the
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
curves on the punctured torus
T-D
. Then in
\widehat{\mathrm{Sk}}(T-D)
we have:

(1.0.3)

Q_{\mathbf{x}}\cdot Q_{\mathbf{y}}=Q_{\mathbf{y}}\cdot Q_{\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}}\cdot Q_{\mathbf{x}}.

Since on a general
2
-dimensional surface, the tubular neighborhood of any two simple closed curves is isomorphic to
T-D
, we get a pentagon relation for the skein algebra of any surface (Corollary
2.12
).

While this paper was being written, the author was informed that this result was also proved in
[
Nak
]
independently.

Abstract




Abstract
Let
X
be a ball quasi-Banach function space,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in(0,\infty)
. In this paper, the authors first introduce the new Herz-type Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H\dot{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\mathcal{HK}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
associated with ball
quasi-Banach function space
X
, via the non-tangential grand maximal function. Then, under some mild assumptions on
X
, the authors establish the real-variable theory for
\mathcal{H\dot{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\mathcal{HK}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, in terms of
maximal function characterizations, atomic and molecular decompositions,
and obtain
the boundedness of some sublinear operators from
\mathcal{H\dot{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to
\mathcal{\dot{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
from
\mathcal{HK}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to
\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. As appliccations, we give two concrete function spaces which are members of Herz-type Hardy spaces associated with ball quasi-Banach function spaces.

1
Introduction

The theory of Herz spaces, initiated by Beurling and Herz, plays a important role in the study of convolution algebra and Fourier multipliers (see
[
3
,
6
,
16
]
).
The investigation of the theory of Herz-type Hardy spaces, which can be traced back to the
late of 1980s in order to give a natural analog to the classical Hardy spaces,
has now been a very interesting topics in Harmonic analysis. In 1989, Chen et al.
[
9
]
and García-Cuerva
[
7
]
introduced now called non-homogeuous Herz-type Hardy spaces and then established their central atomic decomposition. After that, Lu et al. considered homogeuous Herz-type Hardy spaces and systematically studied Herz-type Hardy space with general
indices, and proved that the Herz-type Hardy space is a suitable local version of the classical
Hardy spaces and enjoys
some real-variable theory, such as maximal function characterizations, the
atomic and the molecular decompositions (see monograph
[
25
]
for details). Due to the above celebrated work of Chen, García-Cuerva and Lu et al., the extension of Herz-type Hardy spaces have been studied extensively and their real-variable theory has been well developed, for instance, weighted Herz-type Hardy spaces
[
24
,
28
]
, Herz-type Hardy spaces with variable exponent
[
33
]
and Herz-Morrey-Hardy spaces with variable exponents
[
36
]
. Very recently, Zhao et al.
[
41
]
studied the mixed Herz-type Hardy
spaces.

On the other hand, in 2017, Sawano et al.
[
29
]
first introduced the ball quasi-Banach
function space
X
, which generalized the Banach function space in
[
5
]
. The ball quasi-Banach function spaces contain many well-known function spaces, for instance,
weighted Lebesgue spaces, Morrey spaces, mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces, and Orlicz-
slice spaces, but they may not be quasi-Banach function spaces (see
[
29
,
40
]
for the details),
which implies that the studies on
X
have more
wide applications. Furthermore, Sawano et al.
[
29
]
introduced and investigated the Hardy space
H_{X}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

associated with
X
, and established their various maximal function characterizations by assuming that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function is bounded
on the
p
-convexification of
X
, and several other characterizations, respectively,
in terms of atoms, molecules, and Lusin-area functions by further assuming that
the Fefferman-Stein vector-valued maximal inequality is bounded on
X
and the powered Hardy-Littlewood maximal
operator is bounded on the associated space of
X
. Indeed, the real-variable theory of the Hardy space

H_{X}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, associated with the ball quasi-Banach function space
X
, provides an appropriate framework
that unifies the theory of variants of Hardy spaces, which have been investigated before, like
classical Hardy spaces, mixed-norm Hardy spaces, variable Hardy spaces, and Orlicz Hardy spaces (see
[
29
, Chapter 7]
for the details). Later, Zhang et al.
[
40
]
and
Wang et al.
[
34
]
introduced the weak Hardy space
WH_{X}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
associated with
X
and developed
its complete real-variable theory. Very recently, Wei et al.
[
35
]
considered Herz-type space associated with
X
. For more recent developments about ball quasi-Banach function spaces on, we refer the reader to
[
8
,
20
,
32
,
37
]
.

Inspired by Lu et al.
[
25
]
and Sawano et al.
[
29
]
, it is natural and interesting to
ask whether
there exist Herz-type Hardy spaces which unify the classical Herz-type Hardy spaces, the Herz-type Hardy spaces with variable
exponent and mixed Herz-type Hardy spaces as special cases. In this paper we shall answer this
problem affirmatively by introducing the new Herz-type Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H\dot{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\mathcal{HK}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
associated with ball
quasi-Banach function space
X
.

To be precise, this article is organized as follows.

In Section 2, we recall some notions concerning the ball (quasi)-Banach function space
X
, and introduce the definitions of the homogeneous Herz-type Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and the non-homogeneous Herz-type Hardy spaces

. Then, we recall some technical lemmas. In Section 3, we establish various maximal function characterizations of

(see

\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\mathcal{H}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
(see Theorem
4.3
below). As an application of the atomic characterization of
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\mathcal{H}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}),
we obtain the boundedness of some sublinear operators from
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and from
\mathcal{H}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to

(see Theorem

below). In Section 5, we establish the molecular characterization of
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

\mathcal{H}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
(see Theorems
5.4
and
5.5
below). In Section 6, we apply the main results obtained in this article to two concrete function spaces, namely, variable Lebesgue spaces

wide range of generality. More precisely, the real-variable theory of
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\mathcal{H}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, in terms of maximal function characterization,

exponent and mixed Herz-type Hardy spaces as special cases.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the definition of the ball quasi-Banach
function space
X
introduced in
[
29
]
. Then, we introduce the definitions of the homogeneous Herz-type Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and the non-homogeneous Herz-type Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
associated with the ball quasi-Banach function spaces
X
.

We always use the symbol
\mathfrak{M}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
denote the set of all measurable functions on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
. For any

x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
r\in(0,\,\infty)
, let
B(x,\,r):=\{y\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:|x-y|<r\}
and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{{B}}:=\{B(x,\,r):x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\ \ {\text{and}}\ \ r\in(0,\,\infty)\}.

Definition 2.1
.

A quasi-Banach space
X\subset\mathfrak{M}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is called a
ball quasi-Banach function space
if it
satisfies

(i)

\|f\|_{X}=0
implies that
f=0
almost everywhere;

(ii)

|g|\leq|f|
almost everywhere implies that
\|g\|_{X}\leq\|f\|_{X}
;

(iii)

0\leq f_{m}\uparrow f
as
m\rightarrow\infty
almost everywhere implies that
\|f_{m}\|_{X}\uparrow\|f\|_{X}
as
m\rightarrow\infty
;

(iv)

B\in\mathfrak{B}
implies that
\chi_{B}\in X
.

Furthermore, a ball quasi-Banach function space
X
is called a ball Banach function
space if the norm of
X
satisfies that for any
f,\,g\in X
,

\displaystyle\|f+g\|_{X}\leq\|f\|_{X}+\|g\|_{X}

and, for any
B\in\mathfrak{B}
, there exists a positive constant
C_{(B)}
, depending on
B
, such that,
for any
f\in X
,

\displaystyle\int_{B}|f(x)|dx\leq C_{(B)}\|f\|_{X}.

Notice that, in Definition
2.1
, if we replace any ball
B
by any bounded measurable set
E
,
we obtain its another equivalent definition.

The associate space
X^{\prime}
of any given ball Banach function space
X
is defined as follows ( see
[
5
,
Chapter 1, Section 2]
or
[
29
]
for more details).

Definition 2.2
.

For any given ball Banach function space
X
, its
associate space (also called the Köthe
dual space)

X^{\prime}
is defined by setting

X^{\prime}:=\left\{f\in\mathfrak{M}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}):\|f\|_{X^{\prime}}<\infty\right\},

where, for any
f\in\mathfrak{M}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
,

\|f\|_{X^{\prime}}:=\sup\left\{\|fg\|_{L^{1}}:g\in X,\|g\|_{X}=1\right\}

and
\|\cdot\|_{X^{\prime}}
is called the associate norm of
\|\cdot\|_{X}
.

Remark 2.3
.

(i)

By
[
29
, Proposition 2.3]
, we obtain that, if
X
is a ball Banach function
space, then its associate space
X^{\prime}
is also a ball Banach function space.

(ii)

As was mentioned in
[
29
, p.9]
and
[
34
, Sect. 5]
, the family of ball Banach function spaces includes Morrey spaces, mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces, variable Lebesgue spaces, weighted
Lebesgue spaces and Orlicz-slice spaces, which are not necessary to be Banach
function spaces.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
40
, Lemma 2.6]

Every ball Banach function space
X
coincides with its second associate
space
X^{\prime\prime}
. In other words, a function
f
belongs to
X
if and only if it belongs to
X^{\prime\prime}

and, in that case,

\|f\|_{X}=\|f\|_{X^{\prime\prime}}.

We also need to recall the definition of the
p
-convexification and the concavity of ball quasi-Banach function spaces, which is a
part of
[
29
, Definition 2.6]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
X
be a ball quasi-Banach function space and
p\in(0,\,\infty)
.

(i)

The
p
-convexification

X^{p}
of
X
is defined by setting

X^{p}:=\left\{f\in\mathfrak{M}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}):|f|^{p}\in X\right\},

equipped with the quasi-norm
\|f\|_{X^{p}}:=\||f|^{p}\|_{X}^{1/p}
.

(ii)

The space
X
is said to be
p
-concave
if there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for any
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset X^{1/p}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|f_{k}\|_{X^{1/p}}\leq C\left\|\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}f_{k}\right\|_{X^{1/p}}.

In particular, when
C=1
,
X
is said to be strictly
p
-concave.

Now we recall definitions of the homogeneous Herz-type spaces
\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and the non-homogeneous Herz-type spaces
\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha(\cdot),\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
associated with ball quasi-Banach function spaces
X
.
Throughout this paper, always let
B_{k}=\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:|x|\leq 2^{k}\}
and
D_{k}=B_{k}\setminus B_{k-1}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Denote briefly the characteristic function
\chi_{D_{k}}
=
\chi_{k}
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\tilde{\chi}_{k}
=
\chi_{k}
if
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\tilde{\chi}_{0}
=
\chi_{B_{0}}
. For any quasi-Banach space
X
, the space
L^{X}_{\text{loc}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
consists of all functions
f\in\mathfrak{M}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

such that
f\chi_{F}\in X
for all compact subsets
F\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.

Definition 2.6
.

[
35
, Definition 2.5]

Let
X
be a ball quasi-Banach function space,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in(0,\infty]
.
The
homogeneous Herz-type space

\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and the
non-homogeneous Herz-type space

{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
associated with ball quasi-Banach function spaces
X
are defined respectively by setting,

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}):=\left\{f\in L^{X}_{\text{loc}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\setminus\{0\}):\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}<\infty\right\}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}):=\left\{f\in L^{X}_{\text{loc}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}):\|f\|_{\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}<\infty\right\},

where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\left\{\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q}\left\|f\chi_{k}\right\|_{X}^{q}\right\}^{1/q}

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\left\{\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}2^{k\alpha q}\left\|f{\tilde{\chi}_{k}}\right\|_{X}^{q}\right\}^{1/q}.

Here, there is the usual modification when
q=\infty
.

In what follows, we introduce the definitions of the homogeneous Herz-type Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and the non-homogeneous Herz-type Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
associated with ball quasi-Banach function spaces
X
, via the non-tangential grand maximal function
M_{N}(f)
.

A
C^{\infty}
function
\varphi
is said to belong to the Schwartz class
{\mathcal{S}}
, if
for every multi-index
\alpha
and
\beta
, we have

\|\varphi\|_{\alpha,\,\beta}:=\displaystyle\sup_{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|<\infty
.
The dual space of
{\mathcal{S}}
, namely, the space of all tempered distributions on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
equipped with the weak-
\ast

topology, is denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
. For any
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
, let
M_{N}f
be the non-tangential grand maximal function of
f
defined by

\displaystyle M_{N}(f)(x):=\sup_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{N}}M_{\varphi}(f)(x),

where
{\mathcal{S}}_{N}:=\{\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}:\ \displaystyle\sup_{|\alpha|,\,|\beta|\leq N}\|\varphi\|_{\alpha,\,\beta}\leq 1\}
and
N>n+1
,
M_{\varphi}(f)
is the non-tangential maximal operator
M_{\varphi}(f)
defined by

\displaystyle M_{\varphi}(f)(x):=\sup_{|y-x|<t}|f*\varphi_{t}(y)|

with
\varphi_{t}(x):=t^{-n}\varphi(x/t)
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
X
be a ball quasi-Banach function space,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
q\in(0,\infty)
and
N>n+1
.
The
homogeneous Herz-type Hardy space

\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and the
non-homogeneous
Herz-type Hardy space

\mathcal{H}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
associated with ball quasi-Banach function spaces
X
are defined respectively by setting,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}:M_{N}(f)\in\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\right\}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\mathcal{{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}:M_{N}(f)\in{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\right\},

where

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}=\left\|M_{N}(f)\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

and

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}=\|M_{N}(f)\|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

Since there is no concrete norm expression for the space
X
, we need a very weak assumption about the boundedness of Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on ball Banach function space
X
and its associated space
X^{\prime}
.

For any
f\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

(the set
of all locally integrable functions on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
),
Hardy-Littlewood maximal function

M_{\mathrm{HL}}(f)
is defined by

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{HL}}(f)(x):=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B(x,\,r)|}\int_{B(x,\,r)}|f(y)|\,dy,

where
B(x,\,r)\in\mathfrak{{B}}
.

Assumption 1.
Let
X
be a ball Banach function space with associate space
X^{\prime}
. Then there exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that for any
f\in X
,

\displaystyle\|M_{\mathrm{HL}}(f)\|_{X}\leq C_{1}\|f\|_{X}

(2.1)

and

\displaystyle\|M_{\mathrm{HL}}(f)\|_{X^{\prime}}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{X^{\prime}},

where the positive constant
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
is independent of
f
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
X
be a ball Banach function space and satisfy Assumption 1. Then there exist a positive constant
C
and
0<\delta_{X},\,\delta_{X^{\prime}}<1

such that for all measurable sets
E\subset B
and all balls
B\in\mathfrak{B}
,

(i)

\frac{\|\chi_{B}\|_{X}}{\|\chi_{E}\|_{X}}\leq C\left(\frac{|B|}{|E|}\right);

(ii)

\frac{\|\chi_{E}\|_{X}}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{X}}\leq C\left(\frac{|E|}{|B|}\right)^{\delta_{X}};

(iii)

\frac{\|\chi_{E}\|_{X^{\prime}}}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{X^{\prime}}}\leq C\left(\frac{|E|}{|B|}\right)^{\delta_{X^{\prime}}};

(iv)

\frac{\|\chi_{B}\|_{X^{\prime}}}{\|\chi_{E}\|_{X^{\prime}}}\leq C\left(\frac{|B|}{|E|}\right).

(ii) and (iii) in Lemma
2.8
are from
[
35
, Lemma 2.4]
. The proof of (i) and (iv) in Lemma
2.8
is similar to
that of
[
19
, Lemma 5]
; we omit the details. Throughout this paper,
\delta_{X}
and
\delta_{X^{\prime}}
are the same as in Lemma
2.8
.

Lemma 2.9
.

[
35
, Theorem 3.2]

Let
X
be a ball quasi-Banach function space and satisfy (
2.1
) in Assumption 1 and
X^{\prime}=X^{*}
, where
X^{*}
denote the dual space of
X
, and let
-n\delta_{X}<\alpha<n\delta_{X^{\prime}}
and
q\in(0,\infty)
. Then Hardy-Littlewood maximal function
M_{\mathrm{HL}}
is bounded on
\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, respectively.

Remark 2.10
.

It is worth mentioning that the condition
X^{\prime}=X^{*}

is satisfied for a
large class of ball Banach function spaces. In fact, it was proved in
[
5
, Chapter 1,
Corollary 4.3]
that a Banach function space
X
has an absolutely continuous norm if
and only if
X^{\prime}=X^{*}
. It is not hard to varify that this conclusion also holds for ball
Banach function spaces. As is known to all, many function spaces, such as Lebesgue
spaces, variable exponent Lebesgue spaces, mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces, Lorentz
spaces and Orlicz spaces have absolutely continuous norms under some mild conditions, and therefore, they satisfy
X^{\prime}=X^{*}

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
X
be a ball quasi-Banach function space and satisfy (
2.1
) in Assumption 1 and
X^{\prime}=X^{*}
, and let
0<\alpha<n\delta_{X^{\prime}}
and
q\in(0,\infty)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\cap L^{X}_{\mathrm{loc}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\setminus\{0\})=\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\cap L^{X}_{\mathrm{loc}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})={\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}).

Proof.

Let
X
,
\alpha
and
q
be as in the present proposition. From
[
31
]
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle M_{N}(f)(x)\leq CM_{\rm HL}(f)(x),\ \ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},

(2.2)

which, combined with Lemma
2.9
, further yield that
M_{N}f
is bounded on
\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, respectively. By this, we easily obtain that Proposition
2.11
holds true.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

[
40

Let
X

\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|f(x)g(x)|\,dx\leq C\|f\|_{X}\|g\|_{X^{\prime}}.

This inequality is named the generalized Hölder inequality.

Lemma 2.13
.

[
35
, Lemma 2.3]

Let
X
be a ball Banach function space with associate space
X^{\prime}
. Then there exists a positive constant
C>0
such that for any

,

\frac{1}{|B|}\|\chi_{B}\|_{X}\|\chi_{B}\|_{X^{\prime}}\leq C.

3
The Maximal Function Characterizations of
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

and
\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

In this section, we show the maximal function characterizations of
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
using
the radial, the non-tangential and the tangential maximal function of a single test function
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
. We first give the notions of the radial and the tangential maximal functions.

Definition 3.1
.

For
f\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
and
\tilde{N}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, define the radial and the tangential maximal functions, respectively as

M^{0}_{\varphi}f(x):=\sup_{t>0}|f\ast\varphi_{t}(x)|,\ \ \ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}

and

T^{\tilde{N}}_{\varphi}f(x):=\sup_{(y,\,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}|(f\ast\varphi_{t})(y)|\left(\frac{t}{|x-y|+t}\right)^{\tilde{N}},\ \ \ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.

Remark 3.2
.

It is immediate that we have the following pointwise estimate between the radial and the non-tangential maximal functions:

M^{0}_{\varphi}f(x)\leq M_{\varphi}f(x),\ \ \ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.

To prove the maximal function characterizations of
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, we need a very weak assumption as follows.

Assumption 2.
Let
X
be a ball Banach function space. For any
p\in(1,\,\infty)
, assume that
X^{p}
is a ball Banach function space, and satisfies that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator

M_{\mathrm{HL}}
is bounded on
X^{p}
and
(X^{p})^{*}=(X^{p})^{\prime}
, where
X^{p}
is as in Definition
2.5
and
(X^{p})^{*}
denote the dual space of
X^{p}
.

Now we state the main result as follows in this section.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be a ball Banach function space and satisfy Assumption 2, and let
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
,
q\in(0,\,\infty)
,
\widetilde{N}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
large enough and
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}
satisfy
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)\,dx\neq 0
. Then for any
f\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}
, the following are mutually equivalent:

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\,\,\left(\text{or}\,\,\,f\in\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\right);

(3.1)

\displaystyle T^{\tilde{N}}_{\varphi}f\in\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\,\,\left(\text{or}\,\,\,T^{\tilde{N}}_{\varphi}f\in{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\right);

(3.2)

\displaystyle M_{\varphi}f\in\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\,\,\left(\text{or}\,\,\,M_{\varphi}f\in{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\right);

(3.3)

\displaystyle M^{0}_{\varphi}f\in\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\,\,\left(\text{or}\,\,\,M^{0}_{\varphi}f\in{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\right);

(3.4)

In this case,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}=\left\|M_{N}(f)\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C_{1}\left\|T^{\tilde{N}}_{\varphi}f\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C_{2}\left\|M_{\varphi}f\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\left\|M^{0}_{\varphi}f\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C_{4}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\left(\text{or}\,\,\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}=\left\|M_{N}(f)\right\|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\leq C^{\prime}_{1}\left\|T^{\tilde{N}}_{\varphi}f\right\|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C^{\prime}_{2}\left\|M_{\varphi}f\right\|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C^{\prime}_{3}\left\|M^{0}_{\varphi}f\right\|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C^{\prime}_{4}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\right),

where the positive constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
C_{3}
and
C_{4}
(
C^{\prime}_{1}
,
C^{\prime}_{2}
,
C^{\prime}_{3}
and
C^{\prime}_{4}
) are independent of
f
.

Lemma 3.4
.

[
13
]

For any
N,\,\widetilde{N}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
N\geq\tilde{N}+n+1
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for any
f\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}
,

M_{N}(f)(x)\leq CT^{\tilde{N}}_{\varphi}(f)(x),\ \ \ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be a ball Banach function space and satisfy Assumption 2, and let
q\in(0,\,\infty)
,
p\in(1,\,\infty)
and
-n\delta_{X}^{*}<\alpha<n\delta_{X^{\prime}}^{*}
, where
\delta_{X}^{*}
and
\delta_{X^{\prime}}^{*}
are the corresponding indices in
Lemma
2.8

(ii)
and
(iii)
respectively when space
X
is replaced by
X^{p}
in Lemma
2.8
.
Then the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator

M_{\mathrm{HL}}
is bounded on
\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X^{p}}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{X^{p}}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, respectively.

Lemma
3.5
is a direct result of Lemma
2.9
.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

We only prove the homogeneous case. The non-homogeneous case is similar. For any
f\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}
and
N,\,\widetilde{N}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
N\geq\tilde{N}+n+1
, from
Remark
3.2
, Lemma
3.4
and the definition of non-tangential grand maximal function
M_{N}f
, we deduce that

M^{0}_{\varphi}f(x)\leq M_{\varphi}f(x)\leq M_{N}(f)(x)\leq CT^{\tilde{N}}_{\varphi}(f)(x),\ \ \ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},

where the positive constant
C
is independent of
f
. Thus, we easily obtain that (
3.2
)
\Rightarrow

(
3.1
)
\Rightarrow
(
3.3
)
\Rightarrow
(
3.4
) holds true. It remains to show (
3.4
)
\Rightarrow
(
3.2
).

Suppose now
M^{0}_{\varphi}f\in\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. For
L,\,N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
,
\epsilon>0
and
f\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}
, define a maximal function obtained from truncation with an additional extra decay term as

u_{\epsilon,L,N}(x):=\sup_{t>0}\,\sup_{|x-y|<Nt<1/\epsilon}\left|(f*\varphi_{t})(y)\right|\left(\frac{Nt}{Nt+\epsilon}\right)^{L}(1+\epsilon N|y|)^{-L},\ \ \ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.

We claim that for any
r\in(0,\,1)
,

\displaystyle\left\|u_{\epsilon,L,N}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq CN^{n/r}\left\|M^{0}_{\varphi}(f)\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

(3.5)

Indeed, let

U_{\epsilon,L,N}(x):=\sup_{t>0}\,\sup_{|x-y|<Nt<1/\epsilon}t\left|\partial_{y}(f*\varphi_{t})(y)\right|\left(\frac{Nt}{Nt+\epsilon}\right)^{L}(1+\epsilon N|y|)^{-L},\ \ \ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.

From the estimates in
[
14
]
, we conclude that there exists integer
L>0
large enough such that for any
q_{1}\in(0,1)
and
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

U_{\epsilon,L,N}(x)\leq C\left[M_{\mathrm{HL}}\left((u_{\epsilon,L,N})^{q_{1}}\right)(x)\right]^{1/q_{1}},

where the positive constant
C
is independent of
\epsilon,\,N
and
f
. Let

\Omega_{\epsilon}:=\left\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:U_{\epsilon,L,N}(x)\leq C_{0}u_{\epsilon,L,N}(x)\right\}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \Omega_{\epsilon}^{\complement}:={{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon},

where
C_{0}
is a positive constant to be specified later. Taking
q_{1}\in(0,\,1)
such that
0<q_{1}\alpha<n\delta_{X^{\prime}}^{*}
, then from Definition
2.5
and Lemma
3.5
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|u_{\epsilon,L,N}\chi_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{\complement}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}^{-1}\left\|U_{\epsilon,L,N}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\leq CC_{0}^{-1}\left\|M_{\mathrm{HL}}\left((u_{\epsilon,L,N})^{q_{1}}\right)\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X^{1/q_{1}}}^{q_{1}\alpha,\,q/q_{1}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}^{1/q_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq CC_{0}^{-1}\left\|(u_{\epsilon,L,N})^{q_{1}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X^{1/q_{1}}}^{q_{1}\alpha,\,q/q_{1}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}^{1/q_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq CC_{0}^{-1}\left\|u_{\epsilon,L,N}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})},

and hence

\displaystyle\left\|u_{\epsilon,L,N}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|u_{\epsilon,L,N}\chi_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}+\left\|u_{\epsilon,L,N}\chi_{\Omega_{\epsilon}^{\complement}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|u_{\epsilon,L,N}\chi_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}+CC_{0}^{-1}\left\|u_{\epsilon,L,N}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

Choosing
C_{0}
sufficiently large, we get

\displaystyle\left\|u_{\epsilon,L,N}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq 2\left\|u_{\epsilon,L,N}\chi_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

Therefore, to show (
3.5
), it suffices to prove that for any
r\in(0,\,1),

\displaystyle\left\|u_{\epsilon,L,N}\chi_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq CN^{n/r}\left\|M^{0}_{\varphi}(f)\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

(3.6)

Next, let’s prove (
3.6
). We first show that for any
x\in\Omega_{\epsilon}
and
r\in(0,\,1)
,

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon,L,N}(x)\leq CN^{n/r}\left[M_{\mathrm{HL}}\left(M^{0}_{\varphi}(f)\right)^{r}(x)\right]^{1/r}.

(3.7)

For any
x\in\Omega_{\epsilon}
, by the definition of
u_{\epsilon,L,N}
, we know that there exists
(y,\,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
such that
|x-y|<Nt<1/\epsilon
and

\displaystyle|(f*\varphi_{t})(y)|\geq\left|(f*\varphi_{t})(y)\right|\left(\frac{Nt}{Nt+\epsilon}\right)^{L}(1+\epsilon N|y|)^{-L}>\frac{u_{\epsilon,L,N}(x)}{2}.

(3.8)

For any
x\in\Omega_{\epsilon}
and
z\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
satisfying
|x-z|<Nt
, by the definition of
\Omega_{\epsilon}
, we get

\displaystyle t\left|\partial_{z}(f*\varphi_{t})(z)\right|\leq C_{0}\left(\frac{Nt}{Nt+\epsilon}\right)^{-L}(1+\epsilon N|y|)^{L}u_{\epsilon,L,N}(x).

Combining with the above two inequalities, we know that if
x\in\Omega_{\epsilon}
,
|x-y|<Nt
and
|x-z|<Nt
,
then
t\left|\partial_{z}(f*\varphi_{t})(z)\right|\leq C_{1}|(f*\varphi_{t})(y)|
. Applying the mean value theorem, we obtain that for
\omega\in B(x,\,Nt)\cap B(y,\,t/(2C_{1}))
,

\displaystyle|(f*\varphi_{t})(\omega)-(f*\varphi_{t})(y)|\leq\left|\partial_{z}(f*\varphi_{t})(z)\right||\omega-y|\leq\frac{(f*\varphi_{t})(y)}{2},

where
z=\theta\omega+(1-\theta)y
and
\theta\in(0,\,1)
. From this and (
3.8
), it follows that if
x\in\Omega_{\epsilon}
and
\omega\in B(x,\,Nt)\cap B(y,\,t/(2C_{1}))
,
then

|(f*\varphi_{t})(\omega)|\geq\frac{(f*\varphi_{t})(y)}{2}\geq\frac{u_{\epsilon,L,N}(x)}{4}.

Thus, for any
r\in(0,\,1)
and
x\in\Omega_{\epsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{HL}}\left(M^{0}_{\varphi}(f)\right)^{r}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{B(x,\,Nt)}\int_{B(x,\,Nt)}\left(M^{0}_{\varphi}(f)(\omega)\right)^{r}d\omega

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{B(x,\,Nt)}\int_{B(x,\,Nt)\cap B(y,\,t/(2C_{1}))}|(f*\varphi_{t})(\omega)|^{r}d\omega

\displaystyle\geq CN^{-n}(u_{\epsilon,L,N}(x))^{r},

which implies that (
3.7
) holds true. Letting
r
sufficiently close to
0
such that
0<r\alpha<n\delta_{X^{\prime}}^{*}
, then from (
3.7
), Definition
2.5
and Lemma
3.5
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|u_{\epsilon,L,N}\chi_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{n/r}\left\|\left[M_{\mathrm{HL}}\left(M^{0}_{\varphi}(f)\right)^{r}\right]^{1/r}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle=CN^{n/r}\left\|M_{\mathrm{HL}}\left(M^{0}_{\varphi}(f)\right)^{r}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X^{1/r}}^{r\alpha,\,q/r}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{n/r}\left\|M^{0}_{\varphi}(f)\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

Therefore, this finishes the proof the claim (
3.5
).

Now we prove that

\displaystyle\left\|T^{\tilde{N}}_{\varphi}(f)\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq CN^{n/r}\left\|M^{0}_{\varphi}(f)\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

For any
f\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}
and
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
N>1
, define

M_{\varphi,\,N}^{*}(f)(x):=\sup_{t>0}\,\sup_{|x-y|<N\,t}\left|(f*\varphi_{t})(y)\right|,\ \ \ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.

From a simple computation, we deduce that

T^{\tilde{N}}_{\varphi}(f)(x)\leq M_{\varphi}(f)(x)+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j\tilde{N}}M_{\varphi,\,2^{j+1}}^{*}(f)(x),\ \ \ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.

From the absolute continuity of
\|\cdot\|_{X}
(see Remark
2.10
), we deduce that
\|\cdot\|_{X}
satisfies the Fatou lemma. By this and the definition of
\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, we obtain that

\left\|M_{\varphi,\,N}^{*}(f)(x)\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq\left\|u_{\epsilon,L,N}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

From the above two estimates and (
3.5
), it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|T^{\tilde{N}}_{\varphi}(f)\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|M_{\varphi}(f)\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}+C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j\tilde{N}}\left\|M_{\varphi,\,2^{j+1}}^{*}(f)\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|M_{\varphi}^{0}(f)\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j(\tilde{N}-n/r)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|M_{\varphi}^{0}(f)\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})},

where
\tilde{N}>n/r
. Thus, (
3.4
)
\Rightarrow
(
3.2
) holds true. This completes the proof Theorem
3.3
.
∎

4
The Atomic Decompositions of
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

In this section, we establish atomic decompositions of the homogeneous Herz-type Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and the non-homogeneous Herz-type Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
associated with ball quasi-Banach function spaces
X
.
We first begin with
the following notions of
(X,\,\alpha,\,s)
-atom.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a ball Banach function space,
\alpha\in[n\delta_{X^{\prime}},\,\infty)
and
s\in[(\alpha-n\delta_{X^{\prime}}),\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.

(1)

A
central
(X,\,\alpha,\,s)
-atom
is a measurable function
a
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
satisfying

(i)

(support)
{\rm{\,supp\,}}a\subset B_{r}:=B(0,\,r)\in\mathfrak{B}
;

(ii)

(size)
\|a\|_{X}\leq{|B(0,\,r)|^{-\alpha/n}}
;

(iii)

(vanishing moment)
\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}a(x)x^{\beta}dx=0
for any
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
with
|\beta|\leq s
.

(2)

A
central
(X,\,\alpha,\,s)
-atom of restricted type
is a measurable function
a
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
satisfying the conditions (ii), (iii) above and

(i)’

{\rm{\,supp\,}}a\subset B_{r}
,
r\geq 1
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
X
be a ball Banach function space,
q\in(0,\,\infty)
,
\alpha\in[n\delta_{X^{\prime}},\,\infty)
and
s\in[(\alpha-n\delta_{X^{\prime}}),\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.

(i)

The
homogeneous Herz-type atom Hardy space

\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}^{\alpha,\,s,\,q}_{X,\,\rm{atom}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
associated with
X
is defined to be the set of all tempered distribution

f\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}
of the form
f=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
,
where
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
are central
(X,\,\alpha,\,s)
-atoms as in Definition
4.1
respectively, supported on
\{B_{r_{j}}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
,
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}

and
\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}<\infty.

Moreover, the quasi-norm of
f\in\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}^{\alpha,\,s,\,q}_{X,\,\rm{atom}}
is defined by

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}^{\alpha,\,s,\,q}_{X,\,\rm{atom}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\inf\left(\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\right)^{1/q},

where the infimum is taken over all above decompositions of
f
.

(ii)

The
non-homogeneous Herz-type atom Hardy space

\mathcal{H}\mathcal{K}^{\alpha,\,s,\,q}_{X,\,\rm{atom}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
associated with
X
is defined to be the set of all tempered distribution

f\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}
of the form
f=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
,
where
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}
are central

(X,\,\alpha,\,s)
-atom of restricted type as in Definition
4.1
respectively, supported on
\{B_{r_{j}}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
with
r_{j}\geq 1
,
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}

and
\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}<\infty.

Moreover, the quasi-norm of
f\in\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}^{\alpha,\,s,\,q}_{X,\,\rm{atom}}
is defined by

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}^{\alpha,\,s,\,q}_{X,\,\rm{atom}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\inf\left(\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\right)^{1/q},

where the infimum is taken over all above decompositions of
f
.

Now we state the main result as follows.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
X
be a ball Banach function space and satisfy Assumption 1, and let
q\in(0,\,\infty)
,
\alpha\in[n\delta_{X^{\prime}},\,\infty)
and
s\in[(\alpha-n\delta_{X^{\prime}}),\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. Then we have

\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})=\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}^{\alpha,\,s,\,q}_{X,\,\rm{atom}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})=\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}^{\alpha,\,s,\,q}_{X,\,\rm{atom}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

with equivalent quasi-norms, respectively.

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

We only prove the homogeneous case. The non-homogeneous case is similar. The proof is divided into two steps.

Step 1.
In this step, we show that

\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}^{\alpha,\,s,\,q}_{X,\,\rm{atom}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\subset\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}).

(4.1)

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}^{\alpha,\,s,\,q}_{X,\,\rm{atom}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. By Definition
4.2
,
we know that there exist
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and
a sequence of central
(X,\,\alpha,\,s)
-atoms
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
,
supported, respectively, on
\{B_{r_{j}}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}

such that

f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\quad{\rm in}\ {\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}

and

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}^{\alpha,\,s,\,q}_{X,\,\rm{atom}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\sim\left(\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\infty.

Here, without loss of generality, suppose that
B_{r_{j}}:=B_{j}=\{x:\,|x|\leq 2^{j}\}
for
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. To prove
f\in\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, we consider two cases:
0<q\leq 1

and
1<q<\infty
.

Case 1:

0<q\leq 1
. It suffices to prove that there exists a positive constant
C
such that for each central

(X,\,\alpha,\,s)
-atom
a
,

\displaystyle\|M_{N}(a)\|_{\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C,

where the constant
C
is independent of
a
. For any given central

(X,\,\alpha,\,s)
-atom
a
with supp
a\subset B_{k_{0}}=\{x:\,|x|\leq 2^{k_{0}}\}
for some
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|M_{N}(a)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}^{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}+3}|B_{k}|^{\alpha q/n}\left\|M_{N}(a)\chi_{k}\right\|_{X}^{q}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\sum_{k=k_{0}+4}^{\infty}|B_{k}|^{\alpha q/n}\left\|M_{N}(a)\chi_{k}\right\|_{X}^{q}

\displaystyle=:{\rm I+II}.

First, let us deal with I. From (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and the size condition of
a
, it follows that

\displaystyle{\rm I}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|M_{N}(a)\right\|_{X}^{q}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}+3}|B_{k}|^{\alpha q/n}\leq\left\|M_{\rm HL}(a)\right\|_{X}^{q}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}+3}|B_{k}|^{\alpha q/n}\leq\|a\|_{X}^{q}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}+3}|B_{k}|^{\alpha q/n}\leq C.

For II, we need a pointwise estimate for
M_{N}(a)(x)
on
D_{k}
. Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
,
s\in{\mathbb{N}}

|\lambda_{j}|2^{-j(k_{0}+\alpha-n)},&\ j>0.\end{array}\right.

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|\mu_{j}|\leq C\left(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq C\|M_{N}f\|_{\dot{K}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

and

|\lambda_{j}|\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}a_{j}^{(i_{\nu})}(x)\phi(x)\,dx\right|\leq C|\mu_{j}|,

which, together with (
4.6
) and the dominated convergence theorem, further implies that

\langle f,\,\phi\rangle=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lim_{\nu\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{j}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}a_{j}^{(i_{\nu})}(x)\phi(x)\,dx=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lambda_{j}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}a_{j}(x)\phi(x)\,dx,

and hence (
4.4
) holds true. Thus, we have

\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\subset\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}^{\alpha,\,s,\,q}_{X,\,\rm{atom}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}).

This finishes the proof of Theorem
4.3
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

If
f\in\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\cap\mathcal{S}
, we can replace
f^{(i)}
by
f
in the proof
of step 2 , and have

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lambda_{j}a_{j}(x)+\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mu_{j}b_{j}(x),

where

\displaystyle\|a_{j}\|_{X}\leq\left|B(0,\,2^{j+1})\right|^{-\alpha/n},\,\,\|b_{j}\|_{X}\leq\left|B(0,\,2^{j+2})\right|^{-\alpha/n},

\displaystyle{\rm{\,supp\,}}a_{j}\subset\tilde{D}_{j,\,\epsilon},\,\,{\rm{\,supp\,}}b_{j}\subset\tilde{D}_{j,\,\epsilon}\cup\tilde{D}_{j+1,\,\epsilon}

and

\displaystyle 0\leq\lambda_{j},\,\mu_{j}\leq C2^{\alpha j}\sum_{k=j-1}^{j+1}\|M_{N}f\chi_{k}\|_{X}

with

.

Next, as an application of the atomic characterizations of
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
in Theorem
4.3
, we obtain the boundedness of some sublinear operators from
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
from
\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
as follows.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
X
be a ball Banach function space and satisfy Assumption 1., and let
q\in(0,\,\infty)
,
\alpha\in[n\delta_{X^{\prime}},\,\infty)
and
s\in[(\alpha-n\delta_{X^{\prime}}),\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. If a sublinear operator
T
satisfies that

(i)

T
is bounded on
X
;

(ii)

there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that
s+\delta>\alpha-n\delta_{X^{\prime}}
, and for any compact
support function
f
with

\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}f(x)\,x^{\beta}dx=0,\,\,\,|\beta|\leq s,

T(f)
satisfies the size condition

\displaystyle|T(f)(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C({\rm diam}\,({\rm supp}\,f))^{s+\delta}|x|^{-(n+s+\delta)}\|f\|_{L^{1}{({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}},

(4.7)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\mathrm{if}\,\,{\rm dist}\,(x,\,{\rm supp}\,f)\geq\frac{|x|}{2}.

Then there exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
independent of
f
such that, for any

f\in\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
f\in\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, respectively,

\|T(f)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C_{1}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

and

\|T(f)\|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

We only need to prove the homogeneous case. The non-homogeneous case can be proved in the similar way. Let
f\in\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. From Theorem
4.3
,
we know that there exist
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
and a sequence of central
(X,\,\alpha,\,s)
-atoms,
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
, supported, respectively,
on
\{{B_{j}}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
such that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\ \ \mathrm{in\ }\ {\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\sim\left(\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\infty.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|T(f)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}^{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q}\left\|T(f)\chi_{k}\right\|_{X}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q}\left(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k-2}|\lambda_{j}|\left\|T(a_{j})\chi_{k}\right\|_{X}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +C\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q}\left(\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|\left\|T(a_{j})\chi_{k}\right\|_{X}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle={\rm S_{1}+S_{2}}.

To deal with
{\rm S_{1}}
, we first estimate
\|T(a_{j})\|_{X}
.
For any
x\in D_{k}
and
j\leq k-2
, by (
4.7
), Lemma
2.12
and the size condition of
a_{j}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle|T(a_{j})(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C2^{j(s+\delta)}|x|^{-(n+s+\delta)}\int_{B_{j}}\left|a_{j}(y)\right|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\,2^{j(s+\delta)}2^{-k(n+s+\delta)}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left|a_{j}(x)\right|\chi_{B_{j}}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\,2^{j(s+\delta)}2^{-k(n+s+\delta)}\left\|a_{j}\right\|_{X}\|\chi_{B_{j}}\|_{X^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,2^{j(s+\delta-\alpha)-k(n+s+\delta)}\|\chi_{B_{j}}\|_{X^{\prime}}.

From this, Lemmas
2.12
and
2.13
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|T(a_{j})\chi_{k}\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq C\,2^{j(s+\delta-\alpha)-k(n+s+\delta)}\|\chi_{B_{j}}\|_{X^{\prime}}\|\chi_{B_{k}}\|_{X}

(4.8)

\displaystyle=C\,2^{j(s+\delta-\alpha)-k(n+s+\delta)}\|\chi_{B_{j}}\|_{X^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\left(|B_{k}|\|\chi_{B_{k}}\|_{X^{\prime}}^{-1}\right)\left(\frac{1}{|B_{k}|}\|\chi_{B_{k}}\|_{X^{\prime}}\|\chi_{B_{k}}\|_{X}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\,2^{j(s+\delta-\alpha)-k(s+\delta)}\frac{\|\chi_{B_{j}}\|_{X^{\prime}}}{\|\chi_{B_{k}}\|_{X^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,2^{(s+\delta+n\delta_{X^{\prime}})(j-k)-j\alpha}.

Now, let’s estimate
{\rm S_{1}}
in the following two cases.

Case 1:

0<q\leq 1
. From (
4.8
), Fubini’s theorem and noting that
n\delta_{X^{\prime}}\leq\alpha<s+\delta+n\delta_{X^{\prime}}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle{\rm S_{1}}

\displaystyle=C\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q}\left(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k-2}|\lambda_{j}|\left\|T(a_{j})\chi_{k}\right\|_{X}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q}\left(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k-2}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}2^{[(s+\delta+n\delta_{X^{\prime}})(j-k)-j\alpha]q}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\sum_{k=j+2}^{\infty}2^{(s+\delta+n\delta_{X^{\prime}}-\alpha)(j-k)q}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}.

Case 2:

1<q\leq\infty
. Since
n\delta_{X^{\prime}}\leq\alpha<s+\delta+n\delta_{X^{\prime}}
, by (
4.8
) and the Hölder inequality and Fubini’s theorem, we obtain that

\displaystyle{\rm S_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q}\left(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k-2}|\lambda_{j}|2^{(s+\delta+n\delta_{X^{\prime}})(j-k)-j\alpha}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k-2}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}2^{(s+\delta+n\delta_{X^{\prime}}-\alpha)(j-k)q/2}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\left(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k-2}2^{(s+\delta+n\delta_{X^{\prime}}-\alpha)(j-k)q^{\prime}/2}\right)^{q/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\sum_{k=j+2}^{\infty}2^{(s+\delta+n\delta_{X^{\prime}}-\alpha)(j-k)q/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}.

Next, let’s estimate
{\rm S_{2}}
. Similarly to the estimate of
{\rm S_{1}}
, we consider two cases.

Case A:

0<q\leq 1
. By the boundedness of
T
on
X
, the size condition of
a_{j}
and Fubini’s theorem, we conclude that

\displaystyle{\rm S_{2}}

\displaystyle=C\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q}\left(\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|\left\|T(a_{j})\chi_{k}\right\|_{X}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q}\left(\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\left\|a_{j}\right\|_{X}^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q}\left(\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\left|B_{j}\right|^{-\alpha q/n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{j+1}2^{(k-j)\alpha q}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}.

Case B:

1<q\leq\infty
. By the Hölder inequality, boundedness of
T
on
X
, the size condition of
a_{j}
and Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle{\rm S_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q}\left(\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\left\|T(a_{j})\chi_{k}\right\|_{X}^{q/2}\right)\left(\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}\left\|T(a_{j})\chi_{k}\right\|_{X}^{q^{\prime}/2}\right)^{q/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q}\left(\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\left\|a_{j}\right\|_{X}^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q}\left(\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\left\|a_{j}\right\|_{X}^{q/2}\right)\left(\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}\left\|a_{j}\right\|_{X}^{q^{\prime}/2}\right)^{q/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q}\left(\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\left|B_{j}\right|^{-\alpha q/(2n)}\right)\left(\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}\left|B_{j}\right|^{-\alpha q^{\prime}/(2n)}\right)^{q/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k\alpha q/2}\left(\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\left|B_{j}\right|^{-\alpha q/(2n)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{j+1}2^{(k-j)\alpha q/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\lambda_{j}|^{q}.

Combining the above estimates related to
{\rm S_{1}}
and
{\rm S_{2}}
, we conclude that

\|T(f)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})},

where
C>0
is independent of
f
. This finishes the proof Theorem
4.5
.
∎

We first begin with

Definition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a ball Banach function space,
\alpha\in[n\delta_{X^{\prime}},\,\infty)

{\mathcal{{R}}}_{X}(M_{l})=\|M_{l}\|_{X}^{a/b}\left\||\cdot|M_{l}(\cdot)\right\|_{X}^{1-a/b}<\infty

\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}M_{l}(x)x^{\beta}dx=0

\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}

.

(2)

When

(\alpha,X;\,s,\epsilon)_{l}

restricted type.

Definition 5.2

Let
X
be a ball Banach function space,
\alpha\in[n\delta_{X^{\prime}},\,\infty)

(\alpha,X;\,s,\epsilon)
-molecule if it satisfies

.

(2)

The following lemma shows that an atom is a molecule.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
X
be a ball Banach function space,
\alpha\in[n\delta_{X^{\prime}},\,\infty)
,
0<q<\infty
and
s\in[(\alpha-n\delta_{X^{\prime}}),\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}

and

(i)

If
M
is a central
(X,\alpha,s)
-atom, then
M
is a central
(\alpha,X;\,s,\epsilon)
-molecule such that
{\mathcal{{R}}}_{X}(M)\leq C
with
C
independent of
M
.

(ii)

If
M
is a central
(X,\alpha,s)
-atom of restricted type, then
M
is a central
(\alpha,X;\,s,\epsilon)
-molecule of restricted type such that
{\mathcal{{R}}}_{X}(M)\leq C
with

.

Proof.

Let

be a central
(X,\alpha,s)
-atom with support on a ball

. It suffices to show that

. From the size condition of

\left\|M\right\|_{X}^{a/b}\left\||\cdot|M(\cdot)\right\|_{X}^{1-a/b}\leq r^{nb(1-a/b)}\left\|M\right\|_{X}\leq Cr^{\alpha}r^{-\alpha}\leq C,

which is wished.
∎

Now there is a position to state the molecular decompositions of

\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
X
be a ball Banach function space and satisfy Assumption 1,
\alpha\in[n\delta_{X^{\prime}},\,\infty)
,
0<q<\infty
and
s\in[(\alpha-n\delta_{X^{\prime}}),\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. Let
\epsilon>\max\{s/n,\,\alpha/n-\delta_{X^{\prime}}\}
,
a=\delta_{X^{\prime}}-\alpha/n+\epsilon
and
b=\delta_{X^{\prime}}+\epsilon
.

(i)

If
0<q\leq 1
, there exists a constant
C
such that for any central
(\alpha,X;\,s,\epsilon)
-molecule (or (
(\alpha,X;\,s,\epsilon)
-molecule of restricted type)
M
,

\|M\|_{\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{\dot{K}}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C\,\,(\text{or}\,\,\|M\|_{\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C).

(ii)

There exists a constant
C
such that for any
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
(or
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
) and dyadic central

(\alpha,X;\,s,\epsilon)_{l}
-molecule (or dyadic central
(\alpha,X;\,s,\epsilon)_{l}
-molecule of restricted type)
M_{l}
,

\|M_{l}\|_{\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{\dot{K}}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C\,\,(\text{or}\,\,\|M_{l}\|_{\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C).

Proof.

We only prove (i) for homogeneous case, the proof for non-homogeneous
case and the proof of (ii) are similar. For any central
(\alpha,X;\,s,\epsilon)
-molecule
M\in X
, it suffice to prove that there is a decomposition

M=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q_{k},

which satisfies that

(a)

there exist a constant
C>0
and a sequences of numbers
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k}
such that

F_{k}=\lambda_{k}a_{k}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|^{p}<\infty,

where each
a_{k}
is a central
(X,\alpha,s)
-atom;

(b)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q_{k}
has a central
(X,\alpha,s)
-atom decomposition.

Set

\sigma=\|M\|_{X}^{-1/\alpha},\,\,\,E_{0}:=\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:|x|\leq\sigma\}

and

E_{k,\,\sigma}=\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:2^{k-1}\sigma<|x|\leq 2^{k}\sigma\},\,\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

Let
B_{k,\,\sigma}=\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:|x|\leq 2^{k}\sigma\}
. We denote by
\chi_{k,\,\sigma}

the characteristic function of
E_{k,\,\sigma}
and by
\chi_{0,\,\sigma}
the characteristic function of
E_{0}
. Then we have

M(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}M(x)\chi_{k,\,\sigma}(x).

Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{s}
the class of all real polynomials of degree no more than
s
. Let

M_{k}(x):=M(x)\chi_{k,\,\sigma}(x)
and let
P_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}M_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{s}
be the unique polynomial satisfying

\int_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}\left(M_{k}(x)-P_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}M_{k}(x)\right)x^{\beta}dx,\,\,\,\beta\leq s.

Denote
Q_{k}:=(P_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}M_{k})\chi_{k,\,\sigma}
and
F_{k}:=M_{k}-Q_{k}
. Therefore, we can write

M=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q_{k},

Now we show (a). We first prove a size estimate for
F_{k}
. Without loss of generality, we can suppose that

{\mathcal{{R}}}_{X}(M)=1
, which implies that

\displaystyle\left\||\cdot|^{nb}M(\cdot)\right\|_{X}=\|M\|_{X}^{-a/(b-a)}=\sigma^{na}.

(5.1)

Choose
\{\varphi_{l}^{k}:|l|\leq s\}\subset\mathcal{P}_{s}
such that

\displaystyle\left\langle\varphi_{\mu}^{k},\,\varphi_{\nu}^{k}\right\rangle_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}=\frac{1}{|E_{k,\,\sigma}|}\int_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}\varphi_{\mu}^{k}(x)\,\varphi_{\nu}^{k}(x)dx=\delta_{\mu\,\nu}.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle Q_{k}(x)=\sum_{|l|\leq s}\left\langle M_{k},\,\varphi_{l}^{k}\right\rangle_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}\varphi_{l}^{k}(x)\ \ \text{for any}\ \ x\in E_{k,\,\sigma}.

(5.2)

By scaling, we obtain that

\displaystyle|Q_{k}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|E_{k,\,\sigma}|}\int_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}|M_{k}(x)|dx.

Thus, for any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, from Lemmas
2.12

and
2.13
, (
5.1
) and
b-a=\alpha/n
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|F_{k}\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\|M_{k}-Q_{k}\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\|M_{k}\|_{X}+\|Q_{k}\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\|M_{k}\|_{X}+\frac{C}{|E_{k,\,\sigma}|}\|M_{k}\|_{X}\|\chi_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}\|_{X^{\prime}}\|\chi_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\|M_{k}\|_{X}+C\|M_{k}\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\||\cdot|^{nb}M(\cdot)\right\|_{X}|2^{k}\sigma|^{-nb}

\displaystyle=C|2^{k}\sigma|^{-nb}|\sigma|^{-na}

\displaystyle=C2^{-kna}|B_{k,\,\sigma}|^{-\alpha}.

Let

\lambda_{k}:=C2^{-kna}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ a_{k}:=\frac{M_{k}-Q_{k}}{\lambda_{k}}.

Then, we have
F_{k}=\lambda_{k}a_{k}
and
a_{k}
is a central
(X,\alpha,s)
-atom supported in
B_{k,\,\sigma}
. Obviously,
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|^{p}<\infty
.

Next, we turn to show (b). Let
\{\psi_{l}^{k}:|l|\leq s\}\subset\mathcal{P}_{s}
be the dual basis of
\{x^{\gamma}:|\gamma|\leq s\}
with respect to the weight
{1}/{|E_{k,\,\sigma}|}
on
E_{k,\,\sigma}
, that is

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi_{l}^{k},\,x^{\gamma}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{|E_{k,\,\sigma}|}\int_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}\psi_{l}^{k}(x)\,x^{\gamma}dx=\delta_{l\,\gamma}.

If set

\varphi_{l}^{k}(x):=\sum_{|\nu|\leq s}\beta_{\nu\,l}^{k}x^{\nu}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \psi_{l}^{k}(x):=\sum_{|\nu|\leq s}\tau_{\nu\,l}^{k}\varphi_{\nu}^{k}(x),

then we have

\tau_{\nu\,l}^{k}=\left\langle\psi_{l}^{k},\,\varphi_{\nu}^{k}\right\rangle=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq s}\beta_{\nu\,\gamma}^{k}\left\langle\psi_{l}^{k},\,x^{\gamma}\right\rangle=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq s}\beta_{\nu\,\gamma}^{k}\delta_{l\,\gamma}=\beta_{\nu\,l}^{k},

which yields that

\displaystyle\psi_{l}^{k}(x):=\sum_{|\nu|\leq s}\tau_{\nu\,l}^{k}\varphi_{\nu}^{k}(x).

(5.3)

For any
x\in E_{k,\,\sigma}
, we have

\langle M_{k},\,\varphi_{l}^{k}\rangle_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}\varphi_{l}^{k}(x)=\langle M_{k},\,\sum_{|\nu|\leq s}\beta_{\nu\,l}^{k}x^{\nu}\rangle_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}\varphi_{l}^{k}(x)=\sum_{|\nu|\leq s}\langle M_{k},\,x^{\nu}\rangle_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}\beta_{\nu\,l}^{k}\varphi_{l}^{k}(x)

By this, (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we obtain that

\displaystyle Q_{k}(x)=\sum_{|l|\leq s}\langle M_{k},\,x^{l}\rangle_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}\psi_{l}^{k}(x),\ \ \text{if}\ \ x\in E_{k,\,\sigma}.

We claim that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|\psi_{l}^{k}(x)|\leq C(2^{k-1}\sigma)^{-|l|}.

(5.4)

Indeed, we denote

E:=\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:1\leq|x|\leq 2\}
,
F:=\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:|x|\leq 1\}
and
\{e_{l}:|l|\leq s\}\subset\mathcal{P}_{s}
satisfying

\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}e_{l}(x)\,x^{\gamma}dx=\delta_{l\,\gamma}
, and
\{{\tilde{e}}_{l}:|l|\leq s\}\subset\mathcal{P}_{s}
satisfying
\frac{1}{|F|}\int_{F}\tilde{e}_{l}(x)\,x^{\gamma}dx=\delta_{l\,\gamma}
. Notice that

\delta_{l\,\gamma}=\frac{1}{|E_{k,\,\sigma}|}\int_{E_{k,\,\sigma}}\psi_{l}^{k}(x)\,x^{\gamma}dx=\frac{1}{E}\int_{E}(2^{k-1}\sigma)^{|\gamma|}\psi_{l}^{k}(2^{k-1}\sigma y)\,y^{\gamma}dy.

Thus, we can get
e_{l}(y)=(2^{k-1}\sigma)^{|l|}\psi_{l}^{k}(2^{k-1}\sigma y)
, which yields that

\psi_{l}^{k}(x)=(2^{k-1}\sigma)^{-|l|}e_{l}\left(\frac{x}{2^{k-1}\sigma}\right),\,\,x\in E_{k,\,\sigma}.

By the similar way, we write

\psi_{l}^{k}(x)=(2^{k-1}\sigma)^{-|l|}\tilde{e}_{l}\left(\frac{x}{\sigma}\right),\,\,x\in F.

By taking
C:=\sup_{l:|l|\leq s}\{\|e_{l}\|_{L^{\infty}},\,\|\tilde{e}_{l}\|_{L^{\infty}}\}
, we obtain that (
5.4
) holds true.

Now we can conclude (b). Let

N_{l}^{k}:=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}|E_{j,\,\sigma}|\langle M_{j},\,x^{l}\rangle_{E_{j,\,\sigma}},\,\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

It is easy to see that

N_{l}^{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|E_{j,\,\sigma}|\langle M_{j},\,x^{l}\rangle_{E_{j,\,\sigma}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{E_{j,\,\sigma}}M_{j}(x)x^{l}dx=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}M_{j}(x)x^{l}dx=0.

For any
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, we can find
B_{\sigma}\subset B_{j,\,\sigma}
so that
|B_{\sigma}|=\min\{1,\,|B_{j,\,\sigma}|\}
. Thus, by Lemmas
2.12

and
2.8
, and (
5.1
), we deduce that

\displaystyle|N_{l}^{k}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\int_{E_{j,\,\sigma}}|M_{j}(x)x^{l}|dx

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\left(2^{j}\sigma\right)^{|l|-nb}\left\||\cdot|^{nb}M_{j}(\cdot)\right\|_{X}\left\|\chi_{B_{j,\,\sigma}}\right\|_{X^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\left(2^{j}\sigma\right)^{|l|-nb}\left\||\cdot|^{nb}M_{j}(\cdot)\right\|_{X}\frac{|B_{j,\,\sigma}|}{|B_{\sigma}|}\left\|\chi_{B_{\sigma}}\right\|_{X^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\left(2^{j}\sigma\right)^{|l|-nb+n}\left\||\cdot|^{nb}M_{j}(\cdot)\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\sigma^{|l|+na-nb+n}2^{j(|l|-nb+n)}

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma^{|l|-\alpha+n}2^{j(|l|-nb+n)},

which, together with (
5.4
),
further yields that

\displaystyle|E_{k,\,\sigma}|^{-1}|N_{l}^{k}\psi_{l}^{k}(x)\chi_{k,\,\sigma}(x)|\leq C\sigma^{-\alpha}2^{-knb}\rightarrow 0\ \ \text{as}\,\,k\rightarrow\infty.

(5.5)

From (
5.4
), Abel’s transform and (
5.5
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|l|\leq s}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k}|E_{j,\,\sigma}|\langle M_{j},\,x^{l}\rangle_{E_{j,\,\sigma}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(|E_{k,\,\sigma}|^{-1}\psi_{l}^{k}(x)\chi_{k,\,\sigma}(x)-|E_{k+1,\,\sigma}|^{-1}\psi_{l}^{k+1}(x)\chi_{k+1,\,\sigma}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|l|\leq s}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(-N_{l}^{k+1}\right)\left(|E_{k,\,\sigma}|^{-1}\psi_{l}^{k}(x)\chi_{k,\,\sigma}(x)-|E_{k+1,\,\sigma}|^{-1}\psi_{l}^{k+1}(x)\chi_{k+1,\,\sigma}(x)\right),

and

\displaystyle\left|N_{l}^{k+1}\left(|E_{k,\,\sigma}|^{-1}\psi_{l}^{k}(x)\chi_{k,\,\sigma}(x)-|E_{k+1,\,\sigma}|^{-1}\psi_{l}^{k+1}(x)\chi_{k+1,\,\sigma}(x)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|N_{l}^{k+1}\right||E_{k+1,\,\sigma}|^{-1}\psi_{l}^{k+1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-(k+1)na}|E_{k+1,\,\sigma}|^{-\alpha/n}.

Let
\lambda_{lk}:=C2^{-(k+1)na}
and

a_{lk}:=\lambda_{lk}^{-1}\left(-N_{l}^{k+1}\right)\left(|E_{k,\,\sigma}|^{-1}\psi_{l}^{k}(x)\chi_{k,\,\sigma}(x)-|E_{k+1,\,\sigma}|^{-1}\psi_{l}^{k+1}(x)\chi_{k+1,\,\sigma}(x)\right).

Therefore, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q_{k}(x)=\sum_{|l|\leq s}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{lk}a_{lk}

with
a_{lk}
a central
(X,\alpha,s)
-atom and
\sum_{|l|\leq s}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{lk}|^{p}<\infty,
which implies that the conclusion (b) holds true. This completes the proof of Lemma
5.6
.
∎

Combining Theorem
4.3
, Remark
4.4
and Lemmas
5.3
and
5.6
, we deduce that
the results of Theorems
5.4
and
5.5
hold true.

6
Applications

In this section, we apply Theorems
4.3
,
4.5
,
5.4
and
5.5
, respectively, to two concrete
examples of ball quasi-Banach function spaces, namely, variable Lebesgue spaces
(see Sect. 6.1 below) and mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces (see Sect. 6.2 below). We point out that, even in these cases, the obtained results include the results for the mixed Herz Hardy space and the Herz-type Hardy spaces with variable exponents as special cases.

6.1
Variable Lebesgue spaces

For any measurable function
p(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)
,
the
variable Lebesgue space

L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the set of
measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\inf\left\{\lambda\in(0,\,\infty):\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\right)^{p(x)}\,dx\leq 1\right\}<\infty.

More properties about
L^{p(\cdot)}
are referred to
[
11
,
12
]
.

For any measurable function
p(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)
, let

p_{-}:=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,ess\,inf\,}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x)\ \ \ {\rm and}\qquad p_{+}:=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,ess\,sup\,}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x).

By
[
12
, Theorem  3.2.13]
and
[
29
]
, we know that
L^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is a ball
quasi-Banach function space if
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
, and
L^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is a ball
Banach function space if
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
.

A
measurable function
p(\cdot)
is said to be
globally log-Hölder continuous
if there exist

C_{\log}(p)
,
C_{\infty}\in(0,\infty)
and
p_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

|p(x)-p(y)|\leq\frac{C_{\log}(p)}{\log(e+\frac{1}{|x-y|})}

and

|p(x)-p_{\infty}|\leq\frac{C_{\infty}}{\log(e+|x|)}.

Let
p(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)
be a globally log-Hölder continuous function satisfying
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
,
q\in(0,\,\infty)
and
X:=L^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. Then
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
are the Herz-type Hardy spaces with variable
exponent
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
(see
[
12
, Definition 2.1]
), respectively. From
[
2
]
and
[
11
]
, we deduce that Assumption 1 is satisfied for
L^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. Thus, the assumption of Theorems
4.3
and
4.5
is satisfied for the variable Lebesgue space

L^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. Applying them to
X:=L^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
p(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)
be a globally log-Hölder continuous function satisfying
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
. Then Theorems
4.3
,
4.5
,
5.4
and
5.5
hold true with
X
replaced by
L^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.

Remark 6.2
.

Let
p(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)
be a globally log-Hölder continuous function satisfying
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
. When
X:=L^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, for any
q\in(0,\,\infty)
, we have
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})=\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})=\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. In particularly, Theorems
4.3
,
4.5
,
5.4
and
5.5
are reduced to
[
12
, Theorems 2.1, 2.2, 3.1 and 3.2]
, respectively.

6.2
Mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces

The theory of mixed-norm function spaces can be traced back
to the work of Benedek and Panzone
[
4
]
.
Later on, in 1970, Lizorkin
[
22
]

further developed both the theory of multipliers of Fourier integrals and estimates of
convolutions in the mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces. In recent years, inspired by
[
4
]
and
[
17
]
, the theory of mixed-norm
function spaces, including mixed-norm Morrey spaces, mixed-norm Hardy spaces,
mixed-norm Besov spaces and mixed-norm Triebel-Lizorkin spaces, has rapidly been developed(see, for instance
[
10
,
15
,
18
,
27
]
). Now, we present the definition of mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces from
[
4
]
as
follows.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
\vec{p}:=(p_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,p_{n})\in(0,\infty)^{n}
. The mixed-norm Lebesgue space

L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is defined to be the set of all measurable functions
f
such that

\|f\|_{L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\left\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|f(x_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,x_{n})|^{p_{1}}dx_{1}\right]^{p_{2}/p_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot dx_{n}\right\}^{1/p_{n}}<\infty

with the usual modifications made when
p_{i}=\infty
for some
i\in\{1,\cdot\cdot\cdot,n\}
.

From
[
29
, p.9]
and
[
34
, Sect. 5]
, we know that
L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is a ball
quasi-Banach function space. Furthermore, it is easy to see that
L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is a ball Banach function space if
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,p_{n})\in(1,\infty)^{n}
.

Let
X:=L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,p_{n})\in(1,\infty)^{n}
and
q\in(0,\,\infty)
. Then
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
are mixed Herz-Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
(see
[
41
, Definitions 2.4 and 2.5]
), respectively. By
[
4
, Theorem 1.a]
and
[
18
, Lemma 3.5]
, we obtain that Assumption 1 is satisfied for
L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. Therefore, the assumption of Theorems
4.3
and
4.5
is satisfied for the mixed-norm Lebesgue space

L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. Applying them to
X:=L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6.4
.

For any
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,p_{n})\in(1,\infty)^{n}
, Theorems
4.3
,
4.5
,
5.4
and
5.5
hold true with
X
replaced by
L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.

Remark 6.5
.

Let
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,p_{n})\in(1,\infty)^{n}
. When
X:=L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, for any
q\in(0,\,\infty)
, we have
\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})=\mathcal{H}\dot{\mathcal{K}}_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{X}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})=\mathcal{H}{\mathcal{K}}_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\alpha,\,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. In particularly, Theorems
4.3
,
4.5
,
5.4
and
5.5
are reduced to
[
41
, Theorems 3.1, 5.1, 4.1 and 4.2]
, respectively.

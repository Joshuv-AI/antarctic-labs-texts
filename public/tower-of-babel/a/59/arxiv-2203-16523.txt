Shifted Witten classes and topological recursion
Séverin Charbonnier, Nitin Kumar Chidambaram, Elba Garcia-Failde, Alessandro Giacchetto

Abstract
. The Witten r r -spin class defines a non-semisimple cohomological field theory. Pandharipande, Pixton and Zvonkine studied two special shifts of the Witten class along two semisimple directions of the associated Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold using the Givental–Teleman reconstruction theorem. We show that the R R -matrix and the translation of these two specific shifts can be constructed from the solutions of two differential equations that generalise the classical Airy differential equation. Using this, we prove that the descendant intersection theory of the shifted Witten classes satisfies topological recursion on two 1 1 -parameter families of spectral curves. By taking the limit as the parameter goes to zero, we prove that the descendant intersection theory of the Witten r r -spin class can be computed by topological recursion on the r r -Airy spectral curve. We finally show that this proof suffices to deduce Witten’s r r -spin conjecture, already proved by Faber, Shadrin and Zvonkine, which claims that the generating series of r r -spin intersection numbers is the tau function of the r r -KdV hierarchy that satisfies the string equation.

1.
Introduction

The Deligne–Mumford compactification of the moduli space of genus
g
curves with
n
marked points
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
has been intensely studied in algebraic geometry, differential geometry and mathematical physics over the past few decades. In 1990, in the context of two-dimensional quantum gravity, Witten formulated his famous conjecture
[
Wit90
]
that the generating series of
\psi
-classes intersection numbers on the moduli space of curves satisfies the KdV integrable hierarchy. This integrability property is very powerful and allows one to calculate the intersection numbers recursively. A year later, this celebrated conjecture was proved by Kontsevich
[
Kon92
]
using a cellular decomposition of the moduli spaces and a matrix model, and the result is widely referred to as the Witten–Kontsevich theorem.

Around the same time, cohomological field theories (CohFTs) were introduced by Kontsevich and Manin
[
KM94
]
to capture the formal properties of the virtual fundamental class in Gromov–Witten theory. They extend the classical notion of topological quantum field theories (TFTs), in the sense that usual linear maps induced by surfaces of genus
g
with
n
boundaries from TFTs are allowed to vary cohomologically in families in CohFTs.

A special class of CohFTs are called semisimple and these have been fully classified by Givental and Teleman
[
Tel12
]
. Moreover, the so-called Teleman reconstruction theorem provides an explicit algorithm to reconstruct the full higher genus theory of semisimple CohFTs starting from the genus zero theory. This reconstruction algorithm can be conveniently encoded in terms of (the local version of) a universal procedure formulated in terms of the geometry of Riemann surfaces known as the topological recursion
[
EO07
]
. Topological recursion recursively constructs from certain initial data, called a (local) spectral curve, a family of differentials that encodes the CohFT correlators
[
DOSS14
]
.

A famous example of a CohFT is the Witten
r
-spin class, defined for each integer
r\geq 2
. The Witten
2
-spin class turns out to be trivial, and thus it leads to no new geometry – its associated correlators are the Witten–Kontsevich
\psi
-classes intersection numbers. We know that they are governed by the KdV hierarchy together with the string equation. Equivalently, they satisfy the topological recursion on the (global) Airy spectral curve. For
r\geq 3
however, the Witten
r
-spin CohFT is non-semisimple and the Teleman reconstruction theorem is not immediately applicable.

A workaround comes from the study of the genus zero sector of the Witten
r
-spin theory, which endows the underlying vector space with the structure of a Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold equivalent to the canonical Frobenius structure on the versal deformation of the
A_{r-1}
-singularity. The algebra on tangent spaces is semisimple outside the discriminant of
A_{r-1}
. In
[
PPZ15
,
PPZ19
]
, Pandharipande–Pixton–Zvonkine studied two special semisimple directions of this Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold, which they refer to as the shifted Witten classes. Their
3
-spin construction yields Pixton’s tautological relations on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, which extend the established Faber–Zagier relations on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
and are conjectured to be the full set of relations in the tautological ring of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

[
Pix13
]
. Although the higher spin relations are implied by those for
r=3
, the construction for general
r
found several applications, like bounds on the Betti numbers of the tautological ring of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
and polynomiality properties in
r
of the Witten class. This polynomiality was employed in
[
KLLS18
,
GKLS19
,
GZ22
]
to set
r=\frac{1}{2}
, yielding a simplified set of relations that imply various properties of the tautological ring of the moduli space of curves.

As mentioned earlier, for every semisimple CohFT it is known that there is a local spectral curve whose topological recursion correlators compute the CohFT correlators. However, it is not clear which families of local spectral curves glue to a global one. This problem was studied in
[
Dun+19
]
using the notion of a Dubrovin superpotential, and the authors show that shifted Witten classes come from global spectral curves.

Our goal is to further explore the two specific shifts of the Witten
r
-spin class considered in
[
PPZ19
]
from the topological recursion perspective, that is to determine and study the two corresponding families of global spectral curves explicitly. We state these results here, but refrain from explaining the notation and details, instead leaving them for the bulk of the paper.

Theorem A

(Theorems
4.1
and
4.4
)
.

The descendant intersection theory of the shifted Witten classes can be computed using topological recursion on the following global spectral curves.

(1)

Let
V_{r}
be the
r

\widehat{\omega}_{g,n}^{r,\epsilon}
computed from the spectral curve on

encode descendant integrals of the
(\epsilon r\cdot v_{r-2})
-shifted Witten

-spin class
\widehat{W}^{r,\epsilon}

\begin{split}&\widehat{\omega}_{g,n}^{r,\epsilon}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\\
&=(-r)^{g-1}\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}=0}^{r-2}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\widehat{W}^{r,\epsilon}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k_{i}\geq 0}\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}d\hat{\xi}^{k_{i},a_{i}}(z_{i})\,,\end{split}

where

(2)

The topological recursion correlators

encode descendant integrals of the

Our method of proof is completely different to the one of

First of all, by taking the limit of the differentials of the topological recursion as

Our motivation and method of proof

One of our initial motivations for this paper was to explore if we could recover Witten’s
r
-spin conjecture, i.e. Faber–Shadrin–Zvonkine theorem, from topological recursion. In addition, we wanted to explicitly investigate all the elements of the identification between semisimple CohFTs and topological recursion
[
DOSS14
]
in the case of shifted Witten classes.

As mentioned earlier, the family of spectral curves corresponding to the shifted Witten class was identified in
[
Dun+19
]
where the function
x
of the spectral curve is the Dubrovin superpotential of the associated Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold. Here we make the identification completely explicit for the shifts along
v_{1}
and
v_{r-2}
, and perform all the calculations necessary to apply the result of
[
DOSS14
]
. The family of spectral curves associated to the
v_{r-2}
-shift corresponds to the
r
-th Lucas polynomials of the second kind, which has interesting properties and to our knowledge has never been considered before in the context of topological recursion.

In this paper, we develop a method to compute the
R
-matrix and translation associated to a spectral curve with simple ramifications, by identifying the objects built out of the spectral curve following the prescription of
[
DOSS14
]
as integral representations of solutions to families of differential equations. For both shifted Witten classes, we find generalisations of the classical Airy differential equation

\ddot{u}(t)=t\,u(t)

in two different directions. We study the asymptotic expansions of solutions to these differential equations thoroughly and find that the
R
-matrices and translations of the Witten classes shifted along the
v_{1}
and
v_{r-2}
directions can be expressed in terms of these expansions.

Relation to existing work

Witten’s conjecture for
r=2
has received many proofs by now, for instance
[
Kon92
,
Mir07
,
OP09
,
And+20
]
. The general
r
case was only proved by Faber–Shadrin–Zvonkine in
[
FSZ10
]
building on the work of Givental
[
Giv03
]
. For
r>2
, even the construction of the Witten
r
-spin class is remarkably intricate. In genus
0
, the construction was first carried out by Witten
[
Wit93
]
using
r
-spin structures, which endow every point in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
with the additional structure of an
r
-th root of the log canonical bundle twisted at the marked points. The construction of the Witten class in higher genera was first carried out by Polishchuk–Vaintrob
[
PV00
]
, and later simplified by Chiodo
[
Chi06
]
using
K
-theoretic methods.

Here, we summarise the topological recursion literature around the Witten
r
-spin class, i.e. the fact that the topological recursion correlators for the
r
-Airy spectral curve compute
r
-spin intersection numbers. The result was originally conjectured by Bouchard and Eynard based on numerical calculations. The proofs of the following results rely on the Faber–Shadrin–Zvonkine result
[
FSZ10
]
.

•

In
[
Dun+19
]
, the authors gave the first proof that
r
-spin intersection numbers satisfy topological recursion applied to the
r
-Airy spectral curve. The theorem is an application of their main result, which claims that the Dubrovin superpotential provides a spectral curve for the CohFT associated to the corresponding Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold. The result then follows from the general consideration that limits of the classical topological recursion applied to a spectral curve with simple ramifications coincide with the corresponding Bouchard–Eynard topological recursion (see
remark

5.3
for subtleties related to limits).

•

In
[
Bor+24
]
, higher quantum Airy structures are introduced as generalisations of Kontsevich–Soibelman quantum Airy structures by allowing differential operators of arbitrary order, instead of just quadratic ones. The authors show that the topological recursion is equivalent to certain
W
-constraints realised as higher quantum Airy structures. In particular, they identify the higher Airy structure corresponding to the topological recursion for the
r
-Airy curve with the
W
-constraints which were proved in
[
AvM92
]
to be equivalent to the
r
-KdV hierarchy together with the string equation. Then, Witten’s
r
-spin conjecture implies the topological recursion result for
r
-spin intersection numbers.

•

In
[
BCEG21
]
, the authors built generalised Kontsevich graphs whose generating series satisfy topological recursion for a large class of spectral curves. This class of spectral curves includes the family of curves corresponding to the Witten class shifted along the
v_{1}
direction. On the other hand, the generating series of graphs is identified with a generalised Kontsevich matrix model, which is showed in
[
AvM92
]
to be the unique tau function of the
r
-KdV hierarchy satisfying the string equation. Again, via Witten’s
r
-spin conjecture, the generating series of certain graphs is identified with the generating series of intersection numbers, which also implies the topological recursion for
r
-spin intersection numbers.

2.
Witten classes and topological recursion

2.1.
Cohomological field theories

Let us recall some facts about cohomological field theories, CohFTs for short, and the Givental action. The main example treated in this article is that of the Witten
r
-spin classes.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space with a non-degenerate symmetric
2
-form
\eta
. A
cohomological field theory
on
(V,\eta)
consists of a collection
\Omega=(\Omega_{g,n})_{2g-2+n>0}
of elements

(2.1)

\Omega_{g,n}\in H^{\bullet}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})\otimes(V^{\ast})^{\otimes n}

satisfying the following axioms.

i)

Each
\Omega_{g,n}
is
S_{n}
-invariant, where the action of the symmetric group
S_{n}
permutes both the marked points of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and the copies of
(V^{\ast})^{\otimes n}
.

ii)

Consider the gluing maps

(2.2)

\displaystyle q\colon

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g-1,n+2}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\,,

\displaystyle r_{h,I}\colon

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,|I|+1}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g-h,|J|+1}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\,,\qquad I\sqcup J=\set{1,\dots,n}.

Then

(2.3)

\displaystyle q^{\ast}\Omega_{g,n}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n})

\displaystyle=\Omega_{g-1,n+2}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}\otimes\eta^{{\dagger}})\,,

\displaystyle r_{h,I}^{\ast}\Omega_{g,n}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n})

\displaystyle=(\Omega_{h,|I|+1}\otimes\Omega_{g-h,|J|+1})\Biggl(\bigotimes_{i\in I}v_{i}\otimes\eta^{{\dagger}}\otimes\bigotimes_{j\in J}v_{j}\Biggr),

where
\eta^{{\dagger}}\in V^{\otimes 2}
is the bivector dual to
\eta
.

If the vector space comes with a distinguished element
\bm{1}\in V
, we can also ask for a third axiom:

iii)

Consider the forgetful map

(2.4)

p\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\,.

Then

(2.5)

\displaystyle p^{\ast}\Omega_{g,n}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n})

\displaystyle=\Omega_{g,n}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}\otimes\bm{1})\,,

\displaystyle\Omega_{0,3}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes\bm{1})

\displaystyle=\eta(v_{1},v_{2})\,.

In this case,
\Omega
is called a
cohomological field theory with a flat unit
.

The structure of a cohomological field theory turns
V
into a commutative algebra. The product structure is known as the
quantum product
, denoted
\bullet
, and is defined as follows:

(2.6)

\eta(v_{1}\bullet v_{2},v_{3})=\Omega_{0,3}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes v_{3})\,.

Commutativity follows from (i), associativity from (ii). If the cohomological field theory has a flat unit, the quantum product is unital, with
\bm{1}\in V
being the identity by (iii).

The degree
0
part of a CohFT

(2.7)

w_{g,n}\coloneqq\deg_{0}{\Omega_{g,n}}\in H^{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})\otimes(V^{\ast})^{\otimes n}\cong(V^{\ast})^{\otimes n}

is again a CohFT. More precisely, it is a
2d
topological field theory
(TFT for short), and is uniquely determined by the values of
\deg_{0}{\Omega_{0,3}}
and by the bilinear form
\eta
(or equivalently, by the associated quantum product). In particular, we will refer to a TFT as being unital and/or semisimple when the associated algebra is so (after extension to
\mathbb{C}
).

In
[
Giv01
]
, Givental defined a certain action on Gromov–Witten potentials, and this action was lifted to CohFTs in the work of Teleman
[
Tel12
]
. We recall here the basic definitions, and refer to
[
PPZ15
]
for more details.

Definition 2.2
.

Fix a vector space
V
with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
\eta
. An
R
-matrix
is an element
R(u)=\mathrm{Id}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}R_{k}u^{k}\in\mathrm{Id}+u\End(V)\llbracket u\rrbracket
satisfying the symplectic condition:

(2.8)

R(u)R^{{\dagger}}(-u)=\mathrm{Id}\,.

Here
R^{{\dagger}}
is the adjoint with respect to
\eta
. The inverse matrix
R^{-1}(u)
also satisfies the symplectic condition.

From a CohFT
\Omega
on
(V,\eta)
together with an
R
-matrix, one can define a new CohFT on
(V,\eta)
, denoted
R\Omega=(R\Omega_{g,n})_{2g-2+n>0}
. This defines a left group action on the set of CohFTs on
(V,\eta)
.

There is another type of action on the space of CohFTs known as a translation.

Definition 2.3
.

A
translation
is an element
T\in u^{2}\,V\llbracket u\rrbracket
, i.e. a
V
-valued power series vanishing in degree
0
and
1
:

(2.9)

T(u)=\sum_{d\geq 1}T_{d}\,u^{d+1}\,,\qquad T_{d}\in V\,.

From a CohFT
\Omega
on
(V,\eta)
together with a translation, one can define a new CohFT on
(V,\eta)
, denoted
T\Omega=(T\Omega_{g,n})_{2g-2+n>0}
. This defines an abelian group action on the set of CohFTs on
(V,\eta)
.

In general, if we start from a CohFT with unit on
(V,\eta,\bm{1})
, acting by an
R
-matrix or by translation does not preserve flatness. However, there is a specific combination of the two which does achieve this.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a CohFT on
(V,\eta,\bm{1})
with a flat unit. Let
R
be an
R
-matrix, and consider the
V
-valued power series

(2.10)

T_{\textup{L}}(u)\coloneqq u\bigl(R^{-1}(u)\bm{1}-\bm{1}\bigr)\,,\qquad T_{\textup{R}}(u)\coloneqq u\bigl(\bm{1}-R^{-1}(u)\bm{1}\bigr)\,.

Then
T_{\textup{L}}R\Omega
and
RT_{\textup{R}}\Omega
coincide, and form a CohFT with a flat unit.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a CohFT on
(V,\eta,\bm{1})
with a flat unit. Let
R
be an
R
-matrix. We define the
unit-preserving action
by
R
as

(2.11)

R.\Omega\coloneqq T_{\textup{L}}R\Omega=RT_{\textup{R}}\Omega\,.

2.1.1.
Teleman’s reconstruction theorem

One of the main tools in the study of CohFTs is Teleman’s reconstruction theorem
[
Tel12
]
, which allows one to reconstruct a homogenous semisimple CohFT from its topological part, an
R
-matrix and a translation. Let us explain the notion of Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds and homogenous CohFTs (which arise naturally in Gromov–Witten theory) briefly before stating Teleman’s reconstruction theorem.

Given a CohFT
\Omega
on
V
, we can naturally endow (possibly a formal neighbourhood of the origin in)
V
with the structure of a Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold by restricting to genus
0
. To be precise, assume that we have a
d
-dimensional vector space
V
with the flat basis
v_{0},\ldots,v_{d-1}
underlying our CohFT
\Omega
. Then, we can define the primary genus
0
potential
F
as

(2.12)

F(t_{0},\ldots,t_{d-1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{0}+\cdots+k_{d-1}=n\\
n\geq 3\end{subarray}}\left(\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}}\Omega_{0,n}(v_{0}^{\otimes k_{0}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{d-1}^{\otimes k_{d-1}})\right)\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}\frac{t_{i}^{k_{i}}}{k_{i}!}\,,

where we view
t_{i}
as the dual coordinate to the basis element
v_{i}
. Assuming that the sum in
equation

2.12
converges,
V
inherits the structure of a Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold with flat coordinates
(t_{0},\ldots,t_{d-1})
. All the information of this Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold is encoded in the potential
F
.

We can equip the tangent space at every point
p
on the Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold
V
with an associative algebra structure given by the
quantum product

(2.13)

\partial_{i}\bullet_{p}\partial_{j}=\sum_{k,\ell=0}^{d-1}\left.\left(\frac{\partial^{3}F}{\partial t_{i}\partial t_{j}\partial t_{k}}\right)\right|_{p}\eta^{k,\ell}\partial_{\ell}\,,

where we introduced the following notation for the vector fields,
\partial_{i}\coloneqq\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}\in H^{0}(V,TV)
, which is associated to the basis vector
v_{i}
.

We can often equip the Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold
V
with an additional grading using the notion of an Euler field. An
Euler field
on a Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold
V
with flat coordinates
(t_{0},\ldots,t_{d-1})
, is an affine vector field
E
satisfying the following conditions.

•

The vector field
E
has the form
E=\sum_{i}(\alpha_{i}t_{i}+\beta_{i})\partial_{i}\,
.

•

The metric
\eta
and the quantum product
\bullet
are eigenfunctions of the Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{E}
with weights
2-\delta
and
1
respectively, where
\delta
is a rational number called the
conformal dimension
.

The Euler field
E
on the Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold
V
can be used to define an action of
E
on the CohFT as follows:

(2.14)

\begin{split}(E.\Omega)_{g,n}\left(\partial_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\partial_{a_{n}}\right)&\coloneqq\left(\deg+\sum_{l=1}^{n}\alpha_{a_{l}}\right)\Omega_{g,n}\left(\partial_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\partial_{a_{n}}\right)\\
&\qquad+p_{*}\Omega_{g,n+1}\left(\partial_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\partial_{a_{n}}\otimes\sum_{i}\beta_{i}\partial_{i}\right).\end{split}

Definition 2.6
.

We say that the CohFT
\Omega
is
homogeneous
if there exists an Euler field
E
such that

(2.15)

(E.\Omega)_{g,n}=\bigl((g-1)\delta+n\bigr)\Omega_{g,n}\,.

Finally, we are ready to state Teleman’s reconstruction theorem.

Theorem 2.7

(Teleman reconstruction
[
Tel12
]
)
.

Let
\Omega_{0,n}
be a genus zero homogeneous semisimple CohFT.

•

There exists a unique homogeneous CohFT
\Omega_{g,n}
that extends
\Omega_{0,n}
in higher genus.

•

This extended CohFT
\Omega_{g,n}
is obtained by first applying a translation action
T(u)
on the topological field theory
w_{g,n}
(determined by
\Omega_{0,3}
), and then an
R
-matrix action
R(u)
to the resulting CohFT.

•

The
R
-matrix and the translation are uniquely specified by the associated Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold and the Euler field.

If the CohFT
\Omega_{0,n}
has a flat unit, the translation and the
R
-matrix action can be combined into a unit preserving
R
-matrix action, and thus the extension
\Omega_{g,n}
is a CohFT with a flat unit.

The
R
-matrix and the translation can be explicitly computed, and we refer the reader to
[
Tel12
,
PPZ15
,
CGG22
]
for a detailed explanation of this algorithm
2
2

2

Teleman’s algorithm requires one to work over
\mathbb C
to compute the
R
-matrix and the translation
T
. However, if the original CohFT
\Omega
is defined over
\mathbb Q
, so is the reconstructed CohFT.
.

2.2.
Witten classes

In this article, we are primarily interested in a certain CohFT known as the Witten
r
-spin CohFT. For
r\geq 2
, let
V=\mathbb{Q}\braket{v_{0},\dots,v_{r-2}}
with pairing
\eta(v_{a},v_{b})=\delta_{a+b,r-2}
and unit
\bm{1}=v_{0}
. The
Witten
r
-spin theory
is an
(r-1)
-dimensional CohFT with a flat unit

(2.16)

W_{g,n}^{r}\colon V^{\otimes n}\longrightarrow H^{\bullet}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})\,.

For
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\{0,\ldots,r-2\}
, the Witten
r
-spin class
W_{g,n}^{r}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})
has pure complex degree

(2.17)

D_{g;a}^{r}=\frac{(r-2)(g-1)+|a|}{r}\,,

where
|a|\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
. If
D_{g;a}^{r}
is not an integer, the corresponding Witten class vanishes. In genus
0
, the construction was first carried out by Witten
[
Wit93
]
using
r
-spin structures, and we briefly recall it here.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}^{r}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
be the moduli space parametrising
r
-th roots of the form

(2.18)

L^{\otimes r}\cong\omega_{C}\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}p_{i}\right),

where
[C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n}]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
and
\omega_{C}
denotes the canonical line bundle on
C
. There is a forgetful map
\pi\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}^{r}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
that forgets the
r
-th root. Consider the vector bundle
\mathcal{V}_{0,n}^{r}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}^{r}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
whose fibre over
[C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n},L]
is
H^{1}(C,L)^{\ast}
. Then the Witten
r
-spin class is defined as the push-forward of the (normalised) top Chern class of
\mathcal{V}_{0,n}^{r}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
:

(2.19)

\frac{1}{r}\,W_{0,n}^{r}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})=\pi_{\ast}c_{\textup{top}}\left(\mathcal{V}_{0,n}^{r}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\right).

The definition of the Witten class in higher genera is much more complicated, since the fibres
H^{1}(C,L)^{\ast}
no longer glue together to form a vector bundle. The construction was first carried out by Polishchuk–Vaintrob
[
PV00
]
, and was later simplified by Chiodo
[
Chi06
]
.

The Witten
r
-spin class is homogeneous with respect to the Euler field

(2.20)

E=\sum_{a=0}^{r-2}\left(1-\frac{a}{r}\right)t_{a}\partial_{a}

and of conformal dimension
\delta=\frac{r-2}{2}
. However, The Witten
r
-spin class is not semisimple, so Teleman’s reconstruction theorem does not apply. A workaround is to consider a shift of the Witten class along a vector
\gamma\in V
.

Definition 2.8
.

The
shifted Witten class

\mathsf{W}^{\gamma}_{g,n}
, shifted along the vector
\gamma\in V
, is defined as

(2.21)

\mathsf{W}^{r,\gamma}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}}):=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{m!}\,p_{m,\ast}W_{g,n+m}^{r}\bigl(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}}\otimes\gamma^{\otimes m}\bigr)\,,

where
p_{m}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
denotes the forgetful map that forgets the last
m
marked points.

The sum appearing in the definition of the shifted Witten class is easily seen to be finite due to degree considerations. Moreover, the term of highest degree in
\mathsf{W}^{\gamma}_{g,n}
is the Witten class
W^{r}_{g,n}
.

For any shift
\gamma
, the classes
\mathsf{W}^{\gamma}_{g,n}
form a CohFT with a flat unit. Moreover, for a generic shift, the shifted Witten class is semisimple and one can apply Teleman’s reconstruction theorem to the shifted class. For two specific
1
-parameter families of shifts, namely
\gamma=\epsilon rv_{r-2}
and
\gamma=\epsilon rv_{1}
, Pandharipande–Pixton–Zvonkine
[
PPZ19
]
computed all the ingredients required for Teleman’s reconstruction theorem as we now describe.

2.2.1.
The
v_{r-2}
shift

Consider the vector
\gamma=\epsilon rv_{r-2}
, where
\epsilon\in\mathbb C
is a formal parameter. For notational convenience, we denote the
(\epsilon rv_{r-2})
-shifted Witten class as

(2.22)

\widehat{W}_{g,n}^{r,\epsilon}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}}):=\mathsf{W}^{r,\epsilon rv_{r-2}}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})\,.

By applying Teleman’s reconstruction theorem,
\widehat{W}_{g,n}^{r,\epsilon}
can be expressed using a unit-preserving
R
-matrix action as

(2.23)

\widehat{W}_{g,n}^{r,\epsilon}=R.\widehat{w}_{g,n}^{r}\,,

where the TFT and the
R
-matrix are given by (cf.
[
PPZ19
, section 2]
)

(2.24)

\widehat{w}_{g,n}^{r}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})=\epsilon^{\frac{(r-2)(g-1)+|a|}{2}}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{g-1}\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}(-1)^{(k-1)(g-1)}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sin\bigl(\frac{(a_{i}+1)k\pi}{r}\bigr)}{\sin^{2g-2+n}(\frac{k\pi}{r})}\,,

(2.25)

R^{-1}(u)_{a}^{a}=\mathsf{B}_{r,a}^{\textup{even}}(\epsilon^{-r/2}u)\,,\qquad R^{-1}(u)_{a}^{r-2-a}=\mathsf{B}_{r,a}^{\textup{odd}}(\epsilon^{-r/2}u)\,,

with the
R
-matrix elements being
0
elsewhere. If
r
is even, the coefficient at the intersection of the diagonals is set to be
1
. Here
\mathsf{B}_{r,a}(u)
are the hypergeometric series (
a=0,\dots,r-2
)

(2.26)

\mathsf{B}_{r,a}(u)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\bigl((2k-1)r-2(a+1)\bigr)\bigl((2k-1)r+2(a+1)\bigr)}{k}\right)\left(-\frac{u}{16r^{2}}\right)^{m},

and we denote by
\mathsf{B}_{r,a}^{\textup{even}}
(resp.
\mathsf{B}_{r,a}^{\textup{odd}}
) the even (resp. odd) summands of
\mathsf{B}_{r,a}
.

2.2.2.
The
v_{1}
shift

Consider the vector
\gamma=\epsilon rv_{1}
, where
\epsilon\in\mathbb C
is a formal parameter. We denote the
(\epsilon rv_{1})
-shifted Witten class as

(2.27)

\widetilde{W}_{g,n}^{r,\epsilon}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}}):=\mathsf{W}^{r,\epsilon rv_{1}}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})\,.

Again,
\widetilde{W}_{g,n}
is semisimple and is given by a unit-preserving action as

(2.28)

\widetilde{W}_{g,n}^{r,\epsilon}=R.\widetilde{w}_{g,n}^{r}\,,

where the TFT and the
R
-matrix elements are given by (cf.
[
PPZ19
, section 4]
)

(2.29)

\widetilde{w}_{g,n}^{r}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})=\epsilon^{\frac{(g-1)(r-2)+|a|}{r-1}}(r-1)^{g}\cdot\delta\,,

(2.30)

(R^{-1}_{m})_{a}^{b}=\left(\frac{1}{r(r-1)\epsilon^{r/(r-1)}}\right)^{m}P_{m}(r,a)\,,\qquad\text{if }b+m\equiv a\pmod{r-1}\,,

and are
0
otherwise. Here
\delta
equals
1
if
g-1\equiv|a|\pmod{r-1}
and
0
otherwise, while the coefficients
P_{m}(r,a)
are computed recursively as

(2.31)

\begin{cases}P_{m}(r,a)-P_{m}(r,a-1)=r\left(m-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{a}{r}\right)P_{m-1}(r,a-1)\,,\qquad\text{for }a=1,\dots,r-2\,,\\
P_{m}(r,0)=P_{m}(r,r-1)\,,\end{cases}

with initial condition
P_{0}=1
.

2.3.
Topological recursion

Topological recursion, TR for short, is a universal procedure that associates a collection of symmetric multidifferentials to a spectral curve, a curve with some extra data
[
EO07
]
. What makes TR especially useful is its applications to enumerative geometry: many counting problems are solved by TR, in the sense that the sought numbers are coefficients of the multidifferentials when expanded in a specific base of differentials.

Definition 2.9
.

A
spectral curve

\mathcal{S}=(\Sigma,x,y,\omega_{0,2})
consists of

•

a Riemann surface
\Sigma
(not necessarily compact or connected);

•

a meromorphic function
x\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{C}
such that the differential
dx
has finitely many zeros (called ramification points)
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}
that are simple;

•

a meromorphic function
y\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{C}
such that
dy
does not vanish at the zeros of
dx
;

•

a symmetric bidifferential
\omega_{0,2}
on
\Sigma\times\Sigma
, with a double pole on the diagonal with biresidue
1
, and no other poles.

The
topological recursion
produces symmetric multidifferentials (also called
correlators
)
\omega_{g,n}
on
\Sigma^{n}
, defined recursively on
2g-2+n>0
as

(2.32)

\begin{split}\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})&\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Res_{z=\alpha_{i}}K_{i}(z_{1},z)\bigg(\omega_{g-1,n+1}(z,\sigma_{i}(z),z_{2},\dots,z_{n})\\
&\qquad\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g_{1}+g_{2}=g\\
N_{1}\sqcup N_{2}=\{2,\dots,n\}\end{subarray}}^{\text{no $(0,1)$}}\omega_{g_{1},1+|N_{1}|}(z,z_{N_{1}})\,\omega_{g_{2},1+|N_{2}|}(\sigma_{i}(z),z_{N_{2}})\bigg)\,,\end{split}

where
K_{i}
, called the topological recursion kernels
3
3

3

In older references, such as
[
EO07
]
, the recursion kernel has an overall minus sign, but the convention of this paper is now considered standard. This minus sign would result in a
(-1)^{n}
rescaling of
\omega_{g,n}
.
, are locally defined in a neighbourhood
U_{i}
of
\alpha_{i}
as

(2.33)

K_{i}(z_{1},z)\coloneqq\frac{\frac{1}{2}\int_{w=\sigma_{i}(z)}^{z}\omega_{0,2}(z_{1},w)}{\bigl(y(z)-y(\sigma_{i}(z))\bigr)dx(z)}\,,

and
\sigma_{i}\colon U_{i}\to U_{i}
is the Galois involution near the ramification point
\alpha_{i}\in U_{i}
. The “no
(0,1)
” in equation (
2.32
) means that one should omit the cases where
(g_{1},1+|N_{1}|)=(0,1)
or
(g_{2},1+|N_{2}|)=(0,1)
. It can be shown that
\omega_{g,n}
is a symmetric meromorphic multidifferential on
\Sigma^{n}
with poles only at the ramification points.

If we relax the assumptions on the spectral curve by allowing the ramification points of
x\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{C}
to be not necessarily simple, we have to apply a generalised version of the topological recursion known as the Bouchard–Eynard topological recursion
[
BE13
,
Bou+14
]
. The Bouchard–Eynard topological recursion was proved to be well-defined (i.e., it produces symmetric correlators) if and only if an
admissibility condition
is satisfied
[
Bor+24
]
.

For the sake of notation and since it is sufficient for this paper, we present the Bouchard–Eynard topological recursion (and state the admissibility condition) only in the case where
x
has a single ramification point where
dx
has a zero of order
r-1
. Around this ramification point, we assume that
y
behaves as

(2.34)

y(z)\sim z^{s-r}+O(z^{s-r+1})\,,

where
1\leq s\leq r+1
. Then the admissibility condition states that
s\equiv\pm 1\pmod{r}
.

Definition 2.10
.

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, set
[r]\coloneqq\{1,\dots,r\}
. Consider an admissible spectral curve such that
dx
has a single zero of degree
r-1
at
z=\alpha
, and let
\sigma
denote a generator of the group of local deck transformations around
z=\alpha
. Let
o\in\Sigma
be an arbitrary base point on the spectral curve. The
Bouchard–Eynard topological recursion
produces symmetric multidifferentials
\omega_{g,n}
on
\Sigma^{n}
recursively on
2g-2+n>0
as:

(2.35)

\begin{split}\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{z\in C}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[r-1]\\
I\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|I|+1}\frac{\int_{w=o}^{z}\omega_{0,2}(z_{1},w)}{\prod_{i\in I}\bigl(\omega_{0,1}(z)-\omega_{0,1}(\sigma^{i}(z))\bigr)}&\\
\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\vdash I\cup\{r\}\\
\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell(J)}N_{i}=\{2,\dots,n\}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell(J)}g_{i}=g+\ell(J)-|I|-1\end{subarray}}^{\textup{no }(0,1)}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(J)}\omega_{g_{i},|J_{i}|+|N_{i}|}(\sigma_{J_{i}}(z),z_{N_{i}})\,,\end{split}

where
C
is a contour around the ramification point
\alpha
,
(\sigma^{1}(z),\dots,\sigma^{r-1}(z),\sigma^{r}(z)=z)
are the images of
z
under the local deck transformation group around
\alpha
, and the notation
\sigma_{J}(z)
stands for
(\sigma^{j}(z))_{j\in J}
and
\sigma
. Finally, in the second sum the cases where
(g_{i},|J_{i}|+|N_{i}|)=(0,1)
are forbidden.

As mentioned previously, the fact that
equation

2.35
produces well-defined symmetric correlators was proved in
[
Bor+24
, theorem E]
.

Remark 2.11
.

When we have a compact spectral curve,
[
BE13
]
defined a global version of the topological recursion, where the main difference from the local version (
2.35
) is that the set of local deck transformations
(\sigma^{1}(z),\ldots,\sigma^{r-1}(z),\sigma^{r}(z))
at the ramification point
z=a
is replaced by the set of global sheet involutions (which does not depend on the ramification index at each ramification point, but rather on the degree of the branched covering
x
). In the case of simple ramification points, they prove that it coincides with the standard definition (
2.32
) of the topological recursion, and that in the setting of
definition

2.10
it coincides with
equation

2.35
.

2.3.1.
TR-CohFT correspondence

The topological recursion correlators are closely related to intersection numbers on the moduli space of curves. First,
[
Eyn14
]
proved that the topological recursion computes certain intersection numbers on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. Afterwards,
[
DOSS14
]
proved that the descendant integrals of a semisimple CohFT are computed by topological recursion on a local spectral curve. Finally,
[
Dun+18
]
established in the converse direction that topological recursion on a
compact
spectral curve computes the descendant integrals of an associated semisimple CohFT (by refining results of
[
Eyn14
]
and connecting it to the Givental formalism). We refer to this collection of results as the “TR-CohFT correspondence”.

We explain how to compute the CohFT associated to a compact spectral curve
\mathcal{S}=(\Sigma,x,y,\omega_{0,2})
following
[
Dun+18
]
. We will work with CohFTs over
\mathbb{C}
. Let us fix a global constant
C\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Choose a local coordinate
\zeta_{i}
on the neighbourhood
U_{i}
of the ramification point
\alpha_{i}
such that
\zeta_{i}(\alpha_{i})=0
and
x-x(\alpha_{i})=-\frac{\zeta_{i}^{2}}{2}
. We define the auxiliary functions
\xi^{i}\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{C}
and the associated differentials
d\xi^{k,i}
as

(2.36)

\xi^{i}(z)\coloneqq\int^{z}\left.\frac{\omega_{0,2}(\zeta_{i},\cdot)}{d\zeta_{i}}\right|_{\zeta_{i}=0}\,,\qquad d\xi^{k,i}(z)\coloneqq d\biggl(\frac{d^{k}}{dx(z)^{k}}\xi^{i}(z)\biggr)\,.

We also set
\Delta^{i}\coloneqq\frac{dy}{d\zeta_{i}}(0)
and
h^{i}\coloneqq C\Delta^{i}
. We define a unital, semisimple TFT on
V\coloneqq\mathbb{C}\braket{e_{1},\dots,e_{r}}
by setting

(2.37)

\eta(e_{i},e_{j})\coloneqq\delta_{i,j}\,,\quad\bm{1}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{r}h^{i}e_{i}\,,\quad w_{g,n}(e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{n}})\coloneqq\delta_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}(h^{i})^{-2g+2-n}\,.

Define the
R
-matrix
R\in\End(V)\llbracket u\rrbracket
and the translation
T\in u^{2}V\llbracket u\rrbracket
by setting

(2.38)

\displaystyle R^{-1}(u)^{j}_{i}

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\sqrt{\frac{u}{2\pi}}\int_{\gamma_{j}}d\xi^{i}\,e^{\frac{1}{u}(x-x(\alpha_{j}))}\,,

(2.39)

\displaystyle T^{i}(u)

\displaystyle\coloneqq uh^{i}-C\sqrt{\frac{u}{2\pi}}\int_{\gamma_{i}}dy\,e^{\frac{1}{u}(x-x(\alpha_{i}))}\,.

Here
\gamma_{i}
is the Lefschetz thimble passing through the critical point
\alpha_{i}
, and the equations are intended as equalities between formal power series in
u
, where on the right-hand side we take the formal asymptotic expansion as
u\to 0
(cf.
appendix

A
). Through the Givental action, we can then define a cohomological field theory

(2.40)

\Omega_{g,n}\coloneqq RTw_{g,n}\in H^{\bullet}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})\otimes(V^{\ast})^{\otimes n}

from the data
(w,R,T)
. One direction of the TR-CohFT correspondence is given by the following theorem (for the other direction, see
[
DOSS14
]
).

Theorem 2.12

(
[
Dun+18
]
)
.

Suppose we have a compact spectral curve with simple ramification points
(\Sigma,x,y,B)
. Then its topological recursion correlators are given by

(2.41)

\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\\
=C^{2g-2+n}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}=1}^{r}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\Omega_{g,n}(e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{n}})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k_{j}\geq 0}\psi_{j}^{k_{j}}d\xi^{k_{j},i_{j}}(z_{j})\,.

3.
Generalised Airy functions

We are interested in two possible generalisations of the Airy differential equation
\ddot{u}(t)=tu(t)
, for which we will present a basis of asymptotic solutions constructed from integral representations. This will generalise the asymptotic formula for the Airy function, valid as
t\to\infty
and
|\arg(t)|<\pi
:

(3.1)

\mathrm{Ai}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{C}e^{\frac{w^{3}}{3}-tw}dw\sim\frac{e^{-\frac{2t^{3/2}}{3}}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}t^{-1/4}\,\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(6k)!}{(2k)!(3k)!}\left(-\frac{1}{576t^{3/2}}\right)^{k}.

Here
C
is the path starting at
e^{-\frac{\pi}{3}}\infty
and ending at
e^{\frac{\pi}{3}}\infty
. We refer to
appendix

A
for some facts about exponential integrals, Lefschetz thimbles and asymptotic expansions.

3.1.
The
\widehat{\mathrm{Ai}}
functions

For fixed integers
r\geq 3
and
0\leq a\leq r-2
, let us consider the second order differential equation

(3.2)

\ddot{u}(t)=t^{r-2}\,u(t)+\frac{a}{t}\,\dot{u}(t)\,.

For fixed
a
, we can define
r-1
solutions that we call
Airy–Lucas functions
, via contour integrals as

(3.3)

\widehat{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,a}^{j}(t)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{(-1)^{j}}}\int_{\hat{C}_{j}}U_{a+1}(w,t)e^{\frac{1}{r}V_{r}(w,t)}dw\,,\qquad\quad j=1,\dots,r-1\,,

where
\sqrt{(-1)^{j}}
is equal to the imaginary unit
\mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1}
if
j
is odd and is equal to
1
if
j
is even. The
U_{n}
and
V_{n}
are Lucas polynomial sequences (cf.
appendix

B
) defined recursively by

(3.4)

\begin{split}&\begin{cases}U_{0}(w,t)=0\,,\\
U_{1}(w,t)=1\,,\\
U_{n}(w,t)=wU_{n-1}(w,t)-tU_{n-2}(w,t)\,,\end{cases}\\
&\begin{cases}V_{0}(w,t)=2\,,\\
V_{1}(w,t)=w\,,\\
V_{n}(w,t)=wV_{n-1}(w,t)-tV_{n-2}(w,t)\,,\end{cases}\end{split}

and the contour
\hat{C}_{j}
is the Lefschetz thimble passing through the critical point
w=2\sqrt{t}\cos(\frac{j\pi}{r})
. Notice that the Airy–Lucas functions are defined for generic phases of

Lemma 3.1
.

The asymptotic behaviour as
t\to\infty
of the Airy–Lucas functions is given by

(3.5)

\widehat{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,a}^{j}(t)\sim\frac{e^{(-1)^{j}\frac{2}{r}t^{r/2}}}{\sqrt{\pi r}}\sin{\left(\frac{(a+1)j\pi}{r}\right)}t^{(2a+2-r)/4}\left(1+O\bigl(t^{-r/2}\bigr)\right).

Proof.

From the recursion relations (
3.4
), it can be easily seen that Lucas sequences are related to Chebyshev polynomials as

U_{n}(w,t)=t^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\,\mathcal{U}_{n-1}\bigl(\tfrac{w}{2\sqrt{t}}\bigr)\,,\qquad V_{n}(w,t)=2t^{\frac{n}{2}}\,\mathcal{T}_{n}\bigl(\tfrac{w}{2\sqrt{t}}\bigr)\,.

Here
\mathcal{T}_{m}
and
\mathcal{U}_{m}
are the Chebyshev polynomials of the first and second kind respectively. By applying the change of variables
z=\frac{w}{2\sqrt{t}}
, we can write

(3.6)

\widehat{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,a}^{j}(t)=\frac{t^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}{\pi\sqrt{(-1)^{j}}}\int_{\hat{\Gamma}_{j}}\mathcal{U}_{a}(z)e^{\frac{2}{r}t^{\frac{r}{2}}\mathcal{T}_{r}(z)}dz\,.

Here
\hat{\Gamma}_{j}
is the Lefschetz thimble in the
z
-plane that passes through the critical point
z=\cos(\frac{j\pi}{r})
. We can now apply the steepest descent method to the function

I(s)=\int_{\Gamma}g(z)e^{sh(z)}dz\,,

with
s=t^{r/2}
,
g(z)=\mathcal{U}_{a}(z)
, and
h(z)=\frac{2}{r}\mathcal{T}_{r}(z)
. This directly gives the following asymptotic expansion:

I(s)\sim g(z_{j})\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{s|h^{\prime\prime}(z_{j})|}}e^{sh(z_{j})}e^{\mathrm{i}\vartheta(z_{j})}\left(1+O\bigl(s^{-1}\bigr)\right),

where the phase
\vartheta
is given by
\vartheta(z_{j})=\frac{\pi-\arg(h^{\prime\prime}(z_{j}))}{2}
. By using the properties of the Chebyshev polynomials, we see that

g(z_{j})=\frac{\sin((a+1)j\pi/r)}{\sin(j\pi/r)}\,,\qquad h(z_{j})=(-1)^{j}\frac{2}{r}\,,\qquad h^{\prime\prime}(z_{j})=(-1)^{j+1}\frac{2r}{\sin^{2}(j\pi/r)}\,.

Thus, we finally get

I(t^{r/2})\sim\sin{\left(\frac{(a+1)j\pi}{r}\right)}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{t^{r/2}r}}e^{(-1)^{j}\frac{2}{r}t^{r/2}}\sqrt{(-1)^{j}}\left(1+O\bigl(t^{-r/2}\bigr)\right).

Plugging this asymptotic expansion into
equation

3.6
gives the statement of the
lemma
.
∎

We can get the full asymptotic expansion by means of the differential equation, and the coefficients of the asymptotic expansion are expressed in terms of the hypergeometric series
\mathsf{B}_{r,a}
defined by
equation

2.26
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The asymptotic expansion as
t\to\infty
of the Airy–Lucas function is given by

(3.7)

\widehat{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,a}^{j}(t)\sim\frac{e^{(-1)^{j}\frac{2}{r}t^{r/2}}}{\sqrt{\pi r}}\,\sin{\left(\frac{(a+1)j\pi}{r}\right)}\,t^{(2a+2-r)/4}\,\mathsf{B}_{r,a}\left((-1)^{j+1}rt^{-r/2}\right).

Proof.

By
lemma

3.1
, we know that the asymptotic expansion of the Airy–Lucas function has the following form:

\widehat{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,a}^{j}(t)\sim\frac{e^{(-1)^{j}\frac{2}{r}t^{r/2}}}{\sqrt{\pi r}}\sin{\left(\frac{(a+1)j\pi}{r}\right)}t^{(2a+2-r)/4}\sum_{m\geq 0}b_{m}(r,a,j)t^{-(rm)/2},

where
b_{0}(r,a,j)=1
. Then, we can plug this ansatz into the differential
equation

3.2
to obtain a recursion for the coefficients
b_{m}(r,a,j)
.
For the sake of notational simplicity, let us define the series

v(t)=\sum_{m\geq 0}b_{m}(r,a,j)t^{(a+1-rm)/2-r/4}\,.

After some elementary algebra, the differential
equation

3.2
yields

\ddot{v}(t)+\left((-1)^{j}2t^{r/2}-a\right)t^{-1}\,\dot{v}(t)+(-1)^{j}\left((\tfrac{r}{2}-a-1)t^{r/2}\right)t^{-2}\,v(t)=0\,.

By picking the power of
t^{(a+1-rm)/2-r/4-2}
, we get

b_{m}(r,a,j)\left[\left(\tfrac{a+1-rm}{2}-\tfrac{r}{4}\right)\left(\tfrac{-a-1-rm}{2}-\tfrac{r}{4}\right)\right]=(-1)^{j}r(m+1)\,b_{m+1}(r,a,j)\,.

This in turns implies that

b_{m+1}(r,a,j)=(-1)^{j}\frac{\big((2m+1)r-(2a+2)\big)\big((2m+1)r+(2a+2)\big)}{16r(m+1)}b_{m}(r,a,j)\,.

We can solve this recursion easily to get

b_{m}(r,a,j)=\frac{(-1)^{jm}}{(16r)^{m}}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\bigl((2k-1)r-2(a+1)\bigr)\bigl((2k-1)r+2(a+1)\bigr)}{k}\,.

Comparing these coefficients with the definition (
2.26
) of
\mathsf{B}_{r,a}
, we get the thesis.
∎

3.2.
The
\widetilde{\mathrm{Ai}}
functions

For
r\geq 3
, let us consider the
r
-th order ordinary differential equation

(3.8)

u^{(r-1)}(t)=(-1)^{r-1}\,t\,u(t)\,,

which is sometimes called higher order Airy equation. We can define
r-1
(independent) solutions via contour integrals that we call
hyper-Airy functions
, as

(3.9)

\widetilde{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,k}(t)\coloneqq\frac{\theta^{k\frac{r-2}{2}}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\tilde{C}_{k}}e^{\frac{w^{r}}{r}-tw}dw\,,\qquad\quad k=0,\dots,r-2\,.

where
\theta=e^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{r-1}}
and
\tilde{C}_{k}
is the Lefschetz thimble passing through the critical point
w=t^{1/(r-1)}\theta^{k}
. As in the previous section, we will be interested in the asymptotic expansion of the above functions, and work in the sectors of the
t
-plane where the Lefschetz thimbles are well-defined.

The hyper-Airy functions indeed satisfy
equation

3.8
:

\begin{split}\left(\frac{d^{r-1}}{dt^{r-1}}-(-1)^{r-1}t\right)\widetilde{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,k}(t)&=(-1)^{r-1}\frac{\theta^{k\frac{r-2}{2}}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\tilde{C}_{k}}(w^{r-1}-t)e^{\frac{w^{r}}{r}-tw}dw\\
&=(-1)^{r-1}\frac{\theta^{k\frac{r-2}{2}}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\tilde{C}_{k}}\frac{d}{dw}e^{\frac{w^{r}}{r}-tw}dw\,,\end{split}

and the right-hand side vanishes by definition of the contours
\tilde{C}_{k}
, since the integrand is exponentially decreasing along the contour. We can compute the asymptotic behaviour of the functions as
t\to\infty
via the steepest descent method.

Lemma 3.3
.

The asymptotic behaviours as
t\to\infty
of the hyper-Airy function and its derivatives are given by

(3.10)

\widetilde{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,k}^{(a)}(t)\sim\frac{(-\theta^{k})^{a}}{\sqrt{2\pi(r-1)}}e^{-\frac{r-1}{r}\theta^{k}t^{\frac{r}{r-1}}}t^{-\frac{r-2a-2}{2(r-1)}}\Bigl(1+O\bigl(t^{-\frac{r}{r-1}}\bigr)\Bigr)\,.

Proof.

With the change of variables
w=t^{1/(r-1)}z
, we find

\widetilde{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,k}^{(a)}(t)=\frac{\theta^{k\frac{r-2}{2}}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\tilde{C}_{k}}(-w)^{a}e^{\frac{w^{r}}{r}-tw}dw=\frac{\theta^{k\frac{r-2}{2}}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}t^{\frac{a+1}{r-1}}\int_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}}(-z)^{a}e^{t^{\frac{r}{r-1}}(\frac{z^{r}}{r}-z)}dz\,.

Here
\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}
is the Lefschetz thimble in the
z
-plane that passes through the critical point
z=\theta^{k}
. We can now apply the steepest descent method to the function

I(s)=\int_{\Gamma}g(z)e^{sh(z)}dz\,,

with
s=t^{\frac{r}{r-1}}
,
g(z)=(-z)^{a}
, and
h(z)=\frac{z^{r}}{r}-z
. As a consequence, we find

I(s)\sim g(z_{k})\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{s|h^{\prime\prime}(z_{k})|}}e^{sh(z_{k})}e^{\mathrm{i}\vartheta(z_{k})}\left(1+O\bigl(s^{-1}\bigr)\right),

where the phase
\vartheta
is given by
\vartheta(z_{k})=\frac{\pi-\arg(h^{\prime\prime}(z_{k}))}{2}
. A simple computation shows that
g(z_{k})=(-\theta^{k})^{a}
,
h(z_{k})=-\frac{r-1}{r}\theta^{k}
, and
h^{\prime\prime}(z_{k})=(r-1)\theta^{k(r-2)}
. Thus,

I(s)\sim(-\theta^{k})^{a}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{s(r-1)}}e^{-\frac{r-1}{r}\theta^{k}s}e^{\mathrm{i}(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{2\pi k(r-2)}{2(r-1)})}\left(1+O\bigl(s^{-1}\bigr)\right).

Taking into account the prefactor and the change of variables
s=t^{\frac{r}{r-1}}
, we find the claimed asymptotic behaviour.
∎

We can get the full asymptotic expansion by means of the differential equation, and the coefficients of the asymptotic expansion are expressed in terms of the polynomials
P_{m}(r,a)
defined by
equation

2.31
.

Proposition 3.4
.

The asymptotic expansions as
t\to\infty
of the hyper-Airy function and its derivatives are given by

(3.11)

\widetilde{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,k}^{(a)}(t)\sim\frac{(-\theta^{k})^{a}}{\sqrt{2\pi(r-1)}}e^{-\frac{r-1}{r}\theta^{k}t^{\frac{r}{r-1}}}t^{-\frac{r-2a-2}{2(r-1)}}\sum_{m\geq 0}P_{m}(r,a)\left(\theta^{-k}\frac{t^{-\frac{r}{r-1}}}{r-1}\right)^{m}.

Proof.

Let us set
u_{a}=\sqrt{2\pi(r-1)}\,\widetilde{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,k}^{(a)}
. From
lemma

3.3
, we know that the asymptotic expansion of
u_{a}
is of the form

u_{a}(t)\sim(-\theta^{k})^{a}e^{-\frac{r-1}{r}\theta^{k}t^{\frac{r}{r-1}}}t^{-\frac{r-2a-2}{2(r-1)}}\sum_{m\geq 0}p_{m}(r,a,k)t^{-m\frac{r}{r-1}}\,,

for some coefficients
p_{m}(r,a,k)
with
p_{0}=1
. Thus, the asymptotic expansion of the derivative of
u_{a-1}
for
a=1,\dots,r-2
reads

\begin{split}\dot{u}_{a-1}(t)\sim(-\theta^{k})^{a-1}e^{-\frac{r-1}{r}\theta^{k}t^{\frac{r}{r-1}}}t^{-\frac{r-2a-2}{2(r-1)}}\sum_{m\geq 0}\biggl(-\theta^{k}p_{m}(r,a-1,k)+&\\
+\frac{r}{r-1}\left(-m+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{a}{r}\right)p_{m-1}(r,a-1,k)\biggr)t^{-m\frac{r}{r-1}}&,\end{split}

which equals

u_{a}(t)\sim(-\theta^{k})^{a}\,e^{-\frac{r-1}{r}\theta^{k}t^{\frac{r}{r-1}}}\,t^{-\frac{r-2a-2}{2(r-1)}}\sum_{m\geq 0}p_{m}(r,a,k)t^{-m\frac{r}{r-1}}\,.

In particular, we get the equality

p_{m}(r,a-1,k)-\theta^{-k}\frac{r}{r-1}\left(-m+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{a}{r}\right)p_{m-1}(r,a-1,k)=p_{m}(r,a,k)\,,

for
a=1,\dots,r-2
. On the other hand, the ODE is equivalent to
u_{r-1}=(-1)^{r-1}t\,u_{0}
, so that we easily find
p_{m}(r,r-1,k)=p_{m}(r,0,k)
. By setting
p_{m}(r,a,k)=(\theta^{k}(r-1))^{-m}P_{m}(r,a,k)
, we obtain the claimed recursion (
2.31
) for the coefficients
P_{m}(r,a,k)
. Notice that
P_{m}(r,a,k)
is independent of
k
, so we can drop it from the notation.
∎

For later convenience, let us introduce the following notation for the asymptotic expansion of the hyper-Airy functions and their derivatives.

Definition 3.5
.

Define the formal power series

(3.12)

\mathsf{A}_{r}^{(a)}(u)=\sum_{m\geq 0}P_{m}(r,a)\left(\frac{u}{r(r-1)}\right)^{m},

so that the asymptotic expansions as
t\to\infty
of the hyper-Airy function and its derivatives can be written as

(3.13)

\widetilde{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,k}^{(a)}(t)\sim\frac{(-\theta^{k})^{a}}{\sqrt{2\pi(r-1)}}\,e^{-\frac{r-1}{r}\theta^{k}t^{\frac{r}{r-1}}}\,t^{-\frac{r-2a-2}{2(r-1)}}\,\mathsf{A}_{r}^{(a)}\bigl(\theta^{-k}\,rt^{-\frac{r}{r-1}}\bigr)\,.

4.
The spectral curves

As explained in
section

2.3
, to every compact spectral curve
\mathcal{S}
one can associate a semisimple CohFT
\Omega_{g,n}=RTw_{g,n}
, constructed through the action of an
R
-matrix and a translation on a TFT, such that the expansion coefficients of the topological recursion correlators
\omega_{g,n}
on a certain basis of differential forms coincide with the CohFT correlators:

(4.1)

\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\Omega_{g,n}(e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{n}})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}\,.

The main goal of this section is to apply the TR-CohFT correspondence to two global spectral curves, proving that the associated correlators are the Witten classes shifted along
v_{r-2}
and
v_{1}
, and establish a connection with the generalised Airy functions.

4.1.
The spectral curve for the
v_{r-2}
shift

The goal of this section is to prove the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
be the
1
-parameter family of spectral curves on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
given by

(4.2)

x(z)=V_{r}(z,\epsilon)\,,\qquad\quad y(z)=z\,,\qquad\quad\omega_{0,2}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}\,,

where
V_{r}
is the
r
-th Lucas polynomial of the second kind. The CohFT associated to
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
is the shifted Witten
r
-spin class
\widehat{W}^{r,\epsilon}
. More precisely, the topological recursion correlators
\widehat{\omega}_{g,n}^{r,\epsilon}
corresponding to the spectral curve
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
are

(4.3)

\begin{split}&\widehat{\omega}_{g,n}^{r,\epsilon}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\\
&\qquad=(-r)^{g-1}\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}=0}^{r-2}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\widehat{W}^{r,\epsilon}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k_{i}\geq 0}\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}d\hat{\xi}^{k_{i},a_{i}}(z_{i})\,,\end{split}

and the differentials
d\hat{\xi}^{k,a}(z)
are given by

(4.4)

\hat{\xi}^{a}(z)=-\frac{U_{r-1-a}(z,\epsilon)}{U_{r}(z,\epsilon)}\,,\qquad\quad d\hat{\xi}^{k,a}(z)=d\left(\left(\frac{1}{rU_{r}(z,\epsilon)}\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{k}\hat{\xi}^{a}(z)\right),

where
U_{p}
is the
p
-th Lucas polynomial of the first kind.

To prove the theorem, let us compute the ingredients of the TR-CohFT correspondence. We refer to
appendix

B
for properties of the Lucas sequences that will be used in this section. We use the identification

\lambda:=\sqrt{\epsilon},

in order to avoid annoying square routes in the following. The ramification points of the spectral curve
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
are given by

(4.5)

x^{\prime}(z)=rU_{r}(z,\lambda^{2})=r\lambda^{r-1}\mathcal{U}_{r-1}\left(\frac{z}{2\lambda}\right)=0\,,

where
\mathcal{U}_{s}
is the
s
-th Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind. The solutions are
\alpha_{k}=2\lambda\cos(\frac{k\pi}{r})
for
k=1,\dots,r-1
, and we have
x(\alpha_{k})=x_{k}=(-1)^{k}2\lambda^{r}
. We choose local coordinates such that

(4.6)

x(z)-x_{k}=-\frac{\zeta_{k}^{2}(z)}{2}\,,

and a determination of the square root such that

(4.7)

\zeta_{k}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(-1)^{k}}}\frac{r\,\lambda^{\frac{r-2}{2}}}{\sqrt{2}\sin(\frac{k\pi}{r})}(z-\alpha_{k})+O\bigl((z-\alpha_{k})^{2}\bigr)\,.

As a consequence,
\Delta^{k}=\sqrt{(-1)^{k}}\,\frac{\sqrt{2}\sin(\frac{k\pi}{r})}{r\lambda^{(r-2)/2}}
. Moreover, we choose the global constant
C=-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{r}\lambda^{\frac{r-2}{2}}
, so that
h^{k}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{r}}\frac{\sin(\frac{k\pi}{r})}{\sqrt{(-1)^{k+1}}}
. In this way, the TFT is given by

(4.8)

\displaystyle V=\mathbb{C}\braket{e_{1},\dots,e_{r-1}}\,,\qquad\eta(e_{k},e_{l})=\delta_{k,l}\,,\qquad\bm{1}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{r}}\,\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}\frac{\sin(\tfrac{k\pi}{r})}{\sqrt{(-1)^{k+1}}}e_{k}\,,

\displaystyle\widehat{w}_{g,n}(e_{k_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{k_{n}})=\delta_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{r}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{(-1)^{k+1}}}{\sin(\tfrac{k\pi}{r})}\right)^{2g-2+n}.

Let us compute the other ingredients of the TR-CohFT correspondence.

Lemma 4.2
.

In the canonical basis
(e_{1},\dots,e_{r-1})
, we have the following:

•

The auxiliary functions are given by

(4.9)

\xi^{k}(z)=-{\textstyle\sqrt{(-1)^{k}}}\,\frac{\sqrt{2}\sin(\frac{k\pi}{r})}{r\lambda^{\frac{r-2}{2}}}\frac{1}{z-2\lambda\cos(\frac{k\pi}{r})}\,.

•

The
R
-matrix elements in the canonical basis (denoted
R^{-1}_{\textup{C}}
) are given by

(4.10)

R^{-1}_{\textup{C}}(u)_{i}^{j}=\frac{2}{r}\frac{\sqrt{(-1)^{j}}}{\sqrt{(-1)^{i}}}\sum_{p=0}^{r-2}\sin\left(\frac{(p+1)i\pi}{r}\right)\sin\left(\frac{(p+1)j\pi}{r}\right)\mathsf{B}_{r,p}\left((-1)^{j+1}\frac{u}{\lambda^{r}}\right).

•

The translation is given by
T(u)=u(\bm{1}-R^{-1}(u)\bm{1})
. Hence, the CohFT
\Omega_{g,n}=RT\widehat{w}_{g,n}
obeys the flat unit axiom.

Proof.

We compute the auxiliary functions as

\begin{split}\xi^{k}(z)&=\int^{z}\frac{B(z_{0},\cdot)}{d\zeta_{k}(z_{0})}\bigg|_{z_{0}=\alpha_{k}}=\Delta^{k}\int^{z}\frac{dz}{(\alpha_{k}-z)^{2}}=-\frac{\Delta^{k}}{z-\alpha_{k}}\,.\end{split}

For the
R
-matrix (we drop the subscript “C” in the proof), we start by integrating by parts:

R^{-1}(u)_{i}^{j}=-\sqrt{\frac{u}{2\pi}}\int_{\gamma_{j}}d\xi^{i}\,e^{\frac{1}{u}(x-x_{j})}=\sqrt{\frac{u}{2\pi}}\int_{\gamma_{j}}\xi^{i}\,e^{\frac{1}{u}(x-x_{j})}\frac{dx}{u}\,.

We perform now the change of variables
z=(\frac{u}{r})^{1/r}w
. A simple computation shows that

\begin{split}\frac{x-x_{j}}{u}&=\frac{1}{u}V_{r}\left((\tfrac{u}{r})^{1/r}w,\lambda^{2}\right)-\frac{x_{j}}{u}=\frac{1}{r}V_{r}\left(w,\lambda^{2}(\tfrac{r}{u})^{2/r}\right)-\frac{x_{j}}{u}\,,\\
\frac{dx}{u}&=\frac{r}{u}U_{r}\left((\tfrac{u}{r})^{1/r}w,\lambda^{2}\right)\left(\frac{u}{r}\right)^{1/r}dw=U_{r}\left(w,\lambda^{2}(\tfrac{r}{u})^{2/r}\right)dw\,.\end{split}

In both expressions, we used the quasi-homogeneity of type
(1,2)
of the Lucas sequences (see
table

1
). Moreover,
w
runs along the Lefschetz thimble
\hat{C}_{j}
passing through the critical point
w=2\lambda\left(\frac{r}{u}\right)^{1/r}\cos(\frac{j\pi}{r})
. As a consequence, we find

\begin{split}R^{-1}(u)^{j}_{i}&=-\Delta^{i}e^{-\frac{x_{j}}{u}}\sqrt{\frac{u}{2\pi}}\int_{\hat{C}_{j}}\frac{U_{r}(w,t)}{(\frac{u}{r})^{1/r}w-\alpha_{i}}e^{\frac{1}{r}V_{r}(w,t)}dw\\
&=-\Delta^{i}e^{-\frac{x_{j}}{u}}\sqrt{\frac{u}{2\pi}}\left(\frac{r}{u}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\int_{\hat{C}_{j}}\frac{U_{r}(w,t)}{w-(\frac{r}{u})^{1/r}\alpha_{i}}e^{\frac{1}{r}V_{r}(w,t)}dw\,,\end{split}

where in the last equation we simply set
t^{1/2}=\lambda\left(\frac{r}{u}\right)^{1/r}
. As
(\frac{r}{u})^{1/r}\alpha_{i}=2\sqrt{t}\cos(\frac{i\pi}{r})
, we can use
lemma

B.3
to get

\begin{split}&R^{-1}(u)^{j}_{i}=\\
&=-\Delta^{i}e^{-\frac{x_{j}}{u}}\sqrt{\frac{u}{2\pi}}\left(\frac{r}{u}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\sum_{p=0}^{r-2}(-1)^{i+1}t^{\frac{r-p-2}{2}}\frac{\sin\bigl(\frac{(p+1)i\pi}{r}\bigr)}{\sin\left(\frac{i\pi}{r}\right)}\int_{\hat{C}_{j}}U_{p+1}(w,t)e^{\frac{1}{r}V_{r}(w,t)}dw\\
&=2\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{r}}e^{-\frac{(-1)^{j}2\lambda^{r}}{u}}\frac{\sqrt{(-1)^{j}}}{\sqrt{(-1)^{i}}}\sum_{p=0}^{r-2}t^{\frac{r-2p-2}{4}}\sin\left(\frac{(p+1)i\pi}{r}\right)\widehat{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,p}^{j}(t)\,.\end{split}

Using the asymptotic expansion of the functions
\widehat{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,p}^{j}
derived in
proposition

3.2
, the above expression simplifies to

R^{-1}(u)^{j}_{i}=\frac{2}{r}\frac{\sqrt{(-1)^{j}}}{\sqrt{(-1)^{i}}}\sum_{p=0}^{r-2}\sin\left(\frac{(p+1)i\pi}{r}\right)\sin\left(\frac{(p+1)j\pi}{r}\right)\mathsf{B}_{r,p}\left((-1)^{j+1}rt^{-r/2}\right)\,.

constructed by the topological recursion in the previous section, and the shifted Witten class, as
\epsilon\to 0
. We will prove that the
r
-Airy curve computes the descendants of the Witten
r
-spin class and use that to prove Witten’s
r
-spin conjecture.

5.1.
The
\epsilon\to 0
limit

For the shifted Witten class, we can use the following theorem of Pandharipande–Pixton–Zvonkine
[
PPZ19
]
.

Proposition 5.1

(
[
PPZ19
, theorems 8, 9]
)
.

The constant coefficient in
\epsilon
of both shifted Witten classes
\widehat{W}^{r,\epsilon}_{g,n}
and
\widetilde{W}^{r,\epsilon}_{g,n}
is the Witten class
W^{r}_{g,n}
:

(5.1)

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\widehat{W}^{r,\epsilon}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\widetilde{W}^{r,\epsilon}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})=W^{r}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})\,.

Moreover, the parts of
\widehat{W}^{r,\epsilon}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})
and
\widetilde{W}^{r,\epsilon}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})
of degree higher than
D^{r}_{g,a}
vanish.

Now, we would like to understand the limit on the topological recursion side. We claim that taking the limit commutes with the topological recursion in the following sense. The limit as
\epsilon\to 0
of both spectral curves
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
gives the following spectral curve
\mathcal{S}_{r}
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, which is known in the literature as the
r
-Airy spectral curve
:

(5.2)

x(z)=z^{r}\,,\qquad\quad y(z)=z\,,\qquad\quad\omega_{0,2}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}\,.

Then, we claim that the correlators constructed by topological recursion on
\mathcal{S}_{r}
coincide with the limit as
\epsilon\to 0
of the correlators constructed by topological recursion on
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
. Below we will prove the claim for a sufficiently general class of spectral curves and show that one of the spectral curves we are interested in fits into this class:
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
. This is enough for our purposes, since both
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
allow us to access the same limit (see
remark

5.5
for comments on the proof of the claim for the other spectral curve
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
). In this section, we make the dependence on
\epsilon
explicit in the topological recursion correlators, in order to avoid any confusion.

A
1
-parameter family of spectral curves on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
indexed by
\epsilon\in\mathbb{C}
is the data, for each
\epsilon\in\mathbb{C}
, of a spectral curve
\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}=(\Sigma,x_{\epsilon},y_{\epsilon},\omega_{0,2}^{\epsilon})
, where the Riemann surface
\Sigma
is common for all
\epsilon
. The functions
x_{\epsilon}
and
y_{\epsilon}
are allowed to vary, as well as the bidifferential
\omega_{0,2}^{\epsilon}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}
be a family of spectral curves indexed by
\epsilon\in\mathbb{C}
such that in a neighbourhood of
\epsilon=0
, they satisfy the following assumptions.

(1)

\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}
is defined by an algebraic equation linear in
x_{\epsilon}
:

(5.3)

P_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon},y_{\epsilon})=A_{\epsilon}(y)+x_{\epsilon}\,B_{\epsilon}(y_{\epsilon})=0\,,

where
A_{\epsilon}(y_{\epsilon})
,
B_{\epsilon}(y_{\epsilon})
are polynomials in
y_{\epsilon}
and
\epsilon
. The Newton’s polygon of this algebraic equation has no interior point, so the Riemann surface
\Sigma
is of genus
0
. As a consequence, the bidifferentials
\omega_{0,2}^{\epsilon}
do not depend on
\epsilon
:
\omega_{0,2}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}
.

(2)

For
\epsilon\neq 0
,
\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}
has
r-1
simple ramification points
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r-1}
, while
\mathcal{S}_{0}
has a single ramification point of degree
r-1
and is admissible in the sense of
[
Bor+24
]
. In addition, we assume that the branch points are distinct, i.e., that
x_{\epsilon}(\alpha_{i})\neq x_{\epsilon}(\alpha_{j})
for any
i\neq j
.

(3)

The multidifferentials
\omega_{g,n}^{\epsilon}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
produced by topological recursion admit limits as
\epsilon\to 0
:

(5.4)

\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\coloneqq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\,\omega_{g,n}^{\epsilon}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\,.

Then the multidifferentials
\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
satisfy the local topological recursion (
2.35
) applied to the spectral curve
\mathcal{S}_{0}
.

Remark 5.3
.

Before proving the statement, we remark that the above proposition does not hold if we completely drop assumption (
1
). It is easy to find examples of families of spectral curves, where the limit of the correlators
does not
(!) coincide with the correlators of the limit spectral curve. However, all three assumptions can be relaxed and a more general statement than the one we prove here will appear in
[
Bor+23
]
. Thus, we content ourselves with the statement above that suffices for our purposes.

Proof.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{C}
be a neighbourhood of
\epsilon=0
for which the assumptions of the proposition hold. For later use, denote by
p_{0}(z)
the coefficient of
y^{r}
in
P_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon},y_{\epsilon})
.

First, for
\epsilon\in U
, topological recursion on
\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}
produces symmetric multidifferentials
\omega_{g,n}^{\epsilon}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
(for
\epsilon=0
this is a consequence of the admissibility condition and
[
Bor+24
, theorem E]
). As a result, the following recursive formula, known as the
global topological recursion
(see
remark

2.11
), makes sense for all
\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}
with
\epsilon\in U
:

(5.5)

\begin{split}\omega_{g,n}^{\epsilon}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})&=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\Res_{z=\alpha_{i}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[r-1]\\
I\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{|I|+1}\int_{w=o}^{z}\omega_{0,2}(z_{1},w)}{\prod_{i\in I}\bigl(\omega_{0,1}^{\epsilon}(z)-\omega_{0,1}^{\epsilon}(\sigma_{i}(z))\bigr)}\\
&\qquad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\vdash I\cup\{r\}\\
\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell(J)}N_{i}=\{2,\dots,n\}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell(J)}g_{i}=g+\ell(J)-|I|-1\end{subarray}}^{\textup{no }(0,1)}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(J)}\omega_{g_{i},|J_{i}|+|N_{i}|}^{\epsilon}(\sigma_{J_{i}}(z),z_{N_{i}})\,,\end{split}

where
o\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
is an arbitrary base point, and the set
(\sigma_{i}(z))_{i\in[r]}
is the set of global sheet involutions – that depend on
\epsilon
– of the degree
r
branched covering
x_{\epsilon}
such that
\sigma_{r}(z)=z
. Moreover, as shown in
[
BE13
, theorem 5]
, it is equivalent to the local definition of the topological recursion (
equation

2.35
for
\epsilon=0
and
equation

2.32
for
\epsilon\in U\setminus\{0\}
).

Second, the first assumption implies that
\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}
is an admissible spectral curve in the sense of
[
BE17
, definition 2.7]
for all
\epsilon\in U
. Namely:

•

the Newton polygon of
\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}
has no interior point,

•

the curve is smooth as an affine curve (however, it is sufficient to check smoothness at the origin
(x_{\epsilon},y_{\epsilon})=(0,0)
).

By the second point and the fact that global topological recursion is well-defined for all
\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}
, we can apply
[
BE17
, theorem 3.26]
. It states that global topological recursion –
equation

5.5
– on
\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}
is equivalent to the residue formula:

(5.6)

0=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\Res_{z=\alpha_{i}}\left(\int_{w=o}^{z}\omega_{0,2}(z_{1},w)\right)Q_{g,n}^{\epsilon}(z,z_{2},\dots,z_{n})\,,

where
o\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
is an arbitrary base point, and the multidifferential
Q_{g,n}^{\epsilon}
is defined by

(5.7)

\begin{split}Q_{g,n}^{\epsilon}(z,z_{2},\dots,z_{n})&=\frac{dx_{\epsilon}(z)}{\partial P_{\epsilon}/\partial y_{\epsilon}(z)}p_{0}(z)\sum_{k=1}^{r}(-1)^{k}y_{\epsilon}(z)^{r-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[r]\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{dx_{\epsilon}(z)^{k}}\\
&\qquad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\vdash I\\
\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell(J)}N_{i}=\{2,\dots,n\}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell(J)}g_{i}=g+\ell(J)-|I|-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(J)}\omega_{g_{i},|J_{i}|+|N_{i}|}^{\epsilon}(\sigma_{J_{i}}(z),z_{N_{i}})\,.\end{split}

By equivalent, we mean that there exists a unique collection of multidifferentials
\omega_{g,n}
on an admissible spectral curve which satisfies the polarization condition (also known as the projection property)

(5.8)

\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\Res_{z=\alpha_{i}}\left(\int_{w=o}^{z}\omega_{0,2}(z_{1},w)\right)\omega_{g,n}(z,z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})

and
equation

5.6
, and that this unique solution is constructed by the global topological recursion.

The object
Q_{g,n}^{\epsilon}(z,z_{2},\dots,z_{n})
is a
1
-form in
z
which, by
[
BE17
, lemma 4.7]
, has poles only at
x_{\epsilon}(z)=x_{\epsilon}(z_{j})
, and in particular it has no pole at the ramification points. Thus, upon taking the limit as
\epsilon\to 0
of
equation

5.7
, we see that the object
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}Q_{g,n}^{\epsilon}(z,z_{2},\dots,z_{n})
is well-defined and has no poles except at coinciding points where
x(z)=x(z_{j})
. Moreover, on the right-hand side we can replace the
\omega_{g,n}^{\epsilon}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
with their limits as
\epsilon\to 0
.

Now, as the unique solution to
equation

5.7
is given by the global topological recursion, we have:

(1)

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\,\omega_{g,n}^{\epsilon}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
;

(2)

the correlators
\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
satisfy the global topological recursion (
equation

5.5
) for
\mathcal{S}_{0}
, and therefore also the local topological recursion on the same curve.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Finally, we are ready to prove the main result of this section (which recovers the results
[
Dun+19
, theorem 7.3]
,
[
Mil16
, theorem 1.1]
and
[
BCEG21
, corollary 4.11]
).

Theorem 5.4
.

The CohFT associated to the
r
-Airy spectral curve on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, given by

(5.9)

x(z)=z^{r}\,,\qquad\quad y(z)=z\,,\qquad\quad\omega_{0,2}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}\,,

is the Witten
r
-spin class
W^{r}_{g,n}
. More precisely, the Bouchard–Eynard topological recursion correlators –
equation

2.35
– corresponding to the
r
-Airy spectral curve are

(5.10)

\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=(-r)^{g-1}\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}=0}^{r-2}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}W^{r}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k_{i}\geq 0}\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}d\xi^{k_{i},a_{i}}(z_{i})\,,

and the differentials
d\xi^{k,a}(z)
are given by

(5.11)

d\xi^{k,a}(z)=(-1)^{k}\,r\,\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{a+1}{r}+k+1\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{a+1}{r}\bigr)}\,\frac{dz}{z^{kr+a+2}}\,.

Proof.

Consider the shifted Witten
r
-spin class
\widetilde{W}^{r,\epsilon}_{g,n}
: for
\epsilon\neq 0
, the correlators
\widetilde{\omega}_{g,n}^{\epsilon}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
computed by topological recursion from the spectral curve
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
satisfy
equation

4.18
.

On the CohFT side, we have already seen in
equation

5.1
that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\widetilde{W}^{r,\epsilon}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})=W^{r}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})\,.

Moreover,
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\,d\tilde{\xi}^{k,a}(z)=d\xi^{k,a}(z)
. Therefore, the correlators of
equation

4.18
have well-defined limits as
\epsilon\to 0
, which are the following:

(5.12)

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\,\widetilde{\omega}_{g,n}^{r,\epsilon}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\\
=(-r)^{g-1}\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}=0}^{r-2}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}W^{r}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k_{i}\geq 0}\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}d\xi^{k_{i},a_{i}}(z_{i})\,.

On the topological recursion side, the family of spectral curves
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
satisfies the assumptions of
proposition

5.2
. Indeed:

(1)

for
\epsilon\neq 0
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
is defined by the algebraic equation:

x-y^{r}+r\,\epsilon\,y=0\,;

(2)

for
\epsilon\neq 0
, the
r-1
ramification points of
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{r,\epsilon}
, namely
\epsilon^{\frac{1}{(r-1)}}e^{\frac{2\pi k\mathrm{i}}{r-1}}
for
k=0,\dots,r-2
, are simple and the branch points are distinct. At
\epsilon=0
, the spectral curve
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}^{r,0}
is the
r
-Airy curve, which is admissible;

(3)

equation

5.12
shows that the multidifferentials
\widetilde{\omega}_{g,n}^{r,\epsilon}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
have well-defined limits as
\epsilon\to 0
.

Therefore, we can apply
proposition

5.2
: for
2g-2+n>0
, local topological recursion on the
r
-Airy spectral curve constructs the correlators

\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=(-r)^{g-1}\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}=0}^{r-2}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}W^{r}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k_{i}\geq 0}\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}d\xi^{k_{i},a_{i}}(z_{i})\,.\qed

Remark 5.5
.

In principle, the above proof should work in the case of the shifted Witten class
\widehat{W}^{r,\epsilon}_{g,n}
as well. However, the corresponding spectral curve
\widehat{S}^{r,\epsilon}
has coinciding branch points (indeed,

5.2.
Witten’s
r
-spin conjecture

In 1993, Witten formulated the conjecture
[
Wit93
]
that the generating series of
r
-spin intersection numbers satisfy the
r
-KdV hierarchy (or
r
-th Gel’fand–Dikii hierarchy) and the string equation. While the conjecture for
r=2
has received many proofs by now
[
Kon92
,
Mir07
,
OP09
,
And+20
]
, the general
r
case was only proved by Faber–Shadrin–Zvonkine in 2010
[
FSZ10
]
.

The generating series studied in Witten’s conjecture is given by

(5.13)

F_{r\textup{-spin}}(\bm{t};\hbar)=\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\geq 0,n\geq 1\\
2g-2+n>0\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\frac{\hbar^{g-1}}{n!}\!\!\!\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}=0}^{r-2}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}W^{r}_{g,n}(v_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{a_{n}})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k_{i}\geq 0}\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}\,t_{k_{i},a_{i}}\,.

In this section we take advantage of the fact that we have proved topological recursion for
r
-spin intersection numbers without making use of the Faber–Shadrin–Zvonkine result, which allows us to deduce Witten’s
r
-spin conjecture from
theorem

5.4
.

Theorem 5.6
.

The generating series of
r
-spin intersection numbers
Z_{r\textup{-spin}}=\exp(F_{r\textup{-spin}})
is the tau function of the
r
-KdV hierarchy satisfying the string equation (unique up to multiplicative constant).

Proof.

Our proof follows from translating the topological recursion for the
r
-Airy spectral curve into the following equivalent settings:

(1)

The Bouchard–Eynard topological recursion for the
\omega_{g,n}
from (
5.10
) is equivalent to the so-called higher abstract loop equations for
\omega_{g,n}
(see
[
BE17
]
or
[
Bor+24
, appendix C]
).

(2)

The higher abstract loop equations for
\omega_{g,n}
are equivalent to a system of differential equations
[
Bor+24
, theorem 5.30]
, which are identified for the
r
-Airy curve with the higher quantum Airy structure associated to the partition function
Z_{(r,r+1)}

[
Bor+24
, section 6.1]
. This higher Airy structure corresponds to specific
W(\mathfrak{gl}_{r})
constraints (we call them
W_{r}
-constraints here), which determine uniquely its solution
Z_{(r,r+1)}
up to a multiplicative constant.

(3)

In
[
AvM92
]
, the authors proved the existence of a tau function
Z_{r\textup{-tau}}
of the
r
-KdV hierarchy satisfying the string equation. They also showed that the
r
-KdV hierarchy together with the string equation imply the
W_{r}
-constraints and that the
W_{r}
-constraints have a unique solution if this exists. Hence they deduce that the
r
-KdV hierarchy and the string equation are equivalent to the
W_{r}
-constraints. From the previous equivalence, we conclude that
Z_{(r,r+1)}=Z_{r\textup{-tau}}
.

(4)

Our
theorem

5.4
tells us that the partition function associated to the differentials
\omega_{g,n}
from (
5.10
), obtained by the Bouchard–Eynard topological recursion applied to the
r
-Airy spectral curve, is
Z_{r\textup{-spin}}
. The second equivalence gives us
Z_{(r,r+1)}=Z_{r\textup{-spin}}
.

We have shown that
Z_{r\textup{-spin}}=Z_{r\textup{-tau}}
, as was needed to recover Witten’s
r
-spin conjecture (Faber–Shadrin–Zvonkine theorem).
∎

Appendix A
Exponential integrals

The aim of this appendix is to discuss the asymptotic behaviour as
s\to\infty
of integrals of the form

(A.1)

I_{\Gamma}(s)=\int_{\Gamma}g(z)e^{sh(z)}dz\,,

where
g
and
h
are polynomials and
\Gamma
is a path in
\mathbb{C}
. In addition, we require that
h
has finitely many critical points which are all non-degenerate, and we denote the set of critical points by
\mathfrak{a}
. In order to make sense of the above integral, the integration cycle
\Gamma
should represent an element of the relative homology
H_{1}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C}_{-T};\mathbb{Z})
for very large
T
, where we set

(A.2)

\mathbb{C}_{-T}=\Set{z\in\mathbb{C}}{\Re(sh(z))\leq-T}\,.

A basis for the relative homology
H_{1}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C}_{-T};\mathbb{Z})
that is best suited for computations of the asymptotic expansion of
I
is
given by the Lefschetz thimbles. Such a basis
(\Gamma_{a})_{a\in\mathfrak{a}}
of
H_{1}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C}_{-T};\mathbb{Z})
is indexed by the critical point of
h
, and it is defined as follows:

(A.3)

\Gamma_{a}=\Set{z(t)}{\lim_{t\to-\infty}z(t)=a},

where
z\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a solution of the steepest descent equation

(A.4)

\frac{dz}{dt}=-\frac{\partial\overline{sh}}{\partial\overline{z}}\,.

From the above definition, we conclude that
\Gamma_{a}
satisfies two key properties: 1) the real part
\Re(sh)
decreases on
\Gamma_{a}
in directions away from
a
, and 2) the imaginary part
\Im(sh)
is constant along
\Gamma_{a}
. Indeed, from the steepest descent equation and the Cauchy–Riemann equation
\partial_{z}\overline{sh}=0
, we deduce that along the flow line

(A.5)

\frac{d\overline{sh}}{dt}=\frac{\partial\overline{sh}}{\partial z}\frac{dz}{dt}+\frac{\partial\overline{sh}}{\partial\overline{z}}\frac{d\overline{z}}{dt}=-\left|\frac{dz}{dt}\right|^{2}.

By taking the real and the imaginary parts of the above equation, we deduce the two key properties:

(A.6)

\frac{d\Re(sh)}{dt}=-\left|\frac{dz}{dt}\right|^{2}\leq 0\,,\qquad\quad\frac{d\Im(sh)}{dt}=0\,.

Once we pick an orientation of
\Gamma_{a}
, it will define a cycle in the relative homology
H_{1}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C}_{-T};\mathbb{Z})
if it is closed. This fails precisely if there is a flow line that starts at
a
at
t=-\infty
and ends at another critical point
b\neq a
at
t=+\infty
. If this is the case, the flow line is called a
Stokes line
. If not, the flow line is called a
Lefschetz thimble
.

Notice that, in order to have a Stokes line connecting the critical points
a
and
b
, a necessary condition is that the imaginary part of
sh(z)
coincides at the two critical points:
\Im(sh(a))=\Im(sh(b))
. In particular, for a generic phase of
s
, this condition is not satisfied and the set of Lefschetz thimbles
(\Gamma_{a})_{a\in\mathfrak{a}}
is a basis of the relative homology
H_{1}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C}_{-T};\mathbb{Z})
. The values of
s
for which a Stokes line occur are also called
Stokes rays
, which divide the complex
s
-plane into open sectors, sometimes called
Stokes sectors
.

The advantage of considering Lefschetz thimbles is that we can easily compute the asymptotic expansion of
I_{\Gamma_{a}}(s)
as
|s|\to\infty
using the steepest descent method, as
I_{\Gamma_{a}}(s)
is dominated by the contribution from the critical point
a
. Indeed, choose local coordinates around
a
such that
h(z)-h(a)=-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{2}
, so that we have a local change of variables
z=z(\zeta)
. Moreover, consider the Taylor expansion

(A.7)

g(z(\zeta))dz(\zeta)=\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}\zeta^{2k}+\text{odd terms}\right)d\zeta\,.

Then we get the following asymptotic expansion for
|s|\to+\infty
with
s
not lying in a Stokes ray:

(A.8)

I_{\Gamma_{a}}(s)\sim\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{s}}e^{sh(a)}\sum_{k\geq 0}(2k-1)!!c_{k}s^{-k}\,,

with the convention
(-1)!!=1
. It easy to check that the first coefficient is given by

Appendix B
On Lucas sequences

In this appendix, we are going to recall some relations between Lucas polynomial sequences
U_{n}(w,t)
,
V_{n}(w,t)
and deduce some expressions for their derivatives. Thanks to such formulae, we can prove a relation that allows us to conclude that the functions
3.3
satisfy the ODE
3.2
.

To begin with, let us recall the recursive definition of Lucas sequences of the first and second kind, denoted respectively as
U_{n}(w,t)
and
V_{n}(w,t)
:

(B.1)

\begin{split}&\begin{cases}U_{0}(w,t)=0\,,\\
U_{1}(w,t)=1\,,\\
U_{n}(w,t)=wU_{n-1}(w,t)-tU_{n-2}(w,t)\,,\end{cases}\\
&\begin{cases}V_{0}(w,t)=2\,,\\
V_{1}(w,t)=w\,,\\
V_{n}(w,t)=wV_{n-1}(w,t)-tV_{n-2}(w,t)\,.\end{cases}\end{split}

Lucas sequences
[
Luc78
]
are related to Chebyshev polynomials, as one can easily check comparing the recurrence relations:

(B.2)

U_{n}(w,t)=t^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\mathcal{U}_{n-1}\bigl(\tfrac{w}{2\sqrt{t}}\bigr)\,,\qquad V_{n}(w,t)=2t^{\frac{n}{2}}\mathcal{T}_{n}\bigl(\tfrac{w}{2\sqrt{t}}\bigr)\,.

Here
\mathcal{T}_{m}
and
\mathcal{U}_{m}
are the Chebyshev polynomials of the first and second kind respectively. Lucas sequences are also related to various integer sequences like the Fibonacci numbers, Mersenne numbers, Pell numbers, Lucas numbers, and Jacobsthal numbers.

Lucas sequences satisfy many properties, some of which are summarised in the following table.

Quadratic fomulae

U_{n}^{2}=t^{n-1}+U_{n+1}U_{n-1}

V_{n}^{2}=-\Delta t^{n-1}+V_{n+1}V_{n-1}

Conversion fomulae

wU_{n}=V_{n}+2tU_{n-1}

wV_{n}=\Delta U_{n}+2tV_{n-1}

Quasi-homogeneity

U_{n}(aw,a^{2}t)=a^{n-1}U_{n}(w,t)

V_{n}(aw,a^{2}t)=a^{n}V_{n}(w,t)

Table 1
.
Some relations satisfied by the Lucas sequences. Here we denote
\Delta=w^{2}-4t
, called the discriminant.

Thanks to their relation to Chebyshev polynomials, it is easy to check the following formulae satisfied by the derivatives of the Lucas sequences. In the following, we will denote the derivative with respect to
t
with a dot and the derivative with respect to
w
with a prime.

Lemma B.1
.

Define the discriminant as
\Delta=w^{2}-4t
. Then

(B.3)

\displaystyle\dot{U}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(n-1)V_{n-1}-wU_{n-1}}{\Delta}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle U_{n}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{nV_{n}-wU_{n}}{\Delta}\,,

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{n}

\displaystyle=-nU_{n-1}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle V_{n}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=nU_{n}\,.

The aim of this appendix is to prove the following result.

Proposition B.2
.

For
a=0,\dots,r-2
and
j=1,\dots,r-1
, the functions

(B.4)

\widehat{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,a}^{j}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{(-1)^{j}}}\int_{\hat{C}_{j}}U_{a+1}(w,t)e^{\frac{1}{r}V_{r}(w,t)}dw

satisfy the differential equation
\ddot{u}(t)=t^{r-2}u(t)+\frac{a}{t}\dot{u}(t)
.

Proof.

Direct computations show that

\left(\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}-t^{r-2}-\frac{a}{t}\frac{d}{dt}\right)\widehat{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,a}^{j}(t)=\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{(-1)^{j}}}\int_{\hat{C}_{j}}\Bigl(\ddot{U}_{a+1}+2\frac{\dot{U}_{a+1}\dot{V}_{r}}{r}+\frac{U_{a+1}\ddot{V}_{r}}{r}+\frac{U_{a+1}(\dot{V}_{r})^{2}}{r^{2}}\\
-t^{r-2}U_{a+1}-\frac{a}{t}\dot{U}_{a+1}-\frac{a}{tr}U_{a+1}\dot{V}_{r}\Bigr)e^{\frac{1}{r}V_{r}(w,t)}dw\,.

We now claim that the integrand on the right hand side equals

\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\biggl[\Bigl(U_{a+1}U_{r-2}+\frac{1}{t}U_{a+1}^{\prime}\Bigr)e^{\frac{1}{r}V_{r}(w,t)}\biggr]\,.

The claim would prove the proposition, since the integral of the above quantity along
\hat{C}_{j}
vanishes (by design,
e^{\frac{1}{r}V_{r}(w,t)}
is exponentially decreasing along the contour). Moreover, the claim is equivalent to the following relation between Lucas sequences:

\ddot{U}_{a+1}+2\frac{\dot{U}_{a+1}\dot{V}_{r}}{r}+\frac{U_{a+1}\ddot{V}_{r}}{r}+\frac{U_{a+1}(\dot{V}_{r})^{2}}{r^{2}}-t^{r-2}U_{a+1}-\frac{a}{t}\dot{U}_{a+1}-\frac{a}{tr}U_{a+1}\dot{V}_{r}=\\
=U_{a+1}^{\prime}U_{r-2}+U_{a+1}U_{r-2}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{t}U_{a+1}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{U_{a+1}U_{r-2}V_{r}^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{U_{a+1}^{\prime}V_{r}^{\prime}}{tr}\,.

In order to prove this expression let us group the terms as follows:

{\color[rgb]{0,0.88,0}\ddot{U}_{a+1}}{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}+2\frac{\dot{U}_{a+1}\dot{V}_{r}}{r}}{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}+\frac{U_{a+1}\ddot{V}_{r}}{r}}{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}+\frac{U_{a+1}(\dot{V}_{r})^{2}}{r^{2}}-t^{r-2}U_{a+1}}{\color[rgb]{0,0.88,0}-\frac{a}{t}\dot{U}_{a+1}}{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}-\frac{a}{tr}U_{a+1}\dot{V}_{r}}=\\
={\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}U_{a+1}^{\prime}U_{r-2}}{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}+U_{a+1}U_{r-2}^{\prime}}{\color[rgb]{0,0.88,0}+\frac{1}{t}U_{a+1}^{\prime\prime}}{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}+\frac{U_{a+1}U_{r-2}V_{r}^{\prime}}{r}}{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}+\frac{U_{a+1}^{\prime}V_{r}^{\prime}}{tr}}\,.

The green terms can be rewritten as

\ddot{U}_{a+1}-\frac{a}{t}\dot{U}_{a+1}-\frac{1}{t}U_{a+1}^{\prime\prime}=U_{a-1}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{a}{t}U_{a}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{t}U_{a+1}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{d}{dw}\left(U_{a-1}^{\prime}+\frac{a}{t}U_{a}-\frac{1}{t}U_{a+1}^{\prime}\right).

We show that the sum of terms to be derived simplifies:

\begin{split}U_{a-1}^{\prime}+&\frac{a}{t}U_{a}-\frac{1}{t}U_{a+1}^{\prime}=\\
&=\frac{1}{t\,\Delta}\left(wU_{a+1}-(a+1)V_{a+1}+a\Delta U_{a}-wtU_{a-1}+(a-1)tV_{a-1}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{t\,\Delta}\Big[-a\big(\underbrace{wV_{a}-2tV_{a-1}-\Delta U_{a}}_{=0}\big)+w\big(\underbrace{wU_{a}-V_{a}-2tU_{a-1}}_{=0}\big)\Big]=0\,.\end{split}

In the first equality, we use
lemma

B.1
; in the second one, the recursive definition of Lucas sequences; in the third one, the conversion formulae of
table

1
. To conclude, the green terms simplify. For the purple terms, we notice that

U_{r-2}+\frac{V_{r}^{\prime}}{tr}=U_{r-2}+\frac{U_{r}}{t}=\frac{w}{t}U_{r-1}\,,

using
lemma

B.1
and the recurrence relation
B.1
. On the other hand,

\begin{split}2\dot{U}_{a+1}-\frac{a}{t}U_{a+1}&=-2\frac{aV_{a}-wU_{a}}{\Delta}-\frac{a}{t}U_{a+1}\\
&=-\frac{w}{t}\frac{(V_{a+1}-wU_{a+1}+aV_{a+1})}{\Delta}\\
&=-\frac{w}{t}\frac{(a+1)V_{a+1}-wU_{a+1}}{\Delta}=-\frac{w}{t}U_{a+1}^{\prime}\,.\end{split}

In the second equality, we used the conversion formulae of
table

1
. Hence, the purple terms simplify. The red terms simplify using
lemma

B.1
:

U_{a+1}\left(\frac{\ddot{V}_{r}}{r}-U_{r-2}^{\prime}\right)=U_{a+1}\bigl(-\dot{U}_{r-1}+\dot{U}_{r-1}\bigr)=0\,.

Similarly, the blue expression simplifies as

\frac{(\dot{V}_{r})^{2}}{r^{2}}-t^{r-2}-\frac{U_{r-2}V_{r}^{\prime}}{r}=U_{r-1}^{2}-t^{r-2}-U_{r-2}U_{r}=0\,.

In the last equality, we used the quadratic formulae of
table

1
. This completes the proof of the above relation between Lucas sequences, hence it proves the proposition.
∎

In the following lemma, we prove a useful relation among the Lucas sequences.

Lemma B.3
.

For any
k\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}
, we have the following identity

(B.5)

\frac{U_{r}(w,t)}{w-2\sqrt{t}\cos\left(\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)}=\sum_{p=0}^{r-2}(-1)^{k+1}t^{\frac{r-p-2}{2}}\frac{\sin\bigl(\frac{(p+1)k\pi}{r}\bigr)}{\sin\left(\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)}U_{p+1}(w,t)\,.

Proof.

First, we use the relation between the Lucas sequences and the Chebyshev polynomials (
B.2
) to write

\frac{U_{r}(w,t)}{w-2\sqrt{t}\cos\left(\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)}=\frac{t^{\frac{r-2}{2}}}{2}\left(\frac{\mathcal{U}_{r-1}(\tfrac{w}{2\sqrt{t}})}{\frac{w}{2\sqrt{t}}-\cos\left(\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)}\right).

As
z=\cos\left(\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)
for any
k\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}
is a root of
U_{r-1}(z)=0
, we have the following expansion

\frac{\mathcal{U}_{r-1}(z)}{z-\cos\left(\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)}=\sum_{p=0}^{r-2}c_{p}\mathcal{U}_{p}(z)\,,

and we would like to determine
c_{p}
. In the following, we will use various properties of Chebyshev polynomials, and refer to
[
DLMF
]
. This can be achieved using the following orthogonality property for the Chebyshev polynomials:

\int_{-1}^{1}\mathcal{U}_{n}(z)\mathcal{U}_{m}(z)\sqrt{1-z^{2}}dz=\frac{\pi}{2}\delta_{n,m}\,.

Solving for
c_{p}
, we find

\begin{split}c_{p}&=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{z-\cos\left(\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)}\mathcal{U}_{r-1}(z)\,\mathcal{U}_{p}(z)\sqrt{1-z^{2}}dz\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{z-\cos\left(\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)}\mathcal{U}_{r-1-p+2j}(z)\sqrt{1-z^{2}}dz\,,\end{split}

where we used the property
\mathcal{U}_{q}(z)\,\mathcal{U}_{p}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\,\mathcal{U}_{q-p+2j}(z)
for
q\geq p
. The integral on the last line can be solved using the formula

\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\sqrt{1-z^{2}}\,\mathcal{U}_{n-1}(z)}{z-y}dz=-\pi\,\mathcal{T}_{n}(y)\,,

which gives

c_{p}=-2\sum_{j=0}^{p}\mathcal{T}_{r-p+2j}\left(\cos\left(\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)\right)=-2\sum_{j=0}^{p}\cos\left(\frac{(r-p+2j)k\pi}{r}\right).

By rewriting the
\cos
functions as exponentials, and using the formula for a geometric series, we can simplify the
c_{p}
to

c_{p}=(-1)^{k+1}2\frac{\sin\bigl(\frac{(p+1)k\pi}{r}\bigr)}{\sin\left(\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)}\,.

By using the equation for
c_{p}
, we finally get

\begin{split}\frac{U_{r}(w,t)}{w-2\sqrt{t}\cos\left(\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)}&=\frac{t^{\frac{r-2}{2}}}{2}\sum_{p=0}^{r-2}(-1)^{k+1}2\frac{\sin\bigl(\frac{(p+1)k\pi}{r}\bigr)}{\sin\left(\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)}\mathcal{U}_{p}\left(\frac{w}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)\\
&=(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{p=0}^{r-2}t^{\frac{r-p-2}{2}}\frac{\sin\bigl(\frac{(p+1)k\pi}{r}\bigr)}{\sin\left(\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)}U_{p+1}\left(w,t\right)\,,\end{split}

where we rewrote the Chebyshev
\mathcal{U}_{p}
polynomials in terms of the Lucas sequences using
equation

B.2
. Thus, we get the statement of the lemma.
∎

Another useful relation is the following.

Lemma B.4
.

We have the following identity

(B.6)

\widehat{\mathrm{Ai}}_{r,0}^{j}(t)=-\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{(-1)^{j}}}\int_{\hat{C}_{j}}wU_{r}(w,t)e^{\frac{1}{r}V_{r}(w,t)}dw\,.

Proof.

As

\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\left(we^{\frac{1}{r}V_{r}(w,t)}\right)=\bigl(1+wU_{r}(w,t)\bigr)e^{\frac{1}{r}V_{r}(w,t)}\,,

we obtain that the integral vanishes:

\int_{\hat{C}_{j}}\bigl(1+wU_{r}(w,t)\bigr)e^{\frac{1}{r}V_{r}(w,t)}dw=0\,,

due to the definition of the Lefschetz thimble
\hat{C}_{j}
. Noting that
U_{1}(w,t)=1
gives the statement of the lemma.
∎

On explicit form of the Kolmogorov constant in the theory of Galton-Watson Branching Processes


Abstract
The paper considers the well-known Galton-Watson stochastic branching process. We are dealing with a non-critical case. In the subcritical case, when the mean of the direct descendants of one particle per generation of the time step is less than 1 1 , the population mean of the number of particles on the positive trajectories of the process stabilizes and approaches 1 / 𝒦 {1}\big/\mathcal{K} , where 𝒦 \mathcal{K} is the so-called Kolmogorov constant. The paper is devoted to the search for an explicit expression of this constant depending on the structural parameters of the process. Our reasoning is essentially based on the Basic Lemma, which describes the asymptotic expansion of the generating function of the distribution of the number of particles. An important role is also played by the asymptotic properties of the transition probabilities of the so-called Q-process and their property convergence to invariant measures. Keywords : Branching process; transition probabilities; Markov chain; Kolmogorov constant; invariant measures; Q-process; Basic Lemma. 2010 MSC: 60J80, 60J85

1
Introduction and main result

The Galton-Watson Branching Process (GWP) is a well-known classical model of population growth.
This process describes the evolution of the population size in a system of monotype particles capable
of death and transformation into a random number of particles of the same type. Although GWP has been
well studied, it seems useful to discuss and clarify the well-known classical facts of GWP theory
in more detail. In this report, we are dealing with a well-known theorem related
to the name of A.Kolmogorov
[
7
]
.

Let the random function
Z(n)
denote the successive population progeny in GWP at the moment

n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, where
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:=\{0\}\cup\left\{{{\mathbb{N}}:=1,2,\ldots}\right\}
.
The sequence of states
\left\{Z(n),n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\right\}
can be expressed as the
following random sum of random variables:

Z(n+1)=\xi_{1}(n)+\xi_{2}(n)+\cdots+\xi_{Z(n)}(n),

where
\xi_{k}(n)
,
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
are independent and identically distributed random variables with the
common offspring law
\textsf{P}\left\{{\xi_{1}(1)=k}\right\}=p_{k}
. They are interpreted as the number of descendants
of the
k
th particle in
n
th generation; see
[
10
, pp. 11–14]
. By virtue of our assumption, the stochastic
system
\left\{Z(n)\right\}
forms a reducible, homogeneous and discrete-time Markov chain with the state space consisting
of two classes:
\mathcal{S}_{0}=\left\{0\right\}\cup{\mathcal{S}}
, where
\mathcal{S}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
, therein
the state
\left\{0\right\}
is an absorbing state, and
\mathcal{S}
is the class of possible essential communicating
states. Its transition probabilities

P_{ij}:=\textsf{P}\bigl\{{Z({n+1})=j\bigm|{Z(n)=i}}\bigr\}=\sum_{k_{1}+\,\cdots\,+k_{i}=j}{p_{k_{1}}p_{k_{2}}\,\ldots\,p_{k_{i}}}

(1.1)

for any
i,j\in\mathcal{S}
, where
p_{j}=P_{1j}
and
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}}{p_{j}}=1
. This means that our GWP is
completely defined by specifying the offspring law
\left\{{p_{k},k\in\mathcal{S}}\right\}
. Conversely, any
chain that satisfies the property (
1.1
) is a GWP with the offspring law
\left\{{p_{k}}\right\}
;
see
[
1
, pp.1–2]
,
[
6
, p.19]
. To avoid trivial cases, in what follows we
assume that
p_{k}\neq 1
and
p_{0}>0
,
p_{0}+p_{1}<1
.

Considering the transition probabilities for
n
steps

P_{ij}(n):=\textsf{P}_{i}\bigl\{{Z(n)=j}\bigr\}=\textsf{P}\bigl\{{Z({n+k})=j\bigm|{Z(k)=i}}\bigr\}\quad\text{for}\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

in conformity with (
1.1
), we find an appropriate probability generating function (GF)

\textsf{E}_{i}s^{Z(n)}:=\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}{P_{ij}(n)s^{j}}=\bigl[{f_{n}(s)}\bigr]^{i},

(1.2)

for
s\in[0,1)
, where the GF
f_{n}(s)=\textsf{E}_{1}s^{Z(n)}
is
n
-fold iteration of GF

f(s):=\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{S}}}{p_{k}s^{k}},

i.e.
f_{n+1}(s)=f\left(f_{n}(s)\right)=f_{n}\left(f(s)\right)
; see.
[
2
, pp. 5–6]
.

Let the series
m:=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{S}}{kp_{k}}
converge. Then
m=f^{\prime}(1-)
is the average of the direct
descendants of one particle over one generation of the time step. Using the formula (
1.2
), in
particular, one can find
\textsf{E}_{1}{Z(n)}=m^{n}
. In accordance with this, three classes of GWP
are distinguished depending on the value of the parameter
m
. The process
\left\{Z(n)\right\}
is
called subcritical if
m<1
, critical if
m=1
, and supercritical if
m>1
respectively.
It is known that the sequence of vanishing probabilities
\bigl\{P_{10}(n)\bigr\}
of one particle at time
n

for all classes tends monotonously to the extinction probability of the process starting from one particle which
we will designate
q:=\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{10}(n)
. For subcritical and critical processes
q=1
,
while in supercritical case (see.
[
11
]
)

q=\inf\bigl\{s\in(0,1]:\,s=f(s)\bigr\}.

In what follows, we will consider only the non-critical case, i.e.,
m\neq 1
, and wherever necessary,
we will write
E
and
P
instead of
\textsf{E}_{1}
and
\textsf{P}_{1}
respectively.
In the case under consideration
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(s)=q
for all
s\in[0,1)
, and this convergence
is uniform in
s\in[0,r]
for any fixed
r<1
; see
[
10
, p. 53]
.

Let us introduce the function
R_{n}(s):=q-f_{n}(s)
.

In 1938 A. Kolmogorov
[
7
]
established that the survival probability

Q(n):=\textsf{P}\bigl\{{Z(n)>0}\bigr\}=R_{n}(0)
of subcritical
process admits the asymptotic representation

Q(n)=\mathcal{K}m^{n}\bigl({1+o(1)}\bigr)\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty,

(1.3)

if and only if
f^{\prime\prime}(1-)<\infty
, where
\mathcal{K}
– a finite positive constant, called the Kolmogorov
constant. Later, A. Nagaev and I. Badalbaev
[
8
]
improved Kolmogorov’s result by proving
the validity of the asymptotic representation (
1.3
) under a lot more weaker condition

\textsf{E}Z(1)\ln^{+}{Z(1)}=\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{S}}}{p_{k}k\ln{k}}<\infty.

[\textsf{x}\ln{\textsf{x}}]

It follows from the representation (
1.3
), and also noted by V.Vatutin
[
11
]
,
on positive trajectories of the process, the average number of particles population stabilizes with
increasing generations number and approaches
{1}\large/\mathcal{K}
. Indeed, (
1.3
) implies that

{\frac{m^{n}}{Q(n)}}={\frac{\textsf{E}Z(n)}{\textsf{P}\bigl\{{Z(n)>0}\bigr\}}}=\textsf{E}\left[{{Z(n)}\bigm|{Z(n)>0}}\right]\approx{1}\big/\mathcal{K}\qquad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty\raise 0.8pt\hbox{.}

In addition to the above, due to the last relation, the constant
\mathcal{K}
can be interpreted as a coefficient
of asymptotic equivalence of the average
\textsf{E}{Z(n)}=m^{n}
of the population size to the survival probability
of the process
{\textsf{P}\bigl\{{Z(n)>0}\bigr\}}
. The absence of an explicit expression for this constant hinders
the completion of a number of limit theorems for subcritical processes. In particular, this was noted long ago in 1957
by V. Zolotarev
[
12
]
. Thus, it is of special interest to determine the explicit expression of this
equivalence coefficient as a function of the numerical parameters of the GWP.

Our aim in this report is to get the explicit form of
\mathcal{K}
. Partly certain result on this issue is already
available due to E.Seneta
[
9
, Theorem 2(1)]
in the sense that under the condition
[\textsf{x}\ln{\textsf{x}}]

this constant can be calculated using the limit parameter
\mu:=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}{k\mu_{k}}
, where

\mu_{k}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\textsf{P}\bigl\{{Z(n)=k\bigm|{Z(n)>0}}\bigr\}
is the limiting-invariant
distribution for subcritical GWP. Namely, this paper proves that

{\frac{m^{n}}{Q(n)}}\longrightarrow{\mu}\qquad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty,

i.e.
\mathcal{K}={1}\big/{\mu}
.
Consider now the random variable
{\mathcal{H}}:=\min\bigl\{{n:Z(n)=0}\bigr\}
, which denotes an extinction time of
the process
\bigl\{Z(n)\bigr\}
with initial state
\bigl\{Z(0)=1\bigr\}
. It is obvious that the parameter
\beta:=f^{\prime}(q)

can be interpreted as the mean of the direct descendants of one particle in the transformed
branching process
\bigl\{Z_{q}(n)\bigr\}
, generated by the Harris-Sevastyanov transformation
f_{q}(s)=f(qs)\big/q
. Note
also that the process
\bigl\{Z_{q}(n)\bigr\}
is subcritical. In this notation, the above result of E.Seneta can be
extended to the non-critical case in the following theorem; see, also,
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Theorem S.

Let
\textsf{P}\bigl\{Z(0)=1\bigr\}=1
and
m\neq{1}
. Then the coefficient
of asymptotic equivalence between the population mean in the process
\bigl\{Z_{q}(n)\bigr\}
and
the survival probability of the process
\bigl\{Z(n)\bigr\}
slowly stabilizes, i.e.

{\frac{\textsf{E}Z_{q}(n)}{\textsf{P}\bigl\{{n<{\mathcal{H}}<\infty}\bigr\}}}={\frac{\beta^{n}}{R_{n}(0)}}=\mathcal{L}_{\beta}\bigl({\beta^{n}}\bigr)\qquad\text{{as}}\quad n\to\infty,

where the function
\mathcal{L}_{\beta}\left(*\right)
slowly varies at infinity in the sense of Karamata.
If, in addition, the condition
[\textsf{x}\ln{\textsf{x}}]
holds, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\textsf{P}_{i}\bigl\{{Z_{n}=k\bigm|{n<{\mathcal{H}}<\infty}}\bigr\}=:\nu_{k}<\infty,

therewith
\left\{{\nu_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}}\right\}
is a limiting-invariant
distribution for the process
\left\{Z(n)\right\}
such that

\mathcal{K}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{L}_{\beta}\bigl({\beta^{n}}\bigr)={\frac{q}{\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}{k\nu_{k}}}}\raise 1.5pt\hbox{.}

Further discussions show that under the Kolmogorov conditions
[
7
]
, the constant
\mathcal{K}

can be explicitly calculated using the structural parameters (moments) of the process
\bigl\{Z(n)\bigr\}
.
Below we formulate the main result of the paper, in which the explicit form of
\mathcal{K}

is found depending on the second factorial moment
f^{\prime\prime}(1-)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
m\neq 1
and
2b_{q}:=f^{\prime\prime}(q)<\infty
. Then

\mathcal{K}={\frac{q}{\,1+q\gamma\,}}\,\raise 1.5pt\hbox{,}

where
\gamma={{b_{q}}\big/{\left(\beta-\beta^{2}\right)}}
.

Corollary 1
.

Let
m<1
. If
2b:=f^{\prime\prime}(1-)<\infty
, then

\mathcal{K}={\frac{1}{\,1+\gamma\,}}\,\raise 1.5pt\hbox{,}

where
\gamma={{b}\big/{\left(m-m^{2}\right)}}
.

Section
2
we devote to the proof of the Theorem
1
.

2
The proof of Theorem
1

We divide the proof of the theorem into several steps.

2.1
A defective but important Lemma

The mean value theorem gives

R_{n+1}(s)=f^{\prime}\bigl({\xi_{n}(s)}\bigr)R_{n}(s),

(2.1)

where
\xi_{n}(s)=q-\theta R_{n}(s)
and
0<\theta<1
. We see that if
s\in[0,q)
then
R_{n}(s)>0
, therefore

\xi_{n}(s)<q
. Since the GF
f(s)
and its derivatives are monotonically nondecreasing, successive application
of (
2.1
) leads to the inequality
R_{n}(s)<q\beta^{n}
. Thence

q-q\beta^{n}<\xi_{n}(s)<q\qquad\text{for}\quad s\in[0,q).

Accordingly

{\frac{R_{n+1}(s)}{\beta}}<R_{n}(s)<{\frac{R_{n+1}(s)}{f^{\prime}\bigl(q-q\beta^{n}\bigr)}}\raise 1.5pt\hbox{.}

On the other hand, for all
s\in[q,1)
we see
R_{n}(s)<0
, so that
\xi_{n}(s)=q+\theta\left|R_{n}(s)\right|>q
.
And in this case, successively applying the formula (
2.1
) and, taking into account the properties of

f(s)
, we obtain the inequality
(q-1)\beta^{n}<R_{n}(s)
or the same as
\left|R_{n}(s)\right|<(1-q)\beta^{n}
. Hence

q<\xi_{n}(s)<q+(1-q)\beta^{n}\qquad\text{for}\quad s\in[q,1).

Then

{\frac{R_{n+1}(s)}{f^{\prime}\bigl(q+(1-q)\beta^{n}\bigr)}}<R_{n}(s)<{\frac{R_{n+1}(s)}{\beta}}\raise 1.5pt\hbox{.}

Based on recent results, we conclude that

{\frac{R_{n+1}(s)}{f^{\prime}\bigl(q_{1}(n)\bigr)}}<R_{n}(s)<{\frac{R_{n+1}(s)}{f^{\prime}\bigl(q_{0}(n)\bigr)}}\qquad\text{for all}\quad s\in[0,1),

(2.2)

where

q_{k}(n):=q+(k-q)\beta^{n}\qquad\text{for}\quad k=0,\,1.

In turn, by the Taylor formula and by iterating over
f(s)
we have the following relation:

R_{n+1}(s)=\beta R_{n}(s)-{\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\bigl(\zeta_{n}(s)\bigr)}{2}}R_{n}^{2}(s)

(2.3)

for all all
s\in[0,1)
, herein
\zeta_{n}(s)
is so that

q_{0}(n)<\zeta_{n}(s)<q_{1}(n)\qquad\text{for all}\quad s\in[0,1).

Accordingly, by the monotone non-decreasing property of GF we obtain

f^{\prime\prime}\bigl(q_{0}(n)\bigr)<f^{\prime\prime}\bigl(\zeta_{n}(s)\bigr)<f^{\prime\prime}\bigl(q_{1}(n)\bigr).

(2.4)

Combining (
2.2
)–(
2.4
) implies

{\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\bigl(q_{0}(n)\bigr)}{2f^{\prime}\bigl(q_{1}(n)\bigr)}}{R_{n}(s)R_{n+1}(s)}<\beta R_{n}(s)-R_{n+1}(s)<{\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\bigl(q_{1}(n)\bigr)}{2f^{\prime}\bigl(q_{0}(n)\bigr)}}{R_{n}(s)R_{n+1}(s)}.

Multiplying these inequalities to
1\big/\bigl({R_{n}(s)R_{n+1}(s)}\bigr)
gives us

{\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\bigl(q_{0}(n)\bigr)}{2f^{\prime}\bigl(q_{1}(n)\bigr)}}<{\frac{\beta}{R_{n+1}(s)}}-{\frac{1}{R_{n}(s)}}<{\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\bigl(q_{1}(n)\bigr)}{2f^{\prime}\bigl(q_{0}(n)\bigr)}}\raise 1.5pt\hbox{.}

(2.5)

Repeated application inequalities (
2.5
) leads to the following ones:

{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\bigl(q_{0}(k)\bigr)}{f^{\prime}\bigl(q_{1}(k)\bigr)}{\beta^{k}}}<{\frac{\beta^{n}}{R_{n}(s)}}-{\frac{1}{1-s}}<{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\bigl(q_{1}(k)\bigr)}{f^{\prime}\bigl(q_{0}(k)\bigr)}{\beta^{k}}}\raise 1.5pt\hbox{.}

Taking limit as
n\to\infty
from here we have estimation

{\frac{\Delta_{1}}{2}}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[{\frac{\beta^{n}}{R_{n}(s)}}-{\frac{1}{1-s}}\right]\leq{\frac{\Delta_{2}}{2}}\raise 1.5pt\hbox{,}

(2.6)

where

\Delta_{1}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\bigl(q_{0}(k)\bigr)}{f^{\prime}\bigl(q_{1}(k)\bigr)}{\beta^{k}}}\qquad{\text{and}}\qquad\Delta_{2}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\bigl(q_{1}(k)\bigr)}{f^{\prime}\bigl(q_{0}(k)\bigr)}{\beta^{k}}}.

Evidently, last two series converge because of

0<q_{0}(n)<q<q_{1}(n)<1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Designating

{\frac{1}{A_{1}(s)}}:={\frac{1}{q-s}}+{\frac{\Delta_{1}}{2}}\qquad{\text{and}}\qquad{\frac{1}{A_{2}(s)}}:={\frac{1}{q-s}}+{\frac{\Delta_{2}}{2}}\raise 1.5pt\hbox{,}

we rewrite the relation (
2.6
) as follows:

{\frac{1}{A_{1}(s)}}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}{\frac{\beta^{n}}{R_{n}(s)}}\leq{\frac{1}{A_{2}(s)}}\raise 1.5pt\hbox{.}

(2.7)

Clearly

{\frac{1}{A_{2}(s)}}-{\frac{1}{A_{1}(s)}}={\frac{\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{1}}{2}}<\infty.

In turn, we see that
{{\beta^{n}}\big/{R_{n}(s)}}
monotonously increases for all
s\in[0,q)

as
n\to\infty
and monotonously increases and decrease in kind for all
s\in[q,1)
. Therefore

{\mathcal{A}_{\delta}(s)}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}{{\beta^{n}}\big/{R_{n}(s)}}
exists and in accordance with
inequalities (
2.7
) there is a positive variable
\delta\in\left[\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\right]

such that

{\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{\delta}(s)}}:={\frac{1}{q-s}}+{\frac{\,\delta\,}{2}}\raise 1.5pt\hbox{.}

(2.8)

So we established the following statement.

Lemma 1
.

If
m\neq{1}
and
f^{\prime\prime}(q)<\infty
, then

{\frac{R_{n}(s)}{\beta^{n}}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{A}_{\delta}(s)}\qquad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty,

(2.9)

where the function
{\mathcal{A}_{\delta}}(s)
is defined in (
2.8
).

The core and only defect of Lemma
1
is the lack of an explicit
\delta
expression
in (
2.8
). In Section
2.2
we will eliminate this defect.

2.2
The Q-process contribution

We begin by recalling the so-called Q-process which is an irreducible homogeneous-discrete-time Markov
chain
\bigl\{W(n)\bigr\}
ith the state space
\mathcal{E}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
. The transition probabilities
of Q-process are

{\mathcal{Q}}_{ij}(n):=\textsf{P}\left\{{W({n+k})=j}\bigm|{W(k)=i}\right\}={\frac{jq^{j-i}}{i\beta^{n}}}P_{ij}(n)\qquad\text{for all}\quad i,j\in{\mathcal{E}},

(2.10)

and for any
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
; see.
[
1
, Sec. I, §14]
.
Put into consideration a GF

w_{n}^{(i)}(s):=\sum\limits_{j\in{\mathcal{E}}}{{\mathcal{Q}}_{ij}(n)s^{j}}.

Then from (
1.2
) and (
2.10
) we have

\displaystyle w_{n}^{(i)}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\in{\mathcal{E}}}{{\frac{jq^{j-i}}{i\beta^{n}}}P_{ij}(n)s^{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{q^{1-i}s}{i\beta^{n}}}\sum\limits_{j\in{\mathcal{E}}}{P_{ij}(n)(qs)^{j-1}}={\frac{qs}{i\beta^{n}}}{\frac{\partial}{\partial{x}}}\left[{\left({{\frac{f_{n}(x)}{q}}}\right)^{i}}\right]_{x=qs}.

Last formula is convenient for using in a following form:

w_{n}^{(i)}(s)=\left[{{\frac{f_{n}(qs)}{q}}}\right]^{i-1}w_{n}(s),

(2.11)

where the GF
w_{n}(s):=w_{n}^{(1)}(s)=\textsf{E}\left[{s^{W(n)}\bigm|{W(0)=1}}\right]
has a form of:

w_{n}(s)=s{\frac{f^{\prime}_{n}(qs)}{\beta^{n}}}\qquad\text{for all}\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(2.12)

Since
f_{n}(s)\to q
uniformly in
s\in[0,r]
for any fixed
r<1
as
{n\to\infty}
, it follows from
(
2.11
) and (
2.12
) that
{{\mathcal{Q}}_{ij}(n)}\big/{{\mathcal{Q}}_{1j}(n)}\to 1

as infinitely growth the number of generations.

Application of iteration for
f(s)
in the relation (
2.11
) leads us to the following functional equation:

w_{n+1}^{(i)}(s)={\frac{w(s)}{f_{q}(s)}}w_{n}^{(i)}\bigl({f_{q}(s)}\bigr),

(2.13)

where
w(s):=w_{1}(s)
and
f_{q}(s)={f(qs)\big/q}
. Thus, Q-process is completely defined by setting the GF

w(s)=s{\frac{f^{\prime}(qs)}{\beta}}\raise 1.5pt\hbox{.}

(2.14)

An evolution of the Q-process is in essentially regulated by the structural parameter
\beta>0
. In fact,
as it has been shown in
[
1
, p. 59, Theorem 2]
, that if
\beta<1
then
\mathcal{E}
is positive
recurrent and,
\mathcal{E}
is transient if
\beta=1
. On the other hand, it is easy to be convinced
that positive recurrent case
\beta<1
of Q-process corresponds to the non-critical case

of GWP. Note that
\beta\leq{1}
and nothing but.

Assume that
\alpha:=w^{\prime}(1-)<\infty
in the case
\beta<1
. Then differentiating (
2.14
)
on the point
s=1
we obtain
\alpha=1+\left({1-\beta}\right)\gamma_{q}
, where

\gamma_{q}:={\frac{qf^{\prime\prime}(q)}{\left({\beta-\beta^{2}}\right)}}\raise 1.5pt\hbox{.}

Further, it follows from (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) that

{\textsf{E}}_{i}W(n)=\left({i-1}\right)\beta^{n}+\textsf{E}W(n)
, where

\textsf{E}W(n)=1+\gamma_{q}\cdot\bigl({1-\beta^{n}}\bigr).

It is known
[

, p. 59, Theorem 2(iv)]
that in this case there exists an invariant measure

\bigl\{\pi_{j}\bigr\}
with respect to the probabilities
{{\mathcal{Q}}_{ij}(n)}
such that

\pi_{j}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{Q}}_{ij}(n)=jq^{j-1}\nu_{j}\qquad\text{for all}\quad i,j\in{\mathcal{E}},

(2.15)

where
\{\nu_{j}\}
are coefficients in a power series expansion of the limit GF

see
[
1
, p. 41, Theorem 3]
.
In conformity with our designation and by Lemma
1
we see
Q(s)=-\mathcal{A}_{\delta}(s)
.
Then
\mathcal{A}_{\delta}(s)=-\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{E}}}{\nu_{j}s^{j}}
. Thus, interpreting
the statement (
2.15
) in the context of GF, we conclude that there exists
a limit GF
\pi(s):=\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{E}}}{\pi_{j}s^{j}}
such that

\pi(s)=\lim_{n\to\infty}w_{n}^{(i)}(s)=-s\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\delta}(qs).

(2.16)

for all
s\in[0,1)
. On the other hand, taking limit as
n\to\infty
in equation (
2.13
) with a combination of
equations (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), leads us to the following Schröder type functional equation:

\pi(s)={\frac{w(s)}{f_{q}(s)}}\pi\bigl({f_{q}(s)}\bigr).

(2.17)

The equation (
2.17
) entails

\pi_{j}=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{E}}}{\pi_{i}{\mathcal{Q}}_{ij}(n)}
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
j\in{\mathcal{E}}
.

Now, due to the form of (
2.8
) and from the relation (
2.16
) immediately follows, that

\pi(1)=1
. The last argument is equivalent to that
\bigl\{\pi_{j}\bigr\}
represents an invariant
distribution. Simultaneously differentiating the equation (

) and taking

we obtain
the mean of distribution
\bigl\{\pi_{j}\bigr\}
as

\pi^{\prime}(1-)=1+\gamma_{q}.

At the same time, the relation (

) implies that
\pi^{\prime}(1-)=1+q\delta
. Hence

\delta={\frac{\gamma_{q}}{\,q\,}}={\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(q)}{\beta\bigl(1-\beta\bigr)}}\raise 1.5pt\hbox{.}

(2.18)

2.3
Basic Lemma

From the representation (
2.8
) and the equality (
2.18
) one can finally obtain an explicit expression for
the limit function

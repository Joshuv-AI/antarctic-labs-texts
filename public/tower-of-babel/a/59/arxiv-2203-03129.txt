When referring to a collection as having

Let

A finite collection

A continuum is

Before we give a geometric construction defining

9

n_{i_{j}}-l+1

For each

be a point in

whose depth is

as

as

. Also for each

and

in

is also

.

It follows, as a consequence of Lemma
1.9
on the collection of all

, that there exists a positive integer

in

whose endpoints are

and

as

. However, since the depth of

in

Assume now that case (2.) occurs for all but finitely many

and, without loss of generality, for every

. Let

be similarly defined as in the proof negating case (1.) above. Since the depth of

For each

is greater than or equal to

as

is a homeomorphism.
∎

Lemma 3.24
.

All but finitely many pairs
h(x_{i})
and
h(x_{j})
, where
i\neq j
, are contained in different schema pockets of the complement of
Y
.

Proof.

Suppose there exists a subsequences
(x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},x_{i_{3}},\ldots)
and a subsequence
(x_{j_{1}},x_{j_{2}},x_{j_{3}},\ldots)
such that

for every

and so that

and

are contained in the same schema pocket of the complement of

. Again, as in the previous proofs, there exists an
M(i)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for every
m\geq M(i)
,
\{h(x_{i_{m}}),h(x_{i_{m}}^{(1)}),\ldots,h(x_{i_{m}}^{(n_{i_{m}})})\}\subset U\cup W
, the members of this set alternating between
U
and
W
. Similarly, there exists an

such that for every

,
\{h(x_{j_{m}}),h(x_{j_{m}}^{(1)}),\ldots,h(x_{j_{m}}^{(n_{j_{m}})})\}\subset U\cup W
, the members of this set alternating between
U
and
W
. Furthermore, for each
m\geq M(i)
, the difference between the depths of

and

is

if and only if

, with the depth of every

being that of
h(x_{i_{m}}^{(l-1)})
, and for each
m\geq M(j)
, the difference between the depths of
h(x_{j_{m}}^{(l)})
and

is

if and only if
|l-l^{\prime}|=1

h(J_{j_{m}}^{(l)})
is being that of

Let

. By Lemma
3.21
and Lemma
3.23
, the depth of

and

are the same as the depths of

and

in

and

Lemma 3.25
.

\mathbf{N}
and
\mathbf{M}
are equivalent sequences of positive integers.

Proof.

By Lemmas
3.21
and
3.23
, there exists a positive integer

such that for every

,

is contained in a schema pocket

of the complement of

is that of

3.24
, with

1.4

such that for every

is that of

can also satisfy
s_{i}\neq s_{k}

3.24
, with

.

Let us assume that

. By the lemmas listed in the previous paragraph, it follows that for every

Theorem 3.26
.

If

4.
Closing Comments and Open Questions

The question of which HDCC admit uncountably many mutually inequivalent planar embeddings is still an open problem. The previously described techniques for constructing
\mathfrak{c}
-many mutually inequivalent planar embeddings for
K
may provide insight into how this more general problem can be solved. However, since any given HDCC,
X
, may possess a highly complex underlying structure on its generalized layers, producing planar embeddings of
X
using similar such techniques can prove difficult in controlling the details of their constructions.

Question 1.
Can the techniques of producing
\mathfrak{c}
-many mutually inequivalent planar embeddings of
K
as in Section 2 and Section 3 be generalized to all non-arc HDCC? If not for all non-arc HDCC, can they be generalized to those whose layer level is finite?

We may also be able to provide a partial answer to the more general question above by exploring HDCC which yield a decomposition similar to that of
K=K_{1}\cup K_{2}\cup\cdots\cup E
as in Section 3. In particular, suppose
X
is an HDCC with left end layer
E_{X}
. Furthermore, suppose
X
can be decomposed as
X=X_{1}\cup X_{2}\cup\cdots\cup E_{X}
, where
X_{i}\cap E_{X}=\emptyset
for each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and so that
X_{i}\cap X_{j}
is a subcontinuum
C_{i}
of
X
if and only if
|i-j|\leq 1
, with
{\textnormal{diam}}(C_{i})\to 0
as
i\to\infty
. We thus pose the following question.

Question 2.
Can any non-arc HDCC possessing a decomposition as described in the previous paragraph be embedded in the plane in
\mathfrak{c}
-many mutually inequivalent ways? If so, can such embeddings be constructed according to sequences of positive even integers greater than or equal to 4 as was done for
K
in section 3?

Is there a planar embedding

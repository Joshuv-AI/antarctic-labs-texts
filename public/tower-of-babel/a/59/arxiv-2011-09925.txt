Introduction

The polygonal domain comprises a set of pairwise-disjoint simple polygons (obstacles) in the plane.
We assume the obstacles in the polygonal domain are placed in a large bounding box.
For any polygonal domain
\cal P
, the
free space

\mathcal{F(P)}
is the closure of bounding box without the union of the interior of all the obstacles in
\cal{P}
.
Any two points
p,q\in{\cal{F(P)}}
are
visible
to each other if the open line segment joining
p
and
q
lies entirely in
\cal{F(P)}
.
A vertex
v
of the polygonal domain is said to be a
visible vertex
to a point
q
whenever
v
is visible to
q
.
For a point
q\in{\cal{F(P)}}
, the
visibility polygon
of
q
, denoted by
VP_{\cal{P}}(q)
, is the maximal set
S
of points in
\cal{F(P)}
, such that each point in
S
is visible to
q
.
(When
\cal P
is clear from the context, the visibility polygon of
q
is denoted by
VP(q)
.)
The
visibility polygon query
problem seeks to preprocess the given polygonal domain
\cal P
so that to efficiently compute
VP_{\cal P}(q)
for any query point
q
located in
\cal{F(P)}
.
Computing visibility polygons is a fundamental problem in computational geometry, and it is studied extensively.
The
(vertex-vertex) visibility graph
of a polygonal domain
\cal P
, denoted by
VG_{\cal P}
, is the undirected graph with its vertex set comprising all the vertices of
\cal P
.
The edge set of
VG_{\cal P}
comprises of every line segement with its endpoints
v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}
being the vertices of
\cal P
such that
v^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime\prime}
are visible to each other among the obstacles of
\cal P^{\prime}
.
The visibility graphs have many applications, ex., in computing geodesic shortest paths, minimum link paths, and in computing the weak visibility polygons.
In the following, we denote the number of vertices of the simple polygon or the polygonal domain by
n
, the number of obstacles in the polygonal domain by
h
, and the set comprising the edges of the visibility graph by
E
.

1.1
Our results

In this paper, two algorithms are proposed.
After preprocessing to build a few data structures, the first algorithm updates data structures that store the visibility polygon of any query point
q
, and the second algorithm updates the data structures that store the visibility graph of the current polygonal domain, both among a set of dynamic polygonal obstacles.
These algorithms update relevant data structures whenever any vertex is inserted to any of the obstacles in the current polygonal domain or whenever any vertex is deleted from any of the obstacles.

•

Let
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
be the visibility polygon of a query point
q
located in the free space of the current polygonal domain
\cal P^{\prime}
.
Also, let
n^{\prime}
be the number of vertices of
\cal P^{\prime}
.
When any vertex
v
is inserted to (resp. deleted from) any of the obstacles of
\cal P^{\prime}
, our algorithm takes
O(k(\lg{|VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)|})+(\lg{n^{\prime}})^{2}+h)
(resp.
O(k(\lg n^{\prime})^{2}+(\lg|VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)|)+h)
) time to update data structures that store
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
.
Here,
k
is the number of combinatorial changes in
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
due to the insertion (resp. deletion) of
v
.
(The combinatorial changes to
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
include vertices inserted, vertices deleted, edges inserted, and edges deleted from
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
.)
Given any query point
q
located in the free space of the current polygonal domain
\cal P^{\prime}
, our output-sensitive visibility polygon query algorithm computes
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
in
O(|VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)|(\lg{n^{\prime}})^{2}+h)
time.
The data structures constructed as part of answering the visibility polygon query algorithm facilitate efficient updations as the polygonal domain changes.
We preprocess the initial input polygonal domain
\cal P
in
O(n(\lg{n})^{2}+h(\lg{h})^{1+\epsilon})
time, and construct data structures of size
O(n)
.
Here,
n
is the number of vertices of
\cal P
,
h
is the number of polygonal obstacles in
\cal P
, and
\epsilon>0
is a small positive constant (resulting from triangulating the free space of
\cal P
using the algorithm in
[
5
]
).

•

Let
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
be the visibility graph of the current polygonal domain
\cal P^{\prime}
.
Also, let
n^{\prime}
be the number of vertices in
\cal P^{\prime}
.
When any vertex
v
is inserted to (resp. deleted from) any of the obstacles of
\cal P^{\prime}
, our algorithm takes
O(k(\lg{n^{\prime}})^{2}+h)
(resp.
O(k((\lg n^{\prime})^{2}+h))
) time to update the visibility graph of
\cal P^{\prime}
.
Here,
k
is the number of combinatorial changes in
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
due to the insertion of
v
.
(The combinatorial changes in
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
include vertices inserted, vertices deleted, edges inserted, and edges deleted from
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
.)
We preprocess the initial input polygonal domain
\cal P
in
O(n(\lg{n})^{2}+h(\lg{h})^{1+\epsilon}+|E|\lg{|E|})
time, and construct data structures of size
O(n+|E|)
.
Here,
n
is the number of vertices of
\cal P
,
h
is the number of polygonal obstacles in
\cal P
,
E
is the set of edges in the visibility graph of
\cal P
, and
\epsilon>0
is a small positive constant (resulting from triangulating the free space of
\cal P
using the algorithm in
[
5
]
).

For convenience, we assume all through the algorithm, the number of polygonal obstacles does not change, and the obstacles are pairwise disjoint.
To our knowledge, these are the first algorithms for maintaining the visibility polygon of any given point and the visibility graph of the polygonal domain among the dynamic polygonal obstacles.
These algorithms obviate computing the visibility polygon of a given point or the visibility graph from scratch whenever the polygonal domain is modified with a vertex insertion or vertex deletion.

Next, we give an overview of our approach.
For any given query point
q
, we compute two binary trees (called visibility trees) to store the visibility polygon of
q
.
These visibility tree data structures in our algorithm are a modification to visibility trees defined in
[
29
]
.
From these trees, we compute the visibility polygon
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
of
q
in the current polygonal domain
\cal P^{\prime}
.
Whenever a new vertex
v
is inserted to an obstacle of the current polygonal domain
\cal P^{\prime}
, first, we perform a ray-shooting query to determine whether
v
is visible to
q
.
If it is not visible, then our algorithm does nothing further.
Otherwise, we traverse the visibility trees in depth-first order to determine all the vertices that are not visible to
q
due to the insertion of
v
.
We update the
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
by removing these vertices from
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
.
As part of this, we also update both the visibility trees.
Similarly, in the case of deletion of a vertex
v
, by performing a ray-shooting query, it is determined whether deleted vertex
v
is visible to
q
.
If the deleted vertex
v
was visible to query point
q
, then the deletion of
v
may cause the addition of some new vertices to
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
.
These new vertices are determined using our output-sensitive visibility polygon query algorithm with
q
as the query point.
Updating
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
involves merging a set of polygons due to these vertices into data structures that store
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
.
Our visibility polygon query algorithm enhances data structures designed in
[
23
]
, so that they work efficiently among dynamic polygonal obstacles.
As in
[
23
]
, our visibility polygon query algorithm computes visibility trees of a query point
q
by determining sequences’ of traingles bounding rays of visibility cones initiated at
q
intersect.
When any ray in a visibility cone is found to strike a point on the boundary of any obstacle
O
, we compute tangents from
q
to
O
.
To compute these tangents and, in turn, for updating visibility cones, our algorithm dynamically maintains hull trees of obstacle boundaries using the algorithm by Overmars and van Leeuwen
[
38
]
.
In updating the visibility graph, we use an important characterization to determine edges of the visibility graph
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
of the polygonal domain
\cal P^{\prime}
that need to be deleted from (resp. included into)
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
due to the insertion (resp. deletion) of a vertex from
\cal P^{\prime}
.

details the algorithms to maintain the visibility polygon of any query point whenever the current polygonal domain is modified with a vertex insertion or with a vertex deletions.
The output-sensitive visibility polygon query algorithm of any given query point is given in Section
4
.
Section
5
details both the preprocessing algorithm and the algorithms for maintaining the visibility graph.
Conclusions are in Section
6
.

2
Preprocessing algorithm and data structures

We first preprocess the input polygonal domain
\cal P
.
Using the algorithms in
[
27
,
28
,
30
]
, we partition the free space
\cal{F(P)}
of
\cal P
into
O(h)
corridors and junctions.
To efficiently compute tangents to dynamic sides of corridors, for each side
S
of every corridor, we construct a hull tree corresponding to
S
with the algorithm in
[
35
,
38
]
.
For locating any vertex that is inserted to (resp. deleted from) any obstacle, and for locating query points, we compute a point location data structure for the corridor structure with the algorithm in
[
20
]
.

As in the algorithm in
[
23
]
, for every corridor
C
, we construct a set
P_{C}
of simple polygons corresponding to corridor
C
.
(Refer to Fig.
2
.)
One of the simple polygons in
P_{C}
, denoted with
P_{4}(C)
, is the corridor
C
itself.
This polygon helps in determining vertices of
C
that are visible to
q
when
q
is located in
C
.
If
q
is not located in
C
, the other three simple polygons in
P_{C}
, denoted by
P_{1}(S_{1}),P_{2}(S_{1}),P_{3}(S_{2})
, each corresponding to a side of
C
, together help in determining vertices of
C
that are visible to
q
.
In specific, two of these simple polygons
P_{1}(S_{1}),P_{2}(S_{1})
correspond to one side
S_{1}
of
C
, and
P_{3}(S_{2})
correspond to the other side
S_{2}
of
C
.
For more details, refer to
[
23
]
.

Whenever an obstacle in the polygonal domain gets modified with a vertex
v

v
resides is updated.
Whenever a side
S
of any corridor
C
changes, we make the changes to
P_{4}(C)
as well as to the simple polygons in
P_{C}
that correspond to side
S
.
In the query phase, using the algorithm in
[
25
]
, we compute visible vertices and constructed vertices (refer to
[
18
]
) in these dynamic simple polygons.
As part of preprocessing required for the algorithm in
[
25
]
, for every corridor
C
, each of the four simple polygons in
P_{C}
are further processed in linear time to construct data structures as required by the algorithm in
[
25
]
.
Analogous to set
P_{C}
of simple polygons for
\cal P
, our algorithm maintains corresponding set
P^{\prime}_{C}
(resp.
P^{\prime\prime}_{C}
) of simple polygons for the current polygonal domain
\cal P^{\prime}
(resp. the updated polygonal domain
\cal P^{\prime\prime}
).

Given a polygonal domain
\cal P
defined with

O(n)
in
O(n(\lg{n})^{2}+h(\lg{h})^{1+\epsilon})
time.
Here,
\epsilon
is a small positive constant resulting from the triangulation of free space
\cal{F(P)}
using the algorithm in
[
5
]
.

Proof

Triangulating
\cal P
using the algorithm in
[
5
]
and computing corridors using the algorithms in
[
27
,
28
,
30
]
takes
O(n+h(\lg{h})^{1+\epsilon})
time.
Construction of point location data structure requires
O(n)
time with the algorithm in
[
20
]
.
Using the algorithm in
[
38
]
, constructing hull trees for all the corridor sides together takes
O(n(\lg n)^{2})
time.
Computing the set
P_{C}
of polygons
[
23
]
for every corridor

, while considering all the corridors together takes
O(n)
time.
And, preprocessing all such simple polygons corresponding to all the corridors for constructing data structures required for the algorithm in
[
25
]
takes
O(n)
time.
The data structures for hull trees and point location data structures are of size
O(n)
.
All the simple polygons constructed for all the corridors together take
O(n)
space.

The query algorithm to construct visibility tree data structures
TVIS_{\cal P^{\prime}}^{B}(q)
and
TVIS_{\cal P^{\prime}}^{U}(q)
for the input query point
q
among the polygonal obstacles of
\cal P^{\prime}
is described in Section

.
The visibility polygon of
q
is determined from the information stored at the nodes of these trees, and our dynamic algorithms update visibility trees as and when the current polygonal domain is modified with vertex insertions and vertex deletions.
Our algorithms for updating the visibility polygon of a point
q\in\cal{F(P^{\prime})}
are provided with the visibility trees
TVIS_{\cal P^{\prime}}^{B}(q)
and
TVIS_{\cal P^{\prime}}^{U}(q)
.

The visibility trees were first defined in
[

]
.
We modify visibility tree structures from
[
29
]
so that they are helpful in our context.
Here we describe the structures from
[
29
]
together with our modifications.
For every corridor
C
that has at least one point on the boundary of
C
that is visible to
q
, there exists at least one node in these trees that corresponds to
C
.
Any node
t
in either of these trees corresponds to a corridor
C^{t}
.
With

, we store a pointer to
C^{t}
, a visibility cone

(with its apex at

), and two red-black balanced binary search trees (refer
[
14
]
), denoted by

.
Refer to Fig.
3
.
The

(resp.
RBT_{R}^{t}
) at node

stores every (constructed) vertex

of
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
that belongs to the left (resp. right) side of corridor
C
whenever
v^{\prime}
lies in
vc^{t}
.
With each point
p
in both of these RBTs, we store the angle ray
qp
makes at
q
.
If a point
p
is stored in
TVIS_{\cal P^{\prime}}^{B}(q)
(resp.
TVIS_{\cal P^{\prime}}^{U}(q)
) then the line segment
qp
is guaranteed to intersect
B
(resp.
U
).
For any point
p
located on the boundary of an obstacle, and for
p
visible to
q
, the sequence of corridors intersected by the line segment
qp
is said to be the
corridor sequence
of
qp
.
We note that for any two points
p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime}
in a corridor
C
, with both
p^{\prime}
and
p^{\prime\prime}
visible to
q
, the corridor sequence of
qp^{\prime}
is not necessarily same as the corridor sequence of
qp^{\prime\prime}
.
Hence, in any visibility tree of
q
, there could be more than one node that corresponds to any corridor.
However, any (constructed) vertex of
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
(or, any vertex of
\cal P^{\prime}
) appears at most once in any of the RBTs stored at the nodes of these visibility trees.
For any two nodes
t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}
of any visibility tree, for any point
p^{\prime}
stored in either
RBT_{L}^{t^{\prime}}
or
RBT_{R}^{t^{\prime}}
, and for any point
p^{\prime\prime}
stored in either
RBT_{L}^{t^{\prime\prime}}
or
RBT_{R}^{t^{\prime\prime}}
, the corridor sequence of
qp^{\prime}
is not equal to the corridor sequence of
qp^{\prime\prime}
.

For every corridor
C^{\prime}
, the list of visibility cones that intersect
C^{\prime}
are stored in a red-black tree, named
VC_{C^{\prime}}
.
In specific, the visibility cones in
VC_{C^{\prime}}
are stored in sorted order with respect to angle left bounding ray of each cone in
VC_{C^{\prime}}
makes at
q
.
In addition, with each visibility cone
vc
in
VC_{C^{\prime}}
, we store the pointer to a node in a visibility tree that saved a visible point belonging to
bd(C^{\prime})\cap vc
.
(Refer to Fig.
3
.)
If no such visible point exists, then the pointer to a node in the visibility tree that represents the corridor nearest to
p
along
qp
, where
p\in C^{\prime}\cap vc
, is stored with
vc
.
Whenever a vertex
v
is inserted (resp. deleted) to (resp. from)
C^{\prime}
, by searching in
VC_{C^{\prime}}
, we determine the visibility cone in which we need to update the visibility polygon of
q
.

Let

.
And, let

.
Also, let

and

3
Maintaining the visibility polygon of any query point

Let
\cal P^{\prime}
be the polygonal domain just before inserting (resp. deleting)
v
to (resp. from) the boundary of an obstacle.
Also, let
v_{i}
and
v_{i+1}
be the vertices between which
v
is located.
We assume the triangle
vv_{i}v_{i+1}
is located in one corridor.
In this section, we devise algorithms for updating the data structures that save the visibility polygon of any given query point when
\cal P^{\prime}
is modified with a vertex insertion or a vertex deletion.
Mainly, we update the satellite data associated with visibility trees
TVIS_{\cal P^{\prime}}^{U}(q)
and
TVIS_{\cal P^{\prime}}^{B}(q)
.
We first describe parts of the algorithm that are common to both the insertion and deletion algorithms.
If
v
is inserted to an obstacle of
\cal P^{\prime}
, then we insert
v
at its corresponding position into at most three simple polygons in
P_{C}
; note that one simple polygon in
P_{C}
correspond to the side of
C
to which
v
is incident.
If
v
is deleted from an obstacle in
\cal P^{\prime}
, then for every simple polygon
P\in P_{C}
, we delete
v
from
P
if
v\in P
.
Then, for each simple polygon in
P_{C}
that got modified, we update the preprocessed data structures needed for determining visibility in dynamic simple polygons
[
25
]
.
Using the algorithm in
[
20
]
for ray-shooting in dynamic simple polygons, we determine whether
v
is visible to
q
among obstacles in
\cal P^{\prime}
.
This is accomplished using simple polygons in
P_{C}
: if
q\in C
, the ray-shooting query with ray
qv
is performed in
P_{4}(C)
; otherwise, if
v
belongs to a side
S_{1}
(resp.
S_{2}
) of a corridor
C^{\prime}(\neq C)
, we query with ray
qv
in each simple polygon in
P_{C^{\prime}}
that corresponds to side
S_{1}
(resp.
S_{2}
).
From the correctness of characterizations in
[
23
]
, it is immediate that we correctly determine whether
v
is visible to
q
.
If
v
is found to be not visible to
q
, then
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
does not change.
In this case, we only update the preprocessed data structures for hull trees of sides of corridor
C
, as well as the data structures for dynamic point location.
We note that all the updations of preprocessed data structures can be accomplished in
O((\lg n)^{2})
time.

Consider the case when
v
is visible to
q
.
In this case, the insertion of
v
(resp. deletion of
v
) may cause the deletion of (resp. insertion of) some vertices from (resp. to) the current visibility polygon
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
.
For every two vertices
v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\in\{v,v_{i},v_{i+1}\}
, we determine the (smaller) angle between rays
qv^{\prime}
and
qv^{\prime\prime}
.
Among these three possible cones, we find the cone
vc_{m}
with the maximum cone angle.
The visible vertices belonging to
vc_{m}
are the potential candidates to be deleted (resp. inserted) from (resp. to)
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
in the insertion (resp. deletion) algorithm.
Let
B_{C_{q}}
be the lower bounding edge of corridor
C_{q}
containing
q
.
Without loss of generality, suppose
vc_{m}
intersects
B_{C_{q}}
.
Noting that
v\in C
, by searching in
VC_{C}
, we determine the visibility cone
vc
in which
qv
lies.
We let
t^{\prime}
be the node saved with
vc
in
VC_{C}
, and let
C^{\prime}
be the corridor referred by
t^{\prime}
.
In the following subsections, we describe the specific details of insertion and deletion algorithms.

3.1
Insertion of a vertex

If corridor
C^{\prime}
is same as corridor
C
(to remind,
v
is in corridor
C
), then
v
is inserted to
RBT_{L}^{t^{\prime}}
(resp.
RBT_{R}^{t^{\prime}}
) of node
t^{\prime}
if
v
is located on the left (resp. right) side of
C^{\prime}
.
In the other sub-case,
C^{\prime}
is not the same as
C
.
This indicates there is no node present in
TVIS^{B}_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
that corresponds to
C
and
vc
, i.e., before the insertion of
v
, there was no point of
bd(C)\cap vc
is visible to
q
.
Let
t_{l}^{\prime},t_{r}^{\prime}
be the left and right children of
t^{\prime}
respectively.
A new node
t^{\prime\prime}
is inserted as a left (resp. right) child of
t^{\prime}
, if
v
is located on the left (resp. right) side of
C
, and the parent of
t_{l}^{\prime}
(resp.
t_{r}^{\prime}
) is changed to
t^{\prime\prime}
.
The visibility cone
vc^{\prime}
associated with
t^{\prime\prime}
is same as the visibility cone
vc
associated with node
t^{\prime}
.
Without loss of generality, suppose
t^{\prime\prime}
is inserted as the left child of
t^{\prime}
.
Let
C^{\prime\prime}
be the corridor associated to node
t_{l}^{\prime}
.
The sequence of corridors
SC_{t^{\prime}t_{l}^{\prime}}
associated to edge
t^{\prime}t_{l}^{\prime}
is splitted into two sequences: the corridor sequence between
C^{\prime}
to
C
along visibility cone
vc
is associated to edge
t^{\prime}t^{\prime\prime}
, and the corridor sequence between
C
and
C^{\prime\prime}
along the visibility cone
vc
is saved with edge
t^{\prime\prime}t_{l}^{\prime}
.
In addition, for each corridor
C^{\prime\prime\prime}
in
SC_{t^{\prime\prime}t_{l}^{\prime}}
, the pointer stored with visibility cone
vc
in
VC_{C^{\prime\prime\prime}}
is modified so that it points to node
t^{\prime\prime}
.

Suppose the rays bounding
vc_{m}

qv_{i}
and
qv_{i+1}
.
(Refer to Fig.

Then, every vertex of
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
continues to be visible to
q
.
Hence, there is no vertex to be deleted from
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
.
However, since
v
is visible to
q
, we need to insert
v
into a RBT of
TVIS^{B}_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
.
When
v
is located on the left (resp. right) side of
C
, if
C^{\prime}
is same as
C
, then
v
is inserted to
RBT_{L}^{t^{\prime}}
(resp.
RBT_{R}^{t^{\prime}}
); otherwise, if
C^{\prime}
is not same as
C
, then vertex
v
is inserted to
RBT_{L}^{t^{\prime\prime}}
(resp.
RBT_{R}^{t^{\prime\prime}}
).

Observation 1

Let
\cal P^{\prime}
be the current polygonal domain.
Let
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
be the visibility polygon of a point
q\in\cal P^{\prime}
.
Whenever a new vertex
v
is inserted to an obstacle of
\cal P^{\prime}
, the set of vertices of
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
that get hidden due to the insertion of
v
are contiguous along the boundary of
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
.
In specific, vertices stored in any red-black tree of any visibility tree hidden due to the insertion of
v
are contiguous at the leaves.

Proof

Let
S=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{j}\}
be the ordered set of vertices stored at a leaf of a red-black tree in
TVIS_{\cal P^{\prime}}^{B}(q)
(resp.
TVIS_{\cal P^{\prime}}^{U}(q)
).
The observation is immediate as the vertices in
S
that fall in
vc_{m}
is a subsequence of
S
.

Suppose the rays bounding
vc_{m}
are
qv_{i+1}
and
qv
.
(Refer to Fig.
5
.)
In this case, due to the insertion of
v
, some vertices of
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
may become not visible to
q
.
To determine these vertices, we do the depth-first traversal of
TVIS_{\cal P^{\prime}}^{B}(q)
, starting from
t^{\prime}
if
C^{\prime}
is same as
C
; otherwise, we do the depth-first traversal of
TVIS_{\cal P^{\prime}}^{B}(q)
, starting from node
t^{\prime\prime}
.
Let
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
be the angles made by rays
qv_{i+1}
and
qv
, respectively at
q
.
Also, let
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}
.
For every red-black tree
T
at every node
t
encountered in this traversal, we search in
T
to find the contiguous list of vertices such that each vertex in that list lies in the cone
vc_{m}
.
By Observation
1
, all the vertices belonging to this list are the ones that needed to be removed from
T
.
Hence, we remove each vertex
v^{\prime}
in this list from
T
, as
v^{\prime}
is no more visible to
q
.
Let
C^{t}
be the corridor referred by node
t
.
During traversal, if visibility cone
vc^{t}
associated with node
t
is found to be lying completely inside
vc_{m}
, we delete the node corresponding to visibility cone
vc^{t}
in
VC_{C^{t}}
.

The handling of the last case in which the
vc_{m}
is bounded by rays
qv_{i}
and
qv
is analogous to the case in which
vc_{m}
is bounded by rays
qv_{i+1}
and
qv
.

Lemma 2

Let
\cal P^{\prime\prime}
be the polygonal domain resultant from inserting a vertex
v
to an obstacle of a polygonal domain
\cal P^{\prime}
.
Also, let
VP_{\cal P^{\prime\prime}}(q)
be the visibility polygon of
q
among obstacles in
\cal P^{\prime\prime}
determined by the algorithm.
Then, any point
p\in VP_{\cal P^{\prime\prime}}(q)
if and only if
p
is visible to
q
among obstacles in
\cal P^{\prime\prime}
.

Proof

Let a vertex
v
be inserted on a side of corridor
C\in\cal P^{\prime}
.
If
v
is visible to
q
, we search in
VC_{C}
to find the visibility cone
vc
in which
v
is lying.
A new node is inserted in the current visibility tree of
q
only if there is no node in that visibility tree that corresponds to corridor
C
and visibility cone
vc
.
This ensures that inside a visibility cone, whenever a corridor has at least one visible point on its side, there exists a node corresponding to it in one of the visibility trees.
The red-black trees stored at any node of visibility trees that corresponds to
C
and
vc
stores all the vertices in
\cal P^{\prime}
and the constructed vertices that belong to
C\cap vc
.
Among the vertices stored in these red-black trees, vertices that are residing inside the cone
vc_{m}
become not visible to
q
after the insertion of
v
.
After inserting
v
into the current visibility tree, we traverse the updated visibility tree and determine these vertices.
At every red-black tree
T
encountered during the traversal, we search for the contiguous section of vertices (Observation
1
) residing in
vc_{m}
and remove it from the current visibility polygon.
It ensures that any vertex belonging to
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
is removed from the current visibility tree only if it has become invisible due to the insertion of
v
.

Lemma 3

Whenever a vertex
v
is inserted to
\cal P^{\prime}
, our algorithm updates the current visibility polygon
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
of
q
in
O(k(\lg|VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)|)+(\lg n^{\prime})^{2}+h)
time.
Here,
k
is the number of combinatorial changes in
VP_{P^{\prime}}(q)
due to the insertion of
v
,
n^{\prime}
is the number of vertices of
\cal P^{\prime}
, and
h
is the number of obstacles in
\cal P^{\prime}
.

Proof

Locating the corridor
C
in which
v
is inserted takes
O((\lg{n^{\prime}})^{2})
time.
Determining whether
v
is visible to
q
with a ray-shooting query in each of the simple polygons in
P_{C}
takes
O((\lg{n})^{2})
time.
By searching in
VC_{C}
, we determine the visibility cone in which
v
is lying.
Every node in
VC_{C}
points to a node in either
TVIS_{\cal P^{\prime}}^{B}(q)
or
TVIS_{\cal P^{\prime}}^{U}(q)
.
Since the total number of nodes in either of these trees is
O(h)
, searching
VC_{C}
takes
O(\lg h)
time.
Updating pointers when a new node is inserted into a visibility tree of
q
takes
O(h)
time.
Traversing red-black trees at the nodes of visibility trees and removing the vertices not visible to
q
takes overall
O(k\lg{|VP^{\prime}(q)|})
time.
Updating data structures constructed in the preprocessing phase takes
O((\lg n^{\prime})^{2})
time.

3.2
Deletion of a vertex

Suppose the rays bounding
vc_{m}
are
qv_{i}
and
qv_{i+1}
.
(Refer to Fig.
7
.)
The deletion of
v
does not change the visibility of any vertex belonging to
\cal P^{\prime}
.
Hence, there is no vertex needs to be included in
VP_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
.
However, since
v
is not going to be visible to
q
, we need to delete
v
from node
t^{\prime}
of
TVIS^{B}_{\cal P^{\prime}}(q)
.
The vertex
v
will be deleted from
RBT_{L}^{t^{\prime}}
(resp.
RBT_{R}^{t^{\prime}}
) if it is located on the left (resp. right) side of
C
.
We determine whether vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{i+1}
are visible to
q
.
If any of them is not visible to
q
, then using the algorithm in
[
25
]
for dynamic simple polygons and with cone
vc_{m}
as input, we determine the endpoints of constructed edges (refer to
[
18
]
) residing in
vc_{m}
which are incident to edge
v_{i}v_{i+1}
.
In addition, we insert these endpoints into
RBT_{L}^{t^{\prime}}
(resp.
RBT_{R}^{t^{\prime}}
) if they are located on the left (resp. right) side of
C
.

O(n(\lg{n})^{2}+h(\lg{h})^{1+\epsilon})

5
Maintaining the visibility graph

In this section, we describe an algorithm to maintain the visibility graph among dynamic polygonal obstacles in the plane.
We first detail the preprocessing algorithm with the input polygonal domain
\cal P
with
n
vertices and
h
polygonal obstacles.
Our algorithm relies on the algorithm for maintaining the visibility polygon among dynamic polygonal obstacles in Section
2
.
Hence, we compute all the data structures as required in Lemma
1
.
In addition, we need the following data structures.
Using
[
29
]
, we construct the visibility graph of
\cal P
in
O(|E|+h\lg n+h(\lg h)^{1+\epsilon})
time.
Here,
E
is the number of edges in the visibility graph of
\cal P
.
For every vertex
v\in\cal P
, we construct a red-black tree (detailed in
[
14

), denoted by
RBT_{v}
, that contains all the visible edges that are incident to
v

Every leaf of
RBT_{v}
represents a unique visible edge that is incident to
v
.
For every visible edge that is determined to be incident to
v
, we insert it to
RBT_{v}
in
O(\lg{|E|})
time.
With every visible edge,
RBT_{v}
stores the angle it makes with the positive
x
-axis.
The left to right order of visible edges stored at the leaves of
RBT_{v}
is the sorted order of visible edges incident to
v
with respect to angle each makes with the positive
x
-axis.
Whenever a vertex is inserted to or deleted from any of the obstacles of the current polygonal domain
\cal P^{\prime}
, as part of updating the visibility graph of
\cal P^{\prime}
, we update these red-black tree data structures as well as the data structures for visibility polygon maintenance (refer to Theorem
4.1
) and return the updated visibility graph.

Lemma 9

Let
v_{i},v
, and
v_{i+1}
be consecutive vertices along an obstacle of
\cal P^{\prime\prime}
.
For every visible edge

of
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
that intersects the triangle
vv_{i}v_{i+1}
, either
(a) at least one of
t
or
u
is visible to
v
, or
(b) there exists a path
R
in

from a vertex
v^{\prime}
to
t
(resp.
u
) such that each edge in
R
intersects the triangle
v_{i}vv_{i+1}
and
v^{\prime}
is visible to
v
.

Proof

If either
t
or
u
is visible to
v
, there is nothing to prove.
Otherwise, among all the edges of
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
that intersect both the triangles
vv_{i}v_{i+1}
and
tuv
, let
t^{\prime}u^{\prime}
be a visible edge in
\cal P^{\prime}
that makes the least angle with the line segment
tu
at either
t^{\prime}=t
or
u^{\prime}=u
.
Without loss of generality, we suppose
u^{\prime}=u
.
(Refer to Fig.
12
.)
Note that both
t
and
u
are reachable from
t^{\prime}
in
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
.
Besides, both the edges
t^{\prime}u
and
ut
intersect the triangle
vv_{i}v_{i+1}
.
Further,
t^{\prime}
is closer to
v
as compared to
t
, with respect to Euclidean distance.
Inductively,
t^{\prime}
is reachable from
v
in
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
as described in the lemma statement.
Since there are finite vertices, there exists some edge

such that

.

5.1
Insertion of a vertex

Let
v_{i}
and
v_{i+1}
be the vertices between which the vertex
v
is inserted.
Also, let
S
be the set of visible edges that intersect the triangle
vv_{i}v_{i+1}
.
We observe that any visible edge
e\in VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
appears in the updated visibility graph
VG_{\cal P^{\prime\prime}}
if and only if
e
does not belong to set
S
.
First, we determine vertices that are visible to
v
.
To accomplish this, we invoke the visibility polygon query algorithm among dynamic obstacles detailed in Section
3
, with
v
as the query point.
A new red-black tree, denoted by
RBT_{v}
is initiated and, for every vertex
v^{\prime}\in\cal P^{\prime}
determined by the visibility query algorithm, we insert the visibility edge
vv^{\prime}
into
RBT_{v}
as well into
RBT_{v^{\prime}}
.
This completes the insertion of new visible edges to the visibility graph
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
.
Next, we determine the set
S
of visible edges of
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
whose endpoints have become not visible to each other after the insertion of
v
.
To efficiently determine edges in
S
, we use Lemma
9
in iteratively finding all the vertices to which visible edges to be removed from
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
are incident.
Let
S_{1}
be the set comprising of all the vertices of
\cal P^{\prime}
visible to
v
.
The points in
S_{1}
are determined from the visibility polygon of
v
.
For each vertex
v^{\prime}\in S_{1}
, we determine all the vertices
v^{\prime\prime}
such that
v^{\prime}v^{\prime\prime}
is a visible edge in
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
and it intersects the triangle
vv_{i}v_{i+1}
.
Without loss of generality, suppose the ray
v^{\prime}v_{i}
makes larger angle with
v^{\prime}v
as compared to
v^{\prime}v_{i+1}
.
Let
vc_{m}
be the cone with rays
v^{\prime}v
and
v^{\prime}v_{i}
bounding it.
The visible edges lying in cone
vc_{m}
are the potential candidates to be deleted from
VG_{\cal P^{\prime}}
.
We search in
RBT_{v^{\prime}}
to find every vertex
v^{\prime\prime}
such that the edge
v^{\prime}v^{\prime\prime}
belongs to cone
vc_{m}
, and delete the edge
v^{\prime}v^{\prime\prime}
from both
RBT_{v^{\prime}}
and
RBT_{v^{\prime\prime}}
.
In specific, for every
j\geq 1
, let
S_{j+1}
be equal to
\bigcup_{v^{\prime}\in S_{j}}S_{v^{\prime}}
.
Following the same procedure, for every
j>1
, for every vertex
v^{\prime}\in S_{j}
and
v^{\prime}\notin S_{j^{\prime}}
with
j^{\prime}<j
, we find the set
S_{j+1}
comprising of all the visible edges in
\cal P^{\prime}
that are incident to
v^{\prime}
and intersect triangle
vv_{i}v_{i+1}
.
If at any point,
S_{j}\subseteq S_{j^{\prime}}
for some
j^{\prime}<j
, we terminate the algorithm.

6
Conclusions

We proposed algorithms for the following problems among dynamic polygonal obstacles in the plane: (i) dynamically updating the visibility polygon of any given query point, (ii) answering visibility polygon queries, and (iii) dynamically maintaining the visibility graph of the polygonal domain.
An immediate extension of this algorithm would handle new obstacles being introduced into the polygonal domain as well as allowing the newly inserted vertex to be in a corridor different from the corridor in which its neighbors reside.
Further, it would be interesting to devise efficient dynamic algorithms for these problems so that the update time complexities mainly depend on the number the combinatorial changes to update the visibility polygon.
In specific, as other parameters are under logarithm, this involves removing the dependency on the number of obstacles in the polygonal domain from the update time complexities.

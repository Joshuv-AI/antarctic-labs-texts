Maximal Codimension Collisions and Invariant Measures for Hard Spheres on a Line
Mark Wilkinson

Abstract
For any N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 , we study invariant measures of the dynamics of N N hard spheres whose centres are constrained to lie on a line. In particular, we study the invariant submanifold ℳ \mathcal{M} of the tangent bundle of the hard sphere billiard table comprising initial data that lead to the simultaneous collision of all N N hard spheres. Firstly, we obtain a characterisation of those continuously-differentiable N N -body scattering maps which generate a billiard dynamics on ℳ \mathcal{M} admitting a canonical weighted Hausdorff measure on ℳ \mathcal{M} (that we term the Liouville measure on ℳ \mathcal{M} ) as an invariant measure. We do this by deriving a second boundary-value problem for a fully nonlinear PDE that all such scattering maps satisfy by necessity. Secondly, by solving a family of functional equations, we find sufficient conditions on measures which are absolutely continuous with respect to the Hausdorff measure in order that they be invariant for billiard flows that conserve momentum and energy. Finally, we show that the unique momentum- and energy-conserving linear N N -body scattering map yields a billiard dynamics which admits the Liouville measure on ℳ \mathcal{M} as an invariant measure.

Introduction

In the theory of billiard dynamics on billiard tables which possess
corners
, it is often the case that trajectories are only constructed for those initial data that do not lead to the corners themselves. Indeed, to quote Smillie (
[
28
]
, pp.360-361), if “the trajectory hits a corner then there may be no good physical principal [sic] which selects one particular continuation. In this case the continuation of the trajectory is undefined.” We also refer the reader to the works (
[
26
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
10
]
) in which corner dynamics is considered. As a result, the associated billiard flow operators are undefined on a topologically- or measure theoretically-negligible subset of the tangent bundle of the table (or a sphere bundle over the table, if one works in a phase space with unit velocities). If one is to try to extend the domain of definition of these billiard flow operators to the whole tangent bundle of the table, thereby extending the definition of a trajectory past those times it hits a corner, one has to deal with the generic
non-uniqueness
of trajectory extension past this time of collision. In order to decide what constitutes a ‘good’ or ‘natural’ extension of the dynamics, we shall consider
analytical
properties of trajectories
in-the-large
on phase space, rather than any physical principle which is otherwise lacking.

One well-studied billiard table with corners, of interest to those both in the pure and applied mathematics communities (see, for example, Bodineau, Gallagher, Saint-Raymond and Simonella
[
7
]
, or Viazovska
[
30
]
in the case the spheres are 8-dimensional), is the set of all admissible centres of mass for a collection of
N
hard spheres evolving in space. Indeed, we denote by
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3N}
the
hard sphere billiard table
given by

\mathcal{B}:=\left\{X=(x_{1},...,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{3N}\,:\,|x_{i}-x_{j}|\geq\varepsilon\hskip 5.69054pt\text{for}\hskip 5.69054pti\neq j\right\},

(1)

with
x_{i}=(x_{i,1},x_{i,2},x_{i,3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
modelling the centre of mass of a sphere labelled by
i\in\{1,...,N\}
, and where
\varepsilon>0
denotes the radius of each of the congruent spheres. Corners in the boundary
\partial\mathcal{B}
of the billiard table correspond to configurations of the centres of mass which characterise simultaneous collision of
M
of these spheres, where
3\leq M\leq N
. In the particular case of momentum- and energy-conserving hard sphere dynamics on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or subsets thereof, with which the present article is largely concerned, the matter of constructing global-in-time billiard trajectories which start from initial data in these high codimension components of the tangent bundle
T\mathcal{B}

. Indeed, one needs to construct a
scattering map
that has range in a non-trivial semi-algebraic subvariety of
\mathbb{R}^{3N}
, which leads to the aforementioned absence of uniqueness in trajectory extension.

In this article, we study the connection between the analytical properties of such scattering maps and the analytical properties of the billiard flows on phase space that they generate. In particular, in the reduced setting of linear hard sphere dynamics on the tangent bundle
T\mathcal{B}
of the hard sphere table
\mathcal{B}
, we use the concept of
invariant measure
(and the PDE involving their densities) to formulate a condition as to what constitutes a ‘good’ extension of billiard trajectory past corners of the table
\mathcal{B}
. By establishing a natural generalisation of
Liouville measure
on the subvariety of
T\mathcal{B}
on which we work, we prove in the case of momentum- and energy-conserving billiard dynamics that there is a
unique
linear scattering map generating a billiard flow on
T\mathcal{B}
that admits this Liouville measure as an invariant measure.

As the construction of scattering maps involves rudimentary (semi-) algebraic geometry, we now set out the problem of their construction.

1.1
A Problem in Algebraic Geometry

To begin, we set up some notation that will be helpful in defining the algebraic varieties with which we work in the sequel. For given
Z=(X,V)\in T\mathcal{B}

1

X_{i}:=x_{\lceil i/3\rceil,[i-1]_{3}+1}

for
i\in\{1,...,3N\}

[i]_{3}:=i\,(\text{mod}\,3)

V\in T_{X}\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3N}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}Y_{3i-2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}V_{3i-2},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}Y_{3i-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}V_{3i-1},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}Y_{3i}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}V_{3i},\end{array}\right.

(2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{3i-1}Y_{3i}-X_{3i}Y_{3i-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{3i-1}V_{3i}-X_{3i}V_{3i-1},\\

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{3i-2}Y_{3i-1}-X_{3i-1}Y_{3i-2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{3i-2}V_{3i-1}-X_{3i-1}V_{3i-2},\end{array}\right.

(3)

\sum_{i=1}^{N}Y_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}V_{i}^{2}.

(4)

3

Additionally, if one stipulates that billiard trajectories

are both left- and right-differentiable on
\mathbb{R}

\sum_{k=1}^{3}\left(X_{3(i-1)+k}-X_{3(j-1)+k}\right)(Y_{3(i-1)+k}-Y_{3(j-1)+k})\leq 0

\Gamma_{X}:=\left\{(i,j)\in\{1,...,N\}^{2}\setminus\Delta_{N}\,:\,\sum_{k=1}^{3}(X_{3(i-1)+k}-X_{3(j-1)+k})^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}\right\}.

These semi-algebraic conditions correspond to the set of all admissible

X\in\partial\mathcal{B}

X\in\partial\mathcal{B}
, we write

to denote the set of all

5

(i,j)\in\Gamma_{X}
. The analogous set of
post-collisional
velocities is denoted by
\mathcal{V}_{X}^{+}:=-\mathcal{V}_{X}^{-}

Given spatial data
X\in\partial\mathcal{B}
in a corner of the table, as well as a velocity vector
V\in\mathcal{V}_{X}^{-}
which is pre-collisional with respect to

N\gg 1
it is not trivial to determine the structure of the algebraic variety
\mathcal{Y}_{Z}\cap\mathcal{V}_{X}^{+}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3N}
for

. We invite the reader to consult Coste
[
11

or Basu, Pollack and Roy (

Scattering Maps and the Flows they Generate

With the above notation in place, one can consider the problem of the construction of maps

There are generically uncountably-many momentum- and energy-conserving scattering maps

\{T^{t}_{\Sigma}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
T\mathcal{B}
. As binary collisions of hard spheres which conserve momentum and energy are uniquely determined – or, in the language of this Section, it holds that
\mathcal{Y}_{Z}\cap\mathcal{V}_{X}^{+}
is a singleton for any

such that

is itself a singleton – the difference between any two flow operators

and

generated by momentum- and energy-conserving scattering maps

is seen only on topologically- or measure-theoretically negligible subsets of
T\mathcal{B}
. Nevertheless, if one is interested in the
global
structure of billiard dynamics on
T\mathcal{B}
, it is important to derive natural analytical criteria on scattering maps
\Sigma
which result in ‘good’ properties of the associated flow operators
T^{t}_{\Sigma}
. The point of view we take in this article arises from the study of
invariant measures
on
T\mathcal{B}
other than the well-known
Liouville measure

\Lambda:=\mathscr{L}_{6N}\,\raisebox{-0.5468pt}{\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{-90.0}{$\lnot$}}}\,T\mathcal{B}
: see Chernov and Markarian (
[
10
]

Problem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
. Suppose
\mathcal{M}\subset T\mathcal{B}
is an invariant manifold for any billiard flow on
T\mathcal{B}
. For a given measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{M}
which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Hausdorff measure on
\mathcal{M}
, find all
\Sigma

T^{t}_{\Sigma}\#\mu=\mu

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

In order to make this problem more tractable to study in the present article, we reduce our study of free motion of hard spheres on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to the case when the centres of mass of the spheres are constrained to lie on a line. The associated Problem
1.1
is still not trivial in this case. As we shall show in the sequel, when
\Sigma
admits some smoothness properties, solving this Problem results in the identification of a second boundary-value problem for a fully-nonlinear PDE characterising all desired scattering maps
\Sigma
: see Trudinger and Wang
[
29
]
for the definition of such boundary-value problems. This simplification on the structure of the billiard table results in the well-studied

1.3
The Scattering Varieties in the ‘One-dimensional’ Case

The structure of the algebraic system introduced above becomes more straightforward when we assume that the motion of the
N
spheres is constrained to lie on a line in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. More precisely, in the particular case that the spatial datum
X\in\partial\mathcal{B}
is of the form

X=(d_{1}a,...,d_{N}a)\in\partial\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3N}

(6)

for some

and

is uncountable. In this article, we identify analytical conditions on the scattering map

Reduction to the Fundamental Billiard Table

\mathcal{P}:=\left\{X=(x_{1},...,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,|x_{i}-x_{j}|\geq\varepsilon\hskip 5.69054pt\text{for}\hskip 2.84526pti\neq j\right\},

1.5
The Universal Invariant Manifold
\mathcal{M}

As discussed above, the motivation for this study stems from the fact that any billiard trajectory on
T\mathcal{B}
which conserves both momentum and energy is not uniquely defined beyond collision for those initial data that lead to it reaching a corner of
\mathcal{B}
. As such, it is natural to distinguish the set of all initial data in
T\mathcal{Q}
which lead to a simultaneous collision of all
N
hard spheres.

Definition 1.5
.

For any
N\geq 3
, we define the set
\mathcal{M}\subset T\mathcal{Q}
to be

\mathcal{M}=\left\{Z=(X,V)\in T\mathcal{Q}\,:\,X+tV=c\mathbf{1}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{for some}\hskip 2.84526ptt,c\in\mathbb{R}\right\},

where
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
denotes the vector all of whose entries are 1.

As we study the case when all
N
spheres in the system collide simultaneously, as opposed to
M<N
spheres colliding simultaneously, the codimension of
\mathcal{M}
in
T\mathcal{Q}
is in this sense
maximal
. Like the manifold
T\mathcal{Q}
in which it is contained, the set
\mathcal{M}
does not admit the structure of a smooth manifold. Rather, it can be shown that
\mathcal{M}
admits the structure of a manifold with corners of dimension
N+2
. Its boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{M}
is given by

\partial\mathcal{M}=\left\{Z=(X,V)\in\mathcal{M}\,:\,x_{i}=x_{j}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69054ptv_{i}=v_{j}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{for some}\hskip 5.69054pti<j\right\}.

We note that the manifold
\mathcal{M}
is
universal
as an invariant submanifold of
T\mathcal{Q}
, in the sense that any billiard flow
\{T^{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
T\mathcal{Q}
admits the property that
T^{t}(Z)\in\mathcal{M}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
if
Z\in\mathcal{M}
. Stated more plainly (and utilising the terminology of Section
2
below), no matter the scattering a flow
\{T^{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathcal{M}
admits, the trajectory
t\mapsto T^{t}(Z)
is a piecewise linear-in-time map admitting a single collision time.

1.6
A Natural Measure on
\mathcal{M}

It has also been discussed above that one analytical means that can be used to determine a ‘natural’ way to extend billiard trajectories is through studying the properties of measures evolved by the associated flow operators to which the trajectories give rise. One ‘natural’ measure on
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2N}
is the restriction of the
(N+2)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure to
\mathcal{M}
, which we subsequently denote by
\mathscr{H}
. We refer the reader to (
[
15
]
, Chapter 2) for properties of the Hausdorff measure. There is a convenient formula which allows one to calculate the volume of subsets of the embedded submanifold
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2N}
as measured by
\mathscr{H}
. We recall that if
\Psi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
is a smooth diffeomorphism for some open
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+2}
, then for any bounded open subset
E\subset\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
it holds that

\mathscr{H}(E)=\int_{\Psi^{-1}(E)}\omega\,d(\mathscr{L}\,\raisebox{-0.5468pt}{\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{-90.0}{$\lnot$}}}\,\Psi^{-1}(E)),

where
\omega
is the
density
associated to the chart map
\Psi
defined pointwise by

\omega(\zeta):=\sqrt{\mathrm{det}(D\Psi(\zeta)^{T}D\Psi(\zeta))}

for
\zeta\in\Omega
. We shall make frequent use of this representation formula in the sequel. Whilst
\mathscr{H}
cannot be an invariant measure for billiard flows defined on
\mathcal{M}
(a claim we prove in Section
3.3
below), we nevertheless find a canonical measure
\lambda
on
\mathcal{M}
which is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathscr{H}
.

1.7
Some Existing Results on the ‘One-dimensional’ Model

We do not aim here to provide a comprehensive review of the literature on billiard dynamics on
T\mathcal{Q}
or
T\mathcal{P}
, drawing upon only some of the most relevant works to which our main results are pertinent. The existence of momentum- and energy-conserving billiard trajectories on
T\mathcal{B}
for
N\geq 3
has been covered, ostensibly, by Alexander in his 1975 Berkeley PhD thesis
[
2
]
. However, his method of construction of global-in-time billiard trajectories on
T\mathcal{B}
(and therefore on
T\mathcal{Q}
) that conserve both momentum and energy holds only for initial points in
T\mathcal{B}
outside a measure-theoretically null set, including those initial points that lead to the simultaneous collision of 3 or more hard spheres (
[
2
]
, page 14). As such, the systematic approach in this article covers the case of initial points his theory cannot handle, but only in the case where motion of the
N
spheres is directed along a line. Let us also mention that the case of momentum- and energy-conserving dynamics of hard spheres on a line whose radii and masses may differ has also been considered by other authors: see, for instance, the work of Murphy
[
25
]
. The system of
infinitely-many
hard spheres on a line has also attracted interest from a number of authors, in particular the works of Dobrushin and Fritz
[
13
]
, Lanford
[
23
]
, as well as Sinai
[
27
]
.

Aside from issues pertaining to the construction of dynamics on
T\mathcal{Q}
, the study of statistical properties of one-dimensional systems has been of interest as a ‘toy’ in the hydrodynamics of particle systems. The study of hydrodynamic limits on
T\mathcal{Q}
is more tractable than its counterpart study on the full hard sphere phase space
T\mathcal{B}
, and has produced Euler-like equations modelling the large-scale evolution of dynamics on
T\mathcal{Q}
as
N\rightarrow\infty
(
[
21
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
19
]
). As this article includes the case
N=3
, let us also mention that ternary collisions in hard sphere systems have also been of recent interest: see, for instance, the work of Ampatzoglou and Pavlović
[
4
]
on the derivation of a Boltzmann equation corresponding to dynamics for ternary particle interactions.

1.9
Rigorous Statements of Main Results

Before we state our main results, we set out a few basic definitions pertaining to billiard dynamics on
T\mathcal{Q}
. For each integer
N\geq 3
, we write
\mathcal{V}^{-}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
to denote the set of pre-collisional velocities

\mathcal{V}^{-}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{N-1}\left\{V\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,V\cdot(e^{N}_{i}-e^{N}_{i+1})\geq 0\right\},

and we write
\mathcal{V}^{+}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
to denote the set of post-collisional velocities

\mathcal{V}^{+}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{N-1}\left\{V\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,V\cdot(e^{N}_{i}-e^{N}_{i+1})\leq 0\right\}.

In what follows, we call any bijection
\sigma:\mathcal{V}^{-}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{+}
an
N
-body scattering map
. Intuitively (and as we shall see in Section
2
below), an
N
-body scattering map is a map that assigns a post-collisional velocity vector to a pre-collisional velocity vector and is, in turn, used to construct trajectories of an associated family of flow operators. Moreover, we say that
\sigma
is a
momentum- and energy-conserving

N
-body scattering map if and only if it satisfies additionally the conservation of linear momentum

\mathsf{P}(\sigma(V))=\mathsf{P}(V)

together with the conservation of kinetic energy

\mathsf{E}(\sigma(V))=\mathsf{E}(V)

for all
V\in\mathcal{V}^{-}
, where
\mathsf{P}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the linear momentum map

\mathsf{P}(V):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}

(9)

and
\mathsf{E}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the energy map

\mathsf{E}(V):=|V|^{2}.

(10)

We note that there is no need to consider angular momentum in the special case of motion on a line, owing to the fact that the polynomials which characterise the conservation of angular momentum reduce to those of the conservation of linear momentum in this case.

As we shall discover below, the restriction of the
(N+2)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure to
\mathcal{M}
is
not
an invariant measure for any billiard flow on
\mathcal{M}
. Nevertheless, there is a canonical invariant measure supported on the interior of
\mathcal{M}
which our calculations reveal in the sequel. As such, we make the following definition for what we term the
Liouville
measure on
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 1.6

(Liouville Measure on
\mathcal{M}
)
.

For
N\geq 3
, we define the
Liouville measure on
\mathcal{M}

\lambda
by

\lambda:=L\mathscr{H},

where the density
L:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is defined pointwise by

L(Z):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\prod_{i<j}\left((v_{i}-v_{j})^{2}+(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N-2}{N(N-1)}}&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptZ\in\mathcal{M}^{\circ},\\
\displaystyle\infty&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Remark 1.7
.

The reason we have adopted the terminology “Liouville measure on
\mathcal{M}
” for
\lambda
relates to the Liouville Theorem of classical statistical mechanics (see Khinchin
[
1
]
, for example). Indeed, as a consequence of Theorem
1.8
below, our billiard flows
\{T^{t}_{\sigma}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
admit the property that, for any measurable subset
E\subset\mathcal{M}
,

\lambda(T^{-t}_{\sigma}(E))=\lambda(E)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. As such, the billiard flows
\{T^{t}_{\sigma}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathcal{M}
‘preserve volumes’ as measured by
\lambda
.

Our first main result characterises all those
N
-body scattering maps which generate a billiard flow on
\mathcal{M}
that admits the Liouville measure
\lambda
as an invariant measure.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
N\geq 3
. Suppose
\sigma\in C^{0}(\mathcal{V}^{-},\mathcal{V}^{+})\cap C^{1}((\mathcal{V}^{-})^{\circ},(\mathcal{V}^{+})^{\circ})
is a given
N
-body scattering map. It holds that
\sigma
generates a billiard flow
\{T^{t}_{\sigma}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathcal{M}
with the property

T^{t}_{\sigma}\#\lambda=\lambda

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if
\sigma
is a classical solution of one the second boundary-value problems:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\mathrm{det}(D\sigma(V))=\frac{H(V)}{H(\sigma(V))}\quad\text{for}\hskip 5.69054ptV\in(\mathcal{V}^{-})^{\circ},\\
\displaystyle\sigma(\mathcal{V}^{-})=\mathcal{V}^{+}\end{array}\right.

(11)

or

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\mathrm{det}(D\sigma(V))=-\frac{H(V)}{H(\sigma(V))}\quad\text{for}\hskip 5.69054ptV\in(\mathcal{V}^{-})^{\circ},\\
\displaystyle\sigma(\mathcal{V}^{-})=\mathcal{V}^{+},\end{array}\right.

(12)

where
H
is defined pointwise by

H(W):=\frac{\left(\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}(w_{i}-w_{j})^{2}\right)^{1/2}}{\left(\displaystyle\prod_{i<j}(w_{i}-w_{j})^{2}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{N(N-1)}}}

(13)

for
W=(w_{1},...,w_{N})\in(\mathcal{V}^{-})^{\circ}\cup(\mathcal{V}^{+})^{\circ}
.

The Jacobian PDE in the second boundary-value problems (
11
) and (
12
) are highly nonlinear and therefore challenging to solve. We shall identify
linear
solutions
\sigma
of these problems below.

Our second main result finds sufficient conditions on the form of the density of an invariant measure of a billiard flow on
\mathcal{M}
which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Liouville measure.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
N\geq 3
. Suppose
\sigma\in C^{0}(\mathcal{V}^{-},\mathcal{V}^{+})\cap C^{1}((\mathcal{V}^{-})^{\circ},(\mathcal{V}^{+})^{\circ})
is a momentum- and energy-conserving
N
-body scattering map, and let
\{T^{t}_{\sigma}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
denote the billiard flow on
\mathcal{M}
it generates. Suppose, moreover, that
\mu\ll\lambda
, with

\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda}=M

for some measurable function
M:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. If

M
is of the form

M(Z)=m_{0}(X\cdot(\mathsf{E}(V)\mathbf{1}-\mathsf{P}(V)V),V)

for
Z=(X,V)\in\mathcal{M}
and some measurable
m_{0}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
admitting the scattering symmetry

m_{0}(y,\sigma(V))=m_{0}(y,V)

(14)

for
y\in\mathbb{R}
and
V\in\mathcal{V}^{-}
, then
T^{t}_{\sigma}\#\mu=\mu
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Finally, our third main result identifies the unique linear
N
-body scattering map that conserves momentum and energy, and in turn shows that the billiard flow it generates admits the Liouville measure on
\mathcal{M}
as an invariant measure.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
N\geq 3
. There exists a unique linear momentum- and energy-conserving
N
-body scattering map
\sigma^{\star}:\mathcal{V}^{-}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{+}
given by

\sigma^{\star}(V):=\left(\frac{2}{N}\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}-I_{N}\right)V

(15)

for all
V\in\mathcal{V}^{-}
. Moreover,
\sigma^{\star}
generates a momentum- and energy-conserving billiard dynamics
\{T^{t}_{\star}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathcal{M}
with the property that

T^{t}_{\star}\#\lambda=\lambda

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 1.11
.

We note that, even though the case of binary interactions is not considered in this article, the formula (
15
) recovers the case of the unique linear 2-body scattering map

\sigma^{\star}(V)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)V

which conserves linear momentum and energy when
N=2
.

1.10
Structure of the Paper

In Section
2
, we introduce some of the basic objects with which we work throughout. In Sections
3
and
4
, in what amounts to a rather involved application of the Change of Variables theorem, we process the information in those integrals under study not only to derive the PDE characterising
N
-body scattering maps of billiard flows on
\mathcal{M}
which admit the Liouville measure
\lambda
on
\mathcal{M}
as an invariant measure, but also a functional equation whose solutions generate measures which are absolutely-continuous with respect to
\lambda
. In Section

N
-body scattering which is a linear map and show, in turn, the billiard flow on
\mathcal{M}
it generates admits
\lambda
as an invariant measure.

2
Basic Auxiliary Objects

We introduce the basic objects of study in what follows. For notational convenience, and to avoid decorating symbols for maps and spaces with too many sub- and superscripts, we do not in general denote dependence of objects on the number of spheres
N\geq 3
under study.

2.1
A Chart for the Interior of
\mathcal{M}

Moving forward, owing to the fact we shall build integrals over
\mathcal{M}
with respect to the Hausdorff measure which are insensitive to the values of maps on the boundary of
\mathcal{M}
, we focus our attention on the interior of
\mathcal{M}
, namely
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}=\mathcal{M}\setminus\partial\mathcal{M}
. In particular, we shall make extensive use of the
chart map

\Psi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
which charts the interior of the codimension
(N-2)
subset
\mathcal{M}\subset T\mathcal{Q}
of
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
pointwise by

\Psi(\zeta):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(e_{i}^{2N}\otimes e_{i}^{2N})\zeta+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(e_{i+N}^{2N}\otimes e_{i}^{N})\psi(\zeta),

for
\zeta=(X,U)\in\Omega
, where

\Omega:=\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}\times(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Delta)

and
X=(x_{1},...,x_{N})\in\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}
with
U=(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Delta
, where
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the diagonal

\Delta:=\left\{U=(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,u_{1}=u_{2}\right\},

and where the
velocity map

\psi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
is defined pointwise by

\psi_{i}(\zeta):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle u_{i}&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054pti\in\{1,2\},\\
\displaystyle\frac{(x_{i}-x_{2})u_{1}-(x_{i}-x_{1})u_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054pti\in\{3,...,N\}.\end{array}\right.

Manifestly, it holds that
\Psi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{M}^{\circ})
and one can check that it is a smooth diffeomorphism. Using this chart,
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
may be characterised in bundle notation as

\mathcal{M}^{\circ}=\bigsqcup_{X\in\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}}F_{X},

where the fibres
F_{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
are defined for each
X
by

F_{X}=\left\{\psi(\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,U\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\right\},

noting that
\zeta=(X,U)
.

Remark 2.1
.

There are many ‘natural’ ways by which one can define a chart map
\Psi
for the interior of
\mathcal{M}
, and our particular choice which slaves the velocity the spheres labelled by
i\in\{3,...,N\}
to those labelled by 1 and 2 admits a signature in our calculations, as we shall see in the sequel.

2.2
Billiard Flows on
\mathcal{M}

In this Section, we set out the analytical properties we require of billiard flows on the manifold
\mathcal{M}
in all that follows. We do not concern ourselves with the construction of flows defined on the full phase space
T\mathcal{Q}
as this has been handled by other authors, viz. Alexander
[
2
]
. The systematic study of dynamics on
\mathcal{M}
has not, to our knowledge, received attention in the literature.

2.2.1
Momentum- and Energy-conserving Billiard Flows on
T\mathcal{Q}

We begin with the following basic definition of billiard flow.

Definition 2.2

(Billiard Flow)
.

We say that a one-parameter family
\{T^{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of maps
T^{t}:T\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow T\mathcal{Q}
is a
billiard flow
on
T\mathcal{Q}
if and only if for each
Z\in T\mathcal{Q}
, it holds that

•

the map
t\mapsto(\Pi_{1}\circ T^{t})(Z)
is a piecewise linear map of class
C^{0}(\mathbb{R},T\mathcal{Q})
which satisfies

\frac{d}{dt_{\pm}}(\Pi_{1}\circ T^{t})(Z)=\lim_{s\rightarrow t\pm}(\Pi_{2}\circ T^{s})(Z)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

•

the map
t\mapsto(\Pi_{2}\circ T^{t})(Z)
is of class
\mathrm{LSC}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap\mathrm{BV}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

We term the members
T^{t}
of the billiard flow
\{T^{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

flow maps
.

We shall work exclusively on the manifold
\mathcal{M}\subset T\mathcal{Q}
. We subsequently abuse notation and denote the restriction
T^{t}|_{\mathcal{M}}
of any flow map
T^{t}
to the manifold
\mathcal{M}
simply by
T^{t}
. As we focus our attention only on those billiard flows which conserve momentum and energy, we also require the following definition.

Definition 2.3

(Momentum- and Energy-conserving Billiard Flow)
.

We say that a billiard flow
\{T^{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
T\mathcal{Q}
is
momentum- and energy-conserving
if and only if for each
Z\in T\mathcal{Q}
it holds that

\mathsf{P}((\Pi_{2}\circ T^{t})(Z))=\mathsf{P}(\Pi_{2}(Z))

and

\mathsf{E}((\Pi_{2}\circ T^{t})(Z))=\mathsf{E}(\Pi_{2}(Z))

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let us once again mention that, in the case of hard sphere motion on a line, the conservation of angular momentum is redundant if the conservation of linear momentum is enforced.

2.2.2
Scattering Maps for Billiard Flows on
\mathcal{M}

We now set out some definitions which are relevant to the simultaneous collision of
N
hard spheres.

Definition 2.4

(Collision Time Map on
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
)
.

Let
N\geq 3
. We write
\tau:\mathcal{M}^{\circ}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to denote the
collision time map on
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
pointwise by

\tau(Z):=-\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{v_{2}-v_{1}}

(16)

for
Z=(X,V)\in\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
.

We note that the definition of
\tau
appears to involve only those variables indexed by 1 and 2. However, it can be checked that

\tau(Z)=-\frac{x_{i}-x_{j}}{v_{i}-v_{j}}

for any
i,j\in\{1,...,N\}
with
i<j
. Nevertheless, owing to our explicit choice of chart map for
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
given in Section
2.1
above, we shall adopt the definition of the collision time map (
16
) involving the indices 1 and 2. With this definition in hand, we may now define what we mean by a
scattering map
of a billiard flow
\{T^{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 2.5

(Scattering Map of a Billiard Flow on
\mathcal{M}
)
.

Let
N\geq 3
. Suppose that
\{T^{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a billiard flow on
T\mathcal{Q}
. Its associated
scattering map

\sigma:\mathcal{V}^{-}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{+}
is defined pointwise by

\sigma(V):=\lim_{t\downarrow\tau(Z)}(\Pi_{2}\circ T^{t})(Z)

for
Z=(0,V)\in\mathcal{M}
.

While every billiard flow gives rise to a scattering map, the converse also holds true. We require the following result, the details of whose proof we defer to Section
2.4
below.

Proposition 2.6
.

Every scattering map
\sigma
generates a billiard flow on
\mathcal{M}
, i.e. for any scattering map
\sigma:\mathcal{V}^{-}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{+}
there exists a billiard flow
\{T^{t}_{\sigma}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathcal{M}
with the property

\sigma(V)=\lim_{t\downarrow\tau(Z)}(\Pi_{2}\circ T^{t}_{\sigma})(Z)

for
Z=(0,V)\in\mathcal{M}
.

2.2.3
Regular Billiard Flows on
\mathcal{M}

Given a billiard flow on
\mathcal{M}
, its scattering map
\sigma
may possess no degree of regularity at all. As our criterion on a flow admitting the Liouville measure
\lambda
as an invariant measure features a first-order PDE (c.f. the statement of Theorem
1.8
), we require the following notion of
regularity
for the billiard flows with which we work.

Definition 2.7

(Regular Billiard Flow)
.

We say that a billiard flow
\{T^{t}_{\sigma}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathcal{M}
is a
regular
billiard flow on
\mathcal{M}
if and only if its scattering map
\sigma
is of class
C^{0}(\mathcal{V}^{-},\mathcal{V}^{+})\cap C^{1}((\mathcal{V}^{-})^{\circ},(\mathcal{V}^{+})^{\circ})
.

2.3
Billiard Trajectories on
\mathcal{M}

In this Section, we define billiard trajectories on
\mathcal{M}
which start from initial data in the interior
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
. In loose terms, this corresponds to those initial data which model the centres of all
N
spheres being a strictly positive distance from one another at time zero. In turn, we shall use these explicit trajectory formulae to write down representation formulae for the associated billiard flow maps in Section
2.4
below. For the moment, we must split our considerations into 2 cases, namely one in which initial data
Z_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
are
pre
-collisional, and one in which the data
Z_{0}
are
post
-collisional.

Suppose that
\sigma\in C^{0}(\mathcal{V}^{-},\mathcal{V}^{+})\cap C^{1}((\mathcal{V}^{-})^{\circ},(\mathcal{V}^{+})^{\circ})
is a given
N
-body scattering map. Let
Z_{0}=(X_{0},V_{0})\in\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
be such that
V_{0}\in(\mathcal{V}^{-})^{\circ}
. The associated
pre-collisional
billiard trajectory
Z_{\sigma}(\cdot;Z_{0}):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
is defined to be

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle Z_{\sigma}(t;Z_{0}):=\\
\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left(I_{2N}+t\sum_{i=1}^{2N}e^{2N}_{i}\otimes e^{2N}_{i+N}\right)Z_{0}&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054pt-\infty<t\leq\tau(Z_{0}),\\
\displaystyle\left(I_{2N}+(t-\tau(Z_{0}))\sum_{i=1}^{N}e_{i}^{2N}\otimes e_{i+N}^{2N}\right)\Sigma_{\sigma}(Z_{0})+t\sum_{i=1}^{N}e_{i}^{2N}\otimes e_{i+N}^{2N}Z_{0}&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054pt\tau(Z_{0})<t<\infty,\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(17)

where
\Sigma_{\sigma}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2N}
denotes the
extended scattering map
associated to the
N
-body scattering
\sigma
defined by

\Sigma_{\sigma}(Z_{0}):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X_{0}\\
\sigma(V_{0})\end{array}\right)

for
Z_{0}=(X_{0},V_{0})\in\mathcal{M}
such that
V_{0}\in\mathcal{V}^{-}
. Similarly, let
Z_{0}=(X_{0},V_{0})\in\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
be such that
V_{0}\in(\mathcal{V}^{+})^{\circ}
. The associated
post-collisional
billiard trajectory
Z_{\sigma}(\cdot;Z_{0}):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
is defined by

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle Z_{\sigma}(t;Z_{0}):=\\
\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left(I_{2N}+(t-\tau(Z_{0}))\sum_{i=1}^{N}e_{i}^{2N}\otimes e_{i+N}^{2N}\right)\Sigma_{\sigma^{-1}}(Z_{0})+t\sum_{i=1}^{N}e_{i}^{2N}\otimes e_{i+N}^{2N}Z_{0}&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054pt-\infty<t\leq\tau(Z_{0}),\\
\displaystyle\left(I_{2N}+t\sum_{i=1}^{2N}e^{2N}_{i}\otimes e^{2N}_{i+N}\right)Z_{0}&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054pt\tau(Z_{0})<t<\infty,\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(18)

where
\Sigma_{\sigma^{-1}}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2N}
denotes the extended scattering map associated to the inverse
\sigma^{-1}
, given pointwise by

\Sigma_{\sigma}(Z_{0}):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X_{0}\\
\sigma^{-1}(V_{0})\end{array}\right)

for
Z_{0}=(X_{0},V_{0})\in\mathcal{M}
such that
V_{0}\in\mathcal{V}^{+}
. We would like to find a billiard flow
\{T^{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathcal{M}
whose associated flow maps
T^{t}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
admit the property that

T^{t}(Z_{0})=Z_{\sigma}(t;Z_{0})

for all
Z_{0}\in\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
and each
fixed

t\in\mathbb{R}
. The reader will note that the trajectory formulae (
17
) and (
18
) do not immediately suggest the form of the flow maps
T^{t}_{\sigma}
to which they are related. We shall consider this below.

2.4
Representation Formulae for Flow Maps on
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}

In this Section, we offer a sketch of the details involved in a proof of Proposition
2.6
above, namely that every
N
-body scattering map
\sigma
generates an associated billiard flow
\{T^{t}_{\sigma}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathcal{M}
. However, as it is all we require, we only consider here the construction of the billiard flow maps on the interior of
\mathcal{M}
in what follows.

Owing to the role that the inverse
\sigma^{-1}
of a given
N
-body scattering map
\sigma
plays in definition of billiard trajectories, moving forward we must distinguish between the cases
t>0
and
t<0

being trivial in that
T^{0}_{\sigma}:=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{M}}
for any choice of
N
-body scattering map
\sigma
. We begin by defining the auxiliary subset
\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}\subset\Omega
by

\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{X\in\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}}\left\{U\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Delta\,:\,u_{1}\leq u_{2}+\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{t}\right\}&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptt>0,\\
\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}\times\left\{U\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,u_{1}<u_{2}\right\}&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptt=0,\\
\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{X\in\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}}\left\{U\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Delta\,:\,u_{1}>u_{2}+\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{t}\right\}&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptt<0,\end{array}\right.

and its counterpart
\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+}\subset\Omega
by

\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{X\in\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}}\left\{U\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Delta\,:\,u_{1}>u_{2}+\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{t}\right\}&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptt>0,\\
\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}\times\left\{U\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,u_{1}>u_{2}\right\}&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptt=0,\\
\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{X\in\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}}\left\{U\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Delta\,:\,u_{1}\leq u_{2}+\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{t}\right\}&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptt<0.\end{array}\right.

We note that
\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}\cup\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+}=\Omega
. Using the subsets
\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}
and
\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+}
of the chart map domain
\Omega=\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}\times(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Delta)
, we now define the important sets

and
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{+}
which will be of focus in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively.

Definition 2.8
.

We define
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-}\subset\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{+}\subset\mathcal{M}
to be

\mathcal{M}_{t}^{\pm}:=\Psi(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{\pm})

for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

In particular, it holds that

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. With these definitions in hand, we may now posit Ansätze for the form of the billiard flow operators
T^{t}_{\sigma}
associated to the trajectories (
17
) and (
18
) above. The reader may verify that the maps
T^{t}_{\sigma}:\mathcal{M}^{\circ}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
defined by

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle T^{t}_{\sigma}(Z_{0}):=\\
\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left(I_{2N}+t\sum_{i=1}^{2N}e^{2N}_{i}\otimes e^{2N}_{i+N}\right)Z_{0}&\quad\text{for}\hskip 5.69054ptZ_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-},\\
\displaystyle\left(I_{2N}+(t-\tau(Z_{0}))\sum_{i=1}^{N}e_{i}^{2N}\otimes e_{i+N}^{2N}\right)\Sigma_{\sigma}(Z_{0})+t\sum_{i=1}^{N}e_{i}^{2N}\otimes e_{i+N}^{2N}Z_{0}&\quad\text{for}\hskip 5.69054ptZ_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{t}^{+}\end{array}\right.\end{array}

for fixed
t>0
, and by

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle T^{t}_{\sigma}(Z_{0}):=\\
\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left(I_{2N}+t\sum_{i=1}^{2N}e^{2N}_{i}\otimes e^{2N}_{i+N}\right)Z_{0}&\quad\text{for}\hskip 5.69054ptZ_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-},\\

for fixed

for any

and all

. In particular, they admit the desired property that

\sigma(V_{0})=\lim_{t\downarrow\tau(Z_{0})}(\Pi_{2}\circ T^{t}_{\sigma})(Z_{0})

for

with

, as well as

\sigma^{-1}(V_{0})=\lim_{t\uparrow\tau(Z_{0})}(\Pi_{2}\circ T^{t}_{\sigma})(Z_{0})

for
Z_{0}=(0,V_{0})
with

. As the precise properties of the
N
-body scattering
\sigma
have not yet been prescribed, other than the prescription that

\mathcal{V}^{-}
to the polytope
\mathcal{V}^{+}
, we cannot say anything about the image sets
T^{t}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{M}_{t}^{+})
for

. As such, we make the following assumptions on the properties of any
N
-body scattering map throughout this article.

Assumption 2.9

(Admissible
N
-body Scattering Map
\sigma
)
.

We say that an
N
-body scattering map
\sigma:\mathcal{V}^{-}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{+}
is
admissible
if and only if it satisfies the following two conditions:

•

\sigma^{-1}(V)=-\sigma(-V)
for all
V\in\mathcal{V}^{+}
;

•

T^{t}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{M}^{-}_{t})\cup T^{t}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{M}^{+}_{t})=\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

The first of the above assumptions, which expresses the inverse scattering map
\sigma
in terms of the scattering map itself, shall be employed in the proof of uniqueness of linear
N
-body scattering that conserves both linear momentum and energy in Section
5
below. It is equivalent to the assumption that the billiard flow
\{T^{t}_{\sigma}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
generated by
\sigma
admits no
arrow of time
: see Landau and Lifschitz
[
22
]
. The second of these assumptions ensures that the billiard flow maps do not map regions of
\mathcal{M}
of positive measure to those of null measure (which can occur if
\sigma
does not conserve the energy of its argument, for instance). In the sequel, we shall refer to an
admissible

N
-body scattering map simply as an
N
-body scattering map.

Remark 2.10
.

In what follows, we subsequently drop the ‘0’ subscript from initial data in
\mathcal{M}
, thereby considering flow maps
T^{t}_{\sigma}
as a function of
Z=(X,V)\in\mathcal{M}
rather than of
Z_{0}=(X_{0},V_{0})
.

3
Analysis over the Region
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-}

In this Section, we consider the properties of billiard flow maps
T^{t}_{\sigma}
only over the region
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-}
as defined in Section
2.4
above. We consider properties of flow maps over the complement
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{+}
in Section
4
below. Our aim here is to find symmetry properties of any measure
\mu\in\mathsf{M}(\mathcal{M})
with
\mu\ll\mathscr{H}
which satisfies the identity

\langle T_{\sigma}^{t}\#\mu,\Phi\rangle=\langle\mu,\Phi\rangle

(19)

for all
\Phi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2N},\mathbb{R})
with the property that the support of
\Phi
satisfies the condition

\mathscr{H}(\mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\cap\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-})>0.

(20)

Indeed, we invoke the following basic lemma which motivates this aim.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
N\geq 3
. It holds that for any bounded measurable subset
E\subset\mathcal{M}
there exists a sequence
\{\Phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2N},\mathbb{R})
with the property that

\mu(E)=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\Phi_{j}\,d\mu

Proof.

This is a consequence of Urysohn’s Lemma: see Munkres (
[
24
]
, Chapter 4).
∎

We shall show in the sequel that identity (
19
) does
not
hold if the measure
\mu
is taken to be the Hausdorff measure
\mathscr{H}
. Rather, we identify a function
L
derived from the ‘shape’ of the manifold
\mathcal{M}
such that
\mu=L\mathscr{H}
satisfies (
19
).

3.1
Integration over Codimension
(N-2)
Surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{2N}

We state without proof the following elementary result on the evaluation of codimension
(N-2)
surface integrals in
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
by way of parametrisation maps.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose
N\geq 3
, and that
\mu\ll\mathscr{H}
is a given measure on
\mathcal{M}
with density
M
. Suppose further that
\Psi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
denotes any diffeomorphism of class
C^{1}
. For any
\Phi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2N},\mathbb{R})
with the property

\mathscr{H}(\mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\cap\mathcal{M})>0,

it holds that

\int_{\mathcal{M}}\Phi\,d\mu=\int_{\Omega}(\Phi\circ\Psi)(M\circ\Psi)\omega\,d\mathscr{L},

where the surface density
\omega\in C^{0}(\Omega,[0,\infty))
is given by

\omega(X,U):=\sqrt{D\Psi(X,U)^{T}D\Psi(X,U)}

for
(X,U)\in\Omega
, and where
\mathscr{L}
denotes the restriction of the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N+2}
to the Lebesgue-measurable subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+2}
.

Proof.

See Duistermaat and Kolk (
[
14
]
, Section 7.3) and Evans and Gariepy (
[
15
]
, Section 3.3.4.D).
∎

We recall from above that for any momentum- and energy-conserving
N
-body scattering map
\sigma
, the billiard flow
\{T^{t}_{\sigma}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
it generates has flow maps of the shape

T^{t}_{\sigma}(Z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left(I_{2N}+t\sum_{i=1}^{2N}e_{i}^{N}\otimes e_{i+N}^{2N}\right)Z&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptZ\in\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-},\\
\displaystyle\left(I_{2N}+(t-\tau(Z))\sum_{i=1}^{N}e_{i}^{2N}\otimes e_{i+N}^{2N}\right)\Sigma_{\sigma}(Z)+t\sum_{i=1}^{N}e_{i}^{2N}\otimes e_{i+N}^{2N}Z&\quad\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptZ\in\mathcal{M}_{t}^{+}\end{array}\right.

for
Z\in\mathcal{M}
and any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. As such, the flow map
T^{t}_{\sigma}
acts as a
shear
operator on
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-}
and its action is independent of the choice of
N
-body scattering map
\sigma
. For any
\Phi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2N},\mathbb{R})
with the property that

\mathscr{H}(\mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\cap\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-})>0,

we find that

\langle T^{t}_{\sigma}\#\mu,\Phi\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\Phi\,d(T^{t}_{\sigma}\#\mu)=\int_{T^{-t}_{\sigma}\circ\Psi(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-})}(\Phi\circ T^{t}_{\sigma}\circ\Psi)(M\circ\Psi)\omega\,d\mathscr{L}.

(21)

In order to be able to process the information in the integral (
21
) above, for any
N\geq 3
, we require the following Lemma on the explicit form of the surface density
\omega:\Omega\rightarrow[0,\infty)
associated to the chart map
\Psi
defined above.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
N\geq 3
, it holds that the surface density
\omega
associated to the chart map
\Psi
is given by

\omega(X,U)=\frac{((u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}+(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2})^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{N-1}}\left(\sum_{i>j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(22)

for
(X,U)\in\Omega
.

Proof.

To produce the general formula (
22
) above, we evaluate the determinant of
D\Psi^{T}D\Psi
for any
N\geq 3
in a systematic manner. Indeed, employing elementary row and column operation matrices, we show that
D\Psi^{T}D\Psi
is equivalent to a lower-triangular matrix-valued map, following which the evaluation of its determinant is trivial.

To begin, it can be shown that the matrix-valued map
D\Psi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2N\times(N+2)}
is given block-wise by

D\Psi(X,U)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}&I_{N+2}&\\
\hline\cr\\
A(X,U)&\displaystyle\left(\frac{u_{1}-u_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right)I_{N-2}&B(X,U)\end{array}\right),

where
A:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{(N-2)\times 2}
is given by

A_{j,1}(X,U):=\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})(x_{2}-x_{j+2})}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}

and

A_{j,2}(X,U):=-\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})(x_{1}-x_{j+2})}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}

for
j\in\{1,...,N-2\}
, while
B:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{(N-2)\times 2}
is given by

B_{j,1}:=-\frac{x_{2}-x_{j+2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}

and

B_{j,2}:=\frac{x_{1}-x_{j+2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}

for
j\in\{1,...,N-2\}
. In turn, it follows that the product map
D\Psi^{T}D\Psi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{(N+2)\times(N+2)}
is given block-wise by

D\Psi(X,U)^{T}D\Psi(X,U)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\alpha(X,U)&\gamma(X,U)^{T}&\delta(X,U)\\
\gamma(X,U)&\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}\right)I_{N-2}&\tau(X,U)\gamma(X,U)\\
\delta(X,U)&\tau(X,U)\gamma(X,U)^{T}&\beta(X,U)\end{array}\right),

(23)

where the block map
\alpha:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
is given by

\alpha_{1,1}(X,U):=1+\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{4}}\sum_{j=3}^{N}(x_{2}-x_{j})^{2},

\alpha_{1,2}(X,U)=\alpha_{2,1}(X,U):=-\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{4}}\sum_{j=3}^{N}(x_{1}-x_{j})(x_{2}-x_{j}),

\alpha_{2,2}(X,U):=1+\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{4}}\sum_{j=3}^{N}(x_{1}-x_{j})^{2},

with
\beta:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
given by

\beta_{1,1}(X,U):=1+\frac{1}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}\sum_{j=3}^{N}(x_{2}-x_{j})^{2},

\beta_{1,2}(X,U)=\beta_{2,1}(X,U):=-\frac{1}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}\sum_{j=3}^{N}(x_{1}-x_{j})(x_{2}-x_{j}),

\beta_{2,2}(X,U):=1+\frac{1}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}\sum_{j=3}^{N}(x_{1}-x_{j})^{2}

for
(X,U)\in\Omega
. Moreover,
\gamma:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{(N-2)\times 2}
is defined by

\gamma_{j,1}(X,U):=\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{3}}(x_{2}-x_{j+2})

and

\gamma_{j,2}(X,U):=-\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{3}}(x_{1}-x_{j+2})

for
j\in\{1,...,N-2\}
, while
\delta:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
is defined by

\delta_{1,1}(X,U):=-\frac{u_{1}-u_{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{3}}\sum_{j=3}^{N}(x_{2}-x_{j})^{2},

\delta_{1,2}(X,U)=\delta_{2,1}(X,U):=\frac{u_{1}-u_{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{3}}\sum_{j=3}^{N}(x_{1}-x_{j})(x_{2}-x_{j}),

\delta_{2,2}(X,U):=-\frac{u_{1}-u_{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{3}}\sum_{j=3}^{N}(x_{1}-x_{j})^{2}

for
(X,U)\in\Omega
. We now claim that for each
(X,U)\in\Omega
the matrix (
23
) can be reduced to lower-triangular form by both elementary row and column operations. Indeed, if we define the elementary matrix
\mathsf{E}(i,j,a)\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+2)\times(N+2)}
by

\mathsf{E}(i,j,a):=I_{N+2}+a(e_{i}^{N+2}\otimes e_{j}^{N+2})

for
i,j\in\{1,...,N+2\}
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
, then it holds that the product of matrices given by

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\left(\prod_{k=1}^{2}\mathsf{E}\left(k,N+k,\frac{u_{1}-u_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right)\right)D\Psi(X,U)^{T}D\Psi(X,U)\left(\prod_{k=1}^{2}\mathsf{E}\left(k,N+k,-\frac{u_{1}-u_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right)\right)\times\\
\displaystyle\prod_{k=3}^{N}\mathsf{E}\left(k,N+1,\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})(x_{2}-x_{k})}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}\right)\prod_{k=3}^{N}\mathsf{E}\left(k,N+2,-\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})(x_{1}-x_{k})}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}\right)\times\\
\displaystyle\mathsf{E}\left(N+1,N+2,\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}(x_{2}-x_{j})^{2}\right)^{-1}\sum_{k=3}^{N}(x_{1}-x_{k})(x_{2}-x_{k})\right)\end{array}

(24)

equals the lower-triangular matrix

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}1&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0&0\\
\star&1&0&0&\dots&0&0&0&0\\
\star&\star&1+\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}&0&\dots&0&0&0&0\\
\star&\star&0&1+\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}&\dots&0&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\star&\star&0&0&\dots&1+\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}&0&0&0\\
\star&\star&0&0&\dots&0&1+\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}&0&0\\
\star&\star&\star&\star&\dots&\star&\star&p(\zeta)&0\\
\star&\star&\star&\star&\dots&\star&\star&\star&q(\zeta)\end{array}\right),

where
\star
denotes an entry whose value is immaterial to the value of the determinant, and where
p,q:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are defined by

p(\zeta):=\frac{1}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(x_{2}-x_{j})^{2}

and

q(\zeta):=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}(x_{2}-x_{j})^{2}\right)^{-1}\sum_{i>j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}

for
\zeta\in\Omega
. As the determinant of a lower-triangular matrix is given by the product of the elements on the diagonal, we find from (
24
) above that

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{2}\mathrm{det}\,\mathsf{E}\left(k,N+k,\frac{u_{1}-u_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right)\mathrm{det}\,\mathsf{E}\left(k,N+k,-\frac{u_{1}-u_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right)\times\\
\displaystyle\prod_{k=3}^{N}\mathrm{det}\,\mathsf{E}\left(k,N+1,\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})(x_{2}-x_{k})}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}\right)\mathrm{det}\,\mathsf{E}\left(k,N+2,-\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})(x_{1}-x_{k})}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}\right)\times\\
\displaystyle\mathrm{det}\,\mathsf{E}\left(N+1,N+2,\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}(x_{2}-x_{j})^{2}\right)^{-1}\sum_{k=3}^{N}(x_{1}-x_{k})(x_{2}-x_{k})\right)\mathrm{det}(D\Psi(\zeta)^{T}D\Psi(\zeta))=\\
\displaystyle\frac{((u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}+(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2})^{N-2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2(N-1)}}\sum_{i>j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}.\end{array}

(25)

Finally, as it holds that

\mathrm{det}(\mathsf{E}(i,j,a))=1

for any
i,j\in\{1,...,N+2\}
and any
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we infer from (
25
) above that

\mathrm{det}(D\Psi(\zeta)^{T}D\Psi(\zeta))=\frac{((u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}+(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2})^{N-2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2(N-1)}}\sum_{i>j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}

for all
\zeta\in\Omega
, from which follows the proof of the Proposition.
∎

3.2
Some Auxiliary Maps and Identities

It is not straightforward to perform a change of coordinates in the integral

\int_{\Omega}\Phi(T^{t}_{\sigma}(\Psi(\zeta)))M(\Psi(\zeta))\omega(\zeta)\,d\mathscr{L}(\zeta)

(26)

with the aim of eliminating the presence of compositions in the argument of
\Phi
, since the dimension of the domain of the map
T^{t}_{\sigma}\circ\Psi
as a subset of Euclidean space does not match the dimension of its range. Given our choice of chart map
\Psi
, it is prudent to define a
reduced flow map

\widetilde{T}^{t}
pointwise by

\widetilde{T}^{t}(\zeta):=\zeta+t\sum_{i=1}^{N}(e_{i}^{N+2}\otimes e_{i+N}^{2N})\Psi(\zeta)

for all
\zeta=(X,U)\in\Omega
and each
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Evidently, this reduced flow map has the property that the dimension of its domain is the same as its range. The reader may check that, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the map
\widetilde{T}^{t}
is a smooth diffeomorphism of
\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}
with inverse given by
(\widetilde{T}^{t})^{-1}=\widetilde{T}^{-t}
. However, from the point of view of (
26
), we must understand how the remaining
N-2
‘excluded’ velocity variables transform when composed with this map. Indeed, for each
N\geq 3
we define the
reduced velocity map

\overline{\psi}:\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N-2}
pointwise by

\overline{\psi}_{i}(\zeta):=\psi_{i+2}(\zeta)

for
\zeta\in\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}
and
i\in\{1,...,N-2\}
. As such, we require the following Lemma, which we state without proof.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
N\geq 3
, it holds that

(\overline{\psi}\circ\widetilde{T}^{-t})(\zeta)=\overline{\psi}(\zeta)

for all
\zeta\in\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-})
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

As a consequence of this Lemma, it follows that

\Phi(\zeta,\overline{\psi}(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta)))=\Phi(\zeta,\overline{\psi}(\zeta))=\Phi(\Psi(\zeta))

(27)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, which is an identity of which we make use in the sequel. We also require the following formula for the Jacobian of the coordinate transformation
\widetilde{T}^{t}
which holds for any
N\geq 3
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
N\geq 3
, it holds that

\mathrm{det}(D\widetilde{T}^{t}(\zeta))=\left(\frac{x_{1}+tu_{1}-x_{2}-tu_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right)^{N-2}

(28)

for each
\zeta=(X,U)\in(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-})^{\circ}
and each
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to show by differentiation that
D\widetilde{T}^{t}:\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{(N+2)\times(N+2)}
is given blockwise by

D\widetilde{T}^{t}(X,U)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c c}I_{2}&0_{2\times(N-2)}&tI_{2}\\
A(X,U,t)&\displaystyle\left(\frac{x_{1}+tu_{1}-x_{2}-tu_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right)I_{N-2}&B(X,U,t)\\
0_{2}&0_{2\times(N-2)}&I_{2}\end{array}\right),

where
A:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{(N-2)\times 2}
is given by

A_{j,1}(X,U,t):=\frac{t(u_{1}-u_{2})(x_{2}-x_{j+2})}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}

and

A_{j,2}(X,U,t):=-\frac{t(u_{1}-u_{2})(x_{1}-x_{j+2})}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}

for
j\in\{1,...,N-2\}
, and
B:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{(N-2)\times 2}
is given by

B_{j,1}(X,U,t):=-\frac{t(x_{2}-x_{j+2})}{x_{1}-x_{2}}

and

B_{j,2}(X,U,t):=\frac{t(x_{1}-x_{j+2})}{x_{1}-x_{2}}

for
j\in\{1,...,N-2\}
. By performing elementary column operations, it holds that the product

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle D\widetilde{T}^{t}(\zeta)\prod_{i=1}^{2}\mathsf{E}(i,N+i,-t)\prod_{j=3}^{N}\mathsf{E}\left(j,N+1,t\frac{x_{2}-x_{j}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right)\times\\
\displaystyle\prod_{k=3}^{N}\mathsf{E}\left(k,N+2,-t\frac{x_{1}-x_{k}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right)\end{array}

equals the lower-triangular matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}1&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0&0\\
0&1&0&0&\dots&0&0&0&0\\
\star&\star&\frac{x_{1}+tu_{1}-x_{2}-tu_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}&0&\dots&0&0&0&0\\
\star&\star&0&\frac{x_{1}+tu_{1}-x_{2}-tu_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}&\dots&0&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\star&\star&0&0&\dots&\frac{x_{1}+tu_{1}-x_{2}-tu_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}&0&0&0\\
\star&\star&0&0&\dots&0&\frac{x_{1}+tu_{1}-x_{2}-tu_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&1&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0&1\\
\end{array}\right)

for
\zeta\in\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}
. Once again, as the determinant of a lower-triangular matrix equals the product of the elements on its diagonal, it follows that

\mathrm{det}(D\widetilde{T}^{t}(X,U))=\left(\frac{x_{1}+tu_{1}-x_{2}-tu_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right)^{N-2}

as claimed in the statement of the Lemma.
∎

We also require the following structural result, whose algebraic proof we also leave to the reader.

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
N\geq 3
, it holds that

\begin{array}[]{c}(\omega\circ\widetilde{T}^{-t})(\zeta)=\\
\displaystyle\left(\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{x_{1}-tu_{1}-x_{2}+tu_{2}}\right)^{N-2}\left(\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}+(x_{1}-tu_{1}-x_{1}+tu_{2})}{(u_{1}-x_{2})^{2}+(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\omega(\zeta)\end{array}

for
\zeta=(X,U)\in\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-})
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

3.3
Derivation of the Density Functional Equation

With the above building blocks of Section
3.2
in place, we may now approach the derivation of a functional equation which, if satisfied by a density
M
, generates an invariant measure
\mu=M\lambda
. For any
\Phi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2N},\mathbb{R})
with the support property
\mathscr{H}(\mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\cap\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-})>0
, we have that

\begin{array}[]{cl}&\langle T^{t}_{\sigma}\#\mu,\Phi\rangle\\
=&\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-})}\Phi(\widetilde{T}^{t}(\zeta),\overline{\psi}(\zeta))M(\zeta,\overline{\psi}(\zeta))\omega(\zeta)\,d\mathscr{L}(\zeta)\\
=&\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}}\Phi(\zeta,\overline{\psi}(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta)))M(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta),\overline{\psi}(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta)))\omega(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta))\left|\mathrm{det}(D\widetilde{T}^{t}(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta)))^{-1}\right|\,d\mathscr{L}(\zeta)\\
\overset{\eqref{translatejaccy}}{=}&\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}}\Phi(\zeta,\overline{\psi}(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta)))M(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta),\overline{\psi}(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta)))\omega(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta))\left|\frac{x_{1}-tu_{1}-x_{2}+tu_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right|^{N-2}\,d\mathscr{L}(\zeta)\\
\overset{\eqref{usefulid}}{=}&\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}}\Phi(\Psi(\zeta))M(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta),\overline{\psi}(\zeta))\omega(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta))\left|\frac{x_{1}-tu_{1}-x_{2}+tu_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right|^{N-2}\,d\mathscr{L}(\zeta),\end{array}

and as by Lemma
3.6

\omega(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta))\left(\frac{x_{1}-tu_{1}-x_{2}+tu_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right)^{N-2}=\left(\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}+(x_{1}-tu_{1}-x_{1}+tu_{2})^{2}}{(u_{1}-x_{2})^{2}+(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\omega(\zeta)

for all
\zeta\in\Omega
, we conclude that

\int_{\mathcal{M}}\Phi\,d(T^{t}_{\sigma}\#\mu)=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\Phi\,d\mu

for all
\Phi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2N},\mathbb{R})
with the aforementioned support property if the density
M
of the measure
\mu
satisfies the identity

\left(\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}+(x_{1}-tu_{1}-x_{1}+tu_{2})^{2}}{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}+(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}M(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta),\overline{\psi}(\zeta))=M(\Psi(\zeta)),

which reads more straightforwardly as

\left(\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}+(x_{1}-tu_{1}-x_{1}+tu_{2})^{2}}{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}+(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}M(X-t\psi(\zeta),\psi(\zeta))=M(X,\psi(\zeta))

(29)

for all
\zeta=(X,U)\in\Omega
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The reader will note that there appears to be a spurious bias on the variables indexed by 1 and 2 in the above identity (
29
). However, this is simply a consequence of our choice of chart map
\Psi
. Indeed, we may rewrite identity (
29
) in such a way it becomes symmetric in all variables. In this direction, we require another structural lemma which we state without proof.

Lemma 3.7
.

For any
N\geq 3
, it holds that

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}+(x_{1}-tu_{1}-x_{1}+tu_{2})^{2}}{(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}+(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}=\\
\displaystyle\frac{(\psi_{i}(\zeta)-\psi_{j}(\zeta))^{2}+(x_{i}-t\psi_{i}(\zeta)-x_{j}+t\psi_{j}(\zeta))^{2}}{(\psi_{i}(\zeta)-\psi_{j}(\zeta))^{2}+(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}}\end{array}

for
i\in\{1,...,N\}
with
i<j
and
\zeta\in\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}
.

Employing the result of Lemma
3.7
above, the identity (
29
) may be rewritten in the more symmetric form as

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\left(\prod_{i<j}\frac{(\psi_{i}-\psi_{j})^{2}+(x_{i}-t\psi_{i}-x_{j}+t\psi_{j})^{2}}{(\psi_{i}-\psi_{j})^{2}+(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{N(N-1)}}M(X-t\psi(X,U),\psi(X,U))=\\
M(X,\psi(X,U)),\end{array}

whence, owing to the fact that
\Psi
is a homeomorphism, it holds that

\left(\prod_{i<j}\frac{(v_{i}-v_{j})^{2}+(x_{i}-tv_{i}-x_{j}+tv_{j})^{2}}{(v_{i}-v_{j})^{2}+(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{N(N-1)}}M(X-tV,V)=M(X,V)

(30)

for all
V\in F_{X}
and
X\in\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}
. It is at this point we acknowledge that the Hausdorff measure
\mathscr{H}
cannot be an invariant measure for any billiard flow on
\mathcal{M}
. We record this observation in the following Lemma.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\{T^{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be a billiard flow on
\mathcal{M}
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, it holds that
T^{t}\#\mathscr{H}\neq\mathscr{H}
.

Proof.

This follows from identity (
30
) above by setting the density
M
to be unity.
∎

It is now possible to simplify the structure of the functional equation (
30
) above. Indeed, defining a new measurable map
m:\sqcup_{X\in\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}}F_{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
pointwise by

m(X,V):=\left(\prod_{i<j}(v_{i}-v_{j})^{2}+(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{N(N-1)}}M(X,V),

it holds that
\langle T^{t}_{\sigma}\#\mu,\Phi\rangle=\langle\mu,\Phi\rangle
for all
\Phi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2N},\mathbb{R})
admitting the support condition
\mathscr{H}(\mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\cap\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-})>0
if
m
satisfies the functional equation

m(X-tV,V)=m(X,V)

(31)

for all
V\in F_{X}
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We shall solve this functional equation in the following Section.

3.4
Solving the Functional Equation

It may appear at first glance that the functional equation (
31
) is trivial. However, this is not the case due to the algebraic relationships between
X
and
V
, owing to the fact that
Z=(X,V)
lies in a non-trivial algebraic subvariety of
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
. As we do not seek to characterise all invariant measures which are absolutely continuous with respect to the Liouville measure
\lambda
, rather seeking only sufficient conditions on their densities, we assume in what follows that the density
M
(and, in turn,
m
) is defined on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
as opposed to simply on the subset
\sqcup_{X\in\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}}F_{X}
. We now record the following Proposition.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
N\geq 3
. Suppose that
m:\mathbb{R}^{2N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a measurable map satisfying the functional equation

m(X-tV,V)=m(X,V)

for all
V\in F_{X}
and all
X\in\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}
. It follows that
m
is necessarily of the form

m(X,V)=m_{0}(X\cdot(\mathsf{E}(V)\mathbf{1}-\mathsf{P}(V)V),V)

for some measurable map
m_{0}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and for all
V\in F_{X}
and
X\in\mathcal{Q}^{\circ}
, where
\mathsf{P}
is the linear momentum map (
9
) and
\mathsf{E}
is the energy map (
10
) above.

Proof.

We begin by noting that
V\in F_{X}
if and only if
X\in G_{V}
, where
G_{V}\subset\mathcal{Q}
is given by

G_{V}:=\left\{y_{1}\left(e_{1}^{N}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{v_{i}-v_{2}}{v_{1}-v_{2}}e_{i}^{N}\right)+y_{2}\left(e_{2}^{N}-\sum_{j=3}^{N}\frac{v_{j}-v_{1}}{v_{1}-v_{2}}e_{j}^{N}\right)\,:\,y_{1}\leq y_{2}\right\}.

As a consequence, the invariant manifold
\mathcal{M}
may be written in bundle notation by ‘switching’ the role of the spatial and velocity variables as

\mathcal{M}=\bigsqcup_{V\in\mathcal{V}}G_{V},

where
\mathcal{V}:=\mathcal{V}^{-}\cup\mathcal{V}^{+}
. Manifestly,
G_{V}
is a subset of the 2-dimensional real vector space
\mathcal{G}_{V}
given by

\mathcal{G}_{V}:=\mathrm{span}\left\{e_{1}^{N}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{v_{i}-v_{2}}{v_{1}-v_{2}}e_{i}^{N},e_{2}^{N}-\sum_{i=3}^{N}\frac{v_{i}-v_{1}}{v_{1}-v_{2}}e_{i}^{N}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Indeed, let us subsequently denote the vectors in the above span by

\gamma_{1}:=e_{1}^{N}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{v_{i}-v_{2}}{v_{1}-v_{2}}e_{i}^{N}

and

\gamma_{2}:=e_{2}^{N}-\sum_{i=3}^{N}\frac{v_{i}-v_{1}}{v_{1}-v_{2}}e_{i}^{N}.

Let
V\in\mathcal{V}
be fixed. By defining the measurable auxiliary map
m_{V}:\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
pointwise by

m_{V}(X):=m(X,V),

the functional equation may be rewritten as

m_{V}(X-tV)=m_{V}(X).

(32)

As a result, we interpret the functional equation rather as a
V
-parametrised functional equation for an unknown function
m_{V}
defined on
G_{V}
. For this given
V
, it holds that if
X\in G_{V}
then
X-tV\in G_{V}
for all suitable
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus, the value of
m_{V}(X)
depends only on the component of
X
which lies in the orthogonal complement of
\mathrm{span}\{V\}
in
\mathcal{G}_{V}
.

It holds that
V\in G_{V}
owing to the fact that
V=v_{1}\gamma_{1}+v_{2}\gamma_{2}
. By employing the Gram-Schmidt algorithm to the basis
\{v_{1}\gamma_{1}+v_{2}\gamma_{2},v_{1}\gamma_{1}-v_{2}\gamma_{2}\}
of
\mathcal{G}_{V}
, the reader may verify that

\mathcal{G}_{V}=\mathrm{span}\left\{V,\mathsf{E}(V)\mathbf{1}-\mathsf{P}(V)V\right\}.

As such, given
X\in\mathcal{G}_{V}
, it holds that

X=aV+b(\mathsf{E}(V)\mathbf{1}-\mathsf{P}(V)V)

(33)

for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
which depend on
X
. Thus, it follows from the
V
-parametrised functional equation (
32
) that

m_{V}(aV+b(\mathsf{E}(V)\mathbf{1}-\mathsf{P}(V)V))=m_{V}((a-t)V+b(\mathsf{E}(V)\mathbf{1}-\mathsf{P}(V)V))

for all
t
. However, by taking
t=a
, it follows immediately from the above that

m_{V}(X)=m_{V}(b(\mathsf{E}(V)\mathbf{1}-\mathsf{P}(V)V)).

(34)

From the identity (
33
) above, it follows that
b
is given explictly in terms of
X
by

b=\frac{X\cdot(\mathsf{E}(V)\mathbf{1}-\mathsf{P}(V)V)}{|\mathsf{E}(V)\mathbf{1}-\mathsf{P}(V)V|^{2}}.

Thus, defining the measurable function
m_{0}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
pointwise by

m_{0}(x,V):=m_{V}\left(\frac{x}{|\mathsf{E}(V)\mathbf{1}-\mathsf{P}(V)V|^{2}}\left(\mathsf{E}(V)\mathbf{1}-\mathsf{P}(V)V\right)\right)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
V\in\mathcal{V}
, it follows in turn from (
34
) that

m(X,V)=m_{0}(X\cdot(\mathsf{E}(V)\mathbf{1}-\mathsf{P}(V)V),V)

as claimed in the statement of the Proposition.
∎

We now have that the measure
\mu=M\mathscr{H}
satisfies the identity

\langle T^{t}_{\sigma}\#\mu,\Phi\rangle=\langle\mu,\Phi\rangle

for all
\Phi
satisfying the support condition
\mathscr{H}(\mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\cap\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-})>0
if the density
M
is of the form

M(X,V)=\left(\prod_{i<j}((v_{i}-v_{j})^{2}+(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2})\right)^{-\frac{N-2}{N(N-1)}}m_{0}(X\cdot(\mathsf{E}(V)\mathbf{1}-\mathsf{P}(V))V,V)

for some measurable function
m_{0}
. Moving forward, we define the density
L
pointwise by

L(X,V):=\left(\prod_{i<j}((v_{i}-v_{j})^{2}+(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2})\right)^{-\frac{N-2}{N(N-1)}}.

We shall use this result in Section
4.3
below when we come to derive a PDE that the scattering map
\sigma
of a billiard flow
\{T^{t}_{\sigma}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
satisfies if it is to admit
\lambda:=L\mathscr{H}
(and, in turn,
\mu
) as an invariant measure.

4
Analysis over the Region
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{+}

In this Section, we consider the properties of the billiard maps
T^{t}_{\sigma}
over the region
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{+}
in order to derive a Jacobian equation that any smooth scattering map
\sigma
satisfies on the interior of
\mathcal{V}^{-}
. In addition, we derive an additional symmetry condition on the density
M
which guarantees that the associated measure
\mu
is invariant under the flow
\{T^{t}_{\sigma}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
generated by
\sigma
. We consider only the case that
t>0
in what follows, as we do not seek to derive the second boundary-value problem that the inverse
N
-body scattering map
\sigma^{-1}
satisfies owing to assumption
2.9
above.

4.1
Further Auxiliary Maps and Identities

Suppose now that
\Phi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2N},\mathbb{R})
is such that
\mathscr{H}(\mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\cap\mathcal{M}_{t}^{+})>0
. As was the case when considering the integral (
26
) above, when considering the new integral given by

\int_{T^{-t}_{\sigma}\circ\Psi(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+})}(\Phi\circ T^{t}_{\sigma}\circ\Psi)(M\circ\Psi)\omega\,d\mathscr{L},

(35)

elimination of the composition
T^{t}_{\sigma}\circ\Psi
from the argument of
\Phi
is once again complicated by the fact that the dimension of the range of this composition is greater than its range. As such, in analogy with Section
3
above we also define the
reduced flow map

\widetilde{T}^{t}_{\sigma}
pointwise by

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\widetilde{T}^{t}_{\sigma}(\zeta):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}e^{N+2}_{i}\otimes e^{N+2}_{i}\zeta+\tau(\zeta)e^{N+2}_{i}\otimes e^{N}_{i}\psi(\zeta)+(t-\tau(\zeta))e^{N+2}_{i}\otimes e^{N}_{i}\sigma(\psi(\zeta))\\
\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{2}e^{N+2}_{N+j}\otimes e^{N}_{j}\sigma(\psi(\zeta))\end{array}

for
\zeta\in\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+}
. We note the fact that
\widetilde{T}_{\sigma}^{t}
is a diffeomorphism with inverse map given pointwise by

(\widetilde{T}_{\sigma}^{t})^{-1}(\zeta)=\widetilde{T}_{\sigma}^{-t}(\zeta).

In this case, the reduced flow map is more complicated in structure and it depends explicitly on the scattering map
\sigma
. Unlike the previous section, we also require a
residual flow map

\overline{T}_{\sigma}
given pointwise by

\overline{T}_{\sigma}(\zeta):=\sum_{j=3}^{N}e^{2N}_{N+j}\otimes e_{j}^{N}\sigma(\psi(\zeta))

for
\zeta\in\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+}
. For the purposes of applying the Change of Variables Theorem in the integral (
35
), we require the following result on the structure of the Jacobian of this reduced flow map
\widetilde{T}^{t}_{\sigma}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
N\geq 3
and
t>0
. It holds that

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\mathrm{det}(D\widetilde{T}^{t}_{\sigma}(\zeta))=\\
\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(\psi(\zeta))-\sigma_{2}(\psi(\zeta))}{u_{1}-u_{2}}\left(\frac{x_{1}+tu_{1}-x_{2}-tu_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right)^{N-2}\mathrm{det}(D\sigma(\psi(\zeta)))\end{array}

for all
\zeta\in(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+})^{\circ}
.

Proof.

It can be shown that the derivative matrix
D\widetilde{T}^{t}_{\sigma}:(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+})^{\circ}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{(N+2)\times(N+2)}
is given block-wise by

D\widetilde{T}^{t}_{\sigma}(\zeta)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\begin{array}[]{cc}A(\zeta,t)&B(\zeta,t)\end{array}&C(\zeta,t)\\
D(\zeta,t)&E(\zeta)\\
\begin{array}[]{cc}F(\zeta,t)&G(\zeta,t)\end{array}&H(\zeta,t)\end{array}\right)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+2)\times(N+2)}

for
\zeta\in(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+})^{\circ}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where the matrix-valued map
A(\cdot,t):(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+})^{\circ}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
is defined pointwise by

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle A_{i,j}(\zeta,t):=\\
\displaystyle\delta_{i,j}+(-1)^{i}\left(\frac{u_{j}-\sigma_{j}(\psi(\zeta))}{u_{1}-u_{2}}+\frac{t-\tau(\zeta)}{\tau(\zeta)}\sum_{k=3}^{N}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\widetilde{\delta}_{i,\ell}x_{\ell}-x_{k}\right)\frac{\partial\sigma_{j}}{\partial v_{k}}(\psi(\zeta))\right),\end{array}

the map
B(\cdot,t):(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+})^{\circ}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2\times(N-2)}
is given by

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle B_{i,j}(\zeta,t):=\\
\displaystyle(-1)^{i}\left((u_{1}-u_{2})^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\widetilde{\delta}_{i,\ell}u_{\ell}-\sigma_{j+2}+\frac{t-\tau(\zeta)}{\tau(\zeta)}\sum_{k=3}^{N}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\widetilde{\delta}_{i\ell}x_{\ell}-x_{k}\right)\frac{\partial\sigma_{j+2}}{\partial v_{k}}(\psi(\zeta))\right),\end{array}

the map
C:(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+})^{\circ}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
is given by

C_{i,j}(\zeta):=(-1)^{i}\frac{1}{\tau(\zeta)}\sum_{k=3}^{N}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\widetilde{\delta}_{i\ell}x_{\ell}-x_{k}\right)\frac{\partial\sigma_{j}}{\partial v_{k}},

the map
D(\cdot,t):(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+})^{\circ}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{(N-2)\times N}
is given by

D_{i,j}(\zeta,t):=\frac{x_{1}+tu_{1}-x_{2}-tu_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\frac{\partial\sigma_{j}}{\partial v_{i+2}}(\psi(\zeta))

the map
E:(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+})^{\circ}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{(N-2)\times 2}
is given by

E_{i,j}(\zeta):=-\frac{1}{\tau(\zeta)}\frac{\partial\sigma_{j}}{\partial v_{i+2}}(\psi(\zeta)),

the map
F(\cdot,t):(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+})^{\circ}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
is given by

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle F_{i,j}(\zeta,t):=\\

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
N\geq 3
. Suppose
\sigma\in C^{0}(\mathcal{V}^{-},\mathcal{V}^{+})\cap C^{1}((\mathcal{V}^{-})^{\circ},(\mathcal{V}^{+})^{\circ})

if and only if

is a classical solution of one the second boundary-value problems:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\mathrm{det}(D\sigma(V))=\pm\frac{F(V)}{F(\sigma(V))}\quad\text{for}\hskip 5.69054ptV\in(\mathcal{V}^{-})^{\circ},\\
\displaystyle\sigma(\mathcal{V}^{-})=\mathcal{V}^{+},\end{array}\right.

where

is defined in (
13

Proof.

We consider the proof of sufficiency to begin. Let
E\subset\mathcal{M}
be a bounded measurable set, and let
\{\Phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2N},\mathbb{R})
denote any sequence with the property that

\lambda(E)=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\Phi_{j}\,d\lambda.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
be given and fixed, and let
\sigma
be an
N
-body scattering map of class
C^{0}(\mathcal{V}^{-},\mathcal{V}^{+})\cap C^{1}((\mathcal{V}^{-})^{\circ},(\mathcal{V}^{+})^{\circ})
. We have that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(T^{t}_{\sigma}\#\lambda)(E)&=&\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\displaystyle\langle T^{t}_{\sigma}\#\lambda,\Phi_{j}\rangle\\
&=&\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{M}^{\circ}}\Phi_{j}(T^{t}_{\sigma}(Z))\,d\lambda(Z)\\
&=&\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\underbrace{\int_{T^{-t}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-})}\Phi_{j}(T^{t}_{\sigma}(Z))\,d\lambda(Z)}_{I_{t}(\Phi_{j}):=}+\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\underbrace{\int_{T^{-t}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{M}_{t}^{+})}\Phi(T^{t}_{\sigma}(Z))\,d\lambda(Z)}_{J_{t}(\Phi_{j}):=}\end{array}

by Assumption
2.9
. Let us focus on the first of these integrals. Now,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}I_{t}(\Phi_{j})&=&\displaystyle\int_{T_{\sigma}^{-t}(\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-})}\Phi_{j}(T^{t}_{\sigma}(Z))\,d\lambda(Z)\\
&=&\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-})}\Phi_{j}(\widetilde{T}^{t}(\zeta),\overline{\psi}(\zeta))L(\zeta,\overline{\psi}(\zeta))\omega(\zeta)d\mathscr{L}(\zeta)\\
&=&\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}}\Phi_{j}(\Psi(\zeta))L(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta),\overline{\psi}(\zeta))\omega(\widetilde{T}^{-t}(\zeta))\left|\frac{x_{1}-tu_{1}-x_{2}+tu_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right|^{N-2}\,d\mathscr{L}(\zeta),\end{array}

and so by Lemma
3.6
it holds that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}I_{t}(\Phi_{j})&=&\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}}\Phi_{j}(\Psi(\zeta))L(\Psi(\zeta))\omega(\zeta)\,d\mathscr{L}(\zeta)\\
&=&\displaystyle\int_{\Psi(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-})}\Phi_{j}(Z)L(Z)\,d\mathscr{H}(Z)\\
&=&\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-}}\Phi_{j}\,d\lambda.\end{array}

Let us now consider the second integral
J_{t}(\Phi_{j})
. Indeed, by way of similar calculations, it holds that

J_{t}(\Phi_{j})=\int_{\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+}}\Phi_{j}(\Psi(\zeta))L(\Psi(\widetilde{T}^{-t}_{\sigma}(\zeta)))\omega(\widetilde{T}^{-t}_{\sigma}(\zeta))\left|\mathrm{det}(D\widetilde{T}^{t}_{\sigma}(\widetilde{T}^{-t}_{\sigma}(\zeta)))\right|^{-1}d\mathscr{L}(\zeta).

By Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, it holds that

\mathrm{det}(D\widetilde{T}^{t}_{\sigma}(\widetilde{T}^{-t}_{\sigma}(\zeta)))^{-1}=\frac{L(\Psi(\zeta))\omega(\zeta)}{L(\Psi(\widetilde{T}^{-t}_{\sigma}(\zeta)))\omega(\widetilde{T}^{-t}_{\sigma}(\zeta))}

for all
\zeta\in\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+}
if and only if

\mathrm{det}(D\widetilde{T}^{t}_{\sigma}(\zeta))^{-1}=\frac{L(\Psi(\widetilde{T}^{t}_{\sigma}(\zeta)))\omega(\widetilde{T}^{t}_{\sigma}(\zeta))}{L(\Psi(\zeta))\omega(\zeta)}

(43)

4

\mathrm{det}(D\sigma(\psi(\zeta)))=\pm\left|\frac{\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}}{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right|^{N-2}\left|\frac{u_{1}-u_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\right|\left(\sum_{i<j}(\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j})^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus, by the assumption that
\sigma
is a classical solution of one of the second boundary-value problems in the statement of the Theorem, it holds that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}J_{t}(\Phi_{j})&=&\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}_{t}^{+}}\Phi_{j}(\Psi(\zeta))L(\Psi(\zeta))\omega(\zeta)d\mathscr{L}(\zeta)\\

As a result, we conclude that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(T^{t}_{\sigma}\#\lambda)(E)&=&\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}I_{t}(\Phi_{j})+\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}J_{t}(\Phi_{j})\\

Proposition 5.1

We begin by adopting the ansatz for

, we seek a representation of the matrix

of the form

\lambda_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
for

and vectors
\{\widehat{a}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
which constitute an orthonomal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The conservation of momentum yields an eigenpair of

. Indeed,

\mathsf{P}(\sigma(V))=\mathsf{P}(V)

if and only if

(A^{T}\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{1})\cdot V=0

(44)

for all
V\in\mathcal{V}^{-}
. However, as

it follows that identity (
44
) holds for
all

V\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By separation,
A^{T}
admits
\mathbf{1}
as an eigenvector with corresponding eigenvalue
1
.

The remaining
N-1
eigenpairs of
A
may be inferred from the fact that
\sigma
maps the polytope
\mathcal{V}^{-}
onto the polytope
\mathcal{V}^{+}
. Indeed, we recall that

\sigma(V)\cdot E_{i}^{N}\geq 0\quad\text{whenever}\quad V\cdot E_{i}^{N}\leq 0

where
E_{i}^{N}=e_{i}^{N}-e_{i+1}^{N}
, which holds if and only if

A^{T}E_{i}^{N}\cdot V\geq 0\quad\text{whenever}\quad E_{i}^{N}\cdot V\leq 0

for
i\in\{1,...,N-1\}
. By a contradiction argument, it follows that

A^{T}E_{i}^{N}=\alpha_{i}E_{i}^{N}

for some
\alpha_{i}<0
. However, it must hold that
\alpha_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
, whence
\alpha_{i}=-1
for
i\in\{1,...,N-1\}
. It may be checked that

\{\mathbf{1},E_{1}^{N},...,E_{N-1}^{N}\}

(45)

constitutes a linearly independent set of vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Noting that
A^{2}=I_{N}
and
A^{T}A=I_{N}
, it follows that
A=A^{T}
. As such, the conservation of linear momentum, kinetic energy, and the range mapping condition
A\mathcal{V}^{-}=\mathcal{V}^{+}
completely determine the matrix
A
. By applying the Gram-Schmidt algorithm to the linearly independent set of vectors (
45
), one finds that

A^{\star}:=\frac{2}{N}\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}e_{k}^{N}\otimes e_{k}^{N},

from which follows the claim of the Proposition.
∎

5.1
A Linear Classical Solution of the Second Boundary-value Problem

We now show that the unique linear
N
-body scattering map that conserves both momentum and energy is a classical solution of the second boundary-value problem for the Jacobian equation. As a result of this, it follows immediately that
\sigma^{\star}
generates a billiard flow
\{T^{t}_{\star}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
with the property that
T^{t}_{\star}\#\lambda=\lambda
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In what follows, the sign adopted in the Jacobian PDE depends on the parity of the dimension
N
with which we work.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose
N\geq 3
. The map
\sigma^{\star}
is a classical solution of the second boundary-value problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\mathrm{det}(D\sigma(V))=(-1)^{N-1}\frac{H(V)}{H(\sigma(V))}\quad\text{for}\hskip 5.69054ptV\in(\mathcal{V}^{-})^{\circ},\\
\sigma(\mathcal{V}^{-})=\mathcal{V}^{+}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

It has already been shown above that the
N
-body scattering map
\sigma^{\star}
satisfies the range condition
\sigma^{\star}(\mathcal{V}^{-})=\mathcal{V}^{+}
. All that remains to be shown is that the smooth map
\sigma^{\star}
satisfies the PDE

\mathrm{det}(D\sigma^{\star}(V))=(-1)^{N-1}\frac{H(V)}{H(\sigma^{\star}(V))}

for all
V\in(\mathcal{V}^{-})^{\circ}
. We begin by noting that

\left(\frac{2}{N}\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}e_{k}^{N}\otimes e_{k}^{N}\right)V\cdot(e_{i}^{N}-e_{j}^{N})=-v_{i}+v_{j},

whence
\sigma_{i}^{\star}(V)-\sigma_{j}^{\star}(V)=-v_{i}+v_{j}
and in turn that

H(\sigma^{\star}(V))=H(V)

for all
V\in\mathcal{V}^{-}
. We also recall that the matrix
A^{\star}
which characterises the linear map
\sigma^{\star}
satisfies

A^{\star}\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}

as well as

A^{\star}E_{i}^{N}=-E_{i}^{N}

for
i\in\{1,...,N-1\}
. As the determinant of
A^{\star}
equals the product of its eigenvalues, it holds that
\mathrm{det}(A^{\star})=(-1)^{N-1}
for all
N\geq 3
and in turn that

\mathrm{det}(D\sigma^{\star}(V))=(-1)^{N-1}

for all
V\in\mathcal{V}^{-}
. The statement of the Proposition follows therefrom.
∎

On Generalizations of the Nonwindowed Scattering Transform
Albert Chua, Matthew Hirn, Anna Little

Abstract
In this paper, we generalize finite depth wavelet scattering transforms, which we formulate as 𝐋 q ​ ( ℝ n ) \mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) norms of a cascade of continuous wavelet transforms (or dyadic wavelet transforms) and contractive nonlinearities. We then provide norms for these operators, prove that these operators are well-defined, and are Lipschitz continuous to the action of C 2 C^{2} diffeomorphisms in specific cases. Lastly, we extend our results to formulate an operator invariant to the action of rotations R ∈ SO ​ ( n ) R\in\text{SO}(n) and an operator that is equivariant to the action of rotations of R ∈ SO ​ ( n ) R\in\text{SO}(n) .

We first provide a review of scattering transforms to motivate this paper. Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a low pass filter (
\hat{\phi}(0)\neq 0
) ,
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
a suitable mother wavelet (
\hat{\psi}(0)=0
), and
G^{+}
be a set of “positive” rotations with determinant
1
. Define a set of rotations and dilations by

\Lambda_{J}:=\{\lambda=2^{j}r:r\in G^{+},j>-J\}\text{ if }J\neq\infty

(1)

and

\Lambda_{\infty}:=\{2^{j}r:r\in G^{+},\,j\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

(2)

Let
\lambda=2^{j}r\in\Lambda_{J}
. Consider the operator

U[\lambda]=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(u)2^{nj}\psi(2^{j}r^{-1}(x-u))\,du\right|

(3)

For a tuple of rotations and dilations in
\Lambda_{J}
, define a path of length
m
as the tuple
p:=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})
and let
\mathcal{P}_{J}
be the set of all finite paths. The scattering propagator for
f\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
p\in\mathcal{P}_{J}
is

U[p]f:=U[\lambda_{m}]\cdots U[\lambda_{1}]f,

(4)

which gathers high frequency information via a cascade of wavelet transforms and nonlinearities. The scattering operator is

\overline{S}{f}(p)=\frac{1}{\mu_{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}U[p]f(x)\,dx

(5)

with
\mu_{p}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}U[p]\delta(x)\,dx.
Additionally, to aggregate features similar to pooling, the author of
[
5
]
define the scattering operator for
f\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
p\in\mathcal{P}_{J}
as

S_{J}[p]f(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}U[p]f(u)2^{-nJ}\phi(2^{-J}(x-u))\,du.

(6)

Additionally, the windowed scattering transform is the set of functions

S_{J}[\mathcal{P}_{J}]f=\{S_{J}[p]f\}_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{J}}.

(7)

This operator is similar to a convolution neural network because along each path (analogous to each layer of a convolutional neural network) a convolution, a nonlinearity is applied, and feature aggregation occurs via the low pass filter. The scattering norm for any set of paths
\Omega
is

\|S_{J}[\Omega]f\|^{2}=\sum_{p\in\Omega}\|S_{J}[p]f\|^{2}_{2}.

(8)

Under very stringent conditions on the mother wavelet, the author of
[
5
]
was able to prove an isometry property for the windowed scattering transform. However, the problem with the admissibility condition in
[
5
]
is that there are very few classes of wavelets that are admissible. The author of
[
5
]
mentions an analytic cubic spline Battle-Lemarié wavelet is admissible in one dimension, but provides no other examples. On a related note,
[
11
]
has shown that scattering coefficients have exponential decay for
n=1
under relatively mild assumptions, but her proof only applies for
n=1
, which makes the admissibility condition still necessary for
n\geq 2
. Additionally, to our knowledge, there are no examples in the literature of wavelets that satisfy the admissibility condition when
n>1
.

The windowed scattering transform has three important properties that are helpful for certain machine learning tasks. The first two are the following:

1.

The windowed scattering transform is a well-defined mapping on
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and nonexpansive. In particular, for all
f,h\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\|S_{J}[P_{J}]f-S_{J}[P_{J}]h\|\leq\|f-h\|_{2}.

(9)

2.

Let the translation of a function be denoted as
L_{c}f(u)=f(u-c).
For certain classes of wavelets, we have

\lim_{J\to\infty}\|S_{J}[P_{J}]f-S_{J}[P_{J}]L_{c}f\|=0

(10)

for all
c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for all
f\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. One can think of this as local translation invariance.

Finally, for the last property, the following definition was used in
[
5
]
for Lipschitz continuity to the action of
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space,
\tau\in C^{2}
, and define the operator
L_{\tau}f(x)=f(x-\tau(x))
. A translation invariant operator
\Phi
is said to be Lipschitz continuous to the action of
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms if for any compact
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists
C_{\Omega}
such that for all
f\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
supported in
\Omega
and all
\tau\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\|\Phi(f)-\Phi(L_{\tau}f)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C_{\Omega}\left(\|D\tau\|_{\infty}+\|D^{2}\tau\|_{\infty}\right)\|f\|_{2}.

(11)

The idea is that the difference in norm is proportional to the size of
\|D\tau\|_{\infty}+\|D^{2}\tau\|_{\infty}
, which indicates how much
L_{\tau}
deforms
f
. In particular, the author of
[
5
]
show that (
11
) holds for the windowed scattering transform.

The concept of stability to diffeomorphisms has become a major point of study after the publication of
[
5
]
. Based on the definition above, there has been a lot of interest in exploring the stability of various operators related to machine learning and data science. For example,
[
7
,
12
]
extend the scattering transform and stability of the scattering transform to graphs and compact Riemannian manifolds, respectively; the authors in
[

]

. Other papers explore stability for different operators with desirable properties for machine learning

,

,

]
.

Although much work has appeared in recent years about operators similar to the scattering transform and about generalizations of the scattering transform, there are still some loose ends left in
[
5
]
that have not been explored yet. First, while the author of
[
5
]
does explore creating a norm for the nonwindowed scattering transform, he does not actually prove the norm is stable to diffeomorphisms.
We consider a less stringent definition for stability to diffeomorphisms in the same spirit as the definition in
[
5
]
for this paper. Let
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be normed vector spaces. Then we say that a translation invariant operator
\Phi:V_{1}\to V_{2}
is said to be Lipschitz continuous to the action of
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms if for any compact
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists
C_{\Omega,\tau}
such that for all
f\in V_{1}
supported in
\Omega
and all
\tau\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\|\Phi(f)-\Phi(L_{\tau}f)\|_{V_{2}}\leq C_{\Omega,\tau}\|f\|_{V_{1}},

(12)

where
C_{\Omega,\tau}\to 0
as
\|D\tau\|_{\infty}+\|D^{2}\tau\|_{\infty}\to 0.
Like with equation (
11
),
\|\Phi(f)-\Phi(L_{\tau}f)\|_{V_{2}}
depends on
\|D\tau\|_{\infty}+\|D^{2}\tau\|_{\infty}
.

Using this definition, we consider a slightly different problem than the author of
[
5
]
did for the nonwindowed scattering transform. The scattering transform introduced in
[
5
]
was a collection of
\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norms of various cascades of dyadic wavelet convolutions and modulus nonlinearities applied to a signal. Here, we extend the definition of the scattering transform to the continuous wavelet transform and for
\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norms with
q\in[1,2]
. For a continuous dilation parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
we define the dilations of
\psi
as:

\forall\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\,,\quad\psi_{\lambda}(x):=\lambda^{-n/2}\psi(\lambda^{-1}x)\,,

which preserves the
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of
\psi
:

\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{2}=\|\psi\|_{2}\,,\quad\forall\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\,.

For the continuous wavelet transform, the one layer wavelet scattering transform with
\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm is the function
S_{\text{cont},q}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined as:

\forall\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\,,\quad S_{\text{cont},q}f(\lambda):=\|f\ast\psi_{\lambda}\|_{q}\,.

(13)

For a dyadic dilation parameter
j\in\mathbb{Z}
we define dilations of
\psi
as:

\forall\,j\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\quad\psi_{j}(x)=2^{-nj}\psi(2^{-j}x)\,,

which preserves the
\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of
\psi
:

\|\psi_{j}\|_{1}=\|\psi\|_{1}\,,\quad\forall\,j\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

The one layer wavelet scattering transform for the dyadic wavelet transform is the function
S_{\text{dyad},q}f:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined as:

\forall\,j\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\quad S_{\text{dyad},q}f(j):=\|f\ast\psi_{j}\|_{q}\,.

(14)

More generally, the
m
-layer wavelet scattering transforms
S_{\text{cont},q}^{m}f:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
S_{\text{dyad},q}^{m}f:\mathbb{Z}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are defined as

\displaystyle S_{\text{cont},q}^{m}f(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})

\displaystyle:=\|||f\ast\psi_{\lambda_{1}}|\ast\psi_{\lambda_{2}}|\ast\cdots|\ast\psi_{\lambda_{m}}\|_{q}\,,

(15)

\displaystyle S_{\text{dyad},q}^{m}f(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})

\displaystyle:=\|||f\ast\psi_{j_{1}}|\ast\psi_{j_{2}}|\ast\cdots|\ast\psi_{j_{m}}\|_{q}\,.

(16)

This is similar to working with a windowed scattering transform with a finite number of layers. However, our operator is different from the operator
S_{J}
in
[
5
]
because it does not contain the filter
A_{J}
to aggregate low frequency information, so the scale parameter in our formulation is not bounded above or below. Additionally, because the averaging filter is replaced
\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norms, our representation is fully translation invariant rather than translation invariant as
J\to\infty
.

As for the significance of using
\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norms to replace the averaging filter, there is one area with direct application: quantum energy regression tasks
[
8
]
, where a representation that is similar to the rotation invariant representation in Section 6.2 has already been used for quantum energy regression.

Given a configuration of atoms, we would like to estimate the ground state energy of the configuration. Suppose we have a molecule with
K
atoms with nuclear charges
z_{k}
and nuclear positions
p_{k}
with
k=1,\ldots,K
. The state
x
of a molecule is given by

x=\{(p_{k},z_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}\,:\,k=1\ldots,K\},

(17)

Due to how we have defined our state, we would like our representation to have the following properties:

1.

Permutation Invariance
: the energy should not depend on the index of the molecules.

2.

Deformation Stability
: small deformations of the molecule should only lead to small changes in energy of the system.

3.

Isometry Invariance
: the energy should be invariant to group actions such as translations, rotations, and other general isometries.

4.

Multiscale Interactions
: molecules have many interactions terms, and these interaction terms depend on the pairwise distance between atoms (i.e. short range covalent bonds and longer range Van Der Waals interactions).

The rotation invariant version of our scattering transform in section 6 satisfies permutation invariance, deformation stability, and has multiscale interactions based on the proofs we’ve provided. We do not prove isometry invariance, but the operator is rotation and translation invariant.

Motivated by DFT theory, the paper
[
8
]
uses a dictionary of one and two layer scattering norms with
q=1
and
q=2
to get (at the time) state-of-the-art results for energy regression tasks for planar molecules. In particular, scattering operators with
q=1
scaled with the number of atoms in the system and
q=2
encoded pairwise interactions. The motivation for using
1<q<2
comes from
[
9
,
10
]
, which based on the Thomas–Fermi–Dirac–von Weizsäcker model
[
18
]
, also use scattering norms with
q=4/3,5/3
. Later papers, like
[
9
,
10
]
, use a similar representation, involving spherical harmonics, for 3D quantum energy regression.

Generalizing to stochastic processes, one can also consider scattering moments
[
5
,
19
]
, which have similar desirable properties as the nonwindowed scattering transform. Applications include, but are not limited to, audio texture synthesis
[
19
]
and cosmology
[
20
]
. The main idea in all these applications is that the nonwindowed scattering transform has desirable mathematical properties and provides a small number of relevant descriptors for high dimensional, complicated data.

Remark 1
.

We can replace all the modulus operators with any contraction mapping (or use different contraction mappings in each layer) in the definition above, and all the proofs in the rest of this paper will still work. In particular, the modulus can be replaced with a complex version of the rectified linear unit (ReLU) nonlinearity,
\max(0,\text{Re}(a_{i}))_{i=1,\ldots,n}
for
a\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, which is a popular choice for complex neural networks. Nonetheless, we will use the modulus operator throughout this paper without any loss of generality.

We provide a general roadmap for this paper. Section 2 will cover notation, basic properties about wavelets and the wavelet scattering operator, and harmonic analysis that will be necessary for the paper. In Section 3, we provide norms for an
m
-layer wavelet scattering transforms and prove that the operators are well defined mappings into specific spaces when
1\leq q\leq 2
. For Section 4, we explore conditions under which the
m
-layer scattering transform is stable to dilations, and we generalize our results to diffeomorphisms in Section 5. Lastly, in Section 6, we formulate two new translation invariant operators that are stable to diffeomorphisms. The first is rotation equivariant, and the second is rotation invariant. Our contributions include, but are not limited to, the following:

1.

We formulate an extension of the dyadic wavelet scattering operator for a finite, arbitrary number of layers with parameter
q\in[1,2]
by applying
\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norms instead of
\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norms. Additionally, we formulate a wavelet scattering operator with
q\in[1,2]
that uses a continuous scale parameter, like the continuous wavelet transform.

2.

We create a new finite depth scattering norm using dyadic and continuous scales in the case when
q\in[1,2]
, and prove that the mappings are well defined and provide theoretical justification for a broader class of wavelets that make the scattering transform Lipchitz continuous to the action of
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms. However, the trade-off is that our stability bound depends on the number of layers.

3.

We provide a condition for norm equivalence in the case of
q=2
that is less stringent.

4.

In the case of
q\in(1,2]
, we prove that our norm is stable to diffeomorphisms
\tau\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
provided that
\|\tau\|_{\infty}<\tfrac{1}{2n}
and the wavelet and its first and second partial derivatives have sufficient decay. In the case of
q=1
, we show stability to dilations.

5.

We extend our formulation to include invariance or equivariance to the action of rotations
R\in\text{SO}(n)
.

2
Notation and Basic Properties

We start by providing basic notation that we will use in this paper and proceed to give basic definitions and properties that will be necessary for our results.

2.1
Function Spaces

Set
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to be the positive real numbers, i.e.
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=(0,\infty).
The gradient of a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is given by
\nabla f
, the Jacobian of a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is given by
Df
, and the Hessian is given by
D^{2}f
. For
1\leq q<\infty
, the
\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is
\|f\|_{q}:=\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{q}\,dx\right]^{1/q}.
When
q=\infty
,
\|f\|_{\infty}:=\text{ess sup}|f|.
We will also use the notation,
\|\Delta f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)-f(y)|
, which should not be mistaken for applying a Laplacian operator. Greek letters with a vector symbol, such as
\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})
, will be a multi-index of nonnegative integers; additionally, we write
|\vec{\alpha}|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
, and the usage will be clear from context. The operator
D^{\vec{\alpha}}
is a multi-index of derivatives:
D^{\vec{\alpha}}f=\frac{\partial^{|\vec{\alpha}|}}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}}f
. For integer
s\geq 0
, we define the function space

\mathbf{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{f\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):D^{\vec{\alpha}}f\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ for }|\vec{\alpha}|\leq s\}.

The Fourier transform of a function
f\in\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the function
\widehat{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined as:

\forall\,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,\quad\widehat{f}(\omega):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)e^{-ix\cdot\omega}\,dx\,.

The Hilbert transform of a function
f\in\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is denoted by
Hf
and is defined as:

Hf(x):=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{|x-y|>\epsilon}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\,dy\,.

The map
H
is a convolution operator in which
f
is convolved against the function
1/x
. We note that

H:\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R})\,,\quad\forall\,1<q<\infty\,,

however the result is not true for
q=1
, i.e., if
f\in\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
it is not necessarily true that
Hf\in\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. We thus introduce the Hardy space. We denote the Hardy space as
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and it consists of those functions
f\in\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that
Hf\in\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
as well. For
f\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
the Hardy space norm is
\|f\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}
, which we define as (see Corollary 2.4.7 of
[
21
]
)

\|f\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}:=\|f\|_{1}+\|Hf\|_{1}\,.

(18)

One can show that if
f\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, then
f
must necessarily have zero average. An important property of the Hilbert transform and convolution is the following:

H(f\ast g)=Hf\ast g=f\ast Hg\,,\quad f\in\mathbf{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R})\,,\,g\in\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R})\,,\quad 1<\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\,.

We have a similar definition for Hardy spaces when
n\geq 2
. For
1\leq j\leq n
, define the
j^{\text{th}}
Riesz transform as

R_{j}f(x)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{|x-y|>\varepsilon}\frac{x_{j}-y_{j}}{|x-y|^{n+1}}f(y)\,dy\,,

(19)

where
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
. The Hardy space
f\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of functions
f
such that
f\in\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
R_{j}f\in\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1\leq j\leq n
as well.
For
f\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the Hardy space norm is
\|f\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, which we define as (see Corollary 2.4.7 of
[
21
]
)

\|f\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|f\|_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|R_{j}f\|_{1}\,.

(20)

2.2
Wavelets

We let
\psi\in\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a wavelet, which means it is a function that is localized in both space and frequency and has zero average, i.e.,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(x)\,du=0\,.

Assume
f\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The continuous wavelet transform
\mathcal{W}f\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
is defined as:

\forall\,(x,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\,,\quad\mathcal{W}f(x,\lambda):=f\ast\psi_{\lambda}(x)\,.

Furthermore, if
\psi
satisfies the following admissibility condition

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|\widehat{\psi}(\lambda\omega)|^{2}}{\lambda}d\lambda=\mathcal{C}_{\psi}\,,\quad\forall\,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\,,

(21)

for some
\mathcal{C}_{\psi}>0
, then we will say that
\psi
is a Littlewood-Paley wavelet for the continuous wavelet transform. If
\psi
satisfies (
21
), one can show that the norm
\mathcal{W}f
computed with a weighted measure
(dx,d\lambda/\lambda^{n+1})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
is well defined:

\|\mathcal{W}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathcal{W}f(x,\lambda)|^{2}\,dx\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f\ast\psi_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}\,dx\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\|f\ast\psi_{\lambda}\|_{2}^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\,.

We note, in fact, that one can show:

\|\mathcal{W}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}=\beta\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\psi}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\,.

where

\beta=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1/2&\text{if }\psi\text{ is real valued}\\
1&\text{if }\psi\text{ is complex valued}\end{array}\right.\,.

(22)

For a function
f\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we define the dyadic wavelet transform
Wf\in\mathbf{\ell}^{2}(\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
as

Wf=\left(f\ast\psi_{j}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\,.

If
\psi
satisfies

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{\psi}(2^{j}\omega)|^{2}=\hat{C}_{\psi},\quad\forall\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\,,

(23)

for some
\hat{C}_{\psi}>0
, then we will say that
\psi
is a Littlewood-Paley wavelet for the dyadic wavelet transform. If
\psi
satisfies (
23
), one can show that the norm
Wf
given below is well defined:

\|Wf\|_{\mathbf{\ell}^{2}(\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{2}:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f\ast\psi_{j}\|_{2}^{2}\,.

In fact, we have the following norm equivalence:

\|Wf\|_{\mathbf{\ell}^{2}(\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{2}=\beta\cdot\hat{C}_{\psi}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\,,

where
\beta
is defined in (
22
).

2.3
Operator Valued Spaces

Consider a Banach space
\mathcal{B}
. Suppose
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathcal{B}
and
x\to\|f(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}
is measurable in the Lebesgue sense. Define
\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1\leq p<\infty
to be

\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|f(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{p}\,dx\,.

Also, for
1\leq p<\infty
, define

\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}_{\mathcal{B}}^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\sup_{\delta>0}\delta\cdot m(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|f(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}>\delta\})^{1/p}\,.

We also have the following relation:

\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}_{\mathcal{B}}^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,.

Note that for
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|f(x)\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx=\|f\|_{p}^{p}\,.

3
Wavelet Scattering is a Bounded Operator

In this section we explore for which
q>0
and
m\geq 1
the wavelet scattering transforms
S_{\text{cont},q}^{m}f
and
S_{\text{dyad},q}^{m}f
are well-defined as functions in some Banach space (i.e., have finite norm), and under what circumstances.

Let
\psi
be a wavelet. We assume that
\psi
has the following properties:

|\psi(x)|\leq A(1+|x|)^{-n-\varepsilon}

(24)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\psi(x-y)-\psi(x)|\,dx\leq A|y|^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\,,

(25)

for some constants
A,\varepsilon^{\prime},\varepsilon>0
and for all
h\neq 0
.

Consider the Littlewood-Paley
G
-function

G_{\psi}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{(0,\infty)}|f\ast t^{-n}\psi(x/t)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/2}\,.

(26)

Let
\mathcal{B}=\mathbf{L}^{2}\left((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t}\right)
. We can rewrite this as a Bochner integral by considering the function
K(x)=(t^{-n/2}\psi_{t}(x))_{t>0}
. This is a mapping
K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathcal{B}
and the function
x\to\|K(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}
is measurable. Also, if we let

\mathcal{T}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}t^{-n/2}\psi_{t}(x-y)f(y)\,dy\right)_{t>0}=\left((t^{-n/2}\psi_{t}\ast f)(x)\right)_{t>0}\,,

we observe that

G_{\psi}(f)(x)=\|\mathcal{T}(f)(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}

and

\|G_{\psi}(f)\|_{p}^{p}=\|\mathcal{T}(f)\|_{L^{p}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\,.

From Problem 6.1.4 of
[
22
]
, the two properties above for the wavelet
\psi
imply that

\|K(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}\leq\frac{c_{n}A}{|x|^{n}}\,,

(27)

and

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{|x|\geq 2|y|}\|K(x-y)-K(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}dx\leq c_{n}^{\prime}A\,,

(28)

where
c_{n}
and
c_{n}^{\prime}
depend only on
n
,
\varepsilon
, and
\varepsilon^{\prime}
. We will omit the dependence on
\varepsilon
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}
throughout the rest of this paper, and this will have no effect on any of our proofs.

Remark 2
.

For the rest of this paper, we will write
G
in place of
G_{\psi}
when referring to the
G
-function because the dependence on the mother wavelet is clear.

Remark 3
.

Note that (
25
) holds under the alternative condition

|\nabla\psi(x)|\leq A(1+|x|)^{-n-1-\epsilon^{\prime}}.

(29)

This is a consequence of Mean Value Theorem.

We have the following result taken from Problem 6.1.4 of
[
22
]
and from Chapter V of
[
23
]
.

Lemma 1

(
[
22
,
23
]
)
.

Assume that
\psi
is defined as above and satisfies (
27
) and (
28
). Then the operator
G
is bounded from
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Also, for
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\mathcal{B}=\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dt/t)
, we have

\|\mathcal{T}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{n}A\max(p,(p-1)^{-1})\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

(30)

for some
C_{n}
. For all
f\in\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we also have

\|\mathcal{T}f\|_{\mathbf{L}_{\mathcal{B}}^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{n}^{\prime}A\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(31)

and

\|\mathcal{T}f\|_{\mathbf{L}_{\mathcal{B}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{n}^{\prime}A\|f\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

(32)

for some
C_{n}^{\prime}
.

Remark 4
.

We can also formulate similar bounds for the Littlewood-Paley
\mathfrak{g}
operator

\mathfrak{g}(f)(x):=\left[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\psi_{j}\ast f(x)|^{2}\right]^{1/2}

(33)

using similar arguments.

Remark 5
.

Let
\psi
be a wavelet that has properties (
24
) and (

). Then with the
\mathbf{L}^{2}
normalized dilations, the Littlewood-Paley
G
-function can be written as:

\displaystyle G(f)(x)

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}|f\ast\psi_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right]^{1/2}\,.

Note that the

.

3.1
The
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
Wavelet Scattering Transform

In this subsection we prove the
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
scattering transforms are bounded operators. More specifically, we prove that
S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}:\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})
, where
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})
has the weighted measure defined by

\|S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}^{2}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}|S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})|^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}

and we show that
\|S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. We also show that
S_{\text{dyad},2}^{m}:\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbf{\ell}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})
, where

\|S_{\text{dyad},2}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{\ell}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})}^{2}:=\sum_{j_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}\ldots\sum_{j_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}|S_{\text{dyad},2}^{m}f(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})|^{2}.

Proposition 2
.

For any wavelet satisfying (
24
) and (
25
), we have
S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}:\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+})
and
S_{\text{dyad},2}^{m}:\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})
.

Proof.

The proof of the dyadic case is essentially identical to the proof given below and is thus omitted. The case of
m=1
follows by an application of Fubini’s Theorem:

\displaystyle\|S_{\text{cont},2}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\|f\ast\psi_{\lambda}\|_{2}^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(f\ast\psi_{\lambda})(x)|^{2}\,dx\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|G(f)(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|^{2}_{2}

by boundedness of the G-function.
Now we proceed by using induction. Assume that we have
\|S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}^{2}\leq C_{m}\|f\|^{2}_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{W}_{t}f=f\ast\psi_{t}
, define
Mf=|f|
, and
U_{\lambda}=MW_{\lambda}
for notational brevity. Then notice that

\||||f*\psi_{\lambda_{1}}|*\psi_{\lambda_{2}}|*\dots*\psi_{\lambda_{m}}|\ast\psi_{\lambda_{m+1}}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\mathcal{W}_{\lambda_{m+1}}U_{\lambda_{m}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f\|_{2}^{2}.

Substituting yields

\displaystyle\|S_{\text{cont},2}^{m+1}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{W}_{\lambda_{m+1}}U_{\lambda_{m}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f\|_{2}^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m+1}}{\lambda_{m+1}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|(U_{\lambda_{m}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\ast\psi_{\lambda_{m+1}}\|_{2}^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{m+1}}{\lambda_{m+1}^{n+1}}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}\|U_{\lambda_{m}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}\|U_{\lambda_{m}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f\|_{2}^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}|S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})|^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{m+1}\|f\|_{2}^{2},

where we used the induction hypothesis in the last line. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3
.

Suppose
\psi
is a Littlewood-Paley wavelet satisfying (
24
) and (
25
). Then
S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f:\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+})
and specifically
\lVert S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f\rVert_{1}=C_{\psi}^{m}\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Also,
S_{\text{dyad},2}^{m}:\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})
and
\lVert S_{\text{dyad},2}^{m}f\rVert_{1}=\hat{C}_{\psi}^{m}\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}
.

Proof.

We only provide the proof of the continuous case again. First consider the case
m=1
. We have:

\displaystyle\lVert S_{\text{cont},2}f\rVert_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\|f\ast\psi_{\lambda}\|_{2}^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\hat{f}\cdot\hat{\psi}_{\lambda}\|_{2}^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\hat{f}(\omega)|^{2}|\hat{\psi}_{\lambda}(\omega)|^{2}\ d\omega\right)\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\hat{\psi}(\lambda\omega)|^{2}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right)|\hat{f}(\omega)|^{2}\ d\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(C_{\psi}|\hat{f}(\omega)|^{2}\right)\ d\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}C_{\psi}\lVert\hat{f}\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=C_{\psi}\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Thus the claim holds for
m=1
. Now assume that it holds through
m
. Then by the inductive hypothesis,

\displaystyle\lVert S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f\rVert_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}\|||f*\psi_{\lambda_{1}}|*\psi_{\lambda_{2}}|*\dots*\psi_{\lambda_{m}}\|_{2}^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}=C_{\psi}^{m}\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Now consider the case of
m+1
. Similar to the previous proposition, we have

\displaystyle\lVert S_{\text{cont},2}^{m+1}f\rVert_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|(U_{\lambda_{m}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\ast\psi_{\lambda_{m+1}}\|_{2}^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{m+1}}{\lambda_{m+1}^{n+1}}\right)\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=C_{\psi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}|S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})|^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=C_{\psi}\lVert S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f\rVert_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}

\displaystyle=C_{\psi}^{m+1}\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Thus, the claim is proven by induction.
∎

3.2
The
\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
Wavelet Scattering Transform

Define the notation
\mathcal{W}_{t}f=f\ast\psi_{t}
,
Mf=|f|
, and
U_{t}=M\mathcal{W}_{t}
. We now try to prove that for
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
S_{\text{cont},1}^{m}:\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})
. The norm for
S_{\text{cont},1}^{m}f
is:

\displaystyle\|S_{\text{cont},1}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}

\displaystyle:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}|S_{\text{cont},1}^{m}f(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{m})|^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\,\frac{d\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}^{n+1}}\,\cdots\,\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|(U_{\lambda_{m-1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\ast\psi_{\lambda_{m}}\right\|_{1}^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\,\frac{d\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}^{n+1}}\,\cdots\,\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}\,.

An analogous result will also hold for the operator
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})
with norm

\|S_{\text{dyad},1}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{\ell}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})}:=\left(\sum_{j_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}\ldots\sum_{j_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}|S_{\text{dyad},1}^{m}f(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Before we begin, we will need an important multiplier property of the individual Riesz Transforms:

\widehat{R_{j}f}(\omega)=-i\frac{\omega_{j}}{|\omega|}\hat{f}(\omega)\,.

(35)

Let
\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
be a multi-index with
n
-elements, and let
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that
\psi
has
k
vanishing moments if for all
|\vec{\alpha}|<k
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\Pi_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\right)\psi(t)dt=0.

(36)

The following lemmas will be necessary.

Lemma 4

(
[
24
]
)
.

Suppose that
\psi
has
N
vanishing moments, let
M>1
be an integer, let
\vec{\alpha}
be defined as before, and let
\vec{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})
be a multi-index. Assume that
\psi
satisfies the following properties:

1.

\psi\in\mathbf{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
s>M+\frac{n}{2}
.

2.

There exists
A>0
and
\epsilon\in[0,1)
such that
\psi
satisfies

|D^{\vec{\alpha}}\psi|\leq A(1+|x|)^{-n-N-|\vec{\alpha}|+\varepsilon}\text{ for }0\leq|\vec{\alpha}|\leq M.

3.

For
0\leq|\vec{\alpha}|\leq M-1
and
|\vec{\beta}|<N+|\vec{\alpha}|
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Pi_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{\beta_{i}}{D}^{\vec{\alpha}}\psi(t)\,dt=0.

Then

|{D}^{\vec{\alpha}}R_{i}\psi(x)|=|R_{i}D^{\vec{\alpha}}\psi(x)|\leq A(1+|x|)^{-n-N-|\vec{\alpha}|+\varepsilon+\delta}

for some
0<\delta<1-\varepsilon
and
D^{\vec{\alpha}}R_{i}\psi
has vanishing moments up to degree
N-1+|\vec{\alpha}|.

An immediate consequence is the following Lemma, which we will provide without proof.

Lemma 5
.

Suppose that
\psi
satisfies the following conditions:

1.

\psi\in\mathbf{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
s>2+\frac{n}{2}
.

2.

There exists
A>0
and
\epsilon\in[0,1)
such that
\psi
satisfies

|D^{\vec{\alpha}}\psi|\leq A(1+|x|)^{-n-2-|\vec{\alpha}|+\varepsilon}\text{ for }0\leq|\vec{\alpha}|\leq 3.

3.

For
0\leq|\vec{\alpha}|\leq 2
and
|\vec{\beta}|<2+|\vec{\alpha}|
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Pi_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{\beta_{i}}{D}^{\vec{\alpha}}\psi(t)\,dt=0.

Then
R_{j}\psi
and all of its first and second partial derivatives have
O((1+|x|)^{-n-1+\eta})
decay for some
\eta\in(0,1)
.

The first implication to take note of is that
R_{j}\psi
is a wavelet with "good" decay of itself and all its first and second partial derivatives. Note that the strict decay on the partial derivatives is necessary for technical reasons in later proofs, but decay on all second partial derivatives can be relaxed for the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\psi
be a wavelet satisfying Lemma
5
and let
S_{\text{cont},1}^{m}
be defined as above. Then for
f\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a constant
C_{m}
such that

\|S_{\text{cont},1}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}\leq C_{m}\|f\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,.

Additionally,
?

\|S_{\text{dyad},1}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{\ell}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})}\leq C_{m}\|f\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

We proceed by induction and only provide a proof for the continuous case because the dyadic case follows by almost identical reasoning. Let
f\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
throughout the proof. By Minkowski’s integral inequality (
[
25
]
, Theorem 202), we have

\displaystyle\|S_{\text{cont},1}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|f\ast\psi_{\lambda}\|_{1}^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f\ast\psi_{\lambda}(x)|\ dx\right)^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f\ast\psi_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(f)(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\|G(f)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

where in the last inequality we used Lemma
1
.

Now we assume that there exists some
m\geq 1
such that

\|S_{\text{cont},1}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}\leq C_{m}\|f\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We have

\displaystyle\|S_{\text{cont},1}^{m+1}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1})}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|(U_{\lambda_{m}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\ast\psi_{\lambda_{m+1}}\right\|_{1}^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\cdots\frac{d\lambda_{m+1}}{\lambda_{m+1}^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|(U_{\lambda_{m}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\ast\psi_{\lambda_{m+1}}\right|\,dx\right)^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\cdots\frac{d\lambda_{m+1}}{\lambda_{m+1}^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|(U_{\lambda_{m}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\ast\psi_{\lambda_{m+1}}\right|^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda_{m+1}}{\lambda_{m+1}^{n+1}}\right]^{1/2}\,dx\right)^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\cdots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(U_{\lambda_{m}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)(x)\,dx\right]^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\cdots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\|G(U_{\lambda_{m}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\|_{1}^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\cdots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\|G(\mathcal{W}_{{\lambda_{m}}}U_{\lambda_{m-1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\|_{1}^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\cdots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}

since the
G
function has a modulus already.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|S_{\text{cont},1}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{W}_{{\lambda_{m}}}U_{\lambda_{m-1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\cdots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}.

Now use the definition of the
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm to write

\|\mathcal{W}_{{\lambda_{m}}}U_{\lambda_{m-1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\mathcal{W}_{{\lambda_{m}}}U_{\lambda_{m-1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|\left(R_{j}\mathcal{W}_{{\lambda_{m}}}\right)(U_{\lambda_{m-1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\right\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Thus, since
R_{j}\mathcal{W}_{{\lambda_{m}}}h=h\ast\left(R_{j}\psi_{\lambda_{m}}\right)
and
R_{j}\psi
wavelet, we can use our induction hypothesis and the previous lemma to get

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{W}_{{\lambda_{m}}}(U_{\lambda_{m-1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\cdots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{W}_{{\lambda_{m}}}(U_{\lambda_{m-1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\cdots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+C\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|\left(R_{j}\mathcal{W}_{{\lambda_{m}}}\right)(U_{\lambda_{m-1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\right\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\cdots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{m+1}\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Thus, the theorem is proved by induction.
∎

The case of
n=1
is a little trickier. We have the following multiplier property for the Hilbert Transform:

\widehat{Hf}(\omega)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}+i\widehat{f}(\omega)&\omega<0\\
-i\widehat{f}(\omega)&\omega>0\end{array}\right.

(37)

Unfortunately, this yields less regularity for
\widehat{Hf}
at the origin without additional assumptions. However, notice that the Hilbert transform commutes with dilations, so in particular:

H(\psi_{\lambda})=H(\psi)_{\lambda}\quad\text{and}\quad H(\psi_{j})=H(\psi)_{j}\,.

Using the calculation of
\widehat{Hf}
in (
37
) we see that

H\psi=-i\psi\,,\quad\text{if }\psi\text{ is complex analytic.}

Thus, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 7
.

Let
\psi
be a complex analytic wavelet such that (
24
) and (
25
) hold. Then for
f\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists a constant
C_{m}
such that

\|S_{\text{cont},1}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}\leq C_{m}\|f\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,.

Additionally,

\|S_{\text{dyad},1}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{\ell}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})}\leq C_{m}\|f\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

3.3
\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
Wavelet Scattering Transform

In this subsection, assume
1<q<2
. We prove that for
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
S_{\text{cont},q}^{m}:\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})
. The norm for
S_{\text{cont},q}^{m}f
is:

\displaystyle\|S_{\text{cont},q}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}^{q}

\displaystyle:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}|S_{\text{cont},q}^{m}f(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{m})|^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\,\frac{d\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}^{n+1}}\,\cdots\,\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\left\|(U_{\lambda_{m-1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\ast\psi_{\lambda_{m}}\right\|_{q}\right)^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\,\frac{d\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}^{n+1}}\,\cdots\,\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right)^{q/2}\,.

There is also an analagous result for

\|S_{\text{dyad},q}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{\ell}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})}^{q}:=\left(\sum_{j_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}\cdots\sum_{j_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}|S_{\text{dyad},q}^{m}f(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{m})|^{2}\right)^{q/2}.

Theorem 8
.

Let
1<q<2
. Also, let
\psi
be a wavelet that satisfies properties (
24
) and (
25
) and let
S_{\text{cont},q}^{m}
and
S_{\text{dyad},q}^{m}
be defined as above. Then there exists a universal constant
C_{m}>0
such that
\|S_{\text{cont},q}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{q}\leq C_{m}\|f\|_{q}^{q}
for all
f\in\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and furthermore
\|S_{\text{dyad},q}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{\ell}^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}^{q}\leq C_{m}\|f\|_{q}^{q}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction and consider the case of
m=1
first. Let
f\in\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For the continuous wavelet transform, we apply Minkowski’s integral inequality:

\displaystyle\|S_{\text{cont},q}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{q}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\|f\ast\psi_{\lambda}\|_{q}\right)^{q}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f\ast\psi_{\lambda}(x)|^{q}\ dx\right)^{2/q}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right]^{q/2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f\ast\psi_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right)^{q/2}\ dx

\displaystyle=\|G(f)\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{q}^{q}.

where in the last inequality we used Theorem
1
.

Now, let us assume that

\|S_{\text{cont},q}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}^{q}\leq C^{m\cdot q}\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{q}\,.

We apply Minkowski’s Integral inequality
[
25
]
to swap and then bound:

\displaystyle\|S_{\text{cont},q}^{m+1}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+1})}^{q}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\left\|(U_{\lambda_{1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\ast\psi_{\lambda_{m+1}}\right\|_{q}\right)^{2/q}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m+1}}{\lambda_{m+1}^{n+1}}\right]^{q/2}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(U_{\lambda_{1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\ast\psi_{\lambda_{m+1}}(x)|^{q}\,dx\right)^{2/q}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m+1}}{\lambda_{m+1}^{n+1}}\right]^{q/2}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(U_{\lambda_{1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\ast\psi_{\lambda_{m+1}}(x)|^{q}\,dx\right)^{2/q}\,\frac{d\lambda_{m+1}}{\lambda_{m+1}^{n+1}}\right]^{\frac{q}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{q}}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right]^{q/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|(U_{\lambda_{1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\ast\psi_{\lambda_{m+1}}(x)|^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda_{m+1}}{\lambda_{m+1}^{n+1}}\right)^{q/2}\,dx\right]^{\frac{2}{q}}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right]^{q/2}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}\|G(U_{\lambda_{1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}f)\|_{q}^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right]^{q/2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{q}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}\|(U_{\lambda_{1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}})f\|_{q}^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right]^{q/2}

\displaystyle=C^{q}\|S_{\text{cont},q}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C^{(m+1)q}\|f\|_{q}^{q}.

This proves the desired claim.
∎

Lemma 9

We make the substitution

\displaystyle L_{\tau}f\ast\psi_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=(1-c)^{-n/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(z)[(1-c)\lambda]^{-n/2}\psi\left(\left[(1-c)\lambda\right]^{-1}\left((1-c)x-z\right)\right)\,dz

\displaystyle=(1-c)^{-n/2}f\ast\psi_{(1-c)\lambda}\left((1-c)x\right)

Lemma 10

Suppose that

\displaystyle\ |\psi(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A}{(1+|x|)^{n+1+\alpha}}\,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle|\nabla\psi(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A}{(1+|x|)^{n+1+\beta}}\,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(41)

\alpha,\beta,\kappa>0

\Psi(x)=(1-c)^{-n/2}\psi_{(1-c)}(x)-\psi(x).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
\alpha<\beta<\kappa<1

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Psi=0

24

\displaystyle|\Psi(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|(1-c)^{-n/2}\psi_{(1-c)}(x)-\psi(x)\right|

\displaystyle=(1-c)^{-n}\left|\psi\left(\frac{x}{(1-c)}\right)-(1-c)^{n}\psi\left(x\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq(1-c)^{-n}\left|\psi\left(\frac{x}{1-c}\right)-\psi\left(\frac{1-c}{1-c}x\right)\right|+(1-c)^{-n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}c^{j}\left|\psi\left(x\right)\right|.

Now use mean value theorem on the first term to choose a point
z

We now use Cauchy-Schwarz to bound the left side:

Since

\displaystyle=(1-t)\frac{x}{1-c}+tx

\displaystyle=\frac{1-t}{1-c}x+\frac{t-tc}{1-c}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1-t+t-tc}{1-c}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1-tc}{1-c}x.

Thus,

\displaystyle|\Psi_{\lambda}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{(1-c)^{n+1}}\frac{A}{\left(1+|x|\right)^{n+\beta}}+\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}c^{j}}{(1-c)^{n+1}}\frac{A}{\left(1+|x|\right)^{n+\alpha}}

\displaystyle|\nabla\Psi(z)|

Theorem 12
.

Suppose that
\psi
is a wavelet that satisfies the conditions of Lemma
10

and
\hat{K}_{n,m}

\|S_{\text{dyad},2}^{m}f-S_{\text{dyad},2}^{m}L_{\tau}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}\leq|c|\cdot\hat{K}_{n,m}\|f\|_{2}

for any
|c|<\tfrac{1}{2n}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
c>0
. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{t}f

\displaystyle=f\ast\psi_{t}

\displaystyle Mf

\displaystyle=|f|

\displaystyle U_{t}

\displaystyle=M\mathcal{W}_{t}

\displaystyle A_{q}f

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{q}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/q}.

It follows that
S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}=A_{2}MW_{\lambda_{m}}U_{\lambda_{m-1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}
. We will also let
V_{m-1}=U_{\lambda_{m-1}}\cdots U_{\lambda_{1}}
, with
V_{0}
being the identity operator, and make a slight abuse of notation by denoting
\mathcal{W}_{\lambda_{m}}
as
\mathcal{W}
. First, we will add and subtract
A_{2}ML_{\tau}\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f
and apply triangle inequality:

\displaystyle\|S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f-S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}L_{\tau}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}

\displaystyle=\|A_{2}M\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f-A_{2}M\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}L_{\tau}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{2}M\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f-A_{2}ML_{\tau}\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}

\displaystyle+\|A_{2}ML_{\tau}\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f-A_{2}M\mathcal{W}V_{m-1},L_{\tau}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}.

We’ll start by bounding the first term. We see that
g=\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f\in\mathbb{\mathbf{L}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

|A_{2}M\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f-A_{2}ML_{\tau}\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f|=\left|\|g\|_{2}-\|L_{\tau}g\|_{2}\right|.

Now use a change of variables:

\|L_{\tau}g\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g((1-c)x)|^{2}\,dx=(1-c)^{-n}\|g\|_{2}^{2}.

It then follows that

\left|\|L_{\tau}g\|_{2}-\|g\|_{2}\right|=\|g\|_{2}\left(\frac{1}{(1-c)^{n/2}}-1\right)\leq\|g\|_{2}\left(\frac{1}{(1-c)^{n}}-1\right).

Taking the scattering norm yields

\displaystyle\|A_{2}M\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f-A_{2}ML_{\tau}\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{(1-c)^{n}}-1\right)^{2}\|S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1-(1-c)^{n}}{(1-c)^{n}}\right)^{2}\|S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{(1-c)^{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}c^{j}\right)^{2}\|S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\left(\frac{2n}{2n-1}\right)^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}c^{j}\right]^{2}\|S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c^{2}\cdot C_{m,n}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

For the second term, apply Minkwoski’s inequality for
2

\displaystyle\|A_{2}ML_{\tau}\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f-A_{2}M\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}L_{\tau}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\|L_{\tau}\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f\|_{2}-\|\mathcal{W}L_{\tau}V_{m-1}f\|_{2}\right|^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\cdots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\|L_{\tau}\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f-\mathcal{W}L_{\tau}V_{m-1}f\|_{2}^{2}\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\cdots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\|A_{2}M[\mathcal{W}V_{m-1},L_{\tau}]f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}.

Now this is a commutator term, and we can now bound:

\displaystyle\|A_{2}M[\mathcal{W}V_{m-1},L_{\tau}]f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\|[\mathcal{W}V_{m-1},L_{\tau}]f\|_{2}^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\cdots\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\||[\mathcal{W}V_{m-1},L_{\tau}]f\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|[\mathcal{W}V_{m-1},L_{\tau}]\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

We examine the commutator term more closely. Without a loss of generality, assume
m\geq 2
. By expanding it, we see that each term contains
[\mathcal{W},L_{\tau}]

\displaystyle\lVert[\mathcal{W}V_{m-1},L_{\tau}]\rVert_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq m\lVert\mathcal{W}\rVert_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{m-1}\lVert M\rVert_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{m-1}\lVert[\mathcal{W},L_{\tau}]\rVert_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{m}\lVert[\mathcal{W},L_{\tau}]\rVert_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Thus, once we bound this quantity appropriately, we will finish the proof. We start by writing

\|[\mathcal{W},L_{\tau}]f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\|(L_{\tau}f)\ast\psi_{\lambda}-L_{\tau}\left(f\ast\psi_{\lambda}\right)\|_{2}^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\,.

By substitution with
z=(1-c)x
and Lemma
9
,

\displaystyle\|(L_{\tau}f)\ast\psi_{\lambda}-L_{\tau}\left(f\ast\psi_{\lambda}\right)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\right(L_{\tau}f\ast\psi_{\lambda}\left)(x)-L_{\tau}\left(f\ast\psi_{\lambda}\right)(x)\right|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|(1-c)^{-n/2}\left(f\ast\psi_{(1-c)\lambda}\right)((1-c)x)-\left(f\ast\psi_{\lambda}\right)((1-c)x)\right|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=(1-c)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|(1-c)^{-n/2}\left(f\ast\psi_{(1-c)\lambda}\right)(z)-\left(f\ast\psi_{\lambda}\right)(z)\right|^{2}\,dz

\displaystyle=(1-c)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f\ast\left((1-c)^{-n/2}\psi_{(1-c)\lambda}-\psi_{\lambda}\right)\right|^{2}\,dz

\displaystyle=(1-c)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\left(f\ast\Psi_{\lambda}\right)(z)\right|^{2}\,dz,

\displaystyle=(1-c)^{-n}\|f\ast\Psi_{\lambda}\|_{2}^{2}\,.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\|(L_{\tau}f)\ast\psi_{\lambda}-L_{\tau}\left(f\ast\psi_{\lambda}\right)\|_{2}^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=(1-c)^{-n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|f\ast\Psi_{\lambda}\|_{2}^{2}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=(1-c)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|f\ast\Psi_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\,dx

\displaystyle=(1-c)^{-n}\left\|\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f\ast\Psi_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{2n}{2n-1}\right)^{n}C_{n,p}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

It follows that

\|S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f-S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}L_{\tau}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}\leq|c|\cdot K_{n,m}\|f\|_{2}

for any
c<\tfrac{1}{2n}
.
∎

for any

shows that:

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\int_{1}^{\infty}K_{\lambda}^{*}K_{\lambda}\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg\rVert^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\lVert\int_{1}^{\infty}K_{\lambda,1}^{*}K_{\lambda,1}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg\rVert^{1/2}+\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\lVert K_{\lambda,2}\rVert^{2}\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}2\lVert K_{\lambda,1}\rVert\lVert K_{\lambda,2}\rVert\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{1/2}

Letting
Q_{j}=K_{2^{j},1}^{*}K_{2^{j},1}
, it is shown in
[
5
]
that:

\displaystyle\lVert K_{\lambda,1}\rVert

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lVert K_{\lambda,2}\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\min\{\lambda^{-n}\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty},\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}\}

\displaystyle\lVert Q_{j}Q_{\ell}\rVert

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}^{2}2^{-|j-\ell|}(\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty})^{4}

so that

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\int_{1}^{\infty}K_{\lambda,1}^{*}K_{\lambda,1}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg\rVert^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\bigg\lVert\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{2^{j},1}^{*}K_{2^{j},1}\log(2)\ dj\bigg\rVert^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\log(2)}\ \bigg\lVert\int_{0}^{\infty}Q_{j}\ dj\bigg\rVert^{1/2}.

We now apply a continuous version of Cotlar’s Lemma (see Ch. 7 of
[
26
]
, Sec. 5.5 for the continuous extension). We define:

\displaystyle\beta(j,\ell)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}C_{n}2^{-|j-\ell|/2}(\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty})^{2}&j\geq 0\text{ and }\ell\geq 0\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Defining
Q_{j}=0
for
j<0
, we have
\lVert Q_{j}^{*}Q_{\ell}\rVert\leq\beta(j,\ell)^{2}
and
\lVert Q_{j}Q_{\ell}^{*}\rVert\leq\beta(j,\ell)^{2}
for all
j,\ell
. Thus by Cotlar’s Lemma:

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{j}\ dj\bigg\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\beta(j,\ell)\ d\ell,

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\int_{0}^{\infty}Q_{j}\ dj\bigg\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j\geq 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta(j,\ell)\ d\ell

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}(\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert H\tau\rVert_{\infty})^{2}\left(\sup_{j\geq 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}2^{-|j-\ell|/2}\ d\ell\right).

Now observing that with the change of variable
\lambda_{1}=2^{j},\lambda_{2}=2^{\ell}
, we have
2^{-|j-\ell|/2}=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\wedge\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\sup_{j\geq 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}2^{-|j-\ell|/2}\ d\ell

\displaystyle=\sup_{\lambda_{1}\geq 1}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda_{1}\wedge\lambda_{2})}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}}\ \frac{d\lambda_{2}}{\ln(2)\lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\sup_{\lambda_{1}\geq 1}\left(\int_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}}\ d\lambda_{2}+\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}{\lambda_{2}^{3/2}}\ d\lambda_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\sup_{\lambda_{1}\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}(2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}-2)+\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\sup_{\lambda_{1}\geq 1}\left(4-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\ln(2)}

and conclude that

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\int_{1}^{\infty}K_{\lambda,1}^{*}K_{\lambda,1}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg\rVert^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 3C_{n}(\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert H\tau\rVert_{\infty}).

Thus we have:

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\int_{1}^{\infty}K_{\lambda}^{*}K_{\lambda}\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg\rVert^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\lVert\int_{1}^{\infty}K_{\lambda,1}^{*}K_{\lambda,1}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg\rVert^{1/2}+\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\lVert K_{\lambda,2}\rVert^{2}\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}2\lVert K_{\lambda,1}\rVert\lVert K_{\lambda,2}\rVert\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{1/2}.

Now we see that there exists a constant
C_{n}
such that

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\int_{1}^{\infty}K_{\lambda,1}^{*}K_{\lambda,1}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg\rVert^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}(\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty})

\displaystyle\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\lVert K_{\lambda,2}\rVert^{2}\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty}\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\lambda^{-2n}\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}2\lVert K_{\lambda,1}\rVert\lVert K_{\lambda,2}\rVert\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}^{1/2}\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty}^{1/2}\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}2\lambda^{-n}\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{1/2}.

and

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\int_{1}^{\infty}K_{\lambda}^{*}K_{\lambda}\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg\rVert^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\left(\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}+\frac{1}{2n}\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty}+\frac{2}{n}\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}^{1/2}\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty}^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\left(\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}+\frac{1}{2n}\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty}+\frac{1}{n}\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}+\frac{1}{n}\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{n}(\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty}).

Finally, we bound
P_{2}
. Note that in the previous section it was observed (shown in
[
5
]
) that

\displaystyle\lVert K_{\lambda,1}\rVert

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lVert K_{\lambda,2}\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\min\{\lambda^{-n}\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty},\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}\}.

The above two inequalities imply

\displaystyle\lVert K_{\lambda}\rVert

\displaystyle=\lVert K_{\lambda,1}+K_{\lambda,2}\rVert\leq\lVert K_{\lambda,1}\rVert+\lVert K_{\lambda,2}\rVert\leq 2C_{n}\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}

so that

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\int_{2^{-\gamma}}^{1}K_{\lambda}^{*}K_{\lambda}\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg\rVert^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{2^{-\gamma}}^{1}\lVert K_{\lambda}\rVert^{2}\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{n}\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}\left(\int_{2^{-\gamma}}^{1}\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{n}\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}\left(-\ln(2^{-\gamma})\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{n}\gamma^{1/2}\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}.

Putting everything together and since
\gamma\geq 1
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\lVert[\mathcal{W},L_{\tau}]\rVert

\displaystyle\leq 2(P_{1}+P_{2}+P_{3})

\displaystyle\leq 4C_{n}\left(\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}+2^{-\gamma}\lVert\Delta\tau\rVert_{\infty}\right)+2C_{n}\gamma^{1/2}\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}+3C_{n}(\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty})

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{n}\left(\gamma\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}+2^{-\gamma}\lVert\Delta\tau\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty}\right).

Choosing
\gamma=\left(\log\frac{\lVert\Delta\tau\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}}\right)\vee 1
gives

\displaystyle\lVert[\mathcal{W},L_{\tau}]\rVert\leq\tilde{C}_{n}\left(\left(\log\frac{\lVert\Delta\tau\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}}\vee 1\right)\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty}\right),

and the lemma is proved.

Theorem 16
.

Assume
\psi
and its first and second order derivatives have decay in
O((1+|x|)^{-n-3})
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(x)\ dx=0
. Then for every

\displaystyle\lVert S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f-S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}L_{\tau}f\rVert_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{m,n}\left(\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}^{2}+\left(\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}\left(\log\frac{\lVert\Delta\tau\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}}\vee 1\right)+\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty}\right)^{2}\right)\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}.

and

\displaystyle\lVert S_{\text{dyad},2}^{m}f-S_{\text{dyad},2}^{m}L_{\tau}f\rVert_{\mathbf{\ell}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\hat{C}_{m,n}\left(\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}^{2}+\left(\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}\left(\log\frac{\lVert\Delta\tau\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}}\vee 1\right)+\lVert D^{2}\tau\rVert_{\infty}\right)^{2}\right)\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

The proof is only provided for the continuous case. We have the following bound for some
C_{m}
:

\displaystyle\lVert S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}f-S_{\text{cont},2}^{m}L_{\tau}f\rVert_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{2}M\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f-A_{2}ML_{\tau}\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}+\|A_{2}M[\mathcal{W}V_{m-1},L_{\tau}]f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{2}M\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f-A_{2}ML_{\tau}\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}+C_{m}^{2}\lVert[\mathcal{W},L_{\tau}]\rVert_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

For the first term, we can mimic the dilation argument to get

|A_{2}M\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f-A_{2}ML_{\tau}\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f|=\left|\|g\|_{2}-\|L_{\tau}g\|_{2}\right|.

The difference is the term with the diffeomorphism. Let
y=\gamma(x)=x-\tau(x)
. Then it follows that
\gamma^{-1}(y)=x
and change of variables implies that

\displaystyle\lVert L_{\tau}f\rVert_{2}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x-\tau(x))|^{2}\ dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(y)|^{2}\,\frac{dy}{|\det(I-D\tau(\gamma^{-1}(y)))|}.

We also have

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+n\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(y)|^{2}\ dy\leq\lVert L_{\tau}f\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-n\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(y)|^{2}\ dy,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+n\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}}\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\lVert L_{\tau}f\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-n\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}}\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Since we have a bound on
\|D\tau\|_{\infty}
, we see that

\frac{1}{1+n\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}}=\frac{1-n\|D\tau\|_{\infty}}{1-n^{2}\|D\tau\|_{\infty}^{2}}\geq 1-n\|D\tau\|_{\infty}

since
1>1-n^{2}\|D\tau\|_{\infty}^{2}>0.
Similarly,

\frac{1}{1-n\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}}=\frac{1+2n\|D\tau\|_{\infty}}{1+n\|D\tau\|_{\infty}-2n^{2}\|D\tau\|_{\infty}^{2}}

and

1+n\|D\tau\|_{\infty}-2n^{2}\|D\tau\|_{\infty}^{2}\geq 1+n\|D\tau\|_{\infty}-\frac{2n^{2}}{2n}\|D\tau\|_{\infty}=1

since
\|D\tau\|_{\infty}\leq\tfrac{1}{2n}
. It follows that
\frac{1}{1-n\lVert D\tau\rVert_{\infty}}\leq 1+2n\|D\tau\|_{\infty}
and

(1-n\|D\tau\|_{\infty})^{1/2}\lVert f\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert L_{\tau}f\rVert_{2}\leq(1+2n\|D\tau\|_{\infty})^{1/2}\lVert f\rVert_{2}.

Since
1-n\|D\tau\|_{\infty}<1
and
1+2n\|D\tau\|_{\infty}>1
, Use the lower bound on
\lVert L_{\tau}f\rVert_{2}
to get

\displaystyle\|f\|_{2}-\lVert L_{\tau}f\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{2}\left(1-(1-n\|D\tau\|_{\infty})^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{2}\left(1-(1-n\|D\tau\|_{\infty})\right)

\displaystyle=n\|D\tau\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{2}.

and the upper bound to get

\displaystyle\lVert L_{\tau}f\rVert_{2}-\|f\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{2}\left((1+2n\|D\tau\|_{\infty})^{1/2}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{2}\left((1+2n\|D\tau\|_{\infty})-1\right)

\displaystyle=2n\|D\tau\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{2}.

Finally, we have

\left|\|f\|_{2}-\lVert L_{\tau}f\rVert_{2}\right|\leq 2n\|D\tau\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{2}

for any
f\in{\mathbf{L}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Now we mimic the argument given for dilation stability to get

\|A_{2}M\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f-A_{2}ML_{\tau}\mathcal{W}V_{m-1}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})}^{2}\leq C\|D\tau\|_{\infty}^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}

for some constant

For the second term, we have

Lemma 17
.

Let

for some constant

Notice that

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\gamma(z))[\det(D\gamma(z))\psi_{\lambda}(\gamma(x)-\gamma(z))-\psi_{\lambda}(x-z)]\,dz\right|^{2}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\det(D\gamma(z))f(\gamma(z))\psi_{\lambda}(\gamma(x)-\gamma(z))\,dz-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\gamma(z))\psi_{\lambda}(x-z)\,dz\right|^{2}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}dx.

Using the change of variables
u=\gamma(z)
, we get

\displaystyle\|Tg\|_{L^{2}_{X}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|L_{\tau}(f\ast\psi_{\lambda})(x)-(L_{\tau}f\ast\psi_{\lambda})(x)\right|^{2}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|[\mathcal{W}_{\lambda},L_{\tau}]f(x)\right|^{2}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|[\mathcal{W}_{\lambda},L_{\tau}]f(x)\right|^{2}\,dx\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}

where the last inequality follows from the

Lemma 19
.

\|S_{\text{cont},q}f-S_{\text{cont},q}L_{\tau}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{q}\leq C_{n,q}\left[\|D\tau\|_{\infty}^{q}+\eta^{q}M_{r}^{q\delta}\left(\|D\tau\|_{\infty}\left(\log\frac{\|\Delta\tau\|_{\infty}}{\|D\tau\|_{\infty}}\vee 1\right)+\|D^{2}\tau\|_{\infty}\right)^{q(1-\delta)}\right]\|f\|_{q}^{q}.

Proof.

We use the same notation as Theorem
12
. Using a nearly identical argument to Corollary
13
, we get

\displaystyle\|S_{\text{cont},q}f-S_{\text{cont},q}L_{\tau}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

\displaystyle=\|A_{q}M\mathcal{W}f-A_{q}M\mathcal{W}L_{\tau}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

\displaystyle=\|A_{q}M\mathcal{W}f-A_{q}ML_{\tau}\mathcal{W}f+A_{q}ML_{\tau}Wf-A_{q}M\mathcal{W}L_{\tau}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{q}M\mathcal{W}f-A_{q}ML_{\tau}\mathcal{W}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|A_{q}ML_{\tau}\mathcal{W}f-A_{q}M\mathcal{W}L_{\tau}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(A_{q}M-A_{q}ML_{\tau})\mathcal{W}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|A_{q}M[\mathcal{W},L_{\tau}]f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

The first term,
\|(A_{q}M-A_{q}ML_{\tau})\mathcal{W}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}
, can be bounded using an argument identical to the
q=2
case. In particular, we can prove that

(1-n\|D\tau\|_{\infty})\lVert f\rVert_{q}\leq(1-n\|D\tau\|_{\infty})^{1/q}\lVert f\rVert_{q}\leq\lVert L_{\tau}f\rVert_{q}

and

\lVert L_{\tau}f\rVert_{q}\leq(1+2n\|D\tau\|_{\infty})^{1/q}\lVert f\rVert_{q}\leq(1+2n\|D\tau\|_{\infty})\lVert f\rVert_{q},

which means

\|(A_{q}M-A_{q}ML_{\tau})\mathcal{W}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{q}\leq C\|D\tau\|_{\infty}^{q}\lVert f\rVert_{q}^{q}.

For the other term,

\displaystyle\|A_{q}M[\mathcal{W},L_{\tau}]f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{q}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(L_{\tau}f\ast\psi_{\lambda})(x)-L_{\tau}(f\ast\psi_{\lambda})(x)|^{q}\,dx\right]^{2/q}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right)^{q/2}.

Now, expand convolution and then use change of variables to get

\displaystyle\|A_{q}M[\mathcal{W},L_{\tau}]f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{q}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\gamma(z))(\det(D\gamma(z))\psi_{\lambda}(\gamma(x)-\gamma(z))-\psi_{\lambda}(x-z))\,dz\right|^{q}\,dx\right]^{2/q}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(z)K_{\lambda}(x,z)\,dz\right|^{q}\,dx\right]^{2/q}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|T_{\lambda}g(x)\right|^{q}\,dx\right]^{2/q}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|T_{\lambda}g(x)\right|^{q}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right]^{q/2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|T_{\lambda}g(x)\right|^{2}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right]^{q/2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|Tg(x)\|^{q}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{+},\tfrac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right)}\,dx

\displaystyle=\|Tg\|^{q}_{\mathbf{L}^{q}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\eta^{q}M_{r}^{q\delta}\left(\|D\tau\|_{\infty}\left(\log\frac{\|\Delta\tau\|_{\infty}}{\|D\tau\|_{\infty}}\vee 1\right)+\|D^{2}\tau\|_{\infty}\right)^{q(1-\delta)}\|f\|_{q}^{q}.

Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

6
Equivariance and Invariance to Rotations

We now consider adding group actions to our scattering transform and prove invariance to rotations. Let
\text{SO}(n)
be the group of
n\times n
rotation matrices. Since
\text{SO}(n)
is a compact Lie group, we can define a Haar measure, say
\mu
, with
\mu(\text{SO}(n))<\infty
. We say that
f\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\text{SO}(n))
if and only if
f
is
\mu
-measurable and
\int_{\text{SO}(n)}|f(r)|^{2}\,d\mu(r)<\infty
.

6.1
Rotation Equivariant Representations

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a wavelet. Define

\psi_{\lambda,R}(x)=\lambda^{-n/2}\psi(\lambda^{-1}R^{-1}x),

where
R\in\text{SO}(n)
is a
n\times n
rotation matrix. The continuous and dyadic wavelet transforms of
f
are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\text{Rot}}f

\displaystyle:=\{f\ast\psi_{\lambda,R}(x):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda\in(0,\infty),R\in\text{SO}(n)\},

\displaystyle W_{\text{Rot}}f

\displaystyle:=\{f\ast\psi_{j,R}(x):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},j\in\mathbb{Z},R\in\text{SO}(n)\}.

We will first consider a translation invariant and rotation equivariant formulation of continuous and dyadic one-layer scattering using

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\text{cont},q}f(\lambda,R)

\displaystyle:=\|f\ast\psi_{\lambda,R}\|_{q},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\text{dyad},q}f(j,R)

\displaystyle:=\|f\ast\psi_{j,R}\|_{q}.

The translation invariance of our representation follows from translation invariance of the norm. For rotation equivariance, notice that if
f_{\tilde{R}}(x):=f(\tilde{R}^{-1}x)
, then we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\text{cont},q}f_{\tilde{R}}(\lambda,R)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}_{\text{cont},q}f(\lambda,\tilde{R}^{-1}R),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\text{dyad},q}f_{\tilde{R}}(j,R)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}_{\text{dyad},q}f(j,\tilde{R}^{-1}R).

Now suppose we have
m
layers again. Then we define our
m
layer transforms by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\text{cont},q}^{m}f(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m},R_{1},\ldots,R_{m})

\displaystyle:=\||f\ast\psi_{\lambda_{1},R_{1}}|\ast\dots|\ast\psi_{\lambda_{m},R_{m}}\|_{q},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\text{dyad},q}^{m}f(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m},R_{1},\ldots,R_{m})

\displaystyle:=\||f\ast\psi_{j_{1},R_{1}}|\ast\dots|\ast\psi_{j_{m},R_{m}}\|_{q}.

and rotation equivariance implies

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\text{cont},q}^{m}f_{\tilde{R}}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m},R_{1},\ldots,R_{m})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}_{\text{cont},q}^{m}f(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m},\tilde{R}^{-1}R_{1},\ldots,\tilde{R}^{-1}R_{m}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\text{dyad},q}^{m}f_{\tilde{R}}(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m},R_{1},\ldots,R_{m})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}_{\text{dyad},q}^{m}f(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m},\tilde{R}^{-1}R_{1},\ldots,\tilde{R}^{-1}R_{m}).

The norm we will use is similar to our previous formulations. Denote the scattering norm for the continuous transform as

\|\mathfrak{S}_{\text{cont},q}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m})\times\text{SO}(n)^{m}}^{q}:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\text{SO}(n)}\dots\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\text{SO}(n)}\||f\ast\psi_{j_{1},R_{1}}|\ast\dots|\ast\psi_{j_{m},R_{m}}\|^{2}_{q}d\mu_{1}(R_{1})\,\frac{d\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n+1}}\dots d\mu_{m}(R_{n})\,\frac{d\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}^{n+1}}.\right)^{q/2}

For the dyadic transform, we denote the norm using

\|\mathfrak{S}_{\text{dyad},q}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{\ell}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})\times\text{SO}(n)^{m}}^{q}:=\left(\sum_{j_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\text{SO}(n)}\dots\sum_{j_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\text{SO}(n)}\||f\ast\psi_{j_{1},R_{1}}|\ast\dots|\ast\psi_{j_{m},R_{m}}\|^{2}_{q}d\mu_{1}(R_{1})\,\dots d\mu_{m}(R_{n})\right)^{q/2}.

We will start by proving that these formulations of the scattering transform are well defined, and prove properties about stability to diffeomorphisms like in previous sections.

Conclusion

We have formulated operators that are translation invariant in
\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, proven these operators are Lipschitz continuous to the action of
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms when
1<q\leq 2
with respect to certain norms, and used these results to formulate rotation invariant/equivariant operators on
\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that are Lipschitz continuous to the action of
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms. One question that was left unanswered was if Lipschitz continuity holds for general diffeomorphisms when
q=1
. This question is harder to answer because
f\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
does not necessarily imply that
L_{\tau}f\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The kernel for the commutator is also singular, which would mean one cannot use extension theorems for Hardy spaces. The answer is most likely no, but we did not construct a counterexample.

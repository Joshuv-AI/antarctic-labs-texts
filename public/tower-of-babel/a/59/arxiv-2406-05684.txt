Introduction

The classical Rademacher theorem
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
asserts that every Lipschitz mapping
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is differentiable almost everywhere. Actually, Rademacher’s result
[
12
, Satz I]
and its generalization
[
14
]
concern the two-dimensional case but they are applicable not only for Lipschitz functions. In particular, the Stepanoff theorem
[
14
, Satz]
asserts that a continuous function
f
is almost everywhere differentiable on the complement of the set
{L^{\infty}(f)=\big\{x:\operatorname{Lip}f(x)=\infty\big\}}

where
\operatorname{Lip}f(x)=\limsup\limits_{u\to x}\frac{1}{\|u-x\|}\big\|f(u)-f(x)\big\|
is the, so called,
big Lipschitz derivative
(or, shortly,
big lip
). Malý
[
9
]
gave a simple proof of the Stepanoff theorem for a function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Besides, there are many generalizations of the Rademacher theorem for Banach spaces (see, for example,
[
8
,
10
]
). If we consider the
little Lipschitz derivative
(or, shortly,
little lip
)
\operatorname{lip}f(x)=\liminf\limits_{r\to 0}\sup\limits_{\|u-x\|<r}\frac{1}{r}\big\|f(u)-f(x)\big\|
instead of
\operatorname{Lip}f
then
f
need not to be almost everywhere differentiable on the complement of
\ell^{\infty}(f)=\big\{x:\operatorname{lip}f(x)=\infty\big\}
(see
[
2
, Theorem 1.4]
).
It is well-known that every differentiable function is
pointwise Lipschitz
(that is
\operatorname{Lip}f<+\infty
). But
C^{1}
-functions have a stronger property: every such function is
locally Lipschitz
. This means that its
local Lipschitz derivative

\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x)=\limsup\limits_{(u,v)\to(x,x)}\frac{1}{\|u-v\|}\big\|f(u)-f(v)\big\|<+\infty
for any
x
.
So, the set
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)=\big\{x:\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}=\infty\big\}
is also interesting for us.

In this paper we deals with the following general problem.

Problem 1

(
\ell
-
L
-
\mathbb{L}
-problem
)
.

Let
X
be a metric space. For which sets
A,B,C\subseteq X
does there exist a continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\ell^{\infty}(f)=A
,
L^{\infty}(f)=B
and
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)=C
?

Also we may consider some partial versions of this problem by omitting one or two of the sets
\ell^{\infty}(f)
,
L^{\infty}(f)
and
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)
. For example, the
\ell
-problem
means the description of the set
\ell^{\infty}(f)
for a continuous function
f
; the
\ell
-
L
-problem
means the description of the sets
\ell^{\infty}(f)
and
L^{\infty}(f)
and so on. In the case where
X=\mathbb{R}
the
L
-problem was solved in
[
4
, Theorem 3.35 and Lemma 2.4]
where the authors proved that
A
has the property
A=L^{\infty}(f)
(even
A=\ell^{\infty}(f)=L^{\infty}(f)
) for some continuous function
f
if and only if
A
is a
G_{\delta}
-set. Moreover, they proved in
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
that for any
F_{\sigma}
-set
A
there is a continuous function
f
with
\ell^{\infty}(f)=A
. But the obvious necessary condition is that
\ell^{\infty}(f)
is
F_{\sigma\delta}
. So, the
\ell
-problem is still open even for
\mathbb{R}
. Hanson
[
7
]
solved
\ell
-
L
-problem in the case where
A=\ell^{\infty}(f)=\varnothing
. Another partial answer to the
\ell
-problem one can find in the recent paper
[
11
]
where the author proved that for any
F_{\sigma\delta}
-set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
of Lebesgue measure zero there is an increasing absolutely continuous function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\ell^{\infty}(f)=A
. We are also interested in the following more general problem.

Problem 2

(
\operatorname{lip}
-
\operatorname{Lip}
-
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}
-problem
)
.

Let
X
be a metric space. For which functions
u,v,w\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
does there exists a continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\operatorname{lip}f=u
,
\operatorname{Lip}f=v
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f=w
?

Of course, we may omit some of the functions
\operatorname{lip}f
,
\operatorname{Lip}f
,
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f
and consider simpler versions of the previous problem like

\operatorname{lip}
-problem
,
\operatorname{lip}
-
\operatorname{Lip}
-problem
and so on. In
[
3
]
the authors solved the
\operatorname{lip}
-problem for a function
u=\mathbf{1}_{E}
where
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and characterized the, so called,
\operatorname{lip}1
subsets of
\mathbb{R}
.

As we have seen, the current results in this direction concern the case
{X=\mathbb{R}}
.
Therefore, we are going to extend some of the previous results to general metric spaces.
Our approach to this problem is based on a generalization of the
Takagi-van der Waerden function to a metric space.
The
classical Takagi-van der Waerden function of type

a>1
(see, for example,
[
1
]
) is, by definition, the
function
f_{a}(x)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}d\big(x,\frac{1}{a^{n}}\mathbb{Z}\big)
,
where
d(x,E)=\inf\limits_{y\in E}|x-y|
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and

E\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. In the case
a=2
or
a=10
we obtain two
famous examples of nowhere differentiable functions which were
constructed independently by Takagi and van der Waerden.
We replace the set
\frac{1}{a^{n}}\mathbb{Z}
by a maximal

\frac{1}{a^{n}}
-separated
set
S_{n}
in a metric space
X
,
add a parameter
b<a
and define a
Takagi-van der Waerden function of type

(a,b)
as
f_{a,b}(x)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}b^{n}d\big(x,S_{n}\big)
,
x\in X
. Then we prove that
\operatorname{Lip}f_{a,b}(x)=+\infty
if
a>b>2
and
x
is a non-isolated point of
X
. To calculate the little Lipschitz derivatives we need some special property of o metric space which we call
hermeticity
. It means that the
shell porosity
(see
[
15
]
)
p^{s}(X,x)\leq\lambda<1
for some
\lambda
and any non-isolated
point
x
in
X
. In particular, every normed space is hermetic. We prove that for any hermetic space
X
there are
a>b>1
such that
\operatorname{lip}f_{a,b}(x)=+\infty
for any non-isolated point
x\in X
. So, in the last section we give a partial answer to the
\ell
-
L
-
\mathbb{L}
-problem for a hermetic space
X
, open sets
A=B
and
C=\overline{A}
.

2
Lipschitz derivatives

Let
X
be a metric space,
a\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
. We always denote the metric on
X
by
|\,\cdot\,-\,\cdot\,|_{X}
and

\displaystyle B(a,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=B_{X}(a,\varepsilon)=\big\{x\in X\colon|x-a|_{X}<\varepsilon\big\},

\displaystyle B[a,\varepsilon]

\displaystyle=B_{X}[a,\varepsilon]=\big\{x\in X\colon|x-a|_{X}\leq\varepsilon\big\}.

Definition 1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces,
f:X\to Y
be a function,
x\in X
. Denote

1.

\|f\|_{\operatorname{lip}}=\sup\limits_{u\neq v\in X}\frac{1}{|u-v|_{X}}\big|f(u)-f(v)\big|_{Y}

2.

\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x)=\limsup\limits_{(u,v)\to(x,x)}\frac{1}{|u-v|_{X}}\big|f(u)-f(v)\big|_{Y}

3.

\operatorname{Lip}f(x)=\limsup\limits_{u\to x}\frac{1}{|u-x|_{X}}\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|_{Y}
;

4.

\operatorname{lip}f(x)=\liminf\limits_{r\to 0^{+}}\sup\limits_{u\in B(x,r)}\frac{1}{r}\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|_{Y}
;

The number
\|f\|_{\operatorname{lip}}
is
Lipschitz constant of
f
. The functions
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f
,
\operatorname{Lip}f
and
\operatorname{lip}f
are called the
local, big and little Lipschitz derivative
respectively.

We denote by
X^{d}
the set of all non-isolated points of
X
. During the whole paper we assume that
\sup\varnothing=0
. As a consequence of this assumption we have
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x)=\operatorname{Lip}f(x)=\operatorname{lip}f(x)=0
for any
x\in X\setminus X^{d}
.

Obviously, if
Y
is a normed space then
\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{lip}}
is an extended seminorm on
Y^{X}
in the sense
[
13
]
. Moreover,
\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{lip}}
is a norm on the space all Lipschitz functions
f\colon{X\to Y}
vanishing at some fixed point in
X
.

We introduce some auxiliary notations:

1.

\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}^{r}f(x)=\big\|f|_{B(x,r)}\big\|_{\operatorname{lip}}=\sup\limits_{u\neq v\in B(x,r)}\frac{1}{|u-v|_{X}}\big|f(u)-f(v)\big|_{Y}

2.

\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)=\sup\limits_{u\in B(x,r)}\frac{1}{r}\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|_{Y},\operatorname{Lip}^{r}_{+}f(x)=\sup\limits_{u\in B[x,r]}\tfrac{1}{r}\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|_{Y}

3.

\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f(x)=\sup\limits_{0<\varrho<r}\operatorname{Lip}^{\varrho}f(x),\ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{lip}_{r}f(x)=\inf\limits_{0<\varrho<r}\operatorname{Lip}^{\varrho}f(x)
;

Therefore, the definitions of the Lipschitz derivatives might be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x)

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{r>0}\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}^{r}f(x)

(1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}f(x)

\displaystyle=\liminf\limits_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)

(2)

Some authors (see, for example,
[
7
,
3
,
4
]
) define
\operatorname{Lip}f
and
\operatorname{lip}f
use the function

\operatorname{Lip}^{r}_{+}f
instead of
\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f
. In the case where
X
is a normed space we have
B[x,r]=\overline{B(x,r)}
.
Therefore,
\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)=\operatorname{Lip}^{r}_{+}f(x)
for any continuous function
f
.
But the previous equality does not hold for the discrete metric on
X
, nonconstant
f
and
r=1
. However, we have the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces and
f\colon X\to Y
be a function.
Then, for any non-isolated point
x\in X
, the following equalities hold

\operatorname{Lip}f(x)=\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)=\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}_{+}^{r}f(x).

Proof.

Denote

\displaystyle\alpha(r)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\rho<r}\operatorname{Lip}^{\rho}f(x),

\displaystyle\beta(r)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\rho<r}\operatorname{Lip}_{+}^{\rho}f(x),

\displaystyle\gamma(r)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\frac{|f(u)-f(x)|_{Y}}{|u-x|_{X}}\colon u\in X,\;0<|u-x|_{X}<r\right\}.

Since
B(x,r)\subseteq B[x,r]
, we have
\alpha(r)\leq\beta(r)
. Next, we have

\displaystyle\beta(r)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\rho<r}\sup_{0<|u-x|_{X}\leq\rho}\tfrac{1}{\rho}|f(u)-f(x)|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0<\rho<r}\sup_{0<|u-x|_{X}\leq\rho}\tfrac{1}{|u-x|_{X}}|f(u)-f(x)|_{Y}=\gamma(r).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\gamma(r)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\rho<r}\sup_{|u-x|_{X}=\rho}\tfrac{1}{\rho}|f(u)-f(x)|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0<\rho<r}\sup_{0<|u-x|_{X}\leq\rho}\tfrac{1}{\rho}|f(u)-f(x)|_{Y}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\rho<r}\operatorname{Lip}_{+}^{\rho}f(x)=\beta(r).

We have shown, that
\alpha(r)\leq\beta(r)=\gamma(r)
for
r>0
.
It remains to show that
\alpha(r)=\beta(r)
,
r>0
.
Let us assume that there exists
r_{0}>0
, such that
\alpha(r_{0})<\beta(r_{0})
.
Denote
\varphi(r)=\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)
and
\psi(r)=\operatorname{Lip}_{+}^{r}f(x)
. Observe, that

\rho\varphi(\rho)\leq\rho\psi(\rho)\leq r\varphi(r),\text{ for }0<\rho<r.

(3)

Since
\beta(r_{0})=\sup\limits_{r<r_{0}}\psi(r)>\alpha(r_{0})
,
there exists
r_{1}<r_{0}
such that
\alpha(r_{0})<\psi(r_{1})
.
Let
\varepsilon=\psi(r_{1})-\alpha(r_{0})>0
. Then, for any
r<r_{0}
,

\varphi(r)+\varepsilon\leq\alpha(r_{0})+\varepsilon=\psi(r_{1})
, so

r_{1}\varphi(r)+r_{1}\varepsilon\leq r_{1}\psi(r_{1})\leq r\varphi(r),\text{ for }r_{1}<r<r_{0},

(4)

where the second inequality follows from (
3
).
Note that

\varphi(r)=\tfrac{1}{r}r\varphi(r)\leq\tfrac{1}{r_{1}}r_{0}\varphi(r_{0}),\text{ for }r_{1}<r<r_{0},

so, the function
\varphi
is bounded on the interval
(r_{1};r_{0})
.
However, by (
4
), we have

0=\lim_{r\to r_{1}^{+}}(r-r_{1})\varphi(r)\geq r_{1}\varepsilon>0,

which is impossible. We have
\alpha(r)=\beta(r)=\gamma(r)
for
r>0
.
But

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)=\inf_{r>0}\alpha(r),

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}_{+}^{r}f(x)=\inf_{r>0}\beta(r)

and

\operatorname{Lip}f(x)=\inf\limits_{r>0}\gamma(r)

and the proof is finished.
∎

Note, that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Lip}_{r^{\prime}}f(x)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \operatorname{lip}_{r}f(x)\geq\operatorname{lip}_{r^{\prime}}f(x)\text{ if }0<r<r^{\prime},

(5)

So, the definitions and the previous proposition yield

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}f(x)

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{r>0}\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f(x)=\lim\limits_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f(x),

(6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{r>0}\operatorname{lip}_{r}f(x)=\lim\limits_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{lip}_{r}f(x).

(7)

Therefore, it is easy to see that the following inequalities hold.

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}_{r}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f(x)\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}^{r}f(x)\text{ for any }r>0,

(8)

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Lip}f(x)\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x).

(9)

Definition 2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces and
\gamma\geq 0
. A function
{f\colon X\to Y}
is called

1.

\gamma
-Lipschitz
if
\|f\|_{\operatorname{lip}}\leq\gamma
;

2.

Lipschitz
if
\|f\|_{\operatorname{lip}}<\infty
;

3.

locally Lipschitz
if
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f<\infty
.

4.

pointwise Lipschitz
if
\operatorname{Lip}f<\infty
;

5.

weakly pointwise Lipschitz
if
\operatorname{lip}f<\infty
.

Denote

1.

\mathbb{L}(f)=\big\{x\in X:\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x)<\infty\big\}
;

2.

\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)=\big\{x\in X:\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x)=\infty\big\}=X\setminus\mathbb{L}(f)
;

3.

L(f)=\big\{x\in X:\operatorname{Lip}f(x)<\infty\big\}
;

4.

L^{\infty}(f)=\big\{x\in X:\operatorname{Lip}f(x)=\infty\big\}=X\setminus L(f)
;

5.

\ell(f)=\big\{x\in X:\operatorname{lip}f(x)<\infty\big\}
;

6.

\ell^{\infty}(f)=\big\{x\in X:\operatorname{lip}f(x)=\infty\big\}=X\setminus\ell(f)
;

Inequalities (
9
) yield the next assertion.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces, and
f:X\to Y
be a function. Then
\mathbb{L}(f)\subseteq L(f)\subseteq\ell(f)
and
\ell^{\infty}(f)\subseteq L^{\infty}(f)\subseteq\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)
.

3
Classification of the Lipschitz derivatives and pairs of Hahn

Now we pass to the investigation of the type of semicontinuity of Lipschitz derivatives of continuous functions.
In
[
4
]
semicontinuity of Lipschitz derivatives of a continuous function

f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
was obtained from the continuity of
\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f
.
But in the general situation this function need not be continuous.
Therefore, we prove semicontinuity of Lipschitz derivatives directly by the definitions.

Definition 3
.

Let
X
be a topological space and
f,g:X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. We say that

1.

f
is
lower semicontinuous
if
f^{-1}\big((\gamma;+\infty]\big)
is an open set for any
\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
;

2.

f
is
upper semicontinuous
if
f^{-1}\big([-\infty;\gamma)\big)
is an open set for any
\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
;

3.

f
is
F_{\sigma}
-lower semicontinuous
if
f^{-1}\big((\gamma;+\infty]\big)
is an
F_{\sigma}
-set for any
{\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}}
;

4.

f
is
F_{\sigma}
-upper semicontinuous
if
f^{-1}\big([-\infty;\gamma)\big)
is an
F_{\sigma}
-set for any
{\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}}
;

5.

(f,g)
is
a pair of Hahn
if
f\leq g
,
f
is upper semicontinuous and
g
is lower semicontinuous;

6.

(f,g)
is
an
F_{\sigma}
-pair of Hahn
if
f\leq g
,
f
is
F_{\sigma}
-lower semicontinuous and
g
is
F_{\sigma}
-upper semicontinuous;

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space,
f:X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be an
(
F_{\sigma}
-)upper
semicontinuous,
g:X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be an (
F_{\sigma}
-)lower semicontinuous and
\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
f^{-1}\big([-\infty,\gamma]\big)
,
g^{-1}\big([\gamma,+\infty]\big)
are

G_{\delta}
-sets (resp.
F_{\sigma\delta}
-sets) and

f^{-1}\big((\gamma,+\infty]\big)
,
g^{-1}\big([-\infty,\gamma)\big)
are
F_{\sigma}
-sets (resp.
G_{\delta\sigma}
-sets).

Proof.

Let
\gamma<+\infty
and
\gamma_{n}\downarrow\gamma
. Since
f^{-1}\big([\gamma_{n};+\infty]\big)
are closed (resp.
G_{\delta}
-set), we conclude that
f^{-1}\big((\gamma;+\infty]\big)=\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}f^{-1}\big([\gamma_{n};+\infty]\big)
is
F_{\sigma}
-sets (resp.
G_{\delta\sigma}
-sets). The proof of the rest assertions is analogical.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a topological space,
f_{n}:X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be an upper (lower) semicontinuous function for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f:X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a function such that
f(x)=\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}(x)
(resp.
f(x)=\inf\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}(x)
) for any
x\in X
. Then
f
is an
F_{\sigma}
-lower (resp.
F_{\sigma}
-upper) semicontinuous function.

Proof.

Let
f(x)=\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}(x)
and
f_{n}
’s are upper semicontinuous. Consider
\gamma<+\infty
. Then
f_{n}^{-1}\big((\gamma+\infty]\big)
is an
F_{\sigma}
-set by Proposition
3.1
. Consequently,

f^{-1}\big((\gamma,+\infty]\big)=\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}^{-1}\big((\gamma+\infty]\big)
is an
F_{\sigma}
-set as well. Thus,
f
is
F_{\sigma}
-lower semicontinuous. The proof of the second case is analogical.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be a metric space,
f:X\to Y
be a continuous function and
r>0
. Then

(i)

\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f:X\to[0;+\infty]
is a lower semicontinuous function;

(ii)

\operatorname{lip}_{r}f:X\to[0;+\infty]
is an upper semicontinuous function;

(iii)

(\operatorname{lip}_{r}f,\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f)
is a pair of Hahn;

(iv)

\operatorname{Lip}f:X\to[0;+\infty]
is an
F_{\sigma}
-upper semicontinuous function;

(v)

\operatorname{lip}f:X\to[0;+\infty]
is a
F_{\sigma}
-lower semicontinuous function;

(vi)

(\operatorname{lip}f,\operatorname{Lip}f)
is an
F_{\sigma}
-pair of Hahn;

(vii)

\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f:X\to[0;+\infty]
is an upper semicontinuous function.

Proof.

(i)
. Fix
r>0
. Let
x_{0}\in X
and

\varphi(u)=|u-s|_{X}\geq|u-x|_{X}-|s-x|_{X}\geq\lambda r-\alpha\varepsilon=\tfrac{3\lambda\varepsilon}{8}-\alpha\varepsilon=3\alpha\varepsilon-\alpha\varepsilon=2\alpha\varepsilon,

and then

\big|\varphi(u)-\varphi(x)\big|\geq\varphi(u)-\varphi(x)\geq 2\alpha\varepsilon-\alpha\varepsilon=\alpha\varepsilon.

Thus, in the both cases we have that
|\varphi(u)-\varphi(x)|\geq\alpha\varepsilon
for some point
{u\in B[x,\varepsilon]}
.
∎

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
X
be a hermetic metric space,
a>b>1
such that

\frac{2b}{a-b}+\frac{1}{b-1}<\frac{H(X)}{8},

(12)

and
f
be TW-function of type
(a,b)
. Then
\operatorname{lip}f(x)=\infty
for any
x\in X^{d}
.

Proof.

Let
S_{n}
,
\varphi_{n}
,
s_{n}
,
r_{n}
be such as in Definition
5
.
Fix
x\in X^{d}
. Let us prove that
\operatorname{lip}f(x)=\infty
.
Since (
12
), we can pick
\lambda>0
such that
\lambda<H(X)
and

\gamma:=\frac{\lambda}{8}-\frac{2b}{a-b}-\frac{1}{b-1}>0.

(13)

Therefore,
RH_{\lambda}(X,x)>0
. Let
\delta=\min\big\{1,RH_{\lambda}(X,x)\big\}
and fix
0<r<\delta
. Set
\varepsilon_{n}=\frac{1}{a^{n}}
. Since
\varepsilon_{0}=1\geq\delta
and
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
, there exists
n=n(r)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\leq r<\varepsilon_{n-1}
. Put
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(r)=\varepsilon_{n}
. Observe that
\varepsilon(r)\to 0
and then
n(r)\to\infty
as
r\to 0^{+}
. Since
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{n}\leq r<\delta\leq RH_{\lambda}(X,x)
we can us Lemma
6.4
for the set
S=S_{n}
and the function
\varphi=\varphi_{n}
. So, there is
u\in B[x,\varepsilon]
with

\big|\varphi(u)-\varphi(x)\big|\geq\tfrac{\lambda\varepsilon}{8}.

Denote
g=s_{n}
and
h=r_{n+1}
. Then

f=g+b^{n}\varphi+h

By Proposition
4.2
the function
g
is Lipschitz with the constant
\frac{b^{n}}{b-1}
and
0\leq h\leq\frac{b^{n+1}}{(a-b)a^{n}}
. Therefore,

\big|g(u)-g(x)\big|\leq\tfrac{b^{n}}{b-1}|u-x|_{X}\leq\tfrac{1}{b-1}b^{n}\varepsilon,

\big|h(u)-h(x)\big|\leq\big|h(u)\big|+\big|h(x)\big|\leq\tfrac{2b^{n+1}}{(a-b)a^{n}}=\tfrac{2b}{a-b}b^{n}\varepsilon,

\big|b^{n}\varphi(u)-b^{n}\varphi(x)\big|\geq b^{n}\tfrac{\lambda\varepsilon}{8}=\tfrac{\lambda}{8}b^{n}\varepsilon.

Thus,

\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|\geq\big|b^{n}\varphi(u)-b^{n}\varphi(x)\big|-\big|g(u)-g(x)\big|-\big|h(u)-h(x)\big|\geq

\geq\tfrac{\lambda}{8}b^{n}\varepsilon-\tfrac{1}{b-1}b^{n}\varepsilon-\tfrac{2b}{a-b}b^{n}\varepsilon=\Big(\tfrac{\lambda}{8}-\tfrac{1}{b-1}-\tfrac{2b}{a-b}\Big)b^{n}\varepsilon=\gamma b^{n}\varepsilon=

=\gamma b^{n}\varepsilon_{n}=\tfrac{\gamma b^{n}}{a^{n}}=\tfrac{\gamma}{a}b^{n}\varepsilon_{n-1}\geq\tfrac{\gamma}{a}b^{n}r.

Since,
|u-x|_{X}\leq\varepsilon<r
and
\frac{1}{r}|f(u)-f(x)|\geq\tfrac{\gamma}{a}b^{n}
, we conclude that

\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)\geq\tfrac{\gamma}{a}b^{n},\mbox{ for any }r<\delta.

But
n=n(r)\to\infty
as

and
b>1
. Thus,

\operatorname{lip}f(x)=\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)\geq\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\tfrac{\gamma}{a}b^{n(r)}=\infty.

So,
\operatorname{lip}f(x)=\infty
for any
x\in X^{d}
.
∎

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
X
be a metric space,
G
be an open subset of
X
,
F=X\setminus G
and
\alpha>0
. Then there exist
\alpha
-Lipschitz functions
g:X\to[0;\alpha]
such that
F=g^{-1}(0)
.

Proof.

If
F=\varnothing
then we put
g(x)=\alpha
for any
x\in X
. Suppose that
F\neq\varnothing
.
Let us define a function
g
as

g(x)=\alpha\min\big\{1,d(x,F)\big\},\ \ \ x\in X.

Consider
x,y\in X
. Since

d(x,F)\leq|x-y|_{X}+d(y,F)
,
we conclude

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=\alpha\min\big\{1,d(x,F)\big\}\leq\alpha\min\big\{1,|x-y|_{X}+d(y,F)\big\}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha|x-y|_{X}+\alpha\min\big\{1,d(y,F)\big\}=\alpha|x-y|_{X}+g(y).

Replacing
x
with
y
we obtain that
g(y)\leq\alpha|x-y|_{X}+g(x)
. Thus,

\big|g(x)-g(y)\big|\leq\alpha|x-y|_{X},\ \ \ \text{for any }x,y\in X,

and then
g
is
\alpha
-Lipschitz. On the other hand, since
F
is closed,

g(x)=0\Leftrightarrow d(x,F)=0\Leftrightarrow x\in F,\ \ \ x\in X.

Hence,
F=g^{-1}(0)
, and then
g
is such as we need.
∎

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
X
be a hermetic metric space and
G\subseteq X^{d}
be an open set in
X
.
Then there exists a continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that
L^{\infty}(f)=\ell^{\infty}(f)=G
and
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)=\overline{G}
.

Proof.

Since

\lim_{b\to\infty}\lim_{a\to\infty}\Big(\frac{2b}{a-b}+\frac{1}{b-1}\Big)=0<\frac{H(X)}{8},

we can chose
b>1
and then
a>b
such that inequality (
12
) holds. Let
h=f_{a,b}
be a TW-function of type
(a,b)
. By Theorem
6.5
and Proposition
4.2
we have that
\operatorname{lip}h(x)=\infty
and
0\leq h(x)\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}

for any
x\in X
, where
\alpha=\frac{a-b}{a}
. Let
F=X\setminus G
and
U=\operatorname{Int}F=X\setminus\overline{G}
.
Let
g
be given by Lemma
7.1
and define the function
f:X\to[0;1]
by
f(x)=g(x)h(x)
for any
x\in X
.

Consider
x\in F
and
u\in X
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|

\displaystyle=\big|g(u)h(u)-g(x)h(x)\big|=g(u)h(u)\leq\tfrac{1}{\alpha}g(u)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\big(g(u)-g(x)\big)\leq\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\alpha\big|u-x\big|_{X}=\big|u-x\big|_{X},

and so,
\operatorname{Lip}f(x)\leq 1
. Hence,
\ell^{\infty}(f)\subseteq L^{\infty}(f)\subseteq X\setminus F=G
.
On the other hand, if
x\in U
, then the above inequality yields
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x)\leq 1
.
Therefore,
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)\subseteq X\setminus U=\overline{G}
.

Now we consider
x\in G
and we want to prove
\operatorname{lip}f(x)=+\infty
.
For any
r>0
and
u\in B(x,r)
we have that

\displaystyle\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|

\displaystyle=\big|g(u)h(u)-g(x)h(x)\big|

\displaystyle=\Big|g(x)\big(h(x)-h(u)\big)-h(u)\big(g(u)-g(x)\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\geq g(x)\big|h(x)-h(u)\big|-h(u)\big|g(u)-g(x)\big|

\displaystyle\geq g(x)\big|h(x)-h(u)\big|-\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\alpha|x-u|

\displaystyle\geq g(x)\big|h(x)-h(u)\big|-r.

Hence,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{u\in B(x,r)}\tfrac{1}{r}\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|

\displaystyle\geq g(x)\sup\limits_{u\in B(x,r)}\tfrac{1}{r}\big|h(u)-h(x)\big|-1

\displaystyle=g(x)\operatorname{Lip}^{r}h(x)-1.

Consequently, since
g(x)>0
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}f(x)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)

\displaystyle\geq g(x)\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}^{r}h(x)-1

\displaystyle=g(x)\operatorname{lip}h(x)-1=+\infty.

Hence,
\operatorname{lip}f(x)=\infty
on
G
, that is

G\subseteq\ell^{\infty}(f)
.
We have already proven that
\ell^{\infty}(f)\subseteq L^{\infty}(f)\subseteq G
and

\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)\subseteq\overline{G}
. So,
G=\ell^{\infty}(f)=L^{\infty}(f)\subseteq\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)
.
Since
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)
is closed, we also have
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)=\overline{G}
.
∎

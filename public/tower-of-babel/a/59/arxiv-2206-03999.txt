A complete metric topology on relative low energy spaces
Prakhar Gupta

Abstract
. In this paper, we show that the low energy spaces in the prescribed singularity case ℰ ψ ​ ( X , θ , ϕ ) \mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi) have a natural topology which is completely metrizable. This topology is stronger than convergence in capacity.

Primary: 32U05; Secondary: 32Q15, 53E30, 54E35

1.
Introduction

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold. By the
dd^{c}
-lemma, any Kähler metric cohomologous to
\omega
is of the form
\omega_{u}:=\omega+dd^{c}u
. This led to studying the space

\mathcal{H}=\{u\in C^{\infty}(X):\omega_{u}:=\omega+dd^{c}u>0\}

of smooth functions on
X
to find a canonical metric in the same cohomology class as
\omega
. Mabuchi
[
Mab87
]
, Semmes
[
Sem92
]
, and Donaldson
[
Don99
]
independently found a Riemannian structure on
\mathcal{H}
given by

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{u}:=\frac{1}{\text{Vol(X)}}\int_{X}\phi\psi\omega^{n}_{u}

for
u\in\mathcal{H}
and
\phi,\psi\in T_{u}\mathcal{H}=C^{\infty}(X)
. Later, Chen
[
Che00
]
showed that this Riemannian structure makes
\mathcal{H}
a metric space
(\mathcal{H},d_{2})
.

Darvas
[
Dar17
]
showed that the completion
\overline{(\mathcal{H},d_{2})}
can be identified with
(\mathcal{E}^{2}(X,\omega),d_{2})
, where
\mathcal{E}^{2}(X,\omega)
is the space of finite energy introduced by Guedj-Zeriahi
[
GZ07
]
. Upon introducing the Finsler type structure on
\mathcal{H}
, Darvas
[
Dar15
]
introduced metrics
d_{p}
on
\mathcal{H}
for
p\geq 1
and showed that their completions are
(\mathcal{E}^{p}(X,\omega),d_{p})
. The metric
(\mathcal{H},d_{1})
and its completion
(\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\omega),d_{1})
were useful in studying special metrics on Kähler manifolds
[
DR17
]
,
[
BDL20
]
,
[
CC21
]
,
[
CC21a
]
.

This led to further attempts to find natural complete metrics on the various subspaces of
\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
for varying
\theta
. Recall that, for a smooth closed
(1,1)
-form
\theta
we say that an integrable function
u:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is
\theta
-psh if locally
u
can be written as a sum of a smooth function and a plurisubharmonic function and
\theta+dd^{c}u\geq 0
in the sense of currents. The following list of works illustrates the interest in finding natural metrics on these spaces.

(1)

Darvas-Di Nezza-Lu
[
DNL21
]
found that the space
\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)
has a complete metric for
\theta
merely big.

(2)

Using approximation by Kähler classes Di Nezza-Lu found that the spaces
\mathcal{E}^{p}(X,\beta)
for
p\geq 1
have a complete metric for
\{\beta\}
a nef and big class in
H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

(3)

In
[
Tru22
]
, Trusiani showed that the space
\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\omega,\phi)
has a complete metric topology where
\omega
is a Kähler form and
\phi\in\text{PSH}(X,\omega)
is a model potential. The space
\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\omega,\phi)
consists of potentials more singular than
\phi
and having finite energy relative to
\phi
. See Section
2
for relevant definitions and results about model potentials and spaces with prescribed singularities.

(4)

Xia
[
Xia19
]
extended this result to show that the spaces
\mathcal{E}^{p}(X,\theta,\phi)
have complete metric space structure for
\theta
having big cohomology class and
\phi\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
a model potential.

(5)

Most recently, Darvas
[
Dar21
]
showed that the space
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\omega)
has a natural complete metric, when
\omega
is Kähler and
\psi
is a low energy weight as introduced by Guedj-Zeriahi
[
GZ07
]
. In the process, Darvas used the geodesics on
\mathcal{H}
introduced by
[
Mab87
]
,
[
Sem92
]
, and
[
Don99
]
.

Note that only
[
Dar21
]
deals with the low energy weights. Working with the low energy space is desirable because

\mathcal{E}(X,\omega)=\bigcup_{\psi}\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\omega)

where the union is over all low energy weights (see
[
GZ07
, Proposition 2.2]
). However, all the finite energy spaces
\mathcal{E}^{p}(X,\omega)
are contained in
\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\omega)\subsetneq\mathcal{E}(X,\omega)
. Another method of measuring distance between potentials
u,v\in\mathcal{E}(X,\omega)
is proposed by Lempert
[
Lem22
]
where he measures the distance
\rho(u,v)
by a function
\rho(u,v):(0,V)\to\mathbb{R}
where
V=\int_{X}\omega^{n}
is the volume of
(X,\omega)
.

In
[
Dar21
]
, the author noted that the metric
d_{\psi}
on the space
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\omega)
satisfies

d_{\psi}(u,v)\leq\int_{X}\psi(u-v)(\omega^{n}_{u}+\omega^{n}_{v})\leq 2^{2n+5}d_{\psi}(u,v)

for any
u,v\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\omega)
. In
[
Dar21
]
, the author asked if the central expression in the above equation can be shown to satisfy a quasi-triangle inequality, without constructing the metric
d_{\psi}
using geodesics in
\mathcal{H}
, and thus show that the spaces
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta)
have a quasi-metric space structure. This is the question we answer in this paper, in a more general context of low energy spaces in the prescribed singularity setting. Note that in the big case and in the prescribed singularity case, there are no
C^{1,1}
geodesics, and hence the methods of
[
Dar21
]
do not work.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\theta
be a closed smooth
(1,1)
-form whose cohomology class is big. Let
\phi\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
be a model potential such that
\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{\phi}>0
. Then for any
u,v\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
,

(1)

I_{\psi}(u,v):=\int_{X}\psi(u-v)(\theta^{n}_{u}+\theta^{n}_{v})

is a quasi-metric. Moreover, the topology induced on
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
by
I_{\psi}
is completely metrizable.

Here we say a few words about the prescribed singularity setting. Let
\phi\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
with
\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{\phi}>0
. We denote by
\text{PSH}(X,\theta,\phi)
the set of
\theta
-psh functions
u
that are more singular than
\phi
, meaning
u\leq\phi+C
for some constant
C
. In particular,
\text{PSH}(X,\theta)=\text{PSH}(X,\theta,V_{\theta})
. The set of relatively full mass potentials is given by

\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)=\{u\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta,\phi):\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{u}=\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{\phi}\}

and the set of relatively finite energy is given by

\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)=\left\{u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi):\int_{X}\psi(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u}<\infty\right\}.

See Section
2.1
to learn more about potentials in prescribed singularity setting.

After this we study some properties of the new topology on
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
. The usual topology on
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
given by
L^{1}(\omega^{n})
is not satisfactory for the purposes of studying Monge-Ampère equation primarily because for
u_{k},u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that
u_{k}\to u
in
L^{1}(\omega^{n})
does not imply that the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measures satisfy
\theta^{n}_{u_{k}}\to\theta^{n}_{u}
. Hence the following result shows that the new topology is stronger and more natural.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
u_{k},u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that
I_{\psi}(u_{k},u)\to 0
as
k\to\infty
then
u_{k}\to u
in capacity and hence
\theta^{n}_{u_{k}}\to\theta^{n}_{u}
weakly as measures. In particular,
\int_{X}|u_{k}-u|\omega^{n}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
as well.

This result is new in the Kähler case when
\psi(t)
grows slower than
|t|
. When
\psi(t)=|t|
and
\phi=V_{\theta}
the model potential with minimal singularity, in
[
BBGZ13
]
the authors show that a closely related functional,

\tilde{I}(u,v)=\int_{X}(u-v)(\theta^{n}_{u}-\theta^{n}_{V})

satisfy the same properties as in Theorem
1.2
. Moreover, in
[
Tru20
]
the author shows the same results using
\tilde{I}
for
\psi(t)=|t|
,
\theta
a Kähler class and
\phi
any model prescribed singularity.

At the end we give an application to the Kähler-Ricci flow. Guedj-Zeriahi
[
GZ17
]
and
[
DL17
]
showed that given a potential
u\in\text{PSH}(X,\omega)
such that
u
has zero Lelong numbers we have smooth function
u_{t}
for
t>0
such that

(2)

\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial t}=\log\left[\frac{(\omega+dd^{c}u_{t})^{n}}{\omega^{n}}\right],u_{t}\to u\text{ in }L^{1}(\omega^{n})\text{ as }t\to 0.

If
\omega_{t}:=\omega+dd^{c}u_{t}
, then the above equation is equivalent to

\frac{\partial\omega_{t}}{\partial t}=-\text{Ric}(\omega_{t})+\text{Ric}(\omega).

Moreover, these functions satisfy
u_{t}\to u
in capacity as
t\to 0
. In case
u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\omega)
we show a stronger convergence
u_{t}\to u
as
t\to 0
in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\omega)
and
u_{t}
satisfy (
2
) then
I_{\psi}(u_{t},u)\to 0
and thus by Theorem
1.2
, we recover that
u_{t}\to u
in capacity and
\omega^{n}_{u_{t}}\to\omega^{n}_{u}
weakly.

Here we point out that Theorem
1.3
shows that any non-pluripolar measure can be approximated by measures with smooth density using Kähler-Ricci flow.

Organization

In Section
2
, we setup the notation and mention all the relevant results required for the theorems we prove. In Section
3
, we show that
I_{\psi}
is a quasi-metric on
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
. In Section
4
, we show that the induced topology on
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
is completely metrizable thereby completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In Section
5
, we discuss some relevant properties of the new topology and prove Theorem
1.2
. In Section
6
, we discuss an application to Kähler Ricci flow and prove Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we fix the notations and give relevant definitions and results.

We work on a fixed Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
. Let
\theta
be a closed smooth
(1,1)
-form on
X
. An integrable function
u:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is a
\theta
-psh if locally
u
can be written as a sum of a smooth function and a plurisubharmonic function and
\theta+dd^{c}u\geq 0
in the sense of currents. We denote by
\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
the set of all
\theta
-psh functions. Let
\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be the cohomology class represented by
\theta
. We say
\alpha
is big if there exists
\varepsilon>0
and
u\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta-\varepsilon\omega)
. See
[
BEGZ10
]
to learn more about pluripotential theory on compact Kähler manifolds in big cohomology classes. In particular, op. cit. describes how to define the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measure
\theta^{n}_{u}
for any
u\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
.

2.1.
Prescribed Singularity setting

In
[
DDL18
]
and
[
DDL21
]
the authors developed the theory of pluripotential theory in prescribed singularity setting. For two potentials
u,v\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
, we say that
u
is
more singular than

v
if
u\leq v+C
for some constant
C
. We denote by
[u]=[v]
the fact that
u
and
v
have the same singularity type. Given a potential
\phi\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
, we denote by
\text{PSH}(X,\theta,\phi)
the set of all potentials
v\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
such that
v
is more singular than
\phi
. To solve the Monge-Ampère equation with prescribed singularity, Darvas-Di Nezza-Lu
[
DDL18
]
defined the space of relatively full mass potentials as

\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi):=\left\{u\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta,\phi):\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{u}=\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{\phi}]\right\}.

Next, we want to define some subspaces of
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
which consists of potentials having relatively finite ‘
\psi
-energy’ for some weight
\psi
. By a weight
\psi
, we mean a function
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\psi
is even, continuous,
\psi(0)=0
,
\psi(\pm\infty)=\infty
, and on
(0,\infty)

\psi
is smooth and increasing. We say
\psi
is
low energy
if
\psi
is concave on
(0,\infty)
and
\psi
is
high energy
if
\psi
is convex on
(0,\infty)
. We denote by
\mathcal{W}^{-}
the set of all low energy weights. For example, the weight
\psi(t)=|t|^{p}
is high energy for
p\geq 1
and low energy for
0<p\leq 1
. For each weight
\psi\in\mathcal{W}^{-}
, we define the space of potentials with finite
\psi
-energy relative to
\phi
as

\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)=\left\{u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi):\int_{X}\psi(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u}<\infty\right\}.

As in the Kähler case, in the prescribed singularity case we also have

\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)=\bigcup_{\psi\in\mathcal{W}^{-}}\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi).

We list a few results about the spaces
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
that we use frequently in the rest of the paper.

Lemma 2.1

(Comparison Principle)
.

[
DDL18
, Corollary 3.6]
Let
\phi\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
and
u,v\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Then

\int_{\{u<v\}}\theta^{n}_{v}\leq\int_{\{u<v\}}\theta^{n}_{u}.

Lemma 2.2

(The Fundamental inequality)
.

[
TV21
, Proposition 3.3]
If
u,v\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta,\phi)
are such that
u\leq v\leq\phi
then

\int_{X}\psi(v-\phi)\theta^{n}_{v}\leq 2^{n+1}\int_{X}\psi(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u}.

Thus for any
u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
and
v\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that
u\leq v
we have
v\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
.

Proof.

We give a simplified proof based on the argument of
[
DDL21
, Lemma 2.4]
.

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi(v-\phi)\theta^{n}_{v}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(t)\theta^{n}_{v}(v<\phi-t)dt

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(2t)\theta^{n}_{v}(v<\phi-2t)dt.

Now observe that
\{v<\phi-2t\}\subset\{u<\frac{v+\phi}{2}-t\}\subset\{u<\phi-t\}
, thus using Lemma
2.1
and the fact that
\theta^{n}_{v}\leq 2^{n}\theta^{n}_{(v+\phi)/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(2t)\theta^{n}_{v}\left(u<\frac{v+\phi}{2}-t\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(2t)\theta^{n}_{(v+\phi)/2}\left(u<\frac{v+\phi}{2}-t\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(2t)\theta^{n}_{u}(u<\phi-t)dt.

Since
\psi
is concave, we get
\psi^{\prime}(2t)\leq\psi^{\prime}(t)
.

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(t)\theta^{n}_{u}(u<\phi-t)dt

\displaystyle=2^{n+1}\int_{X}\psi(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u}.

∎

Lemma 2.3

(Integrability)
.

Given
u,v\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
we have

\int_{X}\psi(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{v}<+\infty.

In particular, if
u,v\leq\phi
, then

\int_{X}\psi(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{v}\leq 2\int_{X}\psi(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u}+2\int_{X}\psi(v-\phi)\theta^{n}_{v}.

Proof.

The proof again generalizes the proof of
[
DDL21
, Lemma 2.5]
and
[
GZ07
, Proposition 2.5]
.

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{v}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(t)\theta^{n}_{v}\{t<\phi-u\}dt

\displaystyle=2\int_{X}\psi^{\prime}(2t)\theta^{n}_{v}\{2t<\phi-u\}dt

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{X}\psi^{\prime}(t)\theta^{n}_{v}\{u<\phi-2t\}dt.

Notice that
\{u<\phi-2t\}\subset\{u<-t+v\}\cup\{v<\phi-t\}
. This gives

\displaystyle\theta^{n}_{v}\{u<\phi-2t\}

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{n}_{v}\{u<-t+v\}+\theta^{n}_{v}\{v<\phi-t\}

and Lemma
2.1
gives

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{n}_{u}\{u<-t+v\}+\theta^{n}_{v}\{v<\phi-t\}.

Notice that
\{u<-t+v\}=\{u-\phi<-t+v-\phi\}
. Since
v-\phi\leq 0
, we get
\{u<-t+v\}\subset\{u-\phi<-t\}.
This gives us

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{n}_{u}\{u-\phi<-t\}+\theta^{n}_{v}\{v-\phi<-t\}.

Using this we get

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{X}\psi^{\prime}(t)(\theta^{n}_{u}\{u-\phi<-t\}+\theta^{n}_{v}\{v-\phi<-t\})

\displaystyle=2\int_{X}\psi(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u}+2\int_{X}\psi(v-\phi)\theta^{n}_{v}.

Now assume
u,v\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
then for some
C
, we have
u,v\leq\phi+C
. Let
\tilde{u}=u-C
and
\tilde{v}=v-C
, so
\tilde{u},\tilde{v}\leq\phi
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{v}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\psi(\tilde{u}-\phi+C)\theta^{n}_{\tilde{v}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\psi(\tilde{u}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{\tilde{v}}+\int_{X}\psi(C)\theta^{n}_{\tilde{v}}<\infty

∎

Corollary 2.4
.

The proposed quasi-metric
I_{\psi}
(see Equation (
1
)) on
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
is always finite.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Then

\displaystyle I_{\psi}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\psi(u-v)(\theta^{n}_{u}+\theta^{n}_{v})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}(\psi(u-\phi)+\psi(v-\phi))(\theta^{n}_{u}+\theta^{n}_{v}).

Here we used
[
Dar21
, Lemma 2.6]
which states that for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, and any
\psi\in\mathcal{W}^{-}
, we have
\psi(a+b)\leq\psi(a)+\psi(b)
. By Lemma
2.3
all the terms in the expression above are finite.
∎

Lemma 2.5

(Domination Principle)
.

[
DDL18
, Proposition 3.11]
. Let
\phi\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
such that
\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{\phi}>0
. Then for
u,v\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
, if
\theta^{n}_{u}(\{u<v\})=0
, then
u\geq v
.

We also need the following slight generalization of
[
DDL21a
, Theorem 2.2]
, removing the assumption of uniform boundedness of
\chi_{k}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\theta^{j}
,
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
be smooth closed
(1,1)
-forms on
X
whose cohomology classes are big. Let
u_{j},u_{j}^{k}\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta^{j})
are such that
u_{j}^{k}\to u_{j}
in capacity as
k\to\infty
. Let
\chi_{k},\chi\geq 0
be quasi-continuous functions such that
\chi_{k}\to\chi
in capacity as
k\to\infty
. Then

\int_{X}\chi\theta^{1}_{u_{1}}\wedge\theta^{2}_{u_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta^{n}_{u_{n}}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\chi_{k}\theta^{1}_{u_{1}^{k}}\wedge\theta^{2}_{u_{2}^{k}}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta^{n}_{u_{n}^{k}}.

Proof.

Consider
\chi_{k,C}=\min(\chi_{k},C)
. Then
\chi_{k,C}
are uniformly bounded and quasi-continuous and
\chi_{k,C}\to\chi_{C}
in capacity. Therefore, using
[
DDL21a
, Theorem 2.2]

\int_{X}\chi_{C}\theta^{1}_{u_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta^{n}_{u_{n}}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\chi_{k,C}\theta^{1}_{u_{1}^{k}}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta^{n}_{u_{n}^{j}}

Since
\chi_{k,C}\leq\chi_{k}
, we have

\int_{X}\chi_{C}\theta^{1}_{u_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta^{n}_{u_{n}}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\chi_{k,C}\theta^{1}_{u_{1}^{k}}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta^{n}_{u_{n}^{k}}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\chi_{k}\theta^{1}_{u_{1}^{k}}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta^{n}_{u_{n}^{k}}

As
\chi_{C}\nearrow\chi
as
C\to\infty
and applying monotone convergence theorem, we get

\int_{X}\chi\theta^{1}_{u_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta^{n}_{u_{n}}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\chi_{k}\theta^{1}_{u_{1}^{k}}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta^{n}_{u_{n}^{k}}.

∎

The following result is mentioned in
[
TV21
, Corollary 3.4]
. We present a proof here for completeness.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\phi\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
such that
\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{\phi}>0
. Let
u_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that

\sup_{j}\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}<\infty.

If
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{1}(\omega^{n})
for some
u\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
, then
u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
.

Proof.

Since there exists
A
such that
\sup_{X}u_{j}\leq A
for all
j
(see
[
GZBook
, Proposition 8.4]
), and the fact that
\psi(a+b)\leq\psi(a)+\psi(b)
for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
we can assume without loss of generality that
u_{j},u\leq\phi
for all
j
.

First, we assume that
u_{j}\searrow u
. This implies that
u_{j}\to u
in capacity. Also,
\psi(u_{k}-\phi)\geq 0
are quasicontinuous functions which converge in capacity to
\psi(u-\phi)
. Thus by Theorem
2.6
, we get that

\int_{X}\psi(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\psi(u_{k}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u_{k}}<\infty.

In general, if
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{1}(\omega^{n})
and if
v_{j}:=(\sup_{k\geq j}u_{k})^{*}
, then
u_{j}\leq v_{j}\leq\phi
. Thus, by Lemma
2.2
we get

\sup_{j}\int_{X}\psi(v_{j}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{v_{j}}\leq 2^{n+1}\sup\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}<\infty.

Since
v_{j}\searrow u
, by the argument above, we get that

\int_{X}\psi(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u}<\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}\psi(v_{j}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{v_{j}}<\infty.

Although this shows that
u
has finite energy, we are yet to show that
u
has full mass.
By
[
DDL18
, Lemma 3.4]
we just need to show that
\int_{\{u\leq\phi-C\}}\theta^{n}_{u^{C}}\to 0
as
C\to\infty
. Here
u^{C}=\max(u,\phi-C)
. First, notice that
v_{j}^{C}\searrow u^{C}
in
j\to\infty
.

\int_{X}\psi(u^{C}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u^{C}}\leq\liminf_{j}\int_{X}\psi(v_{j}^{C}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{v^{C}_{j}}\leq 2^{n+1}\liminf_{j}\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}.

Thus

\int_{\{u\leq\phi-C\}}\theta^{n}_{u^{C}}\leq\frac{1}{\psi(C)}\int_{X}\psi(u^{C}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u^{C}}\leq\frac{2^{n+1}}{\psi(C)}\sup_{j}\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}\to 0

as
C\to\infty
. This finishes the proof that
u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
.
∎

2.2.
The operator
P_{\theta}(u,v)

The notion of model potential and model type singularity was introduced by Darvas-Di Nezza-Lu
[
DDL18
]
to solve the Monge-Ampère equation in the prescribed singularity setting. In particular, in
[
DDL21
]
, the authors showed that if
\phi
is a model potential and if
\mu
is a non-pluripolar positive measure such that
\mu(X)=\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{\phi}>0
, then there unique (upto a constant)
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that
\theta^{n}_{u}=\mu
.

Recall that for an upper semicontinuous function
f
, we define
P_{\theta}(f)
to be the largest
\theta
-psh function lying below
f
. In particular,

P_{\theta}(f):=\sup\{v\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta):v\leq f\}.

When
u,v\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
, we say
P_{\theta}(u,v):=P_{\theta}(\min(u,v))
. For
\phi\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
, the envelope of its singularity type is defined as

P_{\theta}[\phi]:=\sup\{v\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta,\phi):v\leq 0\}.

A potential
\phi\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
is called a
model potential
if
\phi=P_{\theta}[\phi]
. Their importance in understanding the space
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
and
\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
and solving the Monge-Ampère equations is described in
[
DDL18
]
and
[
DDL21
]
. In the rest of the section
\phi
is a model potential.

The following part is adapted from
[
DDL18a
]
. We will use the following lemma repeatedly in the arguments ahead.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\mu
be a non-pluripolar measure such that
0<\mu(X)<\infty
and
\lambda>0
. Let
u,v\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
satisfy

\theta^{n}_{u}\geq e^{\lambda u-w}\mu,\theta^{n}_{v}\leq e^{\lambda v-w}\mu

for some Borel measurable function
w:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
that is bounded from above. Then
u\leq v
on
X
.

Proof.

The proof is same as in
[
DDL18a
, Lemma 2.5]
, adapted to the prescribed singularity case. Using the comparison principle, we have

\int_{\{v<u\}}e^{\lambda u-w}\mu\leq\int_{\{v<u\}}\theta^{n}_{u}\leq\int_{\{v<u\}}\theta^{n}_{v}\leq\int_{\{v<u\}}e^{\lambda v-w}\mu\leq\int_{\{v<u\}}e^{\lambda u-w}d\mu.

Thus all the expressions are equal and we get

\int_{\{v<u\}}\left(e^{\lambda u}-e^{\lambda v}\right)e^{-w}\mu=0.

Since
w
is bounded from above,
e^{-w}
is never 0. Thus
\mu\{v<u\}=0
and hence
\theta^{n}_{v}(\{v<u\})=0
. Now the domination principle (see Lemma
2.5
) implies that
u\leq v
.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
u,v\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Then

P_{\theta}(u,v):=\sup\{w\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta):w\leq\min(u,v)\}\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi).

In particular, if
u,v\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
, then
P_{\theta}(u,v)\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
.

Proof.

Let
u_{j}=\max(u,\phi-j)
and
v_{j}=\max(v,\phi-j)
. Then
u_{j}
and
v_{j}
have the same singularity type as
\phi
. Thus by Lemma
2.10
below, there is a unique function
\varphi_{j}\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
with the same singularity type as
\phi
such that

(3)

\theta^{n}_{\varphi_{j}}=e^{\varphi_{j}-u_{j}}\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}+e^{\varphi_{j}-v_{j}}\theta^{n}_{v_{j}}.

Notice that
\theta^{n}_{\varphi_{j}}\geq e^{\varphi_{j}-u_{j}}\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}
. Defining
\mu=e^{-u_{j}}\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}
, we see that Lemma
2.8
implies that
\varphi_{j}\leq u_{j}
, and similarly,
\varphi_{j}\leq v_{j}
. Therefore,
\varphi_{j}\leq\min(u_{j},v_{j})
. Consequently,
\varphi_{j}\leq P_{\theta}(u_{j},v_{j})
. Now we claim that

(4)

\sup_{j}\int_{X}\psi(\varphi_{j}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{\varphi_{j}}<\infty.

By Equation (
3
), it is enough to show that

(5)

\sup_{j}\int_{X}\psi(\varphi_{j}-\phi)e^{\varphi_{j}-u_{j}}\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}<\infty.

Again, using the fact that
\psi(a+b)\leq\psi(a)+\psi(b)
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi(\varphi_{j}-\phi)e^{\varphi_{j}-u_{j}}\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\psi(\varphi_{j}-u_{j})e^{\varphi_{j}-u_{j}}\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}+\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\phi)e^{\varphi_{j}-u_{j}}\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}.

Since
\psi(t)e^{t}\leq C
for some fixed
C
and for all
t\leq 0
, observing
\varphi_{j}\leq u_{j}
we get that

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{\phi}+\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}.

As
u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
, we get Equation (
5
) and consequently Equation (
4
).

Since
\varphi_{j}\leq u_{j}\leq u_{1}
, we get that
\sup_{X}\varphi_{j}
is uniformly bounded. By the proof of Lemma
5.1
, we also get that
\varphi_{j}\not\to-\infty
uniformly. Thus upto choosing a subsequence, we get that there exists
\varphi\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
such that
\varphi_{j}\to\varphi
in
L^{1}(\omega^{n})
. By Lemma
2.7
we get that
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Since
\varphi_{j}\leq P_{\theta}(u_{j},v_{j})
, we get that
\varphi\leq P_{\theta}(u,v)
. Thus Lemma
2.2
tells us that
P_{\theta}(u,v)\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
.

∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
u,v\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that
u,v
have the same singularity type as
\phi
. Then there exists a unique
\varphi\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta,\phi)
with the same singularity type as
\phi
such that

\theta^{n}_{\varphi}=e^{\varphi-u}\theta^{n}_{u}+e^{\varphi-v}\theta^{n}_{v}.

Proof.

First we show uniqueness. Let
\tilde{\varphi}
be another solution. Let
\mu=e^{u}\theta^{n}_{v}+e^{v}\theta^{n}_{u}
and
w=u+v
. Then
\mu(X)<\infty
and
w
is bounded from above. Notice that
\theta^{n}_{\varphi}=e^{\varphi-w}\mu
and
\theta^{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}=e^{\tilde{\varphi}-w}\mu
. By Lemma
2.8
we get that
\varphi=\tilde{\varphi}
.

Let
u_{j}=\max(u,-j)
. Note that
u_{j}
is no longer a
\theta
-psh function, but now it is a bounded function. Consider the measure

\mu_{j}=e^{-u_{j}}\theta^{n}_{u}+e^{-v_{j}}\theta^{n}_{v}.

Then
\mu_{j}
is a non-pluripolar positive measure. By
[
DDL18
, Theorem 4.23]
, we get that there exists a unique
\varphi_{j}\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that

\theta^{n}_{\varphi_{j}}=e^{\varphi_{j}}\mu_{j}.

Since
u
and
v
have the same singularity type, there exists
C
such that
\sup_{X}|u-v|\leq 2C
. Consider the function

w=\frac{u+v}{2}-C-(n+1)\ln 2.

Observing
\theta^{n}_{u},\theta^{n}_{v}\leq 2^{n}\theta^{n}_{w}
, we get

\theta^{n}_{w}\geq e^{w}\mu_{j}.

Now Lemma
2.8
implies that
w\leq\varphi_{j}
. As
w
has relative minimal singularity type we obtain
\varphi_{j}
has relative minimal singularity type as well. Notice that for
j\geq k
, we have
\mu_{j}\geq\mu_{k}
. Thus,

\theta^{n}_{\varphi_{j}}\geq e^{\varphi_{j}}\mu_{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\theta^{n}_{\varphi_{k}}=e^{\varphi_{k}}\mu_{k}.

Lemma
2.8
again shows that
\varphi_{k}\geq\varphi_{j}
. Thus
\varphi_{j}
is decreases as
j\to\infty
. Let
\varphi=\lim_{j}\varphi_{j}
. Then
w\leq\varphi
thus
\varphi
has the relative minimal singularity type and by continuity of non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère operator under decreasing sequences, we get that

\theta^{n}_{\varphi}=e^{\varphi-u}\theta^{n}_{u}+e^{\varphi-v}\theta^{n}_{v}.

∎

2.3.
Metrics from a quasi-metric

If we relax the condition of the triangle inequality from the definition of a metric space, we obtain what we call a quasi-metric space.

Definition 2.11

(Quasi-metric space)
.

Given a set
X
a function
\rho:X\times X\to[0,\infty)
is a quasi-metric if it satisfies

(1)

(Non-degeneracy) For any
x,y\in X
,
\rho(x,y)=0
iff
x=y
.

(2)

(Symmetry) For all
x,y\in X
,
\rho(x,y)=\rho(y,x)
.

(3)

(Quasi-triangle inequality)There exists
C\geq 1
such that
\rho(x,y)\leq C(\rho(x,z)+\rho(y,z))
for all
x,y,z\in X
.

In
[
PS09
]
the authors show that given any quasi-metric, we can construct a metric comparable to the quasi-metric using a
p
-chain approach. In particular, if we consider
d_{p}:X\times X\to[0,\infty)
given by

d_{p}(x,y)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=0}\rho(x_{n},x_{n+1})^{p}:x_{0},\dots,x_{n+1}\in X\text{ such that }x=x_{0},y=x_{n+1}\right\}

then
d_{p}
is symmetric, satisfies triangle inequality, but in general
d_{p}(x,y)=0
even if
x\neq y
. But if
0<p\leq 1
is chosen such that
(2C)^{p}=2
, then
d_{p}
is non-degenerate and moreover,

d_{p}(x,y)\leq\rho(x,y)^{p}\leq 4d_{p}(x,y).

This shows that if for
x_{n},x\in X
we have
\rho(x_{n},x)\to 0
iff
d_{p}(x_{k},x)\to 0
. Thus
\rho
and
d_{p}
induce the same topology.

3.
Quasi-metric on
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)

In this section we prove that the expression

I_{\psi}(u,v)=\int_{X}\psi(u-v)(\theta^{n}_{u}+\theta^{n}_{v})

is a quasi-metric on
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
where
\phi\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
is such that
\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{\phi}>0
.

Theorem 3.1

(Quasi-triangle inequality)
.

Let
\phi\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
be such that
\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{\phi}>0
, then for any
u,v,w\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
, the functional

I_{\psi}(u,v)=\int_{X}\psi(u-v)(\theta^{n}_{u}+\theta^{n}_{v})

satisfies

I_{\psi}(u,v)\leq C(I_{\psi}(u,w)+I_{\psi}(v,w))

for
C=8\cdot 3^{n+1}
.

Proof.

The proof is inspired from the proof in
[
GLZ19
, Theorem 1.6]
. We start with

(6)

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi(u-v)\theta^{n}_{u}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta^{n}_{u}(\{\psi(u-v)>t\})dt.

Changing the variable
t=\psi(2s)
, we get
dt=2\psi^{\prime}(2s)ds
. Thus

(7)

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(2s)\theta^{n}_{u}(\{\psi(u-v)>\psi(2s)\})ds

(8)

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(2s)\theta^{n}_{u}(\{|u-v|>2s\})ds.

Observe that
\{w-s\leq u<v-2s\}\subset\{w<\frac{u+2v}{3}-\frac{s}{3}\}
and
\{u<v-2s\}\subset\{u<w-s\}\cup\{w<\frac{u+2v}{3}-\frac{s}{3}\}
to obtain

(9)

\displaystyle\theta^{n}_{u}(\{u<v-2s\})

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{n}_{u}(\{u<w-s\})+\theta^{n}_{u}\left(\left\{w<\frac{u+2v}{3}-\frac{s}{3}\right\}\right).

Since
\theta^{n}_{u}\leq 3^{n}\theta^{n}_{\frac{u+2v}{3}}
and
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta,\phi)
is convex
[
DDL18
, Corollary 3.15]
, we get

(10)

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{n}_{u}(\{u<w-s\})+3^{n}\theta^{n}_{\frac{u+2v}{3}}\left(\left\{w<\frac{u+2v}{3}-\frac{s}{3}\right\}\right).

Now Lemma
2.1
gives

(11)

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{n}_{u}(\{u<w-s\})+3^{n}\theta^{n}_{w}\left(\left\{w<\frac{u+2v}{3}-\frac{s}{3}\right\}\right).

Again by the comparison principle we have

\displaystyle\theta^{n}_{u}(\{v<u-2s\})

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{n}_{v}(\{v<u-2s\}).

By a similar computation as in Equation (
11
) we get

(12)

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{n}_{v}(\{v<w-s\})+3^{n}\theta^{n}_{w}\left(\left\{w<\frac{v+2u}{3}-\frac{s}{3}\right\}\right).

Combining Equation (
11
) and (
12
) we get

\displaystyle\theta^{n}_{u}(\{|u-v|>2s\})

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{n}_{u}(\{u<v-2s\})+\theta^{n}_{u}(\{v<u-2s\})

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{n}_{u}(\{u<w-s\})+3^{n}\theta^{n}_{w}\left(\left\{w<\frac{u+2v}{3}-\frac{s}{3}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\theta^{n}_{v}(\{v<w-s\})+3^{n}\theta^{n}_{w}\left(\left\{w<\frac{v+2u}{3}-\frac{s}{3}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{n}_{u}(\{|u-w|>s\})+3^{n}\theta^{n}_{w}\left(\left\{\left|w-\frac{u+2v}{3}\right|>\frac{s}{3}\right\}\right)

(13)

\displaystyle+\theta^{n}_{v}(\{|v-w|>s\})+3^{n}\theta^{n}_{w}\left(\left\{\left|w-\frac{v+2u}{3}\right|>\frac{s}{3}\right\}\right).

Combining Equations (
8
) and (
13
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi(u-v)\theta^{n}_{u}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(2s)\theta^{n}_{u}(\{|u-w|>s\})ds

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(2s)\theta^{n}_{v}(\{|v-w|>s\})ds

\displaystyle+2\cdot 3^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(2s)\theta^{n}_{w}\left(\left\{\left|w-\frac{u+2v}{3}\right|>\frac{s}{3}\right\}\right)ds

(14)

\displaystyle+2\cdot 3^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(2s)\theta^{n}_{w}\left(\left\{\left|w-\frac{v+2u}{3}\right|>\frac{s}{3}\right\}\right)ds.

Since
\psi
is concave, therefore the slope is decreasing. Hence
\psi^{\prime}(2s)\leq\psi^{\prime}(s)
and
\psi^{\prime}(2s)<\psi^{\prime}(s/3)
. Using this in Equation (
14
) we get

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s)\theta^{n}_{u}(\{|u-w|>s\})ds

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s)\theta^{n}_{v}(\{|v-w|>s\})ds

\displaystyle+2\cdot 3^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s/3)\theta^{n}_{w}\left(\left\{\left|w-\frac{u+2v}{3}\right|>\frac{s}{3}\right\}\right)ds

(15)

\displaystyle+2\cdot 3^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s/3)\theta^{n}_{w}\left(\left\{\left|w-\frac{v+2u}{3}\right|>\frac{s}{3}\right\}\right)ds.

Changing the variable again
t=\psi(s)
in the first two terms and
t=\psi(s/3)
in the last two terms, we get

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta^{n}_{u}(\{\psi(u-w)>t\})dt

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta^{n}_{v}(\{\psi(v-w)>t\})dt

\displaystyle+2\cdot 3^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta^{n}_{w}\left(\left\{\psi\left(w-\frac{u+2v}{3}\right)>t\right\}\right)dt

(16)

\displaystyle+2\cdot 3^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta^{n}_{w}\left(\left\{\psi\left(w-\frac{v+2u}{3}\right)>t\right\}\right)dt.

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi(u-v)\theta^{n}_{u}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{X}\psi(u-w)\theta^{n}_{u}+2\int_{X}\psi(v-w)\theta^{n}_{v}

\displaystyle+2\cdot 3^{n+1}\int_{X}\psi\left(w-\frac{u+2v}{3}\right)\theta^{n}_{w}

(17)

\displaystyle+2\cdot 3^{n+1}\int_{X}\psi\left(w-\frac{v+2u}{3}\right)\theta^{n}_{w}.

Using the fact that
\psi(a+b)\leq\psi(a)+\psi(b)
for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, (see
[
Dar21
, Lemma 2.6]
) we get

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{X}\psi(u-w)\theta^{n}_{u}+2\int_{X}\psi(v-w)\theta^{n}_{v}

\displaystyle+2\cdot 3^{n+1}\int_{X}\left(\psi\left(\frac{w-u}{3}\right)+\psi\left(\frac{2(w-v)}{3}\right)\right)\theta^{n}_{w}

(18)

\displaystyle+2\cdot 3^{n+1}\int_{X}\left(\psi\left(\frac{w-v}{3}\right)+\psi\left(\frac{2(w-u)}{3}\right)\right)\theta^{n}_{w}.

Since
\psi
is increasing in
(0,\infty)
and symmetric, we get that
\psi(x/3)\leq\psi(x)
and
\psi(2x/3)\leq\psi(x)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, thus

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{X}\psi(u-w)\theta^{n}_{u}+2\int_{X}\psi(v-w)\theta^{n}_{v}

(19)

\displaystyle+4\cdot 3^{n+1}\int_{X}\psi(w-u)\theta^{n}_{w}+4\cdot 3^{n+1}\int_{X}\psi(w-v)\theta^{n}_{w}

(20)

\displaystyle\leq 4\cdot 3^{n+1}(I(u,w)+I(v,w)).

A similar computation for
\int_{X}\psi(u-v)\theta^{n}_{v}
gives

(21)

\displaystyle I(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq 8\cdot 3^{n+1}(I(u,w)+I(v,w)).

This finishes the proof of quasi-triangle inequality.
∎

Theorem 3.2

(Non-degeneracy)
.

If
u,v\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
, such that
I_{\psi}(u,v)=0
, then
u=v
.

Proof.

Let
I_{\psi}(u,v)=0
. Then

\int_{X}\psi(u-v)\theta^{n}_{u}+\int_{X}\psi(u-v)\theta^{n}_{v}=0.

Thus we have
u=v
almost everywhere with respect to
\theta^{n}_{u}
and
\theta^{n}_{v}
. Using Lemma
2.5
, we get that
u=v
.
∎

The symmetry of
I_{\psi}(u,v)
follows from the fact that the weight function is an even function. This finishes the proof of the fact that
I_{\psi}
is a quasi-metric on
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
.

4.
Completeness

In this section we finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
by showing that the quasi-metric is complete.

We need the following lemma to construct a monotone sequence and show that it converges.

Theorem 4.1

(Pythagorean Identity)
.

Let
\phi\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
and let
u,v\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
. We know that
\max(u,v)\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Moreover, they satisfy

I_{\psi}(u,v)=I_{\psi}(\max(u,v),u)+I_{\psi}(\max(u,v),v).

Proof.

\displaystyle I_{\psi}(\max(u,v),u)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\psi(\max(u,v)-u)(\theta^{n}_{\max(u,v)}+\theta^{n}_{u})

\displaystyle=\int_{\{v>u\}}\psi(v-u)(\theta_{\max(u,v)}^{n}+\theta^{n}_{u}).

Since
u\mapsto\theta^{n}_{u}
is plurifine local, we have
\mathds{1}_{\{v>u\}}\theta^{n}_{\max(u,v)}=\mathds{1}_{\{v>u\}}\theta^{n}_{v}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{v>u\}}\psi(v-u)(\theta^{n}_{v}+\theta^{n}_{u}).

Similarly,

\displaystyle I_{\psi}(\max(u,v),v)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\psi(\max(u,v)-v)(\theta^{n}_{\max(u,v)}+\theta^{n}_{v})

and the same computation as before gives

\displaystyle=\int_{\{u>v\}}\psi(u-v)(\theta^{n}_{u}+\theta^{n}_{v}).

Adding the two gives

\displaystyle I_{\psi}(\max(u,v),u)+I_{\psi}(\max(u,v),v)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\psi(u-v)(\theta^{n}_{u}+\theta^{n}_{v})

\displaystyle=I_{\psi}(u,v).

∎

Proposition 4.2
.

If
u_{j},u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that
u_{j}\searrow u
or
u_{j}\nearrow u
, then

\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-u)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}\to 0.

Consequently, by the the monotone convergence theorem
I_{\psi}(u_{j},u)\to 0
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
u_{j},u\leq\phi\leq 0
. First, we assume that
\phi-L\leq u_{j},u\leq\phi
. We also assume that
u_{j}\searrow u
and the proof for
u_{j}\nearrow u
is similar. Thus
0\leq u_{j}-u\leq L
, which implies
0\leq\psi(u_{j}-u)\leq\psi(L)
. For some large enough
A
,
\theta\leq A\omega
. Thus
\text{PSH}(X,\theta)\subset\text{PSH}(X,A\omega)
, and we get that
u_{j},u\in\text{PSH}(X,A\omega)
. Hence they are quasi-continuous (at least with respect to
\text{Cap}_{\omega}
). So we can find an open set
O
such that
u_{j},u
are continuous on
X\setminus O
and
\text{Cap}_{\omega}(O)<\varepsilon
. The next part follows the proof in
[
DDL18a
, Corollary 2.12]
adapted to our case.
We have

\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-u)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}=\int_{X\setminus O}\psi(u_{j}-u)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}+\int_{O}\psi(u_{j}-u)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}.

Since
X\setminus O
is compact and
\psi(u_{j}-u)
are continuous functions decreasing to
0
, by Dini’s theorem, we get that the convergence is uniform. So for large enough
j
,
\psi(u_{j}-u)\leq\varepsilon
on
X\setminus O
.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{O}\psi(u_{j}-u)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\psi(L)\int_{O}\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\psi(L)L^{n}\text{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}(O)

\displaystyle\leq\psi(L)L^{n}f(\text{Cap}_{\omega}(O))

\displaystyle\leq\psi(L)L^{n}f(\varepsilon).

Here we used
[
Lu21
, Theorem 1.1]
with
(\theta_{1},\psi_{1})=(\theta,\phi)
and
(\theta_{2},\psi_{2})=(\omega,0)
and thus
\text{Cap}_{\theta,\phi}\leq f(\text{Cap}_{\omega})
where
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is continuous and
f(0)=0
. Combining these two results we get that for large enough
j
,

\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-u)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}\leq\varepsilon\text{vol}(\theta^{n})+\tilde{C}f(\varepsilon).

Therefore,
\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-u)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}\to 0
.

Now we remove the assumption that
\phi-L\leq u_{j}\leq\phi
. Let
u_{j}^{L}=\max\{u_{j},\phi-L\}
and
u^{L}=\max\{u,\phi-L\}
.
We want to show that

\left|\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-u)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}-\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}^{L}-u^{L})\theta^{n}_{u_{j}^{L}}\right|\to 0\text{ as }L\to\infty

uniformly with respect to
j
. Since
u_{j}\searrow u
, we have that
u_{j}\geq u
. On the set
\{u>\phi-L\}
, we have
u^{L}=u
and
u_{j}^{L}=u_{j}
. Using the fact that Monge-Ampère measures are plurifine, we get that

\int_{\{u>\phi-L\}}\psi(u_{j}-u)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}=\int_{\{u>\phi-L\}}\psi(u_{j}^{L}-u^{L})\theta^{n}_{u_{j}^{L}}.

Thus we need to show that

\left|\int_{\{u\leq\phi-L\}}\psi(u_{j}-u)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}-\int_{\{u\leq\phi-L\}}\psi(u_{j}^{L}-u^{L})\theta^{n}_{u_{j}^{L}}\right|\to 0.

We will show that both the terms go to 0 as
L\to\infty
independent of
j
.

Notice that since
u_{j}\geq u
we have
u_{j}-u=(\phi-u)-(\phi-u_{j})\leq\phi-u
. Since
\psi
is increasing in
(0,\infty)
we have
\psi(u_{j}-u)\leq\psi(\phi-u)=\psi(u-\phi)
. Now we can construct a weight
\tilde{\psi}
such that
\tilde{\psi}
is still even,
\tilde{\psi}(0)=0
and
\tilde{\psi}_{|(0,\infty)}
is smooth increasing and concave such that
\psi(t)\leq\tilde{\psi}(t)
and
\psi(t)/\tilde{\psi}(t)\searrow 0
as
t\to\infty
. Moreover, we require that
u\in\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{\psi}}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Since
u_{j}\geq u
, we have
u_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{\psi}}(X,\theta,\phi)
as well. Now

\displaystyle\int_{\{u\leq\phi-L\}}\psi(u_{j}-u)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{u\leq\phi-L\}}\psi(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\psi(L)}{\tilde{\psi}(L)}\int_{\{u\leq\phi-L\}}\tilde{\psi}(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}.

Since
u,u_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{\psi}}(X,\theta,\phi)
, Lemma
2.3
tells us

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{\psi(L)}{\tilde{\psi}(L)}\left(\int_{X}\tilde{\psi}(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u}+\int_{X}\tilde{\psi}(u_{j}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}\right).

Using Lemma
2.2
and noticing that
u\leq u_{j}\leq\phi
we get that

\displaystyle\leq 2(2^{n+1}+1)\frac{\psi(L)}{\tilde{\psi}(L)}\int_{X}\tilde{\psi}(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u}.

Since
u
has finite
\tilde{\psi}
energy,
\int_{X}\tilde{\psi}(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u}
is finite and
\psi(L)/\tilde{\psi}(L)\to 0
as
L\to\infty
independent of
j
. The same proof shows that
\int_{\{u\leq\phi-L\}}\psi(u_{j}^{L}-u^{L})\theta^{n}_{u_{j}^{L}}\to 0
independent of
j
.

We can also modify the proof to show that it works when
u_{j}\nearrow u
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
u_{k}\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
be a decreasing that is
I_{\psi}
-Cauchy: for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N
such that
I_{\psi}(u_{j},u_{k})\leq\varepsilon
for
j,k\geq N
. Then
\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}=:u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
and
I_{\psi}(u_{k},u)\to 0
.

Proof.

Find a subsequence of
\{u_{k}\}
and still denote it by
\{u_{k}\}
such that

I_{\psi}(u_{k},u_{k+1})\leq C^{-2k}

where
C
is the same constant as in Theorem
3.1
. Let
u=\lim_{k}u_{k}
. We have to show
u_{k}\not\equiv-\infty
. We have
I_{\psi}(u_{k},\phi)
is bounded as
k\to\infty
. To see this, repeated application of Theorem
3.1
gives

\displaystyle I_{\psi}(\phi,u_{k})

\displaystyle\leq C(I_{\psi}(\phi,u_{1})+I_{\psi}(u_{1},u_{k}))

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(\phi,u_{1})+C^{2}I_{\psi}(u_{1},u_{2})+C^{2}I_{\psi}(u_{2},u_{k}).

Doing this
k
times we get

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(\phi,u_{1})+\sum_{j=2}^{k}C^{j}I_{\psi}(u_{j-1},u_{j})

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(\phi,u_{1})+\sum_{j=2}^{k}C^{j}C^{-2j+2}

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(\phi,u_{1})+C^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}C^{-j}

\displaystyle=CI_{\psi}(\phi,u_{1})+\frac{C}{C-1}=\tilde{C}.

This gives us

\int_{X}\psi(u_{k}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{\phi}\leq I(\phi,u_{k})\leq\tilde{C}.

Without loss of generality we can assue that
u_{1}\leq\phi
so
u_{k}\leq u_{1}\leq\phi
. Since
u_{k}
is decreasing, we have
u_{k}-\phi
is decreasing, thus
\psi(u_{k}-\phi)
is increasing. Applying monotone convergence theorem we get that

\int_{X}\psi(u-\phi)\theta^{n}_{\phi}\leq\tilde{C}.

Thus
u\not\equiv-\infty
. Hence
u\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
. Since
u_{j}\searrow u
and

\int_{X}\psi(u_{k}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u_{k}}\leq I_{\psi}(u_{k},\phi)\leq\tilde{C}

using Lemma
2.7
we get that
u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta)
. Now we need to show that
I_{\psi}(u_{k},u)\to 0
. This means

\int_{X}\psi(u_{k}-u)\theta^{n}_{u}+\int_{X}\psi(u_{k}-u)\theta^{n}_{u_{k}}\to 0.

For the first integral, we notice that
u_{k}\geq u
, thus
u_{k}-u\geq 0
and
u_{k}-u\searrow 0
and thus
\psi(u_{k}-u)\searrow 0
. Thus we can apply the monotone convergence theorem to get

\int_{X}\psi(u_{k}-u)\theta^{n}_{u}\to 0.

To show that

\int_{X}\psi(u_{k}-u)\theta^{n}_{u_{k}}\to 0

we use Proposition
4.2
. Thus
I_{\psi}(u_{k},u)\to 0
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\{u_{k}\}\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
be such that

I_{\psi}(u_{k},u_{k+1})\leq C^{-2k}.

Then
u_{k}
are uniformly bounded from above.

Proof.

Let

v_{k}=\max\{u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\}.

Then repeated application of Theorem
3.1
and
4.1
gives

\displaystyle I_{\psi}(\phi,v_{k})

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(\phi,u_{1})+CI_{\psi}(u_{1},\max\{u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\})

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(\phi,u_{1})+CI_{\psi}(u_{1},\max\{u_{2},\dots,u_{k}\})

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(\phi,u_{1})+C^{2}I_{\psi}(u_{1},u_{2})+C^{2}I_{\psi}(u_{2},\max\{u_{2},\dots,u_{k}\})

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(\phi,u_{1})+C^{2}I_{\psi}(u_{1},u_{2})+C^{2}I_{\psi}(u_{2},\max\{u_{3},\dots,u_{k}\}).

Doing this
k
times we get

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(\phi,u_{1})+C^{2}I_{\psi}(u_{1},u_{2})+\dots+C^{k}I_{\psi}(u_{k-1},u_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}.

Therefore,

\int_{X}\psi(v_{k}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{\phi}\leq\tilde{C}.

Let
w_{k}=\max\{\phi,v_{k}\}\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta)
. Then
w_{k}-\phi\geq 0
and as
v_{k}
is pointwise increasing, we get that
w_{k}-\phi
is pointwise increasing. As
\psi
is increasing on
[0,\infty)
, we get that
\psi(w_{k}-\phi)
is pointwise increasing. Let
w:=\lim_{k\to\infty}w_{k}
. Then
\psi(w-\phi)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\nearrow\psi(w_{k}-\phi)
. The monotone convergence theorem implies

\int_{X}\psi(w-\phi)\theta^{n}_{\phi}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\psi(w_{k}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{\phi}\leq\int_{X}\psi(v_{k}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{\phi}\leq\tilde{C}.

Thus the set
K=\{\psi(w-\phi)\leq(\tilde{C}+1)/\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{\phi}\}
has positive
\theta^{n}_{\phi}
-measure. Thus
K
is not
\text{PSH}(X,\omega)
-polar. We can find a constant
A
such that
\theta\leq A\omega
. Then
\text{PSH}(X,\theta)\subset\text{PSH}(X,A\omega)
.

Consider the set

\mathcal{F}_{K}=\{\varphi\in\text{PSH}(X,A\omega):\sup_{K}\varphi=0\}.

Since
K
is not
\text{PSH}(X,A\omega)
-polar, we know that
\mathcal{F}_{K}
is compact in
L^{1}(\omega^{n})
-topology. This follows from
[
GZ05
, Theorem 4.7]
. Let
\alpha_{k}=\sup_{K}v_{k}
. Let
e
be such that
\psi(e)=(\tilde{C}+1)/\int_{X}\theta^{n}_{\phi}
. Then
K=\{w-\phi\leq e\}
. Notice that
\phi\leq 0
gives

\alpha_{k}=\sup_{K}v_{k}\leq\sup_{K}(v_{k}-\phi)\leq\sup_{K}(w-\phi)\leq e.

Define
\tilde{v}_{k}=v_{k}-\alpha_{k}
. Then
\tilde{v}_{k}\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)\subset\text{PSH}(X,A\omega)
. Since
\sup_{K}\tilde{v}_{k}=0
, we have
\tilde{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{F}_{K}
. Since
\mathcal{F}_{K}
is relatively compact, we have
\{\tilde{v}_{k}\}
is relatively compact in
L^{1}(X)
topology and hence uniformly bounded from above. Therefore
v_{k}=\tilde{v}_{k}+\alpha_{k}
are uniformly bounded from above and hence
u_{k}\leq v_{k}
are uniformly bounded from above.
∎

The following theorem is the last step in proving completeness of the quasi-metric.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\{u_{j}\}\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
be an
I_{\psi}
-Cauchy sequence. Then there exists
u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that
I_{\psi}(u_{j},u)\to 0
.

Proof.

Extract a subsequence and still denote it by
\{u_{j}\}
such that

I_{\psi}(u_{j},u_{j+1})\leq C^{-2j}

where
C
is the same constant as in Theorem
3.1
. By Lemma
4.4

\{u_{j}\}
are uniformly bounded from above. Thus

v_{j}:=(\sup_{k\geq j}u_{k})^{*}\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta).

Since
u_{j}\leq v_{j}
we know using Lemma
2.2
that
v_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
. Define

v_{j}^{l}:=\max\{u_{j},u_{j+1},\dots,u_{j+l}\}.

Then
v_{j}^{l}\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
and
v_{j}^{l}\nearrow v_{j}
almost everywhere. Lemma
2.7
implies
I_{\psi}(v_{j}^{l},v_{j})\to 0
as
l\to\infty
. Using quasi-triangle inequality twice we get that
I_{\psi}(v_{j},v_{j+1})\leq C^{2}\lim_{l\to\infty}I_{\psi}(v_{j}^{l+1},v_{j+1}^{l})
. Now,

\displaystyle I_{\psi}(v_{j}^{l+1},v_{j+1}^{l})

\displaystyle=I_{\psi}(\max\{u_{j},v_{j+1}^{l}\},v_{j+1}^{l}).

Using Theroem
4.1
, we get

\displaystyle\leq I_{\psi}(u_{j},v_{j+1}^{l}).

Using Theorem
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(u_{j},u_{j+1})+CI_{\psi}(u_{j+1},\max\{u_{j+1},v_{j+2}^{l-1}\}).

This again by Theorem
4.1
gives us

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(u_{j},u_{j+1})+CI_{\psi}(u_{j+1},v_{j+2}^{l-1}).

Applying this
l
times we get

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{l}C^{k}I_{\psi}(u_{j+k-1},u_{j+k})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{l}C^{k}C^{-2j-2k+2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}C^{-2j-k+2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{-2j+2}\frac{1}{C-1}=C^{-2j}\frac{C^{2}}{C-1}.

Thus we obtain that

I_{\psi}(v_{j},v_{j+1})\leq C^{-2j}\frac{C^{4}}{C-1}.

This shows that
\{v_{j}\}
is a decreasing
I_{\psi}
-Cauchy sequence. Thus by Lemma
4.3
, we get that there exists
v\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that
I_{\psi}(v_{j},v)\to 0
.

Now we want to show that
\{u_{j}\}
and
\{v_{j}\}
are equivalent sequences, i.e.,
I_{\psi}(u_{j},v_{j})\to 0
. Then
I_{\psi}(u_{j},v)\leq CI_{\psi}(u_{j},v_{j})+CI_{\psi}(v_{j},v)
. As both the terms go to 0, we get that
I_{\psi}(u_{j},v)\to 0
.

Now,

\displaystyle I_{\psi}(u_{j},v_{j}^{l})

\displaystyle=I_{\psi}(u_{j},\max\{u_{j},v_{j+1}^{l-1}\})

\displaystyle\leq I_{\psi}(u_{j},v_{j+1}^{l-1})

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(u_{j},u_{j+1})+CI_{\psi}(u_{j+1},v_{j+1}^{l-1})

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(u_{j},u_{j+1})+CI_{\psi}(u_{j+1},v_{j+2}^{l-2})

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(u_{j},u_{j+1})+C^{2}I_{\psi}(u_{j+1},u_{j+2})+C^{2}I_{\psi}(u_{j+2},v_{j+2}^{l-2}).

Doing this
l
times we get

\displaystyle\leq CI_{\psi}(u_{j},u_{j+1})+C^{2}I_{\psi}(u_{j+1},u_{j+2})+\dots C^{l}I_{\psi}(u_{j+l-1},u_{j+l})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{l}C^{k}C^{-2k-2j+2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{-2j}\frac{C^{2}}{C-1}.

Thus

I_{\psi}(u_{j},v_{j})\leq CI_{\psi}(u_{j},v_{j}^{l})+CI_{\psi}(v_{j}^{l},v_{j})\leq C^{-2j}\frac{C^{3}}{C-1}

by taking limit
l\to\infty
. Thus
I_{\psi}(u_{j},v_{j})\to 0
. Hence we have found
v\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that
I_{\psi}(u_{j},v)\to 0
. Thus
(\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi),I_{\psi})
is a completely metrizable topological space when topologized with the quasi-metric
I_{\psi}
.
∎

This finishes the proof for completeness for of the quasi-metric
I_{\psi}
on the space
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
u_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
be a decreasing sequence and
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
be such that

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\varphi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}<\infty.

Then
u=\lim_{j}u_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
.

Proof.

First we show that
u_{j}\not\to-\infty
uniformly. If, on the contrary,
u\to\infty
uniformly, up to choosing a subsequence and relabeling, we can assume that
u_{j}<-j
. Choose an
A
such that
\theta^{n}_{\varphi}\{\varphi>-A\}>0
. Now

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\varphi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}\{\psi(u_{j}-\varphi)>t\}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}\{|u_{j}-\varphi|>s\}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}\{\varphi<u_{j}-s\}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}\{u_{j}<\varphi-s\}ds.

Using comparison principle and the fact that
\psi^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
, we get

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s)\theta_{\varphi}\{u_{j}<\varphi-s\}ds.

Since
u_{j}<-j
, we get that
\{-j<\varphi-s\}\subset\{u_{j}<\varphi-s\}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s)\theta^{n}_{\varphi}\{-j<\varphi-s\}ds.

Choosing
j>A
, we can write

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{j-A}\psi^{\prime}(s)\theta^{n}_{\varphi}\{\varphi>s-j\}ds.

Again, we notice that
s<j-A
implies that
\{\varphi>-A\}\subset\{\varphi>s-j\}
, which gives

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{j-A}\psi^{\prime}(s)\theta^{n}_{\varphi}\{\varphi>-A\}ds

\displaystyle=\theta^{n}_{\varphi}\{\varphi>-A\}\psi(j-A)\to\infty

as
j\to\infty
.
This is a contradiction to the fact that
\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\varphi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}
is bounded in
j
.

Moreover we observe that
I_{\psi}(u_{j},\varphi)
is bounded. Notice that, like in the previous argument, we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\varphi)\theta^{n}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s)\theta^{n}_{\varphi}\{u_{j}<\varphi-s\}ds+\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s)\theta^{n}_{\varphi}\{\varphi<u_{j}-s\}.

Using comparison principle for the first expression and the fact that
u_{j}\leq u_{1}
in the second expression, we get that

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}\{u_{j}<\varphi-s\}ds+\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s)\theta^{n}_{\varphi}\{\varphi<u_{1}-s\}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\varphi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}+\int_{X}\psi(u_{1}-\varphi)\theta^{n}_{\varphi}.

Thus
\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\varphi)\theta^{n}_{\varphi}
is bounded from above independent of
j
.
Now notice that

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq I_{\psi}(\phi,u_{j}).

By Quasi-triangle inequality for
I_{\psi}
, we get

\displaystyle\leq C(I_{\psi}(\phi,\varphi)+I_{\psi}(u_{j},\varphi))

\displaystyle\leq C\left(I_{\psi}(\phi,\varphi)+\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\varphi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}+\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\varphi)\theta^{n}_{\varphi}\right).

Thus
\int_{X}\psi(u_{j}-\phi)\theta^{n}_{u_{j}}
is bounded from above independent of
j
and
u=\lim_{j}u_{j}\in\text{PSH}(X,\theta)
, thus by Lemma
2.7
we get that
u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
.

Corollary 5.3
.

The
I_{\psi}
-topology on
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
is stronger than the usual
L^{1}(\omega^{n})
topology on
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
. More precisely, if
u_{k},u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that
I_{\psi}(u_{k},u)\to 0
, then

\int_{X}|u_{k}-u|\omega^{n}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

It is enough to show
L^{1}(\omega^{n})
convergence for a subsequence. Let
u_{j_{k}}
be the subsequence provided by Theorem
5.2
and
v_{j_{k}}
and
w_{j_{k}}
be corresponding monotone sequences. Then
v_{j_{k}}\leq u_{j_{k}}\leq w_{j_{k}}
and
v_{j_{k}}\leq u\leq w_{j_{k}}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{X}|u_{j_{k}}-u|\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}(w_{j_{k}}-v_{j_{k}})\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}(w_{j_{k}}-u)\omega^{n}+\int_{X}(u-v_{j_{k}})\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\to 0

by the monotone convergence theorem. Thus the new topology is stronger and has more open, thus closed sets.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

If
u_{j},u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
, such that
I_{\psi}(u_{j},u)\to 0
as
j\to\infty
, then
u_{j}\to u
in capacity.

Proof.

It is enough to show the convergence in capacity for a subsequence. Let
u_{j_{k}}
be a subsequence as provided by Theorem
5.2
and
v_{j_{k}}
and
w_{j_{k}}
are corresponding monotone sequences converging to
u
. We get that
v_{j_{k}}\to u
and
w_{j_{k}}\to u
in capacity. We want to claim that
u_{j_{k}}\to u
in capacity as well. Fix
\varepsilon>0
.

\{|u_{j_{k}}-u|>\varepsilon\}\subset\{w_{j_{k}}-v_{j_{k}}>\varepsilon\}\subset\{w_{j_{k}}-u>\varepsilon/2\}\cup\{u-v_{j_{k}}>\varepsilon/2\}.

Taking capacity of above sets we get

\text{Cap}_{\omega}\{|u_{j_{k}}-u|>\varepsilon\}\leq\text{Cap}_{\omega}\{w_{j_{k}}-u>\varepsilon/2\}+\text{Cap}_{\omega}\{u-v_{j_{k}}>\varepsilon/2\}

and taking limit
k\to\infty
we get
\lim_{k\to\infty}\text{Cap}_{\omega}\{|u_{j_{k}}-u|>\varepsilon\}=0
.

Since for any subsequence
u_{j_{k}}
of
u_{j}
we can find another subsequence
u_{j_{k_{l}}}
for which
\lim_{l\to\infty}\text{Cap}_{\omega}\{|u_{j_{k_{l}}}-u|>\varepsilon\}=0
, we get that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\text{Cap}_{\omega}\{|u_{j}-u|>\varepsilon\}=0
. Thus
I_{\psi}
convergence implies convergence in capacity.
∎

Corollary 5.5

(Weak convergence of measures)
.

If
u_{k},u\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\theta,\phi)
such that
I_{\psi}(u_{k},u)\to 0
, then
\theta^{n}_{u_{k}}\to\theta^{n}_{u}
weakly as measures.

Proof.

Since
u_{k}\to u
in
I_{\psi}
, Theorem
5.4
shows that
u_{k}\to u
in capacity. Since
u_{k},u
have full mass,
[
DDL18
, Theorem 2.3]
(see also
[
Xin96
, Theorem 1]
and
[
Xin09
, Theorem 1]
) implies that
\theta^{n}_{u_{k}}\to\theta^{n}_{u}
weakly.
∎

6.
Kähler Ricci flow

In
[
GZ17
]
, authors showed that we can start Kähler Ricci flow from any potential
\varphi
with zero Lelong numbers. They showed that if
\varphi\in\text{PSH}(X,\omega)
has zero Lelong numbers then there exist smooth potentials
\varphi_{t}
for small time such that

(22)

\frac{\partial\varphi_{t}}{\partial t}=\log\left[\frac{(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}}{\omega^{n}}\right],\varphi_{t}\to\varphi\text{ in }L^{1}(\omega^{n})\text{ as }t\to 0.

Di Nezza-Lu
[
DL17
, Corollary 5.2]
further showed that
\varphi_{j}\to\varphi
in capacity.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\varphi\leq-1
. In
[
GZ17
, Lemma 2.9]
, the authors showed that (for
\beta=1
and
\alpha=1
) for a continuous
\omega
-psh function
u
, satisfying

(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}=e^{u-2\varphi}\omega^{n}

we have

(1-2t)\varphi(x)+tu(x)+n(t\ln t-t)\leq\varphi_{t}.

Since
\varphi\leq-1
, we have
\varphi\leq(1-2t)\varphi
for small
t
and since
u
is continuous there exists some constant
C
such that
C\leq u
. Combining these, we get

\varphi(x)+tC+n(t\ln t-t)\leq\varphi_{t}

or

\varphi(x)\leq\varphi_{t}-tC-n(t\ln t-t).

Notice that
-tC-n(t\ln t-t)=f(t)
satisfies
f(t)\to 0
as
t\to 0
.

Since
\varphi_{t}\to\varphi
in
L^{1}(\omega^{n})
as
t\to 0
, we also obtain that
\varphi_{t}+f(t)
converge to
\varphi
in
L^{1}(\omega^{n})
as
t\to 0
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Given
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\omega)
and a sequence
\varphi_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\omega)
such that

\varphi\leq\varphi_{j}

and
\varphi_{j}\to\varphi
in
L^{1}(\omega^{n})
as
j\to\infty
, then
I_{\psi}(\varphi_{j},\varphi)\to 0
as
j\to\infty
..

Proof.

Since
\varphi_{j}\to\varphi
in
L^{1}(\omega^{n})
, we get a subsequence
\varphi_{j_{k}}
such that
\varphi_{j_{k}}\to\varphi
, a.e.. Consider the functions

v_{j}=\sup_{k\geq j}\varphi_{j_{k}}.

Then
v_{j}^{*}\in\text{PSH}(X,\omega)
and
v_{j}^{*}=v_{j}
except on a pluripolar set. Moreover, since
v_{j}^{*}\searrow\varphi

\omega^{n}
almost everywhere, we get that
v_{j}^{*}\searrow\varphi
everywhere.

This shows that
v_{j}\searrow\varphi
except on a pluripolar set. This means
\lim_{j\to\infty}\sup_{k\geq j}\varphi_{k_{j}}=\varphi
except on a pluripolar set. Therefore

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\varphi_{j_{k}}=\varphi

except on a pluripolar set. Since
\varphi_{j_{k}}\geq\varphi
, we automatically have

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\varphi_{j_{k}}\geq\varphi

everywhere.

Therefore,
\varphi_{j_{k}}\to\varphi
except on a pluripolar set. Using
\varphi\leq\varphi_{j_{k}}\leq v_{k}
and that
v_{k}\searrow\varphi
as
k\to\infty
, we get

(23)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\psi(\varphi_{j_{k}}-\varphi)\omega^{n}_{\varphi}\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\psi(v_{k}-\varphi)\omega^{n}_{\varphi}=0.

Moreover, since
\varphi_{j_{k}}\geq\varphi
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi(\varphi_{j_{k}}-\varphi)\omega^{n}_{\varphi_{j_{k}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s)\omega^{n}_{\varphi_{j_{k}}}(\varphi_{j_{k}}>\varphi+s)ds

Using comparison theorem, we get

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(s)\omega^{n}_{\varphi}(\varphi_{j_{k}}>\varphi+s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\psi(\varphi_{j_{k}}-\varphi)\omega^{n}_{\varphi}.

Taking limit we get

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\psi(\varphi_{j_{k}}-\varphi)\omega^{n}_{\varphi_{j_{k}}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\psi(\varphi_{j_{k}}-\varphi)\omega^{n}_{\varphi}=0.

Combining these two we get

\lim_{k\to\infty}I_{\psi}(\varphi_{j_{k}},\varphi)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\psi(\varphi_{j_{k}}-\varphi)(\omega^{n}_{\varphi_{j_{k}}}+\omega^{n}_{\varphi})=0.

Since this holds for any subsequence of
\varphi_{j}
, we get that

I_{\psi}(\varphi_{j},\varphi)\to 0.

∎

The following corollary generalizes the
[
GZ17
, Proposition 5.2]
where they show that
\varphi_{t}\to\varphi
in energy if
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\omega)
.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}_{\psi}(X,\omega)
. Let
\varphi_{t}
be the solution to Equation (
22
). Then
I_{\psi}(\varphi_{t},\varphi)\to 0
as
t\to 0
.

Proof.

We saw earlier that the assumptions imply that

\varphi\leq\varphi_{t}+f(t)

where
f(t)\to 0
as
t\to 0
. Since
\varphi_{t}+f(t)
are also
\theta
-psh functions which converge in
L^{1}(\omega^{n})
to
\varphi
as
t\to 0
, using Lemma
6.1
, we get that
I_{\psi}(\varphi_{t},\varphi)\to 0
as
t\to 0
.
∎

Note that using Theorem
5.4
and Corollary
5.5
we obtain that
\varphi_{t}\to\varphi
in capacity and
\omega^{n}_{\varphi_{t}}\to\omega^{n}_{\varphi}
as
t\to 0
, thus finishing the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

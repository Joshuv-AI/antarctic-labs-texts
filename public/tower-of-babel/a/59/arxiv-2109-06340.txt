Harmonic flow of Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) \mathrm{Spin}(7) -structures
Shubham Dwivedi, Eric Loubeau & Henrique Sá Earp

Abstract
We formulate and study the isometric flow of Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) \mathrm{Spin}(7) -structures on compact 8 8 -manifolds, as an instance of the harmonic flow of geometric structures. Starting from a general perspective, we establish Shi-type estimates and a correspondence between harmonic solitons and self-similar solutions for arbitrary isometric flows of H H -structures. We then specialise to H = Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) ⊂ SO ⁡ ( 8 ) H=\mathrm{Spin}(7)\subset\mathrm{SO}(8) , obtaining conditions for long-time existence, via a monotonicity formula along the flow, which leads to an ε \varepsilon -regularity theorem. Moreover, we prove Cheeger-Gromov and Hamilton-type compactness theorems for the solutions of the harmonic flow, and we characterise Type- I \mathrm{I} singularities as being modelled on shrinking solitons. We also establish a Bryant-type description of isometric Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) \mathrm{Spin}(7) -structures, based on squares of spinors, which may be of independent interest.

1.  Introduction

We formulate and study the harmonic flow of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures on a compact, oriented and spinnable
8
-manifold. Since the flow runs among isometric
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures, this problem fits naturally in the recent thread of activity on isometric/harmonic flows of

-structures
[

,

,

,

]

[

[

]

By contrast, to our knowledge, there have yet been no studies exploring
any
flow of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures, ever since the fundamental reference
[

]
.
We organise therefore the exposition around the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow, even though several analytic results are actually obtained for arbitrary

-structures, and can thus be seen as advances in the general theory of

formulated in
[

. Namely, in §

-structures, along the lines of
[

]
. This leads to a general definition of harmonic

[

, §2.5]

While our approach builds upon previous broad-ranging results on the harmonic flow of geometric structures from
[

2.
H
-Structures, homogeneous sections and harmonic flow

In many respects,
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures should be seen as a particular occurrence of
H
-structures and some of the analytical properties of the harmonic flow are best formulated in the general setting of sections of Riemannian submersions with totally geodesic fibres.

In §
2.1
, we briefly review the unifying abstract framework required to define the harmonicity of
H
-structures, by mediation of their corresponding sections, describe their deformation theory and classify the solitons of the harmonic section flow. For further details, the reader is kindly referred to
[
LS19
]
and references therein.
Subsequently, in §
2.2
, we adopt the wider perspective of sections of Riemannian submersions in order to prove a very general Shi-type estimate, yielding fractional-polynomial time-bounds on all derivatives of sections along the flow.

2.1
H
-Structures

A
geometric structure
defined by an
H
-invariant tensor
\xi\in\mathcal{T}^{p,q}(M)
on a manifold

Assuming

A sufficient setup for the development of this general theory consists of the following data:

\bullet

2.2
Harmonic sections

In this sub-section, we take a slight detour through Riemannian submersions
\pi:(N,\eta)\to(M,g)
with totally geodesic fibres, and their sections
\sigma:M\to N

defines a vertical distribution in

and the metric
\eta

If

E^{\mathcal{V}}(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma|^{2}\,\vol.

This defines a variational principle for sections of

, by the Euler-Lagrange equation:

\tau^{\mathcal{V}}(\sigma)=0.

Since

is a Riemannian submersion with totally geodesic fibres,

is actually the vertical part of the tension field

from harmonic maps theory
[
Woo86
,
Woo97
]
.
The analytic study of the corresponding heat equation

(2.16)

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{t}}{dt}=\tau^{\mathcal{V}}(\sigma_{t})

aims at finding sufficient conditions for convergence towards a harmonic section.
This will require the local expression of the vertical second fundamental form, which will lead to a Weitzenböck formula on the swapping of derivatives for the vertical tension field and, taking inner-products, an adapted Bochner formula. Under bounded geometry, we obtain a Bochner-type inequality along the harmonic flow.

As a consequence of these formulas, we prove wide-ranging Shi-type estimates on the derivatives of solutions of the harmonic flow. This result naturally holds for
H
-structures and will be exploited for
H=\mathrm{Spin}(7)\subset\mathrm{SO}{(8)}
later on.

we get

\displaystyle\tilde{\Gamma}^{w}_{uj}

\displaystyle=dy^{w}(\nabla_{\tilde{\partial}_{u}}\tilde{\partial}_{j})=dy^{w}(\nabla_{\tilde{\partial}_{u}}\tilde{\partial}^{z}_{j})+\tilde{\partial}_{u}(\eta_{jv}\eta^{vw})+\eta_{jv}\eta^{vt}\tilde{\Gamma}^{w}_{tu}.

Since the fibres are totally geodesic, O’Neill’s tensor
T
vanishes and
\mathcal{V}\nabla_{\tilde{\partial}_{u}}\tilde{\partial}^{z}_{j}=0
.
Therefore, as
\tilde{\partial}^{z}_{j}
is basic, one has

\nabla_{\tilde{\partial}_{u}}\tilde{\partial}^{z}_{j}=\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}_{ju}\tilde{\partial}^{z}_{k}

and, as we already know that
dy^{w}(\tilde{\partial}^{z}_{k})=-\eta_{kv}\eta^{vw}
, we obtain

\displaystyle dy^{w}(\nabla_{\tilde{\partial}_{u}}\tilde{\partial}^{z}_{j})

\displaystyle=-\eta_{kv}\eta^{vw}\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}_{ju}.

In conclusion, the vertical second fundamental form is

(2.18)

\displaystyle\alpha(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})=

\displaystyle\ \Big\{\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}-\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{k}}+\tilde{\Gamma}^{u}_{vw}\frac{\partial\sigma^{v}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\sigma^{w}}{\partial x_{j}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial\sigma^{v}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big(\tilde{\Gamma}^{u}_{iv}+\eta_{ks}\eta^{su}\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}_{iv}\Big)+\eta_{js}\eta^{sv}\eta_{kw}\eta^{wu}\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}_{iv}

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial\sigma^{v}}{\partial x_{i}}\Big(\tilde{\Gamma}^{u}_{jv}+\eta_{ks}\eta^{su}\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}_{jv}\Big)+\eta_{is}\eta^{sv}\eta_{kw}\eta^{wu}\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}_{jv}

\displaystyle+\Big(\tilde{\Gamma}^{u}_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}dy^{u}(\mathcal{V}[\tilde{\partial}^{z}_{i},\tilde{\partial}^{z}_{j}])\Big)\Big\}\tilde{\partial}_{u}

with

-2g_{kj}\eta^{uv}\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}_{iu}=dy^{v}(\mathcal{V}[\tilde{\partial}^{z}_{i},\tilde{\partial}^{z}_{j}]).

Remark 2.13
.

The vertical second fundamental form of a submersion, unlike its usual counterpart, is not symmetric. In some cases, the non-symmetric part of
\alpha
can be expressed in terms of curvature, e.g. if
\mathcal{H}
is a
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-connection on a vector bundle, then it is equal to
\frac{1}{2}(\pi^{*}R)
, see
[
Bes08
, 9.53, 9.54 and 9.65]
and
[
ONe66
, §5]
. When
\pi:N\to M
is a homogeneous fibre bundle, the skew-symmetric part of vertical second fundamental form is
\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{*}\Phi
, where
\Phi
is the curvature two-form on
N

[
Woo03
, Thm 3.5]
.

Since
\pi
is a Riemannian submersion, we have that
\tau^{\mathcal{V}}(\sigma)=\tr{\alpha}
, so tracing the above expression yields the vertical tension field of
\sigma
:

\displaystyle\tau^{\mathcal{V}}(\sigma)=

\displaystyle\ g^{ij}\Big\{\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}+\tilde{\Gamma}^{u}_{rs}\frac{\partial\sigma^{r}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\sigma^{s}}{\partial x_{j}}-\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{k}}

\displaystyle+2\Big(\tilde{\Gamma}^{u}_{ir}\frac{\partial\sigma^{r}}{\partial x_{j}}+\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}_{ir}\Big(\frac{\partial\sigma^{r}}{\partial x_{j}}+\eta_{js}\eta^{sr}\Big)\eta_{kw}\eta^{wu}\Big)+\tilde{\Gamma}^{u}_{ij}\Big\}\tilde{\partial}_{u},

where
1\leq i,j,k\leq m
and
m+1\leq r,s,u,w\leq n
.

Remark 2.14
.

To obtain the equation of the harmonic flow, work with the Cartesian product
M\times\mathbb{R}
and write
\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial t}
as
d\sigma(\partial_{t})
. Then, since each
\sigma_{t}
is a section and
\sigma^{l}
is constant,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=d\sigma(\partial_{t})=\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial t}\tilde{\partial}_{u}+\frac{\partial\sigma^{l}}{\partial t}\tilde{\partial}_{l}=\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial t}\tilde{\partial}_{u}=d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{t}).

2.2.2
Weitzenböck and Bochner formulas

We assume the coordinates
\{x_{i}\}
are normal at the point
p
, so that
\partial_{k}(g^{ij})|_{p}=\frac{\partial g^{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}|_{p}=0
, and evaluate all quantities at
p
. Given a section
\sigma:M\to N
, we denote by
\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}
the pullback of the vertical distribution to the base manifold
M
.
By standard arguments, we have a Weitzenböck formula for the vertical tension:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\partial_{k}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\tau^{\mathcal{V}}(\sigma)=

\displaystyle\ \nabla_{\partial_{k}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\Big(g^{ij}\alpha(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ g^{ij}\Big\{R^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}(\partial_{k},\partial_{i})(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{j}))+(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(R^{M}(\partial_{i},\partial_{k})\partial_{j})

\displaystyle+\nabla_{\partial_{i}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\nabla_{\partial_{j}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{k}))+\nabla_{\partial_{i}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\Big(\alpha(\partial_{k},\partial_{j})-\alpha(\partial_{j},\partial_{k})\Big)-(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(\nabla_{\partial_{i}}\nabla_{\partial_{j}}\partial_{k})\Big\}.

Recall from (
2.18
) that

\displaystyle\alpha(\partial_{k},\partial_{j})-\alpha(\partial_{j},\partial_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}([\tilde{\partial}^{z}_{j},\tilde{\partial}^{z}_{k}])=2g_{ij}\eta^{uv}\tilde{\Gamma}^{i}_{ku}\tilde{\partial}_{v},

so that

\nabla_{\partial_{i}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\Big(\alpha(\partial_{k},\partial_{j})-\alpha(\partial_{j},\partial_{k})\Big)

is a curvature term.

Let us now compute the Laplacian of the energy density:

\displaystyle-\Delta e(\sigma)

\displaystyle=-g^{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{|d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma|^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\Bigg[g^{kl}(\nabla^{2}_{\partial_{i},\partial_{j}}\eta)(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{k}),d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{l}))+4g^{kl}(\nabla_{\partial_{j}}\eta)(\nabla^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}_{\partial_{i}}(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{k})),d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{l}))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\partial^{2}g^{kl}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\eta(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{k}),d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{l}))

\displaystyle\quad+2g^{kl}\eta(\nabla^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}_{\partial_{i}}(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{k})),\nabla^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}_{\partial_{j}}(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{l})))+2g^{kl}\eta(\nabla^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}_{\partial_{i}}\nabla^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}_{\partial_{j}}(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{k})),d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{l}))\Bigg]

The covariant derivative of the target metric
\eta
by the pull-back connection is

\displaystyle\nabla_{\partial_{j}}\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{j}}\nabla_{\tilde{\partial}_{u}}\eta+\nabla_{\tilde{\partial}_{j}}\eta

and its second covariant derivatives are

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{\partial_{i},\partial_{j}}\eta=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial\sigma^{v}}{\partial x_{i}}\nabla^{2}_{\tilde{\partial}_{v},\tilde{\partial}_{u}}\eta+\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{j}}\nabla^{2}_{\tilde{\partial}_{i},\tilde{\partial}_{u}}\eta+\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{i}}\nabla^{2}_{\tilde{\partial}_{u},\tilde{\partial}_{j}}\eta+\nabla^{2}_{\tilde{\partial}_{i},\tilde{\partial}_{j}}\eta

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma^{w}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial\sigma^{v}}{\partial x_{i}}\tilde{\Gamma}^{w}_{uv}+\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{j}}\tilde{\Gamma}^{w}_{ui}+\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{i}}\tilde{\Gamma}^{w}_{uj}+\tilde{\Gamma}^{w}_{ij}\Big)\nabla_{\tilde{\partial}_{w}}\eta

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{j}}\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}_{ui}+\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{i}}\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}_{uj}+\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}_{ij}\Big)\nabla_{\tilde{\partial}_{k}}\eta.

On the other hand

\displaystyle\nabla^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}_{\partial_{i}}(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{k}))=\nabla^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}_{\partial_{i}}\Big[\Big(\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{k}}+\eta_{ks}\eta^{su}\Big)\tilde{\partial}_{u}\Big]

\displaystyle=\Big[\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{k}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(\eta_{ks}\eta^{su})+\Big(\frac{\partial\sigma^{w}}{\partial x_{k}}+\eta_{ks}\eta^{sw}\Big)\Big(\frac{\partial\sigma^{v}}{\partial x_{i}}\tilde{\Gamma}^{u}_{wv}+\tilde{\Gamma}^{u}_{wi}\Big)\Big]\tilde{\partial}_{u}+\Big(\frac{\partial\sigma^{w}}{\partial x_{k}}+\eta_{ks}\eta^{sw}\Big)\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}_{wi}\tilde{\partial}_{k}.

Therefore, using the Weitzenböck formula and properties of the Levi-Civita connection yields a Bochner formula for
\sigma
:

\displaystyle-\Delta e(\sigma)=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}g^{kl}\Big(\frac{\partial\sigma^{t}}{\partial x_{k}}+\eta_{ks}\eta^{st}\Big)\Big(\frac{\partial\sigma^{r}}{\partial x_{l}}+\eta_{ls}\eta^{sr}\Big)\Bigg[\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial\sigma^{v}}{\partial x_{i}}\nabla^{2}_{\tilde{\partial}_{v},\tilde{\partial}_{u}}\eta+\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{j}}\nabla^{2}_{\tilde{\partial}_{i},\tilde{\partial}_{u}}\eta+\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial x_{i}}\nabla^{2}_{\tilde{\partial}_{u},\tilde{\partial}_{j}}\eta

\displaystyle+\nabla^{2}_{\tilde{\partial}_{i},\tilde{\partial}_{j}}\eta\Bigg](\tilde{\partial}_{t},\tilde{\partial}_{r})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}g^{kl}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\Big(\frac{\partial\sigma^{t}}{\partial x_{k}}+\eta_{ks}\eta^{st}\Big)\Big(\frac{\partial\sigma^{r}}{\partial x_{l}}+\eta_{ls}\eta^{sr}\Big)\eta_{tr}-|\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma|^{2}

\displaystyle+g^{ij}g^{kl}\eta\Big(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{l}),R^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}(\partial_{k},\partial_{i})(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{j}))+(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(R^{M}(\partial_{i},\partial_{k})\partial_{j})

\displaystyle+\nabla_{\partial_{i}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\Big(2g_{ij}\eta^{uv}\tilde{\Gamma}^{i}_{ku}\tilde{\partial}_{v}\Big)-\Big(\frac{\partial\Gamma^{m}_{jk}}{\partial x_{i}}+\Gamma^{p}_{jk}\Gamma^{m}_{ip}\Big)d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{m})\Big)-g^{kl}\eta(\nabla_{\partial_{k}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\tau^{\mathcal{V}}(\sigma),d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{l})).

Then, still under no assumption on
\sigma
being harmonic, we obtain a Bochner-type inequality:

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\sigma:M\to N
be a section of a Riemannian submersion
\pi:(N,\eta)\to(M,g)
between compact manifolds, with totally geodesic fibres, then there exists a universal constant
C>0
depending only on
(M,g)
and
(N,\eta)
such that

\displaystyle-\Delta e(\sigma)\leq-|\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma|^{2}+C\Big(e^{2}(\sigma)+e(\sigma)+1\Big)-(\nabla\tau^{\mathcal{V}}(\sigma),d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma).

2.2.3
Shi-type estimates along (
HSF
)

We obtain global derivative estimates along the harmonic flow, with a technique used by Shi
[
Shi89
]
for the Ricci flow, Lotay–Wei
[
LW17
]
for the Laplacian flow of closed
{\rm G}_{2}
-structures and Dwivedi
et al.

[
DGK21
]
for the isometric flow of
{\rm G}_{2}
-structures. This will later on allow us to establish long-time existence conditions of the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,t_{1}[}
be a solution of the harmonic section flow (
HSF
), and assume that there exist
K\geq 1
and constants
B_{j}
,
0\leq j\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
D\geq 0
, such that

|T|\leq K,\quad|\nabla g|,\ |\nabla\eta|\leq DK,\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla^{j}R^{M}|,\ |\nabla^{j}R^{N}|\leq B_{j}K^{j+2},\quad\forall\ j\geq 0.

Then, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist constants
C_{m}=C_{m}(g,\eta)
such that

|\nabla^{m}T|\leq C_{m}Kt^{-m/2},\quad\forall\ t\in[0,\frac{1}{K^{4}}].

Proof.

Locally the Weitzenböck formula for
H
-structures becomes

\displaystyle\nabla_{\partial_{k}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\tau^{\mathcal{V}}(\sigma)=

\displaystyle\ g^{ij}\Big\{R^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}(\partial_{k},\partial_{i})(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{j}))+(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(R^{M}(\partial_{i},\partial_{k})\partial_{j})+\nabla_{\partial_{i}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\nabla_{\partial_{j}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{k}))

\displaystyle+2\nabla_{\partial_{i}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\Big(g_{lj}\eta^{uv}\tilde{\Gamma}^{l}_{ku}\tilde{\partial}_{v}\Big)-(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(\nabla_{\partial_{i}}\nabla_{\partial_{j}}\partial_{k})\Big\}+\frac{\partial g^{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}\alpha(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})-g^{ij}(\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(\partial_{k},\nabla_{\partial_{i}}\partial_{j}).

Along the harmonic flow, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\partial_{k}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial t}=

\displaystyle\ g^{ij}\Big\{R^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}(\partial_{k},\partial_{i})(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{j}))+(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(R^{M}(\partial_{i},\partial_{k})\partial_{j})+\nabla_{\partial_{i}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\nabla_{\partial_{j}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{k}))

\displaystyle+2\nabla_{\partial_{i}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\Big(g_{lj}\eta^{uv}\tilde{\Gamma}^{l}_{ku}\tilde{\partial}_{v}\Big)-(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(\nabla_{\partial_{i}}\nabla_{\partial_{j}}\partial_{k})\Big\}+\frac{\partial g^{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}\alpha(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})-g^{ij}(\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(\partial_{k},\nabla_{\partial_{i}}\partial_{j}).

Since

\displaystyle\nabla_{\partial_{k}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\Big[(\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(\partial_{k},\partial_{t})-(\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(\partial_{t},\partial_{k})\Big]+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{k}))

and

\displaystyle\Big[(\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(\partial_{k},\partial_{t})-(\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(\partial_{t},\partial_{k})\Big]=\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial t}\nabla^{N}_{\tilde{\partial}_{k}}\tilde{\partial}_{u}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(\eta_{ks}\eta^{su}\Big)\tilde{\partial}_{u}-\eta_{ks}\eta^{su}\frac{\partial\sigma^{v}}{\partial t}\nabla^{N}_{\tilde{\partial}_{v}}\tilde{\partial}_{u},

the flow equation for
d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma
is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{k}))=-\frac{\partial\sigma^{u}}{\partial t}\nabla^{N}_{\tilde{\partial}_{k}}\tilde{\partial}_{u}+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(\eta_{ks}\eta^{su}\Big)\tilde{\partial}_{u}+\eta_{ks}\eta^{su}\frac{\partial\sigma^{v}}{\partial t}\nabla^{N}_{\tilde{\partial}_{v}}\tilde{\partial}_{u}

\displaystyle+g^{ij}\Big\{R^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}(\partial_{k},\partial_{i})(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{j}))+(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(R^{M}(\partial_{i},\partial_{k})\partial_{j})+\nabla_{\partial_{i}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\nabla_{\partial_{j}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\partial_{k}))

\displaystyle+2\nabla_{\partial_{i}}^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}\Big(g_{lj}\eta^{uv}\tilde{\Gamma}^{l}_{ku}\tilde{\partial}_{v}\Big)-(d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(\nabla_{\partial_{i}}\nabla_{\partial_{j}}\partial_{k})\Big\}+\frac{\partial g^{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}\alpha(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})-g^{ij}(\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma)(\partial_{k},\nabla_{\partial_{i}}\partial_{j}).

We will only indicate the main steps of the induction, using the Ricci identity (
1.7
).
Write
T
for
d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma
and use a schematic expression for the flow of
T
. In particular, note that the curvature term
R^{\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}
of the pull-back connection will contribute instances of
T
.

(2.19)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}T

\displaystyle=\nabla T*\nabla\eta+T^{3}*R^{N}+T*R^{M}+\Delta T+\nabla T*\nabla g+\nabla g*\nabla\eta+T*R^{N}

so the schematic expression for the flow of
\nabla T
is

(2.20)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nabla T=

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}T*\nabla\eta+\nabla T*T*R^{N}+\nabla T*T^{2}*R^{N}+T^{4}*\nabla R^{N}+\nabla T*R^{M}+T*\nabla R^{M}

(2.21)

\displaystyle+\Delta(\nabla T)+\nabla^{2}T*\nabla g+R^{M}*\nabla\eta+\nabla g*T*R^{N}+\nabla T*R^{N}+T^{2}*\nabla R^{N}

since
\nabla(R^{N}\circ\sigma)=T*\nabla R^{N}
,
\nabla(\Delta T)=\Delta(\nabla T)+\nabla T*R^{N}+T*\nabla R^{M}
and
\nabla(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}T)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\nabla T)+R^{N}*T
.

Therefore the evolution equation for
|T|^{2}
is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|T|^{2}=

\displaystyle\ 2\langle\nabla T*\nabla\eta,T\rangle+2\langle T^{3}*R^{N},T\rangle+2\langle T*R^{M},T\rangle+2\langle\Delta T,T\rangle+2\langle\nabla T*\nabla g,T\rangle

\displaystyle+2\langle\nabla g*\nabla\eta,T\rangle+2\langle T*R^{N},T\rangle,

so

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|T|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\ \Delta|T|^{2}-2|\nabla T|^{2}+C|\nabla\eta||T||\nabla T|+C|T|^{4}|R^{N}|+C|T|^{2}|R^{M}|+C|\nabla g||T||\nabla T|

\displaystyle+C|\nabla g||\nabla\eta||T|+C|R^{N}||T|^{2}

and the evolution equation for
\nabla T
implies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\nabla T|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\Delta|\nabla T|^{2}-2|\nabla^{2}T|^{2}+C|\nabla\eta||\nabla T||\nabla^{2}T|+C|R^{N}||T||\nabla T|^{2}+C|R^{N}||T|^{2}|\nabla T|^{2}

\displaystyle+C|\nabla R^{N}||T|^{4}|\nabla T|+C|R^{M}||\nabla T|^{2}+C|\nabla R^{M}||T||\nabla T|

\displaystyle+C|\nabla g||\nabla T||\nabla^{2}T|+C|R^{M}||\nabla\eta||\nabla T|+C|\nabla g||R^{N}||T||\nabla T|+C|R^{N}||\nabla T|^{2}

\displaystyle+C|\nabla R^{N}||T|^{2}|\nabla T|.

The hypotheses imply that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|T|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta|T|^{2}-2|\nabla T|^{2}+CK|T||\nabla T|+CK^{2}|T|^{4}+CK^{2}|T|^{2}+CK^{2}|T|

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\nabla T|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\Delta|\nabla T|^{2}-2|\nabla^{2}T|^{2}+CK|\nabla T||\nabla^{2}T|+CK^{2}|T||\nabla T|^{2}+CK^{2}|T|^{2}|\nabla T|^{2}

\displaystyle+CK^{3}|T|^{4}|\nabla T|+CK^{2}|\nabla T|^{2}+CK^{3}|T||\nabla T|+CK^{3}|\nabla T|+CK^{3}|T|^{2}|\nabla T|.

Base step
: Let
f=t|\nabla T|^{2}+\beta|T|^{2}
, for a constant
\beta
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=

\displaystyle|\nabla T|^{2}+t\frac{\partial|\nabla T|^{2}}{\partial t}+\beta\frac{\partial|T|^{2}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\nabla T|^{2}+t\Big(\Delta|\nabla T|^{2}-2|\nabla^{2}T|^{2}+CK|\nabla T||\nabla^{2}T|+CK^{2}|T||\nabla T|^{2}+CK^{2}|T|^{2}|\nabla T|^{2}

\displaystyle+CK^{3}|T|^{4}|\nabla T|+CK^{2}|\nabla T|^{2}+CK^{3}|T||\nabla T|+CK^{3}|\nabla T|+CK^{3}|T|^{2}|\nabla T|\Big)

\displaystyle+\beta\Big(\Delta|T|^{2}-2|\nabla T|^{2}+CK^{2}|\nabla T|+CK^{6}\Big).

By Young’s inequality and
|T|\leq K
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\leq

\displaystyle|\nabla T|^{2}+\Delta f-(2-\varepsilon)t|\nabla^{2}T|^{2}+t\Big(CK^{4}|\nabla T|^{2}+CK^{10}\Big)

\displaystyle+\beta\Big(-(2-\varepsilon)|\nabla T|^{2}+CK^{4}+CK^{6}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Delta f+(C-\beta(2-\varepsilon))|\nabla T|^{2}+C\beta K^{6},

so, for
\beta
large enough,

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\leq\Delta f+C\beta K^{6}.

Since
f(x,0)\leq\beta K^{2}
, by the maximum principle and the assumption
0\leq t\leq 1/K^{4}
,

\displaystyle\sup f(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq\beta K^{2}+C\beta tK^{6}\leq cK^{2}.

Therefore
t|\nabla T|^{2}\leq CK^{2}
, that is
|\nabla T|\leq CKt^{-1/2}
, and this proves the base step.

Induction step
: From (
2.19
), we obtain the schematic expression for the flow of
\nabla^{m}T
based on

\begin{aligned} a)&\nabla^{m}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}T=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nabla^{m}T+\nabla^{m-1}(T*R^{N})\\
b)&\nabla^{m}(\Delta T)=\Delta(\nabla^{m}T)+\sum_{i=0}^{m}\nabla^{m-i}T*\nabla^{i}R^{M}\\
c)&\nabla^{m}(T*R^{N})=\sum_{a+b=m}\nabla^{a}T*\nabla^{b}(R^{N}\circ\sigma)\\
d)&\nabla^{m}(T^{3}*R^{N})=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\nabla^{i}(T^{3})*\nabla^{m-i}(R^{N}\circ\sigma)\\
h)&\nabla^{m}(\nabla T*\nabla\eta)=\nabla^{m+1}T*\nabla\eta+\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\nabla^{i+1}T*\nabla^{m-i}(\nabla\eta\circ\sigma)\end{aligned}\hskip-36.135pt\begin{aligned} e)&\nabla^{m}(T*R^{M})=\sum_{a+b=m}\nabla^{a}T*\nabla^{b}R^{M}\\
f)&\nabla^{m}(\nabla T*\nabla g)=\sum_{a+b=m}\nabla^{a+1}T*\nabla^{b+1}g\\
g)&\nabla^{m}(\nabla g*\nabla\eta)=\sum_{a+b=m}\nabla^{a+1}g*\nabla^{b}(\nabla\eta\circ\sigma)\end{aligned}

and the Faà-di Bruno formula
[
Faà55
]
:

\nabla^{b}(R^{N}\circ\sigma)=\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{n}jb_{j}=b}\Big(\nabla^{b_{1}+\dots+b_{j}}R^{N}\Big)*\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\nabla^{j-1}T\Big)^{b_{j}}

where
\prod
is a product of
*
’s. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(|\nabla^{m}T|^{2})=

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla^{m}(\nabla T*\nabla\eta),\nabla^{m}T\rangle+2\langle\nabla^{m}(T^{3}*R^{N}),\nabla^{m}T\rangle+2\langle\nabla^{m}(T*R^{M}),\nabla^{m}T\rangle

\displaystyle+2\langle\nabla^{m}(\Delta T),\nabla^{m}T\rangle+2\langle\nabla^{m}(\nabla T*\nabla g),\nabla^{m}T\rangle+2\langle\nabla^{m}(\nabla g*\nabla\eta),\nabla^{m}T\rangle

\displaystyle+2\langle\nabla^{m}(T*R^{N}),\nabla^{m}T\rangle-2\langle\nabla^{m-1}(T*R^{N}),\nabla^{m}T\rangle.

A combination of the chain rule, the above formulas and the hypotheses yields

\displaystyle|\nabla^{b}(R^{N}\circ\sigma)|

\displaystyle\leq CK^{2}t^{-b/2};

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla^{m}(\Delta T),\nabla^{m}T\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\Delta|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}-2|\nabla^{m+1}T|^{2}+CK^{2}|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}+CK^{3}t^{-m/2}|\nabla^{m}T|;

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla^{m}(T*R^{M}),\nabla^{m}T\rangle

\displaystyle\leq CK^{2}|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}+CK^{3}t^{-m/2}|\nabla^{m}T|;

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla^{m}(T*R^{N}),\nabla^{m}T\rangle

\displaystyle\leq CK^{2}|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}+CK^{3}t^{-m/2}|\nabla^{m}T|;

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla^{m}(\nabla T*\nabla g),\nabla^{m}T\rangle

\displaystyle\leq CK|\nabla^{m+1}T||\nabla^{m}T|+CK^{2}|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}+CK^{2}t^{-(m+1)/2}|\nabla^{m}T|;

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla^{m}(T^{3}*R^{N}),\nabla^{m}T\rangle

\displaystyle\leq CK^{4}|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}+CK^{5}t^{-m/2}|\nabla^{m}T|;

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla^{m}(\nabla T*\nabla\eta),\nabla^{m}T\rangle

\displaystyle\leq CK|\nabla^{m}T||\nabla^{m+1}T|+CK^{2}|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}+CK^{3}t^{-m/2}|\nabla^{m}T|;

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla^{m}(\nabla g*\nabla\eta),\nabla^{m}T\rangle

\displaystyle\leq CK^{3}t^{-m/2}|\nabla^{m}T|+CK^{3}t^{-(m-1)/2}|\nabla^{m}T|;

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla^{m-1}(T*R^{N}),\nabla^{m}T\rangle

\displaystyle\leq CKt^{-m/2}|\nabla^{m}T|.

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(|\nabla^{m}T|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\Delta|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}-2|\nabla^{m+1}T|^{2}+CK|\nabla^{m}T||\nabla^{m+1}T|+CK^{2}(1+K^{2})|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}

\displaystyle+CK^{2}(K+K^{3})t^{-m/2}|\nabla^{m}T|+CK^{2}t^{-(m+1)/2}|\nabla^{m}T|+CK^{3}t^{-(m-1)/2}|\nabla^{m}T|.

Now, by Young’s inequality, for
\varepsilon
small enough, and using
K\geq 1
and
t\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(|\nabla^{m}T|^{2})\leq

\displaystyle\Delta|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}-|\nabla^{m+1}T|^{2}+CK^{2}|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}+CK^{2}t^{-1/2}|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}+CK^{5}t^{-m/2}|\nabla^{m}T|

\displaystyle+CK^{2}t^{-(m+1)/2}|\nabla^{m}T|

This remains true replacing
k
by
m-k
so that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(|\nabla^{m-k}T|^{2})\leq

\displaystyle\Delta|\nabla^{m-k}T|^{2}-|\nabla^{m-k+1}T|^{2}+CK^{4}t^{-(m-k)}+CK^{4}t^{-1/2}t^{-(m-k)}

\displaystyle+CK^{6}t^{-(m-k)/2}t^{-(m-k)/2}+CK^{3}t^{-(m-k+1)/2}t^{-(m-k)/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Delta|\nabla^{m-k}T|^{2}-|\nabla^{m-k+1}T|^{2}+CK^{4}t^{-(m-k)}+CK^{4}t^{-1/2}t^{-(m-k)}.

Let
f_{m}=t^{m}|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}+\beta_{m}\sum_{k}\alpha^{m}_{k}t^{m-k}|\nabla^{m-k}T|^{2}
.

Combining the previous estimates and Young’s inequality again,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{m}}{\partial t}\leq

\displaystyle\Delta f_{m}+\Big(CK^{2}t^{m}+CK^{6}t^{m}+CK^{2}t^{(2m-1)/2}+(C+m)t^{m-1}-\beta_{m}\alpha^{m}_{1}t^{m-1}\Big)|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}

\displaystyle+\beta_{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}((m-k)\alpha^{m}_{k}-\alpha^{m}_{k+1})t^{m-k-1}|\nabla^{m-k}T|^{2}

\displaystyle+C\beta_{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\alpha^{m}_{k}(K^{4}+K^{4}t^{-1/2})+CK^{4}.

But
(m-k)\alpha^{m}_{k}-\alpha^{m}_{k+1}=0
, for
1\leq k\leq m-1
, and for
\beta_{m}
large enough, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{m}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta f_{m}+CK^{4}t^{-1/2}+CK^{4}.

Since
f_{m}(0)=\beta_{m}\alpha^{m}_{m}|T|^{2}\leq\beta_{m}\alpha^{m}_{m}K^{2}
, we invoke the maximum principle to conclude that

\displaystyle\sup f_{m}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{m}\alpha^{m}_{m}K^{2}+CK^{4}t^{1/2}+CK^{4}t

\displaystyle\leq CK^{2}.

Hence
t^{m}|\nabla^{m}T|^{2}\leq CK^{2}
, that is the
m
-induction step
|\nabla^{m}T|\leq CKt^{-m/2}
, provided
0\leq t\leq 1/K^{4}
.
∎

applies in particular to

3.  Preliminaries on
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures

3.1
Definitions and basic properties

In this section, we review the notion of a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on an
8
-dimensional manifold
M
and the associated decomposition of differential forms. We also discuss the torsion tensor of a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure. More details can be found in
[
Joy00
, Chapter 10]
and
[
Kar08
]
. In particular, we closely follow
[
Kar08
]
in the discussion below.

A
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on
M^{8}
is a reduction of the structure group of the frame bundle
\mathrm{Fr}(M)
to the Lie group
\mathrm{Spin}(7)\subset\mathrm{SO}(8)
. From the point of view of differential geometry, a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on
M
is a
4
-form
\Phi
on
M
. The existence of such a structure is a
topological condition
. The space of
4
-forms which determine a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on
M
is a subbundle
\mathcal{A}
of
\Omega^{4}(M)
, called the bundle of
admissible

4
-forms. This is
not
a vector subbundle and it is not even an open subbundle, unlike the case for
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures.

A
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi
determines a Riemannian metric and an orientation on
M
in a nonlinear way. Let
p\in M
and extend a non-zero tangent vector
v\in T_{p}M
to a local frame
\{v,e_{1},\cdots,e_{7}\}
near
p
. Define

\displaystyle B_{ij}(v)

\displaystyle=((e_{i}\lrcorner v\lrcorner\Phi)\wedge(e_{j}\lrcorner v\lrcorner\Phi)\wedge(v\lrcorner\Phi))(e_{1},\cdots,e_{7}),

\displaystyle A(v)

\displaystyle=((v\lrcorner\Phi)\wedge\Phi)(e_{1},\cdots,e_{7}).

Then the metric induced by
\Phi
is given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle(g_{\Phi}(v,v))^{2}=-\frac{7^{3}}{6^{\frac{7}{3}}}\frac{(\textup{det}\ B_{ij}(v))^{\frac{1}{3}}}{A(v)^{3}}.

The metric and the orientation determine a Hodge star operator
\star
, and the
4
-form is
self-dual
, i.e.,
\star\Phi=\Phi
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\nabla
be the Levi-Civita connection of the metric
g_{\Phi}
. The pair
(M^{8},\Phi)
is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-manifold
if
\nabla\Phi=0
. This is a non-linear partial differential equation for
\Phi
, since
\nabla
depends on
g
, which in turn depends non-linearly on
\Phi
. A
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-manifold has Riemannian holonomy contained in the subgroup
\mathrm{Spin}(7)\subset\mathrm{SO}(8)
. Such a parallel
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure is also called
torsion-free
.

we have

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{1}}_{+}\otimes X+X\otimes{\mathbf{1}}_{+},e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\wedge e_{k}\wedge e_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathbf{1}}_{+},(e_{i}(e_{j}(e_{k}(e_{l}X))))\rangle+\langle X,(e_{i}(e_{j}(e_{k}(e_{l}{\mathbf{1}}_{+}))))\rangle.

Identifying
{\mathbf{1}}_{+}
with the identity of
\mathbb{O}
and defining the
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi_{0}
by
[
SW17
, Thm 5.20]

we obtain

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{1}}_{+}\otimes X+X\otimes{\mathbf{1}}_{+},e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\wedge e_{k}\wedge e_{l}\rangle=2\Phi_{0}(e_{i}X,e_{j},e_{k},e_{l}).

To compute the term
[X\otimes X]_{4}
, we again take its inner-product with
e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\wedge e_{k}\wedge e_{l}\in\Lambda^{4}\mathbb{R}^{8}
:

\langle X\otimes X,e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\wedge e_{k}\wedge e_{l}\rangle=\varepsilon(X,e_{l}(e_{k}(e_{j}(e_{i}X)))),

where
\epsilon
is an inner-product on
\mathbb{P}
.
If
\Phi_{0}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})\neq 0
, then

e_{l}(e_{k}(e_{j}(e_{i}X)))=\begin{cases}\phantom{-}\Phi_{0}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})X,\text{ if }X=e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l}\\
-\Phi_{0}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})X\ \ \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Decomposing
X=X_{ijkl}+X_{*}
, with
X_{ijkl}\in\Spa(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon(X,e_{l}(e_{k}(e_{j}(e_{i}X))))

\displaystyle=\Phi_{0}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})(|X_{ijkl}|^{2}-|X_{*}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\Phi_{0}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})(2|X_{ijkl}|^{2}-|X|^{2}).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle 4(X\wedge(X\lrcorner\Phi_{0}))(e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\wedge e_{k}\wedge e_{l})

\displaystyle=|X_{ijkl}|^{2}\Phi_{0}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l}),

and, at least for such quadruples, the following relation holds:

\langle X\otimes X,e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\wedge e_{k}\wedge e_{l}\rangle=(8(X\wedge(X\lrcorner\Phi_{0}))-|X|^{2}\Phi_{0})(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l}).

This formula remains valid for all the other quadruples, so long as
X
is pure imaginary.

Summing up the three computations, we have:

[X\otimes X]_{4}=(f^{2}-|X|^{2})\Phi_{0}+2f\Theta_{0,X}+8(X\wedge(X\lrcorner\Phi_{0})),

where
\Theta_{0,X}(a,b,c,d)=\Phi_{0}(a\cdot X,b,c,d)
is a four-form.

4.  Harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow

4.1
Derivation of the flow

In this section we define the harmonic flow of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures and prove that it is a negative gradient flow.

Definition 4.1

(Isometric
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures.)
.

Two
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
on
M
are called
isometric
if they induce the same Riemannian metric, that is, if
g_{\Phi_{1}}=g_{\Phi_{2}}
. We will denote by
\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
the space of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures that are isometric to a given
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\Phi_{0}
be a fixed initial
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on
M
. The
energy functional

E
on the set
\llbracket\Phi_{0}\rrbracket
is

(4.1)

\displaystyle E(\Phi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|T_{\Phi}|^{2}\vol_{g_{\Phi}}

where
T_{\Phi}
is the torsion of
\Phi
.

At this point it is worth noticing that, whereas the energy functional (
4.1
) could be considered over
all

\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures, in the present setting it is being considered only over isometric
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures. The natural question here, inspired by harmonic map theory, is whether, given a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi_{0}
, there is a “best”
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure in
\llbracket\Phi_{0}\rrbracket
. An obvious way to study this question is to consider the negative gradient flow of
E
.

4.1.1
Direct approach: variational calculus

The most general flow of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures
[
Kar08
]
is given by

(4.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}=A\diamond\Phi=(h+X)\diamond\Phi

where
A_{ij}=h_{ij}+X_{ij}
with
h_{ij}\in S^{2}
and
X_{ij}\in\Omega^{2}_{7}
. Thus, the most general flow of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures is given by a time-dependent family of symmetric
2
-tensors and a time-dependent family of
2
-forms lying in the subspace
\Omega^{2}_{7}
. In this case the evolution of the metric is given by

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=2h_{ij}.

We start by considering the variation of the torsion
T
with respect to variations of the
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures that preserve the metric.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(\Phi_{t})_{t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}
be a smooth family of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures in the class
\llbracket\Phi_{0}\rrbracket
. By (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we can write
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}\Phi_{t}=X\diamond\Phi
for some
X\in\Omega^{2}_{7}
. If
T_{t}
is the torsion of
\Phi_{t}
then

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}(T_{t})_{m;is}=X_{ip}T_{m;ps}-X_{sp}T_{m;pi}+\pi_{7}(\nabla_{m}X_{is}).

Proof.

As the
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures are varying in an isometry class,
g_{t}=g_{0}
for all
t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
and hence the covariant derivative
\nabla
is independent of
t
and so is the volume form. Since
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}\Phi_{t}=X\diamond\Phi
, by
[
Kar08
, Lemma 3.3]
,
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}\nabla\Phi_{t}=X\diamond\nabla\Phi+\nabla X\diamond\Phi
. Explicitly, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}(\Phi_{t})_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=X_{ip}\Phi_{pjkl}+X_{jp}\Phi_{ipkl}+X_{kp}\Phi_{ijpl}+X_{lp}\Phi_{ijkp},

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}(\nabla_{m}(\Phi_{t})_{ijkl})

\displaystyle=X_{ip}\nabla_{m}\Phi_{pjkl}+X_{jp}\nabla_{m}\Phi_{ipkl}+X_{kp}\nabla_{m}\Phi_{ijpl}+X_{lp}\nabla_{m}\Phi_{ijkp}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\quad+(\nabla_{m}X_{ip})\Phi_{pjkl}+(\nabla_{m}X_{jp})\Phi_{ipkl}+(\nabla_{m}X_{kp})\Phi_{ijpl}+(\nabla_{m}X_{lp})\Phi_{ijkp}.

Using the above equations and (
3.29
), we compute

\displaystyle 96\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}(T_{t})_{m;is}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}((\nabla_{m}\Phi_{ijkl})\Phi_{sjkl})

\displaystyle=\Phi_{sjkl}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}(\nabla_{m}(\Phi_{t})_{ijkl})+(\nabla_{m}\Phi_{ijkl})\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}(\Phi_{t})_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=\Big[X_{ip}\nabla_{m}\Phi_{pjkl}+X_{jp}\nabla_{m}\Phi_{ipkl}+X_{kp}\nabla_{m}\Phi_{ijpl}+X_{lp}\nabla_{m}\Phi_{ijkp}

\displaystyle\quad+(\nabla_{m}X_{ip})\Phi_{pjkl}+(\nabla_{m}X_{jp})\Phi_{ipkl}+(\nabla_{m}X_{kp})\Phi_{ijpl}+(\nabla_{m}X_{lp})\Phi_{ijkp}\Big]\Phi_{sjkl}

\displaystyle\quad+\nabla_{m}\Phi_{ijkl}\Big[X_{ip}\Phi_{pjkl}+X_{jp}\Phi_{ipkl}+X_{kp}\Phi_{ijpl}+X_{lp}\Phi_{ijkp}\Big].

Using the contraction identities (
3.21
)-(
3.24
), the above becomes

\displaystyle 96\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}(T_{t})_{m;is}

\displaystyle=96X_{ip}T_{m;ps}-96X_{sp}T_{m;pi}+42\nabla_{m}X_{is}+\nabla_{m}X_{jp}(6g_{is}g_{pj}-6g_{ij}g_{ps}-4\Phi_{ipsj})

\displaystyle\quad+\nabla_{m}X_{kp}(6g_{is}g_{pk}-6g_{ik}g_{ps}-4\Phi_{ipsk})+\nabla_{m}X_{lp}(6g_{is}g_{pl}-6g_{il}g_{ps}-4\Phi_{ipsl})

\displaystyle=96X_{ip}T_{m;ps}-96X_{sp}T_{m;pi}+42\nabla_{m}X_{is}-18\nabla_{m}X_{is}-12\nabla_{m}X_{jp}\Phi_{isjp}

\displaystyle=96X_{ip}T_{m;ps}-96X_{sp}T_{m;pi}+24\nabla_{m}X_{is}-12\nabla_{m}X_{jp}\Phi_{isjp}

which, using (
3.10
) for the last two terms, is precisely (
4.4
).
∎

Let
E
be the energy functional from (
4.1
), restricted to the isometry class
\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures. The following result motivates our formulation of the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow, cf. Definition

.

Lemma 4.4
.

The gradient of
16E:\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
at the point
\Phi
is
-\Div T\diamond\Phi
, where
T
is the torsion of
\Phi
. In other words, if
(\Phi_{t})_{t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}
is a smooth family of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures in
\llbracket\Phi_{0}\rrbracket
and
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\Phi_{t}=\xi
, then

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}16E(\Phi_{t})=-\int_{M}\langle\Div T\diamond\Phi,\xi\rangle\vol.

Proof.

We use Proposition
4.3
to compute

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}E(\Phi_{t})

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}(T_{t})_{m;is}(T_{t})_{m;is}\vol

\displaystyle=\int_{M}T_{m;is}(X_{ip}T_{m;ps}-X_{sp}T_{m;pi}+\pi_{7}(\nabla_{m}X_{is}))\vol

\displaystyle=\int_{M}T_{m;is}(X_{ip}T_{m;ps}-X_{sp}T_{m;pi}+\frac{1}{4}\nabla_{m}X_{is}-\frac{1}{8}\nabla_{m}X_{jp}\Phi_{isjp})\vol.

The first two terms vanish, as
T_{m;is}T_{m;ps}
and
T_{m;is}T_{m;pi}
are symmetric in
i,\ p
and
s,\ p
, respectively, and
X\in\Omega^{2}_{7}
. We integrate by parts on the remaining two terms:

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}E(\Phi_{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{M}-\frac{1}{4}\nabla_{m}T_{m;is}X_{is}+\frac{1}{8}\nabla_{m}T_{m;is}X_{jp}\Phi_{isjp}\vol

\displaystyle=\int_{M}-\frac{1}{4}\nabla_{m}T_{m;is}X_{is}-\frac{3}{4}\nabla_{m}T_{m;is}X_{is}\vol

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}\langle\Div T,X\rangle\vol.

Using Proposition
3.3
–
2.
, we see that
A,B\in\Omega^{2}_{7}
satisfy
\langle A\diamond\Phi,B\diamond\Phi\rangle=16\langle A,B\rangle
, so the above equation becomes

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}16E(\Phi_{t})=-16\int_{M}\langle\Div T,X\rangle\vol=\int_{M}\langle\Div T\diamond\Phi,X\diamond\Phi\rangle\vol.

The result now follows from the splitting
\Omega^{4}=\Omega^{4}_{1}\oplus\Omega^{4}_{7}\oplus\Omega^{4}_{27}\oplus\Omega^{4}_{35}
,
with
\Omega^{4}_{7}=\{Y\diamond\Phi\mid Y\in\Omega^{2}_{7}\}
and
T_{m;is}\in\Omega^{2}_{7}
, for each
m
.
∎

Comparing (
4.2
) and (
1.1
), we see that

for the harmonic flow, and hence it is indeed a flow of isometric
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures.

4.1.2
Alternative approach: representation theory

The harmonicity condition for
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures can be inferred as an instance of the general framework in §
2.1
, stemming from the representation theory of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
.
The key ingredient is the connection form
f
, which identifies the vertical of the tangent bundle of the twistor space with
\mathfrak{m}
, the (naturally reductive) complement of
\mathfrak{so}(7)=\mathfrak{spin}(7))\subset\mathfrak{so}(8)
. Sections of this space correspond to
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures and restricting ourselves to the vertical part means we only look at variations through
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures.

If
\tilde{\Phi}
is the universal
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure [cf. (
2.5
)], then the connection form is characterised by

\nabla_{A}\tilde{\Phi}=f(A).\tilde{\Phi}.

Identifying
\mathfrak{so}(8)\simeq\Omega^{2}
, the reductive complement
\mathfrak{m}
is then identified with
\Omega^{2}_{7}
and
f(A).\tilde{\Phi}
should be understood as the the natural
\mathrm{GL}(8)
-action encoded in the diamond operator (
3.13
). We invert the
\diamond
operator with a triple contraction
\lrcorner_{3}
:

(\beta\diamond\Phi)\lrcorner_{3}\Phi=96\beta.

The connection form is then given by
96f(A)=\nabla_{A}\tilde{\Phi}\lrcorner_{3}\tilde{\Phi}
and, pulling back by a section
\sigma
of the twistor space, we obtain:

f(d\sigma(X))=\tfrac{1}{96}(\nabla_{X}\Phi)\lrcorner_{3}\Phi.

Everything now follows from this expression,
which is precisely the torsion
T(X)
of (
3.29
).
Then the (vertical) energy density is
|d^{\mathcal{V}}\sigma|^{2}=|T|^{2}
, so the energy is precisely the
L^{2}
-norm of the torsion. The vertical tension field is

I(\tau^{\mathcal{V}}(\sigma))=\sum_{1}^{8}\nabla_{e_{i}}(T(e_{i}))-T(\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{i})=\Div T

and the flow
\frac{d\sigma_{t}}{dt}=\tau^{\mathcal{V}}(\sigma_{t})
is equivalent to
I(\frac{d\sigma_{t}}{dt})=I(\tau^{\mathcal{V}}(\sigma_{t}))

where
I
plays the role of an extended connection
f
on
M\times\mathbb{R}
.
We know that
I(\tau^{\mathcal{V}}(\sigma_{t}))=\Div T_{t}
and, generalising to
M\times\mathbb{R}
(or at least an interval) all the previous objects, we have

I\left(\frac{d\sigma_{t}}{dt}\right)=\tfrac{1}{96}\frac{d{\Phi}_{t}}{dt}\lrcorner_{3}{\Phi}_{t}.

On the other hand, since
\Div T_{t}=\tfrac{1}{96}(\Div T_{t}\diamond{\Phi}_{t})\lrcorner_{3}{\Phi}_{t}\in\Omega^{2}_{7}
,
and
\lrcorner_{3}{\Phi}_{t}
is an isomorphism on
\Omega^{2}_{7}
(its kernel being
\Omega^{2}_{21}
), we recover indeed

\frac{d{\Phi}_{t}}{dt}=\Div T_{t}\diamond{\Phi}_{t}.

Remark 4.5
.

This flow is the vertical part of the harmonic map flow, so short time existence of (
1.1
) follows from general properties of parabolic equations, cf.
[
LS19
]
.

Definition 4.17
.

Let
\{M_{i}\}
be a sequence of
8
-manifolds and let
p_{i}\in M_{i}
, for each
i
. Let
\Phi_{i}
be a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on
M_{i}
such that the associated Riemannian metric
g_{i}
on
M_{i}
are complete, for each
i
. Let
M
be an
8
-manifold with
p\in M
and
\Phi
a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on
M
. We say that

\displaystyle(M_{i},\Phi_{i},p_{i})\rightarrow(M,\Phi,p)\ \ \ \ \textup{as}\ i\rightarrow\infty

if there exists a sequence of compact subsets
\Omega_{i}\subset M
exhausting
M
with
p_{i}\in\textup{int}(\Omega_{i})
for each
i
and a sequence of diffeomorphisms
F_{i}:\Omega_{i}\rightarrow F_{i}(\Omega_{i})\subset M_{i}
with
F_{i}(p)=p_{i}
such that on every compact subset
K
of
M
and for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
i_{0}=i_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that for
i\geq i_{0}
,

\displaystyle\underset{x\in K}{\textup{sup}}\ |\nabla^{k}(F_{i}^{*}\Phi_{i}-\Phi)|_{g_{0}}<\varepsilon,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \textup{for}\ k\geq 0,

uniformly on
K
. Here the covariant derivative is with respect to any fixed metric
g_{0}
on
M
.

The proof of the following compactness theorem is similar in spirit to the compactness theorems for complete pointed Riemannian manifolds
[
Ham95
, Theorem 2.3]
, and for
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures by Lotay–Wei
[
LW17
, Theorem 7.1]
.

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
(M_{i}^{8},\Phi_{i},g_{i})
be a sequence of smooth manifolds with
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures and with the induced Riemannian metrics, and suppose that, for each
i
, we have
p_{i}\in M_{i}
and
g_{i}
is complete. Suppose further, that

(4.15)

\displaystyle\underset{i}{\textup{sup}}\ \underset{x\in M_{i}}{\textup{sup}}\ \left(|\nabla^{k}_{g_{i}}\mathrm{R}_{g_{i}}(x)|^{2}_{g_{i}}+|\nabla^{k+1}_{g_{i}}T_{i}(x)|^{2}_{g_{i}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty,\quad\forall\ k\geq 0,

where
T_{i}
,
\mathrm{R}_{g_{i}}
are the torsion of
\Phi_{i}
and the curvature of
g_{i}
, respectively, and the injectivity radius of
(M_{i},g_{i})
at
p_{i}
satisfies

\displaystyle\textup{inf}\ inj(M_{i},g_{i},p_{i})>0,\quad\forall\ i.

Then there exist a manifold
(M^{8},\Phi)
with a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure and a point
p\in M
such that, subsequentially,

\displaystyle(M_{i},\Phi_{i},p_{i})\rightarrow(M,\Phi,p),\quad as\ i\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we will continue to use the index
i
, even after taking a subsequence. By (
4.15
), we have a uniform bound on the Riemann curvature tensor, and the injectivity radius is positive, so the Cheeger–Gromov compactness theorem for complete pointed Riemannian manifolds
[
Ham95
, Theorem 2.3]
yields a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{8},g)
and a point
p\in M
such that, after passing to a subsequence,

(4.16)

\displaystyle(M_{i},g_{i},p_{i})\rightarrow(M,g,p),\quad\textup{as}\ i\rightarrow\infty.

Explicitly, there exist nested compact sets
\Omega_{i}\subset M
exhausting
M
, with
p\in\textup{int}(\Omega_{i})
for each
i
, and diffeomorphisms
F_{i}:\Omega_{i}\rightarrow F_{i}(\Omega_{i})\subset M_{i}
with
F_{i}(p)=p_{i}
, such that, on any compact subset
K\subset M
and for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
i_{0}=i_{0}(\varepsilon)
for which the following hold uniformly on
K
:

\displaystyle\underset{x\in K}{\textup{sup}}\ |\nabla^{k}(F_{i}^{*}g_{i}-g)|_{g_{0}}<\varepsilon,\quad\text{for}\quad k\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad i\geq i_{0},

Fixing
i
sufficiently large, let us work on the compact subset
\Omega_{i}\subset M
. Since
\{\Omega_{i}\}
is nested, for each
j\geq 0
we have
\Omega_{i}\subset\Omega_{i+j}
and a diffeomorphism
F_{i+j}:\Omega_{i+j}\rightarrow F_{i+j}(\Omega_{i+j})\subset M_{i+j}
. Define the restricted diffeomorphism

\displaystyle F_{i,j}=\left.F_{i+j}\right|_{\Omega_{i}}:\Omega_{i}\rightarrow F_{i+j}(\Omega_{i})\subset M_{i+j},\quad\forall\ j\geq 0.

The convergence in (
4.16
) implies that the sequence
\{g_{i,j}=F_{i,j}^{*}g_{i+j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
of Riemmanian metrics on
\Omega_{i}
converges to
g_{i,\infty}=g
on
\Omega_{i}
, as
j\rightarrow\infty
.

Let
\nabla,\ \nabla_{i,j}
be the Levi-Civita connections of
g
and
g_{i,j}
on
\Omega_{i}
, respectively. Let
h=g-g_{i,j}
and
A=\nabla-\nabla_{i,j}
be the difference of the metrics and their connections on
\Omega_{i}
, respectively. Then locally,

\displaystyle A^{c}_{ab}=\frac{1}{2}(g_{i,j})^{cd}(\nabla_{a}h_{bd}+\nabla_{b}h_{ad}-\nabla_{d}h_{ab}).

Since
g_{i,j}\rightarrow g
smoothly on
\Omega_{i}
, as
j\rightarrow\infty
,
g_{i,j}
and
g
are equivalent for large enough
j
, and hence we can take the norms with respect to either of these metrics. Moreover,
|\nabla^{k}h|_{g}\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty
, for all
k\geq 0
. Hence,
A
is uniformly bounded with respect to
g
for all large
j
. Since
\nabla g=0
,

\displaystyle\nabla^{k}A^{c}_{ab}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\nabla^{(k+1-l)}(g_{i,j})^{cd}(\nabla^{l}\nabla_{a}h_{bd}+\nabla^{l}\nabla_{b}h_{ad}-\nabla^{l}\nabla_{d}h_{ab})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\nabla^{(k+1-l)}(g^{cd}-(g_{i,j})^{cd})(\nabla^{l}\nabla_{a}h_{bd}+\nabla^{l}\nabla_{b}h_{ad}-\nabla^{l}\nabla_{d}h_{ab}).

Thus there exist constants
c_{k}
for
k\geq 0
such that
|\nabla^{k}A|_{g}\leq c_{k}
for all
j\geq 0
.

Using the diffeomorphisms
F_{i,j}
, we can define
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures
\Phi_{i,j}=F^{*}_{i,j}\Phi_{i+j}
on
\Omega_{i}
by pulling back the
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi_{i+j}
on
M_{i+j}
. We next estimate
|\nabla^{k}\Phi_{i,j}|_{g}
. Since
g
and
g_{i,j}
are equivalent for large
j
,
|\Phi_{i,j}|_{g}\leq c_{0}|\Phi_{i,j}|_{g_{i,j}}\leq 8c_{0}
for some constant
c_{0}
. Next, observe trivially that

\displaystyle\nabla\Phi_{i,j}=\nabla_{i,j}\Phi_{i,j}+(\nabla-\nabla_{i,j})\Phi_{i,j},

and since
A
is uniformly bounded, there is a constant
c_{1}
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla\Phi_{i,j}|_{g}\leq c_{0}|\nabla_{i,j}\Phi_{i,j}|_{g_{i,j}}+C|A|_{g}|\Phi_{i,j}|_{g}\leq c_{1}.

Similarly, for
\nabla^{2}{\Phi_{i,j}}
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\Phi_{i,j}=\nabla^{2}_{i,j}\Phi_{i,j}+(\nabla-\nabla_{i,j})\nabla_{i,j}\Phi_{i,j}+(\nabla(\nabla-\nabla_{i,j}))\Phi_{i,j},

and so, since
A
,
\nabla A
are uniformly bounded, there is a constant
c_{2}
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}\Phi_{i,j}|_{g}\leq C|\nabla^{2}_{i,j}\Phi_{i,j}|_{g_{i,j}}+C|A|_{g}|\nabla_{i,j}\Phi_{i,j}|_{g}+C|\nabla A|_{g}|\Phi|_{g}\leq c_{2}.

For
k\geq 2
, we have the estimate

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}\Phi_{i,j}|_{g}\leq C\sum_{l=0}^{k}|A|^{l}_{g}|\nabla^{k-l}_{i,j}\Phi_{i,j}|_{g_{i,j}}+C\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}|\nabla^{l}A|_{g}|\nabla^{k-1-l}\Phi_{i,j}|_{g}.

By the assumption (
4.15
), covariant derivatives of the torsion
T_{i}
of all orders are uniformly bounded, so using (
3.29
) and the estimate
|\nabla^{k}A|_{g}\leq c_{k}
, by an induction argument, we can show the existence of constants
c_{k}
for
k\geq 0
such that
|\nabla^{k}\Phi_{i,j}|_{g}\leq c_{k}
on
\Omega_{i}
for all
j,k\geq 0
.

Since we have uniform bounds on covariant derivatives of all orders of
\Phi_{i,j}
, the Arzelà-Ascoli Theorem (see eg.
[
AH11
, Corollary 9.14]
) implies that there exist a
4
-form
\Phi_{i,\infty}
and a subsequence of
\Phi_{i,j}
in
j
, which we still denote by
\Phi_{i,j}
, that converges to
\Phi_{i,\infty}
smoothly on
\Omega_{i}
. In other words,

(4.17)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}(\Phi_{i,j}-\Phi_{i,\infty})|_{g}\rightarrow 0,\quad\textup{as}\ j\rightarrow\infty,

uniformly on
\Omega_{i}
, for all
k\geq 0
.

We prove that
\Phi_{i,\infty}
is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on
\Omega_{i}
. Since each
\Phi_{i,j}
is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on
\Omega_{i}
with associated metric
g_{i,j}
, we have the following relation. Let
u,v,w
be any vector fields on
\Omega_{i}\subset M
, then

\displaystyle(u\lrcorner v\lrcorner\Phi_{i,j})\wedge(u\lrcorner w\lrcorner\Phi_{i,j})\wedge\Phi_{i,j}=6(g_{i,j}(u,u)g_{i,j}(v,w)-g_{i,j}(u,v)g_{i,j}(u,w))\vol_{i,j}.

Letting
j\rightarrow\infty
in the previous equation gives

(4.18)

\displaystyle(u\lrcorner v\lrcorner\Phi_{i,\infty})\wedge(u\lrcorner w\lrcorner\Phi_{i,\infty})\wedge\Phi_{i,\infty}=6(g_{i,\infty}(u,u)g_{i,\infty}(v,w)-g_{i,\infty}(u,v)g_{i,\infty}(u,w))\vol_{i,\infty}.

Since, by the Cheeger-Gromov compactness Theorem, the limit
g_{i,\infty}
is a Riemannian metric on
\Omega_{i}
and
\vol_{i,\infty}
is its volume form, (
4.18
) implies that
\Phi_{i,\infty}
is an admissible or a non-degenerate
4
-form and hence it is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on
\Omega_{i}
with the associated metric
g_{i,\infty}=g
.

We now go from
\Omega_{i}
to
M
. Since
\{\Omega_{i}\}
is nested, we denote the inclusion map of
\Omega_{i}
into
\Omega_{n}
by

\displaystyle I_{in}:\Omega_{i}\rightarrow\Omega_{n},\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq i.

As before, for each
\Omega_{n}
, we have
g_{n,j}
,
\Phi_{n,j}
which, after passing to a subsequence, converge to
g_{n,\infty}
,
\Phi_{n,\infty}
respectively as
j\rightarrow\infty
. By definition,

\displaystyle I^{*}_{in}g_{n,j}=g_{i,j}\quad\text{and}\quad I^{*}_{in}\Phi_{n,j}=\Phi_{i,j}.

Note that the pull-back
I^{*}_{in}
is independent of
j
, hence by taking
j\rightarrow\infty
in the above expression, we get

(4.19)

\displaystyle I^{*}_{in}g_{n,\infty}=g_{i,\infty}\quad\text{and}\quad I^{*}_{in}\Phi_{n,\infty}=\Phi_{i,\infty}.

Thus, from (
4.19
), there exists a
4
-form
\Phi
on
M
, which is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure with associated metric
g
such that

(4.20)

\displaystyle I^{*}_{i}g=g_{i,\infty}\quad\text{and}\quad I^{*}_{i}\Phi=\Phi_{i,\infty},

where
I_{i}:\Omega_{i}\rightarrow M
is the inclusion map.

Finally, we show that
(M_{i},\Phi_{i},p_{i})
converges to
(M,\Phi,p)
. Let
K
be any compact subset of
M
. Since
\{\Omega_{i}\}
exhausts
M
, there exists
i_{0}
such that
K
is contained in
\Omega_{i}
for all
i\geq i_{0}
. Fix an
i
such that
K\subset\Omega_{i}
. Then by (
4.17
), on
K
we have

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}(F^{*}_{l}\Phi_{l}-\Phi)|_{g}

\displaystyle=|\nabla^{k}(F^{*}_{i+j}\Phi_{i+j}-\Phi)|_{g},\ \ \ \ \textup{where}\ l=i+j,

\displaystyle=|\nabla^{k}(\Phi_{i,j}-\Phi_{i,\infty})|_{g}\rightarrow 0\ \ \ \ \textup{as}\ l\rightarrow\infty

for all
k\geq 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

We note that, if all the metrics in the sequence
(M_{i},\Phi_{i},g_{i})
are the same, then the limiting
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi
induces the same metric.

4.4.2
Compactness for the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow

We now state and prove the compactness theorem for solutions of the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow (
1.1
).

Theorem 4.19
.

Let
M_{i}
be a sequence of compact
8
-manifolds and let
p_{i}\in M_{i}
for each
i
. Let
\{\Phi_{i}(t)\}
be a sequence of solutions to the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow (
1.1
) on
M_{i}
for
t\in(a,b)
, with
-\infty\leq a<0<b\leq\infty
. Suppose that

(4.21)

\displaystyle\underset{i}{\textup{sup}}\ \underset{x\in M_{i},t\in(a,b)}{\textup{sup}}|T_{i}(x,t)|_{g_{i}}<\infty,

where
T_{i}
denotes the torsion of
\Phi_{i}(t)
, and the injectivity radius satisfies

(4.22)

\displaystyle\underset{i}{\textup{inf}}\ inj(M_{i},g_{i}(0),p_{i})>0.

Suppose further that there are uniform constants
C_{k}
, for all
k\geq 0
, such that

(4.23)

\displaystyle\underset{i}{\textup{sup}}\ |\nabla^{k}\mathrm{R}_{i}|_{g_{i}}\leq C_{k}.

Then there exist an
8
-manifold
M
, a point
p\in M
and a solution
\Phi(t)
of the flow (
1.1
) on
M
for
t\in(a,b)
such that, after passing to a subsequence,

\displaystyle(M_{i},\Phi_{i}(t),p_{i})\longrightarrow(M,\Phi(t),p),\quad\textup{as }i\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

The proof is similar in spirit to that of the compactness theorem for the isometric flow of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures in
[
DGK21
, Theorem 3.13]
. The
idea is to show that the bounds on the
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure and on its covariant derivatives and time derivatives at time
t=0
extend to subsequent times, in the presence of additional bounds on the torsion and its derivatives for all time. We give the details below.

From the derivative estimates Corollary
4.10
and (
4.21
), we have

(4.24)

|\nabla^{m}_{g_{i}(t)}T_{i}(x,t)|\leq C_{m}.

Since each
M_{i}
is compact, we know
|\mathrm{R}_{i}|_{g_{i}}
is bounded, and assumption (
4.22
) allows us to use Theorem
4.18
for
t=0
to extract a subsequence of
(M_{i},\Phi_{i}(0),p_{i})
which converges to a complete limit
(M,\Phi_{\infty}(0),p)
. That is, there exist compact subsets
\Omega_{i}\subset M
exhausting
M
with
p\in\text{int}(\Omega_{i})
, for each
i
, and diffeomorphisms
F_{i}:\Omega_{i}\rightarrow F_{i}(\Omega_{i})\subset M_{i}
with
F_{i}(p)=p_{i}
, such that
F_{i}^{*}g_{i}(0)\rightarrow g_{\infty}(0)
and
F_{i}^{*}\Phi_{i}(0)\rightarrow\Phi_{\infty}(0)
smoothly on any compact subset
\Omega\subset M
as
i\rightarrow\infty
.

Fix a compact subset
\Omega\times[c,d]\subset M\times(a,b)
and let
i
be sufficiently large so that
\Omega\subset\Omega_{i}
. Let
\bar{g}_{i}(t)=F_{i}^{*}g_{i}(t)
. Now since
\Phi_{i}(t)
are all solutions to the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow, we have
g_{i}(t)=g_{i}(0)
for each
i
. Thus we trivially have

\sup_{\Omega\times[c,d]}|\nabla^{m}_{\bar{g}_{i}(0)}\bar{g}_{i}(t)|_{\bar{g}_{i}(0)}=0.

Since the limit metric
g_{\infty}(0)
is uniformly equivalent to
g_{i}(0)
, we get

\sup_{\Omega\times[c,d]}|\nabla^{m}_{\bar{g}_{i}(\infty)}\bar{g}_{i}(t)|_{\bar{g}_{i}(\infty)}\leq C_{m}

for some positive constants
C_{m}
and similarly

\sup_{\Omega\times[c,d]}\Big|\frac{\partial^{l}}{\partial t^{l}}\nabla^{m}_{\bar{g}_{\infty}(0)}\bar{g}_{i}(t)\Big|_{\bar{g}_{\infty}(0)}\leq C_{m,l}

for some positive constants
C_{m,l}
.

Now let
\bar{\Phi}_{i}(t)=F_{i}^{*}\Phi_{i}(t)
. Then
\bar{\Phi}_{i}(t)
is a sequence of solutions of the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow on
\Omega\subset M
for
t\in[c,d]
. Using (
4.24
) and proceeding in a similar way as in
[
DGK21
, Claim 3.9]
for
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures, we deduce that there exist constants
C_{m}
, independent of
i
, such that

(4.25)

\sup_{\Omega\times[c,d]}|\nabla^{m}_{\bar{g}_{i}(0)}\bar{\Phi}_{i}(t)|_{\bar{g}_{i}(0)}\leq C_{m}

and since
\bar{g}_{i}(0)
and
\bar{\Phi}(0)
converge uniformly to
g_{\infty}(0)
and
\bar{\Phi}_{\infty}(0)
with all their covariant derivatives on
\Omega
, we have

(4.26)

\sup_{\Omega\times[c,d]}|\nabla^{m}_{\bar{g}_{\infty}(0)}\bar{\Phi}_{i}(t)|_{\bar{g}_{\infty}(0)}\leq C_{m}.

Moreover, because the time derivatives can be written in terms of spatial derivatives using the evolution equations of the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow, we get, for some uniform constants
C_{m,l}
,

(4.27)

\sup_{\Omega\times[c,d]}\Big|\frac{\partial^{l}}{\partial t^{l}}\nabla^{m}_{\bar{g}_{\infty}(0)}\bar{\Phi}_{i}(t)\Big|_{\bar{g}_{\infty}(0)}\leq C_{m,l}.

It now follows from the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem that there exists a subsequence of
\bar{\Phi}_{i}(t)
that converges smoothly on
\Omega\times[c,d]
. A diagonal subsequence argument then produces a subsequence that converges smoothly on any compact subset of
M\times(a,b)
to a solution
\bar{\Phi}_{\infty}(t)
of the isometric flow.
∎

5.  Monotonicity, entropy and
\varepsilon
-regularity of the
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow

In this section, just like in the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
case, we first introduce a quantity
\Theta
that is
almost monotonic
along (
1.1
). We derive the evolution of
\Theta
in Lemma
5.1
below, and use it to prove the almost monotonicity formula. We then introduce an
entropy functional
, and use an
\varepsilon
-regularity theorem to prove that small initial entropy implies long-time existence and convergence to a critical point. The proofs of several results in this section are similar to the corresponding theorems for
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures in
[
DGK21
]
, so in some cases we will only sketch the proofs and refer the reader to that source for further details.

5.1
Almost monotonicity formula

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold. For
x_{0}\in M
, let
u
be the fundamental solution of the backward heat equation, starting with the delta function at
x_{0}

[
Ham93
]
:

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\Delta\Big)u=0,\quad\lim_{t\to t_{0}}u=\delta_{x_{0}}

and set
u=\frac{e^{-f}}{\big(4\pi(t_{0}-t)\big)^{4}}.

For a solution
\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\in[0,t_{0})}
of the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow on
(M,g)
, we define the function

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Theta_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\Phi(t))=(t_{0}-t)\int_{M}|T_{\Phi(t)}|^{2}u\,\vol.

The following lemma is a direct adaptation of
[
DGK21
, Lemma 5.2]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Theta_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\Phi(t))=

\displaystyle-2(t_{0}-t)\int_{M}|\Div T-\nabla f\lrcorner T|^{2}u\vol

\displaystyle-2(t_{0}-t)\int_{M}\big(\nabla_{m}\nabla_{l}u-\frac{\nabla_{m}u\nabla_{l}u}{u}+\frac{ug_{ml}}{2(t_{0}-t)}\big)T_{m;is}T_{l;is}\vol

\displaystyle-(t_{0}-t)\int_{M}uR_{mlis}(2T_{l;ir}T_{m;rs}-2T_{m;ir}T_{l;rs}+\tfrac{1}{4}R_{mlis}-\tfrac{1}{8}R_{mlab}\Phi_{abis})

(5.2)

\displaystyle-2(t_{0}-t)\int_{M}T_{m;is}u\nabla_{l}R_{mlis}\vol.

Proof.

By direct computation, and using (
4.12
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Theta=

\displaystyle\ \int_{M}(t_{0}-t)u\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|T|^{2}-|T|^{2}u+(t_{0}-t)|T|^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u\,\vol

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int_{M}2(t_{0}-t)uT_{m;is}\Big(\nabla_{r}T_{r;ip}T_{m;ps}-\nabla_{r}T_{r;sp}T_{m;pi}+\pi_{7}(\nabla_{m}(\nabla_{r}T_{r;is})))\Big)

\displaystyle-|T|^{2}u-(t_{0}-t)|T|^{2}\Delta u\,\vol,

but
T_{m;is}\nabla_{r}T_{r;ip}T_{m;ps}=0
,
T_{m;is}\nabla_{r}T_{r;sp}T_{m;pi}=0
and
T_{m;is}\in\Omega^{2}_{7}
, therefore

\frac{d}{dt}\Theta=\int_{M}2(t_{0}-t)uT_{m;is}(\nabla_{m}(\nabla_{r}T_{r;is}))-|T|^{2}u-(t_{0}-t)|T|^{2}\Delta u\,\vol.

Integrating by parts and using the
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-Bianchi identity (
3.30
),
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Theta=

\displaystyle\ \int_{M}-2(t_{0}-t)\Big(|\Div T|^{2}u+\nabla_{m}uT_{m;is}\nabla_{r}T_{r;is}\Big)-|T|^{2}u+2(t_{0}-t)\Big(T_{m;is}\nabla_{l}u\nabla_{m}T_{l;is}

\displaystyle+2T_{m;is}\nabla_{l}uT_{l;ir}T_{m;rs}-2T_{m;is}\nabla_{l}uT_{m;ir}T_{l;rs}+\tfrac{1}{4}T_{m;is}\nabla_{l}uR_{mlis}-\tfrac{1}{8}T_{m;is}\nabla_{l}uR_{mlab}\Phi_{abis}\Big)\,\vol,

but, as before,

T_{m;is}T_{l;ir}T_{m;rs}=0,\quad T_{m;is}T_{m;ir}T_{l;rs}=0,\quad\text{and}\quad T_{m;is}(\tfrac{1}{4}R_{mlis}-\tfrac{1}{8}R_{mlab}\Phi_{abis})=T_{m;is}R_{mlis},

so

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Theta=

\displaystyle\ \int_{M}-2(t_{0}-t)\Big(|\Div T|^{2}u+\nabla_{m}uT_{m;is}\nabla_{r}T_{r;is}\Big)-|T|^{2}u

\displaystyle+2(t_{0}-t)\Big(T_{m;is}\nabla_{l}u\nabla_{m}T_{l;is}+T_{m;is}\nabla_{l}uR_{mlis}\Big)\,\vol

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int_{M}-2(t_{0}-t)\Big(|\Div T|^{2}u-2\langle\Div T,\nabla f\lrcorner T\rangle u\Big)

\displaystyle-2(t_{0}-t)\int_{M}\big(\nabla_{m}\nabla_{l}u+\frac{ug_{ml}}{2(t_{0}-t)}\big)T_{m;is}T_{l;is}

\displaystyle-(t_{0}-t)\int_{M}uR_{mlis}(2T_{l;ir}T_{m;rs}-2T_{m;ir}T_{l;rs}+\tfrac{1}{4}R_{mlis}-\tfrac{1}{8}R_{mlab}\Phi_{abis})

\displaystyle-2(t_{0}-t)\int_{M}T_{m;is}u\nabla_{l}R_{mlis}\vol.\qed

Theorem 5.2

(almost monotonicity formula)
.

Let
\{\Phi(t)\}
be a solution of the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow (
1.1
) on
(M^{8},g)
.

1.

If
M
is compact, then, for any
0<\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<t_{0}
, there exist

,

2.

When

Proof.

We sketch the proof following
[
DGK21
, Theorem 5.3]
and rely on Lemma

.

1.

The third and fourth terms of (
5.2
) are bounded by

C(1+\Theta(\Phi(t))),

due to the bounded geometry of
(M,g)
, Young’s inequality and
\int_{M}u\,\vol=1
.

For the second term of (

), use
[

]
and the decreasing of

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Theta(\Phi(t))\leq

\displaystyle-2(t_{0}-t)\int_{M}|\Div T-\nabla f\lrcorner T|^{2}u

\displaystyle+C_{1}\Big(1+\log\big(\frac{B}{(t_{0}-t)^{4}}\big)\Big)\Theta(\Phi(t))+C_{2}(1+E(\Phi(0)))\vol.

To control the logarithmic term, let

The claim is then obtained by integration over

On

∎

One needs to introduce the quantity
\Theta
because the energy functional

is not scale-invariant, hence it is not sufficient to control the small-scale behaviour of a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure. However, we would like to prove long-time existence and convergence of the flow when the initial energy is bounded (which is a reasonable assumption) and hence will need to bound
\Theta(\Phi(t))
in terms of the initial energy
E(\Phi(0))
for

. This fails for small times
t_{0}
, which can be observed from (

). Thus, we require a quantity sturdier than
\Theta
.
Motivated by analogous functionals for the mean curvature flow
[
CM12
]
, the high dimensional Yang–Mills flow
[
BKS17
]
, the harmonic map heat flow
[
KS16
]
and the isometric flow of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures
[

]
, we define the following

Definition 5.3
.

Let
(M^{8},\Phi,g)
be a compact manifold with a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)

\delta{(x,t)}
as

we define

(5.3)

\displaystyle\lambda(\Phi,\sigma)=\underset{(x,t)\in M\times(0,\sigma]}{\textup{max}}\left\{t\int_{M}|T_{\Phi}|^{2}(y)u_{(x,t)}(y,0)\vol\right\}.

One should think of
\sigma
as the “scale” at which we are analyzing the flow. Since

is compact, the maximum in (

We need the next lemma for the proof of the

[

and uses the analysis on the backwards heat kernel from
[

.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(M^{8},g)
be compact and
E_{0}>0
. For every
\varepsilon>0

\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,g,E_{0})

\bar{r}=\bar{r}(\varepsilon,g,E_{0})>0

Suppose
\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\in[0,t_{0})}

\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow (
1.1
), with induced metric

We can now state the
\varepsilon

\mathrm{Spin}(7)

Theorem 5.5

(
\varepsilon
-regularity)
.

Let
(M^{8},g)
be compact and
E_{0}>0
. There exist
\varepsilon,\ \bar{\rho}>0
such that, for every
\rho\in(0,\bar{\rho}]
, there exist
r\in(0,\rho)
and
C<\infty
such that the following holds:

Suppose
\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\in[0,t_{0})}
is a solution of the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow (
1.1
), with induced metric
g
, satisfying
E(\Phi(0))\leq E_{0}
. Whenever

\displaystyle\Theta_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\Phi(t_{0}-\rho^{2}))<\varepsilon,\quad\text{for some }x_{0}\in M,

then, setting
\Lambda_{r}(x,t)=\textup{min}\ \left(1-r^{-1}d_{g}(x_{0},x),\sqrt{1-r^{-2}(t_{0}-t)}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{r}(x,t)|T_{\Phi}(x,t)|\leq\frac{C}{r},\quad\forall\ (x,t)\in B(x_{0},r)\times[t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0}].

Proof.

Since the proof is similar to the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
case in
[
DGK21
, Theorem 5.7]
, we only sketch the main ideas. The proof is by contradiction. If the claim were not true, then for any sequences
\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 0
and
\bar{\rho}_{i}\rightarrow 0
, there would exist
\rho_{i}\in(0,\bar{\rho}_{i}]
such that, for any
r_{i}\in(0,\rho_{i})
and
C_{i}\rightarrow\infty
, there exist counterexamples
\{(M,\Phi_{i}(t))\}_{t\in[0,t_{i})}
with
g_{\Phi_{i}(t)}=g
,
E(\Phi(0))\leq E_{0}
, and
x_{i}\in M
, such that

\displaystyle\Theta_{(x_{i},t_{i})}(\Phi_{i}(t_{i}-{\rho_{i}}^{2}))<\varepsilon_{i},

while

(5.4)

\displaystyle r_{i}\left(\underset{(x,t)\in B(x_{i},r_{i})\times[t_{i}-{r_{i}}^{2},t_{i}]}{\textup{max}}\Lambda_{r_{i}}(x,t)|T_{\Phi_{i}}(x,t)|\right)>C_{i}.

Let
(\bar{x},\bar{t})\in B(x_{i},r_{i})\times[t_{i}-{r_{i}}^{2},t_{i}]
be the point where the maximum is attained and set
Q_{i}:=|T_{\Phi_{i}}(\bar{x_{i}},\bar{t_{i}})|
.

The idea now is to use the rescaled flow (see §
4.3.1
)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi_{i}}(t)

\displaystyle={Q_{i}}^{4}\Phi_{i}(\bar{t_{i}}+t{Q_{i}}^{-2}),\quad t\leq 0,

\displaystyle g_{i}

\displaystyle={Q_{i}}^{2}g,

and the pointed sequence
\{(M,\widetilde{\Phi_{i}}(t),g_{i},\bar{x}_{i})\}
. By the definition of

, we have

|T_{\widetilde{\Phi_{i}}}(x,t)|\leq\frac{\Lambda_{i}(\bar{x_{i}},\bar{t_{i}})}{\Lambda_{i}(x,\bar{t_{i}}+t{Q_{i}}^{-2})}\leq 2\quad\text{and}\quad|T_{\widetilde{\Phi_{i}}}(\bar{x}_{i},0)|=1.

We can then apply the derivative estimates and the compactness Theorem
4.19
to deduce that the sequence of pointed solutions subsequentially converges to a limit ancient solution of the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow
({\mathbb{R}}^{8},\Phi_{\infty}(t),g_{\textup{Eucl}},0)_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
with

(5.5)

\displaystyle|T_{\Phi_{\infty}}(0,0)|=1.

On the other hand, using the almost monotonicity formula Theorem
5.2
, the scale invariance of

gives

, which is not possible due to (

∎

5.2
Long-time existence

In this section, we prove that the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow on a compact manifold
M
, under smallness assumptions of the initial data, exists for all time and converges to a harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure. We start with the following convexity result for the energy functional
E
along the flow.

Lemma 5.7
.

Along a solution of the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow (
1.1
) on compact
(M,g)
,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}E(\Phi(t))

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}(\Lambda-3|T|^{2})|\Div T|^{2}\vol

where
\Lambda
is the first non-zero eigenvalue of the rough Laplacian of
g
on two-forms.

Proof.

From (
4.4
),

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}E(\Phi(t))

\displaystyle=-2\int_{M}\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Div T,\Div T\rangle\vol

\displaystyle=-2\int_{M}\nabla_{m}(\nabla_{r}T_{r;ip}T_{m;ps}-\nabla_{r}T_{r;sp}T_{m;pi}+\pi_{7}(\nabla_{m}(\nabla_{r}T_{r;is})))\nabla_{a}T_{a;is}\vol

\displaystyle=2\int_{M}2\nabla_{r}T_{r;ip}T_{m;ps}\nabla_{m}(\nabla_{a}T_{a;is})+|\pi_{7}\nabla_{m}(\nabla_{r}T_{r;is})|^{2}\vol.

Now
\Div T\in\Omega^{2}_{7}
, so that
(\Div T)_{is}\Phi_{isab}=-6(\Div T)_{ab}
, and therefore

\displaystyle-6\nabla_{m}(\Div T)_{ab}=\nabla_{m}(\Div T)_{is}\Phi_{isab}+2(\Div T)_{is}T_{m;iq}\Phi_{qsab}-6(\Div T)_{qb}T_{m;aq}-6(\Div T)_{aq}T_{m;bq}.

We use this fact to expand the second term in the integral:

\displaystyle|\pi_{7}\nabla_{m}(\nabla_{r}T_{r;is})|^{2}=\langle\pi_{7}\nabla_{m}(\nabla_{r}T_{r;is}),\nabla_{m}(\nabla_{r}T_{r;is})\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big(\tfrac{1}{4}(\nabla_{m}\Div T)_{ij}-\tfrac{1}{8}(\nabla_{m}\Div T)_{ab}\Phi_{ijab}\Big)(\nabla_{m}\Div T)_{ij}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}|\nabla_{m}(\Div T)|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{8}(\nabla_{m}\Div T)_{ab}\Phi_{ijab}\nabla_{m}(\Div T)_{ij}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}|\nabla_{m}(\Div T)|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{8}(\nabla_{m}\Div T)_{ab}\Big(-2(\Div T)_{is}T_{m;iq}\Phi_{qsab}+6(\Div T)_{qb}T_{m;aq}+6(\Div T)_{aq}T_{m;bq}

\displaystyle\qquad-6(\nabla_{m}\Div T)_{ab}\Big)

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{m}(\Div T)|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}(\nabla_{m}\Div T)_{ab}(\Div T)_{is}T_{m;iq}\Phi_{qsab}-\tfrac{3}{4}\Big((\Div T)_{qb}T_{m;aq}+(\Div T)_{aq}T_{m;bq}\Big)(\nabla_{m}\Div T)_{ab}

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{m}(\Div T)|^{2}-\tfrac{3}{4}\Big((\Div T)_{qb}T_{m;aq}+(\Div T)_{aq}T_{m;bq}\Big)(\nabla_{m}\Div T)_{ab}

\displaystyle\qquad+\tfrac{1}{4}(\Div T)_{is}T_{m;iq}\Big(-2(\Div T)_{ir}T_{m;ip}\Phi_{prqs}+6(\Div T)_{ps}T_{m;qp}+6(\Div T)_{qp}T_{m;sp}-6(\nabla_{m}\Div T)_{qs}\Big)

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{m}(\Div T)|^{2}-\tfrac{3}{4}\Big((\Div T)_{qb}T_{m;aq}+(\Div T)_{aq}T_{m;bq}\Big)(\nabla_{m}\Div T)_{ab}

\displaystyle\qquad+\tfrac{3}{2}(\Div T)_{is}T_{m;iq}(\Div T)_{ps}T_{m;qp}+\tfrac{3}{2}(\Div T)_{is}T_{m;iq}(\Div T)_{qp}T_{m;sp}-\tfrac{3}{2}(\Div T)_{is}T_{m;iq}(\nabla_{m}\Div T)_{qs}

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{m}(\Div T)|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{4}\Big((\Div T)_{qb}T_{m;aq}-(\Div T)_{aq}T_{m;bq}\Big)(\nabla_{m}\Div T)_{ab}

\displaystyle\qquad+\tfrac{3}{2}(\Div T)_{is}T_{m;iq}(\Div T)_{ps}T_{m;qp}+\tfrac{3}{2}(\Div T)_{is}T_{m;iq}(\Div T)_{qp}T_{m;sp}

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{m}(\Div T)|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}(\Div T)_{is}T_{m;iq}\Big((\Div T)_{ps}T_{m;qp}+(\Div T)_{qp}T_{m;sp}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq|\nabla_{m}(\Div T)|^{2}-3|\Div T|^{2}|T|^{2}.

\displaystyle\therefore\qquad\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}E(\Phi(t))

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}|\nabla_{m}(\Div T)|^{2}-3|\Div T|^{2}|T|^{2}\vol

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}(\Lambda-3|T|^{2})|\Div T|^{2}\vol,

where
\Lambda
is the first non-zero eigenvalue of the rough Laplacian on
2
-forms.
On a compact manifold, the kernel of that rough Laplacian consists indeed of parallel
2
-forms, so it is orthogonal to
\Div T
.
∎

The following general interpolation result can be proved in a similar way as
[
DGK21
, Lemma 5.12]
.

Lemma 5.8

(interpolation)
.

Let
(M,\Phi,g)
be a compact
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure manifold with torsion
T=T_{\Phi}
. Suppose
|\nabla T|\leq C
and non-collapsing at every
x\in M
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\vol(B(x,r))\geq v_{0}r^{8},\quad\text{for}\quad 0<r\leq 1,

for some constant
v_{0}(M,g)>0
. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta(\varepsilon,C,v_{0})\geq 0
such that, if

(5.7)

\displaystyle E(\Phi)<\delta,

then
|T|<\varepsilon
.

We now prove Theorem
C
. First, we prove that if the torsion of the initial
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
is pointwise small, then (
1.1
) exists for all time and converges to a critical point of the energy functional. Then Corollary
5.6
, the derivative estimates from Corollary
4.10
, and the interpolation Lemma
5.8
imply that, if the initial entropy is small, then the torsion does eventually become pointwise small.

Theorem 5.9

(small initial torsion gives long-time existence)
.

Let
(M,\Phi_{0},g)
be a compact
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure manifold. For every
\delta>0
, there exists
\varepsilon(\delta,g)>0
such that, if
|T_{\Phi_{0}}|<\varepsilon
, then a harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow (
1.1
) starting at
\Phi_{0}
exists for all time and converges subsequentially smoothly to a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi_{\infty}
such that

\Div T_{\Phi_{\infty}}=0,\quad|T_{\Phi_{\infty}}|<\delta.

Proof.

We only sketch the proof, since it is very similar to that of the isometric
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-flow
[
DGK21
, Theorem 5.13]
.
By the doubling-time estimate [Proposition
4.9
], given
\delta>0
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0

such that, whenever
|T_{\Phi_{0}}|<\varepsilon_{0}
,

(5.8)

\displaystyle t_{*}:=\textup{max}\{t\geq 0\ :\ |T_{\Phi(t)}|\leq 2\varepsilon_{0}\}>\delta.

We prove that
t_{*}=\infty
, hence that a flow with small initial torsion exists for all time. By contradiction, suppose
t_{*}<\infty
. Then, by the derivative estimates of Corollary
4.10
for the time interval
[t_{*}-\delta,t_{*}]
, there is a constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(g,\varepsilon_{0})
such that

(5.9)

\displaystyle|\nabla T_{\Phi(t_{*})}|<c_{0}.

Note that (
1.1
) is the negative gradient flow of
E(\Phi(t))
and hence
E(\Phi(t_{*}))\leq E(\Phi(0))=E(\Phi_{0})
. Given
a>0
, the interpolation Lemma
5.8
yields a constant
\gamma_{a}=\gamma_{a}(c_{0})>0
such that, if
E(\Phi_{0})<\gamma_{a}
, then
|T_{\Phi(t_{*})}|<a
.
Now, if
\varepsilon<\textup{min}\{\varepsilon_{0},\gamma_{2\varepsilon_{0}}\}
, then
|T_{\Phi(t_{*})}|<2\varepsilon
, which contradicts the maximality of
t_{*}
, as defined in (
5.8
). Thus
t_{*}=\infty
and the flow exists for all time.

Denote by
\Lambda
the first non-zero eigenvalue of the Laplacian acting on
2
-forms. Note from (
5.6
) that, if
|T|^{2}\leq\frac{\Lambda}{6}
, then

(5.10)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}|\Div T_{\Phi(t)}|^{2}\vol=-\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}E(\Phi(t))\leq-\frac{\Lambda}{2}\int_{M}|\Div T_{\Phi(t)}|^{2}\vol.

Let
a_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{6}}
. If we take
\varepsilon<\textup{min}\{\varepsilon,\gamma_{2\varepsilon_{0}},\gamma_{a_{0}}\}
, then from the previous paragraph we obtain existence of the flow for all time and satisfying (
5.10
). Thus, we get the decay estimate

(5.11)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\Div T_{\Phi(t)}|^{2}\vol\leq e^{-\frac{\Lambda t}{2}}\int_{M}|\Div T_{\Phi(0)}|^{2}\vol,\quad\forall\ t\geq 0.

Now, for every
s_{1}<s_{2}
, we have

(5.12)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\Phi(s_{2})-\Phi(s_{1})|\vol

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\left|\partial_{t}\Phi(s)\right|ds\vol=\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\int_{M}|\Div T_{\Phi(s)}|\vol ds

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\left(\int_{M}|\Div T_{\Phi(s)}|^{2}\vol\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}e^{-\frac{\Lambda s}{4}}ds.

Hence
\Phi(t)
converges exponentially fast to a unique limit
\Phi_{\infty}
in
L^{1}
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

Moreover, the uniform torsion bound gives estimates on all derivatives of the torsion. Given any sequence
t_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
, a subsequence of
\{\Phi(t_{n})\}
will converge smoothly to a limit, which must be
\Phi_{\infty}
by uniqueness. Therefore, the flow
\{\Phi(t)\}
converges smoothly to
\Phi_{\infty}
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. Inequality (
5.11
) implies that
\Div T_{\Phi_{\infty}}=0
, and choosing
\varepsilon>0
small enough, we can also achieve
|T_{\Phi_{\infty}}|<\delta
, using the interpolation Lemma
5.8
.
∎

We finally prove that small initial entropy implies long-time existence and convergence.

Theorem 5.10

(small entropy)
.

On a compact
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure manifold
(M,\Phi_{0},g)
, there exist constants
C_{k}(M,g)<+\infty
, such that the following holds. For each
\varepsilon>0
and
\sigma>0
, there exists
\lambda_{\varepsilon}(g,\sigma)>0
such that, if the entropy (
5.3
) satisfies

(5.13)

\displaystyle\lambda(\Phi_{0},\sigma)<\lambda_{\varepsilon},

then the torsion becomes eventually pointwise small along the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow (
1.1
) starting at
\Phi_{0}
. Therefore the flow exists for all time and subsequentially converges to a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi_{\infty}
such that

\Div T_{\Phi_{\infty}}=0,\quad|T_{\Phi_{\infty}}|<\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla^{k}T_{\Phi_{\infty}}|<C_{k},\,\forall\ k\geq 1.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.6
, if
\lambda_{\varepsilon}>0
is small enough, then the torsion along the flow satisfies

, for all

. Thus, by the derivative estimates in Corollary

,
\Phi(\tau)
satisfies

for some constant

. Hence by the interpolation Lemma
5.8
, if
\lambda_{\varepsilon}>0
is even smaller then
|T_{\Phi(\tau)}|<\varepsilon
. By Theorem
5.9
, the flow then converges smoothly to a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)

\Phi_{\infty}
with divergence-free torsion and derivative bounds.
∎

5.3
Singularity structure

We investigate the singularities of the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow. Let
\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\in[0,\tau)}
be a solution to (
1.1
) on a compact manifold
(M,g)
, with a finite time singularity at
\tau<+\infty
.

5.3.1
The singular set
S

Let
\varepsilon
and
\bar{\rho}
be the quantities from the
\varepsilon
-regularity Theorem
5.5
. We define the
singular set
of the flow by

(5.14)

\displaystyle S=\{x\in M\ :\ \Theta_{(x,\tau)}(\Phi(\tau-\rho^{2}))\geq\varepsilon,\ \textup{for\ all}\ \rho\in(0,\bar{\rho}]\}.

The following lemma explains why
S
is called the singular set of the flow.

Lemma 5.11
.

The harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow
\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\in[0,\tau)}
restricted to
M\setminus S
converges as
t\rightarrow\tau
, smoothly and uniformly away from
S
, to a smooth harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi(\tau)
on
M\setminus S
. Moreover, for every
x\in S
, there is a sequence
(x_{i},t_{i})\to(x,\tau)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{i}|T_{\Phi}(x_{i},t_{i})|=\infty.

Thus,
S
is indeed the singular set of the flow.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.5
, for every
x\in M\setminus S
, there exist
r_{x}>0
and
C_{x}<\infty
such that, setting

\Lambda_{r_{x}}(y,t)=\textup{min}\ \left(1-\frac{d_{g}(x,y)}{r_{x}},\sqrt{1-\frac{\tau-t}{{r_{x}}^{2}}}\right),

one has

\Lambda_{r_{x}}(y,t)|T_{\Phi}(y,t)|\leq\frac{C_{x}}{r_{x}},\quad\forall\ (y,t)\in B(x,r_{x})\times[\tau-{r_{x}}^{2},\tau].

So, for
\hat{r}_{x}=\frac{1}{2}r_{x}
, we have

T_{\Phi}(y,t)\leq\widehat{C}_{x},\quad\forall\ (y,t)\in B(x,\hat{r}_{x})\times[\tau-{\hat{r}_{x}}^{2},\tau].

Hence, by the local derivative estimates of Corollary
4.16
, there exist constants
C_{x,j}
such that

|\nabla^{j}T_{\Phi}(y,t)|\leq C_{x,j},\quad\forall\ (y,t)\in B(x,\frac{\hat{r}_{x}}{2})\times[\tau-\frac{{\hat{r}_{x}}^{2}}{4},\tau],\quad\forall\ j\geq 1.

Thus as
t\rightarrow\tau
, the flow
\Phi(t)
converges smoothly and uniformly away from
S
to a harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi(\tau)
.
∎

5.3.2
Proof of Theorem
D

Note from (
5.14
) that
S
is a closed set. We therefore estimate
\mathcal{H}^{6}(S)
, where
\mathcal{H}^{6}
denotes the
6
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
(M,g)
.

Let
S^{\prime}
be any subset of
S
. As in
[
GH96
]
, there is
S^{\prime\prime}\subset S^{\prime}
such that

(5.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{6}(S^{\prime\prime})\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{H}^{6}(S^{\prime})

and

\displaystyle u_{S^{\prime\prime}}(y,s)=\int_{S^{\prime\prime}}u_{(x,\tau)}(y,s)\vol(x)

satisfies

(5.16)

\displaystyle u_{S^{\prime\prime}}(y,s)\leq\frac{C}{\tau-s},

for some constant
C
depending only on
g
. Thus, for every
\rho\in(0,\bar{\rho})
, using (
5.14
) with the same
\varepsilon
and
S^{\prime\prime}\subset S
, we get

\displaystyle\varepsilon\mathcal{H}^{6}(S^{\prime\prime})=\int_{S^{\prime\prime}}\varepsilon d\mathcal{H}^{6}(x)\leq\int_{S^{\prime\prime}}\Theta_{(x,\tau)}(\Phi(\tau-\rho^{2}))d\mathcal{H}^{6}(x).

Using the definition of
\Theta
, the estimate (
5.16
) and the hypothesis (
1.5
), we estimate

\displaystyle\varepsilon\mathcal{H}^{6}(S^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{S^{\prime\prime}}\int_{M}\rho^{2}|T(y,\tau-\rho^{2})|^{2}u_{(x,\tau)}(y,\tau-\rho^{2})\vol(y)d\mathcal{H}^{6}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\rho^{2}|T(y,\tau-\rho^{2})|^{2}u_{S^{\prime\prime}}(y,\tau-\rho^{2})\vol(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M}|T(y,\tau-\rho^{2})|^{2}\vol(y)

\displaystyle\leq CE_{0}.

Since
S^{\prime}
was arbitrary, the result follows.

5.3.3
Soliton model at Type-I singularities

Let
\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\in[0,\tau]}
be a solution to (
1.1
) that exists for some maximal time
\tau
. From the evolution of the norm of the torsion tensor
T
along (
1.1
) in Proposition
4.14
, and the maximum principle, we see that the rate of blow-up of the norm of the torsion tensor must be at least

\displaystyle\underset{x\in M}{\textup{sup}}|T(t)|\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{C(\tau-t)}}.

Definition 5.13
.

The flow
\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\in[0,\tau]}
is said to have a
Type-I singularity at
\tau
if

\displaystyle\underset{x\in M}{\textup{sup}}|T(t)|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{C(\tau-t)}}.

Using the almost monotonicity formula from Theorem
5.2
as in
[
GH96
]
and
[
DGK21
, Theorem 5.20]
, we see that a sequence of blow-ups at a Type-I singularity admits a subsequence that converges to a shrinking soliton (cf. §
4.2.1
) of the flow:

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\in[0,\tau)}
be the maximal smooth harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow on
(M^{8},\Phi_{0})
starting at
\Phi_{0}
. Suppose that the flow encounters a Type-I singularity at
\tau<\infty
. Let
x\in M
and
\lambda_{i}\searrow 0
, and consider the rescaled sequence
\Phi_{i}(t)={\lambda_{i}}^{-3}\Phi(\tau-{\lambda_{i}}^{2}t)
. Then, after possibly passing to a subsequence,
(M,\Phi_{i}(t),x)
converges smoothly to an ancient harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow
\{\Phi_{\infty}(t)\}_{t<0}
on
({\mathbb{R}}^{8},g_{\textup{Eucl}})
induced by a shrinking soliton, i.e.

\displaystyle\Div T_{\Phi_{\infty}}(x,t)=-\frac{x}{2t}\lrcorner T_{\Phi_{\infty}}.

Moreover
x\in M\setminus S
if, and only if,
\{\Phi_{\infty}(t)\}
is the stationary flow induced by a torsion-free
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on
({\mathbb{R}}^{8},g_{\textup{Eucl}})
.

Remark 5.15
.

It would be very interesting to get explicit examples of shrinking, steady or expanding harmonic solitons, even for
\mathbb{R}^{8}
. On the contrary, if one could prove that there do not exist any shrinking solitons on
\mathbb{R}^{8}
, then the previous theorem tells us that the harmonic
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-flow has no Type-I singularities.

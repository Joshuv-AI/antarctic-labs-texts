Intersections of Poisson k k -flats in constant curvature spaces
Carina Betken, Daniel Hug, Christoph Thäle

Abstract
Poisson processes in the space of k k -dimensional totally geodesic subspaces ( k k -flats) in a d d -dimensional standard space of constant curvature κ ∈ { − 1 , 0 , 1 } \kappa\in\{-1,0,1\} are studied, whose distributions are invariant under the isometries of the space. We consider the intersection processes of order m m together with their ( d − m ⁡ ( d − k ) ) (d-m(d-k)) -dimensional Hausdorff measure within a geodesic ball of radius r r . Asymptotic normality for fixed r r is shown as the intensity of the underlying Poisson process tends to infinity for all m m satisfying d − m ⁡ ( d − k ) ≥ 0 d-m(d-k)\geq 0 . For κ ∈ { − 1 , 0 } \kappa\in\{-1,0\} the problem is also approached in the set-up where the intensity is fixed and r r tends to infinity. Again, if 2 ​ k ≤ d + 1 2k\leq d+1 a central limit theorem is shown for all possible values of m m . However, while for κ = 0 \kappa=0 asymptotic normality still holds if 2 ​ k > d + 1 2k>d+1 , we prove for κ = − 1 \kappa=-1 convergence to a non-Gaussian infinitely divisible limit distribution in the special case m = 1 m=1 . The proof of asymptotic normality is based on the analysis of variances and general bounds available from the Malliavin–Stein method. We also show for general κ ∈ { − 1 , 0 , 1 } \kappa\in\{-1,0,1\} that, roughly speaking, the variances within a general observation window W W are maximal if and only if W W is a geodesic ball having the same volume as W W . Along the way we derive a new integral-geometric formula of Blaschke–Petkantschin type in a standard space of constant curvature. Keywords . Blaschke–Petkantschin formula, central limit theorem, constant curvature space, Malliavin–Stein method, integral geometry, stochastic geometry, Poisson k k -flat process, random measure, U-statistic. Introduction and statement of the results

Stochastic geometry deals with the development and the probabilistic and geometric analysis of models for complex spatial random structures, typically in a Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of dimension
d\geq 2
. However, in recent years also stochastic geometry in non-Euclidean and especially in spherical and hyperbolic spaces has become an active field of research. The aim of this branch of stochastic geometry is to distinguish those properties of a random geometric system which are universal to some extent from the ones which are sensitive to the underlying geometry, especially to the curvature of the underlying space. We mention by way of example the studies

,

on geometric random graphs and networks. The present paper continues this line of research and naturally connects to the articles

In this paper we deal with a
d
-dimensional standard space
\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}
of constant curvature
\kappa\in\{-1,0,1\}
. For

we denote by

the space of
k

\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}

\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)

\mu_{k,\kappa}

2.1
for a detailed description. Next, for

\eta_{t,\kappa}

\displaystyle F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{(E_{1},\ldots,E_{m})\in\eta_{t,\kappa,\neq}^{m}}\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{d-m(d-k)}(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{m}\cap W)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\mathbbm{1}\{\text{dim}(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{m})=d-m(d-k)\}.

counts the number of points in

m
-tuples of
k
-flats from
\eta_{t,\kappa}
. In classical stochastic geometry in Euclidean space, that is, for
\kappa=0

[
24
,
28
,
36
]
on different levels of generality. In fact, there are two basic set-ups for which one can study the fluctuations of
F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}
:

(i)

For a fixed Borel set
W\subset\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}
with
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{d}(W)\in(0,\infty)
, define

\widehat{F}_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}:=\frac{F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}-\mathbb{E}F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}}},

(1.2)

and consider the asymptotics as
t\to\infty

(ii)

For
\kappa\in\{-1,0\}
and for each

B_{r,\kappa}^{d}
be a geodesic ball in
\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}
, define

\widetilde{F}_{r,t,\kappa}^{(m)}:=\frac{F_{B_{r,\kappa}^{d},t,\kappa}^{(m)}-\mathbb{E}F_{B_{r,\kappa}^{d},t,\kappa}^{(m)}}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}F_{B_{r,\kappa}^{d},t,\kappa}^{(m)}}},

(1.3)

and for fixed

r\to\infty

We start by considering the set-up described in (i). To measure the speed of convergence in the central limit theorem, we write
{\rm d}_{W}(X,Y)
for the Wasserstein distance and
{\rm d}_{K}(X,Y)
for the Kolmogorov distance between two random variables

{\rm d}_{\diamondsuit}(X,Y):=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{F}_{\diamondsuit}}|\mathbb{E}h(X)-\mathbb{E}h(Y)|,\qquad\diamondsuit\in\{W,K\},

where
\mathcal{F}_{W}
is the class of Lipschitz functions on
\mathbb{R}
with Lipschitz constant
\leq 1
and
\mathcal{F}_{K}
is the class of indicator functions of intervals of the form
(-\infty,x]
,

. The constants

in the forthcoming theorems depend on the dimension

Theorem 1.1

(Central limit theorem for large intensities)
.

Let
\kappa\in\{-1,0,1\}
and consider a Poisson process of
k
-flats in
\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
and
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}
. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
d-m(d-k)\geq 0
. Let
N
be a standard Gaussian random variable,
\diamondsuit\in\{K,W\}
and let
W\subset\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}
be a Borel set with
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{d}(W)\in(0,\infty)
. Then there is a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

{\rm d}_{\diamondsuit}(\widehat{F}_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)},N)\leq C\,t^{-1/2}

for all
t\geq 1
. In particular,
\widehat{F}_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}
satisfies a central limit theorem, as
t\to\infty
.

In fact, Theorem
1.1
is a direct consequence of the general quantitative central limit theorem for Poisson U-statistics in
[
45
, Theorem 4.7]
and
[
50
, Theorem 4.2]
, see also
[
14

and Section
3.2

It is an important observation that in Euclidean space, that is, for
\kappa=0
, and if we take for
W
a ball of radius
r>0
, the set-up considered in Theorem
1.1
is – up to rescaling – equivalent to considering a fixed intensity

and letting

grow to infinity at an appropriate speed. However, the equivalence breaks down for
\kappa=-1
. In fact, it was already shown in
[
26

that in hyperbolic space no central limit theorem holds, and an extension of this finding is stated as Theorem

of the present paper. Since in the Euclidean case
\kappa=0
we have for all

and

with

that

{\rm d}_{\diamondsuit}(\widetilde{F}_{r,t,0}^{(m)},N)\leq C\,r^{-\frac{d-k}{2}}

for
r\geq 1

C\in(0,\infty)
is a constant, depending only on

,
k
and

, we can from now on restrict our attention to the case
\kappa=-1
of hyperbolic space. In fact, by the compactness of the spherical space
\mathbf{M}_{1}^{d}
, spherical caps are bounded, which is the reason why in set-up (ii) we have restricted ourselves to the two non-compact space forms corresponding to
\kappa\in\{-1,0\}
. For simplicity of notation, let us assume that
t=1
in what follows. Moreover we write
\widetilde{F}_{r}^{(m)}
for
\widetilde{F}_{r,1,-1}^{(m)}
,
\mathbb{H}^{d}
for
\mathbf{M}_{-1}^{d}
,
\AOp_{h}(d,k)
instead of
\AOp_{-1}(d,k)
, and
\mu_{k}
for
\mu_{k,-1}
. We are now in the position to formulate a quantitative central limit theorem for

, for particular choices of the parameters

,

Theorem 1.2

(Central limit theorem for large radii and
\kappa=-1
)
.

Consider a Poisson process of
k
-flats in
\mathbb{H}^{d}

be a standard Gaussian random variable and

(i)

If

\displaystyle{\rm d}_{\diamondsuit}(\widetilde{F}_{r}^{(m)},N)\leq C_{1}\,\begin{cases}e^{-\frac{r}{2}(d-2k+1)}&:\text{ for }k\geq 1,\\

\end{cases}

(iii)

For

Conjecture.

Consider a Poisson process of

In the following theorem,

Theorem 1.4

(Non-Gaussian fluctuations for
m=1
and

)
.

Consider a Poisson process of
k
-flats in
\mathbb{H}^{d}
, where

and

. If

\displaystyle\frac{F_{r}^{(1)}-\mathbb{E}F_{r}^{(1)}}{e^{r(k-1)}}\xlongrightarrow{D}\frac{\omega_{k}}{(k-1)2^{k-2}}\,Z\quad\text{ as }r\rightarrow\infty,

where
Z
is the infinitely divisible, centred random variable given by

Z:=\lim_{T\to\infty}\Big(\sum_{s\in\zeta\cap[0,T]}\cosh^{-(k-1)}s-\frac{\omega_{d-k}}{d-k}\sinh^{d-k}T\Big)

(1.7)

and
\zeta
is an inhomogeneous Poisson process on
[0,\infty)
with intensity function given above.

Remark 1.5
.

(i)

By Proposition
3.1
below, the rescaling
e^{r(k-1)}
in the previous theorem is of the same order as
\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}F_{r}^{(1)}}
as
r\rightarrow\infty
, up to a multiplicative constant.

(ii)

As in
[
32
, Remark 2.3]
one shows by means of a martingale argument that the limit in (
1.7
) exists almost surely and in
L^{2}
. The fact that
Z
is infinitely divisible follows from the Lévi–Khinchin formula and the explicit representation (
4.2
) of the characteristic function of
Z
, which we establish in the course of the proof of Theorem
1.4
. The latter also shows that
Z
has no Gaussian component.
To explain the centering in (
1.7
), we consider

Y_{T}:=\int\indi\{s\in[0,T]\}\cosh^{-(k-1)}s\,\zeta(\textup{d}s).

Then

\mathbb{E}Y_{T}=\omega_{d-k}\int_{0}^{T}\cosh s\,\sinh^{d-k-1}s\,\textup{d}s=\frac{\omega_{d-k}}{d-k}\sinh^{d-k}T

and

\operatorname{Var}Y_{T}=\omega_{d-k}\int_{0}^{T}\cosh^{2-k}s\,\sinh^{d-k-1}s\,\textup{d}s.

If
2k>d+1
, then
\lim\limits_{T\to\infty}\operatorname{Var}Y_{T}<\infty
which justifies the martingale argument mentioned above.

The Lévy measure of
Z
is concentrated on
(0,1)
and arises as the image measure of the Lebesgue measure on
(0,\infty)
with density
s\mapsto\omega_{d-k}\cosh^{k}s\,\sinh^{d-k-1}s
under the mapping
s\mapsto\cosh^{-(k-1)}s
. Its Lebesgue density equals

\rho(y)=\frac{\omega_{d-k}}{{k-1}}y^{-\frac{d+k-2}{k-1}}\left(1-y^{\frac{2}{k-1}}\right)^{\frac{d-k}{2}-1},\qquad y\in(0,1).

Clearly,
\rho
has a singularity at
0
and the Lebesgue integral of
\rho
over
(0,1)
is infinite. Moreover, the integrability of the function
y\mapsto y^{2}\rho(y)
on
(0,1)
can be seen from

2-\frac{d+k-2}{k-1}>-1\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad d+1<2k.

These findings are consistent with the results obtained in
[
32
]
in the case where
k=d-1
.

Bounds for the growth of the variances as functions of the radius of a geodesic ball play an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.2
and especially Theorem
1.4
, see Proposition
3.1
below. Since we have explicit and unified formulas for the variances of the functionals
F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}
in an arbitrary observation window

and for general
\kappa\in\{-1,0,1\}
, it is natural to ask in this generality for which shapes

the variances are maximal. The answer is given by Theorem

, which states that the variances are maximal if
W
is a geodesic ball in
\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}
which has the same volume as

. It seems that this result is new even in the Euclidean case
\kappa=0
.

Theorem 1.6

(Variance inequality and maximal variances)
.

Consider a Poisson process of
k
-flats in
\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}
with
\kappa\in\{-1,0,1\}
,
d\geq 2
, and
k\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
. Let
W\subset\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa}
be a Borel set with
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{d}(W)\in(0,\infty)
, let
t>0
, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
d-m(d-k)\geq 0
. In addition, suppose that
W
is contained in a spherical cap of radius
\pi/4
if
\kappa=1
.
If
B_{W}\subset\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}
is a geodesic ball with
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{d}(W)=\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{d}(B_{W})
, then

\operatorname{Var}F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}\leq\operatorname{Var}F_{B_{W},t,\kappa}^{(m)}.

Equality holds if and only if there is an isometry
\varphi
of
\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa}
such that
W=\varphi(B_{W})
, up to sets of
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{d}
-measure zero.

Remark 1.7
.

(i)

We remark that the lower bound for
\operatorname{Var}F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}
in Euclidean space is zero. For
d=2
this can be checked by taking in
[
24
, Lemma 6.1]
a rectangle with side lengths

and

, and then letting

. Similar examples are possible in higher dimensions as well.

(ii)

A corresponding inequality also holds for the covariances between
F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m_{1})}
and
F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m_{2})}
, where
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy

for
i\in\{1,2\}
.

(iii)

If
k=0
, then
m=1
and
\operatorname{Var}F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(1)}=t\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\kappa}(W)
. For this reason Theorem
1.6
only deals with the case
k\geq 1
.

(iv)

For
\kappa=0
and the volumes (in the appropriate dimensions) of the intersection processes of a Poisson hyperplane process, Heinrich has asked for the shape of an observation window (of given volume) such that the asymptotic variance under homothetic scaling of the window is maximal (see
[
24
, Section 6]
). Theorem
1.6
and its proof answers this question in generalized form. Some related chord power integrals are discussed in
[
25
]
.

A crucial tool in the proof of Theorem
1.6
is a general sharp Riesz rearrangement inequality from
[
12
]
and the following integral-geometric transformation formula of Blaschke–Petkantschin type for constant curvature spaces, which is of independent interest and which we could not locate in the existing literature. To present it, we need the modified sine function
\sn_{\kappa}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, for
\kappa\in\{-1,0,1\}
, which is defined as

\sn_{\kappa}(r):=\begin{cases}\sin r,&\kappa=1,\\
r,&\kappa=0,\\
\sinh r,&\kappa=-1,\end{cases}

(1.8)

for
r\geq 0
. Recall that

denotes the intrinsic metric of
\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa}
.

Theorem 1.8

(Blaschke–Petkantschin type formula)
.

Let
\kappa\in\{-1,0,1\}
,
d\geq 2
, and
k\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
. If
f:\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa}\times\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa}\to[0,\infty]
is a measurable function, then

\displaystyle\int_{\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)}\int_{E}\int_{E}f(x,y)\sn_{\kappa}^{d-k}d_{\kappa}(x,y)\,\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{k}(\textup{d}x)\,\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{k}(\textup{d}y)\,\mu_{k,\kappa}(\textup{d}E)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\omega_{k}}{\omega_{d}}\int_{\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}}f(x,y)\,\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\kappa}(\textup{d}x)\,\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\kappa}(\textup{d}y).

(1.9)

The Euclidean case
\kappa=0
of Theorem
1.8
is known in more general form, see
[
19
, Lemma 5.5]
, where priority is given to
[
46
]
. The approach in
[
19
]
(for which help by Eva Vedel Jensen is acknowledged, see also
[
52
]
) is different from the present argument, also in the Euclidean case. We derive the result for
k\geq 2
from the special case
k=1
by another basic integral-geometric relation. For the case
k=1
we provide a completely new approach in hyperbolic space (
\kappa=-1
) which allows us to deduce the result from the Euclidean Blaschke–Petkantschin formula via a suitable model of hyperbolic space (compare
[
47
, Equation (18.2)]
for an approach via differential forms in the special case
k=1
). The current argument has the advantage of working in the same way in all three space forms simultaneously.

2
Preliminaries and preparations

2.1
The standard spaces of constant curvature

In this paper, we work in a
d
-dimensional standard space of constant curvature
\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa}
with
\kappa\in\{-1,0,1\}
and intrinsic metric
d_{\kappa}
. An arbitrarily fixed reference point in
\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa}
(the “origin”) will be denoted by
p
. As the canonical model space for
\mathbf{M}^{d}_{0}
, we use the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with Euclidean scalar product
\bullet
and norm
\|\cdot\|
, and we choose
p=o
. The Euclidean unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
will be the model space for
\mathbf{M}^{d}_{1}
, and often it is convenient to choose an orthogonal coordinate system
(o,e_{1},\ldots,e_{n+1})
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that
p=e_{n+1}
(the “north pole”). Instead of
\mathbf{M}^{d}_{-1}
we prefer to write
\mathbb{H}^{d}
if only the hyperbolic space is considered. The Beltrami–Klein model (sometimes also called projective ball model), based on the open Euclidean unit ball
{\sf B}^{d}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, will be a useful model space for the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{d}
. For this model space the choice
p=o
is convenient. For more specific information on the Beltrami–Klein model, we refer to
[
44
, Chapter 6]
.

Recall that for
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}

\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)
denotes the space of
k
-dimensional totally geodesic submanifolds of
\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa}
, which we call
k
-geodesics or
k
-flats, for short. We write
\GOp_{\kappa}(d,k)
for the space of those elements of
\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)
that pass through the previously fixed origin
p
of
\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa}
. In particular, we write
\AOp_{h}(d,k)
for
\AOp_{-1}(d,k)
and
\GOp_{h}(d,k)
for
\GOp_{-1}(d,k)
when we are working in the hyperbolic space only. In the model space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of
\mathbf{M}_{0}^{d}
the
k
-flats are
k
-dimensional affine subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In the model space
\mathbb{S}^{d}
of
\mathbf{M}_{1}^{d}
, the
k
-flats are
k
-dimensional great subspheres of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, which arise as intersections of the
d
-dimensional unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with
(k+1)
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, that is elements of
\GOp_{0}(d+1,k+1)
. In the Beltrami–Klein model for
\mathbf{M}_{-1}^{d}
, the
k
-flats are the non-empty intersections of
k
-dimensional affine subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the
d
-dimensional open unit ball
{\sf B}^{d}
.

Since the isometry group
I(\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa})
of
\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa}
is unimodular (for
\kappa=-1
, see
[
2
, Proposition C.4.11]
or
[
23
, Chapter X, Proposition 1.4]
together with the fact that
I(\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa})
is semi-simple as a Lie group) and
\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)
is a homogeneous
I(\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa})
-space, there exists an
I(\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa})
-invariant measure on
\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)
, which is unique up to a constant factor. We write
\mu_{k,\kappa}
for this measure and use the abbreviation
\mu_{k}
if
\kappa=-1
. The normalization of
\mu_{k,0}
is chosen as in
[
49
]
and the normalization (and parametrization) of
\mu_{k}
will be as in
[
26
, Equation (6)]
. More precisely, if we denote by
d_{h}
the intrinsic metric of
\mathbb{H}^{d}
, then

\displaystyle\mu_{k}(B)=\int_{\GOp_{h}(d,d-k)}\int_{L}\cosh^{k}d_{h}(x,p)\,\indi\{H(L,x)\in B\}\ \mathcal{H}^{d-k}(\textup{d}x)\,\nu_{d-k,h}(\textup{d}L)

(2.1)

for a Borel set
B\subset\AOp_{h}(d,k)
, where
H(L,x)
denotes the
k
-flat passing through
x\in L
that is orthogonal to
L
at
x
,
\nu_{d-k,h}
is the Borel probability measure on the space
\GOp_{h}(d,d-k)
, which is invariant under all isometries that fix the origin
p
, and
\mathcal{H}^{d-k}=\mathcal{H}_{-1}^{d-k}
stands for the Hausdorff measure on
L
induced by the hyperbolic distance (see also the discussion below). If
k=0
, then (
2.1
) specializes to
\mu_{0}=\mathcal{H}^{d}
. Since
\AOp_{1}(d,k)
is a compact space, the measure
\mu_{k,1}
is often normalized as a probability measure. Instead we choose the normalization so that
\mu_{k,1}(\AOp_{1}(d,k))=\omega_{d+1}/\omega_{k+1}
. These choices ensure that the Crofton formula
[
26
, Lemma 2]
(see also
[
9
]
) holds in all three space forms with the same constants, provided that the normalization of the Hausdorff measures is chosen in a natural way. Namely, Hausdorff measures
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\kappa}
, for
s\geq 0
, are defined (in each case) with respect to the underlying Riemannian metric (or the intrinsic metric)
d_{\kappa}
and for
s=d
they yield the natural volume measure on
\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}
. If a Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\kappa}
is applied on a
k
-flat
E
(with the induced Riemannian metric), we do not indicate
E
in our notation (in particular if
E
is clear from the context), since we always have
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\kappa}=\mathcal{H}^{k}_{E,\kappa}
, where
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{E,\kappa}
denotes the respective Hausdorff measure within
E
.

We are now prepared to present the Crofton formula for the standard spaces
\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}
. For the notion of Hausdorff rectifiability we refer to
[
26
, Lemma 9, Remark 8]
(and the literature cited there) and remark that, for example, all compact (geodesically) convex sets having the appropriate dimension satisfy this property.

Lemma 2.1

(Crofton formula)
.

Let
0\leq i\leq k\leq d-1
, and let
W\subset\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}
with
\kappa\in\{-1,0,1\}
be a Borel set which is Hausdorff
(d+i-k)
-rectifiable. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)}\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{i}(W\cap E)\,\mu_{k,\kappa}(\textup{d}E)=\frac{\omega_{d+1}\omega_{i+1}}{\omega_{k+1}\omega_{d-k+i+1}}\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{d+i-k}(W).

(2.2)

2.2
Representation as Poisson U-statistics

Let
\eta
be a Poisson process on a measurable space
\mathbb{X}
with a non-atomic intensity measure. We then call a functional
F=F(\eta)
a Poisson U-statistic of order
m\in\mathbb{N}
if
F
can be represented as

\displaystyle F(\eta)=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\eta_{\neq}^{m}}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}),

with some measurable function
f:\mathbb{X}^{m}\to[0,\infty]
, which is symmetric in its arguments and where
\eta_{\neq}^{m}
denotes the set of all
m
-tuples of distinct points in the support of
\eta
. We call
f
a kernel function for
F
. Poisson U-statistics have a variety of applications in stochastic geometry and we refer to
[
35
,
36
,
45
]
for further background material.

We now fix a Borel set
W\subset\mathbf{M}_{\kappa}^{d}
and consider the Poisson U-statistic
F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}
of order
m
on the space
\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)
with kernel
f_{\kappa}:\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)^{m}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
given by

f_{\kappa}(E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}):=\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{d-m(d-k)}(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{m}\cap W)\,\indi\{\text{dim}(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{m})=d-m(d-k)\},

while the underlying Poisson process
\eta_{t,\kappa}
on
\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)
has intensity measure
t\mu_{k,\kappa}
.
The Poisson U-statistic
F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}
admits the Wiener–Itô chaos decomposition

\displaystyle{F}_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}=\mathbb{E}[{F}_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}]+I_{1}(f_{W,t,\kappa,1}^{(m)})+\ldots+I_{m}(f_{W,t,\kappa,m}^{(m)})

(2.3)

with functions
f_{W,t,\kappa,i}^{(m)}:\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)^{i}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, given by

\displaystyle f_{W,t,\kappa,i}^{(m)}(E_{1},\ldots,E_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad=\binom{m}{i}\frac{t^{m-i}}{m!}\int_{\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)^{m-i}}\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{d-m(d-k)}(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{i}\cap E_{i+1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{m}\cap W)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\indi\{\text{dim}(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{m})=d-m(d-k)\}\ \mu_{k,\kappa}^{m-i}(\textup{d}(E_{i+1},\ldots,E_{m})),

(2.4)

where
I_{i}(\,\cdot\,)
stands for the Wiener–Itô integral with respect to the compensated Poisson process
\eta_{t,\kappa}-\mu_{k,\kappa}
, see
[
35
, Chapter 12]
for further details. Note that for
\kappa=-1
and
t=1
the choice
W=B_{r,-1}^{d}=:B^{d}_{r}
yields

F_{r}^{(m)}=F_{B^{d}_{r},1,-1}^{(m)}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{F}^{(m)}_{r}=\frac{F_{r}^{(m)}-\mathbb{E}F_{r}^{(m)}}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}F_{r}^{(m)}}},

(2.5)

for the functionals
F_{r}^{(m)}
and
\widetilde{F}_{r}^{(m)}
involved in Theorem
1.2
.

The following lemma (roughly speaking) shows that
generically
the indicator on the right-hand side of (
2.2
) is one if
\kappa=-1
and the intersection of the
k
-flats
E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}
is non-empty, while for
\kappa\in\{0,1\}
this is always the case. For convenience, we assign to the empty set the dimension
-1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\kappa\in\{-1,0,1\}
. Let
d\geq 2
,
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}
and
r\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{d}{d-k}\rfloor\}
. Then, for
\mu_{k,\kappa}^{r}
-almost all
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{r})\in\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)^{r}
,

\operatorname{dim}(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{r})\begin{cases}\in\{-1,d-r(d-k)\},&\text{if }\kappa=-1,\\
=d-r(d-k),&\text{if }\kappa\in\{0,1\}.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
k=0
, then
r=1
,
E_{1}
is a point and the assertion holds with
\operatorname{dim}(E_{1})=0
for each
\kappa\in\{-1,0,1\}
. We can thus assume that
k\geq 1
. We first consider the case
\kappa=1
. With
E_{i}\in\AOp_{1}(d,k)
we associate the linear subspace
U_{i}\in\GOp_{0}(d+1,k+1)
spanned by
E_{i}
. Then,
[
49
, Lemma 13.2.1]
(or
[
48
, Lemma 4.4.1]
) and an induction argument yield that

\operatorname{dim}(U_{1}\cap\ldots\cap U_{r})=(k+1)r-(r-1)(d+1)=d-r(d-k)+1

for almost all
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})\in\GOp_{0}(d+1,k+1)^{r}
with respect to the
r
-fold product measure of the Haar measure on
\GOp_{0}(d+1,k+1)
. Hence the assertion follows from the fact that
E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{r}=U_{1}\cap\ldots\cap U_{r}\cap\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

For
\kappa=-1
and
k=d-1
, the assertion has been proven in
[
26
]
. We now extend the argument to the remaining cases
k\in\{1,\ldots,d-2\}
. For each
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
we obtain a “random uniform”
k
-flat
E_{j}
as the intersection of
d-k
“independent random uniform” hyperplanes
H^{(j)}_{1},\ldots,H^{(j)}_{d-k}\in\AOp_{h}(d,d-1)
, that is, for
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
there are hyperplanes
H^{(j)}_{1},\ldots,H^{(j)}_{d-k}
(all “independent”) such that

E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{r}=\bigcap_{j=1}^{r}(H^{(j)}_{1}\cap\ldots\cap H^{(j)}_{d-k}).

More explicitly, by an
r
-fold application of
[
26
, Lemma 4]
we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\AOp_{h}(d,k)^{r}}\indi\{\operatorname{dim}(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{r})\notin\{-1,d-r(d-k)\}\}\,\mu_{k}^{r}(\textup{d}(E_{1},\ldots,E_{r}))

\displaystyle=c(d,k)^{-r}\int_{\AOp_{h}(d,d-1)^{r(d-k)}}\indi\Big\{\operatorname{dim}(\bigcap_{j=1}^{r}(H^{(j)}_{1}\cap\ldots\cap H^{(j)}_{d-k}))\notin\{-1,d-r(d-k)\}\Big\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\mu_{d-1}^{r(d-k)}(\textup{d}(H_{1}^{(1)},\ldots,H_{d-k}^{(r)})),

(2.6)

where

c(d,k)=\frac{\omega_{k+1}}{\omega_{d+1}}\Big(\frac{\omega_{d+1}}{\omega_{d}}\Big)^{d-k}.

It follows from
[
26
, Lemma 3]
that the right-hand side of (
2.2
) vanishes, which implies that the integrand on the left side must vanish as well, for
\mu_{k}^{r}
-almost all
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{r})\in\AOp_{h}(d,k)^{r}
. This proves the claim.

Finally, for
\kappa=0
the proof (first for
k=d-1
, but then for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
) essentially follows in the same way as in the case
\kappa=-1
. We only have to observe that if
\operatorname{dim}(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{r})\neq d-r(d-k)
, then by basic facts of linear algebra we have
\operatorname{dim}(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{r})>d-r(d-k)
, in particular
E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{r}\neq\varnothing
.
∎

The next lemma extends Lemma 4 in
[
26
]
. We write
\AOp^{\ast}_{\kappa}(d,k)^{r}
for the set of all
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{r})\in\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)^{r}
with
E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{r}\neq\varnothing
if
\kappa=-1
and set
\AOp^{\ast}_{\kappa}(d,k)^{r}:=\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)^{r}
if
\kappa\in\{0,1\}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\kappa\in\{-1,0,1\}
. Let
d\geq 2
,
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}
and
r\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{d}{d-k}\rfloor\}
. If

f:\AOp^{\ast}_{\kappa}(d,k)^{r}\to\mathbb{R}
is a nonnegative, measurable function, then

\displaystyle\int_{\AOp^{\ast}_{\kappa}(d,k)^{r}}f(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{r})\,\mu^{r}_{k,\kappa}(\textup{d}(E_{1},\ldots,E_{r}))

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\omega_{d-r(d-k)+1}}{\omega_{d+1}}\left(\frac{\omega_{d+1}}{\omega_{k+1}}\right)^{r}\int_{\AOp_{\kappa}(d,d-r(d-k))}f(E)\,\mu_{d-r(d-k),\kappa}(\textup{d}E).

Proof.

In the proof, we can proceed as in the proof of Lemma 4 in
[
26
]
. We first observe that both sides of the asserted equation define isometry invariant Haar measures. Then we apply Lemma
2.1

r+1
times to see that the constant is chosen correctly.

Alternatively, observing first that Lemma 4 in
[
26
]
holds for
\kappa\in\{-1,0,1\}
, we can apply this lemma
r+1
times to obtain the assertion. Proceeding in this way, the constant on the right side is

\frac{c(d,d-r(d-k))}{c(d,k)^{r}},

which equals the constant given in the statement of the lemma.
∎

A consequence of the representation for
F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}
as given in (
2.3
), is the following exact expression for its variance in terms of the functions
f_{W,t,\kappa,i}^{(m)}
. From
[
35
, Proposition 12.12]

we obtain

\operatorname{Var}F_{W,t,\kappa}^{(m)}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}i!\,t^{2m-i}A_{W,\kappa,i}^{(m)},

(2.7)

where
A_{W,\kappa,i}^{(m)}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
is given by

\begin{split}A_{W,\kappa,i}^{(m)}:=&\binom{m}{i}^{2}\int_{\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)^{i}}\Bigg(\frac{1}{m!}\int_{\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)^{m-i}}\ \mathcal{H}^{d-m(d-k)}_{\kappa}(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{i}\cap E_{i+1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{m}\cap W)\\
&\times\indi\{\text{dim}(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{m})=d-m(d-k)\}\,\mu_{k,\kappa}^{m-i}(\textup{d}(E_{i+1},\ldots,E_{m}))\Bigg)^{2}\\
&\times\mu_{k,\kappa}^{i}(\textup{d}(E_{1},\ldots,E_{i})).\end{split}

Note that in this formula we can restrict the integration to
m
-tuples of flats with non-empty intersections. Then Lemma
2.2
shows that the indicator function can be replaced by
1
. Moreover,
\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)^{m-i}
can be replaced by
\AOp_{\kappa}^{\ast}(d,k)^{m-i}
and
\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)^{i}
by
\AOp_{\kappa}^{\ast}(d,k)^{i}
. Then, for
\mu_{k,\kappa}^{i}
-almost all
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{i})\in\AOp_{\kappa}^{\ast}(d,k)^{i}
, we have
\operatorname{dim}(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{i})=d-i(d-k)
. Applying first Lemma
2.3
to the integrations with respect to
(E_{i+1},\ldots,E_{m})
and to
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{i})
, and then Lemma
2.1
with respect to
F=E_{i+1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{m}
and the
(d-i(d-k))
-rectifiable set
E\cap W=E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{i}\cap W
, we obtain

\displaystyle A_{W,\kappa,i}^{(m)}

\displaystyle=\binom{m}{i}^{2}\frac{1}{(m!)^{2}}\frac{c(d,d-(m-i)(d-k))^{2}}{c(d,k)^{2(m-i)}}\frac{c(d,d-i(d-k))}{c(d,k)^{i}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{\AOp_{\kappa}(d,d-i(d-k))}\left(\int_{\AOp_{\kappa}(d,d-(m-i)(d-k))}\mathcal{H}^{d-m(d-k)}_{\kappa}(E\cap W\cap F)\,\mu_{d-(m-i)(d-k),\kappa}(\textup{d}F)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mu_{d-i(d-k),\kappa}(\textup{d}E)

\displaystyle=\binom{m}{i}^{2}\frac{1}{(m!)^{2}}\frac{c(d,d-(m-i)(d-k))^{2}c(d,d-i(d-k))}{c(d,k)^{2(m-i)}c(d,k)^{i}}\left(\frac{\omega_{d+1}\omega_{d-m(d-k)+1}}{\omega_{d-(m-i)(d-k)+1}\omega_{d-i(d-k)+1}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{\AOp_{\kappa}(d,d-i(d-k))}\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{d-i(d-k)}(E\cap W)^{2}\,\mu_{d-i(d-k),\kappa}(\textup{d}E).

After simplification of the constants, we finally get

\displaystyle A_{W,\kappa,i}^{(m)}=

\displaystyle\binom{m}{i}^{2}\frac{1}{(m!)^{2}}\Big(\frac{\omega_{d+1}}{\omega_{k+1}}\Big)^{2m-i}\frac{\omega_{d-m(d-k)+1}^{2}}{\omega_{d+1}\omega_{d-i(d-k)+1}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\AOp_{\kappa}(d,d-i(d-k))}\ \mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{d-i(d-k)}(E\cap W)^{2}\ \mu_{d-i(d-k),\kappa}(\textup{d}E)

(2.8)

for
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.

The same reasoning also shows that (
2.2
) simplifies to

f^{(m)}_{W,t,\kappa,i}(E_{1},\ldots,E_{i})=\binom{m}{i}\frac{t^{m-i}}{m!}\left(\frac{\omega_{d+1}}{\omega_{k+1}}\right)^{m-i}\frac{\omega_{d-m(d-k)+1}}{\omega_{d-i(d-k)+1}}\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}^{d-i(d-k)}(E_{1}\cap\ldots\cap E_{i}\cap W)

(2.9)

for
\mu_{k,\kappa}^{i}
-almost all
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{i})\in\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)^{i}
. Thus we have

t^{2m-i}A_{W,\kappa,i}^{(m)}=\int_{\AOp_{\kappa}(d,k)^{i}}f^{(m)}_{W,t,\kappa,i}(E_{1},\ldots,E_{i})^{2}\,(t\mu_{k,\kappa})^{i}(\textup{d}(E_{1},\ldots,E_{i})),

which is consistent with (
2.3
) and the isometry property of the Wiener–Itô integrals.

2.3
Integral asymptotics in the hyperbolic case

In this paper we already have and below will further encounter integral expressions of the form

\int_{\AOp_{h}(d,k)}\mathcal{H}^{k}(E\cap B_{r}^{d})^{\ell}\,\mu_{k}(\textup{d}E),

(2.10)

where
B_{r}^{d}=B_{r,-1}^{d}
is a
d
-dimensional hyperbolic ball of radius
r>0
,
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that in this section we restrict ourselves to the case of the hyperbolic space with
\kappa=-1
and use the notation introduced in Section
2.1
. In particular, we will have to deal with the asymptotics of integrals of the form (
2.10
), as
r\to\infty
. Such an asymptotic analysis was already carried out in
[
26
, Lemma 16]
and we recall the result here for completeness and in order to keep this paper self-contained.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
r\geq 1
and
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}
. For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
there exist constants
c,C>0
, depending only on
k,\ell
and
d
, such that

\displaystyle c\,g(k,\ell,d,r)\

\displaystyle\leq\ \int_{\AOp_{h}(d,k)}\mathcal{H}^{k}(E\cap B_{r}^{d})^{\ell}\,\mu_{k}(\textup{d}E)\ \leq\ C\,g(k,\ell,d,r)

with

\displaystyle g(k,\ell,d,r)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}e^{r(d-1)}&:\,\ell(k-1)<d-1,\\
re^{r(d-1)}&:\,\ell(k-1)=d-1,\\
e^{\ell r(k-1)}&:\,\ell(k-1)>d-1.\end{cases}

The previous lemma allows us to obtain the following asymptotic result for the quantities

A_{r,i}^{(m)}:=A_{B_{r}^{d},-1,i}^{(m)}

(2.11)

for which we derived in (
2.8
) a simplified representation in terms of an integral of the form (
2.10
) with
\ell=2
.

In order to improve the readability of the subsequent arguments and results, we introduce the following notations:
Let
\mathbb{X}
be a set. We write
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
(
f(x)\gtrsim g(x)
) for functions
f,g:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
f(x)\leq Cg(x)
(
f(x)\geq Cg(x)
) for all
x\in\mathbb{X}
and
f(x)\asymp g(x)
if there exist constants
c,C>0
such that
c\ f(x)\leq g(x)\leq Cf(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{X}
. For a family of functions depending on some parameter
r
we write
f_{r}(x)\sim f(x)
if
f_{r}(x)/f(x)\rightarrow 1
as
r\rightarrow\infty
for each
x\in\mathbb{X}
. If we work in a
d
-dimensional standard space of constant curvature
\mathbf{M}^{d}_{\kappa}
,
\kappa\in\{-1,0,1\}
, the occurring constants may depend on the dimension
d
(any dependence on other parameters can be subsumed under the dependence on
d
).

The following result deals again with the hyperbolic case.

Lemma 2.5
.

For all
r\geq 1
,
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
,

\displaystyle A_{r,i}^{(m)}\

\displaystyle\asymp\begin{cases}e^{r(d-1)}&:\,2i(d-k)>d-1,\\
re^{r(d-1)}&:\,2i(d-k)=d-1,\\
e^{2r(d-i(d-k)-1)}&:\,2i(d-k)<d-1.\end{cases}

In particular, if
2k<d+1
, then

A_{r,i}^{(m)}\asymp e^{r(d-1)}\quad\text{for }i\in\{1,\ldots,m\},

(2.12)

and if
2k=d+1
, then

A_{r,1}^{(m)}\asymp re^{r(d-1)}\quad\text{ and }\quad A_{r,i}^{(m)}\asymp e^{r(d-1)}\quad\text{for }i\in\{2,\ldots,m\}.

(2.13)

Proof.

Following Lemma
2.4
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\AOp_{h}(d,d-i(d-k))}\ \mathcal{H}^{d-i(d-k)}(E\cap B_{r}^{d})^{2}\ \mu_{d-i(d-k)}(\textup{d}E)\asymp g(d-i(d-k),2,d,r),

so that

\displaystyle A_{r,i}^{(m)}\ \asymp\begin{cases}e^{r(d-1)}&:\,2(d-i(d-k)-1)<d-1,\\

if and only if

is equivalent to

and
2(d-i(d-k)-1)=d-1

, then we have

, and if
2k=d+1
, then

, yielding (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), respectively.
∎

3.1
Asymptotic variance

A crucial ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.2
is an asymptotic analysis of the variance of the random variables

. It turns out that the precise growth of

depends on the dimension parameter

relative to the space dimension

, as the following result shows.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
F_{r}^{(m)}
be the random variable defined at (

), for an underlying Poisson process on

with

and intensity

. Then, as

,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}F_{r}^{(m)}\asymp\begin{cases}e^{r(d-1)}&:\,2k<d+1,\\
re^{r(d-1)}&:\,2k=d+1,\\
e^{2r(k-1)}&:\,2k>d+1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Recalling (
2.7
), (
2.8
) and (
2.11
) we need to determine the order of
A_{r,i}^{(m)}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. We have to distinguish three cases. For
2k<d+1
the claim directly follows from (
2.12
) and for
2k={d+1}
from (
2.13
), respectively. If
2k>d+1
, then
d-1>2(d-k)
and it follows from Lemma
2.5
that the term
A_{r,1}^{(m)}
is of the order
e^{2r(k-1)}
. Moreover, since
2(k-1)>d-1
the term
A_{r,i}^{(m)}
is of lower order for

.
∎

We start with some preparations. For

and

with

for the distance of

from

\mathcal{H}^{k}(E\cap B^{d}_{r})=f_{r}\circ d_{h}(E),\qquad E\in\AOp_{h}(d,k).

Let
\eta
denote a hyperbolic
k

\mathbb{H}^{d}
with intensity measure
\mu_{k}
with
\mu_{k}
as introduced in Section
2.1
. For the image measure
{d_{h}}_{\sharp}\mu_{k}
of
\mu_{k}
under

, we get

{d_{h}}_{\sharp}\mu_{k}=\omega_{d-k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\indi\{s\in\cdot\}\,\cosh^{k}s\,\sinh^{d-k-1}s\,\textup{d}s,

see
[
13
, Sections 3.4-5]
for the required transformation in hyperbolic space. Note that since

F_{r}=\int f_{r}\circ d_{h}(E)\,\eta(\textup{d}E),

and since the characteristic function of

), we obtain for

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{\mathrm{i}\xi F_{r}}]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\int_{\AOp_{h}(d,k)}(e^{\mathrm{i}\xi(f_{r}\circ d_{h})(E)}-1)\,\mu_{k}(\textup{d}E)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{r}(e^{\mathrm{i}\xi f_{r}(s)}-1)\,({d_{h}}_{\sharp}\mu_{k})(\textup{d}s)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\omega_{d-k}\int_{0}^{r}(e^{\mathrm{i}\xi f_{r}(s)}-1)\cosh^{k}s\,\sinh^{d-k-1}s\,\textup{d}s\right).

The following lemma determines the asymptotic behaviour of

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
s\in[0,\infty)
. Then
g_{r}(s)\sim g(s):=\frac{\omega_{k}}{(k-1)2^{k-1}}\cosh^{-(k-1)}s
, as

Proof.

According to
[
44
, Theorem 3.5.3]
it holds that

\displaystyle g_{r}(s)=e^{-r(k-1)}\omega_{k}\int_{0}^{\arccosh(\frac{\cosh r}{\cosh s})}\sinh^{k-1}u\,\textup{d}u.

(4.1)

Denoting by
o(1)
a sequence which converges to zero as

, we have

\displaystyle\arccosh z=\log(z+\sqrt{z^{2}-1})=\log(2z)+o(1),\quad\text{ as }z\rightarrow\infty.

Thus, for any fixed

, we obtain

\displaystyle\arccosh\Big(\frac{\cosh r}{\cosh s}\Big)=\log(2\cosh r)-\log(\cosh s)+o(1)=r-\log(\cosh s)+o(1).

Next we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{z}\sinh^{k-1}u\,\textup{d}u\sim\frac{e^{(k-1)z}}{(k-1)2^{k-1}},\quad\text{ as }z\rightarrow\infty.

Combining this relation for

\displaystyle g_{r}(s)

\displaystyle\sim e^{-r(k-1)}\frac{\omega_{k}}{(k-1)2^{k-1}}e^{(k-1)(r-\log(\cosh s)+o(1))}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{k}}{(k-1)2^{k-1}}e^{(k-1)(-\log(\cosh s)+o(1))}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\omega_{k}}{(k-1)2^{k-1}}\cosh^{-(k-1)}s,

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

In order to conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\psi_{r}(\xi)=\psi(\xi):=\exp\left(\omega_{d-k}\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{\mathrm{i}\xi g(s)}-1-\mathrm{i}\xi g(s))\cosh^{k}s\,\sinh^{d-k-1}s\,\textup{d}s\right)

(e^{\mathrm{i}\xi g_{r}(s)}-1-\mathrm{i}\xi g_{r}(s))\cosh^{k}s\,\sinh^{d-k-1}s

\displaystyle|e^{\mathrm{i}\xi g_{r}(s)}-1-\mathrm{i}\xi g_{r}(s)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\xi^{2}g_{r}(s)^{2},

(see, e.g.,
[

Using in addition that

\displaystyle|\big(e^{\mathrm{i}\xi g_{r}(s)}-1-\mathrm{i}\xi g_{r}(s)\big)\cosh^{k}s\,\sinh^{d-k-1}s|\leq\frac{1}{2}\xi^{2}g_{r}(s)^{2}e^{s(d-1)}.

Furthermore,
[
26

\displaystyle|\big(e^{\mathrm{i}\xi g_{r}(s)}-1-\mathrm{i}\xi g_{r}(s)\big)\cosh^{k}s\,\sinh^{d-k-1}s|\leq\frac{1}{2}\xi^{2}\frac{\omega_{k}^{2}}{(k-1)^{2}}e^{-s(2k-(d+1))}.

s\geq 0
, independent of

converge in distribution to a random variable

with characteristic function

.

As in the introduction, for

define the random variable

Y_{T}:=\sum_{s\in\zeta\cap[0,T]}h(s)

with

,

.
Using once again

\psi_{Y_{T}}(\xi)=\exp\Big(\omega_{d-k}\int_{0}^{T}(e^{\mathrm{i}\xi h(s)}-1)\cosh^{k}s\sinh^{d-k-1}s\,\textup{d}s\Big),\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

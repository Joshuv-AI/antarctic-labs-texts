Mean-field backward stochastic differential equations and nonlocal PDEs with quadratic growth
Tao Hao, Ying Hu, Shanjian Tang, Jiaqiang Wen

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we study general mean-field backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs, for short) with quadratic growth. First, the existence and uniqueness of local and global solutions are proved with some new ideas for a one-dimensional mean-field BSDE when the generator
g\big(t,Y,Z,\mathbb{P}_{Y},\mathbb{P}_{Z}\big)
has a quadratic growth in
Z
and the terminal value is bounded. Second, a comparison theorem for the general mean-field BSDEs is obtained with the Girsanov transform. Third, we prove the convergence of the particle systems to the mean-field BSDEs with quadratic growth, and the convergence rate is also given. Finally, in this framework, we use the mean-field BSDE to provide a probabilistic representation for the viscosity solution of a nonlocal partial differential equation (PDE, for short) as an extended nonlinear Feynman-Kac formula, which yields the existence and uniqueness of the solution to the PDE.

Key words:
Backward stochastic differential equation, mean-field, quadratic growth, partial differential equation, McKean-Vlasov equation.

AMS subject classifications.
60H10, 60H30

Introduction

Mean-field stochastic differential equations (SDEs, for short), also called McKean-Vlasov equations, can be traced back to the work of Kac
[
25
]
in the 1950s.
Recently, inspired by particle systems, the mean-field backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs, for short) were introduced by Buckdahn, Djehiche, Li, and Peng
[
8
]
and Buckdahn, Li, and Peng
[
9
]
. Since then, mean-field BSDEs and the related nonlocal partial differential equations (PDEs, for short) have received an intensive attention.
However, up to now, most works on mean-field BSDEs assume the linear growth of the generators, which essentially hinders the theory’s further development and application. This paper will comprehensively study mean-field BSDEs with quadratic growth, including the existence and uniqueness result, the comparison theorem, the particle systems, and their applications to PDEs.
To be more precise, we describe the problem in detail.

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete filtered probability space on which a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion
\{W_{t}\;;0\leqslant t<\infty\}
is defined, where
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
is the natural filtration of
W
augmented by all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets in
\mathscr{F}
. For a finite time
T>0
, consider the following general mean-field BSDE:

Y_{t}=\eta+\int_{t}^{T}g\big(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}},\mathbb{P}_{Z_{s}}\big)ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad t\in[0,T],

(1.1)

where the random variable
\eta
is called the
terminal value
and the coefficient
g
is called the
generator
.
The unknown processes, called an adapted solution of (
1.1
), are the pair
(Y,Z)
of
\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes, with

\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}}
and
\mathbb{P}_{Z_{s}}
being the laws (or distributions) of
Y_{s}
and
Z_{s}
, respectively.
Here, by the word “general”, we mean that the generator
g
depends on the marginal distributions of the unknown processes rather than merely their expectations.
In what follows, BSDE (
1.1
) is called a
quadratic
mean-field BSDE or a mean-field BSDE
with quadratic growth
if the generator
g
has a quadratic growth in the second unknown argument
Z
, and the terminal value
\eta
is called
bounded
if it is essentially bounded.

When the marginal laws of both unknown processes
Y
and
Z
appear merely via their expectations, the mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) was studied by Buckdahn, Djehiche, Li, and Peng
[
8
]
and Buckdahn, Li, and Peng
[
9
]
, where the existence, uniqueness, a comparison theorem, and the relation with a nonlocal PDE are given when the coefficients are uniformly Lipschitz continuous.
Recently, the derivative of a functional
\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with respect to the measure argument is introduced by Lions
[
32
]
at
Collège de France
. Since then, this definition has been adopted in many works.
For instance, Chassagneux, Crisan, and Delarue
[
14
]
(see also Carmona and Delarue
[
12
]
) studied the general mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) coupled with a McKean-Vlasov forward equation, and proved that this class of equations admit unique adapted solutions under uniformly Lipschitz continuous coefficients. Buckdahn, Li, Peng, and Rainer
[
10
]
studied the general forward mean-field stochastic differential equations and the associated PDEs. Li
[
31
]
studied the general mean-field forward-backward SDEs with jumps and associated nonlocal quasi-linear integral-PDEs.
Besides, for the applications of the mean-field framework in stochastic control problems,
Yong
[
39
]
studied a linear-quadratic optimal control problem of mean-field SDEs,
and Buckdahn, Chen, and Li
[
7
]
studied the partial derivative with respect to the measure and its application to general controlled mean-field systems.

On the one hand, when the generator
g
is independent of
(\mathbb{P}_{Y},\mathbb{P}_{Z})
, the general mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) is reduced to the following BSDE:

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\eta+\int_{t}^{T}g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad~0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

(1.2)

which were introduced by Pardoux and Peng
[
33
]
, where the existence and uniqueness were obtained for the case of uniformly Lipschitz continuous coefficients.
From then on, BSDEs have received an extensive attention in various fields of
partial differential equations (see Pardoux and Peng
[
34
]
), mathematical finance (see El Karoui, Peng, and Quenez
[
17
]
), and stochastic optimal control (see Yong and Zhou
[
40
]
), to mention a few.
At the same time, due to various applications as well as the list of open problems proposed by Peng
[
36
]
, many efforts have been made to relax the existing conditions on the generator
g
of BSDE (
1.2
) for the existence and/or uniqueness of adapted solutions.
For instance, Lepeltier and San Martin
[
29
]
obtained the existence of adapted solutions to BSDEs when the generator
g
is continuous and of linear growth in
(Y,Z)
.
In 2000, Kobylanski
[
26
]
proved the existence and uniqueness result on adapted solution of a one-dimensional BSDE (
1.2
) when the generator
g
is of quadratic growth in
Z
and the terminal value
\eta
is bounded.
Along this way, the existence and uniqueness result on BSDEs (
1.2
) with quadratic growth, in the one-dimensional situation with unbounded terminal values, was obtained by Briand and Hu
[
5
,
6
]
and Bahlali, Eddahbi, and Ouknine
[
1
]
;
the multi-dimensional situation with bounded terminal values was studied by Hu and Tang
[
24
]
and Xing and Zitkovic
[
38
]
;
and the multi-dimensional situation with unbounded terminal values was investigated by Fan, Hu and Tang
[
18
]
, under various conditions and with different methods.
Some other recent developments concerning the quadratic BSDEs can be found in
Barrieu and El Karoui
[
2
]
, Delbaen, Hu, and Bao
[
16
]
, Fan, Hu, and Tang
[
19
]
, Hu, Li, and Wen
[
23
]
, and so on.

On the other hand, the last two decades, stimulated by the broad applications and the open problem proposed by Peng
[
36
]
, also see a lot of efforts at relaxing the conditions on the generator
g
of the mean-field BSDEs (
1.1
).
When the generator
g
depends on the expectation of the unknown processes
(Y,Z)
,
Cheridito and Nam
[
15
]
discussed the existence of adapted solutions to a class of mean-field BSDEs with quadratic growth;
Hibon, Hu, and Tang
[
22
]
studied the existence and uniqueness of adapted solutions to one-dimensional mean-field BSDEs with quadratic growth, and Hao, Wen, and Xiong
[
21
]
studied a class of multi-dimensional quadratic mean-field BSDEs with small terminal values.
However, to our best knowledge, there are few works concerning the quadratic mean-field BSDEs when the generator
g
depends on the laws of unknown processes
(Y,Z)
, let alone the particle systems.

In this paper, by introducing some new ideas, we study the one-dimensional quadratic mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) with bounded terminal values.
Firstly, we construct a local solution for the general mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) (see
Theorem 3.3
) by borrowing some ideas of Hu and Tang
[
24
]
and using the fixed-point principle. It should be noted that the generator
g
has a general growth with respect to
Y
(see
3.1
), and the choice of the radius of the centered ball is more straightforward compared to that of Hu and Tang
[
24
, Theorem 2.2]
.
Secondly, using different methods, we prove the existence and uniqueness of the global solutions of mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) under the following four cases:
(i) the generator
g
has a quadratic growth with respect to
Z
and is bounded with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{Z}
(see
Theorem 3.5
) ;
(ii) the generator
g
has a strictly quadratic growth with respect to
Z
and has a sub-quadratic growth with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{Z}
(see
Theorem 3.8
);
(iii) the generator
g
has a quadratic and sub-quadratic growth with respect to
Z
and
\mathbb{P}_{Z}
, respectively, with the growth coefficient of
g
in
\mathbb{P}_{Z}
being sufficiently small (see
Theorem 3.9
);
(iv) the generator
g(t,Y,Z,\mathbb{P}_{Y},\mathbb{P}_{Z}):=g_{1}(t,Z)+g_{2}(t,\mathbb{P}_{Z})
, where both functionals
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
have a quadratic growth in their last arguments (see
3.11
).
Thirdly, to receive a wider application, we give a comparison theorem for this class of BSDEs (see
Theorem 4.1
) with the Girsanov transform.
Fourthly, we consider the following system of
N
particles: for
t\in[0,T]
,

Y^{i}_{t}=\eta^{i}+\int_{t}^{T}\!\!\!g\Big(s,Y^{i}_{s},Z^{i,i}_{s},\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{Y^{i}_{s}},\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{Z^{i,i}_{s}}\Big)ds-\int_{t}^{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}Z^{i,j}_{s}dW^{j}_{s},

(1.3)

where
\delta
is the Dirac measure,
\{\eta^{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant N\}
are
N
independent copies of
\eta
, and

\{W^{j},1\leqslant j\leqslant N\}
are
N
independent
d
-dimensional Brownian motions.
With Lions’ idea and the law of large numbers, we prove that the mean-field limit of the
N
-particle system (
1.3
) converges to the mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) (see
Theorem 5.2
) when
N
tends to infinity. Moreover, we obtain the rate of convergence (see
Theorem 5.9
) when the generator
g
does not depend on the law of
Z
.
Finally, we use the mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) with quadratic growth to prove the existence and uniqueness of the viscosity solution of a nonlocal PDE, and thus extend the nonlinear Feynman-Kac formula of Buckdahn, Li, and Peng
[
9
]
with linear growth to the case of quadratic growth (see
Theorem 6.6
).
In our nonlinear Feynman-Kac formula, the generator
g
depends on the distribution only via the expectation of the state process
(Y,Z)
. Since Wasserstein space has no local compactness, it remains a challenging topic in the theory of viscosity solutions to allow the generator to depend on the distribution in a general way (see Wu and Zhang
[
37
]
).

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present some preliminary notations and results. In Section
3
, we prove the existence and uniqueness of the local and global solutions to the general mean-field BSDE (
1.1
). In Section
4
, a comparison theorem is proved.
We study the particle systems for mean-field BSDEs in Section
5
, where the convergence and its rate are given for the particle systems. In Section
6
, we provide the relationship between the solution of the mean-field BSDE with quadratic growth and the viscosity solution of the related nonlocal PDE. Section
7
concludes the results.

3
Existence and Uniqueness

In this section, we study the existence and uniqueness of an adapted solution to a mean-field BSDE (

) with quadratic growth. In particular, we consider the one-dimensional situation, i.e.,

. We shall study the local and global solutions and divide this section into two parts: Subsection

In the following, suppose that

\mathscr{F}_{t}
-progressively measurable process,

,

,

3.1
Local solution

In this subsection, we prove the existence and uniqueness of local solutions of mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) with quadratic growth.

Assumption 1
.

The terminal value
\eta:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and the generator
g:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

d\mathbb{P}\times dt
-a.e.
(\omega,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T]
, for
(y,z,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

|g(w,t,y,z,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})|\leqslant\theta_{t}(\omega)+\phi(|y|)+\frac{\gamma}{2}|z|^{2}+\phi_{0}\big({\cal W}_{2}(\mu_{1},\delta_{\{0\}})\big)+\gamma_{0}{\cal W}_{2}(\mu_{2},\delta_{\{0\}})^{1+\alpha}.

(ii)

d\mathbb{P}\times dt
-a.e.
(\omega,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T]
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}&|g(\omega,t,y,z,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})-g(\omega,t,\bar{y},\bar{z},\bar{\mu}_{1},\bar{\mu}_{2})|\\
&\leqslant\phi\Big(|y|\vee|\bar{y}|\vee{\cal W}_{2}(\mu_{1},\delta_{\{0\}})\vee{\cal W}_{2}(\bar{\mu}_{1},\delta_{\{0\}})\Big)\\
&\quad\times\bigg[\Big(1+|z|+|\bar{z}|+{\cal W}_{2}(\mu_{2},\delta_{\{0\}})+{\cal W}_{2}(\bar{\mu}_{2},\delta_{\{0\}})\Big)\Big(|y-\bar{y}|+|z-\bar{z}|+{\cal W}_{2}(\mu_{1},\bar{\mu}_{1})\Big)\\
&\qquad+\Big(1+{\cal W}_{2}(\mu_{2},\delta_{\{0\}})^{\alpha}+{\cal W}_{2}(\bar{\mu}_{2},\delta_{\{0\}})^{\alpha}\Big){\cal W}_{2}(\mu_{2},\bar{\mu}_{2})\bigg]\end{array}

for any

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that for any given processes
(P,Q)\in S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
the terminal value
\eta:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and the generator
g:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfy the conditions:

(i)

d\mathbb{P}\times dt
-a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

|g(\omega,t,z)|\leqslant\theta_{t}(\omega)+\phi(|P_{t}|)+\frac{\gamma}{2}|z|^{2}+\phi_{0}\big(\|P_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)+\gamma_{0}\|Q_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1+\alpha}.

(ii)

d\mathbb{P}\times dt
-a.e.
t\in[0,T],
for every
z,\bar{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

|g(\omega,t,z)-g(\omega,t,\bar{z})|\leqslant\phi\big(|P_{t}|\vee\|P_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)\cdot\big(1+|z|+|\bar{z}|+2\|Q_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)|z-\bar{z}|.

(iii)

For two positive constants
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
,

\|\eta\|_{\infty}\leqslant K_{1}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\theta_{t}\,dt\Bigr\|_{\infty}\leqslant K_{2}.

Then the following backward stochastic differential equation

Y_{t}=\eta+\int_{t}^{T}\!\!\!g(s,Z_{s})\,ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}\,dW_{s},\quad t\in[0,T],

admits a unique adapted solution
(Y,Z)\in S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, for any
t\in[0,T]
and stopping time
\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T]
, we have

\displaystyle|Y_{t}|\leqslant

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{\infty}+\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\theta_{s}\,ds\Bigr\|_{\infty}+(T-t)\widehat{\phi+\phi_{0}}^{t}+\gamma_{0}\|Q\|^{1+\alpha}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}(T-t)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}

(3.2)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{T}\!\!|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\Big]\leqslant\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\exp\big(2\gamma\|\eta\|_{\infty}\big)+\frac{2}{\gamma}\exp\big(2\gamma\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T)}\big)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\theta_{s}\,ds\Bigr\|_{\infty}+(T-t)\widehat{\phi+\phi_{0}}^{t}+\gamma_{0}\|Q\|^{1+\alpha}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}(T-t)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\bigg),

where

\widehat{\phi+\phi_{0}}^{t}:=(\phi+\phi_{0})(\|P\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T)}).

Proof.

Using Hölder’s inequality, we have that for
t\in[0,T]
,

\int_{t}^{T}(\mathbb{E}[|Q_{s}|^{2}])^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}ds\leqslant\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[|Q_{s}|^{2}]ds\Big)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}(T-t)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\leqslant\|Q\|^{1+\alpha}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}(T-t)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}.

Then, for a constant
\ell\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\exp\Big\{\ell\gamma\gamma_{0}\int_{t}^{T}\|Q_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1+\alpha}ds\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle=\exp\Big\{\ell\gamma\gamma_{0}\int_{t}^{T}\big(\mathbb{E}[|Q_{s}|^{2}]\big)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}ds\Big\}\leqslant\exp\Big\{\ell\gamma\gamma_{0}\|Q\|^{1+\alpha}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}(T-t)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\Big\}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\exp\bigg\{l\gamma|\eta|+\ell\gamma\int_{t}^{T}\!\!\Big[(\theta_{s}(\omega)+\phi(|P_{s}|)+\phi_{0}\big(\|P_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)+\gamma_{0}\|Q_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1+\alpha}\Big]ds\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp^{\ell\gamma}\left\{\|\eta\|_{\infty}+\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\theta_{s}\,ds\Bigr\|_{\infty}\!\!+(T-t)\widehat{\phi+\phi_{0}}^{t}+\gamma_{0}\|Q\|^{1+\alpha}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}(T-t)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\right\}<\infty.

In view of the conditions (i)-(iii),
we see from Briand and Hu
[
6
, Proposition 3 and Corollary 4]
that BSDE (
3.1
) admits a solution
(Y,Z)
such that

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds<\infty,

and

\displaystyle|Y_{t}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\eta\|_{\infty}+\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\theta_{s}\,ds\Bigr\|_{\infty}+(T-t)\widehat{\phi+\phi_{0}}^{t}+\gamma_{0}\|Q\|^{1+\alpha}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}(T-t)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}},

which implies that the inequality (
3.2
) holds and thus
Y\in S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
.

We now prove that
Z\cdot W
is a BMO martingale. Applying Itô-Tanaka’s formula to the term
\exp\big\{2\gamma|Y_{\tau}|\big\}
with
\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T]
, we have that

\displaystyle\exp\big\{2\gamma|Y_{\tau}|\big\}+2\gamma^{2}\int_{\tau}^{T}\!\!\!\exp\big\{2\gamma|Y_{s}|\big\}|Z_{s}|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\exp\big\{2\gamma|\eta|\big\}+2\gamma\int_{\tau}^{T}\!\!\!\exp\big\{2\gamma|Y_{s}|\big\}g(s,Z_{s})ds+2\gamma\int_{\tau}^{T}\!\!\!\exp\big\{2\gamma|Y_{s}|\big\}Z_{s}\,dW_{s}.

In view of the condition (i), we have

\displaystyle\exp\big\{2\gamma|Y_{\tau}|\big\}+2\gamma^{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{T}\!\!\!\exp\big\{2\gamma|Y_{s}|\big\}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\Big]\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\big[\exp\big\{2\gamma|\eta|\big\}\big]

\displaystyle+2\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\!\!\int_{\tau}^{T}\!\!\!\exp\big\{2\gamma|Y_{s}|\big\}\Big[\theta_{s}+\phi(|P_{s}|)+\frac{\gamma}{2}|Z_{s}|^{2}+\phi_{0}\big(\|P_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)+\gamma_{0}\|Q_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1+\alpha}\Big]ds.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\gamma^{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\!\!\int_{\tau}^{T}\!\!|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\leqslant\gamma^{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\!\!\int_{\tau}^{T}\!\!\!\exp\big\{2\gamma|Y_{s}|\big\}|Z_{s}|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\big\{2\gamma\|\eta\|_{\infty}\big\}+2\gamma\left(\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\theta_{s}\,ds\Bigr\|_{\infty}+(T-t)\widehat{\phi+\phi_{0}}^{t}+\gamma_{0}\|Q\|^{1+\alpha}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}(T-t)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left\{2\gamma\|Y\|_{S^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}\right\}.

Hence, we have inequality (
3.3
) and thereby
Z\in{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In other words,
Z\cdot W
is a BMO martingale.

Finally, we prove the uniqueness. In fact, in view of the condition (ii) with
(U,V)\in S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, similar to the proof of Hu and Tang
[
24
, Lemma 2.1]
, we can use the Girsanov transform to derive a comparison result on the solutions of BSDE (
3.1
), which yields the desired result.

Theorem 3.3

Under

3.2

Moreover, for any

\displaystyle|Y_{t}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\eta\|_{\infty}+\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{s}\,ds\Bigr\|_{\infty}+(T-t)\widehat{\phi+\phi_{0}}^{t}+\gamma_{0}\|Q\|^{1+\alpha}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}(T-t)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\int_{\tau}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\leqslant

\displaystyle\times\left(\ \Bigl\|\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\theta_{s}\,ds\Bigr\|_{\infty}+(T-t)\widehat{\phi+\phi_{0}}^{t}+\gamma_{0}\|Q\|^{1+\alpha}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}(T-t)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\right).

\displaystyle|I_{2,s}|\leqslant

or
K_{2}
in (

Consequently, using Hölder’s inequality, we estimate the second term in the right hand side of (

\displaystyle 2\phi(L_{1})^{2}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}(1+\|Q_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\alpha}+\|\bar{Q}_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\alpha})\|\Delta Q_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}ds\Big)^{2}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\phi(L_{1})^{2}\int_{t}^{T}||\Delta Q_{s}||^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}ds\cdot\int_{t}^{T}\Big(1+\|Q_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\alpha}+\|\bar{Q}_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\alpha}\Big)^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant 6\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}\phi(L_{1})^{2}(T^{\alpha}+2L_{2})\|\Delta Q\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)},

Now from (

\displaystyle|\Delta Y_{t}|^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}\int_{t}^{T}|\Delta Z_{s}|^{2}ds\leqslant

\displaystyle\ 32\varepsilon\phi\big(L_{1}\big)^{2}\big(T+4c_{2}L_{2}+2L_{2}\big)\|\Delta P\|^{2}_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T)}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\ +6\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}\phi\big(L_{1}\big)^{2}(T^{\alpha}+2L_{2})\|\Delta Q\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}.

Moreover, thanks to

and

\displaystyle\|\Delta Y\|^{2}_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(T-\varepsilon,T)}+c_{1}^{2}\|\Delta Z\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(T-\varepsilon,T)}

\displaystyle\leqslant 32\varepsilon\phi\big(L_{1}\big)^{2}\big(T+4c_{2}L_{2}+2L_{2}\big)\|\Delta P\|^{2}_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(T-\varepsilon,T)}

Under

Now, due to that

\displaystyle\Delta Y_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}\Delta Z_{s}d\widetilde{W}_{s}=\int_{t}^{T}\Big[g(s,Y_{s},\bar{Z}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}},\mathbb{P}_{Z_{s}})-g(s,\bar{Y}_{s},\bar{Z}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{\bar{Y}_{s}},\mathbb{P}_{\bar{Z}_{s}})\Big]ds,

Remark 3.6
.

Hibon, Hu, and Tang

For this, we let

be the local time of the process
Y
at time
0
. Denote

\Psi(t,x)\triangleq\exp\Big\{\gamma x+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\theta_{s}+\beta|Y_{s}|+\beta_{0}\|Y_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\gamma_{0}\|Z_{s}\|^{1+\alpha}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)ds\Big\}.

Applying Itô-Tanaka’s formula to
\Psi(t,|Y_{t}|)
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle d\Psi(t,|Y_{t}|)=

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}(t,|Y_{t}|)dt+\frac{1}{2}\Psi_{xx}(t,|Y_{t}|)|Z_{t}|^{2}dt

\displaystyle+\Psi_{x}(t,|Y_{t}|)\Big[-\mathop{\rm sgn}(Y_{t})g(t,Y_{t},Z_{t},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{t}},\mathbb{P}_{Z_{t}})dt+\mathop{\rm sgn}(Y_{t})Z_{t}dW_{t}+dL_{t}\Big].

Notice that

\Psi_{t}(t,x)=\gamma\Psi(t,x)\Big(\theta_{t}+\beta|Y_{t}|+\beta_{0}\|Y_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\gamma_{0}\|Z_{t}\|^{1+\alpha}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big),

and

\Psi_{x}(t,x)=\gamma\Psi(t,x)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Psi_{xx}(t,x)=\gamma^{2}\Psi(t,x).

Then from the condition (i) of
3
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle d\Psi(t,|Y_{t}|)

\displaystyle=\gamma\Psi(t,|Y_{t}|)\Big[-\mathop{\rm sgn}(Y_{t})g(t,Y_{t},Z_{t},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{t}},\mathbb{P}_{Z_{t}})+\theta_{t}+\beta|Y_{t}|+\beta_{0}\|Y_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}\|Z_{t}\|^{1+\alpha}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma|Z_{t}|^{2}\Big]dt+\gamma\Psi(t,|Y_{t}|)\mathop{\rm sgn}(Y_{t})Z_{t}dW_{t}+\gamma\Psi(t,|Y_{t}|)dL_{t}

\displaystyle\geqslant\gamma\Psi(t,|Y_{t}|)\mathop{\rm sgn}(Y_{t})Z_{t}dW_{t}.

Integrating from
t
to
T
firstly and then taking conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\cdot]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\exp\big(\gamma|Y_{t}|\big)\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}_{t}\exp\Big\{\gamma|\eta|+\gamma\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\theta_{s}+\beta|Y_{s}|+\beta_{0}\|Y_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\gamma_{0}\|Z_{s}\|^{1+\alpha}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)ds\Big\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \exp\Big\{\gamma(K_{1}+K_{2})+\gamma\int_{t}^{T}(\beta+\beta_{0})\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(s,T)}ds+\gamma\gamma_{0}\int_{t}^{T}\|Z_{s}\|^{1+\alpha}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}ds\Big\},

where we have used the condition (iii) of
1
. Therefore, it yields that for
t\in[T-\kappa,T]
,

|Y_{t}|\leqslant(K_{1}+K_{2})+\int_{t}^{T}(\beta+\beta_{0})\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(s,T)}ds+\gamma_{0}\int_{t}^{T}\|Z_{s}\|^{1+\alpha}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}ds.

(3.27)

The last term in the right hand side of (
3.27
) is estimated below.
Since we have from Young’s inequality that for positive numbers
a
and
b
,

ab^{1+\alpha}=\left(\left(\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\right)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}}a^{\frac{2}{1-\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\left(\frac{2}{1+\alpha}b^{2}\right)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\leqslant b^{2}+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\right)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}}a^{\frac{2}{1-\alpha}},

(3.28)

it follows for a positive constant
\varepsilon_{0}
to be determined later (taking

a=\frac{2\gamma_{0}}{\tilde{\gamma}\varepsilon_{0}}
and
b=\|Z_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
in the preceding inequality)

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}\|Z_{s}\|^{1+\alpha}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\gamma}\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\Big[\frac{2\gamma_{0}}{\tilde{\gamma}\varepsilon_{0}}\|Z_{s}\|^{1+\alpha}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big]\leqslant\frac{\tilde{\gamma}\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\mathbb{E}|Z_{s}|^{2}+L_{3}.

In view of inequality (
3.27
), we arrive at the following inequality: for
t\in[T-\kappa,T]
,

|Y_{t}|\leqslant(K_{1}+K_{2})+\int_{t}^{T}(\beta+\beta_{0})\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(s,T)}ds+L_{3}T+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\tilde{\gamma}\varepsilon_{0}}{2}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds.

(3.29)

The last term in the right hand side of the last inequality is estimated below. We define the stopping time

\tau_{k}:=T\wedge\inf\Big\{s\in[t,T]:\int_{t}^{s}|Z_{r}|^{2}dr\geqslant k\Big\},

with the convention that
\inf\emptyset=\infty
.
Then the condition (
3.20
) allows us to show that

\displaystyle Y_{t}-Y_{\tau_{k}}=\int_{t}^{\tau_{k}}g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}},\mathbb{P}_{Z_{s}})ds-\int_{t}^{\tau_{k}}Z_{s}dW_{s}

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{t}^{\tau_{k}}\Big(\frac{\tilde{\gamma}}{2}|Z_{s}|^{2}-\theta_{s}(\omega)-\beta|Y_{s}|-\beta_{0}\|Y_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\gamma_{0}\|Z_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1+\alpha}\Big)ds-\int_{t}^{\tau_{k}}Z_{s}dW_{s}.

In view of the boundness of
\theta
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\tau_{k}}\frac{\tilde{\gamma}}{2}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds

(3.30)

\displaystyle\leqslant Y_{t}-Y_{\tau_{k}}+\int_{t}^{\tau_{k}}\big(\theta_{s}(\omega)+\beta|Y_{s}|+\beta_{0}\|Y_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\gamma_{0}\|Z_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1+\alpha}\big)ds+\int_{t}^{\tau_{k}}Z_{s}dW_{s}

\displaystyle\leqslant[2+(\beta+\beta_{0})T]\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T)}+K_{2}+\int_{t}^{\tau_{k}}\gamma_{0}\|Z_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1+\alpha}ds+\int_{t}^{\tau_{k}}Z_{s}dW_{s}.

Multiplying
\varepsilon_{0}
and taking the expectation on both sides of the above inequality, and using Fatou’s lemma, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\tilde{\gamma}\varepsilon_{0}}{2}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}[2+(\beta+\beta_{0})T]\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T)}+\varepsilon_{0}K_{2}+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\varepsilon_{0}\gamma_{0}\|Z_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1+\alpha}ds.

(3.31)

On the other hand, taking
a=\frac{4\gamma_{0}}{\tilde{\gamma}}
and
b=\|Z_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
in the inequality (
3.28
), it yields

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}\gamma_{0}\|Z_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1+\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\gamma}\varepsilon_{0}}{4}\bigg[\frac{4\gamma_{0}}{\tilde{\gamma}}\|Z_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1+\alpha}\bigg]\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{\tilde{\gamma}\varepsilon_{0}}{4}|Z_{s}|^{2}\Big]+L_{4}.

(3.32)

Combining (
3.31
) and (
3.32
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\tilde{\gamma}\varepsilon_{0}}{2}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\leqslant 2\varepsilon_{0}[2+(\beta+\beta_{0})T]\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T)}+2\varepsilon_{0}K_{2}+2L_{4}T.

(3.33)

Hence, from (
3.29
) and (
3.33
), one gets

\displaystyle\displaystyle|Y_{t}|\leqslant

\displaystyle(K_{1}+K_{2})+\int_{t}^{T}(\beta+\beta_{0})\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(s,T)}ds+L_{3}T

\displaystyle+2\varepsilon_{0}[2+(\beta+\beta_{0})T]\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T)}+2\varepsilon_{0}K_{2}+2L_{4}T.

Setting

\varepsilon_{0}:=\frac{1}{4[2+(\beta+\beta_{0})T]},

it follows

\displaystyle\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T)}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2(K_{1}+K_{2})+2L_{3}T+4\varepsilon_{0}K_{2}+4L_{4}T+2\int_{t}^{T}(\beta+\beta_{0})\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(s,T)}ds.

Using Gronwall’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T)}\leqslant L_{5},\quad~\forall t\in[T-\kappa,T].

In particular,

\displaystyle\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(T-\kappa,T)}\leqslant L_{5}.

(3.34)

In addition, taking the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\cdot]
on both sides of (
3.30
), similar to (
3.31
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\tilde{\gamma}\varepsilon_{0}}{2}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}[2+(\beta+\beta_{0})T]\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T)}+\varepsilon_{0}K_{2}+\int_{t}^{T}\varepsilon_{0}\gamma_{0}\|Z_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1+\alpha}ds.

(3.35)

From (
3.32
) and (
3.35
), we see

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\tilde{\gamma}\varepsilon_{0}}{2}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}[2+(\beta+\beta_{0})T]\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T)}+\varepsilon_{0}K_{2}+L_{4}T+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\tilde{\gamma}\varepsilon_{0}}{4}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds.

(3.36)

In view of (
3.33
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\tilde{\gamma}\varepsilon_{0}}{2}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\leqslant 2\varepsilon_{0}[2+(\beta+\beta_{0})T]\|Y\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T)}+2\varepsilon_{0}K_{2}+2L_{4}T.

Combining the definition of
\varepsilon_{0}
and (
3.34
), we have

\|Z\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(T-\kappa,T)}\leqslant L_{6}.

Step 2.
We prove the existence and uniqueness of global adapted solutions.

Note that the main idea in proving the existence and uniqueness result on the mean-field BSDE is twofold. Firstly, we employ
Theorem 3.3
to establish the unique solution of the mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) on small time intervals. Secondly, we utilize
Step 1
to demonstrate that the obtained solution
(Y,Z)
satisfies the estimates (
3.26
) over the whole interval
[0,T]
. The detailed proof is structured into several steps. Let us proceed with the first step.

First, let
\kappa_{0}=\frac{\varepsilon^{*}}{2}
, where the positive constant
\varepsilon^{*}
is given in
Theorem 3.3
. Since
\|\eta\|\leqslant K_{1}
,
Theorem 3.3
implies that the mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) has a unique adapted solution
(Y^{(1)},Z^{(1)})\in S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(T-\kappa_{0},T)\times{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(T-\kappa_{0},T)
on the interval
[T-\kappa_{0},T]
.
In addition,
Step 1
implies that

\|Y^{(1)}\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(T-\kappa_{0},T)}\leqslant L_{5}\quad~\text{and}\quad~\|Z^{(1)}\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(T-\kappa_{0},T)}\leqslant L_{6}.

(3.37)

Second, we prove the existence and uniqueness result on the small interval
[T-2\kappa_{0},T-\kappa_{0}]
. Note that here the terminal value is
Y^{(1)}_{T-\kappa_{0}}
, which is bounded by
L_{5}
from (
3.37
).
From
Theorem 3.3
again we have that the mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) has a unique adapted solution

(Y^{(2)},Z^{(2)})\in S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(T-2\kappa_{0},T-\kappa_{0})\times{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(T-2\kappa_{0},T-\kappa_{0})

on the interval
[T-2\kappa_{0},T-\kappa_{0}]
. Define for
t\in[T-\kappa_{0},T]
,

\left(\tilde{Y}_{t},\tilde{Z}_{t}\right):=\left(Y^{(1)}_{t},Z^{(1)}_{t}\right)\,\chi_{[T-\kappa_{0},T]}(t)+\left(Y^{(2)}_{t},Z^{(2)}_{t}\right)\,\chi_{[T-2\kappa_{0},T-\kappa_{0})}(t).

Then
(\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})
is the unique solution to
the mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) on the interval
[T-2\kappa_{0},T]
.
And similar to
Step 1
, we have

\|Y\|_{S^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(T-2\kappa_{0},T)}\leqslant L_{5}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|Z\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(T-2\kappa_{0},T)}\leqslant L_{6}.

Finally, set
m\triangleq[\frac{T}{\kappa_{0}}]+1
.
Repeating
m
times the previous procedure, we establish the existence and uniqueness of a solution
(Y,Z)
for the mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) on the interval
[0,T]
. Furthermore, we have

\|Y\|_{S^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)}\leqslant L_{5}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|Z\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)}\leqslant L_{6}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof.

4
Comparison Theorem

In this section, we present a comparison theorem for the solutions of the mean-field BSDE (

1.1
) as the BSDE (
1.1
) with parameters
(g,\eta)
, which indicates that the generator is

and the terminal value is
\eta
.

Theorem 4.1

(Comparison principle)
.

Let the parameters
(g,\eta)
and
(\bar{g},\bar{\eta})
satisfy

2
.
Denote by
(Y,Z)
and
(\bar{Y},\bar{Z})
the global adapted solutions of mean-field BSDE
(
1.1
) with parameters
(\eta,g)
and

, respectively.
In addition, suppose that

(i)

One of both generators
g
and
\bar{g}
does not depend on
\mu_{2}
.

(ii)

The other one of both generators
g
and
\bar{g}
is nondecreasing with respect to

\mu_{1}

K

for every

and

Before presenting the proof of the comparison theorem, we would like to provide some remarks.

Remark 4.2
.

If either the generator
g
depends on
\mathbb{P}_{Z}
or
g
decreases with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{Y}
, then the comparison theorem will not hold. Counterexamples illustrating this can be found in Buckdahn, Li, and Peng
[
9
, Example 3.1, Example 3.2]
.

Remark 4.3
.

Under the scenario where the mean-field term in the generator appears as an expectation, Buckdahn, Li, and Peng
[
9
, Example 3.2 and Theorem 3.2]
impose a condition called
\mathbf{Condition\ (L)}
for the comparison property of mean-field BSDEs, which can be restated as follows:

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\mathbf{Condition\ (L)}\quad\hbox{the generator $g$ (or $\bar{g}$) is nondecreasing in the mean-field term $y^{\prime}$.}\end{array}

One way to interpret this condition is that if the derivative of
g
(or
\bar{g}
) with respect to the mean-field term
y^{\prime}
exists, it should not be less than zero.
In the context of measure-dependence, assumption
\mathrm{(ii)}
in
Theorem 4.1
serves as an equivalent assumption to
\mathbf{Condition\ (L)}
.

Remark 4.4
.

Here are sufficient conditions for
\mathrm{(ii)}
in
Theorem 4.1
.
Assume that the function
g(\omega,t,y,z,\cdot,\mu_{2})
is differentiable with respect to

\mu_{1}
for every
(\omega,t,y,z,\mu_{2})\in\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
If there is a positive constant
K
such that for every
(\omega,t,y,z,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},a)\in\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathbb{R}

holds true. For more details, see Li, Liang, and Zhang
[

\displaystyle\delta Y_{t}=

\displaystyle\delta\eta+\int_{t}^{T}\big[g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{{Y_{s}}})-\bar{g}(s,\bar{Y}_{s},\bar{Z}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{\bar{Y}_{s}},\mathbb{P}_{\bar{Z}_{s}})\big]ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{T}\delta Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

\displaystyle g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}})-\bar{g}(s,\bar{Y}_{s},\bar{Z}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{\bar{Y}_{s}},\mathbb{P}_{\bar{Z}_{s}})

satisfies the following equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-d\Delta Y_{t}^{i}=\big[g(t,Y_{t}^{i},Z_{t}^{i,i},\nu_{t}^{N},\mu_{t}^{N})-g(t,\bar{Y}_{t}^{i},\bar{Z}_{t}^{i},\bar{\nu}_{t},\bar{\mu}_{t})\big]dt-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\Delta Z_{t}^{i,j}dW^{j}_{t},\quad t\in[0,T];\\
\quad\Delta Y^{i}_{T}=0,\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle g(t,Y_{t}^{i},Z_{t}^{i,i},\nu_{t}^{N},\mu_{t}^{N})-g(t,\bar{Y}_{t}^{i},\bar{Z}_{t}^{i},\bar{\nu}_{t},\bar{\mu}_{t})=J_{1,t}+J_{2,t},

with

J_{1,t}\triangleq g(t,Y_{t}^{i},Z_{t}^{i,i},\nu_{t}^{N},\mu_{t}^{N})-g(t,\bar{Y}_{t}^{i},Z_{t}^{i,i},\bar{\nu}_{t},\bar{\mu}_{t}),

and

J_{2,t}\triangleq g(t,\bar{Y}_{t}^{i},Z_{t}^{i,i},\bar{\nu}_{t},\bar{\mu}_{t})-g(t,\bar{Y}_{t}^{i},\bar{Z}_{t}^{i,i},\bar{\nu}_{t},\bar{\mu}_{t}).

On the term
J_{2,t}
, by
6
, there is a process
\Gamma^{i}
such that

J_{2,t}=\Gamma^{i}_{t}\Delta Z_{t}^{i,i},

where

|\Gamma^{i}_{t}|\leqslant\phi(|\bar{Y}^{i}_{t}|\vee||\bar{Y}^{i}_{t}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)})(1+|Z^{i,i}_{t}|+|\bar{Z}^{i,i}_{t}|).

By (
3.18
) and (
5.5
), for each
i
, we have
||\Gamma^{i}||_{{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)}\leqslant C
for a constant
C
depending only on
(K,K_{1},K_{3},T)
.
Now, thanks to Girsanov theorem, we have

-d\Delta Y_{t}^{i}=J_{1,t}dt-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\Delta Z_{t}^{i,j}d\widetilde{W}^{j,i}_{t},\quad t\in[0,T].

Here
\widetilde{W}^{j,i}
depends on
i
, which means one only changes the
i
-th component of
(W^{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,W^{N})
,
i.e.,

\widetilde{W}^{j,i}_{t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle W^{j}_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma^{i}_{r}dr,\quad\ j=i;\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle W^{j}_{t},\qquad\qquad\quad\ \ \ j\neq i.\end{array}\right.

Then
(\widetilde{W}^{j,i})_{1\leqslant j\leqslant N}
is an N-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}^{i}
-Brownian motion with

d\mathbb{Q}^{i}=\mathscr{E}^{i}(\Gamma\cdot W^{j})_{0}^{T}d\mathbb{P}
, where
\Gamma=(0,\cdot\cdot\cdot,0,\Gamma^{i},0,\cdot\cdot\cdot,0).

From Briand et al.
[
4
]
, it follows for fixed
i
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{i}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Delta Y_{t}^{i}|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\Delta Z_{t}^{i,j}|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{i}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{p}_{2}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})dt+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{p}_{2}(\mu_{t}^{N},\bar{\mu}_{t})dt\Big].

Notice that
\|\Gamma\cdot W^{j}\|_{BMO}
and
\|\Gamma\cdot W^{j}\|_{{BMO}_{q}},q>2
are equivalent, from
2.2
there exists a constant
p_{0}>1

such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[(\mathscr{E}^{i}(\Gamma\cdot W^{j})_{0}^{T})^{p_{0}}\Big]\leqslant C_{p_{0}}.

For this
p_{0}
, by summing over
i
on both sides of (
5.8
) and then applying Hölder inequality, we have for
p\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{i}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Delta Y_{t}^{i}|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\Delta Z_{t}^{i,j}|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{i}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{p}_{2}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})dt+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{p}_{2}(\mu_{t}^{N},\bar{\mu}_{t})dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathscr{E}^{i}(\Gamma\cdot W^{j})_{0}^{T}\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{p}_{2}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})dt+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{p}_{2}(\mu_{t}^{N},\bar{\mu}_{t})dt\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Big[(\mathscr{E}^{i}(\Gamma\cdot W^{j})_{0}^{T})^{p_{0}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{p}_{2}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})dt+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{p}_{2}(\mu_{t}^{N},\bar{\mu}_{t})dt\Big)^{q_{0}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{p}_{2}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})dt+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{p}_{2}(\mu_{t}^{N},\bar{\mu}_{t})dt\Big)^{q_{0}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{pq_{0}}_{2}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})dt+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{pq_{0}}_{2}(\mu_{t}^{N},\bar{\mu}_{t})dt\Big]^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}},

where
q_{0}=\frac{p_{0}}{p_{0}-1}>1
.

On the other hand, it is easy to see that
d\mathbb{P}=\mathscr{E}^{i}(-\Gamma\cdot\widetilde{W}^{j,i})_{0}^{T}d\mathbb{Q}^{i}.

Similar to the above argument, for fixed
i
,
thanks to
2.2
there exists a constant
p^{\prime}_{0}>1
such that

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{i}}\Big[\Big(\mathscr{E}^{i}(-\Gamma\cdot\widetilde{W}^{j,i})_{0}^{T}\Big)^{p^{\prime}_{0}}\Big]\leqslant C_{p^{\prime}_{0}}.

For the above
p^{\prime}_{0}
, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Hölder inequality allow us to show that for any
p\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Delta Y_{t}^{i}|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\Delta Z_{t}^{i,j}|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{i}}\Big[\mathscr{E}^{i}(-\Gamma\cdot\widetilde{W}^{j,i})_{0}^{T}\cdot\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Delta Y_{t}^{i}|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\Delta Z_{t}^{i,j}|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big)\Big]\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{i}}\Big[\Big(\mathscr{E}^{i}(-\Gamma\cdot\widetilde{W}^{j,i})_{0}^{T}\Big)^{p^{\prime}_{0}}\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{0}}}

\displaystyle\cdot\bigg\{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{i}}\Big[\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Delta Y_{t}^{i}|^{pq^{\prime}_{0}}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\Delta Z_{t}^{i,j}|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{pq^{\prime}_{0}}{2}}\Big)\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{i}}\Big[\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Delta Y_{t}^{i}|^{pq^{\prime}_{0}}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\Delta Z_{t}^{i,j}|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{pq^{\prime}_{0}}{2}}\Big)\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\bigg\{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{i}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Delta Y_{t}^{i}|^{pq^{\prime}_{0}}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\Delta Z_{t}^{i,j}|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{pq^{\prime}_{0}}{2}}\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}_{0}}},

where
q^{\prime}_{0}=\frac{p^{\prime}_{0}}{p^{\prime}_{0}-1}>1
.

Finally, combining the above equalities we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Delta Y_{t}^{i}|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\Delta Z_{t}^{i,j}|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{pq_{0}q^{\prime}_{0}}_{2}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})dt+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{pq_{0}q^{\prime}_{0}}_{2}(\mu_{t}^{N},\bar{\mu}_{t})dt\Big]^{\frac{1}{q_{0}q^{\prime}_{0}}}.

Thanks to the exchangeability of the
(Y_{t},Z_{t})
, we have the item (ii).
∎

Remark 5.3
.

In BSDE (
1.1
),
if the generator
g
does not depend on the law of
Z
, then the inequalities appeared in
Theorem 5.2
become that, for any
p\geqslant 2
and for

i=1,\cdots,N,

\displaystyle\mathrm{(i)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\Big\{\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\Delta Y_{t}^{i}|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|\Delta Z^{i,j}_{t}|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{pq_{0}q^{\prime}_{0}}_{2}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})dt\Big]^{\frac{1}{q_{0}q^{\prime}_{0}}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{(ii)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\Delta Y_{t}^{i}|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|\Delta Z^{i,j}_{t}|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}^{pq_{0}q^{\prime}_{0}}_{2}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})dt\Big]^{\frac{1}{q_{0}q^{\prime}_{0}}}.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
6
hold true and assume that
g
does not depend on the law of
Z
. Then, for
p>2
, there
exist two constants
q_{1},q^{\prime}_{1}>1
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]\leqslant CN^{-\frac{1}{2q_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}}.

Proof.

Let
(\bar{Y}^{i},\bar{Z}^{i})
be the solution of (
5.2
), and let
(\widetilde{Y}^{i},\widetilde{Z}^{i}),i=1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,N
be i.i.d. copies of
(\bar{Y}^{i},\bar{Z}^{i})

such that

\widetilde{Y}^{i}_{t}=\eta^{i}+\int_{t}^{T}g(s,\widetilde{Y}^{i}_{s},\widetilde{Z}^{i}_{s},\bar{\nu}_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{Z}^{i}_{s}dW^{i}_{s},\quad t\in[0,T].

(5.9)

Then

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}^{i}_{t}-Y^{i}_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{T}g(s,\widetilde{Y}^{i}_{s},\widetilde{Z}^{i}_{s},\bar{\nu}_{s})-g(s,Y^{i}_{s},\widetilde{Z}^{i}_{s},\bar{\nu}_{s})+g(s,Y^{i}_{s},\widetilde{Z}^{i}_{s},\bar{\nu}_{s})-g(s,Y^{i}_{s},Z^{i,i}_{s},\bar{\nu}_{s})

(5.10)

\displaystyle+g(s,Y^{i}_{s},Z^{i,i}_{s},\bar{\nu}_{s})-g(s,Y^{i}_{s},Z^{i,i}_{s},\nu^{N}_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\delta_{ij}\widetilde{Z}^{i}_{s}-Z^{i,j}_{s}\Big)dW^{j}_{s},

where
\delta_{ij}=1,
if
i=j
; or else, it equals to
0
.
Since

\left\{\begin{aligned} &g(s,Y^{i}_{s},\widetilde{Z}^{i}_{s},\bar{\nu}_{s})-g(s,Y^{i}_{s},Z^{i,i}_{s},\bar{\nu}_{s})=\Theta^{i}_{s}(\widetilde{Z}^{i}_{s}-Z^{i,i}_{s});\\
&|\Theta^{i}_{s}|\leqslant\phi(|Y_{s}^{i}|\vee\|Y_{s}^{i}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})(1+|\widetilde{Z}^{i}_{s}|+|Z^{i,i}_{s}|),\end{aligned}\right.

so for given
\bar{\nu}_{s}
, the equation (
5.9
) is a standard BSDE with quadratic growth. Thanks to Hu and Tang
[
24
, Theorem 2.3]
,
|\widetilde{Z}^{i}|
belongs to
{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}[0,T]
. Hence, according to
5.1
, one can know that

\Theta^{i}
also belongs to
{\cal Z}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}[0,T]
. Define
\widehat{W}^{j,i}(s)=W^{j}(s)-\int_{0}^{s}\Theta^{i}_{r}dr
. Then

(\widehat{W}^{j,i})_{1\leqslant j\leqslant N}
is an
N
-dimensional Brownian motion under the probability
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}
, which is defined by
d\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}=\mathscr{E}^{i}(\Theta\cdot W^{j})_{0}^{T}d\mathbb{P}
with
\Theta=(0,\cdot\cdot\cdot,0,\Theta^{i},0,\cdot\cdot\cdot,0).

Consequently, (
5.10
) can be written as

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}^{i}_{t}-Y^{i}_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{T}g(s,\widetilde{Y}^{i}_{s},\widetilde{Z}^{i}_{s},\bar{\nu}_{s})-g(s,Y^{i}_{s},\widetilde{Z}^{i}_{s},\bar{\nu}_{s})+g(s,Y^{i}_{s},Z^{i,i}_{s},\bar{\nu}_{s})-g(s,Y^{i}_{s},Z^{i,i}_{s},\nu^{N}_{s})ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\delta_{ij}\widetilde{Z}^{i}_{s}-Z^{i,j}_{s}\Big)d\widehat{W}^{j,i}_{s}.

Thanks to Briand et al.
[
4
, Proposition 3.2]
, we have for fixed
i
and for
p>2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{Y}^{i}_{t}-Y^{i}_{t}|^{p}\Big]\leqslant C\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{s}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{s})ds\Big].

Hence, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality allows to show that for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\bigg[{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{t}^{N},\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N})\bigg]\leqslant\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|Y^{i}_{t}-\widetilde{Y}^{i}_{t}|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\bigg[|Y^{i}_{t}-\widetilde{Y}^{i}_{t}|^{p}\bigg]\leqslant\frac{C}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{s}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{s})ds\Big],

where
\widetilde{\nu}^{N}_{t}
is defined similarly to
{\nu}^{N}_{t}
as mentioned in (
5.3
), but with the substitution of
{Y}^{i}_{t}
by
\widetilde{Y}^{i}_{t}
.
By summing over
i
on both sides of the past inequality, we have, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\bigg[{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{t}^{N},\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N})\bigg]\leqslant C\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{s}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{s})ds\Big].

From this and the triangle inequality, we know, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\bigg[{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\bigg]\leqslant\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\bigg[\Big({\cal W}_{2}(\nu_{t}^{N},\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N})+{\cal W}_{2}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{p}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\bigg[({\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{t}^{N},\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N})+{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t}))\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{s}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{s})ds\Big]+2^{p}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\bigg[{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t}))\bigg].

Using Gronwall’s inequality, we deduce, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]\leqslant C\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big].

(5.11)

On the one hand, similar to
Theorem 5.2
, thanks to
2.2
, there exists a constant
p_{1}>1
such that

\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}\mathbb{E}\Big[(\mathscr{E}^{i}(\Theta\cdot W^{j})_{0}^{T})^{p_{1}}\Big]\leqslant C_{p_{1}}.

Define
q_{1}=\frac{p_{1}}{p_{1}-1}
. Clearly,
q_{1}>1.
From Hölder inequality, we have, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]\leqslant\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathscr{E}^{i}(\Theta\cdot W^{j})_{0}^{T}\cdot{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigg(\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[(\mathscr{E}^{i}(\Theta\cdot W^{j})_{0}^{T})^{p_{1}}\Big]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\cdot\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{pq_{1}}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\bigg)

(5.12)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{pq_{1}}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}.

On the other hand, clearly,
d\mathbb{P}=\mathscr{E}^{i}((-\Theta)\cdot\widehat{W}^{j,i})_{0}^{T}d\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}
. For fixed
i
, from
2.2
there exists a constant
p^{\prime}_{1}>1
such that

\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[(\mathscr{E}^{i}((-\Theta)\cdot\widehat{W}^{j,i})_{0}^{T})^{p^{\prime}_{1}}\Big]\leqslant C_{p^{\prime}_{1}}.

Define
q^{\prime}_{1}=\frac{p^{\prime}_{1}}{p^{\prime}_{1}-1}
. Obviously,
q^{\prime}_{1}>1.
Then one has, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[\mathscr{E}^{i}((-\Theta)\cdot\widehat{W}^{j,i})_{0}^{T}\cdot{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]

(5.13)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigg(\Big\{\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[(\mathscr{E}^{i}((-\Theta)\cdot\widehat{W}^{j,i})_{0}^{T})^{p^{\prime}_{1}}\Big]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{1}}}\cdot\Big\{\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{pq^{\prime}_{1}}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}_{1}}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big\{\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{pq^{\prime}_{1}}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}_{1}}}.

According to (
5.11
)-(
5.13
), we have from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]\leqslant C\bigg\{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{pq^{\prime}_{1}}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}_{1}}}

(5.14)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\bigg\{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{pq^{\prime}_{1}}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}_{1}}}\leqslant C\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{q_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}}.

Next, we show for
t\in[0,T]
and
p>2
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{2}^{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\leqslant{\cal W}_{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}^{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t}).

(5.15)

In fact, from the definition of
p
-Wasserstein metric, for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
\rho_{0}\in{\cal H}
, where
{\cal H}
is
the set of probability measures
\rho
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the first and second marginal laws
\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N}
and
\bar{\nu}_{t}
,
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|x-y|^{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}\rho_{0}(dx,dy)\leqslant{\cal W}_{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}^{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})+\varepsilon.

From Hölder inequality, we have for
t\in[0,T]
and
p>2
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{2}^{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})=\inf_{\rho\in{\cal H}}\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|x-y|^{2}\rho(dx,dy)\bigg\}^{\frac{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|x-y|^{2}\rho_{0}(dx,dy)\bigg\}^{\frac{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|x-y|^{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}\rho_{0}(dx,dy)\leqslant{\cal W}_{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}^{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})+\varepsilon.

Letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
we have
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,
{\cal W}_{2}^{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\leqslant{\cal W}_{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}^{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t}).

Combining (
5.14
) and (
5.15
) we have from Fournier and Guillin
[
20
, Theorem 1]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[{\cal W}_{2}^{p}(\nu_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]\leqslant C\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[{\cal W}_{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}^{pq_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}(\widetilde{\nu}_{t}^{N},\bar{\nu}_{t})\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{q_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}}\leqslant CN^{-\frac{1}{2q_{1}q^{\prime}_{1}}},\quad t\in[0,T].

This completes the proof.
∎

and

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
6
be in force and assume that the generator
g
is independent of the law of
Z
.
Then, for
p\geqslant 2
, there exist four constants
q_{0},q^{\prime}_{0},q_{1},q^{\prime}_{1}>1
and
a positive constant
C

6
Applications to PDE

The master equation can be regarded as a PDE in the Wasserstein space, whose state variable refers to the distribution of some state process. However, finding the viscosity solution of the master equation is always a challenging topic. This is due to the Wasserstein space lacking local compactness, which is a necessary element in viscosity theory (see Wu and Zhang
[
37
]
).
Furthermore, studying general mean-field BSDEs and the associated master equations with quadratic growth adds further complexity to the problem. In addition to quadratic growth, the nonlinearity of the coefficient
g
in mean-field BSDEs can lead to time-inconsistency of the value function. The regularity of the value function under weak formulation is also more involved, even for the Lipschitz case.
Due to these challenges, instead of considering the distribution of the state process, it may be more appropriate to consider situations where the coefficients depend on the expectation of the state process. This alternative approach takes into account the expectations of the state process, rather than its distribution.

In detail, for any initial pair
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and frozen
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we consider the following mean-field forward-backward stochastic differential equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX^{t,x}_{s}&=\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\big[b(s,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{s})^{\prime},X^{t,x}_{s})]ds+\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\sigma(s,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{s})^{\prime},X^{t,x}_{s})\big]dW_{s},\\
-dY^{t,x}_{s}&=\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\big[g(s,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{s})^{\prime},X^{t,x}_{s},(Y^{0,x_{0}}_{s})^{\prime},Y^{t,x}_{s},Z^{t,x}_{s})\big]ds-Z^{t,x}_{s}dW_{s},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T,\\
X^{t,x}_{t}&=x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad~Y^{t,x}_{T}=\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\big[\Phi((X^{0,x_{0}}_{T})^{\prime},X^{t,x}_{T})\big],\end{aligned}\right.

(6.1)

where the coefficients
b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}

satisfy the following condition.

Assumption 8
.

There is a positive constant
C
such that for any

,

|b(t,x^{\prime},x)-b(t,\bar{x}^{\prime},\bar{x})|+|\sigma(t,x^{\prime},x)-\sigma(t,\bar{x}^{\prime},\bar{x})|\leqslant C(|x^{\prime}-\bar{x}^{\prime}|+|x-\bar{x}|).

Then under
8

On the other hand, for the coefficients

Remark 6.2

Proposition 6.3
.

We define the value function as follows:

u(t,x)\triangleq Y^{t,x}_{t},\quad~t\in[0,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(6.2)

Now, we would like to connect the forward-backward system (

) with quadratic growth to the following nonlocal partial differential equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial v(t,x)}{\partial t}+{\cal L}v(t,x)\\
&+\mathbb{E}\Big[g\Big(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x,v(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}}),v(t,x),Dv(t,x)\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x)\big]^{\intercal}\Big)\Big]=0,\\
&\qquad(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
&v(T,x)=\mathbb{E}\big[\Phi(X_{T}^{0,x_{0}},x)\big],\quad~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{aligned}\right.

(6.3)

where

\displaystyle{\cal L}v(t,x)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x)\big]\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x)\big]^{\intercal}D^{2}v(t,x)\Big)

\displaystyle+Dv(t,x)\mathbb{E}\big[b(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x)\big]^{\intercal}.

It should be pointed out that in (
6.3
), the terminology nonlocal means

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[g\Big(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x,v(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}}),v(t,x),Dv(t,x)\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x)\big]^{\intercal}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\Big(t,x^{\prime},x,v(t,x^{\prime}),v(t,x),Dv(t,x)\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x)\big]^{\intercal}\Big)\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}^{0,x_{0}}}(dx^{\prime}),

where
X^{0,x_{0}}
is the solution of the forward equation of (

) with the initial pair
(0,x_{0})
.

In the following, we prove that the value function
u
defined in (
6.2
) is the viscosity solution of PDE (
6.3
). For this, we extend the approach of

[
9
]
developed in the framework of mean-field BSDEs with linear growth to that of quadratic growth.
First, we recall the definition of a viscosity solution of PDE (
6.3
). For more details about the viscosity solutions, we refer the reader to Crandall, Ishii, and Lions
[
13
]
.

Note that for Euclidean space
\mathbb{H}
, denote by

C_{p}(\mathbb{H})
the set of continuous function on
\mathbb{H}
, who grows at most like a polynomial function of the variable
x
at infinity; and denote by

C_{l,b}^{k}(\mathbb{H})
the set of functions of class
C^{k}
on
\mathbb{H}
, whose partial derivatives of order less than or equal to
k
are bounded.

Definition 6.4

(Viscosity solution)
.

A real-valued continuous function
v\in C_{p}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called

(i)

a viscosity subsolution to PDE (
6.3
), if

v(T,x)\leqslant\mathbb{E}\big[\Phi(X_{T}^{0,x_{0}},x)\big]
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in addition,
if for arbitrary
\psi\in C^{3}_{l,b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
(t^{*},x^{*})\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

such that
v-\psi
attains its local maximum at
(t^{*},x^{*}),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi(t^{*},x^{*})}{\partial t}+{\cal L}\psi(t^{*},x^{*})

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\Big[g\Big(t^{*},X_{t^{*}}^{0,x_{0}},x^{*},v(t^{*},X_{t^{*}}^{0,x_{0}}),v(t^{*},x^{*}),\mathbb{E}[\sigma(t^{*},X_{t^{*}}^{0,x_{0}},x^{*})]^{\intercal}D\psi(t^{*},x^{*})\Big)\Big]\geqslant 0.

(ii)

a viscosity supersolution to PDE (
6.3
), if

v(T,x)\geqslant\mathbb{E}\big[\Phi(X_{T}^{0,x_{0}},x)\big]
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in addition,
if for arbitrary
\psi\in C^{3}_{l,b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
(t^{*},x^{*})\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

such that
v-\psi
attains its local minimum at
(t^{*},x^{*}),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi(t^{*},x^{*})}{\partial t}+{\cal L}\psi(t^{*},x^{*})

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\Big[g\Big(t^{*},X_{t^{*}}^{0,x_{0}},x^{*},v(t^{*},X_{t^{*}}^{0,x_{0}}),v(t^{*},x^{*}),\mathbb{E}[\sigma(t^{*},X_{t^{*}}^{0,x_{0}},x^{*})]^{\intercal}D\psi(t^{*},x^{*})\Big)\Big]\leqslant 0.

(iii)

a viscosity solution to PDE (
6.3
),
if it is both a viscosity subsolution and a viscosity supersolution.

Lemma 6.5
.

Under
8
and
9
, for any
t\in[0,T]

and
\xi\in L_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

u(t,\xi)=Y^{t,\xi}_{t}.

(6.4)

Proof.

The proof is essentially an adaptation of Proposition 4.7 of Peng
[
35
]
, so we sketch it.
First, we assert that (
6.4
) holds true for a simple random variable
\xi\in L_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
with a form below

\xi=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}x_{j}\chi_{B_{j}},

(6.5)

where
\{B_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}
is a finite partition of
(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{t})
and

x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
j=1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,M.

For each
x_{j}
, let
X^{t,x_{j}}
be the solution of the following mean-field SDE

X^{t,x_{j}}_{s}=x_{j}+\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\big[b(r,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,x_{j}}_{r})\big]dr+\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\big[\sigma(r,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,x_{j}}_{r})\big]dW_{r},\ t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

and let
(Y^{t,x_{j}},Z^{t,x_{j}})
be the solution of the mean-field BSDE

\displaystyle Y^{t,x_{j}}_{s}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\big[\Phi((X^{0,x_{0}}_{T})^{\prime},X^{t,x_{j}}_{T})\big]+\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\big[g(r,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,x_{j}}_{r},(Y^{0,x_{0}}_{r})^{\prime},Y^{t,x_{j}}_{r},Z^{t,x_{j}}_{r})\big]dr

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{s}Z^{t,x_{j}}_{r}dW_{r},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T.

Multiplying
\chi_{A_{j}}
on both sides of the above two equations and then summing up with respect to
j
, note that
\sum_{j}\psi(x_{j})\chi_{A_{j}}=\psi(\sum_{j}x_{j}\chi_{A_{j}})
, one gets

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}X^{t,x_{j}}_{s}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}x_{j}+\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\Big[b(r,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{r})^{\prime},\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}X^{t,x_{j}}_{r})\Big]dr

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\Big[\sigma(r,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{r})^{\prime},\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}X^{t,x_{j}}_{r})\Big]dW_{r},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

and for
t\leqslant s\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}Y^{t,x_{j}}_{s}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\Big[\Phi\Big((X^{0,x_{0}}_{T})^{\prime},\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}X^{t,x_{j}}_{T}\Big)\Big]-\int_{t}^{s}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}Z^{t,x_{j}}_{r}dW_{r}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\Big[g\Big(r,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{r})^{\prime},\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}X^{t,x_{j}}_{r},(Y^{0,x_{0}}_{r})^{\prime},\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}Y^{t,x_{j}}_{r},\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}Z^{t,x_{j}}_{r}\Big)\Big]dr.

Then from the existence and uniqueness of the mean-field BSDE with quadratic growth (see
Theorem 3.5
), we conclude that for
t\leqslant s\leqslant T
,

X^{t,\xi}_{s}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}X^{t,x_{j}}_{s},\quad\ Y^{t,\xi}_{s}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}Y^{t,x_{j}}_{s},\quad\ Z^{t,\xi}_{s}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}Z^{t,x_{j}}_{s}.

Finally, from the definition of
u(t,x)
, it yields

Y^{t,\xi}_{t}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}Y^{t,x_{j}}_{t}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\chi_{A_{j}}u(t,x_{j})=u(t,\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}x_{j}\chi_{A_{j}})=u(t,\xi).

Now, for given
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a sequence of simple variables
\{\xi_{j}\}
admitting the form (
6.5
) that converges to
\xi
in
L^{2}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Since the flow
(t,x)\mapsto X^{t,x}_{s}
and
(t,x,s)\mapsto Y^{t,x}_{s}
are continuous,

u(t,x)
is continuous with respect to
(t,x)
and note the fact
Y^{t,\xi_{j}}_{t}=u(t,\xi_{j})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|Y^{t,\xi}_{t}-u(t,\xi)|^{2}\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[|Y^{t,\xi}_{t}-Y^{t,\xi_{j}}_{t}+Y^{t,\xi_{j}}_{t}-u(t,\xi_{j})+u(t,\xi_{j})-u(t,\xi)|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\mathbb{E}\big[|Y^{t,\xi}_{t}-Y^{t,\xi_{j}}_{t}|^{2}\big]+\mathbb{E}\big[|u(t,\xi_{j})-u(t,\xi)|^{2}\big]\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad j\rightarrow\infty.

Hence, we have (
6.4
).
∎

Based on the above preparation, now we state the main result of this section.

Theorem 6.6

(Feynman-Kac formula)
.

Under
8
and
9
, the value function
u
defined in (
6.2
) is the unique viscosity solution of PDE (
6.3
).

Proof.

From
6.5
and the uniqueness of the mean-field forward-backward SDE (
6.1
) with the initial pair
(t,x)=(0,x_{0})
, we have that

Y^{0,x_{0}}_{t}=Y^{t,X^{0,x_{0}}_{t}}_{t}=u(t,X^{0,x_{0}}_{t}),\quad~t\in[0,T].

(6.6)

Based on the value of
X^{0,x_{0}}
and
Y^{0,x_{0}}
, one can define that

\displaystyle\tilde{b}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[b(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x)\big],\quad\tilde{\sigma}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x)\big],

(6.7)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(t,x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[g(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x,Y_{t}^{0,x_{0}},y,z)\big],\quad\tilde{\Phi}(x)=\mathbb{E}\big[\Phi(X_{T}^{0,x_{0}},x)\big].

By
6.2
, it is easy to check that the parameters
(\tilde{b},\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{g},\tilde{\Phi})
satisfy the assumptions
\mathrm{(H4)}
and
\mathrm{(H5)}
of Kobylanski
[
26
]
.
In view of Theorem 3.8 of
[
26
]
, the function
u
is a viscosity solution to the following PDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\big(\tilde{\sigma}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}(t,x)^{\intercal}D^{2}u(t,x)\big)+\tilde{b}(t,x)^{\intercal}Du(t,x)\\
&\quad+\tilde{g}\big(t,x,u(t,x),\tilde{\sigma}(t,x)^{\intercal}Du(t,x)\big)=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\\
&u(T,x)=\tilde{\Phi}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

Finally, from the definitions of the parameters
(\tilde{b},\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{g},\tilde{\Phi})
and (
6.6
), we see that
u
is also a viscosity solution to PDE (
6.3
).

Next, we prove the uniqueness of PDE (
6.3
).
Let both
u^{1}
and
u^{2}
be the viscosity solutions of PDE (
6.3
).
For any
t\in[0,T]
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we set

\displaystyle g^{1}(t,x,y,z)=\mathbb{E}\Big[g\Big(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x,u^{1}(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}}),y,z\Big)\Big],

\displaystyle g^{2}(t,x,y,z)=\mathbb{E}\Big[g\Big(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x,u^{2}(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}}),y,z\Big)\Big].

Then, for
i=1,2
,
u^{i}
is a viscosity solution to the following PDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u^{i}(t,x)}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\big(\tilde{\sigma}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}(t,x)^{\intercal}D^{2}u^{i}(t,x)\big)+\tilde{b}(t,x)^{\intercal}Du^{i}(t,x)\\
&\quad+g^{i}\big(t,x,u^{i}(t,x),\tilde{\sigma}(t,x)^{\intercal}Du^{i}(t,x)\big)=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\\
&u(T,x)=\tilde{\Phi}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\tilde{b},\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\Phi}
is given in (
6.7

Again, thanks to Kobylanski
[

admits the following
probabilistic interpretation:

u^{i}(t,x)=Y^{t,x,i}_{t},\quad~(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

(6.8)

where
(Y^{t,x,i},Z^{t,x,i})
is the solution to the following quadratic BSDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} -dY^{t,x,i}_{s}&=g^{i}(s,X^{t,x}_{s},Y^{t,x,i}_{s},Z^{t,x,i}_{s})ds-Z^{t,x,i}_{s}dW_{s},\quad\ s\in[t,T];\\
Y_{T}^{t,x,i}&=\tilde{\Phi}(x),\quad\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

(6.9)

Now, we let
(t,x)=(0,x_{0})
in (
6.8
).
Similar to
6.5
, from the continuity of
u^{i}
and the uniqueness of the solution to BSDE (

), one has

\displaystyle u^{i}(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}})=Y^{0,x_{0},i}_{t},\quad\ t\in[0,T].

Finally, from the definitions of
(g^{i},\tilde{b},\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\Phi})
,
we have that the pair
(Y^{t,x,i},Z^{t,x,i})
is the adapted solution to the following quadratic BSDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} -dY^{t,x,i}_{s}&=\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\big[g^{i}(s,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{s})^{\prime},X^{t,x}_{s},(Y^{0,x_{0},i}_{s})^{\prime},Y^{t,x,i}_{s},Z^{t,x,i}_{s})\big]ds-Z^{t,x,i}_{s}dW_{s},\quad s\in[t,T];\\
Y_{T}^{t,x,i}&=\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\big[\Phi((X_{T}^{0,x_{0}})^{\prime},X_{T}^{t,x})\big],\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then
6.3
implies that

Y_{s}^{t,x,1}=Y_{s}^{t,x,2},\quad\ t\leqslant s\leqslant T.

In particular, as

u^{1}(t,x)=Y_{t}^{t,x,1}=Y_{t}^{t,x,2}=u^{2}(t,x),\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.7
.

Theorem 6.6
establishes the relation between the solution of mean-field BSDEs and the viscosity solution of the nonlocal PDEs with quadratic growth, which extends the related result of Kobylanski
[
26
]
to the mean-field framework and extends the nonlinear Feynman-Kac formula of Buckdahn, Li, and Peng
[
9
]
with linear growth to that of quadratic growth.

7
Concluding Remark

We initiate the study of general mean-field BSDEs (
1.1
) and give the existence, uniqueness, and comparison results for one-dimensional mean-field BSDEs (
1.1
) with quadratic growth and with bounded terminal value by introducing some new ideas.
Besides, we obtain the convergence of particle systems for the mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) with quadratic growth and give the rate of convergence when generator
g
is independent of the law of
Z
. However, the convergence rate is still an open problem if the generator
g
depends on the law of
Z
.
Finally, in this framework, when the generator
g
depends on the expectation of the state process
(Y,Z)
, we used the mean-field BSDEs (
1.1
) to prove the existence and uniqueness of the viscosity solutions of the nonlocal PDEs (
6.3
), which extend the nonlinear Feynman-Kac formula of Buckdahn, Li, and Peng
[
9
]
to that of quadratic growth.
On the other hand, when the generator
g
depends on the distribution of
Z
, the existence and uniqueness of viscosity solutions of related PDEs are intriguing and challenging, and remains to be studied in the future.

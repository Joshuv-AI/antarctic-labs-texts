Nakajima’s creation operators and the Kirwan map
Jakub Koncki, Magdalena Zielenkiewicz

Abstract
. We consider the Hilbert scheme of points in the affine complex plane. We find explicit formulas for the Nakajima’s creation operators and their K \KTh -theoretic counterparts in terms of the Kirwan map. We obtain a description of the action of Nakajima’s creation operators on the Chern classes of the tautological bundle.



1.
Introduction

The Hilbert scheme of
n
points in the complex plane
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}:=\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
is a quasi-projective variety parametrizing zero-dimensional subschemes of degree
n
in the complex affine plane
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. It is a well-studied object, and a lot is known about its geometry. Many results on the Hilbert scheme of points in the plane have non-obvious applications elsewhere, from geometry
[
Boissiere2
,
NY
]
to algebra and representation theory
[
GorS1
,
Wei
,
BG
]
, mathematical physics
[
Carl
,
Gorsky
]
and combinatorics
[
Haiman2
,
Haiman
]
. While it has been intensively studied ever since the 1960’s, it is still an active research topic
[
OR
,
Pandha
,
Przez
]
and there remain open questions to be addressed.

Many connections between the Hilbert scheme of points in the plane and other branches of mathematics have been unearthed by the Nakajima–Grojnowski
[
Nak1
,
Gro
]
milestone result which endowed the sum of all the rational homology groups with a structure of a representation of the Heisenberg algebra. Such a structure can be obtained by constructing certain operators on homology, which satisfy the commutation relations of the Heisenberg algebra. In Nakajima’s construction such operators, called creation and annihilation operators, are given by correspondences defined by subvarieties in the nested Hilbert scheme. Originally defined as operators in homology, one can extend them to equivariant cohomology using a similar construction, see
[
Evain
]
. The situation is more complicated for
\KTh
-theory, as pointed out already by Nakajima (see
[
Nak1
, Question 8.35]
). There are, however, generalizations of the Nakajima–Grojnowski result to
\KTh
-theory.
In
[
FT
]
, the actions of the shuffle and the Ding-Iohara algebras are constructed on the equivariant
\KTh
-theory. In turn, in
[
SV
]
, a subalgebra of the convolution algebra in the equivariant
\KTh
-theory of
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
is studied and is identified with the elliptic Hall algebra.

Most of this paper is devoted to
\KTh
-theory of the Hilbert scheme of points in the plane, and the final chapters present implications of the
\KTh
-theoretical results to cohomology. While equivariant cohomology of the Hilbert scheme of points has been extensively studied (see e.g.
[
ES
,
Lehn
,
Evain
,
Cheah2
,
GS
]
), there remain questions which do not have a satisfactory answer. One such question, stated in
[
Nak1
, Question 9.6]
, is how do the creation operators act on the Chern classes of the tautological bundle on the Hilbert scheme. We present an answer to this question. A similar problem is considered in
[
Lehn
]
. There Chern classes of the tautological bundle are expressed in Nakajima’s basis. Results presented here and in
[
Lehn
]
are connected, yet not directly comparable, see Remark
for a detailed discussion.

Our result is an expression for the action of the Nakajima’s operators
\mathfrak{q}_{i}
on the characteristic classes of the tautological bundle
\mathcal{V}_{n}
in terms of the Kirwan map

\kappa:\mathbb{Z}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1}]^{\Sigma_{n}}\to\operatorname{H}^{\ast}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})\,.

Here
\Sigma_{n}
denotes the symmetric group and the map
\kappa
sends variables
x_{i}
to the Chern roots of the tautological bundle
\mathcal{V}_{n}
. Chern classes of
\mathcal{V}_{n}
are images of the elementary symmetric polynomials.
We prove a formula for their images under
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
, expressed in the Chern classes of the tautological
bundle
\mathcal{V}_{n+1}
as well as the power-sum polynomials of its Chern roots
{P^{n+1}_{k}}

\mathfrak{q}_{1}(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}_{n}))=\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{m}(m+1)\cdot c_{k-m}(\mathcal{V}_{n+1})\cdot{P^{n+1}_{m}}\,.

It turns out that the power sum basis of the symmetric polynomial ring is better suited for our formulas. Our main result is presented below.

The standard action of the two-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{2}
on
\mathbb{C}^{2}

\KTh

[

for cohomological computations,
[

]
for

[

for elliptic cohomology.

Partitions, Young diagrams and symmetric functions

Let

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{l})

\sum_{i=1}^{l}\lambda_{i}=n

\lambda\dashv n

\lambda
is a partition of
n
. We represent each partition by a Young diagram with
n

l
columns of lengths
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{l}
. We think of the boxes in the Young diagram as pairs of natural numbers, and draw the Young diagrams accordingly, e.g.

\lambda=(3,1)

(0,0)
, the top box in the first column is
(0,2)

(1,0)
. The motivation for this convention is that we want to identify boxes in the Young diagram with monomials in two variables

Remark 2.1
.

The arm and leg lengths of a box
\bullet
are typically denoted
a(\bullet),l(\bullet)
in the literature, and the partition
\lambda
is omitted in the notation. We choose to keep
\lambda
, as in many inductive arguments we consider the same box in different diagrams. We also use
b
for denoting the leg length, so that we can keep
i,j,k,l,m,n
as the standard choices for denoting natural numbers.

For arguments involving inductive procedures with Young diagrams, we often use the following constructions: adding or removing a box and removing the first column of the diagram. We use the following notation.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{l})
be a nonempty partition.

(1)

We denote by
\lambda[1]
the set of all Young diagrams which are obtained from
\lambda
by adding one box in such a way that the result is a Young diagram.

(2)

We denote by
\tilde{\lambda}
the partition obtained from
\lambda
by removing the first column, i.e.

\tilde{\lambda}=(\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{l})\,.

Let
\Sigma_{n}
be the permutation group of
n
elements. When we work with symmetric polynomials in
n+1
variables, we index the variables from
0
, so we consider elements of the ring
\mathbb{Z}[x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{\Sigma_{n+1}}
. We denote by

i

e_{i}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{0\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{i}\leq n}x_{j_{1}}\dots x_{j_{i}},

and by
p_{i}

i

p_{i}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}x_{j}^{i}\,.

In particular
for

p_{0}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}x_{j}^{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}1=n+1\,.

For a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l})
, we set

e_{\lambda}:=\prod_{i=1}^{l}e_{\lambda_{i}}\,,\qquad p_{\lambda}:=\prod_{i=1}^{l}p_{\lambda_{i}}\,.

Finally, we assume that

have coefficient
1
, so that

3x^{2}y^{3}

3.
Equivariant K-theory

Let
\mathbb{T}\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{\operatorname{rank}\mathbb{T}}
be an algebraic torus. We call elements of the free abelian group

\Hom(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})
characters.
Let
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{T})
denote the representation ring of the torus
\mathbb{T}
. Every finitely dimensional
\mathbb{T}
-representation decomposes as a direct sum of one-dimensional representations, therefore

(1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{T})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\Hom(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})]\,.

Let
X
be a quasiprojective complex
\mathbb{T}
-variety. The algebraic
\KTh
-theory of
X
, denoted
\KTh(X)
, is
the Grothendieck group of isomorphism classes of algebraic vector bundles on
X
. The equivariant algebraic
\KTh
-theory of
X
, denoted
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
, is
the Grothendieck group of isomorphism classes of
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant algebraic vector bundles on
X
. We do not distinguish in the notation between a bundle and its
\KTh
-theory class, when it is clear from the context.

It follows from (
1
) that the equivariant K-theory of a point is isomorphic to the ring of Laurent polynomials

\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\simeq\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{T})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\Hom(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})]\simeq\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm},\dots,t_{\operatorname{rank}\mathbb{T}}^{\pm}]\,,

where
t_{1},\dots,t_{\operatorname{rank}\mathbb{T}}
are the coordinate characters. The equivariant
\KTh
-theory of an arbitrary
\mathbb{T}
-variety is a
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)
-module.

Remark 3.1
.

In this paper we also work with torus equivariant cohomology
\operatorname{H}^{\ast}_{\mathbb{T}}
. Let
t_{i}
be a coordinate character pf
\mathbb{T}
and
\mathbb{C}_{t_{i}}
the corresponding
\mathbb{T}
-representation. For simplicity we use
t_{i}
to also refer to the class of this representation in K-theory and its first Chern class in cohomology. With this convention, we have

\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\simeq\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm},\dots,t_{\operatorname{rank}\mathbb{T}}^{\pm}]\,,\qquad\operatorname{H}^{\ast}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\simeq\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\dots,t_{\operatorname{rank}\mathbb{T}}]\,.

Let
S\subset\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)
be the multiplicative system consisting of all the nonzero elements. We denote the localized K-theory ring of
X
by
S^{-1}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
. The localization theorem
[
Tho
, Theorem 2.1]
implies that the restriction map induces an isomorphism

S^{-1}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\simeq S^{-1}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X^{\mathbb{T}})\,.

Let
X
be a smooth projective
\mathbb{T}
-variety such that the fixed point set
X^{\mathbb{T}}
is finite.
Then the equivariant K-theory of
X
is a free
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)
-module.
In this case we have an inclusion of rings

\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\subset S^{-1}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\simeq S^{-1}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X^{\mathbb{T}})\,.

Therefore, restriction to the fixed point set induces the inclusion

\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\hookrightarrow\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X^{\mathbb{T}})\simeq\bigoplus_{x\in X^{\mathbb{T}}}\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm},\dots,t_{\operatorname{rank}\mathbb{T}}^{\pm}]\,.

Remark 3.2
.

In the above reasoning one may weaken the assumption that
X
is projective. It is enough to assume that there exists a one parameter subgroup of
\mathbb{T}
such that the Białynicki-Birula cells (see
[
B-B1
]
) cover
X
. The proof is an induction on Białynicki-Birula skeleta, similar to
[
FRW
, Section 9]
.

For an equivariant complex vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over a
\mathbb{T}
-variety
X
we define its
\KTh
-theoretic Euler class as

\operatorname{eu}(\mathcal{E}):=\sum_{k=0}^{{\rm rk}(\mathcal{E})}(-1)^{k}[\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{E}^{*}]\in\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\,.

It is a multiplicative class such that for
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant line bundles
\mathcal{L}
we have

\operatorname{eu}(\mathcal{L}):=1-\mathcal{L}^{*}\,.

The Lefschetz-Riemann-Roch formula (LRR for short) allows to compute push-forwards in equivariant
\KTh
-theory using restriction to the fixed point set. Let us recall it here.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
Tho
, Theorem 3.5]
and
[
CG
, Theorem 5.11.7]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth
\mathbb{T}
-varieties. Suppose that the fixed point sets
X^{\mathbb{T}}
and
Y^{\mathbb{T}}
are finite. Let
p:X\to Y
be an equivariant proper morphism.
For a fixed point
y\in Y^{\mathbb{T}}
and an arbitrary class
\alpha\in S^{-1}K_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
we have

\frac{(p_{*}\alpha)_{|y}}{\operatorname{eu}(T_{y}Y)}=\sum_{x\in p^{-1}(y)\cap X^{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\alpha_{|x}}{\operatorname{eu}(T_{x}X)}\in S^{-1}K_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\,.

Let
m
be a natural number and
X
a
\mathbb{T}
-variety. The
m_{th}
Adams operator
\psi^{m}
is a map

\psi^{m}:\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\to\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\,,

which was introduced in the non equivariant setting in
[
Adams
]
,
see
[
Koeck
]
for equivariant version and
[
RR
]
for a survey. Let us recall its basic properties

Proposition 3.4
.

•

The Adams operator
\psi^{m}
is a ring homomorphism.

•

For an equivariant line bundle
\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{\mathbb{T}}(X)
we have

\psi^{m}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{L}^{m}\,.

•

The Adams operator is a natural transformation with respect to pullbacks, i.e. for any
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant map
f:Y\to X
we have

f^{*}\circ\psi^{m}=\psi^{m}\circ f^{*}\,.

The Adams operators usually do not commute with the pushforwards, yet there is a Riemann-Roch type formula.

Definition 3.5

(
[
RR
, Chapter I §6]
)
.

The Bott’s cannibalistic class
\theta^{m}
is a multiplicative characteristic class of vector bundles such that

\theta^{m}(\mathcal{L})=\frac{1-\mathcal{L}^{m}}{1-\mathcal{L}}\in\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\,,

for any
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant line bundle
\mathcal{L}
.

Theorem 3.6

(Adams-Riemann-Roch
[
Koeck
, Theorem 4.5]
,
[
RR
, Theorem 7.6]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth
\mathbb{T}
-varieties and
\pi:X\to Y
be an equivariant proper morphism. Denote by
T_{\pi}=TX-f^{*}TY
the virtual tangent bundle to
\pi
.
For a class
\alpha\in K_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
we have

\pi_{*}\left(\frac{\psi^{m}(\alpha)}{\theta^{m}(T^{*}_{\pi})}\right)=\psi^{m}(\pi_{*}(\alpha))\,.

Remark 3.7
.

The above equality holds in the localised equivariant K-theory of
Y
with rational coefficients. Localisation is necessary to make sense of dividing by the Bott class.

4.1.
Definition and torus action

Let
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}:=\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
be the Hilbert scheme of
n
points in the complex plane. It is a quasi-projective variety parametrising zero-dimensional subschemes of
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
of length
n
. Set-theoretically,
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
can be identified with the set of ideals
I\lhd\mathbb{C}[x,y]
such that the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\mathbb{C}[x,y]/I
has dimension
n
, i.e.,

\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}:=\{I\lhd\mathbb{C}[x,y]|\,\dim\mathbb{C}[x,y]/I=n\}\,.

The Hilbert scheme comes with a universal flat family

F_{n}\subset\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}\times\mathbb{C}^{2}\,,

whose fibre over a subscheme
Z\in\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
is
Z
, treated as a subscheme in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. The push-forward of the sheaf of regular functions on
F_{n}
along the projection
p:F_{n}\to H_{n}
defines (a sheaf of sections of) a rank
n
vector bundle on
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}

\mathcal{V}_{n}=p_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{F_{n}}\in\operatorname{Vect}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})\,.

We call it the
universal rank
n
vector bundle on
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
. The fibre of the universal bundle
\mathcal{V}_{n}
over
I
is the
n
-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{C}[x,y]/I
.

Let
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{2}
be the algebraic torus. The standard
\mathbb{T}
–action on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
given by

(t_{1},t_{2})\cdot(x,y):=(t_{1}x,t_{2}y)

induces an action on polynomials
f\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]
, via

Remark 4.1
.

It is commonplace not to distinguish in the notation between the partition

The Hilbert scheme

is a smooth irreducible quasi-projective variety of dimension

]
. The tangent space at the point

can be described as the vector space of

At a fixed point

equips this

. Its weights can be read from the corresponding Young diagram. Denote by

the coordinate characters of the torus
\mathbb{T}
.
Then

Each pair of integers

and we call

Remark 4.2
.

Note that with the above convention each box

4.2.
Equivariant cohomology and K-theory

Consider a one-parameter subgroup
\sigma:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{T}
given by

\sigma(t)=(t,t^{N})\,,

for a sufficiently big integer
N
. The fixed point sets
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}}
and
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\sigma}

coincide. Moreover, the Białynicki-Birula cells cover the Hilbert scheme, see
[
ESBBdec
]
or
[
YiNing
, Lemma 1.2.4]
. Therefore,
for nonequivariant cohomology and
\KTh
-theory we have the following isomorphisms of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules

\KTh(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})\simeq\operatorname{H}^{\ast}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}};\mathbb{Z})\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda\dashv n}\mathbb{Z}\,.

Analogous result holds in the equivariant case. Denote by
q,t
the coordinate characters of the torus
\mathbb{T}
. Then

\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\simeq\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1},t^{\pm 1}]\,,\qquad\operatorname{H}^{\ast}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\simeq\mathbb{Z}[q,t]\,.

Białynicki-Birula decomposition implies that
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda\dashv n}\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm},t^{\pm}]
as
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)
-modules and
\operatorname{H}^{\ast}_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}};\mathbb{Z})\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda\dashv n}\mathbb{Z}[q,t]
as
\operatorname{H}_{\mathbb{T}}^{\ast}(pt)
-modules. In particular
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
is a free
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)
-module and
\operatorname{H}^{\ast}_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
is a free
\operatorname{H}^{\ast}_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)
-module.

Let

be the inclusion of the fixed point

into the Hilbert scheme. Given a class

, its

is its pullback along this inclusion, i.e.

We denote it by

or
\mathcal{E}_{|\lambda}
.
The localization theorems (see Section
3
) and the Białynicki-Birula theorem (see Remark
3.2
) imply the following.

Proposition 4.3
.

Restriction to the fixed point variety induces an inclusion of rings

\displaystyle\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}})\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda\dashv n}\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm},t^{\pm}]\,,

\displaystyle\operatorname{H}^{\ast}_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\ast}_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}})\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda\dashv n}\mathbb{Z}[q,t]\,.

As a direct consequence, in order to prove equality of two classes in

or
\operatorname{H}^{\ast}_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})

are equal.

5.
Nested Hilbert Scheme

5.1.
Definition and torus action

The nested Hilbert scheme

\Delta_{J}
is obtained from

by adding one box. We represent such pairs of diagrams by shading a box in the bigger diagram, representing the added box.

Therefore, a fixed point in

of

and a choice of a corner box

5.2.
Restriction to a one-dimensional subtorus

Consider a one-dimensional coordinate subtorus of
\mathbb{T}
:

{\mathbb{T}_{y}}=\{(1,t_{2})\in\mathbb{T}\}\,.

A reasoning analogous to the one in Section 1.2 of
[

]
implies the following description of the fixed point set

Proposition 5.1
.

The fixed point set
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n,n+1}}^{\mathbb{T}_{y}}
is a disjoint union of affine spaces. Each affine space contains exactly one fixed point of the big torus
\mathbb{T}
.

Corollary 5.2
.

The restriction map

\KTh_{\mathbb{T}_{y}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n,n+1}}^{\mathbb{T}_{y}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}_{y}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n,n+1}}^{\mathbb{T}})

The Białynicki-Birula decomposition and the localization theorem imply the following result.

Proposition 5.3
.

The equivariant
\KTh
-theory of the nested Hilbert scheme
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}_{y}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n,n+1}})
is a free
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}_{y}}(pt)
-module.
The restriction to the fixed point set induces an inclusion of rings

\KTh_{\mathbb{T}_{y}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n,n+1}})\hookrightarrow\KTh_{\mathbb{T}_{y}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n,n+1}}^{\mathbb{T}_{y}})\,.

Corollary 5.4
.

The composition

\KTh_{\mathbb{T}_{y}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n,n+1}})\hookrightarrow\KTh_{\mathbb{T}_{y}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n,n+1}}^{\mathbb{T}_{y}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}_{y}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n,n+1}}^{\mathbb{T}})

As a direct consequence, in order to prove equality of two classes in
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}_{y}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n,n+1}})

\mathbb{T}
are equal.

Abstract
. The negative cyclic homology for a differential graded algebra over the rational field has a quotient of the Hochschild homology as a direct summand if the S S -action is trivial. With this fact, we show that the string bracket in the sense of Chas and Sullivan is reduced to the loop product followed by the BV operator on the loop homology provided the given manifold is BV-exact . The reduction is indeed derived from the equivalence between the BV-exactness and the triviality of the S S -action. Moreover, it is proved that a Lie bracket on the loop cohomology of the classifying space of a connected compact Lie group possesses the same reduction. By using these results, we consider the non-triviality of string brackets. We also show that a simply-connected space with positive weights is BV-exact. Furthermore, the higher BV-exactness is discussed featuring the cobar-type Eilenberg-Moore spectral sequence.

In order to introduce uniformly the loop product due to Chas and Sullivan and the dual loop coproduct due to Chataur and Menichi, we recall the notion of a Gorenstein DGA introduced by Félix, Halperin and Thomas in
[
FHT_G
]
.

Let
A
be an augmented DGA over
{\mathbb{K}}
. We call
A
a
Gorenstein algebra
of dimension
d

if

(3.4)

\dim\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}({\mathbb{K}},A)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }*\neq d,\\
1&\text{if }*=d.\end{cases}

Here
\operatorname{Ext}
is defined by using semifree resolutions;
see Appendix of
[
FHT_G
]
for details.
A path-connected space
M
is called a
Gorenstein space

of dimension
d
if the polynomial de Rham algebra
A_{PL}(M)
is a Gorenstein algebra of dimension
d
.

The result
[
FHT_G
, Theorem 3.1]
implies that a simply-connected Poincaré duality space, for example
a simply-connected closed orientable manifold of dimension
d
, is a Gorenstein space of dimension
d
.
It follows from
[
FHT_G
, Proposition 3.2]
that the classifying space
BG
of a connected compact Lie group
G
is also a
Gorenstein space of dimension
-\dim G
.
The following result due to Félix and Thomas is a key to defining the loop product and the loop coproduct on the loop homology of a Gorenstein space.

Theorem 3.5
.

(
[
F-T
, Theorem 12]
)

be a simply-connected Gorenstein space of dimension

whose cohomology with coefficients in

is of finite type. Then

for any integer

,
where
A_{PL}(M)
is considered an
A_{PL}(M^{n})
-module via the diagonal map

under the isomorphism

Remark 3.13
.

The product
\bullet
on
{\mathbb{H}}_{*}(LM)

is associative and graded commutative if
M
is a simply-connected Poincaré duality space (see
[
K-M-N
, Proposition 2.7]
).
So is the product
\odot
on
\mathbb{H}^{*}(LM)

if
M
is the classifying space
BG
of a connected Lie group
G

(see
[
C-M
]
and
[

, Theorem B.1]
).
Moreover, so are both of
\bullet
and
\odot
if
M
is a Gorenstein space with

(see
[
Naito
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
W3
, Theorem 1.5]
).

Remark 3.14
.

By the same fashion as above, a Gorenstein space is defined on an arbitrary field
{\mathbb{K}}
.
Then Theorem
3.5
remains true after replacing

with the singular cochain algebra of

with coefficients in
{\mathbb{K}}
. That is the original assertion in
[
F-T_ST
]
. Moreover, the constructions of the loop product and the loop coproduct are applicable to the Gorenstein space

; that is, those products are defined
on the singular cohomology of

with coefficient in
{\mathbb{K}}
; see
[
F-T_ST
]
.
However, we only use such an algebra defined on a field of characteristic zero for our purpose.

\displaystyle\Delta(a\odot b\odot c)

\displaystyle-\Delta(a)\odot b\odot c-(-1)^{\mathopen{\parallel}a\mathclose{\parallel}}a\odot\Delta(b)\odot c-(-1)^{\mathopen{\parallel}a\mathclose{\parallel}+\mathopen{\parallel}b\mathclose{\parallel}}a\odot b\odot\Delta(c),

where

stands for the degree of
an element

\displaystyle\{\pi(x),\pi(y)\}=(-1)^{\mathopen{\parallel}x\mathclose{\parallel}-1}\Delta(\pi(x)\odot\pi(y))=-\pi[x,y]\ \ \text{and}

\displaystyle-(-1)^{\mathopen{\parallel}x\mathclose{\parallel}+\mathopen{\parallel}y\mathclose{\parallel}}[x,[y,z]]

(4.7)

In order to prove the “only if” part of Theorem

Definition 4.13

Let

be a sequence of complexes and chain maps (of arbitrary degrees).

i)

The sequence is

if the sequence of cohomology

ii)

The sequence is
Z

if the sequence of modules of cycles

iii)

[

The sequence is

(at

)
if the sequence is exact (as a sequence of underlying graded modules),

H
-exact and
Z
-exact.

iv)

The sequence is
weakly proper exact
at
M_{2}

if the sequence is exact and
H
-exact.

The following lemma is useful to prove the proper exactness from the weak proper exactness of a given sequence.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
M_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{0}}M_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}M_{2}\xrightarrow{f_{2}}M_{3}\xrightarrow{f_{3}}M_{4}
be a sequence of complexes which is
proper exact at
M_{2}
and
weakly proper exact at
M_{1}
and
M_{3}
.
Then it is proper exact also at
M_{3}
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we assume that the degrees of the chain maps are zero.
We show that
\operatorname{Ker}Z(f_{3})\subset\operatorname{Im}Z(f_{2})
.
For any
x_{3}
in
\operatorname{Ker}Z(f_{3})
, there exists an element
y_{2}\in M_{2}

such that
f_{2}(y_{2})=x_{3}
by the exactness at
M_{3}
.
By the proper exactness at
M_{2}
,
we see that
dy_{2}=f_{1}(y_{1})
for some
y_{1}\in Z(M_{1})
.
Since
H(f_{1})[y_{1}]=[dy_{2}]=0
,
it follows from the
H
-exactness at
M_{1}

that
y_{1}-f_{0}y_{0}=dz
for some
[y_{0}]\in H(M_{0})
and

.
It is readily seen that
f_{2}(y_{2}-f_{1}z)=f_{2}y_{2}=x_{3}
and

. We have the result.
∎

It is proved that the weak proper exactness for a long sequence yields the proper exactness.

Proposition 4.15
.

A weakly proper exact sequence
0\to M_{0}\to M_{1}\to M_{2}\to\cdots
starting from

is always proper exact.

Proof.

Since the sequence

is weakly proper exact at

and proper exact at

4.14
that the sequence is proper exact at
M_{0}
.
Similarly, the sequence

0\to 0\to M_{0}\to M_{1}\to M_{2}

gives proper exactness at
M_{1}
.
By repeating this argument, we can prove the proper exactness at
M_{n}
for all

.
∎

Remark 4.16

By the same argument as in the proof above,

which asserts that

5.1.
Dual string cobrackets for classifying spaces

We begin by considering the string bracket for the classifying space of a connected Lie group of rank one.

Example 5.1

The result
[

\mathbb{H}^{*}(LBG;\mathbb{Q})

\mathcal{H}^{*}(LBSU(2)):=H_{S^{1}}^{*+3+1}(LBSU(2);\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{*}:=\mathcal{H}^{*}(LBSU(2))

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle(\widetilde{HH}_{*}(\Omega)/\operatorname{Im}\Delta)_{-*-3-2}\oplus(\mathbb{Q}[u])_{-*-3-1}

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\{x,x^{2},\ldots,x^{n},\ldots\}\oplus\mathbb{Q}\{1,u,u^{2},\ldots,u^{k},\ldots\}

as vector spaces, where
\Omega
denotes
the Sullivan minimal model for

\mathopen{|}x^{n}\mathclose{|}=4n-5

\mathopen{|}1\mathclose{|}=-4
for

and

The formula in
[

Next we consider the dual string cobracket for the classifying space of
G
with arbitrary rank.

Proposition 5.2
.

For each
n
, the
n
-fold dual string cobracket
[{\mathcal{H}},[{\mathcal{H}},\ldots,[{\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{H}}]\cdots]]
is non-trivial on
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}:=H^{*+\dim G+1}_{S^{1}}(LBG;{\mathbb{K}})

Proof.

For the case where
\operatorname{rank}G=1

5.1
above implies the result. We assume that
N:=\operatorname{rank}G\geq 2
.
Recall the result
[
K-M
, Theorem 4.3]
which asserts that the loop cohomology
{\mathbb{H}}^{*}(LBG):=H^{*+\dim G}(LBG)
is isomorphic to the tensor product of algebra
H^{*}(BG)\otimes H_{-*}(G)={\mathbb{K}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}]\otimes\wedge(x_{1}^{\vee},\ldots,x_{N}^{\vee})
equipped with the BV operator
\Delta
given by

\Delta(x_{i}^{\vee}x_{j}^{\vee})=\Delta(y_{i}y_{j})=\Delta(x_{i}^{\vee})=\Delta(y_{i})=0

\Delta(y_{i}x_{j}^{\vee})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}0\ \ \text{if}\ i\neq j,\\

(5.4)

\Delta(y_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots y_{N}^{k_{N}}x_{i_{1}}^{\vee}\cdots x_{i_{s}}^{\vee})=\sum_{1\leq j\leq s}(-1)^{d_{j}}k_{i_{j}}y_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{j}}^{k_{i_{j}}-1}\cdots y_{N}^{k_{N}}x_{i_{1}}^{\vee}\cdots\widehat{x_{i_{j}}^{\vee}}\cdots x_{i_{s}}^{\vee},

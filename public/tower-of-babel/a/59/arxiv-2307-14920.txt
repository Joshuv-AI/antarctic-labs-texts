Confronting axial-vector form factor from lattice QCD with MINERvA antineutrino-proton data Preprint: LA-UR-23-28229,CETUP-2023-001
Oleksandr Tomalak, Rajan Gupta, Tanmoy Bhattacharya

Abstract
We compare recent MINERvA antineutrino-hydrogen charged-current measurements to phenomenological predictions of the axial-vector form factor based on fits to all available electron scattering and deuterium bubble-chamber data and to representative lattice-QCD (LQCD) determination by the PNDME Collaboration. While there is 1 1 – 2 ​ σ 2\sigma agreement in the cross section with MINERvA data for each bin in Q 2 Q^{2} , we identify three regions with different relevance and opportunity for LQCD predictions. For Q 2 ≲ 0.2 ​ GeV 2 Q^{2}\lesssim 0.2\penalty\ \mathrm{GeV}^{2} , the phenomenological extractions have large number of data points and LQCD is competitive, while MINERvA data have large errors. For 0.2 ​ GeV 2 ≲ Q 2 ≲ 1 ​ GeV 2 0.2\penalty\ \mathrm{GeV}^{2}\lesssim Q^{2}\lesssim 1\penalty\ \mathrm{GeV}^{2} , LQCD is competitive with the MINERvA determination, and both give values larger than from phenomenological extraction. For Q 2 > 1 ​ GeV 2 Q^{2}>1\penalty\ \mathrm{GeV}^{2} , the MINERvA data are the most precise. Our analysis indicates that with improving precision of MINERvA-like experiments and LQCD data, the uncertainty in the nucleon axial-vector form factor will be steadily reduced.

Removal of contributions due to excited states. All correlation functions calculated on the lattice get contributions from all excited states that couple to, and are thus created, by the interpolating operators used. This problem can be severe for nucleons especially if towers of multihadron states, starting with the

Satisfying, to within the expected size of discretization errors, the partially conserved axial current (PCAC) relation between the three form factors, axial

To summarize, we have compared (anti)neutrino-nucleon charged-current elastic cross sections based on fits to the well-known deuterium bubble-chamber data and new measurements by the MINERvA Collaboration with a theoretical analysis using a representative LQCD calculation of the nucleon axial-vector form factor. We have identified three regions of
Q^{2}
in which to assess the strengths and weaknesses of experimental measurements and lattice calculations. We anticipate that LQCD will provide the best estimates for the axial-vector form factor for
Q^{2}\lesssim 0.2\penalty\ \mathrm{GeV}^{2}
. The reason is that with the current LQCD method, as the lattice size is increased at fixed
a
or as
a
is decreased, the value of
Q^{2}_{\rm max}
at which data with good statistical precision can be obtained shrinks to
Q^{2}_{\max}\lesssim 0.2
GeV
2
. Over the region
0.2\penalty\ \mathrm{GeV}^{2}\lesssim Q^{2}\lesssim 1\penalty\ \mathrm{GeV}^{2}
, both LQCD and antineutrino on hydrogen experiments will provide increasingly precise data that will lead to growing confidence in both. For
Q^{2}>1\penalty\ \mathrm{GeV}^{2}
, experimental measurements are the best near-term option since novel lattice methodology is needed to control the systematics that grow with
Q^{2}
.

We anticipate significant progress in LQCD data over the next five years due to increase in both statistics that will allow control over excited states contributions through the inclusion of three or more states in the spectral decomposition of correlation functions
Jang et al. 2020
;
Jang et al. 2023
, and the values of
\{a,M_{\pi}\}
at which simulations are done, which will improve the chiral-continuum fits used to remove the associated systematics. However, to reach percent-level accuracy, novel methods
Bali et al. 2016
;
Gao et al. 2021
are needed to remove both excited-state contributions and discretization errors with requisite precision.

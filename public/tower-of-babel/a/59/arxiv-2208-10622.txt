Associative Submanifolds of Squashed 3 3 -Sasakian Manifolds
Gavin Ball, Jesse Madnick

Abstract
Every compact 3 3 -Sasakian 7 7 -manifold M M admits a canonical 2 2 -parameter family of co-closed G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -structures φ a , b \varphi_{a,b} for a , b > 0 a,b>0 , as well as a foliation by φ a , b \varphi_{a,b} -associative 3 3 -folds whose leaf space X X is a positive quaternion-Kähler 4 4 -orbifold. We prove that associative 3 3 -folds in ( M , φ a , b ) (M,\varphi_{a,b}) that are ruled by a certain type of geodesic are in correspondence with pseudo-holomorphic curves in the almost-complex 8 8 -manifold Z × S 2 Z\times S^{2} , where Z Z is the twistor space of X X equipped with its strict nearly-Kähler structure. As an application, we construct infinitely many topological types of non-trivial, compact associative 3 3 -folds in the squashed 7 7 -spheres ( S 7 , φ a , b ) (S^{7},\varphi_{a,b}) and squashed exceptional Aloff-Wallach spaces ( N 1 , 1 , φ a , b ) (N_{1,1},\varphi_{a,b}) . Topologically, our examples are circle bundles over a genus g g surface, for any g ≥ 0 g\geq 0 .

Let
(M,\varphi)

7

\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi

3
-fold

3

Associative submanifolds are conjectured to play an important role in defining an enumerative invariant of torsion-free

-structures counting
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instantons
[

\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures
\varphi_{t}

3
-folds are difficult to construct, making conjectures hard to test. In this work, we construct examples of compact
3
-folds in squashed 3-Sasakian 7-manifolds

that are associative with respect to a
2
-parameter family of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures.

A second motivation for this work comes from
\mathrm{Spin}(7)

\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures, there is a distinguished class of

\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures

\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-manifold. Associative submanifolds in nearly-parallel
7
-manifolds

provide models for conically singular Cayley manifolds in the cone
(\mathrm{C}(M),g_{\mathrm{C}})=(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times M,dr^{2}+r^{2}g)
.

Examples of associative submanifolds in nearly-parallel
7

[
15

, Lotay
[

]
, Kawai
[

On

In this work, we study associative
3
-folds in
(M,\varphi_{a,b})
, where
M
is compact. The simplest examples are the
canonical leaves
, i.e., those associatives that are tangent to
\mathsf{A}
. Indeed, the distribution
\mathsf{A}\subset TM
is integrable, yielding a
canonical foliation

\mathcal{F}_{A}
of
M
. The canonical leaves turn out to be totally-geodesic,
\varphi_{a,b}
-associative
3
-folds in

with constant positive sectional curvature. It is known that the leaf space

is a positive quaternion-Kähler
4
-orbifold, and that the projection

is a principal
\mathrm{SU}(2)
- or
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-orbibundle
[

\lx@sectionsign

3
-spheres.

To obtain new examples, we consider a more general geometric ansatz, studying those associatives in
(M,\varphi_{a,b})
that are ruled by
Hopf circles
, those closed geodesics that lie in a canonical leaf. In other words, a Hopf circle is an orbit of the locally free
S^{1}
-action on
M
generated by the the Reeb field
A_{w}:=w_{1}I_{1}(\partial_{r})+w_{2}I_{2}(\partial_{r})+w_{3}I_{3}(\partial_{r})

w=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})\in S^{2}
. Each quotient space
M/\langle A_{w}\rangle
is diffeomorphic to the twistor space
Z
of
X
, and the projection
p_{w}\colon M\to Z
is a principal
S^{1}
-orbibundle
[
7
,
\lx@sectionsign
7.5.1,
\lx@sectionsign
13.3.1]
. For example, when
M=S^{7}
, we have
Z=\mathbb{CP}^{3}
, and each
p_{w}\colon S^{7}\to\mathbb{CP}^{3}
is a complex Hopf fibration.

The space of Hopf circles in
M
is diffeomorphic to
Z\times S^{2}
, so any Hopf-ruled
3
-dimensional submanifold of

(not necessarily associative) can be viewed as a surface in
Z\times S^{2}
. Conversely, any surface
\Sigma\subset Z\times S^{2}
gives a corresponding Hopf-ruled
3
-fold
\Gamma(\Sigma)\subset M
. We then ask which surfaces in
Z\times S^{2}
yield
associative
Hopf-ruled
3
-folds in
M
. The Correspondence Theorem
1.1
provides the answer: it is the
J
-holomorphic curves in
Z\times S^{2}
. Here,
J
is the product almost-complex structure on
Z\times S^{2}
, where
Z
carries its standard strict nearly-Kähler structure.

Recalling that that
Z\to X
is an
S^{2}
-bundle, we view
Z\times S^{2}\to X
as an
(S^{2}\times S^{2})
-bundle in the obvious way. We can now state the result:

Theorem 1.1

(Correspondence Theorem)
.

Let
(M^{7},\varphi_{a,b})
be a compact squashed
3
-Sasakian
7
-manifold, where
a,b>0
are fixed. Let
Z
be the twistor space of
X:=M/\mathcal{F}_{A}
.

(a)

Every Hopf-ruled associative
3
-fold in
(M^{7},\varphi_{a,b})
is locally of the form
\Gamma(\Sigma)
for some
J
-holomorphic curve
\Sigma\subset Z\times S^{2}
.

(b)

Let
\Sigma\subset Z\times S^{2}
be a
J
-holomorphic curve.

1.

If
\Sigma
does not lie in an
(S^{2}\times S^{2})
-fiber, then there exists a discrete set
D\subset\Sigma
such that
\Gamma(\Sigma-D)\subset M
is a Hopf-ruled associative
3
-fold.

2.

If
\Sigma
lies in an
(S^{2}\times S^{2})
-fiber, then
\Gamma(\Sigma)
is a subset of a canonical leaf.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
(b) gives more than the statement. Indeed, let
\Sigma_{g}
be a compact surface of genus

. In
\lx@sectionsign
4.4
, we observe that if

, the surface

is a
J
-holomorphic curve that does not lie in an
(S^{2}\times S^{2})
-fiber and has

is a compact associative
3
-fold in

S^{1}
-bundle over

As an application, we consider the case of

equipped with any of its

.

Fix
a,b>0
. For every

, there exists a non-trivial compact associative

Note that non-trivial compact

-associative

[

\widehat{\varphi}

\mathrm{Spin}(7)

Similarly, we may apply our theory to

Every compact Riemann surface

.

Fix

1.1
Discussion: A Geometric View of the Correspondence Theorem

We wish to explain in heuristic terms the geometric idea behind the Correspondence Theorem
1.1
in the case of the
7
-sphere equipped with one of the co-closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures
\varphi_{a,b}
. Viewing
M=S^{7}\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}
and
X=S^{4}\cong\mathbb{HP}^{1}
, note that each fiber of the quaternionic Hopf fibration
h\colon S^{7}\to S^{4}
is a
3
-sphere given by the intersection of
S^{7}
with a quaternionic line
H
. There is an
S^{2}
-family of identifications
w\colon H\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathbb{C}^{2}
, each of which yields a complex Hopf fibration
p_{w}\colon S^{7}\to\mathbb{CP}^{3}
. A Hopf circle in
S^{7}
is an
S^{1}
-fiber
p_{w}^{-1}(z)
for some fixed
w\in S^{2}
and
z\in\mathbb{CP}^{3}
.

Now, for every immersed pseudo-holomorphic curve
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{CP}^{3}
and every fixed
w\in S^{2}
, the pre-image
p_{w}^{-1}(f(\Sigma))\subset S^{7}
is a trivial associative
3
-fold in
S^{7}
, where “pseudo-holomorphic” is meant with respect to the strict nearly-Kähler structure on
\mathbb{CP}^{3}
. A natural question, then, is whether one can generalize the class of trivial associatives by allowing the ruling direction
w\in S^{2}
to vary with
z\in\Sigma
in some fashion. In other words, which pairs of “directrix surface”
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{CP}^{3}
and “ruling function”
w\colon\Sigma\to S^{2}
result in an immersed Hopf-ruled
3
-fold

\bigcup_{z\in\Sigma}p^{-1}_{w(z)}(f(\Sigma))\subset S^{7}

(1.1)

that is associative? Theorem
1.1
answers this by relating
\varphi_{a,b}
-associatives in
S^{7}
of the form (
1.1
) to pairs
(f,w)\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{CP}^{3}\times S^{2}
consisting of a pseudo-holomorphic map
f
and holomorphic map
w
for which
\text{rank}(f,w)=2
.

The technical point is that certain pairs of directrix surface
f
and ruling function
w
yield Hopf-ruled
3
-folds
\Gamma(f,w)\subset S^{7}
that are not immersed. This sort of issue is endemic to the theory of ruled submanifolds, occurring even in the classical case of ruled surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Indeed, while one would like to say that any function
\Gamma\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
of the form
\Gamma(s,t):=\mathbf{f}(s)+t\mathbf{w}(s)
with
\mathbf{f}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{w}=0
and
\|\mathbf{w}\|=1
parametrizes a ruled surface, certain pairs of (
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{w}
) result in degenerations. For example, taking
\mathbf{f}(s)=\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{w}(s)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\cos(s),\sin(s),1)
, the resulting map
\Gamma
parameterizes a cone in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, but is not an immersion on
\{(s,t)\colon t=0\}
. In our situation, assuming that
f
is pseudo-holomorphic, that
w
is holomorphic, and that the image of
(f,w)
doesn’t lie in an
(S^{2}\times S^{2})
-fiber, Theorem
1.1
(b) shows that one only need remove a discrete set
D
from
\Sigma
to obtain an immersed Hopf-ruled associative
\Gamma(\Sigma-D)\subset S^{7}
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-Structures and Associative
3
-folds

Let
M^{7}
be a smooth
7
-manifold. A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
on
M
is a
3
-form
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
such that at each
x\in M
, the symmetric bilinear form
B_{\varphi}|_{x}\colon\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T^{*}_{x}M)\to\Lambda^{7}(T^{*}_{x}M)
given by
B_{\varphi}(u,v):=\textstyle\frac{1}{6}(\iota_{u}\varphi)\wedge(\iota_{v}\varphi)\wedge\varphi
is definite. This is equivalent to the condition that at each
x\in M
, there exists a frame
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{7})
at
x
for which

\varphi|_{x}=e^{123}+e^{145}+e^{167}+e^{246}-e^{257}-e^{347}-e^{356}

where
(e^{1},\ldots,e^{7})
is the dual coframe and
e^{ijk}:=e^{i}\wedge e^{j}\wedge e^{k}
. It is well-known that a
7
-manifold admits a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure if and only if it is orientable and spin. A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
naturally induces a Riemannian metric
g_{\varphi}
and orientation
\mathrm{vol}_{\varphi}\in\Omega^{7}(M)
via

\displaystyle\textstyle g_{\varphi}(u,v)\,\mathrm{vol}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\frac{1}{6}(\iota_{u}\varphi)\wedge(\iota_{v}\varphi)\wedge\varphi

Two
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures
\varphi
,
\widehat{\varphi}
on
M
are called
isometric
if
g_{\varphi}=g_{\widehat{\varphi}}
.

A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
is said to be
co-closed
if
d\ast\varphi=0
. All of the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures considered in this work will be co-closed. If
\varphi
is co-closed, then it is well-known that

d\varphi=\tau_{0}\ast\!\varphi+\ast\tau_{3}

for a unique function
\tau_{0}\in\Omega^{0}(M)
and
3
-form
\tau_{3}\in\Omega^{3}_{27}(M)=\Gamma(\Lambda^{3}_{27}(M))
, where here

\Lambda^{3}_{27}(M):=\left\{\gamma\in\Lambda^{3}(T^{*}M)\colon\gamma\wedge\varphi=0\text{ and }\gamma\wedge\ast\varphi=0\right\}\!.

A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi
is called
nearly-parallel
if
d\varphi=\lambda\ast\!\varphi
for some for some non-zero constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. It is clear that every nearly-parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is co-closed. If
\varphi
is a nearly-parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure, then the induced metric
g_{\varphi}
is Einstein with
\text{Scal}(g_{\varphi})=\frac{21}{8}\lambda^{2}>0
.

An associative
3
-plane is an oriented
3
-dimensional subspace
E\subset T_{x}M
such that
\varphi|_{E}=\mathrm{vol}_{E}
. An
associative
3
-fold
is an oriented submanifold
\Sigma^{3}\subset(M^{7},\varphi)
for which each tangent space
T_{x}\Sigma\subset T_{x}M
is an associative
3
-plane — i.e., a submanifold satisfying
\varphi|_{\Sigma}=\pm\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}
. Associative
3
-folds are fundamental sub-objects in
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-geometry, and are formally analogous to holomorphic curves in Calabi-Yau
3
-folds.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
(M^{7},\varphi)
be a
7

\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi

\Sigma\subset M

\mathrm{G}_{2}

\mathrm{SO}(4)\leq\mathrm{G}_{2}

is an
\mathrm{SO}(4)
-vector bundle as well. In particular, is shown in
[
3
]
that
\left.\Lambda^{3}_{27}(M)\right|_{\Sigma}
decomposes into
\mathrm{SO}(4)
-irreducible vector subbundles

\left.\Lambda^{3}_{27}(M)\right|_{\Sigma}=(\Lambda^{3}_{27})_{0,0}\oplus(\Lambda^{3}_{27})_{0,4}\oplus(\Lambda^{3}_{27})_{1,1}\oplus(\Lambda^{3}_{27})_{1,3}\oplus(\Lambda^{3}_{27})_{2,2}

of ranks
1,5,4,8
, and
9
, respectively. Moreover, there is a natural
\mathrm{SO}(4)
-vector bundle isomorphism
\natural\colon N\Sigma\to(\Lambda^{3}_{27})_{1,1}
. In this language, the mean curvature vector field
H\in\Gamma(N\Sigma)
of the associative

2.5
Hyperkähler and Quaternion-Kähler Manifolds

In this section, we quickly review the definitions of hyperkähler structures, hyperkähler metrics, quaternion-Kähler structures, and quaternion-Kähler metrics.

We begin by recalling the Lie groups
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
and
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
. Identifying
\mathbb{R}^{4n}
with
\mathbb{H}^{n},
multiplication by quaternions
i,

j,
and
k
gives rise to three complex structures
I_{0},

J_{0}
and
K_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{4n}
satisfying the quaternion relation
I_{0}J_{0}=K_{0}.
Lowering an index using the Euclidean metric
g
gives three symplectic forms
\omega_{I_{0}},

\omega_{J_{0}},
and
\omega_{K_{0}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{4n}
. The Lie group
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
may be defined as the simultaneous
\mathrm{GL}_{4n}(\mathbb{R})
-stabilizer of these three 2-forms and the group
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
may be defined as the
\mathrm{GL}_{4n}(\mathbb{R})
-stabilizer of the 4-form

\Theta_{0}=\omega_{I_{0}}^{2}+\omega_{J_{0}}^{2}+\omega_{K_{0}}^{2}.

One can check that both
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
and
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
fix a positive definite inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{4n}
, so we have
\mathrm{Sp}(n)<\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)<\mathrm{SO}(4n)
. Explicitly, for
n=2
in an appropriate basis
(e^{0},\ldots,e^{7})
of
\mathbb{R}^{8*}
we have

\displaystyle\omega_{I_{0}}

\displaystyle=e^{01}+e^{23}-e^{45}-e^{67},

\displaystyle\omega_{J_{0}}

\displaystyle=e^{02}-e^{13}-e^{46}+e^{57},

\displaystyle\omega_{K_{0}}

\displaystyle=e^{03}+e^{12}+e^{47}+e^{56}.

While this basis of
\mathbb{R}^{8\ast}
is non-standard, it has the advantage of aligning with common conventions in
\mathrm{G}_{2}
geometry.

We now specialize to real dimension
8
. Let
Q
be an
8
-manifold. An
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-structure
on
Q
consists of data
(g,(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}),(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}))
, where
g
is a Riemannian metric,
(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
are
g
-orthogonal almost-complex structures satisfying the quaternionic relations
I_{1}I_{2}=I_{3}
, etc., and where
\omega_{p}:=g(I_{p}\cdot,\cdot)
are the corresponding Kähler forms. A
hyperkähler structure
is an
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-structure for which each
I_{p}
is integrable and each
\omega_{p}
is closed. Equivalently, it is a torsion-free
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-structure. A
hyperkähler metric
on
Q
is a Riemanninan metric
g
for which
\mathrm{Hol}(g)\leq\mathrm{Sp}(2)
. Such metrics are Ricci-flat. If
(g,(I_{p}),(\omega_{p}))
is a hyperkähler structure, then
g
is a hyperkähler metric. Conversely, if
g
is a hyperkähler metric, then
g
arises from a hyperkähler structure.

An
\mathrm{Sp}(2)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-structure
on
Q
is a pair
(g,\mathsf{W})
consisting of a Riemannian metric
g
and a rank 3 subbundle
\mathsf{W}\subset\mathrm{End}(TQ)
such that locally
\mathsf{W}
admits a basis
\{I,J,K\}
consisting of
g
-orthogonal almost-complex structures with
IJ=K
. Equivalently, it is a
4
-form
\Theta\in\Omega^{4}(Q)
such that at each
x\in Q
, there is a coframe
L\colon T_{x}Q\to\mathbb{R}^{8}
for which
\Theta|_{x}=L^{*}\Theta_{0}
. A
quaternion-Kähler structure
is an
\mathrm{Sp}(2)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-structure for which the
4
-form
\Theta\in\Omega^{4}(Q)
is
g
-parallel. A
quaternion-Kähler metric
on
Q
is a Riemanninan metric
g
for which
\mathrm{Hol}(g)\leq\mathrm{Sp}(2)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
. Such metrics are Einstein. If
(g,\mathsf{W})
is a QK structure, then
g
is a QK metric. Conversely, if
g
is a QK metric, then
g
arises from a QK structure.

Finally, we explain the relationship between the above objects. First, note that every
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-structure
(g,(I_{p}),(\omega_{p}))
induces an
\mathrm{Sp}(2)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-structure
(g,\mathsf{W})
by setting
\mathsf{W}:=\text{span}(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
, or equivalently, setting
\Theta:=\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}+\omega_{3}^{2}
. Moreover, if the
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-structure is hyperkähler, then the induced
\mathrm{Sp}(2)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-structure is quaternion-Kähler and
g
is Ricci-flat. Conversely, if a quaternion-Kähler structure
(g,\mathsf{W})
is such that
g
is Ricci-flat, then
\mathrm{Hol}^{0}(g)\leq\mathrm{Sp}(2)
, so
(g,\mathsf{W})
is locally induced from a hyperkähler structure.

In particular, suppose that
Q=(\text{C}(M),g_{\mathrm{C}})
is an
8
-dimensional cone for which
g_{\mathrm{C}}
is a QK metric. Since an Einstein cone metric is Ricci-flat, it follows that
g_{\mathrm{C}}
is Ricci-flat, and hence
g_{\mathrm{C}}
is a hyperkähler metric. We will exploit this observation in
\lx@sectionsign
3.2
.

3.3.1

Let

and squashed Aloff-Wallach space

in §

Remark 4.9
.

Let
(M,\varphi_{a,b},\mathsf{A})
be a squashed
3
-Sasakian
7
-manifold. What follows is a heuristic description of the Hopf-ruled associative
3
-folds

. Choose a trivialization of
\mathsf{A}\subset TM
, so that the projections

(for

) from

are well-defined. If

where the “directrix surface”

Note that while the 1-forms
\eta_{i}
do not descend to
Z,
the subbundles
T_{i}=\mathrm{span}(\eta_{i})
are well-defined on
\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{T}^{2}
and we have
T^{*}Z=T_{1}\oplus T_{2}\oplus T_{3}
.

Now suppose that
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{T}^{2}
is a
J_{\mathrm{NK}}
-holomorphic curve. The metric
g_{\mathrm{NK}}
induces a hermitian structure
h_{\Sigma}
on
\Sigma.
Let
\mathcal{B}(\Sigma)\to\Sigma
denote the associated
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle of unitary coframes on
\Sigma,
with
\mathbb{C}
-valued tautological 1-form
\sigma\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\Sigma);\mathbb{C})
and Levi-Civita connection form
\phi\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\Sigma);\mathfrak{u}(1))
. The structure equations of
\mathcal{B}(\Sigma)
are:

\displaystyle d\sigma

\displaystyle=i\phi\wedge\sigma,\>\>\>

\displaystyle d\phi

\displaystyle=iK\sigma\wedge\overline{\sigma},

where
K
is the Gaussian curvature of
h_{\Sigma}.

Let
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma)
denote
\mathcal{B}(\Sigma)\times_{\Sigma}f^{*}\mathrm{SU}(3)
, a
\mathrm{T}^{3}
-bundle over
\Sigma.
The fact that
\Sigma
is a
J_{\mathrm{NK}}
-holomorphic curve implies by Lemma
4.5
(b) that there exist
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
on
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma)
such that

\eta_{i}=A_{i}\sigma,\>\>i=1,2,3.

The function
A_{i}
measures the length of the projection of
T^{*}\Sigma
to
T_{i}.
Note that

|A_{1}|^{2}+|A_{2}|^{2}+|A_{3}|^{2}=1.

The structure equations (
5.3
) imply that there exist
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions
B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}
on
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma)
such that

\displaystyle dA_{1}

\displaystyle=B_{1}\sigma-iA_{1}\phi-iA_{1}\psi+iA_{1}\kappa,

(5.4)

\displaystyle dA_{2}

\displaystyle=B_{2}\sigma-iA_{2}\phi-iA_{2}\psi-iA_{2}\kappa,

\displaystyle dA_{3}

\displaystyle=B_{3}\sigma-iA_{3}\phi+2iA_{3}\psi.

Proposition 5.2
.

The cubic form
C=A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}\sigma^{3}
descends to
\Sigma
to define a holomorphic section of the holomorphic line bundle
\mathrm{Sym}^{3}\left(T^{*}\Sigma\right).

Proof.

The equations (
5.4
) imply that

d(A_{1}A_{2}A_{3})=\left(B_{1}A_{2}A_{3}+B_{2}A_{3}A_{1}+B_{3}A_{1}A_{2}\right)\sigma-3iA_{1}A_{2}A_{3}\phi,

so
A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}\sigma^{3}
descends to a well-defined section of
\mathrm{Sym}^{3}\left(T^{*}\Sigma\right).
The absence of a
(0,1)
-term in
d(A_{1}A_{2}A_{3})
implies this section is holomorphic.
∎

Definition 5.3
.

We shall say a
J
-holomorphic curve
\Sigma\to Z
is
superholomorphic
if
C
is identically zero on
\Sigma.

Remark 5.4
.

Any rational curve
\mathbb{CP}^{1}\to Z
is superholomorphic, because
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
does not support any non-vanishing holomorphic cubic forms.

Example 5.5

(Lifts of curves in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
)
.

The space
\mathrm{SU}(3)
may also be identified with the Hermitian frame bundle of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. Suppose
f\colon S\to\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is a holomorphic curve. We may adapt frames up to a
\mathrm{T}^{2}
-ambiguity at a point
p\in S
so that
p=\operatorname{span}(\mathbf{e}_{1})
and
d\mathbf{e}_{1}\in\operatorname{span}(\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2})
along
S.
The resulting map
S\to\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{T}^{2}
is known as the
Frenet frame
of
S.
It is straightfoward to check that each of the three maps
f_{i}:S\to\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{T}^{2}

\displaystyle f_{1}:\,p\mapsto\left(\mathbf{e}_{1},\operatorname{span}(\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2})\right)

\displaystyle f_{2}:\,p\mapsto\left(\mathbf{e}_{2},\operatorname{span}(\mathbf{e}_{2},\mathbf{e}_{3})\right)

\displaystyle f_{3}:\,p\mapsto\left(\mathbf{e}_{3},\operatorname{span}(\mathbf{e}_{3},\mathbf{e}_{1})\right)

gives a superholomorphic curve
f_{i}(S)\subset\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{T}^{2}
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{T}^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Theorem 5.6
.

Any superholomorphic curve
\Sigma\subset\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{T}^{2}
arises as the image
f_{i}(S)
of a holomorphic curve in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
under one of the three maps
f_{i}
defined in Example
5.5
.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{T}^{2}
be a superholomorphic curve, so that
A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}
vanishes on
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma).
By (
5.4
),
A_{1},

A_{2},
and
A_{3}
represent holomorphic sections of bundles over
\Sigma,
so at least one of them must vanish identically on
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma).

If
A_{3}
vanishes identically on
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma),
the structure equations (
5.2
) imply that
\tau_{1}(\Sigma)
is a holomorphic curve in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
and
\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2},\mathbf{e}_{3}
is its Frenet frame. It follows that
\Sigma=f_{1}\left(\tau_{1}(\Sigma)\right).

The calculations are identical in the cases where
A_{1}
or
A_{2}
vanish identically on
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma),
up to a permutation of the
\mathbf{e}_{i},

f_{i},
and
q_{i}.

∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Fix
i\in\left\{1,2,3\right\}.
Every compact Riemann surface can be conformally immersed in
\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{T}^{2}
as a superholomorphic curve with
A_{i}=0.

.

The cubic form
C

]

]

5.2.3
Associative submanifolds in squashed
N_{1,1}

Applying the results of §
5.2.2
together with Theorem
4.6
(b) we are able to construct non-trivial examples of associative submanifolds in
N_{1,1}.
Here, an associative 3-fold is considered
trivial
if it is of the form
p_{w}^{-1}(\Sigma)
for a fixed projection
p_{w}:N_{1,1}\to\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{T}^{2}
and pseudo-holomorphic curve
\Sigma\subset\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{T}^{2}.

Theorem 5.9
.

Fix
a,b>0
. For every
g\geq 0
, there exists a non-trivial compact associative
3
-fold in
(N_{1,1},\varphi_{a,b})
diffeomorphic to an
S^{1}
-bundle over a genus
g
surface.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma_{g}
be a compact surface of genus
g
. For each
g\geq 0
, by Corollary
5.7
, there exists an immersed pseudo-holomorphic curve
f\colon\Sigma_{g}\to\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{T}^{2}
with
A_{1}=0
. By virtue of the condition
A_{1}=0,

f
is horizontal for the twistor projection
\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{CP}^{2}.
Equip
\Sigma_{g}
with the induced complex structure, and let
w\colon\Sigma_{g}\to S^{2}
be a holomorphic map. By Proposition
4.8
, the surface
(f,w)\colon\Sigma_{g}\to\left(\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{T}^{2}\right)\times S^{2}
is a
J
-holomorphic curve nowhere tangent to an
(S^{2}\times S^{2})
-fiber. Thus, for any
a,b>0
, Theorem
4.6
(b) implies that
\Gamma(f,w)\subset N_{1,1}
is a
\varphi_{a,b}
-associative
3
-fold diffeomorphic to an
S^{1}
-bundle over
\Sigma_{g}
. If
w\colon\Sigma_{g}\to S^{2}
is chosen to be non-constant, then
\Gamma(f,w)\subset N_{1,1}
is non-trivial.
∎

Abstract
. We initiate a systematic study of intrinsic dimensional versions of classical functional inequalities which capture refined properties of the underlying objects. We focus on model spaces: Euclidean space, Hamming cube, and manifolds of constant curvature. In the latter settings, our intrinsic dimensional functional inequalities improve on a series of known results and lead to new Hamilton-type matrix inequalities. Our proofs rely on scaling, tensorization, and stochastic methods.

Primary: 39B62; Secondary: 26D10, 58J65, 60J60.

Key words.
Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities, Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities, scaling, Föllmer process, Hamilton-type matrix inequalities.

1.
Introduction

This work focuses on the development of
intrinsic
dimensional versions of classical functional inequalities. In order to explain the meaning of “intrinsic" in this context it is best to start with an important example. The logarithmic Sobolev inequality in Gauss space
[
61
,
36
]
asserts that for every nice-enough absolutely continuous probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\gamma_{n})\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{I}(\mu\|\gamma_{n}),

(1)

where
\gamma_{n}
is the standard Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here,

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\nu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\intop\nolimits\log\Big(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu}\Big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu

(2)

is the
relative entropy
of
\mu
with respect to
\nu
and

\mathsf{I}(\mu\|\nu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\intop\nolimits\Big|\nabla\log\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu}\Big|^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu=\intop\nolimits\frac{|\nabla(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu/\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu)|^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu/\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu

(3)

is the
relative Fisher information
of
\mu
with respect to
\nu
, provided that
\nu<\!\!\!<\mu
.

Gross’ motivation for (
1
) was to find a substitute for the Euclidean Sobolev inequalities holds in infinite-dimensional spaces (which was needed in constructive quantum field theories). Sobolev inequalities have the feature that the dimension
n
of the ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
appears explicitly in the constants of the inequalities, which leads to triviality upon taking the limit
n\to\infty
. In contrast, the constant
1/2
appearing in (
1
) is
dimension-free
, leading to (
1
) being well-defined in infinite dimensions. On the other hand, as was already observed by Stam
[
61
]
, (
1
) can in fact be improved if the dimension
n
is taken into account. To see this improvement we first apply a standard change of measure (see
[
66
]
) which shows that (
1
) is equivalent to

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})-\mathsf{H}(\gamma_{n}\|\lambda_{n})\leq\frac{\mathsf{I}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})-n}{2},

(4)

where
\lambda_{n}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
dimensional
log-Sobolev inequality of
[
20
]
,

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})-\mathsf{H}(\gamma_{n}\|\lambda_{n})\leq\frac{n}{2}\log\left(\frac{\mathsf{I}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})}{n}\right),

(5)

improves upon (
4
) as can be seen from the inequality
\log s\leq s-1
for
s\in(0,\infty)
. It is clear that when the Fisher information is large, (
5
) provides an exponential refinement over (
4
). Despite this quantitative improvement, (
5
) suffers from a lack of sensitivity to the
intrinsic
dimension of
\mu
. To see this, suppose that
\mu
is of the form
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n-k}(x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{n})
, where
k<n
and
\tilde{\mu}
is an absolutely continuous probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Then (
5
) rephrased in terms of
\tilde{\mu}
asserts that

\mathsf{H}(\tilde{\mu}\|\lambda_{k})-\mathsf{H}(\gamma_{k}\|\lambda_{k})\leq\frac{n}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{\mathsf{I}(\tilde{\mu}\|\lambda_{k})-k}{n}\right),

(6)

which deteriorates to (
4
) as the
ambient
dimension
n
increases, despite the fact that the intrinsic dimension
k
of
\mu
is fixed. In other words, (
5
) is insensitive to the structure of
\mu
. In
[
27
, p. 12]
, Dembo showed that (
5
) can be further improved to an inequality which captures the intrinsic dimension of
\mu
:

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})-\mathsf{H}(\gamma_{n}\|\lambda_{n})\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\det\mathscr{I}(\mu\|\lambda_{n}),

(7)

where

\mathscr{I}(\mu\|\nu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\intop\nolimits\frac{(\nabla(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu/\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu))^{\otimes 2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu/\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu

(8)

is the
relative Fisher information matrix
of
\mu
with respect to
\nu
. Observe that

\mathsf{I}(\mu\|\nu)=\mathrm{tr}\,\mathscr{I}(\mu\|\nu),

(9)

and thus (
7
) improves on (
5
) by the elementary inequality
\log\det C\leq n\log\tfrac{\mathrm{tr}\,C}{n}
which holds for every
n\times n
positive semidefinite matrix
C
. In particular, both sides of (
7
) behave additively with respect to product measures: Plugging in
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu=\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n-k}
into (
7
) yields

\mathsf{H}(\tilde{\mu}\|\lambda_{k})-\mathsf{H}(\gamma_{k}\|\lambda_{k})\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\det\mathscr{I}(\tilde{\mu}\|\lambda_{k})

(10)

which captures correctly the intrinsic dimension of
\mu
.
More generally, by considering the eigenvalues of the Fisher information matrix, (
7
) can quantify the extent to which
\mu
degenerates along each eigenvector direction.

The goal of this work is to initiate a systematic study of intrinsic dimensional versions of classical functional inequalities. We focus on some important model spaces: Euclidean space, Hamming cube, and space forms (manifolds of constant sectional curvature). These model spaces have historically played a crucial role in the development of functional inequalities and their study has been the impetus leading to fruitful generalizations and abstractions; see the monograph
[
9
]
. In view of the richness of the subject, our intrinsic dimensional functional inequalities on these spaces improve on multiple classical inequalities from the literature. The tools required to establish intrinsic dimensional functional inequalities in each of the models spaces will depend on the unique characteristics of the space itself:
scaling
(Euclidean space),
tensorization
(Hamming cube), and
stochastic methods
(space forms). In the rest of the introduction we will review each of these methods and present examples of the intrinsic dimensional functional inequalities which follow. We defer the statements of many of our results to the main body of the paper; see the following brief summary:

10

1.1.
Euclidean spaces: scaling

Most classical functional inequalities on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are coordinate-free results phrased in a coordinate-dependent way. As such, they can often be substantially refined when expressed in a suitable basis. Concretely, the correct basis is found by performing a change of variables of the form
x\mapsto Ax
and then optimizing over a prescribed class of symmetries
A\in\mathsf{G}\subseteq\mathsf{GL}_{n}
. Let us remark that explicit improvements of this form can be obtained only when it is possible to solve these optimization problems, which is not always the case. These improvements are moreover motivated by the study of equality cases. When a functional inequality has a non-constant function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
as an equality case, then the refined inequality obtained in the manner described above would be saturated by all functions of the form
h_{A}(x)=h(Ax)
, where
A\in\mathsf{G}
. This principle has already been applied by Dembo
[
27
]
in the case of the Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality (see also
[
30
,
14
]
and Section
2
below). In the first part of the paper we shall present more applications of this idea to other important functional inequalities in Euclidean space and further consequences.

1.1.1.
Beckner inequalities

In
[
13
]
, Beckner proved that any smooth function
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies the estimates

\forall\ p\in[1,2),\qquad\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}-\|u\|_{L_{p}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}\leq(2-p)\|\nabla u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}.

(11)

This family of inequalities interpolates between the Gaussian Poincaré inequality (corresponding to
p=1
) and Gross’ logarithmic Sobolev inequality
[
36
]
which arises as a limit when
p\to 2^{-}
. We refer to the influential work of Latała and Oleszkiewicz
[
42
]
as well as
[
9
, Section 7.6]
for examples of Beckner-type inequalities satisfied by non-Gaussian measures.

In
[
28
, Corollary 4]
, Dolbeault and Toscani proposed a
dimensional
refinement of Beckner’s inequality (
11
) for functions satisfying a second moment normalization condition. More specifically, they showed that if a function
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies the normalization condition

\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{2}u(x)^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}(x)=n\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2},

(12)

then

\forall p\in[1,2),\qquad\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}\varphi_{p,n}\left(1-\frac{\|u\|_{L_{p}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}}{\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}}\right)\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2},

(13)

where the function
\varphi_{p,n}
is given by

\forall\ s\in(0,1),\qquad\varphi_{p,n}(s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{n}{4}\Big((1-s)^{-\frac{2p}{n(2-p)}}-1\Big).

(14)

Observe that (
13
) improves upon (
11
) up to the value of the implicit constant as

\forall\ s\in(0,1),\qquad\varphi_{p,n}(s)\geq\frac{p}{2(2-p)}\log\Big(\frac{1}{1-s}\Big)\geq\frac{p}{2(2-p)}\cdot s.

(15)

The improvement (
13
) becomes particularly substantial when
\|u\|_{L_{p}(\gamma_{n})}<\!\!\!<\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}
.

In the spirit of the matricial refinement (
7
) over the dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
5
), we present the following refinement of (
13
) for functions whose second moment
matrix
is appropriately normalized.

Theorem 1
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that

\forall\ i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\qquad\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x_{i}x_{j}u(x)^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}(x)=\delta_{ij}\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2},

(16)

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta. Then, we have

\forall\ p\in[1,2),\qquad\frac{\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}-\|u\|_{L_{p}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}}{\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}}\leq 1-\left[\det\left(\frac{4}{\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla u)^{\otimes 2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}+\mathsf{Id}_{n}\right)\right]^{-\frac{2-p}{2p}}.

(17)

Applying the inequality
\det C\leq\left(\tfrac{\mathrm{tr}C}{n}\right)^{n}
and rearranging, we see that (
17
) strengthens (
13
).

1.1.2.
Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequalities

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality
[
34
,
53
]
asserts that for every
p,q,r,s\in[1,\infty)
and
\theta\in[0,1]
satisfying the constraint

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta}{q}+\Big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{n}\Big)(1-\theta),

(18)

there exists a universal (optimal) constant
C^{p,q,r,s}>0
such that every
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies

\|u\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C^{p,q,r,s}\|u\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\|\nabla u\|_{L_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\ell_{s}^{n})}^{1-\theta},

(19)

where we use the standard notation

\|\nabla u\|_{L_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\ell_{s}^{n})}=\left(\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}|\partial_{i}u(x)|^{s}\Big)^{r/s}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/r}.

(20)

In the special case
r\in(1,n)
and
\theta=0
, inequality (
19
) boils down to the classical Sobolev inequality
[
59
,
60
]
. The endpoint case
r=1
and
\theta=0
was due to
[
34
,
53
]
and the corresponding optimal constant for
s=2
was found by Federer, Fleming and Rishel
[
31
,
32
]
. The optimal constant in the range
r\in(1,n)
and
\theta=0
for
s=2
was discovered by Aubin and Talenti
[
2
,
62
]
. The logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
4
) can be obtained as an endpoint case of the Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequality (
19
) with the optimal constant when
s=2
(see
[
26
, Section 1]
). Finally, the optimal constant
C^{p,q,r,s}
for general parameters was found by Cordero-Erausquin, Nazaret and Villani in
[
22
, Section 3]
. In this paper, we present a refined inequality for
r=s
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
p,q,r\in[1,\infty)
,
\theta\in[0,1]
and
C^{p,q,r,r}>0
be such that (
19
) is satisfied for all functions
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
r=s
under the constraint (
18
). Then, for every
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\|u\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C^{p,q,r,r}n^{\frac{1-\theta}{r}}\|u\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\Big(\prodop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}\|\partial_{j}u\|_{L_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)^{\frac{1-\theta}{n}}.

(21)

The inequality (
21
) improves on (
19
) by the arithmetic mean-geometric mean inequality so Theorem
2
asserts that Euclidean Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequalities, that is, inequalities of the form (
19
) with the choice of parameter
r=2
, self-improve via scaling. In particular, (
21
) captures the fact (absent from (
19
)) that
\partial_{i}u\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
implies that
u\equiv 0
under any
L_{s}
-integrability assumption for
u

1.2.
Product spaces: tensorization

If
(\Omega,\pi)
is a probability space, then for a measurable function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
we shall denote its entropy with respect to
\pi
by

\mathrm{Ent}_{\pi}[f]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}f\log f\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi-\Big(\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}f\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi\Big)\,\log\Big(\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}f\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi\Big).

(22)

The usefulness of logarithmic Sobolev inequalities in probability and geometry stems largely from the fact that entropy satisfies a simple yet powerful tensorization principle, rendering them dimension-free estimates
[
43
]
. In the interesting work
[
55
]
, Polyanskiy and Samorodnitsky introduced a family of more general inequalities for Markov semigroups called
nonlinear
logarithmic Sobolev inequalities (see also
[
36
,
66
,
23
,
24
,
20
,
50
,
57
]
for previous occurrences of such estimates in the literature and applications). Let
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a Markov semigroup acting on measurable functions
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
with stationary measure
\pi
. Following
[
55
]
, we say that
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
satisfies the
(p,\Phi)
–LSI, where
p\geq 1
and
\Phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a concave, continuous function with
\Phi(0)=0
, if for every measurable function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we have

\mathrm{Ent}_{\pi}[f^{p}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[f^{p}]\Phi\left(\frac{\mathscr{E}(f,f^{p-1})}{\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[f^{p}]}\right),

(23)

where
\mathscr{E}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Dirichlet form corresponding to
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. As usual, the term
\mathscr{E}(f,f^{p-1})
is interpreted as
\mathscr{E}(f,\log f)
in the endpoint case
p=1
.

In
[
55
, Theorem 1]
, the authors proved a dimensional tensorization property for nonlinear log-Sobolev inequalities asserting that if
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
satisfies the
(p,\Phi)
–LSI, then for any
n\geq 1
, the product semigroup
\{P_{t}^{\otimes n}\}_{t\geq 0}
with stationary measure
\pi^{n}
satisfies the
\big(p,n\Phi(\tfrac{1}{n}\cdot)\big)
–LSI:

\mathrm{Ent}_{\pi^{n}}[f^{p}]\leq n\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{n}}[f^{p}]\Phi\left(\frac{\mathscr{E}(f,f^{p-1})}{n\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{n}}[f^{p}]}\right).

(24)

By considering functions
f
of the form
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\tilde{f}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
, for
k<n
, we see that (
24
) suffers from the problem of incorporating the ambient dimension
n
into the constant, thus ignoring the structure of
f
. In the Euclidean setting, we overcame this issue by finding the correct basis via an optimization procedure over the cone of positive semidefinite matrices. In contrast, such an approach is not suitable on the Hamming cube due to its discrete nature. Our solution to this problem is to refine tensorization instead of scaling. Indeed, as a consequence of a more general tensorization principle (see Theorem
18
below), we shall prove the following stronger nonlinear logarithmic Sobolev inequality for product spaces.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(\Omega,\pi,\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0})
be a stationary Markov semigroup satisfying the
(p,\Phi)
–LSI for some
p\geq 1
and some concave, continuous function
\Phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\Phi(0)=0
. Then, for any
n\geq 1
, every measurable function
f:\Omega^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfies

\mathrm{Ent}_{\pi^{n}}[f^{p}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{n}}[f^{p}]\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{n}}\big[\mathscr{E}_{i}(f,f^{p-1})\big]}{\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{n}}[f^{p}]}\right),

(25)

where
\mathscr{E}_{i}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Dirichlet form associated with the
i
-th component of the semigroup
\{P_{t}^{\otimes n}\}_{t\geq 0}
.

It follows readily from Jensen’s inequality that

\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{n}}\big[\mathscr{E}_{i}(f,f^{p-1})\big]}{\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{n}}[f^{p}]}\right)\leq n\Phi\left(\frac{\mathscr{E}(f,f^{p-1})}{n\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{n}}[f^{p}]}\right),

(26)

where
\mathscr{E}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Dirichlet form associated to
\{P_{t}^{\otimes n}\}_{t\geq 0}
and thus (
25
) indeed strengthens (
24
). Moreover, in
[
55
, Theorems 4 and 6]
, the authors found the
optimal
functions
\Phi_{p}
such that the
(p,\Phi_{p})
-LSI is satisfied on the one-dimensional Hamming cube
\{0,1\}
equipped with the uniform measure. Tensorizing their result via Theorem
3
, one deduces an improved nonlinear logarithmic Sobolev inequality on the Hamming cube
\{0,1\}^{n}
.

1.3.
Space forms: stochastic methods

In order to explain our intrinsic dimensional functional inequalities on space forms we first recall the notion of
local
logarithmic Sobolev inequalities. Starting with the Euclidean setting, fix
T\geq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda_{n}}=\frac{fP_{T}\delta_{x}}{P_{T}f(x)}
where
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac mass at
x
,
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a nonnegative function, and
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the Euclidean heat semigroup given by
P_{t}h(x):=\intop\nolimits h(x+\sqrt{t}z)d\gamma_{n}(z)
. Plugging
\mu
into (
5
) yields (after integration by parts and using the explicit form of
P_{T}\delta_{x}
),

\displaystyle P_{T}(f\log f)(x)-P_{T}f(x)\log P_{T}f(x)\leq\frac{T}{2}P_{T}\Delta f(x)+\frac{n}{2}P_{T}f(x)\log\left(1-\frac{T}{n}\frac{P_{T}(f\Delta\log f)(x)}{P_{T}f(x)}\right).

(27)

The inequality (
27
) is the
local
dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
10
]
. While (
27
) provides an
upper
bound on the (local) entropy, the
reverse
local dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality
[
10
]
provides a
lower
bound,

\displaystyle P_{T}(f\log f)(x)-P_{T}f(x)\log P_{T}f(x)\geq\frac{T}{2}P_{T}\Delta f(x)-\frac{n}{2}P_{T}f(x)\log\left(1+\frac{T}{n}\Delta\log P_{T}f(x)\right).

(28)

Analogously, we can use (
7
), instead of (
5
), to get the local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle P_{T}(f\log f)(x)-P_{T}f(x)\log P_{T}f(x)\leq\frac{T}{2}P_{T}\Delta f(x)+\frac{1}{2}P_{T}f(x)\log\det\left(\mathsf{Id}_{n}-T\frac{P_{T}(f\nabla^{2}\log f(x))}{P_{T}f(x)}\right),

(29)

which improves on (
27
). As for a
reverse
local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we will establish below (Theorem
32
) that

\displaystyle P_{T}(f\log f)(x)-P_{T}f(x)\log P_{T}f(x)\geq\frac{T}{2}\Delta P_{T}f(x)-\frac{1}{2}P_{T}f(x)\log\det\left(\mathsf{Id}_{n}+T\nabla^{2}\log P_{T}f(x)\right),

(30)

which improves on (
28
).

Turning to the manifold setting, local dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities exist on manifolds in forms which account for both the dimension of the manifold as well as the Ricci curvature
[
6
]
. In light of the existence of the local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities on Euclidean spaces (
29
) and (
30
), we wish to understand whether such inequalities can also exist on manifolds. Upon closer inspection, however, it is clear that inequalities such as (
29
) and (
30
) cannot hold if the only curvature information given pertains to the Ricci tensor. On a conceptual level, the difference between the dimensional and intrinsic dimensional inequalities is that the former provide information about the
trace
of the Fisher information matrix, while the latter provide information about the
full spectrum
. Hence, while information on the trace of the Riemann tensor, i.e., Ricci curvature, suffices to yield a dimensional inequality, information on the full Riemann tensor, i.e., sectional curvature, should be required to give an intrinsic dimensional inequality.

A concrete manifestation of this intuition is exhibited by the inequalities of Li–Yau and Hamilton
[
47
,
37
]
. As was realized in
[
10
]
, the reverse local dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
28
) implicitly implies the Li–Yau inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad-\Delta\log P_{T}f(x)\leq\frac{n}{T},

(31)

since the argument in the log term of (
28
) must be nonnegative. Analogously, the reverse local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
30
) implies Hamilton’s inequality,

\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad-\nabla^{2}\log P_{T}f(x)\preceq\frac{1}{T}\mathsf{Id}_{n},

(32)

where
\preceq
is the order of positive semidefinite matrices. In the manifold setting, the Li–Yau inequality, which is a statement about the
trace
of the Hessian of
\log P_{T}f
, holds under a nonnegativity assumption on the
trace
of the Riemann tensor, namely the Ricci tensor
[
47
,
68
]
. Indeed, Bakry and Ledoux
[
10
]
(see also the follow-up work
[
6
]
) established (reverse) local dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities on manifolds with nonnegative
Ricci
curvature which imply the Li–Yau inequality. In contrast, Hamilton’s inequality, which is a statement about the
Hessian
of
\log P_{T}f
, requires the manifold to have nonnegative sectional curvature (and also to be Einstein), which is an assumption on the
full spectrum
of the Riemann tensor
[
37
]
. It follows that if local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities were to hold, then information about the sectional curvature should be provided.

In this work we establish local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities as well as Hamilton-type matrix inequalities for space forms: Euclidean spaces, spheres, and hyperbolic spaces. In addition to serving as the model spaces for functional inequalities on manifolds, these spaces are the simplest non-trivial examples of manifolds where we could hope for local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities to hold. The methods of scaling and tensorization which worked, respectively, for Euclidean spaces and product spaces no longer apply on curved spaces as they lack product and homogeneity structures. Hence, we take a different route and build on the stochastic approach of Lehec
[
44
,
45
]
and Eldan, Lehec, and Shenfeld
[
30
]
towards logarithmic Sobolev inequalities. We start by stating our local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev on space forms while deferring precise definitions to Part
II
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with constant sectional curvature
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
with the associated heat semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. Fix
T>0
,
x\in\mathsf{M}
, a smooth positive function
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\intop\nolimits_{M}f\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}\delta_{x}=1
, and let
\mu
be the probability measure with
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}\delta_{x}}=f
. Define the 2-tensor
C(t)=\frac{e^{n\kappa t}}{n\kappa}A+tB
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
where
A,B
are the 2-tensors given by

\begin{cases}A=-e^{-n\kappa T}\big(P_{T}\nabla^{2}f(x)-\frac{1}{n}P_{T}\Delta f(x)\cdot\mathsf{g}\big)\\
B=\Big(\frac{(n-1)\kappa}{2}-\frac{\Delta P_{T}f(x)}{n}\Big)\cdot\mathsf{g}.\end{cases}

(33)

Then, we have the local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}&P_{T}(f\log f)(x)-P_{T}f(x)\log P_{T}f(x)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\mathrm{tr}\Big[e^{C(t)-C(T)}\Big(\mathsf{g}+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\big(\nabla\log f\big)^{\otimes 2}\!\!\intop\nolimits_{t}^{T}e^{2C(s)-2C(T)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{-1}\!\!\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\big(\nabla\log f\big)^{\otimes 2}e^{C(t)-C(T)}\Big]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

(34)

and the reverse local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}&P_{T}(f\log f)(x)-P_{T}f(x)\log P_{T}f(x)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathrm{tr}\Big[e^{C(t)-C(0)}\Big(\mathsf{g}-\big(\nabla\log P_{T}f(x)\big)^{\otimes 2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}e^{2C(s)-2C(0)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{-1}\big(\nabla\log P_{T}f(x)\big)^{\otimes 2}e^{C(t)-C(0)}\Big]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(35)

As will become clear from the proof of Theorem
4
, the theorem is not optimal and follows from a more powerful “master " matrix differential inequality (section
10.3
). There are other inequalities which can be deduced from the master matrix differential inequality, specifically in space forms with nonpositive sectional curvature. In particular, we prove Hamilton-type matrix inequalities for the heat equation:

Theorem 5
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with constant nonpositive sectional curvature
\kappa\leq 0
. Let
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the associated heat semigroup and let
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
be a positive function. Then, for every
x\in\mathsf{M}
and every
T\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{if, either }\kappa=0,\text{ or }\kappa<0\text{ and }\frac{4}{n^{2}\kappa}\frac{\Delta P_{T}f(x)}{P_{T}f(x)}=1,\quad\text{then}\quad-\nabla^{2}\log P_{T}f(x)\preceq\frac{1}{T}\mathsf{Id}_{n}\quad\forall x\in\mathsf{M}.\end{split}

(36)

Further,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{if}\quad\frac{4}{n^{2}\kappa}\frac{\Delta P_{T}f(x)}{P_{T}f(x)}>1,\\
&\text{then}\quad-\nabla^{2}\log P_{T}f(x)\preceq\frac{n\kappa}{2}\left\{\sqrt{\frac{4}{n^{2}\kappa}\frac{\Delta P_{T}f(x)}{P_{T}f(x)}-1}\cot\left(\frac{n\kappa T}{2}\sqrt{\frac{4}{n^{2}\kappa}\frac{\Delta P_{T}f(x)}{P_{T}f(x)}-1}\right)-1\right\}\mathsf{Id}_{n}.\end{split}

(37)

In flat space, where
\kappa=0
, Theorem
5
reduces to (
32
), namely, Hamilton’s matrix inequality
[
37
, Corollary 4.4]
. In hyperbolic spaces, Theorem
5
is completely new. The constraint
\frac{4}{n^{2}\kappa}\frac{\Delta P_{T}f(x)}{P_{T}f(x)}>1
is natural. Indeed, Theorem
5
is a matrix version of the improved Li-Yau inequality of Bakry, Bolley, and Gentil—see Remark
34
.

Going beyond matrix inequalities, we can use our master matrix differential inequality to obtain another form of local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities.

Theorem 6
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be the
n
-dimensional hyperbolic space with sectional curvature
\kappa<0
with the associated heat semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. Fix
T>0
,
x\in\mathsf{M}
, a smooth positive function
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\intop\nolimits_{M}f\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}\delta_{x}=1
,
and let
\mu
be the probability measure with
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}\delta_{x}}=f
.
Then, with

\displaystyle\lambda\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{n^{2}\kappa^{2}}{4}\left\{\frac{4}{n^{2}\kappa}\Delta P_{T}f(x)-1\right\},\quad\alpha_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}\arctan\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\left(\sigma_{i}+\frac{n\kappa}{2}\right)\right)&\text{if }\lambda<0,\\
-\frac{2}{2\sigma_{i}+n\kappa}&\text{if }\lambda=0,\\
\arctanh\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\lambda}}\left(\sigma_{i}+\frac{n\kappa}{2}\right)\right)&\text{if }\lambda>0,\\
\end{cases}

(38)

we have the local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{T}(f\log f)(x)-P_{T}f(x)\log P_{T}f(x)\leq-\frac{P_{T}\Delta f(x)}{2}-\frac{n^{2}\kappa T}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\begin{cases}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\cos(\alpha_{i})}{\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}T+\alpha_{i})}\right)&\text{if }\lambda>0\\
\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}}{T+\alpha_{i}}\right)&\text{if }\lambda=0\\
\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\cosh(\alpha_{i})}{\cosh(\sqrt{-\lambda}T+\alpha_{i})}\right)&\text{if }\lambda<0\\
\end{cases}\end{split}

(39)

where
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are the eigenvalues of
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[-\nabla^{2}\log f]
, and the reverse local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{T}(f\log f)(x)-P_{T}f(x)\log P_{T}f(x)\geq-\frac{P_{T}\Delta f(x)}{2}-\frac{n^{2}\kappa T}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\begin{cases}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\cos(\alpha_{i})}{\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}T+\alpha_{i})}\right)&\text{if }\lambda>0\\
\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}}{T+\alpha_{i}}\right)&\text{if }\lambda=0\\
\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\cosh(\alpha_{i})}{\cosh(\sqrt{-\lambda}T+\alpha_{i})}\right)&\text{if }\lambda<0\\
\end{cases}\end{split}

(40)

where
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are the eigenvalues of
-\nabla^{2}\log P_{T}f(x)
.

Part I
Euclidean and product spaces: scaling and tensorization

2.
Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities in Euclidean spaces and Cramér–Rao bounds

In this section we discuss strengthenings of logarithmic Sobolev inequalities for measures on Euclidean spaces by means of scaling. In addition, we derive an application of these inequalities to Bayesian Cramér–Rao bounds.

2.1.
Warm-up: Gross’ inequality

The Euclidean reformulation (
4
) of the logarithmic Sobolev inequality in Gauss space
[
36
]
asserts that if
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a probability density, then

\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\mathsf{H}(\gamma_{n}\|\lambda_{n})\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f(x)|^{2}}{f(x)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-n\right).

(41)

Fix such a density
f
and consider the reparametrized density
f_{A}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
which is given by
f_{A}(x)=(\det A)\cdot f(Ax)
, where
A\in\mathsf{GL}_{n}
is a positive definite matrix. Applying (
41
) for
f_{A}
we get

\begin{split}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x&+\log\det A-\mathsf{H}(\gamma_{n}\|\lambda_{n})=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{A}(x)\log f_{A}(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\mathsf{H}(\gamma_{n}\|\lambda_{n})\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f_{A}(x)|^{2}}{f_{A}(x)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-n\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|A\cdot\nabla f(x)|^{2}}{f(x)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-n\right),\end{split}

(42)

which after rearranging becomes

\begin{split}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\mathsf{H}(\gamma_{n}\|\lambda_{n})&\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|A\cdot\nabla f(x)|^{2}}{f(x)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\log\det A^{2}-n\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{tr}\big(A^{2}\cdot\mathscr{I}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})\big)-\log\det A^{2}-n\right).\end{split}

(43)

For the optimal choice of matrix
A=\mathscr{I}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})^{-1/2}
, (
43
) readily becomes Dembo’s inequality (
7
). Observe that in this argument we made critical use of the change of variables formula for the Lebesgue measure, i.e., that
\lambda_{n}(AK)=(\det A)\cdot\lambda_{n}(K)
for any Borel
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
A\in\mathsf{GL}_{n}
. While Lebesgue is the only measure on Euclidean space satisfying such an invariance property under all linear transformations, in the next section we shall observe that a weaker self-improvement can be deduced for measures which behave well under diagonal linear maps.

2.2.
Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities for homogeneous measures

Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\geq 0
. An absolutely continuous measure
\rho
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with density
w:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is called
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
-homogeneous if for every
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}>0
,

\forall\ x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad w(t_{1}x_{1},\ldots,t_{n}x_{n})=t_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{p_{n}}w(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

(44)

Theorem 7
.

Fix
c_{1},c_{2}>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\geq 0
and let
\rho
be a
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
-homogeneous measure such that for any Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\rho)\leq c_{1}\mathsf{I}(\mu\|\rho)+c_{2}.

(45)

Then, for any Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with positive differentiable density
f
, we have

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\rho)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}(1+p_{k})\log\left(\frac{2ec_{1}}{1+p_{k}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\partial_{k}f(y))^{2}}{f(y)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(y)\right)+c_{2}.

(46)

The existence of homogeneous measures
\rho
satisfying inequalities of the form (
45
), as well as more general
entropy-energy inequalities
follows, for instance, from
[
9
, Proposition 7.3.1]
.

Proof of Theorem
7
.

Let
f=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}
be an arbitrary positive function with
\rho
-integral equal to 1 and fix
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}>0
. The measure
\mu_{t}
with density
x\mapsto f_{t}(x)=t_{1}^{1+p_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{1+p_{n}}f(t_{1}x_{1},\ldots,t_{n}x_{n})
with respect to
\rho
is a probability measure, as

\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{1+p_{i}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(t_{1}x_{1},\ldots,t_{n}x_{n})w(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{p_{i}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y)w(t_{1}^{-1}y_{1},\ldots,t_{n}^{-1}y_{n})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=1,

(47)

where we made the change of variables
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})=(t_{1}x_{1},\ldots,t_{n}x_{n})
. We have,

\begin{split}&\mathsf{H}(\mu_{t}\|\rho)=\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{1+p_{i}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(t_{1}x_{1},\ldots,t_{n}x_{n})\left\{\log f(t_{1}x_{1},\ldots,t_{n}x_{n})+\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}(1+p_{k})\log t_{k}\right\}w(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{p_{i}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y)\left\{\log f(y)+\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}(1+p_{k})\log t_{k}\right\}w(t_{1}^{-1}y_{1},\ldots,t_{n}^{-1}y_{n})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\rho)+\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}(1+p_{k})\log t_{k}.\end{split}

Similarly, assuming in addition that
f
is differentiable, for every
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we have
\partial_{k}f_{t}(x)=t_{k}\prodop\displaylimits_{i}t_{i}^{1+p_{i}}\partial_{k}f(t_{1}x_{1},\ldots,t_{n}x_{n})
and thus

\begin{split}\mathsf{I}(\mu_{t}\|\rho)&=\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}^{2}\cdot\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{1+p_{i}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\partial_{k}f(t_{1}x_{1},\ldots,t_{n}x_{n}))^{2}}{f(t_{1}x_{1},\ldots,t_{n}x_{n})}w(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}^{2}\cdot\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{p_{i}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\partial_{k}f(y))^{2}}{f(y)}w(t_{1}^{-1}y_{1},\ldots,t_{n}^{-1}y_{n})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}^{2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\partial_{k}f(y))^{2}}{f(y)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(y).\end{split}

(48)

Therefore, applying (
45
) for
\mu_{t}
and reorganizing the terms, we deduce that

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\rho)\leq\inf_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\geq 0}\left\{c_{1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}^{2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\partial_{k}f(y))^{2}}{f(y)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(y)-\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}(1+p_{k})\log t_{k}+c_{2}\right\}.

It is now elementary to check that the above infimum is attained when

\forall\ k\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\qquad t_{k}^{2}=\frac{1+p_{k}}{2c_{1}}\Big(\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\partial_{k}f(y))^{2}}{f(y)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(y)\Big)^{-1}

(49)

and plugging this choice of parameters completes the proof.
∎

Specifically for Lebesgue measure, Theorem
7
implies that if
\mu
has differentiable density
f
,

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})-\mathsf{H}(\gamma_{n}\|\lambda_{n})\leq\frac{1}{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}\log\left(\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\partial_{k}f(y))^{2}}{f(y)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right),

(50)

which is weaker than Dembo’s inequality (
7
) in view of the elementary estimate
\det C\leq\prodop\displaylimits_{s}C_{ss}
which holds for all positive semidefinite matrices
C
. On the other hand, (
50
) combined with Jensen’s inequality implies (
5
). We refer to
[
7
,
10
,
5
,
6
]
for further dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities and applications to Li–Yau-type estimates
[
47
]
, hypercontractivity
[
51
,
19
,
52
,
12
,
4
]
and heat kernel estimates
[
4
,
7
]
.

2.3.
A Bayesian Cramér–Rao bound

In
[
1
]
, Aras, Lee, Pananjady and Courtade observed that logarithmic Sobolev inequalities formally imply Bayesian Cramér–Rao bounds, thus extending some results of Efroimovich
[
29
]
for Gaussian measures. In this section, we investigate similar applications of intrinsic dimensional log-Sobolev inequalities in the spirit of (
46
) and (
7
).

Following
[
1
]
, we work in the setting of parametric statistics. Let
\{\mu_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
be a family of probability measures on a measurable space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F})
. Assume moreover that there exists a dominating
\sigma
-finite measure
\lambda
on
\Omega
such that
\mu_{\theta}
has a positive density with respect to
\lambda
,

\forall\ \theta\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{\theta}(x)=f(x;\theta)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).

(51)

We shall assume throughout that each function
\theta\mapsto f(x;\theta)
is smooth and that

\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\nabla_{\theta}f(x;\theta)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)=0

(52)

for almost every
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The Fisher information of the parametric family
\{\mu_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is

\forall\ \theta\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\mathscr{J}(\theta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla_{\theta}f(x;\theta)|^{2}}{f(x;\theta)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).

(53)

Finally, if
\pi
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote the mutual information of
\pi
with the family
\{\mu_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
by

I\big(\pi;\{\mu_{\theta}\}\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}f(x;\theta)\log\left(\frac{f(x;\theta)}{\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x,\phi)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(\phi)}\right)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(\theta).

(54)

The main result of
[
1
, Theorem 1]
specified to the standard Gaussian measure
\gamma_{n}
asserts that for every absolutely continuous probability measure
\pi
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

I\big(\pi;\{\mu_{\theta}\}\big)+\mathsf{H}(\pi\|\gamma_{n})\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathsf{I}(\pi\|\gamma_{n})+\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathscr{J}(\theta)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(\theta)\Big).

(55)

Inequality (
55
) implies the Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
1
) since choosing
\mu_{\theta}=\lambda
independently of
\theta
, the terms
I(\pi;\{\mu_{\theta}\})
and
\mathscr{J}(\theta)
both vanish. We present inequalities in the spirit of (
55
) for homogeneous measures satisfying a log-Sobolev inequality of the form (
45
).

Theorem 8
.

Fix
c_{1},c_{2}>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\geq 0
and let
\rho
be a
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
-homogeneous measure such that for any Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\rho)\leq c_{1}\mathsf{I}(\mu\|\rho)+c_{2}.

(56)

Then, for every parametric family
\{\mu_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and every absolutely continuous measure
\pi
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose density with respect to
\rho
is
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we have

\begin{split}&I\big(\pi;\{\mu_{\theta}\}\big)+\mathsf{H}(\pi\|\rho)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}(1+p_{k})\log\left(\frac{2ec_{1}}{1+p_{k}}\Big(\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\partial_{k}h(\theta))^{2}}{h(\theta)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\theta)+\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\frac{(\partial_{\theta_{k}}f(x;\theta))^{2}}{f(x;\theta)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(\theta)\Big)\right)+c_{2}.\end{split}

(57)

Observe that the terms inside the logarithm on the right-hand side are the
k
-th component of the Fisher informations
\mathsf{I}(\pi|\rho)
and
\mathscr{J}(\theta)
respectively, in analogy with Theorem
7
.

Proof of Theorem
8
.

Consider the function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
given by

\forall\ x\in\Omega,\qquad f(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x;\theta)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(\theta)

(58)

and observe that

\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}f(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)=\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x;\theta)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(\theta)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{\theta}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(\theta)=1.

(59)

Moreover, for
x\in\Omega
, consider the function
h_{x}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
given by

\forall\ \theta\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad h_{x}(\theta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{h(\theta)f(x;\theta)}{f(x)}

(60)

and notice that the measure
\nu_{x}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{x}(\theta)=h_{x}(\theta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\theta)
is a probability measure since

\nu_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{h(\theta)f(x;\theta)}{f(x)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\theta)=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x;\theta)}{f(x)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(\theta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:cramer-f}}}{{=}}1.

(61)

By Theorem
7
and the assumption on
\rho
, for every
x\in\Omega
we have

\mathsf{H}(\nu_{x}\|\rho)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}(1+p_{k})\log\Big(\frac{2ec_{1}}{1+p_{k}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\partial_{k}h_{x}(\theta))^{2}}{h_{x}(\theta)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\theta)\Big)+c_{2}.

(62)

Integrating this inequality with respect to the probability measure
f(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)
, we get

\begin{split}\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\mathsf{H}(\nu_{x}\|\rho)f(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)&\leq\frac{1}{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}(1+p_{k})\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\log\Big(\frac{2ec_{1}}{1+p_{k}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\partial_{k}h_{x}(\theta))^{2}}{h_{x}(\theta)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\theta)\Big)f(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)+c_{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}(1+p_{k})\log\Big(\frac{2ec_{1}}{1+p_{k}}\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\partial_{k}h_{x}(\theta))^{2}}{h_{x}(\theta)}f(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\theta)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\Big)+c_{2},\end{split}

(63)

where the last line follows from Jensen’s inequality. Moreover, by definition we have

\begin{split}\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\mathsf{H}(\nu_{x}\|\rho)f(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)&=\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(\theta)f(x;\theta)\log\Big(\frac{h(\theta)f(x;\theta)}{f(x)}\Big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\theta)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\\
&=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(\theta)\log h(\theta)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\theta)+\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}f(x;\theta)\log\big(\frac{f(x;\theta)}{f(x)}\big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(\theta)\\
&=\mathsf{H}(\pi\|\rho)+I\big(\pi;\{\mu_{\theta}\}\big).\end{split}

(64)

Similarly, computing the integral on the right-hand side of (
63
), gives

\begin{split}\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}&\frac{(\partial_{k}h_{x}(\theta))^{2}}{h_{x}(\theta)}f(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\theta)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)=\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\big(f(x;\theta)\partial_{k}h(\theta)+h(\theta)\partial_{\theta_{k}}f(x;\theta)\big)^{2}}{h(\theta)f(x;\theta)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\theta)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\\
&=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\partial_{k}h(\theta))^{2}}{h(\theta)}\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}f(x;\theta)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\theta)+2\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{k}h(\theta)\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\partial_{\theta_{k}}f(x;\theta)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\theta)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(\theta)\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\frac{(\partial_{\theta_{k}}f(x;\theta))^{2}}{f(x;\theta)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\theta)\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:regu}}}{{=}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\partial_{k}h(\theta))^{2}}{h(\theta)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(\theta)+\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\frac{(\partial_{\theta_{k}}f(x;\theta))^{2}}{f(x;\theta)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(\theta).\end{split}

Combining everything, we deduce the desired inequality.
∎

Remark 9
.

In the case of the Gaussian measure
\rho=\gamma_{n}
, we have at our disposal the intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
7
). Repeating the same proof mutatis mutandis while replacing (
46
) with (
7
), we conclude that for any probability measure
\pi
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose density with respect to
\gamma_{n}
is
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and for every parametric family
\{\mu_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, we have

I\big(\pi;\{\mu_{\theta}\}\big)+\mathsf{H}(\pi\|\gamma_{n})\leq\frac{\mathrm{tr}\mathsf{M}_{2,\pi}-n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\log\det\Big(2\mathsf{Id}_{n}+\mathscr{I}(\pi\|\gamma_{n})+\!\!\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\!\!\!\intop\nolimits_{\Omega}\frac{(\nabla_{\theta}f(x;\theta))^{\otimes 2}}{f(x;\theta)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(\theta)-\mathsf{M}_{2,\pi}\Big),

where
\mathsf{M}_{2,\pi}=\intop\nolimits\theta^{\otimes 2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(\theta)
. This recovers a result of Efroimovich
[
29
, Theorem 5]
. Combining the inequalities
\log\det C\leq n\log\tfrac{\mathrm{tr}C}{n}
and
\log y\leq y-1
, which hold for all
y>0
and all
n\times n
positive definite matrices
C
, we see that Efroimovich’s inequality is a strengthening of (
55
).

3.
Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequalities

In this section we shall prove Theorem
2
:

Theorem 10
.

Let
p,q,r\in[1,\infty)
,
\theta\in[0,1]
and
C^{p,q,r,r}>0
be such that

\|u\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C^{p,q,r,r}\|u\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\|\nabla u\|_{L_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\ell_{r}^{n})}^{1-\theta}

(65)

is satisfied for all functions
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under the constraint

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta}{q}+\Big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{n}\Big)(1-\theta).

(66)

Then, for every
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\|u\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C^{p,q,r,r}n^{\frac{1-\theta}{r}}\|u\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\Big(\prodop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}\|\partial_{j}u\|_{L_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)^{\frac{1-\theta}{n}}.

(67)

Proof.

Fix
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and consider the function
u_{t}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
given by

\forall\ x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad u_{t}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}u(t_{1}x_{1},\ldots,t_{n}x_{n}).

(68)

Then, for
s\geq 1
we have

\|u_{t}\|_{L_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\Big(\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t_{1}x_{1},\ldots,t_{n}x_{n})|^{s}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{1/s}=\prodop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}t_{j}^{-1/s}\|u\|_{L_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(69)

and

\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\ell_{r}^{n})}^{r}=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|\partial_{i}u_{t}\|_{L_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{r}\prodop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}t_{j}^{-1}\|\partial_{i}u\|_{L_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}.

(70)

Therefore, applying (
65
) to
u_{t}
and rearranging, we deduce that

\begin{split}\|u\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&\leq C^{p,q,r,r}\Big(\prodop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}t_{j}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\theta}{q}-\frac{1-\theta}{r}}\|u\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{r}\|\partial_{i}u\|_{L_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}\Big)^{\frac{1-\theta}{r}}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:gn}}}{{=}}C^{p,q,r,r}\Big(\prodop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}t_{j}\Big)^{-\frac{1-\theta}{n}}\|u\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{r}\|\partial_{i}u\|_{L_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}\Big)^{\frac{1-\theta}{r}}\end{split}

(71)

for every
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}>0
. Choosing

t_{i}=\|\partial_{i}u\|_{L_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{-1}

(72)

gives the desired inequality (
67
).
∎

4.
Beckner inequalities

In this section we shall prove Theorem
1
:

Theorem 11
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that

\forall\ i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\qquad\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x_{i}x_{j}u(x)^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}(x)=\delta_{ij}\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2},

(73)

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta. Then, we have

\forall\ p\in[1,2),\qquad\frac{\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}-\|u\|_{L_{p}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}}{\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}}\leq 1-\det\left(\frac{4}{\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla u)^{\otimes 2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}+\mathsf{Id}_{n}\right)^{-\frac{2-p}{2p}}.

(74)

For the proof of Theorem
11
we shall use the intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
7
) which takes the following simple form for appropriately normalized functions in Gauss space.

Lemma 12
.

Let
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}=1
and

\forall\ i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\qquad\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x_{i}x_{j}u(x)^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}(x)=\delta_{ij}.

(75)

Then, we have

\mathrm{Ent}_{\gamma_{n}}[u^{2}]\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\det\left(4\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla u)^{\otimes 2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}+\mathsf{Id}_{n}\right).

(76)

Proof.

Let
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfy the assumptions of the lemma and define
f(x)=u(x)^{2}\tfrac{\exp(-|x|^{2}/2)}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}
which is the density of a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, we have

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})-\mathsf{H}(\gamma_{n}\|\lambda_{n})=\mathrm{Ent}_{\gamma_{n}}[u^{2}]-\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{2}u(x)^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}(x)-\frac{n}{2}\log 2\pi+\frac{n}{2}\log 2\pi e\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:lem-constr}}}{{=}}\mathrm{Ent}_{\gamma_{n}}[u^{2}].

(77)

On the other hand, for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we compute

\partial_{k}f(x)=\big(2u(x)\partial_{k}u(x)-x_{k}u(x)^{2}\big)\frac{e^{-|x|^{2}/2}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}

(78)

and thus for
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we get

\mathscr{I}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})_{ij}=4\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{i}u\partial_{j}u\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}-2\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(x_{j}\partial_{i}u(x)+x_{i}\partial_{j}u(x)\big)u(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}(x)+\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x_{i}x_{j}u(x)^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}(x).

(79)

For
i\neq j
, integration by parts gives

2\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x_{j}\partial_{i}u(x)u(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}(x)=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{i}\big(x_{j}u(x)^{2}\big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}(x)=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x_{i}x_{j}u(x)^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:lem-constr}}}{{=}}0,

(80)

whereas for
i=j
, again by integration by parts,

2\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x_{i}\partial_{i}u(x)u(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}(x)=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{i}\big(x_{i}u(x)^{2}\big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}(x)-\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x)^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}(x)=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x_{i}^{2}u(x)^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}(x)-1\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:lem-constr}}}{{=}}0.

Plugging the above in (
79
) and using (
75
) again for the last term, we deduce that

\mathscr{I}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})=4\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla u)^{\otimes 2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}+\mathsf{Id}_{n}

(81)

and the conclusion of the lemma follows from (
7
).
∎

Equipped with Lemma
12
, we proceed to the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
11
.

Assume, without loss of generality, that
\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}=1
. Combining a lemma of Dolbeault and Toscani
[
28
, Lemma 5]
(see also
[
42
]
) with Lemma
12
, we get that

\frac{1}{\|u\|_{L_{p}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}}=\frac{\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}}{\|u\|_{L_{p}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}}\leq\exp\left(\frac{2-p}{p}\mathrm{Ent}_{\gamma_{n}}[u^{2}]\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:lem-beckner}}}{{\leq}}\det\left(4\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla u)^{\otimes 2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}+\mathsf{Id}_{n}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2p}}.

(82)

Therefore,

1-\|u\|_{L_{p}(\gamma_{n})}^{2}\leq 1-\det\left(4\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla u)^{\otimes 2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n}+\mathsf{Id}_{n}\right)^{-\frac{2-p}{2p}},

(83)

which is the desired estimate under the normalization
\|u\|_{L_{2}(\gamma_{n})}=1
.
∎

5.
q
-logarithmic Sobolev inequalities

Following Bobkov and Zegarlinski
[
18
]
(see also
[
11
]
) we say that a probability measure
\mu
on the real line satisfies the
q
-logarithmic Sobolev inequality with constant
C>0
if for any
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
we have

\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu}[|f|^{q}]\leq C\intop\nolimits{R}|f^{\prime}(x)|^{q}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

(84)

Standard tensorization principles show that if (
84
) holds, then for any
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu^{n}}[|f|^{q}]\leq C\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{i}f(x)|^{q}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{n}(x),

(85)

where
\mu^{n}=\mu\otimes\mu\otimes\cdots\otimes\mu
is the product measure of i.i.d. coordinates distributed like
\mu
. In particular, it has been established in
[
18
, Corollary 5.6]
(see also
[
16
, Section 5]
) that the measure
\mu_{p}
with density
\tfrac{1}{Z_{p}}e^{-|x|^{p}}
, where
p>2
, satisfies the
q
-logarithmic Sobolev inequality for
q=\tfrac{p}{p-1}
with some constant
C_{q}>1
. In order to investigate scale-invariant refinements of (
85
) for this family of measures in the spirit of (
50
), we first need to formulate them as Euclidean inequalities.

Theorem 13
.

For any
q\in(1,2)
, there exists a constant
\tilde{C}_{q}>0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with positive differentiable density
g
,

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})\leq\tilde{C}_{q}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\Big|\frac{\partial_{i}g(x)}{g(x)}\Big|^{q}+|x_{i}|^{\frac{q}{q-1}}\right)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

(86)

Proof.

For
p=\frac{q}{q-1}>2
consider the probability measure
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}(x)=\tfrac{e^{-|x|^{p}}}{Z_{p}}
on
\mathbb{R}
, where the normalizing constant is
Z_{p}=2\Gamma(1+1/p)>2
. Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with differentiable density
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and consider the function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
given by

\forall\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad f(x)=Z_{p}^{n/q}g(x)^{1/q}e^{\|x\|_{p}^{p}/q},

(87)

which satisfies
\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{q}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}^{n}(x)=1
. Therefore, the
q
-logarithmic Sobolev inequality for
\mu_{p}^{n}
applied to the function
f
implies that

\frac{1}{Z_{p}^{n}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{q}e^{-\|x\|_{p}^{p}}\log f(x)^{q}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu_{p}^{n}}[f^{q}]\leq\frac{C_{q}}{Z_{p}^{n}}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{i}f(x)|^{q}e^{-\|x\|_{p}^{p}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

(88)

Observe that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{Z_{p}^{n}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{q}e^{-\|x\|_{p}^{p}}\log f(x)^{q}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x&=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\log\big(Z_{p}^{n}g(x)e^{\|x\|_{p}^{p}}\big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})+\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|x\|_{p}^{p}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)+\log Z_{p}^{n},\end{split}

(89)

and for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

\frac{1}{Z_{p}^{n}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{i}f(x)|^{q}e^{-\|x\|_{p}^{p}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{q^{q}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\frac{\partial_{i}g(x)}{g(x)}+p\mathrm{sign}(x_{i})|x_{i}|^{p-1}\Big|^{q}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

(90)

Therefore, rearranging (
88
) we deduce that

\begin{split}\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})&\leq\frac{C_{q}}{q^{q}}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\frac{\partial_{i}g(x)}{g(x)}+p\mathrm{sign}(x_{i})|x_{i}|^{p-1}\Big|^{q}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)-\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x_{i}|^{p}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)-\log Z_{p}^{n}\\
&\leq\tilde{C}_{q}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\Big|\frac{\partial_{i}g(x)}{g(x)}\Big|^{q}+|x_{i}|^{p}\right)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\end{split}

(91)

for some different constant
\tilde{C}_{q}>0
and the proof is complete.
∎

This Euclidean weakening of the
q
-logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
85
) for
\mu_{p}^{n}
makes it amenable to refinements via scaling.

Theorem 14
.

For any
q\in(1,2)
and
p=\tfrac{q}{q-1}
, there exists a constant
\tilde{C}_{q}>0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\ \inf_{t_{i}>0}\Big\{\tilde{C}_{q}t_{i}^{q}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\frac{\partial_{i}g(x)}{g(x)}\Big|^{q}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)+\frac{\tilde{C}_{q}}{t_{i}^{p}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x_{i}|^{p}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)-\log t_{i}\Big\}.

(92)

Proof.

Fix
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}>0
and consider the probability measure
\mu_{t}
whose density is given by
x\mapsto g_{t}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}t_{1}\cdots t_{n}g(t_{1}x_{1},\ldots,t_{n}x_{n})
. Then, we have

\mathsf{H}(\mu_{t}\|\lambda_{n})=\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\log t_{i}

(93)

and for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\Big|\frac{\partial_{i}g_{t}(x)}{g_{t}(x)}\Big|^{q}+|x_{i}|^{p}\right)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(t_{i}^{q}\Big|\frac{\partial_{i}g(x)}{g(x)}\Big|^{q}+\frac{|x_{i}|^{p}}{t_{i}^{p}}\right)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

(94)

Therefore, applying (
86
) to
\mu_{t}
and rearranging, we deduce that

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\Big\{\tilde{C}_{q}t_{i}^{q}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\frac{\partial_{i}g(x)}{g(x)}\Big|^{q}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)+\frac{\tilde{C}_{q}}{t_{i}^{p}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x_{i}|^{p}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)-\log t_{i}\Big\}

(95)

and taking an infimum over
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}>0
completes the proof.
∎

6.
Beyond linear rescalings

The simple idea of the previous sections can be summarized as follows. Let

\mathscr{K}(f)\leq\mathscr{L}(f)

(96)

be a functional inequality valid for regular enough functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and fix a subgroup of symmetries
\mathsf{G}\subseteq\mathsf{GL}_{n}
. For a fixed
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
for which inequality (
96
) is valid and
A\in\mathsf{G}
, consider the function
f_{A}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
f_{A}(x)=f(Ax)
.
If
(
96
) applied to
f_{A}
can be rearranged to an upper bound for
\mathscr{K}(f)
of the form

\mathscr{K}(f)\leq\widetilde{L}(f,A),

(97)

then taking an infimum over
A\in\mathsf{G}
yields a stronger inequality as (
96
) just amounts to the choice
A=\mathsf{Id}_{n}
. Observe that enhancing inequalities in this way, always produces a larger family of extremals. For instance, (
4
) becomes an equality only when
\mu
is a translate of
\gamma_{n}
, (
5
) becomes an equality when
\mu
is a Gaussian measure with covariance matrix of the form
\sigma\mathsf{Id}_{n}
, where
\sigma>0
, and (
7
) becomes an equality for any Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In this section, we will discuss the possibility of refining functional inequalities by using changes of variables via
nonlinear
maps and we shall illustrate this in the case of the logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
4
). Let
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth diffeomorphism and for a measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a differentiable density
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
consider the measure
\mu_{T}
whose density is given by
f_{T}(x)=(f\circ T)(x)|\det\mathsf{D}T(x)|
, where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathsf{D}T\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is the differential of
T
. We need the following computations for the relative entropy and
Fisher information of
\mu_{T}
.

Lemma 15
.

In the setting above,

\mathsf{H}(\mu_{T}\|\lambda_{n})=\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})+\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\log|\det\mathsf{D}T(T^{-1}(x))|\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

(98)

and

\mathsf{I}(\mu_{T}\|\lambda_{n})=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\frac{\mathsf{D}T(T^{-1}(x))\cdot\nabla f(x)}{f(x)}+\nabla\log|\det\mathsf{D}T(T^{-1}(x))|\right|^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

(99)

The proof is a straightforward computation using a change of variables and is thus omitted. These formulas along with the fact that any absolutely continuous measure can be transported to
\gamma_{n}
give rise to the following variational formula for relative entropy on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 16
.

Let
\mu
be an absolutely continuous measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then,

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})-\mathsf{H}(\gamma_{n}\|\lambda_{n})=\min_{T\in\mathrm{Diff}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\psi(T)

(100)

with equality if
T
is a transport map from
\mu
to
\gamma_{n}
, where

\psi(T)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\frac{\mathsf{D}T(T^{-1}(x))\cdot\nabla f(x)}{f(x)}+\nabla\log|\det\mathsf{D}T(T^{-1}(x))|\right|^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)-\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\log|\det\mathsf{D}T(T^{-1}(x))|\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

Proof.

Applying the logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
4
) to
\mu_{T}
and using Lemma
15
, we get

\begin{split}\mathsf{H}(\mu\|\lambda_{n})+\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}&\log|\det\mathsf{D}T(T^{-1}(x))|\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)-\mathsf{H}(\gamma_{n}\|\lambda_{n})\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\frac{\mathsf{D}T(T^{-1}(x))\cdot\nabla f(x)}{f(x)}+\nabla\log|\det\mathsf{D}T(T^{-1}(x))|\right|^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x),\end{split}

(101)

with equality only if
\mu_{T}=\gamma_{n}
. The existence of a map
T
transporting
\mu
to
\gamma_{n}
is a classical fact in optimal transport going back to at least
[

]
(see also
[

]
).
∎

We are not aware of a proof of (

7.
Tensorization of nonlinear logarithmic Sobolev inequalities in product spaces

Let
I
be a countable set,
\{(\mathbb X_{i},\mu_{i})\}_{i\in I}
a family of probability spaces where
\mathbb X_{i}
is countable and denote their product space by
(\mathbb X,\mu)=(\prodop\displaylimits_{i\in I}\mathbb X_{i},\otimes_{i\in I}\mu_{i})
. For a point
x=(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathbb{X}
and
i\in I
, we shall denote by
x_{\sim i}
the point
(x_{j})_{j\neq i}\in\prodop\displaylimits_{j\neq i}\mathbb{X}_{j}
and by
\mu_{\sim i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\otimes_{j\neq i}\mu_{j}
. Moreover, for a point
z\in\prodop\displaylimits_{j\neq i}\mathbb{X}_{j}
and a function
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}
, we shall
denote by
f_{z}:\mathbb{X}_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
the restriction of
f
given by

\forall\ y\in\mathbb{X}_{i},\qquad f_{z}(y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f(z,y).

(103)

For each
i\in I
, let
B_{i}
be a functional acting on measurable functions
g:\mathbb\prodop\displaylimits_{j\in J}\mathbb{X}_{j}\to\mathbb{R}
for any
J\subseteq I
. We shall say that the family of functionals
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in I}

disintegrates
if it satisfies the identities

\forall\ i\in I,\qquad\intop\nolimits B_{i}\big(f_{x_{\sim i}}\big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{\sim i}(x_{\sim i})=B_{i}(f).

(104)

Our main tensorization principle for nonlinear entropy inequalities is the following.

Theorem 18
.

Fix a countable set
I
and two collections of functionals
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in I}
,
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in I}
which disintegrate in the above sense. Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a concave function and suppose that, for any
i\in I
, every function
f_{i}:\mathbb X_{i}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfies the inequality

\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu_{i}}[f_{i}]\leq Q_{i}(f_{i})+\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{i}}[f_{i}]\Phi\left(\frac{M_{i}(f_{i})-Q_{i}(f_{i})}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{i}}[f_{i}]}\right).

(105)

Then, every function
f:\mathbb X\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfies

\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu}[f]\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}Q_{i}(f)+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}\Phi\left(\frac{M_{i}(f)-Q_{i}(f)}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]}\right).

(106)

Proof.

Combining the subadditivity of entropy and the assumptions of the theorem (including the disintegration of
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in I}
) we get that, for every
f:\mathbb X\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\begin{split}\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu}[f]&\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}\intop\nolimits\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu_{i}}[f_{x_{\sim i}}]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{\sim i}(x_{\sim i})\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:tensor-assumption}}}{{\leq}}\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}\intop\nolimits\left[Q_{i}(f_{x_{\sim i}})+\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{i}}[f_{x_{\sim i}}]\Phi\left(\frac{M_{i}(f_{x_{\sim i}})-Q_{i}(f_{x_{\sim i}})}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{i}}[f_{x_{\sim i}}]}\right)\right]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{\sim i}(x_{\sim i})\\
&=\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}Q_{i}(f)+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}\intop\nolimits\Phi\left(\frac{M_{i}(f_{x_{\sim i}})-Q_{i}(f_{x_{\sim i}})}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{i}}[f_{x_{\sim i}}]}\right)\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{i}}[f_{x_{\sim i}}]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{\sim i}(x_{\sim i}).\end{split}

(107)

Since
\intop\nolimits\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{i}}[f_{x_{\sim i}}]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{\sim i}(x_{\sim i})=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]
, and
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{i}}[f_{x_{\sim i}}]\geq 0
, it follows that
\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{i}}[f_{x_{\sim i}}]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{\sim i}(x_{\sim i})
defines a probability measure on
\prodop\displaylimits_{j\neq i}\mathbb X_{j}
. Hence, by Jensen’s inequality and disintegration, we get

\begin{split}\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu}[f]&\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}Q_{i}(f)+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}\Phi\left(\intop\nolimits\frac{M_{i}(f_{x_{\sim i}})-Q_{i}(f_{x_{\sim i}})}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{i}}[f_{x_{\sim i}}]}\cdot\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{i}}[f_{x_{\sim i}}]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{\sim i}(x_{\sim i})\right)\\
&=\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}Q_{i}(f)+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}\Phi\left(\frac{M_{i}(f)-Q_{i}(f)}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]}\right).\end{split}

(108)

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

8.1.
The frame bundle

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be a complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. The
orthonormal frame bundle

\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})
of
\mathsf{M}
is the set of all pairs of the form
(x,{\bf u})
, where
x\in\mathsf{M}
and
{\bf u}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to T_{x}\mathsf{M}
is a Euclidean isometry. We shall denote by
\pi:\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})\to\mathsf{M}
the natural projection given by
\pi(x,{\bf u})=x
. Any scalar-valued function
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
admits a natural
lift

{\bf f}:\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\forall\ (x,{\bf u})\in\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M}),\qquad{\bf f}(x,{\bf u})=f(x).

(109)

Abusing notation, we shall often identify the pair
(x,{\bf u})\in\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})
with the isomorphism
{\bf u}
. The frame bundle
\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})
is equipped with Bochner’s horizontal Laplacian

\Delta_{\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}H_{i}^{2},

(110)

and can be verified (see
[
38
, Proposition 3.1.2]
) that the lift
\bf{f}
of any function
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

\forall\ {\bf u}\in\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M}),\qquad\Delta_{\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})}{\bf f}({\bf u})=\Delta f(\pi{\bf u}),

(111)

where
\Delta
is the Laplace–Beltrami operator of
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
.

A curve
\{{\bf u}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
in
\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})
is called
horizontal
if for every
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the vector field
\{{\bf u}_{t}a\}_{t\in[0,1]}
is parallel along the curve
\{\pi{\bf u}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
in
\mathsf{M}
. A tangent vector
{\bf X}\in T_{{\bf u}}\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})
is called horizontal if it is the tangent vector of a horizontal curve passing from
{\bf u}
. For any vector
X\in T_{\pi{\bf u}}\mathsf{M}
there exists a unique horizontal vector
{\bf X}\in T_{{\bf u}}\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})
such that
\pi_{\ast}{\bf X}=X
; we say that
{\bf X}
is the horizontal lift of
X
at
{\bf u}
. Let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
i
-th fundamental
horizontal vector field

\mathsf{H}_{i}
evaluated at a point
{\bf u}\in\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})
is the horizontal lift of the vector
{\bf u}e_{i}\in T_{\pi{\bf u}}\mathsf{M}
. Thus, for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the lift
{\bf f}
of a function
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

\forall\ {\bf u}\in\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M}),\qquad\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf f}({\bf u})=\nabla_{{\bf u}e_{i}}f(\pi{\bf u}).

(112)

A vector field on
\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})
is called
horizontal
if it lies in the span of
\{\mathsf{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{H}_{n}\}
. We denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{hor}}
the natural inner product on the space of horizontal vector fields on
\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})
given by

\left\langle\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\mathsf{H}_{i},\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\mathsf{H}_{i}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{hor}}=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}W_{i}.

(113)

Moreover, we shall denote by
\nabla_{\mathrm{hor}}{\bf f}=(\mathsf{H}_{1}{\bf f},\ldots\mathsf{H}_{n}{\bf f})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the
horizontal gradient
of a given function
{\bf f}:\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})\to\mathbb{R}
. In this terminology, the horizontal Laplacian takes the form
\Delta_{\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})}{\bf f}=\sumop\displaylimits_{i}\mathsf{H}_{i}^{2}{\bf f}
.

We record for future reference the following very useful expression for the action of the commutator of
\Delta_{\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})}
with
\mathsf{H}_{i}
on lifted functions.

Lemma 21
.

If
f:(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function, then for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, its lift
{\bf f}
satisfies

\forall\ {\bf u}\in\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M}),\qquad\Delta_{\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf f}({\bf u})-\mathsf{H}_{i}\Delta_{\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})}{\bf f}({\bf u})=\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla f,{\bf u}e_{i})(\pi{\bf u}),

(114)

where
\mathrm{Ric}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Ricci tensor on
\mathsf{M}
.

Proof.

We shall follow the notation of
[
38
, Section 5.5]
. For
i,k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, it follows from
[
38
, Lemma 5.5.1]
that the commutator
[\mathsf{H}_{i},\mathsf{H}_{k}]
is a vertical vector field and thus
[\mathsf{H}_{i},\mathsf{H}_{k}]{\bf f}=0
, i.e.

\mathsf{H}_{k}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf f}=\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{k}{\bf f}.

(115)

Therefore, we have

\mathsf{H}_{k}^{2}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf f}=\mathsf{H}_{k}\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{k}{\bf f}=[\mathsf{H}_{k},\mathsf{H}_{i}]\mathsf{H}_{k}{\bf f}+\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{k}^{2}{\bf f}.

(116)

Substituting the expression of
[
38
, Lemma 5.5.1]
for
[\mathsf{H}_{k},\mathsf{H}_{i}]
, we get

[\mathsf{H}_{k},\mathsf{H}_{i}]\mathsf{H}_{k}{\bf f}=-\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b}\Omega_{ki}^{ab}V_{ab}\mathsf{H}_{k}{\bf f}=-\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b}\Omega_{ki}^{ab}[V_{ab},\mathsf{H}_{k}]{\bf f},

(117)

where in the last identity we used that
V_{ab}{\bf f}=0
. Again, by
[
38
, Lemma 5.5.1]
, if we denote by
A_{ab}^{k\ell}
the number
\tfrac{1}{2}
for
(a,b)=(k,\ell)
and
-\tfrac{1}{2}
for
(a,b)=(\ell,k)
, and zero otherwise, we obtain

-\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b}\Omega_{ki}^{ab}[V_{ab},\mathsf{H}_{k}]{\bf f}=-\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b,\ell}\Omega_{ki}^{ab}A_{ab}^{k\ell}\mathsf{H}_{\ell}{\bf f}=\frac{1}{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell}\big\{\Omega_{ki}^{\ell k}-\Omega_{ki}^{k\ell}\big\}\mathsf{H}_{\ell}{\bf f}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell}\Omega_{ki}^{\ell k}\mathsf{H}_{\ell}{\bf f},

(118)

where the antisymmetry of
\Omega
on the top indices follows from its definition in
[
38
, p. 153]
as it is an
\mathfrak{o}(d)
-valued tensor. Combining (
116
), (
117
), (
118
) and summing over
k
, we deduce that

\Delta_{\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf f}({\bf u})-\mathsf{H}_{i}\Delta_{\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})}{\bf f}({\bf u})=\sumop\displaylimits_{k,\ell}\Omega_{ki}^{\ell k}\mathsf{H}_{\ell}{\bf f}({\bf u})=\sumop\displaylimits_{k,\ell}\Omega_{ki}^{\ell k}\nabla_{{\bf u}e_{\ell}}f(\pi{\bf u}).

(119)

Now, observe that by the definition of
\Omega
in terms of the Riemann tensor
\mathsf{R}
of
\mathsf{M}
in
[
38
, p. 149]
,

\Omega_{ki}^{\ell k}=\mathsf{g}\big(\mathsf{R}({\bf u}e_{k},{\bf u}e_{i}){\bf u}e_{k},{\bf u}e_{\ell}\big)

(120)

and the conclusion follows from the definition of Ricci curvature.
∎

8.2.
Brownian motion on manifolds

Let
W_{t}=(W_{t}^{1},\ldots,W_{t}^{n})
be a standard Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be a complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. We consider the following stochastic differential equation on the frame bundle
\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})
,

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\Phi_{t}=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{H}_{i}(\Phi_{t})\circ\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{i},

(121)

where the shorthand notation
\circ
refers to the Stratonovitch integral. In Itô terms, the above SDE asserts that for every smooth
g:\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}g(\Phi_{t})=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{H}_{i}g(\Phi_{t})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{i}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})}g(\Phi_{t})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

(122)

For any initial condition
\Phi_{0}={\bf u}\in\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})
, this equation has a strong solution which does not blow up in finite time if the Ricci curvature of
\mathsf{M}
is bounded from below by any constant
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
(see
[
38
, Theorem 4.2.4]
and
[
63
]
for a sufficient and almost necessary condition for stochastic completeness). We denote by
B_{t}=\pi\Phi_{t}
, where
t\geq 0
, the
Brownian motion
on
\mathsf{M}
whose starting point is
x=\pi{\bf u}\in\mathsf{M}
. Applying (
122
), we deduce that for any smooth function
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
, the Brownian motion
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
satisfies the SDE

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}f(B_{t})=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\Phi_{t}^{i}f(B_{t})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{i}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta f(B_{t})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

(123)

8.3.
The Föllmer process and Lehec’s formula

In this section we introduce an analogue of the classical Föllmer process
[
58
,
33
,
46
]
on Riemannian manifolds (see also
[
38
, Section 5.4]
). We then present a result of Lehec
[
45
]
who used this process to give a stochastic proof of the dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality for manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below (see
[
10
,
6
]
for more general statements proven via semigroup arguments).

Let
W_{t}=(W_{t}^{1},\ldots,W_{t}^{n})
be a standard Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be a complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold whose Ricci curvature is bounded from below. We shall denote by
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
the volume measure on
\mathsf{M}
and by
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
the heat semigroup on
\mathsf{M}
. Recall that for a smooth function
h:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
, the action of the heat flow
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
g
is characterized by the ordinary differential equation

\forall\ t>0,\ x\in\mathsf{M},\qquad\frac{\partial P_{t}h}{\partial t}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta P_{t}h(x)

(124)

with initial condition
P_{0}h=h
on
\mathsf{M}
. We recall that the heat semigroup and the Laplacian commute:
\Delta P_{t}h=P_{t}\Delta h
, and we write
P_{t}\nabla^{2}f(x)
for the 2-tensor on
T_{x}\mathsf{M}
identified with the symmetric matrix
(P_{t}\nabla^{2}f(\Phi_{0}e_{i},\Phi_{0}e_{j})(x))_{i,j=1}^{n}
. Note that
P_{t}
and

\nabla^{2}

do not
commute (cf. Theorem
24
).

For a positive function
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
T>0
, we consider the following system of stochastic differential equations with respect to
(\Psi_{t},X_{t})\in\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})\times\mathsf{M}

\begin{cases}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\Psi_{t}=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{H}_{i}(\Psi_{t})\circ\big(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{i}+\Psi_{t}^{-1}\nabla\log P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\big)\\
X_{t}=\pi\Psi_{t}\end{cases},

(125)

where the notation
\circ
again refers to the Stratonovitch integral. It is known (see
[
45
, Theorem 7]
) that if
f
is a smooth-enough positive function, then for any initial condition
\Psi_{0}={\bf u}\in\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})
, the system (
125
) has a strong solution on
[0,T]
. In
[
45
, Theorem 7]
, Lehec proved the manifold version of an important representation formula for relative entropy in terms of the
Föllmer process

X_{t}
, first proven in their earlier work
[
44
]
.

Theorem 22

(Lehec)
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be a complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold whose Ricci curvature is bounded from below and fix a smooth enough positive density function
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
T>0
. If
\{X_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
is a solution of (
125
) with initial condition
\Psi_{0}={\bf u}
and
\pi{\bf u}=x
, then the relative entropy of the measure
\mu
with density
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}\delta_{x}}=f
is

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|P_{T}\delta_{x})=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\big|\nabla\log P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\big|^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right].

(126)

where
|v|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathsf{g}_{x}(v,v)
for
x\in\mathsf{M}
and
v\in T_{x}\mathsf{M}
.

It is worth pointing out that, in view of the decay and regularity of the heat kernel on space forms (see, e.g.,
[
21
, Chapter 6]
and
[
48
,
25
,
35
]
), it suffices to assume that the functions for which we wish to prove the logarithmic Sobolev inequalities of Theorem
4
are Lipschitz and bounded away from 0. Therefore, the regularity conditions required for the function
f
in Lehec’s theorem will always be tacitly assumed to hold.

We record for future reference the following computations (see also
[
38
, Equations (5.5.2) – (5.5.4)]
) on the SDE satisfied by partial derivatives of the logarithm of the heat kernel.

Lemma 23
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be a complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and fix a smooth enough positive density function
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
T>0
. Denote by
{F}_{t}:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
the function given by

\forall\ x\in\mathsf{M},\qquad{F}_{t}(x)=\log P_{T-t}f(x)

(127)

and by
{\bf F}_{t}
the lift of
F_{t}
onto
\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})
. If
\{X_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
is a solution of (
125
) and
\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a standard Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we have

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})=\big\langle\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t}),\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{t}\big\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Ric}\big(\nabla F_{t},\Psi_{t}e_{i}\big)(X_{t})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

(128)

Proof.

Using Itô’s formula and (
125
), we get (omitting the dependence on
\Psi_{t}
on the right-hand side of (
129
))

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})=\big\langle\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t},\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{t}\big\rangle+\left\{\frac{\partial\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}+\langle\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t},\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}{\bf F}_{t}\rangle\right\}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

(129)

Observe that the function
{F}_{t}
satisfies the equation

\frac{\partial{\bf F}_{t}}{\partial t}=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})}{\bf F}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\big|\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}{\bf F}_{t}\big|^{2},

(130)

which, after applying
\mathsf{H}_{i}
on both sides, gives

\frac{\partial\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}}{\partial t}=\mathsf{H}_{i}\frac{\partial{\bf F}_{t}}{\partial t}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}_{i}\Delta_{\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})}{\bf F}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}_{i}\big|\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}{\bf F}_{t}\big|^{2}.

(131)

Moreover, we have

\mathsf{H}_{i}\big|\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}{\bf F}_{t}\big|^{2}=\mathsf{H}_{i}\langle\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}{\bf F}_{t},\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}{\bf F}_{t}\rangle=2\langle\mathsf{H}_{i}\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}{\bf F}_{t},\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}{\bf F}_{t}\rangle=2\langle\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t},\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}{\bf F}_{t}\rangle,

(132)

where in the last identity we use that
[\mathsf{H}_{i},\mathsf{H}_{k}]{\bf h}=0
for any lifted function
{\bf h}
on
\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})

[
38
, Lemma 5.5.1]
. Substituting (
131
) and (
132
) in (
129
), we finally obtain

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})=\big\langle\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t}),\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{t}\big\rangle+\frac{1}{2}[\Delta_{\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})},\mathsf{H}_{i}]{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

(133)

and the desired identity follows immediately from Lemma
21
.
∎

8.4.
The heat flow on space forms

The classical Bochner formula (see, e.g.,
[
65
]
) implies that if
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
is a Riemannian manifold with constant Ricci curvature
\mathrm{Ric}=\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\forall\ t\geq 0,\qquad\nabla P_{t}f=e^{-\kappa t/2}P_{t}\nabla f

(134)

for every smooth function
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
. In
[
65
]
, Wang investigated commutation relations of this form for second order derivatives instead of the gradient
\nabla
. We shall use the following result.

Theorem 24

(Wang)
.

A Riemannian manifold
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
of dimension
n
has constant sectional curvature
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if the Hessian tensor of every smooth function
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

\forall\ r\geq 0,\qquad\nabla^{2}P_{r}f=e^{-n\kappa r}P_{t}\nabla^{2}f+\frac{1-e^{-n\kappa r}}{n}P_{r}\Delta f\cdot\mathsf{g}.

(135)

9.
Intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality in space forms

Having explained the necessary background we can now present Theorem
4
. We first recall that when
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}\delta_{x}}=f
, we have

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|P_{T}\delta_{x})=P_{T}(f\log f)(x)-P_{T}f(x)\log P_{T}f(x).

Theorem 25
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with constant sectional curvature
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
with the associated heat semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. Fix
T>0
,
x\in\mathsf{M}
, a smooth positive function
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\intop\nolimits_{M}f\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}\delta_{x}=1
, and let
\mu
be the probability measure with
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}\delta_{x}}=f
. Define the 2-tensor
C(t)=\frac{e^{n\kappa t}}{n\kappa}A+tB
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
A,B
are 2-tensors given by

\begin{cases}A=-e^{-n\kappa T}\big(P_{T}\nabla^{2}f(x)-\frac{1}{n}P_{T}\Delta f(x)\cdot\mathsf{g}\big)\\
B=\Big(\frac{(n-1)\kappa}{2}-\frac{\Delta P_{T}f(x)}{n}\Big)\cdot\mathsf{g},\end{cases}

(136)

and let
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\big(\nabla\log f\big)^{\otimes 2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}\log{\bf f}(\Psi_{T})\big)^{\otimes 2}\big]
. Then, we have the local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathsf{H}(\mu\|P_{T}\delta_{x})\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\mathrm{tr}\Big[e^{C(t)-C(T)}\Big(\mathsf{g}+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\big(\nabla\log f\big)^{\otimes 2}\!\!\intop\nolimits_{t}^{T}e^{2C(s)-2C(T)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{-1}\!\!\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\big(\nabla\log f\big)^{\otimes 2}e^{C(t)-C(T)}\Big]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

(137)

and the reverse local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathsf{H}(\mu\|P_{T}\delta_{x})\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathrm{tr}\Big[e^{C(t)-C(0)}\Big(\mathsf{g}-\big(\nabla\log P_{T}f(x)\big)^{\otimes 2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}e^{2C(s)-C(0)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{-1}\big(\nabla\log P_{T}f(x)\big)^{\otimes 2}e^{C(t)-C(0)}\Big]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(138)

The proof of Theorem
25
(see also the stronger Theorem
30
) is modeled after the stochastic proof by Eldan, Lehec, and Shenfeld
[
30
]
of the intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality in flat space (
7
) (and a weaker reverse inequality
[
30
, Theorem 3]
). A basic ingredient of this approach is deriving a stochastic differential equation for the
tensor
whose trace is the term
\big|\nabla\log P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\big|^{2}
in (
126
). This is the content of the next lemma for which we establish the following notation. Let
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a Brownian motion on
\mathsf{M}
with
B_{0}=x
. As before, we denote by
F_{t}
the function
\log P_{T-t}f
and by
{\bf F_{t}}
its horizontal lift on
\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M})
. Moreover, we shall denote by
G_{t}
the function
\exp F_{t}=P_{T-t}f
and by
{\bf G}_{t}=\exp{\bf F}_{t}
its lift. Consider the random matrices
\mathsf{Q}(t),\mathsf{P}(t)\in\mathbb M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
(the space of
n\times n
square matrices over
\mathbb{R}
) given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathsf{Q}_{ij}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F_{t}}(\Psi_{t})=\mathsf{H}_{j}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F_{t}}(\Psi_{t})=\mathsf{Q}_{ji}(t),\\
&\mathsf{P}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathsf{Q}(t)^{2}.\end{split}

(139)

We can now derive the aforementioned stochastic differential equation.

Lemma 26
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be a Riemannian manifold. In the terminology above, for every
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, there exists a martingale
\{M_{ij}(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
such that for
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F_{t}}(\Psi_{t})\cdot\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})=M_{ij}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}\mathrm{Ric}\big(\nabla F_{s}(X_{s}),\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{s}(\Psi_{s})\cdot\Psi_{s}e_{j}+\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{s}(\Psi_{s})\cdot\Psi_{s}e_{i}\big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}\mathsf{P}_{ij}(s)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Proof.

Observe that by the chain rule, we have (omitting the dependence on
\Psi_{t}
on the right-hand side below)

\mathsf{Q}_{ij}(t)=\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}=\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{j}\log{\bf G}_{t}=\frac{\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf G}_{t}}{{\bf G}_{t}}-\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}\cdot\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}

(140)

and by the definition and symmetry of the matrix
\mathsf{Q}(t)
,

\mathsf{P}_{ij}(t)=\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}\mathsf{Q}_{ik}(t)\mathsf{Q}_{kj}(t)=\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}\mathsf{H}_{k}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}\cdot\mathsf{H}_{k}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}=\left\langle\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t},\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}\right\rangle.

(141)

Combining Itô’s product rule with Lemma
23
, we get that for
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

\begin{split}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\big\{\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F_{t}}(\Psi_{t})\cdot&\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})\big\}=\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})+\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})\\
&=\left\{\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Ric}\big(\nabla F_{t},\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}\cdot\Psi_{t}e_{j}+\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}\cdot\Psi_{t}e_{i}\big)+\left\langle\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t},\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}\right\rangle\right\}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\quad+\left\langle\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}+\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t},\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{t}\right\rangle,\end{split}

(142)

where in the right-hand side we again omitted the dependence on
\Psi_{t}
and
X_{t}
. Denoting the term in the last line by
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathsf{M}_{ij}(t)
, it is clear that
\{M_{ij}(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
is a martingale and (
142
) becomes

\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F_{t}}(\Psi_{t})\cdot\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})=\mathsf{M}_{ij}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}\mathrm{Ric}\big(\nabla F_{s}(X_{s}),\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{s}(\Psi_{s})\cdot\Psi_{s}e_{j}+\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{s}(\Psi_{s})\cdot\Psi_{s}e_{i}\big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}\mathsf{P}_{ij}(s)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

where we also used (
141
). This is the desired identity.
∎

The stochastic differential equation of Lemma
26
will allow us to derive a differential equation for

\forall\ t\in(0,T),\qquad v_{ij}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})\cdot\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})\big],\qquad i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\};

(143)

note that with this notation, (
126
) reads
\mathsf{H}(\mu\|P_{T}\delta_{x})=\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathrm{tr}\big[v(t)\big]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t
. We will then turn the differential equation into a differential
inequality
from which Theorem
30
and Theorem
25
shall follow. To derive the differential equation for
v(t)
we start by defining

m(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}[-\mathsf{Q}(t)]\quad\text{and}\quad n(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{P}(t)].

(144)

Assuming that the underlying manifold
\mathsf{M}
is Einstein and taking expectations, we deduce the following differential equation for
v(t)
.

Lemma 27
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be an Einstein manifold with constant Ricci curvature
\mathrm{Ric}=\rho
for some
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
. For every
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
t\in(0,T)
, we have

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v_{ij}(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}=n_{ij}(t)+\rho v_{ij}(t).

(145)

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{M}
has constant Ricci curvature
\rho
, we have

\mathrm{Ric}\big(\nabla F_{s}(X_{s}),\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{s}(\Psi_{s})\cdot\Psi_{s}e_{j}\big)=\rho\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{s}(\Psi_{s})\cdot\mathsf{g}(\nabla F_{s}(X_{s}),\Psi_{s}e_{j})=\rho\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{s}(\Psi_{s})\cdot\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{s}(\Psi_{s}).

(146)

Plugging this in the rightmost term of Lemma
26
, we get that

\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F_{t}}(\Psi_{t})\cdot\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})=\mathsf{M}_{ij}(t)+\rho\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{s}(\Psi_{s})\cdot\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf F}_{s}(\Psi_{s})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}\mathsf{P}_{ij}(s)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

(147)

The result follows after taking expectation (since
\mathbb{E}M_{ij}(t)=M_{ij}(0)=0
) and differentiating.
∎

In order to turn (
145
) into a differential
inequality
we will use Jensen’s inequality
n(t)\succeq m(t)^{2}
where we used
\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{Q}^{2}
. To use the latter inequality we need to better understand the term
m(t)
. On manifolds of constant curvature,
m(t)
takes the following simple form.

Lemma 28
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with constant sectional curvature
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
. For every
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
t\in(0,T)
, we have

m_{ij}(t)=v_{ij}(t)-e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}P_{T}\nabla^{2}f(\Phi_{0}e_{i},\Phi_{0}e_{j})(x)-\frac{1-e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}}{n}P_{T}\Delta f(x)\cdot\delta_{ij}.

(148)

Proof.

Taking expectations in (
140
), we obtain

m_{ij}(t)=\mathbb{E}[-\mathsf{Q}_{ij}(t)]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Q1}}}{{=}}v_{ij}(t)-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf G}_{t}(\Psi_{t})}{{\bf G}_{t}(\Psi_{t})}\right].

(149)

It follows from (
125
) and (
121
) that
\Psi_{t}
has law
f(B_{T})
with respect to
\Phi_{t}
for every
t\in[0,T]
(see also the proof of
[
45
, Theorem 7]
for an argument based on Girsanov’s theorem). Therefore, by the tower property of conditional expectation, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf G}_{t}(\Psi_{t})}{{\bf G}_{t}(\Psi_{t})}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf G}_{t}(\Phi_{t})}{{\bf G}_{t}(\Phi_{t})}f(B_{T})\right]&=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf G}_{t}(\Phi_{t})}{{\bf G}_{t}(\Phi_{t})}\mathbb{E}\big[f(B_{T})\big|\{\Phi_{r}\}_{r\leq t}\big]\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf G}_{t}(\Phi_{t})}{{\bf G}_{t}(\Phi_{t})}P_{T-t}f(B_{t})\right]=\mathbb{E}\big[\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf G}_{t}(\Phi_{t})\big].\end{split}

(150)

Recall that for any function
h:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
with horizontal lift
{\bf h}
, we have

\forall\ {\bf u}\in\mathscr{O}(\mathsf{M}),\qquad\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf h}({\bf u})=\nabla^{2}h({\bf u}e_{i},{\bf u}e_{j})(\pi{\bf u}),

(151)

see, e.g.,
[
38
, Equation (2.2.3)]
. Combining (
151
) with Theorem
24
, we deduce that

\begin{split}\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{j}&{\bf G}_{t}(\Phi_{t})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:hess-ident}}}{{=}}\nabla^{2}P_{T-t}f(\Phi_{t}e_{i},\Phi_{t}e_{j})(B_{t})\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:wang}}}{{=}}e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}P_{T-t}\nabla^{2}f(\Phi_{t}e_{i},\Phi_{t}e_{j})(B_{t})+\frac{1-e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}}{n}P_{T-t}\Delta f(B_{t})\cdot\mathsf{g}(\Phi_{t}e_{i},\Phi_{t}e_{j})\\
&=e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}P_{T-t}\nabla^{2}f(\Phi_{t}e_{i},\Phi_{t}e_{j})(B_{t})+\frac{1-e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}}{n}P_{T-t}\Delta f(B_{t})\cdot\delta_{ij},\end{split}

(152)

where in the last equality we used that
\{\Phi_{t}e_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{t}e_{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
T_{B_{t}}\mathsf{M}
. Taking expectations on both sides, we get

\mathbb{E}\big[\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf G}_{t}(\Phi_{t})\big]=e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}\mathbb{E}\big[P_{T-t}\nabla^{2}f(\Phi_{t}e_{i},\Phi_{t}e_{j})(B_{t})\big]+\frac{1-e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}}{n}\mathbb{E}\big[P_{T-t}\Delta f(B_{t})\big]\cdot\delta_{ij}.

(153)

By the definition
[
65
, Equation (1.2)]
of the action of
\{P_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}
on tensors, we have

\mathbb{E}\big[P_{T-t}\nabla^{2}f(\Phi_{t}e_{i},\Phi_{t}e_{j})(B_{t})\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[\nabla^{2}f(\Phi_{T}e_{i},\Phi_{T}e_{j})(B_{T})\big]=P_{T}\nabla^{2}f(\Phi_{0}e_{i},\Phi_{0}e_{j})(x),

where the last identity follows from the definition of stochastic parallel transport given by
\{\Phi_{s}\circ\Phi_{0}^{-1}\}_{s\geq 0}
(see
[
38
, Section 2.3]
). Similarly, we have

\mathbb{E}\big[P_{T-t}\Delta f(B_{t})\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta f(B_{T})\big]=P_{T}\Delta f(x)

(154)

and combining everything we deduce that

\mathbb{E}\big[\mathsf{H}_{i}\mathsf{H}_{j}{\bf G}_{t}(\Phi_{t})\big]=e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}P_{T}\nabla^{2}f(\Phi_{0}e_{i},\Phi_{0}e_{j})(x)+\frac{1-e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}}{n}P_{T}\Delta f(x)\cdot\delta_{ij}.

(155)

Plugging (
155
) and (
150
) in (
149
) completes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to derive the differential inequality for
v(t)
. For simplicity, we shall denote by
c_{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}P_{T}\Delta f(x)
and by
J_{T}
the symmetric matrix with

(J_{T})_{ij}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}P_{T}\nabla^{2}f(\Phi_{0}e_{i},\Phi_{0}e_{j})(x)-\frac{1}{n}P_{T}\Delta f(x)\cdot\delta_{ij},

(156)

which satisfies
\mathrm{tr}J_{T}=0
. Combining all of the above, we get the following matrix inequality:

Proposition 29
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with constant sectional curvature
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
. For every
t\in(0,T)
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\succeq v(t)^{2}-\Big(e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}J_{T}+\tfrac{c_{T}}{n}\cdot\mathsf{Id}_{n}\Big)&v(t)-v(t)\Big(e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}J_{T}+\tfrac{c_{T}}{n}\cdot\mathsf{Id}_{n}\Big)\\
&+\Big(e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}J_{T}+\tfrac{c_{T}}{n}\cdot\mathsf{Id}_{n}\Big)^{2}+(n-1)\kappa v(t),\end{split}

(157)

so in particular,

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\succeq v(t)^{2}+\Big(\big(\tfrac{(n-1)\kappa}{2}-\tfrac{c_{T}}{n}\big)\cdot\mathsf{Id}_{n}-e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}J_{T}\Big)v(t)+v(t)\Big(\big(\tfrac{(n-1)\kappa}{2}-\tfrac{c_{T}}{n}\big)\cdot\mathsf{Id}_{n}-e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}J_{T}\Big),

(158)

where
\succeq
is the inequalities in the positive semidefinite ordering.

Proof.

Combining the matrix Jensen inequality

n(t)=\mathbb{E}\big[Q(t)^{2}\big]\succeq\mathbb{E}\big[-Q(t)\big]^{2}=m(t)^{2}

(159)

with (
145
), (
148
) and expanding, we get (
157
)

\begin{split}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\succeq v(t)^{2}-\Big(e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}J_{T}+\tfrac{c_{T}}{n}\cdot\mathsf{Id}_{n}\Big)&v(t)-v(t)\Big(e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}J_{T}+\tfrac{c_{T}}{n}\cdot\mathsf{Id}_{n}\Big)\\
&+\Big(e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}J_{T}+\tfrac{c_{T}}{n}\cdot\mathsf{Id}_{n}\Big)^{2}+(n-1)\kappa v(t).\end{split}

(160)

The inequality (
158
) follows since the squared matrix is positive semidefinite.
∎

Proposition
29
allows us to deduce the following local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities which are, however, non-explicit.

Theorem 30
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with constant sectional curvature
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
. Fix
T>0
,
x\in\mathsf{M}
, a smooth positive function
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\intop\nolimits_{M}f\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}\delta_{x}=1
, and let
\mu
be the probability measure with
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}\delta_{x}}=f
. Suppose there is a family of matrices
U(t)\in\mathbb M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
for
t\in[0,T]
which solves the equation

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}U(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}=U(t)^{2}-\Big(e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}J_{T}+\tfrac{c_{T}}{n}\cdot\mathsf{Id}_{n}\Big)&U(t)-U(t)\Big(e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}J_{T}+\tfrac{c_{T}}{n}\cdot\mathsf{Id}_{n}\Big)\\
&+\Big(e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}J_{T}+\tfrac{c_{T}}{n}\cdot\mathsf{Id}_{n}\Big)^{2}+(n-1)\kappa U(t),\end{split}

(161)

with either initial condition
U(0):=v(0)
or
U(T):=v(T)
. Then, we have the local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|P_{T}\delta_{x})\leq\ \frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathrm{tr}\big[U(t)\big]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\quad U(T)=\mathbb{E}\big(\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}\log f(\Psi_{T})\big)^{\otimes 2}.

(162)

and the reverse local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|P_{T}\delta_{x})\geq\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathrm{tr}\big[U(t)\big]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\quad U(0)=\big(\nabla^{\mathrm{hor}}\log P_{T}f(x)\big)^{\otimes 2}.

(163)

Proof.

Lehec’s formula (
126
) implies

\mathsf{H}(\mu\|P_{T}\delta_{x})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:lehec-formula}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\big|\nabla F_{t}(X_{t})\big|^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right]=\frac{1}{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F}_{t}(\Psi_{t})^{2}\big]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathrm{tr}\big[v(t)\big]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

(164)

For the reverse local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality, we note that
U(0)=v(0)
so the result follows by (

Lemma 31

T>\varepsilon>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}

\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}

\forall\ t\in(\varepsilon,T),\qquad\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\succeq V(t)^{2}+\big(e^{\gamma t}A+B\big)V(t)+V(t)\big(e^{\gamma t}A+B\big)

\forall\ t\in[\varepsilon,T],\qquad V(t)\preceq e^{C(t)-C(T)}\Big(\mathsf{Id}_{n}+V(T)\intop\nolimits_{t}^{T}e^{2C(s)-2C(T)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{-1}V(T)e^{C(t)-C(T)}.

(166)

\forall\ t\in[\varepsilon,T],\qquad V(t)\succeq e^{C(t)-C(\varepsilon)}\Big(\mathsf{Id}_{n}-V(\varepsilon)\intop\nolimits_{\varepsilon}^{t}e^{2C(s)-2C(\varepsilon)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{-1}V(\varepsilon)e^{C(t)-C(\varepsilon)}.

(167)

Moreover, the right-hand side of (

t\in(\varepsilon,T)

Proof.

Since
A
and

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}e^{C(t)}=\big(e^{\gamma t}A+B\big)e^{C(t)}=e^{C(t)}\big(e^{\gamma t}A+B\big).

(168)

near
\varepsilon

t\in(\varepsilon,t_{\max})

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(t)^{-1}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\preceq-\mathsf{Id}_{n}-V(t)^{-1}C^{\prime}(t)-C^{\prime}(t)V(t)^{-1},

(169)

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\big[e^{C(t)}V(t)^{-1}e^{C(t)}\big]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:der-expo}}}{{=}}e^{C(t)}\big(C^{\prime}(t)V(t)^{-1}+\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(t)^{-1}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}+V(t)^{-1}C^{\prime}(t)\Big)e^{C(t)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ODE-for-V-1}}}{{\preceq}}-e^{2C(t)},

(170)

Semigroup vs. stochastic interpolation.
The idea of writing the relative entropy as an integral of a
gradient term
goes back to the beginning of the Bakry–Émery theory of functional inequalities (see
[
8
,
4
]
or
[
9
, Section 5.5]
). Such gradient terms often satisfy differential inequalities à la Proposition
29
which allow for the use of comparison principles in the spirit of Lemma
31
. For instance, Lehec in
[
45
]
considered the scalar-valued function
\alpha:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\forall\ t\in[0,T],\qquad\alpha(t)=\mathbb{E}\big[|\nabla F_{t}(X_{t})|^{2}\big]

(187)

and showed that

\forall\ t\in(0,T),\qquad\alpha^{\prime}(t)\geq\frac{1}{n}\big(\alpha(t)-c_{T}\big)^{2}+(n-1)\kappa\alpha(t)\geq\frac{\alpha(t)}{n}\big(\alpha(t)+n(n-1)\kappa-2c_{T}\big).

(188)

Applying a standard comparison principle to the latter inequality, he then derived a dimensional upper bound for the relative entropy, see
[
45
, Equation (25)]
. It is worth pointing out that (
188
) is also a consequence of (
157
) after taking traces and using the elementary inequality
\mathrm{tr}C^{2}\geq\tfrac{(\mathrm{tr}C)^{2}}{n}
which holds for all
n\times n
positive semidefinite matrices
C
. A close inspection of the arguments of this section reveals that the logarithmic Sobolev inequality of Theorem
25
is a strengthening of Lehec’s result for manifolds of constant sectional curvature.

By reasoning similar to (
150
), for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\big[(\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf F_{t}}(\Psi_{t}))^{2}\big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{(\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf G}_{t}(\Psi_{t}))^{2}}{{\bf G}_{t}(\Psi_{t})^{2}}\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{(\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf G}_{t}(\Phi_{t}))^{2}}{{(\bf G}_{t}(\Phi_{t}))^{2}}f(B_{T})\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{(\mathsf{H}_{i}{\bf G}_{t}(\Phi_{t}))^{2}}{{\bf G}_{t}(\Phi_{t})}\Big]

(189)

and thus

\forall\ t\in(0,T),\qquad\alpha(t)=P_{t}\left[\frac{|\nabla P_{T-t}f|^{2}}{P_{T-t}f}\right](x).

(190)

This semigroup representation of
\alpha(t)
was used by Bakry, Bolley and Gentil in
[
6
, p. 405]
to give an independent proof of inequality (
188
) for semigroups satisfying the curvature dimension condition
\mathsf{CD}(\rho,n)
, where
\rho=(n-1)\kappa
(observe that
\alpha(t)
is denoted by
\Lambda^{\prime}(t)
in their paper). Their main result
[
6
, Theorem 2.2]
improves upon
[
45
, Equation (25)]
as they did not disregard the nonnegative constant in the second inequality of (
188
) and thus get to apply a tighter comparison principle. One could implement a similar strategy in the matricial setting treated here, by replacing the matrix inequality (
158
) with the stronger inequality (
157
) and solving the corresponding ordinary differential equation. However, solutions of this equation appear to be non-explicit, so we chose the comparatively simpler presentation of Theorem
25
for clarity of the exposition. If one were to implement this reasoning, it is clear from the proofs of this section that the resulting inequality would improve upon
[
6
, Theorem 2.2]
.

10.1.
Matrix inequalities

The main result of this section is the following Hamilton-type matrix inequality, namely, Theorem

Theorem 33
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with constant nonpositive sectional curvature
\kappa\leq 0

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{if, either }\kappa=0,\text{ or }\kappa<0\text{ and }\frac{4}{n^{2}\kappa}\frac{\Delta P_{T}f(x)}{P_{T}f(x)}=1,\quad\text{then}\quad-\nabla^{2}\log P_{T}f(x)\preceq\frac{1}{T}\mathsf{Id}_{n}\quad\forall x\in\mathsf{M},\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{if}\quad\frac{4}{n^{2}\kappa}\frac{\Delta P_{T}f(x)}{P_{T}f(x)})>1,\\

Remark 34
.

To put Theorem

[
6

(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})

n

(193)

\displaystyle-\Delta\log P_{T}f(x)<

(194)

10.2.
Intrinsic dimensional local logarithmic Sobolev inequalities in hyperbolic spaces

In this section we prove local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequalities for the hyperbolic space, namely, Theorem
6
. The inequalities provided by Theorem
40
below will be obtained as a consequence of the differential inequality of Lemma
35
together with the following simple observation:

Lemma 39
.

Let
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
\intop\nolimits_{M}f\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}\delta_{x}=1

and let
\mu
be the probability measure with
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}\delta_{x}}=f
. Then,

\displaystyle P_{T}(f\log f)(x)-P_{T}f(x)\log P_{T}f(x)=H(\mu|P_{T}\delta_{x})=-\frac{P_{T}\Delta f(x)}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathrm{tr}[m(t)]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

(222)

Proof.

By Theorem
22
, (
143
), and (
199
),

H(\mu|P_{T}\delta_{x})=\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathrm{tr}[v(t)]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathrm{tr}[m(t)]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathrm{tr}[u(t)]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

(223)

where we recall (
198
),

u(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}e^{-n\kappa(T-t)}J_{T}+\frac{P_{T}\Delta f(x)}{n}\cdot\mathsf{g}

(224)

with
(J_{T})_{ij}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}P_{T}\nabla^{2}f(\Phi_{0}e_{i},\Phi_{0}e_{j})(x)-\frac{1}{n}P_{T}\Delta f(x)\cdot\delta_{ij}
. The proof is complete since
\mathrm{tr}[J_{T}]=0
.
∎

Theorem 40
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{g})
be the
n
-dimensional hyperbolic space with sectional curvature
\kappa<0
with the associated heat semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. Fix
T>0
,
x\in\mathsf{M}
, a smooth positive function
f:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\intop\nolimits_{M}f\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}\delta_{x}=1
,
and let
\mu
be the probability measure with
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}\delta_{x}}=f
.
Then, with

\displaystyle\lambda\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{n^{2}\kappa^{2}}{4}\left\{\frac{4}{n^{2}\kappa}\Delta P_{T}f(x)-1\right\},\quad\alpha_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}\arctan\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\left(\sigma_{i}+\frac{n\kappa}{2}\right)\right)&\text{if }\lambda<0,\\
-\frac{2}{2\sigma_{i}+n\kappa}&\text{if }\lambda=0,\\
\arctanh\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\lambda}}\left(\sigma_{i}+\frac{n\kappa}{2}\right)\right)&\text{if }\lambda>0,\\
\end{cases}

(225)

we have the local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{T}(f\log f)(x)-P_{T}f(x)\log P_{T}f(x)\leq-\frac{P_{T}\Delta f(x)}{2}-\frac{n^{2}\kappa T}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\begin{cases}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\cos(\alpha_{i})}{\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}T+\alpha_{i})}\right)&\text{if }\lambda>0\\
\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}}{T+\alpha_{i}}\right)&\text{if }\lambda=0\\
\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\cosh(\alpha_{i})}{\cosh(\sqrt{-\lambda}T+\alpha_{i})}\right)&\text{if }\lambda<0\\
\end{cases}\end{split}

(226)

where
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are the eigenvalues of
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[-\nabla^{2}\log f]
, and the reverse local intrinsic dimensional logarithmic Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{T}(f\log f)(x)-P_{T}f(x)\log P_{T}f(x)\geq-\frac{P_{T}\Delta f(x)}{2}-\frac{n^{2}\kappa T}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\begin{cases}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\cos(\alpha_{i})}{\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}T+\alpha_{i})}\right)&\text{if }\lambda>0\\
\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}}{T+\alpha_{i}}\right)&\text{if }\lambda=0\\
\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\cosh(\alpha_{i})}{\cosh(\sqrt{-\lambda}T+\alpha_{i})}\right)&\text{if }\lambda<0\\
\end{cases}\end{split}

(227)

where
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are the eigenvalues of
-\nabla^{2}\log P_{T}f(x)

Proof.

Given any basis
\{\theta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}

\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\forall\ t\in[0,T],\qquad\mathrm{tr}[m(t)]\geq\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i,\lambda}(t)\right)-\frac{n^{2}\kappa}{2}

10.3.
Discussion

We conclude this section by discussing the roles of matrix differential inequalities in our proofs.

Matrix differential inequalities.
The master matrix differential inequality (
157
), which is at the core of all of our proofs, can be expressed either in terms of
v(t)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\succeq v(t)^{2}-u(t)v(t)-v(t)u(t)+u(t)^{2}+(n-1)\kappa v(t),

(236)

or in terms of
m(t)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\succeq m(t)^{2}+n\kappa m(t)-\kappa v(t)+\kappa c_{T}\cdot\mathsf{g}.

(237)

The inequalities (
236
) and (
237
) are equivalent and contain the same information. In particular, in
flat
space forms, where
\kappa=0
, both inequalities are of the form
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\succeq W(t)^{2}
. In curved spaces, there are two different ways to proceed from (
236
) and (
237
):

(1)

Omit the term
u(t)^{2}
from (
236
) to get

\displaystyle v(t)\succeq U(t)

(238)

where

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}U(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}=U(t)^{2}-u(t)U(t)-U(t)u(t)+(n-1)\kappa U(t).

(239)

The point of omitting
u^{2}(t)
is that equation (
239
) can be solved explicitly, in contrast to the equation resulting if we keep the
u^{2}(t)
term.
1
1

1

If we take the trace in (
236
) then the equation with the
u(t)^{2}
term can be solved explicitly—see the end of section
9
. However, if we do so we would get the ambient dimension
n
rather than the intrinsic dimension.

(2)

Omit the term
-\kappa v(t)
from (
237
), which can be done only in
negatively
curved space forms
to get

\displaystyle m(t)\succeq U(t)

(240)

where

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}U(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}=U(t)^{2}+n\kappa U(t)+\kappa c_{T}\cdot\mathsf{g}.

(241)

Again, the point of omitting
-\kappa v(t)
is so that (
241
) can be solved explicitly. Note that in flat spaces, there is no loss in omitting
-\kappa v(t)
.

Matrix vs. trace differential inequalities.

The proofs of Theorem
40
and Theorem
25
proceed along similar but different lines. Both proofs start by establishing an inequality of the form

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\succeq\mathcal{F}(W(t))

(242)

for some quadratic functional
\mathcal{F}
. The goal is to bound
\mathrm{tr}[W(t)]
which can be achieve by two means. Letting
\{U(t)\}
be the solution to

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}U(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}=\mathcal{F}(U(t))

(243)

we could:

(1)

Argue that
W(t)\succeq U(t)
and then take the trace on both sides to get

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}[W(t)]\geq\mathrm{tr}[U(t)].

(244)

This is the method used to prove Theorem
25
and Theorem
32
(with different functionals
\mathcal{F}
).

(2)

When
\mathcal{F}
has scalar (rather than matrix) coefficients, it holds that

\langle\mathcal{F}(W(t))\theta,\theta\rangle\geq\mathcal{F}(\langle W(t)\theta,\theta\rangle)

(245)

with strict inequality unless
\theta
is an eigenvector of
W(t)
. We can then
\{\theta_{i}\}
to be any basis and let
\phi_{i,W}(t):=\langle W(t)\theta_{i},\theta_{i}\rangle
,
\phi_{i,U}(t):=\langle U(t)\theta_{i},\theta_{i}\rangle
so

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi_{i,W}(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\geq\mathcal{F}(\phi_{i,W}(t)),\quad\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi_{i,U}(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}=\mathcal{F}(\phi_{i,U}(t)),

(246)

which shows
\phi_{i,W}(t)\geq\phi_{i,U}(t)
. Hence, for any basis
\{\theta_{i}\}
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}[W(t)]=\sumop\displaylimits_{i}\phi_{i,W}(t)\geq\sumop\displaylimits_{i}\phi_{i,U}(t)=\mathrm{tr}[U(t)].

(247)

This is the method used to prove Theorem
40
.

While both methods lead to the inequality

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}[W(t)]\geq\mathrm{tr}[U(t)],

(248)

the second method is weaker since the inequality
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi_{i,W}(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\geq\mathcal{F}(\phi_{i,W}(t))
is weaker in principle than
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\succeq\mathcal{F}(W(t))
unless
\theta_{i}
is an eigenvector of
W(t)
. However, for the purpose of proving an inequality for the
trace
, there is no loss since the trace is invariant under rotations so for each
t
we can introduce a rotation
R(t)
which takes
\{\theta_{i}\}
to the eigenvectors of
W(t)
or
U(t)
.

Abstract
. In this paper we provide necessary and sufficient (KKT) conditions for global optimality for a new class of possibly nonconvex quadratically constrained quadratic programming (QCQP) problems, denoted by S-QCQP. The class consists of QCQP problems where the matrices of the quadratic components are formed by a scalar times the identity matrix. Our result relies on a generalized version of the S-Lemma, stated in the context of general QCQP problems. Moreover, we prove the exactness of the SDP and the SOCP relaxations for S-QCQP.

x=x^{*}

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x^{*})\geq 0
.
Since
\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x^{*})\leq 0

\forall\,k\in\{1,...,m\}
, it must be
\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x^{*})=0

\forall\,k\in\{1,...,m\}
which proves (ii) of (
3.8
).
By (
3.10
), for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(3.11)

\gamma_{0}J(x)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)\geq\gamma_{0}J(x^{*})

Hence
L(x):=\gamma_{0}J(x)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)
attains its minimum over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
at
x^{*}
.
We can apply
Theorem 2
for a twice continuously differentiable function
L(x)
. The necessary optimality conditions
\nabla_{x}L(x^{*})=0
and
\nabla^{2}_{x}L(x^{*})\succeq 0
are respectively equivalent to
the conditions (i) and (iii) of (
3.8
), which finishes the proof of the first part.

Suppose now that (
3.9
) holds, i.e., there exists a point
x_{0}
such that
f_{k}(x_{0})<0

\forall\,k=1,...,m
.
If it were
\gamma_{0}=0
, then by (
3.10
), it would be
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which would contradict (
3.9
). Hence
\gamma_{0}>0
and the Fritz-John conditions becomes the KKT condition, i.e. (
KKT
) holds.

To complete the proof, we show that conditions (
KKT
) are also sufficient for optimality.
Assume that there exists
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
which is feasible to (
QCQP
) and
(\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\backslash\textbf{0}_{m}
such that (
KKT
) holds.
The Lagrangian for (
QCQP
) is:

(3.12)

L(x,\gamma):=J(x)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)=x^{T}A(\gamma)x+2b(\gamma)^{T}x+c(\gamma)

with
A(\gamma)=A_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}A_{k}
,
b(\gamma)=b_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}b_{k}
and
c(\gamma)=c_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}c_{k}
.

Notice that the Lagrangian
L(x,\gamma)
is convex with respect to
x
, since
A(\gamma)=A_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}A_{k}\succeq 0
by (
KKT
).

Hence
x^{*}
such that
\nabla_{x}L(x^{*})=0
is the minimum of
L(x,\gamma)
for
\gamma
fixed, by
Theorem 3
.

By (
KKT
),
\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x^{*})=0
for
k=1,...,m
. We have

(3.13)

J(x)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)\geq J(x^{*})+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x^{*})=J(x^{*})\ \ \forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For any
x
feasible for (
QCQP
),
f_{k}(x)\leq 0
and hence

(3.14)

J(x)\geq J(x)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}f_{k}(x)

Combining (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), for any
x
feasible for (
QCQP
), we have

(3.15)

J(x)\geq J(x^{*})

which proves that
x^{*}
is a global minimum for (
QCQP
).
∎

4.
Global minima characterization for (S-QCQP)

In the present section we use the results of Section
3
to provide (
KKT
) characterization of global minima for (
S-QCQP
). The main result of this section is Theorem
8
.

Let
x^{*}
be the global minimum of (
S-QCQP
).
As in (
7
), we use the notation

f_{0}(x):=J(x)-J(x^{*})=a_{J}\|x\|^{2}+b_{J}^{T}x-a_{J}\|x^{*}\|^{2}-2b_{J}^{T}x^{*}

In the case of (
S-QCQP
), the set
\Omega_{0}
, defined in (
3.2
), take the form

\Omega_{0}:=\{(f_{0}(x),f_{1}(x),..,f_{m}(x))|x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}+int\mathbb{R}^{m+1}_{+}

where

f_{k}(x):=a_{k}\|x\|^{2}+2b_{k}^{T}x+c_{k}\ \ k=0,...,m

with
a_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 8
.

Consider problem (
S-QCQP
) with
m+1<n
. The set
\Omega_{0}
defined in (
3.2
) is convex.

Proof.

In order to show that
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
is convex, take any
v:=(v_{0},...,v_{m}),w:=(w_{0},...,w_{m})\in\Omega_{0}
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
.
There exist
x_{v},x_{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(4.1)

f_{k}(x_{v})<v_{k}\ \ \text{and }f_{k}(x_{w})<w_{k}\ \ \forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}.

Consider the convex combination
\lambda v+(1-\lambda)w
. Let
(\lambda v+(1-\lambda)w)_{k}
be the
k
-th component of
\lambda v+(1-\lambda)w
.
By (
4.1
), we have

(4.2)

\lambda f_{k}(x_{v})+(1-\lambda)f_{k}(x_{w})<(\lambda v+(1-\lambda)w)_{k}\ \ \forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}.

In order to prove that
\Omega_{0}
is convex, we show that the convex combination

\lambda v+(1-\lambda)w
belongs to
\Omega_{0}
, i.e. there exists
\tilde{x}
such that
\forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}

(4.3)

f_{k}(\tilde{x})<(\lambda v+(1-\lambda)w)_{k}.

Formulas (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) together imply that
\Omega_{0}
is convex if there exists
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}

(4.4)

f_{k}(\tilde{x})\leq\lambda f_{k}(x_{v})+(1-\lambda)f_{k}(x_{w}).

Note that if
x_{v}=x_{w}
, Then (
4.4
) trivially holds for
\tilde{x}=x_{v}=x_{w}
. From now on, we assume that
x_{v}
and
x_{w}
are distinct vectors,
x_{v}\neq x_{w}
. .
Let us take
\tilde{x}
such that

(4.5)

\tilde{x}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ \|x\|^{2}=\lambda\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2}\}.

In general, the set
\mathcal{S}^{n}
is a sphere centered at zero with radius
\lambda\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2}
. In particular case, when both
x_{v}=x_{w}=0
the set
\mathcal{S}^{n}
reduces to
\{0\}
, but this is impossible, since we assumed that
x_{v}
and
x_{w}
are distinct vectors.

Clearly,
\tilde{x}
satisfies (
4.3
) if
\forall\,k\in\{0,...,m\}
:

(4.6)

\begin{split}&f_{k}(\tilde{x})\leq\lambda f_{k}(x_{v})+(1-\lambda)f_{k}(x_{w})<\lambda v_{k}+(1-\lambda)w_{k}.\\
&a_{k}\|\tilde{x}\|^{2}+2b_{k}^{T}\tilde{x}+c_{k}\leq a_{k}(\lambda\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2})+2b_{k}^{T}(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w})+c_{k}.\end{split}

To ensure (
4.6
), we show that we can choose
\tilde{x}
satisfying (
4.5
) such that

(4.7)

\begin{split}&b_{k}^{T}\tilde{x}\leq b_{k}^{T}(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}),\\
&b_{k}^{T}(\tilde{x}-(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}))\leq 0.\end{split}

Define
y:=\tilde{x}-(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Observe that
y=y(\lambda,\tilde{x})
, where
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
\tilde{x}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
.
Clearly, the solution set of the system of inequalities

b_{k}^{T}y\leq 0\ \ k\in\{0,...,m\}

includes all the solutions of the system of homogeneous equations

(4.8)

\begin{split}&b_{k}^{T}y=0\ \ k\in\{0,...,m\}\\
&\begin{bmatrix}b^{0}_{1}&\cdots&b^{0}_{n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
b^{m}_{1}&\cdots&b^{m}_{n}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}y_{1}\\
\vdots\\
y_{n}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{bmatrix}\end{split}

In the sequel we will look for
y=\tilde{x}-(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
among the solutions of the system of equations (
4.8
). Let

B=\begin{bmatrix}b^{0}_{1}&\cdots&b^{0}_{n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
b^{m}_{1}&\cdots&b^{m}_{n}\end{bmatrix}

By assumption,
rank(B)\leq m+1<n
.
The rank-nullity theorem states that the solutions of system (
4.8
) form a vector space of dimension
p=n-rank(B)
.

Let
u_{1},...,u_{p}
be a basis for the vector space
of solutions to system (
4.8
).
Given some scalars
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i\in\{1,...,p\}

a solution
y
of (
4.8
) can be written as
y=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\alpha_{i}u_{i}
.

To complete the proof, we need to show that there exist some scalars
\alpha_{i}

i\in\{1,...,p\}
such that

(4.9)

\tilde{x}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\alpha_{i}u_{i}+\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}\in\mathcal{S}^{n},

where
\mathcal{S}^{n}
is defined as in (
4.5
).
In fact, by choosing
\tilde{x}
as in (
4.9
),
y=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\alpha_{i}u_{i}
is a solution of (
4.8
) and
\tilde{x}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
satisfies (
4.7
).
We have to prove that one can choose
\alpha_{i}
,
i=1,...,p
such that

(4.10)

\begin{split}&\tilde{x}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\alpha_{i}u_{i}+\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}\\
&\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\alpha_{i}u_{i}+\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}\|^{2}=\lambda\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2}\end{split}

Below we show that we can only consider
y
of the form
y=\alpha_{*}u_{*}
, where an index
{*}
is chosen arbitrarily from
\{1,...,p\}.
Indeed, given an index
*\in\{1,...,p\}
, set
\alpha_{i}=0
for
i\in\{1,...,p\}\setminus\{*\}
and
\alpha_{*}\neq 0
. With this choice of
\alpha_{i}
,
i=1,...,p
we can rewrite (
4.10
) as

\tilde{x}=\alpha_{*}u_{*}+\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}

and consequently, we need to find
\alpha_{*}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(4.11)

\begin{split}&\|\alpha_{*}u_{*}+\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}\|^{2}=\\
&=\alpha_{*}^{2}\|u_{*}\|^{2}+\|\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}\|^{2}+2\alpha_{*}\langle u_{*},\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}\rangle=\\
&=\lambda\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2}\end{split}

By Corollary 2.14 of
[
5
]
,

(4.12)

\|(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w})\|^{2}=\lambda\|x_{v}\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\|x_{w}\|^{2}-\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x_{v}-x_{w}\|^{2}.

Hence, (
4.11
) becomes

(4.13)

\alpha_{*}^{2}\|u_{*}\|^{2}+2\alpha_{*}\langle u_{*},\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w}\rangle-\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x_{v}-x_{w}\|^{2}=0

Note that by (
4.13
) it must be
\alpha_{*}\neq 0
since
x_{v}
and
x_{w}
are distinct vectors.
There exists
\alpha_{*}
such that (
4.13
) holds (i.e.
\tilde{x}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
), if and only if

\displaystyle\Delta:=(\langle u_{*},(\lambda x_{v}+(1-\lambda)x_{w})\rangle)^{2}+\lambda(1-\lambda)\|u_{*}\|^{2}\|x_{v}-x_{w}\|^{2}\geq 0,

which holds for every
x_{v},x_{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

By the definition of
y
and (
4.9
),
y(\tilde{x})=a_{*}u_{*}
belongs to the vector space of the solutions of (
4.8
).
Hence, (
4.7
) holds by choosing
\tilde{x}
as in (
4.9
), with
\alpha_{i}=0
for
i\in\{1,...,p\}\setminus\{*\}
and
\alpha_{*}\neq 0
satisfying (
4.13
).
∎

Remark 2
.

(1)

Observe that the assumption

) conditions are necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for (

.

Consider (

) such that

.
Let

(

∎

.

5.
SDP and SOCP relaxations exactness for (S-QCQP)

Problem (
QCQP
) can be rewritten as

(5.1)

\begin{split}&Minimize_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,Tr(A_{J},X)+2b_{J}^{T}x\\
&s.t.\ \ Tr(A_{k},X)+2b_{k}^{T}x+c_{k}\leq 0\,k=1,...,m\\
&X=xx^{T}\end{split}

It is possible to relax the constraint
X=xx^{T}
with
X-xx^{T}\succeq 0
, which is equivalent to the semidefinite constraint

\begin{pmatrix}1&x^{T}\\
x&X\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0
.
We obtain the semidefinite or Shor relaxation for (
QCQP
),
[
31
]
:

(SDP)

\begin{split}&Minimize_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,Tr(A_{J},X)+2b_{J}^{T}x\\
&s.t.\ \ Tr(A_{k},X)+2b_{k}^{T}x+c_{k}\leq 0\,k=1,...,m\\
&\begin{pmatrix}1&x^{T}\\
x&X\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0\end{split}

Consider a diagonal (
QCQP
), which means that the matrices
A_{k}

k=J,1,...,m
are diagonal.
Let
\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the vector of all the diagonal entries of
A_{k},\,\,\forall\,k
.
Then it is possible to rewrite a diagonal (
QCQP
) as follow:

(5.2)

\begin{split}&Minimize_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ \ \alpha_{J}^{T}y+2b_{J}^{T}x\\
&s.t.\ \ \alpha_{k}^{T}y+2b_{k}^{T}x+c_{j}\leq 0\ \ (\forall\,k\in\{1,...,m\})\\
&s.t.\ \ x_{i}^{2}-y_{i}=0,\ \ \forall\,i\end{split}

If we relax the constraints with
x_{i}^{2}-y_{i}\leq 0
(
\forall\,i
),
we obtain the following convex relaxation as in
[
6
]
:

(SDP2)

\begin{split}&Minimize_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ \ \alpha_{J}^{T}y+2b_{J}^{T}x\\
&s.t.\ \ \alpha_{k}^{T}y+2b_{k}^{T}x+c_{k}\leq 0\ \ (\forall\,k\in\{1,...,m\})\\
&s.t.\ \ x_{i}^{2}-y_{i}\leq 0,\ \ \forall\,i\end{split}

For diagonal QCQP, (
SDP2
) and (
SDP
) are equivalent, as stated in the next theorem:

Theorem 10
.

Consider a diagonal (
QCQP
). The SDP relaxation, (
SDP
), can be rewritten as the relaxation (
SDP2
).

Proof.

Let
(X,x)
be feasible for (
SDP
), then
X-xx^{T}\succeq 0
. The principal minors of order 1 of
X-xx^{T}
are
X_{ii}-x_{i}x_{i}

i=1,...,n
, i.e. the elements on the main diagonal of the symmetric matrix
X-xx^{T}
. If
X-xx^{T}\succeq 0
then a necessary condition for
X-xx^{T}
to be positive semidefinite is
X_{ii}\geq x_{i}x_{i}=x_{i}^{2}

i=1,...,n
(Sylvester criterion).
Since
A_{k}\in S^{n}
,
k=J,1,...,m
, are diagonal then
diag(A_{k})^{T}diag(X)=Tr(A_{k}X)\ \forall\,k
.
So, only the elements in the main diagonal of
X
are relevant for the objective function and the constraints of (
SDP
).
We can substitute the constraint
\begin{pmatrix}X&x\\
x^{T}&1\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0
with
X_{ii}\geq x_{i}^{2}\ \ \forall\,i\in\{1,...,n\}
.

Taking
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
y_{j}=X_{jj}\ \ \forall\,j\in\{1,...,n\}
, the proof is complete.
∎

Since (
SDP2
) is a convex problem, under the Slater condition, the KKT conditions are necessary and sufficient for global optimality.
Consider the (
SDP2
) relaxation for (
S-QCQP
). We have that every component of
\alpha_{k}
,
k=J,1,...,m
, is equal to a constant
a_{k}
. For
\nu_{i}\geq 0\,\,i=1,...,n
and
\gamma_{k}\geq 0\,\,k=1,...,m
, The KKT conditions for the (
SDP2
) relaxation for (
S-QCQP
) are:

(5.3)

\displaystyle a_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}a_{k}-\nu_{i}=0\ \ \ \forall i\in\{1,...,n\}

(5.4)

\displaystyle 2b_{Ji}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}b_{ki}+2\nu_{i}x_{i}=0\ \ \ \forall i\in\{1,...,n\}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}(a_{k}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}+2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}b_{ki}x_{i}+c_{k})=0\ \ \forall\,k\in\{1,...,m\}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\nu_{i}(x_{i}^{2}-y_{i})=0\ \ \ \forall i

(5.7)

\displaystyle f_{k}(y,x):=a_{k}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}+2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}b_{ki}x_{i}+c_{k}\leq 0\ \ \forall\,k\in\{1,...,m\}

(5.8)

\displaystyle x_{i}^{2}-y_{i}\leq 0\ \ \ \forall i\in\{1,...,n\}

The necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for (
S-QCQP
) are (
KKT
). They can be rewritten, for
\gamma_{k}\geq 0\,\,k=1,...,m
, as follow:

(5.9)

\displaystyle a_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}a_{k}\geq 0

(5.10)

\displaystyle 2b_{J}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}b_{k}+2[a_{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}a_{k}]x=0

(5.11)

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}(a_{k}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}+2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}b_{ki}x_{i}+c_{k})=0\ \ \forall\,k\in\{1,...,m\}

(5.12)

\displaystyle f_{k}(x):=a_{k}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}+2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}b_{ki}x_{i}+c_{k}\leq 0\ \ \forall\,k\in\{1,...,m\}\

Theorem 11
.

Consider (
S-QCQP
) such that
m+1<n
.
Let
Assumption 2
holds and

x^{*}
be a global minimizer of (
S-QCQP
).
Assume that there exists
x_{0}
such that
f_{k}(x_{0})<0

\forall\,k\{1,...,m\}
. Then (
SDP2
) is exact and
(x^{*},y^{*})
, with
y^{*}_{i}=(x^{*})^{2}\,\forall\,i\in\{1,...,n\}
is a global optimum for (
SDP2
).

Proof.

By
Theorem 7
, the conditions (
5.9
)-(
5.12
) are necessary and sufficient for the optimality of (
S-QCQP
).
Hence
x^{*}
fulfills all the conditions (
5.9
)-(
5.12
).
Now consider the conditions (
5.3
)-(
5.8
).
By setting
x_{i}^{2}=y_{i}\,\forall\,i
, the system composed by (
5.3
)-(
5.8
) can be rewritten as (
5.9
)-(
5.12
). Hence,
(x^{*},y^{*})
, with
y^{*}_{i}=(x^{*})^{2}\,\forall\,i
fulfill also the KKT conditions (
5.3
)-(
5.8
) for (

w^{2}=(1+2\gamma_{1}a_{1}+2\gamma_{2}a_{2})^{2}=1+4\sum\limits_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}^{2}a_{i}^{2}+8\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}a_{1}a_{2}+4\sum\limits_{i\in I}a_{i}\gamma_{i}

The polynomial quadratic equation becomes

(6.14)

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}^{2}[\|b_{i}\|^{2}-2\frac{a_{i}}{a_{1}}b_{1}^{T}b_{i}+4\frac{c_{1}}{a_{1}}a_{i}^{2}]+\sum\limits_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}[4a_{i}\frac{c_{1}}{a_{1}}-\frac{b_{1}^{T}b_{i}}{a_{1}}]-\\
&\hskip 40.00006pt-2\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}(\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}\|b_{1}\|^{2}+4a_{1}a_{2})+\frac{c_{1}}{a_{1}}=0\end{split}

∎

Conclusions

We provide necessary and sufficient (KKT) conditions for global optimality for a new class of possibly nonconvex quadratically constrained quadratic programming (QCQP) problems, denoted by (
S-QCQP
).
Our result relies on a generalized version of the S-Lemma,
Theorem 6
, stated in the context of general (
QCQP
) problems.
Hence, we believe that this paper can also be the groundwork for providing conditions for global optimality for other classes of (
QCQP
).
In section 5, we prove the exactness of the SDP and the SOCP relaxations for (
S-QCQP
).
In section 6, we solve the (KKT) condition for S-QCQP when the number of constraints
m=2
and
n>>m

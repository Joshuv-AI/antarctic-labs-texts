The Wavefront Set: Bounds for the Langlands parameter
Dan Ciubotaru, Ju-Lee Kim

Abstract
. We are interested in the interplay between the Langlands parameter of a smooth irreducible representation of the reductive p p -adic group and the geometric wavefront set of the representation, an invariant that appears naturally in the local character expansion of Howe [ Ho ] and Harish-Chandra [ HC ] .



1.
Introduction: a conjecture

Let
{\mathsf{k}}
be a nonarchimedean field with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\bar{\mathsf{k}}
be a separable closure of
{\mathsf{k}}
and let
K\subset\bar{\mathsf{k}}
denote the maximal unramified extension. Let
W_{\mathsf{k}}
be the Weil group with inertia subgroup
I_{\mathsf{k}}
and norm
||~||
. Let
W^{\prime}_{\mathsf{k}}
be the Weil-Deligne group, i.e.,
W^{\prime}_{\mathsf{k}}=W_{\mathsf{k}}\ltimes{\mathbb{C}}
, where the action is defined by
w\cdot z=||w||z
,
w\in W_{\mathsf{k}}
,
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
\mathsf{Fr}
denote the geometric Frobenius element, which generates
W_{\mathsf{k}}/I_{\mathsf{k}}
.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a reductive
{\mathsf{k}}
-group that splits over an unramified extension. The
pure inner forms
of
{\mathbf{G}}
, in the sense of
[
Vo
]
are parametrized by the first Galois cohomology group
H^{1}({\mathsf{k}},{\mathbf{G}})
. For every
\omega\in H^{1}({\mathsf{k}},{\mathbf{G}})
, let
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})
denote the corresponding pure inner form. Let
G^{\vee}
be the complex dual group to
{\mathbf{G}}
. Let
{}^{L}\!G=G^{\vee}\rtimes W_{\mathsf{k}}
be the L-group.

1.1.
Langlands parameters

We briefly recall the conjectural Langlands parametrization of the irreducible
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})
-representations, in the formulation of
[
Vo
]
, generalizing the Kazhdan-Lusztig geometric parametrization of representations with Iwahori-fixed vectors. A Langlands parameter is a group homomorphism

(1.1)

\varphi:W^{\prime}_{\mathsf{k}}\to{}^{L}\!G

satisfying certain compatibility conditions. Let
A_{\varphi}
denote the group of components of the centralizer in
G^{\vee}
of the image of
\varphi
. One expects a one-to-one correspondence

\sqcup_{\omega\in H^{1}({\mathsf{k}},{\mathbf{G}})}\mathsf{Irr}{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})\leftrightarrow G^{\vee}\text{-conjugacy classes of }(\varphi,\phi),

where
\varphi
is a Langlands parameter and
\phi
is an irreducible representation of
A_{\varphi}
. The pairs
(\varphi,\phi)
are called enhanced Langlands parameters. Let
\Pi(\varphi)
denote the Langlands packet, i.e., the set of representations parametrized by a fixed (
G^{\vee}
-orbit of)
\varphi
. We need the geometric reformulation of this correspondence.

Fix a group homomorphism
\lambda:I_{\mathsf{k}}\to{}^{L}\!G
with finite image. The enhanced Langlands parameters for which
\varphi|_{I_{\mathsf{k}}}=\lambda
admit the following interpretation. Let
H^{\vee}={G^{\vee}}^{\lambda(I_{\mathsf{k}})}
, a possibly disconnected reductive group, and
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\vee}={\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}}^{\lambda(I_{\mathsf{k}})}
, a reductive subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
, denote the fixed points.

Let
s=\varphi(\mathsf{Fr})\in{}^{L}\!G
be a semisimple element. Since
\mathsf{Fr}
normalizes
I_{\mathsf{k}}
, it follows that
s
normalizes
H^{\vee}
(and
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\vee}
). Let
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\vee}_{q}
denote the
q
-eigenspace of
\mathrm{Ad}(s)
. Let
H^{\vee}(s)
be the centralizer of
s
in
H^{\vee}
. Then
H^{\vee}(s)
acts on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\vee}_{q}
with finitely many orbits. There is a one-to-one correspondence between
G^{\vee}
-orbits of enhanced Langlands parameters
(\varphi,\phi)
such that
\varphi(\mathsf{Fr})=s
and
\varphi|_{I_{\mathsf{k}}}=\lambda
, and pairs
({{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee},\mathcal{L}^{\vee})
where

•

{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}
is an
H^{\vee}(s)
-orbit in
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\vee}_{q}
;

•

\mathcal{L}^{\vee}
is an irreducible
H^{\vee}(s)
-equivariant local system supported on
{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}
.

Denote by
\Pi(\lambda,s,{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee})=\Pi(\varphi)
the corresponding L-packet, each of whose member
\pi(\lambda,s,{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee},{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee})
should be parametrized by the equivariant local systems
\mathcal{L}^{\vee}
on
{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}
.
A desideratum is that
\Pi(\lambda,s,{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee})
consists of tempered representations if and only if
{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}
is dense in
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\vee}_{q}
.

1.2.
Aubert-Zelevinsky involution

Let
{\mathsf{AZ}}
denote the normalized Aubert-Zelevinsky involution
[
Au
, Definition 1.5, Corollary 3.9]
on
\mathsf{Irr}{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})
for every form
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})
. If
\varphi_{\pi}
is the Langlands parameter of
\pi
, then it is expected that
\varphi_{\pi}(W_{\mathsf{k}})
and
\varphi_{{\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi)}(W_{\mathsf{k}})
are
G^{\vee}
conjugate.
Moreover, if
\pi=\pi(\lambda,s,{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee},{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee})
, it is expected that the Langlands parameter of
{\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi)
is related to that of
\pi
via the Fourier transform
[
KS
, §3.7]
on the appropriate space of perverse sheaves of Langlands parameters, see also
[
EM
, §3]
. This is known to hold for all representations with Iwahori-fixed vectors by
[
EM
, Theorem 0.1]
, and more generally it is expected to hold for unipotent representations in the sense of Lusztig.

More precisely, suppose that
\pi
has Iwahori-fixed vectors, so
\lambda(I_{\mathsf{k}})=1
and that
G^{\vee}
is simply connected. The geometric space of parameters consists of irreducible
G^{\vee}(s)
-equivariant perverse sheaves on the complex vector space
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q}
. The connected reductive group
G^{\vee}(s)
acts with finitely many orbits on
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q}
. This implies that
G^{\vee}(s)
also acts with finitely many orbits on the dual vector space
(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q})^{*}
. Identify
(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q})^{*}
with
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q^{-1}}
by using a fixed nondegenerate invariant symmetric form on
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
. Pyasetsky’s duality
[
Ze
]
says that there exists a one-to-one correspondence between the orbits in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q}
and in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q^{-1}}
,
{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}\to P({{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee})
, given as follows. For each
{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q}
,
P({{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee})\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q^{-1}}
is the unique orbit such that the conormal bundles have the same closures

\overline{T_{{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q}}=\overline{T_{P({{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee})}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q^{-1}}}

in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q^{-1}}
.

Let
\operatorname{FT}
denote the Fourier transform from
G^{\vee}(s)
-equivariant perverse sheaves on
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q}
to
G^{\vee}(s)
-equivariant perverse sheaves on
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q^{-1}}
,
[
EM
, §3.2]
. Let
\mathsf{IC}({{\mathcal{O}}},{{\mathcal{L}}})
denote the simple perverse sheaf given by the intersection cohomology complex defined by the equivariant irreducible local system
{{\mathcal{L}}}
supported on
{{\mathcal{O}}}
. Then
\operatorname{FT}(\mathsf{IC}({{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee},{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee}))=\mathsf{IC}({{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{FT}},{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{FT}})
for an orbit
{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{FT}}
in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q^{-1}}
and an equivariant local system
{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{FT}}
. If
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}=\mathfrak{gl}(n)
, so that all local systems that appear are trivial,
[
EM
, Proposition 7.2]
shows that

{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{FT}}=P({{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}).

In general however, this is not the case, as one can see already for
G^{\vee}=Sp(4)
(
[
EM
, Remark 7.5]
). We identify, as we may, the
G^{\vee}(s)
-orbits on
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q^{-1}}
with the
G^{\vee}(s^{-1})
-orbits on
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q}
. Then the main result (Theorem 0.1) in
loc. cit.
is that

(1.2)

{\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi(s,{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee},{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee}))^{*}=\pi(s^{-1},{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{FT}},{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{FT}}),

where
*
in the left hand side denotes the contragredient. In our conjectures below, we will be interested in the nilpotent part of the Langlands parameter of
{\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi)
, that is in
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{\operatorname{FT}}=G^{\vee}\cdot{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{FT}}.

1.3.
Spaltenstein and Lusztig-Sommers dualities

Let
G
denote a complex group with maximal torus
T
with dual
G^{\vee}
and dual maximal torus
T^{\vee}
. Let
W
denote the Weyl group of
(G,T)
and
W^{\vee}
the Weyl group of
(G^{\vee},T^{\vee})
. Of course,
W\cong W^{\vee}
. Let
{{\mathcal{U}}}_{G}
and
{{\mathcal{U}}}_{G^{\vee}}
denote the sets of unipotent elements in
G
and
G^{\vee}
respectively. Let
G\backslash{{\mathcal{U}}}_{G}
,
G^{\vee}\backslash{{\mathcal{U}}}_{G^{\vee}}
denote the corresponding sets of conjugacy classes.

Let
d:G\backslash{{\mathcal{U}}}_{G}\to G^{\vee}\backslash{{\mathcal{U}}}_{G^{\vee}}
and
d^{\vee}:G^{\vee}\backslash{{\mathcal{U}}}_{G^{\vee}}\to G\backslash{{\mathcal{U}}}_{G}
denote the duality maps defined by Spaltenstein
[
Sp
]
. The images of
d,d^{\vee}
consist of the set of special unipotent conjugacy classes.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
. We will also make use of the duality map
d_{S}:G\backslash G\to G^{\vee}\backslash{{\mathcal{U}}}_{G^{\vee}}
from conjugacy classes of
G
to unipotent conjugacy classes of
G^{\vee}
, defined by Lusztig
[
Lu1
, §13.3]
in the case of special conjugacy classes and generalized by Sommers
[
So
]
. Suppose
x\in G
has a Jordan decomposition
x=su
and assume
s\in T
. Let
W(s)
denote the centralizer of
s
in
W
, this is the Weyl group of the centralizer
G(s)
. Let
E^{W(s)}_{u,1}
denote the Springer
W(s)
-representation attached to the trivial local system for the unipotent class of
u
in
G(s)
. Consider the induced representation
\operatorname{Ind}_{W(s)}^{W}(E^{W(s)}_{u,1})
. There exists a unique irreducible
W
-representation
\hat{E}
in this induced representation such that
b_{\hat{E}}=b_{E^{W(s)}_{u,1}}
, where
b
denotes the lowest harmonic degree. Regard
\hat{E}
as an irreducible representation of
W^{\vee}
. By Springer’s correspondence for
G^{\vee}
, there exists a unique unipotent conjugacy class
u^{\vee}
in
G^{\vee}
such that
\hat{E}
is realized in the top cohomology of the Springer fiber for
u^{\vee}
. Define
d_{S}(x)=u^{\vee}
. We recall that

d_{S}|_{G\backslash{{\mathcal{U}}}_{G}}=d.

We will often identify the unipotent classes in
G^{\vee}
with the nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
and think of the image of the map
d_{S}
as lying in the nilpotent cone of
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
.

One can flip the roles of
G
and
G^{\vee}
and have the dual map
d^{\vee}_{S}:G^{\vee}\to G
.

1.4.
Unramified wavefront set

If
\pi
is an admissible
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})
-representation, let
{}^{K}\!\mathsf{WF}(\pi)
denote the unramified wavefront set of
\pi
as in
[
Ok
]
. This is a (finite) collection of nilpotent
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}(K)
-orbits in
\mathbf{\mathfrak{g}}^{\omega}(K)
. Using the parametrization in
loc. cit.
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of nilpotent
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}(K)
-orbits and pairs

({{\mathcal{O}}},C):\ {{\mathcal{O}}}\text{ nilpotent ${\mathbf{G}}(\bar{\mathsf{k}})$-orbit in }\mathfrak{g}(\bar{\mathsf{k}}),\ C\text{ conjugacy class in }A({{\mathcal{O}}}),

where
A({{\mathcal{O}}})
is a group of components of the centralizer
C_{{\mathbf{G}}(\bar{\mathsf{k}}}(u)
in
{\mathbf{G}}(\bar{\mathsf{k}})
of a representative
u
of
{{\mathcal{O}}}
. By abuse of notation, we think of
{}^{K}\!\mathsf{WF}(\pi)
as a collection of such pairs
({{\mathcal{O}}},C)
. For each
{{\mathcal{O}}}
choose a representative
u\in{{\mathcal{O}}}
. For each
C
, we may choose a semisimple representative
\tau
in
C_{{\mathbf{G}}(\bar{\mathsf{k}})}(u)
. Choosing an isomorphism
\bar{k}\cong\mathbb{C}
, we may identify
{\mathbf{G}}(\bar{\mathsf{k}})
with the complex group
G={\mathbf{G}}(\mathbb{C})
with the same root datum, and think of the duality maps in section
1.3
as maps between
{\mathbf{G}}(\bar{\mathsf{k}})
and
G^{\vee}
. Write

d_{S}({{\mathcal{O}}},C)=d_{S}(\tau u),

which is independent of the choice of
u
and
\tau
.
Also write

d_{S}({}^{K}\!\mathsf{WF}(\pi))=\bigcup_{({{\mathcal{O}}},C)\in{}^{K}\!\mathsf{WF}(\pi)}d_{S}({{\mathcal{O}}},C),

a union of nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
. Define
d({}^{K}\!\mathsf{WF}(\pi))
similarly.

Also in
[
Ok
]
, the notion of
canonical unramified wavefront set

{}^{K}\!\underline{\mathsf{WF}}(\pi)
was introduced. This is a collection of classes under a certain equivalence in
{}^{K}\!\mathsf{WF}(\pi)
. The invariant
{}^{K}\!\underline{\mathsf{WF}}(\pi)
is a set of pairs

({{\mathcal{O}}},\bar{C}):\ {{\mathcal{O}}}\text{ nilpotent $G$-orbit in }\mathfrak{g},\ \bar{C}\text{ conjugacy class in }\bar{A}({{\mathcal{O}}}),

where
\bar{A}({{\mathcal{O}}})
is Lusztig’s canonical quotient. The duality
d_{S}
factors through the canonical quotient, so we may talk about
d_{S}({}^{K}\!\underline{\mathsf{WF}}(\pi))
.

1.5.
Conjectures

Let
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi)
denote the geometric wavefront set of an admissible representation of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})
. This is a collection of adjoint nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}(\bar{\mathsf{k}})
. The nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}(\bar{\mathsf{k}})
are ordered with respect to the partial order given by the Zariski closure. The nilpotent orbits in
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi)
are by definition non-comparable in the closure order. In almost all known examples,
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi)
consists of a single nilpotent orbit if
\pi
is irreducible, but not always as recent examples of Tsai
[
Ts
]
have shown.

If
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}
are two sets of nilpotent orbits, we write

\mathcal{S}_{1}\leq\mathcal{S}_{2},

if for every
\mathcal{O}_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, there exists
\mathcal{O}_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{1}\leq\mathcal{O}_{2}
in the closure order.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
\pi(\lambda,s,{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee},{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee})
be an irreducible
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})
-representation in the L-packet
\Pi(\varphi)=\Pi(\lambda,s,{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee})
. Then

(1)

{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi))\leq d^{\vee}(G^{\vee}\cdot{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}).
Equivalently,

(1’)

{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}\subset\overline{d({}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi)))}
.

Of course, the conjecture is contingent on the existence of a local Langlands correspondence.

Remark 1.2
.

While this paper was being finalized, we learned that Conjecture
1.1
, as well as the connection with Conjecture
1.9
and Jiang’s conjecture, have also been formulated independently and studied for classical groups by Hazeltine, Liu, Lo, and Shahidi
[
HLLS
]
.

In terms of the discussion around (
1.2
), the conjecture could be rephrased as

(1.3)

{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi)\leq d^{\vee}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{FT}}),

whenever we know (or expect) that the action of
{\mathsf{AZ}}
on the space of geometric parameter is given by the geometric Fourier transform
\operatorname{FT}
. This is the case for unipotent representations.

Remark 1.3
.

The conjecture aims to generalize the recent results for Lusztig unipotent representations (trivial
\varphi(I_{\mathsf{k}})
) with real infinitesimal character, see
[
CMBO1
]
, and for unipotent supercuspidal representations
[
CMBO2
]
. In these cases, the upper bound is an equality.

But even in the case of unipotent representations there is an example for the split group
E_{7}

[
CMBO1
, §1]
that shows that for unipotent representations with nonreal infinitesimal character, the upper bound can be strict.

At the end of section
5
, we will comment on other possible versions of the conjecture involving the duality
d_{S}
and
{}^{K}\!\underline{\mathsf{WF}}(\pi)
.

For
{\mathsf{GL}}(n)
, Conjecture
1.1
is true, in fact a stronger statement holds.

Theorem 1.4

(Mœglin-Waldspurger
[
MW
, II.2]
)
.

For every irreducible
{\mathsf{GL}}(n,{\mathsf{k}})
-representation
\pi
,

(1.4)

{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi))=d^{\vee}(\mathbb{O}_{\pi}^{\vee}),

where
{\mathbb{O}}_{\pi}^{\vee}
is the
G^{\vee}
-saturation of the nilpotent Langlands parameter of
\pi
.

To exemplify Conjecture
1.1
, we will verify it for the depth-zero supercuspidal representations of classical groups, using the Langlands parametrization of Lust-Stevens
[
LuSt
]
, and of
G_{2}
, using Aubert-Xu
[
AX
]
.

Remark 1.5
.

If the representation
\pi(s,{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee},{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee})
is generic, then its geometric wavefront set is the principal orbit. For the conjecture to hold, we would need
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{FT}}=0
, so
{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}
would need to be the open dense orbit in
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\vee}_{q}
. Moreover, when
H^{\vee}(s)
is connected,
{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee}
would have to be the trivial local system.

In the case of supercuspidal representations, Conjecture
1.1
takes a simpler, more concrete form. If
\pi
is supercuspidal, the geometric Langlands parameter is expected to satisfy

(1)

{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}={{\mathcal{O}}}_{s}^{\vee}
, the unique open dense
H^{\vee}(s)
-orbit in
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\vee}_{q}
(this is the case whenever the representation
\pi
is tempered).

(2)

In addition, it is immediate from the definition that

(1.5)

{\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi)=\pi.

Conjecture 1.6

(Supercuspidals)
.

Let
\pi(\lambda,s,{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}_{s},{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee})
be an irreducible supercuspidal
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})
-representation in the L-packet
\Pi(\varphi)=\Pi(s,{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}_{s})
. Set
{\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{s}=G^{\vee}\cdot{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}_{s}
. Then

(1)

{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi)\leq d^{\vee}({\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{s}).

Equivalently,
{\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{s}\leq d({}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi))
.

(2)

If in addition,
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})
is split and
\pi
is depth-zero compactly induced from a cuspidal representation of the maximal hyperspecial parahoric subgroup, then

{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi)=d^{\vee}({\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{s}).

The first part of the conjecture is clearly just a particular case of Conjecture
1.1
.

Remark 1.7
.

Notice that Conjecture
1.6
is vacuously true for the nonsingular supercuspidal representations, in the sense of Kaletha
[
Kal
]
, because
{\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{s}=0
so
d^{\vee}({\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{s})
is the principal geometric orbit. In this case, the upper bound is attained only for the generic constituents of the L-packets. In section
3.1
, we will compute the wavefront sets of the regular depth-zero supercuspidals parameterized by Kazhdan-Varshavsky
[
KV
]
and DeBacker-Reeder
[
DBR
]
.

In section
4
, we will prove

Theorem 1.8

(Depth-zero supercuspidals of classical groups)
.

Suppose
{\mathbf{G}}({\mathsf{k}})
is a symplectic, special orthogonal, or unitary group. Conjecture
1.6
holds for all depth-zero supercuspidal representations, using the Langlands parametrization of Lust-Stevens
[
LuSt
]
.

We mention that Conjectures
1.1
and
1.6
are compatible with a conjecture about Arthur packets formulated by D. Jiang. Suppose
\varphi
is a tempered L-parameter. Then
{\mathsf{AZ}}(\Pi(\varphi))
is expected to be an Arthur packet. Let
\pi^{\prime}\in{\mathsf{AZ}}(\Pi(\varphi))
be one of these “anti-tempered” representations. Then
\pi^{\prime}={\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi(\lambda,s,{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}_{s},{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee}))
for some local system
{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee}
. Conjecture
1.1
then says that we should have

(1.6)

{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi^{\prime})\leq d^{\vee}(G^{\vee}\cdot{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}_{s}).

The orbit
G^{\vee}\cdot{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}_{s}
is precisely the “Arthur
SL(2)
”
{\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{A,\varphi}
attached to the Arthur packet
{\mathsf{AZ}}(\Pi(\varphi))
. In other words, the expectation is

(1.7)

{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi^{\prime})\leq d^{\vee}({\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{A,\varphi}),

for all
\pi^{\prime}\in{\mathsf{AZ}}(\Pi(\varphi))
. This is known to hold (with equality) if
\varphi
is a “real” unramified L-parameter, see
[
CMBO3
]
. Jiang’s conjecture
[
Ji
, Conjecture 4.2]
says that this inequality holds for
all
Arthur packets, when they are defined (not just the above anti-tempered packets) and moreover that there exists one constituent of the Arthur packet where the equality is achieved. One can naturally reformulate Conjecture
1.1
in this case as follows:

Conjecture 1.9
.

Let
\pi(\lambda,s,{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee},{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee})
be an irreducible representation in the tempered L-packet
\Pi(\varphi)=\Pi(\lambda,s,{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee})
. Then

(1)

{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi))\leq d^{\vee}(G^{\vee}\cdot{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}).

(2)

Equality in (1) is achieved for some
\pi
in the L-packet.

In section
5
, we will verify Conjecture 1.9 for
G_{2}
, using the Langlands correspondences from
[
AX
,
GS1
,
GS2
]
and the wavefront set results of
[
JLS
,
LoSa
]
.

In section
2
, we will explain that Conjecture
1.9
(via the usual desiderata of Langlands correspondence) implies Conjecture
1.1
.

Remark 1.10
.

We look at the two extreme cases of Conjectures
1.1
and
1.9
.

(a)

Firstly, let us call an irreducible representation
\pi

anti-generic
if
{\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi)
is generic. If
\pi
is anti-generic, then
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi))=\mathcal{O}_{\textup{reg}}
, the regular nilpotent orbit. The only way that the upper bound conjecture could hold then is if the Langlands nilpotent parameter of
\pi
is
0
. In other words, for every anti-generic representation the image of
\mathbb{C}\subset W_{\mathsf{k}}^{\prime}
under the Langlands parameter is trivial.

If
\pi
is unipotent anti-generic, then it is known that it is spherical (with respect to the maximal special parahoric) and the desired condition on the Langlands parameter holds.

If
\pi
is supercuspidal, since
{\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi)=\pi
, the conjecture implies that every generic supercuspidal representation has trivial nilpotent Langlands parameter.

(b)

Secondly, suppose
\mathcal{O}^{\vee}=0
, so
d^{\vee}(G^{\vee}\cdot\mathcal{O}^{\vee})=\mathcal{O}_{\textup{reg}}.
Then Conjecture
1.9
(1) is trivially satisfied, but (2) says that there should exist a representation
\pi
in the tempered L-packet such that
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi))=\mathcal{O}_{\textup{reg}}
. In other words, every non-singular tempered L-packet should contain an anti-generic representation.

2.
Parabolic induction

In this section, we discuss the compatibility of our conjectures with parabolic induction. Let
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{M}\mathbf{N}
be a
\mathsf{k}
-rational parabolic subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}
with a
\mathsf{k}
-rational Levi subgroup
M=\mathbf{M}({\mathsf{k}})
.

If
\pi=\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathbf{P}({\mathsf{k}})}^{{\mathbf{G}}({\mathsf{k}})}(\pi_{M})
is a parabolically induced representation, where
\pi_{M}
is an admissible representation of
M
, then the relation between the wavefront sets is well known, see Waldspurger
[
Wa
]
:

(2.1)

{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi)=\mathsf{ind}_{\mathbf{M}}^{{\mathbf{G}}}({}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi_{M})),

where
\mathsf{ind}_{\mathbf{M}}^{{\mathbf{G}}}
denotes the Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction of nilpotent orbits.

We need to use several known facts about the induction of nilpotent orbits.

Lemma 2.1
.

(1)

If
\mathcal{O}_{1}\leq\mathcal{O}_{2}
are two nilpotent
\mathbf{M}
-orbits in
\mathfrak{m}
, then
\mathsf{ind}_{\mathbf{M}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathcal{O}_{1})\leq\mathsf{ind}_{\mathbf{M}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathcal{O}_{2}).

(2)

If
\mathcal{O}_{1}^{\vee}\leq\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\vee}
are two nilpotent
G^{\vee}
-orbits in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
, then
d^{\vee}(\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\vee})\leq d^{\vee}(\mathcal{O}_{1}^{\vee})
.

(3)

If
\mathcal{O}^{\vee}
is a nilpotent
M^{\vee}
-orbits in
\mathfrak{m}^{\vee}
, then
d^{\vee}(G^{\vee}\cdot\mathcal{O}^{\vee})=\mathsf{ind}_{\mathbf{M}}^{\mathbf{G}}(d^{\vee}(\mathcal{O}^{\vee})).

Proof.

Claim (1) is immediate from the definition, or for example from the formula
\overline{\mathsf{ind}_{\mathbf{M}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathcal{O})}=\mathbf{G}\cdot(\overline{\mathcal{O}}+\mathfrak{n})
, see
[
CM
, proof of Theorem 7.1.3]
.

Claims (2) and (3) are in
[
BV
, Proposition A2 (a),(c)]
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Conjecture
1.9
(1) implies Conjecture
1.1
.

Proof.

We need to prove that if

{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\!\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi))\leq d^{\vee}({\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{\pi})

holds for all irreducible tempered representations of reductive groups, then it holds for all irreducible representations. Here
{\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{\pi}
denotes the nilpotent
G^{\vee}
-orbit of the Langlands parameter of
\pi
. By the parabolic part of the Langlands classification, every irreducible
\pi
is a quotient of a standard module
i(\mathbf{P},\sigma_{\nu})=i_{\mathbf{P}({\mathsf{k}})}^{\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})}(\sigma_{\nu})
,
\sigma_{\nu}=\sigma\otimes\nu
, where
\sigma
is an irreducible tempered
\mathbf{M}({\mathsf{k}})
-representation and
\nu
is an unramified character of
\mathbf{M}({\mathsf{k}})
.

Since the involution
{\mathsf{AZ}}
commutes with parabolic induction
[
Au
, Theorem 1.7]
, i.e., in the Grothendieck group

{\mathsf{AZ}}(i_{\mathbf{P}({\mathsf{k}})}^{\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})}(\sigma_{\nu}))=i_{\mathbf{P}({\mathsf{k}})}^{\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\sigma)_{\nu}),

it follows that
{\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi)
is a subquotient of
i_{\mathbf{P}({\mathsf{k}})}^{\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\sigma)_{\nu})
. By the definition of the wavefront set then

{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\!\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi))\leq{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\!\mathsf{WF}(i_{\mathbf{P}({\mathsf{k}})}^{\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\sigma)_{\nu})={}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\!\mathsf{WF}(i_{\mathbf{P}({\mathsf{k}})}^{\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\sigma))),

where the equality comes from the fact that the local character expansion is valid on neighborhoods of compact elements, and so it does not see twists by unramified characters. By (
2.1
), it follows that

{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\!\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi))\leq\mathsf{ind}_{\mathbf{M}}^{{\mathbf{G}}}({}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\!\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\sigma))).

Now assuming Conjecture
1.9
for tempered modules, we have
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\!\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\sigma))\leq d^{\vee}_{M^{\vee}}({\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{\sigma})
. Then Lemma
2.1
(1),(3) implies

{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\!\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi))\leq\mathsf{ind}_{\mathbf{M}}^{{\mathbf{G}}}(d^{\vee}_{M^{\vee}}({\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{\sigma}))=d^{\vee}(G^{\vee}\cdot{\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{\sigma}).

Recall that
\pi
is the Langlands quotient of
i(\mathbf{P},\sigma_{\nu})
, hence by the desiderata of the Langlands correspondence,
{\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{\pi}=G^{\vee}\cdot{\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{\sigma}.

∎

3.
Depth-zero supercuspidal representations

Let
\pi
be a depth-zero supercuspidal representation. This is realized as follows: there exists a maximal parahoric subgroup
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}_{x}(\mathfrak{o})
(corresponding to a point
x
in the Bruhat-Tits building), and an irreducible cuspidal representation
\sigma
of the finite group
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}_{x}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
such that
\pi
is a direct summand of
\textrm{c-Ind}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}_{x}(\mathfrak{o})}^{{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}({\mathsf{k}})}(\widetilde{\sigma})
, where
\widetilde{\sigma}
is the pullback of
\sigma
to
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}_{x}(\mathfrak{o})
. Write
\pi=\pi(x,\sigma)
.

The finite analogue of the wavefront set for
\sigma
is the
Kawanaka wavefront
set
\mathsf{WF}(\sigma)
,
[
Kaw
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
CMBO2
, Theorem 3.0.4]
)
.

The canonical unramified wavefront set of
\pi(x,\sigma)
is

{}^{K}\!\underline{\mathsf{WF}}(\pi(x,\sigma))=(\mathsf{WF}(\sigma),x).

Lusztig gave an explicit description of
\mathsf{WF}(\mu)
,
\mu
an irreducible representation of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}_{x}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
in terms of his parametrization
[
Lu1
, Theorem 4.23]
. Let
({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}_{x})^{\vee}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
be the dual finite reductive group. Then
\mu
is parametrized by a triple
(\tau,u_{0}^{\vee},y)
, where
\tau
is a semisimple element in
({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}_{x})^{\vee}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
,
u_{0}^{\vee}
is a special unipotent element in the centralizer
C_{({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}_{x})^{\vee}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})}(\tau)
and
y
is an element of the unipotent family
C_{u_{0}^{\vee}}
parametrized by
u_{0}^{\vee}
. The element
y
will not be important for us. Let
\widetilde{C}_{u_{0}^{\vee}}=C_{\widetilde{u}_{0}^{\vee}}
be the family obtained by applying Alvis-Curtis duality to
C_{u_{0}^{\vee}}
. Let
d_{S}^{\vee}
be the duality map in this finite field setting, this is defined exactly as above, see
[
Lu1
, §13.3]
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
Lu2
, Theorem 11.2]
)
.

With the above notation,
\mathsf{WF}(\mu)=d_{S}^{\vee}(\tau\widetilde{u}_{0}^{\vee})
. If
\mu=\sigma
is cuspidal, then
\mathsf{WF}(\sigma)=d_{S}^{\vee}(\tau u_{0}^{\vee})
.

This means that the canonical unramified wavefront set of a supercuspidal representation
\pi(x,\sigma)
is

(3.1)

{}^{K}\!\underline{\mathsf{WF}}(\pi(x,\sigma))=(d_{S}^{\vee}(\tau u_{0}^{\vee}),x).

Since we are in a depth-zero case, the Langlands parameter
\varphi
is trivial on the wild inertia group, and the image
\varphi(I_{\mathsf{k}})
is a cyclic group generated by an element
\lambda\in{}^{L}\!G
of finite order prime to
q
. Hence
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\vee}={\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}}^{\lambda}
is a pseudo-Levi subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
. Conjecture
1.6
is equivalent to

(3.2)

G\cdot d_{S}^{\vee}(\tau u_{0}^{\vee})\leq d^{\vee}(G^{\vee}\cdot{{\mathcal{O}}}_{s}^{\vee}).

We do not know a uniform way to attach the Langlands parameter to a depth-zero supercuspidal representation, so we will only verify the conjecture in a number of cases that exist in the literature.

3.1.
Regular supercuspidal representations

For all regular depth-zero supercuspidal representations
[
KV
,
DBR
]
, the conjecture is trivially true since in that case
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\vee}
is just a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
, so the only possible Langlands nilpotent element is
0
. Hence the conjecture just says that
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi)\leq{{\mathcal{O}}}_{p}
, where
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{p}
is the principal
{\mathbf{G}}(\bar{\mathsf{k}})
-orbit, which is true, but trivial. On the other hand,
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi)={{\mathcal{O}}}_{p}
if and only if
\pi
is a generic representation, and this is not always the case. In general,
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi)
can be equal to the
{\mathbf{G}}(\bar{\mathsf{k}})
-saturation of the principal nilpotent orbit in a maximal pseudo-Levi, as we explain next.

Let
I_{t}=I_{\mathsf{k}}/I^{+}_{\mathsf{k}}
be the tame quotient of the inertia group, where
I^{+}_{\mathsf{k}}
is the wild inertia subgroup. The depth-zero condition means that the parameter
\lambda
in the introduction factors through
I_{t}
. Write the L-group as
{}^{L}G=G^{\vee}\rtimes\langle\theta^{\vee}\rangle
, where
\theta^{\vee}
is a finite-order automorphism giving the inner class of
{\mathbf{G}}
. Suppose
G^{\vee}
is semisimple. In the terminology of DeBacker and Reeder, a tame regular semisimple elliptic Langlands parameter is given a
W^{\vee}
-conjugacy class of pairs
(\lambda,w)
, where:

(1)

a continuous homomorphism
\lambda:I_{t}\to T^{\vee}
such that
Z_{G^{\vee}}(\lambda)=T^{\vee}
, and

(2)

a Weyl group element
w\in W^{\vee}=N_{G^{\vee}}(T^{\vee})/T^{\vee}
such that
w^{\vee}\circ\theta^{\vee}\circ\lambda^{q}=\lambda
and
{T^{\vee}}^{w^{\vee}\theta^{\vee}}
is finite.

The element
w^{\vee}\theta^{\vee}
is the image of the inverse Frobenius under the Langlands homomorphism
\varphi
. Then a complete (enhanced) Langlands parameter is given by a triple
(\lambda,w,\rho)
, where
\rho
is an irreducible representation of the finite group
{T^{\vee}}^{w^{\vee}\theta^{\vee}}=Z_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi).
If
X
is the weight lattice of
{\mathbf{G}}
, and
W
is the Weyl group of
{\mathbf{G}}
,
\theta
the Galois automorphism giving
\theta^{\vee}
, there is a natural isomorphism

(3.3)

X/(1-w\theta)X\longleftrightarrow\mathsf{Irr}~{T^{\vee}}^{w^{\vee}\theta^{\vee}},\quad x\mapsto\rho_{x}.

By abuse of notation, denote
x
also the preimage in
X
of an element in
X/(1-w\theta)X
. Let
\pi(\lambda,w,0,x)
denote the supercuspidal representation attached to
(\lambda,w,x)
in
loc. cit
. The
0
here stands for the zero nilpotent orbit, i.e., the image of the
SL(2)
under the Langlands parameter must be trivial (since
{\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}}^{\lambda(I_{\mathsf{k}})}=\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
).

Proposition 3.3
.

The geometric wavefront set
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi(\lambda,w,0,x))
is the
{\mathbf{G}}(\bar{\mathsf{k}})
-saturation of the principal nilpotent orbit of the maximal pseudo-Levi subgroup
Z_{{\mathbf{G}}(\bar{\mathsf{k}})}(x).

Proof.

The claim follows at once, after we recall the construction of
\pi(\lambda,w,0,x)
. The element
x
defines a cocycle
u_{x}\in H^{1}({\mathsf{k}},{\mathbf{G}})
which gives the inner twist
\omega_{x}
of
{\mathbf{G}}
and
F_{x}
a twisted Frobenius map and a maximal parahoric subgroup
{\mathbf{G}}_{x}
. Then
{\mathbf{G}}_{x}^{F_{x}}
is a maximal parahoric subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega_{x}}({\mathsf{k}})
. The pair
(w,x)
defines an
F_{x}
-minisotropic torus
T_{x}
and
\lambda
defines a depth-zero character
\chi_{x}
of
T_{x}
. Then Deligne-Lusztig induction defines an irreducible cuspidal representation of the reductive quotient
\overline{{\mathbf{G}}_{x}^{F_{x}}}

\kappa_{x}=\pm R_{T_{x}}^{\overline{{\mathbf{G}}_{x}^{F_{x}}}}(\chi_{x}).

The important thing for us is that this cuspidal representation is generic, in the sense that the Kawanaka wavefront set is principal. The supercuspidal representation is compactly induced

\pi(\lambda,w,0,x)=\operatorname{ind}_{Z^{F_{x}}{\mathbf{G}}_{x}^{F_{x}}}^{{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega_{x}}({\mathsf{k}})}(\kappa_{x}).

The wavefront set is given by Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Conjecture
1.6
clearly holds. Moreover, so does Conjecture
1.9
(2) since the L-packet of supercuspidals contains a generic supercuspidal representation.

4.
Depth-zero representations of classical groups

In
[
LuSt
]
, Lust and Stevens constructed L-packets of depth-zero supercuspidal representations of classical groups. We will verify Conjecture 1.6 for these
L
-packets.
Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a connected reductive group of classical type with connected center over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,
q
odd. Let
F
be the Frobenius map. Let
{\mathcal{G}}^{*}
be the dual group over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with dual Frobenius also denoted
F
. Let
\mathsf{Irr}{\mathcal{G}}^{F}
be the set of equivalence classes of irreducible complex
{\mathcal{G}}^{F}
-representations. According to Lusztig’s classification, we can partition

\mathsf{Irr}{\mathcal{G}}^{F}=\sqcup_{s}\mathsf{Irr}_{s}{\mathcal{G}}^{F},

where
s
runs over the conjugacy classes of semisimple elements in
{{\mathcal{G}}^{*}}^{F}
. The piece
\mathsf{Irr}_{1}{\mathcal{G}}^{F}
is the set of irreducible unipotent representations. Since
Z({\mathcal{G}})
is connected, the rational semisimple conjugacy classes in
{\mathcal{G}}^{*}
are in one-to-one correspondence with the geometric
F
-stable semisimple conjugacy classes in
{\mathcal{G}}^{*}

[
Car
, Proposition 3.7.3]
.

The above decomposition is realized as follows. By
[
Car
, Theorem 4.4.6]
, there exists a natural one-to-one correspondence between geometric conjugacy classes of pairs
(T,\theta)
, where
{\mathcal{T}}
is a maximal
F
-stable torus in
{\mathcal{G}}
and
\theta
is a character of
T
, and
F
-stable semisimple conjugacy classes in
{\mathcal{G}}^{*}
. Let
s\in{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\subset{\mathcal{G}}^{*}
, then
\mathsf{Irr}_{s}{\mathcal{G}}^{F}
consists of all the constituents of the induced representations
R_{{\mathcal{T}}}^{{\mathcal{G}}}(s)
where we think of
s
as a character of
T
.

Lusztig’s Jordan decomposition says that there is a one-to-one correspondence

\Psi_{s}^{{\mathcal{G}}}:\mathsf{Irr}_{s}{\mathcal{G}}^{F}\longleftrightarrow\mathsf{Irr}_{1}({\mathcal{G}}_{s}^{F}),

where
{\mathcal{G}}_{s}
is the endoscopic group of
{\mathcal{G}}
with dual
{\mathcal{G}}^{*}_{s}:=Z_{{\mathcal{G}}^{*}}(s)
. To have cuspidal representations, we need
Z({\mathcal{G}}^{*})^{0}
and
Z({\mathcal{G}}_{s}^{*})^{0}
to have the same
F
-rank. In this case,
\Psi_{s}^{{\mathcal{G}}}
gives a correspondence of cuspidal represenattions. In other words, the set of cuspidal
{\mathcal{G}}^{F}
-representations are in one-to-one correspondence with
{\mathcal{G}}^{*}
-pairs
(s,\tau)
where
s\in{{\mathcal{G}}^{*}}^{F}
is semisimple of equal rank and
\tau
is a cuspidal unipotent representation of
{\mathcal{G}}_{s}^{F}
.

For classical groups, the classification of cuspidal unipotent representations shows that there is at most one cuspidal unipotent representation. On the other hand, the conjugacy classes of
s\in{{\mathcal{G}}^{*}}^{F}
are given by characteristic polynomials
P_{s}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
,

P_{s}(x)=\prod_{P}P(x)^{a_{P}},

where the product runs over all irreducible self-dual monic polynomials over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and nonnegative integers
a_{P}
such that
\sum_{P}a_{P}\deg(P)=\dim V
, where
V
is the defining
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-representation of
{\mathcal{G}}^{*}
. In order for the semisimple class to parametrize a cuspidal representation, one needs:

(1)

if
P(x)\neq x\pm 1
, then
a_{P}=\frac{1}{2}(m_{P}^{2}+m_{P})
for some integer
m_{P}\geq 0.

(2)

if we set
a_{\pm}=a_{x\mp 1}
, there are some conditions on
a_{\pm}
depending on the type of the group, see
[
LuSt
, §7.2]
.

Example 4.1
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{G}}=Sp(2n)
. If
P(x)
appears in
P_{s}(x)
and
\deg(P)>1
, then necessarily
\deg(P)
is even. We denote
\deg(P)=2k_{P}
,
k_{P}>0
integer. Otherwise,

a_{+}=2(m_{+}^{2}+m_{+})+1,\quad a_{-}=2m_{-}^{2},

for some nonnegative integers
m_{\pm}
. Then a cuspidal representation of
Sp(2n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is uniquely determined by a collection of nonnegative integers
m_{\pm}
,

such that

(4.1)

\sum_{\deg(P)>1}k_{P}\frac{m_{P}^{2}+m_{P}}{2}+(m_{+}^{2}+m_{+})+m_{-}^{2}=n.

The
\deg(P)>1
terms, if they appear, correspond to endoscopic unitary groups, whereas the
m_{+}
term corresponds to an endoscopic
Sp
subgroup and
m_{-}
to an endoscopic even orthogonal group.

4.1.

Let

(m_{\pm}^{(i)},m_{P}^{(i)},k_{P}^{(i)})

Symplectic groups

To account for an example of an anti-tempered unipotent

-representation for which
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}(\pi)<d^{\vee}({\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{\pi})
(strict inequality), Okada proposed another, more precise form of the relation between the wavefront set of unipotent representations and the Langlands parameter:

(5.1)

d_{S}({}^{K}\underline{\mathsf{WF}}(\pi))=G^{\vee}\cdot{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{FT}}.

This holds for unipotent representations with real infinitesimal character
[
CMBO1
]
, and there are no known counterexamples for unipotent representations. However, for more general representations, this equality can’t always hold as stated, for example, because of the case of nongeneric regular depth-zero supercuspidals, see section

.

A natural attempt to fix it is to intersect with
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\vee}={\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}}^{\varphi(I_{\mathsf{k}})}

\overline{d_{S}({}^{K}\underline{\mathsf{WF}}(\pi))}\cap{\mathfrak{h}}^{\vee}
is an irreducible

-variety,
s=\varphi(\mathsf{Fr})
, hence the closure of a nilpotent
H^{\vee}(s)
-orbit
{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}
such that

(5.2)

{\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}=G^{\vee}\cdot{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{FT}}.

Often this seems to be the case (for example, this resolves trivially the case of regular depth-zero supercuspidals), but again not always, as the
G_{2}
examples above show.

More precisely, consider
\pi
in the sixth row with
{\mathbb{O}}^{\vee}_{\pi}=A_{1}
and
{}^{K}\underline{\mathsf{WF}}(\pi)=(G_{2}(a_{1}),1)

and
d_{S}({}^{K}\underline{\mathsf{WF}}(\pi))=G_{2}(a_{1})

\overline{d_{S}({}^{K}\underline{\mathsf{WF}}(\pi))}\cap{\mathfrak{h}}^{\vee}

Proposition 5.5
.

Conjecture
1.9
holds for all depth-zero discrete series L-packets of
G_{2}
that contain a supercuspidal representation in the parametrization of
[
AX
]

Proof.

The parametrization of the unipotent discrete series L-packets agrees with the Kazhdan-Lusztig parametrization
[
KL

, hence the claim is already covered by
[
CMBO1
]
in the case of real infinitesimal character.

For the regular supercuspidal L-packets, the claim is immediate and has already been discussed in section
3.1

For the non-unipotent mixed L-packets, notice that Table
2
shows that the conjectural upper bound is attained by the supercuspidal in the L-packet. So it only remains to check that if
\pi
is a non-supercuspidal representation in the L-packet, then
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi))\leq d^{\vee}({\mathbb{O}}^{\vee})

In fact, the previous proof can be adapted to conclude the following result (which is already known in more generality from
[
JLS
,
LoSa
]
).

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible unipotent representation of
G_{2}
. Then either
\pi
is generic, or
\pi
is trivial, or
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi))=G_{2}(a_{1})
.

Proof.

Suppose
\pi
is not generic. Then
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi))\leq G_{2}(a_{1})
. Consider the parahoric restriction
\mathsf{res}_{K_{0}}(\pi)
of
\pi
with respect to the maximal hyperspecial subgroup
K_{0}
. This is a unipotent representation of
G_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and as such, its geometric Kawanaka wavefront set
\mathsf{WF}_{q}(\mathsf{res}_{K_{0}}(\pi))
is special. The only special orbits in
G_{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q})
are the regular, subregular, and the trivial orbit. The only way
\mathsf{WF}_{q}(\mathsf{res}_{K_{0}}(\pi))
is the trivial orbit is if
\mathsf{res}_{K_{0}}(\pi)
is a trivial
G_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
-representation. In particular, this means that
\pi
is a
K_{0}
-spherical representation and in the equivalence of categories with the Iwahori-Hecke algebra, the corresponding Iwahori-Hecke algebra module only contains the trivial
K_{0}
-type. This implies that
\pi
must be an unramified character, but since the group is
G_{2}
, it must be the trivial representation.

By
[
Ok
, Theorem 5.4]
,
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi))
is greater than the lift to
G
of
\mathsf{WF}_{q}(\mathsf{res}_{K_{0}}(\pi))
. So if
\pi
is not trivial, it follows that
{}^{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}\mathsf{WF}({\mathsf{AZ}}(\pi))\geq G_{2}(a_{1})
and this completes the proof.

∎

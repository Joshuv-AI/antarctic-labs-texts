Abstract
. We consider the real eigenvalues of the elliptic Ginibre matrix indexed by the non-Hermiticity parameter τ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] \tau\in[0,1] , and present a Harer-Zagier type recursion formula for the even moments in the form of an 11 11 -term recurrence relation. For the symmetric GOE case ( τ = 1 \tau=1 ), it reduces to a known 5-term recurrence relation. On the other hand, for the asymmetric cases when τ < 1 \tau<1 , the recursion formula is new, even in the special case of the well-studied Ginibre ensemble ( τ = 0 \tau=0 ), where it reduces to a 3-term recurrence. For the proof, we derive a seventh-order linear differential equation for the moment generating function.



1.
Introduction and main results

Random Matrix Theory (RMT) enjoys an intimate connection with various branches of mathematics and physics
[
2
]
. One prominent illustration of this relationship is the Harer-Zagier formula
[
59
]
, which stands as a well-known example demonstrating the combinatorial and topological significance inherent in RMT statistics.
While the Harer-Zagier formula originates in the study of the moduli space of curves, it also gives rise to a fundamental formula in the study of spectral moments of classical random matrix ensembles (cf.
[
25
]
).

To be more concrete, let us consider the Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE)
X^{\rm GUE}
picked randomly with respect to the measure proportional to
e^{-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}X^{2}}\,dX
on the space
\mathcal{H}_{N}
of
N\times N
Hermitian matrices.
Here,
dX
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathcal{H}_{N}\cong\mathbb{R}^{N^{2}}
.
One of the most basic observables of the GUE is its
p
-th spectral moment

(1.1)

M_{p}^{\rm GUE}:=\mathbb{E}\Big[\operatorname{Tr}(X^{\rm GUE})^{p}\Big],\qquad p\in\mathbb{N}.

Note here that, due to symmetry, all odd moments vanish. On the other hand, it possesses non-trivial even moments.
For instance, the first few even moments are given by

M_{0}^{\rm GUE}=N,\qquad M_{2}^{\rm GUE}=N^{2},\qquad M_{4}^{\rm GUE}=2N^{3}+N,\qquad M_{6}^{\rm GUE}=5N^{4}+10N^{2}.

In
[
59
]
, it was demonstrated using the Wick calculus that the spectral moments satisfy the
3
-term recursion formula

(1.2)

(p+1)M_{2p}^{\rm GUE}=(4p-2)N\,M_{2p-2}^{\rm GUE}+(p-1)(2p-1)(2p-3)M_{2p-4}^{\rm GUE}.

This recursion formula yields that for any non-negative integer
p,

(1.3)

M_{2p}^{\rm GUE}=\sum_{g=0}^{\lfloor p/2\rfloor}c(g;p)\,N^{p+1-2g}.

Here, the coefficients
c(g;p)
can be identified with the number of pairings of the edges of a
2p
-gon, dual to a map on a compact Riemann surface of genus
g
.
For this reason, a formula of the type (
1.3
) is commonly referred to as the genus expansion in RMT.
Indeed, this type of formula provides one of the earliest examples of a topological expansion in the theory of matrix integrals
[
12
]
.
In particular, the leading coefficient
c(0;p)
is given by the
p
-th Catalan number

(1.4)

c(0;p)=\frac{1}{p+1}\binom{2p}{p}.

Since the Catalan number corresponds to the even moments of the Wigner’s semicircle law

(1.5)

d\mu_{\rm sc}(x):=\frac{\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}{2\pi}\,\mathbbm{1}_{(-2,2)}(x)\,dx,

this leading coefficient (
1.4
) gives rise to the convergence of the GUE density towards (
1.5
). Furthermore, when combined with the loop equation formalism, the expansion (
1.3
) can be utilized to derive the large-
N
expansion of the densities, cf.
[
84
]
.

The Harer-Zagier formula (
1.2
) was revisited by Haagerup and Thorbjørnsen in
[
60
]
.
They re-derived this recursion formula using a closed-form expression for the moment-generating function, which is given in terms of a confluent hypergeometric function, cf. (
3.22
).
Furthermore, they obtained a similar formula for the Laguerre Unitary Ensemble (LUE).
Additional discussions on the LUE spectral moments can be found in
[
28
,
32
,
30
]
, and similar work on the Jacobi Unitary Ensemble (JUE) is detailed in
[
55
]
.
Notably, for all these classical GUE, LUE, and JUE models, the spectral moments can be evaluated in closed form using hypergeometric polynomials
[
29
]
.
We also refer to
[
48
,
26
,
72
,
63
,
19
]
for discrete extensions of the above-mentioned results and to
[
41
]
for an extension of (
1.2
) to the
d
-dimensional Fermi gas in an harmonic trap.
In the context of RMT, the Harer-Zagier type formulas can be employed to derive (small) deviation inequalities for extreme eigenvalues
[
60
,
62
,
64
]
, which play an important role in the study of the associated universality problems, see e.g.
[
36
,
35
]
.
Furthermore, as previously mentioned, it can be used to study the finite-size corrections of the densities or the counting statistics
[
75
,
11
,
49
,
85
,
31
]
.
For further mathematical implementation beyond RMT, we refer the reader to a recent work
[
54
]
, which delves into expressing the Harer-Zagier formula within the context of noncommutative geometry.
Furthermore, for applications of these formulas in the context of the time-delay matrix of quantum dots and
\tau
-function theory, see
[
68
,
69
,
70
,
27
,
30
,
67
]
and references therein.

Beyond the unitary invariant ensembles, it is natural to investigate the Harer-Zagier type formula for random matrices with orthogonal symmetry.
The single-most fundamental model in this class is perhaps the Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble (GOE)
X^{\rm GOE}
that follows the probability distribution proportional to
e^{-\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{Tr}X^{2}}\,dX
, where in this case
dX
is the Lebesgue measure on the space of symmetric matrices
\mathcal{S}_{N}\cong\mathbb{R}^{N(N+1)/2}
.
Similar to the above, we write

(1.6)

M_{p}^{\rm GOE}:=\mathbb{E}\Big[\operatorname{Tr}(X^{\rm GOE})^{p}\Big],\qquad p\in\mathbb{N}

for the
p
-th spectral moment of the GOE.
For instance, we have the following explicit evaluations

\displaystyle M_{0}^{\rm GOE}=N,\qquad M_{2}^{\rm GOE}=N^{2}+N,\qquad M_{4}^{\rm GOE}=2N^{3}+5N^{2}+N,\qquad M_{6}^{\rm GOE}=5N^{4}+22N^{3}+52N^{2}+41N.

It is trivial but noteworthy for the latter discussion that
M_{0}^{\rm GUE}=M_{0}^{\rm GOE}=N
since they coincide with the number of eigenvalues.

As is widely recognized, the integrable structure of orthogonal ensembles is considerably more intricate when compared to their unitary counterparts (cf.
[
1
]
).
This complexity leads to a delay in the investigation of the Harer-Zagier type formula for the GOE.
Notably, Ledoux demonstrated in
[
64
, Theorem 2]
that the GOE spectral moments satisfy the following
5
-term recurrence relation

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(p+1)M_{2p}^{\rm GOE}&=(4p-1)(2N-1)M_{2p-2}^{\rm GOE}+(2p-3)(10p^{2}-9p-8N^{2}+8N)M_{2p-4}^{\rm GOE}\\
&\quad-5(2p-3)(2p-4)(2p-5)(2N-1)M_{2p-6}^{\rm GOE}\\
&\quad-2(2p-3)(2p-4)(2p-5)(2p-6)(2p-7)M_{2p-8}^{\rm GOE}.\end{split}

We also refer to
[
71
]
for a combinatorial aspect of this formula.
In
[
64
]
, the formula (
1.7
) follows from a linear differential equation for the moment generating function (MGF), see also

[
76
]
and references therein for more recent work.
The method of Ledoux relies on elementary yet nontrivial analysis employing Gaussian integration by parts and certain properties of the Hermite polynomials. This method can also be applied to re-derive the recursion (
1.2
) for the GUE.
From the viewpoint of the integrable structure, the additional technical difficulty for the GOE, compared to the GUE, is that the
1
-point function (
3.12
) of the GOE consists of two parts.
One of these parts is indeed the Christoffel-Darboux kernel of the Hermite polynomials, which coincides with the GUE density.
For the GUE density part, one can make use of the classical Christoffel-Darboux formula.

While there has been extensive study on the spectral moments of Hermitian random matrix ensembles, their non-Hermitian counterparts remain relatively unexplored, and we aim to make contributions in this direction.
In non-Hermitian random matrix theory, the basic model typically considered is the Ginibre matrix, see
[
16
,
17
]
for recent reviews.
In particular, what distinguishes the real Ginibre matrix, referred to as GinOE, from its complex or quaternionic counterparts, GinUE and GinSE respectively, is the presence of purely real eigenvalues, see Figure
1
.
We shall focus on the statistics of real eigenvalues of real random matrices.
Compared to Hermitian random matrices, the study of real eigenvalues of asymmetric random matrices has additional conceptual and technical challenges:

•

the number of real eigenvalues is random;

•

the classical Christoffel-Darboux formula does not apply.

We refer to
[
38
,
43
,
66
,
77
,
78
,
3
,

,
20
,
22
,
42
,

,

]
and references therein for recent work on real eigenvalues of various asymmetric random matrices.

In this work, we consider the elliptic GinOE, the real random matrices
X\equiv X_{\tau}
that are distributed according to the probability measure proportional to

(1.8)

\exp\Big(-\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(X^{T}X-\tau X^{2})}{2(1-\tau^{2})}\Big)\,dX,\qquad\tau\in[0,1],

where
dX
is the Lebesgue measure on the space
\mathbb{R}^{N^{2}}
of
N\times N
matrices with real elements, and
X^{T}
is the transpose of
X
.
Here, the parameter
\tau
is known as the non-Hermiticity parameter.
Alternatively, the elliptic GinOE can be defined as

(1.9)

X_{\tau}:=\sqrt{\frac{1+\tau}{2}}\,S_{+}+\sqrt{\frac{1-\tau}{2}}\,S_{-},\qquad S_{\pm}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(G\pm G^{T}).

Here
G
is an
N\times N
GinOE matrix, the random matrix with all independent Gaussian entries
\textup{N}[0,1/N]
.
Notice that the symmetrisation
S_{+}
of the GinOE coincides with the GOE.
The elliptic random matrix model stands out as a well-known model, seamlessly bridging fundamental concepts in non-Hermitian and Hermitian random matrix theories.
Namely, for
\tau=0
, it recovers the GinOE, whereas in the limit
\tau\uparrow 1
, it recovers the GOE.
We refer the reader to
[
16
, Section 2.3]
and
[
17
, Sections 2.8 and 5.5]
for reviews as well as comprehensive references on the elliptic Ginibre ensembles. See also
[
74
,
53
,
42
,
58
,
21
]
for some very recent works on the elliptic Ginibre matrices.

From now on, we shall focus on the case that the matrix dimension
N
is even.
The odd
N
case can also be analysed but requires further non-trivial computations
[
79
,
44
]
.
Let us denote by
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\mathcal{N}_{\tau}}
the real eigenvalues of the elliptic GinOE, where
\mathcal{N}_{\tau}
is the number of real eigenvalues.
Along the discussions above, we write

(1.10)

M_{p}\equiv M_{p,\tau}:=\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{\mathcal{N}_{\tau}}\lambda_{j}^{p}\Big]

for the
p
-th spectral moment of real eigenvalues of the elliptic GinOE.
In particular, the zero-th moment

(1.11)

M_{0,\tau}=\mathbb{E}\mathcal{N}_{\tau}

corresponds to the expected number of real eigenvalues.
In contrast to the GUE or GOE cases, the evaluation of the spectral moments of the elliptic GinOE does not permit simple formulas and requires highly non-trivial analysis.
For instance, it was shown in
[
46
]
that
M_{0,\tau}
can be evaluated as

(1.12)

M_{0,\tau}=\Big(\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{\Gamma(2k+\frac{1}{2})}{(2k)!}{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\,;\,-2k+\frac{1}{2}\,;\,-\frac{\tau}{1-\tau}\Big),

where
{}_{2}F_{1}
is the hypergeometric function defined by

(1.13)

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z):=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(a+s)\Gamma(b+s)}{\Gamma(c+s)s!}z^{s},\quad(|z|<1)

and by the analytic continuation otherwise.
The expression (
1.12
) results from intricate computations as well as extensive simplifications, utilizing the skew-orthogonal polynomial formalism developed by Forrester and Nagao in
[
45
,
46
]
.
For the reader’s convenience, we provide a brief exposition of this formalism and the derivation of (
1.12
) in Appendix
A
.
For the extremal case when
\tau=0,1
, the formula (
1.12
) reduces to

(1.14)

M_{0,\tau}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}\dfrac{(4k-1)!!}{(4k)!!}&\textup{if }\tau=0,\\
N&\textup{if }\tau=1.\end{cases}

This formula for the GinOE case
\tau=0
was first obtained in the work
[
33
]
of Edelman, Kostlan and Shub.
On the other hand, for the GOE case
\tau=1
, this is trivial because GOE matrices are inherently symmetric.
This can also be interpreted as the random variable
\mathcal{N}_{\tau}
becoming deterministic in the Hermitian limit
\tau\uparrow 1
.
We mention that the large-
N
asymptotic behaviours of
M_{0,\tau}
have been extensively studied in recent works
[
20
,
22
]
.

We now present our main result.
For this purpose, for
l=\{1,\dots,10\},
let

(1.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{A}_{l}\equiv\mathfrak{A}_{l}(p;\tau)&:=\frac{1}{(1+6\tau+2N(1-\tau^{2}))(4+17\tau+6\tau^{2}+2N(1-\tau^{2}))^{2}}\\
&\quad\times\frac{1}{256(p-4)(p-3)(p-2)}\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{10-l,7\}}(2p-k-2l+1)_{k+2l-3}\,\mathfrak{a}_{k,k+2l-3},\end{split}

where
(a)_{n}=a(a+1)\dots(a+n-1)
is the Pochhammer symbol and
\mathfrak{a}_{l,k}\equiv\mathfrak{a}_{l,k}(\tau)
is the coefficient of the
t^{k}
-term in the polynomial
A_{l}(t)
(see (
2.11
) below).
Here,
A_{l}(t)
’s are odd or even polynomials of degree
17-l
that are given in (
2.10
).

Theorem 1.1

(
Recursion formula of the elliptic GinOE
)
.

Let
\tau\in[0,1].

Then for any integer
p\geq 10
and even integer
N\geq 2
, we have the 11-term recurrence relation

(1.16)

2(2p+1)(1-\tau)^{6}\,M_{2p,\tau}=\sum_{l=1}^{10}\mathfrak{A}_{l}(p;\tau)\,M_{2p-2l,\tau},

where
\mathfrak{A}_{l}
’s are given by (
1.15
).

We note that while it is possible to write the coefficients
\mathfrak{A}_{l}
explicitly, the resulting expressions are seemingly complicated, see Section
2
.
Nevertheless, when considering the extremal cases
\tau=0,1
, we encounter dramatic simplifications:

(1.17)

\mathfrak{A}_{l}=0,\qquad\begin{cases}\textup{if }\tau=0\textup{ and }l\geq 3,\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\textup{if }\tau=1\textup{ and }l\leq 5.\end{cases}

This leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2

(
Recursion formula of the GinOE and GOE
)
.

For any even integer
N\geq 2
, we have the following.

(i)

The GinOE case (
\tau=0
)
.
For any integer
p\geq 2
, we have the 3-term recurrence relation

(1.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}2(2p+1)M_{2p,0}&=(2p-1)(6p+4N-5)M_{2p-2,0}-(2p-3)(2p+N-4)(2p+2N-3)M_{2p-4,0}.\end{split}

(ii)

The GOE case (
\tau=1
)
. For any integer
p\geq 4
, the recurrence relation (
1.7
) holds.

We mention that Corollary
1.2
(ii) reproduces the result
[
64
, Theorem 2]
of Ledoux.
On the other hand, the recurrence relation (
1.18
) for real eigenvalues of the GinOE matrix has not been reported in the literature to the best of our knowledge.
After the present work, the GinOE case has been further investigated in a recent literature
[
18
]
.

The key ingredient for the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the moment generating function (the Laplace transform)

(1.19)

M(t)\equiv M_{\tau}(t):=\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{\mathcal{N}_{\tau}}e^{t\,\lambda_{j}}\Big]

of real eigenvalues. As previously remarked, the recursion formula of the spectral moments can be derived from a linear differential equations for
M_{\tau}
, cf. (
3.32
).
The differential equation for
M_{\tau}
is formulated in the following theorem, which is of independent interest.

Theorem 1.3

(
Differential equation for the MGF of the elliptic GinOE
)
.

Let
\tau\in[0,1].
Then for any even integer
N\geq 2
, the moment generating function
M_{\tau}(t)
satisfies the seventh-order differential equation

(1.20)

\sum_{k=0}^{7}A_{k}(t)M^{(k)}_{\tau}(t)=0,

where the polynomials
A_{k}
’s are given in (
2.10
).

The existence of such a linear differential equation for the moment generating function is already far from obvious.
Furthermore, it is crucial to highlight that the coefficients in (
1.20
) are polynomials. This, in turn, results in a finite-term recurrence relation for the spectral moments.

For the extremal cases, explicit evaluations of the polynomials
A_{k}
are provided in Example
2.3
, where we once again observe significant simplifications. As a consequence, we have the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 1.4

(
Differential equation for the MGF of the GinOE and GOE
)
.

For any even integer
N\geq 2
, we have the following.

(i)

The GinOE case (
\tau=0
)
. For
\tau=0
, we have

(1.21)

\sum_{k=1}^{7}C_{k}(t)M^{(k)}_{0}(t)=0,

where

(1.22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{7}(t)&=2\,t^{4},\qquad C_{6}(t)=-3\,t^{5}+2\,t^{3},\qquad C_{5}(t)=t^{6}-(4N+13)\,t^{4}-42\,t^{2},\\
C_{4}(t)&=(3N+5)\,t^{5}+4(N+10)\,t^{3}+120\,t,\qquad C_{3}(t)=(N-1)(2N+3)\,t^{4}+36N\,t^{2}-120,\\
C_{2}(t)&=-6(N+1)^{2}\,t^{3}-120(N+1)\,t,\qquad C_{1}(t)=6(N+1)^{2}\,t^{2}+120(N+1).\end{split}

(ii)

The GOE case (
\tau=1
)
. For
\tau=1
, we have

(1.23)

\sum_{k=0}^{4}C_{k}(t)M^{(k)}_{1}(t)=0,

where

(1.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{4}(t)&=t,\qquad C_{3}(t)=5,\qquad C_{2}(t)=-5t^{3}-(8N-4)t,\\
C_{1}(t)&=-36\,t^{2}-20N+10,\qquad C_{0}(t)=4\,t^{5}+(20N-10)\,t^{3}+(16N^{2}-16N-44)t.\end{split}

We stress that Corollary
1.4
(ii) for the GOE was previously derived and used in
[
64
, Section 3]
.
On the other hand, the differential equation (
1.21
) for the GinOE is new to our best knowledge.
However, the spectral moments of the GinOE real and complex eigenvalues combined were studied in
[
47
,
82
]
, see also
[
18
]
.

Let us discuss Corollary
1.4
(i) from the viewpoint of the large-
N
limit of the GinOE.

Remark 1.5

(
Large-
N
expansion of the GinOE
)
.

For the GinOE case
\tau=0
, we define the rescaled moment generating function

(1.25)

\widetilde{M}(t):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}M\Big(\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\Big)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{tx}\rho_{N}(x)\,dx,

where
\rho_{N}(x):=R_{N}(\sqrt{N}x)
is the rescaled density, see (
3.10
) for its explicit formula.
By using (
1.21
) and the change of variables, one can observe that
\widetilde{M}
satisfies the differential equation

(1.26)

\sum_{k=1}^{7}\widetilde{C}_{k}(t)\,\widetilde{M}^{(k)}(t)=0,\qquad\widetilde{C}_{k}(t):=N^{k/2}\,C_{k}\Big(\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\Big).

Expanding
\widetilde{C}_{k}
’s for large-
N
, this equation can be rewritten as

(1.27)

\mathfrak{D}(t)\Big[\widetilde{M}(t)\Big]=0,\qquad\mathfrak{D}(t):=\mathfrak{D}_{0}(t)+\frac{\mathfrak{D}_{1}(t)}{N}+\frac{\mathfrak{D}_{1}(t)}{N^{2}},

where the linear differential operators
\mathfrak{D}_{k}
’s are given by

(1.28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{D}_{0}(t)&:=2\,t^{4}\,\partial_{t}^{7}+2\,t^{3}\,\partial_{t}^{6}-(4t^{4}+42t^{2})\,\partial_{t}^{5}+(4t^{3}+120\,t)\,\partial_{t}^{4}\\
&\quad+(2t^{4}+36t^{2}-120)\,\partial_{t}^{3}-(6t^{3}+120t)\,\partial_{t}^{2}+(6t^{2}+120)\,\partial_{t},\end{split}

(1.29)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{D}_{1}(t)&:=-3\,t^{5}\,\partial_{t}^{6}-13\,t^{4}\,\partial_{t}^{5}+(3\,t^{5}+40\,t^{3})\,\partial_{t}^{4}+t^{4}\,\partial_{t}^{3}-(12t^{3}+120t)\,\partial_{t}^{2}+(12t^{2}+120)\,\partial_{t},\end{split}

(1.30)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{D}_{2}(t)&:=t^{6}\,\partial_{t}^{5}+5t^{2}\,\partial_{t}^{4}-3\,t^{4}\,\partial_{t}^{3}-6t^{3}\,\partial_{t}^{2}+6t^{2}\,\partial_{t}.\end{split}

On the one hand, it is well known that the rescaled density
\rho_{N}(x)
satisfies the expansion

(1.31)

\rho_{N}(x)=\rho_{(0)}(x)+\rho_{(1/2)}(x)\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}+O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big)

in the sense of distribution, where

(1.32)

\rho_{(0)}(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\mathbbm{1}_{(-1,1)}(x),\qquad\rho_{(1/2)}(x):=\frac{1}{4}\Big(\delta(x-1)+\delta(x+1)\Big),

see e.g.
[
17
, pp. 33–34]
. Then it follows that

(1.33)

\widetilde{M}(t)=\widetilde{M}_{(0)}(t)+\widetilde{M}_{(1/2)}(t)\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}+O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big),

where

(1.34)

\widetilde{M}_{(0)}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{tx}\rho_{(0)}(x)\,dx=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\sinh(t)}{t},\qquad\widetilde{M}_{(1/2)}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{tx}\rho_{(1/2)}(x)\,dx=\frac{\cosh(t)}{2}.

Furthermore, by (
1.27
), we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(t)\Big[\widetilde{M}(t)\Big]=\Big[\mathfrak{D}_{0}(t)+\frac{\mathfrak{D}_{1}(t)}{N}+\frac{\mathfrak{D}_{1}(t)}{N^{2}}\Big]\Big[\widetilde{M}_{(0)}(t)+\widetilde{M}_{(1/2)}(t)\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}+O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big)\Big],

which in turn implies

(1.35)

\mathfrak{D}_{0}(t)\Big[\widetilde{M}_{(0)}(t)\Big]=\mathfrak{D}_{0}(t)\Big[\widetilde{M}_{(1/2)}(t)\Big]=0.

Since we have the explicit formulas (
1.34
), one can directly check that these identities hold.

In the opposite direction, one can make use of (
1.34
) to find the factorisations

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{D}_{0}(t)&=2(t^{3}\,\partial_{t}^{3}-6t^{2}\,\partial_{t}^{2}+15t\,\partial_{t}-15)\circ(t\,\partial_{t}^{2}+4\,\partial_{t}-t)\circ(\partial_{t}^{2}-1)\\
&=2(t^{3}\,\partial_{t}^{3}-6t^{2}\,\partial_{t}^{2}+15t\,\partial_{t}-15)\circ(\partial_{t}^{2}-1)\circ(t\,\partial_{t}^{2}+2\partial_{t}-t).\end{split}

We mention that
\widetilde{M}_{(1/2)}(t)
is annihilated by the the rightmost differential operator in the first line, while
\widetilde{M}_{(0)}(t)
is annihilated by the the rightmost differential operator in the second line.
Furthermore, since the general solution of the differential equation

(t^{3}\,\partial_{t}^{3}-6t^{2}\,\partial_{t}^{2}+15t\,\partial_{t}-15)f(t)=0

is of the form
f(t)=c_{1}t+c_{2}t^{3}+c_{3}t^{5}
, one can further factorise and obtain

(1.36)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{D}_{0}(t)&=2(t\,\partial_{t}-a)\circ(t\,\partial_{t}-b)\circ(t\,\partial_{t}-c)\circ(t\,\partial_{t}^{2}+4\,\partial_{t}-t)\circ(\partial_{t}^{2}-1)\\
&=2(t\,\partial_{t}-a)\circ(t\,\partial_{t}-b)\circ(t\,\partial_{t}-c)\circ(\partial_{t}^{2}-1)\circ(t\,\partial_{t}^{2}+2\partial_{t}-t),\end{split}

where
(a,b,c)
is a permutation of
(1,3,5)
.

Remark 1.6

(
Large-
N
expansion of the elliptic GinOE
)
.

Continuing the discussions from the previous remark, it is natural to consider the large-
N
expansion of the elliptic GinOE for a general value of
\tau\in[0,1]
.
Once again, we introduce the rescaled density
\rho_{N}(x):=R_{N}(\sqrt{N}x)
.
In the study of the large-
N
asymptotics of the elliptic Ginibre matrices, one needs to distinguish the following two different regimes.

•

Strong non-Hermiticity
. This is the case where
\tau
is fixed in the interval
[0,1)
.
In this regime, it was shown in
[
46
, Section 6.2]
that

(1.37)

\rho_{N}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(1-\tau^{2})}}\,\mathbbm{1}_{(-1-\tau,1+\tau)}(x)+o(1).

Therefore, one can observe that

(1.38)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2p}\,\rho_{N}(x)\,dx\sim M_{2p}^{\rm s},\qquad M_{2p}^{\rm s}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(1-\tau^{2})}}\frac{2}{2p+1}(1+\tau)^{2p+1}.

•

Weak non-Hermiticity
. This is the case that
\tau=1-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{N}
for a fixed
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
.
This regime was introduced by Fyodorov, Khoruzhenko and Sommers
[
50
,
51
]
in the study of the critical transition of the elliptic GinUE statistics, see
[
6
,
8
]
for further references and physical applications.
In this regime, it was shown in
[
20
, Theorem 2.3]
that

(1.39)

\rho_{N}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\operatorname{erf}\Big(\frac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}}\Big)\,\mathbbm{1}_{(-2,2)}(x)+o(\sqrt{N}).

We mention that this formula was previously proposed by Efetov in
[
34
]
.
Note that the leading order density in (
1.39
) interpolates the uniform density in (
1.37
) with the Wigner’s semi-circle law (
1.5
).
Using (
1.39
), one can show that

(1.40)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2p}\,\rho_{N}(x)\,dx\sim\sqrt{N}\,M_{2p}^{\rm w},\qquad M_{2p}^{\rm w}:=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{2p+1}}{2n+1}\frac{\Gamma(n+\frac{3}{2})\Gamma(p+\frac{1}{2})}{n!\,(n+p+1)!}\,(-\alpha^{2})^{n}.

We stress that in the Hermitian limit
\alpha\to 0
, the moment
M_{2p}^{\rm w}
recovers the Catalan number in (
1.4
), i.e.

(1.41)

\lim_{\alpha\to 0}M_{2p}^{\rm w}=c(0;p).

In the opposite limit
\alpha\to\infty
, one can also match
M_{2p}^{\rm w}
with
M_{2p}^{\rm s}
, see
[
20
]
for a similar discussion.
Let us also mention that
M_{2p}^{\rm w}
can be written in terms of the the modified Bessel function of the first kind

(1.42)

I_{\nu}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(z/2)^{2k+\nu}}{k!\,\Gamma(\nu+k+1)},

see e.g.
[
73
, Chapter 10]
. To be more precise, it is of the form

(1.43)

M_{2p}^{\rm w}=\Big[r_{0,2p}\,I_{0}\Big(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\Big)+r_{1,2p}\,I_{1}\Big(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\Big)\Big]e^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}},

where

and

are some rational functions of

For instance, the first few values of

and
r_{1,2p}
are given by

(1.44)

\displaystyle r_{0,0}=1,\qquad r_{0,2}=\frac{8(2\alpha^{4}-\alpha^{2})}{5\alpha^{4}},\qquad r_{0,4}=\frac{64(4\alpha^{8}-6\alpha^{6}+15\alpha^{4}-24\alpha^{2})}{9\alpha^{8}},

(1.45)

\displaystyle r_{1,0}=1,\qquad r_{1,2}=\frac{8(2\alpha^{4}-3\alpha^{2}+4)}{5\alpha^{4}},\qquad r_{1,4}=\frac{64(4\alpha^{8}-10\alpha^{6}+27\alpha^{4}-60\alpha^{2}+96)}{9\alpha^{8}}.

In Appendix
A

1.40
) and (
1.43

We have discussed the leading-order asymptotics of the spectral moments for both the strong and weak non-Hermiticity regimes.

The starting point of the proof is the integrable structure and skew-orthogonal polynomial representation of the

H_{k}(x):=(-1)^{k}e^{x^{2}}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}e^{-x^{2}}.

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{N}^{(2)}(x)&:=\frac{(\tau/2)^{N-\frac{3}{2}}}{1+\tau}\frac{1}{(N-2)!}\frac{e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}H_{N-1}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\Big)\int_{0}^{x}e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}}H_{N-2}\Big(\frac{u}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\Big)\,du.\end{split}

(3.8)

\displaystyle R_{N}^{(1)}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{e^{-x^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{x^{2k}}{k!}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{1}{(N-2)!}\Gamma(N-1,x^{2}),

Appendix A

In this appendix, we give a brief exposition of the integrable structure of real eigenvalues of the elliptic GinOE established by Forrester and Nagao in
[
46
]
, along with additional details on certain computations.
In addition to this, we prove (
1.40
) and (

) in Remark

.

Let us write

(A.1)

C_{k}(x):=\Big(\frac{\tau}{2}\Big)^{k/2}H_{k}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\Big)

for the scaled monic Hermite polynomials.
Note that

(A.2)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}C_{n}(x)=nC_{n-1}(x),\qquad x\,C_{n}(x)=C_{n+1}(x)+n\tau\,C_{n-1}(x).

It was shown in
[
46

that

(A.3)

p_{2k}(x):=C_{2k}(x),\qquad p_{2k+1}(x):=C_{2k+1}(x)-2k\,C_{2k-1}(x).

form skew-orthogonal polynomials associated with real eigenvalues of the elliptic GinOE.
This is indeed one of the very few examples of explicit construction of skew-orthogonal polynomials for asymmetric random matrices in the symmetry class of the GinOE.
(See
[
7
]
for another example in the context of the chiral GinOE.)
We also denote

(A.4)

\begin{split}\Phi_{k}(x)&:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,\textup{sgn}(x-y)\,p_{k}(y)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}}\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{k}(y)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}}\,dy-2\int_{x}^{\infty}p_{k}(y)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}}\,dy.\end{split}

Then one can notice that

(A.5)

\frac{d}{dx}\Phi_{k}(x)=2\,p_{k}(x)e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}}.

Proposition A.1
.

For any even integer
N\geq 2
and

(A.7)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{\Phi_{2k+1}(x)p_{2k}(x)}{(2k)!}

\displaystyle=2(1+\tau)e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}}\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{C_{2k}(x)^{2}}{(2k)!},

(A.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{\Phi_{2k}(x)p_{2k+1}(x)}{(2k)!}

\displaystyle=2(1+\tau)e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}}\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-2}\frac{C_{2k+1}(x)^{2}}{(2k+1)!}+\frac{C_{N-1}(x)\Phi_{N-2}(x)}{(N-2)!}.

In particular, we have

(A.9)

R_{N}(x)=\frac{e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{1+\tau}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{C_{k}(x)^{2}}{k!}+\frac{e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}}}{2\sqrt{2\pi}(1+\tau)}\frac{C_{N-1}(x)\Phi_{N-2}(x)}{(N-2)!}

Proof.

Using (
A.2

\displaystyle p_{2k+1}(x)

(A.12)

\displaystyle p_{2k+2}(x)-(2k+1)p_{2k}(x)

On the other hand, since

\displaystyle\Phi_{2k}(x)p_{2k+1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\operatorname{sgn}(x-y)p_{2k}(y)p_{2k+1}(x)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}}\,dy

\displaystyle=(1+\tau)\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-2}\frac{C_{2k+1}(x)}{(2k+1)!}\bigg(\int_{-\infty}^{x}\frac{d}{dy}\Big[e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}}C_{2k+1}(y)\Big]\,dy-\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dy}\Big[e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}}C_{2k+1}(y)\Big]\,dy\bigg).

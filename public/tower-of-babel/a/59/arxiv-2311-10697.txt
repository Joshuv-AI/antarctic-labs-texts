PEFT-MedAware: Large Language Model for Medical Awareness Journal: Artificial Intelligence in Medicine
Keivalya Pandya

Abstract
Chat models are capable of answering a wide range of questions, however, the accuracy of their responses is highly uncertain. In this research, we propose a specialized peft-MedAware model where we utilize parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) to enhance the Falcon-1b large language model on specialized MedQuAD data consisting of 16,407 medical QA pairs, leveraging only 0.44% of its trainable parameters to enhance computational efficiency. The paper adopts data preprocessing and PEFT to optimize model performance, complemented by a BitsAndBytesConfig for efficient transformer training. The resulting model was capable of outperforming other LLMs in medical question-answering tasks in specific domains with greater accuracy utilizing limited computational resources making it suitable for deployment in resource-constrained environments. We propose further improvements through expanded datasets, larger models, and feedback mechanisms for sustained medical relevancy. Our work highlights the efficiency gains and specialized capabilities of PEFT in medical AI, outpacing standard models in precision without extensive resource demands. The proposed model and data are released for research purposes only.

This research is driven by the pressing need to address this gap. While Large Language Models (LLMs) have the potential to revolutionize medical awareness, their effectiveness hinges on their ability to provide accurate and detailed information tailored to specific medical queries.

LLMs play a crucial role in advancing medical awareness. The medical field is a complex, ever-evolving domain with a constant influx of new research and discoveries. People often turn to the internet to find answers to their health-related questions. LLMs have the potential to revolutionize this information-seeking process by offering accurate, contextually relevant, and up-to-date medical information.

3
Dataset

MedAware was trained on the MedQuAD
[
8
]
dataset, a large-scale medical question-answering dataset of 47,457 QA pairs created from 12 NIH websites. The collection covers 37 question types (e.g. Treatment, Diagnosis, Side Effects) associated with diseases, drugs, and other medical entities such as tests. It is observed that out of all these QA pairs (as mentioned officially), more than half of them are left unanswered leaving us with 16,407 QA pairs along with the question type. The dataset is well-annotated, with each question-answer pair labeled with the type of evidence that supports the answer. Since each QA in MedQuAD is unique, we do not divide the dataset into train and test sets.

Model Training

4.1
Parameter-Efficient Fine Tuning (PEFT)

Training large language models requires millions, or even billions, of parameters. Although these parameters are necessary to capture complex patterns in a linguistic context, they pose computational, memory, and energy management challenges. Traditional optimization methods struggle to strike a balance between better performance and computational efficiency. PEFT on subtly optimizing the parameters of the model, ensuring the best use of computational resources. This method is especially important when dealing with large objects.
[
9
]
By identifying and fine-tuning only the most influential parameters, the computational overhead is significantly reduced. This streamlined approach minimizes the time and resources required for training, making it feasible to experiment with various hyperparameters and configurations efficiently. The model that has been used contains over 700 million parameters out of which we utilize 3 million trainable parameters (that is, 0.44% of overall parameters) to fine-tune based on our dataset.

Model Setting

While configuring our parameters we have loaded our model using 4-bit precision which reduces the memory footprint and enables the training process to efficiently handle our chosen model and large datasets.
[
11
]
This reduction in precision does not compromise the ability of the model to learn complex patterns. Moreover, we have used the double quantization technique where the 4-bit precision is employed in a more nuanced manner, which ensures that even with reduced bit precision, the model can capture intricate features of the data, enhancing its learning capabilities while maintaining computational efficiency. The 16-bit binary floating-point representation for computational operations strikes a balance between computational efficiency and numerical accuracy.

The model conserves memory and computational resources by using lower-bit precision and optimized quantization techniques. This reduction in requirements enables the training of more extensive models or facilitates the simultaneous training of multiple models without overwhelming the system resources especially while accessing Google Colab T4 Runtime for a limited amount of time. This also leads to faster training times. With reduced computational complexity, the model can iterate through epochs more swiftly.

6
Conclusion

As technology advances, the specialized knowledge of platforms is redefining the landscape of medical consultations. The PEFT based model fine-tuning reduces the computational overhead significantly. By conserving memory, reducing computational complexity, and optimizing resources, our research benefits from faster training times and improved efficiency. We have observed that the PEFT approach, along with optimized quantization techniques makes the training process easier and efficient. While general AI models provide valuable information, they lack the depth required for accurate diagnoses. ChatGPT, despite its vast knowledge base, provides a generic overview of potential causes, lacking the specificity needed for an accurate diagnosis. Its response, while informative, lacks the depth required for medical accuracy. Baize-healthcare’s response is vague and non-committal. It acknowledges the symptoms but does not delve into specific conditions. While it encourages consulting a doctor, it does not provide any concrete information, leaving the user uncertain about potential causes. On the contrary, our proposed model peft-MedAware’s response demonstrates a remarkable level of precision and expertise. This ability to offer a specific diagnosis stems from its specialized focus on medical scenarios.

The model has been deployed to HuggingFace as https://huggingface.co/keivalya/peft-MedAware and this space carries all the updated information regarding the model.

7
Limitations

7.1
Safety and Access Control

Users must keep their data safe and private when feeding it into the system. Be mindful especially with sensitive details to prevent any unauthorized access or data leaks. Plus, access to the model and its results should only be given to authorized people. To make sure this happens, using the right access controls and authentication methods is important. These measures help keep the system in check and make sure it’s used responsibly.

7.2
Ethical Considerations

Language models, despite their impressive capabilities, can unintentionally inherit biases from the data they’re trained on. To counter this, it’s crucial to stay vigilant and work continuously to promote inclusivity and fairness. Additionally, we must consider the well-being of the users by protecting them from misinformation and ensuring that the model’s responses don’t encourage harmful activities. Providing clear disclaimers is essential, especially when the model’s limitations could influence users’ decisions.

2.
KSBA surfaces and exotic
4
-manifolds

Let
0<q<m
be coprime integers. A
cyclic quotient singularity
is a singularity locally isomorphic to the germ at
(0,0)
of the quotient of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
by the action of
(x,y)\mapsto(\mu x,\mu^{q}y)
, where
\mu
is an
m
-th primitive root of
1
. The notation for this singularity is
\frac{1}{m}(1,q)
.

Any normal two-dimensional singularity
(P\in W^{\prime})
admits a
minimal resolution
, this is, a birational morphism
\pi\colon X\to W^{\prime}
such that
X
is nonsingular, the pre-image of
P
is a collection of exceptional curves
\exc(\pi)
, none of them being a
(-1)
-curve
1
1

1

In a nonsingular surface a
(-p)
-curve is a
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{1}
whose self-intersection is equal to
-p
.
, and
\pi|_{X\setminus\exc(\pi)}
is an isomorphism. If
(P\in W^{\prime})
is a cyclic quotient singularity of type
\frac{1}{m}(1,q)
, then
\exc(\pi)
is a chain of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
’s
E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}
such that
E_{i}^{2}=-e_{i}\leq-2
, where the
e_{i}
are obtained through the associated Hirzebruch-Jung continued fraction
\frac{m}{q}=[e_{1},\ldots,e_{s}]
. We define its length as
s
. We have the numerical relation
K_{X}\equiv\pi^{*}(K_{W^{\prime}})+\sum_{i=1}^{s}d_{i}E_{i}
, where
-1<d_{i}\leq 0
are the discrepancies of the exceptional curves.

Definition 2.1
.

A
T-singularity
is a cyclic quotient singularity of type
\frac{1}{dn^{2}}(1,dna-1)
where
d\geq 1
, and
0<a<n
with gcd
(n,a)=1
. A
Wahl singularity
is a T-singularity with
d=1
. Its exceptional divisor in its minimal resolution will be called
Wahl chain
.

Remark 2.2
.

Any T-singularity or Du Val singularity
(P\in W^{\prime})
can be partially resolved
\phi\colon W\to W^{\prime}
so that
\phi^{*}(K_{W^{\prime}})\equiv K_{W}
. We call them
M-resolutions

[
BC94
]
. For a Du Val singularity, its M-resolution is just the minimal resolution. For a T-singularity
\frac{1}{dn^{2}}(1,dna-1)
, its M-resolution has as exceptional divisor a chain of
d-1

{\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{1}
’s passing through
d
Wahl singularities
\frac{1}{n^{2}}(1,na-1)
.

T-singularities and Du Val singularities are key to describing all deformations of an arbitrary quotient singularity by means of its P-resolutions. This was approached in the foundational work of Kollár and Shepherd-Barron
[
KSB88
]
, where a compactification for the moduli space of surfaces of general type is introduced, nowadays known as the KSBA compactification. Earlier than that work, Wahl introduces T-singularities in
[
W81
]
as examples admitting a smoothing with constant
K^{2}
, and in
[
LW86
, Proposition 5.9]
Looijenga and Wahl show that T-singularities (and Du Val type A singularities) are the only cyclic quotient singularities admitting a smoothing with that property. In
[
KSB88
]
it is proved that T-singularities and Du Val singularities are precisely the quotient singularities that admit a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothing, this is, a deformation over a disk with nonsingular general fibers so that the underlying 3-fold has a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier canonical class.

One way to construct new complex surfaces is by considering
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothings of a surface
W
with only Wahl singularities. This approach has been successful in finding new surfaces of general type with particular properties (see e.g.
[
LP07
]
,
[
PPS09a
]
,
[
PPS09b
]
). To guarantee the existence of a smoothing, it is typically shown that there a no local-to-global obstructions to deforming
W
. This is a strong condition for surfaces in general, where it is not easy to prove the existence of complex smoothings in other ways.

Instead, Fintushel and Stern
[
FS97
]
(see also Park
[
P97
]
for a complete generality) introduced the
rational blowdown
technique. This is a surgical procedure on a closed 4-manifold that contains disjoint Wahl chains, obtaining a new smooth closed 4-manifold by removing a small neighborhood of the Wahl chains and attaching rational homology balls instead. These are the Milnor fibers of the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothings of the corresponding Wahl singularities. (See
[
SSW08
]
for more on the relation between these two viewpoints.) In
[
Sy98
,
Sy01
]
, Symington shows that one can give a symplectic structure to the rational blowdown, provided that we start with a symplectic 4-manifold and the 2-spheres in the Wahl chains are symplectic.

A nonsingular complex projective surface
X
admits a symplectic form inherited from some embedding in projective space. Thus, if
X
contains disjoint Wahl chains, then we can apply Symington’s theorem to construct a symplectic rational blowdown
Y
along the Wahl chains in
X
.

We recall that a divisor
D
on an irreducible projective surface
Z
is nef if
D\cdot\Gamma\geq 0
for all curves
\Gamma\subset Z
. In addition
D
is big if and only if
D^{2}>0

[
L04
, Theorem 2.2.16]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
W^{\prime}
be a normal projective surface with only T-singularities and Du Val singularities such that
K_{W^{\prime}}
is ample. Let
\phi\colon W\to W^{\prime}
be the M-resolution of
W^{\prime}
, and let
\pi\colon X\to W
be the minimal resolution of
W
. Then the rational blowdown
Y
of
\exc(\pi)
is a (smoothly) minimal symplectic 4-manifold.

Proof.

Let
E_{i}
be the curves in
\exc(\pi)
, and let
F_{i}
(resp.
G_{i}
) be the proper transforms in
X
of the exceptional curves of
\phi
over T-singularities (resp. Du Val singularities). First we claim there is
N>0
, and integers
n_{i},m_{i},s_{i}\geq 0
such that

H:=\pi^{*}(NK_{W})-\sum_{i}n_{i}E_{i}-\sum_{i}m_{i}F_{i}-\sum_{i}s_{i}G_{i}

is ample. We know that there are integers
n_{i},m_{i},s_{i}\geq 0
such that

\big(\sum_{i}n_{i}E_{i}+\sum_{i}m_{i}F_{i}+\sum_{i}s_{i}G_{i}\big)\cdot\Gamma<0,

for all
\Gamma=E_{j},F_{j},G_{j}
as the intersection matrices for exceptional curves over singularities are negative definite.

We know that
K_{W}\equiv\phi^{*}(K_{W^{\prime}})
is nef and big. Then by
[
L04
, Proposition 2.2.6]
(Kodaira’s lemma), there is
M>0
(sufficiently divisible) such that

H^{0}\big(X,\pi^{*}(MK_{W})-\sum_{i}n_{i}E_{i}-\sum_{i}m_{i}F_{i}-\sum_{i}s_{i}G_{i}\big)\neq 0,

and so we have that
H_{0}=\pi^{*}(MK_{W})-\sum_{i}n_{i}E_{i}-\sum_{i}m_{i}F_{i}-\sum_{i}s_{i}G_{i}
is linearly equivalent to some fixed effective divisor. Thus there could be a finite number of curves (in that fixed divisor) which may have a nonpositive intersection with
H_{0}
. As
H_{0}\cdot\Gamma>0
for all
\Gamma=E_{j},F_{j},G_{j}
and they are the only curves that intersect zero with
\pi^{*}(MK_{W})
(for the rest of curves it has to be positive as
K_{W^{\prime}}
is ample), we can find
N>M
so that
H=H_{0}+\pi^{*}((N-M)K_{W})
intersects any curve in
X
positively, and
H^{2}>0
(as
K_{W^{\prime}}
is ample). Hence by the Nakai-Moishezon criterion
[
L04
, Theorem 1.2.23]
, we see that
H
is ample.

Let us assume we took a higher multiple to make
H
very ample. In this way, we have an embedding of
X
into projective space via
H
. Let
X^{0}
be
X
minus the Wahl chains in
\exc(\pi)
, and let
W^{0}
be
W
minus the Wahl singularities. We have that
H|_{X^{0}}
is
\pi^{*}(NK_{W})|_{X^{0}}-\sum_{i}m_{i}F_{i}-\sum_{i}s_{i}G_{i}
. We have the symplectic form
\omega_{X}
induced by the complex structure in the class of
H
. As
Y
is the rational blowdown of
X
at the Wahl chains in
\exc(\pi)
, we have that
H_{2}(W^{0},{\mathbb{Z}})\subset H_{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
, and we also have
H_{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})\subset H_{2}(W,{\mathbb{Z}})
as the Milnor fibers a rational homology balls. Therefore, the Symington’s symplectic form
\omega_{Y}
on
Y
is in the class represented by
NK_{Y}-\sum_{i}m_{i}F_{i}-\sum_{i}s_{i}G_{i}
.

By
[
Li06
, Proposition 2.1]
, if
Y
is not minimal (in a smooth sense), then there is a symplectic
(-1)
-sphere
E
in
Y
. But then

0<\int_{E}\omega_{Y}|_{E}=E\cdot(NK_{Y}-\sum_{i}m_{i}F_{i}-\sum_{i}s_{i}G_{i})<0,

as none of the
F_{i}
,
G_{i}
are
(-1)
-spheres, which is a contradiction. Therefore
Y
is minimal.

∎

Remark 2.4
.

We note that if
W^{\prime}=W
, i.e. only Wahl singularities on
W^{\prime}
, then
\omega_{Y}
is in the class of
NK_{Y}
. We can also get a symplectic form

in the class of

starting with the

in Theorem

, by means of constructing

Throughout this paper we will only consider those

We recall that
\chi_{\text{top}}(Y)=\chi_{\text{top}}(W)
and
K_{Y}^{2}=K_{W}^{2}
for the rational blowdown in Theorem
2.3
. Let
(b^{+},b^{-})
be the signature of the unimodular quadratic form on

. Then we have the second Betti number
b_{2}(Y)=b^{+}+b^{-}=\chi_{\text{top}}(W)-2
, and for its signature we have
b^{+}-b^{-}=\frac{1}{3}(K^{2}_{W}-2\chi_{\text{top}}(W))
. Therefore

b^{+}=2\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{W})-1\ \ \ \ \ b^{-}=10\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{W})-K^{2}_{W}-1,

as the Noether’s formula

holds for

. In particular, the intersection form cannot be negative definite. It cannot be positive definite either as that would imply

, and by the orbifold Bogomolov-Miyaoka-Yau inequality (see e.g.
[

]
) we would have

, where

, and so

, a contradiction. Therefore the quadratic form must be indefinite. By Freedman’s theorem, the oriented homeomorphism type of

is completely determined by its intersection form, and as it must be indefinite, it is determined by

,

, and its parity (see e.g.
[
BHPV04
, Chapter IX]
).

In this paper, we will only consider the case when the intersection form is odd, so

is homeomorphic to
b^{+}{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\#b^{-}\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
with
b^{-}>0
. A consequence of
[

is the following.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let

be the minimal symplectic 4-manifold constructed in Theorem

is simply-connected and it has an odd intersection form, then

is an exotic
b^{+}{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\#b^{-}\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
with

following what is done in
[
RU19
, Lemma 2.8]
, so the bound ends up being

s\leq 4K_{W}^{2}+1.

This bound is optimal by
[
RU19
]
, and it is not true for
W
rational. Two boundedness problems on these surfaces
W
remain open:

(1)

Find bounds depending on
K_{W}^{2}
which involve all singularities at once, and

(2)

Find bounds depending on
K_{W}^{2}
when
W
is a rational surface.

Problem (1) involves hard combinatorics, as one has to deal with “bad” configurations of
(-1)
-curves and Wahl chains in
X
. That analysis would allow us to know and classify those bad configurations. Problem (2) was partially worked out in
[
RU19
, Theorem 1.3]
, but we do not have yet a real bound depending only on
K_{W}^{2}
.

We are going to focus on the configuration of rational curves
\sigma(\exc(\pi))
. The objective is to find a connection between the problem of the existence of a
W
with the required properties, and log Chern numbers of
\sigma(\exc(\pi))
. In this way, by applying the log BMY inequality, we will produce constraints for the existence of
W
.

We recall the log Chern numbers of a configuration of curves
\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}\}
in a surface
Z
; Cf.
[
Urz10
]
. Let
\phi\colon\tilde{Z}\to Z
be a log resolution of
D=\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}
, in the sense that
\phi
is a birational morphism such that the (reduced) pull-back
\tilde{D}
of
D
has only simple normal crossings, and any exceptional
(-1)
-curve in
\tilde{D}
intersects at least two other curves in
\tilde{D}
. Then the log Chern numbers of
(Z,D)
are defined via the Chern classes of
\Omega_{\tilde{Z}}^{1}(\log\tilde{D})
as
\bar{c}_{1}^{2}(Z,D):=c_{1}(\Omega_{\tilde{Z}}^{1}(\log\tilde{D})^{*})^{2}
and
\bar{c}_{2}(Z,D):=c_{2}(\Omega_{\tilde{Z}}^{1}(\log\tilde{D})^{*})
. We have
\bar{c}_{1}^{2}(Z,D)=(K_{\tilde{Z}}+\tilde{D})^{2}
and
\bar{c}_{2}(Z,D)=\chi_{\text{top}}(\tilde{Z}\setminus\tilde{D})
. It can be proved that they do not depend on the log resolution. (See
[
EFU22
,
Urz22
]
for a systematic study of these invariants when
Z={\mathbb{P}}^{2}
and
D
is a configuration of lines.) We will only consider the following type of configurations.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
Z
be a nonsingular projective surface. A
nodal simple crossings configuration
is a collection of projective curves
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{r}
such that:

•

The curve
C_{i}
is nodal and rational with
\nu_{i}
nodes, and

•

At a common point of
C_{i}
and
C_{j}
there are different tangent directions.

Hence, the divisor
D=\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}
has only simple crossings singularities, which are locally of the form
(x-a_{1}y)(x-a_{2}y)\cdots(x-a_{k}y)=0
at
(0,0)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
with
a_{i}\neq a_{j}
. Such a point will be called
k-point
. The number of k-points in
D
will be denoted by
t_{k}
. We denote the number of nodes as

\nu:=\sum_{i}\nu_{i}.

The numbers
\nu
and
t_{2}
are not necessarily comparable.

Proposition 3.3
.

We have
\bar{c}_{1}^{2}(Z,D)=K_{Z}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}^{2}+\sum_{k\geq 2}(3k-4)t_{k}-4r+2\nu,
and
\bar{c}_{2}(Z,D)=\chi_{\text{top}}(Z)+\sum_{k\geq 2}(k-1)t_{k}-2r.

Proof.

Let
Z^{\prime}\to Z
be the blow-up at all nodes from the
C_{i}
. These nodes could be isolated, or they could be at k-points with
k>2
. Let
D^{\prime}
be the reduced pull-back of
D
in
Z^{\prime}
. By
[
Urz10
]
, There are formulas for the log Chern numbers of
(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})
, and they translate to

\bar{c}_{1}^{2}(Z,D)=K_{Z}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}^{2}+\sum_{k\geq 2}(3k-4)t_{k}+4\sum_{i=1}^{r}(p_{a}(C_{i})-1)-2\nu,

and

\bar{c}_{2}(Z,D)=\chi_{\text{top}}(Z)+\sum_{k\geq 2}(k-1)t_{k}+2\sum_{i=1}^{r}(p_{a}(C_{i})-1)-2\nu.

But the curves
C_{i}
are rational, and so
p_{a}(C_{i})=\nu_{i}
for all
i
.
∎

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\{{C^{\prime}}_{1},\ldots,{C^{\prime}}_{r^{\prime}}\}
be any configuration of curves on a nonsingular surface
Z^{\prime}
, and let
D^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{r^{\prime}}{C^{\prime}}_{i}
. We define

P(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime}):=\sum_{i=1}^{r^{\prime}}\left({C^{\prime}}_{i}^{2}+5-\sum_{P\in\sing(D^{\prime})}m_{P}({C^{\prime}}_{i})^{2}\right),

where
m_{P}(C)
is the multiplicity of
P\in C
, and

K(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime}):=K_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}+2r^{\prime}-|\sing(D^{\prime})|-P(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime}).

Note that if
D^{\prime}
is a collection of
\ell
disjoint Wahl chains, then
P(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})=\ell
and
K(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})=K_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}+r^{\prime}
by the particular properties of Wahl chains
[
KSB88
, Proposition 3.11]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
D^{\prime}=C^{\prime}_{1}+\ldots+C^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}
be any configuration of curves on a nonsingular surface
Z^{\prime}
. If
E
is a nonsingular curve not in
D^{\prime}
, intersecting it at
n
nonsingular points of
D^{\prime}
and
m
singular points of
D^{\prime}
, then
P(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime}+E)=P(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})+E^{2}+5-2n-m
, and
K(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime}+E)=K(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})-E^{2}-3+n+m
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
D^{\prime}=C^{\prime}_{1}+\ldots+C^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}
be any configuration of curves on a nonsingular surface
Z^{\prime}
. Let
Z^{\prime\prime}\to Z^{\prime}
be the blow-up at a point
P
in
\sing(D^{\prime})
, and let
D^{\prime\prime}
be the strict transform of
D^{\prime}
. Then
P(Z^{\prime\prime},D^{\prime\prime})=P(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})
and
K(Z^{\prime\prime},D^{\prime\prime})=K(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})
. In particular, if the singularity of
D^{\prime}
at
P
is a node and
E
is the exceptional
(-1)
-curve, then
P(Z^{\prime\prime},D^{\prime\prime}+E)=P(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})
and
K(Z^{\prime\prime},D^{\prime\prime}+E)=K(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Both propositions are direct from the formulas in Definition
3.4
.
∎

From now on, let
D=\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}
be a nodal simple crossings configuration on
Z
.

Proposition 3.7
.

We have

P(Z,D)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}^{2}+5r-\sum_{k\geq 2}kt_{k}-2\nu,

and

K(Z,D)=K_{Z}^{2}-3r-\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}^{2}+\sum_{k\geq 2}(k-1)t_{k}+2\nu.

Let
\sigma^{\prime}\colon Z^{\prime}\to Z
be the blow-up at all the k-points of
D
with
k>2
. Let us choose
t_{k}^{0}
of the
t_{k}
exceptional curves for each
k>2
. Let
D^{\prime}
be the (reduced) pull-back of
D
minus the
\sum_{k>2}t_{k}^{0}
chosen exceptional curves. Then, by Propositions
3.5
and
3.6
, we can compute

P(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})=P(Z,D)-\sum_{k\geq 3}(2k-4)(t_{k}-t_{k}^{0}),

and
K(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})=K(Z,D)+\sum_{k\geq 3}(k-2)(t_{k}-t_{k}^{0})
.

We note that further blow-ups at the notes of
D^{\prime}
and additions of the corresponding exceptional curves will not change
P
and
K
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
W
be a normal projective surface with
\ell
Wahl singularities, and
K_{W}
big and nef. Let
\pi\colon X\to W
be its minimal resolution. Assume that there is a birational morphism
\sigma\colon X\to Z
such that
\sigma(\exc(\pi))
is a nodal simple crossings configuration of rational curves
D=C_{1}+\ldots+C_{r}
. Then,

K_{W}^{2}\leq 12(1+p_{g}(Z))-\frac{1}{3}K_{Z}^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\ell-\frac{1}{3}r+\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k\geq 3}(k-2)t_{k}^{0}.

Moreover,

\bar{c}_{1}^{2}(Z,D)=K_{Z}^{2}-\ell+r+2\sum_{k\geq 3}(k-2)t_{k}^{0},

\bar{c}_{2}(Z,D)=12(1+p_{g}(Z))-\ell-K_{W}^{2}+\sum_{k\geq 3}(k-2)t_{k}^{0},

and

2r=K_{W}^{2}-K_{Z}^{2}+\ell+\sum_{k\geq 2}(k-1)t_{k}-\sum_{k\geq 3}(k-2)t_{k}^{0}.

As we defined earlier, here
t_{k}^{0}
is the number of exceptional curves over
k
-points with
k>2
which are not part of
\exc(\pi)
.

Proof.

As before, let
\sigma^{\prime}\colon Z^{\prime}\to Z
be the blow-up at all the k-points of
D
with
k>2
. We have already computed
P(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})
and
K(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})
where
D^{\prime}
is the pull-back of
D
taking out the exceptional curves of
\sigma^{\prime}
that will not be part of the Wahl chains in
X
. As we want to calculate the log Chern numbers of the pair
(Z,D)
, we will consider all exceptional divisors of the log-resolution
Z^{\prime}\to Z
of
D
. It turns out that including them all imposes more restrictions on
K_{W}^{2}
than not considering some of them.

On the other hand, we know that
P(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})=\ell
and
K(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})=K_{W}^{2}
by Proposition
3.6
, and the observation at the end of Definition
3.4
. We can isolate the terms
\sum_{k}kt_{k}
and
\sum_{k}t_{k}
from the formulas for
P(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})
and
K(Z^{\prime},D^{\prime})
, and evaluate them on the formulas for
\bar{c}_{1}^{2}(Z,D)
and
\bar{c}_{2}(Z,D)
to obtain the last three claims in the theorem. Here we are using that
b_{1}(Z)=0
, and Noether’s formula
12\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{Z})=K_{Z}^{2}+\chi_{\text{top}}(Z)
as well.

For the first claim, we apply the log Bogomolov-Miyaoka-Yau inequality as expressed by Langer in
[
L03
, Theorem 0.1]
. For that we need to have a log canonical pair
(X,D^{\prime\prime})
so that a multiple of
K_{X}+D^{\prime\prime}
is effective. We are taking as
D^{\prime\prime}
the (reduced) pull-back of
D
by
\sigma
. Hence
D^{\prime\prime}=\exc(\pi)+\sum_{i}G_{i}
where
G_{i}
are the exceptional curves of
\sigma
. We also know that
K_{X}\equiv\pi^{*}(K_{W})+\sum_{i}d_{i}C_{i}
, where
-1<d_{i}<0
,
C_{i}\in\exc(\pi)
, and by assumption
\pi^{*}(K_{W})
is big and nef, so it is a sum of an ample divisor plus some effective divisor. Therefore some multiple of
K_{X}+D^{\prime\prime}
is indeed effective.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

With the same hypothesis of Theorem
3.8
, assume in addition that the image of
\exc(\pi)
under
\sigma
has only nodes as singularities. Then

7K_{W}^{2}\leq 12+6b_{2}(Z)+5K_{Z}^{2}-5\ell-t_{2}.

Corollary 3.10
.

With the same hypothesis of Theorem
3.8
, assume in addition that
t_{k}^{0}=0
for all
k>2
and
K_{Z}^{2}=0
. Then

7K_{W}^{2}\leq 12+6b_{2}(Z)-5\ell-\sum_{k\geq 2}(k-1)t_{k}.

4.
Obstructions to complex smoothings

In this section, we analyze the potential complex deformations of a normal projective surface
W
with
\ell
Wahl singularities, and
K_{W}
big and nef based on
[
H04
,
H12
]
. As in the introduction, let
T_{W}:=\mathcal{H}\text{om}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{W}}(\Omega_{W}^{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{W})
where
\Omega_{W}^{1}
is the sheaf of differentials on
W
.

A deformation (proper, flat morphism)
(W\subset{\mathscr{W}})\to(0\in{\mathbb{D}})
over a nonsingular curve germ
{\mathbb{D}}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein if
K_{{\mathscr{W}}}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier. Hence, for each singularity we have either a locally trivial deformation, or a smoothing with Milnor number zero. It is called
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein smoothing if in addition
W_{t}
(the fiber over
t
) is nonsingular for
t\neq 0
. We assume that
W_{0}
is a reduced fiber isomorphic to
W
. We know that there is a versal deformation space
\Def_{QG}(W)
which parametrizes all
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein deformations of
W
. A main question for us is whether this space contains smoothings. For this, we want to study
{\mathbb{T}}_{QG,W}^{0}
, the vector space of infinitesimal automorphisms of
W
;
{\mathbb{T}}_{QG,W}^{1}
, the vector space of
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein first order deformations of
W
; and
{\mathbb{T}}_{QG,W}^{2}
, the vector space of obstructions for
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein deformations.

In general, it is a theorem that for proper schemes
W
over a field
k
, Aut
(W)
is a group scheme locally of finite type over
k
, and its tangent space at the identity is
H^{0}(W,T_{W})
. In our case, since
k={\mathbb{C}}
, Aut
(W)
is reduced, so if Aut
(W)
is finite, then
H^{0}(W,T_{W})=0
. But Aut
(W)
is indeed finite when
K_{W}
is big by a well-known theorem of Iitaka
[
Ii82
, Section 11.1 for definitions, Theorem 11.12 for the result]
. On the other hand, it is shown in
[
H04
, Lemma 3.8]
that
{\mathbb{T}}_{QG,W}^{0}=H^{0}(W,T_{W})
, as there is an isomorphism of sheaves
T_{W}\simeq\mathcal{T}_{QG,W}^{0}
. Therefore
{\mathbb{T}}_{QG,W}^{0}=0
.

For the analysis of the two other vector spaces, we first consider the minimal resolution of singularities
\pi\colon X\to W
. We have that
\pi_{*}T_{X}=T_{W}
(see
[
BW74
, Proposition 1.2]
). By the Leray spectral sequence of low-degree terms (see
[
NSW08
, Lemma (2.1.3)]
,
[
H12
, Section 3]
), we have the exact sequence

0\to H^{1}(W,T_{W})\to{\mathbb{T}}^{1}_{QG,W}\to H^{0}(W,\mathcal{T}^{1}_{QG,W})\to H^{2}(W,T_{W})\to{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{QG,W}\to 0.

This happens because
\mathcal{T}^{i}_{QG,W}
is supported on the isolated singularities of
W
for
i>0
, and
\mathcal{T}^{2}_{QG,W}=0
as the local canonical covers of
W
are complete intersections (see
[
H04
, p.227]
). The general set-up is described in
[
H12
, Section 3]
. The vector space
H^{0}(W,\mathcal{T}^{1}_{QG,W})
is the direct sum of the one-dimensional tangent spaces of the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein deformations of each Wahl singularity, i.e.
H^{0}(W,\mathcal{T}^{1}_{QG,W})={\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}
. If
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{QG,W}=0
, then
\Def_{QG}(W)
is nonsingular (this happens, for example, when
H^{2}(W,T_{W})=0
). The vector space
H^{1}(W,T_{W})
parametrizes equisingular deformations of
W
. These dimensions are related to the invariants of
W
as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
W
be a normal projective surface with
\ell
Wahl singularities, and
K_{W}
big. Then

\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}{\mathbb{T}}^{1}_{QG,W}=10\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{W})-2K_{W}^{2}+\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{QG,W},

and so
h^{2}(T_{W})=\ell+h^{1}(T_{W})+2K_{W}^{2}-10\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{W})
.

Proof.

Let us consider the minimal resolution
\pi\colon X\to W
with exceptional divisor
E=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}E_{i}
, where
E_{i}
is the exceptional Wahl chain over each Wahl singularity. In particular,
E
is a simple normal crossings divisor. We have the short exact sequence

0\to T_{X}(-\log E)\to T_{X}\to\bigoplus_{C\in E}N_{C/X}\to 0,

where
N_{C/X}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(C)
is the normal sheaf of
C
in
X
. As in
[
LP07
, Lemma 1]
, we have
R^{i}\pi_{*}T_{X}(-\log E)=0
for
i=1,2
, and we know that
\pi_{*}T_{X}=T_{W}
. This implies

h^{2}(T_{W})-h^{1}(T_{W})=\chi(W,T_{W})=\chi(X,T_{X}(-\log E)),

as
h^{0}(T_{W})=0
. We now use the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch theorem for the locally free sheaf
T_{X}(-\log E)
. Its exponential Chern character is

\text{ch}(T_{X}(-\log(E)))=2-(K_{X}+E)+\frac{1}{2}\big((K_{X}+E)^{2}-2\chi_{\text{top}}(X\setminus E)\big).

From this we compute
\chi(X,T_{X}(-\log(E)))=2K_{W}^{2}-10\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{W})+\ell
. The result follows from the Leray exact sequence above.
∎

If
\text{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}H^{2}(W,T_{W})=0
, then
\Def_{QG}(W)
is nonsingular and any local deformations of the singularities in
W
may be glued to obtain a global deformation of
W
. We say in this case that there are no
local-to-global
obstructions to deform
W
. For instance, if
K_{W}^{2}=5
and
p_{g}(W)=q(W)=0
, then we have
h^{2}(W,T_{W})>0
and
\text{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}{\mathbb{T}}^{1}_{{\text{QG}},W}=\text{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{{\text{QG}},W}
.

For the examples in the following sections, we will start with an elliptic fibration, and we will consider singular fibers and multi-sections as part of the configurations of rational curves. We also want to concretely compute
H^{2}(T_{W})
, which is the same as computing
H^{2}(X,T_{X}(-\log E))
as we just saw.

Let
Z\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{1}
be an elliptic fibration with sections, and
D
a configuration of rational curves
D
in
Z
. Consider a composition of blow-ups

\sigma\colon X=Z_{n}\to Z_{n-1}\to\ldots\to Z_{0}=Z,

as in the previous section, where each intermediate

is the blow-up at a single point. We successively construct a divisor
D_{i+1}
in
Z_{i+1}
with curves in the proper transform of
D_{i}
plus some exceptional curves. We will keep track of
H^{2}(Z_{i},T_{Z_{i}}(-\log D_{i}))
as we add or erase curves, applying the following principles. The dimension of
H^{2}(Z_{i+1},T_{Z_{i+1}}(-\log D_{i+1}))
is equal to the dimension of
H^{2}(Z_{i},T_{Z_{i}}(-\log D_{i}))
when
D_{i}
has simple normal crossings and
D_{i+1}
is the reduced pullback of
D_{i}
. Furthermore, the dimension of
H^{2}(Z_{i},T_{Z_{i}}(-\log D_{i}^{\prime}))
is equal to the dimension of
H^{2}(Z_{i},T_{Z_{i}}(-\log D_{i}))
if

(-1)

D_{i}^{\prime}
is obtained from
D_{i}
by adding or erasing
(-1)
-curves that are transversal to the rest of
D_{i}
. This applies to removing the
(-1)
-curve of the reduced pullback of
D_{i-1}
, if the blow-up was made at a node. (see e.g.
[
PSU13
, Proposition 4.2 and 4.3]
.

(-2)

D_{i}^{\prime}
is obtained from
D_{i}
by adding any Du Val configuration of
(-2)
-curves that are disjoint from
D_{i}

[
PSU13
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f\colon Z\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{1}
be an elliptic fibration with at least one section. Assume that it has
n
fibers
F_{i}
of type
I_{1}
and
m
fibers
G_{j}
of type
I_{k}
with
k>1
. (They may not be all the singular fibers of

.) Let
\sigma^{\prime}\colon Z^{\prime}\to Z
be the blow-up at the nodes of the
F_{i}
. Let
F_{i}
(resp.
G_{i}
) be the proper transform of
F_{i}
(resp.
G_{i}
), and let
H_{i}
be the exceptional curves of
\sigma^{\prime}
. Then

H^{2}\Big(Z^{\prime},T_{Z^{\prime}}\big(-\log(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(F_{i}+H_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}\big)\Big)\geq m+n-2+p_{g}(Z).

Proof.

This follows
[
LP07
, Proposition 7]
. We have
K_{Z^{\prime}}\sim(p_{g}(Z)-1)F+\sum_{i=1}^{n}H_{i}
, where

is a general fiber of
Z^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{1}
. Consider the inclusion

f^{*}(\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{1}}^{1}(n+m))\otimes K_{Z^{\prime}}\to\Omega_{Z^{\prime}}^{1}\big(\log(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(F_{i}+H_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}\big)\otimes K_{Z^{\prime}}.

The inequality follows by Serre duality.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

With the same hypothesis as in the previous lemma, let us assume that
p_{g}(Z)=0
. Then

H^{2}\Big(Z^{\prime},T_{Z^{\prime}}\big(-\log(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(F_{i}+H_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}\big)\Big)=m+n-2.

Proof.

First, by the
(-1)
principle, we have

H^{2}\big(Z^{\prime},T_{Z^{\prime}}\big(-\log(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(F_{i}+H_{i})\big)\big)=H^{2}\big(Z^{\prime},T_{Z^{\prime}}\big(-\log(\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}\big)\big).

We have the inclusions

H^{0}(\Omega_{Z^{\prime}}^{1}\big(\log\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}\big)\otimes K_{Z^{\prime}})\subset H^{0}(\Omega_{Z^{\prime}}^{1}\big(\log\sum_{i=2}^{n}F_{i}\big)\otimes K_{Z^{\prime}}+F_{1})\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \

\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \subset H^{0}(\Omega_{Z^{\prime}}^{1}\big(\log\sum_{i=2}^{n}F_{i}\big)\otimes\sum_{i=2}^{n}H_{i})\subset H^{0}(\Omega_{Z^{\prime}}^{1}\big(\log\sum_{i=2}^{n}F_{i}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}H_{i}\big)),

and by the long exact sequence in cohomology applied to the residue sequence

0\to\Omega_{Z^{\prime}}^{1}\to\Omega_{Z^{\prime}}^{1}\big(\log\sum_{i=2}^{n}F_{i}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}H_{i}\big)\to\bigoplus{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{i}}\oplus\bigoplus{\mathcal{O}}_{H_{i}}\to 0,

we obtain that
H^{0}(\Omega_{Z^{\prime}}^{1}\big(\log\sum_{i=2}^{n}F_{i}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}H_{i}\big))
is the kernel of the Chern map

H^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{i}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{H_{i}})\to H^{1}(\Omega_{Z^{\prime}}^{1}).

This kernel has dimension
n-2
, as we have
n-1
fibers with two components each.

By the (-2) principle, we can add the configurations
G^{\prime}_{i}
which are the
G_{i}
minus one
(-2)
-curve without altering the dimension, so

h^{0}(\Omega_{Z^{\prime}}^{1}\big(\log\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}G^{\prime}_{i}\big)\otimes K_{Z^{\prime}})\leq n-2,

and by the (-1) principle we can add the
H_{i}
, so

, this dimension is also greater than

∎

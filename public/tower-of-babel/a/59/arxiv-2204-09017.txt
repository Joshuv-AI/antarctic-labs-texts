Short time quaternion quadratic phase Fourier transform and its uncertainty principles
Bivek Gupta, Amit K. Verma

Abstract
In this paper, we extend the quadratic phase Fourier transform of a complex valued functions to that of the quaternion valued functions of two variables. We call it the quaternion quadratic phase Fourier transform (QQPFT). Based on the relation between the QQPFT and the quaternion Fourier transform (QFT) we obtain the sharp Hausdorff-Young inequality for QQPFT. We define the short time quaternion quadratic phase Fourier transform (STQQPFT) and explore some of its properties including inner product relation and inversion formula. We find its relation with that of the 2D quaternion ambiguity function and the quaternion Wigner-Ville distribution associated with QQPFT and obtain the Lieb’s uncertainty and entropy uncertainty principles for these three transforms.



Keywords
: Quaternion Quadratic Phase Fourier Transform; Short Time Quaternion Quadratic Phase Fourier Transform; Lieb’s Uncertainty Principle

3
Quaternion quadratic phase Fourier transform (QQPFT)

In this section we give a definition of quaternion quadratic phase Fourier transform (QQPFT) and study its important properties.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\wedge_{l}=(A_{l},B_{l},C_{l},D_{l},E_{l}),A_{l},B_{l},C_{l},D_{l},E_{l}\in\mathbb{R}~\mbox{and}~B_{l}\neq 0~\mbox{for}~l=1,2
. The quaternion quadratic phase Fourier transform (QQPFT) of
f(\boldsymbol{t})\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),~\boldsymbol{t}=(t_{1},t_{2}),
is defined by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f)(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(t_{1},\xi_{1})f(\boldsymbol{t})\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(t_{2},\xi_{2})d\boldsymbol{t},~\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

(11)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(t_{1},\xi_{1})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-i\left(A_{1}t_{1}^{2}+B_{1}t_{1}\xi_{1}+C_{1}\xi_{1}^{2}+D_{1}t_{1}+E_{1}\xi_{1}\right)}

(12)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{2}}(t_{2},\xi_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-j\left(A_{2}t_{2}^{2}+B_{2}t_{2}\xi_{2}+C_{2}\xi_{2}^{2}+D_{2}t_{2}+E_{2}\xi_{2}\right)}.

(13)

The corresponding inversion formula is given by

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{t})=|B_{1}B_{2}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(t_{1},\xi_{1})}(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\overline{\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{2}}(t_{2},\xi_{2})}d\boldsymbol{\xi}

(14)

3.1
Relation between QQPFT and QFT

We now see an important relation between the QQPFT and the QFT, which plays a vital role in obtaining the sharp Hausdorff-Young inequality for the QQPFT.

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f)(\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-i\left(A_{1}t_{1}^{2}+B_{1}t_{1}\xi_{1}+C_{1}\xi_{1}^{2}+D_{1}t_{1}+E_{1}\xi_{1}\right)}f(\boldsymbol{t})e^{-j\left(A_{2}t_{2}^{2}+B_{2}t_{2}\xi_{2}+C_{2}\xi_{2}^{2}+D_{2}t_{2}+E_{2}\xi_{2}\right)}d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle=e^{-i\left(C_{1}\xi^{2}+E_{1}\xi_{1}\right)}\left\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-iB_{1}t_{1}\xi_{1}}\tilde{f}(\boldsymbol{t})e^{-jB_{2}t_{2}\xi_{2}}d\boldsymbol{t}\right\}e^{-j\left(C_{2}\xi^{2}+E_{2}\xi_{2}\right)},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(\boldsymbol{t})=e^{-i\left(A_{1}t_{i}^{2}+D_{1}t_{1}\right)}f(\boldsymbol{t})e^{-j\left(A_{2}t_{i}^{2}+D_{2}t_{2}\right)}.

(15)

Thus,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f)(\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle=e^{-i\left(C_{1}\xi^{2}+E_{1}\xi_{1}\right)}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}}\tilde{f}\right)(B_{1}\xi_{1},B_{2}\xi_{2})e^{-j\left(C_{2}\xi^{2}+E_{2}\xi_{2}\right)}

(16)

where

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}}\tilde{f}\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-it_{1}\xi_{1}}\tilde{f}(\boldsymbol{t})\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-jt_{2}\xi_{2}}d\boldsymbol{t}.

(17)

Based on this relation between QQPFT and the QFT, we obtain the following important inequality.

Theorem 3.1
.

(Sharp Hausdorff-Young Inequality): Let
1\leq p\leq 2,

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),
then

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}A_{p}^{2}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

(18)

where
A_{p}=\left(\frac{p^{\frac{1}{p}}}{q^{\frac{1}{q}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Using the relation between the QQPFT and the QFT, we get

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}}\tilde{f}\right)(B_{1}\xi_{1},B_{2}\xi_{2})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)\frac{1}{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\|\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Using the sharp Hausdorff-Young inequality (
[
29
]
) for the QFT, we get

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}A_{p}^{2}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Substituting
\tilde{f},
from (
15
), we get (
18
). This completes the proof.
∎

.

(Parseval’s formula):
Let

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle=|B_{1}B_{2}|\langle\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f,\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}g\rangle.

This proof equation (

Definition 3.2
.

[

20
]

The Rènyi entropy of a probability density function

\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(P)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[P(\boldsymbol{t})]^{\alpha}d\boldsymbol{t}\right),~\alpha>0,\alpha\neq 1.

(21)

If

then (
21

\displaystyle E(P)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}P(\boldsymbol{t})\log[P(\boldsymbol{t})]d\boldsymbol{t}

Theorem 3.3
.

If
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<1
and
\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=2,
then

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(|f|^{2})+H_{\beta}\left(\left|\sqrt{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\right)\geq-\log(|B_{1}B_{2}|)-2\log(2\pi)-\left(\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log(2\alpha)+\frac{1}{1-\beta}\log(2\beta)\right).

Proof.

By Hausdorff-Young inequality (
18
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}A_{p}^{2}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(\boldsymbol{t})|^{p}d\boldsymbol{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Putting

and

in equation (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{|B_{1}B_{2}|}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\sqrt{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2\beta}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}\leq\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2\beta}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}A_{2\alpha}^{2}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(\boldsymbol{t})|^{2\alpha}d\boldsymbol{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}.

This implies

\displaystyle\frac{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{1}{\beta}-1}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\beta}}A^{4}_{2\alpha}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(\boldsymbol{t})|^{2\alpha}d\boldsymbol{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\sqrt{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2\beta}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}.

(24)

Since
\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=2,

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}=\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}.

(25)

Raising to the power

in (

\displaystyle\frac{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{-1}}{(2\pi)^{(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\beta})(\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha})}A^{\frac{4\alpha}{1-\alpha}}_{2\alpha}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(\boldsymbol{t})|^{2\alpha}d\boldsymbol{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\sqrt{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2\beta}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\beta}}.

Taking

on both sides, we get

\displaystyle-\log(|B_{1}B_{2}|)-\log

\displaystyle\left((2\pi)^{(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\beta})(\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha})}A^{\frac{4\alpha}{1-\alpha}}_{2\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(\boldsymbol{t})|^{2\alpha}d\boldsymbol{t}\right)+\frac{1}{1-\beta}\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\sqrt{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2\alpha}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right).

(26)

Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(|f|^{2})+H_{\beta}\left(\left|\sqrt{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\right)\geq-\log(|B_{1}B_{2}|)-2\log(2\pi)-\left(\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log(2\alpha)+\frac{1}{1-\beta}\log(2\beta)\right).

(27)

This is the Rènyi entropy UP for QQPFT.
∎

Remark 1
.

If

then
\beta\rightarrow 1
and in this case equation (
27
) can be written as

\displaystyle E(|f|^{2})+E\left(\left|\sqrt{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\right)\geq-\log(|B_{1}B_{2}|)-2\log(2\pi)+2-\log 4,

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07182pt\mbox{i.e.,}~E(|f|^{2})+E\left(\left|\sqrt{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\right)\geq\log\left(\frac{e^{2}}{16\pi^{2}|B_{1}B_{2}|}\right).

(28)

This is the Shannon entropy UP for QQPFT.

4
Short time quaternion quadratic phase Fourier transform

In this section we give the definition of the STQQPFT and study its properties. We obtain its relation with that of the quaternion AF and the quaternion WVD associated with the QQPFT.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\wedge_{l}=(A_{l},B_{l},C_{l},D_{l},E_{l}),A_{l},B_{l},C_{l},D_{l},E_{l}\in\mathbb{R}~\mbox{and}~B_{l}\neq 0~\mbox{for}~l=1,2
. The short time quaternion quadratic phase Fourier transform (STQQPFT) of a function
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with respect to a quaternion window function (QWF)
g\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is defined by

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(t_{1},\xi_{1})f(\boldsymbol{t})\overline{g(\boldsymbol{t}-\boldsymbol{x})}\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(t_{2},\xi_{2})d\boldsymbol{t},~(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(t_{1},\xi_{1})
and
\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(t_{2},\xi_{2})
are given by equations (
12
) and (
13
), respectively.

We now derive some of the basic properties of the STQQPFT. But before that we state the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{t}=(t_{1},t_{2}),\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},r\in\mathbb{R}.
Then the kernel
\mathcal{K}^{1}_{\wedge_{1}}(t_{1},\xi_{1})
and
\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(t_{2},\xi_{2})
satisfy the following

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(t_{1}+rk_{1},\xi_{1})=\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}\left(t_{1},\xi_{1}+\frac{2rk_{1}A_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)\phi^{i}_{\wedge_{1},r}(k_{1},\xi_{1}),

(29)

where

\displaystyle\phi^{i}_{\wedge_{1},r}(k_{1},\xi_{1})=e^{-i\left(A_{1}r^{2}k_{1}^{2}+D_{1}rk_{1}+B_{1}rk_{1}\xi_{1}-\frac{4r^{2}A_{1}^{2}C_{1}k_{1}^{2}}{B_{1}^{2}}-\frac{4rA_{1}C_{1}k_{1}\xi_{1}}{B_{1}}-\frac{2rA_{1}k_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)}

(30)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(t_{2}+rk_{2},\xi_{2})=\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}\left(t_{2},\xi_{2}+\frac{2rk_{2}A_{2}}{B_{2}}\right)\phi^{j}_{\wedge_{2},r}(k_{2},\xi_{2}),

(31)

where

\displaystyle\phi^{j}_{\wedge_{2},r}(k_{2},\xi_{2})=e^{-j\left(A_{2}r^{2}k_{2}^{2}+D_{2}rk_{2}+B_{2}rk_{2}\xi_{2}-\frac{4r^{2}A_{2}^{2}C_{2}k_{2}^{2}}{B_{2}^{2}}-\frac{4rA_{2}C_{2}k_{2}\xi_{2}}{B_{2}}-\frac{2rA_{2}k_{2}}{B_{2}}\right)}.

(32)

Proof.

From the definition of
\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}},
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(t_{1}+rk_{1},\xi_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-i\left\{A_{1}(t_{1}+rk_{1})^{2}+B_{1}(t_{1}+rk_{1})\xi_{1}+C_{1}\xi_{1}^{2}+D_{1}(t_{1}+rk_{1})+E_{1}\xi_{1}\right\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-i\left\{A_{1}t_{1}^{2}+B_{1}t_{1}\left(\xi_{1}+\frac{2rA_{1}k_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)+D_{1}t_{1}+C_{1}\xi_{1}^{2}+E_{1}\xi_{1}+B_{1}rk_{1}\xi_{1}\right\}}e^{-i(A_{1}r^{2}k_{1}^{2}+D_{1}rk_{1})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-i\left\{A_{1}t_{1}^{2}+B_{1}t_{1}\left(\xi_{1}+\frac{2rA_{1}k_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)+D_{1}t_{1}+C_{1}\left(\xi_{1}+\frac{2rA_{1}k_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)^{2}+E_{1}\left(\xi_{1}+\frac{2rA_{1}k_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)\right\}}\phi^{i}_{\wedge_{1},r}(k_{1},\xi_{1}),

\displaystyle\mbox{i.e.,}~\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(t_{1}+rk_{1},\xi_{1})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}\left(t_{1},\xi_{1}+\frac{2rk_{1}A_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)\phi^{i}_{\wedge_{1},r}(k_{1},\xi_{1}).

This proves equation (
29
). Similarly, equation (
31
) can be proved.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

(Inner product relation): If

be two QWFs and

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g_{1}}f_{1},\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g_{2}}f_{2}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\langle f_{1}(\overline{g_{1}},\overline{g_{2}}),f_{2}\rangle.

(36)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}d\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}\{f_{1}(\cdot)\overline{g_{1}(\cdot-\boldsymbol{x})}\}\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\}d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(\boldsymbol{t})\overline{g(\boldsymbol{t}-\boldsymbol{x})}|^{2}d\boldsymbol{t}\right\}d\boldsymbol{x},~\mbox{using Parseval's Identity}

\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g_{1}}f_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}).
Similarly,
\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g_{2}}f_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Now,

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g_{1}}f_{1},\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g_{2}}f_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=Sc\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}\{f_{1}(\cdot)\overline{g_{1}(\cdot-\boldsymbol{x})}\}\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\overline{\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}\{f_{2}(\cdot)\overline{g_{2}(\cdot-\boldsymbol{x})}\}\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})}d\boldsymbol{x}d\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}Sc\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f_{1}(\boldsymbol{t})\overline{g_{1}(\boldsymbol{t}-\boldsymbol{x})}~\overline{f_{2}(\boldsymbol{t})\overline{g_{2}(\boldsymbol{t}-\boldsymbol{x})}}d\boldsymbol{t}\right\}d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}Sc\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f_{1}(\boldsymbol{t})\left(\overline{g_{1}},\overline{g_{2}}\right)\overline{f_{2}(\boldsymbol{t})}d\boldsymbol{t}.

Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g_{1}}f_{1},\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g_{2}}f_{2}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\langle f_{1}\left(\overline{g_{1}},\overline{g_{2}}\right),f_{2}\rangle.

This finishes the proof.

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{A}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}\left(t_{1},\xi_{1}-\frac{A_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)\phi^{i}_{\wedge_{1},-\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1},\xi_{1})f(\mathbb{\boldsymbol{t}})\overline{g(\boldsymbol{t}-\boldsymbol{x})}\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}\left(t_{2},\xi_{2}-\frac{A_{2}x_{2}}{B_{2}}\right)\phi^{j}_{\wedge_{2},-\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2},\xi_{2})d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle=\phi^{i}_{\wedge_{1},-\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1},\xi_{1})\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}\left(t_{1},\xi_{1}-\frac{A_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)f(\mathbb{\boldsymbol{t}})\overline{g(\boldsymbol{t}-\boldsymbol{x})}\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}\left(t_{2},\xi_{2}-\frac{A_{2}x_{2}}{B_{2}}\right)d\boldsymbol{t}\right\}\phi^{j}_{\wedge_{2},-\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2},\xi_{2}).

This gives

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{A}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})=\phi^{i}_{\wedge_{1},-\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1},\xi_{1})\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}})\phi^{j}_{\wedge_{2},-\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2},\xi_{2}).

This completes the proof.

∎

Definition 4.3
.

The two-sided quaternion quadratic phase Wigner-Ville distribution (QQPWVD) of
f,g\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),
is defined by

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{W}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(t_{1},\xi_{1})f\left(\boldsymbol{x}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{t}\right)\overline{g\left(\boldsymbol{x}-\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{t}\right)}\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}\left(t_{2},\xi_{2}\right)d\boldsymbol{t},

where
\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(t_{1},\xi_{1})
and
\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(t_{2},\xi_{2})
are given by equations (
12
) and (
13
) respectively.

The following theorem gives the relation between the QQPWVD and the STQQPFT.

Theorem 4.6
.

If
g
be a QWF and
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),
then

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{W}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})=4\psi^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(x_{1},\xi_{1})\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1}^{\prime},\wedge_{2}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{H},\tilde{g}}f\right)(2\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}})\psi^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(x_{2},\xi_{2}),~\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}}=\left(\xi_{1}-\frac{4A_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}},\xi_{2}-\frac{4A_{2}x_{2}}{B_{2}}\right)

where
\wedge_{l}^{\prime}=(4A_{l},2B_{l},C_{l},2D_{l},E_{l}),~l=1,2,

\tilde{g}(\boldsymbol{t})=g(-\boldsymbol{t}),

\psi^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(x_{1},\xi_{1})=e^{-i\left(4A_{1}x_{1}^{2}-2B_{1}x_{1}\xi_{1}-2D_{1}x_{1}-\frac{16A_{1}^{2}C_{1}x_{1}^{2}}{B_{1}^{2}}+\frac{8A_{1}C_{1}x_{1}\xi_{1}}{B_{1}}+\frac{4A_{1}E_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)}

and

\psi^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(x_{2},\xi_{2})=e^{-j\left(4A_{2}x_{2}^{2}-2B_{2}x_{2}\xi_{2}-2D_{2}x_{2}-\frac{16A_{2}^{2}C_{2}x_{2}^{2}}{B_{2}^{2}}+\frac{8A_{2}C_{2}x_{2}\xi_{2}}{B_{2}}+\frac{4A_{2}E_{2}x_{2}}{B_{2}}\right)}.

Proof.

From the definition of
\mathcal{W}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g),
we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{W}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle=4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(2(t_{1}-x_{1}),\xi_{1})f(\boldsymbol{t})\overline{g(2\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{t})}\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(2(t_{2}-x_{2}),\xi_{2})d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle=4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(2(t_{1}-x_{1}),\xi_{1})f(\boldsymbol{t})\overline{\tilde{g}(\boldsymbol{t}-2\boldsymbol{x})}\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(2(t_{2}-x_{2}),\xi_{2})d\boldsymbol{t}.

(38)

Now from the definition of
\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}},
in equation (
12
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(2(t_{1}-x_{1}),\xi_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-i(4A_{1}t_{1}^{2}-8A_{1}x_{1}t_{1}+2B_{1}t_{1}\xi_{1}+2D_{1}t_{1}+E_{1}\xi_{1}+C_{1}\xi_{2})}e^{-i(4A_{1}x_{1}^{2}-2B_{1}x_{1}\xi_{1}-2D_{1}x_{1})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-i\left\{(4A_{1})t_{1}^{2}+2B_{1}\left(\xi_{1}-\frac{4A_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)+C_{1}\left(\xi_{1}-\frac{4A_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)^{2}+(2D_{1})t_{1}+E_{1}\left(\xi_{1}-\frac{4A_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)\left(\xi_{1}-\frac{4A_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)\right\}}\psi^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(x_{1},\xi_{1})

i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(2(t_{1}-x_{1}),\xi_{1})=\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}^{\prime}}\left(t_{1},\xi_{1}-\frac{4A_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)\psi^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(x_{1},\xi_{1}).

(39)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(2(t_{2}-x_{2}),\xi_{2})=\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}^{\prime}}\left(t_{2},\xi_{2}-\frac{4A_{2}x_{2}}{B_{2}}\right)\psi^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(x_{2},\xi_{2}).

(40)

Using equations (
39
) and (
40
) in (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{W}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle=4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}^{\prime}}\left(t_{1},\xi_{1}-\frac{4A_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)\psi^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(x_{1},\xi_{1})f(\boldsymbol{t})\overline{\tilde{g}(\boldsymbol{t}-2\boldsymbol{x})}\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}^{\prime}}\left(t_{2},\xi_{2}-\frac{4A_{2}x_{2}}{B_{2}}\right)\psi^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(x_{2},\xi_{2})d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle=4\psi^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(x_{1},\xi_{1})\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\wedge_{1}^{\prime}}\left(t_{1},\xi_{1}-\frac{4A_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}}\right)f(\boldsymbol{t})\overline{\tilde{g}(\boldsymbol{t}-2\boldsymbol{x})}\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\wedge_{2}^{\prime}}\left(t_{2},\xi_{2}-\frac{4A_{2}x_{2}}{B_{2}}\right)d\boldsymbol{t}\right\}\psi^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(x_{2},\xi_{2}).

This gives

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{W}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})=4\psi^{i}_{\wedge_{1}}(x_{1},\xi_{1})\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1}^{\prime},\wedge_{2}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{H},\tilde{g}}f\right)(2\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}})\psi^{j}_{\wedge_{2}}(x_{2},\xi_{2}).

This completes the proof.
∎

4.2
Uncertainty principle for STQQPFT

The Heisenberg’s UP gives the information about a function and its FT, it says that the function cannot be highly localized in both time and frequency domain. Wilczok (
[
39
]
) introduced a new class of UP that compares the localization of a functions with the localization of its wavelet transform, analogous to the Heisenberg UP governing the localization of the complex valued function and the corresponding FT. Gupta et al.
[
21
]
obtained the Lieb’s and Donoho-Stark’s UP for the linear canonical wavelet transform and obtained the lower bound of the measure of its essential support.

Here, we prove the Lieb’s UP for the STQQPFT, QQPWVD and QQPAF. Analogous result for the classical STFT and the windowed linear canonical transform can be found in
[
19
]
and
[
26
]
respectively. Before we move forward, let us first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

(Lieb’s inequality)
Let
g
be a QWF,
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
2\leq q<\infty.
Then

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(41)

Proof.

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}\{f(\cdot)\overline{g(\cdot-\boldsymbol{x})}\}\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

(42)

Using Hausdorff-Young inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A^{2}_{p}(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|f(\boldsymbol{t})\overline{g(\boldsymbol{t}-\boldsymbol{x})}\right|^{p}d\boldsymbol{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{2}_{p}(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(\boldsymbol{t})|^{p}|\tilde{g}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{t})|^{p}d\boldsymbol{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},~\tilde{g}(\boldsymbol{t})=g(-\boldsymbol{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{2}_{p}(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\left\{\left(|f|^{p}\star|\tilde{g}|^{p}\right)(\boldsymbol{x})\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{x}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\leq\frac{A^{2q}_{p}(2\pi)^{q(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\{\left(|f|^{p}\star|\tilde{g}|^{p}\right)(\boldsymbol{x})\right\}^{\frac{q}{p}}d\boldsymbol{x}.

This gives

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{x}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A^{2}_{p}(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\{\left(|f|^{p}\star|\tilde{g}|^{p}\right)(\boldsymbol{x})\right\}^{\frac{q}{p}}d\boldsymbol{x}\right]^{\frac{q}{p}\cdot\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{2}_{p}(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\left\||f|^{p}\star|\tilde{g}|^{p}\right\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{L^{\frac{q}{p}}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(43)

Now we see that, if
k=\frac{2}{p},~l=\frac{q}{p},
then
k\geq 1
and
\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{k}=1+\frac{1}{l}.

Since
|f|^{p},~|\tilde{g}|^{p}\in L^{k}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),
we get, by Young’s inequality

\displaystyle\left\||f|^{p}\star|\tilde{g}|^{p}\right\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{L^{\frac{q}{p}}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq A_{k}^{4}A_{l^{\prime}}^{2}\|f\|^{p}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\tilde{g}\|^{p}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(44)

Therefore, from equations (
4.2
) and (
44
), it follows that

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{x}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}A_{p}^{2}A_{k}^{\frac{4}{p}}A_{l^{\prime}}^{\frac{2}{p}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

(45)

where
A_{r}=\left(\frac{r^{\frac{1}{r}}}{r^{\prime\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},~\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}=1.

Now, we have

\displaystyle A_{p}^{2}A_{k}^{\frac{4}{p}}A_{l^{\prime}}^{\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{\frac{1}{p}}}{q\frac{1}{q}}\cdot\frac{k}{{k^{\prime}}^{\frac{2}{k^{\prime}p}}}\cdot\frac{{l^{\prime}}^{\frac{1}{pl^{\prime}}}}{\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}},~\mbox{since}~k=\frac{2}{q},~l=\frac{q}{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{q^{\frac{2}{q}}}\cdot\frac{{l^{\prime}}^{\frac{1}{pl^{\prime}}}}{{k^{\prime}}^{\frac{2}{k^{\prime}p}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{q^{\frac{2}{q}}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{q-p}{pq}},~\mbox{since}~k^{\prime}=2l^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}.

(46)

Thus using equation (
4.2
) in (
45
), we get

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{x}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

4.3
Lieb’s uncertainty principle

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\epsilon\geq 0
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be measurable. A function
F\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be
\epsilon-
concentrated on
\Omega
if

\|\chi_{\Omega^{c}}F\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\epsilon\|F\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
\chi_{\Omega}
denotes the indicator function on
\Omega.

If
0\leq\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{2},
then majority of the energy is concentrated on
\Omega
and
\Omega
is said to be the essential support of
F.
Support of
F
is contained in
\Omega,
if
\epsilon=0.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
g
be a QWF and
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),
such that
f\neq 0.
Let
\epsilon\geq 0
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a measurable set. If
\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f,
on
\Omega,
is
\epsilon-
concentrated, then for every
q>2

\displaystyle|\Omega|\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}(1-\epsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\frac{4}{q-2}}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f
is
\epsilon-
concentrated on
\Omega,
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\Omega^{c}}\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

This implies

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\Omega}\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\geq\frac{1}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}(1-\epsilon^{2})\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(47)

Now, using Holder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\Omega}\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\chi_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right)^{\frac{q}{q-2}}d\boldsymbol{x}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\}^{\frac{q}{2}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-2}}d\boldsymbol{x}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\}^{\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle=|\Omega|^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\left\|\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Using, the Lieb’s inequality (
41
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\Omega}\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq|\Omega|^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{2}{q}-\frac{2}{p}}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{2}{q}}}\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(48)

From equation (
47
) and equation (
48
), we get

\displaystyle|\Omega|^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{2}{q}-\frac{2}{p}}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|^{\frac{2}{q}}}\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\geq\frac{1}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}(1-\epsilon^{2}).

This gives

\displaystyle|\Omega|\geq\frac{1}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}(2\pi)^{2(1-\frac{2}{q})\frac{q}{q-2}}(1-\epsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\frac{4}{q-2}},~\mbox{since}~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1

\displaystyle\mbox{i.e.,}~|\Omega|\geq\frac{1}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}(2\pi)^{2}(1-\epsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\frac{4}{q-2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3
.

Taking
\epsilon=0,
in the above theorem, we get the following lower bound for the support of
\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f

\displaystyle\left|\rm{supp}\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)\right|\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\lim_{q\rightarrow 2+}\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\frac{4}{q-2}}

\displaystyle\mbox{i.e.,}~\left|\rm{supp}\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)\right|\geq\frac{(2\pi e)^{2}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}.

(49)

i.e., measure of the support of
\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\geq\frac{(2\pi e)^{2}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}.

Corollary 4.8.1
.

Let
g
be a QWF and
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),
such that
f\neq 0.
Let
\epsilon\geq 0
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
is measurable. If
\mathcal{A}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g),
on
\Omega,
is
\epsilon-
concentrated, then for every
q>2

\displaystyle|\Omega|\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}(1-\epsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\frac{4}{q-2}}.

(50)

In particular, if
\epsilon=0,
then

\displaystyle\left|\rm{supp}\left(\mathcal{A}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)\right)\right|\geq\frac{(2\pi e)^{2}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}.

(51)

Proof.

From theorem
4.5
, it follows that

\left|\left(\mathcal{A}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|=\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}})\right|,~\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}}=\left(\xi_{1}-\frac{A_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}},\xi_{2}-\frac{A_{2}x_{2}}{B_{2}}\right).

Since
\mathcal{A}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)
is
\epsilon-
concentrated on
\Omega,
it can be shown that
\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f
is
\epsilon-
concentrated on
P^{-1}\Omega,
where
P
is the non-singular matrix given by

\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&0\\
0&1&0&0\\
\frac{A_{1}}{B_{1}}&0&1&0\\
0&\frac{A_{2}}{B_{2}}&0&1\end{bmatrix}

and
P^{-1}\Omega=\{P^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega\}.
So, by theorem
4.8
, we have

\displaystyle|P^{-1}\Omega|\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}(1-\epsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\left(\frac{4}{q-2}\right)}.

This gives

\displaystyle|\Omega|\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}(1-\epsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\left(\frac{4}{q-2}\right)},~\mbox{since}~det(P^{-1})=1.

This proves equation (
50
).
∎

Corollary 4.8.2
.

Let
g
be a QWF and
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),
such that
f\neq 0.
Let
\epsilon\geq 0
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}

\mathcal{W}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g),
on
\Omega,
is
\epsilon-

\displaystyle|\Omega|\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{16|B_{1}B_{2}|}(1-\epsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\frac{4}{q-2}}.

(52)

In particular, if
\epsilon=0,
then

\displaystyle\left|\rm{supp}\left(\mathcal{W}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)\right)\right|\geq\frac{(\pi e)^{2}}{4|B_{1}B_{2}|}.

(53)

Proof.

From theorem
4.6
, it follows that

\left|\left(\mathcal{W}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|=4\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1}^{\prime},\wedge_{2}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{H},\tilde{g}}f\right)(2\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}})\right|,~\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}}=\left(\xi_{1}-\frac{4A_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}},\xi_{2}-\frac{4A_{2}x_{2}}{B_{2}}\right).

Since
\mathcal{W}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)
is
\epsilon-
concentrated on
\Omega,
it can be shown that
\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1}^{\prime},\wedge_{2}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f
is
\epsilon-
concentrated on
P^{-1}\Omega,
where
P
is the non-singular matrix given by

\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2}&0&0&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}&0&0\\
\frac{4A_{1}}{B_{1}}&0&1&0\\
0&\frac{4A_{2}}{B_{2}}&0&1\end{bmatrix}
. So, by theorem
4.8
, we have

\displaystyle|P^{-1}\Omega|\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{4|B_{1}B_{2}|}(1-\epsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\left(\frac{4}{q-2}\right)}.

This gives

\displaystyle|\Omega|\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{16|B_{1}B_{2}|}(1-\epsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\left(\frac{4}{q-2}\right)},~\mbox{since}~det(P^{-1})=4.

This proves equation (
52
).
∎

4.4
Entropy uncertainty principle

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
g
be a QWF such that
\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=1,
then

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{S}(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2})\geq\frac{2}{|B_{1}B_{2}|},

(54)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{S}(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2})=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\log\left(\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\right)d\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{d}\xi.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle I(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2},q)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{x}d\boldsymbol{\xi}.

(55)

Then using (
55
) in (
37
), we get

\displaystyle I(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2},2)=\frac{1}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}.

(56)

Also, from (
41
) and (
56
), it can be shown that

\displaystyle I(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2},q)\leq\frac{(2\pi)^{2-q}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{2}.

(57)

Define, for
\lambda>0,

\displaystyle R(\lambda)=\frac{I(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2},2)-I(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2},2+2\lambda).}{\lambda}

Then

\displaystyle R(\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}\left\{\frac{1}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}-\frac{(2\pi)^{-2\lambda}}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}\left(\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\right)^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{\lambda|B_{1}B_{2}|}\left\{1-\frac{1}{(1+\lambda^{2})}\right\}

i.e.,

\displaystyle R(\lambda)>\frac{2+\lambda}{|B_{1}B_{2}|(1+\lambda)^{2}}.

(58)

Assume that
\mathcal{E}_{S}(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2})<\infty,
otherwise (
63
) is obvious.

Now using the inequality
1+\lambda\log a\leq a^{\lambda},~\lambda>0,
we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\left(1-\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2\lambda}\right)\leq-\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\log\left(\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\right).

(59)

Since,
-\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\log\left(\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\right)
is integrable, in view of equation (
59
), using Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}R(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}\left\{\frac{1}{\lambda}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\left(1-\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2\lambda}\right)\right\}d\boldsymbol{x}d\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{S}(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}).

(60)

Again from (
58
), we get

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0+}R(\lambda)\geq\frac{2}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}.

(61)

Thus from (
4.4
) and (
61
), we have equation (
63
). This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.9.1
.

Let
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
g
be a QWF such that
\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=1,
then

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{A}(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2})\geq\frac{2}{|B_{1}B_{2}|},

(62)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{A}(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2})=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{H}}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\log\left(\left|\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{H}}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\right)d\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{d}\xi.

Proof.

From theorem
4.5
, it follows that

\left|\left(\mathcal{A}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|=\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}})\right|,~\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}}=\left(\xi_{1}-\frac{A_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}},\xi_{2}-\frac{A_{2}x_{2}}{B_{2}}\right).

So, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{A}(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2})

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}})\right|^{2}\log\left(\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}})\right|^{2}\right)d\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{S}(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}).

Thus using theorem
4.9
, we have equation (
62
).
∎

Corollary 4.9.2
.

Let
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
g
be a QWF such that
\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=1.
Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{W}(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2})\geq\frac{2-\log 16}{|B_{1}B_{2}|},

(63)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{W}(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2})=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{W}_{\mathbb{H}}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\log\left(\left|\left(\mathcal{W}_{\mathbb{H}}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\right)d\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{d}\xi.

Proof.

From theorem
4.6
, it follows that

\left|\left(\mathcal{W}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H}}(f,g)\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|=4\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1}^{\prime},\wedge_{2}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{H},\tilde{g}}f\right)(2\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}})\right|,~\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}}=\left(\xi_{1}-\frac{4A_{1}x_{1}}{B_{1}},\xi_{2}-\frac{4A_{2}x_{2}}{B_{2}}\right).

So, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{W}(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2})

\displaystyle=-16\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1}^{\prime},\wedge_{2}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{H},\tilde{g}}f\right)(2\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}})\right|^{2}\log\left(16\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1}^{\prime},\wedge_{2}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{H},\tilde{g}}f\right)(2\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{x}})\right|^{2}\right)d\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{d}\xi

\displaystyle=-4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1}^{\prime},\wedge_{2}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{H},\tilde{g}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\log\left(16\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1}^{\prime},\wedge_{2}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{H},\tilde{g}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\right)d\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{d}\xi

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\log 16}{|4B_{1}B_{2}|}-4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1}^{\prime},\wedge_{2}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{H},\tilde{g}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\log\left(\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1}^{\prime},\wedge_{2}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{H},\tilde{g}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\right)d\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{d}\xi

\displaystyle=-\frac{\log 16}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}+4\mathcal{E}_{S}(f,\tilde{g},\wedge_{1}^{\prime},\wedge_{2}^{\prime}).

Therefore, using theorem
4.9
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{W}(f,g,\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2})\geq\frac{2-\log 16}{|B_{1}B_{2}|}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

5
Conclusions

In this article, we have studied the Parseval’s identity, sharp Hausdorff-Young inequality for the two sided QQPFT of quaternion valued functions. Based on the sharp Hausdorff-Young inequality we have obtained the sharper Rènyi entropy UP for the propose QPFT of quaternion valued functions. We have extended the STQPFT of complex valued functions to the functions of quaternion valued and studied the properties like boundedness, linearity, translation and scaling. We have also obtained the inner product relation and inversion formula for the proposed two sided STQQPFT. We have also obtained the relations of STQQPFT with that of the QQPAF and the QQPWVD of the quaternion valued function associated with the QQPFT. We have obtained the sharper version of the Lieb’s and entropy UPs for all these three transform based on the sharp Hausdorff-Young inequality for the QQPFT.

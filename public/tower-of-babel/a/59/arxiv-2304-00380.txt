SN1987A cooling due to Plasmon-Plasmon scattering in the Randall-Sundrum Model


Abstract
The light braneworld radion, stabilized via the Goldberger-Wise mechanism in the Randall-Sundrum model, can be produced copiously inside the supernova core due to plasmon-plasmon annihilations. The radion, thus produced, subsequently decays to a neutrino-antineutrino pair and takes away the energy released in the SN1987A explosion. In the scenario with q = 1 q=1 , we find ⟨ ϕ ⟩ = 178 \langle\phi\rangle=178 GeV for m ϕ = 30 m_{\phi}=30 MeV. Keywords Randall-Sundrum model, Radion, Supernovae cooling, Tsallis statistics

5
Conclusion

We have studied the impact of a light-stabilized Randall-Sundrum radion in the supernova SN1987A cooling. The radion produced due to plasmon-plasmon collisions in the outer crust of the supernova core can decay into a pair of neutrinos which take away energy released in the supernova SN1987A explosion. Assuming that the energy loss rate
\dot{\varepsilon}(\gamma_{P}\gamma_{P}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\nu_{\tau}\overline{\nu}_{\tau})\leq 7.288\times 10^{-27}\rm{GeV}
, we obtain a lower bound on the radion vev
\langle\phi\rangle
which depends on the radion mass
m_{\phi}
and the deformation parameter
q
. We find the lower bound on
\langle\phi\rangle\sim 197
GeV,
200
GeV,
671
GeV,
3221
GeV and
27462
GeV for
m_{\phi}=50
MeV corresponding to
q=1.05,\;1.10,\;1.15,\;1.17
and
1.2
, respectively. In the
q=1
scenario (undeformed), we find the lower bound on
\langle\phi\rangle=197(407)
GeV corresponding to
m_{\phi}=50(100)
MeV.

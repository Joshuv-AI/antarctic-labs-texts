Randomized quasi-Monte Carlo and Owen’s boundary growth condition: A spectral analysis
Yang Liu

Abstract
. In this work, we analyze the convergence rate of randomized quasi-Monte Carlo (RQMC) methods under Owen’s boundary growth condition [Owen, 2006] via spectral analysis. Specifically, we examine the RQMC estimator variance for the two commonly studied sequences: the lattice rule and the Sobol’ sequence. We apply the Fourier transform and Walsh–Fourier transform, respectively. Assuming certain regularity conditions, our findings reveal that the asymptotic convergence rate of the RQMC estimator’s variance closely aligns with the exponent specified in Owen’s boundary growth condition for both sequence types. We also provide analysis for certain discontinuous integrands.

1.
Introduction

The quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) method utilizes deterministic low-discrepancy sequences (LDS) to compute integrals. The Koksma–Hlawka inequality indicates that, for integrands whose variation in the Hardy–Krause sense is finite, the QMC integration error is bounded by the product of the Hardy–Krause variation and the star-discrepancy of the point set. However, the Koksma–Hlawka inequality fails to be informative for certain singular integrands in
L^{2}
. For functions with boundary unboundedness,
[
28
]
characterizes a boundary growth condition and establishes connections with the asymptotic QMC convergence rates. Meanwhile,
[
15
]
find that problem-specific lattice rules can achieve dimension-independent
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
worst-case convergence rate. Additionally,
[
10
]
study the convergence of randomly scrambled Sobol’ sequence for integrands with discontinuities.

The randomized QMC (RQMC) achieves unbiased mean estimators by randomizing the LDSs. The variance of the RQMC estimator has been explored in the previous literature.
[
16
]
investigate the randomly-shifted lattice rule (RSLR) using the Fourier transform.
[
24
]
analyzes Sobol’ sequences through Haar wavelets. An equivalent formulation using the Walsh–Fourier transform is revealed in Chapter 13 of
[
5
]
. In this work, we conduct a spectral analysis of the RQMC estimator variance for both lattice rules and Sobol’ sequences concerning certain types of singular integrands. Assuming these integrands exhibit boundary unboundedness, interior discontinuities, or both, we leverage Fourier and Walsh–Fourier transforms to investigate their spectral properties.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
provides background information and notations related to the RQMC. Section
3
analyzes the RSLR and scrambled Sobol’ sequences through spectral analysis. Section
4
further analyzes Sobol’ sequences for discontinuous integrands. Section
5
presents numerical results. Finally, section
6
concludes the paper and discusses possible further directions.

2.2.
Lattice rule

In this work, we specifically discuss the rank-1 lattice rule. Given a generating vector
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
, the lattice rule with
N

(1)

\left\{i\frac{\bm{z}}{N}\ \textrm{mod}\ 1:i=0,1,\dotsc,N-1\right\}.

The lattice rule can be extensible, meaning that when
N

Extensible lattice rules are often addressed to as “lattice sequences”. Analysis of lattice sequences can be found, for instance, in
[
12
]
.

The choice of the generating vector
\bm{z}
influences the integration error of the lattice rule. Approaches for optimizing the generating vector are discussed in works such as
[
14
,
2
]
and more recently in
[
18
]
.

2.3.
Sobol’ sequence

We first introduce the definition of a
(t,m,s)
-net in base

(t,m,s)

E_{\bm{\ell},\bm{k}}=\prod_{j=1}^{s}\left[\frac{{k}_{j}}{b^{{\ell}_{j}}},\frac{{k}_{j}+1}{b^{{\ell}_{j}}}\right),

The Sobol’ sequence is an example of a

Regarding randomization techniques,

2.4.
Owen’s boundary growth condition

[
28
]
proposes the boundary growth condition to characterize the unbounded integrand
f
on
[0,1]^{s}
, which is given as follows.

Assumption 1

(Owen’s boundary growth condition
[
28
]
)
.

Let
f\in L^{2}([0,1]^{s})
, and the derivative
\frac{\partial^{\mathfrak{u}}f}{\partial\bm{t}_{\mathfrak{u}}}
exists on
(0,1)^{s}
for
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1:s
. Then the boundary growth condition is given by

(3)

\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{\mathfrak{u}}f}{\partial\bm{t}_{\mathfrak{u}}}\right\rvert\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{s}\phi(t_{j})^{-\mathbbm{1}_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}-A_{j}},

for all
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq\{1,\dotsc,s\}
,
\max_{j}A_{j}<1/2
and
\phi:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
,
\phi(t)\coloneqq\min(t,1-t)
.

[
28
]
proves that for integrands satisfying the condition (
3
), the
L^{1}
convergence rate of RQMC using low-discrepancy sequences is
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+\epsilon+A^{*}})
for
\epsilon>0
and
A^{*}=\max_{j}A_{j}
as defined in Section
2.1
.
[
11
]
and
[
6
]
have derived the RQMC
L^{2}
convergence rate
\mathcal{O}(n^{-1+\epsilon+A^{*}})
for the scrambled Sobol’ sequence. The former applies the Koksma–Hlawka inequality, while the latter establishes results based on the pairwise correlations on randomized QMC quadrature points.

In this work, we investigate the
L^{2}
convergence rates of the randomized lattice rule and Sobol’ sequence. In the next section, we perform a spectral analysis of these two low-discrepancy sequences under the boundary growth condition assumptions.

3.
Spectral Analysis of RQMC and Owen’s boundary growth condition

In this section, we explore the variance of the RSLR and scrambled Sobol’ sequence using the Fourier and Walsh–Fourier transformations, respectively.

3.1.
Lattice Rule

We consider the integrand to be periodic. Let
f:\mathbb{T}^{s}=[0,1]^{s}\to\mathbb{R}
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{s})
.
Consider lattice points
\{\bm{t}_{i}\in[0,1]^{s}:i=0,\dotsc,N-1\}
given by (
1
) with a generating vector
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
and a random shift
\bm{\Delta}\sim\mathcal{U}([0,1]^{s})
. The RSLR estimator
I_{N}^{\textrm{lat}}\coloneqq I_{N}^{\textrm{lat}}(f)
is then defined as

(4)

\displaystyle I_{N}^{\textrm{lat}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}f({\bm{t}_{i}+\bm{\Delta}}).

In the following, we introduce a lemma about the variance of
I_{N}^{\textrm{lat}}(f)
.

Lemma 1

(Variance of RSLR estimator
[
16
]
)
.

The variance of the RSLR integration estimator,
{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{lat}}\right]}
, is given by

(5)

{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{lat}}\right]}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s},\bm{n}\cdot\bm{z}\equiv 0(\textrm{mod }N),\\
\bm{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left\lvert c_{\bm{n}}\right\rvert^{2},

where
c_{\bm{n}}
are the Fourier coefficients of
f
, given by

c_{\bm{n}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{s}}f(\bm{t})e^{-i2\pi\bm{n}\cdot\bm{t}}d\bm{t},

and the set
\{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}:\bm{n}\cdot\bm{z}\equiv 0\mod N\}
is known as the dual lattice corresponding to the
N
-point rank-1 lattice rule generated by
\bm{z}
. To summarize: the variance of the RSLR estimator is the summation of the squared magnitudes of Fourier coefficients of the integrand over the dual lattice except the origin.

Remark 1

(Monte Carlo estimator variance)
.

Let
I_{N}
denote the usual Monte Carlo estimator. Then the variance of
I_{N}
is given by

(6)

{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}\right]}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s},\\
\bm{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left\lvert c_{\bm{n}}\right\rvert^{2}.

The variance of the Monte Carlo estimator depends on the sum of the squared magnitudes of the Fourier coefficients of the integrand at all non-zero frequencies. For instance, the reader can refer to
[
31
]
for derivations.

Remark 2

(Fourier coefficients and integration error)
.

The Fourier coefficients play an important role in the integration error of deterministic lattice rules. Specifically,
consider a lattice rule
\{\bm{t}_{i}\in[0,1]^{s}:i=0,\dotsc,N-1\}

\bm{z}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}

(7)

{I-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}f(\bm{t}_{i})}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s},\bm{n}\cdot\bm{z}\equiv 0(\textrm{mod }N),\\
\bm{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}c_{\bm{n}},

where

[

5

4
,

There exists a generating vector

{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{lat}}\right]}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-2\alpha}(\log N)^{2\alpha s}).

Lemma 3
.

Consider a one-dimensional function
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
, whose derivative

\lvert c_{n}\rvert\lesssim\begin{cases}n^{-1+A},&\text{if}\ 0<A<1/2,\\

The proof, following a classical approach to the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, is provided to Appendix
A

When
{A}<0
, we only achieve convergence at the rate of
\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})
by following the derivations in Appendix
A

Assumption 2
.

Let
\bar{A}<1
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Consider a 1-d function
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
. We assume the following conditions:

(1)

The
(k-1)
-th derivative,
\frac{\partial^{k-1}f}{\partial t^{k-1}}

\mathbb{T}=[0,1]
.

(2)

The

\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}
is piecewise continuous on
\mathbb{T}
with a finite number of discontinuities.

(3)

The
(k+1)
-th derivative
\frac{\partial^{k+1}f}{\partial t^{k+1}}
satisfies the boundary growth condition (
3

\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{k+1}f}{\partial t^{k+1}}\right\rvert\lesssim\phi(t)^{-1-\bar{A}}
.

When
k=0
, we define

\frac{\partial^{-1}f}{\partial t^{-1}}(t_{0})\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t_{0}}f(t)dt.

Lemma 4
.

For a function
f
satisfying Assumption
2
, the Fourier coefficients
c_{n}
satisfy the following estimates:

(12)

\lvert c_{n}\rvert\lesssim\begin{cases}n^{-1-k},&\text{if}\ \bar{A}<0,\\
n^{-1-k}\log n,&\text{if}\ \bar{A}=0,\\
n^{-1-k+\bar{A}},&\text{if}\ 0<\bar{A}<1.\end{cases}

Proof.

We start with the integration by parts.

(13)

\begin{split}c_{n}\cdot(-i2\pi n)^{k}&=\int_{0}^{1}(-i2\pi n)^{k}f(t)e^{-i2\pi nt}dt\\
&=-\int_{0}^{1}(-i2\pi n)^{k-1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}e^{-i2\pi nt}dt+\underbrace{(-i2\pi n)^{k-1}f(t)e^{-i2\pi nt}\mid_{0}^{1}}_{=0}\\
&\ \vdots\\
&=(-1)^{k}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}e^{-i2\pi nt}dt+\underbrace{\frac{\partial^{(k-1)}f}{\partial t^{k-1}}e^{-i2\pi nt}\mid_{0}^{1}}_{=0},\end{split}

where we have used the continuity of
f
,
\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}
,
\dotsc
,
\frac{\partial^{(k-1)}f}{\partial t^{k-1}}
on
\mathbb{T}
. Notice that we can use the same shifting technique as in the proof of Lemma
3
. We have

(14)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t)e^{-i2\pi nt}dt=-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}\left(t+\frac{1}{2n}\right)e^{-i2\pi nt}dt,

and

(15)

c_{n}\cdot(-i2\pi n)^{k}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t)-\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}\left(t+\frac{1}{2n}\right)\right)e^{-i2\pi nt}dt.

We derive the following upper bound for
\lvert c_{n}\rvert
:

(16)

\lvert c_{n}\rvert\lesssim{n^{-k}}\int_{0}^{1}\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t)-\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}\left(t+\frac{1}{2n}\right)\right\rvert dt.

Without loss of generality, assume that the piecewise continuous function
\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}
has finitely many discontinuities at points
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{d}
where
d<+\infty
and
0\leq x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{d}\leq 1
.

We denote the set
\Omega_{d}=\{{t}\in[0,1]:\exists i=1,\dotsc,d,x_{i}\in[t,t+\frac{1}{2n}]\}
, i.e. the set of
t\in[0,1]
such that the interval
[t,t+\frac{1}{2n}]
contains at least one discontinuity point.
When
t\in\Omega_{d}
and
\bar{A}>0
, we have

(17)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{d}}\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t)-\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}\left(t+\frac{1}{2n}\right)\right\rvert dt&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{t_{i}\in[t,t+\frac{1}{2n}]}\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t)\right\rvert+\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t+\frac{1}{2n})\right\rvert dt\\
&\leq 2d\int_{t_{i}\in[t,t+\frac{1}{2n}]}\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t)\right\rvert+\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t+\frac{1}{2n})\right\rvert dt\\
&\lesssim\int_{[0,\frac{1}{2n}]}\phi(t)^{-\bar{A}}dt.\end{split}

Notice that

(18)

\int_{[0,\frac{1}{2n}]}\phi(t)^{-\bar{A}}dt\lesssim\begin{cases}n^{-1+\bar{A}},&\text{if}\ 0<\bar{A}<1,\\
n^{-1}\log(n),&\text{if}\ \bar{A}=0.\end{cases}

Notice that when
\bar{A}<0
, the function
\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}
is bounded. Thus, when
t\in\Omega_{d}
and
\bar{A}<0
, we have

(19)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{d}}\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t)-\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}\left(t+\frac{1}{2n}\right)\right\rvert dt&\lesssim 2d\int_{t_{i}\in[t,t+\frac{1}{2n}]}\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t)\right\rvert+\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t+\frac{1}{2n})\right\rvert dt\lesssim n^{-1}.\end{split}

When

, following the proof in Appendix

, we have,

\int_{[0,1]\setminus\Omega_{d}}\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t)-\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}\left(t+\frac{1}{2n}\right)\right\rvert dt\leq\int_{[0,1]}\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t)-\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}\left(t+\frac{1}{2n}\right)\right\rvert dt\lesssim\begin{cases}n^{-1+\bar{A}},&\text{if}\ 0<\bar{A}<1,\\
n^{-1}\log(n),&\text{if}\ \bar{A}=0,\\
n^{-1},&\text{if}\ \bar{A}<0.\end{cases}

Thus, we have

(20)

\begin{split}\lvert c_{n}\rvert&\lesssim n^{-k}\left(\int_{[0,1]\setminus\Omega_{d}}\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t)-\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}\left(t+\frac{1}{2n}\right)\right\rvert dt+\int_{\Omega_{d}}\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}(t)-\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}\left(t+\frac{1}{2n}\right)\right\rvert dt\right)\\
&\lesssim\begin{cases}n^{-1-k+\bar{A}},&\text{if}\ 0<\bar{A}<1,\\
n^{-1-k}\log(n),&\text{if}\ \bar{A}=0,\\
n^{-1-k},&\text{if}\ \bar{A}<0.\end{cases}\end{split}

This concludes the proof.
∎

Under Assumption
2
, Lemma
4
extends the result of Lemma
3
. To interpret the result, consider that both the boundary unboundedness and the interior discontinuities can affect the convergence rate. When
\bar{A}\geq 0
, the boundary unboundedness dominates the convergence rate. Conversely, when
\bar{A}<0
, the
k
-th derivative
\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}
is bounded at the boundary, and the convergence rate is dominated by the discontinuities.

Remark 3

(Comparison with Zaremba’s condition)
.

Compared to Zaremba’s sufficient condition (
10
), which assumes that
\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}
is of bounded variation in the Hardy–Krause sense, our approach relaxes this assumption. Specifically, when
\bar{A}=0
, we assume that
\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}
is piecewise continuous but not necessarily of bounded variation in the Hardy–Krause sense, given that
\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial t^{k}}\lesssim\log(\phi(t))
. Nevertheless, our relaxation leads to a slower convergence rate by a logarithmic factor.

In the following, we study the multidimensional cases, focusing on the first-order mixed partial derivatives of the integrand. The cases involving higher-order mixed partial derivatives can be analyzed similarly using integration by parts and are not detailed in this work.

Lemma 5
.

If
f
satisfies Assumption

with
0<A_{j}<1/2
,
j=1,\dotsc,s
, then

(21)

\lvert c_{\bm{n}}\rvert\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{s}r_{1}(n_{j})^{-1+A_{j}},

with
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
,
r_{1}(n_{j})\coloneqq\max\{1,\lvert n_{j}\rvert\}
for
j=1,\dotsc,s
.

Proof.

Let
\frac{1}{\bm{n}}=(\frac{1}{{n}_{1}},\dotsc,\frac{1}{{n}_{s}})
for
n_{1},\dotsc,n_{s}\neq 0
. For
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1:s
, consider the vector
\bm{v}_{\mathfrak{u}}=\frac{1}{2\bm{n}}^{\mathfrak{u}}:0^{-\mathfrak{u}}
, i.e.,
(\bm{v}_{\mathfrak{u}})_{j}=\frac{1}{2n_{j}}
when
j\in\mathfrak{u}
and 0 otherwise. We have

(22)

\begin{split}c_{\bm{n}}&=\int_{[0,1]^{s}}f(\bm{t})e^{-i2\pi\bm{n}\cdot\bm{t}}d\bm{t}=\int_{[0,1]^{s}}f(\bm{t}+\bm{v}_{\mathfrak{u}})e^{-i2\pi\bm{n}\cdot(\bm{t}+\bm{v}_{\mathfrak{u}})}d\bm{t}=(-1)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}\int_{[0,1]^{s}}f(\bm{t}+\bm{v}_{\mathfrak{u}})e^{-i2\pi\bm{n}\cdot\bm{t}}d\bm{t}.\end{split}

By summing over all
2^{s}
sets
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1:s
in (
22
), we have

(23)

\begin{split}\lvert c_{\bm{n}}\rvert&\leq\frac{1}{2^{s}}\int_{[0,1]^{s}}\left\lvert\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1:s}(-1)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}f(\bm{t}+\bm{v}_{\mathfrak{u}})\right\rvert d\bm{t}:=\frac{1}{2^{s}}\int_{[0,1]^{s}}\left\lvert\Delta(f;\bm{t},\bm{t}+\frac{1}{2\bm{n}})\right\rvert d\bm{t},\end{split}

where we use the notation
\Delta(f;\bm{t},\bm{t}+\frac{1}{2\bm{n}})
from
[
27
]
to denote the alternating sum over the vertices of the hyperrectangle
[\bm{t},\bm{t}+\frac{1}{2\bm{n}}]
. We proceed the derivation as follows:

(24)

\begin{split}\left\lvert c_{\bm{n}}\right\rvert&\leq\frac{1}{2^{s}}\int_{[0,1]^{s}}\left\lvert\Delta(f;\bm{t},\bm{t}+\frac{1}{2\bm{n}})\right\rvert d\bm{t}\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{s}}\int_{[0,1]^{s}}\left\lvert\int_{[\bm{t},\bm{t}+\frac{1}{2\bm{n}}\textrm{ mod }1]}\partial^{1:s}f(\bm{\tau})d\bm{\tau}\right\rvert d\bm{t}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2^{s}}\int_{[0,1]^{s}}\int_{[\bm{t},\bm{t}+\frac{1}{2\bm{n}}\textrm{ mod }1]}\left\lvert\partial^{1:s}f(\bm{\tau})\right\rvert d\bm{\tau}d\bm{t}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{s}}\int_{[0,1]^{s}}\int_{[\bm{t},\bm{t}+\frac{1}{2\bm{n}}\textrm{ mod }1]}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\phi({\tau}_{j})^{-1-A_{j}}d\bm{\tau}d\bm{t}\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{s}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\int_{[0,1]}\int_{[{t}_{j},{t}_{j}+\frac{1}{2{n}_{j}}\textrm{ mod }1]}\phi({\tau}_{j})^{-1-A_{j}}d{\tau}_{j}d{t}_{j}\\
&\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{s}{n}_{j}^{-1+A_{j}},\end{split}

where notice that the notation
[\bm{t},\bm{t}+\frac{1}{2\bm{n}}\textrm{ mod }1]
from the third line refers to the definition in Section
2.1
and the last line follows by applying similar arguments as in the proof of Appendix
A
.

Now we consider the case when some
{n}_{j}=0
for
j=1,\dotsc,s
. Let
\mathfrak{u}
be a nonempty proper subset of
1:s
such that
n_{j}\neq 0
if and only if
j\in\mathfrak{u}
. Let
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
and
\bm{n}_{\mathfrak{u}}=\bm{n}^{\mathfrak{u}}:\bm{0}^{-\mathfrak{u}}
. Similarly to the above derivations, we have the following bounds:

(25)

\begin{split}\left\lvert c_{\bm{n}}\right\rvert&\leq\frac{1}{2^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}\int_{[0,1]^{s}}\left\lvert\Delta(f;\bm{t},\bm{t}+\frac{1}{2\bm{n}_{\mathfrak{u}}})\right\rvert d\bm{t}\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}\int_{[0,1]^{s}}\left\lvert\int_{[\bm{t}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{t}_{\mathfrak{u}}+\frac{1}{2\bm{n}_{\mathfrak{u}}}\textrm{ mod }1]}\partial^{\mathfrak{u}}f(\bm{\tau}^{\mathfrak{u}}:\bm{t}^{-\mathfrak{u}})d\bm{\tau}^{\mathfrak{u}}\right\rvert d\bm{t}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}\int_{[0,1]^{s}}\int_{[\bm{t}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{t}_{\mathfrak{u}}+\frac{1}{2\bm{n}_{\mathfrak{u}}}\textrm{ mod }1]}\left\lvert\partial^{\mathfrak{u}}f(\bm{\tau}^{\mathfrak{u}}:\bm{t}^{-\mathfrak{u}})\right\rvert d\bm{\tau}^{\mathfrak{u}}d\bm{t}\\
&\lesssim\int_{[0,1]^{s}}\int_{[\bm{t}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{t}_{\mathfrak{u}}+\frac{1}{2\bm{n}_{\mathfrak{u}}}\textrm{ mod }1]}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\phi({\tau}_{j})^{-1-A_{j}}d\bm{\tau}^{\mathfrak{u}}\prod_{k\notin\mathfrak{u}}\phi({t}_{k})^{-A_{k}}d\bm{t}\\
&=\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\int_{[0,1]}\int_{[{t}_{j},{t}_{j}+\frac{1}{2{n}_{j}}\textrm{ mod }1]}\phi({\tau}_{j})^{-1-A_{j}}d{\tau}_{j}d{t}_{j}\cdot\prod_{k\notin\mathfrak{u}}\int_{[0,1]}\phi({t}_{k})^{-A_{k}}d{t}_{k}\\
&\lesssim\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}{n}_{j}^{-1+A_{j}}.\end{split}

By combining (
24
) and (
25
), we reach the conclusion.
∎

The result of Lemma
5
can be further extended to functions with axis-parallel discontinuities, as stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 6
.

Let
f:[0,1]^{s}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function of the form
f=\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}\cdot g
, where
\Omega=[\bm{a},\bm{b}]\subseteq[0,1]^{s}
is a hyperrectangle. Assume that the first-order mixed partial derivatives
\frac{\partial^{\mathfrak{u}}g}{\partial\bm{t}_{\mathfrak{u}}}
are defined on
(0,1)^{s}
and satisfy the boundary growth condition (
3
) with
0<A_{j}<1/2
for
j=1,\dotsc,s
. Then, the Fourier coefficients
c_{\bm{n}}
satisfy

(26)

\lvert c_{\bm{n}}\rvert\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{s}r_{1}(n_{j})^{-1+A_{j}}.

The proof can be found in Appendix
B
.

In this section, we have established the relationship between the integrand regularity and the Fourier coefficients decay. We found that the Fourier coefficients decay rate is dominated by boundary unboundedness and the interior discontinuities of the function
f
or its derivatives. Moreover, we demonstrate how Fourier coefficients decay affects the variance of the RSLR estimator. In the next section, we study the Sobol’ sequence using spectral analysis.

3.2.
Sobol’ sequence

Let
N=2^{m}
, and define
I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}\coloneqq I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}(f)
as the scrambled
(t,m,s)
-Sobol’ sequence estimator of the integral of
f
.
[
24
,
25
]
use ANOVA decomposition and the Haar wavelet basis to derive the variance of
I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}
. Following
[
24
]
, let the multiresolution ANOVA decomposition of
f
be given by:
f=\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1:s}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}f_{\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}}
. The variance of
I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}
can be expressed as:

(27)

{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}\right]}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}}\sigma^{2}_{\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}},

where
\sigma^{2}_{\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}}={\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[f_{\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}}\right]}=\int_{[0,1]^{s}}f^{2}_{\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}}(\bm{t})d\bm{t}
. The term
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}}
is referred to as the “gain coefficient” in
[
24
]
, which is a quantity depending on the net parameters. Recently,
[
30
]
derived an explicit formula for the gain coefficient in base 2. Additionally,
[
7
]
derived an upper bound for the gain coefficients in general prime bases.

[
5
]
apply the Walsh basis and derive an equivalent formulation of the variance:

(28)

{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}\right]}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}}\Gamma_{\bm{\ell}}\sigma^{2}_{\bm{\ell}}.

It is worth mentioning that
\Gamma_{\bm{\ell}}
in (
28
) equals
\Gamma_{1:s,\bm{\ell}}
in (
27
). An alternative formulation of the variance in terms of the sum of squared Walsh coefficients over the dual net is presented in
[
17
]
.

In this work, we follow the notations from
[
5
]
for simplicity. Next, we study the decay properties of
\sigma^{2}_{\bm{\ell}}
. We first consider the Walsh decomposition of
f
:

(29)

f(\bm{t})=\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}}\bar{f}({\bm{\ell}}){}_{2}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{\ell}}(\bm{t}),

where
\bar{f}
refers to the Walsh–Fourier coefficients. The Walsh function
{}_{2}\mathrm{wal}_{\ell}
is introduced in Appendix
C
. Following the notations in
[
4
,
5
]
, for
\bm{\ell}=\left({\ell}_{1},\dotsc,{\ell}_{s}\right)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
, we define the index set

(30)

L_{\bm{\ell}}=\{\bm{k}=({k}_{1},\dotsc,{k}_{s})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}:\left\lfloor 2^{{\ell}_{j}-1}\right\rfloor\leq k_{j}<2^{{\ell}_{j}}\ \textrm{for}\ 1\leq j\leq s\}.

Then
\sigma^{2}_{\bm{\ell}}
is given by

(31)

\sigma^{2}_{\bm{\ell}}=\sum_{\bm{k}\in L_{\bm{\ell}}}\lvert\bar{f}(\bm{k})\rvert^{2}={\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[\beta_{\bm{\ell}}\right]},

where the function
\beta_{\bm{\ell}}
is given by the Walsh expansion of
f
within the index set
L_{\bm{\ell}}
, i.e.,

(32)

\beta_{\bm{\ell}}(\bm{t})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in L_{\bm{\ell}}}\bar{f}(\bm{k}){}_{2}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{t}).

To proceed with the summations of the Walsh–Fourier coefficients
\bar{f}
over the set
L_{\bm{\ell}}
, we define another set:

(33)

T_{\bm{\ell}}=\{\bm{k}=({k}_{1},\dotsc,{k}_{s})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}:0\leq{k}_{j}<2^{\ell_{j}}\ \textrm{for}\ 1\leq j\leq s\}.

Next, define the operator
\Delta_{j}
as set subtraction:

(34)

\Delta_{j}T_{\bm{\ell}}=T_{\bm{\ell}}\setminus T_{\bm{\ell}-\bm{e}_{j}},

where
\bm{e}_{j}
is the
j
-th standard unit vector with value 1 in coordinate
j
and 0 elsewhere. When
{\ell}_{j}=0
, we define
T_{\bm{\ell}-\bm{e}_{j}}\coloneqq\varnothing
. Define
\bigotimes
as the operator composition, i.e.,
\bigotimes_{j=1}^{s}\Delta_{j}=\Delta_{s}\circ\Delta_{s-1}\circ\cdots\circ\Delta_{1}
. Using this notation, we can express the set
L_{\bm{\ell}}
as:

(35)

L_{\bm{\ell}}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{s}\Delta_{j}T_{\bm{\ell}}.

We now present a lemma that provides an explicit expression for the Walsh series over the index set
T_{\bm{\ell}}
, which is essential for analyzing the properties of
\sigma^{2}_{\bm{\ell}}
.

Lemma 7

(Walsh series in
T_{\bm{\ell}}
)
.

Given the function
f\in L^{2}([0,1]^{s})
and the index set
T_{\bm{\ell}}
as defined in (
33
), we have the expression for the Walsh series in base 2 over the index set
T_{\bm{\ell}}
as

(36)

\sum_{\bm{k}\in T_{\bm{\ell}}}\bar{f}(\bm{k}){}_{2}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{t})=\prod_{j=1}^{s}2^{\ell_{j}}\int_{\cap_{j=1}^{s}\left\lfloor{y}_{j}2^{{\ell}_{j}}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor{t}_{j}2^{{\ell}_{j}}\right\rfloor}f(\bm{y})d\bm{y}.

The proof utilizes the Walsh–Dirichlet kernel and is provided in Appendix
C
.

Using Lemma
36
, the Walsh series in
L_{\bm{\ell}}
can be expressed as follows:

(37)

\begin{split}\beta_{\bm{\ell}}(\bm{t})&=\sum_{\bm{k}\in L_{\bm{\ell}}}\bar{f}(\bm{k}){}_{2}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{t})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\bigotimes_{j=1}^{s}\Delta_{j}T_{\bm{\ell}}}\bar{f}(\bm{k}){}_{2}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{t})\\
&=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{s}\Delta_{j}\sum_{\bm{k}\in T_{\bm{\ell}}}\bar{f}(\bm{k}){}_{2}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{t})\\
&=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{s}\Delta_{j}\prod_{j=1}^{s}2^{\ell_{j}}\int_{\cap_{j=1}^{s}\left\lfloor{y}_{j}2^{{\ell}_{j}}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor{t}_{j}2^{{\ell}_{j}}\right\rfloor}f(\bm{y})d\bm{y},\end{split}

where in the last two equalities we have abused the notation
\Delta_{j}
by defining

(38)

\Delta_{j}\sum_{\bm{k}\in T_{\bm{\ell}}}\bar{f}(\bm{k}){}_{2}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{t})=\begin{cases}\sum_{\bm{k}\in T_{\bm{\ell}}}\bar{f}(\bm{k}){}_{2}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{t})-\sum_{\bm{k}\in T_{\bm{\ell}-\bm{e}_{j}}}\bar{f}(\bm{k}){}_{2}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{t}),&\ \mathrm{if}\ {\ell}_{j}>1,\\
\sum_{\bm{k}\in T_{\bm{\ell}}}\bar{f}(\bm{k}){}_{2}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{t}),&\ \mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We can further express
\beta_{\bm{\ell}}
in (
37
). First, we consider the case in 1 dimension. When
{\ell}=0
,
\beta_{0}=\int_{0}^{1}f(y)dy
. For
\ell\neq 0
, the Equation (
37
) simplifies to

(39)

\begin{split}\beta_{\ell}(t)&=2^{\ell}\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor t2^{\ell}\right\rfloor}f(y)dy-2^{\ell-1}\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell-1}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor t2^{\ell-1}\right\rfloor}f(y)dy\\
&=\begin{cases}2^{\ell-1}\left(\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor t2^{\ell}\right\rfloor}f(y)dy-\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor t2^{\ell}+1\right\rfloor}f(y)dy\right),&\text{if}\ \left\lfloor t2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor t2^{\ell-1}\right\rfloor,\\
2^{\ell-1}\left(\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor t2^{\ell}\right\rfloor}f(y)dy-\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor t2^{\ell}-1\right\rfloor}f(y)dy\right),&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\\
&=2^{\ell-1}\left(\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor t2^{\ell}\right\rfloor}f(y)dy-\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=2\left\lfloor t2^{\ell-1}\right\rfloor+\xi_{\ell}}f(y)dy\right),\end{split}

where
\xi_{\ell}(t)=\left\lfloor t2^{\ell}\right\rfloor-2\left\lfloor t2^{\ell-1}\right\rfloor+1\textrm{ mod }2
.
Notice that the condition
\left\lfloor t2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor t2^{\ell-1}\right\rfloor
indicates that the
\ell
-th digit of
t
in base 2 is 0. Otherwise, the
\ell
-th digit is 1. The last line simplifies the expression by introducing the term
\xi_{\ell}
, which is the complement of
\left\lfloor t2^{\ell}\right\rfloor-2\left\lfloor t2^{\ell-1}\right\rfloor
in base 2.

In the multidimensional case, for
\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
, we derive the following result by extending the one-dimensional case through induction.

(40)

\beta_{\bm{\ell}}(\bm{t})=\prod_{j=1}^{s}2^{{\ell}_{j}-1}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq 1:s}(-1)^{\lvert\mathfrak{v}\rvert}\int_{\cap_{j=1}^{s}\left\lfloor y_{j}2^{{\ell}_{j}}\right\rfloor=2\left\lfloor t_{j}2^{{\ell}_{j}-1}\right\rfloor+\xi_{\bm{\ell},j}}f(y)dy\right),

with
\xi_{\bm{\ell},j}=\left\lfloor t2^{{\ell}_{j}}\right\rfloor-2\left\lfloor t2^{{\ell}_{j}-1}\right\rfloor+\mathbbm{1}_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}\textrm{ mod }2
. When
\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
and some
{\ell}_{j}\neq 0
if and only if
j\in\mathfrak{u}
,
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1:s
, we have

(41)

\beta_{\bm{\ell}}(\bm{t})=\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}2^{{\ell}_{j}-1}\prod_{k\notin\mathfrak{u}}2^{\bm{\ell}_{k}}\ \left(\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}}(-1)^{\lvert\mathfrak{v}\rvert}\int_{[0,1]^{\lvert-\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}\int_{\cap_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\left\lfloor\bm{y}_{j}2^{{\ell}_{j}}\right\rfloor=2\left\lfloor\bm{t}_{j}2^{{\ell}_{j}-1}\right\rfloor+\xi_{\bm{\ell},j}}f(\bm{y})d\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}}d\bm{y}_{-\mathfrak{u}}\right).

We now derive upper bounds for
\sigma^{2}_{\bm{\ell}}
using the expressions for
\beta_{\bm{\ell}}
given in (
39
), (
40
) and (
41
). We begin by studying the one-dimensional case in the following lemma.

Lemma 8
.

For an integrand
f\in L^{2}([0,1])
, which satisfies the boundary growth condition (
3
) with
-1/2<A<1/2
, the term
\sigma^{2}_{\ell}\coloneqq\sigma^{2}_{\ell}(f)
, given by (
28
), satisfies the following decay:

(42)

\sigma^{2}_{\ell}\lesssim 2^{(2A-1)\ell}.

Proof.

Following (
31
) and (
39
), for
\ell>0
, we can express
\sigma^{2}_{\ell}
as

(43)

\begin{split}\sigma^{2}_{\ell}&=\int_{[0,1]}\beta^{2}_{\ell}(x)dx=2^{2\ell-2}\int_{[0,1]}\left(\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor t2^{\ell}\right\rfloor}f(y)dy-\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=2\left\lfloor t2^{\ell-1}\right\rfloor+\xi_{\ell}}f(y)dy\right)^{2}dt\\
&=2^{2\ell-2}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{\ell}-1}2^{-\ell}\left(\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=k}f(y)dy-\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=k+1}f(y)dy\right)^{2}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{k\textrm{ mod }2=0}\\
&+2^{2\ell-2}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{\ell}-1}2^{-\ell}\left(\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=k}f(y)dy-\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=k-1}f(y)dy\right)^{2}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{k\textrm{ mod }2=1}\\
&=2^{2\ell-2}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{\ell-1}-1}2^{-\ell}\cdot 2\left(\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=2k}f(y)dy-\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=2k+1}f(y)dy\right)^{2}\\
&\leq 2^{\ell-1}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{\ell-1}-1}\left(\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=2k}\lvert f(y)-f(y+2^{-\ell})\rvert dy\right)^{2}.\end{split}

We first consider the case
\ell=1
. When
A\neq 0
, we have

(44)

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{\ell-1}-1}\left(\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=2k}\lvert f(y)-f(y+2^{-\ell})\rvert dy\right)^{2}&\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{{y}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{y}_{0}^{-A-1}d{y}_{0}+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{{y}+\frac{1}{2}}(1-{y}_{0})^{-A-1}d{y}_{0}\right)d{y}\right)^{2}\\
&=\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{A}\left[{y}^{-A}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-{y}\right)^{-A}-2\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-A}\right]\right)^{2}\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{A(1-A)}\right)^{2}2^{2A-2}.\end{split}

When
A=0
, we have

(45)

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{\ell-1}-1}\left(\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=2k}\lvert f(y)-f(y+2^{-\ell})\rvert dy\right)^{2}&=\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{{y}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{y}_{0}^{-1}d{y}_{0}+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{{y}+2^{-1}}(1-{y}_{0})^{-1}d{y}_{0}\right)d{y}\right)^{2}\\
&=\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(2\log\frac{1}{2}-\log({y})-\log(\frac{1}{2}-{y})\right)d{y}\right)^{2}\\
&=1.\end{split}

Next, we consider the case
\ell>1
. First, let
A\neq 0
. For
k=0,\dotsc,2^{\ell-2}-1
, which correspond to
y_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-2^{-\ell})
under the condition
\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=2k
, we have

(46)

\begin{split}\lvert f(y_{0})-f(y_{0}+2^{-\ell})\rvert&=\left\lvert\int_{y_{0}}^{y_{0}+2^{-\ell}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}dy\right\rvert\leq\int_{y_{0}}^{y_{0}+2^{-\ell}}\left\lvert\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right\rvert dy\lesssim\int_{y_{0}}^{y_{0}+2^{-\ell}}y^{-1-A}dy\\
&=\frac{1}{A}\left[y_{0}^{-A}-(y_{0}+2^{-\ell})^{-A}\right].\end{split}

For
k=2^{\ell-2},\dotsc,2^{{\ell}-1}-1
, which correspond to the range
y_{0}\in(\frac{1}{2},1-2^{-\ell})
under the condition
\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=2k
, we have

(47)

\begin{split}\lvert f(y_{0})-f(y_{0}+2^{-\ell})\rvert&=\left\lvert\int_{y_{0}}^{y_{0}+2^{-\ell}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}dy\right\rvert\leq\int_{y_{0}}^{y_{0}+2^{-\ell}}\left\lvert\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right\rvert dy\lesssim\int_{y_{0}}^{y_{0}+2^{-\ell}}(1-y)^{-1-A}dy\\
&=\frac{1}{A}\left[(1-y_{0}-2^{-\ell})^{-A}-(1-y_{0})^{-A}\right].\end{split}

The equations (
46
) and (
47
) cover all the cases for
k=0,\dotsc,2^{\ell-1}-1
in (
43
). Given the symmetric results in (
46
) and (
47
), it suffices to consider the cases
k=0,\dotsc,2^{\ell-2}-1
in the following derivations. We have

(48)

\begin{split}\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=2k}\lvert f(y)-f(y+2^{-\ell})\rvert dy&\lesssim\frac{1}{A}\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=2k}y^{-A}-(y+2^{-\ell})^{-A}dy\\
&=\frac{1}{A}\int_{2k\cdot 2^{-\ell}}^{(2k+1)2^{-\ell}}y^{-A}-(y+2^{-\ell})^{-A}dy\\
&=\frac{1}{A(1-A)}\left[2\cdot\left(2^{-\ell}(2k+1)\right)^{1-A}-\left(2^{-\ell}(2k)\right)^{1-A}-\left(2^{-\ell}(2k+2)\right)^{1-A}\right]\\
&=\frac{2^{-\ell(1-A)}}{A(1-A)}\left[2\cdot\left(2k+1\right)^{1-A}-\left(2k\right)^{1-A}-\left(2k+2\right)^{1-A}\right].\end{split}

When
k=0
,
2\cdot\left(2k+1\right)^{1-A}-\left(2k\right)^{1-A}-\left(2k+2\right)^{1-A}=2-2^{1-A}
. For
k\geq 1
, we define the auxiliary function
\varXi:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
,
\varXi(x)=x^{1-A},A\neq 0
. We apply the Taylor expansion to obtain

(49)

\begin{split}(2k)^{1-A}&=(2k+1)^{1-A}-(1-A)(2k+1)^{-A}+\frac{\varXi^{\prime\prime}(2k+1)}{2!}+\cdots+(-1)^{m}\frac{\varXi^{(m)}(2k+1)}{m!}+\cdots,\\
(2k+2)^{1-A}&=(2k+1)^{1-A}+(1-A)(2k+1)^{-A}+\frac{\varXi^{\prime\prime}(2k+1)}{2!}+\cdots+(1)^{m}\frac{\varXi^{(m)}(2k+1)}{m!}+\cdots.\end{split}

Then for
k\geq 1
, we have

(50)

2\cdot\left(2k+1\right)^{1-A}-\left(2k\right)^{1-A}-\left(2k+2\right)^{1-A}=-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}2\cdot\frac{\varXi^{(2m)}(2k+1)}{(2m)!}.

Thus, an upper bound for
\sigma^{2}_{\ell}
is given by

(51)

\begin{split}\sigma^{2}_{\ell}&\leq 2^{\ell-1}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{\ell-1}-1}\left(\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=2k}\lvert f(y)-f(y+2^{-\ell})\rvert dy\right)^{2}\\
&\lesssim 2^{\ell-1}\cdot 2\sum_{k=0}^{2^{\ell-2}-1}2^{-2\ell(1-A)}\left(2\cdot\left(2k+1\right)^{1-A}-\left(2k\right)^{1-A}-\left(2k+2\right)^{1-A}\right)^{2}\\
&\lesssim 2^{\ell(2A-1)}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{\ell-2}-1}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{-\varXi^{(2m)}(2k+1)}{(2m)!}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

For
A\in(-1/2,0)\cup(0,1/2)
,
k\geq 1
, we have

(52)

\begin{split}\left\lvert\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{-\varXi^{(2m)}(2k+1)}{(2m)!}\right\rvert&=\left\lvert\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2m-2+A)(2m-1+A)\cdots A(1-A)(2k+1)^{-2m+1-A}}{(2m)!}\right\rvert\\
&\lesssim\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(2k+1)^{-2m+1-A}\\
&=\frac{(2k+1)^{-1-A}}{1-(2k+1)^{-2}}\leq{(2k+1)^{-1-A}}.\end{split}

Notice that,

(53)

\sum_{k=1}^{2^{\ell-2}-1}(2k+1)^{-2-2A}<\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-2-2A}<+\infty.

Thus we have,

(54)

\sigma^{2}_{\ell}\lesssim 2^{(2A-1)\ell}.

Now we consider the case
\ell>1
and
A=0
. From (
43
), we have

(55)

\begin{split}\sigma^{2}_{\ell}&\leq 2^{\ell-1}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{\ell-1}-1}\left(\int_{\left\lfloor y2^{\ell}\right\rfloor=2k}\lvert f(y)-f(y+2^{-\ell})\rvert dy\right)^{2}\\
&\lesssim 2^{\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{\ell-2}-1}\left(\int_{2k\cdot 2^{-\ell}}^{(2k+1)2^{-\ell}}\log(y+2^{-\ell})-\log(y)dy\right)^{2}\\
&=2^{\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{\ell-2}-1}\left(\varTheta\left((2k+2)2^{-\ell}\right)+\varTheta(2k\cdot 2^{-\ell})-2\varTheta((2k+1)\cdot 2^{-\ell})\right)^{2},\end{split}

where in the second line we use the symmetry of the boundary growth condition and in the third line we define the auxiliary function
\varTheta:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
,
\varTheta(x)=x\log x-x
. The derivatives of
\varTheta
are given by
\varTheta^{(2m)}(2k+1)=(2m-2)!(2k+1)^{-2m+1}
, for
m=1,\dotsc,\infty
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, similar to earlier derivations we have

(56)

\begin{split}\varTheta\left((2k+2)2^{-\ell}\right)&=\varTheta\left((2k+1)2^{-\ell}\right)+2^{-\ell}\varTheta^{\prime}\left((2k+1)2^{-\ell}\right)+\cdots+(2^{-\ell})^{m}\frac{\varTheta^{(m)}((2k+1)2^{-\ell})}{m!}+\cdots\\
\varTheta\left((2k)2^{-\ell}\right)&=\varTheta\left((2k+1)2^{-\ell}\right)-2^{-\ell}\varTheta^{\prime}\left((2k+1)2^{-\ell}\right)+\cdots+(-2^{-\ell})^{m}\frac{\varTheta^{(m)}((2k+1)2^{-\ell})}{m!}+\cdots,\end{split}

and

(57)

\begin{split}\varTheta\left((2k+2)2^{-\ell}\right)+\varTheta(2k\cdot 2^{-\ell})-2\varTheta((2k+1)\cdot 2^{-\ell})&=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}2(2^{-\ell})^{2m}\frac{\varTheta^{(2m)}((2k+1)2^{-\ell})}{(2m)!}\\
&=2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(2^{-\ell})^{2m}\frac{(2m-2)!((2k+1)2^{-\ell})^{-2m+1}}{(2m)!}\\
&=2^{-\ell+1}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2m-2)!(2k+1)^{-2m+1}}{(2m)!}\\
&\lesssim 2^{-\ell+1}(2k+1)^{-1}.\end{split}

Finally, for
\ell>1
and
A=0
, we have

(58)

\sigma^{2}_{\ell}\lesssim 2^{\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{\ell-2}-1}2^{-2\ell}(2k+1)^{-2}\leq 2^{-\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2k+1)^{-2}\lesssim 2^{-\ell}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

We present the following lemma for the multidimensional case.

Lemma 9
.

We consider an integrand
f\in L^{2}([0,1]^{s})
that satisfies the boundary growth condition (
3
) with
-1/2<A^{*}<1/2
, where
A^{*}=\max_{j=1}^{s}A_{j}
. Given
\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
with
{\ell}_{j}\neq 0
if and only if
j\in\mathfrak{u}\subsetneq 1:s
, the term
\sigma^{2}_{\bm{\ell}}\coloneqq\sigma^{2}_{\bm{\ell}}(f)
, given by (
28
), satisfies the following decay:

(59)

\sigma^{2}_{\bm{\ell}}\lesssim 2^{(2A^{*}-1)\lvert\bm{\ell}\rvert}.

Proof.

Following (
41
), we have

(60)

\begin{split}\sigma^{2}_{\bm{\ell}}&=\int_{[0,1]^{s}}\left\lvert\beta_{\bm{\ell}}(\bm{t})\right\rvert^{2}d\bm{t}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}\\
{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\leq 2^{\bar{\bm{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{u}}}-1}\end{subarray}}\left(\int_{[0,1]^{\lvert-\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}\int_{\cap_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\left\lfloor{y}_{j}2^{{\ell}_{j}}\right\rfloor=2\bm{k}_{j}}\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}}(-1)^{\lvert\mathfrak{v}\rvert}f\left(\bm{y}^{\mathfrak{v}}:\left(\bm{y}+2^{-\bm{\ell}}\right)^{\mathfrak{u}-\mathfrak{v}}\mid\bm{y}_{-\mathfrak{u}}\right)d\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}}d\bm{y}_{-\mathfrak{u}}\right)^{2}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}\\
{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\leq 2^{\bar{\bm{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{u}}}-1}\end{subarray}}\left(\int_{(E_{\bm{\ell},2\bm{k}})_{\mathfrak{u}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}}(-1)^{\lvert\mathfrak{v}\rvert}\int_{[0,1]^{\lvert-\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}f\left(\bm{y}^{\mathfrak{v}}:\left(\bm{y}+2^{-\bm{\ell}}\right)^{\mathfrak{u}-\mathfrak{v}}\mid\bm{y}_{-\mathfrak{u}}\right)d\bm{y}_{-\mathfrak{u}}d\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}}\right)^{2}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}\\
{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\leq 2^{\bar{\bm{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{u}}}-1}\end{subarray}}\left(\int_{{(E_{\bm{\ell},2\bm{k}})_{\mathfrak{u}}}}\int_{[\bm{y},\bm{y}+2^{-\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}}]}\partial^{\mathfrak{u}}\int_{[0,1]^{\lvert-\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}f(\bm{y}_{0}\mid\bm{y}_{-\mathfrak{u}})d\bm{y}_{-\mathfrak{u}}d\bm{y}_{0}d\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}}\right)^{2}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}\\
{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\leq 2^{\bar{\bm{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{u}}}-1}\end{subarray}}\left(\int_{{(E_{\bm{\ell},2\bm{k}})_{\mathfrak{u}}}}\int_{[\bm{y},\bm{y}+2^{-\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}}]}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\phi({y}_{0,j})^{-A_{j}-1}d\bm{y}_{0}d\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}}\right)^{2},\end{split}

where we denote
\bar{\bm{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{u}}=\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}-\bm{1}
, with the subtraction taken component-wise.
We have

(61)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}\\
{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\leq 2^{\bar{\bm{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{u}}}-1}\end{subarray}}\left(\int_{{(E_{\bm{\ell},2\bm{k}})_{\mathfrak{u}}}}\int_{[\bm{y},\bm{y}+2^{-\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}}]}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\phi({y}_{0,j})^{-A_{j}-1}d\bm{y}_{0}d\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}}\right)^{2}\\
&=\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{k}_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\\
{{k}_{j}\leq 2^{{{\ell}}_{j}-1}-1}\end{subarray}}\left(\int_{\lfloor y_{j}2^{\ell_{j}}\rfloor=2k_{j}}\int_{[{y}_{j},{y}_{j}+2^{-{\ell}_{j}}]}\phi({y}_{0,j})^{-A_{j}-1}d{y}_{0,j}d{y}_{j}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Refer to Equations (
43
)-(
58
), we have

(62)

\sigma_{\bm{\ell}}^{2}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{s}2^{(2A_{j}-1){\ell}_{j}}\lesssim 2^{(2A^{*}-1)\lvert\bm{\ell}\rvert}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 1
.

Let
f\in L^{2}[0,1]^{s}
satisfy the boundary growth condition (
3
) with
-1/2<A^{*}<1/2
, the variance of the RQMC estimator with scrambled Sobol’ sequence
I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}
satisfies

(63)

{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}\right]}=\mathcal{O}(N^{2A^{*}-2}\log(N)^{s-1}).

Proof.

From Lemma
9
, we obtain
\sigma^{2}_{\bm{\ell}}\lesssim 2^{(2A^{*}-1)\lvert\bm{\ell}\rvert}
. The rest of the proof follows the arguments Theorem 3 of
[
29
]
and Theorem 13.25 of
[
5
]
.
∎

Notice that in our proof, the convergence rate of the Sobol’ sequence does not exceed
\mathcal{O}(N^{-3/2})
, even if the integrand has better regularity. This observation aligns with the remarks of
[
5
]
, who introduce higher-order digital nets to exploit faster convergence rate. In contrast, the convergence rate of the RSLR largely depends on the integrand regularity.

Moreover, for an integrand satisfying the boundary growth condition (
3
) with
-1/2<A^{*}<-1/2
, the optimal convergence rate that we obtain is
\mathcal{O}(N^{-3+\delta}\log(N)^{s-1})
, for any
\delta>0
. This rate closely approximates
\mathcal{O}(N^{-3}(\log N)^{s-1})
, as established in
[
33
,
29
]
for Lipschitz continuous integrands. However, our analysis does not require the Lipschitz condition.

4.
Unbounded integrands with interior discontinuities

In this section, we aim to extend the analysis of the Sobol’ sequence to discontinuous integrands along with the unboundedness as characterized in the boundary growth condition (
3
) outlined in Section
2
. Specifically, following
[
10
]
, we consider an integrand of the form
f(\bm{y})=g(\bm{y})\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\bm{y}\in\Omega}
, where
\Omega\subset[0,1]^{s}
. Moreover, we assume that
\Omega
has a partially axis-parallel structure, i.e.,

(64)

\Omega=\Omega_{\mathfrak{u}}\times\prod_{j\notin\mathfrak{u}}[a_{j},b_{j}],

where
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1:s
,
\Omega_{\mathfrak{u}}
is a Lebesgue measurable subset of
\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}[0,1]
, and
0\leq a_{j}<b_{j}\leq 1
for all
j\notin\mathfrak{u}
. Following
[
10
]
, we introduce the following condition on the set
\Omega
.

Definition 2

(Minkowski content
[
1
]
)
.

Let
(\partial\Omega)_{\epsilon}
be the outer parallel body of the boundary
\partial\Omega
of
\Omega
at a distance
\epsilon
. If the limit

\mathcal{M}(\partial\Omega)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\lambda_{s}((\partial\Omega)_{\epsilon})}{\epsilon},

exists and is finite, then
\Omega
is said to admit an
(s-1)
-dimensional Minkowski content with respect to the s-dimensional Lebesgue measure
\lambda_{s}
.

As discussed in
[
10
]
, when
\Omega_{\mathfrak{u}}
admits a
\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert-1
-dimensional Minkowski content, the convergence rate of the RQMC estimator for integrands of the form
f=\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}g
is given by
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert-2}}(\log n)^{\frac{2s}{2\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert-1}})
, if
g
has bounded Hardy–Krause variation. The result improves to
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}})
if
g\equiv 1
. In a further exploration
[
9
]
, the author considers the case when
g
is unbounded and satisfies Owen’s boundary growth condition (
3
), which is exactly the scenario we consider in this section. The author shows that the convergence rate of the RQMC estimator with the Sobol’ sequence is
\mathcal{O}(n^{(A^{*}-1)(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert-2})}(\log n)^{\frac{(1-A^{*})s}{2\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert-1}})
. This results suggests a multiplicative effect arising from both the discontinuity and the boundary unboundedness. In the subsequent sections, we perform a spectral analysis for such discontinuous integrands.

We start by presenting two lemmas that will be used in the following sections. The first lemma is an excerpt from Lemma 3.4 in
[
10
]
.

Lemma 10

(Excerpt from Lemma 3.4 in
[
10
]
)
.

Consider a vector
\bm{r}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
with identical components, where
{r}_{j}=r
for all
j=1,\dotsc,s
,
r\in\mathbb{N}
. We define the elementary interval
E_{\bm{r},\bm{k}}
as:

(65)

E_{\bm{r},\bm{k}}=\prod_{j=1}^{s}\left[\frac{{k}_{j}}{{r}},\frac{{k}_{j}+1}{r}\right),\quad 0\leq{k}_{j}\leq r-1.

Let
{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{r}}
denote the set of the elementary intervals
E_{\bm{r},\bm{k}}
that intersect with
\Omega
, and let
{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{bdy}}^{r}}
denote the set of elementary intervals
E_{\bm{r},\bm{k}}
that intersect with
\partial\Omega
. The cardinality of
{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{bdy}}^{r}}
has the following upper bound:

(66)

\left\lvert\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{bdy}}^{r}\right\rvert\lesssim r^{s-1}.

Furthermore,
{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{r}}
can be expressed as the union of at most
\left\lvert\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{bdy}}^{r}\right\rvert\lesssim r^{s-1}
axis-parallel sets of the form
[\bm{a},\bm{b})
.

Notice that in Lemma

, the elementary intervals
E_{\bm{r},\bm{k}}
are isotropic, with a length of
1/r
in each dimension. This differs from the elementary intervals
E_{\bm{\ell},\bm{k}}
in (
2
).

The second Lemma discusses integration within each of the elementary interval.

Lemma 11
.

Let
f=\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}\cdot g
, where
g
satisfies the boundary growth condition (
3
) with
A^{*}<\frac{1}{2}
and
\Omega\subset[0,1]^{s}
. For
{\bm{\ell}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
, define
\bar{\bm{\ell}}\coloneqq\max(\bm{\ell}-\bm{1},0)
, where
\bm{1}
is the vector of 1’s and both subtraction and maximum are taken component-wise. We also define
f(\bm{y})\coloneqq 0
when
\bm{y}\notin[0,1)^{s}
for the notation simplicity in the derivations. Then, for all
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
such that
0\leq{k}_{j}\leq 2^{\bar{{\ell}}_{j}}-1
for
j=1,\dotsc,s
, the integration of the alternating sum
\Delta(f;\bm{y},\bm{y}+2^{-\bm{\ell}})
over the elementary interval
E_{{\bm{\ell}},2\bm{k}}
have the following upper bound:

(67)

\left(\int_{E_{\bm{\ell},2\bm{k}}}\Delta(f;\bm{y},\bm{y}+2^{-\bm{\ell}})d\bm{y}\right)^{2}\lesssim 2^{-\lvert\bm{\ell}\rvert}.

Proof.

Notice that we have the following inequality:

(68)

\left(\int_{E_{\bm{\ell},2\bm{k}}}\Delta(f;\bm{y},\bm{y}+2^{-\bm{\ell}})d\bm{y}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\int_{E_{\bm{\ell},2\bm{k}}}\left\lvert\Delta(f;\bm{y},\bm{y}+2^{-\bm{\ell}})\right\rvert d\bm{y}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\int_{E_{\bar{\bm{\ell}},\bm{k}}}\left\lvert f(\bm{y})\right\rvert d\bm{y}\right)^{2}.

When
A^{*}>0
, the integration in each elementary interval
E_{\bar{\bm{\ell}},\bm{k}}
has the following upper bound:

(69)

\begin{split}\left(\int_{E_{\bar{\bm{\ell}},\bm{k}}}\left\lvert f(\bm{y})\right\rvert d\bm{y}\right)^{2}&\lesssim\left(\int_{[0,2^{-\bar{\bm{\ell}}}]}\bm{y}^{-A^{*}}d\bm{y}\right)^{2}=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{s}2^{-\bar{\bm{\ell}}_{j}(1-A^{*})}\right)^{2}\leq 2^{-\lvert\bar{\bm{\ell}}\rvert}\leq 2^{-s}2^{-\lvert{\bm{\ell}}\rvert}.\end{split}

When
A^{*}=0
, we have

(70)

\begin{split}\left(\int_{E_{\bar{\bm{\ell}},\bm{k}}}\left\lvert f(\bm{y})\right\rvert d\bm{y}\right)^{2}&\lesssim\left(\int_{[0,2^{-\bar{\bm{\ell}}}]}-\log(\bm{y})d\bm{y}\right)^{2}\lesssim 2^{-2\lvert\bar{\bm{\ell}}\rvert}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\left(1+\bar{\bm{\ell}}_{j}\right)^{2}\lesssim 2^{-\lvert\bm{\ell}\rvert}.\end{split}

Otherwise
\left\lvert f(\bm{y})\right\rvert
is bounded in each elementary interval and

(71)

\begin{split}\left(\int_{E_{\bar{\bm{\ell}},\bm{k}}}\left\lvert f(\bm{y})\right\rvert d\bm{y}\right)^{2}&\leq\int_{E_{\bar{\bm{\ell}},\bm{k}}}f^{2}(\bm{y})d\bm{y}\leq\int_{[0,2^{-\bar{\bm{\ell}}}]}1d\bm{y}\leq 2^{-s}2^{-\lvert\bm{\ell}\rvert}.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
General discontinuous integrand

We consider a general discontinuous integrand,
f=\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}\cdot g
, where
\Omega
admits an
s-1

For
\bm{r}
and
r
defined in (
65
), recall the definition
{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{r}}\coloneqq\{\bigcup E_{\bm{r},\bm{k}}:E_{\bm{r},\bm{k}}\cap\Omega\neq\varnothing,0\leq{k}_{j}\leq{r}-1,j\in 1:s\}

Define
f_{r}\coloneqq\mathbbm{1}_{{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{r}}}g
. Then we have the following inequality:

(74)

{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}(f)\right]}\leq 2{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}(f-f_{r})\right]}+2{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}(f_{r})\right]}.

From Lemma
10
,
{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{r}}
can be expressed as the union of at most
r^{s-1}
axis-parallel sets. From Lemma
13
, for a fixed
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

(75)

{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}(f_{r})\right]}\lesssim r^{s-1}\left(N^{-2}(\log N)^{s-1}+N^{2A^{*}-2}(\log N)^{s-1}\right).

Notice that the support of
f-f_{r}
is contained in
{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{bdy}}^{r}}
. For a bounded
f
, i.e., when
A^{*}<0
, we have

(76)

{\mathbb{E}\mspace{-2mu}\left[(f-f_{r})^{2}\right]}\lesssim r^{s-1}r^{-s}=r^{-1}.

Then we obtain

(77)

\begin{split}{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}(f-f_{r})\right]}&\leq\frac{1}{N}{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[f-f_{r}\right]}\leq\frac{1}{N}{\mathbb{E}\mspace{-2mu}\left[(f-f_{r})^{2}\right]}\lesssim r^{-1}N^{-1},\end{split}

and consequently,

(78)

\begin{split}{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}(f)\right]}&\lesssim r^{-1}N^{-1}+r^{s-1}N^{-2}(\log N)^{s-1}+r^{s-1}N^{2A^{*}-2}(\log N)^{s-1}\\
&\lesssim r^{-1}N^{-1}+r^{s-1}N^{-2}(\log N)^{s-1}.\end{split}

We choose
r=N^{\frac{1}{s}}(\log N)^{\frac{1}{s}-1}
to balance the two terms, yielding

(79)

{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}(f)\right]}\lesssim N^{-1-\frac{1}{s}}\left(\log N\right)^{1-\frac{1}{s}}.

When
A^{*}\geq 0
, we apply the Hölder inequality to obtain

(80)

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}\mspace{-2mu}\left[(f-f_{r})^{2}\right]}&=\int_{[0,1]^{s}}f(\bm{y})^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{r}}\setminus\Omega}d\bm{y}\leq\int_{[0,1]^{s}}f(\bm{y})^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{bdy}}^{r}}d\bm{y}\\
&\leq\left(\int_{[0,1]^{s}}f(\bm{y})^{2p}\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{[0,1]^{s}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{bdy}}^{r}}^{q}d\bm{y}\right)^{1/q},\end{split}

where
1/p+1/q=1
. We choose
2A^{*}p<1
to ensure the integrability of

, and consequently
1/q=1-2A^{*}-\delta
for
0<\delta<1-2A^{*}
. We have

(81)

\begin{split}{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}(f-f_{r})\right]}&\leq\frac{1}{N}{\mathbb{E}\mspace{-2mu}\left[(f-f_{r})^{2}\right]}\leq\frac{1}{N}\left(\int_{[0,1]^{s}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{bdy}}^{r}}^{q}d\bm{y}d\bm{y}\right)^{1-2A^{*}-\delta}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{N}(r^{s-1}r^{-s})^{1-2A^{*}-\delta}\\
&=r^{-1+2A^{*}+\delta}N^{-1},\end{split}

and

(82)

{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}(f)\right]}\lesssim r^{-1+2A^{*}+\delta}N^{-1}+r^{s-1}N^{2A^{*}-2}(\log N)^{s-1}.

We choose
r=N^{\frac{1-2A^{*}}{s-2A^{*}-\delta}}(\log N)^{\frac{1-s}{s-2A^{*}-\delta}}
to balance the two terms and obtain

(83)

\begin{split}{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[I_{N}^{\mathrm{sob}}(f)\right]}&\lesssim N^{-1+\frac{(1-2A^{*})(-1+2A^{*}+\delta)}{s-2A^{*}-\delta}}\left(\log N\right)^{\frac{(s-1)(1-2A^{*}-\delta)}{s-2A^{*}-\delta}}.\end{split}

The results are summarized in the following Lemma.

Lemma 14
.

For
f=g\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}
, where
g
satisfies the boundary growth condition (
3
) with
-1/2<A^{*}<1/2
and
\Omega
admits an
s-1

6.
Conclusions

This work presents a comparative study of the RSLR and randomly scrambled Sobol’ sequences through spectral analysis. We analyze integrands with infinite Hardy–Krause variation due to the boundary unboundedness or interior discontinuities. We establish sufficient conditions under which these integrands achieve specific convergence rates and compare our findings to classical results. Using Owen’s boundary growth conditions, we provide fractional-order convergence rates that bridge the gaps between integer differentiability orders and their associated convergence rates.

Both low-discrepancy sequences under consideration are insensitive to axis-parallel discontinuities. For general discontinuous integrands with Sobol’ sequences, the convergence rates observed in the numerical results are faster than our theoretical bounds. One potential reason could be that the Hölder inequality applied in (
80
) does not provide optimal upper bounds. Another reason can be the loose upper bound in Lemma
13
. Future work could aim to refine these upper bounds.

The effect of the discontinuity interface on the RSLR remains unclear.
[
8
]
studies the Fourier transform of indicator functions on
\mathbb{R}^{s}
and reveals its dependency on the boundary curvature. Future work could extend the analysis in
[
8
]
to study the decay of Fourier coefficients, for instance, for the unbounded and discontinuous integrands examined in this work.

Furthermore, this study could facilitate the variance reduction techniques in the RQMC settings. For instance, in the context of importance sampling for QMC—as discussed in
[
11
,
19
]
—future efforts could consider leveraging spectral properties in additional to minimizing the second-order moment when searching for the optimal proposal distribution.

Appendix C
Walsh functions

Walsh (1923) introduced a system of functions denoted by
{}_{b}{\mathrm{wal}}_{k}
.

Definition 3

(Walsh functions in 1-D)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
b
-adic expansion
k=\kappa_{0}+\kappa_{1}b+\kappa_{2}b^{2}+\cdots
. For
b\geq 2
we denote by
\omega_{b}
the primitive
b
th root of unity
\omega_{b}=e^{2\pi i/b}
. Then the
b
-ary Walsh function
{}_{b}{\mathrm{wal}}_{k}
is defined by

{}_{b}{\mathrm{wal}}_{k}(x)=\omega_{b}^{\kappa_{0}\xi_{1}+\kappa_{1}\xi_{2}+\cdots}

for
x\in[0,1)
with
b
-adic expansion
x=\xi_{1}b^{-1}+\xi_{2}b^{-2}+\cdots
(unique in the sense that infinitely many of the digits
\xi_{i}
must be different from
b-1
).

The Walsh function in multiple dimensions is constructed by tensorizing the one-dimensional function.

In the following we present the proof of Lemma
36
.

Proof.

For
\bm{k}=({k}_{1},\dotsc,{k}_{s})
, the
s
-dimensional Walsh-Dirichlet kernel is given by

(C.1)

\begin{split}D_{\bm{\ell}}(\bm{z})&=\sum_{{k}_{1}=0}^{b^{{\ell}_{1}}-1}\cdots\sum_{{k}_{s}=0}^{b^{{\ell}_{s}}-1}{}_{b}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{z})=\sum_{{k}_{1}=0}^{b^{{\ell}_{1}}-1}\cdots\sum_{{k}_{s}=0}^{b^{{\ell}_{s}}-1}\prod_{j=1}^{s}{}_{b}{\mathrm{wal}}_{{k}_{j}}({z}_{j})=\prod_{j=1}^{s}b^{{\ell}_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{{z}_{j}\in[0,b^{-{\ell}_{j}})}=\mathbbm{1}_{\bm{z}\in[0,b^{-\bm{\ell}})}\prod_{j=1}^{s}b^{{\ell}_{j}}.\end{split}

Notice that the Walsh–Fourier coefficient is given by

(C.2)

\bar{f}(\bm{k})=\int_{[0,1]^{s}}f(\bm{t})\left({}_{b}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{t})\right)^{*}d\bm{t}.

Substitute (
C.2
) into (

(C.3)

\begin{split}\sum_{\bm{k}\in T_{\bm{\ell}}}\bar{f}(\bm{k}){}_{2}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{t})&=\sum_{\bm{k}\in T_{\bm{\ell}}}\int_{[0,1]^{s}}f(\bm{y})\left({}_{2}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{y})\right)^{*}{}_{2}{\mathrm{wal}}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{t})d\bm{y}\\

where

denotes the digit-wise subtraction modulo

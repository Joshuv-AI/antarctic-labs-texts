Perfect partitions of a random set of integers
Boris Pittel

Abstract
. Let X 1 , … , X n X_{1},\dots,X_{n} be independent integers distributed uniformly on { 1 , … , M } \{1,\dots,M\} , M = M ⁡ ( n ) → ∞ M=M(n)\to\infty however slow. A partition S S of [ n ] [n] into ν \nu non-empty subsets S 1 , … , S ν S_{1},\dots,S_{\nu} is called perfect, if all ν \nu values ∑ j ∈ S α X j \sum_{j\in S_{\alpha}}X_{j} are equal. For a perfect partition to exist, ∑ j X j \sum_{j}X_{j} has to be divisible by ν \nu . For ν = 2 \nu=2 , Borgs et al. proved, among other results, that, conditioned on ∑ j X j \sum_{j}X_{j} being even, with high probability a perfect partition exists if κ := lim n log ⁡ M > 1 log ⁡ 2 \kappa:=\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}>\tfrac{1}{\log 2} , and that w.h.p. no perfect partition exists if κ < 1 log ⁡ 2 \kappa<\tfrac{1}{\log 2} . We prove that w.h.p. no perfect partition exists if ν ≥ 3 \nu\geq 3 and κ < 2 log ⁡ ν \kappa<\tfrac{2}{\log\nu} . We identify the range of κ \kappa in which the expected number of perfect partitions is exponentially high. We show that for κ > 2 ​ ( ν − 1 ) log ⁡ [ ( 1 − 2 ​ ν − 2 ) − 1 ] \kappa>\tfrac{2(\nu-1)}{\log[(1-2\nu^{-2})^{-1}]} the total number of perfect partitions is exponentially high with probability ≳ ( 1 + ν 2 ) − 1 \gtrsim(1+\nu^{2})^{-1} .

1.
Introduction and main result

To liberally cite from Wikipedia
[
12
]
the partition problem is to determine if a given multiset of integers can be partitioned into two subsets such that the sums of numbers in the subsets are the same. An optimization version of the partition problem is to partition the multiset into two subsets such that the absolute value of the difference between the sums is minimized. In the multiway partition problem, there is an integer
\nu
and the problem is to determine whether the multiset can be partitioned into
\nu
subsets, each of the same total sum. We are interested in the case when the integers to be partitioned are random.

Specifically we have integers
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
, which are independent, each distributed uniformly on

[M]:=\{1,\dots,M\}
. Given
\nu\geq 2
, consider an ordered partition of
[n]
into non-empty subsets
S_{1},\dots,S_{\nu}
,
which we denote
\mathbb{S}
.
A partition
\mathbb{S}
of
[n]
is called
perfect
if
\sum_{j\in S_{\alpha}}X_{j}\equiv\tfrac{1}{\nu}\sum_{j\in[n]}X_{j}
,
\alpha\in[\nu]
. For a perfect partition to exist, it is necessary that
\sum_{j\in[n]}X_{j}
is divisible by
\nu
. For
\nu=2
, Borgs, Chayes, and Pittel
[
3
]
proved the following result, among a series of other, sharper claims. Conditioned on
\sum_{j}X_{j}
even, with high probability, a perfect partition exists (does not exist) if
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}>\tfrac{1}{\log 2}
(if
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}<\tfrac{1}{\log 2}
). In a follow-up paper Borgs, Chayes, Mertens, and Pittel
[
4
]
analyzed a constrained version, when the cardinalities of the two sets are given. A constrained version with
k
sets of the same cardinality was analyzed later by Bovier and Kurkova
[
5
]
. We refer the reader to Graham
[
7
]
, Gent and Walsh
[
6
]
, Hayes
[
8
]
, Karmarkar, Karp, Lueker, and Odlyzko
[
9
]
, Mertens
[
10
]
, and
Yakir
[
13
]
,
for the long history of the problem and its various aspects, from computational issues to conceptual connections with statistical physics.

In 2021 Tim Varghese and George Varghese
[
11
]
posed a problem of extending probabilistic analysis of perfect partitions into two subsets to multiway partitions, i.e. partitions of a set of random integers into
\nu>2
subsets with the same sum of integers in each subset. Here is what we have found.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\nu\geq 3
. Suppose
M=M(n)\to\infty
,
M=exp(O(n))
.
(1)

Expected number of perfect partitions tends to zero, if
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}<\tfrac{2}{\log\nu}
.
(2)
Expected number of perfect partitions is exponentially large

(a)
for
\nu=3
and
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}\in\bigl(\tfrac{2}{\log 3},\infty\bigr)\setminus\{2,4\}
,
and
(b)
for
\nu\geq 4
and
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}\in\bigl(\tfrac{2}{\eta(\nu)},\infty\bigr)\setminus\{2(\nu-1)\}
,
\eta(\nu)
being a single root of
\tfrac{e^{(\nu-1)\eta}}{\nu e\eta}-1
in
\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\nu-1},\tfrac{2\log\nu}{\nu-1}\bigr)
.

We conjecture that
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}=2(\nu-1)
for
\nu\geq 3
, and
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}=2
for
\nu=3
need not be excluded for
(2)
to hold. What happens when
\nu>3
and
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}\in\bigl(\tfrac{2}{\log\nu},\tfrac{2}{\eta(\nu)}\bigr)
?
Our second result is

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\nu\geq 3
. If
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}>\tfrac{2(\nu-1)}{\log[(1-2\nu^{-2})^{-1}]}
, then with the
limiting probability
\gtrsim(1+\nu^{2})^{-1}
the total number of perfect partitions is exponentially large, of the same order of magnitude as the expected number of perfect partitions.

The lower bound
(1+\nu^{2})^{-1}
is below
\nu^{-1}\!=\!\lim_{M\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\sum_{j\in[n]}X_{j}\!\equiv\!0(\text{mod }\nu)\bigr)
, uniformly for
n\geq 1
. (A simple, elegant proof of the latter was conveyed to me by Huseyin Acan
[
1
]
.)

2.
Integral formulas

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
\nu>2
. Let
Z_{n}
denote the random total number of all perfect ordered partitions of
[n]
into
\nu
non-empty
subsets.
(1)
Then

(2.1)

\displaystyle Z_{n}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\nu}}\!\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}}\prod_{j\in[n]}f(\mathbb{x},X_{j})\,\,d\mathbb{x},\quad\mathbb{x}:=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{\nu}\},

\displaystyle f(\mathbb{x},\eta):=\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\exp\bigl(iy_{\alpha}\eta\bigr),\quad y_{\alpha}=y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x}):=\nu x_{\alpha}-\sum_{\beta\in[\nu]}x_{\beta}.

Consequently, denoting by
X
a random variable distributed as each of
X_{j}
, we have

(2.2)

\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_{n}\bigr]=\!\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\nu}}\int_{\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}}\mathbb{E}^{n}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]d\mathbb{x}.

(2)
Furthermore

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2\nu}}\!\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}^{n}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\overline{f(\mathbb{x}^{\prime},X)}\bigr]\,\,d\mathbb{x}d\mathbb{x}^{\prime}.

Proof.

(1)

Given an ordered partition
\mathbb{S}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{\nu}\}
of
[n]
into non-empty subsets
S_{\alpha}
, define
Y_{\alpha}:=\sum_{j\in S_{\alpha}}X_{j}
, so that
\sum_{\beta\in[\nu]}Y_{\beta}=\sum_{j\in[n]}X_{j}
.
\mathbb{S}
is perfect if and only if

(2.4)

\nu\cdot Y_{\alpha}-\sum_{\beta\in[\nu]}Y_{\beta}=0,\quad\alpha\in[\nu].

Clearly, for a perfect partition to exist,
\sum_{j\in[n]}X_{j}
must be divisible by
\nu
.
Let
\mathbb{I}(A)
denote the indicator of an event
A
. Then

\mathbb{I}(\mathbb{S}\text{ perfect})=\prod_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\mathbb{I}\biggl(\nu\cdot Y_{\alpha}-\sum_{\beta\in[\nu]}Y_{\beta}=0\biggr)\\
=\prod_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{x\in[-\pi,\pi]}\exp\biggl[ix\biggl(\nu\cdot Y_{\alpha}-\sum_{\beta\in[\nu]}Y_{\beta}\biggr)\biggr]\,dx\\
=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\nu}}\int_{\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}}\exp\biggl[i\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}x_{\alpha}\biggl(\nu\cdot Y_{\alpha}-\sum_{\beta\in[\nu]}Y_{\beta}\biggr)\biggr]\,d\mathbb{x},\quad\mathbb{x}:=\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in[\nu]}.

Summing the above equation over all ordered partitions
\mathbb{S}
with nonempty
S_{\alpha}
’s, and interchanging summation and integration on RHS, we obtain

(2.5)

Z_{n}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\nu}}\int_{\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}}\sum_{\mathbb{S}}\exp\biggl[i\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}x_{\alpha}\biggl(\nu\cdot Y_{\alpha}-\sum_{\beta\in[\nu]}Y_{\beta}\biggr)\biggr]\,d\mathbb{x}.

What if
\mathbb{S}
is such that some
S_{\alpha_{0}}=\emptyset
? In this case
Y_{\alpha_{0}}=\sum_{j\in S_{\alpha_{0}}}X_{j}=0
; so the factor by
ix_{\alpha_{0}}
in the exponent sum is
-\sum_{j\in[n]}X_{j}\neq 0
. Then the integral of the corresponding exponential
function over the (hyper)cube
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
is zero. Therefore we may, and will
extend the sum in (
2.5
) to all ordered sequences
\mathbb{S}
of sets
S_{1},S_{2},\dots,S_{\nu}
,
empty or non-empty
, that form a partition of
[n]
. Regrouping summands in the exponent we obtain

\displaystyle\exp\biggl[i\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}x_{\alpha}\biggl(\nu\cdot Y_{\alpha}-\sum_{\beta\in[\nu]}Y_{\beta}\biggr)\biggr]=\prod_{j\in[n]}\exp(i\sigma_{j}X_{j}),

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\sigma_{j}=\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}x_{\alpha}\Bigl(\nu\,\delta_{\alpha}(j)-1\Bigr),\quad\delta_{\alpha}(j):=\mathbb{I}(j\in S_{\alpha}).

Such a
relaxed
partition
\mathbb{S}
is uniquely determined by values of
\delta_{\alpha}(j)\in\{0,1\}
, subject to
\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\delta_{\alpha}(j)=1
, (
j\in[n]
), but not to
\sum_{j\in[n]}\delta_{\alpha}(j)\geq 1
, (
\alpha\in[\nu]
), because empty
S_{\alpha}
are allowed now. Thus,
\delta_{\alpha}(j)

vary
independently
for different
j
. So, introducing

\Sigma(\mathbb{x}):=\biggl\{\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}x_{\alpha}\bigl(\nu\,\delta_{\alpha}-1\bigr):\delta_{\gamma}\in\{0,1\},\,\sum_{\gamma\in[\nu]}\delta_{\gamma}=1\biggr\},

for the sum over relaxed partitions we obtain

\sum_{\mathbb{S}}\exp\biggl[i\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}x_{\alpha}\biggl(\nu\cdot Y_{\alpha}-\sum_{\beta\in[\nu]}Y_{\beta}\biggr)\biggr]=\prod_{j\in[n]}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma(\mathbb{x})}\exp(iX_{j}\sigma)\\
=\prod_{j\in[n]}\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\exp\biggl[i\biggl(\nu x_{\alpha}-\sum_{\beta\in[\nu]}x_{\beta}\biggr)X_{j}\biggr]=\prod_{j\in[n]}\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\exp\bigl(iy_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})X_{j}\bigr).

We conclude that

Z_{n}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\nu}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}}\prod_{j\in[n]}\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\exp\bigl(iy_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})X_{j}\bigr)\,\,d\mathbb{x},

which is (
2.1
) in the lemma. Equating the expectations of both sides and using independence of
X_{j}
’s, we get (
2.2
).

(2)
The equation (
2.1
) implies that

Z_{n}^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2\nu}}\!\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime}}\prod_{j\in[n]}f(\mathbb{x},X_{j})\overline{f(\mathbb{x}^{\prime},X_{j})}\,\,d\mathbb{x}d\mathbb{x}^{\prime},

so that

\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{2}]=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2\nu}}\!\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}^{n}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\overline{f(\mathbb{x}^{\prime},X)}\bigr]\,\,d\mathbb{x}d\mathbb{x}^{\prime}.

∎

3.
Expected number of perfect partitions

Using Lemma
2.1
, we prove

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\nu\geq 3
. Suppose that
M=M(n)\to\infty
however slowly. If
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}<\tfrac{2}{\log\nu}
, then
\lim\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]=0
.
(i)
Suppose
\nu=3
. If
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}\in\bigl(\tfrac{2}{\log 3},\infty\bigr)\setminus\{2,4\}
, then

(3.1)

\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_{n}\bigr]=(1+o(1))\frac{\nu^{n}}{M^{\nu-1}}\cdot\frac{\nu^{\nu-3/2}}{(2\pi\nu c_{M}n)^{\frac{\nu-1}{2}}}\to\infty,\\

c_{\scriptscriptstyle M}:=M^{-2}\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]=1/3+O(M^{-1})
.
(ii)
Suppose
\nu\geq 4
. Then (
3.1
) holds if
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}\in\bigl(\tfrac{2}{\eta(\nu)},\infty)\setminus\{2(\nu-1)\}
; here
\eta(\nu)\in\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\nu-1},\tfrac{2\log\nu}{\nu-1}\bigr)
is the larger of two roots of
\tfrac{e^{(\nu-1)\eta}}{\nu e\eta}-1
, the smaller one being below
\tfrac{1}{\nu-1}
.

Note.
It is unclear whether
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]\to\infty
if

\nu\geq 3
and
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}=2(\nu-1)
, or if
\nu=3
and
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}=2
. Perhaps
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]\to\infty
in all these cases, albeit at a rate lower then for
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}
arbitrarily close to, but different from

2(\nu-1)
for
\nu\geq 3
, and from
2
for
\nu=3
, respectively. The case

\nu\geq 4
,
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}\in\bigl(\tfrac{2}{\log\nu},\tfrac{2}{\eta(\nu)}\bigr)
remains open as well.

Proof.

By (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_{n}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\!\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\nu}}\!\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}}\!\!\!\mathbb{E}^{n}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]d\mathbb{x},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\mathbb{E}\bigl[e^{iy_{\alpha}X}\bigr],\quad y_{\alpha}=y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x}):=\nu x_{\alpha}-\sum_{\beta\in[\nu]}x_{\beta}.

(I)
Since
X
is integer-valued,
\Big|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]\Big|
certainly attains its maximum
\nu
at every
\mathbb{x}
such that for each
\alpha\in[\nu]
we have

y_{\alpha}=y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})=k_{\alpha}\pi
, and
k_{\alpha}
is even. Here necessarily
\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}k_{\alpha}=0
, since
\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}y_{a}(\mathbb{x})=0
. Given such
\mathbb{k}=\{k_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in[\nu]}
, the set of
\mathbb{x}
satisfying

(3.2)

\nu x_{\alpha}-\sum_{\beta\in[\nu]}x_{\beta}=k_{\alpha}\pi,\quad(\alpha\in[\nu]),

is a straight line
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
, since the
\nu\times\nu
matrix on the LHS has rank
\nu-1
. We will prove that
\Big|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]\Big|<\nu
everywhere else.
Let us enumerate the lines
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
that contain
interior points
of the cube
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
.
To this end notice that each line’s parametric equation is
x_{\alpha}=\nu^{-1}(t+k_{\alpha}\pi)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. This line contains an interior point
\mathbb{x}
of the cube
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
if and only if for some
t

\nu^{-1}(t+k_{\alpha}\pi)\in(-\pi,\pi),\quad\forall\,\alpha\in[\nu],

or equivalently

(3.3)

-\pi(\nu+\min k_{\alpha})<t<\pi(\nu-\max k_{\alpha}).

In particular,
\max k_{\alpha}-\min k_{\alpha}\leq 2(\nu-1)
, since
k_{\alpha}
are even.
For
\nu=2
we have
k_{1}=k_{2}=0
since
k_{1}+k_{2}=0
. From now on we focus on
\nu>2
. We consider these (admissible) lines only, since any other line can share at most one point with the cube.
And the set of all such touch points is finite.

Indeed, suppose
\mathcal{L}
does not contain interior points of the cube. If
\mathcal{L}
’s segment is in the cube, then this segment, i.e. a non-zero multiple of
\mathbb{e}:=(1,\dots,1)\in E^{\nu}
, belongs to one of
2\nu
faces, cubes of dimension
\nu-1
. Hence the segment is orthogonal to a coordinate vector
(0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots 0)\in E^{\nu}
– contradiction. So
\mathcal{L}
shares at most one point with the cube. Any such touch point
\mathbb{x}
satisfies
y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})=\pi k_{\alpha}
,
(\alpha\in[\nu])
, and it has to have a component
x_{\alpha^{\prime}}=\pi
, and a component
x_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}=-\pi
, for that matter. Otherwise
\mathcal{L}
would have contained interior points of the cube. The linear system
y_{a}(\mathbb{x})=k_{\alpha}\pi
,
x_{\alpha^{\prime}}=\pi
has at most one solution in the cube, because the associated matrix has rank
\nu
. So, since
|k_{\alpha}|\leq 2(\nu-1)
,
\alpha\in[\nu]
, the number of such solutions (i.e. touch points) is at most
\nu(2\nu-1)^{\nu}
.

By (
3.3
),
\mathbb{k}=\{k_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in[\nu]}
, which consists of even numbers adding up to
0
, is admissible if and only if

d(\mathbb{k}):=2\nu+\min_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}-\max_{\alpha}k_{a}\geq 2,

since the range of the parameter
t
is
\pi d(\mathbb{k})
, and
the length of the corresponding line segment is

(3.4)

L(\mathbb{k})=\pi d(\mathbb{k})\nu^{-1/2}=\pi\nu^{-1/2}\bigl(2\nu+\min_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}-\max_{\alpha}k_{a}\bigr).

Let even numbers
a\leq 0\leq b
be generic values of
\min_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}
and
\max_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}
.
Let
\mathcal{N}(a,b)
be the total number of admissible
\{k_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in[\nu]}
with parameters
a
,
b
. Obviously
\mathcal{N}(a,a)=\mathbb{I}(a=0)
, and
\mathcal{N}(a,a+2)=0
. Consider
r:=b-a\in[4,2(\nu-1)]
,
2(\nu-1)
coming from
d(\mathbb{k})\geq 2
. A generic
\{k_{\alpha}\}
with given
a=\min_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}
,
b=\max_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}
, contains some
\mu_{1}>0
components equal
a
, and
\mu_{2}>0
components
equal
b
, with
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}\leq\nu
, and
\mu_{3}:=\nu-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}
remaining even components, with values strictly between
a
and
b
, such that the total sum of all
\nu
components is zero. Consequently, if
b\geq a+4
then

(3.5)

\displaystyle N(a,b)

\displaystyle=[\zeta^{0}]\sum_{\mu_{1},\,\mu_{2}>0}\frac{\nu!}{\mu_{1}!\,\mu_{2}!\,\mu_{3}!}\cdot\zeta^{\mu_{1}a+\mu_{2}b}\biggl(\sum_{\text{even }j=a+2}^{b-2}\zeta^{j}\biggr)^{\mu_{3}},

with the last sum equal

(3.6)

\zeta^{a+2}\sum_{\text{even }j=0}^{r-4}\zeta^{j}=\tfrac{\zeta^{a+2}(1-\zeta^{r-2})}{1-\zeta^{2}},\quad r:=b-a.

So, by inclusion-exclusion, the RHS function of
\zeta
in (
3.5
) equals

\biggl[\zeta^{a}+\zeta^{b}+\frac{\zeta^{a+2}(1-\zeta^{r-2})}{1-\zeta^{2}}\biggr]^{\nu}-\biggl[\zeta^{a}+\frac{\zeta^{a+2}(1-\zeta^{r-2})}{1-\zeta^{2}}\biggr]^{\nu}\\
-\biggl[\zeta^{b}+\frac{\zeta^{a+2}(1-\zeta^{r-2})}{1-\zeta^{2}}\biggr]^{\nu}+\biggl[\frac{\zeta^{a+2}(1-\zeta^{r-2})}{1-\zeta^{2}}\biggr]^{\nu}\\
=\zeta^{a\nu}\biggl[\biggr(\frac{1-\zeta^{r+2}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\biggr)^{\nu}-\biggr(\frac{1-\zeta^{r}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\biggr)^{\nu}-\biggl(\frac{\zeta^{2}-\zeta^{r+2}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\biggr)^{\nu}+\biggl(\frac{\zeta^{2}-\zeta^{r}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\biggr)^{\nu}\biggr].

So, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(a,b)

\displaystyle=[\zeta^{-a\nu}]\mathcal{F}_{r}(\zeta),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{r}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\biggr(\frac{1-\zeta^{r+2}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\biggr)^{\nu}-\biggr(\frac{1-\zeta^{r}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\biggr)^{\nu}

\displaystyle-\biggl(\frac{\zeta^{2}-\zeta^{r+2}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\biggr)^{\nu}+\biggl(\frac{\zeta^{2}-\zeta^{r}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\biggr)^{\nu}.

Since
b=a+r>0
, we must have
\mathcal{N}(a,b)=0
for
a\leq-r
. As a partial validation, one can check that the top RHS
of (
3.7
) is indeed zero for
a\leq-r
, and also for
a=0
.
What we will actually need is

(3.8)

\mathcal{N}(r):=\sum_{\text{even }a<0}\mathcal{N}(a,a+r),\quad\text{even }r\in[4,2(\nu-1)],

the total number of the relevant
\nu
-tuples of even integers
\{k_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in[\nu]}
adding up to
0
, such that
\max_{\alpha\in[\nu]}k_{\alpha}-\min_{\alpha\in[\nu]}k_{\alpha}=r
. In fact, ultimately we are after a single number, namely

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(\nu):=2\nu+\sum_{\text{even }r\in[4,2(\nu-1)]}(2\nu-r)\mathcal{N}(r),

since
\pi\nu^{-1/2}\mathcal{M}
is the total length of the in-cube segments of lines for even tuples

\mathbb{k}
. Combining (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) and changing the order of double summation we obtain

(3.9)

\mathcal{M}=2\nu+\sum_{\text{even }a\leq 0}[\zeta^{-a\nu}]\sum_{\text{even }r\in[4,2(\nu-1)]}(2\nu-r)\mathcal{F}_{r}(\zeta).

We included
a=0
since
\mathcal{F}_{r}(0)=0
. Using the formula for
\mathcal{F}_{r}(\zeta)
, and telescoping, we get: with
r
even,

(3.10)

\sum_{r\in[4,2(\nu-1)]}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathcal{F}_{r}(\zeta)\!=\biggr(\!\frac{1-\zeta^{2\nu}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\!\biggr)^{\nu}\!-\biggr(\!\frac{1-\zeta^{4}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\!\biggr)^{\nu}\!-\biggl(\!\frac{\zeta^{2}-\zeta^{2\nu}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\!\biggr)^{\nu}\!\!+\biggl(\!\frac{\zeta^{2}-\zeta^{4}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\!\biggr)^{\nu}\\
=\biggr(\!\frac{1-\zeta^{2\nu}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\!\biggr)^{\nu}-\biggl(\!\frac{\zeta^{2}-\zeta^{2\nu}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\!\biggr)^{\nu}-(1+\zeta^{2})^{\nu}+\zeta^{2\nu}:=S_{1}(\zeta^{2}).

Then

\sum_{\text{even }a\leq 0}[\zeta^{-a\nu}]\sum_{\text{even }r\in[4,2(\nu-1)]}\mathcal{F}_{r}(\zeta)=\sum_{\alpha\geq 0}[z^{\alpha\nu}]S_{1}(z),

and using a simple formula

(3.11)

\sum_{\alpha\geq 0}\,[z^{\alpha\nu}]\,S(z)=\tfrac{1}{\nu}\sum_{j=0}^{\nu-1}S(z_{j}),\quad z_{j}:=\exp\bigl(i\tfrac{2\pi j}{\nu}\bigr),

we obtain

(3.12)

\sum_{\alpha\geq 0}[z^{\alpha\nu}]S_{1}(z)=\tfrac{1}{\nu}\sum_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\Bigl[\Bigl(\tfrac{1-z_{j}^{\nu}}{1-z_{j}}\Bigr)^{\nu}-\Bigl(\tfrac{1-z_{j}^{\nu-1}}{1-z_{j}}\Bigr)^{\nu}\Bigr]-1\\
=\tfrac{1}{\nu}\bigl[\nu^{\nu}-(\nu-1)^{\nu}-(\nu-1)(-1)^{\nu}\bigr]-1.

Combinatorially, the RHS is the total number of all tuples associated with lines
containing interior points
of the cube
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
, distinct from the diagonal
\mathbb{x}=\gamma\mathbb{e}
, i.e.
k_{\alpha}\equiv 0
. In particular, it is
0
for
\nu=2
, and
it is
6=3!
, as it should be: the former because the single admissible
2
-tuple is
\{0,0\}
, and the latter because
the admissible tuples
\{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\}\neq\{0,0,0\}
are
3!
permutations of the single tuple
\{-2,0,2\}
.

Similarly telescoping again,

(3.13)

\sum_{\text{even }r\in[4,2(\nu-1)]}\!\!\!\!\!\!r\,\mathcal{F}_{r}(\zeta)=2\nu\biggr(\!\frac{1-\zeta^{2\nu}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\!\biggr)^{\nu}\!-2\nu\zeta^{2\nu}\biggl(\!\frac{1-\zeta^{2(\nu-1)}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\!\biggr)^{\nu}\\
\quad+2\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\text{even }r\in[6,2\nu]}\biggl[-\biggl(\frac{1-\zeta^{r}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\biggr)^{\nu}+\zeta^{2\nu}\biggl(\frac{1-\zeta^{r-2}}{1-\zeta^{2}}\biggr)^{\nu}\biggr]\\
\quad-4(1+\zeta^{2})^{\nu}+4\zeta^{2\nu}.

Now, analogously to (
3.12
), we will replace
\zeta^{2}
with
z
, making
\zeta^{2\nu}=z^{\nu}=1
for all
z_{j}=\exp\bigl(i\tfrac{2\pi j}{\nu}\bigr)
. Hence we may, and will replace in (
3.13
) the factor
\zeta^{2\nu}
with
1
beforehand, and make the middle sum perfectly amenable to another round of telescoping. Diagrammatically,

\displaystyle\sum_{\text{even }r\in[4,2(\nu-1)]}\!\!\!\!\!\!r\,\mathcal{F}_{r}(\zeta)\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle S_{2}(z):=2(\nu-1)\Bigl(\tfrac{1-z^{\nu}}{1-z}\Bigr)^{\nu}

\displaystyle-2\nu\Bigl(\tfrac{1-z^{\nu-1}}{1-z}\Bigr)^{\nu}-2(1+z)^{\nu}+4z^{\nu}.

And

(3.14)

\sum_{\text{even }a\leq 0}[\zeta^{-a\nu}]\sum_{\text{even }r\in[4,2(\nu-1)]}r\mathcal{F}_{r}(\zeta)\\
=\sum_{\alpha\geq 0}[z^{\alpha\nu}]S_{2}(z)=\tfrac{2}{\nu}\sum_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\Bigl[(\nu-1)\Bigl(\tfrac{1-z_{j}^{\nu}}{1-z_{j}}\Bigr)^{\nu}-\nu\Bigl(\tfrac{1-z_{j}^{\nu-1}}{1-z_{j}}\Bigr)^{\nu}\Bigr]\\
=2\bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{\nu}\bigr)-2(\nu-1)^{\nu}-2(\nu-1)(-1)^{\nu}.

Combining (
3.4
), (
3.12
), and (
3.14
), we obtain

(3.15)

\mathcal{M}=2\nu\Bigl(1+\sum_{\text{even }a\leq 0}[\zeta^{-a\nu}]\sum_{\text{even }r\in[4,2(\nu-1)]}\mathcal{F}_{r}(\zeta)\Bigr)\\
-\sum_{\text{even }a\leq 0}[\zeta^{-a\nu}]\sum_{\text{even }r\in[4,2(\nu-1)]}r\mathcal{F}_{r}(\zeta)=2\nu^{\nu-1}.

Combining (
3.4
) and (
3.15
), we have proved

Lemma 3.2
.

The total length of the in-cube segments of lines for even tuples
\mathbb{k}
is
2\pi\nu^{\nu-3/2}
.

The reason that asymptotically only
\pi\nu^{-1/2}\mathcal{M}
matters is that the dominant contribution to
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]
comes from thin, parallel, cylinders in the cube
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
, each enclosing its own line segment, with the integrand asymptotically translation-invariant along the line segment, and dependent only on the Euclidean distance from
\mathbb{x}
to the point on this segment with the same
\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}x_{\alpha}
.

(II)
To prove this claim, our first step is to upper bound
\Big|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]\Big|
for
\mathbb{x}
’s outside the lines

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}=\{\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}:y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})=k_{\alpha}\pi\},\quad\sum_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}=0,\,\,k_{\alpha}\text{ are all even}.

(We remind that
y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})=\nu x_{\alpha}-\sum_{\beta}x_{\beta}
.) Start with

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbb{x},X)]:=\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\phi(y_{\alpha}),\quad y_{\alpha}=y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x}),

\displaystyle\phi(y)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}[e^{iyX}]=\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{j\in[M]}e^{iyj}=\frac{e^{iy}(e^{iyM}-1)}{M(e^{iy}-1)},

so that

(3.16)

\Big|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]\Big|\leq\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}|\phi(y_{\alpha})|,\quad|\phi(y)|=\Big|\frac{\sin(My/2)}{M\sin(y/2)}\Big|.

To proceed,
given
|y|\leq 2\pi(\nu-1)
, define an even integer
k(y)
by condition:

\min_{\text{even }k:|k|\leq 2(\nu-1)}|y-k\pi|=|y-k(y)\pi|.

So,
k(y)\pi
is an even multiple of
\pi
closest to
y
among all even multiples
k\pi
’s with
|k|\leq 2(\nu-1)
. If
y
is not an odd multiple of
\pi
, then
k(y)
is uniquely defined. So,
z(y):=y-k(y)\pi
is uniquely defined for almost all
y
, and for those
y
we have
\phi(y)=\phi(z(y))
, since
\phi
is
2\pi
-periodic. Furthermore,
|z(y)|
is the distance from those
y
to the set of even multiples of
\pi
, and as such it has a continuous extension to all
y
. Therefore,
|z(y)|<\pi
for almost all
y
. Slightly abusing notation, from now on
let
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
stand for the original cube
minus
the set of
\mathbb{x}
such that at least one
y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})
is an odd multiple of
\pi
. The discarded subset has zero Lebesgue measure. Thus, for
\mathbb{x}
in the (reduced) cube
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
, we have: for each
\alpha\in[\nu]
,
z(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x}))=y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x}))\pi
is well defined, and
denoting
z_{\alpha}=z(y_{\alpha}(x))
,
k_{\alpha}=k(y_{\alpha}(x))
,

(3.17)

\Big|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]\Big|\leq\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}|\phi(z_{\alpha})|,\quad\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}z_{\alpha}=-\pi\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}k_{\alpha}.

So, we need to upper bound
|\phi(z_{\alpha})|
,
(\alpha\in[\nu])
, for each of these subsets. Let
z\in[-\pi,\pi]
. Pick
b>1
. If
|\sin(z/2)|\geq b/M
, then
|\phi(z)|\leq b^{-1}
. Also,
|\phi(z)|\leq\pi/(M|z|)
,
because
\sin\eta\geq 2\eta/\pi
for
\eta\in(0,\pi/2)
. Now
|\sin(z/2)|\leq b/M
if and only if
|z|\leq b_{0}(M)/M
, where
b_{0}(M)
is uniquely defined for
M\geq b
by the condition
\sin(b_{0}/(2M))=b/M
with
b_{0}/(2M)\in(0,\pi/2]
. A closer look shows that, given
b
,

(3.18)

b_{0}(M)=2b\bigl[1+O(b^{2}/M^{2})\bigr],\quad M\to\infty.

Therefore
|\phi(z)|\leq b^{-1}
if
|z|\geq b_{0}(M)/M
, implying that

(3.19)

|\phi(z)|\leq\min\bigl\{\pi/(M|z|);b^{-1}\bigr\},\quad\text{for }|z|\geq b_{0}(M)/M.

Consider
|z|\leq b_{0}(M)/M
, and set
z=\xi/M
, i.e.,
|\xi|\leq b_{0}(M)\sim 2b
for large
M
. Then

|\phi(z)|=\Big|\frac{\sin(\xi/2)}{M\sin(\xi/(2M))}\Big|=|\psi(\xi)|\cdot\bigl|1+O(\xi^{2}/M^{2})\bigr|,\quad\psi(\xi):=\frac{\sin(\xi/2)}{\xi/2}.

Since

\psi^{2}(\xi)=\frac{1-\cos(\xi)}{\xi^{2}/2}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\tfrac{2(-1)^{j-1}}{(2j)!}\xi^{2(j-1)},

which is an alternating series with decreasing terms for
|\xi|\leq 3\sqrt{2}
, we obtain that

\psi^{2}(\xi)\leq 1-\tfrac{\xi^{2}}{30},\quad(|\xi|\leq 3\sqrt{2}).

Therefore,
|\phi(z)|^{2}\leq 1-\tfrac{\xi^{2}}{31}
for
|\xi|\leq 3\sqrt{2}
and
M
sufficiently large.
Furthermore,
\psi^{2}(\xi)\leq\tfrac{2}{9}
for
|\xi|\geq 3\sqrt{2}
. So, given
b
, there exists
M_{1}(b)
such that
|\phi(z)|^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{3}

for
|\xi|\in[3\sqrt{2},b_{0}(M)]
and
M\geq M_{1}(b)
. Invoking (
3.18
), we have: there exists
M(b)\geq M_{1}(b)
such that for
M\geq M(b)

(3.20)

|\phi(z)|^{2}\leq 1-\lambda(b)\xi^{2},\quad\xi:=Mz,\,|\xi|\leq b_{0}(M),\quad\lambda:=\min\{\tfrac{1}{31},\tfrac{1}{7b^{2}}\}.

It follows from (
3.19
) and (
3.20
) that
|\phi(z)|<1
for
0<|z|\leq\pi
. Consequently, by (
3.16
), (
3.17
), we have
\Big|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]\Big|<\nu
, unless
z_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})\equiv 0
,
(\alpha\in[\nu])
. Moreover, using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, and
1-\gamma\leq e^{-\gamma}
, we obtain

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
. With
z_{\alpha}=z_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})
, denote
A(\mathbb{x})=\{\alpha\in[\nu]:\,|z_{\alpha}|\leq b_{0}(M)/M\}
,
a(\mathbb{x}):=|A(\mathbb{x})|
, and define
\xi_{\alpha}=\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x}):=Mz_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in[\nu]
. Given
b>0
, for
M\geq M(b)
there exists
\lambda=\lambda(b)>0
such that

(3.21)

\Big|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]\Big|^{2}\leq\nu\biggl(\sum_{\alpha\in A(\mathbb{x})}|\phi(z_{\alpha})|^{2}+\sum_{\alpha\in A^{c}(\mathbb{x})}\!\!\!{\min}^{2}\bigl\{\tfrac{\pi}{|\xi_{\alpha}|};b^{-1}\bigr\}\biggr)\\
\leq\nu\biggl[a(\mathbb{x})\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda}{a(\mathbb{x})}\sum_{\alpha\in A(\mathbb{x})}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}\Bigr)+\sum_{\alpha\in A^{c}(\mathbb{x})}\!\!\!{\min}^{2}\bigl\{\tfrac{\pi}{|\xi_{\alpha}|};b^{-1}\bigr\}\biggr].

if
A(\mathbb{x})\neq\emptyset
; otherwise the first term within the brackets is set zero.

Note.
Of course, a similar inequality holds for
\Big|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]\Big|
, but with

unsquared

{\min}\bigl\{\tfrac{\pi}{|\xi_{\alpha}|};b^{-1}\bigr\}
and without factor
\nu
on the RHS. The inequality (
3.21
) has a useful advantage:
{\min}^{2}\bigl\{\tfrac{\pi}{|\xi|};b^{-1}\bigr\}
is integrable on
(-\infty,\infty)
.
There is a price to pay though: to get a
bound for
\big|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]\big|^{n}
, and then to integrate it over a peripheral part of the (reduced) cube
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
in question, we will have to raise both sides of the above inequality to the
(n/2)
-th power. So, while working on upper bounds, we assume that
n
is even, and define
N=n/2
. For odd
n
we would raise the RHS to the integer power
N\!:=\!(n-1)/2
and multiply the result by
\nu\geq\Big|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]\Big|
.

The next step is to use Lemma
3.3
in order to upper bound contribution to
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]
coming from a vast part of the cube
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Define
D=D_{1}\cup D_{2}
,

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\{\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}:\,a(\mathbb{x})<\nu\Bigr\},

\displaystyle D_{2}

\displaystyle=\biggl\{\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}:\,a(\mathbb{x})=\nu,\,\,\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\xi^{2}_{\alpha}\geq\tfrac{\log^{2}n}{n}\biggr\}.

where
\xi_{\alpha}=Mz_{\alpha}
,
z_{\alpha}=y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}\pi
,
\min_{\text{even }k}|y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k\pi|=|y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}\pi|
. (Notice at once that

D^{c}=\biggl\{\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}:a(\mathbb{x})=\nu,\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}<\tfrac{\log^{2}n}{n}\biggr\}\\
\ =\biggl\{\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}:\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}<\tfrac{\log^{2}n}{n}\biggr\},

the last equality holding if
\tfrac{\log^{2}n}{n}\leq b_{0}(M)(=O(b))
.) For
M=e^{O(n)}\to\infty
, and
N=n/2
, within factor
c(b)
we have

(3.22)

I_{n}:=\int\limits_{D}\big|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]\big|^{n}\,d\mathbb{x}\\
\quad\leq\tfrac{\nu^{2N}}{M^{\nu-1}}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda\log^{2}N}{4\nu}\Bigr)+\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tfrac{(\nu e^{O(b^{-2})})^{N}}{M},&&\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}<1,\\
&\Bigl(\tfrac{\nu Ne^{O(b^{-2})}}{e\log M}\Bigr)^{N},&&\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}\in(1,\nu-1),\\
&\tfrac{\bigl(\nu(\nu-1)e^{O(b^{-2})}\bigr)^{N}}{M^{\nu-1}},&&\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}>\nu-1.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

(i)
Consider
\mathbb{x}\in D_{1}
, so that
|A(\mathbb{x})|\in[0,\nu-1]
. Let
I_{n,1}
(
I_{n,1}(a)
resp.) denote the contribution of
D_{1}
(of
D_{1}(a):=\{\mathbb{x}\in D_{1}:|A(\mathbb{x})|=a\}
resp.) to
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]
. For
a>0
and
\mathbb{x}\in D_{1}(a)
, we use (
3.21
)
and a union-type bound to get

\big|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]\big|^{n}\leq\nu^{N}\sum_{A\subset[\nu]:|A|=a}\biggl[a\exp\Bigl(\!-\tfrac{\lambda}{a}\sum_{\alpha\in A}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}\Bigr)\cdot I(\max_{\alpha\in A}|\xi_{\alpha}|\leq b_{0})\\
+\sum_{\beta\in A^{c}}\mathbb{I}(|\xi_{\beta}|\in[b_{0},\pi M])\,{\min}^{2}\bigl\{\tfrac{\pi}{|\xi_{\beta}|};b^{-1}\bigr\}\!\biggr]^{N},\quad\xi_{\gamma}=\xi_{\gamma}(\mathbb{x}),\,\,\gamma\in[\nu].

Each of
\binom{\nu}{a}
terms contributes equally to the integral of the RHS over
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
. Therefore, by multinomial formula
with
\mathbb{r}:=(r_{1},r_{a+1},\dots,r_{\nu})
,

(3.23)

I_{n,1}(a)\leq\nu^{N}\binom{\nu}{a}\sum_{\|\mathbb{r}\|=N}\frac{N!}{r_{1}!\,r_{a+1}!\,\cdots r_{\nu}!}\\
\times\!\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}}\biggl[a^{r_{1}}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda r_{1}}{a}\sum_{\alpha\in[a]}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}\Bigr)\cdot I(\max_{\alpha\in[a]}|\xi_{\alpha}|\leq b_{0})\\
\times\prod_{\beta=a+1}^{\nu}\mathbb{I}(|\xi_{\beta}|\in[b_{0},\pi M])\,{\min}^{2r_{\beta}}\bigl\{\tfrac{\pi}{|\xi_{\beta}|};b^{-1}\bigr\}\biggr]\,d\mathbb{x}.

Recall that

\{\xi_{\alpha}\}=\{\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})\}
is the piecewise affine function, with the same matrix of rank
\nu-1
on each of at most

(2\nu-1)^{\nu}
disjoint subsets forming a partition of
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
.
To bound the integral in (
3.23
) we will switch from
x_{\alpha}
to
\xi_{\alpha}
for all but a single component
x_{\mu}
, choosing
\mu

dependent on

\mathbb{r}
.
No matter what
\mu
is, the Jacobian factor is the same,
namely
(M^{\nu-1}\nu^{\nu-2})^{-1}
, on every subset of the resulting partition.
Let
\beta\in\{a+1,\dots,\nu\}
; if
\xi_{\beta}
is among the new variables, then–upon integration of
\mathbb{r}
-th term–variable
\xi_{\beta}
contributes a factor

(3.24)

2\int\limits_{b_{0}}^{\pi M}\!\!\Bigl(\!\min\bigl\{\tfrac{\pi}{\xi};b^{-1}\!\bigr\}\!\Bigr)^{2r_{\beta}}\!\!d\xi\!\left\{\begin{aligned} \!\!&=\!2(\pi M-b_{0})\!\leq\!2\pi M,&&r_{\beta}=0,\\
\!\!&\leq\!\tfrac{2(\pi b-b_{0})}{b^{2r_{\beta}}}+\tfrac{2\pi}{b^{2r_{\beta}-1}(2r_{\beta}-1)}\!\leq\!\tfrac{4\pi}{b^{2r_{\beta}-1}},&&r_{\beta}\geq 1.\end{aligned}\right.

We will control the factors for
r_{\beta}\geq 1
by choosing
b
large. The case
r_{\beta}=0
is the crux of the matter. Since
a\geq 1
, the number of zeros in
\{r_{a+1},\dots,r_{\nu}\}
can be as high as
\nu-a
, if
\mu\in A
, thus leading to factor
M^{\nu-a}
in a bound
for the product of these integrals. However, we may and will select
\mu\in A^{c}
, if
r_{\mu}
is one of the zeros in
\{r_{a+1},\dots,r_{\nu}\}
. For this choice,

2\int_{b_{0}}^{\pi M}\Bigl(\min\bigl\{\tfrac{\pi}{\xi_{\mu}};b^{-1}\bigr\}\Bigr)^{2r_{\mu}}d\xi_{\mu}\approx 2\pi M
is replaced with

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}1\,dx_{\mu}=2\pi
. Hence, the worst-case bound becomes
M^{\nu-a-1}
instead! Only if there are no zeros in

\{r_{a+1},\dots,r_{\nu}\}
do we choose
\mu\in A
.

Since
\mu\leq\nu
, we obtain

(3.25)

\displaystyle I_{n,1}(a)

\displaystyle\leq\nu^{N}\binom{\nu}{a}\tfrac{(2\nu-1)^{\nu}\nu}{M^{\nu-1}\nu^{\nu-2}}\sum_{\|\mathbb{r}\|=N}\frac{N!\,a^{r_{1}}}{r_{1}!\,r_{a+1}!\cdots r_{\nu}!}\cdot J(\mathbb{r}),

\displaystyle J(\mathbb{r})

\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{x_{\mu},\,\{\xi_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\neq\mu}}\!\!\!\!\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda r_{1}}{a}\sum_{\alpha\in[a]}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}\Bigr)\cdot I(\max_{\alpha\in[a]}|\xi_{\alpha}|\leq b_{0})

\displaystyle\times\prod_{\beta=a+1}^{\nu}\mathbb{I}(|\xi_{\beta}|\in[b_{0},\pi M])\,{\min}^{2r_{\beta}}\bigl\{\tfrac{\pi}{|\xi_{\beta}|};b^{-1}\bigr\}\,\,dx_{\mu}\prod_{\gamma\neq\mu}d\xi_{\gamma}.

Let
\sigma(\mathbb{r})
stand for the number of zeros in
\{r_{a+1},\dots,r_{\nu}\}
. If
\sigma(\mathbb{r})>0
, then we choose
\mu\in\{a+1,\dots,\nu\}
such that
r_{\mu}=0
. Rather crudely, we have

\int\limits_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{a}}\!\!\!\!\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda r_{1}}{a}\sum_{\alpha\in[a]}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}\Bigr)\cdot I(\max_{\alpha\in[a]}|\xi_{\alpha}|\leq b_{0})\prod_{\alpha\in[a]}d\xi_{\alpha}\leq(2b_{0})^{a}=O(b^{a}).

Furthermore, using (
3.24
) and

\sum_{\beta\in[a+1,\,\nu]:\,r_{\beta}>0}(2r_{\beta}-1)=2(N-r_{1})-\bigl[\nu-a-\sigma(\mathbb{r})\bigr],

we bound

\int\limits_{x_{\mu},\,\{\xi_{\gamma}\}_{\mu\neq\gamma>a}}\prod_{\beta=a+1}^{\nu}\mathbb{I}(|\xi_{\beta}|\in[b_{0},\pi M])\,{\min}^{2r_{\beta}}\bigl\{\tfrac{\pi}{|\xi_{\beta}|};b^{-1}\bigr\}\,\,dx_{\mu}\prod_{\mu\neq\gamma>a}d\xi_{\gamma}\\
\leq 2\pi\cdot 2^{\nu-a-1}\prod_{\mu\neq\gamma>a}\int\limits_{b_{0}}^{\pi M}\!\!\Bigl(\!\min\bigl\{\tfrac{\pi}{\xi};b^{-1}\!\bigr\}\!\Bigr)^{2r_{\gamma}}\!\!d\xi\!\\
\leq 2^{\nu-a}\pi(\pi M)^{\sigma(\mathbb{r})-1}(4\pi)^{\nu-a-\sigma(\mathbb{r})}b^{-2(N-r_{1})+(\nu-a-\sigma(\mathbb{r}))}\\
\leq c_{1}M^{\sigma(\mathbb{r})-1}b^{-2(N-r_{1})}\leq c_{1}M^{\nu-a-1}b^{-2(N-r_{1})}.\\

Here
c_{1}
, and
c_{j}
below depend on
b
only. So,
J(\mathbb{r})\leq c_{2}M^{\nu-a-1}b^{-2(N-r_{1})}
if
\sigma(\mathbb{r})>0
.
Likewise, if
\sigma(\mathbb{r})=0
then
J(\mathbb{r})\!\leq\!c_{3}b^{-2(N-r_{1})}\!\leq\!c_{3}M^{\nu-a-1}b^{-2(N-r_{1})}
, since

\nu>a
. Therefore
J(\mathbb{r})\leq c_{4}M^{\nu-a-1}b^{-2(N-r_{1})}
. Plugging this bound into (
3.25
) we have: for
a\in[1,\nu-1]
,

(3.26)

I_{n,1}(a)\leq\tfrac{c_{5}\nu^{N}}{M^{\nu-1}}\sum_{\|\mathbb{r}\|=N}\frac{M^{\nu-a-1}N!}{\prod\limits_{s}r_{s}!}\cdot a^{r_{1}}b^{-2(N-r_{1})}\\
=\tfrac{c_{5}\nu^{N}}{M^{\nu-1}}\cdot M^{\nu-a-1}\sum_{\|\mathbb{r}\|=N}\frac{N!}{\prod\limits_{s}r_{s}!}\,a^{r_{1}}\prod_{s>a}b^{-2r_{s}}\\
\leq\tfrac{c_{5}\nu^{N}}{M^{\nu-1}}\cdot M^{\nu-a-1}\bigl(a+\nu b^{-2}\bigr)^{N}.

For
a=0
, instead of the bound (
3.23
) we get

\big|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]\big|^{n}\leq\nu^{N}\biggl(\sum_{\beta=1}^{\nu}\mathbb{I}(|\xi_{\beta}|\in[b_{0},\pi M])\,{\min}^{2}\bigl\{\tfrac{\pi}{|\xi_{\beta}|};b^{-1}\bigr\}\!\biggr)^{N}\\
=\nu^{N}\sum_{\|\mathbb{r}\|=N}\frac{N!}{r_{1}!\cdots r_{\nu}!}\prod_{\beta=1}^{\nu}\mathbb{I}(|\xi_{\beta}|\in[b_{0},\pi M])\,{\min}^{2r_{\beta}}\bigl\{\tfrac{\pi}{|\xi_{\beta}|};b^{-1}\bigr\},

\mathbb{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{\nu})
. And, analogously to (
3.26
), we obtain

(3.27)

I_{n,1}(0)\leq\tfrac{c_{6}\nu^{N}}{M^{\nu-1}}\cdot M^{\nu-2}b^{-2N}.

Why
M^{\nu-2}
? Because
\sigma(\mathbb{r})\leq\nu-1
, and if
\sigma(\mathbb{r})=\nu-1
, then we select
\mu
for which
r_{\mu}=0
.

Adding (

) and (

(3.28)

I_{n,1}\leq\tfrac{c_{7}\nu^{N}}{M}\biggl(b^{-2N}+\sum_{a=1}^{\nu-1}M^{-a+1}(a+\nu b^{-2})^{N}\biggr).

Let us simplify (

,

&\tfrac{N}{\log M}-\tfrac{\nu}{b^{2}},&&\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}\in(1+\tfrac{\nu}{b^{2}},\nu-1+\tfrac{\nu}{b^{2}}),\\
&\nu-1,&&\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}>\nu-1+\tfrac{\nu}{b^{2}}.\end{aligned}\right.

Consequently

\psi(a(\nu))=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\bigl(e^{O(b^{-2})}\bigr)^{N},&&\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}<1+\tfrac{\nu}{b^{2}},\\
&M\Bigl(\tfrac{Ne^{O(b^{-2})}}{e\log M}\Bigr)^{N},&&\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}\in(1+\tfrac{\nu}{b^{2}},\nu-1+\tfrac{\nu}{b^{2}}),\\
&M^{-\nu+2}\bigl((\nu-1)e^{O(b^{-2})}\bigr)^{N},&&\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}>\nu-1+\tfrac{\nu}{b^{2}}.\end{aligned}\right.

The sum in (
3.28
) is below

, and

can be chosen arbitrarily
large. So, for
M=e^{O(n)}\to\infty
and
b
sufficiently large, we have

(3.29)

I_{n,1}\leq c_{8}\cdot\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tfrac{\bigl(\nu e^{O(b^{-2})}\bigr)^{N}}{M},&&\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}<1,\\
&\Bigl(\tfrac{\nu Ne^{O(b^{-2})}}{e\log M}\Bigr)^{N},&&\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}\in(1,\nu-1),\\
&\tfrac{\bigl(\nu(\nu-1)e^{O(b^{-2})}\bigr)^{N}}{M^{\nu-1}},&&\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}>\nu-1.\end{aligned}\right.

(ii)
Suppose
\mathbb{x}\in D_{2}
. Let
I_{n,2}
denote the contribution of
D_{2}
to
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]
.
We switch from
x_{\alpha}
to
\xi_{\alpha}
for all but a single
x_{\mu}
such that
|\xi_{\mu}|=\min_{\alpha}|\xi_{\alpha}|
.
So, here the cube
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
is a finite disjoint union of subsets, and the choice of the new variables depends
on a subset in such a way that the condition
\sum_{a\in[\nu]}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}\geq\tfrac{\log^{2}n}{n}
implies that

\sum_{a\neq\mu}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}\geq\tfrac{(\nu-1)\log^{2}n}{\nu n}\geq\tfrac{\log^{2}N}{4N}
.

Analogously to
I_{n,1}
, by Lemma
3.3
we have

I_{n,2}\leq\tfrac{c_{1}\nu^{2N}}{M^{\nu-1}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\limits_{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu-1}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}\geq\tfrac{\log^{2}N}{4N}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda N}{\nu}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu-1}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}\Bigr)\,\prod_{\alpha=1}^{\nu-1}d\xi_{\alpha}.

Here, and below,
c_{j}
is independent of
b
and
\lambda=\lambda(b)
. Using spherical coordinates, and denoting
\zeta_{0}=\bigl(\tfrac{\lambda}{4\nu}\bigr)^{1/2}\log N
, we bound the last integral by

\displaystyle c_{2}\!\!\!\int\limits_{\rho\geq\frac{\log N}{\sqrt{4N}}}\!\!\!\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda N}{\nu}\rho^{2}\Bigr)\rho^{\nu-2}\,d\rho

\displaystyle=c_{3}\,(\lambda N)^{-(\nu-1)/2}\!\!\int\limits_{\zeta\geq\zeta_{0}}e^{-\zeta^{2}/2}\zeta^{\nu-2}\,d\zeta

\displaystyle\leq c_{4}\,\lambda^{-1}N^{-(\nu-1)/2}(\log N)^{\nu-3}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda\log^{2}N}{4\nu}\Bigr).

The last bound follows from integrating the LHS integral (call it
I
) by parts once, and bounding the residual integral by
I\cdot(\nu-3)\zeta_{0}^{-2}
. We conclude that

(3.30)

I_{n,2}\leq\tfrac{c_{5}\,\nu^{2N}}{\lambda M^{\nu-1}}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda\log^{2}N}{4\nu}\Bigr).

Adding (
3.29
) and (
3.30
) we complete the proof of Lemma
3.4
.
∎

(III)
So, it remains to sharply evaluate an asymptotic contribution to the integral

\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_{n}\bigr]=\!\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\nu}}\!\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}}\!\!\!\mathbb{E}^{n}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]d\mathbb{x},

that comes from
C:=[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}\setminus D
, or more explicitly from
\mathbb{x}
’s in the (reduced) cube
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
with
\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}<n^{-1}\log^{2}n
,
where
\xi_{\alpha}=\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})
are uniquely defined by

\xi_{\alpha}=Mz_{\alpha},\quad z_{\alpha}=y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})\pi,\quad|z_{\alpha}|=\min_{\text{even }k}|y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k\pi|.

That is,
\{k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})\}
is an even tuple. To begin,

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\bigl(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})\pi\bigr)^{2}=M^{-2}\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}<M^{-2}n^{-1}\log^{2}n

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\Longrightarrow\sum_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})=0\Longrightarrow\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}=0,

if
n
is large enough
. Indeed
\sum_{\alpha}y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})\equiv 0
; so if
\sum_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})\neq 0
, then

\Big|\sum_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})\Big|\geq 2
, and for large
n
we have a contradiction:

4\pi^{2}\leq\Bigl|\sum_{\alpha}(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})\pi)\Bigr|^{2}\leq\nu\sum_{\alpha}(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})\pi)^{2}\leq\tfrac{\nu\log^{2}n}{M^{2}n}.

Conversely, if
\{y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})\}
and an even tuple

\mathbb{k}=\{k_{\alpha}\}
,
\bigl(\sum_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}=0\bigr)
, satisfy
\sum_{\alpha}\bigl(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}\pi\bigr)^{2}<M^{-2}n^{-1}\log^{2}n
,
then
\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}\leq n^{-1}\log^{2}n
. Moreover,
\xi_{\alpha}=M(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}\pi)
, if
n
is large. Indeed,
\xi_{\alpha}=M(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}^{\prime}\pi)
, where
|y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}^{\prime}\pi)|=\min_{\text{even }\kappa}|y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\kappa\pi|
, and the conditions

\sum_{\alpha}\bigl(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}\pi\bigr)^{2},\quad\sum_{\alpha}\bigl(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}^{\prime}\pi\bigr)^{2}<M^{-2}n^{-1}\log^{2}n,

combined with the triangle inequality, imply that
\|\mathbb{k}-\mathbb{k}^{\prime}\|\leq\tfrac{2\log n}{\pi Mn^{1/2}}
.

Therefore,
C=\cup_{\mathbb{k}}C_{\mathbb{k}}
where
\mathbb{k}=\{k_{\alpha}\}
, with even
k_{\alpha}
adding up to
0
, and

C_{\mathbb{k}}:=\Bigl\{\mathbb{x}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}:\sum_{\alpha}\bigl(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}\pi\bigr)^{2}<M^{-2}n^{-1}\log^{2}n\Bigr\}.

And, for large
n
, by the triangle inequality
C_{\mathbb{k}_{1}}\cap\,C_{\mathbb{k}_{2}}=\emptyset
if
\mathbb{k}_{1}\neq\mathbb{k}_{2}
.

Consider the line
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
given by
y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})=k_{\alpha}\pi
,
(\alpha\in[\nu])
, for any such tuple
\mathbb{k}
.
(In the part
(I)
we enumerated all
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
that contain interior points of
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
.) A generic
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
is given by its parametric equation

(3.31)

x_{\alpha}(t)=\nu^{-1}(t+k_{\alpha}\pi),\quad\alpha\in[\nu].

The lines are parallel to each other, running in the direction of
\mathbb{e}=(1,\dots,1)
, and crossing at
90
degrees each of the (hyper)planes
\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}x_{\alpha}=t
, with
\mathbb{x}(t):=\{x_{\alpha}(t)\}_{\alpha\in[\nu]}
being the
intersection point. Using

x_{\alpha}=\nu^{-1}\Bigl(\sum_{\beta}x_{\beta}+y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})\Bigr),

and (
3.31
), we obtain: if
\mathbb{x}=\{x_{\alpha}\}\in C_{\mathbb{k}}
then, with
t:=\sum_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}
,

(3.32)

\displaystyle x_{\alpha}-x_{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}\pi}{\nu}=\tfrac{\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})}{M\nu}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\|\mathbb{x}-\mathbb{x}(t)\|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{(M\nu)^{2}}\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{x})\leq\tfrac{\log^{2}n}{(M\nu)^{2}n}.

Conversely, if
t=\sum_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}
, then
\|\mathbb{x}-\mathbb{x}(t)\|^{2}\leq\tfrac{\log^{2}n}{(M\nu)^{2}n}
implies that
\mathbb{x}\in C_{\mathbb{k}}
.

Consider a
\nu
-dimensional cylinder
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{k}}
enclosing the line
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
, such that each of its cross-sections formed by planes
\sum_{a}x_{\alpha}=t

orthogonal
to
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
is a
(\nu-1)
-dimensional (hyper)sphere in that plane, of radius
r_{n}:=(M\nu)^{-1}n^{-1/2}\log n
, which is centered at the common point
\mathbb{x}(t)
of the plane and the line
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
. We denote
the sphere
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{x}(t),r_{n})
.

The above discussion
means the following.
If
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
contains interior points of
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
,
then
C_{\mathbb{k}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{k}}\cap[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
. And shortly we will see that the contribution of
C_{\mathbb{k}}
to
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]
is
of exact order

L(\mathbb{k})\nu^{n}M^{-\nu+1}n^{-\tfrac{\nu-1}{2}},

where
L(\mathbb{k})\geq 2\pi\nu^{-1/2}
is the length of line segment of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
in
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
, see (
3.4
).

In the part
(I)
we proved that if
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
contains no interior points of
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
, then
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
may only touch
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
at a single point
\mathbb{x}_{\mathbb{k}}=\{x_{\mathbb{k},\alpha}\}
, such that
x_{\mathbb{k},\alpha_{1}}=\pi
and
x_{\mathbb{k},\alpha_{2}}=-\pi
for some
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in[\nu]
. In this case,
C_{\mathbb{k}}
is the union
of the parallel line segments
\mathbb{x}(t)=\tilde{\mathbb{x}}_{\mathbb{k}}+t\mathbb{e}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
, where
\sum_{\alpha}\tilde{\mathbb{x}}_{\mathbb{k},\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha}x_{\mathbb{k},\alpha}
, and
\|\tilde{\mathbb{x}}_{\mathbb{k}}-\mathbb{x}_{\mathbb{k}}\|\leq r_{n}
. Using
x_{\mathbb{k},\alpha_{1}}=\pi
and
x_{\mathbb{k},\alpha_{2}}=-\pi
we obtain that
t\in[x_{\mathbb{k},\alpha_{2}}-\tilde{x}_{\mathbb{k},\alpha_{2}},x_{\mathbb{k},\alpha_{1}}-\tilde{x}_{\mathbb{k},\alpha_{1}}]
. Since
|y|
is convex, we see then that
|t|\leq r_{n}
.
So,
C_{\mathbb{k}}
is enclosed in a sub-cylinder of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{k}}
sandwiched between two parallel cross-sections, for
t_{1}=\sum_{\alpha}x_{\mathbb{k},\alpha}-r_{n}
and
t_{2}=\sum_{\alpha}x_{\mathbb{k},\alpha}+r_{n}
, that are at distance
O(r_{n})
from each other.
Consequently, contribution of
C_{\mathbb{k}}
to
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]
is at most of order
r_{n}\nu^{n}M^{-\nu+1}n^{-\tfrac{\nu-1}{2}}
. And we know that the total number of the points
\mathbb{x}_{\mathbb{k}}
is
\nu(2\nu-1)^{\nu}
, at most. So,
the contribution to
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]
from the sets
C_{\mathbb{k}}
with the lines

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
merely touching the cube
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
is relatively negligible as
n\to\infty
.
Therefore, we focus on
C_{\mathbb{k}}
with lines
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
enumerated in
(I)
.

For
\mathbb{x}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{x}(t),r_{n})
, we set
\xi_{\alpha}=M(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-k_{\alpha}\pi)=M\nu(x_{\alpha}-x_{\alpha}(t))
, so that
\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}\leq n^{-1}\log^{2}n
and
\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}=0
. Then

\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]=\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\mathbb{E}\bigl[e^{iy_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})X}\bigr]=\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\mathbb{E}\bigl[e^{i\xi_{\alpha}M^{-1}X}\bigr]\\
=\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\Bigl[1+i\tfrac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{M}\xi_{\alpha}-\tfrac{\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]}{2M^{2}}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}+O\bigl(|\xi_{\alpha}|^{3}\tfrac{\mathbb{E}[X^{3}]}{M^{3}}\bigr)\Bigr]\\
=\nu-\tfrac{\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]}{2M^{2}}\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}+O\Bigl(\tfrac{\mathbb{E}[X^{3}]}{M^{3}}\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}|\xi_{\alpha}|^{3}\Bigr)\\
=\nu\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{c_{\scriptscriptstyle M}}{2\nu}\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}+O(n^{-3/2}\log^{3}n)\Bigr)\\
=\nu\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{c_{\scriptscriptstyle M}}{2\nu}(M\nu)^{2}\|\mathbb{x}-\mathbb{x}(t)\|^{2}+O(n^{-3/2}\log^{3}n)\Bigr);

here
c_{\scriptscriptstyle M}=\tfrac{\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]}{M^{2}}
,
t=\sum_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}
. So, uniformly for
\mathbb{x}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{x}(t),r_{n})
, we have

(3.33)

\mathbb{E}^{n}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\bigr]=\nu^{n}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{c_{\scriptscriptstyle M}n}{2\nu}(M\nu)^{2}\|\mathbb{x}-\mathbb{x}(t)\|^{2}+O(n^{-1/2}\log^{3}n)\Bigr).

With
\rho_{n}:=n^{-1/2}\log n\to 0
, admissibility issue arises when
|t-t_{j}|=O(M^{-1}\rho_{n})
, where
t_{1}:=-\pi(\nu+\min k_{\alpha})
and
t_{2}:=\pi(\nu-\max k_{\alpha})
are the endpoints of
t
’s range.
Ignoring this lack of complete homogeneity, and using orthogonality of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{x}(t),r_{n})
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{k}}
, we replace the contribution of
C_{\mathbb{k}}
to the integral representing
E[Z_{n}]
with the integral of the RHS in (
3.33
) over the sphere
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{x}(t),r_{n})
, times
C_{\mathbb{k}}
’s length

L(\mathbb{k}):=\|\mathbb{x}(t_{2})-\mathbb{x}(t_{1})\|=\nu^{-1/2}\pi\bigl(2\nu+\min_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}-\max_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}\bigr)\geq 2\pi\nu^{-1/2},

see (
3.4
). So, introducing
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{0},\rho)
, the
(\nu-1)
-dimensional sphere of radius
\rho
, centered at the origin
\mathbb{0}\in E^{\nu-1}
, and using

\text{Volume}\bigl(\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{0},\rho)\bigr)=\tfrac{\pi^{\frac{\nu-1}{2}}\rho^{\nu-1}}{\Gamma(\tfrac{\nu-1}{2}+1)},

we obtain: within factor
1+o(1)
, contribution of
C_{\mathbb{k}}
to

\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]
is

\frac{\nu^{n}L(\mathbb{k})}{(2\pi)^{\nu}(M\nu)^{\nu-1}}\int_{\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{0},\rho_{n})}e^{-\frac{c_{M}n}{2\nu}\rho^{2}}\,dV\\
=\frac{\nu^{n}L(\mathbb{k})}{(2\pi)^{\nu}(M\nu)^{\nu-1}}\cdot\frac{\pi^{\tfrac{\nu-1}{2}}\tfrac{\nu-1}{2}}{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{\nu-1}{2}+1\bigr)}\bigl(\tfrac{2\nu}{c_{M}n}\bigr)^{\tfrac{\nu-1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\tfrac{c_{M}n\rho_{n}^{2}}{\nu}}e^{-z}z^{\tfrac{\nu-1}{2}-1}\,dz\\
=(1+o(1))\frac{\nu^{n}}{M^{\nu-1}}\cdot\frac{L(\mathbb{k})}{2\pi(2\pi\nu c_{\scriptscriptstyle M}n)^{\tfrac{\nu-1}{2}}}.

Indeed, the second line integral is asymptotic to
\Gamma(\tfrac{\nu-1}{2})
, and for the third line we used
\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)
. (This estimate confirms our earlier claim concerning the contribution of
every such
C_{\mathbb{k}}
to
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]
.)
Combining the estimate with Lemmas
3.2
and
3.4
, we have:

(3.34)

\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_{n}\bigr]=(1+o(1))\frac{\nu^{n}}{M^{\nu-1}}\cdot\frac{\nu^{\nu-3/2}}{(2\pi\nu c_{\scriptscriptstyle M}n)^{\frac{\nu-1}{2}}}+O(\mathcal{R}_{n}),\\

where (with
N=n/2
)

(3.35)

\mathcal{R}_{n}=\tfrac{\nu^{n}}{M^{\nu-1}}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda\log^{2}N}{4\nu}\Bigr)\\
+\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tfrac{\bigl(\nu e^{O(b^{-2})}\bigr)^{N}}{M},&&\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}<1,\\
&\Bigl(\tfrac{\nu Ne^{O(b^{-2})}}{e\log M}\Bigr)^{N},&&\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}\in(1,\nu-1),\\
&\tfrac{\bigl(\nu(\nu-1)e^{O(b^{-2})}\bigr)^{N}}{M^{\nu-1}},&&\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}>\nu-1.\end{aligned}\right.\\

We will refer to this vertical stack as top, middle, and bottom remainders.

(IV)
Let us have a close look at the estimate above.

(a)

\nu=3
. Suppose that
\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}\in\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\log 3},\infty\bigr)\setminus\{1,2\}
. Then
\tfrac{3^{2N}}{M^{2}}
grows exponentially fast, hence

\tfrac{3^{2N}}{M^{2}}\gg\tfrac{\bigl(3e^{O(b^{-2})}\bigr)^{N}}{M}
(top remainder term in (
3.35
)), for
b
chosen sufficiently large.
So, if
\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}\in\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\log 3},1\bigr)
, then the above formula for
\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_{n}\bigr]
implies that

(3.36)

\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_{n}\bigr]=(1+o(1))\frac{\nu^{n}}{M^{\nu-1}}\cdot\frac{\nu^{\nu-3/2}}{(2\pi\nu c_{\scriptscriptstyle M}n)^{\frac{\nu-1}{2}}}\Big|_{\nu=3}\to\infty.

Let
\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}\in(1,2)
; (
3.36
) continues to hold for
b
large enough, provided that

\tfrac{3^{2N}}{M^{2}}\gg\Bigl(\tfrac{\nu Ne^{O(b^{-2})}}{e\log M}\Bigr)^{N},

middle remainder in (
3.35
). This inequality holds if
\rho:=\lim\tfrac{\log M}{N}\in(0.5,1)
satisfies
(3e^{-\rho})^{2}>\tfrac{3}{e\rho}
. And it is indeed so, since

\min\{9\tau e^{-2\tau}:\,\tau\in[1/2,1]\}=\tfrac{9}{e^{2}}>\tfrac{3}{e}.

Let
\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}\in(2,\infty)
; (
3.36
) again holds for
b
large enough, since
3^{n}\gg 6^{N}
, see bottom remainder in (
3.35
). On the other hand, if
\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}<\tfrac{1}{\log 3}
, then
\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}<1
, and using top remainder
from (
3.35
) we see that
\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_{n}\bigr]=O\Bigl(\tfrac{(3e^{O(b^{-2})})^{N}}{M}\Bigr)\to 0
, for
b
sufficiently large.

(b)
Let now
\nu>3
. (
3.36
) holds if
\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}>\nu-1
. Indeed, by (
3.35
),

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{R}_{n}=\tfrac{\nu^{2N}}{M^{\nu-1}}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda\log^{2}N}{4\nu}\Bigr)+\tfrac{\bigl(\nu(\nu-1)e^{O(b^{-2})}\bigr)^{N}}{M^{\nu-1}},

\displaystyle\tfrac{\nu^{2N}}{M^{\nu-1}}=\exp\Bigl[2\log M\cdot\log\nu\cdot\bigl(\tfrac{N}{\log M}-\tfrac{\nu-1}{2\log\nu}\bigr)\Bigr]\to\infty,

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,\,\frac{\tfrac{\nu^{2N}}{M^{\nu-1}}}{\tfrac{\bigl(\nu(\nu-1)e^{O(b^{-2})}\bigr)^{N}}{M^{\nu-1}}}=\bigl(\tfrac{\nu}{\nu-1}e^{O(b^{-2})}\bigr)^{N}\to\infty,

if
b
is sufficiently large, since
\tfrac{\nu-1}{2\log\nu}<\nu-1
.

Consider now
\tfrac{\nu-1}{2\log\nu}<\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}<\nu-1
. Since
\min_{\nu>3}\tfrac{\nu-1}{2\log\nu}=\tfrac{4-1}{2\log 4}>1

(notice that
\tfrac{3-1}{2\log 3}<1
), we use middle remainder from (
3.35
) to obtain

\mathcal{R}_{n}=\tfrac{\nu^{2N}}{M^{\nu-1}}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda\log^{2}N}{4\nu}\Bigr)+\Bigl(\tfrac{\nu Ne^{O(b^{-2})}}{e\log M}\Bigr)^{N}.

Therefore

(3.37)

\frac{\mathcal{R}_{n}}{\tfrac{\nu^{n}}{M^{\nu-1}}}=\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda\log^{2}N}{4\nu}\Bigr)+\Bigl(\tfrac{e^{(\nu-1)\eta_{n}}+O(b^{-2})}{\nu e\eta_{n}}\Bigr)^{N},\quad\eta_{n}:=\tfrac{\log M}{N},

i.e.
\lim\eta_{n}\in\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\nu-1},\tfrac{2\log\nu}{\nu-1}\bigr)
. The function
f_{\nu}(\eta):=\tfrac{e^{(\nu-1)\eta}}{\nu e\eta}
increases with
\eta>(\nu-1)^{-1}
, and

f_{\nu}((\nu-1)^{-1})=1-\tfrac{1}{\nu}<1,\quad f_{\nu}\bigl(\tfrac{2\log\nu}{\nu-1}\bigr)=\tfrac{\nu(\nu-1)}{2e\log\nu}>1,\,\,\forall\nu>3.

So, there exists a unique root
\eta(\nu)\in\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\nu-1},\tfrac{2\log\nu}{\nu-1}\bigr)
of
f_{\nu}(\eta)-1
. Using (
3.37
), we conclude that for
\nu>3
the equation
(
3.36
) holds for
\tfrac{1}{\eta(\nu)}<\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}<\nu-1
, if
b
is chosen large enough.

Finally, like the case
\nu=3
,
\lim\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]=0
if
\lim\tfrac{N}{\log M}<\tfrac{1}{\log\nu}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

∎

4.
Second order moment of the number of perfect partitions

From Theorem
3.1
we know that, for
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}<\tfrac{2}{\log\nu}
, we have
\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_{n}\bigr]\to 0
, so that
\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}>0)\leq\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]\to 0
. Furthermore, we stated the conditions on
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}
under which
\lim\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]=\infty
.
These conditions make it plausible, but certainly do not imply, that
\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}>0)
approaches
1
, or even that
\lim\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}>0)>0
.
In general, if the first order moment grows very fast, it may well portend that the standard deviation grows even faster.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}>\tfrac{2(\nu-1)}{\log[(1-2\nu^{-2})^{-1}]}
.
Then with the limiting probability
\gtrsim(1+\nu^{2})^{-1}
,

Z_{n}
is of order
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]
, i.e. the number of perfect partitions is exponentially large.

Note.
The limiting probability is certainly below

\tfrac{1}{\nu}\!=\!\lim\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in[n]}X_{j}\!\!\equiv 0(\text{mod }\nu)\Bigr).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

(4.1)

\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{2}]=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2\nu}}\!\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}^{n}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\overline{f(\mathbb{x}^{\prime},X)}\bigr]\,\,d\mathbb{x}d\mathbb{x}^{\prime}.

Here, with
\phi(y)=\mathbb{E}[e^{iyX}]
, we have

(4.2)

\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\overline{f(\mathbb{x}^{\prime},X)}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\biggl[\biggl(\sum_{\alpha\in[\nu]}\exp(iy_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})X)\biggr)\biggl(\sum_{\beta\in[\nu]}\exp(-iy_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})X)\biggr)\biggl]\\
=\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in[\nu]}\exp\bigl(i(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))X)\biggr]=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in[\nu]}\phi\bigl(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\bigr).

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\nu^{-2})
, and introduce

D_{\varepsilon}=\Bigl\{\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}:\min_{\alpha,\beta\in[\nu]}\big|\phi(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))\big|\leq 1-\varepsilon\nu^{2}\Bigr\}.

For
(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\in D_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in[\nu]}|\phi\bigl(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\bigr)|\leq(1-\varepsilon\nu^{2})+\nu^{2}-1=\nu^{2}(1-\varepsilon).

So,
\mathcal{E}[Z_{n}^{2};D_{\varepsilon}]
, the contribution of
D_{\varepsilon}
to
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{2}]
, is at most
\nu^{2n}(1-\varepsilon)^{n}
.

Let
(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\in D_{\varepsilon}^{c}
, so that
\big|\phi(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))\big|>1-\varepsilon\nu^{2}
for all
\alpha,\,\beta
. If

y=y_{\alpha,\beta}:=y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\neq k\pi
,
k
even, then

|\phi(y)|=\bigg|\tfrac{e^{iy}(e^{iMy}-1)}{M(e^{iy}-1)}\bigg|=\tfrac{\bigl(1-\cos(My)\bigr)^{1/2}}{M(1-\cos y)^{1/2}}=\bigg|\tfrac{\sin(My/2)}{M\sin(y/2)}\biggr|>1-\varepsilon\nu^{2}.\\

Let
k_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\pi
be the even multiple of
\pi
closest to
y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})
, and set

z_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})=y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})-k_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\pi
; then
|z_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})|\leq\pi
, and

\phi(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))=\phi(z_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))
. Therefore

1-\varepsilon\nu^{2}\leq|\phi(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))|=|\phi(z_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))|\\
\leq\frac{1}{M|\sin(z_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})/2)|}\leq\tfrac{\pi}{M|z_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})|},

whence

|y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})-k_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\pi|=|z_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})|\leq\tfrac{\pi}{M(1-\varepsilon\nu^{2})}=O(M^{-1}).

Since
\sum_{\alpha}y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})=\sum_{\beta}y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})=0
, we obtain then

\displaystyle y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\tfrac{\pi}{\nu}k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})=O(M^{-1}),\quad k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime}):=\sum_{\beta}k_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})-\tfrac{\pi}{\nu}\kappa_{\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})=O(M^{-1}),\quad\kappa_{\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime}):=\sum_{\alpha}k_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime}).

Since
\nu
is fixed and
M\to\infty
,
\tfrac{\pi}{\nu}k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})
(
\tfrac{\pi}{\nu}\kappa_{\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})
resp.) is again an even multiple of
\tfrac{\pi}{\nu}
closest to
y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})
(
y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})
resp.), whence

k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})
(
\kappa_{\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})
resp.) depends only on
\mathbb{x}
(on
\mathbb{x}^{\prime}
resp.).
Combining the last three equations, and again using
\sum_{\alpha}y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})=0
,
\sum_{\beta}y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tfrac{\pi}{\nu}\cdot(k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\kappa_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))=\pi k_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})+O(M^{-1}),

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})=O(M^{-1}),\quad\sum_{\beta}\kappa_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})=O(M^{-1}),

implying that for
M
large

k_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})=\tfrac{1}{\nu}(k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\kappa_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})),\quad\sum_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})=\sum_{\beta}\kappa_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})=0.

(In particular, all
\nu^{2}
differences
k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\kappa_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})
are divisible by
2\nu
.) The first identity above implies that
z(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})=M^{-1}\bigl(\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\eta_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\bigr)
, where

\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x}):=M\bigl(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\tfrac{\pi}{\nu}k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})\bigr),\quad\eta_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime}):=M\bigl(y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})-\tfrac{\pi}{\nu}k_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\bigr),

and by the second identity,
\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})=0
,
\sum_{\beta}\eta_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})=0
. So,

\phi(z_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))=\phi\bigl(M^{-1}(\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\eta_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))\bigr).

and
|\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\eta_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})|
is uniformly bounded for
(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\in D_{\varepsilon}^{c}
.
Now, for each
b>0
, there exists
\lambda=\lambda(b)>0
such that
u^{-1}|\sin u|\in[1-2\lambda u^{2},1-\lambda^{2}]
for
u\in(0,b]
.
Since

|\phi(z)|=\biggl|\tfrac{\sin(Mz/2)}{Mz/2}\biggr|\cdot\biggl|\tfrac{z/2}{\sin(z/2)}\biggr|,

we see then that, for
(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\in D_{\varepsilon}^{c}
and an absolute constant
\lambda^{\prime}>0
,

(4.3)

\sum_{\alpha,\beta}|\phi\bigl(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\bigr)|\leq\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\Bigl(1-\lambda^{\prime}(\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\eta_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))^{2}\Bigr)\\
\leq\nu^{2}\exp\biggl(-\tfrac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\nu^{2}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\eta_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))^{2}\biggr)\\
=\nu^{2}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\nu^{2}}\bigl(\|\boldsymbol{\xi}(\mathbb{x})\|^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{\eta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\|^{2}\bigr)\Bigr),

since
\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})=\sum_{\beta}\eta_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})=0
.

Let
\mathcal{E}[Z_{n}^{2};D_{\varepsilon}^{c}]
be the contribution to
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{2}]
from
(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\in D_{\varepsilon}^{c}
such that

\max\bigl\{\|\boldsymbol{\xi}(\mathbb{x})\|,\,\|\boldsymbol{\eta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\|\bigr\}\geq\tfrac{\log n}{n^{1/2}}.

Using (
4.3
), similarly to the part
(ii)
of the proof of Lemma
3.4
, we obtain

\mathcal{E}[Z_{n}^{2};D_{\varepsilon}^{c}]\leq c_{1}(\nu)\tfrac{\nu^{2n}}{\lambda^{\prime}M^{2(\nu-1)}}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda^{\prime}\log^{2}n}{\nu^{2}}\Bigr).

Consequently

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[Z_{n}^{2};D_{\varepsilon}]+\mathcal{E}[Z_{n}^{2};D_{\varepsilon}^{c}]

\displaystyle=O\biggl(\nu^{2n}(1-\varepsilon)^{n}+\tfrac{\nu^{2n}}{\lambda^{\prime}M^{2(\nu-1)}}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda^{\prime}\log^{2}n}{\nu^{2}}\Bigr)\biggr)

\displaystyle=O\biggl(\tfrac{\nu^{2n}}{\lambda^{\prime}M^{2(\nu-1)}}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{\lambda^{\prime}\log^{2}n}{\nu^{2}}\Bigr)\biggr),

provided that

(4.5)

\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}>\frac{2(\nu-1)}{\log\tfrac{1}{1-\nu^{-2}}}.

It remains to sharply estimate
\mathcal{E}[Z_{n}^{2};\mathcal{C}]
, the contribution to
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{2}]
from

\mathcal{C}^{*}:=\Bigl\{(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\in D_{\varepsilon}^{c}:\max\bigl\{\|\boldsymbol{\xi}(\mathbb{x})\|,\,\|\boldsymbol{\eta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\|\bigr\}\leq\tfrac{\log n}{n^{1/2}}\Bigr\}.

Analogously to
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]
, (see the part
(III)
of the proof of Theorem
3.1
),
\mathcal{C}
is the disjoint union of
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\mathbb{k},\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
,
over all pairs
\{\mathbb{k},\boldsymbol{\kappa}\}
of
\nu
-long even tuples
\mathbb{k}=\{k_{\alpha}\}
,
\boldsymbol{\kappa}=\{\kappa_{\beta}\}
, with
\sum_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta}\kappa_{\beta}=0
,
k_{\alpha}-\kappa_{\beta}\equiv 0(\text{mod }2\nu)
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\mathbb{k},\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=\Bigl\{(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\in D_{\varepsilon}^{c}:

\displaystyle\max\bigl\{\|\boldsymbol{\xi}(\mathbb{x})\|,\|\boldsymbol{\eta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\|\bigr\}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{\log n}{n^{1/2}},\,k_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})\!=k_{\alpha},\,\kappa_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\!=\!\kappa_{\beta}\Bigr\}.

Geometrically,
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\mathbb{k},\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
is the Cartesian product of two thin
\nu
-dimensional cylinders, enclosing respectively the line
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mathbb{k}}
and the line
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
,
given by the parametric equations

(4.6)

x_{\alpha}(t)=\nu^{-1}\bigl(t+\tfrac{\pi}{\nu}k_{\alpha}\bigr),\,\alpha\in[\nu],\quad x^{\prime}_{\beta}(t^{\prime})=\nu^{-1}\bigl(t^{\prime}+\tfrac{\pi}{\nu}\kappa_{\beta}\bigr),\,\beta\in[\nu].

As in the case of
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]
, the dominant contributors to
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{2}]
are the pairs
\{\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mathbb{k}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\}
that contain interior points of their respective cubes
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
. However this time we need to consider only the pairs for which the differences
k_{\alpha}-\kappa_{\beta}
are all divisible by
2\nu
. Each of the cross-sections of the two cylinders formed by planes
\sum_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}=t
,
and
\sum_{\beta}x^{\prime}_{\beta}=t^{\prime}
respectively, is the
(\nu-1)
-dimensional sphere of radius
r_{n}:=(M\nu)^{-1}n^{-1/2}\log n
, coming from

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{x}-\mathbb{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{(M\nu)^{2}}\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{x})\leq\tfrac{\log^{2}n}{(M\nu)^{2}n},

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{x}^{\prime}-\mathbb{x}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{(M\nu)^{2}}\sum_{\beta}\eta_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\leq\tfrac{\log^{2}n}{(M\nu)^{2}n}.

For
(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\mathbb{k},\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
, we have

\phi(y_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-y_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))=\phi(z_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))=\phi\bigl(M^{-1}(\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\eta_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))\bigr)\\
=1+i\tfrac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{M}(\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\eta_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))-\tfrac{\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]}{2M^{2}}(\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\eta_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime}))^{2}+O\bigl(|\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})-\eta_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})|^{3}\bigr).

Summing this equation over
\alpha,\beta\in[\nu]
, and using
\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{x})=0
,
\sum_{\beta}\eta_{\beta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})=0
, we transform (
4.2
) into

\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\overline{f(\mathbb{x}^{\prime},X)}\bigr]=\nu^{2}-\tfrac{\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]}{2M^{2}}\bigl(\|\boldsymbol{\xi}(\mathbb{x})\|^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{\eta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\|^{2}\bigr)\\
\quad+O\bigl(\|\boldsymbol{\xi}(\mathbb{x})\|^{3}+\|\boldsymbol{\eta}(\mathbb{x}^{\prime})\|^{3}\bigr)\\
=\nu^{2}\Bigl(1-\tfrac{M^{2}c_{M}}{2}\bigl(\|\mathbb{x}-\mathbb{x}(t)\|^{2}+\|\mathbb{x}^{\prime}-\mathbb{x}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})\|^{2}\bigr)\\
+O\bigl(M^{3}\bigl(\|\mathbb{x}-\mathbb{x}(t)\|^{3}+\|\mathbb{x}^{\prime}-\mathbb{x}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})\|^{3}\bigr)\Bigr)\\
=\nu^{2}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{M^{2}c_{M}}{2}\bigl(\|\mathbb{x}-\mathbb{x}(t)\|^{2}+\|\mathbb{x}^{\prime}-\mathbb{x}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})\|^{2}\bigr)+O(n^{-3/2}\log^{3}n)\Bigr).

So

\mathbb{E}^{n}\bigl[f(\mathbb{x},X)\overline{f(\mathbb{x}^{\prime},X)}\bigr]\\
=\nu^{2n}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{M^{2}nc_{M}}{2}\bigl(\|\mathbb{x}-\mathbb{x}(t)\|^{2}+\|\mathbb{x}^{\prime}-\mathbb{x}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})\|^{2}\bigr)+O(n^{-1/2}\log^{3}n)\Bigr).

So
\mathcal{E}\bigl[Z_{n}^{2};\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\mathbb{k},\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\bigr]
, the contribution of
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\mathbb{k},\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
to
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{2}]
, is given by

\mathcal{E}\bigl[Z_{n}^{2};\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\mathbb{k},\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\bigr]=(1+o(1))\tfrac{\nu^{2n}L^{*}(\mathbb{k})L^{*}(\boldsymbol{\kappa})}{(2\pi)^{2\nu}(M\nu)^{2(\nu-1)}}\biggl(\int_{\rho\geq 0}e^{-\tfrac{c_{M}n}{2\nu}\rho^{2}}\,dV(\rho)\biggr)^{2},

V(\rho)
being the volume of
(\nu-1)
-dimensional sphere of radius
\rho
. Here
L^{*}(\mathbb{k})
and

L^{*}(\boldsymbol{\kappa})
are the lengths of segments of
\mathcal{L}^{*}(\mathbb{k})
and
\mathcal{L}^{*}(\boldsymbol{\kappa})
within the respective cubes
[-\pi,\pi]^{\nu}
. Summing over all admissible pairs
\{\mathbb{k},\boldsymbol{\kappa}\}
, we have

\mathcal{E}\bigl[Z_{n}^{2};\mathcal{C}^{*}\bigr]\!=\!(1+o(1))\biggl(\!\tfrac{\nu^{n}}{(2\pi)^{\nu}(M\nu)^{\nu-1}}\int_{\rho\geq 0}e^{-\tfrac{c_{M}n}{2\nu}\rho^{2}}\,dV(\rho)\!\biggr)^{2}\sum_{\{\mathbb{k},\boldsymbol{\kappa}\}}\!L^{*}(\mathbb{k})L^{*}(\boldsymbol{\kappa}).

We upper bound the last product by
(L^{*})^{2}
,
L^{*}:=\sum_{\mathbb{k}}L^{*}(\mathbb{k})
, thus replacing the condition that
all
k_{\alpha}-\kappa_{\beta}
are divisible by
2\nu
with the weaker condition that
marginally
all the differences
k_{\alpha}-k_{\alpha^{\prime}}
and all the differences
\kappa_{\beta}-\kappa_{\beta^{\prime}}
are divisible by
2\nu
.

By (
4.6
), for the interior segment of
\mathcal{L}^{*}(\mathbb{k})
we have

-\pi\bigl(\nu+\tfrac{\min k_{\alpha}}{\nu}\bigr)<t<\pi\bigl(\nu-\tfrac{\max k_{\alpha}}{\nu}\bigr).

So,

L^{*}(\mathbb{k})=\pi d^{*}(\mathbb{k})\nu^{-1/2},\quad d^{*}(\mathbb{k}):=2\nu-\tfrac{r}{\nu},\,\,r:=\max k_{\alpha}-\min k_{\alpha},

and
d^{*}(\mathbb{k})>0
if either
k_{\alpha}\equiv 0
, or
\tfrac{r}{2\nu}\in[\nu-1]
. Consequently

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbb{k}}d^{*}(\mathbb{k})

\displaystyle=2\nu(1+\mathcal{M}_{1}^{*})-\tfrac{1}{\nu}\mathcal{M}_{2}^{*},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tfrac{r}{2\nu}\in[\nu-1]}\mathcal{N}^{*}(r),\quad\mathcal{M}_{2}^{*}=\sum_{\tfrac{r}{2\nu}\in[\nu-1]}r\mathcal{N}^{*}(r),

Here, following closely the line enumeration in the part
(I)
of the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\mathcal{N}^{*}(r)=\sum_{\text{even }a\leq 0}[\zeta^{-a\nu}]\mathcal{F}^{*}_{r}(\zeta),\quad\tfrac{r}{2\nu}\in[\nu-1],

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{*}_{r}(\zeta)=\bigl(\tfrac{1-\zeta^{r+2\nu}}{1-\zeta^{2\nu}}\bigr)^{\nu}-\bigl(\tfrac{1-\zeta^{r}}{1-\zeta^{2\nu}}\bigr)^{\nu}-\bigl(\tfrac{\zeta^{2\nu}-\zeta^{r+2\nu}}{1-\zeta^{2\nu}}\bigr)^{\nu}+\bigl(\tfrac{\zeta^{2\nu}-\zeta^{r}}{1-\zeta^{2\nu}}\bigr)^{\nu}.

So

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\text{even }a\leq 0}[\zeta^{-a\nu}]\sum_{\tfrac{r}{2\nu}\in[\nu-1]}\mathcal{F}^{*}_{r}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\text{even }a\leq 0}[\zeta^{-a\nu}]\Bigl(\bigl(\tfrac{1-\zeta^{2\nu^{2}}}{1-\zeta^{2\nu}}\bigr)^{\nu}-\bigl(\tfrac{\zeta^{2\nu}-\zeta^{2\nu^{2}}}{1-\zeta^{2\nu}}\bigr)^{\nu}-1\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\geq 0}[t^{\alpha}]\Bigl[\bigl(\tfrac{1-t^{\nu}}{1-t}\bigr)^{\nu}-\bigl(\tfrac{t-t^{\nu}}{1-t}\bigr)^{\nu}-1\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 1}\Bigl[\bigl(\tfrac{1-t^{\nu}}{1-t}\bigr)^{\nu}-\bigl(\tfrac{t-t^{\nu}}{1-t}\bigr)^{\nu}-1\Bigr]=\nu^{\nu}-(\nu-1)^{\nu}-1.

Next, with a bit of telescoping again,

\sum_{\tfrac{r}{2\nu}\in[\nu-1]}r\mathcal{F}^{*}_{r}(\zeta)=2\nu(\nu-1)\bigl(\tfrac{1-\zeta^{2\nu^{2}}}{1-\zeta^{2\nu}}\bigr)^{\nu}\\
-(2\nu(\nu-1)\zeta^{2\nu^{2}}+2\nu)\bigl(\tfrac{1-\zeta^{2\nu(\nu-1)}}{1-\zeta^{2\nu}}\bigr)^{\nu}\\
+2\nu(\zeta^{2\nu^{2}}-1)\sum_{j=1}^{\nu-2}\bigl(\tfrac{1-\zeta^{2\nu j}}{1-\zeta^{2\nu}}\bigr)^{\nu}=:S^{*}(\zeta^{2\nu}).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\text{even }a\leq 0}[\zeta^{-a\nu}]\sum_{\tfrac{r}{2\nu}\in[\nu-1]}r\mathcal{F}^{*}_{r}(\zeta)=\sum_{\alpha\geq 0}[t^{\alpha}]S^{*}(t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 1}S^{*}(t)=2\nu(\nu-1)\nu^{\nu}-2\nu^{2}(\nu-1)^{\nu}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbb{k}}d^{*}(\mathbb{k})

\displaystyle=2\nu(1+\mathcal{M}_{1}^{*})-\tfrac{1}{\nu}\mathcal{M}_{2}^{*}

\displaystyle=2\nu(\nu^{\nu}-(\nu-1)^{\nu})-2(\nu-1)\nu^{\nu}+2\nu(\nu-1)^{\nu}=2\nu^{\nu},

so that
L^{*}(\mathbb{k})=2\pi\nu^{\nu-1/2}
. So,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\bigl[Z_{n}^{2};\mathcal{C}^{*}\bigr]\!

\displaystyle\leq\!(1+o(1))\biggl(\!\tfrac{\nu^{n}\cdot 2\pi\nu^{\nu-1/2}}{(2\pi)^{\nu}(M\nu)^{\nu-1}}\int_{\rho\geq 0}e^{-\tfrac{c_{M}n}{2\nu}\rho^{2}}\,dV(\rho)\!\biggr)^{2}

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\left(\tfrac{\nu^{n}}{M^{\nu}-1}\cdot\frac{\nu^{\nu-1/2}}{(2\pi\nu c_{M}n)^{\tfrac{\nu-1}{2}}}\right)^{2}.

In combination with (
4.4
), and (
3.34
), this leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq(1+o(1))\left(\tfrac{\nu^{n}}{M^{\nu}-1}\cdot\frac{\nu^{\nu-1/2}}{(2\pi\nu c_{M}n)^{\tfrac{\nu-1}{2}}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\nu^{2}\mathbb{E}^{2}[Z_{n}],

provided that
\lim\tfrac{n}{\log M}>\tfrac{2(\nu-1)}{\log[(1-2\nu^{-2})^{-1}]}
.
By Cantellli’s inequality (Billingsley
[
2
]
),

\liminf\mathbb{P}\bigl(Z_{n}\geq\delta\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]\bigr)\geq\lim\tfrac{(1-\delta)^{2}}{(1-\delta)^{2}+\tfrac{\mathbb{E}[Z^{2}_{n}]}{\mathbb{E}^{2}[Z_{n}]}}\geq\tfrac{(1-\delta)^{2}}{(1-\delta)^{2}+\nu^{2}},\quad\forall\,\delta\in(0,1).\\

∎

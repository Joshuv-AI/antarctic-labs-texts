Trimaximal Mixing and Extended Magic Symmetry in a Model of Neutrino Mass Matrix
Labh Singh, Tapender, Monal Kashav, Surender Verma

Abstract
The trimaximal mixing scheme (TM 2 ) results in “magic” neutrino mass matrix ( M ν M_{\nu} ) which is known to accommodate neutrino oscillation data. In this paper, we propose a phenomenological ansatz for M ν M_{\nu} by extending the magic symmetry that leads to further reduction in the number of free parameters, thereby, increasing the predictability of the model. The neutrino mixing parameters, effective Majorana mass m e ​ e m_{ee} and C ​ P CP invariants ( J C ​ P , I 1 , I 2 J_{CP},I_{1},I_{2} ) are found to exhibit strong correlations for TM 2 mixing paradigm. One of the generic feature of the model is the requirement of non-maximal θ 23 \theta_{23} for possible C ​ P CP violation measurable in neutrino oscillation experiments. The observables m e ​ e m_{ee} and sum of neutrino masses ( ∑ m i \sum m_{i} ) have imperative implications for yet unknown neutrino mass hierarchy. For inverted hierarchy, the lower bound on m e ​ e > 0.02 m_{ee}>0.02 eV, predicted by the model, is found to be within the sensitivity reach of the 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta decay experiments. Also, cosmological bound of 0.12 0.12 eV on ∑ m i \sum m_{i} , at 95% CL, refutes inverted hierarchy implying TM 2 with normal hierarchy as the only viable possibility in the model. We have, also, illustrated a scenario wherein such a construction of the neutrino mass matrix can be realized using Δ ⁡ ( 54 ) \Delta(54) symmetry in the framework of Type-I+II seesaw mechanism. Keywords: Neutrino mass matrix; phenomenology; Majorana neutrino; Neutrinoless double beta decay; C ​ P CP Violation.

5
Conclusions

In conclusion, we have considered an extension of the magic symmetry ansatz within the paradigm of TM
2

M_{\nu}
is, also, equal to the
“magic sum”
. This phenomenological ansatz exhibits strong correlations and homoscedasticity amongst the physical observables signifying the high predictability of the model. Some interesting phenomenological consequences of the model are:

•

In order to have
CP

\theta_{23}
is predicted to be non-maximal i.e. for
\theta_{23}\neq 45^{o}
.

•

The observables

2
(with IH) which can be probed in

decay shall refute IH for TM
2
mixing.

•

Though we have considered a relatively stable and conservative bound, on

, of 1 eV in the numerical analysis but the current bound from Planck data combined with WMAP and BAO of 0.12 eV at 95% CL refutes IH in the model (Fig.

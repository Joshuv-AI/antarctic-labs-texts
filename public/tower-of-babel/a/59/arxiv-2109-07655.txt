Abstract
. A congruence is a surface in the Grassmannian Gr ⁡ ( 2 , 4 ) {\rm Gr}(2,4) . In this paper, we consider the normalization of congruence of bitangents to a hypersurface in ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} . We call it the Fano congruence of bitangents. We give a criterion for smoothness of the Fano congruence of bitangents and describe explicitly their degenerations in a general Lefschetz pencil in the space of hypersurfaces in ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} .



1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper we will work over the field of complex numbers.

Let
{\rm Gr}(2,4)
be the Grassmannian of lines in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. A surface in
{\rm Gr}(2,4)
is called a congruence (cf.
[
1
]
).
Let
Y
be a hypersurface of degree
d\geq 4
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. By the congruence of bitangents to
Y
we mean the space
B(Y)
of lines in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
which is tangent to
Y
at two points. Indeed
B(Y)
is a smooth irreducible surface if
Y
is a smooth quartic and contains no line (cf
[
7
]
).
For
d\geq 5
, if
Y
has only isolated singularities and its dual variety
Y^{*}\subset(\mathbb{P}^{3})^{*}
is an irreducible hypersurface, then
B(Y)
is a congruence (see Proposition
2.2
). It is well known that
B(Y)
is not smooth unless
Y
is a quartic surface even when
Y
is general (see Lemma 4.3 in
[
1
]
).

In this paper we are going to study the Fano congruence of bitangents to a hypersurface of degree
d\geq 5
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
defined as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
Y
be a hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
of degree
d\geq 5
. The Fano congruence of bitangents to
Y
is the space of
P=(\ell,p_{1}+p_{2},q_{1}+\cdots+q_{d-4})
with
\ell\in{\rm Gr}(2,4)
and
p_{i},q_{j}\in\ell
such that
Y\cap\ell
contains
2p_{1}+2p_{2}+q_{1}+\cdots+q_{d-4}
as a subscheme. We denote the Fano congruence of bitangents to
Y
by
S(Y)
in this paper.

Let
\phi_{Y}:S(Y)\rightarrow B(Y)
be the natural morphism. Then
\phi_{Y}
is a finite morphism if
Y
contains no line.
The space
B(Y)
had been studied in detail in
[
1
]
and
[
4
]
. But in this paper, we focus on
S(Y)
instead of
B(Y)
because
S(Y)
is a smooth projective surface for general
Y
(see Proposition
2.4
) and their degenerations in a general Lefschetz pencil in the space of hypersurfaces
Y
can be described (see Theorem
1.2
). In particular, special fiber in a general Lefschetz pencil admits at worst isolated singularities or has double points of rank two along a smooth irreducible curve.
For arbitrary
Y
,
S(Y)
is smooth at any point
P
such that
\ell\not\subset Y
,
Y
is smooth at
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, and
\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\cap\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{d-4}\}=\emptyset
(see Theorem
3.3
).

The subject of this paper is to prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\pi_{L}:\mathcal{Y}=\{Y_{b}\}_{b\in L}\rightarrow L
be a general Lefschetz pencil of hypersurfaces of degree

in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and
let

be the singular values of
\pi_{L}
. Then each
b\in L
,

is a projective surface with singularities as follows.

(i)

If

is singular at some point

such that
Y_{b}
is smooth at
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, then
P
is an isolated singular point of
S(Y)
, and the followings are satisfied:

(a)

p_{1}\neq p_{2}
,
\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\cap\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{d-4}\}\neq\emptyset
, and
q_{1},\ldots,q_{d-4}
are distinct.

(b)

if
p_{1}\in\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{d-4}\}
and
p_{2}\not\in\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{d-4}\}
, then
\hat{T}_{p_{1}}Y_{b}=\hat{T}_{p_{2}}Y_{b}
and

is a plane curve with a cuspidal singularity at

. Here

at

(ii)

For each

, let

be the space of

. Then

(iii)

\phi_{Y_{b}}:S(Y_{b})\rightarrow B(Y_{b})

as a subscheme of the fiber product of two projective vector bundles

on
{\rm Gr}(2,4)
where

is the dual of the tautological vector bundle
\mathcal{I}
on
{\rm Gr}(2,4)
. We also combine local computational analysis with global methods from intersection theory.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

be a hypersurface of degree

admitting only isolated singularities. Assume that the dual variety

of

is an irreducible hypersurface. Then

has dimension 2.

Proof.

Let
{\rm Tan}(Y)=S_{2,1^{d-2}}(Y)\subset G
be the space of lines in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
meeting

with multiplicity
\geq 2
at some point. We first claim that
{\rm Tan}(Y)
is irreducible of dimension 3.
Let
\mathcal{T}\subset G\times Y
be the space of
(\ell,p)\in G\times Y
such that
\ell
meets
Y
with multipicity
\geq 2
at
p
. For each point
p\in Y
, the fiber of the projection morphism
\mathcal{T}\rightarrow Y
over

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
if

is smooth at

, and to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
if

is singular at

\mathcal{T}
is irreducible of dimension 3 because

has only isolated singular points. Therefore the image of the projection
\mathcal{T}\rightarrow G
which is equal to
{\rm Tan}(Y)
is irreducible of dimension 3.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the space of pairs
(p,H)\in Y\times(\mathbb{P}^{3})^{*}
such that
p\in H
. Let
\phi_{1}:\mathcal{K}\rightarrow Y
and
\phi_{2}:\mathcal{K}\rightarrow(\mathbb{P}^{3})^{*}
be the natural projection morphisms.
Each fiber of
\phi_{1}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and hence
\mathcal{K}
is irreducible since
Y
is irreducible. Therefore for general
H
outside codimension 2 subscheme of
(\mathbb{P}^{3})^{*}
, the fiber

is irreducible (see

]
). Therefore when we take a general

so that
H=\hat{T}_{p}Y
for some smooth point

, the intersection
C_{p}:=\hat{T}_{p}Y\cap Y
is an irreducible plane curve.

Suppose that
B(Y)
has an irreducible component

of dimension 3. Since
{\rm Tan}(Y)
is irreducible of dimension 3 and
D\subset{\rm Tan}(Y)
,
D
is equal to
{\rm Tan}(Y)
. This means that any tangent line of

is bitangent to
Y
.
Take a general point
p\in Y
so that
C_{p}=\hat{T}_{p}Y\cap Y
is an irreducible curve in
\hat{T}_{p}Y\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}
. By composing the normalization

and the projection map
\hat{T}_{p}Y\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
from a point

we obtain a finite morphism

. Since any tangent line is bitangent by our assumption,
\nu
is branched over all points of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
which is impossible. We are done.
∎

3.

We introduce the following notation for convenience.

\{((a_{0},a_{1},b_{0},b_{1}),[g^{2}h+a_{0}t_{0}g_{d-1}+a_{1}t_{1}g_{d-1}+b_{0}t_{0}h_{d-1}+b_{1}t_{1}h_{d-1}])\ |\ a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\}

which is contained in the subspace
\mathbb{C}^{4}_{a_{0},a_{1},b_{0},b_{1}}\times T_{[g^{2}h]}\mathbb{P}(B_{Y})
of
\mathbb{C}^{4}_{a_{0},a_{1},b_{0},b_{1}}\times T_{[g^{2}h]}\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}[t_{0},t_{1}]_{d})

From this and (
3.2
), we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\dim d\varphi(T_{P}\mathcal{F})\cap T_{\varphi(P)}G_{Y}

\displaystyle=4-(\dim B_{Y}-\dim A_{P}\cap B_{Y})=4-(\dim<A_{P},B_{Y}>-\dim A_{P}).

Since
\varphi(S(Y))=\varphi(\mathcal{F})\cap G_{Y}
, we have

\dim d\varphi(T_{P}S(Y))\leq\dim d\varphi(T_{P}\mathcal{F})\cap T_{\varphi(P)}G_{Y}=4-(\dim<A_{P},B_{Y}>-\dim A_{P}).

From this and (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle 2\leq\dim T_{P}S(Y)

\displaystyle\leq\dim T_{P}\mathcal{F}-\dim d\varphi(T_{P}\mathcal{F})+\dim d\varphi(T_{P}S(Y))

\displaystyle\leq(d+2)-(3+\dim A_{P})+(4-\dim<A_{P},B_{Y}>-\dim A_{P})

\displaystyle=d+3-\dim<A_{P},B_{Y}>.

Therefore if
\dim<A_{P},B_{Y}>=d+1
then
\dim T_{P}S(Y)=2
, and hence we get the first statement. (
3.2
) says that if

then
\varphi
is immersed at
P
and thus so is
\phi_{Y}
because
\phi_{Y}
is locally a restriction of
\varphi
at

. We are done.
∎

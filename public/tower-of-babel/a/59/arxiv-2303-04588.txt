Let

{\rm\mathcal{A}}

For any vertex

Lee et al.
[
4

introduced the concept of group-magic graphs as below.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
{\rm\mathcal{A}}
be an abelian group.
A graph
G=(V,E)
is said to be
{\rm\mathcal{A}}
-magic if there exists a labeling
\ell:E\rightarrow{\rm\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{0\}
such that the induced vertex labeling
\ell^{+}:V\rightarrow{\rm\mathcal{A}}
defined by
\ell^{+}(v)=\sum_{uv\in E}\ell(uv)
is a constant map.

More results about group-magic graphs, we refer to
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
8
]
.
Based on the concept of group-magic graph, Kamatchi et al.
[
2
]
introduced the concept of group vertex magic graphs.

Definition 1.2
.

A mapping
\ell:V(G)\rightarrow{\rm\mathcal{A}}\rm\setminus\{0\}
is said to be an
{\rm\mathcal{A}}
-vertex magic labeling of
G
if there exists an element
\mu
of
{\rm\mathcal{A}}
such that
\omega(v)=\sum_{u\in N(v)}\ell(u)=\mu
for any vertex
v
of
G
.
A graph
G
that admits such a labeling is called an
{\rm\mathcal{A}}
-vertex magic graph and
\mu
is said a magic constant.
If
G
is
{\rm\mathcal{A}}
-vertex magic graph for any nontrivial abelian group
{\rm\mathcal{A}}
, then
G
is called a group vertex magic graph.

After proposing the concept of group vertex magic graphs, Kamatchi et al. give a characterization of
V_{4}
-vertex magic trees with diameter at most
4
.
Subsequently, K.M. Sabeel et al.
[
6

-vertex magicness of trees with diameter

More generally, K.M. Sabeel and K. Paramasivam

-vertex magic trees of diameter at most

{\rm\mathcal{A}}
.
Moreover, S. Balamoorthy et al.
[

The graph
G
is unicyclic if it contains only one cycle and
G
is bicyclic if it contains exactly two cycles.
In this paper, we give a characterization of unicyclic graphs with diameter at most

Moreover, we consider the group vertex magicness of bicyclic graphs with diameter

Before we get into the discussion, some useful notations are listed here.
The distance

between vertices

is the length of a shortest path from

to
v

The diameter of

.
A vertex

is a pendant vertex and the unique vertex adjacent to

A vertex

A vertex

v

If vertex

is adjacent to two or more pendant vertices, then

An element

is called a square if
g\neq 0
and there exists
h\in{\rm\mathcal{A}}
such that
g=2h
.
Obviously,
h\neq 0
.
Given a group
{\rm\mathcal{A}}
,

is the least positive integer

.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
{\rm\mathcal{A}}
be a abelian group with
|{\rm\mathcal{A}}|\geq 3

Hence, for any
g\in{\rm\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{0\}

g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n}\in{\rm\mathcal{A}}\rm\setminus\{0\}

g=g_{1}+g_{2}+\ldots+g_{n}

Proof.

We complete the proof by induction.
The result is true for

, since for any
g\in{\rm\mathcal{A}}
,
g=g_{1}+(g-g_{1})
holds for any
g_{1}\in{\rm\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{0\}

Now suppose the result is true for

Then
g=g_{1}+g_{2}+\cdots+g_{n-1}
for
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n-1}\in{\rm\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{0\}
.
As there exist
g_{n-1}^{\prime},g_{n}^{\prime}\in{\rm\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{0\}
such that
g_{n-1}=g_{n-1}^{\prime}+g_{n}^{\prime}
, we have

g=g_{1}+g_{2}+\cdots+g_{n-1}^{\prime}+g_{n}^{\prime}
.
Thus, the result follows.
∎

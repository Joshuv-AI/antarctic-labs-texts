Thermodynamic class II Szekeres-Szafron solutions. Regular models
Bartolomé Coll, Joan Josep Ferrando and, Juan Antonio Sáez

Abstract
In a recent paper (Coll et al 2019 Class. Quantum Grav. 36 175004) we have studied a family of Szekeres-Szafron solutions of class II in local thermal equilibrium (singular models). In this paper we deal with a similar study for all other class II Szekeres-Szafron solutions without symmetries. These models in local thermal equilibrium (regular models) are analyzed and their associated thermodynamic schemes are obtained. In particular, we focus on the subfamily of solutions which are compatible with the generic ideal gas equation of state ( p = k ~ ​ n ​ Θ p=\tilde{k}n\Theta ), and we analyze in depth two notable interpretations that follow on from the choice of two specific thermodynamic schemes: firstly, as a generic ideal gas in local thermal equilibrium and, secondly, as a model having the homogeneous temperature of the FLRW limit. The models above are shown to fulfill the general necessary macroscopic requirements for physical reality (positivity of matter density, internal energy and temperature, energy conditions and compressibility conditions) in wide domains of the spacetime.

Introduction

An important task in Relativity is to study the physical interpretation of the formal perfect fluid solutions to the Einstein field equations. At present, wide families of
such solutions are known but lack a specific physical meaning. Many of them have been obtained by imposing geometric constraints that simplify the integration of the field equations: static or stationary solutions, or invariance under other isometry groups such as spherically symmetric solutions; or restrictions on the curvature tensor, as in the case of algebraically special solutions. Kinematic constraints on fluid flow have also been imposed a priori in looking for new solutions: geodesic, irrotational, shear-free or non-expanding solutions have been considered. In any case, very few solutions have been interpreted as physically realistic fluids.

Towards a physical interpretation of the perfect fluid solutions

Perfect fluid solutions of the Einstein equations have usually been obtained by imposing symmetries or other geometric properties on the gravitational field, or by requiring kinematic constraints

2.2
Procedure to determine physically admissible perfect fluid solutions

2.3
Perfect fluid solutions modeling non-perfect fluids

The relativistic thermodynamic theory of irreversible processes was developed by Eckart
[
2
]
and is currently known as the
standard irreversible thermodynamics
. Israel and Stewart
[
30
,
31
]
proposed an
extended irreversible thermodynamics
that solves the causal shortcomings of Eckart’s theory. For a broad manual on extended thermodynamics see
[
32
]
, and a comprehensive summary of both the standard and the extended approaches can be found in
[
33
]
.

According to the theory of thermodynamics of irreversible processes the transport coefficients of thermal conductivity, of shear-viscosity, and of bulk-viscosity play an important role. They appear in the constitutive equations linking dissipative fluxes (anisotropic pressures, bulk viscous pressure and energy flux) with the kinematic coefficients of fluid flow (shear, expansion and acceleration).

The perfect fluid approximation can be considered when the transport coefficients of a fluid vanish (or are negligible). In this case, the energetic evolution of this fluid does not differ from that of a perfect fluid. Moreover, Eckart’s thermodynamic theory reduces down to the fundamental system of the perfect fluid hydrodynamics exposed in subsection
2.1
above.

A non-perfect fluid is a fluid with at least a non-zero transport coefficient. For this fluid, the energetic evolution is, generically, described by an energy tensor with energy flux and anisotropic pressures. However, when a non-perfect fluid admits particular evolutions in which the dissipative fluxes vanish, these evolutions are well described by a perfect energy tensor, and are usually called
equilibrium states

[
33
]
. Moreover, all the thermodynamic relations of the perfect fluid hydrodynamics remain valid. Furthermore, the shear, the expansion and the acceleration of the fluid undergo strong restrictions as a consequence of the constitutive equations. For such equilibrium states
[
33
]
:

-

If the shear viscosity coefficient does not vanish, then the fluid shear vanishes.

-

If the bulk viscosity coefficient does not vanish, then the fluid expansion vanishes.

-

If the thermal conductivity coefficient does not vanish, then the fluid acceleration is constrained by the relation:

a=-\perp\mathop{\rm d\!}\nolimits\ln\Theta\,,

(12)

where
\perp
denotes the orthogonal projection to the fluid velocity.

After these considerations it seems reasonable to look for perfect fluid solutions of the Einstein equations that describe both (i) a thermodynamic perfect fluid in local thermal equilibrium, and (ii) a non-perfect fluid in equilibrium. In this paper we present some Szekeres-Szafron solutions that can describe an inviscid (with negligible shear and bulk viscosity coefficients) non-perfect fluid (with non-vanishing thermal conductivity).

For the sake of clarity, from now on we compress the expression of the hydrodynamic variables by defining two functions

\displaystyle p=-2\frac{\ddot{\phi}}{\phi}-\tau\,,\qquad\qquad\quad\tau=\tau(t)\equiv\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{\phi^{2}}+\frac{k}{\phi^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\rho=3\tau-\frac{2\,\varepsilon\,\dot{\xi}}{\phi^{2}(\varepsilon\beta+Q)}\,,\qquad\xi=\xi(t)\equiv\phi^{2}\dot{\beta}\,.

\dot{\xi}\equiv(\phi^{2}\dot{\beta})^{\cdot}=-\phi\dot{\phi}\dot{\beta}+k\beta+1\,.

For the strict SS metrics (

Q=\left[\frac{2\dot{\xi}}{\phi^{2}(3\tau-\rho)}-\beta\right]\equiv Q(\rho,p)\,.

The expressions (

[

Proposition 3

The thermodynamic schemes associated with the regular models (

) are determined by two arbitrary functions
s(Q)
,
s^{\prime}(Q)\not=0
, and
r(Q)\not=0
. The specific entropy
s
, the matter density

, and the temperature

are given by:

\displaystyle s=s(Q)\equiv s(\rho,p)\,;\qquad\ n=\frac{1}{\phi^{3}(\beta+Q)r(Q)}\equiv n(\rho,p)\,;

(34)

\displaystyle\Theta=\ell(Q)\lambda(t)+m(Q)\mu(t)\equiv\Theta(\rho,p)\,,

(35)

\displaystyle\ell(Q)\equiv\frac{r^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\,,\qquad m(Q)\equiv\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}[Qr^{\prime}+r]\,,

(36)

\displaystyle\lambda(t)\equiv-2\phi\dot{\xi}+\beta\phi^{3}(p+3\tau)\,,\quad\quad\mu(t)\equiv\phi^{3}(p+3\tau)\,.

(37)

Note that we also use the prime for the derivative of any function depending on a sole variable.

4.3
The indicatrix function: speed of sound

The square of the speed of sound in terms of the hydrodynamic variables,
c_{s}^{2}=\chi(\rho,p)

\dot{\rho}
by taking into account (

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}=\tilde{A}\rho^{2}+\tilde{B}\rho+\tilde{C}\,,

(38)

\displaystyle\tilde{A}\equiv\frac{\xi}{2\dot{\xi}}\,,\quad\tilde{B}\equiv-\frac{3\dot{\phi}}{\phi}+(p-3\tau)\tilde{A}\,,\quad\tilde{C}\equiv-\frac{3\dot{\phi}}{\phi}p-3\tau p\tilde{A}\,.

(39)

Consequently, we have the following result:

Proposition 4

For the regular models (
26
), the square of the speed of sound takes the expression:

c_{s}^{2}=\chi(\rho,p)\equiv\frac{1}{{\cal A}(p)\rho^{2}+{\cal B}(p)\rho+{\cal C}(p)}\,,

(40)

where
{\cal A}
,
{\cal B}
and

are the functions of

(and then of

\hskip-34.1433pt{\cal A}(p)\equiv\frac{\xi}{2\dot{\xi}\dot{p}}\,,\qquad{\cal B}(p)\equiv-\frac{3\dot{\phi}}{\phi\dot{p}}+(p-3\tau){\cal A}\,,\qquad{\cal C}(p)\equiv-\frac{3\dot{\phi}}{\phi\dot{p}}p-3\tau p{\cal A}\,.

(41)

Note that (
40
) provides an expression of the indicatrix function which is implicit in the variable

. For a specific solution
(\phi(t),\beta(t))
of the equation (
25

p(t)
from (

. Then, the explicit form of

can be obtained (see forthcoming sections).

In this section we have achieved steps 1, 2 and 4 of the procedure proposed in subsection
2.2
. We could formally impose the restrictions required in step 3 (energy and compressibility conditions H
1
), and we would get inequalities involving third order derivatives of the generic metric functions. Nevertheless, it may be more practical to make this study for a subclass of solutions that fulfills complementary physical requirements, and once we have obtained the explicit form of

5
Models with the hydrodynamic behavior of a generic ideal gas

Now we will analyze when the regular models considered above are compatible with the equation of state of a
generic ideal gas
, namely:

p=\tilde{k}n\Theta\,,\qquad\quad\tilde{k}\equiv{k_{B}\over m}\,.

(42)

In
[
5
]
we have solved the direct problem for the generic ideal gases by studying the hydrodynamic constraints that equation (
42
) imposes, and in
[
6
]
we have analyzed the compressibility condition H
1
for this particular case. These results can be summarized in the following.

Lemma 2

A perfect energy tensor
T=\{u,\rho,p\}
represents the evolution of a generic ideal gas in l.t.e. if, and only if, it fulfills the ideal gas sonic condition:

\hskip-14.22636pt{\rm S^{\rm G}}:\qquad\quad\chi=\chi(\pi)\not=\pi\,,\qquad\chi=\frac{\dot{p}}{\dot{\rho}}\,,\qquad\pi=\frac{p}{\rho}\,.

(43)

For a fluid with
\chi=\chi(\pi)
, compressibility conditions
{\rm H}_{1}
given in (
10

\hskip-14.22636pt{\rm H}_{1}^{\rm G}:\qquad\quad 0<\chi<1\,,\qquad\zeta\equiv(1+\pi)(\chi-\pi)\chi^{\prime}+2\chi(1-\chi)>0\,.

(44)

On the other hand, although there are continuous media with negative pressure, the equation of sate (
42
) and the positivity conditions P given in (
7
) imply a non-negative thermodynamic pressure,
p\geq 0
. Moreover here we shall consider non-shift perfect fluids (
\rho\not=p
). Consequently, the energy conditions E given in (
2
) become:

\hskip-14.22636pt{\rm E}^{\rm G}:\qquad\qquad\rho>0\,,\qquad 0\leq\pi<1\,,\quad\pi=\frac{p}{\rho}\,.

(45)

Then, in order to study the solutions with the hydrodynamic behavior of a generic ideal gas, we can
fairly modify the procedure exposed in subsection
2.2
:

Step 1’

Determine the subfamily of the ideal gas solutions by imposing the ideal gas sonic condition S
G
.

Step 2’

Obtain, for this subfamily, the coordinate dependence of the hydrodynamic quantities
u
,
\rho
,
p
, and the indicatrix function
c_{s}^{2}=\chi(\pi)
.

Step 3’

Analyze, for the ideal gas solutions, the hydrodynamic general constraints for physical reality, namely, the energy conditions E
G
and the compressibility conditions H
{}_{1}^{\rm G}
.

Step 4’

Obtain the thermodynamic schemes
\{n,\epsilon,s,\Theta\}
associated with the ideal gas solutions.

Step 5’

Analyze, for some physically relevant thermodynamic schemes
\{n,\epsilon,s,\Theta\}
, the thermodynamic general constraints for physical reality, namely, the positivity conditions P and the compressibility condition H
2
.

5.1
Study of the ideal sonic condition S

From the expression of the indicatrix function (

The regular models with an indicatrix function of the form (

\hskip-48.36967ptc_{1}\equiv\frac{\xi p^{2}}{2\dot{\xi}\dot{p}}\not=0,\qquad c_{2}\equiv-3\frac{\dot{\phi}}{\phi}\frac{p}{\dot{p}}+c_{1}\!\left(\!1-3\frac{\tau}{p}\right),\qquad c_{3}\equiv-3\frac{\dot{\phi}}{\phi}\frac{p}{\dot{p}}-3c_{1}\frac{\tau}{p}.

(47)

From the expressions (

Then, the third equation in (

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}=-\frac{\dot{\phi}}{\phi}\,\xi+k\beta+1\,,\qquad\xi=\phi^{2}\dot{\beta}\,,

\displaystyle 2c_{1}\,\dot{\xi}=\left(\frac{p^{2}}{\dot{p}}\right)\xi\,.

A first accurate analysis of the equations leads to the following result, which will be proved in

Lemma 4

In following sections we focus on cases (ii) and (iv), and we study in detail the five steps required for analyzing the physical reality of the solutions.

6
Ideal parabolic regular models

6.1
Metric and hydrodynamic variables: energy density, pressure and speed of sound

In case (ii) we have a parabolic model (

In addition, we obtain

Proposition 5

The ideal parabolic regular models have a metric line element of the form

\mathop{\rm d\!}\nolimits s^{2}=-\mathop{\rm d\!}\nolimits t^{2}+\phi^{2}[(\varepsilon\beta+Q)^{2}\mathop{\rm d\!}\nolimits z^{2}+\mathop{\rm d\!}\nolimits x^{2}+\mathop{\rm d\!}\nolimits y^{2}]\,,

where

and
\beta(t)

(55)

\phi(t)=\left[\frac{3}{2}\kappa\gamma\,(t-t_{0})\right]^{\frac{2}{3\gamma}},\qquad\beta=\beta(\phi)\equiv\frac{2\,}{\kappa^{2}(9\gamma^{2}-4)}\phi^{3\gamma-2}.

(56)

Moreover the pressure

\displaystyle p=\frac{3\kappa^{2}(\gamma-1)}{\phi^{3\gamma}}\,,

(57)

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{3\kappa^{2}}{\phi^{3\gamma}}\left[1-\frac{2\varepsilon\gamma(3\gamma-2)}{\kappa^{2}(9\gamma^{2}-4)\phi^{2-3\gamma}\,Q+2\varepsilon}\right]\,.

(58)

And the speed of sound is given by:

c_{s}^{2}=\chi(\pi)\equiv\frac{3\,\gamma^{2}\,\pi^{2}}{(\pi+1)[(3\gamma+1)\pi-(\gamma-1)]}\,,\qquad\pi\equiv\frac{p}{\rho}\,.

(59)

6.2
Analysis of the solutions. Energy conditions

It is worth remarking the following qualities of the solutions in proposition
5
:

(i)

The metric depends on three arbitrary functions of

V_{1}(z)
,
V_{2}(z)
and

, and two effective parameters,

(ii)

Expression (

determines the strength of the density

(iii)

The only solutions admitting a

(iv)

In expanding models the solutions approach to the FLRW

-law models for early times (

\gamma
-models.

(v)

The solutions belong to the family of metrics considered by Szafron and Wainwright
[
37

, which was the first generalization with non-vanishing pressure of the Szekeres dust solutions.

Now we achieve the analysis of the energy conditions E
G
by obtaining the spacetime domains where these conditions (

57

58
) for the pressure and density we obtain (

Consequently,

The ideal parabolic regular models in proposition

fulfill the energy conditions

given in (

\kappa^{2}\frac{(3\gamma-2)(2-\gamma)}{\gamma-1}\,Q>\phi^{3\gamma-2}\,,\qquad\quad\kappa^{2}(9\gamma^{2}-4)\,Q<-2\phi^{3\gamma-2}\,.

(61)

Note that, for contracting models (

6.3
Compressibility conditions

Now we will complete step 3’ for the ideal parabolic regular models by analyzing the compressibility conditions H
{}_{1}^{\rm G}

\hskip-56.9055pt\chi^{\prime}(\pi)=\frac{6\,\gamma^{2}\,\pi[(\gamma+1)\pi-(\gamma-1)]}{(\pi+1)^{2}[(3\gamma+1)\pi-(\gamma-1)]^{2}}\,,\quad\chi(0)=0\,,\quad\chi(1)=\frac{3\gamma^{2}}{4(\gamma+1)}\,.

Proposition 7

The ideal parabolic regular models in proposition

6.6
Models with the FLRW-limit temperature

8
Summary and work in progress

In this paper we have accomplished the task started in
[
23
]
: the study of class II Szekeres-Szafron metrics without symmetries, which can be interpreted as a thermodynamic perfect fluid in local thermal equilibrium. Our analysis leads to two families, the singular models (considered in
[
23
]
) and the regular models (considered here). The thermodynamic schemes associated with these thermodynamic solutions have been outlined. We have analyzed in depth the solutions with a significant physical interpretation: those compatible with the equation of state of a generic ideal gas. In the case of the regular models two subfamilies must be distinguished, the
ideal parabolic regular models
(
k=0
) and the
ideal non-parabolic regular models
(
k=\pm 1
). Here we have analyzed the macroscopic constraints for physical reality (energy conditions, positivity conditions and compressibility conditions) for these two subfamilies, and a similar analysis has been carried out in
[
23
]
for the
ideal singular models
.

In local thermal equilibrium, the indicatrix function
\chi
is a function of state which gives the square of the speed of sound in terms of the hydrodynamic quantities
\rho
and
p
,
c_{s}^{2}=\chi(\rho,p)
. This function plays an important role in our study of the thermodynamic properties of the solutions. In dealing with ideal models the indicatrix function depends on the sole hydrodynamic quantity
\pi=p/\rho
,
\chi=\chi(\pi)
, and on an adiabatic-like index
\gamma\in]1,2[
. The ideal non-parabolic regular models are only compatible with
\gamma=4/3
(ultra-relativistic gas), and the indicatrix function is given by the function
\chi_{1}(\pi)
given in (
87
). It is worth considering the two other families of ideal models for
\gamma=4/3
. In this case, the singular models have precisely the same expression
\chi_{1}(\pi)
for the indicatrix function. We have
\chi_{1}(0)=0
and
\chi_{1}(1/3)=1/3
, which are the values that take the indicatrix function,
\chi_{s}(\pi)
, of a relativistic Synge gas. But,
\chi_{1}^{\prime}(0)\not=\chi_{s}^{\prime}(0)
and
\chi_{1}^{\prime}(1/3)\not=\chi_{s}^{\prime}(1/3)
. Thus, the ideal singular models (with
\gamma=4/3
) and the ideal non-parabolic regular models approach the Synge gas at low and high temperatures, but only at zero order (see Figure 1).

On the other hand, the parabolic regular models with
\gamma=4/3
have an indicatrix function
\chi_{2}(\pi)
that can be obtained from (
59
). This function does not fulfill the compressibility conditions for small values of
\pi
, but
\chi_{2}(1/3)=\chi_{s}(1/3)=1/3
and
\chi_{2}^{\prime}(1/3)=\chi_{s}^{\prime}(1/3)=1/2
. Thus, the ideal parabolic regular models (with
\gamma=4/3
) are not valid for describe low temperature states, but they approach at first order the Synge gas at high temperatures (see Figure 1).

\chi=\chi(\pi)

It is worth remarking that both the non-parabolic regular models and the singular models evolve to the (homogeneous) FLRW
\gamma
-law models in the expanding case, and the inhomogeneities increase with time in the contracting models. Nevertheless, the
parabolic regular models evolve to the (homogeneous) FLRW
\gamma
-law models in the contracting case, and the inhomogeneities increase with time in the expanding models.

Further work will be devoted to analyzing the Szekeres-Szafron solutions of class II with a G
3
group of isometries. They are necessarily thermodynamic solutions, but two basic problems remain open: (i) the determination of the associated thermodynamic schemes, and (ii) the detailed analysis of the ideal gas models. Moreover, these studies will allow us to interpret the (barotropic) Kantowski-Sachs metrics as a thermodynamic perfect fluid in isentropic evolution.

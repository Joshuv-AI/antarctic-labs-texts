Unifying Diffusion Models’ Latent Space, with Applications to CycleDiffusion and Guidance
Chen Henry Wu, Fernando De la Torre

Abstract
Diffusion models have achieved unprecedented performance in generative modeling. The commonly-adopted formulation of the latent code of diffusion models is a sequence of gradually denoised samples, as opposed to the simpler (e.g., Gaussian) latent space of GANs, VAEs, and normalizing flows. This paper provides an alternative, Gaussian formulation of the latent space of diffusion models, as well as a reconstructable DPM-Encoder that maps images into the latent space. While our formulation is purely based on the definition of diffusion models, we demonstrate several intriguing consequences. (1) Empirically, we observe that a common latent space emerges from two diffusion models trained independently on related domains. In light of this finding, we propose CycleDiffusion , which uses DPM-Encoder for unpaired image-to-image translation. Furthermore, applying CycleDiffusion to text-to-image diffusion models, we show that large-scale text-to-image diffusion models can be used as zero-shot image-to-image editors. (2) One can guide pre-trained diffusion models and GANs by controlling the latent codes in a unified, plug-and-play formulation based on energy-based models. Using the CLIP model and a face recognition model as guidance, we demonstrate that diffusion models have better coverage of low-density sub-populations and individuals than GANs. 1 1 1 The code is publicly available at https://github.com/ChenWu98/cycle-diffusion .

Diffusion models

We show that some intriguing consequences emerge from our definition of the latent space of diffusion models and our DPM-Encoder. First, observations have been made that, given two diffusion models, a fixed “random seed” produces similar images

Under our formulation, we formalize “similar images” via an upper bound of image distances. 3.3
CycleDiffusion: Image-to-Image Translation with DPM-Encoder

Given two stochastic DPMs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
that model two distributions
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, several researchers and practitioners have found that sampling with the same “random seed” leads to similar images
Nichol et al. 2022
. To formalize “similar images”, we provide an upper bound of image distances based on assumptions about the trained DPMs, shown at the end of this subsection. Based on this finding, we propose a simple unpaired image-to-image translation method, CycleDiffusion. Given a source image
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
, we use DPM-Encoder to encode it as
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}
and then decode it as
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\hat{\bm{x}}}=G_{2}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}})
:

{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}\sim\mathrm{DPMEnc}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}},G_{1}),\quad{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\hat{\bm{x}}}=G_{2}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}).

(7)

We can also apply CycleDiffusion to text-to-image diffusion models by defining
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
as image distributions conditioned on two texts. Let
G_{\bm{t}}
be a text-to-image diffusion model conditioned on text
\bm{t}
. Given a source image
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}}

{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}}

{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\hat{\bm{x}}}
to be generated.

An analysis for image similarity with fixed
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}
.
We analyze the image similarity using text-to-image diffusion models. Suppose the text-to-image model has the following two properties:

1.

Conditioned on the same text, similar noisy images lead to similar enough mean predictions. Formally,
\bm{\mu}_{T}(\bm{x}_{t},t|\bm{t})
is
K_{t}
-Lipschitz, i.e.,
\|\bm{\mu}_{T}(\bm{x}_{t},t|\bm{t})-\bm{\mu}_{T}(\hat{\bm{x}}_{t},t|\bm{t})\|\leq K_{t}\|\bm{x}_{t}-\hat{\bm{x}}_{t}\|
.

2.

Given the same image, the two texts lead to similar predictions. Formally,
\|\bm{\mu}_{T}(\hat{\bm{x}}_{t},t|\bm{t})-\bm{\mu}_{T}(\hat{\bm{x}}_{t},t|\hat{\bm{t}})\|\leq S_{t}
. Intuitively, a smaller difference between
\bm{t}
and
\hat{\bm{t}}
gives us a smaller
S_{t}
.

Let
B_{t}
be the upper bound of
\|\bm{x}_{t}-\hat{\bm{x}}_{t}\|_{2}
at time step
t
when the same latent code
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}
is used for sampling (i.e.,
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}}=G_{\bm{t}}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}})
and
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\hat{\bm{x}}_{0}}=G_{\hat{\bm{t}}}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}})
). We have
B_{T}=0
because
\|{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{x}_{T}}-{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\hat{\bm{x}}_{T}}\|_{2}=0
, and
B_{0}
is the upper bound for the generated images
\|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}}-{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\hat{\bm{x}}}\|_{2}
. The upper bound

can be propagated through time, from

to
0

B_{t-1}\leq(K_{t}+1)B_{t}+S_{t}.

3.4
Unified Plug-and-Play Guidance for Generative Models

Prior works showed that guidance for generative models can be achieved in the latent space
Nguyen et al. 2017
;
Nie et al. 2021
;
Wu et al. 2022
. Specifically, given a condition
{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\mathcal{C}}
, one can define the guided image distribution as an energy-based model (EBM):
p({\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}}|{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\mathcal{C}})\propto p_{{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}}}({\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}})e^{-\lambda_{{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\mathcal{C}}}E({\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}}|{{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\mathcal{C}}})}
.
Sampling for
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}}\sim p({\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}}|{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\mathcal{C}})
is equivalent to
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}\sim p_{{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}|{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\mathcal{C}}),{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}}=G({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}})
, where
p({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}|{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\mathcal{C}})\propto p_{{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}})e^{-\lambda_{{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\mathcal{C}}}E(G({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}})|{{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\mathcal{C}}})}
.
Examples of the energy function
E({\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}}|{{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\mathcal{C}}})
are provided in Section
4.3
. To sample
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}\sim p({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}|{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\mathcal{C}})
, one can use any model-agnostic samplers. For example, Langevin dynamics
Welling & Teh 2011
starts from
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}^{\langle 0\rangle}}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{I})
and samples
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}:=\bm{z}^{\langle n\rangle}}
iteratively through

\begin{split}{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}^{\langle k+1\rangle}}={\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}^{\langle k\rangle}}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\nabla_{{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}}\Big(\log p_{{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}^{\langle k\rangle}})-E\big(G({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}^{\langle k\rangle}})|{{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\mathcal{C}}}\big)\Big)+\sqrt{\sigma}\bm{\omega}^{\langle k\rangle},\quad\bm{\omega}^{\langle k\rangle}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{I}).\end{split}

(10)

Metrics:

4.3
Unified Plug-and-Play Guidance for Diffusion Models and GANs

Previous methods for conditional sampling from (
aka
guiding) diffusion models require training the guidance model on noisy images
Dhariwal & Nichol 2021

Liu et al. 2021

Nguyen et al. 2017

5
Conclusions and Discussion

This paper provides a unified view of pre-trained generative models by reformulating the latent space of diffusion models. While this reformulation is purely definitional, we show that it allows us to use diffusion models in similar ways as CycleGANs
Zhu et al. 2017
and GANs. Our CycleDiffusion achieves impressive performance on unpaired image-to-image translation (with two diffusion models trained on two domains independently) and zero-shot image-to-image translation (with text-to-image diffusion models). Our definition of latent code also allows diffusion models to be guided in the same way as GANs (i.e., plug-and-play, without finetuning on noisy images), and results show that diffusion models have broader coverage of sub-populations and individuals than GANs.

Besides the interesting results, it is worth noting that this paper raised more questions than provided answers. We have provided a formal analysis of the common latent space of stochastic DPMs via the bounded distance between images (Section
3.3
), but it still needs further study. Notably,
Khrulkov & Oseledets 2022
and
Su et al. 2022
studied deterministic DPMs based on optimal transport.
Furthermore,
efficient
plug-and-play guidance for stochastic DPMs on high-resolution images with many diffusion steps still remains open. These topics can be further explored in future studies.

A.3
Score-based Generative Modeling with SDE

Song et al. 2021b
proposed a unified view of DDPM and score matching with Langevin dynamics (SMLD) as different stochastic differential equations (SDEs). Since the randomness in their sampling algorithms purely come from Gaussian noise, we can incorporate their models and sampling methods into our framework. As a demonstration, we show how to define
\bm{\mu}_{T}(\bm{x}_{t},t)
and
\bm{\sigma}_{t}
for their predictor-only sampling with reverse diffusion samplers. Given a forward SDE:

\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)\mathrm{d}t+\bm{\sigma}(t)\odot\mathrm{d}\bm{w},

(19)

the reverse-time SDE is

\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=[\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-\bm{\sigma}(t)^{2}\odot\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x})]\mathrm{d}t+\bm{\sigma}(t)\odot\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}}.

(20)

Suppose the forward SDE is discretized in the following form:

\bm{x}_{t+1}=\bm{x}_{t}+\bm{f}_{t}(\bm{x}_{t})+\bm{\sigma}_{t}\odot\bm{z}_{t},\quad t=0,\ldots,T-1,\quad\bm{z}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{I}).

(21)

Reverse diffusion samplers discretize the reserve-time SDE in a similar form:

\begin{split}&\bm{x}_{t-1}=\bm{x}_{t}-\bm{f}_{t}(\bm{x}_{t})+\bm{\sigma}_{t}^{2}\odot\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x}_{t},t)+\bm{\sigma}_{t}\odot\bm{\epsilon}_{t},\quad t=1,\ldots,T,\quad\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{I}),\end{split}

(22)

where
\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}
is a neural network trained to match the score
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x})
. By comparing Eq. (
12
) and Eq. (
22
), we have
\bm{\mu}_{T}(\bm{x}_{t},t):=\bm{x}_{t}-\bm{f}_{t}(\bm{x}_{t})+\bm{\sigma}_{t}^{2}\odot\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x}_{t},t)
.

Mathematical Details of DPM-Encoder

In this section, we provide details of our DPM-Encoder introduced in Section
3.2
, which samples
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}\sim\mathrm{DPMEnc}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}},G)
. For each image
\bm{x}:=\bm{x}_{0}
, stochastic DPMs define a posterior distribution over the noisy images
\bm{x}_{1:T}
, denoted as
q(\bm{x}_{1:T}|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}})

Ho et al. 2020
;
Song et al. 2021a
. To be self-contained, we provide details of this posterior distribution for different diffusion models.

DDPM’s posterior
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}(\bm{x}_{1:T}|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}})
:
We follow the notation in
Ho et al. 2020
.

{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}(\bm{x}_{1:T}|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}}):=\prod_{t=1}^{T}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{x}_{t-1}),\quad{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{x}_{t-1}):=\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{x}_{t};\sqrt{1-{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\beta_{t}}}\bm{x}_{t-1},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\beta_{t}}\mathbf{I}\right).

(26)

DDIM’s posterior
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}q}(\bm{x}_{1:T}|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}})
:
We follow the notation in
Song et al. 2021a
.

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}q}(\bm{x}_{1:T}|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}}):={\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}q}(\bm{x}_{T}|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}})\prod_{t=2}^{T}{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}q}(\bm{x}_{t-1}|\bm{x}_{t},{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}}),

(27)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}q}(\bm{x}_{T}|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}})=\mathcal{N}(\sqrt{{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\alpha_{T}}}{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}},\left(1-{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\alpha_{T}}\right)\bm{I}),

(28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}q}(\bm{x}_{t-1}|\bm{x}_{t},{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}})=\mathcal{N}\left(\sqrt{{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\alpha_{t-1}}}{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}}+\sqrt{1-{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\alpha_{t-1}}-\sigma_{t}^{2}}\cdot\frac{\bm{x}_{t}-\sqrt{{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\alpha_{t}}}{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}}}{\sqrt{1-{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\alpha_{t}}}},\sigma_{t}^{2}\bm{I}\right),\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{where }\sigma_{t}=\eta\sqrt{\left(1-{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\alpha_{t-1}}\right)/\left(1-{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\alpha_{t}}\right)}\sqrt{1-{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\alpha_{t}}/{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\alpha_{t-1}}}.\end{split}

(29)

Based on the posterior distribution
q(\bm{x}_{1:T}|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}})
, DPM-Encoder samples the latent code
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}
by first sampling noisy images
\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{x}_{T}}
from
q(\bm{x}_{1:T}|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}})
and computing the
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}}
according to Eq. (
1
) and Eq. (
12
). Formally, we define the sampling process
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}\sim\mathrm{DPMEnc}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}},G)
as

\begin{split}&\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{T-1},{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{x}_{T}}\sim q(\bm{x}_{1:T}|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}}),\quad{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}}=\big(\bm{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{T}(\bm{x}_{t},t)\big)/\bm{\sigma}_{t},\quad t=T,\ldots,1,\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}:=\big(\bm{x}_{T}\oplus\bm{\epsilon}_{T}\oplus\cdots\oplus\bm{\epsilon}_{2}\oplus\bm{\epsilon}_{1}\big)}.\end{split}

(30)

DPM-Encoder guarantees perfect reconstruction. The proof is straightforward, provided as follows.

Proposition 1
.

(Invertibility of DPM-Encoder) For each
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}\sim\mathrm{DPMEnc}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}}|{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}},G)
defined in Eq. (
30
), we have
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}}={\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bar{\bm{x}}}:=G({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}})
, where
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bar{\bm{x}}}:=G({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{z}})
is defined as

\begin{split}&\bar{\bm{x}}_{T-1}=\bm{\mu}_{T}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{x}_{T}},T)+\bm{\sigma}_{T}\odot{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{\epsilon}_{T}},\\
&\bar{\bm{x}}_{t-1}=\bm{\mu}_{T}(\bar{\bm{x}}_{t},t)+\bm{\sigma}_{t}\odot{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}},\quad T>t>0,\\
&{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bar{\bm{x}}:=\bar{\bm{x}}_{0}}.\end{split}

(31)

Proof.

We prove
\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}=\bm{x}_{t}
for all
T-1\geq t\geq 0
by induction. The proposition holds when
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bar{\bm{x}}_{0}}={\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\bm{x}_{0}}
. To begin with,
\bar{\bm{x}}_{T-1}=\bm{x}_{T-1}
because

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{x}}_{T-1}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}_{T}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{x}_{T}},T)+\bm{\sigma}_{T}\odot{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{\epsilon}_{T}}

(32)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}_{T}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{x}_{T}},T)+\bm{\sigma}_{T}\odot\big(\bm{x}_{T-1}-\bm{\mu}_{T}({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{x}_{T}},T)\big)/\bm{\sigma}_{T}=\bm{x}_{T-1}.

(33)

For
T-1\geq t>0
, when
\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}=\bm{x}_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{x}}_{t-1}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}_{T}(\bar{\bm{x}}_{t},t)+\bm{\sigma}_{t}\odot{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}}

(34)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}_{T}(\bm{x}_{t},t)+\bm{\sigma}_{t}\odot{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}}

(35)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}_{T}(\bm{x}_{t},t)+\bm{\sigma}_{t}\odot\big(\bm{x}_{t-1}-\bm{\mu}_{T}(\bm{x}_{t},t)\big)/\bm{\sigma}_{t}=\bm{x}_{t-1}.

(36)

Appendix H
Societal Impact

In general, improved generative modeling makes it easier to generate fake media (e.g., DeepFakes;
Westerlund 2019
;
Vaccari & Chadwick 2020
) and privacy leaks (e.g., identity-conditioned human face synthesis, information leaks from large-scale pre-training data of text-to-image diffusion models). Additionally, in the particular case of this paper, one could encounter biases image editing as a result of applying CycleDiffusion to text-to-image diffusion models that reflect the natural biases in large text-image pre-training data. On the other hand, improved generative modeling can bring benefits to synthesis of humans and new ways of human communication in AR/VR. Moreover, we point out that there exist many current research works and tools that can efficiently detect fake media or can manage privacy leaks during pre-training. We encourage researchers and practitioners to consider these risks and remedies when using the methods developed in this paper.

Boundary element methods for the magnetic field integral equation on polyhedra Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Van Chien Le, Kristof Cools

Abstract
This paper provides a rigorous analysis of boundary element methods for the magnetic field integral equation on Lipschitz polyhedra. The magnetic field integral equation is widely used in practical applications to model electromagnetic scattering by a perfectly conducting body. The governing operator is shown to be coercive by means of the electric field integral operator with a purely imaginary wave number. Consequently, the continuous variational problem is uniquely solvable, given that the wave number does not belong to the spectrum of the interior Maxwell’s problem. A Petrov-Galerkin discretization scheme is then introduced, employing Raviart-Thomas boundary elements for the solution space and Buffa-Christiansen boundary elements for the test space. Under a mild assumption depending only on the geometrical domain, the corresponding discrete inf-sup condition is proven, implying the unique solvability of the discrete problem. An asymptotically quasi-optimal error estimate for numerical solutions is established, and the convergence rate of the numerical scheme is examined. In addition, the resulting matrix system is shown to be well-conditioned regardless of the mesh refinement. Some numerical results are presented to support the theoretical analysis.

Introduction

The numerical computation of electromagnetic fields scattered by a perfectly conducting body
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
usually leads to solving a Dirichlet boundary-value problem of the electric wave equation in the exterior unbounded domain
\Omega^{c}:=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
, supplemented with the Silver-Müller radiation condition at infinity. Based on the Stratton-Chu representation formula, it boils down to solving one of two standard boundary integral equations, namely the electric field integral equation (EFIE) and the magnetic field integral equation (MFIE).

With boundary element methods (BEMs) in mind, we assume that the domain
\Omega
is a polyhedron with Lipschitz continuous boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
that only consists of flat faces
\Gamma_{j}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,N_{F}
. The extension of the results to Lipschitz curvilinear polyhedra is straightforward. On polyhedral boundary
\Gamma
, the definition of Sobolev spaces
\Hs^{s}(\Gamma)
, in particular with
\left|{s}\right|\geq 1
, and differential operators are not obvious as those on smooth surfaces. By localization to each face
\Gamma_{j}
, Buffa and Ciarlet successfully developed functional setting for Maxwell’s equations on Lipschitz polyhedra, for which tangential traces of vector fields in
\HHs(\textbf{{curl}},\Omega)
, their trace spaces and relevant surface differential operators were appropriately characterized, see
[
2
,
4
,
5
]
. All ingredients necessary to develop a standard functional theory, including integration by parts formulas and Hodge decompositions, were also provided. A generalization for general Lipschitz domains was then presented in
[
7
]
.

Based on the developed functional theory for Maxwell’s equations on Lipschitz domains, a natural BEM for the EFIE on polyhedra was studied in
[
26
]
. The continuous and discrete variational problems are coercive with respect to continuous and semi-discrete Hodge decompositions, respectively. The Galerkin discretization of the EFIE employing Raviart-Thomas boundary elements was analyzed, exhibiting the quasi-optimal convergence of numerical solutions. Another discretization scheme for the EFIE on non-smooth Lipschitz domains was proposed in
[
6
]
, using standard boundary elements rather than Raviart-Thomas elements. On polyhedral surfaces, an asymptotically quasi-optimal convergence behaviour was also achieved. BEMs for Maxwell transmission problems on Lipschitz domains were investigated in
[
10
]
. The Galerkin discretization with Raviart-Thomas spaces was considered, and the results were applied to electromagnetic scattering by perfect electric conductors and by dielectric bodies. In the case of the perfect conductor scattering, only the EFIE was examined, also yielding quasi-optimal numerical solutions. To determine the actual rate of convergence on polyhedral domains, the authors of
[
6
]
and
[
26
]
all took into account the singularities of solution to Maxwell’s equations, which were investigated in
[
19
]
. More specifically, the convergence rate of BEMs for the EFIE is limited by the regularity parameter
s^{\ast}
of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\Gamma
, which depends on the geometry of
\Gamma
in neighborhoods of vertices only.

In addition to the EFIE, BEMs for combined field integral equations (CFIEs) on Lipschitz domains were investigated in
[
9
,
29
]
. When the wave number belongs to the spectrum of the interior Maxwell’s problem, the EFIE and MFIE are not uniquely solvable, resulting in resonant instabilities of Galerkin discretization systems. CFIEs owe their name to an appropriate combination of the Maxwell single and double layer potentials, thereby getting rid of the spurious resonances. In
[
9
]
, the authors exploited the coercivity of the EFIE operator by applying a compact regularization to the double layer part. The continuous and discrete variational problems were proven to be uniquely solvable at all wave numbers. A Galerkin boundary element discretization was proposed, whose numerical solutions satisfy an asymptotically quasi-optimal error estimate. The paper
[
29
]
introduced a preconditioned CFIE on which the EFIE and MFIE operators are preconditioned by their counterparts of a purely imaginary wave number. By means of a Calderón projection formula, the governing CFIE operator was cast into an equivalent formulation that, up to a compact perturbation, is
S_{i\kappa}
-coercive, with
S_{i\kappa}
the EFIE operator of purely imaginary wave number
i\kappa
. A Galerkin discretization of the mixed variational problem using the lowest-order Raviart-Thomas space and its dual Buffa-Christiansen space was analyzed, producing quasi-optimal numerical solutions. Moreover, the resulting matrix systems were shown to be well-conditioned on fine meshes and stable at resonant frequencies. Nonetheless, the proposed discretization scheme in
[
29
]
is not the one typically used by practitioners. In practice, the MFIE part of CFIEs is commonly discretized using Raviart-Thomas boundary elements for the solution space and Buffa-Christiansen elements for the test space. For some examples, the reader is referred to
[
1
,
32
,
30
,
31
]
.

This paper aims to provide a rigorous analysis of BEMs for the MFIE on Lipschitz polyhedra. Like the EFIE and CFIEs, the MFIE is widely used in practical applications to model the electromagnetic scattering by perfect electric conductors. Despite its utility, however, attention paid to the MFIE is not at the same level with those for the EFIE and CFIEs. This is evident in the relatively limited number of papers devoting to BEMs for the MFIE, with most of them contributed by practitioners, see, e.g.,
[
36
,
20
,
21
,
17
,
27
,
28
]
. On smooth surfaces, the MFIE operator is well-known to be a Fredholm operator of second kind
[
8
]
. The existence and uniqueness of a solution to the continuous variational problem of the MFIE has been shown for smooth domains, provided that the wave number is not an eigenvalue of the interior Maxwell’s problem, see, e.g.,
[
8
]
. In the discrete setting, the existence does not necessarily hold true, since the boundary element subspace may not be dual to itself. More precisely, the Galerkin discretization of the identity operator (the principal part of the MFIE operator) using lowest-order Raviart-Thomas elements has been proven to not satisfy the discrete inf-sup condition, see
[
13
, Section 3.1]
. As a result, the coercivity of the corresponding Galerkin discretization of the MFIE does not hold true.

The contribution of this paper is twofold. First, we prove that the continuous variational problem of the MFIE is also uniquely solvable on Lipschitz domains, of course provided that the wave number does not belong to the spectrum of the interior Maxwell’s problem. More particularly, we show that the MFIE operator is
S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
-coercive up to a compact perturbation, for any
\kappa^{\prime}>0
. It is noteworthy that on non-smooth Lipschitz surfaces, the average of exterior and interior traces of the double layer potential operator is not necessarily compact. Secondly, we analyze the mixed discretization scheme for the MFIE introduced in
[
17
]
, when the boundary
\Gamma
is Lipschitz continuous. The authors of
[
17
]
proposed a Petrov-Galerkin discretization of the MFIE employing Raviart-Thomas boundary elements for the solution space and Buffa-Christiansen boundary elements for the test space, on which they exploited the duality between the Buffa-Christiansen space and the Raviart-Thomas space
[
3
]
. Given that the average double layer boundary integral operator is compact on smooth surfaces, this duality supplies the coercivity. When the surface
\Gamma
is solely Lipschitz continuous, the coercivity of the mixed discretization remains valid if an additional stability condition is satisfied, which only depends on the boundary
\Gamma
. This condition is mild as it is fulfilled for typical geometries in practical applications. As a consequence, the asymptotic quasi-optimality of numerical solutions is obtained. In addition, we show that the resulting matrix system is well-conditioned regardless of the boundary mesh refinement. To end this paragraph, we state here the essential assumption on the wave number of Maxwell’s equations and stop repeating it in the remainder of the paper.

Assumption 1
.

The wave number is bounded away from the spectrum of the interior Maxwell’s problem.

This paper is organized as follows. The next section recalls the standard functional setting for Maxwell’s equations on Lipschitz polyhedra, including the tangential trace operators, trace spaces, relevant tangential differential operators and an integration by parts formula. Section

The numerical verification of the stability condition is discussed in Section
6
. Section
7
presents some numerical results aiming to support the numerical analysis. Finally, we provide some conclusion remarks and discuss some possibilities for future works in Section
8

\left({\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}}\right)_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}:=\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u},S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},\qquad\qquad\quad\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma),

is a Hilbert space. Its induced norm is denoted by
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}
. Please note that the two spaces
\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}
and
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
only differ in their equipped norms. In what follows, the space
\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}
is only used when its inner product and norm are involved or for a compact notation, whereas
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
is used as the space of their elements (independent of
\kappa^{\prime}
).

The next results concern the contraction properties of the operators
2C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
and
\tfrac{1}{2}Id\pm C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
. The latter were shown in
[
39
]
by extending the idea in
[
38
, Theorem 3.1]
for Laplace’s equation. In the following, we prove the contraction of
2C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
with respect to the norm
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}
by means of the Calderón projection formulas for Maxwell’s equations.

Lemma 7
(Contraction property).

For any
\kappa^{\prime}>0
, there holds that

\displaystyle\left\langle{\boldsymbol{v},C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}=\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u},C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma),

(8)

\displaystyle\left\lVert{C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\leq\beta\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}},

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma),

(9)

where the constant
\beta>0
is defined by

\beta(\kappa^{\prime},\Gamma):=\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{4}-\dfrac{{\kappa^{\prime}}^{2}}{C_{S}^{2}}}<\dfrac{1}{2},\qquad\quad C_{S}(\kappa^{\prime},\Gamma):=\sup_{\boldsymbol{u}\in\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\dfrac{\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}}.

(10)

Proof.

The symmetry of
C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
with respect to
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}
can be obtained by following the lines in the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 3.9]
, with the help of the integration by parts formula (
1
). For the contraction property of
2C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
, we need the following Calderón projection formulas, which are derived from the Stratton-Chu representation formula (
6
) for
\sigma=i\kappa^{\prime}
and
[
8
, Formulation 35]

\displaystyle C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\circ S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\circ C_{i\kappa^{\prime}},

\displaystyle C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\circ C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4}Id+{\kappa^{\prime}}^{2}S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\circ S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}.

Based on these identities and the symmetry of
S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
and
C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\left\lVert{C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u},\left({S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\circ C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}}\right)\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u},\left({C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\circ C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\circ S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}}\right)\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4}\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u},S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}+{\kappa^{\prime}}^{2}\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u},\left({S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\circ S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\circ S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}}\right)\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}-{\kappa^{\prime}}^{2}\left\lVert{S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left({\dfrac{1}{4}-\dfrac{{\kappa^{\prime}}^{2}}{C_{S}^{2}}}\right)\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}.

The contraction properties of
\tfrac{1}{2}Id\pm C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
with respect to
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}
can be achieved as a consequence of (
9
).

Corollary 8
.

For any
\kappa^{\prime}>0
, the operators
\tfrac{1}{2}Id\pm C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
are contractions with respect to the norm
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}
. In particular, for all
\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
, it holds that

\left({\dfrac{1}{2}-\beta}\right)\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\leq\left\lVert{\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id\pm C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}}\right)\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\leq\left({\dfrac{1}{2}+\beta}\right)\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}.

(11)

The following
S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
-coercivity of
\tfrac{1}{2}Id\pm C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
is crucial in proving the coercivity of the MFIE operator in the next section.

Lemma 9
(
S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
-coercivity).

For any
\kappa^{\prime}>0
, there holds that

\left({\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id\pm C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}}\right)\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}}\right)_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\geq\left({\dfrac{1}{4}-\beta^{2}}\right)\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma).

(12)

Proof.

For all
\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left({\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id\pm C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}}\right)\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}}\right)_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}}\right)_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\pm\left({C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}}\right)_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}-\left\lVert{C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}-\dfrac{1}{4}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}-\left\lVert{C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\geq\left({\dfrac{1}{4}-\beta^{2}}\right)\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}.

Remark 10
.

The contraction properties (
9
) and (
11
) remain valid when replacing
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}
by the equivalent norm
\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\cdot}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}
. The space
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
equipped with this norm also defines a Hilbert space. In addition, analogous to (
12
), the operators
\tfrac{1}{2}Id\pm C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
are also
S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}^{-1}
-coercive, i.e., for all
\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
, it holds that

\left\langle{\left({S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\circ\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id\pm C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}}\right)}\right)\boldsymbol{u},\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}\geq\left({\dfrac{1}{4}-\beta^{2}}\right)\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}.

4
The magnetic field integral equation

In this section, we derive the MFIE and show the unique solvability of its continuous variational formulation. Taking the exterior Neumann trace
\gamma_{N}^{+}
of the Stratton-Chu representation formula (
6
) with wave number
\sigma=\kappa
gives the standard MFIE of the form

\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{\kappa}}\right)(\boldsymbol{u})=\boldsymbol{f},

(13)

where
\boldsymbol{u}:=\gamma_{N}^{+}\boldsymbol{e}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
is the unknown exterior Neumann trace of the scattered electric field, and
\boldsymbol{f}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
is given via the Dirichlet boundary condition (
4
) as

\boldsymbol{f}:=\kappa^{2}S_{\kappa}(\gamma^{+}_{D}\boldsymbol{e}^{in}).

4.1
Flawed idea

One may think of deriving the variational formulation of (
13
) using the
\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}
-inner product, i.e.,

\left\langle{\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{\kappa}}\right)\boldsymbol{u},S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}=\left\langle{\boldsymbol{f},S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},\qquad\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma).

(14)

Due to Lemmas
5
and
9
, the governing MFIE operator is compact perturbation of a
\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}
-elliptic operator, for any
\kappa^{\prime}>0
. Given that it is injective (i.e., Assumption
1
is satisfied), the variational formulation (
14
) has a unique solution
\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
. Moreover, the Galerkin method converges quasi-optimally when applied to this formulation, see
[
37
, Theorem 4.2.9]
.

Despite that, the equation (
14
) involves three boundary integrals rather than two as in standard variational formulations for boundary integral equations (e.g., for EFIE in
[
26
]
). Computing the entries of the Galerkin matrices for (
14
) requires the computation of integrals over all of
\Gamma
, even when the basis functions have support on small subsets of
\Gamma
. This disadvantage leads the assembling time of (
14
) even exceeding the time for solving it, making the formulation (
14
) undesired in practice.

4.2
Variational formulation

The appropriate variational formulation of (
13
) is with respect to the duality pairing
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}
: find
\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
such that

b(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})=\left\langle{\boldsymbol{f},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},\qquad\qquad\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma),

(15)

where sesquilinear form
b:\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)\times\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)\to\mathbb{C}
is defined by

b(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}):=\left\langle{\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{\kappa}}\right)\boldsymbol{u},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},\qquad\qquad\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma).

On smooth surfaces, it is well-known that the operator
C_{\kappa}
is compact, see, e.g.,
[
33
, Section 5.4]
or
[
8
, Lemma 11]
. As a result, the self-duality of the space
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
with respect to
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}
ensures the coercivity of the sesquilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)
. More precisely, for any
\kappa^{\prime}>0
, it holds that

\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}\in\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\dfrac{\left|{\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|}{\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}}=\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}},\qquad\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma).

(16)

To prove this identity, one just need to take into account the fact that

\left|{\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|=\left|{\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u},\left({S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\circ S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}}\right)\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|\leq\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}},

for all
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
, and that the equality occurs when
\boldsymbol{v}=S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u}
. The relation (
16
) implies that both the inf-sup and the boundedness constants (with respect to the norms specified in (
16
)) of the duality pairing
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}
are 1.

On polyhedral boundaries, the compactness of
C_{\kappa}
does not necessarily hold true. In this paper, we exploit the compactness of the operator
C_{\kappa}-C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
(cf. Lemma
5
) and the
S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
-coercivity stated in (
12
) to prove the coercivity of the sesquilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)
. For that reason, we split
b(\cdot,\cdot)
into a principal part
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and a compact sesquilinear form
t(\cdot,\cdot)
as follows

a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}):=\left\langle{\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}}\right)\boldsymbol{u},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},\qquad\quad t(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}):=\left\langle{\left({C_{\kappa}-C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}}\right)\boldsymbol{u},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

for some
\kappa^{\prime}>0
. The
S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
-coercivity of the operator
\tfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
(cf. Lemma
9
) can be reformulated in terms of the sesquilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
as

a(\boldsymbol{u},S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u})\geq\left({\dfrac{1}{4}-\beta^{2}}\right)\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}},\qquad\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma).

(17)

The following generalized Gårding inequality for
b(\cdot,\cdot)
is an immediate consequence of the inequality (
17
).

Theorem 11
(Generalized Gårding inequality).

For any
\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}>0
, it holds that

\left|{b(\boldsymbol{u},S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u})-t(\boldsymbol{u},S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u})}\right|\geq\left({\dfrac{1}{4}-\beta^{2}}\right)\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma).

(18)

Finally, the unique solvability of the variational problem (
15
) follows from the injectivity of the MFIE (i.e., Assumption
1
) and the generalized Gårding inequality (
18
), by means of a Fredholm alternative argument (cf.
[
6
, Proposition 3]
).

Corollary 12
.

Let Assumption
1
be satisfied. Then, there exists a unique
\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
that solves (
15
).

5
Galerkin discretization

In this section, we analyze the Galerkin boundary element discretization scheme for the variational formulation (
15
) that was firstly introduced in
[
17
]
.

5.1
Galerkin boundary element discretization

Let
(\Gamma_{h})_{h>0}
be a family of shape-regular, quasi-uniform triangulations of the polyhedral surface
\Gamma

[
14
]
. The parameter
h
stands for the meshwidth, which is the length of the longest edge of triangulation
\Gamma_{h}
. We denote by
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
\mathcal{E}_{h}
, respectively, the sets of all triangles and edges of
\Gamma_{h}
. On each triangle
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we equip the lowest-order triangular Raviart-Thomas space
[
35
]

\RT_{0}(T):=\left\{{\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\boldsymbol{a}+b\boldsymbol{x}:\boldsymbol{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{2},b\in\mathbb{C}}\right\}.

This local space gives rise to the global
\textup{div}_{\Gamma}
-conforming boundary element space

\UUs_{h}:=\left\{{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma):\left.\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right|_{T}\in\RT_{0}(T),\,\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\right\},

which is endowed with the edge degrees of freedom
[
26
]

\phi_{e}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}):=\int_{e}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\times\textbf{n}_{j})\cdot\,\mathrm{d}s,

for all
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
, where
\textbf{n}_{j}
is the normal of a face
\Gamma_{j}
in whose the closure of
e
is contained. The boundary element space
\UUs_{h}
is also called the Rao-Wilton-Glisson space
[
34
]
.

The Raviart-Thomas boundary elements have been widely used in the Galerkin discretization of the EFIE and CFIEs, both for the solution space and the test space, see, e.g.,
[
26
,
9
]
. These discretization schemes are primarily based on the ellipticity of the operator
S_{\kappa}
. Unfortunately, this method fails when applied to the MFIE, even on smooth surfaces. It has been shown in
[
13
, Section 3.1]
that there exist
C>0
and
\WWs_{h}\subset\UUs_{h}
such that

\sup_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\WWs_{h}}\dfrac{\left|{\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\overline{\boldsymbol{w}_{h}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{w}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}}\leq Ch^{-1/2}.

In other words, the subspace
\UUs_{h}
is not dual to itself with respect to the pairing
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}
.
In fact, there is ample numerical evidence that for a very broad class of situations that are of practical interest, the discretization matrix of the anti-symmetric pairing
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}
using the boundary element space
\UUs_{h}
is singular, with a nullspace whose dimension is a sizeable fraction of the dimension of
\UUs_{h}

[
18
]
.

In order to preserve the inf-sup condition for the Galerkin discretization of
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}
, the authors of
[
17
]
proposed the test space
\VVs_{h}\subset\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
consisting of the Buffa-Christiansen boundary elements
[
3
]
(see Figure
1
). More particularly,
\dim\VVs_{h}=\dim\UUs_{h}
and the following duality between
\UUs_{h}
and
\VVs_{h}
holds

\inf_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}}\dfrac{\left|{\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}}\geq\alpha,

(19)

for some constant
\alpha(\kappa^{\prime},\Gamma)>0
independent of
h
, with
\kappa^{\prime}>0
. Obviously,
\alpha\leq 1
due to (
16
). In what follows,
\alpha
represents the maximal value that still fulfills (
19
). Then, the Petrov-Galerkin boundary element discretization of the variational formulation (
15
) reads as: find
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}
such that

b(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})=\left\langle{\boldsymbol{f},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},\qquad\qquad\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}.

(20)

The following lemma presents the discrete inf-sup condition for the sesquilinear form

restricted on
\UUs_{h}\times\VVs_{h}
. This condition is a weaker discrete counterpart of the

Lemma 13
.

For any
\kappa^{\prime}>0
, the following inf-sup condition is satisfied

\inf_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}}\dfrac{\left|{a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})}\right|}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}}\geq\dfrac{\alpha}{2}-\beta.

(21)

Proof.

The following estimate holds true due to the discrete inf-sup condition (
19
) and the contraction property (
9
)

\displaystyle\inf_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}}\dfrac{\left|{a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})}\right|}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\inf_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}}\dfrac{\left|{\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\quad-\sup_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}}\dfrac{\left|{\left\langle{C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{\alpha}{2}-\sup_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}}\dfrac{\left\lVert{C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}}\geq\dfrac{\alpha}{2}-\beta.

The constant
\tfrac{\alpha}{2}-\beta
is only dependent of
\kappa^{\prime}
and
\Gamma
, but independent of
h
. Moreover, it can be non-positive. In order to ensure that the inf-sup condition (
21
) is satisfied with a positive constant, we make the following assumption.

Assumption 14
(Stability condition).

There is a real number
\kappa^{\prime}_{0}>0
such that

\alpha(\kappa^{\prime}_{0},\Gamma)>2\beta(\kappa^{\prime}_{0},\Gamma),

(22)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are defined by (
19
) and (
10
), respectively.

Now, we shall show the discrete inf-sup condition for the sesquilinear form
b:\UUs_{h}\times\VVs_{h}\to\mathbb{C}
, which is crucial in establishing the unique solvability of the discrete variational problem (
20
). The next result can be proven by following the hints in
[
22
, Exercise 26.5]
, with the approximability of
\UUs_{h}
and
\VVs_{h}
in
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
stated in
[
8
, Corollary 5]
and
[
3
]
, respectively. We leave the complete proof for the reader.

Lemma 15
.

Let
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}_{0}
fulfill Assumptions
1
and
14
, respectively. Then, there exist an
h_{0}>0
and a constant
\gamma>0
such that for all
h<h_{0}

\inf_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}}\dfrac{\left|{b(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})}\right|}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa_{0}^{\prime}}}\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa_{0}^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa_{0}^{\prime}}}}\geq\gamma.

(23)

The unique solvability of the discrete variational problem (
20
) and the asymptotic quasi-optimality of the discrete solutions immediately follow from Lemma
15
, see
[
37
, Theorem 4.2.1]
.

Theorem 16
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
14
be satisfied. Then, there exists an
h_{0}>0
such that for all
h<h_{0}
, the discrete problem (
20
) has a unique solution
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}
. In addition, the discrete solutions
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
converge to the solution
\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
of the problem (
15
) and satisfy the asymptotically quasi-optimal error estimate

\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}\leq C\inf_{\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{w}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)},

(24)

for some constant
C>0
depending only on
\Gamma
and
\kappa
.

It is worth mentioning that the quasi-optimal error estimate (
24
) alone does not imply the actual convergence rate of the Galerkin discretization scheme (
20
). When investigating the convergence rate of a Galerkin discretization scheme for Maxwell’s equations on polyhedral domains, we must take into account the singularities of the solutions, as shown by Costabel and Dauge in
[
19
]
. In particular, the error of best approximations by lowest-order Raviart-Thomas boundary elements was established in
[
26
, Lemma 8.1]
, which is limited by a regularity exponent
s^{\ast}>0
of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{\Gamma}
. The parameter
s^{\ast}
depends only on the geometry of
\Gamma
in neighborhoods of vertices. Moreover, there exist polyhedral vertices for which
s^{\ast}>0
is arbitrarily small, as demonstrated in
[
6
]
. We refer the reader to
[
6
, Theorem 8]
and
[
26
, Theorem 2.1]
for more details on the regularity parameter
s^{\ast}
. Now, we are in the position to restate Lemma 8.1 in
[
26
]
, which we exploit for our convergence analysis.

Lemma 17
.

If
\boldsymbol{z}\in\HHs^{s}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma),s>0
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
, it holds that

\inf_{\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{w}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}\leq Ch^{\min\left\{{\tfrac{3}{2}-\varepsilon,s+\tfrac{1}{2}-\varepsilon,1+s^{\ast},s+s^{\ast}}\right\}}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{z}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{s}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)},

with constant
C>0
depending only on
\Gamma,s^{\ast}
and
\varepsilon
.

Finally, we are able to establish the convergence rate of the Galerkin discretization (
20
) of the MFIE, employing Raviart-Thomas boundary elements for the solution space and Buffa-Christiansen boundary elements for the test space.

Theorem 18
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
14
be satisfied. In addition, we assume that the solution to (
15
) satisfies
\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{s}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma),s>0
. Then, there exists an
h_{0}>0
such that for all
h<h_{0}
and for any
\varepsilon>0
, the unique solution
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}
to (
20
) satisfies

\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}\leq Ch^{\min\left\{{\tfrac{3}{2}-\varepsilon,s+\tfrac{1}{2}-\varepsilon,1+s^{\ast},s+s^{\ast}}\right\}}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{s}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)},

(25)

with constant
C>0
depending only on
\Gamma,\kappa,s^{\ast}
and
\varepsilon
.

5.2
Matrix representation

Let
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{1},\boldsymbol{u}_{2},\ldots,\boldsymbol{u}_{N}\}
and
\{\boldsymbol{v}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}_{2},\ldots,\boldsymbol{v}_{N}\}
be bases of the boundary element spaces
\UUs_{h}
and
\VVs_{h}
, respectively, with
N:=\dim\UUs_{h}=\dim\VVs_{h}
. The solution
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}
to (
20
) can be represented by its expansion coefficient vector
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}:=(\widehat{u}_{1},\widehat{u}_{2},\ldots,\widehat{u}_{N})^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
such that

\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{u}_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}.

(26)

Next, the Galerkin matrices of the sesquilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)
and the pairing
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}
are respectively defined by

{[\BBs]}_{mn}:=b(\boldsymbol{u}_{n},\boldsymbol{v}_{m}),\qquad\qquad\quad{[\DDs]}_{mn}:=\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u}_{n},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{m}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

with
m,n=1,2,\ldots,N
. Then, the coefficient vector
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}
is the solution to the following matrix system

\BBs\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}=\boldsymbol{b},

(27)

where the right-hand side vector
{[\boldsymbol{b}]}_{m}:=\left\langle{\boldsymbol{f},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{m}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}
. The solvability of (
27
) is a direct consequence of Theorem
16
.

Corollary 19
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
14
be satisfied. Then, there exists an
h_{0}>0
such that for all
h<h_{0}
, the matrix equation (
27
) has a unique solution
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. In addition, the corresponding
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}
defined by (
26
) is a quasi-optimal approximation to the solution
\boldsymbol{u}
of (
15
) in the sense of (
24
).

Now, we investigate the conditioning property of the system (
27
). Let
\lambda_{\max}(\AAs)
and
\lambda_{\min}(\AAs)
be the maximal and minimal (by moduli) eigenvalues of a square matrix
\AAs
, respectively. The spectral condition number of
\AAs
is then defined by

\cond(\AAs):=\dfrac{\left|{\lambda_{\max}(\AAs)}\right|}{\left|{\lambda_{\min}(\AAs)}\right|}.

The following result shows that the problem (
27
) is well-conditioned regardless of the boundary mesh refinement.

Theorem 20
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
14
be satisfied. Then, there exist an
h_{0}>0
and a constant
C>0
such that for all
h<h_{0}

\cond(\DDs^{-1}\BBs)\leq C.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 2.1]
. Let the subspaces
\UUs_{h}
and
\VVs_{h}
be equipped with the norm
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}_{0}}}
, where
\kappa^{\prime}_{0}
fulfills Assumption
14
. We denote by
B_{h}:\UUs_{h}\to\VVs_{h}^{\prime}
and
D_{h}:\UUs_{h}\to\VVs_{h}^{\prime}
the bounded linear operators associated with the sesquilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)
and the pairing
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}
, respectively. The following estimates hold true due to (
16
), (
19
) and (
23
)

\displaystyle\left\lVert{D_{h}}\right\rVert_{\UUs_{h}\to\VVs_{h}^{\prime}}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\left\lVert{D_{h}^{-1}}\right\rVert_{\VVs_{h}^{\prime}\to\UUs_{h}}\leq\alpha^{-1},

\displaystyle\left\lVert{B_{h}}\right\rVert_{\UUs_{h}\to\VVs_{h}^{\prime}}\leq\left\lVert{b}\right\rVert,

\displaystyle\left\lVert{B_{h}^{-1}}\right\rVert_{\VVs_{h}^{\prime}\to\UUs_{h}}\leq\gamma^{-1}.

Next, let
\mathbb{C}^{N}
be equipped with the norm
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}_{0}}}
via the coefficient isomorphism with respect to the basis
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{1},\boldsymbol{u}_{2},\ldots,\boldsymbol{u}_{N}\}
. Then, there hold that

\displaystyle\left|{\lambda_{\max}(\DDs^{-1}\BBs)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\DDs^{-1}\BBs}\right\rVert\leq\alpha^{-1}\left\lVert{b}\right\rVert,

\displaystyle\left|{\lambda_{\max}(\BBs^{-1}\DDs)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\BBs^{-1}\DDs}\right\rVert\leq\gamma^{-1}.

Hence, we can conclude

\cond(\DDs^{-1}\BBs)=\left|{\lambda_{\max}(\DDs^{-1}\BBs)}\right|\left|{\lambda_{\max}(\BBs^{-1}\DDs)}\right|\leq\dfrac{\left\lVert{b}\right\rVert}{\gamma\alpha}.

Remark 21
.

In the paper
[
12
]
, the authors introduced a family of 3D open-book Lipschitz polyhedra on which the Galerkin discretization of second-kind boundary integral equations for the Laplace and Helmholtz problems do not converge. The negative claims in that paper do not contradict the present results, since in this paper we consider the relevant operators in the natural trace space
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
rather than the space
\LLs^{2}(\Gamma)
as in
[
12
]
.

Remark 22
.

The introduction of the purely imaginary wave number
i\kappa^{\prime}
(and
\kappa^{\prime}_{0}
) is just to check the “quality” of
\Gamma
whether the proposed Galerkin method for the MFIE converges on that domain. Once Assumption
14
is verified, the solvability of the discrete problem (
20
), the quasi-optimal error estimate and the convergence of numerical solutions are independent of
\kappa^{\prime}_{0}
. However, the bound of the condition number in Theorem
20
might be dependent of
\kappa^{\prime}_{0}
via the constants
\alpha
and
\gamma
.

6
Verification of Assumption
14

Assumption
14
, along with Assumption
1
, gives a sufficient condition for the unique solvability of the discrete problem (
20
). However, verifying Assumption
14
for each domain is not straightforward. In this section, we propose a numerical scheme to approximately compute the constants
\alpha
and
\beta
, then verify this assumption in practice.

We first evaluate the two terms in the denominator of (
19
). For
\kappa^{\prime}>0
, let
\SSs_{\kappa^{\prime}}
be the Galerkin matrix of the
\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}
-inner product
\left({\cdot,\cdot}\right)_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}:\UUs_{h}\times\UUs_{h}\to\mathbb{C}
. Then, the
\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}
-norm of
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}
can be expressed as the
\textbf{{l}}^{2}
-norm of its coefficient vector
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}
as follows

\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}=\sqrt{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{\mathsf{T}}\SSs_{\kappa^{\prime}}\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}=\left\lVert{\SSs_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{1/2}\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\textbf{{l}}^{2}}.

(28)

In order to evaluate
\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}
, with
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}
, we approximate
S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}
by its projection
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\in\widetilde{\UUs}_{h}
with respect to the
\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}
-inner product, i.e.,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\in\widetilde{\UUs}_{h}
is the unique solution to the following equation

\left\langle{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h},S_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\overline{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}_{h}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}=-\left\langle{\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\overline{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}_{h}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},\qquad\qquad\quad\forall\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}_{h}\in\widetilde{\UUs}_{h}.

(29)

Here, the subspace
\widetilde{\UUs}_{h}
consists of all Raviart-Thomas boundary elements defined on the barycentric refinement
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{h}
of
\Gamma_{h}
. Please note that
\UUs_{h}\subset\widetilde{\UUs}_{h},\VVs_{h}\subset\widetilde{\UUs}_{h}
, and
\dim\widetilde{\UUs}_{h}=6\dim\UUs_{h}=6\dim\VVs_{h}=6N
. Let
\widetilde{\SSs}_{\kappa^{\prime}}
and
\GGs
be the Galerkin matrices of the sesquilinear forms defined by the left-hand side and right-hand side of (
29
), respectively. Then, the coefficient vector
\widehat{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}
of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}
with respect to the basis of
\widetilde{\UUs}_{h}
is determined by

\widehat{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}=\widetilde{\SSs}_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{-1}\GGs\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},

where
\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}
is the coefficient vector of
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}
with respect to the basis
\{\boldsymbol{v}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}_{2},\ldots,\boldsymbol{v}_{N}\}
. Thus,
\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}
is approximated by

\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\approx\left\lVert{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}=\sqrt{\widehat{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}^{\mathsf{T}}\widetilde{\SSs}_{\kappa^{\prime}}\widehat{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}}=\left\lVert{\left({\GGs^{\mathsf{T}}\widetilde{\SSs}_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{-1}\GGs}\right)^{1/2}\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\textbf{{l}}^{2}}.

(30)

Let
\MMs_{\kappa^{\prime}}:=\GGs^{\mathsf{T}}\widetilde{\SSs}_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{-1}\GGs
. The matrix
\MMs_{\kappa^{\prime}}
is positive-definite, as
\widetilde{\SSs}_{\kappa^{\prime}}
is positive-definite and
\GGs
is injective. Based on the matrix representations (
28
) and (
30
), the left-hand side of the inf-sup condition (
19
) can be expressed in the following form

\displaystyle\inf_{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}}\dfrac{\left|{\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}\left\lVert{S^{-1}_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\kappa^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\approx\inf_{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}}\sup_{\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}}\dfrac{\left|{\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}^{\mathsf{T}}\DDs\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}\right|}{\left\lVert{\SSs_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{1/2}\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\textbf{{l}}^{2}}\left\lVert{\MMs_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{1/2}\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\textbf{{l}}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}}\sup_{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{h}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}}\dfrac{\left|{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{h}^{\mathsf{T}}\MMs_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{-\mathsf{T}/2}\DDs\SSs_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{-1/2}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}}\right|}{\left\lVert{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\textbf{{l}}^{2}}\left\lVert{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\textbf{{l}}^{2}}},

where
\MMs_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{-\mathsf{T}/2}=\left({\MMs_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{-1/2}}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}
. Hence, for a sufficiently small
h>0
, the constant
\alpha
in (
19
) can be approximated by

\alpha\approx\left|{\lambda_{\min}\left({\MMs_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{-\mathsf{T}/2}\DDs\SSs_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{-1/2}}\right)}\right|.

Please bear in mind that the space
\mathbb{C}^{N}
is now equipped with the Euclidean
\textbf{{l}}^{2}
-norm. Analogously, taking into account the property (
9
),
\beta
can be estimated as

\beta\approx\left|{\lambda_{\max}\left({\MMs_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{-\mathsf{T}/2}\KKs_{\kappa^{\prime}}\SSs_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{-1/2}}\right)}\right|,

(31)

where
\KKs_{\kappa^{\prime}}
is the Galerkin matrix associated with the operator
C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}
, i.e.,

{[\KKs_{\kappa^{\prime}}]}_{mn}:=\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u}_{n},C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{m}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},\qquad\quad m,n=1,2,\ldots,N.

Finally, we can verify Assumption
14
for the boundary
\Gamma
by checking if the condition
\tfrac{\alpha}{2}-\beta>0
is fulfilled by some
\kappa^{\prime}>0
.

Remark 23
.

Another scheme to approximate the constant
\beta
is to replace the Galerkin matrix
\KKs_{\kappa^{\prime}}
by its counterpart
\widetilde{\KKs}_{\kappa^{\prime}}
tested with
\UUs_{h}
, i.e.,

{[\widetilde{\KKs}_{\kappa^{\prime}}]}_{mn}:=\left\langle{\boldsymbol{u}_{n},C_{i\kappa^{\prime}}\overline{\boldsymbol{u}_{m}}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},\qquad\qquad m,n=1,2,\ldots,N.

Then, the matrices
\GGs
and
\MMs_{\kappa^{\prime}}
must also be changed accordingly (the latter is denoted by
\widetilde{\MMs}_{\kappa^{\prime}}
). The advantage of this approach is the symmetry of the matrix
\widetilde{\KKs}_{\kappa^{\prime}}
, due to (
8
). As a consequence, all eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\MMs}_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{-\mathsf{T}/2}\widetilde{\KKs}_{\kappa^{\prime}}\SSs_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{-1/2}
are real, and its maximal eigenvalue
\left|{\lambda_{\max}}\right|
might give a better approximation of
\beta
. The disadvantage is that we have to additionally compute the matrix
\widetilde{\MMs}_{\kappa^{\prime}}
and its square root inverse, which is quite expensive. From authors’ experience, these two approaches produce similar results. Therefore, the scheme (
31
) is recommended.

Remark 24
.

From authors’ experience, Assumption
14
is satisfied for typical geometries in practical applications, usually with larger
\kappa^{\prime}>0
for less regular domains (see Figure
3
for an example). When discretizing the integral operators with larger
\kappa^{\prime}
, finer meshes and/or more quadrature points are required to accurately compute the non-zero entries of the corresponding Galerkin matrices. As a result, the verification step may cost more than the computational cost of solving the MFIE itself. Therefore, verifying Assumption
14
beforehand is usually not desirable in practice.

8
Conclusions

We have provided rigorous analysis of a Galerkin boundary element discretization of the magnetic field integral equation on Lipschitz polyhedra. The continuous variational problem has been shown to be uniquely solvable, provided that the wave number does not belong to the spectrum of the interior Maxwell’s problem. A Petrov-Galerkin discretization of the variational formulation employing Raviart-Thomas boundary elements for the solution space and Buffa-Christiansen elements for the test space has been analyzed. Under a stability condition, the solvability and the well-conditioning of the discrete system have been proven, and the convergence rate of the discretization scheme has been investigated. A numerical scheme to verify the stability condition has been proposed, showing that this condition is unproblematic in practice. Some numerical results have also been presented, corroborating the theoretical analysis as well as the effect of the singularities of Maxwell’s solutions on polyhedra to the convergence rate. The analyzed discretization scheme has been widely used in practical applications.

In future works, we shall investigate Galerkin discretization schemes for combined field integral equations and single source integral equations that employ the proposed discretization scheme for the double layer part.

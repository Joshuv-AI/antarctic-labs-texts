Infinite-horizon Fuk-Nagaev inequalities
A.J.E.M. Janssen, B. Zwart

Abstract
We develop explicit bounds for the tail of the distribution for the all-time supremum of a random walk with negative drift, where the increments have a truncated heavy-tailed distribution. As an application, we consider a ruin problem in the presence of re-insurance. AMS subject classification: 60F10, 91B30 Keywords: random walk, heavy tails, concentration bounds, re-insurance.



1
Introduction and main results

Let
X_{i},i\geq 1,
be a real-valued i.i.d. sequence with
\mu=\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}\right]<0
,
\mu_{\beta}=\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{1}|^{\beta}\right]<\infty
for some
\beta>1
, and
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{sX_{1}}\right]=\infty
for
s>0
, so that
X_{1}
is heavy-tailed. Define for
y>0
,
S_{0}(y)=0,S_{n}(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\min\{X_{i},y\},n\geq 1,
and

M(y)=\sup_{n\geq 0}S_{n}(y).

(1.1)

We derive explicit bounds for
\mathbb{P}\left(M(y)>x\right)
, motivated by our desire to understand the impact of truncation on the behavior of
M(y)
. Truncated heavy tails naturally occur in risk theory
[
4
]
, where large claims can be re-insured. Other applications can be found in queueing theory, in which large jobs may get terminated
[
14
]
, and scale-free random graphs, where the degree of a vertex cannot be larger than the size of the graph
[
15
,
20
]
.

Asymptotic properties of
\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}(y)>x\right)
where
x
and
y
go to
\infty
at a rate comparable to
n
have been analyzed by Chakrabarty
[
7
,
8
]
.
Asymptotics for the finite-time ruin probability for various re-insurance contracts have been derived in
[
2
,
5
,
9
,
19
,
10
]
.

For
M(y)
, such results seem unavailable.
If
y
is fixed, Cramér-Lundberg theory
[
3
]
implies that

\mathbb{P}\left(M(y)>x\right)\leq e^{-\gamma(y)x},

(1.2)

with
\gamma(y)
the unique strictly positive solution of the Cramér-Lundberg equation

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{s\min\{X_{1},y\}}\right]=1,

(1.3)

so that
\gamma(y)=\sup\{s\geq 0:\mathbb{E}\left[e^{s\min\{X_{1},y\}}\right]\leq 1\}
.
Since
X_{1}
is heavy-tailed,
\gamma(y)\rightarrow 0
as
y\rightarrow\infty
. In

[
4
]
, the authors derive asymptotic expansions for
\gamma(y)
in the slightly different setting of a Lévy process with truncated jumps;
their results correspond to the case where
\mathbb{P}\left(X_{1}>x\right)
is either regularly varying or of the form
e^{-\sqrt{x}}
.

We mainly focus on non-asymptotic estimates. In particular, we aim to derive upper bounds for
\mathbb{P}\left(M(y)>x\right)
. Such bounds can be valuable, as convergence rates of asymptotic estimates can be slow for heavy-tailed random variables
[
17
]
.
Our first main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a
y_{\beta}<\infty
such that

\mathbb{P}\left(M(y)>x\right)\leq y^{-(\beta-1)x/y},\hskip 28.45274ptx>0,y>y_{\beta}.

(1.4)

An explicit expression for
y_{\beta}
is given in (
2.23
) below.
Theorem
1.1
is aimed at random walks with step size distributions for which
\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}^{k}\right]=\infty
for some
k>0
.
Our next result covers cases where
\mathbb{P}\left(X_{1}>x\right)
has a lighter tail, e.g.,
\mathbb{P}\left(X_{1}>x\right)
is of the form
\exp\{-(\log x)^{\xi}\},\xi>1
(which includes log-normal tails) or

\exp\{-x^{\xi}\},\xi\in(0,1)
(which includes Weibull tails). Define

q(x)=-\log\mathbb{P}\left(X_{1}>x\right).

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
\mu_{2}<\infty
and suppose that there exists a
\kappa\in(0,1)
and
y_{\kappa}>0
such that

(\log y)^{1+\kappa}\leq q(y)\leq y^{1-\kappa}
for
y\geq y_{\kappa}
, and that
q(y)
is concave.
Then, for every
\eta<1
, there exists a
y_{\eta}^{*}<\infty
such that

\mathbb{P}\left(M(y)>x\right)\leq\left(\frac{y\mathbb{P}\left(X_{1}>y\right)}{\eta|\mu|}\right)^{\frac{x}{y}},\hskip 28.45274ptx>0,y>y^{*}_{\eta}.

(1.5)

An explicit expression for
y^{*}_{\eta}
is given in (
3.18
) below.

Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
complement similar bounds for
\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}(y)>x\right)
, which are known as Fuk-Nagaev inequalities after
[
12
]
.
The approach to proving such inequalities, surveyed in
[
18
]
, has been modified in
[
6
]
and
[
11
]
to investigate
\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{k\leq n}S_{k}(y)>x\right)
; our results do not seem to follow from the estimates in
[
6
,
11
]
. Related bounds for
M(\infty)
have been derived in
[
16
]
, which are shown to be sharp (for large
x
and/or
\mu
small) for distributions with power-law tails.

A key step in the approach in
[
18
]
and the other cited works includes using the Chernoff bound for
\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}(y)>x\right)
and deriving
convenient upper bounds for
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{s\min\{X,y\}}\right]
.
Our proof ideas are similar, but we rely on (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) instead, and derive lower bounds for
\gamma(y)
.
We have tried to strike a balance between the accuracy of such lower bounds, and user-friendliness (in terms of explicitness)
of the final expressions (
1.4
), (
1.5
). This is partly made possible by restricting the range of
y
over which these bounds apply.
It is possible to extend this range, at the expense of making the bound less sharp for larger values of
y
, using the explicit form of
y_{\beta}
and
y_{\eta}^{*}

given in (
2.23
) and (
3.18
) below. For more details, we refer to Sections
2
and
3
.
For a general framework that can be applied to develop sharp estimates for
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{s\min\{X,y\}}\right]
, we refer to
[
13
]
.

To illustrate the applicability and accuracy of our bounds, we consider a variation of the Sparre-Andersen risk model where claims to exceed a value

y
are re-insured.
We analyze the infinite-time ruin probability
P_{a}(x)
if the initial capital is
x
, and
y
is chosen as
y=ax
, where
a
is a fixed constant and
x\rightarrow\infty
, using Theorem
1.1
.
Finite-time ruin versions of this re-insurance problem have been analyzed in
[
5
,
19
]
using
sample-path large deviation principles for heavy tails.
Extending such a result to the infinite horizon case requires an additional interchange of limit argument. In Section
4
below, we illustrate how our bounds can help in this regard.

The result in Section
4
shows that if
y=ax
, our bounds almost predict the correct asymptotic behavior,
except for certain round-offs. Informally, if claims sizes have a tail of the form
x^{-\alpha}
, ruin occurs due to the consequence of
\lceil 1/a\rceil
large claims, leading to a ruin probability which behaves like
x^{-(\alpha-1)\lceil 1/a\rceil}
, while Theorem
1.1
would suggest
a behavior of
x^{-(\alpha-1)/a}
.

The remainder of this article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
.
The proof of Theorem
1.2
is given in Section
3
. The application to a risk model is given
in Section
4
.
The appendix presents the results from
[
13
]
that are needed in the main body of the paper.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We use the following notation:
x^{+}=\max\{x,0\}
,
F(x)=\mathbb{P}\left(X_{1}>x\right)
,
\bar{F}(x)=1-F(x)
. Indicator functions are written as
I(\cdot)
.
Denote

\mu_{\beta}^{+}=\mathbb{E}\left[(X_{1}^{+})^{\beta}\right]
,

\mu_{\beta}^{-}=\mathbb{E}\left[((-X_{1})^{+})^{\beta}\right]
.
We drop
\beta
from these expressions if
\beta=1
, for example,
\mu^{-}=\mathbb{E}\left[(-X_{1})^{+}\right]
.
We write, for
\beta>1
,
\mu_{\beta}=\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{1}|^{\beta}\right]
. Observe that
\mu_{\beta}=\mu_{\beta}^{+}+\mu_{\beta}^{-}
.
When we apply this identity, we always take
\beta>1
and warn that, in contrast,
\mu=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}
.

In view of the inequality (
1.2
), Equation (
1.4
) holds if we can show that there exists a
y_{\beta}
such that
\gamma(y)\geq s(y)=((\beta-1)\log y)/y
for
y>y_{\beta}
.
For this, using the equation (
1.3
) for
\gamma(y)
, it is sufficient to show that
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{s(y)\min\{X_{1},y\}}\right]\leq 1
.
We therefore derive an appropriate bound for
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{s\min\{X_{1},y\}}\right]
.
Let
\delta=\min\{2,\beta\}
. We have, for
s>0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{s\min\{X_{1},y\}}\right]=\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{sx}dF(x)+\int_{0}^{y}e^{sx}dG(x),

(2.1)

with
G(x)=\mathbb{P}\left(\min\{X_{1},y\}\leq x\right)
.
We estimate

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{sx}dF(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{0}(e^{sx}-1-sx)dF(x)+\int_{-\infty}^{0}(1+sx)dF(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{e^{sx}-1-sx}{(-sx)^{\delta}}s^{\delta}(-x)^{\delta}dF(x)+F(0)-s\mu^{-}

\displaystyle\leq s^{\delta}MG_{\delta}\mu_{\delta}^{-}+F(0)-s\mu^{-},

(2.2)

where (see the Appendix)

MG_{\delta}=\max_{u\geq 0}\frac{e^{-u}-1+u}{u^{\delta}}\leq\left(\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)^{\delta-1}(2-\delta)^{2-\delta}\leq\frac{1}{\delta}.

(2.3)

Next, we bound the integral
\int_{0}^{y}e^{sx}dG(x)
using arguments similar to those in the proof of Lemma 1.4 of
[
18
]
. Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}e^{sx}dG(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{y}(e^{sx}-1-sx)dG(x)+\int_{0}^{y}(1+sx)dG(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{y}(e^{sx}-1-sx)dG(x)+\bar{F}(0)+s\mu^{+}.

(2.4)

To bound the remaining integral at the right-hand side of (
2.4
), we distinguish between the cases that
0\leq s\leq\beta/y
and
s>\beta/y
. In the former case, we have

\int_{0}^{y}(e^{sx}-1-sx)dG(x)\leq\int_{0}^{\beta/s}(e^{sx}-1-sx)dG(x).

(2.5)

Recalling
\delta=\min\{2,\beta\}
, we have for
0\leq x\leq\beta/s
,

0\leq\frac{e^{sx}-1-sx}{(sx)^{\delta}}=e^{sx}\frac{1-(1+sx)e^{-sx}}{(sx)^{\delta}}\leq e^{sx}ME_{\delta}\leq e^{\beta}ME_{\delta},

(2.6)

where, see the Appendix,

ME_{\delta}=\max_{v\geq 0}\frac{1-(1+v)e^{-v}}{v^{\delta}}\leq\frac{(2-\delta)^{2-\delta}}{4-\delta}\leq\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2}\delta}.

(2.7)

Hence, for the case
0\leq s\leq\beta/y
, we have

\int_{0}^{y}(e^{sx}-1-sx)dG(x)\leq e^{\beta}ME_{\delta}\int_{0}^{\beta/s}(sx)^{\delta}dG(x)\leq e^{\beta}s^{\delta}ME_{\delta}\mu_{\delta}^{+}.

(2.8)

If
s>\beta/y
, we have from (
2.8
),

\int_{0}^{y}(e^{sx}-1-sx)dG(x)\leq e^{\beta}s^{\delta}ME_{\delta}\mu_{\delta}^{+}+\int_{\beta/s}^{y}(e^{sx}-1-sx)dG(x).

(2.9)

Now, observe that

\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{e^{sx}-1-sx}{x^{\beta}}\right]=x^{-\beta-1}(e^{sx}(sx-\beta)+(\beta-1)sx+\beta)>0

(2.10)

for
x\geq\beta/s
, and so

\int_{\beta/s}^{y}(e^{sx}-1-sx)dG(x)=\int_{\beta/s}^{y}\frac{e^{sx}-1-sx}{x^{\beta}}x^{\beta}dG(x)\leq\frac{e^{sy}-1-sy}{y^{\beta}}\mu_{\beta}^{+}.

(2.11)

We conclude that, when
s\geq\beta/y
,

\int_{0}^{y}(e^{sx}-1-sx)dG(x)\leq e^{\beta}s^{\delta}ME_{\delta}\mu_{\delta}^{+}+\frac{e^{sy}-1-sy}{y^{\beta}}\mu_{\beta}^{+}.

(2.12)

Evidently (see (
2.8
)), the bound (
2.12
) also holds when
0\leq s\leq\beta/y
.
Returning to (
2.1
) and using (
2.2
), (
2.4
), (
2.8
), (
2.12
), we get

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{s\min\{X_{1},y\}}\right]\leq s^{\delta}MG_{\delta}\mu_{\delta}^{-}+F(0)-s\mu^{-}+\bar{F}(0)+s\mu^{+}+e^{\beta}s^{\delta}ME_{\delta}\mu_{\delta}^{+}+\frac{e^{sy}-1-sy}{y^{\beta}}\mu_{\beta}^{+}.

(2.13)

Using that
F(0)+\bar{F}(0)=1
and
\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}=\mu
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{s\min\{X_{1},y\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 1+s\mu+s^{\delta}MG_{\delta}\mu_{\delta}^{-}+e^{\beta}s^{\delta}ME_{\delta}\mu_{\delta}^{+}+\frac{e^{sy}-1-sy}{y^{\beta}}\mu_{\beta}^{+}

\displaystyle=1+s\left(\mu+s^{\delta-1}MG_{\delta}\mu_{\delta}^{-}+e^{\beta}s^{\delta-1}ME_{\delta}\mu_{\delta}^{+}+\frac{e^{sy}-1-sy}{sy^{\beta}}\mu_{\beta}^{+}\right).

(2.14)

We consider this with
s=s(y)=\frac{1}{y}(\beta-1)\log y
, so that
e^{sy}=y^{\beta-1}
.
For
y>1
we therefore obtain,

0\leq\frac{e^{sy}-1-sy}{sy^{\beta}}=\frac{y^{\beta-1}-1-sy}{y^{\beta-1}(\beta-1)\log y}\leq\frac{1}{(\beta-1)\log y}.

(2.15)

Concluding, we get for
y>1
and
s=\frac{1}{y}(\beta-1)\log y
,

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{s\min\{X_{1},y\}}\right]\leq 1+s\left(\mu+s^{\delta-1}(MG_{\delta}\mu_{\delta}^{-}+e^{\beta}ME_{\delta}\mu_{\delta}^{+})+\frac{\mu_{\beta}^{+}}{(\beta-1)\log y}\right).

(2.16)

The quantity on the right-hand side between brackets is negative for large enough
y
, since
\mu<0
. To quantify this statement, write

K=\mu_{\delta}^{-}MG_{\delta}+e^{\beta}ME_{\delta}\mu_{\delta}^{+},\hskip 14.22636ptL=\mu_{\beta}^{+},

(2.17)

so that the relevant quantity in (
2.16
) takes the form

Q=\mu+\left(Ks^{\delta-1}+\frac{L}{(\beta-1)\log y}\right)=\mu+\frac{L+(\beta-1)Ks^{\delta-1}\log y}{(\beta-1)\log y}.

(2.18)

With
y>1
and
s=\frac{1}{y}(\beta-1)\log y
, observe that

(\beta-1)s^{\delta-1}\log y=(\beta-1)^{\delta}\frac{(\log y)^{\delta}}{y^{\delta-1}}\leq C(\beta-1)^{\delta},

(2.19)

where

C=\max_{y>1}\frac{(\log y)^{\delta}}{y^{\delta-1}}=\left(\max_{y>1}\frac{\log y}{y^{\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}}}\right)^{\delta}=\left(\frac{\delta}{(\delta-1)e}\right)^{\delta},

(2.20)

with maximizing
y
given by
\exp\{\delta/(\delta-1)\}
. Thus,

(\beta-1)s^{\delta-1}\log y\leq\left(\frac{\beta-1}{\delta-1}\frac{\delta}{e}\right)^{\delta}.

(2.21)

Consequently, the quantity
Q
in (
2.18
) is negative when

Q\leq\mu+\frac{L+K\left(\frac{\beta-1}{\delta-1}\frac{\delta}{e}\right)^{\delta}}{(\beta-1)\log y}.

(2.22)

Hence, the quantity
Q
in (
2.18
) is negative when

y>y_{\beta}=\exp\left\{\frac{L+K\left(\frac{\beta-1}{\delta-1}\frac{\delta}{e}\right)^{\delta}}{(\beta-1)|\mu|}\right\}.

(2.23)

Here,
K
and
L
are given in (
2.17
) where
MG_{\delta}
and
ME_{\delta}
can be bounded using (
2.3
) and (
2.7
).

Thus, for
y>y_{\beta}
, it follows that
\gamma(y)\geq\frac{1}{y}(\beta-1)\log y
.
Inserting this bound into (
1.2
) completes the proof
of (
1.4
).

Remarks.

1.

Upon inspection of the proof, at the expense of increasing the threshold
y_{\beta}
, we could have chosen

s=s(y)=((\beta-1)\log y+\log\log y+\xi)/y
with
e^{\xi}<(\beta-1)|\mu|/\mu_{\beta}^{+}
, which would lead to a slightly sharper bound
of the form

\mathbb{P}\left(M(y)>x\right)\leq y^{-(\beta-1)\frac{x}{y}}(\log y)^{-\frac{x}{y}}e^{-\gamma\frac{x}{y}},\hskip 28.45274ptx>0,y>y_{\beta}.

(2.24)

2.

If one wishes an upper bound valid for all
y
, one may simply use

\mathbb{P}\left(M(y)>x\right)\leq(y/y_{\beta})^{-(\beta-1)x/y}.

(2.25)

3
Proof of Theorem
1.2

As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we bound
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{s\min\{X_{1},y\}}\right]
,
now using the assumptions made in the formulation of Theorem
1.2
.
For a fixed
\eta\in(0,1)
, we then make a particular choice for
s=s(y)
, and we construct a
y_{\eta}^{*}
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{s\min\{X_{1},y\}}\right]\leq 1
for
y\geq y_{\eta}^{*}
and
s=s(y)
. By Cramér-Lundberg theory, this implies
\gamma(y)\geq s(y)
, and inserting this inequality into (
1.2
), we obtain (
1.5
).

Let
s>0
and
y>1/s
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{s\min\{X_{1},y\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{sx}dF(x)+e^{sy}\bar{F}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{sx}dF(x)+\bar{F}(0)+\int_{0}^{1/s}se^{sx}\bar{F}(x)dx+\int_{1/s}^{y}se^{sx}\bar{F}(x)dx.

(3.1)

We bound the three integrals in (
3.1
). For the first integral, we can use (
2.2
), where we observe that
\delta=\min\{2,\beta\}=2
since
\mu_{2}<\infty
,
and that
MG_{2}=1/2
. Thus, we have

\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{sx}dF(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\mu_{2}^{-}+F(0)-s\mu^{-}.

(3.2)

To bound the second integral in (
3.1
), we use the inequality
e^{z}\leq 1+ze^{z}
with
z=sx
and the inequalities

\int_{0}^{u}\bar{F}(x)dx\leq\mu^{+},\hskip 14.22636pt\int_{0}^{u}2x\bar{F}(x)dx\leq\mu_{2}^{+},\hskip 14.22636ptu\geq 0,

(3.3)

to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/s}se^{sx}\bar{F}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1/s}s(1+sxe^{sx})\bar{F}(x)dx\leq s\int_{0}^{1/s}\bar{F}(x)dx+\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\int_{0}^{1/s}2xe\bar{F}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq s\mu^{+}+s^{2}\frac{e}{2}\mu_{2}^{+}.

(3.4)

To bound the third integral in (
3.1
),
note that
sx-q(x)
is convex, so that

sx-q(x)\leq\max\{1-q(1/s),sy-q(y)\},\hskip 28.45274ptx\in[1/s,y].

(3.5)

Consequently,

\int_{1/s}^{y}se^{sx}\bar{F}(x)dx=\int_{1/s}^{y}se^{sx-q(x)}dx\leq sy\max\{e^{1-q(1/s)},e^{sy-q(y)}\}.

(3.6)

From (
3.2
), (
3.4
) and (
3.6
), we then see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{s\min\{X_{1},y\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\mu_{2}^{-}+F(0)-s\mu^{-})+\bar{F}(0)+(s\mu^{+}+s^{2}\frac{e}{2}\mu_{2}^{+})+sy\max\{e^{1-q(1/s)},e^{sy-q(y)}\}

\displaystyle=1+s\left(\mu+s\frac{e}{2}\mu_{2}^{+}+\frac{1}{2}s\mu_{2}^{-}+y\max\{e^{1-q(1/s)},e^{sy-q(y)}\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1+s\left(\mu+s\frac{e}{2}\mu_{2}+y\max\{e^{1-q(1/s)},e^{sy-q(y)}\}\right).

(3.7)

In the last display, we used that
\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}=\mu,F(0)+\bar{F}(0)=1
, and
\mu_{2}^{+}+\mu_{2}^{-}=\mu_{2}
.
We now want to choose
s(y)
such that the expression

Q=\mu+s\frac{e}{2}\mu_{2}+y\max\{e^{1-q(1/s)},e^{sy-q(y)}\}\leq 0,

(3.8)

when
y
is sufficiently large.
We set

s=s(y):=\frac{1}{y}(q(y)-\log y+r),\hskip 14.22636ptr=\log(|\mu|\eta),

(3.9)

with
\eta\in(0,1)
fixed. Then, we have

Q=\mu+s\frac{e}{2}\mu_{2}+\max\{ye^{1-q(1/s)},e^{r}\}.

(3.10)

We now invoke the assumption made in Theorem
1.2
that there exists a
\kappa\in(0,1)
and
y_{\kappa}>0
such that

(\log y)^{1+\kappa}\leq q(y)\leq y^{1-\kappa},\hskip 14.22636pty\geq y_{\kappa}.

(3.11)

We have

y\exp\{-q(1/s(y))\}\leq y\exp\{-q(y^{\kappa})\},

(3.12)

when
y\geq\max\{e^{r},y_{\kappa}\}
. Indeed, by monotonicity of
q
, the inequality in (
3.12
) is equivalent to

\frac{1}{s(y)}=\frac{y}{q(y)-\log y+r}\geq y^{\kappa},

(3.13)

and this follows from the second inequality in (
3.11
) and
y\geq e^{r}
. Furthermore, from the first inequality in (
3.11
), we have

q(y^{\kappa})\geq(\log(y^{\kappa}))^{1+\kappa}=(\kappa\log y)^{1+\kappa},\hskip 14.22636pty\geq y_{\kappa}^{1/\kappa}.

(3.14)

Now, define

y_{r}=\max\left\{\sup\{y:ye^{-(\kappa\ln y)^{\kappa+1}}\geq e^{r-1}\},e^{r},y_{\kappa}^{1/\kappa}\right\}.

(3.15)

Then, by (
3.12
), (
3.14
), and the definition of
y_{r}
in (3.33), for
y\geq y_{r}
,

\max\{ye^{1-q(1/s)},e^{r}\}\leq\max\{ye^{1-(\kappa\ln y)^{\kappa+1}},e^{r}\}\leq e^{r}.

(3.16)

Therefore,
Q
in (
3.8
) and (
3.10
) is bounded by

Q\leq\mu+\frac{1}{2}es\mu_{2}+e^{r}\leq\mu+\frac{1}{2}e\mu_{2}y^{-\kappa}+e^{r},\hskip 14.22636pty\geq y_{r},

(3.17)

where the latter inequality follows from the definition of
s=s(y)
in (
3.9
) and the second inequality in (
3.11
).
Since
e^{r}=\eta|\mu|
, we conclude that
Q\leq 0
when
y\geq y_{\eta}^{*}
, where

y_{\eta}^{*}=\max\left\{y_{r},\left(\frac{e\mu_{2}/2}{|\mu|(1-\eta)}\right)^{1/\kappa}\right\}.

(3.18)

We conclude that
s(y)\leq\gamma(y)
for
y>y_{\eta}^{*}
. The proof of Theorem
1.2
now follows by combining the inequalities
s(y)\leq\gamma(y)
and (
1.2
), and using the definition of
s(y)
in (

4
Application to a re-insurance risk model

Consider an insurance problem where premiums come in at rate
c
, claim sizes
B_{i},i\geq 1
are i.i.d.  and the time between claims is governed by a renewal process, with
A_{i},i\geq 1
, inter-renewal times which are independent of the claim sizes. Claim sizes exceeding a value of
y=ax
are covered by a re-insurer, with
x
the initial capital and
a
a fixed constant. The ruin probability
P_{a}(x)

equals

P_{a}(x)=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\geq 0}\sum_{i=1}^{n}[B_{i}I(B_{i}<ax)-cA_{i}]>x\right),

(4.1)

recalling that
I(\cdot)
is the indicator function.
We assume
c\mathbb{E}\left[A_{i}\right]>\mathbb{E}\left[B_{i}\right]
so that
P_{a}(x)\rightarrow 0
. We wish to understand the impact of
a
on the convergence rate of
P_{a}(x)
and aim to
illustrate the applicability of Theorem
1.1
. To simplify our presentation, we assume
A_{i}\equiv 1/c
.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
\mathbb{P}\left(B_{1}>x\right)=L(x)x^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha>1
, and
L(cx)/L(x)\rightarrow 1
for
c>0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
, and
1/a
is not an integer, then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

P_{a}(x)=(L(x)x^{-(\alpha-1)})^{\lceil 1/a\rceil}C(1+o(1)).

(4.2)

The intuition behind this result is that ruin is caused by
\lceil 1/a\rceil
large claims, similar to what has been established in finite-horizon problems
[
1
,
9
,
10
]
.
To prove Proposition
4.1
,
we reduce the problem to analyzing the ruin probability over a large but finite time-horizon of length
Tx
with
T\in(0,\infty)
. To this end, we use the bounds

P_{a}(x)\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\leq Tx}\sum_{i=1}^{n}[B_{i}-1]\geq x;\sup_{n\leq Tx}B_{i}<ax\right)=:P_{a,T}(x),

(4.3)

and

P_{a}(x)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\leq Tx}\sum_{i=1}^{n}[B_{i}I(B_{i}<ax)-1]\geq x\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\geq Tx}\sum_{i=1}^{n}[B_{i}I(B_{i}<ax)-1]\geq x\right).

(4.4)

The first term in the right-hand side of (
4.4
) can be upper bounded by

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\leq Tx}\sum_{i=1}^{n}[B_{i}I(B_{i}<ax)-1]\geq x\mid\sup_{i\leq Tx}B_{i}<ax\right)=P_{a,T}(x)/\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{i\leq Tx}B_{i}<ax\right).

(4.5)

To bound the second term, define
X_{i}=B_{i}-1
, note that
B_{i}I(B_{i}<ax)-1\leq\min\{X_{i},ax\}
, to obtain.

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\geq Tx}\sum_{i=1}^{n}[B_{i}I(B_{i}<ax)-1]\geq x\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\geq Tx}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\min\{X_{i},ax\}\geq x\right).

(4.6)

This expression can be bounded further by observing that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\geq Tx}\sum_{i=1}^{n}[B_{i}I(B_{i}<ax)-1]\geq x\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\geq Tx}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\min\{X_{i},ax\}\geq x\right).

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\geq Tx}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\min\{X_{i},ax\}\geq x,\sum_{i=1}^{Tx}\min\{X_{i},ax\}\leq Tx/2\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{Tx}\min\{X_{i},ax\}\geq Tx/2\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\geq Tx}\sum_{i=Tx+1}^{n}\min\{X_{i},ax\}\geq xT/2\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\geq 0}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\min\{X_{i},ax\}\geq Tx/2\right)

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{P}\left(M(ax)>Tx/2\right),

(4.7)

with
M(ax)
being the protagonist of this paper, defined in (
1.1
).
Substituting (
4.5
), (
4.7
) into (
4.4
), we obtain

P_{a}(x)\leq P_{a,T}(x)/\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{i\leq Tx}B_{i}<ax\right)+2\mathbb{P}\left(M(ax)>Tx/2\right).

(4.8)

Since
\mathbb{E}\left[B_{i}\right]<\infty
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{i\leq Tx}B_{i}<ax\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(B_{1}<ax\right)^{\lfloor Tx\rfloor}\rightarrow 1.

(4.9)

Consequently, it suffices to establish the asymptotic behavior of

P_{a,T}(x)
and to show that

\mathbb{P}\left(M(ax)>Tx/2\right)=o(P_{a,T}(x))

(4.10)

for a suitably chosen
T
.
In Section 5.1 of
[
19
]
, it is shown that, given
a
is such that
1/a
is not an integer, there exists a constant
C
such that

P_{a,T}(x)\sim C(L(x)x^{-(\alpha-1)})^{\lceil 1/a\rceil}(1+o(1)).

(4.11)

The constant
C
can be expressed as

C=\int_{u\in[0,T]^{k},x\in(0,\infty)^{k}}\alpha^{k}\prod_{i}x_{i}^{-1-\alpha}I(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}I(u_{i}\leq t)-t\geq 1;\sup_{i\leq k}x_{i}\leq a)du_{1}...du_{k}dx_{1}...dx_{,}

with
k=\lceil 1/a\rceil
.
From this representation, it follows that
C\in(0,\infty)
if
1/a
is not an integer. In addition,
C
is constant in
T
for
T>1+a
.
Next, using (
1.4
), we see that, for every
\beta\in(1,\alpha)
, and
x
large enough,

\mathbb{P}\left(M(ax)>Tx/2\right)\leq(ax)^{-(\beta-1)T/(2a)},

(4.12)

which, using (
4.11
), is
o(P_{a,T}(x))
for
T>\frac{\alpha-1}{\beta-1}a\lceil 1/a\rceil
, concluding the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Remarks.

1.

To derive tail asymptotics for the ruin probability if
\mathbb{P}\left(X_{1}>x\right)
is of Weibull-type, we expect that it is
possible to apply the sample path large-deviations results in
[
5
]
to derive tail asymptotics for
P_{a,T}(x)
, and combine them with the
bound (
4.8
) and Theorem
1.2
; a fully worked-out argument would require a careful investigation of a quasi-variational problem, extending the analysis in Section 4 of
[
5
]
, which is beyond the scope of this study.

2.

In this section, we took
A_{i}
constant. The case of non-deterministic
A_{i}
may be dealt with by using bounds of the form

\sup_{n\geq 0}\sum_{i=1}^{n}[B_{i}I(B_{i}<ax)-cA_{i}]\leq\sup_{n\geq 0}\sum_{i=1}^{n}[B_{i}I(B_{i}<ax)-c^{\prime}]+\sup_{n\geq 0}\sum_{i=1}^{n}[c^{\prime}-cA_{i}],

with
c^{\prime}\in(\mathbb{E}\left[B_{i}\right],c\mathbb{E}\left[A_{i}\right])
. The second term on the right-hand side of this inequality is independent of the first term. It has a moment generation function that is finite in a neighborhood of the origin
and therefore does not impact the asymptotic behavior of
P_{a}(x)
.

Appendix A
Bounding Taylor approximation errors

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, the following quantities appear:

ME_{\delta}=\max_{s\geq 0}\frac{1-(1+s)e^{-s}}{s^{\delta}},\hskip 28.45274pt\delta\in[0,2],

(A.1)

MG_{\delta}=\max_{u\geq 0}\frac{e^{-u}-1+u}{u^{\delta}},\hskip 28.45274pt\delta\in[1,2].

(A.2)

We analyze these quantities using the machinery developed in
[
13
]
, which contains a framework to bound Taylor polynomial approximation errors for the exponential function in the presence of a power weight function. For the case that the degree
n
of the Taylor polynomial equals 1, this leads to consideration of quantities
ME_{1,\delta}
and
MG_{1,\delta}
that agree with (
A.1
) and (
A.2
), from which the degree
n=1
has been omitted for brevity. In this appendix, we summarize the results of
[
13
]
for the case
n=1
that are relevant to this paper. We have

ME_{0}=1,ME_{2}=\frac{1}{2},MG_{1}=1,MG_{2}=\frac{1}{2}.

(A.3)

Furthermore,
ME_{\delta}
is a continuous, log-convex function of
\delta\in[0,2]
, and
MG_{\delta}
is a continuous, log-convex function of
\delta\in[1,2]
. For
\delta\in(0,2)
, the maximum
ME_{\delta}
of

E_{\delta}(s)=\frac{1-(1+s)e^{-s}}{s^{\delta}}

(A.4)

over
s\geq 0
is assumed by the unique positive solution
s=s(\delta)
of the equation

e^{s}=1+s+\frac{1}{\delta}s^{2}.

(A.5)

For
\delta\in(0,1)
, the maximum
MG_{\delta}
of

G_{\delta}(u)=\frac{e^{-u}-1+u}{u^{\delta}}

(A.6)

over
u\geq 0
is assumed by the unique positive solution
u=u(\delta)
of the equation

e^{-u}=1-\frac{\delta u}{u+\delta}.

(A.7)

An upper bound for
ME_{\delta},\delta\in[0,2]
follows from Proposition 2 in
[
13
]
in the form

F_{2}(2-\delta);\hskip 28.45274ptF_{2}(s)=\frac{1}{\delta}\frac{s^{2-\delta}}{1+s+\frac{1}{\delta}s^{2}},\hskip 28.45274pts\geq 0.

(A.8)

Thus, we have

ME_{\delta}\leq\frac{(2-\delta)^{2-\delta}}{4-\delta}=:UE_{\delta},\hskip 28.45274pt\delta\in[0,2].

(A.9)

An upper bound for
MG_{2},\delta\in[1,2]
is found using the approach given at the end of Subsection 2.2 of
[
13
]
in the form

H\left(\frac{(2-\delta)\delta}{\delta-1}\right);\hskip 28.45274ptH(u)=\frac{u^{2-\delta}}{u+\delta},\hskip 28.45274ptu\geq 0.

(A.10)

Thus, we have

MG_{\delta}\leq\left(\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)^{\delta-1}(2-\delta)^{2-\delta}=:UG_{\delta},\hskip 28.45274pt\delta\in[1,2].

(A.11)

The bounds in (
A.9
) and (
A.11
) are relatively sharp, see Figure 1 and Figure 2 below
to be added

ME_{\delta}\leq\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2}\delta},\hskip 14.22636pt\delta\in[0,2];\hskip 28.45274ptMG_{\delta}\leq\frac{1}{\delta},\hskip 14.22636pt\delta\in[1,2].

(A.12)

The bound for
MG_{\delta}
also appears in the proof of Lemma 1.4 of

]

A.12

A.9
) and (

The first bound in (

) follows via (

(A.13)

To establish (

f_{E}(\delta)
is continuous in
\delta\in[0,2]

f_{E}(0)=0=f_{E}(2)

Hence,

(A.17)

f_{G}(\delta)

\delta\in(1,2)

A.15

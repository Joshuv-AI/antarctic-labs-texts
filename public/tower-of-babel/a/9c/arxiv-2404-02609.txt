Some properties of a modified Hilbert transform
Matteo Ferrari

Abstract
Recently, Steinbach et al. introduced a novel operator ℋ T : L 2 ​ ( 0 , T ) → L 2 ​ ( 0 , T ) \mathcal{H}_{T}:L^{2}(0,T)\to L^{2}(0,T) , known as the modified Hilbert transform. This operator has shown its significance in space-time formulations related to the heat and wave equations. In this paper, we establish a direct connection between the modified Hilbert transform ℋ T \mathcal{H}_{T} and the canonical Hilbert transform ℋ \mathcal{H} . Specifically, we prove the relationship ℋ T ​ φ = − ℋ ​ φ ~ \mathcal{H}_{T}\varphi=-\mathcal{H}\widetilde{\varphi} , where φ ∈ L 2 ​ ( 0 , T ) \varphi\in L^{2}(0,T) and φ ~ \widetilde{\varphi} is a suitable extension of φ \varphi over the entire ℝ \mathbb{R} . By leveraging this crucial result, we derive some properties of ℋ T \mathcal{H}_{T} , including a new inversion formula, that emerge as immediate consequences of well-established findings on ℋ \mathcal{H} .

2
Proofs of the main result

In this section, we present three distinct proofs of Theorem
2
. The first proof relies on the integral representation
2
, while the second proof utilizes an alternative definition of the Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}
specifically designed for periodic functions. Finally, the third proof employs Fourier series.

2.1
Proof based on the integral representation

The first proof is established by leveraging the Laurent series expansion of the cosecant function, as presented in
[
4
, Formula (9.3.30)]

\csc(z)=\frac{1}{z}+2z\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{z^{2}-k^{2}\pi^{2}}.

(6)

This series converges absolutely and uniformly for
|z|<\pi
, in fact for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

\left|\csc(z)-\frac{1}{z}\right|\leq\frac{2|z|}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\left|\left(\frac{z}{k\pi}\right)^{2}-1\right|}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\leq\frac{|z|}{3}.

Starting from the definitions of Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}
and
\widetilde{\varphi}
in (
5
), we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\left[\ldots+\pvv\int_{-3T}^{-T}\frac{\widetilde{\varphi}(s)}{t-s}\differential s+\pvv\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\widetilde{\varphi}(s)}{t-s}\differential s+\pvv\int_{T}^{3T}\frac{\widetilde{\varphi}(s)}{t-s}\differential s+\ldots\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\left[\ldots-\pvv\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\widetilde{\varphi}(s)}{t-s-2T}\differential s+\pvv\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\widetilde{\varphi}(s)}{t-s}\differential s-\pvv\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\widetilde{\varphi}(s)}{t-s+2T}\differential s+\ldots\right]

where we have used the alternating signs periodicity of
\widetilde{\varphi}
. Therefore, we can compactly write

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\pvv\int_{-T}^{T}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)\frac{(-1)^{k}}{t-s+2kT}\differential s.

By interchanging the integral and the summation (which is possible due to the uniform convergence of the series), and utilizing (
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\pvv\int_{-T}^{T}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)\left[\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{t-s+2kT}\right]\differential s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\pvv\int_{-T}^{T}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)\left[\frac{1}{t-s}+2(t-s)\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(t-s)^{2}-4k^{2}T^{2}}\right]\differential s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2T}\pvv\int_{-T}^{T}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)\csc\left(\frac{\pi(t-s)}{2T}\right)\differential s.

From the latter, we obtain the modified Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}_{T}
in integral form (
2
) by recalling the definition (
5
), and writing

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2T}\pvv\int_{-T}^{0}\varphi(-s)\csc\left(\frac{\pi(t-s)}{2T}\right)\differential s+\frac{1}{2T}\pvv\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(s)\csc\left(\frac{\pi(t-s)}{2T}\right)\differential s

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2T}\pvv\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(s)\csc\left(\frac{\pi(t+s)}{2T}\right)\differential s-\frac{1}{2T}\pvv\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(s)\csc\left(\frac{\pi(t-s)}{2T}\right)\differential s

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{H}_{T}\varphi(t).

2.2
Proof based on the Hilbert transform for periodic functions

Given
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T)
, we observe that
\widetilde{\varphi}
as in (
5
) is actually periodic with a period of
4T
, and it is known that the Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}
for a periodic function can be calculated using (see
[
6
]
):

\mathcal{H}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)=\frac{1}{4T}\pvv\int_{-2T}^{2T}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)\cot\left(\frac{\pi(t-s)}{4T}\right)\differential s.

(7)

Let us expand the four contributions of the integral above to recover
\mathcal{H}_{T}\varphi

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4T}\left[\pvv\int_{-2T}^{-T}\varphi(s+2T)\cot\left(\frac{\pi(t-s)}{4T}\right)\differential s-\pvv\int_{-T}^{0}\varphi(-s)\cot\left(\frac{\pi(t-s)}{4T}\right)\differential s\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 12.80365pt\left.+\pvv\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(s)\cot\left(\frac{\pi(t-s)}{4T}\right)\differential s+\pvv\int_{T}^{2T}\varphi(2T-s)\cot\left(\frac{\pi(t-s)}{4T}\right)\differential s\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4T}\pvv\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(s)\cot\left(\frac{\pi(t-s+2T)}{4T}\right)\differential s-\frac{1}{4T}\pvv\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(s)\cot\left(\frac{\pi(t+s)}{4T}\right)\differential s

\displaystyle\hskip 12.80365pt+\frac{1}{4T}\pvv\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(s)\cot\left(\frac{\pi(t-s)}{4T}\right)\differential s+\frac{1}{4T}\pvv\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(s)\cot\left(\frac{\pi(t+s-2T)}{4T}\right)\differential s.

Finally, using the trigonometric formula

\displaystyle 2\csc(2x)=-\cot\left(x\pm\frac{\pi}{2}\right)+\cot(x),\quad|x|<\frac{\pi}{2},

(8)

we again conclude that
\mathcal{H}_{T}\varphi=-\mathcal{H}\widetilde{\varphi}
in
L^{2}(0,T)
.

2.3
Proof based on Fourier series

For
f
with period
2T
in
L^{2}(-T,T)
, the circular Hilbert transform is defined as (see
[
5
, Section 6.4]
)

\mathcal{H}f(t)=\mathrm{i}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[f_{-k}e^{-\mathrm{i}k\pi\frac{t}{T}}-f_{k}e^{\mathrm{i}k\pi\frac{t}{T}}\right]\quad\text{with}\quad f_{k}=\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}f(s)e^{-\mathrm{i}k\pi\frac{s}{T}}\differential s.

Let us verify that this definition is consistent with the original definition of the modified Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}_{T}
by Steinbach and Zank (
1
). That is, we will demonstrate that for a given function
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T)
, we have
\mathcal{H}\widetilde{\varphi}=-\mathcal{H}_{T}\varphi
in
L^{2}(0,T)
, with
\widetilde{\varphi}
as in (
5
). Recalling that
\widetilde{\varphi}
is a periodic function with period
4T
, let us begin by writing

\mathcal{H}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)=\mathrm{i}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[\widetilde{\varphi}_{-k}e^{-\mathrm{i}k\pi\frac{t}{2T}}-\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}e^{\mathrm{i}k\pi\frac{t}{2T}}\right],

and readily one can verify

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}=\frac{1}{4T}\int_{-2T}^{2T}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)e^{-\mathrm{i}k\pi\frac{s}{2T}}\differential s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4T}\int_{0}^{2T}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)\left[e^{-\mathrm{i}k\pi\frac{s}{2T}}-e^{\mathrm{i}k\pi\frac{s}{2T}}\right]\differential s

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2T}\int_{0}^{2T}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)\sin\left(k\pi\frac{s}{2T}\right)\differential s=-\widetilde{\varphi}_{-k}.

Hence, we continue

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[e^{-\mathrm{i}k\pi\frac{t}{2T}}+e^{\mathrm{i}k\pi\frac{t}{2T}}\right]\frac{1}{2T}\int_{0}^{2T}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)\sin\left(k\pi\frac{s}{2T}\right)\differential s

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2\cos\left(k\pi\frac{t}{2T}\right)\frac{1}{2T}\int_{0}^{2T}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)\sin\left(k\pi\frac{s}{2T}\right)\differential s.

Moreover, we can now write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2T}\int_{0}^{2T}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)\sin\left(k\pi\frac{s}{2T}\right)\differential s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2T}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(s)\sin\left(k\pi\frac{s}{2T}\right)\differential s+\frac{1}{2T}\int_{T}^{2T}\varphi(2T-s)\sin\left(k\pi\frac{s}{2T}\right)\differential s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2T}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(s)\left[\sin\left(k\pi\frac{s}{2T}\right)-\sin(k \pi)\sin\left(k\pi\frac{s}{2T}\right)\right]\differential s

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&k\text{~ even},\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(s)\sin\left(k\pi\frac{s}{2T}\right)\differential s}&k\text{~ odd}.\end{cases}

Finally, we conclude

\mathcal{H}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\cos\left((2k+1)\pi\frac{t}{2T}\right)\frac{2}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)\sin\left((2k+1)\pi\frac{s}{2T}\right)\differential s=-\mathcal{H}_{T}\varphi(t).

3
Consequences of the main result

In this section we show simple consequences of Theorem
2
.

3.1
Inversion formula

For
f\in L^{2}(-T,T)
and periodic with period
2T
, the inversion formula holds (see
[
5
, Formula (6.35)]
)

\mathcal{H}^{2}f(t)=-f(t)+\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}f(s)\differential s,\quad\quad\text{in~}L^{2}(-T,T).

(9)

For
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T)
, we can calculate

\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{H}_{T}\varphi)(t)=-\mathcal{H}^{2}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)=\widetilde{\varphi}(t)-\frac{1}{4T}\int_{-2T}^{2T}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)\differential s=\varphi(t),\quad\quad\text{in~}L^{2}(0,T),

since
\widetilde{\varphi}
is an odd function.

3.2
Alternative formula

For an odd function
f\in L^{2}(-T,T)
with period
2T
, it can be shown that

\mathcal{H}f(t)=\frac{1}{T}\pvv\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\frac{\sin\left(\pi\frac{s}{T}\right)}{\cos\left(\pi\frac{s}{T}\right)-\cos\left(\pi\frac{t}{T}\right)}\differential s,\quad\quad\text{in~}L^{2}(-T,T).

Therefore, we obtain the alternative formula in
L^{2}(0,T)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{T}\varphi(t)=-\mathcal{H}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2T}\pvv\int_{0}^{2T}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)\frac{\sin\left(\pi\frac{s}{2T}\right)}{\cos\left(\pi\frac{s}{2T}\right)-\cos\left(\pi\frac{t}{2T}\right)}\differential s

\displaystyle=-\frac{\cos\left(\pi\frac{t}{2T}\right)}{T}\pvv\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(s)\frac{\sin\left(\pi\frac{s}{2T}\right)}{\cos^{2}\left(\pi\frac{s}{2T}\right)-\cos^{2}\left(\pi\frac{t}{2T}\right)}\differential s.

This formula can also be deduced from equation (
2
) using trigonometric identities.

\widetilde{\varphi}

\partial_{t}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)_{|_{(-2T,2T)}}=\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}\varphi(s+2T)&s\in(-2T,-T),\\

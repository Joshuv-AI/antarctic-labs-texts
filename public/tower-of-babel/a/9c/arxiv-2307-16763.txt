Abstract
. We deal with a wide class of nonlinear nonlocal equations led by integro-differential operators of order ( s , p ) (s,p) , with summability exponent p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) and differentiability exponent s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) , whose prototype is the fractional subLaplacian in the Heisenberg group. We present very recent boundedness and regularity estimates (up to the boundary) for the involved weak solutions, and we introduce the nonlocal counterpart of the Perron Method in the Heisenberg group, by recalling some results on the fractional obstacle problem. Throughout the paper we also list various related open problems.

1.
Introduction

In this notes we deal with a wide class of nonlinear and nonlocal integro-differential operators, defined on suitable Sobolev spaces, whose explicit expression is given by

(1.1)

{\mathcal{L}}u(\xi)\equiv{\mathcal{L}}_{s,p}u(\xi):=P.\penalty\ \!V.\int_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}\frac{|u(\xi)-u(\eta)|^{p-2}(u(\xi)-u(\eta))}{d_{\textrm{o}}(\eta^{-1}\circ\xi)^{Q+sp}}\,{\rm d}\eta,\qquad\xi\in{\mathds{H}^{n}},

where
p\in(1,\infty)
,
s\in(0,1)
,
Q\equiv 2n+2
is the homogeneous dimension and
d_{\textrm{o}}
is a homogeneous norm on
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
; see Section
2
below for the precise definitions and further details.

We firstly focus on the linear case when
p=2
. Integro-differential operators as in the form in (
1.1
) do arise as a generalization of the classical
conformally invariant
fractional subLaplacian
(-\Delta_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}})^{(s)}
on
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
which was first introduced in
[
10
]
via the spectral formula

(-\Delta_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}})^{s}:=2^{s}\lvert T\rvert^{s}\frac{{\bf\Gamma}\big(-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}\lvert T\rvert^{-1}+\frac{1+s}{2}\big)}{{\bf\Gamma}\big(-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}\lvert T\rvert^{-1}+\frac{1-s}{2}\big)},\qquad s\in(0,1),

with
{\bf\Gamma}(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{x-1}e^{-t}\,{\rm d}t
being the Euler Gamma function,
\Delta_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}
being the classical Kohn-Spencer subLaplacian in
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
, and
T=\partial_{t}
being the vertical vector field.

As proven in
[
81
, Proposition 4.1]
the following representation formula holds true

(1.2)

(-\Delta_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}})^{s}u(\xi)\,:=\,C(n,s)\,P.\penalty\ \!V.\int_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}\frac{u(\xi)-u(\eta)}{|\eta^{-1}\circ\xi|_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}^{Q+2s}}\,{\rm d}\eta,\qquad\xi\in{\mathds{H}^{n}},

where
C(n,s)
is a positive constant which depends only on
n
and
s
.

Notice that the operator defined in Formula (
1.2
) above does not coincide, for any
s\in(0,1)
, with the classical power of the subLaplacian
-\Delta_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}^{s}
defined via heat kernel

(1.3)

-\Delta_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}^{s}u(\xi):=-\frac{s}{{\bf\Gamma}(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{1+s}}\big(P_{t}u(\xi)-u(\xi)\big)\,{\rm d}t,\qquad u\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathds{H}^{n}}),

with
P_{t}u(\xi):=e^{-\Delta_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}t}u(\xi)
being the heat semigroup built by Folland in
[
41
]
.

In the last decades, many relevant results have been achieved in the study of fractional operators in the Heisenberg group both in the form (
1.3
) and (
1.2
). As an example, we just recall the paper
[
81
]

1.2
). We also refer the reader to
[

Moreover, very important results have been obtained based on the construction of fractional operators via a Caffarelli-Silvestre-type extension as seen in the Euclidean framework in
[

. In particular, we mention the recent series of papers
[

2.1.
The Heisenberg group

We start by introducing some definitions and results about the Heisenberg group. For further details we refer to the book
[
9
]
by Bonfiglioli, Lanconelli and Uguzzoni.

The Heisenberg group
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
is the Lie group which has
{\mathds{R}}^{n}_{x}\times{\mathds{R}}^{n}_{y}\times{\mathds{R}}_{t}
as background manifold and whose group law
\circ
and dilations
\delta_{\lambda}
are given by

\xi\circ\xi^{\prime}:=\big(x+x^{\prime},\,y+y^{\prime},\,t+t^{\prime}+2\langle y,x^{\prime}\rangle-2\langle x,y^{\prime}\rangle\big),\qquad\forall\xi=(x,y,t),\xi^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in{\mathds{H}^{n}}

and

\delta_{\lambda}(\xi):=\big(\lambda x,\,\lambda y,\,\lambda^{2}t\big),

respectively.
It can be checked that the identity element is the origin
0
, the inverse
\xi^{-1}=-\xi
. The Jacobian of
\delta_{\lambda}
is equal to
\lambda^{2n+2}
, and the number
Q\equiv 2n+2
is usually called
homogeneous dimension
of
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
.

The Jacobian basis of the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
of
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
is given by the following vector fields

X_{j}:=\partial_{x_{j}}+2y_{j}\partial_{t},\quad X_{n+j}:=\partial_{y_{j}}-2x_{j}\partial_{t},\quad 1\leq j\leq n,\quad T:=\partial_{t}.

Moreover, a simple computation shows that

[X_{j},X_{n+j}]:=X_{j}X_{n+j}-X_{n+j}X_{j}=-4\partial_{t},\quad\text{for every}\penalty\ 1\leq j\leq n,

so that

\textup{rank}\Big(\textup{Lie}\{X_{1},\dots,X_{2n}\}\Big)\ =\ 2n+1.

The group
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
is thus a
Carnot group
with the following stratification
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
of the Lie algebra,

\mathfrak{h}^{n}=\textup{span}\{X_{1},\dots,X_{2n}\}\oplus\textup{span}\{T\}.

Given a domain
\Omega\subset{\mathds{H}^{n}}
, for any
u\in C^{1}(\Omega;\,{\mathds{R}})
we define the
horizontal gradient

\nabla_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}u
of
u
by

\nabla_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}u(\xi):=\Big(X_{1}u(\xi),\dots,X_{2n}u(\xi)\Big).

Definition 2.1
.

A
homogeneous norm
on
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
is a continuous function
(
with respect to the Euclidean topology
)

{d_{\rm o}}:{\mathds{H}^{n}}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
such that:

(i)

{d_{\rm o}}(\delta_{\lambda}(\xi))=\lambda{d_{\rm o}}(\xi)
, for every
\lambda>0
and every
\xi\in{\mathds{H}^{n}}
;

(ii)

{d_{\rm o}}(\xi)=0
if and only if
\xi=0
.

Moreover, we say that the homogeneous norm
{d_{\rm o}}
is
symmetric
if
{d_{\rm o}}(\xi^{-1})={d_{\rm o}}(\xi)
, for any
\xi\in{\mathds{H}^{n}}
.

Fixed an homogeneous norm
d_{\rm o}
on
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
, the function
\Psi
defined on the set of all pairs of elements of
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
by

\Psi(\xi,\eta):={d_{\rm o}}(\eta^{-1}\circ\xi),

is a pseudometric on
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
.

Homogenous norms are not in general proper norms on
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
. However, for any homogeneous norm
d_{\rm o}
on
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
we have that there exists a constant
\Lambda>0
such that

\Lambda^{-1}\lvert\xi\rvert_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}\leq d_{\rm o}(\xi)\leq\Lambda\lvert\xi\rvert_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}},\quad\forall\xi\in{\mathds{H}^{n}},\penalty\ \lvert\xi\rvert_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}:=\left((\lvert x\rvert^{2}+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

The function
\lvert\cdot\rvert_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}
in the display above is
the Korányi distance
in the Heisenberg group, and it is actually a norm; for the proof we refer to
[
27
]
and to Example 5.1 in
[
6
]
.

For any fixed
\xi_{0}\in{\mathds{H}^{n}}
and
R>0
, we denote by
B_{R}(\xi_{0})
the ball with center
\xi_{0}
and radius
R
, given by

B_{R}(\xi_{0}):=\Big\{\xi\in{\mathds{H}^{n}}:|\xi_{0}^{-1}\circ\xi|_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}<R\Big\}.

2.2.
The fractional Sobolev spaces

We recall now some definitions and a few related basic results about our fractional functional setting. For further details, we refer the reader to
[
2
,
52
]
; see also
[
34
]

for
the basics of the fractional Sobolev spaces in the Euclidean setting.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
s\in(0,1)
, and let
u:{\mathds{H}^{n}}\rightarrow{\mathds{R}}
be a measurable function.
The fractional Sobolev spaces
HW^{s,p}
on the Heisenberg group is defined by

HW^{s,p}({\mathds{H}^{n}}):=\Big\{u\in L^{p}({\mathds{H}^{n}}):\frac{\lvert u(\xi)-u(\eta)\rvert}{\lvert\eta^{-1}\circ\xi\rvert^{s+\frac{Q}{p}}}\in L^{p}({\mathds{H}^{n}}\times{\mathds{H}^{n}})\Big\},

endowed with the natural fractional norm

\|u\|_{HW^{s,p}({\mathds{H}^{n}})}:=\Big(\int_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\,{\rm d}\xi+\underbrace{\int_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}\int_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}\frac{\lvert u(\xi)-u(\eta)\rvert^{p}}{\lvert\eta^{-1}\circ\xi\rvert^{Q+sp}}\,{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\eta}_{=:[u]_{HW^{s,p}({\mathds{H}^{n}})}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\qquad u\in HW^{s,p}({\mathds{H}^{n}}).

In a similar fashion, given a domain
\Omega\subset{\mathds{H}^{n}}
, one can define the fractional Sobolev space
HW^{s,p}(\Omega)
. By
HW^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
we denote the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
HW^{s,p}({\mathds{H}^{n}})
. Conversely, if
v\in HW^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime})
with
\Omega\Subset\Omega^{\prime}
and
v=0
outside of
\Omega
a. ​e., then
v
has a representative in
HW_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
as well.

As expected, one can prove that classical embedding theorems still hold in our fractional functional setting. In particular, we refer to the result in
[
52
]
, where the authors prove the following

Theorem 2.2

(Theorem 2.5 in
[
52
]
)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in(0,1)
such that
sp<Q
. For any measurable compactly supported function
u:{\mathds{H}^{n}}\rightarrow{\mathds{R}}
there exists a positive constant
c\equiv c(n,p,s)
such that

\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p^{*}}({\mathds{H}^{n}})}\,\leq\,c\,[u]^{p}_{HW^{s,p}({\mathds{H}^{n}})}\,,

where
p^{*}={Qp}/{(Q-sp)}
is the critical Sobolev exponent.

For an analogous fractional Sobolev embedding on stratified Lie groups we refer to
[
47
, Theorem 1]
, where the authors also investigate some relevant properties of nonlinear fractional eigenvalue problems on stratified Lie groups. Moreover, for further results on fractional Sobolev embedding on stratified Lie groups we also refer to
[
48
]
.

In the event that
sp>Q
we have a classical Morrey-type embedding.

Theorem 2.3

(Theorem 1.5 in
[
2
]
)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
s\in(0,1)
such that
sp>Q
. For any

we have that

\|u\|_{C^{0,\beta}({\mathds{H}^{n}})}\leq c\|u\|_{HW^{s,p}({\mathds{H}^{n}})},

where
c\equiv c(n,s,p)>0
and
\beta=(sp-Q)/p
.

Moreover, by applying the same strategy developed in
[
5

, one can prove a fractional Poincaré-type inequality.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
s\in(0,1)
and
u\in HW^{s,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. Then, for any

we have that

\int_{B_{r}(\xi_{0})}|u-(u)_{r}|^{p}\,{\rm d}\xi\leq c\,r^{sp}\int_{B_{r}(\xi_{0})}\int_{B_{r}(\xi_{0})}\frac{|u(\xi)-u(\eta)|^{p}}{|\eta^{-1}\circ\xi|_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}^{Q+sp}}\,{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\eta,

where
c\equiv c(n,p)>0
and
(u)_{r}:=\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(\xi_{0})}u\,{\rm d}\xi
.

\begin{cases}{\mathcal{L}}u=f(\cdot,u)&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\[2.15277pt]

We have the following

Definition 2.5

(
Fractional weak solutions
. See Section 2.2 in
[

A function

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}\int_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}\frac{|u(\xi)-u(\eta)|^{p-2}(u(\xi)-u(\eta))(\psi(\xi)-\psi(\eta))}{d_{\textrm{o}}(\eta^{-1}\circ\xi)^{Q+sp}}\,{\rm d}\xi\,{\rm d}\eta

A function

Few remarks are in order. Firstly, the requirement that

can be weakened assuming

Lemma 2.6

(
Tail estimate
. See Lemma 2.9 in
[
78
]
)
.

Let
u
be a weak supersolution in
B_{2r}(\xi_{0})
. Then, there exists
c\equiv c(n,p,s,\Lambda)>0
such that

\displaystyle\textup{Tail}(u_{+};\xi_{0},r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,r^{\frac{sp-1-Q}{p-1}}[u]_{HW^{h,p-1}(B_{r}(\xi_{0}))}+c\,r^{-\frac{Q}{p-1}}\|u\|_{L^{p-1}(B_{r}(\xi_{0}))}

\displaystyle+\,c\,\textup{Tail}(u_{-};\xi_{0},r)+c\,r^{\frac{sp}{p-1}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\xi_{0}))}^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

with

h:=\max\left\{0,\frac{sp-1}{p-1}\right\}<s.

In particular, if

is a weak supersolution in an open set
\Omega

u\in L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathds{H}^{n}})

Proof.

Firstly, we write the weak formulation, for nonnegative
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{r/2}(\xi_{0}))
such that
\phi\equiv 1
in
B_{r/4}(\xi_{0})
, with
0\leq\phi\leq 1
and

. We have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}f(\xi,u)\phi(\xi){\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(\xi_{0})}\int_{B_{r}(\xi_{0})}\frac{|u(\xi)-u(\eta)|^{p-2}\big(u(\xi)-u(\eta)\big)\big(\phi(\xi)-\phi(\eta)\big)}{d_{\textrm{o}}(\eta^{-1}\circ\xi)^{Q+sp}}{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\eta

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}\setminus B_{r}(\xi_{0})}\int_{B_{r/2}(\xi_{0})}\frac{|u(\xi)-u(\eta)|^{p-2}\big(u(\xi)-u(\eta)\big)\phi(\xi)}{d_{\textrm{o}}(\eta^{-1}\circ\xi)^{Q+sp}}{\rm d}\xi{\rm d}\eta

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

The left-hand side can be treated recalling the regularity assumptions on the nonlinearity

\int_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}f(\xi,u)\phi(\xi){\rm d}\xi\geq-c\,r^{Q}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\xi_{0}))}.

The first term can be easily estimated using
|\phi(\xi)-\phi(\eta)|\leq 8|\eta^{-1}\circ\xi|_{{\mathds{H}^{n}}}/r
to get

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq\frac{c}{r^{\min\{sp,1\}}}\left[u\right]_{HW^{h,p-1}(B_{r}(\xi_{0}))}^{p-1}

In order to estimate the second term, we can observe that

\displaystyle|u(\xi)-u(\eta)|^{p-2}\big(u(\xi)-u(\eta)\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\big(u_{+}^{p-1}(\xi)+u_{-}^{p-1}(\eta)\big)-u_{+}^{p-1}(\eta),

and thus

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,r^{-sp}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p-1}(B_{r}(\xi_{0}))}^{p-1}+c\,r^{Q-sp}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\xi_{0},r)^{p-1}

\displaystyle-\frac{r^{Q-sp}}{c}{\rm Tail}(u;\xi_{0},r)^{p-1}.

By combining all the displays above, we obtain the desired estimate. The second statement plainly follows by an application of Hölder’s Inequality.

3.1.
Fundamental estimates

We start recalling some fundamental estimates contained in
[

5.
The Perron method

In this section we investigate the Dirichlet problem (
2.1
) in a very general setting by proving the existence of a generalized solution in the sense of Perron. As well known, Perron’s Method is a classical tool in Potential Theory and it provides the existence of a solution for the Dirichlet problem related to the Laplace equation in an arbitrary open set
\Omega\subset{\mathds{R}}^{n}
and for arbitrary boundary datum
g
, without “regularity assumption”.
Roughly speaking, it simply works by finding the least superharmonic function greater or equal to the datum
g
on the boundary
\partial\Omega
, and it can be applied once knowing some helpful properties of superharmonic functions, such as comparison and maximum principles, and barriers techniques.

For a deep treatment of the classical Potential Theory in stratified Lie groups, we refer the interested reader to the monograph
[
9
]
by Bonfiglioli, Lanconelli and Uguzzoni.

Let us focus on our fractional framework. The starting point is into giving a proper definition of upper / lower class of functions.

Definition 5.1

(

. See Definition 5.1 in
[

]
)

Let
\Omega
be an open and bounded subset of
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
and assume that

\mathcal{U}_{g}
as the family of all functions

such that

(i)

almost everywhere in

a.​ e. in

6.
Possible further developments

Despite the increasing interest in nonlocal equations whose underlying geometry is that of stratified Lie groups, there are still several questions which remain open. Below, we list just a few possible developments related to the results presented in the present notes.

Firstly, it is worth remarking that in all the results stated in the preceding sections, we treat general weak solutions, namely by truncation and handling the resulting error term as a right-hand side in the spirit of De Giorgi-Nash-Moser.
However, one could deal with the same family of operators by focusing solely on bounded viscosity solutions in the spirit of Krylov-Safonov, as e. ​g.
[
85
,
56
]
.
Consequently, a natural open problem is whether or not, and under which assumptions on the involved quantities, the viscosity solutions to nonlocal equations in
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
are indeed weak solutions, and vice versa.

Also, in the same spirit of the series of papers by Brasco, Lindgren, and Schikorra
[
11
,
12
]
, one would expect higher differentiability. Similarly, one can investigate possible
self-improving properties of the solutions to (
2.1
) as in the recent nonlocal Gehring-type theorems proven in
[
59
,
82
]
and
[
83
]
.

Furthermore, one could expect Hölder continuity and other regularity results for the solutions to a strictly related class of problems; that is, by adding in (
2.1
) a quasilinear operator modeled on the subelliptic
p
-Laplacian. Apart from the recent paper
[
71
]
, the comprehension of mixed operators on stratified Lie groups is still not clearly understood as in the case of its Euclidean counterpart. Thus, regularity theory and related properties of weak solutions to
{\mathcal{L}}u=0
when
{\mathcal{L}}
is a mixed type operator are almost unknown. Clearly, one can expect that several results in the Euclidean setting, as e. ​g., in
[
8
,
30
,
43
,
84
,
66
,
14
,
72
]
do still hold for mixed-type operators in the Heisenberg group.

In a similar fashion of nonlocal operators,
one could expect nonlocal Härnack inequalities and other regularity results for the solutions to a strictly related class of problems; that is, by adding in (
2.1
) a second integro-differential operator,
\mathcal{L}_{t,q;\alpha}
of differentiability exponent
t>s
and summability growth
q>1
, controlled by the zero set of a modulating coefficient
\alpha\equiv\alpha(\xi,\eta)
; that is, the so-called nonlocal double phase problem, in the same spirit of the Euclidean case treated in
[
31
,
15
]
. In these direction very fundamental regularity results have been recently obtained both for double phase nonlocal equations in
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
in the relevant paper
[
39
]
.

Moreover, we also remark that local boundedness, Hölder continuity and Harnack inequality have been recently obtained for nonlocal equations in
{\mathds{H}^{n}}
with non-standard growth in
[
38
]
, generalizing in the subRiemannian setting the estimates achieved in
[
20
,
13
]
.

Lastly, it is natural to wonder whether or not the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser-type regularity results developed for weak solutions to (
2.1
) can be generalized to the parabolic framework; i. ​e.
\partial_{t}u(t,\xi)={\mathcal{L}}u(t,\xi)
in
{\mathds{H}^{n}}\times{\mathds{R}}
, where the leading operator
{\mathcal{L}}
is defined by (
1.1
).
In the fractional Euclidean setting, several remarkable achievements have been recently established in
[
87
,
35
]
such as the boundedness estimates together with Hölder continuity for the superlinear case
p\geq 2
, the latter generalized for any value of the integrability exponent in
[
62
,
3
]
, and Harnack-type inequalities
[
55
,
88
]
.
In the fractional subRiemannian setting of the Heisenberg group to our knowledge almost all the natural questions are unsolved. Nevertheless, a first result establishing existence for solution to the related Cauchy-Dirichlet problem can be found in the recent paper
[
49
]
.

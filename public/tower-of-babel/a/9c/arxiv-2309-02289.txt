An operator preconditioned combined field integral equation for electromagnetic scattering Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Van Chien Le, Kristof Cools

Abstract
This paper aims to address two issues of integral equations for the scattering of time-harmonic electromagnetic waves by a perfect electric conductor with Lipschitz continuous boundary: ill-conditioned boundary element Galerkin matrices on fine meshes and instability at spurious resonant frequencies. The remedy to ill-conditioned matrices is operator preconditioning, and resonant instability is eliminated by means of a combined field integral equation. Exterior traces of single and double layer potentials are complemented by their interior counterparts for a purely imaginary wave number. We derive the corresponding variational formulation in the natural trace space for electromagnetic fields and establish its well-posedness for all wave numbers. A Galerkin discretization scheme is employed using conforming edge boundary elements on dual meshes, which produces well-conditioned discrete linear systems of the variational formulation. Some numerical results are also provided to support the numerical analysis.

Introduction

1.1
The scattering boundary value problem

This paper concerns the scattering of electromagnetic waves by a perfect electric conductor, which plays a fundamental role in computational electromagnetics. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be an open bounded domain with a connected Lipschitz boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
. The exterior region
\Omega^{c}:=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is filled by a homogeneous, isotropic, and linear material with permittivity
\epsilon
and permeability
\mu
, both are positive constants in
\Omega^{c}
. Electromagnetic waves propagating outside
\Omega
are excited by a time-harmonic incident electric field
\boldsymbol{e}^{in}
of angular frequency
\omega>0
. Therefore, we can switch to a frequency-domain problem with unknown complex-valued spatial functions. The scattered electric field
\boldsymbol{e}
satisfies the following exterior Dirichlet boundary-value problem for the electric wave equation
[
19
, Section 6.4]

\displaystyle\textbf{{curl}}\,\textbf{{curl}}\,\boldsymbol{e}-\kappa^{2}\boldsymbol{e}=\boldsymbol{0}

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\Omega^{c},

(1)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{e}\times\textbf{n}=-\boldsymbol{e}^{in}\times\textbf{n}

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad\Gamma,

(2)

supplemented with the Silver-Müller radiation condition

\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{\partial B_{r}}\left|{\textbf{{curl}}\,\boldsymbol{e}\times\textbf{n}+i\kappa(\textbf{n}\times\boldsymbol{e})\times\textbf{n}}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s=0.

(3)

Here,
\kappa=\omega\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}>0
is the wave number,
n
is the unit normal vector on
\Gamma
pointing from
\Omega
to
\Omega^{c}
, and
B_{r}
is the ball of radius

centered at
0
. We refer the reader to Rellich’s lemma
[
15
,
40
]
for the existence and uniqueness of a solution to (
1
)-(

).

1.2
Challenges in boundary integral equations

Boundary integral equations have become the most popular method to solve electromagnetic scattering problems in unbounded domains. Based on the integral representation formulas for solutions to Maxwell’s equations, this method poses an alternative problem on the boundary of domains, leading to discrete systems of much smaller size. Prominent examples are the electric and magnetic field integral equations (EFIE and MFIE). This paper aims to address two issues of boundary integral equations for electromagnetic scattering by perfectly conducting bodies with Lipschitz continuous boundary: instability at spurious resonant frequencies (in short, resonant instability) and ill-conditioned Galerkin boundary element matrices on fine meshes. These issues arise when
\kappa^{2}
is close to a resonant frequency (the former), or when the discrete problem involves a large number of unknowns (the latter), both manifest themselves in the ill-conditioning of the discrete linear systems.

1.3
Previous and related works

Resonant frequencies are Dirichlet or Neumann eigenvalues of the
curl

curl
-operator inside
\Omega
, at which the standard boundary integral equations are not uniquely solvable. Among some approaches to overcome the resonant instability (see, e.g.,
[
26
,
28
]
), combined field integral equations (CFIEs) are vastly more popular than others. CFIEs owe their name to an appropriate combination of the single and double layer potentials. This class of integral equations for electromagnetic scattering was pioneered by Panich in
[
41
]
. A regularized CFIE was then introduced by Kress in
[
34
]
. Both formulations are only applicable for domains with sufficiently smooth boundaries (specifically, belonging to the class
\Cs^{2}
), which are not suitable for the numerical implementation of the boundary element method. With the advancement in numerical analysis of Maxwell’s equations in Lipschitz domains (see, e.g.,
[
8
,
9
,
11
,
14
]
), some coercive CFIEs were proposed by Buffa and Hiptmair in
[
13
]
, and by Steinbach and Windisch in
[
44
]
, which are applicable for domains with Lipschitz continuous boundaries.

Despite the fact that CFIEs are well-posed at all frequencies, they may produce ill-conditioned matrices when involving a large number of discrete unknowns, which leads the numerical resolution by means of iterative schemes to be extremely expensive. The typical approach to curing this challenge is to employ an algebraic preconditioner. Some preconditioners for CFIEs were presented in
[
2
,
37
,
6
,
5
,
1
]
. All the treatments primarily rely on the fact that the MFIE operator on a sufficiently smooth surface is a Fredholm operator of second kind. Unfortunately, this special property is no longer valid for non-smooth Lipschitz domains.

In computational engineering, numerous studies have been done on operator preconditioned CFIEs for electromagnetic scattering. For instance, three different CFIEs were introduced in
[
20
]
, where exterior EFIE and MFIE operators of a purely imaginary wave number serve as preconditioners. The unique solvability and the well-conditioning of Galerkin discretization matrices of these formulations were proven for spheres and surfaces obtained by smooth deformations of a sphere. In spite of their similar numerical behaviors, the CFIE which consists of the EFIE preconditioned by its counterpart and the MFIE (without a preconditioner) has attracted the most attention from engineers because of its computational simplicity. The uniqueness of a solution to this formulation was proven in
[
3
]
, and some stable and accurate discretization schemes were proposed in
[
4
,
21
]
. A different approach was introduced in
[
38
]
, where the exterior EFIE and MFIE operators are complemented by their interior counterparts of a purely imaginary wave number. A proof of the injectivity of the governing operator was provided. Moreover, it has been shown that a Galerkin discretization of this Yukawa-Calderón CFIE in conjunction with a discrete Helmholtz decomposition and a rescaling procedure results in linear systems that are well-conditioned at low frequencies and numerical experiments demonstrated well-conditioning for fine meshes. Both the stability at low frequencies and for fine meshes are maintained when multiply connected scatterers are considered and when round-off and quadrature errors are introduced.

1.4
Novelty and contributions

These attractive properties justify additional analysis of integral equations of the class introduced in
[
38
]
. In this paper, we investigate an indirect equation on Lipschitz domains. The indirect formulation is based on a potential representation for the scattered electric field
\boldsymbol{e}
. Our main contributions are twofold: proving the well-posedness of the governing CFIE for all wave numbers and proposing a stable Galerkin boundary element discretization. The uniqueness of a solution is established by means of the ellipticity of the EFIE operator with a purely imaginary wave number. A generalized Gårding inequality is achieved by leveraging a Calderón projection formula. In engineering papers, a proof of the coercivity of CFIE operators is typically omitted and in the analysis it is assumed that the MFIE operator is a Fredholm operator of second kind. However, this assumption is only valid for sufficiently smooth domains. In Lipschitz domains, the injectivity and a generalized Gårding inequality of the governing operator are necessary and sufficient for the unique solvability of the CFIE because of the Fredholm alternative. A mixed Galerkin discretization is then introduced using
\textup{div}_{\Gamma}
-conforming boundary elements on a pair of dual meshes. The unique solvability and the uniform boundedness of condition number of resulting linear systems are shown. It is important to note that the discretization scheme introduced in this paper differs from what practitioners typically use and in particular differs from the scheme studied in
[
38
]
.

The combined field integral equation

In this section, we propose a new CFIE which yields a unique solution to (
1
)-(
3
) for any wave number
\kappa>0
. A common strategy when combining the Maxwell single layer potential
\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{\kappa}_{SL}
and the Maxwell double layer potential
\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{\kappa}_{DL}
is to introduce a compact regularization that targets either
\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{\kappa}_{SL}
or
\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{\kappa}_{DL}
(see
[
34
]
and
[
13
]
). In contrast to this approach, in the following formulation, the potentials
\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{\kappa}_{SL}
and
\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{\kappa}_{DL}
are respectively complemented by their counterparts
\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{i\kappa}_{SL}
and
\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{i\kappa}_{DL}
for purely imaginary wave number. This way of combining the operators has been investigated for direct CFIEs, whose unknowns are the physical electric current on the surface, see, e.g.,
[
20
,
38
]
. In this paper, we investigate an indirect formulation that is based on a potential representation for the scattered electric field
\boldsymbol{e}
. In particular, we consider the following ansatz

\boldsymbol{e}=\left({i\eta\,\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{\kappa}_{SL}\circ\gamma_{D}^{-}\circ\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{i\kappa}_{SL}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{\kappa}_{DL}\circ\gamma_{D}^{-}\circ\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{i\kappa}_{DL}}\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi}),

(12)

where
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
and

\eta\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. Taking the exterior tangential trace
\gamma^{+}_{D}
of (
12
) results in the CFIE

\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=-\gamma_{D}^{+}\boldsymbol{e}^{in},

(13)

where the boundary integral operator

\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}:=i\eta\,S_{\kappa}\circ S_{i\kappa}+\left({-\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{\kappa}}\right)\circ\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right).

(14)

From now on, we fix the coupling parameter
\eta\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and keep it constant. The particular impact of this parameter on the CFIE will be discussed later. As
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
is its own dual with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}
, the variational formulation of (
13
) reads as: find
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
such that, for all
\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)

\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}=-\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},\gamma_{D}^{+}\boldsymbol{e}^{in}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}.

(15)

4.1
Uniqueness

The most important property of CFIEs which distinguishes them from the standard boundary integral equations is the uniqueness of a solution for any wave number.

Theorem 7
(Uniqueness).

For any wave number
\kappa>0
, the integral equation (
13
) has at most one solution
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
be a solution to the homogeneous equation

\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\boldsymbol{0}.

It is clear that the scattered electric field
\boldsymbol{e}
given by (
12
) is a solution to the exterior problem (
1
) and (
3
) with the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition
\boldsymbol{e}\times\textbf{n}=\boldsymbol{0}
. Referring to Relich’s lemma, we can conclude that
\boldsymbol{e}=\boldsymbol{0}
in
\Omega^{c}
. Therefore, the jump relations (
11
) can be invoked to get the interior traces

\gamma_{D}^{-}\boldsymbol{e}=\left({\gamma_{D}^{-}\circ\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{i\kappa}_{DL}}\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi}),\qquad\qquad\qquad\gamma_{N}^{-}\boldsymbol{e}=i\eta\left({\gamma_{D}^{-}\circ\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{i\kappa}_{SL}}\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi}).

On one hand, the integration by parts formula (
4
) gives us that

\left\langle{\gamma_{D}^{-}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}},\gamma_{N}^{-}\boldsymbol{e}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}=\int_{\Omega}\left({\kappa^{2}\left|{\boldsymbol{e}(\boldsymbol{x})}\right|^{2}-\left|{\textbf{{curl}}\,\boldsymbol{e}(\boldsymbol{x})}\right|^{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}.

On the other hand, the relations (
9
) allow us to deduce that

\displaystyle\left\langle{\gamma_{D}^{-}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}},\gamma_{N}^{-}\boldsymbol{e}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle=i\eta\left\langle{\left({\gamma_{D}^{-}\circ\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{i\kappa}_{DL}}\right)(\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}),\left({\gamma_{D}^{-}\circ\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{i\kappa}_{SL}}\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi})}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle=i\eta\int_{\Omega}\left({\kappa^{2}\left|{\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{i\kappa}_{SL}(\boldsymbol{\xi})(\boldsymbol{x})}\right|^{2}+\left|{\textbf{{curl}}\,\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{i\kappa}_{SL}(\boldsymbol{\xi})(\boldsymbol{x})}\right|^{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\in i\mathbb{R}.

These arguments together with the continuity of the trace operator
\gamma_{D}^{-}:\HHs(\textbf{{curl}},\Omega)\to\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
(cf. Theorem
2
) imply that

0=\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{i\kappa}_{SL}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}\right\rVert_{\HHs(\textbf{{curl}},\Omega)}\geq C\left\lVert{S_{i\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)},

for some positive constant
C
.
This means
S_{i\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=0
in
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
. Finally, we invoke the
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
-ellipticity of
S_{i\kappa}
in Corollary
6
to conclude that
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\boldsymbol{0}
, or equivalently, the CFIE (
13
) has at most one solution in
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
.

4.2
Coercivity

It is well-known that if the integral operator
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
is a Fredholm operator of index
0
, then the injectivity of
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
(i.e., Theorem
7
) also implies its surjectivity by a Fredholm alternative argument. Therefore, the next task is to prove that the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
satisfies a generalized Gårding inequality.

Theorem 8
(Coercivity).

For any wave number
\kappa>0
, there exist a positive constant
C
, an isomorphism
\Theta_{\Gamma}:\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)\to\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
, and a compact sesquilinear form
2
2

2

Let
\Hs
be a normed space over
\mathbb{C}
and
\Hs^{\prime}
its dual space. A sesquilinear form
a:\Hs\times\Hs\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be compact if its associated operator
A:\Hs\to\Hs^{\prime}
is compact, i.e.,

a(u,\overline{v})=\left\langle{Au,v}\right\rangle_{\Hs^{\prime}\times\Hs},\qquad\qquad\forall u,v\in\Hs.

c:\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)\times\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\left|{\left\langle{\Theta_{\Gamma}\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{u})}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}+c(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})}\right|\geq C\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma).

(16)

Proof.

We rewrite the integral operator
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
in the following form

\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}=\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\kappa}+R_{\kappa},

(17)

where
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\kappa}:\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)\to\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
is given by

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\kappa}:=i\eta\,\widetilde{S}_{i\kappa}\circ S_{i\kappa}+\left({-\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right)\circ\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right),

with

S_{i\kappa}=A_{i\kappa}-\kappa^{-2}\textbf{{curl}}_{\Gamma}\circ V_{i\kappa}\circ\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\qquad\quad\widetilde{S}_{i\kappa}=A_{i\kappa}+\kappa^{-2}\textbf{{curl}}_{\Gamma}\circ V_{i\kappa}\circ\textup{div}_{\Gamma}.

Here, the operator
{R_{\kappa}}:\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)\to\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
reads as

{R_{\kappa}}:=i\eta\,\delta S_{\kappa}\circ S_{i\kappa}+\delta C_{\kappa}\circ\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right),

which, according to Lemma
4
, is a compact operator.
The following Calderón projection identity can be deduced from the representation formula (
7
) and
[
12
, Formula (35)]

\left({-\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right)\circ\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right)=\kappa^{2}S_{i\kappa}\circ S_{i\kappa}.

(18)

Next, let us denote

\displaystyle M_{i\kappa}

\displaystyle:=\left({i\eta\,\widetilde{S}_{i\kappa}+\kappa^{2}S_{i\kappa}}\right)

(19)

\displaystyle=\left({\kappa^{2}+i\eta}\right)A_{i\kappa}-\left({1-i\eta\kappa^{-2}}\right)\textbf{{curl}}_{\Gamma}\circ V_{i\kappa}\circ\textup{div}_{\Gamma}.

By means of Theorem
5
, one can easily see that the operator
M_{i\kappa}
is
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
-elliptic in the sense

\displaystyle\Real\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},M_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\kappa^{2}\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},A_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}+\left\langle{\textup{div}_{\Gamma}\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},\left({V_{i\kappa}\circ\textup{div}_{\Gamma}}\right)\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

(20)

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}^{2},

for some constant
C_{1}>0
independent of
\boldsymbol{u}
. Then,
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\kappa}
can be rewritten as

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\kappa}=M_{i\kappa}\circ S_{i\kappa},

and hence

\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}=M_{i\kappa}\circ S_{i\kappa}+R_{\kappa}.

Finally, taking into account that the operator
S_{i\kappa}
is also
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
-elliptic (cf. Corollary
6
), we can conclude

\displaystyle\left|{\left\langle{S_{i\kappa}\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}-\left\langle{S_{i\kappa}\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},R_{\kappa}\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\left\langle{S_{i\kappa}\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},\left({M_{i\kappa}\circ S_{i\kappa}}\right)\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}\left\lVert{S_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}\geq C\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)},

for all
\boldsymbol{u}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
and for some constant
C>0
independent of
\boldsymbol{u}
. In other words, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
satisfies the generalized Gårding inequality (
16
) with the isomorphism
\Theta_{\Gamma}:=S_{i\kappa}
and the compact sesquilinear form
c
defined by

c(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}):=-\left\langle{S_{i\kappa}\overline{\boldsymbol{v}},R_{\kappa}\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},\qquad\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma).

The injectivity of
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
(i.e., Theorem
7
) and the generalized Gårding inequality (
16
) imply that the variational problem (
15
) has a unique solution
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
(cf.
[
10
, Proposition 3]
).

5
Galerkin discretization

This section aims to introduce a Galerkin boundary element discretization scheme for the variational problem (
15
), i.e.,

\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}=-\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},\gamma_{D}^{+}\boldsymbol{e}^{in}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

where
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
is given by

\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}=M_{i\kappa}\circ S_{i\kappa}+i\eta\,\delta S_{\kappa}\circ S_{i\kappa}+\delta C_{\kappa}\circ\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right).

The operator
M_{i\kappa}
is defined by (
19
) and satisfies the
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
-ellipticity (
20
). The analysis in this section holds for any wave number
\kappa>0
. Therefore, we omit its specification.

5.1
Mixed variational formulation

Since
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
consists of composition operators, a direct Galerkin discretization of (
15
) is not possible. A typical approach is to switch (
15
) to a mixed variational formulation. To that end, we introduce two auxiliary unknowns
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
and
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
as follows

\boldsymbol{\varphi}:=S_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{\xi},\qquad\quad\text{and}\qquad\quad\boldsymbol{\psi}:=\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}.

(21)

For the sake of brevity, in what follows, we denote by
\XXs_{\Gamma}
the space
\left({\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}\right)^{3}
equipped with its Euclidean norm.
Then, the mixed variational formulation of (
13
) reads as: find
\boldsymbol{x}:=\left({\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)\in\XXs_{\Gamma}
such that for all
\boldsymbol{y}:=(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w})\in\XXs_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},M_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}+\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},\delta C_{\kappa}\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle+i\eta\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},\delta S_{\kappa}\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},\gamma_{D}^{+}\boldsymbol{e}^{in}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

(22)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}},S_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle-\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{w}},\left({\tfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle-\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{w}},\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle=0.

Let
b=a+t
be the sesquilinear form associated with (
22
), where
a:\XXs_{\Gamma}\times\XXs_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{C}
and
t:\XXs_{\Gamma}\times\XXs_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{C}
are defined by

\displaystyle a(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle:=\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},M_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}+\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}},S_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}+\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{w}},\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\qquad\,-\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}-\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{w}},\left({\tfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

\displaystyle t(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle:=\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},\delta C_{\kappa}\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}+i\eta\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}},\delta S_{\kappa}\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}.

The sesquilinear form
t
is compact and the principal part
a
satisfies the following
\Theta
-coercivity condition.

Lemma 9
.

There exist a constant
C>0
and an isomorphism
\Theta:\XXs_{\Gamma}\to\XXs_{\Gamma}
such that

\left|{a\left({\boldsymbol{x},\Theta\boldsymbol{x}}\right)}\right|\geq C\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\Gamma}},\qquad\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{x}\in\XXs_{\Gamma}.

(23)

Proof.

For all
\boldsymbol{x}:=(\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\psi})\in\XXs_{\Gamma}
, we define the isomorphism
\Theta:\XXs_{\Gamma}\to\XXs_{\Gamma}
by

\Theta\boldsymbol{x}:=\left({\boldsymbol{\varphi},\alpha\boldsymbol{\xi},\beta S_{i\kappa}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right),

(24)

with some positive constants
\alpha
and
\beta
. Since the operator
S_{i\kappa}
is
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
-elliptic, its inverse
S_{i\kappa}^{-1}:\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)\to\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
exists and is continuous. The following estimate holds

\displaystyle\left|{a\left({\boldsymbol{x},\Theta\boldsymbol{x}}\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|{\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{\varphi}},M_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}+\alpha\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}},S_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}+\beta\left\langle{S_{i\kappa}^{-1}\overline{\boldsymbol{\psi}},\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|

(25)

\displaystyle-\alpha\left|{\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|-\beta\left|{\left\langle{S_{i\kappa}^{-1}\overline{\boldsymbol{\psi}},\left({\tfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|.

Due to the
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
-ellipticity and the continuity of
M_{i\kappa}
and
S_{i\kappa}

and
C_{3}
such that

\displaystyle\Real\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{\varphi}},M_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)},

\displaystyle\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}},S_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)},

\displaystyle\left\langle{S_{i\kappa}^{-1}\overline{\boldsymbol{\psi}},\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}\left\lVert{S_{i\kappa}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}\geq C_{3}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)},

for all

\displaystyle\left|{\left\langle{S_{i\kappa}^{-1}\overline{\boldsymbol{\psi}},\left({\tfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|\leq\varepsilon\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}+C_{\varepsilon}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)},

(26)

\boldsymbol{y}_{h}:=\left({\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h}}\right)\in\YYs_{h}

\displaystyle\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}},M_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}+\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}},\delta C_{\kappa}\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle+i\eta\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}},\delta S_{\kappa}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}},\gamma_{D}^{+}\boldsymbol{e}^{in}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

(28)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}},S_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle-\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{w}_{h}},\left({\tfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle-\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{w}_{h}},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle=0.

The following result is analogous to Lemma
9
.

Lemma 10
.

There exist a constant
C>0
independent of
h
and an isomorphism
\Theta_{h}:\XXs_{h}\to\YYs_{h}
such that

\left|{a\left({\boldsymbol{x}_{h},\Theta_{h}\boldsymbol{x}_{h}}\right)}\right|\geq C\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}_{h}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\Gamma}},\qquad\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{x}_{h}\in\XXs_{h}.

(29)

Proof.

Let us firstly define the operators
I_{h}:\UUs_{h}\to\VVs_{h}^{\prime}
and
S_{h}:\VVs_{h}\to\VVs_{h}^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle\left\langle{I_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\VVs_{h}^{\prime}\times\VVs_{h}}

\displaystyle:=\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h},\,\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\VVs_{h},

(30)

\displaystyle\left\langle{S_{h}\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\VVs_{h}^{\prime}\times\VVs_{h}}

\displaystyle:=\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}},S_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{w}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}.

Since the operator
S_{i\kappa}
is
\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)
-elliptic,
S_{h}
is invertible following the Lax-Milgram theorem. The discrete inf-sup condition (
27
) implies that the operator
I_{h}
is injective. Hence,
I_{h}
is bijective as
\dim\VVs_{h}=\dim\UUs_{h}
. Thus,
S_{h}^{-1}\circ I_{h}
is an isomorphism between
\UUs_{h}
and
\VVs_{h}
. In addition, the following identity holds

\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}},(S_{i\kappa}\circ S_{h}^{-1}\circ I_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}=\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},\qquad\quad\forall\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h},\,\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}.

(31)

On one hand, choosing
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=(S_{h}^{-1}\circ I_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
in (
31
) leads to the following estimate

\left\lVert{(S_{h}^{-1}\circ I_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}\leq C\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}.

(32)

On the other hand, the identity (
31
) and the uniform discrete inf-sup condition (
27
) allow us to deduce that

\displaystyle C_{1}\left\lVert{(S_{h}^{-1}\circ I_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}\setminus\left\{{\boldsymbol{0}}\right\}}\dfrac{\left|{\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}},(S_{i\kappa}\circ S_{h}^{-1}\circ I_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}\setminus\left\{{\boldsymbol{0}}\right\}}\dfrac{\left|{\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}}\right|}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}}

(33)

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)},

for all
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}
. Now, we can define the isomorphism
\Theta_{h}:\XXs_{h}\to\YYs_{h}
by

\Theta_{h}\boldsymbol{x}_{h}:=\left({\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{h},\alpha\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\beta(S_{h}^{-1}\circ I_{h})\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}}\right),\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{x}_{h}=\left({\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{h},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}}\right)\in\XXs_{h},

(34)

with some positive
h
-independent constants
\alpha
and
\beta
. Following the lines of the proof of Lemma
9
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
h
such that

\left|{a\left({\boldsymbol{x}_{h},\Theta_{h}\boldsymbol{x}_{h}}\right)}\right|\geq C\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}_{h}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\Gamma}},\qquad\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{x}_{h}\in\XXs_{h}.

(35)

Here, the uniform boundedness (
32
) and the following estimate were used

\displaystyle\left\langle{(S_{h}^{-1}\circ I_{h})\overline{\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{(S_{h}^{-1}\circ I_{h})\overline{\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}},(S_{i\kappa}\circ S_{h}^{-1}\circ I_{h})\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}\left\lVert{(S_{h}^{-1}\circ I_{h})\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}.

We note that
\alpha
and
\beta
in (
34
) are not necessarily the same as in (
24
), and the positive
h
-independent constants
C,C_{1}
and
C_{2}
can be different in different contexts.

The following discrete inf-sup condition is crucial for proving the unique solvability of the discrete variational problem (
28
). This result hints at a generalization of
[
43
, Theorem 4.2.9]
for compact perturbations of a
T
-coercive operator. An equivalent result for compactly perturbed bijective operators is stated in
[
25
, Exercise 26.5]
.

Lemma 11
.

There exist an
h_{0}>0
and a constant
C>0
such that for all
h<h_{0}

\displaystyle\sup_{\boldsymbol{y}_{h}\in\YYs_{h}\setminus\left\{{\boldsymbol{0}}\right\}}\dfrac{\left|{b(\boldsymbol{x}_{h},\boldsymbol{y}_{h})}\right|}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{y}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\Gamma}}}\geq C\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\Gamma}},

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{x}_{h}\in\XXs_{h}.

(36)

Proof.

Our proof mainly follows the approach in the proof of
[
43
, Theorem 4.2.9]
. Let us define the operators
B:\XXs_{\Gamma}\to\XXs_{\Gamma}^{\prime}
and
B_{h}:\XXs_{h}\to\YYs_{h}^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle\left\langle{B\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}}\right\rangle_{\XXs_{\Gamma}^{\prime}\times\XXs_{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle:=b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}),

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\XXs_{\Gamma},

\displaystyle\left\langle{B_{h}\boldsymbol{x}_{h},\boldsymbol{y}_{h}}\right\rangle_{\YYs_{h}^{\prime}\times\YYs_{h}}

\displaystyle:=b(\boldsymbol{x}_{h},\boldsymbol{y}_{h}),

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{x}_{h}\in\XXs_{h},\boldsymbol{y}_{h}\in\YYs_{h}.

We prove the discrete inf-sup condition (
36
) by contradiction. For this purpose, we assume that there exists a sequence
\{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}\}_{h}
with
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}:=(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}_{h},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{h})\in\XXs_{h}
and
\left\lVert{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\Gamma}}=1
such that

\left\lVert{B_{h}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\YYs_{h}^{\prime}}\rightarrow 0,\qquad\quad\text{for }\,h\rightarrow 0.

(37)

Since
\left\{{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}}\right\}_{h}
is bounded in
\XXs_{\Gamma}
, there exists a subsequence (also denoted by
\left\{{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}}\right\}_{h}
) such that

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}\qquad\text{in }\,\XXs_{\Gamma},\qquad\quad\text{for }\,h\rightarrow 0.

Following the lines of the proof of
[
43
, Theorem 4.2.9]
, we have that
B\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}=0
in
\XXs_{\Gamma}^{\prime}
. This result together with the injectivity of
B
(i.e., the uniqueness of a solution to (
22
)) implies that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}=\boldsymbol{0}
.

Next, we show the strong convergence
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}\to\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}
in
\XXs_{\Gamma}
. According to Lemma
10
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left|{a\left({\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h},\Theta_{h}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}}\right)}\right|\geq C\left\lVert{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\Gamma}}=C\left\lVert{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\Gamma}},

(38)

with
\Theta_{h}
defined in (
34
). The last equality holds due to
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}=\boldsymbol{0}
. As the sesquilinear form
t
is compact, there exists a subsequence of
\left\{{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}}\right\}_{h}
(also denoted by
\left\{{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}}\right\}_{h}
) such that

\sup_{\boldsymbol{y}\in\XXs_{\Gamma}\setminus\left\{{\boldsymbol{0}}\right\},\,\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{y}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\Gamma}}=1}\left|{t(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h},\boldsymbol{y})-t(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}},\boldsymbol{y})}\right|=:\delta_{h}\to 0\qquad\quad\text{for }\,h\to 0.

Then, by means of the uniform boundedness (
32
) and the fact
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}=\boldsymbol{0}
, it holds that

\left|{t(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h},\Theta_{h}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h})}\right|=\left|{t(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h},\Theta_{h}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h})-t(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}},\Theta_{h}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h})}\right|\leq\delta_{h}\left\lVert{\Theta_{h}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\Gamma}}\leq C_{1}\delta_{h}=:\tilde{\delta}_{h}.

(39)

Now, combining (
38
) and (
39
) together leads us to the following estimate

\left|{b(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h},\Theta_{h}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h})}\right|=\left|{a(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h},\Theta_{h}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h})+t(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h},\Theta_{h}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h})}\right|\geq C\left\lVert{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\Gamma}}-\tilde{\delta}_{h}.

Finally, taking the assumption (
37
) into account, we end up with

C\left\lVert{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\XXs_{\Gamma}}\leq\left|{b(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h},\Theta_{h}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h})}\right|+\tilde{\delta}_{h}\to 0\qquad\quad\text{for }\,h\to 0.

It means that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}\to\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}=\boldsymbol{0}
when
h\to 0
, which is a contradiction to the assumption
\left\lVert{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\Gamma}}=1
. Therefore, the discrete inf-sup condition (
36
) is satisfied.

The unique solvability of the discrete variational problem (
28
) and the convergence of Galerkin solutions are a direct consequence of Lemma
11
, see
[
43
, Theorem 4.2.1]
.

Theorem 12
.

There exists an
h_{0}>0
such that for all
h<h_{0}
, the discrete problem (
28
) has a unique solution
\boldsymbol{x}_{h}\in\XXs_{h}
. In addition, the discrete solutions
\boldsymbol{x}_{h}
converge to the solution
\boldsymbol{x}\in\XXs_{\Gamma}
of the problem (
22
) and satisfy the quasi-optimal error estimate

\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\Gamma}}\leq C\min_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}\in\XXs_{h}}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\XXs_{\Gamma}},

(40)

for some constant
C>0
independent of
h
.

5.3
Matrix representation

Now, we examine the matrix representation of the Galerkin discrete system (
28
) and its conditioning property. Let
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{1},\boldsymbol{u}_{2},\ldots,\boldsymbol{u}_{N}\}
and
\{\boldsymbol{v}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}_{2},\ldots,\boldsymbol{v}_{N}\}
be bases of
\UUs_{h}
and
\VVs_{h}
, respectively, with
N:=\dim\UUs_{h}=\dim\VVs_{h}
. We introduce the following Galerkin matrices

\displaystyle{[\GGs]}_{mn}:=\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{m}},\boldsymbol{u}_{n}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

\displaystyle{[\MMs]}_{mn}:=\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}_{m}},M_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{u}_{n}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

\displaystyle{[\SSs]}_{mn}:=\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{m}},S_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{v}_{n}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

\displaystyle{[\ZZs]}_{mn}:=i\eta\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}_{m}},\delta S_{\kappa}\boldsymbol{u}_{n}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

\displaystyle{[\CCs]}_{mn}:=\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}_{m}},\delta C_{\kappa}\boldsymbol{u}_{n}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

\displaystyle{[\KKs]}_{mn}:=\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{m}},\left({\tfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right)\boldsymbol{v}_{n}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

with
m,n=1,2,\ldots,N
. A discrete solution
\boldsymbol{x}_{h}\in\XXs_{h}
to (
28
) can be represented as

\boldsymbol{x}_{h}=(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h},\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{h},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h})=\sum_{m=1}^{N}(\widehat{\xi}_{m}\boldsymbol{v}_{m},\widehat{\varphi}_{m}\boldsymbol{u}_{m},\widehat{\psi}_{m}\boldsymbol{u}_{m}).

(41)

Then, the expansion coefficient vector
[\widehat{\boldsymbol{x}}_{h}]_{m}:=[(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}_{h},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{h})]_{m}:=(\widehat{\xi}_{m},\widehat{\varphi}_{m},\widehat{\psi}_{m})
is the solution to the following block matrix system

\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{0}&\MMs+\ZZs&\CCs\\
\SSs&-\GGs&\boldsymbol{0}\\
\KKs&\boldsymbol{0}&-\GGs\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}\\
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}_{h}\\
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{h}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{b}\\
\boldsymbol{0}\\
\boldsymbol{0}\end{pmatrix},

(42)

where the right-hand side vector
{[\boldsymbol{b}]}_{m}:=-\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}_{m}},\gamma^{+}_{D}\boldsymbol{e}^{in}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}
. Since the matrix
\GGs
is invertible (cf. the discrete inf-sup condition (
27
)), the Schur complement of
\GGs
in (
42
) reads as

\LLs\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}:=\left({\MMs\GGs^{-1}\SSs+\ZZs\GGs^{-1}\SSs+\CCs\GGs^{-1}\KKs}\right)\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}=\boldsymbol{b}.

(43)

This matrix equation represents a boundary element discretization of the variational formulation (
15
). The following result is an immediate consequence of Theorem
12
.

Corollary 13
.

There exists an
h_{0}>0
such that for all
h<h_{0}
, the matrix equation (
43
) has a unique solution
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{h}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. In addition, the corresponding
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}\in\VVs_{h}
defined by (
41
) is a quasi-optimal approximation to the continuous solution
\boldsymbol{\xi}
of (
15
) in the sense of (
40
).

Next, let
\lambda_{\max}(\AAs)
and
\lambda_{\min}(\AAs)
be the maximal and minimal (by moduli) eigenvalues of a square matrix
\AAs
, respectively. The spectral condition number of
\AAs
is defined by

\cond(\AAs):=\dfrac{\left|{\lambda_{\max}(\AAs)}\right|}{\left|{\lambda_{\min}(\AAs)}\right|}.

We end up with the following conditioning property of the matrix system (
43
).

Theorem 14
.

There exist an
h_{0}>0
and a constant
C>0
such that for all meshwidth
h<h_{0}

\cond(\GGs^{-\mathsf{T}}\LLs)\leq C.

Proof.

We can rewrite the matrix
\LLs
in the following form

\LLs=\left({\MMs+\ZZs+\CCs\GGs^{-1}\KKs\SSs^{-1}\GGs}\right)\GGs^{-1}\SSs=:\DDs\GGs^{-1}\SSs.

The invertibility of
\LLs
implies that
\DDs
is also invertible. Let
l_{h}:\VVs_{h}\times\UUs_{h}\to\mathbb{C}
and
d_{h}:\UUs_{h}\times\UUs_{h}\to\mathbb{C}
be the sesquilinear forms that produce the matrices
\LLs
and
\DDs
, respectively. Obviously,
d_{h}
is uniformly bounded. The discrete inf-sup condition (
36
) implies the uniform discrete inf-sup condition for
l_{h}
, which allows us to deduce that

\displaystyle\sup_{\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}}\dfrac{\left|{d_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})}\right|}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}}\dfrac{\left|{l_{h}\left({(S_{h}^{-1}\circ I_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h}}\right)}\right|}{\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}}

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}\left\lVert{(S_{h}^{-1}\circ I_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}\left\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\rVert_{\HHs^{-1/2}_{\times}(\textup{div}_{\Gamma},\Gamma)},

for all
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\UUs_{h}
and for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
independent of
h
. The operators
I_{h}:\UUs_{h}\to\VVs_{h}^{\prime}
and
S_{h}:\VVs_{h}\to\VVs_{h}^{\prime}
are defined by (
30
).
The last inequality holds due to the inequality (
33
). According to
[
16
]
and
[
30
, Theorem 2.1]
, the spectral condition number of the matrix
\GGs^{-\mathsf{T}}\DDs\GGs^{-1}\SSs
is uniformly bounded.

Remark 15
.

In computational engineering, the direct counterpart of the CFIE (
13
) is more commonly used. It can be derived from the Stratton-Chu representation formula (
7
). More specifically, taking the exterior tangential and Neumann traces of (
7
) for the solution
\boldsymbol{e}
of (
1
)-(
3
) respectively gives the standard EFIE and MFIE

S_{\kappa}(\gamma_{N}^{+}\boldsymbol{e})=\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{\kappa}}\right)(\gamma_{D}^{+}\boldsymbol{e}^{in}),\qquad\quad\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{\kappa}}\right)(\gamma_{N}^{+}\boldsymbol{e})=\kappa^{2}S_{\kappa}(\gamma_{D}^{+}\boldsymbol{e}^{in}).

Left multiplying the EFIE and MFIE by
i\eta S_{i\kappa}
and
-\tfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}
, respectively, then combining the resulting equations together leads us to the following CFIE

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\kappa}(\gamma_{N}^{+}\boldsymbol{e})

\displaystyle:=\left({i\eta\,S_{i\kappa}\circ S_{\kappa}+\left({-\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right)\circ\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{\kappa}}\right)}\right)(\gamma_{N}^{+}\boldsymbol{e})

(44)

\displaystyle=\left({i\eta\,S_{i\kappa}\circ\left({\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{\kappa}}\right)+\kappa^{2}\left({-\dfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa}}\right)\circ S_{\kappa}}\right)(\gamma_{D}^{+}\boldsymbol{e}^{in}).

The direct formulation (
44
) is similar to the Yukawa-Calderón CFIE introduced in
[
38
]
. The injectivity and coercivity of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\kappa}
can be obtained by following the same approaches in the proof of Theorems
7
and
8
. The injectivity of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\kappa}
was also shown in
[
38
]
, which was based on a standard approach from
[
6
]
.

A typical Galerkin boundary element discretization of the operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\kappa}
reads as (see, e.g.,
[
38
,
36
]
)

\widetilde{\LLs}:=\SSs\GGs^{-\mathsf{T}}\widetilde{\SSs}+\NNs\GGs^{-1}\widetilde{\NNs},

where the Galerkin matrices are given by

\displaystyle[\SSs]_{mn}:=\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{m}},S_{i\kappa}\boldsymbol{v}_{n}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},\

\displaystyle[\widetilde{\SSs}]_{mn}:=i\eta\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}_{m}},S_{\kappa}\boldsymbol{u}_{n}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

\displaystyle[\NNs]_{mn}:=\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{m}},(-\tfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{i\kappa})\,\boldsymbol{u}_{n}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma},

\displaystyle[\widetilde{\NNs}]_{mn}:=\left\langle{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}_{m}},(\tfrac{1}{2}Id+C_{\kappa})\,\boldsymbol{u}_{n}}\right\rangle_{\times,\Gamma}.

Several numerical experiments in
[
38
,
36
]
showed that the corresponding discrete problem is uniquely solvable for all wave numbers
\kappa>0
, and the matrix
\widetilde{\LLs}
is well-conditioned both when a fine mesh is used and when a low frequency is considered. A similar discretization scheme can also be applied for the variational problem (
15
). However, proofs of the corresponding discrete inf-sup conditions and the uniform boundedness of condition number are still missing from literature. We note that those proofs do not directly benefit from the arguments established in Lemma
11
and Theorem
14
. Therefore, they require a separate examination, which falls beyond the scope of this paper.

Remark 16
.

The CFIE formulation (
13
) can be generalized when replacing the purely imaginary wave number
i\kappa
by any wave number
i\kappa^{\prime}
with
\kappa^{\prime}>0
. The analysis can immediately be extended to that case.

6
Numerical results

In this section, we perform some numerical experiments to evaluate the performance of the proposed CFIE and its Galerkin discretization. The scattering of electromagnetic waves by a sphere and a unit cube are considered. In all experiments, excitation is provided by an incident plane wave with electric field

\boldsymbol{e}^{in}(\boldsymbol{x})=\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}\exp(i\kappa\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}),

where
\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}
and
\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}
stand for the unit vectors along the
x
-axis and
z
-axis, respectively.

6.1
Sphere

We start with the scattering by a perfectly conducting sphere of radius
1\mathrm{m}
, centered at the origin of the coordinate system.

The first experiment aims at demonstrating the solvability of the proposed CFIE (
13
) and the convergence of its Galerkin discretization (
43
). We choose
\kappa=4.4934
which is a resonant wave number associated with the unit sphere (see
[
32
, Section 7.2]
). The exact solution
\boldsymbol{e}_{\mathrm{exct}}
to the problem (
1
)-(
3
) can be derived via the famous Mie series, see
[
39
, Section 9.5]
and
[
32
, Section 7.4.3]
.

The unit sphere is approximated by planar triangulations with meshwidth
h
. The discrete solutions
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{h}
are computed by solving the matrix equation (
43
) using the GMRES iterative method with tolerance
\varepsilon_{0}=10^{-12}
. The corresponding scattered fields
\boldsymbol{e}_{h}
are computed based on the potential ansatz (
12
). The coupling parameter
\eta=-\kappa^{2}
is chosen, and four purely imaginary wave numbers
i\kappa^{\prime}
(instead of
i\kappa)
, with
\kappa^{\prime}/\kappa=0.1,1,5
and
10
, are examined, with the aim to support the assertion in Remark
16
. The following average pointwise error between

and

\mathrm{err}_{h}:=\dfrac{1}{N_{S}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{S}}\left({\left|{\boldsymbol{e}_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})-\boldsymbol{e}_{\mathrm{exct}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})}\right|^{2}+\left|{\textbf{{curl}}\,\boldsymbol{e}_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})-\textbf{{curl}}\,\boldsymbol{e}_{\mathrm{exct}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})}\right|^{2}}\right)^{1/2},

where
\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\ldots,\boldsymbol{x}_{N_{S}}
, with
N_{S}=5000
, are the evaluation points uniformly distributed on the centered sphere of radius
2\mathrm{m}
. Figure
1

(left)
depicts the average pointwise error with respect to

for different
\kappa^{\prime}

7
Conclusions

In this contribution, we have introduced a boundary integral equation for electromagnetic scattering by a perfect electric conductor with Lipschitz continuous boundary. This equation yields a unique solution for all wave numbers. In addition, we have also proposed a Galerkin boundary element discretization for the integral equation, for which the unique solvability, the convergence and the well-conditioning have been demonstrated.

In a forthcoming work, the convergence rate of the proposed discretization scheme should be investigated. To boost the robustness of the numerical scheme, particularly for complex geometries with corners, other pairs of dual meshes and the corresponding boundary elements should also be considered.

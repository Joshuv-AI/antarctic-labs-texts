Donaldson-Thomas invariants from tropical disks Thanks: The second author was supported by the National Science Foundation RTG Grant DMS-1246989, and later by the Starter Grant “Categorified Donaldson-Thomas Theory” no. 759967 of the European Research Council.
Man-Wai Cheung, Travis Mandel

Abstract
. We prove that the quantum DT-invariants associated to quivers with genteel potential can be expressed in terms of certain refined counts of tropical disks. This is based on a quantum version of Bridgeland’s description of cluster scattering diagrams in terms of stabilitiy conditions, plus a new version of the description of scattering diagrams in terms of tropical disk counts. The weights with which the tropical disks are counted are expressed in terms of motivic integrals of certain quiver flag varieties. We also show via explicit counterexample that Hall algebra broken lines do not result in consistent Hall algebra theta functions, i.e., they violate the extension of a lemma of Carl-Pumperla-Siebert from the classical setting.

1.2.
Hall algebra broken lines violate the Carl-Pumperla-Siebert Lemma

One might hope (as we had hoped) that Theorem
1.1
holds without applying the integration maps, i.e., as an identity in the Hall algebra. Unfortunately, this fails as a result of the fact that elements of the Hall algebra with parallel dimension vectors need not commute (although we see that the result does hold after modding out by the ideal generated by these commutators). In §
5
, we show that similar issues cause problems for theta functions.

As in
[
CPS
,
GHK15
,
GHS
]
, the construction of theta functions in
[
GHKK18
]
is based on enumerating broken lines (an abridged version of tropical disks). This enumeration depends on the designated endpoint of the broken lines, but according to
[
CPS
, §4]
, different choices of endpoint are related by path-ordered product, essentially meaning that these choices glue to give well-defined global functions on the mirror.
[
Man
, Thm. 2.14]
gives a refined version of this lemma of Carl-Pumperla-Siebert, implying that the analogous gluing property holds for quantum theta functions, cf. Lemma
5.2
. Refining further,
[
Che16
,
Che
]
defines Hall algebra broken lines, and from these one might hope to define Hall algebra theta functions. Unfortunately, this is not a well-behaved notion:

Proposition 1.2

(Prop.
5.4
in the main text)
.

The Carl-Pumperla-Siebert Lemma does not hold for Hall algebra broken lines.

Our proof is via the explicit construction of a counterexample for an
A_{3}
-quiver, cf. §
5.3
.

1.3.
Motivation

When
B
has rank
2
, the tropical disk counts of Theorem
1.1
can be replaced with tropical curve counts, cf.
[
GPS10
, Thm. 2.8]
and
[
FS15
, Cor. 4.9]
. In higher-dimensions this is only the case for certain limits of choices of
{\theta}
, cf.
[
Man
, Thm 3.7]
. The tropical curve versions are nice because in the classical limit they can be related via
[
NS06
]
to log Gromov-Witten invariants, cf.
[
GPS10
, Prop. 5.3]
. As the authors have learned from Mark Gross, the classical versions of our tropical disk counts should also have an algebraic Gromov-Witten theoretic meaning: according to the announced result
[
GS18
, Thm. 2.14]
, they should be related to certain
punctured
Gromov-Witten invariants (one also expects the existence of correspoding holomorphic disk counts defined from the perspective of open Gromov-Witten theory, e.g., as in
[
Lin
]
for the case of K3 surfaces). One expects DT/GW correspondence results to follow from
[
GS18
, Thm. 2.14]
combined with
[
Bri17
, Lem. 11.4]
.

On the other hand, the quantum tropical curve counts in rank
2
are Block-Göttsche invariants
[
BG16
]
, which have been related to higher-genus Gromov-Witten invariants in
[
Bou19
,
Bou
]
and to real curve counts in
[
Mik17
]
. Upcoming work of the second author will extend the correspondence of
[
Mik17
]
to higher-dimensions, although an extension to tropical disks is still more distant. Still, we hope that Theorem
1.1
will lead to new refined DT/GW correspondence results, and we further hope that this correspondence will be enriched by our interpretation of tropical ribbon multiplicities in terms of moduli of composition series.

A version of Theorem
1.1
for bipartite quivers was previously observed in
[
FS15
, Thm. 5.3]
. Their argument was based on the observation that in these cases, (
1
) is equivalent to a representation-theoretic formula of Manschot-Pioline-Sen
[
MPS11
]
. We therefore hope that our result may be related to some generalization of this MPS formula. With this in mind, we strongly suspect that our tropical counts are closely related to the attractor flow trees studied by physicists, cf.
[
AP19
]
in particular, as well as
[
KS14
]
.

We note that
[
LMY19
]
, which appeared immediately after this paper was first posted, deals with similar problems on scattering diagrams and tropical disks using a differential-geometric perspective.

Lemma 2.3

(

Now, for any representation

By Lemma

2.5.
The quantum torus algebra

Let
\mathbb{C}_{t}[N^{\oplus}]
denote the
quantum torus algebra
, by which we mean the
N^{\oplus}
-graded algebra defined by:

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{t}[N^{\oplus}]\coloneqq\mathbb{C}_{\reg}(t)[z^{n}:n\in N^{\oplus}]/\langle z^{n_{1}}z^{n_{2}}=t^{B(n_{1},n_{2})}z^{n_{1}+n_{2}}:n_{1},n_{2}\in N^{\oplus}\rangle

(the monomials
z^{n}
adjoined here are non-commuting). This forms a
Poisson algebra
under the bracket

(14)

\displaystyle\{a,b\}\coloneqq\frac{[a,b]}{t-t^{-1}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\{z^{n_{1}},z^{n_{2}}\}=[B(n_{1},n_{2})]_{t}z^{n_{1}+n_{2}},

where for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(15)

\displaystyle[a]_{t}\coloneqq\frac{t^{a}-t^{-a}}{t-t^{-1}}=\sign(a)(t^{-|a|+1}+t^{-|a|+3}+\ldots+t^{|a|-3}+t^{|a|-1}).

The usual commutative algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[N^{\oplus}]
also forms a Poisson algebra, with bracket defined by

(16)

\displaystyle\{z^{n_{1}},z^{n_{2}}\}\coloneqq B(n_{1},n_{2})z^{n_{1}+n_{2}}.

Note that there is a surjective homomorphism of Poisson algebras defined by

\displaystyle\pi_{t\mapsto 1}:\mathbb{C}_{t}[N^{\oplus}]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[N^{\oplus}],\quad t\mapsto 1,~z^{n}\mapsto z^{n}.

Remark 2.6
.

Note that
\mathbb{C}_{t}[N^{\oplus}]
viewed as a Lie algebra with its Poisson bracket is isomorphic as a Lie algebra to
(t-t^{-1})^{-1}\cdot\mathbb{C}_{t}[N^{\oplus}]
with its commutator bracket via the map

x\mapsto\frac{x}{t-t^{-1}}.

We may thus view
\pi_{t\mapsto 1}
as a Lie algebra homomorphism
(t-t^{-1})^{-1}\cdot\mathbb{C}_{t}[N^{\oplus}]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[N^{\oplus}]
.
Similarly, as noted in
[
Bri17
, §5.9]
,
H_{\reg}(Q,W)
with the bracket from (
9
) is isomorphic as a Lie algebra to
(t-t^{-1})^{-1}\cdot H_{\reg}(Q,W)
with its commutator bracket. In §
2.6
, we will discuss the “integration map”
\mathcal{I}=\pi_{t\mapsto 1}\circ\mathcal{I}_{t}
as a homomorphism of Poisson algebras
H_{\reg}(Q,W)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[N^{\oplus}]
, but this can also be viewed as a homomorphism of Lie algebras
(t-t^{-1})^{-1}\cdot H_{\reg}(Q,W)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[N^{\oplus}]
. Similarly, we may view the quantum integration map
\mathcal{I}_{t}:H_{\reg}(Q,W)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{t}[N^{\oplus}]
as a Lie algebra homomorphism
(t-t^{-1})^{-1}\cdot H_{\reg}(Q,W)\rightarrow(t-t^{-1})^{-1}\mathbb{C}_{t}[N^{\oplus}]
.

In place of the quantum torus algebra
{\mathbb{C}}_{t}[N^{\oplus}]
considered above, one may use the quantum tropical vertex group of
[
KS11
, §6.1]
or the quantum torus Lie algebra of
[
DMa
, §2.2.3]
. These alternatives are nice because they still admit well-defined Poisson algebra maps
\pi_{t\mapsto 1}
to
{\mathbb{C}}[N^{\oplus}]
, but now the Poisson bracket for the domain is simply the commutator bracket. While this is often convenient, we shall not use this viewpoint here.

2.6.
The integration map

There are several constructions of
(quantum) integration maps
in the literature, i.e., homomorphisms (of algebras, Lie algebras, or Poisson algebras) from
H(Q,W)
or
H_{\reg}(Q,W)
to the (quantum) torus algebra. Reineke
[
Rei03
, Lem. 6.1]
first constructed the analog of such a quantum integration map for finitary Hall algebras associated to quivers without potential. Joyce
[
Joy07
, §6]
then constructed classical and quantum integration maps with domain
H_{\reg}(Q,0)
. The classical version of Joyce’s map (of Lie algebras) was generalized to quivers with potential in
[
JS12
, §7]
(cf.
[
Bri17
, Thm. 11.1]
for an interpretation as a map of Poisson algebras). On the other hand, a very general construction of algebra homomorphisms from a full Hall algebra to the “motivic quantum torus algebra” (which can then be further integrated to the usual quantum torus algebra) has been outlined by Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
KS
, §6]
. Making this more precise and more algebraic, in
[
KS11
, §7]
, Kontsevich and Soibelman defined a (monodromic) mixed Hodge structure (building off Saito’s theory of mixed Hodge modules
[
Sai90
]
) on the equivariant cohomology of the vanishing cycle complex, and then
[
DMb
]
and
[
Dav18
]
built on these ideas to rigorously define a quantum integration map
\mathcal{I}_{t}
.

We give a brief sketch of this
integration map

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{t}:H_{\reg}(Q,W)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{t}[N^{\oplus}]

essentially as in
[
Dav18
, §3.3]
. We then use this to compute the integration in the simplest cases. We note that by the definitions of the Poisson structures in (
9
) and (
14
), it is clear that
\mathcal{I}_{t}
being a map of algebras implies it is also a map of Poisson algebras, thus also giving maps of Lie algebras as in Remark
2.6
.

Recall that
\mathcal{M}
is the moduli stack of objects in
\operatorname{rep}(Q,W)\coloneqq\bmod{\mathbb{C}}Q/I_{W}
. Let
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
be the moduli stack of objects in
\operatorname{rep}(Q,0)
. Given an arrow
a\in Q_{1}
, let
t(a),h(a)\in Q_{0}
denote the tail and head of
a
respectively. For any
i\in Q_{0}
and
d\in N^{\oplus}
, let
d_{i}
denote the corresponding component of
d
. Denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{d}^{\circ}\coloneqq\prod_{a\in Q_{1}}\Hom_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathbb{C}}^{d_{t(a)}},{\mathbb{C}}^{d_{h(a)}}),

and

\displaystyle\GL_{d}\coloneqq\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\GL_{d_{i}}(\mathbb{C}).

Then
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}=\bigsqcup_{d\in N^{\oplus}}\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\circ}
where
\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\circ}
is the stack-theoretic quotient

(17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\circ}=\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{d}^{\circ}/\GL_{d},

where the action by
\GL_{d}
is the one induced by the conjugation action of
\GL_{d_{i}}(\mathbb{C})
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{d_{i}}
for each
i\in Q_{0}
.

Viewing elements of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{d}^{\circ}
as modules over the path-algebra
{\mathbb{C}}Q
, we see that multiplication by
W
gives an endomorphism of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{d}^{\circ}
. Since the trace is invariant under the action of
\GL_{d}
, we obtain a function

\Tr(W):\mathcal{M}^{\circ}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},

the critical locus of which recovers
\mathcal{M}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\operatorname{crit}(\Tr(W))\subset\mathcal{M}^{\circ}.

Let
Y
be a smooth complex variety and let
f:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a regular function on
Y
. The corresponding vanishing cycle functor
\varphi_{f}
is defined as follows (following
[
Dav18
, §3.1]
, also cf.
[
KS11
, §7.2]
). Let
Y_{0}\coloneqq f^{-1}(0)
, and let
Y_{\leq 0}\coloneqq f^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0})
. For a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
Y
and
U
an analytic open subset of
Y
, define

\displaystyle\Gamma_{X_{\leq 0}}\mathcal{F}(U)\coloneqq\ker\left(\mathcal{F}(U)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}(U\setminus(U\cap X_{\leq 0})\right).

Then
\varphi_{f}:=(R\Gamma_{X\leq 0}\mathcal{F})[1]|_{X_{0}}
.

The stacks
\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\circ}
for
d\in N^{\oplus}
are not quite smooth complex varieties, but each is a quotient of a smooth complex variety by the action of an algebraic group, cf. (
17
). One can thus extend the definition of
\varphi_{f}
to regular functions
f
on
\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\circ}
using an equivariant version of the vanishing cycle construction as in
[
DM20
, §2.2]
.

Let
\underline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{d}
denote the constant sheaf on
\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\circ}
. For each
u\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, we can define
\varphi_{\Tr(W)/u}\underline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{d}
. Now consider
[X\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{d}]\in H_{\reg}(Q,W)
. Composing with the inclusion
\mathcal{M}_{d}\subset\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\circ}
, we can consider the pullback

\displaystyle\varphi^{X}_{\Tr(W)/u}\underline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{d}\coloneqq(X\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\circ})^{*}\varphi_{\Tr(W)/u}\underline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{d}.

This sheaf
\varphi^{X}_{\Tr(W)/u}\underline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{d}
on
X
in fact has the structure of a mixed Hodge module on
X
, and so the compactly supported cohomology
H^{*}_{c}(X,\varphi^{X}_{\Tr(W)/u}\underline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{d})
has a cohomologically graded rational mixed Hodge structure. Recall here that a rational mixed Hodge structure is a finite-dimensional vector space
V
over
\mathbb{Q}
, plus the data of an ascending filtration
W_{*}
of
V
(the weight filtration) and a descending filtration
F^{*}
of
V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
(the Hodge filtration) such that the filtration induced by
F^{*}
on

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{n}(V):=W_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}/W_{n-1}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}

determines a pure Hodge structure of weight
n
. By a cohomologically graded rational mixed Hodge structure on
H^{*}_{c}(X,\varphi^{X}_{\Tr(W)/u}\underline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{d})
, we mean a rational mixed Hodge structure on
H^{i}_{c}(X,\varphi^{X}_{\Tr(W)/u}\underline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{d})
for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let us abbreviate
H^{*}_{c}(X,\varphi^{X}_{\Tr(W)/u}\underline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{d})
as simply
H^{*}_{d}
. Up to isomorphism, the sheaf
\varphi^{X}_{\Tr(W)/u}\underline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{d}
is independent of
u
. However, there may be non-trivial monodromy
\mu
on
H^{*}_{d}
as
u
travels around the origin in
\mathbb{C}
. This
\mu
is quasi-unipotent, i.e., the eigenvalues are roots of unity. Let
\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{n}(H^{i}_{d})_{1}
denote the generalized eigenspace for the possible eigenvalue
1
of
\mu
, and let
\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{n}(H^{i}_{d})_{\neq 1}
denote the direct sum of the generalized eigenspaces for all eigenvalues of
\mu
other than
1
. Finally, the quantum integration map
\mathcal{I}_{t}
is defined by taking the Serre polynomial (cf.
[
DL01
, §3.1.3]
, also
[
KS
, p. 69]
) defined as follows:

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{t}([X\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{d}])\coloneqq t^{\chi(d,d)}z^{d}\sum_{i,n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{i}

\displaystyle\left(\dim(\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{n}(H^{i}_{d})_{1})(-t)^{n}+\dim(\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{n}(H^{i}_{d})_{\neq 1})(-t)^{n+1}\right),

where
\chi
is defined as in (
3
).

The map of (
18
) above is essentially the same as that of
[
Dav18
, (18)]
, although the two look somewhat different. The
t^{\chi(d,d)}
-factor in our (
18
) is simply to account for the twisting of the monoidal structure in
[
Dav18
, (16)]
. The extra factor of
(-t)
on the

-term in our (

) is needed because
[
Dav18
]
actually works with the category of monodromic mixed Hodge modules, a difference which results in a shift for part of the weight filtration. See
[
Dav
, Prop. 2.5]
for details on the relationship between these two perspectives.
[
Dav18
, Prop. 3.13]
thus yields the following:

Proposition 2.7

(
[
Dav18
]
, Prop. 3.13)
.

\mathcal{I}_{t}:H_{\reg}(Q,W)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{t}[N^{\oplus}]
is a homomorphism of
{\mathbb{C}}_{\reg}(t)
-algebras.

This construction simplifies quite a bit for
[f:X\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{d}]
with
\Tr(W)|_{\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\circ}}=0
and
X
a smooth projective variety. In this case,
\varphi_{\Tr(W)}=\id
, and so
\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{n}(H^{i}_{d})
equals
H_{c}^{n}(X,\underline{{\mathbb{Q}}})
if

and vanishes otherwise. Recalling the definition of
\Upsilon
from (

, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{t}([f:X\rightarrow\mathcal{M}])=\Upsilon(X)t^{\chi(d,d)}z^{d}.

In particular, if

(e.g., for

equals the quantum integration map of

, §6]
.

Example 2.9
.

Recall
\kappa_{ki}\coloneqq[\Spec\mathbb{C}\rightarrow p_{S_{i}^{\oplus k}}=\mathcal{M}_{ke_{i}}]

,

(the Poincaré polynomial of a point), and

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{t}(\kappa_{i})=tz^{e_{i}}.

As a check, one can use (

with

, §7]
and
[

, Thm. 11.1]
are always (even for nonzero

) given by the

limit of (

and

to the composition algebra

in which all our computations will lie. Since

agrees with the classical integration maps of

, §7]
and

, Thm. 11.1]

.

3.
Scattering diagrams from Hall algebras

3.1.
Background on scattering diagrams

Here we review the basic definitions and properties of scattering diagrams from the perspective useful for understanding the Hall algebra scattering diagrams of
[
Bri17
]
.

Let
\Lambda
denote a finite-rank lattice equipped with a
\mathbb{Z}
-valued skew-symmetric form
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
. Let
\Lambda^{\vee}\coloneqq\Hom(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})
be the dual lattice, and let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\Lambda\oplus\Lambda^{\vee}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
denote the dual pairing. We have a map

\displaystyle p^{*}:\Lambda

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Lambda^{\vee}

(21)

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\mapsto\{\cdot,n\}.

Fix a strictly convex rational polyhedral cone
\sigma_{\Lambda^{\oplus}}\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\Lambda^{\oplus}\coloneqq\sigma_{\Lambda^{\oplus}}\cap\Lambda
, and let
\Lambda^{+}\coloneqq\Lambda^{\oplus}\setminus\{0\}
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{n\in\Lambda^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{n}
be a Lie algebra graded by
\Lambda^{+}
, meaning that
[\mathfrak{g}_{n_{1}},\mathfrak{g}_{n_{2}}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{n_{1}+n_{2}}
. We say that
\mathfrak{g}
is
skew-symmetric
with respect to
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
if

(22)

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{g}_{n_{1}},\mathfrak{g}_{n_{2}}]=0\mbox{~whenever~}\{n_{1},n_{2}\}=0.

For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, let

k\Lambda^{+}\coloneqq\{n_{1}+\ldots+n_{k}\in\Lambda^{+}|n_{i}\in\Lambda^{+}\mbox{ for each }i=1,\ldots,k\}.

Let
\mathfrak{g}^{\geq k}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{n\in k\Lambda^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{n}
. Note that
\mathfrak{g}^{\geq k}
is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{k}
denote the nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{\geq k}
, and let
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\coloneqq\varprojlim\mathfrak{g}_{k}
. We have corresponding Lie groups
G\coloneqq\exp\mathfrak{g}
,
G_{k}\coloneqq\exp\mathfrak{g}_{k}
, and
\widehat{G}\coloneqq\exp\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}=\varprojlim G_{k}
.

For each
n\in\Lambda^{+}
, we have a Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{n}^{\parallel}\coloneqq\prod_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}\mathfrak{g}_{kn}\subset\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
. We say that
\mathfrak{g}
has
Abelian walls
if each
\mathfrak{g}_{n}^{\parallel}
is Abelian. In particular,
\mathfrak{g}
has Abelian walls whenever
\mathfrak{g}
is skew-symmetric. Let
G_{n}^{\parallel}\coloneqq\exp(\mathfrak{g}_{n}^{\parallel})\subset\widehat{G}
.

The Abelian walls condition is usually assumed to hold when working with scattering diagrams, but when defining Hall algebra scattering diagrams, one needs a slight generalization as in
[
Bri17
, §2]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
wall
in
\Lambda^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
over
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
is data of the form
(\mathfrak{d},g_{\mathfrak{d}})
, where:

•

g_{\mathfrak{d}}\in\mathfrak{g}_{n_{\mathfrak{d}}}^{\parallel}
for some primitive

Theorem 3.5

Let

-graded Lie algebra, and let

We note that an earlier version of this paper claimed existence more generally, but we have since realized that proving the consistency of the scattering diagram

requires the Abelian walls condition, and so our argument was flawed. Fortunately, the existence of the Hall algebra scattering diagram is already given by

Proof.

As noted above, the only new statement is the uniqueness statement in the case of non-Abelian walls. We prove this using an argument inspired by that
[
GHKK18
, Lem. C.7]
. Let
\mathfrak{D},\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
be two consistent scattering diagrams over
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
with incoming walls
\mathfrak{D}_{\In}
as in the statement of the theorem. We shall prove by induction on
k
that
\mathfrak{D}^{k}
and
(\mathfrak{D}^{\prime})^{k}
are equivalent over
\mathfrak{g}_{k}
for each
k
, and then the equivalence of
\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
follows. Note that
\mathfrak{D}^{1}
and
(\mathfrak{D}^{\prime})^{1}
are both equivalent to the trivial scattering diagram, hence to each other.

Now suppose that
\mathfrak{D}^{k}
and
(\mathfrak{D}^{\prime})^{k}
are equivalent over
\mathfrak{g}_{k}
. Let
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime\prime}
be a scattering diagram over
\mathfrak{g}_{k+1}
such that

g_{x,\mathfrak{D}^{\prime\prime}}=g_{x,\mathfrak{D}^{k+1}}-g_{x,(\mathfrak{D}^{\prime})^{k+1}}

for each general

.
Since
\mathfrak{D}^{k}
and
(\mathfrak{D}^{\prime})^{k}
are equivalent over
\mathfrak{g}_{k}
, we must have

, hence

is central in
\mathfrak{g}_{k+1}
. Hence,
(\mathfrak{D}^{\prime})^{k}\cup\mathfrak{D}^{\prime\prime}
is a well-defined scattering diagram over
\mathfrak{g}_{k+1}
, and by Lemma
3.3
it is equivalent to
\mathfrak{D}^{k+1}
. Our goal now is to show that
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime\prime}
is equivalent to the trivial scattering diagram.

Since both
\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
were assumed to be consistent, and the scattering functions of

are all central in

,

must also be consistent (over

). Furthermore, this consistency plus centrality of the scattering functions implies that, up to equivalence, the support of every wall of

is an entire affine hyperplane in

. But then all walls of
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime\prime}
(up to equivalence) are incoming, and since the incoming walls of
\mathfrak{D}
and

are the same, this implies that

is equivalent to the trivial scattering diagram over

∎

Remark 3.13

4.1.
Tropical disks

We now introduce the tropical disks whose enumerations will be related to the scattering diagrams of §

Let

•

\displaystyle\Delta(s)=w(E_{s})u_{E_{s}}.

denote the set of flags

,

, of
\Gamma
. The

of a tropical disk is the data of
\Gamma
,
w
, and
\epsilon

. Note that the type of a tropical disk determines its degree.

Similarly, the

of a tropical ribbon is the data of the type of the associated tropical disk, plus the data of the ribbon structure, i.e., the data of the cyclic orderings at each vertex.

Let

be a tuple of affine-linear subspaces

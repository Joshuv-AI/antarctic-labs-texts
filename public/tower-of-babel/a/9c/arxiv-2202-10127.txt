On solutions of the Diophantine equation F n 1 + F n 2 + F n 3 + F n 4 = 2 a F_{n_{1}}+F_{n_{2}}+F_{n_{3}}+F_{n_{4}}=2^{a}
Pagdame TIEBEKABE & \& Ismaïla DIOUF

Abstract
Let ( F n ) n ≥ 0 (F_{n})_{n\geq 0} be the Fibonacci sequence given by F 0 = 0 , F 1 = 1 F_{0}=0,F_{1}=1 and F n + 2 = F n + 1 + F n F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_{n} for n ≥ 0 n\geq 0 . In this paper, we solve all powers of two which are sums of four Fibonacci numbers with a few exceptions that we characterize.



Keywords
: Linear forms in logarithm; Diophantine equations; Fibonacci sequence; Lucas sequence; perfect powers.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 11B39, 11J86, 11D61.

Auxiliary results

In this section, we give some important known definitions, proprieties, theorem and lemmas.

Definition 2.1
.

For all algebraic numbers
\gamma
, we define its measure by the following identity :

{\rm M}(\gamma)=|a_{d}|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}\max\{1,|\gamma_{i}|\},

where
\gamma_{i}
are the roots of
f(x)=a_{d}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}(x-\gamma_{i})
is the minimal polynomial of
\gamma
.

Let us define now another height, deduced from the last one, called the absolute logarithmic height.

Definition 2.2
.

( Absolute logarithmic height)

For a non-zero algebraic number of degree
d
on
\mathbb{Q}
where the minimal polynomial on
\mathbb{Z}
is
f(x)=a_{d}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}(x-\gamma_{i})
, we denote by

h(\gamma)=\dfrac{1}{d}\left(\log|a_{d}|+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\log\max\{1,|\gamma_{i}|\}\right)=\dfrac{1}{d}\log{\rm M}(\gamma).

the usual logarithmic absolute height of
\gamma
.

The following properties of the logarithmic height are well-known:

•

h(\gamma\pm\eta)\leq h(\gamma)+h(\eta)+\log 2
;

•

h(\gamma\eta^{\pm 1})\leq h(\gamma)+h(\eta)
;

•

h(\gamma^{k})=|k|h(\gamma)\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The
n
th Fibonacci number can be represented as

F_{n}=\dfrac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\sqrt{5}}\quad\text{for all}\quad n\geqslant 0.

where
(\alpha,\beta):=((1+\sqrt{5})/2,(1-\sqrt{5})/2)
. The following inequalities

\alpha^{n-2}\leqslant F_{n}\leqslant\alpha^{n-1}

are well-known to hold for all
n\geqslant 1
and can be proved by induction on
n
.
The following theorem is deduced from Corollary
2.3
of Matveev
[
15
]
.

.

(Matveev
[
15
]
)

Let
n\geq 1
an integer. Let
\mathbb{L}
be a field of algebraic number of degree

. Let
\eta_{1}
, …,
\eta_{l}
non-zero elements of
\mathbb{L}
and let

b_{1}
,
b_{2}

b_{l}
integers,

and

\Lambda:=\eta_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots\eta_{l}^{b_{l}}-1=\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}\eta_{i}^{b_{i}}\right)-1.

Let
A_{1}
, …,

reals numbers such that

Assume that

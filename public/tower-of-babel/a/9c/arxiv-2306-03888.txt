On Newton polytopes of Lagrangian augmentations
Orsola Capovilla-Searle, Roger Casals

Abstract
. This note explores the use of Newton polytopes in the study of Lagrangian fillings of Legendrian submanifolds. In particular, we show that Newton polytopes associated to augmented values of Reeb chords can distinguish infinitely many distinct Lagrangian fillings, both for Legendrian links and higher-dimensional Legendrian spheres. The computations we perform work in finite characteristic, which significantly simplifies arguments and also allows us to show that there exist Legendrian links with infinitely many non-orientable exact Lagrangian fillings.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 53D10. Secondary: 53D15, 57R17.

1.
Introduction

The object of this note is to start exploring Newton polytopes associated to augmentations of the Legendrian dg-algebra. We show that integer point counts for the Newton polytopes of augmented Reeb chords are an effective invariant that can distinguish infinitely many fillings. Two advantages are that computations with Newton polytopes are significantly simpler and more robust than previously employed methods, such as monomial counts, and that they can distinguish infinitely many augmentations even in finite characteristic. We also present explicit computations and applications, including the first construction of a Legendrian sphere in all dimensions with infinitely many Lagrangian fillings, and the first construction of Legendrian links with infinitely many non-orientable Lagrangian fillings; in both cases fillings are distinguished by studying Newton polytopes associated to the corresponding augmentations and working in characteristic two.

1.1.
Scientific context

Exact Lagrangian fillings of Legendrian submanifolds have been a central object of study in contact and symplectic topology
[
EES09
,
ST13
,
HS14
,
EHK16
,
Pan17
,
CG22
]
. Recently,
[
CG22
]
constructed Legendrian links in the contact Darboux 3-ball
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
with infinitely many orientable exact Lagrangian fillings, distinct up to compactly supported Hamiltonian isotopy. Further examples of such Legendrians links in
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
were found in
[
GSW20
,
CGG+22
,
CW23
,
CZ22
]
using methods from cluster algebras and the microlocal theory of sheaves.

The first, and currently only, Floer-theoretic method to distinguish such infinite families was introduced in
[
CN22
]
by L. Ng and the second author. The techniques developed in
[
CN22
]
, especially the effective use of the
E
-invariant in
[
CN22
, Section 6]
, require the Legendrian dg-algebra and the augmentations induced by Lagrangian fillings to be both constructed and computable over
\mathbb{Z}
. This involves a fair amount of technical details and it is desirable to have a simpler, more directly computable, Floer-theoretic way to distinguish the infinite families of augmentations coming from
\vartheta
-loops. The current note presents Newton polytopes as a versatile and effective alternative.

In
[
Pan17
]
, Y. Pan used the count of monomials of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})]
-augmented values of Reeb chords to distinguish a Catalan number of Lagrangian fillings
L\subset(\mathbb{D}^{4},\lambda_{st})
for the max-tb
(2,n)
torus links
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
. The same counts of such monomials for the Lagrangians associated to
\vartheta
-loops are more challenging and, even when computable, typically not able to show that the
\vartheta
-orbits are entire. Newton polytopes associated to multivariable Laurent polynomials are often more robust invariants than the precise count of monomials and contain a significant amount of geometric information, see e.g.
[
Ber75
,
Kou76
,
Var76
,
Hov78
,
MTY19
,
KKE21
]
. Through a variety of explicit computations and applications, this note will illustrate that Newton polytopes are useful in the study of the Legendrian contact dg-algebra.

1.2.
Main results

Let
(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R),\partial_{\Lambda})
denote the Legendrian contact dg-algebra of a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},\xi_{std})
over a commutative unital ground ring
R
, see
[
Che02
,
EES05
,
CN22
]
. An exact Lagrangian filling
L
of
\Lambda
induces a dg-algebra map
\varepsilon_{L}:(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R),\partial_{\Lambda})\rightarrow(R,0)
, referred to as the augmentation
\varepsilon_{L}
associated to
L
. Two Hamiltonian isotopic exact Lagrangian fillings induce dg-algebra homotopic augmentations, cf.
[
EHK16
,
Kar19
]
. See Section
2
for more details.

First, consider a Legendrian link
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
and the
\vartheta
-loops introduced by L. Ng and the second author in
[
CN22
]
. Denote by
\mathcal{A}(\vartheta)\in\mbox{Aut}(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R),\partial_{\Lambda})
the dg-algebra automorphism induced by a Legendrian
\vartheta
-loop based at
\Lambda
. By definition, the
\vartheta
-orbit of an augmentation
\varepsilon_{L}
induced by an exact Lagrangian filling
L
of
\Lambda
is said to be entire if
\varepsilon_{L}\circ\mathcal{A}(\vartheta)^{n}\not\simeq\varepsilon_{L}\circ\mathcal{A}(\vartheta)^{m}
for distinct
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. In such a case, the Lagrangian fillings of
\Lambda
obtained by concatenating
L
with a power of the Lagrangian trace of
\vartheta
are pairwise not compactly supported Hamiltonian isotopic.

Let us focus on two of the smallest Legendrian links for which entire
\vartheta
-orbits are detected over
\mathbb{Z}
, as established in
[
CN22
]
. Let
\Lambda(\beta_{11})
, resp.
\Lambda_{1}
, denote the Legendrian link whose
(-1)
-closure is the positive braid
(\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}\sigma_{2})^{4}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}
, resp.
(\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})^{3}\sigma_{1}
. See Figure
1
for a schematic of the Legendrian isotopy loop
\vartheta
of
\Lambda(\beta_{11})
that we consider. Our first result shows that, even over a ring of finite characteristic, integral point counts of Newton polytopes associated to the augmentations in the
\vartheta
-orbit pairwise distinguish them:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
be either
\Lambda(\beta_{11})
or
\Lambda_{1}
. Then there exists a
\vartheta
-loop, a Reeb chord
\rho\in\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
and an orientable exact Lagrangian filling
L
with augmentation

\varepsilon_{L}:(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;\mathbb{Z}_{2}),\partial_{\Lambda})\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})],0),

such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
the Newton polytopes in
\mathbb{R}^{b_{1}(L)}
of the augmented values
(\varepsilon_{L}\circ\mathcal{A}(\vartheta)^{n})(\rho)
are pairwise non-equivalent, even up to a
\operatorname{GL}(b_{1}(L),\mathbb{Z})
transformation, where
b_{1}(L)=\mbox{rk}(H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z}))
denotes the first Betti number of
L
.

In particular, the
\vartheta
-orbit of the augmentation

\varepsilon_{L}:(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;\mathbb{Z}_{2}),\partial_{\Lambda})\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})],0),

is entire, even over the characteristic 2 ring
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})]
.

Figure 1.
The Lagrangian projection of the Legendrian link
\Lambda(\beta_{11})
associated to the 4-stranded braid
(\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}\sigma_{2})^{4}\sigma_{3}\sigma_{1}
. The
\vartheta
-loop for this Legendrian link is obtained by moving the rightmost
\sigma_{1}
crossing around the braid, as indicated by the purple dashed arrow.

First, the Newton polytopes in Theorem
1.1
are distinguished by their integral point counts, which are invariant under linear integral transformations. These Newton polytope invariants are simpler to compute than the
E
-invariant used in
[
CN22
]
and they have the additional advantage of working over finite characteristic. Second, in these instances, monomial counts of the Laurent polynomials
(\varepsilon_{L}\circ\mathcal{A}(\vartheta)^{n})(\rho)
do not pairwise distinguish them; see Subsection
3.3
. In a sense, the Newton polytope of
(\varepsilon_{L}\circ\mathcal{A}(\vartheta)^{n})(\rho)
seems to be a more robust quantity than its monomial count.

It follows from Theorem
1.1
that
\Lambda(\beta_{11})
and
\Lambda_{1}
have infinitely many distinct exact Lagrangian fillings distinguished by augmentations over characteristic
2
. These links were first studied in
[
CN22
]
and are currently some of the smallest Legendrian links known to have infinitely many exact Lagrangian fillings in terms of genus and number of link components. Theorem
1.1
can then be used to prove that many more Legendrian links have infinitely many Lagrangian fillings that are also distinguished over finite characteristic:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
be a Legendrian link with no Reeb chords of degree -1. Suppose that there exists an exact decomposable Lagrangian cobordism from either
\Lambda(\beta_{11})
or
\Lambda_{1}
to
\Lambda
. Then
\Lambda
has infinitely many exact Lagrangian fillings distinguished by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})]
-augmentations.

A more general, if technical, version of Theorem
1.2
can be found in Section
3.4
. In a nutshell, Newton polytopes are used in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
in the following manner. Consider an integral lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
, whose points we interpret as monomials in the variables
s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}
, i.e. the lattice is the character lattice of the torus associated to the Laurent polynomial ring in the variables
s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}
. Consider a Laurent polynomial
f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}c_{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}]
in this ring; note that, by definition,
c_{\alpha}\neq 0
only for finitely many indices
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. By definition, its Newton polytope
N(f)
is the lattice polytope in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
given by the convex hull of the exponent vectors in
f
, i.e.

N(f):=\mbox{Conv}\{\alpha~|~c_{\alpha}\neq 0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}.

For any two embedded exact Lagrangian fillings
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
of a Legendrian link
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
with equal first betti number
k
, and for any Reeb chord
a
of
\Lambda
, the induced augmentations
\varepsilon_{L_{1}}(a)
and
\varepsilon_{L_{2}}(a)
are Laurent polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})]\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}[s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}]
. The group of unital
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}]
-automorphisms is isomorphic to
\operatorname{GL}(k,\mathbb{Z})
. Therefore, it suffices to find
\operatorname{GL}(k;\mathbb{Z})
invariants of Laurent polynomials to distinguish
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})]
-valued augmentations
\varepsilon_{L}
and thus their corresponding exact Lagrangian fillings. The
\operatorname{GL}(k;\mathbb{Z})
-invariant that we use is the number of lattice points of the associated Newton polytopes.

Computing augmentations with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L,;\mathbb{Z})]
or
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L,\Lambda;\mathbb{Z})]
coefficients, as opposed to computing them with
\mathbb{Z}[H_{1}(L,\Lambda;\mathbb{Z})]
or
\mathbb{Z}[H_{1}(L,\Lambda;\mathbb{Z})]
, is simpler. It also has the advantage that we can then distinguish non-orientable exact Lagrangian fillings, which have been less studied than their orientable counterparts. See
[
CCPR+22
]
for some results on the existence of non-orientable exact Lagrangian fillings. Section
4.2
uses lattice invariants of Newton polytopes to distinguish sets of non-orientable exact Lagrangian fillings. In particular, we prove the following result:

Theorem 1.3
.

There exist Legendrian knots
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
with infinitely many distinct non-orientable exact Lagrangian fillings.

In higher dimensions, front spinning previous examples of the second author, in
[
CG22
]
or
[
CN22
]
, produces higher-dimensional Legendrian submanifolds
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},\xi_{st})
with infinitely many Lagrangian fillings, see
[
Gol22
, Theorem 1.1]
. Nevertheless, these Legendrians are never spheres. Two disadvantages of this are that, first, they are not topologically as simple as possible and, second, it is not possible to attach a Weinstein handle (resp. perform contact surgery) along them to produce interesting symplectic manifolds (resp. contact manifolds). It is therefore desirable to construct Legendrian spheres
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},\xi_{st})
in all dimensions with infinitely many Lagrangian fillings. We achieve this in Section
3.5
, also using Newton polytopes, where we prove the following result:

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
n\geq 2
, there exist Legendrian spheres
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},\xi_{std})

with infinitely many exact Lagrangian fillings, distinct up to compactly supported Hamiltonian isotopy. In addition, these fillings are distinguished by augmentations over rings of characteristic two.

Following
[
CG22
, Corollary 1.10]
, attaching Weinstein
n
-handles along these Legendrian spheres produces
(2n+2)
-dimensional Weinstein manifolds which are homotopic to
S^{n+1}
and have infinitely many closed embedded exact Lagrangian submanifolds, distinct up to Hamiltonian isotopy in the Weinstein manifold but smoothly isotopic.

Finally, a natural question is which lattice polytopes can occur as the Newton polytopes we consider. That is, which lattice polytopes are Newton polytopes of
\varepsilon_{L}(\rho)
, where
L
is an embedded exact Lagrangian filling of a Legendrian
\Lambda
and
\rho
a Reeb chord in
\Lambda
. A priori, it is not even clear if and when top-dimensional polytopes, i.e. of dimension equal to
b_{1}
of a Lagrangian filling, and which ones, appear as Newton polytopes of augmented Reeb values. As a starting step, we show in Proposition
4.1
that the max-tb Legendrian knots
\Lambda(2,n)\subset(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
, presented as the rainbow closure of
\sigma_{1}^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
odd, are such that for any degree zero Reeb chord
\rho
of
\Lambda(2,n)
there exist an augmentation
\varepsilon_{L}
– induced by a Lagrangian filling
L
– such that the Newton polytope
N(\varepsilon_{L}(\rho))
is the top-dimensional standard
(n-1)
-simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.

2.
Preliminaries on the Legendrian dg-algebra

In this section we present the necessary preliminaries on the Legendrian contact dg-algebra, in line with
[
Che02

EHK16
,
CN22
]
.

2.1.
Legendrian links and Lagrangian fillings

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},\xi_{std})
denote the standard contact Darboux ball, with
\xi_{std}=\ker\{dz-\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}dx_{i}\}
. By definition, a Legendrian submanifold is a smooth
n
-dimensional submanifold such that
T_{p}\Lambda\subset\xi_{p}
for all
p\in\Lambda
. See
[
Arn90
]
for more background on contact structures and their Legendrian submanifolds.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Lambda_{+},\Lambda_{-}\subset(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},\xi_{std})
be Legendrian submanifolds.
An exact Lagrangian cobordism
L
from
\Lambda_{-}
to
\Lambda_{+}
is an embedded Lagrangian
(n+1)
-dimensional submanifold in the symplectization
(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},d(e^{t}(dz-\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}dx_{i})))

that has cylindrical ends and is exact in the following sense:
for some
N>0
,

(1)

L\cap([-N,N]\times Y)
is compact,

(2)

L\cap((N,\infty)\times Y)=(N,\infty)\times\Lambda_{+}
and
L\cap((-\infty,-N)\times Y)=(-\infty,-N)\times\Lambda_{-}
,

(3)

there exists a function
f:L\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and constants
\mathfrak{c}_{\pm}
such that

e^{t}\alpha|_{L}=df
, where
f|_{(-\infty,-N)\times\Lambda_{-}}=\mathfrak{c}_{-}
, and
f|_{(N,\infty)\times\Lambda_{+}}=\mathfrak{c}_{+}
.

By definition, an exact Lagrangian filling
L
of a Legendrian link
\Lambda
is an exact Lagrangian cobordism from the empty set
\emptyset
to the Legendrian link
\Lambda
.
\Box

Exact Lagrangian fillings can be studied via Legendrian invariants that are functorial over exact Lagrangian cobordisms, such as the Legendrian differential graded algebra
(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R),\partial_{\Lambda})
, see
[
Che02
]
for
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
and
[
EES07
]
for generalizations; see
[
EN22
]
for a survey. The underlying algebra
\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R)
is a unital graded algebra over a unital coefficient ring
R
that is generated, freely commutatively, by the set of Reeb chords of
\Lambda
. A Reeb chord of
\Lambda
is a trajectory of the Reeb vector field, in this case
\partial_{z}
, that begins and ends on
\Lambda
. The grading on
\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R)
is defined on the Reeb chord generators via a Conley-Zehnder index, and is defined on a word as the sum of the gradings of the letters in the word. The differential
\partial_{\Lambda}
counts rigid
\mathcal{J}
-holomorphic disks in the symplectization with boundary on
\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda
. See
[
Che02
,
EN22
]
for further details.

For a Legendrian link
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
, the Legendrian dg-algebra
(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R),\partial_{\Lambda})
can be computed combinatorially from a generic Lagrangian projection of
\Lambda
, where the Lagrangian projection is given by
\Pi_{xy}(x,y,z)=(x,y)
. The other useful projection is the front projection, given by
\Pi_{xz}(x,y,z)=(x,z)
; the translation from a front projection of a Legendrian link to a Lagrangian projection can performed via
[
Ng03
]
.
The Reeb chords of
\Lambda
correspond to crossings in the Lagrangian projection, and the rigid
\mathcal{J}
-holomorphic disks that the differential counts are immersed punctured disks
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\partial(\Delta)\subset\Pi_{xy}(\Lambda)
where the punctures map to convex corners of the crossings; see
[
EN22
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
divide
2rot(\Lambda)
, where
rot(\Lambda)
denotes the rotation number of
\Lambda
. An
\ell
-graded augmentation is a dg-algebra map
\varepsilon:(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R),\partial_{\Lambda})\rightarrow(R,0)
such that
\varepsilon
maps all elements of degree not divisible by
\ell
to
0
. If
\ell=1
then
\varepsilon
is said to be ungraded, and if
\ell=0
then
\varepsilon
is said to be graded.
\Box

In this note we only use
\ell
-graded augmentations with
\ell=0,1
.

Exact Lagrangian cobordisms
L
between Legendrians
\Lambda_{\pm}\subset(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
induce dga (dg-algebra) maps

\Phi_{L}:(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda_{+};\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})]),\partial_{\Lambda_{+}})\rightarrow(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda_{-};\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})]),\partial_{\Lambda_{-}}).

If
L
is an exact Lagrangian filling of a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}_{std}
with Maslov number
\ell
, then
L
induces an
\ell
-graded augmentation

\varepsilon_{L}:(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})]),\partial_{\Lambda})\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})],0),

where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})]
is in grading
0
modulo
\ell
and it has trivial differential as a dg-algebra. This was first shown for
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
coefficients in
[
EHK16
, Theorem 1.2]
, and later upgraded to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})]

[

]
. Moreover, Karlsson observed in
[

that this result can be extended to arbitrary exact Lagrangian cobordisms of Legendrians in the higher-dimensional Darboux balls. Adding in the hypothesis that

extended the dga maps to
\mathbb{Z}[H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})]
-coefficients. In line with
[
CN22

Definition 2.3
.

A
k
-system of
\ell
-graded augmentations of a Legendrian link
\Lambda
is a unital dg-algebra map

\varepsilon:(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R),\partial_{\Lambda})\rightarrow(R[s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}],0),

so that
\varepsilon\circ\partial_{\Lambda}=0
and
\varepsilon(1)=1
, and such that
\varepsilon
maps all elements of degree not divisible by
\ell
to
0
. Two
k
-systems of
\ell
-graded augmentations

\varepsilon:\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R)\rightarrow R[s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}]~\text{and}~\varepsilon^{\prime}:\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R)\rightarrow R[s_{1}^{\prime\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\prime\pm 1}]

are equivalent if there exists an
R
-linear automorphism

\phi:R[s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}]\rightarrow R[s_{1}^{\prime\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\prime\pm 1}]

such that
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\phi\circ\varepsilon
.
\Box

We will need the group of automorphisms of the
R
-algebra of Laurent polynomials for
R
a unital domain. The units of
R[\mathbb{Z}^{k}]
are well-understood, e.g. see
[
Neh09
, Theorem 3.(b)]
. Since any automorphism of
R[\mathbb{Z}^{k}]
must send the generator of each

-summand to a unit, the automorphism group of

is as follows.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
R
be a unital integral domain and
R^{*}
its group of units. The group of unital
R
-linear automorphisms of
R[s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}]
is isomorphic to the semidirect product
(R^{*})^{k}\rtimes_{\psi}\operatorname{GL}(k;\mathbb{Z})
, where

\psi:\operatorname{GL}(k;\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow Aut((R^{*})^{k}),\quad\psi_{A}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})=(\alpha_{1}^{a_{11}},\ldots,\alpha_{k}^{a_{kk}}),\quad A=(a_{ij})\in\operatorname{GL}(k,\mathbb{Z}).

Remark 2.5
.

Therefore, the group of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-linear automorphisms of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}]
is
\operatorname{GL}(k;\mathbb{Z})
. For

, the group of
R
-linear automorphisms of

. In this note

suffices for our computations, but they could be upgraded to

\Lambda

3.
Newton polytopes distinguishing Lagrangian fillings

In this section we show that Newton polytopes can be used to distinguish augmentations induced by exact Lagrangian fillings of Legendrian links, in particular proving Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.4
. Section
3.1
provides the necessary background on Newton polytopes. In Section
3.2
we collect the lemmas on powers of matrices with Laurent polynomial coefficients that will be used to compute Newton polytopes of augmentations in Section
3.3
. In Section
3.3
we prove Theorem
1.1
. In Section
3.4
we consider Legendrians related by exact Lagrangian cobordisms and prove Theorem
1.2
. Finally, in Section
3.5
we study constructions of higher dimensional Legendrians and prove Theorem
1.4
. Throughout this section
R
is a base (unital) domain, which we take to be
R=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for all of our applications.

3.1.
Background on Newton polytopes.

Let
\mathcal{H}=B(\mathbb{Z}^{k})=\{Bx~|~x\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}\}
be an integral lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, where
B
is a basis of the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. The collection
lin\mathcal{H}=\{Be_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
, where
e_{i}
are the standard vectors of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, are said to be the linear spanning vectors of
\mathcal{H}
. Given two integral lattices
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, a linear map
\phi:lin\mathcal{H}\rightarrow lin\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
that induces a bijection from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is said to be unimodular, a.k.a. a lattice transformation. The group of lattice transformations for lattices of rank
k
is the general linear group
\operatorname{GL}(k;\mathbb{Z})
.

Definition 3.1
.

Consider an integral lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
whose points we interpret as monomials in the variables
s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}
; we denote this by
\mathbb{Z}^{k}\simeq\langle s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}\rangle
. For any Laurent polynomial
f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}c_{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\in R[s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}]
, its Newton polytope
N(f)
is the lattice polytope in
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
given by the convex hull of the exponent vectors in
f
, i.e.

N(f):=\mbox{Conv}\{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}|~c_{\alpha}\neq 0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}.

Note that, by definition of a Laurent polynomial, only finitely many indices
\alpha~\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
have non-zero coefficient
c_{\alpha}\neq 0
.

\Box

Consider a
k
-system of augmentations
\varepsilon:(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R),\partial_{\Lambda})\rightarrow(R[s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}],0)
and an element
a\in(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R),\partial_{\Lambda})
. The evaluation
\varepsilon(a)\in R[s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}]
is a Laurent polynomial. Let
N(\varepsilon,a)\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
denote the Newton polytope of this Laurent polynomial
\varepsilon(a)
. Note that if
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{L}
is induced by an exact Lagrangian filling
L
of
\Lambda
, the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{k}=\langle s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s_{k}^{\pm 1}\rangle\cong H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})
is isomorphic to
H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})
, which is free of rank
k
, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be integral lattices. Two integral lattice polytopes
N\subset\mathcal{H}
and
N^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
are said to be unimodular equivalent if there exists a lattice transformation
\phi\in\operatorname{GL}(k,\mathbb{Z})
such that
\phi(N\cap\mathcal{H})=N^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, mapping the vertices of
N
to those of
N^{\prime}
.
\Box

Definition 3.3
.

An element
a\in(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R),\partial_{\Lambda})
is said to be Newton infinite (over
R
) if the collection of integral lattice polytopes
N(\varepsilon,a)
, where
\varepsilon:\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;R)\rightarrow R[H_{1}(L,\mathbb{Z})]
is induced by a Lagrangian filling
L
of
\Lambda
, is infinite up to unimodular equivalence.
\Box

Remark 3.4
.

By Lemma
2.6
, if an element
a
of
(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda;\mathbb{Z}_{2}),\partial_{\Lambda})
is Newton infinite and it is a cycle, i.e.
\partial_{\Lambda}a=0
, then the Legendrian link
\Lambda
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[\mathcal{H}]
aug-infinite for a lattice
\mathcal{H}\simeq H_{1}(L,\mathbb{Z})
, for some Lagrangian filling
L
of
\Lambda
.
\Box

Remark 3.5
.

Suppose that
\Lambda
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[\mathcal{H}]
aug-infinite and has no
(-1)
degree Reeb chords, so every
0
graded Reeb chord is a cycle in the dga of
\Lambda
. Let
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a collection of
k
-systems of augmentations, induced by exact Lagrangian fillings of
\Lambda
, witnessing the aug-infinite property. Then, for every pair
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a Reeb chord
x_{ij}
of
\Lambda
such that
N(\varepsilon_{i},x_{ij})
and
N(\varepsilon_{j},x_{ij})
are not unimodular equivalent. In addition, since
\Lambda
has finitely many Reeb chords, there exists a subsequence of augmentations
\varepsilon_{i_{l}}
such that for a single Reeb chord
x
of
\Lambda
the polytopes
N(\varepsilon_{i_{l}},x)
and
N(\varepsilon_{i_{l^{\prime}}},x)
are not unimodular equivalent for
l\neq l^{\prime}
. Thus, there is a Reeb chord
x
that is Newton infinite.
\Box

The invariants of unimodular equivalence that we use are as follows. The following lemma is standard, e.g. proven in
[
HNP20
, Corollary 2.75]
:

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be an integral lattice and
N\subset\mathcal{H}
an integral polytope. The
number of interior lattice points
\mbox{int}(N)\cap\mathcal{H}
, the number of boundary lattice points
\partial N\cap\mathcal{H}
, and the volume
\mbox{vol}(N)
are invariant under unimodular equivalence.

3.2.
Linear algebra lemmata.

We use the following lemmata to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
R
be a commutative unital ring. Consider

M_{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{i}&b_{i}\\
c_{i}&d_{i}\end{array}\right)\in M_{2}(R)
matrices over a commutative ring
R
,
1\leq i\leq r
,
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Fix a unit
u\in R
and define new matrices
M^{\prime}_{i}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{i}&b_{i}u\\
c_{i}u^{-1}&d_{i}\end{array}\right)
.

If the product
M_{1}\cdots M_{r}
is
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)
, then the product
M_{1}^{\prime}\cdots M_{r}^{\prime}
is
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&bu\\
cu^{-1}&d\end{array}\right)
.

In particular, the diagonal entries of these two products agree.

Proof.

Note that
M_{i}^{\prime}
is conjugate to
M_{i}
via

M^{\prime}_{i}=U^{-1}M_{i}U,\qquad\mbox{where }U:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&u\end{array}\right).

Therefore
M_{1}^{\prime}\cdots M_{r}^{\prime}=U^{-1}\cdot M_{1}\cdots M_{r}\cdot U
as required.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

For
n\geq 2
, the upper left entry of the
n^{th}
power of the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{x}+\tilde{y}&1\\
\tilde{x}&1\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}[\tilde{x},\tilde{y}])

is a polynomial
\alpha^{n}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[\tilde{x},\tilde{y}]
containing terms
\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{n},
and
\tilde{y}^{n}
. In addition, the Newton polytope in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
associated to
\alpha^{n}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
, when understood as a Laurent polynomial, is the convex hull of these three monomials.

Proof.

Let us denote the matrix in the statement by
M
. The entries of
M^{n}
are polynomials of degree (at most)
n
in the entries of
M
and therefore are themselves polynomials of degree at most
n
in
\tilde{x},\tilde{y}
. Let us simplify notation and write
x,y
for
\tilde{x},\tilde{y}
.

First, let us argue that the upper left entry of
M^{n}
contains only one monomial of the form
y^{k}
,
0\leq k\leq n
, and it is precisely
y^{n}
, i.e.
k=n
and with coefficient equal to one, even over
\mathbb{Z}
. Indeed, monomials of this form
y^{k}
are the monomials remaining when we set
x=0
in
M^{n}
. Therefore, they are the monomials in the upper left entry of the
n
th power of

M|_{x=0}=\begin{pmatrix}y&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\mbox{ which is }(M|_{x=0})^{n}=\begin{pmatrix}y^{n}&1\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Second, let us argue that the monomials of the form
x^{k}
,
1\leq k\leq n
in the upper left entry of
M^{n}
always contain
x
and
x^{n}
, i.e. the cases
k=1,n
and both with coefficient equal to one. Again, monomials of this form
x^{k}
are the monomials remaining when we set
y=0
in
M^{n}
. Hence, they are the monomials appearing in the upper left entry of the
n
th power of

M|_{y=0}=\begin{pmatrix}x&1\\
x&1\end{pmatrix},\mbox{ which is }(M|_{x=0})^{n}=(1+x)^{n-1}\begin{pmatrix}x&1\\
x&1\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Since the upper left entry is
x(1+x)^{n-1}
, it always contains the monomials
x
and
x^{n}
. This proves the first statement: the polynomial
\alpha^{n}(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x,y]
contains always terms
x,x^{n}
and
y^{n}
with coefficient equal to one.

Finally, we note that the Newton polytope of
\alpha^{n}(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x,y]
is in fact the convex hull of (the points in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
associated to)
x,x^{n}
and
y^{n}
. Indeed, we have proven that
\alpha^{n}(x,y)
contains
x,x^{n}
and
y^{n}
and
does not
contain any term of the form
c\cdot y^{k}
, where
0\leq k\leq n-1
and
c
is a constant. In addition, all others monomials of
\alpha^{n}(x,y)
are of degree at most
n
, and not powers of
y
, and thus lie in the convex hull of
x,x^{n}
and
y^{n}
.
∎

Lemma
3.8
and its proof illustrate an advantage of using Newton polytopes: there is no need to compute the entire Laurent polynomial entries, it suffices to keep track of monomials which generate the convex hull. In the particular matrix above, the number of Laurent monomials per coefficient varies as a function of the power
n
but it suffices to focus only on the 3 monomials
\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{n},\tilde{y}^{n}
for each
n
.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
i:\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\langle x,y\rangle}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an embedding of the form
i(x,y)=(x,y,\iota(x,y))
, where
\iota(x,y):\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d-2}
is a linear map,
d\geq 2
.
Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
be two distinct numbers. Then there does not exist a change of coordinates
\phi\in\operatorname{GL}(d,\mathbb{Z})
such that

\phi^{*}(i(\alpha^{n}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})))=i(\alpha^{m}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.8
, the Newton polytope of
\alpha^{n}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
is unimodular equivalent to the convex hull of the points
(1,0),(n,0),(0,n)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\langle\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\rangle}
. Such a lattice polytope has
2n
boundary lattice points and area
\frac{n}{2}(n-1)
. Pick’s theorem implies that the number of interior lattice points is
\frac{1}{2}(n^{2}-3n)+1
. By Lemma
3.6
, the number of lattice points of a polytope in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a
\operatorname{GL}(d,\mathbb{Z})
lattice invariant and thus
i(N(\alpha^{n}))
is not unimodular equivalent to
i(N(\alpha^{m}))
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if
n\neq m
.
∎

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
\Lambda_{\pm}\subset(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
be two Legendrian links such that there exists a decomposable Lagrangian cobordism
L
from
\Lambda_{-}
to
\Lambda_{+}
, both in the symplectization of
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
. Suppose that
\Lambda_{\pm}
have no
(-1)
-degree Reeb chords. In addition, suppose that there exists a Newton infinite element
\rho_{-}\in\mathcal{A}(\Lambda_{-})
such that there is a collection of Newton polytopes
N(\varepsilon,a)
witnessing the Newton infinite property each with a different number of lattice points.

Then
\Lambda_{+}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{Z}]
aug-infinite, where
\mathcal{H}\simeq H_{1}(L_{-},\mathbb{Z})
for some Lagrangian filling
L_{-}
of
\Lambda_{-}
.

Proof.

It suffices to consider a single pinch move; then we have an isomorphism
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L_{+};\mathbb{Z})]\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L_{-};\mathbb{Z}),s^{\pm 1}]
, where
s
is the variable keeping track of the unstable arc of the unique saddle point of
L
. By
[
EHK16
]
, the dga map induced by a pinch move at a contractible (or proper) Reeb chord
c
of
\Lambda_{+}
satisfies
\Phi_{L}(c)=s
. In addition, for any Reeb chord
a\neq c
of
\Lambda_{+}
, we have

\Phi_{L}(a)=a+w(a)s^{-1}

where
w(a)
is either zero or some word of Reeb chords of
\Lambda_{+}
not containing
a
or
c
.

Let
\Phi^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{L}:(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda_{+};\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L_{-};\mathbb{Z})]),\partial_{\Lambda_{+}})\rightarrow(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda_{-};\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L_{-};\mathbb{Z})]),\partial_{\Lambda_{-}})
denote the dga map
\Phi_{L}
where
s=1
. Observe that
\Phi^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{L}
respects the height filtration, and so the Reeb chords in
w(a)
all have height less than
a
. This implies that
\Phi^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{L}
is surjective. In particular, there exists
\rho_{+}\in\mathcal{A}(\Lambda_{+})
such that
\Phi^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{L}(\rho_{+})=\rho_{-}
. Let
L_{-}^{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, be an infinite family of distinct Lagrangian fillings of
\Lambda_{-}
such that the collection of Newton polytopes
N(\varepsilon_{L_{-}^{i}},\rho_{-})
is infinite, up to unimodular equivalence, and they can be distinguished by lattice point counts. This family of fillings exists by hypothesis and we can (and do) assume, after possibly considering a subcollection, that the count of lattice points strictly increases with
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Let us denote
b=b_{1}(L_{-}^{i})
for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

We claim that the collection of Newton polytopes
N(\varepsilon_{L\cup L_{-}^{i}},\rho_{+})=N(\varepsilon_{L_{-}^{i}}\circ\Phi^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{L},\rho_{+})
is also infinite, up to unimodular equivalence. This will conclude the proof. Indeed, the explicit description of
\Phi_{L}
above implies that each such polytope
N(\varepsilon_{L_{-}^{i}}\circ\Phi^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{L},\rho_{+})
is the convex hull of lattice points in
\mathbb{R}^{b+1}
of two types:

(i)

Lattice points in
\mathbb{R}^{b+1}
that lie in the hyperplane
\{s=0\}
. Their convex hull in the hyperplane is precisely the polytope
N(\varepsilon_{L_{-}^{i}},\rho_{-})
in
\mathbb{R}^{b}=\{s=0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{b+1}
.

(ii)

Lattice points
\mathbb{R}^{b+1}
that lie in the hyperplane
\{s=-1\}
.

By hypothesis, the number of lattice points of
N(\varepsilon_{L_{-}^{i}},\rho_{-})
is strictly increasing with
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
N(\varepsilon_{L_{-}^{i}},\rho_{-})\subset N(\varepsilon_{L\cup L_{-}^{i}},\rho_{+})
is a facet, the lattice points in
N(\varepsilon_{L_{-}^{i}},\rho_{-})
contribute to the lattice point count for
N(\varepsilon_{L\cup L_{-}^{i}},\rho_{+})
. Therefore, there exists an infinite subcollection of the set of Newton polytopes
\{N(\varepsilon_{L\cup L_{-}^{i}},\rho_{+})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
whose lattice point count is strictly increasing. This proves the claim and therefore the required statement.
∎

be its Legendrian

-spun, where we are spinning the front projection of
\Lambda
about by an axis placed to the right of the front projection of

[

now with

allows us to conclude that
\Sigma_{S^{m}}\Lambda

\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(\Sigma_{S^{n-1}}L);\mathbb{Z}]
aug-infinite because

is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[\mathcal{H}]
aug-infinite.

Let
p\in\Lambda
be the right cusp of the front projection of
\Lambda
with the highest

(n-1)
-dimensional isotropic sphere

. Note that

is the boundary of the
n

whose front is exactly flat and with boundary (the front of)

. The interior of

by construction. Set

for ease of notation.

Performing Legendrian surgery on

is now diffeomorphic to

. See
[

]
, or e.g.
[

]
, for Legendrian surgeries. By construction, there exists an exact Lagrangian cobordism

from

to
\Sigma_{S^{m}}\Lambda_{S}
in the symplectization of
(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},\xi_{std})
. Finally, by
[

, Theorem 1.1]
and its proof, the induced dga map

\Phi_{W_{S}}:(\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{S^{m}}\Lambda_{S};\mathbb{Z}_{2}),\partial_{\Sigma_{S^{m}}\Lambda_{S}})\rightarrow(\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{S^{m}}\Lambda;\mathbb{Z}_{2});\partial_{\Sigma_{S^{m}}\Lambda})

is surjective. Now, there is a Legendrian representative for
\Sigma_{S^{m}}\Lambda_{S}
such that its set of Reeb chords is given by the set of Reeb chords of (a representative of)
\Sigma_{S^{m}}\Lambda
and a Reeb chord

with grading
|c_{S}|=n-k-1=0
. Since
\Lambda
has no
(-1)
-degree Reeb chords, then
\Sigma_{S^{m}}\Lambda
has no
(-1)
degree Reeb chords, and then neither does
\Sigma_{S^{m}}\Lambda_{S}
. An adaptation of the proof of Proposition
3.15
to higher dimensions will now conclude that
\Sigma_{S^{m}}\Lambda_{S}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(\Sigma_{S^{m}}L\circ W_{S});\mathbb{Z}]
aug-infinite, as follows.

The choice of
\Lambda=\tilde{\Lambda}
above gives a Reeb chord
\rho
of
\Lambda
such that:

(1)

The Reeb chord
\rho
is Newton infinite with respect to a collection of augmentations
\{\varepsilon_{L}^{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, associated to fillings

of
\Lambda
, and the Newton polytopes in the collection

,

, can be pairwise distinguished by their lattice point counts.

(2)

The above item also holds after spinning, i.e. there exists a degree zero Reeb chord
\tilde{\rho}
of
\Sigma_{S^{m}}\Lambda
, which corresponds to
\rho
after an appropriate Morsification of the front of
\Sigma_{S^{m}}\Lambda
, such that
\tilde{\rho}
is Newton infinite with respect to a collection of augmentations
\{\varepsilon_{\Sigma_{S^{m}}L}^{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and the Newton polytopes in the collection

are pairwise distinguished by their lattice point counts.

and the specifics of front spinning, see

, Lemma 3.3]

above occurs near a cusp of

4.
Two Final Remarks

In this last section we present two brief results which hopefully can help start expanding the study of Newton polytopes in Floer theory. First, we show in Subsection
4.1
that for any degree 0 Reeb chord of the max-tb torus knots
\Lambda(2,n)
, expressed as the rainbow closure of
\sigma_{1}^{n}
, there exists a Lagrangian filling whose augmentation sends that Reeb chord to a Laurent polynomial whose Newton polytope is a top-dimensional simplex. Second, in Subsection
4.2
we distinguish non-orientable Lagrangian fillings and prove Theorem
1.3
.

4.1.
Top-dimensional simplices as Newton polytopes for augmentations

It would be interesting to explore fully what type of lattice polytopes can appear as
N(\varepsilon_{L},\rho)
, where
L
is a Lagrangian filling of a Legendrian knot
\Lambda
and
\rho
a Reeb chord of
\Lambda
. To start, we show that top-dimensional simplices always appear for one of the simplest types of Legendrian knots, in the following sense:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Lambda(2,n)
be the max-tb representative of the
(2,n)
-torus knot,
n
odd, presented as the rainbow closure of the positive braid
\sigma_{1}^{n}
. For every zero graded Reeb chord
b
of
\Lambda(2,n)
, there exists a Lagrangian filling
L
with induced augmentation
\varepsilon_{L}
such that the Newton polytope
N(\varepsilon_{L},b)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is
(
equivalent to
)
the standard top-dimensional simplex with volume
\frac{1}{(n-1)!}
.

Proof.

Let us draw
\Lambda(2,n)
in the Lagrangian projection, as the Ng resolution of the rainbow closure of the positive braid
\sigma_{1}^{n}
. The degree 0 Reeb chords correspond to the crossings of
\sigma_{1}^{n}
. Suppose that
b
is the
i
-th crossing, starting the labeling from the left. To construct
L
, we consider the following pinching sequence:

(1)

First pinch the crossings to the right of
b
in left-to-right order.

(2)

Then, pinch the crossings to the left of
b
, in right-to-left order

(3)

Finally pinch the crossing
b
itself.

For example, consider
\Lambda(2,7)
presented as the rainbow closure of
\sigma_{1}^{7}
. Suppose that
b=b_{5}
is the 5th crossing (degree 0 Reeb chord) of
\sigma_{1}^{7}
counting from the left. Then the pinching sequence is given by
b_{6},b_{7},b_{4},b_{3},b_{2},b_{1},b_{5}
. Indeed, the crossings to the right of
b=b_{5}
are
b_{6},b_{7}
ordered left-to-right, so we first pinch
b_{6}
and then
b_{7}
. Similarly, the crossings to the left of
b=b_{5}
are
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4}
ordered left-to-right: we first pinch starting from the rightmost crossing
b_{4}
and then
b_{3}
, then
b_{2}
and finally
b_{1}
, going right-to-left. The last step is pinching
b=b_{5}
itself.

Note that it does not matter in what order we intersperse the opening of the crossings to the right of
b
and to the left of
b
; we chose first to pinch those on the right just for specificity. After these
n
pinch moves, we have two max-tb unknots that we can fill with two minimum cobordisms each to obtain the Lagrangian filling
L
, and thus an augmentation
\varepsilon_{L}
. The
\varepsilon_{L}
-augmented value of
b
is

\varepsilon_{L}(b)=s_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}s_{j}^{-1}\cdot\left(\prod_{k=j+1}^{i-1}s_{k}^{-2}\right)+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}s_{j}^{-1}\cdot\left(\prod_{k=i+1}^{j-1}s_{k}^{-2}\right).

This is readily computed via the same methods as in
[
EHK16
,
Pan17
]
.
By construction, the relation
\varepsilon_{L}(s_{1}s_{2}\ldots s_{n})=1
holds, and therefore we set
s_{i}=s^{-1}_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot s_{i-1}^{-1}\cdot s_{i+1}^{-1}\cdot\ldots\cdot s^{-1}_{n-1}s_{n}^{-1}
. The Newton polytope
N(\varepsilon_{L},b)
is then a polytope in
\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, the lattice associated to
s_{1},\ldots,s_{i-1},s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{n}
. It is the convex hull of the
n
monomials in
\varepsilon_{L}(b)
. Thanks to the computation above,
N(\varepsilon_{L},b)
is in fact the convex hull of the following points in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
:

(a)

(Type-R) The lattice points associated to the monomials coming from pinchings to the right of
b
, which is the third summand in
\varepsilon_{L}(b)
:

(0,\ldots,0,-1_{i+1},0,\ldots,0),(0,\ldots,0,-2_{i+1},-1_{i+2},0,\ldots,0),(0,\ldots,0,-2_{i+1},-2_{i+2},-1_{i+3},\ldots,0),

\ldots,(0,\ldots,0,-2_{i+1},-2_{i+2},-2_{i+3},\ldots,-1_{n-2},0),(0,\ldots,0,-2_{i+1},-2_{i+2},-2_{i+3},\ldots,-1_{n-1}),

where the subindices indicate the component of the point.

(b)

(Type-L) The lattice points associated to the monomials coming from pinchings to the left of
b
, which is the second summand in
\varepsilon_{L}(b)
:

(0,\ldots,0,-1_{i-1},0,\ldots,0),(0,\ldots,0,-1_{i-2},-2_{i-1},0,\ldots,0),(0,\ldots,0,-1_{i-3},-2,-2_{i-1},\ldots,0),

\ldots,(0,-1_{2},-2_{3},\ldots,-2_{i-1},0,\ldots,0),(-1_{1},-2_{2},\ldots,-2_{i-1},0,\ldots,0).

(c)

The lattice point
(-1,-1,\ldots,-1)
associated to the monomial
s_{i}=s^{-1}_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot s_{i-1}^{-1}\cdot s_{i+1}^{-1}\cdot\ldots\cdot s^{-1}_{n-1}s_{n}^{-1}
, which is the first summand of
\varepsilon_{L}(b)
.

Let us translate
N(\varepsilon_{L},b)
by adding the constant vector
(1,\ldots,1)
. The lattice point in type (c) above becomes the origin. The resulting
(n-1)
lattice points can then be considered as vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, centered at the origin. Let
S\in M_{n-1}(\mathbb{Z})
be the matrix constructed by using these vectors as rows, for a choice of row permutation. It suffices to argue that
S\in\operatorname{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{Z})
, i.e.
|\det(S)|=1
. Given
m,l\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the matrix
O_{l,m}\in M_{l,m}(\mathbb{Z})
all of whose entries are
1
, and the matrix
D_{m}\in M_{m}(\mathbb{Z})
which has entries equal to
0
in the diagonal, entries equal to
1
above the diagonal, and entries equal to
-1
below the diagonal. Then
S
is given in block notation as

S=\begin{pmatrix}D_{i-1}&\vline&O_{i-1,n-i}\\
\hline\cr O_{n-i,i-1}&\vline&D_{n-i}\end{pmatrix}\in M_{n-1}(\mathbb{Z}).

Consider matrices
\mathbb{O}_{i-1,n-i},\mathbb{O}_{n-i,i-1}
such that each odd row starts on the left with
1
, each even row starts on the left with
-1
, and for each row (even or odd) the rest of the entries alternate between
1
and
-1
, so that the entries of a row are always alternating when read left to right. Let
\mathbb{D}_{m}\in M_{m}(\mathbb{Z})
be a matrix with diagonal entries
0
, and such that: to the right of each (zero) diagonal entry, the entries in that piece of the row alternate between
\pm 1
starting with
-1
to the right of the diagonal entry, and to the south of each diagonal entry, the entries in that piece of the column alternate between
\pm 1
starting with
1
right below the diagonal entry. Then
S
admits the explicit inverse

S^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{D}_{i-1}&\vline&\mathbb{O}_{i-1,n-i}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{O}_{n-i,i-1}&\vline&\mathbb{D}_{n-i}\end{pmatrix}\in M_{n-1}(\mathbb{Z}),

where the equality
S\cdot S^{-1}=\mbox{Id}
is verified directly. Therefore
|\det(S)|=1
.
∎

4.2.
Distinct non-orientable exact Lagrangian fillings

The existence and classification of non-orientable exact Lagrangian fillings is less understood than that of their orientable counterparts. By distinguishing augmentations with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[H_{1}(L,\Lambda;\mathbb{Z})]
coefficients we can distinguish non-orientable exact Lagrangian fillings. First, we must establish how to keep track of generators of relative homology for non-orientable decomposable Lagrangian cobordisms
L
.

4.2.1.
Non-orientable Lagrangian cobordisms

By definition, a non-orientable pinch move in the Lagrangian projection replaces a negative crossing corresponding to a contractible proper Reeb chord with its
0
-resolution as shown in Figure
5
. If two Legendrians
\Lambda_{-}
and
\Lambda_{+}
are related by an non-orientable pinch move, then there exists an exact Lagrangian non-orientable cobordism from
\Lambda_{-}
to
\Lambda_{+}
which is topologically a non-orientable
1
-handle, i.e. a twice punctured
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{2}
union a collection of trivial cylinders, all with boundary in
\Lambda_{+}\sqcup\Lambda_{-}
.

Let
\Lambda
be a Legendrian link in
(\mathbf{R}^{3},\xi_{std})
and
L
an exact decomposable Lagrangian filling of
\Lambda
. For such cobordisms, we give a relative basis of
H_{1}(L,\Lambda;\mathbb{Z})
in a standardized manner following
[
EHK16
,
Pan17
,
CN22
]
, encoded by basepoints on the Legendrian slices of
L
. The set up in this non-orientable case is similar to
[
CN22
]
, as follows.

Assume that there is a collection of oriented arcs and circles on
L
such that all circles are contained in the interior of
L
, and the oriented arcs
\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}\}
are transverse to
\Lambda_{+}
and
\Lambda_{-}
at their endpoints on
\Lambda_{+}
and
\Lambda_{-}
. Then the induced dga chain map
\Phi_{L}
is defined by counting holomorphic disks with boundary on
L
and boundary punctures mapping to Reeb chords on
\Lambda_{+}
and
\Lambda_{-}
. One can assume that the curves intersect
\gamma_{i}
transversely, and that no arc
\gamma_{i}
has an endpoint on a Reeb chord of
\Lambda_{+}
or
\Lambda_{-}
. Let
\{\gamma_{i_{1}},\ldots,\gamma_{i_{p}}\}
denote the subset of arcs with at least one endpoint on
\Lambda_{+}
and let
\{\gamma_{j_{1}},\ldots,\gamma_{j_{q}}\}
denote the subset of arcs with at least one endpoint on
\Lambda_{-}
. We view these endpoints
\gamma_{i}
as basepoints
s_{i}
on
\Lambda_{\pm}
respectively. The dga of
\Lambda_{+}
equipped with the basepoints associated to
\gamma_{i_{1}},\ldots,\gamma_{i_{p}}
has coefficient ring
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[s^{\pm 1}_{i_{1}},\ldots,s^{\pm 1}_{i_{p}}]
, while the dga of
\Lambda_{-}
equipped with the basepoints associated to
\gamma_{j_{1}},\ldots,\gamma_{j_{q}}
has coefficient ring
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[s^{\pm 1}_{j_{1}},\ldots,s^{\pm 1}_{j_{q}}]
. We tensor both dgas with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,s^{\pm 1}_{k}]
in order to define
\Phi_{L}
as a chain map of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots s_{k}^{\pm 1}]
-algebras. If
\Delta
is a holomorphic disk contributing to
\Phi_{L}
then we give it the coefficient
s_{1}^{n_{1}(\Delta)}\cdots s_{k}^{n_{k}(\Delta)}\in R[s_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots s_{k}^{\pm 1}]
where

is the signed intersection number of
\partial\Delta
with
\gamma_{i}
. Observe that if we have a basis of generators
\{\gamma_{i}\}
for
H_{1}(L,\Lambda_{+}\cup\Lambda_{-};\mathbb{Z})
, then for each holomorphic (punctured) disk
\Delta
,
n_{i}(\Delta)
allows us to keep track of the relative homology class of
\partial\Delta
.

Suppose that
L
is constructed using a sequence of Legendrian isotopies,
n
pinch moves and (at the end)
k
minimum cobordisms. (See end of Subsection
2
above to recall these concepts, right before Remark
2.8
.) Let
\Lambda_{0}
be an unlinked set of
k
max-tb Legendrian unknots. Let
\Lambda_{i}
denote the Legendrian link obtained from
\Lambda_{0}
after the
i
th reverse pinch move on
\Lambda_{0}
and let
L_{i}
denote the exact Lagrangian cobordism from
\Lambda_{i}
to
\Lambda_{i+1}
. Place
k

t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}
on the Legendrian
\Lambda=\Lambda_{+}
being filled. Each
\Lambda_{i}
will have two additional basepoints than
\Lambda_{i+1}
, which we label
s_{i}^{\pm 1}
,
\Lambda_{0}
contains the
k
basepoints
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}
, and
2n
basepoints
s_{i}^{\pm 1}
for

. Let
\tau_{i}
denote the arc traced out by the basepoints
t_{i}
. The two basepoints
s_{i}
will be the endpoints of an arc
\sigma_{i}
from
\Lambda_{0}
to itself. While the cobordism itself may be non-orientable, we assume that each Legendrian
\Lambda_{i}
and each arc

and

For any orientable or non-orientable exact filling

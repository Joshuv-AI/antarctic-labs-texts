Smooth affine group schemes over the dual numbers
Matthieu Romagny, Dajano Tossici

Abstract
In this article we provide an equivalence between the category of smooth affine group schemes over the ring of generalized dual numbers k ⁡ [ I ] k[I] , and the category of extensions of the form 1 → Lie ⁡ ( G , I ) → E → G → 1 1\to\Lie(G,I)\to E\to G\to 1 where G G is a smooth affine group scheme over k k . Here k k is an arbitrary commutative ring and k ⁡ [ I ] = k ⊕ I k[I]=k\oplus I with I 2 = 0 I^{2}=0 . The equivalence is given by Weil restriction, and we provide a quasi-inverse which we call Weil extension . It is compatible with the exact structures and the O k \mathbb{O}_{k} -module stack structures on both categories. Our constructions rely on the use of the group algebra scheme of an affine group scheme; we introduce this object and establish its main properties. As an application, we establish a Dieudonné classification for smooth, commutative, unipotent group schemes over k ⁡ [ I ] k[I] when k k is a perfect field.



Keywords.
Group scheme, deformation, dual numbers, adjoint representation, Weil restriction, Dieudonné classification of unipotent groups

1.1.
Motivation, results, plan of the article

1.1.0. Motivation.

The starting point of our work is a relation between deformations
and group extensions. To explain the idea, let
G
be an affine,
flat, finitely presented

k
-group scheme. It is shown in Illusie’s book
[
Il72
]

that the set of isomorphism classes of deformations
of
G
over
k[I]
is in bijection with the cohomology group

\mathrm{H}^{2}(BG,\underline{\ell}{}_{G}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\otimes I)
,
see chap. VII, thm 3.2.1 in
loc. cit
. Here, the
coefficients of cohomology are the derived dual of the
equivariant cotangent complex
\underline{\ell}{}_{G}\in D(BG)
,
tensored (in the derived sense) by
I
viewed as the coherent
sheaf it defines on the fpqc site of
BG
, also equal to
the vector bundle
\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})
.
If we assume moreover that
G
is smooth, then
the augmentation
\underline{\ell}{}_{G}\to\omega^{1}_{G}

to the sheaf of invariant differential 1-forms is a
quasi-isomorphism and it follows that

\underline{\ell}{}_{G}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\simeq\Lie G
.
Since coherent cohomology of
BG
is isomorphic to group
cohomology of
G
, the cohomology group of interest ends up
being
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\Lie(G,I))
where

\Lie(G,I)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Lie G\otimes\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})
.
The latter cohomology group is meaningful also in the theory
of group extensions, where it is known to classify isomorphism
classes of extensions of
G
by
\Lie(G,I)
viewed as a

G
-module via the adjoint representation, see

[
DG70
, chap. II, § 3, no 3.3 and III, § 6, no 2.1]
.

In this paper, our aim is to give a direct algebro-geometric
construction of this correspondence between deformations
and group extensions.
Our main result is that the Weil restriction
functor
h_{*}
provides such a construction. Thereby, we obtain
a categorification of a link that has been available only as a
bijection between sets of isomorphism classes. This improvement
is crucial for a better understanding of
k[I]
-group schemes,
since in applications most groups occur as kernels or quotients
of morphisms. We illustrate this by giving a Dieudonné-type
theory for unipotent group schemes. Natural extensions
of our result to more general thickenings
of the base, or to non-smooth group schemes, would have further
interesting applications. Since we wish to show our main results
to the reader without further delay, we postpone the discussion
of these applications to
1.1
below.

1.1.1. Results.

Our main result is an equivalence between the category of smooth,
affine
k[I]
-group schemes and a certain category of extensions
of
k
-group schemes. However, for reasons that are discussed below,
it is convenient for us to work with group schemes slightly more
general than smooth ones.
We say that a morphism of schemes
X\to S
is

differentially flat
if both
X
and
{}^{1}_{X/S}

are flat over
S
. Examples of differentially flat group schemes
include smooth group schemes, pullbacks from the spectrum
of a field, Tate-Oort group schemes with parameter
a=0
in
characteristic
p
, flat vector bundles i.e.
\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})

with
\mathscr{F}
flat over the base, and truncated Barsotti-Tate
groups of level
n
over a base where
p^{n}=0

[
Il85
, 2.2.1]
. If
\mathscr{G}
is a
k[I]
-group scheme, we call

rigidification
of
\mathscr{G}
an isomorphism of
k[I]
-schemes

\sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h^{*}\mathscr{G}_{k}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathscr{G}
that lifts the identity of the
scheme
\mathscr{G}_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=i^{*}\mathscr{G}
. Such a map need not be a morphism of
group schemes. We say that
\mathscr{G}
is
rigid
if it admits
a rigidification. Examples of rigid group schemes include
smooth group schemes, pullbacks from
\Spec(k)
, and groups
of multiplicative type. Finally a
deformation
of a
flat
k
-group scheme
G
over
k[I]
is a pair composed of
a flat
k[I]
-group scheme
\mathscr{G}
and an isomorphism
of
k
-group schemes
\mathscr{G}_{k}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}G
.

Let
\Gr\!/k[I]
be the category of affine, differentially
flat, rigid
k[I]
-group schemes (this includes all smooth
affine
k[I]
-group schemes). The morphisms in this
category are the morphisms of
k[I]
-group schemes.
Let
\Ext(I)/k
be the category of extensions of the form

1\to\Lie(G,I)\to E\to G\to 1
where
G
is an affine
differentially flat
k
-group scheme, and “extension” means
that
E\to G
is an fpqc torsor under
\Lie(G,I)
. The
morphisms in this category are the commutative diagrams:

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Lie(G,I)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\diff\!\psi}
\textstyle{E\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{1\>}
\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Lie(G^{\prime},I)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{G^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{1}

where
\diff\!\psi=\Lie(\psi)
is the differential of

.
Usually such a morphism will be denoted simply

\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\to E^{\prime}
.

The categories
\Gr\!/k[I]
and
\Ext(I)/k
are exact categories.
They are also fpqc stacks over
\Spec(k)
equipped with the
structure of
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-module stacks fibred in groupoids
over
\Gr/k
. This means that there exist notions of
sum

and
scalar multiple
for objects of
\Gr\!/k[I]
and

\Ext(I)/k
(for extensions, the sum is the Baer sum); these
structures are described
in
1.2
.
We can now state our main result.

(1)
The Weil restriction functor provides an equivalence:

h_{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Gr\!/k[I]\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\Ext(I)/k.

This equivalence commutes with base changes on
\Spec(k)
.

(2)
If
1\to\mathscr{G}^{\prime}\to\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{G}^{\prime\prime}\to 1
is an
exact sequence in
\Gr\!/k[I]
, then

1\to h_{*}\mathscr{G}^{\prime}\to h_{*}\mathscr{G}\to h_{*}\mathscr{G}^{\prime\prime}
is exact in
\Ext(I)/k
.
If moreover
\mathscr{G}^{\prime}
is smooth then

1\to h_{*}\mathscr{G}^{\prime}\to h_{*}\mathscr{G}\to h_{*}\mathscr{G}^{\prime\prime}\to 1
is exact. In particular,

h_{*}
is an exact equivalence between the subcategories
of smooth objects endowed with their natural exact structure.

(3)
The equivalence
h_{*}
is a morphism
of
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-module stacks fibred over
\Gr\!/k
, i.e.
it transforms
the addition and scalar multiplication of deformations
of a fixed
G\in\Gr\!/k
into the Baer sum and scalar
multiplication of extensions.

(4)
Let
P
be one of the properties of
group schemes over a field: of finite type, smooth, connected,
unipotent, split unipotent, solvable, commutative. Say that
a group scheme over an arbitrary ring has property
P
if
it is flat and its fibres have
P
. Then
\mathscr{G}\in\Gr\!/k[I]

has property
P
if and only if the
k
-group scheme
E=h_{*}\mathscr{G}

has
P
.

In order to show that
h_{*}
is an equivalence, we build
a quasi-inverse
h^{\mbox{\rm\tiny+}}
which we call

Weil extension
. The construction and study of this
functor is the hardest part of the proof.

As an application of the Theorem, we prove a Dieudonné
classification for smooth, commutative, unipotent group
schemes over the generalized dual numbers of a perfect
field
k
. This takes the form of an
exact equivalence of categories with a category of
extensions of smooth, erasable Dieudonné modules.
Here is the precise statement (we refer to
Section
6
for
the definition of all undefined terms).

Let
\SCU/k[I]
be the category of smooth, commutative,
unipotent
k[I]
-group schemes. Let

\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}I\mbox{-}\Mod
be the category of
I
-extensions
of smooth erasable Dieudonné modules. Then the Dieudonné
functor
\underline{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\CU/k\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}\Mod

induces a contravariant equivalence of categories:

\mathscr{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\SCU/k[I]\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}I\mbox{-}\Mod

that sends
\mathscr{U}
to the extension

0\to\underline{M}(\mathscr{U}_{k})\to\underline{M}(h_{*}\mathscr{U}))\to\underline{M}(\Lie(\mathscr{U}_{k},I))\to 0
.

1.1.2. Comments.

An important tool in many of our arguments is the

group algebra scheme
. It provides a common framework
to conduct computations in the groups and their tangent bundles
simultaneously. It allows us to describe conveniently the
Weil restriction of a group scheme, and is essential in the
proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Since we are not aware of any treatment of the group algebra
scheme in the literature, we give a detailed treatment
in Section
2
. We point out that this concept
is useful in other situations; in particular it allows to work
out easily the deformation theory of smooth affine group schemes,
as we show in Subsection
A.3
.

Let us say a word on the assumptions.
The choice to work with differentially flat group schemes
instead of simply smooth ones is not just motivated by the
search for maximal generality or aesthetic reasons. It is also
extremely useful because when working with an affine,
smooth group scheme
G
, we use our results also
for the group algebra
\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]
in the course of proving
the main theorem; and the group algebra

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]
is differentially flat and rigid, but usually
infinite-dimensional and hence not smooth.

There are at least two advantages to work over generalized dual
numbers
k[I]
rather than simply the usual ring
k[\varepsilon]

with
\varepsilon^{2}=0
. The first is that in order to prove that our
equivalence of categories respects the
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-module stack
structure, we have to introduce the ring
k[I]
with the
two-dimensional
k
-module
I=k\varepsilon+k\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Indeed, this is
needed to describe the sum of deformations and the Baer sum
of extensions. The other advantage is that since arbitrary local
Artin
k
-algebras are filtered by Artin
k
-algebras whose
maximal ideal has square zero, our results may be useful in
handling deformations along more general thickenings.

1.1.3. Further developments.

Our results have several desirable generalizations. Here are the two
most natural directions. First, one may wish to relax the assumptions
on the group schemes and consider non-affine or non-smooth group
schemes; second one may wish to consider more general thickenings
than that given by the dual numbers. Let us explain how our personal
work indicates a specific axis for research. In previous work
of the authors with Ariane Mézard
[
MRT13
]
, we studied models
of the group schemes of roots of unity
\mu_{p^{n}}
over
p
-adic rings.
As a result, we raised a conjecture which says in essence that every
such model can be equipped with a cohomological theory that
generalizes the Kummer theory available on the generic fibre.
In the process of trying to
prove the conjecture, we encountered various character groups of
smooth and finite unipotent group schemes over truncated discrete
valuation rings. In order to compute these, it is therefore desirable
to obtain a statement similar to Theorem
1.1

in this context. The present paper can be seen as
the first part of this programme, carried out in the simplest case;
we plan to realize the second part of the programme by using the

derived Weil restriction
or
derived Greenberg functor

in place of the usual Weil restriction.

The present Section
1
ends with
material of preliminary nature on the description of the

\mathbb{O}_{k}
-module stack structure of the categories
\Gr\!/k[I]

and
\Ext(I)/k
and on Weil restriction.
In Section
2
we introduce group
algebra schemes. In Section
3
we
describe the functor
h_{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Gr\!/k[I]\to\Ext(I)/k
, in

, while in

we prove that these
functors are quasi-inverse and we complete the proof
of Theorem
1.1
. Finally, in
Section
6
we derive the Dieudonné
classification for smooth commutative unipotent group schemes
over the dual numbers. In the Appendices, we review notions
from differential calculus (tangent bundle, Lie algebra and
exponentials) and module categories (Picard categories with
scalar multiplication) in the level of generality
needed in the paper.

1.2.
The
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-module stack structure of

\Gr\!/k[I]
and
\Ext(I)/k

Both categories
\Gr\!/k[I]
and
\Ext(I)/k
are endowed with
the structure of
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-module stacks in groupoids over

\Gr\!/k
. The reader who wishes to see the full-fledged
definition is invited to read
Appendix

.
In rough terms, once a
k
-group scheme
G
is fixed, the

\mathbb{O}_{k}
-module category structure boils down to an addition law
by which one can add deformations (resp. extensions) of
G
,
and an external law by which one can multiply a deformation
(resp. an extension) by scalars of the ring scheme
\mathbb{O}_{k}
.
Here is a description of these structures.

1.2.0. The
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-module stack

\Gr\!/k[I]\to\Gr\!/k
.

Let
G\in\Gr\!/k
be fixed.
Let
\mathscr{G}_{1},\mathscr{G}_{2}\in\Gr\!/k[I]
with identifications

i^{*}\mathscr{G}_{1}\simeq G\simeq i^{*}\mathscr{G}_{2}
. The addition is obtained
by a two-step process. First we glue these group schemes along
their common closed subscheme
G

\mathscr{G}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathscr{G}_{1}\amalg_{G}\mathscr{G}_{2}.

This lives over the scheme

\Spec(k[I])\times_{\Spec(k)}\Spec(k[I])=\Spec(k[I\oplus I])
.
The properties of gluing along infinitesimal thickenings are
studied in the Stacks Project
[
SP
]
. We point out some
statements relevant to our situation:
existence of the coproduct in

Tag 07RV
,
a list of properties preserved by gluing in

Tag 07RX
,
gluing of modules in

Tag 08KU

and preservation of flatness of modules in

Tag 07RW
.
It follows from these results that
\mathscr{G}^{\prime}
is an object of

\Gr\!/k[I\oplus I]
. Then we form the desired sum

\mathscr{G}_{1}+\mathscr{G}_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=j^{*}(\mathscr{G}^{\prime})=j^{*}(\mathscr{G}_{1}\amalg_{G}\mathscr{G}_{2})

by pullback along the closed immersion

j\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Spec(k[I])\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\Spec(k[I\oplus I])

The neutral element for this addition is the group scheme

is given by rescaling using the scheme map

which is multiplication by

. The axioms of

-module stacks can be
checked but we leave the details to the courageous reader.

1.3.
Weil restriction generalities

We briefly give the main definitions and notations related to
Weil restriction; we refer to
[
BLR90
, § 7.6]
for
more details. Let
h\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Spec(k^{\prime})\to\Spec(k)
be a finite,
locally free morphism of affine schemes. Let
(\Sch/k)
be the
category of
k
-schemes and
(\Fun/k)
the category of
functors
(\Sch/k)^{\circ}\to(\Sets)
. The Yoneda functor
embeds the former category into the latter. By sending a
morphism of functors
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X^{\prime}\to\Spec(k^{\prime})
to the morphism

h\circ f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X^{\prime}\to\Spec(k)
we obtain a functor

h_{!}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(\Fun/k^{\prime})\to(\Fun/k)
. Sometimes we will refer to
h_{!}X^{\prime}

as
the
k^{\prime}
-functor
X^{\prime}
viewed as a
k
-functor
and the
notation
h_{!}
will be omitted. The pullback functor

h^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(\Fun/k)\to(\Fun/k^{\prime})
is right adjoint to
h_{!}
, in particular

(h^{*}X)(S^{\prime})=X(h_{!}S^{\prime})
for all
k^{\prime}
-schemes
S^{\prime}
. The Weil
restriction functor
h_{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(\Fun/k^{\prime})\to(\Fun/k)
is right adjoint
to
h^{*}
, in particular we have
(h_{*}X^{\prime})(S)=X^{\prime}(h^{*}S)
for all
k
-schemes

S
. Thus we have a sequence of adjoint functors:

h_{!},h^{*},h_{*}.

The functors
h_{!}
and
h^{*}
preserve the subcategories of schemes.
The same is true for
h_{*}
if
h
is radicial, a case which covers
our needs (see
[
BLR90
, § 7.6]
for refined representability
results). We write

\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}1\longrightarrow h_{*}h^{*}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h^{*}h_{*}\longrightarrow 1

for the unit and counit of the
(h^{*},h_{*})
-adjunction.
If
X
is a separated
k
-scheme then
\alpha_{X}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\to h_{*}h^{*}X
is a
closed immersion. If
X^{\prime}
is a
k^{\prime}
-group (resp. algebra)
functor (resp. scheme), then also
h_{*}X^{\prime}
is a
k
-group
(resp. algebra) functor (resp. scheme). If moreover

X^{\prime}\to\Spec(k^{\prime})
is smooth of relative dimension
n
, then

h_{*}X^{\prime}\to\Spec(k)
is smooth of relative dimension
n[k^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]

where
[k^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]
is the locally constant rank of
h
.
Quite often, it is simpler to consider functors defined on the
subcategory of affine schemes; the functors
h_{!}
,
h^{*}
,
h_{*}

are defined similarly in this context.

2.
Group algebras of group schemes

Let
G
be an affine
k
-group scheme. In this subsection, we
explain the construction of the group algebra
\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]
,
which is the analogue in the setting of algebraic geometry
of the usual group algebra of abstract discrete groups.
Note that for a finite constant group scheme, the set
\mathbb{O}_{k}[G](k)

of
k
-rational points of the group algebra and the usual
group algebra
k[G]
are isomorphic, but for other groups they
do not have much in common in general; this will be emphasized
below.
Since we are not aware of any appearance of the group algebra
in the literature, we give a somewhat detailed treatment,
including examples
(
2.2
–
2.2
),
basic properties (
2.3
) and
the universal property
(
2.3
).

2.1.
Linear algebra schemes

2.1.0. Vector bundles.

Let
S=\Spec(k)
.
As in
[
EGA

1
new

]
, we call
vector bundle

an
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-module scheme of the form
\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})=\Spec\Sym(\mathscr{F})

where
\mathscr{F}
is a quasi-coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-module and
\Sym(\mathscr{F})

is its symmetric algebra. We say
smooth vector bundle

if
\mathscr{F}
is locally free of finite rank.
If
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\to S
is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, we set

\mathbb{V}(X/S)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{V}(f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
so

\mathbb{V}(X/S)(T)=\Hom_{{\mathcal{O}}_{T}\mbox{-}\Mod}((f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X})_{T},{\mathcal{O}}_{T})

for all
S
-schemes
T
. There is a canonical
S
-morphism

\nu_{X}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\longrightarrow\mathbb{V}(X/S)

which is initial among all
S
-morphisms from
X

to a vector bundle. We call it the
vector bundle envelope

of
X/S
. If
X
is affine over
S
, the map
\nu_{X}
is a closed
immersion because it is induced by the surjective morphism of
algebras
\Sym(f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X})\longrightarrow f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
induced by the identity

f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\longrightarrow f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
.

2.1.1. Base change and ring scheme.

If
h\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Spec(k^{\prime})\to\Spec(k)
is a morphism
of affine schemes, there is a morphism of
k^{\prime}
-ring schemes

h^{*}\mathbb{O}_{k}\to\mathbb{O}_{k^{\prime}}
which is the identity on points, hence
an isomorphism of ring schemes. However, whereas the target
has a natural structure of
\mathbb{O}_{k^{\prime}}
-algebra, the source
does not. For this reason, the pullback of module functors
or module schemes along
h
is defined as

M\mapsto h^{*}M\otimes_{h^{*}\mathbb{O}_{k}}\mathbb{O}_{k^{\prime}}
, as
is familiar for the pullback of modules on ringed spaces.
Usually we will write simply
h^{*}M
.

For later use, we give some complements on the case where

k^{\prime}=k[I]
is the ring of generalized dual numbers, for some
finite free
k
-module
I
.
We will identify
I
and its dual
I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}=\Hom_{k}(I,k)

with the coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{\Spec(k)}
-modules they define,
thus we have the vector bundle
\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})
.
For each
k
-algebra
R
, we have
R[I]=R\oplus I\otimes_{k}R

where
IR\simeq I\otimes_{k}R
has square 0.
This decomposition functorial in
R
gives rise to a direct
sum decomposition of
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-module schemes:

h_{*}\mathbb{O}_{k[I]}=\mathbb{O}_{k}\oplus\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}).

It is natural to use the notation
\mathbb{O}_{k}[\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})]

for the
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra scheme on the right-hand side,
however we will write more
simply
\mathbb{O}_{k}[I]
. Now we move up on
\Spec(k[I])
, where
there is a morphism of
\mathbb{O}_{k[I]}
-module
schemes
h^{*}\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\to\mathbb{O}_{k[I]}
defined for all

k[I]
-algebras
R
as the morphism
I\otimes_{k}R\to R
,

i\otimes x\mapsto i_{R}x
where
i_{R}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=i1_{R}
. According to
what we said before, the pullback
h^{*}\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})
has the
module structure such that a section
a\in\mathbb{O}_{k[I]}
acts by

a\cdot i\otimes x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=i\otimes ax
. The image of

h^{*}\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\to\mathbb{O}_{k[I]}
is the ideal

I\!\cdot\!\mathbb{O}_{k[I]}
defined by
(I\!\cdot\!\mathbb{O}_{k[I]})(R)=IR

for all
k[I]
-algebras
R
. There is an exact sequence
of
\mathbb{O}_{k[I]}
-module schemes:

0\longrightarrow I\!\cdot\!\mathbb{O}_{k[I]}\longrightarrow\mathbb{O}_{k[I]}\longrightarrow i_{*}\mathbb{O}_{k}\longrightarrow 0.

2.1.2.
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-Algebra schemes.

Here we define a category
(\mathbb{O}_{k}\mbox{-}\Alg)
of

linear
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra schemes
and give a summary
of elementary properties.
For us an
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra scheme
is a
k
-scheme
D

endowed with two internal composition laws

+,\times\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}D\times D\to D
called addition and multiplication
possessing two neutral sections
0,1\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Spec(k)\to D
, and an external
law
\cdot\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{O}_{k}\times D\to D
, such that for each
k
-algebra
R

the tuple
(D(R),+,\times,0,1,\cdot)
is an associative unitary

R
-algebra. In particular
(D,+,0)
is a commutative group
scheme, and
(D(R),\times,1)
is a (possibly noncommutative)
monoid. We say that
D
is a
linear
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra scheme

if its underlying
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-module scheme is a vector bundle.
In this case
\mathscr{F}
can be recovered as the “dual bundle”
sheaf
\mathscr{F}=\Hom_{\mathbb{O}_{k}\mbox{-}\Mod}(D,\mathbb{O}_{k})
, the
Zariski sheaf over
S
whose sections over an open
U
are the
morphisms of
{\mathbb{O}_{k}}_{|U}
-modules
D_{|U}\to{\mathbb{O}_{k}}_{|U}

(read
[
EGA

1
new

, 9.4.9]
between the lines). For an affine

\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra scheme in our sense, the comultiplication
is a map
\Delta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Sym(\mathscr{F})\to\Sym(\mathscr{F})\otimes\Sym(\mathscr{F})

and the bilinearity of
m
implies that this map is
induced from a map
\Delta_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathscr{F}
. Finally
we point out two constructions on linear
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra schemes.
The first is the tensor product
D\otimes_{\mathbb{O}_{k}}D^{\prime}
, which
as a functor is defined as
R\mapsto D(R)\otimes_{R}D^{\prime}(R)
.
If
D=\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})
and
D^{\prime}=\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F}^{\prime})
with
\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
locally free
of finite ranks, then

D\otimes_{\mathbb{O}_{k}}D^{\prime}\simeq\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathscr{F}^{\prime})
.
The second construction is the group of units. We
observe that for any linear
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra scheme
D
,
the subfunctor
D^{\times}\subset D
of (multiplicative) units
is the preimage under the multiplication
D\times D\to D

of the unit section
1\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Spec(k)\to D
and is therefore
representable by an affine scheme. This gives rise to the

group of units
functor

(\mathbb{O}_{k}\mbox{-}\Alg)\to(k\mbox{-}\Gr)
,
D\mapsto D^{\times}

where
(k\mbox{-}\Gr)
is the category of affine
k
-group schemes.

2.1.3. Remark.

We do not know if an

\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra scheme whose underlying scheme is affine
over
S
is always of the form
\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})
.

2.2.
Group algebra: construction and examples

Let
G=\Spec(A)
be an affine
k
-group scheme. We write

(u,v)\mapsto u\star v
or sometimes simply
(u,v)\mapsto uv

the multiplication of
G
. This operation extends to the
vector bundle envelope
\mathbb{V}(G/k)
, as follows.
Let
\Delta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to A\otimes_{k}A
be the comultiplication.
For each
k
-algebra
R
, we have

\mathbb{V}(G/k)(R)=\Hom_{k\mbox{-}\Mod}(A,R)
.
If
u,v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to R
are morphisms of
k
-modules, we consider the
composition
u\star v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(u\otimes v)\circ\Delta
:

u\star v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow}}A\otimes_{k}A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u\otimes v}}{{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow}}R.

Here the map
u\otimes v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\otimes_{k}A\to R
is

a\otimes b\mapsto u(a)v(b)
.

2.2.0. Definition.

The
group algebra of the
k
-group scheme
G
:

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\mathbb{V}(G/k),+,\star),

is the vector bundle
\mathbb{V}(G/k)
endowed with the product just
defined. We write
\nu_{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\hookrightarrow\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]
for the closed
immersion as in paragraph
2.1
.

We check below that
\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]
is a linear
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra scheme.
Apart from
G(R)
, there is another noteworthy subset inside

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G](R)
, namely the set
\Der_{G}(R)
of
k
-module maps
d\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to R

which are
u
-derivations for some
k
-algebra map
u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to R

(which need not be determined by
d
); a more accurate notation
would be
\Der({\mathcal{O}}_{G},\mathbb{O}_{k})(R)
but we favour lightness.
Here are the first basic properties coming out of the construction.

2.2.1. Proposition.

Let
G
be an affine
k
-group scheme. Let
\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]

and
\Der_{G}
be as described above.

The tuple
\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\mathbb{V}(G/k),+,\star)
is
a linear
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra scheme.

As a
k
-scheme,
\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]
is
k
-flat (resp. has

k
-projective function ring) iff
G
has the same property.

The composition
G\hookrightarrow\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]^{\times}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]

is a closed embedding, hence also
G\hookrightarrow\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]^{\times}
.

The subfunctor
\Der_{G}\subset\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]
is stable
by multiplication by
G
on the left and on the right, so
it acquires left and right
G
-actions.
More precisely, if
u,v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to R
are maps of algebras,

d\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to R
is a
u
-derivation and
d^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to R
is a

v
-derivation, then
d\star v
and
u\star d^{\prime}
are

(u\star v)
-derivations.

Omitted.

\square

2.2.2. Remark on Hopf algebra structure.

If
G
is a finite, locally free commutative
k
-group scheme,
then the multiplication of its function ring induces a
comultiplication on
\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]
making it into a
Hopf
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra scheme. Moreover the
k
-algebra

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G](k)
is the ring of functions of the Cartier dual

G^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
. This Hopf algebra structure highlights
Examples
2.2

and
2.2
below.

2.2.3. Example 1: finite locally free groups.

If
G
is a finite locally free
k
-group scheme, the algebra

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]
is a smooth vector bundle of rank
[G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Spec(k)]

and its group of units is the complement
in
\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]
of the Cartier divisor equal to the zero locus
of the determinant of the left regular representation:

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}}}{{\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow}}\End_{\mathbb{O}_{k}\mbox{-}\Mod}(\mathbb{O}_{k}[G])\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\det}}{{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{A}^{1}.

If moreover
G
is the finite constant
k
-group scheme
defined by a finite abstract group
\Gamma
, then
\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]

is isomorphic to the algebra scheme defined by the abstract
group algebra
k[\Gamma]
, that is to say

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G](R)\simeq R[\Gamma]
functorially in
R
.

2.2.4. Example 2: the additive group.

Let
G=\mathbb{G}_{a}=\Spec(k[x])
. For a
k
-algebra
R
, let

R\langle\!\langle t\rangle\!\rangle=R[[t,t^{[2]},t^{[3]},\dots]]

be the
R
-algebra of divided power formal power series in one
variable
t
, with
t^{[i]}t^{[j]}={i+j\choose i}t^{[i+j]}

for all
i,j\geqslant 0
. Setting

\mathbb{O}\langle\!\langle t\rangle\!\rangle(R)=R\langle\!\langle t\rangle\!\rangle
,
we have an isomorphism

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathbb{O}\langle\!\langle t\rangle\!\rangle
given
by:

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G](R)\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}R\langle\!\langle t\rangle\!\rangle\quad,\quad(k[x]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u}}{{\longrightarrow}}R)\longmapsto\sumop\displaylimits_{i\geqslant 0}u(x^{i})t^{i}.

If
k
is a ring of characteristic
p>0
, let
H=\alpha_{p}

be the kernel of Frobenius. The algebra

\mathbb{O}_{k}[H](R)
is identified with the
R
-subalgebra of

R\langle\!\langle t\rangle\!\rangle
generated by
t
, which
is isomorphic to
R[t]/(t^{p})
because
t^{p}=pt^{[p]}=0
.

2.2.5. Example 3: the multiplicative group.

Let
G=\mathbb{G}_{m}=\Spec(k[x,1/x])
. Let
R^{\mathbb{Z}}
be
the product algebra, whose elements are sequences with componentwise
addition and multiplication. We have an isomorphism

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\prodop\displaylimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{O}_{k}
given by:

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G](R)\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}R^{\mathbb{Z}}\quad,\quad(k[x]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u}}{{\longrightarrow}}R)\longmapsto\{u(x^{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}.

More generally, for any torus
T
with
character group
X(T)
, we have

\mathbb{O}_{k}[T]\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\prodop\displaylimits_{i\in X(T)}\mathbb{O}_{k}
.
Let
H=\mu_{n}
be the subgroup of
n
-th roots of
unity. The algebra
\mathbb{O}_{k}[H](R)
is identified with
the
R
-subalgebra of
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-invariants

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G](R)^{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}
, composed of sequences
\{r_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}

such that
r_{i+nj}=r_{i}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.3.
Properties: functoriality and adjointness

Here are some functoriality properties of the
group algebra.

2.3.0. Proposition.

Let
G
be an affine
k
-group scheme.
The formation of the group algebra
\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]
:

is functorial in
G
and faithful;

commutes with base change
k^{\prime}/k
;

is compatible with products: there is a canonical
isomorphism

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{O}_{k}}\mathbb{O}_{k}[H]\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathbb{O}_{k}[G\times H]
;

is compatible with Weil restriction: if

h\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Spec(k^{\prime})\to\Spec(k)
is a finite locally free morphism
of schemes, there is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra schemes

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{O}_{k}}h_{*}\mathbb{O}_{k^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}h_{*}h^{*}\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]
.

(1) Any map of affine
k
-group schemes

G=\Spec(A)\to H=\Spec(B)

gives rise to a map of
k
-Hopf algebras
B\to A

and then by precomposition to a map of
R
-algebras

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G](R)\to\mathbb{O}_{k}[H](R)
which is functorial in
R
.
Faithfulness follows from the fact that
G\hookrightarrow\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]

is a closed immersion.

(2) The isomorphism

\Hom_{k^{\prime}\mbox{-}\Mod}(A\otimes_{k}k^{\prime},R^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\Hom_{k\mbox{-}\Mod}(A,R^{\prime})
, functorial in the
k^{\prime}
-algebra
R^{\prime}
,
gives an isomorphism

\mathbb{O}_{k^{\prime}}[G_{k^{\prime}}]\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\times_{\Spec(k)}\Spec(k^{\prime})
.

(3) Write
G=\Spec(A)
and
H=\Spec(B)
.
To a pair of
k
-module maps
u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to R
and
v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B\to R
we
attach the map
u\otimes v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\otimes_{k}B\to R
,

a\otimes b\mapsto u(a)v(b)
. This defines an isomorphism

\Hom_{k\mbox{-}\Mod}(A,R)\otimes_{R}\Hom_{k\mbox{-}\Mod}(B,R)\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\Hom_{k\mbox{-}\Mod}(A\otimes_{k}B,R)

which is functorial in
R
. The result follows.

(4) If
D
is an
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra scheme, the
functorial
R
-algebra maps

\begin{array}[]{rcl}D(R)\otimes_{R}(R\otimes_{k}k^{\prime})&\longrightarrow&D(R\otimes_{k}k^{\prime})\\
d\otimes r^{\prime}&\longmapsto&r^{\prime}d\end{array}

fit together to give a morphism

D\otimes_{\mathbb{O}_{k}}h_{*}\mathbb{O}_{k^{\prime}}\to h_{*}h^{*}D
.
In case
D=\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]
and
h
finite locally free, this is
none other than the isomorphism

\Hom_{k\mbox{-}\Mod}(A,R)\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\Hom_{k\mbox{-}\Mod}(A,R\otimes_{k}k^{\prime})
.

\square

Finally we prove the adjointness property. We recall
from paragraph
2.1

(see also Remark
2.1
)
that
(\mathbb{O}_{k}\mbox{-}\Alg)

is the category of
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra schemes whose
underlying
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-module scheme is of the form

\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})=\Spec\Sym(\mathscr{F})
for some quasi-coherent

{\mathcal{O}}_{\Spec(k)}
-module
\mathscr{F}
, and that
(k\mbox{-}\Gr)

is the category of affine
k
-group schemes.

2.3.1. Theorem (Adjointness property of the group algebra).

The group algebra functor is left adjoint to the group of
units functor. In other words, for all affine
k
-group schemes

G
and all linear
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-algebra schemes
D
, the map that
sends a morphism of algebra schemes
\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\to D
to the
composition
G\subset\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]^{\times}\to D^{\times}
gives a
bifunctorial bijection:

\Hom_{\mathbb{O}_{k}\mbox{-}\Alg}(\mathbb{O}_{k}[G],D)\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\Hom_{k\mbox{-}\Gr}(G,D^{\times}).

We describe a map in the other direction and we leave to the
reader the proof that it is an inverse. Let
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\to D^{\times}

be a morphism of
k
-group schemes. We will construct a map of
functors
f^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\to D
. We know from
paragraph
2.1
that
D=\mathbb{V}(F)
where

F
is a
k
-module, and that the comultiplication

\Delta_{D}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Sym(F)\to\Sym(F)\otimes\Sym(F)
is induced by a
morphism
\Delta_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}F\to F\otimes F
. Consider the composition

G\to D^{\times}\subset D
and let
g\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Sym(F)\to A
be the
corresponding map of algebras. For each
k
-algebra
R
we
have the equalities
\mathbb{O}_{k}[G](R)=\Hom_{k\mbox{-}\Mod}(A,R)
and

D(R)=\Hom_{k\mbox{-}\Mod}(F,R)
. We define
f^{\prime}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{O}_{k}[G](R)&\longrightarrow&D(R)\\
(u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to R)&\longmapsto&(u\circ g\circ i\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}F\to R)\end{array}

where
i\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}F\hookrightarrow\Sym(F)
is the inclusion as the degree 1
piece in the symmetric algebra. The map
f^{\prime}
is a map of
modules, and we only have to check that it respects the
multiplication. Let
u,v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to R
be module homomorphisms.
We have the following commutative diagram:

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Delta_{G}}
\textstyle{A\otimes A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{u\otimes v}
\textstyle{R}
\textstyle{\Sym(F)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Delta_{D}}
\scriptstyle{g}

3.1.
Weil restriction of the group algebra

The starting point is the following definition and lemma.
Let

-algebra and

Let

determined by the identity

3.1.0. Definition.

Let

be a

-algebra and

-algebra. We say

-linear map

and

Note that since

3.1.1. Lemma.

Let

be a
k[I]
-algebra and

a
k
-algebra.

Each morphism of
k[I]
-modules

-compatible

Each morphism of
k[I]
-algebras

is of the form

as above with

for all

is a
{\bar{v}}
-derivation.

(1) Using the decomposition

we can write

for some unique
k
-linear
maps

and
v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\to I\otimes_{k}R
. Then

is

-linear if and only if

for all

and

is

-compatible and

.

(2) The condition

.

-derivation, i.e.

\square

3.1.2. Proposition.

Let

-group scheme

Let

\square

3.3.
Extension structure of the Weil restriction

Let
\mathscr{G}
be a
k[I]
-group scheme. The notion of

for
\mathscr{G}
and the property that

\mathscr{G}
be

are defined in

.
Here are some remarks.

If
\sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h^{*}\mathscr{G}_{k}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathscr{G}
is a rigidification,
then
\sigma(1)^{-1}\cdot\sigma
is another. Therefore if there
exists a rigidification
\sigma
, we may always assume moreover
that
\sigma(1)=1
.

By the infinitesimal lifting criterion, all
smooth affine
k[I]
-group schemes are rigid. By Cartier
duality,
k[I]
-group schemes of multiplicative type are
rigid.

If
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{G}^{\prime}
is a morphism
between rigid

-group schemes, it is not always possible
to choose rigidifications for
\mathscr{G}
and
\mathscr{G}^{\prime}

that are compatible in the sense that

\sigma^{\prime}\circ h^{*}\alpha=\alpha\circ\sigma
.
For instance let

and let
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{G}_{a}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}
be
the morphism defined by
\alpha(x)=\varepsilon x
. Then
\mathscr{G}
and

\mathscr{G}^{\prime}
are rigid but since
h^{*}\alpha=0
, there do not
exist compatible rigidifications.

3.3.0. Lemma.

Let
\mathscr{G}
be a
k[I]
-group scheme such that the
restriction homomorphism
\mathscr{G}_{k}=i^{*}\mathscr{G}
is
k
-flat. Let

\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=i^{*}\beta_{\mathscr{G}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h_{*}\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{G}_{k}
. Then the adjunction

\Hom_{\Sch/k[I]}(h^{*}\mathscr{G}_{k},\mathscr{G})=\Hom_{\Sch/k}(\mathscr{G}_{k},h_{*}\mathscr{G})

induces a bijection between rigidifications of
\mathscr{G}
and sections
of
\pi
.

Let

4.
Weil extension

In this section, we construct a functor
h^{\mbox{\tiny+}}

called
Weil extension
which is a quasi-inverse
to the functor
h_{*}
of Weil restriction described in
the previous section. The idea
behind the construction is that one can recover a

k[I]
-group scheme
\mathscr{G}
from the extension
E=h_{*}\mathscr{G}

by looking at the target of the adjunction

\beta_{\mathscr{G}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h^{*}E=h^{*}h_{*}\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{G}
. In turn, in order to
reconstruct the faithfully flat morphism
\beta_{\mathscr{G}}

it is enough to know its kernel
K
. In the case where

\mathscr{G}
is a constant group
h^{*}G
, which in other words is the
case where
E
is a
tangent bundle
\T(G,I)
, Proposition
3.2
(4)
hints the correct expression
K=\{(x,g)\in h^{*}E;\ g=\exp(-x)\}
.
The definition of
K
for general extensions

1\to\Lie(G,I)\to E\to G\to 1

where
G
is an affine, differentially flat
k
-group scheme,
builds on this intuition.

4.1.
Hochschild extensions

The construction of an extension from a 2-cocycle is
well-known; we recall it to set up the notations.
Recall from
[
DG70
, chap. II, § 3, no 2]
that if
G
is
a
k
-group functor and
M
is a
k
-
G
-module functor,
then a
Hochschild extension
or simply
H
-extension

of
G
by
M
is an exact sequence of group functors

1\longrightarrow M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}G

such that
\pi
has a section (which is not required to
be a morphism of groups). From a given section
s\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\to E
,
we can produce a unique morphism
c\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\times G\to M
such that

i(c(g,g^{\prime}))\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=s(g)s(g^{\prime})s(gg^{\prime})^{-1}
.
This is a 2-cocycle, i.e. it satisfies the identity

c(g,g^{\prime})+c(gg^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime})=g\cdot c(g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime})+c(g,g^{\prime}g^{\prime\prime}).

Note that we may always replace
s
by the section

G\to E
,
g\mapsto s(1)^{-1}s(g)
to obtain a section
such that
s(1)=1
. When this is the
case, we have
c(g,1)=c(1,g^{\prime})=0
for all
g,g^{\prime}
and we say
that
c
is
normalized
.
Conversely, starting from a cocycle
c
, the functor

E_{c}=M\times G
with multiplication defined by

(x,g)\cdot(x^{\prime},g^{\prime})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\big(x+g\cdot x^{\prime}+c(g,g^{\prime}),gg^{\prime}\big)

is an
H
-extension.
The map
s\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\to E_{c}
,
g\mapsto(0,g)
is a possible
choice of section for
\pi
. It follows from the previous
comments that we may always change the cocycle into a
normalized cocycle.

Dieudonné theory for unipotent groups
over the dual numbers

In this section, as an application of Theorem
1.1
,
we give a classification of smooth, unipotent group schemes
over the dual numbers of a perfect field
k
, in terms
of extensions of Dieudonné modules. So throughout the
section, the ring
k
is a perfect field of characteristic

p>0
.

6.1.
Reminder on Dieudonné theory

We denote by
W
the Witt ring scheme over
k
and
\Fr,\V

its Frobenius and Verschiebung endomorphisms.
For all
n\geqslant 1
, we write
W_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=W/V^{n}W
the ring scheme
of Witt vectors of length
n
. We use the same notation also
for these operators over the
R
-points, with
R
a
k
-algebra.
We also define
\widetilde{\V}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}W_{n}\longrightarrow W_{n+1}
as the morphism
induced on
W_{n}
by the composition

\textstyle{W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\V}
\textstyle{W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{n+1}}
\textstyle{W_{n+1}}

where
\pi_{n+1}
is the natural projection.

The
Dieudonné ring
\mathbb{D}
is the
W(k)
-algebra
generated by two variables
\mathbb{F}
and
\mathbb{V}
with the relations:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathbb{F}x&=\Fr(x)\mathbb{F}\\
x\mathbb{V}&=\mathbb{V}\Fr(x)\\
\mathbb{F}\mathbb{V}&=\mathbb{V}\mathbb{F}=p,\end{array}

for varying
x\in W(k)
.
A
Dieudonné module
is a left
\mathbb{D}
-module.
A Dieudonné module
M
is called
erasable
if for any

m\in M
there exists a positive integer
n
such that
\mathbb{V}^{n}m=0
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra. Then, for any
n\geqslant 1
, we make

W_{n}(R)
a left
\mathbb{D}
-module with the rules:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathbb{F}\cdot u&=\Fr(u)\\
\mathbb{V}\cdot u&=\V(u)\\
x\cdot u&=\Fr^{1-n}(x)u\end{array}

for all
u\in W_{n}(R)
and
x\in W(k)
.
The twist in the latter definition is designed to make

\widetilde{V}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}W_{n}(R)\to W_{n+1}(R)
a morphism of
\mathbb{D}
-modules,
see Demazure and Gabriel
[
DG70
, chap. V, § 1, no 3.3]
.
All of this is functorial
in
R
and gives
W_{n}
a structure of
\mathbb{D}
-module scheme.
In particular,
\End_{k}(W_{n})
is a
\mathbb{D}
-module. According
to
[
DG70
, chap. V, § 1, no 3.4]
the morphism

\mathbb{D}\to\End_{k}(W_{n})
induces an isomorphism of
\mathbb{D}
-modules:

\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}V^{n}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\End_{k}(W_{n}).

If
U
is a commutative, unipotent
k
-group scheme, the set

\Hom_{k}(U,W_{n})
is a Dieudonné module with its structure
given by postcomposition, i.e. for any
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}U\to W_{n}
:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathbb{F}\cdot f&=\Fr\circ f\\
\mathbb{V}\cdot f&=\V\circ f\\
x\cdot f&=\Fr^{1-n}(x)f,\end{array}

all
x\in W(k)
.
We define the
Dieudonné module of
U
as:

\underline{M}(U)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{n}}}\Hom_{k}(U,W_{n})

where the transition maps of the inductive system are
induced by
\widetilde{V}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}W_{n}\to W_{n+1}
. Since
\Hom_{k}(U,W_{n})

is killed by
\mathbb{V}^{n}
and
\underline{M}(U)
is a union of these
subgroups, we see that
\underline{M}(U)
is erasable. If
M

is a Dieudonné
module, we define its
Frobenius twist
M^{(p)}
as the
module with underlying group
M^{(p)}=M
and
\mathbb{D}
-module
structure given by:

\mathbb{F}_{M^{(p)}}=\mathbb{F}_{M},\quad\mathbb{V}_{M^{(p)}}=\mathbb{V}_{M},\quad x_{M^{(p)}}=\Fr^{-1}(x)_{M}\quad\mbox{for all }x\in W(k).

Then the maps
\mathbb{F}_{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M^{(p)}\to M
and
\mathbb{V}_{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\to M^{(p)}

are visibly
\mathbb{D}
-linear. Moreover, let
\CU/k
be the category
of commutative unipotent
k
-group schemes; according to

[
DG70
, chap. V, § 1, 4.5]
we have a canonical isomorphism

\underline{M}(U)^{(p)}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\underline{M}(U^{(p)})
for all

U\in\CU
with Frobenius twist
U^{(p)}
.

6.1.0. Theorem.

Let
\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}\Mod
be the category of Dieudonné
modules. The contravariant functor

\underline{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\CU/k\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}\Mod

is exact, fully faithful with essential image the full
subcategory of erasable Dieudonné modules. It
transforms the Frobenius (resp. Verschiebung) of
U
into
the Frobenius (resp. Verschiebung) of
\underline{M}(U)
.
Moreover a unipotent group scheme
U
is of finite type
if and only if
\underline{M}(U)
is of finite type.

See
[
DG70
, chap. V, § 1, 4.3, 4.4, 4.5]
.

\square

6.2.
Dieudonné theory over the dual numbers

Before stating our Dieudonné classification, we need to
define the notions of
Lie algebra
and
smoothness

of Dieudonné modules. We let

{\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}\Mod}^{e}\subset{\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}\Mod}
be the
subcategory of erasable
\mathbb{D}
-modules.

6.2.0. Definition.

Let
M\in\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}\Mod
be a Dieudonné module.
We define the
Lie algebra of
M
by:

\Lie M\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(M/\mathbb{F}M)\otimes_{k}k[\mathbb{F}].

If
I
a finite dimensional
k
-vector space,
the
I
-Lie algebra of
M
is

\Lie(M,I)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Lie M\otimes_{k}I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
.
This gives rise to endofunctors
\Lie(-)
and
\Lie(-,I)

of the category
{\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}\Mod}^{e}
.

6.2.1. Proposition.

We have an isomorphism, functorial in
U\in\CU/k
:

\Lie(\underline{M}(U),I)\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\underline{M}(\Lie(U,I)).

We start with the case of dimension one
I=k
, so

\Lie(U,k\varepsilon)=\Lie(U)
. Let
U^{\prime}
be the kernel of Frobenius
in
U
and
M=\underline{M}(U)
,
M^{\prime}=\underline{M}(U^{\prime})
.
We have an exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow U^{\prime}\longrightarrow U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Fr_{U}}}{{\longrightarrow}}U^{(p)}.

We deduce isomorphisms
\Lie U\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\Lie U^{\prime}
and

M^{\prime}=M/\mathbb{F}M
. In the sequel set
L_{U}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\Lie U)(k)
,
a
k
-vector space. Since
U^{\prime}
is a finite commutative group scheme, according to Fontaine
[
Fo77
, chap. III, 4.2]
there is a canonical isomorphism:

\eta_{U^{\prime}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}M^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}L_{U^{\prime}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}.

We deduce a composed isomorphism
\eta_{U}
as follows:

M/\mathbb{F}M\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}M^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}M^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta_{U^{\prime}}}}{{\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}}}L_{U^{\prime}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}L_{U}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}.

From the isomorphism of
k
-group schemes

\Lie U\simeq\mathbb{V}(L_{U}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})
, we deduce

M(\Lie U)=\Hom_{k}(\mathbb{V}(L_{U}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}),\mathbb{G}_{a})\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}L_{U}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\otimes k[\mathbb{F}].

By tensoring
\eta_{U}
with
k[\mathbb{F}]
, we find

\Lie M=(M/\mathbb{F}M)\otimes_{k}k[\mathbb{F}]\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}L_{U}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\otimes k[\mathbb{F}]\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}M(\Lie U).

The result for general
I
follows since

\Lie(U,I)\simeq\Lie U\otimes_{\mathbb{O}_{k}}\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})

and

\Lie(M,I)=\Lie M\otimes_{k}I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
.

\square

We can characterize the functor
\Lie

on Dieudonné modules by its values on the modules

\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n}
.

6.2.2. Proposition.

There exists a unique covariant functor

\mathscr{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}\Mod}^{e}\to{\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}\Mod}^{e}

with the following properties:

\mathscr{L}
is right exact and commutes with filtering
inductive limits;

\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n})=k[\mathbb{F}]^{n}
for all
n\geqslant 1
;

\mathscr{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\End(\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n})\to\End(k[\mathbb{F}]^{n})
sends

•

\mathbb{F}
to
0
;

•

\mathbb{V}
to the endomorphism

(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1})\mapsto(0,a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n-2})
;

•

and multiplication by
x=(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)\in W(k)

to the diagonal endomorphism with diagonal entries

(x_{0},x_{0}^{p},\dots,x_{0}^{p^{n-1}})
.

Uniqueness. The key
is the fact that
\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n}
is a projective generator of
the full subcategory
C_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=({\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}\Mod}^{e})^{\mathbb{V}^{n}=0}

of objects killed by
\mathbb{V}^{n}
. More precisely, since any
erasable
\mathbb{D}
-module is a filtering union of its submodules
of finite type, property (1) implies that
\mathscr{L}
is determined
by its restriction to the subcategory of modules of finite type.
Any finite type module
M
is killed by
\mathbb{V}^{n}
for some

n\geqslant 1
. Since
\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n}
is noetherian, for any

M\in C_{n}
there exist
r,s
and an exact sequence:

(\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n})^{s}\longrightarrow(\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n})^{r}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0,

and for any morphism
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\to M^{\prime}
in
C_{n}
there is a
commutative diagram:

\textstyle{(\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n})^{s}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{(\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n})^{r}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{(\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n})^{s^{\prime}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n})^{r^{\prime}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

Since
\mathscr{L}
is right exact, this implies that
\mathscr{L}(M)

is determined by the values of
\mathscr{L}((\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n})^{r})
for
variable
r
, and
\mathscr{L}(f)
is determined by the values
of
\mathscr{L}(g)
for variable
g
as above. Again since
\mathscr{L}

is right exact, it is additive. Hence
\mathscr{L}((\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n})^{r})

is determined by
\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n})
which is prescribed in (2).
Similarly
\mathscr{L}(g)
is determined by the values of
\mathscr{L}
on the
various maps

\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n}\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n})^{r}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n}
. Since
\End(\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n})=\mathbb{D}/\mathbb{D}\mathbb{V}^{n}

is generated by
W(k)
,
F
and
V
, the assignment in (3)
ensures uniqueness of
\mathscr{L}
.

Existence. Because of
Proposition
6.2
, it is enough to
check that the functors
\mathscr{L}(M(-))
and
M(\Lie(-))
take
the same values on the Witt groups
U=W_{n}
and the
endomorphisms of these groups. This is a simple computation
which is left to the reader.

\square

We come to the notion of smoothness.
It is known that a
k
-group scheme of finite type
U
is
smooth if and only if its relative Frobenius

\Fr_{U/k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}U\to U^{(p)}
is an epimorphism of
k
-group schemes.
This motivates the following definition.

6.2.3. Definition.

An erasable Dieudonné module
M
is called
smooth

if it is of finite type and its Frobenius morphism

\Fr_{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M^{(p)}\to M
is a monomorphism.

6.2.4. Definition.

An
I
-extension
in
{\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}\Mod}^{e}
is
an extension of smooth erasable Dieudonné modules of
the form

0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M^{\prime}\longrightarrow\Lie(M,I)\longrightarrow 0.

A
morphism of
I
-extensions
is a morphism of
extensions in the usual sense, that is, a morphism of
short exact sequences of
\mathbb{D}
-modules.

6.2.5. Theorem.

Let
\SCU/k[I]
be the category of smooth, commutative,
unipotent (i.e. with unipotent special fibre)

k[I]
-group schemes. Let

\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}I\mbox{-}\Mod
be the category of

I
-extensions of smooth erasable Dieudonné modules.
Then the Dieudonné functor
M
induces a contravariant
equivalence of categories:

\mathscr{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\SCU/k[I]\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}\mbox{-}I\mbox{-}\Mod

that sends
\mathscr{U}
to the extension

0\to\underline{M}(\mathscr{U}_{k})\to\underline{M}(h_{*}\mathscr{U}))\to\underline{M}(\Lie(\mathscr{U}_{k},I))\to 0
.
A quasi-inverse functor is obtained by sending an extension

0\to M\to M^{\prime}\to\Lie(M,I)\to 0
to the Weil extension

h^{\mbox{\rm\tiny+}}(\underline{U}(M^{\prime}))
of the extension

0\to\Lie(\underline{U}(M),I)\to\underline{U}(M^{\prime})\to\underline{U}(M)\to 0
, where
\underline{U}
is a quasi-inverse
for
\underline{M}
.

It suffices to put together Theorem
1.1

and Theorem
6.1
. In little more
detail, let
\mathscr{U}
be a smooth, commutative, unipotent group
scheme over the ring of dual numbers
k[I]
, and let
U=\mathscr{U}_{k}

be its special fibre. By Theorem
1.1

this datum is equivalent to an extension

0\longrightarrow\Lie(U,I)\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow 0

with
E=h_{*}\mathscr{U}
smooth, commutative, unipotent. By
Theorem
6.1
this is equivalent
to an extension

0\longrightarrow\underline{M}(U)\longrightarrow\underline{M}(E)\longrightarrow\underline{M}(\Lie(U,I))\longrightarrow 0.

Since

\Lie(\underline{M}(U),I)\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\underline{M}(\Lie(U,I))

by Proposition
6.2
,
we obtain an
I
-extension as desired.

\square

Appendix A. Differential calculus on group schemes

In this appendix
we review the notions of tangent bundle and Lie algebra in the
required generality. We introduce the exponential morphism
of a
k
-group scheme and we establish its main properties,
including some special properties needed in the paper. Finally
we show how the use of the group algebra allows to recover
easily the deformation theory of smooth affine group schemes.
Proofs are often omitted, especially in
A.1

and
A.2
.

A.1.
Tangent bundle and Lie algebra

Let
k
be a ring and
I
a free
k
-module of finite rank
r\geqslant 1

with dual
I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}=\Hom_{k}(I,k)
.
Let
k[I]
be the algebra of dual numbers, i.e.
k[I]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=k\oplus I

with multiplication determined by the condition
I^{2}=0
.
Let
h\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Spec(k[I])\to\Spec(k)
be the structure map and

i\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Spec(k)\to\Spec(k[I])
the closed immersion. Basic
structure facts on the ring schemes
\mathbb{O}_{k}
and
\mathbb{O}_{k[I]}

are recalled in Paragraph
2.1
.

Let
G
be a
k
-group scheme with unit section

e\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Spec(k)\to G
. The
tangent bundle of
G/k
relative
to
I
is the
k
-group scheme defined by:

\T(G,I)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=h_{*}h^{*}G.

The
(h^{*},h_{*})
adjunction (see
Subsection
1.3
) provides
two morphisms of group schemes:

\alpha_{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\longrightarrow\T(G,I)\quad,\quad\beta_{h^{*}G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h^{*}\T(G,I)=h^{*}h_{*}h^{*}G\longrightarrow h^{*}G.

From these we derive
\pi_{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=i^{*}\beta_{h^{*}G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\T(G,I)\to G

and the
Lie algebra of
G/k
relative to
I
:

\Lie(G,I)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\ker(\pi_{G}).

The map
\T(G,I)\to G
is a vector bundle which can be
described in terms of derivations. If we identify the

k
-module
I
with the corresponding locally free sheaf
on
\Spec(k)
, then for all points
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S\to G
with
values in a
k
-scheme
S
we have
(
[
SGA3.1
, Exposé II, Prop. 3.3]
):

\Hom_{G}(S,\T(G,I))\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\Der^{f}({\mathcal{O}}_{G},f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\otimes_{k}I).

In particular
\Lie(G,I)\to\Spec(k)
is an
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-Lie algebra
scheme such that for all
S/k
we have

\Hom_{k}(S,\Lie(G,I))\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\Der^{e}({\mathcal{O}}_{G},e_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\otimes_{k}I).

It supports the
adjoint representation
, i.e. the action
of
G
by conjugation inside
\T(G,I)
:

\Ad\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\to\GL(\Lie(G,I))\quad,\quad\Ad(g)(x)=\alpha_{G}(g)x\alpha_{G}(g)^{-1}

for all points
g
of
G
and
x
of
\Lie(G,I)
.
Applying the functor Lie, that is differentiating
at the unit section of
G
, we obtain the infinitesimal
adjoint representation of the Lie algebra:

\ad\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Lie G\to\End(\Lie(G,I))).

When
I=k\varepsilon
, from
\ad
we deduce the bilinear form
called
bracket

[-,-]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Lie G\times\Lie G\to\Lie G
.
That is, we have

[x,y]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\ad x)(y)
for all points
x,y
of
\Lie G
.

The tangent bundle
\T(G,I)
carries a structure of extension
as follows. By the triangle identity of adjunction,

h^{*}\alpha_{G}
is a section of
\beta_{h^{*}G}
, hence
\alpha_{G}

is a section of
\pi_{G}
. Letting
\gamma_{G}

be the inclusion of
\Lie(G,I)
into
\T(G,I)
,
we have a split exact sequence:

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Lie(G,I)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma_{G}}
\textstyle{\T(G,I)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{G}}
\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{G}}
\textstyle{1.}

This is an exact sequence of functors, hence
also an exact sequence of group schemes (i.e. of fppf
sheaves). Let
m
be the multiplication of
\T(G,I)
.
The splitting gives rise to an isomorphism of
k
-schemes:

\textstyle{\varrho_{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Lie(G,I)\times G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma_{G}\times\alpha_{G}}
\textstyle{\T(G,I)\times\T(G,I)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{m}
\textstyle{\T(G,I),}

that is, any point of
\T(G,I)
may be written uniquely as
a product
\gamma_{G}(x)\cdot\alpha_{G}(g)
for some points

x\in\Lie(G,I)
and
g\in G
. We will sometimes
write briefly
(x,g)=\gamma_{G}(x)\cdot\alpha_{G}(g)
to denote
this point of
\T(G,I)
. The conjugation action of
G
on

\Lie(G,I)
related
to the extension structure is given by the adjoint action,
thus the group structure of
\T(G,I)
can be described by:

(x,g)\cdot(x^{\prime},g^{\prime})=(x+\Ad(g)x^{\prime},gg^{\prime}).

The dependence of
\T(G,I)
and
\Lie(G,I)
on
I
can be
described further. If
I=k\varepsilon
so that
k[I]=k[\varepsilon]
with

\varepsilon^{2}=0
, we write simply
\T\!G=\T(G,k\varepsilon)
and

\Lie G=\Lie(G,k\varepsilon)
and we call them the
tangent bundle

and the
Lie algebra
of
G
. Let
u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\to\Spec(k)
be the structure map. For general
I
, the isomorphisms

\Der^{f}({\mathcal{O}}_{G},f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S})\otimes_{k}I\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\Der^{f}({\mathcal{O}}_{G},f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\otimes_{k}I)

functorial in
S/G
induce an isomorphism of vector bundles:

\T\!G\otimes_{\,\mathbb{O}_{G}}\!u^{*}\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\T(G,I).

Similarly, the isomorphisms
\Der({\mathcal{O}}_{G},f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S})\otimes_{k}I\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\Der({\mathcal{O}}_{G},f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\otimes_{k}I)

functorial in
S/k
induce an isomorphism of
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-Lie
algebra schemes:

\Lie G\otimes\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\Lie(G,I).

With
G
acting trivially on
\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})

A.3.
Deformations of affine group schemes

In this subsection, we illustrate the usefulness of the group
algebra in two ways. First, in
Proposition

we show how concepts of differential calculus can be handled
very conveniently using the group algebra. We include the
examples of the adjoint action and the Lie bracket. The
results of Proposition
A.2

can be derived painlessly in a similar fashion. Then
in Proposition

we show how
to recover directly the fact that isomorphism

of a smooth, affine

-group

Let

The tangent bundle

\mathbb{O}_{k}[G][I]=\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\oplus\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\!\cdot\!I

We have a commutative diagram of affine monoid schemes:

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\underset{}{\Lie G\otimes\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\underset{}{\T(G,I)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\underset{}{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{1}
\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\underset{}{\overset{}{(\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\!\cdot\!I,+)}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{a_{1}+a_{2}\mapsto a_{1}}
\textstyle{\overset{}{(\mathbb{O}_{k}[G],\star)}}

a_{2}\in\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\!\cdot\!I

The map

\Lie G\otimes\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\!\cdot\!I,+)

The adjoint action

A.3.1. Remark.

More generally, for all finite free
k

which can be expressed as the bracket defined by the

[x,y]=xy-yx

(1) By Proposition

we have an isomorphism of

\T(\mathbb{O}_{k}[G],I)=h_{*}h^{*}\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{O}_{k}}h_{*}\mathbb{O}_{k[I]}\simeq\mathbb{O}_{k}[G][I].

, and we have

\T\nu_{G}(f)=u+v\in\mathbb{O}_{k}[G][I](R).

This is the central vertical map in the pictured diagram.
The rest is clear.

(2) Using the inclusions of multiplicative monoids

\alpha_{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\hookrightarrow\T\!G

(3) In order to compute

A.3.2. Proposition.

Let
k
be a base ring and let
G
be an affine
k
-group scheme.

The set of
k[I]
-group scheme structures on the
scheme
h^{*}G
that lift the
k
-group scheme structure of
G

is in bijection with the set of 2-cocycles
c\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\times G\to\Lie G
.

The set of isomorphism classes of rigid
deformations of
G
over
k[I]
is in bijection with

\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\Lie(G,I))
,
the second group cohomology of
G
with coefficients in the adjoint
representation
\Lie(G,I)\simeq\Lie G\otimes\mathbb{V}(I^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})
.

(1) We want to deform the multiplication

m\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\times G\longrightarrow G,\quad(u,v)\mapsto uv
into a multiplication

\widetilde{m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h^{*}G\times h^{*}G\to h^{*}G
which by adjunction
we can view as a map:

G\times G\to h_{*}h^{*}G=\T(G,I),\quad(u,v)\mapsto u\odot v.

We use embeddings inside the group algebra as
in Proposition
A.3
; thus both
targets of
m
and
\widetilde{m}
are embedded in
\mathbb{O}_{k}[G][I]
.
The condition that
\widetilde{m}
equals

is that these morphisms agree after composition with the

\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{O}_{k}[G][I]\to\mathbb{O}_{k}[G]
. In other words the
condition is that for all points

, the element

equals 1 modulo
I
. Since this is also
a point of
\T(G,I)
, it belongs to
\Lie(G,I)
. Hence we can write

u\odot v=(1+c(u,v))uv

for some

. The associativity constraint

gives
the cocycle relation:

c(uv,w)+c(u,v)=c(u,vw)+uc(v,w)u^{-1}.

Conversely, if

is a 2-cocycle,
we define

u\odot v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(1+c(u,v))uv.

The cocycle identity gives the associativity of this map.
Moreover, inverses for this law exist and are given by the formula

. So we obtain a

-group scheme

.

(2) Let
\mathscr{G}
be a rigid deformation of
G
. Choosing an

gives rise to a cocycle

as explained in (1). Choosing another isomorphism

\varphi_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathscr{G}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}h^{*}G
, we have an automorphism

\xi=\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}^{-1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h^{*}G\to h^{*}G

which restricts to the identity on

. The map

obtained by adjunction is of the form

for some morphism
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\to\Lie(G,I)
.
This means that
\xi(u)=(1+\psi(u))u
.
We want to see how the multiplication is transformed by the change
of isomorphism. If

then

a short computation shows that that the map

to

[1+(\psi(u^{\prime}v^{\prime})+c(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})-\psi(u^{\prime})-\Ad(u^{\prime})\psi(v^{\prime}))]u^{\prime}v^{\prime}

We see that

and the class

does not depend on the

\mathscr{G}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}h^{*}G
. To obtain the inverse

and attaches the deformation

\square

Appendix B. Module stacks in groupoids

Both categories
\Gr\!/k[I]
and
\Ext(I)/k
are endowed
with the structure of
\mathbb{O}_{k}
-module stacks in
groupoids over
\Gr\!/k
and the purpose of this Appendix is
to explain what this means. In the two cases this seems to be a
well-known fact, but we were able to locate only very few
discussions of this topic in the literature. In fact, the
additive part of the structure, which goes by the name of a
“Picard category”, is well documented, a landmark being
Deligne’s exposé in
[
SGA4.3
]
, Exp. XVIII, § 1.4.
However, the linear part of the structure, that is the

\mathbb{O}_{k}
-scalar multiplication and its interplay with the
additive structure, is almost absent from the literature.
Subsections 2.3, 2.4
and 2.5 of Osserman
[
Os10
]
are a first step, but the
author writes:
Although it is possible to
[define scalar multiplication maps] on a categorical
level as we did with addition, expressing the proper conditions
for associativity and distributivity isomorphisms becomes
substantially more complicated
. Here we simply provide a
definition in due form. An extended version of the article
available on the authors’ webpage contains a treatment including
a few basic results to highlight the nontrivial features of
the theory.

We start with the definition of Picard categories. The
alternative phrase
commutative group groupoids
is a
more accurate name to refer to them, but we stick with the
traditional name.
In order to make the axioms reader-friendly, we adopt a
simplified description for the multifunctors involved, e.g.
the associativity isomorphism
a\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}T_{1}\to T_{2}
between
the trifunctors
T_{1},T_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}P\times P\times P\to P
given by

T_{1}=+\circ(+\times\id)
and
T_{2}=+\circ(\id\times+)

is given in the form of isomorphisms

a_{x,y,z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(x+y)+z\to x+(y+z)
functorial in
x,y,z\in P
.

B.0. Definition.

Let
P
be a category and
+\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}P\times P\to P
a bifunctor.

An
associativity constraint
for
+
is an
isomorphism of functors
a_{x,y,z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(x+y)+z\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}x+(y+z)

such that the pentagon axiom (
[
SGA4.3
]
, Exp. XVIII, 1.4.1)
is satisfied. It is called
trivial
or
strict

if
a_{x,y,z}=\id
for all
x,y,z\in P
.

A
commutativity constraint
for
+
is an
isomorphism of functors
c_{x,y}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}x+y\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}y+x
which
satisfies
c_{y,x}\circ c_{x,y}=\id_{x+y}
for all
x,y\in P
.
It is called
trivial
or
strict
if
c_{x,y}=\id

for all
x,y\in P
.

The associativity and commutativity constraints
a

and
c
are
compatible
if the hexagon axiom
(
[
SGA4.3
]
, Exp. XVIII, 1.4.1) is satisfied.

A
neutral element
for
+
is an object

0\in P
with an isomorphism
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}0+0\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}0
.

In other mathematical contexts, associativity constraints
are called
associators
and commutativity
constraints are called
symmetric braidings
.

B.1. Definition.

Let
(P_{1},+)
and
(P_{2},+)
be categories endowed with

Let
a_{1},a_{2}

and
(P_{2},+)

(F,\varphi_{F})

is

B.2. Definition.

A
Picard category
is a quadruple
(P,+,a,c)
composed
of a nonempty groupoid
P
, a bifunctor
+\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}P\times P\to P

with compatible associativity and commutativity constraints

a
and
c
, such that for each
x\in P

the functor
P\to P
,
y\mapsto x+y
is an equivalence.

Any Picard category
P
has a neutral element
0

which is unique up to a unique isomorphism
(
[
SGA4.3
]
, Exp. XVIII, 1.4.4). Moreover, for each

x,y\in P
the set of morphisms
\Hom(x,y)
is either empty
or a torsor under the group
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Aut(0)
. More precisely, the
functors
+x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}P\to P
and
x+\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}P\to P
induce the same
bijection
G\to\Aut(x)
,
\varphi\mapsto\varphi+\id_{x}
.
Viewing this bijection as an identification, the set

\Hom(x,y)
with its right
\Aut(x)
-action and left

\Aut(y)
-action becomes a pseudo-
G
-bitorsor, i.e.
it is either empty or a
G
-bitorsor.

B.3. Definition.

Let
P_{1},P_{2}
be Picard categories.

An
additive functor

is a pair
(F,\varphi_{F})
where
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}P_{1}\to P_{2}
is a
functor and
\varphi_{F,x,y}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}F(x+y)\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}F(x)+F(y)
is an
isomorphism of functors that is compatible with associativity
and commutativity constraints.

Let
F,G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}P_{1}\to P_{2}
be additive functors.
A
morphism of additive functors
is a morphism of functors

u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}F\to G
such that the following diagram is commutative:

\textstyle{F(x+y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{u_{x+y}}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{F}}
\textstyle{G(x+y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{G}}
\textstyle{F(x)+F(y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{u_{x}+u_{y}}
\textstyle{G(x)+G(y).}

We emphasize that since a Picard category is a groupoid
(that is, all its morphisms are isomorphisms), all
morphisms of additive functors
u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}F\to G
are isomorphisms.

The
category of additive functors

\Hom(P_{1},P_{2})

is itself a Picard category
(
[
SGA4.3
]
, Exp. XVIII, 1.4.7). Additive functors can be
composed and the identity functors behave as neutral elements.
In the particular case where
P_{1}=P_{2}=P
, along with its
addition law, the Picard category
\End(P)=\Hom(P,P)
enjoys
an internal multiplication given by composition. Note that
in this case multiplication is strictly associative,
because so is composition of functors in categories.

In fact
\End(P)
is a
ring category
, but in order
to introduce module groupoids, we do not
actually need to define what is such a thing.

B.4. Definition.

Let
\Lambda
be a commutative ring.
A
\Lambda
-module groupoid

For each

(F_{\lambda},\varphi_{F_{\lambda}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}P\to P

(F,\psi_{F})

The product of simple modules over KLR algebras and quiver Grassmannians
Yingjin Bi

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the product of two simple modules over KLR algebras using the quiver Grassmannians for Dynkin quivers. More precisely, we establish a bridge between the Induction functor on the category of modules over KLR algebras and the irreducible components of quiver Grassmannians for Dynkin quivers via a sort of extension varieties, which is an analog of the extension group in Hall algebras. As a result, we give a necessary condition when the product of two simple modules over a KLR algebra is simple using the set of irreducible components of quiver Grassmannians. In particular, in some special cases, we provide proof for the conjecture recently proposed by Lapid and Minguez.

1.
Introduction

Let
\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}
be a simple Lie algebra of simply laced type and
U_{q}(\operatorname{\mathfrak{n}})
be the half-part of the corresponding quantum group. In
[
Lus91
]
, Lusztig introduced the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}
of
U_{q}(\operatorname{\mathfrak{n}})
via the geometrization of
U_{q}(\operatorname{\mathfrak{n}})
. We call the dual of
\mathbf{B}
the dual canonical basis, which is denoted by
\mathbf{B}^{*}
. The Hopf algebra
U_{q}(\operatorname{\mathfrak{n}})
induces a multiplication on its dual space. We denote by
A_{q}(\operatorname{\mathfrak{n}})
the dual space of
U_{q}(\operatorname{\mathfrak{n}})
.

It is crucial to study the product of two dual canonical basis elements. For instance, in
[
BZ92
]
Berenstein and Zelevinsky conjectured that the product of two dual canonical basis elements
\mathbf{b}_{1}^{*}
and
\mathbf{b}_{2}^{*}
, up to some
q
-power scaling, belongs in
\mathbf{B}^{*}
if and only if
\mathbf{b}_{1}^{*}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{*}=q^{n}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{*}\mathbf{b}_{1}^{*}
for some integer
n
. However, Leclerc showed that this conjecture is not true in general, (see
[
Lec03
]
), and then proposed the notion of real dual canonical base elements
\mathbf{b}^{*}
,
i.e.

\mathbf{b}^{*}\mathbf{b}^{*}\in q^{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{B}^{*}
. To modify
[
BZ92
, Conjecture 1.7]
, Leclerc proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
Lec03
, Conjecture 3.1]

Let
\mathbf{b}_{1}^{*}
and
\mathbf{b}_{2}^{*}
be two dual canonical base elements of
\mathcal{A}_{q}(\operatorname{\mathfrak{n}})
such that one of them is real. Suppose that
\mathbf{b}_{1}^{*}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{*}\notin q^{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{B}^{*}
. The expression of
\mathbf{b}_{1}^{*}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{*}
is of the following form:

(1.1)

\mathbf{b}_{1}^{*}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{*}=q^{m}\mathbf{b}^{\prime*}+q^{s}\mathbf{b}^{\prime\prime*}+\sum_{\mathbf{b}^{*}\neq\mathbf{b}^{\prime*},\mathbf{b}^{\prime\prime*}}\gamma_{\mathbf{b}_{1}^{*},\mathbf{b}_{2}^{*}}^{\mathbf{b}^{*}}(q)\mathbf{b}^{*}

where
\mathbf{b}^{\prime*}\neq\mathbf{b}^{\prime\prime*}
and
m<s\in\mathbb{Z}
, and for any
\mathbf{b}^{*}\neq\mathbf{b}^{\prime*},\mathbf{b}^{\prime\prime*}
, we have

(1.2)

\gamma_{\mathbf{b}_{1}^{*},\mathbf{b}_{2}^{*}}^{\mathbf{b}^{*}}(q)\in q^{m+1}\mathbb{Z}[q]\cap q^{s-1}\mathbb{Z}[q^{-1}].

One of the powerful approaches is to categorify of the algebra
U_{q}(\operatorname{\mathfrak{n}})
( or
A_{q}(\operatorname{\mathfrak{n}})
) using the categories of modules over quiver Hecke algebras
R_{Q}
, which is introduced by Khovanov and Lauda in
[
KL09
]
. This allows us to regard the dual canonical base elements as the simple modules over
R_{Q}
, see
[
VV11
]
for more details.

Based on these results, Kang, Kashiwara, Kim, and Oh use the notion of
R
-matrix to prove Leclerc’s conjecture
1.1
, (see
[
KKKO18
]
). Meanwhile, Lapid and Minguez proposed the
[
LM22
, Conjecture 5.1]
, which states that
\mathbf{b}^{\prime*}
and
\mathbf{b}^{\prime\prime*}
are related to the generic extensions of irreducible components of Lusztig’s nilpotent varieties. In other words, they study the product of two dual canonical basis elements in terms of representations of preprojective algebras.

However, the
R
-matrices on the tensor product of two simple modules over
R
can’t reveal the explicit knowledge of basis
\mathbf{B}^{*}
, such as the combinatorics knowledge of
\mathbf{B}^{*}
. The natural indexing of dual canonical basis is the set of Kostant partitions. It is natural to ask the following question: How to describe the condition
\mathbf{b}_{1}^{*}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{*}\in q^{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{B}^{*}
in terms of the Kostant partition indexing.

Before starting this, let us review some notations concerning this topic. Let us consider an element
\alpha
in
Q^{+}:=\mathbb{N}[\alpha_{i}]_{i\in I}
and denote by
\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})
the set of Kostant partitions indexing the isomorphic class (up to shifted) of simple modules over the algebra
R_{\alpha}
,
i.e.
the
\alpha
-part of
R_{Q}
. We remark that
\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})
can be thought of as the set of isomorphic class of representations of
Q
with dimension vector
\alpha
. Here is our question:

Problem 1.2
.

Let
{\alpha,\beta}
be two elements in
Q^{+}
and
({\mu,\nu})\in\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})\times\operatorname{KP}({\beta})
be a pair of Kostant partitions. Describe the condition on the pair
({\mu,\nu})
such that the Induction
L(\mu)\circ L(\nu)
of the simple modules
L(\mu)
and
L(\nu)
is also a simple module.

This question is proposed by Kleshchev and Ram in
[
KR11
, Problem 7.6]
. But they used the words of the set of vertices of
Q
to describe this condition rather than the Kostant partitions. In type
A_{n}
, Lapid and Minguez use the combinatorial conditions of multisegments to deal with this problem, see
[
LM18
]
,
[
LM20
]
,
[
LM22
]
for more details. Inspired by their works, we will use the representation theory of quivers to describe the condition in the Dynkin case.

The goal of the present paper is to establish a bridge between representations of
Q
and modules of
R_{Q}
. More precisely, we will answer Problem
1.2
in terms of the irreducible components of quiver Grassmannians for the quiver
Q
. We will also give a sufficient condition when
q^{n}L(\mu\star\nu)\cong\operatorname{soc}L({\mu})\circ L({\nu})
for some integer
n
in terms of the irreducible components of quiver Grassmannians. In other words, we give a proof for
[
LM22
, Conjecture 5.1]
in some special cases. Let me explain this more explicitly.

First, let
\gamma\in Q^{+}
and a partition
\gamma={\alpha+\beta}
, the set
\operatorname{KP}({\gamma})
is identified with the set of
G_{\gamma}
-orbits in
E_{\gamma}
in Dynkin cases. We define a
partial ordering
on
\operatorname{KP}({\gamma})
so that
\lambda^{\prime}\leq\lambda
if and only if
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}\subset\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
where
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}
refers to the
G_{\gamma}
-orbit corresponding to
\lambda
in
E_{\gamma}
and
\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
refers to closure of the orbit
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
(see
[
CB93
]
for more details). For a pair
({\mu,\nu})\in\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})\times\operatorname{KP}({\beta})
, one defines
(\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})\leq({\mu,\nu})
by
\mu^{\prime}\leq\mu
and
\nu^{\prime}\leq\nu
.

Let us define the
quiver Grassmannian
of the representation
M_{\lambda}
associated with
\lambda\in\operatorname{KP}({\gamma})
. Roughly speaking, it is a variety classifying the subrepresentations of
M_{\lambda}
with dimension vector
\beta
. Namely,

{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})=\left\{{W\in{\operatorname{Gr}}({\beta,\gamma})}\mid\,{\text{ $M_{\lambda}(W)\subset W$ }}\right\}

where
{\operatorname{Gr}}({\beta,\gamma})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\operatorname{Gr}}(\beta_{i},\gamma_{i})
is the product of Grassmannians of the vector space
\mathbb{C}^{\gamma_{i}}
with dimension
\beta_{i}
for all
i\in[1,n]
. Here we write
\beta=\sum_{i\in I}\beta_{i}\alpha_{i}
and
\gamma=\sum_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}\alpha_{i}
. It is well-known that
{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})
is a projective variety.

Let us consider the set of the irreducible components of
{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})
: Following
[
CER12
, Lemma 2.4]
, we have

(1.3)

{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})=\bigcup_{({\mu,\nu})\in\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})\times\operatorname{KP}({\beta})}\overline{{\operatorname{Gr}}({\mu,\nu},\lambda)}

where
{\operatorname{Gr}}({\mu,\nu},\lambda)
refers to the subvariety consisting of
W\in{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})
such that the restriction of
M_{\lambda}
to
W
is isomorphic to
M_{\nu}
,
i.e.

(M_{\lambda})_{\mid W}\cong M_{\nu}
and the restriction of
M_{\lambda}
to the quotient space
V/W
is isomorphic to
M_{\mu}
, namely,
(M_{\lambda})_{\mid V/W}\cong M_{\mu}
, and
\overline{{\operatorname{Gr}}({\mu,\nu},\lambda)}
refers to its Zarisky closure.

In order to describe the set of irreducible components of
{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})
, we introduce the notion of generic pairs. Following
[
CFR13
, Definition 7.3]
, we call
({\mu,\nu})
a
generic pair
of
\lambda
if there exists no
\nu^{\prime}<\nu
such that
(M_{\lambda})_{W^{\prime}}\cong M_{\nu^{\prime}}
and
(M_{\lambda})_{V/W^{\prime}}\cong M_{\mu}
for some subspace
W^{\prime}\in{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})
and there exists no
\mu^{\prime}<\mu
such that
(M_{\lambda})_{W^{\prime}}\cong M_{\nu}
and
(M_{\lambda})_{V/W^{\prime}}\cong M_{\mu^{\prime}}
for some subspace
W^{\prime\prime}\in{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})
. Thanks to Professor Cerulli Irelli and Reineke, we have the following Lemma

Lemma 1.3
.

[
Cerulli Irelli and Reineke
]
Under the above assumption, the set of irreducible components of
{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})
is identified with the following set

\operatorname{ext}_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{ger}(\lambda)=\left\{(\mu,\nu)\in\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})\times\operatorname{KP}({\beta})\,\middle|\,\begin{minipage}[220.50885pt]$({\mu,\nu})$ is a generic pair of $\lambda$ and\\
$\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{Q}({M_{\nu}},{M_{\lambda}})=\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{Q}({M_{\nu}},{M_{\nu}})+\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{Q}({M_{\nu}},{M_{\mu}})$
\end{minipage}\right\}

Here we introduce the main notion of this paper.

Definition 1.4
.

We call a pair
({\mu,\nu})\in\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})\times\operatorname{KP}({\beta})
a
support pair
if for any
\lambda<\mu\oplus\nu
we have
(\mu,\nu)\notin\operatorname{ext}_{\alpha,\beta}^{ger}(\lambda)
. This notion is inspired by the support of the image of simple perverse sheaf
\operatorname{IC}(\lambda)
for
\lambda
under the Restriction functor.

Here is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
Q
be a Dynkin quiver and
L(\mu)
and
L(\nu)
be two simple modules over quiver Hecke algebra
R_{Q}
, where
\mu,\nu
are their Kostant partitions respectively. If
L(\mu)\circ L(\nu)
is a simple module, then
({\mu,\nu})
is a support pair. In particular, for any non-trivial extension
\lambda\in\operatorname{ext}({\mu,\nu})
, then we have

\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{Q}(M_{\nu},M_{\mu}\oplus M_{\nu})>\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{Q}(M_{\nu},M_{\lambda})\text{ if $({\mu,\nu})$ is a generic pair for $\lambda$,}

and

\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{Q}(M_{\mu},M_{\mu}\oplus M_{\nu})>\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{Q}(M_{\mu},M_{\lambda})\text{ if $(\nu,\mu)$ is a generic pair for $\lambda$,}

Remark 1.6
.

This theorem reveals a relationship between representations of
Q
and the Induction of two simple modules of
R_{Q}
. We see that the condition that
L({\mu})\circ L({\nu})
is a simple module is a quite subtle condition. It is difficult to describe this condition in general.

Let us go back to Conjecture
1.1
. Based on
[
LM22
, Conjecture 5.1]
, we consider the generic extension
\mu*\nu
of any pair
({\mu,\nu})\in\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})\times\operatorname{KP}({\beta})
. Namely, the module
M_{\mu*\nu}
satisfies
M_{\mu*\nu}\in\operatorname{Ext}_{Q}^{1}({M_{\mu}},{M_{\nu}})
and
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ext}_{Q}^{1}({M_{\mu*\nu}},{M_{\mu*\nu}})
is minimal with respect to all elements in
\operatorname{Ext}_{Q}^{1}({M_{\mu}},{M_{\nu}})
. We remark that
\mu*\nu
always less than or equal to
\mu\star\nu
in the sense of Lapid and Minguez, but
\mu*\nu=\mu\star\nu
holds in most of the examples considered in
[
LM22
]
. Therefore, the following theorem gives an answer to that conjecture in some special cases.

Theorem 1.7
.

Under the above assumption, if
({\mu,\nu})\in\operatorname{ext}_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{ger}(\mu*\nu)
, then
q^{n}L(\mu*\nu)
is a submodule of
L(\mu)\circ L(\nu)
for some integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 1.8
.

The difficulty is to connect the representations of
Q
and modules of
\Lambda_{Q}
. After obtaining this connection, it is possible to give an answer to that conjecture. Although this theorem is not good enough to prove
[
LM22
, Conjecture 5.1]
, this is an attempt to prove this conjecture.

The strategy used in this paper

(1)

Firstly, we transform the product
L({\lambda})\circ L({\mu})
into the Induction functor on the indecomposable projective modules over
R_{Q}
:
\mathop{\text{\rm ind}}\nolimits_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{{\alpha+\beta}}\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
via the bilinear paring
[
KL09
, Section 2.5]
.

(2)

Second, we transform
\mathop{\text{\rm ind}}\nolimits_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{{\alpha+\beta}}\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
into the Induction functor on simple perverse sheaves on representation space:
\operatorname{Res}_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{IC}(\nu)
via the results in
[
VV11
]
and
[
McN17
]
.

(3)

Thirdly, we study the geometric properties of
\operatorname{Res}_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{IC}(\nu)
given in
[
Sch09
]
,
[
Lus91
]
and
[
Lus93
]
using the graded quiver varieties as in
[
LP13
]
and
[
VV03
]

(4)

At last we connect the
\operatorname{Res}_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{{\alpha+\beta}}\operatorname{IC}(\nu)
with quiver Grassmannians given in
[
Rei01
]
and
[
CER12
]
.

2.
Premise

In this section, we recall some basic facts on quiver representation theory for Dynkin quivers.

2.1.
Notations

In this paper, a quiver
Q=(I,\Omega)
consists of a set of vertices
I
and a set of arrows
\Omega
. Denote by
n
the number of vertices if there is no danger of confusion. For an arrow
h
, one denotes its source and target by
s(h)
and
t(h)
, respectively. For our purposes, we often assume that
Q
is of Dynkin type. We assume that the vertex set
I
is endowed with an order so that
k<l
if there exists an arrow from
i_{k}
to
i_{l}
.

Let
C_{Q}
be the Cartan matrix for
Q
, simply write
C
.
We write
\alpha_{i}
for its simple roots and
Q^{+}=\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{i}]_{i\in I}
for the monoid generated by
\alpha_{i}
for all
i\in I
. The matrix
C
induces a bilinear form on
Q
, which is denoted by
(-,-)
. Set another bilinear form on
Q^{+}
by

(2.1)

\langle{\alpha},{\beta}\rangle=\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}-\sum_{h\in\Omega}\alpha_{s(h)}\beta_{t(h)}

It is well known that
({\alpha,\beta})=\langle{\alpha},{\beta}\rangle+\langle{\beta},{\alpha}\rangle
. We write
W
for its Weyl group generated by simple reflections
s_{i}
associated with simple roots
\alpha_{i}
for any
i\in I
. For any
w\in W
, one denotes by
l(w)
the length of
w
.
R^{+}
denotes the set of positive roots. The ground field in this paper is the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
, we sometimes write
k
for simplicity.

Let
w_{0}\in W
be the longest element in
W
. For a reduced expression of
w_{0}={s}_{i_{m}}\cdots{s}_{i_{2}}{s}_{i_{1}}
which is source-adapted with
\Omega
, we obtain the positive root set
R^{+}
by

(2.2)

\alpha_{i_{1}},\ s_{i_{1}}(\alpha_{i_{2}}),\ \cdots,\ {s}_{i_{1}}{s}_{i_{2}}\cdots{s}_{i_{k-1}}(\alpha_{i_{k}}),\ \cdots,\ {s}_{i_{1}}{s}_{i_{2}}\cdots{s}_{i_{m-1}}(\alpha_{i_{m}}).

Let us denote them by
\beta_{k}={s}_{i_{1}}{s}_{i_{2}}\cdots{s}_{i_{k-1}}(\alpha_{i_{k}})
for
1\leq k\leq m
and give them an order so that
\beta_{l}<\beta_{k}
if and only if
l<k
. In other words, a reduced expression yields an ordering on
R^{+}
.

2.2.
Representations of Dynkin quivers

Let
M=(V,x)
be a representation of
Q
where
V
is a
I
-graded vector space and
x=(x_{h})_{h\in\Omega}
is a tuple of matrices
x_{h}:\,V_{s(h)}\to V_{t(h)}
. We sometimes write the matrices
x
for the representation
M
if there is no danger of confusion. For a representation
M=(V,x)
, one denote by
\underline{\operatorname{dim}}M
its dimension vector
\sum_{i\in I}\operatorname{dim}(V_{i})\alpha_{i}\in Q^{+}
. Given two representations
M,N
of
Q
, we denote by
\operatorname{Hom}_{Q}(M,N)
the vector space of
Q
-morphisms between
M
and
N
and write
[M,N]
(resp.
[M,N]^{1}
) for
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{Q}(M,N)
(resp.
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{Q}(M,N)
). One has

(2.3)

\displaystyle\langle\underline{\operatorname{dim}}M,\underline{\operatorname{dim}}N\rangle=[M,N]-[M,N]^{1}.

For any vertex
i\in I
, we associate the indecomposable projective (resp. injective) representation
P_{i}
(resp.
I_{i}
).

Denote by
M_{\beta}
the indecomposable module corresponding to the root
\beta
. If the order of positive roots is given as before, it follows

(2.4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{Q}(M_{\beta_{a}},M_{\beta_{b}})=\operatorname{Ext}_{Q}(M_{\beta_{b}},M_{\beta_{a}})=0\qquad\text{for $\beta_{a}<\beta_{b}$}.

Let
\alpha\in Q^{+}
and
V
be a
I
-graded space such that
\underline{\operatorname{dim}}V=\alpha
, one denotes by
G_{\alpha}
the group
\prod_{i\in I}{\operatorname{GL}}(V_{i})
and by
E_{\alpha}
the representation space
\oplus_{h\in\Omega}\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(V_{s(h)},V_{t(h)})
endowed with a
G_{\alpha}
action by
g\cdot x=(g_{t(h)}x_{h}g_{s(h)}^{-1})_{h\in\Omega}
. For two
I
-graded vector spaces
V,W
, we denote by
\operatorname{Hom}_{I}(W,V)
the space
\oplus_{i\in I}\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(W_{i},V_{i})
and by
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Omega}(W,V)
the space
\oplus_{h\in\Omega}\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(W_{s(h)},V_{t(h)})
.

2.3.
Kostant Partitions

Consider the set of the
G_{\alpha}
-orbits in
E_{\alpha}
, which is denoted by
\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})
. For an element
\lambda\in\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})
, we denote by
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}
the
G_{\alpha}
-orbit corresponding to
\lambda
, and write
d_{\lambda}
for the dimension of the orbit
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}
.

One defines an ordering on
\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})
so that
\lambda^{\prime}\leq\lambda
if
\mathbb{O}_{\lambda}\subset\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
. Let us recall some properties of
\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})
. In Dykin cases, by Gabriel’s Theorem, one can write any element
\lambda
in
\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})
as

(2.5)

\lambda=({\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{2},\cdots,{\lambda}_{s})

where
\lambda_{k}\in R^{+}
for any
k\in[1,s]
such that
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{s}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}=\alpha
. We call
\lambda
as in (
2.5
) a
Kostant partition
of
\alpha
. Denote by
M_{\lambda}
the module corresponding to the Kostant partition
\lambda\in\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})
.

Example 2.1
.

Consider the type
A_{n}
, and fix an orientation of
A_{n}
as follows:

(2.6)

1\longrightarrow 2\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow n

The set
R^{+}
of positive roots coincides with the set of segments
[a,b]
such that
a\leq b
. The ordering of
R^{+}
is given by

[i,j]<[k,l]\ \text{if $i<k$ or $i=k$ and $j<l$}

Any indecomposable representations of
A_{n}
are of the form
M[a,b]
such that

\underline{\operatorname{dim}}{}M[a,b]=\sum\limits_{a\leq i\leq b}\alpha_{i}.

2.4.
Extension of Kostant partitions

In this section, we study the extension groups of representations of
Q
in terms of Kostant partitions.
Let
{\alpha,\beta},\gamma\in Q^{+}
such that
{\alpha+\beta}=\gamma
, let
\mu\in\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})
and
\nu\in\operatorname{KP}({\beta})
. We write
\operatorname{ext}({\mu,\nu})
for the subset of
\operatorname{KP}({\gamma})
consisting of Kostant partitions
\lambda
such that

(2.7)

0\to M_{\nu}\to M_{\lambda}\to M_{\mu}\to 0

In other words,
\operatorname{ext}({\mu,\nu})
is the Kostant partitions whose modules in
\operatorname{Ext}_{Q}^{1}({M_{\mu}},{M_{\nu}})
.

Similarly, for a Kostant partition
\lambda
, we define
\operatorname{ext}_{{\alpha,\beta}}(\lambda)
as the subset of
\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})\times\operatorname{KP}({\beta})
consisting of pairs
({\mu,\nu})
satisfies condition (
2.7
).

Definition 2.2
.

For a
\lambda\in\operatorname{KP}({\gamma})
and a partition
\gamma={\alpha+\beta}
, we define
\operatorname{ext}_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{min}}({\alpha,\beta})
by

\qquad\quad\operatorname{ext}_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{min}}({\alpha,\beta}):=\left\{(\mu,\nu)\in\operatorname{ext}_{{\alpha,\beta}}(\lambda)\,\middle|\,\begin{minipage}[220.50885pt]there is no pair $(\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})\in\operatorname{ext}_{{\alpha,\beta}}(\lambda)$
satisfying $(\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})<(\mu,\nu)$
\end{minipage}\right\}

Here by
(\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})<({\mu,\nu})
, we mean the condition
(\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})\leq({\mu,\nu})
and
(\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})\neq({\mu,\nu})
.

Example 2.3
.

Given a Kostant partition
\lambda\in\operatorname{KP}({\gamma})
. If the representation
M_{\lambda}
admits a decomposition
M_{\lambda}=M_{\mu}\oplus M_{\nu}
such that
\mu\in\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})
and
\nu\in\operatorname{KP}({\beta})
, then it is easy to see that

({\mu,\nu})\in\operatorname{ext}_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{\operatorname{min}}(\lambda)

However, the elements in
\operatorname{ext}_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{\operatorname{min}}(\lambda)
don’t satisfy
M_{\lambda}=M_{\mu}\oplus M_{\nu}
in general. For example: Consider the case
A_{3}
, let
\lambda=[1,3]+[2,2]
and
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
and
\beta=\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}
. It is easy to see that
\operatorname{ext}_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{\operatorname{min}}(\lambda)=\{([1,2],[2,3])\}
.

3.
Hall maps for Representation spaces

We will next review the Hall maps for representation spaces (see
[
Sch09

4.2.
Irreducible components of quiver Grassmannians

In this section, we will consider the set of irreducible components of quiver Grassmannians. Let us fix a partition of
\gamma={\alpha+\beta}
and a Kostant partition
\lambda\in\operatorname{KP}({\gamma})
. By
[

]
, the irreducible components of
{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})
are the closure of the following subvarieties

for some

. Similarly, these irreducible components are the closure of the subvariety

. Therefore, for such a

, there exists a

such that

\overline{S}_{N}=\overline{T}_{M}

In this case, the subvariety

is an open subvariety of the component
\overline{S}_{N}
.

Definition 4.6
.

We define a pair
({\mu,\nu})
as a
component pair
of
\lambda
if
\overline{S}_{M_{\nu}}=\overline{T}_{M_{\mu}}
is a component of
{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})
. Therefore, the set of irreducible components of

is equal to the set of component pairs
({\mu,\nu})
of

. We denote it by
\operatorname{ext}_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{ger}(\lambda)

Remark 4.7
.

Note that for any component pair
({\mu,\nu})
we see that
M_{\mu}
is the generic quotient of
M_{\lambda}
by
M_{\nu}
,
i.e.
there exists no
\mu^{\prime}<\mu
such that

0\to M_{\nu}\to M_{\lambda}\to M_{\mu^{\prime}}\to 0

and
M_{\nu}
is the generic subrepresentation of
M_{\lambda}
with quotient
M_{\mu}
,
i.e.
there exists no
\nu^{\prime}<\nu
such that

We call
({\mu,\nu})
a
generic pair
if
M_{\mu}
is the generic quotient of
M_{\lambda}
by
M_{\nu}
and
M_{\nu}
is the generic subrepresentation of
M_{\lambda}
with quotient
M_{\mu}
. Therefore, a component pair is a generic pair. But a generic pair is not a component pair in general.

Thanks to Cerulli Irelli and Reineke, the following lemma gives a description of component pairs.

Lemma 4.8
.

[
Cerulli Irelli and Reineke
]
Let
{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})
be the Grassmannian quiver for representation
M_{\lambda}
, the subvariety
\overline{S}_{N}
is an irreducible component of
{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})
if and only if

(4.8)

[N,M_{\lambda}]-[N,N]=[N,T]

here
T
is the generic quotient of
M_{\lambda}
by
N
, (see Remark
4.7
).

Proof.

Let us assume that
[N,M_{\lambda}]-[N,N]=[N,T]
. It is well known that
\operatorname{dim}\overline{S}_{N}=[N,M_{\lambda}]-[N,N]
and tangent space at
N
is
T_{N}{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})=\operatorname{Hom}_{Q}({N},{T})
(see
[
CER12
, Lemma 2.3;2.4]
). Since

[N,T]=\operatorname{dim}T_{N}{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})\geq\operatorname{dim}T_{N}\overline{S}_{N}\geq\operatorname{dim}\overline{S}_{N}=[N,M_{\lambda}]-[N,N]

then the equation (

) implies that

is a nonsigular point in
\overline{S}_{N}
. Meanwhile, by
[
Hub17
, Lemma 3.1]
, the identity
\operatorname{dim}T_{N}{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})=\operatorname{dim}T_{N}\overline{S}_{N}
implies that
\overline{S}_{N}
is an irreducible component of
{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})
.

If
\overline{S}_{N}
is an irreducible component of
{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})
, let us take a nonsingular point

in
\overline{S}_{N}
. This means
\operatorname{dim}T_{g\cdot N}\overline{S}_{N}=\operatorname{dim}T_{g\cdot N}{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\beta}(M_{\lambda})=[N,T]
. The fact that

is a nonsingular point implies
\operatorname{dim}T_{g\cdot N}\overline{S}_{N}=\operatorname{dim}\overline{S}_{N}=[N,M_{\lambda}]-[N,N]
. Hence, we obtain

[N,M_{\lambda}]-[N,N]=[N,T]

∎

Therefore, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4.9
.

[
Cerulli Irelli and Reineke
]

Proof.

It follows from the above lemma.

6.1.
Lusztig’s sheaves

Let
\alpha\in Q^{+}
and
\langle I\rangle_{\alpha}
be the set of words
[{i}_{1}{i}_{2}\cdots{i}_{r}]
of
I

\sum_{k=1}^{r}\alpha_{i_{k}}=\alpha

\mathbf{i}=[{i}_{1}{i}_{2}\cdots{i}_{r}]\in\langle I\rangle_{\alpha}

(6.1)

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{i}}:=\{(0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\cdots\subset V_{r-1}\subset V_{r}=V)\mid\,\text{ such that $\operatorname{dim}V_{k}/V_{k-1}=\alpha_{i_{k}}$}\}

There exists a vector bundle over
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{i}}
, which is defined as follows:

(6.2)

\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\mathbf{i}}:=\{(\phi,x)\mid\,\text{ $\phi\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{i}}$ and $x\in E_{\alpha}(Q)$ such that $x(V_{k})\subset V_{k}$}\}

It yields a proper map:

(6.3)

\begin{split}p:\,\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\mathbf{i}}&\to E_{\alpha}(Q)\\
(\phi,x)&\mapsto x\end{split}

Let’s revisit the perverse sheaves on the representation variety
E_{\alpha}(Q)
. As both
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\mathbf{i}}
and

are smooth spaces, there exist constant perverse sheaves on them:

for a parameter
h_{\mathbf{i}}
, known as Lusztig’s sheaf. For simplicity, we will not delve into the specifics of
h_{\mathbf{i}}
in this discussion. According to the BBD Decomposition theorem, Lusztig’s sheaves can be expressed as a direct sum of simple perverse sheaves on
E_{\alpha}(Q)
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
denote the collection of simple perverse sheaves present in a component of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{i}}
for all word
\mathbf{i}\in\langle I\rangle_{\alpha}
. We will denote this collection as
\mathcal{P}
when there is no risk of confusion. Since the simple perverse sheaves can be represented as
\operatorname{IC}(X,\lambda)
where

denotes locally closed smooth subspaces of
E_{\alpha}(Q)
,
\lambda
represents a local system on
X
, and
\operatorname{IC}(X,\lambda)
is the simple perverse sheaf corresponding to them. To simplify matters, we will refer to
\lambda
or
\operatorname{IC}(\lambda)
instead of
\operatorname{IC}(X,\lambda)
. In a word, we get

(6.4)

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{i}}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}L_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda)\boxtimes\operatorname{IC}(\lambda)

where
L_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda)
are
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector spaces. We denote by
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
the semisimple full subcategory of

generated by
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
.

In particular, in Dynkin cases, all the simple perverse sheaves are of the form

7.
Quiver Hecke algebras

Let us first recall the notion of KLR algebras. Set
m_{i,j}
as the number of arrows from
i
to
j
for
i,j\in I
, and

q_{i,j}(u,v)=\begin{cases}0&\text{$i=j$}\\
(v-u)^{m_{i,j}}(u-v)^{m_{j,i}}&\text{$i\neq j$}\end{cases}

Definition 7.1
.

Let
\alpha=\sum_{i}c_{i}\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{N}[I]
and denote
ht(\alpha)=\sum_{i}c_{i}=n
. The
KLR algebra
(quiver Hecke algebra)
R_{\alpha}
is an associative
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm}]
-algebra generated by

\{e_{\mathbf{i}}|\mathbf{i}\in\langle I\rangle_{\alpha}\}\cup\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}\cup\{\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{n-1}\}

subject to the following relations

•

x_{k}x_{l}=x_{l}x_{k}
;

•

the elements
\{e_{\textbf{i}}\mid\textbf{i}\in\langle I\rangle_{\alpha}\}
are mutually orthogonal idempotents whose sum is the identity
e_{\alpha}\in R_{\alpha}
;

•

x_{k}e_{\textbf{i}}=e_{\textbf{i}}x_{k}
and
\tau_{k}e_{\textbf{i}}=e_{t_{k}\textbf{i}}\tau_{k}
;

•

(\tau_{k}x_{l}-x_{t_{k}(l)}\tau_{k})e_{\textbf{i}}=\delta_{i_{k},i_{k+1}}(\delta_{k+1,l}-\delta_{k,l})e_{\mathbf{i}}

•

\tau_{k}^{2}e_{\textbf{i}}=q_{i_{k},i_{k+1}}(x_{k},x_{k+1})e_{\mathbf{i}}

•

\tau_{k}\tau_{l}=\tau_{l}\tau_{k}\ \text{if $\mid k-l\mid>1$}

•

(\tau_{k+1}\tau_{k}\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k}\tau_{k+1}\tau_{k})e_{\textbf{i}}=\delta_{i_{k},i_{k+2}}\frac{q_{i_{k},i_{k+1}}(x_{k},x_{k+1})-q_{i_{k},i_{k+1}}(x_{k+2},x_{k+1})}{x_{k}-x_{k+2}}e_{\mathbf{i}}

where
t_{k}
is the simple translation of
n
such that
t_{k}(k)=k+1,t_{k}(k+1)=k
and
t_{k}(l)=l
for
l\neq k,\,k+1
.

We remark that
R_{\alpha}
is an associated
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebra by setting
\deg x_{i}=2
and
\deg\tau_{k}e_{\mathbf{i}}=-(\alpha_{i_{k}},\alpha_{i_{k+1}})
. Therefore, we can consider the

-graded modules over

. For a locally finite dimensional module

\operatorname{Dim}M=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{dim}M_{i}q^{i}

And set

so that

for all

, which leads to the formula

\operatorname{Dim}M[1]=q\operatorname{Dim}M

\hom(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{j}}\boxtimes\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{k}},\operatorname{Res}_{{\alpha,\beta}}(M))=e_{\mathbf{j}}e_{\mathbf{k}}M=\hom(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{j}}\star\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{k}},M)=\hom(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{j}\mathbf{k}},M)

then for any two projective modules
\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{P}_{2}
, we obtain

(7.8)

\hom(\mathbb{P}_{1}\boxtimes\mathbb{P}_{2},\operatorname{Res}_{{\alpha,\beta}}(M))=\hom(\mathbb{P}_{1}\star\mathbb{P}_{2},M)

7.3.
Two product of simple modules

We will give an interpretation of the Jordan-Holder filtration of the Induction of two simple modules over
R_{Q}
using the Decomposition (
6.7
).

Lemma 7.9
.

For
\gamma\in Q^{+}
and a given
\mathcal{L}_{a}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma}
, let
\mathbb{P}_{a}
be its corresponding projective module. Suppose that

\operatorname{Res}_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{\gamma}(\mathcal{L}_{a})=\sum\limits_{\mu\in\operatorname{KP}({\alpha}),\nu\in\operatorname{KP}({\beta})}J_{a}({\mu,\nu})(\operatorname{IC}_{\mu}\boxtimes\operatorname{IC}_{\nu})

Then we get

\langle\mathbb{P}_{a},\,L(\mu)\circ L(\nu)\rangle=\operatorname{Dim}J_{a}(\mu,\nu)

Proof.

Recall
\langle\mathbb{P}_{a},\,L(\mu)\circ L(\nu)\rangle=\langle\operatorname{Res}\mathbb{P}_{a},L(\mu)\boxtimes L(\nu)\rangle
. By Definition (
7.4
), we get

\displaystyle\langle\operatorname{Res}\mathbb{P}_{a},L(\kappa)\boxtimes L(\nu)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum\limits_{\mu^{\prime}\in\operatorname{KP}({\alpha}),\nu^{\prime}\in\operatorname{KP}({\beta})}J_{a}(\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})\boxtimes(\mathbb{P}_{\mu^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{\nu^{\prime}}),L(\mu)\boxtimes L(\nu)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{a}(\mu,\nu)

∎

Since
L(\mu)\circ L(\nu)
is finite dimensional, it admits a Jordan-Hölder filtration. We will show that the multiplicity of
L(\lambda)
in this filtration, denoted by
[L(\mu)\circ L(\nu)):L(\lambda)]
, is equal to
\operatorname{Dim}\hom_{R}(\mathbb{P}_{\lambda},L(\mu)\circ L(\nu))
. Namely,

(7.9)

\operatorname{Dim}\hom_{R}(\mathbb{P}_{\lambda},L(\mu)\circ L(\nu))=[L(\mu)\circ L(\nu):L(\lambda)]

We show this by induction on the length of this filtration. If
L(\mu)\circ L(\nu)
is a simple module, there is nothing to prove. Assume this statement is true for modules
M
with shorter filtrations compared to the filtration length of
L(\mu)\circ L(\nu)
. Considering the following short exact sequence:

0\to M\to L(\mu)\circ L(\nu)\to N\to 0

and then applying the functor
\hom_{R}(\mathbb{P}_{\lambda},-)
to it, we obtain the following short exact sequence:

0\to\hom_{R}(\mathbb{P}_{\lambda},M)\to\hom_{R}(\mathbb{P}_{\lambda},L(\mu)\circ L(\nu))\to\hom_{R}(\mathbb{P}_{\lambda},N)\to 0

This means

\operatorname{Dim}\hom_{R}(\mathbb{P}_{\lambda},L(\mu)\circ L(\nu))=\operatorname{Dim}\hom_{R}(\mathbb{P}_{\lambda},M)+\operatorname{Dim}\hom_{R}(\mathbb{P}_{\lambda},N)

On the other hands, the multiplicity of
L(\lambda)
in
L(\mu)\circ L(\nu)
is equal to

[L(\mu)\circ L(\nu):L(\lambda)]=[M:L(\lambda)]+[N:L(\lambda)]

According to our hypothesis, it can be inferred that
[M:L(\lambda)]=\operatorname{Dim}\hom_{R}(\mathbb{P}_{\lambda},M)
,
[N:L(\lambda)]=\operatorname{Dim}\hom_{R}(\mathbb{P}_{\lambda},N)
, and then
\hom_{R}(\mathbb{P}_{\lambda},L(\mu)\circ L(\nu))=[L({\mu})\circ L({\nu}):L(\lambda)]
.

Using the above lemma, we get the following theorem.

Theorem 7.10
.

Let
\gamma={\alpha+\beta}
be a partition of
\gamma
,
\lambda
be a Kostant partition of
\gamma
, and
{\mu,\nu}
be two Kostant partitions in
\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})
and
\operatorname{KP}({\beta})
. We have

[L(\mu)\circ L(\nu):L(\lambda)]=[\operatorname{Res}_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{\gamma}\operatorname{IC}(\lambda):\operatorname{IC}(\mu)\boxtimes\operatorname{IC}(\nu)]=K_{\lambda}({\mu,\nu})

where
[\operatorname{Res}_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{\gamma}\operatorname{IC}(\lambda):\operatorname{IC}(\mu)\boxtimes\operatorname{IC}(\nu)]
refers to the multiplicity of
\operatorname{IC}(\mu)\boxtimes\operatorname{IC}(\nu)
in
\operatorname{Res}_{{\alpha,\beta}}^{\gamma}\operatorname{IC}(\lambda)
as Lemma
7.9
. In particular, for
\lambda\in\operatorname{ext}({\mu,\nu})

K_{\lambda}({\mu,\nu})
is less than and equal to

2e_{\lambda}({\mu,\nu})+d_{\lambda}({\mu,\nu})

If
({\mu,\nu})\in\operatorname{ext}_{\alpha,\beta}^{ger}(\lambda)
, then

K_{\lambda}({\mu,\nu})=q^{\epsilon(v_{1},\alpha,v_{2},\beta)}\operatorname{P}_{q}({{\operatorname{Gr}}_{v_{1}-v_{\mu}}(CK(M_{\mu}))\times{\operatorname{Gr}}_{v_{2}-v_{\nu}}(CK(M_{\nu}))})

where
e_{\lambda}({\mu,\nu})
is the dimension of
\mathcal{E}xt_{\lambda}({\mu},{\nu})
, (see
4.3
).

Proof.

As previously mentioned, we have
[L(\kappa)\circ L(\nu)):L(\lambda)]=\operatorname{Dim}\hom_{R}(\mathbb{P}_{\lambda},L(\kappa)\circ L(\nu))
. By the above Lemma and Theorem
6.9
, we get required results.
∎

Proposition 7.11
.

Suppose that
Q
is a quiver without loops, (not necessary for Dynkin quivers). Let

7.4.
Support pairs

In this section, we give the notion of a support pair. This will give a necessary condition for some conjectures introduced in
[
LM22
, Conjecture 1.3]
and
[
KR11
, Probelem 7.6]
. However, we should remark that this condition is not sufficient to answer these conjectures. Because this notion just focuses on the supports of the perverse sheaves on representation spaces
E_{\alpha}
. This will omit some information about the decomposition (
6.7
). One of the main reasons is that the closure of orbits in
E_{\alpha}
is singular in general. The singularity makes this approach more difficult than we expect. Therefore, we limit our attention to the support of perverse sheaves
\operatorname{IC}(\lambda)
.

Definition 7.15
.

We call a pair
({\mu,\nu})\in\operatorname{KP}({\alpha})\times\operatorname{KP}({\beta})
a
support pair
if and only if for any
\lambda<\mu\oplus\nu
we have
(\mu,\nu)\notin\operatorname{ext}_{\alpha,\beta}^{ger}(\lambda)
. This notion is inspired by Theorem
6.9

For
[
KR11
, Probelem 7.6]
, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 7.16
.

Let
L(\mu)
and
L(\nu)
be two simple modules over
R_{Q}
where
\mu,\nu
are their Kostant partitions, respectively. If
L(\mu)\circ L(\nu)
is a simple module, then
({\mu,\nu})
is a support pair. In other words, for any non-trivial extension
\lambda\in\operatorname{ext}({\mu,\nu})
, we have

[M_{\nu},M_{\mu}\oplus M_{\nu}]>[M_{\nu},M_{\lambda}]\quad\text{ if $({\mu,\nu})$ is a generic pair for $\lambda$}

and

[M_{\mu},M_{\mu}\oplus M_{\nu}]>[M_{\mu},M_{\lambda}]\quad\text{ if $(\nu,\mu)$ is a generic pair for $\lambda$}

Proof.

It is easy to see that
L(\mu)\circ L(\nu)
is a simple module if and only if
K_{\lambda}({\mu,\nu})=0
for all
\lambda<\mu\oplus\nu
. Theorem
6.9
implies
({\mu,\nu})\notin\operatorname{ext}_{\alpha,\beta}^{ger}(\lambda)
. Otherwise,

K_{\lambda}({\mu,\nu})=q^{\epsilon(v_{1},\alpha,v_{2},\beta)}\operatorname{P}_{q}({{\operatorname{Gr}}_{v_{1}-v_{\mu}}(CK(M_{\mu}))\times{\operatorname{Gr}}_{v_{2}-v_{\nu}}(CK(M_{\nu}))})\neq 0

By the definition of support pair, we have that

[M_{\nu},M_{\mu}\oplus M_{\nu}]>[M_{\nu},M_{\lambda}]\quad\text{ if $({\mu,\nu})$ is a generic pair for $\lambda$}

Because
L(\mu)\circ L(\nu)
is a simple module if and only if
L(\nu)\circ L(\mu)
is a simple module, we see that

[M_{\mu},M_{\mu}\oplus M_{\nu}]>[M_{\mu},M_{\lambda}]

∎

Another useful application of this theorem is related to
[
VV03
, Conjecture 4.3]
.

Remark 7.17
.

Recall the

Theorem 7.18
.

Let
{\alpha+\beta}=\gamma
be a partition of
\gamma\in Q^{+}

\lambda=\mu*\nu

\mathcal{E}xt_{\lambda}({\mu},{\nu})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\Omega}(U,W)

\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{\lambda}=E_{\gamma}

For others

is maximal for all Kostant partitions

Statistics for Iwasawa invariants of elliptic curves, III
Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. Given a prime p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 , a conjecture of Greenberg predicts that the μ \mu -invariant of the p p -primary Selmer group should vanish for most elliptic curves with good ordinary reduction at p p . In support of this conjecture, I show that the 5 5 -primary Iwasawa μ \mu - and λ \lambda -invariants simultaneously vanish for an explicit positive density of elliptic curves E / ℚ E_{/\mathbb{Q}} . The elliptic curves in question have good ordinary reduction at 5 5 , and are ordered by their height . The results are proven by leveraging work of Bhargava and Shankar on the distribution of 5 5 -Selmer groups of elliptic curves defined over ℚ \mathbb{Q} .

Motivation and historical context

Let

\bigcup_{n\geq 1}K(\mu_{p^{n}})

\mathbb{Z}_{p}

of

It is a longstanding conjecture due to Iwasawa that

is an abelian Galois extension, the conjecture was proven by Ferrero and Washington

It is natural to investigate generalizations of Iwasawa theory to motives and their Selmer groups. Mazur
[
Maz72
]
initiated the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves (and abelian varieties) with good ordinary reduction at a prime

. Following seminal work of Rubin, Kato, Greenberg and others, the Iwasawa theory of Galois representations associated to motives came into further prominence. The main algebraic objects of study are the
p
-primary Selmer groups associated to elliptic curves, considered over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of a number field. When the elliptic curve in question has good ordinary reduction at an odd prime

, Kato
[
Kat04
]
and Rubin
[
Rub81
]
showed that the associated Selmer groups are cotorsion over the Iwasawa algebra. This allows one to associated Iwasawa
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants
\mu_{p}(E)
and
\lambda_{p}(E)
respectively to these Selmer groups. These invariants encode the growth properties of
p
-primary Selmer groups, Mordell–Weil groups and Tate-Shafarevich groups in cyclotomic towers. In this context, there is an outstanding conjecture due to Greenberg. Given an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
and a prime number
p
, set
\bar{\rho}_{E,p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

Conjecture 1.1

(Greenberg)
.

Let
p
be an odd prime number and

be an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at
p
. Suppose that the Galois representation

-invariant associated to the

-primary Selmer group of

1.2.
Main result

Before stating our main result, I introduce some notation. Any elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
admits a unique globally minimal Weierstrass equation. In other words,
E
is isomorphic to a unique elliptic curve
E_{A,B}
with minimal Weierstrass equation

E_{A,B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B,

where
A,B
are integers such that
\operatorname{gcd}(A^{3},B^{2})
is not divisible by
d^{12}
for any integer

. Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the set of all globally minimal Weierstrass equations over
\mathbb{Z}
. The

is defined as
\operatorname{ht}(E)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{max}\{|A|^{3},B^{2}\}
. Given a real number

\mathcal{C}(X)
to denote the set of all globally minimal Weierstrass equations
E_{A,B}
such that
\operatorname{ht}(E_{A,B})\leq X
. One identifies
\mathcal{C}(X)
with the set

\begin{Bmatrix}&\mathinner{\!\left\lvert A\right\rvert}\leq\sqrt[3]{X},\ \mathinner{\!\left\lvert B\right\rvert}\leq\sqrt{X}\\
(A,B)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}&\quad 4A^{3}+27B^{2}\neq 0\\
&\textrm{for all primes }\ell\textrm{ if }\ell^{4}|A,\textrm{then }\ell^{6}\nmid B\end{Bmatrix}.

Theorem A
.

There is a set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
of
positive density
such that have good ordinary reduction at
5
for which
\mu_{5}(E)
and
\lambda_{5}(E)
are

. In fact, the lower density of this set is explicitly bounded below as follows

\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{E\in\mathcal{C}(X)\mid\mu_{5}(E)=\lambda_{5}(E)=0\}}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)}\geq\frac{\zeta(10)\times\prod_{\ell\geq 7}\left(1-2\ell^{-2}+\ell^{-3}\right)}{157286400}.

For an elliptic curve

for which the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{5}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
, an explicit criterion due to Greenberg shows that the
\mu_{5}
- and
\lambda_{5}
-invariants vanish when certain local invariants vanish, along with certain local invariants, cf. Proposition

. On the other hand, Bhargava and Shankar
[

]
study the distribution of
5
-Selmer groups. In particular, it is shown that a positive density of such curves

. Our result is proven by adapting their methods to show that Greenberg’s criterion is satisfied for an explicit positive density of elliptic curves.

1.3.
Related work

In
[
KR21
]
, Kundu and I studied related questions for a general prime number
p\geq 5
. The density bounds asserted by Theorems 4.3, 4.4 and 4.5 in

are expressed in terms of a certain quantity
\mathfrak{d}_{p}^{(1)}
, which is the proportion of elliptic curves

, ordered by height such that
\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^{\infty}]=0
. There are no general upper bounds known for

[

]
. Thus, in the work
[

]
, it is shown that for

, the Iwasawa invariants

and

vanish for a positive density of elliptic curves, provided one assumes Delaunay’s heuristic. The main feature of the present work is that although the results are only proven only for

1.4.
Organization and methodology

2.
Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves

In this section, I discuss preliminaries on the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
with good ordinary reduction at a prime number
p
. I begin by discussing the properties of Selmer groups associated to elliptic curves defined over number fields. Then I introduce Selmer groups considered over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, and consider these as modules over the Iwasawa algebra. The Iwasawa
\mu
- and
\lambda
-invariants are then defined. I conclude the section with Proposition

, which is a numerical criterion that gives conditions for the vanishing of these invariants. For a more comprehensive account on the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves, see for instance
[
Gre99
,
Gre01
]
.

2.1.
Selmer groups associated to elliptic curves

Let
p\geq 5
be a prime number and

be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}

\bar{F}

(resp.

over

\Sigma_{F}

\bar{F}_{v}/F_{v}
be an algebraic closure. Given a natural number

to be the
n
-torsion subgroup of

. Set
\operatorname{G}_{F}
(resp.
\operatorname{G}_{F_{v}}

\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/F)
(resp.
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{F}_{v}/F_{v})

\iota_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}

j_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Given a prime

\bar{F}_{v}

\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}

Setting

\operatorname{res}_{v}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{1}(F,E[p^{\infty}])\rightarrow H^{1}(F_{v},E)[p^{\infty}]

Here, the second map arises from the Kummer sequence. Setting
\Phi_{F}
to denote the product

, the
p
-primary Selmer group of

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left(H^{1}(F,E[p^{\infty}])\xrightarrow{\Phi_{F}}\prod_{v}H^{1}(F_{v},E)[p^{\infty}]\right).

On the other hand, the

-Selmer group of

is defined as follows

\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/F)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left(H^{1}(F,E[p])\rightarrow\prod_{v}H^{1}(F_{v},E)[p]\right).

The
p
-primary Selmer group fits into a natural short exact sequence

0\rightarrow E(F)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F)\rightarrow\Sh(E/F)[p^{\infty}]\rightarrow 0.

The Shafarevich–Tate conjecture predicts that
\Sh(E/F)[p^{\infty}]
is finite. In particular, this conjecture implies that

\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F)=\operatorname{rank}E(F).

Iwasawa theory of Selmer groups

Set
\mu_{p^{\infty}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcup_{n}\mu_{p^{n}}

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varinjlim_{n}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{n})

. Let

is finitely generated (resp. torsion) as a module over
\Lambda
. Suppose that

\Lambda
. Let

be cofinitely generated and cotorsion

-module

whose Pontryagin dual is given as a direct sum of cyclic torsion

(2.1)

(M^{\prime})^{\vee}\simeq\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\frac{\Lambda}{(p^{n_{i}})}\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\frac{\Lambda}{(f_{j}(T))}\right).

Density results

Lemma 3.1

With respect to notation above, one has the following asymptotic

I discuss the notion of what it means for a subset
\Phi\subseteq\mathcal{C}
to be defined by
congruence conditions
. Given a prime

, a subset
\Phi_{\ell}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
is defined by a congruence condition if there is

•

an integer
n_{\ell}\geq 6
,

•

a set of residue classes

\bar{\Phi}_{\ell}\subseteq\{(\bar{A},\bar{B})\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m_{\ell}}\times\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m_{\ell}}\mid\ell^{4}\nmid\bar{A}\text{ or }\ell^{6}\nmid\bar{B}\},

\Phi_{\ell}

such that

\left(A\mod{\ell^{m_{\ell}}},B\mod{\ell^{m_{\ell}}}\right)\in\bar{\Phi}_{\ell}.

The

is defined as follows

\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{\ell})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\#\bar{\Phi}_{\ell}}{\#\{(\bar{A},\bar{B})\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m_{\ell}}\times\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m_{\ell}}\mid\ell^{4}\nmid\bar{A}\text{ or }\ell^{6}\nmid\bar{B}\}}=\frac{\#\bar{\Phi}_{\ell}}{(1-\ell^{-10})\ell^{2m_{\ell}}}.

If

\mathfrak{d}(\Phi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi(X)}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)},

\begin{split}&\overline{\mathfrak{d}}(\Phi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi(X)}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)},\\
&\underline{\mathfrak{d}}(\Phi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi(X)}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)}.\end{split}

Given a positive real number
Z
, set
\Phi_{Z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcap_{\ell\leq Z}\Phi_{\ell}
, where
\ell
ranges over the prime numbers
\leq Z
. Recall from Lemma
3.1
that

\#\mathcal{C}(X)\sim\frac{4X^{5/6}}{\zeta(10)}.

Since there are only finitely many local conditions defining
\Phi_{Z}
, I have that

\begin{split}\#\Phi_{Z}(X)&\sim\left(\prod_{\ell\leq Z}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{\ell})\right)\times\#\mathcal{C}(X)\\
&\sim\left(\prod_{\ell\leq Z}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{\ell})\right)\times\frac{4X^{5/6}}{\zeta(10)}.\end{split}

Since
\Phi(X)
is contained in
\Phi_{Z}(X)
, I find that

(3.1)

\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi(X)}{X^{5/6}}\leq\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi_{Z}(X)}{X^{5/6}}=\left(\prod_{\ell\leq Z}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{\ell})\right)\times\frac{4}{\zeta(10)}.

Thus, taking
Z\rightarrow\infty
, I find that

\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi(X)}{X^{5/6}}\leq\left(\prod_{\ell\leq Z}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{\ell})\right)\times\frac{4}{\zeta(10)}.

Since

\Phi_{Z}(X)\subseteq\Phi(X)\cup\left(\bigcup_{\ell>Z}\Phi_{\ell}^{\prime}(X)\right),

I find that

\begin{split}\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi(X)}{X^{5/6}}\geq&\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi_{Z}(X)}{X^{5/6}}-C\sum_{\ell>Z}\left(1-\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{\ell})\right),\\
=&\left(\prod_{\ell\leq Z}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{\ell})\right)\times\frac{4X^{5/6}}{\zeta(10)}-C\sum_{\ell>Z}\left(1-\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{\ell})\right).\end{split}

Letting
Z\rightarrow\infty
, I find that

(3.2)

\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi(X)}{X^{5/6}}\geq\left(\prod_{\ell}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{\ell})\right)\times\frac{4X^{5/6}}{\zeta(10)}.

From (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), I deduce that

\#\Phi(X)\sim\left(\prod_{\ell}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{\ell})\right)\frac{4X^{5/6}}{\zeta(10)}.

∎

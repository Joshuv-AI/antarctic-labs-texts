1.
Introduction

A fundamental problem in representation theory is the description of all irreducible representations. We are interested in the polynomial representation the invariant differential operators with respect to the generalized symmetric group.
We know that the direct image of a simple module for a proper map in semi-simple by the Decomposition Theorem
[
5
]
. The simplest case is when the map
\pi:X=\operatorname{spec}B\to Y=\operatorname{spec}A
is finite, in which case it is easy to give an elementary and wholly algebraic proof, using essentially the (generic) correspondence with the differential Galois group, which equals the ordinary Galois group
G
. The irreducible submodule of the direct image are in one-to-one correspondence with the irreducible representations of
G
(see
[
9
]
). In this paper we make the differential structure more explicit in the case of the invariants of the complex reflection group
G(r,n),\ B=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\subset A=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{G(r,n)}.

We explicitly study the simple component of the direct image
\pi_{+}({\cal O}_{X})
of the polynomial ring
{\cal O}_{X}
as a
\mathcal{D}
-module under the map
\pi:\operatorname{spec}{\cal O}_{X}\to\operatorname{spec}{\cal O}_{Y}
where
{\cal O}_{Y}={\cal O}_{X}^{G(r,n)};
the ring of invariant polynomials under the action of
{G(r,n)}.

We describe the generators of the simple components of
\pi_{+}({\cal O}_{X})
and their multiplicities as in
[
9
]
.
We thus establish the decomposition structure of
\pi_{+}({\cal O}_{X})
by means of the higher Specht polynomials. This proof uses the fact that the irreducible
\mathcal{D}
-submodules of
\pi_{+}({\cal O}_{X})
are in one-to-one correspondence with irreducible representations of
G(r,n)
.

Secondly we elaborate a
\mathcal{D}

\pi.

Finally we describe the action of invariant differential operators on higher Specht polynomials.
The higher Specht polynomials (introduced combinatorially
[

]

This paper generalizes results on modules over the Weyl algebra appeared in
[

]
and
[

The case

Let

-module. The tensor product

is a right

.
Let us consider the

Using the standard transposition for

It is clear that

2.2.
Higher Specht Polynomials for Reflections group

In this subsection we recall some facts about the representation of the generalized symmetric group
G(r,n)

[
1
]
.
Let
{{\cal S}}_{n}
be the group of permutations of the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}/r{\mathbb{Z}}
be the cyclic group of order
r
.

The generalized symmetric group
G(r,n)
is the semi-direct product of
({\mathbb{Z}}/r{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
with
{{\cal S}}_{n}
, written as
({\mathbb{Z}}/r{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\rtimes{{\cal S}}_{n}
, where
({\mathbb{Z}}/r{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
is the direct product of
n
copies of
{\mathbb{Z}}/r{\mathbb{Z}}
. Let
\xi
be a primitive
r
-th root of 1.

({\mathbb{Z}}/r{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\rtimes{{\cal S}}_{n}=\{(\xi^{i_{1}},\ldots,\xi^{i_{n}};\sigma)|\ i_{k}\in\mathbb{N},\ \sigma\in{{\cal S}}_{n}\}
, whose product is given by

(\xi^{i_{1}},\ldots,\xi^{i_{n}};\sigma)(\xi^{j_{1}},\ldots,\xi^{j_{n}};\pi)=(\xi^{i_{1}+j_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}},\ldots,\xi^{i_{n}+j_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}},;\sigma\pi).

Let
{\cal O}_{X}=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the ring of polynomials in
n
indeterminates on which the group
G(r,n)
acts as follows:

(\xi^{i_{1}},\ldots,\xi^{i_{n}};\sigma)f=f(\xi^{i_{\sigma(1)}}x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\xi^{i_{\sigma(n)}}x_{\sigma(n)};\sigma),

where
f\in{\cal O}_{X}
and
(\xi^{i_{1}},\ldots,\xi^{i_{n}};\sigma)\in G(r,n)
. It is known that the fundamental invariants under this action are given by the elementary symmetric functions
e_{j}(x_{1}^{r},\ldots,x_{n}^{r}),\ 1\leq j\leq n.
Let
J_{+}
be the ideal of
{\cal O}_{X}
generated by these fundamental invariants and
\Lambda={\cal O}_{X}/J_{+}
be the quotient ring. It is also known that the
G(r,n)
-module
\Lambda
is isomorphic to the group ring
\mathbb{C}[G(r,n)]
, namely the left regular representation. A description of all irreducible components of
\Lambda
is known in
[
1
]
, in terms of what is called ”higher Specht polynomials”. The irreducible representation of
G(r,n)
are parametrized by the
r
-tuple of Young diagrams
(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{r})
with
|\lambda^{1}|+\cdots+|\lambda^{r}|=n.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{r,n}
be the set of
r
-tuples of Young diagrams
{\bf\lambda}=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{r})
with
|\lambda^{1}|+\cdots+|\lambda^{r}|=n
. By filling each cell with a positive integer in such a way that every

r
-tableau
T=(T^{1},\ldots,T^{r})
of shape
{\bf{\bf\lambda}}=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{r})
. When the number
k

. The set of
r
-tableaux of shape
{\bf\lambda}
is denoted by
{\mbox{Tab}}({\bf\lambda}).
An

-tableau

is said to be standard if the numbers are increasing on each column and each row of

. The set of
r
-standard tableaux of shape
{\bf\lambda}
is denoted by
{\mbox{STab}}({\bf\lambda}).

Let
S=(S^{1},\ldots,S^{r})\in{\mbox{STab}}({\bf\lambda})
. We associate a word

in the following way. First we read each column of the component
S^{1}
from the bottom to the top starting from the left. We continue this procedure for the tableau
S^{2}
and so on. For word
w(S)
we define index

inductively as follows. The number 1 in the word
w(S)
has the index

If the number

and the number has number

has index

(resp.

). Finally, assigning the indices to the corresponding cells, we get a shape

Let
T=(T^{1},\ldots,T^{r})
be an
r
-tableau of shape
{\bf\lambda}
. For each component

the Young symmetrizer
{\mathbf{e}}_{T^{\nu}}
of
T^{\nu}
is defined by

{\mathbf{e}}_{T^{\nu}}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{T^{\nu}}}\sum_{\sigma\in R(T^{\nu})\ \tau\in C(T^{\nu})}\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\tau\sigma,

where

,

and

are the

and

of

respectively.

We may regard a tableau

which assigns to a cell

For

\displaystyle x_{T^{\nu}}^{ri(S)^{\nu}}=\prod_{\xi\in\lambda^{\nu}}x_{T^{\nu}(\xi)}^{ri(S)^{\nu}(\xi)}.

The following is the fundamental result in

]

Theorem 2.2
.

(1)

The space

(2)

(4)

We have the irreducible decomposition

Theorem 2.3
.

The higher Specht polynomials in

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\lambda\in{\cal P}_{r,n}
and
{\bf D}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{V}
such that
{\bf D}(F_{T}^{S})\neq 0
for a higher Specht polynomial
F_{T}^{S}
with
S,T\in{\mbox{STab}}(\lambda)
. Then the image of the
\mathbb{C}[G(r,n)]
-module
V_{S}({\lambda})
by
{\bf D}
is an
\mathbb{C}[G(r,n)]
-module isomorphic to
V_{S}({\lambda})
. In others words, the actions of the differential operators of
{\mathcal{D}}_{V}
on the higher Specht polynomials generate isomorphic copies of the corresponding irreducible
\mathbb{C}[G(r,n)]
-module.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in{\cal P}_{r,n},{\bf D}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{V}
such that
{\bf D}(F_{T}^{S})\neq 0
for
S,T\in{\mbox{STab}}(\lambda)
and set
W^{S}_{\bf D}({\lambda})={\bf D}(V_{S}({\lambda}))
the image of the module
V_{S}({\lambda})
under the map
{\bf D}
. By Theorem 2.2, the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
V_{S}({\lambda})
is equipped with a basis
{{\cal F}}=\{F_{T}^{S};T\in{\mbox{STab}}(\lambda)\}
, then
W^{S}_{\bf D}({\lambda})
is the vector space spanned by the set
\{{\bf D}(F_{T}^{S});\ T\in{\mbox{STab}}(\lambda)\}
. The elements of
\{F_{T}^{S};T\in{\mbox{STab}}(\lambda)\}
are linearly independent over
{\mathcal{D}}_{V}
, otherwise the direct sum in Theorem 4.7 cannot hold. It follows that the elements
\{{\bf D}(F_{T}^{S});T\in{\mbox{STab}}(\lambda)\}
are linear independent over
\mathbb{C}
. Hence
\{{\bf D}(F_{T}^{S});T\in{\mbox{STab}}(\lambda)\}
is a basis of
W^{S}_{\bf D}({\lambda})
over
\mathbb{C}
. Since
{\bf D}
commute with elements of
\mathbb{C}[G(r,n)],\ W^{S}_{\bf D}({\lambda})
is an
\mathbb{C}[G(r,n)]
-module isomorphic to
V_{S}({\lambda}).

∎

On some Frobenius type divisibility results in a premodular category
Sebastian Burciu

Abstract




Abstract

In this note some new Frobenius type divisibility results are obtained for premodular categories. In particular, we extend
[
Yu20
, Corollary 3.4]
from the settings of super-modular categories to arbitrary pseudo-unitary premodular categories.

1.
Introduction

In
[
Yu20
]
the author showed that in a slightly-degenerate braided fusion category one has that
\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{2\dim(Y)^{2}}\in\mathbb{A}
for any simple object
Y
of
\mathcal{C}
. The main goal of this note is to generalize this result in the settings of a premodular fusion category with pointed Müger center, i.e. all the objects of this center are invertible.

Our first main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a premodular category and
{\mathcal{D}}
a fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. If
{\mathcal{D}}\cap{\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C})}=\mathrm{Vec}
then

(1.2)

\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{\dim(Y)^{2}}\in\mathbb{A}

for any simple object
Y
in
{\mathcal{D}}
.

Moreover, in the case of a pointed Müger center we prove that:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pseudo-unitary premodular fusion category with
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C})\subseteq{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pt}}}
.

(1)

For any simple object
\mathcal{C}
of
Y
one has

(1.4)

\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C}))}{\mathrm{FPdim}(Y)^{2}}\in\mathbb{A}.

(2)

If the action of
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C})
on the simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
is free then

(1.5)

\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C}))\mathrm{FPdim}(Y)^{2}}\in\mathbb{A}.

Recall that
{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pt}}}
denotes the maximal pointed subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. Note that the second item of the above Theorem generalizes
[
Yu20
, Corollary 3.4]
in the case of a pseudo-unitary premodular category.

Shortly, this note is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the basic properties of fusion categories needed through this paper. In Subsection
2.1
we briefly recall the adjoint algebra and the characters of simple objects of pivotal fusion categories as defined in
[
Shi17
]
.

In Section
3
we construct a Hecke type algebras associated to the right cosets of a fusion subcategory of a given fusion category. In the same section we prove orthogonality relations for Hecke algebras. In Section
4
we prove the main results stated above.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall the main properties of premodular fusion categories that are needed through the paper. For the basic theory of fusion categories, we refer the reader to
[
EGNO15
]
. Recall that a fusion category is by definition, a semisimple finite tensor category.

Throughout this note
\mathcal{C}
denotes a fusion category over
\mathbb{C}
with eventual additional properties. Let
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C}):=\{X_{0},X_{1},\dots,X_{m}\}
be a complete set of representatives for the isomorphism classes of simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
. By
K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
we denote the Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{C}
and let
K(\mathcal{C}):=K_{0}(\mathcal{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
.

Recall that a
pivotal structure
of a rigid monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is an isomorphism
j:\id_{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow()^{**}
of monoidal functors. A pivotal monoidal category is a rigid monoidal category endowed with a pivotal structure. For any pivotal structure one can construct categorical dimensions
\dim(X)
of any object
X
of
\mathcal{C}
. In fact, the map
\dim:K(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathbb{C},[X]\mapsto\dim(X)
is an algebra morphism.

A pivotal structure a on a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is called
spherical
, see
[
EGNO15
]
if

\dim(V)=\dim(V^{*})
for any object
V
of
\mathcal{C}
. A tensor category is spherical if it is
equipped with a spherical structure.Given a fusion category there is a unique algebra morphism
\mathrm{FPdim}:K(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{i})>0
for any
X_{i}\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
. Then
\mathrm{FPdim}
is called the Frobenius-Perron morphism.

Recall that a braided fusion category is called
premodular
if it has a spherical structure. Equivalently, a premodular category is a braided fusion category equipped with a twist
(also called a balanced structure), see
[
EGNO15
, Section 8.10 ]
for details.

A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is called
pseudo-unitary
if
\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=\dim(\mathcal{C})
. If such is the case,
then by
[
ENO05
, Proposition 8.23]
,
\mathcal{C}
admits a unique spherical structure with respect to which the categorical dimensions of each simple coincides with its Frobenius-Perron dimension, i.e.
\mathrm{FPdim}(X)=\dim(X)
for any object
X
of
\mathcal{C}
.

2.1.
Adjoint algebra and internal characters

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category and
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
be its Drinfeld center. The forgetful functor
F:{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
admits a right adjoint functor
R:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
and let
Z:=FR:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
. Then
Z
is a Hopf comonad and by
[
Shi17
, Section 2.6]
one has that

(2.1)

Z(V)\simeq\int_{X\in\mathcal{C}}X\otimes V\otimes X^{*}

It is known that
A:=R(\mathbf{1})
has the structure of central commutative algebra in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
.

We denote by
m:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
the multiplication and by
u:\mathbf{1}\rightarrow A
the unit of the
adjoint algebra
A=Z(\mathbf{1})
.

The vector space
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}:=\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{1},A)
is called
the set of central elements.
For
a,b\in\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
, we set
a\textperiodcentered b:=m\circ(a\otimes b)
. Then the set
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra with respect to this operation.
The space
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C}):=\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,\mathbf{1})
is called the
space of class functions
of
\mathcal{C}
. The space
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
is a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra with the multiplication defined
f\star g:=f\circ Z(g)\circ\delta_{\mathbf{1}}.
for any
f,g\in\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
. Here
\delta:Z\rightarrow Z^{2}
is the comultiplication structure of the Hopf comonad
Z
, see
[
Shi17
]
for details. To any object
X
of
\mathcal{C}
Shimizu assigned in
[
Shi17
, Section 3]
a class function
\mathrm{ch}(X)
called
the internal character
. It is defined as a partial trace of the canonical left action
\rho_{X}:A\otimes X\rightarrow\mathbf{1}
. By
[
Shi17
, Theorem 3.10]
one has that
\mathrm{ch}(X\otimes Y)=\mathrm{ch}(X)\mathrm{ch}(Y)
for any two objects
X
and
Y
of
\mathcal{C}
and
K(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
as algebras.

There is a also non-degenerate a paring

\langle\;,\;\rangle:\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})\times\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathbf{1}
, given by
\langle f,a\rangle\id_{\mathbf{1}}=f\circ a,
for all
f\in\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
and
a\in\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
.

For any simple object
X_{i}
of
\mathcal{C}
we denote by
\chi_{i}:=\mathrm{ch}(X_{i})
its associated character. The central element space
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
has a basis of primitive orthogonal idempotents
E_{i}
such that
<\chi_{i},E_{j}>=\delta_{i,j}d_{i}
for all
i,j
.

Recall
R:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
is a right adjoint to the forgetful functor
F:{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
. As explained in
[
Shi17
, Theorem 3.8]
this adjunction gives an isomorphism of algebras

(2.2)

\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathrm{End}_{{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}}(R(\mathbf{1})),\;\;\chi\mapsto Z(\chi)\circ\delta_{\mathbf{1}}.

Since
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
is also fusion category we can write
R(\mathbf{1})=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{m}\mathcal{C}^{j}
as a direct sum of simple objects in
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
. Recall that
\mathcal{C}^{j}
are called
conjugacy classes
for
\mathcal{C}
.

For the rest of this section let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category with a commutative Grothendieck ring
K(\mathcal{C})
. Since
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
is also commutative semisimple
\mathbb{C}
-algebra has

(2.3)

\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\bigoplus_{j=0}^{m}\mathbb{C}F_{j}

where
F_{j}
are the central primitive idempotents of
\mathcal{C}
. We define
\mathcal{J}:=\{0,\dots,m\}
.

Since
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
is a commutative algebra it follows from Equation (
2.2
) that
R(\mathbf{1})
is multiplicity free, i.e. each conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}^{j}
appears with multiplicity
1
inside
R(\mathbf{1})
. Moreover we fix the canonical bijection
F_{j}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{j}
for which the image of
F_{j}
under the canonical adjunction isomorphism from Equation (
2.2
) is the projection of
R(\mathbf{1})
on
\mathcal{C}^{j}
.

With this bijection, the second orthogonality from
[
Shi17
, Theorem 6.10]
can be written as:

(2.4)

\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mu_{l}(\chi_{i})\mu_{k}(\chi_{i^{*}})=\delta_{l,k}\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{\dim(\mathcal{C}^{k})}

where
\mu_{j}:\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the set of algebra maps of
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
. In terms of Equation (
2.3
) one has
\mu_{j}(F_{k})=\delta_{j,k}
.

Following
[
Bur20
]
we use the following definition:

Definition 2.5
.

To any fusion subcategory
{\mathcal{D}}
we associate a set of indices
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
such that:

(2.6)

\lambda_{{\mathcal{D}}}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{D}}}F_{j}.

In this settings, Equation (4.20) from
[
Bur20
]
gives that

(2.7)

\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{{\mathcal{D}}}}\dim(\mathcal{C}^{j})=\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{\dim({\mathcal{D}})}.

3.
Hecke algebras of cosets

For a fusion subcategory
{\mathcal{D}}
of
\mathcal{C}
we denote by
\simeq^{r}_{\mathcal{D}}
the equivalence relation on the set of simple objects
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
of
\mathcal{C}
. It is given by
X\simeq^{r}_{\mathcal{D}}Y
if and only if there is a simple object
S
of
{\mathcal{D}}
such that
X
is a constituent of
Y\otimes S
. An equivalence class of
\simeq^{r}_{\mathcal{D}}
is called a
right coset
of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}
. We denote by
{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}
the set of equivalence classes (right cosets) with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}
. It was shown in
[
BB15
]
that

(3.1)

X\simeq^{r}_{{\mathcal{D}}}Y\iff\frac{[X]R_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathrm{FPdim}(X)}=\frac{[Y]R_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathrm{FPdim}(Y)}

where
R_{\mathcal{D}}
is the regular element of the fusion subcategory
{\mathcal{D}}
,
R_{\mathcal{D}}=\sum_{X\in\mathrm{Irr}({\mathcal{D}})}\mathrm{FPdim}(X)[X]\in K(\mathcal{C})
. In this case it can be shown that

(3.2)

\frac{[X]R_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathrm{FPdim}(X)}=\frac{[Y]R_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathrm{FPdim}(Y)}=\frac{R_{m}}{\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{m})}

where
m\in{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}
is the right coset corresponding to
X
and
Y
and and
R_{m}:=\sum_{Z\in m}\mathrm{FPdim}(Z)[Z]\in K(\mathcal{C})
is the regular element corresponding to any coset
m
.

Based on Equation (
3.2
) it is easy to verify that if
K(\mathcal{C})
is commutative then for all
m,n,p\in{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}

(3.3)

\frac{R_{m}}{\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{m})}\frac{R_{n}}{\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{n})}=\sum_{p\in{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}}H^{p}_{mn}\frac{R_{p}}{\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{p})}

where
H^{p}_{mn}
are (integral) scalars given by

H^{p}_{mn}=\big(\sum_{Z\in p}\mathrm{FPdim}(Z)N^{Z}_{XY}\big)

where
X\in m
and
Y\in n
are arbitrarily chosen representatives for each coset
m,n\in{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}
. Thus in the case
K(\mathcal{C})
is commutative, the vector subspace
\mathcal{H}({\mathcal{D}}):=\{R_{m}\;\big|\;m\in{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}\}
of
K(\mathcal{C})
generated by the regular elements
R_{m}
is in fact a subalgebra of
K(\mathcal{C})
. It is called the
Hecke algebra
of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}
. Note that Equation (
3.2
) implies
\mathcal{H}({\mathcal{D}})=K(\mathcal{C})R_{{\mathcal{D}}}
inside
K(\mathcal{C})
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a fusion subcategory of a pseudo-unitary fusion category
\mathcal{C}
with a commutative Grothendieck ring. Then the dimension of the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}({\mathcal{D}})
equals the cardinality of
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{D}}
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{C}
is pseudo-unitary then as above
K(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
and
R_{\mathcal{D}}
corresponds to the idempotent cointegral
\lambda_{\mathcal{D}}
. Then from Equation (
2.3
) in this case one has:

\mathcal{H}({\mathcal{D}})=\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})\lambda_{\mathcal{D}}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{D}}}\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})F_{j}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{D}}}\mathbb{C}F_{j}

by Equation (
2.6
).
∎

General Hecke orthogonality

For any
t\in{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}
let
t^{*}:=\{X^{*}\;|\;X\in t\}.
It is easy to see that in the case of a commutative Grothendieck ring, if
t\in{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}
is a right coset then
t^{*}
is also a right coset. Indeed, since
K(\mathcal{C})
is commutative one has
([X])R_{\mathcal{D}})^{*}=R_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}[X]^{*}=R_{\mathcal{D}}[X^{*}]
since
R_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}=R_{\mathcal{D}}
.

For any
t\in{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}
choose a representative simple object
X_{t}
of
\mathcal{C}
belonging to this coset such that
X_{t}^{*}={X_{t^{*}}}
. Denote also
\chi_{t}:=\chi(X_{t})\in\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
.

Equation (
2.6
) implies that

\mathcal{H}({\mathcal{D}})=\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})\lambda_{\mathcal{D}}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{D}}}\mathbb{C}F^{j}

Therefore
\widehat{\mathcal{H}({\mathcal{D}})}=\{\mu_{j},\;|\;j\in J_{{\mathcal{D}}}\}
are the all linear characters of
\mathcal{H}({\mathcal{D}})
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
be a fusion subcategory of a pseudo-unitary fusion category
\mathcal{C}
with commutative Grothendieck ring. Then we have the following:

(1)

The first orthogonality relation for
\mathcal{H}({\mathcal{D}})
:

(3.6)

\sum_{t\in{{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}}}\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{t})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{t})^{2}}\mu_{k}(\chi_{t})\mu_{l}(\chi_{t^{*}})=\delta_{l,k}\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}^{k})},\;\text{for any}\;k,l\in{\mathcal{J}_{{\mathcal{D}}}},

where
R_{t}=\sum_{\chi_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{t}}d_{i}\chi_{i}
.

(2)

The second orthogonality relation for
\mathcal{H}({\mathcal{D}})
:

(3.7)

\sum_{k=0}^{r}\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}^{k})\mu_{k}({\chi_{t}})\mu_{k}(\chi_{s^{*}})=\delta_{s,t}\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{t})\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{s})\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{t})},\;\text{for all}\;t,s\in{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is a pseudo-unitary fusion category one has
\lambda_{\mathcal{D}}=\frac{R_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{\mathcal{D}})}
for any fusion subcategory and
\dim(\mathcal{C})=\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
. Moreover
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
is also pseudo-unitary and therefore
\dim(\mathcal{C}^{k})=\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}^{k})
for any conjugacy class of
\mathcal{C}
.

Let
j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{D}}
.
If
X_{i}\in t
for some
t\in{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}
by applying
\mu_{j}
to Equation (
3.1
) one has

(3.8)

\frac{\mu_{j}(\chi_{i})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{i})}=\frac{\mu_{j}(\chi_{t})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{t})}=\mu_{j}(\frac{R_{t}}{\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{t})}).

Note that since
j\in{\mathcal{J}_{{\mathcal{D}}}}
Equation (
2.6
) implies that
\mu_{j}(\lambda_{\mathcal{D}})=1
.

The second orthogonality relation for
\mathcal{C}
from Equation (
2.4
) can be written as

\sum_{t\in{{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}}}\big(\sum_{X_{i}\in t}\mu_{l}(\chi_{i})\mu_{k}(\chi_{i^{*}})\big)=\delta_{l,k}\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}^{k})}.

If
l,k\in\mathcal{J}_{{\mathcal{D}}}
, using Equation (
3.8
) one obtains Equation (
3.6
) from above. Indeed,

\displaystyle\delta_{l,k}\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}^{k})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in{{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}}}\sum_{X_{i}\in t}\mu_{l}(\chi_{i})\mu_{k}(\chi_{i^{*}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in{{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}}}\sum_{X_{i}\in t}\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{i})\mu_{l}(\chi_{t})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{t})}\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{i})\mu_{k}(\chi_{t^{*}})}{\mathrm{FPdim}({X_{t^{*}}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in{{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}}}\frac{\mu_{k}(\chi_{t^{*}})\mu_{l}(\chi_{t})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{t})^{2}}\big(\sum_{X_{i}\in t}\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{i})^{2}\big)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in{{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}}}\frac{\mu_{k}(\chi_{t^{*}})\mu_{l}(\chi_{t})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{t})^{2}}\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{t})

The second orthogonality relation follows as usually from the fact that for complex matrices
(BA=I_{n}\implies AB=I_{n})
. Indeed, denote

a_{tk}:=\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}^{k})\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{t})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}}\frac{\mu_{k}(\chi_{t})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{t})},\;k\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{D}},t\in{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}

and

b_{lt}:=\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}^{l})\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{t})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}}\frac{\mu_{l}(\chi_{t^{*}})}{\mathrm{FPdim}({X_{t^{*}}})},\;l\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{D}},t\in{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}.

With these notations it is easy to see that the equality
BA=I_{n}
(where
n:=|{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}|=|\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{D}}|
) is equivalent to the first orthogonality relations. Then the second orthogonality relations are equivalent to
AB=I_{n}
.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\delta_{s,t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}a_{tk}b_{ks}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}^{k})\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{t})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}}\frac{\mu_{k}(\chi_{t})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{t})}\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}^{k})\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{s})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}}\frac{\mu_{k}(\chi_{s^{*}})}{\mathrm{FPdim}({X_{s^{*}}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{t})\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{s})}}{\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{t})\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{s})\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}^{k})\mu_{k}(\chi_{t})\mu_{k}(\chi_{s^{*}})

∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a fusion subcategory of a pseudo-unitary fusion category
\mathcal{C}
.
With the above notations one has:

(1)

For any right coset
t\in{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}
and any representative
X_{t}\in t
one has

(3.10)

\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{t})^{2}\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{t})}\in\mathbb{A}.

(2)

Suppose that
{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pt}}}
acts freely on the simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
. Then

(3.11)

\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{\mathrm{FPdim}({\mathcal{D}})\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}^{j})}\in\mathbb{A}

for all
j\in\mathcal{J}_{{\mathcal{D}}}
.

Proof.

(1)

It follows from Equation (
3.7
) for
s=t
since
\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}^{k})\in\mathbb{A}
and
\mu_{k}(\chi_{t})\in\mathbb{A}
.

(2)

Note that in this case, since
{\mathcal{D}}
acts freely, one has
\mathrm{FPdim}(R_{m})=\mathrm{FPdim}({\mathcal{D}})\mathrm{FPdim}(X_{m})^{2}.
Then equation (
3.6
) can be written as

\sum_{t\in{{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}}}\mu_{k}(\chi_{t})\mu_{l}(\chi_{t^{*}})=\delta_{l,k}\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{\mathrm{FPdim}({\mathcal{D}})\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}^{k})},\;\text{for any}\;k,l\in{\mathcal{J}_{{\mathcal{D}}}}.

Put
k=l
above, and since
\mu_{k}(\chi_{t})\in\mathbb{A}
the divisibility result follows.

∎

Lemma 3.12
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{D}},\mathcal{A}
are fusion subcategories of a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
. If
m\in{\big(\mathcal{C}\big/{\mathcal{D}}\big)_{r}}
is a right coset of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}
then the set

\mathcal{A}\cap m
, if not empty,
is a right coset
\mathcal{A}
with respect to
\mathcal{A}\cap{\mathcal{D}}
.

Proof.

Let
X,Y\in\mathcal{A}\cap m
be any two simple objects. We have to show that
X\simeq^{r}_{\mathcal{A}\cap{\mathcal{D}}}Y
as objects of
\mathcal{A}
. Since
X,Y
are in the same right coset with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}
, by definition, there is
Z\in{\mathcal{D}}
such that
m(X,Y\otimes Z)>0
. This implies
m(Z,Y^{*}\otimes X)>0
which in turn gives that
Z\in\mathcal{A}
since both
X,Y\in\mathcal{A}
. Thus
Z\in{\mathcal{D}}\cap\mathcal{A}
. It follows that
X,Y
are in the same coset of
\mathcal{A}
with respect to
\mathcal{A}\cap{\mathcal{D}}
. ∎

4.
Divisibility results for premodular categories

4.1.
Definition of the braided partition function
M

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a premodular (i.e. braided and spherical) fusion category. We keep all the notations from the previous section, in particular
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})=\{X_{0},X_{1},\dots,X_{m}\}
and
d_{i}:=\dim(X_{i})
for all
i
. By
[
Shi17
, Example 6.14]
there is
\mathbb{C}
-algebra map
f_{Q}:{\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
given by the following formula:

(4.1)

f_{Q}(\chi_{i})=\sum_{i^{\prime}=0}^{m}\frac{s_{ii^{\prime}}}{d_{i^{\prime}}}E_{i^{\prime}}.

where
S=(s_{ij})
is the
S
-matrix of
\mathcal{C}
.

Given a fusion subcategory
{\mathcal{D}}
of a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, the notion of
Müger centralizer of
{\mathcal{D}}

was introduced in
[
DGNO10
]
. Two objects
X
and
Y
of
\mathcal{C}
centralize each other if
c_{X,Y}c_{Y,X}=\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes Y}
.

The centralizer
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
is defined as the fusion subcategory
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{C}
generated by all simple objects
Y
of
\mathcal{C}
centralizing any object objects
X
of
{\mathcal{D}}
. In the premodular case
X_{i}
centralizes
X_{i^{\prime}}
if and only if
s_{ii^{\prime}}=d_{i}d_{i^{\prime}}
, (see also
[
Müg03
]
). In particular, the centralizer
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{C}
is also denoted by
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C})
and it is called the Müger center of
\mathcal{C}
.

Since
f_{Q}(F_{j})
is an idempotent element of
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
one may write:

f_{Q}(F_{j})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}_{j}}E_{i}

for some subset
\mathcal{A}_{j}\subseteq\{0,\dots,m\}
. Note that the set
\mathcal{A}_{j}
might be empty precisely when
f_{Q}(F_{j})=0
. Denote by
\mathcal{J}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{J}:=\{0,1,\dots,m\}
the set of all indices
j
with
\mathcal{A}_{j}
not a empty set. Since
f_{Q}(1)=1
we obtain in this way a partition for the set of all irreducible representations
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})=\bigsqcup_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}}\mathcal{\tilde{A}}_{j}
where, to be precise,
\mathcal{\tilde{A}}_{j}=\{[X_{i}]\;|\;i\in\mathcal{A}_{j}\}
. For any index
0\leq i\leq m
we denoted by
M(i)
the unique index
j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}
such that
i\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
. One obtains a (unique) function

M:\{0,1\dots,m\}\rightarrow\mathcal{J}_{2}
with the property that
E_{i}f_{Q}(F_{M(i)})\neq 0
for all
i\in\{0,1\dots,m\}
.

Since
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
is a semisimple commutative algebra it follows that
F_{j}
form a
\mathbb{C}
-linear basis for
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
. Then for any irreducible character
\chi_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\alpha_{ij}F_{j}
for some
\alpha_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Since

for all

),
for all indices

4.2.
Cosets with respect to
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C})

Recall the notion of cosets with respect to a fusion subcategory from Section
3
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Two simple objects
X_{i},C_{i^{\prime}}
of a pseudo premodular fusion category
\mathcal{C}
are in the same coset with respect to
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C})
if and only if
M(i)=M(i^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
\chi_{i}:=\chi(X_{i})
and
\chi_{i^{\prime}}:=\chi(X_{i^{\prime}})
. Suppose that the two characters
\chi_{i}
and
\chi_{i^{\prime}}
are in the same coset with respect to
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C})
. Then as above from Equation (
3.2
) one has:

\frac{\chi_{i}\lambda_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}}{d_{i}}=\frac{\chi_{i^{\prime}}\lambda_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}}{d_{i^{\prime}}}.

On the other hand
f_{Q}(\lambda_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}})=1
by
[
Bur20
, Corollary 5.8]
, and applying
f_{Q}
to the above Equation one has:

f_{Q}(\frac{\chi_{i}\lambda_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}}{d_{i}})=\frac{1}{\dim(\mathcal{C}^{M(i)})}\mathrm{C}_{{}_{M(i)}}=f_{Q}(\frac{\chi_{i^{\prime}}\lambda_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}}{d_{i^{\prime}}})=\frac{1}{\dim(\mathcal{C}^{M(i^{\prime})})}\mathrm{C}_{{}_{M(i^{\prime})}}

which proves that
M(i)=M(i^{\prime})
.

Therefore every set
\mathcal{\widetilde{A}}_{j}:=\{X_{i}\;\big|\;M(i)=j\}
is a union of right cosets with respect to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. Clearly, by its definition, the number of the non-empty sets
\mathcal{A}_{j}
equals the cardinality of
\mathcal{J}_{2}
.

Note that the relation
f_{Q}(\lambda_{\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C}}))=1
implies also that
\mathcal{J}_{2}=\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C})}
. Then from Proposition (
3.4
) it follows that

(4.11)

\dim\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C}))=\big|\mathcal{J}_{2}\big|.

On the other hand, Equation (
4.11
) implies that the number of right cosets also equals the cardinal of
\mathcal{J}_{2}
. Thus each
\mathcal{A}_{j}
consists of a single right coset with respect to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
and the proof is finished.
∎

For any
j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}
we denote by
\mathcal{R}_{j}:=\{X_{i}\;|\;M(i)=j\}
. The above theorem implies that
\mathcal{R}_{j}
with
j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}
are exactly the right cosets of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C})
. Denote also
{\mathrm{R}}_{j}:=\sum\limits_{X_{i}\in\mathcal{R}_{j}}d_{i}\chi_{i}\in\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
the regular part of their characters.

Quotients, pure existential completions and Arithmetic Universes
Maria Emilia Maietti, Davide Trotta

Abstract
We provide a new description of Joyal’s arithmetic universes through a characterization of the exact and regular completions of pure existential completions. We show that the regular and exact completions of the pure existential completion of an elementary doctrine P P are equivalent to the 𝗋𝖾𝗀 / 𝗅𝖾𝗑 \mathsf{reg}/\mathsf{lex} and 𝖾𝗑 / 𝗅𝖾𝗑 \mathsf{ex}/\mathsf{lex} -completions, respectively, of the category of predicates of P P . This result generalizes a previous one by the first author with F. Pasquali and G. Rosolini about doctrines equipped with Hilbert’s ϵ \epsilon -operators. Thanks to this characterization, each arithmetic universe in the sense of Joyal can be seen as the exact completion of the pure existential completion of the doctrine of predicates of its Skolem theory. In particular, the initial arithmetic universe in the standard category of ZFC-sets turns out to be the completion with exact quotients of the doctrine of recursively enumerable predicates.



Dedicated to Pieter Hofstra for his inspiring creative work.

1
Introduction

This paper provides a new contribution to the description of Joyal’s arithmetic universes thought the application of a new characterization of free completions of elementary Lawvere doctrines.

Free completions of categories with quotients are ubiquitous in category theory. In particular those leading to exact and regular categories in
[
2
,
4
]
have been widely studied in the literature of category theory, with applications both
to mathematics and computer science, see
[
33
,
28
,
29
,
24
,
38
,
37
,
21
]
.

Such free completions are also involved in the construction of arithmetic universes introduced by A. Joyal to prove Gödel incompleteness theorems in some lectures (still unpublished) in the seventies and recalled in
[
15
]
(see
[
20
,
39
]
for more information). A more general abstract definition of arithmetic universe as list-arithmetic pretopos has been proposed in
[
20
]
.

In more detail, an arithmetic universe in the sense of Joyal can be described as the exact completion – in the sense of
[
2
]
– of the lex category of predicates of a given Skolem theory. In particular, the initial arithmetic universe within the standard category of ZFC-sets turns out to be the exact completion of the lex category of primitive recursive predicates.
In recent years, the regular and exact completions of a lex category have been proved to be instances of more general completions of certain Lawvere doctrines in
[
24
,
23
,
25
]
.

More precisely, the exact completion of a lex category
[
2
,
4
]
, also known as
\mathsf{ex}/\mathsf{lex}
-completion, and the exact completion of a regular category, referred to as
\mathsf{ex}/\mathsf{reg}
-completion, have been proven to be an instance of a more general exact completion
\mathsf{Ex}(P)
relative to an elementary, pure existential doctrine
P
.

The construction of the exact category
\mathsf{Ex}(P)
is, essentially, achieved through the tripos-to-topos construction developed by J.M.E. Hyland, P.T. Johnstone and A.M. Pitts in
[
12
,
31
]
, applied to an elementary existential doctrine.
Similarly, the regular completion of a lex category, also called
\mathsf{reg}/\mathsf{lex}
-completion, introduced in
[
3
,
4
]
has been proved to be a specific instance of a more general construction for doctrines in
[
22
]
, which is the regular completion
\mathsf{Reg}(P)
of an elementary, pure existential doctrine
P
.
The regular and exact completions of doctrines are related via the
\mathsf{ex}/\mathsf{reg}
-completion, namely we have that
\mathsf{Ex}(P)\equiv(\mathsf{Reg}(P))_{\mathsf{ex}/\mathsf{reg}}

[
25
]
.

In this paper we provide a characterization which further relates the exact and regular completions of a lex category
with the notion of exact completion of an elementary existential doctrine as presented in
[
24
,
23
,
25
]
by involving
a third kind of free completion, namely the
pure existential completion
of an elementary doctrine.

The notion of
existential completion
was introduced by the second author in his PhD thesis and later published in
[
34
]
, and it is a construction that freely adds existential quantifiers to a given primary doctrine, along with a class
\Lambda
of base morphisms that is closed under compositions, pullbacks and isomorphisms. To distinguish some particularly interesting instances, this free construction has been renamed as
generalized existential completion
in
[
27
]
.
Following the terminology in
[
27
]
, the
pure existential completion
is
the instance of the generalized existential completion where the class
\Lambda
consists of product projections,
while we call
full existential completion
the instance where
\Lambda
is the class of all the base morphisms.
Furthermore, the pure existential completion also coincides with the restriction to faithful fibrations of the
simple coproduct (or sum) completion
of a fibration employed by P. Hofstra in
[
10
]
, while
the full existential completion coincides with the
\exists
-completion introduced by J. Frey in
[
7
,
6
]
and a particular case of it has also played a significant role in the works by P. Hofstra
[
9
,
8
]
.

In this work, we show that the regular and exact completions of the pure existential completion of an elementary doctrine
P
are equivalent to the
\mathsf{reg}/\mathsf{lex}
and
\mathsf{ex}/\mathsf{lex}
-completions, respectively, of the category of predicates of
P
.

In detail, we show that for an elementary, pure existential doctrine
P
and an elementary subdoctrine
P^{\prime}
of
P
on the same base category,
the regular completion
\mathsf{Reg}(P)
of
P
corresponds to the
\mathsf{reg}/\mathsf{lex}
-completion
({{\mathpzc{Prd}\kern-1.72218pt_{{P^{\prime}}}}})_{\mathsf{reg}/\mathsf{lex}}
of the category of predicates
{{\mathpzc{Prd}\kern-1.72218pt_{{P^{\prime}}}}}
of
P^{\prime}

(via an equivalence induced by the canonical embedding of
{{\mathpzc{Prd}\kern-1.72218pt_{{P^{\prime}}}}}
into
\mathsf{Reg}(P)
)
if and only if
P
is the pure existential completion of
P^{\prime}
(Theorem
56
).

Then, by combining this result with the aforementioned decomposition of exact completions, we immediately deduce
that the exact completion
\mathsf{Ex}(P)
of
P
corresponds to the
\mathsf{ex}/\mathsf{lex}
-completion
({{\mathpzc{Prd}\kern-1.72218pt_{{P^{\prime}}}}})_{\mathsf{ex}/\mathsf{lex}}
of the category of predicates
{{\mathpzc{Prd}\kern-1.72218pt_{{P^{\prime}}}}}
of
P^{\prime}

(again via an equivalence induced by the canonical embedding of
{{\mathpzc{Prd}\kern-1.72218pt_{{P^{\prime}}}}}
into
\mathsf{Ex}(P)
)
if and only if
P
is the pure existential completion of
P^{\prime}
(Corollary
57
).

The crucial intuition is that there is a tight connection between
regular projective objects
of
\mathsf{Reg}(P)
and
pure existential free elements
of
P
. In particular, we show that whenever
P
is the pure existential completion of
P^{\prime}
(and hence the elements of
P^{\prime}
are pure existential free elements according to
[
27
]
), we can use
P^{\prime}
to define a projective cover for
\mathsf{Reg}(P)
which in addition satisfies the property that every object of
\mathsf{Reg}(P)
can be embedded into a projective of this cover.

Our characterization generalizes a previous one in
[
22
]
by the first author with F. Pasquali and G. Rosolini about doctrines equipped with Hilbert’s
\epsilon
-operators
for the fact that in
[
27
]
we showed that a doctrine is equipped with Hilbert’s
\epsilon
-operators if and only if it is equivalent to the pure existential completion of itself.

Then, we apply our characterization to deduce that each arithmetic universe turns out to be the exact completion – in the sense of
[
25
]
– of the pure existential completion of the doctrine of predicates of a given Skolem theory.
As a consequence we deduce that the initial arithmetic universe in the standard category of ZFC-sets is the completion with exact quotients of the doctrine of recursively enumerable
predicates.

Another notable application of our characterization, already published in
[
36
]
by employing our main theorem first presented in
[
26
]
(with a different proof), regards the exact completion of Gödel hyperdoctrines introduced in
[
36
,
35
]
as an equivalent presentation of the restriction to faithful fibrations of Hofstra’s Dialectica fibrations
[
10
]
.

Elementary and existential doctrines

The term

of seminal papers
[

]

some definitions which will be useful in the following. The reader can
find all the details about the theory of elementary and pure existential

[

,

,

,

]

Definition 1
.

A
primary doctrine
is a functor

P\colon\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{InfSl}
from the opposite of a category
\mathcal{C}
with finite products to the category
\mathsf{InfSl}
of inf-semilattices.

We will use the notation
\alpha\wedge\beta
to denote the binary inf of
\alpha
and
\beta
in
P(A)
and
\top_{A}
to denote the top element of
P(A)
.

Definition 2
.

A primary doctrine
P\colon\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{InfSl}
is
elementary
if for every
A
in
\mathcal{C}
there exists an object
\delta_{A}
in
P(A\times A)
, called
fibered equality
, such that

1.

the assignment

\exists_{\langle\operatorname{id}_{A},\operatorname{id}_{A}\rangle}(\alpha):=P_{\pi_{1}}(\alpha)\wedge\delta_{A}

for an element
\alpha
of
P(A)
determines a left adjoint to
P_{\langle\operatorname{id}_{A},\operatorname{id}_{A}\rangle}\colon P(A\times A)\to P(A)
;

2.

for every morphism
e
of the form
\langle\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{2}\rangle\colon X\times A\to X\times A\times A
in
\mathcal{C}
, the assignment

\exists_{e}(\alpha):=P_{\langle\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\rangle}(\alpha)\wedge P_{\langle\pi_{2},\pi_{3}\rangle}(\delta_{A})

for
\alpha
in
P(X\times A)
determines a left adjoint to
P_{e}\colon P(X\times A\times A)\to P(X\times A)
.

Example 3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{=}
be the
(\top,\wedge,=)
-fragment of Intuitionistic Logic, i.e. the fragment with true constant, conjunctions and equality, called Horn-fragment in
[
14
, Sec. D.1.1, p. 810]
. Let
\mathbb{T}
be a theory in such a fragment. Let us denote by
\mathcal{V}
the syntactic category whose objects are contexts (up to
\alpha
-equivalence), and arrows are term substitutions. Consider the functor

\mathsf{LT}_{=}^{\mathbb{T}}\colon\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{InfSl}

defined on a given context
\Gamma
of
\mathcal{V}
by taking
\mathsf{LT}_{=}^{\mathbb{T}}(\Gamma)
as the Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra of well-formed formulas
of
\mathcal{L}_{=}
with free variables in
\Gamma
and on a substitution morphism between contexts by taking the substitution homomorphism between formulas of
the Lindenbaum-Tarski algebras. The functor
\mathsf{LT}_{=}^{\mathbb{T}}
is an elementary doctrine.

.

A primary doctrine
P\colon\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{InfSl}

A
and

\mathcal{C}
for any product projection

{P_{\pi_{A}}}\colon P(A)\to P(A\times B)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\times B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\times\operatorname{id}_{B}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{A}}

is a

of

.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{=,\exists}
be the

Remark 6
.

In a pure existential elementary doctrine, for every arrow

The following examples are discussed in

Example 7

Let

Example 8

Let

We denote by

which preserve the pure existential structure. Similarly, we denote by

3
The pure existential completion

In
[
34
]
the second author introduced a free construction, called
existential completion
, that freely adds left adjoints along a given class of morphisms
\Lambda
(closed under pullbacks, compositions and isomorphisms) to a given primary doctrine. Such a notion has been renamed
generalized existential completion
in
[
27
]
to distinguish some of its relevant instances. Following the terminology in
[
27
]
, the
pure existential completion
is
the instance of the generalized existential completion where the class
\Lambda
consists of product projections.

In
[
27
]
we provided an intrinsic characterization of generalized existential completions through the notion of
existential free elements
with respect to
\Lambda
. Here, we present a further version of this characterization (Theorem
25
) only for the pure existential completion (but it can be smoothly extended to all generalized existential completions) by introducing the notion of
pure existential free elements of an existential doctrine
P
relative to a subdoctrine
P^{\prime}
which slightly generalizes the notion of
pure existential free objects of a doctrine
P
. This result will be relevant in the proof of Theorem
56
.

Since we will mainly perform our calculations on pure existential completions by just referring to their intrinsic characterizations, here we do not recall the original construction from
[
34
]
. We just remind from
[
34
]
that the pure existential completion
{P}^{\exists}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{InfSl}
of a given primary doctrine
P\colon\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{InfSl}
provides a 2-adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{PD}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{ED}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\exists}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

from the 2-category
\mathsf{PD}
of primary doctrines into the 2-category
\mathsf{ED}
of pure existential doctrines
[
34
]
and this justifies its name. Moreover, the pure existential completion preserves the elementary structure as shown in
[
34
]
and this is necessarily so as shown in
[
27
, Thm. 6.1]
.

Now, we start by giving the main definitions necessary for the intrinsic characterization of the pure existential completion.

For the rest of this section, let
P\colon\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{InfSl}
be a fixed pure existential doctrine, and let
P^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{InfSl}
be a fixed subdoctrine of
P
.

Definition 12
.

An element
\alpha
of the fibre
P(A)
is said to be a
pure existential splitting
if
for every projection
\pi_{A}\colon A\times B\to A
and for every element
\beta
of the fibre
P(A\times B)
, whenever
\alpha=\exists_{\pi_{A}}(\beta)
holds
then there exists an arrow
h\colon A\to B
such that

\alpha=P_{\langle\operatorname{id}_{A},h\rangle}(\beta)
. Moreover,
\alpha
is said to be
pure existential free
if for every morphism
f\colon B\to A
,
P_{f}(\alpha)
is a pure existential splitting.

In the following proposition we recall from
[
27
, Prop. 4.4]
a useful equivalent characterization of pure existential splitting elements that is a strengthening of the usual Existence Property:

Proposition 13
.

Let
\alpha
be an element of the fibre
P(A)
. Then
\alpha
is pure existential splitting if and only if for every projection
\pi_{A}\colon A\times B\to A

and for every element
\beta
of the fibre
P(A\times B)
, whenever
\alpha\leq\exists_{\pi_{A}}(\beta)
holds then there exists an arrow
h\colon A\to B
such that
\alpha\leq P_{\langle\operatorname{id}_{A},h\rangle}(\beta)
.

Definition 14
.

P
satisfies the
Rule of Choice
, for short
(RC)
, if whenever

\top_{A}\leq\exists_{\pi_{A}}(\beta)

there exists an arrow
h\colon A\to B
such that

\top_{A}\leq P_{\langle\operatorname{id}_{A},h\rangle}(\beta)
.

Remark 15
.

Observe that
P
satisfies (RC) if and only if for every object
A
of
\mathcal{C}
, the element
\top_{A}\in P(A)
is a pure existential splitting.

Definition 16
.

We say that an element
\alpha
of the fibre
P(A)
is
covered
by an element
\beta\in P(A\times B)
if
\alpha=\exists_{\pi_{A}}(\beta)
.

Definition 17
.

We say that
P
has
enough pure existential free elements
if for every object
A
of
\mathcal{C}
, any element
\alpha\in P(A)
is covered by some pure existential free element
\beta\in P(A\times B)
for some object

of
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 18
.

Observe that if all elements of a subdoctrine
P^{\prime}

of a pure existential doctrine

are pure existential splitting for
P
then they are also pure existential free for
P
, being the doctrine
P^{\prime}
closed under re-indexing.
It also holds that, if a doctrine has enough pure existential free elements then every pure existential splitting is pure existential free. We refer to
[
27
, Lem. 4.11]
for a proof of this fact.

Definition 19
.

We say that
P^{\prime}
is a
pure existential cover
of
P
if for any object
A
of
\mathcal{C}
, every element
\alpha^{\prime}
of
P^{\prime}(A)
is a pure existential splitting for
P
(and hence pure existential free) and every element
\alpha
of
P(A)
is covered by an element of
P^{\prime}
.

We summarize in the following proposition some useful properties, and refer to
[
27
]
for all the details:

Lemma 20
.

The following two results hold:

•

if
P
is the pure existential completion of a primary doctrine
P^{\prime}
then
P^{\prime}
is a pure existential cover of
P
;

•

if
P^{\prime}
is a pure existential cover of
P
, then the existential free elements of
P
coincides exactly with the elements of
P^{\prime}
. Hence, if a pure existential cover exists, it is unique.

7
Conclusion

We have provided a new description of Joyal’s arithmetic universes
[
20
]
as an application of a characterization of regular and exact completions of pure existential completions of elementary doctrines. This characterization extends a previous one proved in
[
22
]
for doctrines equipped with Hilbert’s
\epsilon
-operators.

In particular, we have proved that for elementary doctrines arising as pure existential completions, their regular and exact completions happen to be equivalent to the
\mathsf{reg}/\mathsf{ex}
and
\mathsf{ex}/\mathsf{lex}
-completions, respectively, of the category of predicates associated with the subdoctrine of their pure existential free elements. To reach this goal, we took advantage of the intrinsic characterization of doctrines arising as pure existential completions presented in
[
27
]
, slightly extended with another equivalent presentation here.

Using these results we have deduced that an arithmetic universe in the sense of Joyal can be seen as the exact completion of the pure existential completion of the doctrine of predicates of its Skolem theory. In particular, the initial arithmetic universe in the standard category of ZFC-sets turns out to be the completion with exact quotients of the doctrine of recursively enumerable predicates.

Other examples of application of our characterization include the so called syntactic category in
[
14
]
associated to the so called regular fragment of first-order logic in
[
14
]
(and its effectivization) and the regular and exact completion of a Gödel hyperdoctrine
[
36
,
35
]
.

As future work, we aim to extend our results to regular and exact completions of other classes of doctrines obtained as generalized existential completions, including the case of the
full existential completion
of primary doctrines in the sense of
[
27
]
, as initiated in
[
26
]
, with applications to sheaf theory.

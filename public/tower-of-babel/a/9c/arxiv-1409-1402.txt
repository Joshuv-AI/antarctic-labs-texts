Central limit theorem for moments of spectral measures of Wigner matrices
Trinh Khanh Duy

Abstract
Spectral measures of Wigner matrices are investigated. The Wigner semicircle law for spectral measures is proved. Regard this as the law of large number, the central limit theorem for moments spectral measure is also derived. The proof is based on moment method and combinatorial method.

Introduction

This paper concerns with real Wigner matrices
X_{N}
of the form

X_{N}(j,i)=X_{N}(i,j):=\frac{\xi_{ij}}{\sqrt{N}},\quad 1\leq i\leq j\leq N.

Here
\{\xi_{ii}\}_{1\leq i}
and
\{\xi_{ij}\}_{1\leq i<j}
are two i.i.d. (independent identically distributed) sequences of mean zero (real) random variables. We require in addition that all moments of
\xi_{11}
and
\xi_{12}
are finite and
\xi_{12}
has unit variance, that is,
{\mathbb{E}}[|\xi_{12}|^{2}]=1
.

Let
\lambda_{1}^{(N)}\leq\lambda_{2}^{(N)}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N}^{(N)}
be the eigenvalues of
X_{N}
and

L_{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\lambda_{i}^{(N)}}

be the empirical distribution (measure) of
X_{N}
, where
\delta
denotes the Dirac measure. Then the Wigner semicircle law claims that as
N
tends to infinity,
L_{N}
converges weakly, in probability, to the semicircle distribution. This means that for any bounded continuous function
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
\langle L_{N},f\rangle
converges in probability to
\langle\sigma,f\rangle
. Here the semicircle distribution, denoted by
\sigma
, is the probability distribution supported on
[-2,2]
with density

\sigma(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{4-x^{2}},(-2\leq x\leq 2).

There are many proofs of the Wigner semicircle law. Let us mention here Wigner’s original one which based on combinatoric arguments. Since the semicircle distribution
\sigma
has compact support, in order to prove the Wigner semicircle law, it is sufficient to show that all moments of
L_{N}
converges in probability to the corresponding moments of
\sigma
, namely, for
k=0,1,2,\dots,

(1)

\langle L_{N},x^{k}\rangle\to\langle\sigma,x^{k}\rangle\text{ in probability as $N\to\infty$. }

The
k
th moment of
L_{N}
can be written as

\langle L_{N},x^{k}\rangle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\lambda_{j}^{(N)})^{k}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{N}^{k}(j,j).

Thus, in some respects, the semicircle law states that the average of the diagonal elements of
X_{N}^{k}
converges in probability to
\langle\sigma,x^{k}\rangle
.

With a little modification, one can show that each diagonal element of
X_{N}^{k}
does converge to
\langle\sigma,x^{k}\rangle
as
N
tends to infinity. In particular, for
k=0,1,2,\dots,

(2)

X_{N}^{k}(1,1)\to\langle\sigma,x^{k}\rangle\text{ in probability as $N\to\infty$}.

On the other hand, there is a probability measure
\nu_{N}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

\langle\nu_{N},x^{k}\rangle=X_{N}^{k}(1,1),\quad k=0,1,2,\dots,

called the spectral measure of
(X_{N},e_{1})
, where
e_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. It then follows that the spectral measure
\nu_{N}
also converges weakly, in probability, to the semicircle distribution because of the compact support of the semicircle distribution.

Regard the convergence in probability of moments as the law of large numbers, the central limit theorem for moments of the empirical distributions
L_{N}
has been derived. It is known that scaled by
N
,

N\left(\langle L_{N},x^{k}\rangle-{\mathbb{E}}[\langle L_{N},x^{k}\rangle]\right)

converges weakly to the Gaussian distribution whose variance depends on the second and fourth moments of
\xi_{11}
and
\xi_{12}
. This and the multidimensional version were studied in
[
1
]
. The main purpose of this paper is to investigate the central limit theorem for moments of the spectral measures
\nu_{N}
, or just the central limit theorem for diagonal elements
X_{N}^{k}(1,1)
. The main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

\bar{S}_{N,k}=\sqrt{N}\left(\langle\nu_{N},x^{k}\rangle-{\mathbb{E}}[\langle\nu_{N},x^{k}\rangle]\right)=\sqrt{N}\left(X_{N}^{k}(1,1)-{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)]\right).

Then there exists a sequence of jointly Gaussian random variables
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=2,3,\dots}
independent of
\zeta
which has the same distribution as
\xi_{11}
such that the following hold.

(i)

For even
k
,

\bar{S}_{N,k}{\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}}\eta_{k}\text{ as }N\to\infty.

(ii)

For odd
k\geq 3
,

\bar{S}_{N,k}{\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}}a_{k}\zeta+\eta_{k}\text{ as }N\to\infty,

where
a_{k}
is a constant.

(iii)

For fixed
K
, the joint distribution of
(\bar{S}_{N,1},\bar{S}_{N,2},\dots,\bar{S}_{N,K})
converges to that of
(\zeta,\eta_{2},a_{3}\zeta+\eta_{3},\dots)
.

Here the symbol “
{\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}}
” is used to denote the weak convergence of random variables.

The moment method is used to prove the central limit theorem. However, to compare with combinatoric arguments in
[
1
]
, the big difference is that every word starts at
1
, as we will see in the next section. To overcome this difficulty, we refine method in
[
1
]
using some idea from
[
3
]
. The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 deals with some combinatorics objects such as Wigner words, CLT sentences and key combinatoric arguments. We prove in Section 3 the Wigner semicircle law for spectral measures and investigate the central limit theorem in Section 4.

2
Words, sentences

This section deals with basic notions and key combinatoric arguments needed in the paper.

We begin with the definition of words. A word
w=\{s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k}\}
is a finite sequence of positive integer numbers called letters. A word is closed if the first and the last letters are the same. The length of
w
is denoted by
{\ell}(w):=k
. The support, denoted by
\supp(w)
, is the set of letters appearing in
w
, and the weight,
\wt(w)
, is defined as the cardinality of
\supp(w)
. If we restrict the condition that
s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k}\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, we call
w
an
N
-word, where
N
is a positive integer number.

Two words
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are called equivalent, denoted by
w_{1}\sim w_{2}
, if there is a bijection from
\supp(w_{1})
onto
\supp(w_{2})
, which maps
w_{1}
to
w_{2}
.

A word
w
is associated with an undirected graph
G_{w}=(V_{w},E_{w})
, with
\wt(w)
vertices
V_{w}=\supp(w)
and
(k-1)
edges
E_{w}=\{(s_{i},s_{i+1}),i=1,2,\dots,k-1\}
. Then the word
w
defines a path/walk on the connected graph
G_{w}
. We define the set of self edges as
E_{w}^{s}=\{e\in E_{w}:e=(u,u),u\in V_{w}\}
and the set of connecting edges as
E_{w}^{c}=E_{w}\setminus E_{w}^{s}
. For
e\in E_{w}
, we use
N_{e}^{w}
to denote the number of times this path traverses the edge
e
(in any direction). Note that equivalent words generate the same graphs (up to graph isomorphism)
G_{w}
and the same passage counts
N_{e}^{w}
.

A sentence
a=(w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n})
is a finite sequence of words of at least one word long. The support of
a
is defined as
\supp(a)=\cup_{i=1}^{n}\supp(w_{i})
, and the weight of
a
,
\wt(a)
, is just the cardinality of
\supp(a)
. Two sentences
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are called equivalent, denoted by
a_{1}\sim a_{2}
, if there is a bijection from
\supp(a_{1})
onto
\supp(a_{2})
, which maps
a_{1}
to
a_{2}
.

A graph
G_{a}=(V_{a},E_{a})
associated with a sentence
a=(w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n})
, where
w_{i}=(s_{1}^{i},s_{2}^{i},\dots,s_{{\ell}(w_{i}))}^{i}),i=1,2,\dots,n
, is the graph with vertices
V_{a}=\supp(a)
and undirected edges

E_{a}=\{(s_{j}^{i},s_{j+1}^{i}):j=1,\dots,{\ell}(w_{i})-1,i=1,2,\dots,n\}.

We define the set of self edges as
E_{a}^{s}=\{e\in E_{a}:e=\{u,u\},u\in V_{a}\}
and the set of connecting edges as
E_{a}^{c}=E_{a}\setminus E_{a}^{s}
.

In words, the graph associated with a sentence is obtained by piecing together the graphs of the individual words. Thus, the graph of a sentence may be disconnected. Note that the sentence
a
defines
n
paths in the graph
G_{a}
. For
e\in E_{a}
, we use
N_{e}^{a}
to denote the number of times the union of these paths traverses the edge
e
(in any direction). We note that equivalent sentences generate the same graphs
G_{a}
and the same passage counts
N_{e}^{a}
.

The paper deals with closed words starting at
1
. Let
{\mathcal{W}}^{(N)}
be the set of all
N
-words starting at
1
. Let
{\mathcal{U}}^{(N)}:=\{w\in{\mathcal{W}}^{(N)}:E_{w}^{s}=\emptyset\}
be the subset of
{\mathcal{W}}^{(N)}
consisting of words with no self-edge, and
{\mathcal{V}}^{(N)}:={\mathcal{W}}^{(N)}\setminus{\mathcal{U}}^{(N)}
. Set

{\mathcal{W}}:=\bigcup_{N=1}^{\infty}{\mathcal{W}}^{(N)},\quad{\mathcal{U}}:=\bigcup_{N=1}^{\infty}{\mathcal{U}}^{(N)},\quad{\mathcal{V}}:=\bigcup_{N=1}^{\infty}{\mathcal{V}}^{(N)}.

Henceforth, the sets
{\mathcal{W}}_{k},{\mathcal{W}}_{k}^{(N)},{\mathcal{U}}_{k},{\mathcal{U}}_{k}^{(N)},{\mathcal{V}}_{k},{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{(N)}
with a subscript
k
, are used to denote the corresponding subsets consisting of words of length
k+1
.

A closed word
w
is called a weak Wigner word if
w
visits each edge of
G_{w}
at least twice. Assume that
w
is a weak Wigner word. Since the graph
G_{w}=(V_{w},E_{w})
of
w
is connected and each edge is visited at least twice, it follows that

\wt(w)=\#V_{w}\leq 1+\#E_{w}\leq 1+\frac{{\ell}(w)-1}{2}=\frac{{\ell}(w)+1}{2}.

A weak Wigner word
w
of weight
\wt(w)=({\ell}(w)+1)/2
is called a Wigner word. We also call a single letter word a Wigner word. Note that
w
is a Wigner word only if its length is an odd number.

Here are some properties of a Wigner word
w
(see
[
1
]
or
[
2
, Section 2.1]
for more details):

(i)

the graph
G_{w}
is a tree, that is, a connected graph with no loop;

(ii)

the set of self edges
E_{w}^{s}
is empty;

(iii)

the path
w
visits each connecting edge exactly twice,
N_{e}^{w}=2
for all
e\in E_{w}
.

A pair of words
(w_{1},w_{2})
is called a weak CLT pair if

(P1)

N_{e}^{a}\geq 2
, for all
e\in E_{a}
, where
a=(w_{1},w_{2})
;

(P2)

E_{w_{1}}\cap E_{w_{2}}\neq\emptyset
.

To study properties of weak CLT pairs, we need the following simple but useful property. It is a special case of the so called “the parity principle” (see
[
1
, Lemma 4.4]
).

Lemma 2.1

(Closed walk on a tree)
.

A closed walk on a tree visit each edge an even of times.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
a=(w_{1},w_{2})
be a weak CLT pair. Then

\wt(a)\leq\frac{{\ell}(w_{1})+{\ell}(w_{2})}{2}-1.

Proof.

Let
G_{a}=(V_{a},E_{a})
be the graph of the sentence
a
. Since the pair
(w_{1},w_{2})
visits each edge at least twice, it follows that

\#E_{a}\leq\frac{{\ell}(w_{1})-1+{\ell}(w_{2})-1}{2}.

In addition,
\wt(a)\leq 1+\#E_{a}
because the graph
G_{a}
is connected.

Now, if
\wt(a)\leq\#E_{a}
, then the conclusion immediately follows. Thus, we only need to consider the case
\wt(a)=1+\#E_{a}
, in which
G_{a}
is a tree. Since
w_{1},w_{2}
are closed walks on the tree
G_{a}
, each word
w_{1},w_{2}
visits any edge
e\in E_{a}
an even of times. Consequently, a common edge of
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
is visited at least four times. Therefore,

\#E_{a}\leq\frac{{\ell}(w_{1})-1+{\ell}(w_{2})-1}{2}-1,

and hence the conclusion follows.
∎

A pair
(w_{1},w_{2})
is called a CLT pair if it is a weak CLT pair and in addition,

\wt((w_{1},w_{2}))=\frac{{\ell}(w_{1})+{\ell}(w_{2})}{2}-1.

Denote by
{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{1},k_{2}}
a set of representatives for equivalence classes of CLT pairs
(u_{1},u_{2})
, where
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are
(k_{1}+k_{2})/2
-words of length
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, respectively, provided that
k_{1}+k_{2}
is even. When
k_{1}+k_{2}
is odd, we set
{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{1},k_{2}}=\emptyset
.

The following lemma introduces some properties of CLT pairs. We omit an easy proof.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
a=(u_{1},u_{2})\in{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{1},k_{2}}
with
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 2
, and
k_{1}+k_{2}
being even. Then either
\wt(a)=1+\#E_{a}
or
\wt(a)=\#E_{a}
. Moreover, the following hold.

(i)

If
\wt(a)=1+\#E_{a}
, then
G_{a}
is a tree and

(a)

N_{e}^{u_{i}}=2
, for all
e\in E_{u_{i}},i=1,2
;

(b)

N_{e}^{a}=2
, for all
e\in E_{a}
except one edge
e_{0}
with
N_{e_{0}}^{a}=4
.

(ii)

If
\wt(a)=\#E_{a}
, then

(a)

N_{e}^{u_{i}}=1
, for some
e\in E_{a},i=1,2
;

(b)

N_{e}^{a}=2
, for all
e\in E_{a}
.

A sentence
a=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
is called a weak CLT sentence if the following conditions hold

(S1)

N_{e}^{a}\geq 2,
for all
e\in E_{a}
;

(S2)

for all
i
, there exists
j\neq i
such that
E_{w_{i}}\cap E_{w_{j}}\neq\emptyset
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
a=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
be a weak CLT sentence. Then

\wt(a)\leq 1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{{\ell}(w_{i})-2}{2}.

A sentence
a=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
is called a CLT sentence if
a
is a weak CLT sentence and the above equality holds, namely,

\wt(a)=1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{{\ell}(w_{i})-2}{2}.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
a=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
be a CLT sentence with
w_{i}\in{\mathcal{U}},i=1,2,\dots,n
. Then the following hold.

(i)

For each
i
, there exists unique
j\neq i
such that
E_{w_{i}}\cap E_{w_{j}}\neq\emptyset
.

(ii)

The number
n
is even and there exists a perfect matching
\sigma\in S_{n}
such that

(a)

a_{i}=(w_{\sigma(2i-1)},w_{\sigma(2i)})
is a CLT pair,
i=1,2,\dots,n/2
;

(b)

\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n/2}
are disjoint sets, where
G_{i}=(V_{i},E_{i})
denotes the graph of
a_{i}
;

(c)

\{\{V_{i}\setminus\{1\}\}\}_{i=1}^{n/2}
are disjoint sets.

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

This lemma is a special case of
[
2
, Lemma 2.1.34]
. However, we mention the proof here because it will be used in the next lemma. Let
a=(w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n})
be a weak CLT sentence, where
w_{i}=\{s_{i,j}\}_{j=1,\dots,{\ell}(w_{i})}
. Let
I=\cup_{i=1}^{n}\{i\}\times\{1,2,\dots,{\ell}(w_{i})-1\}
and let
A
be an
n
rows left-justified table whose entries are the edges of
a
, namely,

A_{ij}=(s_{i,j},s_{i,j+1}),\quad(i,j)\in I.

Let
G_{a}=(V_{a},E_{a})
be the graph of the sentence
a
. Note that
G_{a}
is a connected graph because every word is a closed word starting at
1
. Let
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be any spanning tree in
G_{a}
. Then we have
\wt(a)=1+\#E^{\prime}
and so in order to proof the lemma, we just have to bound
\#E^{\prime}
.

Now let
X=\{X_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in I}
be a table of the same “shape” as
A
, but with all entries equal either to
0
or
1
. We call
X
an edge-bounding table if the following conditions hold:

(E1)

for all
(i,j)\in I
, if
X_{ij}=1
, then
A_{ij}\in E^{\prime}
;

(E2)

for each
e\in E^{\prime}
, there exist distinct
(i_{1},j_{1}),(i_{2},j_{2})\in I
such that
X_{i_{1},j_{1}}=X_{i_{2},j_{2}}=1
and
A_{i_{1},j_{1}}=A_{i_{2},j_{2}}=e
;

(E3)

for each
e\in E^{\prime}
and index
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, if
e
appears in the
i
th row of
A
, then there exists
(i,j)\in I
such that
A_{ij}=e
and
X_{ij}=1
.

For an edge-bounding table
X
, the corresponding quantity
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in I}X_{ij}
bounds
\#E^{\prime}
, whence the terminology. At least one edge-bounding table exists, namely the table with a
1
in position
(i,j)
for each
(i,j)\in I
such that
A_{ij}\in E^{\prime}
and
0
’s elsewhere. Now let
X
be an edge-bounding table such that for some index
i_{0}
all the entries of
X
in the
i_{0}
th row are equal to
1
. Then all egdes of
w_{i_{0}}
belongs to
E^{\prime}
. In other words,
w_{i_{0}}
is a closed walk in the tree
G^{\prime}
, hence every entry in the
i_{0}
th row of
A
appears there an even number of times and a
fortiori
at least twice. Now choose
(i_{0},j_{0})\in I
such that
A_{(i_{0},j_{0})}\in E^{\prime}
appears in more than one row of
A
. Let
Y
be the table obtained by replacing the entry
1
of
X
in position
(i_{0},j_{0})
by the entry
0
. Then it is not difficult to check that
Y
is again an edge-bounding table. Proceeding in this way we can find an edge-bounding table with
0
appearing at least once in every row, and hence we have

\#E^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\#I-n)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}({\ell}(w_{i})-2)}{2}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

(i) Assume that
a=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
is a CLT sentence with
w_{i}\in{\mathcal{U}},i=1,2,\dots,n
. Let
G_{a},G^{\prime}
be the graph of
a
and the spanning tree as in the proof of Lemma
2.4
. Moreover, let
X
be an edge-bounding table satisfying the condition that at least one entry is
0
in each row. Then, recall that

\#E^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in I}X_{ij}\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}({\ell}(w_{i})-2)}{2}.

Therefore, the above two inequalities must become equalities by the definition of CLT sentence. Consequently, the edge-bounding table
X
has exactly one
0
-entry in each row. For each
i
, let
e_{i}
denote the edge
A_{ij}
at the position
X_{ij}=0
. Note that by the first property (property (E1)) of the edge-bounding table
X
,

(*)

all edges of
w_{i}
, except at most one edge
e_{i}
, belong to
E^{\prime}
.

We claim that for each
i
, there is a unique
\check{i}\neq i
such that
e_{\check{i}}=e_{i}
. This claim is shown as follows.

Let

N^{\prime}_{e}:=\#\{(i,j)\in I:X_{ij}=1,A_{ij}=e\}.

Then the two equalities imply that
N^{\prime}_{e}=2
for all
e\in E^{\prime}
.

Uniqueness.
Assume that there are at least three words
w_{i_{1}},w_{i_{2}},w_{i_{3}}
such that
e_{i_{1}}=e_{i_{2}}=e_{i_{3}}=(s,\bar{s})
. Since we consider words which do not contain self edge, assume without loss of generality that
s\neq 1
. Then each word
w_{i_{k}}
contains a walk on the tree
G^{\prime}
from
1
to
s
(or from
s
to
1
), which can be chosen to traverse only those edges
A_{i_{k},j}
with
X_{i_{k},j}=1
. Therefore, there exists some edge
e
with
N^{\prime}_{e}\geq 3
, which is a contradiction.

Existence.
Now fix some index
i
. Then either
e_{i}\not\in E^{\prime}
or
e_{i}\in E^{\prime}
.

Case 1:
e_{i}\not\in E^{\prime}
. In this case,
N_{e}^{w_{i}}=1
by (*). Thus,
e_{i}\in E_{w_{i_{1}}}
for some
i_{1}\not=i
because
N_{e}^{a}\geq 2
(see property (S1)). It also follows from (*) that
e_{i_{1}}=e_{i}
. Assume that
e_{i}=(s,\bar{s})
and
w_{i}
is a walk
1\to s\to\bar{s}\to 1
. The word
w_{i_{1}}
may be either
1\to s\to\bar{s}\to 1
or
1\to\bar{s}\to s\to 1
. We construct a new word/walk
w_{i}\vee w_{i_{1}}
as follows. Walk from
1
to
s
by
w_{i}
, then go to
\bar{s}
by
w_{i_{1}}
, an back to
1
by
w_{i}
. A new word
w_{i}\vee w_{i_{1}}
of length
{\ell}(w_{i})+{\ell}(w_{i_{1}})-3
is a closed walk on a tree
G^{\prime}
, and thus
N_{e}^{w_{i}\vee w_{i_{1}}}
is even, and hence is at least
2
. It follows that
N_{e}^{w_{i}\vee w_{i_{1}}}=2
because it is bounded by
N^{\prime}_{e}
.

Case 2.
e_{i}\in E^{\prime}
. In this case,
w_{i}
is a closed walk on the tree
G^{\prime}
, which implies that
N_{e_{i}}^{w_{i}}
is even. Moreover, it is bounded by
1+N^{\prime}_{e}=3
. Thus
N_{e_{i}}^{w_{i}}=2
. Therefore, in the
i
th row, there is only one pair
(i,j)
such that
X_{i,j}=1
and
A_{ij}=e_{i}
. By property (E2) of edge-bounding table, there is another pair
(i_{1},j_{1})
such that
X_{i_{1},j_{1}}=1
and
A_{i_{1},j_{1}}=e_{i}
. Note that
i_{1}\neq i
.

Next, we show that
e_{i_{1}}=e_{i}
. Indeed, assume to the contrary that
e_{i_{1}}\neq e_{i}
. There are two cases to consider.

•

if
e_{i_{1}}\in E^{\prime}
, then by the same argument as in the beginning of case 2, it follows that
N_{e_{i}}^{w_{i_{1}}}=2
, therefore
N^{\prime}_{e_{i}}\geq 3
, which is a contradiction;

•

if
e_{i_{1}}\not\in E^{\prime}
, then by case 1, there exists
i_{2}
with
e_{i_{2}}=e_{i_{1}}
and
N_{e_{i}}^{w_{i_{1}}\vee w_{i_{2}}}=2
. It also follows that
N^{\prime}_{e}\geq 3
, the same contradiction.

We also construct a new word/walk
w_{i}\vee w_{i_{1}}
as in case 1.

(ii) It is clear that
n
must be an even number because
n
words
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}
can be partition in pairs which have the same
e_{i}
. We construct a permutation
\sigma
on
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
as follows. Let

\begin{cases}\sigma(1)=1,&\\
\sigma(2)=j,&\text{if $(w_{1},w_{j})$ is a pair.}\end{cases}

Then by induction, we define for
i=2,3,\dots,n/2
,

\begin{cases}\sigma(2i+1)=\min\{\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{\sigma(1),\dots,\sigma(2i)\},&\\
\sigma(2i+2)=j,&\text{if $(w_{\sigma(2i+1)},w_{j})$ is a pair.}\end{cases}

It is clear that
\sigma
is a perfect matching. Moreover words/walks
w_{\sigma(2i-1)}\vee w_{\sigma(2i)}
are distinct walks on the tree
G^{\prime}
. The rest of lemma follows.
∎

3
The Wigner semicircle law for spectral measures

In this section, we will show that spectral measures of Wigner matrices also converge weakly, in probability, to the semicircle distribution.
Recall that
\{\xi_{ij}\}_{1\leq i\leq j}
are independent real random variables with the following properties:

(i)

\{\xi_{ii}\}_{1\leq i}
is an i.i.d. sequence with
{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{11}]=0
and
{\mathbb{E}}[|\xi_{11}|^{p}]<\infty,p=2,3,\dots
;

(ii)

\{\xi_{ij}\}_{1\leq i<j}
is another i.i.d. sequence with
{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{12}]=0,{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{12}^{2}]=1
and
{\mathbb{E}}[|\xi_{12}|^{p}]<\infty,p=3,4,\dots.

Recall also that the Wigner matrix
X_{N}
is defined as

X_{N}(i,j)=X_{N}(j,i)=\frac{\xi_{ij}}{\sqrt{N}},\quad 1\leq i\leq j\leq N.

We begin with the following expression for
X_{N}^{k}(1,1)
,

\displaystyle X_{N}^{k}(1,1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k-1}=1}^{N}X_{1,i_{1}}X_{i_{1},i_{2}}\cdots X_{i_{k-1},1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{\frac{k}{2}}}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k-1}=1}^{N}\xi_{(1,i_{1})}\xi_{(i_{1},i_{2})}\cdots\xi_{(i_{k-1},1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{\frac{k}{2}}}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k}^{(N)}}T_{w},

where
T_{w}=\prod_{e\in E_{w}}\xi_{e}^{N_{e}^{w}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(i)

For odd
k
,

{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)]\to 0\text{ as }N\to\infty.

(ii)

For even
k
,

{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)]\to C_{k/2}\text{ as }N\to\infty,

where
C_{n}
denotes the
n
th Catalan number,

C_{n}=\frac{\begin{pmatrix}2n\\
n\end{pmatrix}}{n+1}=\frac{(2n)!}{(n+1)!n!},

which is the numbers of equivalence classes of Wigner words of length
2n+1
.

Proof.

It is clear that

{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)]=\frac{1}{N^{k/2}}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k}^{(N)}}{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w}].

Recall that
T_{w}=\prod_{e\in E_{w}}\xi_{e}^{N_{e}^{w}}
, which implies that
{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w}]=\prod_{e\in E_{w}}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{e}^{N_{e}^{w}}]
. Thus
E[T_{w}]=0
unless
w
is a weak Wigner word.

Let
{\mathcal{W}}_{k;t}
denotes a set of representatives for equivalence classes of weak Wigner words
w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k}^{(t)}
of weight
t
. Then for
N\geq t
, given a word
w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k,t}
, there are exactly

C_{N,t}:=(N-1)(N-2)\cdots(N-t+1)

words in
{\mathcal{W}}_{k}^{(N)}
that are equivalent to
w
.

Since the weight of a weak Wigner word of length
k+1
is bounded by
(k/2+1)
, and two equivalent words have the same graphs, we can rewrite the expression of
{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)]
as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{k/2}}\sum_{t\leq\frac{k}{2}+1}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k;t}}\sum_{w^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k}^{(N)}:w^{\prime}\sim w}{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{k/2}}\sum_{t\leq\frac{k}{2}+1}C_{N,t}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k;t}}{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\leq\frac{k}{2}+1}\frac{C_{N,t}}{N^{k/2}}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k;t}}{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w}].

Note that as
N\to\infty
,
C_{N,t}/N^{t-1}\to 1
. Note also that the cardinality of
{\mathcal{W}}_{k;t}
is finite and that
{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w}]<\infty
because all moments of
\{\xi_{ij}\}
are finite. Therefore, as
N\to\infty
,

{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)]\to\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $k$ is odd,}\\
\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k;k/2+1}}{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w}],&\text{if $k$ is even}.\end{cases}

Finally,
w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k;k/2+1}
means that
w
is a Wigner word, and hence
{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w}]=1
by properties of Wigner words. Thus for even number
k
, the limit of
{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)]
is equal to the number of equivalence classes of Wigner words of length
k+1
, which is nothing but the
(k/2)
th Catalan number. The lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

It holds that

{\mathbb{E}}[(X_{N}^{k}(1,1)-{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)])^{2}]\to 0\text{ as }N\to\infty.

Proof.

We begin with the following expression

X_{N}^{k}(1,1)-{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)]=\frac{1}{N^{k/2}}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k}^{(N)}}(T_{w}-{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w}])=:\frac{1}{N^{k/2}}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k}^{(N)}}\bar{T}_{w}.

Here
\bar{T}_{w}:=T_{w}-{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w}]
. Then

(X_{N}^{k}(1,1)-{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)])^{2}=\frac{1}{N^{k}}\sum_{w_{1},w_{2}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k}^{(N)}}\bar{T}_{w_{1}}\bar{T}_{w_{2}}=\frac{1}{N^{k}}\sum_{w_{1},w_{2}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k}^{(N)}}\bar{T}_{(w_{1},w_{2})},

where
\bar{T}_{(w_{1},w_{2})}:=\bar{T}_{w_{1}}\bar{T}_{w_{2}}
.

It is clear that
{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{(w_{1},w_{2})}]=0
unless
(w_{1},w_{2})
is a weak CLT pair. Similar argument as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
with noting that
\wt((w_{1},w_{2}))\leq k
if
(w_{1},w_{2})
is a weak CLT pair, we have

{\mathbb{E}}[(X_{N}^{k}(1,1)-{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)])^{2}]=\sum_{t\leq k}\frac{C_{N,t}}{N^{k}}\sum_{(w_{1},w_{2})\in{\mathcal{W}}_{k,k;t}}{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{(w_{1},w_{2})}].

Here
{\mathcal{W}}_{k,k;t}
denotes a set of representatives for equivalence classes of weak CLT pair/sentence
(w_{1},w_{2})
of weight
t
, where
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are both
t
-words of length
k+1
. Therefore

{\mathbb{E}}[(X_{N}^{k}(1,1)-{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)])^{2}]\to 0\text{ as }N\to\infty,

which completes the proof.
∎

As a direct consequence of Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.3
.

As
N\to\infty
,
X_{N}^{k}(1,1)
converges in
L^{2}
, and hence, converges in probability to
\langle\sigma,x^{k}\rangle
.

We are now in a position to investigate the semicircle law for spectral measures of Wigner matrices.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
A
be a real symmetric matrix of degree
N
and
v
be a unit vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. Then the spectral measure
\mu
of
(A,v)
is the probability measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{k}\mu(dx)=(A^{k}v,v),\quad k=0,1,2,\dots,

where
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
denotes the inner product in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.

Let
A
be a real symmetric matrix. Let
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N}
be the eigenvalues of
A
, and let
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{N}
be corresponding eigenvectors which are chosen to be an orthonormal system of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. Then the spectral decomposition of
A
can be written as

A=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}v_{j}v_{j}^{T}.

Consequently,

A^{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}^{k}v_{j}v_{j}^{T},

and thus,

(A^{k}v,v)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}^{k}(v,v_{j})^{2}.

Therefore, the spectral measure of
(A,v)
is given by

\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(v,v_{j})^{2}\delta_{\lambda_{j}}.

Now let
\nu_{N}
be the spectral measure of
(X_{N},e_{1})
, where
e_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. Then by definition,

\langle\nu_{N},x^{k}\rangle=(X_{N}^{k}e_{1},e_{1})=X_{N}^{k}(1,1).

Theorem 3.5
.

(i)

The
k
th moment of
\nu_{N}
converges in probability to that of the semicircle law, namely,

\langle\nu_{N},x^{k}\rangle\to\langle\sigma,x^{k}\rangle\text{ in probability as $N\to\infty$}.

(ii)

The spectral measure
\nu_{N}
converges weakly, in probability, to the semicircle distribution.

Proof.

The statement (i) is just Lemma
3.3
.

Since
\sigma
has compact support, we will show that (ii) follows from (i). Indeed,
let
f
be a bounded continuous function on
{\mathbb{R}}
. We need to prove that

\langle\nu_{N},f\rangle\to\langle\sigma,f\rangle\text{ in probability as $N\to\infty$.}

Recall that
\sigma
is supported in
[-2,2]
, which implies that
\langle\sigma,x^{2k}\rangle\leq 2^{2k}
. Let
B>2
be fixed. Then, for
k=0,1,\dots
,

\displaystyle|\langle\nu_{N},x^{k}{\bf 1}_{\{|x|>B\}}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}x^{k}{\bf 1}_{\{|x|>B\}}d\nu_{N}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|x|^{k}{\bf 1}_{\{|x|>B\}}d\nu_{N}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{B^{2n-k}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}x^{2n}d\nu_{N}(x)=\frac{\langle\nu_{N},x^{2n}\rangle}{B^{2n-k}},\text{ for $k<2n$}.

By letting
N\to\infty
, we obtain

|\langle\nu_{N},x^{k}{\bf 1}_{\{|x|>B\}}\rangle|\leq\frac{\langle\nu_{N},x^{2n}\rangle}{B^{2n-k}}\underset{\text{as $N\to\infty$ }}{\overset{\text{in probability}}{\longrightarrow}}\frac{\langle\sigma,x^{2n}\rangle}{B^{2n-k}}\leq\frac{2^{2n}}{B^{2n-k}}.

Note that
2^{2n}/B^{2n-k}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Thus

\langle\nu_{N},x^{k}{\bf 1}_{\{|x|>B\}}\rangle\to 0\text{ in probability as $N\to\infty$.}

Consequently, for any polynomial
Q
,

(3)

\langle\nu_{N},Q{\bf 1}_{\{|x|>B\}}\rangle\to 0\text{ in probability as $N\to\infty$.}

Given
\varepsilon>0
, there is a polynomial
Q
such that

\sup_{|x|\leq B}|f(x)-Q(x)|\leq\varepsilon.

Then consider the following decomposition

\displaystyle\langle\nu_{N},f\rangle-\langle\sigma,f\rangle=\langle\nu_{N},f{\bf 1}_{\{|x|>B\}}\rangle+\langle\nu_{N},(f-Q){\bf 1}_{\{|x|\leq B\}}\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\nu_{N},Q{\bf 1}_{\{|x|>B\}}\rangle+(\langle\nu_{N},Q\rangle-\langle\sigma,Q\rangle)+\langle\sigma,Q-f\rangle.

The first term and the third term converges to
0
in probability by (
3
). The fourth term converges to
0
in probability by (i) of this theorem. Finally, the second term and the fifth term is bounded by
\varepsilon
. Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, it follows that
\langle\nu_{N},f\rangle
converges to
\langle\sigma,f\rangle
in probability. The proof is complete.
∎

4
Central limit theorem for moments of spectral measures

This section investigates weak limits of moments of spectral measures, more precisely, the weak limits of
\sqrt{N}(X_{N}^{k}(1,1)-{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)])
as
N
tends to infinity.

4.1
Zero diagonal

Recall that

X_{N}^{k}(1,1)=\frac{1}{N^{\frac{k}{2}}}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}^{(N)}_{k}}T_{w},

where
T_{w}=\prod_{e\in E_{w}}\xi_{e}^{N_{e}^{w}}
.

Let

\displaystyle Y_{N,k}

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{N}\left(\frac{1}{N^{\frac{k}{2}}}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{U}}^{(N)}_{k}}(T_{w}-{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w}])\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{\frac{k-1}{2}}}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{U}}^{(N)}_{k}}\bar{T}_{w}

\displaystyle\left(=\sqrt{N}(X_{N}^{k}(1,1)-{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)]),\text{ if }\xi_{11}=0\right).

For a sentence
a=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
, we denote

\bar{T}_{a}=\bar{T}_{(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})}=\bar{T}_{w_{1}}\cdots\bar{T}_{w_{n}}.

Next, we consider
{\mathbb{E}}[Y_{N,k_{1}}Y_{N,k_{2}}]
for fixed
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 2
. It is clear that

{\mathbb{E}}[Y_{N,k_{1}}Y_{N,k_{2}}]=\frac{1}{N^{\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}}{2}-1}}\sum_{w_{1}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{(N)}_{k_{1}},w_{2}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{(N)}_{k_{2}}}{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{(w_{1},w_{2})}].

Lemma 4.1
.

For
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 2
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[Y_{N,k_{1}}Y_{N,k_{2}}]=\sum_{(w_{1},w_{2})\in{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{1},k_{2}}}{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{(w_{1},w_{2})}].

The limit is positive, if
k_{1}+k_{2}
is even, and only depends on the second and the fourth moments of
\xi_{12}
. It is zero, if
k_{1}+k_{2}
is an odd number.

Proof.

It is clear that
{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{(w_{1},w_{2})}]=0
unless
(w_{1},w_{2})
is a weak CLT pair. Let
{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{1},k_{2}}^{(t)}
denote a set of representatives for equivalence classes of weak CLT pairs
(w_{1},w_{2})
of weight
t
, where
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are
t
-words of lengths
k_{1}+1
and
k_{2}+1
, respectively. By Lemma
2.2
,
t\leq(k_{1}+k_{2})/2
unless
{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{1},k_{2}}^{(t)}=\emptyset
. For
t=(k_{1}+k_{2})/2
, the set
{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{1},k_{2}}^{(t)}
is just a set of representatives for equivalence classes of CLT pairs
{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{1},k_{2}}
. An argument similar to Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[Y_{N,k_{1}}Y_{N,k_{2}}]=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $k_{1}+k_{2}$ is odd,}\\
\sum\limits_{(w_{1},w_{2})\in{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{1},k_{2}}}{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{(w_{1},w_{2})}],&\text{if $k_{1}+k_{2}$ is even}.\end{cases}

Next, let
(w_{1},w_{2})\in{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{1},k_{2}}
. If
\wt(a)=1+\#E_{a}
, then by Lemma
2.3
(i),
{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w_{i}}]=1,i=1,2
. Moreover,
{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w_{1}}T_{w_{2}}]={\mathbb{E}}[\prod_{e\in E_{a}}\xi_{e}^{N_{e}^{a}}]={\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{e_{0}}^{4}]={\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{12}^{4}]
, where
e_{0}
is the only edge with
N_{e_{0}}^{a}=4
. Thus

{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{(w_{1},w_{2})}]={\mathbb{E}}[T_{w_{1}}T_{w_{2}}]-{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w_{1}}]{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w_{2}}]={\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{12}^{4}]-1\geq 0.

The last inequality holds because
{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{12}^{4}]-1={\mathbb{E}}[(\xi_{12}^{2}-1)^{2}]\geq 0
.

Now, if
\wt(a)=\#E_{a}
, then
{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w_{i}}]=0
because there exists an edge which is visited only one time by
w_{i},i=1,2
. Further, since each edge is visited exactly two times by
(w_{1},w_{2})
, it follows that
{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w_{1}}T_{w_{2}}]=1
. Combining those we have

{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{(w_{1},w_{2})}]=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $\wt(a)=\#E_{a}$},\\
{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{12}^{4}]-1\geq 0,&\text{if $\#\wt(a)=1+E_{a}$}.\end{cases}

Finally, the set of CLT pairs
a=(w_{1},w_{2})
for which
\wt(a)=\#E_{a}
is not empty. Thus, the rest of this lemma follows.
∎

By an argument similar to the previous lemma, Lemma
2.4
implies the following statement.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{n}\geq 2
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}Y_{N,k_{i}}\right]=\sum_{(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}}{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})}].

Here
{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}
denotes a set of representatives for equivalence classes of CLT sentences
a=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
, where
w_{i}\in{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{i}}^{(t)}
,
t=1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}-1}{2}
.

Let

A(k_{1},k_{2}):=\sum_{(w_{1},w_{2})\in{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{1},k_{2}}}{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{(w_{1},w_{2})}].

Then the matrix
(A(k,l))_{k,l=2,3,\dots}
is symmetric. Each finite block
(A(k,l))_{k,l=2}^{n}
is positive semidefinite because it is the limit of the covariance matrix of random variables
(Y_{N,k})_{k=2,\dots,n}
. Thus, there exists a sequence of mean zero jointly Gaussian random variables
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=2,3,\dots}
defined on the same probability space such that

{\mathbb{E}}[\eta_{k}\eta_{l}]=A(k,l).

Lemma 4.3
.

For even number
n
,

(4)

\sum_{(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in{\mathcal{U}}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}}{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})}]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in S_{n}\\
\sigma\text{: perfect matching }\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{n/2}A(k_{\sigma(2i-1)},k_{\sigma(2i)}).

Proof.

It is a direct consequence of Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

The joint distribution of
\{Y_{N,k}\}_{k=2}^{K}
converges to that of
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=2}^{K}
as
N
tends to infinity for any fixed
K\geq 2
.

Proof.

The left hand side of (
4
) is exactly the Wick formula for the expectation

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{k_{i}}\right].

Thus, for any even number
n
, and for any
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 2
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}Y_{N,k_{i}}\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{k_{i}}\right].

This also holds if
n
is odd, in which both sides are zero. Therefore, the joint distribution of
\{Y_{N,k}\}_{k=2}^{K}
converges to that of
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=2}^{K}
because Gaussian distributions are characterized by their moments.
∎

4.2
General case

Let

Z_{N,k}=\frac{1}{N^{\frac{k-1}{2}}}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{(N)}}(T_{w}-{\mathbb{E}}[T_{w}])=\frac{1}{N^{\frac{k-1}{2}}}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{(N)}}\bar{T}_{w}.

It is clear that
{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{N,k}]=0
. We consider

{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{N,k}^{2}]=\frac{1}{N^{k-1}}\sum_{w_{1},w_{2}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{(N)}}{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{(w_{1},w_{2})}].

Recall that
(w_{1},w_{2})
is a weak CLT pair if

(P1)

N_{e}^{a}\geq 2
, for all
e\in E_{a}
, where
a=(w_{1},w_{2})
;

(P2)

E_{w_{1}}\cap E_{w_{2}}\neq\emptyset
.

For a word
w\in{\mathcal{V}}
, let
\check{w}\in{\mathcal{U}}
be the word constructed from
w
by deleting every adjacent same letter. Then the graph of
\check{w}
is obtained from that of
w
by removing all self edges. The following lemma refines Lemma
2.2

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
w_{1},w_{2}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}
be a weak CLT pair. Then

(i)

\wt((w_{1},w_{2}))\leq k
, if
k
is odd;

(ii)

\wt((w_{1},w_{2}))\leq k-1
, if
k
is even.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
2.2
. Let
\check{w}_{1},\check{w}_{2}\in{\mathcal{U}}
be the words obtained from
w_{1},w_{2}
by deleting every adjacent same letter. Let
\check{a}=(\check{w}_{1},\check{w}_{2})
. Then
N_{e}^{\check{a}}\geq 2
for all
e\in E_{\check{a}}
. Let
G_{\check{a}}=(V_{\check{a}},E_{\check{a}})
be the graph of
\check{a}
. Note that
G_{\check{a}}
is connected because both
\check{w}_{1}
and
\check{w}_{2}
are words started from
1
. Note also that
\wt(a)=\wt(\check{a})
. Since
N_{e}^{\check{a}}\geq 2
for all
e\in{\mathbb{E}}_{\check{a}}
, it follows that

\#E_{\check{a}}\leq\frac{1}{2}(l(\check{w}_{1})-1+l(\check{w}_{2})-1)\leq\frac{1}{2}(l(w_{1})-2+l(w_{2})-2)=k-1.

The last inequality holds because
l(\check{w}_{i})\leq l(w_{i})-1=k,i=1,2
. Thus

\wt(a)=\wt(\check{a})\leq 1+\#E_{\check{a}}\leq k.

Next, we show that
\wt(a)=k
does not hold if
k
is even. Indeed, assume that
\wt(a)=k
. It follows that
\wt(a)=\wt(\check{a})=1+\#E_{\check{a}}
, and hence the graph
G_{\check{a}}
is a tree. In this case, it also implies that
l(\check{w}_{i})={\ell}(w_{i})-1=k,i=1,2
. Thus
\check{w}_{i}
is a closed walk of length
k
, which is even, on the tree
G_{\check{a}}
, which is impossible. The lemma is proved.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{k,k;t}
denote a set of representatives for equivalence classes of weak CLT pairs
(w_{1},w_{2})
, where
w_{1},w_{2}\in{\mathcal{V}}
are
t
-words of length
k+1
. Then similarly to Lemma
2.2
, we can show that

(5)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{N,k}^{2}]=\sum_{(w_{1},w_{2})\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k,k;k}}{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{(w_{1},w_{2})}],

which is zero if
k

For odd

{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
denote a set of representatives for equivalent classes of words
w
of length

, for which
N_{(1,1)}^{w}=1
and
\check{w}
is a Wigner word. Let
a_{k}

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
k\geq 3
be an odd number. Let
(w_{1},w_{2})\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k,k;k}
. Then the following hold.

(i)

w_{i}
is equivalent to some element of
{\mathcal{A}}_{k},i=1,2
.

(ii)

\supp(w_{1})\cap\supp(w_{2})=\{1\}
.

(iii)

{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{w_{1},w_{2}}]={\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{11}^{2}]
.

(iv)

(6)

\sum_{(w_{1},w_{2})\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k,k;k}}{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{T}_{(w_{1},w_{2})}]=a_{k}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{11}^{2}].

Proof.

Let
\check{a}
be as in the proof of Lemma
4.5
. Recall that, in this case, both
\check{w}_{1}
and
\check{w}_{2}
are walks of length
k
on the tree
G_{\check{a}}
and
\check{w}_{i}
visit each of it edges exactly twice,
i=1,2
. Thus
\check{w}_{1}
and
\check{w}_{2}
are Wigner words. Moreover,
N_{e}^{\check{a}}=2
for all

, which implies that
\supp(\check{w}_{1})\cap\supp(\check{w}_{2})=\{1\}
. Now, it follows from the condition (P2),
E_{w_{1}}\cap E_{w_{2}}\neq\emptyset
, that
(1,1)
must be a common edge of

and

. Therefore, we obtain (i) and also (ii).

(iii) and (iv) are direct consequences of (i) and (ii).

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
k
be an odd number. Then the following hold.

(i)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{11}Z_{N,k}]=a_{k}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{11}^{2}].

(ii)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[(Z_{N,k}-a_{k}\xi_{11})^{2}]=0.

Proof.

(i)
It follows from the definition of
Z_{N,k}
that

{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{11}Z_{N,k}]=\frac{1}{N^{\frac{k-1}{2}}}\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{(N)}}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{11}\bar{T}_{w}].

It is clear that
{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{11}\bar{T}_{w}]=0
unless a word
w
satisfies the following conditions

•

N_{(1,1)}^{w}\geq 1
;

•

N_{e}^{w}\geq 2
for all
e\in E_{w}\setminus\{(1,1)\}
.

Assume that a word
w
satisfies the above conditions. Let
\check{w}
be the simplified word of
w
. Then
\check{w}
is a word of length at most
k
, which visits each edge at least twice. Thus,

\wt(w)=\wt(\check{w})\leq\#E_{\check{a}}+1\leq\frac{k-1}{2}+1=\frac{k+1}{2}.

The equality
\wt(w)=(k+1)/2
holds if
\check{w}
is a Wigner word of length
k
, or equivalently, if
w
is equivalent to some word in
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
.

Now by a standard argument as in the proof of Lemma
3.1

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{11}Z_{N,k}]=\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{A}}_{k}}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{11}\bar{T}_{w}]=a_{k}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{11}^{2}].

(ii) follows from (i), the limit (
5
) and the expression (
6
). The lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

(i)

For even
k
,
Z_{N,k}
converges in probability to zero.

(ii)

For odd
k
,
Z_{N,k}
converges in probability to
a_{k}\xi_{11}
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\zeta
be a random variable which has the same distribution as
\xi_{11}
and is independent of
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 2}
. Let
\bar{S}_{N,k}=\sqrt{N}(X_{N}^{k}(1,1)-{\mathbb{E}}[X_{N}^{k}(1,1)])
. Then the following holds.

(i)

For even
k
,

\bar{S}_{N,k}{\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}}\eta_{k}\text{ as }N\to\infty.

(ii)

For odd
k\geq 3
,

\bar{S}_{N,k}{\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}}a_{k}\zeta+\eta_{k}\text{ as }N\to\infty.

(iii)

For fixed
K
, the joint distribution of
(\bar{S}_{N,1},\bar{S}_{N,2},\dots,\bar{S}_{N,K})
converges to that of
(\zeta,\eta_{2},a_{3}\zeta+\eta_{3},\dots)
.

Proof.

We only need to prove (iii). Let
a_{1}=1,Y_{N,1}=0
and
Z_{N,1}=\xi_{11}
.
For even
k
, let
a_{k}=0
. Note that

\bar{S}_{N,k}=Y_{N,k}+Z_{N,k}=Y_{N,k}+a_{k}\xi_{11}+(Z_{N,k}-a_{k}\xi_{11}).

For any real numbers
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
, we consider

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\alpha_{k}\bar{S}_{N,k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{K}\alpha_{k}Y_{N,k}+(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}a_{k})\xi_{11}+\sum_{k=2}^{K}\alpha_{k}(Z_{N,k}-a_{k}\xi_{11})

\displaystyle=:S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}.

As
N\to\infty
,
S_{1}
converges in distribution to
\sum_{k=2}^{K}\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}
by Theorem
4.4
. Since
S_{1}
is independent of
\xi_{11}
, it follows that
S_{1}+S_{2}
converges in distribution to
\sum_{k=2}^{K}\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}+(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}a_{k})\zeta
as
N
tends to infinity. Finally,
S_{3}
converges in probability to zero by Lemma
4.8
. Therefore,

\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\alpha_{k}\bar{S}_{N,k}}{\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}(a_{k}\zeta+\eta_{k})\text{ as }N\to\infty.

The theorem is proved.
∎

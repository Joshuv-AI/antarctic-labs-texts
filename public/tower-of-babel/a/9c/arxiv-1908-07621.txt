Algebraic relations between harmonic and anti-harmonic moments of plane polygons
Yurii Burman, Ralf Fröberg, Boris Shapiro

Abstract
. In this paper we describe the algebraic relations satisfied by the harmonic and anti-harmonic moments of simply connected, but not necessarily convex planar polygons with a given number of vertices.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 44A60; Secondary 31B20

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Basic notions and background

Let
\mu
be a a finite compactly supported Borel measure in the plane

\mathbb{R}^{2}=\mathbb{C}
. For
j=0,1,\dots
, its
j
-th
harmonic moment
is a
complex number given by:

m_{j}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}z^{j}\,d\mu(z).

Analogously, its
j
-th
anti-harmonic moment
is given by:

\bar{m}_{j}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\bar{z}^{j}\,d\mu(z).

A function

\mathfrak{u}_{\mu}(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\ln\vphantom{z-\xi}\left|\smash{z-\xi}\right|\,d\mu(\xi)

is called the
logarithmic potential
of
\mu
. It is harmonic
outside the support of
\mu
and well-defined almost everywhere in

\mathbb{C}
. The germ of
\mathfrak{u}_{\mu}(z)
at
\infty
is determined by
the sequence of harmonic moments
\{m_{j}(\mu)\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
: the
Taylor expansion at
\infty
of the
Cauchy transform
of
\mu

defined as

\mathfrak{C}_{\mu}(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{d\mu(\xi)}{z-\xi}=\frac{\mathfrak{\partial}\mathfrak{u}_{\mu}(z)}{\partial z},

is

\mathfrak{C}_{\mu}(z)=\frac{m_{0}(\mu)}{z}+\frac{m_{1}(\mu)}{z^{2}}+\frac{m_{2}(\mu)}{z^{3}}+\dots.

The problem of the recovery of a measure from its logarithmic
potential at
\infty
(alias
“the inverse problem in logarithmic
potential theory”
) is a classical area of potential analysis going
back to the early 1920s and still quite active. One of its milestones
is the fundamental paper
[
16
]
, in which P.S. Novikov proved
that Lebesgue measures of two different star-shaped domains cannot have the same logarithmic potential near
\infty
. In
other words, sequences of harmonic moments of Lebesgue measures of two
star-shaped plane domains cannot coincide.

For non-star-shaped domains a similar statement is false: see e.g.

[
4
, p. 333]
for examples of pairs of non-convex polygons
having the same logarithmic potentials near
\infty
.

In this century the problem reappeared in mathematical physics in
connection with integrable systems and the Hele-Shaw flow, see
e.g.
[
13
,
15
]
. In particular, in
[
15
]
S. Natanzon
and A. Zabrodin extended Novikov’s result showing that harmonic
moments can be used as “coordinates” on the set of all star-shaped
domains. Their results imply that for any sequence of numbers

\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\dots
, there exists a star-shaped domain whose
Lebesgue measure
\mu
satisfies the conditions
m_{j}(\mu)=\alpha_{j}
,

j=0,1,\dots
.

This claim is no longer true if one considers moments of Lebesgue
measures of polygons with a fixed number
n
of vertices. The harmonic
moments (and anti-harmonic) moments of such polygons are algebraically
dependent and the main goal of the present article is to describe
these dependencies.

1.2.
The main object: polygonal measures, an explicit formula and complexification

A plane polygon
P\subset\mathbb{C}
is determined by its sequence of
vertices
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
ordered counterclockwise, but not
every sequence of
n
points in
\mathbb{C}
is a sequence of vertices for
some simply connected polygon. It is natural to generalize the notion
of the Lebesgue measure of a polygon as follows.

Let
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}),\;a_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
be a sequence of points
with
a_{j}=(x_{j},y_{j})
,
j=1,\dots,n
. Instead of
x_{j}
and
y_{j}

we are going to use more convenient coordinates
z_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j}
and

\bar{z}_{j}=x_{j}-iy_{j}
. For brevity, denote
\mathbf{z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
and
\bar{\mathbf{z}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}(\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n})
.

If all
a_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
(that is, all
x_{j}
and
y_{j}
are
real) then every
\bar{z}_{j}
is indeed the complex conjugate of
z_{j}
;
if we identify
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\mathbb{C}
as
(x,y)\leftrightarrow x+iy
then

a_{j}
becomes
z_{j}
. We will call such situation “the case of real
vertices”. In general, though,
\mathbf{z}
and
\bar{\mathbf{z}}
are
n
-tuples of
independent complex variables.

Define an
oriented closed polygonal curve

\Gamma_{\bf a}
by

\Gamma_{\bf a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}\overset{\longrightarrow}{[a_{1},a_{2}]}\cup\dots\cup\overset{\longrightarrow}{[a_{n-1},a_{n}]}\cup\overset{\longrightarrow}{[a_{n},a_{1}]}.

Fix an auxiliary convex polygon
P_{*}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the vertices

w_{1},\dots,w_{n}
ordered counterclockwise, and let
\mathcal{T}
be its
triangulation
, i.e. a set of diagonals of
P_{*}
having no common
internal points and cutting
P_{*}
into triangles. Let
F_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}:P_{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
be the map sending every
w_{j}
to
a_{j}
and affine on
every triangle of the triangulation. (It is easy to show that
F_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}
exists and is unique and continuous.)

The image
\Delta_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}=F_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}(P_{*})\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a
polygonal disk in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
bounded by
\Gamma_{a}
. The disk
\Delta_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
is piecewise immersed (though
F_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}

is not always an immersion); for generic
\bf a
this disk is
embedded.

The disk
\Delta_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}
supports the measure
\mu_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}=(F_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}})_{*}dxdy
which is the direct image of the Lebesgue
measure on
P
under
F_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}
; we call
\mu_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}
a
polygonal measure
. The disk
\Delta_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}
itself depends on
the triangulation
\mathcal{T}
, but certain integrals with respect to

\mu_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}
do not.

Theorem 1.1
.

(i)

Let
h:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a holomorphic
function of two variables. Then the integral
\int_{\Delta_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}}h\,d\mu_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}
does not depend on the
triangulation
\mathcal{T}
and is equal to
\int_{\Gamma_{\bf a}}\omega
,
where
\omega
is any
1
-form such that
d\omega=-\frac{i}{2}h\,dz\wedge d\bar{z}
.

(ii)

If the vertices
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
are real then

\mu_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}
is independent of
\mathcal{T}
. It is supported on a
compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and its density at a point
q\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma_{\bf a}
equals the linking number of the
1
-cycle

\Gamma_{\bf a}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the
0
-cycle
q-\infty
. In
particular, if
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
are vertices of a simply
connected polygon listed counterclockwise then
\mu_{a,\mathcal{T}}
is
the Lebesgue measure of this polygon.

The theorem follows easily from the Stokes’ theorem; see Section

2
for a detailed proof. The last claim in assertion

(ii)
explains the term “polygonal measure”.

Corollary 1.2

(of assertion
(i)
)
.

The harmonic moment
m_{j}(\mu_{{\bf a},\mathcal{T}})
of the polygonal
measure does not depend on the triangulation
\mathcal{T}
.

The following lemma is straightforward:

Lemma 1.3
.

Assume that the triangulation
\mathcal{T}
contains the diagonal
(1,m)

which divides
\mathcal{T}
into two parts,
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}
. If
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
,
a^{\prime}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
and
a^{\prime\prime}=(a_{1},a_{m},a_{m+1},\dots,a_{n})
, then
\Delta_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}=\Delta_{\bf a^{\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}\cup\Delta_{\bf a^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}}
and
\mu_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}=\mu_{\bf a^{\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}+\mu_{\bf a^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}}
.

Corollary 1.4
.

If
\mathcal{T}
consists of the edges
(1,3),(1,4),\dots,(1,n-1)
, then

\mu_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}=\mu_{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}}+\mu_{a_{1}a_{3}a_{4}}+\dots+\mu_{a_{1}a_{n-1}a_{n}}.

(The right-hand side of the latter formula contains measures supported
on triangles, so there is no need to specify a triangulation). Notice
that if the vertices are real then, up to a sign,
\mu_{a_{1}a_{k}a_{k+1}}
is the Lebesgue measure of a (possibly degenerate)
triangle with the vertices
a_{1}
,
a_{k}
and
a_{k+1}
. If this
triangle is degenerate then
\mu_{a_{1}a_{k}a_{k+1}}
is zero, otherwise
the sign is taken to be
+
if the triangle is oriented
counterclockwise, and
-
if clockwise.

Take again
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}),a_{j}=(x_{j},y_{j})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
;
z_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j},\bar{z}_{j}=x_{j}-iy_{j}
. Obviously,
z_{j}
and
\bar{z}_{j}

determine
a_{j}
since
x_{j}=(z_{j}+\bar{z}_{j})/2
and
y_{j}=(z_{j}-\bar{z}_{j})/2i
. Set

\nu_{k}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}\binom{k}{2}m_{k-2}(\mu_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}})\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\nu}_{k}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})=\binom{k}{2}\bar{m}_{k-2}(\mu_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}).

In particular,
\nu_{0}=\nu_{1}=\bar{\nu}_{0}=\bar{\nu}_{1}=0
for all

(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})
. By Corollary
1.2
both sides of the
equalities are independent of
\mathcal{T}
.

Remark 1.5
.

The index shift
(k-2)\mapsto k
used above is convenient since the
normalized moments
\nu_{k}
and
\bar{\nu}_{k}
are homogeneous of
degree
k
with respect to the dilatations on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. In other
words,
\nu_{k}(t\mathbf{z},t\bar{\mathbf{z}})=t^{k}\nu_{k}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})
where
t\mathbf{z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}(tz_{1},\dots,tz_{n})
.

The following theorem provides explicit formulas for
\nu_{k}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})

and
\bar{\nu}_{k}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})
.

Theorem 1.6
.

For any positive integer
k\geq 2
one has

\nu_{k}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})=\frac{i}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\bar{z}_{j}-\bar{z}_{j+1})\frac{z_{j}^{k}-z_{j+1}^{k}}{z_{j}-z_{j+1}}\\
=\frac{i}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\bar{z}_{j}-\bar{z}_{j+1})(z_{j}^{k-1}+z_{j}^{k-2}z_{j+1}+\dots+z_{j+1}^{k-1}).

(1.1)

The anti-harmonic moment
\bar{\nu}_{k}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})
is given by the same
formula (
1.1
) with
\mathbf{z}
and
\bar{\mathbf{z}}
interchanged.

Observe that, up to the factor
i/4
, each normalized harmonic and
anti-harmonic moment of a polygonal measure is a polynomial with
integer coefficients in the variables
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
and
\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n}
. Notice additionally that
\bar{\nu}_{2}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})=-\nu_{2}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})
.

1.3.
Main results about the relations between the moments

In this paper, the problem of describing the algebraic relations among
the moments of polygons will be understood in two different ways which
we call the
algebraic
and the
geometric
approaches
respectively. The algebraic approach amounts to finding the algebraic
relations between the polynomials
\nu_{j}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})
,
j=2,3,\dots
, while the geometric approach deals with finding algebraic
relations including both
\nu_{j}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})
and
\bar{\nu}_{j}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})
,

j=2,3,\dots
.

In case of the algebraic approach our main result is relatively
simple. Namely, all harmonic moments can be expressed as rational
functions of the first
2n-2
moments
\nu_{2}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}}),\dots,\nu_{2n-1}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})
, and these
2n-2
moments are algebraically
independent. More precisely, denote by
\mathfrak{F}_{n}
the field extension of

\mathbb{C}
generated by the sequence of polynomials

\{\nu_{j}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})\}_{j=2}^{\infty}
.

Theorem 1.7
.

(i)

\mathfrak{F}_{n}=\mathbb{C}(\nu_{2},\dots,\nu_{2n-1})
and is isomorphic to the field of
rational functions in
2n-2
independent complex variables.

(ii)

\mathfrak{F}_{n}\supset\mathbb{C}(\mathbf{z})^{S_{n}}
, where

\mathbb{C}(\mathbf{z})^{S_{n}}
is the field of symmetric rational functions of

z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
.

Explicit formulas expressing harmonic moments
\nu_{j}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})
with

j\geq 2n
via the first
2n-2
are given by rational
functions. However all their denominators are powers of one fixed
polynomial
\mathfrak{D}_{n}
, the determinant of the matrix (
2.6
)
below. In fact, if one considers the
ring extension

\mathcal{R}_{n}
of

\mathbb{C}
(as opposed to field) generated by the sequence of polynomials

\{\nu_{j}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})\}_{j=2}^{\infty}
, the situation is as follows.

Theorem 1.8
.

(i)

The ring
\mathcal{R}_{n}=\mathbb{C}[\nu_{2},\nu_{3},\dots]
is not generated by any finite collection of harmonic
moments
\nu_{2},\dots,\nu_{N}
.

(ii)

For the polynomial
\mathfrak{D}_{n}\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}}]
given by the determinant of (
2.6
),
the localization
\left.\mathcal{R}_{n}\right|_{\textstyle\mathfrak{D}_{n}}
is isomorphic to

\mathbb{C}[\nu_{2},\dots,\nu_{2n-1}]\left[\dfrac{1}{\mathfrak{D}_{n}}\right]
.

Notice that
\mathcal{R}_{n}
does not contain the ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{z}]^{S_{n}}
of symmetric
polynomials in the variables
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
as a subring since the
expression of the basic (e.g. elementary) symmetric polynomials via

\nu_{2},\dots,\nu_{2n-1}
involves division by some powers of
\mathfrak{D}_{n}
.

Further, in the geometric approach we consider the field extension

\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}
of
\mathbb{C}
generated by both sequences

\{\nu_{j}\}_{j=2}^{\infty}
and
\{\bar{\nu}_{j}\}_{j=2}^{\infty}
. (Recall
that
\nu_{2}=-\bar{\nu}_{2}
). Here the situation is more complicated.

Theorem 1.9
.

(i)

The field
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}
is generated by the
first
4n-5
harmonic and anti-harmonic moments
\nu_{2},\nu_{3},\bar{\nu}_{3},\dots,\nu_{2n-1},\bar{\nu}_{2n-1}
.

(ii)

The field
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}
contains a
subfield
H=\mathbb{C}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})^{S_{n}\times S_{n}}
of rational
functions symmetric with respect to two groups of variables
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
and
\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n}
separately.

(iii)

\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}
is an algebraic extension
of
H
generated by the single element
\nu_{2}
. The degree
d_{n}

of this extension equals
n!(n-1)!
if
n
is odd and

2((n-1)!)^{2}
if
n
is even.

Remark 1.10
.

Notice that any algebraic extension of a field of characteristics
zero is generated by a single element. So the essence of assertion

(iii)
of Theorem
1.9
is that a specific element

\nu_{2}
is a generator. We describe the Galois closure of this
extension and its Galois group later in Section
4
.

Algebraic relations between the usual moments of polygons and
polytopes in several special situations were discussed in a recent
(joint with K. Kohn and B. Sturmfels) paper
[
11
]
of the third
author. For algebraic domains, the relations between the moments were
studied in e.g.
[
12
, Section 3]
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section
2

contains detailed proofs of Theorems
1.1
,

1.6
,
1.7
, and
1.8
, as
well as some formulas related to the logarithmic potential. In Section

3
we describe the action of the group
S_{n}\times S_{n}
on
the field
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}
and prove Theorem
1.9
. In Section

4
we describe the Galois group of the (Galois closure
of the) extension
\mathbb{C}\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}
. Section

5
contains explicit description of this extension
and its Galois group in the simplest case
n=3
(i.e. for
triangles). We finish the paper with some questions expressing our
outlook to the further development of the subject, see
Section
6
.

2.
Proofs and additional results

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

To settle assertion
(i)
, notice first that since

h(z,\bar{z})
is holomorphic in both variables, the
2
-form

h(z,\bar{z})\,dz\wedge d\bar{z}
is closed and therefore exact:

h(z,\bar{z})\,dz\wedge d\bar{z}=d\omega
for some
1
-form

\omega
. If
n=3
then there exists only one triangulation; the
equality
-\frac{i}{2}\int_{\Delta_{a}}h\,dz\wedge d\bar{z}=\int_{\Gamma_{a}}\omega
follows from the Stokes’ theorem.

Next apply the formulas for
n=3
to each triangle of the
triangulation
\mathcal{T}
and add them. Observe that the integrals over
the internal diagonals of the triangulation contribute two equal
terms with the opposite signs and therefore cancel, while the
integrals over the “sides of the polygon” (i.e., segments of

\Gamma_{\bf a}
) appear once and survive in the total sum. On the
other hand, all the maps
F_{\bf a,\mathcal{T}}
are the same on the sides
of the polygon
P
and map them to the same line
\Gamma_{\bf a}
;
thus, the integrals over
\Gamma_{\bf a}
are the same for all possible
triangulations, which proves assertion
(i)
for any
positive integer
n\geq 3
.

Assertion
(ii)
is evident for
n=3
. Indeed, for
q
lying
outside the triangle
\Gamma_{\bf a}
, the linking number of
q-\infty
with
\Gamma_{\bf a}
is
0
, and for
q
lying inside the
triangle, it equals
\pm 1
; the choice of the sign depends on the
orientation of the triangle. Notice now that the map sending

\Gamma_{\bf a}
to the polygonal measure is additive, i.e. if
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
,
{\bf a}^{\prime}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
,
{\bf a}^{\prime\prime}=(a_{1},a_{m},a_{m+1},\dots,a_{n})
then
\Gamma_{\bf a}=\Gamma_{\bf a^{\prime}}+\Gamma_{\bf a^{\prime\prime}}
as
1
-cycles, and therefore the linking number of
q-\infty
with
\Gamma_{\bf a}
is equal to the sum of its linking
numbers with
\Gamma_{\bf a^{\prime}}
and
\Gamma_{\bf a^{\prime\prime}}
. This observation
together with Lemma
1.3
allow us to finish the proof by
induction on
n
.
∎

Now we are ready to derive an explicit formula for the moments of
polygonal measures.

.

For a harmonic moment
\nu_{k}
we are in the situation of Theorem

with
h(z,\bar{z})=\frac{ik}{4}(k-1)z^{k-2}
, so
one can take
\omega=kz^{k-1}\,dz

[p,q]\subset\mathbb{C}
as
z=p(1-t)+tq

, then

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\bar{q}-\bar{p}}{q-p}\,d((p(1-t)+tq)^{k})=\frac{i}{4}(\bar{q}-\bar{p})\frac{q^{k}-p^{k}}{q-p},

Equation (
1.1
) follows now from Theorem

1.1
.

Define now, following
[
17
]
, the
normalized generating
function
\Psi_{\mu}(w)
for harmonic moments
of a measure
\mu
as

\Psi_{\mu}(w)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\nu_{j}(\mu)w^{j-2}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{j+2}{2}m_{j}(\mu)w^{j}.

(2.1)

\Psi_{\mu}(w)
is closely related to the Cauchy transform
\mathfrak{C}_{\mu}(z)
at
\infty
. Namely,

\Psi_{\mu}(w)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dw^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}m_{j}(\mu)w^{j+2}\right).

At the same time for a compactly supported measure
\mu
and
sufficiently large
\vphantom{z}\left|\smash{z}\right|
one has
w\mathfrak{C}_{\mu}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}m_{j}(\mu)/z^{j}
. Hence, if
\vphantom{w}\left|\smash{w}\right|
is
sufficiently small, then

\Psi_{\mu}(w)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dw^{2}}\left(w\mathfrak{C}_{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)\right).

Similar multivariate generating functions were recently considered in

[
9
]
.

For
n=3
we will sometimes denote

D_{i,j,k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}\nu_{2}(z_{i},z_{j},z_{k},\bar{z}_{i},\bar{z}_{j},\bar{z}_{k}).

where the subscripts
i,j,k
vary as appropriate. Note that, for any

n
, if the vertices are real (that is,
\mathbf{z}
and
\bar{\mathbf{z}}
are complex
conjugate) and the points
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
are vertices of a simply
connected polygon then the moment
\nu_{2}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})
is equal to the
(signed) area of the polygon; so
D_{i,j,k}
is the area of a triangle
with the vetices
z_{i},z_{j},z_{k}
.

Important in our consideration is the following observation which can
be found in
[
17
]
:

Proposition 2.1
.

\Psi_{\mu_{\mathbf{z}}}(w)=\frac{D_{123}}{(1-z_{1}w)(1-z_{2}w)(1-z_{3}w)}.

(2.2)

Proof.

Substitution of (
1.1
) into (

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mu_{\mathbf{z}}}(w)

3.

The group

while the second copy acts on
\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n}

(\sigma,\tau)

R(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})_{(\sigma\tau)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}R(z_{\sigma(1)},\dots,z_{\sigma(n)},\bar{z}_{\tau{(1)}},\dots,\bar{z}_{\tau(n)}).

In particular,
{\nu_{j}}_{(\sigma,\tau)}

permutation of variables in the
j

{\nu_{j}}_{(\sigma,\tau)}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})

also the
j

{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})

where

a_{j}=((z_{\sigma(j)}+\bar{z}_{\tau(j)})/2,(z_{\sigma(j)}-\bar{z}_{\tau(j)})/(2i))

\nu_{j}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})

{\nu_{j}}_{(\sigma,\sigma)}(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})

Observe that every

Theorem
1.6

M_{(\operatorname{\mathrm{i}d}\nolimits,\operatorname{\mathrm{i}d}\nolimits)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&0&\dots&0&-1\\
-1&0&1&0&\dots&0&0\\

\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\

(3.1)

Further, for an arbitrary pair

Expression for

4.
Galois group of the equation satisfied by
\nu_{2}

Assertion
(iii)
of Theorem
1.9
claims that the
minimal polynomial
P(t)
for the element
\nu_{2}
generates the
algebraic extension of the field
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}}]^{S_{n}\times S_{n}}
of
degree

d_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}n!(n-1)!&\text{if $n$ is odd,}\\
2((n-1)!)^{2}&\text{if $n$ is even.}\end{cases}

This extension is not Galois; its Galois closure is the field generated
by all the roots of
P
, that is, by
{\nu_{2}}_{(\sigma,\tau)}
for all

\sigma,\tau\in S_{n}
. In this section we calculate the Galois group of
the closure, or, equivalently, the Galois group of the polynomial
P(t)
. To do this,
we need to describe the algebraic dependencies between the polynomials

{\nu_{2}}_{(\sigma,\tau)}
; by definition, the Galois group of

is
the subgroup of

preserving all these dependencies.

Denote by
\mathfrak{M}_{n}
the linear span of the set of all
n\times n
-matrices

where
(\sigma,\tau)\in S_{n}\times S_{n}
, see equation (

) above and
the text following it.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
n\geq 3
, the space
\mathfrak{M}_{n}\subset\operatorname{\mathrm{M}at}\nolimits_{n}
consists of all

n\times n
-matrices with vanishing row and column sums for each row
and column.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathfrak{P}
is the standard
n
-dimensional permutation
representation of the group
S_{n}
. By equation (
3.2
)
the matrix
M_{(\sigma,\tau)}
belongs to the image of
\mathfrak{P}
. The
representation
\mathfrak{P}
is reducible; it splits into the trivial

1
-dimensional representation in the subspace
V_{0}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

spanned by the vector
v_{0}=(1,1,\dots,1)
and the irreducible
representation of dimension
(n-1)
in the space
V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}\{(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\mid z_{1}+\dots+z_{n}=0\}
. So both
V_{0}
and
V
are

M_{(\sigma,\tau)}
.

The representation of
S_{n}
on

is trivial, so

\left.C\right|_{V_{0}}=\operatorname{\mathrm{i}d}\nolimits
, so that
M_{(\sigma,\tau)}=0
on the

V_{0}
. Therefore the sum of matrix elements of

M_{(\sigma,\tau)}
in every row and column vanishes.

According to (
3.2
) the lemma is equivalent to the
following statement: for every linear operator
X:V\to V
there
exist constants
a_{\sigma,\tau}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_{n}}a_{\sigma,\tau}\mathfrak{P}[\sigma^{-1}(C-C^{-1})\tau]=X
. A
standard result in representation theory says that the image of
an irreducible representation of the group algebra of any finite
group is the full matrix algebra of the representation space; so,
for any linear operator
Y:V\to V
there exist constants
a_{\tau},\tau\in S_{n}
such that
\sum_{\tau\in S_{n}}a_{\tau}\mathfrak{P}[\tau]=Y

on
V
, see e.g.
[
21
]
. Now Lemma
4.1
is equivalent to the
statement that
for any linear operator
X:V\to V
, there exist operators

Y_{\sigma},\sigma\in S_{n}
such that

\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\mathfrak{P}[\sigma^{-1}(C-C^{-1})]Y_{\sigma}=X.

(4.1)

To prove this claim observe that the operator
M_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\scriptsize def}}}{{=}}\mathfrak{P}[C-C^{-1}]:V\to V
is nonzero; so,
the linear hull
W\subset V
of the spaces
\mathfrak{P}[\sigma^{-1}(C-C^{-1})](V)\subset V
,
\sigma\in S_{n}
, is
S_{n}
-invariant and
nonzero. The representation
V
is irreducible which implies that

. In other words, there exist not necessarily distinct permutations
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}\in S_{n}
and vectors

, form a basis in
V
.

Let

be the constants such that

for all
j
. Define the operators

given by:

Y_{\sigma}(e_{j})=\sum_{i:\sigma_{i}=\sigma}u_{ij}w_{i}.

(if no
\sigma_{i}
equals
\sigma

and it does not enter
(
4.1
)). An immediate check shows that
(
4.1
) holds and the claim follows. Lemma

is settled.
∎

To move further, for
1\leq i,j\leq n-1
, denote by
\phi_{ij}\in\mathfrak{M}_{n}
the matrix whose entries equal to
1
at positions
(i,j)
and

(n,n)
, to
-1
at positions
(i,n)
and
(n,j)

Lemma 4.2
.

(i)

For any
n\geq 3
,

(ii)

n\geq 3

with

.

the subalgebra generated by the

{\nu_{2}}_{(\sigma,\tau)}
with

Proposition 4.3
.

For any
n\geq 3
, one has

\mathcal{A}_{n}\simeq\frac{\mathbb{C}[D]}{\langle I_{2}\rangle},

(4.2)

where
D=(d_{ij})
is a
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix with variable
entries
d_{ij}
and
\langle I_{2}\rangle
is the ideal generated
by all
2\times 2
-minors of

Proof.

Observe that
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is generated by the linear space of bilinear

\mathfrak{M}_{n}

4.2
the
(n-1)^{2}

\phi_{ij}

coming from

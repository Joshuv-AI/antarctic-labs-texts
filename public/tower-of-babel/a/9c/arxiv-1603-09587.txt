On the number of lattice convex chains
Nathanaël Enriquez

Abstract
An asymptotic formula is presented for the number of planar lattice convex polygonal lines joining the origin to a distant point of the diagonal. The formula involves the non-trivial zeros of the zeta function and leads to a necessary and sufficient condition for the Riemann Hypothesis to hold.

A convex chain with
k
segments is defined as a sequence of points
(x_{i},y_{i})_{0\leq i\leq k}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{k-1}\leq x_{k}
,
0=y_{0}\leq y_{1}<y_{2}<\cdots<y_{k-1}<y_{k}
and

0\leq\frac{y_{1}-y_{0}}{x_{1}-x_{0}}<\cdots<\frac{y_{k}-y_{k-1}}{x_{k}-x_{k-1}}\leq+\infty

(see Figure
1
).
The point
(x_{k},y_{k})
will be called the endpoint of the chain.
This paper deals with the enumeration of convex chains with endpoint
(n,n)
on the diagonal, where the number of segments is not fixed.

Figure 1:
Example of a convex chain with 5 segments

In 1979, Arnold
[
1
]
considered the question
of the number of equivalence classes of convex lattice polygons having
a prescribed area
A
(we say that two polygons having their vertices
on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
are equivalent if one is the image of the other by an affine
automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
). Arnold obtained bounds of order
A^{1/3}
up to a
\log A
factor.
In 1992, Bárány and Pach removed this extra factor in
[
4
]
. A extension in higher dimension involving the volume to the power
\frac{d-1}{d+1}
was achieved by Bárány and Vershik in
[
2
]
.
Later, Vershik changed the constraint in this problem and raised the
question of the number, and typical shape, of convex lattice polygons
included in a large box
[-n,n]^{2}
.
The cornerstone in this problem is the estimation of the number
p(n)

of convex polygonal chains with vertices in
(\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,n])^{2}
and
joining
(0,0)
to
(n,n)
(or more generally
(n,cn)
for some
c>0
).
In 1994, a solution to this problem was found independently by
Bárány
[
3
]
, Vershik
[
12
]
and Sinai
[
10
]
.
Namely, they showed by different methods that, as
n\to\infty
,

p(n)=\exp\left[3\kappa^{1/3}n^{2/3}(1+o(1))\right],\qquad\text{where }\kappa=\frac{\zeta(3)}{\zeta(2)},

and that the limit shape of a typical convex polygonal chain is the
arc of parabola tangent to the sides of the square,
which maximizes the affine perimeter.

Note that the approach of Sinai was recently made rigorous and extended
by Bogachev and Zarbaliev
[
6
]
.
This is also this approach that we choose in this paper and we make use
of a proposition of this latter paper.
Here, we go further and fully exploit Sinai’s probabilistic model by
giving an exact integral representation of the partition function and
by making a precise asymptotic analysis of it.
After our first post of this paper on arXiv, the cardinality of this set
p(n)
was recorded by Koutschan in the OEIS as sequence A267862
[
9
]
.

The appearance of the values of the Riemann zeta function in the above
formula suggests the arithmetic aspects of the problem.
In this paper, we establish a connection between the combinatorial
analysis of the number of convex chains and the zeros of Riemann’s
zeta function.

Theorem 1
.

Let
p(n)
denote the number of lattice convex chains with endpoint
(n,n)
.
As
n
goes to infinity,

p(n)\sim\frac{e^{-2\zeta^{\prime}(-1)}}{(2\pi)^{7/6}\sqrt{3}\kappa^{1/18}n^{17/18}}\exp\left[3\kappa^{1/3}n^{2/3}+I_{\mathrm{crit}}\left(\left(\frac{\kappa}{n}\right)^{1/3}\right)\right],

(1)

where
\kappa=\zeta(3)/\zeta(2)
and where the function

I_{\mathrm{crit}}
will be defined later by equation (
7
).

Moreover, under the assumption that the zeros
\rho
of the Riemann
zeta function inside the critical strip
0<\Re(\rho)<1
are simple, this function can
be expressed as

I_{\mathrm{crit}}(\beta)=\sum_{\rho}\frac{\Gamma(\rho)\zeta(\rho+1)\zeta(\rho-1)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)\beta^{\rho}},

where the precise meaning of the series will be given by (
9
).

A straightforward corollary of this theorem is that,
if Riemann’s Hypothesis was to hold, the oscillatory term

I_{\mathrm{crit}}((\kappa/n)^{1/3})
inside the exponential would be
roughly of order
n^{1/6}
.
The two statements are actually equivalent, as we will show in Subsection
3.2
:

Theorem 2
.

The condition

\forall\epsilon>0,\quad\log p(n)=3\kappa^{\frac{1}{3}}n^{\frac{2}{3}}+O(n^{\frac{1}{6}+\epsilon})

(H)

holds if and only if Riemann’s Hypothesis does.

In
[
5
]
, Bodini, Duchon, Jacquot and Mutafchiev
presented a precise asymptotic analysis of digitally convex polyominoes,
based on generating functions and on the saddle point
method. This problem turns out to be strongly related to the enumeration
of convex chains. In the last section, we show how a slight modification
of Sinai’s model makes it possible to enumerate polyominoes.

1
A statistical mechanical model

We start this paper by reminding the correspondence between lattice
convex chains and non negative integer-valued functions on the set of
pairs of coprime positive integers.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of primitive vectors, that is to say, the set of
all vectors
(x,y)
whose coordinates are coprime positive integers,
including the pairs
(0,1)
and
(1,0)
.
As already used in Jarník
[
8
]
, the space of lattice
increasing convex chains starting from the origin is in one-to-one
correspondence with the space
\Omega
of nonnegative integer-valued
functions
\omega:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with finite support (that is to say,

\omega(\mathbf{v})\neq 0
only for finitely many
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}
):

•

The function
\omega
associated to the convex chain

(x_{i},y_{i})_{0\leq i\leq k}
is defined for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}

by
\omega(\mathbf{v})=\gcd(x_{i+1}-x_{i},y_{i+1}-y_{i})
if there exists

i\in\{0,\dots,k\}
such that
(x_{i+1}-x_{i},y_{i+1}-y_{i})
is
proportional to
\mathbf{v}
, and
\omega(\mathbf{v})=0
otherwise.

•

The inverse map is obtained by adding up the vectors

\omega(\mathbf{v})\,\mathbf{v}
by increasing slope order.
In particular, the endpoint of the chain is equal to

\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}}\omega(\mathbf{v})\,\mathbf{v}.

1.1
Description of Sinai’s model and overall strategy

We endow the space
\Omega
with Boltzmann-like probability
measures
\mathbb{P}_{\bm{\beta}}
depending on two parameters
\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in(0,+\infty)^{2}
and characterized by the condition that the random variables

(\omega(\mathbf{v}))_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}}
are independent and geometrically
distributed with parameter
e^{-\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v}}
, respectively.
In this setting,

\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{P}_{\bm{\beta}}[\omega(\mathbf{v})\neq 0]<\infty,

hence, by the Borel–Cantelli lemma, the function
\omega
has almost surely finite support and we can write

\mathbb{P}_{\bm{\beta}}(\omega)=\prod_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}}\left(1-e^{-\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v}}\right)e^{-\omega(\mathbf{v})\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v}}=\frac{1}{Z(\bm{\beta})}\exp\left(-\bm{\beta}\cdot\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}}\omega(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}\right),

where the
partition function

Z(\bm{\beta})
is given by

Z(\bm{\beta})=\prod_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}}\left(1-e^{-\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v}}\right)^{-1}

(2)

(the “
\cdot
” notation denotes the canonical inner product of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
).

Since, as noticed above,
\mathbf{X}(\omega)=\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}}\omega(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}
is the endpoint of the chain corresponding to
\omega
, and
\mathbb{P}_{\bm{\beta}}(\omega)
is
proportional to
\exp(-\beta\cdot\mathbf{X}(\omega))
,
the conditional distribution induced by
\mathbb{P}_{\bm{\beta}}

on the set of convex chains ending at
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},n_{2})
is the uniform
distribution on this latter set.
By the same argument, we obtain the following formula which will be instrumental
in the proof:

\mathbb{P}_{\bm{\beta}}\left[\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{n}\right]=p(\mathbf{n})\frac{e^{-\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{n}}}{Z(\bm{\beta})}.

(3)

In order to get a formula for
p(\mathbf{n})
, our strategy is to choose the
two parameters so that
\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\beta}}[\mathbf{X}]=\mathbf{n}
.
This will indeed lead to an asymptotic equality of
\mathbb{P}_{\bm{\beta}}[\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{n}]
due to a local limit result.
Together with the analysis of the partition function, this local limit
result will constitute the key of the proof.

1.2
Integral representation of the partition function

It turns out that the Mellin inversion formula leads to an exact
integral representation of the logarithmic partition function
\log Z

of Sinai’s model in terms of the Euler
\Gamma
function, the Riemann

\zeta
function, and the modified Barnes zeta function

\chi(s;\bm{\beta}):=\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{(\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v})^{s}},\qquad\Re(s)>2.

Lemma 1.1
.

For all
\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in(0,+\infty)^{2}
,

\log Z(\bm{\beta})=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{3-i\infty}^{3+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)\zeta(s+1)}{\zeta(s)}\chi(s;\bm{\beta})\,ds.

(4)

Proof.

We first take logarithms in (
2
) and then expand in Taylor series,

\log Z(\bm{\beta})=-\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}}\log\left(1-e^{-\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v}}\right)=\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{1}{m}e^{-m\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v}}.

Now, we make use of the Mellin inversion formula

e^{-z}=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\Gamma(s)z^{-s}ds,

which holds for every real numbers
z>0
and
c>0
.
For all
c>0
, we know, from the exponential decrease of
\Gamma
,
that its integral on
c+i\mathbb{R}
is absolutely convergent.
Taking
c>2
in order to ensure that
\sup_{s\in c+i\mathbb{R}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\left|\frac{1}{m^{s+1}}\right|<\infty
and

\sup_{s\in c+i\mathbb{R}}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}}\left|\frac{1}{(\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v})^{s}}\right|<\infty
, we can apply Fubini’s theorem and write

\displaystyle\log Z(\bm{\beta})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{1}{m}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{(m\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v})^{s}}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\zeta(s+1)\Gamma(s)\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{(\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v})^{s}}ds.

To conclude the proof of (
4
), notice that the partition
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}\setminus\{0\}=\bigsqcup_{d\geq 1}(d\mathcal{P})
translates into the identity of
Dirichlet series

\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{(\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v})^{s}}=\sum_{d\geq 1}\frac{1}{d^{s}}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{(\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v})^{s}}.\qed

Our main contribution is to exploit this integral representation in
order to push further the estimates of
\log Z
, leading to a precise
estimate of
p(n,n)
. As Sinai does in his paper, we could also get
precise asymptotics for
p(n,cn)
, but for the sake of readability of
the paper, we limit our scope to
p(n,n)
, which leads to a choice of
equal parameters
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=\beta
.
In this case, we observe by elementary manipulations, that for
\Re(s)>2
,

\chi(s;(\beta,\beta))=\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{(\beta v_{1}+\beta v_{2})^{s}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{n+1}{\beta^{s}n^{s}}\\
=\frac{\zeta(s-1)+\zeta(s)}{\beta^{s}}.

(5)

Therefore, equation (
4

\log Z(\beta,\beta)=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{3-i\infty}^{3+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)\zeta(s+1)(\zeta(s-1)+\zeta(s))}{\zeta(s)\beta^{s}}\,ds.

(6)

3.1
First theorem

Let us recall that the endpoint is defined by

), we are able to state

\displaystyle\log p(n)

\displaystyle=2n\beta+\log Z(\beta,\beta)+\log\frac{\kappa^{1/3}}{2\pi\sqrt{3}}-\frac{4}{3}\log n+o(1)

\displaystyle=3\kappa^{1/3}n^{2/3}+I_{\mathrm{crit}}\left(\left(\frac{\kappa}{n}\right)^{1/3}\right)-\frac{17}{18}\log n+\log\left(\frac{e^{-2\zeta^{\prime}(-1)}}{(2\pi)^{7/6}\sqrt{3}\kappa^{1/18}}\right)+o(1).

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1
.

3.2
Second theorem

As we observed in the proof of statement
(i)
of
Lemma
2.2
, the fact that the additional term

I_{\mathrm{crit}}((\kappa/n)^{1/3})
is at most of order
o(n^{1/3})

follows from the existence of large zero-free regions of the Riemann
zeta function in the critical strip.
This estimation can be considerably improved if one assumes Riemann’s
Hypothesis, namely that all zeros of the zeta function in the critical
strip lie actually on the line
\Re(s)=\frac{1}{2}
.
Under this assumption, the oscillating term

I_{\mathrm{crit}}((\kappa/n)^{1/3})
is at most of order

O(n^{\frac{1}{6}+\epsilon})
for all
\epsilon>0
.
Indeed, the right-side
\gamma_{\mathrm{right}}
of the contour of integration in formula (
7
) can be replaced by a vertical line arbitrarily close to the critical line. This writing implies that, for all
\epsilon>0
,
I_{\mathrm{crit}}(\beta)=O(\beta^{-\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon})
as
\beta
goes to 0.
Hence, Riemann’s Hypothesis implies (
H
).

Conversely, let us assume that hypothesis (
H
) holds. By Theorem
1
, for all
\epsilon>0
,
I_{\mathrm{crit}}((\kappa/n)^{\frac{1}{3}}))=O(n^{\frac{1}{6}+\epsilon})
.
Since
I_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\prime}(\beta)=o(\beta^{-2})
by statement
(i)
of Lemma
2.2
, this implies that for all
\epsilon>0
,
I_{\mathrm{crit}}(\beta)=O(\beta^{-\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon})
.
Statement
(iii)
of Lemma
2.2
then yields

\forall\epsilon>0,\quad\log Z(\beta,\beta)-\frac{\kappa}{\beta^{2}}=O\left(\frac{1}{\beta^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}\right)

(H’)

as
\beta\to 0
.
Now, we compute the Mellin transform of
\log Z(\beta,\beta)=\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{1}{m}e^{-m\beta(v_{1}+v_{2})}
for
\Re(s)>2
by manipulations similar to the proof of Lemma
1.1
:

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\log Z(\beta,\beta)\beta^{s-1}d\beta=\frac{\Gamma(s)\zeta(s+1)(\zeta(s-1)+\zeta(s))}{\zeta(s)}.

We obtain therefore the following identity for
\Re(s)>2
:

\frac{\Gamma(s)\zeta(s+1)(\zeta(s-1)+\zeta(s))}{\zeta(s)}-\frac{\kappa}{s-2}=\\
\int_{0}^{1}\left(\log Z(\beta,\beta)-\frac{\kappa}{\beta^{2}}\right)\beta^{s-1}d\beta+\int_{1}^{+\infty}\log Z(\beta,\beta)\beta^{s-1}d\beta.

Condition (
H’
) implies that the right-hand side function is holomorphic in the region
\Re(s)>1/2
, hence defining a holomorphic continuation of the left-hand side function in the same region.
Since
\Gamma(s)
,
\zeta(s+1)
and
\zeta(s-1)
do not vanish in the region
0<\Re(s)<1
, this prevents
\zeta(s)
from having zeros in the region
1/2<\Re(s)<1
. As a consequence of the functional equation satisfied by
\zeta
(see
[
11
]
), the distribution of the nontrivial zeros is symmetric with respect to the critical line. Hence, Riemann’s Hypothesis is implied by (
H
).

Numerical considerations

In view of numerical computations, one can truncate the series defining

I_{\mathrm{crit}}
in
(

)
at the two conjugate zeros of

\frac{1}{2}\pm i14.1347

21.0220
and the
exponential decrease of the
\Gamma

10^{4}

Therefore, with a relative precision of

I_{\mathrm{crit}}(\beta)\approx\frac{6.0240\cdot 10^{-11}\cos(14.1347\log\beta)+9.5848\cdot 10^{-10}\sin(14.1347\log\beta)}{\sqrt{\beta}}

for usual values of
\beta=(\kappa/n)^{1/3}
.
In addition, one can see from this approximation that for a large range of
values of
n
, the importance of the oscillatory term in the exponential
part of (
1
) is actually extremely small in comparison
with the polynomial pre-factor.
It is therefore likely that formula

\frac{e^{-2\zeta^{\prime}(-1)}}{(2\pi)^{7/6}\sqrt{3}\kappa^{1/18}n^{17/18}}\exp\left[3\kappa^{1/3}n^{2/3}\right]

gives a sharp estimation of
p(n)
as long as
n
is “large” but less than
10^{60}
. A vague hint of this is given by the exact value of
p(100)=26878385993387721255010
computed by Vaclav Kotesovec
[
9
]
which differs from the analytic formula leading to

2.4\cdot 10^{22}
only by

.

4.2
Digitally convex polyominoes

We present here a modification of our model which leads to
an asymptotic analysis of digitally convex polyominoes as in

[
5
]
.
Our probabilistic approach notably differs from the saddle point method used in
this paper. We were not able to follow all the steps of the computations
in
[
5
]
, and we obtain eventually a slightly
different result.

Let us first recall that a digitally convex polyomino is the set of all
cells of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
included in a bounded convex region of the plane.
The contour of a digitally convex polyomino can be decomposed into four
specifiable sub-paths through the standard decomposition of polyominoes.
In
[
5
]
, the authors focus on one of these
paths, namely the one joining the rightmost lowest point to the highest
rightmost one. More precisely, they want to find the asymptotics of the
number
\widetilde{p}(n)
of such paths with total length
n
.

Taking the convex hull of such a path defines a one-to-one correspondence
with (increasing) convex chains having no horizontal segment.
For this reason, we slightly modify the model by changing the set
\mathcal{P}

into
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}=\mathcal{P}\setminus\{(1,0)\}
and the probability
measure on configurations which are now integer-valued functions on

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}
:

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\bm{\beta}}(\omega)=\prod_{\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}}\left(1-e^{-\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v}}\right)e^{-\omega(\mathbf{v})\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v}}=\frac{1}{\widetilde{Z}(\bm{\beta})}\exp\left(-\bm{\beta}\cdot\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}}\omega(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}\right)

where
\bm{\beta}=(\beta,\beta)
and

\widetilde{Z}(\bm{\beta})=\prod_{\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}}\left(1-e^{-\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{v}}\right)^{-1}.

Again, the ending point is equal to

\mathbf{X}(\omega)=(X_{1}(\omega),X_{2}(\omega))=\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}}\omega(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}.

Moreover the connection between combinatorics and the probabilistic
model is encompassed in the formula

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\bm{\beta}}[X_{1}+X_{2}=n]=\frac{\widetilde{p}(n)}{\widetilde{Z}(\beta,\beta)}e^{-\beta n}.

A computation similar to (
5
) shows that the integral
representation (
6
) of the logarithmic partition function
is changed into

\log\widetilde{Z}(\beta,\beta)=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)\zeta(s+1)\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)\beta^{s}}\,ds,

which leads to the following asymptotic expansion as
\beta
tends to
0
:

\log\widetilde{Z}(\beta,\beta)=\frac{\kappa}{\beta^{2}}+I_{\mathrm{crit}}(\beta)+\frac{1}{6}\log\frac{1}{\beta}+C+o(1),

where the constants
\kappa
and
C
are still
\zeta(3)/\zeta(2)
and
-2\zeta^{\prime}(-1)-\frac{1}{6}\log(2\pi)
.
Both formulas appeared already in the proof of Proposition 2.2 of
[
5
]
.
In order to have the correct calibration
\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\beta}}[X_{1}+X_{2}]=n
for the
length of the path, we choose
\beta>0
depending on
n
such that

-\frac{d}{d\beta}\log\widetilde{Z}(\beta,\beta)=n
.
This leads to the following analogue to (
10
), as
\beta\to 0
:

\frac{1}{\beta^{3}}=\frac{n}{2\kappa}+\frac{1}{2\kappa}I_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\prime}\left(\left(\frac{2\kappa}{n}\right)^{1/3}\right)-\frac{1}{12\kappa}\left(\frac{n}{2\kappa}\right)^{1/3}+o(n^{1/3}).

The variance
\sigma^{2}_{n}
of
X_{1}+X_{2}
can be computed by applying to the vector
(1,1)
the quadratic form given by the covariance matrix of
(X_{1},X_{2})
whose asymptotics at the first order coincide with the ones computed in last section:

\sigma_{n}^{2}\sim\begin{pmatrix}1&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2\kappa}{\beta^{4}}&\frac{\kappa}{\beta^{4}}\\
\frac{\kappa}{\beta^{4}}&\frac{2\kappa}{\beta^{4}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}=\frac{6\kappa}{\beta^{4}}\sim\frac{3n^{4/3}}{2^{1/3}\kappa^{1/3}}.

As in the previous model, a local limit result applies to the discrete one-dimensional random variable
X_{1}+X_{2}
: the probability that this random variable is equal to its mean is asymptotically equal to

\mathbb{P}_{\bm{\beta}}[X_{1}+X_{2}=n]\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{n}^{2}}}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{3\pi}}\left(\frac{\kappa}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\frac{1}{n^{\frac{2}{3}}}.

We do not provide here a complete proof of this estimate, but this can be shown by checking the assumptions of
[
7
]
*Proposition 7.1, which is close from what was done in
[
6
]
.
We obtain finally, as
n\to\infty
,

\widetilde{p}(n)\sim\left(\frac{\kappa}{4}\right)^{\frac{5}{18}}\frac{e^{-2\zeta^{\prime}(-1)}}{\sqrt{3}\pi^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{1}{n^{\frac{11}{18}}}\exp\left[3\left(\frac{\kappa}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}n^{\frac{2}{3}}+I_{\mathrm{crit}}\left(\left(\frac{2\kappa}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\right)\right].

This amends the constant in the prefactor term of
[
5
]
*Proposition 2.5.

Appendix A
Analytic continuation of the Barnes function

We introduce here the Barnes zeta function defined for all
\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in[0,+\infty)^{2}
,
w>0
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
in the region
\Re(s)>2
by

\zeta_{2}(s,w;\bm{\beta}):=\sum_{v_{1},v_{2}\geq 0}\frac{1}{(w+\beta_{1}v_{1}+\beta_{2}v_{2})^{s}}.

Lemma A.1
.

The meromorphic continuation of
\zeta_{2}(s,w;\bm{\beta})
to the half-plane
\Re(s)>1
is given by

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}(s,w;\bm{\beta})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}\frac{w^{-s+2}}{(s-1)(s-2)}+\frac{(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})w^{-s+1}}{2\beta_{1}\beta_{2}(s-1)}+\frac{w^{-s}}{4}

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\{y\}-\frac{1}{2}}{(w+\beta_{2}y)^{s}}\,dy-\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\{x\}-\frac{1}{2}}{(w+\beta_{1}x)^{s}}\,dx

\displaystyle-s\frac{\beta_{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\{y\}-\frac{1}{2}}{(w+\beta_{2}y)^{s+1}}dy-s\frac{\beta_{1}}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\{x\}-\frac{1}{2}}{(w+\beta_{1}x)^{s+1}}dx

\displaystyle+s(s+1)\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{(\{x\}-\frac{1}{2})(\{y\}-\frac{1}{2})}{(w+\beta_{1}x+\beta_{2}y)^{s+2}}\,dxdy.

Proof.

We need only prove that the equality holds for
\Re(s)>2
since
the right-hand side of the equation is meromorphic in
\Re(s)>1

with a single pole at
s=2
.
Let
\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor
denote the fractional part of
x
.
We apply the Euler–Maclaurin formula to the partial summation
defined by
F(x)=\sum_{n_{2}\geq 0}(w+\beta_{1}x+\beta_{2}n_{2})^{-s}
,
leading to

\sum_{n_{1}\geq 1}F(n_{1})=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x)\,dx-\frac{F(0)}{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}(\{x\}-\frac{1}{2})F^{\prime}(x)\,dx.

We use again the Euler-Maclaurin formula for each of the summations in
n_{2}
.
∎

We state here the results we need on the modified Barnes zeta function

\chi
.

For all

(i)

The function

which is

(ii)

satisfying
\epsilon<\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}<\frac{1}{\epsilon}

Elementary manipulations on sums yield

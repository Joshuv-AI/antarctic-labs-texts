Hopf Monoids in Perturbative Algebraic Quantum Field Theory Thanks: This paper is an abridged version of ‘Species-theoretic foundations of perturbative quantum field theory’, arXiv:2009.09969
William Norledge

Abstract
. We develop an algebraic formalism for perturbative quantum field theory (pQFT) which is based on Joyal’s combinatorial species. We show that certain basic structures of pQFT are correctly viewed as algebraic structures internal to species, constructed with respect to the Cauchy monoidal product. Aspects of this formalism have appeared in the physics literature, particularly in the work of Bogoliubov-Shirkov, Steinmann, Ruelle, and Epstein-Glaser-Stora . In this paper, we give a fully explicit account in terms of modern theory developed by Aguiar-Mahajan . We describe the central construction of causal perturbation theory as a homomorphism from the Hopf monoid of set compositions, decorated with local observables, into the Wick algebra of microcausal polynomial observables. The operator-valued distributions called (generalized) time-ordered products and (generalized) retarded products are obtained as images of fundamental elements of this Hopf monoid under the curried homomorphism. The perturbative S-matrix scheme corresponds to the so-called universal series, and the property of causal factorization is naturally expressed in terms of the action of the Hopf monoid on itself by Hopf powers, called the Tits product. Given a system of fully renormalized time-ordered products, the perturbative construction of the corresponding interacting products is via an up biderivation of the Hopf monoid, which recovers Bogoliubov’s formula.

u(\mathtt{H}_{F})=u(\mathtt{H}_{(S_{1})})\mathtt{H}_{(S_{2})}\dots\mathtt{H}_{(S_{k})}+\ \,\cdots\ \,+\mathtt{H}_{(S_{1})}\dots\mathtt{H}_{(S_{k-1})}u(\mathtt{H}_{(S_{k})}).

An up derivation must have
u(\mathtt{H}_{(\,)})=0
, since
\mathtt{1}_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}=\mathtt{H}_{(\,)}
. An up coderivation
u
of
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
is a morphism of species

u:\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\to\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\prime},\qquad\mathtt{H}_{F}\mapsto u(\mathtt{H}_{F})\qquad\quad\text{such that}\qquad\Delta_{\ast S,T}\big(u(\mathtt{H}_{F})\big)=u(\mathtt{H}_{F|_{S}})\otimes\mathtt{H}_{F|_{T}}.

In particular, an up coderivation must have

\Delta_{\ast S,T}\big(u(\mathtt{H}_{(I)})\big)=u(\mathtt{H}_{(S)})\otimes\mathtt{H}_{(T)}.

Therefore, an up biderivation
u
of
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
must have

u(\mathtt{H}_{(i)})=a_{1}\mathtt{H}_{(\ast,i)}+a_{2}\mathtt{H}_{(\ast i)}+a_{3}\mathtt{H}_{(i,\ast)}\qquad\quad\text{where}\qquad a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}=0\in\mathbb{C}.

Motivated by this, given
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
, we define an up derivation
u_{a,b}
of
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
by

(10)

u_{a,b}:\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\to\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\prime},\qquad u_{a,b}(\mathtt{H}_{(I)}):=-a\mathtt{H}_{(\ast,I)}+(a+b)\mathtt{H}_{(\ast I)}-b\mathtt{H}_{(I,\ast)}.

Towards an explicit description, consider the following example for
I=\{1,2,3\}
,

\displaystyle u_{a,b}(\mathtt{H}_{(12,3)})

\displaystyle=\,u_{a,b}(\mathtt{H}_{(12)})\mathtt{H}_{(3)}+\mathtt{H}_{(12)}u_{a,b}(\mathtt{H}_{(3)})

\displaystyle=(-a\mathtt{H}_{(\ast,12)}+(a+b)\mathtt{H}_{(\ast 12)}-b\mathtt{H}_{(12,\ast)})\mathtt{H}_{(3)}+\mathtt{H}_{(12)}(-a\mathtt{H}_{(\ast,3)}+(a+b)\mathtt{H}_{(\ast 3)}-b\mathtt{H}_{(3,\ast)})

\displaystyle=-a\mathtt{H}_{(\ast,12,3)}+(a+b)\mathtt{H}_{(\ast 12,3)}-b\mathtt{H}_{(12,\ast,3)})-a\mathtt{H}_{(12,\ast,3)}+(a+b)\mathtt{H}_{(12,\ast 3)}-b\mathtt{H}_{(12,3,\ast)}.

From this, we see that in general

u_{a,b}(\mathtt{H}_{F})=\sum_{1\leq m\leq k}-a\mathtt{H}_{(S_{1},\dots,\ast,S_{m},\dots,S_{k})}+(a+b)\mathtt{H}_{(S_{1},\dots,\ast S_{m},\dots,S_{k})}-b\mathtt{H}_{(S_{1},\dots,S_{m},\ast,\dots,S_{k})}.

Theorem 2.1
.

Given
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
, the morphism of species

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\to\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\prime},\qquad\mathtt{H}_{F}\mapsto u_{a,b}(\mathtt{H}_{F})

is an up biderivation of
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
(it follows this gives
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
the structure of a Hopf
L
-algebra).

Proof.

In the following, for
F=(S_{1},\dots,S_{k})
a composition of
I
and
S\subseteq I
, we write

(U_{1},\dots,U_{k}):=(S_{1}\cap S,\dots,S_{k}\cap S).

In general,
(U_{1},\dots,U_{k})
is a decomposition of
I
.

First,
u_{a,b}
defines a derivation of
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
by construction. To see that
u_{a,b}
also defines a coderivation, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\ast S,T}\big(u_{a,b}(\mathtt{H}_{F})\big)\ =\

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \Delta_{\ast S,T}\Bigg(\sum_{1\leq m\leq k}-a\mathtt{H}_{(S_{1},\dots,\ast,S_{m},\dots,S_{k})}+(a+b)\mathtt{H}_{(S_{1},\dots,\ast S_{m},\dots,S_{k})}-b\mathtt{H}_{(S_{1},\dots,S_{m},\ast,\dots,S_{k})}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \Bigg(\sum_{1\leq m\leq k}-a\mathtt{H}_{(U_{1},\dots,\ast,U_{m},\dots,U_{k})_{+}}+(a+b)\mathtt{H}_{(U_{1},\dots,\ast U_{m},\dots,U_{k})_{+}}-b\mathtt{H}_{(U_{1},\dots,U_{m},\ast,\dots,U_{k})_{+}}\Bigg)\otimes\mathtt{H}_{F|_{T}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\[2.0pt]
U_{m}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}-a\mathtt{H}_{(U_{1},\dots,\ast,U_{m},\dots,U_{k})_{+}}+(a+b)\mathtt{H}_{(U_{1},\dots,\ast U_{m},\dots,U_{k})_{+}}-b\mathtt{H}_{(U_{1},\dots,U_{m},\ast,\dots,U_{k})_{+}}\Bigg)\otimes\mathtt{H}_{F|_{T}}

\displaystyle+\ \underbrace{\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\[2.0pt]
U_{m}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\big(-a+(a+b)-b\big)\,\mathtt{H}_{(U_{1},\dots,U_{m-1},\ast,U_{m+1},\dots,U_{k})_{+}}\Bigg)}_{=0}\otimes\,\mathtt{H}_{F|_{T}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ u(\mathtt{H}_{F|_{S}})\otimes\mathtt{H}_{F|_{T}}.

Therefore
u_{a,b}
is a biderivation of
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
. ∎

2.2.
The Steinmann Arrows

We now recall the Steinmann arrows for
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
, whose precise definition is due to Epstein-Glaser-Stora
[
EGS75
, p.82-83]
. The Steinmann arrows were first considered by Steinmann in settings where
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
is represented as operator-valued distributions
[
Ste60b
, Section 3]
.

Let the
retarded Steinmann arrow
be the up biderivation of
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
given by

(11)

\ast\downarrow(-):\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\to\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\prime},\qquad\ast\downarrow\mathtt{H}_{F}:=u_{1,0}(\mathtt{H}_{F})=\sum_{1\leq m\leq k}-\mathtt{H}_{(S_{1},\dots,\ast,S_{m},\dots,S_{k})}+\mathtt{H}_{(S_{1},\dots,\ast S_{m},\dots,S_{k})}.

Let the
advanced Steinmann arrow
be the up biderivation of
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
given by

(12)

\ast\uparrow(-):\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\to\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\prime},\qquad\ast\uparrow\mathtt{H}_{F}:=u_{0,1}(\mathtt{H}_{F})=\sum_{1\leq m\leq k}\mathtt{H}_{(S_{1},\dots,\ast S_{m},\dots,S_{k})}-\mathtt{H}_{(S_{1},\dots,S_{m},\ast,\dots,S_{k})}.

We use this arrow notation from now on instead of ‘
u
’ in order to match the physics literature. In particular

\ast\downarrow\mathtt{H}_{(I)}=-\mathtt{H}_{(\ast,I)}+\mathtt{H}_{(\ast I)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ast\uparrow\mathtt{H}_{(I)}=\mathtt{H}_{(\ast I)}-\mathtt{H}_{(I,\ast)}.

We have

\ast\uparrow\mathtt{H}_{F}\,-\,\ast\downarrow\mathtt{H}_{F}=u_{-1,1}(\mathtt{H}_{F})=[\mathtt{H}_{(\ast)},\mathtt{H}_{F}].

This identity appears often in the physics literature for operator-valued distributions, e.g.
[
Ste60b
, Equation 13]
,
[
EG73
, Equation 83]
. The biderivation
u_{-1,1}
gives
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
the structure of a Hopf
L
-algebra. This
L
-action is the restriction of the adjoint representation of
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
. Notice the Steinmann arrows are commutative up operators. By
[
Nor20
, Proposition 5.4]
, we can restrict them to obtain up derivations of
Zie
,

\ast\downarrow(-):\textbf{Zie}\to\textbf{Zie}^{\prime},\qquad\mathtt{D}_{\mathcal{S}}\mapsto\ast\downarrow\mathtt{D}_{\mathcal{S}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ast\uparrow(-):\textbf{Zie}\to\textbf{Zie}^{\prime},\qquad\mathtt{D}_{\mathcal{S}}\mapsto\ast\uparrow\mathtt{D}_{\mathcal{S}}.

Following
[
Nor20
, Section 5]
, the Steinmann arrows equip
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
with the structure of a Hopf
E
-algebra
(and
Zie
with the structure of a Lie
E
-algebra) in two ways. The details are as follows. First,
E
is the
exponential species
, given by

\textbf{E}[I]:=\mathbb{C}\qquad\text{for all}\quad I\in\textsf{S}.

We denote

\mu_{S,T}:\textbf{E}[S]\otimes\textbf{E}[T]=\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{C}=\textbf{E}[I],\qquad\mathtt{H}_{S}\otimes\mathtt{H}_{T}\mapsto\mathtt{H}_{I}.

We have the following

[
Nor20
, Equation 23]

\textbf{E}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\to\boldsymbol{\Sigma},\qquad\mathtt{H}_{Y}\otimes\mathtt{a}\,\mapsto\,Y\!\downarrow\mathtt{a}:=\underbrace{y_{r}\downarrow\circ\cdots\circ y_{1}\downarrow}_{\text{invariant of the order}}(\mathtt{a})

and

\textbf{E}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\to\boldsymbol{\Sigma},\qquad\mathtt{H}_{Y}\otimes\mathtt{a}\,\mapsto\,Y\!\uparrow\mathtt{a}:=\underbrace{y_{r}\uparrow\circ\cdots\circ y_{1}\uparrow}_{\text{invariant of the order}}(\mathtt{a})

as usual. In particular,

and
\ast\downarrow(-)

-modules.

By inspecting the definitions, we see that

Y\!\downarrow\mathtt{H}_{(I)}=\mathtt{R}_{(Y;I)}:=\!\sum_{Y_{1}\sqcup Y_{2}=Y}\overline{\mathtt{H}}_{(Y_{1})}\mathtt{H}_{(Y_{2}\sqcup I)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad Y\!\uparrow\mathtt{H}_{(I)}=\mathtt{A}_{(Y;I)}:=\!\sum_{Y_{1}\sqcup Y_{2}=Y}\mathtt{H}_{(Y_{1}\sqcup I)}\overline{\mathtt{H}}_{(Y_{2})}.

The sums are over all decompositions

of

Proposition 2.3
.

Given a cell
\mathcal{S}
over

, we have

Y\downarrow\mathtt{D}_{\mathcal{S}}=\mathtt{D}_{Y\downarrow\mathcal{S}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad Y\uparrow\mathtt{D}_{\mathcal{S}}=\mathtt{D}_{Y\uparrow\mathcal{S}}.

We consider the retarded case

The space of all field configurations, denoted

Remark 5.1

We can always pick an isomorphism

Let a

By taking compexified compactly supported distributional sections

6.
Observables

An off-shell
observable

O
is a smooth functional of field configurations into the complex numbers,

\emph{\textsf{O}}:\Gamma(E)\to\mathbb{C},\qquad\Phi\mapsto\emph{\textsf{O}}(\Phi).

The space of all observables is denoted
Obs
. We can pointwise multiply observables, sometimes called the
normal ordered product
, so that observables form a commutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra,

\text{Obs}\otimes\text{Obs}\to\text{Obs},\qquad\emph{\textsf{O}}_{1}\otimes\emph{\textsf{O}}_{2}\mapsto\emph{\textsf{O}}_{1}\cdot\emph{\textsf{O}}_{2}

where

\emph{\textsf{O}}_{1}\cdot\emph{\textsf{O}}_{2}(\Phi):=\!\!\!\underbrace{\emph{\textsf{O}}_{1}(\Phi)\emph{\textsf{O}}_{2}(\Phi)}_{\text{multiplication in $\mathbb{C}$}}\!\!\!.

Thus, we may form the commutative algebra in species
\textbf{U}_{\text{Obs}}
, given by
\textbf{U}_{\text{Obs}}[I]=\text{Obs}
.

A
linear observable

\emph{\textsf{O}}\in\text{Obs}
is an observable which is additionally a linear functional, that is

\emph{\textsf{O}}(\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2})=\emph{\textsf{O}}(\Phi_{1})+\emph{\textsf{O}}(\Phi_{2})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\emph{\textsf{O}}(c\Phi)=c\emph{\textsf{O}}(\Phi)\qquad\text{for}\quad c\in\mathbb{C}.

The space of linear observables is denoted
\text{LinObs}\subset\text{Obs}
. In particular, for each spacetime event
x\in\mathcal{X}
, we have the
field observable

\boldsymbol{\Phi}(x)\in\text{LinObs}
, given by

\boldsymbol{\Phi}(x):\Gamma(E)\to\mathbb{C},\qquad\Phi\mapsto\Phi(x).

We now show how linear observables and so-called polynomial observables arise species-theoretically, via (generalized) systems of products for the species
E
and
\textbf{X}=\mathcal{P}(\textbf{E})
.

Let
X
denote the species given by
\textbf{X}\big[\{i\}\big]:=\mathbb{C}
for singletons and
\textbf{X}[I]:=0
otherwise. We denote

. We have the following morphism of species,

\textbf{X}\otimes{{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{\prime}}^{\mathbb{C}}}_{\!\!\!\!\!\!\text{cp}}(E^{\ast})\to\textbf{U}_{\text{Obs}},\qquad\mathtt{H}_{i}\otimes\alpha\mapsto\bigg(\Phi\mapsto\int_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\big\langle\alpha(x),\Phi(x)\big\rangle\text{dvol}_{\mathcal{X}}\bigg).

This is like a system of products for
X

{{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{\prime}}^{\mathbb{C}}}_{\!\!\!\!\!\!\text{cp}}(E^{\ast})

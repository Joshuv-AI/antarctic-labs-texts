On the Structure of Singularities of Weak Mean Curvature Flows with Mean Curvature Bounds
Maxwell Stolarski

Abstract
. This paper studies singularities of mean curvature flows with integral mean curvature bounds H ∈ L ∞ ​ L l ​ o ​ c p H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc} for some p ∈ ( n , ∞ ] p\in(n,\infty] . For such flows, any tangent flow is given by the flow of a stationary cone 𝐂 \mathbf{C} . When p = ∞ p=\infty and 𝐂 \mathbf{C} is a regular cone, we prove that the tangent flow is unique. These results hold for general integral Brakke flows of arbitrary codimension in an open subset U ⊆ ℝ N U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N} with H ∈ L ∞ ​ L l ​ o ​ c p H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc} . 1.
Introduction

A time-dependent family of embeddings
F:M^{n}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is said to evolve by mean curvature flow if

\partial_{t}F=H

where
H=\vec{H}_{M_{t}}
denotes the mean curvature vector of the embedded submanifold
M_{t}=F(M\times\{t\})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Submanifolds evolving by mean curvature flow often develop singularities in finite time
T<\infty
.
Huisken
[
Hui84
]
showed that the second fundamental form
A
of a compact hypersurface
M_{t}
evolving by mean curvature flow always blows up at a finite-time singularity
T<\infty
, that is,

\limsup_{t\nearrow T}\sup_{x\in M_{t}}|A|=\infty.

Given Huisken’s result
[
Hui84
]
, it is natural to ask if the trace of the second fundamental form, namely the mean curvature
H=\tr A
, must also blow up at finite-time singularities of the mean curvature flow.

[
Sto23
]
answered this question in the negative by showing the mean curvature flow solutions constructed by Velázquez
[
Vel94
]
develop finite-time singularities even though
H
remains uniformly bounded
\sup_{t\in[0,T)}\sup_{x\in M_{t}}|H|<\infty
.
In dimension
n=7
,
[
ADS21
]
further showed how to extend these mean curvature flow solutions
M_{t}^{7}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{8}
to weak mean curvature flows defined for later times
t\in[0,T+\epsilon)

in such a way that
H
remains uniformly bounded and the flow has an isolated singularity at
(\mathbf{0},T)\in\mathbb{R}^{8}\times[0,T+\epsilon)
.

Given that there exist smooth mean curvature flows which develop singularities with bounded mean curvature
[
Vel94
,
Sto23
,
ADS21
]
,
the focus of this current article is to instead study the singularities of
any
mean curvature flow
M^{n}_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with uniform mean curvature bounds.
In this general setting, uniform mean curvature bounds along the flow allow us to incorporate the well-developed theory of varifolds with bounds on their first variation.
In particular, we leverage that theory to obtain the following result which holds more generally for weak mean curvature flows of arbitrary codimension with integral mean curvature bounds:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
2\leq n<N
and let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open.
Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
be an integral
n
-dimensional Brakke flow in
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with locally uniformly bounded areas and generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
for some
p\in(n,\infty]
.

Except for a countable set of times
t\in(a,b)
,
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
equals the Brakke flow of a family of integer rectifiable
n
-varifolds
(V_{t})_{t\in(a,b]}
that extends to the final time-slice
t=b
.

For any
(x_{0},t_{0})\in U\times(a,b]
, any tangent flow of
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
is given by the static flow of a stationary cone
\mathbf{C}
.
The stationary cones
\mathbf{C}
that arise as tangent flows to
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
are exactly the tangent cones to
V_{t_{0}}
at
x_{0}
.

Note that the stationary cones
\mathbf{C}
in Theorem
1.1
are generally integer rectifiable
n
-varifolds that are dilation invariant and have zero first variation (see Lemma
2.6
and Theorem
2.10
for more precise statements).

Given a Brakke flow
(\mu_{t})
, tangent flows at
(x_{0},t_{0})
are, simply-speaking, subsequential limits of parabolic rescalings of
(\mu_{t})
based at
(x_{0},t_{0})
.
Analogously, tangent cones of a varifold
V_{t_{0}}
at
x_{0}
are subsequential limits of spatial rescalings of
V_{t_{0}}
based at
x_{0}
.
Thanks to suitable monotonicity formulas and compactness theorems, tangent flows and tangent cones always exist.
However, the uniqueness of tangent cones and tangent flows, that is independence of subsequence, is generally an open problem.
This uniqueness question is fundamental for singularity analysis and regularity.

Theorem
1.1
in particular shows that, for flows with mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
,
uniqueness of the tangent
flow
of
(\mu_{t})
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
is equivalent to uniqueness of the tangent
cone
of
V_{t_{0}}
at
x_{0}
.
Because of this correspondence, we are able to prove the following uniqueness result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
2\leq n<N
and let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open.
Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
be an integral
n
-dimensional Brakke flow in
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with locally uniformly bounded areas and generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
.

If a tangent flow to
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
at
(x_{0},t_{0})\in U\times(a,b]
is given by the static flow of a regular cone
\mathbf{C}
(with multiplicity one),
then this is the unique tangent flow to
(\mu_{t})
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
.

It is worth mentioning some related uniqueness results.
Huisken’s monotonicity formula ensures tangent flows are always self-similarly shrinking
\Sigma_{t}=\sqrt{-t}\Sigma_{-1}
for
t<0
.
There are various uniqueness results depending on
\Sigma=\Sigma_{-1}
.
When
\Sigma
is compact,
[
Sch14
]
proved that if
\sqrt{-t}\Sigma
is a tangent flow of a mean curvature flow
(M_{t})
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
, then it’s the unique tangent flow at
(x_{0},t_{0})
.

[
CM15
]
proved uniqueness of the tangent flow
\sqrt{-t}\Sigma
when
\Sigma=\mathbb{R}^{n-k}\times\mathbb{S}^{k}
is a generalized cylinder.

[
CS21
]
proved uniqueness of the tangent flow when
\Sigma
is smooth and asymptotically conical.

[
Zhu20
,
LZ24
]
obtained additional generalizations of the uniqueness results in
[
CM15
,
CS21
]
, respectively.

Importantly, the uniqueness results in
[
CS21
,
LZ24
]
require
\Sigma
to be smooth and do not apply when
\Sigma=\mathbf{C}
is a minimal cone for example.
The first uniqueness result for tangent flows given by non-smooth
\Sigma
came in
[
LSS22
]
which showed that,
for 2-dimensional Lagrangian mean curvature flows
L^{2}_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2}
, tangent flows given by a transverse pair of planes
\Sigma=P_{1}\cup P_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2}
are unique.
The uniqueness result Theorem
1.2
here applies to non-smooth minimal cones
\Sigma=\mathbf{C}
which arise as tangent flows to general integral Brakke flows of any codimension in an open subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
, albeit under the assumption of a uniform mean curvature bound
H\in L^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{loc}
.

Under additional hypotheses, we can also describe an accompanying limit flow that arises as a more general blow-up limit around a singularity.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M_{t}^{n})_{t\in(a,b)}
be a smooth, properly embedded mean curvature flow in an open subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
.

If the tangent flow of
\mathcal{M}
at
(x,b)\in U\times\{b\}
is given by the static flow of a generalized Simons cone
\mathbf{C}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
(with multiplicity one) and
\mathbf{C}
is area minimizing,
then there exists a sequence
(x_{i},t_{i})\in U\times(a,b)
with
\lim_{i\to\infty}(x_{i},t_{i})=(x,b)
and
a sequence
\lambda_{i}\searrow 0
such that the sequence of rescaled mean curvature flows

\mathcal{M}_{i}=\mathcal{D}_{\lambda_{i}^{-1}}(\mathcal{M}-(x_{i},t_{i}))

converges to the static flow of a smooth Hardt-Simon minimal surface.

We refer the reader to Section
4
for the definitions relevant to Theorem
1.3
.
For now, we simply note that the mean curvature flow solutions constructed in
[
Vel94
]
provide examples of mean curvature flows satisfying Theorem
1.3
.
Theorem
1.3
states that general mean curvature flows with
H\in L^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{loc}
in some sense mimic the dynamics of Velázquez’s mean curvature flow solutions
[
Vel94
]
near Simons cone singularities.

The paper is organized as follows:
In Section
2
, we establish notation, specify definitions, and obtain some general results used throughout the paper.
In particular, Theorem
1.1
is proven here.
Section
3
proves the uniqueness of tangent flows given by regular stationary cones, Theorem
1.2
.
In Section
4
, we obtain refined dynamics of mean curvature flows near regular stationary cones and prove Theorem
1.3
.
Finally, Appendix
A
reviews some well-known results about integral varifolds with generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{p}_{loc}
that are cited throughout the paper.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Brakke Flows with
H
Bounds

Definition 2.1
.

Let
2\leq n<N
,
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open, and
p\in(1,\infty]
.
Let
V
be an integer rectifiable
n
-varifold in
U
and
\mu_{V}
be the associated measure on
U
.
We say that
V
has
generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{p}_{loc}(U)
if
there exists a Borel function
H:U\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
H\in L^{p}_{loc}(U,d\mu_{V})
such that the first variation
\delta V
satisfies

\displaystyle\delta V(X)=-\int H\cdot Xd\mu_{V}\qquad\forall X\in C^{1}_{c}(U,\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Remark 2.2
.

Observe that, by the definition given in Definition
2.1
, if
V
has generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{p}_{loc}(U)
then
V
has no generalized boundary in
U
.
This convention differs somewhat from the existing literature but we adopt it nonetheless to simplify the statements in the remainder of the paper.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
2\leq n<N
,
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open, and
p\in(1,\infty]
.
Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
be an
n
-dimensional integral Brakke flow in
U
.
Recall that, for a.e.
t\in(a,b)
, there exists an integer rectifiable
n
-varifold
V_{t}
such that
\mu_{V_{t}}=\mu_{t}
.

We say the Brakke flow
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
has
generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))

if
for a.e.
t\in(a,b)
there exists an integer rectifiable
n
-varifold
V_{t}
with generalized mean curvature
H_{t}\in L^{p}_{loc}(U)

such that
\mu_{V_{t}}=\mu_{t}
and

\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}(K\times(a,b))}\doteqdot\esssup_{t\in(a,b)}\|H_{t}\|_{L^{p}(K,d\mu_{t})}<\infty\qquad(\forall K\Subset U).

Assumption 2.4
.

Let
2\leq n<N
,
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open subset,
-\infty<a<b<\infty
.
Throughout, we assume
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
is an
n
-dimensional integral Brakke flow in
U

such that
(\mu_{t})
has locally uniformly bounded areas, that is,

\sup_{t\in(a,b)}\mu_{t}(K)<\infty\qquad\forall K\Subset U.

For simplicity, we will abbreviate this assumption as “
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
is an integral
n
-Brakke flow in
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with locally uniformly bounded areas” in the remainder of the paper.

We will also often assume that, for some
p\in(1,\infty]
,

(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}\text{ has generalized mean curvature }H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}(U\times(a,b)),

but this assumption will be indicated in each statement.

Remark 2.5
.

The assumption that the flow has locally uniformly bounded areas is quite mild.
For example, it holds for Brakke flows
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[a,b)}
starting from initial data
\mu_{a}
with locally bounded areas, i.e.
\mu_{a}(K)<\infty
for all
K\Subset U
.
Indeed, this can be seen by using Brakke’s inequality with suitably defined spherically shrinking test functions.

On the other hand, the assumption that the flow has generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
is much more restrictive.
Nonetheless,
[
Sto23
]
shows that even smooth mean curvature flows
(M_{t}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1})_{t\in(a,b)}
with
H\in L^{\infty}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times(a,b))
can develop singularities at the final time
t=b
.
Combined with the work of
[
ADS21
]
, there are non-smooth Brakke flows with
H\in L^{\infty}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(a,b))
with mild singularities and small singular sets, informally speaking.

The next lemma shows that Brakke flows with
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}
can be changed at countably many times to get a Brakke flow which is a varifold with
H\in L^{p}_{loc}
at
every
time.
Moreover, the flow naturally extends to the final time-slice.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
be an integral
n
-Brakke flow in
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with locally uniformly bounded areas and generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
for some
p\in(1,\infty]
.

Then for all
t\in(a,b]
, there exists a unique integer rectifiable
n
-varifold
V_{t}
with generalized mean curvature in
H_{V_{t}}\in L^{p}_{loc}(U)
such that:

(1)

\mu_{t}\leq\lim_{t^{\prime}\nearrow t}\mu_{t}=\mu_{V_{t}}
for all
t\in(a,b)
,

(2)

\mu_{t}=\mu_{V_{t}}
for all but countably many
t\in(a,b)
,

(3)

for all
t\in(a,b]
and all
K\Subset U

\mu_{V_{t}}(K)\leq\sup_{\tau\in(a,b)}\mu_{\tau}(K)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ \|H_{V_{t}}\|_{L^{p}(K)}\leq\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}(K\times(a,b))}

(4)

for all
t\in(a,b]

\lim_{t^{\prime}\nearrow t}V_{t^{\prime}}(f)=V_{t}(f)\qquad\forall f\in C^{0}_{c}(G(n,U))

and

(5)

(\mu_{V_{t}})_{t\in(a,b]}
is an
n
-dimensional integral Brakke flow in
U
.

Proof.

Let
t\in(a,b]
.
Since
(\mu_{t})
has
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
,
there exists a sequence of times
t_{j}\nearrow t
(with
t_{j}<t
) and integer rectifiable
n
-varifolds
\tilde{V}_{j}
with generalized mean curvature
H_{\tilde{V}_{j}}
in
L^{p}_{loc}(U)
such that

\mu_{t_{j}}=\mu_{\tilde{V}_{j}}
and

\|H_{\tilde{V}_{j}}\|_{L^{p}(K,d\mu_{t_{j}})}\leq\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}(K\times(a,b))}\doteqdot C_{K}<\infty\qquad\forall K\Subset U.

By compactness Lemma
A.1
,
there exists a subsequence (still denoted
\tilde{V}_{j}
) and an integer rectifiable
n
-varifold
V_{t}

with locally bounded areas and generalized mean curvature
H_{V_{t}}\in L^{p}_{loc}(U)

such that

\tilde{V}_{j}\rightharpoonup V_{t}
as varifolds and

\mu_{V_{t}}(K)\leq\sup_{\tau\in(a,b)}\mu_{\tau}(K)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|H_{V_{t}}\|_{L^{\infty}(K,d\mu_{V_{t}})}\leq C_{K}\qquad\forall K\Subset U.

This defines the varifold
V_{t}
for any
t\in(a,b]
and proves it satisfies (3).

By
[
Ilm94
, 7.2(ii)]
,
for any
f\in C^{0}_{c}(U,\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
and any
t\in(a,b)

(2.1)

\mu_{t}(f)\leq\lim_{s\nearrow t}\mu_{s}(f)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\mu_{\tilde{V}_{j}}(f)=\mu_{V_{t}}(f).

This proves (1).
For (2), simply note that
[
Ilm94
, 7.2(iii)]
implies the first inequality in (
2.1
) is an equality for all but countably many times
t\in(a,b)
.
Note additionally that the equality
\lim_{s\nearrow t}\mu_{s}=\mu_{V_{t}}
in (1) implies the varifold
V_{t}
is unique.

To prove (4), let
t\in(a,b]
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U,\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
.
Take a sequence
t_{j}\nearrow t
.
By
[
Ilm94
, 7.2(i)]
, there exists
C_{f}
such that
\mu_{t}(f)-C_{f}t
is decreasing in
t
.
It follows that, for any
j
,

\displaystyle\mu_{V_{t}}(f)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\nearrow t}(\mu_{s}(f)-C_{f}s)+C_{f}t

\displaystyle(1)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{t_{j}}(f)-C_{f}t_{j}+C_{f}t

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{V_{t_{j}}}(f)+C_{f}(t-t_{j})

\displaystyle(1).

Taking
j\to\infty
gives
\mu_{V_{t}}(f)\leq\liminf_{j}\mu_{V_{t_{j}}}(f)
.
For the reverse inequality, let
\epsilon>0
.
Recall
\mu_{V_{t}}(f)=\lim_{s\nearrow t}\mu_{t}(f)
and similarly for the the
V_{t_{j}}
.
Thus, there exists
s=s(\epsilon)<t

|s-t|<\epsilon

|\mu_{V_{t}}(f)-\mu_{s}(f)|<\epsilon.

There exists
J

s<t_{j}<t

j>J
.
It follows that, for

\displaystyle\mu_{V_{t_{j}}}(f)-\mu_{V_{t}}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{V_{t_{j}}}(f)-\mu_{s}(f)+\epsilon

\displaystyle=\lim_{\sigma\nearrow t_{j}}(\mu_{\sigma}(f)-C_{f}\sigma)+C_{f}t_{j}-\mu_{s}(f)+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{s}(f)-C_{f}s+C_{f}t_{j}-\mu_{s}(f)+\epsilon

\displaystyle=C_{f}(t_{j}-s)+\epsilon.

Taking
j\to\infty
then
\epsilon\to 0
gives
\limsup_{j}\mu_{V_{t_{j}}}(f)\leq\mu_{V_{t}}(f)
.
Since the sequence
t_{j}\nearrow t
and

were arbitrary, it follows that

\lim_{t^{\prime}\nearrow t}\mu_{V_{t^{\prime}}}(f)=\mu_{V_{t}}(f)\qquad\forall f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U,\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}).

Convergence as varifolds then follows from Lemma
A.2
and completes the proof of (4).

To prove (5), it suffices to check Brakke’s inequality.
Let
a<t_{0}<t_{1}\leq b
and

with

.
Then

\displaystyle\mu_{V_{t_{1}}}(f)-\mu_{V_{t_{0}}}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{s\nearrow t_{1}}\mu_{s}(f)-\mu_{t_{0}}(f)

\displaystyle(1)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{s\nearrow t_{1}}\int_{t_{0}}^{s}\int\partial_{t}f+\nabla f\cdot H-|H|^{2}fd\mu_{t}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\int\partial_{t}f+\nabla f\cdot H-|H|^{2}fd\mu_{V_{t}}dt

\displaystyle(2).

2.2.
Huisken’s Monotonicity Formula and Gaussian Density

Let

\Phi_{x_{0},t_{0}}(x,t)\doteqdot\frac{1}{\left(4\pi(t_{0}-t)\right)^{n/2}}e^{-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{4(t_{0}-t)}}\qquad(t<t_{0})

denote the backwards heat kernel based at
(x_{0},t_{0})
.
Let

\varphi_{x_{0},t_{0};r}(x,t)\doteqdot\left(1-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}-2n(t_{0}-t)}{r}\right)^{3}_{+}\qquad(t\leq t_{0})

denote the spherically shrinking localization function based at
(x_{0},t_{0})
with scale
r>0
.

Huisken’s monotonicity formula and its localized analogue for Brakke flows states

(2.2)

\int\Phi_{x_{0},t_{0}}(\cdot,t)\varphi_{x_{0},t_{0};r}(\cdot,t)d\mu_{t}-\int\Phi_{x_{0},t_{0}}(\cdot,s)\varphi_{x_{0},t_{0};r}(\cdot,s)d\mu_{s}\\
\leq-\int_{s}^{t}\int\left|H+\frac{(x-x_{0})^{\perp}}{2(t_{0}-\tau)}\right|^{2}\Phi_{x_{0},t_{0}}\varphi_{x_{0},t_{0};r}d\mu_{\tau}d\tau\leq 0

for all
s<t<t_{0}
and
r>0
such that
(\mu_{t})
is a Brakke flow on
B_{r+2n(t_{0}-s)}(x_{0})\times[s,t]
.
Moreover, for Brakke flows
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
defined in
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
and points
x_{0}\in B_{2r_{0}}(x_{0})\subseteq U
, the Gaussian density

(2.3)

\Theta_{\mu}(x_{0},t_{0})\doteqdot\lim_{t\nearrow t_{0}}\int\Phi_{x_{0},t_{0}}(\cdot,t)\varphi_{x_{0},t_{0};r}(\cdot,t)d\mu_{t}

is well-defined for
t_{0}\in(a,b]
and independent of
r\in(0,r_{0})
.

For an
n
-dimensional Brakke flow
(\mu_{t})
and
\lambda>0
, define the parabolically dilated Brakke flow
\mathcal{D}_{x_{0},t_{0};\lambda}\mu
based at
(x_{0},t_{0})
to be

\left[\mathcal{D}_{x_{0},t_{0};\lambda}\mu\right]_{t}(A)\doteqdot\lambda^{n}\mu_{t_{0}+t\lambda^{-2}}(\lambda^{-1}A+x_{0}).

Often, we omit the basepoint
x_{0},t_{0}
and simply write
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\mu
when the basepoint is clear from context.

2.3.
Equivalent Notions of Density

Recall the density of varifold
V
at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is given by

\theta_{V}(x_{0})\doteqdot\lim_{\rho\searrow 0}\frac{\mu_{V}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\omega_{n}\rho^{n}}

when the limit exists and where
\omega_{n}
denotes the volume of the unit
n
-ball.
In the remainder of the article, we use
\eta_{x_{0},\lambda}
to denote the spatial translation and dilation

\eta_{x_{0},\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N},\qquad\eta_{x_{0},\lambda}(x)\doteqdot\lambda(x-x_{0}).

\eta_{x_{0},\lambda}
naturally induces a map of integer rectifiable
n
-varifolds which we denote by
(\eta_{x_{0},\lambda})_{\sharp}
.
Specifically, if
V=(\Gamma,\theta)
, then
(\eta_{x_{0},\lambda})_{\sharp}V=(\eta_{x_{0},\lambda}(\Gamma),\theta\circ\eta_{x_{0},\lambda}^{-1})
.
When the basepoint
x_{0}
is clear from context or
x_{0}=0
, we shall often simply write
\eta_{\lambda}
for simplicity.

When
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}
with
p>n
, the monotonicity formula (
A.3
) for varifolds gives a characterization of subsequential blow-ups of time-slices.

Lemma 2.7

(Existence of Tangent Cones of Time-Slices)
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
be an integral
n
-Brakke flow in
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with locally uniformly bounded areas (Assumption
2.4
) and
generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
for some
p\in(n,\infty]
.
Let
(V_{t})_{t\in(a,b]}
be the family of varifolds as in Lemma
2.6
.

For any
(x_{0},t_{0})\in U\times(a,b]
and any sequence
\lambda_{i}\nearrow+\infty
,
there exists a subsequence (still denoted
\lambda_{i}
)
and an integer rectifiable
n
-varifold
\mathbf{C}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

\left(\eta_{x_{0},\lambda_{i}}\right)_{\sharp}V_{t_{0}}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{C}\qquad\text{as varifolds in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Moreover,
\mathbf{C}
is a stationary, dilation invariant varifold in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with

\frac{\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(B_{r}(0))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}=\theta_{V_{t_{0}}}(x_{0})=\lim_{\rho\searrow 0}\frac{\mu_{V_{t_{0}}}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\omega_{n}\rho^{n}}\qquad\forall\,0<r<\infty.

Any
\mathbf{C}
that arises as such a limit is called a
tangent cone
of
V_{t_{0}}
at
x_{0}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
,
V_{t_{0}}
has generalized mean curvature
H=H_{V_{t_{0}}}\in L^{p}_{loc}(U)
.
It follows that the dilations
V_{i}\doteqdot(\eta_{x_{0},\lambda_{i}})_{\sharp}V_{t_{0}}
have generalized mean curvature
H_{i}\in L^{p}_{loc}(\lambda_{i}(U-x_{0}))
with

(2.4)

\|H_{i}\|_{L^{p}(K,d\mu_{V_{i}})}\leq\lambda_{i}^{\frac{n}{p}-1}\|H_{V_{t_{0}}}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{B}_{R}(x_{0}),d\mu_{V_{t_{0}}})}

for all
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
compact and
i\gg 1
sufficiently large so that
\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}K+x_{0}\subseteq\overline{B}_{R}(x_{0})\Subset U
.
In particular,
\lim_{i\to\infty}\|H_{i}\|_{L^{p}(K,d\mu_{V_{i}})}=0
.

Since
p>n
, there is the monotonicity formula (
A.3
) for integral
n
-varifolds with
H\in L^{p}_{loc}
(Proposition
A.3
, see also
[
Sim14
, Ch. 4, §4]
).
In particular, the density

\theta_{V_{t_{0}}}(x_{0})\doteqdot\lim_{\rho\searrow 0}\frac{\mu_{V_{t_{0}}}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\omega_{n}\rho^{n}}

is well-defined
and the blow-up sequence
V_{i}
has uniform local area bounds.

It now follows from compactness (Lemma
A.1
)
that there is a subsequence (still denoted
V_{i}
) and an integer rectifiable
n
-varifold
\mathbf{C}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
V_{i}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{C}
.
By (
2.4
) and Lemma
A.1
,

\|H_{\mathbf{C}}\|_{L^{p}(K,d\mu_{\mathbf{C}})}\leq\limsup_{i}\|H_{V_{i}}\|_{L^{p}(K,d\mu_{V_{i}})}=0,

which implies
\mathbf{C}
is stationary.
Moreover, the convergence
V_{i}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{C}
implies

\frac{\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(B_{r}(0))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}=\theta_{V_{t_{0}}}(x_{0})\qquad\forall 0<r<\infty.

The monotonicity formula (
A.3
) for stationary varifolds finally implies
\mathbf{C}
is dilation invariant, that is

(\eta_{0,\lambda})_{\sharp}\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{C}\qquad\forall 0<\lambda<\infty

(see e.g.
[
Sim14
, Ch. 8, §5]
).
∎

We will show in Lemma
2.9
below that Huisken’s monotonicity formula (
2.2
) similarly allows us to extract tangent
flows
from
space-time
blow-ups.
First, we show local uniform area
ratio
bounds follow from the local uniform area bounds.

Lemma 2.8

(Area Ratio Bounds)
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
be an integral
n
-Brakke flow in
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with locally uniformly bounded areas (Assumption
2.4
)
and generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
for some
p\in(n,\infty]
.

For any
K\Subset U
, there exists
C
such that

\sup_{t\in(a,b)}\sup_{B_{r}(x)\subseteq K}\frac{\mu_{t}(B_{r}(x))}{r^{n}}\leq C.

Proof.

The proof will proceed by combining the monotonicity formula (
A.3
) with the local uniform area bounds.
Given
K\Subset U
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

K^{\prime}\doteqdot\overline{B_{\epsilon}(K)}=\overline{\bigcup_{x\in K}B_{\epsilon}(x)}\Subset U.

Let

R_{0}\doteqdot\sup\{r>0:B_{r}(x)\subseteq K\text{ for some }x\},\qquad R_{1}\doteqdot\max\{R_{0},1\}.

For any
B_{r}(x)\subseteq K
,
there exists
R\in[\max\{\epsilon,r\},R_{1}]
such that
B_{r}(x)\subseteq B_{R}(x)\subseteq K^{\prime}
.
Let
(V_{t})_{t\in(a,b]}
be the family of varifolds as in Lemma
2.6
and let
t\in(a,b)
.
The monotonicity formula (
A.3
) implies

\frac{\mu_{V_{t}}(B_{r}(x))}{r^{n}}\leq\frac{\mu_{V_{t}}(B_{r}(x))}{r^{n}}e^{\frac{\|H_{V_{t}}\|}{1-n/p}r}+e^{\frac{\|H_{V_{t}}\|}{1-n/p}r}\leq\frac{\mu_{V_{t}}(B_{R}(x))}{R^{n}}e^{\frac{\|H_{V_{t}}\|}{1-n/p}R}+e^{\frac{\|H_{V_{t}}\|}{1-n/p}R}

where
\|H_{V_{t}}\|=\|H_{V_{t}}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}(x),d\mu_{V_{t}})}
.
It then follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{t}(B_{r}(x))}{r^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu_{V_{t}}(B_{r}(x))}{r^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu_{V_{t}}(B_{R}(x))}{R^{n}}e^{\frac{\|H_{V_{t}}\|_{L^{p}(K^{\prime})}}{1-n/p}R}+e^{\frac{\|H\|_{L^{p}(K^{\prime})}}{1-n/p}R}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu_{V_{t}}(K^{\prime})}{\epsilon^{n}}e^{\frac{\|H_{V_{t}}\|_{L^{p}(K^{\prime})}}{1-n/p}R_{1}}+e^{\frac{\|H\|_{L^{p}(K^{\prime})}}{1-n/p}R_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sup_{\tau\in(a,b)}\mu_{\tau}(K^{\prime})}{\epsilon^{n}}e^{\frac{\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}(K^{\prime}\times(a,b))}}{1-n/p}R_{1}}+e^{\frac{\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}(K^{\prime}\times(a,b))}}{1-n/p}R_{1}}<\infty.

Taking the supremum over
t\in(a,b)
and
B_{r}(x)\subseteq K
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.9

(Existence of Tangent Flows)
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
be an integral
n
-Brakke flow in
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with locally uniformly bounded areas and generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
for some
p\in(n,\infty]
.

For any
(x_{0},t_{0})\in U\times(a,b]
and any sequence
\lambda_{i}\nearrow+\infty
,
there exists a subsequence (still denoted
\lambda_{i}
) and
an integer rectifiable
n
-varifold
\mathbf{C}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that for any
t<0

\left(\mathcal{D}_{x_{0},t_{0};\lambda_{i}}\mu\right)_{t}\rightharpoonup\mu_{\mathbf{C}}\qquad\text{as $i\to\infty$ (weakly as measures)}.

Moreover,
\mathbf{C}
is a stationary, dilation invariant varifold in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with

\Theta_{\mu}(x_{0},t_{0})=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}\int e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mu_{\mathbf{C}}.

Any
\mathbf{C}
that arises as such a limit is called a
tangent flow
of
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
.

Proof.

Fix
(x_{0},t_{0})\in U\times(a,b]
.
By Lemma
2.8
,
(\mu_{t})
has local uniform area ratio bounds in a neighborhood of
(x_{0},t_{0})
.
It follows from Huisken’s monotonicity formula (
2.2
) and the compactness of Brakke flows with local uniform area bounds that there exists a Brakke flow
(\mu^{\infty}_{t})_{t<0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}

and a subsequence (still denoted
\lambda_{i}
) such that

\left(\mathcal{D}_{\lambda_{i}}\mu\right)_{t}=\left(\mathcal{D}_{x_{0},t_{0};\lambda_{i}}\mu\right)_{t}\rightharpoonup\mu^{\infty}_{t}\qquad\forall t<0

(see
[
Ilm
, Lemma 8]
or
[
Whi94
]
).
Moreover,
\mu^{\infty}
is a shrinker for
t<0
in the sense that

(2.5)

\mu_{t}(A)=(-t)^{n/2}\mu_{-1}(A/\sqrt{-t})\qquad\forall t<0

and, for any
t<0
,
\mu^{\infty}_{t}
is an integer rectifiable
n
-varifold with

(2.6)

H+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2(-t)}=0\qquad\text{for }\mu^{\infty}_{t}\text{-a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Additionally,

\Theta_{\mu}(x_{0},t_{0})=\frac{1}{(4\pi(-t))^{n/2}}\int e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4(-t)}}d\mu^{\infty}_{t}\qquad\forall t<0.

Because
(\mu_{t})
is a Brakke flow with
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}
,
there exists
t<0
such that, for all
i
,

(\mathcal{D}_{\lambda_{i}}\mu)_{t}
is represented by an integer rectifiable
n
-varifold
V_{i}
with
H_{V_{i}}\in L^{p}_{loc}(\lambda_{i}U)
and, for any
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\|H_{V_{i}}\|_{L^{p}(K,d\mu_{V_{i}})}\leq\lambda_{i}^{\frac{n}{p}-1}\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}(B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\times(a,b))}

for all
i\gg 1
sufficiently large such that
\lambda_{i}^{-1}K+x_{0}\subseteq B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
and
\overline{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}\Subset U
.
Since
p>n
,

\lim_{i\to\infty}\|H_{V_{i}}\|_{L^{p}(K,d\mu_{V_{i}})}=0\qquad\forall K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Since
\mu_{V_{i}}\rightharpoonup\mu^{\infty}_{t}
,
the compactness of varifolds with mean curvature bounds Lemma
A.1
and Lemma
A.2
imply that
\mu^{\infty}_{t}=\mu_{\mathbf{C}}
for some integer rectifiable
n
-varifold
\mathbf{C}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
which is stationary (
H_{\mathbf{C}}=0
).
It then follows from (
2.6
) that
x^{\perp}=0

\mu_{\mathbf{C}}
-a.e. and thus
\mathbf{C}
is dilation invariant (see e.g.
[
Sim14
, Ch. 8, §5]
).
Finally, (
2.5
) implies
\mu^{\infty}_{t}=\mu_{\mathbf{C}}
for all
t<0
.
This completes the proof.
∎

A priori, the tangent
cones
from Lemma
2.7
could be entirely unrelated to the tangent
flows
from Lemma
2.9
.
Indeed, the two limiting objects arise from entirely different blow-up sequences.
The next theorem, however, proves that the tangent cones from Lemma
2.7
exactly correspond to tangent flows from Lemma
2.9
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
be an integral
n
-Brakke flow in
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with locally uniformly bounded areas and generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
for some
p\in(n,\infty]
.
Let
(V_{t})_{t\in(a,b]}
be the family of varifolds as in Lemma
2.6
.

For any
(x_{0},t_{0})\in U\times(a,b]
,

\theta_{V_{t_{0}}}(x_{0})=\Theta_{\mu}(x_{0},t_{0}).

For any sequence
\lambda_{i}\nearrow+\infty
, there exists a subsequence (still denoted
\lambda_{i}
) such that there are limiting integral
n
-varifolds
\mathbf{C},\tilde{\mathbf{C}}
as in Lemmas
2.7
,
2.9
respectively, and in fact
\mathbf{C}=\tilde{\mathbf{C}}
.

Proof.

By translation, we can assume without loss of generality that
(x_{0},t_{0})=(\mathbf{0},0)\in U\times(a,b]
.
For any sequence
\lambda_{i}\nearrow+\infty
, denote the rescalings

V^{i}_{0}\doteqdot\left(\eta_{\lambda_{i}}\right)_{\sharp}V_{0},\qquad\mu^{i}_{t}\doteqdot(\mathcal{D}_{\lambda_{i}}\mu)_{t}.

Lemmas
2.7
and
2.9
imply there exists a subsequence (still denoted by index
i
) such that

V^{i}_{0}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{C},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mu^{i}_{t}\rightharpoonup\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}\quad(\forall t<0),

where
\mathbf{C},\tilde{\mathbf{C}}
are stationary, dilation invariant, integral
n
-varifolds.
Additionally,

\displaystyle\theta_{V_{0}}(\mathbf{0})=\frac{\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(B_{r}(0))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\quad(\forall 0<r<\infty)\qquad\text{ and}

\displaystyle\Theta_{\mu}(\mathbf{0},0)=\frac{1}{(4\pi(-t))^{n/2}}\int e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4(-t)}}d\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}\quad(\forall t<0).

We first claim that
\mu_{\mathbf{C}}\leq\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}
.
Let
f\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
f\geq 0
. Say
\supp f\subseteq B_{R}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(f)={}

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\mu_{V^{i}_{0}}(f)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{t^{\prime}\nearrow 0}\mu^{i}_{t^{\prime}}(f)

\displaystyle(\text{Lemma \ref{lem nice representative}})

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{t^{\prime}\nearrow 0}\left(\mu^{i}_{-1}(f)+\int_{-1}^{t^{\prime}}\int H_{i}\cdot\nabla f-|H_{i}|^{2}f\,d\mu^{i}_{t}dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\mu^{i}_{-1}(f)+\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{-1}^{0}\int|H_{i}||\nabla f|\,d\mu^{i}_{t}dt

\displaystyle(f\geq 0)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(f)+\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{-1}^{0}\int|H_{i}||\nabla f|\,d\mu^{i}_{t}dt.

For any
0<\delta\ll 1
, the integral term can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{-1}^{0}\int|H_{i}||\nabla f|\,d\mu^{i}_{t}dt

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\|f\|_{C^{1}}\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{B_{R}}|H_{i}|d\mu^{i}_{t}dt

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\|f\|_{C^{1}}\lambda_{i}^{n-1}\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{B_{R\lambda_{i}^{-1}}}|H|d\mu_{t\lambda_{i}^{-2}}dt

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\|f\|_{C^{1}}\lambda_{i}^{n+1}\int_{-\lambda_{i}^{-2}}^{0}\int_{B_{R\lambda_{i}^{-1}}}|H|d\mu_{\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle(\tau=t\lambda_{i}^{-2})

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\|f\|_{C^{1}}\lambda_{i}^{n+1}\int_{-\lambda_{i}^{-2}}^{0}\left(\int_{B_{R\lambda_{i}^{-1}}}|H|^{p}d\mu_{\tau}\right)^{1/p}\mu_{\tau}(B_{R\lambda_{i}^{-1}})^{\frac{p-1}{p}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{1}}\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}(B_{\delta}\times(a,b))}\lim_{i\to\infty}\lambda_{i}^{n-1}\left(\sup_{-\lambda_{i}^{-2}\leq\tau\leq 0}\mu_{\tau}(B_{R\lambda_{i}^{-1}})\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{1}}\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}(B_{\delta}\times(a,b))}R^{\frac{n(p-1)}{p}}\lim_{i\to\infty}\lambda_{i}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}\left(\sup_{-\lambda_{i}^{-2}\leq\tau\leq 0}\frac{\mu_{\tau}(B_{R\lambda_{i}^{-1}})}{R^{n}\lambda_{i}^{-n}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

By Lemma
2.8
, the
\frac{\mu_{\tau}(B_{R\lambda_{i}^{-1}})}{R^{n}\lambda_{i}^{-n}}
term is uniformly bounded above by say
C_{0}<\infty
.
Since
p>n
,

\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(f)\leq\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(f)+C_{0}\|f\|_{C^{1}}\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}(B_{\delta}\times(a,b))}R^{\frac{n(p-1)}{p}}C_{0}\lim_{i\to\infty}\lambda_{i}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}=\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(f).

Thus,
\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(f)\leq\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(f)
for all
f\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
f\geq 0
.
It then follows from a limiting argument that
\mu_{\mathbf{C}}\leq\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}
.

Note that, since
\mathbf{C}
is dilation invariant,

\frac{\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(B_{r}(\mathbf{0}))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}=\frac{1}{(4\pi(-t))^{n/2}}\int e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4(-t)}}d\mu_{\mathbf{C}}\qquad\forall 0<-t,r<\infty

and similarly for
\tilde{\mathbf{C}}
.

\mu_{\mathbf{C}}\leq\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}
implies

\frac{\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(B_{r}(\mathbf{0}))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\leq\frac{\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(B_{r}(\mathbf{0}))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\qquad\forall 0<r<\infty.

We claim that the reverse inequality

\frac{\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(B_{r}(\mathbf{0}))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\geq\frac{\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(B_{r}(\mathbf{0}))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}

also holds (for some or equivalently all
0<r<\infty
).
First, observe there exists a time
\tau<0
such that
\mu_{\tau\lambda_{i}^{-2}}
is an integral
n
-varifold with generalized mean curvature
H_{\tau\lambda_{i}^{-2}}\in L^{p}_{loc}(U)
for all
i
and

\|H_{\tau\lambda_{i}^{-2}}\|_{L^{p}(K)}\leq\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}(K\times(a,b))}<\infty\qquad(\forall K\Subset U),

where as usual
H
denotes the mean curvature of the flow
(\mu_{t})
.
Fix
R>0
such that
\overline{B}_{R}=\overline{B}_{R}(\mathbf{0})\Subset U
.
Let

F_{i}(\rho)\doteqdot e^{\|H_{\tau\lambda_{i}^{-2}}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\frac{1}{1-\frac{n}{p}}\rho^{1-\frac{n}{p}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\overline{F}(\rho)\doteqdot e^{\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{p}(B_{R}\times(a,b))}\frac{1}{1-\frac{n}{p}}\rho^{1-\frac{n}{p}}}

as in the monotonicity formula (
A.3
).
Note

1\leq F_{i}(\rho)\leq\overline{F}(\rho)\qquad\forall\rho>0.

It follows from the monotonicity formula (
A.3
) that, for any
0<\rho<R
and any
r>0
,

\displaystyle r^{-n}\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(B_{r})

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}r^{-n}\mu^{i}_{\tau}(B_{r})

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\mu_{\tau\lambda_{i}^{-2}}(B_{r\lambda_{i}^{-1}})}{r^{n}\lambda_{i}^{-n}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{F_{i}(r\lambda_{i}^{-1})}\left(F_{i}(r\lambda_{i}^{-1})\frac{\mu_{\tau\lambda_{i}^{-2}}(B_{r\lambda_{i}^{-1}})}{r^{n}\lambda_{i}^{-n}}+F_{i}(r\lambda_{i}^{-1})-F_{i}(r\lambda_{i}^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{F_{i}(r\lambda_{i}^{-1})}\left[F_{i}(\rho)\frac{\mu_{\tau\lambda_{i}^{-2}}(B_{\rho})}{\rho^{n}}+F_{i}(\rho)-F_{i}(r\lambda_{i}^{-1})\right]

(
A.3
)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{i\to\infty}\left[\overline{F}(\rho)\frac{\mu_{\tau\lambda_{i}^{-2}}(B_{\rho})}{\rho^{n}}+\overline{F}(\rho)-1\right]

\displaystyle=\overline{F}(\rho)\frac{\mu_{V_{0}}(B_{\rho})}{\rho^{n}}+\overline{F}(\rho)-1.

Thus,

\frac{\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(B_{r})}{r^{n}}\leq\overline{F}(\rho)\frac{\mu_{V_{0}}(B_{\rho})}{\rho^{n}}+\overline{F}(\rho)-1.

Taking
\rho\searrow 0
reveals

\frac{\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(B_{r})}{r^{n}}\leq\lim_{\rho\searrow 0}\overline{F}(\rho)\frac{\mu_{V_{0}}(B_{\rho})}{\rho^{n}}+\overline{F}(\rho)-1=\lim_{\rho\searrow 0}\frac{\mu_{V_{0}}(B_{\rho})}{\rho^{n}}=\omega_{n}\theta_{V_{0}}(\mathbf{0})=\frac{\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(B_{r})}{r^{n}}.

In summary, we have shown that

\mu_{\mathbf{C}}\leq\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(B_{r})}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\geq\frac{\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(B_{r})}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\quad\forall 0<r<\infty.

In particular,
\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(B_{r})=\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(B_{r})
for all
0<r<\infty
. It follows that
\mathbf{C}=\tilde{\mathbf{C}}
.
Indeed, if not, then there exists a bounded subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(A)<\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(A)
.
Taking
R>0
large enough so that
B_{R}\supset A
would then imply

\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(B_{R})=\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(B_{R}\setminus A)+\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(A)<\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(B_{R}\setminus A)+\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(A)=\mu_{\tilde{\mathbf{C}}}(B_{R})=\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(B_{R})

a contradiction.

Thus,
\mathbf{C}=\tilde{\mathbf{C}}
and in particular

\theta_{V_{0}}(\mathbf{0})=\Theta_{\mu}(\mathbf{0},0).

This completes the proof.
∎

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.10
, we deduce that the uniqueness of tangent
cones
of time-slices is equivalent to the uniqueness of tangent
flows
.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
be an integral
n
-Brakke flow in
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with locally uniformly bounded areas and generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
for some
p\in(n,\infty]
.
Let
(V_{t})_{t\in(a,b]}
be the family of varifolds as in Lemma
2.6
.

Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in U\times(a,b]
.
Then

\{\mathbf{C}:\mathbf{C}\text{ is a tangent cone of $V_{t_{0}}$ at $x_{0}$}\}\\
=\{\mathbf{C}:\mu_{\mathbf{C}}\text{ is a tangent flow of $(\mu_{t})$ at $(x_{0},t_{0})$}\}.

In particular, the tangent cone
V_{t_{0}}
at
x_{0}
is unique if and only if the tangent flow of
(\mu_{t})
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
is unique.
In other words,

(\eta_{x_{0},\lambda_{i}})_{\sharp}V_{t_{0}}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{C}

for every sequence
\lambda_{i}\nearrow+\infty
if and only if

(\mathcal{D}_{x_{0},t_{0};\lambda_{i}}\mu)_{t}\rightharpoonup\mu_{\mathbf{C}}\qquad(\forall t<0)

for every sequence
\lambda_{i}\nearrow+\infty
.

Remark 2.12
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
be an integral Brakke flow with mean curvature bounds as in Theorem
2.10
, and consider the associated flow of varifolds
(V_{t})_{t\in(a,b]}
as in Lemma
2.6
.
By combining Theorem
2.10
with White’s local regularity theorem
[
Whi05
]
(and its generalization to integral
n
-Brakke flows
[
ST22
]
),
it follows that
if
t_{0}\in(a,b]
and
V_{t_{0}}
also has
unit density
(i.e.
\theta_{V_{t_{0}}}(x)=1
for
\mu_{V_{t_{0}}}
-a.e.
x\in U
),
then the singular set of the flow
\mathcal{M}=(V_{t})_{t\in(a,t_{0}]}
in the
t=t_{0}
time-slice has
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure 0, that is
\mathcal{H}^{n}(\text{sing}_{t_{0}}\mathcal{M})=0
.
This recovers Brakke’s main regularity theorem
[
Bra78
, 6.12]

in the special case of flows with mean curvature bounds (see also
[
Eck04
, Theorem 5.3]
and
[
KT14
, Theorem 3.2]
).
We refer the interested reader to
[
Bra78
,
Eck04
,
KT14
]
for precise definitions.

3.
Uniqueness of Tangent Flows Given By Regular Cones

Definition 3.1
.

Define the
link

L(\mathbf{C})
of a dilation invariant set
\mathbf{C}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
to be

L(\mathbf{C})\doteqdot\mathbf{C}\cap\mathbb{S}^{N-1}.

\mathbf{C}
is said to be a
regular cone
if
L(\mathbf{C})
is a smooth, (properly) embedded submanifold of
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
.

If
\mathbf{C}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a regular cone, then
\mathbf{C}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
is a smooth submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and we write
\mathbf{C}^{n}
when
\mathbf{C}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
has dimension
n
.
Note that a regular cone
\mathbf{C}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
naturally gives a dilation invariant integral
n
-varifold (of multiplicity one) with associated measure
\mathcal{H}^{n}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\mathbf{C}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

The main result of this section is the following uniqueness result which restates Theorem
1.2
:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
be an integral
n
-Brakke flow in
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with locally uniformly bounded areas and generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
.
If
(\mu_{t})
has a tangent flow
\mu_{\mathbf{C}}
at
(x_{0},t_{0})\in U\times(a,b]
given by a regular cone
\mathbf{C}^{n}
(with multiplicity one),
then
\mu_{\mathbf{C}}
is the unique tangent flow of
(\mu_{t})
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
.

The proof essentially follows from
[
Sim83
, §7, Theorem 5]
applied to the time slice
V_{t_{0}}
.
However,
[
Sim83
, §7, Theorem 5]
requires a regularity assumption
[
Sim83
, equation (7.23)]
which we must verify for
V_{t_{0}}
.
Informally, this regularity assumption states that if
V_{t_{0}}
is a small
C^{1,\alpha}
-graph over the regular cone
\mathbf{C}
in an annulus, then the mean curvature
H_{V_{t_{0}}}
of
V_{t_{0}}
has interior
C^{2}
-bounds.

Since
V_{t_{0}}
comes from a Brakke flow, we can prove
V_{t_{0}}
satisfies this regularity assumption
[
Sim83
, equation (7.23)]
as follows:

(1)

show that
C^{1,\alpha}
-graphicality propagates outward in space and backward in time (Lemma
3.7
), and

(2)

apply interior estimates to improve the
C^{1,\alpha}
bounds to
C^{\infty}
estimates for
V_{t}
and apply interior estimates to the evolution equation for
H=H_{V_{t}}
to obtain
\|H\|_{C^{2}}\lesssim\|H\|_{C^{0}}
(Lemma
3.8
).

The remainder of this section rigorously carries out this argument to prove Theorem
3.2
.

Definition 3.3
.

In what follows we use
A_{r,R}(x_{0})
to denote the open annulus
A_{r,R}(x_{0})=B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0})
and
A_{r,R}=A_{r,R}(\mathbf{0})
.

For
\mathbf{C}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
a regular cone, we slightly abuse notation and write
u:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\to T^{\perp}\mathbf{C}
to mean a function

u:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
u(x)\in T^{\perp}_{x}\mathbf{C}
for all
x\in\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}
.
For
u:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\to T^{\perp}\mathbf{C}
, denote

G_{r,R}(u)\doteqdot\left\{\frac{x+u(x)}{\sqrt{1+\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}}}:x\in\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Note that if
\mathbf{C}
is a regular cone and
u:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\to T^{\perp}\mathbf{C}
is a
C^{2}
-function with
\frac{u(x)}{|x|}
sufficiently small in
C^{1}
, then
G_{r,R}(u)
is a properly embedded
C^{2}
-submanifold.

Definition 3.4
.

For
\mathbf{C}
a regular cone, let
\|\cdot\|_{C^{k}_{*}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{*}}
(
0<\alpha\leq 1
) denote the standard
C^{k}
and
C^{k,\alpha}
norms respectively.
For example, if
u:\mathbf{C}\cap\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
then

\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{*}(\mathbf{C}\cap\Omega)}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sup_{x\in\mathbf{C}\cap\Omega}|\nabla^{j}_{\mathbf{C}}u|(x)+\sup_{x\neq y\in\mathbf{C}\cap\Omega}\frac{|\nabla^{k}_{\mathbf{C}}u(x)-\nabla^{k}_{\mathbf{C}}u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

For
0<r<R<\infty
and
u:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
, define scale-invariant
C^{k}
and
C^{k,\alpha}
norms by

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{k}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R})}

\displaystyle\doteqdot\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sup_{x\in\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}}|x|^{j-1}\sup_{y\in B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)\cap\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}}|\nabla^{j}_{\mathbf{C}}u|(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sup_{x\in\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}}|x|^{j-1}\|\nabla^{j}_{\mathbf{C}}u\|_{C^{0}(B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)\cap\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R})}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R})}

\displaystyle\doteqdot{}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{k}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R})}+\sup_{x\in\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}}|x|^{k-1+\alpha}\sup_{y\neq z\in B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)\cap\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}}\frac{|\nabla^{k}_{\mathbf{C}}u(y)-\nabla^{k}_{\mathbf{C}}u(z)|}{|y-z|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{k}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R})}+\sup_{x\in\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}}|x|^{k-1+\alpha}[\nabla^{k}u]_{C^{\alpha}_{*}(B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)\cap\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R})}

where
[\,\cdot\,]_{C^{\alpha}_{*}}
denotes the standard
C^{\alpha}
semi-norm.

For functions
u:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b)\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
that also depend on time
t\in(a,b)
,
denote backward parabolic neighborhoods as

P_{r}(x,t)\doteqdot B_{r}(x)\times(t-r^{2},t)

and define scale-invariant
C^{k}
and
C^{k,\alpha}
norms by

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{k}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b))}

\displaystyle\doteqdot{}

\displaystyle\sum_{2i+j\leq k}\sup_{(x,t)\in\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b)}|x|^{2i+j-1}\|\partial_{t}^{i}\nabla_{\mathbf{C}}^{j}u\|_{C^{0}_{*}(P_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x,t)\cap(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b)))}

\displaystyle+\sum_{0<\frac{k-2i-j}{2}<1}\sup_{(x,t)\in\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b)}|x|^{k-1+\alpha}[\partial_{t}^{i}\nabla_{\mathbf{C}}^{j}u]_{t,C^{\frac{k-2i-j}{2}}_{*}(P_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x,t)\cap(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b)))},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b))}

\displaystyle\doteqdot{}

\displaystyle\sum_{2i+j\leq k}\sup_{(x,t)\in\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b)}|x|^{2i+j-1}\|\partial_{t}^{i}\nabla_{\mathbf{C}}^{j}u\|_{C^{0}_{*}(P_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x,t)\cap(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b)))}

\displaystyle+\sum_{2i+j=k}\sup_{(x,t)\in\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b)}|x|^{k-1+\alpha}[\partial_{t}^{i}\nabla_{\mathbf{C}}^{j}u]_{x,C^{\alpha}_{*}(P_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x,t)\cap(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b)))}

\displaystyle+\sum_{0<\frac{k+\alpha-2i-j}{2}<1}\sup_{(x,t)\in\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b)}|x|^{k-1+\alpha}[\partial_{t}^{i}\nabla_{\mathbf{C}}^{j}u]_{t,C^{\frac{k+\alpha-2i-j}{2}}_{*}(P_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x,t)\cap(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b)))}.

Here,
[\,\cdot\,]_{x,C^{\alpha}_{*}}
and
[\,\cdot\,]_{t,C^{\alpha}_{*}}
denote the standard
C^{\alpha}
semi-norms in the variables
x
and
t
respectively.
Namely,

\displaystyle[u]_{x,C^{\alpha}_{*}(\mathbf{C}\times(a,b)\cap\Omega)}

\displaystyle\doteqdot\sup_{(x,t)\neq(x^{\prime},t)\in\mathbf{C}\times(a,b)\cap\Omega}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(x^{\prime},t)|}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\alpha}},\text{ and}

\displaystyle[u]_{t,C^{\alpha}_{*}(\mathbf{C}\times(a,b)\cap\Omega)}

\displaystyle\doteqdot\sup_{(x,t)\neq(x,t^{\prime})\in\mathbf{C}\times(a,b)\cap\Omega}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(x,t^{\prime})|}{|t-t^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}.

Since we use the
C^{1,\alpha}
norm most often, we note explicitly that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b))}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sup_{x,t}|x|^{-1}\sup_{(y,s)\in P_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x,t)}|u(y,s)|+\sup_{x,t}\sup_{(y,s)\in P_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x,t)}|\nabla_{\mathbf{C}}u(y,s)|

\displaystyle+\sup_{x,t}|x|^{\alpha}\sup_{(y,s)\neq(y^{\prime},s)\in P_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x,t)}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbf{C}}u(y,s)-\nabla_{\mathbf{C}}u(y^{\prime},s)|}{|y-y^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle+\sup_{x,t}|x|^{\alpha}\sup_{(y,s)\neq(y,s^{\prime})\in P_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x,t)}\frac{|u(y,s)-u(y,s^{\prime})|}{|s-s^{\prime}|^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}}

where also the suprema above are restricted to points in
\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b)
.

Remark 3.5
.

While the
C^{k,\alpha}
norms defined above are somewhat non-standard,
they have been chosen so that they satisfy the following properties, the proofs which have been left as exercises to the reader.

(1)

(Parabolic Scaling Invariance) If
u:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b)\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\lambda>0
, and
\tilde{u}:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\lambda r,\lambda R}\times(\lambda^{2}a,\lambda^{2}b)\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is given by
\tilde{u}(x,t)=\lambda u(x/\lambda,t/\lambda^{2})
then

\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\lambda r,\lambda R}\times(\lambda^{2}a,\lambda^{2}b))}=\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b))}.

(2)

(Time Translation Invariance) If
u:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b)\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\tilde{u}:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a+t_{0},b+t_{0})\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is given by
\tilde{u}(x,t)=u(x,t-t_{0})
then

\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a+t_{0},b+t_{0}))}=\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b))}.

(3)

(Equivalent to Standard Hölder Norms) There exists
C=C(r,R,k,\alpha)
such that

C^{-1}\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{*}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b))}\leq\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b))}\leq C\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{*}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{r,R}\times(a,b))}.

Similar properties hold for the space-time
C^{k}
norms and the spatial
C^{k}
and
C^{k,\alpha}
norms.

Remark 3.6
.

The choice of using radius
\frac{|x|}{2}
balls in the above definitions was somewhat arbitrary.
Indeed, it can be shown through a covering argument that, for any
L>1
, replacing “
\frac{|x|}{2}
” with “
\frac{|x|}{L}
” in the above definitions gives an equivalent norm, that is, the norms differ by a factor of
C=C(k,\alpha,L)
.

Lemma 3.7

(Graphicality propagates out and back)
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
be an integral
n
-Brakke flow in
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with locally uniformly bounded areas and generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{p}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
for some
p\in(n,\infty]
.
Let
(V_{t})_{t\in(a,b]}
be the associated integral
n
-Brakke flow from Lemma
2.6
.

Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in U\times(a,b]
and let
\mathbf{C}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a minimal regular cone.
For any
\epsilon>0
,
there exists
r_{0},\delta>0
such that the following holds for all
0<\rho<r_{0}
:

if

(V_{t_{0}}-x_{0})\cap A_{\rho/2,\rho}=G_{\rho/2,\rho}(u)

for some
u:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho/2,\rho}\to T^{\perp}C
with

\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho/2,\rho})}\leq\delta,

then

(V_{t}-x_{0})\cap A_{\rho/4,2\rho}=G_{\rho/4,2\rho}(\tilde{u}(\cdot,t))\qquad(\forall t\in[t_{0}-4\rho^{2},t_{0}])

for some extension
\tilde{u}:(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho/4,2\rho})\times[t_{0}-4\rho^{2},t_{0}]\to T^{\perp}\mathbf{C}
of
u
with

\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho/4,2\rho}\times[t_{0}-4\rho^{2},t_{0}])}\leq\epsilon.

Proof.

By translation, assume without loss of generality that
(x_{0},t_{0})=(\mathbf{0},0)
.
Suppose the lemma were false for the sake of contradiction.
Then we can take a sequence
r_{i}=\delta_{i}\searrow 0
and obtain
\rho_{i}\in(0,r_{i})
where the implication fails.
That is,

V_{0}\cap A_{\rho_{i}/2,\rho_{i}}=G_{\rho_{i}/2,\rho_{i}}(u_{i})
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
-graph over
\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho_{i}/2,\rho_{i}}
with
\|u_{i}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho_{i}/2,\rho_{i}})}\leq\delta_{i}
,
but

(V_{t})
is not a
C^{1,\alpha}
-graph over
\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho_{i}/4,2\rho_{i}}\times[t_{0}-4\rho_{i}^{2},t_{0}]
with
C^{1,\alpha}
-norm bounded by
\epsilon
in this region.

Parabolically dilate
V_{t}
by
\lambda_{i}\doteqdot\frac{1}{\rho_{i}}\to+\infty

to obtain
V^{i}_{t}\doteqdot(\eta_{\lambda_{i}})_{\sharp}V_{t\lambda_{i}^{-2}}
and set
\mu^{i}_{t}\doteqdot\mu_{V^{i}_{t}}
.
After passing to a subsequence, Theorem
2.10
applied to the Brakke flow
(\mu_{V_{t}})
implies there exists a stationary, dilation invariant varifold
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}
such that

V^{i}_{0}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{C}^{\prime}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\mu^{i}_{t}\rightharpoonup\mu_{\mathbf{C}^{\prime}}\quad(\forall t<0)

as
i\to\infty
.
Since
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}
is dilation invariant and

\displaystyle V_{0}^{i}\cap A_{1/2,1}=G_{1/2,1}\left(\lambda_{i}u_{i}(\cdot/\lambda_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\text{with }\|\lambda_{i}u_{i}(\cdot/\lambda_{i})\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{1/2,1})}=\|u_{i}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho_{i}/2,\rho_{i}})}\leq\delta_{i}\to 0,

it follows that in fact
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}=\mathbf{C}
.

Now the stationary flow
(\mu^{\infty}_{t}=\mu_{C})_{t\leq 0}
given by
\mathbf{C}
has Gaussian density

\Theta_{\mu^{\infty}}(x,t)=1\qquad\forall(x,t)\in\overline{A_{1/6,4}}\times[-6,0]

since
\mathbf{C}
has smooth link.
The upper semi-continuity of Gaussian density then implies that for any
\sigma>0

\Theta_{\mu^{i}}(x,t)<1+\sigma\qquad\forall(x,t)\in\overline{A_{1/6,4}}\times[-6,0]

for all
i\gg 1
sufficiently large.

By White’s local regularity theorem
[
Whi05
]
(and its generalization to integral Brakke flows
[
ST22
]
),
it follows that, for
i\gg 1
,

V^{i}_{t}
is a smooth mean curvature flow in
A_{1/6,4}\times[-6,0]
with second fundamental form bounded by a dimensional constant
C=C(N)<\infty
in
A_{1/5,3}\times[-5,0]
.
Interior regularity for mean curvature flow then implies that the convergence
V^{i}_{t}\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{}\mathbf{C}
is smooth on
A_{1/4,2}\times[-4,0]
.
It follows that, for all
i\gg 1
,

V^{i}_{t}\cap A_{1/4,2}=G_{1/4,2}(\tilde{w}_{i}(\cdot,t))\qquad(\forall t\in[-4,0])

for some
\tilde{w}_{i}:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{1/4,2}\times[-4,0]\to T^{\perp}C
extending
\lambda_{i}u_{i}(\cdot/\lambda_{i})
with

\|\tilde{w}_{i}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}(C\cap A_{1/4,2}\times[-4,0])}\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{}0.

Since

\|\tilde{w}_{i}(\cdot,0)\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{1/2,1})}=\|\lambda_{i}\tilde{u}_{i}(\cdot/\lambda_{i})\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{1/2,1})}\leq\delta_{i}\to 0,

and derivatives of
\tilde{w}_{i}
converge to 0 on
A_{1/4,2}\times[-4,0]
, we have that in fact

\|\tilde{w}_{i}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{1/4,2}\times[-4,0])}\leq\epsilon

for all
i\gg 1
.
Undoing dilations gives that for
i\gg 1

V_{t}\cap A_{\rho_{i}/4,2\rho_{i}}=G_{\rho_{i}/4,2\rho_{i}}(\tilde{u}_{i}(\cdot,t))\qquad(\forall t\in[-4\rho_{i}^{2},0])

for
\tilde{u}_{i}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\tilde{w}_{i}(x\lambda_{i},t\lambda_{i}^{2}):\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho_{i}/4,2\rho_{i}}\to T^{\perp}\mathbf{C}

extending
u_{i}
and

\|\tilde{u}_{i}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho_{i}/4,2\rho_{i}}\times[-4\rho_{i}^{2},0])}\leq\epsilon,

which contradicts the choice of the
r_{i},\delta_{i},\rho_{i}
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
be an integral
n
-Brakke flow in
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with locally uniformly bounded areas and generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{loc}(U\times(a,b))
.
Let
(V_{t})_{t\in(a,b]}
be the associated family of varifolds as in Lemma
2.6
.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in U\times(a,b]
and
let
\mathbf{C}
be a regular cone.

There exists
\beta,C,r_{0}>0
such that for all
0<\rho<r_{0}
the following holds:

if

(V_{t_{0}}-x_{0})\cap A_{\rho/2,\rho}=G_{\rho/2,\rho}(u)

for some
u:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho/2,\rho}\to T^{\perp}\mathbf{C}
with

\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho/2,\rho})}\leq\beta,

then for any
0<\sigma<\rho

|\nabla_{V_{t_{0}}}H_{V_{t_{0}}}|\leq\frac{C\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}(A_{\rho/2,\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\rho^{2},t_{0}))}}{\sigma}\\
\leq\frac{C\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}(A_{r_{0}/2,r_{0}}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-r_{0}^{2},t_{0}))}}{\sigma}<\infty\quad\text{and}

|\nabla^{2}_{V_{t_{0}}}H_{V_{t_{0}}}|\leq\frac{C\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}(A_{\rho/2,\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\rho^{2},t_{0}))}}{\sigma^{2}}\\

on
V_{t_{0}}\cap A_{\rho/2+\sigma,\rho-\sigma}(x_{0})

Proof.

Throughout, we assume
0<r_{0}\ll 1

\overline{B}_{2r_{0}}(x_{0})\Subset U
and
(t_{0}-4r_{0}^{2},t_{0})\subseteq(a,b)
.
By translation, assume without loss of generality that
(x_{0},t_{0})=(\mathbf{0},0)
.
Assume
0<\rho<r_{0}
and

V_{0}\cap A_{\rho/2,\rho}=G_{\rho/2,\rho}(u)

for some
u:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho/2,\rho}\to T^{\perp}\mathbf{C}
with

\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho/2,\rho})}\leq\beta.

By Lemma
3.7
, for any
\epsilon>0
, we can assume
\beta,r_{0}\ll 1
are sufficiently small (depending on
\epsilon
) so that

V_{t}\cap A_{\rho/4,2\rho}=G_{\rho/4,2\rho}(\tilde{u}(\cdot,t))\qquad\forall t\in[-4\rho^{2},0]

for some extension
\tilde{u}:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho/4,2\rho}\times[-4\rho^{2},0]\to T^{\perp}\mathbf{C}
of
u
with

\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\rho/4,2\rho}\times[-4\rho^{2},0])}\leq\epsilon.

Consider the parabolically rescaled flow
W_{t}\doteqdot(\eta_{1/\rho})_{\sharp}V_{t\rho^{2}}
and note

W_{t}\cap A_{1/4,2}=G_{1/4,2}(\tilde{w}(\cdot,t))\qquad\forall t\in[-4,0]

where

\tilde{w}(x,t)\doteqdot\frac{1}{\rho}\tilde{u}(x\rho,t\rho^{2}),\qquad\|\tilde{w}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(A_{1/4,2}\times[-4,0])}=\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(A_{\rho/4,2\rho}\times[-4\rho^{2},0])}\leq\epsilon.

If
\epsilon=\epsilon(N,\mathbf{C})
is sufficiently small (depending only on
N
and
\mathbf{C}
),
interior estimates (see e.g.
[
ST22
]
or
[

]
)
imply that
W_{t}
is a smooth mean curvature flow on
A_{1/3,3/2}\times[-3,0]

with derivative bounds on the second fundamental form

of the form

(3.1)

\sup_{(x,t)\in A_{1/3,3/2}\times[-3,0]}|\nabla^{k}_{W_{t}}A_{W_{t}}|\leq C_{k}=C_{k}(N,\mathbf{C})\qquad(\forall k\in\mathbb{N}).

In this region

is a smooth mean curvature flow, the mean curvature

satisfies an evolution equation of the form

\partial_{t}H=\Delta H+\nabla A*H+A*\nabla H+A*A*H

(see
[

).
The bounds (
3.1
) imply (
3.2
) is a linear parabolic PDE system for

in the domain

-bounds on the coefficients that depend only on

and

Interior estimates for parabolic systems (see e.g.
[

]
)
therefore imply that for some

(3.3)

\sup_{A_{1/2+\sigma,1-\sigma}}|\nabla^{2}_{W_{0}}H_{W_{0}}|\leq\frac{C}{\sigma^{2}}\sup_{A_{1/2,1}\times[-1,0]}|H_{W_{t}}|\qquad(\forall 0<\sigma<1).

In terms of
V_{t}
, (
3.3
) becomes

\displaystyle\sup_{A_{\rho/2+\sigma,\rho-\sigma}}|\nabla^{2}_{V_{0}}H_{V_{0}}|={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}\sup_{A_{1/2+\sigma/\rho,1-\sigma/\rho}}|\nabla^{2}_{W_{0}}H_{W_{0}}|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(\sigma/\rho)^{2}\rho^{3}}\sup_{A_{1/2,1}\times[-1,0]}|H_{W_{t}}|

(
3.3
)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\sigma^{2}\rho}\sup_{A_{1/2,1}\times[-1,0]}|H_{W_{t}}|

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\sigma^{2}}\sup_{A_{\rho/2,\rho}\times[-\rho^{2},0]}|H_{V_{t}}|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\sigma^{2}}\|H\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}(A_{\rho/2,\rho}\times(-\rho^{2},0))}

\displaystyle(\text{Lemma }\ref{lem nice representative})

for all
0<\sigma<\rho
.
Note that in the last line

denotes the mean curvature of the Brakke flow
(\mu_{t})_{t\in(a,b)}
.
An analogous argument applies to estimate
|\nabla_{V_{0}}H_{V_{0}}|
.
∎

We can now prove Theorem

by adapting the argument
[

, §7]
used for tangent cones of stationary varifolds.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Throughout, we use
(V_{t})_{t\in(a,b]}
to denote the associated family of varifolds given by Lemma
2.6
.
By translation, assume without loss of generality that
(x_{0},t_{0})=(\mathbf{0},0)
.
By Corollary
2.11
, it suffices to show
\mathbf{C}
is the unique tangent cone of
V_{0}
at
\mathbf{0}
.

By Theorem
2.10
, there exists some sequence
\lambda_{k}\nearrow+\infty
such that

V_{k}\doteqdot(\eta_{\lambda_{k}})_{\sharp}V_{0}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{C}.

and
\theta_{V_{k}}(\mathbf{0})=\frac{\mu_{\mathbf{C}}(B_{1})}{\omega_{n}}
for all
k
. Note that the mean curvature
H_{V_{k}}
of

is bounded by

\|H_{V_{k}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2})}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\|H_{V_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2/\lambda_{k}})}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\|H_{V_{t}}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}(B_{\delta}\times(-\delta,0])}\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}0

so long as
\overline{B_{\delta}}\times(-\delta,0)\subseteq U\times(a,b)
and

2/\lambda_{k}<\delta
.
Since
\mathbf{C}
is smooth away from

,

[

]
(see also
[
Sim83
, Ch. 5]
)
implies that

is smooth for all

sufficiently large, and the convergence

is smooth.
In particular, for

V_{k}\cap A_{1/2,1}=G_{1/2,1}(u_{k})

for some

with

\|u_{k}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(A_{1/2,1})}\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}0.

Lemma
3.8
implies

satisfies property
[
Sim83

and therefore
[
Sim83

applies.
That is, for

V_{k}\cap B_{1}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}=G_{0,1}(\tilde{u}_{k})

for some extension

\lim_{\rho\searrow 0}\frac{\tilde{u}_{k}(\rho\omega)}{\rho}=\zeta_{k}(\omega)\qquad(\omega\in L(\mathbf{C}))

Since the

Undoing the dilations reveals that, for a fixed

(3.4)

\displaystyle V_{0}\cap B_{1/\lambda_{k}}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}=G_{0,1/\lambda_{k}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\tilde{u}_{k}(\lambda_{k}x)\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\tilde{u}_{k}(\lambda_{k}\rho\omega)}{\rho}\xrightarrow[\rho\searrow 0]{C^{2}(L(\mathbf{C}))}0.

The uniqueness of the tangent cone now follows.
∎

4.
Pinching Hardt-Simon Minimal Surfaces

Throughout this section, we restrict to the case where
(M_{t}^{n})_{t\in[-T,0)}
is a smooth mean curvature flow of properly embedded hypersurfaces in an open subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

\mathcal{M}=\bigcup_{t\in[-T,0)}M_{t}\times\{t\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}
denotes its space-time track.
Fix a regular cone
\mathbf{C}^{n}_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and let
\mathcal{C}=\{A\cdot\mathbf{C}_{0}:A\in O(n+1)\}
denote all rotations of the cone.
We generally use
\mathbf{C}
to denote a rotation of the cone
\mathbf{C}_{0}
, that is
\mathbf{C}\in\mathcal{C}
.

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.3
,
which says that if
\mathcal{M}
has bounded
H
and develops a singularity with tangent flow given by an area-minimizing quadratic cone, then there is a type II blow-up limit given by a Hardt-Simon minimal surface (see Subsection
4.2
for the relevant definitions).

4.1.
Flows Near a Regular Cone

We begin with general results for mean curvature flows locally close to a regular cone
\mathbf{C}\in\mathcal{C}
.
The approach here was inspired by
[
LSS22
, Section 8]
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say
\mathcal{M}
is
\epsilon
-close to
\mathbf{C}\in\mathcal{C}
if

\mathcal{M}
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
-graph on
\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{-1}}\times[-\epsilon^{-2},-\epsilon^{2}]
with
C^{1,\alpha}
norm at most
\epsilon
.
In other words,

M_{t}\cap A_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{-1}}=G_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{-1}}(u(\cdot,t))\qquad\forall t\in[-\epsilon^{-2},-\epsilon^{2}]

for some
u:\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{-1}}\times[-\epsilon^{-2},-\epsilon^{2}]\to T^{\perp}\mathbf{C}
with

\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{C}\cap A_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{-1}}\times[-\epsilon^{-2},-\epsilon^{2}])}\leq\epsilon.

We say
\mathcal{M}
is
\epsilon
-close to
\mathbf{C}\in\mathcal{C}
at
X=(x,t)
if
\mathcal{M}-X
is
\epsilon
-close to
\mathbf{C}
.

Throughout the remainder of this section,
\epsilon
is always assumed to be less than some small constant
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(n,\mathbf{C}_{0})
that depends only on the regular cone
\mathbf{C}_{0}
and implicitly its dimension
n
.

Definition 4.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is
\epsilon
-close to
\mathbf{C}\in\mathcal{C}
at
X
for some
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
.
Define

1\in[\lambda_{*}(X),\lambda^{*}(X)]\subseteq[0,\infty]

to be the largest interval such that,
for all
\lambda\in[\lambda_{*}(X),\lambda^{*}(X)]
,

\mathcal{D}_{\lambda^{-1}}(\mathcal{M}-X)
is
\epsilon
-close to some
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}=\mathbf{C}^{\prime}_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{C}
at
(\mathbf{0},0)
.

Occasionally, we may write
\lambda_{*}(X;\mathcal{M},\epsilon)
or
\lambda^{*}(X;\mathcal{M},\epsilon)
to emphasize the dependence on
\mathcal{M}
and
\epsilon
.
Observe that
\lambda_{*}(X)
and
\lambda^{*}(X)
are continuous in the basepoint
X
.

We note the following consequence of pseudolocality for mean curvature flow in our setting.

Lemma 4.3
.

Define constants

0<c_{\mathbf{C}_{0}}\doteqdot\frac{1}{2}\sup_{x\in L(\mathbf{C}_{0})}|A_{\mathbf{C}_{0}}|(x)<C_{\mathbf{C}_{0}}\doteqdot 2\sup_{x\in L(\mathbf{C}_{0})}|A_{\mathbf{C}_{0}}|(x)<\infty.

There exists
C=C(n,\mathbf{C}_{0})\gg 1
and
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(n,\mathbf{C}_{0})\ll 1
such that
if
\mathcal{M}
is
\epsilon
-close to
\mathbf{C}\in\mathcal{C}
at
X=(x,t)
for some
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
,
then

(4.1)

\frac{c_{\mathbf{C}_{0}}}{\rho}\leq\sup_{|y-x|=\rho}|A_{M_{t}}|(y)\leq\frac{C_{\mathbf{C}_{0}}}{\rho}\qquad\forall\,(\epsilon+C)\lambda_{*}(X)\leq\rho\leq(\epsilon^{-1}-C)\lambda^{*}(X).

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in[\lambda_{*}(X),\lambda^{*}(X)]
and consider
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\doteqdot\mathcal{D}_{\lambda^{-1}}(\mathcal{M}-X)
.
By definition
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
can be written as a
C^{1,\alpha}
-graph over some
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
on
A_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{-1}}\times[-\epsilon^{-2},-\epsilon^{2}]
, and the
C^{1,\alpha}
-norm is bounded by
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
.

Let
\delta=\delta(n,\mathbf{C}_{0})\ll 1
denote some small constant to be determined which depends only on
n
and
\mathbf{C}_{0}
.
If
\epsilon_{0}\ll 1
is sufficiently small (depending also on
\delta
) and
C\gg 1
is sufficiently large (depending also on
\delta
),
then pseudolocality for mean curvature flow
[
INS19
, Theorem 1.5]
implies that
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
can be written as a Lipschitz graph over
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}
on
A_{\epsilon+C,\epsilon^{-1}-C}\times[-\epsilon^{-2},0]
.
Namely, if
M^{\prime}_{t}
denotes the time
t

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, then

M^{\prime}_{t}\cap A_{\epsilon+C,\epsilon^{-1}-C}=G_{\epsilon+C,\epsilon^{-1}-C}(u(\cdot,t))\qquad\forall t\in[-\epsilon^{-2},0]

for some

with

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{0,1}(\mathbf{C}^{\prime}\cap A_{\epsilon+C,\epsilon^{-1}-C})}\\
\leq C^{\prime}\left(\sup_{x\in\mathbf{C}^{\prime}\cap A_{\epsilon+C,\epsilon^{-1}-C}}\frac{|u(x,t)|}{|x|}+\sup_{x\neq y\in\mathbf{C}^{\prime}\cap A_{\epsilon+C,\epsilon^{-1}-C}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|}{|x-y|}\right)\leq C^{\prime}\delta\\
\forall t\in[-\epsilon^{-2},0],\text{ (where $C^{\prime}$ is a universal constant).}

Interior estimates for mean curvature flow
[
EH91
]
then imply

satisfies
C^{2}
-estimates on
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}\cap A_{\epsilon+C+1,\epsilon^{-1}-C-1}\times[-\epsilon^{-2}+1,0]
of the form

\|u\|_{C^{2}(\mathbf{C}^{\prime}\cap A_{\epsilon+C+1,\epsilon^{-1}-C-1}\times[-\epsilon^{-2}+1,0])}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\delta

for some constant
C^{\prime\prime}=C^{\prime\prime}(n,\mathbf{C}_{0})
.

If
\delta\ll 1
is sufficiently small depending on
n
and
\mathbf{C}_{0}
, then this
C^{2}
-closeness of
M_{0}^{\prime}
to the cone
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}
implies curvature estimates of the form

\frac{c_{\mathbf{C}}}{\rho}\leq\sup_{|y|=\rho}|A_{M^{\prime}_{0}}|(y)\leq\frac{C_{\mathbf{C}}}{\rho}\qquad\forall\epsilon+C+1\leq\rho\leq\epsilon^{-1}-C-1.

The estimate (
4.1
) now follows from undoing the dilation
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda^{-1}}
and translation and letting
\lambda
vary in
[\lambda_{*}(X),\lambda^{*}(X)]
.
Note that, since
\delta=\delta(n,\mathbf{C}_{0})
, the dependence of
\epsilon_{0}
and
C
on
\delta
can instead be regarded as a dependence on
n
and
\mathbf{C}_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(n,\mathbf{C}_{0})
such that the following holds for all
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
:
if
(M_{t}^{n})_{t\in[-T,0)}
is smooth in

and its space-time track

is
\epsilon
-close to
\mathbf{C}\in\mathcal{C}
at

\lambda_{*}(X)>0
.

Proof.

If not, (
4.1
) implies

|A_{M_{t}}|(x)=\lim_{\rho\searrow 0}\sup_{|y-x|=\rho}|A_{M_{t}}|(y)=+\infty,

which contradicts the smoothness of the flow at
(x,t)
. ∎

Next, we show that
\lambda_{*}(x,t)
satisfies a strict inequality in an annulus.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(n,\mathbf{C}_{0})\ll 1
is sufficiently small (depending on

), then
there exists a constant
C=C(n,\mathbf{C}_{0})
such that

\lambda_{*}(y,t)>\lambda_{*}(x,t)\qquad\forall y\in A_{\lambda_{*}(x,t)[\epsilon+C],\lambda^{*}(x,t)[\epsilon^{-1}-C]}(x).

Proof.

Fix
C=C(n,\mathbf{C}_{0})\geq 1
as in Lemma
4.3
.
Assume
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(n,\mathbf{C}_{0})\ll 1
is small enough so that Lemma
4.3

Let
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
.
To simplify notation, denote

\displaystyle r_{0}

\displaystyle\doteqdot(\epsilon+C)\lambda_{*}(x,t),

\displaystyle R_{0}

\displaystyle\doteqdot(\epsilon^{-1}-C)\lambda^{*}(x,t),

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle\doteqdot(\epsilon+100C)\lambda_{*}(x,t),

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle\doteqdot(\epsilon^{-1}-100C)\lambda^{*}(x,t).

Let

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that

Then

(\epsilon+C)\lambda_{*}(y,t)\leq r_{0}\leq(\epsilon^{-1}-C)\lambda^{*}(y,t)

.
Lemma
4.3
therefore gives curvature estimates of the form

\frac{c_{\mathbf{C}_{0}}}{r_{0}}\leq\sup_{|y^{\prime}-y|=r_{0}}|A_{M_{t}}|(y^{\prime}).

Observe also that the sphere
\partial B(y,r_{0})
is contained in the annulus

based at
x
.
Indeed,

implies

\displaystyle|y^{\prime}-x|

\displaystyle\geq|y-x|-|y^{\prime}-y|

\displaystyle>r_{1}-r_{0}

\displaystyle\geq(1+98C)\lambda_{*}(x,t)

\displaystyle>(\epsilon+C)\lambda_{*}(x,t)

.
Therefore, Lemma

also applies and gives

\displaystyle\sup_{|y^{\prime}-y|=r_{0}}|A_{M_{t}}|(y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{|y^{\prime}-y|=r_{0}}\frac{C_{\mathbf{C}_{0}}}{|y^{\prime}-x|}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{|y^{\prime}-y|=r_{0}}\frac{C_{\mathbf{C}_{0}}}{|y-x|-|y^{\prime}-y|}

\displaystyle<\frac{C_{\mathbf{C}_{0}}}{r_{1}-r_{0}}

\displaystyle<\frac{C_{\mathbf{C}_{0}}}{4r_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{\mathbf{C}_{0}}}{r_{0}},

which contradicts estimate (

).
This completes the proof after relabelling

to
C
. ∎

The collection of integer rectifiable
n
-varifolds in
U
with locally uniformly bounded area and locally uniformly bounded generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{p}_{loc}(U)
is weakly compact.

In other words, if
V_{i}
is a sequence of integral
n
-varifolds in
U
such that

(1)

the
V_{i}
have locally uniformly bounded area, i.e.

\sup_{i}\mu_{V_{i}}(K)<\infty\qquad(\forall K\Subset U),

and

(2)

the
V_{i}
have generalized mean curvature
H_{i}\in L^{p}_{loc}(U)

with uniform
L^{p}_{loc}(U)
bounds, i.e.

\sup_{i}\|H_{i}\|_{L^{p}(K,d\mu_{V_{i}})}<\infty\qquad(\forall K\Subset U),

then there exists a subsequence
V_{i_{j}}
and an integral
n
-varifold
V_{\infty}
in
U
such that

V_{i_{j}}\rightharpoonup V_{\infty}
weakly as varifolds.

Moreover,
V_{\infty}
has

\mu_{V_{\infty}}(K)\leq\limsup_{i}\mu_{V_{i}}(K)\qquad(\forall K\Subset U)

and generalized mean curvature
H_{\infty}\in L^{p}_{loc}(U)

with

\|H_{\infty}\|_{L^{p}(K,d\mu_{V_{\infty}})}\leq\limsup_{i}\|H_{i}\|_{L^{p}(K,d\mu_{V_{i}})}\qquad(\forall K\Subset U).

Proof.

Allard’s compactness theorem
[
All72
]
gives compactness under the weaker assumption where (2) is replaced by the bound

\sup_{i}|\delta V_{i}(X)|\leq C_{K}\|X\|_{C^{0}(K)}\qquad\forall X\in C^{1}_{c}(K,\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,K\Subset U.

Therefore, there exists a subsequential limit
V_{i_{j}}\rightharpoonup V_{\infty}

such that
V_{\infty}
is an integral
n
-varifold
with locally finite area and the weaker property that

|\delta V_{\infty}(X)|\leq C_{K}\|X\|_{C^{0}}\qquad\forall X\in C^{1}_{c}(K,\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,K\Subset U.

However, convergence as varifolds
V_{i_{j}}\rightharpoonup V_{\infty}
implies
that for any
K\subseteq U
compact and
X\in C^{1}_{c}(K,\mathbb{R}^{N})

(A.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &|\delta V_{\infty}(X)|\\
={}&\lim_{j\to\infty}|\delta V_{i_{j}}(X)|\\
={}&\lim_{j\to\infty}\left|\int H_{i_{j}}\cdot Xd\mu_{V_{i_{j}}}\right|\\
\leq{}&\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left(\limsup_{i\to\infty}\|H_{i}\|_{L^{p}_{loc}(K,d\mu_{V_{i}})}\right)\left(\int|X|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}d\mu_{V_{\infty}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}&&\text{if }p\in(1,\infty),\\
&\left(\limsup_{i\to\infty}\|H_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}_{loc}(K,d\mu_{V_{i}})}\right)\int|X|d\mu_{V_{\infty}}&&\text{if }p=\infty.\end{aligned}\right.\end{aligned}

The Radon-Nikodym theorem then implies that
\delta V_{\infty}=-H_{\infty}\in L^{p}_{loc}(U,d\mu_{V_{\infty}})
.
(
A.1
) then also implies

\|H_{\infty}\|_{L^{p}(K,d\mu_{V_{\infty}})}\leq\limsup_{i}\|H_{i}\|_{L^{p}(K,d\mu_{V_{i}})}.

∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
2\leq n<N
,
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open, and
p\in(1,\infty]
.
Let
(V_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}}
be a collection of integer rectifiable
n
-varifolds with generalized mean curvature
H_{i}\in L^{p}_{loc}(U)
.
Assume

\sup_{i}\mu_{V_{i}}(K)+\sup_{i}\|H_{i}\|_{L^{p}(K,d\mu_{V_{i}})}<\infty\qquad(\forall K\Subset U).

If

(A.2)

\int fd\mu_{V_{i}}\xrightarrow{i\to\infty}\int fd\mu_{V_{\infty}}\qquad\forall f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\text{ with }f\geq 0,

then
V_{i}\rightharpoonup V_{\infty}
as varifolds as
i\to\infty
.

Proof.

We first show
d\mu_{V_{i}}\rightharpoonup d\mu_{V_{\infty}}
.

Let
f\in C^{0}_{c}(U)
.
There exists
\delta>0
and a compact set
K\subseteq U
such that

\supp f\subseteq B_{\delta}(\supp f)\subseteq K\subseteq U

where
B_{\delta}(\supp f)
denotes the radius
\delta
neighborhood of
\supp f
.
Denote

C_{K}\doteqdot\sup_{i}\mu_{V_{i}}(K)<\infty.

Let
\epsilon>0
.
Split
f=f_{+}-f_{-}
into positive and negative parts.
Convolving
f_{+},f_{-}
with a suitable mollifiers, we can find
\tilde{f}_{+},\tilde{f}_{-}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
such that

\tilde{f}_{\pm}\geq 0,\qquad\supp\tilde{f}_{\pm}\subseteq K,\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\|\tilde{f}_{\pm}-f_{\pm}\|_{C^{0}}<\frac{1}{6C_{K}}\epsilon.

Then

\displaystyle\left|\int fd\mu_{V_{i}}-\int fd\mu_{V_{\infty}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\left|\int f_{+}d\mu_{V_{i}}-\int f_{+}d\mu_{V_{\infty}}\right|+\left|\int f_{-}d\mu_{V_{i}}-\int f_{-}d\mu_{V_{\infty}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\left|\int f_{+}-\tilde{f}_{+}d\mu_{V_{i}}\right|+\left|\int\tilde{f}_{+}d\mu_{V_{i}}-\int\tilde{f}_{+}d\mu_{V_{\infty}}\right|+\left|\int f_{+}-\tilde{f}_{+}d\mu_{V_{\infty}}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int f_{-}-\tilde{f}_{-}d\mu_{V_{i}}\right|+\left|\int\tilde{f}_{-}d\mu_{V_{i}}-\int\tilde{f}_{-}d\mu_{V_{\infty}}\right|+\left|\int f_{-}-\tilde{f}_{-}d\mu_{V_{\infty}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle 4\|f-\tilde{f}\|_{C^{0}}C_{K}+\left|\int\tilde{f}_{+}d\mu_{V_{i}}-\int\tilde{f}_{+}d\mu_{V_{\infty}}\right|+\left|\int\tilde{f}_{-}d\mu_{V_{i}}-\int\tilde{f}_{-}d\mu_{V_{\infty}}\right|

\displaystyle<{}

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}\epsilon+\left|\int\tilde{f}_{+}d\mu_{V_{i}}-\int\tilde{f}_{+}d\mu_{V_{\infty}}\right|+\left|\int\tilde{f}_{-}d\mu_{V_{i}}-\int\tilde{f}_{-}d\mu_{V_{\infty}}\right|

\displaystyle<{}

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle(\forall i\gg 1)

where the last inequality follows by assumption (
A.2
).
This completes the proof that
d\mu_{V_{i}}\rightharpoonup d\mu_{V_{\infty}}.

Next, we prove the varifold convergence
V_{i}\rightharpoonup V_{\infty}
.
Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
V_{i}\not\rightharpoonup V_{\infty}
.
Then there exists
f\in C^{0}_{c}(G(n,U))
,
\epsilon>0
,
and a subsequence
V_{i_{j}}
such that

\left|\int fdV_{i_{j}}-\int fdV_{\infty}\right|>\epsilon\qquad\forall j.

Since the
V_{i_{j}}
have locally uniformly bounded areas and locally uniformly bounded generalized mean curvatures
H_{i_{j}}\in L^{p}_{loc}(U)
,
Lemma
A.1
implies there exists an integer rectifiable
n
-varifold
V_{\infty}^{\prime}

and a subsequence still denoted
V_{i_{j}}
such that
V_{i_{j}}\rightharpoonup V_{\infty}^{\prime}
.
In particular,

d\mu_{V_{\infty}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}d\mu_{V_{i_{j}}}=d\mu_{V_{\infty}^{\prime}}.

Because
V_{\infty},V_{\infty}^{\prime}
are integer rectifiable
n
-varifolds with
d\mu_{V_{\infty}}=d\mu_{V_{\infty}^{\prime}}
,

V_{\infty}=V_{\infty}^{\prime}
.
We then have a contradiction that
V_{i_{j}}\not\rightharpoonup V_{\infty}=V_{\infty}^{\prime}
and
V_{i_{j}}\rightharpoonup V_{\infty}^{\prime}=V_{\infty}.

This contradiction proves that in fact
V_{i}\rightharpoonup V_{\infty}
.
∎

Proposition A.3

(Monotonicity formula)
.

Let
2\leq n<N
,
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
open, and
p\in(n,\infty]
.
Let
V
be an integer rectifiable
n
-varifold in
U
with generalized mean curvature
H\in L^{p}_{loc}(U)
.
Let
x_{0}\in U
and
\overline{B_{R}(x_{0})}\subseteq U
.
Then for any
0<\sigma<\rho<R

(A.3)

\left(e^{\frac{\|H\|}{1-\frac{n}{p}}\rho^{1-\frac{n}{p}}}\frac{\mu_{V}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n}}+e^{\frac{\|H\|}{1-\frac{n}{p}}\rho^{1-\frac{n}{p}}}-1\right)\\
-\left(e^{\frac{\|H\|}{1-\frac{n}{p}}\sigma^{1-\frac{n}{p}}}\frac{\mu_{V}(B_{\sigma}(x_{0}))}{\sigma^{n}}+e^{\frac{\|H\|}{1-\frac{n}{p}}\sigma^{1-\frac{n}{p}}}-1\right)\\
\geq\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\sigma}(x_{0})}\frac{|(x-x_{0})^{\perp}|^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+2}}d\mu_{V}\geq 0

where
\|H\|=\|H\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}(x_{0}),d\mu_{V})}
.

In particular,

e^{\frac{\|H\|}{1-\frac{n}{p}}\rho^{1-\frac{n}{p}}}\frac{\mu_{V}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n}}+e^{\frac{\|H\|}{1-\frac{n}{p}}\rho^{1-\frac{n}{p}}}-1\text{ is non-decreasing in }\rho

and

\theta_{V}(x_{0})\doteqdot\lim_{\rho\searrow 0}\frac{\mu_{V}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n}}\text{ exists.}

Proof.

The following proof follows
[
Sim14
, Ch. 4, §4]
.
We provide the proof for
p\in(n,\infty)
and let the reader make the necessary adjustments to the proof in the case of
p=\infty
.

For any
0<\rho<R/(1+\epsilon)
,

(A.4)

\frac{d}{d\rho}(\rho^{-n}I(\rho))=\rho^{-n}\frac{d}{d\rho}J(\rho)-\rho^{-n}\int\rho^{-1}(x-x_{0})\cdot H\varphi_{\epsilon}(|x-x_{0}|/\rho)d\mu_{V}(x)

where
\varphi_{\epsilon}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a smooth, non-increasing function with
\varphi_{\epsilon}(s)\equiv 1
for
s\in[0,1]
and
\supp\varphi_{\epsilon}\subseteq[0,1+\epsilon]
,

\displaystyle x_{0}\in U\text{ and }\overline{B_{R}(x_{0})}\subseteq U,

\displaystyle I(\rho)\doteqdot\int\varphi_{\epsilon}(|x-x_{0}|/\rho)d\mu_{V}(x)\geq 0,\text{ and}

\displaystyle J(\rho)\doteqdot\int\frac{|(x-x_{0})^{\perp}|^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}\varphi_{\epsilon}(|x-x_{0}|/\rho)d\mu_{V}(x)\geq 0.

The integral on the right-hand side of (
A.4
) can be estimated by

\displaystyle\left|\rho^{-n}\int\frac{x-x_{0}}{\rho}\cdot H\varphi_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{\rho}\right)d\mu_{V}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)\rho^{-n}\|H\|_{L^{p}(B_{(1+\epsilon)\rho}(x_{0}),d\mu_{V})}(I(\rho))^{1-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)\rho^{-n/p}\|H\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}(x_{0}),d\mu_{V})}(\rho^{-n}I(\rho))^{1-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\left(0<\rho<\frac{R}{1+\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)\rho^{-n/p}\|H\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}(x_{0}),d\mu_{V})}(1+\rho^{-n}I(\rho))

where in the last step we used
a\geq 0\implies a^{1-1/p}\leq 1+a
.
Inserting this estimate into (
A.4
) yields

(A.5)

\frac{d}{d\rho}(1+\rho^{-n}I(\rho))\geq\rho^{-n}\frac{d}{d\rho}J(\rho)-(1+\epsilon)\rho^{-n/p}\|H\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}(x_{0}),d\mu_{V})}(1+\rho^{-n}I(\rho))\\
\forall 0<\rho<R/(1+\epsilon).

To simplify the notation, we write
\|H\|=\|H\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}(x_{0}),d\mu_{V})}
for the remainder of the proof.
Multiplying (
A.5
) by the integrating factor

F_{\epsilon}(\rho)\doteqdot e^{\int_{0}^{\rho}(1+\epsilon)\|H\|\tilde{\rho}^{-n/p}d\tilde{\rho}}=e^{\frac{(1+\epsilon)\|H\|}{1-n/p}\rho^{1-n/p}}\geq 1

and using the fact that
\frac{d}{d\rho}J\geq 0

yields

\frac{d}{d\rho}\left(F_{\epsilon}(\rho)\rho^{-n}I(\rho)+F_{\epsilon}(\rho)-1\right)\geq F_{\epsilon}(\rho)\rho^{-n}\frac{d}{d\rho}J\geq\rho^{-n}\frac{d}{d\rho}J.

Integrating from
\sigma
to
\rho
then gives

\left(F_{\epsilon}(\rho)\frac{I_{\epsilon}(\rho)}{\rho^{n}}+F_{\epsilon}(\rho)-1\right)-\left(F_{\epsilon}(\sigma)\frac{I_{\epsilon}(\sigma)}{\sigma^{n}}+F_{\epsilon}(\sigma)-1\right)\geq\int_{\sigma}^{\rho}\tilde{\rho}^{-n}\frac{d}{d\tilde{\rho}}Jd\tilde{\rho}.

Using that
\frac{d}{d\rho}\left(\varphi_{\epsilon}(|x-x_{0}|/\rho)\right)
is supported on the region
\rho\leq|x-x_{0}|\leq(1+\epsilon)\rho
,
it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma}^{\rho}\tilde{\rho}^{-n}\frac{d}{d\tilde{\rho}}Jd\tilde{\rho}

\displaystyle=\int_{\sigma}^{\rho}\int\frac{|(x-x_{0})^{\perp}|^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}\tilde{\rho}^{-n}\frac{d}{d\tilde{\rho}}\left(\varphi_{\epsilon}(|x-x_{0}|/\rho)\right)\,d\mu_{V}d\tilde{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\sigma}^{\rho}\int\frac{|(x-x_{0})^{\perp}|^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+2}}\frac{d}{d\tilde{\rho}}\left(\varphi_{\epsilon}(|x-x_{0}|/\rho)\right)\,d\mu_{V}d\tilde{\rho}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{|(x-x_{0})^{\perp}|^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+2}}\left(\varphi_{\epsilon}(|x-x_{0}|/\rho)-\varphi_{\epsilon}(|x-x_{0}|/\sigma)\right)\,d\mu_{V}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})\setminus B_{(1+\epsilon)\sigma}(x_{0})}\frac{|(x-x_{0})^{\perp}|^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+2}}\,d\mu_{V}.

Letting
\epsilon\searrow 0
finally yields

\left(e^{\frac{\|H\|}{1-\frac{n}{p}}\rho^{1-\frac{n}{p}}}\frac{\mu_{V}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n}}+e^{\frac{\|H\|}{1-\frac{n}{p}}\rho^{1-\frac{n}{p}}}-1\right)\\
-\left(e^{\frac{\|H\|}{1-\frac{n}{p}}\sigma^{1-\frac{n}{p}}}\frac{\mu_{V}(B_{\sigma}(x_{0}))}{\sigma^{n}}+e^{\frac{\|H\|}{1-\frac{n}{p}}\sigma^{1-\frac{n}{p}}}-1\right)\\
\geq\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\sigma}(x_{0})}\frac{|(x-x_{0})^{\perp}|^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+2}}d\mu_{V}\geq 0.

In particular, we have monotonicity of

\rho\mapsto e^{\frac{\|H\|}{1-\frac{n}{p}}\rho^{1-\frac{n}{p}}}\frac{\mu_{V}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n}}+e^{\frac{\|H\|}{1-\frac{n}{p}}\rho^{1-\frac{n}{p}}}-1.

Hence, its limit as
\rho\searrow 0
exists and thus
\theta_{V}(x_{0})
is well-defined.
∎

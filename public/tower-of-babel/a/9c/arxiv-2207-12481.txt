Abstract
. Let ℬ \mathcal{B} be a star-algebra with a state ϕ \phi , and t > 0 t>0 . Through a Fock space construction, we define two states Φ t \Phi_{t} and Ψ t \Psi_{t} on the tensor algebra 𝒯 ⁡ ( ℬ , ϕ ) \mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B},\phi) such that under the natural map ( ℬ , ϕ ) → ( 𝒯 ⁡ ( ℬ , ϕ ) , Φ t , Ψ t ) (\mathcal{B},\phi)\rightarrow(\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B},\phi),\Phi_{t},\Psi_{t}) , free independence of arguments leads to free independence, while Boolean independence of centered arguments leads to conditionally free independence. The construction gives a new operator realization of the ( 1 + t ) (1+t) ’th free convolution power of any joint (star) distribution. We also compute several von Neumann algebras which arise.

1.
Introduction

Early results on the addition of freely independent random variables and free convolution emphasized the parallels with the theorems in classical probability. One of the first results exhibiting qualitatively new behavior in the free case was obtained by Nica and Speicher in

[
NS96
]
. They showed that for any (joint) distribution
\mu
, its free convolution power
\mu^{\boxplus(1+t)}
is defined for any real
t\geq 0
. Related results were obtained by random matrix methods by Voiculescu in the appendix of
[
NS96
]
; by a Fock space representation
[
Shl97
]
; by complex analytic methods in
[
BV95
,
BB04
]
; and in the operator-valued setting in
[
ABFN13
,
Shl13
]
.

Classically, one can only expect such behavior for infinitely divisible distributions. So the key illustrative example was the Bernoulli distribution, whose convolution powers (the binomial distributions) are only defined for integer powers, but whose free convolution powers (the free binomial distributions) are defined for any power
1+t\geq 1
. The free binomial distributions are the Kesten-McKay distributions in the symmetric case, and more generally belong to the free Meixner class.

In
[
Ans07
]
, the first author introduced the class of multivariate free Meixner states as joint distributions of operators obtained via a Fock space construction with depth two action
[
AM21
]
. Again, free multinomial distribution was proved to be the key example of a non-infinitely-divisible multivariate free Meixner distribution. Here the free multinomial distribution is the free convolution power of the joint distribution of several orthogonal projections.

In this paper we show that the preceding paragraph is somewhat misleading. There is nothing special about the free multinomial distribution. For any fixed
t\geq 0
, and any
\ast
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
with a state
\phi
, we construct a Fock space such that for any
n
-tuple of centered elements
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
, the joint distribution of the corresponding operators
X(f_{1}),\ldots,X(f_{n})
on the Fock space is the
(1+t)
’th free convolution power of the joint distribution of
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
. One gets the free multinomial distribution by taking the
f_{i}
’s to be centered versions of orthogonal projections. One difference from the construction in
[
Shl97
]
is that
X(f)
is symmetric for
f
symmetric, and thus the result also holds for their joint
\ast
-distributions.

We state the full results in terms of the tensor algebra. Denote
\mathcal{B}^{\circ}=\ker\phi
, and let
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B},\phi)=\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B}^{\circ})
be the tensor algebra of
\mathcal{B}^{\circ}
. It will be convenient to denote the element of
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
corresponding to

by

, so that
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
is identified with the algebra of polynomials in
\left\{X(f):f\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ}\right\}
(subject to linearity relations). We construct explicit Fock representations for two (families of) states
\Phi_{t}
and
\Psi_{t}
on
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
with the following properties.

For

It is a familiar fact that under the Gaussian functor, orthogonal vectors in a Hilbert space correspond to independent (Gaussian) operators on the symmetric Fock space, and under the semicircular functor, orthogonal vectors in a Hilbert space correspond to free (semicircular) operators on the full Fock space. Similarly, the usual/free (compound) Poisson Segal quantization maps elements of an algebra which are strongly orthogonal (in the sense that their product is zero), to independent/free compound Poisson operators. The theorem above lists somewhat similar properties, with orthogonality of initial elements replaced by their free/Boolean independence.

As a by-product, we find a curious formula for Boolean cumulants of centered elements:

B^{\phi}[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}]=\phi[f_{1}\Lambda(f_{2},\Lambda(f_{3},\ldots,\Lambda(f_{n-1},f_{n})))]

for
n\geq 3
, where
\Lambda(f,g)=fg-\phi[fg]
.

2.
Background

2.1.
Partitions

A
partition

\pi
of a subset
S\subset\mathbb{N}
is a collection of disjoint subsets of
S
(called
blocks
of
\pi
) whose union equals
S
. We will use
i\overset{\pi}{\thicksim}j
to say that
i
and
j
are in the same block of
\pi
. In this paper, we will only be concerned with partitions of
[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.

Let
\mathrm{NC}(n)
denote the set of
noncrossing partitions
over
[n]
, that is, those partitions
\pi
such that there are no
i<j<k<\ell
such that
i\overset{\pi}{\thicksim}k
and
j\overset{\pi}{\thicksim}\ell
unless all four are in the same block.

A block
V
of a noncrossing partition
\pi
is called inner if for some other block
W\in\pi
, there exist
j\in V
and
i,k\in W
such that
i<j<k
. Otherwise
V
is called outer. Denote
\Inner(\pi)
the inner blocks of
\pi
, and
\Outer(\pi)
the outer blocks.

The set
\widetilde{\mathrm{NC}}(n)
of irreducible partitions is the set of noncrossing partitions with a single outer block, which is denoted by
V_{out}(\pi)
.
\widetilde{\mathrm{NC}}_{ns}(n)
is the set of all such partitions with no singleton blocks.

Finally, let
\mathrm{Int}(n)
denote the
interval partitions
over
[n]
, that is, those partitions
\pi
such that whenever
i<j
and
i\overset{\pi}{\thicksim}j
, we have
i\overset{\pi}{\thicksim}k
for all
i<k<j
.

2.2.
Distributions and von Neumann algebras

A noncommutative probability space
(\mathcal{A},\phi)
is a unital algebra
\mathcal{A}
with a linear functional
\phi
on it. A noncommutative
\ast
-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\phi)
is a unital
\ast
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
with a positive linear functional
\phi
on it.

For a noncommutative
\ast
-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\phi)
, denote by
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\phi)
the corresponding GNS Hilbert space. If
\mathcal{A}
is represented on
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\phi)
by bounded operators (in particular, if
\mathcal{A}
is a
C^{\ast}
-algebra), denote by
W^{\ast}(\mathcal{A},\phi)
the von Neumann algebra obtained as the weak closure of
\mathcal{A}
in this representation.

We will also need the following result, which follows easily from the uniqueness of the GNS construction.

Lemma 1
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\phi)
and
(\mathcal{B},\psi)
be two noncommutative
\ast
-probability spaces, and
F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
a surjective
\ast
-homomorphism such that
\phi=\psi\circ F
. Then
W^{\ast}(\mathcal{A},\phi)\simeq W^{\ast}(\mathcal{B},\psi)
.

2.3.
Independence

Let
(\mathcal{A},\phi)
be a noncommutative probability space.

•

Subalgebras
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{k}\subset(\mathcal{A},\phi)
are
freely independent
, or
free
, with respect to
\phi
if whenever

a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{u(i)}\text{ such that }u(1)\neq u(2)\neq\ldots\neq u(n)\text{ and }\phi[a_{i}]=0\,\forall i=1,\ldots,n,\text{ then}

\phi[a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{n}]=0.

Elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
are
\ast
-free if the
\ast
-subalgebras they generate are freely independent.

•

Subalgebras
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{k}\subset(\mathcal{A},\phi)
that do not contain the unit are
Boolean independent
with respect to
\phi
if whenever

a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{u(i)}\text{ such that }u(1)\neq u(2)\neq\ldots\neq u(n),\text{ then}

\phi[a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{n}]=\phi[a_{1}]\phi[a_{2}]\ldots\phi[a_{n}].

Elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
are
\ast
-Boolean independent if the non-unital
\ast
-subalgebras they generate are freely independent.

•

Let
\psi
be a second state on
\mathcal{A}
. Subalgebras
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{k}\subset\mathcal{A}
are
conditionally free
in
(\mathcal{A},\phi,\psi)
if whenever

a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{u(i)}\text{ such that }u(1)\neq u(2)\neq\ldots\neq u(n)\text{ and }\psi[a_{i}]=0\,\forall i=1,\ldots,n,\text{ then}

\phi[a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{n}]=\phi[a_{1}]\phi[a_{2}]\ldots\phi[a_{n}].

2.4.
Cumulants

For a family of multilinear functionals
\left\{F_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
and a partition
\pi\in\mathrm{NC}(n)
, denote

F_{\pi}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]=\prod_{V\in\pi}F_{\left|V\right|}[a_{i}:i\in V],

where the argument of each factor are taken in the order of their appearance in the block.

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
.

•

Their free cumulants
R^{\phi}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]
with respect to
\phi
are defined recursively via

\phi[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{NC}(n)}R^{\phi}_{\pi}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}].

•

Their Boolean cumulants
B^{\phi}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]
with respect to
\phi
are defined recursively via

\phi[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Int}(n)}B^{\phi}_{\pi}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}].

•

Their conditionally free cumulants
R^{\phi,\psi}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]
with respect to
(\phi,\psi)
are defined recursively via

\phi[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{NC}(n)}\left(\prod_{V\in\Inner(\pi)}R^{\psi}_{\left|V\right|}[a_{i}:i\in V]\right)\left(\prod_{U\in\Outer(\pi)}R^{\phi,\psi}_{\left|U\right|}[a_{i}:i\in U]\right).

Theorem
.

•

Subalgebras
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\subset(\mathcal{A},\phi)
are freely independent if and only if for any
a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{u(i)}
,

R^{\phi}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]=0

unless all
u(1)=u(2)=\ldots
.

•

Non-unital subalgebras
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\subset(\mathcal{A},\phi)
are Boolean independent if and only if for any
a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{u(i)}
,

B^{\phi}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]=0

unless all
u(1)=u(2)=\ldots
.

•

Suppose the subalgebras
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\subset(\mathcal{A},\phi,\psi)
are freely independent in
(\mathcal{A},\psi)
. They are conditionally free if and only if for any
a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{u(i)}
,

R^{\phi,\psi}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]=0

unless all
u(1)=u(2)=\ldots
.

As usual, we denote by
\boxplus,\uplus
the (additive) free and Boolean convolutions.

2.5.
Free products

We will use the notation from
[
Dyk93
]
. For von Neumann algebras
A
and
B
with traces
\tau_{A}
and
\tau_{B}
,
\underset{\alpha}{A}\oplus\underset{\beta}{B}

and

whose associated trace
\tau(a,b)=\alpha\tau_{A}(a)+\beta\tau_{B}(b)
. In the case that one of the numbers
\alpha,\beta
equals zero, say for example

, we will take
\underset{\alpha}{A}\oplus\underset{\beta}{B}
to denote the algebra
A
with trace
\tau_{A}
. If we also want to give names to the projections corresponding to the identity elements of
A
and
B
, we will write
\underset{\alpha}{\overset{p}{A}}\oplus\overset{q}{\underset{\beta}{B}}
, meaning

and

For
r>1
, Dykema and Radulescu have independently introduced interpolated free group factors
L(\mathbb{F}_{r})
. These are II
1
factors such that for
r\in\mathbb{N}
,

is the von Neumann algebra of the free group on
r

In a
W^{\ast}
-noncommutative probability space
(\mathcal{A},\tau)
, let
\mathcal{R}
be a copy of the hyperfinite II
1
factor, and
\omega=\left\{X^{(t)}:t\in T\right\}
a free semicircular family free from
\mathcal{R}
. Choose self-adjoint projections
p_{t}\in\mathcal{R}
such that

. Then
L(\mathbb{F}_{r})
is isomorphic to
W^{\ast}(\mathcal{R},\left\{p_{t}X^{(t)}p_{t}:t\in T\right\})
. Then the isomorphism class of
L(\mathbb{F}_{r})
depends only on
r
and not on the choice of the projections. Dykema and Radulescu also proved that for any
r,s>1
, the free product
L(\mathbb{F}_{r})\ast L(\mathbb{F}_{s})\simeq L(\mathbb{F}_{r+s})
, as well as the compression relation

L(\mathbb{F}_{r})_{s}=L\left(\mathbb{F}\left(1+\frac{r-1}{s^{2}}\right)\right).

In
[
Dyk93
]
, Dykema derived formulas for the reduced free products of direct sums of finite dimensional algebras and free group factors, with faithful tracial states. We refer the reader to that paper for specific results, and in particular for the notion of free dimension.

\Gamma_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{A},\rho)=W^{\ast}(X(f):f\in\mathcal{A})=W^{\ast}(X(f):f\in\mathcal{A}^{sa}).

Suppose that the following conditions hold:

\Lambda(g^{\ast}\otimes f^{\ast})^{\ast}=\Lambda(f\otimes g),

f\gamma[gh]-\gamma[fg]h=\Lambda(f\otimes\Lambda(g\otimes h))-\Lambda(\Lambda(f\otimes g)\otimes h),

and

\gamma[f]\rho[g]=\rho[f]\gamma[g].

Then the vacuum vector is cyclic and separating for
\Gamma_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{A},\rho)
. If in addition
\rho
is tracial, the vacuum state is tracial on
\Gamma_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{A},\rho)
.

In this paper we will consider the case where
\gamma
is scalar-valued, and moreover
\gamma
and
\rho
are proportional to each other. A priori this gives a two-parameter family, but we show that a particular choice of a one-parameter family is natural.

Remark 1
.

In the case of
\gamma:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
, it is essential that
\mathcal{A}
be an algebra. In the scalar-valued construction above, it suffices for
\mathcal{A}
to be a vector space such that
\Lambda:\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
.

In
[
AM21
]
, it was also assumed that
\rho
is faithful. The conclusions in Proposition
1
(a,b,c) in fact hold without this assumption.

3.
Constructions and cumulants

3.1.
States on the tensor algebra

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a vector space with an involution
\ast
. Denote by
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{V})=\mathbb{C}1\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{V}^{\otimes n}
the tensor algebra of
\mathcal{V}
, with tensor multiplication and involution. It has the universal property that any
\ast
-linear map from
\mathcal{V}
to a
\ast
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
has a unique extension to a
\ast
-homomorphism from
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{V})
to
\mathcal{A}
.

Throughout the paper, let
\mathcal{B}
be a unital
\ast
-algebra, and
\phi
a unital
\ast
-linear functional on
\mathcal{B}
. Denote
\mathcal{B}^{\circ}=\ker\phi
. Then we can form the tensor algebra
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B}^{\circ})
, which (to emphasize the dependence on
\phi
) we will denote typically denote
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
.

Proposition 2
.

\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
does not depend on
\phi
.

Proof.

Take the quotient of the tensor algebra
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B})
by the relation

(4)

f_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{i-1}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes f_{i+1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{n}=f_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{i-1}\otimes f_{i+1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{n},

for each
n
and each
1\leq i\leq n
. We denote the resulting algebra by
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\mathcal{B})
and call it the reduced tensor algebra of
\mathcal{B}
. We now claim that for any
\phi
,
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B}^{\circ})=\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
is
\ast
-isomorphic to
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\mathcal{B})
. Indeed, the linear extension of the map
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\mathcal{B})\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B}^{\circ})
,

f_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{n}\mapsto\sum_{S\subset[n]}\prod_{i\in S}\phi[f_{i}]\bigotimes_{j\not\in S}f_{i}^{\circ}

is well defined on
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\mathcal{B})
, and is a bijective
\ast
-homomorphism.
∎

To distinguish elements
f\in\mathcal{B}
from their counterparts in
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\mathcal{B})
, we will denote the element
f_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{r}(\mathcal{B})
by
X(f_{1})\ldots X(f_{n})
, with
X(1_{\mathcal{B}})=1
. This notation is consistent with the multiplication, involution, and relation on
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\mathcal{B})
, as well as the isomorphism with any
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B}^{\circ})=\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
.

Definition 2
.

For
t\geq 0
, define two families of states on
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B}^{\circ})=\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
by

(5)

\Phi_{t}[X(f_{1})\ldots X(f_{n})]=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{NC}_{ns}(n)}(1+t)^{\left|\Outer(\pi)\right|}t^{\left|\Inner(\pi)\right|}\prod_{V\in\pi}\phi[W(V)],

and

(6)

\Psi_{t}[X(f_{1})\ldots X(f_{n})]=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{NC}_{ns}(n)}t^{\left|\pi\right|}\prod_{V\in\pi}\phi[W(V)].

We will have much better descriptions of these states in terms of their free, Boolean, and conditionally free cumulants.

3.2.
Three Fock space constructions

Construction 2
.

Let
(\mathcal{B},\phi)
be a noncommutative
\ast
-probability space, so that
\mathcal{B}
is a unital
\ast
-algebra, and
\phi
is a (not necessarily faithful) state on it. Using the GNS construction for
\mathcal{B}
, we may construct a pointed Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
(that is, a Hilbert space with a unit vector
\Omega
) such that

•

\mathcal{B}
acts on
\mathcal{H}
by densely defined, possibly unbounded operators whose domains contain
\Omega
.

•

\Omega
is cyclic for
\mathcal{B}
, and
\phi=\left\langle{\cdot\Omega},{\Omega}\right\rangle
.

Denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\circ}
the orthogonal complement in
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathbb{C}\Omega
. Note that for any
f\in\mathcal{B}
,
f\Omega-\phi[f]\Omega\in\mathcal{H}^{\circ}
. Denote

\mathcal{B}^{\circ}=\left\{g\in\mathcal{B}:\phi[g]=0\right\},

so that
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}^{\circ}\oplus\mathbb{C}
. Finally denote

(7)

\Lambda(f,g)=fg-\phi[fg].

Fix
t\geq 0
. On the Fock space
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{H}^{\circ},t)=\mathbb{C}\Omega\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}^{\circ})^{\otimes n}
, define the inner product by the linear extension of

\left\langle{\xi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\xi_{n}},{\eta_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\eta_{n}}\right\rangle_{t}=\delta_{n=k}(t+1)t^{n-1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle{\xi_{i}},{\eta_{i}}\right\rangle

For
f\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ}
, define

\displaystyle a^{+}(f)(\xi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=(f\Omega)\otimes\xi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\xi_{n},\quad a^{+}(f)(\Omega)=f\Omega,

\displaystyle a^{-}_{\phi}(f)(\xi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=\left\langle{f\xi_{1}},{\Omega}\right\rangle\xi_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes\xi_{n},

\displaystyle a^{-}(f)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}ta^{-}_{\phi}(f)&\text{ on }(\mathcal{H}^{\circ})^{\otimes n},n\geq 2,\\
(1+t)a^{-}_{\phi}(f)&\text{ on }(\mathcal{H}^{\circ})^{\otimes n},n=1,\\
0&\text{ on }(\mathcal{H}^{\circ})^{\otimes n},n=0,\end{cases}

\displaystyle a^{0}(f)(\xi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=(f\xi_{1}-\left\langle{f\xi_{1}},{\Omega}\right\rangle\Omega)\otimes\xi_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes\xi_{n},\quad a^{0}(f)\Omega=0.

For
f\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ}
, denote

X(f,t)=a^{+}(f)+a^{-}(f)+a^{0}(f).

For
f\in\mathcal{B}
, denote
X(f,t)=X(f-\phi[f],t)+\phi[f]
.

The specific choice of the annihilation operator (and inner product) is justified in Remark
2
and
8
.

Notation 1
.

Denote

\Gamma^{\Phi}_{a}(\mathcal{B},\phi;t)=\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(\left\{X(f,t):f\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ}\right\},1)=\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(\left\{X(f,t):f\in(\mathcal{B}^{\circ})^{sa}\right\},1).

Denote by

\Gamma^{\Phi}_{w}(\mathcal{B},\phi;t)=W^{\ast}(\left\{X(f,t):f\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ}\right\},1)

the corresponding von Neumann algebra. The vacuum state
\Phi=\left\langle{\cdot\Omega},{\Omega}\right\rangle
is a state on each of these algebras.

Remark 2
.

Except for
\mathcal{B}^{\circ}
not necessarily being an algebra, and the linear functional not necessarily being faithful, the construction above fits into the framework of
[
AM21
]
, with the linear functional
\rho=(1+t)\phi

.

(a)

The inner product

is non-degenerate for

.

(b)

Each

in the natural sense.

(c)

\Omega
is cyclic for
\Gamma^{\Phi}_{a}(\mathcal{B},\phi;t)
.

(d)

For
n\geq 2
and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ}
, the moments with respect to
\Phi
are

(8)

\Phi[X(f_{1},t),\ldots,X(f_{n},t)]=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{NC}_{ns}(n)}(1+t)^{\left|\Outer(\pi)\right|}t^{\left|\Inner(\pi)\right|}\prod_{V\in\pi}\phi[W(V)],

are

(e)

If
\mathcal{B}
is a

-algebra, then, denoting by

the operator norm on

. Indeed, Proposition

(f)

Theorem 4

(b)

(c)

(d)

(e)

Part (a) follows by the universal property of the tensor algebra. (b) follows by comparing Definition

are bounded, and

For (d), suppose

for some

\vec{\xi}
in the highest tensor power by

. It suffices to show that this sum is zero.

\begin{split}0&=\pi_{\Phi,t}[\vec{\xi}]\Omega=\sum_{i=1}^{N}X(f_{1}^{(i)},t)\ldots X(f_{n}^{(i)},t)\Omega+\text{ terms in lower tensor components}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{1}^{(i)}\Omega\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{n}^{(i)}\Omega+\text{ terms in lower tensor components},\end{split}

and so

3.3.
Cumulants, convolutions, and independence

Proposition 4
.

Let
\left\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\right\}\subset\mathcal{B}^{\circ}
. Then

R^{\Phi_{t}}[X(f_{1}),\ldots,X(f_{n})]=(1+t)R^{\phi}[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}].

Therefore we have the relation between joint distributions

\mu^{\Phi_{t}}_{X(f_{1}),\ldots X(f_{n})}=\mu_{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}}^{\boxplus(1+t)}.

Proof.

By Proposition
3
, we have

R^{\phi}[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}]=R^{\Phi}[X(f_{1},0),\ldots,X(f_{n},0)]=R^{\Phi_{t}}[X(f_{1}),\ldots,X(f_{n})].

From Equation (
10
), we see that

R^{\Phi}[X(f_{1},t),\ldots,X(f_{n},t)]=(1+t)R^{\Phi}[X(f_{1},0),\ldots,X(f_{n},0)].

∎

Remark 3
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\mathbb{E},\mathcal{C})
be a
\mathcal{C}
-valued probability space. For every c.p. map
\eta
on
\mathcal{C}
, one can give a version of Construction
2
, with the inner product

\left\langle{f_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{n}},{g_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes g_{n}}\right\rangle_{\eta}\\
=\delta_{n=k}((1+\eta)\circ\mathbb{E})\left[g_{n}^{\ast}(\eta\circ\mathbb{E})\left[g_{n-1}^{\ast}(\eta\circ\mathbb{E})\left[\ldots(\eta\circ\mathbb{E})[g_{1}^{\ast}f_{1}]\ldots\right]f_{n-1}\right]f_{n}\right]

on
\mathcal{C}\Omega\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}(\mathcal{A}^{\circ})^{\otimes n}
(but not on
\mathcal{C}\Omega\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}(\mathcal{A}^{\circ})^{\otimes_{\mathcal{C}}n}
).
Then the corresponding forms of Proposition
3
and Corollary
1
hold with the same proof. In Proposition
4
, the formula

R[X(f_{1},\eta),\ldots,X(f_{n},\eta)]=(1+\eta)[R[X(f_{1},0),\ldots,X(f_{n},0)]].

holds as well. Note however that, according to the standard definition of
\mathcal{C}
-valued distribution, this is not sufficient to claim that the joint distribution of the variables on the left-hand side is the
(1+\eta)
’th free convolution power of the joint distribution of the variables on the right-hand side.

.

are orthogonal projections adding up to the identity, then so are

,
\ldots
,

is free multinomial in the sense of Section 4.6 of
[

]
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\left\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\right\}\subset\mathcal{B}^{\circ}

(a)

The free cumulants with respect to the state

are

\begin{split}R^{\Psi_{t}}[X(f_{1}),\ldots,X(f_{n})]&=tB^{\phi}[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}].\end{split}

\mu^{\Psi_{t}}_{X(f_{1}),\ldots X(f_{n})}=\mathbb{B}_{t}(\mu_{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}})^{\uplus t}=\left(\mu^{\Phi_{t}}_{X(f_{1}),\ldots X(f_{n})}\right)^{\uplus\frac{t}{1+t}},

where

is the Belinschi-Nica transformation.

(b)

The conditionally free cumulants with respect to the pair

are

R^{({\Phi_{t}},{\Psi_{t}})}[X(f_{1}),\ldots,X(f_{n})]=(1+t)B^{\phi}[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}].

Proof.

For (a), combine equation (

) with Corollary

]

\begin{split}B^{\Psi_{t}}[X(f_{1}),\ldots,X(f_{n})]&=\sum_{\pi\in\widetilde{NC}(n)}R^{\Psi_{t}}_{\pi}[X(f_{1}),\ldots,X(f_{n})]\\

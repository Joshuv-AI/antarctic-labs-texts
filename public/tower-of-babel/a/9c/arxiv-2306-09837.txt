Abstract
. We study families of analytic semigroups, acting in a Banach space, and depending on a parameter, and give sufficient conditions for existence of uniform with respect to the parameter norm bounds using spectral properties of the respective semigroup generators. In particular, we use estimates of the resolvent operators of the generators along vertical segments to estimate the growth/decay rate of the norm for the family of analytic semigroups. These results are applied to prove the Lyapunov linear stability of planar traveling waves of systems of reaction-diffusion equations, and the bidomain equation, important in electrophysiology.

Introduction

The problem of finding optimal bounds for the norm of a semigroup of linear operators is very important and well-studied in the asymptotic theory of
semigroups,
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
15
,
17
]
. Of particular importance are characterizations that relate the growth/decay bounds to the spectral properties of the generator of the semigroup. We mention here the celebrated Gearhard-Prüss Theorem,
[
7
,
18
]
, and refer to
[
2
, Section 5.7]
for further references or to
[
9
,
10
,
14
]
for more recent results.

For families of semigroups depending on a parameter, the problem of finding estimates for their norms that are
uniform
with respect to the parameter is significantly less studied.
In this paper we aim to find sufficient conditions that guarantee uniform with respect to the parameter exponential decay estimates for families of analytic semigroups in Banach spaces.
These results are important in the study of Lyapunov linearized stability of traveling waves in systems of partial differential equations. In many instances, such as parabolic systems of partial differential equations, the linearization along the wave is a
sectorial
operator, hence it generates an
analytic semigroup
. In the case when the linearization along a planar wave is a differential operator in a multidimensional domain, the generator of the semigroup is typically similar to a multiplication operator by an operator valued function of certain parameter, the dual variable. The Lyapunov linearized stability problem is thus equivalent to the existence of
uniform
with respect to the parameter bounds for a family of analytic semigroups. We present two specific cases, arising in the theory of reaction-diffusion systems and the bidomain equation, illustrating these ideas in Section
4
and Section
5
.

It is well-known that for any strongly continuous semigroup of linear operators
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
on a Banach space
{\mathbb{X}}
there exist two constants
L\geq 1
and
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(1.1)

\|T(t)\|\leq Le^{\omega t}\;\mbox{for any}\;t\geq 0.

We recall that the infimum (which might not be the minimum) of all
\omega
for which there exists a constant
L\geq 1
such that (1.1) holds is called the semigroup
growth bound
, and is denoted
\omega_{0}(T)
. Moreover, in the case of an analytic semigroup one has
\omega_{0}(T)={\mathrm{s}}(A):=\sup\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma(A)
, where
{\mathrm{s}}(A)
is the
spectral bound
and
\sigma(A)
is the spectrum of the generator
A
of the semigroup
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
. We stress that it is much harder to find a direct formula, or even an estimate, for the constant
L
in (
1.1
), unless one imposes additional conditions on the analytic semigroup
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
or its generator. A classical example of such condition reads as follows: If
A
is the generator of an analytic semigroup
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
A-\omega I_{\mathbb{X}}
is dissipative, then (
1.1
) holds for
L=1
, cf., e.g.,
[
2
, Proposition 3.7.16]
.

In the case of
families
of semigroups whose generators depend on a parameter
\alpha
, real or complex, the constants
L
and
\omega
from (
1.1
) might depend on
\alpha
as well. By studying the spectrum of the generator and the properties of its resolvent, one can find a convenient growth rate. However, even if we use more advanced results, such as the
celebrated Gearhard-Prüss theorem (
[
7
,
18
]
) or later results of Helfer-Sjöstrand (
[
9
,
10
]
), the constant
L=L(\alpha)
is quite often such that
\sup_{\alpha}L(\alpha)=\infty
.

Usually, one aims at finding the best possible decay rate or the smallest growth rate, that is, the smallest
\omega
in (
1.1
), may be at the expanse of making the constant
L
large. However, in this paper, studying exponential decay and stability of a family of semigroups depending on a parameter, we are not necessarily interested in the most optimal decay rate, but rather just in the order of magnitude of the decay rate when the parameter changes. In this context we are willing to give up some decay to get a constant in front of the exponential term which is uniform with respect to the parameters in the system. In a sense, this philosophy is opposite to what has been used in many papers, for example, in
[
9
,
10
,
14
]
.

In the current paper we study uniform stability of a family of semigroups of operators, whose generators are bounded perturbations of a sectorial operator. We start with assumptions on the operator.

Hypothesis (H1).

We assume that
A:\rm{dom}(A)\subseteq{\mathbb{X}}\to{\mathbb{X}}
is a
sectorial
linear operator, that is, there exists
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
and
M_{0}>0
such that

(1.2)

\Omega_{a,\theta}:=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\lambda\neq a,|\mathrm{arg}(\lambda)-a|<\theta\}\subseteq\rho(A),\;\|R(\lambda,A)\|\leq\frac{M_{0}}{|\lambda-a|}\;\mbox{for any}\;\lambda\in\Omega_{a,\theta}.

We recall that there are several concepts of sectorial operators in the literature. In the current paper we use the spectral conditions given above in (
1.2
). See Remark
2.2
for a more detailed discussion.

It is well-known that if
A
satisfies Hypothesis (H1) then it generates an analytic semigroup that can be evaluated using contour integration along a counterclockwise oriented path surrounding the spectrum of
A
. These formulas allow us to find estimates of the type (
1.1
) and find explicit formulas not only for
\omega
, but also for
L
.
We revisit several results known in the literature with explicit aim of controlling the constant
L
, which are very useful in the sequel, see Lemma
2.4
and Remark
2.5
. Next, we add to Hypothesis (H1) some more assumptions on
A
.

Hypothesis (H2).
We assume the following conditions on the spectrum of the operator
A
:

(i)

\sup\,\mathrm{Re}\big(\sigma(A)\setminus\{0\}\big)\leq-\nu
for some
\nu>0
;

(ii)

0
is a
semisimple eigenvalue
of finite multiplicity of
A
.

If the linear operator
A
is obtained as the linearization of a PDE along a traveling wave, then Hypothesis (H2) is equivalent to what is sometimes called
conditional exponential stability
of the wave. This situation is very common, see examples from Section
4
and Section
5
.

Next, we consider a family of operators
A_{\alpha}:=A+E(\alpha)
,
\alpha\geq 0
, where the perturbation
E:[0,\infty)\to\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}})
is such that
E(0)=0
. We denote by
\{T_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
the semigroup of linear operators generated by
A_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\geq 0
. We assume the following conditions on the perturbed family of operators.

Hypothesis (H3).
The function
E:[0,\infty)\to\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}})
is continuous in the operator norm topology. Moreover, there exists an increasing function
q:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that

(i)

There exists
M_{1}>0
independent of
\alpha
such that
\sigma(A_{\alpha})\subset\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{Re}\lambda\leq-q(\alpha)\}
and

(1.3)

\|R(\lambda,A_{\alpha})\|\leq\frac{M_{1}}{\mathrm{Re}\lambda+q(\alpha)}\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>-q(\alpha),\;\alpha\geq 0;

(ii)

q(\alpha)=q_{1}\alpha
for any
\alpha\in[0,q_{2}]
, for some
q_{1},q_{2}>0
;

(iii)

\ell:=\limsup\limits_{\alpha\to\infty}\frac{\|E(\alpha)\|}{q(\alpha)}<\infty
.

We note that the estimate (
1.3
) is necessary for the uniform in
\alpha
exponential stability of the family of semigroups generated by
A_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\geq 0
. In Section
4
and Section
5
we present examples of families of operators that satisfy Hypothesis (H3). Moreover, a condition on the spectrum of the perturbed operator
A_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\geq 0
, is needed to achieve uniform in
\alpha
exponential stability of the semigroups
\{T_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
,
\alpha\geq 0
. Indeed, in general the spectrum of the perturbed operator
A_{\alpha}
might not be stable, even if the space
{\mathbb{X}}
is finite dimensional and
E(\alpha)=\alpha W_{0}
,
\alpha\geq 0
, where
W_{0}
is a constant, bounded, self-adjoint, uniformly negative definite linear operator. See Appendix
A
for simple counterexamples.

Assuming Hypotheses (H1)-(H3) we show that the family of operators
A_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\geq 0
, is uniformly sectorial, that is
A_{\alpha}
satisfies (
1.2
) and all the relevant constants can be chosen independently on
\alpha
. Using this crucial result, we can prove that the family of semigroups generated by
A_{\alpha}
is exponentially stable uniformly for
\alpha\geq\delta
, for any
\delta>0
. To prove the uniform in
\alpha
exponential estimate for the norm of the semigroup
\{T_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
for
\alpha
in a neighborhood of
0
, we first find a spectral decomposition of the space
{\mathbb{X}}
into two subspaces invariant under
A_{\alpha}
. In this decomposition one spectral subspace is such that the spectrum of the restriction
A_{\alpha}
is uniformly bounded away from the imaginary axis, and the other subspace is finite dimensional. To construct such a decomposition we use a transformation operator borrowed from
the classical work of Kato
[
13
, Chapter II, Section 4.2]
and Daletskii–Krein,
[
5
, Chapter 4, Section 1]
. To ensure that the spectral and semigroup estimates are uniformly bounded in
\alpha
, we assume the following.

Hypothesis (H4).
The function
E_{0}:(0,\infty)\to\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}})
defined by
E_{0}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\alpha}E_{0}(\alpha)
has the property

(1.4)

\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1]}\|E_{0}(\alpha)\|<\infty.

This hypothesis guarantees that there exists a positive number
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
independent of

\alpha
, such that the circle of radius
\frac{\nu}{4}
centered at the origin is contained in the resolvent set
\rho(A_{\alpha})
, for any
\alpha\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, where
\nu>0
was introduced in Hypothesis (H2) (see Lemma
3.6
for more details). This result is essential in finding the desired spectral decomposition of the space
{\mathbb{X}}
. Once this step is achieved, we use contour integration and the uniform sectorial property of the family of generators
A_{\alpha}
to prove the uniform in
\alpha
exponential stability of the restriction of the semigroup generated by
A_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, to the subspace where its spectrum is bounded away from the imaginary axis uniformly in
\alpha
.

Next, we turn our attention to the restriction of
A_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, to the finite dimensional subspace where all of its eigenvalues are of order
\mathscr{O}(\alpha)
. The most important step here is to find an expansion of this restriction near
\alpha=0
, which is so that all the eigenvalues of the leading order term are with negative real part. Moreover, the remainder of the leading order term is of order
o(\alpha)
and bounded uniformly in
\alpha
in a neighborhood of
0
. To be able to obtain such an expansion we need the following additional smoothness assumption on the perturbation.

Hypothesis (H5)
.
There exists
r:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, a continuous, increasing function such that
r(0)=0
,
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}r(\alpha)=\infty
, and the operator valued function
E_{0}
defined in (
1.4
) can be extended continuously at
0
and satisfies the inequality

(1.5)

\|E_{0}(\alpha)-E_{0}(0)\|\leq r(\alpha)\quad\mbox{for any}\quad\alpha\geq 0.

We note that Hypotheses (H4) and (H5) are automatically satisfied if the perturbation
E
is analytic. The first main result of the paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume Hypotheses (H1)-(H5). Then, for an effectively computed in equation (
3.100
) below function
M:(0,1)\to(0,\infty)
, independent of the perturbation variable
\alpha\geq 0
, the following estimate holds,

(1.6)

\|T_{\alpha}(t)\|\leq M(\varkappa)e^{-\varkappa q(\alpha)t}\;\mbox{for any}\;t\geq 0,\alpha\geq 0,\varkappa\in(0,1).

The function
M
depends on the unperturbed operator
A
,
E_{0}(0)
and the functions
\|E(\cdot)\|
,
q
,
r
and related constants introduced in Hypotheses (H3)-(H5).

In part, assumption (H3)(i) yields that
\omega_{0}(T_{\alpha})={\mathrm{s}}(A_{\alpha})\leq-q(\alpha)
as the semigroup
\{T_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is analytic for any
\alpha\geq 0
. Of course, we cannot replace the decay rate
-\varkappa q(\alpha)
,
\varkappa\in(0,1)
, in (
1.6
) by
\omega_{0}(T_{\alpha})
, and thus by
-q(\alpha)
, unless additional hypotheses are imposed. In many applications, however, we are mostly interested merely in the order of the decay rate, and (
1.6
) captures this feature.

Next, we turn our attention to the special case when
0
is a
simple
eigenvalue of the unperturbed operator
A
. The conclusion of Theorem
1.1
can be obtained in this case without assuming Hypothesis (H5). Arguing in the same way as in the general case, we first establish an exponential estimate similar to (
1.6
) for
\alpha
away from
0
. In addition, when
\alpha
is close to
0
we can decompose the space
{\mathbb{X}}
in the sum of two invariant for
A_{\alpha}
subspaces: One is such that the spectrum of the restriction of
A_{\alpha}
to the subspace is away from the imaginary axis, and another is a one dimensional subspace. A modification of Hypothesis (H2) is imposed next.

Hypothesis (H2’).
We assume the following conditions on the spectrum of the operator
A
:

(i)

\sup\,\mathrm{Re}\big(\sigma(A)\setminus\{0\}\big)\leq-\nu
for some
\nu>0
;

(ii)

0
is a
simple eigenvalue
of
A
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume Hypotheses (H1) (H2’), (H3) and (H4). Then, for an effectively computed in equation (
3.107
) below function
N:(0,1)\to(0,\infty)
, independent of the perturbation variable
\alpha\geq 0
, the following estimate holds,

(1.7)

\|T_{\alpha}(t)\|\leq N(\varkappa)e^{-\varkappa q(\alpha)t}\;\mbox{for any}\;t\geq 0,\alpha\geq 0,\varkappa\in(0,1).

The function
N
depends on the unperturbed operator
A
and the functions
\|E(\cdot)\|
,
q
and related constants introduced in Hypothesis (H3).

Next, we present applications of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
to Lyapunov linear stability of planar traveling waves in reaction-diffusion systems and the bidomain equation. The two models are known to exhibit planar traveling waves. The linearization of each of them along the wave (in the moving frame variables) is similar via the Fourier Transform to a multiplication operator by an operator valued function of a certain parameter (the dual variable). Thus, the Lyapunov linear stability of a planar traveling wave can be obtained by studying the stability of a family of analytic semigroups which is uniform with respect to the parameter.

The reaction-diffusion system

(1.8)

u_{t}=D\Delta_{x}u+F(u),\;t\geq 0,\;x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})^{\mathrm{T}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m},\;u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k},

has exponentially localized planar traveling wave solutions, that is, solutions of the form
u(x,t)=\overline{h}(x_{1}-ct)
, under appropriate conditions on the matrix
D\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k\times k}
and the vector valued function
F:{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
. Considering the equation in the moving frame variable
y=x-ct\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{1}}}
, with
\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{1}}}=(1,0,\dots,0)^{\mathrm{T}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, the linearization along the planar wave
\overline{h}
is given by
{\mathscr{L}}=D\Delta_{y}+cI_{k}\partial_{y_{1}}+{\mathscr{M}}_{F^{\prime}(\overline{h})}
, considered as a densely defined linear operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})
. Here
{\mathscr{M}}_{F^{\prime}(\overline{h})}
denotes the operator of multiplication on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})
by the bounded, matrix valued function
F^{\prime}(\overline{h}(y_{1}))
. Taking Fourier Transform in the variables
(y_{2},\dots,y_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m-1}
, the linear operator
{\mathscr{L}}
is unitary equivalent to
{\mathscr{M}}_{\widehat{L}}
the operator of multiplication on
L^{2}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{m-1},L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})\big)
by the operator valued function

\widehat{L}:{\mathbb{R}}^{m-1}\to\mathscr{B}\big(H^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k}),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})\big)
, defined by
\widehat{L}(\xi)=D\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}+cI_{k}\partial_{y_{1}}+{\mathscr{M}}_{V(\cdot,\xi)}
, where
V(y_{1},\xi)=F^{\prime}(\overline{h}(y_{1}))-|\xi|^{2}D
. We refer to Section
4
for a more detailed discussion on this topic. Next, we assume the following hypothesis.

Hypothesis (RD).
The spectrum of
\widehat{L}(0)=D\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}+cI_{k}\partial_{y_{1}}+{\mathscr{M}}_{F^{\prime}(\overline{h}(\cdot))}
, the linearization along the one-dimensional problem, satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

\sup\,\mathrm{Re}\big(\sigma(\widehat{L}(0))\setminus\{0\}\big)\leq-\nu
for some
\nu>0
;

(ii)

0
is a
semisimple eigenvalue
of finite multiplicity of
\widehat{L}(0)
.

We are now ready to formulate our Lyapunov stability result for the case of reaction-diffusion equations where the diffusion rates of various components of
u
in (
1.8
) are close to each other.

Proposition 1.3
.

Assume Hypothesis (RD) and that the diffusion matrix
D
is sufficiently close to
dI_{k}
, for some
d>0
, in the sense described in equation (
4.19
) below. Then the family of semigroups generated by
\widehat{L}(\xi)
is stable uniformly with respect to
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m-1}
. In particular, the front
\overline{h}
is Lyapunov linearly stable.

Next, we briefly recall the bidomain model in electrophysiology studied by Matano and Mori, see, e.g.,
[
16
]
,

(1.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\nabla_{x}\cdot(A_{i}\nabla_{x}u_{i})+f(u),\\
\nabla_{x}\cdot(A_{i}\nabla_{x}u_{i}+A_{e}\nabla_{x}u_{e})=0,\\
u=u_{i}-u_{e},\end{array}\right.t\geq 0,\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

Here, the scalar functions
u_{i}
and
u_{e}
represent the intracellular and extracellular voltages,
A_{i},A_{e}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2\times 2}
are symmetric, positive definite matrices. Typically one has

(1.10)

A_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}1+\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}&0\\
0&1+\nu_{2}-\nu_{1}\end{bmatrix},\quad A_{e}=\begin{bmatrix}1-\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}&0\\
0&1-\nu_{2}+\nu_{1}\end{bmatrix},\;\mbox{with}\;|\nu_{1}\pm\nu_{2}|<1.

The function
f
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{3}
and
of bistable type, for example
0
and
1
are two stable zeros of
f
, and there exists a unique unstable zero of
f
in the interval
(0,1)
. For a very detailed discussion regarding the applications and importance of the bidomain Allen-Cahn model to cardiac electrophysiology we refer to
[
16
]
and the references therein.

The bidomain model (
1.9
) has planar wave solutions of the form

(u,u_{i},u_{e})(x,t)=(\overline{w},\overline{w}_{i},\overline{w}_{e})(x_{1}\cos\gamma+x_{2}\sin\gamma-ct),\;x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},t\geq 0,c,\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Next, we pass to the moving frame coordinate system
(y_{1},y_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, where the new axes are chosen such that the wave travels in the direction of
y_{1}
. Moreover, we note that last two equations of the system (
1.9
) are linear. Linearizing the system along the traveling wave solution and eliminating the variables
u_{i}
and
u_{e}
, we obtain that the linearization is given by

{\mathscr{A}}=-{\mathscr{L}}_{\gamma}+c\partial_{y_{1}}+{\mathscr{M}}_{f^{\prime}(\overline{w})}
. The linear operator
{\mathscr{L}}_{\gamma}:H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
is given as the Fourier multiplier
{\mathscr{L}}_{\gamma}={\mathscr{F}}^{-1}{\mathscr{M}}_{Q_{\gamma}}{\mathscr{F}}
. The function
Q_{\gamma}
is a rational function whose coefficients depend on
\nu_{1}
,
\nu_{2}
and
\gamma
only. Moreover, it can be represented as follows:

(1.11)

Q_{\gamma}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\xi_{2}^{2}\Big(p\Big(\frac{\xi_{1}}{\xi_{2}}\Big)+g\Big(\frac{\xi_{1}}{\xi_{2}}\Big)\Big),&\xi_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}},\,\xi_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\},\\
N_{0}^{2}\xi_{1}^{2},&\xi_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}},\,\xi_{2}=0,\end{array}\right.\;\mbox{where},

(1.12)

p(s)=N_{0}^{2}(s-\eta_{1})^{2}+\eta_{0},\quad g(s)=\frac{\beta_{1}s+\beta_{0}}{s^{2}+1}.

The constants
N_{0}
,
\beta_{0}
,
\beta_{1}
,
\eta_{0}
and
\eta_{1}
depend on
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
and
\gamma
only. Taking Fourier Transform
{\mathscr{F}}_{2}
with respect to
y_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we note that the linear operator
{\mathscr{A}}
is unitary equivalent to
{\mathscr{M}}_{\widehat{A}}
the operator of multiplication on
L^{2}\big({\mathbb{R}},L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big)
by the operator valued function

\widehat{A}:{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathscr{B}\big(H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big)
,
\widehat{A}(\xi_{2})=-{\mathscr{F}}_{1}^{-1}{\mathscr{M}}_{Q_{\gamma}(\cdot,\xi_{2})}{\mathscr{F}}_{1}+c\partial_{y_{1}}+{\mathscr{M}}_{f^{\prime}(\overline{w})}
.
Here
{\mathscr{F}}_{1}
denotes the Fourier transform with respect to the variable
y_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
For more details we refer to Section
5
and
[
16
]
.

From (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) it follows that
\widehat{A}(0)
is a second order differential operator and there exists
M_{\mathrm{b}}>0
such that

(1.13)

\big\|R\big(\lambda,\widehat{A}(0)\big)\big\|\leq\frac{M_{\mathrm{b}}}{|\lambda|}\;\mbox{whenever}\;\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\geq 0,\lambda\neq 0.

We recall the following result from
[
16
, Corollary 3.3]
:

(1.14)

\overline{w}\;\,\mbox{is spectrally stable provided that}\;\,\eta_{0}>M_{\mathrm{b}}g_{\Delta}-\overline{g}.

Here
g_{\mathrm{inf}}=\inf_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}g(s)
,
g_{\mathrm{sup}}=\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}g(s)
,
\overline{g}=\frac{g_{\mathrm{inf}}+g_{\mathrm{sup}}}{2}
,
g_{\Delta}=\frac{g_{\mathrm{sup}}-g_{\mathrm{inf}}}{2}
, while
\eta_{0}
was introduced in (
1.12
) and
M_{\mathrm{b}}
was introduced in (
1.13
). In
[
16
]
the authors show that (
1.14
) is met for certain values of the parameters. In this paper we prove that the sufficient condition of (
1.14
) guarantees that the planar front
\overline{w}
is Lyapunov linearly stable.

Proposition 1.4
.

Assume that
\eta_{0}>M_{\mathrm{b}}g_{\Delta}-\overline{g}
. Then, the family of semigroups generated by
\widehat{A}(\xi_{2})
is stable uniformly with respect to
\xi_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. In particular, the front
\overline{w}
is Lyapunov linearly stable.

A glossary of notation.

L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{X}})
,
p\geq 1

{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with values in a Banach space
{\mathbb{X}}
, associated with the Lebesgue measure
{\mathrm{d}}x
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{X}})
,
s>0
, is the usual Sobolev space of
{\mathbb{X}}
valued functions. The open disc in
{\mathbb{C}}
centered at
a
of radius
\varepsilon>0
is denoted by
D(a,\varepsilon)
.
The identity operator on a Banach space
{\mathbb{X}}
is denoted by
I_{\mathbb{X}}
. The set of bounded linear operators from a Banach space
{\mathbb{X}}
to itself is denoted by
{\mathscr{B}}({\mathbb{X}})
.
For an operator
B
on a Banach space
{\mathbb{X}}
, we use
\text{\rm{dom}}(B)
,
\ker B
,
{\rm im}B
,
\sigma(B)
, and
B_{|{\mathbb{W}}}
to denote the domain, kernel, range, spectrum, adjoint and the restriction of
B
to a subspace
{\mathbb{W}}
of
{\mathbb{X}}
. In the case when the space is a Hilbert space
B^{*}
denotes the adjoint operator. We denote by
\sigma_{\mathrm{disc}}(B)
the set of isolated eigenvalues of finite algebraic multiplicity of the linear operator
B
, and by
\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(B)
its complement in the spectrum of
B
. The direct sum of two subspaces
{\mathbb{W}}_{1}
and
{\mathbb{W}}_{2}
is denoted by
{\mathbb{W}}_{1}\oplus{\mathbb{W}}_{2}
. The operator of multiplication by a function

is denoted by
{\mathscr{M}}_{g}
. We use
\omega_{0}(T)

to denote the growth bound of a semigroup

.

2.
Norm estimates of semigroups generated by sectorial operators

In this section we assume that
{\mathbb{X}}
is a Banach space and
A:\rm{dom}(A)\subseteq{\mathbb{X}}\to{\mathbb{X}}
is a sectorial operator generating an analytic semigroup of linear operators denoted
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
. There are various concepts of sectorial operators relevant to our setup that we are going to briefly discuss below.

First, we recall that for any
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
we defined the sector with vertex at
a
of angle
\theta
the set

(2.1)

\Omega_{a,\theta}=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\lambda\neq a,|\mathrm{arg}(\lambda)-a|<\theta\}.

One can readily check that

(2.2)

{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega_{a,\theta}=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{Re}\lambda\leq a,|\mathrm{Im}\lambda|\leq(\mathrm{Re}\lambda-a)\tan\theta\}.

In the literature on semigroup of linear operators (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
15
,
17
]
) the definition of a sectorial operator is given using its spectral properties, see (
1.2
). In the case of linear operators on a Hilbert space, in particular differential operators, several classical texts (
[
20
]
) define sectorial operators using the numerical range. We recall that if
{\mathbb{H}}
is a Hilbert space and
B:\text{\rm{dom}}(B)\subseteq{\mathbb{H}}\to{\mathbb{H}}
is a closed, densely defined linear operator, the numerical range of
B
is defined by

(2.3)

W(B)=\{\langle Bh,h\rangle:h\in\text{\rm{dom}}(B),\|h\|=1\}.

Definition 2.1
.

The operator
B:\text{\rm{dom}}(B)\subseteq{\mathbb{H}}\to{\mathbb{H}}
is said to be
numerical range sectorial
if there exists
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
such that
W(B)\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega_{a,\theta}
.

Remark 2.2
.

From
[
3
, Theorem 1.4]
one can readily check that any
numerical range sectorial
operator is
sectorial
, but not vice versa. Indeed, if
W(B)\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega_{a,\theta}
for some
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
, since
\Omega_{a,\theta}
is open we have
\sigma(B)\subseteq\overline{W(B)}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega_{a,\theta}
. In addition,

(2.4)

\|R(\lambda,B)\|\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{dist}(\lambda,S_{a,\theta})}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{dist}(\lambda-a,S_{0,\theta})}.

Another simple computation shows that

(2.5)

{\rm dist}(z,S_{0,\theta})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|z|,&|{\rm arg}z|<\theta-\frac{\pi}{2},\\
|z|\sin{(\theta-|{\rm arg}z|)},&\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}\leq|{\rm arg}z|<\theta,\end{array}\right.

for any
z\in\Omega_{0,\theta}
. From (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) we infer that

(2.6)

\|R(\lambda,B)\|\leq\frac{\csc{(\theta-\varphi)}}{|\lambda-a|}\;\mbox{for any}\;\lambda\in\Omega_{a,\varphi}\;\mbox{and any}\;\varphi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\theta),

and so
B
is sectorial.

It is well-known that if the linear operator
A:\text{\rm{dom}}(A)\subseteq{\mathbb{X}}\to{\mathbb{X}}
satisfies Hypothesis (H1) then
\|T(t)\|\leq Le^{at}
for any
t\geq 0
, for some
L>0
. Our first task is to revisit the proof of this result and for each
\varphi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\theta)
find a constant
L=L(\varphi)
, not necessarily optimal, satisfying the estimate that depends only on
\varphi
and
M_{0}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume Hypothesis (H1). Then,

(2.7)

\|T(t)\|\leq\frac{M_{0}(e\varphi-\sec\varphi)}{\pi}e^{at}\;\mbox{for any}\;t\geq 0\;\mbox{and any}\;\varphi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\theta).

Proof.

We fix
\varphi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\theta)
,
t>0
and
\varepsilon>0
. We introduce the curves in the complex plane given by

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\pm}_{a,\varphi,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{arg}(\lambda-a)=\pm\varphi,|\lambda-a|\geq\varepsilon\}=\{a+se^{\pm{\mathrm{i}}\varphi}:s\geq\varepsilon\}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_{a,\varphi,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:|\mathrm{arg}(\lambda-a)|\leq\varphi,|\lambda-a|=\varepsilon\}=\{a+\varepsilon e^{{\mathrm{i}}\zeta}:-\varphi\leq\zeta\leq\varphi\}.

The path
\Lambda_{a,\varphi,\varepsilon}
is defined as the union
\Lambda^{-}_{a,\varphi,\varepsilon}\cup\Lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_{a,\varphi,\varepsilon}\cup\Lambda^{+}_{a,\varphi,\varepsilon}
oriented counterclockwise. Since
\Lambda_{a,\varphi,\frac{\varepsilon}{t}}\subseteq\Omega_{a,\theta}
for any
t>0
and
\varepsilon>0
we have (see
[
15
, Chapter 2]
)

(2.9)

T(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\int_{\Lambda_{a,\varphi,\frac{\varepsilon}{t}}}e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A){\mathrm{d}}\lambda\;\mbox{for any}\;t>0,\varepsilon>0.

Next, we estimate the contour integrals above using (
1.2
). Changing variables we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda^{\pm}_{a,\varphi,\frac{\varepsilon}{t}}}e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A){\mathrm{d}}\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{\varepsilon}{t}}^{\infty}e^{(a+se^{\pm{\mathrm{i}}\varphi})t}R(a+se^{\pm{\mathrm{i}}\varphi},A)\,e^{\pm{\mathrm{i}}\varphi}{\mathrm{d}}s

(2.10)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{at\pm{\mathrm{i}}\varphi}}{t}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}e^{\xi e^{\pm{\mathrm{i}}\varphi}}R\big(a+\frac{\xi}{t}e^{\pm{\mathrm{i}}\varphi},A\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi.

Since
\Lambda^{\pm}_{a,\varphi,\frac{\varepsilon}{t}}\subseteq\Omega_{a,\theta}
for any
t>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, from (
1.2
) and (
2
) we obtain that

(2.11)

\Big\|\int_{\Lambda^{\pm}_{a,\varphi,\frac{\varepsilon}{t}}}e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A){\mathrm{d}}\lambda\Big\|\leq\frac{e^{at}}{t}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}e^{\xi\cos\varphi}\frac{M_{0}}{|\frac{\xi}{t}e^{\pm{\mathrm{i}}\varphi}|}{\mathrm{d}}\xi=M_{0}e^{at}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{e^{\xi\cos\varphi}}{\xi}{\mathrm{d}}\xi.

Similarly, since
\Lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_{a,\varphi,\frac{\varepsilon}{t}}\subseteq\Omega_{a,\theta}
for any
t>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, one can readily check that

(2.12)

\Big\|\int_{\Lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_{a,\varphi,\frac{\varepsilon}{t}}}e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A){\mathrm{d}}\lambda\Big\|=\Big\|\frac{\varepsilon{\mathrm{i}}e^{at}}{t}\int_{-\varphi}^{\varphi}e^{\varepsilon e^{{\mathrm{i}}\zeta}}R(a+\frac{\varepsilon}{t}e^{{\mathrm{i}}\zeta},A){\mathrm{d}}\zeta\Big\|\leq M_{0}e^{at}\int_{-\varphi}^{\varphi}e^{\varepsilon\cos\zeta}{\mathrm{d}}\zeta.

From (
2.9
), (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) we conclude that

(2.13)

\|T(t)\|\leq M_{0}C_{0}(\varphi)e^{at}\;\mbox{for any}\;t\geq 0,\;\mbox{where}\;C_{0}(\varphi):=\frac{1}{\pi}\inf_{\varepsilon>0}\Big(\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{e^{\xi\cos\varphi}}{\xi}{\mathrm{d}}\xi+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\varphi}^{\varphi}e^{\varepsilon\cos\zeta}{\mathrm{d}}\zeta\Big).

Finally, we note that

(2.14)

C_{0}(\varphi)\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\Big(\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{\xi\cos\varphi}}{\xi}{\mathrm{d}}\xi+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\varphi}^{\varphi}e^{\cos\zeta}{\mathrm{d}}\zeta\Big)\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\Big(\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{\xi\cos\varphi}{\mathrm{d}}\xi+e\varphi\Big)=\frac{e\varphi-\sec\varphi}{\pi},

proving the lemma.
∎

Next, we discuss how to improve estimate (
2.7
), provided that the sectorial semigroup generator
A
is such that
\sup\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma(A)<a
, where
a
is the vertex of the sector. Such an estimate is important since for many second order elliptic differential operators one can immediately prove they are sectorial using Gårding inequality, but typically the vertex is positive. To formulate our result we introduce the operator-valued function

(2.15)

{\mathscr{V}}_{A}:(0,\infty)\times(\mathrm{s}(A),\infty)\times(\frac{\pi}{2},\theta)\to\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}}),\;{\mathscr{V}}_{A}(t,\mu,\varphi)=\int_{-(a-\mu)|\tan\varphi|}^{(a-\mu)|\tan\varphi|}e^{{\mathrm{i}}st}R(\mu+{\mathrm{i}}s,A){\mathrm{d}}s.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume Hypothesis (H1) and that the vertex
a
from (
1.2
) is such that
a>{\mathrm{s}}(A)=\sup\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma(A)
. Then,

(2.16)

\|T(t)\|\leq\frac{M_{0}e^{\mu t}}{\pi(a-\mu)t}+\frac{e^{\mu t}}{2\pi}\sup_{t>0}\|{\mathscr{V}}_{A}(t,\mu,\varphi)\|\quad\mbox{for any}\quad t>0,\mu\in\big(\mathrm{s}(A),a\big),\varphi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\theta).

Proof.

The proof uses the typical contour integral representation for sectorial operators. First we fix
\mu>s(A)
and
\varphi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\theta)
and set
b=(a-\mu)|\tan\varphi|
and
c=(a-\mu)|\sec\varphi|
. Next, we introduce the curves in the complex plane given by

(2.17)

\Gamma^{\pm}_{a,\mu,\varphi}=\{a+se^{\pm{\mathrm{i}}\varphi}:s\geq c\},\quad\Gamma^{\mathrm{c}}_{a,\mu,\varphi}=\{\mu+{\mathrm{i}}s:-b\leq s\leq b\}.

The path
\Gamma_{a,\varphi,\varepsilon}
is defined as the union
\Gamma^{-}_{a,\varphi,\varepsilon}\cup\,\Gamma^{\mathrm{c}}_{a,\varphi,\varepsilon}\cup\,\Gamma^{+}_{a,\varphi,\varepsilon}
oriented counterclockwise.

Figure 1. A plot of the spectrum of
A
, the sector
\Omega_{a,\theta}
and the path
\Gamma_{a,\mu,\varphi}
in the case when the vertex
a
is positive.

We note that this path is contained in the resolvent set of
A
and surrounds, the spectrum of
A
counterclockwise. It follows that (see, e.g.,
[
15
, Chapter 2]
)

(2.18)

T(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\int_{\Gamma_{a,\mu,\varphi}}e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A){\mathrm{d}}\lambda\;\mbox{for any}\;t>0.

Since
\Gamma^{\mathrm{c}}_{a,\mu,\varphi}\subseteq\Omega_{a,\theta}
,
a=\mu-c\cos\varphi
from (
1.2
) and (
2.17
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{\Gamma^{\pm}_{a,\mu,\varphi}}e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A){\mathrm{d}}\lambda\Big\|

\displaystyle=\Big\|\int_{c}^{\infty}e^{(a+se^{\pm{\mathrm{i}}\varphi})t}R(a+se^{\pm{\mathrm{i}}\varphi},A)\,e^{\pm{\mathrm{i}}\varphi}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big\|\leq\int_{c}^{\infty}e^{(a+s\cos\varphi)t}\,\frac{M_{0}}{s}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle=\int_{c}^{\infty}e^{(\mu+(s-c)\cos\varphi)t}\,\frac{M_{0}}{s}{\mathrm{d}}s=M_{0}e^{\mu t}\int_{c}^{\infty}\frac{e^{(s-c)\cos\varphi t}}{s}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle=M_{0}e^{\mu t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\xi t}|\sec\varphi|}{c+|\sec\varphi|\xi}{\mathrm{d}}\xi=M_{0}e^{\mu t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\xi t}}{a-\mu+\xi}{\mathrm{d}}\xi

(2.19)

\displaystyle\leq M_{0}e^{\mu t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\xi t}}{a-\mu}{\mathrm{d}}\xi=\frac{M_{0}e^{\mu t}}{(a-\mu)t}.

Unlike
\Lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_{a,\varphi,\varepsilon}
used in Lemma
2.3
above,
\Gamma^{\mathrm{c}}_{a,\mu,\varphi}
is not contained in the sector
\Omega_{a,\theta}
. To finish the proof of the lemma, we note that

(2.20)

\Big\|\int_{\Gamma^{\mathrm{c}}_{a,\mu,\varphi}}e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A){\mathrm{d}}\lambda\Big\|=\Big\|{\mathrm{i}}e^{\mu t}\int_{-b}^{b}e^{{\mathrm{i}}st}R(\mu+{\mathrm{i}}s,A){\mathrm{d}}s\Big\|\leq e^{\mu t}\sup_{t>0}\|{\mathscr{V}}_{A}(t,\mu,\varphi)\|.

The estimate (
2.16
) follows shortly from (
2.18
), (
2
) and (
2.20
).
∎

We note that surprisingly the estimate (
2.16
) is more useful in the case when
a>0
. The main reason is that if
a>0
and
\mu=-\varkappa q(\alpha)
, with the function
q
as in Hypothesis (H3) and
\varkappa\in(0,1)
, then the constant
\frac{M_{0}}{\pi(a-\mu)}
from (
2.16
) is uniformly bounded by
\frac{M_{0}}{\pi a}
. If
a=0
and
\mu=-\varkappa q(\alpha)
with
\alpha
close to
0
, the constant
\frac{M_{0}}{\pi(a-\mu)}
is of order
\mathscr{O}(\frac{1}{\alpha})
, making the estimate (
2.16
) not that useful.

Remark 2.5
.

Assuming Hypothesis (H1) and that
a>{\mathrm{s}}(A)
, one can readily check that

(2.21)

\sup_{t>0}\|{\mathscr{V}}_{A}(t,\mu,\varphi)\|\leq 2(a-\mu)|\tan\varphi|\sup\{\|R(\mu+{\mathrm{i}}s,A)\|:|s|\leq(a-\mu)|\tan\varphi|\}<\infty

for any
\mu\in(s(A),a)
and
\varphi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\theta)
. From Lemma
2.4
it follows that

(2.22)

\|T(t)\|\leq\frac{M_{0}e^{\mu t}}{\pi(a-\mu)t}+\frac{(a-\mu)|\tan\varphi|e^{\mu t}}{\pi}\sup\{\|R(\mu+{\mathrm{i}}s,A)\|:|s|\leq(a-\mu)|\tan\varphi|\}

for any
t>0
and any
\mu>\mathrm{s}(A)
,
\varphi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\theta)
. This result for the special case of analytic semigroups on Banach spaces resembles the famous Gearhart-Prüss result for semigroups on Hilbert spaces (
[
7
,
18
]
), which was optimized by Helffer and Sjöstrand (
[
9
,
10
]
).
Indeed, the Gearhart-Prüss theorem says that a strongly continuous semigroup is exponentially stable provided its generator has no spectrum in the right half plane and its resolvent operator is bounded along the whole imaginary axis.
In case of
analytic
semigroups, however, it is enough to estimate in (
2.22
) the resolvent operator of
A
along a
bounded
vertical segment, not the entire vertical line as it is needed for
C_{0}
-semigroups that are not necessarily analytic.

Next, we collect a couple of other properties of the operator-valued function
{\mathscr{V}}_{A}
. In particular, we are interested to find an alternative formula involving convolutions.

Remark 2.6
.

Setting
b=(a-\mu)|\tan\varphi|
and using the fact that the resolvent operator of
A
is the Laplace Transform of the analytic semigroup
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, it follows that

\displaystyle{\mathscr{V}}_{A}(t,\mu,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{-b}^{b}e^{{\mathrm{i}}st}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\mu+{\mathrm{i}}s)\xi}T(\xi){\mathrm{d}}\xi{\mathrm{d}}s=\int_{-b}^{b}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi)s}e^{-\mu\xi}T(\xi){\mathrm{d}}\xi{\mathrm{d}}s

(2.23)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{-b}^{b}e^{{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi)s}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big)e^{-\mu\xi}T(\xi){\mathrm{d}}\xi=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin{\big((a-\mu)(t-\xi)|\tan\varphi|\big)}}{t-\xi}e^{-\mu\xi}T(\xi){\mathrm{d}}\xi

for any
\mu>s(A)
and
\varphi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\theta)
. This representation as a convolution shows that the estimate (
2.16
) is somewhat similar to convolution type the results of Latushkin and Yurov for the case of general semigroups on Banach spaces (
[
14
, Theorem 1.2]
).

Remark 2.7
.

From Hypothesis (H2)(ii) we infer that
0
is a pole of order
1
of
R(\cdot,A)
, hence
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}{\lambda R(\lambda,A)}
does exist in the operator norm. We infer that the operator valued function

(2.24)

\lambda\to\lambda R(\lambda,A):\Omega_{a,\theta}\cup\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{Re}\lambda>-\nu\}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}})

can be extended analytically to
\Omega_{a,\theta}\cup\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{Re}\lambda>-\nu\}
. For simplicity, we will use the same notation for this function and set
\big(\lambda R(\lambda,A)\big)_{|\lambda=0}=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}{\lambda R(\lambda,A)}
.

Next, we recall a sufficient condition that guaranties that a linear operator is sectorial due to A. Lunardi (
[
15
]
).

Lemma 2.8
.

Assume that
A:\text{\rm{dom}}(A)\subseteq{\mathbb{X}}\to{\mathbb{X}}
is a linear operator and
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(2.25)

\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:{\rm Re}\lambda\geq\omega\}\subseteq\rho(A)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\lambda R(\lambda,A)\|\leq N_{0}\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad{\rm Re}\lambda\geq\omega.

Then, the operator
A
is sectorial. More precisely,

(2.26)

\Omega_{\omega,\tau}\subseteq\rho(A)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|R(\lambda,A)\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{4N_{0}^{2}+1}}{|\lambda-\omega|}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad\lambda\in\Omega_{\omega,\tau},

where
\tau=\pi-\arctan(2N_{0})
.

It is well-known that any bounded perturbation of a sectorial operator is also sectorial. To finish this section, we discuss a sufficient condition that guarantees
that the angle of the sector remains the same.

Lemma 2.9
.

Assume Hypothesis (H1) and
W\in\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}})
. Then,

(2.27)

\Omega_{{\overline{a}},\theta}\subseteq\rho(A+W),\;\|R(\lambda,A+W)\|\leq\frac{2M_{0}(1+\csc\theta)}{|\lambda-{\overline{a}}|}\;\mbox{for any}\;\lambda\in\Omega_{{\overline{a}},\theta},

where
{\overline{a}}=a+2M_{0}\|W\|\csc\theta
.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda\in\Omega_{{\overline{a}},\theta}
. Since
{\overline{a}}>a
, we have
\lambda\in\Omega_{a,\theta}\subseteq\rho(A)
. Moreover, from Hypothesis (H1) we obtain that

(2.28)

|\lambda-a|\geq\mathrm{dist}(a,\Omega_{{\overline{a}},\theta})=({\overline{a}}-a)\sin\theta=2M\|W\|,\;\mbox{and thus}\;\|WR(\lambda,A)\|\leq\frac{M_{0}\|W\|}{|\lambda-a|}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

It follows that
I_{{\mathbb{X}}}-WR(\lambda,A)
is invertible with bounded inverse and
\|\big(I_{{\mathbb{X}}}-WR(\lambda,A)\big)^{-1}\|\leq 2
. Since
\lambda I_{{\mathbb{X}}}-A-W=\big(I_{{\mathbb{X}}}-WR(\lambda,A)\big)(\lambda I_{{\mathbb{X}}}-A)
, from (
2.28
) we infer that
\lambda\in\rho(A+W)
and

\displaystyle\|R(\lambda,A+W)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|R(\lambda,A)\|\|\big(I_{{\mathbb{X}}}-WR(\lambda,A)\big)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{2M_{0}}{|\lambda-a|}=\frac{2M_{0}\big|\lambda-a-2M_{0}\|W\|\csc\theta\big|}{|\lambda-{\overline{a}}|\,|\lambda-a|}

(2.29)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2M_{0}}{|\lambda-{\overline{a}}|}\Big(1+\frac{2M_{0}\|W\|\csc\theta}{|\lambda-a|}\Big)\leq\frac{2M_{0}(1+\csc\theta)}{|\lambda-{\overline{a}}|},

proving the lemma. ∎

3.
Norm estimates of families of analytic semigroups

In this section we discuss the uniform exponential stability of families of analytic semigroups whose generators are bounded perturbations of a sectorial operator satisfying Hypotheses (H1) and (H2). Throughout this section we assume that
E:[0,\infty)\to\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}})
is such that
E(0)=0
, and the family of operators denotes by
A_{\alpha}:=A+E(\alpha)
,
\alpha\geq 0
, satisfies Hypothesis (H3). In addition, we recall that
\{T_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
denotes the semigroup of linear operators generated by
A_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\geq 0
.

3.1.
Basic Properties of families of analytic semigroups

First, we will show that the operator
A_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\geq 0
, is sectorial and all the constants from (
1.2
) can be chosen independent of
\alpha
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume Hypotheses (H1)–(H3). Then, there exist
{\widetilde{a}}>0
,
{\widetilde{\theta}}\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
and
{\widetilde{M}}>0
, independent of
\alpha\geq 0
, such that

(3.1)

\Omega_{{\widetilde{a}},{\widetilde{\theta}}}\subseteq\rho(A_{\alpha}),\quad\|R(\lambda,A_{\alpha})\|\leq\frac{{\widetilde{M}}}{|\lambda-{\widetilde{a}}|}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad\lambda\in\Omega_{{\widetilde{a}},{\widetilde{\theta}}},\;\alpha\geq 0.

The constants
{\widetilde{a}}
,
{\widetilde{\theta}}
and
{\widetilde{M}}
depend on the unperturbed operator
A
and the functions
\|E(\cdot)\|
and
q
and related constants in Hypotheses (H3).

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
a>0
, where
a
is the vertex of the sector from Hypothesis (H1). From Hypothesis (H3)(iii) we have there exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that
\|E(\alpha)\|\leq(\ell+1)q(\alpha)
for any
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
. From Hypotheses (H2) and (H3) respectively, it is clear that
\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{Re}\lambda\geq a+1\}\subseteq\rho(A)\cap\rho(A_{\alpha})
for any
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
. Moreover,

(3.2)

R(\lambda,A_{\alpha})-R(\lambda,A)=R(\lambda,A_{\alpha})E(\alpha)R(\lambda,A)\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad{\rm Re}\lambda\geq a+1,\;\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}.

From (
3.2
), Hypothesis (H1) and Hypothesis (H3)(i) we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\lambda R(\lambda,A_{\alpha})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda R(\lambda,A)\|+\|R(\lambda,A_{\alpha})\|\,\|E(\alpha)\|\,\|\lambda R(\lambda,A)\|\leq\frac{M_{0}|\lambda|}{|\lambda-a|}\Big(1+\frac{M_{1}\|E(\alpha)\|}{\mathrm{Re}\lambda+q(\alpha)}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M_{0}|\lambda|}{|\lambda-a|}\Big(1+\frac{M_{1}\|E(\alpha)\|}{q(\alpha)}\Big)\leq\frac{|\lambda|}{|\lambda-a|}M_{0}\big(1+M_{1}(\ell+1)\big)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq M_{0}\big(1+M_{1}(\ell+1)\big)\Big(1+\frac{a}{|\lambda-a|}\Big)\leq M_{0}(1+a)\big(1+M_{1}(\ell+1)\big)

whenever
{\rm Re}\lambda\geq a+1
and
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
. From Hypothesis (H3) we infer that
\sup_{\alpha\in[0,\alpha_{0}]}\|E(\alpha)\|<\infty
. Lemma
2.9
yields

(3.4)

\Omega_{a_{1},\theta}\subseteq\rho(A_{\alpha}),\quad\|R(\lambda,A_{\alpha})\|\leq\frac{2M_{0}(1+\csc\theta)}{|\lambda-a_{1}|}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad\lambda\in\Omega_{a_{1},\theta},\;\alpha\in[0,\alpha_{0}],

where
a_{1}=a+2M_{0}\sup_{\alpha\in[0,\alpha_{0}]}\|E(\alpha)\|\csc\theta\geq a
. It follows that
\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{Re}\lambda\geq a_{1}+1\}\subseteq\rho(A_{\alpha})
for any
\alpha\in[0,\alpha_{0}]
and

(3.5)

\|\lambda R(\lambda,A_{\alpha})\|\leq\frac{2M_{0}(1+\csc\theta)|\lambda|}{|\lambda-a_{1}|}\leq 2M_{0}(1+\csc\theta)\Big(1+\frac{a_{1}}{|\lambda-a_{1}|}\Big)\leq 2M_{0}(1+\csc\theta)(1+a_{1})

whenever
{\rm Re}\lambda\geq a_{1}+1
and
\alpha\in[0,\alpha_{0}]
. Since
a_{1}\geq a
from (
3.1
) and (
3.5
) it follows that
\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{Re}\lambda\geq a_{1}+1\}\subseteq\rho(A_{\alpha})
for any
\alpha\geq 0
and

(3.6)

\|\lambda R(\lambda,A_{\alpha})\|\leq M_{0}(1+a_{1})\big(1+\max\{\csc\theta,M_{1}(\ell+1)\}\big)\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad{\rm Re}\lambda\geq a_{1}+1,\alpha\geq 0.

Setting
{\widetilde{a}}=a_{1}+1
,
{\widetilde{N}}=M_{0}(1+a_{1})(1+\max\{\csc\theta,M_{1}(\ell+1)\})
,
{\widetilde{M}}=\sqrt{4{\widetilde{N}}^{2}+1}
and
{\widetilde{\theta}}=\pi-\arctan(2{\widetilde{N}})
, from Lemma
2.8
and (
3.6
) we infer that

(3.7)

\Omega_{{\widetilde{a}},{\widetilde{\theta}}}=\Omega_{a_{1}+1,{\widetilde{\theta}}}\subseteq\rho(A_{\alpha})\;\mbox{and}\;\|R(\lambda,A_{\alpha})\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{4{\widetilde{N}}^{2}+1}}{|\lambda-a_{1}-1|}=\frac{{\widetilde{M}}}{|\lambda-{\widetilde{a}}|}\;\mbox{for any}\;\lambda\in\Omega_{{\widetilde{a}},{\widetilde{\theta}}},\,\alpha\geq 0,

proving the lemma.
∎

In the next lemma we apply Lemma
2.4
to estimate the norm of the semigroup
\{T_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
for
\alpha
away from
0
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume Hypotheses (H1)–(H3). Then the following estimate holds,

(3.8)

\|T_{\alpha}(t)\|\leq\overline{M}(\varkappa,\alpha)e^{-\varkappa q(\alpha)t}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad t\geq 0,\;\alpha>0,\;\varkappa\in(0,1).

Here the function
\overline{M}:(0,1)\times(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is defined by

(3.9)

\begin{split}\overline{M}(\varkappa,\alpha)&=\frac{1}{\pi}\max\Bigg\{\frac{{\widetilde{M}}q(\alpha)}{{\widetilde{a}}+\varkappa q(\alpha)}+\frac{M_{1}\big({\widetilde{a}}+\varkappa q(\alpha)\big)|\tan(\frac{{\widetilde{\theta}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4})|}{(1-\varkappa)q(\alpha)},\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\frac{{\widetilde{M}}\Big(e(2{\widetilde{\theta}}+\pi)-4\sec(\frac{{\widetilde{\theta}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4})\Big)}{4}e^{\frac{{\widetilde{a}}}{q(\alpha)}+1}\Bigg\}.\end{split}

In particular, for any
\delta>0
the family of semigroups
\{T_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is exponentially stable uniformly for
\alpha\geq\delta
.

Proof.

Fix
\varkappa\in(0,1)
,
\alpha>0
and let
\mu=-\varkappa q(\alpha)
. We set
\varphi=\frac{{\widetilde{\theta}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4}\in(\frac{\pi}{2},{\widetilde{\theta}})
and
\widetilde{b}=({\widetilde{a}}-\mu)|\tan\varphi|
. From Hypothesis (H3) we have
\mu>\rm{s}(A_{\alpha})
and

(3.10)

\|R(\mu+{\mathrm{i}}s,A_{\alpha})\|\leq\frac{M_{1}}{{\rm Re}(\mu+{\mathrm{i}}s)+q(\alpha)}=\frac{M_{1}}{\mu+q(\alpha)}=\frac{M_{1}}{(1-\varkappa)q(\alpha)}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Using definition (
2.15
) leads to

(3.11)

\|{\mathscr{V}}_{A_{\alpha}}(t,\mu,\varphi)\|=\Big\|\int_{-\widetilde{b}}^{\widetilde{b}}e^{{\mathrm{i}}st}R(\mu+{\mathrm{i}}s,A_{\alpha}){\mathrm{d}}s\Big\|\leq\frac{2M_{1}\widetilde{b}}{(1-\varkappa)q(\alpha)}=\frac{2M_{1}({\widetilde{a}}-\mu)|\tan(\frac{{\widetilde{\theta}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4})|}{(1-\varkappa)q(\alpha)}

for any
t>0
. From (
3.1
), (
3.11
) and Lemma
2.4
it follows that

\displaystyle\|T_{\alpha}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\widetilde{M}}e^{\mu t}}{\pi({\widetilde{a}}-\mu)t}+\frac{e^{\mu t}}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{2M_{1}({\widetilde{a}}-\mu)|\tan(\frac{{\widetilde{\theta}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4})|}{(1-\varkappa)q(\alpha)}

(3.12)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\varkappa q(\alpha)t}}{\pi}\Bigg(\frac{{\widetilde{M}}}{({\widetilde{a}}+\varkappa q(\alpha))t}+\frac{M_{1}({\widetilde{a}}+\varkappa q(\alpha))|\tan(\frac{{\widetilde{\theta}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4})|}{(1-\varkappa)q(\alpha)}\Bigg)\;\mbox{for any}\;t>0.

If
t\geq\frac{1}{q(\alpha)}
then
({\widetilde{a}}+\varkappa q(\alpha))t\geq\frac{{\widetilde{a}}}{q(\alpha)}+\varkappa
, thus from (
3.1
) we have

(3.13)

\|T_{\alpha}(t)\|\leq\frac{e^{-\varkappa q(\alpha)t}}{\pi}\Big(\frac{{\widetilde{M}}q(\alpha)}{{\widetilde{a}}+\varkappa q(\alpha)}+\frac{M_{1}({\widetilde{a}}+\varkappa q(\alpha))|\tan(\frac{{\widetilde{\theta}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4})|}{(1-\varkappa)q(\alpha)}\Big)\;\mbox{for any}\;t\geq\frac{1}{q(\alpha)}.

From Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
3.1
we infer

\displaystyle\|T_{\alpha}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\widetilde{M}}(e\varphi-\sec\varphi)}{\pi}e^{{\widetilde{a}}t}=\frac{{\widetilde{M}}\Big(e(2{\widetilde{\theta}}+\pi)-4\sec(\frac{{\widetilde{\theta}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4})\Big)}{4\pi}e^{({\widetilde{a}}+\varkappa q(\alpha))t}e^{-\varkappa q(\alpha)t}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\widetilde{M}}\Big(e(2{\widetilde{\theta}}+\pi)-4\sec(\frac{{\widetilde{\theta}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4})\Big)}{4\pi}e^{\frac{{\widetilde{a}}}{q(\alpha)}+1}e^{-\varkappa q(\alpha)t}

for any
t\in\big[0,\frac{1}{q(\alpha)}\big)
. The estimate (
3.8
) follows from (
3.13
) and (
3.1
), with
\overline{M}(\varkappa,\alpha)
given by (
3.9
). One can readily check that

(3.15)

\overline{M}(\varkappa,\alpha)\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\max\Bigg\{\frac{{\widetilde{M}}}{\varkappa}+\frac{M_{1}|\tan(\frac{{\widetilde{\theta}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4})|\big(\frac{{\widetilde{a}}}{q(\alpha)}+\varkappa\big)}{(1-\varkappa)},\frac{{\widetilde{M}}\Big(e(2{\widetilde{\theta}}+\pi)-4\sec(\frac{{\widetilde{\theta}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4})\Big)}{4}e^{\frac{{\widetilde{a}}}{q(\alpha)}+1}\Bigg\}.

Since
\inf_{\alpha\geq\delta}q(\alpha)=q(\delta)>0
for any
\delta>0
, by Hypothesis (H3), from (
3.8
) and (
3.15
) it follows that
for any
\delta>0
the family of semigroups
\{T_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is exponentially stable uniformly for
\alpha\geq\delta
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

In the case when the space
{\mathbb{X}}
is a
Hilbert
space one can use the results of B. Helfer and J. Sjöstrand (
[
9
,
10
]
) to obtain an estimate similar to (
3.8
). Indeed, from Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
3.1
it follows that

(3.16)

\|T_{\alpha}(t)\|\leq\frac{{\widetilde{M}}\Big(e(2{\widetilde{\theta}}+\pi)-4\sec(\frac{{\widetilde{\theta}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4})\Big)}{4\pi}e^{{\widetilde{a}}t}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad t>0,\;\alpha>0.

From Hypothesis (H3)(i) we have

(3.17)

\sup_{{\rm Re}\lambda\geq-\varkappa q(\alpha)}\|R(\lambda,A_{\alpha})\|\leq\sup_{{\rm Re}\lambda\geq-\varkappa q(\alpha)}\frac{M_{1}}{\mathrm{Re}\lambda+q(\alpha)}\leq\frac{M_{1}}{(1-\varkappa)q(\alpha)}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad\alpha>0,\;\varkappa\in(0,1).

From
[
9
, Proposition 2.1]
we obtain that

(3.18)

\|T_{\alpha}(t)\|\leq\overline{N}(\varkappa,\alpha)e^{-\varkappa q(\alpha)t}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad t>0,\;\alpha>0,

where the function
\overline{N}:(0,1)\times(0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
is defined by

(3.19)

\overline{N}(\varkappa,\alpha)=\frac{{\widetilde{M}}\Big(e(2{\widetilde{\theta}}+\pi)-4\sec(\frac{{\widetilde{\theta}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4})\Big)}{4\pi}\Bigg(1+\frac{{\widetilde{M}}\Big(e(2{\widetilde{\theta}}+\pi)-4\sec(\frac{{\widetilde{\theta}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4})\Big)}{2\pi}\Big(1+\frac{M_{1}({\widetilde{a}}+\varkappa q(\alpha)}{(1-\varkappa)q(\alpha)}\Big)\Bigg).

Here
{\widetilde{M}}
,
{\widetilde{\theta}}
and
{\widetilde{a}}
are obtained in Lemma
3.1
and
M_{1}
is taken from Hypothesis (H3) (i). We note that
\overline{N}(\varkappa,\alpha)=\mathscr{O}(\frac{1}{q(\alpha)})
and
\overline{M}(\varkappa,\alpha)=\mathscr{O}(\frac{1}{q(\alpha)})
as
\alpha\to 0
, which shows that regardless of what approach is used one can conclude uniform exponential stability of the family of semigroups
\{T_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
for
\alpha\geq\delta
, for any
\delta>0
, but not for
\alpha\geq 0
. Also, from (
3.19
) we note that
\sup_{\alpha\geq 0}\overline{N}(\varkappa,\alpha)<\infty
only if
{\widetilde{a}}=0
, which occurs only when the family of semigroups
\{T_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
,
\alpha\geq 0
, is bounded uniformly in
\alpha\geq 0
, a property which is as difficult to establish as the result of exponential decay of the family of semigroups with uniform in
\alpha
constants.

3.2.
Norm Estimates of
\{T_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
when
\alpha
is in a neighborhood of
0
.

To estimate the norm of the semigroup
\{T_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
when
\alpha
is in a neighborhood of
0
, we need a different approach. The key part of the argument is to find a spectral decomposition of the Banach space
{\mathbb{X}}
, into a sum of two closed subspaces invariant under the semigroup
\{T_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, such that the spectrum of the restriction of
A_{\alpha}
to the two subspaces is either away from the imaginary axis or made up entirely of eigenvalues.
We first look for such a decomposition of the linear operator
A
. From Hypothesis (H2) we have the spectrum of
A
has two disjoint parts, separated by the circle
\partial D(0,\frac{\nu}{2})
. We define
P_{0}\in\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}})
as the spectral projection relative to the spectral subset
\{0\}
of
\sigma(A)
, given by the formula

(3.20)

P_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\int_{\partial D(0,\frac{\nu}{2})}R(\lambda,A){\mathrm{d}}\lambda.

Remark 3.4
.

We collect several well-known properties of the spectral projection
P_{0}
, see e.g.,
[
6
,
15
,
17
]
:

(i)

\mathrm{Im}P_{0}\subset\text{\rm{dom}}(A)
;

(ii)

\mathrm{Im}P_{0}
and
\mathrm{Ker}P_{0}
are invariant under
A
and
T(t)
for any
t\geq 0
;

(iii)

The linear operator
{\widetilde{A}}:=A_{|\text{\rm{dom}}(A)\cap\mathrm{Ker}P_{0}}
is the generator of the semigroup
\{{\widetilde{T}}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}=\{T(t)_{|\mathrm{Ker}P_{0}}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathrm{Ker}P_{0}
;

(iv)

\sigma({\widetilde{A}})=\sigma(A)\setminus\{0\}
.

In the next lemma we aim to refine the properties of
P_{0}
by making use of Hypotheses (H1)–(H2).

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume Hypotheses (H1)–(H2). Then, the following assertions hold true

(i)

\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{Im}P_{0}<\infty
and
A_{|\mathrm{Im}P_{0}}\equiv 0
;

(ii)

The operator
{\widetilde{A}}
is sectorial, and thus it generates an analytic semigroup. Moreover, there exists
M_{2}>0
such that

(3.21)

\|{\widetilde{T}}(t)\|\leq M_{2}e^{-\frac{7\nu}{8}t}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad t>0;

(iii)

The linear operators
A
and
T(t)
,
t\geq 0
, respectively, have the representations

(3.22)

A=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&{\widetilde{A}}\end{bmatrix},\quad T(t)=\begin{bmatrix}I_{\mathrm{Im}P_{0}}&0\\
0&{\widetilde{T}}(t)\end{bmatrix},\;t\geq 0,

with respect to the decomposition
{\mathbb{X}}=\mathrm{Im}P_{0}\oplus\mathrm{Ker}P_{0}
(direct sum, not necessarily orthogonal).

Proof.

(i) Since
0
is a semisimple eigenvalue of finite multiplicity of
A
we infer that
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{Im}P_{0}<\infty
. Moreover, from Remark
3.4
(i) and since
A
is a closed linear operator we have

(3.23)

AP_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\int_{\partial D(0,\frac{\nu}{2})}AR(\lambda,A){\mathrm{d}}\lambda=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\int_{\partial D(0,\frac{\nu}{2})}\big(\lambda R(\lambda,A)-I_{{\mathbb{X}}}\big){\mathrm{d}}\lambda.

By Remark
2.7
we know that the function
\lambda\to\lambda R(\lambda,A):\Omega_{a,\theta}\cup\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{Re}\lambda>-\nu\}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}})

can be extended analytically to
\Omega_{a,\theta}\cup\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{Re}\lambda>-\nu\}
, therefore from (
3.23
) we obtain that
AP_{0}\equiv 0
, hence
A_{|\mathrm{Im}P_{0}}\equiv 0
.

(ii) From Remark
3.4
(iv) and Hypothesis (H1) we have
\Omega_{a,\theta}\subset\rho(A)\subset\rho({\widetilde{A}})
and
R(\lambda,{\widetilde{A}})=R(\lambda,A)_{|\mathrm{Ker}P_{0}}
for any
\lambda\in\Omega_{a,\theta}
, hence

(3.24)

\|R(\lambda,{\widetilde{A}})\|\leq\|R(\lambda,A)\|\leq\frac{M}{|\lambda-a|}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad\lambda\in\Omega_{a,\theta}.

We conclude that the linear operator
{\widetilde{A}}
is sectorial, therefore the semigroup
\{{\widetilde{T}}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is analytic. From Hypothesis (H2)(i) it follows that

(3.25)

\omega_{0}({\widetilde{A}})=\sup\mathrm{Re}\sigma({\widetilde{A}})=\sup\mathrm{Re}\big(\sigma(A)\setminus\{0\}\big)\leq-\nu<-\frac{7\nu}{8}.

From the definition of the growth rate of a semigroup one immediately concludes that

(3.26)

M_{2}=\sup_{t\geq 0}\big(e^{\frac{7\nu}{8}t}\|{\widetilde{T}}(t)\|\big)<\infty,

proving (ii). Assertion (iii) follows shortly from (i) and Remark
3.4
(ii) and (iii).
∎

To construct a representation of
A_{\alpha}
similar to (
3.22
) for the case when
\alpha
is in the neighborhood of
0
, we utilize the standard transformation/conjugation operators, see
[
13
, Chapter II, Section 4.2]
and
[
5
, Chapter 4, Section 1]
. First, we need to show that
the circle
\partial D(0,\frac{\nu}{2})
separates
\sigma(A_{\alpha})
into two disjoint spectral subsets for any
\alpha
in a neighborhood of
0
. For the remainder of the section, we need to assume Hypothesis (H4) in order to ensure that all the spectral and semigroup bounds are
\alpha
-independent.
Moreover, we recal that from (
1.4
) and Hypothesis (H3) we can immediately infer that
E_{0}
is continuous on
(0,\infty)
in
\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}})
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume Hypotheses (H1)–(H4). Then,

(3.27)

{\mathscr{E}}_{\nu}:=\Big\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{Re}\lambda\geq-\frac{3\nu}{4},\;|\lambda|\geq\frac{\nu}{4}\Big\}\subset\rho(A_{\alpha})\;\mbox{and}\;\|R(\lambda,A_{\alpha})\|\leq\frac{16M_{2}}{\nu}

for any
\lambda\in{\mathscr{E}}_{\nu},\,\alpha\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, where

(3.28)

\varepsilon_{0}:=\min\Big\{\frac{\nu}{16M_{2}\sup_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\|E_{0}(\alpha)\|+1},1\Big\}

and
M_{2}
is defined in (
3.26
).

Proof.

First, we collect some properties of the linear operator
{\widetilde{A}}
. From Remark
3.4
(iv) and Lemma
3.5
(ii), respectively, we have

(3.29)

\sigma({\widetilde{A}})\subset\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{Re}\lambda\leq-\nu\}\;\mbox{and}\;\|R(\lambda,{\widetilde{A}})\|\leq\frac{M_{2}}{\mathrm{Re}\lambda+\frac{7\nu}{8}}\;\;\mbox{whenever}\;\mathrm{Re}\lambda>-\frac{7\nu}{8}.

In addition, from Lemma
3.5
(iii) it follows that the resolvent operator of
A
has the representation

(3.30)

R(\lambda,A)=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\lambda}I_{\mathrm{Im}P_{0}}&0\\
0&R(\lambda,{\widetilde{A}})\end{bmatrix}\;\;\mbox{whenever}\;\mathrm{Re}\lambda>-\nu,\;\lambda\neq 0.

with respect to the decomposition
{\mathbb{X}}=\mathrm{Im}P_{0}\oplus\mathrm{Ker}P_{0}
.
From (
3.29
) and (
3.30
) it follows that

(3.31)

\|R(\lambda,A)\|\leq\max\Big\{\frac{1}{|\lambda|},\frac{M_{2}}{\mathrm{Re}\lambda+\frac{7\nu}{8}}\Big\}\leq\max\Big\{\frac{4}{\nu},\frac{8M_{2}}{\nu}\Big\}=\frac{8M_{2}}{\nu}\;\mbox{for any}\;\lambda\in{\mathscr{E}}_{\nu}.

Fix
\lambda\in{\mathscr{E}}_{\nu}
and
\alpha\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, where
\varepsilon_{0}
is defined in (
3.28
). Then,

(3.32)

\lambda I_{{\mathbb{X}}}-A_{\alpha}=\lambda I_{{\mathbb{X}}}-A-E(\alpha)=\Big(I_{{\mathbb{X}}}-E(\alpha)R(\lambda,A)\Big)(\lambda I_{{\mathbb{X}}}-A).

Moreover, from (
1.4
) and (
3.31
) we obtain that

(3.33)

\|E(\alpha)R(\lambda,A)\|\leq\frac{8M_{2}\alpha}{\nu}\|E_{0}(\alpha)\|\leq\frac{8M_{2}\varepsilon_{0}}{\nu}\sup_{\alpha\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]}\|E_{0}(\alpha)\|\leq\frac{1}{2},

which implies that
I_{{\mathbb{X}}}-E(\alpha)R(\lambda,A)
is invertible and
\Big\|\big(I_{{\mathbb{X}}}-E(\alpha)R(\lambda,A)\big)^{-1}\Big\|\leq 2
. From (
3.31
) and (
3.32
) we infer that

{\mathscr{E}}_{\nu}\subset\rho(A_{\alpha})\;\mbox{and}\;\|R(\lambda,A_{\alpha})\|\leq\frac{16M_{2}}{\nu}\;\mbox{for any}\;\lambda\in{\mathscr{E}}_{\nu},\;\alpha\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}],

proving the lemma.
∎

Next, we note that assertion (
3.27
) allows us to define the spectral projection

(3.34)

P_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\int_{\partial D(0,\frac{\nu}{2})}R(\lambda,A_{\alpha}){\mathrm{d}}\lambda,\quad\alpha\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}].

Remark 3.7
.

Clearly,
P_{\alpha}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\mathrm{Im}P_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{Ker}P_{\alpha}
are invariant under
A_{\alpha}
and
T_{\alpha}(t)
for any

,
\alpha\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]

(ii)

\mathrm{Im}P_{\alpha}\subset\text{\rm{dom}}(A)
for any

;

(iii)

The linear operator
{\widetilde{A}}_{\alpha}:=(A_{\alpha})_{|\text{\rm{dom}}(A)\cap\mathrm{Ker}P_{\alpha}}
is the generator of the semigroup
\{{\widetilde{T}}_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}=\{T(t)_{|\mathrm{Ker}P_{\alpha}}\}_{t\geq 0}
on

for any

(iv)

In the next lemma we show that the family of spectral projections

is continuous in the operator norm.

Lemma 3.8
.

Assume Hypotheses (H1)–(H4). Then, the function

\alpha\rightarrow P_{\alpha}:[0,\varepsilon_{0}]\to\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}})
is continuous in the operator norm. Moreover, the following estimate holds

(3.35)

\|P_{\alpha_{1}}-P_{\alpha_{2}}\|\leq\frac{128M_{2}^{2}}{\nu}\|E(\alpha_{1})-E(\alpha_{2})\|\quad\mbox{for any}\quad\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}].

Proof.

Since
A_{\alpha}=A+E(\alpha)
for any
\alpha\geq 0
, from (
3.27
) we immediately infer that

(3.36)

R(\lambda,A_{\alpha_{1}})-R(\lambda,A_{\alpha_{2}})=R(\lambda,A_{\alpha_{1}})\big(E(\alpha_{1})-E(\alpha_{2})\big)R(\lambda,A_{\alpha_{2}})

for any
\lambda\in{\mathscr{E}}_{\nu}
,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
. From (
3.27
), (
3.34
) and (
3.36
) it follows that

\displaystyle\|P_{\alpha_{1}}-P_{\alpha_{2}}\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big\|\int_{\partial D(0,\frac{\nu}{2})}R(\lambda,A_{\alpha_{1}})\big(E(\alpha_{1})-E(\alpha_{2})\big)R(\lambda,A_{\alpha_{2}}){\mathrm{d}}\lambda\Big\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathrm{length}\big(\partial D(0,\frac{\nu}{2})\big)}{2\pi}\sup_{|\lambda|=\frac{\nu}{2}}\|R(\lambda,A_{\alpha_{1}})\|\,\|E(\alpha_{1})-E(\alpha_{2})\|\,\sup_{|\lambda|=\frac{\nu}{2}}\|R(\lambda,A_{\alpha_{2}})\|

(3.37)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{128M_{2}^{2}}{\nu}\|E(\alpha_{1})-E(\alpha_{2})\|\quad\mbox{for any}\quad\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}].

The lemma follows shortly from (
3.2
) and since
E
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
in
\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}})
.
∎

Following
[
5
, Chapter 4]
we introduce the operator transformation/conjugation function associated to the family of projections
\{P_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]}
as follows:

(3.38)

U:[0,\varepsilon_{0}]\to\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}}),\quad U(\alpha)=P_{\alpha}P_{0}+(I_{\mathbb{X}}-P_{\alpha})(I_{\mathbb{X}}-P_{0}).

The properties of the operator transformation/conjugation function
U
defined above are collected in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.9
.

Assume Hypotheses (H1)–(H4). Then, the following assertions hold true

(i)

The function
U
is continuous on
[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
in the
\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}})
operator norm;

(ii)

U(\alpha)
is an invertible operator for any
\alpha\in[0,\varepsilon_{1}]
, where

(3.39)

\varepsilon_{1}:=\min\Big\{\frac{\nu}{256M_{2}^{2}(16M_{2}+1)\sup_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\|E_{0}(\alpha)\|+1},1\Big\}

and
M_{2}
is defined in (
3.26
);

(iii)

The function
U(\cdot)^{-1}
is continuous on
[0,\varepsilon_{1}]
in the
\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{X}})
operator norm;

(iv)

P_{\alpha}=U(\alpha)P_{0}U(\alpha)^{-1}
for any
\alpha\in[0,\varepsilon_{1}]
.

Proof.

Assertion (i) follows immediately from (
3.38
) and Lemma
3.8
.

(ii) Since
P_{0}
is a projection one can readily check that

4.

We note that

is a standing wave solution of (

We recall that
{\mathscr{M}}_{F^{\prime}(\overline{h})}
denotes the operator of multiplication on

by the bounded, matrix valued function

, while

is considered as a closed, densely defined linear operator on

with domain

is unitary equivalent to

, the operator of multiplication acting on

\widehat{L}:{\mathbb{R}}^{m-1}\to\mathscr{B}\big(H^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k}),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})\big),\;\widehat{L}(\xi)=D\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}+cI_{k}\partial_{y_{1}}+{\mathscr{M}}_{V(\cdot,\xi)},

where

is defined by

(4.6)

For any

the linear operator
\widehat{L}(\xi)
can be considered as a closed, densely defined linear operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})
with domain

.

It is well-known that elliptic operators generate analytic semigroups, see, e.g.,

2

11
,
15
,
17
,
21

22

. In the next lemma we show that condition (

\widehat{L}(0)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\inf\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma(D)>0
then the linear operator
\widehat{L}(\xi)
, defined in (
4.5
), is sectorial, for any
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m-1}
, hence it generates an analytic semigroup. In particular,
\widehat{L}(0)
satisfies Hypothesis (H1).

Proof.

Let
d_{0}=\inf\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma(D)>0
. First, we show that the linear operator
D\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}
is sectorial. Denoting by
\mathscr{F}_{1}
the Fourier Transform with respect to the variable
y_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, one can readily check that

(4.7)

D\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}=\mathscr{F}_{1}^{-1}M_{\widehat{D}}\mathscr{F}_{1},\;\mbox{where}\;\widehat{D}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{k\times k}\;\mbox{is defined by}\;\widehat{D}(\xi_{1})=-\xi_{1}^{2}D.

Since
D\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k\times k}
we have

(4.8)

\omega_{0}(-D)=\sup\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma(-D)=-\inf\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma(D)=-d_{0}<0.

From the definition of the growth rate of a semigroup we obtain that there exists
C_{0}>0
such that

(4.9)

\|e^{-tD}\|\leq C_{0}e^{-\frac{d_{0}}{2}t}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad t\geq 0.

Since
\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\geq-\frac{d_{0}}{2}\}\subset\rho(-D)
we have

(4.10)

\lambda(\lambda I_{k}+D)^{-1}=I_{k}-D(\lambda I_{k}+D)^{-1}=I_{k}-D\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}e^{-tD}{\mathrm{d}}t\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>-\frac{d_{0}}{2}.

Therefore, from (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) we immediately obtain

(4.11)

\sup_{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\geq 0}\|\lambda(\lambda I_{k}+D)^{-1}\|\leq 1+\frac{2\|D\|C_{0}}{d_{0}}<\infty.

From Lemma
2.8
it follows that there exists
{\widetilde{C}}_{0}\geq 1
and
\varphi_{0}\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
such that

(4.12)

\Omega_{0,\varphi_{0}}\subset\rho(-D),\quad\|(\lambda I_{k}+D)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{{\widetilde{C}}_{0}}{|\lambda|}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad\lambda\in\Omega_{0,\varphi_{0}}.

Since
\frac{\lambda}{\xi_{1}^{2}}\in\Omega_{0,\varphi_{0}}
for any
\lambda\in\Omega_{0,\varphi_{0}}
and
\xi_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
, from (
4.7
) and (
4.12
) we conclude that

(4.13)

\Omega_{0,\varphi_{0}}\subset\rho\big(\widehat{D}(\xi_{1})\big),\quad\big\|\big(\lambda I_{k}-\widehat{D}(\xi_{1})\big)^{-1}\big\|\leq\frac{{\widetilde{C}}_{0}}{|\lambda|}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad\lambda\in\Omega_{0,\varphi_{0}},\,\xi_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}},

which is equivalent to

(4.14)

\Omega_{0,\varphi_{0}}\subset\rho(M_{\widehat{D}}),\quad\|R(\lambda,M_{\widehat{D}})\|\leq\frac{{\widetilde{C}}_{0}}{|\lambda|}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad\lambda\in\Omega_{0,\varphi_{0}}.

From (
4.14
) we infer that the linear operator
M_{\widehat{D}}
, and hence
D\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}
, is a sectorial operator.
Since
D\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k\times k}
is an invertible matrix one can readily check that

(4.15)

\|\partial_{y_{1}}u\|_{2}^{2}=-\langle\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})}=-\langle D\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}u,(D^{-1})^{*}u\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})}\leq\|D^{-1}\|\,\|D\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}u\|_{2}\,\|u\|_{2}\

for any
u\in H^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})
, which implies that

(4.16)

\|\partial_{y_{1}}u\|_{2}\leq\|D^{-1}\|^{1/2}\,\|D\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}u\|_{2}^{1/2}\,\|u\|_{2}^{1/2}\leq\|D^{-1}\|^{1/2}\,\|u\|_{\text{\rm{dom}}(D\partial_{y_{1}}^{2})}^{1/2}\,\|u\|_{2}^{1/2}

for any
u\in\text{\rm{dom}}(D\partial_{y_{1}}^{2})=H^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})
. Applying the results from
[
15
, Chapter 2]
, we infer that
D\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}+cI_{k}\partial_{y_{1}}
is also a sectorial operator. Since
\overline{h}
, and thus

, are bounded functions, from Lemma
2.9
and (

) we conclude that
\widehat{L}(\xi)
is a sectorial operator for any
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m-1}
, proving the lemma.
∎

Next, we note that the operator valued function

has the representation

(4.17)

\widehat{L}(\xi)=\widehat{L}(0)+E_{\mathrm{RD}}(|\xi|^{2})\;\mbox{for any}\;\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m-1},

where

, is defined by
E_{\mathrm{RD}}(\alpha)=-\alpha{\mathscr{M}}_{D}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Assuming Hypothesis (RD) from the Introduction and (
4.1
), from Lemma
4.1
we derive that
\widehat{L}(0)
satisfies Hypotheses (H1)-(H2). Hence, it follows from Lemma

that the semigroup generated by
\widehat{L}(0)
has a block representation of the form (
3.22
) and satisfies the estimate (
3.21
). We infer that the semigroup generated by
\widehat{L}(0)
is bounded, that is
{\widetilde{M}}_{0}=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|e^{t\widehat{L}(0)}\|<\infty
, which implies that

(4.18)

\|R(\lambda,\widehat{L}(0))\|\leq\frac{{\widetilde{M}}_{0}}{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda}\;\mbox{whenver}\;\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>0.

In many applications, the diffusion rates of various components of the vector valued function
u
in (
1.8
) are close to each other. In this case we can prove the Lyapunov linear stability of the front
\overline{h}
using the results of Section
3
. First, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume Hypothesis (RD) and that the matrix
D\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k\times k}
is sufficiently close to a diagonal matrix in the sense that there exists some
d>0
such that for
{\widetilde{M}}_{0}
from (
4.18
) one has,

(4.19)

\|D-dI_{k}\|<\frac{d}{{\widetilde{M}}_{0}}.

Then, the family of operators

,
\alpha\geq 0
, satisfies Hypotheses (H1)-(H5).

Proof.

Denoting by
J=D-dI_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k\times k}
we have
\|J\|<\frac{d}{{\widetilde{M}}_{0}}
. One can readily check that
\sigma(J)\subset D(0,\frac{d}{{\widetilde{M}}_{0}})
. Since
{\widetilde{M}}_{0}=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|e^{t\widehat{L}(0)}\|\geq 1
, we have
\inf\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma(J)>-d
, which implies that
\inf\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma(D)>0
. From Remark
4.2
we conclude that the linear operator
\widehat{L}(0)
satisfies Hypotheses (H1) and (H2).

Next, we study the spectrum of

. Since
\frac{\|J\|{\widetilde{M}}_{0}}{d}<1
, there exists
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
\frac{\|J\|{\widetilde{M}}_{0}}{d}<(1-\delta_{0})^{2}
. Next, we fix
\alpha\geq 0
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\mathrm{Re}\lambda>-\delta_{0}\alpha d
. Then, from Hypothesis (RD) we note that
\lambda+\alpha d\in\rho\big(\widehat{L}(0)\big)
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\lambda I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})}-A_{\mathrm{RD}}(\alpha)=(\lambda+\alpha d)I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})}-\widehat{L}(0)+\alpha{\mathscr{M}}_{J}

(4.20)

\displaystyle\qquad=\Big(I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})}+\alpha{\mathscr{M}}_{J}R\big(\lambda+\alpha d,\widehat{L}(0)\big)\Big)\big((\lambda+\alpha d)I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})}-\widehat{L}(0)\big).

From (
4.18
) we obtain

(4.21)

\big\|\alpha{\mathscr{M}}_{J}R\big(\lambda+\alpha d,\widehat{L}(0)\big)\big\|\leq\frac{\alpha\|J\|{\widetilde{M}}_{0}}{\mathrm{Re}\lambda+\alpha d}\leq\frac{\|J\|{\widetilde{M}}_{0}}{(1-\delta_{0})d}\leq 1-\delta_{0}<1,

which implies that
I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})}+\alpha{\mathscr{M}}_{J}R\big(\lambda+\alpha d,\widehat{L}(0)\big)
is invertible and

(4.22)

\Big\|\Big(I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})}+\alpha{\mathscr{M}}_{J}R\big(\lambda+\alpha d,\widehat{L}(0)\big)\Big)^{-1}\Big\|\leq\frac{1}{\delta_{0}}.

From (
4
) and (
4.22
) we infer that
\lambda\in\rho\big(A_{\mathrm{RD}}(\alpha)\big)
and

\displaystyle\big\|R\big(\lambda,A_{\mathrm{RD}}(\alpha)\big)\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\big\|R\big(\lambda+\alpha d,\widehat{L}(0)\big)\big\|\,\Big\|\Big(I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}^{k})}+\alpha{\mathscr{M}}_{J}R\big(\lambda+\alpha d,\widehat{L}(0)\big)\Big)^{-1}\Big\|

(4.23)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\widetilde{M}}_{0}}{\delta_{0}(\mathrm{Re}\lambda+\alpha d)}\leq\frac{{\widetilde{M}}_{0}}{\delta_{0}(\mathrm{Re}\lambda+\delta_{0}\alpha d)}.

We conclude that

(4.24)

\sigma\big(A_{\mathrm{RD}}(\alpha)\big)\subseteq\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{Re}\lambda\leq-q(\alpha)\}\;\mbox{and}\;\big\|R\big(\lambda,A_{\mathrm{RD}}(\alpha)\big)\big\|\leq\frac{{\widetilde{M}}_{0}}{\delta_{0}(\mathrm{Re}\lambda+q(\alpha))}

whenever
\mathrm{Re}\lambda>-q(\alpha)
, where the function
q:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is defined by
q(\alpha)=\delta_{0}\alpha d
. Since
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\frac{\|E_{\mathrm{RD}}(\alpha)\|}{q(\alpha)}=\frac{\|D\|}{\delta_{0}d}
, Hypothesis (H3) is satisfied. From (
4.17
) one can readily check that Hypotheses (H4) and (H5) are satisfied.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section, Proposition
1.3
, assuming that
D\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k\times k}
is sufficiently close to
dI_{k}
in the sense that
\|D-dI_{k}\|<\frac{d}{{\widetilde{M}}_{0}}

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

First, we recall that
{\widetilde{M}}_{0}=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|e^{t\widehat{L}(0)}\|
.
From Lemma
4.3
and Theorem
1.1
it follows that the family of semigroups generated by
A_{\mathrm{RD}}(\alpha)
,
\alpha\geq 0
, is uniformly stable. Setting
\varkappa=\frac{1}{2}
in Theorem

we obtain that there exists a constant

such that

\|e^{tA_{\mathrm{RD}}(\alpha)}\|\leq\widehat{M}_{0}e^{-\frac{\delta_{0}\alpha d}{2}t}\;\mbox{for any}\;t\geq 0,\,\alpha\geq 0.

Using the identity

for any

, from (

) we infer that

(4.26)

Since
{\mathscr{L}}
, the linearization along the front

∎

5.
Applications to linear stability of planar fronts in the bidomain equation

In this section we give yet another application of our results to the Lypunov linear stability of planar traveling waves (fronts) in the bidomain Allen-Cahn model (
1.9
). In
[
16
]
the authors show that the model has
spectrally stable
planar traveling waves (fronts). Our goal is to show that the same condition also guarantees the Lyapunov linear stability of such fronts. Following
[
16
]
we consider equation (
1.9
) on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. It is well-known that (
1.9
) has planar traveling waves, that is, solutions of the form

(5.1)

(u,u_{i},u_{e})(x,t)=(\overline{w},\overline{w}_{i},\overline{w}_{e})(x_{1}\cos\gamma+x_{2}\sin\gamma-ct),\;x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},t\geq 0,

for some
c,\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Moreover, the profile
\overline{w}
can be chosen such that

(5.2)

\overline{w}\;\mbox{is decreasing},\;\lim_{s\to-\infty}e^{-\rho_{0}s}(\overline{w}(s)-1)=\lim_{s\to\infty}e^{\rho_{0}s}\overline{w}(s)=0,\;\mbox{for some}\;\rho_{0}>0.

Making in (
1.9
) the change of variables

(5.3)

y=\left(\begin{matrix}y_{1}\\
y_{2}\end{matrix}\right):=R_{-\gamma}\left(\begin{matrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{matrix}\right)-\left(\begin{matrix}ct\\
0\end{matrix}\right),\quad\mbox{where}\quad R_{\gamma}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\gamma&-\sin\gamma\\
\sin\gamma&\cos\gamma\end{bmatrix},

we obtain the system

(5.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\nabla_{y}\cdot(A_{i,\gamma}\nabla_{y}u_{i})+c\partial_{y_{1}}u+f(u),\\
\nabla_{y}\cdot(A_{i,\gamma}\nabla_{y}u_{i}+A_{e,\gamma}\nabla_{y}u_{e})=0,\\
u=u_{i}-u_{e}.\end{array}\right.t\geq 0,\;y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

Here, the symmetric, positive definite matrices
A_{i,\gamma}
and
A_{e,\gamma}
are defined by

(5.5)

A_{i,\gamma}=R_{\gamma}A_{i}R_{-\gamma},\quad A_{e,\gamma}=R_{\gamma}A_{e}R_{-\gamma}.

In the new coordinate system the front
(\overline{w},\overline{w}_{i},\overline{w}_{e})
travels along the
y_{1}
-axis and it is a standing wave solution of (
5.4
) depending only on
y_{1}
. In addition, the second and third equation of (
5.4
) are linear equations. Therefore, the linearization of (
5.4
) along the front
(\overline{w},\overline{w}_{i},\overline{w}_{e})
is given by

(5.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v_{t}=\nabla_{y}\cdot(A_{i,\gamma}\nabla_{y}v_{i})+c\partial_{y_{1}}v+f^{\prime}(\overline{w}(y_{1}))v,\\
\nabla_{y}\cdot(A_{i,\gamma}\nabla_{y}v_{i}+A_{e,\gamma}\nabla_{y}v_{e})=0,\\
v=v_{i}-v_{e}.\end{array}\right.t\geq 0,\;y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

Following
[
16
]
, we consider this system in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},{\mathbb{C}}^{3})
. Taking Fourier Transform in
y=(y_{1},y_{2})
, denoted by
{\mathscr{F}}
, we can eliminate the variables
v_{i}
and
v_{e}
from (
5.6
) to obtain the following equation

(5.7)

v_{t}={\mathscr{A}}v,\;t\geq 0,\;\quad\mbox{where}\quad{\mathscr{A}}=-{\mathscr{L}}_{\gamma}+c\partial_{y_{1}}+{\mathscr{M}}_{f^{\prime}(\overline{w})}.

The linear operator
{\mathscr{L}}_{\gamma}:H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
is defined as the Fourier multiplier

(5.8)

{\mathscr{L}}_{\gamma}={\mathscr{F}}^{-1}{\mathscr{M}}_{Q_{\gamma}}{\mathscr{F}},\;Q_{\gamma}(\xi)=\frac{Q_{i,\gamma}(\xi)Q_{e,\gamma}(\xi)}{Q_{i,\gamma}(\xi)+Q_{e,\gamma}(\xi)},\;Q_{i/e,\gamma}(\xi)=\xi^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot A_{i/e,\gamma}\xi,\;\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

We recall that the wave
\overline{w}
is called Lyapunov linearly stable if the semigroup generated by
{\mathscr{A}}
is bounded.

Taking Fourier Transform with respect to
y_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, denoted by
{\mathscr{F}}_{2}
, we note that the linear operator
{\mathscr{A}}
is unitary equivalent to
{\mathscr{M}}_{\widehat{A}}
, the operator of multiplication on

\widehat{A}:{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathscr{B}\big(H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big),\;\widehat{A}(\xi_{2})=-{\mathscr{F}}_{1}^{-1}{\mathscr{M}}_{Q_{\gamma}(\cdot,\xi_{2})}{\mathscr{F}}_{1}+c\partial_{y_{1}}+{\mathscr{M}}_{f^{\prime}(\overline{w})}.

Appendix A
Some Examples

In this section we give two examples to show that by perturbing an operator satisfying Hypotheses (H1) and (H2) we might have unstable spectrum, even if the space is finite dimensional and the perturbation is a self-adjoint, bounded, uniformly negative definite operator.

Example A.1
.

Let
J_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}
and
Z_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}1&b_{0}\\
b_{0}&\frac{1}{4}\end{bmatrix}
with
b_{0}\in\big(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1-\sqrt{2}}{2}\big)
.
One can readily check that

(A.1)

\Big\langle Z_{0}\left(\begin{matrix}u\\
v\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}u\\
v\end{matrix}\right)\Big\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}=|u|^{2}+2b_{0}\mathrm{Re}(u\overline{v})+\frac{1}{4}|v|^{2}\geq(|u|-b_{0}|v|)^{2}+\Big(\frac{1}{4}-b_{0}^{2}\Big)|v|^{2}\;\mbox{for any}\;u,v\in{\mathbb{C}}.

From (
A.1
) we infer that
Z_{0}
is a symmetric, positive definite matrix. Since
b_{0}\in\big(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1-\sqrt{2}}{2}\big)

This example shows that by perturbing an operator that satisfies Hypotheses (H1) and (H2) we might generate unstable point spectrum, even if the perturbation is symmetric, negative definite.

Generalized convergence of the deep BSDE method: a step towards fully-coupled FBSDEs and applications in stochastic control Journal: TBA
Balint Negyesi, Zhipeng Huang, Cornelis W. Oosterlee

Abstract
We are concerned with high-dimensional coupled FBSDE systems approximated by the deep BSDE method of Han et al. (2018). It was shown by Han and Long (2020) that the errors induced by the deep BSDE method admit a posteriori estimate depending on the loss function, whenever the backward equation only couples into the forward diffusion through the Y Y process. We generalize this result to drift coefficients that may also depend on Z Z , and give sufficient conditions for convergence under standard assumptions. The resulting conditions are directly verifiable for any equation. Consequently, unlike in earlier theory, our convergence analysis enables the treatment of FBSDEs stemming from stochastic optimal control problems. In particular, we provide a theoretical justification for the non-convergence of the deep BSDE method observed in recent literature, and present direct guidelines for when convergence can be guaranteed in practice. Our theoretical findings are supported by several numerical experiments in high-dimensional settings.

Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the numerical approximation of a system of coupled forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDE) over a finite time interval

\displaystyle\coloneqq b\left(t_{i},X_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1},Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1},Z_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1}\right)-b\big(t_{i},X_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2},Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2},Z_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2}\big),\delta\sigma_{i}\coloneqq\sigma\left(t_{i},X_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1},Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1}\right)-\sigma\left(t_{i},X_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2},Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2}\right),

(14)

\displaystyle\delta f_{i}

\displaystyle\coloneqq f\left(t_{i},X_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1},Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1},Z_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1}\right)-f\left(t_{i},X_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2},Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2},Z_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2}\right).

(15)

Then we have

\displaystyle\delta X_{i+1}

\displaystyle=\delta X_{i}+\delta b_{i}h+\delta\sigma_{i}\Delta W_{i},

(16)

\displaystyle\delta Y_{i}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}+\delta f_{i}h\right],

(17)

and (
9
) also gives

\delta Z_{i}=\frac{1}{h}\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\Delta W_{i}^{\top}\right].

(18)

By the martingale representation theorem, there exists an
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted square-integrable process
\left\{\delta Z_{t}\right\}_{t_{i}\leq t\leq t_{i+1}}
such that

\delta Y_{i+1}=\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]+\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\delta Z_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{t},

(19)

which, together with (
17
), implies

\delta Y_{i+1}=\delta Y_{i}-\delta f_{i}h+\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\delta Z_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{t}.

(20)

From (
16
) and (
20
), noting that
\delta X_{i},\delta Y_{i},\delta b_{i},\delta\sigma_{i}
and
\delta f_{i}
are all
\mathcal{F}_{t_{i}}
measurable, and
\mathbb{E}_{i}[\Delta W_{i}]=0
,
\mathbb{E}_{i}[\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}Z_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{t}]=0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{i+1}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{i}+\delta b_{i}h\right\|^{2}\right]+h\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta\sigma_{i}\right\|^{2}\right],

(21)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i+1}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i}-\delta f_{i}h\right\|^{2}\right]+\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Z_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}t,

(22)

where we also used a Fubini argument. We proceed in steps, controlling each of the terms above.

Step 1
.

Estimate for
\delta X_{n}
.

By assumptions
1
,
2
, and the root-mean-square and geometric mean inequality (RMS-GM inequality), we have that for any
\lambda_{1}>0

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{i+1}\right\|^{2}\right]\begin{aligned} &=\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta b_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]h^{2}+h\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta\sigma_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]\\
&+2h\mathbb{E}\left[\left(b\left(t_{i},X_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1},Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1},Z_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1}\right)-b\left(t_{i},X_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2},Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1},Z_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1}\right)\right)^{\top}\delta X_{i}\right]\\
&+2h\mathbb{E}\left[\left(b\left(t_{i},X_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2},Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1},Z_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1}\right)-b\left(t_{i},X_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2},Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2},Z_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2}\right)\right)^{\top}\delta X_{i}\right]\\
\end{aligned}\\
&\leq\begin{aligned} &\left(1+\left(2k^{b}+\lambda_{1}+L^{\sigma}_{x}+L^{b}_{x}h\right)h\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]\\
&+\left(\left(\lambda_{1}^{-1}+h\right)L^{b}_{y}+L^{\sigma}_{y}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]h+\left(L^{b}_{z}h+\lambda_{1}^{-1}L^{b}_{z}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Z_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]h.\end{aligned}\end{aligned}

(23)

Recalling the definition of
C_{1},K_{1},K_{2}
from (
10
), we subsequently gather

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{i+1}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq(1+K_{1}h)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]+K_{2}h\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]+C_{1}h\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Z_{i}\right\|^{2}\right].

(24)

Notice that
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{0}\right\|^{2}\right]=0
, and thus by induction, we have that for any
1\leq n\leq N
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{n}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(1+K_{1}h)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{0}\right\|^{2}\right]+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(1+K_{1}h)^{n-1-i}K_{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]h

(25)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(1+K_{1}h)^{n-1-i}C_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Z_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]h

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{2}h\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e^{K_{1}(n-i-1)h}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]+C_{1}h\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e^{K_{1}(n-i-1)h}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Z_{i}\right\|^{2}\right],

where we used the inequality
(1+x)\leq e^{x}
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}
.
We remark that due to the coupling of
Z
in the drift, the last term of the right-hand side above is not present in
[
24
]
.

Step 2
.

Estimate for
\delta Y_{n}
.

We employ a similar approach as in step 1. Using assumption
2
and the RMS-GM inequality, we obtain for any
\lambda_{2}>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i+1}\right\|^{2}\right]\begin{aligned} &\geq\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]+\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Z_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}t\\
&-2h\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f\left(t_{i},X_{i}^{1,\pi},Y_{i}^{1,\pi},Z_{i}^{1,\pi}\right)-f\left(t_{i},X_{i}^{1,\pi},Y_{i}^{2,\pi},Z_{i}^{1,\pi}\right)\right)^{\top}\delta Y_{i}\right]\\
&-2h\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f\left(t_{i},X_{i}^{1,\pi},Y_{i}^{2,\pi},Z_{i}^{1,\pi}\right)-f\left(t_{i},X_{i}^{2,\pi},Y_{i}^{2,\pi},Z_{i}^{2,\pi}\right)\right)^{\top}\delta Y_{i}\right]\end{aligned}\\
&\geq\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]+\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Z_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}t-2k^{f}h\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]\\
&-\left(\lambda_{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]+\lambda_{2}^{-1}\left(L^{f}_{x}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]+L^{f}_{z}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Z_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]\right)\right)h.\end{aligned}\end{aligned}

(26)

To deal with the integral term in the last inequality, we derive the following relation via Ito’s isometry, (
20
) and (
18
)

\delta Z_{i}=\frac{1}{h}\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\delta Z_{t}\mathrm{d}t\right].

(27)

Then, by the Jensen- and Cauchy-Schwartz inequalities and the Fubini theorem, we derive a lower bound for the integral term

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Z_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]h

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{q}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\delta Z_{i}\right)_{j,k}^{2}\right]h=\sum_{j=1}^{q}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{h}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\left(\delta Z_{t}\right)_{j,k}\mathrm{d}t\right]\right)^{2}\right]

(28)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{q}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{h}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\left(\delta Z_{t}\right)_{j,k}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{q}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\delta Z_{t}\right)_{j,k}^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}t=\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Z_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}t,

where
(\cdot)_{j,k}
denotes the
(j,k)
-entry of the matrix.
Combining (
26
) with (
28
) gives

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i+1}\right\|^{2}\right]\geq\begin{aligned} &\left(1-\left(2k^{f}+\lambda_{2}\right)h\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]+\left(1-L^{f}_{z}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Z_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]h\\
&-L^{f}_{x}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]h.\end{aligned}

(29)

Now, for any
\lambda_{2}>L^{f}_{z}\geq 0
, and sufficiently small
h
satisfying
\left(2k^{f}+\lambda_{2}\right)h<1
, this implies

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\left(1-\left(2k^{f}+\lambda_{2}\right)h\right)^{-1}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i+1}\right\|^{2}\right]+L^{f}_{x}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]h\right).

(30)

Recalling the definitions of
K_{3},K_{4}
in (
10
), we subsequently gather by induction that for any
0\leq n\leq N-1

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{n}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq e^{K_{3}(N-n)h}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{N}\right\|^{2}\right]+K_{4}\sum_{i=n}^{N-1}e^{K_{3}(i-n)h}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]h.

(31)

We remark that this estimate coincides with the one of
[
24
, Lemma 1]
.

∎

Due to the coupling of
Z
in the drift coefficient of the forward diffusion, we need an additional estimate to handle the extra
\mathbb{E}[\|\delta Z_{i}\|^{2}]
term in the estimate for
\mathbb{E}[\|\delta X_{i}\|^{2}]
. One of our main contributions is to establish the following lemma for this purpose.

Lemma 2
.

Under the setting of lemma
1
, for any
\lambda_{3}>2mL^{f}_{z}
and sufficiently small
h
, let us define

\displaystyle C_{2}\coloneqq 2((h+\lambda_{3}^{-1})L^{f}_{y}+\lambda_{3}),\quad C_{3}\coloneqq 2(h+\lambda_{3}^{-1}),\quad C_{4}\coloneqq(1-mC_{3}L^{f}_{z})^{-1}m.

(32)

Then we have
C_{4}>0
, furthermore, the following estimates also hold

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Y_{i}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1+C_{2}h)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]+C_{3}hL^{f}_{x}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta X_{i}\|^{2}\right]+C_{3}hL^{f}_{z}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Z_{i}\|^{2}\right],

(33)

\displaystyle h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}[\|\delta Z_{i}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}C_{2}h\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]+C_{3}hL^{f}_{x}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta X_{i}\|^{2}\right]\right)+C_{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Y_{N}\|^{2}\right].

(34)

Proof.

We take the squares of both sides of (
17
) and use the
\epsilon
-Young inequality to get

\left\|\delta Y_{i}\right\|^{2}\leq(1+\lambda_{3}h)\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right\|^{2}+(1+(\lambda_{3}h)^{-1})\left\|h\delta f_{i}\right\|^{2},

(35)

which holds for any
\lambda_{3}>0
, independent of
h>0
.
Taking expectations on both sides and using the Lipschitz continuity of
f
established by assumption
2
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1+\lambda_{3}h)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]+\left(h+\lambda_{3}^{-1}\right)h\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta f_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]

(36)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\lambda_{3}h)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]+(h+\lambda_{3}^{-1})h\left(L^{f}_{x}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta X_{i}\|^{2}\right]+L^{f}_{y}\mathbb{E}[\|\delta Y_{i}\|^{2}]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+L^{f}_{z}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Z_{i}\|^{2}\right]\right).

Therefore by a rearrangement

\displaystyle(1-(h+\lambda_{3}^{-1})hL^{f}_{y})\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle(1+\lambda_{3}h)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]

(37)

\displaystyle+(h+\lambda_{3}^{-1})hL^{f}_{x}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta X_{i}\|^{2}\right]+\left(h+\lambda_{3}^{-1}\right)hL^{f}_{z}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Z_{i}\|^{2}\right].

Consequently, for any
\lambda_{3}>0
and sufficiently small
h
, we obtain the following estimate, for
i=0,1,\ldots,N-1

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle(1+C_{2}h)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i}[\delta Y_{i+1}]\right\|^{2}\right]+C_{3}hL^{f}_{x}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta X_{i}\|^{2}\right]+C_{3}hL^{f}_{z}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Z_{i}\|^{2}\right],

(38)

where we used the definitions in (
32
). This proves (
33
).

Next, we derive the estimate for
Z
. Recalling the definition in (
18
), we get

\displaystyle h\delta Z_{i}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\Delta W_{i}^{\top}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\left(\delta Y_{i+1}-\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right)\Delta W_{i}^{\top}\right].

(39)

Taking the Frobenius norm on both sides and applying the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality then yields

\displaystyle h\left\|\delta Z_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\left(\delta Y_{i+1}-\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right)\Delta W_{i}^{\top}\right]\right\|

(40)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i+1}-\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\left\|\Delta W_{i}^{\top}\right\|^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i+1}-\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(hm)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which leads to

\displaystyle h\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Z_{i}\|^{2}\right]\leq m\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{i+1}-\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]=m\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Y_{i+1}\|^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]\right).

(41)

Summing both sides from
0
to
N-1
and using the estimate (
38
), we gather

\displaystyle h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Z_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq m\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Y_{i+1}\|^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]\right)

(42)

\displaystyle=m\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Y_{i}\|^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]\right)+\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Y_{N}\|^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\delta Y_{1}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq m\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Y_{i}\|^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]\right)+m\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Y_{N}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq m\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left(C_{2}h\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\delta Y_{i+1}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]+C_{3}hL^{f}_{x}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]+C_{3}hL^{f}_{z}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Z_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]\right)

\displaystyle+m\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Y_{N}\|^{2}\right].

Recalling
C_{4}
in (
32
), it is easy to check that for any
\lambda_{3}>2mL^{f}_{z}
and sufficiently small
h>0
, we have
C_{4}>0
and therefore

\displaystyle h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Z_{i}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left(C_{2}h\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i}[\delta Y_{i+1}]\right\|^{2}\right]+C_{3}hL^{f}_{x}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta X_{i}\|^{2}\right]\right)+C_{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Y_{N}\|^{2}\right].

(43)

∎

Remark 3
.

As shown in the proofs of lemma
1
and
2
, the constants

,

Theorem 3

(Convergence of the deep BSDE method)
.

Suppose assumptions
1
-
5

2

\displaystyle\bar{B}

\displaystyle\coloneqq e^{\max(-\bar{K}_{1}T,0)}L^{f}_{x}\bar{C}_{1}\bar{C}_{4}\bar{C}_{3}\frac{e^{\bar{K}_{1}T}-1}{\bar{K}_{1}}+e^{\max(-\bar{K}_{1}T,0)}\bar{C}_{1}\bar{C}_{4}L^{g}_{x}(1+\lambda_{4})e^{\bar{K}_{1}T},

(44)

\displaystyle\bar{A}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{aligned} &\left(L^{g}_{x}(1+\lambda_{4})e^{\left(\bar{K}_{1}+\bar{K}_{3}\right)T}+\frac{\bar{K}_{4}}{\bar{K}_{1}+\bar{K}_{3}}\left(e^{\left(\bar{K}_{1}+\bar{K}_{3}\right)T}-1\right)\right)\\
&\times\left(1-\bar{B}\right)^{-1}\left(\bar{K}_{2}\frac{1-e^{-\left(\bar{K}_{1}+\bar{K}_{3}\right)T}}{\bar{K}_{1}+\bar{K}_{3}}+e^{\max(-\bar{K}_{1}T,0)}\bar{C}_{1}\bar{C}_{4}\bar{C}_{2}\frac{1-e^{-\bar{K}_{3}T}}{\bar{K}_{3}}\right),\end{aligned}

\bar{K}_{j}\coloneqq\lim_{h\to 0}K_{j},\bar{C}_{j}\coloneqq\lim_{h\to 0}C_{j}

If

\displaystyle\inf_{\lambda_{1}>0,\lambda_{2}>L^{f}_{z},\lambda_{3}>2mL^{f}_{z},\lambda_{4}>0}\max(\bar{B},\bar{A})<1,

(46)

then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
\mathbb{E}[\|x_{0}\|^{2}],\mathscr{L},T,\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
,
\lambda_{3}

\lambda_{4}
, such that for sufficiently small

, it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(\mathbb{E}[\|X_{t}-\hat{X}_{t}^{\pi}\|^{2}]&+\mathbb{E}[\|Y_{t}-\hat{Y}_{t}^{\pi}\|^{2}]\big)+\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\|Z_{t}-\hat{Z}_{t}^{\pi}\|^{2}]\mathrm{d}t\leq C\big(h+\mathbb{E}[\|g\left(X_{T}^{\pi}\right)-Y_{T}^{\pi}\|^{2}]\big),\end{aligned}

(47)

where
\hat{X}_{t}^{\pi}\coloneqq X_{t_{i}}^{\pi},\hat{Y}_{t}^{\pi}\coloneqq Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi},\hat{Z}_{t}^{\pi}\coloneqq Z_{t_{i}}^{\pi}
for

.

Proof.

Let
X_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1}=X_{t_{i}}^{\pi},Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1}=Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi},Z_{t_{i}}^{\pi,1}=

Z_{t_{i}}^{\pi}
given by the Euler scheme (

), and
X_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2}=\bar{X}_{t_{i}}^{\pi},Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2}=\bar{Y}_{t_{i}}^{\pi},Z_{t_{i}}^{\pi,2}=\bar{Z}_{t_{i}}^{\pi}
given by implicit scheme (
7
). Both of them solve (
9
), and therefore we can apply lemma
1
to bound their differences. In what follows, we use the same notations as in the proof of lemma
1
.

First, using the RMS-GM inequality, for any
\lambda_{4}>0
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\delta Y_{N}\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\equiv\mathbb{E}\big[\|g(\bar{X}_{T}^{\pi})-Y_{T}^{\pi}\|^{2}\big]\leq(1+\lambda_{4}^{-1})\mathbb{E}\big[\|g(X_{T}^{\pi})-Y_{T}^{\pi}\|^{2}\big]+L^{g}_{x}(1+\lambda_{4})\mathbb{E}\big[\|\delta X_{N}\|^{2}\big].

(48)

Let

\mathcal{X}\coloneqq\max_{0\leq n\leq N}e^{-K_{1}nh}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta X_{n}\right\|^{2}\right],\qquad\mathcal{Y}\coloneqq\max_{0\leq n\leq N}e^{K_{3}nh}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta Y_{n}\right\|^{2}\right].

(49)

From estimate (

) in lemma

, we derive the following by multiplying with

\displaystyle e^{K_{3}nh}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Y_{n}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{K_{3}T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta Y_{N}\|^{2}\right]+K_{4}\sum_{i=n}^{N-1}e^{K_{3}ih}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta X_{i}\|^{2}\right]h

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{K_{3}T}\left(\left(1+\lambda_{4}^{-1}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\|g(X_{T}^{\pi})-Y_{T}^{\pi}\|^{2}\right]+L^{g}_{x}(1+\lambda_{4})\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta X_{N}\|^{2}\right]\right)+K_{4}\sum_{i=n}^{N-1}e^{K_{3}ih}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\delta X_{i}\|^{2}\right]h

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{K_{3}T}(1+\lambda_{4}^{-1})\mathbb{E}\left[\|g(X_{T}^{\pi})-Y_{T}^{\pi}\|^{2}\right]+\left(L^{g}_{x}(1+\lambda_{4})e^{(K_{1}+K_{3})T}+K_{4}\sum_{i=n}^{N-1}e^{(K_{1}+K_{3})ih}h\right)\mathcal{X},

where we used the definition of
\mathcal{X}

Maximizing over

Remark 5

In

(1)

such approach would change the probability measure under which

(2)

it is common that the reformulated FBSDE does not satisfy the theoretical assumptions needed for convergence while the original one does. For instance, a linear

in section

[

and it is applicable to a wider class of FBSDEs;

(3)

with the same approach, one could remove the entire drift to the driver, and simulate a reformulated FBSDE with zero drift, but this, for the two reasons above, is rarely done in practice.

Finally, let us derive a lower bound for
\bar{B}
in (
44
) by computing the infimum of
\bar{B}
over all possible choices of
\lambda
s. Notice that
\bar{B}
does not depend on
\lambda_{2}
, decreases in
\lambda_{3}
and increases in
\lambda_{4}
, and therefore we shall mainly look at
\lambda_{1}
. Let

\displaystyle\bar{B}_{\ell}\coloneqq\inf_{\lambda_{1}>0,\lambda_{3}>2mL^{f}_{z},\lambda_{4}>0}\bar{B}.

(78)

(1)

If
\lambda_{1}\geq\max(-2k^{b}-L^{\sigma}_{x},0)
, then
\bar{B}
admits a unique stationary point along the
\lambda_{1}
direction, and

\displaystyle\bar{B}_{\ell}=mL^{b}_{z}L_{x}^{g}e^{(2k^{b}+T^{-1}+L^{\sigma}_{x})T}T,\qquad\arg\inf_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4}}\bar{B}=(1/T,+\infty,0).

(79)

(2)

If
0<\lambda_{1}<\max(-2k^{b}-L^{\sigma}_{x},0)
, then
\bar{B}
is convex in
\lambda_{1}
but there is not stationary point in this range, and

\displaystyle\bar{B}_{\ell}=m\frac{L^{b}_{z}}{-2k^{b}-L^{\sigma}_{x}}L_{x}^{g},\qquad\arg\inf_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4}}\bar{B}=(-2k^{b}-L^{\sigma}_{x},+\infty,0).

(80)

This result is particularly useful in practice and can serve as a preliminary test for the convergence of a given FBSDE. In fact, given an equation and all its relevant Lipschitz constants, if we find
\bar{B}_{\ell}\geq 1
, then we know that the conditions of theorem
3
cannot be satisfied and that the deep BSDE algorithm is less likely to converge. On the other hand, if
\bar{B}_{\ell}
is less than 1 or in particular even close to 0, we may check if
\bar{A}<1
, which can be solved efficiently by a numerical constrained minimization method ranging different
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
,
\lambda_{3}
and
\lambda_{4}
.

6
Conclusion

In this paper a generalized proof for the convergence of the deep BSDE method was given. Our main contributions can be summarized as follows. We extended the convergence analysis of
[

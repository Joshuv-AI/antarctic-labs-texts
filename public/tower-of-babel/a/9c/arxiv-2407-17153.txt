Abstract
. A numerical semigroup S S is coated with odd elements (Coe-semigroup), if { x − 1 , x + 1 } ⊆ S \left\{x-1,x+1\right\}\subseteq S for all odd element x x in S S . In this note, we will study this kind of numerical semigroups. In particular, we are interested in the study of the Frobenius number, gender and embedding dimension of a numerical semigroup of this type.



1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{Z}}
be the set of integers an let
{\mathbb{N}}=\left\{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}\penalty\ |\penalty\ z\geq 0\right\}
.
A submonoid of
({\mathbb{N}},+)
is a subset of
{\mathbb{N}}
closed under addition, containing the zero element. A submonoid with finite complement in
{\mathbb{N}}
is a numerical semigroup.

A numerical semigroup,
S
, is coated with odd elements, if
\left\{x-1,x+1\right\}\subseteq S
for all odd element
x
in
S
. Henceforth known as
Coe-semigroup
, which will be the object of study in this work.

If
S
is a numerical semigroup, then
{\rm{m}}(S)={\rm min}(S\backslash\{0\})
,
{\rm F}(S)={\rm max}({\mathbb{Z}}\backslash S)
and
{\rm{g}}(S)
the cardinalty of
{\mathbb{N}}\backslash S
are three importants invariants of
S
known as
multiplicity
,
Frobenius number
and
gender
of
S
, respectively.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a nonempty subset of
{\mathbb{N}}
, we denote by
\left\langle\mathcal{A}\right\rangle
the submonoid of
({\mathbb{N}},+)
generated by
\mathcal{A}
, that is,

\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\,a_{i}\penalty\ |\penalty\ n\in{\mathbb{N}}\backslash\left\{0\right\},\penalty\ a_{i}\in\mathcal{A},\penalty\ \lambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}\penalty\ \text{for all}\penalty\ i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\right\}.

In
[
9
, Lemma
2.1
]
, we can see that
\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle
is a numerical semigroup if and only if
\gcd(\mathcal{A})=1
.

If
M
is a submonoid of
({\mathbb{N}},+)
and
M=\left\langle\mathcal{A}\right\rangle
then we say that
\mathcal{A}
is a system of generators of
M
. Moreover, if
M\neq\left\langle\mathcal{B}\right\rangle
for all
\mathcal{B}\varsubsetneq\mathcal{A}
, then we say that
\mathcal{A}
is a minimal system of generators of
S
. In
[
9
, Corollary 2.8]
, we see that, every submonoid of
({\mathbb{N}},+)
admits an unique minimal system of generators, which is finite. We denote by
{\rm msg}(M)
the minimal system of generators of
M
and its elements are called minimal generators. The cardinality of
{\rm msg}(M)
is called the embedding dimension of
M
and it is denoted by
{\rm{e}}(M)
.

The Frobenius problem (see
[
6
]
) consists of finding formulas for the Frobenius number and the gender of a numerical semigroup in terms of its minimal system of generators. This problem was solved in
[
11
]
, for numerical semigroups with embedding dimension two. At present, this problem is open for embedding dimension greater than or equal to three.

In
[
12
]
Wilf conjectured that if
S
is a numerical semigroup then
{\rm{e}}(S){\rm{g}}(S)\leq({\rm{e}}(S)-1)({\rm F}(S)+1)
. This question is still widely open and it is one of the most important problems in numerical semigroups theory. A very good source of the state of the art of this problem is
[
3
]
.

Denote by
\mathscr{C}=\left\{S\penalty\ |\penalty\ S\penalty\ \text{is an Coe-semigroup}\right\}
. In Section
2
, we will show that, if
S\in\mathscr{C}
then
S\cup\{{\rm F}(S),{\rm F}(S)-1\}\in\mathscr{C}
. This result will be used in Section 3, to order the elements of
\mathscr{C}
in a rooted tree.
We will characterize the sons of a vertex in this tree and this will allow us to give an algorithm procedure to obtain recursively the elements of
\mathscr{C}
.

In Section
4
, we will see that given
k
an odd positive integer, then
\mathscr{C}(k)=\left\{S\penalty\ |\penalty\ S\penalty\ \text{is an Coe-semigroup and}\penalty\ k\in S\right\}
is a finite set. Moreover, given
p
a positive integer,
\mathscr{C}\big(Frob\leq p\big)=\left\{S\penalty\ |\penalty\ S\penalty\ \text{is an Coe-semigroup with}\penalty\ {\rm F}(S)\leq p\right\}
and
\mathscr{C}\big(gen\leq p\big)=\left\{S\penalty\ |\penalty\ S\penalty\ \text{is an Coe-semigroup with}\penalty\ {\rm{g}}(S)\leq p\right\}
are also finite sets. Following the same line of previous section, we are going to order these three families of semigroups in a rooted tree.

An Coe-monoid is a submonoid of
({\mathbb{N}},+)
which can be expressed as intersection of Coe-semigroups. It is clear that, the intersection of Coe-monoids is an Coe-monoid. This allows us to introduce the smallest Coe-monoid containing a subset
X
of
{\mathbb{N}}
, denoted by
Coe(X)
. In Section
5
, we will show that a submonoid
M
of
({\mathbb{N}},+)
is an Coe-monoid if and only if either
M\subseteq\left\{2k\penalty\ |\penalty\ k\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
or
M
is an Coe-semigroup. We will give an algorithm to compute
Coe(X)
and will see that
Coe(X)
is an Coe-semigroup if and only if
X
contains at least an odd element.

In
[
9
, Proposition
2.10
]
, it is shown that if
S
is a numerical semigroup, then
{\rm{e}}(S)\leq{\rm{m}}(S)
.
We say that
S
has maximal embedding dimension (MED-semigroup) if
{\rm{e}}(S)={\rm{m}}(S)
. This class of numerical semigroups has been well studied in the semigroup literature (see for instance
[
2
]
). In Section
6
, we will study the Coe-semigroups which are MED-semigroups.

Finally, in Section
7
, we will study the Coe-semigroups with an unique odd minimal generator. We will show that this kind of numerical semigroups are equal to the set
\big\{T\penalty\ |\penalty\ T=2S\cup\big(\{2s+1\}+2S\big),\penalty\ S\penalty\ \text{is a numerical semigroup and}\penalty\ \{s,s+1\}\subseteq S\big\}
. We will give formulas for

,
{\rm{g}}(T)
and

as a function of
{\rm F}(S)
,
{\rm{g}}(S)
and

. From these results, we will prove that if

verifies the Wilf’s conjecture, then

also verifies the same conjecture. In addition, we will solve the Frobenius problem for Coe-semigroups with embedding dimension three.

2.
First results

Note that
{\mathbb{N}}
is an Coe-semigroup with
{\rm{m}}({\mathbb{N}})=1
and
{\rm F}({\mathbb{N}})=-1
. Hence, if
S
is a numerical semigroup and
S\neq{\mathbb{N}}
, then we let us consider that
{\rm{m}}(S)\in{\mathbb{N}}\backslash\{0,1\}
and
{\rm F}(S)\in{\mathbb{N}}\backslash\{0\}
.

Proposition 1
.

If
S
is an Coe-semigroup and
S\neq{\mathbb{N}}
, then
{\rm{m}}(S)
is an even integer and
{\rm F}(S)
is an odd integer.

Proof.

As
S\neq{\mathbb{N}}
, then
{\rm{m}}(S)\geq 2
and
{\rm F}(S)\geq 1
. If
{\rm{m}}(S)
is odd, then we get that
{\rm{m}}(S)-1\in S
, a contradiction. If
{\rm F}(S)
is even, then
{\rm F}(S)+1
is an odd element belongs to
S
. Hence, we obtain that
\big({\rm F}(S)+1\big)-1={\rm F}(S)\in S
, a contradiction again.
∎

Proposition 2
.

Let
S
be a numerical semigroup. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

S
is an Coe-semigroup.

(2)

\left\{x-1,x+1\right\}\subseteq S
for all odd element
x
in
{\rm msg}(S)
.

Proof.

1)\penalty\ \textit{implies}\penalty\ 2)
Trivial.

2)\penalty\ \textit{implies}\penalty\ 1)
Let
s
be an odd element in
S
. Clearly, there exists an odd element
x
in
{\rm msg}(S)
, such that
s-x\in S
. Then, we obtain that
s-1=x-1+s-x\in S
and
s+1=x+1+s-x\in S
and so
S
is an Coe-semigroup.
∎

Example 3
.

By applying Proposition
2
, we have that
S=\langle 4,6,7\rangle
is an Coe-semigroup, because
\left\{7-1,7+1\right\}\subseteq S
.

The next result has immediate proof.

Lemma 4
.

If
S
is a numerical semigroup such that
S\neq{\mathbb{N}}
, then
S\cup\{{\rm F}(S)\}
and
S\cup\{{\rm F}(S)-1,{\rm F}(S)\}
are also numerical semigroups.

Observe that, if
S
is an Coe-semigroup such that
S\neq{\mathbb{N}}
, then we have that
S\cup\{{\rm F}(S)\}
is not necessary an Coe-semigroup. In fact,
S=\left\{0,6,10,\rightarrow\right\}
is an Coe-semigroup, but
S\cup\{{\rm F}(S)\}=\left\{0,6,9,10,\rightarrow\right\}
is not an Coe-semigroup. In addition, if
S
is an Coe-semigroup then
{\rm F}(S)-1
may or may not belong to
S
. Indeed, we have that
S=\left\{0,6,10,\rightarrow\right\}
is an Coe-semigroup such that
{\rm F}(S)-1\not\in S
and
T=\left\{0,6,8,10,\rightarrow\right\}
is an Coe-semigroup such that
{\rm F}(T)-1\in T
.

Lemma 5
.

If
S
is an Coe-semigroup such that
S\neq{\mathbb{N}}
, then
S\cup\{{\rm F}(S)-1,{\rm F}(S)\}
is an Coe-semigroup.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
4
, we obtain that
S\cup\{{\rm F}(S)-1,{\rm F}(S)\}
is a numerical semigroup. We know that, by Proposition
1
,
{\rm F}(S)
is an odd integer. As
\{{\rm F}(S)-1,{\rm F}(S)+1\}\subseteq S\cup\{{\rm F}(S)-1,{\rm F}(S)\}
, we deduce that
S\cup\{{\rm F}(S)-1,{\rm F}(S)\}
is an Coe-semigroup.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
5
, we have all the ingredients needed to give a recursive way of calculating a sequence of Coe-semigroups. For a given an Coe-semigroup
S
:

•

S_{0}=S
,

•

S_{n+1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}S_{n}\cup\left\{{\rm F}(S_{n})-1,{\rm F}(S_{n})\right\}&\hbox{if }S_{n}\neq{\mathbb{N}}\\
{\mathbb{N}}&\hbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

The next result is trivial.

Proposition 6
.

If
S
is an Coe-semigroup, then there exists a sequence of Coe-semigroups,
S=S_{0}\subsetneq S_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq S_{k}={\mathbb{N}}
. Furthermore, the cardinality of
S_{i+1}\backslash S_{i}
is
1
or
2
for all
i\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,k-1\right\}
.

We will refer to the previous sequence as the chain of Coe-semigrops associated to
S
and
k
is the length of this chain, denoted by
l_{S}
.

The following result is easy to prove.

Proposition 7
.

Let
S
be an Coe-semigroup. The cardinality of the set
\left\{x\in{\mathbb{N}}\backslash S\penalty\ |\penalty\ x\penalty\ \text{is odd}\right\}
is equal to
l_{S}
.

Example 8
.

Clearly
S=\langle 6,8,13,14,15,17\rangle=\left\{0,6,8,12,\rightarrow\right\}
is an Coe-semigroup. The chain of Coe-semigrops associated to
S
is the following:
S=S_{0}\subsetneq S_{1}=\left\{0,6,8,10\rightarrow\right\}\subsetneq S_{2}=\left\{0,6,8,\rightarrow\right\}\subsetneq S_{3}=\left\{0,6,\rightarrow\right\}\subsetneq S_{4}=\left\{0,4\rightarrow\right\}\subsetneq S_{5}=\left\{0,2\rightarrow\right\}\subsetneq S_{6}={\mathbb{N}}
. The cardinality of
\left\{x\in{\mathbb{N}}\backslash S\penalty\ |\penalty\ x\penalty\ \text{is odd}\right\}=\left\{1,3,5,7,9,11\right\}
is

.

3.
The tree of Coe-semigroups

A graph
G
is a pair
(V,E)
where
V
is a nonempty set and
E
is a subset of
\left\{(u,v)\penalty\ |\penalty\ u,v\in V,u\neq v\right\}
. The elements of
V
and
E
are called vertices and edges, respectively. A path of length
n
connecting the vertices
u
and
v
of
G
is a sequence of
n
distinct edges of the form
(v_{0},v_{1}),(v_{1},v_{2}),\ldots,(v_{n-1},v_{n})
with
v_{0}=u
and
v_{n}=v
.

A graph
G
is a tree if there exists a vertex
r
(known as the root of
G
) such that for every other vertex
v
of
G
, there exists a unique path connecting
v
and
r
. If
(u,v)
is an edge of the tree then we say that
u
is a son of
v
.

Our main goal in this section will be to build the tree whose vertex set is
\mathscr{C}=\left\{S\penalty\ |\penalty\ S\penalty\ \text{is an Coe-semigroup}\right\}
.

We define the graph
G\big(\mathscr{C}\big)
as the graph whose vertices are elements of
\mathscr{C}
and
(S,T)\in\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}
is an edge if
T=S\cup\left\{{\rm F}(S)-1,{\rm F}(S)\right\}
.

As a consequence of Proposition
6
, we have the following.

Proposition 9
.

The graph
G\big(\mathscr{C}\big)
is a tree with root equal to
{\mathbb{N}}
.

It is clear that we can be build recursively the tree
G\big(\mathscr{C}\big)
, starting in
{\mathbb{N}}
and we connecting each vertex with its sons.
Hence, we need to characterize the sons of an arbitrary vertex of this tree.

Lemma 10

[

Let

S\backslash\{x\}

The next result is easy to prove.

Lemma 11

Let
S
be a numerical semigroup such that
S\neq{\mathbb{N}}
and
\{x,x+1\}\subseteq S

S\backslash\{x,x+1\}

Let

4.
Examples of finite trees

Given

If

5.
Coe-monoids

It is well known that the intersection of finitely many numerical semigroups is a numerical semigroup. Hence, the next result is easy to prove.

Proposition 28
.

The intersection of finitely many Coe-semigroups is an Coe-semigroup.

Note that the previous result does not hold for infinite intersections. In fact, by using Proposition
2
, we can deduce that
\langle 2,2k+1\rangle
is an Coe-semigroup for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\bigcap_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\langle 2,2k+1\rangle=\langle 2\rangle
is not a numerical semigroup.

Clearly, the intersection (finite or infinite) of numerical semigroups is a submonoid of
({\mathbb{N}},+)
. An Coe-monoid is a submonoid of
({\mathbb{N}},+)
which can be expressed as intersection of Coe-semigroups. Besides, the intersection of Coe-monoids is an Coe-monoid. In view of this, given
X
a subset of
{\mathbb{N}}
we can define the Coe-monoid generated by
X
as the intersection of all Coe-monoids containing
X
, denoted by
Coe(X)
. Then, we have that
Coe(X)
is the smallest Coe-monoid containing
X
.

The next result has immediate proof.

Proposition 29
.

If
X
is a subset of
{\mathbb{N}}
, then
Coe(X
) is the intersection of all Coe-semigroups that containing
X
.

If

is an Coe-monoid and

is a subset of
{\mathbb{N}}
such that
M=Coe(X)
, then we say that
X
is an coe-system of generators of
M
.

The following properties are a direct consequence of the definitions.

Lemma 30
.

Let

and

be subsets of

and let

be an Coe-monoid. Then the following conditions hold:

(1)

(3)

Let

In 1978, H. S. Wilf (see

Proposition 50
.

Let
S
be a numerical semigroup,
\left\{s,s+1\right\}\subseteq S
and
T=2S\cup\big(\{2s+1\}+2S\big)
. If
S
verifies the Wilf’s conjecture, then
T
also verifies the same conjecture.

Proof.

As
{\rm{n}}(T)={\rm F}(T)+1-{\rm{g}}(T)
, then by Proposition
48
, we have that
{\rm{n}}(T)=2{\rm F}(S)+(2s+1)+1-(2{\rm{g}}(S)+s)=2({\rm F}(S)+1-{\rm{g}}(S))+s=2{\rm{n}}(S)+s
. We have that
T
verifies the Wilf’s conjecture if

{\rm{g}}(T)\leq({\rm{e}}(T)-1)n(T)\Longleftrightarrow

2{\rm{g}}(S)+s\leq{\rm{e}}(S)(2{\rm{n}}(S)+s)\Longleftrightarrow

{\rm{g}}(S)\leq({\rm{e}}(S)-1){\rm{n}}(S)+{\rm{n}}(S)+\frac{({\rm{e}}(S)-1)s}{2}.

Since
S
verifies the Wilf’s conjecture, then
{\rm{g}}(S)\leq({\rm{e}}(S)-1)n(S)
,

and
\frac{({\rm{e}}(S)-1)s}{2}\geq 0
and therefore

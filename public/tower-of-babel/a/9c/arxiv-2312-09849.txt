On The Equivariant Tamagawa Number Conjecture
Samit Dasgupta, Mahesh Kakde, Jesse Silliman

Abstract




Abstract.

Let
F
be a totally real field and
K
a finite abelian CM extension of
F
. Using class field theory, we show that our previous result giving a strong form of the Brumer–Stark conjecture implies the minus part of the equivariant Tamagawa number conjecture for the Tate motive associated to
K/F
. We work integrally over
\mathbf{Z}
, in particular the prime 2 is not inverted. This note can be viewed as our perspective on the recent work of Bullach, Burns, Daoud, and Seo. Following their philosophy, we show that the functorial properties (i.e. norm compatibilities) connecting the strong Brumer–Stark conjecture for varying number fields actually implies the minus part of the
equivariant Tamagawa number conjecture.

1
Introduction

Let
F
be a totally real field and
K
a finite abelian CM extension of
F
. Write

G_{K}={\rm Gal}(K/F).

In a previous paper with Jiuya Wang
[
bsapet
]
, we proved refined versions of the Brumer–Stark conjecture for
K/F
. In this note, we consider the minus part of the Equivariant Tamagawa Number Conjecture for the Tate motive attached to
K/F
, and call this “the ETNC.” In a recent preprint
[
bbds
]
, Bullach, Burns, Daoud, and Seo develop the theory of
scarcity of Euler systems
and as a consequence prove that our results on the strong Brumer–Stark conjecture proved in
[
dk
]
actually imply the ETNC over
\mathbf{Z}[1/2]
. The purpose of this note is to give our perspective on this implication yielding a concise proof of the ETNC. We work integrally over
\mathbf{Z}
rather than
\mathbf{Z}[1/2]
. While we do not directly apply any results or arguments of
[
bbds
]
, our method is directly inspired by theirs—by passing to a suitable inverse limit, the discrepancy between strong Brumer–Stark and the ETNC becomes a non-zerodivisor and hence can be inverted.

We begin by stating the ETNC, following Kurihara’s formulation
[
kurihara
]
.
First we must set some notation.
Let
c\in G_{K}
denote the unique complex conjugation and define

\mathbf{Z}[G_{K}]_{-}=\mathbf{Z}[G_{K}]/(c+1).

For any
G_{K}
-module
M
, we write

M_{-}=M\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}[G_{K}]}\mathbf{Z}[G_{K}]_{-}=M/(c+1)M.

Let
S_{\infty}
denote the set of infinite primes of
F
, and let
S_{\text{ram}}(K/F)
denote the set of finite primes of
F
ramified in
K
.
Let

S=S(K)=S_{\infty}\cup S_{\text{ram}}(K/F).

Let
T
denote a finite set of primes of
F
, disjoint from
S(K)
, satisfying the usual condition of Deligne–Ribet.
Let
\nabla_{S}^{T}(K)
denote the Ritter–Weiss
G_{K}
-module defined in
[
dk
]
*Appendix A. This is a transpose of the Selmer group denoted

\mathcal{S}_{K,S}^{T}
in
[
kurihara
]
and
\mathcal{S}_{S,T}(\mathbb{G}_{m/K})
in
[
bks
]
.

The module
\nabla_{S}^{T}(K)
is the cokernel of a map

f_{S}^{T}(K)\colon V_{S^{\prime}}^{T}(K)\longrightarrow B_{S^{\prime}}(K),

(1)

where
S^{\prime}
is an auxiliary set of places containing
S
satisfying certain conditions, and
V_{S^{\prime}}^{T}(K)
and
B_{S^{\prime}}(K)
are free
\mathbf{Z}[G_{K}]
-modules of rank
(\#S^{\prime}-1)
. All of these objects are defined in §
2.1
.
For any CM subfield
E\subset K
containing
F
, the
G_{E}
-module
V_{S^{\prime}}^{T}(E)
may be canonically identified with the space of
{\rm Gal}(K/E)
co-invariants of
V_{S^{\prime}}^{T}(K)
, and similarly for the modules denoted
B
. Therefore, a choice of bases for the domain and codomain of
f_{K}=f_{S}^{T}(K)
naturally induces bases for the domain and codomain of
f_{E}=f_{S(E)}^{T}(E)
. Let
f_{K,-}
denote the map obtained from
f_{K}
after projecting to the minus side (i.e. tensoring with
\mathbf{Z}[G_{K}]_{-}
over
\mathbf{Z}[G_{K}]
).
The formulation of the minus part of the ETNC that we prove in this paper is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Fix bases for the domain and codomain of
f_{K}=f_{S}^{T}(K)
, thereby inducing bases for the domain and codomain of
f_{E}=f_{S(E)}^{T}(E)
for all CM fields
E\subset K
containing
F
.
There exists a unit
y_{K}\in(\mathbf{Z}[G_{K}]_{-})^{*}
such that

\det(f_{E,-})=\red_{K/E}({y}_{K})\cdot\Theta_{S(E),T}(E/F)\text{ in }\mathbf{Z}[G_{E}]_{-}

(2)

for all
E
, where
\red_{K/E}({y}_{K})
denotes the canonical reduction of
y_{K}
in
(\mathbf{Z}[G_{E}]_{-})^{*}
.

Here
\Theta_{S(E),T}(E/F)
is the usual Stickelberger element (see
[
bsapet
]
*§1).

1.1
Consequences

Before sketching our proof of Theorem
1
, we connect our formulation to others and describe some important consequences.

•

The statement of Theorem
1
is clearly equivalent to the projection to the minus side of Kurihara’s conjecture given in
[
kurihara
]
*Conjecture 3.4.

•

In
[
kurihara
]
*Proposition 3.5, Kurihara proves that the minus part of his statement is equivalent to the minus part of the “Leading Term Conjecture” (LTC) stated in
[
bks
]
. The statement of the LTC and a discussion of its relationship to Theorem
1
is given in §
A.3
.

•

Burns, Kurihara, and Sano prove that their LTC is equivalent to the original form of the ETNC stated by Burns and Flach in
[
bf
]
.

Theorem 1 is known to imply the following:

•

The Brumer–Stark conjecture and the higher rank Brumer–Stark conjecture due to Rubin
[
rubin
]
.

•

The integral Gross-Stark conjecture stated by Gross as a refinement to his
p
-adic Stark conjecture
[
gross
]
, and
the higher rank version stated by Popescu and called the Gross–Rubin–Stark conjecture
[
pcmi
]
*Conjecture 5.3.3.

•

The minus part of the “refined class number formula for
\mathbf{G}_{m}
” due independently to Mazur and Rubin
[
mr
]
and Sano
[
sano
]
.

In fact, using the refined construction of Ritter-Weiss modules at
p=2
given in our previous work
[
bsapet
]
, one obtains stronger versions of all of these results, where
\Theta_{S,T}
is replaced by

\begin{cases}\Theta_{S,T}/2^{n-2}&\text{ if }K/F\text{ is unramified at all finite places,}\\
\Theta_{S,T}/2^{n-1}&\text{ if }K/F\text{ is ramified at some finite place.}\end{cases}

This is discussed in Appendix
A.2
.

Finally, while a proof of the implication does not seem to appear in the literature, the ETNC should imply the Iwasawa Main Conjecture at
p=2
for totally real fields (see
[
atsuta
]
for the statement and discussion of the Main Conjecture at
p=2
).

1.2
Altering the smoothing and depletion sets

In the remainder of the introduction, we sketch the proof of Theorem
1
. As is well-known, one may prove the result prime by prime. In Appendix
A
we prove the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 2
.

In order to prove Theorem
1
, it suffices to prove the statement tensored with
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
(Theorem
3
below) for every prime
p
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
p
be a prime.
Fix notation as in Theorem
1
.
There exists a unit
y_{K}\in(\mathbf{Z}_{p}[G_{K}]_{-})^{*}
such that

\det(f_{E,-})=\red_{K/E}({y}_{K})\cdot\Theta_{S(E),T}(E/F)\text{ in }\mathbf{Z}_{p}[G_{E}]_{-}

for all
E
.

Note that we are working with all primes
p
, including
p=2
. In order to apply the results of
[
bsapet
]
, we must alter the smoothing and depletion sets to mirror the setting there. Define

\Sigma(K)=S_{\infty}\cup\{v\in S_{\text{ram}}(K/F)\colon v\mid p\},\quad\Sigma^{\prime}(K)=T\cup\{v\in S_{\text{ram}}(K/F)\colon v\nmid p\}.

(3)

Write
\Sigma=\Sigma(K),\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma^{\prime}(K)
.
As above, there are
\mathbf{Z}[G_{K}]
-modules
V_{S^{\prime}}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K)
and
B_{S^{\prime}}(K)
and a canonical map

f_{\Sigma}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K)\colon V_{S^{\prime}}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K)\longrightarrow B_{S^{\prime}}(K).

The Ritter–Weiss module
\nabla_{\Sigma}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K)
is defined to be the cokernel of the map
f_{\Sigma}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K).

If we note by a subscript
p
the tensor product with
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
, then we prove in
[
dk
]
that the
\mathbf{Z}_{p}[G_{K}]
-modules
V_{S^{\prime}}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K)_{p}
and
B_{S^{\prime}}(K)_{p}
are both free of rank

(\#S^{\prime}-1)
. Therefore
\nabla_{\Sigma}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K)_{p}
is quadratically presented, and we proved (in
[
dk
]
for
p
odd and in
[
bsapet
]
for
p=2
):

Theorem 4
.

We have

\Fitt_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}[G_{K}]_{-}}(\nabla_{\Sigma}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K)_{p,-})=(\Theta_{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}(K/F)).

(4)

The left side of (
4
) is by definition the image in
\mathbf{Z}_{p}[G_{K}]_{-}
of the ideal generated by the determinant of
f_{\Sigma}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K)_{p}
with respect to any choice of bases for its domain and codomain. As above, fixing such bases induces canonical bases for the domain and codomain of

f_{\Sigma(E)}^{\Sigma^{\prime}(K)}(E)_{p}\colon V_{S^{\prime}}^{\Sigma^{\prime}(K)}(E)_{p}\longrightarrow B_{S^{\prime}}(E)_{p}

for any CM subfield
E\subset K
containing
F
. Note that we have altered the depletion set
\Sigma
, but have intentionally left the smoothing set
\Sigma^{\prime}

and the auxiliary set
S^{\prime}
the same to ensure that the co-invariance property mentioned above holds. Our altered version of the
p
-part of the ETNC is the following.

Theorem 5
.

Fix bases for the domain and codomain of
f_{K}=f_{\Sigma}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K)_{p}
, thereby inducing bases for the domain and codomain of
f_{E}=f_{\Sigma(E)}^{\Sigma^{\prime}(K)}(E)_{p}
for all CM fields
E\subset K
containing
F
.
There exists a unit
y_{K}\in(\mathbf{Z}_{p}[G_{K}]_{-})^{*}
such that

\det(f_{E,-})=\red_{K/E}({y}_{K})\cdot\Theta_{\Sigma(E),\Sigma^{\prime}(K)}(E/F)\text{ in }\mathbf{Z}_{p}[G_{E}]_{-}

(5)

for all
E
.

In Appendix
A
, we show:

Lemma 6
.

Theorem
5
implies Theorem
3
.

1.3
Euler Systems

In view of the discussion above, to prove Theorem
1
it suffices to prove that Theorem
4
implies Theorem
5
.
Theorem
4
(or more precisely, an easily deduced corollary that replaces the smoothing set
\Sigma^{\prime}(E)
by
\Sigma^{\prime}(K)
) implies that for each
E\subset K
we have a unit
y_{E}\in(\mathbf{Z}_{p}[G_{E}]_{-})^{*}
such that

\det(f_{E,-})={y}_{E}\cdot\Theta_{\Sigma(E),\Sigma^{\prime}(K)}(E/F).

(6)

We need to show that the
y_{E}
can be chosen to equal
\red_{K/E}({y}_{K})
.
As we explain below, functorial properties imply that the
y_{E}
satisfy certain norm compatibilities, also known as
Euler system relations
.
To describe this, it is convenient to work for each
E
with the quotient of
\mathbf{Z}_{p}[G_{E}]_{-}
on which
\Theta_{\Sigma(E),\Sigma^{\prime}(K)}(E/F)
is a non-zerodivisor. To this end, define
R_{E}=e_{E}\cdot\mathbf{Z}_{p}[G_{E}]_{-}
, where

e_{E}=\prod_{v\in\Sigma(E)}(1-\mathrm{N}G_{E,v}/\#G_{E,v})

(7)

is the idempotent corresponding to the factor on which
\Theta_{\Sigma(E),\Sigma^{\prime}(K)}(E/F)
is supported. Here
G_{E,v}\subset G_{E}
denotes the
decomposition group at
v
, and

\mathrm{N}G_{E,v}=\sum_{\sigma\in G_{E,v}}\sigma

is its norm.
It suffices to prove (
5
) after projecting to
R_{E}
, since it is easy to show that the projection to
(1-e_{E})\mathbf{Z}_{p}[G_{E}]_{-}
of both sides of the equation vanish.
In §
3.1
, we prove:

Proposition 7
.

For every pair of CM fields
E^{\prime},E
such that
F\subset E\subset E^{\prime}\subset K
, we have

e_{E}(\red_{E^{\prime}/E}({y}_{E^{\prime}})-y_{E})P(E^{\prime}/E)=0\text{ in }R_{E},

(8)

where

P(E^{\prime}/E)=\prod_{v\in\Sigma(E^{\prime})-\Sigma(E)}(1-\sigma_{v}^{-1}).

(9)

The troublesome Euler factor
P(E^{\prime}/E)
, which can be a zerodivisor,
is all that prevents one from immediately deducing the ETNC. This leads to the following definition.

Definition 8
.

Consider a set of elements
(z_{E})_{E\subset K}
with
z_{E}\in R_{E}
, indexed by the CM fields
E\subset K
containing
F
. We say this family is
norm compatible
if for every pair of CM fields
E^{\prime},E
such that
F\subset E\subset E^{\prime}\subset K
, we have

(e_{E}\red_{E^{\prime}/E}(\tilde{z}_{E^{\prime}})-z_{E})P(E^{\prime}/E)=0\text{ in }R_{E}.

Here

\tilde{z}_{E^{\prime}}
denotes any lift of
z_{E^{\prime}}
to
\mathbf{Z}_{p}[G_{E^{\prime}}]_{-}
. While
\red_{E^{\prime}/E}(\tilde{z}_{E^{\prime}})
depends on the lift chosen (even its image in
R_{E}
depends on this choice), it is easy to check that
e_{E}\red_{E^{\prime}/E}(\tilde{z}_{E^{\prime}})P(E^{\prime}/E)
is independent of this choice, so the definition is well-formed.

With this definition, Proposition
7
states that
(e_{E}y_{E})_{E\subset K}
is a norm compatible family.

Definition 9
.

Suppose we are given a norm compatible set of elements
(z_{E})_{E\subset K}
, with
z_{E}\in R_{E}
. Let
K^{\prime}/F
be a CM abelian extension such that
K^{\prime}\supset K
. We say that the family
(z_{E})_{E\subset K}

can be extended to

K^{\prime}
if there exists a norm compatible family
(z_{E}^{\prime})_{E\subset K^{\prime}}
with
z^{\prime}_{E}\in R_{E}
such that
z^{\prime}_{E}=z_{E}
for
E\subset K
.

In the language of
[
bbds
]
, the result below describes the “scarcity of Euler systems.”

Proposition 10
.

Suppose we are given a norm compatible set of elements
(z_{E})_{E\subset K}
, with
z_{E}\in R_{E}
. Suppose that for each CM abelian extension
K^{\prime}/F
such that

K^{\prime}\supset K\quad\text{ and }\quad\Sigma(K^{\prime})=\Sigma(K),

the family
(z_{E})_{E\subset K}
can be extended to a norm compatible family
(z^{\prime}_{E})_{E\subset K^{\prime}}
with
z^{\prime}_{E}\in R_{E}
. Then there exists a lift
\tilde{z}_{K}
of
z_{K}
to
\mathbf{Z}_{p}[G_{K}]_{-}
such that

e_{E}\red_{K/E}(\tilde{z}_{K})=z_{E}\text{ in }R_{E}

for all
E\subset K
.

Proposition
10
is proven in §
3.2
. In Proposition
17
of §
3.1
, we will show that the norm compatible family
(e_{E}y_{E})_{E\subset K}
can indeed be lifted to an arbitrary CM abelian extension
K^{\prime}/F
containing
K
, and we also prove Proposition
7
. These statements together yield the desired result that Theorem
4
implies Theorem
5
.

2.1
Ritter–Weiss modules

In this section we let

denote an arbitrary Galois extension of number fields, with
G={\rm Gal}(K/F)
. Let
\Sigma
and

denote finite disjoint sets of places of

.
We impose the condition that

We assume in this paper that

2.2
Functorial Properties

We now recall some properties of the map
f_{\Sigma}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}
described above.
Let
E/F
denote any field extension contained in
K
.
Fix a prime
p
.
We prove in
[
dk
]
that if the sets of primes
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
satisfy certain conditions, then the modules
V_{S^{\prime}}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(E)_{p}
and
B_{S^{\prime}}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(E)_{p}
are free
\mathbf{Z}_{p}[G_{E}]
-modules of rank
(\#S^{\prime}-1)
.
After choosing bases, it therefore makes sense to consider the determinant of the map

f_{\Sigma}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(E)_{p}\colon V_{S^{\prime}}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(E)_{p}\longrightarrow B_{S^{\prime}}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(E)_{p}.

We now consider three specific cases, each of which satisfies the necessary conditions:

1.

f_{\Sigma}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(E)_{p}
, where
\Sigma=\Sigma(K),\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma^{\prime}(K)
are defined in (
3
).

2.

f_{\Sigma-\{v\}}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}(E)_{p}
, where
v\in\Sigma

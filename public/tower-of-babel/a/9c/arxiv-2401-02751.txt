1.
introduction

The theory of asymptotic prime divisors was initiated by Ratliff
[
19
]
. Brodmann
[
4
, p. 16]
showed that when
M
is a finite module over a Noetherian commutative ring
A
and
I
is an ideal of
A
, then the sequences of sets of associated prime ideals
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}(I^{n}M/I^{n+1}M)
and
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}(M/I^{n}M)
,
n=1,2,\ldots
are ultimately constant. Brodmann’s result has many applications and has been generalized in various forms. In
[
16
, Proposition 2]
, McAdam and Eakin proved that if
R
is a standard graded
Noetherian ring with
R_{0}=A
and
M
is a finitely generated graded
A
-module, then the set of
primes
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}(M_{n})
is stable for all large
n
. They also showed that Brodmann’s result followed
from their result concerning the stability of primes associated to homogeneous components of
graded modules.
Later Brodmann,
[
5
, Theorem 2(i) and 12(i)]
proved that if
J
is an ideal in
A
then
\operatorname{grade}(J,I^{n}M/I^{n+1}M)
and
\operatorname{grade}(J,M/I^{n}M)
stabilize. He used this result to give a refinement of an inequality due to Burch,
[
5
, Theorem 2(ii)]
. For a nice introduction to the theory of asymptotic primes see
[
17
]
.

In
[
18
, Theorem 1]
the authors prove that for each
i\geq 0
the sets

\operatorname{Ass}_{A}\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(M,A/I^{n})
and
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(M,I^{n}/I^{n+1})
stabilize. Later in
[
11
, 3.5]
the authors proved that for finitely generated
A
-modules
M,N
the sets
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(M,N/I^{n}N)
and
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(M,N/I^{n}N)
are stable.

In
[
14
, Theorem 2]
it was shown that if
A
is local and
\ell(M\otimes_{A}N)
is finite then the functions
n\rightarrow\ell(\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(M,N/I^{n}N))
and
n\rightarrow\ell(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,N/I^{n}N))
are of polynomial type. In
[
21
, Corollary 4]

the result was generalized by just assuming that
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(M,N/I^{n}N)
is of finite length for all
n
then the function
n\rightarrow\ell(\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A}(M,N/I^{n}N))
is of polynomial type (similar result holds for

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,N/I^{n}N)
).

Recently in a far-reaching generalization T.Se,
[
20
, 2.4, 2.7, 2.13]
, showed that if
F
is a coherent covariant
A
-linear functor from the category of finitely generated
A
-modules to itself the sets
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}(F(I^{n}M/I^{n+1}M))
,
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}(F(M/I^{n}M))
,and the values
\operatorname{grade}(J,F(I^{n}M/I^{n+1}M))
and
\operatorname{grade}(J,F(M/I^{n}M))
become independent of
n
for large
n
. We note that if
N
is a finitely generated
A
-module then the functors
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(N,-)
and
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(N,-)
are coherent for all
i\geq 0
; see
[
7
, 2.4, 2.5]
. In
[
3
, 3.3]
it is proved that if
F(M/I^{n}M)
has finite length for all
n
then
the function
n\rightarrow\ell(F(M/I^{n}M))
is of polynomial type.

In
[
10
, 2.1]
the authors proved that if
\mathcal{R}
is a standard graded subring of a polynomial ring
\mathcal{S}=A[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
with
\mathcal{R}_{0}=A
then the set
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}\mathcal{S}_{n}/\mathcal{R}_{n}
is stable for all
n\gg 0
.
In
[
8
, 1.1]
, this result was generalized as follows: Let
\mathcal{R},\mathcal{S}
be standard graded rings with
\mathcal{R}_{0}=\mathcal{S}_{0}=A
and we have an inclusion of graded rings
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
. Assume
M
is a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}
-module and
N
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{S}
module. If
M\subseteq N
as graded
\mathcal{R}
-modules then
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}N_{n}/M_{n}
is stable for
n\gg 0
. Furthermore if
J
is an ideal of
A
then
\operatorname{grade}(J,N_{n}/M_{n})
is constant for
n\gg 0
, see
[
8
, 3.1]
.

1.1
.

In view of
[
20
]
we may ask that if
F
is a coherent functor and
M,N
as above then

(1)

is

\operatorname{Ass}_{A}F(N_{n}/M_{n})
stable for
n\gg 0
?

(2)

if
J
is an ideal in
A
then is
\operatorname{grade}(J,F(N_{n}/M_{n}))
is constant for
n\gg 0
?

(3)

if
F(N_{n}/M_{n})
has finite length for all
n
then is
the function

n\rightarrow\ell(F(N_{n}/M_{n}))
of polynomial type?

The techniques in
[
20
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
8
]
do not generalize to deal with this case. In this paper we show all the above questions have a positive answer.

1.2
.

Our technique to prove the above assertions is categorical. Let
\mathcal{R}
be a standard graded ring with
\mathcal{R}_{0}=A
. We concoct a full subcategory
\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})
of
\operatorname{{}^{*}Mod}(\mathcal{R})
, the category of all graded
\mathcal{R}
-modules, with four properties:
if
X=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}X_{n}\in\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})
then

(1)

X_{n}
is finitely generated
A
-module for all
n
and
X_{n}=0
for
n\ll 0
.

(2)

\operatorname{Ass}_{A}X_{n}
is stable for all
n\gg 0

(3)

If
\ell(X_{n})
is finite for all
n
then the function
n\rightarrow\ell(X_{n})
is of polynomial type.

(4)

If
F
is a coherent functor on the category of finitely generated
A
-modules then
F(X)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F(X_{n})\in\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})
.

A formal consequence of the above four properties is that if
J
is an ideal of
A
then there exists
c_{J}^{X}
such that
\operatorname{grade}(J,X_{n})=\operatorname{grade}(J,X_{c_{J}^{X}})
for all
n\geq c_{J}^{X}
.
As a consequence we give an affirmative answer to all the questions in
1.1
.

1.3
.

We now describe the category that we are interested in.

Let
A
be a Noetherian ring and let
\mathcal{R}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{R}_{n}
be a standard graded ring with
\mathcal{R}_{0}=A
. Let
\operatorname{{}^{*}mod}(\mathcal{R})
denote the category of all finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}
-modules and let
\operatorname{{}^{*}Mod}(\mathcal{R})
denote the category of all graded
\mathcal{R}
-modules. Define
\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})
a full subcategory of
\operatorname{{}^{*}Mod}(\mathcal{R})
iteratively as follows:

Define
\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})(0)=\operatorname{{}^{*}mod}(\mathcal{R})
.

A graded
\mathcal{R}
-module
X\in\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})(1)
if there exists a standard graded
A
-algebra
S
with
R\subseteq S
(as graded rings) with
R_{0}=S_{0}
and there exists
M\in\operatorname{{}^{*}mod}(R)
and
N\in\operatorname{{}^{*}mod}(S)
with
M\subseteq N
(as graded
\mathcal{R}
-modules) and
X=N/M
. Clearly
\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})(0)\subseteq\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})(1)
.

A graded
\mathcal{R}
-module
Y\in\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})(2)
if there exists
X\in\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})(1)
and
D\in\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})(0)
such that there exists an exact sequence of graded
\mathcal{R}
-modules

0\rightarrow X\rightarrow Y\rightarrow D\rightarrow 0.

Clearly
\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})(1)\subseteq\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})(2)
. We define
\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})=\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})(2)
.

The main theorem of this paper is

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian ring and let
\mathcal{R}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{R}_{n}
be a standard graded ring with
\mathcal{R}_{0}=A
. Let
X=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}X_{n}\in\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})
. Then

(1)

X_{i}
is finitely generated
A
-module for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
X_{i}=0
for
i\ll 0
.

(2)

There exists
n_{0}
such that
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}X_{n}=\operatorname{Ass}_{A}X_{n_{0}}
for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

(3)

If
A
is local and
X_{n}
has finite length as an
A
-module for all
n
then there exists
P_{X}(z)\in\mathbb{Q}[z]
such that
P_{X}(n)=\ell_{A}(X_{n})
for all
n\gg 0
.

(4)

If
F
is a coherent functor on the category of finitely generated
A
-modules then
F(X)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F(X_{n})\in\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})
.

(5)

For an ideal
J
in
A
, there exists
c_{J}
such that

\operatorname{grade}(J,X_{n})=\operatorname{grade}(J,X_{c_{J}})
for all
n\geq c_{J}
.

We now describe in brief the contents of this paper. In section two we discuss some preparatory results on
\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{R})
that we need. In section three we recall the notion of coherent functors and prove that if

1.4
and give an affirmative answer to the questions raised in
1.1
.

3.
Coherent functors

Coherent functors in the general setting of abelian categories were introduced by Auslander,
[
2
]
. In our setting, coherent functors on the category of modules over a Noetherian commutative ring, an extensive treatment was made by Hartshorne,
[
7
]
.
Let us first recall the notion of coherent functors. Let
A
be a Noetherian ring and let
\operatorname{mod}(A)
denote the category of finitely generated
A
-modules.
Let
\mathfrak{F}
denote the category of all covariant
A
-linear functors from
\operatorname{mod}(A)
to
\operatorname{mod}(A)
. Morphisms in
\mathfrak{F}
are given by natural transformation of functors. It is easily proved that
\mathfrak{F}
is an abelian category. Let
U\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
. Set
h_{U}(-)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(U,-)
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}
.

(1)

F
is said to be representable if
F\cong h_{U}
for some
U\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
.

(2)

F
is said to be coherent if there exists an exact sequence of functors

h_{U}\rightarrow h_{V}\rightarrow F\rightarrow 0.

Remark 3.2
.

A natural transformation of
A
-linear functors
h_{U}\rightarrow h_{V}
is induced by a unique
A
-linear map
V\rightarrow U
, see
[
7
, 1,2]
.

3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{R}_{n}
be a standard graded ring with
\mathcal{R}_{0}=A
. Let
X=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}X_{n}\in\operatorname{{}^{*}Mod}(\mathcal{R})
. Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}
. Set
F(X)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F(X_{n})
. We note that if
U\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
then
h_{U}(X)
is an
\mathcal{R}
-module. Furthermore if

is a natural transformation where

and
\theta

then
\theta^{*}_{X}\colon h_{U}(X)\rightarrow h_{V}(X)
is
\mathcal{R}
-linear. It follows that if
F\in\mathfrak{F}
is coherent then

is an
\mathcal{R}
-module.

3.4
.

(with hypotheses as in

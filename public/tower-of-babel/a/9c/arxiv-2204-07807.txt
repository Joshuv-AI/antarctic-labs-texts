Deformation of rigid Galois representations and cohomology of certain quaternionic unitary Shimura variety
Haining Wang

Abstract
. In this article, we use deformation theory of Galois representations valued in the symplectic group of degree four to prove a freeness result for the cohomology of certain quaternionic unitary Shimura variety over the universal deformation ring for certain type of residual representation satisfying a property called rigidity. This result plays an important role in the proof of the arithmetic level raising theorem for the symplectic similitude group of degree four over the field of rational numbers by the author.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathrm{F}
be a totally real field. In this article, we study deformations of Galois representations of
\mathrm{F}
valued in
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}

This quaternionic unitary Shimura variety is closely related to the classical Hilbert-Siegel modular variety. Their relation is similar to that of classical modular curves with Shimura curves associated to indefinite quaternion algebras. The deformation problem we imposed on the residual representation is tailored to have applications in proving the arithmetic level raising theorem in

\rho_{\pi,\lambda}:G_{\mathrm{F}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(E_{\lambda})

up to conjugation, such that for every isomorphism
\iota_{l}:\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{\sim}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
inducing the prime
\lambda
, the representations
\rho_{\pi,\lambda}\otimes_{E_{\lambda}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
and
\rho_{\pi,\iota}
are conjugate.

We fix such a strong coefficient field
E
for
\pi
. We assume that
\rho_{\pi,\iota_{l}}
is absolutely irreducible and so is
\rho_{\pi,\lambda}
. Therefore we can consider the residual Galois representation

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}:G_{\mathrm{F}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(k).

Let
\Sigma
be the smallest set containing
\Sigma_{\mathrm{bad}}
such that
\pi
is unramified for every non-archimedean place
v
of
\mathrm{F}

not in it. We will establish that
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is rigid under suitable hypothesis. Before that we need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
v
be a non-archimedean place of
\mathrm{F}
and suppose that
l\nmid\prod^{4}_{i=1}q^{i}_{v}-1
. Let
A\in\mathrm{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
and
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are two liftings of
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
valued in
A
. If
\pi_{1,v}
and
\pi_{2,v}
are two irreducible admissible representation of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathrm{F}_{v})
giving the the representations
\rho_{1,v}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
and
\rho_{2,v}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
via the local Langlands correspondence. Then
\pi_{1,v}
and
\pi_{2,v}
are in the same Bernstein component.

Proof.

Let
\Pi_{1,v}
and
\Pi_{2,v}
be the transfer of
\pi_{1,v}
and
\pi_{2,v}
as representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathrm{F}_{v})
. Then
\Pi_{1,v}
and
\Pi_{2,v}
lie on the same Bernstein component by
[
LTXZZa
, Corollary 3.4.10]
. It then follows that
\pi_{1,v}
and
\pi_{2,v}
are in the same Bernstein component as well.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Suppose
\pi
is a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{F}})
with trivial central character of weight
(k_{v},l_{v})_{v\mid\infty}
where
k_{v},l_{v}\geq 3
and
k_{v}\equiv l_{v}\mod 2
for all
v\mid\infty
. We assume that the image
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}(G_{\mathrm{F}})
contains
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{l})
for sufficiently large
l
. Then the representation
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is rigid for the pair
(\Sigma,\emptyset)
for infinitely many choices of
l
.

Proof.

To establish that
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is rigid, we need to establish the four conditions in Definition
4.1
. We begin by choose
l
such that

(1)

\Sigma_{l}\cap\Sigma=\emptyset
;

(2)

l\geq k_{v}+l_{v}-1
for all
v\mid\infty
;

(3)

l\geq q^{4}_{v}
for all
v\in\Sigma
.

The all four conditions but
(1)
is clear for
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
by Theorem
5.1
. We need to verify
(1)
for it. Let
\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}
be a collection
\{\mathcal{D}_{v},v\in\Sigma\}
be a collection of local deformation problems, each of which is an irreducible component of
\mathrm{R}^{\square}_{v}
for
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}|_{G_{\mathrm{F}_{v}}}
for
v\in\Sigma
. Consider the following global deformation problem

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma})=(\overline{\rho}_{{\pi},\lambda},\epsilon^{-3}_{l},\Sigma\cup\Sigma_{l},\{\mathcal{D}_{v}\}_{v\in\Sigma\cup\Sigma_{l}})

where
\mathcal{D}_{v}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}
for
v\in\Sigma
and
\mathcal{D}_{v}=\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{FL}}_{v}
for
v\in\Sigma_{l}
. By
[
GG12
, Corollary 7.5.1]
, the universal deformation ring
\mathrm{R}^{\mathrm{univ}}_{\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma})}
is finite over
\mathcal{O}
of rank at least
1
. In particular, we know
\mathrm{R}^{\mathrm{univ}}_{\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma})}[1/l]
is non-zero. A
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
point of
\mathrm{Spec}\phantom{.}\mathrm{R}^{\mathrm{univ}}_{\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma})}[1/l]
gives rise to cuspidal representation
\pi(\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma})
of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{F}})
such that

•

\pi(\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma})
is unramified away from
\Sigma
;

•

There is an open compact subgroup
\mathrm{K}_{v}=\mathrm{K}_{v}(\pi_{v})
depending only on
\pi_{v}
such that
\pi(\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma})^{\mathrm{K}_{v}}_{v}\neq 0
;

•

The weights
(k_{v},l_{v})_{v\mid\infty}
are cohomological in the Fontaine-Laffaille range:
k_{v}\geq l_{v}\geq 3
and
k_{v}+l_{v}\leq l+1
.

•

The representations
\rho_{\pi(\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}),\iota_{l}}
and
\rho_{\pi,\lambda}
are residually isomorphic.

Note the second property is a direct consequence of Lemma
5.4
and the fact that representations on the same Bernstein component has the same level. It is clear that there are only finitely many cuspidal representations of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{F}})
of general type up to isomorphism satisfying the first three properties. Finally by strong multiplicity one for representations of general type, we can choose
l
large enough so that
\pi
itself is the unique cuspidal representations of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{F}})
of general type satisfying all
4
properties.

Suppose we have two different collections
\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}_{\Sigma}
of local deformation problems. We need to show that
\pi(\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma})
and
\pi(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}_{\Sigma})
are not isomorphic. For this, we will show that
\pi_{v}
for each
v\in\Sigma
gives rise to a smooth point on
\mathrm{Spec}\phantom{.}\mathrm{R}^{\square}_{v}[1/l]
and hence can not lie on two different irreducible components. This follows from
[
BCGP
, Lemma 7.1.3]
in the usual manner as
\pi_{v}
gives rise to a pure local Galois representation. It follows that if
l
is large enough as above, then
\mathcal{D}_{v}=\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{min}}_{v}
for all
v\in\Sigma
and hence
(1)
of Definition
4.1
is verified. Thus
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is rigid.
∎

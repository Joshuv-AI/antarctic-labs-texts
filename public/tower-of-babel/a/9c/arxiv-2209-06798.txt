Lifting N ∞ N_{\infty} operads from conjugacy data
Scott Balchin, Ethan MacBrough, Kyle Ormsby

Abstract
. We isolate a class of groups — called lossless groups — for which homotopy classes of G G - N ∞ N_{\infty} operads are in bijection with certain restricted transfer systems on the poset of conjugacy classes Sub ⁡ ( G ) / G \operatorname{Sub}(G)/G .



1.
Introduction

The concept of an
N_{\infty}
operad, as introduced by Blumberg–Hill in
[
BH15
]
, provides an equivariant analogue of
E_{\infty}
operads which captures multiplicative norm maps on equivariant commutative ring spectra. Further work by various authors proved that homotopy category of
N_{\infty}
operads can be identified with far simpler structures called indexing systems
[
BP21
,
GW18
,
Rub21a
]
. This was then distilled into the identification of
transfer systems
which are purely combinatorial representations of
N_{\infty}
operads on the subgroup lattice of the group in question
[
BBR21
,
Rub21b
]
. This has led to computable approaches to understand the structures of
N_{\infty}
operads for a given group.

In
[
BBR21
]
, the collection of
N_{\infty}
operads for the cyclic groups
C_{p^{n}}
were classified via the use of transfer systems on the lattice
\operatorname{Sub}(C_{p^{n}})\cong[n]
. This concept was abstracted in
[
FOO+22
]
where the notion of transfer systems was developed for an arbitrary finite poset, and shown in particular to be in bijection with weak factorization systems when the poset was moreover a complete lattice. This approach has already provided fruitful results such as classifications of model structures on total orders in
[
BOOR22
]
.

This abstraction provides a strict generalization. Indeed, in
[
Jak74
]
, those lattices
L
which arise as
\operatorname{Sub}(G)
for an arbitrary group
G
are classified (this was refined to the Abelian case in
[
Con11
]
). For example, the lattice in
Figure 1
does not appear as
\operatorname{Sub}(G)
for any
G
.

Figure 1.
A lattice which is not of the form
\operatorname{Sub}(G)
for any
G
.

In this paper, we move back to the roots of the classification of
N_{\infty}
operads, with a view to improve the computational tools available. In particular, we will make the first serious foray into the realm of non-Abelian groups. In the definition of
N_{\infty}
operads, conjugation must be taken into account. One may naively hope that this is superfluous, and that one can just work with transfer systems on the poset
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
, that is, the collection of subgroups up to conjugacy. This, however, does not work in general.

Indeed, this is already noted in an example of Rubin
[
Rub21b
]
, as we now recall. If one considers the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
, then there are three conjugate copies of
D_{4}
(the dihedral group of order
8
) living inside it. Moreover, there are three double-transpositions in
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
which generate three conjugate copies of
C_{2}
. It follows that to define a transfer system for
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
it is not enough to just declare that we have the relation
C_{2}\,\mathcal{R}\,D_{4}
, we must also keep track of which copies of
C_{2}
are related to which copies of
D_{4}
, something that is lost when working up to conjugacy.

There are cases, however, where it is possible to work up to conjugacy; again, such an example is observed by Rubin
[
Rub21b
]
. Moving down in the world of symmetric groups, consider
G=\mathfrak{S}_{3}
. The subgroup lattice here takes the form displayed in
Figure 2
.

Figure 2.
The lattice
\operatorname{Sub}(G)
for
G=\mathfrak{S}_{3}
. A red node is a copy of
C_{2}
, while the blue node is a copy of
C_{3}
.

The key point is that we can treat all copies of
C_{2}
as essentially being the same subgroup. We refer the reader to
Section 2.1
for the definition of a transfer system for what follows. Suppose that we have a transfer system with the relation
1\,\mathcal{R}\,\langle(12)\rangle
. As transfer systems are required to be closed under conjugation, this implies that we necessarily have
1\,\mathcal{R}\,\langle\tau\rangle
for every transposition
\tau
. Dually if we have
\langle(12)\rangle\,\mathcal{R}\,\mathfrak{S}_{3}
then we also have
\langle\tau\rangle\,\mathcal{R}\,\mathfrak{S}_{3}
for every transposition
\tau
. Next, we use that fact that in a transfer system if we have
H\,\mathcal{R}\,K
and
L\,\mathcal{R}\,K
then we also have
(H\cap L)\,\mathcal{R}\,K
. In particular, if we remember that we really have three distinct copies of
C_{2}
, it follows that if we have
\langle(12)\rangle\,\mathcal{R}\,\mathfrak{S}_{3}
, then we also have
\langle(23)\rangle\,\mathcal{R}\,\mathfrak{S}_{3}
, and as such we have
1\,\mathcal{R}\,\mathfrak{S}_{3}
by this intersection property.

It turns out that this is the only condition that one needs to impose in this case. So we may do exactly as we want, study transfer systems on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
, which is of the form
Figure 3

The goal of this paper is to isolate a class of groups for which
G
-transfer systems can be explicitly characterized as certain restricted transfer systems on the poset
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
in this fashion. The class of groups that we will isolate here are the
lossless groups
(see

). Not only does this provide a non-trivial structural result, it also equips us with powerful computational tools to classify
N_{\infty}
operads for a wide range of non-Abelian groups, and provides a better conceptual understanding of the structures involved.

For example, let us assume that we wish to study the collection of
G
-
N_{\infty}
operads for
G=D_{9}
. The lattice that we need to consider,
\operatorname{Sub}(D_{9})
, is displayed in
Figure 4
.

for

. The colored nodes in the rows indicate different conjugacy classes of subgroups.

Hoping to find patterns or structure on this lattice is a daunting task. However, the results presented here will allow us to instead explore structure in the much more manageable — and human friendly —
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
as displayed in
Figure 5
. In
[
BMO22
]
we shall undertake this exploration, and provide a recursive algorithm for computing
N_{\infty}
operads for the dihedral groups
D_{p^{n}}
for all

(

).

In
Section 2
we will introduce the main object of study of this paper, the
lossless groups
, and prove that there is a bijection between
G
-transfer systems for a lossless group
G
and
liftable transfer systems
on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
. We record the main theorem here.

Theorem

(
Section 2.1
)
.

Let
G
be a lossless group. Then there is a bijection between homotopy classes of
G
-
N_{\infty}
operads and liftable transfer systems on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
.

In fact, in a sense made precise in
Section 2
, the above theorem characterizes lossless groups.

We then continue in
Section 2.2
and prove that many groups of interest are in fact lossless. In
Section 3
we direct our attention to a particularly nice class of lossless groups, namely the
metacyclic Frobenius groups
. For these groups we have a detailed understanding of both the form of
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
and the lifting conditions required to determine a
G
-transfer system. We apply this theory in
Section 4
to demonstrate how the theory aids computations. Finally, in
Section 5
we outline a potential strategy for dealing with lossy groups. In particular we shall focus on the case of
G=SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
which is an important family of groups in the study of topological modular forms with level structures
[
HL16
]
.

Conventions

Throughout, we shall use the following conventions for group theory:

•

H\leqslant G
designates
H
as a subgroup of
G
.

•

N\trianglelefteqslant G
designates
N
as a normal subgroup.

•

If
g\in G
then
{}^{g}H:=gHg^{-1}
(thus
{}^{g}({}^{h}H)={}^{(gh)}H
for all
g,h\in G
).

•

For
G
a group we write
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
for the poset of conjugacy classes of subgroups of
G
.

•

For
n>2
, we write
D_{n}
for the dihedral group of order
2n
.

2.
Transfer systems on lossless groups

In this section we will introduce the class of
lossless groups
, which allow us to study
G
-transfer systems using only categorical transfer systems on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
. After proving the basic structural results about these groups, we identify several simple criteria for when a group is lossless, and also provide several examples of how groups can fail to be lossless.

2.1.
General results

We first recall the definitions of
G
-transfer systems and categorical transfer systems. We refer the reader to
[
BBR21
,
FOO+22
]
for further details.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group. A (
G
-)
transfer system
is a relation
\mathcal{R}
on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)
refining inclusion satisfying the following:

•

(reflexivity)
H\,\mathcal{R}\,H
for all
H\leqslant G
;

•

(transitivity)
K\,\mathcal{R}\,H
and
L\,\mathcal{R}\,K
implies
L\,\mathcal{R}\,H
;

•

(closed under conjugation)
K\,\mathcal{R}\,H
implies that
{}^{g}K\,\mathcal{R}\,{}^{g}H
for all
g\in G
;

•

(closed under restriction)
K\,\mathcal{R}\,H
and
L\leqslant H
implies
(K{\cap}L)\,\mathcal{R}\,L
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
BBR21
]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Then there is a bijection between the set of transfer systems on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)
and the set of
G
-
N_{\infty}
operads.

In
[
FOO+22
]
, a notion of an abstract categorical transfer system was introduced for an arbitrary poset, but with a particular focus on when the poset in question is a lattice. In fact, despite presenting the definition for general posets, the authors only make serious use of their definition in the setting of lattices. Since
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
may not be a lattice when
G
is non-commutative, we are forced to think seriously about more general posets. The definition we present below is
not
equivalent to the definition given in
[
FOO+22
]
, since we require restriction closure for arbitrary maximal lower bounds, rather than just when a unique meet exists. However, it is straightforward to verify the two definitions coincide when the poset is a lattice. For an element
x
in some poset
\mathcal{P}
, let
x^{\downarrow}
denote the down-set of
x
in
\mathcal{P}
, i.e. the set of all
y\leqslant x
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P},\leqslant)
be a poset. A (categorical)
transfer system
on
\mathcal{P}
consists of a partial order
\mathcal{R}
on
\mathcal{P}
that refines
\leqslant
and such that whenever
x\,\mathcal{R}\,y
and
z\leqslant y
, then for all maximal
w\in x^{\downarrow}\cap z^{\downarrow}
we have
w\,\mathcal{R}\,z
.

Remark 2.4
.

Using this definition, Theorem 4.13 in
[
FOO+22
]
(categorical transfer systems on a lattice are in natural bijection with weak factorization systems) can be generalized to arbitrary posets. This gives some evidence that our definition is the “morally correct” one for non-lattice posets. More pragmatically, the stronger definition is necessary to make
Section 3
work.

Warning 2.5
.

Until this point in the literature, only transfer systems on Abelian groups have been seriously considered. In this case,
G
-transfer systems are in bijection with categorical transfer systems on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)\cong\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
, and as such there is no distinction to be made. We are primarily concerned with non-Abelian groups in this paper, and as such, one needs to be careful what they mean.

We now introduce the notion of a lossless group. This definition was isolated to capture exactly the groups needed for our applications, and we have been unable to find this class of groups studied previously in the literature.

Definition 2.6
.

A
lossless group
is a group
G
such that for all pairs of subgroups
K\leqslant H
such that
{}^{g}K\leqslant H
for some
g\in G
, there exists some
h\in N_{G}(H)
such that
{}^{h}K={}^{g}K
. A group which is not lossless will be called
lossy
.

Remark 2.7
.

An equivalent succinct way of phrasing this definition is that for all
H\leqslant G
, the fusion of subgroups of
H
is controlled by
N_{G}(H)
. Note that we do not require fusion to be controlled on elements as is common in group theoretic literature. This corresponds in the previous definition to the fact that we only require
h^{-1}g\in N_{G}(K)
, rather than requiring
h^{-1}g\in C_{G}(K)
.

In the following definition,
[H]
denotes the conjugacy class of a subgroup
H\leqslant G
. We also adopt the notational convention of writing
K\to H\in\mathcal{R}
as shorthand for
K\,\mathcal{R}\,H
; we find this notational flexibility useful, especially as the notation for a given transfer system becomes more complex.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
G
be a group and
\pi\colon\operatorname{Sub}(G)\to\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
be the quotient map of posets.

•

For a categorical transfer system
\mathcal{R}
on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
, we define
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R})
to be the relation on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)
such that
K\to H\in\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R})
if and only if
[K]\to[H]\in\mathcal{R}
. We then define
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{R})
to be the
G
-transfer system generated by
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R})
.

•

For a
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{R}
, we define
\pi_{*}(\mathcal{R})
to be the relation on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
where
[K]\to[H]\in\pi_{*}(\mathcal{R})
if and only if there exist some pair of subgroups
K^{\prime}\leqslant H^{\prime}
with
[K]=[K^{\prime}]
and
[H]=[H^{\prime}]
, such that
K^{\prime}\to H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
.

We start with an observation that we can push any
G
-transfer system to a categorical transfer system on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
provided that
G
is lossless.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
G
be a lossless group. Then for all
G
-transfer systems
\mathcal{R}
,
\pi_{*}(\mathcal{R})
is a categorical transfer system on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=\pi_{*}(\mathcal{R})
. Suppose
[K]\,\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\,[H]\,\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\,[L]
and let there be lifts (i.e., representatives in the conjugacy class)
K^{\prime}\,\mathcal{R}\,H^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime\prime}\,\mathcal{R}\,L^{\prime}
. Then
[H^{\prime}]=[H]=[H^{\prime\prime}]
implies we can find
g\in G
for which
{}^{g}H^{\prime}=H^{\prime\prime}
. Then
{}^{g}K^{\prime}\,\mathcal{R}\,L^{\prime}
is a lift of
[K]\to[L]
. Thus
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
is transitive. Note that this part does not use the assumption that
G
is lossless.

Now suppose
[K]\,\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\,[H]
and
[L]\leqslant[H]
, and suppose
[M]
is maximal among
[M]\leqslant[K]
and
[M]\leqslant[L]
. We can assume without loss of generality that
K\,\mathcal{R}\,H
is a lift and
L\leqslant H
and
M\leqslant K
. Let
g\in G
such that
{}^{g}M\leqslant L
. Since
G
is lossless we can assume
g\in N_{G}(H)
. Thus
{}^{g}K\,\mathcal{R}\,H
and
{}^{g}M\leqslant{}^{g}K\cap L
. But
[{}^{g}K\cap L]\leqslant[K],[L]
, so by maximality
{}^{g}M
must be conjugate to
{}^{g}K\cap L
, and hence for order reasons we have
{}^{g}M={}^{g}K\cap L
. But since
\,\mathcal{R}\,
is a transfer system we have
{}^{g}M\,\mathcal{R}\,L
as
{}^{g}M=({}^{g}K\cap L)
, and hence
[M]\,\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\,[L]
.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

If
G
is lossless then
\pi^{*}\dashv\pi_{*}
is a Galois connection between
G
-transfer systems and categorical transfer systems on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
. Furthermore, the unit of this adjunction is the identity, i.e., for all
G
-transfer systems
\mathcal{R}
we have
\mathcal{R}=\pi^{-1}(\pi_{*}(\mathcal{R}))=\pi^{*}(\pi_{*}(\mathcal{R}))
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a
G
-transfer system and
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
a categorical transfer system on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
. By definition,
\mathcal{R}\geqslant\pi^{*}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})
if and only if
\mathcal{R}\geqslant\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})
if and only if
[K]\,\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\,[H]
implies
K\,\mathcal{R}\,H
. On the other hand, we have
\pi_{*}(\mathcal{R})\geqslant\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
if and only if
[K]\,\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\,[H]
implies there exists some
K^{\prime}\leqslant H^{\prime}
with
[K^{\prime}]=[K]
and
[H^{\prime}]=[H]
such that
K^{\prime}\,\mathcal{R}\,H^{\prime}
.

Thus we need to show
K^{\prime}\,\mathcal{R}\,H^{\prime}
implies
K\,\mathcal{R}\,H
. Or in other words, we need to show that if
G
is lossless, then for any
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{R}
and any two pairs
K\leqslant H
,
K^{\prime}\leqslant H^{\prime}
such that
[K]=[K^{\prime}]
and
[H]=[H^{\prime}]
, we have
K\,\mathcal{R}\,H
if and only if
K^{\prime}\,\mathcal{R}\,H^{\prime}
. By symmetry we can suppose
K\,\mathcal{R}\,H
and we want to show this implies
K^{\prime}\,\mathcal{R}\,H^{\prime}
. But
[H]=[H^{\prime}]
implies by definition we can find some
g\in G
such that
{}^{g}H=H^{\prime}
, and by conjugation closure we have
{}^{g}K\,\mathcal{R}\,{}^{g}H
, so we might as well assume
H=H^{\prime}
. Let
h\in G
such that
{}^{h}K=K^{\prime}
. Then we have
K,{}^{h}K\leqslant H
, so since
G
is lossless we can assume
h\in N_{G}(H)
. But then again using conjugation closure we have
(K^{\prime}={}^{h}K)\,\mathcal{R}\,({}^{h}H=H=H^{\prime})
as claimed.

From the definition of
\pi^{-1}(-)
, this also shows
\mathcal{R}=\pi^{-1}(\pi_{*}(\mathcal{R}))
for any
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{R}
. Since
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})
is defined to be the smallest
G
-transfer system containing
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{R}
is a
G
-transfer system by definition, this also implies
\mathcal{R}=\pi^{*}(\pi_{*}(\mathcal{R}))
and hence the unit is the identity.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

If
G
is lossless then every
G
-transfer system can be lifted from a categorical transfer system on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
.

We note that a strong converse to this corollary also holds:

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
G
be an arbitrary (finite) group. If every
G
-transfer system can be written as
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{R})
for some arbitrary
relation

\mathcal{R}
on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
, then
G
is lossless.

Proof.

Let
S
be some set of arrows in
\operatorname{Sub}(G)
, and let
\mathcal{R}
be the
G
-transfer system generated by
S
. By the explicit construction of the transfer system generated by a set of arrows given in
[
Rub21b
, Appendix B]
, one can show that
K\to H\in\mathcal{R}
necessarily implies that there must exist some
K^{\prime}\to H^{\prime}\in S
and some
g\in G
such that
K\leqslant{}^{g}K^{\prime}
and
H\leqslant{}^{g}H^{\prime}
.

Now suppose
K,{}^{g}K\leqslant H
, and let
\mathcal{R}
be the
G
-transfer system generated by
K\to H
. Suppose
\mathcal{R}=\pi^{*}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})
. Since
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})
is generated by
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})
, there must be some
K^{\prime}\to H^{\prime}\in\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})
and some
h\in G
such that
K\leqslant{}^{h}K^{\prime}
and
H\leqslant{}^{h}H^{\prime}
. But also
\mathcal{R}
is generated by
S=\{K\to H\}
, so there must exist some
k\in G
such that
K^{\prime}\leqslant{}^{k}K
and
H^{\prime}\leqslant{}^{k}H^{\prime}
. For order reasons this forces
K={}^{h}K^{\prime}
and
H={}^{h}H^{\prime}
, so
[K]\to[H]=[K^{\prime}]\to[H^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
.

But we also have
[{}^{g}K]\to[H]
and
[K]\to[H]\in\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
and
[H]=[K]
, so by definition of
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})
we must have
{}^{g}K\to H\in\mathcal{R}
. Thus we can find
h\in G
such that
{}^{g}K\leqslant{}^{h}K
and
H\leqslant{}^{h}H
, and again for order reasons this forces
{}^{g}K={}^{h}K
and
H={}^{h}H
, i.e.,
K
and
{}^{g}K
are conjugate by way of
h\in N_{G}(H)
. Since
K,{}^{g}K\leqslant H
was arbitrary, this shows
G
is lossless.
∎

Remark 2.13
.

By
Section 2.1
and the general theory of Galois connections, when
G
is lossless we have a bijection between
G
-transfer systems and categorical transfer systems
\mathcal{R}
on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
such that
\mathcal{R}=\pi_{*}(\pi^{*}(\mathcal{R}))
. We call such transfer systems
liftable
. For example, considering
G=\mathfrak{S}_{3}
, as mentioned in the introduction a categorical transfer system is liftable if and only if whenever we have
\tau\to\mathfrak{S}_{3}
for any transposition
\tau
we also have
1\to\mathfrak{S}_{3}
(c.f.,
Figure 3
and the surrounding discussion).

In general the condition
\mathcal{R}=\pi_{*}(\pi^{*}(\mathcal{R}))
is not very evocative. In
Section 3
we give a more concrete set of conditions for a categorical transfer system to be liftable, but for general (lossless)
G
these conditions can still be rather opaque and difficult to verify. In
Section 3
we will consider some special cases where the lifting conditions are simple enough to be visually intuitive, like in the case of
G=\mathfrak{S}_{3}
.

The following corollary summarizes the results of this section.

Corollary 2.14
.

If
G
is a lossless group, then the function
\pi^{*}
from liftable transfer systems on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
to transfer systems for
G
(i.e., homotopy classes of
G
-
N_{\infty}
operads) is a bijection; furthermore, for a general finite group
G
, bijectivity of
\pi^{*}
(when restricted to liftable transfer systems) implies that

is lossless.

2.2.
Examples and counterexamples of lossless groups

We continue with some observations regarding lossless groups, and provide some non-trivial examples of interesting families of lossless groups. Note that clearly any Abelian group is lossless.

In general it appears that the class of lossless groups is rather poorly behaved under group-theoretic operations. However, we can prove that lossless groups at least play nicely with quotients.

Lemma 2.15
.

Any quotient of a lossless group is lossless.

Proof.

This follows directly from the standard equivariant poset isomorphism between
\operatorname{Sub}(G/N)
and the interval
[N,G]\subseteq\operatorname{Sub}(G)^{N}
for all
N\trianglelefteqslant G
.
∎

Before we continue, let us recall the theory of Hall subgroups, and Hall’s theorem, which can be seen as a generalization of Sylow’s theorem in the solvable case.

Definition 2.17

A

Theorem 2.18

(Hall’s Theorem

Let

\pi

Proposition 2.19
.

Any (finite) solvable T-group is lossless.

Proof.

A theorem of Peng tells us that a group

Suppose that

is a (super)solvable T-group, and let

. We will prove by induction on the number of prime divisors of

that

is pronormal. By Peng’s theorem this holds in the case where

∎

Although the solvable T-groups form a fairly large class of groups, this class notably excludes most interesting

The following result is trivial, but will show that

Proposition 2.20
.

Let

3.
Lifting criteria

In
Section 2.1
we saw that for a lossless group
G
,
G
-transfer systems are in bijection with liftable transfer systems on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
. However as discussed in
Section 2.1
, explicitly identifying which transfer systems on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
are liftable can be extremely difficult in general. In this section we consider some special cases where the lifting conditions are tangible. We first begin with a generality.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a lossless group, and let
\mathcal{R}
be a categorical transfer system on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
. Then
\mathcal{R}
is liftable if and only if for all
[K]\,\mathcal{R}\,[H]
and any
K^{\prime}\leqslant H
with
[K^{\prime}]=[K]
we have
[K\cap K^{\prime}]\,\mathcal{R}\,[H]
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{R}
is liftable and
K,K^{\prime}\leqslant H
are as in the statement of the lemma. Then
K\to H,K^{\prime}\to H\in\pi^{*}(\mathcal{R})
by definition, so by restriction-closure and transitivity we have
(K\cap K^{\prime})\to K^{\prime}\to H\in\pi^{*}(\mathcal{R})
and hence
[K\cap K^{\prime}]\to[H]\in\pi_{*}(\pi^{*}(\mathcal{R}))=\mathcal{R}
.

Now suppose conversely that
\mathcal{R}
is a categorical transfer system on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
such that the condition in the statement of the lemma holds. We claim that
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R})
is a
G
-transfer system. Note that if this claim is true then

\pi_{*}(\pi^{*}(\mathcal{R}))=\pi_{*}(\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R}))\leqslant\mathcal{R}\leqslant\pi_{*}(\pi^{*}(\mathcal{R}))

where the first inequality follows directly from the definition of
\pi_{*}
and
\pi^{-1}
and the last inequality follows from
\pi^{*}\dashv\pi_{*}
.

Clearly
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R})
is a conjugation-closed partial order refining
\leqslant
. All we need to show is that it is restriction-closed. In other words, we need to show that given
K\to H\in\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R})
and
L\leqslant H
we have
(K{\cap}L)\to L\in\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R})
. We prove this by induction on the tuple
(K,H,L)
. That is, assuming the claim is true for all tuples
(K^{\prime},H^{\prime},L^{\prime})
with
K^{\prime}\leqslant K,L^{\prime}\leqslant L,H^{\prime}\leqslant H
, and at least one of these inequalities is strict, we want to show this implies the claim for
(K,H,L)
. The base case where
K=H=L
is the trivial subgroup holds by reflexivity of
\mathcal{R}
.

By the induction hypothesis with
K
and
H
fixed but
L^{\prime}<L
, we can assume
(K\cap L)\to L^{\prime}\in\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R})
for all
L^{\prime}<L
with
K\cap L\leqslant L^{\prime}
. Let
M\leqslant L
such that
[K\cap L]\leqslant[M]
and
[M]
is maximal among
[M]\leqslant[K],[L]
. Let
g\in G
such that
{}^{g}(K\cap L)\leqslant M
. Since
G
is lossless we can assume
g\in N_{G}(L)
. Thus
{}^{g^{-1}}M\leqslant L
and
[M]=[{}^{g^{-1}}M]
, so we can assume without loss of generality that
K\cap L\leqslant M
.

If
M<L
, then by the induction hypothesis we have
K\cap L\to M\in\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R})
and hence by definition
[K\cap L]\,\mathcal{R}\,[M]
. But since
\mathcal{R}
is a categorical transfer system,
[M]
is maximal for
[M]\leqslant[K],[L]\leqslant[H]
, and
[K]\,\mathcal{R}\,[H]
, we have
[K\cap L]\,\mathcal{R}\,[M]\,\mathcal{R}\,[L]
, and hence
K\cap L\to L\in\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R})
.

On the other hand, if
M=L
then
[L]\leqslant[K]
, so
{}^{g^{-1}}L\leqslant K
for some
g\in G
, and again since
G
is lossless we can assume
g\in N_{G}(H)
. Thus
K,{}^{g}K\leqslant H
and
[K]\,\mathcal{R}\,[H]
, so by hypothesis we have
[K\cap{}^{g}K]\,\mathcal{R}\,[H]
. By restriction-closure this then implies
[K\cap{}^{g}K]\,\mathcal{R}\,[{}^{g}K]
, so
K\cap{}^{g}K\to{}^{g}K\in\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R})
. If
{}^{g}K<H
, then by the induction hypothesis with
K^{\prime}=K\cap{}^{g}K
,
H^{\prime}={}^{g}K
, and
L^{\prime}=L
we obtain
K\cap{}^{g}K\cap L=K\cap L\to L\in\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{R})
. But if
{}^{g}K=H
then also
K=H
and hence
K\cap L=L
so there’s nothing to show in this case.
∎

Although
Section 3
is nice in its generality, and is certainly more explicit than the basic definition, checking it still requires understanding subtle details about the way that subgroups of
G
embed into each other. Thankfully, for certain groups the subgroup structure is nice enough to make this condition particularly explicit. Some of the results described below hold in greater generality than stated, but the purpose of this section is mainly illustrative so we avoid excess generality. We recall that, in essence, a
Frobenius group
is a transitive permutation group on a finite set such that no non-trivial element fixes more than one point, and some non-trivial element fixes a point. They can be characterized as those groups
G
possessing a proper, nontrivial subgroup
T
(called the
Frobenius complement
) such that
T\cap{}^{g}T
is the trivial subgroup for every
g\in G\smallsetminus T
. The identity element along with members of
G\smallsetminus\bigcup_{g\in G}{}^{g}T
form the
Frobenius kernel
of
G
.

Definition 3.2
.

A
metacyclic Frobenius group
(mcF group) is a Frobenius group
G
such that both the kernel
N\trianglelefteqslant G
and the complement
T\leqslant G
are cyclic groups.

Remark 3.3
.

Any mcF group is of the form

G\cong\mathbb{Z}/n\rtimes T

where
T
is a cyclic subgroup of
(\mathbb{Z}/n)^{\times}
such that
x-1\in(\mathbb{Z}/n)^{\times}
for all
x\neq 1\in T
. Conversely every group of this form is an mcF group. For a general mcF group
G
, we will assume that we have passed through this bijection, and for clarity, write shall write
N
for the group
(\mathbb{Z}/n)
. That is,
G\cong N\rtimes T
.

Remark 3.4
.

Although not immediately obvious from the definition, one can show that a group
G
is metacyclic Frobenius if and only if
G
is both a metacyclic group and a Frobenius group, explaining the naming choice. This collection of groups also implicitly appears in work of Khukhro–Makareno
[
KM13a
,
KM13b
]
, but the authors are not aware of any other place that they have been studied.

Example 3.5
.

Let
n
be odd. Then the dihedral group
D_{n}=\mathbb{Z}/n\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2
of order

is a mcF group.

Example 3.6
.

Let

be any prime. Then

, the group of affine linear transformations of the finite field

, is a mcF group. Here we are using Gauss’s observation that
(\mathbb{Z}/n)^{\times}
is cyclic when

.

Definition 3.7
.

Let

. We call

the

.

Lemma 3.8
.

Any subgroup

is itself an mcF group with kernel

. If

then

is a cyclic group.

Proof.

This follows immediately from
[

.

Lemma 3.9
.

A subgroup
K
of an mcF group

is normal if and only if
K\leqslant N
or
N_{K}=N
.

Proof.

If
N_{K}=N
then
K=KN
is the preimage of
KN/N\leqslant G/N
, and since
G/N\cong T
is cyclic this implies
KN/N\trianglelefteqslant G/N
and hence
K\trianglelefteqslant G
. If
K\leqslant N
then
K
is characteristic in
N\trianglelefteqslant G
and hence again
K\trianglelefteqslant G
.

Conversely suppose
N_{K}\neq N
and
K\not\leqslant N
. Let
g\in K\smallsetminus N
and

. Since

is cyclic and
\operatorname{ord}(h)=\operatorname{ord}(ghg^{-1})
, we can write

, and since

is Frobenius and

,

must be a unit mod

. But if

is normal then

and hence

, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Any mcF group
G

. Furthermore, any two subgroups with the same order are conjugate in

.

Proof.

Solvability follows from the fact that

and

are cyclic by definition. The fact that

and
Section 3
.

Now suppose
K,K^{\prime}\leqslant G
and
|K|=|K^{\prime}|
. Since
N
is a normal Hall subgroup of
G
, we also have
|N_{K}|=|N_{K^{\prime}}|
and hence
N_{K}=N_{K^{\prime}}
since
N
is cyclic. Let
S,S^{\prime}
be complementary Hall subgroups of
K
and
K^{\prime}
, respectively. Then we can extend
S,S^{\prime}
to Hall subgroups
T,T^{\prime}
of
G
, and by Hall’s theorem we can find
g\in G
such that
{}^{g}T=T^{\prime}
. Then
{}^{g}S,S^{\prime}\leqslant T^{\prime}
are subgroups of the cyclic group
T^{\prime}
and
|{}^{g}S|=|S^{\prime}|
, so
{}^{g}S=S^{\prime}
. Thus
{}^{g}K={}^{g}(N_{K}S)=N_{K}S^{\prime}=K^{\prime}
.
∎

The main property that makes the lifting conditions for mcF groups simple comes from the following proposition.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
G
be an mcF group, and let
K\leqslant G
. For all
g\in G\smallsetminus N_{G}(K)
, we have
K\cap{}^{g}K=N_{K}
.

Proof.

Since
N_{K}
is a normal subgroup of
K
, we have
N_{K}\leqslant K\cap{}^{g}K
for all
g\in G
. To complete the proof, we show that the existence of
x\in(K\cap{}^{g^{-1}}K)\smallsetminus N_{K}
implies that
g\in N_{G}(K)
. Fix such an
x
. Then
\operatorname{ord}(x)
cannot divide
|N_{K}|
, so after replacing
x
with some power we can assume
\operatorname{ord}(x)
is coprime to
|N_{K}|
. Let
S\leqslant K
be a Hall subgroup complementary to
N_{K}
such that
x\in S
, and let
T\leqslant K
be a Hall subgroup complementary to
N_{K}
such that
gxg^{-1}\in T
. Let
S^{\prime}\geqslant S
and
T^{\prime}\geqslant T
be Hall subgroups of
G
complementary to
N
. Then by Hall’s theorem we can find
h\in G
such that
{}^{hg}S^{\prime}=T^{\prime}
, and since
e\neq gxg^{-1}\in{}^{g}S^{\prime}\cap T^{\prime}
this implies
{}^{hg}S^{\prime}\cap{}^{g}S^{\prime}\neq\{e\}
. By
[
Fei57
]
, this implies we must have
T^{\prime}={}^{hg}S^{\prime}={}^{g}S^{\prime}
. Since
T^{\prime}
is cyclic it has a unique subgroup of order
|T|=|{}^{g}S|
, so this then also implies
T={}^{g}S
. But then
g\in N_{G}(K)
as

{}^{g}K={}^{g}(N_{K}S)=N_{K}T=K.

∎

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
G
be an mcF group, and
\mathcal{R}
a categorical transfer system on
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
. Then
\mathcal{R}
is liftable if and only if whenever
[K]\,\mathcal{R}\,[H]
with
N_{K}\neq N_{H}
, we have
[N_{K}]\,\mathcal{R}\,[H]
(or equivalently
[N_{K}]\,\mathcal{R}\,[K]
).

Proof.

By
Section 3
, for the if direction we need to check for all
K,{}^{g}K\leqslant H
that either
K={}^{g}K
or
K\cap{}^{g}K=N_{K}
, but this follows directly from
Section 3
. For the only if direction we need to check that
N_{K}\neq N_{H}
implies there exists some
g\in G
with
{}^{g}K\leqslant H
such that
N_{K}=K\cap{}^{g}K
. But
N_{K}\neq N_{H}
implies by
Section 3
that
K
is not normal in
H
, and hence by
Section 3
we can find some
g\in H
such that
K\cap{}^{g}K=N_{K}
, and of course
{}^{g}K\leqslant{}^{g}H=H
.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

We can make this result more visually intuitive as follows. By
Section 3
, the map

5.
Strategies for lossy groups

In the previous sections we have explored lossless groups, and shown that they provide a convenient computational framework for computing transfer systems, especially when restricted to nicer subclasses such as metacyclic Frobenius groups. Although we’ve shown in
Section 2.2
that several important classes of groups are lossless, more complicated groups that may arise in practice tend to be lossy. Although lossy groups prevent us from working directly with
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
, with some cleverness it may still be the case that we can obtain simpler representations of transfer systems for lossy groups. In this final section we speculatively discuss one possible strategy for dealing with lossy groups in the case of
G=\mathsf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
where
p
is prime.

If
p=2,3,
or
5
, then we have seen in
Section 2.2
that
G
is lossless, but for
p>5
these groups are always lossy. On the other hand, if
p\equiv\pm 3\mod 8
then these groups are very close to being lossless. When
p\equiv\pm 1\mod 8
, the lossy binary octahedral subgroups add to the lossyness, but even in this case
G
only has a few deviations from losslessness. In the speculations that follow we focus on the simple case of
p\equiv\pm 3\mod 8
, but with some work it should be feasible to extend our constructions to work in general. In
Figure 15
we provide a schematic for
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
where
G=\mathsf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{13})
. The general goal is to represent
G
-transfer systems as a pair of abstract transfer systems on two small posets related to
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
, subject to a compatibility condition. For
p\equiv\pm 1\mod 8
one would likely need to use more than two posets, but the same general technique should still apply.

Let
H_{-}\cong\mathsf{Dic}_{(p-1)/2}
be the normalizer of the diagonal matrices, and
H_{+}\cong\mathsf{Dic}_{(p+1)/2}
the normalizer of some maximal non-split torus. (Here
\mathsf{Dic}_{n}
is the
dicyclic group
of order
4n
as in
Section 2.2
). Let
Z=H_{1}\cap H_{2}\cong C_{2}
be the center of
G
.

Recall that for a group
G
, the
Frattini subgroup
of
G
is defined as intersection of all maximal subgroups of
G

[
Gor80
, §5.1]
. The maximal subgroups of
\mathsf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
can be deduced from
[
Kin05
]
since the Frattini subgroup of
\mathsf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is equal to its center. When
p\equiv\pm 3\mod 8
, these maximal subgroups are

(1)

normalizers of torii, which are dicyclic as described above,

(2)

Borel subgroups, which are isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}\rtimes\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
with
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
acting on
\mathbb{F}_{p}
as multiplication by
x^{2}
, and

(3)

binary tetrahedral or binary icosahedral subgroups.

The Borel subgroups are universally lossless, and the binary tetrahedral/icosahedral subgroups are isomorphic to
\mathsf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
and
\mathsf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{5})
which again are universally lossless. Furthermore, if we let
\epsilon=\pm 1
accordingly as
p+\epsilon\equiv 4\mod 8
, then the normalizer of a torus of order
p-\epsilon
is a dicyclic group of order
2(p-\epsilon)\equiv 4\mod 8
, and hence is also universally lossless. Thus the only non-universally lossless maximal subgroups are the normalizers of torii of order
p+\epsilon
, which are all conjugate to
H_{\epsilon}
.

By
[
CJ09
]
, any two cyclic subgroups of
G
with the same order are conjugate. The subgroup
H_{\epsilon}
contains three conjugacy classes of subgroups isomorphic to
C_{4}
, and hence when we embed into
\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
these three copies of
C_{4}
must be mapped to the same conjugacy class. But if
C_{4},{}^{g}C_{4}\leqslant K\leqslant H_{\epsilon}
is not contained in any other maximal subgroup (e.g.,
K=H_{\epsilon}
itself), then
N_{G}(K)\leqslant H_{\epsilon}
, so
C_{4},{}^{g}C_{4}
cannot be conjugate in
N_{G}(K)
. This is what causes

to be lossy. On the other hand, when
p\equiv\pm 3\mod 8
this appears to be the only obstacle preventing losslessness.

Let
D_{G}
be the poset
\operatorname{Sub}(H_{\epsilon})/H_{\epsilon}
with an additional top vertex
[G]
. (For

, this is depicted in

.) Let

be the subposet on objects
[H]
such that either
H=G
or
H

\psi^{D}\colon D_{G}\to\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
and
\psi^{U}\colon U_{G}\to\operatorname{Sub}(G)/G
. Let

(depicted in
Figure 16
for

). We let

be the canonical embedding, and we let
\phi^{U}\colon I_{G}\to U_{G}
be the restriction of
\psi^{D}
. For any abstract transfer system
\mathcal{R}
on

\phi^{D}_{*}(\mathcal{R})=\operatorname{im}(\phi^{D}|_{\mathcal{R}})
and similarly for

.

Definition 5.1
.

A
split transfer system
is a triple of catgorical transfer systems
\mathcal{R}_{D}
,
\mathcal{R}_{I}
,
\mathcal{R}_{U}
on

,

(1)

[C_{4}]\,\mathcal{R}\,[G]

[C_{4}]\,\mathcal{R}\,[G]
for all conjugacy classes of
[C_{4}]\in D_{G}
, and

(2)

and
\phi^{U}_{*}(\mathcal{R}_{I})=\mathcal{R}_{U}\cap\operatorname{im}\phi^{U}
.

For every split transfer system
(\mathcal{R}_{D},\mathcal{R}_{I},\mathcal{R}_{U})
, we can define a reflexive relation
\mathcal{R}
on

{}^{g}K
of

if and only if

So we suppose
H<G

H\leqslant L

if and only if
[K]\,\mathcal{R}_{U}\,[H]

. Then we set

if and only if
[{}^{g}K]\,\mathcal{R}_{D}\,[{}^{g}H]
.

Conversely, if
\mathcal{R}
is a transfer system on

(1)

(3)

Turán Problems for Mixed Graphs
Nitya Mani and Edward Yu

Abstract
. We investigate natural Turán problems for mixed graphs, generalizations of graphs where edges can be either directed or undirected. We study a natural Turán density coefficient that measures how large a fraction of directed edges an F F -free mixed graph can have; we establish an analogue of the Erdős-Stone-Simonovits theorem and give a variational characterization of the Turán density coefficient of any mixed graph (along with an associated extremal F F -free family). This characterization enables us to highlight an important divergence between classical extremal numbers and the Turán density coefficient. We show that Turán density coefficients can be irrational, but are always algebraic; for every positive integer k k , we construct a family of mixed graphs whose Turán density coefficient has algebraic degree k k .



1.
Introduction

One of the earliest results in extremal graph theory (or indeed, classical combinatorics) was a result of Mantel in 1907, which showed that an
n
-vertex graph which does not contain a triangle has at most
\frac{n^{2}}{4}
edges
[
23
]
.
A central problem in extremal graph theory has been to determine the
extremal number

\ex(n,F)
of
F
-free graphs, the maximum number of edges that an
n
-vertex graph can have if it does not contain a copy of
F
as a subgraph. This question is referred to as Turán’s problem, due to his famous result in 1941 characterizing
\ex(n,K_{r})
where
K_{r}
is a
clique
or
complete graph
on
r
vertices
[
33
]
. Turán’s theorem was generalized to all non-bipartite forbidden graphs
F
in the following result:

Theorem 1.1

(Erdős-Stone-Simonovits, 1946
[
9
,
11
]
)
.

Let
\upchi(F)
denote the
chromatic number
of
F
, the minimum number of colors required in a proper vertex-coloring of
F
(where no two adjacent vertices have the same color). Then, we have that

\ex(n,F)=\left(1-\frac{1}{\upchi(F)-1}+o(1)\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}.

The above theorem asymptotically resolves Turán’s problem whenever
F
is not bipartite (i.e.,
\upchi(F)>2
). The case of bipartite
F
is still widely open and a very active area of research. Kövári-Sós-Turán
[
19
]
gave a general upper bound that is believed to be tight for complete bipartite graphs
K_{s,t}
; the bound is known to be tight for
K_{2,2},K_{3,3}

[
13
]
and was recently shown to be tight when
t
is sufficiently large relative to
s
in breakthrough work leveraging randomized algebraic constructions
[
6
]
; see
[
13
]
for a comprehensive survey of bipartite Turán results.

Motivated by this line of inquiry, a more general, fundamental family of questions in extremal combinatorics are the so-called
Turán-type questions
, which seek to understand how large a graph-like object can be when we forbid certain substructures from appearing. Perhaps the most well-known such question is the hypergraph Turán problem, a rich and fertile area of research which has attracted much attention and where even very simple-seeming questions are wide open; see
[
17
]
for a survey of such extremal hypergraph results. Other Turán-type problems of substantial interest include the rainbow Turán problem (on edge-colored graphs)
[
18
]
and the directed-graph Turán problem
[
4
]
.

In this work, we study natural Turán problems for
mixed graphs
, graph-like objects that can have both directed and undirected edges.

Definition 1.2
.

A
mixed graph

G=(V,E)
consists of vertex set
V
and edge set
E
. Each edge
e\in E
is a pair of distinct vertices
u,v\in V
,
that is either
undirected
(denoted
e=uv=vu
) or
directed
(denoted
e=u\widecheck{v}=\widecheck{v}u
).
For a directed edge
u\widecheck{v}
, the vertex
v
is the
head
vertex of the edge, and
u
the
tail
. There is at most one edge on any pair of vertices.
Two vertices are considered adjacent if they are connected by any edge, regardless of whether the edge is directed or undirected.

Let
e_{\mathrm{u}}\left(G\right)
and
e_{\mathrm{d}}\left(G\right)
be the number of undirected and directed edges in mixed graph
G
respectively, and let
\alpha(G)=\frac{e_{\mathrm{u}}\left(G\right)}{\binom{v(G)}{2}}
and
\beta(G)=\frac{e_{\mathrm{d}}\left(G\right)}{\binom{v(G)}{2}}
be the
undirected
and
directed

edge densities
of
G
, respectively.

Mixed graphs naturally appear in a variety of enumerative, combinatorial, and theoretical computer science applications.
Mixed graphs have been previously studied in a variety of contexts including spectral graph theory
[
20
,
28
]
and the extremal degree-diameter problem
[
22
,
32
]
.
They also arise naturally in the context of theoretical computer science, including the extremal problems of coloring
[
29
,
26
,
14
]
and job scheduling
[
30
]
; they have useful applications in object classification and labeling, social network models, and inference on Bayesian networks
[
34
,
7
,
25
,
31
]
.
Specific Turán problems on special families of mixed graphs (such as geodetic mixed graphs in
[
32
]
) have also been explored in recent work.
Here, we make a systematic study of a natural Turán type quantity on mixed graphs, leveraging the below notion of subgraphs that arises in recent applications e.g.
[
8
,
2
]
.

Definition 1.3
.

We say a mixed graph
F
is a
subgraph
of
G
(
F\subseteq G
) if
F
can be obtained from
G
by deleting vertices, deleting edges, and forgetting edge directions. We say
G
is
F
-free
if
F
is not a subgraph of
G
(see
Figure

1
).

Figure 1
.
Both
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are subgraphs of
G
;
F_{1}
is not a subgraph of

.

In an initial attempt to study extremal questions on mixed graphs, one might first consider a “naive” extremal problem, maximizing the edge density
\alpha(G)+\beta(G)
over all
F
-free
G
.
However, this notion is uninteresting for mixed graphs; for all
F
with at least one directed edge,
F\not\subseteq K_{n}
and
\alpha(K_{n})+\beta(K_{n})=1
, hence the maximal value of
\alpha(G)+\beta(G)
over
F
-free
G
would always be
1
.

The above motivates the question we study here, trying to understand the
tradeoff
between directed and undirected edges in
F
-free mixed graphs. More formally, we study the following extremal quantity:

Definition 1.4
.

Let
F
be a mixed graph. The
Turán density coefficient

\theta(F)
is the maximum
\rho
such that

\alpha(G)+\rho\beta(G)\leq 1+o(1)

over all
F
-free
n
-vertex mixed graphs
G
.
If
\beta(G)=o(1)
over
F
-free
n
-vertex
G
, we say
\theta(F)=\infty
.

Note that for all mixed graphs
F
, it is true that
\theta(F)\geq 1
, because
\alpha(G)+\beta(G)\leq 1
for all mixed graphs
G
(every pair of vertices is connected by at most one edge).

We verify that this notion is well-defined in
2.1
. Throughout this article, we will primarily focus on the “dense” case where
\theta(F)<\infty
, as opposed to the “degenerate” case where
\theta(F)=\infty
and
F
is bipartite.

The above extremal quantity naturally arises in theoretical computer science problems, notably the Bollobás-Brightwell-Leader conjecture about the number of distinct
k
-SAT functions (posed in 2003
[
3
]
) was recently resolved for all
k\geq 2
in a two-part argument
[
8
]
that first reduced this open problem to a bound on the Turán density coefficient of a triangle like mixed hypergraph (using a suitable generalization of the above mixed graph notion) and then subsequently gave a sufficiently strong bound on the associated Turán density coefficient to prove the conjecture
[
2
]
(the
k=2
case of the Bollobás-Brightwell-Leader conjecture, originally proved in
[
1
]
, can be reduced to proving that if
F
is a triangle with a single directed edge, then
\theta(F)>\log_{2}3
). Other seemingly unrelated questions can also be restated as Turán problems on mixed graphs; for example, extremal bounds on the number of edges in the square of an undirected graph (as in
[
12
]
) can be reduced to showing that
\theta(F)=2
for the mixed triangle described above.

In our work, we completely characterize the Turán density coefficients of mixed graphs, observing some similarities to Turán densities of undirected graphs but also several key differences, which in part motivate the study of mixed Turán coefficients. We will see for instance, that there is a natural oriented analogue of being bipartite (
Definition

1.5
) and that we can use this notion in conjunction with the chromatic number of the underlying undirected graph to give an analogue of the Erdős-Stone-Simonovits theorem for mixed graphs.

Definition 1.5
.

A mixed graph is
uncollapsible
if some two head vertices are adjacent or some two tail vertices are adjacent. Otherwise, it is
collapsible
.

Roughly speaking, collapsible mixed graphs will contain a large blowup of at least one directed edge (see
Section

3
for details).

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
F
be a mixed graph. Then, if
\upchi(F)
is the chromatic number of the underlying undirected graph of
F
, we have the following:

(i)

\theta(F)=1
if and only if
F
is uncollapsible.

(ii)

\theta(F)=\infty
if and only if
F
admits a proper 2-coloring of the vertices such that all head vertices are the same color. Otherwise
\theta(F)\leq 2
.

(iii)

If
F
has at most one directed edge, then

\theta(F)=1+\frac{1}{\upchi(F)-2}.

(iv)

Otherwise, we have that

1+\frac{1}{\upchi(F)}\leq\theta(F)\leq 1+\frac{1}{\upchi(F)-2}.

Remark 1.7
.

At first glance, it may be unclear if the above result is tight in general. In fact, both ends of the above inequality can be attained by infinitely many mixed graphs
F
; the general upper bound is achieved by mixed graphs with a single directed edge (as noted in case (iii)) and a tight instance of the lower bound is given in
Example

3.11
.

The above Erdős-Stone-Simonovits-style result falls short of a characterization of the Turán density coefficient
\theta(F)
. However, we are able to completely determine
\theta(F)
with a variational characterization of
\theta(F)
that determines the Turán density coefficient as the minimum of a finite number of solutions to finite dimensional optimization problems. We give an informal statement below and defer the formal statement to
Theorem

4.13
.

Theorem 1.8

(Informal)
.

Let
F
be a mixed graph with at least one directed edge where
\theta(F)\in(1,\infty)
. There is some family
\mathcal{B}_{F}
of mixed graphs on at most
v(F)
vertices, such that

\theta(F)=\min_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{F}}\left\{\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\triangle^{v(B)-1}}\left\{\frac{1-\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}U_{B}\mathbf{y}}{\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}D_{B}\mathbf{y}}\right\}\right\},

where
B
is represented by
(U_{B},D_{B})
, an ordered pair of matrices that encode the undirected and directed edges of
B
, and
\triangle^{v(B)-1}
is the simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{v(B)}
.

To show the above result, we leverage and generalize a technique of Brown, Erdős, and Simonovits
[
4
]
.
Our result sets the Turán coefficient of mixed graphs in sharp contrast to more general Turán type problems; for instance, graph homomorphism inequalities are in general not even necessarily
decidable

[
15
]
, which is a very different situation than the finite-time algorithm to verify bounds on
\theta(F)
implied by the above representation.

The above expression nevertheless seems somewhat complicated, and one might hope that a simple modification of
Theorem

1.6
might be sufficient to understand
\theta(F)
in general.
However, the extremal behavior of general mixed graphs turns out to be much more nuanced than the case of undirected graphs.
In
Proposition

5.1
we construct a relatively simple mixed graph
F
with irrational density coefficient,
in contrast with the Erdős-Stone-Simonovits theorem which implies graph Turán densities are of the form
1-1/k
for positive integer
k
.
Based on this, one might wonder how complicated
\theta(F)
can be.
The complexity of Turán-type quantities in discrete structures varies considerably.
Turán densities of graphs are always rational, as noted above, as are digraphs
[
5
]
.
On the other hand, there is a range of results on the existence of hypergraphs with irrational Turán densities and related concepts. For instance,
[
35
]
constructed a relatively simple example of a
3
-graph with irrational Lagrangian densities; it was recently shown in
[
16
]
that there exist hypergraphs with irrational Turán densities; and Turán densities of families of hypergraphs can have arbitrarily high algebraic degree
[
21
,
27
]
;
and families of multigraphs can even have transcedental Turán densities
[
24
]
.
Here, we show that Turán density coefficients of mixed graphs are algebraic:

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
F
be a mixed graph such that
\theta(F)<\infty
. Then
\theta(F)
is an algebraic number.

Furthermore, every algebraic degree is actually attained in the Turán density coefficient of some finite family of mixed graphs (where for family
\mathcal{F}
of mixed graphs,
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-free if
G
is
F
-free for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
). This suggests a particularly rich structure encoded by the Turán density coefficients of mixed graphs.

Theorem 1.10
.

For every positive integer
k
there exists a finite family of mixed graphs having Turán density coefficient with algebraic degree
k
.

It remains an interesting question: does every algebraic degree arise as the Turán density coefficient of a
single
mixed graph? In other words, for every positive integer
k
does there exist a mixed graph
F
such that
\theta(F)
has algebraic degree
k
?

2.2.
Bipartite and undirected mixed graphs

As a warmup, we first discuss two very simple families of mixed graphs. A
bipartite
mixed graph is a mixed graph with
\upchi(F)\leq 2
.

We establish our first results on
\theta(F)
, for some simple families
F
. These will also be useful later on in
Section

3
. For
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, let
K_{a,b}
denote the undirected complete bipartite graph with parts of size
a
and
b
. Let
K_{\overrightarrow{a,b}}=(A\sqcup B,E)
denote the mixed graph with underlying undirected graph

(with
|A|=a,|B|=b
) such that all edges in
K_{\overrightarrow{a,b}}
are directed
A\to B
. By applying the Erdős-Stone-Simonovits theorem, we obtain an analogue of the classical observation that undirected graphs free of fixed bipartite subgraph

must have

edges; we find that mixed graphs free of some fixed subgraph

can only have
o(n^{2})

Proposition 2.6
.

For mixed graph
F
, we have
\theta(F)=\infty
if and only if
F\subseteq K_{\overrightarrow{t,t}}
for some

.
Otherwise
\theta(F)\leq 2
.

Proof.

The main effort is to show that
\theta(F)=\infty
for

for

and suppose to the contrary there was a sequence
\{G_{n}\}
of

-free mixed graphs with

\beta(G_{n})>\delta

Fix

Note that the subgraph

. By our choice of

4.
A variational characterization of
\theta(F)

This section gives a variational characterization of
\theta(F)
, showing that
\theta(F)
is the solution to a finite dimensional optimization problem. Essentially, we show that
\theta(F)
is the minimum of a function over a finite set of
mixed adjacency matrices
, defined precisely below.
This characterization gives an explicit method of computing
\theta(F)
for a general given mixed graph
F
, in contrast to
Theorem

1.6
which may not provide a tight bound for a specific mixed graph
F
. In
Section

5
, we leverage this characterization to study the algebraicity and algebraic degree of
\theta(F).

4.1.
A weighted extremal problem

We begin by considering a slightly different extremal problem on mixed graphs; we will try to maximize a weighted edge count over
F
-free mixed graphs but fix the weight of directed edges in advance.

Definition 4.1
.

For
\rho\in(1,\infty)
and mixed graph
G
, we define the
\rho
-weighted edge count
of
G
as
w_{\rho}\left(G\right):=e_{\mathrm{u}}\left(G\right)+\rho\cdot e_{\mathrm{d}}\left(G\right)
, i.e., we count weighted edges where undirected edges have weight
1
and directed edges have weight
\rho
. The
\rho
-density
is given by
w_{\rho}(G)/\tbinom{v(G)}{2}
.

In this section, we are motivated by the following question: given a forbidden mixed graph
F
and fixed
\rho\in(1,\infty)
, what is the asymptotic maximum of the
\rho
-density of a sequence of distinct
F
-free graphs?
This question is closely tied to the problems studied in the previous section;
in fact,
\theta(F)
is the largest value of
\rho
such that for every sequence
\{G_{n}\}
of distinct
F
-free graphs,

(4.1)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\;\frac{w_{\rho}\left(G_{n}\right)}{\binom{v(G_{n})}{2}}\leq 1.

We later employ this fact in our variational characterization of
\theta(F)
. Throughout this section, we consider
\rho\in(1,\infty)
to be a fixed constant.

The primary result of this section,
Theorem

4.11
, establishes that we can construct a family of asymptotically maximal (in
\rho
-density) family of mixed graphs by carefully selecting a sufficiently dense but small mixed graph and taking an (asymmetric) blowup. The subsequent analysis adapts the primary technique used in previous work of Brown, Erdős, and Simonovits to study extremal problems in directed graphs
[
4
]
. Our primary objects of study will be
mixed adjacency matrices
, which resemble hypergraph Lagrangians
[
17
]
and patterns
[
27
]
in form and purpose.

Definition 4.2

(Mixed adjacency matrix)
.

A
mixed adjacency matrix

A=(U,D)
is an ordered pair of
r\times r
matrices where
U
and
D
satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

U
is a symmetric matrix in
\{0,1\}^{r\times r}
.

(ii)

D_{ij}\in\{0,2\}
for all
i,j\in[r]
, and
D_{ij}\neq 0
implies that
D_{ji}=0
. Thus,
D_{ii}=0
for all
i\in[r]
.

(iii)

For all
i,j\in[r]
, at most one of
D_{ij}
and
U_{ij}
is nonzero.

We say that
r
is the
size
of
A
,
that
U
is the
undirected part
of
A
,
and that
D
is the
directed part
of
A
.

A mixed adjacency matrix
(U,D)
of size
r
can be thought of as a “template” for constructing associated mixed graphs; the elements of
U
and

As the name suggests, for mixed adjacency matrices that are not condensed, we can further compress them by deleting (matching) rows and columns.

4.2.
The variational characterization

We will now use
Lemma

4.10
and
Theorem

4.11
to give a variational characterization of
\theta(F)
.

Observation 4.12
.

Fix mixed adjacency matrix
A
. Then
g_{\rho}(A)
is continuous in
\rho
for
\rho\in(1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
A=(U,D)
. Since
\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}D\mathbf{y}\leq 1
for any
\mathbf{y}\in\triangle^{r-1}
, for all
\varepsilon>0
we have

g_{\rho}(A)\leq g_{\rho+\varepsilon}(A)=\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\triangle^{r-1}}\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}(U+(\rho+\varepsilon)D)\mathbf{y}\leq\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\triangle^{r-1}}\left(\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}(U+\rho D)\mathbf{y}+\varepsilon\right)=g_{\rho}(A)+\varepsilon,

and similarly

g_{\rho}(A)\geq g_{\rho-\varepsilon}(A)\geq g_{\rho}(A)-\varepsilon.

Continuity follows directly.
∎

We are now ready to give a formal variational characterization of
\theta(F).

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
F
be a mixed graph with at least one directed edge such that
\theta(F)\in(1,\infty)
. For each

, let

be its size. Then,

\theta(F)=\min_{B\in\mathcal{M}_{F}\setminus\{K\}}\left\{\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\triangle^{r-1}}\left\{\frac{1-\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}U\mathbf{y}}{\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}D\mathbf{y}}\right\}\right\},

where we consider
\frac{1-\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}U\mathbf{y}}{\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}D\mathbf{y}}
to be
\infty
if
\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}D\mathbf{y}=0
.
Note that this implies
g_{\theta(F)}(B^{*})=1
, where
B^{*}
is the mixed adjacency matrix attaining the above minimum.

Proof.

Consider any
B\in\mathcal{M}_{F}\setminus\{K\}
of size
r
and
\mathbf{y}\in\triangle^{r-1}
. We first claim that

\theta(F)\leq\frac{1-\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}U\mathbf{y}}{\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}D\mathbf{y}}.

If not, we can find some mixed adjacency matrix

and
\mathbf{y}\in\triangle^{r-1}
where

. This would imply that

, contradicting (
4.1
) by
Theorem

4.11
.

It remains to exhibit

and

achieving the aforementioned upper bound. By applying
Theorem

4.11
to
\rho_{k}=\theta(F)+\frac{1}{k}
for each

, we find for each

where

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}v(G)=n\\

Since

∎

5.
Algebraic degree of
\theta(F)

Thus far, the explicit results we have obtained bears striking resemblance to classical extremal graph theory. For instance,
Theorem

1.6
shows that the Turán density coefficients of a mixed graph with one directed edge is exactly characterized by the Turán density of its underlying undirected graph, and that in general this is not far from true numerically. However, when we think about the space of
values
the Turán density coefficient can take on, the analogy to undirected graphs will turn out to break down in a striking manner.

Turán densities of undirected graphs

are always of the form

that Turán density coefficients are always algebraic, this is in some sense the “strongest” result we could hope for; we prove

1.10
, an analogue of a corresponding hypergraph Turán result of
[

]
, showing that there are finite families of mixed graphs

such that

has arbitrarily high algebraic degree.

r^{\prime}
such that
\theta(\mathcal{F})=\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\triangle^{r^{\prime}-1}}\frac{1-\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}U^{\prime}\mathbf{y}}{\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}D^{\prime}\mathbf{y}}
. By
Definition

4.9
,
B^{\prime}
is
\mathcal{F}
-free,
B^{\prime}\left\llbracket\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket
has complete underlying undirected graph,
D^{\prime}\neq\mathbf{0}
, and
U^{\prime}_{ii}=0
for all

; furthermore
r^{\prime}\leq r
since
\overrightarrow{K_{r+1}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
.
Since
B^{\prime}\left\llbracket\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket
is
\mathcal{F}
-free with
r^{\prime}\leq r<r+1
vertices, for some
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
it is true that
B^{\prime}\left\llbracket\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket\subseteq B\left\llbracket t\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket
. Since both
B
and
B^{\prime}
have complete underlying undirected graphs, in fact, we must have
B^{\prime}\left\llbracket\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket\subseteq B\left\llbracket\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket
, i.e.,
B^{\prime}\trianglelefteq B
, implying that

\theta(\mathcal{F})=\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\triangle^{r^{\prime}-1}}\left\{\frac{1-\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}U^{\prime}\mathbf{y}}{\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}D^{\prime}\mathbf{y}}\right\}\geq\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\triangle^{r-1}}\left\{\frac{1-\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}U\mathbf{y}}{\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}D\mathbf{y}}\right\}.

The reverse inequality follows since
B
is
\mathcal{F}
-free by definition of
\theta(\mathcal{F})
.
∎

Define a sequence of mixed adjacency matrices
\{B_{k}=(U_{k},D_{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
by
B_{0}=([0],[0])
and

B_{k+1}=\left(\begin{bmatrix}{\tiny\begin{matrix}0&0\\
0&0\end{matrix}}&{\tiny\begin{matrix}0&\cdots&0\\
1&\cdots&1\end{matrix}}\\
{\tiny\begin{matrix}0&1\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
0&1\end{matrix}}&{\Huge U_{k}}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}{\tiny\begin{matrix}0&2\\
0&0\end{matrix}}&{\tiny\begin{matrix}2&\cdots&2\\
0&\cdots&0\end{matrix}}\\
{\tiny\begin{matrix}0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0\end{matrix}}&{\Huge D_{k}}\end{bmatrix}\right)

so that
B_{k}
has size
2k+1
. For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, let
\mathcal{F}_{k}
be the finite family given by
Lemma

5.2
for
B_{k}
, so

\theta(\mathcal{F}_{k})=\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\triangle^{2k}}\left\{\frac{1-\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}U_{k}\mathbf{y}}{\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}D_{k}\mathbf{y}}\right\}.

Since
B_{k}\vartriangleleft B_{k+1}
, we have
\theta(\mathcal{F}_{k})\geq\theta(\mathcal{F}_{k+1})
.

Definition 5.3
.

For mixed adjacency matrix
B
of size
r
and
\rho\in(1,\infty)
, call vector
\mathbf{y}^{*}\in\triangle^{r-1}
a
maximizing vector of
B
with respect to
\rho
if

{\mathbf{y}^{*}}^{\intercal}B_{\rho}\mathbf{y}^{*}=g_{\rho}(B)=\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\triangle^{r-1}}\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}B_{\rho}\mathbf{y}.

Proposition 5.4
.

Suppose
\mathbf{y}^{*}=(y^{*}_{1},\dots,y^{*}_{2k+1})
is a maximizing vector of
B_{k}
with respect to some
\rho\in\big(1,\theta(\mathcal{F}_{k})\big]
.
Then, the vector

\left(u^{*},v^{*},(1-u^{*}-v^{*})y^{*}_{1},\dots,(1-u^{*}-v^{*})y^{*}_{2k+1}\right),

where
u^{*}=\frac{\rho(2-g_{\rho}(B_{k}))-1}{4\rho-2\rho g_{\rho}(B_{k})-1}

and
v^{*}=\frac{\rho(1-g_{\rho}(B_{k}))}{4\rho-2\rho g_{\rho}(B_{k})-1}
,
is maximizing for
B_{k+1}
with respect to
\rho
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{y}=(u,v,y_{1},\dots,y_{2k+1})
be a vector of length
2k+3
; and let
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{1-u-v}(y_{1},\dots,y_{2k+1})
. Then we have

\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}(B_{k+1})_{\rho}\mathbf{y}=2\rho u(1-u)+2v(1-u-v)+(1-u-v)^{2}{\mathbf{y}^{\prime}}^{\intercal}(B_{k})_{\rho}\mathbf{y}^{\prime}.

From above, we see there exists some maximizing vector of
B_{k+1}
with respect to
\rho
(that we term
\widehat{\mathbf{y}}=(u,v,\widehat{y}_{1},\dots,\widehat{y}_{2k+1})
) such that
(\widehat{y}_{1},\dots,\widehat{y}_{2k+1})=(1-u-v)\mathbf{y}^{*}
.
We define

f(u,v):=\widehat{\mathbf{y}}^{\intercal}(B_{k+1})_{\rho}\widehat{\mathbf{y}}=2\rho u(1-u)+2v(1-v)-2uv+(1-u-v)^{2}g_{\rho}(B_{k}).

We then compute the Hessian of
f(u,v)
for
(u,v)\in\triangle^{2}
as

\nabla^{2}f(u,v)=\begin{bmatrix}2g_{\rho}(B_{k})-4\rho&2g_{\rho}(B_{k})-2\\
2g_{\rho}(B_{k})-2&2g_{\rho}(B_{k})-4\end{bmatrix}.

Since
\rho\in(1,\theta(\mathcal{F}_{k})]
we have that
g_{\rho}(B_{k})\in[0,1]
. Thus for any vector
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
,

\mathbf{x}^{\intercal}\left(\nabla^{2}f(u,v)\right)\mathbf{x}=-\left((4\rho-2)x_{1}^{2}+2x_{2}^{2}+(2-2g_{\rho}(B_{k}))(x_{1}+x_{2})^{2}\right)<0.

Therefore,
\nabla^{2}f(u,v)
is negative definite, implying that

f(u,v)
is concave over
\triangle^{2}
.
At
u=u^{*}
and
v=v^{*}
(for
u^{*},v^{*}
as given above which satisfy
(u^{*},v^{*})\in\triangle^{2}
),
we have

\frac{\partial\big(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}^{\intercal}(B_{k+1})_{\rho}\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\big)}{\partial u}=\frac{\partial\big(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}^{\intercal}(B_{k+1})_{\rho}\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\big)}{\partial v}=0,

hence these values of maximize
f(u,v)
and the conclusion follows.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

For
\rho\in\big(1,\theta(\mathcal{F}_{k})\big]
,

g_{\rho}(B_{k+1})=\frac{\rho^{2}(2-g_{\rho}(B_{k}))}{2\rho(2-g_{\rho}(B_{k}))-1}.

We are now ready to prove
Theorem

1.10
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.10
.

For all
k\geq 0
define the polynomials
P_{k}(\rho),Q_{k}(\rho)
recursively by

\displaystyle P_{0}(\rho)=0;

\displaystyle\quad Q_{0}(\rho)=1;

\displaystyle P_{k+1}(\rho)=\rho^{2}(2Q_{k}(\rho)-P_{k}(\rho));

\displaystyle\quad Q_{k+1}(\rho)=(4\rho-1)Q_{k}(\rho)-2\rho P_{k}(\rho).

By induction on
k
, we can observe the following for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
:

(i)

g_{\rho}(B_{k})=\frac{P_{k}(\rho)}{Q_{k}(\rho)}
for all
\rho\leq\theta(\mathcal{F}_{k-1})
(by
Corollary

5.5
);

(ii)

P_{k}
has degree
2k
, and
Q_{k}
has degree
2k-1
;

(iii)

Q_{k}(0)=\pm 1
;

(iv)

\rho^{2}\mid P_{k}
,
P_{k}
has all even coefficients, and its leading coefficient is
\pm 2
.

Hence for
k\geq 1
we know

P_{k}(\rho)-Q_{k}(\rho)=\big(\rho^{2}(2Q_{k-1}(\rho)-P_{k-1}(\rho))\big)-\big(2\rho(2Q_{k-1}(\rho)-P_{k-1}(\rho))-Q_{k-1}(\rho)\big)

is a polynomial of degree
2k
, with constant term
\pm 1
, all other coefficients even, and leading coefficient
\pm 2
. Hence the reciprocal polynomial of
P_{k}-Q_{k}
(i.e., the polynomial with the coefficients of
P_{k}-Q_{k}
in reverse order) is irreducible over
\mathbb{Z}[\rho]
by Eisenstein’s criterion on the prime
2
, which implies
P_{k}-Q_{k}
itself is irreducible as well.

Finally, since
\theta(\mathcal{F}_{k})\leq\theta(\mathcal{F}_{k-1})
and
g_{\theta(\mathcal{F}_{k})}(B_{k})=1
, we have by condition (1) that
P_{k}(\rho)=Q_{k}(\rho)
. In particular,
\theta(\mathcal{F}_{k})
must be a root of
P_{k}(\rho)-Q_{k}(\rho)
, which is an irreducible polynomial of degree
2k
with integer coefficients. Hence
\theta(\mathcal{F}_{k})
has algebraic degree
2k
, implying that for every even algebraic degree
2k
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we have some family
\mathcal{F}_{k}
with
\theta(\mathcal{F}_{k})
having algebraic degree
2k
(
Proposition

5.1
gives an example mixed graph
F
where
\theta(F)
has algebraic degree
2
).

For odd algebraic degrees, let
B_{k}^{\prime}
be the principal submatrix obtained by deleting the first row/column from the matrices in
B_{k}
; define
\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\prime}
for
B_{k}^{\prime}
in the same fashion as
\mathcal{F}_{k}
is defined for
B_{k}
. By repeating essentially the same argument as above, we find that
\theta(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\prime})
has algebraic degree
2k-1
for all
k\geq 1
, which means all odd algebraic degrees are attainable.
∎

Appendix A
Mixed adjacency matrices

We generalize the main arguments of
[
4
]
to the setting of mixed graphs. The main ideas bear strong resemblance to those in
[
4
]
, but the notion of subgraphs in mixed graphs makes the analysis more difficult. Unless otherwise specified, in this section, we consider
\rho\in(1,\infty)
to be a fixed constant. Also, we fix a mixed adjacency matrix
A
of size
r
in most of the section.

Proposition A.1
.

Fix
\rho\in(1,\infty)
and let
A
be a size
r
mixed adjacency matrix. For all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r}
,

\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}^{\intercal}A_{\rho}\mathbf{x}-\frac{1}{2}\norm{\vb x}_{1}\leq w_{\rho}\left(A\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}\right\rrbracket\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}^{\intercal}A_{\rho}\mathbf{x}.

Proof.

This is similar to Equation (1) of
[
4
]
.
Let
A=(U,D)
and
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})
. Using notation from
Definition

4.3
, let
A\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}\right\rrbracket
have components
W_{1},\dots,W_{r}
.
The counts of undirected edges and directed edges between vertex components
W_{i}
and
W_{j}
are
\frac{1}{2}(U_{ij}+U_{ji})x_{i}x_{j}
and

\frac{1}{2}(D_{ij}+D_{ji})x_{i}x_{j}
, respectively, and the number of undirected edges within component
W_{i}
is

U_{ii}\binom{x_{i}}{2}
.
Summing yields the desired result.
∎

We note a crude bound on the density of any mixed adjacency matrix that will be useful later;
g_{\rho}(A)<\rho
, as for each mixed adjacency matrix
A
, all terms of
{A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}
are at most
\rho
.

We will establish a link between the density
g_{\rho}(A)
and the asymptotic maximum of
w_{\rho}\left(A\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}\right\rrbracket\right)
as
\norm{\vb x}_{1}
grows large. To do this, we will consider the following analogue of Definition 2 in
[
4
]
:

Definition A.2

(Maximal mixed matrix graph)
.

Let
A
be a mixed adjacency matrix of size
r
, choose
\rho\in(1,\infty)
, and let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

(i)

Let
\mathbf{x}^{(n)}_{\rho,A}
maximize

w_{\rho}\left(A\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}\right\rrbracket\right)

over all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r}
with
\norm{\vb{x}}_{1}=n
.

(ii)

Let
\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,A}^{(n)}\coloneqq A\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}^{(n)}_{\rho,A}\right\rrbracket
be the
maximal mixed matrix graph
on
n
vertices.

In essence,
\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,A}^{(n)}
is the
n
-vertex asymmetric blowup of
A
with the largest weighted edge count of all such blowups.
By applying
Definition

A.2
and
Proposition

A.1
we can draw the following conclusion, similar to Equation 3 in
[
4
]
:

Proposition A.3
.

Let
A
be a mixed adjacency matrix and
\rho\in(1,\infty)
. For all positive integers
n
,

w_{\rho}\left(\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,A}^{(n)}\right)=\frac{n^{2}}{2}g_{\rho}(A)+o\left(n^{2}\right).

A.1.
Convergence

We first observe that maximal mixed graphs are roughly regular, in a suitable weighted sense via a Zykov symmetrization-style argument.

Lemma A.4

(Zykov symmetrization)
.

Let
A
be a mixed adjacency matrix,
n
be a positive integer, and
\rho\in(1,\infty)
.
Let the
\rho
-weighted degree
of a vertex
v\in V(G)
be
\deg_{\rho}v:=\degu v+\rho\degd v
;
Then
\absolutevalue{\degw v_1 - \degw v_2}\leq\rho
for any vertices
v_{1},v_{2}
of
\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,A}^{(n)}
.

Proof.

Let
A=(U,D)
, and
\mathbf{x}^{(n)}_{\rho,A}
be the vector from
Definition

A.2
.
By the construction given in
Definition

4.3
, if
v_{1},v_{2}
are in the same component
W_{i}
then
\deg_{\rho}{v_{1}}=\deg_{\rho}{v_{2}}
.
Thus, assume to the contrary (without loss of generality) that there are
v_{1}\in W_{1}
and
v_{2}\in W_{2}
such that
\deg_{\rho}{v_{1}}>\deg_{\rho}{v_{2}}+\rho
.

Consider the vector
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}=\mathbf{x}^{(n)}_{\rho,A}+(1,-1,0,\dots,0)
(i.e., the mixed graph that results from moving vertex
v_{2}
from
W_{2}
to
W_{1}
). We have
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r}
and
\norm{\vb{x}'}_{1}=n
, but

w_{\rho}\left(A\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\right\rrbracket\right)=w_{\rho}\left(A\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}^{(n)}_{\rho,A}\right\rrbracket\right)+\deg_{\rho}{v_{1}}-\deg_{\rho}{v_{2}}-\big({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\big)_{12}+U_{11}>w_{\rho}\left(A\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}^{(n)}_{\rho,A}\right\rrbracket\right),

where the last inequality follows from

\deg_{\rho}{v_{1}}-\deg_{\rho}{v_{2}}>\rho\geq\left({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)_{12}

and
U_{11}\geq 0
.
This contradicts the maximality of
\mathbf{x}^{(n)}_{\rho,A}
.
∎

We will now prove the convergence lemma,

Lemma

4.10
.
The bulk of the proof is in several claims. Roughly speaking, the first claim establishes a relation between (
4.2
) and the argmax of
\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}A_{\rho}\mathbf{y}
, the second shows that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{x}_{\rho,A}^{(n)}
exists, and the third shows that the limit has the desired properties.

Claim A.5
.

Any vector
\mathbf{y}
which maximizes
\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}A_{\rho}\mathbf{y}
subject to
\mathbf{y}\in\triangle^{r-1}
satisfies (
4.2
).

Proof.

This follows via Lagrange multipliers. Since

\nabla\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}A_{\rho}\mathbf{y}=\left(A_{\rho}+A_{\rho}^{\intercal}\right)\mathbf{y}=2\left({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)\mathbf{y}\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla\norm{\vb{y}}_{1}=\mathbf{1},

a maximizing vector
\mathbf{y}^{*}\in\triangle^{r-1}
of
\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}A_{\rho}\mathbf{y}
either has some coordinate equal to zero (i.e., is on the boundary of
\triangle^{r-1}
), or satisfies

0=\Big(\nabla\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}A_{\rho}\mathbf{y}-\lambda\nabla\norm{\vb{y}}_{1}\Big)\Big|_{\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{y}^{*}}=2\left({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)\mathbf{y}^{*}-\lambda\mathbf{1}

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

We dispose of the first case: suppose some coordinate of
\mathbf{y}^{*}
were equal to zero; let
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}
be the
(r-1)
-dimensional vector with that coordinate removed from
\mathbf{y}^{*}
, and
A^{\prime}
be the principal submatrix with the row and column corresponding to that coordinate removed. Then we have
g_{\rho}(A^{\prime})\geq{\mathbf{y}^{\prime}}^{\intercal}A^{\prime}\mathbf{y}^{\prime}={\mathbf{y}^{*}}^{\intercal}A^{\prime}\mathbf{y}^{*}=g_{\rho}(A)
, which implies
g_{\rho}(A^{\prime})=g_{\rho}(A)
, contradicting the fact that
A
is condensed.

In the second case, we have

\lambda(\mathbf{y}^{*})^{\intercal}\mathbf{1}=2(\mathbf{y}^{*})^{\intercal}\left({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)\mathbf{y}^{*}=2g_{\rho}(A)

which implies
\lambda=\frac{2g_{\rho}(A)}{(\mathbf{y}^{*})^{\intercal}\mathbf{1}}=\frac{2g_{\rho}(A)}{\norm{\vb{y}^*}_{1}}=2g_{\rho}(A)
; hence

2\left({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)\mathbf{y}^{*}=\lambda\mathbf{1}=2g_{\rho}(A)\mathbf{1}.

∎

Claim A.6
.

If (
4.2
) has a solution, then it is unique and has all coordinates strictly positive.

Proof.

If
\mathbf{y}^{*}
is a solution to (
4.2
) then

(\mathbf{y}^{*})^{\intercal}A_{\rho}\mathbf{y}^{*}=(\mathbf{y}^{*})^{\intercal}({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}})\mathbf{y}^{*}=(\mathbf{y}^{*})^{\intercal}\cdot g_{\rho}(A)\mathbf{1}=g_{\rho}(A),

the last equality being true since
\norm{\vb{y}^*}_{1}=1
.

We first show that no solution can have coordinates that are
0
.
Suppose to the contrary that (
4.2
) has a solution
\mathbf{y}_{0}

with some coordinate equal to
0
.
Let
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}
be the
(r-1)
-dimensional vector with that coordinate removed from

\mathbf{y}_{0}
, and let
A^{\prime}
be the principal submatrix of
A
with the row and column corresponding to that coordinate removed.
Then
\left(\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right)^{\intercal}A^{\prime}_{\rho}\mathbf{y}^{\prime}=\mathbf{y}_{0}^{\intercal}A_{\rho}\mathbf{y}_{0}=g_{\rho}(A)
,
contradicting the fact that
A
is condensed.

Now we show that there cannot be more than one solution. Suppose for the sake of
contradiction that there are two distinct vectors with nonnegative coordinates
which both satisfy (
4.2
), which means any affine combination of them does as well.
A suitable combination produces a vector satisfying (
4.2
)
with all coordinates nonnegative and at least one coordinate
0
,
reducing to the first case.
∎

Claim A.7
.

For any limit point
\widehat{\mathbf{y}}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{r})\in\triangle^{r-1}
of
\left\{\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{x}_{\rho,A}^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
,
\widehat{\mathbf{y}}
is a solution of (
4.2
).

Proof.

By
Proposition

A.1
and
Proposition

A.3
,

(A.1)

\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{x}^{(n)}_{\rho,A}\right)^{\intercal}A_{\rho}\left(\mathbf{x}^{(n)}_{\rho,A}\right)=w_{\rho}\left(A\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}^{(n)}_{\rho,A}\right\rrbracket\right)+O(n)=\frac{n^{2}}{2}g_{\rho}(A)+o(n^{2}).

Let
0<n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots
be a sequence of positive integers such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\mathbf{x}^{(n_{k})}_{\rho,A}=\widehat{\mathbf{y}}
. Then, (
A.1
)
implies that
\widehat{\mathbf{y}}^{\intercal}A_{\rho}\widehat{\mathbf{y}}=g_{\rho}(A)
.
Now for all integers

,
write
\mathbf{x}^{(n_{k})}_{\rho,A}=\left(x^{(n_{k})}_{1},\ldots,x^{(n_{k})}_{r}\right)
,
and let
v_{1},v_{2}
be two vertices
in
A\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}^{(n_{k})}_{\rho,A}\right\rrbracket
. By

A.4
,
all weighted degrees of the vertices are equal up to a difference of at most

Using notation from

4.3
, assume without loss of generality that

and
v_{2}\in W_{2}
. Let
A=(U,D)
. Recall that

have almost equal weighted degree. Taking the limit

, we have quantitatively that

\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)_{1j}y_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)_{2j}y_{j},

i.e., the first two coordinates of the vector

\left({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)\widehat{\mathbf{y}}
are equal. Repeating this reasoning, we see that all coordinates in the vector are equal, i.e.,

\left({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)\widehat{\mathbf{y}}=c\mathbf{1}

.
Since
g_{\rho}(A)=\widehat{\mathbf{y}}^{\intercal}A_{\rho}\widehat{\mathbf{y}}=\widehat{\mathbf{y}}^{\intercal}(A_{\rho})^{\intercal}\widehat{\mathbf{y}}
, we have

g_{\rho}(A)=\widehat{\mathbf{y}}^{\intercal}({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}})\widehat{\mathbf{y}}=\widehat{\mathbf{y}}^{\intercal}(c,c,\dots,c)=c\cdot\norm{\widehat{\vb{y}}}_{1}=c,

which means indeed
({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}})\widehat{\mathbf{y}}=g_{\rho}(A)\mathbf{1}
, so
(

) holds for
\widehat{\mathbf{y}}
.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

4.10
.

Now we are ready to prove the lemma.
Combining the three claims gives the desired conclusion:

. the sequence

∎

We can now strengthen our earlier Zykov symmetrization argument in a similar fashion to Lemma 1 of

Proposition A.10

(Lagrangians)

Fix

\left({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)_{ij}>U_{ii}

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{y}^{*}_{\rho,A}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{r})

i=1,j=2
with

\sum_{k=3}^{r}\left({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)_{1k}y_{k}\geq\sum_{k=3}^{r}\left({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)_{2k}y_{k}.

Let
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}=(y_{1}+y_{2},0,y_{3},\dots,y_{r})
. By
Lemma

4.10
,
\mathbf{y}^{*}_{\rho,A}
is the unique optimal vector, so

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\left(\mathbf{y}^{*}_{\rho,A}\right)^{\intercal}A_{\rho}\mathbf{y}^{*}_{\rho,A}-\left(\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right)^{\intercal}A\mathbf{y}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=y_{2}^{2}\left(U_{22}-U_{11}\right)+y_{2}\sum_{k=3}^{r}2\left(\left({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)_{2k}-\left({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)_{1k}\right)y_{k}+2\big(({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}})_{12}-U_{11}\big)y_{1}y_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\big(({A}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}})_{12}-U_{11}\big)y_{1}y_{2}.\qed

Importantly, the above implies that if all
U_{ii}
are equal, then
A\left\llbracket\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket
has
complete underlying undirected graph
, meaning every pair of vertices is connected by some edge.

A.2.
Augmentation of mixed adjacency matrices

Here, we strengthen §3 of
[
4
]
, generalizing the results to mixed graphs. Our primary goal is to find a small mixed adjacency matrix that yields a
\rho
-weighted blowup of high density. We will construct such mixed adjacency matrix by showing that we can grow a template by systematically adding vertices in a way that guarantees that the density increases at each step.
(In later sections we will analyze the procedure given here to ensure that it terminates after finitely many steps at an “optimal” mixed graph to blow up).

We term this addition process
augmentation
; we begin with a condensed mixed adjacency matrix
A
of size
r
, then add a row and column to
A
, equivalent to adding a new vertex part
W_{r+1}
(as in
Definition

4.3
). This forms a new mixed adjacency matrix
A^{\prime}
of size
r+1
and higher density.
The key result,
Lemma

A.13
, will enable us to find an asymptotically maximal mixed adjacency matrix by repeated augmentation.

Definition A.11

(Augmentation)
.

Fix
\rho\in(1,\infty)
.
Let
B=(U,D)
be a mixed adjacency matrix of size
r+1
with
U_{(r+1)(r+1)}=0
.
Let
A=(U^{\prime},D^{\prime})
be the principal submatrix of
B
obtained by removing the

(r+1)
th row and column from both
U
and
D
.
Further suppose
A
is condensed with respect to
\rho
, with optimal vector

\mathbf{y}^{*}_{\rho,A}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{r})
, and

(A.2)

\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left({B}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)_{(r+1)j}y_{j}>g_{\rho}(A).

Then we say that
B
is obtained from
A
by
augmentation
, denoted by
A\xrightarrow{\operatorname{aug}}B
.

As alluded to earlier, we can see (via an identical argument to that in Lemma 2 of
[
4
]
) that the augmented matrix has higher density than the submatrix it arose from.

Proposition A.12
.

If
A\xrightarrow{\operatorname{aug}}B
then
g_{\rho}(A)<g_{\rho}(B)
.

In our process of repeated augmentation, we will repeatedly leverage the following result, which gives a useful condition for when we can augment a mixed adjacency matrix while preserving the property that a relatively large blowup of the matrix remains a subgraph of some specified mixed graph
G
(we will later take
G
to be a large
F
-free mixed graph, thereby using
G
as a verification that our augmentations remain
F
-free).

Lemma A.13

(Augmentation lemma)
.

Fix
\rho\in(1,\infty)
and let
A
be a condensed mixed adjacency matrix with respect to
\rho
,
and
\varepsilon
be a positive real. For all
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
there exists
N=N(A,\rho,\varepsilon,m)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
for any mixed graph
G
on
n
vertices (
n
sufficiently large) that satisfies

(i)

\deg_{\rho}v\geq(g_{\rho}(A)+\varepsilon)n
for all vertices
v\in V(G)
,

(ii)

\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,A}^{(N)}\subseteq G
,

there exists a mixed adjacency matrix
B
such that
A\xrightarrow{\operatorname{aug}}B
,
and
\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,B}^{(m)}\subseteq G
.

Proof.

Take
N
to be a fixed large integer (we precisely choose its value later in the argument).
For any mixed graph
G
on
n
vertices that satisfies the lemma conditions,
divide
V(G)
into two parts:
V_{1}
, the vertices of some copy of

\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,A}^{(N)}\subset G
, and
V_{2}=V(G)\backslash V_{1}
.
Further partition
V_{1}=W_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup W_{r}
(where

is the size of

)
according to

4.3
.
This partitioning is illustrated in

.

.

Claim A.14
.

Let
V^{\prime}\subseteq V_{2}
be the set of all vertices

such that
\deg_{\rho}(v,V_{1})\geq(g_{\rho}(A)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})N

\absolutevalue{V'}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{8\rho}n
for sufficiently large

, and any mixed graph
G
on
n
vertices satisfying the two conditions of the lemma.

Proof.

By condition (i), the weighted edge count between

and

is

\sum_{v\in V_{2}}\deg_{\rho}(v,V_{1})=\sum_{u\in V_{1}}\deg_{\rho}(u,V_{2})\geq(g_{\rho}(A)+\varepsilon)(n-N)N-\rho N.

When

(g_{\rho}(A)+\varepsilon)(n-N)N-\rho N\geq(g_{\rho}(A)+\frac{3\varepsilon}{4})(n-N)N

\deg_{\rho}(v,V_{1})\leq\rho N

\displaystyle\left(g_{\rho}(A)+\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}\right)(n-N)N\leq\sum_{v\in V_{2}}\deg_{\rho}(v,V_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V_{2}\setminus V^{\prime}}\deg_{\rho}(v,V_{1})+\sum_{v\in V^{\prime}}(v,V_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\left(g_{\rho}(A)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)N(n-N)+\rho N\absolutevalue{V'},

which simplifies to
\absolutevalue{V'}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{4\rho}(n-N)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{8\rho}n
.
∎

There are only

distinct ways for a vertex in

to be connected to

. Hence there exists

with at least
\frac{\varepsilon}{8\rho}\frac{n}{4^{N}}
vertices, such that all vertices

are
connected identically to vertices in

, and

.

Claim A.15
.

For vertex
v\in V^{\prime\prime}
and

,
the weighted degree of

with respect to

is

(A.3)

\deg_{\rho}(v,W_{j})\leq\left({B}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)_{(r+1)j}\absolutevalue{W_j}+\frac{\varepsilon N}{4r}.

Proof.

We examine all four cases in the construction of

above. Since

, we have

\deg_{\rho}(v,W_{j})\leq\rho\absolutevalue{W_j}
, so in cases (i) and (ii) where

In case (iii), since

\deg_{\rho}(v,W_{j})<\rho\frac{\varepsilon}{8r\rho}N<\frac{\varepsilon N}{4r}
.
∎

Claim A.16
.

When

.

Proof.

By

, for each vertex

Combining this with

, we get

\left(g_{\rho}(A)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)N\leq\deg_{\rho}(v,V_{1})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left({B}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{sym}}\right)_{(r+1)j}\absolutevalue{W_j}+\frac{\varepsilon N}{4}.

Dividing by

,

Let

.
Recall that

. So when

which means

by

.
∎

Finally, we will show that

.

, if

.
Thus

By construction of

, when

.
∎

A.3.
Extremal mixed adjacency matrices

We consider “extremal” mixed adjacency matrices whose blowups always yield
F
-free mixed graphs.

We begin with size
1
mixed adjacency matrices and consider all matrices which can be obtained by successive augmentation series; we certify that there are only finitely many matrices, and that the one with highest density can be blown up into asymptotically extremal
F
-free mixed graphs.

We first define a notion of
containment
: roughly speaking, the mixed adjacency matrix
A
is contained in a sequence of mixed graphs
\{G^{(n)}\}
if every mixed graph of the form
A\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}\right\rrbracket
is a subgraph of some
G\in\{G^{(n)}\}
.

Definition A.17

(Containment)
.

For
\rho\in(1,\infty)
, let
A
be a condensed mixed adjacency matrix with respect to
\rho
,
and let
\left\{G^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
be a sequence of mixed graphs.
We say
A
is
contained
in the sequence if for any positive
integer
m
, there exists integer
n
such that
\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,A}^{(m)}\subseteq G^{(n)}
.

It is easy to see by
Corollary

A.8
that the following three conditions are equivalent:

(i)

A
is contained in the sequence
\left\{G^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
;

(ii)

there is an infinite sequence of integers
m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots
such that for each
m_{i}
in the sequence, there exists integer
n
so that
\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,A}^{(m_{i})}\subseteq G^{(n)}
;

(iii)

for any positive integer
t
, there exists integer
n
such that
A\left\llbracket t\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket\subseteq G^{(n)}
.

Now, following the proof of Theorem 1 in
[
4
]
, we briefly discuss the Zarankiewicz problem
[
36
]
corresponding to the
\rho
-weighted extremal problem; the extremal mixed graphs that arise will in fact be the large mixed graphs
G
on which we later apply the augmentation lemma (
Lemma

A.13
).

Definition A.18
.

Let
\rho\in(1,\infty)
,
F
be a mixed graph, and
n
be a positive integer. Let

d^{(n)}_{\rho,F}:=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}v(G)=n\\
F\not\subseteq G\end{subarray}}\left\{\min_{v\in V(G)}\deg_{\rho}v\right\}\quad,\quad Z^{(n)}_{\rho(F)}:=\argmax_{\begin{subarray}{c}v(G)=n\\
F\not\subseteq G\end{subarray}}\left\{\min_{v\in V(G)}\deg_{\rho}v\right\},

and let
a_{\rho,F}^{*}:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}
.
Then there exists an infinite sequence of increasing integers
N_{\rho,F}=(n_{1},n_{2},\dots)

such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n_{j})}}{n_{j}}=a_{\rho,F}^{*}.

In other words
d^{(n)}_{\rho,F}
is the maximal minimum-degree across all
n
vertex
F
-free mixed graphs, and
Z^{(n)}_{\rho(F)}
is a mixed graph achieving this maximum.
We will use
Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}
as the large
F
-free mixed graph
G
in
Lemma

A.13
; by construction all of its vertices have high minimum degree (equal to
d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}
).

Denote the two mixed adjacency matrices of size
1
by
\mathbf{0}=([0],[0])
and
K=([1],[0])
. The latter is named thus because
K\left\llbracket t\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket
is the complete graph
K_{t}
.

Proposition A.19
.

Let
F
be a mixed graph with
\theta(F)\in(1,\infty)
. For any
\rho\in(1,\infty)
,
K
is the only condensed mixed adjacency matrix with all diagonal elements of its undirected part equal to
1
which can be contained in the sequence
\left\{Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in N_{\rho,F}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that any condensed mixed adjacency matrix
A=(U,D)
of size at least
2

with all diagonal elements of
U
equal to
1
cannot satisfy this property.
By
Proposition

A.10
,
\{D_{12},D_{21}\}=\{2,0\}
; without loss of generality suppose
D_{12}=2
.
For any positive integer
t
, there exists
n\in N_{\rho,F}
such that

A\left\llbracket t\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket\subseteq Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}
. We have
F\not\subseteq A[t\mathbf{1}]
since
Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}
is
F
-free by
Definition

A.18
. Now consider the mixed adjacency matrix

A^{\prime}=\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}0&2\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\right).

We have
A^{\prime}\left\llbracket t\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket\subseteq A\left\llbracket t\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket
, so

F\not\subseteq A^{\prime}\left\llbracket t\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket
for all positive integers
t
,
which means
F\not\subseteq M(x,s)
(recall
Definition

3.2
) for any positive integer
s
and any
x\in(0,1)
.
This implies
\theta(F)=1
by
Proposition

3.3
, contradiction.
∎

Note that, since
\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,\mathbf{0}}^{(m)}
is simply an empty graph on
m
vertices,
\mathbf{0}
is contained in
\left\{Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in N_{\rho,F}}
for all mixed graphs
F
and
\rho\in(1,\infty)
.
Based on the above proposition, we now define
\mathcal{E}_{\rho,F}=\{\mathbf{0},K\}
if
K
is contained in
\left\{Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in N_{\rho,F}}
, and
\{\mathbf{0}\}
otherwise.

We will leverage
\mathcal{E}_{\rho,F}
to obtain an extremal
F
-free family of mixed graphs. We first suppose that
K\in\mathcal{E}_{\rho,F}
.

Proposition A.20
.

Let
\rho\in(1,\infty)
and let
F
be a mixed graph with
\theta(F)\in(1,\infty)
.
If
K\in\mathcal{E}_{\rho,F}
then the sequence
\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}
converges and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}=1=g_{\rho}(K).

Proof.

Since
K
is contained in the sequence
\left\{Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in N_{\rho,F}}
,
for any positive integer
t
there exists
n\in N_{\rho,F}

such that the undirected complete graph
K_{t}=K\left\llbracket t\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket\subseteq Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}
, thus
F\not\subseteq K_{t}
for all
t
, implying
d_{\rho,F}^{(t)}\geq t-1
for all positive integers
t
. Hence

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{n-1}{n}=1.

Suppose to the contrary that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}>1
,
then there exists

\varepsilon>0
such that
d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}>(1+\varepsilon)n
for all sufficiently large
n\in N_{\rho,F}
.
All conditions for
Lemma

A.13
on
K
and
Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}
are met for large
n
, so there exists mixed adjacency matrix
B
such that
K\xrightarrow{\operatorname{aug}}B
,
and
\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,B}^{(m)}\subseteq Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}
.
This implies
F\not\subseteq\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,B}^{(m)}
. Since augmentation increases density, there are only two possible
B
values:

B=\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}0&2\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\text{ or }\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
2&0\end{bmatrix}\right).

By the same reasoning as in the proof of
Proposition

A.19
,
we must have
F\not\subseteq M(x,t)
for any positive integer
t
and real number
x\in(0,1)
, thus
\theta(F)=1
, contradiction.
Therefore
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}\leq 1
.

Hence we conclude that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}=1=g_{\rho}(K).

∎

The case where
K\not\in\mathcal{E}_{\rho,F}
is more complicated but follows the same premise.

Definition A.21
.

Let
F
be a mixed graph with
\theta(F)\in(1,\infty)
. Given
\rho\in(1,\infty)
,
suppose
K\notin\mathcal{E}_{\rho,F}
.
Define
\mathcal{B}_{\rho,F}
as the set of all mixed adjacency matrices
B
which are condensed with respect to
\rho
, and such that
B
is contained
in the sequence of mixed graphs
\left\{Z^{(n)}_{\rho,F}\right\}_{n\in N_{\rho,F}}
, and
B
is obtainable from some finite (possibly empty) sequence

\mathbf{0}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{aug}}B_{1}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{sub}}B_{2}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{aug}}B_{3}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{sub}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\operatorname{sub}}B_{2k}=B.

We would like to choose the mixed adjacency matrix
B
of maximal density from
\mathcal{B}_{\rho,F}
. The following proposition ensures that this is in fact possible:

Proposition A.22

(Finiteness)
.

If
K\notin\mathcal{E}_{\rho,F}
, then the set
\mathcal{B}_{\rho,F}
is finite.

Proof.

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
\mathcal{B}_{\rho,F}
is infinite. Note that the undirected part of each
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho,F}
has all diagonal elements equal to zero, since augmentation only adds
0
s to the diagonals.

Because there are only finitely many mixed adjacency matrices
of any given size, there are arbitrarily large mixed adjacency matrices

B\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho,F}
contained in
the sequence
\left\{Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in N_{\rho,F}}
.

Take
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho,F}
of arbitrarily large size. Let
B^{\prime}=(U^{\prime},D^{\prime})
be the mixed adjacency matrix of the same size, with
U^{\prime}
having all
0
s on the diagonal and all
1
s elsewhere, and
D^{\prime}
having all elements
0
.
By
Proposition

A.10
, each pair of vertices in
B\left\llbracket\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket
are connected by
an edge, so
B^{\prime}\left\llbracket\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket\subseteq B\left\llbracket\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket
. Hence the fact that
B
is contained in

\left\{Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in N_{\rho,F}}
implies that
B^{\prime}
is as well,
which means we can find arbitrarily large complete undirected graphs contained in

\left\{Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in N_{\rho,F}}
.
Therefore
K
is contained in
\left\{Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in N_{\rho,F}}
, implying
K\in\mathcal{E}_{\rho,F}
, contradiction.
∎

Now we give the aforementioned result on
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}
.

Proposition A.23
.

Let
F
be a mixed graph with
\theta(F)\in(1,\infty)
, and
\rho\in(1,\infty)
.
If
K\notin\mathcal{E}_{\rho,F}
, then the sequence
\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}
converges and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}=\max_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho,F}}\big\{g_{\rho}(B)\big\}.

Proof.

Let
B^{*}
be any mixed adjacency matrix in
\mathcal{B}_{\rho,F}
with maximal density.
By
Proposition

A.3
and
Lemma

A.4
, given any positive integer
n
,
all vertices
v
in
\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,B^{*}}^{(n)}
have weighted degree
\deg_{\rho}v=g_{\rho}\left(B^{*}\right)n+o(n)
.
Since
F\not\subseteq\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,B^{*}}^{(n)}
, we have
d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}\geq g_{\rho}\left(B^{*}\right)n+o(n)
for all
n
, and

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}\geq g_{\rho}\left(B^{*}\right).

On the other hand, suppose

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in N_{\rho,F}\\
n\to\infty\end{subarray}}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}>g_{\rho}\left(B^{*}\right).

There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that any sufficiently large
n\in N_{\rho,F}
satisfies
d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}>\left(g_{\rho}\left(B^{*}\right)+\varepsilon\right)n
.
For any
m
, take
N=N(A,\rho,\varepsilon,m)
from
Lemma

A.13
.
There exists some
n\in N_{\rho,F}
such that
\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,B^{*}}^{(N)}\subseteq Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}
. We established earlier that
\deg_{\rho}v>\left(g_{\rho}\left(B^{*}\right)+\varepsilon\right)n
for all vertices
v
of
Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}
. Hence
Lemma

A.13
on
B^{*}
and
Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}
for large
n
, states that
there exist mixed adjacency matrix
B^{\prime}
such that
B^{*}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{aug}}B^{\prime}
(dependent on
m
and
n=n(m)
) and
\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,B^{\prime}}^{(m)}\subseteq Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}
.
We then take
B^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{sub}}B^{\prime\prime}
then
B^{\prime\prime}
is condensed and
\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,B^{\prime\prime}}^{(m)}\subseteq Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}
.

Note there are only finitely many such
B^{\prime\prime}
because the size of each is at most one more than the size of
B^{*}
. Thus there is at least one such
B^{\prime\prime}
such that
\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,B^{\prime\prime}}^{(m)}\subseteq Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}
is true for infinitely many pairs
m,n(m)
. This
B^{\prime\prime}
is contained in the sequence
\left\{Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in N_{\rho,F}}
, so
B^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho,F}
and
g_{\rho}(B^{\prime\prime})>g_{\rho}\left(B^{*}\right)
, contradicting the maximality of
g_{\rho}\left(B^{*}\right)
in
\mathcal{B}_{\rho,F}
. Therefore

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}\leq g_{\rho}(B^{*}).

∎

Combining the two cases,
Proposition

A.20
and
Proposition

A.23
allow for the following definition and corollary:

Definition A.24

(Extremal mixed adjacency matrix)
.

Let
F
be a mixed graph such that
\theta(F)\in(1,\infty)
, and let
\rho\in(1,\infty)
. We use
B_{\rho,F}^{*}
to denote the
extremal mixed adjacency matrix
for
F
with respect to
\rho
, defined by the following:

(i)

B_{\rho,F}^{*}\coloneqq K
if
K\in\mathcal{E}_{\rho,F}
;

(ii)

B_{\rho,F}^{*}\coloneqq\argmax_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho,F}}\;g_{\rho}(B)
otherwise.

Corollary A.25
.

For a mixed graph
F
with
\theta(F)\in(1,\infty)
, and for
\rho\in(1,\infty)
,
the sequence
\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}
converges to the density of its extremal mixed adjacency matrix, namely

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}=g_{\rho}\left(B_{\rho,F}^{*}\right).

Furthermore,
B_{\rho,F}^{*}
is contained in the sequence of mixed graphs
\left\{Z_{\rho,F}^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in N_{\rho,F}}
and therefore

F\not\subseteq\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,B_{\rho,F}^{*}}^{(n)}
for any positive integer
n
.

Our aim is now to show that the maximal graphs obtained by optimally blowing up
B_{\rho,F}^{*}
are asymptotically extremal. We first make this notion precise in
Definition

A.26
, then prove it in
Lemma

A.27
.

Definition A.26

(Asymptotically extremal sequence)
.

Let
F
be a mixed graph; we say that the sequence of mixed graphs
\left\{G^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
, where each
G^{(n)}
has
n
vertices, is
asymptotically extremal
for
F
(with respect to
\rho
) if

•

F\not\subseteq G^{(n)}
for any
n
;

•

for any
\varepsilon>0
, for sufficiently large
n
, any
n
-vertex mixed graph
G
such that
w_{\rho}\left(G\right)>w_{\rho}\left(G^{(n)}\right)+\varepsilon n^{2}
must satisfy
F\subseteq G
.

Lemma A.27

(Extremal lemma)
.

Let
F
be a mixed graph with
\theta(F)\in(1,\infty)
, and
\rho\in(1,\infty)
. The sequence of maximal mixed graphs
\left\{\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,B_{\rho,F}^{*}}^{(n)}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
is asymptotically extremal for
F
.

Proof.

We use
B^{*}
to denote
B_{\rho,F}^{*}
in this proof.
Let
\varepsilon
be a positive real and
G
be an
n
-vertex mixed graph (for large
n
) such that
w_{\rho}\left(G\right)>w_{\rho}\left(\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,B^{*}}^{(n)}\right)+\varepsilon n^{2}
.
By
Proposition

A.3
, when
n
is sufficiently large,

w_{\rho}\left(\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,B^{*}}^{(n)}\right)>\left(g_{\rho}(B^{*})-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}
,
so
w_{\rho}\left(G\right)>\left(g_{\rho}(B^{*})+\frac{3\varepsilon}{2}\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}
.

Claim A.28
.

When
n
is sufficiently large, there exists a subgraph
H\subseteq G
on
k
vertices where

\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{2\rho}}n\leq k\leq n
, and

\min_{v\in V(H)}\deg_{\rho}v>\left(g_{\rho}(B^{*})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)k.

(Here
\deg_{\rho}v
refers to the weighted degree of
v
in
H
.)

Proof.

Construct a sequence of mixed graphs
G^{(n)},G^{(n-1)},\dots
such that
G^{(n)}=G

and
G^{(n-j)}
is obtained from
G^{(n-j+1)}
by removing a vertex
v
with

\deg_{\rho}v\leq\left(g_{\rho}(B^{*})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)(n-j+1)
. The sequence terminates when such a vertex does not exist.
Then when
n
is sufficiently large,
G^{(n-j)}
in the sequence has

\displaystyle w_{\rho}\left(G^{(n-j)}\right)

\displaystyle>\left(g_{\rho}(B^{*})+\frac{3\varepsilon}{2}\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\left(g_{\rho}(B^{*})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\frac{j(2n-j+1)}{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(g_{\rho}(B^{*})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\frac{(n-j)^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon n^{2}+O(n)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon n^{2}.

But
w_{\rho}\left(G^{(n-j)}\right)\leq\rho\binom{n-j}{2}
,
so
\rho\frac{(n-j)^{2}}{2}>\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon n^{2}
, which implies

n-j\geq\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{2\rho}}n
, i.e., the sequence of mixed graphs must terminate
at some graph
H
on
k\geq\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{2\rho}}n
vertices.
Then, by construction,

\min_{v\in V(H)}\deg_{\rho}v>\left(g_{\rho}(B^{*})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)k.

∎

Now we can finish the proof of the lemma. For the sake of contradiction, assume there exists

\varepsilon>0
, an infinite sequence of integers
n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots
, and mixed graphs
G_{j}

on
n_{j}
vertices, such that for all sufficiently large
j
,

w_{\rho}\left(G_{j}\right)>w_{\rho}\left(\mathfrak{G}_{\rho,B^{*}}^{(n_{j})}\right)+\varepsilon n_{j}^{2}
and
F\not\subseteq G_{j}
.
By
A.28
, there is a subgraph
H_{j}\subseteq G_{j}

(therefore
F\not\subseteq H_{j}
) on
k_{j}
vertices with

\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{2\rho}}n_{j}\leq k_{j}\leq n_{j}
and

\min_{v\in V(H_{j})}\deg_{\rho}v>\left(g_{\rho}(B^{*})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)k_{j}
.
Therefore
\frac{d^{(k_{j})}_{\rho,F}}{k_{j}}>g_{\rho}(B^{*})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for all sufficiently large
j

(recall
Definition

A.18
). Thus

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}\geq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(k_{j})}}{k_{j}}\geq g_{\rho}(B^{*})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

But this contradicts
Corollary

A.25
which states that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{\rho,F}^{(n)}}{n}=g_{\rho}(B^{*})
.
∎

Finally, note that while the set
\mathcal{B}_{\rho,F}
is finite, its size can vary based on the value of
\rho
. We now must relate this to
\mathcal{M}_{F}
, as defined in
Section

4.1
.

Proposition A.29
.

Let
F
be a mixed graph with at least one directed edge and
\theta(F)\in(1,\infty)
. Then
B_{\rho,F}^{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{F}
for any
\rho\in(1,\infty)
.

Proof.

If
K\in\mathcal{E}_{\rho,F}
, then
B_{\rho,F}^{*}=K\in\mathcal{M}_{F}
by
Definition

A.24
.
Otherwise
K\not\in\mathcal{E}_{\rho,F}
. Let
B_{\rho,F}^{*}=(U,D)
.

Conditions (i) and (iii) of
Definition

4.9
are met by
Definition

A.21
, and condition (v) is met by
Proposition

A.10
.
Note that if
D
is the zero matrix then
g(B_{\rho,F}^{*})=\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\triangle^{r-1}}\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}U\mathbf{y}<1
, contradiction, so (iv) is true.
By (iv) we know
B_{\rho,F}^{*}\left\llbracket\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket
is a complete mixed graph with at least one directed edge. Hence its size is at most
\upchi(F^{\rhd})-1
, since otherwise for large enough
t^{\prime}
and
t
, we would have
F\subseteq F^{\rhd}[t]\subseteq B_{\rho,F}^{*}\left\llbracket t^{\prime}\mathbf{1}\right\rrbracket
, contradiction. This means (ii) is true as well, so all conditions are satisfied and
B_{\rho,F}^{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{F}
.
∎

The main result follows:

Proof of
Theorem

4.11
.

By
Lemma

A.27
,

\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v(G)\to\infty\\
F\not\subseteq G\end{subarray}}w_{\rho}\left(G\right)/\tbinom{n}{2}=g_{\rho}(B_{\rho,F}^{*}).

By
Proposition

A.29
, we have
B_{\rho,F}^{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{F}
. Furthermore,
B
is
F
-free for all
B\in\mathcal{M}_{F}
; thus
Lemma

A.27
implies
g_{\rho}(B)\leq g_{\rho}(B_{\rho,F}^{*})
. Hence

\max_{B\in\mathcal{M}_{F}}g_{\rho}(B)=g_{\rho}(B_{\rho,F}^{*}),

and the theorem is proved.
∎

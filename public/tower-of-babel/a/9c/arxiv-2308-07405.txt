1.
Introduction

Mantel’s Theorem (see
[
8
]
,1907) and the subsequent renowned Turán’s Theorem (see
[
10
]
, 1941) initiated the field of extremal graph theory. Turán’s Theorem states that for any graph
G
with
n
vertices, if the number of edges exceeds the extremal number
t_{n,k-1}
(explained in the following paragraphs), the graph must contain a clique
K_{k}
as a subgraph. Notably, Erdős and Simonovits (in
[
4
]
, 1983) later observed that if a graph contains
\Omega(n^{2})
more edges than the aforementioned extremal number, then the number of clique
K_{k}
contained in
G
is at least
\Omega(n^{2})
. This insight was further extended to encompass all graphs and hypergraphs. This crucial discovery, later dubbed the “supersaturation phenomenon” has significantly influenced contemporary research in extremal combinatorics. For instance, the recent breakthrough known as the ”Hypergraph Containers” method finds its applications mainly when the supersaturation results are applicable. For a deeper understanding, consult the papers and surveys
[
9
]
,
[
1
]
, and
[
2
]
.

In this paper, we direct our attention to the colored version of extremal problems.
Consider an edge-colored graph
G
, where we denote
e(G)
and
c(G)
the number of edges and colors in
G
, respectively. A
rainbow clique
K_{k}
within
G
refers to
k
-complete subgraph where all the edge colors are distinct. In 2014, Li, Ning, Xu and Zhang
[
6
]
established specific sufficient conditions for the presence of a rainbow triangle in terms of the sum of the number of colors and edges within the host graph
G
. One of these conditions is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
6
]
)
Let
G
be a colored graph on
n
vertices satisfying
e(G)+c(G)\geq{n\choose 2}+n
. Then
G
contains a rainbow triangle.

Later in 2016, Xu, Hu, Wang and Zhang
[
12
]
extended the condition for the existence of a rainbow clique
K_{k}
, where
k\geq 4
. Let
T_{n,k}
represent the Turán graph, defined as a complete
k
-partite graph with
n
vertices, ensuring the parts have the most equal sizes possible. This construction guarantees a unique graph up to isomorphism. We denote
t_{n,k}
as the number of edges of
T_{n,k}
, also known as the Turán number. The generalized condition can be concisely expressed as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
12
]
)
Let
G
be an edge-colored graph on
n
vertices satisfying
e(G)+c(G)\geq{n\choose 2}+t_{n,k-2}+2
, then
G
contains a rainbow clique
K_{k}
, where
k\geq 4
.

It is natural to consider the subsequent problem of demonstrating an abundance of rainbow cliques in the spirit of Erdős and Simonovits.
Our first result, stated below, offers a supersaturation theorem in this context.
Assuming the common criterion of substantial
e(G)+c(G)
values, the outcome showcases the existence of
\Omega(n^{k})
rainbow cliques.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be an edge-colored graph on
n
vertices. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
\delta>0
such that if
e(G)+c(G)\geq(1+\frac{k-3}{k-2}+2\varepsilon){n\choose 2}
and
k\geq 3
, then for sufficiently large
n
,
G
contains at least
\delta n^{k}
rainbow cliques
K_{k}
.

This result demonstrates a notable enhancement over Theorem 4 of the paper
[
7
]
, which specifically addressed the case of
k=3
. Additionally, it is worth noting that
[
7
]
required a more stringent condition, namely, a large minimum color degree of the graph.
Our proof introduces a modification of the classical method known as the Varnavides averaging argument (refer to
[
11
]
).

It is also possible to describe extremal colored graphs for this extremal problem. The paper
[
5
]
offers insights into characterizing extremal graphs
G
that lack rainbow triangles when
e(G)+c(G)
reaches its maximum. Additionally, in
[
3
]
, the authors characterized the edge-colored graphs without rainbow
K_{k}
for
k\geq 6
that maximize the sum of edge number and color number. Specifically, if a graph
G
on
n
vertices satisfies
e(G)+c(G)={n\choose 2}+t_{n,k-2}+1
and does not contain a rainbow
K_{k}
, then
G
is complete and contains a rainbow
T_{n,k-2}
.
The graph
G
can be obtained as follows, up to isomorphism.

(1)

Firstly, divide
n
vertices into
k-2
parts as evenly as possible.

(2)

Then, assign pairwise distinct colors to all the edges between different parts, and assign all the edges within each part a new color.

This construction yields the desired extremal graphs under the given conditions.

In the same paper
[
3
]
, the cases
k=4
and
k=5
were also considered, but were regarded as having ”little hope” for arriving at a nice characterization.

In this paper, we complete this puzzle by characterizing the extremal graphs that do not have a rainbow clique
K_{k}
, for
k=4
and 5. We demonstrate that when
n
is slightly larger than
k
, the extremal graphs without a rainbow
K_{4}
(or
K_{5}
) are also complete and have the same coloring pattern with the ones without rainbow
K_{k}
,
k\geq 6
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be an edge-colored graph with
n
vertices. If
e(G)+c(G)={n\choose 2}+t_{n,k-2}+1
, where either
k=4
and
n\geq 8
, or
k=5
and
n\geq 9
, and
G
does not have a rainbow
K_{k}
, then
G
satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

G
is complete and

(2)

G
has a rainbow
T_{n,k-2}
and all the edges within every part are colored alike with a new color.

Our result is sharp in the following sense. One of the reasons why the cases
k=4
and
k=5
are considered difficult in the paper
[
3
]
is that counterexamples can be found that do not satisfy the pattern described above. For instance, in
[
3
]
, an example for the case
k=5
and
n=8
was provided (see Figure 3 in their paper). In Section 2 below,
We will further provide an example of

k=4
and
n=7
. However, surprisingly, our theorem above shows that these counterexamples are the best that can be obtained.

2.
Preliminary

In this paper, we focus exclusively on simple and undirected graphs. The edge coloring of graph
G
is not necessarily proper. For any edge
e\in E(G)
, we define
c(e)=c
to be the color of
e
. We define a color
c
to be
saturated
at a vertex
v
if there exists an edge
e
adjacent to
v
with
c(e)=c
, and the color
c
does not appear in
G-v
. The saturated degree of vertex
v
, denoted as
d_{G}^{s}(v)
, represents the number of colors that are saturated at
v
in graph
G
. When there is no confusion, we can simply write
d^{s}(v)
instead of
d_{G}^{s}(v)
. Let
d(v)
be the degree of vertex
v
. It is evident that
d^{s}(v)\leq d(v)
and
\sum_{v\in V(G)}d^{s}(v)\leq 2c(G)
.
We provide some estimates regarding the Turán number
t_{n,k}
which will be needed in the proofs. Let
n=pk+i
, where
0\leq i\leq k-1
.

(2.1)

\displaystyle t_{n,k}=\frac{(k-1)(n^{2}-i^{2})}{2k}+\binom{i}{2}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{(k-1)n^{2}}{2k}-\frac{n}{4}\leq t_{n,k}\leq\lfloor\frac{(k-1)n^{2}}{2k}\rfloor

(2.3)

\displaystyle t_{n+1,k}-t_{n.k}=n-\frac{n-i}{k}

If
G
is a complete graph edge-colored by
t_{n,k-1}+1
colors and does not contain a rainbow
K_{k}
,
according to Theorem
1.2
, we know that for any
v\in V(G)
,

c(G-v)\leq t_{n-1,k-2}+1
and
d^{s}(v)\geq t_{n,k-2}-t_{n-1,k-2}
.
In the case where
n
is divisible by
k-2
,
the bounds of the summation of saturated degrees in
G
become special.
Since
n(t_{n,k-2}-t_{n-1,k-2})=(k-3)n^{2}/(k-2)=2t_{n,k-2}
, we can deduce that

(2.4)

\displaystyle 2t_{n,k-1}+2=2c(G)\geq\sum_{v\in V(G)}d^{s}(v)\geq n(t_{n,k-2}-t_{n-1,k-2})=2t_{n,k-2}.

We can also consider an alternative method for calculating the summation of saturated degrees in
G
. Let
c_{t}
be the number of colors that saturated by
t
vertices, for
t=0,1,2
. Then
2c_{2}+c_{1}=\sum_{v\in V(G)}d^{s}(v)
.

The first lemma we use several times is a property directly from the argument in Lemma 11 of
[
3
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
3
]
, Lemma 11)
Let
G
be an edge-colored graph on
n
vertices such that
e(G)+c(G)={n\choose 2}+t_{n,k-2}+1
and
G
does not contain a rainbow
K_{k}
, for
n>k\geq 4
, then there is a vertex
v
in

e(G-v)+c(G-v)={n-1\choose 2}+t_{n-1,k-2}+1
.

The following lemma can be found in
[
5
]
, although here we consider a slightly different scenario. The proof is provided in Appendix.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
e(G)+c(G)=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}-1
and
G
does not contain a rainbow triangle, monochromatic
C_{3}
or
P_{4}
, then
G
is a complete graph
K_{n}
and
c(ij)=i
, where
ij=ji\in E(G)
, with

.

Remark 2.3
.

Note that if
c(K_{4})=3
, there are only two possibilities of an edge-colored
K_{4}
with no rainbow triangle. One possibility is as described in Lemma
2.2
. The other possibility is that there is a monochromatic
C_{4}
, and the other two edges are colored with two new distinct colors.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
K_{6}^{-}
be an edge-colored graph obtained by deleting an edge from
K_{6}
. Let the vertex set be
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
, where
V_{1}=\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
contains the two non-adjacent vertices with degree 4, and
K_{6}^{-}[V_{2}]=K_{4}
. If
c(K_{6}^{-})=11
and

has no rainbow
K_{4}
, then there is rainbow
K_{2,4}
in
K_{6}^{-}
, and either
K_{6}^{-}[V_{2}]
has neither a rainbow triangle nor a monochromatic
C_{3}
and
P_{4}
, or
K_{6}^{-}[V_{2}]
has a monochromatic
C_{4}
.

Proof.

Since
K_{6}^{-}
does not contain a rainbow
K_{4}
, if follows that for any vertex
v
in
K_{6}^{-}
,
d(v)+d^{s}(v)\geq 8
. This is because if
e(K_{6}^{-}-v)+c(K_{6}^{-}-v)\geq 18
and by Theorem
1.2
, there would be a rainbow
K_{4}
in
K_{6}^{-}-v
. By simple computation, for the vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
with
d(v_{1})=d(v_{2})=4
, we have
d^{s}(v_{1})=d^{s}(v_{2})=4
. Therefore, we obtain a rainbow
K_{2,4}
in

. Regarding

, we have that
K_{6}^{-}[V_{2}]=K_{4}
and
K_{6}^{-}[V_{2}]
has three new colors other than the eight colors in the rainbow

. It is important to note that there is no rainbow triangle with at least two new colors in

, because if there were, there would be a rainbow
K_{4}
in
K_{6}^{-}
. Consequently, there is no any rainbow triangle in
K_{6}^{-}[V_{2}]
. Based on the previous argument, there are only two ways of coloring
K_{4}
such that there is no rainbow triangle.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

If

is edge-colored with

The possible solutions of the above are

3.
Counting Rainbow Cliques

In this section we prove Theorem
1.3
. Let
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
denote two sets of vertices in
G
. We define the bipartite graph with bipartition
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
as
G[V_{1},V_{2}]
, and the induced subgraph with vertex set
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
as
G[V_{1}\cup V_{2}]
. Additionally,
e(V_{1},V_{2})
and
c(V_{1},V_{2})
represent the number of edges and colors in
G[V_{1},V_{2}]
, respectively.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. For any subgraph
F
of
G
with
m
vertices, where
m<n
is also sufficiently large, if
e(F)+c(F)\geq(1+\frac{k-3}{k-2}+\varepsilon){m\choose 2}
, then by Theorem
1.2
,
F
contains a rainbow clique. Then we consider all
m
-vertex spanning subgraphs
M
in
G
. The subgraphs that satisfy
e(M)+c(M)\geq(1+\frac{k-3}{k-2}+\varepsilon){m\choose 2}
and provide a rainbow clique are referred to as the
good
ones. Let
\eta\in(0,1)
be a value such that there are
\eta{n\choose m}
good ones, while the non-good ones are
(1-\eta){n\choose m}
.

Now we consider
\sum_{|M|=m}(e(M)+c(M))
. Firstly, since we count every edge
e\in M
for
{n-2\choose m-2}
times, so we have

(3.1)

\sum_{|M|=m}e(M)=e(G){n-2\choose m-2}.

Secondly, each color appeared in
\sum_{|M|=m}c(M)
is counted for at least
{n-2\choose m-2}
times. So we have

(3.2)

\sum_{|M|=m}c(M)\geq c(G){n-2\choose m-2}.

Therefore, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{|M|=m}(e(M)+c(M))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(e(G)+c(G)){n-2\choose m-2}.

(3.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\frac{k-3}{2(k-2)}+2\varepsilon){n\choose 2}{n-2\choose m-2}

The left side is at most

(3.5)

\eta{n\choose m}\cdot 2\cdot{m\choose 2}+(1-\eta){n\choose m}{m\choose 2}(1+\frac{k-3}{k-2}+\varepsilon)

So

(3.6)

\displaystyle 1+\frac{k-3}{k-2}+2\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\eta+(1-\eta)(1+\frac{k-3}{k-2}+\varepsilon)

(3.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\eta+(1+\frac{k-3}{k-2}+\varepsilon)-\eta(1+\frac{k-3}{k-2}+\varepsilon)

Therefore,
\eta\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\frac{k-3}{k-2}-\varepsilon}
. On the other hand, each rainbow clique
K_{k}
is contained in at most
{n-k\choose m-k}
many
m
-sets. Therefore, there exists a
\delta
such that the number of rainbow clique
K_{k}
is at least

(3.8)

\frac{\eta{n\choose m}}{{n-k\choose m-k}}=\frac{\eta{n\choose k}}{{m\choose k}}=\delta n^{k}

∎

\displaystyle\leq

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be a complete graph with
n
vertices, edge-colored with
t_{n,k-2}+1
colors, where
k=4
and
n\geq 8
, or
k=5
and
n\geq 9
. If
G
does not contain a rainbow
K_{k}
, then
G
contains a rainbow
T_{n,k-2}
as a subgraph.

Proof.

We will prove the lemma by induction on
n
.
The lemma is true for the base cases where
k=4
and
n=8
, as shown in Lemma
2.9
, and for
k=5
and
n=9
, as shown in Lemma
2.10
. Now, let us assume that the lemma holds for graphs with fewer than
n
vertices
(with
n\geq 9
for
k=4
and
n\geq 10
for
k=5
).
From Lemma
2.1
, we ascertain the existence of a vertex
v\in V(G)
with
d^{s}(v)=t_{n,k-2}-t_{n-1,k-2}
and
c(G-v)=t_{n-1,k-2}+1
.
By the induction hypothesis, we know that

contains a rainbow

.
Given that

, according to Lemma

, all edges within the partite sets of the rainbow

share a common color distinct from those in the rainbow

. We can partition the vertices of

Lemma 4.3
.

If

For

\displaystyle d(v)+d^{s}(v)=n-1+t_{n,k-2}-t_{n-1,k-2}.

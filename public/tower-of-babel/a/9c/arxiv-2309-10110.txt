Topological modes and spectral flows in inhomogeneous PT-symmetric continuous media
Yichen Fu, Hong Qin

Abstract
In classical Hermitian continuous media, the spectral-flow index of topological modes is linked to the bulk topology via index theorem. However, the interface between two bulks is usually non-Hermitian due to the inhomogeneities of system parameters. We show that the connection between topological modes and bulk topology still exists despite the non-Hermiticity at the interface if the system is endowed with PT symmetry. The theoretical framework developed is applied to the Hall magnetohydrodynamic model to identify a topological mode called topological Alfvén-sound wave in magnetized plasmas.

II
A two-band model

Before presenting the result for a general inhomogeneous PT-symmetric continuous media, we consider the following two-band non-Hermitian Hamiltonians as simple examples of asymptotically Hermitian systems,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}[\lambda;x,\partial_{x}]=\begin{pmatrix}m(x)&\lambda-\partial_{x}\\
\lambda+\partial_{x}&-m(x)\end{pmatrix}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon m^{\prime}(x)\sigma^{\alpha}.

(1)

Here,
m(x)
is a real function of
x

.
\lambda

\sigma^{\alpha}(\alpha=0,x,y,z)
is an identity matrix or one of the Pauli matrices, and
\epsilon

For a given Hamiltonian, when all derivatives of the system parameters are set to zero, it will be called the asymptotic Hamiltonian of the original Hamiltonian. An inhomogeneous non-Hermitian continuous system is called asymptotically Hermitian if its asymptotic Hamiltonian is Hermitian.

To study the possible topological modes of
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}[\lambda;x,\partial_{x}]

H_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}=\mathscr{W}(\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{\alpha})

\displaystyle H_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}[\lambda;x,k]=\begin{pmatrix}m(x)&\lambda-\mathrm{i}k\\
\lambda+\mathrm{i}k&-m(x)\end{pmatrix}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon m^{\prime}\sigma^{\alpha}.

(2)

The asymptotic Hamiltonian

\omega^{\pm}=\pm\sqrt{m^{2}+k^{2}+\lambda^{2}}

;

Here, we show why

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{y}\to\mathcal{\widetilde{H}}_{\epsilon}^{y}=e^{-\epsilon m}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{y}e^{\epsilon m}=\mathcal{H}_{0}^{y}.

(3)

Next, we interpret this result as a consequence of the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian being PT-symmetric. In the current context, time-reversal

IV
Topological Alfvén-sound wave

is the asymptotic Hamiltonian that does not depend on the spatial derivatives of the equilibrium field, and

and

We assume the equilibrium is inhomogeneous only in the

\displaystyle\dfrac{v_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}(x)}{2}+\dfrac{v_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}(x)}{\gamma}=\mathrm{Const.}

\displaystyle\zeta\doteq\dfrac{v_{\mathrm{s}}}{v_{\mathrm{A}}}=\sqrt{1+\dfrac{1}{4}k_{z}^{2}d_{\mathrm{i}}^{2}}-\dfrac{1}{2}k_{z}d_{\mathrm{i}}.

See Appendix

for details. The question to answer is whether there is a spectral flow of

V
Summary

We studied topological edge modes and spectral flows in non-Hermitian inhomogeneous continuous media that are PT-symmetric and asymptotically Hermitian. For these media, if the asymptotic symbol
H_{0}
supports a Weyl point of two-fold degeneracy, then the non-Hermitian operator
\mathcal{H}
of the system admits topological edge modes with real eigenfrequencies, as characterized by a spectral flow near the Weyl point. In other words, with the protection of PT-symmetry, the spectral flow induced by a Weyl point in the Hermitian bulk is robust against non-Hermitian perturbation at the interface in classical continuous media. As an application of the general theory developed, we identified a topological edge mode called topological Alfvén-sound wave using the non-Hermitian Hall MHD model. The analysis reported in this paper could be applied to search for topological modes in a wide range of non-Hermitian PT-symmetric inhomogeneous continuous media in fluids and plasmas.

Analytical solution without anti-Hermitian part

When

Faure 2023
;
Qin and Fu 2023
;
Delplace 2022

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\prime}=R^{-1}\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{H}}R=\begin{pmatrix}{x}/\kappa&(\lambda-\partial_{x})/\kappa\,\\
\kappa(\lambda+\partial_{x})&-\kappa{x}\end{pmatrix}

(28)

Define the following variables for convenience,

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\doteq\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\kappa+\dfrac{1}{\kappa}\right),\quad\mu_{2}\doteq\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\kappa-\dfrac{1}{\kappa}\right).

(29)

In terms of Pauli matrices,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\prime}=&-\mu_{2}x\sigma^{0}+(\mu_{2}\partial_{x}+\mu_{1}\lambda)\sigma^{x}\\
&-\mathrm{i}(\mu_{1}\partial_{x}+\mu_{2}\lambda)\sigma^{y}+\mu_{1}x\sigma^{z}.\end{split}

(30)

Making a cyclic rotation of Pauli matrices
(\sigma^{0},\sigma^{x},\sigma^{y},\sigma^{z})\to(\sigma^{0},\sigma^{z},\sigma^{x},\sigma^{y})
through a similarity transformation,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\prime}
is transformed to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\prime\prime}=&-\mu_{2}x\sigma^{0}+(\mu_{2}\partial_{x}+\mu_{1}\lambda)\sigma^{z}\\
&-\mathrm{i}(\mu_{1}\partial_{x}+\mu_{2}\lambda)\sigma^{x}+\mu_{1}x\sigma^{y}.\end{split}

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}\lambda-\sqrt{2}\mu_{2}\hat{a}^{\dagger}&\mathrm{i}(-\mu_{2}\lambda-\sqrt{2}\mu_{1}\hat{a})\\

(32)

Here,

\displaystyle\hat{a}\doteq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x+\partial_{x}\right),\quad\hat{a}^{\dagger}\doteq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x-\partial_{x}\right)

(33)

are the ladder operators.

Define functions
|n;\delta\rangle

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle x|n;\delta\rangle&\doteq\varphi_{n}(x+\sqrt{2}\delta),\\
\varphi_{n}(x)&\doteq\frac{1}{\left(2^{n}n!\sqrt{\pi}\right)^{1/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-{x^{2}}/{2}}H_{n}(x),\end{split}

(34)

where

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}^{\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}|n;\delta_{n}^{\pm}\rangle\\
\mathrm{i}\gamma_{n}^{\pm}|n+1;\delta_{n}^{\pm}\rangle\end{pmatrix},\quad n=0,1,2,\ldots,

(35)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma_{n}^{\pm}&=\dfrac{-\lambda\pm\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+n+1}}{\sqrt{n+1}},\\
\delta_{n}^{\pm}&=\mp\dfrac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+n+1}.\end{split}

(36)

The corresponding eigenvalues are

\displaystyle\omega_{n}^{\pm}=\pm\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}\kappa}{1+\kappa^{2}}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+n+1}.

(37)

We also find the eigenvector and eigenvalue of the spectral flow of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{H}}

\displaystyle\Psi_{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
|0,\delta_{-1}\rangle\end{pmatrix},

(38)

\displaystyle\delta_{-1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}\lambda,\quad\omega_{-1}=-\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}\kappa}{1+\kappa^{2}}\lambda.

(39)

Appendix B
The Hall MHD model

Within the MHD model, which focuses on low frequency and long-wavelength phenomena, the displacement current
\partial_{t}\mathbf{E}
in Maxwell’s equations is ignored. When studying plasma waves, the dynamics of plasma is usually assumed to be adiabatic with the equation of state
p/\rho^{\gamma}=\mathrm{Const.}
, where
p
is plasma pressure,
\rho
is plasma mass density, and
\gamma
is ratio of specific heats. With this assumption, the equations of motions for
\rho,

\mathbf{v}
,
\mathbf{B}
, and
p
are

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{v})=0,

(45)

\displaystyle\rho\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{v}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{B}-\nabla p,

(46)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{B}=-\nabla\times\mathbf{E},

(47)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathrm{d}p}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\gamma p\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v},

(48)

where
\mathbf{v}
is the plasma macroscopic velocity, and
\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}t\doteq\partial_{t}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla
. The plasma current
\mathbf{j}
is related to the magnetic field
\mathbf{B}
by the Ampere’s law

\displaystyle\quad\mu_{0}\mathbf{j}=\nabla\times\mathbf{B},

(49)

where
\mu_{0}
is the vacuum permeability. Since the displacement current is ignored,
\mathbf{E}
is obtained from the generalized Ohm’s law. Different forms of Ohm’s laws lead to different MHD models. In the Hall MHD approximation, the electron inertia is ignored (
m_{\mathrm{e}}\to 0
) and the plasma is assumed to be infinitely conductive. The Ohm’s law could be written as
Hameiri and Torasso 2004

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}=\dfrac{\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{B}-\nabla p_{\mathrm{e}}}{n_{\mathrm{e}}e},

(50)

where
e>0
is the elementary charge,
n_{\mathrm{e}}
is the electron number density,
p_{\mathrm{e}}
is the electron pressure.

Combining Faraday’s law and Ohm’s law, we can eliminate electric field
\mathbf{E}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\mathbf{B}=&\nabla\times(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})\\
&-\nabla\times\left(\dfrac{\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{B}}{n_{\mathrm{e}}e}\right)+\nabla\times\left(\dfrac{\nabla p_{\mathrm{e}}}{n_{\mathrm{e}}e}\right).\end{split}

(51)

If the electrons are assumed to be barotropic
Hameiri and Torasso 2004
, i.e., the electron pressure is a function of electron number density,
p_{\mathrm{e}}=p_{\mathrm{e}}(n_{\mathrm{e}})
, the last term in Eq. (
51
) vanishes.

Consider an equilibrium without flow and with homogeneous densities
\rho
and
n_{\mathrm{e}}
. Hereafter, we use symbols without subscripts such
\mathbf{B}
,
p
, and
\mathbf{j}
to indicate equilibrium, time-independent fields. From Eq. (
46
), the equilibrium fields
\mathbf{B}
and
p
satisfy the pressure balance,

\displaystyle\nabla p=\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{B}=\dfrac{1}{\mu_{0}}(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})\times\mathbf{B}.

(52)

For a given equilibrium, the perturbed field
\left(\rho_{1},\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{B}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{1}\right)
are governed by the linearized system,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{1}=

\displaystyle-\rho\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}_{1},

(53)

\displaystyle\rho\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}_{1}=

\displaystyle\mathbf{j}_{1}\times\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{B}_{1}-\nabla p_{1},

(54)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\mathbf{B}_{1}=&\nabla\times(\mathbf{v}_{1}\times\mathbf{B})\\
&-\dfrac{1}{en_{e}}\nabla\times(\,\mathbf{j}_{1}\times\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{B}_{1}),\end{split}

(55)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p_{1}=

\displaystyle-\mathbf{v}_{1}\cdot\nabla p-\gamma p\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}_{1}.

(56)

Equation (
53
) is decoupled from the others. In terms of the following normalized variables

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\doteq\sqrt{\rho}\mathbf{v}_{1},\quad\tilde{\mathbf{B}}\doteq\dfrac{\mathbf{B}_{1}}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}}},\quad\tilde{p}\doteq\dfrac{p_{1}}{\sqrt{\gamma p}},

(57)

the linearized equations become are

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}=

\displaystyle(\nabla\times\tilde{\mathbf{B}})\times\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{A}}+(\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{A}})\times\tilde{\mathbf{B}}-\nabla(v_{\mathrm{s}}\tilde{p}),

(58)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\tilde{\mathbf{B}}=&\nabla{\times}(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\times\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{A}})\\
&-\sqrt{\dfrac{\rho}{\mu_{0}}}\dfrac{1}{n_{\mathrm{e}}e}\nabla{\times}\left[(\nabla{\times}\tilde{\mathbf{B}}){\times}\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{A}}\right]\\
&-\sqrt{\dfrac{\rho}{\mu_{0}}}\dfrac{1}{n_{\mathrm{e}}e}\nabla{\times}\left[(\nabla{\times}\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{A}}){\times}\tilde{\mathbf{B}}\right]\end{split}

(59)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{p}=

\displaystyle-v_{\mathrm{s}}\nabla\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{v}}-(2/\gamma)\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\cdot\nabla v_{\mathrm{s}},

(60)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{A}}\doteq\dfrac{\mathbf{B}}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}\rho}},\quad v_{\mathrm{s}}\doteq\sqrt{\dfrac{\gamma p}{\rho}}

(61)

are Alfvén velocity and sound velocity. Let
n_{\mathrm{i}},m_{\mathrm{i}},q_{\mathrm{i}}
denote the number density, mass, and charge of ions. Using the quasi-neutrality condition
n_{\mathrm{e}}e=n_{\mathrm{i}}q_{\mathrm{i}}
, the coefficient
\sqrt{\rho\mu_{0}/n_{\mathrm{e}}e}
in Eq. (
59
) can be simplified as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sqrt{\dfrac{\rho}{\mu_{0}}}\dfrac{1}{n_{\mathrm{e}}e}=&\sqrt{\dfrac{n_{\mathrm{i}}m_{\mathrm{i}}}{1/(\varepsilon_{0}c^{2})}}\dfrac{1}{n_{\mathrm{i}}q_{\mathrm{i}}}\\
=&c\left/\sqrt{\dfrac{n_{\mathrm{i}}q_{\mathrm{i}}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{0}m_{\mathrm{i}}}}\right.=\dfrac{c}{\omega_{\mathrm{pi}}}=d_{\mathrm{i}},\end{split}

(62)

where
\epsilon_{0}
is the vacuum permittivity,
c
is the light speed,
\omega_{\mathrm{pi}}
is the ion plasma frequency, and
d_{\mathrm{i}}
is known as the ion skin depth. The final linearized equations for Hall MHD become Eqs. (
16
)-(
18
) in the main text. In the limit of
d_{\mathrm{i}}\to 0
, the system reduces to the ideal MHD model where the right-hand of Eq. (
50
) vanishes.

To study the dispersion relation and wave topology in the bulk region, we assume the equilibrium magnetic field
\mathbf{B}
and pressure
p
are constants. In this case, the wave fields
\Psi=(\tilde{\mathbf{v}},\tilde{\mathbf{B}},\tilde{p})^{\intercal}
are governed by
\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}\Psi=\mathcal{H}_{0}\Psi
, where
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is the asymptotic Hamiltonian shown in Eq. (
20
). The corresponding symbol is

\displaystyle{H}_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{A}}\mathbf{k})^{\intercal}-\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{A}}{\cdot}\mathbf{k}\,\,&v_{\mathrm{s}}\mathbf{k}\\[3.0pt]
(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{A}}\mathbf{k})-\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{A}}{\cdot}\mathbf{k}\,\,&\mathrm{i}d_{\mathrm{i}}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{A}}{\cdot}\mathbf{k})\mathbf{k}\times&0\\[3.0pt]
v_{\mathrm{s}}\mathbf{k}^{\intercal}&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(63)

It is clear that
H_{0}
and
\mathcal{H}_{0}
are Hermitian.

Let magnetic field is in the

-direction and
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{A}}=v_{\mathrm{A}}\mathbf{e}_{z}

Now, we approximate the Hall MHD model by a two-band non-Hermitian Hamiltonian near the degeneracy (Weyl) point between the Alfvén wave and slow wave as determined by
H_{0}
. The index (topological charge) of the Weyl point will be calculated from the two-band approximation of
H_{0}
, and the spectral flow is explicitly solved for. According to the naming convention defined by the inequality (
64
), the Alfvén wave and slow wave are switched at the Weyl point. But in the neighborhood of the Weyl point, the two branches are physically the Alfvén wave and the sound wave. Therefore, a proper name for this edge mode is topological Alfvén-sound wave (TASW).

Assume
\zeta_{0}=v_{\mathrm{s0}}/v_{\mathrm{A0}}<1
at the Weyl point. The location of the Weyl point in the momentum space is found to be
(k_{x},k_{y},k_{z})=\left(0,0,\left(1/\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{0}\right)/d_{i}\right)
with eigenvalue
\omega_{0}=(v_{\mathrm{A0}}^{2}-v_{\mathrm{s0}}^{2})/(d_{\mathrm{i}}v_{\mathrm{A0}})
. At the Weyl point, the two unit eigenvectors are

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,0,1,0,0,0,1)^{\intercal},

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\left(v_{\mathrm{A0}}^{2}+v_{\mathrm{s0}}^{2}\right)}}(-\mathrm{i}v_{\mathrm{A0}},-v_{\mathrm{A0}},0,\mathrm{i}v_{\mathrm{s0}},v_{\mathrm{s0}},0,0)^{\intercal}.

Since PT symmetry is unbroken at the Weyl point,
\Psi_{1,2}
are also the eigenvectors of the
\mathcal{PT}
operator with eigenvalue 1, namely
\mathcal{PT}\Psi_{i}=\Psi_{i}
.

In the
(k_{x},k_{y},x)
space near the Weyl point, we can expand the inhomogeneous equilibrium field,
v_{\mathrm{A}}(x)=v_{\mathrm{A0}}+v_{\mathrm{A0}}^{\prime}x
,
v_{\mathrm{s}}(x)=v_{\mathrm{s0}}+v_{\mathrm{s0}}^{\prime}x
, and approximate
H_{0}
by the following two-band operator,

\displaystyle M_{0}\doteq\bm{\Psi}^{\dagger}\Bigg[v_{\mathrm{A}}(x)\dfrac{\partial H_{0}}{\partial v_{\mathrm{A}}}+v_{\mathrm{s}}(x)\dfrac{\partial H_{0}}{\partial v_{\mathrm{s}}}

(82)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+k_{y}\dfrac{\partial H_{0}}{\partial k_{y}}+k_{x}\dfrac{\partial H_{0}}{\partial k_{x}}-\omega_{0}\mathrm{I}_{2}\Bigg]\bm{\Psi}

(83)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{v_{\mathrm{A0}}^{2}-v_{\mathrm{s0}}^{2}}{d_{\mathrm{i}}v_{\mathrm{A0}}}\dfrac{v_{\mathrm{s0}}^{\prime}}{v_{\mathrm{s0}}}x-\omega_{0}&-\dfrac{v_{\mathrm{A0}}v_{\mathrm{s0}}(k_{y}+\mathrm{i}k_{x})}{2\sqrt{v_{\mathrm{A0}}^{2}+v_{\mathrm{s0}}^{2}}}\\[15.0pt]
-\dfrac{v_{\mathrm{A0}}v_{\mathrm{s0}}(k_{y}-\mathrm{i}k_{x})}{2\sqrt{v_{\mathrm{A0}}^{2}+v_{\mathrm{s0}}^{2}}}&\dfrac{v_{\mathrm{A0}}^{2}-v_{\mathrm{s0}}^{2}}{d_{\mathrm{i}}v_{\mathrm{A0}}}\dfrac{v_{\mathrm{A0}}^{\prime}}{v_{\mathrm{A0}}}x-\omega_{0}\end{pmatrix}

(84)

Here,
\bm{\Psi}=(\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2})^{\intercal}
, and the
\omega_{0}I_{2}
term can be ignored. All derivatives of
H_{0}
are evaluated at
(k_{x},k_{y},x)=(0,0,0)
. The topological charge of Weyl point is defined to be the Chern number of the slow wave bundle over a 2D surface in the
(k_{x},k_{y},x)
space surrounding the Weyl point
Faure 2023
;
Qin and Fu 2023
. Without loss of generality, assume
v_{\mathrm{A0}}^{\prime}<0
, and thus
v_{\mathrm{s0}}^{\prime}>0
from the pressure balance in Eq. (
21
). Let
\alpha\doteq 1-v_{\mathrm{A0}}^{\prime}v_{\mathrm{s0}}/(2v_{\mathrm{s0}}^{\prime}v_{\mathrm{A0}})>0
. In terms of the following scaled variables,

\displaystyle\tilde{x}\doteq\frac{v_{\mathrm{A0}}^{2}-v_{\mathrm{s0}}^{2}}{2d_{\mathrm{i}}v_{\mathrm{A0}}v_{\mathrm{s0}}}v_{\mathrm{s0}}^{\prime}\alpha x,

(85)

\displaystyle\tilde{k}_{x}\doteq\frac{v_{\mathrm{A0}}v_{\mathrm{s0}}}{2\sqrt{v_{\mathrm{A0}}^{2}+v_{\mathrm{s0}}^{2}}}k_{x},\quad\tilde{k}_{y}\doteq\frac{v_{\mathrm{A0}}v_{\mathrm{s0}}}{2\sqrt{v_{\mathrm{A0}}^{2}+v_{\mathrm{s0}}^{2}}}k_{y},

(86)

M_{0}
is simplified to

\displaystyle M_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}2\tilde{x}/\alpha&-\tilde{k}_{y}-\mathrm{i}\tilde{k}_{x}\\
-\tilde{k}_{y}+\mathrm{i}\tilde{k}_{x}&-2(\alpha-1)\tilde{x}/\alpha\end{pmatrix},

(87)

Its eigenvalues and eigenvectors are

\displaystyle\omega_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\tilde{x}\left(\dfrac{2}{\alpha}-1\right)\pm\sqrt{\tilde{k}_{x}^{2}+\tilde{k}_{y}^{2}+\tilde{x}^{2}},

(88)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\left[\dfrac{\tilde{x}\pm\sqrt{\tilde{k}_{x}^{2}+\tilde{k}_{y}^{2}+\tilde{x}^{2}}}{\mathrm{i}{\tilde{k}_{x}}-{\tilde{k}_{y}}},1\right]^{\intercal}.

(89)

On an infinitesimal 2D sphere in the
(\tilde{x},\tilde{k}_{x},\tilde{k}_{y})
space centered at
(\tilde{x},\tilde{k}_{x},\tilde{k}_{y})=(0,0,0)
, the unit eigenvectors can be written in the spherical coordinates as

\displaystyle\Psi_{+}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\\[5.0pt]
\mathrm{i}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}\end{pmatrix},\quad\Psi_{-}=\begin{pmatrix}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\\[5.0pt]

(90)

Here, the spherical coordinate is defined by

,

,

On polynomial automorphisms of Nagata type
Huarcaya, Jorge A. C. 1, Palacios, Joe 2,3

Abstract
. We define a family of polynomial ring homomorphisms generalising the Nagata automorphism, and relate the condition of non-vanishing of their Jacobian determinant with a certain linear partial differential equation. We also give a condition on that family to get wild automorphisms.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
14R10, 13B25, 13F20, 35F05

1.
Introduction

In what follows
F
will be a field of characteristic
0
unless otherwise stated.
The polynomials:

\displaystyle f(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=x-2y(zx+y^{2})-z(zx+y^{2})^{2},

\displaystyle g(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=y+z(zx+y^{2}),

\displaystyle h(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=z.

define an automorphism on
F[x,y,z]
called Nagata automorphism
[
1
]
.
The determinant of the Jacobian matrix of this automorphism es
1
.

In the following paragraph we recall the notion of tame automorphisms of polynomials. Let
1\leq i\leq n
, the
i
th
elementary automorphism

\Phi^{(i)}\colon F^{n}\to F^{n}
is the automorphism defined as

\Phi^{(i)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\Big(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i}+f(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n}),x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n}\Big)

and its inverse is the automorphism

{\Phi^{(i)}}^{-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\Big(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i}-f(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n}),x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n}\Big)\,.

One may notice that the Jacobian matriz of
\Phi^{(i)}
has determinant
1
.

An automorphism is
tame
if it is a finite composition of elementary and linear automorphisms. An automorphism that is not tame is called
wild
.

In 1972, Nagata conjectured that the Nagata automorphism is wild. In
[
2
, Corollary 9]
, the authors proved this conjecture.

In this work, we define ring homomorphisms on
F[x,y,z]
, given in (
2
), extending the Nagata automorphism which are wild automorphisms under some assumptions, see Corollary
1
). We also relate the condition of non-vanishing of their Jacobian determinant with a certain linear partial differential equation, see Theorem
1
. As a consequence, we prove that those ring homomorphisms on
F[x,y,z]
with nonzero Jacobian determinant, are in fact automorphisms, see Theorem
2
.

2.
Nagata polynomial map

For a polynomial
\varphi\in F[x,y,z]
, we set

(1)

\begin{split}f(x,y,z)&=x-2y\varphi-z\varphi^{2}\,,\\
g(x,y,z)&=y+z\varphi\,,\\
h(x,y,z)&=z\,.\end{split}

The triplet
(f,g,h)
induces a ring homomorphism on
F[x,y,z]
. Note that
(f,g,h)
is the identity when one takes
\varphi=0
, so
(f,g,h)
may be seen as a perturbation of the identity. If
\varphi=xz+y^{2}
then
(f,g,h)
is the Nagata automorphism.

The Milnor number of
(f,g,h)
is the dimension

\mu(f,g,h)=\dim_{F}\frac{F[x,y,z]}{\langle f,g,h\rangle}

as vector space over
F
.

Lemma 1
.

If
(f,g,h)
is a ring homomorphism as in (
2
), then
\mu(f,g,h)=1
.

Proof.

One has,

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=(y+z\varphi)-\varphi z=g-\varphi z,

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=(x-2y\varphi-z\varphi^{2})+2(y+z\varphi)\varphi-\varphi^{2}z=f+2\varphi g-\varphi^{2}z,

hence we get an equality
\langle f,g,h\rangle=\langle x,y,z\rangle
of polynomial ideals, and this implies that
\mu(f,g,h)=1
.
∎

Remark 1
.

Note that
(f,g,h)
is not always an automorphism for any
\varphi
despite the fact that
\mu(f,g,h)=1
by Lemma
1
. For instance, if
\varphi=x
or
\varphi=y
, then
(f,g,h)
is not an automorphism.
In the first case,
(f,g,h)
sends both points
(0,0,1)
and
(-1,1,1)
to
(0,0,1)
. Theorem
2
provides a necessary and sufficient condition on
\varphi
such that
(f,g,h)
is an automorphism.

Proposition 1
.

The Jacobian matrix
J(f,g,h)
has nonzero determinant in
F
if and only if
\varphi
satisfies the linear PDE:

-2y\varphi_{x}+z\varphi_{y}=0\,.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle f_{x}

\displaystyle=1-2y\varphi_{x}-2z\varphi\varphi_{x},

\displaystyle f_{y}

\displaystyle=-2\varphi_{x}-2y\varphi_{y}-2z\varphi\varphi_{y},

\displaystyle g_{x}

\displaystyle=z\varphi_{x},

\displaystyle g_{y}

\displaystyle=1+z\varphi_{y}\,.

Hence the Jacobian matrix
J(f,g,h)
is

\begin{pmatrix}1-2y\varphi_{x}-2z\varphi\varphi_{x}&-2\varphi_{x}-2y\varphi_{y}-2z\varphi\varphi_{y}&f_{z}\\
z\varphi_{x}&1+z\varphi_{y}&g_{z}\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}

whose determinant is

\begin{vmatrix}1-2y\varphi_{x}-2z\varphi\varphi_{x}&-2\varphi_{x}-2y\varphi_{y}-2z\varphi\varphi_{y}\\
z\varphi_{x}&1+z\varphi_{y}\end{vmatrix}=1-2y\varphi_{x}+z\varphi_{y}\,.

If
-2y\varphi_{x}+z\varphi_{y}=0
then the determinant of
J(f,g,h)
is
1
. Conversely, if the determinant of
J(f,g,h)
is a constant
c\in F-\{0\}
, then

1-2y\varphi_{x}+z\varphi_{y}=c\,.

However this equality is only possible when
c=1
, therefore
-2y\varphi_{x}+z\varphi_{y}=0
.
∎

Proposition 2
.

Let
p\in F[t_{1},t_{2}]
and set
\varphi=p(xz+y^{2},z)
in
F[x,y,z]
. Then
the ring homomorphism
(f,g,h)
given in (
2
)
is an automorphism on
F[x,y,z]
with inverse:

(2)

\begin{split}f^{\prime}(x,y,z)&=x+2y\varphi-z\varphi^{2},\\
g^{\prime}(x,y,z)&=y-z\varphi,\\
h^{\prime}(x,y,z)&=z\,.\end{split}

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\varphi(f^{\prime},g^{\prime},h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=p(f^{\prime}z+g^{\prime 2},z)

\displaystyle=p\Big((x+2y\varphi-z\varphi^{2})z+(y-z\varphi)^{2},z\Big)

\displaystyle=p(xz+y^{2},z)

\displaystyle=\varphi\,,

thus
\varphi(f^{\prime},g^{\prime},h^{\prime})=\varphi
. Hence,

\displaystyle f(f^{\prime},g^{\prime},h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}-2g^{\prime}\varphi(f^{\prime},g^{\prime},h^{\prime})-z\varphi^{2}(f^{\prime},g^{\prime},h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(x+2y\varphi-z\varphi^{2})-2(y-z\varphi)\varphi-z\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle=x\,.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle g(f^{\prime},g^{\prime},h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=g^{\prime}+z\varphi(f^{\prime},g^{\prime},h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(y-z\varphi)+z\varphi

\displaystyle=y\,.

Therefore, the composite
(f,g,h)\circ(f^{\prime},g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
is the identity. Similarly, one verifies that
(f^{\prime},g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\circ(f,g,h)
is also the identity.
∎

For a polynomial
\psi
, we denote by
\overline{\psi}
the highest degree homogeneous component of
\psi
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\varphi\in F[x,y,z]
and suppose that
(f,g,h)
is an automorphism on
F[x,y,z]
of the form (
2
). If one the partial derivatives
\overline{\varphi}_{x},\overline{\varphi}_{y}
is nonzero, then the automorphism
(f,g,h)
is wild.

Proof.

Let
\overline{f},\overline{g},\overline{h}
be the highest homogeneous component of
f,g,h
, respectively. Since
\overline{\varphi}_{x}
or
\overline{\varphi}_{y}
is nonzero, we have
\deg(\overline{\varphi})>0
, so

\begin{split}\overline{f}&=-z\overline{\varphi}^{2}\,,\\
\overline{g}&=z\overline{\varphi}\,,\\
\overline{h}&=z\,.\end{split}

Hence,

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{x}\overline{g}_{y}-\overline{f}_{y}\overline{g}_{x}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{y}\overline{g}_{z}-\overline{f}_{z}\overline{g}_{y}

\displaystyle=-z\,\overline{\varphi}^{2}\overline{\varphi}_{y}

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{x}\overline{g}_{z}-\overline{f}_{z}\overline{g}_{x}

\displaystyle=-z\,\overline{\varphi}^{2}\overline{\varphi}_{x}

\displaystyle\overline{g}_{x}\overline{h}_{y}-\overline{g}_{y}\overline{h}_{x}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\overline{g}_{y}\overline{h}_{z}-\overline{g}_{z}\overline{h}_{y}

\displaystyle=z\,\overline{\varphi}_{y}

\displaystyle\overline{g}_{x}\overline{h}_{z}-\overline{g}_{z}\overline{h}_{x}

\displaystyle=z\,\overline{\varphi}_{x}

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{x}\overline{h}_{y}-\overline{f}_{y}\overline{h}_{x}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{y}\overline{h}_{z}-\overline{f}_{z}\overline{h}_{y}

\displaystyle=-2z\,\overline{\varphi}_{y}

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{x}\overline{h}_{z}-\overline{f}_{z}\overline{h}_{x}

\displaystyle=-2z\,\overline{\varphi}_{x}\,.

By
[
3
, Corollary 2]
or
[
2
, Lemma 1]
, we deduce that
\overline{f},\overline{g},\overline{h}
are mutually algebraically independent. On the other hand, from the hypothesis
\overline{\varphi}\in F[x,y,z]
is a homogeneous polynomial of positive degree such that
\overline{\varphi}\not\in F[z]
, it is not difficult to see that none of the polynomials
\overline{f},\overline{g},\overline{h}
belongs to the subalgebra generated by the other two polynomials. Therefore, by
[
2
, Corollary 8]

(f,g,h)
is wild.
∎

We give the following definition in order to determine the highest weighted homogeneous component of the polynomial
\varphi=p(xz+y^{2},z)
.

Definition 1
.

Let us fix algebraically independent indeterminates
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
over
F
. For
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 1}
, we write
t^{k}
to denote the monomial
t_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{k_{n}}
. A polynomial
h\in F[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]
can be written as
h=\sum_{k}a_{k}t^{k}
.

(1)

The
support of
h
, denoted by
\mathrm{supp}(h)
, is defined as the set

\mathrm{supp}(h)=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}:a_{k}\neq 0\}\,.

(2)

Let
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 1}
. The degree of
h
with respect to
v
, denoted by
d_{v}(h)
, is the maximum of the scalar product
\langle v,k\rangle
such that
a_{k}\neq 0
. We denote by
h^{v}
the sum of those terms
a_{k}t^{k}
such that
\langle v,k\rangle=d_{v}(p)
. In particular, if
v=(1,\dots,1)
then
\deg(h)=d_{v}(h)
and
h^{v}=\overline{h}
.

(3)

We say that
h
is weighted homogeneous with respect to
v
when
h^{v}=h
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
p\in F[t_{1},t_{2}]
and
v=(2,1)
. If
\varphi=p(xz+y^{2},z)\in F[x,y,z]
, then
\deg(\varphi)=d_{v}(p)
and
\overline{\varphi}=p^{v}(xz+y^{2},z)
. In particular, if
p
is weighted homogeneous with respect to
v=(2,1)
, then
\varphi
is homogeneous.

Proof.

Let us write the polymomial
p
as
p=\sum_{k}a_{k}t^{k}
. One has

\displaystyle\varphi=\sum_{k}a_{k}(xz+y^{2})^{k_{1}}z^{k_{2}}=\sum_{k}a_{k}\sum_{n=0}^{k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}}{n}x^{k_{1}-n}y^{2n}z^{k_{1}+k_{2}-n}\,.

From the last equality, we observe that

(3)

\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)=\Big\{(k_{1}-n,2n,k_{1}+k_{2}-n):k\in\mathrm{supp}(p)\,\textnormal{and}\,\,0\leq n\leq k_{1}\Big\}\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\deg(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{k_{1}-n+2n+k_{1}+k_{2}-n:(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathrm{supp}(p)\,\textnormal{and}\,0\leq n\leq k_{1}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{2k_{1}+k_{2}:(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathrm{supp}(p)\Big\}

(4)

\displaystyle=d_{v}(p)\,.

Hence, by the equalities given in (
3
) and (
4
), the highest degree homogeneous component of
\varphi
is computed as follows

\displaystyle\overline{\varphi}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathrm{supp}(p)\\
0\leq n\leq k_{1}\\
2k_{1}+k_{2}=d_{v}(p)\end{subarray}}a_{k}\sum_{n=0}^{k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}}{n}x^{k_{1}-n}y^{2n}z^{k_{1}+k_{2}-n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathrm{supp}(p)\\
0\leq n\leq k_{1}\\
2k_{1}+k_{2}=d_{v}(p)\end{subarray}}a_{k}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}}{n}x^{k_{1}-n}z^{k_{1}-n}y^{2n}\right)z^{k_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathrm{supp}(p)\\
0\leq n\leq k_{1}\\
2k_{1}+k_{2}=d_{v}(p)\end{subarray}}a_{k}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}}{n}(xz)^{k_{1}-n}y^{2n}\right)z^{k_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathrm{supp}(p)\\
2k_{1}+k_{2}=d_{v}(p)\end{subarray}}\left(xz+y^{2}\right)^{k_{1}}z^{k_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{v}(xz+y^{2},z)\,,

and it proves the assertion.
∎

Example 1
.

If
\varphi(x,y,z)=p(xz+y^{2},z)
, then it is not always true that
\overline{\varphi}(x,y,z)=\overline{p}(xz+y^{2},z)
. Indeed, consider
p(t_{1},t_{2})=t_{1}^{2}-t_{2}^{3}+t_{1}t_{2}^{2}\in F[s,t]
, then by a immediate computation one has

\overline{\varphi}=\left(xz+y^{2}\right)^{2}+\left(xz+y^{2}\right)z^{2}\neq-z^{3}+\left(xz+y^{2}\right)z^{2}=\overline{p}(xz+y^{2},z)\,.

However, it is easy to verify that if
v=(2,1)
, then
d_{v}(p)=4
and
p^{v}=t_{1}^{2}+t_{1}t_{2}^{2}
. Moreover, by direct computation one has

\overline{\varphi}=\left(xz+y^{2}\right)^{2}+\left(xz+y^{2}\right)z^{2}=p^{v}(xz+y^{2},z)\,.

Corollary 1
.

Let
p\in F[t_{1},t_{2}]
and set
\varphi=p(xz+y^{2},z)
in
F[x,y,z]
. If the partial derivative
(p^{v})_{t_{1}}
is nonzero, then the corresponding ring homomorphism
(f,g,h)
given in (
2
) is a wild automorphism.

Proof.

By Proposition
2
,
(f,g,h)
is an automorphism and Lemma
2
establishes the equality
\overline{\varphi}=p^{v}(xz+y^{2},z)
. Hence the fact that
(p^{v})_{t_{1}}\neq 0
implies that
\overline{\varphi}_{x}\neq 0
and
\overline{\varphi}_{y}\neq 0
. By Proposition
3
, the automorphism
(f,g,h)
is wild.
∎

Example 2
.

Let
\varphi=(xz+y^{2})^{n}
with
n\geq 1
. Corollary
1
implies that

\displaystyle f(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=x-2y(xz+y^{2})^{n}-z(xz+y^{2})^{2n},

\displaystyle g(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=y+z(xz+y^{2})^{n},

\displaystyle h(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=z,

is a wild automorphism.

Example 3
.

Let
\varphi(x,y,z)=p(xz+y^{2},z)
, where
p(t_{1},t_{2})=t_{1}^{2}-t_{2}^{3}+t_{1}t_{2}^{2}\in F[s,t]
as in Example
1
. Since
p^{v}=t_{1}^{2}+t_{1}t_{2}^{2}
and
(p^{v})_{t_{1}}=2t_{1}+t_{1}^{2}\neq 0
, Corollary
1
implies that corresponding ring homomorphism
(f,g,h)
is a wild automorphism.

3.
Statement of the main results

The main results in this work are the following:

Theorem 1
.

The linear PDE

(5)

-2y\varphi_{x}+z\varphi_{y}=0

has as general solution in
F[x,y,z]
, polynomials that can be written as:

\varphi=p(xz+y^{2},z)\,,

where
p\in F[t_{1},t_{2}]
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\varphi\in F[x,y,z]
and set

\displaystyle f(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=x-2y\varphi-z\varphi^{2}\,,

\displaystyle g(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=y+z\varphi\,,

\displaystyle h(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=z\,.

Then, the determinant of the Jacobian matrix
J(f,g,h)
is a nonzero constant in
F
if and only if
(f,g,h)
is an automorphism on
F[x,y,z]
.
In this case, there exists a polynomial
p\in F[t_{1},t_{2}]
such that
\varphi=p(xz+y^{2},z)
, moreover the inverse of
(f,g,h)
is given in (
2
).

The proofs of these results are given after the following previous results.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\varphi=\varphi^{0}+\varphi^{1}+\cdots+\varphi^{n}
be a solution of the linear PDE:

(6)

A(x,y,z)\varphi_{x}+B(x,y,z)\varphi_{y}+C(x,y,x)\varphi_{z}=0\,,

where each
\varphi^{k}\in F[x,y.z]
is the homogeneous component of
\varphi
of degree
k
for
0\leq k\leq n
, and
A,B,C
are homogeneous polynomials of the same degree. Then each homogeneous component
\varphi^{k}
is also a solution of the equation (
6
).

Proof.

Replacing
\varphi=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\varphi^{k}
into the linear PDE (
5
), we obtain:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(A(x,y,z)\varphi^{k}_{x}+B(x,y,z)\varphi^{k}_{y}+C(x,y,x)\varphi^{k}_{z}\Big)=0

which is a sum of homogeneous polynomials of different degrees, therefore:

A(x,y,z)\varphi^{k}_{x}+B(x,y,z)\varphi^{k}_{y}+C(x,y,x)\varphi^{k}_{z}=0\,\,\,\textnormal{for all}\,\,k=0,1,\dots,n\,.

∎

By the previous lemma, to find the general solution of the equation (
5
), it is enough to deal with its homogeneous polynomials components.

Let
\varphi\in F[x,y,z]
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
n
, if we consider the canonical isomorphism of rings
F[x,y,z]\cong F[x,y][z]
, then we can suppose that
\varphi
is written as:

(7)

\varphi=\varphi^{n}+\varphi^{n-1}z+\varphi^{n-2}z^{2}+\dots+\varphi^{1}z^{n-1}+\varphi^{0}z^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi^{n-k}z^{k}+\varphi^{0}z^{n}

with
\varphi^{k}\in F[x,y]
homogeneous polynomials of degree
k
for
k=0,1,\dots,n
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\varphi
be a homogeneous polynomial in
F[x,y,z]
of degree
n
that is a solution of the linear PDE (
5
). Then,
\varphi^{n}=ay^{n}
and
\varphi^{1}=bx
, where
a
and
b
are constants in
F
, and moreover

2y\varphi^{n-k-1}_{x}=\varphi^{n-k}_{y}

for all
k=0,1,\dots,n-2
.

Proof.

Let us derivate
\varphi
with respect to
x
and
y
:

\displaystyle\varphi_{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi^{n-k}_{x}z^{k}=\varphi_{x}^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\varphi^{n-k}_{x}z^{k}\,,

\displaystyle\varphi_{y}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi^{n-k}_{y}z^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\varphi^{n-k}_{y}z^{k}+\varphi^{1}_{y}z^{n-1}\,.

Substituting these equalities in the equation (
5
), we have

-2y\varphi_{x}^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}-2y\varphi^{n-k}_{x}z^{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\varphi^{n-k}_{y}z^{k+1}+\varphi^{1}_{y}z^{n}=0\,,

and by index rescaling, we get

-2y\varphi_{x}^{n}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}-2y\varphi^{n-k-1}_{x}z^{k+1}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\varphi^{n-k}_{y}z^{k+1}+\varphi^{1}_{y}z^{n}=0\,.

Hence,

\displaystyle-2y\varphi_{x}^{n}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\left(-2y\varphi^{n-k-1}_{x}+\varphi^{n-k}_{y}\right)z^{k+1}+\varphi^{1}_{y}z^{n}=0\,.

This equation gives the equalities
\varphi^{n}_{x}=0
and
\varphi^{1}_{y}=0
, so
\varphi^{n}=ay^{n}
and
\varphi^{1}=bx
for some constants
a,b\in F
.
Moreover,

(8)

2y\varphi^{n-k-1}_{x}=\varphi^{n-k}_{y}\,\,\,\textnormal{para todo}\,\,\,k=0,1,\dots,n-2\,.

∎

The following result, will enable us to know how a solution can be written of the equation (
5
).

Proposition 4
.

Let
\varphi\in F[x,y,z]
be a homogeneous solution of the linear PDE (
5
).
We have the following assertions:

(
a
)

If
\varphi
has degree
2n
, then

\varphi=a(xz+y^{2})^{n}+z\psi

where
a\in F
and
\psi\in F[x,y,z]
is a homogeneous polynomial which is also a solution of the equation (
5
) of degree
2n-1
.

(
b
)

If
\varphi
has degree
2n+1
, then

\varphi=z\psi

where
\psi\in F[x,y,z]
is homogeneous of degree
2n
which is also a solution of (
5
) of degree
2n
.

Proof.

(
a
). Let us suppose that:

(9)

\varphi=\varphi^{2n}+\varphi^{2n-1}z+\cdots+\varphi^{1}z^{2n-1}+\varphi^{0}z^{2n}

with
\varphi^{2n-k}
be a homogeneous polynomial in
F[x,y]
of degree
2n-k
for
0\leq k\leq 2n
. By Lemma
4
, we get the following equalities:

(10)

\displaystyle\varphi^{2n}

\displaystyle=ay^{2n}\,\,\,\,\textnormal{and}\,\,\,\,\varphi^{1}=bx

(11)

\displaystyle 2y\varphi^{2n-k-1}_{x}

\displaystyle=\varphi^{2n-k}_{y}\,\,\,\textnormal{para todo}\,\,\,k=0,1,\dots,2n-2\,.

We claim that there are constants
a,a^{2n-k}_{j}
, with
1\leq k\leq n
y
0\leq j\leq k-1
, such that:

(12)

\displaystyle\varphi^{2n-k}

\displaystyle=a\binom{n}{k}y^{2n-2k}x^{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a^{2n-k}_{k-1-j}y^{2n-2k+1+j}x^{k-1-j}

Indeed, taking
k=0
, from these equalities (
10
) and (
11
) we find that:

2y\varphi^{2n-1}_{x}=\varphi^{2n}_{y}=2nay^{2n-1}

so
\varphi_{x}^{2n-1}=nay^{2n-2}
. Hence, by integrating with respect to
x
, we obtain

\varphi^{2n-1}=nay^{2n-2}x+a^{2n-1}_{0}y^{2n-1}\,,

which can be written as

\displaystyle\varphi^{2n-1}=a\binom{n}{1}y^{2n-2}x+a^{2n-1}_{0}y^{2n-1}\,,

thus (
12
) is true for
k=1
. Suppose we have (

Conclusions

Theorem

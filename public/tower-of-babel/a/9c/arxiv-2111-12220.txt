Notes on quantum coherence with l 1 l_{1} -norm and convex-roof l 1 l_{1} -norm
Jiayao Zhu 1 , Jian Ma 1,2 , Tinggui Zhang 1,2,†

Abstract
Abstract In this work, we evaluate quantum coherence using the l 1 l_{1} -norm and convex-roof l 1 l_{1} -norm and obtain several new results. First, we provide some new general triangle-like inequalities of quantum coherence, with results better than existing ones. Second, for some special three-dimensional quantum states, a method for calculating the convex-roof l 1 l_{1} -norm is presented. Lastly, we offer distinct upper bounds in the l 1 l_{1} -norm measure of coherence based on the quantum state itself. Keywords Quantum coherence; l 1 l_{1} -norm; Triangle-like inequalities; Convex-roof l 1 l_{1} -norm

I
Introduction

Quantum coherence stems from the superposition of quantum states; it reflects the ability of quantum states to display quantum coherence effects.
It is the most basic property distinguishing quantum physics from classical physics, and it is also an important quantum mechanical property that is widely used in quantum
information processing and quantum computing. Consequently, quantum information science and the derived quantum technology offer advantages that the corresponding
classical informatics and technology cannot provide, such as the theoretical security of quantum communication
1
, ultra-fast quantum computing
2
, and quantum precision measurements with accuracies exceeding the classical limit
3
. Additionally,
quantum coherence is widely used in quantum biology
4
;
5
, quantum thermodynamics
6
;
7
, and quantum computing
8
;
9
.
Therefore, quantifying the quantum coherence in a resource framework is important and meaningful, and the study of quantum coherence plays an important role in furthering the frontiers of physics research.

Quantum coherence and entanglement are two characteristics of the quantum world. Quantum entanglement describes bipartite or multiple systems, whereas quantum coherence is defined for single systems.
With the rapid developments in quantum information science, researchers have found that, similar to entanglement, quantum coherence can be treated as a physical resource. However, a core issue in this field is the
quantification of coherence. Adopting some of the methods used with entanglement, Baumgratz et al. established a
quantitative theory of coherence as a resource along with four necessary conditions that should be satisfied by any appropriate measure of coherence
10
.

Most of the measures of quantum coherence are based on distances, defined in terms of the minimum distance between the selected quantum state and the given
set of incoherent states
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}
. These incoherent states are quantum states that are diagonalizable under a certain reference basis
\left\{{|i\rangle}\right\}_{i=1}^{d}
in
the Hilbert space, that is,
\delta=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{d}{{\delta_{i}}|i\rangle\langle i|}
. A measure of coherence is defined as follows:

{C_{D}}\left(\rho\right)=\mathop{\min}\limits_{\delta\in\tilde{\mathcal{I}}}D\left({\rho,\delta}\right),

where

D\left({\rho,\delta}\right)
denotes a certain measure of the
distance between
\rho
and
\delta
.

Baumgratz and his coworkers found that the relative entropy of coherence

C_{re}(\rho)=S(\rho_{diag})-S(\rho),

where
S
is the von Neumann entropy and
{\rho_{diag}}
denotes the state obtained from
\rho
by deleting all off-diagonal elements and the
l_{1}
-norm of coherence

{C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)=\sum\limits_{i\neq j}{\left|{{\rho_{ij}}}\right|},

are both appropriate measures of coherence.

Thus far, many suitable measures have been reported, such as the
robustness of coherence
11
, maximum relative entropy of
coherence
12
, entanglement-based coherence
measurement
13
, average quantum coherence
mjmt

coherence
sdru

l_{1}
-norm of coherence has
continued to be significant for research. Recently, it was
discovered that the
l_{1}
-norm of coherence can be used to
describe wave-particle duality
fcle

Ref.

can be expressed
as a convex combination of two pure states, i.e.

\rho={p_{1}}|{\psi_{1}}\rangle\langle{\psi_{1}}|+{p_{2}}|{\psi_{2}}\rangle\langle{\psi_{2}}|,

a triangle-like inequality can be established as follows:

\displaystyle\left|{E\left({\sqrt{{p_{1}}}|{\psi_{1}}\rangle}\right)-E\left({\sqrt{{p_{2}}}|{\psi_{2}}\rangle}\right)}\right|\leq E(\rho)

\displaystyle\leq E\left({\sqrt{{p_{1}}}|{\psi_{1}}\rangle}\right)+E\left({\sqrt{{p_{2}}}|{\psi_{2}}\rangle}\right),

where

Ref.

-norm measure of coherence for a convex combination of

Both Ref.

To date, many scholars have estimated the coherence of a quantum state, providing lower and upper bounds in different forms. However, we find that some of

On basis of the state decomposition of a quantum state,
Ref.
14
provides the following conclusion:

Lemma 1
.

If a quantum state
\rho
can be expressed as a convex combination
of two states, that is,
\rho=p_{1}\rho_{1}+p_{2}\rho_{2}
, then we
have

\left|{{p_{1}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{1}})-{p_{2}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{2}})}\right|\leq{C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho).

Moreover, Ref.
15
provides a triangle-like inequality in the

l_{1}
-norm measure of coherence.

Lemma 2
.

If the state
\rho
can be expressed as a convex combination of
n(n\geq 2)
states, that is,
\rho=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{{p_{i}}{\rho_{i}}}
, then
{C_{{l_{1}}}}\left(\rho\right)
satisfies the
following triangle-like inequality

\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{\left|{G_{k}^{(n-1)}-{p_{k}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{k}})}\right|}\leq{C_{{l_{1}}}}\left(\rho\right)\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{p_{k}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{k}})},

where
G_{k}^{(n-1)},k=1,2,\cdots,n
are the lower bounds of
\sum\nolimits_{i\neq k}^{n}{{p_{i}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}}\left({\frac{{\sum\nolimits_{j\neq k}^{n}{{p_{j}}{\rho_{j}}}}}{{\sum\nolimits_{t\neq k}^{n}{{p_{t}}}}}}\right)
.

We can further develop the conclusion of Lemma 2. However, we first introduce a useful lemma followed by a new
triangle-like inequality regarding the
l_{1}
-norm considering the
state decomposition of a quantum state.

Lemma 3
.

Let
{a_{i}}\geq 0(i=1,2,\cdots,n)
and not be complete zeros. If
b\geq{a_{i}},i=1,2,\cdots,n
, then we obtain

\displaystyle b\geq\frac{{\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{a_{i}^{2}}}}{{\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{a_{i}}}}\geq\frac{1}{n}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{a_{i}}.

Proof:
Based on these assumptions, we have

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{a_{i}^{2}}\leq\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{a_{i}}b=b\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{a_{i}}.

Note that
{a_{1}},\cdots,{a_{n}}
are not complete zeros; thus,

\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{a_{i}}>0
. Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle b\geq\frac{{\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{a_{i}^{2}}}}{{\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{a_{i}}}}.

Moreover, using the inequality

\displaystyle{\displaystyle({x_{1}}+{x_{2}}+\cdots+{x_{n}})^{2}}\leq n(x_{\rm{1}}^{\rm{2}}+x_{\rm{2}}^{\rm{2}}+\cdots+x_{n}^{2}),

we can directly obtain

\displaystyle\frac{{\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{a_{i}^{2}}}}{{\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{a_{i}}}}\geq\frac{1}{n}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{a_{i}}.

Next, we present the general triangle-like inequality based on the

Theorem 1

If the state

{A_{i}}=\left|{G_{i}^{(n-1)}-{p_{i}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{i}})}\right|.

It should be noted that we only consider the case where

G_{k}^{(n-1)}-{p_{k}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{k}}),k=1,2,\cdots,n

are not all zeros. If

{C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)

Proof:
First, we analyze the right-hand side of Eq.(
1

G_{1}^{(1)}={p_{2}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{2}}),G_{2}^{(1)}={p_{1}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{1}}).

(2)

Therefore, from Lemma 2, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[{(G_{1}^{(1)}+{p_{1}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{1}}))+(G_{2}^{(1)}+{p_{2}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{2}}))}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[{2({p_{1}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{1}})+{p_{2}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{2}}))}\right]

\displaystyle={p_{1}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{1}})+{p_{2}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{2}})\geq{C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho).

Hence, the conclusion is true when

.

Assuming that the conclusion is true when
n=m

\forall k\in\{1,2,\cdots,m+1\}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\neq k}{{\textstyle{\frac{{p_{i}}}{1-{p_{k}}}}}{\rho_{i}}}

remains a density matrix. According to Lemma 2, we have

\displaystyle{C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)

\displaystyle={C_{{l_{1}}}}((1-{p_{k}})(\sum\limits_{i\neq k}{{\textstyle{\frac{{p_{i}}}{1-{p_{k}}}}}{\rho_{i}}})+{p_{k}}{\rho_{k}})

\displaystyle(m+1){C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m+1}{{C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)}\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m+1}(G_{k}^{(m)}+{p_{k}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{k}})).

\displaystyle{\displaystyle C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)\leq\frac{1}{{m+1}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m+1}(G_{k}^{(m)}+{p_{k}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho_{k}})).

\displaystyle\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}(\rho)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{q_{k}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}(|{\phi_{k}}\rangle)}.

Let
|{\phi_{k}}\rangle=\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{d}{{\phi_{kj}}{e^{i{\theta_{kj}}}}|j\rangle},k=1,2,\cdots,n
, where
{\phi_{kj}}
are non-negative real numbers and
{\theta_{kj}}
are
real numbers. Thus,

\displaystyle|{\phi_{k}}\rangle\langle{\phi_{k}}|

\displaystyle=\left({\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{d}{{\phi_{kj}}{e^{i{\theta_{kj}}}}|j\rangle}}\right)\left({\sum\nolimits_{t=1}^{d}{{\phi_{kt}}{e^{-i{\theta_{kt}}}}\langle t|}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left({\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{d}{\sum\nolimits_{t=1}^{d}{{\phi_{kj}}{\phi_{kt}}{e^{i({\theta_{kj}}-{\theta_{kt}})}}|j\rangle\langle t|}}}\right).

Therefore,

{C_{{l_{1}}}}(|{\phi_{k}}\rangle)=\sum\limits_{i\neq j}{{\phi_{ki}}{\phi_{kj}}}.

(8)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{q_{k}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}(|{\phi_{k}}\rangle)}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{q_{k}}\sum\limits_{i\neq j}{{\phi_{ki}}{\phi_{kj}}}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{q_{k}}\frac{{\sum\limits_{i\neq j}{{\phi_{ki}}{\phi_{kj}}}}}{{1+\sum\limits_{i\neq j}{{\phi_{ki}}{\phi_{kj}}}}}}\geq\frac{{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{q_{k}^{2}\sum\limits_{i\neq j}{{\phi_{ki}}{\phi_{kj}}}}}}{{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{q_{k}}(1+\sum\limits_{i\neq j}{{\phi_{ki}}{\phi_{kj}}})}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{q_{k}^{2}\sum\limits_{i\neq j}{{\phi_{ki}}{\phi_{kj}}}}}}{{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{p_{k}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}(|{\psi_{k}}\rangle)}+1}}=\frac{{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{\sum\limits_{i\neq j}{{{\left({\sqrt{q_{k}{\phi_{ki}}{\phi_{kj}}}}\right)}^{2}}}}}}{{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{p_{k}}{C_{{l_{1}}}}(|{\psi_{k}}\rangle)}+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{{\sum\limits_{i\neq j}{{{\left({\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{\sqrt{q_{k}{\phi_{ki}}{\phi_{kj}}}}}\right)}^{2}}}}}{B}\geq\frac{{\sum\limits_{i\neq j}{{{\left|{{\rho_{ij}}}\right|}^{2}}}}}{B}\geq\frac{{{{\left|{{\rho_{st}}}\right|}^{2}}}}{B}.

We can also prove that the upper bound provided by theorem

4
is better than that provided by Lemma

6
.

Theorem 5
.

If a mixed state
\rho
can be expressed as a convex combination of
n
pure states,
|{\psi_{1}}\rangle,|{\psi_{2}}\rangle,\cdots,|{\psi_{n}}\rangle
, that is,

\rho=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{{p_{i}}|{\psi_{i}}\rangle\langle{\psi_{i}}|},

then it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{\left[{\mathaccent 869{G}_{k}^{(n-1)}+{p_{k}}\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}(|{\psi_{k}}\rangle)}\right]}\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{p_{k}}\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}(|{\psi_{k}}\rangle)}.

Proof:

Assume that a mixed state
\rho
can be expressed as a convex combination of
n
pure states
|{\psi_{1}}\rangle,|{\psi_{2}}\rangle,\cdots,|{\psi_{n}}\rangle
, that is,

\rho=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}{{p_{i}}|{\psi_{i}}\rangle\langle{\psi_{i}}|}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{\left[{\mathaccent 869{G}_{k}^{(n-1)}+{p_{k}}\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}(|{\psi_{k}}\rangle)}\right]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{\mathaccent 869{G}_{k}^{(n-1)}}+\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{p_{k}}\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}(|{\psi_{k}}\rangle)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{(1-{p_{k}})\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}(\sum\limits_{i\neq k}{{\textstyle{\frac{{p_{i}}}{1-{p_{k}}}}}|{\psi_{i}}\rangle})}+\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{p_{k}}\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}(|{\psi_{k}}\rangle)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{(1-{p_{k}})\sum\limits_{i\neq k}{{\textstyle{\frac{{p_{i}}}{1-{p_{k}}}}}\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}(|{\psi_{i}}\rangle)}}+\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{p_{k}}\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}(|{\psi_{k}}\rangle)}

\displaystyle=\frac{{(n-1)}}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{p_{k}}\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}(|{\psi_{k}}\rangle)}+\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{p_{k}}\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}(|{\psi_{k}}\rangle)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{p_{k}}\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}(|{\psi_{k}}\rangle)}.

IV
Different upper bounds of coherence

In this section, we detail the evaluation of the coherence of a
quantum state using its properties. Ref.
16
, using the
dimension of a quantum state, recorded the following conclusion:

Lemma 7
.

For any
d
-dimensional quantum state
\rho
, it holds that

{C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)\leq d-1
. Moreover,
{C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)

reaches the upper bound when
\rho
is in the maximally mixed state:

|\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{{\sqrt{d}}}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{d}{|i\rangle}.

However, in some cases, the evaluation provided by Lemma
7
is
highly inaccurate. For example,

\rho=diag\{{\lambda_{1}},{\lambda_{2}},\cdots,{\lambda_{d}}\}.

With additional conditions, Lemma
7
can be improved. To this end,
we introduce the following lemma
20
.

Lemma 8
.

Consider that
A=[{a_{ij}}]
is positive semi-definite and that for some
k\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
, it holds that
{a_{kk}}=0
. Then, for each
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
,
we have
{a_{ik}}={a_{ki}}=0
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\rho
be a
d
-dimensional quantum state. If there exists
r

null elements in the main diagonal of
\rho
, then

\displaystyle{\displaystyle C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)\leq d-r-1.

(9)

Proof:

Assume that
\rho
is a
d
-dimensional quantum state satisfying

{\rho_{{k_{i}},{k_{i}}}}\neq 0,i=1,2,\cdots,d-r({k_{1}}<{k_{2}}<\cdots<k_{d-r}).

Let the matrix of
\rho
under reference basis
\left\{{|i\rangle}\right\}_{i=1}^{d}
be

\rho=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{{\rho_{11}}}&{{\rho_{12}}}&\cdots&{{\rho_{1d}}}\\
{{\rho_{21}}}&{{\rho_{22}}}&\cdots&{{\rho_{2d}}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
{{\rho_{d1}}}&{{\rho_{d2}}}&\cdots&{{\rho_{dd}}},\end{array}}\right).

Define

\rho^{\prime}=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{{\rho_{{k_{1}},{k_{1}}}}}&*&\cdots&*\\
&{{\rho_{{k_{2}},{k_{2}}}}}&&*\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
&*&\cdots&{{\rho_{{k_{d-r}},{k_{d-r}}}}}\end{array}}\right).

where
\rho^{\prime}
is obtained via the permutation of each element of

\rho
whose row index or column index is
k_{i},i=1,2,\cdots,d-r

in the same order. Furthermore, all the
principal minors of
\rho^{\prime}
are the principal minors of
\rho
.
Therefore, all the principal minors of
\rho^{\prime}
are non-negative, given that
\rho
is positive semi-definite.

In addition,

tr(\rho^{\prime})=tr(\rho)=1.

Hence,
\rho^{\prime}
is a density matrix, the dimension of which is
d-r
.

According to Lemma
7
and Lemma
8
, we can directly
obtain

{C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)={C_{{l_{1}}}}({\rho^{\prime}})\leq d-r-1.

Furthermore, we provide another two distinct upper bounds of the
l_{1}
-norm.

Theorem 7
.

Assume that
\rho
is a
d
-dimensional quantum state. For convenience, the matrix of
\rho
under the reference basis
\left\{{|i\rangle}\right\}_{i=1}^{d}
is still denoted by
\rho
.
All integers
k(2\leq k\leq d)
are assembled such that
{{\rho_{k,1}},{\rho_{k+1,2}},\cdots{\rho_{d,d-k+1}}}
are not complete zeros in a set
{S_{\rho}}
.
Defining the number of elements in
{S_{\rho}}
as
t
, we have

{C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)\leq 2t.

Proof:

Assume that
\rho
is a
d
-dimensional quantum state. Let

{S_{\rho}}=\left\{{r_{1},r_{2},\cdots,r_{t}}\right\}.

All the principal minors of
\rho
are non-negative because
\rho
is positive semi-definite, that is,

\displaystyle\rho\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}i&j\\
i&j\end{array}}\right)=\left|{\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{{\rho_{ii}}}&{{\rho_{ij}}}\\
{{\rho_{ji}}}&{{\rho_{jj}}}\end{array}}\right|={\rho_{ii}}{\rho_{jj}}-{\left|{{\rho_{ij}}}\right|^{2}}\geq 0.

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\left|{{\rho_{ij}}}\right|\leq\sqrt{{\rho_{ii}}{\rho_{jj}}}\leq\frac{1}{2}({\rho_{ii}}+{\rho_{jj}}).

It should be noted that
\rho
is Hermite; hence, it follows that

\displaystyle{C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\neq j}{\left|{{\rho_{ij}}}\right|}=2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d-{r_{i}}+1}{\left|{{\rho_{{r_{i}}+j-1,j}}}\right|}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d-{r_{i}}+1}{\frac{{\left({{\rho_{{r_{i}}+j-1,{r_{i}}+j-1}}+{\rho_{jj}}}\right)}}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}{{\textstyle{\frac{{\rho_{jj}}}{2}}}}}+2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d-{r_{i}}+1}{\frac{{{\rho_{{r_{i}}+j-1,{r_{i}}+j-1}}}}{2}}}

\displaystyle=t+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d-{r_{i}}+1}{{\rho_{{r_{i}}+j-1,{r_{i}}+j-1}}}}.

We declare that, among the following
t(d+1)-({r_{1}}+{r_{2}}+\cdots+{r_{t}})
numbers

\displaystyle\rho_{{r_{1}},{r_{1}}},\rho_{{r_{1}}+1,{r_{1}}+1},\cdots,\rho_{d,d},\cdots,

\displaystyle\rho_{{r_{t}},{r_{t}}},\rho_{{r_{t}}+1,{r_{t}}+1},\cdots,\rho_{d,d},

the same one appears
t
times at most; thus,

\displaystyle{C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\leq t+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d-{r_{i}}+1}{{\rho_{{r_{i}}+j-1,{r_{i}}+j-1}}}}

\displaystyle\leq t+t({\rho_{11}}+\cdots+{\rho_{dd}})=2t.

Theorem 8
.

Let
\rho
be a
d
-dimensional
(d\geq 2)
quantum state. If there
exists
{\rho_{ij}}
such that
|{\rho_{ij}}|=0
, then we have

\displaystyle{\displaystyle C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)\leq\sqrt{\left[{d(d-1)-1}\right]\mu}\leq d-1,

(10)

where
\mu=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}{\rho_{{}_{kk}}^{2}}
and
{\lambda_{k}}\left({k=1,2,\cdots,d}\right)
are the eigenvalues of
\rho
.

Proof:
Let
\rho
be a
d
-dimensional quantum state.
According to the spectral decomposition theorem, there exists a
unitary matrix
U
such that

\displaystyle\rho=U\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{{\lambda_{1}}}&&&\\
&{{\lambda_{2}}}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&{{\lambda_{d}}},\end{array}}\right){U^{+}}.

Then,
\rho^{2}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle{\rho^{2}}

\displaystyle=U\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{{\lambda_{1}}}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&{{\lambda_{d}}}\end{array}}\right){U^{+}}U\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{{\lambda_{1}}}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&{{\lambda_{d}}}\end{array}}\right){U^{+}}

\displaystyle=U\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{\lambda_{\rm{1}}^{\rm{2}}}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&{\lambda_{d}^{2}}\end{array}}\right){U^{+}}.

Following the expression of
\rho^{2}
established above, we obtain

\displaystyle tr({\rho^{2}})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}{|{\rho_{ij}}{|^{2}}}}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}.

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\neq j}{|{\rho_{ij}}{|^{2}}}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}{\rho_{{}_{kk}}^{2}}.

Now, we introduce the modified Cauchy inequality
21
. Assume that

V
is a product space; thus, for any
x,y,z\in V
, where
\|z\|=1
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left|{(x,y)}\right|^{2}}\leq{\left\|x\right\|^{2}}{\left\|y\right\|^{2}}-G(x,y,z),

where

G(x,y,z)={\left({\left\|x\right\|(y,z)-\left\|y\right\|(x,z)}\right)^{2}}.

The equality holds only if
x,y,z
are linearly dependent.

Now, we attempt to determine an upper bound of
{C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)
using this useful tool. Let
V={\mathbb{R}^{n}}(n\geq 2)
and the standard inner product be taken as the fixed product. Subsequently,
x,y,z
is selected in the following manner:

\displaystyle x=({x_{1}},{x_{2}},\cdots,{x_{n}}),{\rm{}}y=(\frac{1}{{\sqrt{n}}},\frac{1}{{\sqrt{n}}},\cdots,\frac{1}{{\sqrt{n}}}),

\displaystyle}}{\rm{\displaystyle z=(0,0,\cdots,1,0,\cdots,0).

where the
i
th element of the
n
-tuple
z
is 1. Owing to the definition of the standard inner product, we have

\displaystyle(x,y)=\frac{1}{{\sqrt{n}}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{{x_{i}}},\left\|x\right\|=\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{x_{i}^{2}}},\left\|y\right\|=1,

\displaystyle(y,z)=\frac{1}{{\sqrt{n}}},(x,z)={x_{i}}.

Using the modified Cauchy inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}{\left({\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{{x_{i}}}}\right)^{2}}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{x_{i}^{2}}.{\left({\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{x_{i}^{2}}}\frac{1}{{\sqrt{n}}}-{x_{i}}}\right)^{2}}.

For the convenience of narration, we introduce the following notations:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{{x_{i}}}:=u,\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{x_{i}^{2}}:=\lambda.

Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}{u^{2}}\leq\lambda-{\left({\sqrt{\lambda}\frac{1}{{\sqrt{n}}}-{x_{i}}}\right)^{2}}.

In other words,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}{u^{2}}-\lambda+\frac{1}{n}\lambda\leq\frac{2}{{\sqrt{n}}}\sqrt{\lambda}{x_{i}}-x_{i}^{2}.

(11)

Note that Eq.(
11
) holds for each
i
; hence,

\frac{1}{n}{u^{2}}-\lambda+\frac{1}{n}\lambda\leq\min\left\{{\left.{\sqrt{\frac{{{\rm{4}}\lambda}}{n}}{x_{i}}-x_{i}^{2}}\right|i=1,\ ldots,n}\right\}.

If
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
, then
{x_{j}}=0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}{u^{2}}-\lambda+\frac{1}{n}\lambda\leq 0.

In other words,

\displaystyle u\leq\sqrt{(n-1)\lambda}.

In summary, it holds that

\displaystyle{\displaystyle C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)=\sum\limits_{i\neq j}{|{\rho_{ij}}|}\leq\sqrt{\left[{d(d-1)-1}\right]\mu},

where
\mu=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}-\sum\limits_{k,=1}^{d}{\rho_{{}_{kk}}^{2}}.

Next, we focus on

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left[{d(d-1)-1}\right]\mu}\leq d-1.

According to the properties of density matrix, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}\leq{\left({\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}{\lambda_{k}}}\right)^{2}}=1,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}{\rho_{{}_{kk}}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{d}{\left({\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}{\rho_{{}_{kk}}}}\right)^{\rm{2}}}=\frac{1}{d}.

Thus,
\mu
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mu\leq 1-\frac{1}{d}=\frac{{d-1}}{d}.

(12)

From Eq.(
12
), we can directly obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left[{d(d-1)-1}\right]\mu}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\left[{d(d-1)-1}\right]\frac{{d-1}}{d}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{{{\left({d-1}\right)}^{2}}-\frac{{d-1}}{d}}\leq d-1.

V
Conclusion and discussion

We studied the properties of quantum coherence based on the

l_{1}
-norm and convex-roof
l_{1}
-norm, thus realizing a better generalized triangular
inequality. Furthermore, we proved that, for a certain type of three-dimensional
quantum state
\rho
, it holds that
{C_{{l_{1}}}}\left(\rho\right)=\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}\left(\rho\right)
. In addition, we offer a few upper bounds for the
l_{1}
-norm in different
forms according to the properties of a quantum state.

For future research, we propose the following conjecture: Let

\rho
be a three-dimensional quantum state with
rank(\rho)\neq 2
; then,

{C_{{l_{1}}}}(\rho)=\mathaccent 869{{C_{{l_{1}}}}}(\rho).

The difficulty lies in calculating the convex-roof

l_{1}
-norm of more general quantum states.

We presented certain previously unreported facts,
which we believe will be helpful in the quantitative estimation of quantum
coherence and other quantum resources.

Uniform resolvent estimates for critical magnetic Schrödinger operators in 2D
Luca Fanelli, Junyong Zhang, Jiqiang Zheng

Abstract
. We study the L p − L q L^{p}-L^{q} -type uniform resolvent estimates for 2D-Schrödinger operators in scaling-critical magnetic fields, involving the Aharonov-Bohm model as a main example. As an application, we prove localization estimates for the eigenvalue of some non self-adjoint zero-order perturbations of the magnetic Hamiltonian.



1.
Introduction

In dimension
n=2
, let us consider a scaling invariant magnetic Schrödinger operator of the form

\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},a}=\Big(i\nabla+\frac{{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})}{|x|}\Big)^{2}+\frac{a(\hat{x})}{|x|^{2}},

(1.1)

where
\hat{x}=\tfrac{x}{|x|}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
a\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{R})
and
{{\bf A}}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1};\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfies the transversality condition

{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})\cdot\hat{x}=0,\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.2)

We recently started a program involving the above operators.
In
[
14
]
, the authors proved
the Strichartz estimates for wave; and later in
[
19
]
, together with Gao and Yin, the last two authors showed the Strichartz estimates for Klein-Gordon by constructing the spectral measure of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},0}
. In
[
18
]
,
we studied
L^{2}
-weighted resolvent estimates.
In this paper, based on the resolvent kernel constructed in
[
19
]
, we aim to study the
L^{p}\to L^{q}
type uniform resolvent inequalities for the purely magnetic operator
\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A},0}
of the form

\|(\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},0}-\sigma)^{-1}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C|\sigma|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}-1},

(1.3)

where
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
and the constant
C
is independent of
\sigma
.

The zero-energy and general dimension version of estimates (
1.3
) are related to the classical Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, which by Fourier transform is equivalent to the Sobolev embedding

\|(-\Delta)^{-\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{s}{n},\quad 1<q,r<\infty,\quad 0<s<n,

(1.4)

or equivalently

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Here the symbol
\lesssim
means as usual that in the right-hand side of the inequality a multiplicative constant is hidden, which does not depend on
u
.
In particular, when
s=2
, this inequality can be regarded as the following resolvent estimate

\|(-\Delta-\sigma)^{-1}u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(\sigma)\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

at the zero energy
\sigma=0
, where
C(\sigma)
is positive dimensional constant depending on
\sigma
. For non-zero energies, we have the following bound

\left\|(-\Delta-\sigma)^{-1}u\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C|\sigma|^{\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})-1}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty),

(1.5)

where
n\geq 3
and
(p,q)
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n+1}\leq\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{2}{n},\ \frac{2n}{n+3}<p<\frac{2n}{n+1},\ \frac{2n}{n-1}<q<\frac{2n}{n-3}.

In this free context
{{\bf A}}\equiv 0
, the above result appears first in the seminal paper by Kenig-Ruiz-Sogge
[
30
]
(in the case

,

n\geq 3

and the references therein for a more general picture). Later, Frank noticed in

Again in dimension

It is a quite natural problem to ask whether the analogous uniform resolvent estimates hold for the Schrödinger operator with magnetic or electric potentials.
There is an extensive literature (see e.g.
[
38
,
28
]
and the references therein for an obviously incomplete picture) on the resolvent estimates of the Schrödinger operator with purely electric potential

\mathcal{L}_{V}=-\Delta+V(x).

(1.8)

It was observed that the uniform resolvent estimates play a fundamental role in the establishment of time-decay estimates or Strichartz estimates, we refer to
[
28
,
27
,
37
,
22
,
26
]
.
For electromagnetic or magnetic cases, there are some results proved by Garcia
[
20
,
21
]
and Cuenin-Kenig
[
11
]
.
In all the above papers, the properties of the potentials about the smooth regularity and decay conditions at infinity are key to this problem.
On one hand, the potentials can usually be quite rough, but have sufficient decay at infinity. On the other hand, unbounded potentials are allowed but they are usually assumed to be smooth. The general philosophy to prove resolvent estimates in
L^{p}
spaces is to combine the
L^{p}
estimates for the free resolvent with weighted
L^{2}
estimates.

In this paper, we focus on purely magnetic potentials that are at critical with respect to the scaling of the free operator. It worths to remark that
most of results
[
20
,
21
,
11
]
needed that the electric potential decays at infinity as
|x|^{-2-\epsilon}
and the magnetic potential

|x|^{-1-\epsilon}
with
\epsilon>0
.
The inverse-square potential
V(x)=a(\hat{x})|x|^{-2}
with
\hat{x}=\frac{x}{|x|}
is a typical scaling-critical electric potential; and any homogeneous vector field of degree -1 is a scaling critical magnetic potential.
In
[
7
]
, Burq-Planchon-Stalker-Tahvildar-Zadeh proved
the
L^{2}
-weighted resolvent estimates

\||x|^{-1}((-\Delta+a(\hat{x})|x|^{-2})-\sigma)^{-1}|x|^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C

hold uniformly in
\sigma
, and then proved local smoothing estimates and Strichartz estimates.
Recently, Bouclet-Mizutani
[
6
]
proved
the weighted resolvent estimates
\|w(\mathcal{L}_{V}-\sigma)^{-1}w^{*}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}
uniformly in
\sigma

where
w
is in a large class of weight functions in Morrey-Campanato spaces.
The assumption on potential
V(x)
in
[
6
]
covers the scaling critical inverse-square type potentials
a|x|^{-2}
.
Later, Mizutani
[
35
]
generalized the
L^{2}
weighted resolvent estimates to the
L^{p}-L^{q}
type uniform Sobolev estimates (
1.5
),
but one needs that
V\in L^{\frac{n}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
n\geq 3
which does not include the inverse-square potential
a|x|^{-2}
.
Very recently, Mizutani and the last two authors
[
36
]

proved the uniform resolvent estimates (
1.5
) which recovers the scaling critical inverse-square type potentials
and the dimension
n\geq 3
. The new ingredient of
[
36
]
was to prove a generalized
L^{2}
weighted resolvent estimates

\||x|^{-s}((-\Delta+a(\hat{x})|x|^{-2})-\sigma)^{-1}|x|^{-(2-s)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C,

hold for some
s
, hence one can obtain
L^{p}-L^{q}
type uniform resolvent estimates. The general philosophy, mentioned above, combining the resolvent estimates of
-\Delta
in
L^{p}
spaces
and weighted
L^{2}
estimates works for
n\geq 3
in
[
36
]
but fails when
n=2
.

In
[
18
]
, Gao, Wang and the last two author considered the electromagnetic operator
\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},a}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by (
1.1
) and prove the following weighted
L^{2}
-estimates

\||x|^{-s}(\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},a}-\sigma)^{-1}|x|^{-(2-s)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C,

(1.9)

where
s\in(1-\mu_{0},1+\mu_{0})
with
\mu_{0}
depending on
{{\bf A}}
and
a
.
In particular, the diamagnetic phenomenon allows to consider negative electric potential which can be singular in the same fashion as the inverse-square potential. It is worth pointing out that the model
\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},a}
is in dimension two in which (
1.9
) fails even for
s=1
when

{\bf A}=0
and
a=0
since the resonance occurs at zero energy in this case.

We focus on the purely magnetic case and
we study the validity of the uniform resolvent estimates (
1.5
) for
the magnetic Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The main example is given by the Aharonov-Bohm potential

a\equiv 0,\qquad{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})=\alpha\Big(-\frac{x_{2}}{|x|},\frac{x_{1}}{|x|}\Big),\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{R},

(1.10)

introduced in
[
1
]
, as an well known model describing some scattering phenomenon arising in regions where the magnetic field is not present.
Notice that the singularity of the Aharonov-Bohm potential is not locally in
L^{2}
, which makes the analysis of this operator peculiar, compared to the usual theory for magnetic Schrödinger Hamiltonians (see
[
37
]
). On the other hand, the scaling invariance helps from the point of view of Fourier Analysis and permits to perform explicit calculus for generalized eigenfunctions, as we shall see in the sequel.

Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\frac{4}{3}
and
4<q\leq\infty
satisfy
\frac{2}{3}\leq\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}<1
and let
\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},0}

be (
1.1
) with
a\equiv 0
.
Then, for
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,+\infty)
, there exists a constant
C=C(p,q)>0
such that

\|(\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},0}-\sigma)^{-1}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C|\sigma|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}-1}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(1.11)

In particular, if
6\leq q<\infty
, then (
1.11
) holds for
q=p^{\prime}
.

Remark 1.1
.

It would be interesting to prove (
1.11
) for
\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},a}
. However,
the resolvent representation was constructed in
[
19
]
to capture both the oscillation and decay properties only for the case with pure magnetic potential, that is,
a\equiv 0
. It would be a bit delicate to recover the effect of the electric potential
a(\hat{x})|x|^{-2}
in the explicitly construction.

Remark 1.2
.

The perturbation argument in
[
36
]
to treat the inverse-square potential breaks down since the uniform Sobolev inequality
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=1
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
fails, as in the free case. This is due to the presence of a logaritmic singularity in the kernel of the Green function, which also appear in the Aharonov-Bohm case (see (
2.13
) and (
4.2
) below).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
follows from the ideas of Gutiérrez
[
23
]
and Kenig-Ruiz-Sogge
[
30
]
.
But we need to establish the Stein-Tomas restriction estimates associated with the variable coefficient operator
\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},0}
(perturbated by
scaling critical magnetic potentials) which
has its own independent interesting. Since the operator
\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},0}
is conically singular, we are in spirit of
[
3
]
(in which Blair-Ford-Marzuola studied the cluster estimates for a conical singular
Schrödinger operator) to prove the Stein-Tomas restriction estimates.
Based on the kernel of resolvent constructed in
[
19
]
,
due to the diffractive effect, the kernel is more singular and different from the kernel in the Euclidean setting.
In contrast to the estimates in weight
L^{2}
space, the establishment of
L^{p}\to L^{q}
-type estimates needs to efficiently exploit the oscillation behavior of the kernel,
and hence we have to modify the celebrating oscillatory integral theory of Stein
[
42
]
and Hörmander
[
25
]
to adapt to the singular kernel.

The most usual applications of results like Theorem
1.1
are concerned with spectral theory. Uniform resolvent estimates are a fundamental tool in order to understand the point spectrum of Hamiltonians of the form

\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A},V}:=\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},0}+V,

(1.12)

being
V:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
a (complex valued) measurable function. These non self-adjoint perturbations of self-adjoint Hamiltonians has been intensively studied in the last decades, due to their interest in Quantum Mechanics (see e.g.
[
2
,
39
]
). In our case, we consider the Aharonov-Bohm Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},0}
as the free Hamiltonian, and
V
as the perturbation. A first natural question is concerned with the absence of eigenvalues for
\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A},V}:=\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},0}+V
, when
V
is sufficiently small in some suitable sense. Notice that in the magnetic-free case the question is critical in dimension
n=2
, due to the lack of Hardy’s inequality. In other words, even a compactly supported potential
V
can create eigenvalues in the spectrum of
-\Delta+V
. On the other hand, due to the presence of the Aharonov-Bohm field, a Hardy inequality holds when the circulation
\alpha
is not an integer number (see
[
34
]
), hence the above question makes sense. An answer to this question is given in
[
9
,
13
]
, where weighted
L^{2}
estimates come into play. In our play, we would like to use the same arguments as in Theorem 2 by
[
15
]
, but unfortunately they strongly rely on the uniform Sobolev estimate, which in our case is false, as remarked above.

On the other hand, another natural problem is concerned with localization estimates for eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A},V}
, in terms of integral norms of
V
. Following Frank (cfr.
[
15
, Theorem 1]
), we prove the following application of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<\gamma\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
be an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A},V}
given in (
1.12
). Then,
if the complex potential
V\in L^{\gamma+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, the following estimate

|\lambda|^{\gamma}\leq C_{\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|V(x)|^{\gamma+1}\,dx

(1.13)

holds, with a positive constant
C_{\gamma}
only depending on
\gamma
.

Remark 1.3
.

Inequality (
1.13
) is related to Lieb-Thirring inequalities, which are usually simpler to prove when
V
is real, since the self-adjoint setting provides useful variational characterizations for the eigenvalues (see e.g.
[
5
]
and the references therein). At our knowledge, the case
V\in\mathbb{C}
was still not known.
Very recently, Bögli and Cuenin showed in
[
4
]
that the bound from above
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{2}
is sharp for the magnetic-free case, disproving a conjecture by Laptev and Safronov
[
33
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

In the magnetic-free case, Frank and Simon obtained in
[
17
]
that the bound can be improved to
0<\gamma\leq 2
, if one involves a stronger norm for
V
in the radial direction. The argument, which is again perturbative, also works in magnetic operators as above. We omit here further details and we address the reader to
[
17
]
.

Remark 1.5
.

By interpolation between (
1.11
) and the trivial estimate

\|(\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}},0}-\sigma)^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\text{dist}\,(\sigma,\mathbb{R}^{+})\|f\|_{L^{2}},

one can obtain bounds for the resolvent (and hence for the eigenvalues) for any value of
\gamma
, as showed in
[
16
]
in the magnetic-free case. Recently, Kwon and Lee showed in
[
31
]
that the resolvent bounds are sharp, while Böglie and Cuenin showed the sharpness of the corresponding eigenvalue bounds in
[
4
]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is identical to the one of Theorem 1 in
[
15
]
. Indeed, the only ingredients which come into play are the resolvent estimates (
1.11
) and the Birman-Schwinger Principle, which in this case holds true (cfr.
[
24
, Theorems 6,7]
). Therefore, we omit here further details of the proof.

0\leq b\leq 2,
we have

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{s}^{k}\Big(\frac{(e^{-s}-1+b^{2})\sinh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{(-s+b^{2})(\alpha s)}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big)\Big|\lesssim 1,~k=0,1,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\partial_{s}\Big[\Big(|\textbf{n}|^{-1/2}a(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|)\Big)\Big(\frac{b\cosh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{b}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big)\Big]\Big|ds\lesssim 1,

(5.5)

and

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\partial_{s}\Big[\Big(|\textbf{n}|^{-1/2}a(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|)-(r_{1}+r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}a(2^{j}(r_{1}+r_{2}))\Big)\Big(\frac{b}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big)\Big]\Big|ds\lesssim 1,

(5.6)

where
|a^{(k)}(r)|\lesssim r^{-k},~\forall\;k\geq 0,r>0.

Proof.

Case 1:
b=0
.

In this case, (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) are obvious, and

\partial_{s}^{k}\Big(\frac{(e^{-s}-1+b^{2})\sinh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{(-s+b^{2})(\alpha s)}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big)=\frac{(e^{-s}-1)\sinh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)}+2\alpha.

Noting that

\displaystyle|e^{-s}-1|\leq 2|s|,~|\sinh(s)|\sim|s|,\quad\forall\;|s|\leq 1,

(5.7)

we obtain

\Big|\frac{(e^{-s}-1)\sinh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)}+2\alpha\Big|\lesssim 1.

(5.8)

Next, we turn to estimate the term

\Big|\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{(e^{-s}-1)\sinh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)}+2\alpha\Big)\Big|=\Big|\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{(e^{-s}-1)\sinh(\alpha s)}{\cosh(s)-1}+2\alpha\Big)\Big|.

(5.9)

By a simple computation, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{(e^{-s}-1)\sinh(\alpha s)}{\cosh(s)-1}+2\alpha\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{[-e^{-s}\sinh(\alpha s)+\alpha(e^{-s}-1)\cosh(\alpha s)](\cosh(s)-1)-(e^{-s}-1)\sinh(\alpha s)\sinh(s)}{(\cosh(s)-1)^{2}}.

Using the Taylor expansion, we can write the above numerator as

\displaystyle\Big[-\big(1-s+\frac{s^{2}}{2!}+o(s^{2})\big)\big(\alpha s+\frac{(\alpha s)^{3}}{3!}+o(s^{3})\big)

\displaystyle+\alpha\big(-s+\frac{s^{2}}{2!}-\frac{s^{3}}{3!}+o(s^{3})\big)\big(1+\frac{s^{2}}{2!}+o(s^{3})\big)\Big]\big(\frac{s^{2}}{2!}+\frac{s^{4}}{4!}+o(s^{3})\big)

\displaystyle-\big(-s+\frac{s^{2}}{2!}-\frac{s^{3}}{3!}+o(s^{3})\big)\big(\alpha s+\frac{(\alpha s)^{3}}{3!}+o(s^{4})\big)\big(s+\frac{s^{3}}{3!}+o(s^{4})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(-\alpha s^{3}+\frac{3\alpha}{4}s^{4}+o(s^{4})\big)-\big(-\alpha s^{3}+\frac{\alpha}{2}s^{4}+o(s^{4})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{4}s^{4}+o(s^{4}),

which shows that the term (
5.9
) is bounded. Thus, we finish the proof of the case
b=0
.

Case 2:
b^{2}\geq\delta_{0}>0
.

By a simple computation, for
k=0,1,
we have

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{s}^{k}\Big(\frac{(e^{-s}-1+b^{2})\sinh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{(-s+b^{2})(\alpha s)}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big)\Big|\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta_{0}},

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{s}^{k}\Big(\frac{b\cosh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{b}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big)\Big|\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta_{0}},

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{s}^{k}\Big(\frac{b}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big)\Big|\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta_{0}}.

On the other hand, writing
r=2^{j}|\textbf{n}|,
one has

\displaystyle\partial_{s}(|\textbf{n}|^{-1/2}a(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|))

(5.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[-\frac{1}{2}a(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|)|\textbf{n}|^{-5/2}+|\textbf{n}|^{-3/2}(2^{j}\partial_{r}(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|))]\cdot r_{1}r_{2}\sinh(s).

By the assumption
r_{1}+r_{2}\sim 1,~r_{1},r_{2}\geq 0
and the properties of
a(r),
it is obvious that
|\partial_{s}(|\textbf{n}|^{-1/2}a(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|))|
is bounded. Thus, one can obtain the case
b^{2}\geq\delta_{0}
of the lemma.

Case 3:
0<b^{2}<\delta_{0}\ll 1.

Whereas the case
s\geq\delta_{0}
is similar to
b^{2}\geq\delta_{0},
we can obtain the same conclusions as Case 2. Therefore, we may assume that
s,b^{2}<\delta_{0}.

We first prove (
5.4
).
For
k=0,
by (
5.7
), we get

\Big|\frac{(e^{-s}-1+b^{2})\sinh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{(-s+b^{2})(\alpha s)}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big|\lesssim\frac{(s+b^{2})s}{s^{2}+b^{2}}\lesssim 1.

For
k=1,
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{(e^{-s}-1+b^{2})\sinh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{(-s+b^{2})(\alpha s)}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{(e^{-s}-1+b^{2})\sinh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{(-s+b^{2})(\alpha s)}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-e^{-s}\sinh(\alpha s)+\alpha(e^{-s}-1+b^{2})\cosh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\sinh(s)\cdot\frac{(e^{-s}-1+b^{2})\sinh(\alpha s)}{(2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha(b^{2}-2s)}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}+\frac{\alpha(-s+b^{2})s^{2}}{(s^{2}/2+b^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle A+B,

where

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle\frac{-e^{-s}\sinh(\alpha s)+\alpha(e^{-s}-1+b^{2})\cosh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{\alpha(b^{2}-2s)}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}},

\displaystyle B=

\displaystyle\sinh(s)\cdot\frac{(e^{-s}-1+b^{2})\sinh(\alpha s)}{(2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2})^{2}}+\frac{\alpha(-s+b^{2})s^{2}}{(s^{2}/2+b^{2})^{2}}.

We will prove that
A
and
B
are bounded respectively by using the Taylor expansion as follows.

Write
\displaystyle A=\frac{A_{1}}{A_{2}}
with

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\big[-e^{-s}\sinh(\alpha s)+\alpha(e^{-s}-1+b^{2})\cosh(\alpha s)\big](s^{2}/2+b^{2})-\alpha(-s+b^{2})s^{2}\big(2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\Big[-\big(1-s+\frac{s^{2}}{2}-\frac{s^{3}}{3!}+\frac{s^{4}}{4!}+o(s^{4})\big)\big((\alpha s)+\frac{(\alpha s)^{3}}{3!}+o(s^{4})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\alpha\big(-s+\frac{s^{2}}{2}-\frac{s^{3}}{3!}+\frac{s^{4}}{4!}+o(s^{4})+b^{2}\big)\big(1+\frac{(\alpha s)^{2}}{2}+\frac{(\alpha s)^{4}}{4!}+o(s^{5})\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad~~\times\big(\frac{s^{2}}{2}+b^{2}\big)-\alpha(-s+b^{2})s^{2}\big[\frac{s^{2}}{2}+\frac{s^{4}}{4!}+o(s^{5})+b^{2}\big],

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=(2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2})(s^{2}/2+b^{2})

\displaystyle=\big[\frac{s^{2}}{2}+\frac{s^{4}}{4!}+o(s^{5})+b^{2}\big]\big[\frac{s^{2}}{2}+b^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{s^{4}}{4}+s^{2}b^{2}+b^{4}+o(s^{5}).

Write
\displaystyle B=\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}
with

\displaystyle B_{1}=

\displaystyle-\sinh(s)(e^{-s}-1+b^{2})\sinh(\alpha s)(s^{2}/2+b^{2})^{2}+\alpha(-s+b^{2})s^{2}(2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Big(s+\frac{s^{3}}{3!}+o(s^{4})\Big)\Big(-s+\frac{s^{2}}{2}-\frac{s^{3}}{3!}+\frac{s^{4}}{4!}+o(s^{4})+b^{2}\Big)\Big((\alpha s)+\frac{(\alpha s)^{3}}{3!}+o(s^{4})\Big)\Big(\frac{s^{2}}{2}+b^{2}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle+\alpha(-s+b^{2})s^{2}\Big(\frac{s^{2}}{2}+\frac{s^{4}}{4!}+o(s^{5})+b^{2}\Big)^{2},

\displaystyle B_{2}=

\displaystyle(2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2})^{2}(s^{2}/2+b^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{s^{2}}{2}+\frac{s^{4}}{4!}+o(s^{5})+b^{2}\Big)^{2}\cdot\Big(\frac{s^{2}}{2}+b^{2}\Big)^{2}.

Since
A_{1},A_{2}
and
B_{1},B_{2}
both have the same order about
s
or
b
respectively, we can deduce that
A
and
B
are bounded.

Now, we turn to prove (
5.5
). Noting that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\partial_{s}\Big[\Big(|\textbf{n}|^{-1/2}a(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|)\Big)\Big(\frac{b\cosh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{b}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big)\Big]\Big|ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\partial_{s}\Big(|\textbf{n}|^{-1/2}a(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|)\Big)\cdot\Big(\frac{b\cosh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{b}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\Big(|\textbf{n}|^{-1/2}a(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|)\Big)\cdot\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{b\cosh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{b}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big)\Big|ds

(5.11)

and using (
5.10
) and the fact that
|\textbf{n}|^{-1/2}a(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|)
is bounded, and

\Big|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{b}{s^{2}+b^{2}}ds\Big|\lesssim 1,

(5.12)

we only need to show

\Big|\partial_{s}^{k}\Big(\frac{\cosh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{1}{\frac{s^{2}}{2}+b^{2}}\Big)\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{s^{2}+b^{2}},\quad k=0,1.

(5.13)

It is easy to show the case
k=0
.

For
k=1,
by a direct computation, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{\cosh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{1}{\frac{s^{2}}{2}+b^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\sinh(\alpha s)}{2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}}-\frac{2\cosh(\alpha s)\sinh(s/2)\cosh(s/2)}{[2\sinh^{2}(s/2)+b^{2}]^{2}}+\frac{s}{(\frac{s^{2}}{2}+b^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle I_{1}-I_{2}+I_{3}.

(5.14)

It is easy to check that

|I_{1}|\lesssim\frac{1}{s^{2}+b^{2}}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle|I_{3}-I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{s}{(\frac{s^{2}}{2}+b^{2})^{2}}-\frac{s}{[2\sinh^{2}(\frac{s}{2})+b^{2}]^{2}}\Big|+\Big|\frac{s}{[2\sinh^{2}(\frac{s}{2})+b^{2}]^{2}}-\frac{2\cosh(\alpha s)\sinh(\frac{s}{2})\cosh(\frac{s}{2})}{[2\sinh^{2}(\frac{s}{2})+b^{2}]^{2}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\frac{2[2\sinh^{2}(\frac{s}{2})+2b^{2}+\frac{s^{2}}{2}]\cdot[\sinh(\frac{s}{2})+\frac{s}{2}]\cdot|\sinh(\frac{s}{2})-\frac{s}{2}|}{(\frac{s^{2}}{2}+b^{2})^{2}[2\sinh^{2}(\frac{s}{2})+b^{2}]^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{|s-2\cosh(\alpha s)\sinh(\frac{s}{2})\cosh(\frac{s}{2})|}{[2\sinh^{2}(\frac{s}{2})+b^{2}]^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s^{2}+b^{2}}.

Hence, we derive (
5.13
) with
k=1
.

Finally, we show (
5.6
).
By (
5.10
) and (
5.12
), we only need to consider the term

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\Big(|\textbf{n}|^{-1/2}a(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|)-(r_{1}+r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}a(2^{j}(r_{1}+r_{2}))\Big)\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{b}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big)\Big|ds.

(5.15)

Since

\displaystyle(r_{1}+r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}-|\textbf{n}|^{-1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\textbf{n}|^{2}-(r_{1}+r_{2})^{2}}{(r_{1}+r_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}|\textbf{n}|^{1/2}((r_{1}+r_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}+|\textbf{n}|^{1/2})(r_{1}+r_{2}+|\textbf{n}|)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\sinh^{2}(\frac{s}{2})r_{1}r_{2}}{(r_{1}+r_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}|\textbf{n}|^{1/2}((r_{1}+r_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}+|\textbf{n}|^{1/2})(r_{1}+r_{2}+|\textbf{n}|)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle s^{2},

we get

\displaystyle\Big||\textbf{n}|^{-1/2}a(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|)-(r_{1}+r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}a(2^{j}(r_{1}+r_{2}))\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big|(r_{1}+r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}-|\textbf{n}|^{-1/2}\Big||a(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|)|+(r_{1}+r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big|a(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|)-a(2^{j}(r_{1}+r_{2}))\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle s^{2}+\big||\textbf{n}|-(r_{1}+r_{2})\big|\lesssim s^{2}.

Plugging this into (
5.15
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\Big(|\textbf{n}|^{-1/2}a(2^{j}|\textbf{n}|)-(r_{1}+r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}a(2^{j}(r_{1}+r_{2}))\Big)\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{b}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\Big)\Big|ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}s^{2}\frac{bs}{(s^{2}/2+b^{2})^{2}}\;ds\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{b}{s^{2}/2+b^{2}}\;ds\lesssim 1.

And so (
5.6
) holds. This concludes the proof of the lemma.

∎

Conics on Barth–Bauer octics
Alex Degtyarev

Abstract
We analyze the configurations of conics and lines on a special class of Kummer octic surfaces. In particular, we bound the number of conics by 176 176 and show that there is a unique surface with 176 176 conics, all irreducible: it admits a faithful action of one of the Mukai groups. Therefore, we also discuss conics and lines on Mukai surfaces: we discover a double plane (ramified at a smooth sextic curve) that contains 8910 8910 smooth conics.

, problem of counting/estimating the number of smooth

[

),

a satisfactory answer is known, see

In this paper, still working with polarized

, smooth

2
.
Remarkably, in our approach we do not need to

For the moment, the sharp upper bound

Recall that the
Kummer surface

\operatorname{Km}(A)
of an abelian surface
A
is
the quotient
A/{\pm 1}
blown up at the sixteen nodes—the images of the
sixteen fixed points of the involution. As is well known,
\operatorname{Km}(A)
is a

K3
-surface equipped with a distinguished collection of sixteen pairwise
disjoint smooth rational curves,
viz.
the exceptional divisors contracted by
the projection
\operatorname{Km}(X)\to A/{\pm 1}
. Conversely
(Nikulin
[
26
]
), any
K3
-surface with sixteen pairwise
disjoint
(-2)
-curves is Kummer.

Extending the construction of Barth–Bauer
[
2
]
and
Bauer
[
3
]
, we define a
Barth–Bauer surface
of
degree
h^{2}=2n\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}
as a
polarized Kummer surface

X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n+1}

with the property that the sixteen Kummer divisors map to sixteen
irreducible conics in
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
. Conjecturally
(see
[
11
,
12
]
), the maximal number of irreducible conics
on a smooth quartic surface is
N_{4}(2)=800
, and this maximum is attained
at a certain Barth–Bauer quartic.
Therefore, in this paper we make an attempt to estimate the maximum

N_{8}(2)
for octic surfaces
by obtaining a complete classification of the
Barth–Bauer octics up to
equiconical deformation
,
i.e.
, deformation in

\mathbb{P}^{5}
preserving the bi-colored
full Fano graph

\operatorname{Fn}X:=\operatorname{Fn}_{1}X\cup\operatorname{Fn}^{*}_{2}X

of lines and irreducible conics on
X
.
Here and below, we use the notation

•

\operatorname{Fn}_{1}X
for the graph of lines on
X
,

•

\operatorname{Fn}_{2}X
for the graph of all reduced conics on
X
, and

•

\operatorname{Fn}_{2}^{*}X\subset\operatorname{Fn}_{2}X
for the induced subgraph of irreducible conics;

in each graph, two vertices
u,v
are connected by an edge of multiplicity

u\cdot v
.
In addition to the Fano graphs
\Gamma
and
connected components of the respective
absolute strata

\mathcal{X}(\Gamma)
in the space
\mathcal{B}
of all Barth–Bauer octics, we also list
the
relative strata

\tilde{\mathcal{X}}(\Gamma,\Omega)\to\mathcal{X}(\Gamma)

consisting of
pairs
(X,\Omega)
, where
X
is a Barth–Bauer octic and
\Omega
is a
distinguished unordered collection of Kummer conics on
X
.

Convention 1.1
.

To avoid ambiguity, we emphasize that we consider
smooth
octics only,

i.e.
, the polarization
h
is assumed very ample. By a
conic
we mean a
reduced algebraic curve
C\subset X

of arithmetic genus
0
and projective degree

c\cdot h=2
. Thus,
C^{2}=-2
and,
since exceptional divisors are not allowed,
for a conic

there are but two possibilities:

•

is irreducible,
i.e.
, it is a true planar conic in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
, or

•

C=C_{1}+C_{2}
splits into a pair of distinct intersecting lines, so that
one has

C_{1}^{2}=C_{2}^{2}=-2
and
C_{1}\cdot h=C_{2}\cdot h=C_{1}\cdot C_{2}=1

(in particular,
C
is still a planar curve).

Theorem 1.2

(see

)
.

The maximal number of conics on a Barth–Bauer octic is

Up to projective transformation, there is a unique

(see

2.2
Barth–Bauer surfaces

According to Nikulin
[

a Kummer surface
(X,\Omega)
defines a canonical Kummer structure on the set
\Omega

its Kummer divisors, and

\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl NS}}\nolimits(X)
is a primitive extension
of

in (

\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl NS}}\nolimits(X)\ni h
, so that each
Kummer divisor

\mathbf{L}\supset\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl NS}}\nolimits(X)\supset\mathbf{S}+\mathbb{Z}h=\mathbf{S}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\tilde{h},\qquad\tilde{h}:=\Omega+h,\quad\tilde{h}^{2}=32+h^{2};

(2.7)

in particular,
h^{2}=0\bmod 4
by (
2.5
).

2.4
Connected components

Given a lattice
L
,
we denote by
\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits^{+\!}(L)
the group of auto-isometries of
L

preserving a
positive sign structure
,
i.e.
, coherent
orientation of all maximal positive definite subspaces of
L\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let
N\supset\mathbf{S}_{h}
be a geometric overlattice, see
2.8
,
and
G\subset\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits_{h}(N)
a fixed subgroup: in what follows, we will have either

G=\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits_{h}(N)
or
G=\mathop{\operator@font stab}\nolimits\Omega
.
Two isometries
\varphi_{i}\colon N\hookrightarrow\mathbf{L}
,
i=1,2
, are said to be

G
-equivalent
if there exists a pair of isometries

g\in G
,
f\in\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits^{+\!}(\mathbf{L})
such that
f\circ\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{2}\circ g
.

Fix a bi-colored graph
\Gamma
and consider geometric finite index extensions

N\supset\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)\ni h\quad\text{such that}\quad\operatorname{Fn}(N,h)=\Gamma.

(2.14)

Using Dolgachev’s
[
18
]
coarse moduli space of lattice
polarized
K3
-surfaces and factoring out the projective group, one easily
concludes (see
[
13
]
) that the connected components of the equiconical stratum

\mathcal{X}(\Gamma)
are of the form
\mathcal{X}(N\hookrightarrow\mathbf{L})
, where

•

N\supset\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)\ni h
is a geometric finite index extension as
in (
2.14
), regarded up to lattice isomorphism preserving
h
, and

•

N\hookrightarrow\mathbf{L}
is an
\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits_{h}(N)
-equivalence class of primitive isometries.

A similar statement holds for the relative stratum
\tilde{\mathcal{X}}(\Gamma,\Omega)
,
except that

•

N
is regarded up to isomorphism preserving
h
and
\Omega
(as a set), and

•

N\hookrightarrow\mathbf{L}
is a
(\mathop{\operator@font stab}\nolimits\Omega)
-equivalence class of primitive isometries.

In both cases, a component
\mathcal{X}(\varphi\colon N\hookrightarrow\mathbf{L})
is real if and only if

\varphi
is equivalent to
g\circ\varphi
for some (equivalently, any)

g\in\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits(\mathbf{L})\smallsetminus\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits^{+\!}(\mathbf{L})
.

Thus, the connected components of the strata associated to a graph
\Gamma

are in a bijection with the
equivalence
classes of the diagrams

\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow N\hookrightarrow\mathbf{L},

(2.15)

where
N
is admissible, the former
arrow is a finite index extension as in (
2.14
), and the latter arrow is a
primitive isometry. The equivalence is up to the group
\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits^{+}(\mathbf{L})
of
auto-isometries preserving the orientation of maximal positive definite
subspaces and appropriate, depending on the kind of strata considered,
subgroup of the group
\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits_{h}(N)
of autoisometries of
N
preserving the
polarization
h
.

\text{$g\in\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits(S)$ extends to~$N$ if and only if $g(\mathcal{K})=\mathcal{K}$}.

list (representatives of) the isomorphism classes

2.

Considering that
\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits_{h}(N)
is known, for (

of the canonical homomorphism
\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits^{+}(T)\to\mathop{\operator@font Aut}\nolimits(\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits T)

If
\mathop{\operator@font rk}\nolimits T=2

[
20
]
of binary quadratic forms (see also

[

If

\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits T

[

]

[

The supports of a vector

In view of
2.9
,
the graphs
\Gamma
to be tried for (
2.15
) are of the form

\Gamma:=\operatorname{Fn}\mathbf{S}_{h}[u_{i}]
, where
\mathbf{S}_{h}[u_{i}]\supset\mathbf{S}_{h}
is a
primitive corank
r
extension generated by
r
extra lines or conics

u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}
.
The next lemma (some parts of which are obvious geometrically)
controlls such extensions by bounding
the intersection indices of lines and conics.

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
be a smooth
K3
-octic,
l_{1},l_{2}\in\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl NS}}\nolimits(X)
a pair of
distinct lines
on
X
, and
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl NS}}\nolimits(X)
a pair of distinct conics. Then one has

l_{1}\cdot l_{2}\leqslant 1,\quad l_{1}\cdot c_{1}\leqslant 2\ (\text{or $1$, if $X$ is a triquadric}),\quad c_{1}\cdot c_{2}\leqslant 2.

Proof.

By the Hodge index theorem, the lattice
\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl NS}}\nolimits(X)
is hyperbolic. Hence, for
any pair of vectors
u,v\in\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl NS}}\nolimits(X)
, one has

\det(\mathbb{Z}h+\mathbb{Z}u+\mathbb{Z}v)\geqslant 0,

(2.19)

with the equality attained if and only if
h,u,v
are linearly dependent.

Applying (
2.19
) to one of the three pairs in the statement, we obtain

l_{1}\cdot l_{2}\leqslant 2,\qquad l_{1}\cdot c_{1}\leqslant 2,\qquad c_{1}\cdot c_{2}\leqslant 3,

and there remains to rule out the possibilities
l_{1}\cdot l_{2}=2
and

c_{1}\cdot c_{2}=3
.

In the former case,
l_{1}\cdot l_{2}=2
, the lattice contains the
2
-isotropic
vector
l_{1}+l_{2}
, see
2.8
(
3
), and the
map
X\to\mathbb{P}^{5}
defined by
h
is two-to-one, see
[
34
]
.

In the latter case,
c_{1}\cdot c_{2}=3
, the determinant (
2.19
) vanishes
and we obtain a relation
2h=c_{1}+c_{2}
. Hence,
h
is divisible by
2
in

\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl NS}}\nolimits(X)
and the map
X\to\mathbb{P}^{5}
is also two-to-one, factoring through the
Veronese embedding
\mathbb{P}^{2}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{5}
,
see
[
34
]
.

For the bound
l_{1}\cdot c_{1}\leqslant 1
, observe that, if
l_{1}\cdot c_{1}=2
, then the vector

is
3
-isotropic

. According

[

]
(see also
[

3.2
Restrictions on extra lines and conics

We start with a few further (
i.e.
, beyond those found in

)

supports of an extra line or conic
u

3.3

3.4

\operatorname{supp}_{1}u\in\mathcal{O}_{*}

h^{2}\in 4\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

Lemma 3.3

(see
[
11
]
)
.

Let
u\notin\mathbf{S}_{h}
be an extra conic
(
line
)
, and let

\mathfrak{u}:=\operatorname{supp}_{1}u,\ p:=\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{u}\mathclose{|},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}:=\operatorname{supp}_{2}u,\ q:=\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\mathclose{|}.

Then, for any pair
\mathfrak{v},\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}\subset\Omega
such that

\mathfrak{v}\in[\mathfrak{u}]_{p},\quad\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}\subset\Omega\smallsetminus\mathfrak{v},\quad\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}\mathclose{|}=q,

there is a conic
(
resp. line
)

v\in\mathbf{S}_{h}[u]
such that

\operatorname{supp}_{1}v=\mathfrak{v}
and
\operatorname{supp}_{2}v=\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

For completeness, we cite the proof found in
[
11
]
.
A set
\mathfrak{v}
as in the statement has the form
\mathfrak{v}=\mathfrak{u}\mathbin{\vartriangle}\mathfrak{o}
for some

\mathfrak{o}\in\mathcal{C}_{*}
such that
2\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{o}\cap\mathfrak{u}\mathclose{|}=\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{o}\mathclose{|}
.
Let
\mathfrak{s}_{+}:=\mathfrak{o}\cap\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
and
pick
\mathfrak{s}_{-}\subset\mathfrak{o}\cap\mathfrak{u}
so that
\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{s}_{-}\mathclose{|}=\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{s}_{+}\mathclose{|}
.
Then, the vector

w:=u+\mathopen{\|}\mathfrak{o}/\mathfrak{u}\mathclose{\|}+\mathfrak{s}_{+}-\mathfrak{s}_{-}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}[u]

has
\operatorname{supp}_{1}w=\mathfrak{u}\mathbin{\vartriangle}\mathfrak{o}
and

\mathfrak{w}^{\prime}:=\operatorname{supp}_{2}w=\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\mathbin{\vartriangle}(\mathfrak{s}_{+}\cup\mathfrak{s}_{-})
,
so that
\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{w}^{\prime}\mathclose{|}=\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\mathclose{|}
.
There remains to let

v:=w+(\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}\smallsetminus\mathfrak{w}^{\prime})-(\mathfrak{w}^{\prime}\smallsetminus\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}).\qed

Lemma 3.4

(
cf.

[
11
]
)
.

If
u\notin\mathbf{S}_{h}
is an extra conic and
\mathfrak{u}:=\operatorname{supp}_{1}u\in\mathcal{O}_{*}\cup\mathcal{K}_{*}
,
then any geometric extension of the lattice
\mathbf{S}_{h}[u]
is generated by
lines over
\mathbf{S}_{h}

Proof.

Assuming the contrary, let
\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}:=\operatorname{supp}_{2}u
and consider the vector

\hat{u}:=\begin{cases}u-\mathopen{\|}\mathfrak{u}/\varnothing\mathclose{\|}+\mathfrak{u}^{\prime},&\mbox{if $\mathfrak{u}\in\mathcal{O}_{*}$},\\
\hbar-u-\mathopen{\|}\bar{\mathfrak{u}}/\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\mathclose{\|},&\mbox{if $\mathfrak{u}\in\mathcal{K}_{*}$}.\end{cases}

We have
\hat{u}\in\mathbf{T}
, see (
2.3
), and, respectively,

\displaystyle\hat{u}^{2}

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}\tfrac{1}{2}\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{u}\mathclose{|}+2\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\mathclose{|}-2,

\displaystyle\quad\hat{u}\cdot h

\displaystyle=\phantom{0}2+\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{u}\mathclose{|}+2\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\mathclose{|}

\displaystyle\text{if $\mathfrak{u}\in\mathcal{O}_{*}$},

\displaystyle\hat{u}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{u}\mathclose{|}+\tfrac{1}{4}h^{2}+4,

\displaystyle\quad\hat{u}\cdot h

\displaystyle=14-\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{u}\mathclose{|}-2\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\mathclose{|}+\tfrac{1}{2}h^{2}

\displaystyle\text{if $\mathfrak{u}\in\mathcal{K}_{*}$}.

In view of (
2.5
), the presence of this vector

rules out
the patterns

,

,

16

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
u\notin\mathbf{S}_{h}
be an extra conic and
assume that

\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}:=\operatorname{supp}_{2}u\neq\varnothing
.
Then the lattice
\mathbf{S}_{h}[u]
has no geometric extensions.

Proof.

According to Tables
2
,
4

and
3.4
, we can assume
that

\mathfrak{u}:=\operatorname{supp}_{1}u\in\mathcal{C}_{12}\smallsetminus\mathcal{K}_{*};

hence, there is a set
\mathfrak{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{4}
such that
\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak{u}\mathclose{|}=2

Using
3.3
, we can change the set
\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
so that

\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\mathclose{|}\geqslant\min\{2,\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\mathclose{|}\}

Pick a singleton
\mathfrak{s}\subset\mathfrak{k}
as follows:

•

\mathfrak{s}\subset\mathfrak{k}\smallsetminus(\mathfrak{u}\cup\mathfrak{u}^{\prime})
if
\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\mathclose{|}=1

•

\mathfrak{s}\subset\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak{u}

\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\mathclose{|}\geqslant 2
.

Then, for the
B2
-conic
v:=\hbar+\mathopen{\|}\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{s}\mathclose{\|}

v\cdot u=-1
and, hence,

u-v
is an exceptional divisor, see

2.8
(
2
).
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
u\notin\mathbf{S}_{h}
be an extra conic,

\mathfrak{u}:=\operatorname{supp}_{1}u
, and

p:=\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{u}\mathclose{|}
.
Then, for any given set
\mathfrak{w}\in[\![\mathfrak{u}]\!]_{16-p}\smallsetminus[\mathfrak{u}]

such

\operatorname{supp}_{1}w=\mathfrak{w}

Proof.

Any set
\mathfrak{w}
as in the statement is of the form
\overline{\mathfrak{v}\mathbin{\vartriangle}\mathfrak{s}}

where
\mathfrak{v}:=\operatorname{supp}_{1}v\in[\mathfrak{u}]_{p}
for an appropriate vector

and
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathcal{K}_{4}
,
\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{s}\cap\mathfrak{v}\mathclose{|}=2
.
Besides, by
3.5
we can assume that

\operatorname{supp}_{2}u=\operatorname{supp}_{2}v=\varnothing
.
Then, it is immediate that the conic

w:=\hbar-\mathopen{\|}\mathfrak{s}/\mathfrak{v}\mathclose{\|}-v
is as required.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

There exists a subset
\mathfrak{r}\in\mathcal{K}_{12}

\mathopen{|}\mathfrak{r}\cap\mathfrak{u}\mathclose{|}=7

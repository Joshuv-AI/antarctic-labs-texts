Reversing orientation homeomorphisms of surfaces
Iryna Kuznietsova, Sergiy Maksymenko

Abstract
Аннотация. english



Let
M
be a connected compact orientable surface,
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a Morse function, and
h:M\to M
be a diffeomorphism which preserves
f
in the sense that
f\circ h=f
.
We will show that if
h
leaves invariant each regular component of each level set of
f
and reverses its orientation, then
h^{2}
is isotopic to the identity map of
M
via
f
-preserving isotopy.

This statement can be regarded as a foliated and a homotopy analogue of a well known observation that every reversing orientation orthogonal isomorphism of a plane has order
2
, i.e. is a mirror symmetry with respect to some line.
The obtained results hold in fact for a larger class of maps with isolated singularities from connected compact orientable surfaces to the real line and the circle.

1.
Introduction

The present paper describes several
foliated
and
homotopy
variants of a ‘‘rigidity’’ property for reversing orientation linear motions of the plane claiming that
every such motion has order
2
.
Though it is motivated by study of deformations of smooth functions on surfaces (and we prove the corresponding statements), the obtained results seem to have an independent interest.

Let
{\mathbb{D}}_{n}=\{r,s\mid r^{n}=s^{2}=1,\,rs=sr^{-1}\}
be the
dihedral
group, i.e. group of symmetries of a right
n
-polygon.
Then each ‘‘reversing orientation’’ element is written as
r^{k}s
for some
k
and has order
2
:

(r^{k}s)^{2}=r^{k}sr^{k}s=r^{k}r^{-k}ss=1.

More generally, let

SO^{-}(2)=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\sin t&\cos t\\
\cos t&-\sin t\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mid t\in[0;2\pi)\right\}

be the
set
of reversing orientation orthogonal maps of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, i.e. the adjacent class of
O(2)/SO(2)
distinct from
SO(2)
.
Then again one easily checks that each element of
SO^{-}(2)
has order
2
.

Another counterpart of this effect is that every motion of
{\mathbb{R}}
which reverses orientation is given by the formula:
f(x)=a-x
for some
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and therefore it has order
2
, i.e.
f(f(x))=x
.

Notice that such a property does not hold for ‘‘non-rigid motions’’, like arbitrary homeomorphisms or diffeomorphisms of
{\mathbb{R}}
or
S^{1}
.
For example, let
h:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be given by
h(x)=-x^{3}
.
It reverses orientation, but
h(h(x))=-(-x^{3})^{3}=x^{9}
, and therefore it is not the identity.

Nevertheless, counterparts of the above rigidity effects for homeomorphisms can still be obtained on a ‘‘homotopy’’ level.

For instance, let
\mathcal{H}(S^{1})
be the group of all homeomorphisms of the circle
S^{1}
and
\mathcal{H}^{+}(S^{1})
, (resp.
\mathcal{H}^{-}(S^{1})
), be the subgroup (resp. subset) consisting of homeomorphisms preserving (resp. reversing) orientation.
Endow these spaces with compact open topologies.
Notice that we have a natural inclusion
O(2)\subset\mathcal{H}(S^{1})
, which
consists of two inclusions

SO^{-}(2)\subset\mathcal{H}^{-}(S^{1})
and
SO(2)\subset\mathcal{H}^{+}(S^{1})
between the corresponding path components.
It is well known and is easy to see
SO^{-}(2)
(resp.
SO(2)
) is a strong deformation retract of
\mathcal{H}^{-}(S^{1})
(resp.
\mathcal{H}^{+}(S^{1})
).
This implies that the map
\mathsf{sq}:\mathcal{H}^{-}(S^{1})\to\mathcal{H}^{+}(S^{1})
defined by
\mathsf{sq}(h)=h^{2}
is null homotopic.

The aim of the present paper is to prove a parametric variant of the above ‘‘rigidity’’ statements for self-homeomorphisms of open subsets of topological products
X\times S^{1}
preserving first coordinate (Theorem
6.2
).
That result will be applied to diffeomorphisms preserving a Morse function on an orientable surface and reversing certain regular components of some of its level-sets (Theorems
3.3
,
3.5
, and
3.6
).

We will restrict ourselves with the following space of smooth maps.

Определение 2.2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}(M,P)
be the set of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
maps
f:M\to P
satisfying the following conditions:

(A1)

The map
f
takes constant value at each connected component of
\partial M
and has no critical points in
\partial M

(A2)

For every critical point

of
f
there is a local presentation

of

near

is a homogeneous polynomial

without multiple factors.

A map

will be called

if

satisfies
(A1)
and all its critical points are non-degenerate.
Notice that due to Morse lemma each Morse map

satisfies the condition
(A2)
with homogeneous polynomials
f_{z}=\pm x^{2}\pm y^{2}
for each critical point

.
Denote by
\mathrm{Morse}(M,P)
the set of all Morse maps
M\to P
.
Then we have the following inclusion:

\mathrm{Morse}(M,P)\subset\mathcal{F}(M,P)\subset\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,P).

Then
\mathrm{Morse}(M,P)
is open and everywhere dense in the subset of smooth functions from
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,P)
satisfying the condition
(A2)
.
Hence
\mathcal{F}(M,P)
consists of
even more that typical maps
M\to P
.

2.3.
Several constructions associated with
f\in\mathcal{F}(M,P)
.

In what follows we will assume that
f\in\mathcal{F}(M,P)
.
Let
\Sigma_{f}
be the set of critical points of
f
.
Then condition
(A2)
implies that each

is isolated.
In the case when

one can also say about local extremes of

, and even about local minimums or maximums if we fix an orientation of

.

A connected component

f^{-1}(c)

(of

).
We will call

Otherwise, it will be called

.

For

.
Then

A submanifold

, where each

is either a regular leaf of

or an

-regular neighborhood of some (regular or critical) leaf of

.

Let
\Gamma_{f}

It follows from axioms

.
We will call

The following statement is evident:

For

(a)

in
\mathrm{Int}\,{M}

2.3.3.
Singular foliation of

Consider finer partition

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

(a)

(b)

(c)

precisely elements of the singular foliation

By

Лемма 2.3.5

Let

3.
Main results

Let
M
be a compact surface,
h:M\to M
a homeomorphism, and
\gamma\subset M
a submanifold of
M
.
Then
\gamma
is
h
-invariant
, whenever
h(\gamma)=\gamma
.

Moreover, suppose
\gamma
is connected and
orientable

h
-invariant submanifold with
\dim\gamma\geq 1
.
Then
\gamma
is
h^{+}
-invariant
(resp.
h^{-}
-invariant
) whenever the restriction
h|_{\gamma}:\gamma\to\gamma
preserves (resp. reverses) orientation of
\gamma
.
If
\gamma
is a fixed point of

, then we assume that
\gamma
is mutually
h^{+}
- and
h^{-}
-invariant.

We will be interesting in the structure of diffeomorphisms preserving

and reversing orientations of some regular leaves of

.
The following easy lemma can be proved similarly to
[

, Lemma 3.5]
.

Лемма 3.1
.

Let

be a connected orientable surface and

be such that every regular leaf of

is
h
-invariant.
Then every critical leaf of
f
in also
h
-invariant and the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

some regular leaf of
f
is
h^{+}
-invariant;

(2)

all regular leaves of
f
are
h^{+}
-invariant;

(3)

h
preserves orientation of
M
.

Контрпример 3.2
.

Lemma

fails for non-orientable surfaces.
Let
M
be a Möbius band, and

be a Morse function having two critical points: one local extreme

and one saddle

.
Let

be the critical leaf of

containing

, and

and

Then it is easy to construct a diffeomorphism

-invariant.

-invariant, while regular leaves of

-invariant.

Denote by

-invariant

Let

.
Then by Lemma

Теорема 3.3

General result

Theorems
3.3
and

Let
M
be a connected compact (not necessarily orientable) surface,
f\in\mathcal{F}(M,P)
, and

.
Let also

\bullet

be the union of

-invariant regular leaves of

\bullet

be all the critical leaves of

\bullet

Evidently,

Лемма 3.5.1
.

Suppose
Z
is orientable.
Let also
\gamma
be a boundary component of
Z
.
Consider the following conditions:

(1)

\gamma\subset\mathrm{Int}\,{M}
;

(2)

\gamma=\partial U\cap\partial Z
for some connected component

of
\overline{M\setminus Z}
;

(3)

h(\gamma)\cap\gamma=\varnothing
;

(4)

.

Then
(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2)

\Rightarrow

(3)
\Leftrightarrow
(4)
.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2)
is evident, and
(3)
\Leftrightarrow
(4)
follows from the observation that
\gamma

(2)
\Rightarrow
(4)

Suppose

Let

.
∎

Thus

3.7.
Structure of the paper

In Section

Section

In Section

We prove Theorem

4.
Shifts along orbits of flows

In this section we extend several results obtained in
[
4
,
8
]
for smooth flows to a continuous situation.
Let
X
be a topological space.

Определение 4.1
.

A continuous map
\mathbf{F}:X\times{\mathbb{R}}\to X
is a
(global) flow on
X
, if
\mathbf{F}_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{X}
and
\mathbf{F}_{s}\circ\mathbf{F}_{t}=\mathbf{F}_{s+t}
for all
s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, where
\mathbf{F}_{s}:X\to X
is given by
\mathbf{F}_{s}(x)=\mathbf{F}(x,s)
.
For
x\in X
the subset
\mathbf{F}(x\times{\mathbb{R}})\subset X
is called the
orbit
of
x
.

It is well known that a
\mathcal{C}^{r}
,
1\leq r\leq\infty
, vector field
F
of a smooth compact manifold
X
tangent to
\partial X
always generates a flow
\mathbf{F}
.

Assume further that
\mathbf{F}
is a flow on a topological space
X
.
Let also
V\subset X
be a subset.
Say that a continuous map
h:V\to X

preserves orbits of
\mathbf{F}
on
V
if
h(\gamma\cap V)\subset\gamma
for every orbit
\gamma
of
\mathbf{F}
.
The latter means that for each
x\in U
there exists a number
\alpha_{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
h(x)=\mathbf{F}(x,\alpha_{x})
.
Notice that in general
\alpha_{x}
is not unique and does not continuously depend on
x
.

Conversely, let
\alpha:V\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous function such that its graph
\Gamma_{\alpha}=\{(x,\alpha(x)\mid x\in V\}
is contained in
W
.
For a global flow any continuous
\alpha:V\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies that condition.
Then one can define the following map
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}:V\to X
by

\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}(z):=\mathbf{F}(z,\alpha(z))=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha(z)}(z).

We will call
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
a
shift along orbits of
\mathbf{F}
by the function
\alpha
, while
\alpha
will be called a
shift function
for
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
.

Notice that
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
preserves orbits of
\mathbf{F}
on
V
, and in general is not a homeomorphism.

Определение 4.2
.

Let
x
be a non-fixed point of
\mathbf{F}
, and
Y
be a topological space.
Let also
U
be an open neighborhood of
x
, and
\phi=(\zeta,\rho):U\to Y\times{\mathbb{R}}
be an open embedding.
Then the pair
(\phi,U)
will be called a
flow-box chart
at
x
, if there exist an
\varepsilon>0
and an open neighborhood
V
of
x
in
X
such that

(4.1)

\phi\circ\mathbf{F}(z,t)=\bigl(\zeta(z),\rho(z)+t\bigr)

for all
(z,t)\in V\times(-\varepsilon;\varepsilon)
.

In other words,
\mathbf{F}
is
locally conjugated
to the flow
\mathbf{G}:(Y\times{\mathbb{R}})\times{\mathbb{R}}\to Y\times{\mathbb{R}}
defined by
\mathbf{G}(y,s,t)=(y,s+t)
, since the identity (
4.1
) can be written as

\phi\circ\mathbf{F}(z,t)=\bigl(\zeta(z),\rho(z)+t\bigr)=\mathbf{G}\bigl(\zeta(z),\rho(z),t\bigr)=\mathbf{G}(\phi(z),t),

i.e.

(4.2)

\phi\circ\mathbf{F}_{t}(z)=\mathbf{G}_{t}\circ\phi(z).

It is well known that each
\mathcal{C}^{2}
flow (generated by some
\mathcal{C}^{1}
vector field) on a smooth manifold admits flow-box charts at each non-fixed point.

Лемма 4.3
.

If
(\phi,U)
is a flow box chart at a non-fixed point
x\in X
of
\mathbf{F}
, then for any
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
the pair
(\phi\circ\mathbf{F}_{-\tau},\mathbf{F}_{\tau}(U))
is a flow box chart at the point
y=\mathbf{F}(x,\tau)
.

Denote
U^{\prime}=\mathbf{F}_{\tau}(U)
and
\phi^{\prime}=\phi\circ\mathbf{F}_{-\tau}
.
Let also
\varepsilon
and
V
be as in Definition
4.1
, and
V^{\prime}=\mathbf{F}_{\tau}(V)
be a neighborhood of
y
.
Then for any
(z,t)\in V^{\prime}\times(-\varepsilon;\varepsilon)
we have that

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}\circ\mathbf{F}_{t}(z)

\displaystyle=(\phi\circ\mathbf{F}_{-\tau})\circ\mathbf{F}_{t}(z)=(\phi\circ\mathbf{F}_{t-\tau})(z)=

\displaystyle=(\phi\circ\mathbf{F}_{t}\bigl(\mathbf{F}_{-\tau}(z)\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{equ:flow_box}}}{{=\!=}}\mathbf{G}_{t}\circ\phi(\mathbf{F}_{-\tau}(z))=\mathbf{G}_{t}\circ\phi^{\prime}(z).\qed

The following lemma shows that for flows admitting flow box charts (e.g. for smooth flows) every orbit preserving map admits a shift function near each non-fixed point.
Moreover, such a function is locally determined by its value at that point.

Лемма 4.4
.

(cf.
[
8
, Lemma 6.1(i)]
).

Suppose a flow
\mathbf{F}:X\times{\mathbb{R}}\to X
has a flow-box
(\phi,U)
at some non-fixed point
x
.
Let also
h:U\to X
be a continuous map preserving orbits of
\mathbf{F}
and such that
h(x)=\mathbf{F}(x,\tau)
for some
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then there exists an open neighborhood
V\subset U
of
x
and a unique continuous function
\alpha:V\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(1)

\alpha(x)=\tau
;

(2)

h(z)=\mathbf{F}(z,\alpha(z))
for all
z\in V
.

If in addition
X
is a manifold of class
\mathcal{C}^{r}
,
(1\leq r\leq\infty)
,
\mathbf{F}
and
h
are
\mathcal{C}^{r}
, and
\phi
is a
\mathcal{C}^{r}
embedding, then
\alpha
is
\mathcal{C}^{r}
as well.

The proof almost literally repeats the arguments of
[
8
, Lemma 6.2]
proved for smooth flows and based on existence of flow box charts.
For completeness we present a short proof for continuous situation.

1) First suppose
\tau=0
, so
h(x)=x
.
Let
V
and
\varepsilon
be the same as in Definition
4.2
.
Decreasing
V
one can also assume that
V\subset U\cap h^{-1}(U)
, so in particular
h(V)\subset U
.
Denote
\hat{V}:=\phi(V)
and
\hat{U}:=\phi(U)
.
Then these sets are open, and we have a well-defined map
\hat{h}=\phi\circ h\circ\phi^{-1}:\hat{V}\to\hat{U}
which preserves orbits of
\mathbf{G}
due to (
4.2
).
This means that
\hat{h}(y,t)=\bigl(y,\eta(y,t)\bigr)
for some continuous function
Y\times{\mathbb{R}}\supset\hat{V}\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \eta\penalty\ }{\mathbb{R}}
.
Define another continuous function
\alpha^{\prime}:\hat{V}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by
\alpha^{\prime}(y,t)=\eta(y,t)-t
.
Then

\hat{h}(y,t)=\bigl(y,t+\alpha^{\prime}(y,t)\bigr)=\mathbf{G}\bigl(y,t,\alpha^{\prime}(y,t)\bigr)=\mathbf{G}_{\alpha^{\prime}}(y,t),

that is
\phi\circ h\circ\phi^{-1}=\hat{h}=\mathbf{G}_{\alpha^{\prime}}
, whence for each
z\in V
we have that

h(z)=\phi^{-1}\circ\mathbf{G}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\circ\phi(z)=\phi^{-1}\circ\mathbf{G}_{\alpha^{\prime}\circ\phi(z)}\circ\phi(z)=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha^{\prime}\circ\phi(z)}(z)=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha^{\prime}\circ\phi}(z).

Thus one can put
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}\circ\phi:V\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
It follows that
\alpha
is continuous.
Moreover, let
\hat{y},\hat{t})=\phi(x)
.
Since
h(x)=x
, we get that
\tilde{h}(\hat{y},\hat{t})=\hat{y},\hat{t})
, whence
\eta(\hat{y},\hat{t})=\hat{t}
, whence
\alpha^{\prime}(\hat{y},\hat{t})=0
, and thus

\alpha(x)=\alpha^{\prime}\circ\phi(x)=\alpha^{\prime}(\hat{y},\hat{t})=0.

2) If
\tau\not=0
, then consider the map
\tilde{h}:U\to X
given by
\tilde{h}=\mathbf{F}_{-\tau}\circ h
.
Then
\tilde{h}(x)=\mathbf{F}_{-\tau}\circ h(x)=\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{F}(x,\tau),-\tau)=x
, whence by 1)
\tilde{h}=\mathbf{F}_{\hat{\alpha}}
for a unique continuous function
\hat{\alpha}:V\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\hat{\alpha}(x)=0
.
Put
\alpha=\hat{\alpha}+\tau
.
Then
\alpha(x)=\tau
and

h(z)=\mathbf{F}_{\tau}\circ\tilde{h}(z)=\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{F}(z,\hat{\alpha}(z)),\tau)=\mathbf{F}(z,\hat{\alpha}(z)+\tau)=\mathbf{F}(z,\alpha(z)).\qed

The formulas for
\alpha
imply that if
X
,
\mathbf{F}
,
\phi
, and
h
are
\mathcal{C}^{r}
,
(1\leq r\leq\infty)
, then
\alpha
is
\mathcal{C}^{r}
as well.

Следствие 4.5
.

Suppose
U\subset X
is an open
connected
subset such that every
x\in U
is non-fixed and admits a flow-box.
Let also
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}:U\to{\mathbb{R}}
be two continuous functions such that
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha^{\prime}}
on
U
.
If
\alpha(x)=\alpha^{\prime}(x)
at some
x\in U
(this holds e.g. if
\mathbf{F}
has at least one non-periodic point in
U
), then
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
on
U
.

The set
A=\{\alpha(y)=\alpha^{\prime}(y)\mid y\in U\}
is closed and by assumption non-empty (contains
x
).
Moreover, by Lemma
4.4
this set is open, whence
A=U
.
∎

Следствие 4.6
.

Let
\mathbf{F}:X\times{\mathbb{R}}\to X
be a continuous flow,
U\subset X
be an open subset such that every point
x\in U
is non-fixed and non-periodic and admits a flow box chart, and

be an orbit preserving map.
Then there exists a unique continuous function
\alpha:U\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
h(x)=\mathbf{F}(x,\alpha(x))
for all

.

If in addition

is a manifold of class

,
(0\leq r\leq\infty)
,
\mathbf{F}
is
\mathcal{C}^{r}
and admits

is
\mathcal{C}^{r}

\alpha
is
\mathcal{C}^{r}
as well.

Since every point

is non-fixed and non-periodic, there exists a unique number
\alpha(x)
such that

.
Moreover, since
\mathbf{F}
admits flow box chart at

that the correspondence

is a continuous function

under the corresponding smoothness assumptions on
X
,
\mathbf{F}
,
\phi
, and

.
∎

9.

Let

be a compact orientable surface,

,
Z
be an
f
-adapted subsurface,
h\in\mathcal{S}(f)
be such that
h(Z)=Z
, and

.
If
h^{m}|_{Z}

by
f
-preserving isotopy, then the following Lemma

gives conditions when one can change

on

so that its
m
-power will be
f
-preserving isotopic to the identity on all of

.
The proof follows the line of
[
8
, Lemma 13.1(3)]
in which

is a
2
-disk or a cylinder.

Лемма 9.1
.

(cf.
[
8
, Lemma 13.1(3)]
)

Let
M
be a connected compact orientable surface,
f\in\mathcal{F}(M,P)
,
Z
be an
f
-adapted subsurface, and
h\in\mathcal{S}(f)
be such that
h(Z)=Z
.
Suppose that the following conditions hold.

(1)

Each component of
Z
contains at least one saddle point of
f
.

(2)

h^{m}
is isotopic in
\mathcal{S}(f)
to a diffeomorphism
\tau
fixed on some neighborhood of
Z

(
by
[
8

this condition holds if there exists a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
\alpha:\penalty\ Z\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
h^{m}|_{Z}=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
)
.

(3)

There exists
m\geq 2
and
a\geq 1
such that connected components of
\overline{M\setminus Z}
can be enumerated as follows:

(9.1)

\begin{array}[]{cccc}Y_{1,0}&Y_{1,1}&\cdots&Y_{1,m-1}\\
Y_{2,0}&Y_{2,1}&\cdots&Y_{2,m-1}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
Y_{a,0}&Y_{a,1}&\cdots&Y_{a,m-1}\end{array}

so that
h(Y_{i,j})=Y_{i,j+1\bmod m}
for all
i,j
, that is
h
cyclically shifts columns in (

).

Then there exists
g\in\mathcal{S}(f)
such that
g=h
on
Z
and
g^{m}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f)
, that is
g^{m}=\mathbf{F}_{\beta}
for some
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function

.

Let

,

, be the union of components from the same column of (

).
Then
h(Y_{j})=Y_{j+1\bmod m}
.
Notice that condition
(3)
implies that
h^{m}(Y_{i,j})=Y_{i,j}
for all
i,j
.

We will show that the the desired diffeomorphism

can be defined by the formula:

g(x)=\begin{cases}h(x),&x\in Z\cup Y_{0}\cup\cdots\cup Y_{m-2},\\
\tau^{-1}\circ h(x),&x\in Y_{m-1}.\end{cases}

Indeed, by definition

on
Z
.
Moreover, as
\tau
is fixed on some neighborhood of
Z
, it also fixed near
Z\cap Y_{m-1}
.
Therefore
g=h=\tau^{-1}\circ h
near
Z\cap Y_{m-1}
, and so

is a well defined
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}

It remains to prove that

for some
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
\beta:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let
\mathbf{F}
be a Hamiltonian flow for
f
.
Since
\tau
and
h^{m}
are isotopic in
\mathcal{S}(f)
, it follows that
\tau^{-1}\circ h^{m}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f)
.
Hence by Lemma
2.3.5
,
\tau^{-1}\circ h^{m}=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
for some
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
\alpha:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

Since
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f)
is a normal subgroup of
\mathcal{S}(f)
, it follows that

h^{j}\circ(\tau^{-1}\circ h^{m})\circ h^{-j}=h^{j}\circ\tau^{-1}\circ h^{m-j}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f),\qquad j=0,\ldots,m-1,

as well.
Therefore, again by Lemma
2.3.5
,
h^{j}\circ\tau^{-1}\circ h^{m-j}=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha_{j}}
for some
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
\alpha_{j}:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

As
\tau
is fixed on some neighborhood of
Z
, it follows that for each
j

\mathbf{F}_{\alpha_{j}}=h^{j}\circ\tau^{-1}\circ h^{m-j}=\tau^{-1}\circ h^{m}=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}\ \text{on}\ Z.

Then the assumption
(1)
that every connected component
Z^{\prime}
of
Z
contains a saddle point, implies that
\mathbf{F}
has a non-closed orbit
\gamma
in

.
Therefore
\alpha=\alpha_{j}
on
\gamma
.
Since
Z^{\prime}
is connected, it follows from local uniqueness of shift functions for

(see Corollary
4.5
) that
\alpha=\alpha_{j}
near

for all
j=0,\ldots,m-1
.
Hence
\alpha=\alpha_{j}
near all of
Z
for all
j=0,\ldots,m-1
.

Thus we obtain a well-defined
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
\beta:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by:

\beta(x)=\begin{cases}\alpha(x),&x\in Z,\\
\alpha_{j}(x),&x\in Y_{j},\ j=0,\ldots,m-1.\end{cases}

We claim that

.

a) Indeed, if

g^{m}(x)=h^{m}(x)=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}(x)=\mathbf{F}_{\beta}(x)
.

b) Also notice that

and
g(Y_{j})=Y_{j+1\bmod m}
.
Then

Y_{j}\ \xrightarrow{\penalty\ h\penalty\ }\ Y_{j+1}\ \xrightarrow{\penalty\ h\penalty\ }\ \cdots\ \xrightarrow{\penalty\ h\penalty\ }\ Y_{m-1}\ \xrightarrow{\penalty\ \tau^{-1}\circ h\penalty\ }\ Y_{0}\ \xrightarrow{\penalty\ h\penalty\ }\ \cdots\ \xrightarrow{\penalty\ h\penalty\ }\ Y_{j}.

which thus coincides with

.
∎

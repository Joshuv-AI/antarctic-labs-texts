Theorem 4.5

(Skorokhod’s Representation Theorem)
.

For any zero-mean square-integrable martingale

\{\sum_{k=1}^{j}Y_{k},\ \mathcal{L}_{j}\mid j\geq 1\}
, there exist a
probability space supporting a (standard) Brownian motion
W
, and
nonnegative variables
\{T_{k}\,,\,\,k\geq 1\}
, such that

\{\sum_{k=1}^{j}Y_{k}\}_{j\geq 1}
and

\{W(\sum_{k=1}^{j}T_{k})\}_{j\geq 1}
have the same
distribution, and, in addition, letting
\mathcal{G}_{0}
be the trivial
\sigma
-algebra (the empty set
and the entire space), and
\mathcal{G}_{j}
, for
j\geq 1

\sigma
-algebra generated by

\{W(t)\mid 0\leq t\leq\tau_{j}\}\,,\,\,\mbox{ where }\tau_{j}:=\sum_{k=1}^{j}T_{k}\,,

then
\tau_{j}
is
\mathcal{G}_{j}
-measurable,
while

E(T_{1}\mid\mathcal{G}_{0})=E(W(T_{1})^{2}\mid\mathcal{G}_{0})

E(T_{j}\mid\mathcal{G}_{j-1})=E\bigl(\bigl(W(\tau_{j})-W(\tau_{j-1})\bigr)^{2}\mid\mathcal{G}_{j-1}\bigr)\,,\quad\forall j\geq 2\,,\quad\mbox{almost surely.}

By the last claim of Theorem
4.5
and properties of Brownian motion

(4.32)

E(T_{j}\mid\mathcal{G}_{j-1})=E(W(T_{j})^{2}\mid\mathcal{G}_{j-1})\,,\qquad\forall j\geq 1\,,

almost surely.
(Indeed, letting
W_{1}
be an
independent copy of
W
we have

in distribution, so that

W(\tau_{j})-W(\tau_{j-1})=W(T_{j})
in
distribution.)

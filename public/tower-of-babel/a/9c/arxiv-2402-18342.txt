Introduction

Denote
\tau_{k}(n)

k

\sum_{X<n\leqslant 2X}\tau_{k}(n)=XP_{k}(\log X)+O(X^{1-1/k+\varepsilon}),

where

is a polynomial of degree

\tau_{k}
within a relatively short interval

[
10

\sum_{X<n\leqslant X+H}\tau_{k}(n)=HP_{k}(\log X)+O(H\log^{(2/3+\varepsilon)k-1}X).

Meanwhile, in a recent paper

\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)=C_{k}H\log^{k-1}X+o(H\log^{k-1}X),

Building on these observations, we say a 1-bounded function

and

Outline of the proof

Our proof draws inspiration from the exploration of the local Fourier uniformity conjecture. Suppose that
\lambda
is the Liouville function.
[
7

predicts that if
H\to_{X}\infty
, then

\int_{X}^{2X}\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{T}}\bigl|\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\lambda(n)e(n\alpha)\bigr|\,\,\mathrm{d}x=o(XH).

This conjecture has been investigated in papers
[

8

\exp\left(C\log^{1/2}X(\log\log X)^{1/2}\right)\leqslant H\leqslant X
by Walsh
[
16
]
. Moreover, under the assumption of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, Walsh
[
17
]
verifies its effectiveness when
H\geqslant(\log X)^{\psi(X)}
where
\psi(X)
tends to infinity arbitrarily slowly.

In the following, we will present a brief outline of our proof, omitting several challenges we encountered. One notable challenge is that in the proof, for distinct purposes, we need to shift and shrink the supported intervals. However, especially when dealing with very short intervals, it becomes difficult to shift and shrink the supported intervals while maintaining local correlation information.

Following the ideas in studying the local Fourier uniformity conjecture, let’s assume

\int_{X}^{2X}\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{T}}\bigl|\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}(\tau_{k}(n)-\tau_{k}^{*}(n))e(n\alpha)\bigr|\,\,\mathrm{d}x\gg XH\log^{k-1}X.

The discretization process allows us to identify
\gg X/H
of
H

such that, for each of these
x

\alpha_{x}
where the local correlation holds,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\bigl|\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}(\tau_{k}(n)-\tau_{k}^{*}(n))e(n\alpha_{x})\bigr|\gg H\log^{k-1}X.

We then apply Ramaré’s identity, serving a similar purpose as Elliott’s inequality in the works
[
7
,
8
,
15

16

17
]

P\approx H^{\varepsilon}
from almost all

\Bigl|\sum_{\frac{x}{p}<n\leqslant\frac{x+H}{p}}\frac{\tau_{k}(n)-\tau_{k}^{*}(n)}{\omega_{(P^{\varepsilon},P]}(n)+1}e(n(p\alpha_{x}))\Bigr|\gg\frac{H\log^{k-1}X}{p},

where

counts the number of prime factors of

in the interval

Because

\displaystyle\Bigl|\sum_{n\in I}\frac{\tau_{k}(n)-\tau_{k}^{*}(n)}{\omega_{(P^{\varepsilon},P]}(n)+1}e(n(p\alpha_{x}))\Bigr|\gg\frac{H\log^{k-1}X}{P}.

We expect that there should only have a few frequencies dominates the above exponential sum, which means that there should be many pairs
(p,(x,\alpha_{x}))
and

such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle p\alpha_{x}\approx q\alpha_{y}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,1)\quad\text{ and }\quad x/p\approx y/q.

To make the information we obtained about

Lemma 2.1

(Shiu’s bound)
.

Let
A\geqslant 1
and
\varepsilon>0
be fixed. Let
X^{\varepsilon}\leqslant H\leqslant X
and
q\leqslant H^{1-\varepsilon}
. Let
f
be a non-negative multiplicative function such that
f(p^{l})\leqslant A^{l}
for every prime power
p^{l}
and
f(n)\ll_{c}n^{c}
for every

\sum_{X<n\leqslant X+H\atop n\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)}f(n)\ll\frac{H}{\phi(q)\log X}\exp\Bigl(\sum_{p\leqslant 2X,p\nmid q}\frac{f(p)}{p}\Bigr).

Proof.

[
12
, Theorem 1]

Lemma 2.2

(Local uniformity of divisor function)
.

Suppose that
\log^{k\log k}X\leqslant H\leqslant X
, then we have

\int_{X}^{2X}\Bigl|H^{-1}\sum_{x\leqslant n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)-x^{-1}\sum_{x<n\leqslant 2x}\tau_{k}(n)\Bigr|\,\,\mathrm{d}x=o(X\log^{k-1}X).

Proof.

[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
.
∎

We’d like to introduce a corollary to this lemma to make the exceptional set result clear.

Corollary 2.3

(Exceptional set and pointwise upper bound)
.

Let
0<\delta<1
be a parameter and
\log^{k\log k}X\leqslant H\leqslant X
. There is an exceptional set
\mathcal{E}\subseteq[X,2X]
with cardinality
\#\mathcal{E}\ll\delta X
and satisfing

(1)

\sum_{x\in\mathcal{E}}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}(\tau_{k}(n)+\tau_{k}^{*}(n))\ll\delta XH\log^{k-1}X
and,

(2)

when
x\in[X,2X]\backslash\mathcal{E}
we have the pointwise bound

\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}(\tau_{k}(n)+\tau_{k}^{*}(n))\ll H\log^{k-1}X.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{E}
is the set of integers
x\in(X,2X]
such that the pointwise bound
\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\ll H\log^{k-1}X
doesn’t hold, it can be deduced from Lemma
2.2
that
\#\mathcal{E}\ll\delta X
and
\sum_{x\in\mathcal{E}}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\ll\delta XH\log^{k-1}X
. Meanwhile, as it can be seen from the notion of the approximant that
\tau_{k}^{*}\leqslant\gamma^{1-k}\tau_{k}
pointwise, one thus has

\sum_{x\in\mathcal{E}}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}(\tau_{k}(n)+\tau_{k}^{*}(n))\leqslant\sum_{x\in\mathcal{E}}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}(1+\gamma^{1-k})\tau_{k}(n)\ll\delta XH\log^{k-1}X,

and when
x\in[X,2X]\backslash\mathcal{E}
one has

\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}(\tau_{k}(n)+\tau_{k}^{*}(n))\leqslant\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}(1+\gamma^{1-k})\tau_{k}(n)\ll H\log^{k-1}X.

∎

Lemma 2.4

(Local divisor correlations on average)
.

Let
0<\delta<1
be a parameter and
\log^{10000k\log k}X\leqslant H\leqslant X^{1/2-\varepsilon}
. There is an exceptional set
\mathcal{E}\subseteq[-H,H]
with cardinality
\#\mathcal{E}\ll\delta^{2}H
such that the following statement holds for all but
O(\delta X)
integers
x\in[X,2X]
:

(1)

(pointwise divisor correlation) If
h\in[-H,H]\backslash\mathcal{E}
we have

\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\tau_{k}(n+h)\ll\delta^{-1}H\log^{2k-2}X;

(2)

(negligible contribution from the exceptional set)

\sum_{h\in\mathcal{E}}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\tau_{k}(n+h)\ll\delta^{2}H^{2}\log^{2k-2}X.

Proof.

Let’s fix an integer
0\neq|h|\leqslant H
for a moment. When
X+H\leqslant n\leqslant 2X-H
, each term
\tau_{k}(n)\tau_{k}(n+h)
occurs
H
times in the expression

\sum_{X+H<x\leqslant 2X-H}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\tau_{k}(n+h).

Simultaneously, by considering the estimate
\sum_{X<x\leqslant X+H}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\tau_{k}(n+h)\ll H^{2}X^{\varepsilon}
, one can deduce from
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
that for each
0\neq|h|\leqslant H
there exists a constant
c_{k,h}>0
such that

\sum_{0<|h|\leqslant H}\Bigl|\sum_{x\sim X}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\tau_{k}(n+h)-c_{k,h}XH\log^{2k-2}X\Bigr|\leqslant\delta^{4}XH^{2}\log^{2k-2}X.

If we set
\mathcal{E}
as the set of
|h|\leqslant H
satisfying

\sum_{x\sim X}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\tau_{k}(n+h)\gg XH\log^{2k-2}X,

it is immediately clear that
\#\mathcal{E}\ll\delta^{4}H
. Moreover, considering the estimate
\sum_{x\sim X}\tau_{k}^{2}(x)\ll X\log^{2k-1}X
, derived from Lemma
2.1
, we can also obtain that

\sum_{x\sim X}\sum_{h\in\mathcal{E}}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\tau_{k}(n+h)\\
\leqslant\sum_{x\sim X}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}^{2}(n)+\sum_{h\in\mathcal{E}\backslash\left\{0\right\}}\sum_{x\sim X}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\tau_{k}(n+h)\\
\ll XH\log^{2k-1}X+\delta^{4}XH^{2}\log^{2k-2}X\ll\delta^{4}XH^{2}\log^{2k-2}X.

Therefore, it follows from the pigeonhole principle that for all but
O(\delta^{2}X)
integers
x\in[X,2X]
we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in\mathcal{E}}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\tau_{k}(n+h)\ll\delta^{2}H^{2}\log^{2k-2}X.

Now we may assume that
h\not\in\mathcal{E}
which means that

\sum_{x\sim X}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\tau_{k}(n+h)\ll XH\log^{2k-2}X.

Similarly, pigeonhole principle indicates that when
x\in[X,2X]
is outside a set with cardinality
O(\delta X)
the inequality

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\tau_{k}(n+h)\ll\delta^{-1}H\log^{2k-2}X

holds. The lemma is established by recognizing that there are, in total, at most
O(\delta X)
integers
x
in the interval
(X,2X]
for which either (
2.1
) or (
2.2
) does not hold.
∎

Lemma 2.5

(Pair of frequencies)
.

Let
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
be small parameters and
Q\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\vec{\alpha}_{1},\vec{\alpha}_{2}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
be frequencies and
p\neq q
be primes not dividing
Q
. Suppose that

(2.3)

\displaystyle p^{j}\alpha_{1}^{(j)}\equiv q^{j}\alpha_{2}^{(j)}+O(\varepsilon^{j}+\varepsilon^{\prime j})\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)\text{ for all }1\leqslant j\leqslant d.

Then there is a frequency
\vec{\alpha}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
such that

p^{j}\alpha^{(j)}\equiv\alpha_{2}^{(j)}+O\bigl(\bigl(\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{q}\bigr)^{j}\bigr)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)\text{ and }q^{j}\alpha^{(j)}\equiv\alpha_{1}^{(j)}+O\Bigl(\bigl(\frac{\varepsilon}{p}\bigr)^{j}\Bigr)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q).

Proof.

We are going to fix the degree
1\leqslant j\leqslant d
in the proof. If we take
p^{j}\beta_{1}^{(j)}\equiv\alpha_{2}^{(j)}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)
and
q^{j}\beta_{2}^{(j)}\equiv\alpha_{1}^{(j)}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)
, the assumption (
2.3
) implies that

(pq)^{j}\beta_{1}^{(j)}\equiv(pq)^{j}\beta_{2}^{(j)}+O\bigl(\varepsilon^{j}+\varepsilon^{\prime j}\bigr)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q).

On the other hand, if it is necessary one can add an integer multiple of
\frac{Q}{p^{j}}
to
\beta_{1}^{(j)}
and an integer multiple of
\frac{Q}{q^{j}}
to
\beta_{2}^{(j)}
to assume that

\beta_{1}^{(j)}\equiv\beta_{2}^{(j)}+O(\frac{Q}{2p^{j}q^{j}})\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q).

Combining these two estimates we have

\beta_{1}^{(j)}\equiv\beta_{2}^{(j)}+O\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon^{j}+\varepsilon^{\prime j}}{p^{j}q^{j}}\Bigr)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q).

Taking
\alpha^{(j)}=\frac{\varepsilon^{j}}{\varepsilon^{j}+\varepsilon^{\prime j}}\beta_{1}^{(j)}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime j}}{\varepsilon^{j}+\varepsilon^{\prime j}}\beta_{2}^{(j)}
, it is easy to see that

p^{j}\alpha^{(j)}-\alpha^{(j)}_{2}\equiv\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime j}p^{j}}{\varepsilon^{j}+\varepsilon^{\prime j}}\bigl(\beta_{2}^{(j)}-\beta_{1}^{(j)}\bigr)\equiv O\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime j}}{q^{j}}\Bigr)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q),

and similarly,
q^{j}\alpha^{(j)}\equiv\alpha_{1}^{(j)}+O\bigl(\frac{\varepsilon^{j}}{p^{j}}\bigr)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)
. Therefore, the frequency
\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha^{(d)},\dots,\alpha^{(1)})
satisfies the conditions of this lemma. ∎

We now explore the situation where there exists an ordered sequence of frequencies, and any two adjacent frequencies in the sequence satisfy condition (
2.3
). Let’s say,
\vec{\alpha}_{1},\dots,\vec{\alpha}_{k+1}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
are frequencies,
p_{1},\dots,p_{k},q_{1},\dots,q_{k}
are distinct primes not dividing
Q
. Suppose that for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
and
1\leqslant j\leqslant d
we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle p_{i}^{j}\alpha_{i}^{(j)}\equiv q_{i}^{j}\alpha_{i+1}^{(j)}+O(\varepsilon^{j})\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q).

The first application of Lemma
2.5
with
\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{\prime}
gives an ordered sequence
\left\{\vec{\alpha}_{2,1},\dots,\vec{\alpha}_{2,k}\right\}
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle p_{i}^{j}\alpha_{2,i}^{(j)}\equiv\alpha_{i+1}^{(j)}+O\Bigl((\frac{\varepsilon}{q_{i}})^{j}\Bigr)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)\text{ and }q_{i}^{j}\alpha_{2,i}^{(j)}\equiv\alpha_{i}^{(j)}+O\Bigl((\frac{\varepsilon}{p_{i}})^{j}\Bigr)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q).

Taking into account the relations between both

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
Q\in\mathbb{N}
,
\left\{\varepsilon,p_{1},\dots,q_{k},(\vec{\alpha}_{1},\dots,\vec{\alpha}_{k+1})\right\}
form a pre-path
\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)
of length

, and
\vec{\alpha}
be the top element of the pyramid. Suppose that
\prod_{i_{1}\leqslant i\leqslant i_{2}}p_{i}\asymp\prod_{i_{1}\leqslant i\leqslant i_{2}}q_{i}
whenever

and

\prod_{i\leqslant i^{\prime}}p_{i}^{j}\prod_{i^{\prime}<i\leqslant k}q_{i}^{j}\alpha^{(j)}\equiv\alpha_{i^{\prime}+1}^{(j)}+O(k\varepsilon^{j})\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q).

Proof.

For simplicity, we may represent
\vec{\alpha}_{i}
as
\vec{\alpha}_{1,i}
, and denote the top element
\vec{\alpha}
as
\vec{\alpha}_{k+1,1}
in the proof.
Our approach follows the principle of the left lower triangular, signifying that we exclusively utilize relations in the lower directions first and then employ relations in the left-lower directions. Assuming
2\leqslant t\leqslant k+1
and
1\leqslant i\leqslant k+2-t
, we assert that for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant d
, the following two congruence equations hold:

(2.6)

\displaystyle q^{j}_{i+t-2}\alpha_{t,i}^{(j)}\equiv\alpha_{t-1,i}^{(j)}+\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon^{j}}{(p_{i}\dots p_{i+t-2})^{j}}\Bigr)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)

and

(2.7)

\displaystyle p^{j}_{i}\alpha_{t,i}^{(j)}\equiv\alpha_{t-1,i+1}^{(j)}+O\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon^{j}}{(q_{i}\dots q_{i+t-2})^{j}}\Bigr)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q).

This claim can be easily confirmed through induction on
t
. When

it follows from (
2.5
). Now we suppose the claim holds for
2\leqslant t\leqslant k
and examine the case for

2.6
) with

and keeping (

) in mind, the triangle inequality demonstrates that

q^{j}_{i+t-1}\alpha_{t,i+1}^{(j)}\equiv p_{i}^{j}\alpha_{t,i}^{(j)}+O\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon^{j}}{(p_{i+1}\dots p_{i+t-1})^{j}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{j}}{(q_{i}\dots q_{i+t-2})^{j}}\Bigr)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q).

The application of Lemma
2.5
with
\varepsilon=\frac{\varepsilon}{p_{i+1}\dots p_{i+t-1}}
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\frac{\varepsilon}{q_{i}\dots q_{i+t-2}}
then gives a frequency
\alpha_{t+1,i}^{(j)}
such that

q^{j}_{i+t-1}\alpha_{t+1,i}^{(j)}\equiv\alpha_{t,i}^{(j)}+O\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon^{j}}{(p_{i}\dots p_{i+t-1})^{j}}\Bigr)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)

and

p_{i}^{j}\alpha_{t+1,i}^{(j)}\equiv\alpha_{t,i+1}^{(j)}+O\bigl(\frac{\varepsilon^{j}}{(q_{i}\dots q_{i+t-1})^{j}}\bigr)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q).

Let’s redirect our focus to the lemma itself. First of all, we aim to establish the relations between
\vec{\alpha}
and the elements that share the same vertical line with
\vec{\alpha}
. Assuming
0\leqslant i^{\prime}\leqslant k
, iteratively applying (
2.6
) with

and considering the given assumption
\prod_{i_{1}\leqslant i\leqslant i_{2}}p_{i}\asymp\prod_{i_{1}\leqslant i\leqslant i_{2}}q_{i}
one can get that

\prod_{i^{\prime}<t\leqslant k}q^{j}_{t}\alpha^{(j)}\equiv\prod_{i^{\prime}<t\leqslant k-1}q_{t}^{j}\Bigl(\alpha_{k,1}^{(j)}+O\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon^{j}}{(p_{1}\dots p_{k})^{j}}\Bigr)\Bigr)\\
\equiv\prod_{i^{\prime}<t\leqslant k-1}q_{t}^{j}\alpha_{k,1}^{(j)}+O\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon^{j}}{\prod_{t\leqslant i^{\prime}}p_{t}^{j}}\Bigr)\\
\equiv\cdots\equiv\alpha_{i^{\prime}+1,1}^{(j)}+O\Bigl((k-i^{\prime})\frac{\varepsilon^{j}}{\prod_{t\leqslant i^{\prime}}p_{t}^{j}}\Bigr)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q).

Next, we consider the situation involving elements in the horizontal line, namely,
\vec{\alpha}_{1,1},\dots,\vec{\alpha}_{1,k+1}
. Suppose that
0\leqslant i^{\prime}\leqslant k
, by employing (
2.7
), we can establish the relation between
\vec{\alpha}_{1,i^{\prime}+1}
and
\vec{\alpha}_{i^{\prime}+1,1}
.

\prod_{i\leqslant i^{\prime}}p_{i}^{j}\alpha_{i^{\prime}+1,1}^{(j)}\equiv\prod_{i\leqslant i^{\prime}-1}p_{i}^{j}\Bigl(\alpha_{i^{\prime},2}^{(j)}+O\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon^{j}}{(q_{1}\dots q_{i^{\prime}})^{j}}\Bigr)\Bigr)\\
\equiv\prod_{i\leqslant i^{\prime}-1}p_{i}^{j}\alpha_{i^{\prime},2}^{(j)}+O(\varepsilon^{j})\equiv\cdots\equiv\alpha_{1,i^{\prime}+1}^{(j)}+O(i^{\prime}\varepsilon^{j})\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q).

Therefore, combining the above two estimates together, one has

\prod_{i\leqslant i^{\prime}}p_{i}^{j}\prod_{i^{\prime}<i\leqslant k}q_{i}^{j}\alpha^{(j)}\equiv\prod_{i\leqslant i^{\prime}}p_{i}^{j}\Bigl(\alpha_{i^{\prime}+1,1}^{(j)}+O\Bigl((k-i^{\prime})\frac{\varepsilon^{j}}{\prod_{t\leqslant i^{\prime}}p_{t}^{j}}\Bigr)\Bigr)\equiv\alpha_{1,i^{\prime}+1}^{(j)}+O(k\varepsilon^{j})\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q),

this concludes the lemma.

One may remove elements in

∎

Lemma 4.3

(Extracting a small prime factor)
.

Suppose that
\exp(\log^{\varepsilon}X)\leqslant H\leqslant X^{1/2-\varepsilon}

\int_{X}^{2X}\sup_{\vec{\alpha}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}\sup_{|x_{0}-x|\leqslant H}\Bigl|\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}(\tau_{k}(n)-\tau^{*}_{k}(n))e\bigl(\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant d}\alpha^{(j)}(n-x_{0})^{j}\bigr)\Bigr|\,\,\mathrm{d}x\geqslant\eta XH\log^{k-1}X.

Then there is a
(c\eta^{2},H)
-configuration

x_{0}

holds for
\gg\eta^{6}P/\log P

, here,
0<t_{n}<1
for each integer

and

Proof.

Building on the second conclusion of Lemma
4.2

(c\eta,H)

(x,\vec{\alpha}_{x})\in\mathcal{J}_{-1}

Recalling Definition
4.1
, we may sum over
(x,\vec{\alpha}_{x})\in\mathcal{J}_{-1}
to conclude that

\eta^{2}X\log^{k-1}X\ll\sum_{(x,\vec{\alpha}_{x})\in\mathcal{J}_{-1}}\Bigl|\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}(1_{S}(n)\tau_{k}(n)-\tau_{k}^{*}(n))e\bigl(\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant d}\alpha_{x}^{(j)}(n-x_{0})^{j}\bigr)\Bigr|.

Based on whether or not
n
has a prime factor in the interval
(Q_{0},Q]
, the right-hand side summation is directly bounded by the sum of the following two expressions:

\sum_{(x,\vec{\alpha}_{x})\in\mathcal{J}_{-1}}\Bigl|\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}1_{(n,\mathcal{P}(Q_{0},Q))>1}(1_{S}(n)\tau_{k}(n)-\tau^{*}_{k}(n))e\bigl(\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant d}\alpha_{x}^{(j)}(n-x_{0})^{j}\bigr)\Bigr|

x:H\text{-separated}\end{subarray}}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\bigl(1_{(n,\mathcal{P}(Q_{0},Q))=1}\tau_{k}(n)+1_{(n,\mathcal{P}(Q_{0},Q))=1}\tau^{*}_{k}(n)\bigr)\ll\sum_{n\sim X}1_{(n,\mathcal{P}(Q_{0},Q))=1}\tau_{k}(n),

by noting that
\tau_{k}^{*}\ll\tau_{k}
pointwise. We are going to show that the second expression is negligible based on our chosen parameters
Q_{0}
and
Q
. In practice, Lemma

\sum_{n\sim X}1_{(n,\mathcal{P}(Q_{0},Q))=1}\tau_{k}(n)\ll\frac{X}{\log X}\exp\Bigl(\sum_{p\leqslant Q_{0}}\frac{k}{p}+\sum_{Q\leqslant p\leqslant 2X}\frac{k}{p}\Bigr)\ll X\log^{k-1}X\bigl(\frac{\log Q_{0}}{\log Q}\bigr)^{k}.

Recalling that

, this term is thereby bounded by

\omega

\eta

Therefore, we may assume that

\eta^{2}X\log^{k-1}X\ll\sum_{(x,\vec{\alpha}_{x})\in\mathcal{J}_{-1}}\Bigl|\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}1_{(n,\mathcal{P}(Q_{0},Q))>1}(1_{S}(n)\tau_{k}(n)-\tau^{*}_{k}(n))e\bigl(\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant d}\alpha_{x}^{(j)}(n-x_{0})^{j}\bigr)\Bigr|.

\eta^{2}X\log^{k-1}X\ll\sum_{(x,\vec{\alpha}_{x})\in\mathcal{J}_{-1}}\sum_{Q_{0}<p\leqslant Q}\Bigl|\sum_{\frac{x}{p}<n\leqslant\frac{x+H}{p}}\frac{1_{S}(pn)\tau_{k}(pn)-\tau^{*}_{k}(pn)}{\omega_{(Q_{0},Q]}(pn)}e\bigl(\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant d}p^{j}\alpha_{x}^{(j)}(n-\frac{x_{0}}{p})^{j}\bigr)\Bigr|.

Noting that the contribution of integers

which are not coprime with

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\sim X\\

Taking note that
1_{S}(pn)=1_{S}(n)

On the other hand, taking note that when

\displaystyle\Bigl|\sum_{\frac{x}{p}<n\leqslant\frac{x+H}{p}}\frac{1_{S}(n)\tau_{k}(n)-t_{n}\tau^{*}_{k}(n)}{\omega_{(Q_{0},Q]}(n)+1}e\bigl(\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant d}p^{j}\alpha_{x}^{(j)}(n-\frac{x_{0}}{p})^{j}\bigr)\Bigr|\gg\eta^{4}\frac{H\log^{k-1}X}{p}.

Lemma 5.1

(Only a few frequencies are inherently dominant)

\left\|q(\beta^{(j)}-\alpha^{(j)})\right\|\ll\eta^{-O(1)}H^{-j}\qquad(1\leqslant j\leqslant d).

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\vec{\alpha}_{1},\dots,\vec{\alpha}_{K}

Square both sides, on noting that
\tau_{k}^{*}\ll\tau_{k}

\eta^{2}H^{2}K\log^{2k-2}X\ll\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\sum_{x<n+h\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\tau_{k}(n+h)\\

where

with respect to the variable
h
. Since the assumption asserts that the contribution from

can be bounded by

\eta^{2}H^{2}K\log^{2k-2}X\ll\sum_{h\in[-H,H]\backslash\mathcal{E}}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\tau_{k}(n)\tau_{k}(n+h)\Bigl|\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant K}e\bigl(\alpha^{(d)}_{i}h^{d}+P(h;\vec{\alpha}_{i},n-z)\bigr)\Bigr|\\

\eta^{10}K^{2}H\ll\sum_{1\leqslant i,i^{\prime}\leqslant K}\Bigl|\sum_{|h|\leqslant H}e\bigl(h^{d}(\alpha_{i}^{(d)}-\alpha_{i^{\prime}}^{(d)})+P(h;\vec{\alpha}_{i},\vec{\alpha}_{i^{\prime}},n-z)\bigr)\Bigr|,

\eta HK\log^{k-1}X\leqslant\sum_{x_{0}}\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant K}\Bigl|\sum_{x_{0}<n\leqslant x_{0}+\eta^{2A}H}\frac{1_{S}(n)\tau_{k}(n)-t_{n}\tau_{k}^{*}(n)}{\omega_{(Q_{0},Q]}(n)+1}e\bigl(\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant d}\alpha_{i}^{(j)}(n-z)^{j}\bigr)\Bigr|\\
\leqslant\sum_{x_{0}}\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant K}\Bigl|\sum_{x_{0}<n\leqslant x_{0}+\eta^{2A}H}\frac{1_{S}(n)\tau_{k}(n)-t_{n}\tau_{k}^{*}(n)}{\omega_{(Q_{0},Q]}(n)+1}\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant d}e\bigl(\gamma^{(j)}(n-z)^{j})e((\alpha_{i}^{(j)}-\gamma^{(j)})(n-z)^{j}\bigr)\Bigr|,

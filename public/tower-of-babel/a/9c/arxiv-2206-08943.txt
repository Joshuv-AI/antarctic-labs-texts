Topological twists of massive SQCD, Part I
Johannes Aspman, Elias Furrer, Jan Manschot School of Mathematics, Trinity College, Dublin 2, Ireland Hamilton Mathematical Institute, Trinity College, Dublin 2

Abstract
We consider topological twists of four-dimensional 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 supersymmetric QCD with gauge group SU ( 2 ) (2) and N f ≤ 3 N_{f}\leq 3 fundamental hypermultiplets. The twists are labelled by a choice of background fluxes for the flavour group, which provides an infinite family of topological partition functions. In this Part I, we demonstrate that in the presence of such fluxes the theories can be formulated for arbitrary gauge bundles on a compact four-manifold. Moreover, we consider arbitrary masses for the hypermultiplets, which introduce new intricacies for the evaluation of the low-energy path integral on the Coulomb branch. We develop techniques for the evaluation of these path integrals. In the forthcoming Part II, we will deal with the explicit evaluation.

Order parameter
u

The order parameter
u
, introduced in (

can be expressed in terms of the running coupling
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
(
14

Nahm:1996di
;
Brandhuber:1996ng
;
Huang:2009md
;
Aspman:2021vhs
;

. As in

, we restrict the domain for
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
to
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})\subseteq\mathbb{H}
, such that

is 1-to-1.
In specific cases,

can be expressed in terms of modular forms. We follow the conventions of
(
Aspman:2021vhs
, Section 2.2)
in picking the solutions. We discuss the order parameters in more detail in the following Subsection

.

The period
da/du

The period
\frac{da}{du}
can be related to the complex structure
\tau
of the curve through
Brandhuber:1996ng

\frac{da}{du}=\frac{1}{6}\sqrt{\frac{g_{2}}{g_{3}}\frac{E_{6}}{E_{4}}},

(26)

where the Weierstraß invariants
g_{2}
,
g_{3}
are polynomials in
u
, and
E_{4}=E_{4}(\tau)
,
E_{6}=E_{6}(\tau)
are the Eisenstein series of modular weights four and six respectively, defined in Eq. (
154
). The square root in (
26
) leads to an ambiguity in the sign of
da/du
. This can be resolved by taking a branch of the square root, for example at weak coupling. On the other hand,
da/du
is not a single-valued function on the
u
-plane. It changes by a sign under monodromies at weak coupling, and transforms with a modular weight under monodromies around strong coupling singularities.

The derivatives
du/d\tau
and
da/d\tau

The derivative
du/d\tau
is related to the period
\frac{da}{du}
through
Matone:1995rx
;
Aspman:2021vhs

\frac{du}{d\tau}=-\frac{16\pi i}{4-N_{f}}\frac{\Delta_{N_{f}}}{P^{\text{M}}_{N_{f}}}\left(\frac{da}{du}\right)^{2},

(27)

where
P^{\text{M}}_{N_{f}}
is a polynomial in
u
. For
N_{f}\leq 3
, it reads

P^{\text{M}}_{N_{f}}=\frac{6}{4-N_{f}}(-1)^{N_{f}}\Lambda_{N_{f}}^{2N_{f}-8}(2g_{2}g_{3}^{\prime}-3g_{2}^{\prime}g_{3}),

(28)

where the prime denotes differentiation with respect to
u
. It appears naturally in differential equations associated with elliptic surfaces (see e.g.
stiller1981
).

When
u
is known in terms of modular forms, it is also straightforward to determine
\frac{du}{d\tau}
by differentiating modular forms
Aspman:2021vhs
. For generic masses
{\boldsymbol{m}}
, we can compute the
q
-series of
u
and thus
\frac{du}{d\tau}
to any order. We will not explicitly need
du/d\tau
for evaluating the
u
-plane integral, but through

\frac{da}{d\tau}=\frac{da}{du}\frac{du}{d\tau}

(29)

we can use it to evaluate
da/d\tau
. This derivative appears as the Jacobian for the change of integration variables from the periods
a
to the couplings
\tau
.

2.4
Fundamental domains

The recent progress in computing
u
-plane integrals has been enabled by mapping the
u
-plane to a modular fundamental domain, on which the
u
-plane integrand can be related to mock modular forms and thus be efficiently evaluated
Malmendier:2008db
;
Griffin:2012kw
;
Malmendier:2012zz
;
Malmendier:2010ss
;
Korpas:2017qdo
;
Moore:2017cmm
;
Korpas:2019ava
;
Korpas:2019cwg
;
Manschot:2021qqe
;
Aspman:2021kfp
;
Korpas:2022tij
. It has been known since the 1990s that the
u
-planes for
\mathcal{N}=2
SQCD with
N_{f}=0,2,3
massless hypermultiplets are modular and correspond to fundamental domains for congruence subgroups of
\text{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})

Nahm:1996di
. On the other hand, the generic mass case including the peculiar role of massless
N_{f}=1
have remained elusive.

The order parameter
u
for a given mass
{\boldsymbol{m}}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{N_{f}})
can be considered as a function

u:\mathbb{H}\to\mathcal{B}_{N_{f}}({\boldsymbol{m}}),

(30)

where
\mathcal{B}_{N_{f}}({\boldsymbol{m}})
is the Coulomb branch of the theory with
N_{f}
hypermultiplets of mass
{\boldsymbol{m}}
. In
Aspman:2021vhs
, it was found that for
N_{f}\geq 1
and generic masses the duality group does not act on
\tau
by fractional linear transformations. This prevents the preimage
u^{-1}(\mathcal{B}_{N_{f}}({\boldsymbol{m}}))
from being a modular fundamental domain for a subgroup of
\text{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
. Instead, we can define a fundamental domain
\mathcal{F}_{N_{f}}({\boldsymbol{m}})
through the equivalence relation
\tau\sim\tau^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow u(\tau)=u(\tau^{\prime})
, such that
u:\mathcal{F}_{N_{f}}({\boldsymbol{m}})\to\mathcal{B}_{N_{f}}({\boldsymbol{m}})
is bijective. In
Aspman:2021vhs
;
Aspman:2021evt
, such fundamental domains for the effective gauge coupling have been found explicitly for
\mathcal{N}=2
SQCD with
N_{f}\leq 4
generic masses. They decompose as

\mathcal{F}_{N_{f}}({\boldsymbol{m}})=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}\mathcal{F},

(31)

where

\mathcal{F}=\text{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}
is the key-hole fundamental domain of
\text{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
, and
\alpha_{j}\in\text{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
are
n\leq 6
maps that are locally constant as functions of the masses
{\boldsymbol{m}}
. More precisely,
\mathcal{F}_{N_{f}}({\boldsymbol{m}})
is constant on any of the finitely many connected components of
\{{\boldsymbol{m}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{f}}\,|\,D(\Delta_{N_{f}})=0\}
, where
D(\Delta_{N_{f}})
is given in (
25
).
We also call
n
the index of the domain
\mathcal{F}_{N_{f}}({\boldsymbol{m}})
.

The geometry of
\mathcal{F}_{N_{f}}({\boldsymbol{m}})
agrees with that of the Coulomb branch
\mathcal{B}_{N_{f}}({\boldsymbol{m}})
. Namely,
\mathcal{B}_{N_{f}}({\boldsymbol{m}})
is the complex plane with
2+N_{f}
singular points removed. In (
31
), those singularities are reflected in the cusps
\alpha_{j}(\infty)
, where the number of
\alpha_{j}
with the same
\alpha_{j}(\infty)
(giving the
width
of the cusp) agrees with the multiplicity of the singularity
u(\alpha_{j}(\infty))
.

The domains
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{N_{f}}({\boldsymbol{m}})
are endowed with further data. For generic masses
{\boldsymbol{m}}
, there are
N_{f}
pairs of branch points on
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
that are connected by branch cuts. The branch points are determined by the non-removable singularities of the rational function
\Delta_{N_{f}}(u)/P^{\text{M}}_{N_{f}}(u)
, as it appears in Matone’s relation (
27
). They correspond to the nontrivial zeros of the discriminant of the sextic equation associated with the SW curve
Aspman:2021vhs
. As the masses are varied, the branch points move on continuous paths in the fundamental domain.

When two branch points collide, there appears an elliptic point, which is a further dedicated point on the domain
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
. These elliptic points
correspond to the superconformal Argyres-Douglas (AD) points
Argyres:1995jj
;
Argyres:1995xn
. By Kodaira’s classification of singular fibres, they are always in the
\text{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
orbit of
i
or
e^{\pi i/3}
. Their presence is also responsible for the index
n
of the domain to be reduced. The possible values are
n=2,3,4
if
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
contains such an elliptic point, and
n=6
otherwise.

The
N_{f}=4
theory furthermore contains a UV coupling
\tau_{\text{\tiny{UV}}}
. Since
u(\tau)\to\infty
for
\tau\to\tau_{\text{\tiny{UV}}}
, it can be viewed as a puncture of the fundamental domain, and has to be excluded from the integration domain.

Let us review the explicit construction of
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
in a few important examples. Many more examples can be found in
Aspman:2021vhs
;
Aspman:2021evt
. Here, we are mainly considering the masses to be small compared to the period
a
, such that the mass singularities discussed in previous sections can be considered as strong coupling singularities, or in other words as cusps on the real line of the fundamental domains. When the masses are increased it is more natural to think of the mass singularities as weakly coupled.

2.5
Monodromies

This section determines the monodromies around the
N_{f}+2
monodromies. We leave the winding numbers
n_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,N_{f}
, for
a_{D}
generic. Starting with the monodromy around infinity,
a\to e^{\pi i}a
, we deduce from the (
10
) that the vector
\Pi
transforms as
\Pi\to{\bf M}_{\infty}\,\Pi
, with
{\bf M}_{\infty}
given by

{\bf M}_{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&4-N_{f}&0&-n_{1}&\cdots&0&-n_{N_{f}}\\
0&-1&0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&n_{1}&1&1&\cdots&0&0\\
0&0&0&1&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&&\ddots&&\\
0&n_{N_{f}}&0&0&\cdots&1&1\\
0&0&0&0&\cdots&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(39)

The monodromy matrix
{\bf M}_{\infty}
is in
\text{SL}(2+2N_{f},\mathbb{Z})
, while it acts on the couplings by a symplectic transformation, i.e. it preserves the symplectic form (
13
). This can be checked by requiring that any monodromy
{\bf M}_{\infty}
satisfies
{\bf M}^{T}{\bf J}{\bf M}={\bf J}
, with

{\bf J}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}^{\oplus N_{f}+1}.

(40)

The action on the couplings
\tau
(
14
),
v_{j}
and
w_{jk}
(
15
) is thus

{\bf M}_{\infty}:\begin{cases}\tau\to\tau+N_{f}-4,\\
v_{j}\to-v_{j}-n_{j},\\
w_{jk}\to w_{jk}+\delta_{jk},\end{cases}

(41)

with
\delta_{jk}
the Kronecker delta.

If we assume that the mass
m_{j}
is large, we can also deduce the monodromies around
a=\tfrac{m_{j}}{\sqrt{2}}
,
j=1,\dots,N_{f}
from the perturbative prepotential (
10
). For
a
encircling
\tfrac{m_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}
counterclockwise,
\Pi\to{\bf M}_{1}\Pi
, we find for the monodromy matrix
{\bf M}_{1}
,

{\bf M}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0&-1&\cdots&0&0\\
0&1&0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&-1&1&1&\cdots&0&0\\
0&0&0&1&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&&\ddots&&\\
0&0&0&0&\cdots&1&0\\
0&0&0&0&\cdots&0&1\end{pmatrix},

(42)

while the
{\bf M}_{j}
for other values of

are given by permutations. Its action on the couplings is

{\bf M}_{j}:\begin{cases}\tau\to\tau+1,\\
v_{k}\to v_{k}-\delta_{jk},\\
w_{kl}\to w_{kl}+\delta_{kl}\delta_{jl}.\end{cases}

(43)

To visualise the monodromies with large
{\rm Im}(\tau)
for
N_{f}=1
, we refer to Fig.
4
(d). In this regime of the masses, there is one monodromy with periodicity 3 and one with periodicity 1.

Besides the monodromies
{\bf M}_{\infty}
and
{\bf M}_{j}
, there are monodromies
{\bf M}_{m}
and
{\bf M}_{d}
around the points where a monopole and a dyon becomes massless, respectively. By requiring that the electro-magnetic charges of the massless particles are
(n_{m},n_{e})=(1,0)
and
(1,-2)

m_{j}\to m_{j}
, and that the other periods only change by a multiple of the vanishing cycle at the corresponding cusp, together with the requirement that

{\bf M}_{\infty}={\bf M}_{m}{\bf M}_{d}\prod_{j=1}^{N_{f}}{\bf M}_{j}.

(44)

For
N_{f}=1
and

, this gives for
{\bf M}_{m}

{\bf M}_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\\
-1&1&0&-(n+1)/2\\
(n+1)/2&0&1&(n+1)^{2}/4\\
0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(45)

This acts on the couplings as

{\bf M}_{m}:\begin{cases}\tau\to\frac{\tau}{-\tau+1},\\
v\to\frac{v+(n+1)\tau/2}{-\tau+1},\\
w\to w+\frac{(v+(n+1)/2)^{2}}{-\tau+1}.\end{cases}

(46)

The monodromy
{\bf M}_{d}
around the dyon singularity for
N_{f}=1

{\bf M}_{d}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&4&0&-n-1\\
-1&3&0&-(n+1)/2\\
(n+1)/2&-n-1&1&(n+1)^{2}/4\\
0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix},

(47)

This acts on the couplings as

{\bf M}_{d}:\begin{cases}\tau\to\frac{-\tau+4}{-\tau+3},\\
v\to\frac{v+(n+1)\tau-n-1}{-\tau+3},\\
w\to w+\frac{(v+(n+1)/2)^{2}}{-\tau+3}.\end{cases}

(48)

We can note that all the above monodromy matrices leave the symplectic form (

For a small mass

3
The UV theory on a four-manifold

We review various aspects of the formulation of the UV theory on a compact smooth four-manifold.

3.1
Aspects of four-manifolds

We let
X
be a smooth, compact, oriented Riemannian four-manifold, with Euler number
\chi=\chi(X)
and signature
\sigma=\sigma(X)=b_{2}^{+}-b_{2}^{-}
. The
u
-plane integral is non-vanishing only for four-manifolds
X
with
b_{2}^{+}\leq 1
. In this article, we consider manifolds with
b_{2}^{+}=1
. Such four-manifolds admit a linear complex structure
\mathcal{J}
on the tangent space
TX_{p}
at each point
p
of
X
. The complex structure varies smoothly on
X
, such that
TX
is a complex bundle. We introduce furthermore the canonical class
K_{X}=-c_{1}(TX)
of
X
, with
c_{1}(TX)
the first Chern class of
TX
. For a manifold
X
with
(b_{1},b_{2}^{+})=(0,1)
, we have that

K_{X}^{2}=8+\sigma(X).

(51)

The middle cohomology
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
of
X
gives rise to the uni-modular lattice
L
. More precisely, we identify
L
with the natural embedding of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{R}
, which mods out the torsion of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. A characteristic element
K\in L
is an element which satisfies
{\boldsymbol{l}}^{2}+B(K,{\boldsymbol{l}})\in 2\mathbb{Z}
for all
{\boldsymbol{l}}\in L
. The Riemann-Roch theorem demonstrates that the canonical class
K_{X}
of
X
is a characteristic element of
L
. The Wu formula furthermore shows that any characteristic vector
K
of
L
is a lift of
w_{2}(X)
.

The quadratic form
Q
of the lattice
L
for a 4-manifold with
(b_{1},b_{2}^{+})=(0,1)
can be brought to a simple standard form depending on whether
Q
is even or odd
Donaldson90
. This divides such manifolds into two classes, for which the evaluation of their
u
-plane integrals needs to be done separately
Korpas:2019cwg
;
Aspman:2023ate
.
The period point
J\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
is defined as the unique class in the forward light cone of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
that satisfies
J=*J
and
J^{2}=1
.

All four-manifolds without torsion and even intersection form admit a Spin structure.
More generally, for any oriented four-manifold one can define a
\text{Spin}^{c}
-structure. The group
\text{Spin}^{c}(4)
can be defined as pairs of unitary
2\times 2
matrices with coinciding determinant,

\text{Spin}^{c}(4)=\{(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathrm{U}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(2)|\det{u_{1}}=\det u_{2}\}.

(52)

There exists a short exact sequence

1\longrightarrow U(1)\longrightarrow\text{Spin}^{c}(4)\longrightarrow SO(4)\longrightarrow 1.

(53)

A
\text{Spin}^{c}
-structure
\mathfrak{s}
on a four-manifold
X
is then a reduction of the structure group of the tangent bundle on
X
, i.e. SO(4), to the group
\text{Spin}^{c}(4)
. The different
\text{Spin}^{c}
-structures correspond to the inequivalent ways of choosing transition functions of the tangent bundle such that the cocycle condition is satisfied. The
\text{Spin}^{c}
-structure defines two rank two hermitian vector bundles
W^{\pm}
. We let
c(\mathfrak{s})
be the first Chern class of the determinant bundles,
c(\mathfrak{s})\coloneqq c_{1}(\det W^{\pm})\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

If
\mathfrak{s}
is the canonical
\text{Spin}^{c}
structure associated to an almost complex structure on
X
, then
c(\mathfrak{s})^{2}=2\chi+3\sigma
.
More generally,

c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})^{2}\equiv\sigma\mod 8.

(54)

3.2
Topological twisting with background fluxes

We discuss in this section topological twisting of theories with fundamental hypermultiplets including background fluxes. The discussion is parallel to the case of
\mathcal{N}=2^{*}

Manschot:2021qqe
, where the hypermultiplet is in the adjoint representation of the gauge group.

We let
(E\to X,\nabla)
be a principal
\text{SU}(2)/\mathbb{Z}_{2}\cong\text{SO}(3)
-bundle with connection
\nabla
. The second Stiefel-Whitney class
w_{2}(E)\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
measures the obstruction to lift
E
to an SU(2) bundle, which will exist locally but not globally if
w_{2}(E)\neq 0
. We denote a lift of
w_{2}(E)
to the middle cohomology lattice

by
\bar{w}_{2}(E)\in L
, and define the ’t Hooft flux
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\bar{w}_{2}(E)/2\in L/2
. The instanton number of the principal bundle is defined as
k=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{X}p_{1}(E)
and satisfies
k\in-{\boldsymbol{\mu}}^{2}+\mathbb{Z}
, where

is the first Pontryagin class.

To formulate the theories with
N_{f}
fundamental hypermultiplets on a compact four-manifold, we perform a topological twist. Coupling the four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2

\text{SU}(2)
theory to background fields means choosing two sets of data:

•

A principal
\text{SU}(2)_{R}
R-symmetry bundle, with connection
\nabla_{R}
,

•

and a principal bundle
\mathcal{L}
with connection for global symmetries (the flavour symmetries)
Manschot:2021qqe
.

The relevant twist for the
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry algebra in four dimensions is the Donaldson-Witten twist. This twist is the local identification of the SU(2)
+
with the diagonal subgroup of the SU(2)
{}_{+}\times
SU(2)
R
factor of the spin lift of the local spin group
\text{Spin}(4)\cong\text{SU}(2)_{+}\times\text{SU}(2)_{-}

Witten:1988ze
.
Alternatively, one can view the fields as sections of a non-trivial R-symmetry bundle, isomorphic to the spin bundle
S^{+}
. Application of this to the representations of the vector multiplet (
4
) and (
7
) gives:

\displaystyle\text{bosons:}\quad

\displaystyle({\bf 2},{\bf 2})\oplus({\bf 1},{\bf 1})\oplus({\bf 1},{\bf 1}),

(55)

\displaystyle\text{fermions:}\quad

\displaystyle({\bf 2},{\bf 2})\oplus({\bf 3},{\bf 1})\oplus({\bf 1},{\bf 1}).

Thus the bosons remain unchanged, a vector and a complex scalar, while the fermions reorganise to a vector, self-dual two-form and real scalar, which we denote as
\psi
,
\chi
and
\eta
, respectively. We note that none of these fields are spinors, and can thus be considered on a non-spin four-manifold. The original supersymmetry generators also transform in the representations for the fermions above. Thus the theory contains a scalar fermionic supercharge
\mathcal{Q}=\epsilon^{\dot{A}\dot{B}}\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\dot{A}\dot{B}}
, whose cohomology provides the operators in the topological theory
Witten:1988ze
.

For the fields of a hypermultiplet, (
6
) and (
7
), one finds

\displaystyle\text{bosons:}\quad

\displaystyle({\bf 1},{\bf 2})\oplus({\bf 1},{\bf 2}),

(56)

\displaystyle\text{fermions:}\quad

\displaystyle({\bf 2},{\bf 1})\oplus({\bf 1},{\bf 2})\oplus({\bf 2},{\bf 1})\oplus({\bf 1},{\bf 2}).

Thus hypermultiplet bosons become spinors, i.e. sections of the spin bundle
S^{+}
, while the fermions are sections of
S^{+}
and
S^{-}
. Thus the twisted hypermultiplets can in this case only be formulated on four-manifolds which are spin, i.e.
w_{2}(X)=0

Hyun:1995mb
;
Moore:1997pc
.

However, if the hypermultiplets are charged under a gauge field or flux, the product of these bundles with
S^{\pm}
may be a Spin
c
bundle,
W^{+}
or
W^{-}

Hyun:1995mb
;
Laba05
;
Manschot:2021qqe
. The latter are defined for arbitrary four-manifolds. For example, an almost complex structure on
X
determines two canonical Spin
c
bundles
W^{\pm}\simeq S^{\pm}\otimes K^{-1/2}_{X}
with
K_{X}
the canonical class determined by the almost complex structure. Since the hypermultiplets are in the fundamental, two-dimensional representation of SU(2), the topologically twisted hypermultiplets are well-defined on a non-spin four-manifold if

.

Let us state this also in terms of the gauge bundle
E
. To this end, we label the two components of the fundamental, two-dimensional representation of SU(2) by
\pm
. The two components are sections of a line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{E}^{\pm 1/2}
with
c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{E})=\bar{w}_{2}(E)
. Of course, the square root
\mathcal{L}_{E}^{1/2}
only exists if
w_{2}(E)\in 2L
. On the other hand, the physical requirement is that

is well defined, or
\bar{w}_{2}(X)+\bar{w}_{2}(E)\in 2L
. Therefore, the obstructions can cancel each other for a suitable choice of

. Thus the topological twisted theory is not well-defined for an arbitrary choice of ’t Hooft flux
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\bar{w}_{2}(E)
; but rather
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
has to satisfy
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\frac{1}{2}\bar{w}_{2}(X)\mod L

, or

\bar{w}_{2}(X)=\bar{w}_{2}(E)\mod 2L.

(57)

To consider more general ’t Hooft fluxes
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
or equivalently

, we can couple the

’th hypermultiplet to a background flux or line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{j}
, with
\mathcal{L}_{j}
possibly different
for each

. We let
\mathcal{E}_{j}=\mathcal{L}_{E}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{j}

Then the requirement that

(58)

which can be satisfied for any

for a suitable choice of

-plane integral for arbitrary

(59)

for each

. This is consistent with (

) for

.

The Chern classes

can also be seen as the splitting classes of the
\text{Spin}(2N_{f})

(60)

The scalar generators of the equivariant cohomology of

are the masses

, which generate the
N_{f}
-dimensional Cartan subalgebra of
\text{Spin}(2N_{f})
. The gauge bundle

is also
\text{Spin}(2N_{f})
equivariant. For generic masses, the flavour group is U(1)
{}^{N_{f}}
, and is enhanced for special loci of the masses, for example to
\text{U}(N_{f})
for equal masses

.

The
\mathcal{Q}
-fixed equations are the non-Abelian monopole equations with
N_{f}
matter fields in the fundamental representation. For generic gauge group

and with representation

\begin{split}&\left(F^{a}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\right)^{+}+\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{f}}\bar{M}^{j}_{(\dot{\alpha}}T^{a}M^{j}_{\dot{\beta})}=0,\\
&\not{D}M^{j}=\sum_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu}\,D_{\mu}M^{j}=0,\end{split}

(61)

where

is a generator of the Lie algebra in the representation
R
. Including the sum over matrix elements, we have

M^{j}_{(\alpha}T^{a}M^{j}_{\beta)}=\sum_{k,l}(M^{j})^{k}_{(\alpha}(T^{a})^{kl}(M^{j})^{l}_{\beta)}.

(62)

We denote the moduli space of solutions to (
61

\mathcal{M}^{Q,N_{f}}_{k,\mathcal{L}_{j}}
, and leave the dependence on the ’t Hooft flux
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
and the metric

implicit. For
N_{f}=4
on

, such moduli spaces are studied in
Gorodentsev:1996cu
.

The moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}^{Q,N_{f}}_{k,\mathcal{L}_{j}}
is non-compact for vanishing masses
bryan1996
;
Moore:1997dj
;
Dedushenko:2017tdw
. This is improved upon turning on masses and localizing with respect to the
\text{U}(1)^{N_{f}}
flavour symmetry,
M^{j}_{\alpha}\to e^{i\,\varphi_{j}}M^{j}_{\alpha}
, which leave invariant the
\mathcal{Q}
-fixed equations (
61

There are two components:

•

the instanton component, with
F^{+}=0
and
M^{j}=0
,
j=1,\dots,N_{f}
. The moduli space for this component is denoted
\mathcal{M}^{\rm i}_{k}
. Since the hypermultiplet fields vanish, this component is associated to the Coulomb branch.

•

the abelian or monopole component, for which a U
(1)
subgroup of the flavour group acts as pure gauge. Here the connection is reducible, and a U(1) subgroup of the SU(2) gauge group is preserved. For generic masses, there are
N_{f}
such components, where
M^{\ell}
is upper or lower triangular for some
\ell
, and
M^{j}=0
for all

. The moduli space of this component is denoted
\mathcal{M}^{{\rm a},j}_{k}
,

. Since some of the hypermultiplet fields are non-vanishing, this component is associated to the Higgs branch
Moore:1997dj
;
Marino:1998uy
.

The instanton component

is non-compact due to point-like instantons. This can be cured using the Uhlenbeck compactification or algebraic-geometric compactifications. We assume that the physical path integral chooses a specific compactification, whose details are however not manifest at the level of the low energy effective field theory other than that the compactification must be in agreement with the correlation functions.

The topological twist for
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric QCD can be further made dependent on a cocycle
\zeta_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{\text{gauge}}
representing the ’t Hooft flux, and
\zeta_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{s}

is equivalent to the cocycle
\zeta_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{\text{gauge}}\zeta_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{s}
being trivialisable. We leave it for future work to explore whether the invariants depend on the choice of trivialisation.

3.3
Correlation functions and moduli spaces

The
\mathcal{Q}
-fixed equations (
61
) include a Dirac equation for each hypermultiplet
j=1,\dots,N_{f}
in the fundamental representation. The corresponding index bundle
W_{k}^{j}
defines an element of the
K
-group of
\mathcal{M}^{\rm i}_{k}
.
Its virtual rank
\text{rk}(W^{j}_{k})
is the formal difference of two infinite dimensions. It is given by an index theorem and reads

\text{rk}(W^{j}_{k})=-k+\frac{1}{4}(c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{j})^{2}-\sigma)\in\mathbb{Z},

(63)

where
c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{j})
is the first Chern class of the bundle
\mathcal{L}_{j}
.
Note that the rhs is
not
an integer for an arbitrary
c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{j})\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. To verify that the rhs is integral for the
c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{j})
’s satisfying (
59
), we rewrite
\text{rk}(W^{j}_{k})
as

\text{rk}(W^{j}_{k})=-(k+{\boldsymbol{\mu}}^{2})-c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{j})\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mu}}+\frac{1}{4}\left((c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{j})+2{\boldsymbol{\mu}})^{2}-\sigma\right).

(64)

Then the first term on the rhs is an integer since
k\in-\frac{1}{4}w_{2}(E)^{2}+\mathbb{Z}
for an
\text{SO}(3)
bundle. The second term is an integer because
c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{j})\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=(\bar{w}_{2}(X)-2{\boldsymbol{\mu}})\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\mod\mathbb{Z}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and the third term is an integer using (
54
) and the fact that
c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{j})+2{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
equals the characteristic class of a
\text{Spin}^{c}
-structure
\mathfrak{s}_{j}
by (

),

c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{j})+2{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=c(\mathfrak{s}_{j}),

(65)

for each

.

The mass
m_{j}
is the equivariant parameter of the U(1) flavour symmetry associated to the
j
’th hypermultiplet.
The equivariant Chern class of
W^{j}_{k}
reads in terms of the splitting class
x_{l}
,

c(W_{k}^{j})=\prod_{l=0}^{-\text{rk}(W^{j}_{k})}(x_{l}+m_{j})=m_{j}^{-\text{rk}(W^{j}_{k})}\sum_{l}\frac{c_{l}(W_{k}^{j})}{m_{j}^{l}}.

(66)

We abbreviate
c_{l}(W_{k}^{j})
to
c_{l,j}
, and let
c(W_{k})=\prod_{j=1}^{N_{f}}c(W_{k}^{j})
.

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{k,{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\mathcal{L}_{j}}^{Q}
for

hypermultiplets corresponds to the vanishing locus of the obstructions for the existence of
N_{f}
zero modes of the Dirac operator. As a result, the virtual complex dimension of the moduli space

is that of the instanton moduli space plus the sum of (typically negative) ranks of the index bundles

\text{vdim}(\mathcal{M}^{Q,N_{f}}_{k,\mathcal{L}_{j}})=\text{vdim}(\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{k})_{N_{f}=0}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{f}}\text{rk}(W_{k}^{j})

;
bryan1996
;
Feehan:2001jc
;
Manschot:2021qqe
.
This gives

\text{vdim}(\mathcal{M}_{k,\mathcal{L}_{j}}^{Q,N_{f}})=(4-N_{f})k+\frac{1}{4}\left(-3\chi-(3+N_{f})\sigma+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{f}}c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{j})^{2}\right).

(67)

It is argued in

that the inclusion of massive matter amounts to inserting an integral of the equivariant Euler class of the Dirac index bundle over the moduli space. Therefore, the correlation functions are the generating functions for the intersection numbers of the standard Donaldson observables and the Poincaré duals to the Chern classes of the various vector bundles.

The correlation functions on
X
in the theory with
N_{f}
massive fundamental hypermultiplets are conjectured to be

\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}\dots\mathcal{O}_{p}\rangle=\sum_{k}\Lambda_{N_{f}}^{{\rm vdim}(\mathcal{M}^{Q,N_{f}}_{k,\mathcal{L}_{j}})}\,\int_{\mathcal{M}_{k,\mathcal{L}_{j}}^{Q,N_{f}}}c(W_{k})\,\omega_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\omega_{p},

(68)

where
\omega_{i}=\mu(\mathcal{O}_{i})
are the Donaldson classes associated to the physical observable
\mathcal{O}_{i}
, and

is the Euler class of the matter bundle

;
Atiyah:1990tm
;
losev1998
;
Cordes:1994fc
. Localising to the fixed point locus in

with respect to

gives

\begin{split}&\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}\dots\mathcal{O}_{p}\rangle=\sum_{k}\Lambda_{N_{f}}^{{\rm vdim}(\mathcal{M}^{Q,N_{f}}_{k,\mathcal{L}_{j}})}\\
&\qquad\qquad\times\int_{\mathcal{M}^{\rm i}_{k}\cup\mathcal{M}^{\rm a}_{k}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{f}}m_{j}^{-\text{rk}(W^{j}_{k})}\sum_{l}\frac{c_{l,j}}{m_{j}^{l}}\right)\,\omega_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\omega_{p}.\end{split}

(69)

where the integral is over the union

of the instanton component

(
LoNeSha

and the monopole component

;

. The equation together with the dimension of the moduli spaces (

and

In the decoupling limit

is from

,

. The powers of

and

work out such that the correlation functions reduce to those of the theory with

\left(\frac{\Lambda_{N_{f}}}{\Lambda_{N_{f}-1}}\right)^{-\alpha}\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}\dots\mathcal{O}_{p}\rangle_{N_{f}}\to\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}\dots\mathcal{O}_{p}\rangle_{N_{f}-1},

(70)

We deduce from (

\begin{split}\alpha&={\rm vdim}(\mathcal{M}^{Q,N_{f}}_{k})+(4-N_{f})\,\text{rk}(W_{k}^{N_{f}})\\

(71)

The overall factor can be accounted for by an overall renormalization in the decoupling limit.

The correlation function has a smooth massless limit

, for which only terms with the top Chern classes contribute. These are given by

\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}\dots\mathcal{O}_{p}\rangle=\sum_{k}\prod_{j=1}^{N_{f}}\Lambda_{N_{f}}^{{\rm vdim}(\mathcal{M}^{Q,N_{f}}_{k,\mathcal{L}_{j}})}\int_{\mathcal{M}^{\rm i}_{k}\cup\mathcal{M}^{\rm a}_{k}}c_{l_{j},j}\,\omega_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\omega_{p}

with

for each

.

By comparing the form degrees (or ghost numbers) of operators with the virtual dimension of the moduli space, one derives selection rules for correlation functions of point and surface operators.
Since

has ghost number 4, the fugacity

naturally has ghost number
-4
. Similarly, we associate ghost number

to the surface

, with the selection rule

-4s-2t+2\sum_{j=1}^{N_{f}}r_{j}=-\text{vdim}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{M}_{k,\mathcal{L}_{j}}^{Q,N_{f}}).

(73)

4.3
Observables and contact terms

The observables in the topologically twisted theories are the point observable or 0-observable

To introduce the surface observable, let

In the effective infrared theory, this operator becomes,

\tilde{I}({\boldsymbol{x}})=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}\pi}\int_{\boldsymbol{x}}\frac{1}{32}\frac{d^{2}u}{da^{2}}\,\psi\wedge\psi-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\frac{du}{da}(F_{-}+D).

Generating functions of correlation functions are obtained by inserting

e^{p\,u/\Lambda_{N_{f}}^{2}+\tilde{I}({\boldsymbol{x}})/\Lambda_{N_{f}}}

(91)

),

and to analytically continue

(

) to the complex number by setting

;

;

Using

that the singularities of

are in one-to-one correspondence with the branch points. In fact, both

are realised as branch points in

around the branch points, and show that they do not give an extra contribution. Furthermore, the solutions to

are shown to be the Argyres-Douglas (AD) points. We exclude them from the integration region, and study their contribution also in Section

. Finally, we know that
\eta^{24}\propto\left(\frac{da}{du}\right)^{12}\Delta_{N_{f}}

the Dedekind eta-function as defined in (

and

does not have poles, we find that

never vanishes. This agrees with the fact that

is the period of a holomorphic differential and therefore is never zero.

We conclude that the functional determinant is singular in

precisely at the branch points and AD points, however with the proper exclusion of those as done in the following Section, it is non-singular and the change of variables is well-defined. This furthermore conveniently solves the problem that there is no natural integration region in

Monodromy around infinity

Let us determine how the

\frac{da}{d\tau}\to-\frac{da}{d\tau}.

Combining (

), (

), (

From (

6
Integration over fundamental domains

As discussed in Sections
2
and
5
,
u
-plane integrals for massive
\mathcal{N}=2
theories with fundamental hypermultiplets include new aspects. This section discusses how to evaluate such integrals (
96
). More concretely, we aim to define and evaluate integrals of the form

\mathcal{I}_{f}=\int_{\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})}d\tau\wedge d\bar{\tau}\,y^{-s}\,f(\tau,\bar{\tau}),

(126)

with
s\leq 1
. The domain
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
is the fundamental domain for the effective coupling
constant as discussed in Section
2.4
, and
f
a
non-holomorphic function of weight
(2-s,2-s)
arising from the topologically twisted
Yang-Mills theory. For
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
a fundamental domain of a congruence subgroup, such integrals (
126
) have been studied in the context of theta lifts of weakly holomorphic modular forms and harmonic Maass forms
Borcherds:1996uda
;
bringmann2017regularized
;
bruinier2004two
as well as one-loop amplitudes in string theory
Lerche:1988np
;
Dixon:1990pc
;
Harvey:1995fq
.

We assume that the integrand
y^{-s}\,f(\tau,\bar{\tau})
can be expressed as

\partial_{\bar{\tau}}\widehat{h}(\tau,\bar{\tau})=y^{-s}\,f(\tau,\bar{\tau}),

(127)

for a suitable function
\widehat{h}(\tau,\bar{\tau})
using mock modular forms. This was indeed the case in
Korpas:2017qdo
;
Korpas:2019ava
;
Manschot:2021qqe
, and will be demonstrated for massive
\mathcal{N}=2
theories with fundamental hypermultiplets.
The integral
\mathcal{I}_{f}
then reads

\mathcal{I}_{f}=-\int_{\partial\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})}d\tau\,\widehat{h}(\tau,\bar{\tau}),

(128)

with
\partial\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
the boundary of
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
. We will carry this out evaluation in Part II
Aspman:2023ate
.

There are a number of aspects to be addressed in order to evaluate
integrals over
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
:

1.

Identifications of boundary components of
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
due to monodromies on the
u
-plane.

2.

Contributions from the cusps, that is
\tau\to i\infty
or

\tau\to\gamma(i\infty)\in\mathbb{Q}
for an element
\gamma\in\text{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.

3.

Contributions from a singular point in the interior of
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
.

4.

Contributions from an elliptic point
p\in\mathbb{H}
of

\text{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.

5.

Branch points and branch cuts.

We will discuss these aspects 1.–5. in the following.

1.
Identifications

The modular transformation induced by monodromies identify components of the boundary of the fundamental
domain
\partial\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
pairwise. Their contributions to the integral
(
128
) vanish, which is, for example, familiar from deriving
valence formulas for modular forms
(
Bruinier08
, Fig. 2)
. See Fig.
4
for an example.

2.
Cusps

Contributions near the cusps require a regularisation

Moore:1997pc
;
Korpas:2019ava
. Such regularisations have been
developed in the context of string amplitudes
Lerche:1988np
;
Dixon:1990pc
;
Harvey:1995fq
and analytic number
theory
Petersson1950
;
Bruinier08
;
1603.03056

Let us first consider the cusp

\tau\to i\infty
. To regularise the divergence, one introduces
a cut-off
\text{Im}\,\tau=Y\gg 1
, and takes the limit
Y\to\infty
after evaluation.
We require that
f
near
i\infty
has a Fourier expansion of the
form
5
5

5

Also if
f
does not satisfy this
requirement, the integral can be regularised as explained in

;
1603.03056

regularisation for the correlators in this paper.

f(\tau,\bar{\tau})=\sum_{m\gg-\infty,n\geq 0}c(m,n)\,q^{m}\,\bar{q}^{n}.

(129)

Then the function
\widehat{h}
has the form,

\widehat{h}(\tau,\bar{\tau})=h(\tau)+2^{s}\int^{i\infty}_{-\bar{\tau}}\frac{f(\tau,-v)}{(-i(v+\tau))^{s}}dv,

(130)

where
h(\tau)
is a weakly holomorphic
q

h(\tau)=\sum_{m\gg-\infty}d(m)\,q^{m}.

(131)

The cusp
\tau\to i\infty
then contributes

\left[\mathcal{I}_{f}\right]_{\infty}=w_{\infty}\,d(0),

(132)

with

(

\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
at

The other cusps can be treated in a similar fashion using modular
transformations. We label the

\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
by

\gamma_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{j}&b_{j}\\

(133)

We let
\tau_{j}=\gamma_{j}\tau

(134)

As a result, the cusp

\left[\mathcal{I}_{f}\right]_{j}=w_{j}\,d_{j}(0).

(135)

3.
Singular points in the interior of
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})

The integrand can be singular at a point
\tau_{\rm s}
in the interior of

\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{m}})
. Such singularities appear typically for deformations of
superconformal theories, such as the
\mathcal{N}=2^{*}
theory and the

N_{f}=4
theory, where the UV
coupling
\tau_{\text{\tiny{UV}}}
gives rise to such a singularity

Manschot:2021qqe
;
Aspman:2021evt
. See Fig.
8
for an example. We
require that the expansion of
f
near such a singularity reads,

f(\tau,\bar{\tau})=\sum_{m\gg-\infty,n\geq 0}c_{s}(m,n)\,(\tau-\tau_{\rm s})^{m}\,(\bar{\tau}-\bar{\tau}_{\rm s})^{n}.

(136)

Then, the anti-derivative
\widehat{h}(\tau,\bar{\tau})
has similar
expansion,

\widehat{h}(\tau,\bar{\tau})=\sum_{m\gg-\infty,n\geq 0}d_{s}(m,n)\,(\tau-\tau_{\rm s})^{m}\,(\bar{\tau}-\bar{\tau}_{\rm s})^{n}.

(137)

The contour integral for a small contour around
\tau_{\rm s}

C_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\rm s})=\left\{\tau=\tau_{\rm s}+\varepsilon\,e^{i\varphi},\varphi\in[0,2\pi)\right\},

(138)

is bounded for such a function. Moreover, in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, the contour integral is finite. We define the “residue” of a non-holomorphic function

\begin{split}\underset{\tau=\tau_{\rm s}}{\text{nRes}}\left[g(\tau,\bar{\tau})\right]&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\oint_{C_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\rm s})}g(\tau,\bar{\tau})\,d\tau.\end{split}

For the expansion (
136

\left[\mathcal{I}_{f}\right]_{s}=2\pi i\,\underset{\tau=\tau_{\rm s}}{\text{nRes}}\left[\widehat{h}(\tau,\bar{\tau})\right]=d_{s}(-1,0),

with
d_{s}(-1,0)
the coefficient in the expansion (
137

4.
Elliptic points

For
\mathcal{N}=2

cut-off

\widehat{h}
has
the following expansion near an elliptic point

\widehat{h}(\tau,\bar{\tau})=\sum_{m\gg-\infty,n\geq 0}d_{\rm e}(m,n)\,(\tau-\tau_{\rm e})^{m}\,(\bar{\tau}-\bar{\tau}_{\rm e})^{n}.

(141)

As a result, the boundary arc around
\tau_{\rm AD}
in

is a

These neighbourhoods have an angle
\frac{2\pi}{k_{e}}

k_{e}=2
for

\tau_{e}=i

for
\tau_{e}=\alpha

Diamond

\mathcal{F}

\mathcal{N}=2

(142)

5.
Branch points and cuts

Branch points and cuts are a new aspect compared to previous
analyses (see for instance Fig.
4
and
5
). We will demonstrate that their contribution vanishes for the
integrands of interest.

We assume that the integrand
f
satisfies

\widehat{h}(\tau,\bar{\tau})=(\tau-\tau_{{\rm bp}})^{n}\,g(\tau,\bar{\tau}),

(143)

with
n\in\mathbb{Z}/2
and

being a real
analytic function near
\tau_{\rm bp}

To treat this type of singularity, we remove a
\delta

and analyse the
\delta\to 0
limit.
Let
C_{\delta}
be the contour

C_{\delta}=\{\tau_{\rm bp}+\delta\,e^{i\theta}\,|\,\theta\in(0,2\pi)\}

(144)

around
\tau_{\rm bp}
with radius
\delta>0
.
Therefore, on the contour

is bounded by

|\widehat{h}|\leq\delta^{n}\,K

(145)

for some

. The integral around the branch point therefore vanishes in the limit,

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}^{\rm bp}_{f}&=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\,\int_{C_{\delta}}\widehat{h}\,|d\tau|\leq\lim_{\delta\to 0}\,\int_{0}^{2\pi}\delta^{n}\,K\,\delta\,d\theta\\
&=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\,2\pi K\delta^{n+1}=0.\end{split}

(146)

The branch points necessarily give rise to branch cuts. For the
purpose of integration, we remove a neighbourhood with distance
r

from the cut, and take the limit
r\to 0
after determining the
integral. Since the value of the integrand is finite near the branch
cut, the contribution to the integral vanishes.

A.1
Theta functions and Eisenstein series

We make use of modular forms for the congruence subgroups
\Gamma_{0}(n)
and
\Gamma^{0}(n)
of
\text{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
. These subgroups are defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(n)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\big|\,c\equiv 0\;\mod n\right\},

(148)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{0}(n)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\big|\,b\equiv 0\;\mod n\right\},

and are related by conjugation with the matrix
\text{diag}(n,1)
. We furthermore define the
principal congruence subgroup

\Gamma(n)
as the subgroup of
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\ni A
with
A\equiv\mathbbm{1}\mod n
. A subgroup
\Gamma
of
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
is called a congruence subgroup if it contains
\Gamma(n)
for some

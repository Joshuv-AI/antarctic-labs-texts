Abstract
. The manuscript is concerned with uniqueness and stability for inverse source problem of determining spatially varying factor f ⁡ ( x ) f(x) of a source term given by R ⁡ ( t ) ​ f ​ ( x ) R(t)f(x) with suitable given R ⁡ ( t ) R(t) in the right hand side of the Schrödinger equation with time independent coefficients. In order to establish these results we provide a simple proof of a logarithmic conditional stability of the Cauchy problem for the Schrödinger equation with time-independent coefficients and the zero Dirichlet boundary conditions on the whole boundary and a proof of uniqueness of solution to the Cauchy problem for the Schrödinger equation with data on an arbitrary small part of a lateral boundary. We do not assume any geometrical constraints on subboundary and a time interval is arbitrary. The key is an integral transform, with a kernel solving a null controllability problem for the 1-D Schrödinger, which changes a solution of the Schrödinger equation to a solution of an elliptic equation.



1.
Introduction and main results

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
C^{2}
-boundary.
We set
x=(x_{1},...,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\partial_{t}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}
,

\partial_{x_{j}}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}
for
1\leq j\leq n
, and

\nabla=(\partial_{x_{1}},...,\partial_{x_{n}})
.
Moreover,
\nu=\nu(x)
denotes the unit outward normal vector to
\partial\Omega

at
x
and
\partial_{\nu}u=\nabla u\cdot\nu
on
\partial\Omega
.
We set
Q:=(0,T)\times\Omega
and
i:=\sqrt{-1}
,
D=(D_{0},D^{\prime}),D^{\prime}=(D_{1},\dots,D_{n}),D_{0}=\frac{1}{i}\partial_{t},D_{j}=\frac{1}{i}\partial_{x_{j}},j\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

We consider the Schrödinger equation:

(1.1)

P(x,D)u:=i\partial_{t}u-\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{j}}(a_{jk}(x)\partial_{x_{k}}u(t,x))+\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}(x)\partial_{x_{j}}u(t,x)+c(x)u(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in Q,

Throughout this article, we make the following assumptions:
the coefficients
a_{jk}

are real-valued functions for all
1\leq j,k\leq n
and

(1.2)

a_{jk}=a_{kj}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}),\quad b_{j}\in W^{1}_{\infty}(\Omega),\quad c\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for all $j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}$}

and there exists a constant
\beta>0
such that

(1.3)

\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}a_{jk}(x)\xi_{j}\xi_{k}\geq\beta\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}^{2}\quad\mbox{for all $x\in\Omega$, $\xi_{1},...,\xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}$}.

We set

A(x,D^{\prime})v:=-\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{j}}(a_{jk}(x)\partial_{x_{k}}v(x))+\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}(x)\partial_{x_{j}}v(x)+c(x)v(x)

for
x\in\Omega
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
be a suboundary and let
T>0
.

We define

H^{1,2}(Q):=\{u\in L^{2}(Q);\,\partial_{t}u,\,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u\in L^{2}(Q)\,\,\mbox{if $|\alpha|\leq 2$}\},

where
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n})\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}
,

|\alpha|:=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
and

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}:=\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{x_{n}}^{\alpha_{n}}
.

In this manuscript we concern with the following inverse problem:

Let
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfy

(1.4)

P(x,D)u=i\partial_{t}u+A(x,D^{\prime})u=R(t)f(x)\quad\mbox{in}\quad Q,\quad u(0,\cdot)=0.

Here
R
is a given real-valued function. Determine the source term
f
based on the following data :
\partial_{\nu}u|_{\Gamma}.

We have

Theorem 1
.

Let pair
(u,f)
satisfies (
1.4
) and
R\in C^{1}[0,T].

(i)
We assume that
u,\partial_{t}u\in H^{1,2}(Q)
and

(1.5)

R(0)\neq 0.

If
u=\partial_{\nu}u=0
on
(0,T)\times\Gamma
with
arbitrary fixed subdomain
\Gamma
of
\partial\Omega
, then

f=0
in
\Omega
and
u=0
in
Q
.

(ii) We assume that
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)),f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and

(1.6)

R
is not identically zero on
[0,T]
.

Let
u=0
on
(0,T)\times\partial\Omega
and

\partial_{\nu}u=0
on
(0,T)\times\Gamma
with
arbitrary fixed subdomain
\Gamma
of
\partial\Omega
, then

f=0
in
\Omega
and
u=0
in
Q
.

Remark.

A function
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
is a
solution (
1.4
) such that
u|_{(0,T)\times\partial\Omega}=\partial_{\nu}u|_{(0,T)\times\Gamma}=0

if

(u,P^{*}(x,D)w)_{L^{2}(Q)}=(Rf,w)_{L^{2}(Q)}\quad\forall w\in H^{1,2}(Q),w=0\quad\mbox{on}\,\,(0,T)\times\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma,\,w(T,\cdot)=0.

We do not know whether
f=0
in
\Omega
and
u=0
in
Q
follow from

u=\partial_{\nu}u=0
on
(0,T)\times\Gamma
under the weaker
assumption (
1.6
).
Moreover, in terms of Theorem
1
, we can prove

Theorem 2
.

Let pair
(u,f)\in H^{1,2}(Q)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfies (
1.4
),
R\in C^{2}[0,T]
. Moreover
\partial^{2}_{t}u,\partial_{t}u\in H^{1,2}(Q)
and
u|_{(0,T)\times\partial\Omega}=0.

We assume (
1.5
) and

\|\partial_{t}u\|_{H^{1,2}(Q)}+\|u\|_{H^{1,2}(Q)}\leq M,

where
M>0
is an arbitrarily chosen constant.
Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(1.7)

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C}{|\log\|\partial_{\nu}u\|_{H^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}|}.

The inverse problem of recovery of a source term in the right hand side of the Schrödinger equation was studied in
Baudoin and Mercado
[
1
]
, Baudouin and Puel
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
Mercado, Osses and Rosier
[
10
]
, Yuan and Yamamoto
[

]
.

The authors, in the above mentioned papers, studied right hand side of more general form, namely
R(t,x)f(x)
and proved uniqueness of determination of the right hand side and Lipschitz stability of the determination. The machinery of these papers is based on the Carleman estimates with boundary for the Schrödinger equation.
Such an approach is based on pioneering paper of A. Bukhgeim and M. Klibanov
[
6
]
. Unfortunately in the case of Schrödinger equation a Carleman estimate with boundary require an existence of an appropriate pseudoconvex function. This condition is restrictive. For example, in case of the principal part of
A
be the Laplace operator, it require for observed part of the boundary
\Gamma
to be sufficiently large. In the case of the general elliptic operator
A
existence of pseudoconvex function implies the non-trapping condition for the Hamiltonian flow with the Hamiltonian
H(x,\xi)=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}a_{jk}(x)\xi_{j}\xi_{k}.

Proof of the theorem
1
and theorem
2
are based on the following uniqueness and conditional stability results
for the Schrödinger equation:

Assuming the zero Dirichlet boundary condition on
the whole lateral boundary
(0,T)\times\partial\Omega
, we can prove
a conditional stability estimate of logarithmic rate.

Theorem 3
.

Let a suboundary
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
and a constant
M>0

be arbitrarily chosen.
Let
u,\partial_{t}u\in H^{1,2}(Q)
satisfy

(1.8)

P(x,D)u:=i\partial_{t}u+A(x,D^{\prime})u=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad Q

and

(1.9)

u=0\quad\mbox{on $(0,T)\times\partial\Omega$}.

If

(1.10)

\|u\|_{H^{1,2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{H^{1,2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\leq M,

then there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
u
such that

(1.11)

\|u\|_{C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq\frac{C}{|\log\|\partial_{\nu}u\|_{H^{1}((0,T)\times\Gamma)}|}.

We emphasize that in addition to (
1.2
) and (
1.3
),
we do not assume any extra conditions for the principal
coefficients
a_{jk}
, no geometrical constraints on
\Gamma
and no
limitation of length of time interval of observation
T.

We remark that if
a_{jk}
satisfies some conditions called the
pseudo-convexity (e.g.,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
Yuan and Yamamoto
[
17
]
) and
\Gamma
is some large portion of

\partial\Omega
, then one can prove the Lipschitz stability rather than
the logarithmic stability. In a particular case where

a_{jj}=1
and
a_{jk}=0
for
j\neq k
and
j,k=1,...,n
,
if
\Gamma\supset\{x\in\partial\Omega;\,(x-x_{0})\cdot\nu(x)\geq 0\}

with some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(e.g.,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
), then

\|u(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\partial_{\nu}u\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Gamma)}.

Here we emphasize that this is unconditional stability without
any a priori condition such as (
1.10
).

The conclusion holds under the a priori boundedness condition (
1.10
) and so
is conditional stability.

Finally we formulate the uniqueness result for the Cauchy problem for the Schrödinger equation with solution from

C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)).

Theorem 4
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
be an arbitrarily chosen subboundary
and
T>0
be arbitrary.
We assume that
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
satisfies (
1.1
).
Then
u=\partial_{\nu}u=0
on

(0,T)\times\Gamma
yields
u=0
in
(0,T)\times\Omega
.

Remark.

Function
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
is solution (
1.1
) such that
u=\partial_{\nu}u=0
on

(0,T)\times\Gamma
if

(u,P^{*}(x,D)w)_{L^{2}(Q)}=0\quad\forall w\in H^{1,2}(Q),

w=\partial_{\nu}w=0\quad\mbox{on}\,\,(0,T)\times\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma,\,w(T,\cdot)=w(0,\cdot)=0.

The unique continuation is a fundamental property of partial differential
equations. In order to conclude that
u=0
in
Q
, the conditions on

\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
and
T>0
depend on the types of partial
differential equations under consideration.
For the parabolic equation
\partial_{t}u+Au=0
, for any subboundary
\Gamma
and
any
T>0
, without any assumptions on
a_{jk}
except for (
1.2
), (
1.3
) we can
prove the unique continuation (e.g., Mizohata
[
11
]
, Saut and
Scheurer
[
14
]
). Here we do not intend to create any comprehensive
references. Moreover, the same sharp unique continuation holds also in the
case where the coefficients depend on
t
and satisfy (
1.2
) and (
1.3
).
Here and henceforth
we do not exploit the minimum regularity of the coefficients.

For the hyperbolic equation
\partial_{t}^{2}u+Au=0
, in the case where

a_{jk}
do not depend on
t
or analytic in
t
, a sharp unique continuation
of global type similar to the parabolic equation holds provided that

T>0
is sufficiently large (e.g., Robbiano
[
13
]
, Tataru
[
15
]
).

Compared with the parabolic and hyperbolic equations, it seems that
the researches on the unique continuation for Schrödinger equations,
are not completed. Although we may be suggested to be able to prove
the sharp unique continuation similar to the parabolic equation by
comprehensive researches on Carleman estimates, we can not find
a short proof in the existing publications, and one of main purposes of this
article is to provide such a self-contained and simple proof.

The logarithmic conditional stability results for the unique continuation of the hyperbolic equation were established in
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1
, statement I

We define the operator by

(2.1)

(Kv)(t):=R(0)v(t)+\int^{t}_{0}R^{\prime}(t-s)v(s)ds,\quad 0<t<T.

Let
u
satisfy (
1.4
) and
u(0,\cdot)=0
in
\Omega
and

u=|\nabla u|=0
on
(0,T)\times\Gamma
.
Consider the equation with respect to
z(t,x)
:

(2.2)

\partial_{t}u(t,x)=(Kz)(t,x),\quad 0<t<T,\,x\in\Omega.

Since
R(0)\neq 0
, the operator
K
is the Volterra operator of the second
kind, and we see that
K^{-1}:H^{1}(0,T)\longrightarrow H^{1}(0,T)
exists and
is bounded. Therefore,
z(\cdot,x)\in H^{1}(0,T)
is well defined for each

x\in\Omega
, and

(2.3)

\partial_{t}u(t,x)=R(0)z(t,x)+\int^{t}_{0}R^{\prime}(t-s)z(s,x)ds,\quad 0<t<T,\,x\in\Omega

by
\partial_{t}u\in H^{1,2}(Q)
.

Since
u(0,\cdot)=0
in
\Omega
and

R(0)z(t,x)+\int^{t}_{0}R^{\prime}(t-s)z(s,x)ds=\partial_{t}\left(\int^{t}_{0}R(t-s)z(s,x)ds\right),

we obtain

u(t,x)=\int^{t}_{0}R(t-s)z(s,x)ds,\quad 0<t<T,\,x\in\Omega,

that is,

(2.4)

u(t,x)=\int^{t}_{0}R(s)z(t-s,x)ds,\quad 0<t<T,\,x\in\Omega.

We will prove that
z\in H^{1,2}(Q)
satisfies

(2.5)

i\partial_{t}z(t,x)+A(x,D^{\prime})z(t,x)=0\quad\mbox{in $(0,T)\times\Omega$},

where
t_{*}\in(0,T)
is some constant, and

(2.6)

z=|\nabla z|=0\quad\mbox{on $(0,T)\times\Gamma$}.

We can readily verify (
2.6
), because
\partial_{t}u=|\nabla\partial_{t}u|=0
on
(0,T)\times\Gamma
implies

Kz=|K(\nabla z)|=0
on
(0,T)\times\Gamma
, so that
the injectivity of
K
directly yields (
2.6
).

Using
\partial_{t}u\in H^{1,2}(Q)\subset C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
by (
2.3
), we have

\partial_{t}u(0,x)=R(0)z(0,x),\quad x\in\Omega.

On the other hand, substituting
t=0
in (
1.4
) we obtain

\partial_{t}u(0,x)=-iR(0)f(x),\quad x\in\Omega.

Hence
R(0)z(0,x)=-iR(0)f(x)
for
x\in\Omega
. By
R(0)\neq 0
, we
reach

(2.7)

z(0,x)=-if(x),\quad x\in\Omega.

Now we will prove (
2.5
). In terms of (
2.4
) and (
2.7
), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)=R(t)z(0,x)+\int^{t}_{0}R(s)\partial_{t}z(t-s,x)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-iR(t)f(x)+\int^{t}_{0}R(s)\partial_{t}z(t-s,x)ds

and

A(x,D^{\prime})u(t,x)=\int^{t}_{0}R(s)A(x,D^{\prime})z(t-s,x)ds,\quad 0<t<T,\,x\in\Omega.

Consequently (
1.4
) implies

\displaystyle-iR(t)f(x)=\partial_{t}u-iA(x,D^{\prime})u(t,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-iR(t)f(x)+\int^{t}_{0}R(s)(\partial_{t}z-iA(x,D^{\prime})z)(t-s,x)ds,

that is,

\int^{t}_{0}R(s)(\partial_{t}z-iA(x,D^{\prime})z)(t-s,x)ds=0,\quad 0<t<T,\,x\in\Omega.

Hence, setting
Z(s):=\|(\partial_{s}z-iA(x,D^{\prime})z)(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for
0<t<T
, we reach

\int^{t}_{0}R(s)Z(t-s)ds=0,\quad 0<t<T.

By the Titchmarsh convolution theorem (e.g., Titchmarsh
[
16
]
),
there exists
t_{*}\in[0,T]
such that

R(s)=0\quad\mbox{for $0<s<T-t_{*}$},\quad Z(s)=0\quad\mbox{for $0<s<t_{*}$}.

Since
R(0)\neq 0
in
[0,T]
, we see that
t^{*}=T.
Thus the verification of (
2.5
) is complete.

From (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) applying the uniqueness theorem
4
we obtain
z(0,\cdot)=0.
Then equality (
2.7
) implies
f=0.

\blacksquare

3.
Proof of Theorem
4
.

In order to prove the second part of theorem
1
we need the uniqueness result for the Cauchy problem for the Schrödinger equation with data on an arbitrary small part of the boundary.

Proof.
Let
\widetilde{\Omega}
be a bounded domain in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary such that
\Omega\subset\widetilde{\Omega}
,

\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma\subset\partial\widetilde{\Omega}
and

\omega=\widetilde{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is an open set. We extend a function
u
by zero on
(0,T)\times\tilde{\Omega}\setminus\Omega
. Function
u
satisfies

(3.1)

P(x,D)u=0\quad\mbox{in}\,\,(0,T)\times\tilde{\Omega},\quad u|_{(0,T)\times\omega}=0.

Let
\mathcal{K}(t,\tau)\in C^{\infty}([-1,1]\times[0,T])
be solution to the boundary controllability problem for the Shrödinger equation:

\displaystyle i\partial_{\tau}\mathcal{K}-\partial^{2}_{t}\mathcal{K}=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad t\in(-1,1)\,\,\tau\in(0,T),

(3.2)

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{K}(-1,\tau)=\psi(\tau)\,\,\mbox{on}\,\,(0,T),\,\quad\mathcal{K}(\cdot,0)=\mathcal{K}(\cdot,T)=0.

Here
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(0,T)
is fixed complex valued function. Control is located on the part of the boundary
t=1.
For existence of such a solution see e.g.
[
9
]
.
Consider the function
w(x)=\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{K}(t,\tau)u(\tau,x)d\tau.
This function satisfies the equation

(3.3)

L(x,D)w=-\partial_{t}^{2}w+A(x,D^{\prime})w=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad(-1,1)\times\tilde{\Omega},\quad w=0\quad\mbox{on}\quad(-1,1)\times\omega.

The second inequality in (
3.3
) follows from (
3.1
) immediately.
In order to prove the first equality we observe that for any
p\in H^{2}_{0}((-1,1)\times\tilde{\Omega})

(w,L^{*}(x,D)p)_{L^{2}((-1,1)\times\tilde{\Omega})}=(w,-\partial_{t}^{2}p+A^{*}(x,D^{\prime})p)_{L^{2}((-1,1)\times\tilde{\Omega})}=

(\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{K}(t,\tau)u(\tau,x)d\tau,(-\partial_{t}^{2}p+A^{*}(x,D^{\prime})p)_{L^{2}((-1,1)\times\tilde{\Omega})}=

(u,-\int_{-1}^{1}\mathcal{K}(t,\tau)\partial_{t}^{2}pdt+A^{*}(x,D^{\prime})\int_{-1}^{1}\mathcal{K}(t,\tau)pdt)_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\tilde{\Omega})}=

(u,-i\partial_{\tau}\int_{-1}^{1}\mathcal{K}(t,\tau)pdt+A^{*}(x,D^{\prime})\int_{-1}^{1}\mathcal{K}(t,\tau)pdt)_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\tilde{\Omega})}=(u,P^{*}(x,D)\tilde{p})_{L^{2}(Q)}=0

where

\tilde{p}(\tau,x)=\int_{-1}^{1}\mathcal{K}(t,\tau)p(t,x)dt.

The equations (
3.3
) are established. By assumptions (
1.2
), (
1.3
) the operator
L
is the second order elliptic operator with
C^{1}
coefficients in the principal part and
L^{\infty}
coefficients in first and zero order terms. Then theorem 8.9.1 from
[
7
]
which establish uniqueness of the Cauchy problem for this operator can be applied. This implies that function
w
is identically equal to zero on the cylinder
(-1,1)\times\Omega.
In particular

w(-1,x)=\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{K}(-1,\tau)u(\tau,x)d\tau=\int_{0}^{T}\psi(\tau)u(\tau,x)d\tau=0\quad\mbox{on}\quad\Omega.

Since the space
C^{\infty}_{0}(0,T)
is dense in
L^{2}(0,T)
we have
u\equiv 0.

Proof of theorem is complete.
\blacksquare

4.
Proof of Theorem
1
, statement II

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that

(4.1)

0\in\mbox{supp}\,R.

Indeed, since
\mbox{supp}\,R
is not empty there exist a point
\tilde{t}\in\mbox{supp}\,R.
Denote by
t_{0}
infimum over such points. Function
u
is zero on
(0,t_{0})\times\Omega.
We replace function
u
and
R
in (
1.4
) by
y(t+t_{0},\cdot)
and
R(t+t_{0})
respectively to obtain (
4.1
).

Next we reduce the inverse problem to the case when
R(0)=0.
Function
w(x)=\int_{0}^{t}u(\tau,x)d\tau
satisfies :

P(x,D)w=\int_{0}^{t}R(\tau)d\tau f(x)\quad\mbox{in}\quad Q,\quad w(0,\cdot)=0,\quad w|_{(0,T)\times\partial\Omega}=\partial_{\nu}w|_{(0,T)\times\Gamma}=0.

Obviously function
\tilde{R}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}R(\tau)d\tau
satisfies

\tilde{R}(0)=0.

Moreover, since
\frac{d\tilde{R}}{dt}=R
by (
1.6
), function
\tilde{R}
is not identically equal zero on
(0,T).

Hence, besides (
1.6
)
we may additionally assume

(4.2)

R(0)=0.

Let
\widetilde{\Omega}
be a bounded domain in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary such that
\Omega\subset\widetilde{\Omega}
,

\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma\subset\partial\widetilde{\Omega}
and

\omega=\widetilde{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is an open set. We extend functions
u
and
f
by zero on
(0,T)\times\tilde{\Omega}\setminus\Omega
.
By our assumptions we have

P(x,D)u=Rf\quad\mbox{in}\quad(0,T)\times\tilde{\Omega},\quad u|_{(0,T)\times\partial\tilde{\Omega}}=0,\,\,u=0\quad\mbox{on}\quad(0,T)\times\omega.

Next we consider function

(4.3)

y(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}R(t-\tau)v(\tau,x)d\tau,\quad x\in\tilde{\Omega},

where function
v\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\tilde{\Omega}))
solves the initial value problem

P(x,D)v=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad(0,T)\times\tilde{\Omega},\quad v(0,\cdot)=-if,\quad v|_{(0,T)\times\partial\tilde{\Omega}}=0.

By (
4.3
)
function
y(t,x)
satisfies the initial and boundary conditions

(4.4)

\quad y|_{(0,T)\times\partial\tilde{\Omega}}=0,\quad y(0,\cdot)=0.

The short computations imply

(4.5)

P(x,D)y=Rf\quad\mbox{in}\quad(0,T)\times\tilde{\Omega}.

Indeed, by (

(6.1)

\|z\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\leq\frac{C}{|\log\|\partial_{\nu}z\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}|}\leq\frac{\tilde{C}}{|\log\|\partial_{\nu}u\|_{H^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}|}.

Then the standard a priori estimate for the Schrödinger equation imply (
1.7
).

\blacksquare

7.
Appendix

Let
\Psi(t,\tau)\in C^{2}([0,3]\times[0,T])
be a function such that
\Psi=\psi.
Consider the controllability problem for the Schrödinger equation with locally distributed control
v
:

\displaystyle MK=i\partial_{\tau}K-\partial^{2}_{t}K=M\Psi+v\,\,\mbox{in}\quad t\in(0,3),\,\tau\in(0,T),\,\,\mbox{supp}\,v\subset(2,3)\times(0,T),

(7.1)

\displaystyle\quad K(0,\tau)=0\,\,\mbox{on}\,\,(0,T),\quad K(\cdot,0)=\Psi(\cdot,0),\quad K(\cdot,T)=\Psi(\cdot,T).

Then function
\Psi-K
would solve the controllability problem (
5
).

In order to solve problem (
7
) it suffice to prove an observability estimate

(7.2)

\|p\|_{L^{2}(G)}\leq C(\|p\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|Mp\|_{L^{2}(G)}),

where
G=(0,3)\times(0,T),G_{1}=(2,3)\times(0,T).

The estimate (
7.2
) imply (see e.g.
[
18
]
) the existence of solution to problem (
7
) such that

\|K\|_{L^{2}(G)}\leq C(\|\Psi(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{2}(0,3)}+\|\Psi(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(0,3)}+\|M\Psi\|_{L^{2}(G)}).

In order to prove (
7.2
) we use the following observability estimate obtained in
[
2
]
: let
b_{1},b_{0}\in L^{\infty}(G)
there exist a constant
C
such that

(7.3)

\|p\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(0,3))}\leq C(\|Mp+b_{1}\partial_{t}p+b_{0}p\|_{L^{2}(G)}+\|p\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(2,3))}).

Consider the function
\tilde{p}(\tau,t)=\rho(t)(\int_{t}^{3}\rho(\tilde{t})p(\tau,\tilde{t})d\tilde{t}-\int_{0}^{3}\rho(\tilde{t})p(\tau,\tilde{t})d\tilde{t}),
where
\rho(t)\in C^{\infty}[0,3],
\rho|_{[0,2]}=1,
\rho|_{[\frac{5}{2},3]}=0.
We derive equation for the function
\tilde{p}
. Observe that

M(\rho p)=\rho Mp+2\partial_{t}\rho\partial_{t}p+\partial^{2}_{t}\rho p.

Integrating this equation on the interval
(t,3)
we have

i\partial_{\tau}\int_{t}^{3}\rho pd\tilde{t}+\partial_{t}(\rho p)+f=M\int_{t}^{3}\rho pd\tilde{t}=M(\int_{t}^{3}\rho pd\tilde{t}-\int_{0}^{3}\rho pd\tilde{t}),

f=\int_{t}^{3}\rho Mpd\tilde{t}+2\int_{t}^{3}\partial_{t}\rho\partial_{t}pd\tilde{t}+\int_{t}^{3}\partial^{2}_{t}\rho pd\tilde{t}.

For the function
f
we have the following estimate

(7.4)

\|f\|_{L^{2}(G)}\leq C(\|Mp\|_{L^{2}(G)}+\|p\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}).

Finally

M\tilde{p}=-\rho f-\partial^{2}_{t}\rho(\int_{t}^{3}\rho pd\tilde{t}-\int_{0}^{3}\rho pd\tilde{t})+2\partial_{t}\rho p.

Applying the estimate (
7.3
) to the above equation and using (
7.4
) we obtain

\|\rho p\|_{L^{2}(G)}\leq C(\|\int_{0}^{3}\rho pd\tilde{t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|Mp\|_{L^{2}(G)}+\|K\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}).

This estimate implies

(7.5)

\|p(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,3)}\leq C\|p\|_{L^{2}(G)}\leq C(\|\int_{0}^{3}\rho pd\tilde{t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|Mp\|_{L^{2}(G)}+\|p\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}).

This estimate and uniqueness compactness argument imply (
7.2
).
Indeed suppose that (
7.5
) fails. Then there exist a sequence
\{p_{n}\}
such that
\|p_{n}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,3)}=1
and

(7.6)

\|p_{n}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,3)}\geq n(\|Mp_{n}\|_{L^{2}(G)}+\|p_{n}\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}).

By (
7.5
) and Holgrem’s uniqueness theorem one can take a converging subsequence, which we denote again
p_{n}
such that
p_{n}(0,\cdot)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n,k}e_{k}(t)\rightarrow 0
weakly in
L^{2}(0,3)
and
e_{k}(t)=\sin(\frac{k\pi t}{T}).
In particularly

(7.7)

b_{n,k}\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\rightarrow+\infty.

By (
7.5
) we have

(7.8)

\|p_{n}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,3)}\leq C\|p_{n}\|_{L^{2}(G)}\leq C\|\int_{0}^{3}\rho p_{n}dt\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+o(1)\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Since the function
p_{n}
is given by formula
p_{n}(\tau,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{ik^{2}\pi^{2}\tau/T^{2}}b_{n,k}e_{k}(t).
Using this formula we obtain

\int_{0}^{3}p_{n}(t,\tau)\rho(t)dt=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{ik^{2}\pi^{2}\tau/T^{2}}b_{n,k}\int_{0}^{3}\rho(t)e_{k}(t)dt.

On the other hand

\|\int_{0}^{3}\rho(t)p_{n}(\tau,t)dt\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(b_{n,k}\int_{0}^{3}\rho(t)e_{k}(t)dt)^{2}.

Taking into account that
\int_{0}^{3}\rho(t)e_{k}(t)dt=O(\frac{1}{k})
and using (
7.7
) we obtain that

\|\int_{0}^{3}\rho(t)p_{n}(\tau,t)dt\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(b_{n,k}\int_{0}^{3}\rho(t)e_{k}(t)dt)^{2}\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty

we obtain the contradiction to (
7.8
).

\blacksquare

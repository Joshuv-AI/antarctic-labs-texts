Frequency space derivation of linear and non-linear memory gravitational wave signals from eccentric binary orbits
Arpan Hait, Subhendra Mohanty, Suraj Prakash

Abstract
The memory effect in gravitational wave (GW) signals is the phenomenon, wherein the relative position of two inertial GW detectors undergoes a permanent displacement owing to the passage of GWs through them. Measurement of the memory signal is an important target for future observations as it establishes a connection between observations with field-theoretic results like the soft-graviton theorems. Theoretically, the memory signal is predicted at the leading order quadrupole formula for sources like binaries in hyperbolic orbits. This can be in the realm of observations by Advanced LIGO, Einstein-Telescope, or LISA for black-holes with masses ∼ \sim O ⁡ ( 10 3 ​ M ⊙ CLOSE O(10^{3}\,M_{\odot} ) scattered by the super-massive black-hole at the galactic center. Apart from the direct memory component there is a non-linear memory signal in the secondary GW emitted from the primary GW chirp-signals emitted by coalescing binaries. In this paper, we compute the gravitational wave signals and their energy spectrum using the field-theoretic method by computing the scattering amplitudes for eccentric elliptical and hyperbolic binary orbits. The field theoretic calculation gives us the gravitational waveforms of linear and non-linear memory signals directly in the frequency space. The frequency domain templates are useful for extracting signals from the data. We compare our results with other calculations of linear and non-linear memory signals in literature and point out novel features we find in our calculations like the presence of log ⁡ ( ω ) \log(\omega) terms in the linear memory from hyperbolic orbits.

II
Gravitational waveform and energy radiated from scattering amplitudes

The probability amplitude of emitting a graviton of polarisation
\epsilon^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}(\vec{n})
from a source with stress-tensor (in the momentum space )
{\widetilde{T}}^{\mu\nu}(k)
is given by

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\lambda}(k_{0},\vec{n}k_{0})=-\iota\,\cfrac{\kappa}{2}\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu}^{*\lambda}(\vec{n})\,{\widetilde{T}}^{\mu\nu}(k_{0},\vec{n}\,k_{0})\,.

(1)

We can express the gravitational wave metric observed at the detector in terms of the probability amplitude of a graviton emission by a source at a distance
r
as,

\displaystyle h_{\alpha\beta}(\vec{x},t)=\cfrac{1}{4\pi r}\,\int\,\cfrac{dk_{0}}{(2\pi)}\,\sum_{\lambda=1}^{2}\,\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(\vec{n})\,{\cal A}_{\lambda}(k_{0},\vec{n}\,k_{0})\,e^{-\iota k_{0}(t-r)}\,.

(2)

The graviton field in Eq. (
2
) is a canonical spin-2 field with mass dimension 1 as it is defined as an expansion of the metric
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\kappa\,h_{\mu\nu}
where
\kappa=\sqrt{32\pi G}
. The metric perturbation identified as gravitational wave is the dimensionless quantity
\widetilde{h}_{\mu\nu}\equiv g_{\mu\nu}-\eta_{\mu\nu}=\kappa\,h_{\mu\nu}
. The expression for the dimensionless gravitational wave in terms of the amplitude is therefore, from Eq. (
2
), given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}_{\alpha\beta}(\vec{x},t)=\cfrac{\kappa}{4\pi r}\,\int\,\cfrac{dk_{0}}{(2\pi)}\,\sum_{\lambda=1}^{2}\,\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(\vec{n})\,{\cal A}_{\lambda}(k_{0},\vec{n}\,k_{0})\,e^{-\iota k_{0}(t-r)}\,.

(3)

This relates the waveform at the detector to the probability amplitude of graviton emission by the source. To relate the waveform at the detector to the source stress-tensor we substitute in Eq. (
3
), the expression for the amplitude given in Eq. (
1
), to obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}_{\alpha\beta}(\vec{x},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\cfrac{\kappa^{2}}{8\pi r}\,\int\,\cfrac{dk_{0}}{2\pi}\,\sum_{\lambda=1}^{2}\,\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(\vec{n})\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu}^{*\lambda}(\vec{n})\,{\widetilde{T}}^{\mu\nu}(k_{0},\vec{n}\,k_{0})\,e^{-\iota k_{0}(t-r)}\,

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\cfrac{4G}{r}\,\int\,\cfrac{dk_{0}}{2\pi}\,\left(\widetilde{T}_{\alpha\beta}(k_{0},\vec{n}\,k_{0})-\cfrac{1}{2}\,\eta_{\alpha\beta}\,{\widetilde{T}^{\mu}}_{\mu}(k_{0},\vec{n}\,k_{0})\right)\,e^{-\iota k_{0}(t-r)}\,.

where we have made use of the completeness relation

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda=1}^{2}\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}(k)\,\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{*\lambda}(k)=\cfrac{1}{2}\,(\eta_{\mu\alpha}\,\eta_{\nu\beta}+\eta_{\mu\beta}\,\eta_{\nu\alpha})-\cfrac{1}{2}\,\eta_{\mu\nu}\,\eta_{\alpha\beta}.

(5)

To obtain the propagating degrees of freedom, we need to project the transverse-traceless (TT) components of the wavefunction constructed in Eq. (
4
).

\displaystyle\Big[\widetilde{h}_{ij}\Big]^{\text{TT}}(\vec{x},t)=-\cfrac{4G}{r}\,\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\vec{n})\,\int\,\cfrac{dk_{0}}{2\pi}\,\left(\widetilde{T}_{kl}(k_{0},\vec{n}\,k_{0})-\cfrac{1}{2}\,\eta_{kl}\,{\widetilde{T}^{\mu}}_{\,\,\,\,\mu}(k_{0},\vec{n}\,k_{0})\right)\,e^{-\iota k_{0}(t-r)}.

(6)

where,
\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\vec{n})
is the transverse-traceless projection operator defined with respect to the direction,
\hat{n}
, of the emitted gravitational wave. The explicit form of the TT projection operator is

\displaystyle\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\hat{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{ik}(\hat{n})P_{jl}(\hat{n})-\cfrac{1}{2}P_{ij}(\hat{n})P_{kl}(\hat{n})=\left(\delta_{ik}-n_{i}n_{k}\right)\left(\delta_{jl}-n_{j}n_{l}\right)-\cfrac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{ij}-n_{i}n_{j}\right)\left(\delta_{kl}-n_{k}n_{l}\right).

(7)

Since
\Lambda_{ij,kl}\,\,\eta_{kl}=0
, the
{\widetilde{T}^{\mu}}_{\,\,\,\,\mu}
term vanishes and we obtain the simpler result,

\displaystyle\Big[\widetilde{h}_{ij}\Big]^{\text{TT}}(\vec{x},t)=-\cfrac{4G}{r}\,\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\vec{n})\,\int\,\cfrac{dk_{0}}{2\pi}\,T_{kl}(k_{0},\vec{n}\,k_{0})\,e^{-\iota k_{0}(t-r)}.

(8)

In frequency space, the observed gravitational wave and the stress-tensor of the source can be related as,

\displaystyle\Big[\widetilde{h}_{ij}\Big]^{\text{TT}}(\vec{x},k_{0})=-\cfrac{4G}{r}\,\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\vec{n})\,T_{kl}(k_{0},\vec{n}\,k_{0}).

(9)

We shall use Eq. (
9
) to compute the gravitational waveform from various sources, like compact binaries in bound and unbound orbits, by computing the stress-tensor of the source in frequency space.

\displaystyle E_{\text{gw}}=\cfrac{\kappa^{2}}{40}\,\,\mu^{2}\,\int d\omega^{\prime}\omega_{0}^{4}\,\nu^{4}\,a^{4}\,\pi\,\Big[\,\underbrace{f_{1}(\nu,e)\,\left(H_{\iota\nu}^{(1)}(\iota e\nu)\right)^{2}+f_{2}(\nu,e)\,\left({H_{\iota\nu}^{(1)}}^{\prime}(\iota e\nu)\right)^{2}}_{f(\nu,e)}\,\Big],

(26)

where
f_{1,2}(\nu,e)
are given in Eq. (
25
). For a two body scattering with distinct asymptotic states the expression for energy radiated has an extra symmetry factor of
(1/2)
compared to Eq. (
17
). This is to compensate for the overcounting for the two body scattering with distinct asymptotic states which needs to be taken into account for the hyperbolic orbit. For the periodic elliptical orbits this symmetry factor is not there.

Thus the spectrum of energy radiated is given by

\displaystyle P(\omega^{\prime})=\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}}{d\omega^{\prime}}=\cfrac{\kappa^{2}}{40}\,\,\mu^{2}\,\omega_{0}^{4}\,\nu^{4}\,a^{4}\,\pi\,\,f(\nu,e)\,,

(27)

Thus, in our approach where we describe the rate of emission through Fermi’s golden rule, and obtain the power spectrum in terms of products of stress-energy tensor components, we reproduce the same expression for
P(\nu)
as that obtained using the quadrupole formula
Garcia-Bellido:2017knh
.

IV.1
Power emitted at zero frequency

Computing the zero frequency limits of the stress-tensors is equivalent to obtaining approximations for them in the

\nu

\to
0 limit. As
\nu e

\to
0, the Hankel function and its first derivative assume the following form:

\displaystyle H_{\iota\nu}^{(1)}(\iota e\nu)\simeq\cfrac{2\iota}{\pi}\ln(\nu e),\hskip 34.14322ptH_{\iota\nu}^{(1)\prime}(\iota e\nu)\simeq\cfrac{2}{\pi\nu e}.

(29)

Substituting the above in the expressions for
T_{xx}
,
T_{yy}
and
T_{xy}
, in Eqs. (
20
) and (
III
) yields,

\displaystyle T_{xx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\cfrac{2\,\mu\,a^{2}\,\omega_{0}}{e^{2}}\bigg[\ln(\nu e)+(e^{2}-1)\bigg],\hskip 11.38092pt\hskip 42.67912ptT_{yy}=-\cfrac{2\,\mu\,a^{2}\,\omega_{0}}{e^{2}}\left(e^{2}-1\right)\bigg[\ln(\nu e)-1\bigg],

\displaystyle T_{xy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,\iota\,\mu\,\nu\,\omega_{0}\,a^{2}\,\sqrt{e^{2}-1}\left[\cfrac{(e^{2}-1)}{e^{2}}\ln(\nu e)-\cfrac{1}{\nu^{2}\,e^{2}}\right]

(30)

Using these, we can rewrite,

\displaystyle T_{ij}(\nu)\,T^{*}_{ji}(\nu)-\cfrac{1}{3}\,|\,T^{i}{}_{i}(\nu)\,|^{2}=4\,\mu^{2}\,\nu^{2}\,a^{4}\left[\widetilde{f}_{1}(\nu,e)(\ln(\nu e))^{2}\,+\,\widetilde{f}_{2}(\nu,e)\ln(\nu e)\,+\,\widetilde{f}_{3}(\nu,e)\right].

(31)

with

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{1}(\nu,e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{2}{e^{4}}(e^{2}-1)^{3}+\cfrac{6-6e^{2}+2e^{4}}{3\,\nu^{2}\,e^{4}},\qquad\qquad\widetilde{f}_{2}(\nu,e)=\cfrac{2\,(e^{2}-1)}{\nu^{2}\,e^{4}}-\cfrac{6\,(e^{2}-1)^{2}}{\nu^{2}\,e^{4}},

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{3}(\nu,e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{2\,(e^{2}-1)}{\nu^{4}\,e^{4}}+\cfrac{2\,(e^{2}-1)^{2}}{\nu^{2}\,e^{4}}.

(32)

The expression for the power spectrum of the gravitational wave, see Eq. (
27
), now becomes,

\displaystyle P(\omega^{\prime})=\cfrac{\kappa^{2}}{5}\,\pi\,\mu^{2}\,a^{4}\,\omega_{0}^{4}\,\nu^{4}\,f(\nu,\,e).

(33)

In the limit
\nu\to 0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\nu\to 0}\nu^{4}\,f(\nu,\,e)=\cfrac{2\,(e^{2}-1)}{\pi^{2}\,e^{4}},

(34)

which is finite and different from zero, except for
e=1
and
e\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle P(\nu=0)=\cfrac{32\,G}{5}\,\mu^{2}\,a^{4}\,\omega_{0}^{4}\,\cfrac{(e^{2}-1)}{e^{4}}.

(35)

The expression for the power emitted at zero frequency matches the results of refs.

and
Grobner:2020fnb

\displaystyle h_{\lambda}(\omega^{\prime},r)=\frac{4G}{r}\epsilon^{ij}_{\lambda}(\vec{n})\,T_{ij}(n,\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle\iota\frac{4G}{r}\Big((T_{xx}-T_{yy})\sin 2\phi\cos\theta+2T_{xy}\cos 2\phi\cos\theta)-2T_{xz}\cos\phi\sin\theta+2T_{yz}\sin\phi\sin\theta\Big)\,.

Comparison with memory waveforms constructed using soft theorems

According to the soft-graviton theorems, the amplitude of a graviton emission from the scattering of
n
-particles of momenta
p_{a}
can be written as the product of a kinematical factor and the amplitude
{\cal A}_{n}(p_{a})
of the n-particle scattering as

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n+1}(p_{a},q)=\frac{\kappa}{2}\epsilon^{*}_{\lambda\mu\nu}\sum_{a=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{p_{a\mu}p_{a\nu}}{p_{a}\cdot q}-\iota\frac{p_{a}^{\mu}q_{\beta}J_{a}^{\beta\nu}}{p_{a}\cdot q}-\frac{q_{\alpha}q_{\beta}J_{a}^{\alpha\mu}J_{a}^{\beta\nu}}{p_{a}\cdot q}\Big){\cal A}_{n}(p_{a}).

(38)

Here,
J_{a}^{\alpha\beta}=x_{a}^{\alpha}\,p_{a}^{\beta}-x_{a}^{\beta}\,p_{a}^{\alpha}+S_{a}^{\alpha\beta}

``a"
. The series of the soft factors are at the same order in the gravitational coupling but in increasing powers of the graviton frequency
q_{0}=\omega
. The leading term goes as
\omega^{-1}
while the sub-leading terms go as
\sim\omega^{0}
and
\sim\omega^{1}
respectively. The gravitational waveform at distance
r
of the radiated soft-graviton of momentum

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}(r,q)=\frac{4G}{r}\Big(\frac{p_{a\mu}p_{a\nu}}{p_{a}\cdot q}-\iota\frac{p_{a}^{\mu}q_{\beta}J_{a}^{\beta\nu}}{p_{a}\cdot q}-\frac{q_{\alpha}q_{\beta}J_{a}^{\alpha\mu}J_{a}^{\beta\nu}}{p_{a}\cdot q}\Big).

(39)

There are logarithmic corrections to the leading order terms suppressed by
G
which give a tail contribution to the linear memory signal (which as a function of time goes as
1/t

;
Sahoo:2018lxl
;
Laddha:2018vbn
;
Saha:2019tub
. The low frequency graviton signal from a generic hard scattering can be written as
Laddha:2018myi

\displaystyle h_{ij}(\omega)=\iota\omega^{-1}A_{ij}+B_{ij}\ln\omega^{-1}+\cdots,

and

;

\displaystyle h_{yy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4G}{r}\cfrac{2\,\mu\,a^{2}\,\omega_{0}}{e^{2}}\left(e^{2}-1\right)\bigg[\ln\left(\frac{\omega e}{\omega_{0}}\right)-1\bigg],

\displaystyle h_{xy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4G}{r}\,\frac{2\,\iota\,\mu\,a^{2}\,\omega_{0}}{e^{2}}\,\sqrt{e^{2}-1}\left[\cfrac{(e^{2}-1)\omega}{\omega_{0}}\ln\left(\frac{\omega e}{\omega_{0}}\right)-\cfrac{\omega_{0}}{\omega\,}\right]\,.

From the above equation, we see that the memory signal in frequency space has both the

). To compare the coefficients

VI
Gravitational radiation from binaries in elliptical orbits

For eccentric elliptical orbits, Peters and Mathews
Peters-1
;
Peters-2
calculated
the average energy and angular momentum emission rates at
Newtonian order. Their calculation has been improved to the 3-PN level
Blanchet:2004ek
;
Arun:2007sg
, including nonlinear tail-effect (which arises from the scattering of gravitational waves by the near-field potential) at 3-PN
Arun:2007rg
. In this section we compute the frequency spectrum of the energy radiated following
Mohanty:1994yi
;
KumarPoddar:2019jxe
;
KumarPoddar:2019ceq
;
KumarPoddar:2019ceq
;
Poddar:2021yjd
. The radiated energy acts as the source term for secondary gravitational waves which carry the non-linear memory signal.

To describe a compact binary system, comprised of stars having masses
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
, in an elliptical orbit, the following quantities are of relevance:

•

Motion around the common centre of mass can be described in terms of the reduced mass
\mu=\cfrac{m_{1}\,m_{2}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}
and the total mass
M=(m_{1}+m_{2})
.

•

The coordinates of the elliptical Keplerian orbit can be parametrized as:

\displaystyle\quad x(\xi)=a\,(\cos\xi-e),\qquad y(\xi)=b\,\sin\xi,\qquad z(\xi)=0,\qquad\cfrac{\omega_{n}^{\prime}}{n}\,t=\omega_{0}\,t=(\xi-e\sin\xi).

(50)

Here,
a
and
b\,\,(=a\sqrt{1-e^{2}},\,\,e<1)
denote the semi-major and semi-minor axes respectively. The variables
e
and
\xi\in(0,2\pi)
refer to the eccentricity of the orbit and the eccentric anomaly respectively.

•

The angular frequency corresponding to the
n^{th}
harmonic is denoted as
\omega_{n}^{\prime}=n\,\omega_{0}
, with
n=\{0,1,2,....\}
being a non-negative integer and the fundamental frequency
\omega_{0}
can be related to the semi-major axis and the total mass of the system as:
\quad\omega_{0}=\left(\cfrac{G\,(m_{1}+m_{2})}{a^{3}}\right)^{1/2}
.

The first step in the computation of the rate of energy radiated involves the evaluation of the stress-tensor components in frequency space. Once again starting with the
xx
component, the calculation proceeds as follows:

\displaystyle T_{xx}(\omega_{n}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{\mu}{T}\,\int_{0}^{T}\,dt\,\dot{x}^{2}(t)e^{\iota\omega_{n}^{\prime}t}=\cfrac{-\iota\,\mu\,\omega_{n}^{\prime}}{T}\,\int_{0}^{T}\,dt\,\dot{x}(t)\,x(t)\,e^{\iota\omega_{n}^{\prime}t}.

(51)

In the above, we have used integration by parts. Next, the parametric form of the orbit coordinates, shown in Eq. (
50
), allows us to write,

\displaystyle\dot{x}\,dt=\cfrac{dx}{d\xi}\,d\xi=-a\sin\xi\,d\xi.

(52)

Using the above transformation and also substituting for
x
and
\omega_{n}^{\prime}\,t
in terms of functions of
\xi
, we get

\displaystyle T_{xx}(\omega_{n}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{\iota\,\mu a^{2}\omega_{n}^{\prime 2}}{2\pi n}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\xi\,\sin\xi\,(\cos\xi-e)\,e^{\iota n(\xi-e\sin\xi)}=-\cfrac{\mu a^{2}\omega_{n}^{\prime 2}}{n}\left[\left(\cfrac{1-e^{2}}{e}\right)J_{n}^{\prime}(ne)-\cfrac{1}{ne^{2}}J_{n}(ne)\right].

(53)

To arrive at the second line of Eq. (
53
), we replaced the trigonometric functions by the corresponding exponential functions and identified the integral form of Bessel functions of first kind,

\displaystyle J_{n}(z)=\cfrac{1}{2\pi}\,\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\,e^{\iota n(\xi-e\sin\xi)}d\xi.

(54)

The final expression in terms of
J_{n}(ne)
and
J^{\prime}_{n}(ne)
is obtained by utilizing the recurrence relations given below:

\displaystyle J_{n-1}(z)+J_{n+1}(z)=\cfrac{2\,n}{z}\,J_{n}(z),\hskip 28.45274ptJ_{n-1}(z)-J_{n+1}(z)=2\,J^{\prime}_{n}(z).

(55)

The other non-zero components, i.e
T_{yy}
and
T_{xy}
can similarly be obtained as:

\displaystyle T_{yy}(\omega_{n}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{\mu}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt\,\dot{y}^{2}(t)e^{\iota\omega_{n}^{\prime}t}=\cfrac{\mu\,\omega_{n}^{\prime 2}\,a^{2}(1-e^{2})}{n}\left[\cfrac{1}{e}J_{n}^{\prime}(ne)-\cfrac{1}{ne^{2}}J_{n}(ne)\right],

\displaystyle T_{xy}(\omega_{n}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{\mu}{T}\,\int_{0}^{T}\,dt\,\dot{x}(t)\dot{y}(t)e^{\iota\omega_{n}^{\prime}t}=\cfrac{\iota\mu\,\omega_{n}^{\prime 2}\,a^{2}\sqrt{(1-e^{2})}}{n}\left[-\left(\cfrac{1-e^{2}}{e^{2}}\right)J_{n}(ne)+\cfrac{1}{ne}J_{n}^{\prime}(ne)\right].

(56)

Substituting for
T_{xx}
,
T_{yy}
and
T_{xy}
using Eqs. (
53
) and (
VI
) gives,

\displaystyle T_{ij}(\omega^{\prime})\,T^{*}_{ji}(\omega^{\prime})-\cfrac{1}{3}\,|\,T^{i}{}_{i}(\omega^{\prime})\,|^{2}=g(n,e).

(57)

where we have defined
g(n,e)
as,

\displaystyle g(n,e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{n}(ne)^{2}\left[\cfrac{2n^{2}}{e^{4}}(1-e^{2})^{3}+\cfrac{6-6e^{2}+2e^{4}}{3e^{4}}\right]+J_{n}^{\prime}(ne)^{2}\left[\cfrac{2n^{2}}{e^{2}}(1-e^{2})^{2}+\cfrac{2(1-e^{2})}{e^{2}}\right]

(58)

\displaystyle+\,J_{n}(ne)J_{n}^{\prime}(ne)\left[\cfrac{(-8+14e^{2}-6e^{4})n}{e^{3}}\right].

In this case, the energy loss due to gravitational radiation as given in Eq. (
17
) becomes,

\displaystyle\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}}{dt}(e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{\kappa^{2}}{8(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{n=0}\cfrac{8\pi}{5}\left[T_{ij}(\omega_{n}^{\prime})T_{ji}^{*}(\omega_{n}^{\prime})-\cfrac{1}{3}|T^{i}_{i}(\omega_{n}^{\prime})|^{2}\right]\omega_{n}^{\prime 2}=\cfrac{32\,G}{20}\,\omega_{0}^{6}\mu^{2}a^{4}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{2}g(n,e).

(59)

The series sum over products of Bessel functions and their derivative, weighted by powers of
n
, can be expressed completely as functions of
e
, using the identities derived in the appendix of
Peters-1
and reproduced in Eq. (
C
). This enables the identification of the eccentricity dependent part of the energy loss as

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}(e)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{2}\,g(n,e)=\cfrac{4}{(1-e^{2})^{7/2}}\left(1+\cfrac{73}{24}\,e^{2}+\cfrac{37}{96}\,e^{4}\right).

(60)

Therefore, the energy radiated as gravitational waves from the binary can be written as:
Poddar:2021yjd

VIII
Conclusions

In this paper we have computed the linear memory signal for eccentric hyperbolic encounters in both frequency and time domain. We performed a field theoretic calculation of the amplitude of graviton emission from a classical stress-tensor and related this amplitude to the gravitational wave signal as a function of frequency. We took the zero-frequency limit of the waveform to identify the memory signal which we computed in the frequency space and taking the Fourier transform also in the time domain. We performed these calculations for hyperbolic orbits with large eccentricity while retaining terms of all orders in eccentricity. We find that in the eccentric hyperbolic orbits the low-frequency memory component has terms which vary with the frequency
\omega=\nu\Omega
of the gravitational waves as
\log(\omega e/\Omega)
. This
\log
dependence is due to the eccentric hyperbolic orbit and is different from the tail terms which occur for any unbound scattering at
{\cal O}(G^{2})
. Our results are in agreement with the expressions obtained for the stress-tensors and the memory waveforms based on the soft-graviton theorems
Laddha:2018myi
;
Sahoo:2018lxl
;
Laddha:2018vbn
;
Saha:2019tub
, once appropriate substitutions are made in the latter to describe the specific case of a hyperbolic encounter.

The non-linear memory which occurs from GW radiated by GW is equally important as it would prove the non-linear nature of graviton-graviton interactions. We have computed the non-linear memory for eccentric elliptical orbits. Highly eccentric orbits are possible if the initial system has three bodies of which one is ejected
Heggie
or by capture from an unbound orbit
Quinlan
;
OLeary:2008myb
. We compute the frequency spectrum energy radiated by the binaries using the field theory technique which gives the energy spectrum directly in the frequency space. This is used as the source term for the secondary gravitational waves, which results in the memory waveform in the frequency space. We use the instantaneous eccentricity as the parameter for tracking the change of frequency and semi-major axis in time due to radiation reaction. We thus obtain the non-linear memory as a function of the instantaneous eccentricity. We then exploited the change in eccentricity with respect to both time and frequency to obtain the non-linear waveform as a function of both time and frequency. The calculation of the non-linear memory has been done up to all orders in eccentricity. These signal templates in frequency and time domain may be useful for extracting the memory signal from data by the upcoming experiments such as Einstein Telescope and LISA.

In this paper we have used a field-theoretic calculation, and the processes considered are at the tree-level and as expected give the same results as those from the classical quadrupole formula. However, there are some advantages to using the field-theoretic formulation.The energy spectrum is necessarily compared with the sensitivity curves from different experiments to determine the particular experiment for a given type of source. Field theory based methods give us the frequency spectrum of gravitational radiation directly whereas the classical calculation is done in the time domain which then involves an additional Fourier transform.

One specific application of the field theory method is illustrated in this paper where we compare the full orbit calculation with the results from the soft-graviton theorems and found an agreement. This can have applications in the future where calculations of scattering amplitudes can be used for computation of gravitational waves from astrophysical objects. The field theory formulation is carried out most straightforwardly in the frequency space. The results of gravitational waveforms can be used directly as templates for matching the signals which is carried out in frequency space. This can ease the computational effort in match filtering the signals.

Appendix A
Derivation of the general expression for non-linear memory waveforms

Non-linear memory is due to the secondary gravitational waves which are emitted by the primary gravitational waves from an oscillating source, such as, a coalescing binary
Thorne:1991
;
Christodoulou:1991
;
Will:1991
;
Favata:2008yd
;
Favata:2010zu
;
Tolish:2014bka
.

The stress-tensor of gravitational waves is related to the energy radiated as

\displaystyle\tau_{ij}^{\text{gw}}=\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}}{dt\,d\Omega}\,n_{i}\,n_{j},

(92)

and the non-linear memory waveform is given by
Thorne:1991
;
Christodoulou:1991
;
Will:1991
,

\displaystyle h_{ij}(t,r\,\hat{n})=\cfrac{4G}{r}\,\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}\,dt^{\prime}\,d\Omega^{\prime}\,\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}}{dt^{\prime}\,d\Omega^{\prime}}\,\left[\cfrac{n^{\prime}_{i}\,n^{\prime}_{j}}{1-\vec{n}^{\prime}\cdot\vec{n}}\right]^{\rm TT}.

(93)

Here
\vec{n}^{\prime}
is the unit vector from the source to the solid angle denoted by
d\Omega^{\prime}
and
\vec{n}
is the unit vector along the line of sight from the source to the detector. In what follows, we outline the derivation of these results.

The source term of the non-linear gravitational waves is the Issacson stress-tensor of the primary gravitational waves, and it is given as

\displaystyle\tau^{\text{gw}}_{ij}=\cfrac{1}{32\pi G}\,\langle\partial_{i}h^{ab}\partial_{j}h_{ab}\rangle.

(94)

Here, the angular brackets encode averaging over time longer than the time period and volumes larger than the wavelengths of the source gravitational waves
h_{ab}
. The source gravitational waves travel outward radially with speed of light and are functions of
(t-r)
, i.e
h_{ab}(t,\vec{x})=h_{ab}(t-r,\Omega)
. This implies we can relate their spatial and temporal derivatives as,

\partial_{i}\,h_{ab}(t-r)=-n_{i}\,\partial_{0}\,h_{ab}(t-r)
, where
n_{i}=x_{i}/r
. Therefore we can write

\displaystyle\tau^{\text{gw}}_{ij}=\cfrac{1}{32\pi G}\,\langle\partial_{i}h^{ab}\partial_{j}h_{ab}\rangle=n_{i}n_{j}\tau^{\text{gw}}_{00}\,.

(95)

We can model the energy density
\tau^{\text{gw}}_{00}
produced from a source and propagating radially outwards on null-rays as

\displaystyle\tau^{\text{gw}}_{00}(t,\vec{x})=\cfrac{1}{r^{2}}\,\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}(t-r,\Omega)}{dt\,d\Omega}.

(96)

where
dE_{\text{gw}}/dt
is the luminosity of the source in gravitational waves and
dE_{\text{gw}}/d\Omega
denotes the angular distribution of the source luminosity. Therefore we can write
\tau_{ij}^{\text{gw}}
in terms of the energy flux as

\displaystyle\tau_{ij}^{\text{gw}}=n_{i}n_{j}\tau_{00}^{\text{gw}}=n_{i}n_{j}\,\cfrac{1}{r^{2}}\,\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}(t-r,\Omega)}{dt\,d\Omega}\,.

(97)

The secondary gravitational waves sourced by the gravitational wave stress-tensor will obey the in-homogenous wave equation:

\displaystyle\square\,h_{ij}=-16\pi G\,\tau^{\text{gw}}_{ij}.

(98)

whose solution assumes the following form,

\displaystyle h^{\text{mem}}_{ij}(t,\vec{x})=4G\int dt^{\prime}\,d^{3}x^{\prime}\,\,\tau^{\text{gw}}_{ij}(t^{\prime},\vec{x}^{\prime})\,\cfrac{\delta\left(t^{\prime}-(t-|\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime}|)\right)}{|\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime}|}.

(99)

We can express the source term, Eq. (
95
), in terms of the null coordinate
u=t^{\prime}-r^{\prime}
as follows,

\displaystyle\tau^{\text{gw}}_{ij}(t^{\prime},\vec{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{n^{\prime}_{i}n^{\prime}_{j}}{{r^{\prime}}^{2}}\,\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}(t^{\prime}-r^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime})}{dt^{\prime}d\Omega^{\prime}}\,=\,\int du\,\,\cfrac{n^{\prime}_{i}n^{\prime}_{j}}{{r^{\prime}}^{2}}\,\,\delta(u-(t^{\prime}-r^{\prime}))\,\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}(u,\Omega)}{dt^{\prime}d\Omega^{\prime}}.

(100)

Substituting the above in Eq. (
99
) we obtain,

\displaystyle h^{\text{mem}}_{ij}(t,\vec{x})=4G\,\int du\,dt^{\prime}\,dr^{\prime}\,{r^{\prime}}^{2}\,d\Omega^{\prime}\,\cfrac{n^{\prime}_{i}n^{\prime}_{j}}{{r^{\prime}}^{2}}\,\cfrac{\delta\left(t^{\prime}-(t-|\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime}|)\right)}{|\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime}|}\,\,\delta(u-(t^{\prime}-r^{\prime}))\,\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}(u,\Omega)}{dt^{\prime}d\Omega^{\prime}}.

(101)

Since the distance to the observer is much larger than the source size,
r\gg r^{\prime}
, we take the approximations

\displaystyle\cfrac{1}{|\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime}|}\simeq\cfrac{1}{r\,(1-\vec{n}^{\prime}\cdot\vec{n})}\,,\qquad\qquad\delta\left(t^{\prime}-(t-|\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime}|)\right)\simeq\delta(t^{\prime}-(t-r)).

(102)

Now we can perform the integral over
r^{\prime}
using the second delta function in Eq. (
101
) and then do the
t^{\prime}
integration using the remaining delta function to obtain

\displaystyle h^{\text{mem}}_{ij}(t,\vec{x})=\cfrac{4G}{r}\,\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}\,du\int_{4\pi}\,d\Omega^{\prime}\,\,\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}(u,\Omega)}{du\,d\Omega^{\prime}}\,\,\cfrac{n^{\prime}_{i}n^{\prime}_{j}}{(1-\vec{n}^{\prime}\cdot\vec{n})}.

(103)

Expression for the corresponding transverse-traceless wavefunction is obtained after multiplication by the projection operator
\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\vec{n})
, defined in Eq. (
7
),

\displaystyle\Big[h^{\text{mem}}_{ij}\Big]^{\text{TT}}(t,\vec{x})=\cfrac{4G}{r}\,\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}du\,\int_{4\pi}\,d\Omega^{\prime}\,\,\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}(u,\Omega)}{du\,d\Omega^{\prime}}\,\,\cfrac{\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\vec{n})\,n^{\prime}_{k}n^{\prime}_{l}}{(1-\vec{n}^{\prime}\cdot\vec{n})},

(104)

Appendix B
Non-linear memory from circular orbits

As an illustration of the above formalism, we have recomputed the known result for non-linear memory associated with binaries in circular orbits
Kenneflick-94
;
Favata:2009ii
;
Divakarla:2021xrd
.

Unlike the elliptical case highlighted in section
VI
, the circular case only exhibits a single frequency mode. Therefore, the expression for rate of energy radiated in the direction
d\Omega^{\prime}
has the following simpler form,

\displaystyle\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}}{dt^{\prime}\,d\Omega^{\prime}}=\cfrac{\kappa^{2}}{4}\int\,\Big(T_{ij}(\omega^{\prime})\,T^{*}_{kl}(\omega^{\prime})\,\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\vec{n}^{\prime})\Big)\,{\omega^{\prime}}^{3}\,2\pi\,\delta(\omega^{\prime}-2\omega_{0})\,\cfrac{d\omega^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}\,2\omega^{\prime}}.

(105)

where
\omega_{0}=\sqrt{G(m_{1}+m_{2})/a^{3}}
is the angular frequency of the Kepler orbit. The stress-energy tensor
T_{ij}
, where
i,j=x,y,z
, can be written in matrix form as:

\displaystyle\textsf{T}\,(\omega_{0})=\cfrac{\mu\,a^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}{2}\,\begin{pmatrix}1&&\iota&&0\\
\iota&&-1&&0\\
0&&0&&0\end{pmatrix}.

(106)

We consider the orientation defined in Fig.
4
, where the axis of rotation of the binary system is counter-clockwise rotated, by an angle
i
, with respect to the
z
axis. This rotation impacts the stress-tensor matrix
T
as follows:

\displaystyle\textsf{T}\rightarrow\textsf{T}^{\prime}=\mathcal{R}\,\textsf{T}\,\mathcal{R}^{\text{T}},\hskip 5.69046pt\text{with}\hskip 5.69046pt\mathcal{R}=\begin{pmatrix}1&&0&&0\\
0&&\cos i&&-\sin i\\
0&&\sin i&&\cos i\end{pmatrix},\hskip 2.84544pt\Rightarrow\hskip 2.84544pt\textsf{T}^{\prime}=\cfrac{\mu\,a^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&&\iota\cos i&&\iota\sin i\\
\iota\cos i&&-\cos^{2}i&&-\cos i\sin i\\
\iota\sin i&&-\cos i\sin i&&-\sin^{2}i\end{pmatrix}.

(107)

The product
T_{ij}\,T^{*}_{kl}\,\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\vec{n}^{\prime})
which can be rewritten entirely as a product of
T_{ij}
’s and
\hat{n}^{\prime}
, using Eq. (
7
), as follows:

\displaystyle T_{ij}\,T^{*}_{kl}\,\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\hat{n}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{ij}\,T^{*}_{ji}-2\,T_{ij}\,T^{*}_{jl}\,n_{i}^{\prime}\,n_{l}^{\prime}+\cfrac{1}{2}\,T_{ij}\,T^{*}_{kl}\,n_{i}^{\prime}\,n_{j}^{\prime}\,n_{k}^{\prime}\,n_{l}^{\prime}+\cfrac{1}{2}\,\left(T_{ii}\,T^{*}_{kl}\,n_{k}^{\prime}\,n_{l}^{\prime}+T_{ij}\,T^{*}_{kk}\,n_{i}^{\prime}\,n_{j}^{\prime}-T_{ii}\,T^{*}_{kk}\right).

(108)

For the circular case both the stress-tensor matrices
\mathsf{T}
and
\mathsf{T}^{\prime}
are already traceless. Therefore, the last three terms, within the parentheses, on the right hand side of Eq. (
108
) vanish. The remaining terms can be evaluated as products of matrices as shown below:

\displaystyle T_{ij}\,T^{*}_{kl}\,\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\hat{n}^{\prime})\,

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,\text{Tr}\,[\mathsf{T}^{\prime}\,\mathsf{T}^{\prime\dagger}]-2\,\left(\hat{n}^{\prime\text{T}}\,\mathsf{T}^{\prime}\right)\cdot\,\left(\mathsf{T}^{\prime\dagger}\hat{n}^{\prime}\right)+\cfrac{1}{2}\,\left(\hat{n}^{\prime\text{T}}\,\mathsf{T}^{\prime}\hat{n}^{\prime}\right)\,\left(\hat{n}^{\prime\text{T}}\,\mathsf{T}^{\prime\dagger}\hat{n}^{\prime}\right)\,.

(109)

Substituting for
\mathsf{T}^{\prime}
,
\hat{n}^{\prime}
in Eq. (
109
) and evaluating the matrix products and traces yields:

\displaystyle T_{ij}\,T^{*}_{kl}\,\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\hat{n}^{\prime})\,

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,\cfrac{\mu^{2}a^{4}\omega_{0}^{4}}{4}\,\cfrac{1}{2}\,\Big(1+6\cos^{2}\theta+\cos^{4}\theta\Big)\,.

(110)

Here,
\theta
is the angle between the axis of rotation of the binary
\vec{L}=\left(0,-\sin i,\cos i\right)
and the direction where the primary graviton emits the second graviton:
\hat{n}^{\prime}=\left(\sin\theta^{\prime}\cos\phi^{\prime},\sin\theta^{\prime}\sin\phi^{\prime},\cos\theta^{\prime}\right)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\cos\theta=-\sin i\sin\theta^{\prime}\sin\phi^{\prime}+\cos i\cos\theta^{\prime}.

(111)

Eq. (
105
) can now be expressed, using the result of Eq. (
110
) as

\displaystyle\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}}{dt^{\prime}\,d\Omega^{\prime}}=\cfrac{G}{2\pi}\,\omega_{0}^{6}\,\mu^{2}a^{4}\,\Big(1+6\cos^{2}\theta+\cos^{4}\theta\Big)\,.

(112)

The Kepler orbital frequency can be written in terms of the semi-major axis
a
, i.e.
\omega_{0}=(GM/a^{3})^{1/2}
(
M=m_{1}+m_{2}
). This allows us to rewrite the expression for the rate of energy radiated as,

\displaystyle\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}}{dt^{\prime}d\Omega^{\prime}}=\cfrac{G^{4}}{2\pi}\cfrac{\mu^{2}M^{3}}{a^{5}}\Big(1+6\cos^{2}\theta+\cos^{4}\theta\Big)\,.

(113)

Substituting the above in Eq. (
103
), we obtain the following expression for the memory signal:

\displaystyle h_{ij}^{\text{mem}}(t,\vec{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{4G}{r}\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}dt^{\prime}\int_{4\pi}d\Omega^{\prime}\,\cfrac{dE_{\text{gw}}(t^{\prime},\Omega)}{dt^{\prime}d\Omega^{\prime}}\cfrac{n^{\prime}_{i}n^{\prime}_{j}}{(1-\hat{n}^{\prime}\cdot\hat{n})}

(114)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{4G}{r}\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\cfrac{G^{4}}{2\pi}\,\cfrac{\mu^{2}M^{3}}{a^{5}}\underbrace{\int_{4\pi}d\Omega^{\prime}\,\Big(1+6\cos^{2}\theta+\cos^{4}\theta\Big)\cfrac{n^{\prime}_{i}n^{\prime}_{j}}{(1-\hat{n}^{\prime}\cdot\hat{n})}}_{\textsf{A}_{ij}}.

where we have replaced
t-r
by
t
in the limit of the integrand as the waves are detected at a fixed
r
and the dependence in
u
then becomes a dependence on time of observation
t
.

To obtain the transverse-traceless part of
h^{\text{mem}}_{ij}

\displaystyle\Big[h_{ij}^{\text{mem}}\Big]^{\text{TT}}(t,\vec{x})

(115)

Substituting for the components of
\hat{n}

and
\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle\Big[\textsf{A}_{xx}\Big]^{\text{TT}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin\theta^{\prime}\,d\theta^{\prime}\,\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\phi^{\prime}\,\left(1+6\cos^{2}\theta+\cos^{4}\theta\right)\,\times\,\cfrac{1}{2}\left(1+\cos\theta^{\prime}\right)\,\sin 2\phi^{\prime}=0.

(116)

The other components are:
\Big[\textsf{A}_{yy}\Big]^{\text{TT}}=-\Big[\textsf{A}_{xx}\Big]^{\text{TT}}

Statistical solutions for the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system
Eduard Feireisl, Mária Lukáčová-Medvi d’ ová

Abstract
We show a general stability result in the framework of strong solutions of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system describing the motion of a compressible viscous and heat conducting gas. As a corollary, we develop a concept of statistical solution in the class of regular solutions “beyond the blow up time”.

Introduction

The goal of the present paper is to develop a mathematical framework for studying the motion of a general compressible, viscous and heat conducting fluid with random (uncertain) data. Basically all well established models
in continuum fluid mechanics, notably the Navier–Stokes system, are known to be well posed in the natural framework of smooth or at least sufficiently regular solutions. Unfortunately, the solutions may develop a finite time blow up for certain data, see Merle et al.
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
, whereas the problem is expected to be ill posed in the class of generalized (weak) solutions, cf. Buckmaster and Vicol
[
5
]

or Albritton et al.
[
2
]
.

Unique dependence of solutions on the data seems mandatory in problems with random parameters. If at least existence of some kind of generalized solution is known, uniqueness can be restored by means a suitable
selection
process, see
[
10
]
. In this case, the associated statistical solutions can be identified
with a Markov semigroup of operators defined on a suitable space of probability distributions. For a weaker
concept of statistical solution based on the weak solutions, we refer to Constantin and Wu
[
8
]
, Foias
[
14
]
, Foias, Rosa, and Temam
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
, Vishik and Fursikov
[
30
]
.
For compressible Euler system statistical solutions were identified with a hierarchie of correlation measures by Fjordholm et al.
[
17
]
.
Unfortunately, for certain class of problems, the existence of a suitable generalized solution is still open. In particular, solutions may loose regularity or even become unbounded in a finite time lap, which excludes any “natural” continuation.

Statistical solutions are inevitable in the analysis of uncertainty quantification methods, such as the collocation, Monte Carlo or stochastic Galerkin methods. These methods are routinely used in engineering, physics, meteorology or medicine to quantify the uncertainty of data.
We refer to monographs and review papers by Abgrall and Mishra
[
1
]
, Le Maître and Knio
[
21
]
, Mishra and Schwab
[
24
]
, Nordström
[
25
]
, Xiu
[
9
]
. Rigorous convergence analysis of these uncertainty quantification methods
leans on uniqueness
and, ideally, continuous dependence of solutions on random parameters and was done only for simplified model problems. For complex system, such as the compressible Navier–Stokes–Fourier equations rigourous
analysis of uncertainty quantification method is not available. Our aim in this paper is to fill this gap and provide a rigorous solution concept for uncertainty quantification analysis.

The
Navier–Stokes–Fourier
(NSF)
system
describes the time
evolution of the mass density
\varrho=\varrho(t,x)
, the (absolute) temperature
\vartheta=\vartheta(t,x)
, and velocity

{\bf u}={\bf u}(t,x)
of a general Newtonian compressible heat conducting fluid:

•

Mass conservation, equation of continuity:

\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})=0.

(1.1)

•

Momentum balance, Newton’s second law:

\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho,\vartheta)={\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})+\varrho{\bf g}.

(1.2)

•

Internal energy balance, First law of thermodynamics:

\partial_{t}(\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta))+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}{\bf q}=\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-p(\varrho,\vartheta){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}+\varrho Q.

(1.3)

For viscous and heat conducting gases, the problem may be formally closed by the following
constitutive
relations
:

•

Equations of state, Boyle–Mariotte law:

p(\varrho,\vartheta)=\varrho\vartheta,\ e(\varrho,\vartheta)=c_{v}\vartheta.

(1.4)

•

Newton’s rheological law:

\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})=\mu\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\nabla_{x}{\bf u}^{t}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I}\right)+\eta{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I},\ \ \mu>0,\eta\geq 0,

(1.5)

•

Fourier’s law:

{\bf q}=-\kappa\nabla_{x}\vartheta,\ \kappa>0.

(1.6)

The initial state of the system at the reference time
t=0
is given through

initial data
:

\varrho(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},\ {\bf u}(0,\cdot)={\bf u}_{0},\ \vartheta(0,\cdot)=\vartheta_{0}.

(1.7)

For simplicity, we assume that all quantities are spatially periodic, meaning the fluid domain can be identified with the flat torus

\mathbb{T}^{3}=\left(\left[-1,1\right]\Big|_{\{-1;1\}}\right)^{3}.

(1.8)

The quantity

D=\Big(\varrho_{0},{\bf u}_{0},\vartheta_{0};{\bf g},Q;c_{v},\mu,\eta,\kappa\Big)

represents the
data
of the problem. Ideally, for any
physically admissible
data and the time

t>0
, the NSF system should admit a unique solution
[\varrho(t,\cdot),\vartheta(t,\cdot),{\bf u}(t,\cdot)]
.
By physically
admissible
we mean the data satisfying

\inf\varrho_{0}>0,\ \inf\vartheta_{0}>0,\ c_{v}>1,\ \mu>0,\ \eta\geq 0,\ \kappa>0.

(1.9)

Hereafter we always tacitly assume that the data we deal with are admissible.

Local existence of regular solutions

(

Let

(1.10)

and

\displaystyle{\bf g}

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle\in BC([0,\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\ \partial_{t}Q\in L^{2}(0,\infty;W^{-1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\ Q\geq 0.

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})).

.

A brief inspection of the existence proof in

In particular,

We define the life span – maximal existence time

– of the local solution,

0<T_{\rm max}=\sup\left\{T>0\ \Big|\ \mbox{regular solution}\ (\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})\ \mbox{exists in}\ [0,T]\right\}\leq\infty.

(1.15)

In accordance with Remark

,

\displaystyle T_{\rm max}

\displaystyle<\infty

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T_{\rm max}-}

\displaystyle\left(\|\varrho(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\|\vartheta(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{2,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\|{\bf u}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{2,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})}+\|\varrho(t,\cdot)^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\right)=\infty.

Note that
\|\varrho^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=\inf_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\varrho^{-1}.

1.2
Conditional regularity

It is still open whether
T_{\rm max}
in Theorem
1.1
may be finite for some data. If this is the case, however, the density and/or temperature must become unbounded at the same time. We report the following
conditional regularity
criterion, see

[
13
, Theorem 1.4]
:

Theorem 1.3

(
Conditional regularity criterion).

Let the data

c_{v}>1,\ \mu>0,\ \eta\geq 0,\kappa>0

be given.
Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, let
[\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u}]
be the local strong solution of the NSF system defined on
[0,T_{\rm max})
.

Then there exists a function
\Lambda
, bounded for bounded values of its argument, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}

\displaystyle\|\varrho(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\vartheta(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{2,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|{\bf u}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{2,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\|\vartheta\|_{W^{2,q}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\|{\bf u}\|_{W^{2,q}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})}^{2}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\partial_{t}\varrho\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\partial_{t}\vartheta\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\partial_{t}{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}\vartheta\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}{\bf u}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})}^{2}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\Big(T,\|\varrho_{0}\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{T}^{3})},\|\vartheta_{0}\|_{W^{2,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})},\|{\bf u}_{0}\|_{W^{2,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})},\|\varrho_{0}^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})},

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|{\bf g}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})},\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Q(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})},\int_{0}^{T}\|{\bf g}\|^{2}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3}))}\,{\rm d}t,\int_{0}^{T}\|Q\|^{2}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}\,{\rm d}t,

\displaystyle\quad\left.\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}{\bf g}\|^{2}_{W^{-1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3}))}\,{\rm d}t,\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}Q\|^{2}_{W^{-1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}\,{\rm d}t,\ \sup_{(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}\varrho,\sup_{(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}\vartheta,c_{v},\mu,\mu^{-1},\eta,\kappa,\kappa^{-1}\right)

(1.17)

for any
0<T<T_{\rm max}
.

Besides the given data, the function
\Lambda
depends only on the uniform norm of
\varrho
and
\vartheta
.
The conclusion of Theorem
1.3
can be therefore equivalently formulated as

\limsup_{t\to T_{\rm max}-}\ \sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(\varrho(t,x)+\vartheta(t,x)\Big)=\infty.

(1.18)

1.3
Data dependence and statistical solutions

In the absence of global–in–time existence results in the class of regular solutions, the weak solutions may represent a suitable alternative,
Unfortunately, the existence theory developed in
[
11
]
is restricted to
a rather specific class of constitutive relations that does not cover (
1.4
)–(
1.6
). What is more,
the available
a priori
bounds are not sufficient to use the framework of the more general measure–valued solutions in the spirit of
[
6
]
. In particular, there is no guarantee the solution
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})

remains continuous up to the blow up time
T_{\rm max}
even in a weak sense. Indeed the only available

a priori
bounds result from the physical principles of
mass conservation

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\varrho(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\varrho_{0}(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x},

(1.19)

energy conservation
(if
{\bf g}=0,\,Q=0
)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+c_{v}\varrho\vartheta\right)(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}|{\bf u}_{0}|^{2}+c_{v}\varrho_{0}\vartheta_{0}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x},

(1.20)

and
entropy production

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\varrho\log\left(\frac{\vartheta^{c_{v}}}{\varrho}\right)(t,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}\geq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\varrho_{0}\log\left(\frac{\vartheta^{c_{v}}_{0}}{\varrho_{0}}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}+\kappa\frac{|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\ {\rm d}s.

(1.21)

Our plan is to develop the theory for random data, notably the concept of
statistical
solution, using the framework of
regular solutions. In view of the blow up criterion (
1.18
), this approach suits well to problems related
to convergence of numerical approximations, where (
1.18
) is very often taken for granted. In addition,
we may also “anticipate” the idea that solutions violating (
1.18
) and thus blowing up in a finite time
are statistically insignificant and can be ignored in large samples of data.

The theory is based on a stability result that may be of independent interest: The life span
T_{\rm max}
is a lower semi–continuous function of with respect to a suitable topology of the data space, see Section
2
. Given a regular solution of the NSF system, any other solution corresponding to a sufficiently close data remains regular at least on the same time interval. Similar results in the context of barotropic fluids were obtained by Bae and Zajaczkowski

[
3
]
, see also

[
4
]
.
Once stability is established, the statistical solutions are obtained via the push forward measure argument, see Section
3
. In order to accommodate the possible blow up of solutions, we modify the original topology of the phase space
in the spirit of the celebrated Jakubowski Theorem
[
18
]
. Finally, in Section
4
we illustrate the theory showing convergence of the Monte-Carlo method.

D_{X,F,P}
,

,

, and

.

, the afore mentioned spaces are endowed with the following topologies:

The

The

\displaystyle\times C([0,T];W^{2,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;W^{2,q}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\cap W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\cap W^{1,2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))

\displaystyle\times C([0,T];W^{2,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;W^{2,q}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3}))

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\cap W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3}))\cap W^{1,2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3}))

C([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{5}).

•

The
data space

D_{X,F,P}=X\times F\times P,

where

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\left[BC[0,\infty;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})\cap L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})\cap W^{1,2}(0,\infty;W^{-1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3}))\right]

\displaystyle\times\left[BC[0,\infty;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\cap L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\cap W^{1,2}(0,\infty;W^{-1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\right],

2.1
Stability in the basic topology

In accordance with Theorem
1.1
, the solution exists on a maximal time interval
[0,T_{\rm max}[D])
as long as
the data
D
are admissible and belong to
D_{X,F,P}
. Motivated by the regularity criterion established in Theorem
1.3
, we introduce a “stopping time”

{T}_{M}[D]=\sup_{\tau\in[0,T_{\rm max})}\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,\tau],x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(\varrho(t,x)+\vartheta(t,x)\Big)<M\right\}=\inf_{\tau>0}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(\varrho(\tau,x)+\vartheta(\tau,x)\Big)\geq M.

(2.1)

Obviously,
{T}_{M}[D]
is a non–decreasing functions of
M
, and, in view of Theorem
1.3
and (
1.18
),

{T}_{M}[D]<T_{\rm max}[D],\ {T}_{M}[D]\nearrow T_{\rm max}[D]\ \mbox{as}\ M\to\infty.

(2.2)

Now, suppose that
D_{n}\to D
in
D_{X,F,P}
, where
D
are admissible data, specifically,

\displaystyle\varrho_{0,n}

\displaystyle\to\varrho_{0}\ \mbox{in}\ W^{1,q}(\mathbb{T}^{3}),\ \mbox{where}\ \varrho_{0}>0,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{0,n}

\displaystyle\to\vartheta_{0}\ \mbox{in}\ W^{2,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}),\ \vartheta_{0}>0,

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{0,n}

\displaystyle\to{\bf u}_{0}\ \mbox{in}\ W^{2,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3}),

(2.3)

\displaystyle{\bf g}_{n}

\displaystyle\to{\bf g}\ \mbox{in}\ BC([0,\infty;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})),\ \partial_{t}{\bf g}_{n}\to\partial_{t}{\bf g}\ \mbox{in}\ L^{2}(0,\infty;W^{-1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})),

\displaystyle Q_{n}

\displaystyle\to Q\ \mbox{in}\ BC([0,\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\ \partial_{t}Q_{n}\to\partial_{t}Q\ \mbox{in}\ L^{2}(0,\infty;W^{-1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),

(2.4)

and

c^{n}_{v}\to c_{v}>1,\ \mu_{n}\to\mu>0,\ \lambda_{n}\to\lambda\geq 0,\ \kappa_{n}\to\kappa>0.

(2.5)

In addition, assume that

{T}_{M}[D_{n}]\to\tau\ \mbox{and fix}\ 0\leq T^{-}\leq\tau<T^{+}.

Finally, consider the sequence
(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n})=(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})[D_{n}](t\wedge{T}_{M}[D_{n}],\cdot)
, specifically

(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n})(t,\cdot)
is the strong solution of the NSF system if
t\leq{T}_{M}(D_{n})
and

(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n}{\bf u}_{n})(t,\cdot)=(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n}{\bf u}_{n})({T}_{M}(D_{n}),\cdot)
if
t>{T}_{M}[D_{n}]
.

Now, it follows from the uniform bounds (
1.3
) and the standard compact embedding relations
for Sobolev spaces that

(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\ \mbox{if precompact in}\ BC([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3});R^{5}),

and, by the same token

\varrho_{n}\to\varrho,\ \vartheta_{n}\to\vartheta,\ {\bf u}_{n}\to{\bf u}

in the weak-(*) topology of the trajectory space
X_{T^{+}}
. Here and hereafter, by the weak-(*) topology on the trajectory space
X_{T}
we mean the weak star topology of the space

\displaystyle X_{T,w-(*)}

\displaystyle=L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\cap W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))

\displaystyle\times L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{2,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;W^{2,q}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\cap W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\cap W^{1,2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))

\displaystyle\times L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{2,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;W^{2,q}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3}))

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\cap W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3}))\cap W^{1,2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})).

The following is easy to check:

•

The limit
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})
is the unique solution of the NSF system in
[0,T^{-}]
corresponding to the limit data
D
.

•

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(\varrho(t,\cdot)+\vartheta(t\cdot)\Big)=M\ \mbox{for any}\ t\geq T^{+}.

As
T^{-}\leq\tau\leq T^{+}
are arbitrary, we conclude

, in other words, the mapping

D\mapsto{T}_{M}[D]\ \mbox{is lower semi--continuous}.

(2.6)

Accordingly,

. We have shown the following result.

(

For any sequence
D_{n}\to D
in
D_{X,F,P}
, where

are admissible data, we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{\rm max}[D_{n}]\geq T_{\rm max}[D],

(2.7)

and

(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})[D_{n}]\to(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})[D]\ \mbox{weakly-(*) in}\ X_{T}

(2.8)

for any

.

3
Statistical solutions

We introduce a suitable topology on the phase space
X
and the corresponding concept of statistical solution
via the push forward measure argument. To simplify presentation, we denote by

{\bf U}=(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})
the solution of the Navier–Stokes system,
{\bf U}_{0}=(\varrho_{0},\vartheta_{0},{\bf u}_{0})
the initial data, and
(f,p)\in F\times P
the driving force and the parameter set, respectively.

In particular,

{\bf U}[{\bf U}_{0},f,p](t,\cdot)
stands for the value of the strong solution of the NSF system emanating from initial
data
{\bf U}_{0}
, driven by the external forcing
f
, and with constitutive parameters
p
evaluated at a time
t
. For the above definition to make sense we may either (i) focus only on
t<T_{\rm max}[{\bf U}_{0},f,p]
or
(ii) define the solution
{\bf U}
“after the blow up time”
T_{\rm max}
. Pursuing the later strategy, we set

{\bf U}(t,\cdot)={\bf U}_{\infty}\equiv(0,0,0)\ \mbox{whenever}\ t\geq T_{\rm max}.

At first glance, this may seem a bit surprising as, apparently, the solution is not “continuous” for
t\to T_{\rm max}
. However such a choice is convenient as (i) the value
{\bf U}_{\infty}
is never reached by any trajectory in finite time (ii)
{\bf U}_{0}={\bf U}_{\infty}
is not an admissible initial state so isolated in the family of initial data.

Our goal in the next section is to modify the topology on the phase
X
in such a way that
{\bf U}\to{\bf U}_{\infty}
for
any solution trajectory whenever
t\to T_{\rm max}
, meaning

\left(\|{\bf U}(t,\cdot)\|_{X}+\|\varrho^{-1}(t,\cdot)\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\right)\to\infty\ \mbox{as}\ t\to T_{\rm max}-.

To this end, we convert
X
to a Riemannian manifold with boundary modelled over
X
.

3.1
Topology on the phase space

The space
X
is a reflexive separable Banach space, in particular a Polish metric space. We consider its
open
subset

X^{+}=\left\{(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})\in X\ \Big|\ \|\varrho^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}<\infty,\ {\|\vartheta^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}<\infty}\right\}.

Note carefully that
X^{+}
is an
open
subset of the Polish space
X
, whence Polish as well.
Finally, we set

X^{+}_{\infty}=X^{+}\cup\{{\bf U}_{\infty}\},\ {\bf U}_{\infty}=(0,0,0).

Next, we introduce a class of functions

\mathcal{C}=\left\{\mathcal{F}\in X^{+}_{\infty}\to R\ \Big|\ \mathcal{F}|_{X^{+}}\in BC(X^{+}),\ \lim_{\|{\bf U}\|_{X}+\|\varrho^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+{\|\vartheta^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\to\infty}\mathcal{F}|_{X^{+}}({\bf U})=\mathcal{F}(0)\right\}.

(3.1)

Our goal is to define a suitable metrics on the space
X^{+}_{\infty}
, for which the class
\mathcal{C}
will coincide with
BC(X_{\infty}^{+}).

To this end, consider the functions – the Fourier coefficients – defined as

F_{{\bf k}}({\bf U})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}{\bf U}\cdot e_{{\bf k}}\ \,{\rm d}{x},\ e_{\bf k}\ \mbox{are normed trigonometric polynomials},\ {\bf k}\in Z^{5},\ {\bf U}\in X,

together with
G:X^{+}_{\infty}\to R
,

G(0)\equiv G({\bf U}_{\infty})=0,\ G({\bf U})=\left(1+\|{\bf U}\|_{X}+\|\varrho^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+{\|\vartheta^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}<\infty}\right)^{-2}\ \mbox{if}\ {\bf U}=(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})\in X^{+}.

Finally, set

Q_{i,{\bf k}}({\bf U})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}G({\bf U})\ \mbox{if}\ i=1,\\
\\
G({\bf U})F_{{\bf k}}({\bf U})\ \mbox{if}\ i=2.\end{array}\right.

Observe that
Q_{i,{\bf k}}\in\mathcal{C}
,
i=1,2
,
{\bf k}\in Z^{5}
.

Proposition 3.1

(
Topology on the phase space
X^{+}_{\infty}
).

Let

d({\bf U};{\bf V})=\sum_{i=1,2,{\bf k}\in Z^{5}}\exp(-|{\bf k}|)\frac{\left|Q_{i,{\bf k}}({\bf U})-Q_{i,{\bf k}}({\bf V})\right|}{1+\left|Q_{i,{\bf k}}({\bf U})-Q_{i,{\bf k}}({\bf V})\right|}.

(3.2)

Then
d
defines a metric on
X^{+}_{\infty}
and
(X^{+}_{\infty};d)
is a Polish space. In addition,
the class
\mathcal{C}
coincides with
BC(X^{+}_{\infty})
– the space of bounded continuous functions on
X^{+}_{\infty}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note carefully that the topology on the space
X^{+}_{\infty}

is not
a one point compactification of the space
X^{+}
in the sense of Alexandroff.

Proof.

Step 1:

To see

First observe

Thus if
{\bf U}\neq 0

, then necessarily
{\bf V}\neq 0
, and

for all

, which yields the desired conclusion.

Step 2:

Next, we show the equivalence

(3.3)

Indeed

G({\bf U}_{n})\to G({\bf U})=\left(1+\|{\bf U}\|_{X}+\|\varrho^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+{\|\vartheta^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\right)^{-2}>0

yields

for all

large enough. In particular,

\left(1+\|{\bf U}_{n}\|_{X}+\|\varrho^{-1}_{n}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+{\|\vartheta_{n}^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\right)^{-2}\to\left(1+\|{\bf U}\|_{X}+\|\varrho^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+{\|\vartheta^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\right)^{-2},

in other words

\|{\bf U}_{n}\|_{X}+\|\varrho^{-1}_{n}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+{\|\vartheta_{n}^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\to\|{\bf U}\|_{X}+\|\varrho^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+{\|\vartheta^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}.

(3.4)

Thus it follows

F_{{\bf k}}({\bf U}_{n})\to F_{{\bf k}}({\bf U})\ \mbox{for any}\ {\bf k}\in Z^{5},

(3.5)

which, together with (
3.4
), yields

{\bf U}_{n}\to{\bf U}\ \mbox{weakly in}\ X.

(3.6)

Since the phase space
X

we deduce, using (

The reverse implication in (

Step 3:

We show the equivalence

\displaystyle d({\bf U}_{n};{\bf U})

\displaystyle\to 0,\ {\bf U}=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mbox{for any}\ M

\displaystyle>0\ \mbox{there exists}\ n(M)\ \mbox{such that for all}\ n\geq n(M)

\displaystyle=0\ \mbox{or}\ \|{\bf U}_{n}\|_{X}+\|\varrho^{-1}_{n}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+{\|\vartheta^{-1}_{n}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}>M

Indeed

Step 4:

On the one hand, given (

Step 5:

Next we show that the metric space

{\bf U}_{n(k)}\in X^{+},\ \|{\bf U}_{n(k)}\|_{X}+\|\varrho^{-1}_{n(k)}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+{\|\vartheta^{-1}_{n(k)}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}}\to Y\in(0,\infty).

(3.9)

(3.10)

It is easy to check that the mapping

t\in[0,\infty)\mapsto{\bf U}[{\bf U}_{0};f;p](t,\cdot)\in X^{+}_{\infty}

(3.11)

is continuous with values in
X_{\infty}^{+}

endowed with the metric
d
introduced in (
3.2
) for any admissible data.

3.3
Continuity of the solution operator with respect to the data

We examine the continuity of the extended operator with respect to the data
D_{n}
. Consider

D_{n}\to D\ \mbox{in}\ D_{X,F,P}

with the corresponding solution

{\bf U}[D_{n}](t,\cdot)\ \mbox{at a specific time}\ t>0.

Suppose first that
t<T_{\rm max}[D].
Then, in view of Theorem
2.1
,

{\bf U}[D_{n}](t,\cdot)\to{\bf U}[D](t,\cdot)\ \mbox{in}\ C(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{5}).

(3.12)

3.4
Autonomous problem and the semigroup property

Suppose that the functions
{\bf g}
and
Q
are independent of time. Accordingly, the data space can be written
as

\displaystyle D_{X,F,P}=X\times F\times P,\ X

\displaystyle=W^{1,q}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\times W^{2,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\times W^{2,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3}),

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{3}),\ P=R^{4}

Now, it is easy to check that the operator
{\bf U}
defined through (
3.10
) enjoys the semigroup property:

{\bf U}[{\bf U}_{0};f;p](t+s,\cdot)={\bf U}\Big[{\bf U}[{\bf U}_{0};f;p](s,\cdot);f;p\Big](t,\cdot),\ s,t\geq 0.

(3.13)

3.5
Statistical solutions

Suppose we are given a complete Borel probability measure
\mathcal{V}
on the data space
D_{X,F,P}=X^{+}\times F\times P
.
We define the push forward measure

\int_{X^{+}_{\infty}}\mathcal{F}({\bf U}_{0}){\rm d}\mathcal{V}_{t}({\bf U}_{0})=\int_{D_{X,F,P}}\mathcal{F}({\bf U}[D](t,\cdot))\ {\rm d}\mathcal{V}(D)\ \mbox{for any}\ \mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{C},

(3.14)

where
{\bf U}
is the extended solution operator defined by (
3.10
). Note that

\displaystyle\int_{X^{+}_{\infty}}\mathcal{F}({\bf U}_{0}){\rm d}\mathcal{V}_{t}({\bf U}_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{X,F,P}}\mathcal{F}({\bf U}[D](t,\cdot))\ {\rm d}\mathcal{V}(D)

\displaystyle\int_{D_{X,F,P}}\mathcal{F}({\bf U}[D](t,\cdot))\mathds{1}_{t<T_{\rm max}[D]}{\rm d}\mathcal{V}(D)+\mathcal{F}(0)\mathcal{V}\{t\geq T_{\rm max}\}.

(3.15)

Thus
(\mathcal{V}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a family of Borel probability measures on
X^{+}_{\infty}
with
\mathcal{V}_{0}=\Pi_{\mathcal{X}^{+}_{\infty}}\mathcal{V}
.

Definition 3.3

(
Statistical solution).

The family of Markov operators

\mathcal{M}_{t}:\mathfrak{P}[D_{X,F,P}]\to\mathfrak{P}[X^{+}_{\infty}],

defined as

\mathcal{M}_{t}(\mathcal{V})=\int_{X^{+}\times F\times P}\delta_{{\bf U}[D](t,\cdot)}{\rm d}\mathcal{V}(D),\ t\geq 0

for any
\mathcal{V}
supported by admissible data is called

statistical solution

of the NSF system.

In addition, the Markov operators
\mathcal{M}_{t}
are regular with the
dual operators

\mathcal{M}^{*}_{t}:BC(X^{+}_{\infty})\to\mathcal{B}(D_{X,F,P})\ \mbox{(Borel functions on the data space)}

\mathcal{M}^{*}_{t}:\mathcal{F}\mapsto\Big[D\mapsto\mathcal{F}({\bf U}[D](t,\cdot))\Big].

There holds

\int_{X^{+}_{\infty}}\mathcal{F}\ {\rm d}\mathcal{M}_{t}(\mathcal{V})=\int_{D_{X,F,T}}\mathcal{M}^{*}_{t}(\mathcal{F})\ {\rm d}\mathcal{V},\ t\geq 0.

Remark 3.4
.

Note that
\mathcal{M}_{t}(\mathcal{V})\{\mathbf{U}_{\infty}\}=\mathcal{V}\{t>T_{\rm max}\},
meaning the measure of the data set
for which the solution blows up before reaching
t
..

Remark 3.5
.

Conformally with the existing theory of
stationary statistical solutions
(see e.g.

[
16
]
), one can define the statistical solutions as measures on the
trajectory space

C([0,\infty);X^{+}_{\infty}),

\mathcal{M}_{t}(\mathcal{V})=\int_{X^{+}\times F\times P}\delta_{{\bf U}[D]}\ {\rm d}\mathcal{V}(D)\in\mathfrak{P}[C([0,\infty);X^{+}_{\infty})].

3.5.1
Disintegration, autonomous problem, semigroup property

We focus on the situation described in Section
3.4
, where the forcing terms
{\bf g}
and
Q
are independent of
t
.
First we use the Disintegration Theorem to write

\int_{D_{X,F,P}}\mathcal{F}(D)\ {\rm d}\mathcal{V}(D)=\int_{F\times P}\left(\int_{X}\mathcal{F}({\bf U}_{0};f;p){\rm d}[\mathcal{V}|(f,p)]({\bf U}_{0})\right){\rm d}\Pi_{F\times P}\mathcal{V}(f,p)

where
\mathcal{V}^{f,p}=\mathcal{V}|(f,p)
is a family of Borel probability measures on
X
.
Given
(f,p)\in F\times P
, the push forward measure
\mathcal{V}^{f,p}_{t}
is defined on data phase space
X^{+}_{\infty}
as

\displaystyle\int_{X^{+}_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}({\bf U}_{0})\ {\rm d}\mathcal{V}^{f,p}_{t}({\bf U}_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\mathcal{F}\Big({\bf U}[{\bf U}_{0};f;p](t,\cdot)\Big)\mathds{1}_{\{t<T_{\rm max}[{\bf U}_{0};f;p]\}}\ {\rm d}[\mathcal{V}|(f,p)]({\bf U}_{0})+\mathcal{F}(0)\mathcal{V}\{t\geq T_{\rm max}\}.

(3.16)

for any
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{C}
.

It follows from the semigroup property of the solution operator established in Section
3.4
that the exists a semigroup of Markov operators

(\mathcal{M}^{f,p}_{t})_{t\geq 0}:\mathfrak{P}[X^{+}_{\infty}]\to\mathfrak{P}[X^{+}_{\infty}],\ \mathcal{V}^{f,p}_{t}=\mathcal{M}^{f,p}_{t}([\mathcal{V}|(f,p)]).

Finally, we assume that the initial data and the forcing/parameter data are independent, meaning

[\mathcal{V}|(f,p)]=\Pi_{X}\mathcal{V},\ \mathcal{V}=\Pi_{X}\mathcal{V}\otimes\Pi_{F\times P}\mathcal{V}.

This leads to the following result:

Theorem 3.6

(
Autonomous problem, semigroup property).

Suppose that
{\bf g}
,
Q
are independent of
t
and that the measure
\mathcal{V}
defined on the data space

D_{X,F,P}=X\times F\times P
and supported by admissible data decomposes as

\mathcal{V}=\Pi_{X}\mathcal{V}\otimes\Pi_{F\times P}\mathcal{V}.

Then the statistical solution introduced in Definition
3.3
can be written in the form

\mathcal{M}_{t}(\mathcal{V})=\int_{F\times P}\mathcal{M}^{f,p}_{t}(\Pi_{X}\mathcal{V})\ {\rm d}\Pi_{F\times P}\mathcal{V}(f,p),\ t\geq 0,

(3.17)

where
(\mathcal{M}^{f,p}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a semigroup of Markov operators:

•

\mathcal{M}^{f,p}_{t}:\mathfrak{P}[X^{+}_{\infty}]\to\mathfrak{P}[X^{+}_{\infty}],

•

\mathcal{M}^{f,p}_{t}(\delta_{{\bf U}_{0}})=\delta_{{\bf U}[{\bf U}_{0},f,p](t,\cdot)},\ t\geq 0,

•

\mathcal{M}^{f,p}_{0}(\nu)=\nu,\ \mathcal{M}^{f,p}_{t+s}(\nu)=\mathcal{M}^{f,p}_{t}(\mathcal{M}^{f,p}_{s}(\nu)),\ s,t\geq 0,

•

\mathcal{M}^{f,p}_{t}\left(\sum_{{i=1}}^{n}\lambda_{i}\nu_{i}\right)=\sum_{{i=1}}^{n}\lambda_{i}\mathcal{M}^{f,p}_{t}\left(\nu_{i}\right),\ \lambda_{i}\geq 0,\ \sum_{{i=1}}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1,\ t\geq 0,

•

t\mapsto\mathcal{M}^{f,p}_{t}(\nu)\ \mbox{is continuous in the narrow topology of}\ \mathfrak{P}[X^{+}_{\infty}].

4
Applications, Monte Carlo approximation of statistical solution

We consider the Monte Carlo type approximation of statistical solutions with random initial data leaving the forcing and parameters
(f,p)
deterministic. We suppose that

(\varrho_{0},\vartheta_{0},{\bf u}_{0})
are admissible random initial data - random variables on a probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{B},\mathbb{P})
.

Consider
N
independent identically distributed (i.i.d.) copies of the data

(\varrho_{0},\vartheta_{0},{\bf u}_{0})
denoted

(\varrho_{0}^{n},\vartheta^{n}_{0},{\bf u}^{n}_{0})_{n=1}^{N}
. To each data we associate the extended solution introduced in (
3.10
),
(\varrho^{n},\vartheta^{n},{\bf u}^{n})[\varrho_{0}^{n},\vartheta^{n}_{0},{\bf u}^{n}_{0};f;p](t,\cdot)
– i.i.d. random variables
for any
t\geq 0
.

Suppose that

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\varrho_{0}\,{\rm d}{x}\right]<\infty,\ \mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}|{\bf u}_{0}|^{2}+c_{v}\varrho_{0}\vartheta_{0}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]<\infty,\ -\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\varrho_{0}\log\left(\frac{\vartheta^{c_{v}}_{0}}{\varrho_{0}}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\right]<\infty,

where
\mathbb{E}
stands for expected value. In view of the
a priori
bounds (
1.19
) – (
1.21
),
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\varrho(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\mathds{1}_{t<T_{\rm max}[\varrho_{0},\vartheta_{0},{\bf u}_{0};f;p]}\right]

\displaystyle<\infty,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\varrho{\bf u}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})}\mathds{1}_{t<T_{\rm max}[\varrho_{0},\vartheta_{0},{\bf u}_{0};f;p]}\right]

\displaystyle<\infty,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left\|\varrho\log\left(\frac{\vartheta^{c_{v}}}{\varrho}\right)(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\mathds{1}_{t<T_{\rm max}[\varrho_{0},\vartheta_{0},{\bf u}_{0};f;p]}\right]

\displaystyle<\infty.

(4.1)

Applying the Banach space variant of Strong Law of Large Numbers, see Ledoux, Talagrand
[
20
, Chapter 7, Corollary 7.10]
, we conclude

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varrho^{n}(t,\cdot)\mathds{1}_{t<T_{\rm max}[\varrho^{n}_{0},\vartheta^{n}_{0},{\bf u}^{n}_{0};f;p]}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{E}\left[\varrho(t,\cdot)\mathds{1}_{t<T_{\rm max}[\varrho_{0},\vartheta_{0},{\bf u}_{0};f;p]}\right]\ \mbox{in}\ L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\ \mathbb{P}\mbox{ a.s.},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varrho^{n}{\bf u}^{n}(t,\cdot)\mathds{1}_{t<T_{\rm max}[\varrho^{n}_{0},\vartheta^{n}_{0},{\bf u}^{n}_{0};f;p]}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{E}\left[\varrho{\bf u}(t,\cdot)\mathds{1}_{t<T_{\rm max}[\varrho_{0},\vartheta_{0},{\bf u}_{0};f;p]}\right]\ \mbox{in}\ L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3};R^{3})\ \mathbb{P}\mbox{ a.s.},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varrho^{n}\log\left(\frac{(\vartheta^{n})^{c_{v}}}{\varrho^{n}}\right)(t,\cdot)\mathds{1}_{t<T_{\rm max}[\varrho^{n}_{0},\vartheta^{n}_{0},{\bf u}^{n}_{0};f;p]}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{E}\left[\varrho\log\left(\frac{\vartheta^{c_{v}}}{\varrho}\right)(t,\cdot)\mathds{1}_{t<T_{\rm max}[\varrho_{0},\vartheta_{0},{\bf u}_{0};f;p]}\right]

\displaystyle\mbox{in}\ L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\ \mathbb{P}\mbox{ a.s.}

(4.2)

To justify the last step in the context of the present theory, we have to observe
that the mapping

\mathcal{G}:(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\varrho,\varrho{\bf u},\varrho\log\left(\frac{\vartheta^{c_{v}}}{\varrho}\right)\right)\ \mbox{if}\ (\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})\in X^{+},\ \vartheta>0,\\
\\
0\ \mbox{if}\ (\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})=(0,0,0)\end{array}\right.

is Borel measurable from
X^{+}_{\infty}
to
L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\times L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\times L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3},R^{3})
.
Indeed if

G_{n}\in BC[0,\infty),\ 0\leq G_{n}\leq 1,\ G_{n}(Y)=Y\ \mbox{for}\ Y\leq n,

then

G_{n}\left(\|{\bf U}\|_{X}+\|\varrho^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\|\vartheta^{-1}\|_{C(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\right)\mathcal{G}({\bf U})\in BC(X^{+}_{\infty};(L^{1})^{3})\to\mathcal{G}({\bf U})\ \mbox{for any}\ {\bf U}\in X^{+}.

Quantum Grassmannians and their Associated Quantum Schubert Varieties at roots of unity
Jason P. Bell, Stéphane Launois, and Alexandra Rogers

Abstract
We study the PI degree of various quantum algebras at roots of unity, including quantum Grassmannians, quantum Schubert varieties, partition subalgebras, and their associated quantum affine spaces. By a theorem of De Concini and Procesi, the PI degree of partition subalgebras and their associated quantum affine spaces is controlled by skew-symmetric integral matrices associated to (Cauchon-Le) diagrams. We prove that the invariant factors of these matrices are always powers of 2. This allows us to compute explicitly the PI degree of partition subalgebras. Our results also apply to certain completely prime (homogeneous) quotients of partition subalgebras. In particular, our results allow us to extend results of Jakobsen and Jøndrup regarding the PI degree of quantum determinantal rings at roots of unity [ JJ01 ] and we present a method to construct an irreducible representation of maximal dimension for quantum determinantal ideals. Building on these results, we use the strong connection between partition subalgebras and quantum Schubert varieties through noncommutative dehomogenisation [ LR08 ] to obtain expressions for the PI degree of quantum Schubert varieties. In particular, we compute the PI degree of quantum Grassmannians.



1
Introduction

The PI degree of a PI algebra is an invariant from which various properties of the algebra can be deduced. For example, Brown and Yakimov
[
BY18
]
showed that, under some mild conditions on a prime PI algebra, knowing its Azumaya locus (an invariant which is linked to the PI degree of the algebra and the PI degree of quotients by maximal ideals) provides valuable information about its discriminant ideal (another invariant with applications in the study of automorphism groups of PI algebras (
[
CPWZ16
]
and
[
CPWZ15
]
)). The PI degree also plays an important role in the representation theory of prime affine PI algebras, giving an upper bound on the dimension of irreducible representations (
[
BG02
, Theorem I.13.5]
). Our present aim is to provide explicit computation of the PI degree of various quantum algebras at roots of unity including quantum Grassmannians, quantum Schubert varieties, partition subalgebras, and associated quantum affine spaces.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an arbitrary field and
1\neq q\in\mathbb{K}^{*}
be a field element. We will specify where we require
q
to be a primitive
\ell^{\text{th}}
root of unity, with
\ell>2
.
We start with the standard (single parameter) quantised coordinate ring of
m\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{K}
, denoted
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
. This is a
\mathbb{K}
-algebra generated by
mn
indeterminates
(X_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{m,n}
subject to some commutation relations which will be recapped in Section
2
. For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{m})
a partition with
n\geq\lambda_{1}
and with associated Young diagram denoted by
Y_{\lambda}
, the partition subalgebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})
of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
is the subalgebra of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
generated by the generators
X_{i,j}
fitting into
Y_{\lambda}
. In particular,
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
is itself a partition subalgebra associated to partition
(n,\dots,n)=n^{m}
. These algebras have appeared naturally in the study of so-called quantum Grassmannians (see for instance
[
LR08
,
LLR08
,
LLN
]
).

By taking the
\mathbb{K}
-algebra generated by all maximal quantum minors of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
we obtain a quantum analogue of the homogeneous coordinate ring of the Grassmannian of the
m
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
. This is called the
quantum Grassmannian
and is denoted
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
. The maximal quantum minors of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
are referred to as
quantum Plücker coordinates
, and the set of quantum Plücker coordinates of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
is denoted by
\Pi_{m,n}
. There is a natural partial order on
\Pi_{m,n}
(see (
1
)) which allows to attach to each quantum Plücker coordinate
\gamma
the so-called quantum Schubert variety
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\gamma}
associated to
\gamma
. This is the quotient of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
by the Schubert ideal associated to
\gamma
, that is, the quotient by the ideal generated by
\Pi_{\gamma}:=\{\alpha\in\Pi_{m,n}|~\alpha\ngeq\gamma\}
. This algebra is a noncommutative deformation of the homogeneous coordinate ring of the Schubert variety associated to the (classical) Plücker coordinate
\gamma
. Note that
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
is itself a quantum Schubert variety (associated to
\gamma=[1,\dots,m]
).

All these algebras are related by noncommutative dehomogenisation
[
LR08
, Theorem 3.1.6]
: the quantum Schubert variety associated to
\gamma
is a noetherian domain and
\overline{\gamma}
is a nonzero normal element of this algebra. This allows us to form the localised ring
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\gamma}[\overline{\gamma}^{-1}]
. The dehomogenisation isomorphism of Lenagan and Rigal then shows that this localisation is a skew-Laurent extension of a partition algebra. More precisely, we have:

\mathcal{O}_{q^{-1}}(Y_{\lambda})[y^{\pm 1};\sigma]\cong\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\gamma}[\overline{\gamma}^{-1}],

where
\sigma
denotes the automorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{q^{-1}}(Y_{\lambda})
defined by
\sigma(X_{i,j})=qX_{i,j}
for all
(i,j)
in the Young diagram of
\lambda
, where
\lambda
is the partition naturally associated to
\gamma
(see Subsection
2.3
for more details).

When
q
is a root of unity, all these algebras are PI, and this dehomogenisation isomorphism provides a way to compute the PI degree of quantum Schubert varieties. First, we should compute the PI-degree of quantum partition subalgebras and then study the effect of a skew-Laurent extension on the PI degree. This is roughly the strategy we will be using in this paper. Before we explain our results, let us introduce more details.

Partition subalgebras can be presented as iterated Ore extensions over
\mathbb{K}
with the indeterminates
X_{i,j}
fitting into the Young diagram of
\lambda
added in the lexicographic order:

\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})=\mathbb{K}[X_{1,1}]\cdots[X_{1,\lambda_{1}};\sigma_{1,\lambda_{1}},\delta_{1,\lambda_{1}}]\dots[X_{m,\lambda_{m}};\sigma_{m,\lambda_{m}},\delta_{m,\lambda_{m}}],

for suitable automorphisms
\sigma_{i,j}
and
\sigma_{i,j}
-skew derivations
\delta_{i,j}
. We note that each automorphism
\sigma_{i,j}
acts by multiplication by powers of
q
on the generators
X_{u,v}
with
(u,v)<_{\mathrm{lex}}(i,j)
. The behaviour of these algebras depend very much on the choice of the parameter
q
. Indeed, it follows from
[
Hay08
, Corollary 4.7]
that
\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})
is PI if and only if
q
is a root of unity.

For the rest of this introduction, we assume that
q
is a primitive
\ell^{\text{th}}
root of unity, with
\ell>2
, so that
\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})
is a PI algebra. In this case, the PI degree of the iterated Ore extension above does not depend on the skew-derivations
\delta_{i,j}
by
[
Hay08
, Corollary 4.7]
. In other words,

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda}))={\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathbb{K}[T_{1,1}]\cdots[T_{1,\lambda_{1}};\sigma_{1,\lambda_{1}}]\dots[T_{m,\lambda_{m}};\sigma_{m,\lambda_{m}}]),

with slightly abusive notation. We denote the iterated Ore extension on the right-hand side by
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})}
. This algebra
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})}
is a (uniparameter) quantum affine space, that is, it is the
\mathbb{K}
-algebra generated by the
T_{i,j}
subject to
T_{i,j}T_{u,v}=q^{a_{(i,j),(u,v)}}T_{u,v}T_{i,j}
for all
(i,j),(u,v)
, where the
a_{(i,j),(u,v)}
are integers coming from the automorphisms
\sigma_{i,j}
. We denote by
M_{\lambda}
the integral skew-symmetric matrix associated to this quantum affine space, that is,
M_{\lambda}=(a_{(i,j),(u,v)})\in M_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
, where the rows and columns of
M_{\lambda}
are indexed by
(i,j)\in Y_{\lambda}
ordered in the lexicographic order.

The above discussion shows that the PI degree of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})
is equal to the PI degree of the (uniparameter) quantum affine space associated to the matrix
M_{\lambda}
. The PI degree of quantum affine spaces at roots of unity has been studied in
[
CP93
]
, where it was shown that this PI degree is controlled by the invariant factors of the skew-symmetric integral matrix
M_{\lambda}
. A priori, computing invariant factors can prove a difficult task. In the present situation, matrix
M_{\lambda}
can be obtained from a combinatorial gadget called
(Cauchon-Le) diagrams
. These objects have naturally appeared in the study of the primitive spectrum of the quantum Grassmannian on one hand
[
LLR08
]
and the cell decomposition of the totally nonnegative Grassmannian on the other hand
[
Pos06
]
. Our first main result asserts that the invariant factors of any matrix associated to a diagram (in the sense of Section
3.2
) are always powers of
2
. Combining with results from
[
BCL12
]
where the dimension of the kernel of
M_{\lambda}
was computed (in terms of the so-called toric permutation associated to a given diagram–see Section
3.3
), we obtain a closed formula for the PI degree of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})
as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Assume that
q
is a primitive
\ell^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity with
\ell
odd. Then

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda}))=\ell^{\frac{N-r}{2}},

where
N=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}
is the number of boxes in
Y_{\lambda}
and
r
is the number of odd cycles in the disjoint cycle decomposition of the toric permutation associated to
\lambda
.

In the case of the partition
\lambda=n^{n}
, we retrieve that the PI degree of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
is equal to
\ell^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}
. This result was first obtained by Jakobsen and Zhang in
[
JZ97
]
with the assumption that
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
, and then reproved in
[
Jon00
]
and
[
JJ01
]
.

We note that our results are not only applicable to partition subalgebras, but also to their completely prime quotients by
Cauchon ideals
in the sense of
[
LLR
, Definition 3.20]
. We illustrate this in the specific case of quantum determinantal rings, that is, the quotient of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{n}(\mathbb{K}))
by the ideal generated by all quantum minors of a given size. More precisely, our results allow us to compute the PI degree of quantum determinantal ideals, thus extending
[
JJ01
, Proposition 6.2]
which was established with the assumption that the base field has characteristic
0
. We also construct an explicit irreducible representation of maximal dimension (equal to the PI degree) for all quantum determinantal rings.

Now that we have a good grasp on the PI degree of partition subalgebras, we turn our attention quantum Schubert varieties. Quantum Schubert varieties are PI precisely when
q
is a root of unity thanks to
[
Hay08
]
, and so we continue to assume that
q
is a primitive
\ell^{\text{th}}
root of unity, with
\ell>2
. In view of the dehomogenisation isomorphism above and
[
Hay08
, Corollary 4.7]
, the computation of the PI degree of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\gamma}
boils down to computing the dimension of the kernel and the invariant factors of the skew-symmetric matrix
M_{\gamma}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M_{\lambda}&{\bf 1}\\
{\bf-1}^{T}&0\end{array}\right)
with
{\bf 1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{m}}
being a column vector consisting of
1
in each entry and with
{\bf-1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{m}}
being a row vector consisting of
-1
in each entry. This naturally leads us to compare the invariant factors of an integral skew-symmetric matrix
M
with the invariant factors of the matrix
M^{E}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M&{\bf 1}\\
{\bf-1}^{T}&0\end{array}\right)
. We refer to the matrix
M^{E}
as the
extended matrix
of
M
. Interestingly, computing the invariant factors of
M_{\lambda}^{E}
is trickier than computing those of
M_{\lambda}
. For instance, invariant factors of
M^{E}
do not need to be powers of
2
anymore. However, by a careful combinatorial study, we prove the following result:

Theorem 2
.

Let
q
be a primitive
\ell^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity such that the smallest prime factor
\ell_{0}
of
\ell
satisfies
\ell_{0}>\min\{m,n,2\}
.

Let
\gamma=[\gamma_{1}<\dots<\gamma_{m}]
be a quantum Plücker coordinate of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
and let
\lambda
be the partition associated to
\gamma
. We denote by
N
the number of boxes in
Y_{\lambda}
and by
r
the number of odd cycles for the toric permutation associated to
\lambda
.

Then the PI degree of the quantum Schubert variety
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\gamma}
is given by

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\gamma}\right)=\begin{cases}\ell^{\frac{N-r}{2}}&\text{ if $\ker(M_{\lambda})\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}$};\\
\ell^{\frac{N-r}{2}+1}&\text{ if $\ker(M_{\lambda})\nsubseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}$}.\end{cases}

It would be interesting to characterise when
\ker(M_{\lambda})\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}
via
\gamma
or
\lambda
. We are able to do so in the case of the quantum Grassmannian. The result suggests that this might indeed be an interesting combinatorial question.

If
i
is a positive integer greater than or equal to
2
, we denote by
\mu_{2}(i)
the 2-adic valuation of
i
; that is, the largest integer
j
such that
2^{j}
divides
i
. Then we have:

Theorem 3
.

Let
q
be a primitive
\ell^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity such that the smallest prime factor
\ell_{0}
of
\ell
satisfies
\ell_{0}>\min\{m,n,2\}
. Then the PI degree of the quantum Schubert variety
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
is given by

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))\right)=\begin{cases}\ell^{\frac{m(n-m)}{2}}&\text{ if }\mu_{2}(m)\neq\mu_{2}(n);\\
\ell^{\frac{m(n-m)-\gcd(m,n)}{2}+1}&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce all the algebras involved and we use results of Haynal
[
Hay08
]
to reduce the computation of their PI degree to the computation of the PI degree of a (uniparameter) quantum affine space. In Section
3
, we explain De Concini-Procesi’s strategy to compute the PI degree of a quantum affine space through the invariant factors of their associated integral skew-symmetric matrix
[
CP93
]
and proceed to prove that these invariant factors are always powers of
2
for integral skew-symmetric matrices coming from a diagram. This allows us to compute the PI degree of partition subalgebras (see Theorem
3.8
) and quantum determinantal rings (see Theorem
3.13
). Section
4
is devoted to the construction of an irreducible representation of maximal dimension for quantum determinantal rings. Finally, in Section
5
, we compare invariant factors of an integral skew-symmetric matrix and its associated extended matrix. This allows us to prove Theorems
2
and
3
.

2.1
Quantum matrices and partition subalgebras

We denote the quantised coordinate ring of

matrices over
\mathbb{K}
by
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))

\mathbb{K}
-algebra generated by

indeterminates
\{X_{i,j}\}_{i,j=1}^{m,n}
subject to the following relations: for
(1,1)\leq(i,j)<(s,t)\leq(m,n)

\displaystyle X_{i,j}X_{s,t}=\begin{cases}X_{s,t}X_{i,j}&i<s,\,j>t;\\

The

D_{q}=\sum_{\pi\in S_{n}}(-q)^{\ell(\pi)}X_{\pi(1),1}\cdots X_{\pi(n),n},

For a pair of subsets

2.2

The

2.4
Partition subalgebras and quantum Schubert varieties at roots of unity

Recall that
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
can be written as an iterated Ore extension of the form:

\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))=\mathbb{K}[X_{11}][X_{12};\sigma_{12},\delta_{12}]\cdots[X_{mn};\sigma_{mn},\delta_{mn}],

where the indeterminates
X_{i,j}
are added in the lexicographic order, and where the
\sigma_{ij}
are automorphisms and the
\delta_{ij}
are left
\sigma_{ij}
-derivations of the appropriate subalgebras.

With a slightly abusive notation, the partition subalgebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})
associated to the partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{m})
with
n\geq\lambda_{1}
can also be written as an iterated Ore extention as follows:

\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})=\mathbb{K}[X_{11}][X_{12};\sigma_{12},\delta_{12}]\cdots[X_{mn};\sigma_{mn},\delta_{mn}],

where the indeterminates
X_{i,j}
with
(i,j)
fitting in the Young diagram
Y_{\lambda}
are added in the lexicographic order. Note that these automorphisms
\sigma_{i,j}
and skew-derivations
\delta_{i,j}
are in general distinct from the ones above. However, we kept the same notation to emphasize the fact that they are defined on the previous generators in the same way in both cases.

Assume
q
is a primitive
\ell^{\mbox{th}}
root of unity. Under this assumption, it follows from
[
Hay08
, Theorem 1.2]
that
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
is a PI algebra. The fact that
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
satisfies the required assumptions to apply
[
Hay08
, Theorem 1.2]
follows from
[
Hay08
, Theorem 2.8]
. More generally, the same arguments can be used to prove that
\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})
is a PI algebra.

It then easily follows from Theorem
2.4
that quantum Schubert varieties are also PI algebra. Note that quantum Schubert algebras are actually PI only under the assumption that
q
is a root of unity.

Moreover, it follows from Theorem
2.4
that:

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{-1}}(Y_{\lambda})[y;\sigma])={\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\gamma}).

In this article, we are interested in computing the PI degree of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})
and
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\gamma}
. It follows from
[
Hay08
, Theorem 1.2]
that

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda}))={\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathbb{K}[T_{11}][T_{12};\sigma_{12}]\cdots[T_{mn};\sigma_{mn}]),

where the indeterminates
T_{i,j}
with
(i,j)
fitting in the Young diagram
Y_{\lambda}
are added in the lexicographic order. We denote this algebra
\mathbb{K}[T_{11}][T_{12};\sigma_{12}]\cdots[T_{mn};\sigma_{mn}]
by
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})}
. Note that once again we have used slightly abusive notation for the automorphisms
\sigma_{i,j}
. These are defined by

\sigma_{i,j}(T_{s,t})=\begin{cases}T_{s,t}&i<s,\,j>t;\\
qT_{s,t}&(i=s,\,j<t)\text{ or }(i<s,\,j=t);\\
T_{s,t}&i<s,\,j<t,\end{cases}

for all
(s,t)<_{\mbox{lex}}(i,j)
such that
(s,t),(i,j)\in Y_{\lambda}
.

We note that the algebra
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})}=\mathbb{K}[T_{11}][T_{12};\sigma_{12}]\cdots[T_{mn};\sigma_{mn}]
is a quantum affine space. Recall that the (uniparameter) quantum affine space
\mathcal{O}_{q^{M}}(\mathbb{K}^{N})
associated to the integral skew-symmetric matrix
M=(a_{i,j})\in M_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
is the
\mathbb{K}
-algebra generated
T_{1},\dots,T_{N}
subject to
T_{i}T_{j}=q^{a_{i,j}}T_{j}T_{i}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq N
.

The above discussion shows that

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda}))={\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{M_{\lambda}}}[T_{1},\dots,T_{N}]),

where
N=\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{m}
is the number of boxes in the Young diagram
Y_{\lambda}
and
M_{\lambda}\in M_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
is the skew-symmetric integral matrix defined as follows: Label the boxes of
Y_{\lambda}
from
1
to
N
in such a way that the labels increase along the rows (from left to right) and down the columns (from top to bottom). Given such a labelling, we construct
M_{\lambda}
according the rule

M_{\lambda}[i,j]=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if square }i\text{ is strictly below or strictly to the right of square }j;\\
-1&\text{ if square }i\text{ is strictly above or strictly to the left of square }j;\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We are now ready to summarise the main result of this section.

Proposition 2.5
.

Assume
q
is a primitive
\ell^{\mbox{th}}
root of unity.

1.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{m})
be a partition. Then
\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda})
is a PI algebra and its PI degree is given by:

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q}(Y_{\lambda}))={\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{M_{\lambda}}}(\mathbb{K}^{N})),

where
M_{\lambda}
is the matrix deduced from
M
by deleting rows and columns indexed by
(i,j)
not fitting in
Y_{\lambda}
.

2.

Let
\gamma
be an indexed set. Then the quantum Schubert variety
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\lambda}
is a PI algebra and

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\lambda})={\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{M_{\gamma}}}(\mathbb{K}^{N})),

where
M_{\gamma}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M_{\lambda}&{\bf 1}\\
{\bf-1}^{T}&0\end{array}\right)
with

being a column vector consisting of

in each entry and with
{\bf-1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{m}}
being a row vector consisting of
-1
in each entry.

In the next section, we develop new results to compute the PI degree of quantum affine spaces attached to matrices coming from (Cauchon-Le) diagrams. This will allow us to give an explicit formula for

thanks to Proposition

3
PI degree of quantum affine spaces associated to diagrams

3.1
The PI Setting

First, we present a general result relating the PI degree of an arbitrary quantum affine space
\mathcal{O}_{q^{M}}(\mathbb{K}^{N})
to properties of its skew-symmetric commutation matrix
M\in M_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
. It is a well-known result that
M
is congruent (in the sense of
[
New72
, Chapter IV]
) to its skew-normal form. We denote the skew normal form by
S
or
\mathrm{Sk}(M)
if it is not clear to which matrix
M
the skew-normal form
S
is congruent, and we write
M\sim_{C}S
. This is a block diagonal matrix of the form

S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&h_{1}&&&&&&&\\
-h_{1}&0&&&&&&&\\
&&0&h_{2}&&&&&\\
&&-h_{2}&0&&&&&\\
&&&&\ddots&&&\\
&&&&&0&h_{s}&\\
&&&&&-h_{s}&0&\\
&&&&&&&\mathbf{0}\end{smallmatrix}\right),

where
\mathbf{0}
is a square matrix of zeros of dimension
\dim(\ker(M))
so that
2s=N-\dim(\ker(M))
, and
h_{1},\,h_{1},\,h_{2},\,h_{2},\,\ldots,\,h_{s},\,h_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
are called the
invariant factors
of
M
. As they always come in pairs, from now on we will avoid repetition and list the invariant factors simply as
h_{1},\,h_{2},\,\ldots,\,h_{s}
. These have the property that
h_{i}|h_{i+1}
for all
i\in\llbracket 1,s\rrbracket
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Take

By
[

, Proposition 7.1]
, the PI degree of this quantum affine space is

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}^{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}^{N}}

\displaystyle{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{M}}(\mathbb{K}^{N}))={\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{M}}((\mathbb{K}^{*})^{N})={\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{S}}((\mathbb{K}^{*})^{N})={\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{S}}(\mathbb{K}^{N})).

.

Let

Definition 4.1
.

An index pair
(I,J)\in\Delta_{n,n}
corresponds to a
final quantum minor

of size

and

or

We denote the set of all final quantum minors of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{n}(\mathbb{K}))
by
\Omega_{n,n}\subseteq\Delta_{n,n}
, and the set of all final quantum minors of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{n}(\mathbb{K}))
of size less than or equal to

by
\Omega_{n,n}^{t}\subseteq\Omega_{n,n}
. Note that all the quantum minors in
\Omega_{n,n}^{t}
“survive” in the quotient algebra

and generate a subalgebra, which we denote by
\mathbb{K}\langle\Omega_{n,n}^{t}\rangle\subseteq R_{t}(M_{n})
. It may also be verified that

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
A=\mathbb{K}\langle\Omega_{n,n}^{t}\rangle
and
\Sigma\subset A
be the multiplicative set generated by
\Omega_{n,n}^{t}
. Then,

(i)

The elements of
\Omega_{n,n}^{t}
commute with each other up to powers of
q
;

(ii)

A\subseteq R_{t}(M_{n})\subseteq A\Sigma^{-1}
;

(iii)

A
is a quantum affine space
\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}})
, for some
M^{\prime}\in M_{2nt-t^{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
, and
A\Sigma^{-1}
is the quantum torus associated to
A
;

(iv)

M^{\prime}\in M_{2nt-t^{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
has rank
2nt-t^{2}-t
and all its invariant factors are powers of
2
.

Proof.

(i)

This result follows from an analogous result for initial quantum minors of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{n}(\mathbb{K}))
: a quantum minor
[I|J]\in\Delta_{n,n}
is called
initial
if
I=\{i,i+1,\ldots,i+t\}
and
J=\{j,j+1,\ldots,j+t\}
, for some
t\in\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket
, and
1\in I\cup J
. Noting that all pairs of initial quantum minors
[I|J],\,[M|N]
satisfying
i=m=1
are
weakly separated
in the sense of Leclerc and Zelevinsky
[
LZ98
]
, we may apply their result
[
LZ98
, Lemma 2.1]
to see that the
[I|J],\,[M|N]
above quasi-commute (that is, commute up to a power of
q
). Applying the transpose automorphism
\tau_{q}^{1}
of
[
PW91
, Proposition 3.7.1(1)]
reveals that any pair of initial quantum minors
[I^{\prime}|J^{\prime}],\,[M^{\prime}|N^{\prime}]
satisfying
j^{\prime}=n^{\prime}=1
also quasi-commute, as is shown in
[
Goo06
, (4-13)]
. Finally,
[
Goo06
, Corollary 6.5]
shows that pairs of initial quantum minors
[I|J],\,[M^{\prime}|N^{\prime}]
satisfying
i=n^{\prime}=1
quasi-commute and hence all initial quantum minors of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{n}(\mathbb{K}))
quasi-commute with each other. This result passes to the final quantum minors of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(M_{n}(\mathbb{K}))
, and hence to all elements of
\Omega_{n,n}^{t}
, by use of the anti-automorphism
\tau_{q}^{2}
of
[
PW91
, Proposition 3.7.1(2)]
and the property found in
[
PW91
, Lemma 4.3.1]
.

(ii)

A\subseteq R_{t}(M_{n})
is immediate. We deduce from part (i) that the elements of
\Omega_{n,n}^{t}
are normal in
A
. They are also regular, since
R_{t}(M_{n})
is a noetherian domain. Therefore,
\Sigma\subseteq A
is an Ore set at which we can localise. We now show that each generator of
R_{t}(M_{n})
is contained in
A\Sigma^{-1}
:

Define sets
Q_{1}:=\llbracket n-t+1,n\rrbracket\times\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket,\;Q_{2}:=\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket\times\llbracket n-t+1,n\rrbracket
, and
Q:=Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}
. To prove
R_{t}(M_{n})\subseteq A\Sigma^{-1}
we first use decreasing induction on
(i,j)\in Q
(for the lexicographic order) to show that
\bar{X}_{i,j}\in A\Sigma^{-1}
, where
\bar{X}_{i,j}\in R_{t}(M_{n})
is the canonical image of the generator
X_{i,j}\in R
. We then show that
\bar{X}_{i,j}\in A\Sigma^{-1}
, for all
(i,j)\in\llbracket 1,n-t\rrbracket\times\llbracket 1,n-t\rrbracket
. This will prove the result for all
(i,j)\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket\times\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
since
\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket\times\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket=Q\cup(\llbracket 1,n-t\rrbracket\times\llbracket 1,n-t\rrbracket)
.

We start with the induction argument. Since
\bar{X}_{i,j}\in A
for all
(i,j)\in(\{n\}\times\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket)\cup(\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket\times\{n\})
, by definition, the first non-trivial
(i,j)\in Q
to prove in the induction is when
(i,j)=(n-1,n-1)
. We take this as our base case.

By the definition of quantum minors, we may write

[\{n-1,n\}|\{n-1,n\}]=\bar{X}_{n-1,n-1}\bar{X}_{n,n}-q\bar{X}_{n-1,n}\bar{X}_{n,n-1}.

Rearranging this we obtain

\bar{X}_{n-1,n-1}=([\{n-1,n\}|\{n-1,n\}]+q\bar{X}_{n-1,n}\bar{X}_{n,n-1})\bar{X}_{n,n}^{-1}\in A\Sigma^{-1}.

Since
[\{n-1,n\}|\{n-1,n\}],\,\bar{X}_{n-1,n},\,\bar{X}_{n,n-1},\,\bar{X}_{n,n}\in A
, and
\bar{X}_{n,n}^{-1}\in A\Sigma^{-1}
, this proves the base case.

For ease of reading we now fix some notation: for some
(i,j)\in Q
, denote by
[I_{i,j}|J_{i,j}]\in\Delta_{n,n}
the final quantum minor which has
i
as its first row index and
j
as its first column index; that is,

I_{i,j}:=\llbracket i,i+\min\{n-i,n-j\}\rrbracket,~~J_{i,j}:=\llbracket j,j+\min\{n-i,n-j\}\rrbracket.

Setting
s_{i,j}:=\min\{n-i,n-j\}
we see that
[I_{i,j}|J_{i,j}]
has size
s_{i,j}+1
and
s_{i,j}+1\in\llbracket 1,t\rrbracket
because
(i,j)\in Q
. Therefore,
[I_{i,j}|J_{i,j}]\in\Omega_{n,n}^{t}\subseteq A
(note that to avoid cumbersome notation we do not distinguished between a quantum minor and its coset). One may also verify that
[I_{i,j}|J_{i,j}]
is generated by sums of monomials in
\bar{X}_{l,k}
, where
(l,k)\geq(i,j)
and
(l,k)\in Q
.

For the inductive step, fix some
(i,j)\in Q
, with
(i,j)<(n-1,n-1)
, and assume that
\bar{X}_{l,k}\in A\Sigma^{-1}
, for all
(i,j)<(l,k)\leq(n,n)
and
(l,k)\in Q
. Consider
\bar{X}_{i,j}
. With the notation above we can rewrite
[I_{i,j}|J_{i,j}]\in A
, using the quantum Laplace relations from
[
NYM93
, Proposition 1.1]
, as

\displaystyle[I_{i,j}|J_{i,j}]=\bar{X}_{i,j}[I_{i+1,j+1}|J_{i+1,j+1}]+\sum_{r=1}^{s_{i,j}}(-q)^{r}\bar{X}_{i,j+r}[I_{i,j}\backslash\{i\}|J_{i,j}\backslash\{j+r\}].

(10)

Since
(i+1,j+1)\in Q
then
[I_{i+1,j+1}|J_{i+1,j+1}]\in\Omega_{n,n}^{t}
and is hence invertible in
A\Sigma^{-1}
. Using the inductive hypothesis we deduce that
[I_{i,j}\backslash\{i\}|J_{i,j}\backslash\{j+r\}]\in A\Sigma^{-1}
, for all
r\in\llbracket 0,s_{i,j}\rrbracket
, as it is the sum of monomials in
\bar{X}_{l,k}
, where
(l,k)>(i,j)
and
(l,k)\in Q
.
Similarly,
\bar{X}_{i,j+r}\in A\Sigma^{-1}
, for all
r\in\llbracket 1,s_{i,j}\rrbracket
, because
(i,j+r)\in Q
and
(i,j+r)>(i,j)
. We may therefore rearrange (
10
) to obtain

\displaystyle\bar{X}_{i,j}=\left([I_{i,j}|J_{i,j}]-\sum_{r=1}^{s_{i,j}}(-q)^{r}\bar{X}_{i,j+r}[I_{i,j}\backslash\{i\}|J_{i,j}\backslash\{j+r\}]\right)[I_{i+1,j+1}|J_{i+1,j+1}]^{-1}\in A\Sigma^{-1}.

This proves the inductive step and we conclude that
\bar{X}_{i,j}\in A\Sigma^{-1}
, for all
(i,j)\in Q
.

For any
(i,j)\in\llbracket 1,n-t\rrbracket\times\llbracket 1,n-t\rrbracket
, define

\hat{I}_{i,j}:=\{i,n-t+1,n-t+2,\ldots,n\},\quad\hat{J}_{i,j}:=\{j,n-t+1,n-t+2,\ldots,n\}

so that
|\hat{I}_{i,j}|=|\hat{J}_{i,j}|=t+1
. Then
[\hat{I}_{i,j}|\hat{J}_{i,j}]=0
in
R_{t}(M_{n})
and hence also in
A
. We may write this as

\displaystyle 0=\bar{X}_{i,j}[\hat{I}_{i,j}\backslash\{i\}|\hat{J}_{i,j}\backslash\{j\}]+\sum_{r=1}^{t}(-q)^{r}\bar{X}_{i,n-t+r}[\hat{I}_{i,j}\backslash\{i\}|\hat{J}_{i,j}\backslash\{n-t+r\}].

(11)

Note that, for all
r\in\llbracket 1,t\rrbracket
, the quantum minor
[\hat{I}_{i,j}\backslash\{i\}|\hat{J}_{i,j}\backslash\{n-t+r\}]
is a sum of monomials in
\bar{X}_{l,k}
, where
l\in\llbracket n-t+1,n\rrbracket
so that
(l,k)\in Q
. By the induction above we deduce that
\bar{X}_{l,k}\in A\Sigma^{-1}
and hence
[\hat{I}_{i,j}\backslash\{i\}|\hat{J}_{i,j}\backslash\{n-t+r\}]\in A\Sigma^{-1}
, for all
r\in\llbracket 1,t\rrbracket
. Furthermore,
[\hat{I}_{i,j}\backslash\{i\}|\hat{J}_{i,j}\backslash\{j\}]=[I_{n-t+1,n-t+1},I_{n-t+1,n-t+1}]\in\Omega_{n,n}^{t}
and is therefore invertible in
A\Sigma^{-1}
. Rearranging (
11
) we obtain

\bar{X}_{i,j}=\left(-\sum_{r=1}^{t}(-q)^{r}\bar{X}_{i,n-t+r}[\hat{I}_{i,j}\backslash\{i\}|\hat{J}_{i,j}\backslash\{n-t+r\}]\right)[\hat{I}_{i,j}\backslash\{i\}|\hat{J}_{i,j}\backslash\{j\}]^{-1}\in A\Sigma^{-1}.

Hence
\bar{X}_{i,j}\in A\Sigma^{-1}
, for all
(i,j)\in\llbracket 1,n-t\rrbracket\times\llbracket 1,n-t\rrbracket
, and, together with the induction proof above, this proves that
R_{t}(M_{n})\subseteq A\Sigma^{-1}
.

(iii)

It was shown in part (i) that elements of
\Omega_{n,n}^{t}
commute with each other up to powers of
q
as determined by a skew-symmetric matrix, which we denote by
M^{\prime}\in M_{2nt-t^{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
. Hence, there is a surjective homomorphism

\displaystyle f:\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}})

\displaystyle{}\longrightarrow A.

(12)

We will use the GK-dimension to show that this surjection is, in fact, an isomorphism.

Claim 1:

\mathrm{GKdim}(A)=\mathrm{GKdim}(R_{t}(M_{n}))=2nt-t^{2}
.

When
q
is not a root of unity then
[
GLL18
, Corollary 4.8]
applied to
R_{t}(M_{n})
, with
J_{w}=\mathcal{I}_{t}
and
w=\llbracket 1,n-t\rrbracket\times\llbracket 1,n-t\rrbracket
, shows that
R_{t}(M_{n})
is Tdeg-stable in the generic case. When
q
is a root of unity then
R_{t}(M_{n})
is a PI algebra which is a noetherian domain. Thus
[
Zha96
, Theorem 5.3]
says that
R_{t}(M_{n})
is also Tdeg-stable in the root of unity case. Taking total rings of fractions of the algebras in part (ii) (possible since all algebras involved are noetherian domains), we obtain

\mathrm{Frac}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{Frac}(R_{t}(M_{n}))\subseteq\mathrm{Frac}(A\Sigma^{-1})=\mathrm{Frac}(A)\quad\implies\quad\mathrm{Frac}(R_{t}(M_{n}))=\mathrm{Frac}(A).

The algebras
A
and
R_{t}(M_{n})
therefore satisfy the conditions of
[
Zha96
, Proposition 3.5(3)]
, which tells us that
\mathrm{GKdim}(A)\geq\mathrm{GKdim}(R_{t}(M_{n}))
. The equality of GK-dimension follows from the basic property that
A\subseteq R_{t}(M_{n})
implies
\mathrm{GKdim}(A)\leq\mathrm{GKdim}(R_{t}(M_{n}))
.

Finally, we see that
\mathrm{GKdim}(R_{t}(M_{n}))=2nt-t^{2}
, using
[
LR08
, Remark 4.2 (iii) & (iv)]
, replacing the
t
with
t+1
to make the result in the paper compatible with our definition of
R_{t}(M_{n})
.

Claim 2:
The map,
f
, in (
12
) is an isomorphism.

From (
12
) and the First Isomorphism Theorem, we have
\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}})/\ker(f)\cong A
and hence

\mathrm{GKdim}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}})/\ker(f)\right)=\mathrm{GKdim}(A)=2nt-t^{2}.

As a consequence of Goldie’s Theorem (see
[
GW04
, Corollary 6.4]
), every nonzero ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}})
contains a regular element. Therefore, if
\ker(f)
were not trivial then, by
[
KL00
, Proposition 3.15]
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{GKdim}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}})/\ker(f)\right)+1

\displaystyle{}\leq\mathrm{GKdim}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}})\right).

(13)

However,
\mathrm{GKdim}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}})\right)=2nt-t^{2}
, by
[
LMO88
, Lemma 2]
, so the inequality in (
13
) becomes
2nt-t^{2}+1\leq 2nt-t^{2}
, which is clearly false. Therefore
\ker(f)
must be trivial and
\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}})\cong A
.

(iv)

In Corollary
3.12
it was shown that
\mathrm{Frac}(R_{t}(M_{n}))\cong\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{\mathcal{M}_{n,t}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}}))
and in Theorem
3.3
and Proposition
3.14
we deduced that
\mathcal{M}_{n,t}
has rank
2nt-t^{2}-t
and that all its invariant factors are powers of
2
. Parts (ii) and (iii) of this lemma imply that
\mathrm{Frac}(R_{t}(M_{n}))=\mathrm{Frac}(A)\cong\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}}))
, therefore

\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}}))\cong\mathrm{Frac}(R_{t}(M_{n}))\cong\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{\mathcal{M}_{n,t}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}})).

Specialising
q
to be a non-root of unity allows us to apply
[
Pan95
, Theorem 2.19]
to this isomorphism, which states that

\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}}))\cong\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{\mathcal{M}_{n,t}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}}))\quad\iff\quad M^{\prime}\sim_{C}\mathcal{M}_{n,t}.

Hence,
M^{\prime}
shares the same invariant factors and rank as
\mathcal{M}_{n,t}
thus proving part (iv) in the case when
q
is not a root of unity. Since the matrix
M^{\prime}
also defines the commutation relations on the quantum affine space
A
when
q
is a root of unity, this also proves part (iv) for any
1\neq q\in\mathbb{K}^{*}
.

∎

For the rest of this section we take
\mathbb{K}
to be algebraically closed as this is required for the application of the Jacobson Density Theorem in Proposition
4.4
(ii). All results that follow in this section will take
q
to be a root of unity, as will be clearly stated.

We start by constructing an irreducible representation of a quantum affine space at a root of unity, with dimension equal to its PI degree. This result is not believed to be new, however, we present a proof of it here to convince the reader that we do indeed get an irreducible representation, as this is used heavily in the results that follow.
The notation of the lemma has been chosen to suit its application in the results to come, in which we will take tensor products of a family of quantum affine planes indexed by
i
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
q
be a primitive
\ell^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity, for some
\ell>1
. Let
\mathbb{K}_{q^{h_{i}}}[x_{i},y_{i}]
denote the quantum affine plane with relations
x_{i}y_{i}=q^{h_{i}}y_{i}x_{i}
, for some
h_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
satisfying
\gcd(h_{i},\ell)=1
. Let
V_{i}
be an
\ell
-dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-vector space with basis
\{v^{(i)}_{1},\ldots,v^{(i)}_{\ell}\}\subseteq V_{i}
and define the map

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}:\mathbb{K}_{q^{h_{i}}}[x_{i},y_{i}]

\displaystyle{}~\longrightarrow~\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(V_{i})

(14)

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle{}~\longmapsto~\varphi_{i}(x_{i})

\displaystyle y_{i}

\displaystyle{}~\longmapsto~\varphi_{i}(y_{i}),

where the endomorphisms
\varphi_{i}(x_{i}),\,\varphi_{i}(y_{i})
act on the basis vectors
v_{j}^{(i)}\in\{v_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,v_{\ell}^{(i)}\}
in the following way:

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(x_{i})\cdot v^{(i)}_{j}

\displaystyle{}=\lambda_{i}q^{(j-1)h_{i}}v^{(i)}_{j},

(15)

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(y_{i})\cdot v^{(i)}_{j}

\displaystyle{}=\begin{cases}v^{(i)}_{j+1},&\text{ if }j\in\llbracket 1,\ell-1\rrbracket;\\
v^{(i)}_{1},&\text{ if }j=\ell,\end{cases}

(16)

for some
\lambda_{i}\in K^{*}
. In particular, we can write
\varphi_{i}(x_{i}),\,\varphi_{i}(y_{i})
as matrices in
M_{\ell}(\mathbb{K})
with respect to this basis:

\varphi_{i}(x_{i})=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda_{i}&&&&\\
&\lambda_{i}q^{h_{i}}&&&\\
&&\lambda_{i}q^{2h_{i}}&&\\
&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&\lambda_{i}q^{(\ell-1)h_{i}}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\qquad\varphi_{i}(y_{i})=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0&\ldots&0&1\\
1&0&\ldots&0&0\\
0&1&\ldots&0&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&\ldots&1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right).

(17)

Then
\varphi_{i}
is a surjective algebra homomorphism which defines an irreducible representation of
\mathbb{K}_{q^{h_{i}}}[x_{i},y_{i}]
on
V
of dimension
\ell
and satisfies the property
\varphi_{i}(x_{i})^{\ell}=\lambda_{i}^{\ell}\Id_{V_{i}}
and
\varphi_{i}(y_{i})^{\ell}=\Id_{V_{i}}
.

Proof.

Using (
15
) and (
16
) it may be verified that
\varphi_{i}(x_{i})\varphi_{i}(y_{i})=q^{h_{i}}\varphi_{i}(y_{i})\varphi_{i}(x_{i})
, so that the relations between generators
x_{i},y_{i}\in\mathbb{K}_{q^{h_{i}}}[x_{i},y_{i}]
are preserved in the image of
\varphi_{i}
. By the universal property of algebras with generators and relations,
\varphi_{i}
then becomes an algebra homomorphism and thus defines a representation of
\mathbb{K}_{q^{h_{i}}}[x_{i},y_{i}]
on
V_{i}
. This gives
V_{i}
a
\mathbb{K}_{q^{h_{i}}}[x_{i},y_{i}]
-module structure with multiplication defined as
rv=\varphi_{i}(r)\cdot v
, for all
r\in\mathbb{K}_{q^{h_{i}}}[x_{i},y_{i}]
and
v\in V
.

To show that
(\varphi_{i},V_{i})
defines an irreducible representation of the quantum affine space, we suppose that there exists a nonzero subspace
W\subseteq V_{i}
which is a sub-representation of
V_{i}
and we argue that
W=V_{i}
. Assume (for contradiction) that
v_{j}^{(i)}\notin W
, for all
j\in\llbracket 1,\ell\rrbracket
, and let
w:=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{j}v^{(i)}_{j}\in W
, with
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}\in\mathbb{K}
, be a nonzero element of
W
which is minimal with respect to the number of nonzero summands. Note that the assumption
v_{j}^{(i)}\notin W
implies that at least two coefficients in
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}\}
are nonzero. Since
W
is a sub-representation then
\varphi_{i}(y_{i})\cdot w\in W
and
\varphi_{i}(x_{i}y_{i})\cdot w\in W
, where

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(y_{i})\cdot w

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{j}v^{(i)}_{j+1}+\alpha_{\ell}v^{(i)}_{1},

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(x_{i}y_{i})\cdot w=\varphi_{i}(x_{i})\cdot(\varphi_{i}(y_{i})\cdot w)

\displaystyle{}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\lambda_{i}q^{jh_{i}}\alpha_{j}v^{(i)}_{j+1}+\lambda_{i}\alpha_{\ell}v^{(i)}_{1}.

We now use these elements of
W
to arrive at a contradiction. If
\alpha_{\ell}\neq 0
then we consider
\varphi_{i}(y_{i}-\lambda_{i}^{-1}x_{i}y_{i})\cdot w\in W
, where

\varphi_{i}(y_{i}-\lambda_{i}^{-1}x_{i}y_{i})\cdot w=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{j}(1-q^{jh_{i}})v^{(i)}_{j+1}.

This is nonzero, since
\ell\nmid jh_{i}
for all
j\in\llbracket 1,\ell-1\rrbracket
, and it has fewer summands than
w
, thus contradicting the minimality of
w
. If, instead,
\alpha_{\ell}=0
and
\alpha_{k}\neq 0
, for some
k\in\llbracket 1,\ell-1\rrbracket
, then we consider the element
\varphi_{i}(y_{i}-q^{-kh_{i}}\lambda_{i}^{-1}x_{i}y_{i})\cdot w
and argue in a similar way to show that this also contradicts the minimality of
w
. Therefore, our assumption is false and there must exist some
j\in\llbracket 1,\ell\rrbracket
such that
v_{j}^{(i)}\in W
. But then
W=V_{i}
, since
\varphi_{i}(y_{i})
permutes all the basis vectors, as can be seen in (
16
). We deduce from this that
(\varphi_{i},V_{i})
is irreducible.

To verify surjectivity of
\varphi_{i}
, note that
V_{i}
is a simple
\mathbb{K}_{q^{h_{i}}}[x_{i},y_{i}]
-module, finite-dimensional over
\mathbb{K}
, and
\mathbb{K}
is algebraically closed. Thus, by Schur’s Lemma,
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}_{q^{h_{i}}}[x_{i},y_{i}]}(V_{i})\cong\mathbb{K}
and we can apply the Jacobson Density Theorem to
R=\mathbb{K}_{q^{h_{i}}}[x_{i},y_{i}]
, with
R^{\prime}=\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}_{q^{h_{i}}}[x_{i},y_{i}]}(V_{i})
, to see that the map
\rho:\mathbb{K}_{q^{h_{i}}}[x_{i},y_{i}]\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(V_{i})
, sending
r
to
f_{r}
, is surjective. Recall that
f_{r}
was defined as
f_{r}(v)=rv
, for all
r\in\mathbb{K}_{q^{h_{i}}}[x_{i},y_{i}]
and
v\in V_{i}
, and this is precisely the definition of
\varphi_{i}(r)
as stated earlier. Hence
\varphi_{i}=\rho
is surjective.

Finally, it is easily verified that
\varphi_{i}(x_{i})^{\ell}=\lambda_{i}^{\ell}\Id_{V_{i}}
and
\varphi_{i}(y_{i})^{\ell}=\Id_{V_{i}}
. This proves the final property in the statement.
∎

Consider a quantum affine space
\mathbb{K}_{q^{M}}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{N}]
for some matrix
M
with a kernel of dimension
t\in\llbracket 0,N-1\rrbracket
and invariant factors
\{h_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}
, where
2s=N-t
. The skew normal form of
M
is
S=EME^{T}\in M_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
, where
E=(e_{i,j})_{i,j}\in M_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
is invertible. We denote the quantum affine space associated to
S
as

D:=\mathbb{K}_{q^{S}}[x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2},\ldots,x_{s},y_{s},z_{1},\ldots,z_{t}].

If
\gcd(h_{i},\ell)=1
, for all
i\in\llbracket 1,s\rrbracket
, then each subalgebra
\mathbb{K}_{q^{h_{i}}}[x_{i},y_{i}]\subseteq D
is a quantum affine plane satisfying the conditions of Lemma
4.3
, hence it must have an irreducible representation
(\varphi_{i},V_{i})
of dimension
\ell
. Let
V:=V_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{s}
so that the dimension of
V
is
\ell^{s}
. Using this notation we state the following proposition:

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
q
be a primitive
\ell^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity, for some
\ell>1
, and
M\in M_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
be a skew-symmetric matrix with invariant factors
h_{1},\ldots,h_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
satisfying
\gcd(h_{i},\ell)=1
, for all
i\in\llbracket 1,s\rrbracket
. The following statements hold:

(i)

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathbb{K}_{q^{M}}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{N}])=\ell^{s}
.

(ii)

There is an algebra homomorphism
\varphi:D\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(V)
which defines an irreducible representation of
D
on
V
of dimension
\ell^{s}
. It is defined using the tensor product of the maps

X_{i,j}\in R_{t}(M_{n})
and
\mathcal{M}_{n,t}^{E}\in M_{2nt-t^{2}+1}(\mathbb{Z})
is the skew-symmetric matrix extended from
\mathcal{M}_{n,t}
.

Proof.

We apply the isomorphism in
[
LR08
, Proof of Proposition 4.3]
, along with
[
LR08
, Remark 4.2(iv)]
, to obtain

R_{t}(M_{n})[y,y^{-1};\phi]\cong\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{n,2n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\gamma}[[\overline{M}]^{-1}],

(24)

where
[M]=[\{n+1,\ldots,3n\}]\in\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{n,2n}(\mathbb{K}))
is a normal element and
[\overline{M}]
is its canonical image in the quotient algebra.

Take
q
to be a root of unity so that
R_{t}(M_{n})
is a PI algebra (as a finite module over a central subalgebra
Z_{0}

[
MR01
, Corollary 13.1.13]
). It is clear from the definition of
\phi
that
y^{\ell}
is a central element and hence that the skew-Laurent extension
R_{t}(M_{n})[y,y^{-1};\phi]
is finite-dimensional over the central subalgebra
\mathbb{K}\langle Z_{0},y^{\ell}\rangle
. Thus it is also a PI algebra. Since the PI degree is invariant under localisation, the isomorphism in (
24
) allows us to deduce

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{n,2n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\gamma})={\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(R_{t}(M_{n})[y;\phi]).

Observing Corollary
3.12
(1) and extending
\mathcal{O}_{q^{\mathcal{M}_{n,t}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}})
by the Ore extension
[y^{\prime};\phi^{\prime}]
, where
\phi^{\prime}(T_{i,j})=q^{-1}T_{i,j}
for all generators
T_{i,j}\in\mathcal{O}_{q^{\mathcal{M}_{n,t}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}})
, we deduce that

\mathrm{Frac}(R_{t}(M_{n})[y;\phi])\cong\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{O}_{q^{\mathcal{M}_{n,t}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}})[y^{\prime};\phi^{\prime}]).

Upon noting the commutation rules of
y^{\prime}
with generators
T_{i,j}
, as defined by the action of
\phi^{\prime}
, we may rewrite the Ore extension
\mathcal{O}_{q^{\mathcal{M}_{n,t}}}(\mathbb{K}^{2nt-t^{2}})[y^{\prime};\phi^{\prime}]
as a quantum affine space in
2nt-t^{2}+1
indeterminates whose commutation matrix is
\mathcal{M}_{n,t}^{E}
. The result of the lemma follows immediately.
∎

By Proposition
2.5
and Lemma
3.1
, the key to compute the PI degree of quantum Schubert varieties is to understand the invariant factors and the dimension of the kernel of
M_{\lambda}^{E}
and
\mathcal{M}_{n,t}^{E}
. Since we know the invariant factors for the matrices
M_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{M}_{n,t}
, we devote the next sections to the study of how these invariants change upon extending the matrix.

5.1
Properties of Arbitrary Extended Matrices

Let
M\in M_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
be an arbitrary skew-symmetric matrix. Let
\{h_{i}(M)\}_{i=1}^{s}
denote the set of (nonzero) invariant factors of
M
and
\{h_{i}(M^{E})\}_{i=1}^{s+1}
denote the set of not necessarily nonzero invariant factors of
M^{E}
, where
h_{i}(M^{E})\neq 0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
and
h_{s+1}(M^{E})
is either 0 or it is the last nonzero invariant factor of
M^{E}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

For a skew-symmetric matrix
M\in M_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
,

\dim(\ker(M^{E}))=\dim(\ker(M))+\epsilon~~\text{ with }~~\epsilon=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\ker(M)\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}\\
-1&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},

where
\mathbf{1}^{\bot}
denotes the orthogonal complement of
\mathbf{1}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

Proof.

Let
W=\{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\mid M\mathbf{w}=0\}=\ker(M)
,
V=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\mid M^{E}\mathbf{v}=0\}=\ker(M^{E})
, and
\mathbf{1}^{\bot}:=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}=0\}
be the orthogonal complement of
\mathbf{1}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

First we prove the identity for
\epsilon=1
. Suppose
W\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}
so that
\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}=0
for all
\mathbf{w}\in W
. Introducing the notation
\mathbf{w}[0]:=(w_{1}w_{2}\ldots w_{N}0)^{T}
for any
\mathbf{w}\in W
we see that
\mathbf{w}[0]\in V
since

M^{E}\cdot\mathbf{w}[0]=\left(\begin{matrix}M&\mathbf{-1}\\
\mathbf{1}^{T}&0\end{matrix}\right)\mathbf{w}[0]=\begin{pmatrix}M\mathbf{w}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf{0}.

This allows us to define an injective map
f:W\rightarrow V;\;\mathbf{w}\mapsto\mathbf{w}[0]
which gives the inequality
\dim(W)\leq\dim(V)-1
, since both
M
and
M^{E}
are skew-symmetric so the parity of the dimensions of their kernels must match the parity of the size of the matrix. To get equality here we will show that
\dim(V)\leq\dim(W)+1
.

Let
V^{\prime}:=\{\mathbf{v}\in V\mid v_{N+1}=0\}\subseteq V
and let
U:=V\backslash V^{\prime}
so that
V=V^{\prime}\sqcup U
. First we note that
\dim(V^{\prime})=\dim(W)
since
V^{\prime}
is precisely the image of the injective map
f
defined above. The inequality before shows that
U\neq\emptyset
since otherwise
V^{\prime}=V
and
\dim(V)=\dim(W)
, thus breaking the inequality
\dim(W)\leq\dim(V)-1
. Therefore there exists some
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n+1})\in U
with
u_{N+1}\neq 0
. If
U=\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{u}\}
then we immediately obtain
\dim(V)=\dim(W)+1
. Suppose instead that there exists some other
\mathbf{u^{\prime}}=(u^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,u^{\prime}_{N+1})\in U
with
u^{\prime}_{N+1}\neq 0
and
\mathbf{u^{\prime}}\neq\mathbf{u}
. With this we see that

u_{N+1}\mathbf{u^{\prime}}-u^{\prime}_{N+1}\mathbf{u}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{N+1}u^{\prime}_{1}-u^{\prime}_{N+1}u_{1}\\
\vdots\\
u_{N+1}u^{\prime}_{N}-u^{\prime}_{N+1}u_{N}\\
u_{N+1}u^{\prime}_{N+1}-u^{\prime}_{N+1}u_{N+1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{N+1}u^{\prime}_{1}-u^{\prime}_{N+1}u_{1}\\
\vdots\\
u_{N+1}u^{\prime}_{N}-u^{\prime}_{N+1}u_{N}\\
0\end{pmatrix}\in V^{\prime}.

Denoting the basis vectors of
V^{\prime}
as
\{\mathbf{b_{i}}\}_{i}\subset V^{\prime}
we write the following:

u_{N+1}\mathbf{u^{\prime}}-u^{\prime}_{N+1}\mathbf{u}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{b_{i}}

for some
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. When rearranged, this equation expresses
u_{N+1}\mathbf{u^{\prime}}
in terms of these basis vectors as well as
u^{\prime}_{N+1}\mathbf{u}
. Since
\mathbf{u^{\prime}}
was arbitrary, we conclude that the set
\{\mathbf{b_{i}}\}_{i}
does not span
V
but that
\{\mathbf{b_{i}}\}_{i}\cup\{\mathbf{u}\}
is a linearly independent spanning set for
V
. Since
\{\mathbf{b_{i}}\}_{i}
is a basis for
V^{\prime}
and
\dim(V^{\prime})=\dim(W)
we conclude that
\dim(V)\leq\dim(W)+1
as desired. This proves the statement of the lemma for
\epsilon=1
.

To prove the statement for
\epsilon=-1
we suppose
W\nsubseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}
and let
W^{\prime}\subseteq W
be the set of all
\mathbf{w}\in W
which are orthogonal to
\mathbf{1}
, i.e.
W^{\prime}=\{w\in W\mid\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}=0\}
. Then by the injective map
f|_{W^{\prime}}:W^{\prime}\rightarrow V
given by
\mathbf{w^{\prime}}\mapsto\mathbf{w^{\prime}}[0]
we obtain the inequality
\dim(W^{\prime})=\dim(W)-1\leq\dim(V)
. Using parity arguments, this means that either
\dim(V)=\dim(W)-1
or
\dim(V)>\dim(W)+1
. Suppose
\dim(V)>\dim(W)+1
and and consider
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
as before so that
\dim(V^{\prime})=\dim(V)-1
. The injective map
V^{\prime}\rightarrow W;\;\mathbf{v^{\prime}}=(v_{1}\ldots v_{N}0)\mapsto=(v_{1}\ldots v_{N})
and our supposition implies

\dim(W)\geq\dim(V^{\prime})=\dim(V)-1\geq\dim(W)\implies\dim(V^{\prime})=\dim(W)

but then the injective map becomes bijective and for each
\mathbf{w}\in W
we have some
\mathbf{w}[0]\in V^{\prime}
meaning that
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{1}^{\bot}
and
W\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}
. This contradicts our initial assumption, therefore we conclude
\dim(V)=\dim(W)-1
.
∎

The proof above used arguments from module theory that relied on
\ker(M)
being a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module with well-defined rank or dimension. When working over the finite field
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
, for prime
p
, the module arguments translate directly to vector space arguments and a similar proof can be used to show:

Corollary 5.4
.

Fix some prime number
p
. Let
\overline{M}:=M\mod{p}\in M_{N}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
and
\overline{M^{E}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\overline{M}&-\bar{\mathbf{1}}\\
\bar{\mathbf{1}}^{T}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in M_{N+1}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
be its extended matrix. Then

\dim(\ker(\overline{M^{E}}))=\dim(\ker(\overline{M}))+\epsilon,~\text{ where }~\epsilon=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\ker(\overline{M})\subseteq\bar{\mathbf{1}}^{\bot};\\
-1&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
\bar{\mathbf{1}}^{\bot}
denotes the orthogonal complement of
\bar{\mathbf{1}}
in
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{N}
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Fix a prime number
p
and let
\overline{M}:=M\mod{p}\in M_{N}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
. Then

\ker(\overline{M})\subseteq\bar{\mathbf{1}}^{\bot}\iff p\mid h_{s^{\prime}+1}(M^{E}),

where
s^{\prime}
is the number of nonzero invariant factors of
\overline{M}
, with
s^{\prime}\leq s
, and
\overline{\mathbf{1}}^{\bot}
denotes the orthogonal complement of
\mathbf{1}
in
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{N}
.

Proof.

From Corollary
5.4
we see that
\ker(\overline{M})\subseteq\overline{\mathbf{1}}^{\bot}
if and only if
\dim(\ker(\overline{M^{E}}))=\dim(\ker(\overline{M}))+1
. Applying
[
Tho79
, Theorem 3]
to
\overline{M}
and
\overline{M^{E}}
to get

h_{i}(\overline{M^{E}})|h_{i}(\overline{M})|h_{i+1}(\overline{M^{E}})\quad\forall\;i\in[1,s^{\prime}]

(25)

we see that
\overline{M^{E}}
has at least as many nonzero invariant factors as
\overline{M}
. Hence the dimension of the kernel increases in the extended matrix if and only if
h_{s^{\prime}+1}(\overline{M^{E}})=\overline{0}
. That is, if and only if
p\mid h_{s^{\prime}+1}(M^{E})
.
∎

5.2

Our results in this section use notation and results proved in
[
BCL12

. In particular, for a permutation
\sigma\in S_{k}
, we denote by
P_{\sigma}
the corresponding
k\times k
permutation matrix defined by
P_{\sigma}[i,j]:=\delta_{j,\sigma(i)}
, where
\delta
denotes the Kronecker symbol.

We now take
M=M(D)\in M_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
to be the matrix corresponding to an
m\times n
diagram
D
with
N
white squares labelled
1,\ldots,N
. Let

be the toric permutation associated to

, where

and

of

Lemma 5.6
.

For any basis element
\mathbf{w}:=\phi(\mathbf{v})\in\ker(M)
corresponding to basis element
\mathbf{v}:=\mathbf{v}^{\eta}\in\ker(P_{\omega}+P_{\sigma})
and odd cycle
\eta
in

\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{j\in\llbracket m+1,m+n\rrbracket|\\
\eta(j)\in\llbracket 1,m\rrbracket\}\end{subarray}}v_{\eta(j)}~~-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{k\in\llbracket 1,m\rrbracket|\\
\eta(k)\in\llbracket m+1,m+n\rrbracket\}\end{subarray}}v_{\eta(k)}.

(26)

Writing
\eta=(R_{1},\,C_{1},\,R_{2},\,C_{2},\,\ldots\,R_{k},\,C_{k})
as contiguous sequences which alternate between increasing subsequences of row-labels (the

\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}=\left(-(-1)^{|R_{1}|}+(-1)^{|R_{1}|+|C_{1}|}-\cdots+(-1)^{|R_{1}|+|C_{1}|+\cdots+|R_{k}|+|C_{k}|}\right)v_{1^{\prime}}.

for all

and
i\in\llbracket 1,m+n\rrbracket
. The element
\mathbf{w}\in\ker(M)
is defined in

to be the vector with entries

, for all

, where

and

From this we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{\text{left}(i)}-v_{\text{up}(i)}.

Proposition 5.7
.

Take
D
to be an
m\times n
diagram.
M:=M(D)
, and let
\{h_{i}(M)\}_{i=1}^{s}
be the nonzero invariant factors of the associated matrix.
Then
h_{i}(M^{E})
is a power of 2, for all
i\in\llbracket 1,s\rrbracket
, and
h_{s+1}(M^{E})
is either 0 or its odd prime factors are bounded above by
\min(m,n)
.

Proof.

Let
M=M(D)
be the matrix corresponding to an
m\times n
diagram. By
[
Tho79
, Theorem 3]
we obtain the following interlacing inequalities:

h_{1}(M^{E})|h_{1}(M),~h_{2}(M^{E})|h_{2}(M),~\ldots,h_{s}(M^{E})|h_{s}(M),

(28)

where the
|
means ‘divides’. From this we see that
h_{i}(M^{E})
are all powers of 2 for
i\in\llbracket 1,s\rrbracket
, but not necessarily for
i=s+1
.

Suppose that
h_{s+1}(M^{E})\neq 0
and that
p|h_{s+1}(M^{E})
for some odd prime
p>2
. In this case, by Proposition
5.3
we deduce that
\ker(M)\nsubseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}
and
\ker(M\mod p)\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}\mod p
. To see this we note that when
h_{s+1}(M^{E})\neq 0
then
\dim(\ker(M^{E}))=\dim(\ker(M))-1
(hence
\ker(M)\nsubseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}
, by Proposition
5.3
) and when
p|h_{s+1}(M^{E})
then
\dim(\ker(M^{E}))<\dim(\ker(M^{E}\mod p))
and
\dim(\ker(M))=\dim(\ker(M\mod p))
, since all
h_{i}(M)
are powers of 2. Applying Proposition
5.3
to
M\mod p
and
M^{E}\mod p
, we obtain

\displaystyle\dim(\ker(M^{E}\mod p))

\displaystyle{}>\dim(\ker(M^{E}))

\displaystyle{}=\dim(\ker(M))-1

\displaystyle{}=\dim(\ker(M\mod p))-1

\displaystyle{}=\dim(\ker(M^{E}\mod p))-\epsilon-1,

where
\epsilon
is defined as in Corollary
5.4
. This implies
\epsilon=1
and thus
\ker(M\mod p)\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}\mod p
.

We now find an upper bound for
p
given that
\ker(M)\nsubseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}
and
\ker(M\mod p)\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}\mod p
. Without loss of generality we will take
m<n
so that
\min(m,n)=m
. Suppose that the disjoint cycle decomposition of
\tau
consists of exactly one odd cycle
\tau=(1^{\prime}\;\tau(1^{\prime})\;\ldots\;\tau^{m+n-1}(1^{\prime}))
, where
1^{\prime}
is the first nonzero entry of
\tau
and we let each successive label
\tau^{i}(1^{\prime})
alternate between row- and column-labels for as long as this is possible. That is, we suppose that
1^{\prime},\tau^{2}(1^{\prime}),\ldots,\tau^{2m-2}(1^{\prime})
are row-labels and
\tau(1^{\prime}),\tau^{3}(1^{\prime}),\ldots,\tau^{2m-1}(1^{\prime}),\tau^{2m}(1^{\prime}),\allowbreak\ldots,\tau^{m+n-1}(1^{\prime})
are column-labels. Thus the pair
\{\tau^{j}(1^{\prime}),\,\tau^{j+1}(1^{\prime})\}
denotes a switch from a row-label to a column-label
m
times (when
j=0,2,\ldots,2m-2
) and from a column-label to a row-label
m
times (when
j=1,3,\ldots,2m-3,m+n-1
). In this case, the kernel of
M
has 1 basis element
\mathbf{w}=\phi(\mathbf{v})
associated to the odd cycle
\tau
and vector
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v^{\tau}}\in\ker(P_{\omega}+P_{\sigma})
. Applying Proposition
5.7
to
\mathbf{w}
we obtain the alternating sum:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{k}=v_{1^{\prime}}-v_{\tau(1^{\prime})}+v_{\tau^{2}(1^{\prime})}-\ldots+v_{\tau^{2m-2}(1^{\prime})}-v_{\tau^{m+n-1}(1^{\prime})}=2mv_{1^{\prime}}=\pm 2m.

(29)

Thus, since
\ker(M\mod p)\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}\mod p
, the sum above must be congruent to
0\mod p
. That is,
p
must be less than
m
, since
p
is odd. A similar argument can be used if
n\leq m
to conclude that
p<n
hence the general result concludes that
p<\min(m,n)
.

It is obvious that if the disjoint cycle decomposition of
\tau
consists of shorter length odd cycles
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{r}
then the absolute value of the sum (
29
) corresponding to basis element
\mathbf{w}
associated to any
\eta_{i}
will be less than
2\min(m,n)
. Hence
2\min(m,n)
is the upper bound for any toric permutation,
\tau
, and hence any diagram,
D
.
∎

M(D)^{E}

5.3
PI degree of Quantum Schubert Varieties associated to quantum determinantal ideals

Recall the matrix
\mathcal{M}_{n,t}
defining the quantum affine space associated to
R_{t}(M_{n})
, for
1\leq t<n
.
Let
2s_{t}:=2nt-t^{2}-t
be its rank so that it has
s_{t}
(pairs of nonzero) invariant factors, which we will denote by
\{h_{i}^{(t)}\}_{i}
.

The aim of this section is to compute the PI degree of the quantum Schubert variety
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{n,2n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\gamma}
when
\gamma=\{1,\ldots,t,n+1,\ldots,2n-t\}
.

Proposition 5.9
.

1.

h_{s_{t}+1}(\mathcal{M}^{E}_{n,t})=0
if and only if
(n=2m\text{ and }t|m)
.

2.

All invariant factors of
\mathcal{M}_{n,t}^{E}
are powers of
2
.

3.

Let
\gamma=\{1,\ldots,t,n+1,\ldots,2n-t\}\in\Pi_{n,2n}
. Let
q
be a primitive
\ell^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity with
\ell
odd. Then,

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{n,2n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\gamma})=\begin{cases}\ell^{\frac{2nt-t^{2}-t}{2}}&\text{if }n=2m\text{ and }t|m,\\
\ell^{\frac{2nt-t^{2}-t}{2}+1}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

1.

For each of the
t
odd cycles
\eta_{i}=(i\;\tau(i)\;\ldots\;\tau^{l}(i))=(R_{1},C_{1},R_{2},C_{2},\ldots,R_{k},C_{k})
in the disjoint cycle decomposition of
\tau
, as calculated in Proposition
3.14
, we denote its corresponding basis element in
\ker(\mathcal{M}_{n,t})
by
\mathbf{w}^{\eta_{i}}=(w^{\eta_{i}}_{1},\ldots,w^{\eta_{i}}_{N})^{T}
. We apply Lemma
5.6
to each
\eta_{i}
, using the fact that
\tau^{|R_{1}|+|C_{1}|+\cdots+|R_{k}|+|C_{k}|}(i)=i
and noting that
1^{\prime}=i
, to obtain:

\sum_{j=1}^{N}w^{\eta_{i}}_{j}=v^{\eta_{i}}_{i}-v^{\eta_{i}}_{\tau^{|R_{1}|}(i)}+v^{\eta_{i}}_{\tau^{|R_{1}|+|C_{1}|}(i)}-\cdots-v^{\eta_{i}}_{\tau^{|R_{1}|+|C_{1}|+|R_{2}|+\cdots+|R_{k}|}(i)}.

We saw in Proposition
3.14
that the odd cycles
\{\eta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{t}
are divided into 3 sets depending on whether
t<n-t,\,n-t<t,
or
n-t=t
, with the cycles in each set sharing similar permutation rules. Recall we can write
n=ut+r
for some
u\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in\llbracket 0,t-1\rrbracket
. Below we look at the cycles in each of these 3 main sets, further dividing one of these sets into 2 subsets based on the value of
r
:

(a)

n-t=t
:
For all
i\in\llbracket 1,t\rrbracket
we have
\eta_{i}=(R_{1},\,C_{1})
, where
|R_{1}|=2
. Therefore,
\sum_{j=1}^{N}w^{\eta_{i}}_{j}=v^{\eta_{i}}_{i}-v^{\eta_{i}}_{\tau^{2}(i)}=v_{i}-v_{i}=0
.

(b)

t<n-t
we split into further subsets:

(i)

r=0
:
For all
i\in\llbracket 1,t\rrbracket
we have
\eta_{i}=(R_{1},C_{1})
where
|R_{1}|=u
. Therefore,

\sum_{j=1}^{N}w^{\eta_{i}}_{j}=v^{\eta_{i}}_{i}-v^{\eta_{i}}_{\tau^{u}(i)}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $u$ is even};\\
2v_{i}&\text{ if $u$ is odd}.\end{cases}

If
u
is even then
n=ut+r=2m^{\prime}t
, for some
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, hence
n=2m
and
t|m
. If
u
is odd then
n=(2m^{\prime}+1)t
is odd when
t
is odd; otherwise
n
is even with
n=2m
and
t\nmid m
. We therefore obtain:

\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}w^{\eta_{i}}_{j}\right|=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }n=2m\text{ and }t|m;\\
2&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(ii)

r\in\llbracket 1,t-1\rrbracket
:
For all
i\in\llbracket 1,t\rrbracket
we have
\eta_{i}=(R_{1},C_{1})
, where
|R_{1}|=u+1
for all
i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
, and
|R_{1}|=u
for all
i\in\llbracket r+1,t\rrbracket
. Therefore, if
u
is odd we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}w^{\eta_{i}}_{j}

\displaystyle{}=\begin{cases}v^{\eta_{i}}_{i}-v^{\eta_{i}}_{\tau^{u+1}(i)}=0&\text{ for all }i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket;\\
v^{\eta_{i}}_{i}-v^{\eta_{i}}_{\tau^{u}(i)}=2v_{i}&\text{ for all }i\in\llbracket r+1,t\rrbracket,\end{cases}

and if
u
is even we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}w^{\eta_{i}}_{j}

\displaystyle{}=\begin{cases}v^{\eta_{i}}_{i}-v^{\eta_{i}}_{\tau^{u+1}(i)}=2v_{i}&\text{ for all }i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket;\\
v^{\eta_{i}}_{i}-v^{\eta_{i}}_{\tau^{u}(i)}=0&\text{ for all }i\in\llbracket r+1,t\rrbracket.\end{cases}

Since
r\in\llbracket 1,t-1\rrbracket
then both of the sets
\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
and
\llbracket r+1,t\rrbracket
are nonempty, therefore, for any
n
there exist
i,k\in\llbracket 1,t\rrbracket
such that
\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}w^{\eta_{i}}_{j}\right|=0
and
\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}w^{\eta_{k}}_{j}\right|=2
.

(c)

0<n-t<t
:
For all
i\in\llbracket 1,t\rrbracket
we have
\eta_{i}=(R_{1},C_{1})
where
|R_{1}|=2
if
i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
and
|R_{1}|=1
if
i\in\llbracket r+1,t\rrbracket
. Therefore,

\sum_{j=1}^{N}w^{\eta_{i}}_{j}=\begin{cases}v^{\eta_{i}}_{i}-v^{\eta_{i}}_{\tau^{2}(i)}=0&\text{ for all }i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket;\\
v^{\eta_{i}}_{i}-v^{\eta_{i}}_{\tau(i)}=2v_{i}&\text{ for all }i\in\llbracket r+1,t\rrbracket,\end{cases}.

If
r=0
then the above sum takes only one value,
2v_{i}
, for all
i\in\llbracket 1,t\rrbracket
. Therefore, for any
n
there exists
i\in\llbracket 1,t\rrbracket
such that
\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}w^{\eta_{i}}_{j}\right|=2
.

If
n=2m
with
t|m
then we are in cases
1a
and
1(b)i
for
u
even. Both of these have
\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}w^{\eta_{i}}_{j}\right|=0
for all
i\in\llbracket 1,t\rrbracket
, hence we have
\ker(\mathcal{M}_{n,t})\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}
and
h_{s_{t}+1}(\mathcal{M}_{n,t}^{E})=0
.

If
n
is odd, or
n=2m
and
t\nmid m
, then we are in cases
1(b)i
for
u
odd,
1(b)ii
, and
1c
. In each of these cases we have at least one cycle
\eta_{i}
with
\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}w^{\eta_{i}}_{j}\right|=2
and no odd cycles with an absolute sum greater than this. Therefore we conclude that
\ker(\mathcal{M}_{n,t})\nsubseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}
and
\ker(\mathcal{M}_{n,t})\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}\mod 2
, hence
h_{s_{t}+1}(\mathcal{M}_{n,t}^{E})\neq 0
. This proves part
1
.

2.

Using
[
Tho79
, Theorem 5]
to obtain
h_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{n,t}^{E})|h_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{n,t})
, for all
i\in\llbracket 1,s_{t}\rrbracket
, it is clear that for all
t\in\llbracket 1,n-1\rrbracket
and
i\in\llbracket 1,s_{t}\rrbracket
,
h_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{n,t}^{E})
is a power of 2. The question is therefore regarding the value of
h_{s_{t}+1}(\mathcal{M}_{n,t}^{E})
.

When
n=2m
with
t|m
we see by part
1
of this lemma that
h_{s_{t}+1}(\mathcal{M}_{n,t}^{E})=0
. Therefore the statement is true for this case. When
n
is not of this form, we note from part
1
of this lemma that
\ker(\mathcal{M}_{n,t})\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}\mod 2
and
\ker(\mathcal{M}_{n,t})\nsubseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}\mod p
for any
p>2
. Therefore, the only prime dividing
h_{s_{t+1}}(\mathcal{M}_{n,t}^{E})
is 2 by Proposition
5.5
. This completes the proof of part
2
.

3.

This result follows immediately from Lemma
5.2
, parts
1
and
2
of this lemma, and Theorem
3.13
.

∎

5.4
PI degree of quantum affine spaces associated to the extended matrix of a diagram matrix and application to quantum Schubert varieties

We are now in position to compute the PI degree of a quantum affine space associated to the extended matrix of a Young diagram matrix. This allows the computation of the PI degree of an arbitrary quantum Schubert variety.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
D
be a diagram on the Young tableau
Y_{\lambda}
with
n=\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{m}>0
. Denote by
N
the number of white boxes, by
M=M(D)
its associated matrix, and by
M^{E}
the extended matrix. Suppose
\tau
has
r
odd cycles and
M
has
s=(N-r)/2
nonzero invariant factors. Let
q
be a primitive
\ell^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity with
\ell
odd and
\ell_{0}
the smallest prime factor of
\ell
. Then the PI degree of the quantum affine space associated to
M^{E}
is

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{E}}}\left(\mathbb{K}^{N+1}\right)\right)=\begin{cases}\ell^{\frac{N-r}{2}}&\text{ if $\ker(M)\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}$};\\
\ell^{\frac{N-r}{2}+1}&\text{ if $\ker(M)\nsubseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}$ and $\ell_{0}>\min(m,n)$};\\
\ell^{\frac{N-r}{2}}\cdot\frac{\ell}{\gcd(h_{s+1}(M^{E}),\ell)}&\text{ if $\ker(M)\nsubseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}$ and $\ell_{0}\leq\min(m,n)$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

This easily follows from Lemma
3.1
, Proposition
5.3
and Proposition
5.7
.

∎

.

, we get the following for the PI degree of
\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{E}}}\left(\mathbb{K}^{N+1}\right)
:

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q^{M^{E}}}\left(\mathbb{K}^{N+1}\right)\right)=\begin{cases}\ell^{3}&\text{ if }\mathrm{gcd}(\ell,3)=1;\\

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
q
be a primitive
\ell^{\mathrm{th}}

\ell_{0}
of

\ell_{0}>\min\{m,n,2\}
.

Let
\gamma=[\gamma_{1}<\dots<\gamma_{m}]
be a quantum Plücker coordinate of

and let
\lambda
be the partition associated to
\gamma
. We denote by
N
the number of boxes in
Y_{\lambda}
and by
r
the number of odd cycles for the toric permutation associated to

(with all boxes being white).

Then the PI degree of the quantum Schubert variety
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\gamma}

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))_{\gamma}\right)=\begin{cases}\ell^{\frac{N-r}{2}}&\text{ if $\ker(M_{\lambda})\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\bot}$};\\

5.5
PI degree of quantum Grassmannians

We conclude this article with a computation of the PI degree of any quantum Grassmannian. The dimension of the kernel of the relevant skew-symmetric integral matrix was computed in
[
LL07
, Proposition 2.4]
. To prove the following theorem, we will just need to prove that the kernel of that matrix is contained in
\mathbf{1}^{\bot}
exactly when this kernel is trivial.

If
i
is a positive integer greater than or equal to
2
, we denote by
\mu_{2}(i)
the 2-adic valuation of
i
; that is, the largest integer
j
such that
2^{j}
divides
i
.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
q
be a primitive
\ell^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity such that the smallest prime factor
\ell_{0}
of
\ell
satisfies
\ell_{0}>\min\{m,n,2\}
.

Then the PI degree of the quantum Schubert variety
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
is given by

{\rm PI}\text{-}{\rm deg}\left(\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))\right)=\begin{cases}\ell^{\frac{m(n-m)}{2}}&\text{ if }\mu_{2}(m)\neq\mu_{2}(n);\\
\ell^{\frac{m(n-m)-\gcd(m,n)}{2}+1}&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Given a positive integer
\eta
, we let
B_{\eta}
denote the
\eta\times\eta
skew-symmetric matrix with
1
’s in every entry above the diagonal. Given positive integers
\mu,\eta
with
\mu\leq\eta
, we let
U_{\eta,\mu}
denote the
\eta\times\mu
matrix given by

(I_{\mu}~|~{\bf 0}_{\mu\times(\eta-\mu)})^{T}
. Finally, let
{\bf 1}_{\eta}
denote the
\eta\times 1
column vector whose entries are all
1
.
Let
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{s}\geq 1
with
\sum\lambda_{i}=N
. Then the matrix
M_{\lambda}
can be expressed as a block
s\times s
matrix in which the
(i,j)
-block is
U_{\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}}
if
i<j
;
-U_{\lambda_{j},\lambda_{i}}^{T}
if
i>j
; and
B_{\lambda_{i}}
if
i=j
. Let
v_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{\lambda_{i}\times 1}
and suppose that

{\bf v}=(v_{1}~|~v_{2}~|~\cdots~|~v_{s})^{T}

is in the kernel of
M_{\lambda}
. We would like to know when this implies that
{\bf 1}_{N}^{T}\cdot{\bf v}=0
.

In order to compute the PI degree of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
, we need to apply Corollary
5.12
in the case when
\gamma=[1\dots m]
since
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))_{[1\dots m]}=\mathcal{O}_{q}(G_{m,n}(\mathbb{K}))
. In this case, the partition
\lambda
is given by
(\lambda_{1}=n-m,\dots,\lambda_{m}=n-m)
.

In this case, we set
B:=B_{n-m}
. If we subtract the
i
-th row from our block matrix
A_{\lambda}
from the
(i+1)
-row of our block matrix, then we get

(-I-B)v_{i}+(B-I)v_{i+1}=0
. Since
B
is skew-symmetric, its eigenvalues are purely imaginary and so
B-I
is invertible. Then
v_{i+1}=(B-I)^{-1}(B+I)v_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,m-1
. In particular, if we let
C:=(B-I)^{-1}(B+I)
, then we see that
v_{j}=C^{j-1}v_{1}
for
j=1,\ldots,m
. Now looking at the first row of our block matrix and multiplying it by
{\bf v}
and using the fact that
{\bf v}
is in the kernel, we see that

-v_{1}-Cv_{1}-\cdots-C^{m-2}v_{1}+BC^{m-1}v_{1}=0.

(30)

Notice that the eigenvalues of
C
are of the form

(\beta+1)/(\beta-1)
with
\beta
a purely imaginary number and since
C
and
B
commute, we see that

v_{1}=0
unless
0
is an eigenvalue of

-I-C-\cdots-C^{m-2}+BC^{m-1}
.
Notice that
B
and
C
commute and are simultaneously diagonalizable (since
B
is skew-symmetric). If
\gamma=(\beta+1)/(\beta-1)
is an eigenvalue of
C
then the corresponding eigenvalue,
\beta
, of
B
is
(\gamma+1)/(\gamma-1)
.
So the eigenvalues of
X:=-I-C-\cdots-C^{n-2}+BC^{n-1}
are of the form

-(\gamma^{m-1}-1)/(\gamma-1)+\gamma^{m-1}(\gamma+1)/(\gamma-1)
. If
X
has a zero eigenvalue then since
\gamma\neq 0
we must have

-(\gamma^{n-1}-1)+\gamma^{n-1}(\gamma+1)=0,

or equivalently

1+\gamma^{m}=0.

Thus
\gamma=\exp(\pi ij/m)
for some odd
j
. Thus the kernel of
A_{\lambda}
in this case is spanned by the vectors of the form
{\bf v}=(v~|~Cv~|~\cdots~|C^{m-1}v)^{T}
with
v
an eigenvector of
B
with eigenvalue
\gamma
with
\gamma^{m}=1
. It is easily checked that the eigenspace (if such an eigenvector exists) associated with
\gamma
is one-dimensional and spanned by
(1,\gamma,\ldots,\gamma^{n-m-1})^{T}
, and that this occurs only if
\gamma^{n-m}=-1
. In other words

(\gamma-1)^{2}{\bf 1}_{mn}^{T}\cdot{\bf v}=(\gamma^{n}-1)(\gamma^{m}-1)=4
. In other words, the kernel is contained in the orthogonal complement of
{\bf 1}_{mn}^{T}
if and only if the kernel of
M_{(n-m,\dots,n-m)}
is zero.

Now, by
[
LL07
, Proposition 2.4]
, the kernel is zero if and only if
\mu_{2}(m)\neq\mu_{2}(n)
, and in the case where
\mu_{2}(m)=\mu_{2}(n)
, the kernel of
M_{(n-m,\dots,n-m)}
has dimension
\gcd(m,n)
. We conclude thanks to Corollary
5.12
.

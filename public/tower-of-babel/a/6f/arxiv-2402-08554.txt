On Harder-Narasimhan slopes of direct images
Siarhei Finski

Abstract




MnLargeSymbols’164
MnLargeSymbols’171

Abstract.

For a polarized family of complex projective manifolds, we study the asymptotic distribution of Harder-Narasimhan slopes of direct image sheaves associated with high tensor powers of the polarization.
We establish a theorem of Mehta-Ramanathan type, showing that this asymptotic distribution can be recovered from the analogous asymptotic distributions associated with base changes of the family over generic curves.

Introduction

Consider a surjective flat holomorphic map
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to B
between complex projective manifolds
X
and
B
of dimensions
n+m
and
m
respectively,
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle over
X
, which is relatively ample with respect to
\pi
.
We fix a
multipolarization

[\omega_{B}]
, which is a collection of Kähler classes
[\omega_{B,i}]\in H^{1,1}(B)
,
i=1,\ldots,m-1
.
The goal of this paper is to study the Harder-Narasimhan
[\omega_{B}]
-slopes of direct image sheaves
E_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=R^{0}\pi_{*}L^{k}
of
L^{k}
for big
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

More precisely, recall that a
slope
(or
[\omega_{B}]
-slope) of a coherent sheaf
\mathscr{E}
over
B
is defined as
\mu(\mathscr{E})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\deg(\mathscr{E})/{\rm{rk}}(\mathscr{E})
, where the degree,
\deg(\mathscr{E})
, is given by
\deg(\mathscr{E})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\int_{B}c_{1}(\det(\mathscr{E}))\cdot[\omega_{B,1}]\cdots[\omega_{B,m-1}]
, where
\det\mathscr{E}
is Knudsen-Mumford determinant of
\mathscr{E}
, see
[
29
]
.
A torsion-free coherent sheaf
\mathscr{E}
is called
semistable
(or
[\omega_{B}]
-
semistable
) if for every coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathscr{E}
, verifying
{\rm{rk}}(\mathcal{F})>0
, we have
\mu(\mathcal{F})\leq\mu(\mathscr{E})
.
When the multipolarization is
symmetric
, i.e.
[\omega_{B,1}]=\cdots=[\omega_{B,m-1}]
, we recover the usual notion of slopes and semistability, as in
[
30
]
.

Recall that any torsion-free coherent sheaf
\mathscr{E}
on
(B,[\omega_{B}])
admits a unique filtration by subsheaves
\mathscr{E}_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,s
, also called
Harder-Narasimhan filtration
:

\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{E}_{s}\supset\mathscr{E}_{s-1}\supset\cdots\supset\mathscr{E}_{1}\supset\mathscr{E}_{0}=\{0\},

(1.1)

such that for any
1\leq i\leq s-1
, the quotient sheaf
\mathscr{E}_{i}/\mathscr{E}_{i-1}
is the maximal semistable (torsion-free) subsheaf of
\mathscr{E}/\mathscr{E}_{i-1}
, i.e. for any subsheaf of
\mathcal{F}
of a (torsion-free) sheaf
\mathscr{E}/\mathscr{E}_{i-1}
, we have
\mu(\mathcal{F})\leq\mu(\mathscr{E}_{i}/\mathscr{E}_{i-1})
and
{\rm{rk}}(\mathcal{F})\leq{\rm{rk}}(\mathscr{E}_{i}/\mathscr{E}_{i-1})
if
\mu(\mathcal{F})=\mu(\mathscr{E}_{i}/\mathscr{E}_{i-1})
.
For the proof, see
[
28
, Theorem 1.6.7]
for symmetric multipolarization and
[
21
, Corollary 2.27]
for the general case.

We define the
Harder-Narasimhan slopes
,
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{{\rm{rk}}(\mathscr{E})}
of
\mathscr{E}
, so that
\mu(\mathscr{E}_{i}/\mathscr{E}_{i-1})
appears among
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{{\rm{rk}}(\mathscr{E})}
exactly
{\rm{rk}}(\mathscr{E}_{i}/\mathscr{E}_{i-1})
times, and the sequence
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{{\rm{rk}}(\mathscr{E})}
is non-increasing.
We call
\mu_{\min}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mu_{{\rm{rk}}(\mathscr{E})}
and
\mu_{\max}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mu_{1}
, the minimal and the maximal slopes respectively.

By the flatness of
\pi
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
big enough (which we assume implicitly from now on), the coherent sheaves
E_{k}
are locally free, cf.
[
24
, Theorem III.9.9]
.
We denote
N_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}={\rm{rk}}(E_{k})
, and let
\mu_{1}^{k},\ldots,\mu_{N_{k}}^{k}
be the Harder-Narasimhan slopes of
E_{k}
. Let
\mu_{\min}^{k},\mu_{\max}^{k}
be the minimal and the maximal slopes.
We define the probability measure
\eta_{k}^{HN}
on as follows

\eta_{k}^{HN}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\delta\Big[\frac{\mu_{i}^{k}}{k}\Big],

(1.2)

where
\delta[x]
is the Dirac mass at
x\in\real
. In Section
2
, we establish the following result, serving as a starting point of the current work.

Theorem 1.1
.

The sequence of measures
\eta_{k}^{HN}
converges weakly, as
k\to\infty
, to a probability measure
\eta^{HN}
on , which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, except perhaps for a point mass at
\esssup\eta^{HN}
.
Also, the limits below exist and relate with
\eta^{HN}
as follows

\eta_{\min}^{HN}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mu_{\min}^{k}}{k}\leq\essinf\eta^{HN},\qquad\eta_{\max}^{HN}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mu_{\max}^{k}}{k}=\esssup\eta^{HN}.

(1.3)

When
\dim B=1
, the convergence part of Theorem
1.1
was established by Chen
[
7
]
.
For this, he verified that Harder-Narasimhan filtrations of
E_{k}
are submultiplicative, see (
2.3
), and
\mu_{\max}^{k}
grows at most linearly in
k
.
Theorem
1.1
then follows from the general theory of bounded submultiplicative filtrations developed by Chen
[
7
]
and Boucksom-Chen
[
4
]
, cf. Theorem
2.2
.
Our contribution in Theorem
1.1
consists in extending the results of Chen for
\dim B>1
.

When the base of the family is a curve, the measure
\eta^{HN}
is easier to study for various reasons: the subsheaves in the Harder-Narasimhan filtration become locally free, there is a very precise relation between the slope and the number of holomorphic sections, cf.
[
8
, Lemma 2.1]
, and the notion of slope doesn’t depend on the choice of multipolarization.

The main goal of this article is to show that
\eta^{HN}
,
\eta_{\min}^{HN}
,
\eta_{\max}^{HN}
can be recovered from the analogous quantities associated with base changes of our family over generic curves in
B
.

For this, we need to assume that the multipolarization
[\omega_{B}]
is
integral
and
very ample
, i.e.
[\omega_{B,i}]\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
and very ample for any
i=1,\ldots,m-1
.
Consider a regular curve
C=C(l)\subset B
given by Bertini theorem as an intersection of
m-1
generic divisors in the classes
l_{i}[\omega_{B,i}]
,
i=1,\ldots,m-1
, where
l\in\mathbb{N}^{*(m-1)}
,
l=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{m-1})
.
It is well-known, cf. Corollary
3.8
, that for such
generic
curves
C
, the Harder-Narasimhan slopes of
E_{k}|_{C}
do not depend on the choice of
C
(for a fixed parameter
l\in\mathbb{N}^{*(m-1)}
).
In particular, the sequence of probability measures
\eta_{k,l}^{HN|C}
, defined for
E_{k}|_{C}
analogously to
\eta_{k}^{HN}
, and their weak limits, as
k\to\infty
, which we denote by
\eta_{l}^{HN|C}
, as well as the limiting minimal and maximal slopes, cf. (
1.3
), which we denote by
\eta_{\min,l}^{HN|C},\eta_{\max,l}^{HN|C}
, are well-defined and exist by Theorem
1.1
.

For
a\in\real^{*}
, we introduce the homothety map
H^{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\real\to\real
,
x\mapsto x/a
.

Theorem 1.2
.

The measures
H^{l_{1}\cdots l_{m-1}}_{*}(\eta_{l}^{HN|C})
converge weakly to
\eta^{HN}
, as
l=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{m-1})\to\infty
(i.e.
l_{i}\to\infty
,
i=1,\ldots,m-1
).
Moreover,

\lim_{l\to\infty}\frac{\eta_{\min,l}^{HN|C}}{l_{1}\cdots l_{m-1}}=\eta_{\min}^{HN},\qquad\lim_{l\to\infty}\frac{\eta_{\max,l}^{HN|C}}{l_{1}\cdots l_{m-1}}=\eta_{\max}^{HN}.

(1.4)

Remark 1.3
.

For the rationale behind the normalization by
l_{1}\cdots l_{m-1}
, see (
2.8
).

As an application of Theorem
1.2
and a result from Xu-Zhuang

\eta_{\min}^{HN}
and
\eta_{\max}^{HN}
.
More precisely, we say that
\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X)
is
[\omega_{B}]
-generically fibered nef (resp. psef) with respect to
\pi
if there is
l_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, such that for any regular curve
C=C(l)\subset B

l=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{m-1})
,

, defined as before Theorem

, the restriction of
\alpha
to
\pi^{-1}(C)
is nef (resp. psef), i.e. for any irreducible curve

in
\pi^{-1}(C)
, the pairing
\int_{C^{\prime}}\alpha
is non negative (resp. the restriction of
\alpha
to
\pi^{-1}(C)
contains a positive current).
For families given by projectivization of vector bundles, equivalent definition was given by Miyaoka
[
37
]
, see also Peternell
[
38
]
.
In analogy with the definition of the nef (resp. psef) threshold, for a class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X)
, we then define the
[\omega_{B}]
-generic nef (resp. psef) threshold with respect to
\pi
as the supremum over all
T\in\real

\alpha-T(\pi^{*}[\omega_{B,0}]/\int_{B}[\omega_{B,0}]\cdots[\omega_{B,m-1}])
is
[\omega_{B}]
-generically fibered nef (resp. psef) with respect to
\pi
for some Kähler class
[\omega_{B,0}]\in H^{1,1}(B,\mathbb{C})
(it is easy to see that the definition is independent on the choice of
[\omega_{B,0}]
, and for relatively ample
\alpha
, this supremum is finite).

Corollary 1.4
.

When the multipolarization
[\omega_{B}]
is integral, the quantity
\eta_{\min}^{HN}
(resp.
\eta_{\max}^{HN}
) equals to
[\omega_{B}]
-generic nef (resp. psef) threshold of
c_{1}(L)
with respect to
\pi
.

As we shall explain in Section
5
, if
\dim B=1
, from the characterization of the pseudoeffective cone
[
5
]
and
[
48
]
, we can get the following numerical formulas

\eta_{\min}^{HN}=\inf_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{irr}}\frac{\int_{C}c_{1}(L)}{{\deg(\pi|_{C})}},\qquad\eta_{\max}^{HN}=\inf_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{mov}}\frac{\int_{C}c_{1}(L)}{{\deg(\pi|_{C})}},

(1.5)

where
\mathcal{C}_{irr}
is the set of irreducible curves

, such that
\pi|_{C}
is surjective,
\mathcal{C}_{mov}\subset\mathcal{C}_{irr}
is the subset of movable curves, i.e. curves which can be put in a family, which cover

is the topological degree of
\pi|_{C}
.
By Corollary

, similar formulas can be established if

.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we use a theorem of Mehta-Ramanathan
[
36
]
, showing that semistability behaves well under restriction over generic curves of sufficiently big degrees.
Using this theorem, in (

with an arbitrary test function on can be calculated as a double limit, where the first limit is taken in

(corresponding to the degree of the generic curve,

(corresponding to the index of the direct image sheaves,

The main content of Theorem

Philosophically, even though we do not literally pursue this direction, this means that there is a certain weak uniformity in the degree of the generic curve required in Mehta-Ramanathan theorem for the vector bundles
E_{k}
for all parameters

.
In order to show that we can change the order of limits, we rely on the techniques of Shatz
[
44
]
, to describe how Harder-Narasimhan slopes behave under the variation of the manifold (in our case, a specialization of the generic curve) and on the analysis of Chen
[
7
]
on the study of submultiplicative filtrations.

A sequence

Submultiplicative nature of Harder-Narasimhan filtrations

The main goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
.
For this, we will first recall some crucial points of the theory of submultiplicative filtrations on section rings.

Recall that a
non-increasing -filtration

\mathcal{F}
of a vector space
V
is a map from to vector subspaces of
V
,
t\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{t}V
, verifying
\mathcal{F}^{t}V\subset\mathcal{F}^{s}V
for
t>s
, and such that
\mathcal{F}^{t}V=V
for sufficiently small
t
and
\mathcal{F}^{t}V=\{0\}
for sufficiently big
t
.
We say that
\mathcal{F}
is
graded
if it respects the grading of
V
.
It is
left-continuous
if for any
t\in\real
, there is
\epsilon_{0}>0
, such that
\mathcal{F}^{t}V=\mathcal{F}^{t-\epsilon}V
for any
0\leq\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
.
All filtrations in this article are assumed to be non-increasing left-continuous and graded if applicable.
We associate with any filtration the probability measure,
\eta(\mathcal{F},V)
, on , which we also call the
jumping measure
, defined as follows

\eta(\mathcal{F},V)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{1}{\dim V}\sum_{j=1}^{\dim V}\delta\big[\eta(\mathcal{F},j)\big],

(2.1)

where
\eta(\mathcal{F},j)
are the
jumping numbers
, defined as follows
\eta(\mathcal{F},j)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sup\{t\in\real\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\dim\mathcal{F}^{t}V\geq j\}
.
Alternatively, we can define this measure as
\eta(\mathcal{F},V)=-\frac{1}{\dim V}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\dim\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}V
.

Now, for an ample line bundle
L
over a complex projective manifold
Y
, define the
section ring

R(Y,L)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\oplus_{k=1}^{\infty}H^{0}(Y,L^{k}).

(2.2)

A graded filtration
\mathcal{F}
on
R(Y,L)
is called
submultiplicative
if for any
t,s\in\real
,
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\mathcal{F}^{t}H^{0}(Y,L^{k})\cdot\mathcal{F}^{s}H^{0}(Y,L^{l})\subset\mathcal{F}^{t+s}H^{0}(Y,L^{k+l}).

(2.3)

We say that
\mathcal{F}
is
bounded
if there is
C>0
, such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
\mathcal{F}^{Ck}H^{0}(Y,L^{k})=\{0\}
.
Remark that it is an immediate consequence of the submultiplicativity and the fact that
R(Y,L)
is a finitely generated ring that there is
C>0
, such that
\mathcal{F}^{-Ck}H^{0}(Y,L^{k})=H^{0}(Y,L^{k})
.

We let
N_{k}=\dim H^{0}(Y,L^{k})
, and denote by
\eta_{\mathcal{F}}(1,k)\geq\cdots\geq\eta_{\mathcal{F}}(N_{k},k)
the
jumping numbers
of the filtration
\mathcal{F}
on
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
.
We denote by
\eta_{\mathcal{F},\max}(k)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\eta_{\mathcal{F}}(1,k)
the maximal and by
\eta_{\mathcal{F},\min}(k)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\eta_{\mathcal{F}}(N_{k},k)
the minimal jumping numbers.
The following result is well-known, cf.
[
7

.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any submultiplicative filtration
\mathcal{F}
on

\eta_{\mathcal{F},\max}(k)
(resp.
\eta_{\mathcal{F},\min}(k)
),

In particular, the following limits exist
\lim_{k\to\infty}\eta_{\mathcal{F},\max}(k)/k
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\eta_{\mathcal{F},\min}(k)/k
, in
\real\cup\{+\infty\}
, and they are finite if
\mathcal{F}
is bounded.

Define the sequence of
jumping measures

\eta_{\mathcal{F},k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, of
\mathcal{F}
on as follows

\eta_{\mathcal{F},k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{k}}\delta\Big[\frac{\eta_{\mathcal{F}}(j,k)}{k}\Big]=H^{k}_{*}\Big(\eta(\mathcal{F},H^{0}(X,L^{k}))\Big),

(2.4)

where

(Chen

Now, we specialize this general theory to the case of Harder-Narasimhan filtrations.
We use the notations from Introduction.
We introduce the (non-increasing) filtrations
\mathcal{F}_{k}(\lambda)
,
\lambda\in\real

E_{k}

\mathcal{F}_{k}(\lambda)

Clearly, the filtration

The main idea behind the above “renaming” of Harder-Narasimhan filtration is that the filtration then becomes submultiplicative.
More precisely, for any
b\in B

\mathcal{F}_{b}

\mathcal{F}_{k}(\lambda)

3

Relative slopes on degenerating families of varieties

The main goal of this section is to describe how Harder-Narasimhan filtrations behave in families.
More precisely, we first recall that in any flat family, Harder-Narasimhan slopes of the generic fiber are well-defined.
Following Shatz
[
44
]
, we then make a relation between the Harder-Narasimhan slopes of the generic fiber and the Harder-Narasimhan slopes of the specialization of the generic fiber.
As an application of this, we show that the sequence of measures composed of Harder-Narasimhan slopes of the restrictions of a given torsion-free sheaf to generic curves of a given degree satisfy certain subadditivity properties in terms of degrees of curves.

More precisely, our setting will be as follows. Let

The set of fibers of

In the second part of this section, we discuss the relation between the slopes of generic and special fibers of the family. For this, we need to introduce a certain order on filtrations.

({\rm{rk}}(\mathcal{F}_{i}),\deg(\mathcal{F}_{i}))

4

Generic base changes and asymptotic distribution of slopes

The main goal of this section is to show that the asymptotic distribution of Harder-Narasimhan slopes of direct images can be calculated through generic base changes, i.e. to establish Theorem
1.2
.
The following famous result will be at the heart of our approach.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any semistable torsion-free coherent sheaf
\mathscr{E}
over a complex projective manifold
B
with an integral multipolarization
[\omega_{B}]
, there is
l_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any generic curve
C\subset B
, which is a complete intersection in
l[\omega_{B}]
,
l=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{m-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{*(m-1)}
,
l_{i}\geq l_{0}
,
i=1,\ldots,m-1
, the restriction of
\mathscr{E}
to
C
is semistable.

Remark 4.2
.

When the multipolarization is symmetric, the theorem is due to Mehta-Ramanathan
[
35
, Theorem 6.1]
, cf. also
[
28
, Theorem 7.11]
and
[
36
]
.
The proof for the general multipolarization remains verbatim.

In particular, from Theorem
4.1
and (
2.8
), we conclude that for any torsion-free coherent sheaf
\mathscr{E}
over
B
, the restriction of Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
\mathscr{E}
over generic curves
C\subset B
as in Theorem
4.1
coincides with the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
\mathscr{E}|_{C}
.

The main difficulty in the proof of Theorem
1.2
is due to the fact that the value
l_{0}
from Theorem
4.1
depends heavily on
\mathscr{E}
.
Despite many works on the effective version of Mehta-Ramanathan theorem, see for example Flenner
[
19
]
or Bogomolov
[
3
]
, cf.
[
28
, §7]
, it seems that all known effective bounds on
l_{0}
depend on the rank and the discriminant of
\mathscr{E}
.
However, in the notations from Introduction, when
k\to\infty
, both rank and discriminant of
E_{k}
tend to infinity, and so it seems implausible that the value
l_{0}
can be chosen uniformly for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Remark also that there are some results that relate the Harder-Narasimhan slopes of a sheaf with the Harder-Narasimhan slopes of its restriction to a curve associated with any fixed value
l_{0}
, see for example Grauert-Mülich theorem, cf.
[
28
, §3]
, or Langer
[
31
, Theorem 3.1]
.
To the best of authors knowledge, none of these implies Theorem
1.2
directly.
Our approach for Theorem
1.2
is then, by necessity, a different one, and it relies on subadditive properties enjoyed by the measures
\eta_{l}^{HN|C}
and
\eta^{HN}
, coming from the submultiplicative nature of Harder-Narasimhan filtrations and the results of Section
3
.

We will first establish the easier part of Theorem
1.2
about the minimal slopes.
For this, we conserve the notations from Introduction and (
3.11
).
Directly from Theorem
4.1
, the remark below it and Lemma
2.11
, we deduce the following result.

Lemma 4.3
.

The following convergence takes place

\lim_{l\to\infty}\frac{\mu^{HN}_{\min}(E_{k}|_{C},l)}{l_{1}\cdots l_{m-1}}=\mu^{HN}_{\min}(E_{k}),

(4.1)

where
l=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{m-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{*(m-1)}
.
Moreover, the above limit becomes stationary for
l
big enough (i.e. for big enough
l_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, and
l_{i}\geq l_{0}
,

), and we always have

\frac{\mu^{HN}_{\min}(E_{k}|_{C},l)}{l_{1}\cdots l_{m-1}}\leq\mu^{HN}_{\min}(E_{k}).

(4.2)

Remark 4.4
.

The same proof shows that the analogous statements hold (with inverse sign in (
4.2
)) for the maximal slope.

We will also use the following statement, the proof of which is left to the reader.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
c_{k,l}\in\real
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
l\in\mathbb{N}^{*(m-1)}
, be such that

increases in

, and the limit
\lim_{l\to\infty}c_{k,l}
exists and satisfies

.
Then
\lim_{l\to\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}c_{k,l}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{l\to\infty}c_{k,l}
.

Proof of the first convergence of (
1.4

By (

) and the definitions of

and

\lim_{l\to\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mu^{HN}_{\min}(E_{k}|_{C},l)}{kl_{1}\cdots l_{m-1}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{l\to\infty}\frac{\mu^{HN}_{\min}(E_{k}|_{C},l)}{kl_{1}\cdots l_{m-1}},

(4.3)

where

goes to infinity along any subsequence which we assume from now on to be multiplicative (as

,

By this and Lemma

Remark 4.6

Curiously, the analogous proof for the maximal slope doesn’t work due to Remark

We will now concentrate on the proof of the rest of Theorem

Theorem 4.7

For any convex non-increasing Lipschitz function

Lemma 4.8

Let

,

We will first establish the weak convergence of measures.

(4.8)

Proposition 4.9

( Chen
[
7
, Proposition 3.3.3]
)
.

For any convex non-increasing function
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\real\to\real
and any submultiplicative filtration
\mathcal{F}
on the ring

is subadditive.

Remark that if the fibers of

were projective spaces, then Theorem
4.7
would already follow from Proposition
4.9

Proposition
4.9

{\rm{Sym}}V
is replaced by a section ring of an arbitrary complex projective manifold.
To circumvent this problem, we need a more refined argument, and our solution, which is inspired by
[
7

More precisely, Noether normalization lemma, cf.
[
14
, Theorem 13.3]
, says that for any projective manifold
Y
of dimension

L
over
Y
, there is

, and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n+1}\in H^{0}(Y,L^{d})

, and such that the subring,
R_{(d)}(Y,L)
, of

, defined as

, is a finitely generated module over the (polynomial) subring

induced by

, i.e.
A={\rm{Sym}}\langle s_{1},\ldots,s_{n+1}\rangle
.

Now, let
\mathcal{F}
be an arbitrary bounded submultiplicative filtration on
R(Y,L)
.
The following proposition says that the spectral measure of the filtration
\mathcal{F}
on

can be reconstructed from the spectral measure of the restriction of the filtration
\mathcal{F}
to

Proposition 4.10

( Chen
[
7
, proof of Theorem 3.4.3]
)

For any convex non-increasing Lipshitz function
\phi

, we have

\Big|\int\phi\cdot dH^{kd}_{*}(\eta(\mathcal{F},A_{kd}))-\int\phi\cdot dH^{kd}_{*}(\eta(\mathcal{F},H^{0}(Y,L^{kd})))\Big|\leq\frac{C}{k}.

(4.11)

Remark 4.11

A combination of Propositions

to prove the weak convergence in Theorem

Our proof for Theorem

In order to explain the latter statement and its proof, we first recall the main steps from

Lemma 4.12

( Chen

(4.12)

where the jumping measures,

We denote by

As

Then, as

, of
R_{(d)}(Y,L)/B
is non-empty.
Hence, the Krull dimension of the
A
-module
R_{(d)}(Y,L)/B
, which is defined as the Krull dimension of
A/{\rm{Ann}}(R_{(d)}(Y,L)/B)
, is strictly smaller that the Krull dimension of
A
.
Since Krull dimension of a module coincides with the degree of its Hilbert polynomial, see Serre
[
43
, Proposition 6, p. 276]
, we conclude that there is a constant such that the dimension of the
k
-th graded part,
H^{0}(Y,L^{kd})/B_{kd}
, of
R_{(d)}(Y,L)/B
is smaller than
Ck^{\dim Y-1}
.
We conclude by this and Lemma
4.12
that for any bounded function
\phi
, there is
C>0
, such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

\Big|\int\phi\cdot dH^{kd}_{*}(\eta(\mathcal{F},B_{kd}))-\int\phi\cdot dH^{kd}_{*}(\eta(\mathcal{F},H^{0}(Y,L^{kd})))\Big|\leq\frac{C}{k}.

(4.13)

By (
4.13
), we see that Proposition
4.10
will follow if we prove the following result.

Lemma 4.13
.

For any homogeneous element
x\in H^{0}(Y,L^{d_{0}})
,
d_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
d|d_{0}
, the spectral measures of the restriction of the filtration
\mathcal{F}
to the submodules
xA
and
A
compare as follows.
For any convex non-increasing function
\phi
on , there is
C>0
, such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

\Big|\int\phi\cdot dH^{kd}_{*}(\eta(\mathcal{F},(xA)_{kd}))-\int\phi\cdot dH^{kd}_{*}(\eta(\mathcal{F},A_{kd}))\Big|\leq\frac{C}{k}.

(4.14)

Proof.

In order to prove this, remark that since the
{\rm{Frac}}(A)
-vector space
{\rm{Frac}}(A)\otimes_{A}R_{(d)}(Y,L)
is finitely generated, there is
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
N\leq r+1
, and homogeneous elements
a_{0}\in A_{d_{1}},a_{1}\in A_{d_{1}+d_{0}},\ldots,a_{N}\in A_{d_{1}+Nd_{0}}
,
a_{N}\neq 0
, for some
d_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, such that
d|d_{1}
and

a_{0}x^{N}+a_{1}x^{N-1}+\cdots+a_{N}=0.

From
[
7
, (61)]
, for any non-increasing function
\psi
on , we have

\int\psi\cdot d\eta(\mathcal{F},A_{kd})\geq\int\psi\cdot d\eta(\mathcal{F},(xA)_{kd+d_{0}})\geq\int\psi\cdot d\eta(\mathcal{F},(a_{N}A)_{kd+d_{1}+Nd_{0}}).

(4.15)

Now, remark that the Krull dimension of the
A
-module
A/a_{N}A
, is strictly smaller that the Krull dimension of
A
, as
a_{N}\neq 0
.
Hence, as in (
4.13
), we deduce that for any bounded function
\psi
on , there is
C>0
, such that we have

\Big|\int\psi\cdot d\eta(\mathcal{F},(a_{N}A)_{kd})-\int\psi\cdot d\eta(\mathcal{F},A_{kd})\Big|\leq\frac{C}{k}.

(4.16)

Also, for any Lipschitz function
\psi
on , and any
d_{3}\in\real
, by the boundness of the filtration
\mathcal{F}
, there is
C>0
, such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

\Big|\int\psi\cdot dH^{kd}_{*}\eta(\mathcal{F},A_{kd})-\int\psi\cdot dH^{kd+d_{3}}_{*}\eta(\mathcal{F},A_{kd})\Big|\leq\frac{C}{k}.

(4.17)

From (
4.15
), (
4.16
), (
4.17
) and the fact that the limit, as
k\to\infty
, of
\int\psi\cdot dH^{kd}_{*}\eta(\mathcal{F},A_{kd})
exists, which follows from Proposition
4.9
, we deduce (
4.14
).
∎

We will now explain that a part of the above reasoning can be done in a family setting.
More precisely, consider a surjective flat holomorphic map
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to B
between complex projective manifolds
X
and
B
of dimensions
n+m
and
m
.
Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle over
X
, which is relatively ample with respect to
\pi
.
We argue that there is a subset
S\subset B
, which is a countable union of analytic subsets, and
d^{*}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that the following holds.
For any
b\in B\setminus S
, there is
d(b)\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, verifying
d(b)\leq d^{*}
, and
s_{1}(b),\ldots,s_{n+1}(b)\in H^{0}(X_{b},L|^{d(b)}_{X_{b}})
, which are algebraically independent in the section ring,
R(X_{b},L|_{X_{b}})
, and such that
R_{(d(b))}(X_{b},L|_{X_{b}})
is a finitely generated module over the (polynomial) subring
A(b)\subset R_{(d(b))}(X_{b},L|_{X_{b}})
induced by
s_{1}(b),\ldots,s_{n+1}(b)
.
Moreover, there are
r^{*},d_{0}^{*}\in\mathbb{N}
and
C>0
, such that for any
b\in B\setminus S
, there are
r(b),d_{0}(b)\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, verifying
r(b)\leq r^{*},d_{0}(b)\leq d_{0}^{*}
, and
x_{1}(b),\ldots,x_{r(b)}(b)\in H^{0}(X_{b},L|^{d_{0}(b)}_{X_{b}})
, such that the
A(b)
-module
B(b)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=x_{1}(b)A(b)+\cdots+x_{r(b)}(b)A(b)
is a free
A(b)
-submodule of
R_{(d(b))}(X_{b},L|_{X_{b}})
, such that

\dim H^{0}(X_{b},L|^{d(b)k}_{X_{b}})-\dim B(b)_{{d(b)k}}\leq Ck^{n-1}.

(4.18)

To see this, we first recall that in an arbitrary vector bundle, the rank of a set of holomorphic sections at a given point is a lower semicontinuous function (in Zariski topology).
We pick an arbitrary
b_{0}\in S
, and construct the sections
s_{1}(b_{0}),\ldots,s_{n+1}(b_{0})\in H^{0}(X_{b_{0}},L|^{d(b_{0})}_{X_{b_{0}}})
by Noether normalization lemma as we did above.
Extend them holomorphically (as sections of
R^{0}\pi_{*}L^{d(b_{0})}
) to
s_{1}(b),\ldots,s_{N(b_{0})}(b)\in H^{0}(X_{b},L|^{d(b_{0})}_{X_{b}})
for
b
in a small neighborhood
U
of
b_{0}
.

We argue that away from a countable union of analytic subsets in
U
, the sections
s_{1}(b),\ldots,s_{n+1}(b)\in H^{0}(X_{b},L|^{d(b_{0})}_{X_{b}})
are algebraically independent.
Indeed, this follows from the above lower semicontinuity property, as for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the set of
k
-homogeneous monomials in
s_{1}(b),\ldots,s_{n+1}(b)
,
b\in U
, is free away from an analytic subset (which is a proper subset of
U
, as it doesn’t contain
b_{0}
).

Now, we fix homogeneous elements
x_{1}(b_{0}),\ldots,x_{r(b_{0})}(b_{0})
, in
R_{(d(b_{0}))}(X_{b_{0}},L|_{X_{b_{0}}})
, such that the associated
A(b_{0})
-module
B(b_{0})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=x_{1}(b_{0})A(b_{0})+\cdots+x_{r(b_{0})}(b_{0})A(b_{0})
is free, and there is
C>0
, such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have (
4.18
) for
b\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=b_{0}
.
We extend
x_{1}(b_{0}),\ldots,x_{r(b_{0})}(b_{0})
holomorphically in a neighborhood of
b_{0}
.
A similar argument to the one before shows that for any
b
in this neighborhood away from a countable union of analytic subsets, the
A(b)
-module
B(b)
, defined analogously to
B(b_{0})
, is free.
Then, since by the flatness,
\dim H^{0}(X_{b},L|^{d(b)k}_{X_{b}})
is a locally constant function of
b\in S
, cf.
[
24
, Theorem III.9.9]
, we conclude that inequality (
4.18
) holds for
b
in this neighborhood away from a countable union of analytic subsets.

We then repeat this procedure for every point
b_{0}\in B
, and use the compactness of
B
, to extract a finite cover of
B
by open subsets.
In conclusion, a direct repetition of the argument in the proof of Proposition
4.10
yields the following result.

Proposition 4.14
.

There is a countable union of proper analytic subsets
S
in
B
, such that the following holds.
For any
D>0
, any sequence
b_{l}\in B\setminus S
,
l\in\mathbb{N}
, any family of submultiplicative filtrations
\mathcal{F}(l)
on
R(X_{b_{l}},L|_{X_{b_{l}}})
, which is uniformly bounded in the sense that the absolute value of the maximal and the minimal jumping numbers on degree
k
-part are bounded by
Dk
(uniformly on
l
), and any convex Lipshitz function
\phi
on , there is
C>0
, such that for any sufficiently divisible
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, and any
l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

\Big|\int\phi\cdot dH^{k}_{*}(\eta(\mathcal{F}(l),A_{k}(b_{l})))-\int\phi\cdot dH^{k}_{*}(\eta(\mathcal{F}(l),H^{0}(X_{b_{l}},L|^{k}_{X_{b_{l}}})))\Big|\leq\frac{C}{k}.

(4.19)

Finally, to finish the argument, we need the following statement.

Proposition 4.15
.

There is
C>0
, such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
l=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{m-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{*(m-1)}
, for any generic regular curve

, which is a complete intersection in

, we have

\mu^{HN}_{\min}(E_{k}|_{C},l)\geq-Ckl_{1}\cdots l_{m-1},\qquad\mu^{HN}_{\max}(E_{k}|_{C},l)\leq Ckl_{1}\cdots l_{m-1}.

(4.20)

Proof.

The result now follows from the linear (in
k
) bounds on
\mu^{HN}_{\min}(E_{k}|_{C},(1,\ldots,1))
,
\mu^{HN}_{\max}(E_{k}|_{C},(1,\ldots,1))
, cf. Proposition
2.10
, and Proposition
3.17
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

We denote by

the subset from Proposition

For any

5

Numerical expressions for asymptotic slopes

The main goal of this section is to prove Corollary
1.4
.
For this, let us first recall the results of Xu-Zhuang from
[
48
]
.
We conserve the notations from the introduction, and assume for the moment that
\dim B=1
.
For a class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X)
, we then define the
nef (resp. psef) threshold of
\alpha
with respect to
\pi
as the supremum over all
T\in\real
, such that
\alpha-T(\pi^{*}[\omega_{B,0}]/\int_{B}[\omega_{B,0}])
is nef (resp. psef) for some Kähler class
[\omega_{B,0}]
on
B
.

Proposition 5.1
.

If
\dim B=1
, then
\eta_{\min}^{HN}
(resp.
\eta_{\max}^{HN}
) equals to nef (resp. psef) threshold of
c_{1}(L)
with respect to
\pi
.
Moreover, we have
\eta_{\min}^{HN}=\essinf\eta^{HN}
.

Proof.

In
[
48
, Proposition 2.28]
, authors proved that
\esssup\eta^{HN}
equals to psef threshold of
c_{1}(L)
with respect to
\pi
.
From this and Theorem
2.2
, we deduce the statement of Proposition
5.1
for
\eta_{\max}^{HN}
.
In the proof of
[
48
, Lemma 2.26]
, authors established that
\eta_{\min}^{HN}
is not smaller than nef threshold of
c_{1}(L)
with respect to
\pi
.
This finishes the proof of Proposition
5.1
, as by Theorem
2.2
, we have
\eta_{\min}^{HN}\leq\essinf\eta^{HN}
, and by
[
48
, Proposition 2.28]
,
\essinf\eta^{HN}
equals to nef threshold of
c_{1}(L)
with respect to
\pi
.
∎

Let us now deduce formulas (
1.5
) from Proposition
5.1
. By the definition of nefness, we see that the nef threshold of
c_{1}(L)
with respect to
\pi
equals

\inf_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{irr}}\frac{\int_{C}c_{1}(L)\cdot\int_{C}\pi|_{C}^{*}[\omega_{B,0}]}{\int_{B}[\omega_{B,0}]},

(5.1)

where
\mathcal{C}_{irr}
was defined before (
1.5
).
This gives us the first formula from (
1.5
) by the definition of the topological degree.
The proof of the second formula is identical, except that it is necessary to use the characterization of the dual to the pseudoeffective cone in terms of movable curves due to Boucksom-Demailly-Paun-Peternell
[
5
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

Directly from Proposition
5.1
, we have

\eta_{\min,l}^{HN|C}=\sup\bigg\{T\in\real\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\text{ the class }c_{1}(L)|_{C}-T\cdot\frac{\pi^{*}[\omega_{B,0}]|_{C}}{\int_{C}[\omega_{B,0}]}\text{ is nef}\bigg\},

(5.2)

where the curves
C=C(l)
are as in Theorem
1.2
.
Similar formulas hold for the maximal asymptotic slope.
We deduce Corollary
1.4
by (
5.2
), the identity
\int_{C}[\omega_{B,0}]=l^{m-1}\cdot\int_{B}[\omega_{B,0}]\cdots[\omega_{B,m-1}]
and Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Let us now give another interpretation of Corollary
1.4
.
We say that a relatively ample
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
L
on
X
is
stably
([\omega_{B,1}],\ldots,[\omega_{B,m-1}])
-generically fibered nef with respect to
\pi
if for some (or any) ample line bundle
L_{0}
on
X
, for any
\epsilon>0
,
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Q}
, the
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
L\otimes L_{0}^{\epsilon}
is
([\omega_{B,1}],\ldots,[\omega_{B,m-1}])
-generically fibered nef with respect to
\pi
.

Proposition 5.2
.

A relatively ample line bundle
L
is stably
([\omega_{B,1}],\ldots,[\omega_{B,m-1}])
-generically fibered nef with respect to
\pi
if and only if
\eta_{\min}^{HN}\geq 0
.

Proof.

We assume first that
\eta_{\min}^{HN}\geq 0
.
By Corollary
1.4
, for any
\epsilon>0
,
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Q}
, the
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
L\otimes\pi^{*}L_{1}^{\epsilon}
is
([\omega_{B,1}],\ldots,[\omega_{B,m-1}])
-generically fibered nef with respect to
\pi
.
But then for any ample line bundle
L_{0}
on
X
, any
\epsilon>0
,
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Q}
, the
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
L\otimes L_{0}^{\epsilon}
is
([\omega_{B,1}],\ldots,[\omega_{B,m-1}])
-generically fibered nef with respect to
\pi
, which means that
L
is stably
([\omega_{B,1}],\ldots,[\omega_{B,m-1}])
-generically fibered nef with respect to
\pi
.

Inversely, assume that
L
is stably
([\omega_{B,1}],\ldots,[\omega_{B,m-1}])
-generically fibered nef with respect to
\pi
.
Let
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
be such that the line bundle
L_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=L\otimes\pi^{*}L_{1}^{N}
is ample (such
N
exists since
L
is relatively ample).
Then for any
\epsilon>0
,
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Q}
, the
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
L\otimes L_{0}^{\epsilon}
is
([\omega_{B,1}],\ldots,[\omega_{B,m-1}])
-generically fibered nef with respect to
\pi
.
However, since

([\omega_{B,1}],\ldots,[\omega_{B,m-1}])
-generic fibered nef threshold of

∎

Remark 5.3

From the proof, we see that in the definition of stably

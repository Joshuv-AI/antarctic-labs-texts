In this paper, we examine iterated Eichler-Shimura integrals
of depth two in more detail. We first recall the definition of usual
Eichler integrals. There are two kinds of them — scalar-valued and
polynomial-valued ones — and they are given as follows. Let
f\in\mathrm{S}_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
,
be a cusp form of level one,
X
a formal variable, and
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
in the Poincaré upper half plane, then the polynomial-valued
Eichler integral
I_{f}(\,\cdot\,;X)
and the scalar-valued Eichler integral
\mathcal{E}_{f}
are given by

\displaystyle I_{f}(\tau;X)=\int_{\tau}^{i\infty}f(z)\,(X-z)^{k-2}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{z}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau)=\int_{\tau}^{i\infty}f(z)\,(\tau-z)^{k-2}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{z}\text{.}

(0.1)

Note that
I_{f}(\tau;\tau)=\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau)
. If
f,g\in\mathrm{S}_{k}
are cusp forms
the depth two Eichler-Shimura integral is given by

\displaystyle I_{f,g}(\tau;X,Y)=\int_{\tau}^{i\infty}f(z)\,(X-z)^{k-2}\,I_{g}(z;Y)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{z}\text{.}

(0.2)

This Eichler-Shimura integral is well-understood and its definition can readily
be generalized to arbitrary depths

,
14
]

We provide a scalar-valued analogue
\mathcal{E}_{f,g}
to

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{f,g}(\tau)=\int_{\tau}^{i\infty}f(z)\,\mathcal{E}_{g}(z)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{z}\text{.}

(0.3)

We are not aware of any previous occurrence of
\mathcal{E}_{f,g}
in the literature, but remark that its definition generalizes the one of
\mathcal{E}_{f}
in a straightforward way. The first main
focus of this paper is to provide a connection between the geometrically motivated

I_{f,g}
and its classical counterpart
\mathcal{E}_{f,g}
paralleling the connection of

I_{f}
to
\mathcal{E}_{f}
. The second main focus of this paper is to provide a
framework that enables the effective computation of
\mathcal{E}_{f,g}
following the
approach taken in
[
1
]
.

We study
\mathcal{E}_{f,g}
and

using the language of vector-valued modular forms.
The work of Brown

]

implies via arguments of Mertens–Raum

that

is a component of a vector-valued modular form. However,

of our first theorem.

There is a
\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)

Echoing

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{\rho}_{f,g}:\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})&\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}\big({\mathbb{C}[X,Y]_{k-2}\oplus\mathbb{C}[X]_{k-2}\oplus\mathbb{C}}\big)\text{,}\\

0&\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X)(\gamma)&\phi_{I_{f}}(\gamma)\\

(0.4)

where for a cusp form

\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}
. We

.

Our characterization of pairs

In Section
3
, we provide a framework
based on vector-valued Eisenstein series
that allows for effective
computation of modular forms of type
\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}
, that is, of forms
that transform like the representation
\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}

[

To prepare for the setup of this framework, we record that the representation

features a function

that behaves similar to a
1

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \psi(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})&=\psi(\gamma_{1})+\psi(\gamma_{2})+\big\langle\phi_{2}(\gamma^{-1}),\phi_{1}(\gamma)\big\rangle\text{,}\quad\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\text{,}\\

We also remark that in the case of

and

Let
k
be an integer, and let
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
be a pair of parabolic cocycles

,

by

\displaystyle E_{k}(\tau;\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}})=\big(E_{k}^{[2]}(\tau;\phi_{1},\phi_{2}),\;E_{k}^{[1]}(\tau;\phi_{1}),\;E_{k}\big)^{T}\text{,}

(0.7)

where

is the classical Eisenstein series of weight

\displaystyle E_{k}^{[1]}(\tau;\phi_{1})=\sum_{[\gamma]\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}\frac{\phi_{1}(\gamma^{-1})}{(c\tau+d)^{k}}\quad\text{and}\quad E_{k}^{[2]}(\tau;\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\sum_{[\gamma]\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}\frac{\psi(\gamma^{-1})}{(c\tau+d)^{k}}\text{.}

The series

is called the generalized second order
Eisenstein series of type
(\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X),\mathbf{1})
associated to
(\phi_{1},1)
and was
a subject of study in
[

, in which it was shown that it
converges absolutely and locally uniformly on

for

, and where its
Fourier series expansion was provided. As for
E_{k}^{[2]}(\tau;\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
,
we show that it converges in the same region and provide its Fourier series expansion

.

To state our last main theorem, we write
\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}
for the graded ring of

\rho

for the

Furthermore, if

\displaystyle(I:f^{\infty})=\big\{g\in M:\exists n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.\,f^{n}g\in I\big\}\text{.}

Recall also that given a parabolic
\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)
-cocycle
\phi
, we have the representation

\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X):\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\to\mathrm{GL}({\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}})

\displaystyle(\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X))(\gamma)=\begin{pmatrix}1&\big\langle\phi(\gamma^{-1}),\;\cdot\;\big\rangle\\

We provide the following theorem.

Theorem C
.

Let
\mathsf{d}\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{\geq k_{0}}(\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}})=\mathrm{span}\;\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}\big\{E_{k}(\tau;\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}})\,:\,k\geq k_{0}\big\}\text{.}

Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}(\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}})=\big(\mathrm{E}_{\geq k_{0}}(\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}})+\iota\big(\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X))\big)\colon\Delta^{\infty}\big)

where
\iota(f,g)=(f,g,0)^{T}
.

Since
\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X))
is described

as the saturation at

Preliminaries

In this section, we define the notation we use throughout the paper, and revisit
the basic theory of Eichler cohomology, extensions of arithmetic types, and vector-valued
modular forms. For further details, we direct the reader to
[
19
]
and
[
16
]
.

The special linear group of degree two over the integers, is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})=\Big\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2\times 2}\,:\,ad-bc=1\Big\}\text{.}

It is a fact that
\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
is generated by the matrices
S=(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix})

and
T=(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})
. The parabolic subgroup

\Gamma_{\infty}\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
is given by
\Gamma_{\infty}=\langle T,-1\rangle
. We also let
U=TS
.

The upper-half plane
\mathbb{H}
is given by
\mathbb{H}=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{Im}(\tau)>0\}
. For

\gamma=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\big)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
and
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
,
the Möbius action is given by

\displaystyle\gamma\tau=\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\text{.}

Given a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
V
, a function
f:\mathbb{H}\to V
, an integer
k
, and an element

\gamma=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\big)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
, we define
a new function
f|_{k}\gamma:\mathbb{H}\to V
by

\displaystyle\big(f\big|_{k}\gamma\big)(\tau)=(c\tau+d)^{-k}f(\gamma\tau)\text{.}

We record that this gives rise to a right-action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
on the space of
holomorphic functions from
\mathbb{H}
to
V
, which we call the slash-action.

Let
V
be a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space with a norm
\|\cdot\|
, and let
f:\mathbb{H}\to V
be
a function. If there exists a real number
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})

it holds uniformly in
\mathrm{Re}(\tau)
that

\displaystyle\big\|\big(f\big|_{k}\gamma\big)(\tau)\big\|=O(\mathrm{Im}(\tau)^{a})\text{ as }\mathrm{Im}(\tau)\to\infty\text{,}

(1.1)

we say that
f
has moderate growth. If instead we have that for all
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
it
holds that

\displaystyle\big\|\big(f\big|_{k}\gamma\big)(\tau)\big\|\to 0\text{ as }\mathrm{Im}(\tau)\to\infty\text{,}

(1.2)

we say that
f
is cuspidal. Note that the conditions (
1.1
)
and (
1.2
) are independent of the choice of norm on
V
.

Let
k
be an integer. Then if
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic function of moderate
growth satisfying that

\displaystyle f\big|_{k}\gamma=f\text{ for all }\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\text{,}

we call
f
a (scalar-valued) modular form of weight
k
. If
f
is cuspidal, it is called a cusp
form. The set of modular forms of weight
k
forms a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
denoted by
\mathrm{M}_{k}
. The corresponding subspace of cusp forms is denoted by
\mathrm{S}_{k}
. If
f\in\mathrm{M}_{k}
,
we let the conjugate modular form
f^{c}
be given by

\displaystyle f^{c}(\tau)=\overline{f(-\overline{\tau})}\text{.}

Note that if
f
has the Fourier series expansion
f(\tau)=\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}e(n\tau)
,
then
f^{c}
has the Fourier series expansion
f^{c}(\tau)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{c_{n}}e(n\tau)
.

Finally, we denote the trivial representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
by
\mathbf{1}
, so that

V(\mathbf{1})=\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbf{1}(\gamma)z=z
for all
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

1.1

Cohomology and extensions of arithmetic types

An arithmetic type is a finite-dimensional complex representation
of a subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
. In the present paper, we restrict our scope
to arithmetic types of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
.

If
\rho
and
\sigma
are arithmetic types, the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space of
(\rho,\sigma)
-cocycles
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{1}(\rho,\sigma)=\big\{f:\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\to\mathrm{Hom}(V(\rho),V(\sigma))\,:\,f(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})=\sigma(\gamma_{1})f(\gamma_{2})+f(\gamma_{1})\rho(\gamma_{2})\big\}\text{,}

and the subspace of
(\rho,\sigma)
-coboundaries is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}^{1}(\rho,\sigma)=\big\{f:\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\to\mathrm{Hom}(V(\rho),V(\sigma))\,:\,\exists h\in\mathrm{Hom}(V(\rho),V(\sigma)).\,f(\gamma)=\sigma(\gamma)h-h\rho(\gamma)\big\}\text{.}

If a cocycle
\phi\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}(\rho,\sigma)
vanishes on every element of
\Gamma_{\infty}
,
we call it parabolic. The space of all parabolic cocycles (or coboundaries)
is denoted by
\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}(\rho,\sigma)
(or
\mathrm{B}^{1}_{\text{pb}}(\rho,\sigma)
).
We will identify
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1},V(\sigma))
with
V(\sigma)
.

Given a parabolic cocycle
\phi\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}(\sigma,\rho)
, we define the arithmetic
type
\rho\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi}\sigma
by
V(\rho\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi}\sigma)=V(\rho)\oplus V(\sigma)
and

\displaystyle(\rho\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi}\sigma)(\gamma)(v,v^{\prime})=\big(\rho(\gamma)v+\phi(\gamma)v^{\prime},\sigma(\gamma)v^{\prime}\big)\text{.}

(1.3)

The first parabolic cohomology group is the quotient

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\text{pb}}(\rho,\sigma)=\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}(\rho,\sigma)/\mathrm{B}^{1}_{\text{pb}}(\rho,\sigma)
. We record
that
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\text{pb}}(\sigma,\rho)
is isomorphic to the group of parabolic extension
classes of
\sigma
by
\rho
, denoted by
\mathrm{Ext}_{\text{pb}}(\sigma,\rho)
. In particular,
the following map is a well-defined isomorphism of groups

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}_{\text{pb}}(\sigma,\rho)\ni[0\to\rho\to\rho\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi}\sigma\to\sigma\to 0]\longmapsto\phi+\mathrm{B}^{1}_{\text{pb}}(\sigma,\rho)\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\text{pb}}(\sigma,\rho)\text{.}

In particular this means that given cocycles
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
, it holds that

\rho\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{1}}\sigma
is isomorphic to
\rho\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}}\sigma
if and only if

\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are cohomologous.

Note that by the cocycle relations, an arbitrary cocycle
\phi\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}(\rho,\sigma)

is fully determined by its value at
S
.

1.2

Vector-valued modular forms

Let
k
be an integer and let
\rho
an be arithmetic type. Given a function
f:\mathbb{H}\to V(\rho)

and an element
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
, we define
f\big|_{k}\gamma:\mathbb{H}\to V(\rho)
by

\displaystyle\big(f\big|_{k,\rho}\gamma\big)(\tau)=\rho(\gamma^{-1})\big(f\big|_{k}\gamma\big)(\tau)\text{.}

A vector-valued modular form of type
\rho
and weight
k
, is a holomorphic function
of moderate growth
f:\mathbb{H}\to V(\rho)
satisfying that

\displaystyle f\big|_{k,\rho}\gamma=f\text{ for all }\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\text{.}

If a vector-valued modular form
f
of type
\rho
and weight
k
is cuspidal,
we call it a cusp form. The space of vector-valued modular forms of weight
k

and type
\rho
is denoted by
\mathrm{M}_{k}(\rho)
. The corresponding subspace of
cusp forms is denoted by
\mathrm{S}_{k}(\rho)
.

We remark that scalar-valued modular forms of weight
k
are the same
as vector-valued modular forms of type
\mathbf{1}
. That is, we have
the equalities
\mathrm{M}_{k}(\mathbf{1})=\mathrm{M}_{k}
and
\mathrm{S}_{k}(\mathbf{1})=\mathrm{S}_{k}
.

For an arithmetic type
\rho
we let the graded module of modular forms of type

\rho
be given by

\displaystyle{\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}(\rho)=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{M}_{k}(\rho)}\text{.}

If
M
is an
\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}
-module,
I\subseteq M
is a submodule of
M
, and

f\in\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}
then we recall that the saturation of
I
at
f
is given by

\displaystyle(I:f^{\infty})=\big\{g\in M:\exists n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.\,f^{n}g\in I\big\}\text{.}

1.3

Symmetric powers

Let
\mathsf{d}\geq 0
be an integer. Then we let
\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}
be the space of polynomials
with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
of degree at most
\mathsf{d}
. We define the arithmetic type

\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)
by
V(\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X))=\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}
, and

\displaystyle\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)(\gamma)p=p\big|_{-\mathsf{d}}\gamma^{-1}=(-cX+a)^{\mathsf{d}}p\big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{dX-b}{-cX+a}$}}\big)\text{,}\quad\text{where }\gamma=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\big)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\text{ and }p\in\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}\text{.}

We remark that
\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)
is a model of the
\mathsf{d}
th symmetric power of the standard
representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
, explaining the notation. The group ring

\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})]
acts linearly on
\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}
by

\displaystyle\gamma.p=\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)(\gamma)p\text{ and }(c_{1}\gamma_{1}+c_{2}\gamma_{2})p=c_{1}(\gamma_{1}.p)+c_{2}(\gamma_{2}.p)\text{,}

where
\gamma,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
and
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
. As mentioned in the introduction
there exists a symmetric pairing
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle:\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}\times\mathbb{C}[Y]_{\mathsf{d}}\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\displaystyle\langle p,q\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{\mathsf{d}}(-1)^{i}\Bigl(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\sfd}{i}$}}\Bigr)^{-1}p_{i}q_{\mathsf{d}-i}\text{,}

satisfying
\langle\gamma.p,\gamma.q\rangle=\langle p,q\rangle
for any
p\in\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}
,
q\in\mathbb{C}[Y]_{\mathsf{d}}
,
and
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
. Since
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
is invariant and bilinear, we
have an equivariant contraction map
\pi:\mathbb{C}[X,Y]_{\mathsf{d}}\to\mathbb{C}
given
by
\pi(p\otimes q)=\langle p,q\rangle
. There is also a related antisymmetric bilinear form

\langle\!\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle\!\rangle:\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle p,q\rangle\!\rangle=\big\langle T^{-1}.p-T.p,\,q\big\rangle\text{.}

If
p=\sum p_{i}X^{i}\in\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}
, then we let
\overline{p}=\sum\overline{p_{i}}X^{i}\in\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}
.
Note that the action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
commutes with conjugation, so that
\gamma.\overline{p}=\overline{\gamma.p}
.

Henceforth, we will identify
\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}\otimes\mathbb{C}[Y]_{\mathsf{d}}
with
\mathbb{C}[X,Y]_{\mathsf{d}}
;
the space of polynomials in
X
and
Y
of degree at most
\mathsf{d}
in
X
and
Y
. Furthermore,
we will use the shorthand notation
\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X,Y):=\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\otimes\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(Y)
. This
coincides with the definition we used in the introduction.

1.4

The Eichler-Shimura isomorphism

We shall now briefly describe the Eichler-Shimura isomorphism between scalar-valued
cusp forms of weight
k\geq 2
and
\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X))
. This exposition
follows
[
12
]
. It holds for arbitrary integers
\mathsf{d}\geq 0
, but the case relevant in this paper is
\mathsf{d}=k-2
.

The space of parabolic
(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X))
-cocycles can be completely
described as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)=\big\{\phi:\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\to\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}\,:\,\phi(-I)=\phi(T)=0\text{ and }(1+S).\phi(S)=(1+U+U^{2}).\phi(S)=0\big\}\text{,}

Note that
\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)
is closed under complex conjugation.
The space
\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}
splits up into its “even part” and “odd part”. That is, we have
\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}=\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}^{+}\oplus\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}^{-}
with

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}^{+}

\displaystyle=\big\{\textstyle\sum_{i=0}^{\mathsf{d}}p_{i}X^{i}\in\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}:p_{2j+1}=0\text{ for }0\leq 2j+1\leq\mathsf{d}\big\}

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}^{-}

\displaystyle=\big\{\textstyle\sum_{i=0}^{\mathsf{d}}p_{i}X^{i}\in\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}:p_{2j}=0\text{ for }0\leq 2j\leq\mathsf{d}\big\}\text{.}

We further write

\displaystyle W_{\mathsf{d}}=\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)(S)\text{,}\quad W_{\mathsf{d}}^{+}=W_{\mathsf{d}}\cap\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}^{+}\text{,}\quad\text{and}\quad W_{\mathsf{d}}^{-}=W_{\mathsf{d}}\cap\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}^{-}\text{.}

Let
f\in\mathrm{S}_{k}
be a cusp form of weight
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
. Then we define
the polynomial-valued Eichler integral of
f
as

\displaystyle I_{f}(\tau;X)=\int_{\tau}^{i\infty}f(z)\,(X-\tau)^{k-2}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{z}\text{,}\quad\text{where }\tau\in\mathbb{H}\text{.}

If the variable is understood from context, it is omitted from the notation. Note
that since
f
vanishes at the cusp,
I_{f}(\tau)
is well-defined. We also
see that for
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
we have that
I_{f}(\tau)\in\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}
. Given
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
,
we let

\displaystyle\phi_{I_{f}}(\gamma)=I_{f}\big|_{0,\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X)}(1-\gamma^{-1})=\int_{\gamma(i\infty)}^{i\infty}f(z)\,(X-\tau)^{k-2}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{z}\text{.}

We have that
\phi_{I_{f}}\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X)\big)
by construction, and
since elements of
\Gamma_{\infty}
stabilize the cusp we also have that

\phi_{I_{f}}\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X)\big)
. We now set
r_{f}=\phi_{I_{f}}(S)\in W_{k-2}
and

\displaystyle r_{f,n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(i\!t)t^{n}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{t}=\Gamma(n+1)(2\pi)^{-n-1}\,\mathrm{L}(f,n+1)\quad\text{for }0\leq n\leq k-2\text{,}

where
\mathrm{L}(f,\;\cdot\;)
denotes the Hecke
\mathrm{L}
-function associated to
f
. By
the binomial theorem, we see that

\displaystyle r_{f}=\sum_{n=0}^{k-2}i^{-n+1}\Bigl(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k-2}{n}$}}\Bigr)r_{n}(f)X^{k-2-n}\text{.}

Hence, we define
r^{+}_{f}\in W_{k-2}^{+}
and
r^{-}_{f}\in W_{k-2}^{-}
by

\displaystyle r^{+}_{f}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq n\leq k-2\\
2\mid n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{n/2}\Bigl(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k-2}{n}$}}\Bigr)r_{f,n}X^{k-2-n}\quad\text{and}\quad r^{-}_{f}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq n\leq k-2\\
2\nmid n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{(n-1)/2}\Bigl(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k-2}{n}$}}\Bigr)r_{f,n}X^{k-2-n}\text{,}

so that
r=r^{-}+ir^{+}
. We can now provide the Eichler-Shimura isomorphism.

Theorem
(Eichler-Shimura).

It holds that the maps

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}_{k}\ni f

\displaystyle\mapsto r^{-}_{f}\in W^{-}_{k-2}\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}_{k}\oplus\mathbb{C}\ni(f,z)

\displaystyle\mapsto r^{+}_{f}+z(X^{k-2}-1)\in W^{+}_{k-2}

are isomorphisms of
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces.

For proofs, see for example
[
18
]
or
[
13
]
.
We now state an important result related to the bilinear form
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle
.
Recall first that the Petersson inner product
(\cdot,\cdot):\mathrm{M}_{k}\times\mathrm{S}_{k}\to\mathbb{C}
is given by

\displaystyle(f,g)=\int_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\backslash\mathbb{H}}f(x+iy)\overline{g(x+iy)}y^{k}\frac{\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{x}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{y}}{y^{2}}

We have the following theorem, due to Haberland and Paşol-Popa.

Theorem
(Haberland
[
10
]
and Paşol-Popa
[
17
]
).

Let
k\geq 2
be an even integer, and let
f,g\in\mathrm{S}_{k}
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle r_{f},\overline{r_{g}}\rangle\!\rangle=-6(2i)^{k-1}(f,g)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle\!\langle r_{f},r_{g}\rangle\!\rangle=0\text{.}

(1.4)

To contextualize the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
in the next section, we recall the following:

Remark 1.1
.

Let
\mathsf{d}\geq 0
be an even integer and let
e=X^{\mathsf{d}}-1\in W_{\mathsf{d}}
. Then
the relations
(1+S).e=0
and
(1+U+U^{2}).e=0
imply that
\langle\!\langle e,q\rangle\!\rangle=0

for all
q\in W_{\mathsf{d}}
. Conversely, the Eichler-Shimura isomorphisms imply that
if an element
p\in W_{\mathsf{d}}
satisfies that
\langle\!\langle p,q\rangle\!\rangle=0
for all

q\in W_{\mathsf{d}}
, then
p\in\mathbb{C}\{e\}
. This means that

(W_{\mathsf{d}}/\mathbb{C}\{e\},\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle)
is a non-degenerate symplectic
vector space, and by applying the Eichler-Shimura isomorphisms to a basis of
orthonormalized Hecke eigenforms one obtains an explicit isomorphism
of
(W_{\mathsf{d}}/\mathbb{C}\{e\},\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle)
with the standard complex
symplectic vector space, given by

\displaystyle\Big(\mathbb{C}^{2D},(x,y)\mapsto x^{T}\begin{pmatrix}0&-1_{D}\\
1_{D}&0\end{pmatrix}y\Big)\text{,}

where
D=\dim(\mathrm{S}_{\mathsf{d}+2})
, and
1_{D}
denotes the
D\times D
identity matrix.

From extensions to Eichler-Shimura integrals

In this section, we show that polynomial- and scalar-valued depth two
Eichler-Shimura integrals can be regarded as components of vector-valued modular
forms of type
\widetilde{\rho}_{f,g}
and
\rho_{f,g}
, respectively.

2.1

The extension
\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}

Let
\mathsf{d}\geq 0
be an even integer. For
\phi\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)
,
we let

\displaystyle\phi^{\vee}(\gamma)(v)=\big\langle\phi(\gamma^{-1}),\,v\big\rangle\text{.}

(2.1)

The invariance of the pairing implies that
\phi^{\vee}\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X),\mathbf{1}\big)
,
and that (
2.1
) defines an isomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces from

\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)
to
\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X),\mathbf{1}\big)
.

Given parabolic cocycles
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)
and
a function
\psi:\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\to\mathbb{C}
, we let

\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\psi}:\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\to\mathrm{GL}\big({\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}[X]_{\mathsf{d}}\oplus\mathbb{C}}\big)
be given
by

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\psi}(\gamma)=\begin{pmatrix}1&\phi_{2}^{\vee}(\gamma)&\psi(\gamma)\\
0&\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)(\gamma)&\phi_{1}(\gamma)\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\text{,}\quad\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\text{.}

(2.2)

We have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\mathrm{pb}}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)
, and let
\psi:\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\to\mathbb{C}

be a function. Then the following are equivalent

1.

\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\psi}
is a representation,

2.

for all
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
it holds that
\psi(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})=\psi(\gamma_{1})+\psi(\gamma_{2})+\phi_{2}^{\vee}(\gamma_{1})\phi_{1}(\gamma_{2})
,

3.

(\phi_{2}^{\vee},\psi)\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\mathrm{pb}}\big(\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{1}}\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}\big)
,

4.

(\psi,\phi_{1})^{T}\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\mathrm{pb}}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)
.

Furthermore, if any of these conditions hold, then

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\psi}=\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{(\phi_{2}^{\vee},\psi)}\big(\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{1}}\mathbf{1}\big)=\big(\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\psi\\
\phi_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\big)}\mathbf{1}\text{.}

Proof.

Let
\rho=\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\psi}
and let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
. Then using
the cocycle relations, and the fact that
\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)
is a representation, we find that

\displaystyle\rho(\gamma_{1})\rho(\gamma_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}1&\phi_{2}^{\vee}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})&\psi(\gamma_{2})+\phi_{2}^{\vee}(\gamma_{1})\phi_{1}(\gamma_{2})+\psi(\gamma_{1})\\
0&\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})&\phi_{1}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\text{.}

We have that
\rho
is a representation if and only if it is a homomorphism
and therefore we see that (i) is equivalent to (ii).

Continuing, we have that
(\phi_{2}^{\vee},\psi)\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{1}}\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}\big)

if and only if

\displaystyle\big(\phi_{2}^{\vee}(\gamma_{2}),\psi(\gamma_{2})\big)+\big(\phi_{2}^{\vee}(\gamma_{1}),\psi(\gamma_{1})\big)\big(\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{1}}\mathbf{1}\big)(\gamma_{2})=\big(\phi_{2}^{\vee}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}),\psi(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})\big)\text{.}

On the other hand, we have that

\displaystyle\big(\phi_{2}^{\vee}(\gamma_{2}),\psi(\gamma_{2})\big)+\big(\phi_{2}^{\vee}(\gamma_{1}),\psi(\gamma_{1})\big)\big(\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{1}}\mathbf{1}\big)(\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle=\big(\phi_{2}^{\vee}(\gamma_{2})+\phi_{2}^{\vee}(\gamma_{1})\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)(\gamma_{2}),\,\psi(\gamma_{2})+\phi_{2}^{\vee}(\gamma_{1})\phi_{1}(\gamma_{2})+\psi(\gamma_{1})\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\phi_{2}^{\vee}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}),\,\psi(\gamma_{1})+\psi(\gamma_{2})+\phi_{2}^{\vee}(\gamma_{1})\phi_{2}(\gamma_{2})\big)\text{.}

Hence, it is clear that (iii) is equivalent to (ii). In the same way, we find that
(iv) is equivalent to (ii), which finishes the proof.

For a fixed pair of cocycles
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
there is at most one function
\psi

satisfying the conditions of Proposition
2.1
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathsf{d}\geq 0
be an even integer and let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\mathrm{pb}}(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X))
.
Then there exists a function
\psi
satisfying the conditions of Proposition
2.1

if and only if
\langle\!\langle\phi_{1}(S),\phi_{2}(S)\rangle\!\rangle=0
. Furthermore, if such a function
exists, it is unique and is given by

\displaystyle\psi(S)=-\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{2}$}}\phi_{2}^{\vee}(S)\phi_{1}(S)\text{.}

Proof.

Let
\psi:\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\to\mathbb{C}
be a function and let
\rho=\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\psi}
.
Recall that
\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
has the presentation
\langle S,T:S^{4}=(ST)^{6}=1\rangle
.
Hence we have that
\rho
is a representation if and only if

\displaystyle\rho(S)^{4}=1\text{ and }(\rho(S)\rho(T))^{6}=1\text{.}

However, we find that

\displaystyle\rho(S)^{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&2\psi(S)+\phi_{2}^{\vee}(S)\phi_{1}(S)\\
0&1_{\mathsf{d}}&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\text{ and}

\displaystyle(\rho(S)\rho(T))^{3}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&3\psi(S)+\phi_{2}^{\vee}((ST)^{2})\phi_{1}(S)+\phi_{2}^{\vee}(ST)\phi_{1}(S)\\
0&1_{\mathsf{d}}&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\text{,}

where
1_{\mathsf{d}}
is the
\mathsf{d}\times\mathsf{d}
identity matrix. Hence
\rho
is a
representation if and only if

\displaystyle 2\psi(S)+\phi_{2}^{\vee}(S)\phi_{1}(S)

\displaystyle=0\text{ and}

\displaystyle 3\psi(S)+\phi_{2}^{\vee}((ST)^{2})\phi_{1}(S)+\phi_{2}^{\vee}(ST)\phi_{1}(S)

\displaystyle=0\text{.}

We have that
\phi_{2}^{\vee}((ST)^{2})=\phi_{2}^{\vee}(ST)+\phi_{2}^{\vee}(ST)ST
, that

\phi_{2}^{\vee}(ST)=\phi_{2}^{\vee}(S)T
, and that
T\phi_{1}(S)=\phi_{1}(TS)
. With
U=TS
this yields
that the above is equivalent to

\displaystyle\psi(S)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\phi_{2}^{\vee}(S)\phi_{1}(S)\text{ and}

\displaystyle\phi_{2}^{\vee}(S)(2\phi_{1}(U)+2\phi_{1}(U^{2})-3\phi_{1}(S))

\displaystyle=0\text{.}

However, applying the identity
(1+U+U^{2}).\phi_{1}(S)=0
to expand
2\phi_{1}(S)
and then
(1+S).\phi_{1}(S)=0
to simplify the expression, we obtain that

2\big(\phi_{1}(U)+\phi_{1}(U^{2})\big)-3\phi_{1}(S)=2\big(\phi_{1}(U)+\phi_{1}(U^{2})+U.\phi_{1}(S)+U^{2}.\phi_{1}(S)\big)-\phi_{1}(S)\\
=2\big(T.\phi_{1}(S)+TST.\phi_{1}(S)+U^{2}.\phi_{1}(S)\big)-\phi_{1}(S)=2T.\phi_{1}(S)-\phi_{1}(S)\text{,}

so that

\phi_{2}^{\vee}(S)\big(2\phi_{1}(U)+2\phi_{1}(U^{2})-3\phi_{1}(S)\big)=\big\langle 2T^{-1}.\phi_{2}(S)-\phi_{2}(S),\,\phi_{1}(S)\big\rangle
.
To finish the proof we have to identify the right hand side with
\langle\!\langle\phi_{2}(S),\phi_{1}(S)\rangle\!\rangle=\big\langle(T^{-1}-T).\phi_{2}(S),\,\phi_{1}(S)\big\rangle
. To this end, note that for any element
H\in\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})]
the expression
\langle H.\phi_{2}(S),\phi_{1}(S)\rangle
only depends on the image of
H
in the double quotient
(1+S)\backslash\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})/\penalty\pm I]/\penalty(1+S)
. Denoting equality in this quotient by
\equiv
, the result follows from
(1+U+U^{2}).\phi_{1}(S)=0
and

\displaystyle T^{-1}-1+T=SU^{-1}-1+US^{-1}=1+(SU^{-1}-1)(1-US^{-1})\equiv 1+(-U^{-1}-1)(1+U)=-U^{-1}-1-U\text{.}

If a pair of cocycles
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)^{2}
satisfies
that
\langle\!\langle\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle\!\rangle=0
, we call it admissible. If

(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
is admissible, then we omit
\psi
from the notation and write

\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}=\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\psi}
.

In this section we describe depth two Eichler-Shimura integrals as components of

\widetilde{\rho}_{f,g}
and
\rho_{f,g}
,
where
f,g\in\mathrm{S}_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
; defined in the introduction, see (
0.4
)
and (
0.5

Recall that for
f,g\in\mathrm{S}_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
, and indeterminates
X
and
Y

we have the depth two polynomial-valued Eichler-Shimura integral
I_{f,g}(\;\cdot\;;X,Y)
and
the depth two scalar-valued Eichler-Shimura integral
\mathcal{E}_{f,g}
, given by

\displaystyle I_{f,g}(\tau;X,Y)=\int_{\tau}^{i\infty}f(z)\,(X-z)^{k-2}\,I_{g}(z;Y)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{z}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{f,g}(\tau)=\int_{\tau}^{i\infty}f(z)\,\mathcal{E}_{g}(z)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{z}\text{,}\quad\tau\in\mathbb{H}\text{,}

and
\mathcal{E}_{g}(\tau)=I_{g}(\tau;\tau)
are the polynomial-valued
Eichler integral and the scalar-valued Eichler integral.

Recall that for
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
we have that

\widetilde{\psi}_{f,g}(\gamma;X,Y)=\int_{\gamma(i\infty)}^{i\infty}f(z)\,(X-z)^{k-2}\,I_{g}(z;Y)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}{z}
. Let
also

\displaystyle\phi_{I_{g}}\cdot\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X):\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Hom}\big(\mathbb{C}[X]_{k-2},\mathbb{C}[X,Y]_{k-2}\big)\quad\text{be given by }

\displaystyle\big(\phi_{I_{g}}\cdot\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X)\big)(\gamma)p

\displaystyle{}=\phi_{I_{g}}(\gamma)\cdot\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X)(\gamma)p\text{.}

With this notation, we have that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}_{f,g}=\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X,Y)\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{(\phi_{I_{g}}\cdot\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X),\psi_{f,g})}\big(\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X)\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{I_{f}}}\mathbf{1}\big)\text{.}

(2.3)

We have that
(I_{f},1)^{T}\in\mathrm{M}_{0}\big(\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X)\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{I_{f}}}\mathbf{1}\big)
, see
[
1
]
,
and for the depth two polynomial-valued Eichler-Shimura integral we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer, and let
f,g\in\mathrm{S}_{k}
. Then the arithmetic
type
\widetilde{\rho}_{f,g}
given by (
0.4
) or (
2.3
)
is well-defined, and we have that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}I_{f,g}\\
I_{f}\\
1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{M}_{0}(\widetilde{\rho}_{f,g})\text{.}

Proof.

For convenience, we use the shorthand notation
|
for
|_{0,\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X,Y)}
,

\phi
for
\phi_{I_{g}}\cdot\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X)
, and
\rho
for
\widetilde{\rho}_{f,g}
.
Through direct calculation, we see that
\phi\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X),\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X,Y)\big)
.
By a standard change of variables (see
[
12
]
), we also obtain
that

\displaystyle\phi_{f,g}(\gamma,\tau):=r\big(I_{f,g}\big|(1-\gamma^{-1})\big)(\tau)=\widetilde{\psi}_{f,g}(\gamma)+\phi(\gamma)I_{f}(\gamma^{-1}\tau)\text{,}\quad\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\text{.}

(2.4)

To finish the proof of the transformation behavior, we record that

\displaystyle\rho(\gamma^{-1})\begin{pmatrix}I_{f,g}(\gamma\tau)\\
I_{f}(\gamma\tau)\\
1\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}I_{f,g}(\tau)\\
I_{f}(\tau)\\
1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{f,g}|(\gamma-1)+\phi(\gamma^{-1})I_{f}(\gamma\tau)+\widetilde{\psi}_{f,g}(\gamma^{-1})\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}=0\text{.}

Let now
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
. Then (
2.4
) implies that

\phi_{f,g}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},\tau)=\widetilde{\psi}_{f,g}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})+\phi(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})I_{f}(\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{1}^{-1}\tau)
. On the
other hand, we have that

\phi_{f,g}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},\tau)=\big(I_{f,g}\big|(1-\gamma_{2}^{-1})|\gamma_{1}^{-1}\big)(\tau)+\big(I_{f,g}\big|(1-\gamma_{1}^{-1})\big)(\tau)\\
=\phi(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})I_{f}(\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{1}^{-1}\tau)+\phi(\gamma_{1})\big(I_{f}(\gamma_{1}^{-1}\tau)-\gamma_{2}.I_{f}(\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{1}^{-1}\tau)\big)+\gamma_{1}.\widetilde{\psi}_{f,g}(\gamma_{2})+\widetilde{\psi}_{f,g}(\gamma_{1})\text{.}

However
(I_{f}\big|_{0,\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X)}(1-\gamma^{-1}))(\tau)
is independent of
\tau
,
and thus we have that

\displaystyle\phi_{I_{f}}(\gamma_{2})=(I_{f}\big|_{0,\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X)}(1-\gamma_{2}^{-1}))(\gamma_{1}^{-1}\tau)=I_{f}(\gamma_{1}^{-1}\tau)-\gamma_{2}.I_{f}(\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{1}^{-1}\tau)\text{.}

We thus obtain the identity

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{f,g}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})=\gamma_{1}.\widetilde{\psi}_{f,g}(\gamma_{2})+\widetilde{\psi}_{f,g}(\gamma_{1})+\phi(\gamma_{1})\phi_{I_{f}}(\gamma_{2})\text{.}

(2.5)

This leads immediately to the fact that

(\phi,\widetilde{\psi}_{f,g})\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X)\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{I_{f}}}\mathbf{1},\,\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X,Y)\big)
,
and hence
\rho
is well-defined.

An analogous statement holds for
\mathcal{E}_{f,g}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer, and let
f,g\in\mathrm{S}_{k}
. Then the arithmetic
type
\rho_{f,g}
given by (
0.5
)
is well-defined, and we have that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{E}_{f,g}\\
I_{f}\\
1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{M}_{0}(\rho_{f,g})\text{.}

Proof.

The argument is parallel to the one for Theorem
2.3
.

Combining Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
with the contraction map

\pi:\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X,Y)\to\mathbf{1}
, we obtain our next theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an even integer, and let
f,g\in\mathrm{S}_{k}
. Then
we have that

\displaystyle\pi_{\ast}\circ\begin{pmatrix}I_{f,g}\\
I_{f}\\
1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{E}_{f,g}\\
I_{f}\\
1\end{pmatrix}\text{,}

where

\pi_{\ast}:\widetilde{\rho}_{f,g}\to\rho_{f,g}
is the push-forward along the map (
0.6
), given by

\pi_{\ast}(p,q,z)=(\pi(p),q,z)
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
and
p\in\mathbb{C}[X]_{k-2}
, then

\displaystyle\pi\big(\phi_{I_{g}}(\gamma)\cdot\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X)(\gamma)p\big)=\big\langle\phi_{I_{g}}(\gamma),\gamma.p\big\rangle=\big\langle\gamma^{-1}.\phi_{I_{g}}(\gamma),p\big\rangle=-\big\langle\phi_{I_{g}}(\gamma^{-1}),p\big\rangle\text{,}

and therefore
\pi\circ(\phi_{I_{g}}\cdot\mathrm{sym}^{k-2}(X))=-\phi_{I_{g}}^{\vee}
.
That is,
\pi_{\ast}
maps
\widetilde{\rho}_{f,g}
to
\rho_{f,g}
as claimed.

We consider the difference

\displaystyle\pi_{\ast}\circ\begin{pmatrix}I_{f,g}\\
I_{f}\\
1\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{E}_{f,g}\\
I_{f}\\
1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\pi\circ I_{f,g}-\mathcal{E}_{f,g}\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{M}_{0}(\rho_{f,g})\text{.}

Since the two bottom components vanish, we conclude that
\pi\circ I_{f,g}-\mathcal{E}_{f,g}\in\mathrm{M}_{0}
. Since
f
and
g
are cusp forms, the zeroth Fourier coefficient of
\pi\circ I_{f,g}-\mathcal{E}_{f,g}
vanishes, and we obtain the equality stated in the theorem.

Remark 2.6
.

Theorem
2.4
implies that the pair of cocycles

(\phi_{I_{f}},-\phi_{I_{g}})
yields a representation, for which we see that
\rho_{f,g}=\rho_{\phi_{I_{f}},-\phi_{I_{g}}}
. Combining this with the orthogonality relation in Theorem
2.2
we obtain an alternate proof of Paşol-Popa’s identity in level
1
, as mentioned in the introduction.

3

Eisenstein series and saturation

Let
k
and
\mathsf{d}\geq 0
be integers, and let
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)^{2}

be admissible. In this section, we provide the Eisenstein series of type
\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}

and weight
k
, converging absolutely and locally uniformly on
\mathbb{H}
for
k>2+\mathsf{d}
.
We also prove a more detailed version of Theorem
C
.

3.1

Eisenstein series

Let
\mathsf{d}\geq 0
and
k>2+\mathsf{d}
be even integers, and let

(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\text{pb}}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)^{2}
be an admissible pair of
parabolic cocycles. Then the weight
k
Eisenstein series of type

\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}
is given by the series

\displaystyle E_{k}(\tau;\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\sum_{[\gamma]\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
0\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\Big|_{k,\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}}\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{[\gamma]\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}\psi(\gamma^{-1})(c\tau+d)^{-k}\\
E_{k}^{[1]}(\tau;\phi_{1})\\
E_{k}\end{pmatrix}\text{,}

where
\psi
is given as in Theorem
2.2
,

E_{k}(\tau)=\sum_{[\gamma]\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}(c\tau+d)^{-k}
and

E_{k}^{[1]}(\tau;\phi_{1})
is the weight
k
generalized second order Eisenstein
series of type
(\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X),\mathbf{1})
associated to
\phi_{1}
, given by

\displaystyle E_{k}^{[1]}(\tau;\phi_{1})=\sum_{[\gamma]\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}\frac{\phi_{1}(\gamma^{-1})}{(c\tau+d)^{k}}\text{.}

By Lemma 3.10 of
[
1
]
, we have that
E_{k}^{[1]}(\tau;\phi_{1})
converges
absolutely and locally uniformly for
k>2+\mathsf{d}
. Its Fourier series expansion is
given in Theorem 3.8 of the same paper. We write

\displaystyle E_{k}^{[2]}(\tau;\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\sum_{[\gamma]\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}\frac{\psi(\gamma^{-1})}{(c\tau+d)^{k}}\text{.}

Note that since
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are parabolic, we have that

\displaystyle\psi(\pm T^{m}\gamma T^{n})=\psi(T^{m}\gamma T^{n})=\psi(\gamma T^{n})=\psi(\gamma)\text{,}

(3.1)

for any
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, so that
\psi
descends to a function on the double
quotient
\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})/\Gamma_{\infty}
.

We now have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathsf{d}\geq 0
and
k>2+\mathsf{d}
be even integers, let
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\mathrm{pb}}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)^{2}

be an admissible pair, and let
\psi
be given as in Theorem
2.2
. Then

E_{k}^{[2]}(\tau;\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
converges absolutely and locally uniformly
on
\mathbb{H}
and has the following Fourier series expansion

\displaystyle E_{k}^{[2]}(\tau;\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\sum_{n\geq 1}e(n\tau)\frac{(-2\pi i)^{k}}{(k-1)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\gamma]\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})/\Gamma_{\infty}\\
[\gamma]\neq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{n^{k-1}e(nd/c)\psi(\gamma^{-1})}{c^{k}}\text{.}

Proof.

Reorganizing the defining series for
E_{k}^{[2]}(\tau;\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{[\gamma]\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}\psi(\gamma^{-1})(c\tau+d)^{-k}=\sum_{[\gamma]\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})/\Gamma_{\infty}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi(T^{-m}\gamma^{-1})(c(\tau+m)+d)^{-k}\text{.}

However, by (
3.1
) we have that
\psi(T^{-m}\gamma^{-1})=\psi(\gamma^{-1})

and thus we obtain the Fourier series expansion by applying Lipschitz’ summation
formula.

As for convergence, we note that its enough to show that

|\psi(\gamma^{-1})|\ll_{\epsilon}|c|^{\mathsf{d}+\epsilon}
where
[\gamma]\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})/\Gamma_{\infty}

and
[\gamma]\neq 1
.

To obtain this bound, we use a bijection between
\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})/\Gamma_{\infty}

and continued fractions. We first have a bijection

s_{1}:\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})/\Gamma_{\infty}\to\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1)\cup\{\infty\}
given by

s_{1}([\gamma])=d^{\prime}/c^{\prime}+\lceil-d^{\prime}/c^{\prime}\rceil
where
(c^{\prime},d^{\prime})=\mathrm{sgn}(c,d)\cdot(c,d)
.
Let now
S
be given by

\displaystyle S=\big\{(0),()\big\}\cup\big\{(0,\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{l})\,:\,l\geq 1,\alpha_{l}\geq 2,\forall 1\leq j<l.\,\alpha_{j}\geq 1\big\}\text{,}\text{.}

Then we have a bijection
s_{2}:S\to\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1)\cup\{\infty\}
, given by

s_{2}(())=\infty
and
s_{2}(0,\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{l})=[0;\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{l}]
,
see
[
11
]
. For convenience, we set
\alpha_{0}=0
.

Let
1\neq[\gamma]\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})/\Gamma_{\infty}
be arbitrary and
let
\alpha=s_{2}^{-1}(s_{1}([\gamma]))
. We then have that

\displaystyle\psi(\gamma^{-1})=\psi\big(T^{-\alpha_{0}}S^{-1}\cdots T^{(-1)^{l+1}\alpha_{l}}S^{-1}\big)=\psi\big(S^{2(l+1)}T^{-\alpha_{0}}S\cdots T^{(-1)^{l+1}\alpha_{l}}S\big)=\psi\big(T^{-\alpha_{0}}S\cdots T^{(-1)^{l+1}\alpha_{l}}S\big)\text{.}

Let
\beta_{i}=(-1)^{l-i+1}\alpha_{l-i}
,
\delta_{-1}=I
,
and
\delta_{i}=T^{\beta_{i}}S\delta_{i-1}
, so that
[\delta_{l}]=[\gamma^{-1}]
. We then
have that

\displaystyle\psi(\delta_{i})-\psi(\delta_{i-1})=\psi(S)+\phi_{2}^{\vee}(S)\phi_{1}(\delta_{i-1})\text{.}

We thus find that

\displaystyle\psi(\delta_{l})=\sum_{i=0}^{l}\big(\psi(\delta_{i})-\psi(\delta_{i-1})\big)=\sum_{i=0}^{l}\phi_{2}^{\vee}(S)\big(\phi_{1}(\delta_{i-1})-\tfrac{1}{2}\phi_{1}(S)\big)\text{.}

Using the bound
\sum_{j=0}^{\mathsf{d}}\binom{d}{j}^{-1}\leq 2+4/\mathsf{d}
, which holds
for
\mathsf{d}\geq 1
, we find that

\displaystyle\big|\phi_{2}^{\vee}(S)\big(\phi_{1}(\delta_{i-1})-\tfrac{1}{2}\phi_{1}(S)\big)\big|\leq 4\|\phi_{2}(S)\|_{1}\big\|\phi_{1}(\delta_{i-1})-\tfrac{1}{2}\phi_{1}(S)\big\|_{1}\text{.}

Hence, we obtain the bound

\displaystyle|\psi(\delta_{l})|\leq 4\|\phi_{2}(S)\|_{1}\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{l+1}{2}$}}\|\phi_{1}(S)\|_{1}+\sum_{i=0}^{l}\|\phi_{1}(\delta_{i-1})\|_{1}\Big)\text{.}

Lemma 3.10 in
[
1
]
tells us that for

\gamma=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\big)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
with
|d|<|c|

we have that
\|\phi_{1}(\gamma^{-1})\|_{1}\leq C_{\mathsf{d}}|c|^{\mathsf{d}}
, for
a constant
C_{\mathsf{d}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. In our case, we have that

\displaystyle|d(\delta_{i}^{-1})/c(\delta_{i}^{-1})|=[\alpha_{l-i};\alpha_{l-i-1},\dots,\alpha_{l}]>1\qquad\text{for }i<l\text{,}

and thus we can apply the bound to
(\delta_{i}^{-1}S)^{-1}
. We have that

S^{-1}.\phi_{1}(\delta_{i})=\phi_{1}(S^{-1}.\delta_{i})-\phi_{1}(S)

and
\|S^{-1}.v\|_{1}=\|v\|_{1}
, and thus we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{l}\|\phi_{1}(\delta_{i-1})\|_{1}\leq(l+1)\|\phi_{1}(S)\|_{1}+\sum_{i=0}^{l}\|\phi_{1}(S^{-1}\delta_{i-1})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq(l+1)\|\phi_{1}(S)\|_{1}+C_{\mathsf{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{l}|c(\delta_{i-1}^{-1}S)|^{\mathsf{d}}

\displaystyle=(l+1)\|\phi_{1}(S)\|_{1}+C_{\mathsf{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{l}|d(\delta_{i-1}^{-1})|^{\mathsf{d}}\text{.}

However,
|d(\delta_{i-1}^{-1})|
is increasing in
i
, whence we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{l}|d(\delta_{i-1}^{-1})|^{\mathsf{d}}\leq(l+1)|d(\delta_{l-1}^{-1})|=(l+1)|c(\gamma)|^{\mathsf{d}}\text{.}

In conclusion, we obtain that
|\psi(\gamma^{-1})|\leq 4\|\phi_{1}(S)\|_{1}(l+1)(\tfrac{3}{2}+C_{\mathsf{d}}|c|^{\mathsf{d}})
. Since
\alpha
corresponds
to a continued fraction, we have that
l+1\ll\log(|c|)
and thus

|\psi(\gamma^{-1})|\ll_{\epsilon}|c|^{\mathsf{d}+\epsilon}
as desired.

3.2

Saturation

Let
\mathsf{d}\geq 0
and
k\geq 2
be even integers, let
0\leq j\leq\mathsf{d}
be an integer,
and let
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\mathrm{pb}}(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X))^{2}
be admissible.

In
[
1
]
, the authors of this paper and Ahlbäck introduced the
j
th
Eisenstein series of type
\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)
and weight
k
, given
by

\displaystyle E_{k}\big(\tau;\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X),j\big)=\sum_{[\gamma]\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
(X-\tau)^{j}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\big|_{k,\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)}\gamma\text{,}\quad\tau\in\mathbb{H}\text{.}

Let
k_{0}>2+\mathsf{d}
be an integer. Then the
\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}
-module of Eisenstein
series of type
\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{\geq k_{0}}\big(\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)=\mathrm{span}\;\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}\big\{E_{k}\big(\tau;\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X),j\big)\,:\,k\geq k_{0}\text{, }0\leq j\leq\mathsf{d}\big\}\text{.}

Theorem 4.3 of
[
1
]
implies that for

\mathsf{d}\geq 0
and
k_{0}>2+\mathsf{d}
it holds that

\displaystyle\big(\mathrm{E}_{\geq k_{0}}\big(\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)+\iota(\mathrm{M}_{\bullet})\colon\Delta^{\infty}\big)=\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}\big(\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)\text{,}

(3.2)

where
\iota(f)=(f,0)^{T}
. In Remark 4.4 of the same paper we mention that the
proof can be generalized to higher depths by induction. Following this approach,
we obtain our last theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathsf{d}\geq 0
be an even integer and
k_{0}>2+\mathsf{d}
be an integer, and

(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}_{\mathrm{pb}}\big(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)\big)^{2}
be admissible. Then
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}(\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}})=\big(\mathrm{E}_{\geq k_{0}}(\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}})+\iota_{1}\big(\mathrm{E}_{\geq k_{0}}(\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X))\big)+\iota_{2}(\mathrm{M}_{\bullet})\colon\Delta^{\infty}\big)\text{,}

where
\iota_{1}(f,g)=(f,g,0)^{T}
and
\iota_{2}(f)=(f,0,0)^{T}
.

Proof.

Consider the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathrm{E}_{\geq k_{0}}(\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}})+\mu_{1}(\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X)))\colon\Delta^{\infty})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathrm{E}_{\geq k_{0}}\colon\Delta^{\infty})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}(\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{M}_{\bullet}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\mapsto f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu_{2}}

where
\mu_{i}(f,g)=(f,g,0)^{T}
and
\nu_{i}(f,g,h)=h
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. It is clear
that the rows are exact and that the diagram commutes. We have that

\mathrm{im}(\nu_{1})\subseteq\mathrm{im}(\nu_{2})
and thus the map

\theta:\mathrm{coker}(\nu_{1})\to\mathrm{coker}(\nu_{2})
given by

\theta(h+\mathrm{im}(\nu_{1}))=h+\mathrm{im}(\nu_{2})
is well-defined. Since

E_{k}=\nu_{1}(E_{k}(\cdot;\phi_{1},\phi_{2}))
it is also injective. The
Four Lemma now implies that the vertical map in the middle column is surjective
and thus an equality. Finally, we conclude the proof by
applying (
3.2
) and observing that

\big(\mathrm{E}_{\geq k_{0}}(\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}})+\mu_{1}\big((\mathrm{E}_{\geq k_{0}}(\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X))+\iota(\mathrm{M}_{\bullet})\colon\Delta^{\infty})\big)\colon\Delta^{\infty}\big)\\
=\big(\mathrm{E}_{\geq k_{0}}(\rho_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}})+\iota_{1}\big(\mathrm{E}_{\geq k_{0}}(\mathbf{1}\mathbin{\mathop{\mathchar 0\relax}}_{\phi_{2}^{\vee}}\mathrm{sym}^{\mathsf{d}}(X))\big)+\iota_{2}(\mathrm{M}_{\bullet})\colon\Delta^{\infty}\big)\text{.}

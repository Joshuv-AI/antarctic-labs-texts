Quantitative tame properties of differentiable functions with controlled derivatives Thanks: Supported by FWF-Project P 32905-N
Armin Rainer

Abstract
. We show that differentiable functions, defined on a convex body K ⊆ ℝ d K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d} , whose derivatives are controlled by a suitable given sequence of positive real numbers share many properties with polynomials. The role of the degree of a polynomial is hereby played by an integer associated with the given sequence of reals, the diameter of K K , and a real parameter linked to the 𝒞 0 \mathcal{C}^{0} -norm of the function. We give quantitative information on the size of the zero set, show that it admits a local parameterization by Sobolev functions, and prove an inequality of Remez-type. From the latter, we deduce several consequences, for instance, a bound on the volume of sublevel sets and a comparison of L p L^{p} -norms reversing Hölder’s inequality. The validity of many of the results only depends on the derivatives up to some finite order; the order can be specified in terms of the given data.

1.
Introduction

Yomdin showed in
[
31
]
that the zero set
Z_{f}
of a non-zero

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-function
f:B\to\mathbb{R}
defined on the
unit ball
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
behaves in many respects as the zero set of a polynomial,
if the partial derivatives of some order
j\geq 2
are sufficiently small on
B
.
To be precise, let
\|f\|_{j,B}:=\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}\|f^{(\alpha)}\|_{B}

and
\|f\|_{B}:=\|f\|_{0,B}=\sup_{x\in B}|f(x)|
.
If, for some
j\geq 2
,

(1)

\|f\|_{j,B}\leq\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}\|f\|_{0,B},

then
Z_{f}
has the following properties:

(a)

There is some ball
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
such that for each affine line
\ell
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

that meets
B^{\prime}
the restriction
f|_{\ell}
has at most
j-1
zeros (counted with multiplicities).

(b)

Z_{f}
is contained in a countable union of compact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-hypersurfaces.

(c)

The
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
Z_{f}
satisfies

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z_{f})\leq C(d,j),

where
C(d,j)
is a positive constant depending only on
d
and
j
.

An important fact (which is needed to get (c)) is that the radius of
B^{\prime}
depends only on
j
.

This result is in drastic contrast to the classical result that any closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

is the zero set of some
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-function.
In fact, by (a), a non-zero function satisfying (
1
) cannot have points of infinite flatness.

In this paper, we will show that the zero sets of smooth functions with a different type of
constraints have similar quantitative tame properties.
Instead of assuming that the Fréchet derivative of
some
order is “small”, as in (
1
),
we ask that the growth of the sequence of derivatives of
all
orders is controlled.

In a spirit similar to
[
31
]
,
Yomdin proved in
[
32
]
a Remez-type inequality for smooth functions involving a “remainder term”
expressible through bounds on the derivatives.
We will prove a Remez-type inequality (without “remainder term”) and
deduce several consequences for smooth functions with controlled
derivatives.

1.1.
Functions with controlled derivatives

Let a positive increasing sequence
(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
and a positive real number
M_{0}>0
be given and set

M_{j}:=M_{0}\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\cdots\mu_{j}
for all
j\geq 1
.
Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
d
-dimensional convex body (i.e., compact with non-empty interior
K^{\circ}
).
We will study
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-functions
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(2)

\|f\|_{j,K}\leq M_{j},\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

Now it is well-known that the qualitative behavior of functions satisfying (
2
)
— let us call them
(M_{j})_{j}
-smooth functions
—
fundamentally depends on the convergence or divergence of the series
\sum_{j}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}
(the

quasianalyticity threshold
).
The series
\sum_{j}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}
and its partial sums play a central role in our
quantitative analysis. In fact, this analysis is based on a quantity
\mathfrak{d}_{(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}}(b)

which depends on the sequence
(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}
and an additional parameter
b>0
.
Here
\delta_{K}:=\operatorname{diam}(K)
is the diameter of
K
.
Roughly speaking,
\mathfrak{d}_{(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}}(b)
is the greatest integer
n
(possibly infinite) such that

\sum_{j=j_{0}(b)+1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}<\delta_{K}e,

where the integer
j_{0}(b)
is explicitly computed from
b
; see
Section

2
for precise definitions.
We call
\mathfrak{d}_{(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}}(b)
the
(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}
-degree
, since it has similar properties as the degree of
a polynomial. Note that it is closely related to the
Bang degree
introduced by
Nazarov, Sodin, and Volberg
[
20
]
.

The
(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}
-degree
\mathfrak{d}_{(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}}(b)
is finite if
\sum_{j\geq j_{0}(b)+1}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}
exceeds
\delta_{K}e

which is always the case provided that the series
\sum_{j}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}
diverges.
But also if
\sum_{j}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}
converges to a large enough sum,

\mathfrak{d}_{(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}}(b)
is finite and contains useful information on functions satisfying (
2
).
In fact, this may occur if
\delta_{K}
is relatively small.

1.2.
The zero set of functions with controlled derivatives

We will see that the zero set
Z_{f}
of
(M_{j})_{j}
-smooth functions
f:K\to\mathbb{R}

has properties similar to (a), (b), and (c) whenever the
(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}
-degree

\mathfrak{d}_{(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}}(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})
is finite.
The parameter
b>0
hereby acts as a lower bound on the
C^{0}
-norm of
f
, that is, we require that
\|f\|_{K}\geq b
.

Let us summarize our results in the following statements (A)–(D):

(A)

There is a ball
B
contained in the interior
K^{\circ}
of
K

whose radius depends only on
K
and
the ratio
\frac{b}{M_{1}}

such that for each affine line
\ell
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

that meets
B
the restriction
f|_{\ell}
has at most
2\mathfrak{d}_{(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}}(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})
zeros (counted with multiplicities).
(
Theorem

4.2
)

The key to this result is a bound for the number of zeros of
(M_{j})_{j}
-smooth univariate functions which goes back to
Bang
[
2
]
; see also
[
20
]
for an
exposition of Bang’s ideas. We revisit Bang’s result in some detail in
Section

3
.

As a consequence of Malgrange’s preparation theorem and Yomdin’s observation
[
31
, Lemma 6]

we obtain:

(B)

Z_{f}
is contained in a countable union of compact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-hypersurfaces. (
Theorem

4.6
)

Then, using a Crofton-type argument, (A) and (B) allow to conclude:

(C)

The
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
Z_{f}
satisfies

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z_{f})\leq C\,\mathfrak{d}_{(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}}(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})\delta_{K}^{d-1},

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
d
and the ratio
\frac{\delta_{B}}{\delta_{K}}
;

B
is the ball from (A). (
Theorem

4.6
)

Combining Malgrange’s preparation theorem with results of Parusiński and Rainer
[
22
]
,
we find that
Z_{f}
locally admits a Sobolev parameterization:

(D)

There is a finite cover of
K
by rectangular boxes
U
with corresponding orthogonal
coordinates
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})=(x^{\prime},x_{d})

such that
Z_{f}\cap U
is contained in the graphs
\{x_{d}=\xi_{i}(x^{\prime})\}

of at most
2\mathfrak{d}_{(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}}(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})
continuous functions
\xi_{i}

of Sobolev class
W^{1,p}
, for all
1\leq p<p_{0}
, where

p_{0}:=\frac{2\mathfrak{d}_{(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}}(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})}{2\mathfrak{d}_{(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}}(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})-1}.

(
Theorem

4.8
)

That means that the partial derivatives
\partial_{k}\xi_{i}
of first order of
\xi_{i}
exist almost everywhere,
agree with the weak partial derivatives, and
\xi_{i}
as well as
\partial_{k}\xi_{i}
are in the Lebesgue space
L^{p}
.

As a by-product, our reasoning shows that (D) remains true for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-functions satisfying (
1
)
for some
j\geq 2
with the role of
2\mathfrak{d}_{(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}}(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})

replaced by
j-1
.

We discuss uniformity of these results at the end of
Section

4
.

1.3.
A Remez-type inequality for functions with controlled derivatives

The classical Remez inequality
[
27
]
states that for a Lebesgue measurable subset
E
of
[0,1]
with
Lebesgue measure
|E|>0

we have

\|p\|_{[0,1]}\leq T_{n}\Big(\frac{2-|E|}{|E|}\Big)\,\|p\|_{E}

for all polynomials
p\in\mathbb{R}[x]
with degree
\deg p\leq n
.
Here
T_{n}(x)=\cos(n\arccos x)
is the
n
-th Chebyshev polynomial.
In higher dimensions, there is the generalization due to Yu. Brudnyi and Ganzburg
[
11
]
:
for a convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a Lebesgue measurable subset
E\subseteq K
with
|E|>0

we have the sharp inequality

\|p\|_{K}\leq T_{n}\Big(\frac{1+(1-\frac{|E|}{|K|})^{1/d}}{1-(1-\frac{|E|}{|K|})^{1/d}}\Big)\,\|p\|_{E}

for all polynomials
p\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
with degree
\deg p\leq n
. It implies

\|p\|_{K}\leq\Big(\frac{4d\,|K|}{|E|}\Big)^{n}\,\|p\|_{E}.

A version of the latter inequality for analytic functions was obtained by A. Brudnyi
[
10
]
, where the role of
n

is played by the
analytic degree
; cf.
Section

6.2
.
As already mentioned there is a version for smooth function due to
[
32
]

which however contains a “remainder term”.

In this paper, we are interested in Remez-type inequalities for
(M_{j})_{j}
-smooth functions
f:K\to\mathbb{R}

(without “remainder term”).
On the unit interval
K=[0,1]
, such an inequality was obtained in
[
20
, Theorem B]
.
We recall and slightly adjust this result in
Theorem

5.1
.
The crucial difference is that, instead of the Bang degree, we work with the
(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}
-degree

\mathfrak{d}_{(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}}(\tfrac{b}{M_{0}})

which in a sense allows more “leeway”: it works for all
(M_{j})_{j}
-smooth functions
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
with
\|f\|_{K}\geq b
.
(In contrast to above, there is no factor
2
in the denominator.)

In this way, we are able to apply the strategy of restriction to
1
-dimensional sections of Brudnyi and Ganzburg
and obtain a Remez-type inequality for
(M_{j})_{j}
-smooth functions in several variables (
Theorem

5.4
).
Actually, the Remez-type inequality is invariant under the
\mathbb{R}^{*}
-action on
f
by multiplication with non-zero constants.
This observation leads to a useful version (
Theorem

5.5
)
for
(M_{j})_{j}
-smooth functions
f
, where
M_{j}=\|f\|_{K}\cdot\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{j}
, for
j\geq 1
, and
M_{0}=\|f\|_{K}>0
,
and with
(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}
-degree
\mathfrak{d}_{(\delta_{K}\mu_{j})_{j}}(1)
.

From this version, we deduce (in a standard way, see for instance

[
9
,
10
]
and
[
11
]
)
a number of consequences for functions with controlled derivatives:

(1)

A bound for the volume of sublevel sets. (
Corollary

5.6
)

(2)

A comparison of
L^{p}
-norms (reversing Hölder’s inequality). (
Corollary

5.8
)

(3)

A bound for the mean oscillation of
\log|f|
. (
Corollary

5.10
and
Corollary

5.11
)

Generally, we deduce the main multivariate results from respective univariate ones by
restriction to affine lines. See
Remark

4.13
for how this relates to
results of Bochnak–Siciak type for controlled functions.

1.4.
Complementary results

In
Section

6.1
and
Section

6.2
, we attempt to clarify the relation between
the
(\mu_{j})_{j}
-degree on the one hand and polynomial and analytic degree on the other hand.
The
(\mu_{j})_{j}
-degree is a more general object, but in a polynomial or analytic setting it is generally
larger than the polynomial or analytic degree, respectively. This is related to the general assumption
that
(\mu_{j})_{j}
is increasing, which is crucial for many of the results.

As already mentioned,
the
(\mu_{j})_{j}
-degree can be finite also in the
non-quasianalytic
setting (i.e., when
\sum_{j}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}
converges).
In fact, we obtain in
Corollary

6.3
a quantitative necessary condition for the existence of

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-functions with uniform bounds,
like (
2
), and compactly supported in
a given ball, in terms of the size of the ball and the
C^{0}
-norm of the function.
For completeness, we sketch a proof of the fact that, if
\sum_{j}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}<\infty
,
each non-empty closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the zero set of a

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-function
f
such that
\|f\|_{j,\mathbb{R}^{d}}\leq A^{j+1}M_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and some
A>0
(
Remark

6.4
).

Bang’s univariate result, i.e.
Proposition

3.2
, not only contains useful information on the numbers of zeros
but also on the locus of points, where derivatives of higher order vanish.
For instance, we get a uniform bound for the number of critical points on certain affine lines (
Proposition

6.5
).

1.5.
Finite determinacy

So far we only considered
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-functions with controlled derivatives of all orders.
Actually, many of our results depend only on the derivatives up to some finite order
and remain true even for functions that are only differentiable up to said finite order.
We will make this precise below, in particular, we will specify the finite order.
To account for this fact, we introduce a customized terminology in
Section

2
,

Section

4.1
, and
Section

5.4
.

1.6.
Notation

We often write
|E|
for the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{d}(E)
of a measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
If not stated otherwise, “measurable” always means “Lebesgue measurable”.
The
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
E
is denoted by
\mathcal{H}^{k}(E)
.

Let
B_{r}(a):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x-a|<r\}

and
\overline{B}_{r}(a):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x-a|\leq r\}
denote the open and closed Euclidean ball
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
centered at
a
with radius
r>0
, respectively.
If
a
is the origin, we simply write

B_{r}:=B_{r}(0)
and
\overline{B}_{r}:=\overline{B}_{r}(0)
.

Throughout the paper, we write
\delta_{K}:=\operatorname{diam}(K)
for the diameter of a set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We denote by
\overline{K}
,
K^{\circ}
, and
\partial K
the closure, the interior, and the boundary of
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively.

The integral part of a real number
x
is denoted by
\lfloor x\rfloor:=\max\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:n\leq x\}
.
Similarly,
\lceil x\rceil:=\min\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:n\geq x\}
, but most of the time we will use

\lceil x\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}:=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n\geq x\}
, where
\mathbb{N}:=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}:=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:n\geq 0\}
.
Similarly, we use

\mathbb{N}_{\geq m}:=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n\geq m\}
,

\mathbb{R}_{>a}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:x>a\}
, and variations thereof.

2.
M^{|N}
-smooth functions and
\mu^{|N}
-degree

2.1.
Admissible weights

Let
(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an infinite increasing sequence of elements in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}
, i.e.,

0<\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\cdots
. We allow that sequence elements attain the value
\infty
.
By the requirement that the sequence is increasing,
if
\mu_{j_{0}}=\infty
, then
\mu_{j}=\infty
for all
j\geq j_{0}
.
We say that such a sequence
(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is an
admissible weight
.
It will be convenient to keep track of the index (if any), where the sequence ceases to be finite. So we write

\displaystyle\mu^{|N}

\displaystyle:=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{N},\infty,\infty,\ldots)\quad\text{ if }\mu_{N}<\infty=\mu_{N+1}\text{ for }N\geq 1,

\displaystyle\mu^{|0}

\displaystyle:=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}=(\infty,\infty,\ldots)\quad\text{ if }\mu_{j}=\infty\text{ for all }j\geq 1,

\displaystyle\mu^{|\infty}

\displaystyle:=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}\quad\text{ if }\mu_{j}<\infty\text{ for all }j\geq 1.

From now on,
we let
N
be an element of
\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\cup\{\infty\}
so that the notation
\mu^{|N}
also includes the case
\mu^{|\infty}
.
(We exclude the case
N=0
, where the weight is worthless.)
Let us adopt the usual conventions for the arithmetic with
\infty
:

\infty\pm 1=\infty
,
\frac{1}{\infty}=0
, and
r\cdot\infty=\infty
if
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}
.

If
\mu^{|N}
is an admissible weight and
M_{0}>0
is any positive real number, then
we call the pair
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
a
full admissible weight
.
With the full admissible weight
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
we associate a sequence
M^{|N}=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
by setting

M_{j}:=M_{0}\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\cdots\mu_{j},\quad j\geq 1.

Then
M_{j}=\infty
if
N<\infty
and
j>N
.
That
\mu^{|N}
is increasing amounts to the property
M^{2}_{j}\leq M_{j-1}M_{j+1}
for
j\geq 1
.
Given a sequence
M^{|N}=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
with this property, we may recover the pair
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
in a unique way by

\mu_{j}:=\frac{M_{j}}{M_{j-1}}\quad\text{ for }1\leq j\leq N\quad\text{ and }\quad\mu_{j}:=\infty\quad\text{ for }j>N,

if
N<\infty
, and simply by
\mu_{j}:=\frac{M_{j}}{M_{j-1}}
for all
j\geq 1
otherwise.
Thus there is a one-to-one correspondence between
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
and
M^{|N}
.
We also call
M^{|N}
a
full admissible weight
.

Notice that, given a full admissible weight
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
and positive constants
r,C>0
,
the pair
(r\mu^{|N},CM_{0})=((r\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1},CM_{0})
is again a full admissible weight;
it corresponds to
(Cr^{j}M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
.

2.2.
M^{|N}
-smooth functions

Let
M^{|N}
be a full admissible weight.
Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex body and
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
a function.
We say that
f
is
M^{|N}
-smooth
(or
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
-smooth
) if

f
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{N}
(in an open neighborhood of
K
) and

(3)

\|f\|_{j,K}\leq M_{j},\quad 0\leq j<N+1,

where we set

\|f\|_{j,K}:=\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}\|f^{(\alpha)}\|_{K}

and
\|f\|_{K}=\|f\|_{0,K}=\sup_{x\in K}|f(x)|
denotes the sup-norm.
If
N=\infty
, this means that
f
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
and
\|f\|_{j,K}\leq M_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.3.
The
\mu^{|N}
-degree

Let
\mu^{|N}
be an admissible weight.
We consider the function
\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}:\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\times\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\to[0,\infty)

defined by

\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}(m,n):=\sum_{j=m}^{n}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}

and extend the definition to
(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\cup\{\infty\})\times(\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\})
by setting

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}(m,n):=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }1\leq m\leq\infty,~n=0,\\
\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}&\text{ if }1\leq m<\infty,~n=\infty,\\
0&\text{ if }m=\infty,~1\leq n\leq\infty.\end{cases}

Note that, if
m>n
, then
\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}(m,n)
is an empty sum and hence has the value
0
.
For
n\geq N
, we have
\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}(m,n)=\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}(m,N)
(since
\frac{1}{\infty}=0
).
The map
(m,n)\mapsto\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}(m,n)
is increasing in
n

and decreasing in
m
(even strictly in the range, where
\mu_{j}
is finite).

For
b>0
let
j_{0}(b)
be the smallest integer
j\in\mathbb{N}
with
j\geq\log b^{-1}
, i.e.,

j_{0}(b):=\lceil\log b^{-1}\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}.

We define the
\mu^{|N}
-degree

\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|N}}(b)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}

by setting

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|N}}(b):=\sup\big\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}(j_{0}(b)+1,n)<e\big\}.

We remark that
\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|N}}(b)\geq j_{0}(b)

and
\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|N}}(b)=j_{0}(b)
occurs precisely if
\mu_{j_{0}(b)+1}\leq 1/e
.
Clearly,
\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|N}}(b)=\infty
if
\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}(j_{0}(b)+1,n)<e
for all
n
.
The map
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\ni(a,b)\mapsto\mathfrak{d}_{a\mu^{|N}}(b)
is increasing in
a
and decreasing in
b
.

We could assign a
\mu^{|N}
-degree to a
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
-smooth function
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
by
putting

\mu^{|N}\text{-degree of }f:=\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|N}}(\tfrac{\|f\|_{[0,1]}}{M_{0}});

like the
Bang degree
in
[
20
]
.
But the unspecified argument
b
allows for more flexibility;
it will however always be related to the ratio of the
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-norm of
f
by
M_{0}
.

If the domain
K
of
f
is a
d
-dimensional convex body,
then, in our results, the diameter
\delta_{K}
of the domain
K
(not the dimension!)
will affect the
\mu^{|N}
-degree as a multiplicative factor of the sequence
\mu^{|N}
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{d}_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|N}}(b).

See, for instance, the proof of
Theorem

4.2
,
where the function defined on
K
is restricted to affine lines and
a rescaling allows to consider it on the interval
[0,1]
.

3.
Zeros of univariate functions

In this section, we revisit some results of Bang
[
2
]
(see also
[
20
]
).
Since we have to rephrase them in our terminology, we give detailed proofs.

3.1.
Bang’s metric theory revisited

Let
M^{|N}=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
be a full admissible weight.
Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a non-trivial compact interval and

f:I\to\mathbb{R}
an
M^{|N}
-smooth function.

Following Bang
[
2
]
, we associate with
f
and
M^{|N}
a sequence
(b_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of functions
defined by

(4)

b_{n}(t)=b_{f,M^{|N},n}(t):=\sup_{j\geq n}\frac{|f^{(j)}(t)|}{e^{j}M_{j}},\quad t\in I,

with the interpretation that
\frac{|f^{(j)}(t)|}{e^{j}M_{j}}=0
if
M_{j}=\infty
(even if
f^{(j)}(t)
is not defined).
In other words, if
N
is finite, then

b_{n}(t)=\sup_{n\leq j\leq N}\frac{|f^{(j)}(t)|}{e^{j}M_{j}}
for
n\leq N
and

b_{n}\equiv 0
for
n>N
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The sequence
(b_{n})_{n\geq 0}
has the following properties.

(i)

e^{-n}\geq b_{n}
for all
n\geq 0
.

(ii)

b_{n-1}\geq b_{n}
and
f^{(n-1)}(t_{0})=0
implies
b_{n-1}(t_{0})=b_{n}(t_{0})
for all
n\geq 1
.

(iii)

For all
k>n

and all distinct
t,s\in I
,

(5)

b_{n}(s)<\max\{b_{n}(t),e^{-k}\}\,e^{e|t-s|\mu_{k}},

where the right-hand side is interpreted as
\infty
if
\mu_{k}=\infty
.
In particular, all
b_{n}
are continuous.

Proof.

(i) and (ii) are obvious (by (
3
)).

Let us prove (iii).
Now (
5
) is trivial if
N
is finite and
k>N
which means that
\mu_{k}=\infty
.
So we may assume that
k<N+1
and that
\mu_{k}
and
M_{k}
are finite.
Let
n\leq j<k
and
t,s\in I
.
Then, by Taylor’s formula, for some
\xi
between
t
and
s
,

\displaystyle\frac{|f^{(j)}(s)|}{e^{j}M_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-j-1}\frac{|f^{(j+i)}(t)|\,|t-s|^{i}}{e^{j}M_{j}\,i!}+\frac{|f^{(k)}(\xi)|\,|t-s|^{k-j}}{e^{j}M_{j}\,(k-j)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-j-1}\frac{M_{j+i}}{M_{j}}\frac{|f^{(j+i)}(t)|}{e^{j+i}M_{j+i}}\frac{(e|t-s|)^{i}}{i!}+e^{-k}\frac{M_{k}}{M_{j}}\frac{|f^{(k)}(\xi)|}{M_{k}}\frac{(e|t-s|)^{k-j}}{(k-j)!}

\displaystyle\leq b_{n}(t)\sum_{i=0}^{k-j-1}\mu_{k}^{i}\frac{(e|t-s|)^{i}}{i!}+e^{-k}\mu_{k}^{k-j}\frac{(e|t-s|)^{k-j}}{(k-j)!}

\displaystyle<\max\{b_{n}(t),e^{-k}\}\,e^{e|t-s|\mu_{k}},

where we used that
(\mu_{j})_{j}
is increasing.
If
j\geq k
, then trivially

\displaystyle\frac{|f^{(j)}(s)|}{e^{j}M_{j}}\leq e^{-j}<\max\{b_{n}(t),e^{-k}\}\,e^{e|t-s|\mu_{k}}.

This implies (
5
).

To see the continuity of the
b_{n}
, we treat separately the cases
N<\infty
and
N=\infty
.
First, if
N
is finite, then
b_{n}
, for
n\leq N
, is a maximum of finitely many continuous functions and
b_{n}\equiv 0
, for
n>N
;
thus continuity is clear.
Second, if
N=\infty
, then
continuity of
b_{n}
, for all
n\geq 0
, follows from (
5
):
fix
t\in I
and a sequence
t_{\nu}\to t
(with
t_{\nu}\neq t
) in
I
.
By (
5
), for each
k>n
and all
\nu
,

b_{n}(t_{\nu})<\max\{b_{n}(t),e^{-k}\}\,e^{e|t-t_{\nu}|\mu_{k}},

whence

\limsup_{\nu\to\infty}b_{n}(t_{\nu})\leq\max\{b_{n}(t),e^{-k}\}.

Since this holds for all
k>n
, we have

\limsup_{\nu\to\infty}b_{n}(t_{\nu})\leq b_{n}(t).

Again by (
5
), for each
k>n
and all
\nu
,

b_{n}(t)<\max\{b_{n}(t_{\nu}),e^{-k}\}\,e^{e|t-t_{\nu}|\mu_{k}},

and so we find

b_{n}(t)\leq\max\{\liminf_{\nu\to\infty}b_{n}(t_{\nu}),e^{-k}\}

for all
k>n
, and therefore

b_{n}(t)\leq\liminf_{\nu\to\infty}b_{n}(t_{\nu}).

It follows that
\lim_{\nu\to\infty}b_{n}(t_{\nu})=b_{n}(t)
.
∎

The following proposition is due to
[
2
]
; we implement several modifications.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
M^{|N}=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
be a full admissible weight.
Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a non-trivial compact interval and

f:I\to\mathbb{R}
an
M^{|N}
-smooth function.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
m+1\leq N
.
Assume that for all
0\leq j\leq m
there is
x_{j}\in I
such that
f^{(j)}(x_{j})=0
.
Let
x_{-1}
be an arbitrary point in
I
.
Then,

(6)

\sum_{j=0}^{m}|x_{j-1}-x_{j}|\geq\frac{1}{e}\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}(j_{0}+1,m+1),

where

(7)

j_{0}=j_{0}(b_{f,M^{|N},0}(x_{-1}))=\lceil\log b_{f,M^{|N},0}(x_{-1})^{-1}\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}.

If
j_{0}\leq m
, then the inequality (
6
) is strict.

Remark 3.3
.

A few remarks are in order.

(1) Note that
j_{0}<\infty
if and only if
there exists
0\leq j\leq N
with
f^{(j)}(x_{-1})\neq 0
.

(2)
The right-hand side of (
6
) is zero
if
j_{0}>m
. In that case, the statement is trivial.
For instance, if
N=\infty
,
we do here not exclude the case that
f
has points of infinite flatness.
If
x_{-1}
is such a point, then
j_{0}=\infty
and
hence
(
6
) is trivially true. In that case,
the assumptions of the proposition are satisfied for the choice
x_{j}:=x_{-1}
,
j=0,\ldots,m
,
entailing that also the left-hand side of (
6
) is zero.

Proof of
Proposition

3.2
.

Let
(b_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be the decreasing sequence of continuous functions (
4
); cf.
Lemma

3.1
.
We will construct a new continuous function
\beta
by tracing through the graphs of the
b_{n}
(for
0\leq n\leq m
)
and switching from
b_{n}
to
b_{n+1}
at
x_{n}
.
For
0\leq k\leq m
,
set
\tau_{k}:=\sum_{j=0}^{k}|x_{j-1}-x_{j}|
and
\tau_{-1}:=0
.
For
t\in[\tau_{n-1},\tau_{n}]
, where
0\leq n\leq m
, define

\beta_{n}(t):=\begin{cases}b_{n}(x_{n-1}+\tau_{n-1}-t)&\text{if }x_{n}<x_{n-1},\\
b_{n}(x_{n-1}-\tau_{n-1}+t)&\text{if }x_{n}\geq x_{n-1}.\end{cases}

Then each
\beta_{n}
is continuous and

\beta_{n}(\tau_{n})=b_{n}(x_{n})=b_{n+1}(x_{n})=\beta_{n+1}(\tau_{n})
; by
Lemma

3.1
.
Thus,

\beta(t):=\beta_{n}(t)\quad\text{ if }t\in[\tau_{n-1},\tau_{n}],~0\leq n\leq m,

defines a continuous function on
[0,\tau_{m}]
.
By
Lemma

3.1
, we have
\beta(t)\leq e^{-n}
for all
t\geq\tau_{n-1}

as well as

(8)

\beta(\tau_{m})=\beta_{m}(\tau_{m})=b_{m}(x_{m})=b_{m+1}(x_{m})\leq e^{-m-1}.

On the other hand, with
j_{0}
as defined in (
7
),

(9)

\beta(0)=\beta_{0}(\tau_{-1})=b_{0}(x_{-1})\geq e^{-j_{0}}.

It might be that
e^{-j_{0}}\leq e^{-m-1}
(including the case
j_{0}=\infty
).
Then the right-hand side of (
6
) is zero so that (
6
) is trivially true.
Thus we may assume that
j_{0}\leq m
.
In view of (
8
) and (
9
),
the range of
\beta
then contains all numbers
e^{-j}
for
j_{0}\leq j\leq m+1
.
So we find a strictly increasing sequence
t_{j}
, for
j_{0}\leq j\leq m+1
,
such that
\beta(t_{j})=e^{-j}

and
\beta(t)>e^{-j}
if

t<t_{j}
(starting in the point
(0,\beta(0))
let
t_{j}
be the first time that the graph of
\beta
meets the horizontal line
with ordinate
e^{-j}
).
Then

(10)

\beta(t_{j-1})<\beta(t_{j})\,e^{e(t_{j}-t_{j-1})\mu_{j}},\quad j_{0}+1\leq j\leq m+1.

To see this, we apply (iii) of
Lemma

3.1
to each
interval in the subdivision of
(t_{j-1},t_{j})
induced by the points
\tau_{n}
between
t_{j-1}
and
t_{j}
,
and notice that, since
t_{j}\leq\tau_{j-1}
(as
\beta(t)\leq e^{-j}
if
t\geq\tau_{j-1}
), we have
n<j
for all such
n
and

\max\{b_{n}(t),e^{-j}\}=b_{n}(t)
for all
t\in(t_{j-1},t_{j})
.

In view of
\beta(t_{j})=e^{-j}
, (
10
)
amounts to

t_{j}-t_{j-1}>\frac{1}{e\mu_{j}},\quad j_{0}+1\leq j\leq m+1.

Summing over
j
, we find

t_{m+1}\geq t_{m+1}-t_{j_{0}}>\frac{1}{e}\sum_{k=j_{0}+1}^{m+1}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}.

Since
\tau_{m}\geq t_{m+1}
, this yields (
6
).
It also shows that the inequality is strict provided that
j_{0}\leq m
.
∎

3.2.
Bounds for the number of zeros

Now it is easy to deduce a lower bound for the length of the interval and
an upper bound for the number of zeros.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
M^{|N}=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
be a full admissible weight.
Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a non-trivial compact interval and

f:I\to\mathbb{R}
an
M^{|N}
-smooth function.
Let
z_{1}\leq z_{2}\leq\cdots\leq z_{m}
be an increasing enumeration of some of the zeros of
f
, where
m\leq N
,
and let
x_{-1}\in I\setminus(z_{1},z_{m})
be arbitrary.
Then we have a lower bound for the length
|I|
of
I
,

(11)

|I|>\frac{1}{e}\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}(j_{0}+1,m),\qquad(j_{0}=\lceil\log b_{f,M^{|N},0}(x_{-1})^{-1}\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}),

and an upper bound for the number of zeros,

(12)

m\leq\mathfrak{d}_{|I|\mu^{|N}}(b_{f,M^{|N},0}(x_{-1})).

Proof.

Suppose that
x_{-1}\leq z_{1}
; if
x_{-1}\geq z_{m}
the proof is similar.
By Rolle’s theorem, there is a sequence of points
x_{-1}\leq x_{0}=z_{1}\leq x_{1}\leq\cdots\leq x_{m-1}

in
I
such that
f^{(j)}(x_{j})=0
for all
0\leq j\leq m-1
.
By (
6
),

|I|\geq\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\geq\frac{1}{e}\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}(j_{0}+1,m),

with strict inequality if
j_{0}\leq m-1
.
If
j_{0}\geq m
, then
\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}(j_{0}+1,m)=0
and
also in that case the inequality is strict, since
I
is assumed to be non-trivial.
So we proved (
11
).
By the definition of
\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|N}}
, also (
12
) follows.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Variations of the argument yield further useful information.
For instance, if
f(x_{-1})\neq 0
and
x_{0}
is an
m
-fold zero of
f
, then

|x_{-1}-x_{0}|\geq\frac{1}{e}\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}(j_{0}+1,m)\geq\frac{1}{e}\Sigma_{\mu^{|N}}(\lceil\log\tfrac{M_{0}}{|f(x_{-1})|}\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}+1,m),

since in this case
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|x_{j}-x_{j-1}|=|x_{-1}-x_{0}|
and

b_{f,M^{|N},0}(x_{-1})\geq\tfrac{|f(x_{-1})|}{M_{0}}
.

In
Corollary

3.4
,
the list of zeros
z_{j}
may not comprise all zeros of
f
.
If
z_{j}
is in the list and
z_{j}
is a multiple zero of
f
, we do not even require that all multiplicities of
z_{j}

appear in the list.

Under an additional assumption, we find that the total number of zeros is finite and get an upper bound for it.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
M^{|\infty}=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
be a full admissible weight.
Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a non-trivial compact interval and

f:I\to\mathbb{R}
an
M^{|\infty}
-smooth function.
Let
x_{-1}\in I
. If

(13)

\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(j_{0}+1,\infty)>|I|e,\qquad(j_{0}=\lceil\log b_{f,M^{|\infty},0}(x_{-1})^{-1}\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}),

then the total number
m
of zeros of
f
in
I
counted with multiplicities is finite.
In that case,
\mathfrak{d}_{|I|\mu^{|\infty}}(b_{f,M^{|\infty},0}(x_{-1}))<\infty
and

(14)

m\leq 2\mathfrak{d}_{|I|\mu^{|\infty}}(b_{f,M^{|\infty},0}(x_{-1})).

For each
x_{-1}\in I
satisfying (
13
)
and not lying strictly between the smallest and the largest zero of
f
, we even have

(15)

m\leq\mathfrak{d}_{|I|\mu^{|\infty}}(b_{f,M^{|\infty},0}(x_{-1})).

If
\mathfrak{d}_{|I|\mu^{|\infty}}(b_{f,M^{|\infty},0}(x_{-1}))=0
, then
f
has no zeros in
I
.

Proof.

By (
13
), it is obvious that
\mathfrak{d}_{|I|\mu^{|\infty}}(b_{f,M^{|\infty},0}(x_{-1}))<\infty
.
Suppose for contradiction that there are infinitely many zeros of
f
.
Then we find an increasing infinite sequence of zeros on the right of
x_{-1}

or a decreasing infinite sequence of zeros on the left of
x_{-1}
(or on both sides).
But that contradicts
Corollary

3.4
.
The upper bounds for positive
m
follow again from
Corollary

3.4
;
if there are
m_{l}
zeros left and
m_{r}
zeros right of
x_{-1}
, then

m=m_{l}+m_{r}\leq 2\mathfrak{d}_{|I|\mu^{|\infty}}(b_{f,M^{|\infty},0}(x_{-1}))
.
In particular, if
f
has zeros in
I
, then
\mathfrak{d}_{|I|\mu^{|\infty}}(b_{f,M^{|\infty},0}(x_{-1}))\neq 0
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

If in
Corollary

3.6
we assume that
f
is an
M^{|N}
-smooth function with finite
N
,
then in general
f
could have more zeros than
N
so that
Corollary

3.4
is not applicable.

On the other hand, in the setting of
Corollary

3.6
we
conclude that the number of zeros of
f
left and right of
x_{-1}

is bounded by

(16)

N:=\mathfrak{d}_{|I|\mu^{|\infty}}(b_{f,M^{|\infty},0}(x_{-1})).

So
a posteriori
we obtain that total number of zeros
m
of
f
in
I

satisfies

(17)

m\leq 2\mathfrak{d}_{|I|\mu^{|N}}(b_{f,M^{|N},0}(x_{-1})),

where
N
is given by (
16
) and
\mu^{|N}
,
M^{|N}

are obtained from
\mu^{|\infty}
,
M^{|\infty}
simply by setting
all elements with index
\geq N+1
equal to
\infty
.
In particular,
the bound on the number of zeros in (
17
)
depends only on the derivatives up order
N
of

f
.

3.3.
Quasianalyticity

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 0}
be an admissible weight.
We see that, if

(18)

\sum_{j}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}=\infty

and
f
is not identically zero,
then condition (
13
) is always satisfied (provided that
j_{0}
is finite) and
Corollary

3.6
applies.
An admissible weight
\mu^{|\infty}
satisfying (
18
) is said to be
quasianalytic
;
if (
18
) is not fulfilled we say that
\mu^{|\infty}
is
non-quasianalytic

(analogously for full admissible weights
M^{|\infty}
).
If we speak of (non-)quasianalytic admissible weights, we always presuppose that
N=\infty
.

In fact, let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a non-trivial compact interval and
let
\mathcal{C}^{M^{|\infty}}(I)
denote the set of all functions
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that
there exists a constant
\rho>0
such that
\|f^{(j)}\|_{I}\leq\rho^{j+1}M_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
\mathcal{C}^{M^{|\infty}}(I)
is called
quasianalytic
if, for all
f\in\mathcal{C}^{M^{|\infty}}(I)
and
x_{0}\in I
,
triviality of the Taylor series
\widehat{f}_{x_{0}}
of
f
at
x_{0}
, i.e.,
\widehat{f}_{x_{0}}=0
,
implies that
f
is identically zero.
By the Denjoy–Carleman theorem (see e.g.
[
25
]
),

\mathcal{C}^{M^{|\infty}}(I)
is quasianalytic if and only if
M^{|\infty}
is quasianalytic
(that is, (
18
) holds).

Note that
Corollary

3.6
implies one direction of this equivalence.
For, assume that
f\in\mathcal{C}^{M^{|\infty}}(I)
,
x_{0}\in I
, and
\widehat{f}_{x_{0}}=0
.
Then there exists
\rho>0
such that
f
is
(\rho\mu^{|\infty},\rho M_{0})
-smooth.
If
f
is not identically zero, we find
x_{-1}\in I
with
f(x_{-1})\neq 0
and thus

j_{0}=\lceil\log b_{f,(\rho^{j+1}M_{j})_{j},0}(x_{-1})^{-1}\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}
is finite.
Since
\widehat{f}_{x_{0}}=0
and thus the number of zeros of
f
is infinite,

Corollary

3.6
implies that (
13
) must be violated.
Since
j_{0}
is finite, we may infer that
(
18
) is violated, that is,
M^{|\infty}
is non-quasianalytic.

4.
The zero set of multivariate functions

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex body.
We will derive quantitative information on the zero set of
M^{|\infty}
-smooth functions
f:K\to\mathbb{R}

in terms of the
\delta_{K}\mu^{|\infty}
-degree; recall that
\delta_{K}
is the diameter of
K
.
It turns out that these results actually depend only on a finite number of derivatives.
To account for this we introduce the following bit of notation.

4.1.
Finite determinacy

Let
M^{|\infty}=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
be a full admissible weight,
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
a convex body,
and
b>0
.
Assume that

(19)

\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(j_{0}+1,\infty)>\delta_{K}e,\qquad(j_{0}=\lceil\log(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})^{-1}\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}).

(Note that (
19
) is in any case satisfied if
M^{|\infty}
is a quasianalytic full admissible weight.
The factor
2
in the definition of
j_{0}
could be replaced by any real number
>1
without
changing the validity of the results.)
Then

(20)

\mathbf{N}:=\mathfrak{d}_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\infty}}(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})

is a nonnegative (finite) integer.
Let

\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
(resp.
M^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
) be the (resp. full) admissible weight
obtained from
\mu^{|\infty}
(resp.
M^{|\infty}
) by setting
all elements with index
\geq\mathbf{N}+2
equal to
\infty
.
Then
\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(j_{0}+1,n)=\Sigma_{\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1}}(j_{0}+1,n)
for
n\leq\mathbf{N}+1
so that

(21)

\mathfrak{d}_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\infty}}(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})=\mathfrak{d}_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1}}(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})=\mathbf{N}.

Furthermore,

(22)

\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(j_{0}+1,\infty)>\Sigma_{\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1}}(j_{0}+1,\infty)=\Sigma_{\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1}}(j_{0}+1,\mathbf{N}+1)\geq\delta_{K}e

which follows easily from the definitions.

Definition 4.1

(Admissible data)
.

We call the triple
(M^{|\infty},K,b)
, where

M^{|\infty}=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
is a full admissible weight,
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
a convex body,
and
b>0
,
admissible data
if (
19
) holds.
In that case,
\mathbf{N}
defined by (
20
) is called the
integer associated with the data
(M^{|\infty},K,b)
.
In this setting,
\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
(resp.
M^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
) will always denote the (resp. full) admissible weight
resulting from
\mu^{|\infty}
(resp.
M^{|\infty}
) by
setting all elements with index
\geq\mathbf{N}+2
equal to
\infty
.

4.2.
Number of zeros on affine lines

Recall that
K^{\circ}
denotes the interior of
K
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(M^{|\infty},K,b)
be admissible data and
\mathbf{N}
the associated integer.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be any
M^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
-smooth function such that
\|f\|_{K}\geq b
.
Then there is a ball
B\subseteq K^{\circ}
,
whose radius only depends on
K
and the ratio
\frac{b}{M_{1}}
,
such that for each affine line
\ell
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that meets
B

the restriction
f|_{\ell}

has at most
2\mathbf{N}
zeros.

Proof.

Consider
U:=\{x\in K:|f(x)|>\frac{b}{2}\}
.
Since
\|f\|_{K}\geq b
, there is
a\in U
with
|f(a)|\geq b
.
For all
x\in B_{s}(a)\cap K
with
s:=\frac{b}{3M_{1}}
, we have

|f(x)-f(a)|\leq\|f\|_{1,K}\,|x-a|\leq M_{1}s=\tfrac{b}{3},

so that

|f(x)|\geq|f(a)|-|f(x)-f(a)|\geq\tfrac{2b}{3}.

It follows that
a\in B_{s}(a)\cap K\subseteq U
.
But the intersection
B_{s}(a)\cap K^{\circ}
contains an open ball
B
,
whose radius depends only on
s
and on the “thickness” of
K
near
a
.

Let
\ell\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any affine line that meets the ball
B
.
Let
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
be the intersection points of
\ell
with the boundary of
K
.
Then
g:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
given by
g(t):=f(x_{0}+t(x_{1}-x_{0}))
defines a
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{N}+1}
-function
satisfying

\|g^{(j)}\|_{[0,1]}\leq|x_{1}-x_{0}|^{j}\|f\|_{j,K}\leq\delta_{K}^{j}M_{j},\quad 0\leq j\leq\mathbf{N}+1.

Thus,
g:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is
(\delta_{K}\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1},M_{0})
-smooth.
For all
t\in[0,1]
with
x_{0}+t(x_{1}-x_{0})\in B
, we find

b_{g,(\delta_{K}^{j}M_{j})_{j},0}(t)=\sup_{j\geq 0}\frac{|g^{(j)}(t)|}{(\delta_{K}e)^{j}M_{j}}\geq\frac{|g(t)|}{M_{0}}>\frac{b}{2M_{0}},

whence
\mathfrak{d}_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1}}(b_{g,(\delta_{K}^{j}M_{j})_{j},0}(t))\leq\mathfrak{d}_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1}}(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})=\mathbf{N}
(see (
21
)).
Fix such a
t
.
Suppose that
g
has (at least)
\mathbf{N}+1
zeros left or right of
t
.
Then
Corollary

3.4
implies

\mathbf{N}+1\leq\mathfrak{d}_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1}}(b_{g,(\delta_{K}^{j}M_{j})_{j},0}(t))\leq\mathbf{N},

a contradiction.
Thus the total number of zeros of
g
, and hence of
f|_{\ell}
, is finite and bounded by
2\mathbf{N}
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

In the setting of
Theorem

4.2
,
if
K=\overline{B}_{r}
, then
\delta_{K}=2r
and the radius of
B
is at least
\frac{1}{2}\min\{\frac{b}{3M_{1}},r\}
.

Proof.

The intersection
B_{s}(a)\cap B_{r}
contains an open ball
B
of radius at least
\frac{1}{2}\min\{s,r\}
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In analogy to
Remark

3.5
,
we can give at each point in
K
a lower bound for the distance to the closest zero of
f
of a specific multiplicity;
often the bound is trivial, but not always:

Let
M^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
be a full admissible weight and
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
a convex body.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be an
M^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
-smooth function and
x_{-1}\in K\setminus Z_{f}
.
Then
f
has no zeros of multiplicity at least
m
, where
m\leq\mathbf{N}+1
,
in the ball
B_{\epsilon_{m}}(x_{-1})
with center
x_{-1}
and radius

\epsilon_{m}:=\frac{1}{e}\Sigma_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1}}(j_{0}+1,m),\qquad(j_{0}=\lceil\log\tfrac{M_{0}}{|f(x_{-1})|}\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}).

This follows from
Remark

3.5
applied to all affine lines through
x_{-1}
.

In particular,
f
has no zeros in
B_{\epsilon_{1}}(x_{-1})
.
But
\epsilon_{1}\neq 0
only if
j_{0}=0
which is equivalent to
|f(x_{-1})|=M_{0}
; in that case
\epsilon_{1}=\frac{1}{\delta_{K}e\mu_{1}}
.
Furthermore,
f
has no zeros that are also critical points in
B_{\epsilon_{2}}(x_{-1})
, where

\epsilon_{2}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\delta_{K}e}(\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}+\frac{1}{\mu_{2}}),&\text{ if }|f(x_{-1})|=M_{0},\\
\frac{1}{\delta_{K}e\mu_{2}},&\text{ if }\frac{M_{0}}{e}\leq|f(x_{-1})|<M_{0},\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

For later use, we remark that the zero set of a function

as in
Theorem

4.2
has zero Lebesgue measure.
This will be a trivial consequence of
Theorem

4.6
, if

is additionally assumed to be of class
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
,
but we will need it without this assumption.

Corollary 4.5
.

In the setting of
Theorem

4.2
,

\mathcal{L}^{d}(Z_{f})=0
.

Proof.

First of all,
f
is continuous so that
Z_{f}
is closed, hence measurable.
The union of all affine lines of a fixed direction meeting
B
form an open cylinder
U
.
By compactness,
K
is covered by finitely many such cylinders
U
.
Now
Z_{f}\cap U
is measurable, whence we can apply Fubini’s theorem to its characteristic function.
By
Theorem

4.2
, this gives
\mathcal{L}^{d}(Z_{f}\cap U)=0
.
∎

4.3.
Hausdorff measure of the zero set

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(M^{|\infty},K,b)
be admissible data and
\mathbf{N}
the associated integer.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be any
M^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
-smooth
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-function such that
\|f\|_{K}\geq b
.
Then the zero set
Z_{f}
is contained in a countable union of compact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-hypersurfaces
and its
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure satisfies

(23)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z_{f})\leq C\,\mathbf{N}\,\delta_{K}^{d-1},

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
d
and on the ratio
\tfrac{\delta_{B}}{\delta_{K}}
and where

B
is the ball from
Theorem

4.2
.
In the case
K=\overline{B}_{r}
, the constant

depends only on
d
and
\min\{\frac{b}{6rM_{1}},\frac{1}{2}\}
;
it blows up as
b\to 0
or

.

Proof.

The first statement follows from Malgrange’s preparation theorem
and
[
31
, Lemma 6]
.
Thus,
we may apply the Crofton-type result
[
31
, Lemma 7]

to conclude (
23
) from
Theorem

4.2
.

If
K=\overline{B}_{r}
,
then we may take
\delta_{B}=\min\{\frac{b}{3M_{1}},r\}
, by
Corollary

4.3
,
so that
\tfrac{\delta_{B}}{\delta_{K}}=\min\{\frac{b}{6rM_{1}},\frac{1}{2}\}
. This also yields the asymptotics of the constant

.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

In

4.6
(and in the next result),

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}

That

4.4.
Sobolev parameterization of the zero set

Let
(M^{|\infty},K,b)
be admissible data and
\mathbf{N}
the associated integer.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be any
M^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
-smooth
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-function such that
\|f\|_{K}\geq b
.

The collection
\mathcal{L}
of all affine lines in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that meet the open ball
B
from
Theorem

4.2

covers
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.,
\mathbb{R}^{d}=\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\ell
.
Fix
z_{0}\in Z_{f}
and any line
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
with
z_{0}\in\ell
.
By
Theorem

4.2
, the multiplicity
n
of
z_{0}
as a zero of
f|_{\ell}
is at most
2\mathbf{N}
.
Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
be an orthogonal coordinate system such that
\ell
coincides with the
x_{d}
-axis.
By Malgrange’s preparation theorem,
there is a rectangular neighborhood
U
of
z_{0}

of the form
U:=\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\alpha_{j}<x_{j}<\beta_{j}\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq d\}

such that
f(x)=p(x)u(x)
for
x\in U
, where
u
does not vanish on
U
and

(24)

p(x^{\prime},x_{d})=x_{d}^{n}+a_{1}(x^{\prime})x_{d}^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{d}(x^{\prime})

is a polynomial in
x_{d}
with
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-coefficients
a_{j}
which are defined on
the projection
U^{\prime}
of
U
onto the first
d-1
coordinates

x^{\prime}:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-1})
.
Thus,

Z_{f}\cap U=Z_{p}\cap U.

Let
[\zeta_{1}(x^{\prime}),\ldots,\zeta_{n}(x^{\prime})]
be the unordered
n
-tuple of complex roots (with multiplicities)
of the polynomial
p(x^{\prime},x_{d})
in
x_{d}

for any
x^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
.
Note that the number of real roots among the
\zeta_{j}(x^{\prime})
may change with varying
x^{\prime}
.
Let
(\xi_{1}(x^{\prime}),\ldots,\xi_{n}(x^{\prime}))
be the
n
-tuple consisting of the real parts of the
\zeta_{j}(x^{\prime})
,
ordered increasingly such that
\xi_{1}(x^{\prime})\leq\xi_{2}(x^{\prime})\leq\cdots\leq\xi_{n}(x^{\prime})
for all
x^{\prime}
.
Since all the real roots among the
\zeta_{j}(x^{\prime})
clearly are also among the
\xi_{i}(x^{\prime})

(with correct multiplicities),
it follows that

\displaystyle Z_{f}\cap U

\displaystyle=Z_{p}\cap U

\displaystyle=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in U:\text{ there is }1\leq j\leq n\text{ with }x_{d}=\zeta_{j}(x^{\prime})\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in U:\text{ there is }1\leq i\leq n\text{ with }x_{d}=\xi_{i}(x^{\prime})\}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma(\xi_{i}),

where
\Gamma(\xi_{i}):=\{(x^{\prime},\xi_{i}(x^{\prime})):x^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}\}
denotes the graph of
\xi_{i}:U^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
.
The increasing order of the
\xi_{i}
implies that each
\xi_{i}
is a
continuous function
U^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}

and so, applying
[
22
, Remark 9]
and
[
23
, Theorem A.1]
(see also
[
21
]
),
we may conclude that each
\xi_{i}
is of Sobolev class
\xi_{i}\in W^{1,p}(U^{\prime})
,
for all
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
,
and
\|\xi_{i}\|_{W^{1,p}(U^{\prime})}
depends uniformly on
f
; see
Remark

4.10
.
In view of
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
, we obtain that
each graph
\Gamma(\xi_{i})
is countably
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-rectifiable and

(25)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\{(x^{\prime},\xi_{i}(x^{\prime})):x^{\prime}\in E\})=\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla\xi_{i}(x^{\prime})|^{2}}\,dx^{\prime},

for all measurable subsets
E\subseteq U^{\prime}
.
The right-hand side of (
25
) depends uniformly
on
f
(see
Remark

4.10
).

Now it is easy to conclude

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(M^{|\infty},K,b)
be admissible data and
\mathbf{N}
the associated integer.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be any
M^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
-smooth
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-function such that
\|f\|_{K}\geq b
.
There is a finite cover of
K
by rectangular boxes
U
with corresponding orthogonal coordinates
(x^{\prime},x_{d})

such that
Z_{f}\cap U
is contained in the graphs
\{x_{d}=\xi_{i}(x^{\prime})\}
of at most
2\mathbf{N}

continuous functions
\xi_{i}

of Sobolev class
W^{1,p}
,
for all
1\leq p<\frac{2\mathbf{N}}{2\mathbf{N}-1}
,
whose
W^{1,p}
-norm depends uniformly on
f
(see
Remark

4.10
).
Furthermore, (
25
) holds.

Note that the Sobolev regularity in the results of
[
22
,
23
]

used above is optimal.

Remark 4.9
.

It is evident from the above arguments
that the statement of
Theorem

4.8
remains true for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-functions
f:K\to\mathbb{R}

that satisfy

\|f\|_{j,K}\leq\frac{1}{2\delta_{K}^{j}}\|f\|_{0,K},\quad\text{ for some }j\geq 2,

where
2\mathbf{N}
is replaced by
j-1
.
Use
[
31
, Theorem 3(ii)]
.

Remark 4.10
.

In general,
the coefficients
a_{j}
of the polynomial (
24
) will no longer be
M^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
-smooth (cf.
[
1
]
).
But the choice of the functions
a_{j}
(and
u
) can be made to depend linearly and continuously (with respect to the
Whitney
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
topology) on
f
(cf.
[
19
]
).
Furthermore, we have uniform bounds for the
W^{1,p}
-norm of the functions
\xi_{i}
in terms of
the
\mathcal{C}^{2\mathbf{N}}
-norm of the
a_{j}
(cf.
[
22
]
and
[
23
]
).

4.5.
Further remarks

Let
(M^{|\infty},K,b)
be admissible data and
\mathbf{N}
the associated integer.

Remark 4.11
.

By definition, the number
\mathbf{N}
depends only on
(M^{|\infty},K,b)
.
The above results on

remain unchanged as long as

satisfies all the respective assumptions.
E.g.,
the bound on
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z_{f})
in (

) is the same as long as

fulfills the assumptions of
Theorem

4.6
.

Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be an
M^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
-smooth function with
\|f\|_{K}\geq b
.
If we set
V(f):=\sup_{x,y\in K}|f(x)-f(y)|
, then

and hence

\|f-c\|_{K}\geq\frac{V(f-c)}{2}=\frac{V(f)}{2}\quad\text{ for all }c\in\mathbb{R}.

On the other hand,

\|f-c\|_{K}\leq M_{0}\quad\text{ if }|c|\leq M_{0}-\|f\|_{K}.

So, if
2b\leq V(f)
,
we get the same uniform results for the level sets
Z_{f-c}=f^{-1}(c)
for all
|c|\leq M_{0}-\|f\|_{K}
.

Remark 4.12
.

If we assume that

is
(\delta_{K}^{-1}\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth with

and accordingly

instead of (

),
then
we get all the above results with both
the
\mu^{|\infty}
-degree

\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(\frac{b}{2M_{0}})

in (

)
independent of
\delta_{K}
.

Remark 4.13
.

A closer inspection of the proof of
Theorem

4.2
reveals that
it would suffice to assume that each restriction
f|_{\ell}:K\cap\ell\to\mathbb{R}
of
f
is
M^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
-smooth,
where
\ell
is any affine line intersecting
K
.
But this is not far from the assumption that

is
M^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
-smooth:
for simplicity assume that

is of class
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
(which is needed in
Theorem

4.6
and

Theorem

4.8
anyway). Then the uniformity of the bounds and the polarization inequality (
[
15
, 7.13.1]
),

\sup_{v\in\overline{B}_{1}}|d^{j}_{v}f(x)|\leq\|d^{j}f(x)\|_{L^{j}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})}\leq(2e)^{j}\sup_{v\in\overline{B}_{1}}|d^{j}_{v}f(x)|,

,

,

[
14
]
and
[
24
]
.

5.
Remez inequality for functions with controlled derivatives

In this section, we prove a Remez-type inequality for
M^{|N}
-smooth (and
\mu^{|N}
-smooth) functions in several variables
and derive several consequences.
Our results are based on a univariate version due to
[
20
]
which we recall in slightly modified form
in
Theorem

5.1
.

5.1.
Definitions and conventions

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight.
Let us assume that there is an increasing continuous
function
\tilde{\mu}:[1,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
that is (piecewise)
\mathcal{C}^{1}
such that

\mu_{j}=\tilde{\mu}(j),\quad j\geq 1.

This is no real restriction, since we may always take
\tilde{\mu}
piecewise affine and work consistently with the left derivative
at points, where
\tilde{\mu}
is not differentiable.

Once we have
\tilde{\mu}
, we define (following
[
20
]
)

\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(n):=\sup_{1\leq s\leq n}\frac{s\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}(s)}{\tilde{\mu}(s)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\Gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(n):=4e^{4+\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(n)},

for all positive integers
n
.
Note that
\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}
and
\Gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}
depend on the choice of
\tilde{\mu}
which is not unique.

In this section, we will again make use of the terminology introduced in
Section

4.1
(see
Definition

4.1
).
But here it is better to replace
b
by
2b
.
So let
(M^{|\infty},K,2b)
be admissible data and
\mathbf{N}
the associated integer
.
Recall that this means the following:

M^{|\infty}
is a full admissible weight,
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
a convex body, and
b>0
such that

(26)

\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(j_{0}+1,\infty)>\delta_{K}e,\qquad(j_{0}=\lceil\log(\tfrac{b}{M_{0}})^{-1}\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}).

The associated nonnegative integer
\mathbf{N}
is given by

\mathbf{N}:=\mathfrak{d}_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\infty}}(\tfrac{b}{M_{0}}).

It satisfies (
21
) and (
22
) with
b
replaced by
2b
,
in particular,

(27)

\mathfrak{d}_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\infty}}(\tfrac{b}{M_{0}})=\mathfrak{d}_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1}}(\tfrac{b}{M_{0}})=\mathbf{N}.

In all occurrences of the Remez inequality for functions with controlled derivatives,
the constant
\Gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(2\mathbf{N})^{2\mathbf{N}}
will appear.
If
\mathfrak{d}_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\infty}}(\frac{b}{M_{0}})=\mathbf{N}=0
it is undefined.
For ease of notation, we set

(28)

C_{\mathbf{N}}:=\Gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(2\mathbf{N})

with the interpretation
C_{\mathbf{N}}^{2\mathbf{N}}=:\infty
if
\mathbf{N}=0
; see
Remark

5.2
.

5.2.
Remez inequality for univariate functions

We recall and slightly modify
[
20
, Theorem B]
.
The statement in
[
20
]
involves the so-called Bang degree

\mathfrak{n}_{f}
(which depends on
f
)
and it is formulated for a quasianalytic full admissible weight
M^{|\infty}
with
M_{0}=1
.
Here we work with
\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(\frac{b}{M_{0}})=\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1}}(\frac{b}{M_{0}})
(which is independent of
f
) instead of
\mathfrak{n}_{f}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(M^{|\infty},[0,1],2b)
be admissible data and
\mathbf{N}
the associated integer.
Let
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be any
M^{|2\mathbf{N}}
-smooth function such that
\|f\|_{[0,1]}\geq b
.
Then for any interval
I\subseteq[0,1]
and any Lebesgue measurable set
E\subseteq I
with
|E|>0
we have

(29)

\|f\|_{I}\leq\Big(\frac{C_{\mathbf{N}}\,|I|}{|E|}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}\|f\|_{E}.

Remark 5.2
.

If
\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(\frac{b}{M_{0}})=\mathbf{N}=0
, then, by the interpretation
C_{\mathbf{N}}^{2\mathbf{N}}=\infty
,
(
29
) is trivially true.

Proof of
Theorem

5.1
.

First of all, we may assume that
M_{0}=1
, by dividing
f
by
M_{0}
.
In fact,
\bar{f}:=\frac{1}{M_{0}}f
is
(\mu^{|2\mathbf{N}},1)
-smooth and
\|\bar{f}\|_{[0,1]}\geq\frac{b}{M_{0}}=:\bar{b}>0

so that (
26
), (
29
), and the integer
\mathbf{N}
remain unchanged.

By
Remark

5.2
, we may assume that
\mathbf{N}>0
and
follow the proof of
[
20
]
;
it involves only derivatives up to order
2\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(b)=2\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1}}(b)=2\mathbf{N}
(cf. (
27
) with
K=[0,1]
and
M_{0}=1
).

In the only place, where the definition of
\mathfrak{n}_{f}
actually plays a role in the argument,
we use the following estimate (cf.
[
20
, p. 72]
):

(30)

\min_{t\in[0,1]}b_{0}(t)=\min_{t\in[0,1]}\sup_{j\geq 0}\frac{|f^{(j)}(t)|}{e^{j}M_{j}}>e^{-\mathbf{N}-1}.

Let us justify (
30
). Suppose it is not true, i.e.,

\min_{t\in[0,1]}b_{0}(t)\leq e^{-\mathbf{N}-1}
.
On the other hand,
\max_{t\in[0,1]}b_{0}(t)\geq b\geq e^{-j_{0}}
, by the definition of
j_{0}
(recall that
M_{0}=1
).
We have
j_{0}<\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1}}(b)+1=\mathbf{N}+1
(cf.
Section

2.3
),
that is
e^{-\mathbf{N}-1}<e^{-j_{0}}
.
Since
b_{0}
is continuous (cf.
Lemma

3.1
),
there is a monotonic sequence
x_{j}\in[0,1]
such that
b_{0}(x_{j})=e^{-j}
for all

j_{0}\leq j\leq\mathbf{N}+1
(cf. the proof of
Proposition

3.2
).
By
Lemma

3.1
, we have

|x_{j}-x_{j-1}|>\frac{1}{e\mu_{j}}

for
j_{0}+1\leq j\leq\mathbf{N}+1

so that

1\geq\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{\mathbf{N}+1}|x_{j}-x_{j-1}|>\frac{1}{e}\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{\mathbf{N}+1}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}},

and thus
\mathbf{N}=\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(\frac{b}{M_{0}})\geq\mathbf{N}+1
, a contradiction.
Hence (
30
) is proved.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

As pointed out in
[
20
]
,
if
\overline{\gamma}_{\tilde{\mu}}:=\sup_{s\geq 1}\frac{s\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}(s)}{\tilde{\mu}(s)}<\infty

(as is the case for
\tilde{\mu}(s)=s
or
\tilde{\mu}(s)=s(\log(s+e))^{\delta}
if
0<\delta\leq 1
),
then in (
29
) the constant
C_{\mathbf{N}}
can be replaced by

\overline{\Gamma}_{\tilde{\mu}}=4e^{4+\overline{\gamma}_{\tilde{\mu}}}
, provided that
\mathbf{N}\neq 0
(see
Remark

5.2
).

5.3.
Remez inequality for multivariate functions

Next we prove a Remez-type inequality for multivariate functions with controlled derivatives.
The proof is inspired by the technique of
[
11
]
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
(M^{|\infty},K,2b)
be admissible data and
\mathbf{N}
the associated integer.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be any
M^{|2\mathbf{N}}
-smooth function and

L\subseteq K
any convex body
such that
\|f\|_{L}\geq b
.
If
E\subseteq L
is a Lebesgue measurable subset with
|E|>0
, then

\|f\|_{L}\leq\Big(\frac{C_{\mathbf{N}}\,|L|^{1/d}}{|L|^{1/d}-(|L|-|E|)^{1/d}}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}\|f\|_{E}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}(E,L)=\mathcal{F}_{M^{|2\mathbf{N}}}(E,L,b)
denote the set of all
M^{|2\mathbf{N}}
-smooth functions
f:K\to\mathbb{R}

satisfying
\|f\|_{L}\geq b
and
\|f\|_{E}\leq 1
.
For
\lambda>0
consider

\mathcal{R}(\lambda,L)=\mathcal{R}_{M^{|2\mathbf{N}}}(\lambda,L,b):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\subseteq L\\
|E|\geq\lambda\end{subarray}}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}(E,L)}\|f\|_{L}.

We claim that

(31)

\mathcal{R}(\lambda,L)\leq\Big(\frac{C_{\mathbf{N}}\,|L|^{1/d}}{|L|^{1/d}-(|L|-\lambda)^{1/d}}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}.

Fix a measurable subset
E\subseteq L
with
|E|\geq\lambda
and
f\in\mathcal{F}(E,L)
.
There is

x_{0}\in L
with
\|f\|_{L}=|f(x_{0})|
.
Let
\ell
be any half-line emanating from
x_{0}

and let
x_{1}
be the (other) intersection point of
\ell
with
\partial L
.
Define
g:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
by
g(t):=f(x_{0}+t(x_{1}-x_{0}))
.
As seen in the proof of
Theorem

4.2
,
g:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is
(\delta_{K}\mu^{|2\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth
and
\|g\|_{[0,1]}\geq|g(0)|=|f(x_{0})|\geq b
.
Thus (in view of (
26
) and since
\|f\|_{E}\leq 1
)
Theorem

5.1
implies

\|f\|_{L}=|f(x_{0})|=|g(0)|\leq\Big(\frac{C_{\mathbf{N}}\,\mathcal{L}^{1}(L\cap\ell)}{\mathcal{L}^{1}(E\cap\ell)}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}},

because of the scaling properties of the
1
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{1}
(induced on
\ell
);
note that
\gamma_{\delta_{K}\tilde{\mu}}=\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}
and hence
\Gamma_{\delta_{K}\tilde{\mu}}=\Gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}
.
Taking the essential infimum over all half-lines emanating from
x_{0}
,
the supremum over all
f\in\mathcal{F}(E,L)
, and the
supremum over all measurable
E\subseteq L
with
|E|\geq\lambda
, we find

\mathcal{R}(\lambda,L)\leq\Big(C_{\mathbf{N}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\subseteq L\\
|E|\geq\lambda\end{subarray}}\essinf_{\ell}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{1}(L\cap\ell)}{\mathcal{L}^{1}(E\cap\ell)}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}},

and, using
[
11
, Lemma 3 and Remark 2]
, we conclude (
31
).

Now we may prove the statement of the theorem.
Let
f
,
L
, and
E
be as in the assumptions of the theorem.
Since
|E|>0
, we have
\|f\|_{E}>0
, by
Corollary

4.5
.
Then
F:=\frac{f}{\|f\|_{E}}
is
\frac{1}{\|f\|_{E}}M^{|2\mathbf{N}}
-smooth and satisfies

\|F\|_{L}\geq\frac{b}{\|f\|_{E}}
and
\|F\|_{E}=1
.
Thus
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\frac{1}{\|f\|_{E}}M^{|2\mathbf{N}}}(E,L,\frac{b}{\|f\|_{E}})
; note that (
26
),
\mathbf{N}
, and
C_{\mathbf{N}}

remain unchanged.
Then (
31
) implies

\|F\|_{L}\leq\Big(\frac{C_{\mathbf{N}}\,|L|^{1/d}}{|L|^{1/d}-(|L|-|E|)^{1/d}}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}.

Since
\|F\|_{L}=\frac{\|f\|_{L}}{\|f\|_{E}}
, this completes the proof.
∎

5.4.
An important consequence

The next result is a simple but important consequence of
Theorem

5.4
.

For its formulation, it is convenient to adapt our terminology.
We say that the pair
(\mu^{|\infty},K)
is admissible data with associated integer
\mathbf{N}
if

\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
is an admissible weight and
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
a convex body such that

(32)

\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(1,\infty)>\delta_{K}e

and

\mathbf{N}:=\mathfrak{d}_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\infty}}(1).

As before,
C_{\mathbf{N}}:=\Gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(2\mathbf{N})
and
C_{\mathbf{N}}^{2\mathbf{N}}=:\infty
if
\mathbf{N}=0
.

We will specialize
Theorem

5.4
to the case
b=M_{0}=\|f\|_{K}
, that is to

(\mu^{|2\mathbf{N}},\|f\|_{K})
-smooth functions
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
.
Recall that, by the definition in
Section

2.2
,

f:K\to\mathbb{R}
is called

(\mu^{|2\mathbf{N}},\|f\|_{K})
-smooth if
\|f\|_{K}>0
and

\|f\|_{j,K}\leq\|f\|_{K}\cdot\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\cdots\mu_{j},\quad 1\leq j\leq 2\mathbf{N}.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(\mu^{|\infty},K)
be admissible data and
\mathbf{N}
the associated integer.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be any
(\mu^{|2\mathbf{N}},\|f\|_{K})
-smooth function.
If
E\subseteq K
is a Lebesgue measurable subset with
|E|>0
, then

(33)

\|f\|_{K}\leq\Big(\frac{C_{\mathbf{N}}\,|K|^{1/d}}{|K|^{1/d}-(|K|-|E|)^{1/d}}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}\|f\|_{E}.

Proof.

Set
L=K
and
b=M_{0}:=\|f\|_{K}
in
Theorem

5.4
.
∎

There is no
a priori
condition on
\|f\|_{K}
, except
\|f\|_{K}>0
, in
Theorem

5.5
.
Visibly, (
33
) is invariant under
the action of
\mathbb{R}^{*}
on
f
.

It can happen that
\mathfrak{d}_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\infty}}(1)=\mathbf{N}=0
, in which case
Theorem

5.5
contains no information
(cf.
Remark

5.2
).
In fact, this occurs precisely if
\delta_{K}\mu_{1}\leq 1/e
.

5.5.
Volume of sublevel sets

Theorem

5.5
has several interesting corollaries.
We begin with a bound on the growth of the volume of sublevel sets.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
(\mu^{|\infty},K)
be admissible data and
\mathbf{N}
the associated integer.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be any
(\mu^{|2\mathbf{N}},\|f\|_{K})
-smooth function.
Then the sublevel set
S_{t}:=\{x\in K:|f(x)|\leq t\}
satisfies

(34)

|S_{t}|\leq C_{\mathbf{N}}d\,|K|\,\Big(\frac{t}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2\mathbf{N}}},\quad t>0,

with the understanding that the right-hand side is
\infty
if
\mathbf{N}=0
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\mathbf{N}\neq 0
and that
|S_{t}|>0
(the inequality being trivial otherwise).
Apply
Theorem

5.5
to
E=S_{t}
.
Then, putting
\theta:=\frac{|S_{t}|}{|K|}
,

\|f\|_{K}\leq\Big(\frac{C_{\mathbf{N}}}{1-(1-\theta)^{1/d}}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}t,

and consequently,

\frac{\theta}{d}\leq 1-(1-\theta)^{1/d}\leq C_{\mathbf{N}}\,\Big(\frac{t}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2\mathbf{N}}}.

The statement follows.
∎

Corollary

5.6
implies useful estimates for the distribution function and the decreasing rearrangement of
f

(more generally, of
f|_{E}
for a measurable subset
E\subseteq K
).
Recall that the distribution function of
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

d_{f}(t):=|\{x\in K:|f(x)|>t\}|=|K|-|S_{t}|

and the decreasing rearrangement of
f
by

f^{*}(y):=\inf\{t>0:d_{f}(t)\leq y\}.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
(\mu^{|\infty},K)
be admissible data and
\mathbf{N}
the associated integer.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be any
(\mu^{|2\mathbf{N}},\|f\|_{K})
-smooth function.
Let
E\subseteq K
be a Lebesgue measurable subset with
|E|>0
.
Then

(35)

(f|_{E})^{*}(|E|\,\lambda)\geq\|f\|_{K}\Big(\frac{|E|}{|K|}\cdot\frac{1-\lambda}{C_{\mathbf{N}}d}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}},\quad\lambda\in(0,1),

where the right-hand side is identically zero if
\mathbf{N}=0
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathbf{N}\neq 0
.
We have

d_{f|_{E}}(t):=|\{x\in E:|f(x)|>t\}|=|E|-|S_{t}\cap E|=|E|\,\lambda_{t},

where
\lambda_{t}:=1-|S_{t}\cap E|/|E|
.
Let
s_{\lambda}
denote the right-hand side of (
35
).
By
Corollary

5.6
,

(36)

d_{f|_{E}}(t)=|E|\,\lambda_{t}>|E|\,\lambda,\quad\text{ if }t\in(0,s_{\lambda}).

Indeed, by (
34
),

\displaystyle|E|(1-\lambda_{t})=|S_{t}\cap E|\leq|S_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathbf{N}}d\,|K|\,\Big(\frac{t}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2\mathbf{N}}}

\displaystyle<C_{\mathbf{N}}d\,|K|\,\Big(\frac{s_{\lambda}}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2\mathbf{N}}}=|E|(1-\lambda).

Now (
36
) implies
(f|_{E})^{*}(|E|\,\lambda)\geq s_{\lambda}
,
and (
35
) is proved.
∎

5.6.
Comparison of
L^{p}
-norms

Let us write

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{\sharp}

\displaystyle:=\Big(\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{1/p},\quad 0<p<\infty,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(E)}^{\sharp}

\displaystyle:=\esssup_{E}|f|,

for the normalized
L^{p}
-norms (respectively, quasinorms if
0<p<1
) of
f
on a measurable set
E
with
0<|E|<\infty
.
Then, as a consequence of Hölder’s inequality,

\|f\|_{L^{q}(E)}^{\sharp}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{\sharp},\quad\text{ if }0<q\leq p\leq\infty.

Indeed, since
p/q\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{E}|f|^{q}\,dx\leq|E|^{1-q/p}\Big(\int_{E}|f|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{q/p}.

For functions with controlled derivatives also suitable opposite inequalities hold.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
(\mu^{|\infty},K)
be admissible data and
\mathbf{N}
the associated integer.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be any
(\mu^{|2\mathbf{N}},\|f\|_{K})
-smooth function.
Let
E\subseteq K
be a Lebesgue measurable subset with
|E|>0
.
Then, for all
0<q<p\leq\infty
,

(37)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(K)}^{\sharp}\leq\Big(\frac{C_{\mathbf{N}}d\,|K|}{|E|}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}(1-\frac{q}{p})}(2q\,\mathbf{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}(\|f\|_{L^{q}(K)}^{\sharp})^{\frac{q}{p}}(\|f\|_{L^{q}(E)}^{\sharp})^{1-\frac{q}{p}}.

In particular,

(38)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(K)}^{\sharp}\leq(C_{\mathbf{N}}d)^{2\mathbf{N}(1-\frac{q}{p})}(2q\,\mathbf{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(K)}^{\sharp}

and

(39)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(K)}^{\sharp}\leq\Big(\frac{C_{\mathbf{N}}d\,|K|}{|E|}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}\,(2q\mathbf{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\|f\|_{L^{q}(E)}^{\sharp}.

Proof.

We may assume that
\mathbf{N}\neq 0
; otherwise the statements are trivially true.
Note that both (
38
) and (
39
) follow from (
37
):
it suffices to specialize to
E=K

and to use
\|f\|_{L^{q}(K)}^{\sharp}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(K)}^{\sharp}
, respectively.

Let us prove (
37
).
We begin with the case
p=\infty
.
Using (
35
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}|f(x)|^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{0}^{|E|}\big((f|_{E})^{*}(y)\big)^{q}\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}\big((f|_{E})^{*}(|E|\,\lambda)\big)^{q}\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\frac{|E|}{C_{\mathbf{N}}d\,|K|}\Big)^{2q\mathbf{N}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\lambda)^{2q\mathbf{N}}\,d\lambda\,\|f\|_{K}^{q}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{|E|}{C_{\mathbf{N}}d\,|K|}\Big)^{2q\mathbf{N}}\frac{1}{2q\mathbf{N}+1}\,\|f\|_{K}^{q},

whence

(40)

\|f\|_{K}\leq\Big(\frac{C_{\mathbf{N}}d\,|K|}{|E|}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}(2q\,\mathbf{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(E)}^{\sharp}

which is (
37
) for
p=\infty
.

If
0<q<p<\infty
, then (
40
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx\leq\|f\|_{K}^{p-q}\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}|f(x)|^{q}\,dx=\|f\|_{K}^{p-q}\,(\|f\|_{L^{q}(K)}^{\sharp})^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\Big(\frac{C_{\mathbf{N}}d\,|K|}{|E|}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}(2q\,\mathbf{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big)^{p-q}\,(\|f\|_{L^{q}(K)}^{\sharp})^{q}(\|f\|_{L^{q}(E)}^{\sharp})^{p-q}

from which (
37
) follows easily.
The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

Bourgain proved
in
[
8
]
the following inequality for polynomials:

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex body of volume
1
and
p\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
a polynomial of degree
n
.
Then, for each
q>0
,

\|p\|_{L^{q}(K)}\leq C(n,q)\,\|p\|_{L^{1}(K)}.

More precisely,

\|p\|_{L^{\psi}(K)}\leq C_{1}\,\|p\|_{L^{1}(K)},

where
L^{\psi}
it the Orlicz space with Orlicz function
\psi(t)=\exp(t^{C_{2}/n})-1
,
where the constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are absolute.

These inequalities have been generalized by Brudnyi
[
10
]
to analytic functions,
where the role of
n
is played by the
analytic degree
; see
Section

6.2
.

In contrast to (
37
), (
38
), and (
39
),
here the constants do not depend on the dimension
d
.
Following Bourgain’s approach,
it should be possible to obtain versions of (
37
), (
38
),
and (
39
) that are independent of the dimension:
use
Theorem

5.5
for
d=1
and
K=[0,1]
to get a replacement for
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
.

5.7.
A bound for the mean oscillation of
\log|f|

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex body.
Recall that the
mean oscillation
of a locally integrable function
g:K\to\mathbb{R}
over a ball
B\subseteq K
is

\operatorname{mo}_{B}(g):=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|g(x)-g_{B}|\,dx,

where

g_{B}:=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}g(x)\,dx.

We have the following bound for the mean oscillation of
\log|f|
if

f
has suitably controlled derivatives.

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
(\mu^{|\infty},K)
be admissible data and
\mathbf{N}
the associated integer.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be any
(\mu^{|2\mathbf{N}},\|f\|_{K})
-smooth function.
Then, for each ball
B\subseteq K
,

(41)

\operatorname{mo}_{B}(\log|f|)\leq 4\mathbf{N}\Big(\log\Big(\frac{C_{\mathbf{N}}d\,|K|}{|B|}\Big)+1\Big)

where the right-hand side is interpreted as
\infty
if
\mathbf{N}=0
.

Proof.

Observe that

\operatorname{mo}_{B}(\log|f|)\leq\frac{2}{|B|}\int_{B}\Big|\log\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big|\,dx.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\Big|\log|f|-\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\log|f|\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\log|f|-\log\|f\|_{K}\Big|+\Big|\log\|f\|_{K}-\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\log|f|\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\log\frac{|f|}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big|+\Big|\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\log\|f\|_{K}-\log|f|\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\log\frac{|f|}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big|+\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\Big|\log\frac{|f|}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big|\,dx.

We may assume that
\mathbf{N}\neq 0
.
Now, by (
35
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\Big|\log\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big|\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{0}^{|B|}\Big|\log\frac{(f|_{B})^{*}(y)}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big|\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\log\frac{(f|_{B})^{*}(|B|\,\lambda)}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big|\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbf{N}\int_{0}^{1}-\log\Big(\frac{|B|}{|K|}\cdot\frac{1-\lambda}{C_{\mathbf{N}}d}\Big)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle=2\mathbf{N}\Big(\log\Big(\frac{C_{\mathbf{N}}d\,|K|}{|B|}\Big)+1\Big),

and (
41
) follows.
∎

Corollary

5.10
has similarity with the log-BMO property of analytic functions

[
10
, Corollary 1.10]
.
But
from (
41
) it seems not possible to deduce that
\log|f|
has bounded mean oscillation,
since the right-hand side tends to infinity if the radius of
B
tends to zero.
Also using (
41
) in the case
K=B
does not help, because then
\mathbf{N}
becomes eventually zero
if
B
gets small enough.

This is related to the fact that we do not have good enough control
away from points in
K
, where
\|f\|_{K}
is attained.
Indeed, if
x\in K
is such that
|f(x)|=\|f\|_{K}
, then

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{balls }B\subseteq K\\
x\in B\end{subarray}}\operatorname{mo}_{B}(\log|f|)\leq 4\mathbf{N}\,\big(\log(C_{\mathbf{N}}d)+1\big)

as follows from the next corollary.

Corollary 5.11
.

Let
(\mu^{|\infty},K)
be admissible data and
\mathbf{N}
the associated integer.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be any
(\mu^{|2\mathbf{N}},\|f\|_{K})
-smooth function.
Then, for each convex body
L\subseteq K
such that
\|f\|_{L}=\|f\|_{K}
,

(42)

\operatorname{mo}_{L}(\log|f|)\leq 4\mathbf{N}\,\big(\log(C_{\mathbf{N}}d)+1\big)

where the right-hand side is interpreted as
\infty
if
\mathbf{N}=0
.

Proof.

If
L\subseteq K
is a convex body such that
\|f\|_{L}=\|f\|_{K}

and
E
is a measurable subset of
L
with
|E|>0
,
then we get

\|f\|_{L}\leq\Big(\frac{C_{\mathbf{N}}\,|L|^{1/d}}{|L|^{1/d}-(|L|-|E|)^{1/d}}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}\|f\|_{E}.

from
Theorem

5.4
(in analogy to
Theorem

5.5
).
For
E:=|\{x\in L:|f(x)|\leq t\}|
we may conclude

|E|\leq C_{\mathbf{N}}d\,|L|\,\Big(\frac{t}{\|f\|_{L}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2\mathbf{N}}},\quad t>0,

(in analogy to
Corollary

5.6
)
and thus

(f|_{L})^{*}(|L|\,\lambda)\geq\|f\|_{L}\Big(\frac{1-\lambda}{C_{\mathbf{N}}d}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}},\quad\lambda\in(0,1),

(in analogy to
Corollary

5.7
). Using this estimate in the computations of the proof of
Corollary

5.10
,
yields (
42
).
∎

6.
Complementary results

In this section, we compare the
\mu^{|\infty}
-degree to the polynomial and the analytic degree, respectively.
Moreover, we discuss the existence of non-quasianalytic bump functions and
show how the technique from
Section

3
can be used to extract information on critical points.

6.1.
The
\mu^{|\infty}
-degree vs. the polynomial degree

What is the relation between the
\mu^{|\infty}
-degree and the usual degree of a polynomial?

Since most of our results are based on restriction to one-dimensional sections,
let us assume that
d=1
.
Let
p\in\mathbb{R}[t]
be a univariate polynomial of degree
n
.
We want to find upper and lower bounds in
n
for the
2\mu^{|\infty}
-degree
\mathfrak{d}_{2\mu^{|\infty}}(1)

for any suitable full admissible weight
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
such that
p:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}

is
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth.
(It is natural to take
N=\infty
, since all derivatives of high order of
p
are identically zero anyway.
The factor
2
appears, since
\delta_{[-1,1]}=2
.)

By Markov’s inequality (
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
),

(43)

\|p^{(k)}\|_{[-1,1]}\leq\frac{n^{2}(n^{2}-1^{2})(n^{2}-2^{2})\cdots(n^{2}-(k-1)^{2})}{1\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdots(2k-1)}\|p\|_{[-1,1]},\quad 1\leq k\leq n.

Thus a most natural full admissible weight
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
such that
p:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth is

(44)

M_{0}:=\|p\|_{[-1,1]},\quad\mu_{j}:=n^{2},\quad j\geq 1.

In fact,
(\mu_{j})_{j}
has to be increasing so that any admissible choice must satisfy
\mu_{j}\geq n^{2}
for all
j\geq 1
.
Making
(\mu_{j})_{j}
bigger increases also
\mathfrak{d}_{2\mu^{|\infty}}(1)
and in that way we
could make
\mathfrak{d}_{2\mu^{|\infty}}(1)
as large as we please.
It is also natural to take the second parameter equal to
1
, since
\frac{b}{M_{0}}=1
for the choice
b=\|p\|_{[-1,1]}

and
b\mapsto\mathfrak{d}_{2\mu^{|\infty}}(b)
is decreasing.

For the choice (
44
) we have

\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(1,m)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}=\frac{m}{n^{2}}

and, consequently,

\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(1)=\lfloor en^{2}\rfloor.

Note that in this case

(45)

2\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(1)\leq\mathfrak{d}_{2\mu^{|\infty}}(1)\leq 2\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(1)+1;

the first inequality is always true, because
(\mu_{j})_{j}
is increasing.

We get a better result for complex polynomials. Let
p\in\mathbb{C}[z]
be a univariate polynomial of degree
n
.
By Bernstein’s inequality (
[
3
]
,
[
28
]
),

\|p^{(k)}\|_{\overline{D}_{1}}\leq\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}\|p\|_{\overline{D}_{1}},\quad k\geq 1,

where
\overline{D}_{1}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq 1\}
is the closed unit disk.
Thus
p|_{\overline{D}_{1}}
is
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth for

M_{0}:=\|p\|_{\overline{D}_{1}},\quad\mu_{j}=Cn,\quad j\geq 1,

where the constant
C>0
accounts for the conversion of the bounds for complex derivatives to real partial derivatives.
In this case, we find

\mathfrak{d}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(1)=\lfloor Cen\rfloor,

and (
45
) is still valid.

Remark 6.1
.

Strictly speaking a
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth function is real valued by definition (see
Section

2.2
).
Its definition clearly makes sense for complex valued functions as well,
but crucial results of the paper are based on Rolle’s theorem.

6.2.
The
\mu^{|\infty}
-degree vs. the analytic degree

The
analytic degree

d_{f}(2\epsilon)
of a holomorphic function
f
on the disk
D_{1+2\epsilon}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1+2\epsilon\}
, for some
\epsilon>0
,
is defined in
[
10
]
as the best constant
d
in the inequality

\|f\|_{I}\leq\Big(\frac{4\,|I|}{|E|}\Big)^{d}\|f\|_{E},

where
I
is any interval in the intersection of a real affine line in
\mathbb{C}
with the unit disk
D_{1}
and
E\subseteq I
is any
measurable subset with
|E|>0
.

By the Cauchy estimates,

\|f^{(k)}\|_{\overline{D}_{1}}\leq\frac{k!\,\|f\|_{D_{1+\epsilon}}}{\epsilon^{k+1}},\quad k\geq 1.

So
f
on
\overline{D}_{1}
is
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth for

M_{0}:=\frac{\|f\|_{D_{1+\epsilon}}}{\epsilon},\quad\mu_{j}:=\frac{Cj}{\epsilon},\quad j\geq 1,

where the constant
C>0
accounts for the conversion of the bounds for complex derivatives to real partial derivatives.
For
\tilde{\mu}(s)=s
we have
\overline{\gamma}_{\tilde{\mu}}=1
and
\overline{\Gamma}_{\tilde{\mu}}=4e^{5}
; see
Remark

5.3
.
By
Theorem

5.1
, we find (as in the proof of
Theorem

5.4
)

\|f\|_{I}\leq\Big(\frac{4e^{5}|I|}{|E|}\Big)^{2\mathfrak{d}_{2\mu^{|\infty}}(\epsilon)}\|f\|_{E}.

Since
(4e^{5})^{2\mathfrak{d}_{2\mu^{|\infty}}(\epsilon)}\leq 4^{10\,\mathfrak{d}_{2\mu^{|\infty}}(\epsilon)}
, we conclude that

d_{f}(2\epsilon)\leq 10\,\mathfrak{d}_{2\mu^{|\infty}}(\epsilon).

We do not know if there is a lower bound for
d_{f}(2\epsilon)
in terms of
\mathfrak{d}_{2\mu^{|\infty}}(\epsilon)
.

Remark 6.2
.

For
\mu^{|\infty}=(j)_{j\geq 1}
,

\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(1,n)
is the partial harmonic series
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j}=:H_{n}
,
and, more generally,
\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(m,n)=H_{n}-H_{m-1}
if
1<m\leq n
.
Let
x\geq 1
and define the positive integer
n(x)
by

(46)

H_{n(x)}\leq x<H_{n(x)+1}.

Comtet
[
12
]
(see also Boas and Wrench
[
4
]
) showed that, for
x\geq 2
,

\Big\lfloor e^{x-\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{e^{x-1}-1}\Big\rfloor\leq n(x)\leq\Big\lfloor e^{x-\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{12}\frac{1}{e^{x-1}-1}\Big\rfloor,

where
\gamma
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant,
which determines one or two possible values of
n(x)
for any
x\geq 2
.
With this formula it is not difficult to find explicit estimates of
\mathfrak{d}_{a\mu^{|\infty}}(b)
, for
a,b>0
.
Note that the possible equality
H_{n(x)}=x
in (
46
), in contrast to the strict inequality in the definition of
\mathfrak{d}_{a\mu^{|\infty}}(1)
,
might effect a deviation of at most
1
between
n(ae)
and
\mathfrak{d}_{a\mu^{|\infty}}(1)
.

6.3.
Conditions for non-quasianalytic bump functions

Let
M^{|\infty}
be a non-quasianalytic full admissible weight.
We may infer from
Theorem

4.2

a quantitative necessary condition for
the existence of
M^{|\infty}
-smooth functions
f
with compact support contained in the interior
K^{\circ}
of the convex body
K
.
Of course, this is most informative if
K
has equal width in all directions, e.g., if
K
is a ball.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
M^{|\infty}
be a non-quasianalytic full admissible weight,
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
a convex body, and
b>0
.
Suppose that
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
is an
M^{|\infty}
-smooth function compactly supported in
K^{\circ}
and
\|f\|_{K}\geq b
.
Then

\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(j_{0}+1,\infty)\leq\delta_{K}e,\qquad(j_{0}=\lceil\log(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})^{-1}\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}).

Proof.

Theorem

4.2
shows that (
19
) must be violated.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

For completeness, we sketch a proof of the following fact:

Let
Z
be any non-empty closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
M^{|\infty}=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
a non-quasianalytic full admissible weight.
There exist
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
A>0

such that
\|f^{(\alpha)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\leq A^{|\alpha|+1}M_{|\alpha|}
, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, and

Z_{f}=Z\subseteq Z(f^{(\alpha)})
for all
\alpha\neq 0
.

By
[
25
, Lemma 2.3 and Corollary 3.5]
,
there exists a non-quasianalytic full admissible weight
L^{|\infty}=(L_{k})_{k\geq 0}
such that

(47)

\Big(\frac{M_{j}}{L_{j}}\Big)^{1/j}\to\infty.

By
[
13
, Theorem 1.4.2]
or
[
25
, Proposition 3.11]
,
there is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-function
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
with
\varphi(0)=1
and support contained in the unit ball
B_{1}
and
A>0
such that

\|\varphi^{(\alpha)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\leq A^{|\alpha|+1}L_{|\alpha|}
for all
\alpha
.
For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Z
, let
d(x):=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{z\in Z}|x-z|
and

\varphi_{x}(y):=\varphi(\frac{y-x}{d(x)})
. Then

\varphi_{x}(x)=1
,
U_{x}:=\{y:\varphi_{x}(y)\neq 0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Z
, and

\|\varphi_{x}^{(\alpha)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=\frac{1}{d(x)^{|\alpha|}}\|\varphi^{(\alpha)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\leq\frac{A^{|\alpha|+1}}{d(x)^{|\alpha|}}L_{|\alpha|}.

The family
\{U_{x}\}
forms an
open cover of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Z

which admits a countable subcover
\{U_{n}:=U_{x_{n}}\}
.
Let
\varphi_{n}:=\varphi_{x_{n}}
,
d_{n}:=1/d(x_{n})
, and
choose constants
s_{n}>0
such that

d_{n}^{j}s_{n}\leq\frac{M_{j}}{L_{j}}\quad\text{ for all }n,j\geq 1.

This is possible by (
47
), since for each
d_{n}
we find
j_{n}
such that
d_{n}\leq\big(\frac{M_{j}}{L_{j}}\big)^{1/j}
for all
j>j_{n}
;
so we may take

s_{n}:=\min\Big\{\min_{j\leq j_{n}}\frac{M_{j}}{d_{n}^{j}L_{j}},1\Big\}.

Then
f:=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{s_{n}}{2^{n}}\varphi_{n}
converges uniformly in all derivatives,

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{s_{n}}{2^{n}}\varphi_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{s_{n}}{2^{n}}\cdot A^{|\alpha|+1}d_{n}^{|\alpha|}L_{|\alpha|}\leq A^{|\alpha|+1}M_{|\alpha|},

so that
f
defines a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
f^{(\alpha)}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{s_{n}}{2^{n}}\varphi_{n}^{(\alpha)}

and
\|f^{(\alpha)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\leq A^{|\alpha|+1}M_{|\alpha|}
for all
\alpha
.
Since the
U_{n}
cover
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Z
,

f
is strictly positive on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Z
and
vanishes, together with all partial derivatives
f^{(\alpha)}
, on
Z
.

6.4.
Critical points

Let
M^{|\infty}
be a full admissible weight and

f:K\to\mathbb{R}
an
M^{|\infty}
-smooth function, where
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a convex body.
In order to get quantitative information on the critical points of
f
,
one can consider the function
g:=|\nabla f|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(\partial_{j}f)^{2}

and apply the results of
Section

4
to
g
.
Note that
g
is
\hat{M}^{|\infty}
-smooth for a full admissible weight

\hat{M}^{|\infty}
which can be computed from
M^{|\infty}

in view of the Faà di Bruno formula.

But the one-dimensional analysis from
Section

3

allows us to extract information in a more direct way.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
M^{|\infty}=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
be a full admissible weight,
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
a convex body, and
b>0

such that

(48)

\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(j_{0}+1,\infty)>2\delta_{K}e,\qquad(j_{0}=\lceil\log(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})^{-1}\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}).

Then
\mathbf{N}:=\mathfrak{d}_{2\delta_{K}\mu^{|\infty}}(\tfrac{b}{2M_{0}})
is a nonnegative integer.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be any
M^{|\mathbf{N}+1}
-smooth function such that
\|f\|_{K}\geq b
.
Let
\ell
be any affine line that meets
B
(i.e. the ball contained in
\{x\in K:|f(x)|>\frac{b}{2}\}
from
Theorem

4.2
)
such that either
\ell\cap Z_{f}\neq\emptyset
or

V(f|_{\ell})\geq 2b
.
Then
f
has at most
2\mathbf{N}
critical points on
\ell
.
Thus at all but possibly
2\mathbf{N}
points of
\ell
the level sets of
f
are
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{N}+1}
-submanifolds.

Notice the factor
2
in (
48
) and in the definition of
\mathbf{N}
.

Proof.

Let
\ell
be an affine line that meets
B
.
If no zero of
f
lies on
\ell
, we may assume without loss of generality that

f
is positive on
\ell
and that

c:=\min_{\ell\cap K}f>0
.
The assumption

V(f|_{\ell})\geq 2b
guarantees that we can
replace
f
by
f-c
; clearly the set of critical points of
f
and
f-c
is the same.
Indeed, cf.
Remark

4.11
,

b\leq\frac{V(f|_{\ell})}{2}\leq\|f-c\|_{\ell\cap K}=\max_{\ell\cap K}f-c\leq M_{0}-c\leq M_{0},

and
\|f-c\|_{j,K}=\|f\|_{j,K}
for all
j\geq 1
.
Since
f-c
has a zero on
\ell
, we may assume
from now on that
f
has a zero on
\ell
.

Now we restrict
f
to
\ell
and work with the
(\delta_{K}\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1},M_{0})
-function
g:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}

as in the proof of

Theorem

4.2
.
Since
\ell
meets
B
, we have
\|g\|_{[0,1]}>\frac{b}{2}
.
And
g
has at least one zero
s_{0}
in
[0,1]
, because
f
vanishes on
\ell
.
As in
Theorem

4.2
,
we see that the number of critical points of
g
left and right of
s_{0}
is finite and bounded by
\mathbf{N}
.
Since each critical point of
f
on
\ell
is a critical point of
g
, the proof is complete.

Let us explain in more detail why we need the factor
2
in the definition of
\mathbf{N}
.
To this end, assume that
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\cdots\leq t_{m_{r}}
is an increasing enumeration of the critical points of
g
that lie to the right of
s_{0}
.
By Rolle’s theorem, we find
t_{1}=s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq\cdots\leq s_{m_{r}-1}
such that
g^{(j)}(s_{j})=0
for
0\leq j\leq m_{r}-1
.
Choose
s_{-1}\in[0,1]
such that
|g(s_{-1})|>\frac{b}{2}
.
If
s_{-1}<s_{0}
, we find, as in the proof of
Corollary

3.4
,

1\geq\sum_{j=0}^{m_{r}-1}|s_{j}-s_{j-1}|>\frac{1}{e}\Sigma_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\infty}}(j_{0}+1,m_{r}),

whence
m_{r}\leq\mathfrak{d}_{\delta_{K}\mu^{|\infty}}(\frac{b}{2M_{0}})
.
If
s_{-1}>s_{0}
, then

\sum_{j=0}^{m_{r}-1}|s_{j}-s_{j-1}|=(s_{-1}-s_{0})+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{r}-1}(s_{j}-s_{j-1})\leq 2

so that we may conclude that
m_{r}\leq\mathfrak{d}_{2\delta_{K}\mu^{|\infty}}(\frac{b}{2M_{0}})
.
In any case
m_{r}\leq\mathbf{N}
.
∎

Concentration and fluctuation phenomena in the localized phase of the pinning model
Giambattista Giacomin, Marco Zamparo

Abstract
We focus on the localized phase of pinning models with i.i.d. site disorder on which we assume only that the moment generating function is bounded in a neighborhood of the origin. We develop quantitative correlation functions estimates for local observables that entail quantitative C ∞ C^{\infty} estimates on the free energy density, showing in particular that its regularity class is at least Gevrey-3 in the whole localized phase. We then explain how a quenched concentration bound and the quenched Central Limit Theorem (CLT) on the number of the pinned sites, i.e., the contact number , can be extracted from the regularity estimates on the free energy: this identifies the thermal fluctuations of the contact number. But the centering sequence in the quenched CLT is random in the sense that it is disorder dependent: we show that the (disorder induced) fluctuations of the centering are on the same scale of the thermal fluctuations by establishing a CLT, with a non degenerate variance, also for the centering. For what concerns the correlation and C ∞ C^{\infty} estimates, our work substantially generalizes and expands the analysis in [ 16 ] that dealt with pinning models with restrictive conditions on the disorder distributions and in which less explicit, non uniform bounds were obtained.

The pinning model and its localized phase

1.1
The pinning model

Set
\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and let

T_{1},T_{2},\cdots
be i.i.d. random variables on a probability
space
(\mathcal{S},\mathfrak{S},\mathsf{P})
valued in

\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Put
S_{0}:=0
and
S_{i}:=T_{1}+\cdots+T_{i}
for

i\in\mathbb{N}
. Regarding
S_{0},S_{1},\ldots
as renewal times with
inter-arrival times
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots
, the number of renewals by
the time
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is

L_{n}:=\sup\big\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}~:~S_{i}\leq n\big\}

and we denote by
S
the renewal set
\{S_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

Given a parameter
h\in\mathbb{R}
and a real sequence

\omega:=\{\omega_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, that we call
charges
and
that model impurities or disorder present in the system, for every

n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we introduce the
pinning model

\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}
defined by the Gibbs change of measure

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}}{\mathrm{d}\mathsf{P}}:=\frac{1}{Z_{n,h}(\omega)}\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{i=1}^{L_{n}}(h+\omega_{S_{i}})}\mathds{1}_{\{n\in S\}}\,,

provided that the
partition function

Z_{n,h}(\omega):=\mathsf{E}[\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{i=1}^{L_{n}}(h+\omega_{S_{i}})}\mathds{1}_{\{n\in S\}}]
is not 0. Here and in what follows we make the usual
convention that empty sums are 0 and empty products are 1. We refer
to
[
12
,
13
,
20
,
29
]
for an explanation of
the origin and relevance of pinning models in applied sciences,
notably physics and biology. In short, we just point out that the
renewal set is interpreted as the ensemble of points, the

contacts
or
pinned sites
, at which a polymer of
n

units, an interface, or another linear structure enters in contact
with a
defect line
, receiving a penalty at the contact point

a
if
h+\omega_{a}<0
or a reward if
h+\omega_{a}>0
. The size of the
contact set
L_{n}
is henceforth referred to as
contact
number
.

The assumptions on the law of inter-arrival times and the charges are
the following.

Assumption 1.1
.

p(t):=\mathsf{P}[T_{1}=t]={\ell(t)}/{t^{\alpha+1}}
for
t\in\mathbb{N}
with

\alpha\geq 0
and a slowly varying function at infinity

\ell
. Moreover,
p(t)>0
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

We recall that a real measurable function
\ell
, defined on

[1,+\infty)
in our case, is slowly varying at infinity if

\ell(z)>0
for all sufficiently large
z
and
\lim_{z\uparrow+\infty}\ell(\lambda z)/\ell(z)=1
for every
\lambda>0

[
4
]
. Besides this, we require that
p(t)>0
, i.e.,

\ell(t)>0
, for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
, which in particular guarantees that

Z_{n,h}(\omega)>0
for every
n
and every
\omega
. We believe
that there is no substantial difficulty in dropping this further
hypothesis, thus generalizing our results to the case in which

p(t)>0
only for
t
sufficiently large. This however would add
one more step of complexity to arguments that are already rather
technical. We remark also that we do not require that
\sum_{t\in\mathbb{N}}p(t)=1-\mathsf{P}[T_{1}=\infty]=1
, so the law
p(\cdot)
is a
sub-probability on
\mathbb{N}
, but this is just a presentation choice
because it is well known that there is no loss of generality in
assuming
\mathsf{P}[T_{1}=\infty]=0
(see, e.g.,
[
13
, pages
14-15]
).

Assumption 1.2
.

The disorder sequence
\omega:=\{\omega_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is sampled
from a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
in such a way
that the canonical projections
\omega\mapsto\omega_{a}
form a
sequence of i.i.d. random variables. Furthermore,

\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{\eta|\omega_{0}|}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]<+\infty
for some
number
\eta>0
and
\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]=0
.

We say that the model is
pure
or
non disordered
if

\mathbb{P}[\{\omega=0\}]=1
, which is tantamount to the condition

\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}^{2}\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]=0
. In this case the model
is explicitly solvable (see
[
12
,
13
,
20
]
).

Under Assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
, a
superadditivity argument shows that the limit defining the
free
energy density
(we will simply say
free energy
henceforth)

f(h):=\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n,h}\bigg]

exists and is finite for all
h\in\mathbb{R}
(see
[
12
, Theorem
4.6]
), thus introducing a non-decreasing Lipschitz
convex function
f
over
\mathbb{R}
. The bounds

Z_{n,h}(\omega)\geq\mathrm{e}^{h+\omega_{n}}p(n)
and

Z_{n,h}(\omega)\geq\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{a=1}^{n}(h+\omega_{a})}p(1)^{n}
give
f(h)\geq\max\{0,h+\log p(1)\}
. Putting
h_{c}:=\inf\{h\in\mathbb{R}:f(h)>0\}
, we
deduce that
-\infty\leq h_{c}<+\infty
and, when
h_{c}>-\infty
, that

\lim_{h\downarrow h_{c}}f(h)=0
and
f(h)=0
for
h\leq h_{c}
. The bound

Z_{n,h}(\omega)\leq\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{a=1}^{n}\max\{0,h+\omega_{a}\}}
yields

f(h)\leq\int_{\Omega}\max\{0,h+\omega_{0}\}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]
, which
shows that
\lim_{h\downarrow h_{c}}f(h)=0
even when
h_{c}=-\infty
. We
say that the model is

•

delocalized
, or that it is in the
delocalized phase
, if
h<h_{c}
(when
h_{c}>-\infty
);

•

critical
if
h=h_{c}
(when
h_{c}>-\infty
);

•

localized
, or that it is in the
localized phase
, if
h>h_{c}
.

This terminology is easily justified by exploiting the convexity
properties of the free energy that, together with the definition of

h_{c}
, readily yield that the mean contact number

\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]=\partial_{h}\log Z_{n,h}
satisfies

\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]]/n=0
for
h<h_{c}

(when
h_{c}>-\infty
) and

\liminf_{n\uparrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]]/n>0
for
h>h_{c}
.
Convexity also assures that the inferior limit is a limit except
possibly for a countable set of values of
h
: we will see that a
byproduct of our analysis is that it is a limit in full generality. We
refer to
[
12
,
13
,
20
,
29
]
for much more
precise results and extensive discussions, including several open
questions (for the delocalized phase we add
[
3
]
and
references therein).

The results we propose here represent a progress in the understanding
of the localized phase of the pinning model. We underline that much
of the existing literature focuses on the case in which

\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{z|\omega_{0}|}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]<+\infty
for every

z>0
, and this implies that
h_{c}>-\infty
. In fact, it suffices that

c:=\log\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{0}}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]<+\infty
to have

h_{c}\geq-c>-\infty
because of the annealed bound, which is Jensen’s
inequality:

\mathbb{E}\big[\log Z_{n,h}\big]\leq\log\mathbb{E}\big[Z_{n,h}\big]=\log\mathsf{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{(h+c)L_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\{n\in S\}}\right]\leq 0

(1)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
h\leq-c
. Next is a more general, almost optimal, result for
deciding whether
h_{c}>-\infty
or
h_{c}=-\infty
. We set

\varrho:=\sup\bigg\{z\geq 0\,:\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{z\omega_{0}}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]<+\infty\bigg\}\in[\eta,+\infty]\,,

where
\eta>0
is introduced in Assumption
1.2
.

Proposition 1.1
.

If
(\alpha+1)\varrho>1
, then
h_{c}>-\infty
. If

(\alpha+1)\varrho<1
, then
h_{c}=-\infty
.

The first part of Proposition
1.1
follows from the basic
fractional moment bound
[
28
]
, while the second part is
obtained via a lower bound on the partition function by restricting to
the renewal trajectory that makes contacts at the points
a
such that

\omega_{a}>\lambda
with a suitably large number
\lambda
(see,
e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 3.18]
). One can tackle the case

(\alpha+1)\varrho=1
and decide whether
h_{c}>-\infty
or not in a
number of cases, but we are not aware of a necessary and sufficient
condition when
(\alpha+1)\varrho=1
.

We remark that in
[
23
]
the analysis of a class of pinning
models with heavy-tailed disorder law is considered, addressing the
issue of disorder relevance, i.e., the effect of disorder at
criticality (see, e.g.,
[
13
, Chapter 4]
for the notion of
disorder relevance). Sharper conditions of the tail behaviour of the
charges are imposed with respect to Assumption
1.2
, but
in the language of Proposition
1.1
we see that in

[
23
]
it is assumed that
\varrho>1
. Therefore, in
particular,
[
23
]
is restricted to the case in which

h_{c}>-\infty
, and this follows directly from (
1
)
without the need of Proposition
1.1
.

1.2
The main results

Most of our results rely, directly or indirectly, on correlation
estimates that generalize analogous results proved in

[
16
]
. In spite of the fact that they are central to
our analysis and that ultimately they can be resumed by saying that

in the localized phase correlations decay exponentially fast
,
they are rather technical and we refer the reader directly to
Section
3
. Here we focus on the following consequences,
where of course requiring a result to hold for every
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
closed (respectively, compact) is equivalent to
requiring it for every
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
with
\inf H>h_{c}

(respectively, with
\inf H>h_{c}
and
\sup H<+\infty
).

Theorem 1.1
.

The free energy
f
is strictly convex and infinitely
differentiable on
(h_{c},+\infty)
, and the following property holds
for
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
: for every compact set
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
and

r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\adjustlimits{\lim}_{n\uparrow\infty}{\sup}_{h\in H}\bigg|\frac{1}{n}\partial^{r}_{h}\log Z_{n,h}(\omega)-\partial_{h}^{r}f(h)\bigg|=\adjustlimits{\lim}_{n\uparrow\infty}{\sup}_{h\in H}\bigg|\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{n}\partial^{r}_{h}\log Z_{n,h}\bigg]-\partial_{h}^{r}f(h)\bigg|=0\,.

Moreover, for every closed set
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
there exists a
constant
c>0
such that for all
r\in\mathbb{N}

\sup_{h\in H}\Big|\partial_{h}^{r}f(h)\Big|\leq c^{r}(r!)^{3}\,.

Theorem
1.1
provides quantitative bounds to the
derivatives of the free energy
f
for the first time, showing in
particular that it is of class Gevrey-3 on
(h_{c},+\infty)
, which
means that for every
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
compact there exists a
constant
c>0
such that
\sup_{h\in H}|\partial_{h}^{r}f(h)|\leq c^{r}(r!)^{3}
for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
. This theorem generalizes
[
16
, Theorem
2.1]
because of the weaker assumptions on the
charges, in fact we do not require that they satisfy some
concentration inequality, and expands it in two respects: a Gevrey
class to which the free energy belongs is identified and convergence
of the derivatives of the finite-volume free energy for typical
realizations of the charges is demonstrated.

Besides, we stress that Theorem
1.1
, like all the results
that follow, is uniform with respect to the free parameter
h
: we
identify a set of charges of full probability for which the results
hold for every
h>h_{c}
.

Remark 1.1
.

In
[
22
]
it is claimed that the pinning model
exhibits a Griffiths singularity in the localized phase,
i.e., a singularity originally discovered in dilute
Ising models where the free energy is
C^{\infty}
but
non-analytic. Griffiths singularities are due to rare but
arbitrarily large atypical region in the environment: imagine a
pinning model in which the charges
\omega_{a}
take only the
values
-1
and
1
with equal probability. Then it is
straightforward to see that the model is localized for
h
equal
to the critical value
h_{-}:=1-\log\mathsf{P}[T_{1}<\infty]
of the homogeneous model in which

\omega_{a}=-1
for every
a
. But in infinite volume one can find
arbitrarily large regions in which the charges are all equal to

-1
: it is therefore believable that these critical regions,
albeit finite, may impact the analyticity of the free energy at

h=h_{-}
. Mathematically, this is an open problem for the
pinning model and for most disordered models, because it turns
out to be very difficult, outside of dilute systems, to decouple
these rare regions from the rest of the system. In

[
14
]
, motivated by the arguments in

[
22
]
, a toy pinning model is introduced and studied:
notably for
\alpha=1/2
this model exhibits a Griffiths
singularity whose precise behaviour is established, in particular
the free energy is in the Gevrey-(3/2) class. In spite of not being
completely implausible that the toy model result holds for the true
model, establishing this appears to be very challenging and how the
Gevrey class depends on the exponent
\alpha
remains elusive even
in the toy model.

We will then show that from Theorem
1.1
one can extract a
quenched concentration bound and a quenched Central Limit Theorem
(CLT) for the contact number in the localized phase, i.e., a
concentration bound and a CLT for the contact number in the localized
phase conditional on a typical realization of the charges. The first
two derivatives of the free energy in the localized phase, which are
the functions
\rho:=\partial_{h}f
and
v:=\partial_{h}^{2}f
on

(h_{c},+\infty)
, play a special role in what follows. We shall refer
to
\rho
as the
contact density
since, recalling that

\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}]=\partial_{h}\log Z_{n,h}(\omega)
,
Theorem
1.1
shows that the following holds for
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
: for
every
h>h_{c}

\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\frac{L_{n}}{n}\bigg]=\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}\bigg[\frac{L_{n}}{n}\bigg]\Bigg]=\partial_{h}f(h)=:\rho(h)\,,

(2)

confirming in addition that the mean contact fraction

\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}/n]]
has a limit when
n
goes to
infinity. Theorem
1.1
also states that
v
is the scaled
limiting variance of
L_{n}
in the localized phase. In fact, as

\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[(L_{n}-\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}])^{2}]=\partial_{h}^{2}\log Z_{n,h}
, Theorem
1.1
implies that also the following is
valid for
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
: for every
h>h_{c}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{L_{n}-\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}]}{\sqrt{n}}\bigg)^{\!\!2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad=\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{L_{n}-\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]}{\sqrt{n}}\bigg)^{\!\!2}\bigg]\Bigg]=\partial_{h}^{2}f(h)=:v(h)\,.

(3)

We stress that
\rho(h)>0
and
v(h)>0
for all
h>h_{c}
, the former
being the hallmark of the localized phase, as seen in the previous
section, and the latter being an expression of the strict convexity
property of
f
.

Theorem 1.2
.

The following properties hold for
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
:

(i)

for every closed set
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
there exists a
constant
\kappa_{\omega}>0
such that for all
u\geq 0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}

\sup_{h\in H}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\big|L_{n}-\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}]\big|>u\Big]\leq 2\exp\left\{-\kappa_{\omega}\frac{u^{2}}{n+u^{5/3}}\right\};

(ii)

for every compact set
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)

\adjustlimits{\limsmallspace}_{n\uparrow\infty}{\sup}_{h\in H}\,\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\,\Bigg|\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\frac{L_{n}-\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}]}{\sqrt{nv(h)}}\leq u\bigg]-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{u}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}z^{2}}\mathrm{d}z\Bigg|=0

with
v(h):=\partial_{h}^{2}f(h)>0
.

Theorem
1.2
involves the random centering

\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]
. It is therefore natural to address the size of
this centering: as a matter of fact, without control on the centering
the CLT turns out to be void of interest. To this aim we propose for
the centering variable a concentration bound, a control on the
expectation, existence of a limiting variance, and the CLT.

Theorem 1.3
.

The following conclusions hold:

(i)

for every closed set
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
there exists a
constant
\kappa>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
u\geq 0

\sup_{h\in H}\mathbb{P}\bigg[\Big|\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]-\mathbb{E}\big[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]\big]\Big|>u\bigg]\leq 2\exp\left\{-\kappa\frac{u^{2}}{n+u^{5/3}}\right\}\,;

(ii)

for every
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
closed there exists a
constant
c>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\sup_{h\in H}\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]\big]-\rho(h)n\Big|\leq c

with
\rho(h):=\partial_{h}f(h)
;

(iii)

there exists a locally Lipschitz continuous function
w

on
(h_{c},+\infty)
such that for every compact set
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)

\adjustlimits{\limsmallspace}_{n\uparrow\infty}{\sup}_{h\in H}\Bigg|\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg(\frac{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]-\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]]}{\sqrt{n}}\bigg)^{\!\!2}\Bigg]-w(h)\Bigg|=0\,;

(iv)

if
\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}^{2}\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]>0
, then
w(h)>0

for all
h>h_{c}
and for every
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
compact

\adjustlimits{\limsmallspace}_{n\uparrow\infty}{\sup}_{h\in H}\,\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\,\Bigg|\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]-\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]]}{\sqrt{nw(h)}}\leq u\bigg]-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{u}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}z^{2}}\mathrm{d}z\Bigg|=0\,.

We highlight the very different nature of the CLTs in Theorems

1.2
and
1.3
: the fluctuations of
the contact number for a typical environment are thermal, whereas the
fluctuations of the centering are due to the disorder. In the absence
of disorder the random centering
\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]
is of course
constant, so parts (i) and (iii) of Theorem
1.3
are
trivial, as well as Proposition
1.2
below. On
the other hand, as soon as the model is disordered, Theorem

1.3
assures among others that the limiting distribution
of the random centering is non degenerate, i.e.,
w(h)>0
for
h>h_{c}
.
The analysis of the variance of
\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]
and
establishing the positivity of
w(h)
in the presence of disorder,
which are important preliminary results in view of formulating the
CLT, require a specific and demanding study. In particular, the
positivity of
w(h)
can be established in a rather straightforward
way when the charges are Gaussian variables via integration by parts,
but leaving the Gaussian framework is not at all straightforward.

Resorting to the concentration bound stated by Theorem

1.3
, we are also able to provide an upper bound for the
magnitude of the fluctuations of the centering for typical
realizations of the charges, in the spirit of Hardy–Littlewood
estimates for random walks
[
21
]
.

Proposition 1.2
.

The following property holds for
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
: for every compact set

H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
there exists a constant
c>0
such that for
all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\sup_{h\in H}\frac{|\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}]-\rho(h)n|}{\sqrt{n\log n}}\leq c\,.

We complete the presentation of our results by an aspect that may
appear at first more specific and less central than the previous ones:
the definition of an
alternative free energy

\mu(h)
. This
impression, which is due to the fact that we have decided not to
present the decay of correlation estimates in the introduction, is
however false because they are the crucial building block of our
analysis and, in turn, their exponential decay relies on the strict
positivity of
\mu(h)
. As already explained in

[
2
,
16
]
,
\mu(h)
is another natural free energy
associated to our model, and we let the following statement introduce
it, where we use

Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}(\omega):=Z_{n,h}(\omega)\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{n}}
.

Proposition 1.3
.

For every
h\in\mathbb{R}
the limit

-\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}}\bigg]=:\mu(h)

(4)

exists and defines a Lipschitz function
\mu
with Lipschitz
constant equal to
1
. Moreover, there exists a constant
c>0
such
that
c\min\{f(h),f(h)^{2}\}\leq\mu(h)\leq f(h)
for all
h
. Hence,

\mu(h)=0
for
h\leq h_{c}
(when
h_{c}>-\infty
) and
\mu(h)>0
for

h>h_{c}
.

A direct consequence of the bounds relating
\mu(h)
and
f(h)
in
Proposition
1.3
is that the function
\mu
is equivalent to

f
as far as detecting the phase transition is concerned, but whether
their behaviour approaching criticality is the same or not is an open
problem when disorder is relevant (see
[
18
]
,
notably Theorem 2.4). This
point is further discussed in
[
7
]
, and in

[
16
,
18
,
7
]
it is also explained why
both
1/\mu(h)
and
1/f(h)
are two natural correlation lengths for
the system: in fact,
\mu(h)
directly enters the correlation bounds
we give and use starting from the next section. One of the important
properties of
\mu(h)
is that it determines the maximal polymer
excursion
M_{n}:=\max\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{L_{n}}\}
in the localized phase as
follows.

Proposition 1.4
.

The following property holds for
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
: for every
h>h_{c}
the sequence

\bigg\{\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}\bigg\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

converges in probability, with respect to the law

\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}
, to
1/\mu(h)
.

Propositions
1.3
and
1.4
generalize
and improve part (1) and part (2), respectively, of
[
16
, Theorem
2.5]
. While Proposition
1.3
generalizes
part (1) of this theorem only for the weaker assumptions on the
charges (see however Remark
1.2
below),
Proposition
1.4
improves part (2) also
because it establishes convergence in probability for almost all the
realizations of the disorder: in fact, the upper bound on the size of
the maximal excursion in
[
16
]
is obtained only in
probability with respect to the disorder. Finally, we note that the
proof of Proposition
1.4
in Section

3.1
yields a result that is uniform in
h

belonging to any compact set
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
, but we have
chosen to present here only the weaker result for readability.

Remark 1.2
.

The definition of
\mu(h)
in
[
2
,
16
]
is
rather
-\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}(1/n)\log\mathbb{E}[1/Z_{n,h}]
. The
two definitions are equivalent if and only if
\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega_{0}}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]<+\infty
, otherwise
\mathbb{E}\left[1/{Z_{n,h}}\right]=+\infty
. For several reasons, see in
particular Corollary
2.1
, considering
\mathbb{E}[1/Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}]
is more natural as well as
necessary if
\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega_{0}}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]=+\infty
. We point out that the superadditivity argument in

[
2
,
16
]
for the existence of
\mu(h)
does
not work when
\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega_{0}}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]=+\infty
and needs to be modified.

1.3
Further comments on the results and comparisons with
[
16
]

Theorem
1.1
generalizes and expands
[
16
, Theorem
2.1]
, while Propositions
1.3
and

1.4
generalize and improve analogous results
stated in
[
16
, Theorem 2.5]
as already pointed
out. On the other hand, Theorems
1.2
and

1.3
are new, as well as Proposition

1.2
. We list here a number of comments about
our findings:

1.

In comparison to
[
16
]
, our hypotheses on disorder are
substantially weaker and completely explicit. In fact, in

[
16
]
it is asked that disorder has the property that
Lipschitz convex functions of the charges satisfy a suitable
concentration inequality. This property is used to show that

\mu(h)>0
for
h>h_{c}
by means of a concentration bound for the
finite-volume free energy. In particular, the concentration
inequality assumed in
[
16
]
requires (super-)Gaussian
tail decay of the charge distribution. Here we just rely on Assumption

1.2
: the boundedness of some exponential moments of the
disorder variable. We achieve a concentration bound for the
finite-volume free energy via a version of the McDiarmid’s inequality
for functions of independent random variables
[
24
]
. In

[
33
]
concentration for the finite-volume free energy has
been achieved with a generalization of Hoeffding’s inequality and
requiring exponential boundedness for
\eta=1
in the language of
Assumption
1.2
.

2.

The correlation estimates in Section
3
are the
technical basis of our results. They build on the ideas in

[
11
,
16
]
:
[
11
]
deals with
disordered Ising models in high temperature or strong external field
regime and exploits cluster expansion estimates, while

[
16
]
deals with pinning and copolymer models till
the critical point, i.e., in the whole localized regime. We
generalize and make more explicit the estimates in

[
16
]
to accommodate the more general set up and to
achieve new results. In particular, with respect to

[
16
]
, the estimates are improved so that they are
amenable to be used in a Birkhoff–sum approach, to which we resort
here to prove the almost sure results with respect to disorder.
Furthermore, they are also improved in the direction of stronger
mixing estimates, like those in Lemma
3.4
and Corollary

3.1
, that are needed for establishing the CLT for the
centering variable.

The mentioned Birkhoff–sum approach, which we use starting from
Section
3.1
, turns out to be a powerful
tool: in particular, it is crucial in establishing almost sure
convergence of the free-energy derivatives in Theorem
1.1
,
which in turn is the basis of the quenched CLT for the contact
number, and in upgrading
[
16
, Theorem 2.5]
to a
full almost sure result in Proposition
1.4
.
We believe that the applications of such approach are not restricted
to the results of this paper and that it could provide further
insight into the localized phase of pinning models in future
works. Moreover, the Birkhoff–sum approach might be of interest
also for the study of pinning models subjected to correlated
disorder with good mixing properties (see
[
8
]
and
references therein).

3.

We stress once more that we have taken care of providing almost
sure results that are uniform in the parameter
h
: by this we mean
that we have identified a set of charges of full probability that
works for every
h>h_{c}
. This is another byproduct of the
Birkhoff–sum approach. The pinning model is usually presented as a
model with two free parameters

[
12
,
13
,
20
,
29
]
:
h
and an amplitude

\beta\geq 0
of the charges, so
\omega_{a}
enters the model via

\beta\omega_{a}
, and then one fixes
\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}^{2}\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]=1
. In this framework, the localized phase is
a region of the parameter plane where the free energy is strictly
positive. For the sake of simplifying the formulas, we have decided
to set
\beta=1
, but we have not fixed the variance of the
charges. There is therefore no loss of generality except that one
may wonder about the uniformity of the results with respect to both

h
and
\beta
together: our approach generalizes in a
straightforward way to obtain such a double uniformity.

4.

Compared to
[
16
, Theorem 2.1]
and to the
results in
[
11
]
, our estimates in Theorem

1.1
control the dependence on the bounds on (all) the
derivatives of the free energy in such a way that a Gevrey class to
which the free energy belongs is identified. The Gevrey class we
find is the same for all the models we consider. Obtaining the
optimal Gevrey class, probably dependent on details of the model
such as the regular variation class of the inter-arrival law and the
regularity properties of the law of the disorder, is a particular
case of the very challenging issue of understanding Griffiths
singularities.

5.

We prove a quenched CLT for the key observable of pinning
models, i.e., the contact number. Disorder induces a number of
technical difficulties, but also a substantial one: since we want to
prove the results for typical realizations of the environment, the
centering constant
\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}]
depends on the
realization
\omega
of the environment. A full result includes
therefore a control on the behaviour of
\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}]
and,
a posteriori, one realizes that thermal and disorder fluctuations
are of the same order, so neither one can be neglected. For this
reason we establish a CLT also for this variable, as well as a
Hardy–Littlewood type-estimate, and we show that the limiting
distribution is degenerate only in the absence of disorder. Non
degeneracy can be demonstrated in a rather straightforward way when
disorder is Gaussian (via integration by parts), but our argument
controls general disorder laws.

6.

In the forthcoming work
[
19
]
we are going to
exploit the estimates we develop here, notably the quenched CLT for
the contact number, to study the pinning model conditioned on the
number of pinned sites. The aim is to obtain sharp results on the
effect of disorder on the so called
big-jump phenomenon
that
has been widely studied in the absence of disorder (see

[
15
,
30
,
31
]
and references therein). A
central role in
[
19
]
is played by the fact that
disorder smooths the pinning transition and first order transitions
do not exist anymore in disorder pinning models

[
6
,
17
]
: this is in strict analogy with
the smoothing phenomenon in the Ising model
[
1
,
9
]

with the notable difference that in pinning models one does know
that the model still has a phase transition. With respect to these
aspects we point out that a pinning model can be rewritten in binary
Ising like variables, with many body interactions of all orders, as
it is taken up and discussed in detail in
[
19
]
.

Organization of the rest of the paper

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section

1.3
. Section
3
presents some
correlation estimates, a first application of which is the proof of
Proposition
1.4
. In Section

4

1.2
. Finally, in Section
5

1.2
, with particular attention to the

1.3
and Proposition

1.2
.

Some basic facts

2.1
Conditional independence in the pinning model

The renewal nature of the pinning model implies a conditional
independence between consecutive stretches of the polymer. We state
this fundamental property in terms of the random variables

X_{a}:=\mathds{1}_{\{a\in S\}}
for
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, which
take value 1 at the contact sites and value 0 at the other
sites. Recall that
S:=\{S_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and note that
X_{0}=1
as

S_{0}:=0
, which can be interpreted by saying that there is a contact
on the left of any site
a\in\mathbb{N}
. Also note that the model can be
recast as

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}}{\mathrm{d}\mathsf{P}}=\frac{1}{Z_{n,h}(\omega)}\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{a=1}^{n}(h+\omega_{a})X_{a}}X_{n}

with partition functions

Z_{n,h}(\omega)=\mathsf{E}[\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{a=1}^{n}(h+\omega_{a})X_{a}}X_{n}]
and

Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}(\omega):=Z_{n,h}(\omega)\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{n}}=\mathsf{E}[\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}(h+\omega_{a})X_{a}}X_{n}]
. In
this setting the contact number reads
L_{n}=\sum_{a=1}^{n}X_{a}
.

The following lemma introduces the mentioned conditional independence,
whose standard proof is omitted (see, e.g.,
[
32
, Proposition
3.1]
). Let
\mathcal{M}_{n}
be the set of functions

\phi:\{0,1\}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
|\phi(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})|\leq 1
for
all
x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}\in\{0,1\}
and denote by
\vartheta
the left
shift that maps the sequence

\omega:=\{\omega_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\Omega
to

\vartheta\omega:=\{\omega_{a+1}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
h\in\mathbb{R}
,
\omega\in\Omega
, integers
0\leq a\leq n
, and
functions
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{a}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{n-a}

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a})\psi(X_{a},\ldots,X_{n})\Big|X_{a}=1\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathsf{E}_{a,h,\omega}\big[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a})\big]\mathsf{E}_{n-a,h,\vartheta^{a}\omega}\big[\psi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{n-a})\big]\,.

In particular, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a})\psi(X_{a},\ldots,X_{n})\Big|X_{a}=1\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a})\big|X_{a}=1\big]\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\psi(X_{a},\ldots,X_{n})\big|X_{a}=1\big]\,.

Remark 2.1
.

We will often refer to Lemma
2.1
using the
following equivalent unconditional versions:

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a})X_{a}\psi(X_{a},\ldots,X_{n})\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a})X_{a}\big]\mathsf{E}_{n-a,h,\vartheta^{a}\omega}\big[\psi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{n-a})\big]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathsf{E}_{a,h,\omega}\big[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a})\big]\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[X_{a}\psi(X_{a},\ldots,X_{n})\big]\,.

Lemma
2.1
is at the basis of a technical estimate that we
use all over the paper to compare configurations where a site is not a
contact with configurations where that site becomes a contact. In
order to present such estimate in the next lemma, we observe that the
properties of slowly varying functions (see
[
4
, Theorem 1.2.1 and
Proposition 1.3.6]
) imply that there exists a constant

\xi>0
such that for all
t,\tau\in\mathbb{N}

\frac{p(t+\tau)}{p(t)p(\tau)}\leq\xi\min\{t^{\xi},\tau^{\xi}\}\,.

(5)

Recall our convention that empty sums are equal to 0 and empty
products are equal to 1.

Lemma 2.2
.

For every
h\in\mathbb{R}
,
\omega:=\{\omega_{b}\}_{b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\Omega
,
integers
0<a<n
, and non-negative real functions

\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{a-1}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{n-a-1}

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a-1})(1-X_{a})\psi(X_{a+1},\ldots,X_{n})\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{a}}\min\big\{a^{\xi},(n-a)^{\xi}\big\}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a-1})X_{a}\psi(X_{a+1},\ldots,X_{n})\Big]\,.

Furthermore, for each
\lambda>0

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a-1})\mathds{1}_{\{T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda\}}(1-X_{a})\psi(X_{a+1},\ldots,X_{n})\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{a}}\min\big\{\lambda^{\xi},a^{\xi},(n-a)^{\xi}\big\}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a-1})\mathds{1}_{\{T_{L_{a}}+T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda\}}X_{a}\psi(X_{a+1},\ldots,X_{n})\Big]\,.

It suffices to prove the second part of the lemma. In fact, the
first part follows from the second one with a number
\lambda\geq n

since for
0<a<n
we have
T_{L_{a}+1}\leq n
and
T_{L_{a}}+T_{L_{a}+1}\leq n
almost surely with respect to the polymer measure

\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}
.

Pick
h\in\mathbb{R}
,
\omega:=\{\omega_{b}\}_{b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\Omega
, integers

0<a<n
, and
\lambda>0
. The lemma uses a standard trick to
introduce the indicator function that there is a contact at site
a

at the expense of a multiplicative constant that depends on the charge
at
a
and on a power of
a
or
n-a
. In detail, we are going to
exploit the two identities

\mathds{1}_{\{T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda\}}(1-X_{a})=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\sum_{j=a+1}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{j-i\leq\lambda\}}X_{i}\prod_{k=i+1}^{j-1}(1-X_{k})X_{j}

(6)

and

\mathds{1}_{\{T_{L_{a}}+T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda\}}X_{a}=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\sum_{j=a+1}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{j-i\leq\lambda\}}X_{i}\prod_{k=i+1}^{a-1}(1-X_{k})X_{a}\prod_{k=a+1}^{j-1}(1-X_{k})X_{j}\,.

(7)

The identity (
6
) decomposes the event that the site

a
is in a polymer excursion of size at most
\lambda
, but there is
no contact in
a
, as a disjoint union of the events that there is a
contact at
i<a
, a contact at
j>a
with
j-i\leq\lambda
, and no contact
between
i
and
j
. Similarly, (
7
) decomposes the
event that the site
a
is a contact separating two excursions of
total size at most
\lambda
according to the positions
i
and
j
of
the first contacts on the left and on the right of
a
, respectively.
Also in this case
j-i\leq\lambda
.

Fix non-negative real functions
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{a-1}
and

\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{n-a-1}
, and put for brevity

\Phi:=\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a-1})
and

\Psi:=\psi(X_{a+1},\ldots,X_{n})
. For
i<a<j
also define the random
quantities
\Phi_{i}:=\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{i},0,\ldots,0)
and

\Psi_{j}:=\psi(0,\ldots,0,X_{j},\ldots,X_{n})
. Since
X_{n}=1
almost surely
under the polymer measure, the identity (
6
) implies

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\Phi\mathds{1}_{\{T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda\}}(1-X_{a})\Psi\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\sum_{j=a+1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\{j-i\leq\lambda\}}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\Phi X_{i}\prod_{k=i+1}^{j-1}(1-X_{k})X_{j}\Psi\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\sum_{j=a+1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\{j-i\leq\lambda\}}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\Phi_{i}X_{i}\prod_{k=i+1}^{j-1}(1-X_{k})X_{j}\Psi_{j}\bigg]\,.

We can invoke Lemma
2.1
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\Phi\mathds{1}_{\{T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda\}}(1-X_{a})\Psi\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\sum_{j=a+1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\{j-i\leq\lambda\}}\mathsf{E}_{j,h,\omega}\big[\Phi_{i}X_{i}\big]\mathsf{E}_{j-i,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{j-i-1}(1-X_{k})\bigg]\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[X_{j}\Psi_{j}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\sum_{j=a+1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\{j-i\leq\lambda\}}\mathsf{E}_{j,h,\omega}\big[\Phi_{i}X_{i}\big]\,\frac{p(j-i)\mathrm{e}^{h+\omega_{j}}}{Z_{j-i,h}(\vartheta^{i}\omega)}\,\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[X_{j}\Psi_{j}\big]\,.

(8)

Similarly, (
7
) and Lemma
2.1
show that

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\Phi\mathds{1}_{\{T_{L_{a}}+T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda\}}X_{a}\Psi\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\sum_{j=a+1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\{j-i\leq\lambda\}}\mathsf{E}_{j,h,\omega}\big[\Phi_{i}X_{i}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\times\mathsf{E}_{j-i,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{a-i-1}(1-X_{k})X_{a-i}\prod_{k=a-i+1}^{j-i-1}(1-X_{k})\bigg]\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[X_{j}\Psi_{j}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\sum_{j=a+1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\{j-i\leq\lambda\}}\mathsf{E}_{j,h,\omega}\big[\Phi_{i}X_{i}\big]\,\frac{p(a-i)p(j-a)\mathrm{e}^{2h+\omega_{a}+\omega_{j}}}{Z_{j-i,h}(\vartheta^{i}\omega)}\,\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[X_{j}\Psi_{j}\big]\,.

(9)

Now we observe that if
i<a<j
with
j-i\leq\lambda
, then the inequality
(
5
) gives

\displaystyle p(j-i)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\xi\min\big\{(a-i)^{\xi},(j-a)^{\xi}\big\}p(a-i)p(j-a)

(10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\xi\min\big\{\lambda^{\xi},a^{\xi},(n-a)^{\xi}\big\}p(a-i)p(j-a)\,.

Therefore, plugging (
10
) in (
8
),
and then comparing with (
9
), we get

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\Phi\mathds{1}_{\{T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda\}}(1-X_{a})\Psi\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{a}}\min\big\{\lambda^{\xi},a^{\xi},(n-a)^{\xi}\big\}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\Phi\mathds{1}_{\{T_{L_{a}}+T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda\}}X_{a}\Psi\Big]\,.~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\qed

Remark 2.2
.

The first part of Lemma
2.2
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a-1})\psi(X_{a+1},\ldots,X_{n})\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\Big[1+\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{a}}\min\big\{a^{\xi},(n-a)^{\xi}\big\}\Big]\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a-1})X_{a}\psi(X_{a+1},\ldots,X_{n})\Big]\,.

This bound with
\phi
and
\psi
identically equal to 1 reads

\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a}]\geq\frac{1}{1+\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{a}}\min\{a^{\xi},(n-a)^{\xi}\}}\,.

The latter, which trivially holds even if

or

, is a
refinement of
[

, Lemma A.1]
.

2.2
A concentration bound under subexponential conditions

A version of the McDiarmid’s inequality for functions of independent
random variables under subexponential conditions is provided in

[
24
]
. We use this result to obtain a concentration bound
for the finite-volume free energies
\log Z_{n,h}=\log\mathsf{E}[\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{a=1}^{n}(h+\omega_{a})X_{a}}X_{n}]
and
\log Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}=\log\mathsf{E}[\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}(h+\omega_{a})X_{a}}X_{n}]
.
Note that

\Big|\log Z_{n,h}(\omega)-\log Z_{n,h^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})\Big|\leq n|h-h^{\prime}|+\sum_{a=1}^{n}|\omega_{a}-\omega_{a}^{\prime}|

(11)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
h
and
h^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{R}
, and

\omega:=\{\omega_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and

\omega^{\prime}:=\{\omega_{a}^{\prime}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
in
\Omega
, as one can easily
verify. The same holds if
Z_{n,h}
is replaced by
Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}
. In particular, bound (
11
) gives

|f(h)-f(h^{\prime})|\leq|h-h^{\prime}|
.

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists a constant
\kappa>0
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
h\in\mathbb{R}
,
and
u\geq 0

\mathbb{P}\bigg[\Big|\log Z_{n,h}-\mathbb{E}\big[\log Z_{n,h}\big]\Big|>u\bigg]\leq 2\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\kappa u^{2}}{n+u}}\,,

and the very same bound holds if
Z_{n,h}
is replaced by
Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}
.

We develop the argument for
Z_{n,h}
, as the argument for

Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}
is identical. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
h\in\mathbb{R}
, and

u\geq 0
. For
\varpi:=\{\varpi_{b}\}_{b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\Omega
and

a\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, let
\Lambda_{\varpi,a}
be the random variable on

\Omega
that maps
\omega:=\{\omega_{b}\}_{b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
to

\Lambda_{\varpi,a}(\omega):=\log Z_{n,h}(\{\varpi_{0},\ldots,\varpi_{a-1},\omega_{a},\varpi_{a+1},\ldots\})\,,

namely,
\Lambda_{\varpi,a}(\omega)
is the finite-volume free energy
for a system that at site
b
has the charge
\varpi_{b}
if
b\neq a

and
\omega_{a}
if
b=a
. An analog of the McDiarmid’s inequality for
functions of independent random variables under subexponential
conditions (see
[
24
, Theorem 4]
) states that if there
exists a constant
c>0
such that for every
\varpi\in\Omega
,

a\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and
q\geq 1
we have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\Lambda_{\varpi,a}-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{\varpi,a}]\big|^{q}\Big]\leq(cq)^{q}\,,

(12)

then

\mathbb{P}\bigg[\Big|\log Z_{n,h}-\mathbb{E}\big[\log Z_{n,h}\big]\Big|>u\bigg]\leq 2\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{u^{2}}{(2\mathrm{e}c)^{2}n+2\mathrm{e}cu}}\,.

Let us show that (
12
) is satisfied with

c:=(2/\eta\mathrm{e})\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{\eta|\omega_{0}|}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]
and

\eta
as in Assumption
1.2
. This proves the theorem
with
1/\kappa:=\max\{2ec,(2\mathrm{e}c)^{2}\}
.

Bound (
11
) yields

|\Lambda_{\varpi,a}(\omega)-\Lambda_{\varpi,a}(\omega^{\prime})|\leq|\omega_{a}-\omega_{a}^{\prime}|
for
\omega:=\{\omega_{b}\}_{b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and

\omega^{\prime}:=\{\omega_{b}^{\prime}\}_{b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
in
\Omega
, which thanks to
Hölder’s inequality gives for
q\geq 1

\displaystyle\Big|\Lambda_{\varpi,a}(\omega)-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{\varpi,a}]\Big|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\Big|\Lambda_{\varpi,a}(\omega)-\Lambda_{\varpi,a}(\omega^{\prime})\Big|^{q}\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{a}-\omega^{\prime}_{a}|^{q}\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}]\leq 2^{q-1}|\omega_{a}|^{q}+2^{q-1}\int_{\Omega}|\omega^{\prime}_{0}|^{q}\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}]\,.

Using the inequality
\zeta^{q}\leq(q/\eta\mathrm{e})^{q}\mathrm{e}^{\eta\zeta}
for

\zeta\geq 0
we finally get

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\Lambda_{\varpi,a}-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{\varpi,a}]\big|^{q}\Big]\leq 2^{q}\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{0}|^{q}\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]\leq\bigg(\frac{2q}{\eta\mathrm{e}}\bigg)^{q}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{\eta|\omega_{0}|}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]\leq(cq)^{q}\,.\qed

2.3
First consequences of concentration

The first consequence of the concentration bounds for the free
energies is the lower bound on
\mu(h)
in Proposition
1.3
,
which is proved in this section. The superadditivity property
exploited in
[
2
,
16
]
to establish the existence
of
\mu(h)
is no longer available because now we work with

Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}
. However, one can show that the sequence

\{\log\mathbb{E}[1/Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}]\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is nearly
superadditive and nearly subadditive: we choose to show the second
property since it follows directly from Lemma

2.2
.

Let us show that the limit defining
\mu(h)
exists for all
h\in\mathbb{R}
.
We remark that
q:=\int_{\Omega}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{0}\leq 0\}}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]>0
because

\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]=0
. Fix
h\in\mathbb{R}
and consider
the sequence whose
n^{\mathrm{th}}
term is
\log[2^{\xi}(\mathrm{e}^{h}+\xi n^{\xi})/q]-\log\mathbb{E}[{1}/{Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}}]
, where the number

\xi
has been defined in (
5
). We claim that such
sequence is subadditive, so that

\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}-({1}/{n})\log\mathbb{E}\big[{1}/{Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}}\big]=:\mu(h)

exists as a consequence. To prove this, we appeal to Lemma

2.2
, through Remark

2.2
, to state that for all
\omega\in\Omega

and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}

\mathsf{E}_{m+n,h,\omega}[X_{m}]\geq\frac{1}{1+\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{m}}\min\{m^{\xi},n^{\xi}\}}\,.

Then we observe that

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{m+n,h,\omega}[X_{m}]=\frac{\mathsf{E}[X_{m}\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{a=1}^{m+n}(h+\omega_{a})X_{a}}X_{m+n}]}{Z_{m+n,h}(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\frac{Z_{m,h}(\omega)Z_{n,h}(\vartheta^{m}\omega)}{Z_{m+n,h}(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{h+\omega_{m}}\frac{Z^{\textbf{-}}_{m,h}(\omega)Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}(\vartheta^{m}\omega)}{Z^{\textbf{-}}_{m+n,h}(\omega)}\,,

where we have used the renewal property in the second equality.
Therefore

\mathrm{e}^{h+\omega_{m}}\frac{Z^{\textbf{-}}_{m,h}(\omega)Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}(\vartheta^{m}\omega)}{Z^{\textbf{-}}_{m+n,h}(\omega)}\geq\frac{1}{1+\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{m}}\min\{m^{\xi},n^{\xi}\}}\,,

namely

\frac{1}{Z^{\textbf{-}}_{m+n,h}(\omega)}\geq\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}^{h+\omega_{m}}+\xi\min\{m^{\xi},n^{\xi}\}}\,\frac{1}{Z^{\textbf{-}}_{m,h}(\omega)}\frac{1}{Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}(\vartheta^{m}\omega)}\,.

At this point, to obtain the desired subadditivity property it
suffices to note that the three factors in the right-hand side of the
last inequality are statistically independent, so by integrating with
respect to
\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]
and exploiting that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}^{h+\omega_{0}}+\xi\min\{m^{\xi},n^{\xi}\}}\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{0}\leq 0\}}}{\mathrm{e}^{h}+\xi\min\{m^{\xi},n^{\xi}\}}\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]

\displaystyle=q\,\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}^{h}+\xi\min\{m^{\xi},n^{\xi}\}}\geq\frac{q}{2^{\xi}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{h}+\xi(m+n)^{\xi}}{(\mathrm{e}^{h}+\xi m^{\xi})(\mathrm{e}^{h}+\xi n^{\xi})}

we complete the task.

The Lipschitz property of
\mu
.
The function
\mu
that
maps
h\in\mathbb{R}
to
\mu(h)
is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz
constant equal to
1
. In fact, since
0\leq\partial_{h}\log Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}(\omega)\leq n
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
h\in\mathbb{R}

we have

\Bigg|\partial_{h}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}}\bigg]\Bigg|=\left|\frac{1}{n}\frac{\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{\partial_{h}\log Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}}{Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}}\Big]}{\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{1}{Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}}\Big]}\right|\leq 1\,.

From here we deduce that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}

\Bigg|\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}}\bigg]-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{Z_{n,h^{\prime}}^{\textbf{-}}}\bigg]\Bigg|\leq|h-h^{\prime}|\,,

which in turn gives
|\mu(h)-\mu(h^{\prime})|\leq|h-h^{\prime}|
by sending
n
to
infinity.

The bounds on
\mu(h)
.
The inequality
\mu(h)\leq f(h)

follows from Jensen’s inequality, which gives for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

h\in\mathbb{R}

-\log\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}}\bigg]\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\log Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[\log Z_{n,h}\big]-h\,.

For a lower bound on
\mu(h)
note that

p(n)/Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}(\omega)\leq 1
, because the left-hand side
is the probability

\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}[T_{1}=n]=\mathrm{e}^{h+\omega_{n}}p(n)/Z_{n,h}(\omega)
, so for
every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
h\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\omega\in\Omega
we have

\frac{p(n)}{Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}(\omega)}\leq\mathds{1}_{\big\{\log Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}(\omega)-\mathbb{E}[\log Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}]<-\frac{1}{2}f(h)n\big\}}+p(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}f(h)n-\mathbb{E}[\log Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}]}\,.

(13)

At the same time, using that
\mathbb{E}[\log Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}]=\mathbb{E}[\log Z_{n,h}]-h
, we can state that
\mathbb{E}[\log Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}]\geq f(h)n-\epsilon n
for any given

\epsilon>0
and all sufficiently large
n
. Thus, integrating
(
13
) with respect to
\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]
and
invoking Theorem
2.1
, we find for all sufficiently
large
n

\displaystyle p(n)\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{Z^{\textbf{-}}_{n,h}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\kappa f(h)^{2}}{4+2f(h)}n}+p(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}f(h)n+\epsilon n}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\kappa}{6}\min\{f(h),f(h)^{2}\}n}+p(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}f(h)n+\epsilon n}\,.

Taking the logarithm, dividing by
n
, and then sending
n
to
infinity, this bound allows us to conclude that
\mu(h)\geq c\min\{f(h),f(h)^{2}\}-\epsilon
with
c:=\min\{\kappa/6,1/2\}>0
, which
implies
\mu(h)\geq c\min\{f(h),f(h)^{2}\}
because
\epsilon
is
arbitrary.
∎

In the proof of Proposition
1.3
we have observed that

\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}[T_{1}=n]=p(n)/Z_{n,h}^{\textbf{-}}(\omega)
. As a
direct consequence, Proposition
1.3
has the following
corollary.

Corollary 2.1
.

For every
h\in\mathbb{R}

\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\cdot}[T_{1}=n]\Big]=-\mu(h)\,.

Corollary
2.1
and the fact that
\mu(h)\geq c\min\{f(h),f(h)^{2}\}>0
for
h>h_{c}
imply that the contact fraction in
the localized phase is small with exponentially small probability. We
let the following lemma to state a precise result.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every closed set
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
there exist constants

\delta>0
and
\gamma>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{h\in H}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\cdot}\big[L_{n}<\delta n\big]\bigg]\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma n}\,.

Measurability of the variable of which we are taking the expectation
in Lemma
2.3
holds because

\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}<\delta n]
is continuous with respect to
h

for any
n
and
\omega
, so that
\sup_{h\in H}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}<\delta n]=\sup_{h\in D}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}<\delta n]
,
D
being a countable dense
subset of
H
. The same argument applies to also the variables of
which we are taking the expectation in Lemmas
3.2
,

3.3
, and
3.4
.

Fix a closed set
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
and define
h_{o}:=\inf H>h_{c}
. Since
\mu(h_{o})>0
by Proposition
1.3
, Corollary

2.1
shows that there exist constants
\gamma_{o}>0
and

G_{o}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathsf{P}_{n,h_{o},\cdot}[T_{1}=n]\Big]\leq G_{o}\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma_{o}n}

(14)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Put
\gamma:=\gamma_{o}/3
and

\delta:=\gamma/\log(1+G_{o}/\gamma)
. We claim that the lemma
holds with such
\delta
and
\gamma
. In fact, for every

n\in\mathbb{N}
,
h\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\omega\in\Omega
we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\big[L_{n}<\delta n\big]

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\mathds{1}_{\{l<\delta n\}}\sum_{0=a_{0}<a_{1}<\cdots<a_{l}=n}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\prod_{i=1}^{l}X_{a_{i-1}}\prod_{k=a_{i-1}+1}^{a_{i}-1}(1-X_{k})X_{a_{i}}\bigg]\,.

On the other hand, repeated applications of Lemma
2.1
show
that for all integers
0=a_{0}<a_{1}<\cdots<a_{l}=n

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\prod_{i=1}^{l}X_{a_{i-1}}\prod_{k=a_{i-1}+1}^{a_{i}-1}(1-X_{k})X_{a_{i}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad=\prod_{i=1}^{l}\mathsf{E}_{a_{i}-a_{i-1},h,\vartheta^{a_{i-1}}\omega}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{a_{i}-a_{i-1}-1}(1-X_{k})\bigg]\mathsf{E}_{a_{i},h,\omega}[X_{a_{i-1}}]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\prod_{i=1}^{l}\mathsf{E}_{a_{i}-a_{i-1},h,\vartheta^{a_{i-1}}\omega}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{a_{i}-a_{i-1}-1}(1-X_{k})\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad=\prod_{i=1}^{l}\mathsf{P}_{a_{i}-a_{i-1},h,\vartheta^{a_{i-1}}\omega}\big[T_{1}=a_{i}-a_{i-1}\big]\,.

Since
\mathsf{P}_{m,h,\omega}[T_{1}=m]=p(m)/Z_{m,h}^{\textbf{-}}(\omega)

with

Z_{m,h}^{\textbf{-}}(\omega)=\mathsf{E}[\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{a=1}^{m-1}(h+\omega_{a})X_{a}}X_{m}]

is decreasing with respect to
h
for any
m
and
\omega
, we deduce
that

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in H}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\big[L_{n}<\delta n\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\mathds{1}_{\{l<\delta n\}}\sum_{0=a_{0}<a_{1}<\cdots<a_{l}=n}\prod_{i=1}^{l}\mathsf{P}_{a_{i}-a_{i-1},h_{o},\vartheta^{a_{i-1}}\omega}\big[T_{1}=a_{i}-a_{i-1}\big]\,.

In this way, integrating with respect to
\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]
, the statistical
independence of the factors

\mathsf{P}_{a_{i}-a_{i-1},h_{o},\vartheta^{a_{i-1}}\cdot}[T_{1}=a_{i}-a_{i-1}]

combined with bound (
14
) leads us to the inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{h\in H}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\big[L_{n}<\delta n\big]\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\mathds{1}_{\{l<\delta n\}}{n-1\choose l-1}G_{o}^{l}\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma_{o}n}\,.

As
(1+G_{o}/\gamma)^{\delta}=\mathrm{e}^{\gamma}
by construction, we can
finally appeal to the bound
\mathds{1}_{\{l<\delta n\}}\leq(1+G_{o}/\gamma)^{\delta n-l}\leq(\gamma/G_{o})^{l}\mathrm{e}^{\gamma n}
and to the
relationship
\gamma_{o}=3\gamma
to get

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{h\in H}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\big[L_{n}<\delta n\big]\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}(\gamma/G_{o})^{l}\mathrm{e}^{\gamma n}{n-1\choose l-1}G_{o}^{l}\mathrm{e}^{-3\gamma n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma(1+\gamma)^{n-1}\mathrm{e}^{-2\gamma n}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma n}\,.~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\qed

3
Correlation estimates

This section introduces the correlation estimates that we use for the
main proofs of the paper. The first lemma we present is concerned with
the covariance of two observables under the polymer measure

\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}
: given two measurable complex functions
\Phi

and
\Psi
over
(\mathcal{S},\mathfrak{S})
we define their
covariance as

\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}[\Phi,\Psi]:=\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\Phi\overline{\Psi}\,\big]-\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\Phi\big]\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\,\overline{\Psi}\,\big]\,,

provided that all the expectations exist. As usual,
\bar{\zeta}
is
the complex conjugate of
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
. The variance

\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}[\Phi,\Phi]
is denoted by

\mathsf{var}_{n,h,\omega}[\Phi]
. The lemma involves two independent copies
of the polymer, so let
\{S_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and

\{S_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be two independent copies of the renewal
process, defined on the product probability space

(\mathcal{S}^{2},\mathfrak{S}^{\otimes 2},\mathsf{P}^{\otimes 2})
. For

n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
h\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\omega\in\Omega
, consider on

(\mathcal{S}^{2},\mathfrak{S}^{\otimes 2})
also the product measure

\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}
and denote by

\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}
the corresponding expectation. Finally,
let
\xi
be the number introduced in (
5
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
h\in\mathbb{R}
,
\omega:=\{\omega_{c}\}_{c\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\Omega
,
integers
0\leq a\leq b\leq n
, and functions
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{a}

and
\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{n-b}

\bigg|\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a}),\psi(X_{b},\ldots,X_{n})\Big]\bigg|\leq 2\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\sum_{j=b+1}^{\infty}\mathsf{E}_{j-i,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{j-i-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]\,.

Moreover

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a},X_{b}]|}{\min\{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a}],\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{b}]\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{i=0}^{a}\sum_{j=b}^{\infty}2\xi(j-i)^{\xi}\mathrm{e}^{-h-\min\{\omega_{a},\omega_{b}\}}\mathsf{E}_{j-i,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{j-i-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]\,.

Fix
h\in\mathbb{R}
,
\omega:=\{\omega_{c}\}_{c\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\Omega
, and
integers
0\leq a\leq b\leq n
. To begin with, we prove
that for every functions
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{a}
and

\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{n-b}

\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}[\Phi,\Psi]=\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}\big[\Phi\overline{\Psi}-\Phi\overline{\Psi^{\prime}}\,\big]=\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\Phi\big(\,\overline{\Psi}-\overline{\Psi^{\prime}}\,\big)\prod_{k=a}^{b}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]\,,

(15)

where we have set
\Phi:=\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a})
,

\Psi:=\psi(X_{b},\ldots,X_{n})
, and
\Psi^{\prime}:=\psi(X_{b}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{n}^{\prime})
for
brevity. The first equality is immediate. Regarding the second
equality, we use the identity

\prod_{k=a}^{b}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{A}\subseteq\{a,\ldots,b\}\\
\mathcal{A}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|\mathcal{A}|}\prod_{k\in\mathcal{A}}X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime}=1

to write down

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[\Phi\overline{\Psi}-\Phi\overline{\Psi^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\Phi(\overline{\Psi}-\overline{\Psi^{\prime}})\prod_{k=a}^{b}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{A}\subseteq\{a,\ldots,b\}\\
\mathcal{A}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|\mathcal{A}|}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\Phi(\overline{\Psi}-\overline{\Psi^{\prime}})\prod_{k\in\mathcal{A}}X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime}\bigg]\,.

The desired result is due to the fact that
\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}[\Phi(\overline{\Psi}-\overline{\Psi^{\prime}})\prod_{k\in\mathcal{A}}X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime}]=0
for any given
non-empty set
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\{a,\ldots,b\}
. To prove this fact we
invoke Lemma
2.1
to state that

\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\Phi\prod_{k\in\mathcal{A}}X_{k}\overline{\Psi}\bigg]=\frac{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\Phi\prod_{k\in\mathcal{A}}X_{k}\big]\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\prod_{k\in\mathcal{A}}X_{k}\overline{\Psi}\,\big]}{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\prod_{k\in\mathcal{A}}X_{k}\big]}\,,

which entails

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\Phi(\overline{\Psi}-\overline{\Psi^{\prime}})\prod_{k\in\mathcal{A}}X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\Phi\prod_{k\in\mathcal{A}}X_{k}\overline{\Psi}\bigg]\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\prod_{k\in\mathcal{A}}X_{k}\bigg]-\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\Phi\prod_{k\in\mathcal{A}}X_{k}\bigg]\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\prod_{k\in\mathcal{A}}X_{k}\overline{\Psi}\bigg]=0\,.

The bound on

\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a}),\psi(X_{b},\ldots,X_{n})]
.

Given functions
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{a}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{n-b}
,
formula (
15
) shows that

\Big|\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\Phi,\Psi\big]\Big|\leq 2\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\prod_{k=a}^{b}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]\,.

The identity

\displaystyle\prod_{k=a}^{b}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\sum_{j=b+1}^{n}X_{i}X_{i}^{\prime}\prod_{k=i+1}^{j-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})X_{j}X_{j}^{\prime}+\prod_{k=a}^{n}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\,,

where we have expanded the left-hand side according to the last
contacts before
a
and the first contacts (if any) after
b
that not
exceed
n
, together with the fact that
X_{n}=1
and
X_{n}^{\prime}=1
almost
surely under the two-polymers measure
\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}
, yields

\Big|\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\Phi,\Psi\big]\Big|\leq 2\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\sum_{j=b+1}^{n}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[X_{i}X_{i}^{\prime}\prod_{k=i+1}^{j-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})X_{j}X_{j}^{\prime}\bigg]\,.

(16)

Finally, we appeal once again to Lemma
2.1
to get the bound

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[X_{i}X_{i}^{\prime}\prod_{k=i+1}^{j-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})X_{j}X_{j}^{\prime}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{x_{i}\in\{0,1\}}\cdots\sum_{x_{j}\in\{0,1\}}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[X_{i}\prod_{k=i+1}^{j-1}(1-x_{k}X_{k})X_{j}\bigg]\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[X_{i}X_{j}\prod_{k=i}^{j}\mathds{1}_{\{X_{k}=x_{k}\}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{x_{i}\in\{0,1\}}\cdots\sum_{x_{j}\in\{0,1\}}\mathsf{E}_{j-i,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{j-i-1}(1-x_{k+i}X_{k})\bigg]\mathsf{E}_{j-i,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}\bigg[\prod_{k=0}^{j-i}\mathds{1}_{\{X_{k}=x_{k+i}\}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathsf{E}_{j-i,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{j-i-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]\,.

(17)

Inequalities (
16
) and (
17
) give the
bound on

\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}[\phi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{a}),\psi(X_{b},\ldots,X_{n})]
stated
by the lemma.

The bounds on
\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a},X_{b}]
.
Formula
(
15
) with the choice
\phi(x_{0},\ldots,x_{a}):=x_{a}
and

\psi(x_{b},\ldots,x_{n}):=x_{b}
shows that both

\Big|\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a},X_{b}]\Big|\leq 2\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[X_{a}\prod_{k=a}^{b}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]

and

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}X_{a}^{\prime}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{a}\geq\lambda,\,T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\tau\}}\geq\frac{\delta}{4}n\big\}}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{r=1}^{2\tau}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\sum_{a\in\mathbb{A}_{r}}X_{a}^{\prime}Y_{a}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{a}\geq\lambda\}}\geq\frac{\delta}{8\tau}n\big\}}\prod_{a\in\mathbb{A}_{r}}(1-X_{a}X_{a}^{\prime})\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{r=1}^{2\tau}\sum_{x_{1}\in\{0,1\}}\cdots\sum_{x_{n}\in\{0,1\}}{}^{r}\!E_{n,h,\omega}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\,\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\{X_{k}=x_{k}\}}\bigg]

with

{}^{r}\!E_{n,h,\omega}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\sum_{a\in\mathbb{A}_{r}}x_{a}Y_{a}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{a}\geq\lambda\}}\geq\frac{\delta}{8\tau}n\big\}}\prod_{a\in\mathbb{A}_{r}}(1-x_{a}X_{a})\bigg]\,.

The last step to obtain bound (
25
) is to show that

{}^{r}\!E_{n,h,\omega}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\leq\bigg(\frac{\xi\tau^{\xi}}{\xi\tau^{\xi}+\mathrm{e}^{h+\lambda}}\bigg)^{\frac{\delta}{8\tau}n}

(28)

uniformly with respect to
r
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. This is
facilitated by the fact that
Y_{a}
and
Y_{b}
for different
a
and
b

in a sublattice of spacing
2\tau
do not overlap, in the sense that
depend on the non-overlapping sets

\{X_{\max\{0,a-\tau+1\}},\ldots,X_{a+\tau-1}\}
and

\{X_{\max\{0,b-\tau+1\}},\ldots,X_{b+\tau-1}\}
of binary variables, as
(
26
) makes evident. Given
r\in\{1,\ldots,2\tau\}

and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\{0,1\}
, to prove (
28
) we expand
the indicator function in
{}^{r}\!E_{n,h,\omega}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
to
get

\displaystyle{}^{r}\!E_{n,h,\omega}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{r}}\mathds{1}_{\{|\mathcal{A}|\geq\frac{\delta}{8\tau}n\}}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\prod_{a\in\mathcal{A}}x_{a}(1-X_{a})Y_{a}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{a}\geq\lambda\}}\prod_{a\in\mathbb{A}_{r}\setminus\mathcal{A}}\big(1-x_{a}Y_{a}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{a}\geq\lambda\}}\big)\bigg]\,.

We are going to relax the constraints that
X_{a}=0
for

a\in\mathcal{A}
. Bearing in mind that
Y_{a}
and
Y_{b}
for distinct

a,b\in\mathbb{A}_{r}
do not overlap, repeated applications of
(
27
) yield for any
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{r}

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\prod_{a\in\mathcal{A}}x_{a}(1-X_{a})Y_{a}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{a}\geq\lambda\}}\prod_{a\in\mathbb{A}_{r}\setminus\mathcal{A}}\big(1-x_{a}Y_{a}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{a}\geq\lambda\}}\big)\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\prod_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{\xi\tau^{\xi}}{\xi\tau^{\xi}+\mathrm{e}^{h+\omega_{a}}}\,\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\prod_{a\in\mathcal{A}}x_{a}Y_{a}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{a}\geq\lambda\}}\prod_{a\in\mathbb{A}_{r}\setminus\mathcal{A}}\big(1-x_{a}Y_{a}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{a}\geq\lambda\}}\big)\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\bigg(\frac{\xi\tau^{\xi}}{\xi\tau^{\xi}+\mathrm{e}^{h+\lambda}}\bigg)^{|\mathcal{A}|}\,\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\prod_{a\in\mathcal{A}}x_{a}Y_{a}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{a}\geq\lambda\}}\prod_{a\in\mathbb{A}_{r}\setminus\mathcal{A}}\big(1-x_{a}Y_{a}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{a}\geq\lambda\}}\big)\bigg]\,.

We conclude that

\displaystyle{}^{r}\!E_{n,h,\omega}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\bigg(\frac{\xi\tau^{\xi}}{\xi\tau^{\xi}+\mathrm{e}^{h+\lambda}}\bigg)^{\frac{\delta}{8\tau}n}\,\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\sum_{a\in\mathbb{A}_{r}}x_{a}Y_{a}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{a}\geq\lambda\}}\geq\frac{\delta}{8\tau}n\bigg]\,,

which proves (
28
).
∎

We can now go into the first application of Lemmas
3.1
and

3.2
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For each closed set
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
there exist constants

\gamma>0
and
G>0
such that for every integers
0\leq a\leq b\leq n

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{h\in H}\,\frac{|\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{a},X_{b}]|}{\min\{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{a}],\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{b}]\}}\bigg]\leq G\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma(b-a)}\,.

Fix any
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
closed. Lemma
3.2
ensures
us the existence of two constants
\gamma_{o}>0
and
G_{o}>0
such that
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sup_{h\in H}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]\Bigg]\leq G_{o}\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma_{o}n}\,.

(29)

The second part of Lemma
3.1
states that for every integers

0\leq a\leq b\leq n
,
h\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\omega:=\{\omega_{c}\}_{c\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\Omega

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a},X_{b}]|}{\min\{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a}],\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{b}]\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{i=0}^{a}\sum_{j=b}^{\infty}2\xi(j-i)^{\xi}\mathrm{e}^{-h-\min\{\omega_{a},\omega_{b}\}}\mathsf{E}_{j-i,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{j-i-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]\,,

(30)

where
\xi
is the number defined in (
5
). Since the
left-hand side of (
30
) is smaller than or equal to

1
, we can further state that

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a},X_{b}]|}{\min\{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a}],\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{b}]\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\mathds{1}_{\big\{|\omega_{a}|>\frac{\gamma_{o}}{3}(b-a)\big\}}+\mathds{1}_{\big\{|\omega_{b}|>\frac{\gamma_{o}}{3}(b-a)\big\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\sum_{i=0}^{a}\sum_{j=b}^{\infty}2\xi(j-i)^{\xi}\mathrm{e}^{-h+\frac{\gamma_{o}}{3}(b-a)}\mathsf{E}_{j-i,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{j-i-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]\,.

In this way, taking the supremum with respect to
h
, defining

h_{o}:=\inf H
, and then integrating with respect to

\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]
, we get thanks to (
29
) and to a
Chernoff–type bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{h\in H}\,\frac{|\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{a},X_{b}]|}{\min\{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{a}],\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{b}]\}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2\int_{\Omega}\mathds{1}_{\big\{|\omega_{0}|>\frac{\gamma_{o}}{3}(b-a)\big\}}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]+\sum_{i=0}^{a}\sum_{j=b}^{\infty}2\xi(j-i)^{\xi}\mathrm{e}^{-h_{o}+\frac{\gamma_{o}}{3}(b-a)}G_{o}\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma_{o}(j-i)}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{\eta|\omega_{0}|-\frac{\eta\gamma_{o}}{3}(b-a)}\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2\xi G_{o}(j+1)j^{\xi}\mathrm{e}^{-h_{o}-\frac{\gamma_{o}}{3}(b-a)-\frac{\gamma_{o}}{3}j}\,,

\eta>0
being the number introduced by Assumption
1.2
.
This shows that the lemma holds with

\gamma:=\min\{\eta\gamma_{o}/3,\gamma_{o}/3\}
and
G:=2\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{\eta|\omega_{0}|}\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2\xi G_{o}(j+1)j^{\xi}\mathrm{e}^{-h_{o}-\gamma_{o}j/3}
.
∎

One corollary of Lemma
3.4
is the following.

Corollary 3.1
.

For each
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
closed there exist constants

\gamma>0
and
G>0
such that for every integers
0\leq a\leq b\leq n

\sup_{h\in H}\bigg|\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{a}]\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{b}]\Big]-\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{a}]\Big]\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{b}]\Big]\bigg|\leq G\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma(b-a)}\,.

Fix a closed set
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
and integers
0\leq a\leq b\leq n
. Put
m:=\lfloor(a+b)/2\rfloor
. Lemma
2.1
gives for all

h\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega\in\Omega

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a}X_{m}]}{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{m}]}-\frac{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a}X_{m}]-\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a}]\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{m}]}{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{m}]}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}_{m,h,\omega}[X_{a}]-\frac{\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a},X_{m}]}{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{m}]}

(31)

and

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{b}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{m}X_{b}]}{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{m}]}-\frac{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{m}X_{b}]-\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{m}]\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{b}]}{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{m}]}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}_{n-m,h,\vartheta^{m}\omega}[X_{b-m}]-\frac{\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{m},X_{b}]}{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{m}]}\,.

(32)

Lemma
3.4
implies that there exist constants
\gamma_{o}>0

and
G_{o}>0
independent of
a
,
b
, and
n
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in H}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{|\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{a},X_{m}]|}{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{m}]}\bigg]\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{h\in H}\frac{|\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{a},X_{m}]|}{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{m}]}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq G_{o}\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma_{o}(m-a)}

\displaystyle\leq G_{o}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\gamma_{o}}{2}(b-a)+\gamma_{o}}

and, similarly,

\sup_{h\in H}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{|\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{m},X_{b}]|}{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{m}]}\bigg]\leq G_{o}\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma_{o}(b-m)}\leq G_{o}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\gamma_{o}}{2}(b-a)}\,.

In this way, since the expectation
\mathsf{E}_{m,h,\cdot}[X_{a}]
and

\mathsf{E}_{n-m,h,\vartheta^{m}\cdot}[X_{b-m}]
are statistically independent,
we can conclude from (
31
) and (
32
)
that for all
h\in H

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{a}]\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{b}]\Big]-\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{a}]\Big]\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{b}]\Big]\bigg|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{|\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{a},X_{m}]|}{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{m}]}\bigg]+2\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{|\mathsf{cov}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{m},X_{b}]|}{\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[X_{m}]}\bigg]\leq 2G_{o}(\mathrm{e}^{\gamma_{o}}+1)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\gamma_{o}}{2}(b-a)}\,,

which proves the corollary with
\gamma:={\gamma_{o}}/{2}
and

G:=2G_{o}(\mathrm{e}^{\gamma_{o}}+1)
.
∎

3.1
First consequences of the correlation estimates

In this section we investigate the maximal polymer excursion

M_{n}:=\max\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{L_{n}}\}
in the localized phase, so providing
a first application of the correlation bounds and proving Proposition

1.4
.

We shall show that for every
h_{o}>h_{c}
and
\epsilon\in(0,1]
and for
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega

\adjustlimits{\limspace}_{n\uparrow\infty}{\sup}_{h\in U_{h_{o},\epsilon}}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}>\frac{1+\epsilon}{\mu(h)}\bigg]=0

(33)

and

\adjustlimits{\limspace}_{n\uparrow\infty}{\sup}_{h\in U_{h_{o},\epsilon}}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}<\frac{1-\epsilon}{\mu(h)}\bigg]=0\,,

(34)

where
U_{h_{o},\epsilon}
is an open interval centered at
h_{o}
with
radius
\min\{\epsilon\mu(h_{o})/8,(h_{o}-h_{c})/2\}>0
. These limits prove
the proposition as follows. For
\epsilon\in(0,1]
, Lindelöf’s lemma
ensures that from the open cover

\{U_{h_{o},\epsilon}\}_{h_{o}\in(h_{c},+\infty)}
of
(h_{c},+\infty)
we
can extract countably many elements corresponding to the points

h_{o}
in a countable set
\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}\subset(h_{c},+\infty)

such that
(h_{c},+\infty)\subseteq\cup_{h_{o}\in\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}}U_{h_{o},\epsilon}
. Let

\Omega_{h_{o},\epsilon}\in\mathcal{F}
be a set of charges with full
probability, that is
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{h_{o},\epsilon}]=1
, for which
(
33
) and (
34
) are valid and
define

\Omega_{o}:=\cap_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\cap_{h_{o}\in\mathbb{H}_{1/i}}\Omega_{h_{o},1/i}
. We
have
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{o}]=1
. By construction, for every

\omega\in\Omega_{o}
,
H\subset(h_{c},+\infty)
compact, and

\epsilon>0
there exist
i\in\mathbb{N}
and finitely many points

h_{1},\ldots,h_{r}
in
\mathbb{H}_{1/i}
such that
\inf_{h\in H}\mu(h)\epsilon>1/i
,
H\subset\cup_{s=1}^{r}U_{h_{s},1/i}
, and

\omega\in\Omega_{h_{s},1/i}
for all
s\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
. We remark
that
\inf_{h\in H}\mu(h)>0
because
\mu
is Lipschitz continuous
and strictly positive throughout
(h_{c},+\infty)
. It therefore
follows from (
33
) and (
34
)
that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\uparrow\infty}\,\sup_{h\in H}\,\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\Bigg[\bigg|\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}-\frac{1}{\mu(h)}\bigg|>\epsilon\Bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\adjustlimits{\limsmallspace}_{n\uparrow\infty}{\maxp}_{s\in\{1,\ldots,r\}}\,\sup_{h\in U_{h_{s},1/i}}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\Bigg[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}>\frac{1+1/i}{\mu(h)}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\adjustlimits{\limsmallspace}_{n\uparrow\infty}{\maxp}_{s\in\{1,\ldots,r\}}\,\sup_{h\in U_{h_{s},1/i}}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\Bigg[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}<\frac{1-1/i}{\mu(h)}\Bigg]=0\,.

This demonstrates that, as
n
goes to infinity,
M_{n}/\log n

converges in probability to
1/\mu(h)
with respect to the polymer
measure
\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
and
h>h_{c}
,
and the result is uniform in
h
belonging to compact sets in the
localized phase.

The limit (
33
).
Fix
h_{o}>h_{c}

and
\epsilon\in(0,1]
and put

\delta_{o}:=\min\{\epsilon\mu_{o}/8,(h_{o}-h_{c})/2\}
with

\mu_{o}:=\mu(h_{o})>0
, so that

U_{h_{o},\epsilon}=(h_{o}-\delta_{o},h_{o}+\delta_{o})
. Noting that

\mu(h)\leq\mu_{o}+\delta_{o}
for
h\in(h_{o}-\delta_{o},h_{o}+\delta_{o})
, as
the function
\mu
is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
equal to
1
by Proposition
1.3
, and setting

m_{n}:=\lfloor\{(1+\epsilon)/(\mu_{o}+\delta_{o})\}\log n\rfloor
for
brevity, we write for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
h\in U_{h_{o},\epsilon}
, and

\omega\in\Omega

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}>\frac{1+\epsilon}{\mu(h)}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}>\frac{1+\epsilon}{\mu_{o}+\delta_{o}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\big[M_{n}>m_{n}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-m_{n}-1}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[X_{i}\prod_{k=i+1}^{i+m_{n}}(1-X_{k})\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-m_{n}-1}\sum_{j=i+m_{n}+1}^{n}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[X_{i}\prod_{k=i+1}^{j-1}(1-X_{k})X_{j}\bigg]\,,

where the second inequality is due to the fact that the condition

M_{n}>m_{n}
implies that there is a contact site

i\in\{0,\ldots,n-m_{n}-1\}
followed by an excursion of size at least

m_{n}
, while the last equality exploits the identity

X_{i}\prod_{k=i+1}^{i+m_{n}}(1-X_{k})=\sum_{j=i+m_{n}+1}^{n}X_{i}\prod_{k=i+1}^{j-1}(1-X_{k})X_{j}+X_{i}\prod_{k=i+1}^{n}(1-X_{k})\,,

as well as the fact that
X_{n}=1
almost surely with respect to the
polymer measure
\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}
. An application of Lemma

2.1
first and a Chernoff–type bound later give

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}>\frac{1+\epsilon}{\mu(h)}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-m_{n}-1}\sum_{j=i+m_{n}+1}^{n}\mathsf{P}_{j-i,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}[T_{1}=j-i]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=m_{n}+1}^{\infty}\mathsf{P}_{j,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}[T_{1}=j]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{e}^{\zeta_{o}j-\zeta_{o}(m_{n}+1)}\,\mathsf{P}_{j,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}[T_{1}=j]\,,

(35)

where we have introduced the number
\zeta_{o}:=(1-\epsilon/4)\mu_{o}>0
. We
now note that for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
h\in(h_{o}-\delta_{o},h_{o}+\delta_{o})
, and

\omega:=\{\omega_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\Omega

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{j,h,\omega}[T_{1}=j]

\displaystyle=\frac{p(j)}{\mathsf{E}[\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{a=1}^{j-1}(h+\omega_{a})X_{a}}X_{j}]}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{\delta_{o}j}\,\frac{p(j)}{\mathsf{E}[\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{a=1}^{j-1}(h_{o}+\omega_{a})X_{a}}X_{j}]}=\mathrm{e}^{\delta_{o}j}\,\mathsf{P}_{j,h_{o},\omega}[T_{1}=j]\,.

Similarly, we have

\mathsf{P}_{j,h,\omega}[T_{1}=j]\geq\mathrm{e}^{-\delta_{o}j}\,\mathsf{P}_{j,h_{o},\omega}[T_{1}=j]\,.

(36)

Thus, (
35
) finally yields

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in U_{h_{o},\epsilon}}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}>\frac{1+\epsilon}{\mu(h)}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\zeta_{o}(m_{n}+1)}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{e}^{(\zeta_{o}+\delta_{o})j}\,\mathsf{P}_{j,h_{o},\vartheta^{i}\omega}[T_{1}=j]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{\zeta_{o}\frac{1+\epsilon}{\mu_{o}+\delta_{o}}}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Lambda(\vartheta^{i}\omega)\,,

(37)

\Lambda
being the random variable on
\Omega
defined as

\Lambda:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{e}^{(\zeta_{o}+\delta_{o})j}\,\mathsf{P}_{j,h_{o},\cdot}[T_{1}=j]\,.

Recalling that
\zeta_{o}:=(1-\epsilon/4)\mu_{o}
and noting that

\delta_{o}\leq\epsilon\mu_{o}/8
by definition, Corollary
2.1

shows that
\mathbb{E}[\Lambda]<+\infty
since
\zeta_{o}+\delta_{o}\leq(1-\epsilon/8)\mu_{o}<\mu_{o}
. In this way, as

\zeta_{o}\frac{1+\epsilon}{\mu_{o}+\delta_{o}}\geq 1+\epsilon\frac{5-2\epsilon}{8+\epsilon}\geq 1+\frac{\epsilon}{3}

because again
\delta_{o}\leq\epsilon\mu_{o}/8
and
\epsilon\leq 1
,
(
33
) follows from (
37
) thanks to
Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem.

The limit (
34
).
The correlation
estimates enter the proof of (
34
) because in order
to exclude that all the excursions are below a certain threshold it
suffices to take into account only the excursions that are included on
well separated segments of the polymer. The dilution procedure that we
implement is even more drastic than this because it
takes into account only excursions of very specific size. For this we
start by fixing
h_{o}>h_{c}
and
\epsilon\in(0,1]
and by putting

\delta_{o}:=\min\{\epsilon\mu_{o}/8,(h_{o}-h_{c})/2\}
with

\mu_{o}:=\mu(h_{o})>0
as before, so that

U_{h_{o},\epsilon}=(h_{o}-\delta_{o},h_{o}+\delta_{o})
. Then, planning to
deal with
r_{n}
excursions of size
m_{n}
and spacing
d_{n}
, we
define

\begin{cases}m_{n}:=\big\lfloor\frac{1-\epsilon}{\mu_{o}-\delta_{o}}\log n\big\rfloor+1\\[3.00003pt]
d_{n}:=\lfloor(\log n)^{2}\rfloor+1\\[3.00003pt]
r_{n}:=\big\lfloor\frac{n}{m_{n}+d_{n}}\big\rfloor\end{cases}\,.

(38)

Set
l_{s}:=s(m_{n}+d_{n})
for

s\in\{0,\ldots,r_{n}-1\}
. Bearing in mind that

\mu(h)\geq\mu_{o}-\delta_{o}
for
h\in(h_{o}-\delta_{o},h_{o}+\delta_{o})
by
the Lipschitz property of
\mu
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
h\in U_{h_{o},\epsilon}
, and
\omega\in\Omega
we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}<\frac{1-\epsilon}{\mu(h)}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}\leq\frac{1-\epsilon}{\mu_{o}-\delta_{o}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}[M_{n}<m_{n}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Bigg[\prod_{s=0}^{r_{n}-1}\bigg\{1-X_{l_{s}}\prod_{k=l_{s}+1}^{l_{s}+m_{n}-1}(1-X_{k})X_{l_{s}+m_{n}}\bigg\}\Bigg]\,,

since the condition
M_{n}<m_{n}
implies that an excursion from the
position
l_{s}
to the position
l_{s}+m_{n}
cannot occur for any choice
of
s
. This bound is the essence of the dilution procedure. Repeated
applications of the first part of Lemma
3.1
give

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}<\frac{1-\epsilon}{\mu(h)}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{s=0}^{r_{n}-1}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[1-X_{l_{s}}\prod_{k=l_{s}+1}^{l_{s}+m_{n}-1}(1-X_{k})X_{l_{s}+m_{n}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{s=0}^{r_{n}-2}\,\sum_{i=0}^{l_{s}+m_{n}-1}\sum_{j=l_{s+1}}^{\infty}\mathsf{E}_{j-i,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{j-i-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]\,.

(39)

We are going to show that for
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega

\displaystyle\adjustlimits{\limsup}_{n\uparrow\infty}{\sup}_{h\in U_{h_{o},\epsilon}}\prod_{s=0}^{r_{n}-1}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[1-X_{l_{s}}\prod_{k=l_{s}+1}^{l_{s}+m_{n}-1}(1-X_{k})X_{l_{s}+m_{n}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}\prod_{s=0}^{r_{n}-1}\Bigg\{1-\inf_{h\in U_{h_{o},\epsilon}}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[X_{l_{s}}\prod_{k=l_{s}+1}^{l_{s}+m_{n}-1}(1-X_{k})X_{l_{s}+m_{n}}\bigg]\Bigg\}=0

(40)

and

\adjustlimits{\limspace}_{n\uparrow\infty}{\sup}_{h\in U_{h_{o},\epsilon}}\sum_{s=0}^{r_{n}-2}\,\sum_{i=0}^{l_{s}+m_{n}-1}\sum_{j=l_{s+1}}^{\infty}\mathsf{E}_{j-i,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{j-i-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]=0\,.

(41)

These limits demonstrate (
34
) via (
39
).

Let us prove (
41
) first, which is the simpler
limit among the two. As
h_{o}-\delta_{o}>h_{c}
because

\delta_{o}\leq(h_{o}-h_{c})/2
by definition, according to Lemma

3.2
there exist constants
\gamma>0
and
G>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sup_{h\in[h_{o}-\delta_{o},+\infty)}\mathsf{E}_{j,h,\cdot}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{j-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]\Bigg]\leq G\,\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma j}

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. In this way, introducing the random variable

\Lambda:=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}j}\sup_{h\in[h_{o}-\delta_{o},+\infty)}\mathsf{E}_{j,h,\cdot}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{j-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]\,,

we have
\mathbb{E}[\Lambda]<+\infty
and

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in U_{h_{o},\epsilon}}\sum_{s=0}^{r_{n}-2}\,\sum_{i=0}^{l_{s}+m_{n}-1}\sum_{j=l_{s+1}}^{\infty}\mathsf{E}_{j-i,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{j-i-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq r_{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=d_{n}}^{\infty}\sup_{h\in[h_{o}-\delta_{o},+\infty)}\mathsf{E}_{j,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{j-1}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]\leq r_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}d_{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Lambda(\vartheta^{i}\omega)

for all
\omega\in\Omega
. Thus, (
41
) is due to
Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem since
r_{n}\leq n
and
d_{n}\geq(\log n)^{2}

according to (
38
).

Regarding (
40
), we use Lemma
2.1
to
write down for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
h\in\mathbb{R}
,
\omega\in\Omega
, and

s\in\{0,\ldots,r_{n}-1\}
the identity

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[X_{l_{s}}\prod_{k=l_{s}+1}^{l_{s}+m_{n}-1}(1-X_{k})X_{l_{s}+m_{n}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{l_{s}}]\,\mathsf{P}_{m_{n},h,\vartheta^{l_{s}}\omega}[T_{1}=m_{n}]\,\mathsf{E}_{n-l_{s},h,\vartheta^{l_{s}}\omega}[X_{m_{n}}]\,.

(42)

The second part of Lemma
2.2
entails for
0<a<n

and
\lambda>0

\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda\}}(1-X_{a})\Big]\leq\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{a}}\lambda^{\xi}\,\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{T_{L_{a}}+T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda\}}X_{a}\Big]

with
\xi
given in (
5
), namely

\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}[T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda]\leq\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda\}}X_{a}\Big]+\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{a}}\lambda^{\xi}\,\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{T_{L_{a}}+T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda\}}X_{a}\Big]\,.

This shows that

\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}[M_{n}\leq\lambda]\leq\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}[T_{L_{a}+1}\leq\lambda]\leq(1+\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{a}}\lambda^{\xi})\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{a}]\,,

which trivially holds even for
a=0
and
a=n
. The choice
a=l_{s}
and

\lambda=\{(1+\epsilon)/\mu(h)\}\log n
yields

\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{l_{s}}]\geq\frac{\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}\leq\frac{1+\epsilon}{\mu(h)}\big]}{1+\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{l_{s}}}\big[\frac{1+\epsilon}{\mu(h)}\big]^{\xi}(\log n)^{\xi}}\geq\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{l_{s}}\geq 0\}}\frac{1-\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}>\frac{1+\epsilon}{\mu(h)}\big]}{1+\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h}\big[\frac{1+\epsilon}{\mu(h)}\big]^{\xi}(\log n)^{\xi}}\,.

Lemma
2.2
, through Remark

2.2
, also shows that

\mathsf{E}_{n-l_{s},h,\vartheta^{l_{s}}\omega}[X_{m_{n}}]\geq\frac{1}{1+\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{l_{s}+m_{n}}}m_{n}^{\xi}}\geq\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{l_{s}+m_{n}}\geq 0\}}\frac{1}{1+\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h}m_{n}^{\xi}}\,.

Thus, having a glance at (
36
) and recalling that

\mu(h)\geq\mu_{o}-\delta_{o}
for
h\in(h_{o}-\delta_{o},h_{o}+\delta_{o})
, from
(
42
) we get

\displaystyle\inf_{h\in U_{h_{o},\epsilon}}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[X_{l_{s}}\prod_{k=l_{s}+1}^{l_{s}+m_{n}-1}(1-X_{k})X_{l_{s}+m_{n}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\geq 2\zeta_{n}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{l_{s}}\geq 0,\,\omega_{l_{s}+m_{n}}\geq 0\}}\Bigg(1-\sup_{h\in U_{h_{o},\epsilon}}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}>\frac{1+\epsilon}{\mu(h)}\bigg]\Bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\times\mathrm{e}^{-\delta_{o}m_{n}}\,\mathsf{P}_{m_{n},h_{o},\vartheta^{l_{s}}\omega}[T_{1}=m_{n}]\,,

(43)

where we have defined the numerical factor

\zeta_{n}:=\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{1}{1+\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h_{o}+\delta_{o}}\big(\frac{1+\epsilon}{\mu_{o}-\delta_{o}}\big)^{\xi}(\log n)^{\xi}}\,\frac{1}{1+\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h_{o}+\delta_{o}}m_{n}^{\xi}}\,.

(44)

Now we remark that (
33
) implies that for
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the
upper bound

\sup_{h\in U_{h_{o},\epsilon}}\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[\frac{M_{n}}{\log n}>\frac{1+\epsilon}{\mu(h)}\bigg]\leq\frac{1}{2}

is valid for all sufficiently large
n
. In this way,
(
43
) yields for
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\uparrow\infty}\prod_{s=0}^{r_{n}-1}\Bigg\{1-\inf_{h\in U_{h_{o},\epsilon}}\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\bigg[X_{l_{s}}\prod_{k=l_{s}+1}^{l_{s}+m_{n}-1}(1-X_{k})X_{l_{s}+m_{n}}\bigg]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\limsup_{n\uparrow\infty}\prod_{s=0}^{r_{n}-1}\bigg(1-\zeta_{n}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{l_{s}}\geq 0,\,\omega_{l_{s}+m_{n}}\geq 0\}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\delta_{o}m_{n}}\,\mathsf{P}_{m_{n},h_{o},\vartheta^{l_{s}}\omega}[T_{1}=m_{n}]\bigg)\,.

(45)

In order to demonstrate (
40
) we must prove that the
right-hand side of (
45
) is 0 for
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
. This is
due to the Borel–Cantelli Lemma since we are going to show that

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E_{n}<+\infty
,

E_{n}:=\int_{\Omega}\prod_{s=0}^{r_{n}-1}\bigg(1-\zeta_{n}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{l_{s}}\geq 0,\,\omega_{l_{s}+m_{n}}\geq 0\}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\delta_{o}m_{n}}\,\mathsf{P}_{m_{n},h_{o},\vartheta^{l_{s}}\omega}[T_{1}=m_{n}]\bigg)\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]

being the expectation of the right-hand side of
(
45
). Note that

c:=\int_{\Omega}\mathds{1}_{\{\omega_{0}\geq 0\}}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]>0

since
\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]=0
and that

\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{P}_{m_{n},h_{o},\cdot}[T_{1}=m_{n}]]\geq\mathrm{e}^{-(\mu_{o}+\delta_{o})(m_{n}-1)+\delta_{o}}

for all sufficiently large
n
by Corollary
2.1
. Also note
that all the factors in the right-hand side of
(
45
) are statistically independent as

\mathsf{P}_{m_{n},h_{o},\vartheta^{l_{s}}\omega}[T_{1}=m_{n}]
depends on

\omega:=\{\omega_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
only through the components

\omega_{l_{s}+1},\ldots,\omega_{l_{s}+m_{n}-1}
. Thus, carrying out the
integration and recalling that
m_{n}:=\lfloor\{(1-\epsilon)/(\mu_{o}-\delta_{o})\}\log n\rfloor+1
, for all sufficiently
large
n
we find

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle=\bigg(1-c^{2}\zeta_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-\delta_{o}m_{n}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathsf{P}_{m_{n},h_{o},\cdot}[T_{1}=m_{n}]\Big]\bigg)^{\!r_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(1-c^{2}\zeta_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-(\mu_{o}+2\delta_{o})(m_{n}-1)}\bigg)^{\!r_{n}}\leq\exp\bigg\{\!-c^{2}\zeta_{n}r_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-(\mu_{o}+2\delta_{o})(m_{n}-1)}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Bigg\{\!-c^{2}\frac{\zeta_{n}r_{n}}{n^{(\mu_{o}+2\delta_{o})\big(\frac{1-\epsilon}{\mu_{o}-\delta_{o}}\big)}}\Bigg\}\,.

On the other hand, using that
\delta_{o}\leq\epsilon\mu_{o}/8
by
definition and that
\epsilon\leq 1
we deduce that

(\mu_{o}+2\delta_{o})\frac{1-\epsilon}{\mu_{o}-\delta_{o}}\leq 1-\epsilon\frac{5+2\epsilon}{8-\epsilon}\leq 1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\,.

In conclusion, for all sufficiently large
n
we have

E_{n}\leq\exp\bigg\{\!-c^{2}\frac{\zeta_{n}r_{n}}{n^{1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}}\bigg\}\,,

which proves that
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E_{n}<+\infty
because, bearing
(
38
) and (
44
) in mind, we see that

\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}(\log n)^{2\xi+2}\zeta_{n}r_{n}/n>0
.
∎

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
about the

1.2
for the contact number
L_{n}
. We start with

observed that

\liminf_{n\uparrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]]/n>0
for

The following lemma, which is inspired by

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
h>h_{c}

\liminf_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{var}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]]}{n}>0\,.

Setting
\#_{n}:=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
for brevity, we shall show
that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
h\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\omega:=\{\omega_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\Omega

\mathsf{var}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}]\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\#_{n}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{h+\omega_{2k}}p(1)^{2}p(2)}{[p(2)+\mathrm{e}^{h+\omega_{2k}}p(1)^{2}]^{2}}\,\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{2k-1}X_{2k+1}]

(46)

and that, for
k\in\{1,\ldots,\#_{n}\}
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{2k-1}X_{2k+1}]

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{1+\xi 2^{\xi}\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{2k-1}}}\frac{1}{1+\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{2k+1}}}\Big(\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{2k}]+\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{2k+1}]\Big)\,.

(47)

These two lower bounds prove the lemma as follows. Pick
h>h_{c}
and
recall that

\delta:=\liminf_{n\uparrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]]/n>0
. Let

\lambda>0
be so small that
\mathbb{P}[\Lambda\leq\lambda]\leq\delta/2
,

\Lambda
being the random variable that maps

\omega:=\{\omega_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\Omega
to

\Lambda(\omega):=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{h+\omega_{1}}p(1)^{2}p(2)}{[p(2)+\mathrm{e}^{h+\omega_{1}}p(1)^{2}]^{2}}\frac{1}{1+\xi 2^{\xi}\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{0}}}\frac{1}{1+\xi\mathrm{e}^{-h-\omega_{2}}}\,.

Combining (
46
) with (
47
) we
see that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega\in\Omega

\displaystyle\mathsf{var}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\#_{n}}\Lambda(\vartheta^{2k-1}\omega)\Big(\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{2k}]+\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{2k+1}]\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{\#_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\{\Lambda(\vartheta^{2k-1}\omega)>\lambda\}}\Big(\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{2k}]+\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{2k+1}]\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{\#_{n}}\Big(\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{2k}]+\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{2k+1}]\Big)-2\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{\#_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\{\Lambda(\vartheta^{2k-1}\omega)\leq\lambda\}}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\,\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}]-2\lambda-2\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{\#_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\{\Lambda(\vartheta^{2k-1}\omega)\leq\lambda\}}\,.

Thus, integrating with respect to
\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]
, dividing by

n
, and then sending
n
to infinity we get

\liminf_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{var}_{n,h,\cdot}[L_{n}]]}{n}\geq\lambda\delta-\lambda\mathbb{P}[\Lambda\leq\lambda]\geq\frac{\lambda\delta}{2}>0\,.

The bound (
46
).
Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

h\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\omega:=\{\omega_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\Omega
. Assume that

n\geq 3
, otherwise (
46
) is trivial. Denoting by

the
\sigma

and recalling that

almost surely under the polymer measure, we

\displaystyle\mathsf{var}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}]

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[L_{n}^{2}\big]-\Big(\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}|\mathfrak{O}]\big]\Big)^{\!2}

\displaystyle\geq\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[L_{n}^{2}\big]-\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\big[\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}|\mathfrak{O}]^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Big[\big\{L_{n}-\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[L_{n}|\mathfrak{O}]\big\}^{\!2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Bigg[\bigg\{\sum_{k=1}^{\#_{n}}\big(X_{2k}-\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{2k}|\mathfrak{O}]\big)\bigg\}^{\!2}\Bigg]\,,

so that it suffices to demonstrate that

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}\Bigg[\bigg\{\sum_{k=1}^{\#_{n}}\big(X_{2k}-\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{2k}|\mathfrak{O}]\big)\bigg\}^{\!2}\Bigg|\mathfrak{O}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\#_{n}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{h+\omega_{2k}}p(1)^{2}p(2)}{[p(2)+\mathrm{e}^{h+\omega_{2k}}p(1)^{2}]^{2}}\,X_{2k-1}X_{2k+1}\,.

(48)

takes value 1 since the right-hand side

in this case. Then, suppose that at least two of these

independent thanks to Lemma

On the other hand, when

also

(51)

We obtain (

) by combining (

) with

The bound (
47
).
Lemma

2.1
shows that for
k\in\{1,\ldots,\#_{n}\}

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[X_{2k-1}X_{2k+1}]

so we can write down the inequality

The first step towards Theorem
1.1
is to bound the
derivatives of
\log Z_{n,h}(\omega)
with respect to

by means of
a Birkhoff sum. The following result is due to Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every
h\in\mathbb{R}
,
\omega\in\Omega
, and integers
n\geq 1
and

r\geq 2

\Big|\partial^{r}_{h}\log Z_{n,h}(\omega)\Big|\leq 2^{r}(r!)^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}j^{r}\mathsf{E}_{j+1,h,\vartheta^{i}\omega}^{\otimes 2}\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{j}(1-X_{k}X_{k}^{\prime})\bigg]\,.

Before going into the proof of Lemma
4.2
we recall a few
elementary facts from the theory of cumulants. The cumulant of order

r\in\mathbb{N}
of the contact number
L_{n}
with respect to the polymer

\mathsf{P}_{n,h,\omega}
is the number

{}^{r\!}K_{n,h,\omega}:=\partial_{\lambda}^{r}\log\mathsf{E}_{n,h,\omega}[\mathrm{e}^{\lambda L_{n}}]|_{\lambda=0}
. It can be expanded as

{}^{r\!}K_{n,h,\omega}=\sum_{a_{1}=1}^{n}\cdots\sum_{a_{r}=1}^{n}U_{n,h,\omega}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\,,

(52)

where

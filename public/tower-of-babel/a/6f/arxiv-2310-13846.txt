A stabilizing effect of advection on planar interfaces in singularly perturbed reaction-diffusion equations
Paul Carter

Abstract
We consider planar traveling fronts between stable steady states in two-component singularly perturbed reaction-diffusion-advection equations, where a small quantity δ 2 \displaystyle\delta^{2} represents the ratio of diffusion coefficients. The fronts under consideration are large amplitude and contain a sharp interface, induced by traversing a fast heteroclinic orbit in a suitable slow fast framework. We explore the effect of advection on the spectral stability of the fronts to long wavelength perturbations in two spatial dimensions. We find that for suitably large advection coefficient ν \displaystyle\nu , the fronts are stable to such perturbations, while they can be unstable for smaller values of ν \displaystyle\nu . In this case, a critical asymptotic scaling ν ∼ δ − 4 / 3 \displaystyle\nu\sim\delta^{-4/3} is obtained at which the onset of instability occurs. The results are applied to a family of traveling fronts in a dryland ecosystem model.

Introduction

We consider two-component reaction-diffusion-advection equations of the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}U_{t}&=\Delta U+F(U,V;\boldsymbol{\mu})\\

2.1
Traveling wave formulation

To capture traveling front solutions which propagate in the direction determined by the advection term, we move into a traveling coordinate frame
\displaystyle\xi=x-ct

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}U_{t}&=U_{\xi\xi}+U_{yy}+cU_{\xi}+F(U,V)\\

To analyze traveling front solutions in (

17

\displaystyle\nu,\delta

The following singular perturbation analysis distinguishes between
\displaystyle 3

\displaystyle 0\leq\nu\leq\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-1})

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle\nu\geq\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-2})

\displaystyle\varepsilon:=\nu^{-1}\ll 1

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-1})\leq\nu\leq\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-2})

\displaystyle r:=\delta^{2}\nu
is taken as the primary singular perturbation parameter. By combining the results in these regions, we are able to describe the slow/fast structure of traveling front solutions for each
\displaystyle(\nu,\delta)
satisfying
\displaystyle\nu\geq 0,0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}

\displaystyle\delta_{0}>0

2.2
Long-wave (in)stability of planar interfaces

Given a traveling wave solution
\displaystyle\phi_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi;r,\delta)=(u_{\mathrm{h}},v_{\mathrm{h}})(\xi;\nu,\delta)

2.1

\displaystyle c=c(\nu,\delta)

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi\to\pm\infty}\phi_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi;\nu,\delta)=(U^{\pm},V^{\pm})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda u&=u_{\xi\xi}+cu_{\xi}-\ell^{2}u+F_{u}(u_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi),v_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi))u+F_{v}(u_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi),v_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi))v\\
\lambda v&=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}v_{\xi\xi}+\left(\nu+c\right)v_{\xi}-\frac{\ell^{2}}{\delta^{2}}v+G_{u}(u_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi),v_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi))u+G_{v}(u_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi),v_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi))v,\end{split}

(2.6)

parameterized by the transverse wavenumber
\displaystyle\ell\in\mathbb{R}
, which can equivalently be written as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}+\ell^{2}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}v\end{pmatrix}.

(2.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{\xi\xi}+c\partial_{\xi}+F_{u}(u_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi),v_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi))&F_{v}(u_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi),v_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi))\\

\displaystyle\ell=0

and
\displaystyle(u,v)=(u_{\mathrm{h}}^{\prime},v_{\mathrm{h}}^{\prime})

Assumption 2
.

(1D stability of the front)

satisfies
\displaystyle\mathrm{spec}\{\mathcal{L}\}\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\lambda<0\}\cup\{0\}

\displaystyle\lambda=0

\displaystyle(u_{\mathrm{h}}^{\prime},v_{\mathrm{h}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle|\ell|\ll 1

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(\ell)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\lambda_{\mathrm{c},2}\ell^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\ell^{4}),\qquad\begin{pmatrix}u_{\mathrm{c}}(\xi;\ell)\\

Substituting into (

which leads to the Fredholm solvability condition

where
\displaystyle(u^{A},v^{A})(\xi)

2.3
Summary of results

By considering the slow fast construction of traveling fronts in the weak advection, intermediate, and strong advection regimes, we determine leading order asymptotics for the critical coefficient
\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{c},2}
(
2.14
) which determines long wavelength instabilities along the front interface. We will see that the sign of this coefficient depends only on information encoded in the fast layer orbit
\displaystyle u_{*}(\xi)
of (
2.5
) in the subspace
\displaystyle v=v_{*}
in the singular slow/fast framework. We impose one additional nondegeneracy assumption

Assumption 3
.

(Nondegeneracy condition) The quantity
\displaystyle G(u^{+}_{*},v_{*})-G(u^{-}_{*},v_{*})\neq 0
.

Under Assumptions
1
–
3
, for a traveling front
\displaystyle\phi_{\mathrm{h}}
which traverses a single fast jump
\displaystyle u_{*}(\xi)
of the reduced equation (
2.5
), we obtain an asymptotic long-wavelength stability criterion which holds throughout the weak advection, strong advection, and intermediate regimes. To summarize, letting
\displaystyle r_{0}
denote a sufficiently small fixed positive constant, for sufficiently small
\displaystyle\delta_{0}>0
we find the following asymptotic stability criteria by determining the sign of
\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{c},2}
for
\displaystyle 0<\delta\ll\delta_{0}\ll 1
:

•

Weak advection regime:

\displaystyle 0\leq\nu\leq\frac{1}{\delta}
. In this regime, to leading order

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{sign}(\lambda_{\mathrm{c},2})=-\text{sign}(F_{*})\times\text{sign}(G_{*})

(2.15)

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle F_{*}:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F_{v}(u_{*}(\xi),v_{*})e^{c_{*}\xi}u_{*}^{\prime}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi,\qquad G_{*}:=G(u^{+}_{*},v_{*})-G(u^{-}_{*},v_{*})

(2.16)

and
\displaystyle u^{\pm}_{*}:=\lim_{\xi\to\pm\infty}u_{*}(\xi)
.

•

Intermediate regime:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta}\leq\nu\leq\frac{r_{0}}{\delta^{2}}
. In this regime, to leading order

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{sign}(\lambda_{\mathrm{c},2})=\text{sign}\left(-1+\frac{M}{\delta^{4}\nu^{3}}\right)

(2.17)

where
\displaystyle M
=
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(1)
with respect to
\displaystyle\delta,\nu
, and
\displaystyle\text{sign}(M)=-\text{sign}(F_{*})\times\text{sign}(G_{*})
. In particular, if
\displaystyle M>0
, then to leading order,
\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{c},2}
changes sign when
\displaystyle\nu\sim M^{1/3}\delta^{-4/3}
.

•

Strong advection regime:

\displaystyle\nu\geq\frac{r_{0}}{\delta^{2}}
. Throughout this regime, to leading order we find that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{sign}(\lambda_{\mathrm{c},2})=-1.

(2.18)

The asymptotic estimates are uniform in sufficiently small
\displaystyle\delta
, so that the three regimes collectively describe the parameter region
\displaystyle\{(\delta,\nu):0<\delta\leq\delta_{0},0\leq\nu<\infty\}
for some suitably small choice of
\displaystyle\delta_{0}>0
. In the weak advection regime, the stability of traveling fronts to long wavelength perturbations is encoded purely in the nonlinearities
\displaystyle F
and
\displaystyle G
, evaluated along the fast heteroclinic orbit
\displaystyle u_{*}(\xi)
. This criterion is analogous to that obtained in
[

in the absence of advection, and in the limit
\displaystyle\nu\to 0
the corresponding expression for
\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{c,2}}
agrees with that found in
[

. As
\displaystyle\nu
increases relative to
\displaystyle\delta
, in the intermediate regime, depending on the nonlinearities
\displaystyle F
and
\displaystyle G
and the relative size of
\displaystyle\nu
with respect to
\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{c},2}
occurring at the critical scaling
\displaystyle\nu\sim\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-4/3})
. Finally, in the strong advection regime, all bistable traveling fronts considered here are stable to long wavelength perturbations. In this sense, the presence of advection has a stabilizing effect on the front as a planar interface.

The remainder of the paper is outlined as follows. In §

3
Weak advection and intermediate regimes

Due to the similarity in the slow/fast geometry associated with the weak advection and intermediate regimes, in this section we consider both regimes, and outline the differences in each case. Taken together, we consider the regime
\displaystyle 0\leq\nu\leq\tfrac{r_{0}}{\delta^{2}}
, or equivalently
\displaystyle 0\leq r\leq r_{0}
, where
\displaystyle r_{0}
is a (yet to be fixed) small parameter. Therefore, we are interested in the behavior of the traveling wave equation (
2.2
) when both
\displaystyle\delta
and
\displaystyle r=\delta^{2}\nu
can be taken as small parameters.

This leads to a system with (up to) three timescales, and there is a distinction between the singular limits obtained by taking
\displaystyle\delta\to 0
with
\displaystyle r
fixed, versus
\displaystyle r\to 0
with
\displaystyle\delta
fixed. Hence the case
\displaystyle 0\leq r\leq r_{0}
needs to be split into two subcases: (i)
\displaystyle r=\bar{r}\delta,\bar{r}\leq\bar{r}_{0}
, corresponding to the weak advection regime and (ii)
\displaystyle\delta=\bar{\delta}r,\bar{\delta}\leq\bar{\delta}_{0}
, corresponding to the intermediate regime. We can choose the quantities
\displaystyle\bar{r}_{0},\bar{\delta}_{0}>1
so that these regimes overlap, and we can understand the slow/fast structure of traveling fronts in the entire region
\displaystyle 0\leq r\leq r_{0},0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
for some small
\displaystyle 0<r_{0},\delta_{0}\ll 1
.

We begin by describing the slow/fast structure of traveling fronts in each case in §
3.1
, followed by a computation of the coefficient
\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{c},2}
in §
3.2
.

3.1

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle r

Assumption 4
.

The projection of the manifold
\displaystyle L^{\mathrm{u}}_{-}
from
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0}^{-}

\displaystyle L^{\mathrm{s}}_{+}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{s,u}}(P^{\pm}):=\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{s,u}}(L^{\mathrm{s,u}}_{\pm})\subset\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{s,u}}(\mathcal{M}^{\pm}_{0})

Case
\displaystyle(ii)
:
\displaystyle\delta=\bar{\delta}r,0<\bar{\delta}\leq\bar{\delta}_{0}

We now consider
\displaystyle r\ll 1
and set
\displaystyle\delta=\bar{\delta}r
in (
2.3

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{\xi}&=p\\
p_{\xi}&=-cp-F(u,v)\\
v_{\xi}&=\bar{\delta}rq\\
q_{\xi}&=-r\left((1+\bar{\delta}^{2}rc)q+\bar{\delta}G(u,v)\right).\end{split}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}_{0}

p_{\xi}&=-cp-F(u,v)\\

Setting
\displaystyle\tau=r\xi

\displaystyle r=0
on the manifolds
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\pm}_{0}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}v_{\tau}&=\bar{\delta}^{2}\tilde{q}\\
\tilde{q}_{\tau}&=-\tilde{q}-G(f^{\pm}(v),v),\end{split}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}^{2}

and
\displaystyle L^{\mathrm{s}}_{+}
. Note that in the limit
\displaystyle\bar{\delta}\to 0

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\pm}_{0}=\left\{\tilde{q}=-G(f^{\pm}(v),v)\right\}\subset\mathcal{M}^{\pm}_{0}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}v_{\tilde{\tau}}&=-G(f^{\pm}(v),v)\end{split}

\displaystyle\tilde{\tau}=\bar{\delta}^{2}\tau

, Assumption

\displaystyle\kappa_{\pm}:=G_{u}(f^{\pm}(V^{\pm})(f^{\pm})^{\prime}(V^{\pm})+G_{v}(f^{\pm}(V^{\pm}),V^{\pm})<0

\displaystyle v=V^{\pm}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\pm}_{0}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\pm}_{0}

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}

, and
\displaystyle L^{\mathrm{s}}_{+}
corresponds to the perturbed stable fiber of
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{+}_{\bar{\delta}}
which meets
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{+}_{\bar{\delta}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}v^{-}_{\tau}(\tau)&=-\bar{\delta}^{2}G(f^{-}(v^{-}(\tau)),v^{-}(\tau))+\mathcal{O}(\bar{\delta}^{4}),\quad\tau<0\\

Remark 3.1
.

Assuming
\displaystyle V^{-}>V^{+}
, without loss of generality, we note that to ensure that Assumption
4

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}
, it suffices to assume that
\displaystyle G(f^{-}(v),v)<0
for all
\displaystyle v\in[V^{+},V^{-})
. This connects the structure of the existence problem in the intermediate regime to that in the strong advection regime; see §
4.1
and Assumption

.

Using this construction in the region of small
\displaystyle\bar{\delta}
, we therefore obtain singular heteroclinic orbits for all
\displaystyle 0<\bar{\delta}<\bar{\delta}_{0}

\displaystyle c=c_{*}+\mathcal{O}(r)
for all
\displaystyle 0<r<r_{0}
for some
\displaystyle r_{0}\ll 1
. By choosing
\displaystyle\bar{r}_{0}>1
in §

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}_{0}>1
above, and possibly taking
\displaystyle r_{0}
and/or
\displaystyle\delta_{0}

3.2.1
Case
\displaystyle(i)
:
\displaystyle r=\bar{r}\delta,0\leq\bar{r}\leq\bar{r}_{0}

We consider the fast

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{\xi\xi}-cu_{\xi}+F_{u}(u_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi),v_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi))u+G_{u}(u_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi),v_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi))v&=0\\

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\bar{\delta}^{2}\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F_{v}(u_{*}(\xi),v_{*})e^{c_{*}\xi}u_{*}^{\prime}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi}{G(u^{+}_{*},v_{*})-G(u^{-}_{*},v_{*})}\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\tau}G(f^{-}(v^{-}(\tau)),v^{-}(\tau)^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{c_{*}\xi}u_{*}^{\prime}(\xi)^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi}+\mathcal{O}\left(\bar{\delta}^{4}\right),

Strong advection regime

We now determine the behavior of the system in the strong advection regime for
\displaystyle r\geq r_{0}

\displaystyle r_{0}
is the small constant from §

4.1
Slow/fast structure of the traveling front

We rescale
\displaystyle q=\frac{\delta}{r}\tilde{q}
and obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{\xi}&=p\\
p_{\xi}&=-cp-F(u,v)\\
v_{\xi}&=\varepsilon\tilde{q}\\
\tilde{q}_{\xi}&=r\left(-\left(1+\varepsilon c\right)\tilde{q}-G(u,v)\right),\end{split}

(4.1)

where we recall
\displaystyle\varepsilon:=\nu^{-1}=\frac{\delta^{2}}{r}
. We now consider this equation in the regime
\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and
\displaystyle r\geq r_{0}
. We use
\displaystyle\varepsilon
as the timescale separation parameter, so that
\displaystyle v
is a slow variable, and
\displaystyle(u,p,\tilde{q})
are fast variables. We note that if
\displaystyle r
is large, then
\displaystyle\tilde{q}
evolves on a third (“super” fast) timescale. Setting
\displaystyle\varepsilon=0
defines the layer problem

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{\xi}&=p\\
p_{\xi}&=-cp-F(u,v)\\
\tilde{q}_{\xi}&=-r\left(\tilde{q}+G(u,v)\right),\end{split}

(4.2)

and the critical manifold

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}:=\{p=0,F(u,v)=0,\tilde{q}=-G(u,v)\},

(4.3)

which admits at least two normally hyperbolic branches
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{\pm}=\left\{p=0,u=f^{\pm}(v),\tilde{q}=-G(f^{\pm}(v),v)\right\}
. The reduced flow on these manifolds is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}v_{\eta}&=-G(f^{\pm}(v),v)\end{split}

(4.4)

with respect to
\displaystyle\eta=\varepsilon\xi
. By Assumption
1
,
\displaystyle G_{u}(f^{\pm}(V^{\pm})(f^{\pm})^{\prime}(V^{\pm})+G_{v}(f^{\pm}(V^{\pm}),V^{\pm})<0
, so that the equilibria
\displaystyle v=V^{\pm}
are repelling on
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\pm}_{0}
. We assume the following.

Assumption 5
.

Without loss of generality, we assume
\displaystyle V^{-}>V^{+}
and that
\displaystyle[V^{+},V^{-}]\subseteq I_{v}^{-}
. Furthermore,
\displaystyle G(f^{-}(v),v)<0
for
\displaystyle v\in[V^{+},V^{-})
.

In this case, the unstable manifold of the equilibrium
\displaystyle v=V^{-}
in the reduced flow (

) includes the segment
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{M}}^{-}_{0}\cap\{v^{+}\leq v<v^{-}\}
, consisting of a single solution; we denote by
\displaystyle v=v^{-}(\eta)

\displaystyle v^{-}(0)=v^{+}

\displaystyle v=V^{+}
in the reduced flow (

) on

in the full system, it is necessary for the fast jump to occur in the subspace

(4.5)

The associated

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{M}}^{+}_{0}

To make this construction uniform in the limit

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle r\geq r_{0}

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle\delta\leq\delta_{0}

4.2

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{c},2}

We now consider the fronts from §
4.1
, using
\displaystyle\varepsilon
as the timescale separation parameter. We consider the fast

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{\xi\xi}-cu_{\xi}+F_{u}(u_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi),v_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi))u+G_{u}(u_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi),v_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi))v&=0\\

(4.7)

and slow

(4.8)

formulations of the adjoint equation, where
\displaystyle\eta=\varepsilon\xi
. Proceeding as in §
3.2.1
, in the fast field, to leading order
\displaystyle u_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi)=u_{*}(\xi),v_{\mathrm{h}}(\xi)=v_{*}
, and
\displaystyle c=c_{*}
, so that
\displaystyle v=\bar{v}_{*}=
constant and
\displaystyle u
satisfies

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{\xi\xi}-c_{*}u_{\xi}+F_{u}(u_{*}(\xi),v_{*})u&=-G_{u}(u_{*}(\xi),v_{*})\bar{v}_{*},\end{split}

which implies that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}0=\bar{v}_{*}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G_{u}(u_{*}(\xi),v_{*})u_{*}^{\prime}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi=\bar{v}_{*}\left(G(u_{*}^{+},v_{*})-G(u_{*}^{-},v_{*})\right),\end{split}

\displaystyle G(u_{*}^{+},v_{*})\neq G(u_{*}^{-},v_{*})
so that
\displaystyle\bar{v}_{*}=0

\displaystyle u=\alpha_{*}u_{*}^{\prime}(\xi)e^{c_{*}\xi}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{u}(f^{-}(v^{-}),v^{-})u+G_{u}(f^{-}(v^{-}),v^{-})v&=0\\

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}-v_{\eta}+\left(G_{v}(f^{-}(v^{-}),v^{-})-F_{v}(f^{-}(v^{-}),v^{-})\frac{G_{u}(f^{-}(v^{-}),v^{-})}{F_{u}(f^{-}(v^{-}),v^{-})}\right)v&=0,\end{split}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{c},2}

6
Discussion

In this work, we examined the effect of advection on the stability of planar fronts in two spatial dimensions, motivated by observations of planar interfaces between desert and vegetated states in water limited ecosystems. In particular, we demonstrated a stabilizing mechanism of advection on a critical eigenvalue associated with long wavelength perturbations. In the context of application to dryalnd ecosystem models, this matches the common observation that interfaces between vegetation and bare soil present in vegetation patterns tend to align perpendicular to sloped terrain, on sufficiently steep slopes. The stability criteria posed in §
2.3
are model-independent, which allows for straightforward application of the results to a wide class of reaction-diffusion-advection models, under modest assumptions.

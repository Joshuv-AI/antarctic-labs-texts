Infinite Limits of Multi-head Transformer Dynamics
Blake Bordelon, Hamza Chaudhry, Cengiz Pehlevan

Abstract
In this work, we analyze various scaling limits of the training dynamics of transformer models in the feature learning regime. We identify the set of parameterizations that admit well-defined infinite width and depth limits, allowing the attention layers to update throughout training–a relevant notion of feature learning in these models. We then use tools from dynamical mean field theory (DMFT) to analyze various infinite limits (infinite key/query dimension, infinite heads, and infinite depth) which have different statistical descriptions depending on which infinite limit is taken and how attention layers are scaled. We provide numerical evidence of convergence to the limits and discuss how the parameterization qualitatively influences learned features.

3.1
Mean Field Theory Treatment of the Learning Dynamics

To obtain the exact infinite limits of interest when scaling dimension-per-head
N
, the number of heads
\mathcal{H}
, or the depth
L
to infinty, we work with a tool from statistical physics known as dynamical mean field theory (DMFT). Classically, this method has been used to analyze high dimensional disordered systems such as spin glasses, random recurrent neural networks, or learning algorithms with high dimensional random data
[
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
]
. Following
[
15
,
11
]
, we use this method to reason about the limiting dynamics of randomly initialized neural networks by tracking a set of deterministic correlation functions (feature and gradient kernels) as well as additional linear-response functions (see Appendix
D
). The core conceptual idea of this method is that in the infinite limit and throughout training, all neurons remain statistically independent and only interact through collective variables (feature kernels, neural network outputs, etc). Further the collective variables can be computed as
averages
over distribution of neurons in each hidden layer or along the residual stream. This DMFT description can be computed using a path integral method that tracks the moment generating function of the preactivations or with a dynamical cavity method (see Appendix
D
).

[

Collapse of Attention Heads

As
N\to\infty
, multi-head self-attention will effectively compute the same outputs as a single-head self-attention block. We theoretically derive this effect in Appendix
E.2.1
and demonstrate it empirically in Figure
2

\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}=1
(
\mu
P) transformers trained for
2500
steps on CIFAR-5M
[

]
, the variance of attention matrices across heads decreases with
N
. However, we note that if the scaling exponent is chosen instead as
\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}=\frac{1}{2}
there is non-decreasing diversity of attention variables across heads. This result is consistent with recent empirical findings that attention variables in
\mu
P transformers converge to the same limiting quantities at large
N

\mathcal{H}
fixed for different initializations and also across model sizes

. This aspect of transformers in the large-

limit is potentially undesirable as some tasks could require learning multiple attention mechanisms. Furthermore, this suggests scaling the model in this limit could increase computational cost without improving performance. To circumvent this, we explore if there exist well defined limits at finite
N
with a diversity of attention variables across heads.

with the inner dimension
N
fixed. Rather than having all kernels concentrate, the kernel of each head of the MHSA block follows a statistically independent stochastic process. This picture was shown to hold at initialization by
Hron et al. [18]
. Here, using a DMFT analysis, we show that it continues to hold throughout training in the feature learning regime.

Result 2
(Infinite Head Limit)

(
Informal
)
The
\mathcal{H}\to\infty
limit of SGD training dynamics in a randomly initialized transformer at any key/query dimension
N
, scaling exponents
\alpha_{\mathcal{A}},\alpha_{L}
, and any depth
L
is governed by head-averaged kernels for pairs of input data
\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}
at training times
t,t^{\prime}
and spatial/token positions
\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
such as

\displaystyle{V}^{\ell,\sigma}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N\mathcal{H}}\sum_{\mathfrak{h}=1}^{\mathcal{H}}\bm{v}^{\ell\sigma}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\bm{v}^{\ell\sigma}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

(9)

which converge to deterministic values as
\mathcal{H}\to\infty
. The attention variables
\{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t),\bm{q}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t),\bm{v}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\bm{x},t),\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)\}
within each head become statistically independent across heads and decouple in their dynamics (but not across dimensions within a head). Each residual stream neuron becomes independent and obeys a single site stochastic process analogous to Result
1
, but with different kernels.

We derive this and the full DMFT in Appendix
E.3
, showing that the joint distribution of head-averaged dynamical quantities satisfies a large deviation principle and the limit can be derived as a saddle point of a DMFT action.

3.4
Infinite Depth Limits

We next describe the infinite depth limits which depend on the choice of
\alpha_{L}
. Below we informally describe the main finding which again uses a DMFT formalism and is based on analyses in recent works on infinite depth networks from
Bordelon et al. [11]
and
Yang et al. [12]
.

Result 3
(Infinite Depth Limit)

(
Informal
)
The training dynamics for
\mathcal{H},L\to\infty
with
L^{-\alpha_{L}}
branch scaling with
\alpha_{L}\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
is described by a differential equation for residual variables
h_{\mathfrak{s}}(\tau,t)
in layer time
\tau=\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{\ell}{L}
for the residual stream

\displaystyle h_{\mathfrak{s}}(\tau,\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle=\beta_{0}\ \delta_{\alpha_{L},\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau}du_{\mathfrak{s}}(\tau^{\prime},\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle\qquad+\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}\beta_{0}^{2}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\int d\bm{x}^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}C_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime},\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})g_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

(10)

where the Brownian motion term
du_{\mathfrak{s}}(\tau,\bm{x},t)
survives in the limit only if
\alpha_{L}=\frac{1}{2}
and has covariance

\displaystyle\left<du_{\mathfrak{s}}(\tau,\bm{x},t)du_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right>=\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\left[\Phi_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})+V^{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\right]

(11)

and the deterministic kernel
C_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})
can be expressed in terms of head-averaged kernels and response functions. The weights inside each hidden MHSA layer or each MLP layer are frozen in the
L\to\infty
limit unless
\alpha_{L}=1
. All response functions are suppressed at
L\to\infty
unless
\alpha_{L}=\frac{1}{2}
.

Below we provide a couple of short comments about this result. The proof and full DMFT is provided in Appendix
E.4
.

1.

At initialization
t=0
, the only term which contributes to the residual stream layer dynamics is the integrated Brownian motion
\int_{0}^{\tau}du(\tau^{\prime})
which survives at infinite depth for
\alpha_{L}=\frac{1}{2}
. For
\alpha_{L}=1
this term disappears in the limit. The structure of
C(\tau)
is also modified by additional response functions at
\alpha_{L}=\frac{1}{2}

[
11
]
which we show disappear for
\alpha_{L}=1
.

2.

The weights within residual blocks (including the MHSA block) can be treated as completely frozen for
\alpha_{L}<1
in the
L\to\infty
limit, which leads to the simplified statistical description of the preactivations in those layers. However, the residual stream variables
h(\tau)
do still obtain
\Theta_{L}(1)
updates. At
\alpha_{L}=1
the weights in the MHSA blocks evolve by
\Theta_{L}(1)
, causing additional feature evolution in the model.

3.

A consequence of our large
N
and large
L
result is that the
N,L\to\infty
limit with
\alpha_{L}=\frac{1}{2}
(the parameterization studied by
[
11
,
12
]
) would lead to
\mathcal{A}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)=0
for all time
t
. Thus the MHSA blocks would only involve average pooling operations over the spatial indices, despite the residual stream kernels
H^{\ell}
updating from feature learning.

In practice, large scale neural networks do not generally operate close to their limit. Given the costs of training large networks, one would ideally operate in a regime where there is a guarantee of consistent improvements with respect to model scale. In pursuit of this goal, we apply our theoretical findings of this paper to training language models on a larger natural language dataset, a Transformer with causal attention blocks trained on the C4 dataset
[

]
with Adam optimizer. As mentioned in
2.2
, while our exact theoretical description of these infinite limits focus on SGD, we can implement an appropriate scaling for Adam which preserves the scale of internal feature updates. This allows us to investigate realistic training dynamics of our LLM as we take the

limits. Training details are provided in Appendix

and

Taken together, these results suggest that scaling different model dimensions do indeed have different effects on training dynamics and final performance. This provides groundwork for future large-scale experiments systematically investigating their trade-offs, thereby identifying compute-optimal scaling of realistic architectures in parameterizations with well-defined limits.

5
Discussion

This paper provided analysis of the infinite head, depth and key/query dimension limits of transformer training in the feature learning regime. We showed that feature learning in
\mu
P multi-head transformers in the limit of
N\to\infty
collapses to single-head self-attention. At finite
N
and infinite heads
\mathcal{H}\to\infty
we showed that there is an alternative limit which maintains a
distribution over attention heads
. We discussed two different large depth limits of transformer training that reduce to differential equations in the residual layer time
\tau
. The depth scaling that maintains feature learning within all MHSA blocks (
\alpha_{L}=1
) causes the initial kernel to lose structure from the initialization as
L\to\infty
, but allows learning of the self-attention variables, whereas the depth scaling that preserves structure from initialization (
\alpha_{L}=\frac{1}{2}
) leads to static layers.

Limitations and Future Directions

Currently exact theoretical analysis of the limit is focused on SGD (and can be easily extended to SGD+momentum
[
15
]
) while Adam is currently only reasoned with rough scaling arguments rather than an exact theoretical description of the limit. Since Adam is most commonly used to train transformers, a theory of the limiting dynamics of Adam in Transformers would be an important future extension. In addition, while we provide an exact asymptotic description of network training, the limiting equations are compute intensive for realistic settings which is why we focus our empirical investigations on training large width networks in the appropriate parameterizations. Lastly our techniques assume that the number of training steps is fixed as the scaling parameters of interest
(N,\mathcal{H},L)
are taken to infinity. However, it would be important to understand learning dynamics in the regime where model size and training times are chosen to balance a compute optimal tradeoff (or perhaps even training longer than compute optimal)
[
8
,
39
,
44
]
. In this regime, harmful finite model-size effects become significant and comparable to the finite training horizon
[
10
,
16
,
17
,
39
]
. Thus stress testing the ideas in this work at larger scales and longer training runs would be an important future direction of research into scaling transformer models.

Appendix C
Simple Heuristic Scaling Analysis

In this section, we heuristically work out the simple scaling analysis to justify the set of parameterizations and learning rates we consider. More detailed theoretical analysis for the limit of SGD training is provided in Appendix
E
where we exactly characterize the
N\to\infty
,
\mathcal{H}\to\infty
and
L\to\infty
limits. We consider taking heads
\mathcal{H}
, inner dimension
N
and depth
L
to infinity separately and attempt to control the scale of gradients and updates.

C.1
Learning Rate Scalings

We show that the correct learning rate scaling for SGD is
\eta=\eta_{0}N\mathcal{H}L^{2\alpha_{L}-1}
. For Adam, the learning rate should be scaled as
\eta=\eta_{0}N^{-1/2}\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}L^{-1+\alpha_{L}}
.

SGD

C.2
Heuristic Analysis of Feature Changes Under SGD

In this section we consider performing a single update on a single example to all weight matrices.

\displaystyle\delta\bm{W}^{\ell}_{O\mathfrak{h}}\sim\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\bm{v}^{\ell\top}_{\mathfrak{h}}

(12)

where
\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\mathcal{H}}
and
\bm{v}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
have
\Theta(1)
entries. Thus, computing a perturbation to the forward pass we find

\displaystyle\delta\bm{h}^{\ell+1}=\delta\bm{h}^{\ell}+\frac{1}{L^{\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{h}=1}^{\mathcal{H}}\left(\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\bm{v}^{\ell\top}_{\mathfrak{h}}\right)\bm{v}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta\bm{h}^{\ell}+\frac{1}{L}\left[\frac{1}{N\mathcal{H}}\sum_{\mathfrak{h}}\bm{v}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}\cdot\bm{v}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}\right]\bm{g}^{\ell+1}

(13)

The term in the brackets is
\Theta(1)
and we see that the perturbation from each layer contributes
\Theta(L^{-1})
. As there are
L
layers, this will give a total change to the final layer
h^{L}
that is
\Theta(1)
.

For the Attention variables, we note that the

\displaystyle\delta\bm{W}^{\ell}_{K\mathfrak{h}}\sim\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{q}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}\bm{h}^{\ell\top}\ ,\ \delta\bm{W}^{\ell}_{Q\mathfrak{h}}\sim\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{k}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}\bm{h}^{\ell\top}

(14)

where
\bm{q}_{\mathfrak{h}},\bm{k}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
are the query and key for head
\mathfrak{h}
and
\bm{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\mathcal{H}}
is the residual stream preactivation. We can thus compute the changes to the keys and queries due to changes in their associated weights

\displaystyle\delta\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{N^{\frac{3}{2}-\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}}\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}}\left(\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{q}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}\bm{h}^{\ell\top}\right)\bm{h}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}N^{1-\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}}}\bm{q}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}H^{\ell}

\displaystyle\delta\bm{q}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{N^{\frac{3}{2}-\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}}\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}}\left(\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{k}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}\bm{h}^{\ell\top}\right)\bm{h}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}N^{1-\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}}}\bm{q}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}H^{\ell}.

(15)

where
H^{\ell}=\frac{1}{N\mathcal{H}}\bm{h}^{\ell}\cdot\bm{h}^{\ell}\sim\Theta(1)
. Combining these changes , we find the following update to the pre-Attention variables
\mathcal{A}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}=\frac{1}{N^{\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}}}\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell}\cdot\bm{q}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{A}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}N}\bm{q}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}\cdot\bm{q}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}H^{\ell}+\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}N}\bm{k}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}\cdot\bm{k}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}H^{\ell}+\frac{1}{L^{2-2\alpha_{L}}N^{2-2\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}}}\mathcal{A}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}(H^{\ell})^{2}

\displaystyle=\Theta(L^{-1+\alpha_{L}}),

(16)

since
\frac{1}{N}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{k},\frac{1}{N}\bm{q}\cdot\bm{q}\sim\Theta(1)
. This update to the attention variable due to changes in
\bm{W}^{\ell}_{K},\bm{W}^{\ell}_{Q}
will clearly die out as
L\to\infty
unless
\alpha_{L}=1
.

C.3
Heuristic Analysis of Feature Changes Under Adam

For Adam, the gradient of each individual parameter entry is approximately normalized by its scale
[
45
]
. Thus the learning rate
\eta
sets the size of the updates. This is why we scale the learning rate as
\eta=\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}
which gives the same scale updates to the weights as SGD

\displaystyle\delta\bm{W}^{\ell}_{O\mathfrak{h}}\approx\eta\ \bm{g}^{\ell+1}\bm{v}^{\ell\top}_{\mathfrak{h}}=\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\bm{v}^{\ell\top}_{\mathfrak{h}}

(17)

Again computing the correction to the forward pass we find

\displaystyle\delta\bm{h}^{\ell+1}=\delta\bm{h}^{\ell}+\frac{1}{L^{\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{h}=1}^{\mathcal{H}}\left(\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\bm{v}^{\ell\top}_{\mathfrak{h}}\right)\bm{v}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta\bm{h}^{\ell}+\frac{1}{L}\left[\frac{1}{N\mathcal{H}}\sum_{\mathfrak{h}}\bm{v}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}\cdot\bm{v}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}\right]\bm{g}^{\ell+1}=\Theta(1)

(18)

Similarly our update generates the same scale of weight updates to the key and query weight matrices

\displaystyle\delta\bm{W}^{\ell}_{K\mathfrak{h}}\sim\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{q}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}\bm{h}^{\ell\top}\ ,\ \delta\bm{W}^{\ell}_{Q\mathfrak{h}}\sim\frac{1}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{k}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}\bm{h}^{\ell\top}

(19)

We can therefore follow the identical argument to identify the scale of the change to the pre-attention variables
\delta\mathcal{A}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}=\Theta(L^{1-\alpha_{L}})
.

C.4
What Counts as Feature Learning for Attention Layers?

Any parameterization with
\alpha_{N}\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
will cause all updates to
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell}
and entries of
\bm{h}^{\ell+1}
to be
\Theta_{N,H,L}(1)
across finite
N
. The entries of
\bm{q}
and
\bm{k}
only move by
\Theta_{N}(1)
if
\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}=1
(
\mu
P scaling). However, we argue that this criterion is not strictly necessary. Rather, feature learning could alternatively be defined in terms of evolution of macroscopic variables (

Appendix D
DMFT Primer and Simple Examples

D.1
Main Conceptual Idea of the Approach

Dynamical mean field theory is a method that was developed in the physics of spin glasses for dealing with dynamical systems that depend on a
fixed source of disorder
. The disorder could be random couplings between sites in a spin glass model
[
34
]
, random connections between neurons in a random recurrent neural network
[
35
]
, random data drawn from a distribution
[
37
,
39
]
or the random initial weights in a deep neural network
[
15
,
11
]
. In our case, we are interested in the last example, where the feature learning dynamics of a randomly initialized transformer is a function of the initial weights in each layer. In what follows, we will give a primer on the main objects which typically appear in a DMFT analysis (the correlation and response functions) to illustrate the main ideas of the approach.

D.2
Example 1: Linear Dynamics with GOE Matrix

In this section, we discuss and derive the DMFT equations for the simplest possible example, a linear dynamical system with a Gaussian symmetric coupling matrix.

In this example we show that the DMFT path integral is computing something non-trivial about the kinds of dynamics induced by a linear dynamical system with a random matrix. In this linear example, the DMFT path integral encodes spectral properties of the random matrix.

Let’s consider the simplest possible example:
\frac{d}{dt}h_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}W_{ij}h_{j}(t)
where
W_{ij}=W_{ji}
is a Gaussian symmetric matrix (GOE). This matrix is
fixed
while the state
\bm{h}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
evolves. The path integral appraoch would tell you that in the
N\to\infty
limit, every neuron
i
has identical statistics given by the stochastic integro-differential equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h(t)=u(t)+\int_{0}^{t}dsR(t,s)h(s)\ ,\ u(t)\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,C(t,s))

\displaystyle C(t,s)=\left<h(t)h(s)\right>\ ,\ R(t,s)=\left<\frac{\delta h(t)}{\delta u(s)}\right>

(20)

where
\left<\cdot\right>
denotes an average over the random variables
u(t)
. In this picture, the averages
\left<\right>
over the noise can also be interpreted as averages over all
N
neurons in the system, each of which are independent. This stochastic equation can be used to close the evolution equations for the correlation
C(t,s)
and linear response function
R(t,s)
.

A generic result of this path integral DMFT picture is

1.

All neurons (all variables
h_{i}
) decouple statistically. The presence of all other neurons only enters through "macroscopic" quantities
C(t,s)
and
R(t,s)
known as the correlation and response functions. The distribution of these functions over random realizations satisfies a large deviations principle where the distribution over
C,R
has the form
p(C,R)\sim e^{-NS(C,R)}
where
S
is the DMFT action obtained from the path integral method.

2.

Extra
memory terms
like
\int_{0}^{t}R(t,s)h(s)
appear which depend on the state at earlier times
s<t
. The Markovian (deterministic) system for
p(\bm{h}|\bm{W})
becomes stochastic and non-markovian after marginalizing
p(\bm{h})=\int d\bm{W}p(\bm{h}|\bm{W})p(\bm{W})
. I would argue these memory terms are not obvious apriori but are systematic to compute in this framework.

Since this toy example is a
linear dynamical system
, one can also identify a connection between the DMFT correlation and response and spectral properties of the random matrix
\bm{W}
. We note that the response has the form

\displaystyle R(t,s)=\frac{1}{N}\text{Tr}\exp\left(W(t-s)\right)=\int d\lambda\rho(\lambda)e^{\lambda(t-s)}

(21)

where
\rho(\lambda)
is the eigenvalue density of
W
. In fact a Fourier transform of our DMFT equation recovers the semicircle law
\rho(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi}\text{Im}R(i\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{[4-\lambda^{2}]_{+}}
for the eigenvalues.

In general, one can think of DMFT as a more powerful version of this method that can also handle nonlinearities.

D.3
Example 2: Deep Linear Network Updates

In this section I will try showing how this DMFT approach can give useful insights into reasoning about learning updates which are not obvious apriori. While our paper advocates for taking depth
L\to\infty
in a residual network, we first thought about simply scaling depth in a standard MLP. Below we show how the proliferation of response terms gives a different predicted scaling with
L
than if we naively disregarded response terms.

Consider a non-residual linear MLP network with
\mu
P/mean-field scaling with
L
hidden layers with
N\to\infty
. Train the model for a single step of gradient descent with learning rate
\eta
on a data point
(x,y)
with
|x|^{2}=1
and
y=1
and output multiplier
1/\gamma_{0}
. The forward pass variables
\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)
and the backward pass variables
\bm{g}^{\ell}(t)
are defined recursively as

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell+1}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}H^{\ell}(t,s)\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(s)

(22)

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{\ell}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(t)+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}G^{\ell+1}(t,s)\bm{h}^{\ell+1}(s)

(23)

Now, naively, one may think that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)
has entries that are independently Gaussian with covariance
H^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})
. However, this is incorrect and the
DMFT response functions
give an additional correction.

The DMFT Equations for this Model

Following the approach of
[
15
,
11
]
, we find the following DMFT equations for the preactivations
h^{\ell}
after
1
step of training

\displaystyle h^{\ell}(0)=u^{\ell}(0)\ ,\ g^{\ell}(0)=r^{\ell}(0)

\displaystyle h^{\ell}(1)=u^{\ell}(1)+A^{\ell-1}(1,0)g^{\ell}(0)+\eta\gamma_{0}H^{\ell-1}(1,0)g^{\ell}(0)

(24)

where the random variables
u^{\ell}(0),u^{\ell}(1)
and
r^{\ell}(0)
have the following covariance structure

\displaystyle\left<u^{\ell}(0)u^{\ell}(0)\right>=H^{\ell-1}(0,0)\ ,\ \left<u^{\ell}(1)u^{\ell}(0)\right>=H^{\ell-1}(1,0)\ ,\ \left<u^{\ell}(1)u^{\ell}(1)\right>=H^{\ell-1}(1,1)

\displaystyle\left<r^{\ell}(0)r^{\ell}(0)\right>=G^{\ell+1}(0,0),

(25)

and the feature kernels
H^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})
,
G^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})
and response functions
A^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})
have the form

\displaystyle H^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})=\left<h^{\ell}(t)h^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\right>\ ,\ G^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})=\left<g^{\ell}(t)g^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\right>

\displaystyle A^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})=\left<\frac{\delta h^{\ell}(t)}{\delta r^{\ell}(t^{\prime})}\right>

(26)

These recursions can be solved with the initial conditions
H^{\ell}(0,0)=1
and
G^{\ell}(0,0)=1
which implies that
H^{\ell}(1,0)=1
so that

\displaystyle A^{\ell}(1,0)=\eta\gamma_{0}+A^{\ell-1}(1,0)=\ell\eta\gamma_{0}

(27)

Using this equation, we find

\displaystyle H^{\ell}(1,1)=\left<h^{\ell}(1)h^{\ell}(1)\right>=H^{\ell-1}(1,1)+\eta^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}\ell^{2}=1+\eta^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}k^{2}

(28)

This is the DMFT prediction for the scale of the feature kernels after a step of training.

Neglecting the DMFT Response Gives Incorrect Depth Scalings for MLPs

However, if we had neglected the DMFT response functions and approximated the dynamics as

\displaystyle H^{\ell}(1,1)=H^{\ell-1}(1,1)+\eta^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}\implies H^{\ell}(1,1)=1+\eta^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}\ \ell

(29)

The feature variance after
t=1
step of gradient descent
H^{\ell}=\left<h^{\ell}(1)^{2}\right>
after
t=1
step, the final layer

\displaystyle H^{L}\sim\begin{cases}1+\frac{1}{3}\eta^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}\ L^{3}&\text{DMFT Response Included (Full DMFT)}\\
1+\eta^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}\ L&\text{DMFT Response Neglected }\end{cases}

(30)

We see that without the response terms we get a completely different scaling prediction with
L
!

Scaled Residual Networks

For
\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}
residual block scaling (
\alpha_{L}=\frac{1}{2}
), the response functions are still important to accurately characterize the dynamics and contribute
\Theta_{L}(1)
corrections to the feature learning dynamics as
L\to\infty
. However, for the
1/L
block multiplier scaling, the response functions do not contribute in the limit. These facts are
not-apriori obvious
but follow from the DMFT analysis (either path integral or cavity approach).

In this section, we derive the limiting equations for the infinite head limit

E.1
Deriving the DMFT Action

In this section we will derive the limiting equations of motion for stochastic gradient descent in the
\mathcal{H}\to\infty
limit. We start by defining the loss function which we aim to minimize

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\int d\bm{x}\ p(\bm{x})\ \ell[f(\bm{x})]

(31)

where
p(\bm{x})
is the data distribution of interest. We note that this can be the population loss or the empirical loss on a finite collection of points. We let
\Delta(x,t)\equiv-\frac{\partial\ell[f(\bm{x},t)]}{\partial f(\bm{x},t)}
represent the error signal on datapoint
\bm{x}
. At each step of training
t
a batch of examples
\mathfrak{B}_{t}=\{\bm{x}_{1}(t),...,\bm{x}_{|\mathfrak{B}_{t}|}(t)\}
is generated and used to estimate a gradient for SGD. In what follows, we let
\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\mathfrak{B}_{t}}
represent averages over the minibatch at time
t

\mathfrak{B}_{t}
are assumed to be given or fixed and are not averaged over as random draws from
p(\bm{x})
, but rather our expectation simply denotes the empirical mean over the minibatch at time
t

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\mathfrak{B}_{t}}[f(\bm{x})]=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{B}_{t}|}\sum_{\bm{x}\in\mathfrak{B}_{t}}f(\bm{x}).

(32)

We start by expressing again the forward pass equations for each layer. To make this analysis more compressed while still capturing all of the interesting aspects, we will first compute the equations of motion in the absence of MLP layers (which were analyzed in prior works, see Appendix
E
) and layernorm (which in the limit it will only apply a deterministic affine transformation to each of the entries of the residual stream and the backward pass gradient variables as we will show explicitly in Appendix
E.6
). In the absence of layernorm, our forward pass has the form

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)=\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)+\frac{\beta_{0}}{L^{\alpha_{L}}}\text{MHSA}\left(\bm{h}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)\right)_{\mathfrak{s}}

(33)

where the MHSA layer is

\displaystyle\text{MHSA}\left(\bm{h}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)\right)_{\mathfrak{s}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{h}\in[\mathcal{H}]}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}\in[\mathcal{S}]}\bm{o}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)\sigma^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle\bm{o}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}_{O\mathfrak{h}}(t)\bm{v}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)

(34)

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{W}^{\ell}_{V\mathfrak{h}}(t){\bm{h}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)=\sigma\left(\bm{\mathcal{A}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\bm{x},t)\right)_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\ ,\ \mathcal{A}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)=\frac{1}{N^{\alpha_{\mathcal{A}}}}\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\bm{q}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)

(35)

To compute the weight dynamics we again introduce the necessary gradient fields which we previously argued have
\Theta(1)
entries

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\equiv\gamma_{0}N\mathcal{H}\frac{\partial f(\bm{x},t)}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}

(36)

We also introduce the following intermediate quantities which are necessary to characterize the backward pass through the attention layer

\displaystyle{M}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)\equiv\frac{1}{N\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}}\ {\bm{g}}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\bm{o}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}(\bm{x},t)\equiv\frac{\partial\sigma^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)}{\partial\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)}

(37)

We need to break up each of the weight matrices into their initial component and their update from SGD

\displaystyle\bm{W}^{\ell}_{O\mathfrak{h}}(t)=\bm{W}^{\ell}_{O\mathfrak{h}}(0)+\frac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\mathfrak{B}_{t^{\prime}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}}\Delta(\bm{x},t^{\prime})\tilde{\bm{g}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t^{\prime})\bm{v}^{\ell\sigma}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t^{\prime})^{\top}

\displaystyle\bm{W}^{\ell}_{V\mathfrak{h}}(t)=\bm{W}^{\ell}_{V\mathfrak{h}}(0)+\frac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\mathfrak{B}_{t^{\prime}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\Delta(\bm{x},t^{\prime})\sigma^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\tilde{\bm{g}}^{\ell}_{O\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t^{\prime})\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t^{\prime})^{\top}

\displaystyle\bm{W}^{\ell}_{K\mathfrak{h}}(t)=\bm{W}^{\ell}_{K\mathfrak{h}}(0)+\frac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\mathfrak{B}_{t^{\prime}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}\Delta(\bm{x},t^{\prime})M_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t^{\prime})\dot{\sigma}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}\bm{q}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t^{\prime}){\bm{h}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t^{\prime})^{\top}

MHSA Layer

We will start by differentiating through the MHSA layer

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\partial\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)^{\top}}\right)^{\top}{\bm{g}}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ {\bm{g}}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)+\frac{\beta_{0}}{L^{\alpha_{L}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)^{\top}}\text{MHSA}(\bm{h}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t))_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\right)^{\top}{\bm{g}}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)

\Theta(1)

E.2.1
Multi-head Attention is Single-Head Attention as
N\to\infty

In this section, we use the derived saddle point equations for the
N\to\infty
limit and argue that they imply that all heads in the MHSA layer learn identical attention matrices and contribute the same feature updates to the residual stream. To do so, we proceed in a three step inductive argument.

1.

First, we show that at initialization, all key, query, value and attention matrices
\{Q_{\mathfrak{h}},K_{\mathfrak{h}},V_{\mathfrak{h}},\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{h}},M_{\mathfrak{h}}\}
are equal across heads.

2.

Next, we show inductively that if these quantities
\{Q_{\mathfrak{h}},K_{\mathfrak{h}},V_{\mathfrak{h}},\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{h}},M_{\mathfrak{h}}\}
are identical across heads up to some time, then that implies that the response functions
R_{\mathfrak{h}}
are also identical across heads up to that time.

3.

Lastly, we show that if the response functions
R_{\mathfrak{h}}
are identical up to some time, then that implies that the MHSA kernels
\{Q_{\mathfrak{h}},K_{\mathfrak{h}},V_{\mathfrak{h}},\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{h}},M_{\mathfrak{h}}\}
will also be identical across heads at future times.

First, we note that, at initialization, all of the MHSA kernels are identical across heads since

\displaystyle\forall\ \mathfrak{h}\in[\mathcal{H}]\quad Q^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},0,0)=K^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},0,0)=V_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},0,0)=H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},0,0)

\displaystyle M^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},0)=A^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},0)=0.

(69)

Next, we need to analyze the response functions under an inductive hypothesis on the equality of the MHSA kernels. We start by noting that all response functions are causal so we can group the response functions that arise from
\chi^{\ell}_{O\mathfrak{h}}
with the feature learning update to the residual stream, writing the following compressed equation for the forward and backward passes

\displaystyle h^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle=h^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{h}=1}^{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}u^{\ell}_{O\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)\sigma^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle+\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}\beta_{0}^{2}L^{-1}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\int d\bm{x}^{\prime}C^{h^{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})g^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

\displaystyle g^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle=g^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}r^{\ell}_{V\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)\sigma^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}r^{\ell}_{Q\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)\dot{\sigma}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)M_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}}r^{\ell}_{K\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}}(\bm{x},t)\dot{\sigma}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}}(\bm{x},t)M_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle+\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}\beta_{0}^{2}L^{-1}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\int d\bm{x}^{\prime}C^{g^{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})h^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

where
C^{h^{\ell}}
and
C^{g^{\ell}}
only involve deterministic
head-averaged
kernels and thus do not carry a
\mathfrak{h}
index. We now derive useful response function identities

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}\frac{\delta h^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}{\delta u^{\ell^{\prime}}_{O\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})}=\Theta(\ell-\ell^{\prime})\delta(t-t^{\prime})\delta(\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime})\sigma^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle+\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}\beta_{0}^{2}L^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\sum_{t^{\prime\prime}<t}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}\int d\bm{x}^{\prime\prime}C^{h^{k}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime\prime},t,t^{\prime\prime})\left(\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}\frac{\delta g^{k+1}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})}{\delta u^{\ell^{\prime}}_{O\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})}\right)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}\frac{\delta g^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})}{\delta u^{\ell^{\prime}}_{O\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})}=\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}\beta_{0}^{2}L^{-1}\sum_{k=\ell}^{L}\sum_{t^{\prime\prime}<t}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}\int d\bm{x}^{\prime\prime}C^{g^{k}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime\prime},t,t^{\prime\prime})\left(\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}\frac{\delta h^{k}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})}{\delta u^{\ell^{\prime}}_{O\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})}\right)

(70)

These equations give the needed response function
R^{g^{\ell+1}\chi^{\ell}_{O}}
. From these equations we immediately see that if
\sigma^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)=\sigma^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)
(which holds under our inductive hypothesis) then
R^{g^{\ell+1}\chi^{\ell}_{O}}_{\mathfrak{h}}=R^{g^{\ell+1}\chi^{\ell}_{O}}_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}
. This same argument is repeated for all other response functions that are computed as derivatives of residual stream variables. We have thus found that

\displaystyle\forall\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\in[\mathcal{H}]\ ,\ R^{g^{\ell+1}\chi^{\ell}_{O}}_{\mathfrak{h}}=R^{g^{\ell+1}\chi^{\ell}_{O}}_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}\ ,\ R^{h^{\ell}\xi^{\ell}_{V}}_{\mathfrak{h}}=R^{h^{\ell}\xi^{\ell}_{V}}_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}\ ,\ R^{h^{\ell}\xi^{\ell}_{K}}_{\mathfrak{h}}=R^{h^{\ell}\xi^{\ell}_{K}}_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}\ ,\ R^{h^{\ell}\xi^{\ell}_{Q}}_{\mathfrak{h}}=R^{h^{\ell}\xi^{\ell}_{Q}}_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}

Now, we can analyze the dynamics of the keys, queries and values within a head using the above property and the original inductive hypothesis that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}},M_{\mathfrak{h}}=M_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}...
for times less than
t
. We will now prove that this implies that these variables will remain the same. We start by examining the keys and queries which have the form

\displaystyle k^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)=u_{K\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)+\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\int d\bm{x}^{\prime}C^{k^{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})q^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

(71)

\displaystyle q^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)=u_{Q\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)+\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\int d\bm{x}^{\prime}C^{q^{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})k^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

(72)

where
C^{k^{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})
and
C^{q^{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})
are two operators that do not carry a
\mathfrak{h}
(are the same across all heads). These relations can be viewed as a linear system of equations. We let
\bm{k}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}=\text{Vec}\{k^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\}_{\mathfrak{s}\bm{x}t}
and analogously
\bm{C}^{k^{\ell}}=\text{Mat}\{C^{k^{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\}
as in the calculations of
Bordelon and Pehlevan [15]
,
Bordelon and Pehlevan [41]
. Using this shorthand, we can express the key/query and attention kernels as

\displaystyle\bm{k}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}=\left[\bm{I}-\bm{C}^{k^{\ell}}\bm{C}^{q^{\ell}}\right]^{-1}\left[\bm{u}_{K\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell}+\bm{C}^{k^{\ell}}\bm{u}^{\ell}_{Q\mathfrak{h}}\right]\ ,\ \bm{q}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}=\left[\bm{I}-\bm{C}^{q^{\ell}}\bm{C}^{k^{\ell}}\right]^{-1}\left[\bm{u}_{Q\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell}+\bm{C}^{q^{\ell}}\bm{u}^{\ell}_{K\mathfrak{h}}\right]

\displaystyle\bm{K}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell}=\left[\bm{I}-\bm{C}^{k^{\ell}}\bm{C}^{q^{\ell}}\right]^{-1}\left[\bm{H}^{\ell}+\bm{C}^{k^{\ell}}\bm{H}^{\ell}\bm{C}^{k^{\ell}\top}\right]\left[\bm{I}-\bm{C}^{k^{\ell}}\bm{C}^{q^{\ell}}\right]^{-1\top}

\displaystyle\bm{Q}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell}=\left[\bm{I}-\bm{C}^{q^{\ell}}\bm{C}^{k^{\ell}}\right]^{-1}\left[\bm{H}^{\ell}+\bm{C}^{q^{\ell}}\bm{H}^{\ell}\bm{C}^{q^{\ell}\top}\right]\left[\bm{I}-\bm{C}^{q^{\ell}}\bm{C}^{k^{\ell}}\right]^{-1\top}

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{A}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}=\left[\bm{I}-\bm{C}^{k^{\ell}}\bm{C}^{q^{\ell}}\right]^{-1}\bm{C}^{k^{\ell}}\bm{H}^{\ell}\bm{C}^{q^{\ell}\top}\left[\bm{I}-\bm{C}^{q^{\ell}}\bm{C}^{k^{\ell}}\right]^{-1\top}

(73)

We thus see that the final kernels
\bm{K}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell},\bm{Q}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell},\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell}
are all identical across heads. An identical argument can be carried out for the value kernel
V_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ell}
and the
M^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}
order parameter.

E.3
Infinite
\mathcal{H}
Limit

In this section, we compute the infinite head limit with
N,L
fixed. This limit is more technically involved than the
N\to\infty
limit which required only a simple saddle point of the full DMFT action over all kernels. At finite
N
we cannot use this technique since the kernels within MHSA blocks are
random variables
. However, as was shown in
Bordelon and Pehlevan [17]
, the DMFT action still contains the necessary information to characterize the distribution over order parameters at finite
N
. In the case of transformers with infinitely many heads, a subset of the order parameters introduced in the previous section will still concentrate as
\mathcal{H}\to\infty
including

\displaystyle H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N\mathcal{H}}\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle G^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N\mathcal{H}}\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle V^{\ell\sigma}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{\mathfrak{h}=1}^{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}}V^{\ell\sigma}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\sigma^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}(\bm{x},t)\sigma^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

and many more correlation and response functions. We will call the full collection of all the necessary head-averaged order parameters
\bm{Q}_{\text{global}}
. Further, not all of the stochastic fields will be relevant to characterize the residual stream. Specifically, only head-averaged fields
\bar{\chi}^{\ell}_{O}
,
\bar{\xi}^{\ell}_{V},\bar{\xi}^{\ell}_{K},\bar{\xi}^{\ell}_{Q}
are relevant. For example, the first of these is defined as

\displaystyle\bar{\chi}^{\ell}_{O\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{h}=1}^{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}\in[\mathcal{S}]}\sigma^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t){\chi}_{O\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{h}=1}^{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}\in[\mathcal{S}]}\sigma^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)\left[u^{\ell}_{O\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},t)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{t^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}\int d\bm{x}^{\prime}R^{v^{\ell},\xi^{\ell}_{O}}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})g^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right]

The saddle point equations for this limit are computed as derivatives with respect to
\bm{Q}_{\text{global}}

only
, reflecting that head-averages will converge as
\mathcal{H}\to\infty

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S}{\partial\bm{Q}_{\text{global}}}=0.

(86)

The final saddle point equations are given in terms of averages over the distribution of heads defined by
\mathcal{Z}_{\text{MHSA}}
which we denote as
\left<\cdot\right>_{\text{MHSA}}
as well as averages over the residual stream which we denote as
\left<\cdot\right>
.

These equations give the following (we suppress the sequence indices to simplify the final expressions)

\displaystyle H^{\ell}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\left<h^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)h^{\ell}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right>\ ,\ G^{\ell}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\left<g^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)g^{\ell}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right>

\displaystyle V^{\ell\sigma}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})=\left<V^{\ell}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\sigma^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)\sigma^{\ell}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right>_{\text{MHSA}}

\displaystyle G^{\ell\sigma}_{O\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})=\left<G^{\ell}_{O}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\sigma^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)\sigma^{\ell}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right>_{\text{MHSA}}

\displaystyle K^{\ell M\dot{\sigma}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})=\left<K^{\ell}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})M^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)M^{\ell}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\dot{\sigma}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)\dot{\sigma}^{\ell}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right>_{\text{MHSA}}

\displaystyle Q^{\ell M\dot{\sigma}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})=\left<Q^{\ell}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})M^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)M^{\ell}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\dot{\sigma}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)\dot{\sigma}^{\ell}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right>_{\text{MHSA}}

\displaystyle R^{g^{\ell+1},\bar{\chi}^{\ell}_{O}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\left<\frac{\delta g^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}{\delta\bar{u}^{\ell}_{O\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})}\right>

\displaystyle R^{h^{\ell},\bar{\xi}_{V}^{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\left<\frac{\delta h^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}{\delta\bar{r}^{\ell}_{V\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})}\right>

\displaystyle R^{h^{\ell},\xi_{K}^{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\left<\frac{\delta h^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}{\delta\bar{r}^{\ell}_{K\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})}\right>

\displaystyle R^{h^{\ell},\xi_{Q}^{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\left<\frac{\delta h^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}{\delta\bar{r}^{\ell}_{Q\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})}\right>

\displaystyle\bar{R}^{v^{\ell}\hat{\xi}^{\ell}_{O}}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N}\text{Tr}\left<\frac{\delta\bm{v}^{\ell\sigma}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}{\delta\bm{r}^{\ell}_{O\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})^{\top}}\right>_{\text{MHSA}}

\displaystyle\bar{R}^{g^{\ell}_{O}\chi^{\ell}_{V}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N}\text{Tr}\left<\frac{\delta\bm{g}^{\ell\sigma}_{O\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}{\delta\bm{u}^{\ell}_{V\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})^{\top}}\right>_{\text{MHSA}}

\displaystyle\bar{R}^{k^{\ell}\chi^{\ell}_{Q}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N}\text{Tr}\left<\frac{\delta\bm{k}^{\ell M\sigma}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}{\delta{\bm{u}}^{\ell}_{Q\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})^{\top}}\right>_{\text{MHSA}}

\displaystyle\bar{R}^{q^{\ell}\chi^{\ell}_{K}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N}\text{Tr}\left<\frac{\delta\bm{q}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}{\delta{{\bm{u}}}^{\ell}_{K\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})^{\top}}\right>_{\text{MHSA}}

(87)

Residual Stream Dynamics

The residual stream satisfies the following single-site dynamics

\displaystyle h^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)=

\displaystyle h^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)+\frac{\beta_{0}}{L^{\alpha_{L}}}\bar{u}^{\ell}_{O\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)+\frac{\beta_{0}}{L^{\alpha}}\sum_{t^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\int d\bm{x}^{\prime}\bar{R}^{v^{\ell}\xi^{\ell}_{O}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})g^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}\beta_{0}^{2}}{L}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}^{\prime}\sim\mathfrak{B}_{t^{\prime}}}V^{\ell\sigma}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})g^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\ ,\ \bar{u}^{\ell}_{O\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,V^{\ell\sigma})

\displaystyle g^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)=

\displaystyle g^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)+\frac{\beta_{0}}{L^{\alpha_{L}}}\left[\bar{r}^{\ell}_{V\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)+\bar{r}^{\ell}_{K\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)+\bar{r}^{\ell}_{Q\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta_{0}}{L^{\alpha}}\sum_{t^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\int d\bm{x}^{\prime}[\bar{R}^{g^{\ell}_{O}\chi^{\ell}_{V}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})+\bar{R}^{k^{\ell}\chi^{\ell}_{Q}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})+\bar{R}^{q^{\ell}\chi^{\ell}_{K}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})]h^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}\beta_{0}^{2}}{L}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}^{\prime}\sim\mathfrak{B}_{t^{\prime}}}[G^{\ell\sigma}_{O\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})+K^{\ell M\dot{\sigma}}_{O\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})+Q^{\ell M\dot{\sigma}}_{O\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})]h^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\bar{r}^{\ell}_{V\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,G^{\ell\sigma}_{O})\ ,\ \bar{r}^{\ell}_{Q\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,Q^{\ell M\dot{\sigma}})\ ,\ \bar{r}^{\ell}_{K\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,Q^{\ell M\dot{\sigma}})

(88)

This matches the result provided in the main text which introduces a compressed

C^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}\beta_{0}}\bar{R}^{v^{\ell}\xi^{\ell}_{O}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})+p_{t^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime})\Delta(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})V^{\ell\sigma}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})

(89)

where
p_{t}(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{B}_{t}|}\sum_{\bm{x}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{B}_{t}}\delta(\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime})
denotes the uniform distribution over the batch
\mathfrak{B}_{t}
.

E.4
Infinite
L
Limits

In this section, we discuss the two large
L
limits. This can be derived formally in two distinct ways. First, one could start with the initial For this section, it suffices to reason about the scale of the Gaussian noise which appears in the residual stream and the contribution from the response functions.

E.4.1
Basic Intuition

Deriving the infinite depth limits To gain intuition for the large
\mathcal{H},L\to\infty
limit, we use the fact that the random variables
\chi^{\ell}=u^{\ell}+R^{\ell}g^{\ell}
decompose into a Gaussian
u^{\ell}
which are uncorrelated across layers and a linear response
R^{\ell}
are response functions. This implies that

\displaystyle h^{L}=\frac{\beta_{0}}{L^{\alpha_{L}}}\sum_{k=1}^{L}u^{k}+\frac{\beta_{0}}{L^{\alpha_{L}}}\sum_{k=1}^{L}R^{k}g^{k}+\frac{\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}\beta_{0}^{2}}{L}\sum_{k=1}^{L}V^{k\sigma}g^{k}

(90)

We first note that the sum of the Gaussians is a zero-mean random variable with standard deviation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{L}u^{k}\sim\mathcal{O}(L^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha_{L}})

(91)

Thus, this integrated random variable will vanish unless
\alpha_{L}=\frac{1}{2}
.

Next, we can investigate the scale of the residual stream response functions. For instance

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h^{\ell}}{\partial u^{k}}=\mathcal{O}\left(L^{-\alpha_{L}}\right)\ ,\ \frac{\partial h^{\ell}}{\partial r^{k}}=\mathcal{O}\left(L^{-\alpha_{L}}\right)\ ,\ \frac{\partial g^{\ell}}{\partial u^{k}}=\mathcal{O}\left(L^{-\alpha_{L}}\right)\ ,\ \frac{\partial g^{\ell}}{\partial r^{k}}=\mathcal{O}\left(L^{-\alpha_{L}}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{\alpha_{L}}}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}R^{k}g^{k}=\mathcal{O}\left(L^{1-2\alpha_{L}}\right)

(92)

As a consequence, we see that the effect of the Gaussian and linear response terms will vanish as
L\to\infty
provided that
\alpha_{L}>\frac{1}{2}
. We will consider first, the case where
\alpha=1
which gives an ODE like limit for the residual updates before moving onto the more involved
\alpha_{L}=\frac{1}{2}
case.

To formally take the
L\to\infty
limit, we redefine all of the preactivation fields and kernels in terms of layer time
\tau
defined as

\displaystyle\tau=\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{\ell}{L}\in[0,1].

(93)

For example, the residual kernels are defined as

\displaystyle H_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\equiv\lim_{L\to\infty}H^{L\tau}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime}).

(94)

The finite difference equations for the residual updates
L(h^{\ell+1}-h^{\ell})\sim\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}h(\tau)
become differential updates (either SDE-like or ODE-like depending on
\alpha_{L}
)
[
46
,
11
]
.

E.4.2
ODE Limit
\alpha_{L}=1

First, we investigate the case of
\alpha_{L}=1
. In this case, the
\mathcal{H},L\to\infty
limit results in a complete disappearance of the
\bar{\chi}^{\ell}_{O},\bar{\xi}^{\ell}_{V},\bar{\xi}^{\ell}_{K},\bar{\xi}^{\ell}_{Q}
fields.

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathfrak{s}}(\tau,\bm{x},t)=\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}\beta_{0}^{2}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}^{\prime}\sim\mathfrak{B}_{t}}\Delta(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})V^{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})g_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

\displaystyle-\partial_{\tau}g_{\mathfrak{s}}(\tau,\bm{x},t)=\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}\beta_{0}^{2}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}^{\prime}\sim\mathfrak{B}_{t}}\Delta(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})[G^{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})+K^{M\dot{\sigma}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})]g_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}\beta_{0}^{2}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}^{\prime}\sim\mathfrak{B}_{t}}\Delta(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})[Q^{M\dot{\sigma}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})]g_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

(95)

E.4.3
SDE Limit
\alpha_{L}=\frac{1}{2}

This
\alpha_{L}=\frac{1}{2}
limit is more technically involved since neither the Gaussian terms from the DMFT nor the response functions vanish. For the Gaussian terms, we note that the sums of the independent Gaussians are all multiplied by
\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sim\sqrt{d\tau}
, which can be interpreted as integrated Brownian motion in the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}u^{k}\to\int_{0}^{\tau}du(\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\left<du(\tau)du(\tau^{\prime})\right>=V^{\sigma}(\tau)\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})d\tau d\tau^{\prime}

(96)

Following the derivation of
Bordelon et al. [11]
, which maintains the exact dependence on the full integrated response and provides the result as an integrated SDE

\displaystyle h_{\mathfrak{s}}(\tau,\bm{x},t)=\beta_{0}\int_{0}^{\tau}d\bar{u}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\tau^{\prime}\bm{x},t)+\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}\beta_{0}^{2}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}^{\prime}\sim\mathfrak{B}_{t^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}C_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})g_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

\displaystyle C_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}\beta_{0}}\bar{R}^{v\xi_{O}}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})+p_{t^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime})\Delta(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})V^{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime}).

(97)

Combining the forward pass equations from the previous two subsection recovers the Result 3 of the main text. This is combined with a complementary equation for the backward pass.

E.5
Effect of MLP Layers

Adding the MLP block to the residual stream can also be easily handled with the methods of the preceeding sections. The forward pass equations in this case take the form

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)=\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)+\frac{\beta_{0}}{L^{\alpha_{L}}}\text{MHSA}\left(\bm{h}^{\ell}(\bm{x},t)\right)_{\mathfrak{s}}

(98)

\displaystyle{\bm{h}}^{\ell+1}(\bm{x},t)=\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)+\frac{\beta_{0}}{L^{\alpha_{L}}}\text{MLP}\left(\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\right),

(99)

where the MLP layer is

\displaystyle\text{MLP}(\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{W}^{\ell,2}\phi\left(\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}\right)\ ,\ \tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell,1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{W}^{\ell,1}{\tilde{\bm{h}}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}

(100)

The following gradient fields are necessary

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\equiv\gamma_{0}N\mathcal{H}\frac{\partial f(\bm{x},t)}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}\ ,\ \tilde{\bm{g}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\equiv\gamma_{0}N\mathcal{H}\frac{\partial f(\bm{x},t)}{\partial\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{g}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\ell,1}(\bm{x},t)\equiv\gamma_{0}N\mathcal{H}\frac{\partial f(\bm{x},t)}{\partial\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}

(101)

\displaystyle\bm{W}^{\ell,2}(t)=\bm{W}^{\ell,2}(0)+\frac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\mathfrak{B}_{t^{\prime}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}}\Delta(\bm{x},t^{\prime})\bm{g}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\ell+1}(\bm{x},t^{\prime})\phi(\tilde{\bm{h}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\ell,1}(\bm{x},t^{\prime}))^{\top}

\displaystyle\bm{W}^{\ell,1}(t)=\bm{W}^{\ell,1}(0)+\frac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\mathfrak{B}_{t^{\prime}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}}\Delta(\bm{x},t^{\prime})\tilde{\bm{g}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\ell,1}(\bm{x},t^{\prime})\bar{\bm{h}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t^{\prime})^{\top}

(102)

The MLP hidden layer dynamics is much simpler to characterize and resembles the structure analyzed in prior works on infinite width networks
[
15
]
.

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)=\tilde{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)+\frac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}\gamma_{0}}{L^{1-\alpha_{L}}}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\mathfrak{B}_{t^{\prime}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}}\Delta(\bm{x},t^{\prime})\tilde{\bm{g}}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\ell,1}(\bm{x},t^{\prime}){H}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})

(103)

Again, we see that the inner dynamics for
\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell,1}
due to the weight updates in this layer scale as
L^{-1+\alpha_{L}}
, suggesting the need to choose
\alpha_{L}=1
if we desire this hidden layer to contribute to the representational updates.

MLP Layer Gradients

For the MLP layer we have the simpler backpropagation equations

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{g}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}{\partial\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)}\right)^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta_{0}}{L^{\alpha}_{L}}\dot{\phi}(\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t))\odot\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{W}^{\ell,2}(t)^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\right]

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{g}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{W}^{\ell,1}(t)^{\top}\tilde{\bm{g}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)

(104)

The components of these fields that depend on initial conditions are

\displaystyle\bm{\xi}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{W}^{\ell,2}(0)^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle\bm{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\bm{W}^{\ell,1}(0)^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)

(105)

MLP Matrices

After utilizing these resolutions of the identity for all
\mathfrak{s},\bm{x},t
, we can integrate over the weights
\bm{W}^{\ell,2}(0)

\displaystyle\ln\mathbb{E}_{\bm{W}^{\ell,2}(0)}\exp\left(-\frac{i}{\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{t\mathfrak{s}}\int d\bm{x}\ \text{Tr}\bm{W}^{\ell,2}(0)^{\top}\left[\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\phi(\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t))^{\top}+\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)^{\top}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{tt^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\int d\bm{x}d\bm{x}^{\prime}\left[\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\Phi^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})+\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})G^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle-i\sum_{tt^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\int d\bm{x}d\bm{x}^{\prime}\left[\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})R^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\right]

(106)

where we introduced the response function

\displaystyle R^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\equiv-\frac{i}{N\mathcal{H}}\phi\left(\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\right)\cdot\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

(107)

We can perform an identical step to integrate over
\bm{W}^{\ell,1}(0)
.
This gives us

\displaystyle\ln\mathbb{E}_{\bm{W}^{\ell,1}(0)}\exp\left(-\frac{i}{\sqrt{N\mathcal{H}}}\sum_{t\mathfrak{s}}\int d\bm{x}\ \text{Tr}\bm{W}^{\ell,1}(0)^{\top}\left[\hat{\tilde{\bm{\chi}}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)^{\top}+\bm{g}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)^{\top}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{tt^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\int d\bm{x}d\bm{x}^{\prime}\left[\hat{\tilde{\bm{\chi}}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\hat{\tilde{\bm{\chi}}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\tilde{H}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})+\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})G^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle-i\sum_{tt^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}\int d\bm{x}d\bm{x}^{\prime}\left[\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell,1}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\tilde{R}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\right]

(108)

where we introduced

\displaystyle\tilde{R}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})=-\frac{i}{N\mathcal{H}}\tilde{\bm{h}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

(109)

E.6
Effect of Layer Norm on the Limiting Process

Layernorm

The derivative of layer-norm
\frac{\partial\bar{\bm{h}}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell\top}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}}
acts as the following in the large
\mathcal{H}
limit

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bar{\bm{h}}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\top}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma^{2}+\epsilon}}\left(\bm{I}-\frac{1}{N\mathcal{H}}\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}\right)-\frac{1}{N\mathcal{H}}\frac{1}{(\sigma^{2}+\epsilon)^{3/2}}\left[\bm{h}-\mu\bm{1}\right]\left[\bm{h}-\mu\bm{1}\right]^{\top}

(110)

In the limit of
\mathcal{H}\to\infty
the variables
\mu=\frac{1}{N\mathcal{H}}\bm{h}\cdot\bm{1}
and
\sigma^{2}=\frac{1}{N\mathcal{H}}|\bm{h}-\mu\bm{1}|^{2}
will become deterministic over random initializations. We thus just have to consider how these types of vectors act on gradients

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\bar{\bm{h}}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\top}}\right)^{\top}\bm{g}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma^{2}+\epsilon}}\left(\bm{g}-\bm{1}\mu_{g}\right)-\frac{1}{(\sigma^{2}+\epsilon)^{3/2}}\left[\bm{h}-\mu\bm{1}\right]\left(\frac{1}{N\mathcal{H}}\left[\bm{h}-\mu\bm{1}\right]^{\top}\bm{g}\right).

(111)

Each of these operations will lead to one inner product that will be self averaging
\mu_{g}=\frac{1}{N\mathcal{H}}\bm{1}\cdot\bm{g}
or
\left(\frac{1}{N\mathcal{H}}\left[\bm{h}-\mu\bm{1}\right]^{\top}\bm{g}\right)
. Thus this operation will not alter the backward pass in terms of scaling.

Compute Resources and Experimental Details

Each of the experimental runs performed in this paper were all performed on single NVIDIA H100 GPU. Each run of the full CIFAR-5M took anywhere from
5

The language model trained on C4 used a context length of 256 and the GPT-tokenizer from Huggingface.

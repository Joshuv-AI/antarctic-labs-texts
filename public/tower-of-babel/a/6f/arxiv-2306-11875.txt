Quartic Gauss sums over primes and metaplectic theta functions
Chantal David, Alexander Dunn, Alia Hamieh and Hua Lin

Abstract
. We improve 1987 estimates of Patterson for sums of quartic Gauss sums over primes. Our Type-I and Type-II estimates feature new ideas, including use of the quadratic large sieve over ℚ ⁡ ( i ) \mathbb{Q}(i) , and Suzuki’s evaluation of the Fourier-Whittaker coefficients of quartic theta functions at squares. We also conjecture asymptotics for certain moments of quartic Gauss sums over primes.

Introduction

1.1.
Background and statement of results

Gauss sums are fundamental objects in number theory.
Gauss
[
Gau73
]
famously gave a closed form formula for the normalised quadratic Gauss sum over
\mathbb{Q}

with prime modulus. Gauss’ evaluation
depended only on the congruence class of the prime modulo
4
.

Gauss sums attached to characters of a fixed order greater than or equal to
3
exhibit a very different behavior.
Kummer in 1846
[
Kum75
, Paper 16,17]
studied the distribution of normalised cubic Gauss sums
(over the Eisenstein quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})
) for small prime moduli, and predicted that the points landed
a ratio of
1:2:3
when plotted on appropriate thirds of the unit circle.
In a breakthrough 1979 paper, Heath-Brown and Patterson
[
HBP79
]

disproved Kummer’s conjecture and showed that the normalised cubic
Gauss sums with prime moduli are equidistributed on the unit circle.
Patterson
[
Pat87
]
generalised
his equidistribution result with Heath-Brown to
n
th order Gauss sums to prime moduli
(working over number fields
K
with
K\supseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
and
n\geq 3
fixed).

Patterson
[
Pat78
]

(with refinements by Heath-Brown and Patterson
[
HBP79
]
)
made a refined conjecture that concerned the asymptotic behavior for
moments of normalised cubic Gauss sums. The conjectured main term in the first moment
explained the small number bias witnessed by Kummer in 1846.
Patterson’s conjecture was established under the Generalised Riemann
Hypothesis by the second author and Radziwiłł
[
DR
]
in 2021, improving
on an unconditional upper bound of Heath Brown
[
HB00
]
established in 2000.

The purpose of this paper is to improve the known estimates in the case of quartic Gauss sums over primes.
As explained in §
1.2
, there are some significant differences when compared to the cubic case.

Before stating our result and conjecture, we need some notation.
Let
e(x):=e^{2\pi ix}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and

\check{e}(z):=e(z+\overline{z})
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
Let
\mathbb{Q}(i)
be the Gaussian quadratic field (class number
1
)
with ring of integers
\mathbb{Z}[i]
. Let
\lambda:=1+i
denote the unique ramified prime.
For
c,\nu\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
with
(c,\lambda)=1
,
the normalised quartic Gauss sum over
\mathbb{Z}[i]

is defined as

\widetilde{g}_{4}(\nu,c):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N(c)}}\sum_{d\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{c}}\Big(\frac{d}{c}\Big)_{4}\check{e}\Big(\frac{\nu d}{c}\Big),

where
\big(\frac{\cdot}{c}\big)_{4}
is the quartic symbol defined in §
3
.
We write
\widetilde{g}_{4}(c):=\widetilde{g}_{4}(1,c)
for short. Let
\Lambda(c)
denote the
von Mangoldt function on
\mathbb{Z}[i]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
R:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a smooth function with compact support
in
[1,2]
. Then for any
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\beta\in\{1,1+\lambda^{3}\}\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
, and
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\\
c\equiv\beta\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{g}_{4}(c)\Big(\frac{\overline{c}}{|c|}\Big)^{\ell}\Lambda(c)R\Big(\frac{N(c)}{X}\Big)\ll_{\varepsilon,R}X^{5/6+\varepsilon}+X^{3/4+\varepsilon}|\ell|^{3/2+\varepsilon},

as
X\rightarrow\infty
.

This substantially improves the bound
\ll_{\ell}X^{19/20}
(for the sum with sharp cutoffs)
from
[
Pat87
, Main Theorem]
.

In analogy with Patterson’s conjecture for the
X^{5/6}
-bias for the first moment of cubic Gauss sums
[
Pat78
,
HBP79
]
,
we propose the following
X^{3/4}
-conjecture for the bias for quartic Gauss sums
over primes.

Conjecture 1.2
.

For
\beta\in\{1,1+\lambda^{3}\}\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
, there exists a constant
b_{\beta}\neq 0
such that for
any
\varepsilon>0
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
we have,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\\
N(c)\leq X\\
c\equiv\beta\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{g}_{4}(c)\Big(\frac{\overline{c}}{|c|}\Big)^{\ell}\Lambda(c)=\begin{cases}b_{\beta}X^{3/4}+O_{\varepsilon}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon})&\text{if}\quad\ell=0\\
O_{\varepsilon,\ell}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon})&\text{if}\quad\ell\neq 0\end{cases},

as
X\rightarrow\infty
.

The conjectured main term of size
\asymp X^{3/4}
in the quartic case is substantially smaller than the main term of size
\asymp X^{5/6}

in the cubic case
[
DR
]
.
Conjecture
1.2
is well out of reach even under the assumption of the
Generalised Riemann Hypothesis for Hecke
L
-functions over
\mathbb{Q}(i)
with Grössenkharakter.
One can see §
1.2
for explanation of the bottleneck in the Type-II sums.

Before giving an overview of the paper in §
1.2
,
we briefly explain how the twisted sums that are the subject of Theorem
1.1
and Conjecture
1.2

are related to the equidistribution for {
\widetilde{g}_{4}(\pi)\}_{\pi}
.
Weyl’s criterion for equidistribution requires
that for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,

(1.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\pi)\leq X\\
\pi\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{g}_{4}(\pi)^{k}=o_{k}\Big(\frac{X}{\log X}\Big)\quad\text{as}\quad X\rightarrow\infty.

We can discard the height two primes
\pi\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}

in (
1.1
) i.e.
N(\pi)=p^{2}
for
p\equiv 3\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
a rational prime,
since the contribution from such primes to (
1.1
) is
O(X^{1/2})
.
Without loss of generality we can assume the sum in (
1.1
) is restricted to height one primes

\pi\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}
i.e
N(\pi)=p\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
is a rational prime.
The formulae (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) imply that
for height one primes

\pi\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}
and
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,

(1.2)

\widetilde{g}_{4}(\pi)^{k}=\Big(\frac{\overline{\pi}}{|\pi|}\Big)^{-2m}\cdot\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\quad k\equiv 0\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}\quad\text{with}\quad m=\tfrac{k}{4}\\
\widetilde{g}_{4}(\pi)&\text{if}\quad k\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}\quad\text{with}\quad m=\tfrac{k-1}{4}\\
-\big(\frac{-1}{\pi}\big)_{4}\big(\frac{\overline{\pi}}{|\pi|}\big)^{-1}&\text{if}\quad k\equiv 2\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}\quad\text{with}\quad m=\tfrac{k-2}{4}\\
-\big(\frac{-1}{\pi}\big)_{4}\big(\frac{\overline{\pi}}{|\pi|}\big)^{-1}\widetilde{g}_{4}(\pi)&\text{if}\quad k\equiv 3\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}\quad\text{with}\quad m=\tfrac{k-3}{4}\end{cases}.

From (
1.2
) it follows that if
k\equiv 0,2\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
, then (
1.1
) is a standard consequence of
the zero-free region for
L
-functions with Grössencharaktern. If

k\equiv 1,3\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
, then it suffices to prove that for
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\beta\in\{1,1+\lambda^{3}\}\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\\
c\equiv\beta\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}\\
N(c)\leq X\end{subarray}}\widetilde{g}_{4}(c)\Big(\frac{\overline{c}}{|c|}\Big)^{\ell}\Lambda(c)=o_{\ell}\Big(\frac{X}{\log X}\Big).

We remark that the Grössencharakter
\big(\frac{-1}{c}\big)_{4}
is constant along the arithmetic progression
c\equiv\beta\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
.

1.2.
Overall strategy of the paper

In this section we explain the main ideas of the paper without paying attention to technicalities.
Here we assume that quartic reciprocity over
\mathbb{Q}(i)
is perfect (i.e. ignore congruence conditions modulo
4
),
pretend that
\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4})\lhd\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[i])
has one cusp, and so forth.
This section can be read independently of the rest of the paper.

For simplicity we will take
\ell=0
in this sketch.
After detecting primes using Vaughan’s identity
[
Vau75
]
we are faced with the sums,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(a)\sim A\\
N(b)\sim B\\
a,b\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}\end{subarray}}\rho_{a}\widetilde{g}_{4}(ab)R\Big(\frac{N(ab)}{X}\Big);

(1.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(a)\sim A\\
N(b)\sim B\\
a,b\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}\end{subarray}}\rho_{a}\phi_{b}\widetilde{g}_{4}(ab)R\Big(\frac{N(ab)}{X}\Big),

where
A,B\geq 1
, and

\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{a})
and
\bm{\phi}=(\phi_{b})

are arbitrary
\mathbb{C}
-valued sequences.
Both sums (
1.3
) and (
1.4
)
are supported on
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
such that
\mu^{2}(ab)=1
.
The sum in (
1.3
) (resp. (
1.4
)) is known as a Type-I
(resp. Type-II) sum. The decomposition of sums involving the von Mangoldt function into these
types of multilinear sums can be found in §
6
.

The Chinese remainder theorem, quartic reciprocity, and change of variables
(cf. (
3.4
)–(
3.6
)) imply that (ignoring the factor involving
C(a,b)
from (
3.6
)),

(1.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}_{4}(ab)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{g}_{4}(a)\widetilde{g}_{4}(b)\Big(\frac{a}{b}\Big)_{2}=\widetilde{g}_{4}(a)\widetilde{g}_{4}(a^{2},b).

After applying the first equality in (
1.5
) to (
1.4
), and relabelling the weights

\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{a})
and
\bm{\phi}=(\phi_{b})
appropriately,
the Type-II sum becomes

(1.6)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(a)\sim A\\
N(b)\sim B\\
a,b\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}\end{subarray}}\rho_{a}\mu^{2}(a)\phi_{b}\mu^{2}(b)\Big(\frac{a}{b}\Big)_{2}R\Big(\frac{N(ab)}{X}\Big).

After Mellin inversion of
R
,
the quadratic large sieve over
\mathbb{Q}(i)
due to Onodera
[
Ono09
]
and Goldmakher–Louvel
[
GL13
]
can be readily applied to (
1.6
). This
yields an upper bound
\ll X^{5/6+\varepsilon}
for
A\in[X^{1/3},X^{2/3}]
.
The quadratic large sieve is recorded in §
5
, and our
bounds for the Type-II sum appear in §
7
.

The Type-I sums are more intricate than their counterparts in the cubic case
[
DR
,
HB00
]
.
After applying the second equality in (
1.5
) to (
1.3
),
and relabelling the weight
\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{a})
appropriately,
the Type-I sum becomes

(1.7)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(a)\sim A\\
a\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}\end{subarray}}\rho_{a}\mu^{2}(a)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(b)\sim B\\
(b,a)=1\\
b\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{g}_{4}(a^{2},b)R\Big(\frac{N(ab)}{X}\Big).

After Mellin inversion of
R
and shifting the contour to
\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2+\varepsilon
,
one needs to understand the Dirichlet series,

\widetilde{\psi}^{(4)}(s,\nu,0):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\\
c\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\widetilde{g}_{4}(\nu,c)}{N(c)^{s}},\quad\text{for}\quad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1\quad\text{and}\quad\nu\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}.

These series have meromorphic continuation to all
\mathbb{C}
and satisfy a functional equation
s\rightarrow 1-s

because they essentially occur as the Fourier-Whittaker coefficients of Kubota’s Eisenstein series
E^{(4)}(w,s,0)
,
w\in\mathbb{H}^{3}
, on the
4
-fold cover of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[i])

[
Kub69
,
Kub71
,
Dia04
]
.
The function
\widetilde{\psi}^{(4)}(s,\nu,0)
has at most
a simple pole at
s=3/4
in the half-plane
\sigma>1/2
, with residue denoted
\psi^{(4)}(\nu)
. For
\varepsilon>0
, the convexity bound is

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}^{(4)}(s,\nu,0)

\displaystyle\ll N(\nu)^{(1/2)(1-\sigma)+\varepsilon}(|s|+1)^{3(1-\sigma)+\varepsilon},

(1.8)

\displaystyle\text{for}\quad\sigma:=\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1/2+\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\nu\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}.

More details concerning Eisenstein series and these Dirichlet series can be found in §
4.1
–§
4.4
.

Understanding the pole term in Type-I is subtle,
and the occurrence of the
\widetilde{g}_{4}(a^{2},b)
in (
1.7
) is crucial.
The residues
\psi^{(4)}(\nu)
appear as the Fourier-Whittaker coefficients of the quartic theta function

\vartheta_{4}(w)
(cf. (
4.34
) and (
4.35
)), and are in general not all determined!
This is in stark contrast to the case of the cubic theta function
\vartheta^{(3)}(w)
, where all the Fourier-Whittaker coefficients were
computed in closed form by Patterson
[
Pat77
]
. Hecke theory and periodicity for the Fourier coefficients of theta functions
on the
n
-fold cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
(
n\geq 2
) essentially reduces the problem to determining
the Fourier coefficients at indices
1,\pi,\ldots,\pi^{n-1}
, for each
\pi
prime. In general there are

n/2-1
(respectively
(n-1)/2-1
) undetermined coefficients among this collection when
n
is even (respectively odd),
one can consult the heuristic
[
Hof93
, pg.75–78]
and
[
BH16
, pg. 1904–1907]
.
Back to the quartic case
n=4
, Suzuki
[
Suz83
]
managed to determine partial information, such as

(1.9)

\psi^{(4)}(a^{2})=\frac{\overline{\widetilde{g}_{4}(a)}}{N(a)^{1/4}},\quad\text{for}\quad a\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}\quad\text{squarefree}.

Thus
\psi^{(4)}(a^{2})\ll N(a)^{-1/4}
, and this
leads to a contribution of
X^{3/4+\varepsilon}
to Type-I.
Note that
\nu\rightarrow a^{2}
in (
1.2
) gives
an increase in conductor for the relevant Dirichlet series in the
a
-aspect that does not occur in the cubic case
[
HB00
]
,
and it is insufficient for Theorem
1.1
to use only
the convex bound
\psi^{(4)}(a^{2})\ll N(a)^{1/4+\varepsilon}
as in
[
Pat87
]
. More information about metaplectic theta functions, their history, and
Patterson’s conjectures about them
appear in §
4.5
and the references therein.

To handle the contour integral near the critical line,
we use an approximate functional equation and the quadratic large sieve in §
9

to essentially prove that

(1.10)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(a)\leq A\\
a\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(a)|\widetilde{\psi}^{(4)}(1/2+\varepsilon+it,a^{2},0)|^{2}\ll_{\varepsilon}A^{3/2+\varepsilon}(|t|+1)^{3+\varepsilon}.

The bound in (
1.10
) saves
A^{1/2}
over the convexity bound on average,
and leads to a contribution
of
X^{1/2+\varepsilon}A^{3/4}\ll X^{3/4+\varepsilon}
to Type-I
for level of distribution
A\leq X^{1/3}
. This is sufficient for our Theorem
1.1
.
With additional work (cf. Remark
9.7
), it seems likely that one could prove a
Lindelöf-on-average (in
a
) bound
\ll A^{1+\varepsilon}(|t|+1)^{3+\varepsilon}
in (
1.10
),
and this would lead to a contribution
X^{1/2+\varepsilon}A^{1/2}\ll X^{3/4+\varepsilon}
to Type-I
for an increased level of distribution
A\leq X^{1/2}
.
This makes no significant difference in view of the
X^{3/4+\varepsilon}
contribution from the pole term,
and the
X^{5/6+\varepsilon}
-bottleneck in Type-II, and so
we refrain from this additional work.

With more effort one could remove the smoothing in Theorem
1.1

at a power saving loss using the
hybrid large sieve of Jutila
[
Jut75
]
(adapted to
\mathbb{Q}(i)
). We opt for the best possible exponent within current technology using a smoothing.

It seems quite difficult to obtain an asymptotic upper bound of
X^{5/6+\varepsilon}
-quality for sums of
n
th order Gauss sums over primes
with
n\geq 5
fixed. The main obstruction is that the relevant Dirichlet series in Type-I has Dirichlet coefficients
\widetilde{g}_{n}(a^{n-2},c)
,
and consequently has a functional equation that behaves like a
\operatorname{GL}_{n-2}
-object in
a
(leading to an increased conductor and difficulty in controlling
the error terms in Type-I).

3.
Quartic Gauss sums

Our basic references for this section are
[
IR90
, Chap. 9]
and
[
Dia04
, §2]
.
Let
\mathbb{Q}(i)
be the Gaussian imaginary quadratic field with ring of integers
\mathbb{Z}[i]
,
and

N(x):=N_{\mathbb{Q}(i)/\mathbb{Q}}(x)=|x|^{2}
denote the norm function.
It is well known that
\mathbb{Q}(i)
has class number one, discriminant
-4
, and
unit group
\mathbb{Z}[i]^{\times}=\{\pm 1,\pm i\}
.
Let
\lambda:=1+i
, the unique prime lying above
2
in
\mathbb{Q}(i)
.
Each ideal
0\neq\mathfrak{c}\unlhd\mathbb{Z}[i]

is principal, and if
(\mathfrak{c},\lambda)=1
, then
\mathfrak{c}
has a unique primary generator
i.e.
\mathfrak{c}=(c)
with
c\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}
.
Each
c\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}
satisfies either

c\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
or
c\equiv 1+\lambda^{3}\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
. The rational prime
2=-i\lambda^{2}
is the only
rational prime that ramifies in
\mathbb{Z}[i]
.
If
p\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
is a rational prime, then
p=\pi\overline{\pi}
in
\mathbb{Z}[i]

where
\pi,\overline{\pi}\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
are distinct primes and

N(\pi)=N(\overline{\pi})=p
. If

p\equiv 3\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
is a rational prime, then
p=\pi
remains inert in
\mathbb{Z}[i]

and
N(\pi)=p^{2}
. Thus we have
N(\pi)\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
for all primes
\pi
with
(\pi)\neq(\lambda)
.

For any prime
\pi\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
with
(\pi)\neq(\lambda)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
with

(\alpha,\pi)=1
, the quartic (resp. quadratic) residue symbol is defined by

\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\Big)_{4}\equiv\alpha^{\frac{N(\pi)-1}{4}}\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\pi},\quad\text{resp.}\quad\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\Big)_{2}\equiv\alpha^{\frac{N(\pi)-1}{2}}\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\pi}.

The quartic symbol (resp. quadratic symbol) takes values in
\{\pm 1,\pm i\}
(resp.
\{\pm 1\}
).
For
\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
with
(\gamma,\lambda)=1
,
(\alpha,\gamma)=1
, and
prime factorisation
\gamma=\pi_{1}\cdots\pi_{k}
, one extends these symbols multiplicatively,

\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\Big)_{4}:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\pi_{i}}\Big)_{4},\quad\text{resp.}\quad\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\Big)_{2}:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\pi_{i}}\Big)_{2}.

Furthermore,

\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\Big)_{4}=\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\Big)_{2}:=0\quad\text{for}\quad\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\quad\text{s.t.}\quad(\alpha,\gamma)\neq 1,\quad(\gamma,\lambda)=1.

Observe that

(3.1)

\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\Big)_{4}^{2}=\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\Big)_{2}\quad\text{for}\quad\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\quad\text{s.t.}\quad(\gamma,\lambda)=1.

For

\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
non-units with
\alpha\equiv\gamma\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}

and
(\alpha,\gamma)=1
,
the biquadratic reciprocity law
[
IR90
, §9.9, Theorem 2]
states that

(3.2)

\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\Big)_{4}=(-1)^{C(\alpha,\gamma)}\Big(\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\Big)_{4},

where

(3.3)

C(\alpha,\gamma)=\frac{(N(\alpha)-1)}{4}\frac{(N(\gamma)-1)}{4}.

There are supplementary laws for the ramified prime and units.
If

\gamma=1+a_{4}\lambda^{4}+a_{5}\lambda^{5}+a_{6}\lambda^{6}+\cdots\quad\text{with}\quad a_{i}\in\{0,1\},

we have

\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\gamma}\Big)_{4}=i^{-a_{4}+2a_{6}}\quad\text{and}\quad\Big(\frac{i}{\gamma}\Big)_{4}=(-1)^{a_{4}+a_{5}}.

If

\gamma=1+\lambda^{3}+a_{4}\lambda^{4}+a_{5}\lambda^{5}+a_{6}\lambda^{6}+\cdots\quad\text{with}\quad a_{i}\in\{0,1\},

then

\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\gamma}\Big)_{4}=-i^{-a_{4}+2a_{6}}\quad\text{and}\quad\Big(\frac{i}{\gamma}\Big)_{4}=i(-1)^{a_{4}+a_{6}}.

Let
e(x):=e^{2\pi ix}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and

\check{e}(z):=e(z+\overline{z})
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
For
c,\nu\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
with
(c,\lambda)=1
,
the quartic Gauss sum over
\mathbb{Z}[i]

is defined as

g_{4}(\nu,c):=\sum_{d\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{c}}\Big(\frac{d}{c}\Big)_{4}\check{e}\Big(\frac{\nu d}{c}\Big).

We write

g_{4}(c):=g_{4}(1,c)
.
For
(r,c)=1
,

(3.4)

g_{4}(r\nu,c)=\overline{\Big(\frac{r}{c}\Big)_{4}}g_{4}(\nu,c).

For
\nu,c,c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
with
(cc^{\prime},\lambda)=1
and
(c,c^{\prime})=1
,
the Chinese Remainder Theorem implies the twisted multiplicativity relation,

(3.5)

g_{4}(\nu,cc^{\prime})=\Big(\frac{c}{c^{\prime}}\Big)_{4}\Big(\frac{c^{\prime}}{c}\Big)_{4}g_{4}(\nu,c)g_{4}(\nu,c^{\prime}).

Note that for
(\nu,cc^{\prime})=1
the relation (
3.5
) also holds for

(c,c^{\prime})\neq 1
(both sides are
0
).
It follows from (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) that for
(c,c^{\prime})=1
we have,

(3.6)

g_{4}(\nu,cc^{\prime})=(-1)^{C(c^{\prime},c)}g_{4}(\nu(c^{\prime})^{2},c)g_{4}(\nu,c^{\prime}).

By (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) it suffices to understand
g_{4}(\pi^{k},\pi^{\ell})
for primes

\pi\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}
and
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
.
Local computations yield

(3.7)

g_{4}(\pi^{k},\pi^{\ell})=\begin{cases}N(\pi)^{k}g_{4}(\pi)&\text{if }\ell=k+1,\quad k\equiv 0\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4},\\
N(\pi)^{k}g_{2}(\pi)&\text{if }\ell=k+1,\quad k\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4},\\
N(\pi)^{k}\big(\frac{-1}{\pi}\big)_{4}\overline{g_{4}(\pi)}&\text{if }\ell=k+1,\quad k\equiv 2\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4},\\
-N(\pi)^{k}&\text{if }\ell=k+1,\quad k\equiv 3\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4},\\
\varphi(\pi^{\ell})&\text{if }k\geq\ell,\quad\ell\equiv 0\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4},\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where

g_{2}(\nu,c):=\sum_{d\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{c}}\Big(\frac{d}{c}\Big)_{2}\check{e}\Big(\frac{\nu d}{c}\Big),

is the (auxiliary) quadratic Gauss sum.
We write

g_{2}(c):=g_{2}(1,c)
.

For degree
2
primes in
\mathbb{Z}[i]
i.e.
\pi\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}
prime such that

N(\pi)=p^{2}
with
p\equiv 3\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
a rational prime,
we have the closed form evaluation,

(3.8)

g_{4}(\pi)=g_{2}(\pi)=p=N(\pi)^{1/2}.

If
\pi\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}
is a degree
1
prime in
\mathbb{Z}[i]
, then
Gauss’ evaluation is

(3.9)

g_{2}(\pi)=\Big(\frac{-1}{\pi}\Big)_{4}N(\pi)^{1/2}.

If
\pi\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}
is a degree
1
prime in
\mathbb{Z}[i]
,
then we have the fourth power formula
[
IR90
, Proposition 9.9.5]
,

(3.10)

g_{4}(\pi)^{4}=\pi^{3}\overline{\pi}.

and the square formula
[
IR90
, Proposition 9.10.1]
,

(3.11)

g_{4}(\pi)^{2}=-\Big(\frac{-1}{\pi}\Big)_{4}N(\pi)^{1/2}\pi.

Observe that (
3.4
)–(
3.5
), (
3.7
)–(
3.8
), and (
3.10
)
imply that for
c\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}
we have

(3.12)

|g_{4}(c)|=\mu^{2}(c)N(c)^{1/2}.

We denote the normalised quartic Gauss sum (with shift) by

(3.13)

\widetilde{g}_{4}(\nu,c):=N(c)^{-1/2}g_{4}(\nu,c).

4.
Metaplectic Eisenstein series and theta functions

Here we introduce the Dirichlet series formed from quartic Gauss sums and explain
their connection to metaplectic Eisenstein series on the 4-fold cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
over
\mathbb{Q}(i)

and their theta functions. These notions were first introduced by Kubota
[
Kub69
,
Kub71
]
.

4.1.
Dirichlet series

For
0\neq\nu\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\beta\in\{1,1+\lambda^{3}\}\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
,
consider the Dirichlet series,

(4.1)

\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(s,\nu,\ell):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\\
c\equiv\beta\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}\end{subarray}}\frac{g_{4}(\nu,c)(\frac{\overline{c}}{|c|})^{\ell}}{N(c)^{s}}.

These series converge absolutely for
\sigma:=\operatorname{Re}(s)>3/2
. We write

\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(s,\nu,0):=\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(s,\nu)
.
The functions
\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(s,\nu,\ell)

have meromorphic continuation to all of
\mathbb{C}
.
Both families are holomorphic in the half-plane
\sigma>1
, unless

\ell=0
, when
\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(s,\nu)
has at most a possible simple pole at
s=5/4

in the half-plane
\sigma>1
. For
0\neq\nu\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
, let

(4.2)

\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(\nu):=\mathrm{Res}_{s=5/4}\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(s,\nu),

These residues are shrouded in mystery.
More details on the meromorphic continuation and residues are provided in
§
4.4
and §
4.5
respectively.

Remark 4.1
.

Suzuki
[
Suz83
, (1.1)]
normalised the Dirichlet series
\psi^{(4)}_{1+\lambda^{3}}(s,\nu,0)
with an extra
-
sign.
In terms of sign normalisation we follow Diaconu
[
Dia04
, (2.14)]
and do not include this normalisation in our
definition (
4.1
) for
\beta=1+\lambda^{3}
.
We account for this sign discrepancy when recording various results of Suzuki
[
Suz83
]
later in this Section.

4.2.
Group action on
\mathbb{H}^{3}
and Laplacian

Let
\mathbb{H}^{3}
denote the hyperbolic 3-space
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
Embed
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{H}^{3}
in the Hamilton quaternions by identifying

i=\sqrt{-1}
with
\hat{i}
and

w=(z(w),v(w))=(z,v)=(x+iy,v)\in\mathbb{H}^{3}
with
x+y\hat{i}+v\hat{j}
, where

1,\hat{i},\hat{j},\hat{k}
denote the unit quaternions.
A point
w=(z,v)
is represented by the matrix
w=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}z&-v\\
v&\overline{z}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
,
and
u\in\mathbb{C}
is represented by the matrix
\widetilde{u}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&0\\
0&\overline{u}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
The discontinuous action
of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on
\mathbb{H}^{3}

is given by

\gamma w=(\widetilde{a}w+\widetilde{b})(\widetilde{c}w+\widetilde{d})^{-1},\quad\gamma=\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}),\quad\text{and}\quad w\in\mathbb{H}^{3}.

In coordinates,

\gamma w=\bigg(\frac{(az+b)\overline{(cz+d)}+a\overline{c}v^{2}}{|cz+d|^{2}+|c|^{2}v^{2}},\frac{v}{|cz+d|^{2}+|c|^{2}v^{2}}\bigg),\quad w=(z,v).

The action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on

\mathbb{H}^{3}
is transitive, and the stabiliser of a point is
\operatorname{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.
The Laplace operator

\Delta:=v^{2}(\partial^{2}/\partial x^{2}+\partial^{2}/\partial y^{2}+\partial^{2}/\partial v^{2})-v\partial/\partial v

acts on
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{3})

and commutes with the action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
.

4.3.
Congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4})
and Kubota multiplier

Consider the congruence subgroup

\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4}):=\{\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[i]):\gamma\equiv I\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{4}}\}.

It has finite volume with respect to the
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant
Haar measure
v^{-3}dzdv
on
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. The Kubota multiplier

\chi:\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4})\rightarrow\{\pm 1,\pm i\}
is defined by

\chi(\gamma)=\begin{cases}\big(\frac{c}{a}\big)_{4}&\text{if }c\neq 0,\\
1&\text{if }c=0.\end{cases}

Biquadratic reciprocity (
3.2
) implies that
\chi
is a character on
\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4})
.
Let
\kappa\in P(\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4}))\subset\mathbb{Q}(i)\cup\{\infty\}
be a cusp of
\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4})
,

\Gamma_{\kappa}
denote the stabiliser of
\kappa

i.e.

\Gamma_{\kappa}=\{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4}):\gamma\kappa=\kappa\}.

A cusp
\kappa\in P(\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4}))
is called essential if the restriction of
\chi
to
\Gamma_{\kappa}
is trivial.
For each cusp
\kappa=\alpha/\gamma
(where
\infty=1/0
), choose a scaling matrix
\sigma_{\kappa}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[i])

i.e.
\sigma_{\kappa}(\infty)=\alpha/\gamma
. We have
\Gamma_{\kappa}=\sigma_{\kappa}\Gamma_{\infty}\sigma_{\kappa}^{-1}
,
where

\Gamma_{\infty}=\big\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\mu\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right):\mu\in\lambda^{4}\mathbb{Z}[i]\big\}.

A complete set of inequivalent essential cusps
[
Suz83
, Prop. 1]
consists of

(4.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}0,&\frac{\lambda^{4}}{1+\lambda^{3}},&&&&&&\\[5.0pt]
\lambda^{2},&i\lambda^{2},&\frac{\lambda^{2}}{1+\lambda^{3}},&\frac{i\lambda^{2}}{1+\lambda^{3}},&\lambda^{3},&\frac{\lambda^{3}}{1+\lambda^{3}},&&\\[5.0pt]
1,&\frac{1}{-1},&1+\lambda^{3},&\frac{1+\lambda^{3}}{-1},&\frac{1}{1+\lambda^{3}},&\frac{1}{-1-\lambda^{3}},&\frac{1+\lambda^{3}}{1-\lambda^{3}},&\frac{1+\lambda^{3}}{-1+\lambda^{3}}\\[5.0pt]
\infty,&\frac{1+\lambda^{3}}{\lambda^{4}},\\[5.0pt]
\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}},&\frac{1+\lambda^{3}}{\lambda^{2}},&\frac{1}{i\lambda^{2}},&\frac{1+\lambda^{3}}{i\lambda^{2}}&\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}},&\frac{1+\lambda^{3}}{\lambda^{3}}\end{array}.\right.

4.4.
Metaplectic Eisenstein series

Here we follow
[
Pat77
, §2-§3]
and
[
Dia04
, §2]
.
For any
2\times 2
matrix
M=(m_{ij})
, consider its
\ell
-fold tensor power

M_{\ell}
. Let
\vec{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell})\in\{1,2\}^{\ell}
and
\vec{j}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{\ell})\in\{1,2\}^{\ell}
be multi-indices.
The entries of the
2^{\ell}\times 2^{\ell}
matrix
M_{\ell}:=M^{\otimes\ell}
are given by

m_{\vec{i}\hskip 0.71114pt\vec{j}}^{(\ell)}=\prod_{r=1}^{\ell}m_{i_{r}j_{r}}.

Set
M_{0}:=1
. We also have
(M^{*})_{\ell}=(M_{\ell})^{*}
, where
∗
denotes the conjugate transpose of the matrix,
and we write
M^{*}_{\ell}
for both. Also note that
(MN)_{\ell}=M_{\ell}N_{\ell}
.

For
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})

and
w\in\mathbb{H}^{3}
given in matrix form, let

j(\gamma,w):=\frac{\widetilde{c}w+\widetilde{d}}{\text{det}(\widetilde{c}w+\widetilde{d})^{1/2}}.

We the have the relations

j(\gamma\gamma^{\prime},w)=j(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}w)j(\gamma^{\prime},w),

and

j(\gamma,w)^{*}=j(\gamma,w)^{-1}.

We write
\vec{1}
and
\vec{2}
for the multi-indices
(1,1,\ldots,1)

and
(2,2,\ldots,2)
respectively, and
j_{\ell}(\gamma,w)
(resp.
j^{*}_{\ell}(\gamma,w)
)
for
j(\gamma,w)_{\ell}
(resp.
j(\gamma,w)_{\ell}^{*}
).

Let
P^{\prime}(\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4}))=\{\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{24}\}
be the complete set of inequivalent
essential cusps of
\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4})
given in (
4.3
).
Let
\kappa_{1}=\infty=1/0
and write
\kappa_{i}=\alpha_{i}/\gamma_{i}
. We
take
\gamma_{i}=1
if
\kappa_{i}=0
.
Set
\Gamma_{i}:=\Gamma_{\kappa_{i}}
and
\sigma_{i}:=\sigma_{\kappa_{i}}
.
The Eisenstein series on
\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4})
attached to the
essential cusp
\kappa_{i}\in P^{\prime}(\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4}))
is given by

E^{(4)}_{i}(w,s,\ell):=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{i}\backslash\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4})}\overline{\chi(\gamma)}j^{*}_{\ell}(\sigma^{-1}_{i}\gamma,w)v(\sigma_{i}^{-1}\gamma w)^{s},\quad w=(z(w),v(w))\in\mathbb{H}^{3},\quad\sigma>2.

The series on the right side converges absolutely for
\sigma>2
.
The Eisenstein series satisfy the transformation law

E^{(4)}_{i}(\gamma w,s,\ell)=\chi(\gamma)j_{\ell}(\gamma,w)E^{(4)}_{i}(w,s,\ell),\quad\text{for all}\quad\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4}),

and each
E_{i}(w,s,0)
is an eigenfunction of the Laplacian
\Delta
.
For
u\in\mathbb{C}
let

(4.4)

K(u,s,\ell):=\int_{\mathbb{C}}(|z|^{2}+1)^{-s-\ell/2}\left(\begin{matrix}z&-1\\
1&\overline{z}\end{matrix}\right)_{\ell}^{*}\check{e}(-uz)dz.

In particular,

(4.5)

K(u,s,0)=(2\pi)^{s}|u|^{s-1}\Gamma(s)^{-1}K_{s-1}(4\pi|u|),

where
K_{\xi}(\cdot)
is the standard
K
-Bessel function of order
\xi\in\mathbb{C}
.

The Eisenstein series
E_{i}^{(4)}
has the Fourier expansion at essential cusp
\kappa_{j}\in P^{\prime}(\Gamma_{1}(\lambda^{4}))

\displaystyle E^{(4)}_{i}(\sigma_{j}w,s,\ell)

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}I_{\ell}v(w)^{s}+\phi^{(4)}_{ij}(s,0,\ell)K(0,s,\ell)I_{\ell}v(w)^{2-s}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\\
\nu\neq 0\end{subarray}}\phi^{(4)}_{ij}(s,\nu,\ell)v(w)^{2-s}K\Big(\frac{\nu v(w)}{\lambda^{4}},s,\ell\Big)\check{e}\Big(\frac{\nu z(w)}{\lambda^{4}}\Big),

where for
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
(
\nu=0
is allowed in this definition) we have

\displaystyle\phi^{(4)}_{ij}(s,\nu,\ell):=\mathcal{V}^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{i}\backslash\Gamma/\Gamma_{j}\\
c_{\gamma}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\overline{\chi(\gamma)}\check{e}\Big(\frac{\nu d_{\gamma}}{\lambda^{4}c_{\gamma}}\Big)(\widetilde{c}_{\gamma})^{*}_{\ell}

\displaystyle N(c_{\gamma})^{-s-\ell/2},\quad\sigma>2,

and
\gamma=\sigma_{i}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
c_{\gamma}&d_{\gamma}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\sigma^{-1}_{j}
,
and
\mathcal{V}
denotes the volume
\mathbb{C}/\lambda^{4}\mathbb{C}
with respect to
dz
.
Observe that each
\phi^{(4)}_{ij}(s,\nu,\ell)
is a diagonal
2^{\ell}\times 2^{\ell}
-matrix
for
1\leq i,j\leq 24
integers. Explicit formulae for the entries of

in (
4.4
)
are given in
[
Dia04

Let

(4.7)

\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(i),\lambda}(s,\ell):=\sum_{m\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}}\frac{\big(\frac{\overline{m}}{|m|}\big)^{4\ell}}{N(m)^{s}},\quad\sigma>1,

be the Hecke

The meromorphic continuation and functional equations for the

[
Sel63

4.5.
Theta functions

Kubota
[
Kub69
,
Kub71
]
introduced the notion of theta functions as residues of
metaplectic Eisenstein series on the
n
-fold cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
for
n\geq 3
.
Kazhdan and Patterson
[
KP84
]
further studied metaplectic theta functions coming from the
n
-fold cover of

\operatorname{GL}_{r}
. The reader may consult the beautiful surveys

[
Hof93
,
CFH12
]
for a summary of this topic in the classical language.

On the
4
-fold cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
we have the quartic theta functions,

\vartheta^{(4)}_{i}(w):=\text{Res}_{s=5/4}E_{i}^{(4)}(w,s,0),\quad w\in\mathbb{H}^{3},\quad i=1,\ldots,24,

and from (
4.5
)–(
4.4
)
we see that they have Fourier expansions,

(4.34)

\vartheta^{(4)}_{i}(w)=\tau^{(4)}_{i}(\infty)v(w)^{3/4}+c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\\
\nu\neq 0\end{subarray}}\tau^{(4)}_{i}(\nu)N(\nu)^{1/8}v(w)K_{1/4}(\pi|\nu|v(w))\check{e}\Big(\frac{\nu z(w)}{\lambda^{4}}\Big),

where
c=2^{3/4}\pi^{5/4}\Gamma(\frac{5}{4})^{-1}
.
The Fourier-Whittaker coefficients of the
\vartheta^{(4)}_{i}(w)
are intimately connected to the
residues
\psi^{(4)}_{1}(\nu)
and
\psi^{(4)}_{1+\lambda^{3}}(\nu)
from (
4.2
).
If
1\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq 24
are such that

\kappa_{i_{1}}=0
and
\kappa_{i_{2}}=\tfrac{\lambda^{4}}{1+\lambda^{3}}
,
then from (
4.5
)–(
4.4
),
(
4.11
)–(
4.13
), and (
4.34
), we have

(4.35)

\mathcal{V}\tau^{(4)}_{i_{1}}(\nu)=\psi^{(4)}_{1}(\nu)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{V}\tau^{(4)}_{i_{2}}(\nu)=-\psi^{(4)}_{1+\lambda^{3}}(\nu)\quad\text{for}\quad 0\neq\nu\in\mathbb{Z}[i].

Discovery of a closed form formula for the Fourier-Whittaker coefficients of theta functions
on the
n
-fold cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}

is a long standing open problem. Patterson
[
Pat77
]

settled this problem in the case of the cubic theta function (3-fold cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
)
using a metaplectic Hecke converse argument. Patterson proved that the Fourier-Whittaker coefficients for the
cubic theta function are essentially cubic Gauss sums.
Unfortunately Patterson’s approach did not extend to the

n
-fold cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
for
n\geq 4
.

Suzuki
[
Suz83
]
obtained partial information
about the residues on the
4
-fold cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
using a different method.
In what follows, let
m\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
be squarefree and satisfy
m\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}
,

0\neq\nu\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
, and
\beta\in\{1,1+\lambda^{3}\}\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
.
Suzuki
[
Suz83
, Theorem 3]
established
periodicity
for biquadrates,

(4.36)

\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(m^{4}\nu)=\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(\nu)\quad\text{when}\quad\quad(m,\nu)=1,

as well as the vanishing property involving cubes
[
Suz83
, Theorem 4]
,

(4.37)

\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(m^{3}\nu)=0\quad\text{when}\quad(m,\nu)=1.

Suzuki’s property concerning squares
[
Suz83
, Theorem 5]
is important for this paper i.e.

(4.38)

\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(m^{2}\nu)=\begin{cases}\frac{\overline{g_{4}(\nu,m)}}{N(m)^{3/4}}\cdot\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(\nu)&\text{if}\quad m\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4},\\
(-1)^{\tfrac{N(\beta)-1}{4}}\cdot\frac{\overline{g_{4}(\nu,m)}}{N(m)^{3/4}}\cdot\psi^{(4)}_{\beta(1+\lambda^{3})}(\nu)&\text{if}\quad m\equiv 1+\lambda^{3}\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}\end{cases},

for
(m,\nu)=1
.

Corollary 4.5
.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
squarefree with
m\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}
and
0\neq\nu\in\mathbb{Z}[i]

with
(m,\nu)=1
, we have

\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(m^{2}\nu)\ll_{\nu}N(m)^{-1/4}.

Remark 4.6
.

Suzuki’s property (
4.38
) with
\nu=1

yields the closed form evaluation of Fourier-Whittaker coefficients of
\vartheta^{(4)}(w)
indexed by
m^{2}

with
m\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
squarefree and satisfying
m\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}

(cf. (
4.34
) and (
4.35
)). On the other hand, one obtains the
inferior bound
\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(m^{2})\ll N(m)^{1/4+\varepsilon}
if only the convexity bound from
Proposition
4.3
is used.

Suzuki
[
Suz83
, Theorem 6]
also proved that

(4.39)

\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(m\nu)=0\quad\text{for}\quad(m,\nu)=1,

unless

(4.40)

g_{2}\Big(\frac{m\nu}{m_{1}},m_{1}\Big)=N(m_{1})^{1/2}\quad\text{for all}\quad m_{1}\text{ s.t. }m_{1}\mid m\text{ with }m_{1}\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{\lambda^{3}}.

The success of Patterson in determining the Fourier-Whittaker coefficients of the theta function on 3-fold cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
,
and Suzuki the partial information (
4.36
)–(
4.40
) on the 4-fold cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
,
can be explained using periodicity and
Hecke relations for the coefficients of theta functions.
See
[
BH89
]
,
[
Hof93
, §2]
, and
[
CFH12
, §2]
for a summary. The properties (
4.36
)–(
4.40
)
say nothing about
\psi^{(4)}_{\beta}(\pi)
for
\pi\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
prime for instance.
Deligne
[
Del80
]
studied the problem from a representation
theoretic point of view and explained that
the inaccessibility of the cases
n\geq 4
on
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
was owed to the fact that
local representations do not have unique Whittaker models.
Kazhdan and Patterson
[
KP84
]
generalised Deligne’s work and showed the periodicity property held
in greater generality.

Patterson
[
Pat84
]
, with refinements by Eckhardt-Patterson
[
EP92
, Conjecture 2.11]
,
conjectured essentially that
\tau^{(4)}_{i}(\pi)^{2}N(\pi)^{1/4}
is proportional
to
\overline{\widetilde{g}_{4}(\pi)}
for
\pi\equiv 1\hskip-4.26773pt\pmod{4}
prime.
Patterson’s quartic conjecture was based on a conjectural identity of his involving
two Dirichlet series that don’t have an Euler product, see
[
BH89
, (3)]
and
[
CFH12
, pg. 154]
.
Patterson’s quartic conjecture remains wide open,
and has been shown
to hold on average by Bump and Hoffstein
[
BH89
, pg. 6-8]
. It has been conjecturally
generalised to the
6
-fold cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}

by Chinta, Friedberg and Hoffstein
[
CFH12
, Conjecture 1]
,
and numerically tested by Bröker and Hoffstein
[
BH16
]
.

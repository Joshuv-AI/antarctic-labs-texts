Proof of Proposition 3.1 in the paper titled “Backstepping control of a class of space-time-varying linear parabolic PDEs via time invariant kernel functions”
Qiaoling Chen 1, Jun Zheng 1,2, Guchuan Zhu 2

Abstract
We provide a detailed proof of Proposition 3.1 in the paper titled “Backstepping control of a class of space-time-varying linear parabolic PDEs via time invariant kernel functions”. In the paper titled “Backstepping control of a class of space-time-varying linear parabolic PDEs via time invariant kernel functions”, we addressed the problem of exponential stabilization and continuous dependence of solutions on initial data in different norms for a class of 1 1 -D linear parabolic PDEs with space-time-varying coefficients under backstepping boundary control. In order to stabilize the system without involving a Gevrey-like condition or the event-triggered scheme, a boundary feedback controller was designed via a time invariant kernel function. By using the approximative Lyapunov method, the exponential stability of the closed-loop system was established in the spatial L p L^{p} -norm and W 1 , p W^{1,p} -norm, respectively, whenever p ∈ [ 1 , + ∞ ] p\in[1,+\infty] . It was also shown that the solution to the considered system depends continuously on the spatial L p L^{p} -norm and W 1 , p W^{1,p} -norm, respectively, of the initial data.



keywords

Backstepping control, parabolic PDEs, approximative Lyapunov method, exponential stabilization, continuous dependence.

n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

We conclude that the series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta G_{n}(\xi,\eta)

converges absolutely and uniformly in
\eta\in[0,1]
and
\xi\in[\eta,2-\eta]
, and that

\displaystyle G(\xi,\eta):=\lim_{n\to\infty}G_{n}(\xi,\eta)

(20)

is a solution to (
13
). Moreover,
G(\xi,\eta)
is continuous in
(\xi,\eta)
. Furthermore, in view of (
2
) with
f\in C\left([0,1]\times[0,1]\right)
and
c_{1}\in C\left([0,1]\right)
, we deduce that
G
is
C^{2}
-continuous. By using the change of variables, we deduce that the problem (
2
) admits a solution
k\in C^{2}\left(D\right)
.

Regarding the uniqueness of solution to (
2
), which is equivalent to the uniqueness of solution to the integral equation (
2
), it suffices to show that any twice continuously differentiable solution of (
2
) can be approximated by the sequence
\{G_{n}(\xi,\eta)\}
constructed via (
15
). More precisely, for any twice continuously differentiable solution
\overline{G}(\xi,\eta)
of (
2
), we intend to show that the estimate

\displaystyle|G_{n}(\xi,\eta)-\overline{G}(\xi,\eta)|\leq\frac{\delta M^{n}}{n!}\left(\xi+\eta\right)^{n}

(21)

holds true for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, where

\displaystyle\delta:=\max_{\eta\in[0,1],\xi\in[\eta,2-\eta]}|\Phi_{\overline{G}}(\xi,\eta)|.

This is because (
21
) implies the uniform convergence of
\{G_{n}(\xi,\eta)\}
to
\overline{G}(\xi,\eta)
, while the uniqueness of
G(\xi,\eta):=\lim_{n\to\infty}G_{n}(\xi,\eta)
leads to
\overline{G}(\xi,\eta)\equiv G(\xi,\eta)
. Thus, the solution to (
2
) is
unique, and consequently, the solution to (
2
) is also unique.

Now we prove (
21
) by induction. First, since
\overline{G}(\xi,\eta)
is a solution to (
2
), using the definition of
\Phi_{\overline{G}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|G_{0}(\xi,\eta)-\overline{G}(\xi,\eta)|=|G_{0}(\xi,\eta)-\left(G_{0}(\xi,\eta)+\Phi_{\overline{G}}(\xi,\eta)\right)|=|\Phi_{\overline{G}}(\xi,\eta)|\leq\delta,

which shows that (
21
) holds true for
n=0
.

Suppose that (
21
) holds true for a general
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, by this assumption and the linearity of
\Phi_{H}(\xi,\eta)
w.r.t.
H
, we have

\displaystyle|G_{n+1}(\xi,\eta)-\overline{G}(\xi,\eta)|=\left|\Phi_{G_{n}}(\xi,\eta)-\Phi_{\overline{G}}(\xi,\eta)\right|=\left|\Phi_{G_{n}-\overline{G}}(\xi,\eta)\right|\leq I_{5}+I_{6}+I_{7}+I_{8},

where

\displaystyle I_{5}:=

\displaystyle\delta\frac{{{\overline{\Lambda}}}M^{n}}{4n!}\int_{\eta}^{\xi}\int_{0}^{\eta}(\tau+s)^{n}\text{d}s\text{d}\tau,

\displaystyle I_{6}:=

\displaystyle\delta\frac{{{\overline{\Lambda}}}M^{n}}{2n!}\int_{0}^{\eta}\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau+s)^{n}\text{d}s\text{d}\tau,

\displaystyle I_{7}:=

\displaystyle\delta\frac{\overline{f}M^{n}}{4n!}\int_{\eta}^{\xi}\int_{0}^{\eta}\int_{z}^{z+\eta-s}(\tau+s)^{n}\text{d}\tau\text{d}s\text{d}z,

\displaystyle I_{8}:=

\displaystyle\delta\frac{\overline{f}M^{n}}{2n!}\int_{0}^{\eta}\int_{0}^{z}\int_{z}^{2z-s}(\tau+s)^{n}\text{d}\tau\text{d}s\text{d}z.

Analogous to the estimates of
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
and
I_{4}
, it holds that

\displaystyle I_{5}\leq

\displaystyle\delta\frac{{{\overline{\Lambda}}}M^{n}}{4n!}\frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)}\left((\xi+\eta)^{n+2}-(2\eta)^{n+2}\right),

\displaystyle I_{6}\leq

\displaystyle\delta\frac{{{\overline{\Lambda}}}M^{n}}{4n!}\frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)}(2\eta)^{n+2},

\displaystyle I_{7}\leq

\displaystyle\delta\frac{\overline{f}M^{n}}{4n!}\frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)}\left((\xi+\eta)^{n+2}-(2\eta)^{n+2}\right),

\displaystyle I_{8}\leq

\displaystyle\delta\frac{\overline{f}M^{n}}{2n!}\frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)}(2\eta)^{n+2}.

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle|G_{n+1}(\xi,\eta)-\overline{G}(\xi,\eta)|\leq\delta\left(\frac{{{{\overline{\Lambda}}}}}{2(n+2)}+\frac{{\overline{f}}}{n+2}\right)\frac{M^{n}}{(n+1)!}(\xi+\eta)^{n+1}\leq{\frac{\delta M^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\left(\xi+\eta\right)^{n+1}},

which implies that (
21
) holds true for
n+1
. Thus, (
21
) holds true for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We conclude that the solution to (
2
) is unique.

Finally, the estimate (
4
) follows immediately from (
20
), (
18
), (
16
), (
17
) and the change of variables. The proof is completed.

Remark 1

For the stabilization problem of linear parabolic PDEs with time invariant coefficients, the “uniqueness of kernel functions” was claimed in
[
7
]
and proved by estimating

\displaystyle|\Delta G(\xi,\eta)|:=|G^{\prime}(\xi,\eta)-G^{\prime\prime}(\xi,\eta)|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{n!}(\xi+\eta)^{n}

(22)

with some constant
C_{0}>0
, where
G^{\prime},G^{\prime\prime}
are two kernel functions; see (39) and (40) of
[
7
]
.

It is worth noting that a gap exists in the proof given in
[
7
]
.
On one hand, if
G^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime\prime}
are given by (see (39) of
[
7
]
)

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(\xi,\eta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}G_{n}(\xi,\eta),~G^{\prime\prime}(\xi,\eta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}G_{n}(\xi,\eta),

(23)

as indicated in
[
7
]
, the estimate (
22
) can be obtained in the same way as in (38) of
[
7
]
.
Therefore the equality of
G^{\prime}=G^{\prime\prime}
can be concluded by letting
n\rightarrow\infty
. However, since the limit of
\sum_{i=0}^{n}G_{i}(\xi,\eta)
is unique, which is known in the limit theory, due to (
23
), the equality of
G^{\prime}=G^{\prime\prime}
holds true trivially. Therefore, the proof presented in
[
7
]

is essentially for the uniqueness of the limit of
\{G_{n}\}
rather than the uniqueness of the solution.

On the other hand, if
G^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime\prime}
are not given by (
23
), without imposing more conditions, the estimate (
22
) cannot be obtained in the same way as in (38) of
[
7
]
.

In order to address the aforementioned issues, as presented in this paper, a feasible idea for proving the uniqueness of kernel functions is to prove that all solutions to integral equations (e.g., (
2
) or (
13
)) can be approximated by the sequence
\{G_{n}\}
. That is to say,
G^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime\prime}
in
[
7
]
have to be with a form of (
23
). Then the uniqueness of the solution is determined by the uniqueness of the limit of
\{G_{n}\}
. Note that, by using this method, the estimate considered in this paper (see (
21
)) is different from (
22
). In particular, (
21
) implies (
22
).

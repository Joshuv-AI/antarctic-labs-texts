Some notes on an identity of Frisch


Abstract
In this note, we show how a combinatorial identity of Frisch can be applied to prove and generalize some well-known identities involving harmonic numbers. We also present some combinatorial identities involving odd harmonic numbers which can be inferred straightforwardly from our results.

1
Preliminaries

The binomial coefficients are defined, for non-negative integers
n
and
m
, by

\binom{n}{m}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{n!}{m!(n-m)!},&\text{$n\geq m$};\\
0,&\text{$n<m$}.\end{cases}

More generally, for complex numbers
r
and
s
, they are defined by

\binom{r}{s}=\frac{\Gamma(r+1)}{\Gamma(s+1)\Gamma(r-s+1)},

where the Gamma function,
\Gamma(z)
, is defined for
\Re(z)>0
by the integral
[
12
]

\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{z-1}\,dt.

The function
\Gamma(z)
can be extended to the whole complex plane by analytic continuation.
It has a simple pole at each of the points
z=\cdots,-3,-2,-1,0
.
The Gamma function extends the classical factorial function to the complex plane:
(z-1)!=\Gamma(z)
;
thereby facilitating the computation of the binomial coefficients at non-integer and non-real values.
Closely related to the Gamma function is the psi or digamma function defined by
\psi(z)=\Gamma^{\prime}(z)/\Gamma(z)
.
It possesses the infinite series expression
[
12
, p. 14]

\psi(z)=-\gamma+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k+1}-\frac{1}{k+z}\right),

(1)

with
\gamma
being the Euler-Mascheroni constant.

Harmonic numbers,
H_{x}
,
x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, defined by the relation

H_{x}=H_{x-1}+\frac{1}{x},\quad H_{0}=0,

(2)

are connected to the digamma function through the fundamental relation

H_{x}=\psi(x+1)+\gamma.

(3)

When each
x
is a positive integer, say
n
, we have the sequence of harmonic numbers,
(H_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
and the recurrence relation (
2
) then gives

H_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k},\quad H_{0}=0.

(4)

The following combinatorial identity is attributed to Frisch
[
7
]
:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{b+k}{c}}=\frac{c}{n+c}\frac{1}{\binom{n+b}{b-c}},\quad\text{$n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}$ and $b,\,c,\,b-c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}$}.

(5)

It is listed in Gould’s compendium
[
8
]
as Identity 4.2 and was used recently by Gould and Quaintance
[
9
]

to prove a new binomial transform identity. Abel
[
1
]
gave a short proof for Frisch’s identity and studied its infinite variant.
In this note, we report how Frisch’s identity (
5
) can be applied to prove and generalize some well-known identities involving harmonic numbers
H_{n}
. We also present some combinatorial identities involving odd harmonic numbers
O_{n}
, which can be inferred straightforwardly from our results. Our line of approach consists essentially of utilizing the fact that derivatives of the generalized binomial coefficients yield harmonic numbers. This method is familiar and has appeared in some important earlier research of other mathematicians
[
5
,
6
,
13
,
14
]
.

2
Results

Theorem 1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
b,\,c,\,b-c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{b+k}{c}}\left(H_{k+b}-H_{k+b-c}\right)=\frac{c}{n+c}\frac{H_{n+b}-H_{b-c}}{\binom{n+b}{n+c}}

(6)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{{n}}{k}\frac{c}{{k+c}}\frac{{H_{k+b}-H_{b-c}}}{{\binom{{k+b}}{{k+c}}}}=\frac{{H_{n+b}-H_{n+b-c}}}{{\binom{{b+n}}{c}}}.

(7)

In particular, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{b+k}{k}}\left(H_{k+b}-H_{k}\right)=\frac{b}{n+b}H_{n+b}

(8)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{{n}}{k}\frac{{H_{k+b}}}{{k+b}}=\frac{{H_{n+b}-H_{n}}}{{b\binom{{b+n}}{b}}}.

(9)

Proof.

In Frisch’s identity (
5
) treat
b
and
c
as complex numbers and differentiate w.r.t.
b
using

\frac{d}{db}\binom{b+k}{c}^{-1}=\binom{b+k}{c}^{-1}\left(\psi(b+k+1-c)-\psi(b+k+1)\right)

(10)

and

\frac{d}{db}\binom{n+b}{n+c}^{-1}=\binom{n+b}{n+c}^{-1}\left(\psi(b+1-c)-\psi(b+n+1)\right),

(11)

where
\psi(z)
is the psi function. Simplify, making use of the fundamental relation (
3
).
Identity (
7
) is the binomial transform of (
6
).
∎

Corollary 2
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
,
b\neq 0
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{(b+k)^{2}}=\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{H_{n+b}-H_{b-1}}{\binom{n+b}{n+1}}.

(12)

Identity (
12
) generalizes the well-known identity

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{(k+1)^{2}}=\frac{H_{n+1}}{n+1},

(13)

which can be proved directly via

\displaystyle(n+1)\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{n+1}{k+1}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+1}{k+1}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}

\displaystyle=H_{n+1}.

Theorem 3
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
b,\,c,\,b-c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{b+k}{c}}\left(H_{k+b-c}-H_{c}\right)=\frac{1}{(n+c)\binom{n+b}{n+c}}\left(\frac{n}{n+c}+c(H_{n+c}-H_{b-c})\right).

(14)

In particular, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\binom{n}{k}H_{k+b}=\frac{1}{n\binom{n+b}{n}}.

(15)

Proof.

Differentiate Frisch’s identity (
5
) w.r.t.
c
using

\frac{d}{dc}\binom{b+k}{c}^{-1}=\binom{b+k}{c}^{-1}\left(\psi(c+1)-\psi(b+k+1-c)\right).

∎

Identity (
15
) generalizes the well-known identity

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}=-\frac{1}{n},

(16)

which is the binomial transform of the sequence
H_{n}
(see
[
4
, p. 34]
).

Corollary 4
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{k\binom{k+b}{k}}=H_{n+b}-H_{b}.

(17)

In particular,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{k\binom{k+n}{k}}=H_{2n}-H_{n}.

(18)

Corollary 5
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{k+b}{k}}H_{k+b}=\frac{b}{n+b}H_{b}-\frac{n}{(n+b)^{2}}.

(19)

Proof.

Set
c=b
in (
14
) to get

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{b+k}{b}}\left(H_{k}-H_{b}\right)=\frac{1}{n+b}\left(\frac{n}{n+b}+bH_{n+b}\right).

Use the fact that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{b+k}{k}}=\frac{b}{n+b}

(20)

and combine with (
8
).
∎

Corollary 6
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{k}{(k+b)^{2}}=\frac{H_{n+b}-H_{b}}{\binom{n+b}{n}}.

(21)

In particular,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{k}{(k+n)^{2}}=\frac{H_{2n}-H_{n}}{\binom{2n}{n}}.

(22)

Proof.

Using the fact that the right hand-side of (
19
) is the binomial transform of
H_{n+b}/\binom{n+b}{n}
we get

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\left(\frac{b}{k+b}H_{b}-\frac{k}{(k+b)^{2}}\right)=\frac{H_{n+b}}{\binom{n+b}{n}}.

Combine this with

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\frac{1}{k+b}=\frac{1}{b\binom{n+b}{b}}

(23)

and the proof is completed.
∎

Remark
.

Comparing (
21
) with (
12
) we see that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\frac{b}{(b+k)^{2}}=\frac{H_{n+b}-H_{b-1}}{\binom{n+b}{n}}

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\frac{k}{(b+k)^{2}}=\frac{-H_{n+b}+H_{b}}{\binom{n+b}{n}},

which upon addition again yield (
23
).

Theorem 7
.

If
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, then

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{(-1)^{k-1}\frac{{\binom{{n}}{k}}}{{\binom{{b+k}}{k}}}H_{k}}=\frac{b}{{n+b}}\left({H_{n+b}-H_{b}}\right)+\frac{n}{{\left({n+b}\right)^{2}}}.

(24)

Proof.

Set
c=b
in (
14
) and use (
20
).
∎

Theorem 8
.

If
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
,
b\neq 0
, then

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{{n}}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k+b)^{3}}=\frac{1}{2n+2}\binom{{n+b}}{{n+1}}^{-1}\left({\left({H_{n+b}-H_{b-1}}\right)^{2}-H_{b-1}^{(2)}+H_{n+b}^{(2)}}\right).

(25)

In particular,

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{{n}}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k+1)^{3}}=\frac{1}{2n+2}\left({H_{n+1}^{2}+H_{n+1}^{(2)}}\right).

(26)

Proof.

Write (
12
) as

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{(b+k)^{2}}=\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{\psi(n+b+1)-\psi(b)}{\binom{n+b}{n+1}}

and differentiate with respect to
b
to obtain

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\frac{{(-1)^{k}}}{{(k+b)^{3}}}\binom{{n}}{k}}\\
&\qquad=\frac{1}{2n+2}\binom{{n+b}}{{n+1}}^{-1}\left({\psi(n+b+1)-\psi(b)}\right)^{2}\\
&\qquad\quad-\frac{1}{{2n+2}}\binom{{n+b}}{{n+1}}^{-1}\left({\psi_{1}(n+b+1)-\psi_{1}(b)}\right),\end{split}

where
\psi_{1}(x)
is the trigamma function defined by

\psi_{1}(x)=\frac{d}{{dx}}\psi(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+x)^{2}}

and
H^{(2)}_{r}
is the
r^{\,th}
second order harmonic number,

H_{r}^{(2)}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{j^{2}}.

The result follows upon using the fact that

\psi\left({x+1}\right)-\psi\left({y+1}\right)=H_{x}-H_{y}

(27)

and

\psi_{1}\left({x+1}\right)-\psi_{1}\left({y+1}\right)=H_{y}^{(2)}-H_{x}^{(2)},

(28)

for
x,y\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
.
∎

Theorem 9
.

If
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left({-1}\right)^{k-1}\binom{{n}}{k}H_{k+b}^{(2)}=\frac{{H_{n+b}-H_{b}}}{{n\binom{{n+b}}{n}}},\quad n\neq 0,

(29)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left({-1}\right)^{k-1}\binom{{n}}{k}\frac{{H_{k+b}-H_{b}}}{{k\binom{{k+b}}{k}}}=H_{n+b}^{(2)}-H_{b}^{(2)},

(30)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left({-1}\right)^{k-1}\binom{{n}}{k}\frac{{H_{k+b}}}{{k\binom{{k+b}}{k}}}=\left({H_{n+b}-H_{b}}\right)H_{b}+H_{n+b}^{(2)}-H_{b}^{(2)}.

(31)

Proof.

Differentiate (
15
) with respect to
b
to get (
29
). Identity (
30
) is the inverse of (
29
). Identity (
31
) is obtained by using (
17
) in (
30
).
∎

Theorem 10
.

If
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, then

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{(-1)^{k-1}\frac{{\binom{{n}}{k}}}{{\binom{{b+k}}{b}}}H_{k}H_{k+b}}&=\frac{1}{{n+b}}\left({bH_{b}-\frac{n}{{n+b}}}\right)\left({H_{n+b}-H_{b}}\right)\\
&\qquad+\frac{b}{{n+b}}\left({H_{n+b}^{(2)}-H_{b}^{(2)}}\right)+\frac{n}{{\left({n+b}\right)^{2}}}\left({\frac{2}{{n+b}}+H_{b}}\right).\end{split}

(32)

Proof.

Write (
24
) as

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{(-1)^{k-1}\frac{{\binom{{n}}{k}}}{{\binom{{b+k}}{k}}}H_{k}}=\frac{b}{{n+b}}\left({\psi(n+b+1)-\psi(b+1)}\right)+\frac{n}{{\left({n+b}\right)^{2}}}

and differentiate with respect to
b
, using (
10
). Use (
24
) again in simplifying the left hand side of the resulting expression.
∎

In particular, for all positive integers
n
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}^{2}=\frac{{H_{n}}}{n}-\frac{2}{n^{2}}

(33)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{{n+k}}{k}}H_{k}H_{k+n}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2}\left(H_{2n}^{(2)}-H_{n}^{(2)}\right)+\frac{1}{2}H_{2n}\left(H_{n}-\frac{1}{2n}\right)-\frac{1}{2}H_{n}H_{n-1}+\frac{1}{4n^{2}}.

(34)

Corollary 11
.

If
n
is a positive integer, then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}\frac{H_{k}}{k}=H_{n}^{(2)}.

(35)

Proof.

Treat identity (
33
) as the binomial transform of the sequence
H_{n}^{2}
. The inverse relation yields

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\frac{H_{k}}{k}+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k^{2}}=H_{n}^{2}.

But (see Boyadzhiev’s book
[
4
, p. 64]
)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(H_{n}^{2}+H_{n}^{(2)}\right)

and the proof is completed.
∎

Remark
.

Identity (
35
) can also be obtained by taking the limit of (
9
) as
b
approaches zero as well as simply setting
b=0
in (
30
) or (
31
).

3
Some identities involving odd harmonic numbers

This section contains some combinatorial identities involving odd harmonic numbers
O_{n}
, which are defined by

O_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2k-1},\quad O_{0}=0.

Obvious relations between harmonic numbers
H_{n}
and odd harmonic numbers
O_{n}
are given by

H_{2n}=\frac{1}{2}H_{n}+O_{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad H_{2n-1}=\frac{1}{2}H_{n-1}+O_{n}.

(36)

Additional relations are contained in the next lemma.

Lemma 1
.

If
n
is an integer, then

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}=2O_{n}-2\ln 2,

(37)

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}-H_{-1/2}=2O_{n},

(38)

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}-H_{1/2}=2\left({O_{n}-1}\right),

(39)

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{-1/2}=2O_{n+1},

(40)

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{1/2}=2\left({O_{n+1}-1}\right),

(41)

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{n-1/2}=\frac{2}{{2n+1}},

(42)

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}-H_{-3/2}=2\left({O_{n}-1}\right),

(43)

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{-3/2}=2\left({O_{n+1}-1}\right).

(44)

Proof.

Use (
3
) as the definition of the harmonic numbers for all complex
n
(excluding zero and the negative integers) and use the known result for the digamma function at half-integer arguments
[
12
, Eq. (51)]
, namely,

\psi(n+1/2)=-\gamma-2\ln 2+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2k-1}.

∎

Lemma 2

(Gould
[
8
, Identities Z.45 and Z.51]
)
.

If
r
and
s
are integers such that
0\leq s\leq r
, then

\binom{{r+1/2}}{s}=\binom{{2r+1}}{{2s}}\binom{{r}}{s}^{-1}2^{-2s}\binom{{2s}}{s}

(45)

and

\binom{{r-1/2}}{s}=\binom{{r}}{s}\binom{{2r-2s}}{{r-s}}^{-1}2^{-2s}\binom{{2r}}{r}.

(46)

We also have

\displaystyle\binom{{r}}{{1/2}}=\dfrac{{2^{2r+1}}}{{\pi\binom{{2r}}{r}}},\quad\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Gould}{}{}, Identity Z.48]}},

(47)

\displaystyle\binom{{r}}{{-1/2}}=\dfrac{{2^{2r+1}}}{{\pi(2r+1)\binom{{2r}}{r}}},

(48)

and

\binom{{r-1/2}}{{r+1}}=-\frac{1}{{r+1}}\binom{{2r}}{r}\frac{1}{{2^{2r+1}}}.

(49)

Proof.

Identity (
48
) follows from the fact that

\binom{{r}}{{-1/2}}=\frac{{r!}}{{(-1/2)!(r+1/2)!}},

while (
49
) is a consequence of

\binom{{r-1/2}}{{r+1}}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{{(r-1/2)!}}{{(r+1)!\sqrt{\pi}}}.

(50)

∎

Theorem 12
.

If
n
is a non-negative integer, then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\left({2k+1}\right)^{2}}=\frac{2^{2n+1}}{n+1}\frac{O_{n+1}}{\binom{2(n+1)}{n+1}},

(51)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\frac{2^{2k+1}}{k+1}\frac{O_{k+1}}{\binom{2(k+1)}{k+1}}=\frac{1}{(2n+1)^{2}},

(52)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{{n}}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{\left({2k-1}\right)^{2}}=2^{2n}\frac{{O_{n}-1}}{\binom{{2n}}{n}},

(53)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{{n}}{k}(-1)^{k-1}2^{2k}\frac{O_{k}}{\binom{{2k}}{k}}=\frac{2n}{(2n-1)^{2}}.

(54)

Proof.

The first identity is obtained by setting
b=1/2
in (
12
) and using (
40
) and (
45
);
while the second is its inverse transform. Identity (
53
) is obtained by setting
b=-1/2
in (
12
)
and using (
43
) and (
49
). To prove identity (
54
) use the inverse binomial transform
of (
53
) to get

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{{n}}{k}(-1)^{k-1}2^{2k}\frac{O_{k}}{\binom{{2k}}{k}}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{{n}}{k}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{2^{2k}}{\binom{{2k}}{k}}=\frac{1}{(2n-1)^{2}}.

But

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{{n}}{k}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{2^{2k}}{\binom{{2k}}{k}}=\frac{1}{2n-1}

(55)

and the proof of (
54
) is completed.
∎

Theorem 13
.

If
n
is a non-negative integer, then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{(-1)^{k+1}\binom{{n}}{k}\frac{{2k-1}}{{2^{2(k-1)}}}\binom{{2(k-1)}}{{k-1}}O_{k}}=\frac{1}{{2^{2(n-1)}}}\binom{{2(n-1)}}{{n-1}}\left({\frac{{2n}}{{2n-1}}-O_{n}}\right),

(57)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{(-1)^{k+1}\binom{{n}}{k}2^{-2k-2}\binom{{2(k+1)}}{{k+1}}(O_{k+1}-1)}=\frac{2^{-2n-2}}{{2n+1}}\binom{{2(n+1)}}{{n+1}}\left({O_{n+1}-\frac{1}{{2n+1}}}\right),

(58)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{(-1)^{k+1}\binom{{n}}{k}2^{-2k}\binom{{2k}}{k}(O_{k}-1)}=2^{-2n}\binom{{2n}}{n}\left({\frac{{2n}}{{2n+1}}+O_{n+1}}\right).

(59)

Proof.

These results follow from (

). To obtain (

), set

,

in (

) and (

). Identity (

) comes from setting

,

in (

), (

) and (

).
∎

Theorem 14
.

If
n
is a positive integer, then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{{n}}{k}(-1)^{k+1}O_{k}=\frac{{2^{2n-1}}}{{n\binom{{2n}}{n}}},

(60)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{{n}}{k}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{2^{2k-1}}{k\binom{{2k}}{k}}=O_{n},

(61)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{{n}}{k}(-1)^{k+1}O_{k+1}=\frac{{2^{2n-1}}}{{n(2n+1)\binom{{2n}}{n}}},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{{n}}{k}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{{2^{2k-1}}}{k(2k+1)\binom{{2k}}{k}}=O_{n+1}-1.

Proof.

Write (
15

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{(-1)^{k+1}\binom{{n}}{k}\left({H_{k+b}-H_{c}}\right)}=\frac{1}{{n\binom{{n+b}}{n}}}.

Evaluate at

Theorem 15
.

If
n
is a non-negative integer, then

Final comments

In these notes, we have demonstrated how a combinatorial identity attributed to Frisch can be used to prove a series of
harmonic number and odd harmonic number identities. Some of the results that we derived are known and here we provide
final comments and give further references.

We begin by pointing out that identity (
33
) can also be found in a recent article by Batir
[
3
, Identity 18]
.
Next, identity (
23
) can be restated as

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\frac{b}{k+b}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k}{b+k}.

This identity is known and two probabilistic proofs were given recently by Peterson
[
11
]
and Nakata
[
10
]
.
Peterson
[
11
]
writes (
12
) in the form

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\left(\frac{b}{b+k}\right)^{2}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k}{b+k}\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{b}{b+k}\right),

and also states an expression for the generalization involving an additional parameter
m

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\left(\frac{b}{b+k}\right)^{m},\qquad m\geq 1.

(79)

In 2019, Bai and Luo
[
2
]
derived a new expression for Peterson’s identity (
79
)
using a partial fraction decomposition and involving generalized harmonic numbers. As applications of their main result, they
stated some harmonic number identities. One of their special cases is our identity (
26
).

It is remarkable that a simple expression for (
79
) is also hidden in Frisch’s identity (
5
).
Indeed, setting
c=1
in (
5
) yields (
23
), i.e.,

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{b+k}=\frac{1}{b\binom{n+b}{b}}.

Differentiating both sides
m
times w.r.t.
b
gives

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(b+k)^{m}}=\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}\frac{d^{m-1}}{db^{m-1}}\frac{1}{b\binom{n+b}{b}},

(80)

which shows such a simple expression.

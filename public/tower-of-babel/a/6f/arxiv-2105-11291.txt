Global Strong and Weak Solutions to the Initial-boundary-value Problem of 2D Compressible MHD System with Large Initial Data and Vacuum
Yazhou C HEN, Bin H UANG, Xiaoding S HI

Abstract
In this paper, we study the barotropic compressible magnetohydrodynamic equations with the shear viscosity being a positive constant and the bulk one being proportional to a power of the density in a general two-dimensional bounded simply connected domain. For initial density allowed to vanish, we prove that the initial-boundary-value problem of 2D compressible MHD system admits the global strong and weak solutions without any restrictions on the size of initial data provided the shear viscosity is a positive constant and bulk one is λ = ρ β \lambda=\rho^{\beta} with β > 4 / 3 \beta>4/3 . As we known, this is the first result concerning the global existence of strong solutions to the compressible MHD system in general two-dimensional bounded domains with large initial data and vacuum.

Introduction

We deal with the viscous barotropic compressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations for isentropic flows in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which can be written as

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\rho u)=0,\\
(\rho u)_{t}+\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla P=\mu\Delta u+\nabla((\mu+\lambda)\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)+H\cdot\nabla H-\frac{1}{2}\nabla|H|^{2},\\
H_{t}+u\cdot\nabla H-H\cdot\nabla u+H\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u=\nu\Delta H,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits H=0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
,
t\geq 0
is time, and
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
is the spatial coordinate. The unknown functions
\rho=\rho(x,t),u=(u_{1},u_{2})(x,t),
and
H=(H_{1},H_{2})(x,t)
denote the fluid density, velocity and magnetic field respectively and the pressure
P
is given by

P=P(\rho)=a\rho^{\gamma},

(1.2)

with the constants
a>0
and
\gamma>1
.
The shear viscosity
\mu
and the bulk one
\lambda
satisfy the following hypothesis (see
[
42
]
):

\mu=const.>0,\quad\lambda=\lambda(\rho)=b\rho^{\beta},

(1.3)

with the positive constants
b
and
\gamma
.
The constant
\nu>0
is the resistivity coefficient which is inversely proportional to the electrical conductivity constant. In what follows, without loss of generality, we set
a=b=1
.
Throughout this paper,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes a simply connected bounded domain with
C^{2,1}
boundary
\partial\Omega
.
In addition, this paper concerns the problem of (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) with the initial data

\displaystyle(\rho,\rho u,H)\big|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},m_{0},H_{0}),\quad\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega,

(1.4)

and the boundary conditions

\displaystyle u\cdot n=0,\,\,\,{\rm curl}u=0,

\displaystyle\text{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.5)

\displaystyle\quad H=0,

\displaystyle\text{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.6)

where
n=(n_{1},n_{2})
is the unit outward normal vector to
\partial\Omega
.

Until now, all the global existence of strong (classical) solutions for the multi-dimensional compressible MHD equations were obtained with some “smallness” assumptions on the initial data. In contrast, positive results without limitation on the size of initial value are rather fewer.
It should be noted that when
H=0

1.1

Vaigant-Kazhikov
[

\beta>3
.
Recently, Huang-Li
[
20

21
]
applied some new ideas based on commutator theory and blow up criterion, and improved the conclusion in periodic case, and even for the Cauchy problem in the whole space (also see
[
23
]
), demanding only
\beta>\frac{4}{3}
. Up to now,
\beta>\frac{4}{3}
still seems to be the best result one may expect. Let us turn back to the compressible MHD system with density-dependent viscosity. Lv-Huang
[

obtained the local strong solutions of Cauchy problem of the two-dimensional compressible MHD equations with vacuum as far field density. Mei
[

established the global well-posedness of classical solutions to the 2D compressible MHD equations with large initial data and vacuum on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

However, for general domains, the theory of large initial data for compressible MHD system is still blank, due to boundary terms do bring some essential difficulties and the classical commutator theory in the case of the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is no longer available for general bounded domains.
Very recently, Fan et al.
[
13
]
study the global existence of strong and weak solutions of compressible Navier-Stokes system with large initial data in general simply connected domains. They get a pointwise representation of the effective viscous flux via applying Green’s function, as a substitute for the commutator for the case of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which plays an important role to derive the upper bound of density

\mathbb{D}

37

G(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big(\ln|x-y|+\ln\big||x|y-\frac{x}{|x|}\big|\Big).

(1.7)

For the Neumann problem in
\mathbb{D}
as follows:

\begin{cases}\triangle F=\mathrm{div}f&\mathrm{in}\,\,\mathbb{D},\\
\frac{\partial F}{\partial n}=f\cdot n&\mathrm{on}\,\,\partial\mathbb{D},\end{cases}

(1.8)

the Green’s identity yields that

has the following integral representation:

F(x)=-\int_{\mathbb{D}}\nabla_{y}G(x,y)\cdot f(y)dy+\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\frac{\partial G}{\partial n}(x,y)F(y)dS_{y}.

(1.9)

For the general simply connected domain
\Omega
, the similar integral representation has been derived by applying the Riemann mapping theorem and the pull-back Green’s function method (see
[
13
, Lemma 3.7]
).
Fortunately, these methods are still available to get a pointwise representation of the effective viscous flux for compressible MHD system, defined by

\displaystyle F\triangleq(2\mu+\lambda)\mathrm{div}u-P-\frac{1}{2}|H|^{2}.

(1.10)

\begin{cases}\partial_{1}\varphi_{1}=\partial_{2}\varphi_{2},\\
\partial_{2}\varphi_{1}=-\partial_{1}\varphi_{2}.\end{cases}

(2.10)

and shares the following crucial properties:

(i)
\varphi(x)
is a one to one holomorphic mapping from
\Omega
to
\mathbb{D}
and maps the boundary
\partial\Omega
onto the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
.

(ii) For any integer

, there exists some constant
C
depending only on
\Omega
and

such that

|\nabla^{k}\varphi(x)|\leq C,\,\,\,\forall x\in\bar{\Omega}.

(2.11)

Consequently,

|\nabla^{k}\varphi(x)-\nabla^{k}\varphi(y)|\leq C|x-y|,\,\,\,\forall x,y\in\bar{\Omega}.

(2.12)

(iii) There exist two positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
such that

c_{1}|x-y|\leq|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leq c_{2}|x-y|.

(2.13)

(iv) Angle is preserved by
\varphi(x)
, that is, for any two smooth curves
\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t):(0,1)\rightarrow\bar{\Omega}
,

\langle\frac{d}{dt}\gamma_{1}(t),\frac{d}{dt}\gamma_{2}(t)\rangle=\langle\frac{d}{dt}\varphi(\gamma_{1}(t)),\frac{d}{dt}\varphi(\gamma_{2}(t))\rangle,\,\,\,\forall t\in(0,1).

(2.14)

(v) For any harmonic function

in
\mathbb{D}
,
h(\varphi(x))
is still harmonic in
\Omega
, that is,

\Delta h(y)=0\ \mathrm{in}\ \mathbb{D}\Rightarrow\Delta h(\varphi(x))=0\ \mathrm{in}\ \Omega.

(2.15)

(vi) Let

be the unit outer normal vector of
\partial\Omega
at
x_{0}
and
\partial\mathbb{D}
at
\varphi(x_{0})
respectively. Then

n\cdot\nabla\varphi(x_{0})=|\nabla\varphi_{1}(x_{0})|\,\widetilde{n}.

(2.16)

We review that

\begin{split}A_{1}^{2}(t)\leq&C\|\sqrt{2\mu+\lambda}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C\|P\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C\|H\|^{4}_{L^{4}}+C\|\omega\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla H\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\\
\leq&C\|\sqrt{2\mu+\lambda}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C\|\omega\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla H\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C,\end{split}

(3.40)

which together with (

\int_{0}^{T}A_{1}^{2}dt\leq C.

(3.41)

Combining (
3.39
) with (
3.41
), applying Gronwall’s inequality, yields (
3.23
) and finishes the proof of Lemma

.

In the following, adapting the ideas in
[
13
]
, we derive the pointwise representation of

by applying Riemann mapping theorem and the pull-back Green’s function method.
Precisely, let
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}):\bar{\Omega}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{D}}
be the conformal mapping which satisfies Lemma

.
We then define the pull back Green’s function

\displaystyle N(x,y)\triangleq-\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big(\ln|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|+\ln\big||\varphi(x)|\varphi(y)-\frac{\varphi(x)}{|\varphi(x)|}\big|\Big).

(3.42)

Since the outer normal derivative of
N
on the boundary

is no longer constant,

is not the “real” Green’s function of
\Omega
in the classical sense but still sufficient for our further calculations. Moreover, since the conformal mapping preserves angles, one immediately has the following conclusion (see
[

\frac{\partial{N}}{\partial n}(x,y_{0})=-\frac{1}{2\pi}|\nabla\varphi_{1}(y_{0})|,

where

is the unit outer normal at

.

Now, we turn to use the pull back Green’s function
N
defined as in (

) to give a pointwise representation of

in
\Omega
via Green’s identity as follows:

Lemma 3.7

Let
F\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})\cap C^{2}(\Omega)
solve the Neumann problem

\begin{cases}\Delta F=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\rho\dot{u}-H\cdot\nabla H)&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial F}{\partial n}=(\rho\dot{u}-H\cdot\nabla H)\cdot n&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(3.44)

and
N
is the pull back Green’s function defined as in (
3.42
). Then for
x\in\Omega
, there holds

F(x)=-\int\nabla_{y}N(x,y)\cdot(\rho\dot{u}+H\cdot\nabla H)(y)dy+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial N}{\partial n}(x,y)F(y)dS_{y}.

(3.45)

Furthermore, for the boundary condition
u\cdot n=0
on
\partial\Omega
, it follows that

F(x)=-\frac{D}{Dt}F_{0}+F_{1}+F_{2}+F_{3},

(3.46)

with

\displaystyle F_{0}=

\displaystyle-\int\partial_{y_{j}}N(x,y)\rho u_{j}(y)dy,

(3.47)

\displaystyle F_{1}=

\displaystyle\int\left[\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}N(x,y)u_{i}(x)+\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}N(x,y)u_{i}(y)\right]\rho u_{j}(y)dy,

(3.48)

\displaystyle F_{2}=

\displaystyle-\int\nabla_{y}N(x,y)\cdot(H\cdot\nabla H)(y)dy,

(3.49)

\displaystyle F_{3}=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial N}{\partial n}(x,y)F(y)dS_{y}.

(3.50)

Proof
.
The proof of (
3.44
) is essentially the same as that in
[
13
, Lemma 3.7]
and we just sketch it for completeness.
Denote
N(x,y)=N_{1}(x,y)+N_{2}(x,y)
, where

N_{1}(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|,\ N_{2}(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\big||\varphi(x)|\varphi(y)-\frac{\varphi(x)}{|\varphi(x)|}\big|.

By (
2.15
),
N_{2}(x,\cdot)
remains harmonic in
\Omega
. Applying Green’s second identity to

and
N_{2}(x,\cdot)
in
\Omega
, we have

-\int N_{2}\Delta Fdy=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial N_{2}}{\partial n}F-N_{2}\frac{\partial F}{\partial n}\right)dS_{y}.

(3.51)

For

in
\Omega\backslash\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)

\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)=\varphi^{-1}(B_{r}(\varphi(x)))
is just the inverse image under

\varphi(x)
of small enough radius

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\backslash\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)}(\Delta N_{1}F-N_{1}\Delta F)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial N_{1}}{\partial n}F-N_{1}\frac{\partial F}{\partial n}\right)dS_{y}-\int_{\partial\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)}\left(\frac{\partial N_{1}}{\partial n}F-N_{1}\frac{\partial F}{\partial n}\right)dS_{y}.

Letting
r\rightarrow 0
and using the fact that

\Delta N_{1}=0
in
\Omega\backslash\widetilde{B}_{r}(x),
we have

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}N_{1}\Delta Fdy=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial N_{1}}{\partial n}F-N_{1}\frac{\partial F}{\partial n}\right)dS_{y}

(3.52)

\displaystyle-\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\int_{\partial\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)}\left(\frac{\partial N_{1}}{\partial n}F-N_{1}\frac{\partial F}{\partial n}\right)dS_{y}.

For
r
small enough,

\begin{split}&\quad\bigg|\int_{\partial\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)}N_{1}\frac{\partial F}{\partial n}dS_{y}\bigg|\leq\int_{\partial\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)}|N_{1}(x,y)|dS_{y}\cdot\sup_{\partial\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)}|\nabla F|\\
&\leq\int_{\partial B_{r}(\varphi(x))}\left|\mathrm{ln}{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}\right|\frac{1}{|\nabla\varphi_{1}(y)|}dS_{\varphi(y)}\cdot\sup_{\partial\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)}|\nabla F|\\
&\leq Cr|\mathrm{ln}\,r|\cdot\sup_{\partial\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)}|\nabla\varphi_{1}|^{-1}\sup_{\partial\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)}|\nabla F|\rightarrow 0\ as\ r\rightarrow 0.\end{split}

(3.53)

and

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{\partial\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)}F\frac{\partial N_{1}}{\partial n}dS_{y}=\int_{\partial\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)}\vec{n}_{y}\cdot\nabla_{y}N_{1}(x,y)F(y)dS_{y}\\
&=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)}\frac{\nabla\varphi(y)[\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)]}{|\nabla\varphi_{1}(y)||\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}\cdot\frac{\nabla\varphi(y)[\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)]}{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{2}}F(y)dS_{y}\\
&=-\frac{1}{2\pi r}\int_{\partial\widetilde{B}_{r}(x)}|\nabla\varphi_{1}(y)|F(y)dS_{y}\\
&=-\frac{1}{2\pi r}\int_{\partial B_{r}(\varphi(x))}F(\varphi^{-1}(\tilde{y}))dS_{\tilde{y}}\rightarrow F(x)\ as\ r\rightarrow 0,\end{split}

(3.54)

where we use (
2.10
) and (
3.43
). Adding (
3.51
) and (
3.52
) together, by using (
3.44
), (
3.53
) and (

\begin{split}F(x)=&\int N(x,y)\mathrm{div}(\rho\dot{u}+H\cdot\nabla H)(y)dy-\int_{\partial\Omega}N(x,y)(\rho\dot{u}+H\cdot\nabla H)\cdot\vec{n}(y)dS_{y}\\
&+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial N}{\partial n}(x,y)F(y)dS_{y}\\
=&-\int\nabla_{y}N(x,y)\cdot(\rho\dot{u}+H\cdot\nabla H)(y)dy+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial N}{\partial n}(x,y)F(y)dS_{y},\end{split}

which gives (
3.45
).
By (
1.1
)
1
and the boundary condition

\begin{split}&-\int\nabla_{y}N(x,y)\cdot\rho\dot{u}(y)dy\\

=&-(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+u\cdot\nabla)\int\partial_{y_{j}}N(x,y)\rho u_{j}(y)dy\\
&+\int\left[\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}N(x,y)u_{i}(x)+\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}N(x,y)u_{i}(y)\right]\rho u_{j}(y)dy.\end{split}

(3.55)

Then we rewrite (

\begin{split}F(x)=&-\frac{D}{Dt}\int\partial_{y_{j}}N(x,y)\rho u_{j}(y)dy\\
&+\int\left[\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}N(x,y)u_{i}(x)+\partial_{y_{i}}\partial_{y_{j}}N(x,y)u_{i}(y)\right]\rho u_{j}(y)dy\\
&-\int\nabla_{y}N(x,y)\cdot(H\cdot\nabla H)(y)dy+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial N}{\partial n}(x,y)F(y)dS_{y}.\end{split}

(3.56)

Proposition 3.8

such that

Proof
.

)

\begin{split}\frac{D}{Dt}\theta(\rho)=&-(2\mu+\lambda)\mathrm{div}u=-F-P-|H|^{2}/2\leq-F\\

First, we state the crucial point-wise estimate on

As mentioned in

Pushforward measures on homogeneous spaces of non-unimodular groups and properties of modular functions
Takashi Satomi

Abstract
This paper shows that a formula for the pushforward measures on the fiber bundle G → G / H G\to G/H of homogeneous space of locally compact groups G G and H H can be written down by using the modular functions. As a result, we obtain the equality Δ G ​ ( h ) Δ G / N ​ ( h ​ N ) = Δ H ​ ( h ) Δ H / N ​ ( h ​ N ) \displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{G}(h)}{\Delta_{G/N}(hN)}=\frac{\Delta_{H}(h)}{\Delta_{H/N}(hN)} (0.1) on the modular functions for any closed subgroups N N and H H of a locally compact group G G with N ⊲ G N\lhd G and N ⊂ H ⊂ G N\subset H\subset G and any h ∈ H h\in H .



Keywords:
locally compact group, homogeneous space, modular function, pushforward measure.

Introduction

We use the notation
H<G
and
H\lhd G
if
H
is a closed subgroup and a closed normal subgroup of a locally compact (Hausdorff) group
G
, respectively.
This paper presents one can write down the pushforward measures on the fiber bundle
p^{G\to G/H}\colon G\to G/H
of the homogeneous space of locally compact groups
G
and
H
with
H<G
by using the modular functions (Theorem
1.4
).
As a result, we obtain an equality on the modular functions (Corollary
1.5
).
That is, we have
\Delta_{G}(h)/\Delta_{G/N}(hN)=\Delta_{H}(h)/\Delta_{H/N}(hN)
for any
N<H<G
with
N\lhd G
and any
h\in H
, where
\Delta_{G}
is the modular function of
G
.
When
H=N
(i.e.,
H\lhd G
), this equality corresponds to a known result
\Delta_{G}|_{H}=\Delta_{H}
(Example
1.6
).

In this paper, we denote by
C(X)
the vector space of continuous functions
X\to\mathbb{C}
, and by
C_{c}(X)\subset C(X)
the the subspace of continuous functions with compact support.
A complex Radon measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space
X
is defined as a linear form of
C_{c}(X)
.
When
p\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
is a continuous map between locally compact Hausdorff spaces
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, the pushforward (whose precise definition will be given in Definition
2.1
) of the measure
\mu\in C_{c}(X_{1})^{*}
is written as
p_{*}(\mu)\in C_{c}(X_{2})^{*}
.
If
p
is proper (i.e., the inverse image of any compact set is also compact), the pushforward
p_{*}(\mu)
can be defined for any Radon measure
\mu\in C_{c}(X_{1})^{*}
.
On the other hand, it needs the properness condition on the support of the measure
\mu\in C_{c}(X_{1})^{*}
for the existance of the pushforward
p_{*}(\mu)
if
p
is not proper.

Definition 1.1
.

We denote by
p^{G\to G/H}\colon G\to G/H
the canonical quotient map for locally compact groups
G
and
H
with
H<G
.
The set
M_{H}(G)\subset C_{c}(G)^{*}
of complex Radon measures is defined as

\displaystyle M_{H}(G)\coloneqq\{\phi(g)dg\mid\phi\in C(G),\;\text{ $p^{G\to G/H}|_{\mathrm{supp}(\phi)}$ is proper }\}.

(1.1)

Here
\mathrm{supp}(\phi)
is the support of
\phi
, and
dg
is a left Haar measure of
G
(but
M_{H}(G)
does not depend on the choice of
dg
).

Example 1.2
.

We have
M_{\{e\}}(G)=\{\phi(g)dg\mid\phi\in C(G)\}
, where
e\in G
is the identity element of
G
.

The following theorem is one of the main theorems of this paper:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\overline{G}\coloneqq G/N
and
\overline{H}\coloneqq H/N
for locally compact groups
G
,
H
, and
N
with
N<H<G
.
We denote by
p^{G\to\overline{G}}\colon G\to\overline{G}
and
p^{\overline{G}\to G/H}\colon\overline{G}\to G/H
the canonical maps.

(1)

One has
M_{H}(G)\subset M_{N}(G)
.

(2)

The pushforwards
(p^{\overline{G}\to G/H}\circ p^{G\to\overline{G}})_{*}(\mu)
and
p_{*}^{\overline{G}\to G/H}\circ p_{*}^{G\to\overline{G}}(\mu)
can be defined for any
\mu\in M_{H}(G)
.
Furthermore, one has

\displaystyle(p^{\overline{G}\to G/H}\circ p^{G\to\overline{G}})_{*}(\mu)=p_{*}^{\overline{G}\to G/H}\circ p_{*}^{G\to\overline{G}}(\mu).

(1.2)

(3)

We assume

and denote by
p^{G/H\to\overline{G}/\overline{H}}\colon G/H\to\overline{G}/\overline{H}
and
p^{\overline{G}\to\overline{G}/\overline{H}}\colon\overline{G}\to\overline{G}/\overline{H}

\displaystyle p_{*}^{G/H\to\overline{G}/\overline{H}}\circ p_{*}^{G\to G/H}(\mu)=p_{*}^{\overline{G}\to\overline{G}/\overline{H}}\circ p_{*}^{G\to\overline{G}}(\mu).

(1.3)

(4)

For
N\lhd G
, one has
p_{*}^{G\to\overline{G}}(\mu)\in M_{\overline{H}}(\overline{G})
for any
\mu\in M_{H}(G)
.

In addition, we can write down the pushforward measure of the fiber bundle
p^{G\to G/H}
by using the modular functions as follows:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
,
H
,

be as in Theorem

We write

as a left Haar measure and

as the modular function of

\displaystyle\int_{G/H}\int_{H}\nu(gh)dhdp_{*}^{G\to G/H}(\phi(g)dg)=\int_{G}\nu(g)\int_{H}\phi(gh)\frac{\Delta_{G}(h)}{\Delta_{H}(h)}dhdg

(1.4)

holds for any

.

As a result, we obtain a property of the modular functions as follows:

Corollary 1.5

Let

,

,

, and

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{G}(h)}{\Delta_{\overline{G}}(\overline{h})}=\frac{\Delta_{H}(h)}{\Delta_{\overline{H}}(\overline{h})}

2
Pushforward measure

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
.
In Subsection

) on locally compact Hausdorff spaces in a general setting and show several properties of this measure to prove Theorem

2.1
Definition and properties of the pushforward measure

In this subsection, we review the definition of the pushforward measure for locally compact Hausdorff spaces.
In addition, some properties of such a measure is also discussed.
For any continuous map
p\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
between locally compact Hausdorff spaces
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, the set
\mathcal{M}(p,X_{1})
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(p,X_{1})\coloneqq\{\mu\in C_{c}(X_{1})^{*}\mid\text{ $p|_{\mathrm{supp}(\mu)}$ is proper }\}.

(2.1)

For any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(p,X_{1})
and
\alpha_{2}\in C_{c}(X_{2})
, there exists
\alpha_{1}\in C_{c}(X_{1})
such that

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(x)=\alpha_{2}\circ p(x)

(2.2)

for any
x\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
by the Tietze extension theorem since
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\cap\mathrm{supp}(\alpha_{2}\circ p)
is compact.
In this case, the integration

\displaystyle\int_{X_{1}}\alpha_{1}\;d\mu

(2.3)

does not depend on the choice of
\alpha_{1}
by the definition of
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
.
Thus, the pushforward measure is defined as follows:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
p\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
be a continuous map between locally compact Hausdorff spaces
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
.
Then the map
p_{*}\colon\mathcal{M}(p,X_{1})\to C_{c}(X_{2})^{*}
is defined as

\displaystyle\int_{X_{2}}\alpha_{2}\;dp_{*}(\mu)\coloneqq\int_{X_{1}}\alpha_{1}\;d\mu

\displaystyle(\mu\in\mathcal{M}(p,X_{1}),\;\alpha_{2}\in C_{c}(X_{2})),

(2.4)

where
\alpha_{1}\in C_{c}(X_{1})
satisfies (
2.2
) for any
x\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
.
This measure
p_{*}(\mu)
is called the pushforward of
\mu
.

The support of the pushforward measure is contained in the image of the original measure as follows:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
, and
p
be as in Definition
2.1
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(p_{*}(\mu))\subset p(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))

(2.5)

holds for any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(p,X_{1})
.

Proof.

Let
x_{2}\in\mathrm{supp}(p_{*}(\mu))
.
Since
p|_{\mathrm{supp}(\mu)}
is a proper map to the locally compact Hausdorff space
X_{2}
by
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(p,X_{1})
, the image
p(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))
of the closed set
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
is also closed.
Thus, it suffices to show
p(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))\cap U\neq\emptyset
for any open neighborhood
U
of
x_{2}
.
Since there exists
\alpha_{2}\in C_{c}(X_{2})
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\alpha_{2})\subset U
and

\displaystyle\int_{X_{2}}\alpha_{2}\;dp_{*}(\mu)\neq 0

(2.6)

by
x_{2}\in\mathrm{supp}(p_{*}(\mu))
, there exists
\alpha_{1}\in C_{c}(X_{1})
such that

\displaystyle\int_{X_{1}}\alpha_{1}\;d\mu\neq 0

(2.7)

and (
2.2
) hold for any
x\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
.
Thus, there exists
x_{1}\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
such that
\alpha_{1}(x_{1})\neq 0
and hence
\alpha_{2}\circ p(x_{1})\neq 0
.
Then

\displaystyle p(x_{1})\in p(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))\cap\mathrm{supp}(\alpha_{2})\subset p(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))\cap U

(2.8)

holds and hence we obtain (
2.5
).
∎

Now we show the following lemma to prove Theorem
1.3
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p^{1,2}\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
and
p^{2,3}\colon X_{2}\to X_{3}
be continuous maps between locally compact Hausdorff spaces.

(1)

One has
\mathcal{M}(p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2},X_{1})\subset\mathcal{M}(p^{1,2},X_{1})
.

(2)

One has
p_{*}^{1,2}(\mathcal{M}(p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2},X_{1}))\subset\mathcal{M}(p^{2,3},X_{2})
.

(3)

One has
(p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2})_{*}=p_{*}^{2,3}\circ p_{*}^{1,2}
.

Proof.

(1)

Let
K\subset X_{2}
be a compact set and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2},X_{1})
.
Then it suffices to show that
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\cap(p^{1,2})^{-1}(K)
is compact.
We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\cap(p^{1,2})^{-1}(K)\subset\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\cap(p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2})^{-1}(p^{2,3}(K)).

(2.9)

Since
p^{2,3}(K)
is compact,
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\cap(p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2})^{-1}(p^{2,3}(K))
is also compact by
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2},X_{1})
.
In addition, the sets
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
and
(p^{1,2})^{-1}(K)
are closed and hence
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\cap(p^{1,2})^{-1}(K)
is compact.

(2)

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2},X_{1})
and
K\subset X_{3}
be a compact set.
It suffices to show that
(p^{2,3})^{-1}(K)\cap\mathrm{supp}(p_{*}^{1,2}(\mu))
is compact.
Then

\displaystyle(p^{2,3})^{-1}(K)\cap\mathrm{supp}(p_{*}^{1,2}(\mu))

\displaystyle\subset(p^{2,3})^{-1}(K)\cap p^{1,2}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))

(2.10)

\displaystyle=p^{1,2}((p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2})^{-1}(K)\cap\mathrm{supp}(\mu))

(2.11)

holds by Lemma
2.2
.
Since
(p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2})^{-1}(K)\cap\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
is compact by
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2},X_{1})
, the set
(p^{2,3})^{-1}(K)\cap\mathrm{supp}(p_{*}^{1,2}(\mu))
is also compact.

(3)

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2},X_{1})
and
\alpha_{3}\in C_{c}(X_{3})
.
Then it suffices to show

\displaystyle\int_{X_{3}}\alpha_{3}\;d(p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2})_{*}(\mu)=\int_{X_{3}}\alpha_{3}\;dp_{*}^{2,3}\circ p_{*}^{1,2}(\mu).

(2.12)

We note that both sides of this equality can be defined by
(1)
and
(2)
.
Then there exists
\alpha_{1}\in C_{c}(X_{1})
such that
\alpha_{1}(x_{1})=\alpha_{3}\circ p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2}(x_{1})
for any
x_{1}\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
.
For any
x_{1},x_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
with
p^{1,2}(x_{1})=p^{1,2}(x_{1}^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(x_{1})=\alpha_{3}\circ p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2}(x_{1})=\alpha_{3}\circ p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2}(x_{1}^{\prime})=\alpha_{1}(x_{1}^{\prime}).

(2.13)

Thus, there exists
\alpha_{2}\in C(p^{1,2}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu)))
such that
\alpha_{2}\circ p^{1,2}(x_{1})=\alpha_{1}(x_{1})
for any
x_{1}\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
.
We can extend
\alpha_{2}
to
X_{2}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\alpha_{2}\in C_{c}(X_{2})
by the compactness of
\mathrm{supp}(\alpha_{1})
and the Tietze extension theorem.
For any
x_{2}\in\mathrm{supp}(p_{*}^{1,2}(\mu))
, there exists
x_{1}\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
such that
x_{2}=p^{1,2}(x_{1})
by Lemma
2.2
.
Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}\circ p^{2,3}(x_{2})=\alpha_{3}\circ p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2}(x_{1})=\alpha_{1}(x_{1})=\alpha_{2}\circ p^{1,2}(x_{1})=\alpha_{2}(x_{2})

(2.14)

holds and hence

\displaystyle\int_{X_{3}}\alpha_{3}\;dp_{*}^{2,3}\circ p_{*}^{1,2}(\mu)=\int_{X_{2}}\alpha_{2}\;dp_{*}^{1,2}(\mu)=\int_{X_{1}}\alpha_{1}\;d\mu=\int_{X_{3}}\alpha_{3}\;d(p^{2,3}\circ p^{1,2})_{*}(\mu)

(2.15)

is obtained.
∎

2.2
Application to homogeneous spaces

By applying the properties of pushforward measures in Subsection

to homogeneous spaces, we can prove Theorem

.
For this purpose, we use the following lemma:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

be a left Haar measure of a locally compact group

.

(1)

\mathrm{supp}(\phi(g)dg)=\mathrm{supp}(\phi)
for any
\phi\in C(G)

(2)

Let

\displaystyle M_{H}(G)=\mathcal{M}(p^{G\to G/H},G)\cap M_{\{e\}}(G).

(3)

Let

,
\overline{G}
, and

be as in Theorem
1.3
(which does not necessarily satisfy

).
Then
\mathcal{M}(p^{G\to G/H},G)\subset\mathcal{M}(p^{G\to\overline{G}},G)

Proof.

(1)

The conclusion is obtained by

(2)

The conclusion is obtained by

(3)

Since

(1)
.

A left Haar measure of quotient groups can be given as the following fact:

\displaystyle=\int_{\overline{G}}\int_{N}\alpha(\overline{gn})\phi(gn)dnd\overline{g}

In this section, we prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{G/H}\int_{H}\nu(gh)dhdp_{*}^{G\to G/H}(\phi(g)dg)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\int_{H}\nu(gh)dh\phi(g)dg

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\int_{G}\nu(gh)\phi(g)dgdh

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\int_{G}\frac{\nu(g)\phi(gh^{-1})}{\Delta_{G}(h)}dgdh

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\nu(g)\int_{H}\frac{\phi(gh^{-1})}{\Delta_{G}(h)}dhdg

(3.4)

by Fubini’s theorem.
Since

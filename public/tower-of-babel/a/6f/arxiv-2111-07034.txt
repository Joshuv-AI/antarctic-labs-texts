Near Threshold Heavy Quarkonium Photoproduction at Large Momentum Transfer
Peng Sun, Xuan-Bo Tong, Feng Yuan

Abstract
Perturbative QCD is applied to investigate the near threshold heavy quarkonium photoproduction at large momentum transfer. We take into account the contributions from the leading three-quark Fock states of the nucleon. The dominant contribution comes from the three-quark Fock state with one unit quark orbital angular momentum (OAM) whereas that from zero quark OAM is suppressed at the threshold. From our analysis, we also show that there is no direct connection between the near threshold heavy quarkonium photoproduction and the gluonic gravitational form factors of the nucleon. Based on the comparison between our result and recent GlueX data of J / ψ J/\psi photoproduction, we make predictions for ψ ′ \psi^{\prime} and Υ \Upsilon (1S,2S) states which can be tested in future experiments.

W_{\gamma p}^{2}\sim M_{V}^{2}\gg(-t)\gg\Lambda_{QCD}^{2}\ ,

for the non-perturbative scale. In addition, throughout the following analysis, we take the threshold limit, i.e.,

Tong:2021ctu

II
Near Threshold Kinematics

The typical Feynman diagram of the two-gluon exchange contributions to the near threshold heavy quarkonium photoproduction is shown in Fig.
1
,

\gamma(k_{\gamma})+N(p_{1})\to J/\psi(k_{\psi})+N^{\prime}(p_{2})\ ,

(4)

where we have used
J/\psi
as an example. In order to make the near threshold expansion more evident, it is useful to examine the relevant kinematics for the scattering amplitude. The center of mass energy and momentum transfer squared can be written as,

\displaystyle W_{\gamma p}^{2}=(k_{\gamma}+p_{1})^{2}=(k_{\psi}+p_{2})^{2}\sim M_{V}^{2}\ ,

(5)

\displaystyle|t|=|(p_{2}-p_{1})^{2}|\ll M_{V}^{2}\ .

(6)

Therefore, we will have the following approximations around the threshold kinematics,

\displaystyle p_{1}\cdot k_{\gamma}\sim p_{1}\cdot k_{\psi}\sim M_{V}^{2}\ ,

(7)

\displaystyle p_{2}\cdot k_{\gamma}\sim p_{2}\cdot k_{\psi}\ll M_{V}^{2}\ .

(8)

In addition, applying the heavy quark mass limit of
M_{V}^{2}\gg(-t)
, we find that the invariant mass of the
t
-channel two gluons is much smaller than heavy quarkonium mass. We will also take the approximation of
M_{c}\approx M_{V}/2
in the non-relativistic limit of the heavy quarkonium system.

Figure 1:
Schematics of two-gluon exchange contribution to the threshold heavy quarkonium production.

The quark propagators in the upper part of the Feynman diagram of Fig.
1
are all in order of
1/M_{V}
. For example, one of the quark propagators can be simplified as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(k_{1}-k_{\psi}/2\right)^{2}-M_{c}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{-k_{1}\cdot k_{\gamma}-k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}

(9)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{-k_{1}\cdot k_{\gamma}}\ ,

where we have applied
|k_{1}^{2}|\sim|k_{2}^{2}|\sim|k_{1}\cdot k_{2}|\sim|t|\ll M_{V}^{2}
. Because
k_{1}
carries certain momentum fraction of the incoming nucleon,
k_{1}\cdot k_{\gamma}
will be order of
M_{V}^{2}
. Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(k_{2}-k_{\psi}/2\right)^{2}-M_{c}^{2}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{-k_{2}\cdot k_{\gamma}}\ .

(10)

The following propagator will also show up in some of the Feynman diagrams,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(k-k_{\psi}/2\right)^{2}-M_{c}^{2}}=\frac{1}{-k\cdot k_{\gamma}}\approx\frac{2}{-M_{V}^{2}}\ ,

(11)

where
k=k_{1}+k_{2}=p_{1}-p_{2}
. In the center of mass frame,
k
is dominated by
p_{1}
because
p_{2}
is soft.

Applying the above approximations, we can simplify the photon-heavy quarkonium transition amplitude. Let us define
\mu
and
\nu
for the polarization indices for
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, respectively, and
\epsilon_{\gamma}
and
\epsilon_{\psi}
for the photon polarization and
J/\psi
polarization vectors, respectively. To further simplify the derivation, we choose the physical polarization for the incoming photon,

\epsilon_{\gamma}\cdot k_{\gamma}=0,~~\epsilon_{\gamma}\cdot p_{1}=0\ .

(12)

With this choice, we notice that the contributions from
\epsilon_{\gamma}\cdot k_{1}
and
\epsilon_{\gamma}\cdot k_{2}
are also suppressed in the heavy quark mass limit. Therefore, we will drop these terms as well. We emphasize, all these approximations have been cross checked by a full computation.

Finally, we have the following expression for the amplitude from the heavy quarkonium side,

{\cal M}_{\psi,ab}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\delta^{ab}N_{\psi}\left[\epsilon_{\psi}^{*}\cdot\epsilon_{\gamma}{\cal W}_{T}^{\mu\nu}+\epsilon_{\psi}^{*}\cdot k{\cal W}_{L}^{\mu\nu}+{\cal W}_{S}^{\mu\nu}\right]}{k_{1}\cdot k_{\gamma}k_{2}\cdot k_{\gamma}}\ ,

(13)

where
a
and
b
represent the color indices for the
t
-channel gluons.
In the above equation,
N_{\psi}
is defined as

N_{\psi}=-\frac{4e_{c}eg_{s}^{2}}{\sqrt{N_{c}M_{V}^{3}}}\psi_{J}(0)\ ,

(14)

where
\psi_{J}(0)
is the wave function of
J/\psi
at the origin and is related to the NRQCD matrix element
Bodwin:1994jh
. The tensor structures
{\cal W}_{T,L,S}^{\mu\nu}
are defined as

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{T}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-k_{1}\cdot k_{\gamma}k_{2}\cdot k_{\gamma}g^{\mu\nu}-k_{1}\cdot k_{2}k_{\gamma}^{\mu}k_{\gamma}^{\nu}

\displaystyle+k_{1}\cdot k_{\gamma}k_{2}^{\mu}k_{\gamma}^{\nu}+k_{2}\cdot k_{\gamma}k_{1}^{\nu}k_{\gamma}^{\mu}

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{L}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{1}\cdot k_{\gamma}\epsilon_{\gamma}^{\nu}k_{2}^{\mu}+k_{2}\cdot k_{\gamma}\epsilon_{\gamma}^{\mu}k_{1}^{\nu}

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{S}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-k_{1}\cdot k_{2}\left(k_{1}\cdot k_{\gamma}\epsilon_{\psi}^{*\mu}\epsilon_{\gamma}^{\nu}+k_{2}\cdot k_{\gamma}\epsilon_{\psi}^{*\nu}\epsilon_{\gamma}^{\mu}\right.

(15)

\displaystyle~~\left.+k_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{\psi}^{*}k_{\gamma}^{\nu}\epsilon_{\gamma}^{\mu}+k_{2}\cdot\epsilon_{\psi}^{*}k_{\gamma}^{\mu}\epsilon_{\gamma}^{\nu}\right)\ .

where
{\cal W}_{T}
and
{\cal W}_{L}
represent the amplitudes for a transversely polarized and longitudinal polarized heavy quarkonium in the final state, respectively, whereas
{\cal W}_{S}
for a subleading term.

Clearly the above amplitude is symmetric under
k_{1},\mu\leftrightarrow k_{2},\nu
. In the above equation, the first term is the leading contribution in the heavy quark mass limit at the threshold. The second and third terms are subleading contributions.

We have also carried out an important cross check for the above results. We compute the full amplitude without any approximation. We then take the leading contribution of the differential cross section (the amplitude squared) in the heavy quark mass limit and threshold limit, and obtain the same result.

II.1
Vanishing of Three-gluon Exchange Contribution

Figure 2:
Typical Feynman diagram from three-gluon exchange. These diagrams vanish because of the
C
-parity conservation.

Before we start our derivations of the threshold scattering amplitudes, we would like to comment on the three-gluon exchange contributions. The two-gluon and three-gluon exchange diagrams were considered in Ref.
Brodsky:2000zc
for the threshold production of
J/\psi
and it was argued that the three-gluon exchange diagrams dominate the differential cross section contributions.

However, we find that the three-gluon exchange diagrams do not contribute in our framework, due to the
C
-parity conservation. This is because the three gluons from the nucleon side carry symmetric color structure (such as
d_{abc}
) while those from the heavy quarkonium (
J/\psi
) side are antisymmetric (such as
f_{abc}
), where
a
,
b
and
c
represent the color indices for the three gluons in the
t
-channel, respectively. Explicitly, from the nucleon side, we have, as shown in Fig.
2
,

\displaystyle\epsilon^{ijk}\epsilon^{lmn}T^{a}_{il}T^{b}_{jm}T^{c}_{kn}\propto d^{abc}\ ,

(16)

where
ijk
and
lmn
represent the color indices for the initial and final three quarks, respectively. Here, we have applied the anti-symmetric color structure for the three-quark Fock state wave function of the nucleon
Ji:2002xn
. On the other hand, for the heavy quarkonium side, we have, instead

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left[T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}\right]=\frac{1}{4}\left(d^{abc}+if^{abc}\right)\ .

(17)

However, because of
J/\psi
is in the
1^{--}
state, the photon-
J/\psi
transition amplitude vanishes for the symmetric color configuration with three gluons, i.e.,
d^{abc}
term from the above vanishes. Combining this with the color structure from the nucleon side, we conclude the three-gluon exchange diagrams do not contribute.

III
Pion Case

In this section, we take the example of pion case to show the detailed of our derivations. In this case, we have photon scatters on the pion target and produces a
J/\psi
in the final state close to the threshold,

\gamma+\pi\to J/\psi+\pi\ ,

(18)

where the dominant contribution is again a two-gluon exchange diagram. The two gluons attach to the two quark lines from the pion target, as shown in Fig.
3
.

Figure 3:
The Feynman diagram contribution to the exclusive
\gamma\pi\to\pi J/\psi
at large momentum transfer. The two gluons attach to the quark and antiquark lines, respectively.

Considering the leading Fock component of the pion, we have

\displaystyle|\pi^{+}\rangle_{u\overline{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d[1]d[2]\psi_{u\overline{d}}(1,2)\frac{\delta_{ij}}{\sqrt{3}}\left[u^{\dagger}_{\uparrow i}(1)\overline{d}^{\dagger}_{\downarrow j}(2)\right.

(19)

\displaystyle\left.-u^{\dagger}_{\downarrow i}(1)\overline{d}^{\dagger}_{\uparrow j}(2)\right]|0\rangle\,

where
i
and
j=1,2,3
are the color indices, and
\uparrow
and
\downarrow
label quark light-cone helicity
+1/2
and
-1/2
, respectively. The color factor
\delta_{ij}/\sqrt{3}
is normalized to 1. The light-cone wave function amplitude
\psi_{u\bar{d}}(1,2)
is a function of quark momenta with argument 1 representing
x_{1}
and
q_{1\perp}
and so on. Since the momentum conservation implies
\vec{q}_{1\perp}+\vec{q}_{2\perp}=0
and
x_{1}+x_{2}=1
,
\psi_{u\bar{d}}(1,2)
depends on variables
x_{1}
and
q_{1\perp}
only. The integration in the above equation is defined as,

\displaystyle\int d[1]d[2]=\int\frac{d^{2}q_{1\perp}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{dx_{1}}{2\sqrt{x_{1}(1-x_{1})}}\ .

(20)

From the light-cone wave function, we obtain the distribution amplitude,

\displaystyle\phi(x)=\int\frac{d^{2}q_{1\perp}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\psi_{u\bar{d}}(1,2)\ .

(21)

The final scattering amplitude of
\gamma+\pi^{+}\to J/\psi+\pi^{+}
can be computed in terms of the above distribution amplitude of pion,

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{\pi}=

\displaystyle\int dx_{1}dy_{1}\phi^{*}(y_{1})\phi(x_{1}){\cal M}_{\psi}^{\mu\nu}(\epsilon_{\gamma},\epsilon_{\psi},x_{1},y_{1})

\displaystyle\times\frac{-g_{s}^{2}C_{F}}{2k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}}{\rm Tr}\left[\not{p}_{2}\gamma^{\mu}\not{p}_{1}\gamma^{\nu}\right]\ ,

(22)

where
{\cal M}_{\psi}^{\mu\nu}
has been given in the previous section.

III.1
Threshold Expansion

At the threshold, the amplitude squared can be further simplified as

\displaystyle|\overline{{\cal A}^{\pi}}|^{2}=G_{\psi}G_{\pi}(t)G_{\pi}^{*}(t)\ ,

(23)

where the spin sum and average have been applied. Here,
G_{\psi}
is defined as

G_{\psi}=|N_{\psi}|^{2}=\frac{384\pi^{2}e_{c}^{2}\alpha(4\pi\alpha_{s})^{2}}{N_{c}^{2}M_{\psi}^{3}}\langle 0|{\cal O}^{\psi}({}^{3}S_{1}^{(1)})|0\rangle\ ,

(24)

where
\langle 0|{\cal O}({}^{3}S_{1})|0\rangle
is the color-singlet NRQCD matrix element for
J/\psi
.
G_{\pi}(t)
is defined as

G_{\pi}(t)=\frac{8\pi\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{t}\int dx_{1}dy_{1}\phi^{*}(y_{1})\phi(x_{1})\frac{1}{x_{1}\bar{x}_{1}y_{1}\bar{y}_{1}}\ ,

(25)

where
C_{F}=(N_{c}^{2}-1)/2N_{c}
,
\bar{x}_{1}=1-x_{1}
and
\bar{y}_{1}=1-y_{1}
. Here, we have neglected high order corrections of
t_{\psi}=-t/M_{V}^{2}
.

III.2
Compared to the Gravitational Form Factors

We now compare the above result to the gluonic gravitational form factors at large momentum transfer, which have been computed in Ref.
Tong:2021ctu
. For convenience, we list the results below. The gluonic gravitation form factors of the pion are defined as

\displaystyle\langle p_{2}|T_{g}^{\mu\nu}|p_{1}\rangle=2\bar{P}^{\mu}\bar{P}^{\nu}A_{g}^{\pi}(t)+\frac{1}{2}(\Delta^{\mu}\Delta^{\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}\Delta^{2})C_{g}^{\pi}(t)

\displaystyle+2m^{2}g^{\mu\nu}\overline{C}_{g}^{\pi}(t)\ ,

(26)

where
T_{g}^{\mu\nu}
is the gluonic energy-momentum tensor in QCD and
m
represents the pion mass.
Here,
\bar{P}=(p_{1}+p_{2})/2
is the average momentum,
\Delta=p_{2}-p_{1}
is the momentum transfer and hence
t=\Delta^{2}
. From the results of Ref.
Tong:2021ctu
, we find

\displaystyle A^{\pi}_{g}(t)=C^{\pi}_{g}(t)=\frac{4m^{2}}{t}\overline{C}_{g}^{\pi}(t)

(27)

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{-t}\int dx_{1}dy_{1}\phi^{*}(y_{1})\phi(x_{1})\left(\frac{1}{x_{1}\bar{x}_{1}}+\frac{1}{y_{1}\bar{y}_{1}}\right)\ .

From the above results, we find that there is no direct connection between
G_{\pi}(t)
of Eq. (
25
) and any of the gravitational form factors of
A_{g}^{\pi}(t)
,
C_{g}^{\pi}(t)
or
\overline{C}_{g}^{\pi}(t)
(Eq. (
27
)). This indicates that we can not directly interpret the near threshold heavy quarkonium photo-production in terms of the gluonic gravitational form factors.

III.3
Compared to the GPD Formalism

As mentioned in the Introduction, the photo-production of heavy quarkonium has been derived in the GPD framework. If we extend these derivations to the near threshold kinematics, we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{\psi}\epsilon_{\psi}^{*}\cdot\epsilon_{\gamma}\int_{-1}^{1}dx\frac{H_{g}^{\pi}(x,\xi,t)}{(x+\xi-i\varepsilon)(x-\xi+i\varepsilon)}\ ,

(28)

for the pion target, where
N_{\psi}
has been given in Eq. (
14
) and

is the skewness parameter. In the threshold limit we take

. In the above equation,

represents the GPD gluon distribution of the pion. The GPD gluon distribution at large momentum transfer can be calculated in terms of the distribution amplitudes as that of the quark GPD in Ref.
Hoodbhoy:2003uu

from there, we will be able to reproduce the scattering amplitude result from the direct computation in the above subsection A. This provides a useful cross check for our derivations.

IV
Nucleon Case: twist-three contributions

Now we turn to the proton cases. We show the typical Feynman diagram in Fig.
4
. To compute these diagrams, we follow the factorization argument for the hard exclusive processes
Brodsky:1981kj
, where the leading contributions come from the three quark Fock state of the nucleon. The three-quark states can be further classified into zero orbital angular momentum (OAM) and nonzero OAM components
Ji:2002xn
. We will first examine the contribution from zero OAM component. This corresponds to the twist-three contribution from the nucleon’s distribution amplitude.

Typical Feynman diagram contributions to the threshold
J/\psi
photoproduction at large momentum transfer from two-gluon exchange.

IV.1
Three-quark Fock State with Zero OAM

Because there is no quark OAM, the total quark spin equals to the nucleon spin. The associated light-cone wave function amplitude is defined as

\displaystyle|P\uparrow\rangle_{1/2}=\int d[1]d[2]d[3]\left(\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}(1,2,3)\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\epsilon^{ijk}}{\sqrt{6}}u^{\dagger}_{i\uparrow}(1)\left(u^{\dagger}_{j\downarrow}(2)d^{\dagger}_{k\uparrow}(3)-d^{\dagger}_{j\downarrow}(2)u^{\dagger}_{k\uparrow}(3)\right)|0\rangle\ ,

(29)

where
ijk
represent the color indices for the three quarks, respectively, and the measure for the quark momentum is,

\displaystyle d[1]d[2]d[3]=\sqrt{2}\frac{dx_{1}dx_{2}dx_{3}}{\sqrt{2x_{1}2x_{2}2x_{3}}}\frac{d^{2}\vec{q}_{1\perp}d^{2}\vec{q}_{2\perp}d^{2}\vec{q}_{3\perp}}{(2\pi)^{9}}

\displaystyle\times(2\pi)^{3}\delta(1-x_{1}-x_{2}-x_{3})\delta^{(2)}(\vec{q}_{1\perp}+\vec{q}_{2\perp}+\vec{q}_{3\perp})\ .

(30)

By integrating over the transverse momenta
q_{i\perp}
, we obtain the twist-three distribution amplitude
Braun:1999te

\displaystyle\Phi_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\sqrt{6}\int[dq_{\perp}]\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}(1,2,3)\ ,

(31)

where
[dq_{\perp}]=\frac{d^{2}\vec{q}_{1\perp}d^{2}\vec{q}_{2\perp}d^{2}\vec{q}_{3\perp}}{(2\pi)^{9}}\delta^{(2)}(\vec{q}_{1\perp}+\vec{q}_{2\perp}+\vec{q}_{3\perp})
.
In this configuration, the three quarks only carry longitudinal momenta to form the nucleon state. The above parameterization applies to both initial and final state nucleons. Of course, their momenta are different. In addition, because the quark helicities are conserved, the nucleon helicity is also conserved.

IV.2
Partonic Scattering Amplitude

Schematically, we can write the helicity-conserved amplitude as

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{3}

\displaystyle=\langle J/\psi(\epsilon_{\psi}),N^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|\gamma(\epsilon_{\gamma}),N_{\uparrow}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int[dx][dy]\Phi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\Phi^{*}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})

\displaystyle~~\times{\cal M}_{\psi}^{\mu\nu}(\epsilon_{\gamma},\epsilon_{\psi})\frac{1}{(-t)^{2}}{\cal H}^{\mu\nu}(\{x\},\{y\})\ ,

(32)

where
\{x\}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
represent the momentum fractions carried by the three quarks,
[dx]=dx_{1}dx_{2}dx_{3}\delta(1-x_{1}-x_{2}-x_{3})
, and
\Phi_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
is the twist-three distribution amplitude of the proton
Lepage:1980fj
;

V
Nucleon case: Twist-four contributions

The twist-four contribution comes from the three-quark Fock state with one unit quark OAM. Two important features emerge for nonzero OAM contributions. First, the partonic scattering amplitudes conserve the quark helicities. However, because of a nonzero OAM for one of the three-quark state, the helicity of the nucleon states will be different. This contributes to the hadron helicity-flip amplitude. Second, in order to get a nonzero contribution, we have to perform the intrinsic transverse momentum expansion for the hard partonic scattering amplitudes
Belitsky:2002kj
, which will introduce an additional suppression factor of
1/(-t)
.

The twist-four distribution amplitudes are related to the three-quark Fock states with one unit of OAM. This can comes from either the initial or final state. For example, if we consider the contribution from the initial state of spin-down nucleon, we can parameterize the Fock state as
Ji:2002xn
,

\displaystyle|p_{1}\downarrow\rangle_{1/2}=\int d[1]d[2]d[3]\left((q_{1}^{x}-iq_{1}^{y})\tilde{\psi}^{(3)}(1,2,3)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(q_{2}^{x}-iq_{2}^{y})\tilde{\psi}^{(4)}(1,2,3)\right)\frac{\epsilon^{ijk}}{\sqrt{6}}

\displaystyle\times\left(u^{\dagger}_{i\downarrow}(1)u^{\dagger}_{j\uparrow}(2)d^{\dagger}_{k\uparrow}(3)-d^{\dagger}_{i\downarrow}(1)u^{\dagger}_{j\uparrow}(2)u^{\dagger}_{k\uparrow}(3)\right)|0\rangle\ ,

where the total quark helicity equals to
+1/2
with nucleon helicity
-1/2

An important step in the computation of twist-four contribution is to perform the collinear expansion of the partonic scattering amplitude in terms of the transverse momenta

VI
Interpretation in Terms of Gravitational Form Factor?

As mentioned in Introduction, the near threshold heavy quarkonium production has been argued to provide a direct access to the gluonic gravitational form factors of the nucleon. However, our explicit calculations for the pion case have shown that there is no direct connection between them.

From the results in previous sections, we have calculated the near threshold photo-production of heavy quarkonium on the nucleon target at large momentum transfer. The gluonic form factors at large
(-t)
have been recently calculated in Ref.
Tong:2021ctu
. We conclude, again, we can not build a direct connection between them.

VI.1
Construct the Gluonic Operator

The above conclusion can be understood from a detailed analysis of the photon-quarkonium transition amplitude. As discussed in Sec. II, this amplitude can be simplified in the heavy quark mass limit,
M_{V}^{2}\gg(-t)
,

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\psi}^{\mu\nu}=N_{\psi}\epsilon_{\psi}^{*}\cdot\epsilon_{\gamma}\frac{k_{\gamma,\alpha}k_{\gamma,\beta}}{k_{1}\cdot k_{\gamma}k_{2}\cdot k_{\gamma}}{\cal W}_{T}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\ .

(58)

Here, we only keep the leading term in this limit. For simplicity, we have also dropped the associated color factors associated with the
t
-channel gluons. In the above,
{\cal W}_{T}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}
is defined as

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{T}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-k_{1}^{\alpha}k_{2}^{\beta}g^{\mu\nu}-k_{1}\cdot k_{2}g^{\alpha\mu}g^{\beta\nu}

(59)

\displaystyle+k_{1}^{\nu}k_{2}^{\beta}g^{\alpha\mu}+k_{2}^{\mu}k_{1}^{\alpha}g^{\beta\nu}\ ,

which can be identified as gluonic operator of
{F^{\alpha}}_{\rho}F^{\beta\rho}
acting on the nucleon state. However, the complete scattering amplitude involves the integral of the momenta
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
with the associated propagators depending on them. In the end, the
\gamma N\to J/\psi N^{\prime}
amplitude can be schematically written as

\displaystyle{\cal A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{\psi}\epsilon_{\psi}^{*}\cdot\epsilon_{\gamma}

(60)

\displaystyle\times\int d^{4}k_{1}d^{4}k_{2}\frac{k_{\gamma,\alpha}k_{\gamma,\beta}}{(k_{1}\cdot k_{\gamma}-i\varepsilon)(k_{2}\cdot k_{\gamma}-i\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\times\int d^{4}\eta_{1}d^{4}\eta_{2}e^{ik_{1}\cdot\eta_{1}+ik_{2}\cdot\eta_{2}}

\displaystyle~~~~\times\langle N^{\prime}|{F^{a,\alpha}}_{\rho}(\eta_{1})F^{a,\beta\rho}(\eta_{2})|N\rangle\ .

Clearly, if we neglect the
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
dependence in the pre-factor of
\frac{1}{(k_{1}\cdot k_{\gamma}-i\varepsilon)(k_{2}\cdot k_{\gamma}-i\varepsilon)}
, the above equation can be identified as a gluonic gravitational form factor of the nucleon state. However, as discussed in Sec. II, this pre-factor comes from the quark propagators in the photon-quarkonium transition amplitude. The complete calculation will have a full dependence on the momentum fractions of the incoming nucleon
p_{1}
carried by the two gluons
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
.

We emphasize that the above discussions apply to all of the kinematics in heavy quarkonium photo-production, including small and large
(-t)
. Therefore, our conclusion is valid in the whole kinematics of this process that there is no direct connection between the near threshold photo-prodution of heavy quarkonium and the gluonic gravitational form factors of the nucleon.

VI.2
Compare to the GPD Formalism

It is interesting to find out that the above
{\cal W}_{T}^{\mu\nu}
can be directly compared to that for the gluon GPD calculations. Gluon GPD is defined through the matrix element
\langle N^{\prime}|F^{+\alpha}F^{+}_{\ \alpha}|N\rangle

-n\cdot k_{1}n\cdot k_{2}g^{\mu\nu}-n^{\mu}n^{\nu}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}+n^{\mu}k_{1}^{\nu}n\cdot k_{2}+n^{\nu}k_{2}^{\mu}n\cdot k_{1}\ ,

(61)

where

for any momentum

k_{1}
and
k_{2}
represent the gluon momenta that couple to the nucleon state, and

for their polarization indices. Clearly, this is the same structure as

Following this argument, the scattering amplitude of

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{P}^{+}}\int\frac{d\eta^{-}}{2\pi}e^{ix\bar{P}^{+}\eta^{-}}\langle N^{\prime}|{F^{a,+}}_{\alpha}(-\frac{\eta^{-}}{2})F^{a,\alpha+}(\frac{\eta^{-}}{2})|N\rangle\ ,

Clearly, from the above GPD formalism, one can only link to the gluonic gravitational form factors by making approximations of no

and the total cross sections near the threshold as functions of
W_{\gamma p}
.

Extending our analysis to other heavy quarkonium states is straightforward and similar formulas can be derived. As a first step, we take the differential cross section from the twist-four contribution at the threshold,

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma(\gamma p\to Vp)}{dt}|_{threshold}=\frac{N_{0}^{V}}{(-t+\Lambda^{2})^{5}}\ ,

(65)

for a heavy quarkonium state
V
. In the heavy quark mass limit, the
t
-dependence only comes from the nucleon side. Therefore, we will assume the above
\Lambda
parameter should be same for all heavy quarkonium states. On the other hand, the normalization factor
N_{0}^{V}
will depend on the quarkonium state in the final state. From the derivations in previous section, we know that the differential cross section is proportional to,

\frac{d\sigma}{dt}\propto\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}(M_{V})\langle 0|{\cal O}^{V}({}^{3}S_{1}^{(1)})|0\rangle}{M_{V}^{7}}\ ,

(66)

from which we derive the ratio between different heavy quarkonium states,

\frac{N_{0}^{V}}{N_{0}}=\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}(M_{V})\langle 0|{\cal O}^{V}({}^{3}S_{1}^{(1)})|0\rangle/M_{V}^{7}}{\alpha_{s}^{2}(M_{\psi})\langle 0|{\cal O}^{\psi}({}^{3}S_{1}^{(1)})|0\rangle/M_{\psi}^{7}}\ .

(67)

Substituting the associated NRQCD matrix elements for
J/\psi
,
\psi^{\prime}
, and
\Upsilon
(1S, 2S) from, e.g., Refs.
Feng:2015sjx
;
Zhan:2021dlu
, we find the following values for the normalization factors,

\displaystyle{N_{0}^{\psi^{\prime}}}=0.20{N_{0}}\ ,

(68)

\displaystyle{N_{0}^{\Upsilon(1S)}}=5\times 10^{-3}{N_{0}}\ ,

(69)

\displaystyle{N_{0}^{\Upsilon(2S)}}=2.5\times 10^{-3}{N_{0}}\ .

(70)

\gamma p\to\psi^{\prime}p

J/\psi

The comparison between different quarkonium states will provide an important confirmation for the production mechanism. The comparison between Charmonium and Bottomonium, in particular, will test the heavy quark limit we have employed in this paper. Meanwhile, the momentum transfer range is much higher for
\Upsilon
as compared to
J/\psi
. This provides a unique opportunity to explore the large momentum transfer region.

VIII
Conclusion

In this paper, we have carried out a detailed derivation of near threshold heavy quarkonium photoproduction at large momentum transfer. We have taken into account the three-quark Fock state of the nucleon with zero and one unit of quark OAM. We found that the contribution from the Fock state with zero quark OAM is suppressed at threshold. The differential cross section is dominated by the contribution from nonzero OAM Fock state and has a power behavior of
1/(-t)^{5}
.

Our power counting predictions are consistent with recent experimental data of near threshold photo-production of
J/\psi
from the GlueX collaboration at JLab. Based on the comparison between our derivation and the experimental data, we have made predictions for
\psi^{\prime}
and
\Upsilon
(1S,2S). All these predictions can be tested at future facilities including the electron-ion colliders
Accardi:2012qut
;
AbdulKhalek:2021gbh
;
Anderle:2021wcy
.

We have also shown that there is no direct connection between the near threshold photo-production of heavy quarkonium state and the gluonic gravitational form factors. The indirect connection can be built through GPD gluon distributions. For example, we can parameterize the gluon GPDs and fit to the experimental data, which, in return, can constrain the associated gravitational form factors.

Appendix A
Gluon GPD for Pion

The gluon GPD for pion is defined as

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\eta^{-}}{2\pi}e^{ix\bar{P}^{+}\eta^{-}}\Big\langle p_{2}\Big|F^{+\mu}_{a}(-\frac{\eta^{-}}{2}){\cal L}_{ab}[-\frac{\eta^{-}}{2},\frac{\eta^{-}}{2}]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times F^{\ +}_{\mu,b}(\frac{\eta^{-}}{2})\Big|p_{1}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{P}^{+}H^{(\pi)}_{g}(x,\xi,t)~,

(71)

where the gluon field strength tensor is
F^{a}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A^{a}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A^{a}_{\mu}-g_{s}f^{abc}A^{b}_{\mu}A^{c}_{\nu}
,
and the gauge link in the adjoint representation is

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{ab}\left[z_{2},z_{1}\right]={\cal P}\ \text{exp}\left[g_{s}\int^{z_{2}}_{z_{1}}\ dz^{-}\ G^{+,c}(z^{-})f^{acb}\right]~.

(72)

{\cal P}
denote the path-ordering operation. In the definition of GPD,
\bar{P}=(p_{1}+p_{2})/2
is the average momentum,
\Delta=p_{2}-p_{1}
is the momentum transfer and
t=\Delta^{2}
. The skewness parameter
\xi
is defined as the projection of the momentum transfer
\Delta
along
\bar{P}
direction,
\xi=-\frac{\Delta^{+}}{2\bar{P}^{+}}.

In the large
(-t)
limit, the gluon GPD of the pion has the following factorization formula:

\displaystyle H^{(\pi)}_{g}(x,\xi,t)=

\displaystyle\int dx_{1}dy_{1}\phi(y_{1})\phi(x_{1}){\cal H}^{(\pi)}(x_{1},y_{1})~\ ,

(73)

where
\phi
represents the leading-twist distribution amplitude of pion. The perturbative function at the leading order can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{(\pi)}(x_{1},y_{1})=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{s}^{2}C_{F}}{-t}\left(\frac{(1-\xi)^{2}}{x_{1}\bar{x}_{1}}+\frac{(1+\xi)^{2}}{y_{1}\bar{y}_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\delta\big[x-(x_{1}-y_{1}+\xi(x_{1}+y_{1}-1))\big]

\displaystyle+\delta\big[x+(x_{1}-y_{1}+\xi(x_{1}+y_{1}-1))\big]\bigg)~.

(74)

The above result is similar to that of the quark GPD calculated in Ref.
Hoodbhoy:2003uu
for the pion.

Appendix B
Gluon GPD for Nucleon

The gluon GPD of nucleon is defined from

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\eta^{-}}{2\pi}e^{ix\bar{P}^{+}\eta^{-}}\Big\langle p_{2},s^{\prime}\Big|F^{+\mu}_{a}(-\frac{\eta^{-}}{2}){\cal L}_{ab}[-\frac{\eta^{-}}{2},\frac{\eta^{-}}{2}]

\displaystyle\quad\times F^{\ +}_{\mu,b}(\frac{\eta^{-}}{2})\Big|p_{1},s\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bigg(H_{g}(x,\xi,t)\bar{U}(p_{2},s^{\prime})\gamma^{+}U(p_{1},s)

\displaystyle\quad+E_{g}(x,\xi,t)\bar{U}(p_{2},s^{\prime})\frac{i\sigma^{+\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}}{2M_{p}}U(p_{1},s)\bigg)~,

(75)

where
s^{\mu}
denote the covariant spin-vector of the proton.

Following the strategy in
Hoodbhoy:2003uu
;
Tong:2021ctu
, the GPD
H_{g}
can be extracted from the helicity conserved amplitude, and one can show that at the large momentum transfer,
H_{g}
follows the following factorization formula:

\displaystyle H_{g}(x,\xi,t)=

\displaystyle\int[dx][dy]\Phi_{3}^{*}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\Phi_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times{\cal H}(\{x\},\{y\})~,

(76)

where
\Phi_{3}
is the twist-3 proton light-cone amplitude
Braun:1999te
, and
{\cal H}
is the hard coefficient and perturbatively calculable. At the leading order, we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal H}(\{x\},\{y\})

\displaystyle=2\tilde{\cal H}+\tilde{\cal H}(y_{1}\leftrightarrow y_{3})~,

(77)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\cal H}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi^{2}\alpha_{s}^{2}C_{B}^{2}}{3t^{2}}\times

(78)

\displaystyle\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{x_{1}+y_{1}+\xi(x_{1}-y_{1})}{\bar{x}_{1}\bar{y}_{1}x_{1}x_{3}y_{1}y_{3}}+\frac{x_{1}+y_{1}+\xi(x_{1}-y_{1})}{\bar{x}_{1}\bar{y}_{1}x_{1}x_{2}y_{1}y_{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\delta\big[x-(x_{1}-y_{1}+\xi(x_{1}+y_{1}-1))\big]

\displaystyle+\delta\big[x+(x_{1}-y_{1}+\xi(x_{1}+y_{1}-1))\big]\bigg)

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{x_{3}+y_{3}+\xi(x_{3}-y_{3})}{\bar{x}_{3}\bar{y}_{3}x_{3}x_{1}y_{3}y_{1}}+\frac{x_{3}+y_{3}+\xi(x_{3}-y_{3})}{\bar{x}_{3}\bar{y}_{3}x_{3}x_{2}y_{3}y_{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\delta\big[x-(x_{3}-y_{3}+\xi(x_{3}+y_{3}-1))\big]

\displaystyle+\delta\big[x+(x_{3}-y_{3}+\xi(x_{3}+y_{3}-1))\big]\bigg)\bigg\}~.

Similar results for the quark GPDs
H_{q}
of the nucleon have been calculated in Ref.
Hoodbhoy:2003uu
. They share the same power behavior at large
(-t)
.

On the other hand, the GPD
E_{g}
at large
(-t)
is calculated from the nucleon helicity-flip amplitude, and the related factorization formula can be written as

\displaystyle E_{g}(x,\xi,t)=\int[dx][dy]\left\{x_{3}\Phi_{4}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}){\cal E}_{\Phi g}(\{x\},\{y\})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+x_{1}\Psi_{4}(x_{2},x_{1},x_{3}){\cal E}_{\Psi g}(\{x\},\{y\})\right\}\ \Phi_{3}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\ ,

(79)

where
\Psi_{4}
and
\Phi_{4}
are the twist-four distribution amplitude of the proton
Braun:2000kw
.
{\cal E}_{g}
can be written as,

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{g}=2\tilde{\cal E}+\tilde{\cal E}^{\prime}~,

(80)

where
\tilde{\cal E}^{\prime}
is obtained from
\tilde{\cal E}
by interchanging
y_{1}
and
y_{3}
. The detailed calculation yields

\displaystyle\tilde{\cal E}_{\Psi}(\{x\},\{y\})=\frac{-C_{B}^{2}M^{2}_{p}}{12(-t)^{3}}(4\pi\alpha_{s})^{2}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Bigg[x_{3}K_{1}\tilde{\delta}[x_{1},y_{1}]\Bigl((1+\xi)^{2}x_{1}\bar{x}_{1}+2\left(1-\xi^{2}\right)y_{3}\bar{x}_{1}

\displaystyle+(1-\xi)^{2}y_{1}y_{2}\Bigr)+\bar{x}_{3}\tilde{K}_{1}\tilde{\delta}[x_{3},y_{3}]\Bigl((1+\xi)^{2}x_{3}\bar{x}_{3}

\displaystyle+(1-\xi)^{2}y_{3}\bar{y}_{3}\Bigr)+x_{3}(\tilde{K}_{2}-K_{2})\tilde{\delta}[x_{2},y_{2}]

\displaystyle\Bigl((1+\xi)^{2}x_{2}\bar{x}_{2}+(1-\xi)^{2}y_{2}\bar{y}_{2}\Bigr)+K_{3}\tilde{\delta}[x_{1},y_{1}]

\displaystyle\Bigl(2\left(1-\xi^{2}\right)\bar{x}_{1}-(1-\xi)^{2}y_{1}\Bigr)+x_{3}(K_{4}+K_{5})

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}[x_{1},y_{1}]\Bigl(2\left(1-\xi^{2}\right)\bar{y}_{1}+(1+\xi)^{2}x_{1}\Bigr)+(\tilde{K}_{4}+\tilde{K}_{5})

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}[x_{3},y_{3}]\Bigl((1+\xi)^{2}x_{3}\bar{x}_{3}+(1-\xi)^{2}y_{3}\bar{y}_{3}\Bigr)\Bigg]+(\xi\leftrightarrow-\xi)~,

(81)

and
\tilde{\cal E}_{\Phi}=\tilde{\cal E}_{\Psi}(1\leftrightarrow 3)
. Here we have used the following notation to express the delta function of

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}[a,b]\equiv

\displaystyle\delta[x-(a-b+\xi(a+b-1))]

\displaystyle+\delta[x+(a-b+\xi(a+b-1))]~.

(82)

The functions

Finite period vectors and Gauss sums
Yeongseong Jo

Abstract
. We study four sums including the Jacquet–Piatetski-Shapiro–Shalika, Flicker, Bump–Friedberg, and Jacquet–Shalika sums associated to irreducible cuspidal representations of general linear groups over finite fields. By computing explicitly, we relate Asai and Bump–Friedberg gamma factors over finite fields to those over nonarchimedean local fields through level zero supercuspidal representation. Via Deligne–Kazhdan close field theory, we prove that exterior square and Bump–Friedberg gamma factors agree with corresponding Artin gamma factors of their associated tamely ramified representations through local Langlands correspondence. We also deduce product formul æ for Asai, Bump–Friedberg, and exterior square gamma factors in terms of Gauss sums. By combining these results, we examine Jacquet–Piatetski-Shapiro–Shalika, Flicker–Rallis, Jacquet–Shalika, and Friedberg–Jacquet periods and vectors and their connections to Rankin-Selberg, Asai, exterior square, and Bump-Friedberg gamma factors, respectively.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

for

In the same fashion,

produces an element

A non-zero vector
v\in V_{\pi}
is called a
Friedberg–Jacquet vector
if
\pi(h)v=v
for every
h\in H_{2m}(\mathbb{F})
.
We now characterize the existence of Friedberg–Jacquet vectors in terms of the non-vanishing sum.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible cuspidal representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
with
n=2m
even. Then
\pi
admits a Friedberg–Jacquet vector if and only if there exists
W_{\pi}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
such that

\sum_{g\in N_{m}(\mathbb{F})\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{F})}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{m}(\mathbb{F})\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{F})}W_{\pi}(J(g,g^{\prime}))\neq 0.

Proof.

We assume that
\pi
has a non-zero Friedberg–Jacquet vector. We equip
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi_{\mathbb{E}/\penalty\mathbb{F}})
with
an inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
in which
\pi
is unitary. We define
W_{\rm FJ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
by

W_{\rm FJ}(g)=\frac{1}{\left|H_{n}(\mathbb{F})\right|}\sum_{p\in P_{n}(\mathbb{F})\cap H_{n}(\mathbb{F})}\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(gp)

for
g\in H_{n}(\mathbb{E})
. Taking the advantage of the average, we see that
W_{\rm FJ}(gh)=W_{\rm FJ}(g)

for all
h\in P_{n}(\mathbb{F})\cap H_{n}(\mathbb{F})
.
Using inclusion
\mathrm{Hom}_{H_{n}(\mathbb{F})}(\pi\restriction_{H_{n}(\mathbb{F})},{\bf 1})\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{P_{n}(\mathbb{F})\cap H_{n}(\mathbb{F})}(\pi\restriction_{P_{n}(\mathbb{F})\cap H_{n}(\mathbb{F})},{\bf 1})
,
we deduce the equality
\mathrm{Hom}_{H_{n}(\mathbb{F})}(\pi\restriction_{H_{n}(\mathbb{F})},{\bf 1})=\mathrm{Hom}_{P_{n}(\mathbb{F})\cap H_{n}(\mathbb{F})}(\pi\restriction_{P_{n}(\mathbb{F})\cap H_{n}(\mathbb{F})},{\bf 1})
from the one-dimensionality of both spaces,
Corollary

5.3
. In this way,
W_{\rm FJ}
produces an element
T_{W_{\rm FJ}}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{H_{n}(\mathbb{F})}(\pi\restriction_{H_{n}(\mathbb{F})},{\bf 1})

stated by
T_{W_{\rm FJ}}(W^{\prime})=(W^{\prime},W_{\rm FJ})
for
W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
, from which it follows that
W_{\rm FJ}
is a Friedberg–Jacquet vector.
Furthermore, the given summation is non-trivial, because
[
Nie19
, Lemma 2.7]
yields

\sum_{g\in N_{m}(\mathbb{F})\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{F})}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{m}(\mathbb{F})\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{F})}W_{\rm FJ}(J(g,g^{\prime}))=\frac{1}{\left|N_{n}(\mathbb{F})\cap H_{n}(\mathbb{F})\right|}\sum_{p\in P_{n}(\mathbb{F})\cap H_{n}(\mathbb{F})}\mathcal{B}_{\pi,\psi}(p)=1.

Conversely, we assume that there exists
W_{\pi}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
such that

\sum_{g\in N_{m}(\mathbb{F})\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{F})}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{m}(\mathbb{F})\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{F})}W_{\pi}(J(g,g^{\prime}))\neq 0.

We define
W^{\sharp}_{\rm FJ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
by

W^{\sharp}_{\rm FJ}(h)=\frac{1}{\left|N_{n}(\mathbb{F})\cap H_{n}(\mathbb{F})\right|}\sum_{g\in H_{n}(\mathbb{F})}W_{\pi}(hg).

Combining

W^{\sharp}_{\rm FJ}(1_{n})=\sum_{g\in N_{m}(\mathbb{F})\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{F})}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{m}(\mathbb{F})\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{F})}W_{\pi}(J(g,g^{\prime}))\neq 0

along with the quasi-invariance property that
W^{\sharp}_{\rm FJ}(hh^{\prime})=W^{\sharp}_{\rm FJ}(h)
for all
h^{\prime}\in H_{n}(\mathbb{F})
,
W^{\sharp}_{\rm FJ}
is a non-zero
Friedberg–Jacquet vector that we seek for.
∎

5.3.
Bump–Freidberg integrals and close field theory

Let
\rho
be level zero supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(F)

\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F})

For

and

\int_{N_{m+1}(F)\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{m+1}(F)}\int_{N_{m}(F)\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{m}(F)}W_{\rho}(J(g,g^{\prime}))\Phi(e_{m+1}g)\left|\det g\right|^{s_{1}}\left|\det g^{\prime}\right|^{s_{2}-s_{1}}\,dg^{\prime}dg

for

[
Mat15

Mat17
]

a complex number, we denote by

the character defined by

\delta_{t}:J(g,g^{\prime})\mapstochar\rightarrow\left|\frac{\det g}{\det g^{\prime}}\right|^{t}.

We denote by
\chi_{n}
characters of
H_{n}(F)
:

\chi_{n}:J(g,g^{\prime})\mapstochar\rightarrow\begin{cases}{\bf 1}_{H_{n}(F)}&\text{for $n=2m$;}\\
\left|\dfrac{\det g}{\det g^{\prime}}\right|&\text{for $n=2m+1$.}\end{cases}

In particular, we are interested in the case for
s_{1}=s+t+1/2
and

,

\mathbb{C}(q^{-s},q^{-t})

\Gamma(s,t,\rho,\mathrm{BF},\psi_{F})

-fractional ideal of Bump–Friedberg integrals

Proof.

The computation for two sides are quite similar. For this reason, we give a detailed proof only in the dual integral

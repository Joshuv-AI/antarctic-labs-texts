A quantitative result for the k k -Hessian equation
Alba Lia Masiello*, Francesco Salerno

Abstract
In this paper, we study a symmetrization that preserves the mixed volume of the sublevel sets of a convex function, under which, a Pólya-Szegő type inequality holds. We refine this symmetrization to obtain a quantitative improvement of the Pólya-Szegő inequality for the k k -Hessian integral, and, with similar arguments, we show a quantitative inequality for the comparison proved by Tso [ 22 ] for solutions to the k k -Hessian equation. As an application of the first result, we prove a quantitative version of the Faber-Krahn and Saint-Venant inequalities for these equations. Keywords: k k -Hessian equation, mixed volumes, symmetrization, Pólya-Szegő principle. MSC 2020: 52A39, 35B35, 35J60, 35J96

Introduction

In recent decades, symmetrization techniques have been extensively studied in relation to the qualitative properties of solutions to second-order elliptic boundary value problems. A classic example is the Schwarz symmetrization, used in spectral analysis, among others, to provide a solution
to very classical problems such as the Faber-Krahn inequality or the Saint-Venant inequality. For instance, Talenti uses the Schwarz symmetrization in
[
20
]
to prove sharp a priori bounds for solutions to the Poisson equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions. The peculiarity of Schwarz symmetrization is that it preserves the volumes of the superlevel sets of a function, producing a radially symmetric function defined on a ball that has the same

L^{p}
norms as the original.
In
[
21
]
, Talenti introduces a new type of symmetrization that preserves the perimeter of the level sets of a function. This symmetrization suitably adapts to the Monge-Ampère problem in the plane, that is

\begin{cases}\mathrm{det}(D^{2}u)=f&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded, open and convex set and
f
is a positive function. The author proves that it is possible to compare the solution to (
1
) with the solution to

\begin{cases}\mathrm{det}(D^{2}v)=f^{0}&\text{ in }\Omega^{*}_{1},\\
v=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega^{*}_{1},\end{cases}

(2)

where
f^{0}
is the Schwarz symmetrization of
f
and
\Omega^{*}_{1}
is the ball with the same perimeter as
\Omega
.

Some years later, Tso in
[
22
]
generalizes the result by Talenti in any dimension, and the setting is the following.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded and convex set, let
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})
be a convex and negative function, and let us consider the following functional

H_{k}(u;\Omega)=\frac{1}{k+1}\int_{\Omega}(-u)S_{k}(D^{2}u)\,dx,

(3)

where
S_{k}(D^{2}u)
is the
k
-Hessian operator, defined as

S_{k}(D^{2}u)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}},\quad k=1,\dots,n,

(4)

being
\lambda_{i}
the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix of
u
. We notice that
S_{k}
is a second-order differential operator and it reduces to the Monge-Ampère operator for
k=n
and to the Laplace operator for
k=1
, when the functional
H_{1}
coincides with the Dirichlet energy.
Tso proves that, for all
k
, there exists a symmetrization that decreases the functional (
3
). Moreover, the author exhibits this symmetrization that preserves the
(k-1)
-th quermassintegral of the sublevelsets of the function
u
. For a convex set
\Omega
, the quermassintegrals are geometric quantities that characterize the shape of the set itself, the precise definition can be found in §
2.1
, while the definition of the associated symmetrization, which we will refer to as
(k-1)
-symmetrized, in §
2.2
.

In the same paper, the author also generalizes the comparison by Talenti for the solution to the Monge-Ampére equation to the
k
-Hessian equation in any dimension, proving that, whenever the problem

\begin{cases}S_{k}(D^{2}u)=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(5)

where
f
is a positive function, admits a convex solution, then
the
(k-1)
-symmetrized of the solution
u

can be pointwise compared with the solution to

\begin{cases}S_{k}(D^{2}u^{0})=f^{0}&\text{in }\Omega_{k-1}^{*},\\
u^{0}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{k-1}^{*},\end{cases}

(6)

where
f^{0}
is the Schwarz symmetrization of
f
and
\Omega^{*}_{k-1}
is the ball with the same
(k-1)
-th quermassintegral,
obtaining

u^{0}(x)\leq u^{*}_{k-1}(x)\leq 0,\quad\forall x\in\Omega^{*}_{k-1}.

(7)

It is not true, in general, that the solution to (
5
) is a convex function. Indeed, it was proved in
[
23
]
that if
f
is a positive
C^{2}
function, then the problem (
5
) admits a
k
-convex solution
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
, in the sense that

S_{i}(D^{2}u)\geq 0,\quad\forall i=1,\cdots,k.

Neverthless, as already highlighted in
[
11
,
18
]
, the result (
7
) can be obtained by relaxing the assumption on
u
and only requiring that it has convex sublevel sets. Some evidence in proving the convexity of the solution are obtained in
[
17
,
18
]
.

The result by Tso is important not only because it introduces a new symmetrization, but also because it allows one to treat some optimization problems. Indeed, as a consequence of the results by Tso, it is possible to prove a Faber-Krahn type inequality for the eigenvalues of the
k
-Hessian operator and a Sain-Venant type inequality for the
k
-th torsional rigidity in a special class of convex sets. These topics interested many authors over the years, we refer for example to
[
4
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
12
,
13
]
. For this reason, these results have also been extended in the anisotropic setting in
[
9
,
10
]
.

The present work aims to improve the results by Tso in a quantitative way, following in the footsteps of the recent paper
[
3
]
, in which the authors provide a quantitative version of the Talenti comparison in
[
20
]
.
The main tool in proving the results by Tso is an isoperimetric inequality for the quermassintegrals, known as Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality (see §
2.1
).
So, to obtain a quantitative version the key role is played by the quantitative quermassintegral isoperimetric inequality due to Groemer and Schneider
[
14
]
. In this work, the authors bound from below the isoperimetric deficit in terms of the Hausdorff asymmetry (see Section
2
, Definition
2.7
, for the precise definition)
\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
, an index that measures the
L^{\infty}
distance of
\Omega
to a ball.
More precisely, our main results are

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded, and convex set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
be a convex function that vanishes on the boundary of
\Omega
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,k,\Omega)
such that

\dfrac{H_{k}(u;\Omega)-H_{k}(u_{k-1}^{*};\Omega_{k-1}^{*})}{{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{k+1}}\geq C_{1}\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}+k+1}(\Omega),

(8)

where
k=1,\dots,n-1
,

C_{1}(n,k,\Omega)=c_{1}(n,k)\zeta_{k-1}^{n-2k}(\Omega),

c_{1}(n,k)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\left(\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n\omega_{n}}\right)^{k+1}\frac{(n+1)\beta_{n}}{2^{\frac{n+3}{2}}k(n-1)(n-k+1)}\left(\frac{2n+3}{2(n+2)}\right)^{n-k},

\beta_{n}
is given in Theorem
2.1
, and
\zeta_{k-1}(\Omega)
is the
(k-1)
-th meanradius of
\Omega
.

Theorem
1.1
allows us to improve the Faber-Krahn and Saint-Venant-type inequalities in a quantitative form as stated in Corollaries
3.1
-
3.2
.

Later, we focus on the Poisson equation (
5
) and we prove a quantitative version of inequality (
7
) with the same techniques of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded and convex set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
f
be a positive and measurable function. Let
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
be a convex solution to (
5
),
u_{k-1}^{*}
be its
(k-1)
-symmetrized,
k=1,\dots,n-1
, and let
u^{0}
be the solution to (
6
). Then there exists a positive constant
C_{2}=C_{2}(n,k)
such that

\frac{\|u_{k-1}^{*}-u^{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{k-1}^{*})}}{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}\geq C_{2}\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+5}{2}}(\Omega),

where

C_{2}(n,k)=\frac{(n+1)(k+1)\beta_{n}\omega_{n-1}}{2^{\frac{n+9}{2}}n^{2}(n+2)(n-1)(n-k+1)\omega_{n}^{2}}.

Theorem
1.2
recalls the main result in
[
3
]
, and also its extension to the Hamilton-Jacobi equation contained in
[
2
]
; here the advantage is that we only consider convex functions defined on convex sets.

The pointwise comparison (
7
) between the symmetrized of the solution to (
5
) and the solution to (
6
)
can be used to prove a comparison between the
k
-Hessian functional of
u
and
u^{0}
. We prove the following

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded and convex set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
f
be a positive and measurable function. Let
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
be a solution to (
5
) with convex sublevel sets and let
u^{0}
be the solution to (
6
), then it holds

H_{k}(u;\Omega)\leq H_{k}(u^{0};\Omega^{*}_{k-1}),

(9)

for all
k=1,\dots,n
.

In Section
3
, we make use of Theorem
1.2
to improve inequality (
9
) in a quantitative way.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded and convex set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
f
be a positive and measurable function. Let
u
be a convex solution to (
5
) and let
u^{0}
be the solution to (
6
). Then there exists a positive constant
C_{3}=C_{3}(n,k)
such that

\frac{H_{k}(u^{0};\Omega^{*}_{k-1})-H_{k}(u;\Omega)}{{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\left\|f\right\|}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}\geq C_{3}\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+5}{2}}(\Omega),

where

C_{3}(n,k)=\left(\frac{2n+3}{2(n+2)}\right)^{n}\frac{\omega_{n-1}\beta_{n}}{n^{2}\omega_{n}^{2}}\frac{(n+1)(k+1)}{k(n-1)(n-k+1)}2^{-\frac{n+7}{2}}

where
\beta_{n}
is given in Theorem
2.1
and
k=1,\dots,n-1
.

We note that the case
k=n
, namely the Monge-Ampère equation, is not covered in this work, except for Theorem
1.3
. In particular, in the first result, we cannot apply the Aleksandrov-Fenchel inequality for such index. Additionally, we cannot prove Theorem
1.2
using the same technique as in the cases

, even if some partial results can be obtained. So, it is clear that Theorem

cannot be extended as a consequence of Theorem

.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
we recall some preliminary tools about convex geometry and quermassintegral symmetrization; in Section

2
Notation and preliminaries

We provide the classical definitions and results that we need in the following. The reader can refer to
[
19
, Chapter 1]
for more details.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega,K\subset\R^{2}
two bounded convex sets. We define the
Minkowski sum

(+)
as

\Omega+K:=\{x+y\,:\,x\in\Omega,\,y\in K\},

Definition 2.2
.

Let
E,F
be two convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
Hausdorff distance
between
E
and
F
is defined as

d_{\mathcal{H}}(E,F):=\inf\left\{\varepsilon>0\,:\,E\subset F+\varepsilon B,\,F\subset E+\varepsilon B\right\},

where
B
is the unitary ball centered at the origin and
F+\varepsilon B
is the Minkowski sum.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex set. The
support
function of
\Omega
is defined as

h(\Omega,u):=\max_{x\in\Omega}(x\cdot u),\qquad u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

The support function allows us to define, for a convex set
\Omega
, two geometrical quantities.

Definition 2.4
.

The
width function

w(\Omega,\cdot)
of
\Omega

is defined as

w(\Omega,u):=h(\Omega,u)+h(\Omega,-u)\quad\text{for }u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

The quantity
w(\Omega,u)
is the thickness of
\Omega
in the direction
u
and it represents the distance between
the two support hyperplanes of
\Omega
orthogonal to
u
. The maximum of the width function,

D(\Omega):=\max_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}w(\Omega,u)

is the diameter of
\Omega
.

The mean value of the width function is called the
mean width
and it is denoted by

w(\Omega):=\frac{2}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h(\Omega,u)\,du.

The
Steiner point

s(\Omega)
is defined via the vector-valued integral

s(\Omega):=\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h(\Omega,u)u\,du

Definition 2.5
.

The
Steiner ball

B_{\Omega}
of a convex set
\Omega
is the ball centered at the Steiner point of
\Omega
with diameter equal to
w(\Omega)
.

Remark 2.1
.

Thanks to the Definition
2.3
of the support function of a convex set, it is possible to characterize the Hausdorff distance as follows

d_{\mathcal{H}}(E,F)={\left\|h(E,\cdot)-h(F,\cdot)\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}.

Now it is clear that

{\left|s(E)-s(F)\right|}\leq nd_{\mathcal{H}}(E,F).

Remark 2.2
.

Let us observe that it is possible to compare the mean width and the diameter of
\Omega
, for any convex set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as the following bounds hold

\frac{2\omega_{n-1}}{n\omega_{n}}\leq\frac{w(\Omega)}{D(\Omega)}\leq 1.

The upper bound follows by the definition of diameter and mean width, while the lower bound is proved in
[
1
]
, and we recall here the proof for the reader’s convenience.

Let
S\subset\Omega
be the diameter segment and let us suppose, without loss of generality, that it can be written as

S=\frac{D(\Omega)}{2}\alpha\textbf{e}_{1},\quad\alpha\in[-1,1]

Then, by the definition of support function, we have that

h(\Omega,u)\geq h(S,u)=\frac{D(\Omega)}{2}\sup_{\alpha\in[-1,1]}\langle\alpha\textbf{e}_{1},u\rangle=\frac{D(\Omega)}{2}|\langle\textbf{e}_{1},u\rangle|

and then, if we write
\overline{u}=(u_{2},\dots,u_{n})

\begin{split}\frac{w(\Omega)}{D(\Omega)}&=\frac{1}{D(\Omega)}\frac{2}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h(\Omega,u)\,du\geq\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\langle\textbf{e}_{1},u\rangle|\,du=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\{u_{1}^{2}+\dots+u_{n}^{2}=1\}}|u_{1}|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(u)\\
&=\frac{2}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{B^{n-1}}\sqrt{1-{\left|\overline{u}\right|}^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-{\left|\overline{u}\right|}^{2}}}\,d\overline{u}=\frac{2\omega_{n-1}}{n\omega_{n}}.\end{split}

2.1
Quermassintegrals

For the content of this section, we will refer to
[
19
]
. Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be a non-empty, bounded, convex set, let
B
be the unitary ball centered at the origin and
\rho>0
. We can write the Steiner formula for the Minkowski sum
\Omega+\rho B
as

{\left|\Omega+\rho B\right|}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}W_{i}(\Omega)\rho^{i}.

(10)

The coefficients
W_{i}(\Omega)
are known in the literature as quermassintegrals of
\Omega
. In particular,
W_{0}(\Omega)={\left|\Omega\right|}
,
nW_{1}(\Omega)=P(\Omega)
and
W_{n}(\Omega)=\omega_{n}
where
\omega_{n}
is the measure of
B
.

Formula (
10
) can be generalized to every quermassintegral, obtaining

W_{j}(\Omega+\rho B)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-j}\binom{n-j}{i}W_{j+i}(\Omega)\rho^{i},\qquad j=0,\ldots,n-1.

(11)

If
\Omega
has
C^{2}
boundary, the quermassintegrals can be written in terms of principal curvatures of
\Omega
. More precisely, denoting with
\sigma_{k}
the
k
-th normalized elementary symmetric function of the principal curvature
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n-1}
of
\partial\Omega
, i.e.

\sigma_{0}=1,\qquad\qquad\sigma_{j}=\binom{n-1}{j}^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{j}\leq n-1}\kappa_{i_{1}}\ldots\kappa_{i_{j}},\qquad j=1,\ldots,n-1,

then, the quermassintegrals can be written as

W_{j}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}\sigma_{j-1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\qquad j=1,\ldots,n-1.

(12)

Furthermore, Aleksandrov-Fenchel inequalities hold true

\biggl(\frac{W_{j}(\Omega)}{\omega_{n}}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{n-j}}\geq\biggl(\frac{W_{i}(\Omega)}{\omega_{n}}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{n-i}},\qquad 0\leq i<j\leq n-1,

(13)

where equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is a ball. When
i=0
and
j=1
, formula (
13
) reduces to the classical isoperimetric inequality, i.e.

P(\Omega)\geq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

As the classical isoperimetric inequality, the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities can be improved in a quantitative form, as proved in
[
14
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Groemer-Schneider)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded convex set with Steiner ball
B_{\Omega}
, then, for
0\leq i<j\leq n-1
, it holds

\displaystyle\dfrac{\omega_{n}^{i-j}W_{j}(\Omega)^{n-i}-W_{i}(\Omega)^{n-j}}{W_{i}(\Omega)^{n-j}}\geq

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{n+1}{n(n-1)}s_{j-i}\left(\frac{W_{n-1}^{2}(\Omega)}{\omega_{n}},W_{n-2}(\Omega)\right)\frac{\beta_{n}}{W_{n-2}^{j-i}(\Omega)}\left(\dfrac{\omega_{n}}{W_{n-1}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega})^{\frac{n+3}{2}},

where

s_{m}(x,y)=\sum_{\nu=0}^{m-1}x^{\nu}y^{m-\nu-1},\quad\beta_{n}=\alpha_{n}\left(\frac{n\omega_{n}}{\omega_{n-1}}\right),

and

\alpha_{n}(c)=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{(n+1)(n+3)}\min\left\{\frac{3}{\pi^{2}n(n+2)2^{n}},\frac{16(c+2)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}}{(c-1)^{n-2}}\right\}.

Remark 2.3
.

Let us observe that by the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities (
13
) and the fact that
j-i\geq 1
, the quantitative result (
14
) can be rewritten as follows

\dfrac{\omega_{n}^{i-j}W_{j}(\Omega)^{n-i}-W_{i}(\Omega)^{n-j}}{W_{i}(\Omega)^{n-j}}\geq\frac{(n+1)\beta_{n}}{n(n-1)\omega_{n}}\left(\frac{\omega_{n}d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega})}{W_{n-1}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{n+3}{2}}.

(15)

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\Omega
be a compact convex set, we define the
k-th mean radius
,
k=1,\dots,n-1
, of
\Omega
as

\zeta_{k}(\Omega)=\left(\frac{W_{k}(\Omega)}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-k}}.

(16)

The quantitative Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities can be rewritten in terms of mean radii, obtaining

\frac{\zeta_{j}^{(n-j)(n-i)}-\zeta_{i}^{(n-j)(n-i)}}{\zeta_{i}^{(n-j)(n-i)}}\geq\frac{(n+1)\beta_{n}}{n(n-1)\omega_{n}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega})}{\zeta_{n-1}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{n+3}{2}}.

(17)

Remark 2.4
.

We observe that, since
w(\Omega)=2\zeta_{n-1}(\Omega)
, Remark
2.2
implies

\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(\Omega)}{D(\Omega)}\geq\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n\omega_{n}}.

(18)

Remark 2.5
.

If
K
is a
j
-dimensional convex set in
\R^{n}
, then the
i
-th quermassintegral,
i=1,\dots,n-j-1
, equals zero, while the others may be non-null.

Definition 2.7
.

The
Hausdorff asymmetry index
is defined as

\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)=\dfrac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega})}{\zeta_{n-1}(\Omega)},

where
B_{\Omega}
is the Steiner ball of
\Omega
.

It is possible to prove that the Hausdorff asymmetry index is bounded: indeed if we denote by

\overline{r}=d(s_{\Omega},\partial\Omega),

it follows that
\overline{r}>0
as the Steiner point
s(\Omega)\in\mathring{\Omega}
(see
[
15
]
), so
B_{\overline{r}}(s(\Omega))\subset\Omega
, and

d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega})\leq d_{\mathcal{H}}(B_{\overline{r}},B_{\Omega})=\zeta_{n-1}(\Omega)-\overline{r}=\leq\zeta_{n-1}(\Omega),

that is

\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\leq 1.

(19)

We now want to prove a "Hausorff counterpart" of the propagation of the Fraenkel asymmetry contained in
[
6
]
, as we aim to adapt their techniques, inspired by an idea contained in
[
16
]
, to our case.

Lemma 2.2

(Propagation of the Hausdorff asymmetry)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, convex set with finite measure and let
U\subset\Omega
,
{\left|U\right|}>0
be such that

d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,U)\leq\dfrac{1}{2(n+2)}d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega}),

(20)

where
B_{\Omega}
is the Steiner ball of
\Omega
.
Then, we have

d_{\mathcal{H}}(U,B_{U})\geq\dfrac{1}{2}d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega}),

(21)

where
B_{U}
is the Steiner ball
of
U
.

Proof.

Let us set

R=\zeta_{n-1}(\Omega),\quad r=\zeta_{n-1}(U),

and let us denote by
\varepsilon_{1}=d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,U)
,
\varepsilon_{2}=d_{\mathcal{H}}(B_{U},B_{R})
, then,
the monotonicity with respect to the inclusion of the mean radii and the Steiner formula for the quermassintegral (
11
) give

\begin{split}R&=\zeta_{n-1}(\Omega)\leq\zeta_{n-1}(U+\varepsilon_{1}B_{1})=\frac{W_{n-1}(U+\varepsilon_{1}B_{1})}{\omega_{n}}=\frac{W_{n-1}(U)+\omega_{n}\varepsilon_{1}}{\omega_{n}}=\frac{W_{n-1}(U)}{\omega_{n}}+\varepsilon_{1}\\
&=r+\varepsilon_{1}=r+d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,U).\end{split}

Moreover,

d_{\mathcal{H}}(B_{R},B_{U})\leq R-r+{\left|s(\Omega)-s(U)\right|}\leq(n+1)d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,U),

(22)

where
s(\Omega)
and
s(U)
are the Steiner point of
\Omega
and
U
respectively.
Now, using the reverse triangular inequality, the hypothesis (
20
) and (
22
), we have

\begin{split}d_{\mathcal{H}}(U,B_{U})&\geq d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{U})-d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,U)\\
&=d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega})+d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{U})-d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega})-d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,U)\\
&\geq d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega})-d_{\mathcal{H}}(B_{U},B_{\Omega})-d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,U)\\
&\geq d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega})-(n+2)d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,U)>\frac{1}{2}d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega}).\end{split}

∎

2.2
Symmetrization

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded and convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\mathcal{A}(\Omega)=\{u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})\,:\,\text{$u$ is strictly convex in $\Omega$ and vanishes on $\partial\Omega$}\}.

We observe that any
u
in
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
has a unique minimum in
\Omega
. Denote

\Omega(\mu)=\{x\in\Omega\,:\,u(x)<\mu\},\quad\partial\Omega(\mu)=\{x\in\Omega\,:\,u(x)=\mu\},

for
\mu\in[m,0]
, where
m
is the minimum of
u
. We define the
k
-symmetrized
of
u
,
k=0,\dots,n-1
, to be

u_{k}^{*}(x)=\sup\{\mu\in[m,0]\,:\,\zeta_{k}(\overline{\Omega(\mu)})<|x|\}

that is a radially symmetric function defined in the ball

Moreover, for

2.3
The
k
-Hessian operator

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\R^{n}
and let
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
. As seen in (
4
), the
k
-Hessian operator is the
k
-th elementary function of the Hessian matrix
D^{2}u
. Except for the case
k=1
where the
k
-Hessian operator reduces to the usual Laplace operator, these operators are fully nonlinear and non-elliptic, unless one restricts to the class of
k
-convex function

\mathcal{A}_{k}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in C^{2}(\Omega):S_{i}(D^{2}u)\geq 0\,\text{ in }\,\Omega,\,i=1,\dots,k\right\}.

The operator
S_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}
is homogeneous of degree 1, and if we denote by

S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{ij}}S_{k}(D^{2}u),

the Euler identity for homogeneous functions gives

S_{k}(D^{2}u)=\frac{1}{k}S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{ij}.

A direct computation shows that the
\left(S_{k}^{1j}(D^{2}u),\dots,S_{k}^{nj}(D^{2}u)\right)
is divergence-free, hence
S_{k}(D^{2}u)
can be written in divergence form

S_{k}(D^{2}u)=\frac{1}{k}\left(S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{j}\right)_{i},

(27)

where the subscripts
i,j
stand for partial differentiation.

If
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
and
t
is a regular point of
u
, on the boundary of
\{u\leq t\}
it is possible to link the
k
-Hessian operator with the
(k-1)
-th curvature

\binom{n-1}{k-1}\sigma_{k-1}=\frac{S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{i}u_{j}}{{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{k+1}}.

(28)

Finally, we report a classic result without proof, for more details see
[
22
]
, Proposition 6.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Sigma(t)
be a smooth family of embedded hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
oriented with respect to the unit inner normal
-\nu
. Then for all
k=0,1,\dots,n-1
we have

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma(t)}\sigma_{k}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=(n-k-1)\int_{\Sigma(t)}\sigma_{k+1}\,\nu\cdot\xi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

where

\xi=\frac{\partial\Sigma(t)}{\partial t}

is the variation vector field.

proof of Theorem

.

The chain of inequalities (

)

d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega(\mu),\Omega)\leq d_{\mathcal{H}}(C(\mu),\Omega)\leq\frac{\mu}{m}D(\Omega)

gives the following inclusions

\begin{split}\left\{\mu\in[m,0]\,:\,\mu\geq\frac{m}{2(n+2)}\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega})}{D(\Omega)}\right\}&\subseteq\left\{\mu\in[m,0]\,:\,d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,C(\mu))<\frac{1}{2(n+2)}d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega})\right\}\\
&\subseteq\left\{\mu\in[m,0]\,:\,d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,\Omega(\mu))<\frac{1}{2(n+2)}d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega})\right\},\end{split}

(29)

where the smallest set is well defined from (
19
).
We define

\overline{\mu}=\inf\left\{\mu\in[m,0]\,:\,\mu\geq\frac{m}{2(n+2)}\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega})}{D(\Omega)}\right\}=\frac{m}{2(n+2)}\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,B_{\Omega})}{D(\Omega)},

(30)

and we observe that for all
\mu\in[\overline{\mu},0]
, the sublevel set
\Omega(\mu)
satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
2.2
, hence

\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega(\mu))\geq\frac{\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)}{2}.

Proceeding as in
[
22
]
, we have

\displaystyle(k+1)H_{k}(u,\Omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(-u)S_{k}(D^{2}u)\,dx=\frac{1}{k}\int_{\Omega}S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{i}u_{j}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\int_{m}^{0}\int_{\partial\Omega(\mu)}\frac{S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{i}u_{j}}{{\left|\nabla u\right|}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,d\mu,

where we have applied the divergence theorem and the Coarea formula. We can now apply identity (
28
) on
\partial\Omega(\mu)
and the Jensen inequality, obtaining

\displaystyle k(k+1)H_{k}(u,\Omega)

\displaystyle=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\int_{m}^{0}\int_{\partial\Omega(\mu)}\sigma_{k-1}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{k}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,d\mu\geq\binom{n-1}{k-1}\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885ptm$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 0$}}$}}\nolimits\dfrac{\left[\int_{\partial\Omega(\mu)}\sigma_{k-1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right]^{k+1}}{\left[\int_{\partial\Omega(\mu)}\frac{\sigma_{k-1}}{{\left|\nabla u\right|}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right]^{k}}\,d\mu.

As a consequence of proposition
2.3
, it follows

\int_{\partial\Omega(\mu)}\frac{\sigma_{k-1}}{{\left|\nabla u\right|}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=n\omega_{n}\zeta_{k-1}^{n-k}(\Omega(\mu))\frac{d\zeta_{k-1}(\Omega(\mu))}{d\mu},

so, recalling the definition of mean radii (
16
) and applying the quantitative isoperimetric inequality (
17
) with
i=k-1
and
j=k
, we have

\begin{split}&k(k+1)H_{k}(u;\Omega)\geq\binom{n-1}{k-1}n\omega_{n}\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885ptm$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 0$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{1}{\left(\frac{d\zeta_{k-1}}{d\mu}\right)^{k}}\left[\frac{\zeta_{k}^{k+1}(\Omega(\mu))}{\zeta_{k-1}^{k}(\Omega(\mu))}\right]^{n-k}\,d\mu\\
&\geq\binom{n-1}{k-1}n\omega_{n}\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885ptm$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 0$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{\zeta_{k-1}^{n-k}(\Omega(\mu))}{\left(\frac{d\zeta_{k-1}}{d\mu}\right)^{k}}\left[1+\frac{(n+1)\beta_{n}}{n(n-1)\omega_{n}}\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega(\mu))\right]^{\frac{k+1}{n-k+1}}\,d\mu\\
&\geq\binom{n-1}{k-1}n\omega_{n}\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885ptm$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 0$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{\zeta_{k-1}^{n-k}(\Omega(\mu))}{\left(\frac{d\zeta_{k-1}}{d\mu}\right)^{k}}\,d\mu\\
&+\binom{n-1}{k-1}n\omega_{n}\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885ptm$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 0$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{\zeta_{k-1}^{n-k}(\Omega(\mu))}{\left(\frac{d\zeta_{k-1}}{d\mu}\right)^{k}}\left[\frac{(k+1)(n+1)\beta_{n}}{2n(n-1)(n-k+1)\omega_{n}}\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega(\mu))\right]\,d\mu.\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from the inequality

(1+x)^{\alpha}\geq 1+\frac{\alpha}{2}x,\,\forall\,\alpha>0,\,\forall\,x\in[0,1].

(31)

Now let us study the two integrals

]
, is precisely

\binom{n-1}{k-1}n\omega_{n}\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885ptm$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 0$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{\zeta_{k-1}^{n-k}(\Omega(\mu))}{\left(\frac{d\zeta_{k-1}}{d\mu}\right)^{k}}\,d\mu=(k+1)kH_{k}(u_{k-1}^{*};\Omega_{k-1}^{*}).

We aim to bound from below the second integral in terms of the Hausdorff asymmetry of
\Omega
.
Let us consider the threshold
\overline{\mu}
defined in (
30
), for
\mu\in[\overline{\mu},0]
we can apply the propagation Lemma
2.2
and the bound (
24
),
obtaining

\begin{split}&\binom{n-1}{k-1}n\omega_{n}\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885ptm$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 0$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{\zeta_{k-1}^{n-k}(\Omega(\mu))}{\left(\frac{d\zeta_{k-1}}{d\mu}\right)^{k}}\left[\frac{(k+1)(n+1)\beta_{n}}{n(n-1)(n-k+1)\omega_{n}}\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega(\mu))\right]\,d\mu\\
&\geq\kappa_{1}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega)\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885pt\overline{\mu}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 0$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{\zeta_{k-1}^{n-k}(\Omega(\mu))}{\left(\frac{d\zeta_{k-1}}{d\mu}\right)^{k}}\,d\mu\geq\kappa_{1}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega)\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885pt\overline{\mu}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 0$}}$}}\nolimits\left(1-\frac{\mu}{m}\right)^{n-k}\frac{\zeta_{k-1}^{n-k}(\Omega)}{\left(\frac{d\zeta_{k-1}}{d\mu}\right)^{k}}\,d\mu\\

where the new constant is

\kappa_{2}(n,k)=\frac{\kappa_{1}(n,k)}{k(k+1)}\left(\frac{2n+3}{2(n+2)}\right)^{n-k}.

Remark 3.1
.

reads as follows: let

(34)

1.1
leads to a quantitative inequality for the eigenvalue of the

-Hessian operator. The eigenvalue of the

-Hessian operator is the only real number

]
and Remark

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded and convex set of

. Assume that the solution

to (

) is

and

\begin{split}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)-\lambda_{k}(\Omega^{*}_{k-1})&\geq\frac{(k+1)H_{k}(u;\Omega)}{\|u\|^{k+1}_{L^{k+1}(\Omega)}}-\frac{(k+1)H_{k}(u^{*}_{k-1};\Omega^{*}_{k-1})}{\|u^{*}_{k-1}{\|^{k+1}_{L^{k+1}(\Omega^{*}_{k-1})}}}\\[8.61108pt]

Let
f^{0}(|x|)
be the Schwartz rearrangement of the function
f
given in problem (
5
).
Let
u
be the solution to (
5
) in
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\cap C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
,
u_{k-1}^{*}
be its
(k-1)
-symmetrized and let
u^{0}
be the solution to (
6
).
We recall the explicit integral representation of the radial solution
u^{0}
:

u^{0}(x)=-\left(\frac{k}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}\int_{|x|}^{R}\left(r^{-n+k}\int_{0}^{r}f^{0}(s)s^{n-1}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}\,dr,

(38)

where
R
is the radius of the ball in which
u^{0}
is defined.

We integrate both sides of the equation (
5
) on the sublevel set
\Omega(\mu)
, and we proceed as in
\lx@sectionsign\ref{section3.1}
, obtaining

\displaystyle k\int_{\Omega(\mu)}f(x)\,dx=

\displaystyle k\int_{\Omega(\mu)}S_{k}(D^{2}u)\,dx=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\int_{\partial\Omega(\mu)}\sigma_{k-1}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{k}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\geq

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(\frac{d\zeta_{k-1}}{d\mu}\right)^{k}}\left[\frac{\zeta_{k}^{k+1}(\Omega(\mu))}{\zeta_{k-1}^{k}(\Omega(\mu))}\right]^{n-k}\geq\binom{n-1}{k-1}n\omega_{n}\frac{\zeta_{k-1}^{n-k}(\Omega(\mu))}{\left(\frac{d\zeta_{k-1}}{d\mu}\right)^{k}}\left(1+\kappa_{3}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega(\mu))\right),

where in the last inequality we have applied
the quantitative quermassintegral inequality (
17
) and the constant is

\kappa_{3}(n,k)=\frac{(n+1)(k+1)\beta_{n}}{2n(n-1)(n-k+1)\omega_{n}}.

On the other hand, we can apply the Hardy-Littlewood inequality and we have

\begin{split}&k\int_{\Omega(\mu)}f\,dx\leq k\int_{0}^{|\Omega(\mu)|}f^{0}\left(\left(\frac{a}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)\,da\leq\\
&k\int_{0}^{\omega_{n}\zeta_{k-1}^{n}(\Omega(\mu))}f^{0}\left(\left(\frac{a}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)\,da=kn\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{\zeta_{k-1}(\mu)}f^{0}(r)r^{n-1}\,dr.\end{split}

If we rewrite the previous inequality in a more compact form, we have

1+\kappa_{3}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega(\mu))\leq\frac{k}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}\left(\frac{d\zeta_{k-1}}{d\mu}\right)^{k}\zeta_{k-1}^{-n+k}(\Omega(\mu))\int_{0}^{\zeta_{k-1}(\mu)}f^{0}(r)r^{n-1}\,dr,

and if we erase both members to the power
1/k
, we get

1+\frac{\kappa_{3}(n,k)}{2k}\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega(\mu))\leq\frac{d\zeta_{k-1}}{d\mu}\left(\frac{k}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}\left(\zeta_{k-1}^{-n+k}(\Omega(\mu))\int_{0}^{\zeta_{k-1}(\mu)}f^{0}(r)r^{n-1}\,dr\right)^{\frac{1}{k}},

that can be rewritten, thanks to (
38
), as

1+\frac{\kappa_{3}(n,k)}{2k}\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega(\mu))\leq\frac{d\zeta_{k-1}}{d\mu}\,(u^{0})^{\prime}(\zeta_{k-1}(\Omega(\mu))).

(39)

Now, let us integrate (
39
) between
\mu
and
0
, with

\mu\in\{\nu\in[m,0]\,:\,\zeta_{k-1}(\Omega(\nu))<|x|\},

(40)

we have

\begin{split}\mu&\geq u^{0}(\zeta_{k-1}(\Omega(\mu)))+\int_{\mu}^{0}\kappa_{4}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega(\nu))\,d\nu\\
&\geq u^{0}(\zeta_{k-1}(\Omega(\mu)))+\int_{u_{k-1}^{*}}^{0}\kappa_{4}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega(\nu))\,d\nu,\end{split}

where the constant is

\kappa_{4}(n,k)=\frac{\kappa_{3}(n,k)}{2k}.

If we pass to the supremum on the set (
40
), by the definition of
(k-1)
-symmetrized of
u
, we have

\begin{split}u_{k-1}^{*}(x)&\geq u^{0}(\zeta_{k-1}(u_{k-1}^{*}(x)))+\int_{u_{k-1}^{*}}^{0}\kappa_{4}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega(\nu))\,d\nu\\
&\geq u^{0}(x)+\int_{u_{k-1}^{*}}^{0}\kappa_{4}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega(\nu))\,d\nu,\end{split}

(41)

here we used the inequality
u^{0}(\zeta_{k-1}(u_{k-1}^{*}(x)))\geq u^{0}(x)
which holds bacause
u^{0}
is increasing.

Once again, we want to bound the integral in the right-hand side of (
41
) in terms of the Hausdorff asymmetry of
\Omega
.
As in the proof of Theorem (
1.1
), we consider the threshold (
30
), so we can apply the propagation Lemma
2.2
, obtaining

\begin{split}u_{k-1}^{*}(x)-u^{0}(x)&\geq\int_{u_{k-1}^{*}}^{0}\kappa_{4}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega(\nu))\,d\nu\\
&\geq\int_{\max\{u_{k-1}^{*}(x),\overline{\mu}\}}^{0}\kappa_{4}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega(\nu))\,d\nu\\
&\geq\kappa_{5}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega)\left(-\max\{\overline{\mu},u_{k-1}^{*}(x)\}\right),\end{split}

(42)

where

\kappa_{5}(n,k)=\kappa_{4}(n,k)2^{-\frac{n+3}{2}}.

From the last inequality, we find that

\|u_{k-1}^{*}-u^{0}\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{k-1}^{*}\right)}\geq\kappa_{5}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega)(-\overline{\mu}),

(43)

that can be rewritten thanks to (
30
) as

\|u_{k-1}^{*}-u^{0}\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{k-1}^{*}\right)}\geq\kappa_{5}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+5}{2}}(\Omega)\left(-\frac{m}{2(n+2)}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(\Omega)}{D(\Omega)}\right).

Finally, from (
18
) the thesis follows.
∎

.

In

]

in the case of the Laplacian, obtaining

\dfrac{{\left\|u^{*}_{0}-u^{0}\right\|}_{\infty}}{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{2-n}{n}}{\left\|f\right\|}_{1}}\geq\widetilde{C}\alpha^{3}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega),

(44)

where
\alpha^{3}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega)
is the
Fraenkel asymmetry
of
\Omega
.
In that case, once a threshold is defined and one obtains a partial result as in (
43
), it is more subtle to conclude and obtain (
44
). In our case, the comparison with cones in Remark
2.7
allows us to conclude more easily.

3.3
Proof of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4

Now, we want to take advantage of inequality (
7
) to prove the comparison on the
k
-Hessian integral
H_{k}
contained in Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The proof is very similar to the proof of the Hardy-Littlewood inequality, indeed it follows from Cavalieri’s principle and Fubini’s Theorem

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(-u)S_{k}(D^{2}u)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(-u)f\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{0}^{f(x)}\,ds\right)\left(\int_{0}^{-u(x)}\,dt\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{f>s\}}(x)\chi_{\{-u>t\}}(x)\,dx\right)\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\left|\{f>s\}\cap\{-u>t\}\right|}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\min\{{\left|\{f>s\}\right|},{\left|\{-u>t\}\right|}\}\,ds\,dt.

Let us observe that the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities (
13
) imply

{\left|-u>t\right|}\leq\omega_{n}\left(\frac{W_{k-1}(-u>t)}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-k+1}}=\omega_{n}\left(\frac{W_{k-1}(-u^{*}_{k-1}>t)}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-k+1}}={\left|-u^{*}_{k-1}>t\right|},

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\{-u^{*}_{k-1}>t\}
is a ball, while from the definition of the Schwarz symmetrization, we have

{\left|\{f>s\}\right|}={\left|\{f^{0}>s\}\right|},

moreover, the sets
\{-u^{*}_{k-1}>t\}
and
\{f^{0}>s\}
are concentric balls, and
this implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(-u)S_{k}(D^{2}u)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\min\{{\left|\{f>s\}\right|},{\left|\{-u>t\}\right|}\}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\min\{|\{f^{0}>s\}|,{\left|\{-u^{*}_{k-1}>t\}\right|}\}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{k-1}^{*}}(-u^{*}_{k-1})f^{0}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega_{k-1}^{*}}(-u^{0})f^{0}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{k-1}^{*}}(-u^{0})S_{k}(D^{2}u^{0})\,dx.

∎

We are now ready for the proof of Theorem
1.4
. Here, we make use of what we proved in Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.

proof of Theorem
1.4
.

From the proof of Theorem
1.3
it follows that

\int_{\Omega}(-u)S_{k}(D^{2}u)\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega_{k-1}^{*}}(-u^{*}_{k-1})f^{0}\,dx,

that combined with (
42
) gives

\displaystyle H_{k}(u^{0},\Omega_{k-1}^{*})-H_{k}(u,\Omega)

\displaystyle\geq H_{k}(u^{0},\Omega_{k-1}^{*})-\int_{\Omega_{k-1}^{*}}(-u^{*}_{k-1})f^{0}\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{k-1}^{*}}(u^{*}_{k-1}-u^{0})f^{0}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\kappa_{5}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega^{*}_{k-1}}f^{0}(-\max\{\overline{\mu},u_{k-1}^{*}\})\,dx.

As the final step, we need to bound the right-hand side, and this can be done by considering that we are integrating a positive function

\displaystyle H_{k}(u^{0},\Omega_{k-1}^{*})-H_{k}(u,\Omega)

\displaystyle\geq\kappa_{5}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega)\int_{u^{*}_{k-1}<\overline{\mu}}f^{0}(-\overline{\mu})\,dx

\displaystyle=\kappa_{5}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega)(-\overline{\mu})\int_{0}^{{\left|u^{*}_{k-1}<\overline{\mu}\right|}}f^{0}\left(\left(\frac{a}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)\,da.

where the last equality holds as both
u_{k-1}^{*}
and
f^{0}
are radially symmetric functions.
Let us recall that the function

\varphi:s\rightarrow\dfrac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}f^{0}

is decreasing as
f^{0}
is decreasing, so we can bound

\int_{0}^{{\left|u^{*}_{k-1}<\overline{\mu}\right|}}f^{0}\left(\frac{a}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\,da\geq\frac{{\left|u^{*}_{k-1}<\overline{\mu}\right|}}{\omega_{n}\zeta_{k-1}^{n}(\Omega)}\int_{0}^{\omega_{n}\zeta_{k-1}^{n}(\Omega)}f^{0}\left(\frac{a}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\,da.

Moreover, the measure of the sublevel set can be bounded, thanks to (
24
) and(
32
)

\begin{split}|u^{*}_{k-1}-\overline{\mu}|&=W_{0}(\Omega^{*}_{k-1}(\overline{\mu}))=\omega_{n}\zeta_{0}^{n}(\Omega^{*}_{k-1}(\overline{\mu}))=\omega_{n}\zeta_{k-1}^{n}(\Omega^{*}_{k-1}(\overline{\mu}))\geq\omega_{n}\left(1-\frac{\overline{\mu}}{m}\right)^{n}\zeta_{k-1}^{n}(\Omega)\\
&\geq\omega_{n}\left(\frac{2n+3}{2(n+2)}\right)^{n}\zeta_{k-1}^{n}(\Omega),\end{split}

and then

H_{k}(u^{0},\Omega_{k-1}^{*})-H_{k}(u,\Omega)\geq\kappa_{5}(n,k)\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{n+3}{2}}(\Omega)(-\overline{\mu})\left(\frac{2n+3}{2(n+2)}\right)^{n}\int_{0}^{\omega_{n}\zeta_{k-1}^{n}(\Omega)}f^{0}\left(\left(\frac{a}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)\,da

As in the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we can bound the threshold
\overline{\mu}
thanks to (
30
) and (
18
), so to obtain

-\overline{\mu}\geq\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{2n(n+2)}\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{\omega_{n}}\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega),

and observing that

\int_{0}^{\omega_{n}\zeta_{k-1}^{n}(\Omega)}f^{0}\left(\left(\frac{a}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)\,da\geq{\left\|f\right\|}_{L^{1}(\Omega)},

then the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Let us observe that also Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.4
hold more in general if we assume that the solution to (
5
) is
p
-convex, for some
p\in(0,1]

Theorem
1.2
reads as follows: there exists a positive constant
\tilde{C}_{2}=\tilde{C}_{2}(n,k,p)
such that

\frac{\|u_{k-1}^{*}-u^{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{k-1}^{*})}}{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}\geq\tilde{C}_{2}\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{p(n+3)+2}{2p}}(\Omega),

\tilde{C}_{2}(n,k)=\frac{(n+1)(k+1)\beta_{n}}{2^{\frac{n+7}{2}}nk(n-1)(n-k+1)\omega_{n}}\left(\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{2(n+2)n\omega_{n})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

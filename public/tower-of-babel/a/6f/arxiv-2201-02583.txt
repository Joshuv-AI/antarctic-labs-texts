Abstract
. We prove a Poisson summation formula for the zero locus of a quadratic form in an even number of variables with no assumption on the support of the functions involved. The key novelty in the formula is that all “boundary terms” are given either by constants or sums over smaller quadrics related to the original quadric. We also discuss the link with the classical problem of estimating the number of solutions of a quadratic form in an even number of variables. To prove the summation formula we compute (the Arthur truncated) theta lift of the trivial representation of SL 2 ​ ( 𝔸 F ) \mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}) . As previously observed by Ginzburg, Rallis, and Soudry, this is an analogue for orthogonal groups on vector spaces of even dimension of the global Schrödinger representation of the metaplectic group.

1.
Introduction

Let
V_{0}=\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2d}
be an even dimensional affine space over a number field
F
and let
Q_{0}
be a nondegenerate anisotropic quadratic form on
V_{0}.
We allow the degenerate special case where
V_{0}=\{0\},
equipped with the trivial quadratic form.
For
i\geq 0
let

(1.1)

\displaystyle V_{i}:=V_{0}\oplus\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2i}.

We equip
V_{i}
with the nondegenerate quadratic form
Q_{i}
defined in §
2.1
. The quadratic form
Q_{i}
is in the Witt class of
Q_{0}.

For
F
-algebras
R
let

(1.2)

\displaystyle X_{i}(R):=\{u\in V_{i}(R):Q_{i}(u)=0\}

and let
X_{i}^{\circ}:=X_{i}-\{0\}
.
Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. If
V_{0}=\{0\}
we always assume that
\ell>1.

In this paper we prove a summation formula for
X_{\ell}
analogous to the Poisson summation formula. It involves the whole family of spaces
X_{i}
for
\ell\geq i\geq 0
.

1.1.
A summation formula

Let
\mathbb{A}_{F}
be the adeles of
F.

Fix an additive character
\psi:F\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}.
We can then define the Weil representation

(1.3)

\displaystyle\rho_{i}:=\rho_{Q_{i},\psi}:\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\times\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))

as usual. We have suppressed the orthogonal group in the Weil representation because it plays no role at the moment (but see the discussion below Theorem
1.3
).
We have an action

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}L^{\vee}:\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})&\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})\\
(g,f)&\longmapsto\left(v\mapsto f(g^{t}v)\right)\end{split}

and thus an action

(1.5)

\displaystyle r_{i}:=\rho_{i}\otimes L^{\vee}:\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\times\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2}).

For
i>0
we define the operator

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I:\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})&\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(X_{i}^{\circ}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))\\
f&\longmapsto\left(\xi\mapsto\int_{N(\mathbb{A}_{F})\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}r_{i}(g)f\left(\xi,0,1\right)d\dot{g}\right).\end{split}

Here
N\leq\mathrm{SL}_{2}
is the unipotent radical of the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices.
The integral
I(f)
is absolutely convergent and defines a function in
C^{\infty}(X^{\circ}_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
by lemmas
3.1
and
3.8
below.

For
i>i^{\prime}\geq 0
we have operators

\displaystyle c_{i}:\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle d_{i,i^{\prime}}:\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}(V_{i^{\prime}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})

defined as in (
6.1
), (
4.4
), and (
6.2
). Briefly,
c_{i}(f)
is the regularized value of
I(f)
at
0\in X_{i}(F).
On the other hand
d_{i,i-1}:=d_{i}
is given by a partial Fourier transform and then restriction to the complement of a hyperbolic plane. One then sets
d_{i,i^{\prime}}=d_{i,i-1}\circ\dots\circ d_{i^{\prime}+1,i^{\prime}}.

By convention,
d_{i,i}
is the identity.
We let

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\wedge}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})

be the usual
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
-equivariant Fourier transform (see (
3.9
)). We extend it to
\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})
by setting
\mathcal{F}_{X_{i}}:=1_{\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\wedge}
.

Let
[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]:=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}).

Our main theorem is the following summation formula:

Theorem 1.1
.

For
f\in\mathcal{S}(V_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})
one has that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(c_{i}(d_{\ell,i}(f))+\sum_{\xi\in X^{\circ}_{i}(F)}I(d_{\ell,i}(f))(\xi)\right)+\kappa d_{\ell,0}(f)(0_{V_{0}},0,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(c_{i}(d_{\ell,i}(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}}(f)))+\sum_{\xi\in X^{\circ}_{i}(F)}I(d_{\ell,i}(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}}(f)))(\xi)\right)+\kappa d_{\ell,0}(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}}(f))(0_{V_{0}},0,0).

Here

\displaystyle\kappa:=\begin{cases}\mathrm{meas}([\mathrm{SL}_{2}])&\textrm{ if }\dim V_{0}=0\\
0&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

The sums over
\xi
are in general infinite, but they are easily seen to be absolutely convergent by lemmas
3.1
and
3.8
below. In general, the
i
th summand on the left and right are not equal.

The key novelty in Theorem
1.1
is that it is valid under no restrictions on the test functions involved and all expressions are explicit and evidently geometric in nature. Poisson summation formulae for
X_{\ell}
and other special classes of varieties exist in the literature; we comment on this in the next paragraph. However Theorem
1.1
is the first instance of a Poisson summation formula for a singular variety in which every term has a manifestly geometric interpretation. We should point out though that two years after the current paper was submitted, the paper
[
GK24
]
was written. In it, the authors use the structure of the minimal representation to prove a Poisson summation formula very similar to Theorem
1.1
in the function field case. They also obtain analogous results for minimal representations on the exceptional groups of type
E_{n}.

If
V_{\ell}
is a split quadratic space and we place additional assumptions on
f
then the theorem is a consequence of more general work of Braverman and Kazhdan
[
BK02
]
. Their work was generalized to arbitrary test functions
f
in
[
CG21
]
, but several terms were given inexplicitly in terms of residues of Eisenstein series.
Related formulae are also established for arbitrary quadratic spaces in
[
Get18
]
and in a special case related to Rankin-Selberg products on
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
in
[
Get20
]
.
The relationship between Eisenstein series and the
V_{0}=\{0\}
case of Theorem
1.1
will be made precise in
[
Hsu
]
.

The interest in general test functions stems from the fact that restrictions on test functions limit the information one can extract from the formula. In more detail, it is expected, and can be verified in some cases, that the terms not corresponding to the open orbit under a suitable group action
correspond to poles of appropriate
L
-functions. Hence choosing test functions that eliminate these contributions hides information about the poles of
L
-functions.

Theorem
1.1
is very closely related to the circle method for quadratic forms and we feel that it will be useful for questions in analytic number theory. To make the relationship transparent we discuss the special case where
F=\mathbb{Q}
in §
9
below. Interestingly, in analytic number theory it is often only the most degenerate terms that are studied.

We expect that the image of the map
I:\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})\to C^{\infty}(X^{\circ}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
is the Schwartz space of
X(\mathbb{A}_{F})
in either the sense of
[
BK02
,
GK19
]
or the sense of
[
GHL23
]
and the map

\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}}:\mathcal{S}(V_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}(V_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})

descends to the Fourier transform on the Schwartz space of
X_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
defined in these references.
This may be proved locally. When
V_{0}=\{0\}
the non-Archimedean part of the theory will be contained in
[
Hsu
]
.
Moreover, in loc. cit. the local analogues of the operators
d_{i}
and
c_{i}
are examined; it turns out that they may be understood in terms of germ expansions at the origin. In particular, at least in the non-Archimedean case, the functions
I(\mathcal{F}_{X_{i}}(f))
,
I(d_{i}(f))
can be recovered from
I(f)
and a similar statement is true for the operators
c_{i}.

In this paper we do not give an alternate formulation of Theorem
1.1
in terms of
I(f)\in C^{\infty}(X^{\circ}_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
as opposed to
f\in\mathcal{S}(V_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2}).
We do prove in §
7
that, at least after grouping the
c_{i}
appropriately, all expressions in Theorem
1.1
can be written in terms of the space of coinvariants

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(X_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F})):=\mathcal{S}(V_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})_{r_{\ell}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))}.

We feel that defining the Schwartz space in this manner and focusing on it instead of a space of functions on
X_{\ell}^{\circ}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
is reasonable, as we now explain.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{BK}}(X_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
be the Schwartz space of
X_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F}),
defined as in
[
CG21
]
(therein
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{BK}}(X_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
is denoted simply by
\mathcal{S}(X_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
).
We expect that the map

\mathcal{S}(X_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))=\mathcal{S}(V_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})_{r_{\ell}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))}\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(X^{\circ}_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))

induced by
I
has image equal to
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{BK}}(X_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F})).
Let us assume this.
Then the map
\mathcal{S}(X_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))\to\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{BK}}(X_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
is formally analogous to a morphism from a stack to a coarse moduli space that resolves the singularities of the coarse moduli space. The analogy is even tighter if one bears in mind Bernstein’s definition of the Schwartz space of a smooth algebraic stack
[
Sak16
]
. For many purposes, the stack is more convenient than the coarse moduli space itself.
For similar reasons, we prefer to work with
\mathcal{S}(X_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
as opposed to
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{BK}}(X_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F})).
Despite this, to alleviate confusion we will not use the notation
\mathcal{S}(X_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
until §
7
.

1.2.
Canonicity

By a
framed flag of quadratic spaces extending
V_{0}
we mean a collection of vector spaces

\displaystyle V_{0}<V_{1}^{\prime}<\dots<V_{\ell}^{\prime}

equipped with a nondegenerate quadratic form
Q^{\prime}_{\ell}
on
V_{\ell}^{\prime}
such that
Q_{\ell}^{\prime}|_{V_{0}}=Q_{0}

together with
v_{i}^{\prime},w^{\prime}_{i}\in V_{i+1}^{\prime}(F)\cap V_{i}^{\prime\perp}(F)
satisfying
Q^{\prime}_{\ell}(v_{i}^{\prime})=Q_{\ell}^{\prime}(w_{i}^{\prime})=0
and
Q^{\prime}_{\ell}(v_{i}^{\prime}+w_{i}^{\prime})=1.

Here
V^{\prime\perp}_{i}\subset V_{\ell}^{\prime}
is the space orthogonal to
V_{i}
with respect to the pairing attached to
Q_{\ell}^{\prime}.

The
standard framed flag of quadratic spaces extending
V_{0}
is

V_{0}<V_{1}<\dots<V_{\ell}

equipped with
e_{\dim V_{0}+i},e_{\dim V_{0}+i+1}
for all
i.

The definition of
I
in (
1.6
) and
d_{i}
and
c_{i}
in (
6.1
), (
4.4
) and (
6.2
) below depends on the standard framed flag of quadratic spaces. We could also define maps
I,

d_{i},

c_{i}
with respect to another framed flag of quadratic spaces extending
V_{0}
and similarly define

X_{i}^{\prime}(R):=\{v\in V_{i}(R):Q_{i}(v)=0\}.

We refer to §
8
for details.

In §
8
we construct the universal framed flag of quadratic spaces extending
V_{0}:

V_{0}<V_{1}^{\mathrm{u}}<\dots<V_{\ell}^{\mathrm{u}}

Let
X_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}\subset V_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}
be the zero locus of the quadratic form
Q_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}.
In loc. cit. we moreover define operators

\displaystyle c_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}:\mathcal{S}(V_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq\ell-1

\displaystyle d_{\ell,i}^{\mathrm{u}}:\mathcal{S}(V_{\ell}^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}(V_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2}),

\displaystyle 0\leq i\leq\ell-1

\displaystyle I:\mathcal{S}(V_{i+1}^{u}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))

\displaystyle\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(X_{i}^{u\circ}(\mathbb{A}_{F})),

\displaystyle 0\leq i\leq\ell-1

and prove the following corollary of Theorem
1.1
:

Corollary 1.2
.

For
f\in\mathcal{S}(V_{\ell}^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})
one has that

\displaystyle c_{\ell}(f)+\sum_{\xi\in X^{\prime\circ}_{\ell}(F)}I(f)(\xi)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\left(c_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}(d_{\ell,i}^{\mathrm{u}}(f))+\sum_{\xi\in X^{\mathrm{u}\circ}_{i}(F)}I(d_{\ell,i}^{\mathrm{u}}(f))(\xi)\right)+\kappa d_{\ell,0}(f)(0_{V_{0}},0,0)

\displaystyle=c_{\ell}(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}^{\prime}}(f))+\sum_{\xi\in X^{\prime\circ}_{\ell}(F)}I(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}^{\prime}}(f))(\xi)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\left(c_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}(d^{\mathrm{u}}_{\ell,i}(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}^{\prime}}(f)))+\sum_{\xi\in X^{\mathrm{u}\circ}_{i}(F)}I(d_{\ell,i}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}^{\prime}}(f)))(\xi)\right)+\kappa d_{\ell,0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}^{\prime}}(f))(0_{V_{0}},0,0).

We do not know how to prove an analogue of Corollary
1.2
without fixing the subspace
V_{0}.
When the quadratic form
Q_{\ell}^{\prime}
is split we have
V_{0}=\{0\}
so this is not an issue.

1.3.
Sketch of the proof of Theorem
1.1

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, for
f\in\mathcal{S}(V_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})

we compute

(1.8)

\displaystyle\int_{[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]}\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)dg

where
\Theta_{f}
is the usual
\Theta
-function and the superscript
T
denotes the usual truncation operator employed by Arthur.

Theorem 1.3
.

There is a polynomial
p_{f}(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{C}[y_{1},y_{2}]
such that

\lim_{T\to\infty}\left(\int_{[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]}\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)dg-p_{f}(T,e^{T})\right)=0.

The constant term of
p_{f}(y_{1},y_{2})
is

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(c_{i}(d_{\ell,i}(\mathcal{F}_{2}(f)))+\sum_{\xi\in X^{\circ}_{i}(F)}I(d_{\ell,i}(\mathcal{F}_{2}(f)))(\xi)\right)+\kappa d_{\ell,0}(\mathcal{F}_{2}(f))(0_{V_{0}},0,0).

Theorem
1.1
then follows upon observing that
\Theta_{\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}_{X}(f))}(g)=\Theta_{\mathcal{F}_{2}(f)}(g)
(see §
6
for more details and the proof of Theorem
1.3
).

We now explain a representation-theoretic interpretation of Theorem
1.3
. For each
i
let
\mathrm{O}_{V_{i}}
(resp.
\mathrm{GO}_{V_{i}}
) be the orthogonal (resp. orthogonal similitude) group of
V_{i}
.
The partial Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{2}:\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})\to\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})
induced by the local partial Fourier transforms in (
3.5
) intertwines the action of
\rho_{i+1}
and
r_{i}
(see Lemma
3.2
). Hence it yields a
\mathbb{C}
-linear isomorphism

\mathcal{F}_{2}:\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})_{\rho_{i+1}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))}\tilde{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})_{r_{i}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))}

that is equivariant with respect to the action of
\mathrm{O}_{V_{i}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
(embedded in
\mathrm{O}_{V_{i+1}}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
in the obvious manner). The left hand side admits an obvious action of
\mathrm{GO}_{V_{i+1}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
and we can define a representation

\sigma_{i}:\mathrm{GO}_{V_{i+1}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\times\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})_{r_{i}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))}\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})_{r_{i}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))}

by transport of structure. We will make the action explicit in Proposition
3.3
below. The representation
\sigma_{i}
is the big theta lift of the trivial representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
.

Theorem
1.3
amounts to an explicit automorphic realization of
\sigma_{i}
. The realization is clearly similar in spirit to the Schrödinger model of the metaplectic representation of the two-fold cover of a symplectic group. In the Archimedean case this analogy is discussed in detail in
[
KM11
]
.
We hope that having such an explicit model will aid in applications analogous to the many uses of the Schrödinger model. In particular the explicit geometric description of the model may aid in its use in unfolding arguments that are so crucial in the theory of integral representations of
L
-functions.

We hasten to point out that it is already known that
\sigma_{i}
is automorphic.
In the case where
V_{0}=\{0\}
it is discussed in detail in
[
GRS97
]
, which contains a wealth of information about the representation and analogues of it for exceptional groups. In ibid., the theta lift is realized as a residue of an Eisenstein series. It is then related to the theta lift of the trivial representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
to
\mathrm{O}_{V_{i}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
in the special case where
F
is totally real using Kudla and Rallis’ regularized Siegel-Weil identity
[
KR94
]
. As far as the author knows, this perspective does not lead to a simpler proof of Theorem
1.3
(and hence Theorem
1.1
) valid for all test functions
f.
This is one of the main features of the current paper. For the purpose of comparing our work with
[
GRS97
]
it is also worth mentioning that we make no use of the theory of Eisenstein series on groups of absolute rank bigger than
1
.
There is another closely related automorphic realization that was studied by Kazhdan and Polishchuk in
[
KP04
]
. However, this automorphic realization was not completely determined. In particular it is not clear how to extract the explicit formulae of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
from
[
KP04
]
.

. In §

and prove several useful properties of them. The proof of Theorem

. In §

, that is, to the anisotropic case. This case requires one additional idea and is treated in §

. We put the results of §

together in §

to prove Theorem
1.3
. We then deduce Theorem
1.1
. In §

we explain how to modify the identity of Theorem
1.1
so that all terms depend only on the image of

in
\mathcal{S}(X_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{F})).

The proof of Corollary
1.2
is contained in §

. In §

we indicate the relationship of our results with classical questions related to the circle method.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Groups

We equip
V_{i}:=V_{0}\oplus\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2i}
with the quadratic form

(2.1)

\displaystyle Q_{i}(x):=\frac{x^{t}J_{i}x}{2},\quad J_{i}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}J_{0}&&&\\
&J&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&J\end{smallmatrix}\right)

where

is the matrix of

and

1&\end{smallmatrix}\right),
with
i
copies of

Let

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle_{i}:=v_{1}^{t}J_{i}v_{2}

be the pairing attached to the quadratic form
Q_{i}.

For

we identify
V_{i^{\prime}}
with the subspace

V_{i^{\prime}}\oplus\{0\}^{2i-2i^{\prime}}<V_{i}.

In particular, we have an obvious isomorphism

(2.3)

\displaystyle V_{i}\oplus\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2}\tilde{\longrightarrow}V_{i+1}.

We let

(2.4)

\displaystyle V_{i}^{\circ}:=V_{i}-\{0\}.

As in the introduction

is the similitude group of
(V_{i},Q_{i})
. Let

(2.5)

\displaystyle\lambda:\mathrm{GO}_{V_{i}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}

be the similitude norm. We identify
\mathrm{GO}_{V_{i}}
with a subgroup of
\mathrm{GO}_{V_{i+1}}
via the embedding given on points in an
F

R
by

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{GO}_{V_{i}}(R)&\longrightarrow\mathrm{GO}_{V_{i+1}}(R)\\
h&\longmapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}h&&\\
&\lambda(h)&\\
&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right).\end{split}

For

(viewed as a column vector) we let

(2.7)

\displaystyle u(x):=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_{V_{i}}&x&\\
&1&\\
-x^{t}J_{i}&-Q_{i}(x)&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)

and set

(2.8)

\displaystyle N_{i+1}(R):=\{u(x):x\in R^{\dim V_{i}}\}.

This is the unipotent radical of a maximal parabolic subgroup of (the neutral component of)
\mathrm{GO}_{V_{i+1}}
.

2.2.
The Weil representation

We define the semidirect product
\mathrm{SL}_{2}\rtimes\mathrm{GO}_{V_{i}}
by stipulating that

(g\rtimes h)(g^{\prime}\rtimes h)=g\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
&\lambda(h)\end{smallmatrix}\right)g^{\prime}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
&\lambda(h)\end{smallmatrix}\right)^{-1}\rtimes hh^{\prime}.

Let
v
be a place of
F
which we omit from notation, writing
F:=F_{v}.

Let

(2.9)

\displaystyle\rho_{i}:=\rho_{i,\psi}:\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)\rtimes\mathrm{GO}_{V_{i}}(F)\times\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(F))\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(F))

be the Weil representation.
Thus
\rho_{i}
is a local factor of the adelic Weil representation in (
1.3
).

For the convenience of the reader, we recall the definition of
\rho_{i}.

For
(h,f)\in\mathrm{GO}_{V_{i}}(F)\times\mathcal{S}(V_{i}(F))
one has

\rho_{i}(I_{2}\rtimes h)f(v)=f(h^{-1}v).

We usually work with the restriction of the Weil representation to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)\rtimes I_{V_{i}},
and hence suppress the similitude group
\mathrm{GO}_{V_{i}}(F)
from notation.

To recall the action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)
we set some notation.
Let

(2.10)

\displaystyle\gamma:=\gamma(Q_{i})

be the Weil index (it is independent of
i

Lemma 4.2
.

One has

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\Psi_{f}(x):=\int_{K}r_{i}(k)f(0,0,x)dk.

One has

\displaystyle Z_{r_{i}}(f,s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}\times K}\chi(a)|a|^{s}r_{i}(k)f(0,0,a)dkd^{\times}a=Z(\Psi_{f},\chi|\cdot|^{s}).

Let

Then we have

&a^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(r_{i}(k)f)(0,0,1)dkd^{\times}a.

Lemma 4.3

treats the case we excluded at the beginning of the current section when we assumed

Altogether this justifies the manipulations below.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
and Poisson summation we obtain

(4.5)

\displaystyle\Theta_{f}(g)=\sum_{\xi\in V_{i+1}(F)}r_{i}(g)\mathcal{F}_{2}(f)(\xi).

\displaystyle\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)dg=\int_{[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]}\Bigg(\sum_{(\xi,\xi^{\prime}_{1},\xi^{\prime}_{2})\in V_{i}(F)\oplus F^{2}}r_{i}(g)\mathcal{F}_{2}(f)(\xi,\xi^{\prime}_{1},\xi_{2}^{\prime})

It is easy to see from Lemma

that

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in X_{i}^{\circ}(F)}\int_{N(\mathbb{A}_{F})\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}|r_{i}(g)\mathcal{F}_{2}(f)|(\xi,0,1)d\dot{g}<\infty\quad\textrm{ and }

The proposition thus follows from the dominated convergence theorem.

Proposition 4.5
.

The integral

\int_{N(\mathbb{A}_{F})\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\mathbbm{1}_{\leq T}(H_{B}(g))r_{i}(g)\mathcal{F}_{2}(f)(0_{V_{i}},0,1)d\dot{g}

converges absolutely and
is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{s_{i}\in\left\{\frac{\dim V_{i}}{2}-1,\,\frac{\dim V_{i}}{2}-2,\,0\right\}}\mathrm{Res}_{s=s_{i}}\frac{e^{Ts}Z_{r_{i}}(\mathcal{F}_{2}(f),s+2-\frac{\dim V_{i}}{2})}{s}+o_{f}(1)

as

.

Proof.

We have

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{N(\mathbb{A}_{F})\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\mathbbm{1}_{\leq T}(H_{B}(g))r_{i}(g)\mathcal{F}_{2}(f)(0_{V_{i}},0,1)d\dot{g}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}\times K}\mathbbm{1}_{\leq T}(\log|a|)\chi(a)|a|^{\dim V_{i}/2}r_{i}(k)\mathcal{F}_{2}(f)(0_{V_{i}},0,a^{-1})\frac{dkd^{\times}a}{|a|^{2}}.\end{split}

Note that this expression is absolutely convergent. Indeed, the integral is supported in
|a|\leq e^{T},
and the integrand is rapidly decreasing as
|a|\to 0.

We wish to apply Mellin inversion to (
4.7
). Define
A_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
as in (
2.15
). For
\mathrm{Re}(s)>0
we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int_{A_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}}\mathbbm{1}_{\leq T}(\log|a|)|a|^{s}d^{\times}a=\frac{e^{Ts}}{s}

and for
\mathrm{Re}(s)
sufficiently small we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}\times K}\chi(a)|a|^{\dim V_{i}/2+s}r_{i}(k)\mathcal{F}_{2}(f)(0_{V_{i}},0,a^{-1})\frac{dkd^{\times}a}{|a|^{2}}=Z_{r_{i}}(\mathcal{F}_{2}(f),2-\tfrac{\dim V_{i}}{2}-s).

Hence (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{i\mathbb{R}+\sigma}e^{Ts}Z_{r_{i}}(\mathcal{F}_{2}(f),s+2-\tfrac{\dim V_{i}}{2})\frac{ds}{s}

for
\sigma
sufficiently large.

We now shift the contour to
\sigma
very small to see that the integral in the proposition is equal to

(4.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{s_{i}\in\left\{\frac{\dim V_{i}}{2}-1,\frac{\dim V_{i}}{2}-2,0\right\}}\mathrm{Res}_{s=s_{i}}\frac{e^{Ts}Z_{r_{i}}(\mathcal{F}_{2}(f),s+2-\tfrac{\dim V_{i}}{2})}{s}+o_{f}(1).

As

5.
Integrals of truncated anisotropic theta functions

Assume for this section that
V_{i}:=V_{0}
is an even dimensional vector space equipped with a nondegenerate anisotropic quadratic form
Q_{0}
. Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(V_{0}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
.
We refer to
\Theta_{f}(g)
as an
anisotropic theta function
.
We allow the special case where
V_{0}=\{0\}
. In this case we define
\mathcal{S}(V_{0}(\mathbb{A}_{F})):=\mathbb{C}
and the Weil representation is taken to be the trivial representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
.

Our aim is to compute
\int_{[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]}\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)dg
. Since
Q_{0}
is anisotropic we cannot reduce this computation to a smaller quadratic space as we did above. Instead, we apply a variant of the classical Rankin-Selberg method. Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2}).
For
g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
let

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Phi_{s}(g):=\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}}\Phi(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&t\end{smallmatrix}\right)g)|t|^{2s}d^{\times}t.

Moreover let

(5.2)

\displaystyle E(g,\Phi_{s}):=\sum_{\xi^{\prime}\in F^{2}-\{0\}}\Phi_{s}(\xi^{\prime}g).

Then, as is well-known,
E(g,\Phi_{s})
converges absolutely for
\mathrm{Re}(s)
large enough and admits a meromorphic continuation to the plane. Its residue at
s=1
is

\frac{\widehat{\Phi}(0)}{2}:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2}}\Phi(x,y)dxdy.

In particular the residue is independent of
g.
For all of this we refer the reader to
[
JZ87
, §1]
. Define

(5.3)

\displaystyle\kappa:=\begin{cases}\mathrm{meas}([\mathrm{SL}_{2}])&\textrm{ if }\dim V_{0}=0\\
0&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Lemma 5.1
.

The integral
\int_{[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]}\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)dg
converges absolutely. If
\dim V_{0}=0

\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]}\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)dg=\kappa f(0).

If
\dim V_{0}\geq 4,
then
\int_{[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]}\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)dg
is a polynomial in
e^{T}
and if
0<\dim V_{0}<4,
it is a polynomial in
e^{-T}.
If
\chi\neq 1
then this polynomial vanishes identically. If
\chi=1
then the constant term of the polynomial is
0.

Proof.

The lemma is easy to check when
\dim V_{0}=0.
Thus for the remainder of the proof we assume
\dim V_{0}>0.

Assume that
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{2})
and
\Phi(\xi^{\prime}k)=\Phi(\xi^{\prime})
for all
(\xi^{\prime},k)\in F^{2}\times K.
Assume moreover that
\widehat{\Phi}(0)\neq 0.

Then by the comments before the statement of Lemma
5.1
we have

\frac{\widehat{\Phi}(0)}{2}\int_{[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]}\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)dg=\int_{[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]}\mathrm{Res}_{s=1}E(g,\Phi_{s})\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)dg.

The function
\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)
is rapidly decreasing on
[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]
(see
[
Art80
]
) and hence using
[
JS81
, Lemma 4.2]
we deduce that the above is equal to

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{s=1}\int_{[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]}E(g,\Phi_{s})\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)dg.

We also deduce that at
s=1
the function
\int_{[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]}E(g,\Phi_{s})\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)dg
has at most a simple pole.
One has

\displaystyle\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)

\displaystyle=\Theta_{f}(g)-\sum_{\gamma\in B(F)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)}\mathbbm{1}_{>T}(H_{B}(\gamma g))\int_{[N]}\Theta_{f}(n\gamma g)dn

\displaystyle=\Theta_{f}(g)-\sum_{\gamma\in B(F)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)}\mathbbm{1}_{>T}(H_{B}(\gamma g))\rho_{0}(\gamma g)f(0).

Thus for
\mathrm{Re}(s)
sufficiently large,

\displaystyle\int_{[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]}E(g,\Phi_{s})\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)dg

\displaystyle=\int_{[\mathrm{SL}_{2}]}\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in B(F)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)}\Phi_{s}(\gamma^{\prime}g)\Big(\Theta_{f}(g)-\sum_{\gamma\in B(F)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)}\mathbbm{1}_{>T}(H_{B}(\gamma g))\rho_{0}(\gamma g)f(0)\Big)dg

\displaystyle=\int_{B(F)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\Phi_{s}(g)\Big(\Theta_{f}(g)-\sum_{\gamma\in B(F)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)}\mathbbm{1}_{>T}(H_{B}(\gamma g))\rho_{0}(\gamma g)f(0)\Big)dg

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{2}(F)N(\mathbb{A}_{F})\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\Phi_{s}(g)\int_{[N]}\Big(\Theta_{f}(ng)-\sum_{\gamma\in B(F)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)}\mathbbm{1}_{>T}(H_{B}(\gamma ng)\rho_{0}(\gamma ng)f(0))\Big)dnd\dot{g}.

The manipulations here are justified since

is of moderate growth for
\mathrm{Re}(s)
large

, Lemma 4.2]
and
\Theta_{f}^{T}(g)
is rapidly decreasing.

Using (

) in §

8.
Canonicity

As in §
1.2
, a
framed flag of quadratic spaces extending
V_{0}
(or more briefly a framed flag) is a collection of vector spaces

(8.1)

\displaystyle V_{0}<V_{1}^{\prime}<\dots<V_{\ell}^{\prime}

equipped with a nondegenerate quadratic form
Q^{\prime}_{\ell}
on
V_{\ell}^{\prime}
such that
Q_{\ell}^{\prime}|_{V_{0}}=Q_{0}

together with
v_{i}^{\prime},w^{\prime}_{i}\in V_{i}^{\prime}(F)\cap V_{i-1}^{\prime\perp}(F)
satisfying
Q^{\prime}_{\ell}(v_{i}^{\prime})=Q_{\ell}^{\prime}(w_{i}^{\prime})=0
and
Q^{\prime}_{\ell}(v_{i}^{\prime}+w^{\prime}_{i})=1.
We call the ordered set
\{v_{i}^{\prime},w_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{\ell}
the
based polarization
of the flag.
By a
morphism
of framed flags

(8.2)

\displaystyle\phi:V_{0}<V_{1}^{\prime}<\dots<V_{\ell}^{\prime}\longrightarrow V_{0}<V_{1}^{\prime\prime}<\dots<V_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}

we mean a morphism of (additive) algebraic groups
\phi:V_{\ell}^{\prime}\to V_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}
that is the identity on
V_{0}
and satisfies

(\phi(v_{i}^{\prime}),\phi(w_{i}^{\prime}))=(v_{i}^{\prime\prime},w_{i}^{\prime\prime})

for
1\leq i\leq\ell.
Denote by
Q_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}
the quadratic form on
V_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}.
If
\phi:V_{\ell}^{\prime}\to V_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}
is a morphism of framed flags then for every
F
-algebra
R
we have
Q^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}(\phi(v^{\prime}))=Q^{\prime}_{\ell}(v^{\prime})
for all
v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell}(R).

By basic linear algebra, given any two framed flags
V_{0}<V_{1}^{\prime}<\dots<V_{\ell}^{\prime}
and
V_{0}<V_{1}^{\prime\prime}<\dots<V_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}
there is a unique morphism as in (
8.2
). In other words:

Lemma 8.1
.

Any object of the category of framed flags extending
V_{0}
is initial. ∎

To fix ideas, we define the universal framed flag
V_{0}<V_{1}^{\mathrm{u}}<\dots<V_{\ell}^{\mathrm{u}}
inductively by setting
(V_{0}^{\mathrm{u}},Q_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}):=(V_{0},Q_{0})
and

V_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}(R):=\{av_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}+bw_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}:a,b\in R\}\oplus V_{i-1}^{\mathrm{u}}(R).

Here we equip
V_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}
with the unique nondegenerate quadratic form

such that

Q_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}(v_{i}^{\mathrm{u}})=Q_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}(w_{i}^{\mathrm{u}})=0
and
Q_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}(v_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}+w_{i}^{\mathrm{u}})=1.

Thus we have declared that
\{v_{i}^{\mathrm{u}},w_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}\}
is a basis of the orthogonal complement of
V_{i-1}^{\mathrm{u}}
in
V_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}
with respect to
Q_{i}.

Now assume that we are given a framed flag

Let

We define

Following (

9.
Application to summation over points on a quadric

Just as the Poisson summation formula gives a canonical method for estimating the number of points in a vector space of a given size, the summation formula of Theorem
1.1
gives a canonical method for estimating the number of points in a quadric of a given size. Given the ubiquity of the Poisson summation formula in analytic number theory (and more generally in analysis) we can imagine many applications of
Theorem
1.1
.

Of course the question of analyzing the number of points on a quadric has a long history, with theta functions and the circle method playing a key role. We point out in particular the author’s work in
[
Get18
]
based on earlier work of Heath-Brown
[
HB96
]
and Duke, Friedlander and Iwaniec
[
DFI93
]
. To make the relationship between the circle method and Theorem
1.1
more transparent we explain Theorem
1.1
in a special case. Let
F=\mathbb{Q}
and let
Q_{\ell}
be a quadratic form with matrix
J_{\ell}
defined as in (
2.1
). We assume that
J_{\ell}\in\mathrm{GL}_{V_{\ell}}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\det J_{\ell}=(-1)^{\ell}
. Assume moreover that
\ell>0
(which is to say that
V_{\ell}
is isotropic) and that
\dim V_{\ell}>4
. The case
\dim V_{\ell}=4
can also be treated, but the formula is slightly more complicated (see
[
Get18
,
HB96
]
). We choose

f=f_{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{V_{\ell+1}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})}\in\mathcal{S}(V_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})\oplus\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{2})

and assume that

d_{\ell}(f_{\infty})=c_{\ell}(f_{\infty})=0.

This is easy enough to arrange.
Then for any
B\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\leq\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}^{\times}

(9.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in X_{\ell}^{\circ}(\mathbb{Q})}I(f)\Big(\frac{\xi}{B}\Big)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in nV_{\ell}(\mathbb{Z})-\{0\}\\
Q_{\ell}(\xi)=0\end{subarray}}n^{\dim V_{\ell}/2-2}I(f_{\infty})\Big(\frac{\xi}{B}\Big).

Here we have used Lemma
3.8
.
Thus one side of our summation formula is a weighted version of a sum familiar from analytic number theory. It is a smoothed count of the number of integral zeros of
Q_{\ell}
of size at most
B.

By Corollary
6.2
the other side is

\displaystyle B^{\dim V_{\ell}-2}c_{\ell}(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}}(f))+B^{\dim V_{\ell}-2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in nV_{\ell}(\mathbb{Z})-\{0\}\\
Q_{\ell}(\xi)=0\end{subarray}}n^{\dim V_{\ell}/2-2}I(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}}(f_{\infty}))\left(B\xi\right)

\displaystyle+B^{\dim V_{\ell}/2}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\left(c_{i}(d_{\ell,i}(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}}(f)))+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in nV_{i}(\mathbb{Z})-\{0\}\\
Q_{i}(\xi)=0\end{subarray}}n^{\dim V_{i}/2-2}I(d_{\ell,i}(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}}(f_{\infty})))\left(\xi\right)\right)

\displaystyle+B^{\dim V_{\ell}/2}\kappa d_{\ell,0}(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}}(f))(0_{V_{0}},0,0).

Thus Theorem
1.1
gives a complete asymptotic expansion of (
9.1
) as a function of
B
in terms of sums over the zero loci of all quadratic forms of rank less than or equal to
\dim V_{\ell}
in the Witt class of
Q_{\ell}.

This goes far beyond what is obtained in the usual circle method, which usually only produces a description of the main term
c_{\ell}(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}}(F))
(but see
[
Get18
,
Sch17
,
Tra20
,
VW18
]
).
The term
c_{\ell}(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}}(f))
is essentially the familiar singular series.
The flexibility of choosing other test functions in
\mathcal{S}(X_{\ell}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}))
allows one to impose congruence conditions on the sum. It is important that one is allowed to choose arbitrary test functions at infinity for classical applications of this formula. More specifically, if one imposed the conditions of
[
BK02
]
, for example, then the term
c_{\ell}(\mathcal{F}_{X_{\ell}}(f))
would be zero, which would render the formula useless for the classical application of counting the number of points of size at most
B
on
X_{\ell}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Fast and Provable Tensor Robust Principal Component Analysis via Scaled Gradient Descent
Harry Dong, Tian Tong, Cong Ma, Yuejie Chi

Abstract
An increasing number of data science and machine learning problems rely on computation with tensors, which better capture the multi-way relationships and interactions of data than matrices. When tapping into this critical advantage, a key challenge is to develop computationally efficient and provably correct algorithms for extracting useful information from tensor data that are simultaneously robust to corruptions and ill-conditioning. This paper tackles tensor robust principal component analysis (RPCA), which aims to recover a low-rank tensor from its observations contaminated by sparse corruptions, under the Tucker decomposition. To minimize the computation and memory footprints, we propose to directly recover the low-dimensional tensor factors—starting from a tailored spectral initialization—via scaled gradient descent (ScaledGD), coupled with an iteration-varying thresholding operation to adaptively remove the impact of corruptions. Theoretically, we establish that the proposed algorithm converges linearly to the true low-rank tensor at a constant rate that is independent with its condition number, as long as the level of corruptions is not too large. Empirically, we demonstrate that the proposed algorithm achieves better and more scalable performance than state-of-the-art tensor RPCA algorithms through synthetic experiments and real-world applications.



Keywords:
low-rank tensors, Tucker decomposition, robust principal component analysis, scaled gradient descent, preconditioning.

1.1
Our approach

In this paper, we propose a novel iterative method for tensor RPCA with provable convergence guarantees. To minimize the memory footprint, we aim to directly estimate the ground truth factors, collected in
\bm{F}_{\star}=(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star})
, via optimizing the following objective function:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\bm{F},\bm{\mathcal{S}})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\big(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}+\bm{\mathcal{S}}-\bm{\mathcal{Y}}\right\|_{\fro}^{2},

(1)

where
\bm{F}=(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}})
and
\bm{\mathcal{S}}
are the optimization variables for the tensor factors and the corruption tensor, respectively. Despite the nonconvexity of the objective function, a simple and intuitive approach is to update the tensor factors via gradient descent, which, unfortunately, converges slowly even when the problem instance is moderately ill-conditioned
[
HWZ20
]
.

On a high level, our proposed method alternates between corruption pruning (i.e., updating
\bm{\mathcal{S}}
) and factor refinements (i.e., updating
\bm{F}
). At the beginning of each iteration, we update the corruption tensor
\bm{\mathcal{S}}
via thresholding the observation residuals as

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}=\mathcal{T}_{\zeta_{t+1}}\left(\bm{\mathcal{Y}}-\big(\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{t}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{t}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t}\right),\qquad t=0,1,\ldots

(2a)

where
\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}
is the update of the corruption tensor at the
t
-th iteration,
\mathcal{T}_{\zeta_{t+1}}\left(\cdot\right)
trims away the entries with magnitudes smaller than an iteration-varying threshold
\zeta_{t+1}
that is carefully orchestrated, e.g., following a geometric decaying schedule. As the estimate of the data tensor
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{t}=\big(\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{t}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{t}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t}
gets more accurate, the observation residual becomes more aligned with the corruptions, therefore the thresholding operator (
2a
) becomes more effective in identifying and removing the impact of corruptions. Turning to the low-rank tensor factors
\bm{F}
, motivated by the recent success of scaled gradient descent (ScaledGD)
[
TMC21a
,
TMC21b
,
TMPB+22
]
for accelerating ill-conditioned low-rank estimation, we propose to
update the tensor factors iteratively by descending along the scaled gradient directions:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{U}_{t+1}^{(k)}&=\bm{U}_{t}^{(k)}-\eta\nabla_{\bm{U}_{t}^{(k)}}\mathcal{L}(\bm{F}_{t},\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1})\big(\breve{\bm{U}}_{t}^{(k)\top}\breve{\bm{U}}_{t}^{(k)}\big)^{-1},\qquad k=1,2,3,\qquad\text{and}\\
\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}&=\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t}-\eta\Big(\big(\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)\top}\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)}\big)^{-1},\big(\bm{U}_{t}^{(2)\top}\bm{U}_{t}^{(2)}\big)^{-1},\big(\bm{U}_{t}^{(3)\top}\bm{U}_{t}^{(3)}\big)^{-1}\Big)\bcdot\nabla_{\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t}}\mathcal{L}(\bm{F}_{t},\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}).\end{split}

(2b)

Here,
\bm{F}_{t}=(\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{t}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{t}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t})
is the estimate of the tensor factors at the
t
-th iteration,
\nabla_{\bm{U}^{(k)}}\mathcal{L}(\bm{F},\bm{\mathcal{S}})
and
\nabla_{\bm{\mathcal{G}}}\mathcal{L}(\bm{F},\bm{\mathcal{S}})
are the partial derivatives of
\mathcal{L}(\bm{F},\bm{\mathcal{S}})
with respect to the corresponding variables,
\eta>0
is the learning rate, and

\displaystyle\breve{\bm{U}}_{t}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\big(\bm{U}_{t}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}_{t}^{(2)}\big)\mathcal{M}_{1}(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t})^{\top},\quad\breve{\bm{U}}_{t}^{(2)}=\big(\bm{U}_{t}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)}\big)\mathcal{M}_{2}(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t})^{\top},\quad\breve{\bm{U}}_{t}^{(3)}=\big(\bm{U}_{t}^{(2)}\otimes\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)}\big)\mathcal{M}_{3}(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t})^{\top}

are used to construct the preconditioned directions of the gradients, with
\otimes
denoting the Kronecker product. With the preconditioners, ScaledGD balances the tensor factors to find better descent directions, the benefits of which are more accentuated in ill-conditioned tensors where the convergence rate of vanilla gradient descent degenerates significantly, while ScaledGD is capable of maintaining a linear rate of convergence regardless of the condition number.

Theoretical guarantees.

Coupled with a tailored spectral initialization scheme, the proposed ScaledGD method converges linearly to the true low-rank tensor in both the Frobenius norm and the entrywise
\ell_{\infty}
norm at a constant rate that is independent of its condition number, as long as the level of corruptions—measured in terms of the fraction of nonzero entries per fiber—does not exceed the order of

\tfrac{1}{\mu^{2}\kappa r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}}
,
where
\mu
and
\kappa
are respectively the incoherence parameter and the condition number of the ground truth tensor
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
(to be formally defined later). This not only enables fast global convergence by virtue of following the scaled gradients rather than the vanilla gradients
[
TMPB+22
]
, but also lends additional robustness to finding the low-rank Tucker decomposition despite the presence of corruptions and gross errors. Moreover, our work provides the first refined entrywise error analysis for tensor RPCA, suggesting the errors are distributed evenly across the entries when the ground low-rank truth tensor is incoherent. To corroborate the theoretical findings, we further demonstrate that the proposed ScaledGD algorithm achieves better and more scalable performance than state-of-the-art matrix and tensor RPCA algorithms through synthetic experiments and real-world applications.

Comparisons to prior art.

While tensor RPCA has been previously investigated under various low-rank tensor decompositions, e.g.,
[
LFC+16
,
AJSN16
,
DBBG19
]
, the development of provably efficient algorithms under the Tucker decomposition remains scarce. The most closely related work is
[
CLX21
]
, which proposed a Riemannian gradient descent algorithm for the same tensor RPCA model as ours. Their algorithm is proven to also achieve a constant rate of convergence—at a higher per-iteration expense—as long as the fraction of outliers per fiber does not exceed the order of
\min\left\{\frac{1}{\mu_{s}^{4}\kappa_{s}^{14}r^{2}\log^{2}n},\,\frac{1}{\mu_{s}^{12}\kappa_{s}^{12}r^{3}}\right\}
(cf.
[
CLX21
, Theorem 5.1]
), where
\mu_{s}
and
\kappa_{s}
are the spikiness parameter and the worst-case condition number of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
, respectively. Using the relation
\mu\leq\mu_{s}^{2}\kappa_{s}^{2}
(cf.
[
CLX21
, Lemma 13.5]
) and
\kappa\leq\kappa_{s}
(cf. (
14
)) to conservatively translate our bound, our algorithm succeeds as long as the corruption level is below the order of
\frac{1}{\mu_{s}^{4}\kappa_{s}^{5}r^{3}}
, which is still significantly higher than that allowed in
[
CLX21
]
, when the outliers are evenly distributed across the fibers. See additional numerical comparisons in Section
4
.

1.2
Related works

Broadly speaking, our work falls under the recent surge of developing both computationally efficient and provably correct algorithms for high-dimensional signal estimation via nonconvex optimization, which has been particularly fruitful for problems with inherent low-rank structures; we refer interested readers to the recent overviews
[
CLC19
,
CC18
]
for further pointers. In the rest of this section, we focus on works that are most closely related to our paper.

Provable algorithms for matrix RPCA.

The matrix RPCA problem, which aims to decompose a low-rank matrix and a sparse matrix from their sum, has been heavily investigated since its introduction in the seminar papers
[
CLMW11
,
CSPW11
]
. Convex relaxation based approaches, which minimize a weighted sum of the nuclear norm of the data matrix and the
\ell_{1}
norm of the corruption matrix, have been demonstrated to achieve near-optimal performance guarantees
[
CLMW11
,
WGR+09
,
CSPW11
,
LCM10
,
CFMY21
,
CC14
]
. However, their computational and memory complexities are prohibitive when applied to large-scale problem instances; for example, solving the resulting semidefinite programs via accelerated proximal gradient descent
[
TY10
]
only results in a sublinear rate of convergence with a per-iteration complexity that scales cubically with the matrix dimension. To address the computational bottleneck, nonconvex methods have been developed to achieve both statistical and computational efficiencies simultaneously
[
NNS+14
,
CCW19
,
GWL16
,
YPCC16
,
TMC21a
,
CLY21
]
. Our tensor RPCA algorithm draws inspiration from
[
TMC21a
,
TMPB+22
]
, which adopt a factored representation of the low-rank object and invoke scaled gradient updates to bypass the dependence of the convergence rate on the condition number. The matrix RPCA method in
[
CLY21
]
differs from
[
TMC21a
]
by using a threshold-based trimming procedure—which we also adopt—rather than a sorting-based one to identify the sparse matrix, for further computational savings.

Provable algorithms for tensor RPCA.

Moving onto tensors, although one could unfold a tensor and feed the resulting matrices into a matrix RPCA algorithm
[
GQ14
,
ZWZM19
]
, destroying the tensor structure through matricizations can result in suboptimal performance because it ignores the higher-order interactions
[
YZ16
]
. Therefore, it is desirable to directly operate in the tensor space. However, tensor algorithms encounter unique issues not present for matrices.
For instance, while it appears straightforward to generalize the convex relaxation approach to tensors, it has been shown that computing the tensor nuclear norm is in fact NP-hard
[
FL18
]
; a similar drawback is applicable to the atomic norm formulation studied in
[
DBBG19
]
. Tensor RPCA has also been studied under different low-rank tensor decompositions, a small number of samples including the tubal rank
[
LFC+16
,
LFC+19
]
and the CP-rank
[
AJSN16
,
DBBG19
]
. These algorithms are not directly comparable with ours which uses the multilinear rank.

Robust low-rank tensor recovery.

Broadly speaking, tensor RPCA concerns with reconstructing a high-dimensional tensor with certain low-dimensional structures from incomplete and corrupted observations. Pertaining to works that deal with the Tucker decomposition,
[
XY19
]
proposed a gradient descent based algorithm for tensor completion,
[
TMPB+22
,
TMC22
]
proposed scaled gradient descent algorithms for tensor regression and tensor completion (which our algorithm also adopts),
[
LZ21
]
proposed a Gauss-Newton algorithm for tensor regression that achieves quadratic convergence,
[
WCW21
]
proposed a Riemannian gradient method with entrywise convergence guarantees, and
[
ARB20
]
studied tensor regression assuming the underlying tensor is simultaneously low-rank and sparse.

(4b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{3}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}\right)

\displaystyle=\bm{U}^{(3)}\mathcal{M}_{3}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}\right)(\bm{U}^{(2)}\otimes\bm{U}^{(1)})^{\top}=\bm{U}^{(3)}\breve{\bm{U}}^{(3)\top},\qquad\breve{\bm{U}}^{(3)}:=\big(\bm{U}^{(2)}\otimes\bm{U}^{(1)}\big)\mathcal{M}_{3}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}\right)^{\top}.

Given two tensors

Main results

2.1
Problem formulation

Suppose that the ground truth tensor
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
with multilinear rank
\bm{r}=(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
admits the following Tucker decomposition

\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}=\big(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star},

(8)

where
\bm{U}^{(1)}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times r_{1}}
,
\bm{U}^{(2)}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}\times r_{2}}
,
\bm{U}^{(3)}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{3}\times r_{3}}
are the factor matrices along each mode, and
\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}\times r_{2}\times r_{3}}
is the core tensor. The Tucker decomposition is not unique since for any
\bm{Q}^{(k)}\in\text{GL}(r_{k})
,
k=1,2,3
, in view of (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\big(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}=\big(\bm{U}^{(1)}\bm{Q}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)}\bm{Q}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}\bm{Q}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{Q}}

where
\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{Q}}=\big((\bm{Q}^{(1)})^{-1},(\bm{Q}^{(2)})^{-1},(\bm{Q}^{(3)})^{-1}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}
. Without loss of generality, to address ambiguity, we set the ground truth
\bm{F}_{\star}=(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star})
to satisfy that for each mode,
\bm{U}_{\star}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}\times r_{k}}
to have orthonormal columns, and

\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right)\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right)^{\top}=\big(\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)}\big)^{2},

(9)

the squared singular value matrix
\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)}
of
\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right)
. This can be easily met, for example, by taking the tensor factors
\bm{F}_{\star}
as the HOSVD of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
.

Observation model and goal.

Suppose that we collect a set of corrupted observations of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
as

\bm{\mathcal{Y}}=\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}+\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\star},

(10)

where
\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}
is the corruption tensor. The problem of tensor RPCA seeks to separate
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
and
\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}
from their sum
\bm{\mathcal{Y}}
as efficiently and accurately as possible.

Key quantities.

Obviously, the tensor RPCA problem is ill-posed without imposing additional constraints on the low-rank tensor
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
and the corruption tensor
\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}
, which are crucial in determining the performance of the proposed algorithm. We first introduce the incoherence parameter of the tensor
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
.

Definition 1

(Incoherence)
.

The incoherence parameter
\mu
of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mu:=\max_{k}\left\{\frac{n_{k}}{r_{k}}\left\lVert\bm{U}_{\star}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}\right\},

(11)

where
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}=\big(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}
is its Tucker decomposition.

The incoherence parameter roughly measures how spread the energy of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
is over its entries—the energy is more spread as
\mu
gets smaller. Moreover, we define a new notion of condition number that measures the conditioning of the ground truth tensor
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
as follows, which is weaker than previously used notions.

Definition 2

(Condition number)
.

The condition number
\kappa
of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
is defined as

\kappa:=\frac{\min_{k}\sigma_{\max}(\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right))}{\min_{k}\sigma_{\min}(\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right))}.

(12)

With slight abuse of terminology, denote

\displaystyle\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star})=\min_{k}\sigma_{\min}(\mathcal{M}_{k}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}))

(13)

as the minimum nonzero singular value of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
.

Remark
1
.

The above-defined condition number can be much smaller than the
worst-case
condition number
\kappa_{s}
used in prior analyses
[
TMPB+22
,
CLX21
,
HWZ20
]
, which is defined as

\kappa_{s}:=\frac{\max_{k}\sigma_{\max}(\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right))}{\min_{k}\sigma_{\min}(\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right))}\geq\frac{\min_{k}\sigma_{\max}(\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right))}{\min_{k}\sigma_{\min}(\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right))}=\kappa.

(14)

Furthermore, the condition number
\kappa
is also upper bounded by the largest condition number of the matricization along different modes, i.e.,
\kappa\leq\max_{k}\kappa_{k}=\max_{k}\frac{\sigma_{\max}(\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right))}{\sigma_{\min}(\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right))}
.

Turning to the corruption tensor, we consider a deterministic sparsity model following the matrix case
[
CSPW11
,
NNS+14
,
YPCC16
]
, where
\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}
contains at most a small fraction of nonzero entries per fiber. This is captured in the following definition.

Definition 3

(
\alpha
-fraction sparsity)
.

The corruption tensor
\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}
is
\alpha
-fraction sparse, i.e.,
\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}\in\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}
, where

\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}\coloneqq\Big\{\bm{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}:\;\|\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{i_{1},i_{2},:}\|_{0}\leq\alpha n_{3},\;\|\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{i_{1},:,i_{3}}\|_{0}\leq\alpha n_{2},\;\|\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{:,i_{2},i_{3}}\|_{0}\leq\alpha n_{1},\\
\mbox{ for all }1\leq i_{k}\leq n_{k},\quad k=1,2,3\Big\}.

(15)

With this setup in hand, we are now ready to describe the proposed algorithm.

2.2
Proposed algorithm

Our algorithm alternates between corruption removal and factor refinements. To remove the corruption, we use the following soft-shrinkage operator that trims the magnitudes of the entries by the amount of some carefully pre-set threshold.

Definition 4

(Soft-shrinkage operator)
.

For an order-
3
tensor
\bm{\mathcal{X}}
, the soft-shrinkage operator
\mathcal{T}_{\zeta}\left(\cdot\right):\,\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
with threshold
\zeta>0
is defined as

\displaystyle\big[\mathcal{T}_{\zeta}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}\right)\big]_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}:=\sgn\big([\bm{\mathcal{X}}]_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\big)\,\cdot\,\max\big(0,\big|[\bm{\mathcal{X}}]_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\big|-\zeta\big).

The soft-shrinkage operator
\mathcal{T}_{\zeta}\left(\right)
sets entries with magnitudes smaller than
\zeta
to
0
, while uniformly shrinking the magnitudes of the other entries by
\zeta
. At the beginning of each iteration, the corruption tensor is updated via

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}=\mathcal{T}_{\zeta_{t+1}}\left(\bm{\mathcal{Y}}-\big(\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{t}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{t}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t}\right),

(16a)

with the schedule
\zeta_{t}
to be specified shortly. With the newly updated estimate of the corruption tensor, the tensor factors are then updated by scaled gradient descent
[
TMPB+22
]
, for which they they are computed according to (
2b
) with respect to
\mathcal{L}(\bm{F}_{t},\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1})
in (
1
):

\displaystyle\bm{U}^{(k)}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\bm{U}^{(k)}_{t}-\eta\nabla_{\bm{U}^{(k)}_{t}}\mathcal{L}(\bm{F}_{t},\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1})\big(\breve{\bm{U}}_{t}^{(k)\top}\breve{\bm{U}}_{t}^{(k)}\big)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(1-\eta)\bm{U}_{t}^{(k)}-\eta\big(\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}\right)-\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{Y}}\right)\big)\breve{\bm{U}}_{t}^{(k)}\big(\breve{\bm{U}}_{t}^{(k)\top}\breve{\bm{U}}_{t}^{(k)}\big)^{-1}

(16b)

for
k=1,2,3
and

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t}-\eta\left(\big(\bm{U}^{(1)\top}_{t}\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)}\big)^{-1},\big(\bm{U}^{(2)\top}_{t}\bm{U}_{t}^{(2)}\big)^{-1},\big(\bm{U}^{(3)\top}_{t}\bm{U}_{t}^{(3)}\big)^{-1}\right)\bcdot\nabla_{\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t}}\mathcal{L}(\bm{F}_{t},\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1})

\displaystyle=(1-\eta)\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t}-\eta\left(\big(\bm{U}^{(1)\top}_{t}\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)}\big)^{-1}\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)\top},\big(\bm{U}^{(2)\top}_{t}\bm{U}_{t}^{(2)}\big)^{-1}\bm{U}_{t}^{(2)\top},\big(\bm{U}^{(3)\top}_{t}\bm{U}_{t}^{(3)}\big)^{-1}\bm{U}_{t}^{(3)\top}\right)\bcdot\left(\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}-\bm{\mathcal{Y}}\right).

(16c)

Here,
\eta>0
is the learning rate, and

\breve{\bm{U}}_{t}^{(1)}:=\big(\bm{U}_{t}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}_{t}^{(2)}\big)\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t}\right)^{\top},\quad\breve{\bm{U}}_{t}^{(2)}:=\big(\bm{U}_{t}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)}\big)\mathcal{M}_{2}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t}\right)^{\top},\;\mbox{and}\quad\breve{\bm{U}}_{t}^{(3)}:=\big(\bm{U}_{t}^{(2)}\otimes\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)}\big)\mathcal{M}_{3}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t}\right)^{\top}.

To complete the algorithm description, we still need to specify how to initialize the algorithm. We will estimate the tensor factors via the spectral method, by computing the HOSVD of the observation after applying the soft-shrinkage operator:

\big(\bm{U}_{0}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{0}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{0}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{0}\big)=\mathcal{H}_{\bm{r}}\left(\bm{\mathcal{Y}}-\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\right),\qquad\mbox{where}\quad\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{0}=\mathcal{T}_{\zeta_{0}}\left(\bm{\mathcal{Y}}\right).

Altogether, we arrive at Algorithm
1
, which we still dub as ScaledGD for simplicity.

Algorithm 1

ScaledGD for tensor robust principal component analysis

Input:
the observed tensor
\bm{\mathcal{Y}}
, the multilinear rank
\bm{r}
, learning rate
\eta
, and threshold schedule
\{\zeta_{t}\}_{t=0}^{T}
.

Initialization:

\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{0}=\mathcal{T}_{\zeta_{0}}\left(\bm{\mathcal{Y}}\right)
and
\big(\bm{U}_{0}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{0}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{0}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{0}\big)=\mathcal{H}_{\bm{r}}\left(\bm{\mathcal{Y}}-\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\right)
.

for

t=0,1,\dots,T-1

do

Update the corruption tensor
\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}
via (
16a
);

Update the tensor factors
\bm{F}_{t+1}=\big(\bm{U}^{(1)}_{t+1},\bm{U}^{(2)}_{t+1},\bm{U}^{(3)}_{t+1},\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}\big)
via (
16b
) and (
16c
);

end

for

Output:
the tensor factors
\bm{F}_{T}=\big(\bm{U}_{T}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{T}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{T}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{T}\big)
.

Computational benefits.

It is worth highlighting that the proposed tensor RPCA algorithm possesses several computational benefits which might be of interest in applications.

•

Advantages over matrix RPCA algorithms.
While it is possible to matricize the input tensor and then apply the matrix RPCA algorithms, they can only exploit the low-rank structure along the mode that the tensor is unfolded, rather than along multiple rows simultaneously as in the tensor RPCA algorithm. In addition, the space complexity of storing and computing the factors is much higher for the matrix RPCA algorithms, where the size of the factors become multiplicative in terms of the tensor dimensions due to unfolding, rather than linear as in the tensor RPCA algorithm.

•

Generalization to
N
-th order tensors.
Although the description of Algorithm
1
is tailored to an order-
3
tensor, our algorithm is easily generalizable to any
N
-th order tensor; in fact, Algorithm
1
can be applied almost verbatim by redefining

\breve{\bm{U}}_{t}^{(k)}=\big(\bm{U}_{t}^{(N)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\bm{U}_{t}^{(k+1)}\otimes\bm{U}_{t}^{(k-1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)}\big)\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t}\right)^{\top},\qquad k=1,\ldots,N

to its natural high-order counterpart. This extension is numerically evaluated in our experiments in Section
4
.

•

Parallelizability.
At each iteration of the proposed algorithm, each tensor factor is updated independently as done in (
16b
) and (
16c
), therefore we can update them in a parallel manner. This improvement becomes more apparent as the order of the tensor increases.

•

Selective modes to update
: If we know the underlying ground truth tensor is only low-rank along certain mode, we can choose to skip the iterative updates of the rest of the modes after initialization to reduce computational costs, which we demonstrate empirically in Section
4.3
.

2.3
Performance guarantees

Motivated by the analysis in
[
TMPB+22
]
, we consider the following distance metric, which not only resolves the ambiguity in the Tucker decomposition, but also takes the preconditioning factor into consideration.

Definition 5

(Distance metric)
.

Letting
\bm{F}:=\big(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}}\big)
and
\bm{F}_{\star}:=\big(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\big)
, denote

\dist^{2}(\bm{F},\bm{F}_{\star}):=\inf_{\bm{Q}^{(k)}\in\GL(r_{k})}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(k)}\bm{Q}^{(k)}-\bm{U}^{(k)}_{\star}\big)\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{\fro}^{2}+\left\lVert\big((\bm{Q}^{(1)})^{-1},(\bm{Q}^{(2)})^{-1},(\bm{Q}^{(3)})^{-1}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}-\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\fro}^{2},

(17)

where we recall
\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)}
is the singular value matrix of
\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right)
,
k=1,2,3
. Moreover, if the infimum is attained at the arguments
\{\bm{Q}^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^{3}
, they are called the optimal alignment matrices between
\bm{F}
and
\bm{F}_{\star}
.

Fortunately, the proposed ScaledGD algorithm (cf. Algorithm
1
) provably recovers the ground truth tensor—as long as the fraction of corruptions is not too large—with proper choices of the tuning parameters, as captured in following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{Y}}=\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}+\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
, where
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
is
\mu
-incoherent with multilinear rank
\bm{r}=(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
, and
\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}
is
\alpha
-sparse. Suppose that the threshold values
\{\zeta_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
obey that
\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\zeta_{0}\leq 2\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}
and
\zeta_{t+1}=\rho\zeta_{t}
,
t\geq 1
, for some properly tuned
\zeta_{1}:=8\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{3}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}}{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star})
and
\frac{1}{7}\leq\eta\leq\frac{1}{4}
, where
\rho=1-0.45\eta
. Then, the iterates
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{t}=\big(\bm{U}_{t}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{t}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{t}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{t}
satisfy

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{t}-\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\fro}

\displaystyle\leq 0.03\rho^{t}\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}),

(18a)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{t}-\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 8\rho^{t}\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{3}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}}{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}),

(18b)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{t}-\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 16\rho^{t-1}\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{3}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}}{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star})

(18c)

for all
t\geq 0
, as long as the level of corruptions obeys

\alpha\leq\frac{c_{0}}{\mu^{2}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\kappa}

for some sufficiently small
c_{0}>0
.

The value of
\rho
was selected to simplify the proof and should not be taken as an optimal convergence rate.
In a nutshell, Theorem
1
has the following immediate consequences:

•

Exact recovery.
Upon appropriate choices of the parameters, if the level of corruptions
\alpha
is small enough, i.e. not exceeding the order of
\frac{1}{\mu^{2}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\kappa}
, we can ensure that the proposed Algorithm
1
exactly recovers the ground truth tensor
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
even when the gross corruptions are arbitrary and adversarial. As mentioned earlier, our result significantly enlarges the range of allowable corruption levels for exact recovery when the outliers are evenly distributed across the fibers, compared with the prior art established in
[
CLX21
]
.

•

Constant linear rate of convergence.
The proposed ScaledGD algorithm (cf. Algorithm
1
) finds the ground truth tensor at a
constant
linear rate, which is independent of the condition number, from a carefully designed spectral initialization. Consequently, the proposed ScaledGD algorithm inherits the computational robustness against ill-conditioning as
[
TMPB+22
]
, even in the presence of gross outliers, as long as the thresholding operations are properly carried out.

•

Refined entrywise error guarantees.
Furthermore, when
\mu=O(1)
and
r=O(1)
, the entrywise error bound (
18b
)—which is smaller than the Frobenius error (
18a
) by a factor of
\sqrt{\frac{1}{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}
—suggests the errors are distributed in an evenly manner across the entries for incoherent and low-rank tensors. The same applies to the entrywise error bound of the sparse tensor (
18c
) which exhibits similar behavior as (
18b
). To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time such a refined entrywise error analysis is established for tensor RPCA.

3
Outline of the analysis

In this section, we outline the proof of Theorem
1
. The proof is inductive in nature, where we aim to establish the following induction hypothesis at all the iterations:

\displaystyle\dist(\bm{F}_{t},\bm{F}_{\star})

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{0}\rho^{t}\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}),

(19a)

\displaystyle\max_{k}\left\{\sqrt{\frac{n_{k}}{r_{k}}}\left\lVert(\bm{U}_{t}^{(k)}\bm{Q}_{t}^{(k)}-\bm{U}_{\star}^{(k)})\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{t}\sqrt{\mu}\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}),

(19b)

where
\rho=1-0.45\eta
,
\epsilon_{0}<0.01
is some sufficiently small constant, and
\{\bm{Q}_{t}^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^{3}
are the optimal alignment matrices between
\bm{F}_{t}
and
\bm{F}_{\star}
. The claims (
18
) in Theorem
1
follow immediately with the aid of Lemma

(see Appendix

-th iteration, assuming it holds at the
t
-th iteration, as long as the corruption level is not too large.

(Distance contraction)
.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{Y}}=\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}+\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}

\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
is
\mu
-incoherent with multilinear rank

, and
\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}
is
\alpha
-sparse.

be the

4.3
Background subtraction via selective mode update

We now apply ScaledGD to the task of background subtraction using videos from the VIRAT dataset
[
OHP+11
]
, where the height and width of the videos are reduced by a factor of 4 due to hardware limitations. The video data can be thought as a multi-way tensor spanning across the height, width, frames, as well as different color channels of the scene. Here, the low-rank tensor corresponds to the background in the video which is fairly static over the frames, and the sparse tensor corresponds to the foreground containing moving objects which takes a small number of pixels. In particular, it is reasonable to assume that the background tensor is low-rank for the mode corresponding to the frames, but full rank in other modes. Motivated by this observation, one might be tempted to selectively only update the core tensor and the factor matrix corresponding to the mode for frames while keeping the other factor matrices fixed after the spectral initialization. We compare the results using this selective mode update strategy with the original ScaledGD algorithm in Figure
5
, where the same hyperparameters are used in both. It can be seen that skipping updates of the full-rank factor matrices produced qualitatively similar results while gaining a significant per-iteration speed-up of about 4.6 to 5 times. We expect the speed improvement to be greater for larger tensors, as more computation can be bypassed.

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we proposed a new algorithm for tensor RPCA, which applies scaled gradient descent with an iteration-varying thresholding operation to adaptively remove the impact of corruptions. The algorithm is demonstrated to be fast, provably accurate, and achieve competitive performance over real-world applications. It opens several interesting directions for future research.

•

Dependency with the condition number from spectral initialization.
As seen from the analysis, the local linear convergence of Algorithm
1
succeeds under a larger range of the sparsity level
\alpha
independent of the condition number
\kappa
. The constraint on
\alpha
with respect to the condition number
\kappa
mainly stems from the spectral initialization, and it is of great interest to see if it is possible to refine the analysis in terms of the dependency with
\kappa
, which likely will require new tools.

•

Missing data.
An important extension is to handle missing data in tensor RPCA, which seeks to recover a low-rank ground truth tensor from its partially observed entries possibly corrupted by gross errors. Our proposed algorithm can be adapted to this case in a straightforward fashion by considering the loss function defined only over the observed entries, and understanding its performance guarantees is a natural step.

•

Streaming data.
An equally interesting direction is to perform tensor RPCA over online and streaming data, where the fibers or slices of the tensor arrive sequentially over time, a situation that is common in data analytics
[
BCL18
,
VN18
]
. It is of great interest to develop low-complexity algorithms that can estimate and track the low-rank tensor factors as quickly as possible.

•

Hyperparameters.
The proposed algorithm contains several hyperparameters that need to be tuned carefully to fully unleash its potential. A recent follow-up
[
DSDC22
]
examined a learned approach based on algorithm unfolding and self-supervised learning to enable automatic hyperparameter tuning. In addition, understanding the performance when the rank is only imperfectly specified is also of great importance, which is closely related to
[
XSCM23
]
.

In view of

\displaystyle\dist(\bm{F}_{0},\bm{F}_{\star})

\bm{U}_{0}^{(k)}
has orthonormal columns. We thus

\displaystyle\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}_{0}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{0}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{0}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{0}-\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\fro}^{2}

Lemma 5
.

Suppose that
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}=(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)})\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}

\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{3}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}}{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\kappa\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}).

By the property of matricizations (cf. (
4
)), we have
\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right)=\bm{U}_{\star}^{(k)}\breve{\bm{U}}_{\star}^{(k)\top}
for any

Furthermore,
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}
is invariant to matricizations, so

\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right)\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert\bm{U}_{\star}^{(k)}\breve{\bm{U}}_{\star}^{(k)\top}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Without loss of generality, we choose
k=1
. It then follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\left\lVert\breve{\bm{U}}_{\star}^{(1)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}

where the second line follows from the definition of incoherence of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
, i.e.,
\left\lVert\bm{U}_{\star}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu r_{k}}{n_{k}}}
, and
\left\lVert\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right)\right\rVert_{\op}=\sigma_{\max}(\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right))
. Applying the above bound for any

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{3}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}}{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\min_{k}\sigma_{\max}(\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right))=\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{3}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}}{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\kappa\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}),

where we have used the definition of
\kappa

.

Given a tensor
\bm{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
, suppose its rank-
\bm{r}
truncated HOSVD is
(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)})\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}
with
\bm{U}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}\times r_{k}}
and
\bm{\mathcal{G}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}\times r_{2}\times r_{3}}
, and
r_{k}\leq n_{k}
for
k=1,2,3
. Then,

\displaystyle\bm{U}^{(k)\top}\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)\breve{\bm{U}}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\breve{\bm{U}}^{(k)\top}\breve{\bm{U}}^{(k)},

(65)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)^{\top}\bm{U}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\bm{U}^{(k)}\breve{\bm{U}}^{(k)\top}\breve{\bm{U}}^{(k)},

(66)

where
\breve{\bm{U}}^{(k)}
is defined in (
4
).

Proof.

Set the full HOSVD of
\bm{\mathcal{T}}
to be
\big(\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}
.
Since
\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(k)}
contains all the left singular vectors of
\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)
, it can be decomposed into the following block structure:

\displaystyle\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(k)}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}^{(k)}&\bar{\bm{U}}^{(k)}\end{bmatrix},

(67)

where
\bar{\bm{U}}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}\times(n_{k}-r_{k})}
contains the bottom
(n_{k}-r_{k})
left singular vectors. The rest of the proof focuses on the first mode (i.e.,
k=1
), while other modes follow from similar arguments.

Let us begin with proving (
65
).
Plugging in the definition of
\breve{\bm{U}}^{(1)}
from
(
4a
)
, we see that

\displaystyle\bm{U}^{(1)\top}\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)\breve{\bm{U}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\bm{U}^{(1)\top}\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(1)}\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}\right)\big(\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(2)}\big)^{\top}\big(\bm{U}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}^{(2)}\big)\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}\right)^{\top}

\displaystyle=\bm{U}^{(1)\top}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}^{(1)}&\bar{\bm{U}}^{(1)}\end{bmatrix}\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}\right)\left(\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}^{(3)\top}\\
\bar{\bm{U}}^{(3)\top}\end{bmatrix}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}^{(2)\top}\\
\bar{\bm{U}}^{(2)\top}\end{bmatrix}\right)\big(\bm{U}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}^{(2)}\big)\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}\right)^{\top},

where the second line uses the block structure (
67
).
By the mixed product property of Kronecker products, we have

\displaystyle\bm{U}^{(1)\top}\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)\breve{\bm{U}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{1}}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}\right)\left(\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}^{(3)\top}\bm{U}^{(3)}\\
\bar{\bm{U}}^{(3)\top}\bm{U}^{(3)}\end{bmatrix}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}^{(2)\top}\bm{U}^{(2)}\\
\bar{\bm{U}}^{(2)\top}\bm{U}^{(2)}\end{bmatrix}\right)\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}\right)^{\top}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{1}}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}\right)\left(\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{3}}\\
\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{2}}\\
\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}\right)\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}\right)^{\top},

(68)

where we used the fact that the singular vectors are orthonormal. Note that

\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{1}}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}\right)\left(\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{3}}\\
\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{2}}\\
\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}\right)=\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\left(\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{1}}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{2}}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{3}}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}\right)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}\times r_{2}r_{3}},

where
\left(\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{1}}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{2}}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{3}}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}\right)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}
is equivalent to trimming off the entries
[\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}]_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}
for all
i_{1}>r_{1}
,
i_{2}>r_{2}
, or
i_{3}>r_{3}
.
Since
\bm{\mathcal{G}}
is the section of
[\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}]_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}
where
1\leq i_{1}\leq r_{1},1\leq i_{2}\leq r_{2},1\leq i_{3}\leq r_{3}

[
VVM12
]
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{1}}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{2}}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{r_{3}}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}\right)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathcal{G}}.

This allows us to simplify (
68
) as

\displaystyle\bm{U}^{(1)\top}\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)\breve{\bm{U}}^{(1)}=\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}\right)\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}\right)^{\top}=\breve{\bm{U}}^{(1)\top}\breve{\bm{U}}^{(1)},

where the last equality follows from the definition of
\breve{\bm{U}}^{(1)}
from (
4
) and
(\bm{U}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}^{(2)})^{\top}(\bm{U}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}^{(2)})=\bm{I}
by construction of the HOSVD. This completes the proof of (
65
).

Turning to (
66
), we begin with the observation

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)^{\top}

\displaystyle=\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(1)}\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}\right)\big(\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(2)}\big)^{\top}\big(\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(2)}\big)\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}\right)^{\top}\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(1)\top}

\displaystyle=\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(1)}\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}\right)\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}\right)^{\top}\bm{U}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(1)\top}.

(69)

By the “all-orthogonal” property in
[
DLDMV00
]
,
\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}\right)\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}\right)^{\top}=(\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(1)})^{2}
, the squared singular value matrix of
\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)
.
By assigning
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(1)}
with the block representation

\displaystyle\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\Sigma}^{(1)}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\bar{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(1)}\end{bmatrix},

where
\bm{\Sigma}^{(1)}=\sqrt{\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}\right)\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}\right)^{\top}}
and
\bar{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(1)}
contain the top
r_{1}
singular values and bottom
n_{1}-r_{1}
singular values, respectively, of
\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)
.
Coupled with same block structure as in (
67
), (
69
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)^{\top}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}^{(1)}&\bar{\bm{U}}^{(1)}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}(\bm{\Sigma}^{(1)})^{2}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&(\bar{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(1)})^{2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}^{(1)\top}\\
\bar{\bm{U}}^{(1)\top}\end{bmatrix}.

Multiply by
\bm{U}^{(1)}
on the right to arrive at

\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\right)^{\top}\bm{U}^{(1)}=\bm{U}^{(1)}(\bm{\Sigma}^{(1)})^{2}=\bm{U}^{(1)}\breve{\bm{U}}^{(1)\top}\breve{\bm{U}}^{(1)}.

∎

E.2
Perturbation bounds

Below is a useful perturbation bound for matrices.

Lemma 7
.

Given two matrices
\bm{U},\bm{U}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
that have full column rank, two invertible matrices
\bar{\bm{Q}},\bm{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
, and a positive definite matrix
\bm{\Sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
. Suppose that
\sigma_{\min}(\bm{U}_{\star})>\left\lVert\bm{U}\bar{\bm{Q}}-\bm{U}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\op}
. Then the following holds true

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bar{\bm{Q}}^{-1}(\bm{Q}-\bar{\bm{Q}})\bm{\Sigma}\right\rVert_{\op}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\lVert\bm{U}(\bm{Q}-\bar{\bm{Q}})\bm{\Sigma}\right\rVert_{\op}}{\sigma_{\min}(\bm{U}_{\star})-\left\lVert\bm{U}\bar{\bm{Q}}-\bm{U}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\op}}.

Proof.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bar{\bm{Q}}^{-1}(\bm{Q}-\bar{\bm{Q}})\bm{\Sigma}\right\rVert_{\op}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\bar{\bm{Q}}^{-1}(\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{U})^{-1}\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{U}(\bm{Q}-\bar{\bm{Q}})\bm{\Sigma}\right\rVert_{\op}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\bar{\bm{Q}}^{-1}(\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{U})^{-1}\bm{U}^{\top}\right\rVert_{\op}\left\lVert\bm{U}(\bm{Q}-\bar{\bm{Q}})\bm{\Sigma}\right\rVert_{\op}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\lVert\bm{U}(\bm{Q}-\bar{\bm{Q}})\bm{\Sigma}\right\rVert_{\op}}{\sigma_{\min}(\bm{U}\bar{\bm{Q}})},

where the last equality comes from the fact that
\bar{\bm{Q}}^{-1}(\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{U})^{-1}\bm{U}^{\top}
and
\bm{U}\bar{\bm{Q}}
are pseudoinverses.
By Weyl’s inequality, we know
|\sigma_{i}(\bm{M})-\sigma_{i}(\bm{M}+\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{M}})|\leq\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{M}}\right\rVert
.
Taking
\bm{M}:=\bm{U}_{\star}
and
\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{M}}:=\bm{U}\bar{\bm{Q}}-\bm{U}_{\star}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bar{\bm{Q}}^{-1}(\bm{Q}-\bar{\bm{Q}})\bm{\Sigma}\right\rVert_{\op}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\lVert\bm{U}(\bm{Q}-\bar{\bm{Q}})\bm{\Sigma}\right\rVert_{\op}}{\sigma_{\min}(\bm{U}_{\star})-\left\lVert\bm{U}\bar{\bm{Q}}-\bm{U}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\op}}

as long as
\sigma_{\min}(\bm{U}_{\star})>\left\lVert\bm{U}\bar{\bm{Q}}-\bm{U}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\op}
.
∎

Lemma 8

(
[
TMPB+22

)
.

Suppose
\bm{F}=(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}})
and
\bm{F}=(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star})
are aligned, and
\dist(\bm{F},\bm{F}_{\star})\leq\epsilon\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star})
for some
0<\epsilon<1
.

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\Delta}_{\bm{\mathcal{G}}}\right)^{\top}(\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)})^{-1}\right\rVert_{\op}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon;

(70a)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(k)}\big(\bm{U}^{(k)\top}\bm{U}^{(k)}\big)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\op}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\epsilon};

(70b)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\breve{\bm{U}}^{(k)}\big(\breve{\bm{U}}^{(k)\top}\breve{\bm{U}}^{(k)}\big)^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{\op}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-\epsilon)^{3}};

(70c)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)}\big(\breve{\bm{U}}^{(k)\top}\breve{\bm{U}}^{(k)}\big)^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{\op}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-\epsilon)^{6}};

(70d)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\breve{\bm{U}}^{(1)}-\breve{\bm{U}}_{\star}^{(1)}\right\rVert_{\fro}\leq\left(1+\epsilon+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{3}\right)

\displaystyle\left(\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(2)}-\bm{U}^{2)}_{\star}\big)\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(2)}\right\rVert_{\fro}+\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(3)}-\bm{U}^{3)}_{\star}\big)\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(3)}\right\rVert_{\fro}+\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{G}}-\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\fro}\right).

(70e)

For (
70e
), similar bounds exist for the other modes.
Furthermore, if
0<\epsilon\leq 0.2
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}-\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\fro}\leq 3\dist(\bm{F},\bm{F}_{\star}).

(71)

The next set of lemmas, which is crucial in our analysis, deals with perturbation bounds when relating a tensor
\bm{\mathcal{X}}=\big(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}
to the ground truth
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
, where the tensor tuples
\bm{F}=(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}})
and
\bm{F}_{\star}=(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star})
are aligned.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
be
\mu
-incoherent with the Tucker decomposition
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}=\big(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}
of rank
\bm{r}=(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
, and
\big\{\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)}\big\}_{k=1,2,3}
be the set of singular value matrices of different matricizations of
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
.
In addition, let
\bm{F}:=(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}})
and
\bm{F}_{\star}:=(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star})
be aligned, where
\bm{\mathcal{X}}=\big(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}
. Suppose

\displaystyle\max_{k}\left\{\sqrt{\frac{n_{k}}{r_{k}}}\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(k)}-\bm{U}^{(k)}_{\star}\big)\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\right\}\leq c\sqrt{\mu}\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star})

(72)

for some
0<c\leq 1
. Then for
k=1,2,3
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(k)}-\bm{U}^{(k)}_{\star}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq c\sqrt{\frac{\mu r_{k}}{n_{k}}},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{\mu r_{k}}{n_{k}}}.

(73)

Proof.

It follows that for all
k
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(k)}-\bm{U}^{(k)}_{\star}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)})}\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(k)}-\bm{U}^{(k)}_{\star}\big)\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq c\sqrt{\frac{\mu r_{k}}{n_{k}}},

where the second inequality follows from (
72
) and
\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star})\leq\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)})
. This completes the proof for the first part of (
73
). With this and the incoherence assumption
\left\lVert\bm{U}_{\star}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu r_{k}}{n_{k}}}
, after applying triangle inequality, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(k)}-\bm{U}^{(k)}_{\star}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}+\left\lVert\bm{U}_{\star}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{\mu r_{k}}{n_{k}}},

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
be
\mu
-incoherent with the Tucker decomposition
\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}=\big(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}
of rank
\bm{r}=(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
. In addition, let
\bm{F}:=(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}})
and
\bm{F}_{\star}:=(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)},\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star})
be aligned, where
\bm{\mathcal{X}}=\big(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}
. For
0<\epsilon_{0}<0.1
and
0<c\leq 1
, if

\displaystyle\dist(\bm{F},\bm{F}_{\star})

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{0}c\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}),

(74a)

\displaystyle\max_{k}\left\{\sqrt{\frac{n_{k}}{r_{k}}}\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(k)}-\bm{U}_{\star}^{(k)}\big)\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq c\sqrt{\mu}\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star})

(74b)

are satisfied, then

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{X}}-\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{3}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}}{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\left(8\epsilon_{0}+7\right)c\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star})\leq 8\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{3}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}}{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}c\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}).

Proof.

We can decompose
\bm{\mathcal{X}}-\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}
into

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{X}}-\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}

\displaystyle=\big(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot(\bm{\mathcal{G}}-\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star})+\big(\bm{U}^{(1)}-\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}

\displaystyle\qquad+\big(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)}-\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}+\big(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}-\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}.

(75)

Then by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{X}}-\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot(\bm{\mathcal{G}}-\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star})\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(1)}-\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}^{(2)}-\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)},\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)},\bm{U}^{(3)}-\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)}\big)\bcdot\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(1)}\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}-\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right)(\bm{U}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}^{(2)})^{\top}\right\rVert_{\infty}}_{=:\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{core}}}+\underbrace{\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(1)}-\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)}\big)\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right)(\bm{U}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}^{(2)})^{\top}\right\rVert_{\infty}}_{=:\mathfrak{A}_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(2)}-\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)}\big)\mathcal{M}_{2}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right)(\bm{U}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)})^{\top}\right\rVert_{\infty}}_{=:\mathfrak{A}_{2}}+\underbrace{\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(3)}-\bm{U}_{\star}^{(3)}\big)\mathcal{M}_{3}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right)(\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)}\otimes\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)})^{\top}\right\rVert_{\infty}}_{=:\mathfrak{A}_{3}},

where the second inequality follows from the invariance of
\ell_{\infty}
norm to matricizations.
We will bound each term separately.

•

For
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{core}}
, it follows from basic norm relations that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{core}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(1)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\left\lVert\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}-\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right)\right\rVert_{\op}\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(3)}\otimes\bm{U}^{(2)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(1)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(2)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(3)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{G}}-\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\fro}\leq 8\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{3}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}}{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\epsilon_{0}c\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}),

where the last inequality follows from
\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{G}}-\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right\rVert_{\fro}\leq\dist(\bm{F},\bm{F}_{\star})\leq\epsilon_{0}c\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star})
by assumption (
74a
) and Lemma
9
by assumption (
74b
).

•

Next, for
\mathfrak{A}_{1}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(1)}-\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)}\big)\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(1)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(2)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(3)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\left\lVert\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right)^{\top}\big(\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(1)}\big)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\op}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(1)}-\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)}\big)\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(1)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(2)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(3)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq 4\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{3}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}}{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}c\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}),

where the equality follows from
\left\lVert\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right)^{\top}\big(\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)}\big)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\op}=1
since
\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right)\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right)^{\top}=\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(k)}\right)^{2}
, and the last inequality follows from the assumption (
74b
) and Lemma
9
by assumption (
74b
).

•

Similarly, for
\mathfrak{A}_{2}
, it follows

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\big(\bm{U}^{(2)}-\bm{U}_{\star}^{(2)}\big)\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(2)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\left\lVert\bm{U}^{(3)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\left\lVert\bm{U}_{\star}^{(1)}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\left\lVert\mathcal{M}_{2}\left(\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\star}\right)^{\top}\big(\bm{\Sigma}_{\star}^{(2)}\big)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\op}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{3}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}}{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}c\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\mathcal{X}}_{\star}).

•

Finally, repeat the same approach for
\mathfrak{A}_{3}

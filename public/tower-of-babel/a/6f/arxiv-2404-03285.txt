Combined DL-UL Distributed Beamforming Design for Cell-Free Massive MIMO
Bikshapathi Gouda, Antti Arvola, Italo Atzeni, Antti Tölli

Abstract
We consider a cell-free massive multiple-input multiple-output system with multi-antenna access points (APs) and user equipments (UEs), where the UEs can be served in both the downlink (DL) and uplink (UL) within a resource block. We tackle the combined optimization of the DL precoders and combiners at the APs and DL UEs, respectively, together with the UL combiners and precoders at the APs and UL UEs, respectively. To this end, we propose distributed beamforming designs enabled by iterative bi-directional training (IBT) and based on the minimum mean squared error criterion. To reduce the IBT overhead and thus enhance the effective DL and UL rates, we carry out the distributed beamforming design by assuming that all the UEs are served solely in the DL and then utilize the obtained beamformers for the DL and UL data transmissions after proper scaling. Numerical results show the superiority of the proposed combined DL-UL distributed beamforming design over separate DL and UL designs, especially with short resource blocks.



I
Introduction

In cell-free massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems, multiple access points (APs) are connected to a central unit (CU) and jointly serve all the user equipments (UEs), eliminating the inter-cell interference and providing a uniform service across the network
[
1
,
2
]
. In this regard, network-wide beamforming designs based on mean squared error (MSE) minimization or zero forcing, which require global channel state information (CSI) of all the APs at the CU, outperform local beamforming designs based on maximum ratio transmission/combining, which require only local CSI at each AP
[
3
,
2
]
. However, optimizing the network-wide beamformers at the CU calls for extensive CSI exchange via backhaul links, which poses severe challenges in terms of delays, backhaul bandwidth, and scalability of the network.

In coordinated cellular systems with time-division duplexing (TDD), the beamformers for the multi-antenna APs and UEs can be locally designed using iterative bi-directional training (IBT) with pilot-aided uplink (UL) and downlink (DL) channel estimation
[
4
]
. Remarkably, IBT applied to cell-free massive MIMO systems enables fully distributed beamforming design at each AP by incorporating effective CSI from the other APs via over-the-air (OTA) signaling
[
5
,
6
]
. Typically, the beamforming design for UEs served in either the DL or UL requires separate IBT procedures. However, it was shown in
[
7
]
that using a common IBT procedure for the DL and UL beamforming designs may reduce the IBT overhead and thus enhance the effective DL and UL rates. Nonetheless,
[
7
]
was limited to a centralized design (i.e., at the CU) and assumed all the UEs to be served in both the DL and UL.

In this paper, we consider a cell-free massive MIMO system with multi-antenna APs and UEs, where the UEs can be served in both the DL and UL within a resource block. We optimize the DL precoders and combiners at the APs and DL UEs, respectively, together with the UL combiners and precoders at the APs and UL UEs, respectively. In contrast to prior works, we consider a general system model with partially overlapping DL and UL UEs, and propose fully distributed beamforming designs enabled by IBT. To reduce the IBT overhead, we carry out the distributed beamforming design by assuming that all the UEs are served solely in the DL. Then: for the DL data transmission, the DL precoders for the UEs served only in the UL are discarded and each AP’s transmit power is redistributed among the precoders for the DL UEs; for the UL data transmission, the DL combiners at the UL UEs are utilized as UL precoders after proper scaling to satisfy the per-UE transmit power constraints. To further reduce the number of precoders at each AP (and thus the IBT overhead), UEs served only in the DL can be paired with UEs served only in the UL, where each pair is assigned a common DL multicast precoder during the IBT. Numerical results demonstrate the superiority of the proposed combined DL-UL distributed beamforming designs over separate DL and UL designs, especially with short resource blocks.

II
System Model

We consider a cell-free massive MIMO system operating in TDD mode with channel reciprocity, where a set of APs
\mathcal{B}
, each equipped with
M
antennas, serves a set of UEs
\mathcal{K}
, each equipped with
N
antennas. Let
\mathbf{H}_{b,k}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{M\times N}
denote the UL channel matrix between UE
k\in\mathcal{K}
and AP
b\in\mathcal{B}
. We assume that both DL and UL data transmissions take place within a resource block. In this context, let
\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}
and
\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}
be the sets of DL and UL UEs, respectively, with
\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}\cup\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}=\mathcal{K}
. The UEs served in both the DL and UL are referred to as
DL-UL
UEs and are included in the set
\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}\textnormal{-}\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}\triangleq\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}\cap\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}
. Likewise, the UEs served only in the DL (resp. UL) are referred to as
DL-only
(resp.
UL-only
) UEs and are included in the set
\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}\textnormal{-only}}\triangleq\mathcal{K}\setminus\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}
(resp.
\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}\textnormal{-only}}\triangleq\mathcal{K}\setminus\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}
). Without loss of generality, we assume a single DL and/or UL data stream per UE. An example of the system model is illustrated in Fig.
1
.

DL data transmission.
Let
\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,k}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{M\times 1}
be the DL precoder used by AP
b
for UE
k\in\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}
. The received signal at UE
k
is

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\triangleq\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\sum_{\bar{k}\in\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}}\mathbf{H}_{b,k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,\bar{k}}d^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{\bar{k}}+\mathbf{z}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times 1},

(1)

where
d^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\sim\mathcal{C}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
is the DL data symbol for UE
k
and
\mathbf{z}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\sim\mathcal{C}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma_{\textnormal{\tiny{UE}}}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N})
is the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) at UE
k
. Subsequently, UE
k
uses its DL combiner
\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times 1}
to obtain a soft estimate of
{d}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}
, i.e.,
\hat{d}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}=(\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k})^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{y}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}
. The corresponding DL signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) is given by

\displaystyle\gamma^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\triangleq\frac{|(\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k})^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{f}_{k,k}|^{2}}{\sum_{\bar{k}\in\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}\setminus\{k\}}|(\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k})^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{f}_{k,\bar{k}}|^{2}+\sigma_{\textnormal{\tiny{UE}}}^{2}\|\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\|^{2}},

(2)

with
\mathbf{f}_{k,\bar{k}}\triangleq\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\mathbf{H}_{b,k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,\bar{k}}
.

UL data transmission.
Let
\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{k}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times 1}
be the UL precoder used by UE
k\in\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}
. The received signal at AP
b
is

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{b}\triangleq\sum_{\bar{k}\in\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}}\mathbf{H}_{b,\bar{k}}\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{\bar{k}}d^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{\bar{k}}+\mathbf{z}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{b}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{M\times 1},

(3)

where
d^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{k}\sim\mathcal{C}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
is the UL data symbol of UE
k
and
\mathbf{z}_{b}\sim\mathcal{C}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma_{\textnormal{\tiny{AP}}}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M})
is the AWGN at AP
b
. Subsequently, each AP
b
uses its UL combiner
\mathbf{w}_{b,k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{M\times 1}
for UE
k
and the APs collectively obtain a soft estimate of
{d}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{k}
, i.e.,
\hat{d}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{k}=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{b,k})^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{y}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{b}
. The corresponding UL SINR is given by

\displaystyle\gamma^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{k}\triangleq\frac{|\mathbf{g}_{k,k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{k}|^{2}}{\sum_{\bar{k}\in\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}\setminus\{k\}}|\mathbf{g}_{\bar{k},k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{\bar{k}}|^{2}+\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\sigma_{\textnormal{\tiny{AP}}}^{2}\|\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{b,k}\|^{2}}.

(4)

with
\mathbf{g}_{\bar{k},k}\triangleq\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\mathbf{H}_{b,\bar{k}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}_{b,k}
.

Finally, the DL and UL sum rates (measured in bps/Hz) are given by
R^{\mathrm{x}}\triangleq\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{x}}}\log_{2}(1+\gamma^{\mathrm{x}}_{k})
, with
\mathrm{x}\in\{\textnormal{DL},\textnormal{UL}\}
. These rates represent upper bounds on the system performance (see
[
5
,
8
]
) and are used as main performance metrics in Section
V
. Next, we present the proposed combined DL-UL distributed beamforming designs with perfect CSI and IBT in Sections
III
and
IV
, respectively.

III
Beamforming Design with Perfect CSI

The proposed combined DL-UL distributed beamforming designs are based on the following principles:

1)
For each DL-UL UE, each AP adopts the same beamformer (up to a scaling) as precoder for the DL data transmission and as combiner for the UL data transmission. Similarly, each DL-UL UE adopts the same beamformer (up to a scaling) as combiner for the DL data transmission and as precoder for the UL data transmission. This approach exploits the structural similarity between the DL and UL beamformers resulting from separate DL and UL designs
[
7
]
.

2)
Adopting the same beamformers (up to a scaling) for the DL and UL data transmissions enables using a single IBT procedure for all the UEs. This leads to a reduced IBT overhead compared with separate DL and UL designs at the cost of extra interference due to the DL-only or UL-only UEs.

To obtain a fully distributed beamforming design at the APs, we consider the minimization of the sum MSE, which provides some in-built fairness among the UEs (and can be further tuned by means of UE-specific weights)
[
8
]
. In the rest of this section, we present the proposed DL-UL distributed beamforming design assuming perfect global CSI. The case with imperfect CSI obtained via IBT is described in Section
IV
.

III-A

UE-Specific Beamforming Design

Assuming that all the UEs are served solely in the DL, the precoders
\{\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,k}\}
at the APs and the combiners
\{\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\}
at the UEs are obtained by solving

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cl}\underset{{\{\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,k},\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\}}}{\mathrm{minimize}}&\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathrm{MSE}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}&\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\|\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,k}\|^{2}\leq\rho_{\textnormal{\tiny{AP}}},\end{array}

where
\rho_{\textnormal{\tiny{AP}}}
is the maximum transmit power of each AP and
\mathrm{MSE}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\triangleq\mathbb{E}\big[|\hat{d}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}-d^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}|^{2}\big]
. Since the objective is convex in
\{\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,k}\}
for fixed
\{\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\}
and vice versa, we use alternating optimization to solve (
III-A
) (as in
[
5
]
).

Optimization of
\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}
.
For fixed
\{\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,k}\}
, the optimal
\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}
is computed by setting
\nabla_{\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}}\big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathrm{MSE}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}\big)=0
, which yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}=\bigg(\sum_{\bar{k}\in\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}\textnormal{-}\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}\cup{\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}\textnormal{-only}}}\cup\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}\textnormal{-only}}}\mathbf{f}_{k,\bar{k}}\mathbf{f}_{k,\bar{k}}^{\mathrm{H}}+\sigma_{\textnormal{\tiny{UE}}}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N}\bigg)^{-1}\mathbf{f}_{k,k}.

(7)

Optimization of
\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,k}
.
For fixed
\{\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\}
, the optimal
\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,k}
is computed by setting
\nabla_{\mathbf{w}_{b,k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}}\big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathrm{MSE}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}\big)=0
, which yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,k}=(\mathbf{\Phi}_{bb}+\lambda_{b}\mathbf{I}_{M})^{-1}(\mathbf{h}_{b,k}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{b,k}),

(8)

where
\mathbf{\Phi}_{b\bar{b}}\triangleq\sum_{\bar{k}\in\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}\textnormal{-}\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}\cup\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}\textnormal{-only}}\cup\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}\textnormal{-only}}}\mathbf{h}_{b,\bar{k}}\mathbf{h}_{\bar{b},\bar{k}}^{\mathrm{H}}
,

\lambda_{b}
is the dual variable corresponding to AP
b
’s transmit power constraint,
\mathbf{h}_{b,k}\triangleq\mathbf{H}_{b,k}\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}
, and
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{b,k}\triangleq\sum_{\bar{b}\in\mathcal{B}\setminus\{b\}}\mathbf{\Phi}_{b\bar{b}}\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{\bar{b},k}
. Furthermore, to ensure the global convergence of (
III-A
) with respect to
\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,k}
, each AP updates
\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,k}
in (
8
) via a best-response (BR) method, while the term
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{b,k}
needs to be acquired from the other APs as detailed in
[
5
]
.

Incorporating all the UEs into the DL beamforming design facilitates the reuse of the DL beamformers for the UL data transmission. Specifically, for a given
k\in\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}
, (
7
) can be reused as UL precoder at the UE and (
8
) can be reused as UL combiner at AP
b
. However, by this approach, the APs unnecessarily aim to cancel interference due to the UL-only UEs in the DL and the DL-only UEs in the UL (reflected by the summations over
\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}\textnormal{-only}}
and
\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}\textnormal{-only}}
, respectively, in
\mathbf{\Phi}_{b\bar{b}}
). Likewise, the UEs unnecessarily aim to cancel the effective interference due to the UL-only UE beamformers in the DL and the DL-only UE beamformers in the UL (reflected by the summations over
\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}\textnormal{-only}}
and
\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}\textnormal{-only}}
, respectively, in (
7
)).

The DL beamformers in (
8
) and (
7
) can be respectively enhanced for the DL data transmission and reused for the UL data transmissions to satisfy the transmit power constraints at the APs and UEs. For the DL data transmission, (
8
) corresponding to UE
k\in\mathcal{K}^{\text{DL}}
is scaled to meet the AP transmit power constraint after discarding the UL-only UEs, i.e.,
a_{b}\mathbf{w}^{\text{DL}}_{b,k}
, with
a_{b}\triangleq{\rho_{\text{BS}}}/\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{\text{DL}}}\|\mathbf{w}^{\text{DL}}_{b,k}\|^{2}
. For the UL data transmission, (
7
) corresponding to UE
k\in\mathcal{K}^{\text{UL}}
is reused as UL precoder and scaled to meet the UE transmit power constraint, i.e.,
\mathbf{v}^{\text{UL}}_{k}=\sqrt{\rho_{\text{UE}}}{\mathbf{v}^{\text{DL}}_{k}}/{\|\mathbf{v}^{\text{DL}}_{k}\|}
, where
\rho_{\text{UE}}
is the maximum transmit power of each UE.

III-B

Beamforming Design with UE Pairing

To further reduce the number of precoders at each AP (and thus the IBT overhead), DL-only UEs can be paired with UL-only UEs and each pair is assigned a common DL multicast precoder during the IBT. Let
\mathcal{G}\triangleq\big\{1,\ldots,\max(|\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}|,|\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}|)\big\}
and let
P_{g}\triangleq(a_{g},b_{g})
denote an ordered pair of DL and UL UEs, with
g\in\mathcal{G}
,
a_{g}\in\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}
, and
b_{g}\in\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}
. In this setting, we have
a_{g}=b_{g}=k
if
k\in\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}\textnormal{-}\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}
or
a_{g}=k\neq\bar{k}=b_{g}
if
k\in\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}\textnormal{-only}}
and
\bar{k}\in\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}\textnormal{-only}}
. Moreover, when
|\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}|\neq|\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}|
, the remaining DL-only or UL-only UEs are paired with a phantom UE indicated by the index
0
. In the example of system model in Fig.
1
, we have
P_{1}=(1,1)
,
P_{2}=(2,4)
,
P_{3}=(3,3)
, and
P_{4}=(5,0)
. By this approach, the precoders
\{\mathbf{w}_{b,g}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}\}
at the APs and the combiners
\{\mathbf{v}_{k}\}
at the UEs are obtained by solving the following virtual multicast problem:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cl}\underset{{\{\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,g},\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\}}}{\mathrm{minimize}}&\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathrm{G\textnormal{-}MSE}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}&\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\|\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,g}\|^{2}\leq\rho_{\textnormal{\tiny{AP}}},\end{array}

with
\mathrm{G\textnormal{-}MSE}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\triangleq\mathbb{E}\big[|(\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k})^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{y}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL-grp}}}_{k}-d^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{g_{k}}|^{2}\big]
, where
g_{k}
represents the index of the pair containing UE
k
and
\mathbf{y}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL-grp}}}_{k}\triangleq\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\sum_{\bar{g}\in\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{H}_{b,k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,\bar{g}}d^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{\bar{g}}+\mathbf{z}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}
. Similar to (
III-A
), the objective in (
III-B
) is convex in
\{\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,g}\}
for fixed
\{\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\}
and vice versa, so we use alternating optimization to solve (
III-B
) (as in
[
8
]
).

Optimization of
\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}
.
For fixed
\{\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,g}\}
, the optimal
\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}
is computed as
\nabla_{\mathbf{v}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}}\big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathrm{G\textnormal{-}MSE}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}\big)=0
, which yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\!=\!\bigg(\sum_{\bar{g}\in\mathcal{G}}(\mathbbm{1}_{\bar{g}}\mathbf{m}_{k,\bar{g}}\mathbf{m}_{k,\bar{g}}^{\mathrm{H}}+\underbrace{\bar{\mathbbm{1}}_{\bar{g}}\mathbf{m}_{k,\bar{g}}\mathbf{m}_{k,\bar{g}}^{\mathrm{H}}}_{\textnormal{extra interference}})\!+\sigma_{\textnormal{\tiny{UE}}}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N}\bigg)^{\!-1}\!\mathbf{m}_{k,g},

(11)

with
\mathbf{m}_{k,g}\triangleq\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\mathbf{H}_{b,k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,g}
,
\bar{\mathbbm{1}}_{g}\triangleq 1-{\mathbbm{1}}_{g}
, and
\mathbbm{1}_{g}=0
if
\{P_{g}\}\cap\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}=\emptyset
or
\{P_{g}\}\cap\mathcal{K}^{\textnormal{\tiny{UL}}}=\emptyset
(otherwise
\mathbbm{1}_{g}=1
). This implies that extra interference exists in the DL (resp. UL) when a UL-only (resp. DL-only) UE is paired with a phantom UE.

Optimization of
\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,g}
.
To reduce the IBT overhead, we use the gradient method to obtain the optimal
\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,g}
for a fixed
\{\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\}
(as in
[
8
]
). Accordingly, let us define

\displaystyle\delta\mathbf{\epsilon}_{b,g}

\displaystyle\triangleq\nabla_{\mathbf{w}_{b,g}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}}\bigg(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathrm{G\textnormal{-}MSE}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}\bigg)

(12)

\displaystyle=-2\bigg(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\mathbf{h}_{b,k}-\mathbf{\varrho}_{b,g}-\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbf{h}_{b,k}\mathbf{h}_{b,k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}_{b,g}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}\bigg),

(13)

where
\mathbf{\varrho}_{b,g}\triangleq\sum_{\bar{b}\in\mathcal{B}\setminus\{b\}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbf{h}_{b,k}\mathbf{h}_{\bar{b},k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}_{\bar{b},g}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}
needs to be acquired from the other APs. Then, the corresponding gradient

update of
\mathbf{w}_{b,g}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}
at iteration
i
is

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{b,g}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}(i)}\triangleq\mathbf{w}_{b,g}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}(i-1)}-\alpha_{\textnormal{\tiny{GB}}}\delta\mathbf{\epsilon}_{b,g},

(14)

where
\alpha_{\textnormal{\tiny{GB}}}
is the step size. The precoders at AP
b
are then scaled to meet the per-AP transmit power constraints, i.e.,
{\mathbf{w}}_{b,g}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}(i)}=\bar{a}_{b}\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{b,g}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}(i)}
, such that
\bar{a}_{b}=\rho_{\textnormal{\tiny{AP}}}/\|\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{b,g}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}(i)}\|^{2}
. While this distributed beamforming design offers the advantage of reducing the IBT overhead and considering interference only from other UE pairs, it also incurs a beamforming loss due to unnecessary multicasting in the presence of UE pairs with DL-only and UL-only UEs. Finally,
\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}
in (
11
) and
\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,g}
in (
14
) are scaled for the DL and UL data transmissions as discussed in Section
III-A
.

IV
Beamforming Design with IBT

The distributed beamforming design described in Section
III-A
can be carried out at each AP and UE by means of IBT. At each IBT iteration, the DL precoders and combiners for all the UEs are updated at each AP and UE, respectively, via precoded pilots.

IV-A

UE-Specific Beamforming Design

Let
\mathbf{p}_{k}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{\tau\times 1}
be the pilot assigned to UE
k
, such that
\mathbf{p}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{p}_{\bar{k}}=\tau
if
k=\bar{k}
and
\mathbf{p}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{p}_{\bar{k}}=0
otherwise.

Computation of
\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}
.
To enable the computation of
\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}
in (
7
) at each UE, each AP
b
transmits the precoded DL pilots
\mathbf{X}_{b}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}\triangleq\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,k}\mathbf{p}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}
. The received signal at UE
k
from all the APs is

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}\triangleq\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\sum_{\bar{k}\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbf{H}_{b,k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,\bar{k}}\mathbf{p}_{\bar{k}}^{\mathrm{H}}+\mathbf{Z}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times\tau},

(15)

where
\mathbf{Z}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}
is the AWGN at UE
k
. Then, UE
k
computes
\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}\simeq\big(\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}(\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}})^{\mathrm{H}}\big)^{\dagger}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}\mathbf{p}_{k},

(16)

where
(\cdot)^{\dagger}
is the pseudoinverse operator. Note that the above
\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{k}
converges to (
7
) as
\tau\rightarrow\infty
.

Computation of
\mathbf{w}^{\textnormal{\tiny{DL}}}_{b,k}
.
To enable the computation of

IV-C

Implementation Details

We adopt a strategy involving a single update of the combiners at all the UEs using a DL signal, and a single update of the precoders at each AP using the UL-1 and UL-2 signals per resource block (the DL signals and the UL-1 and UL-2 signals can be co-located with the DL and UL data, respectively).

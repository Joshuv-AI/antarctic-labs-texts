Almost synchronous quantum correlations
Thomas Vidick

Abstract
The study of quantum correlation sets initiated by Tsirelson in the 1980s and originally motivated by questions in the foundations of quantum mechanics has more recently been tied to questions in quantum cryptography, complexity theory, operator space theory, group theory, and more. Synchronous correlation sets introduced in [Paulsen et. al, JFA 2016] are a subclass of correlations that has proven particularly useful to study and arises naturally in applications. We show that any correlation that is almost synchronous, in a natural ℓ 1 \ell_{1} sense, arises from a state and measurement operators that are well-approximated by a convex combination of projective measurements on a maximally entangled state. This extends a result of [Paulsen et. al, JFA 2016] which applies to exactly synchronous correlations. Crucially, the quality of approximation is independent of the dimension of the Hilbert spaces or of the size of the correlation. Our result allows one to reduce the analysis of many classes of nonlocal games, including rigidity properties, to the case of strategies using maximally entangled states which are generally easier to manipulate.

Introduction

For finite sets
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
a
quantum correlation
is an element of the set

\displaystyle C_{q}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\;=\;

\displaystyle\{\big(\langle\psi|A^{x}_{a}\otimes B^{y}_{b}|\psi\rangle\big)_{xyab}\,:\;|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\;,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{X},\,y\in\mathcal{Y},\;\{A^{x}_{a}\}_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\text{ POVM on }\mathcal{H}_{A},\;\{B^{y}_{b}\}_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\text{ POVM on }\mathcal{H}_{B}\big\}\;,

where
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
range over all finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and a POVM (positive operator-valued measure) on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a collection of positive semidefinite operators on
\mathcal{H}
that sum to identity. For each
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
the set
C_{q}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is convex, as can be seen by taking direct sums, but there are
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
such that it is not closed
[
1
]
. We write
C_{q}
for the union of
C_{q}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
over all finite
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
.

A
strategy
is a tuple
\mathscr{S}=(|\psi\rangle,A,B)
such that
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
is a state (i.e. a unit vector),
A=\{A^{x}_{a}\}
a collection of POVM on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
B=\{B^{y}_{b}\}
a collection of POVM on
\mathcal{H}_{B}
. (The finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
as well as the index sets
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are generally left implicit in the notation.) Given a strategy
\mathscr{S}
we say that
\mathscr{S}

induces
the correlation
(C_{x,y,a,b}=\langle\psi|A^{x}_{a}\otimes B^{y}_{b}|\psi\rangle)_{xyab}
.

The study of the set of quantum correlations
C_{q}
and its relation to the set of classical correlations
C
, defined as the convex hull of those correlations that can be induced using a state
|\psi\rangle
that is a tensor product

|\psi\rangle=|\psi_{A}\rangle\otimes|\psi_{B}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
,
is of importance in the foundations of quantum mechanics. The fact that
C\subsetneq C_{q}
, as first shown by Bell
[
2
]
and often termed “quantum nonlocality,” underlies the field of device-independent quantum cryptography and gives rise to the study of entanglement witnesses, protocols for delegated quantum computation, and questions in quantum complexity theory; we refer to
[
3
]
for references. Following the foundational work of Tsirelson
[
4
]
multiple variants of the set of quantum correlations have been introduced and their study is connected to a range of problems in mathematics including operator space theory
[
5
,
6
]
, group theory
[
7
]
and combinatorics
[
8
]
.

In this paper we consider a subset of
C_{q}
introduced in
[
9
]
and called the
synchronous set

C_{q}^{s}
. It is defined as the union of all
C_{q}^{s}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A})
where
C_{q}^{s}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A})
is the subset of
C_{q}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A})
that contains all those correlations
C
which satisfy
C_{x,x,a,b}=0
whenever
a\neq b
. This set arises naturally in the study of certain classes of nonlocal games. In general a nonlocal game
\mathfrak{G}
is specified by a distribution
\nu
on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
and a function
D:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}\to\{0,1\}
. A nonlocal game gives rise to a linear function on
C_{q}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
through the quantity

\omega_{q}(\mathfrak{G};C)\,=\,\sum_{x,y}\,\nu(x,y)\,\sum_{a,b}\,D(x,y,a,b)C_{x,y,a,b}\;.

Given a game
\mathfrak{G}
one is interested in its
quantum value

\omega_{q}(\mathfrak{G})
, which is defined as the supremum over all
C\in C_{q}
of
\omega_{q}(\mathfrak{G};C)
. A game
\mathfrak{G}
such that
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{Y}
,
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}
,
\nu(x,x)>0
for all
x
and
D(a,b|x,x)=0
for all
x
and
a\neq b
is called a
synchronous game
. Any such game has the property that
\omega_{q}(\mathfrak{G};C)=1
can only be obtained by a
C\in C_{q}^{s}
. Synchronous games arise naturally in applications; see e.g. the classes of graph homomorphism games
[
10
]
or linear system games
[
11
]
. (Linear system games are projection games, which can be turned into synchronous games by taking their “square”; see
[
12
]
.)
The set
C_{q}^{s}
retains most of the interesting geometric aspects of
C_{q}
, and in particular it is convex and non-closed
[
12
]
.

A key property of synchronous correlations that makes them more amenable to study is the following fact shown in
[
9
]
. For every synchronous correlation
C
there is a family of strategies
\{\mathscr{S}^{\lambda}=(|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle,A^{\lambda},B^{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
and a measure
\mu
on
\Lambda
such that for each
\lambda
,
|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle=|\psi_{me}(d_{\lambda})\rangle
with

|\psi_{me}(d_{\lambda})\rangle\,=\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{\lambda}}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\lambda}}\,|u_{i}\rangle|u_{i}\rangle\,\in\,\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\;,

(1)

where
d_{\lambda}=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})
and
\{|u_{i}\rangle:\,1\leq i\leq d_{\lambda}\}
is an orthonormal family in
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
, each measurement
\{A^{\lambda,x}_{a}\}
and
\{B^{\lambda,y}_{b}\}
consists entirely of projections, and moreover for all
x,y,a,b
we have

C_{x,y,a,b}\,=\,\int_{\lambda}\langle\psi_{\lambda}|A^{\lambda,x}_{a}\otimes B^{\lambda,y}_{b}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\,d\mu(\lambda)\;.

(2)

When
|\psi_{me}(d_{\lambda})\rangle
takes the form in (
1
)
we can express

\langle\psi_{\lambda}|A^{\lambda,x}_{a}\otimes B^{\lambda,y}_{b}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\,=\,\frac{1}{d_{\lambda}}\,\mbox{\rm Tr}\big({A}^{\lambda,x}_{a}({B}^{\lambda,y}_{b})^{T}\big)\;,

(3)

where
\mbox{\rm Tr}(\cdot)
is the usual matrix trace and
X^{T}
denotes the transpose with respect to the basis
\{|u_{i}\rangle\}
. The fact that synchronous correlations are “tracial” in the sense given by (
2
) and (
3
) contributes largely to their appeal.
In contrast there are correlations
C\in C_{q}
such that
C
cannot be induced, even approximately, using a convex combination of strategies using states of the form (
1
) in any dimension; see
[
13
]
for an example. Such correlations tend to be more difficult to study and their main interest lies in their existence, e.g. they can provide entanglement witnesses for states that are not maximally entangled.

Our results.

We consider strategies
\mathscr{S}=(|\psi\rangle,A,B)
that are
almost
synchronous, where the default to synchronicity is measured by the quantity

\delta_{\textrm{sync}}(C;\nu)\,=\,\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\nu}\,\sum_{a\neq b}\,C_{x,x,a,b}\;,

(4)

where
\nu
is some distribution on
\mathcal{X}
. This averaged
\ell_{1}
distance is motivated by applications to nonlocal games, which we describe below.
Informally our main result is that any strategy
\mathscr{S}
that induces a correlation
C
is well-approximated by a convex combination of strategies
\mathscr{S}^{\lambda}
each using a maximally entangled state, where the approximation is controlled by
\delta_{\textrm{sync}}(C;\nu)
for any
\nu
(
\nu
also enters in the measure of approximation between
\mathscr{S}
and the
\mathscr{S}^{\lambda}
) and, crucially for applications, does not depend on the dimension of
|\psi\rangle
or the size of the sets
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{A}
. In particular each
\mathscr{S}^{\lambda}
gives rise to a synchronous correlation
C^{\lambda}
such that
\int_{\lambda}C^{\lambda}\approx C
in a suitable
\ell_{1}
sense. Moreover, and crucially for the applications that we describe next, specific structural properties of the
\mathscr{S}^{\lambda}
, such as algebraic relations between some of the measurement operators, can be transferred to the strategy
\mathscr{S}
. A simplified version of our theorem specialized to the case of a single measurement can be stated as follows.

Theorem
.

There are universal constants
c,C>0
such that the following holds. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
a state. Then there is a finite set
\Lambda
, a distribution
\mu
on
\Lambda
, and for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
a state
|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle
that is maximally entangled on a subspace
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
such that letting
\rho
be the reduced density of
|\psi\rangle
on the first factor and
\rho_{\lambda}
the reduced density of
|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle
on
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
,

\rho\,=\,\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{\lambda\sim\mu}\big[\,\rho_{\lambda}\,\big]\;.

(5)

Moreover, let
\mathcal{A}
be a finite set and
\{A_{a}\}_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
an arbitrary measurement on
\mathcal{H}
. Then there is
a projective measurement
\{A^{\lambda}_{a}\}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
such that

\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{\lambda\sim\mu}\Big[\sum_{a}\big\|\big(A_{a}-A^{\lambda}_{a}\big)\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big\|^{2}\,\Big]\,\leq\,C\,\Big(1-\sum_{a}\langle\psi|A_{a}\otimes A_{a}|\psi\rangle\Big)^{c}\;.

(6)

For the complete statement and additional remarks, see Theorem
3.1
. The first part of the theorem, (
5
), is very simple to obtain; it is the second part that is meaningful. In particular, since
|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle
is a maximally entangled state the approximation on the left-hand side can be seen as a form of weighted approximation over certain (overlapping) diagonal blocks of
A
.
The fact that the spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
and the states
|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle
depend on
|\psi\rangle
only allows us to apply the theorem repeatedly for different measurements in order to decompose an arbitrary strategy as a convex combination of projective maximally entangled strategies, with the right-hand side in (
6
) replaced by
C\delta_{\textrm{sync}}(C;\nu)^{c}
for a
\nu
of one’s choice (which naturally will also appear on the left-hand side).

A consequence of the theorem is that any
C\in\overline{C_{q}}
which is also synchronous can be approximated by elements of
C_{q}^{s}
; this is because any sequence of approximations to
C
taken from
C_{q}
must, by definition, be almost synchronous and so Theorem
3.1
can be applied. (For this observation it is crucial that the approximation provided in Theorem
3.1
does not depend on the dimension of the Hilbert spaces; however, it could depend on the size of
C
.) This particular application was already shown in
[
12
, Theorem 3.6]
.

Our result and its formulation are motivated by the study of nonlocal games.
For a strategy
\mathscr{S}
we write
\omega_{q}(\mathfrak{G};\mathscr{S})
for
\omega_{q}(\mathfrak{G};C)
where
C
is the correlation induced by
\mathscr{S}
. Recall that the game value
\omega_{q}(\mathfrak{G})
is the supremum over all strategies of
\omega_{q}(\mathfrak{G};\mathscr{S})
. The fact that the supremum is taken over
C_{q}
and not
C_{q}^{s}
is motivated by applications to entanglement tests, cryptography, and complexity theory, as in those contexts there is no a priori reason to enforce hard constraints of the form
C_{x,x,a,b}=0
; indeed, such a constraint cannot be verified with absolute confidence in any statistical test.

Given a game and a strategy
\mathscr{S}
for it it is possible to obtain statistical confidence that
\omega_{q}(\mathfrak{G};\mathscr{S})\geq\omega_{q}(\mathfrak{G})-\varepsilon
for finite
\varepsilon>0
by playing the game many times. For this reason the characterization of nearly-optimal strategies plays a central role in applications of nonlocality. Recall that a synchronous game has the property that
D(x,x,a,b)=0
whenever
a\neq b
. Given a synchronous game
\mathfrak{G}
such that furthermore
\omega_{q}(\mathfrak{G})=1
it follows that any strategy
\mathscr{S}
for
\mathfrak{G}
such that
\omega_{q}(\mathfrak{G};\mathscr{S})\geq\omega_{q}(\mathfrak{G})-\varepsilon
must satisfy
\delta_{\textrm{sync}}(\mathscr{S};\nu_{diag})=O(\varepsilon)
, where
\nu_{diag}(x)=\nu(x,x)/(\sum_{x^{\prime}}\nu(x^{\prime},x^{\prime}))
and the constant implicit in the
O(\cdot)
notation will in general depend on the weight that
\nu
places on the diagonal. (In particular a better bound on
\delta_{sync}
will be obtained in cases when the distribution
\nu
is
not
a uniform distribution, as the uniform distribution places weight
\approx\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|}
on the diagonal, which can be quite small.) Thus nearly-optimal strategies in synchronous games give rise to almost synchronous correlations. This conclusion may also hold for games that are not necessarily synchronous, for example because the sets
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are disjoint; an example is the class of projection games that we consider in Section
4.2
. Examples of projection games include linear system games
[
11
]
and games such as the low-degree test
[
14
]
that play an important role in complexity theory.

Given the importance of studying nearly-optimal strategies, the fact that for many games any nearly-optimal strategy is almost synchronous ought to be useful. Our work allows one to reduce the analysis of almost synchronous strategies to that of exactly synchronous strategies in a broad variety of settings. The most direct application of our results is to the study of the phenomenon of rigidity, which seeks to extract necessary conditions of any strategy that is nearly-optimal for a certain game. Informally, our results imply that a general rigidity result for a synchronous game can be obtained in an automatic manner from a rigidity result that applies only to perfectly synchronous strategies. In order for the implication to not lose factors depending on the size of the game in the approximation quality for the rigidity statement it is sufficient that a high success probability in the game implies a low
\delta_{\textrm{sync}}(\mathscr{S};\nu)
for
\nu
the marginal distribution on either player’s questions in the game; see Corollary
4.1
and the remarks following it for further discussion.
To give just one example, the entire analysis carried out in the recent
[
15
]
could be simplified by making all calculations with the maximally entangled state only, making manipulations of the “state-dependent distance” far easier to carry out. We refer Section
4.1
for a precise formulation of how our main result can be used in this context as well as another application, to showing algebraic relations between measurement operators.

Discussion.

Given an almost synchronous strategy
\mathscr{S}=(|\psi\rangle,A,B)
it is not hard to show that the state and operators that underlie the strategy behave in an “approximately” cyclic manner, e.g. letting
\rho_{A}
denote the reduced density of
|\psi\rangle
on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
it holds that
\|A^{x}_{a}\rho_{A}-\rho_{A}A^{x}_{a}\|_{1}\approx 0
for all
x,a
where
\|\cdot\|_{1}
denotes the Schatten-
1
norm; see e.g.
[
16
, Lemma 3.7]
for a precise statement. The strength of our result lies in showing that such relations imply an approximate decomposition in terms of maximally entangled strategies, where crucially the approximation quality does not depend on the dimension of the Hilbert space nor on the size of the sets
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{A}
or
\mathcal{B}
. A similar decomposition implicitly appears in
[
7
]
, where it is used to reduce the analysis of nearly-optimal strategies for a specific linear system game to the case of maximally entangled strategies; in the context of that paper the reduction is motivated by a connection with the study of approximate representations of a certain finitely presented group. The main technical ingredient that enables the reduction in
[
7
]
is also the main ingredient in the present paper, which can be seen as a direct generalization of the work done there. Informally the key idea is to write any density matrix
\rho
as a convex combination of projections
\chi_{\geq\sqrt{\lambda}}(\rho)
, where
\lambda
is any non-negative real and
\chi_{\geq\sqrt{\lambda}}
is the indicator of the interval
[\sqrt{\lambda},+\infty)
; see Lemma
2.11
. The main additional observation needed is a calculation which originally appears in
[
17
]
and is restated as Lemma
2.12
below; informally, the calculation allows to transfer approximate commutation conditions such as those obtained in
[
16
, Lemma 3.7]
for any almost synchronous strategy to the same conditions, evaluated on the matrix
\chi_{\geq\sqrt{\lambda}}(\rho)
. The latter is a scaled multiple of the identity and is thus directly related to a maximally entangled state.

Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

We use
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
to denote finite sets.
We use
\mathcal{H}
to denote a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, which we generally endow with a canonical orthonormal basis
\{|i\rangle|\,i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\}
with
d=\dim(\mathcal{H})
. We use
\|\cdot\|
to denote the operator norm (largest singular value) on
\mathcal{H}
.
\mbox{\rm Tr}(\cdot)
is the trace on
\mathcal{H}
and
\|\cdot\|_{F}
the Frobenius norm
\|X\|_{F}=\mbox{\rm Tr}(X^{\dagger}X)^{1/2}
for any operator
X
on
\mathcal{H}
, where
X^{\dagger}
is the conjugate-transpose.
A positive operator-valued measure (POVM), or
measurement
for short, on
\mathcal{H}
is a finite collection of positive semidefinite operators
\{A_{a}\}_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
such that
\sum_{a}A_{a}=\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits
. A measurement
\{A_{a}\}
is
projective
if each
A_{a}
is a projection.

We use
\poly(\delta)
to denote any real-valued function
f
such that there exists constants
C,c>0
with
|f(\delta)|\leq C\delta^{c}
for all non-negative real
\delta
. The precise function
f
as well as the constants
c,C
may differ each time the notation is used. For a distribution
\nu
on a finite set
\mathcal{X}
we write
\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\nu}
for the expectation with respect to
x
with distribution
\nu
.

2.2
Strategies, correlations and games

Definition 2.1

(Strategies and correlations)
.

A
strategy

\mathscr{S}
is a tuple
(|\psi\rangle,A,B)
where
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{\textsf{A}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\textsf{B}}
is a quantum state and
A=\{A^{x}_{a}\}
(resp.
B=\{B^{y}_{b}\}
) is a collection of measurements on
\mathcal{H}
indexed by
x\in\mathcal{X}
and with outcomes
a\in\mathcal{A}
(resp.
y\in\mathcal{Y}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}
). Any strategy induces a
correlation
, which is the collection of real numbers

C_{xyab}\,=\,\langle\psi|A^{x}_{a}\otimes B^{y}_{b}|\psi\rangle\;,\qquad\forall(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\;,\quad\forall(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}\;.

The set of all correlations that arise from strategies of this form is denoted

C_{q}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{X}||\mathcal{Y}||\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|}\;.

Definition 2.2

(Synchronous correlations)
.

For finite sets
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{A}
a correlation
C=(C_{x,y,a,b})\in C_{q}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A})
is called
synchronous
if
C_{x,x,a,b}=0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
such that
a\neq b
. Given a distribution
\nu
on
\mathcal{X}
recall the definition of
\delta_{\textrm{sync}}(C;\nu)
in (
4
).
Given a strategy
\mathscr{S}
we also write
\delta_{\textrm{sync}}(\mathscr{S};\nu)
for
\delta_{\textrm{sync}}(C;\nu)
where
C
is the correlation induced by
\mathscr{S}
.

Definition 2.3

(PME strategies)
.

A strategy
\mathcal{S}=(|\psi\rangle,A,B)
is
symmetric
if
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{Y}
,
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}
,
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
takes the form

|\psi\rangle\,=\,\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|u_{i}\rangle|u_{i}\rangle\,\in\,\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}\;,

(7)

where the
\lambda_{i}
are non-negative and
\{|u_{i}\rangle\}
orthonormal, and for every
x,a
,
A^{x}_{a}=(B^{x}_{a})^{T}
with the transpose being taken with respect to the
\{|u_{i}\rangle\}
. Note that this implies that
\mathcal{H}_{\textsf{A}}=\mathcal{H}_{\textsf{B}}
and that
|\psi\rangle
has the same reduced density on either subsystem. For a symmetric strategy we write it as
\mathscr{S}=(|\psi\rangle,A)
. A strategy is
projective
if all
A^{x}_{a}
and
B^{y}_{b}
are projections. It is
maximally entangled
if
|\psi\rangle
is a maximally entangled state (
1
) on
\mathcal{H}_{\textsf{A}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\textsf{B}}
. We use the acronym “PME” to denote “symmetric projective maximally entangled”.

Observe that a correlation defined from a PME strategy is synchronous. (A converse to this statement is shown in
[
9
]
, i.e. every synchronous strategy arises from (a convex combination of) PME strategies.) To see this, first recall
Ando’s formula
: for any
X,Y
and
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
of the form (
7
) with reduced density
\rho
it holds that

\langle\psi|X\otimes Y|\psi\rangle\,=\,\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(X\rho^{1/2}Y^{T}\rho^{1/2}\big)\;,

(8)

where the transpose is taken with respect to the basis
\{|u_{i}\rangle\}
as in (
7
). Now for a PME strategy
\mathscr{S}=(|\psi\rangle,A)
and any
\nu
write

\displaystyle\delta_{\textrm{sync}}(\mathscr{S},\nu)

\displaystyle=1-\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\nu}\sum_{a}\,\langle\psi|A^{x}_{a}\otimes(A^{x}_{a})^{T}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=1-\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\nu}\sum_{a}\,\langle\psi|(A^{x}_{a})^{2}\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=1-\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\nu}\sum_{a}\,\langle\psi|A^{x}_{a}\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=0\;,

where the first equality is by definition, the second uses (
8
) together with the fact that for a PME strategy the reduced density of
|\psi\rangle
on either system is proportional to the identity, hence commutes with any operator, the third equality uses that all
A^{x}_{a}
are projections and the last that they sum to identity.

We reproduce a definition from
[
16
]
.

Definition 2.4

(Local
(\varepsilon,\nu)
-dilation)
.

Given
\varepsilon\geq 0
, a distribution
\nu
on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
and two strategies
\mathscr{S}=(|\psi\rangle,A,B)
and
\tilde{\mathscr{S}}=(|\tilde{\psi}\rangle,\tilde{A},\tilde{B})
we say that
\tilde{\mathscr{S}}
is a
local
(\varepsilon,\nu)
-dilation
of
\mathscr{S}
if there exists isometries
V_{A}:\mathcal{H}_{A}\to\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{A}
and
V_{B}:\mathcal{H}_{B}\to\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{B}
and a state
|\mathsf{aux}\rangle\in\mathcal{K}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{B}
such that

\displaystyle\big\|(V_{A}\otimes V_{B})|\psi\rangle-|\tilde{\psi}\rangle\otimes|\mathsf{aux}\rangle\big\|\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\varepsilon\;,

\displaystyle\Big(\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{(x,y)\sim\nu}\sum_{a,b}\big\|(V_{A}\otimes V_{B})\big(A^{x}_{a}\otimes B^{y}_{b}\big)|\psi\rangle-\big(\big(\tilde{A}^{x}_{a}\otimes\tilde{B}^{y}_{b}\big)|\tilde{\psi}\rangle\big)\otimes|\mathsf{aux}\rangle\big\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\varepsilon\;.

In
[
16
]

Consistency

We show elementary and generally well-known lemmata that will be useful in the proofs. The first lemma relates two different measures of state-dependent distance between measurements on
\mathcal{H}

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\gamma,\delta\geq 0
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
a state. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a finite set and for each
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
\{A^{x}_{a}\}_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
a projective measurement and
\{M^{x}_{a}\}_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
an arbitrary measurement on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\mu
be a distribution on
\mathcal{X}
. Let

\delta\,=\,1-\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\langle\psi|A^{x}_{a}\otimes(A^{x}_{a})^{T}|\psi\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma=1-\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\langle\psi|A^{x}_{a}\otimes(M^{x}_{a})^{T}|\psi\rangle\;.

(9)

Then

(\gamma-\delta)^{2}\,\leq\,\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\langle\psi|\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits\otimes\big(A^{x}_{a}-M^{x}_{a}\big)^{2}|\psi\rangle\,\leq\,2\,\gamma+2\sqrt{2\delta}\;.

(10)

Proof.

We start with the left inequality:

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=1-\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a}\langle\psi|A^{x}_{a}\otimes(M^{x}_{a})^{T}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a}\langle\psi|A^{x}_{a}\otimes\big(A^{x}_{a}-M^{x}_{a}\big)^{T}|\psi\rangle+1-\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a}\langle\psi|A^{x}_{a}\otimes(A^{x}_{a})^{T}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a}\langle\psi|\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits\otimes\big(A^{x}_{a}-M^{x}_{a}\big)^{2}|\psi\rangle\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a}\langle\psi|(A^{x}_{a})^{2}\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi\rangle\Big)^{1/2}+\delta

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a}\langle\psi|\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits\otimes\big(A^{x}_{a}-M^{x}_{a}\big)^{2}|\psi\rangle\Big)^{1/2}+\delta\;,

where the first inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz and the last uses that for each
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
\{A^{x}_{a}\}_{a}
is a measurement.

For the right inequality,

\displaystyle\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a}

\displaystyle\langle\psi|\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits\otimes\big(A^{x}_{a}-M^{x}_{a}\big)^{2}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a}\langle\psi|\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits\otimes\big((A^{x}_{a})^{2}+(M^{x}_{a})^{2}\big)|\psi\rangle-2\Re\Big(\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a}\langle\psi|\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits\otimes A^{x}_{a}M^{x}_{a}|\psi\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2-2\Re\Big((1-\gamma)-\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a}\langle\psi|\big(A^{x}_{a}\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits-\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits\otimes(A^{x}_{a})^{T}\big)(\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits\otimes(M^{x}_{a})^{T})|\psi\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\gamma+2\Big(\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a}\langle\psi|\big(A^{x}_{a}\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits-\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits\otimes(A^{x}_{a})^{T}\big)^{2}|\psi\rangle\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mu}\sum_{a}\langle\psi|\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits\otimes((M^{x}_{a})^{T})^{2}|\psi\rangle\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\gamma+2\sqrt{2\delta}\;,

where the first inequality uses that
\{A^{x}_{a}\}_{a}
and
\{M^{x}_{a}\}_{a}
are measurements, the second uses the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the last the definition of
\delta
and that for each
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
\{A^{x}_{a}\}
is a projective measurement and
\{M^{x}_{a}\}_{a}
is a measurement.
∎

Given a density matrix

of

|\psi\rangle\,=\,\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|u_{i}\rangle|u_{i}\rangle\;,

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
and
\{A_{a}\}
and
\{B_{a}\}
measurements on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
respectively. Let
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
be the reduced density of
|\psi\rangle
on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
respectively. Let
|\psi_{A}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
|\psi_{B}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
be the canonical purifications of
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
respectively. Then

\sum_{a}\langle\psi|A_{a}\otimes B_{a}|\psi\rangle\,\leq\,\Big(\sum_{a}\langle\psi_{A}|A_{a}\otimes A_{a}^{T}|\psi_{A}\rangle\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{a}\langle\psi_{B}|B_{a}\otimes B_{a}^{T}|\psi_{B}\rangle\Big)^{1/2}\;.

Proof.

Let
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}|u_{j}\rangle|v_{j}\rangle
be the Schmidt decomposition. Let
K=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}|u_{j}\rangle\langle v_{j}|
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\langle\psi|A_{a}\otimes B_{a}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(A_{a}K\overline{B}_{a}K^{\dagger}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{a}\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(A_{a}\sqrt{KK^{\dagger}}A_{a}\sqrt{KK^{\dagger}}\big)\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{a}\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(\overline{B_{a}}\sqrt{K^{\dagger}K}\overline{B_{a}}\sqrt{K^{\dagger}K}\big)\Big)^{1/2}\;,

where the inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz. Using that
\rho_{A}=KK^{\dagger}
and
\rho_{B}=K^{\dagger}K
, this concludes the proof.
∎

The following result shows that an approximately consistent strategy is always close to a projective strategy. The result first appears in

]
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
0\leq\delta\leq 1
. Let
|\psi\rangle
be a state on
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
whose reduced densities on either subsystem are identical. Let
k
be an integer and
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
positive semidefinite operators on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\sum_{i}Q_{i}=\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits
. Let

\delta\,=\,1-\sum_{i}\,\langle\psi|Q_{i}\otimes Q_{i}^{T}|\psi\rangle\;.

Then there exists orthogonal projections

on

such that
\sum_{i}P_{i}=\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits
and

\sum_{i}\langle\psi|(P_{i}-Q_{i})^{2}\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi\rangle\,\leq\,O\big(\delta^{1/4}\big)\;.

(18)

3
Main result

The following is our main result. It states that a strategy that induces a correlation which is almost synchronous must be proportionately close, in a precise sense, to a projective maximally entangled strategy.

Theorem 3.1
.

There are universal constants
c,C>0
such that the following holds. Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{A}
be finite sets and
\nu
a distribution on
\mathcal{X}
. Let
\mathscr{S}=(|\psi\rangle,A)
be a symmetric strategy and
\delta=\delta_{\textrm{sync}}(\mathscr{S};\nu)
.
Then there is a measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and a family of Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
(both depending on
|\psi\rangle
only) such that the following holds.
For every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
there is a maximally entangled state
|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
and for each
x
a projective measurement
\{A^{\lambda,x}_{a}\}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
such that

1.

Letting
\rho
be the reduced density of
|\psi\rangle
on
\mathcal{H}
and
\rho_{\lambda}
the totally mixed state on
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
,

\rho\,=\,\int_{\lambda}\rho_{\lambda}d\lambda\;.

(19)

2.

The
\{\mathscr{S}_{\lambda}=(|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle,A^{\lambda})\}
provide an approximate decomposition of
\mathscr{S}
as a convex sum of projective maximally entangled (PME) strategies, in the following sense:

\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\nu}\sum_{a}\int_{\lambda}\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(\big(A^{x}_{a}-A^{\lambda,x}_{a}\big)^{2}\rho_{\lambda}\big)d\mu(\lambda)\,\leq\,C\,\delta^{c}\;.

(20)

The key point in Theorem
3.1
is that the error estimates are independent of the dimension of
\mathcal{H}
and of the size of the sets
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{A}
. We remark that the integral over
\lambda
can be written as a finite convex sum. This is evident from the definition of
\rho_{\lambda}
as a multiple of the projection
P_{\lambda}
defined in (
34
). Since
\mathcal{H}
is finite-dimensional
\rho
has a discrete spectrum and
P_{\lambda}
takes on a finite set of values.

We note that the theorem does not imply that
|\psi\rangle
itself is close to a maximally entangled state. Rather, (
19
) implies that
after tracing an ancilla
, which contains the index
\lambda
, this is the case. It is not hard to see that this is unavoidable by considering a game such that there exists multiple optimal strategies for the game that are not unitarily equivalent. For example one can consider a linear system game that tests the group generated by the Pauli matrices
\sigma_{X},\sigma_{Z}
and
\sigma_{Y}
; this can be obtained from three copies of the Magic Square game as in e.g.
[
19
, Appendix A]
. This game can be won with probability
1
using any state of the form

|\psi\rangle\,=\,|\phi^{+}\rangle_{A_{1}B_{1}}|\phi^{+}\rangle_{A_{2}B_{2}}\big(\alpha|00\rangle_{A_{3}B_{3}}+\beta|11\rangle_{A_{3}B_{3}}\big)\;,

where
|\phi^{+}\rangle
is an EPR pair (rank-
2
maximally entangled state) and the measurement operators are block-diagonal with respect to the third system (i.e.
X=X^{\prime}_{A_{1}A_{2}}\otimes|0\rangle\!\langle 0|_{A_{3}}+X^{\prime\prime}_{A_{1}A_{2}}\otimes|1\rangle\!\langle 1|_{A_{3}}
for the first player). Crucially the measurement operator’s dependence on the third system cannot be removed by a local unitary, because the
X^{\prime}
and
X^{\prime\prime}
components are not unitarily related. Although the strategy cannot be locally dilated to a maximally entangled strategy in the sense of Definition
2.4
it is not hard to see that it nevertheless has a decomposition of the form promised by Theorem
3.1
.

4
Applications to nonlocal games

We give two applications of Theorem
3.1
. The first is to transferring “rigidity” statements obtained for PME strategies to the general case. The second is to the class of projection games.

4.1
Application to rigidity

As mentioned in the introduction, Theorem
3.1
allows one to transfer rigidity statements shown for PME strategies to general strategies. We do not have a general all-purpose statement demonstrating this. Instead we give two simple corollaries that are meant to describe sample applications. The first corollary considers a situation important in complexity theory, where one aims to show that a large family of measurements that constitute a successful strategy in a certain game must in some sense be consistent with a single larger measurement that “explains” it; see Subsection
4.1.1
. The second corollary considers a typical midpoint in a proof of rigidity, where one uses the game condition to derive certain algebraic relations on the measurements that constitute a successful strategy, which are then shown to impose a further structure; see Subsection
4.1.2
.

4.1.1
Application to showing classical soundness

Our first application arises in complexity theory when one is trying to show that quantum strategies in a certain nonlocal game obey a certain “global” structure. We first state the corollary and then describe a typical application of it.

\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x\sim\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\sum_{g:\,g(x)=a}\langle\psi|A^{x}_{a}\otimes G_{g}|\psi\rangle\,\geq\,1-\poly(m)\cdot(\poly(\varepsilon)+\poly(d/q))\;.

(42)

Proof of Corollary
4.1
.

Fix a symmetric projective strategy
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}=(|\psi\rangle,A)
in
\mathfrak{G}
and let
\omega
denote its success probability.
For each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
let
\mathscr{S}_{\lambda}=(|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle,A^{\lambda})
be the PME strategy promised by Theorem
3.1
and
\omega_{\lambda}
its probability of success in
\mathfrak{G}
. Let
\{M^{\lambda,y}_{b}\}
be the family of measurements promised by the assumption of Corollary
4.1
, i.e. such that

\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{(x,y)\sim p}\sum_{a}\langle\psi_{\lambda}|A^{x,\lambda}\otimes M^{\lambda,y}_{[g_{xy}(a)]}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\,\geq\,\kappa(\omega_{\lambda})\;.

Averaging with respect to the probability measure with density
d\mu(\lambda)
and using that
\kappa
is assumed to be convex monotone it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda}\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x,y}\sum_{a}\langle\psi_{\lambda}|A^{x,\lambda}_{a}\otimes M^{\lambda,y}_{[g_{xy}(a)]}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle d\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq\kappa\Big(\int_{\lambda}\omega_{\lambda}d\mu(\lambda)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\kappa(\omega^{\prime})\;,

(43)

where
\omega^{\prime}=\omega-\poly(\delta)
by Corollary
3.3
.

Claim 4.3
.

The following holds:

\int_{\lambda}\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x,y}\sum_{a}\langle\psi_{\lambda}|A^{x}_{a}\otimes M^{\lambda,y}_{[g_{xy}(a)]}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle d\mu(\lambda)\,\geq\,\kappa(\omega^{\prime})-\poly(\delta)\;.

(44)

Proof.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
we have

\displaystyle\big|\langle\psi_{\lambda}|\big(A^{x}_{a}-A^{x,\lambda}_{a}\big)

\displaystyle\otimes M^{\lambda,y}_{[g_{xy}(a)]}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big|\langle\psi_{\lambda}|\big(A^{x}_{a}-A^{x,\lambda}_{a}\big)A^{x}_{a}\otimes M^{\lambda,y}_{[g_{xy}(a)]}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big|+\big|\langle\psi_{\lambda}|A^{x,\lambda}_{a}\big(A^{x}_{a}-A^{x,\lambda}_{a}\big)\otimes M^{\lambda,y}_{[g_{xy}(a)]}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\big(A^{x}_{a}-A^{x,\lambda}_{a}\big)\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big\|\big(\big\|A^{x,\lambda}_{a}\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big\|+\big\|A^{x}_{a}\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big\|\big)\;,

(45)

where the first inequality uses that both
A^{x}_{a}
and
A^{\lambda,x}_{a}
are projections and the second inequality uses
\|M^{\lambda,y}_{[g_{xy}(a)]}\|\leq 1
for all
x,y
and
a
. Averaging over
\lambda
,

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda}\Big|\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x,y}\sum_{a}

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{\lambda}|\big(A^{x}_{a}-A^{\lambda,x}_{a}\big)\otimes M^{\lambda,y}_{[g_{xy}(a)]}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\Big|d\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\lambda}\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x}\sum_{a}\big\|\big(A^{x}_{a}-A^{x,\lambda}_{a}\big)\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big\|\big(\big\|A^{x,\lambda}_{a}\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big\|+\big\|A^{x}_{a}\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big\|\big)d\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\lambda}\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x}\sum_{a}\big\|\big(A^{x}_{a}-A^{x,\lambda}_{a}\big)\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big\|^{2}d\mu(\lambda)\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\Big(\int_{\lambda}\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x}\sum_{a}\big(\big\|A^{x,\lambda}_{a}\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big\|+\big\|A^{x}_{a}\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big\|\big)^{2}d\mu(\lambda)\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\poly(\delta)\;,

where the first inequality uses (
45
), the second is the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the last uses (
20
) to bound the first term, since for every
\lambda
,
x
and
a
,

\big\|\big(A^{x}_{a}-A^{x,\lambda}_{a}\big)\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\big\|^{2}\,=\,\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(\big(A^{x}_{a}-A^{x,\lambda}_{a}\big)^{2}\rho_{\lambda}\big)\;.

∎

For each
y,b
define

M^{y}_{b}\,=\,\rho^{-1/2}\Big(\int_{\lambda}\frac{1}{\mbox{\rm Tr}(P_{\lambda})}P_{\lambda}M^{\lambda,y}_{b}P_{\lambda}d\mu(\lambda)\Big)\rho^{-1/2}\;,

and note that
M^{y}_{b}\geq 0
and

\sum_{b}M^{y}_{b}\,=\,\rho^{-1/2}\Big(\int_{\lambda}\frac{1}{\mbox{\rm Tr}(\lambda)}P_{\lambda}d\mu(\lambda)\Big)\rho^{-1/2}\,=\,\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits\;,

(46)

by (
19
). Thus for each
y
,
\{M^{y}_{b}\}
is a valid measurement. Moreover, using (
8
) we get

\displaystyle\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x,y}\sum_{a}\langle\psi|A^{x}_{a}\otimes M^{y}_{[g_{xy}(a)]}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x,y}\sum_{a}\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(A^{x}_{a}\rho^{1/2}M^{y}_{[g_{xy}(a)]}\rho^{1/2}\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda}\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x,y}\sum_{a}\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(A^{x}_{a}P_{\lambda}M^{\lambda,y}_{[g_{xy}(a)]}P_{\lambda}\big)d\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda}\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x,y}\sum_{a}\langle\psi_{\lambda}|A^{x}_{a}\otimes M^{\lambda,y}_{[g_{xy}(a)]}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle d\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq\kappa(\omega^{\prime})-\poly(\delta)\;,

where the last inequality is by (

).

4.1.2
Application to showing algebraic relations

Our second application concerns rigidity statements that go through algebraic relations, as is exemplified by the rigidity proofs for games such as the CHSH game, the Magic Square game, as well as more general classes of games; see e.g.
[
20
]
for an exposition of this approach.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A},\nu,D)
be a symmetric nonlocal game. Suppose that
\{0,1\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and for any symmetric projective strategy
\mathscr{S}=(|\psi\rangle,A)
in
\mathfrak{G}
, for
x\in\{0,1\}
,
\{A^{x}_{0},A^{x}_{1}\}
is a two-outcome measurement that can be represented as an observable
A^{x}=A^{x}_{0}-A^{x}_{1}
.
Suppose that the following statement holds for some concave monotone non-decreasing function
\kappa:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

For every
\omega\in[0,1]
and symmetric PME strategy
\mathscr{S}=(|\psi\rangle,A)
that succeeds with probability
\omega
in
\mathfrak{G}
it holds that

\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(\big(A^{0}A^{1}-A^{1}A^{0}\big)^{2}\rho\big)\leq\kappa(1-\omega)\;.

(47)

Then the same statement extends to arbitrary symmetric projective strategies
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}=(|\psi\rangle,A)
, with the right-hand side in (
47
) replaced by
\kappa(\omega+\poly(\delta))+\poly(\delta)
where

\delta\,=\,\max\big\{\delta_{\textrm{sync}}(\mathscr{S}^{\prime};q)\,,\;\delta_{\textrm{sync}}(\mathscr{S}^{\prime};\nu)\big\}

(48)

with
q
the uniform distribution on
\{0,1\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
\nu
the marginal of the game distribution on
\mathcal{X}
.

Since the aim of the corollary is to give a “toy” application of our results we sketch the proof but omit the details.

Proof sketch.

Fix a symmetric projective strategy
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}=(|\psi\rangle,A)
in
\mathfrak{G}
and let
\omega
denote its success probability.
For each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
let
\mathscr{S}_{\lambda}=(|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle,A^{\lambda})
be the PME strategy promised by Theorem
3.1
and
\omega_{\lambda}
its probability of success in
\mathfrak{G}
. Further let
\tilde{\mathscr{S}}_{\lambda}=(|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle,A)
.
First we claim that by an argument similar to the derivation of (
22
) in the proof of Corollary
3.3
it holds that

\int_{\lambda}\delta_{\textrm{sync}}(\tilde{\mathscr{S}}_{\lambda};q)d\lambda\,=\,\poly(\delta)\;,

(49)

where to show this we use that the definition of
\delta
in (
48
) involves measuring almost synchronicity under
q
.
We may then achieve the desired conclusion as follows. First we note that

\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(\big(A^{0}A^{1}-A^{1}A^{0}\big)^{2}\rho\big)\,=\,\int_{\lambda}\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(\big(A^{0}A^{1}-A^{1}A^{0}\big)^{2}\rho_{\lambda}\big)d\lambda

(50)

by (
19
). Next we use (
49
) to show

\int_{\lambda}\Big|\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(\big(A^{0}A^{1}-A^{1}A^{0}\big)^{2}\rho_{\lambda}\big)\,-\,\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(\big(A^{\lambda,0}A^{\lambda,1}-A^{\lambda,1}A^{\lambda,0}\big)^{2}\rho_{\lambda}\big)\Big|\,=\,\poly(\delta)\;,

(51)

where (
20
) is used, informally, to “switch” operators from one side of the tensor product to the other so that (
49
) can be applied to each operator in an expansion of the square in turn. Finally, the second term on the left-hand side in (
51
) is at most
\kappa(\omega-\poly(\delta))
using the assumption made in the corollary, Jensen’s inequality, and the fact that by Corollary
3.3
it holds that
\int_{\lambda}\omega_{\lambda}d\lambda\geq\omega-\poly(\delta)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

4.2
Extension to projection games

Theorem
3.1
applies to almost consistent symmetric strategies. In this section we give an example of how the results of the theorem can be applied to a family of games such that success in the game naturally implies a bound on consistency. This partially extends the main result in
[
21
]
, with the caveat that our result applies only to projection games, and not the more general “weak projection games” considered in
[
21
]
; it is not hard to see that this is necessary to obtain a “robust” result of the kind we obtain here.

Definition 4.5
.

A game
\mathfrak{G}=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\nu,D)
is a
projection game
if for each
(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
there is
f_{xy}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
such that
D(a,b|x,y)=0
if
b\neq f_{xy}(a)
.

Theorem 4.6
.

There are universal constants
c,C>0
such that the following holds. Let
\mathfrak{G}=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\nu,D)
be a projection game and
\mathscr{S}=(|\psi\rangle,A,B)
a strategy for
\mathfrak{G}
that succeeds with probability
1-\varepsilon
, for some
0\leq\varepsilon\leq 1
.
Then there is a measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and a family of Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\textsf{A}}
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
(both depending on
|\psi\rangle
only) such that the following holds.
For every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
there is a PME strategy
\mathscr{S}_{\lambda}=(|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle,A^{\lambda},B)
for
\mathfrak{G}
such that
|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle
is a maximally entangled state on
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
and moreover if
\omega_{\lambda}
is the success probability of
\mathscr{S}_{\lambda}
in
\mathfrak{G}
then

\int_{\lambda}\omega_{\lambda}d\mu(\lambda)\,\geq\,1-C\,\varepsilon^{c}\;.

(52)

Proof.

Applying Naimark’s theorem (Lemma
2.5
), extending
|\psi\rangle
if necessary we may assume that for every
x,y
,
\{A^{x}_{a}\}
and
\{B^{y}_{b}\}
are projective measurements.
For each
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
a\in\mathcal{A}
let

B^{x}_{a}=\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{y\sim\nu_{x}}\sum_{b}D(a,b|x,y)B^{y}_{b}\;,

where for
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
\nu_{x}
is the conditional distribution of
\nu
on
\mathcal{Y}
, conditioned on
x
. The assumption that
\mathfrak{G}
is a projection game implies that for every
x
,
\sum_{a}B^{x}_{a}\leq\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits
. Let
|\psi_{A}\rangle
and
|\psi_{B}\rangle
be the canonical purifications of the reduced density of
|\psi\rangle
on
\mathcal{H}_{\textsf{A}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\textsf{B}}
respectively.
Using Lemma
2.9
,

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x}\sum_{a}\langle\psi|A^{x}_{a}\otimes B^{x}_{a}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x}\sum_{a}\langle\psi_{A}|A^{x}_{a}\otimes(A^{x}_{a})^{T}|\psi_{A}\rangle\big)\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x}\sum_{a}\langle\psi_{B}|B^{x}_{a}\otimes(B^{x}_{a})^{T}|\psi_{B}\rangle\big)\Big)^{1/2}\;,

which implies that

\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x}\sum_{a}\langle\psi_{A}|A^{x}_{a}\otimes(A^{x}_{a})^{T}|\psi_{A}\rangle\,\geq\,(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\geq 1-2\varepsilon\;.

(53)

Eq. (
53
) shows that the symmetric projective strategy
\mathscr{S}_{A}=(|\psi_{A}\rangle,A)
satisfies
\delta_{\textrm{sync}}(\mathscr{S}_{A},\nu)\leq 2\varepsilon
. Thus we can apply Theorem
3.1
. Let
\mu
,
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\textsf{A}}
,
A^{\lambda}
be as promised by the theorem. Since
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{{\textsf{B}}})\geq d
for each
\lambda
we can find a purification
|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle_{{\textsf{AB}}}
of
\rho_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}/\mbox{\rm Tr}(P_{\lambda})
on
\mathcal{H}_{\textsf{A}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\textsf{B}}
; note that
|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle_{{\textsf{AB}}}
is maximally entangled.

Next we note that

\displaystyle\Big|\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x}\sum_{a}\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(A^{x}_{a}B^{x}_{a}\rho\big)

\displaystyle-\int_{\lambda}\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x}\sum_{a}\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(A^{\lambda,x}_{a}B^{x}_{a}\rho_{\lambda}\big)d\mu(\lambda)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\lambda}\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x}\sum_{a}\mbox{\rm Tr}\big((A^{x}_{a}-A^{\lambda,x}_{a})^{2}\rho_{\lambda}d\mu(\lambda)\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{\lambda}\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x}\sum_{a}\mbox{\rm Tr}\big((B^{x}_{a})^{2}\rho_{\lambda}d\mu(\lambda)\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\poly(\varepsilon)\;,

(54)

where the first inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz and uses (
19
) and the second uses (
53
) to bound the first term by (
20
) and that for all
x
,
\sum_{a}B^{x}_{a}\leq\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits
to bound the second by
1
. Using that
A^{\lambda,x}_{a}\rho_{\lambda}=\rho_{\lambda}A^{\lambda,x}_{a}
since
\rho_{\lambda}
is totally mixed and
\{A^{\lambda,x}_{a}\}
is supported on it we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda}\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x}\sum_{a}\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(A^{\lambda,x}_{a}B^{x}_{a}\rho_{\lambda}\big)d\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda}\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x}\sum_{a}\frac{1}{\mbox{\rm Tr}(P_{\lambda})}\mbox{\rm Tr}\big(A^{\lambda,x}_{a}P_{\lambda}B^{x}_{a}P_{\lambda}\big)d\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda}\mathop{\textsc{E}}_{x}\sum_{a}\langle\psi_{\lambda}|A^{\lambda,x}_{a}\otimes B^{x}_{a}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle d\mu(\lambda)\;.

(55)

Eq. (
54
) and (
55
) together give (
52
).

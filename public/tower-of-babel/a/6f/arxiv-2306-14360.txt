M z M_{z} -invariant subspaces in the Bloch space
Adem Limani & Artur Nicolau

Abstract
We consider weak-star closed invariant subspaces of the shift operator in the classical Bloch space. We prove that any bounded analytic function decomposes into two factors, one which is cyclic and another one generating a proper invariant subspace, satisfying a permanence property, which in a certain way is opposite to cyclicity. Singular inner functions play the crucial role in this decomposition. We show in several different ways that the description of shift invariant subspaces generated by inner functions in the Bloch spaces deviates substantially from the corresponding description in the Bergman spaces, provided by the celebrated Korenblum-Roberts Theorem. Furthermore, the relationship between invertibility and cyclicity is also investigated and we provide an invertible function in the Bloch space which is not cyclic therein. Our results answer several open questions stated in the early nineties.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the open unit disc in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
and let

be the classical Bloch space of analytic functions

Taking the closure of analytic polynomials in the norm above we obtain the so-called little Bloch space

consisting of functions

\lim_{|z|\to 1^{-}}(1-|z|)f^{\prime}(z)=0.

Functions in the Bloch space are intimately related to conformal mappings, and as such they are regarded as a crucial objects in geometric function theory, see
[
21
]
for further details. Let
H^{\infty}
designate the Banach space of bounded analytic functions

in
\mathbb{D}

of the Bergman spaces

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}dA(z)<\infty,

, see for instance
[
4
]

-invariant subspaces in

, and to adjust for the fact that

In this note, we shall mainly be concerned with
M_{z}
-invariant subspaces in

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mu
be a positive, finite, Borel, singular measure in

(i)

(ii)

Theorem 1.3

Let

Theorem 1.7

Let

Theorem 1.8

(2)

We now turn our attention to the problem of determining the relationship between invertibility and cyclicity, which is a vastly investigated topic in various spaces of analytic functions. It it is well-known that in the setting of commutative Banach algebras of analytic functions with units, the notions of cyclicity and invertibility are equivalent, while moving towards classes of analytic function for which the algebra property fails, such as the classical Dirichlet spaces and the Hardy spaces, invertibility is strictly stronger than cyclicity, see
[
11
]
. However, the situation for Bergman spaces attracted considerable amount of attention and remained open for quite some time until it was resolved by Borichev and Hedenmalm in
[
8
]
. In their deep work, they constructed invertible functions in the Bergman Spaces, which are not cyclic therein, showing that the two notions are quite different in that setting. Our next result asserts that a similar phenomenon occurs in the Bloch space. This answers Problem 2 from
[
3
]
in the negative.

Theorem 1.9
.

There exists
f\in\mathcal{B}
with
1/f\in\mathcal{B}
such that
f
is not cyclic in
\mathcal{B}
.

The crucial property of a function

as above is that
\left\lvert f^{\prime}(z)\right\rvert
is as huge as possible for a considerably large set of points
z\in\mathbb{D}
. This aligns well with the principal philosophy surrounding the work in
[
8
]
, that an impediment to cyclicity of a Bergman space function is that the function enjoys maximal growth at a ”massive” set in
\mathbb{D}
.

Our final result should be viewed as a pre-cautionary warning when considering weak-star closures of
M_{z}
-invariant subspaces generated by Bloch functions. In fact it may be viewed as a Bloch space version of Theorem 1.2 by Borichev and Hedenmalm in
[
9
]
. It asserts that sequential limits are not enough in order to capture the behavior of
M_{z}
-invariant subspaces in
\mathcal{B}
. Recall that classical outer functions in
\mathcal{B}
are always cyclic therein.

Theorem 1.10
.

There exists an outer function
f\in\mathcal{B}
with
1/f\in H^{\infty}
, such that the set of all weak-star sequential limits in
\mathcal{B}
of functions of the type
f(z)Q(z)
, where

is an analytic polynomial, is a proper subspace of
\mathcal{B}
.

2
Beurling-type theorems and model spaces

In this section, we shall keep a broad point of view and illustrate a close relationship between Beurling-type theorems on
M_{z}
-invariant subspaces generated by inner functions
\Theta
and the ample of functions with sufficiently regular boundary values belonging the model space
K_{\Theta}

2.1
Regular spaces

A Banach space
X
of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
will be referred to as a

if the following three properties hold:

(i)

X\subseteq H^{1}
.

(ii)

The set of analytic polynomials and the set of functions analytic in a neighborhood of

are dense in
X
.

(iii)

For any
\ell
in the dual space
X^{*}
, we have
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,\left\lvert\ell(z^{n})\right\rvert^{1/n}\leq 1.

Assumption
(i)
ensures that the boundary values of functions in
X
are integrable on
\partial\mathbb{D}
and by the closed graph theorem, there exists a constant

such that
\|f\|_{1}\leq C\|f\|_{X}
for any

. Assumption
(ii)
implies that

is a separable Banach space, thus Helly’s selection theorem ensures that any closed and bounded set in the norm of its Banach space dual

is sequentially compact wrt to the weak-star topology. In conjunction with

-pairing

\lim_{r\to 1-}\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}f(r\zeta)\overline{g(r\zeta)}dm(\zeta),\qquad f\in X,g\in X^{\prime}.

Note that condition

also gives the containment

. For our purposes, we shall equip

with the weak-star topology. Let us briefly verify that

is indeed a regular space. Note that if

Hence

as required. It is clear that analytic polynomials are dense in

holds, condition (iii) is also fulfilled as the Taylor coefficients of Bloch functions are well-known to be bounded, see

and their counter-parts involving multiple derivatives.

Let

]
, in the context of Bergman spaces. Let

2.4
An abstract decomposition for singular measures

Inspired from the Korenblum-Roberts Theorem on
M_{z}
-invariant subspaces generated by inner functions, this section is devoted to extending their result to the context of Regular Spaces. Here we shall follow ideas developed by Roberts in
[
28
]
, with certain required adaptations in order to deal with our general framework.

Theorem 2.8

(Abstract decomposition)
.

Let
X
be a regular space and let
S_{\mu}
be a singular inner function. Then there exists a unique decomposition of
\mu=\mu_{P}+\mu_{C}
(up to sets of
\mu
-measure zero) into mutually singular parts
\mu_{P},\mu_{C}
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

S_{\mu_{P}}
has the permanence property in
X^{\prime}
, that is,

\left[S_{\mu_{P}}\right]_{X^{\prime}}\cap H^{\infty}\subseteq S_{\mu_{P}}H^{\infty}
,

(ii)

S_{\mu_{C}}
is weak-star cyclic in
X^{\prime}
, that is,
\left[S_{\mu_{C}}\right]_{X^{\prime}}=X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
\mu\lvert_{E}
denote the restriction of
\mu
to the Borel set
E
. Consider the the following collection of Borel subsets of
\partial\mathbb{D}

\mathcal{M}(X^{\prime},\mu):=\left\{E\subset\partial\mathbb{D}:\,\text{E Borel set},\,\,\,\mu(E)>0,\left[S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}\right]_{X^{\prime}}\cap H^{\infty}\subseteq S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}H^{\infty}\right\}.

If the set
\mathcal{M}(X^{\prime},\mu)
is empty, then we simply take
\mu_{C}=\mu
. For the being moment, we shall assume that the collection
\mathcal{M}(X^{\prime},\mu)
is non-empty, and our goal is to primarily establish that
\mathcal{M}(X^{\prime},\mu)
is closed under the formation of unions. To this end, pick two sets
E,F\in\mathcal{M}(X^{\prime},\mu)
which we may assume are not contained in one or another. Set
\nu:=\mu\lvert_{E\cup F}
and note that the containment of
K_{S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}}^{1}\subset K_{S_{\nu}}^{1}
in conjunction with (
3
) implies

\left[S_{\nu}\right]_{X^{\prime}}\cap H^{\infty}=\left(X\cap K_{S_{\nu}}^{1}\right)^{\perp}\cap H^{\infty}\subseteq(X\cap K_{S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}}^{1})^{\perp}\cap H^{\infty}=\left[S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}\right]_{X^{\prime}}\cap H^{\infty}\subseteq S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}H^{\infty}.

Therefore, any

is divisible by
S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}
, and by switching the roles of
E
and
F
, we deduce in a similar way that
f
is also divisible by
S_{\mu\lvert_{F}}
. Altogether, we obtain
f/S_{\nu}\in H^{\infty}
and therefore
E\cup F\in\mathcal{M}(X^{\prime},\mu)
. Now consider the quantity

\gamma(X^{\prime},\mu):=\sup\left\{\mu(E):\,E\in\mathcal{M}(X^{\prime},\mu)\right\},

and observe that since
\mathcal{M}(X^{\prime},\mu)
is closed under finite unions, we can find a sequence of Borel sets
\{E_{n}\}_{n}
with
E_{n}\in\mathcal{M}(X^{\prime},\mu)
and
E_{n}\subset E_{n+1}
for any
n
, such that
\mu(E_{n})\to\gamma(X^{\prime},\mu)
as
n\to\infty
. Set
E:=\cup_{n}E_{n}
and we claim that our candidates are given by
\mu_{P}=\mu\lvert_{E}
and
\mu_{C}=\mu\lvert_{\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus E}
. We first check that
E\in\mathcal{M}(X^{\prime},\mu)
. To this end, let
f\in\left[S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}\right]_{X^{\prime}}\cap H^{\infty}
and note that since each
E_{n}\in\mathcal{M}(X^{\prime},\mu)
, an application of (
3
) implies

\left[S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}\right]_{X^{\prime}}\cap H^{\infty}=(X\cap K_{S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}}^{1})^{\perp}\cap H^{\infty}\subseteq(X\cap K_{S_{\mu\lvert_{E_{n}}}}^{1})^{\perp}\cap H^{\infty}=\left[S_{\mu\lvert_{E_{n}}}\right]_{X^{\prime}}\cap H^{\infty}\subseteq S_{\mu\lvert_{E_{n}}}H^{\infty},

for all
n
. Hence for each
n
, there exists
\{h_{n}\}_{n}\subset H^{\infty}
such that
f=S_{\mu\lvert_{E_{n}}}h_{n}
. But since
\left\lVert h_{n}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty},

we may extract a subsequence
\{h_{n_{k}}\}_{k}
that converges pointwise in
\mathbb{D}
to a certain function

. Now since the sequence of sets
\{E_{n}\}_{n}
is increasing, we actually obtain

. This proves that

and consequently we have established claim
(i)
with
\mu_{P}=\mu\lvert_{E}
.

We now proceed to establish claim
(ii)
by showing that if
\mu_{C}:=\mu\lvert_{\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus E}
then
S_{\mu_{C}}
is weak-star cyclic in
X^{\prime}
. To this end, suppose that
S_{\mu_{C}}
is not weak-star cyclic in
X^{\prime}
, then there exists a non-trivial

\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\overline{g(\zeta)}S_{\mu_{C}}(\zeta)\zeta^{n}dm(\zeta)=0,\qquad n\geq 0.

By the F. and M. Riesz Theorem, there exists a function

such that

This section is devoted to showing that inner factors of certain weighted BMOA spaces give rise to the permanence property in

3.1
Inner factors in weighted BMOA

Let

equipped with the semi-norm

\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{BMO_{w}}:=\sup_{I}\frac{1}{w(|I|)}\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}|h-h_{I}|dm<\infty.

It is straightforward to verify the containment

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{w(t)}{t}dt<\infty,

(5)

then functions in

extend continuously to
\partial\mathbb{D}
with modulus of continuity not exceeding a constant multiple of
\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{w(t)}{t}dt

contains discontinuous and unbounded functions.
See
[
31
]
for details on these matters. Analytic counter-parts of the above defined spaces will be important, namely we consider the spaces
BMOA_{w}:=H^{2}\cap BMO_{w}(\partial\mathbb{D})
and

, hence the containment
A_{w}\subseteq BMOA_{w}
holds. Moving forward, we shall now restrict our attention to majorants

satisfying the following condition. There exists a constant
0<\gamma<1
such that

w(t)/t^{\gamma}\text{ is non-increasing on }[0,1].

(6)

We now state our main result in this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
w
be a majorant satisfying condition (
6
) and the Dini condition (

). Then any non-trivial singular inner factor

of a function in

satisfies the permanence property in
\mathcal{B}
, that is,
\left[S_{\mu}\right]_{\mathcal{B}}\cap H^{\infty}=S_{\mu}H^{\infty}.
In particular, no non-trivial singular inner divisor of a function in

can be cyclic in
\mathcal{B}
.

An important class of singular inner factors of

-functions are in fact provided by singular inner factors of

-functions, which turn out to be intimately connected to sets of finite

-entropy due to a deep result by Shirokov in

. For the moment being, we shall primarily establish ?THM?
and then illustrate how ?THM?
can be derived as a corollary. In the pursuit towards proving ?THM?
, we shall need a couple of preparatory results. The first lemma can be found in a slight greater generality in

18

(see Proposition 2.6 therein for further details).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
w
be a majorant satisfying condition (
6
). Then the
BMO_{w}
semi-norm is equivalent to the Garsia-type semi-norm, that is,

\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{BMO_{w}}\asymp\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{w(1-|z|)}\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\left\lvert h(\zeta)-P(h)(z)\right\rvert P_{z}(\zeta)dm(\zeta)

where

denotes the Poisson extension of

on

and
P_{z}(\zeta)=(1-|z|^{2})|1-\overline{z}\zeta|^{-2}
,

,
\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, is the Poisson kernel on
\mathbb{D}
.

With ?THM?
at hand, we may derive the following result on the Cauchy projection.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
w
be a majorant satisfying condition (
6
) and the Dini condition (
5
). Then the Cauchy projection
P_{+}
maps

continuously into

.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary

and observe that

P_{+}(h)^{\prime}(z)=\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\frac{\overline{\zeta}h(\zeta)}{(1-\overline{\zeta}z)^{2}}dm(\zeta)=\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\frac{\overline{\zeta}\left(h(\zeta)-P(h)(z)\right)}{(1-\overline{\zeta}z)^{2}}dm(\zeta),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

According to ?THM?
, we have

\left\lvert P_{+}(h)^{\prime}(z)\right\rvert\leq\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\frac{\left\lvert h(\zeta)-P(h)(z)\right\rvert}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2}}dm(\zeta)\lesssim\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{BMO_{w}}\frac{w(1-|z|)}{1-|z|},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

It now readily follows that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left\lvert P_{+}(h)^{\prime}(z)\right\rvert dA(z)\lesssim\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{BMO_{w}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{w(t)}{t}dt,

which shows that

The following result on division and multiplication by inner functions on weighted

Theorem 3.4

Let

Proof of ?THM?
.

Let

be a singular inner factor of a function

. Given

4
The permanence property and
W^{1}

4.1
The permanence property and invisibility

Let
\mu
be a positive finte Borel singular measure on
\partial\mathbb{D}
. It was already mentioned in the introduction that the permanence property holds for singular inner functions in
W^{1}
. Our first observation in this section provides a simple way for inducing the permanence property in
\mathcal{B}
of singular inner functions. Let
\mu
be a positive finite singular Borel measure on
\partial\mathbb{D}
and assume

\sup\left\{\mu(E):S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}\in W^{1}\right\}>0.

(7)

We may pick a sequence of Borel sets
\{E_{N}\}_{N}
with the property that each
S_{\mu\lvert_{E_{N}}}\in W^{1}
and such that
\mu(E_{N})
converges to the supremum above. Observe that whenever
\phi,\psi
are bounded functions in
W^{1}
, so is their product
\phi\psi
, and thus we may without loss of generality, assume that the
E_{N}\subseteq E_{N+1}
for any
N
. Now let
E:=\cup_{N}E_{N}
and set
\mu_{0}:=\mu-\mu\lvert_{E}
and observe that by maximality, there exists no non-trivial
\nu_{0}\leq\mu_{0}
with
S_{\nu_{0}}\in W^{1}
. Measures satisfying the above condition are declared to be
W^{1}
invisible. As previously observed in the introduction, each
S_{\mu_{E_{N}}}
satisfies the permanence property in
\mathcal{B}
and thus according to the monotonicity principle in ?THM?
, so does
S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}
. Our discussion can thus be summarized in the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mu
be a positive finite singular Borel measure on
\partial\mathbb{D}
and
\mu\lvert_{E}
denote the corresponding piece in the decomposition of
\mu
appearing in the previous paragraph. Then
S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}
satisfies the permanence property in
\mathcal{B}
, that is,
\left[S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}\right]_{\mathcal{B}}\cap H^{\infty}\subseteq S_{\mu\lvert_{E}}H^{\infty}.

As a consequence, if
S_{\mu}
is cyclic in
\mathcal{B}
, then
\mu
is
W^{1}
invisible.

4.2
Singular inner functions in
W^{1}

This section is devoted to study singular inner functions in
W^{1}
. For any arc
I\subseteq\partial\mathbb{D}
, we denote its associated Carleson square by

Q_{I}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:z/|z|\in I,\,\,1-|z|\leq|I|\right\},

and we let
T_{I}=\{z\in Q_{I}:1-|z|\geq|I|/2\}
be the top-half of
Q_{I}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists a universal constant
C>0
, such that for any singular measure
\mu
on
\partial\mathbb{D}
and any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
with
\mu(I)=0
, the associated singular inner function
S_{\mu}
satisfies

\int_{Q_{I}}\left\lvert S_{\mu}^{\prime}(z)\right\rvert dA(z)\leq C|I|.

Proof.

Let
\{I_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
denote the Whitney decomposition of
I
satisfying

\text{dist}(I_{k},\partial I)=|I_{k}|=\frac{1}{3\cdot 2^{|k|}}|I|,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

We first observe that there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that

\int_{Q_{I}\setminus\bigcup_{k}Q_{I_{k}}}|S^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)|dA(z)\leq\int_{Q_{I}\setminus\bigcup_{k}Q_{I_{k}}}\frac{dA(z)}{1-|z|^{2}}\leq C|I|.

(8)

It remains to estimate the integral of
|S^{\prime}_{\mu}|
on
\cup_{k}Q_{I_{k}}
. To this end, for each Whitney arc
I_{k}
, we denote by
\xi_{k}
its center and let
z_{k}=(1-|I_{k}|)\xi_{k}
. Note that there exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that
c^{-1}|\zeta-z_{k}|\leq|\zeta-z|\leq c|\zeta-z_{k}|
for any
z\in Q_{I_{k}}
and any
\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus I
.
Since
\mu(I)=0
the Poisson extension
P(\mu)
of
\mu
satisfies

P(\mu)(z)=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-|z_{k}|^{2}}\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus I}\frac{1-|z_{k}|^{2}}{|\zeta-z|^{2}}d\mu(\zeta)\asymp(1-|z|)\frac{P(\mu)(z_{k})}{|I_{k}|},\qquad z\in Q_{I_{k}}.

Now using this in conjunction with the obvious estimate

(1-|z|^{2})|S^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)|\leq 2P(\mu)(z)\exp(-P(\mu)(z)),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

it follows that there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that

\int_{Q_{I_{k}}}|S^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)|dA(z)\lesssim\frac{P(\mu)(z_{k})}{|I_{k}|}\int_{Q_{I_{k}}}\exp\left(-C(1-|z|)\frac{P(\mu)(z_{k})}{|I_{k}|}\right)dA(z).

A straightforward computation of the integral above gives

\int_{Q_{I_{k}}}|S^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)|dA(z)\lesssim|I_{k}|.

Summing over
k
completes the proof.
∎

We shall now locate the critical domain of integration in order for
S_{\mu}
to belong to
W^{1}
, which will be convenient for our further developments.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\mu
be a positive singular Borel measure supported on a closed set
E\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then
S_{\mu}
belongs to
W^{1}
if and only if

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}\\
I\cap E\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\int_{T_{I}}\left\lvert S^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)\right\rvert dA(z)<\infty,

where
\mathcal{D}
denotes the collection of dyadic arcs on
\partial\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that the above condition implies that
S_{\mu}\in W^{1}
. To this end, let
\mathcal{G}
denote the collection of maximal dyadic arcs which do not intersect
E
. Observe that we can write

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left\lvert S^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)\right\rvert dA(z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}\\
I\cap E\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\int_{T_{I}}\left\lvert S^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)\right\rvert dA(z)+\sum_{I\in\mathcal{G}}\int_{Q_{I}}\left\lvert S^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)\right\rvert dA(z)

Note that ?THM?
gives

\sum_{I\in\mathcal{G}}\int_{Q_{I}}\left\lvert S^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)\right\rvert dA(z)\leq C\sum_{I\in\mathcal{G}}|I|<\infty,

which is enough to prove the desired claim.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain the following sufficient condition for membership of
S_{\mu}
in
W^{1}
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mu
be a positive finite singular Borel measure supported on a Beurling Carleson set of the unit circle. Then
S_{\mu}
belongs to
W^{1}
.

Proof.

It is well known that a closed set
E\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
of Lebesgue measure zero is a Beurling Carleson set if and only if

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}\\
I\cap E\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}|I|<\infty,

see
[
10
]
. The claim now follows from Schwarz Lemma and ?THM?
.

∎

4.3
No estimate from below implies cyclicity in
\mathcal{B}

This subsection is devoted to ?THM?
, which essentially asserts that no condition on the modulus of continuity on
\mu
alone is an impediment for
S_{\mu}
to be a member in

. According to the discussion in the introduction, this implies that there cannot be any estimate from below of a singular inner function which ensures it to be cyclic in

Proof of ?THM?
.

We shall divide the proof in three different steps.

Step 1: Construction of
\mu

Let
\{n_{l}\}_{l}
be an increasing sequence of positive integers to be specified later according to the majorant
w

\mu(\partial\mathbb{D})=1
and consider the subcollection

of every other dyadic arc of generation
n_{1}

and
\text{dist}(I^{(1)}_{j},I^{(1)}_{j+1})=2^{-n_{1}}
for any
j
. On these arcs, we set
\mu(I^{(1)}_{j})=2|I^{(1)}_{j}|
for each
j
, and note that

\sum_{j}\mu(I^{(1)}_{j})=2\sum_{j}|I^{(1)}_{j}|=1.

Hence
\mu
spreads its mass precisely on the collection
G_{1}

G_{2}=\{I^{2}_{j}\}_{j}
of dyadic arcs of generation
n_{2}
contained in

I^{(1)}_{k}

n_{2}
to include in our subcollection. Thus
|I^{(2)}_{j}|=2^{-n_{2}}
and
\text{dist}(I^{(2)}_{j},I^{(2)}_{j+1})\geq 2^{-n_{2}}
for each
j
. With this at hand, we now set
\mu(I^{(2)}_{j})=2^{2}|I^{(2}_{j}|
for each

and observe that for any arc

\sum_{I^{(2)}_{j}\subset I^{1}_{k}}\mu(I^{(2)}_{j})=2^{2}\sum_{I^{(2)}_{j}\subset I^{(1)}_{k}}|I^{(2)}_{j}|=2|I^{(1)}_{k}|=\mu(I^{(1)}_{k}).

Hence inside each
I^{(1)}_{k}
, we again evenly distribute the mass of
\mu
on every other dyadic arc of generation
n_{2}
, and denote this joint collection by
G_{2}=\{I^{(2)}_{j}\}
.
We proceed by induction. Assume the first
l-1

of every other dyadic arc contained in an arc of

Note also that for each

\sum_{k}|I^{(l)}_{k}|=2^{-l}.

(9)

Now extend

to a Borel measure on

Step 2: Verifying condition

We shall now specify a choice for the sequence of positive integers

With this choice, we have

We first check that condition (i) holds for dyadic arcs
I

and pick a positive integer

where in the last step we have used the property that

whenever

holds.

Step 3: Verifying condition
(ii)

To this end, for each pair of positive integers

\Omega^{(l)}_{k}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:2^{-n_{l+1}}<1-|z|\leq 2^{-n_{l}},\,z/|z|\in I^{(l)}_{k}\right\}

of ?THM?

\sum_{k,l}\int_{\Omega^{(l)}_{k}}\left\lvert S_{\mu}^{\prime}(z)\right\rvert dA(z)<\infty.

(10)

For

(I)
. To this end, we shall decompose it into two pieces by rewriting

(I)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j:I^{(l+1)}_{j}\subseteq I^{(l)}_{k}\end{subarray}}\int_{I^{(l+1)}_{j}}\frac{2\zeta}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}2^{l+1}dm(\zeta)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j:I^{(l+1)}_{j}\subseteq I^{(l)}_{k}\end{subarray}}\int_{I^{(l+1)}_{j}}\frac{2\zeta}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}\left(d\mu(\zeta)-2^{l+1}dm(\zeta)\right)\\
=A+B.

Denote by
\xi^{(l+1)}_{j}
the center of
I^{(l+1)}_{j}
and observe that since
\mu(I^{(l+1)}_{j})=2^{l+1}|I^{(l+1)}_{j}|
, we can add a cancellative term to
B
and write

B=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j:I^{(l+1)}_{j}\subseteq I^{(l)}_{k}\end{subarray}}\int_{I^{(l+1)}_{j}}\left(\frac{2\zeta}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}-\frac{2\xi^{(l+1)}_{j}}{(\xi^{(l+1)}_{j}-z)^{2}}\right)\left(d\mu(\zeta)-2^{l+1}dm(\zeta)\right).

Now using the mean value Theorem together and the fact that
|\zeta-z|\gtrsim|\xi^{(l+1)}_{j}-z|
whenever
\zeta\in I^{(l+1)}_{j}
and
z\in\Omega_{k}^{(l)}
, we get

|B|\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j:I^{(l+1)}_{j}\subseteq I^{(l)}_{k}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(I^{(l+1)}_{j})+2^{l+1}|I^{(l+1)}_{j}|}{|\xi^{(l+1)}_{j}-z|^{3}}|I^{(l+1)}_{j}|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j:I^{(l+1)}_{j}\subseteq I^{(l)}_{k}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{l+2}|I^{(l+1)}_{j}|^{2}}{|\xi^{(l+1)}_{j}-z|^{3}}.

(13)

A straightforward calculation shows that

\int_{\Omega_{k}^{(l)}}\frac{dA(z)}{|\xi^{(l+1)}_{j}-z|^{3}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|I^{(l+1)}_{j}|}.

(14)

Combining this with (
13
), we conclude

\int_{\Omega_{k}^{(l)}}|B|dA\lesssim 2^{l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j:I^{(l+1)}_{j}\subseteq I^{(l)}_{k}\end{subarray}}|I^{(l+1)}_{j}|\leq 2^{l}|I^{(l)}_{k}|.

Hence it only remains to estimate the quantity
A
. Denote by
I+|I|
the arc
I
rotated by
|I|
-units and observe that by construction of the dyadic arcs
I^{(l+1)}_{j}
, we have

\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j:I^{(l+1)}_{j}\subseteq I^{(l)}_{k}\end{subarray}}I^{(l+1)}_{j}\cup\left(I^{(l+1)}_{j}+|I^{(l+1)}_{j}|\right)=I^{(l)}_{k}.

Applying the mean value Theorem as before, we get

\left\lvert\int_{I^{(l+1)}_{j}}\frac{\zeta dm(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}-\int_{I^{(l+1)}_{j}+|I^{(l+1)}_{j}|}\frac{\zeta dm(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}\right\rvert\lesssim\frac{|I^{(l+1)}_{j}|^{2}}{|z-\xi^{(l+1)}_{j}|^{3}},\quad z\in\Omega_{k}^{(l)},\quad j,l,k\geq 1.

Hence

\int_{\Omega^{(l)}_{k}}\left\lvert A-2^{l}\int_{I^{(l)}_{k}}\frac{2\zeta dm(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}\right\rvert dA(z)\lesssim\\
\int_{\Omega^{(l)}_{k}}2^{l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j:I^{(l+1)}_{j}\subseteq I^{(l)}_{k}\end{subarray}}\left\lvert\int_{I^{(l+1)}_{j}}\frac{\zeta dm(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}-\int_{I^{(l+1)}_{j}+|I^{(l+1)}_{j}|}\frac{\zeta dm(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}\right\rvert dA(z)\lesssim\\
2^{l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j:I^{(l+1)}_{j}\subseteq I^{(l)}_{k}\end{subarray}}|I^{(l+1)}_{j}|^{2}\int_{\Omega^{(l)}_{k}}\frac{dA(z)}{\left\lvert\xi^{l+1}_{j}-z\right\rvert^{3}}\lesssim 2^{l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j:I^{(l+1)}_{j}\subseteq I^{(l)}_{k}\end{subarray}}|I^{(l+1)}_{j}|\leq 2^{l}|I^{l}_{k}|.

In the penultimate step, we have used (
14
). In order to finish the proof, it just remains to verify that

\int_{\Omega^{(l)}_{k}}\left\lvert\int_{I^{(l)}_{k}}\frac{\zeta dm(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}\right\rvert dA(z)\lesssim|I^{(l)}_{k}|.

(15)

To this end, let
I^{(l)}_{k}=(a^{(l)}_{k},b^{(l)}_{k})
and observe that fixed
z\in\mathbb{D}
, the primitive of
\zeta/(\zeta-z)^{2}
is explicitly given by
\log(\zeta-z)-z/(\zeta-z)
, which allows us to compute

\int_{I^{(l)}_{k}}\frac{\zeta dm(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}=\log\left(\frac{b^{(l)}_{k}-z}{a_{k}^{(l)}-z}\right)+\frac{z|I^{(l)}_{k}|}{(b^{(l)}_{k}-z)(a_{k}^{(l)}-z)}.

Then

\left\lvert\int_{I^{(l)}_{k}}\frac{\zeta dm(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}\right\rvert\lesssim\frac{|I^{(l)}_{k}|}{|b^{(l)}_{k}-z||a_{k}^{(l)}-z|},\qquad z\in\Omega^{(l)}_{k},

and thus

\int_{\Omega^{(l)}_{k}}\left\lvert\int_{I^{(l)}_{k}}\frac{\zeta dm(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}\right\rvert dA(z)\lesssim|I^{(l)}_{k}|\int_{\Omega_{k}^{(l)}}\frac{dA(z)}{|b^{(l)}_{k}-z||a_{k}^{(l)}-z|}.

Since either
|z-b_{k}^{(l)}|\geq|I^{(l)}_{k}|/2
or
|z-a_{k}^{(l)}|\geq|I^{(l)}_{k}|/2
for any
z\in\Omega^{(l)}_{k}
, we deduce

\int_{\Omega_{k}^{(l)}}\frac{dA(z)}{|b^{(l)}_{k}-z||a_{k}^{(l)}-z|}\lesssim 1.

This gives (
15
) and finishes the proof.
∎

4.4
The permanence property discriminates no compact set

Here we devote our attention to the proof of ?THM?
, which roughly asserts that any compact set on the unit circle of Lebesgue measure zero can support a singular measure
\mu
for which the associated singular inner function is a member of
W^{1}
. This implies that no condition on the carrier set of a singular measure
\mu
alone can describe the permanence property of
S_{\mu}
in the Bloch space.

Proof of ?THM?
.

The proof is divided in three steps.

Step 1: Constructing a covering of
E

We first construct by induction an appropriate sequence of coverings of
E
by dyadic arcs. Set
\mathcal{G}_{0}:=\{\partial\mathbb{D}\}
and assume that a covering
\mathcal{G}_{k}=\{I^{(k)}_{j}:j\geq 1\}
of
E
by dyadic arcs has already been constructed
and
E\cap I^{(k)}_{j}\neq\emptyset
for any
j\geq 1
. For any
I\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
we denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}(I)
the collection of maximal dyadic subarcs
\widetilde{J}
of
I
such that at least one of the two dyadic children of
\widetilde{J}
does not intersect
E
. Note that
\widetilde{G}(I)=\{I\}
if one of the two dyadic children of
I\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
does not intersect
E
. Moreover, by maximality of the collection
\widetilde{G}(I)
, for each
\widetilde{J}\in\widetilde{G}(I)
there exists exactly one dyadic child
J
of
\widetilde{J}
with
J\cap E\neq\emptyset
.
Now consider

\mathcal{G}(I):=\left\{J:J\,\,\text{dyadic child of some}\,\,\widetilde{J}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}(I),\text{ with }J\cap E\neq\emptyset\right\}.

The set
\mathcal{G}_{k+1}:=\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{G}_{k}}\mathcal{G}(I)
is again a covering of
E
that will be denoted by
\mathcal{G}_{k+1}=\{I^{(k+1)}_{j}:j\geq 1\}.
Since
E
is a closed set of Lebesgue measure zero, for any arc
I\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
, the union of the arcs of the collection
\widetilde{G}(I)
covers almost every point of
I
. Hence the collection
\{\mathcal{G}_{k}\}_{k}
satisfies the following packing condition: for any
I^{(k)}_{l}\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in\mathcal{G}_{k+1}\\
J\subset I^{(k)}_{l}\end{subarray}}|J|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 1\\
I^{(k+1)}_{j}\subseteq I^{(k)}_{l}\end{subarray}}|I^{(k+1)}_{j}|=\frac{|I^{(k)}_{l}|}{2},\qquad k,l\geq 1.

(16)

Observe that by construction
E=\cap_{k}\cup_{J\in\mathcal{G}_{k}}J
. Indeed, the inclusion
\subseteq
follows from the fact that each
\mathcal{G}_{k}
is a covering of
E
, while if
\zeta_{0}\notin E
, then there exists a dyadic arc
I_{0}
containing
\zeta_{0}
with
I_{0}\cap E=\emptyset
. Hence for every sufficiently large
k
, we must have
\zeta_{0}\in I_{0}\subset\cap_{J\in\mathcal{G}_{k}}\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus J
, which establishes the reverse inclusion
\supseteq
.

Step 2: Constructing the measure
\mu
:

We now construct
\mu
by declaring its mass on dyadic arcs. Initially, we set
\mu(\partial\mathbb{D})=1
. Now let
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
be a dyadic arc and assume, by means of induction, that
\mu(I)
has already been defined in such a way that
\mu(I)=0
whenever
I\cap E=\emptyset
. Denote by
I_{+},I_{-}
the dyadic children of
I
. If both
I_{+},I_{-}
happens to meet
E
, then we distribute the mass evenly by declaring

\frac{\mu(I_{+})}{|I_{+}|}=\frac{\mu(I_{-})}{|I_{-}|}=\frac{\mu(I)}{|I|}.

If not, say
I_{+}\cap E=\emptyset
, then we distribute all the mass to
I_{-}
by setting

\frac{\mu(I_{-})}{|I_{-}|}=2\frac{\mu(I)}{|I|},\qquad\mu(I_{+})=0.

Indeed, this is consistent since

\frac{\mu(I)}{|I|}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mu(I_{+})}{|I_{+}|}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mu(I_{-})}{|I_{-}|}.

Observe that by construction, the support of
\mu
is equal to
E=\cap_{k}\cup_{J\in\mathcal{G}_{k}}J
which has Lebesgue measure zero. Hence
\mu
is a non-trivial singular measure. Moreover, it is clear that for any dyadic arc
I
with
I\cap E\neq\emptyset
, we have
\mu(I)\geq|I|
. Observe also that by construction, for any
I^{(k)}_{j}\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
, we have

\frac{\mu(I^{(k)}_{j})}{|I^{(k)}_{j}|}\geq 2^{k},\quad j,k\geq 1.

(17)

Step 3: Verifying condition
(ii)

It now remains to prove that the associated singular inner function
S_{\mu}
belongs to
W^{1}
. To this end, for each
k\geq 1
and
I^{(k)}_{j}\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
consider the region

\Omega^{(k)}_{j}:=Q_{I^{(k)}_{j}}\setminus\bigcup_{\widetilde{J}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}(I^{(k)}_{j})}Q_{\widetilde{J}}.

According to (iii) of ?THM?
, it is sufficient to establish that

\sum_{k,j}\int_{\Omega^{(k)}_{j}}\left\lvert S_{\mu}^{\prime}\right\rvert dA<\infty.

Next we shall make yet another reduction. Let
I=I^{(k)}_{j}
and denote by
\mu_{I}
the restriction of
\mu
to
I
. According to ?THM?
, there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that

\int_{Q_{I}}|S^{\prime}_{\mu_{\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus I}}(z)|dA(z)\leq C|I|.

Hence using (
16
), we get

\sum_{k,j}\int_{\Omega^{(k)}_{j}}|S^{\prime}_{\mu_{\partial\mathbb{D}\setminus I^{(k)}_{j}}}(z)|dA(z)\leq C\sum_{k,j}|I^{(k)}_{j}|=\sum_{k\geq 1}2^{-k}=C.

With this observation at hand, it then suffices to show that

\sum_{k,j}\int_{\Omega^{(k)}_{j}}|S_{\mu_{I^{(k)}_{j}}}^{\prime}(z)|dA(z)<\infty.

(18)

Using (
17
) in conjunction with standard estimates of Poisson kernels, there exists a universal constant
c>0
such that

|S_{\mu_{I^{(k)}_{j}}}^{\prime}(z)|\leq e^{-c2^{k}}|H(\mu_{I^{(k)}_{j}})^{\prime}(z)|,\quad z\in\Omega^{(k)}_{j},k,j\geq 1.

(19)

Thus (
18
) follows from the estimate

\int_{\Omega^{(k)}_{j}}|H(\mu_{I^{(k)}_{j}})^{\prime}(z)|dA(z)\lesssim\mu(I^{(k)}_{j})\quad j,k\geq 1.

whose proof is quite similar to the proof of estimate (
12
). We omit the details.

∎

5
Cyclicity and invertibility

5.1
A sufficient condition for cyclicity

This subsection is devoted to proving ?THM?
. A finite complex Borel measure
\nu
on
\partial\mathbb{D}
(not necessarily singular) is said to be a
Zygmund measure
if

\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert_{*}:=\,\sup\,\left\lvert\frac{\nu(I)}{|I|}-\frac{\nu(I^{\prime})}{|I^{\prime}|}\right\rvert<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all pairs of contiguous arcs
I,I^{\prime}
of the same length. For the sake of future references, we shall state the following result due to Duren, Shapiro and Shields in
[
16
]
below, see also
[
15
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

A finite complex Borel measure
\nu
on
\partial\mathbb{D}
is a Zygmund measure if and only if its Herglotz transform defined as

H(\nu)(z)=\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}d\nu(\zeta),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

belongs to
\mathcal{B}
. In this case, there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that

\left\lvert H(\nu)(z)-H(\nu)(w)\right\rvert\leq C\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert_{*}\beta(z,w),\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D},

where
\beta(z,w)
denotes the hyperbolic distance on
\mathbb{D}
between the points
z
and
w
. Moreover, the following asymptotic relation holds:

P(\nu)(z)=\frac{\nu(I_{z})}{|I_{z}|}+\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert_{*},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

(20)

where
I_{z}\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
denotes the arc centered at
z/|z|
of length
1-|z|
.

We will also use an auxiliary result that has appeared in the setting of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in
[
15
]
. We shall need it in the context of the unit circle where its proof is an exercise left to the reader.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\nu
be a finite Borel measure on
\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then there exists an absolute constant
C>0
, such that

(1-|z|)\left\lvert H(\nu)^{\prime}(z)\right\rvert\leq C\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{(1-|z|)}{|e^{it}-|z||^{3}}\left\lvert\nu(I(z,t))-\nu(I(z,-t))\right\rvert dt,\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

where
I(z,t)
denotes the smallest arc joining
z/|z|
to
ze^{it}/|z|
if
z\neq 0
and
I(0,t)
is the arc joining 1 and
e^{it}
.

Given an arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
and
C>0
let
CI
denotethe arc with the same center as
I
and length
C|I|
. We will also need the following technical result.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\nu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
\partial\mathbb{D}
. Assume there exists a constant
C=C(\nu)>0
such that

\left\lvert\frac{\nu(I)}{|I|}-\frac{\nu(I^{\prime})}{|I^{\prime}|}\right\rvert\leq Ce^{-\nu(I)/|I|},

(21)

for any pair of contiguous arcs
I,I^{\prime}\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
of the same length. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any integer
n
with
|n|\leq\delta e^{\nu(I)/|I|}
we have

\left\lvert\frac{\nu(2^{n}I)}{|2^{n}I|}-\frac{\nu(I)}{|I|}\right\rvert\leq\min\{\varepsilon n,1\}.

(22)

Proof.

Note that the assumption gives that
\nu
is a Zygmund measure. Moreover (
21
) gives that there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\left\lvert\frac{\nu(2I)}{2I}-\frac{\nu(I)}{|I|}\right\rvert\leq C_{1}e^{-\max\{\nu(2I)/|2I|,\nu(I)/|I|\}},

for any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
. Using the elementary estimate
\left\lvert e^{x}-e^{y}\right\rvert\leq e^{\max\{x,y\}}|x-y|
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, we deduce

\left\lvert e^{\nu(2I)/|2I|}-e^{\nu(I)/|I|}\right\rvert\leq C_{1},

for any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
. Hence for any integer
k
we have

\left\lvert e^{\nu(2^{k}I)/|2^{k}I|}-e^{\nu(I)/|I|}\right\rvert\leq C_{1}|k|.

(23)

Now if
|n|\leq(2C_{1})^{-1}e^{\nu(I)/|I|}
, we obtain

\left\lvert e^{\nu(2^{n}I)/|2^{n}I|}-e^{\nu(I)/|I|}\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{\nu(I)/|I|}.

(24)

Hence

\left\lvert\frac{\nu(2^{n}I)}{|2^{n}I|}-\frac{\nu(I)}{|I|}\right\rvert\leq 1.

Note that to check (
22
) we can assume that
\nu(I)/|I|
is large. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Let
M=M(\varepsilon)>0
be a large number to be fixed later. Use (
23
) to pick
\delta>0
such that
\nu(2^{k}I)/|2^{k}I|>M
if
k
is an integer with
|k|<\delta e^{\nu(I)/|I|}
. Then assumption (
21
) gives that

\left\lvert\frac{\nu(2^{k}I)}{|2^{k}I|}-\frac{\nu(2^{k-1}I)}{|2^{k-1}I|}\right\rvert\lesssim e^{-M},\quad\text{ if }|k|\leq\delta e^{\nu(I)/|I|}.

We deduce that

\left\lvert\frac{\nu(2^{n}I)}{|2^{n}I|}-\frac{\nu(I)}{|I|}\right\rvert\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\lvert\frac{\nu(2^{k}I)}{|2^{k}I|}-\frac{\nu(2^{k-1}I)}{|2^{k-1}I|}\right\rvert\lesssim e^{-M}n,\quad\text{ if }|n|\leq\delta e^{\nu(I)/|I|}.

and taking
M\lesssim\log(1/\varepsilon)
, estimate (
22
) follows.
∎

We are now ready to prove ?THM?
.

Proof of ?THM?
.

It is sufficient to find a sequence of bounded analytic functions
h_{n}
such that:

(i)

\sup_{n}\left\lVert h_{n}f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}<\infty
,

(ii)

h_{n}(z)f(z)\to 1
for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Set
r_{n}:=1-2^{-n}
and consider
h_{n}(z):=1/f(r_{n}z)
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Note that condition
(ii)
is trivially satisfied, hence it remains to verify
(i)
. Observe that we can write

(1-|z|)\left(\frac{f(z)}{f(r_{n}z)}\right)^{\prime}=(1-|z|)\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(r_{n}z)}\right)-(1-|z|)\left(\frac{r_{n}f(z)f^{\prime}(r_{n}z)}{f^{2}(r_{n}z)}\right)=:A+B.

We proceed by estimating both terms separately.

Step 1: Estimation of B

Set
f=\exp(-H(\nu))
where
H(\nu)
is the Herglotz transform of
the Herglotz-Nevanlinna measure
\nu
of
f
. Observing that
\nu
is necessarily a Zygmund measure, we may apply ?THM?
to deduce

(1-|r_{n}z|)\left\lvert\frac{r_{n}f^{\prime}(r_{n}z)}{f(r_{n}z)}\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert H(\nu)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}\lesssim\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert_{*},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

and thus

\left\lvert B\right\rvert\lesssim\frac{1-|z|}{1-|r_{n}z|}\frac{\left\lvert f(z)\right\rvert}{\left\lvert f(r_{n}z)\right\rvert}=\frac{1-|z|}{1-|r_{n}z|}\exp\left(P(\nu)(r_{n}z)-P(\nu)(z)\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

(25)

where
P(\nu)
denotes the Poisson extension of
\nu
. Now for
|z|\leq r_{n}
, we clearly have
\beta(z,r_{n}z)\lesssim 1
. An application of ?THM?
then gives
\left\lvert P(\nu)(r_{n}z)-P(\nu)(z)\right\rvert\lesssim 1
if
|z|\leq r_{n}
.
and hence

\sup_{|z|\leq r_{n}}|B|\lesssim 1.

We may thus fix
z\in\mathbb{D}
with
|z|\geq r_{n}
. Note that in order to estimate the right hand side of (
25
), we may without loss of generality assume that
P(\nu)(r_{n}z)
is large, which according to ?THM?
is equivalent to
\nu(I_{r_{n}z})/|I_{r_{n}z}|
being large. Doing so, we pick a large number
R>0
for which
\nu(I_{r_{n}z})/|I_{r_{n}z}|>R
, to be specified momentarily. Observe that condition
(
1
)
, then implies that

\left\lvert\frac{\nu(I)}{|I|}-\frac{\nu(I/2)}{|I/2|}\right\rvert\leq Ce^{-R}

(26)

for any arc
I\subseteq I_{r_{n}z}
. Let
N:=N(n,z)
denote the largest positive integer satisfying

N\leq\log\left(\frac{|I_{r_{n}z}|}{|I_{z}|}\right)=\log\left(\frac{1-|r_{n}z|}{1-|z|}\right).

By means of iterating the estimate (
26
)
N
times, we obtain

\left\lvert\frac{\nu(I_{z})}{|I_{z}|}-\frac{\nu(I_{r_{n}z})}{|I_{r_{n}z}|}\right\rvert\leq CNe^{-R}\leq Ce^{-R}\log\left(\frac{1-|r_{n}z|}{1-|z|}\right).

Yet another application of ?THM?
allows us to express

P(\nu)(r_{n}z)-P(\nu)(z)=\frac{\nu(I_{r_{n}z})}{|I_{r_{n}z}|}-\frac{\nu(I_{z})}{|I_{z}|}+\mathcal{O}(\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert_{*})

and thus we get

\left\lvert P(\nu)(r_{n}z)-P(\nu)(z)\right\rvert\leq Ce^{-R}\log\left(\frac{1-|r_{n}z|}{1-|z|}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert_{*}).

Now it is just a matter of choosing
R>0
sufficiently large so that
Ce^{-R}<1
, which ultimately yields

|B|\lesssim\left(\frac{1-|z|}{1-|r_{n}z|}\right)^{1-Ce^{-R}}\lesssim 1.

Step 2: Estimation of A

By definition, we readily have

\left\lvert A\right\rvert\lesssim(1-|z|)\left\lvert H(\nu)^{\prime}(z)\right\rvert\exp\left(P(\nu)(r_{n}z)-P(\nu)(z)\right),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

(27)

As observed previously
\beta(z,r_{n}z)\lesssim 1
whenever
|z|\leq r_{n}
. Hence using that
\nu
is a Zygmund measure, we have
(1-|z|)|H(\mu)^{\prime}(z)|\lesssim 1
and
|P(\nu)(r_{n}z)-P(\nu)(z)|\lesssim 1
if
|z|\leq r_{n}
. This gives

\sup_{|z|\leq r_{n}}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\lesssim 1.

In what follows, we shall thus fix
z\in\mathbb{D}
with
|z|>r_{n}
and moreover, we may also assume that
P(\nu)(r_{n}z)
is sufficiently large. Denote by
I(z,t)
the smallest arc on
\partial\mathbb{D}
joining
z/|z|
with
ze^{it}/|z|
. An application of ?THM?
gives

(1-|z|)\left\lvert H(\nu)^{\prime}(z)\right\rvert\lesssim\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{(1-|z|)}{|e^{it}-|z||^{3}}\left\lvert\nu(I(z,t))-\nu(I(z,-t))\right\rvert dt.

Decomposing the integral according to the intervals
I(k)=\{t\in[0,2\pi]:|t|\leq 2^{k}(1-|z|)\}
, we get

(1-|z|)\left\lvert H(\nu)^{\prime}(z)\right\rvert\lesssim(1-|z|)^{-2}\int_{I(1)}\left\lvert\nu(I(z,t))-\nu(I(z,-t))\right\rvert dt\\
+\sum_{k>0}2^{-3k}(1-|z|)^{-2}\int_{I(k+1)\setminus I(k)}\left\lvert\nu(I(z,t))-\nu(I(z,-t))\right\rvert dt.

Let
T=T(n,z)
denote the integer part of
\log_{2}(2^{-n}/(1-|z|))
. Observe that if
t\in I(k)
with
k\leq T
, then
I(z,t)
is contained in a fixed multiple of
I_{r_{n}z}
. This in conjunction with the assumption (

) yields

Hence we get

\sum_{0\leq k<T}2^{-3k}(1-|z|)^{-2}\int_{I(k)}\left\lvert\nu(I(z,t))-\nu(I(z,-t))\right\rvert dt\lesssim\exp\left(-\frac{\nu(I_{r_{n}z}}{|I_{r_{n}z}|}\right).

Fix

with

The assumption (

\left\lvert\frac{\nu(I(z,t))}{|I(z,t)|}-\frac{\nu(I(z,-t))}{|I(z,-t)|}\right\rvert\lesssim\exp\left(-\frac{\nu(I(z,t)}{|I(z,t)|}\right).

Using estimate (

\left\lvert\frac{\nu(I(z,t))}{|I(z,t)|}-\frac{\nu(I(z,-t))}{|I(z,-t)|}\right\rvert\lesssim\exp\left(-\frac{\nu(I_{r_{n}z})}{|I_{r_{n}z}|}\right),\quad\text{ if }\log_{2}(\frac{2^{k}(1-|z|)}{2^{-n}})\leq\delta e^{\frac{\nu(I_{r_{n}z})}{|I_{r_{n}z}|}}.

Let

On the other hand, using that

\sum_{k>T^{*}}2^{-3k}(1-|z|)^{-2}\int_{I(k+1)}\left\lvert\nu(I(z,t))-\nu(I(z,-t))\right\rvert dt\lesssim\exp\left(-\frac{\nu(I_{r_{n}z})}{|I_{r_{n}z}|}\right).

With these estimates at hand, we may now return back to (

5.3
Invertibility does not imply cyclicity in
\mathcal{B}

This section is devoted to a self-contained proof of ?THM?
.

Proof of ?THM?
.

Let
E
be a sequence of complex numbers containing the origin such that both
\mathbb{C}\setminus E
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus E^{-1}
are open sets which do not contain arbitrarily large discs, that is, there exists
R_{0}>0
such that no disc of radius
R_{0}
is contained in either
\mathbb{C}\setminus E
or
\mathbb{C}\setminus E^{-1}
. Here
E^{-1}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:1/w\in E\}
. Let
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbf{C}\setminus E
be an analytic universal covering map. According to Bloch’s Theorem, we have that both
f
and
1/f
belong to
\mathcal{B}
. Next we shall show that
f
is not cyclic in
\mathcal{B}
.

Assume
f
has non-tangential limit, say
L
, at a point of the unit circle. Since
f
is a covering map,
L
must be either infinity or a point in
E
. Since
E
is countable, Privalov’s Theorem gives that
f
can only have non-tangential limit at a set of Lebesgue measure zero of points of the unit circle. By Plessner’s Theorem (for instance, see p. 205 in
[
21
]
), its non-tangential cluster set at almost every point of the unit circle is the whole extended plane, that is,

\overline{{\cap}_{0<r<1}f(\Gamma_{r}(\xi))}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\},\quad m\text{- a.e. }\xi\in\partial\mathbb{D}.

(34)

Here
\Gamma_{r}(\xi)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z-\xi|<M(1-|z|),|z|>r\}
denotes the truncated Stolz angle of some fixed aperture
M>1
with vertex at the point
\xi\in\partial\mathbb{D}
.

Next we will show that
\mathcal{A}(f)=\{fh\in\mathcal{B}:h\in H^{\infty}\}
is weak-star closed in
\mathcal{B}
. Since
\mathcal{A}(f)
is convex, it suffices by the Krein-Smulian Theorem to show that it is weak-star sequentially closed. To this end, let
h_{n}\in H^{\infty}
such that
fh_{n}
tends to
F
weak-star in
\mathcal{B}
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left\lvert(1-|z|^{2})(f^{\prime}(z)h_{n}(z)+f(z)h^{\prime}_{n}(z))\right\rvert\leq C.

(35)

Fix
a\in\mathbf{C}\setminus E
and use (
34
) to show that for almost every
\xi\in\partial\mathbb{D}
there exist points
z_{k}(\xi)\in\mathbb{D}
tending non-tangentially to
\xi
such that
f(z_{k}(\xi))\to a
. Since
(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|\geq\text{dist}(f(z),E)
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
(see
[
5
]
), we deduce that there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\liminf_{k\to\infty}(1-|z_{k}(\xi)|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z_{k}(\xi))|>c

(36)

Note that Bloch’s Theorem shows that if an analytic function
F
in the unit disc has non-tangential limit at a given point
\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, then
(1-|z|)|F^{\prime}(z)|\to 0
as
z\in\mathbb{D}
tends non-tangentially to
\zeta
. Since
h_{n}
has non-tangential limit along almost every radius, for each integer
n
we have

\lim(1-|z|)|h^{\prime}_{n}(z)|=0,\quad m\text{- a.e. }\xi\in\partial\mathbb{D},

where the limit is taken as
z\in\mathbb{D}
tends to
\xi
non-tangentially. Applying (
35
) and (
36
) we deduce that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}|h_{n}(z_{k}(\xi))|\leq C,\quad m\text{- a.e. }\xi\in\partial\mathbb{D},\quad n=1,2,\ldots.

Hence
\|h_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq C
,
n=1,2,\ldots
and we may extract a subsequence of
\{h_{n}\}
which converges pointwise in
\mathbb{D}
to a function
h\in H^{\infty}
and thus
F=fh
. We conclude that
\mathcal{A}(f)
is weak-star closed in
\mathcal{B}
. Now
\left[f\right]_{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}(f)
and it is clear that
\mathcal{A}(f)
is a proper subspace of
\mathcal{B}
. We conclude that
f
is not cyclic in
\mathcal{B}
.

∎

A problem of weak-star sequential closure in
\mathcal{B}

In the proof of ?THM?
we will need to construct functions in
\mathcal{B}
which enjoy maximal radial growth at considerable large subset of
\mathbb{D}

Proposition 5.4
.

There exists
f\in\text{BMOA}\subset\mathcal{B}
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

The function
1/f
belongs to
H^{\infty}
.

(ii)

For some
\delta>0
, the set
E(f):=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\left\lvert f(z)\right\rvert\geq\delta\log(\frac{1}{1-|z|})\right\}

accumulates everywhere on the unit circle
\partial\mathbb{D}
, that is,
\partial\mathbb{D}\subset\overline{E(f)}
.

Observe that such an
f
must necessarily be outer, hence it is cyclic
\mathcal{B}
. The set of points for which the Bloch function
f
attains its maximal growth are located in the set
E(f)
. In the context of cyclic vectors in growth spaces, Hedenmalm and Borichev observed, roughly speaking that cyclic functions therein cannot grow ”maximally” on too ”massive” sets. See
[
9
]
. Our construction below is deeply inspired from this idea. Taking ?THM?
for granted, we first deduce ?THM?
.

Proof of ?THM?
.

Let
f\in BMOA
be given by Proposition
. Let
\{Q_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence of analytic polynomials with
\sup_{n}\left\lVert fQ_{n}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}<\infty
. It follows that

\sup_{n}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\log^{-1}\left(\frac{e}{1-|z|}\right)\left\lvert f(z)Q_{n}(z)\right\rvert<\infty.

However, this implies

\sup_{n}\sup_{z\in E(f)}\left\lvert Q_{n}(z)\right\rvert<\infty.

Now the assumption on
E(f)
in conjunction with the maximum principle implies that
\sup_{n}\left\lVert Q_{n}\right\rVert_{H^{\infty}}<\infty.
By Helly’s selection theorem, we can extract a subsequence
\{Q_{n_{k}}\}_{k}
which converges pointwise on
\mathbb{D}
to some function
h\in H^{\infty}
. Hence any weak star sequential limit of
\{fQ_{n}\}
is of the form
fh
with
h\in H^{\infty}
. But functions of that form have finite radial limits at
m
-a.e on
\partial\mathbb{D}
, hence the set
\{fh\in\mathcal{B}:h\in H^{\infty}\}
is a proper subspace of
\mathcal{B}
, which completes the proof.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
0<\delta<1
. Then there exists a positive finite Borel measure
\nu
on
\partial\mathbb{D}
satisfying the following property: For any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
there exists a dyadic subarc
J\subset I
, such that
\nu(J)\geq|J|^{1-\delta}.

Proof.

Fix
0<\eta<1
to be specifiec later. We shall define the measure
\nu
by declaring its mass on dyadic arcs. First we set
\nu(\partial\mathbb{D})=1
.
Inductively, assume that
\nu(J)
has already been defined for some dyadic arc
J\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
. We denote by
J_{+},J_{-}
the right and left dyadic children of
J
, respectively. We shall assign a larger portion of the available mass to the right child than to the left by declaring

\frac{\nu(J_{\pm})}{|J_{\pm}|}=(1\pm\eta)\frac{\nu(J)}{|J|}.

Now it is a standard fact in measure theory that this construction gives rise to a uniquely defined positive finite Borel measure
\nu
on
\partial\mathbb{D}
. Hence it suffices to verify that
\nu
has the required property. We may without loss of generality assume that
I
is a dyadic arc. Now let
J_{n}\subset I
be the rightmost dyadic subarc of
I
with
|J_{n}|=2^{-n}|I|
. Then by construction, we have

\frac{\nu(J_{n})}{|J_{n}|}=(1+\eta)^{n}\frac{\nu(I)}{|I|}\geq\frac{1}{|J_{n}|^{\delta}}=\frac{2^{\delta n}}{|I|^{\delta}}

whenever
(1+\eta)^{n}/2^{n\delta}\geq|I|^{1-\delta}/\nu(I)
. However, choosing
0<\eta<1
so that
2^{\delta}<1+\eta
, the above inequality will certainly hold for sufficiently large
n
. This finishes the proof.

Proof of ?THM?

Let
\nu
be the positive finite Borel measure given by ?THM?
. Consider an analytic selfmap
b=b_{\nu}
of
\mathbb{D}
defined via the Herglotz transform of

\frac{1+b(z)}{1-b(z)}=\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}d\nu(\zeta),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

In other words,
\nu
is the so-called Aleksandrov-Clark measure for

Note that since
f

1/f\in H^{\infty}

E(f)
contains the entire unit circle. Indeed, the reason for our choice of
f
stems from the observation that the set
E(f)
contains

\widetilde{E}(b):=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1+b(z)}{1-b(z)}\right)\geq(1-|z|)^{-\delta}\right\},

thus it suffices to check that the closure of the smaller set
\widetilde{E}(b)
contains
\partial\mathbb{D}
. To this end, fix a small
\varepsilon>0
and let
\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be an arbitrary point. If
0<\delta^{\prime}<1
is fixed, then an application of ?THM?
shows that for any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
containing
\zeta
with length at most
\varepsilon/2
, there exists a small dyadic arc

(not necessarily containing
\zeta

. Let
\xi_{J}
denote the center of the arc

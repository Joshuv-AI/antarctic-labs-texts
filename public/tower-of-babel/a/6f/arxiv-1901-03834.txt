On the projections of the multifractal packing dimension for q > 1 q>1
Bilel Selmi

Abstract
The aim of this article is to study the behavior of the multifractal packing function B μ ​ ( q ) B_{\mu}(q) under projections in Euclidean space for q > 1 q>1 . We show that B μ ​ ( q ) B_{\mu}(q) is preserved under almost every orthogonal projection. As an application, we study the multifractal analysis of the projections of a measure. In particular, we obtain general results for the multifractal analysis of the orthogonal projections on m m -dimensional linear subspaces of a measure μ \mu satisfying the multifractal formalism.

We start by recalling the multifractal formalism
introduced by O’Neil in

The functions

and

family of Borel subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[

3
Main result

Let
\mu
be a compactly supported probability measure on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

an alternative characterization of the generalized upper

-spectrum of

&x&\longmapsto&\min\Big\{1,\>r^{s}|x|^{-s}\Big\},\end{array}

and

\mu\ast\phi_{r}^{s}(x)=\int\min\Big\{1,\>r^{s}|x-y|^{-s}\Big\}d\mu(y).

Let

be a compact
subset of
\supp\mu
. For
1\leq s\leq n
and

, write

N_{\mu,r}^{q,s}(E)=\int_{E}\Big(\mu\ast\phi_{r/3}^{s}(x)\Big)^{q-1}d\mu(x),

and

\overline{\tau}_{\mu}^{q,s}(E)=\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log N_{\mu,r}^{q,s}(E)}{-\log r}\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{\tau}_{\mu}^{q,s}(E)=\displaystyle\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log N_{\mu,r}^{q,s}(E)}{-\log r}.

The definition of these dimensions is, frankly, messy, indirect and
unappealing. In an attempt to make the concept more attractive, we
present here an alternative approach to the dimension

\overline{\tau}_{\mu}^{q,s}
and his application to projections in
terms of a potential obtained by convolving
\mu
with a certain
kernel. For
E
a compact subset of
\supp\mu
we can try to
decompose
E
into a countable number of pieces

in
such a way that the largest piece has as small a dimension as
possible. The present approach was first used by Falconer in

[

and further developed by O’Neil in

[
28

. This idea leads to the following modified
dimension in terms of the convolutions:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{\mu}^{q,s}(E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\sup_{1\leq i<\infty}\overline{\tau}_{\mu}^{q,s}(E_{i}),\;\;E\subset\bigcup_{i}E_{i}\;\;\text{with each $E_{i}$ compact }\right\}

and

\mathfrak{T}_{\mu}^{s}(q)=\mathfrak{T}_{\mu}^{q,s}(\supp\mu)\quad\text{for
all}\quad s\geq 1.

-dimensional linear subspaces of

. Also, for a Borel probability measure
\mu

with compact support

and for

, we denote by

, the projection of

,

Since

whenever these integrals exist. Then for all

,

and

, we have

\mu\ast\phi_{r}^{m}(x)=\int\mu_{V}(B(x_{V},r))dV=\int\min\Big\{1,\>r^{m}|x-y|^{-m}\Big\}d\mu(y).

-dimensional

5
Application

When
\mu
obeys the multifractal formalism over some interval, we
are interested in knowing whether or not this property is preserved
after orthogonal projections on
\gamma_{n,m}
-almost every linear

m
-dimensional subspaces.

This section is devoted to study the behavior of projections of
measures obeying to the multifractal formalism. More precisely, we
prove that for
q>1
if the multifractal formalism holds for
\mu

at
\alpha=-B^{\prime}_{\mu}(q)
, it holds for
\mu_{V}
for

\gamma_{n,m}
-almost every
V\in G_{n,m}
. Before detailing our
results let us recall the multifractal formalism introduced by
O’Neil. For
\alpha\geq 0
, let

{E}_{\mu}(\alpha)=\left\{x\in\supp\mu;\;\;\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\log\Big(\mu B(x,3r)\Big)}{\log r}=\alpha\right\}.

We mention that in the last decade there has been a great interest
for the multifractal analysis and positive results have been written
in various situations (see for example
[
4
,
5
,
26
,
27
]
).

The function
B_{\mu}(q)
is related to the multifractal spectrum of
the measure
\mu
. More precisely,

f^{*}(\alpha)=\displaystyle\inf_{\beta}\big(\alpha\beta+f(\beta)\big)

denotes the Legendre transform of the function
f,
it has been
proved in
[
4
,
5
,
26
,
27
]
a lower and upper bound estimate
of the singularity spectrum using the Legendre transform of the
function
B_{\mu}(q)
. The following theorem is a consequence of the
multifractal formalism developed in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 5.1

Let
\mu
be a compactly supported Borel probability measure on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
q\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that

1.

\mathscr{H}_{\mu}^{q,B_{\mu}(q)}(\supp\mu)>0,

2.

B_{\mu}
is differentiable at
q
.

Then,

\displaystyle\dim_{H}E_{\mu}\big(-B^{\prime}_{\mu}(q)\big)=\dim_{P}E_{\mu}\big(-B^{\prime}_{\mu}(q)\big)=B^{*}_{\mu}\big(-B^{\prime}_{\mu}(q)\big)=b^{*}_{\mu}\big(-B^{\prime}_{\mu}(q)\big).

Here
\dim_{H}
and
\dim_{P}
denote, respectively, the Hausdorff and
the packing dimension, see
[
26
]
for precise definitions of
this.

The following proposition is established in
[
28
]
.

Proposition 5.1

Let
\mu
be a compactly supported Borel probability measure on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
q\geq 1
and all
V\in G_{n,m}
, we have

b_{\mu_{V}}(q)\geq\max\Big(m(1-q),b_{\mu}(q)\Big).

In the following, we study the validity of the multifractal
formalism under projection. More specifically, we obtain general
result for the multifractal analysis of the orthogonal projections
on
m
-dimensional linear subspaces of measure
\mu
satisfying the
multifractal formalism.

Theorem 5.2

Let
\mu
be a compactly supported Borel probability measure on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
q>1
. Suppose that

(\mathsf{H}_{1})

\mathscr{H}_{\mu}^{q,B_{\mu}(q)}(\supp\mu)>0,

(\mathsf{H}_{2})

B_{\mu}
is differentiable at
q
,

(\mathsf{H}_{3})

({E}_{i})_{i}
be a cover
of
\supp\mu
by a countable collection of compact sets is such that

b_{\mu}^{q}(E_{i}\cap\supp\mu)\geq\max(-m,m(1-q))
for all
i
.

Then, for
\gamma_{n,m}
-almost every
V\in G_{n,m}
,

\displaystyle\dim_{P}{E}_{\mu_{V}}\big(-B^{\prime}_{\mu}(q)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{H}{E}_{\mu_{V}}\big(-B^{\prime}_{\mu}(q)\big)=\dim_{H}E_{\mu}\big(-B^{\prime}_{\mu}(q)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{P}E_{\mu}\big(-B^{\prime}_{\mu}(q)\big)=B^{*}_{\mu}\big(-B^{\prime}_{\mu}(q)\big)=b^{*}_{\mu}\big(-B^{\prime}_{\mu}(q)\big).

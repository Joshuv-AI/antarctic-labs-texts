A family of elliptic curves with non-trivial Shafarevich-Tate group
Debopam Chakraborty, Vinodkumar Ghale

Abstract




2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 11G05, 11G07; Secondary 11R29, 51M04

Abstract

Rank computation of an elliptic curve is one of the most important problems in number theory due to its importance in various open questions in number theory. A common way to understand this rank is via understanding the Selmer group and the Shafarevich-Tate group of an elliptic curve. We construct a family of Heronian elliptic curves of rank
0
and
2
-Selmer rank
2
. This, in turn, produces a family of elliptic curves such that the Shafarevich-Tate group is isomorphic to the Klein four-group.

1.
Introduction

The rational points
E(\mathbb{Q})
for an elliptic curve
E
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
form a finitely generated abelian group by Mordell-Weil theorem. It is a fundamental question in number theory to understand the rank
r(E/\mathbb{Q})
of
E(\mathbb{Q})
, also known as the algebraic or Mordell-Weil rank. No known algorithm exists to find the rank of an elliptic curve. A full
p
-descent method (cf.
[
18
]
, Proposition X.
4
) generates an exact sequence

0\rightarrow E(\mathbb{Q})/pE(\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow Sel_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[p]\rightarrow 0

where
\text{Sel}_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})
denotes the
p
-Selmer group and
\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})
denotes the Tate-Shafarevich group, both described in detail for
p=2
in the next section. The importance of the Selmer and the Shafarevich-Tate group in the rank computation of an elliptic curve follows from the above
p
-descent exact sequence as
r(E/\mathbb{Q})=s_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})-\text{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[p]
where
s_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})=\text{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\text{Sel}_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})-\text{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}E(\mathbb{Q})[p]
denotes the
p
-Selmer rank of
E
.

Due to Cassels-Tate pairing (cf.
[
6
]
), the finiteness of the
p
-primary part of
\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^{\infty}]
would imply that
\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[p]
has even
\mathbb{F}_{p}
dimension, hence
s_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})
and
r(E/\mathbb{Q})
have the same parity. The finiteness of
\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^{\infty}]
implies
p-Selmer rank one conjecture
which states that
r(E/\mathbb{Q})=1
whenever
s_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})=1
. This conjecture has been verified for
p\geq 5
under certain assumptions (cf.
[
22
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
19
]
, and
[
3
]
).

Very little is yet to be known about the
p
-Selmer rank one conjecture for
p=2
even though the computation of a full
2
-descent is easiest in practice and provides the best tool as of yet to compute
r(E/\mathbb{Q})
. There has been a growing interest in
2
-Selmer group computation for different families of elliptic curves, as evident through the works of Klagsbrun-Mazur-Rubin in
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
, and the work of Mazur-Rubin in
[
16
]
.

D. R. Heath-Brown examined the size of the
2
-Selmer group of the congruent number elliptic curve
E_{n}:y^{2}=x^{3}-n^{2}x
in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
. As an appendix to
[
2
]
, Monsky showed that the
2
-Selmer rank
s_{2}(E_{n}/\mathbb{Q})=2m-\text{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(M)
when
m
is the number of distinct odd prime factors of
n
and
M
is a
2m\times 2m
matrix known as
Monsky matrix
. Thus the problem of finding the
2
-Selmer group for a congruent elliptic curve becomes an intricate application of linear algebra over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

An immediate generalization of the congruent number problem is the existence of positive integers
n
, which are areas of triangles with rational sides without the constraint of being a right-angle triangle. Such triangles are called
Heron triangles
. In
[
10
]
, Goins and Maddox showed the existence of an elliptic curve similar to the congruent elliptic curve that determines whether
n
is the area of a Heron triangle. This triangle is known as
Heron triangle
and is defined later. Even though the Monsly matrix calculation for
2
-Selmer rank is unavailable in this setup, recently, some work towards the rank computation of Heronian elliptic curves has been done. In
[
7
]
, Dujella and Peral showed elliptic curves of higher ranks associated with Heron triangles. In
[
11
]
, Halbeisen and Hungerbühler showed the existence of elliptic curves of rank at least two associated with Heron triangles. In a recent work by Chakraborty et al.
[
4
]
, a family of Heronian elliptic curves with a
2
-Selmer rank of precisely
1
was presented.

In this article, we consider primes
p\equiv 1\pmod{8}
and
q
such that
2q=p^{2}+1
. We examine the group structure of elliptic curves associated with Heron triangles of area
p
having one of the angles as
\theta
such that
\tau=\tan\frac{\theta}{2}=\frac{1}{p}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p
be a prime congruent to
1
modulo
8
and
q
be a prime such that
2q=p^{2}+1
. Then rank

of the elliptic curve
E_{p}:y^{2}=x(x-1)(x+p^{2})
is precisely zero, and
\Sha({E_{p}/\mathbb{Q}})[2]\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

We note that the rank computation for the case
p\equiv 3,5,7\pmod{8}
has already been covered in
[

]
though the work did not involve the calculation of
\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})

2.
Local Solutions for the Homogeneous Spaces

A full
2
-descent method for an elliptic curve is elaborately described in
[
18
]
, Proposition X.
1.4
. In particular, for a Heronian elliptic curve a similar background was presented in
[
4
]
. We notice that
E_{p}
is a Heronian elliptic curve associated to a Heron triangle of area
p
and one of the angle
\theta
such that
\tan\frac{\theta}{2}=p^{-1}
.

If
\phi
denotes the
2
-descent map, then from
[
18
]
(Proposition X.
1.4
), we can say that
A=\{(1,1),(-1,-1),(1,2q),(-1,-2q)\}
is the image of
E_{p}(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}
under
\phi
. Moreover, if
(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathbb{Q}(S,2)\times\mathbb{Q}(S,2)
is a pair that is not in the image of one of the three points
O,(0,0),(1,0)
under
\phi
, where
\mathbb{Q}(S,2)=\left\{\pm 1,\;\pm 2,\;\pm p,\;\pm q,\;\pm 2p,\;\pm 2q,\;\pm pq,\;\pm 2pq\right\}
, then
(b_{1},b_{2})
is the image of a point
P=(x,y)\in E_{p}(\mathbb{Q})/2E_{p}(\mathbb{Q})
if and only if the equations

(2.1)

\displaystyle b_{1}z_{1}^{2}-b_{2}z_{2}^{2}=1,

(2.2)

\displaystyle b_{1}z_{1}^{2}-b_{1}b_{2}z_{3}^{2}=-p^{2},

have a solution
(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}\times\mathbb{Q}^{*}\times\mathbb{Q}

The smooth curves given by equations (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are called
homogeneous space
of
E_{p}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Computing
E_{p}(\mathbb{Q})/2E_{p}(\mathbb{Q})
boils down to determining the existence of
\mathbb{Q}
-rational points in these spaces. The image of
E_{p}(\mathbb{Q})/2E_{p}({\mathbb{Q}})
under the
2
-descent map is contained in a subgroup of
\mathbb{Q}(S,2)\times\mathbb{Q}(S,2)
known as the
2
-Selmer group

\mbox{Sel}_{2}(E_{p}/\mathbb{Q})
, which, as mentioned earlier, fits into an exact sequence (see Chapter X,
[
18
]
, Theorem X
.4.2
)

(2.3)

0\longrightarrow E_{p}(\mathbb{Q})/2E_{p}(\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\mbox{Sel}_{2}(E_{p}/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\Sha(E_{p}/\mathbb{Q})[2]\longrightarrow 0.

The elements in
\mbox{Sel}_{2}(E_{p}/\mathbb{Q})
correspond to the pairs
(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathbb{Q}(S,2)\times\mathbb{Q}(S,2)
such that the system of equations (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) has non-trivial local solutions in
\mathbb{Q}_{l}
at all primes
l
of
\mathbb{Q}
including infinity. We note that
\#\,E_{p}(\mathbb{Q})/2E_{p}(\mathbb{Q})=2^{2+r(E_{p})}
and write
\#\,\mbox{Sel}_{2}(E_{p}/\mathbb{Q})=2^{2+s(E_{p})}
, where
s(E_{p})
is referred as the
2
-Selmer rank. We have
0\leq r(E_{p})\leq s(E_{p})
always.

We now examine the properties of the
l
-adic solutions for (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) that are associated with the
2
-Selmer group. We use the well-known fact that any
l
-adic number
a
can be written as
a=l^{n}\cdot u\text{ where }n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{l}^{*}
. Notice that the
l
-adic valuation
v_{l}(a)
of
a
is just
n
. We first prove the following result for all odd primes
l
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) have a solution
(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\in\mathbb{Q}_{l}\times\mathbb{Q}_{l}\times\mathbb{Q}_{l}
for any prime
l
. If
v_{l}(z_{i})<0
for any one
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, then
v_{l}(z_{1})=v_{l}(z_{2})=v_{l}(z_{3})=-k<0
for some integer
k
.

Proof.

Let
z_{i}=l^{k_{i}}u_{i}
, where
k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
u_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{l}^{*}
for
i=\{1,2,3\}
. Then
v_{l}(z_{i})=k_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,2,3\}
.

Suppose
k_{1}<0
. Then from (
2.1
) one can get that

b_{1}u_{1}^{2}-b_{2}u_{2}^{2}l^{2(k_{2}-k_{1})}=l^{-2k_{1}}.

If
k_{2}>k_{1}
, then
l^{2}
must divide
b_{1}
, a contradiction as
b_{1}
is square-free. Hence
k_{2}\leq k_{1}<0
. Now if
k_{2}<k_{1}<0
then again from (
2.1
) we get

b_{1}u_{1}^{2}l^{2(k_{1}-k_{2})}-b_{2}u_{2}^{2}=l^{-2k_{2}},

which implies
l^{2}
must divide
b_{2}
, a contradiction again. Hence if
k_{1}<0
, then we have
k_{1}=k_{2}=-k<0
for some integer
k
. For
k_{2}<0
, one similarly gets
k_{1}=k_{2}=-k<0
.

From (
2.2
), we have

b_{1}u_{1}^{2}-b_{1}b_{2}u_{3}^{2}l^{2(k_{3}-k_{1})}=-p^{2}\cdot l^{-2k_{1}}.

If
k_{1}<0
and
k_{3}>k_{1}
, then
l^{2}
must divide
b_{1}
, a contradiction as before. Hence
k_{3}\leq k_{1}<0
if
k_{1}<0
. For
k_{3}<k_{1}<0
,
we can rewrite the above equation as

(2.4)

b_{1}u_{1}^{2}l^{2(k_{1}-k_{3})}-b_{1}b_{2}u_{3}^{2}=-p^{2}\cdot l^{-2k_{3}},

which implies
l^{2}
must divide
b_{1}b_{2}
, i.e.,
l=2,p
or
q
. If
l=p
, then from (
2.4
) we arrive at the contradiction that
p^{3}
divides
b_{1}b_{2}
whereas
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
are square-free. For
l=2
and
q
, one can notice from (
2.2
) that if
k_{3}\leq-2
, then
l^{3}
divides
b_{1}b_{2}
, a contradiction again. This in turn implies
k_{3}=-1
and hence
k_{1}\geq 0
which contradicts the assumption that
k_{1}<0
. Hence
k_{1}<0\implies k_{3}=k_{1}
.

Now, suppose
k_{3}<0
. If
k_{1}<0
, then from the previous part we already established
k_{1}=k_{2}=k_{3}=-k
for some positive integer
k
. So without loss of generality, we can assume
k_{1}\geq k_{3}
. If
k_{3}<k_{1}
and
k_{3}<0
, then as mentioned previously in this proof, one can get that
b_{1}b_{2}\equiv 0\pmod{l^{2}}
and
l=2
or
q
. Now we subtract (
2.2
) from (
2.1
) and observe that

b_{1}b_{2}u_{3}^{2}-b_{2}u_{2}^{2}l^{2(k_{2}-k_{3})}=2q\cdot l^{-2k_{3}}.

If
k_{2}>k_{3}
, we get a contradiction that
l^{3}
divides
b_{1}b_{2}
for
l=2,q
. Therefore,
k_{2}\leq k_{3}<0
but then by the first part,
k_{1}=k_{2}\leq k_{3}
, a contradiction to the assumption
k_{1}>k_{3}
. Hence
k_{3}<0\implies k_{1}=k_{3}
. Together, now we obtain
k_{1}=k_{2}=k_{3}=-k<0
for some integer
k
if
k_{1}<0
or
k_{2}<0
or
k_{3}<0
.
∎

The corollary below now follows immediately from the above lemma.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let the system of equations (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) has a solution
(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\in\mathbb{Q}_{l}\times\mathbb{Q}_{l}\times\mathbb{Q}_{l}
for any prime
l
. Then either
v_{l}(z_{i})\geq 0
for all
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, or
v_{l}(z_{1})=v_{l}(z_{2})=v_{l}(z_{3})=-k<0
for some positive integer
k
.

3.
Bounding the Size of the
2
-Selmer Group

In this section, we bound the size of the
2
-Selmer group of the Heronian elliptic curve
E_{p}
. We first note that it is enough to only look into one
(b_{1},b_{2})\in[(b_{1},b_{2})]\in\text{Im}(\phi)/E_{p}(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}
. As
E_{p}(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}=\{(-1,-1),(1,2q),(-1,-2q),(1,1)\}
, we can immediately observe that without loss of generality, one can only focus on
(b_{1},b_{2})
such that
b_{2}
is odd and
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
both positive whenever their product
b_{1}b_{2}
is positive. We limit the size of
\mbox{Sel}_{2}(E_{p}/\mathbb{Q})
by ruling out local solutions for homogeneous spaces corresponding to certain pairs
(b_{1},b_{2})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(b_{1},b_{2})\not\in\{(1,1),(1,q),(p,1),(p,q)\}
. Then the corresponding homogeneous space can not have local solutions for all primes
l\leq\infty
.

Proof.

Let the homogeneous space corresponding to
(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathbb{Q}(S,2)\times\mathbb{Q}(S,2)
have real solutions. Then
b_{1}>0
and
b_{2}<0
implies
-p^{2}>0
in (
2.2
), which is absurd. Similarly,
b_{1}<0
and
b_{2}>0
implies
1<0
in (
2.1
), contradiction again. Thus, the homogeneous space corresponding to
(b_{1},b_{2})
has no
l
-adic solutions for
l=\infty
if
b_{1}b_{2}<0
.

If
\gcd(b_{1},b_{2})\equiv 0\pmod{p}
and
v_{p}(z_{i})<0
for any
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, then from Corollary
2.2
and equation (
2.2
), one can get
p^{2}
divides
b_{1}
, a contradiction. Otherwise from Corollary
2.2
and equation (
2.1
), one can get
p
divides
1
, again a contradiction. Hence
\gcd(b_{1},b_{2})\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Moreover, if
p
divides
b_{2}
, then from Corollary
2.2
, either
p
divides
2q
(subtracting (
2.2
) from (
2.1
)) or
p
divides both
b_{1},b_{2}
(from (
2.1
)), contradiction either way.

If
q
divides
b_{1}
, then from Corollary
2.2
and subtracting (
2.2
) from (
2.1
), one get that
q
divides
b_{2}
if
v_{q}(z_{i})\geq 0
and so,
q
divides
1
from (
2.1
), a contradiction. Otherwise, again from Corollary
2.2
and (
2.1
), one gets that
q
divides
b_{2}
and hence from (
2.2
),
b_{1}\equiv 0\pmod{q^{2}}
, a contradiction.

We now show that for the existence of local solutions everywhere,
b_{1}
needs to be odd always. Otherwise, either
b_{1}
even and
z_{1},z_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
implies that
p^{2}\equiv 0\pmod{2}
, a contradiction. Else, from Corollary
2.2
and (
2.1
), one can see that
b_{2}
is even, a contradiction from the assumption made above.

Now we can see for a homogeneous space corresponding to
(b_{1},b_{2})
to have possible local solution everywhere
b_{1}\in\{1,p\}
and
b_{2}\in\{1,q\}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

4.
Mordell-Weil Rank and ideal class group

In this section, we cover the pairs
(p,1)
and
(1,q)
. We use the fact that due to Hilbert’s class field theorem, the existence of an unramified abelian extension of degree
n
of a number field
K
is equivalent to the class number
h(K)\equiv 0\pmod{n}
. For brevity, we mention the original result of Hilbert below.

Theorem 4.1

(Hilbert’s Class Field
[
17
]
)
.

The Hilbert class field
E
of a number field
K
is the maximal unramified abelian extension of
K
. Its degree over
K
equals the class number of
K
, and
\text{Gal}(E/K)
is canonically isomorphic to the ideal class group of
K
.

We now state and prove that the homogeneous spaces corresponding to
(p,1)
and
(1,q)
have no rational solutions.

Lemma 4.2
.

The homogeneous spaces corresponding to
(p,1)
and
(1,q)
have no rational solutions.

Proof.

Let
z_{i}=\frac{a_{i}}{d_{i}}
for
i=1,2,3
is a rational solution set for equations (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) where the rational numbers
z_{i}
are in their lowest form i.e.
\gcd(a_{i},d_{i})=1
for all
i=1,2,3
. It can be shown easily that
d_{1}^{2}=d_{2}^{2}=d_{3}^{2}=d^{2}
for some integer
d
. So now we have the following three equations;

(4.1)

\displaystyle b_{1}a_{1}^{2}-b_{2}a_{2}^{2}=d^{2},

(4.2)

\displaystyle b_{1}a_{1}^{2}-b_{1}b_{2}a_{3}^{2}=-p^{2}\cdot d^{2},

(4.3)

\displaystyle b_{1}b_{2}a_{3}^{2}-b_{2}a_{2}^{2}=2q\cdot d^{2},

For the case
(p,1)
, one can trivially notice that
d
is even; hence,
a_{i}
is odd for each
i=1,2,3
. Now from (
4.2
),
d^{2}\equiv a_{1}^{2}-a_{3}^{2}\equiv 0\pmod{8}
, hence
d\equiv 0\pmod{4}
. A straightforward calculation shows that there are no common odd prime factors of
a_{1}+a_{3}
and
a_{1}-a_{3}
. Assuming
a_{i}\geq 0
for all
i=1,2,3
, equation (
4.2
) then implies that one of the two possibilities of
a_{1}+a_{3}
and
a_{1}-a_{3}
is
a_{1}+a_{3}=p\cdot 2^{n_{1}}\cdot m_{1}^{2}
and
a_{1}-a_{3}=-2^{n_{2}}\cdot m_{2}^{2}
where
m=m_{1}m_{2}
is odd,
n=n_{1}+n_{2}\geq 4
and
d^{2}=2^{n}\cdot m^{2}
. The fact that
a_{3}
is odd and
2a_{3}=p\cdot 2^{n_{1}}\cdot m_{1}^{2}+2^{n_{2}}\cdot m_{2}^{2}
now implies that either
a_{3}=p\cdot 2^{n-2}\cdot m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}
or
a_{3}=p\cdot m_{1}^{2}+2^{n-2}\cdot
. In either way,
a_{3}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Same is true for the case when
a_{1}+a_{3}=2^{n_{1}}\cdot m_{1}^{2},\text{ }a_{1}-a_{3}=-p\cdot 2^{n_{2}}\cdot m_{2}^{2}
.

Now if one defines
\alpha=a_{3}+d\sqrt{p}\in\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
, then from (
4.2
) we get
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)=a_{1}^{2}
where
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
. Because
\gcd(a_{1},a_{3})=1
can be proved easily, one can also observe that
\gcd(\alpha,\bar{\alpha})=1
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, the ring of integers of
K
, where
\bar{\alpha}=a_{3}-d\sqrt{p}
. This in turn implies that
\alpha\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{a}^{2}
for some ideal
\mathfrak{a}
which implies no finite primes except possibly primes above
2
ramifies in
K(\sqrt{\alpha})/K
(cf.
[
14
]
,
[
5
]
). But
\alpha=a_{3}+d\sqrt{p}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
implies
2
also does not ramify in
K(\sqrt{\alpha})/K
. It is also clear that infinite primes also do not ramify in
K(\sqrt{\alpha})/K
as
K(\sqrt{\alpha})\subset\mathbb{R}
. Hence from Hilbert’s class field theorem, we can conclude
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
has an even class number whenever the homogeneous space corresponding to
(p,1)
has a rational solution. But it is well known that the class number of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
is always odd (cf.
[
8
]
). Hence the result follows.

We now look into the case
(b_{1},b_{2})=(1,q)
. Similarly to the previous case, we can show that
d\equiv 0\pmod{4}
and
a_{3}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
again. From (
4.3
), this implies
\alpha=a_{3}+d\sqrt{2}
again gives an unramifed abelian quadratic extension
K(\sqrt{\alpha})/K
where
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})
. Hence existence of rational solution to homogeneous space corresponding to
(1,q)
implies
h(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}))
is even, a contradiction as
h(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}))=1
.
∎

5.
Everywhere Local Solution

Now we prove that the homogeneous spaces corresponding to
(p,1)
and
(1,q)
have local solutions everywhere. To lift a solution for a polynomial
f(x)
modulo a prime
l
to a solution for
f(x)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{l}
, we will need Hensel’s lemma. For the sake of clarity we first state Hensel’s lemma below.

Theorem 5.1

(Hensel’s Lemma (see
[
21
]
, Theorem A
.2
))
.

If
f(x)
is a polynomial with coefficients that are
l
-adic integers and
f(x_{1})\equiv 0\pmod{l}
for
x_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, then if
f^{\prime}(x_{1})\not\equiv 0\pmod{l}
, there exists an
l
-adic integer
x
with
x\equiv x_{1}\pmod{l}
such that
f(x)=0

Now we are in a position to look into the local solutions for the homogeneous spaces corresponding to
(p,1)
and
(1,q)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

The homogeneous spaces corresponding to
(p,1)
and
(1,q)
have local solutions everywhere for
l\leq\infty
.

Proof.

For

,

,

We can choose a solution
(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\in\mathbb{F}_{l}\times\mathbb{F}_{l}\times\mathbb{F}_{l}

implies

divides
1
from (

), a contradiction. Similarly,
z_{1}\equiv z_{3}\equiv 0\pmod{l}
implies
-p\equiv 0\pmod{l^{2}}
from (
2.2
), contradiction again. One can now suitably choose two of

and
z_{3}
to convert equations (
2.1
) and (

\mathbb{F}_{l}
. That common solution can be lifted to

via Hensel’s lemma.

For
l=p
, we first notice that
\big(\frac{-1}{p}\big)=1
as

. Hence there exists

. Now fixing
z_{1}=1
in equation (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we can immediately see that
z_{2}=a
and
z_{3}=1
are two simple roots of now two single variable polynomials and hence can be lifted to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

For
l=3
and

, using Lemma
2.1
, we look into solutions for the equations

in
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. Fixing

are two simple solutions and hence can be lifted to solution in

for the equations mentioned above. Diving by

, will then give rise to solutions in

For

On composition ideals and dual ideals of bounded holomorphic mappings
María de Gádor Cabrera-Padilla, Antonio Jiménez-Vargas, David Ruiz-Casternado

Abstract
. Applying a linearization theorem due to J. Mujica [ 17 ] , we study the ideals of bounded holomorphic mappings ℋ ∞ ∘ ℐ \mathcal{H}^{\infty}\circ\mathcal{I} generated by composition with an operator ideal ℐ \mathcal{I} . The bounded-holomorphic dual ideal of ℐ \mathcal{I} is introduced and its elements are characterized as those that admit a factorization through ℐ dual \mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}} . For complex Banach spaces E E and F F , we also analyze new ideals of bounded holomorphic mappings from an open subset U ⊆ E U\subseteq E to F F such as p p -integral holomorphic mappings and p p -nuclear holomorphic mappings with 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty . We prove that every p p -integral ( p p -nuclear) holomorphic mapping from U U to F F has relatively weakly compact (compact) range.

Introduction

Let
E
and
F
be complex Banach spaces and let
U
be an open subset of
E
. A mapping
f\colon U\to F
is said to be
locally compact (resp., locally weakly compact, locally Rosenthal, locally Asplund)
if every point
x\in U
has a neighborhood
U_{x}\subseteq U
such that
f(U_{x})
is relatively compact (resp., relatively weakly compact, Rosenthal, Asplund) in
F
.

R. M. Aron and M. Schottenloher
[
4
]
proved that every locally compact holomorphic mapping
f\colon E\to F
admits a factorization of the form
f=T\circ g
, where
G
is a Banach space,
T\colon G\to F
belongs to the ideal of compact operators and
g\colon E\to G
is holomorphic. Analogous results were stated by R. Ryan
[
21
]
, M. Lindström
[
15
]
and N. Robertson
[
20
]
for locally weakly compact holomorphic mappings, locally Rosenthal holomorphic mappings and locally Asplund holomorphic mappings, respectively, with
T
belonging to the corresponding ideal of bounded linear operators.

In
[
13
]
, M. González and J. M. Gutiérrez provided a unified approach to this problem by introducing a new class of holomorphic mappings which allowed them to extend the aforementioned factorizations to every closed surjective operator ideal.

Since many operator ideals
\mathcal{I}
can be naturally associated to polynomial ideals
\mathcal{P}
, R. Aron, G. Botelho, D. Pellegrino and P. Rueda
[
2
]
initiated a research program whose objective was to relate those holomorphic mappings
f
that admit a factorization
f=T\circ g
, where
T
belongs to an operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
and
g
is holomorphic, with those
f
whose derivatives belong to the associated composition polynomial ideal
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{P}
.

Our aim is to present here some advances in this research program on the factorization of bounded holomorphic mappings
f=T\circ g
, where
T
is in an operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
and
g
is a bounded holomorphic mapping (see Section 3 in
[
2
]
). Some recent contributions to linearization of holomorphic functions and to composition ideals are due to D. Baweja and M. Gupta
[
5
]
, and G. Botelho, V. Fávaro and J. Mujica
[
7
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
1
, we set up the linearization theorem of bounded holomorphic mappings due to J. Mujica
[
17
]
that will be a key tool to obtain our results.

In the spirit of the definition of operator ideal, we introduce in Section
2
the concept of ideal of (bounded) holomorphic mappings. Then, we adapt to the holomorphic setting a method to produce operator ideals from a given operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
and so we obtain ideals of bounded holomorphic mappings by composition with linear operators of
\mathcal{I}
. This useful technique has been applied with the same purpose in different settings as, for example, polynomial and holomorphic
[
2
]
, polynomial
[
3
]
, polynomial and multilinear
[
8
]
and Lipschitz settings
[
1
,
22
]
.

In Section
3
, we see that some known ideals of holomorphic mappings and bounded holomorphic mappings can be produced by this procedure as, for instance, ideals of holomorphic mappings with local range or (global) range of bounded type.

Furthermore, we introduce and analyze new ideals of bounded holomorphic mappings such as
p
-integral and
p
-nuclear holomorphic mappings with
1\leq p<\infty
. We prove that these types of ideals are generated by the method of composition, but also give some examples of ideals of bounded holomorphic mappings that can not be produced in this way.

The study of holomorphic mappings on Banach spaces which have relatively (weakly) compact range was initiated by J. Mujica in
[
17
]
and continued by J. M. Sepulcre and the last two authors in
[
14
]
. We prove here that every
p
-nuclear (
p
-integral) holomorphic mapping from
U
to
F
has relatively compact (weakly compact) range.

On the other hand, the dual ideal
\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}
of an operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
is the operator ideal formed by all bounded linear operators
T\colon E\to F
such that its adjoint operator
T^{*}
belongs to
\mathcal{I}(F^{*},E^{*})
. This procedure to produce ideals of linear operators has been applied to generate ideals of other types of mappings: polynomials and multilinear mappings between Banach spaces
[
18
,
12
]
, homogeneous polynomials between Banach spaces
[
6
]
and Lipschitz mappings from metric spaces into Banach spaces
[
1
,
22
]
.

Motivated by these works and with the aid of the transpose of a bounded holomorphic mapping, we carry out in Section
4
a similar study of the dual procedure in the setting of bounded holomorphic mappings. To be more precise, we introduce the bounded-holomorphic dual
(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}
of an operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
and prove that
(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}
is not only a bounded-holomorphic ideal but also belongs to the class of composition ideals, that is, the bounded holomorphic mappings belonging to
(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}
are exactly those that admit a factorization through
\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}
.

We refer to the monograph by A. Pietsch
[
19
]
for the theory of operator ideals, the book by J. Diestel, H. Jarchow and A. Tonge
[
11
]
for the theory of integral and nuclear operators, and the book by J. Mujica
[
16
]
for the theory of holomorphic mappings on infinite-dimensional spaces.

1.
Preliminaries

From now on,
E
and
F
will denote complex Banach spaces and
U
an open subset of
E
.

Let us recall that a mapping
f\colon U\to F
is said to be
holomorphic
if for each
a\in U
there exist an open ball
B(a,r)
with center at
a
and radius
r>0
contained in
U
and a sequence of continuous
m
-homogeneous polynomials
(P_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
from
E
into
F
such that

f(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P_{m}(x-a),

where the series converges uniformly for
x\in B(a,r)
. A mapping
P\colon E\to F
is said to be a
continuous
m
-homogeneous polynomial
if there exists a continuous
m
-linear mapping
A\colon E\to F
such that
P(x)=A(x,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(m)}}{{\ldots}},x)
for all
x\in E
.

If
U\subseteq E
and
V\subseteq F
are open sets,
\mathcal{H}(U,V)
will represent the set of all holomorphic mappings from
U
to
V
. We will denote by
\mathcal{H}(U,F)
the linear space of all holomorphic mappings from
U
into
F
, and by
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
the subspace of all
f\in\mathcal{H}(U,F)
such that
f(U)
is bounded in
F
.
In the case
F=\mathbb{C}
, we will write
\mathcal{H}(U)
and
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
instead of
\mathcal{H}(U,\mathbb{C})
and
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,\mathbb{C})
, respectively.

It is known that the linear space
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
, equipped with the uniform norm:

\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}=\sup\left\{\left|f(x)\right|\colon x\in U\right\}\qquad\left(f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)\right),

is a dual Banach space. We denote by
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)
the
geometric predual of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
. Let us recall that
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)
is the norm-closed linear hull in
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)^{*}
of the set
\left\{\delta(x)\colon x\in U\right\}
of evaluation functionals defined by

\delta(x)(f)=f(x)\qquad\left(f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)\right).

The following linearization theorem by J. Mujica
[
17
]
will be an essential tool to establish our results.

Given a complex Banach space
E
, we will denote by
B_{E}
,
S_{E}
and
E^{*}
the closed unit ball, the unit sphere and the dual space of
E
, respectively. If
E
and
F
are Banach spaces,
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
denotes the Banach space of all continuous linear operators from
E
into
F
with the operator canonical norm.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
17
, Theorem 2.1]

Let
E
be a complex Banach space and
U
be an open subset of
E
.

(1)

\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
is isometrically isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)^{*}
, under the mapping
J_{U}\colon\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)\to\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)^{*}
given by

J_{U}(f)(\gamma)=\gamma(f)\qquad\left(\gamma\in\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),\;f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)\right).

(2)

The mapping
g_{U}\colon U\to\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)
defined by
g_{U}(x)=\delta(x)
for all
x\in U
is holomorphic with
g_{U}(U)\subseteq S_{\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)}
.

(3)

For each complex Banach space
F
and each mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
, there exists a unique operator
T_{f}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
such that
T_{f}\circ g_{U}=f
. Furthermore,
\left\|T_{f}\right\|=\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}
.

(4)

The mapping
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
onto
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
.
\hfill\qed

We now recall some concepts and results of the theory of operator ideals which have been borrowed from
[

,

]
.

An operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
(see definition in
[
19
, 1.1.1]
) is said to be:

(1)

closed
if
\mathcal{I}(E,F)
is closed in
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
for all Banach spaces
E
and
F

[
19
, Section 4.2]
.

(2)

injective
if given an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(E,F)
, a Banach space
G
and an injective operator with closed range
\iota\in\mathcal{L}(F,G)
, we have that
T\in\mathcal{I}(E,F)
whenever
\iota\circ T\in\mathcal{I}(E,G)

[

.

(3)

surjective
if given an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(E,F)
, a Banach space
G
and a surjective operator
\pi\in\mathcal{L}(G,E)
, we have that
T\in\mathcal{I}(E,F)
whenever
T\circ\pi\in\mathcal{I}(G,F)

[

.

An operator

is said to be

if
T(B_{E})
is relatively compact (resp., separable, relatively weakly compact, Rosenthal, Asplund) in

. We denote by
\mathcal{F}(E,F)
,
\overline{\mathcal{F}}(E,F)
,
\mathcal{K}(E,F)
,
\mathcal{S}(E,F)
,
\mathcal{W}(E,F)
,
\mathcal{R}(E,F)
and
\mathcal{A}(E,F)
the ideals of bounded finite-rank linear operators, approximable linear operators (i.e., operators which are the norm limits of bounded finite-rank operators), compact linear operators, bounded separable linear operators, weakly compact linear operators, Rosenthal linear operators and Asplund linear operators from
E
into
F
, respectively. The following inclusions are known:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(E,F)

\displaystyle\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{F}}(E,F)\subseteq\mathcal{K}(E,F)\subseteq\mathcal{W}(E,F)\subseteq\mathcal{R}(E,F)\cap\mathcal{A}(E,F),

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(E,F)

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{S}(E,F).

2.
Ideal and composition ideal of bounded holomorphic mappings

Inspired by the preceding definitions, we introduce the concept of an ideal of (bounded) holomorphic mappings and its different properties.

Definition 2.1
.

An ideal of holomorphic mappings (or simply, a holomorphic ideal) is a subclass
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}}
of the class
\mathcal{H}
of all holomorphic mappings such that for any complex Banach space
E
, any open subset
U
of
E
and any complex Banach space
F
, the components

\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}}(U,F):=\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}}\cap\mathcal{H}(U,F)

satisfy the following three properties:

(I1)

\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}}(U,F)
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{H}(U,F)
.

(I2)

For any
g\in\mathcal{H}(U)
and
y\in F
, the mapping
g\cdot y\colon x\mapsto g(x)y
from
U
to
F
is in
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}}(U,F)
.

(I3)

The ideal property: If
H,G
are complex Banach spaces,
V
is an open subset of
H
,
h\in\mathcal{H}(V,U)
,
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}}(U,F)
and
S\in\mathcal{L}(F,G)
, then
S\circ f\circ h
is in
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}}(V,G)
.

An ideal of bounded holomorphic mappings (or simply, a bounded-holomorphic ideal) is a subclass
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
of the form
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}=\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}}\cap\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
, where
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}}
is a holomorphic ideal.

A bounded-holomorphic ideal
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
is said to be:

(1)

closed if each component
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
with the topology of supremum norm.

(2)

injective if for any mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
, any complex Banach space
G
and any isometric linear embedding
\iota\colon F\to G
, we have that
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
whenever
\iota\circ f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,G)
.

(3)

surjective if for any mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
, any open subset
V
of a complex Banach space
G
and any surjective mapping
\pi\in\mathcal{H}(V,U)
, we have that
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
whenever
f\circ\pi\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(V,F)
.

A bounded-holomorphic ideal
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
is said to be normed (Banach) if there exists a function
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}\colon\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}\to\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
such that for every complex Banach space
E
, every open subset
U
of
E
and every complex Banach space
F
, the following three conditions are satisfied:

(N1)

(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}})
is a normed (Banach) space with
\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
.

(N2)

\left\|g\cdot y\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}=\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\left\|y\right\|
for all
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
and
y\in F
.

(N3)

If
H,G
are complex Banach spaces,
V
is an open subset of
H
,
h\in\mathcal{H}(V,U)
,
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
and
S\in\mathcal{L}(F,G)
, then
\left\|S\circ f\circ h\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}\leq\left\|S\right\|\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}
.

Remark 2.2
.

According to Definition
2.1
, ideals of holomorphic mappings always contain the finite rank maps. So, when restricted to the case of homogeneous polynomials, our definition does not recover the classical notion of polynomial ideals (a polynomial ideal contains the finite type maps, and not necessarily the finite rank maps). We must point out that our notion of ideal of holomorphic mappings is more related to the notion of hyper-ideals of polynomials (which always contain the finite rank maps) rather than to the notion of polynomial ideals. For the theory of hyper-ideals of polynomials, see, e.g.,
[
9
]
.

We now recall the composition method to produce ideals of holomorphic mappings. This linear method has been applied in different contexts: multilinear, polynomial, Lipschitz and holomorphic (see, for example,
[
1
,
2
,
8
,
13
,
22
]
).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
E,F
be complex Banach spaces and
U
be an open set in
E
. Given an operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
, a mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}(U,F)
(resp.
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
) belongs to the composition ideal
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}
(resp.
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
), and we write
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}(U,F)
(resp.
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
), if there are a complex Banach space
G
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{I}(G,F)
and a mapping
g\in\mathcal{H}(U,G)
(resp.
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,G)
) such that
f=T\circ g
.

If
(\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}})
is a normed operator ideal and
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
, we denote

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}=\inf\left\{\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\right\},

where the infimum is extended over all representations of
f
as above.

The following result states the linearization of the members of the composition ideal
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
. The first part was proved in
[
2
]
, but we have included it here for the convenience of the reader.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
2
, Theorem 3.2]

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an operator ideal and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

f
belongs to
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
.

(2)

Its linearization
T_{f}
is in
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
.

If
(\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}})
is a normed operator ideal, we have that
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}=||T_{f}||_{\mathcal{I}}
and the infimum
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
is attained at
T_{f}\circ g_{U}
(Mujica’s factorization of
f
). Furthermore, the mapping
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
(\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
onto
(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}})
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: If
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
, then there are a complex Banach space
G
, a mapping
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,G)
and an operator
T\in\mathcal{I}(G,F)
such that
f=T\circ g
. Since
f=T_{f}\circ g_{U}
and
g=T_{g}\circ g_{U}
by Theorem
1.1
, it follows that
T_{f}\circ g_{U}=T\circ T_{g}\circ g_{U}
which implies that
T_{f}=T\circ T_{g}
by the linear denseness of
g_{U}(U)
in
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)
, and thus
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
by the ideal property of
\mathcal{I}
. Further, if
(\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}})
is normed, we have

\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}=\left\|T\circ T_{g}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\leq\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|T_{g}\right\|=\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|g\right\|_{\infty},

and taking the infimum over all representations of
f
, we deduce that
\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
: If
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
, then
f=T_{f}\circ g_{U}\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
since
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)
is a complex Banach space and
g_{U}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U))
by Theorem
1.1
. Moreover, if
(\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}})
is normed, we have

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}=\left\|T_{f}\circ g_{U}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}\leq\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|g_{U}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}.

Finally, the last assertion of the statement easily follows by applying the above proof and Theorem
1.1
.
∎

We now see that some properties of the operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
are transferred to the composition ideal
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
.

Corollary 2.5
.

If
\mathcal{I}
is a closed (resp., normed, Banach) operator ideal, then
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
is a closed (resp., normed, Banach) bounded-holomorphic ideal.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an operator ideal. We have:

(I1)

If
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
and
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
, then
T_{\alpha_{1}f_{1}+\alpha_{2}f_{2}}=\alpha_{1}T_{f_{1}}+\alpha_{2}T_{f_{1}}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
by Theorems
1.1
and
2.4
. Hence
\alpha_{1}f_{1}+\alpha_{2}f_{2}\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
by Theorem
2.4
. Therefore
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
.

(I2)

Given
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
and
y\in F
, we can write
g\cdot y=T_{g\cdot y}\circ g_{U}
, where
g_{U}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,G^{\infty}(U))
and
T_{g\cdot y}\in\mathcal{F}(G^{\infty}(U),F)
by Theorem
1.1
and
[
17
, p. 872]
, and this tells us that
g\cdot y\in\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
and since always
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
, we conclude that
g\cdot y\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
.

(I3)

Let
H,G
be complex Banach spaces,
V
be an open subset of
H
,
h\in\mathcal{H}(V,U)
,
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
and
S\in\mathcal{L}(F,G)
. By
[
14
, Corollary 1.4]
, there exists a unique operator
\widehat{h}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(V),\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U))
such that
\widehat{h}\circ g_{V}=g_{U}\circ h
. Furthermore,
||\widehat{h}||=1
. Since

S\circ f\circ h=S\circ(T_{f}\circ g_{U})\circ h=(S\circ T_{f}\circ\widehat{h})\circ g_{V},

with
S\circ T_{f}\circ\widehat{h}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(V),G)
and
g_{V}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(V,\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(V))
, we have
S\circ f\circ h\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(V,G)
.

This proves that
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
is a bounded-holomorphic ideal.

We now show that
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
is closed whenever
\mathcal{I}
is so. Let
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
and let
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
such that
\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{\infty}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Since
T_{f_{n}}\in\mathcal{I}(G^{\infty}(U),F)
by Theorem
2.4
and
\left\|T_{f_{n}}-T_{f}\right\|=\left\|T_{f_{n}-f}\right\|=\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{\infty}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
, and thus
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
by Theorem
2.4
.

Assume now that the operator ideal
(\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}})
is normed. We have:

(N1)

Since
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}=\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
by Theorem
2.4
, it easily follows that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
is a norm on
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
and

\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|T_{f}\right\|\leq\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}=\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}

for all
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
.

(N2)

Given
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
and
y\in F
, we have

\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\left\|y\right\|=\left\|g\cdot y\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|g\cdot y\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}},

and conversely, since
g\cdot y=M_{y}\circ g
where
M_{y}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C},F)\subseteq\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{C},F)
is the operator defined
M_{y}(\lambda)=\lambda y
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\left\|g\cdot y\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}\leq\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\left\|M_{y}\right\|=\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\left\|y\right\|.

(N3)

Following the above proof of (I3), we have

\displaystyle\left\|S\circ f\circ h\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\left\|(S\circ T_{f}\circ\widehat{h})\circ g_{V}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}\leq\left\|S\circ T_{f}\circ\widehat{h}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|g_{V}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|S\right\|\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|\widehat{h}\right\|\left\|g_{V}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|S\right\|\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}.

So, we have proved that the ideal
(\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
is normed.

Finally, since
(\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
is isometrically isomorphic to
(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}})
by Theorem
2.4
, then
(\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
is a Banach ideal whenever
(\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}})
is so.
∎

We finish this section with some properties of bounded-holomorphic ideals which can be easily deduced from Theorems
1.1
and
2.4
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
be two operator ideals. We have:

(1)

If
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)\subseteq\mathcal{J}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
, then
\mathcal{I}(G^{\infty}(U),F)\subseteq\mathcal{J}(G^{\infty}(U),F)
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)=\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
, then
\mathcal{I}(G^{\infty}(U),F)=\mathcal{L}(G^{\infty}(U),F)
.

(3)

If the identity operator
\mathrm{id}_{F}\in\mathcal{I}(F,F)
, then
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)=\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
.
\hfill\qed

3.
Examples of composition ideals of bounded holomorphic mappings

We have divided this section into four parts. In Subsection
3.1
, we will recall that some known ideals of holomorphic mappings
f
with local range of bounded type (for example, with compact, weakly compact, Rosenthal or Asplund range) are generated by composition of a holomorphic mapping
g
with an operator
T
in the corresponding operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
.

If
f
is in addition bounded, one cannot assure in general that the function
g
is also bounded. However, this is possible if we consider some smaller classes of such ideals. To be more precise, in Subsection
3.2
we will show that the ideals of bounded holomorphic mappings with (global) range of bounded type are generated by composition of a bounded holomorphic mapping
g
with an operator
T
in
\mathcal{I}
.

Finally, motivated by the ideals of
p
-integral operators and
p
-nuclear operators between Banach spaces for
1\leq p<\infty
(see, for example,
[
11
]
), we will introduce in Subsections
3.3
and
3.4
the analogs in the holomorphic setting and state that such ideals of bounded holomorphic mappings are generated by the method of composition.

3.1.
Holomorphic mappings with local range of bounded type

Let
\mathcal{H}_{k}(U,F)
(resp.,
\mathcal{H}_{w}(U,F)
,
\mathcal{H}_{r}(U,F)
,
\mathcal{H}_{a}(U,F)
) denote the linear subspace of all locally compact (resp., locally weakly compact, locally Rosenthal, locally Asplund) mappings of
\mathcal{H}(U,F)
. In the bounded case, we write

\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{i}(U,F)=\mathcal{H}_{i}(U,F)\cap\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)\qquad(i=k,w,r,a).

It is clear that

\mathcal{H}_{k}(U,F)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{w}(U,F)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{r}(U,F)\cap\mathcal{H}_{a}(U,F).

Proposition 3.1
.

For
i=k,w,r,a
,
\mathcal{H}_{i}
is a holomorphic ideal and
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{i}
is a bounded-holomorphic ideal.

Proof.

Let
i=k,w,r,a
. Clearly,
\mathcal{H}_{i}(U,F)
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{H}(U,F)
. Given
g\in\mathcal{H}(U)
and
y\in F
, it is clear that
g\cdot y\in\mathcal{H}(U,F)
with
(g\cdot y)(U)=g(U)y
. Since
g
is locally bounded by
[
16
, Lemma 5.6]
, every point
x\in U
has a neighborhood
U_{x}\subseteq U
such that
g(U_{x})
is bounded in
\mathbb{C}
, that is, relatively compact in
\mathbb{C}
. Hence
(g\cdot y)(U_{x})=g(U_{x})y
is relatively compact in
F
and thus
g\cdot y\in\mathcal{H}_{k}(U,F)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{i}(U,F)
.

To prove the ideal property of
\mathcal{H}_{i}(U,F)
, let
H,G
be complex Banach spaces,
V
be an open subset of
H
,
h\in\mathcal{H}(V,U)
,
f\in\mathcal{H}_{i}(U,F)
and
S\in\mathcal{L}(F,G)
. Let
x\in V
. Then there exists a neighborhood of
h(x)
,
U_{h(x)}\subseteq U
, such that
f(U_{h(x)})
is relatively compact (resp., relatively weakly compact, Rosenthal, Asplund) in
F
. Denote
V_{x}=h^{-1}(U_{h(x)})
. Hence
S(f(h(V_{x}))
is relatively compact (resp., relatively weakly compact, Rosenthal, Asplund) in
G
, and thus
S\circ f\circ h\in\mathcal{H}_{i}(V,G)
. This proves that
\mathcal{H}_{i}
is a holomorphic ideal. Hence
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{i}
is a bounded-holomorphic ideal.

∎

Some known results show that the holomorphic ideals
\mathcal{H}_{i}
for
i=k,w,r,a
are generated by the method of composition with an operator ideal. Namely, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\circ\mathcal{H}(E,F)=H_{k}(E,F)\qquad

\displaystyle\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{AroSch-76}{}{}]}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\circ\mathcal{H}(E,F)=H_{w}(E,F)\qquad

\displaystyle\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Rya-88}{}{}]}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{H}(E,F)=H_{r}(E,F)\qquad

\displaystyle\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Lin-89}{}{}]}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{H}(E,F)=H_{a}(E,F)\qquad

\displaystyle\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Rob-92}{}{}]}}.

More generally, let
\mathcal{U}
be a closed surjective operator ideal and let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{U}}(F)
be the collection of all
A\subseteq F
so that
A\subseteq T(B_{G})
for some complex Banach space
G
and some operator
T\in\mathcal{U}(G,F)
. Let
H^{\mathcal{U}}(E,F)
denote the space of all
f\in\mathcal{H}(E,F)
such that each
x\in E
has a neighborhood
V_{x}\subseteq E
with
f(V_{x})\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{U}}(F)
. In
[
13
, Theorem 6]
, it is proved that

\mathcal{U}\circ\mathcal{H}(E,F)=H^{\mathcal{U}}(E,F).

See also the paper
[
2
]
for a study of such spaces as associated to composition ideals of polynomials.

3.2.
Holomorphic mappings with range of bounded type

Let
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}}(U,F)
(resp.,
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{W}}(U,F)
,
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{R}}(U,F)
,
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{A}}(U,F)
) denote the linear space of all holomorphic mappings
f\colon U\to F
such that
f(U)
is relatively compact (resp., relatively weakly compact, Rosenthal, Asplund) in
F
. Note that
f(U)
is a bounded subset of
F
in all the cases.

We will also consider the spaces:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(U,F)

\displaystyle=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)\colon\mathrm{lin}(f(U))\text{ is finite-dimensional in }F\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}(U,F)

\displaystyle=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)\colon\exists(f_{n})_{n}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}(U,F)\;|\;\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{\infty}\to 0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{S}}(U,F)

\displaystyle=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)\colon f(U)\text{ is separable in }F\right\}.

Clearly, we have the inclusions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(U,F)

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}(U,F)\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}}(U,F)\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{W}}(U,F)\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{R}}(U,F)\cap\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{A}}(U,F),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}}(U,F)

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{S}}(U,F),

and

\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}(U,F)\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{i}(U,F)\qquad((\mathcal{I},i)=(\mathcal{K},k),(\mathcal{W},w),(\mathcal{R},r),(\mathcal{A},a)).

Proposition 3.2
.

For
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{F},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{S},\overline{\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A}
, the set
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}
is a bounded-holomorphic ideal. Furthermore, we have:

(1)

\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}
is not closed if and only if
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{F}
.

(2)

\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}
is injective and surjective whenever
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K},\mathcal{S},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

The first assertion follows with a proof similar to that of Proposition
3.1
. Applying that the mapping
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}(U,F)
onto
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
(see
[
17
, Propositions 3.1 and 3.4]
for
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{F},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{W}
and
[
14
, Corollary 2.11]
for
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{S},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A}
), and that
\mathcal{I}
is not closed for the operator norm if and only if
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{F}
, we deduce the equivalence in
(1)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K},\mathcal{S},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A}
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. On the one hand, assume that
\iota\circ f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}(U,G)
for any complex Banach space
G
and any isometric linear embedding
\iota\colon F\to G
. Since
\iota\circ T_{f}=T_{\iota\circ f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),G)
by
[
17
, Propositions 3.1 and 3.4]
and
[
14
, Corollary 2.11]
, and the operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
is injective (see, for example,
[
13
, p. 471]
), it follows that
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
, thus
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}(U,F)
by
[
17
, Propositions 3.1 and 3.4]
and
[
14
, Corollary 2.11]
, and this proves that
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}
is injective.

On the other hand, suppose that
f\circ\pi\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(V,F)
, where
V
is an open subset of a complex Banach space
G
and
\pi\in\mathcal{H}(V,U)
is surjective. By
[
14
, Corollary 1.4]
, there exists a unique operator
\widehat{\pi}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(V),\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U))
such that
g_{U}\circ\pi=\widehat{\pi}\circ g_{V}
. Since
T_{f}\circ\widehat{\pi}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(V),F)
and
T_{f}\circ\widehat{\pi}\circ g_{V}=T_{f}\circ g_{U}\circ\pi=f\circ\pi
, we deduce that
T_{f\circ\pi}=T_{f}\circ\widehat{\pi}
by Theorem
1.1
. Since
T_{f}\circ\widehat{\pi}=T_{f\circ\pi}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(V),F)
by
[
17
, Propositions 3.1 and 3.4]
and
[
14
, Corollary 2.11]
, and the operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
is surjective
[
13
, p. 471]
, we have that
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
, hence
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}(U,F)
by
[
17
, Propositions 3.1 and 3.4]
and
[
14
, Corollary 2.11]
, and thus
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}
is surjective.
∎

We next see that the preceding bounded-holomorphic ideals are generated by composition with the corresponding operator ideal.

Proposition 3.3
.

For
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{F},\overline{\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{S},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A}
, we have
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}=\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
and
\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{F},\overline{\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{S},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A}
. Note first that
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}(U,F)=\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. Indeed, if
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}(U,F)
, then
f=T_{f}\circ g_{U}
where
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
by
[
17
, Propositions 3.1 and 3.4]
and
[
14
, Corollary 2.11]
, and thus
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. Conversely, if
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
, then
f=T\circ g
for some complex Banach space
G
,
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,G)
and
T\in\mathcal{I}(G,F)
. If
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{F}
or
\mathcal{I}=\overline{\mathcal{F}}
, then
f
has a finite rank or
f
can be approximated by bounded finite-rank holomorphic mappings, respectively. If
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K},\mathcal{S},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A}
, since
g(U)
is bounded in
G
, it follows that
f(U)=T(g(U))
is relatively compact (resp., separable, relatively weakly compact, Rosenthal, Asplund) in
F
. Hence
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}(U,F)
, as required.

Now, if
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}
denotes the operator norm, recall that the mappings

\displaystyle f\in(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty})

\displaystyle\mapsto T_{f}\in(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}),

\displaystyle f\in(\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})

\displaystyle\mapsto T_{f}\in(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}),

are isometric isomorphisms (see
[
17
, Propositions 3.1 and 3.4]
and
[
14
, Corollary 2.11]
for the first, and Theorem
2.4
for the second). Hence we have

\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}=\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}(U,F)
.
∎

3.3.
p
-integral holomorphic mappings

Following
[
11
, p. 93]
, given two Banach spaces
E
,
F
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we denote by
\mathcal{I}_{p}(E,F)
the Banach space of all
p
-integral linear operators
T\colon E\to F
with the norm

\iota_{p}(T)=\inf\left\{\left\|A\right\|\left\|B\right\|\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all
p
-integral factorizations
(A,I^{\mu}_{\infty,p},B)
of
T
in the form

\kappa_{F}\circ T=A\circ I^{\mu}_{\infty,p}\circ B\colon E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{\infty}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle I^{\mu}_{\infty,p}}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{p}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\rightarrow}}F^{**},

where
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
is a probability measure space,
A\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu),F^{**})
and
B\in\mathcal{L}(E,L_{\infty}(\mu))
. As usual,
I^{\mu}_{\infty,p}\colon L_{\infty}(\mu)\to L_{p}(\mu)
is the formal identity, and
\kappa_{F}\colon F\to F^{**}
is the canonical isometric embedding.

Let
p^{*}
denote the conjugate index of
p\in[1,\infty]
defined by
p^{*}=p/(p-1)
if
p\neq 1
,
p^{*}=\infty
if
p=1
, and
p^{*}=1
if
p=\infty
.

The concept of
p
-integral linear operator motivates us to introduce the holomorphic analog as follows.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
E,F
be complex Banach spaces,
U
be an open subset of
E
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. A mapping
f\colon U\to F
is said to be
p
-integral holomorphic if there exist a probability measure space
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu),F^{**})
and a mapping
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,L_{\infty}(\mu))
giving rise to the commutative diagram:

U
F
F^{**}
L_{\infty}(\mu)
L_{p}(\mu)
f
\kappa_{F}
g
I^{\mu}_{\infty,p}
T

The triple
(T,I^{\mu}_{\infty,p},g)
is called a
p
-integral holomorphic factorization of
f
. We denote

\iota^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)=\inf\left\{\left\|T\right\|\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\right\},

where the infimum is extended over all such factorizations of
f
. Let
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)
denote the set of all
p
-integral holomorphic mappings from
U
into
F
.

We now study the linearization of
p
-integral holomorphic mappings.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. Then
f\colon U\to F
is
p
-integral holomorphic if and only if its linearization
T_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\to F
is
p
-integral. In this case,

\iota_{p}(T_{f})=\iota_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f).

Furthermore, the mapping
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
(\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F),\iota_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
onto
(\mathcal{I}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F),\iota_{p})
.

Proof.

If
f\colon U\to F
is
p
-integral holomorphic, then we have

\kappa_{F}\circ f=T\circ I^{\mu}_{\infty,p}\circ g\colon U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{\infty}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle I^{\mu}_{\infty,p}}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{p}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\rightarrow}}F^{**},

where
(T,I^{\mu}_{\infty,p},g)
is a
p
-integral holomorphic factorization of
f
. Applying Theorem
1.1
, we obtain

\kappa_{F}\circ T_{f}\circ g_{U}=T\circ I^{\mu}_{\infty,p}\circ T_{g}\circ g_{U}\colon U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{U}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T_{g}}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{\infty}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle I^{\mu}_{\infty,p}}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{p}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\rightarrow}}F^{**},

By the denseness of
\mathrm{lin}(g_{U}(U))
in
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)
, we deduce that

\kappa_{F}\circ T_{f}=T\circ I^{\mu}_{\infty,p}\circ T_{g}\colon\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T_{g}}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{\infty}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle I^{\mu}_{\infty,p}}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{p}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\rightarrow}}F^{**}

and therefore
T_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\to F
is
p
-integral. Furthermore, we have

\iota_{p}(T_{f})\leq\left\|T\right\|\left\|T_{g}\right\|=\left\|T\right\|\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}

and taking infimum over all the
p
-integral holomorphic factorization of
f
, we deduce

\iota_{p}(T_{f})\leq\iota_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f).

Conversely, if
T_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\to F
is
p
-integral, we have

\kappa_{F}\circ T_{f}=T\circ I^{\mu}_{\infty,p}\circ S\colon\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{\infty}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle I^{\mu}_{\infty,p}}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{p}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\rightarrow}}F^{**}

where
(T,I^{\mu}_{\infty,p},S)
is a
p
-integral factorization of
T_{f}
. Note that
g:=S\circ g_{U}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,L_{\infty}(\mu))
, and since

\kappa_{F}\circ f=T\circ I^{\mu}_{\infty,p}\circ g\colon U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{\infty}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle I^{\mu}_{\infty,p}}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{p}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\rightarrow}}F^{**}

we conclude that
f
is
p
-integral holomorphic. Furthermore, we have

\iota_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)\leq\left\|T\right\|\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|T\right\|\left\|S\right\|,

and taking infimum over all the
p
-integral factorization of
T_{f}
, we deduce

\iota_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)\leq\iota_{p}(T_{f}).

To prove the last assertion of the statement, it suffices to show that the mapping
f\mapsto T_{f}
from
\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
to
\mathcal{I}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
is surjective. Take
T\in\mathcal{I}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
and then
T=T_{f}
for some
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
by Theorem
1.1
. Hence
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
and this implies that
f\in\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
by the above proof.
∎

Combining Theorem
2.4
and Proposition
3.5
, we deduce that the bounded-holomorphic ideal
\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
is generated by composition with the operator ideal
\mathcal{I}_{p}
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then
\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}=\mathcal{I}_{p}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
and
\iota_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)=\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}_{p}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
. In particular,
\left(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p},\iota^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}\right)
is a Banach ideal of bounded holomorphic mappings.
\hfill\qed

We next see that if a mapping is 1-integral holomorphic, then it is
p
-integral holomorphic for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
. Then
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{q}(U,F)
and
\iota^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{q}(f)\leq\iota^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)
for each
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)
.

Proof.

It follows immediately from Proposition
3.5
and
[
11
, Proposition 5.1]
.
∎

We finish our study of
p
-integral holomorphic mappings with a property of their ranges.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Every
p
-integral holomorphic mapping
f\colon U\to F
has relatively weakly compact range.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
. Then
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
by Proposition
3.5
, hence
T_{f}\in\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
by
[
11
, Proposition 5.5 and Theorem 2.17]
, and thus
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{W}}(U,F)
by
[
17
, Proposition 3.4]
.
∎

3.4.
p
-nuclear holomorphic mappings

Given Banach spaces
E
,
F
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we denote by
\mathcal{N}_{p}(E,F)
the Banach space of all
p
-nuclear linear operators
T\colon E\to F
with the norm

\nu_{p}(T)=\inf\left\{\left\|A\right\|\left\|M_{\lambda}\right\|\left\|B\right\|\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all such
p
-nuclear factorizations
(A,M_{\lambda},B)
of
T
in the form

T=A\circ M_{\lambda}\circ B\colon E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B}}{{\rightarrow}}\ell_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M_{\lambda}}}{{\rightarrow}}\ell_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\rightarrow}}F,

where
A\in\mathcal{L}(\ell_{p},F)
,
B\in\mathcal{L}(E,\ell_{\infty})
and
M_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(\ell_{\infty},\ell_{p})
is a diagonal operator induced by a sequence
\lambda\in\ell_{p}
(see
[
11
, p. 111]
).

In analogy with this concept, we introduce the following variant in the holomorphic setting.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
E,F
be complex Banach spaces,
U
be an open subset of
E
and
1\leq p<\infty
. A mapping
f\colon U\to F
is said to be
p
-nuclear holomorphic if there exist an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\ell_{p},F)
, a mapping
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,\ell_{\infty})
and a diagonal operator
M_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(\ell_{\infty},\ell_{p})
induced by a sequence
\lambda\in\ell_{p}
such that
f=T\circ M_{\lambda}\circ g
, that is, the following diagram commutes:

U
F
\ell_{\infty}
\ell_{p}
f
g
M_{\lambda}
T

The triple
(T,M_{\lambda},g)
is called a
p
-nuclear holomorphic factorization of
f
. We set

\nu^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)=\inf\left\{\left\|T\right\|\left\|M_{\lambda}\right\|\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\right\},

where the infimum is extended over all such factorizations of
f
. Let
\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)
denote the set of all
p
-nuclear holomorphic mappings from
U
into
F
.

A study on
p
-nuclear holomorphic mappings similar to that of the preceding subsection on
p
-integral holomorphic mappings is developed next.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. Then
f\colon U\to F
is
p
-nuclear holomorphic if and only if its linearization
T_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\to F
is
p
-nuclear. In this case,

\nu_{p}(T_{f})=\nu_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f).

Furthermore, the mapping
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
(\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F),\nu_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
onto
(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F),\nu_{p})
.

Proof.

If
f\colon U\to F
is
p
-nuclear holomorphic, then we have

f=T\circ M_{\lambda}\circ g\colon U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}\ell_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M_{\lambda}}}{{\rightarrow}}\ell_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\rightarrow}}F,

where
(T,M_{\lambda},g)
is a
p
-nuclear holomorphic factorization of
f
. Using Theorem
1.1
, we obtain

T_{f}\circ g_{U}=T\circ M_{\lambda}\circ T_{g}\circ g_{U}\colon U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{U}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T_{g}}}{{\rightarrow}}\ell_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M_{\lambda}}}{{\rightarrow}}\ell_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\rightarrow}}F.

By the denseness of
\mathrm{lin}(g_{U})
in
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)
, we deduce that

T_{f}=T\circ M_{\lambda}\circ T_{g}\colon\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T_{g}}}{{\rightarrow}}\ell_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M_{\lambda}}}{{\rightarrow}}\ell_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\rightarrow}}F,

and therefore
T_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\to F
is
p
-nuclear. Furthermore, we have

\nu_{p}(T_{f})\leq\left\|T\right\|\left\|M_{\lambda}\right\|\left\|T_{g}\right\|=\left\|T\right\|\left\|M_{\lambda}\right\|\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}

and since we were working with an arbitrary
p
-nuclear holomorphic factorization for
f
, we obtain

\nu_{p}(T_{f})\leq\nu_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f).

Conversely, if
T_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\to F
is
p
-nuclear, we have

T_{f}=T\circ M_{\lambda}\circ S\colon\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S}}{{\rightarrow}}\ell_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M_{\lambda}}}{{\rightarrow}}\ell_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\rightarrow}}F,

where
(T,M_{\lambda},S)
is a
p
-nuclear factorization of
T_{f}
. Note that
g:=S\circ g_{U}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,\ell_{\infty})
and since

f=T\circ M_{\lambda}\circ g\colon U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}\ell_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M_{\lambda}}}{{\rightarrow}}\ell_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\rightarrow}}F,

we conclude that
f
is
p
-nuclear holomorphic. Furthermore, we infer that

\nu_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)\leq\left\|T\right\|\left\|M_{\lambda}\right\|\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|T\right\|\left\|M_{\lambda}\right\|\left\|S\right\|,

and this ensures that

\nu_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)\leq\nu_{p}(T_{f}).

To prove the last assertion of the statement, it suffices to show that the mapping
f\mapsto T_{f}
from
\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
to
\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
is surjective. Take
T\in\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
and then
T=T_{f}
for some
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
by Theorem
1.1
. Hence
T_{f}\in\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
and this implies that
f\in\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
by the above proof.
∎

Using Theorem
2.4
and Proposition
3.10
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then
\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}=\mathcal{N}_{p}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
and
\nu_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)=\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{N}_{p}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
. In particular,
\left(\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p},\nu^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}\right)
is a Banach ideal of bounded holomorphic mappings.
\hfill\qed

The following result follows from Proposition
3.10
and
[
11
, Corollary 5.24 (b)]
.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
1\leq p<q<\infty
. Then
\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{q}(U,F)
and
\nu^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{q}(f)\leq\nu^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)
for each
f\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)
.
\hfill\qed

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Every
p
-nuclear holomorphic mapping
f\colon U\to F
has relatively compact range.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
. Then
T_{f}\in\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
by Proposition
3.10
, hence
T_{f}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
by
[
11
, Corollary 5.24 (a)]
, and thus
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}}(U,F)
by
[
17
, Proposition 3.4]
.
∎

As in the linear case
[
11
, Theorem 5.27]
and in the Lipschitz case
[
22
, Theorem 2.12]
,
p
-nuclear holomorphic mappings admit the following factorization.

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. Then
f\in\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
if and only if there exist a Banach space
G
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{K}(G,F)
and a mapping
g\in\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,G)
such that
f=T\circ g
.

Proof.

If
f\in\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
, then
T_{f}\in\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
by Proposition
3.10
. Then Theorem 5.27 in
[
11
]
shows that there exist a complex Banach space
G
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{K}(G,F)
and an operator
S\in\mathcal{I}_{p}(G^{\infty}(U),G)
such that
T_{f}=T\circ S
. Hence we have
f=T_{f}\circ g_{U}=T\circ S\circ g_{U}=T\circ g
, where
g=S\circ g_{U}\in\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
by Corollary
3.6
.

Conversely, if there exist a Banach space
G
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{K}(G,F)
and a mapping
g\in\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,G)
such that
f=T\circ g
, then
T_{f}\circ g_{U}=T\circ T_{g}\circ g_{U}
which gives
T_{f}=T\circ T_{g}
where
T_{g}\in\mathcal{I}_{p}(G^{\infty}(U),F)
by Proposition
3.5
. Hence
T_{f}\in\mathcal{N}_{p}(G^{\infty}(U),F)
by
[
11
, Theorem 5.27]
, and so
f\in\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
by Proposition
3.5
.
∎

Next, we study the inclusion relationships between the new classes of bounded holomorphic mappings considered. In a clear parallel to the linear case, we have the following.

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
.

(1)

\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)
and
\iota^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)\leq\nu^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)
for all
f\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)
.

(2)

If
F
is finite-dimensional, then
\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)=\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)
with
\nu^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)=\iota^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)
for all
f\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)
.

Proof.

(1) If
f\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)
, then
T_{f}\in\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
with
\nu_{p}(T_{f})=\nu_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)
by Proposition
3.10
. Since
\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
with
\iota_{p}(T)\leq\nu_{p}(T)
for all
T\in\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
by
[
11
, Corollary 5.24 (c)]
, it follows that
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
. Hence
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)
with
\iota_{p}(T_{f})=\iota_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)
by Proposition
3.5
, and further
\iota^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)=\iota_{p}(T_{f})\leq\nu_{p}(T_{f})=\nu^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)
.

(2) Assume that
F
is finite-dimensional. If
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)
, then
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
with
\iota_{p}(T_{f})=\iota_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)
by Proposition
3.5
. Hence
T_{f}\in\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
with
\nu_{p}(T_{f})=\iota_{p}(T_{f})
by
[
11
, Theorem 5.26]
. It follows that
f\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(U,F)
with
\nu^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)=\nu_{p}(T_{f})
by Proposition
3.10
, and so
\nu^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)=\iota^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)
.
∎

In general, a bounded-holomorphic ideal
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
does not coincide with
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
as we see below.

Examples 3.16
.

The ideal
\mathcal{H}_{w}^{\infty}
of locally weakly compact bounded holomorphic mappings does not coincide with
\mathcal{W}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
. For example, let
\mathring{\mathbb{D}}
be the open unit disc in
\mathbb{C}
, and let
f\colon\mathring{\mathbb{D}}\to c_{0}
be the mapping defined by
f(z)=(z^{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
. By Example 3.2 in
[
17
]
,
f
is in
\mathcal{H}_{k}^{\infty}(\mathring{\mathbb{D}},c_{0})
but
f
is not in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\infty}(\mathring{\mathbb{D}},c_{0})
. Hence
T_{f}
fails to belong to
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(\mathring{\mathbb{D}}),c_{0})
by
[
17
, Proposition 3.4 (b)]
. So by Theorem
2.4

f
is not in
\mathcal{W}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
. The same example shows that in general
\mathcal{H}_{k}^{\infty}\neq\mathcal{K}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
(see Example 3.3 in
[
2
]
).

4.
Dual ideal of bounded holomorphic mappings

According to
[
19
, Section 4.4]
, the dual ideal of an operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
is defined by

\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}(E,F)=\left\{T\in\mathcal{L}(E,F)\colon T^{*}\in\mathcal{I}(F^{*},E^{*})\right\},

where
E
and
F
are Banach spaces and
T^{*}
is the adjoint operator of
T
. It is well known that
\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}
is also an operator ideal. Moreover, if
(\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}})
is a normed or Banach operator ideal, then
\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}
is so equipped with the norm

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}}=\left\|T^{*}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}.

In order to introduce the concept of bounded-holomorphic dual of an operator ideal, we will first need a holomorphic variant of the concept of adjoint operator between Banach spaces.

Definition 4.1
.

[
4
,
21
]

Let
E,F
be complex Banach spaces and
U
be an open subset of
E
. The transpose of a bounded holomorphic mapping
f\colon U\to F
is the mapping
f^{t}\colon F^{*}\to\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
defined by

f^{t}(y^{*})=y^{*}\circ f\qquad(y^{*}\in F^{*}).

It is easy to show (see, for example,
[
14
, Proposition 1.11]
) that
f^{t}
is a continuous linear operator with
||f^{t}||=\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}
. Moreover,
f^{t}=J_{U}^{-1}\circ(T_{f})^{*}
, where
J_{U}\colon\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)\to\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)^{*}
is the isometric isomorphism defined in Theorem
1.1
.

Definition 4.2
.

Given an operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
, the bounded-holomorphic dual of
\mathcal{I}
is the set

(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}(U,F)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)\colon f^{t}\in\mathcal{I}(F^{*},\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))\right\}.

If
(\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}})
is a normed operator ideal and
f\in(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}
, define

\left\|f\right\|_{(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}}=\left\|f^{t}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}.

Next result assures that the bounded holomorphic mappings belonging to the bounded-holomorphic dual of an operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
are exactly those that factorize through
\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}
. The proof of this fact mimics the proofs of its polynomial version
[
6
, Theorem 2.2]
and its Lipschitz version
[
1
, Theorem 3.9]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an operator ideal. Then the transpose
f^{t}
of a bounded holomorphic mapping
f
belongs to
\mathcal{I}
if and only if
f
admits a factorization
f=T\circ g
where
g
is a bounded holomorphic mapping and the adjoint operator
T^{*}
of the bounded linear operator
T
belongs to
\mathcal{I}
, that is,

(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}=\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}.

Moreover, if
(\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}})
is a normed operator ideal, then

\left\|f\right\|_{(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}}=\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}

for all
f\in(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}(U,F)
. Then
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
and
f^{t}\in\mathcal{I}(F^{*},\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))
. By Theorem
1.1
, there exists
T_{f}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
such that
f=T_{f}\circ g_{U}
. Since
(T_{f})^{*}=J_{U}\circ f^{t}\in\mathcal{I}(F^{*},\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)^{*})
, the ideal property of
\mathcal{I}
yields that
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
. Hence
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
with
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}=\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}}
by Theorem
2.4
, and this proves the inclusion

(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}(U,F)\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F).

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}}=\left\|(T_{f})^{*}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}=\left\|J_{U}\circ f^{t}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|J_{U}\right\|\left\|f^{t}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}=\left\|f^{t}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}=\left\|f\right\|_{(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}}.

Conversely, let
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. Then there are a complex Banach space
G
, a mapping
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,G)
and an operator
T\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}(G,F)
such that
f=T\circ g
. Given
y^{*}\in F^{*}
, we have

f^{t}(y^{*})=(T\circ g)^{t}(y^{*})=y^{*}\circ(T\circ g)=(y^{*}\circ T)\circ g=T^{*}(y^{*})\circ g=g^{t}(T^{*}(y^{*}))=(g^{t}\circ T^{*})(y^{*}),

and thus
f^{t}=g^{t}\circ T^{*}
. Since
T^{*}\in\mathcal{I}(F^{*},G^{*})
and
g^{t}\in\mathcal{L}(G^{*},\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))
, we obtain that
f^{t}\in\mathcal{I}(F^{*},\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))
. Hence
f\in(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}(U,F)
and this shows that

\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)\subseteq(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}(U,F).

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}}

\displaystyle=\left\|f^{t}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}=\left\|g^{t}\circ T^{*}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|g^{t}\right\|\left\|T^{*}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}=\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}},

and taking the infimum over all representations
T\circ g
of
f
, we conclude that

\left\|f\right\|_{(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}.

∎

Theorems 2.1, 2.2, 2.6 and 2.7 in
[
14
]
can be deduced from our preceding results.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{F},\overline{\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{W}
. Then a bounded holomorphic mapping belongs to
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}(U,F)
if and only if its transpose belongs to
\mathcal{I}(F^{*},\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))
.

Proof.

We have

\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{I}}=\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}=\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{dual}}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}=(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{dual}},

where the first equality follows from Proposition
3.3
, the second from
[
19
, Proposition 4.4.7]
and the third from Theorem
4.3
.
∎

With a proof similar to the above but replacing
[
19
, Proposition 4.4.7]
by
[
11
, Theorem 5.15]
, we obtain the following result on 1-integral holomorphic mappings.

Corollary 4.5
.

A bounded holomorphic mapping belongs to
\mathcal{I}_{1}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
if and only if its transpose belongs to
\mathcal{I}_{1}(F^{*},\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))
.
\hfill\qed

Regarding dual ideals, general representation theorems using topological tensor products provide useful tools for the linear case, even for the Lipschitz and multilinear cases. For future
research, it would be interesting to study a holomorphic analog for this kind of dual representations. It could provide another point of view more in the direction of the Defant–Floret book
[
10
]
focusing on tensor products, which could open the door to more general approaches.

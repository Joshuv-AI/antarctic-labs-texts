Thermodynamics and criticality of su ⁡ ( m ) \mathrm{su}(m) spin chains of Haldane–Shastry type
Federico Finkel, Artemio González-López

Abstract
We study the thermodynamics and critical behavior of su ⁡ ( m ) \mathrm{su}(m) spin chains of Haldane–Shastry type at zero chemical potential, both in the A N − 1 A_{N-1} and B ​ C N BC_{N} cases. We evaluate in closed form the free energy per spin for arbitrary values of m m , from which we derive explicit formulas for the energy, entropy and specific heat per spin. In particular, we find that the specific heat features a single Schottky peak, whose temperature is well approximated for m ≲ 10 m\lesssim 10 by the corresponding temperature for an m m -level system with uniformly spaced levels. We show that at low temperatures the free energy per spin of the models under study behaves as that of a one-dimensional conformal field theory with central charge c = m − 1 c=m-1 (with the only exception of the Frahm–Inozemtsev chain with zero value of its parameter). However, from a detailed study of the ground state degeneracy and the low-energy excitations, we conclude that these models are only critical in the antiferromagnetic case, with a few exceptions that we fully specify.

As mentioned in the Introduction, one of the hallmarks of criticality is the low-temperature

As mentioned in the Introduction, to ascertain the criticality of a quantum system we must also
examine the degeneracy of its ground state and study its low-energy excitations. Both of these

Consider, first, the PF, FI and HS-B chains, whose dispersion relation

\delta(s_{i},s_{i}+1)=0

s_{i}<s_{i+1}

\mathbf{s}_{g}

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{g}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{N-rm},1,\dots,m,\dots,1,\dots,m),

(30)

\displaystyle 1<s_{1}<\cdots<s_{N-rm}\,.

The ground state degeneracy is therefore

Let us now turn to the study of the low energy excitations over the ground state in the possible
critical cases identified in the previous paragraph. To begin with, consider the fermionic

\mathrm{su}(m)
HS chain. A low energy excitation over a ground state with bond vector
\mathbf{s}_{g}
is

Remark 2

The PF chain is known to possess Yangian invariance

V
Conclusions

In this paper we have completely determined in closed form the thermodynamics of the
\mathrm{su}(m)
spin
chains of Haldane–Shastry type (with zero chemical potential) (
4
)-(
8
) for
all
m
. Our method relies on the fact that the energy spectrum of these models coincides with
that of an appropriate vertex model, which makes it possible to express their thermodynamic free
energy as an integral involving the Perron eigenvalue of a position-dependent
m\times m
transfer
matrix. We have been able to compute this eigenvalue in closed form for arbitrary values of
m
by
applying the classical Perron theorem on positive matrices and some of its consequences. This
yields an explicit expression for the free energy per spin of these models, which fully determines
their thermodynamics. We have found that at sufficiently high temperatures the thermodynamic
functions of the
\mathrm{su}(m)
HS-type chains behave qualitatively as those of an
m
-level system with
uniformly spaced levels. In particular, for all values of
m
the specific heat features a single
Schottky peak, whose temperature is close to that of the corresponding
m
-level system for

m\lesssim 10
.

Using our explicit formula for the free energy per spin, we have also examined the critical
behavior of the
\mathrm{su}(m)
HS-type chains (
4
)-(
8
). We have first shown that the
low-temperature behavior of the free energy coincides with that of a CFT with central charge

c=m-1
, both in the ferromagnetic (bosonic) and the antiferromagnetic (fermionic) regimes (with

\gamma>0
for the FI chain). This low temperature behavior of the free energy is, however, only a
necessary condition for criticality. To ascertain whether the models under study are critical or
not, we have used the motif-based description of their spectrum to study the degeneracy of their
ground state and the existence of low-energy excitations with a linear energy-momentum relation.
In this way we have shown that the antiferromagnetic chains are all critical (again, with
\gamma>0

for the FI chain), whereas in the ferromagnetic case only the
\mathrm{su}(2)
and
\mathrm{su}(3)
HS-B chain
(with
\varepsilon=-1
in the latter case) are critical.

Our main result does not appear to be easily generalizable to the case of non-zero chemical
potential, and in fact the known expressions of the thermodynamic functions in the latter case for
low values of
m
have a more complicated structure than those found in this paper. Note, however,
that our closed-form expression for the Perron eigenvalue in the zero chemical potential case
could be used to identify and approximate this eigenvalue for sufficiently small values of the
chemical potentials, which would in turn yield a corresponding approximation for the thermodynamic
functions.

0\leqslant y\leqslant 1
. From Eq. (
23
) we have

f_{\pm}(T)-f_{\pm}(0)=-T^{2}\big(g(T)-mg(mT)\big)

(33)

with

g(T)=\frac{1}{2}\,T^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{\beta}\ln(1-e^{-y})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{y}}\,.

Since

\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln(1-e^{-y})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{y}}-\int_{0}^{\beta}\ln(1-e^{-y})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{y}}\right|\\
=-\int_{\beta}^{\infty}\ln(1-e^{-y})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{y}}=O(T^{1/2}e^{-\beta})

we can write

\displaystyle g(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,T^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln(1-e^{-y})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{y}}+O(e^{-\beta})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\,\zeta(3/2)T^{-1/2}+O(e^{-\beta}),

where
\zeta(z):=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}n^{-z}
is Riemann’s zeta function. From Eq. (
33
) we finally
obtain

f_{\pm}(T)-f_{\pm}(0)\\
=-\left(\sqrt{m}-1\right)\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\,\zeta(3/2)T^{3/2}+O(T^{2}e^{-\beta/m}),

(34)

which shows that the FI chain is not critical for
\gamma=0
.

Consider next the PF chain. In this case we simply have

\int_{0}^{1}\ln(1-e^{-\beta x})\,\mathrm{d}x=T\int_{0}^{\beta}(1-e^{-y})\,\mathrm{d}y=-\frac{\pi^{2}T}{6}+O(Te^{-\beta}),

and from (
22
) we find

f_{\pm}(T)-f_{\pm}(0)=-(m-1)\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{6}+O(T^{2}e^{-\beta/m}).

This is in agreement with Eq. (
24
), since in this case
{\mathcal{E}}(x)=x
.

Let us next deal with the FI and HS-B chains with
\gamma>0
, for which we respectively have

({\mathcal{E}}^{-1})^{\prime}(z)=\begin{cases}(\gamma^{2}+4z)^{-1/2},&0\leqslant z\leqslant\gamma+1\\[3.0pt]
\left[(\gamma+1)^{2}-2z)\right]^{-1/2},&0\leqslant z\leqslant\gamma+\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Since in both cases all the derivatives of
{\mathcal{E}}(z)
are bounded in their respective domains we
have

({\mathcal{E}}^{-1})^{\prime}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}+O(z^{n+1})\,,\quad c_{k}:=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{k+1}({\mathcal{E}}^{-1})}{\mathrm{d}z^{k+1}}\right|_{z=0}.

From these formulas we obtain the estimates

\displaystyle g(T)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\beta{\mathcal{E}}(1)}\ln\left(1-e^{-y}\right)({\mathcal{E}}^{-1})^{\prime}(Ty)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}T^{k}\int_{0}^{\beta{\mathcal{E}}(1)}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}\,\ln(1-e^{-y})\,\mathrm{d}y+O(T^{n+1}).

Moreover, since

\int_{\beta{\mathcal{E}}(1)}^{\infty}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}\,\ln(1-e^{-y})\,\mathrm{d}y=O(\beta^{k}e^{-\beta{\mathcal{E}}(1)}).

the upper limit in all of the integrals above can be pushed to infinity at the cost of an
exponentially small term. We thus have

\displaystyle g(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}T^{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}\,\ln(1-e^{-y})\,\mathrm{d}y+O(T^{n+1})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}\zeta(k+2)T^{k}+O(T^{n+1})\,,

which is equivalent to the infinite asymptotic expansion

g(T)\sim-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}\zeta(k+2)T^{k}.

(35)

By Eq. (
33
), the latter formula yields the following asymptotic series for the free
energy per spin of the FI and HS-B chains with
\gamma>0
:

f_{\pm}(T)-f_{\pm}(0)\sim-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(m^{k+1}-1)c_{k}\zeta(k+2)T^{k+2}.

(36)

The coefficients
c_{k}
can be easily computed in both cases, with the result

c_{k}=\begin{cases}(-2)^{k}(2k-1)!!\,\gamma^{-(2k+1)}&(\text{FI})\\[6.0pt]
(-1)^{k}(2k-1)!!\,(\gamma+1)^{-(2k+1)}&(\text{HS-B}),\end{cases}

where
(-1)!!:=1
. In particular, from Eqs. (
10
)-(
13
) it easily follows that the
first term in the asymptotic series (
36
) coincides with Eq. (
24
).

The above argument must be slightly modified to deal with the HS chain and the HS-B chain with

\gamma=0
, since in both cases
({\mathcal{E}}^{-1})^{\prime}
becomes infinite at the right endpoint
{\mathcal{E}}(1)
. For
instance, for the HS chain we have

({\mathcal{E}}^{-1})^{\prime}(z)=(1-4z)^{-1/2},\quad 0\leqslant z\leqslant 1/4;

note that in this case
{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}
is the inverse of
{\mathcal{E}}:[0,1/2]\to[0,1/4]
, since
{\mathcal{E}}(x)
is
increasing over
[0,1/2]
and symmetric about the half-point
x=1/2
. Using Eq. (
27
) and
performing the change of variable
\beta{\mathcal{E}}{x}=y
we again arrive at Eq. (
33
), where

g(T)
is now given by

g(T)=2\int_{0}^{\beta/4}\ln\left(1-e^{-y}\right)(1-4Ty)^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}y.

(37)

To deal with the divergence of the last term at the upper limit of the integral, we first note that

(1-4z)^{-1/2}=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}(2n+1)!!}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n+1}}{\mathrm{d}z^{n+1}}(1-4z)^{\frac{2n+1}{2}}\,.

We next define

h(z)=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}(2n+1)!!}\left[(1-4z)^{\frac{2n+1}{2}}-\sum_{k=0}^{2n+1}c_{k}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}\right],

with

c_{k}=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{k}}{\mathrm{d}z^{k}}\right|_{z=0}(1-4z)^{\frac{2n+1}{2}},

so that
h(z)=O(z^{2n+2})
. Differentiating
n+1
times the expression for
h(z)
we arrive at the
identity

\displaystyle(1-4z)^{-1/2}

\displaystyle=h^{(n+1)}(z)+\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}(2n+1)!!}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n+1}}{\mathrm{d}z^{n+1}}\sum_{k=n+1}^{2n+1}c_{k}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=h^{(n+1)}(z)+\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}(2n+1)!!}\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{n+j+1}\frac{z^{j}}{j!}\,.

Taking into account that

c_{n+j+1}=(2n+1)!!(2j-1)!!(-1)^{n+1}2^{n+j+1}

we finally obtain

(1-4z)^{-1/2}=h^{(n+1)}(z)+\sum_{j=0}^{n}2^{j}(2j-1)!!\,\frac{z^{j}}{j!}.

Substituting this expression into Eq. (
37
) yields

\displaystyle g(T)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}2^{j+1}(2j-1)!!\,T^{j}\int_{0}^{\beta/4}\frac{y^{j}}{j!}\ln(1-e^{-y})\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+2\beta^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\beta/4}\ln(1-e^{-y})\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n+1}}{\mathrm{d}y^{n+1}}h(Ty)\mathrm{d}y.

The asymptotic expansion of the first term is straightforward:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}2^{j+1}(2j-1)!!\,T^{j}\int_{0}^{\beta/4}\frac{y^{j}}{j!}\ln(1-e^{-y})\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}2^{j+1}(2j-1)!!\,T^{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{j}}{j!}\ln(1-e^{-y})\mathrm{d}y+O(\beta^{n}e^{-\beta/4})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=0}^{n}2^{j+1}(2j-1)!!\,\zeta(j+2)T^{j}+O(\beta^{n}e^{-\beta/4})\,.

(38)

We claim that the second term in the previous equation for
g(T)
is
O(T^{n+1})
. This is easily
proved by integrating by parts
n+1
times:

\displaystyle\beta^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\beta/4}\ln(1-e^{-y})\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n+1}}{\mathrm{d}y^{n+1}}h(Ty)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left.(-1)^{n-k}\beta^{n+1-k}\varphi^{(n-k)}(y)h^{(k)}(Ty)\right|^{\beta/4}_{0}

\displaystyle\hphantom{={}}+(-\beta)^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\beta/4}\varphi^{(n+1)}(y)h(Ty)\,\mathrm{d}y,

with
\varphi(y):=\ln(1-e^{-y})
. The boundary terms vanish at
y=0
,
since
h(z)=O(z^{2n+2})
. On the other hand,

\beta^{n-k+1}\varphi^{(n-k)}(\beta/4)h^{(k)}(1/4)=O(\beta^{n-k+1}e^{-\beta/4})

for
k=0,\dots,n
, as the first
n
derivatives of
h
are bounded. Thus the boundary terms are

O(\beta^{n+1}e^{-\beta/4})
, while

\beta^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\beta/4}\varphi^{(n+1)}(y)h(Ty)\,\mathrm{d}y=O(T^{n+1}),

since
h(Ty)=O((Ty)^{2n+2})
and the integral
\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{2n+2}\varphi^{(n+1)}(y)\mathrm{d}y
is
convergent. Putting all of the above together we obtain the asymptotic series

g(T)\sim-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{k+1}(2k-1)!!\,\zeta(k+2)T^{k},

from which it follows that

f_{\pm}(T)-f_{\pm}(0)\sim-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(m^{k+1}-1)2^{k+1}(2k-1)!!\,\zeta(k+2)T^{k+2}\,.

(39)

In particular, truncating the series after the first term and using Eq. (
10
) we obtain
Eq. (
28
).

Consider, finally, the HS-B chain with
\gamma=0
, for which
{\mathcal{E}}(x)=x(1-x/2)
. From Eqs. (
22
)
and (
27
) it easily follows that

f_{\pm}(T)-f_{\pm}(0)=2\left(f_{\text{HS},\pm}(T/2)-f_{\text{HS},\pm}(0)\right)\,,

whence we obtain the asymptotic series

f_{\pm}(T)-f_{\pm}(0)\sim-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(m^{k+1}-1)(2k-1)!!\,\zeta(k+2)T^{k+2}\,.

Again, the first term in this series is easily seen to yield Eq. (
24
).

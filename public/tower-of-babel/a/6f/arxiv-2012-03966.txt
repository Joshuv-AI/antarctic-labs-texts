Spanier-Whitehead duality for Topological coHochschild homology
Haldun Özgür Bayındır, Maximilien Péroux

Abstract
. In this work, we compute the topological coHochschild homology (coTHH) of interesting coalgebras such as the Steenrod algebra spectrum. For this, we start by extending the Hess-Shipley definition of coTHH to ∞ \infty -categories, following the Nikolaus-Scholze approach to THH. Furthermore, we prove that coTHH of what we call quasi-proper coalgebras can be obtained from THH via Spanier-Whitehead duality which provides further insight into coTHH and its relationship to THH.

1.
Introduction

Background

Topological Hochschild homology (THH) for ring spectra extends the notion of Hochschild homology for algebras. The topological persepctive provides new insight on rings.
For instance, the Hochschild homology of the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a divided power algebra, but Bökstedt shows that the topological Hochschild homology of its Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectrum
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a polynomial ring. More recent examples can be found in the work of
[
BMS19
]
.
THH plays also an important role as an invariant for algebras in spectra thanks to its connections with
K
-theory via the Dennis trace map. Furthermore, THH is of interest for string topology. For a connected topological space
X
, there is an equivalence

\mathsf{THH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Omega X)\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathscr{L}X,

where
\mathscr{L}X
denotes the free loop space on
X
. This goes back to
[
Lod98
, Theorem 7.3.11]
; see
[
NS18
, IV.3.2, IV.3.3]
for a modern reference.

Topological coHochschild homology (coTHH) is a homology theory for coalgebras in spectra introduced by Hess-Shipley in
[
HS21
]
, analogous to THH for ring spectra.
Furthermore, coTHH extends the notion of coHochschild homology (coHH) introduced in
[
Doi81
]
and
[
HPS09
]
to coalgebras in a symmetric monoidal model category
[
BGH+18
, 2.3]
.
One reason why this invariant of coalgebras is important is because coalgebraic structures are shown to contain valuable information regarding topological spaces. For instance,
[
RWZ20
]
establish that homotopy type of a topological space is entirely captured by the coalgebraic structure of its singular chain complex. Furthermore, Hess and Shipley prove that Waldhausen
K
-theory of a topological space
X
can be obtained using the category of comodules over the coalgebra
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X

[
HS16
]
.

Just like THH, topological coHochschild homology also provides a new model for free loop spaces. Namely, Hess and Shipley show that there is an equivalence

\mathsf{coTHH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X)\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathscr{L}X

for every “EMSS-good space”
X
; in particular, for every simply connected space
X

[
HS21
]
.
Connecting with the result above, for every EMSS-good space
X
, one obtains an instance of Koszul duality between THH and coTHH:

\mathsf{THH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Omega X)\simeq\mathsf{coTHH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X).

In
[
BGH+18
, 2.3]
, the authors construct a coBökstedt spectral sequence for coTHH and show that this spectral sequence has a “
\square
-Hopf algebra structure”, i.e. it is endowed with a comultiplicative structure that is compatible with its multiplicative structure. The
E_{2}
-page of this spectral sequence is computed for various interesting coalgebra spectra in
[
Kla22
]
. In
[
BGS22
]
, Bohmann, Gerhardt and Shipley use the
\square
-Hopf algebra structure on the coBökstedt spectral sequence and the equivalence above to obtain homology computations for various free loop spaces generalizing earlier computations of
[
KY97
]
.
Recently, Ayala and Francis defined factorization cohomology which generalizes coTHH for
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-coalgebras to obtain a Poincaré duality result for factorization homology
[
AF21
]
.
From work in progress, the second author and Klanderman provide a shadow structure in the sense of
[
Pon10
]
for coHH, resulting in new interesting bicategorical traces for coHH
[
KP
]
.

Computations

In this work, we compute the topological coHochschild homology of new coalgebras not considered before. For this, we carry the definition of coTHH to the
\infty
-categorical setting and we prove a duality relationship between coTHH and THH which provides further insight to coTHH, and also to THH.

Our main computation is the coHochschild homology groups of the Steenrod algebra spectrum
{[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}
. Here
{[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}
denotes the spectrum of
\mathbb{S}
-module endomorphisms of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Firstly, we show that
{[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}
inherits the structure of an
{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}
-
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra, i.e. an
{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra in
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules, as it is the linear dual of the
{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}
-
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge H\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Note that this does not follow by the correspondence between
{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}
-algebras and
{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}
-coalgebras on dualizable objects (Proposition
3.11
), given by the dualization functor. This is because
{[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}
is not dualizable in
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules as the Steenrod algebra

\mathcal{A}\cong\pi_{*}\left({[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}\right)

is infinite dimensional, see Example
3.9
. On the other hand, our Theorem
5.8
, which generalizes the correspondence on dualizable objects, does equip
{[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}
with the structure of an
{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}
-
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra coming from the
{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}
-
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra structure of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge H\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Using our new duality result for coTHH (Theorem
1.3
), together with Bökstedt periodicity, we compute the coHochschild homology groups of
{[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
7.7
)
.

There is an equivalence of graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules:

\pi_{*}\left(\mathsf{coTHH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left({[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}\right)\right)\cong\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{-2}],

where
\lvert x_{-2}\rvert=-2
.

Moreover, using Theorem
1.3
, we do the following computations.

•

Theorem
7.2
: there is an
{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra structure on
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}
as an
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-module, and its coHochschild homology is given by:

\pi_{*}\left(\mathsf{coTHH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})\right)\cong\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(x_{1})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}[[t]],

where
\lvert t\rvert=0
and
\lvert x_{1}\rvert=1
. This completes the result of
[
Kla22
]
in which the
E_{2}
-page of the relative coBökstedt spectral sequence from
[
BGH+18
]
computing the homotopy groups above was calculated.

•

7.8
: there is an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra structure on
\Omega H\mathbb{F}_{p}
as an
H\mathbb{Z}
-module and its topological coHochschild homology is given by:

\pi_{*}\left(\mathsf{coTHH}^{H\mathbb{Z}}(\Omega H\mathbb{F}_{p})\right)\cong\Omega\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{-2}],

where
\lvert x_{-2}\rvert=-2
and
\Omega
on the right hand side denotes the functor that decreases the grading by 1.

From model categories to higher categories

We mentioned earlier the importance of coalgebraic structures in spectra. However, there is no model category of spectra that accurately captures the homotopy theory of its coalgebras, see
[
PS19
]
and
[
Pér22b
, 5.6]
.
An
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra in spectra is a spectrum endowed with a comultiplication that is coassociative and counital up to higher homotopy.
Given an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra, there is not, in general, a choice of monoidal model category of spectra in which the
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra structure can be rigidified to a strictly coassociative counital coalgebra structure.
The major disadvantage of model categories is that the Spanier-Whitehead duality, that relates
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebras with
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebras in finite spectra (see
[
Lur18
, 3.2.5]
), cannot be realized at the level of strict algebras and coalgebras. Essentially, model categories currently can only capture coalgebras of the form
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X
induced by the diagonal on the space
X
. It is for this very reason that the work of
[
HS21
]
,
[
BGH+18
]
, and
[
Kla22
]
is limited to the computation of
\mathsf{coTHH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X)
.

If a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
is endowed with an internal hom
[-,-]
, we can define the notion of linear duality that generalizes the classical dual of a vector space over a field, or the Spanier-Whitehead dual of a spectrum. For
X
an object in
\mathcal{C}
we denote
X^{\vee}=[X,\mathbb{I}]
the linear dual of
X
, where
\mathbb{I}
denotes the monoidal unit of
\mathcal{C}
.
If
C
is a coalgebra in
\mathcal{C}
, then
C^{\vee}
is always an algebra in
\mathcal{C}
.
It is well-known that algebras and coalgebras in finite dimensional vector spaces are anti-equivalent, see
[
Swe69
, I.1.1]
. Similarly, by
[
Lur18
, 3.2.5]
(see also Corollary
3.13
below), the linear dual provides an anti-equivalence between
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebras and
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebras in dualizable objects in
\mathcal{C}
.

To be able to incorporate Spanier-Whitehead duality into our computational framework, we extend the definition of coTHH to any symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
, following the THH approach of
[
NS18
]
. Indeed, our computations provide an instance demonstrating the strength of
\infty
-categories for computational purposes.

A duality relationship between coTHH and THH

Using the anti-equivalence between
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebras and
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebras in dualizable objects mentioned above, and our
\infty
-categorical definition of coTHH, we prove the following theorem that relates THH and coTHH of algebras and coalgebras in dualizable objects via the linear dual.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
R
be an
{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}
-ring spectrum.
If
C
is an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra over
R
, whose underlying
R
-module is dualizable, then there is an equivalence of
R
-module spectra

\mathsf{coTHH}^{R}(C)\simeq\left(\mathsf{THH}^{R}(C^{\vee})\right)^{\vee}.

We in fact generalize the anti-equivalence on dualizable objects to larger subclasses of algebras and coalgebras where the dualization functor is strong monoidal, see Theorem
3.31
. We also extend the duality relationship between THH and coTHH to these new subclasses. The main ingredient for these generalizations is our notions of
quasi-dualizability
(Definition
3.20
) and
quasi-proper
coalgebras (Definition
3.27
) which are weaker conditions than dualizability, see Remark
3.22
. We show in Theorem
4.1
that the equivalence in Theorem
1.2
also holds for quasi-proper
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebras.

With specific examples in mind, such as the Steenrod algebra spectrum mentioned earlier, we are led to consider a more general class of
HA
-coalgebras than those whose underlying
HA
-modules are dualizable. Here
A
denotes a discrete commutative ring.
Through careful Tor and Ext spectral sequence considerations, we are able to show that these
HA
-coalgebras are indeed quasi-proper, see Section
5
. This provides Theorem
1.3
below, which extends Theorem
1.2
for
HA
-coalgebras. Indeed, this generalization turns out to be very useful for computations. For instance, our computation of the coTHH of the Steenrod algebra spectrum relies on this generalization.

We say an
HA
-module
X
is of
finite type
if
\pi_{i}X
is a finitely generated
A
-module for each
i
. Recall that a ring
A
is said to have finite global dimension if there is an integer
d
such that every
A
-module
M
admits a projective resolution of length at most
d
.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
A
be a discrete commutative Noetherian ring with finite global dimension and let
C
be a connective or coconnective
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra over
HA
of finite type. Then there is an equivalence of
HA
-module spectra

\mathsf{coTHH}^{HA}(C)\simeq\left(\mathsf{THH}^{HA}(C^{\vee})\right)^{\vee}.

We obtain the following new insight on THH and coTHH.

•

Example
6.4
: The suspension spectrum of a finite CW-complex
X
is a dualizable object in spectra. When
X
is simply connected, one has

\left(\mathsf{THH}((\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X)^{\vee})\right)^{\vee}\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathscr{L}X,

due to
[
Kuh04
]
and
[
Mal17
]
. We obtain a new proof of this equality using Theorem
1.2
and the results of
[
HS21
]
. Indeed, this generalizes the equality above to EMSS-good finite CW-complexes. Furthermore, this provides the following Koszul duality relationship for THH:

\left(\mathsf{THH}((\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X)^{\vee})\right)^{\vee}\simeq\mathsf{THH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Omega X).

•

Example
6.6
: if
G
is a compact Lie group, then the Thom spectrum
G^{-\tau}
of its stable normal bundle is endowed with an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra structure in spectra, induced by the group multiplication of
G
, and its coTHH is given by:

\mathsf{coTHH}(G^{-\tau})\simeq\left(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathscr{L}BG\right)^{\vee},

where
\mathscr{L}BG
is the free loop space of the classifying space of
G
. By
[
Pér22b
]
, such a computation could not have been considered in the framework of
[
HS21
]
.

Outline

In Section

. Section

We provide here, in Definition

Cocommutative coalgebras in

.
Taking the opposite yields a coCartesian fibration
{\widehat{p}}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}:(\widehat{\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
.
This coCartesian fibration provides a
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal structure on
\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}
.
Notice moreover that if we view
\mathcal{O}
as a
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal category, then its
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal structure is given by the coCartesian fibration
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{id}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
. Thus the associated Cartesian fibration
\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}}\rightarrow(\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}
is simply
(\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{id}}}{{\rightarrow}}(\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}
. Hence, taking opposites again, the opposite
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal structure of
\mathcal{O}
is given by the coCartesian fibration
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{id}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
, i.e. is
\mathcal{O}
again. Hence we obtain:

\mathsf{Fun}^{\mathsf{colax}}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{C})=(\mathsf{Fun}^{\mathsf{lax}}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}})){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\simeq(\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}})){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}=\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}).\qed

Remark 2.6
.

A functor

\mathcal{O}
-monoidal functor if and only if its opposite

\mathcal{O}
-monoidal functor. This follows from

\mathcal{O}
-monoidal.

In order to define the cyclic bar and cobar construction in the

Let

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
be a strong symmetric monoidal functor between symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories. Then:

F\circ\mathsf{Bar}_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{C}}(-)\simeq\mathsf{Bar}_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{D}}(-)\circ F.

Dually, we also obtain:

F\circ\mathsf{coBar}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{C}}(-)\simeq\mathsf{coBar}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(-)\circ F.

Proof.

By
[
Kee20
, 4.4]
, we obtain the commutative diagram of
\infty
-categories:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{C}^{\otimes})_{\mathsf{act}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{D}^{\otimes})_{\mathsf{act}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{\mathsf{act}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\otimes}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\otimes}

Let
A
be an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebra in
\mathcal{C}
. By the above result, we obtain that the composite

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{Assoc}^{\otimes})_{\mathsf{act}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{C}^{\otimes})_{\mathsf{act}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\otimes}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\otimes}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

is equivalent to the composite

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{Assoc}^{\otimes})_{\mathsf{act}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{D}^{\otimes})_{\mathsf{act}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(A)^{\otimes}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\otimes}

Therefore, we obtain
F\left(\mathsf{Bar}_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{C}}(A)\right)\simeq\mathsf{Bar}_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{D}}(F(A))
. We obtain the dual statement for cobar by applying our above argument to the strong symmetric monoidal functor
F{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}:\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}
.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
\mathsf{M}
be a combinatorial symmetric monoidal model category.
Let
C
be a coassociative coalgebra in
\mathsf{M}
that is cofibrant as an object in
\mathsf{M}
.
Then
\mathsf{coTHH}^{\mathsf{M}}(C)
is weakly equivalent to
\mathsf{coTHH}^{N(\mathsf{M}_{c})\left[\mathsf{W}^{-1}\right]}(C)
.

Proof.

In
[
Pér22b
, 3.1]
, there is a functor of
\infty
-categories:

\iota:N(\mathsf{coAlg}(\mathsf{M}_{c}))\big[{{\mathsf{W}^{\prime}}^{-1}}\big]\longrightarrow\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}\left({N(\mathsf{M}_{c})\left[\mathsf{W}^{-1}\right]}\right),

which is the identity on objects in
{N(\mathsf{M}_{c})\left[\mathsf{W}^{-1}\right]}
. Here
{\mathsf{W}^{\prime}}
denotes the weak equivalences in
\mathsf{M}
that are also maps in
\mathsf{CoAlg}(\mathsf{M}_{c})
.
The functor
\iota
allows one to consider the (strictly) coassociative counital coalgebra
C
in
\mathsf{M}
as an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra
\iota(C)
in the Dwyer-Kan localization of
\mathsf{M}
.
Since the localization
N(\mathsf{M}_{c})\rightarrow{N(\mathsf{M}_{c})\left[\mathsf{W}^{-1}\right]}
is a strong symmetric monoidal functor, then by Remark
2.7
,
[
Lur17
, 1.3.4.25]
, and Lemma
2.12
, the two definitions of the cyclic cobar complexes agree. The desired result follows by
[
Lur17
, 1.3.4.23]
, as the homotopy limit of the cyclic cobar construction of

in
\mathsf{M}
is equivalent to the totalization of the cyclic cobar construction of

in the Dwyer-Kan localization of

.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

In the proof of Proposition
2.13
, we are not claiming that the
\infty
-category
N(\mathsf{coAlg}(\mathsf{M}_{c}))[{{\mathsf{W}^{\prime}}^{-1}}]
is the Dwyer-Kan localization of a model structure on
\mathsf{CoAlg}(\mathsf{M})
as such a model structure may not exist, see Remark

below. Here we are instead referring to the Dwyer-Kan localization of the
\infty
-category
N(\mathsf{coAlg}(\mathsf{M}_{c}))
with respect to the class of edges induced by the weak equivalences

, as in
[

, 1.3.4.1]
.

Remark 2.15
.

The model category approach faces several challenges. First of all, given a combinatorial symmetric monoidal model category
\mathsf{M}
, there are no conditions in general to lift the model structure on
\mathsf{M}
to a model structure on the category of (strictly) coassociative counital coalgebras
\mathsf{CoAlg}(\mathsf{M})
. In the cases where a model structure exists, the model structure of
\mathsf{M}
is generally replaced by a Quillen equivalent one that is not a monoidal model category, see
[
HKRS17
,
GKR20
]
. Second, given a model structure in
\mathsf{CoAlg}(\mathsf{M})
induced by one on
\mathsf{M}
, there is no reason to expect that strictly coassociative coalgebras in
\mathsf{M}
are equivalent to
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebras in the Dwyer-Kan localization of
\mathsf{M}
, unlike the case for algebras (as in
[
Lur17
, 4.1.8.4]
). As a matter of fact, there is indication that these do
not
correspond. In particular, for
\mathsf{M}
any symmetric monoidal model category of spectra, such as symmetric spectra or orthogonal spectra, it is shown that strictly coassociative coalgebras do
not
correspond to
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebras in the
\infty
-category of spectra
\mathsf{Sp}
, see
[
PS19
]
and
[
Pér22b
, 5.6]
.

3.
Duality Between Algebras and Coalgebras

From a coalgebra, one can always obtain an associated algebra by taking the dual. The converse is in general not true. We make this fact precise in this section.
We also show here equivalences between subclasses of algebras and coalgebras that we introduce.

3.1.
The Linear Dual Functor

We introduce the notion of dualization for closed symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories. We recall the well-known result that the dual of a coalgebra is always an algebra.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a closed symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category. Denote

[-,-]:\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\times\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C},

its internal hom defined by the universal property
\mathcal{C}(X\otimes Y,Z)\simeq\mathcal{C}(X,[Y,Z]).
It is lax symmetric monoidal, see
[
Hin15
, A.5.3]
or
[
Hau20
, I.3]
.
Let
\mathbb{I}
be the unit of the symmetric monoidal structure of
\mathcal{C}
.
For any object
X
in
\mathcal{C}
, define
X^{\vee}
to be
[X,\mathbb{I}]
. This defines a lax symmetric monoidal functor:

(-)^{\vee}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{C},

called the
linear dual functor
. We denote its opposite
((-)^{\vee})^{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}
again by:

(-)^{\vee}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}},

and also refer to it as the linear dual functor.

Example 3.2
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is the nerve of the category of vector spaces over a field
k
, then the linear dual functor
(-)^{\vee}
is precisely the dual of a vector space over
k
.

Example 3.3
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is the
\infty
-category of spectra
\mathsf{Sp}
, then the linear dual is precisely the Spanier-Whitehead dual.

The linear dual is what is called a
self-adjoint (contravariant) functor
: a property that we describe in the following result.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a closed symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category. Then we obtain an adjunction:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}

Proof.

This is a classical result in ordinary categories. It follows from the string of equivalences:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}(X^{\vee},Y)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(Y,X^{\vee})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(Y\otimes X,\mathbb{I})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(X\otimes Y,\mathbb{I})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(X,Y^{\vee}),

for every
X
and
Y
in
\mathcal{C}
. Here we used the natural symmetry equivalence
X\otimes Y\simeq Y\otimes X
. If we choose
Y=X^{\vee}
, then the identity morphism
X^{\vee}\rightarrow X^{\vee}
in
\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}
defines a natural morphism
X\rightarrow X^{\vee\vee}
via the equivalence

\mathcal{C}(X,X^{\vee\vee})\simeq\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}(X^{\vee},X^{\vee}),

defined above. This provides the unit of the adjunction (see
[
Lur09
, 5.2.2.8]
).
∎

The right adjoint functor
(-)^{\vee}:\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
, being lax symmetric monoidal, lifts to the category of algebras

(-)^{\vee}:\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\simeq{\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}})}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}),

for any
\infty
-operad
\mathcal{O}
. This functor provides a description of the well-known fact that the dual of a coalgebra is always an algebra.
Since limits in algebras are computed in their underlying category, the linear dual
(-)^{\vee}:\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\rightarrow\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})
remains a limit-preserving functor.

However, as the linear dual is not strong monoidal in general, it does not lift to a functor
(-)^{\vee}:\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathcal{O}}(C){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}
.
In other words, if
A
is an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra in
\mathcal{C}
, then we cannot claim
A^{\vee}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-coalgebra in
\mathcal{C}
in general. We are interested in the cases where an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra
A
induces an
\mathcal{O}
-coalgebra structure on its linear dual via a comultiplication induced by:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A\otimes A)^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\vee}\otimes A^{\vee},}

where the vertical map is an equivalence.
We are also interesting in knowing conditions for which the linear dual functor
(-)^{\vee}:\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\rightarrow\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})
is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories.

3.2.
Proper Algebras and Coalgebras

It is a well-known result that algebras and coalgebras over finite dimensional vector spaces are anti-equivalent. We remind here the result of
[
Lur18
]
that shows this in greater generality.

Definition 3.5

(
[
Lur17
, 4.6.1]
)
.

An object
X
is said to be
dualizable
in a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
if there exists another object
Y
in
\mathcal{C}
, together with maps:

e:Y\otimes X\rightarrow\mathbb{I},\qquad c:\mathbb{I}\rightarrow X\otimes Y,

for which the composite maps:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\simeq\mathbb{I}\otimes X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\otimes Y\otimes X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\otimes\mathbb{I}\simeq X,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c\otimes\mathsf{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}\otimes e}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y\simeq Y\otimes\mathbb{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y\otimes X\otimes Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{I}\otimes Y\simeq Y,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}\otimes c}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e\otimes\mathsf{id}}

are homotopic to the identity. In other words, an object
X
is dualizable if and only if it is dualizable in the homotopy category
h\mathcal{C}
, see
[
Lur17
, 4.6.1.6]
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{fd}}
denote the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
spanned by the dualizable objects. By
[
Lur18
, 3.2.4]
, the tensor product of
\mathcal{C}
is closed in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{fd}}
and thus
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{fd}}
is also symmetric monoidal.

Example 3.6

(
[
Lur18
, 3.2.1]
)
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is the nerve of the category of vector spaces over a field
k
, then the dualizable objects are precisely the finite dimensional vector spaces over
k
.

Example 3.7
.

The dualizable objects in the
\infty
-category of spectra
\mathsf{Sp}
are precisely the finite spectra.

Example 3.8

(
[
Lur18
, 3.2.3]
)
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring spectrum. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the
\infty
-category of
R
-modules
\mathsf{Mod}_{R}(\mathsf{Sp})
. Then the dualizable objects in
\mathcal{C}
are precisely the compact objects in
\mathcal{C}
, i.e. the perfect
R
-modules. An
R
-module is said to be perfect if it lies in the smallest subcategory of
\textup{Ho}(\mathsf{Mod}_{R}(\mathsf{Sp}))
that contains
R
and is closed under triangles and retracts.

Example 3.9
.

Let
k
be a field. An
Hk
-module
X
in
\mathsf{Sp}
is dualizable in
\mathsf{Mod}_{Hk}(\mathsf{Sp})
if and only if
\pi_{*}X
is finitely generated as a graded
k
-module.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a closed symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category. If
X
is dualizable in
\mathcal{C}
, then its dual is equivalent to the linear dual
X^{\vee}
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a dualizable object in
\mathcal{C}
, with dual
Y
. Then
Y\otimes-:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
is a right adjoint of
X\otimes-:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
. The unit and counit of the adjunction are induced by the maps
e:Y\otimes X\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
and
c:\mathbb{I}\rightarrow X\otimes Y
. The universal property of tensor product with its internal hom gives the equivalence of functors on
\mathcal{C}
:

[X,-]\simeq Y\otimes-,

as they are both the right adjoint of the functor
X\otimes-:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
.
Then in particular we get the equivalence
X^{\vee}\simeq Y
.
∎

Proposition 3.11

(
[
Lur18
, 3.2.4]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a closed symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category. Then the linear dual determines an equivalence of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories:

(-)^{\vee}:(\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{fd}}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{fd}},

with the inverse equivalence given by itself.

Definition 3.12

(
[
Lur17
, 4.6.4.2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category. Let
\mathcal{O}
be an
\infty
-operad. An
\mathcal{O}
-algebra

in
\mathcal{C}
is said to be

if
A
is dualizable as an object in
\mathcal{C}
. Similarly, we say that an
\mathcal{O}
-coalgebra
C
in
\mathcal{C}
is
proper
if it is dualizable as an object in
\mathcal{C}
.

There is an anti-equivalence between proper algebras and proper coalgebras, over any
\infty
-operad, given by the linear dual functor. This follows directly from Proposition

.

Corollary 3.13

(
[
Lur18
, 3.2.5]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a closed symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category. Let
\mathcal{O}
be an
\infty
-operad. Then the linear dual induces an equivalence:

(-)^{\vee}:\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{fd}}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{fd}}),

where the inverse equivalence is given by itself.

3.3.
Quasi-Proper Algebras and Coalgebras

We want to generalize the anti-equivalence between proper algebras and coalgebras of Corollary
3.13
, by considering a larger class of algebras and coalgebras. We do so in Theorem
3.31
below. We introduce the notions of quasi-proper algebras and coalgebras. This allows us to extend the equivalence between THH and coTHH (Theorem
1.2
) in the next section, see Theorem
4.1
.
Given
A
an algebra, we are also seeking conditions for its linear dual
A^{\vee}
to be a coalgebra.

Condition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a closed symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category.
We say an object
X
in
\mathcal{C}
satisfies this condition if the lax symmetric monoidal structure map of the linear dual functor
(-)^{\vee}:\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
:

{(X^{\vee})}^{\otimes n}\longrightarrow{(X^{\otimes n})}^{\vee}

is an equivalence in
\mathcal{C}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Condition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a closed symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category.
We say an object
X
in
\mathcal{C}
satisfies this condition if the natural unit map from the self-adjoint property of the linear dual

X\longrightarrow X^{\vee\vee}

is an equivalence in
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 3.14
.

In Condition
1
, the case
n=0
is stating that
\mathbb{I}^{\vee}\simeq\mathbb{I}
which is always satisfied, where
\mathbb{I}
is the unit of the closed symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
. The case
n=1
is also always satisfied for any
X
in
\mathcal{C}
.

We provide examples of spectra and examples of chain complexes that do not satisfy these conditions. These examples provide a justification for the hypothesis of Theorem
1.3
.

Example 3.15
.

The Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectrum
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
satisfies Condition
1
but does not satisfy Condition
2
in the
\infty
-category of spectra
\mathsf{Sp}
.
It is known that the Spanier–Whitehead dual of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
is trivial
[
Lin76
]
. This shows that

H\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\vee\vee}\simeq 0

and that
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
does not satisfy Condition
2
in
\mathsf{Sp}
. Furthermore, we have the equivalences

[H\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\wedge n},\mathbb{S}]\simeq[H\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\wedge(n-1)},[H\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{S}]]\simeq 0

and therefore both sides of the map in Condition
1
are trivial. Therefore,
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
satisfies Condition 1 in
\mathsf{Sp}
.

Example 3.16
.

We provide here an example of an object that satisfies Condition
2
but not Condition
1
in the
\infty
-category of
Hk
-modules
\mathsf{Mod}_{Hk}(\mathsf{Sp})
, where
k
is a field. The homotopy category of
Hk
-modules is equivalent to the category of graded
k
-modules as a symmetric monoidal category. This follows from the Künneth and Ext spectral sequences in
[
EKMM97
, IV.4.1]
.
Let
X
denote the
Hk
-module corresponding to the chain complex
k[x_{1},x_{1}^{-1}]
with trivial differentials where
\lvert x_{1}\rvert=1
. Note that the homology of this chain complex and therefore the homotopy of the corresponding
Hk
-module is
k[x_{1},x_{1}^{-1}]
. Since dualization in
Hk
-modules results in graded
k
-module dualization at the level of homotopy groups, we have:

\pi_{*}(X^{\vee})\cong k[x_{1},x_{1}^{-1}]^{\vee}\cong k[x_{1},x_{1}^{-1}].

By inspection, it is clear that
X
satisfies Condition
2
as an
Hk
-module. Note that
X\wedge_{Hk}X
has countably infinite dimensional homotopy groups at each degree. For instance, the basis for degree zero homotopy group is given by
x_{1}^{i}\otimes x_{1}^{-i}
over all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore
(X\wedge_{Hk}X)^{\vee}
has
uncountably
infinite dimensional homotopy at each degree. On the other hand,
X^{\vee}\wedge_{Hk}X^{\vee}
has countably infinite dimensional homotopy at each degree. Therefore the map

X^{\vee}\wedge_{Hk}X^{\vee}\to(X\wedge_{Hk}X)^{\vee}

cannot be surjective in homotopy showing that
X
does not satisfy Condition
1
. This example justifies the (co)connectivity hypothesis in Theorem
1.3
.

Example 3.17
.

Let
X
be a countably infinite dimensional
k
-vector space where
k
is a field. Let
HX
denote the corresponding
Hk
-module. Neil Strickland explains
1

1

https://mathoverflow.net/questions/56255/duals-and-tensor-products
that
HX
does not satisfy Condition
1
in
Hk
-modules because the map mentioned in Condition
1
is not surjective in homotopy for
HX
. To see this, let
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a basis for
X
. We consider the map in Condition
1
for the case

. At the level of homotopy, this map

\varphi\colon\thinspace X^{\vee}\otimes_{k}X^{\vee}\to(X\otimes_{k}X)^{\vee}

is given by:

\varphi(f\otimes_{k}g)(x\otimes_{k}y)=f(x)g(y).

The

-linear map

given by

if

and
h(x_{i}\otimes_{k}x_{i})=1
for all

is not in the image of

. This is due to the fact that the matrix

has infinite rank where the matrix

Furthermore, dualization increases cardinality of an infinite dimensional vector space. In particular,

has a larger cardinality than

and therefore

does not satisfy Condition

either.

We now record some results that capture if the linear dual preserves the conditions above.

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a closed symmetric monoidal
\infty

We have the equivalence:

∎

Remark 3.22
.

Corollary
3.21
shows that we have the implications:

\text{dualizable}\Rightarrow\text{quasi-dualizable}\Rightarrow\text{weak quasi-dualizable}.

Example
3.15
provides an instance of an object that is weak quasi-dualizable but not quasi-dualizable; therefore, weak quasi-dualizability is a strictly weaker condition than quasi-dualizability. For a Noetherian ring
A
with finite global dimension, we show in Section

that a (co)connective finite type

-module has unbounded homotopy, then it is not dualizable due to Proposition

is quasi-dualizable but
not
dualizable in

-modules.

Remark 3.23
.

The distinction made in the previous remark is not seen in the classical case.
Indeed, let
\mathcal{C}
be the nerve the category of vector spaces over a field
k
.
We saw in Example
3.6
that a vector space is dualizable if and only if it is finite dimensional. In fact, the discussion in Example
3.17
shows that infinite dimensional vector spaces cannot satisfy Condition

nor Condition

. Therefore, given a vector space

over

, the following are equivalent:

(1)

is finite dimensional,

(2)

is dualizable,

(3)

is quasi-dualizable,

(4)

is weak quasi-dualizable, i.e. satisfies Condition

,

(5)

V
satisfies Condition
2
.

Proposition 3.25
.

Let
\mathcal{C}

Let

We prove the above proposition after showing the following lemma.

Lemma 3.26
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories. Let
\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{C}}
and
\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{D}}
be the unit objects of the monoidal structures of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
respectively. Let
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
be a lax symmetric monoidal functor. If for all objects
X
and
Y
in
\mathcal{C}
, the natural maps
F(X)\otimes F(Y)\rightarrow F(X\otimes Y)
are equivalences, and the natural map
\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow F(\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{C}})
is an equivalence, then
F
is a strong symmetric monoidal functor.

Proof.

Let
p:\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}\rightarrow N(\mathsf{Fin}_{*})
and
q:\mathcal{D}^{\otimes}\rightarrow N(\mathsf{Fin}_{*})
be the coCartesian fibrations that endow
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
of a symmetric monoidal structure. We need to show that
F
sends
p
-coCartesian lifts to
q
-coCartesian lifts.

We first recall how we obtain the natural maps
F(X)\otimes F(Y)\rightarrow F(X\otimes Y)
.
Let
m:\langle 2\rangle\rightarrow\langle 1\rangle
be the map in
\mathsf{Fin}_{*}
that sends both
1
and
2
to
1
. Let
X
and
Y
be objects in
\mathcal{C}
. On the one hand, we have the
p
-coCartesian lift of
m
with respect to
(X,Y)
given by the edge
(X,Y)\rightarrow X\otimes Y
in
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}
that represents the tensor product:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{\langle 2\rangle}^{\otimes}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m_{!}}

Similarly, we obtain the
q
-coCartesian lift
(F(X),F(Y))\rightarrow F(X)\otimes F(Y)
in
\mathcal{D}^{\otimes}
. On the other hand, since
m
is not an inert morphism in
\mathsf{Fin}_{*}
, then the induced map
F(X,Y)\rightarrow F(X\otimes Y)
is not a priori a
q
-coCartesian lift. However, since the Segal condition
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}_{\langle n\rangle}\simeq\mathcal{C}^{\times n}
is induced by the inert morphisms
\langle n\rangle\rightarrow\langle 1\rangle
, and
F
is lax symmetric monoidal, we obtain an equivalence
F(X,Y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\rightarrow}}(F(X),F(Y))
.
By the universal property of coCartesian lifts, we obtain a unique map up to contractible choice
F(X)\otimes F(Y)\rightarrow F(X\otimes Y)
such that we have the commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F(X),F(Y))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(X)\otimes F(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(X\otimes Y).}

If we assume that the dashed map is an equivalence, then by unicity of the coCartesian lifts, we see that
F
sends
p
-coCartesian lifts of
m
to
q
-coCartesian lifts.

Similarly, the unique map
\iota:\langle 0\rangle\rightarrow\langle 1\rangle
in
\mathsf{Fin}_{*}
induces the map
\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow F(\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{C}})
, and if we require it to be an equivalence,
F
sends
p
-coCartesian lifts of
\iota
to a
q
-coCartesian lift.

Notice that morphisms in
\mathsf{Fin}_{*}
are generated under wedge sum, composition, inert morphisms and the maps
\iota
and
m
. If
\phi:\langle n\rangle\rightarrow\langle k\rangle
and
\phi^{\prime}:\langle n^{\prime}\rangle\rightarrow\langle k^{\prime}\rangle
are maps in
\mathsf{Fin}_{*}
, then by the Segal condition and the universality of coCartesian lifts, we obtain an equivalence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{\langle n+n^{\prime}\rangle}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{\langle k+k^{\prime}\rangle}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{\langle n\rangle}\times\mathcal{C}_{\langle n^{\prime}\rangle}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{\langle k\rangle}\times\mathcal{C}_{\langle k^{\prime}\rangle}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\phi\vee\phi^{\prime})_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{!}\times\phi^{\prime}_{!}}

Therefore
F
sends
p
-coCartesian lifts to
q
-coCartesian lifts.
∎

Proof of Proposition

.

For any object

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}

is strong symmetric monoidal, by applying the dual of Lemma

Theorem 3.31
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a closed symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category. Let
\mathcal{O}
be an
\infty
-operad. Then the linear dual functor induces an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

(-)^{\vee}:((\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}))_{\star}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}))_{\star}

where the inverse equivalence is also given by the linear dual functor
(-)^{\vee}
.

Proof.

We first begin to show that the linear dual functor

(-)^{\vee}:\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\rightarrow\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}),

restricts and corestricts to the functor:

(-)^{\vee}:((\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}))_{\star}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\longrightarrow(\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}))_{\star}.

Let
C
be a quasi-proper
\mathcal{O}
-coalgebra in
\mathcal{C}
. Then by assumption, its linear dual
C^{\vee}
satisfies Condition
1
as an object in
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover, it also satisfies Condition
2
by Lemma
3.18
. Thus
C^{\vee}
is a quasi-proper
\mathcal{O}
-algebra in
\mathcal{C}
.

We now provide its inverse. In Proposition
3.25
, we have shown that the linear dual functor lifts to a functor

(-)^{\vee}:(\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}))_{\circ}\longrightarrow(\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}.

We now show it restricts and corestricts to:

(-)^{\vee}:(\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}))_{\star}\longrightarrow((\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}))_{\star}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}.

We need to verify that for
A
a quasi-proper
\mathcal{O}
-algebra in
\mathcal{C}
, then
A^{\vee}
is a quasi-proper
\mathcal{O}
-coalgebra in
\mathcal{C}
. Since
A
satisfies Condition
2
as an object in
\mathcal{C}
, then so does
A^{\vee}
by Lemma
3.18
. Since
A
satisfies Condition
1
and that
A^{\vee\vee}\simeq A
, then
A^{\vee\vee}
satisfies Condition
1
as an object of
\mathcal{C}
. Thus
A^{\vee}
is indeed a quasi-proper
\mathcal{O}
-coalgebra.

We now argue that given a quasi-proper
\mathcal{O}
-algebra
A
, the unit map
A\rightarrow A^{\vee\vee}
in
\mathcal{C}
of the adjunction of Proposition
3.4
is a map in
\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})
.
If we let
X
be a quasi-dualizable object in
\mathcal{C}
, then as in the proof of Proposition
3.25
, the linear dual functor restricts to a strong monoidal functor
\mathcal{C}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}
. Notice that its image is precisely
(\mathcal{C}_{X^{\vee}}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}
.
Restricting the linear dual functor
\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
to
(\mathcal{C}_{X^{\vee}}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}
, we obtain a lax symmetric monoidal functor
(\mathcal{C}_{X^{\vee}}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\rightarrow(\mathcal{C}_{X})
as we have the equivalence of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories
\mathcal{C}_{X}\simeq\mathcal{C}_{X^{\vee\vee}}
by
[
Lur17
, 2.1.3.8]
as
X
satisfies Condition
2
.
Therefore, the adjunction of Proposition
3.4
restricts and corestricts to the full subcategories:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{C}_{X^{\vee}}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}

The left adjoint is strong symmetric monoidal and thus lifts to an adjunction:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}_{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}\left((\mathcal{C}_{X^{\vee}}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\right).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}

If we apply the discussion above to a quasi-proper algebra
X=A
, we obtain in particular that the unit of the above adjunction
A\rightarrow A^{\vee\vee}
is a map of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras in
\mathcal{C}
. We can argue similarly to show that
C\rightarrow C^{\vee\vee}
is a map of
\mathcal{O}
-coalgebras for
C
a quasi-proper
\mathcal{O}
-coalgebra.

We can therefore lift the adjunction of Proposition
3.4
to an adjunction:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}))_{\star}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces((\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}))_{\star}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}

By
[
Lur17
, 3.2.2.6]
, if
A
is a quasi-proper
\mathcal{O}
-algebra in
\mathcal{C}
, then unit map of the adjunction
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}A^{\vee\vee}
is an equivalence of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras in
\mathcal{C}
. Similarly if
C
is a quasi-proper
\mathcal{O}
-coalgebra in
\mathcal{C}
, then the map
C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}C^{\vee\vee}
is an equivalence of
\mathcal{O}
-coalgebras in
\mathcal{C}
.
Therefore the unit and counit of the adjunction are equivalences. Thus the adjunction is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories.
∎

Remark 3.32
.

In
[
Pér22a
, 3.9]
, it was shown that for
\mathcal{C}
a presentably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category, the linear dual functor
(-)^{\vee}:\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\rightarrow\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})
is a right adjoint, for any essentially small
\infty
-operad
\mathcal{O}
. Its left adjoint:

(-)^{\circ}:\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}},

is called the
finite dual
. Our arguments above show that
A^{\circ}\simeq A^{\vee}
, as
\mathcal{O}
-coalgebras in
\mathcal{C}
, for any weak quasi-dualizable
\mathcal{O}
-algebra
A
in
\mathcal{C}
. This follows by unicity of left adjoint functors.

4.
Duality between THH and coTHH

We show here one of our main results: there is a duality between topological Hochschild homology and topological coHochschild homology. Recall that we have introduced the notion of weak quasi-proper algebra in Definition
3.24
and quasi-proper coalgebra in Definition
3.27
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a closed symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category that admits geometric realizations and totalizations.

(i)

Let
A
be a weak quasi-proper
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebra in
\mathcal{C}
. Then:

\Big(\mathsf{THH}^{\mathcal{C}}(A)\Big)^{\vee}\simeq\mathsf{coTHH}^{\mathcal{C}}(A^{\vee}).

(ii)

Let

be a quasi-proper
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra in
\mathcal{C}
. Then:

\mathsf{coTHH}^{\mathcal{C}}(C)\simeq\Big(\mathsf{THH}^{\mathcal{C}}(C^{\vee})\Big)^{\vee}.

We shall prove the above theorem at the end of the section. We first make some observations.

Remark 4.2
.

Even for
C
a proper
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra, we have examples where:

\Big(\mathsf{coTHH}^{\mathcal{C}}(C)\Big)^{\vee}\not\simeq\mathsf{THH}^{\mathcal{C}}(C^{\vee}),

as shown in Remark
7.6
.

Remark 4.3
.

Let
A
be a weak quasi-proper
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebra in
\mathcal{C}
. Combining
(i)
of Theorem
4.1
above with the unit of the adjunction of Proposition
3.4
, we obtain a natural map in
A
, dashed in the diagram below:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{THH}^{\mathcal{C}}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Big(\mathsf{THH}^{\mathcal{C}}(A)\Big)^{\vee\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Big(\mathsf{coTHH}^{\mathcal{C}}(A^{\vee})\Big)^{\vee}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

Similarly, let
C
be a quasi-proper
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra in
\mathcal{C}
. Combining
(ii)
of Theorem
4.1
above with the unit of the adjunction of Proposition

, we obtain a natural map in

, dashed in the diagram below:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{THH}^{\mathcal{C}}(C^{\vee})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Big(\mathsf{THH}^{\mathcal{C}}(C^{\vee})\Big)^{\vee\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Big(\mathsf{coTHH}^{\mathcal{C}}(C)\Big)^{\vee}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

As mentioned in the previous remark, these maps are

Remark 4.4
.

Let

be a quasi-proper
\mathbb{S}
-coalgebra.
Combining the natural map in the above remark with the trace map in

-theory, we obtain:

\textup{K}(C^{\vee})\longrightarrow\mathsf{THH}(C^{\vee})\longrightarrow\Big(\mathsf{coTHH}(C)\Big)^{\vee}.

We therefore obtain a map by applying the Spanier-Whitehead dual:

\mathsf{coTHH}(C)\longrightarrow\textup{K}(C^{\vee})^{\vee},

as the composite

\mathsf{coTHH}(C)\longrightarrow\Big(\mathsf{THH}(C^{\vee})\Big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow\textup{K}(C^{\vee})^{\vee}
. By adjointness, this provides a map

\textup{K}(C^{\vee})\wedge\mathsf{coTHH}(C)\longrightarrow\mathbb{S}.

We now prove Theorem
4.1
. It essentially follows from the linear dual assigning the cyclic bar construction of a weak quasi-proper algebra

:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\otimes A\otimes A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\otimes A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A.}

to the cyclic cobar construction of the linear dual coalgebra

:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A\otimes A\otimes A)^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A\otimes A)^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\vee}\otimes A^{\vee}\otimes A^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\vee}\otimes A^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\vee}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

We make precise the above idea in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a closed symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category.
Let
A
be a weak quasi-proper
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebra in
\mathcal{C}
.
Then we obtain an equivalence of cosimplicial objects in
\mathcal{C}
:

\left(\mathsf{Bar}_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{C}}(A)\right)^{\vee}\simeq\mathsf{coBar}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(A^{\vee}\right).

Proof.

For
A
a weak quasi-proper
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebra in
\mathcal{C}
, we consider the subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{A}
of
\mathcal{C}
just as in the proof of Proposition
3.25
. Notice that
A
is also an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebra in
\mathcal{C}_{A}
. Since the composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}

is strong symmetric monoidal, by Lemma

, we obtain an equivalence:

\left(\mathsf{Bar}_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{C}_{A}}(A)\right)^{\vee}\simeq\mathsf{Bar}_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}}(A^{\vee}).

By applying again Lemma

on the strong symmetric monoidal functor

, we have:

\left(\mathsf{Bar}_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{C}_{A}}(A)\right)^{\vee}\simeq\left(\mathsf{Bar}_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{C}}(A)\right)^{\vee}.

By definition, we also obtain:

\mathsf{Bar}_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}}(A^{\vee})\simeq\mathsf{coBar}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{C}}(A^{\vee}).

Combining the above equivalences, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We first prove
(i)
. By Lemma
4.5
, we have:

\left(\mathsf{Bar}_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{C}}(A)\right)^{\vee}\simeq\mathsf{coBar}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{C}}(A^{\vee}).

The functor
(-)^{\vee}:\mathcal{C}{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
, as a right adjoint (see Proposition
3.4
), preserves limits (or sends colimits to limits when regarded as a contravariant functor). Therefore, if we take the totalization of the cosimplicial objects on each side of the equivalence above we obtain the desired equivalence:

\Big(\mathsf{THH}^{\mathcal{C}}(A)\Big)^{\vee}\simeq\mathsf{coTHH}^{\mathcal{C}}(A^{\vee}).

We now prove
(ii)
. Let
C
be a quasi-proper
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebra in
\mathcal{C}
. Then
C^{\vee}
is a quasi-proper
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebra and in particular weak quasi-proper, by Theorem
3.31
. Therefore we can apply
(i)
to
A=C^{\vee}
, and we obtain:

\Big(\mathsf{THH}^{\mathcal{C}}(C^{\vee})\Big)^{\vee}\simeq\mathsf{coTHH}^{\mathcal{C}}(C^{\vee\vee}).

By Theorem
3.31
, we have
C^{\vee\vee}\simeq C
as
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebras in
\mathcal{C}
. Thus we obtain:

\mathsf{coTHH}^{\mathcal{C}}(C^{\vee\vee})\simeq\mathsf{coTHH}^{\mathcal{C}}(C).

Combining the two equivalences, we have obtained the desired result.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

In Remark 2.6 of

]
, Hess and Shipley claim a duality relation between coHochschild homology and Hochschild homology in

for a general coalgebra

. This is due to our Examples

and

showing that the linear dual functor is not strong monoidal without these assumptions.

The rest of this subsection is devoted to the proof of Lemmas
5.6
and
5.7
below. For this, we begin with an identification of dualizable objects in
HA
-modules, see Proposition
5.3
. From Proposition
5.3
, we deduce that for a connective
C
as in Theorem

, every Postnikov section of

is dualizable and therefore satisfies the relevant conditions. In the proofs of Lemmas

and
5.7
, we consider the Postnikov section maps

for various

to prove that
C
also satisfies Conditions
1
and
2
and
C^{\wedge_{HA}n}
satisfies Condition

as desired. For coconnective

, we use the connective cover functors instead of the Postnikov section functors and argue similarly.

Recall from Example
3.8

that dualizable objects in
HA
-modules are precisely what are called the perfect

-modules, see
[
EKMM97
, III.7.9]
and
[
Lur18
, 3.2.3]
. An
HA
-module is said to be perfect if it lies in the smallest subcategory of the homotopy category of
HA
-modules that contains
HA
and is closed under triangles and retracts. For instance, finite coproducts of shifted copies of
HA
are examples of perfect
HA
-modules.

Since
A
is Noetherian of finite projective dimension, every finitely generated
A
-module
M
admits a finite length resolution of finitely generated projective
A
-modules. This shows that for every finitely generated
A
-module
M
,
HM
is perfect and therefore dualizable in
HA
-modules. Furthermore, we have the following proposition that characterize dualizable objects in
HA
-modules.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
A
be a discrete Noetherian ring of finite global dimension.
An
HA
-module
N
is dualizable if and only if
\pi_{*}N
is finitely generated as a graded
A
-module, i.e.
\pi_{i}N
is a finitely generated
A
-module for every
i
and
\pi_{i}N\neq 0
for only finitely many
i
.

Proof.

Let
N
be a dualizable
HA
-module. This implies that
N
can be obtained from
HA
via taking finitely many triangles and retracts. The conclusion is immediate for the case
N=HA
as
\pi_{*}HA=A
is a finitely generated
A
-module. Therefore, it is sufficient to show that the property of having finitely generated homotopy is closed under retracts and triangles.
The closeness under retracts follows form the fact that the codomain of a surjective map is finitely generated when the domain is finitely generated. For triangles, assume that there is a triangle

M\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow L,

in
HA
-modules where
M
and
L
have finitely generated homotopy groups. We need to show that
N
also has finitely generated homotopy groups. From the induced long exact sequence, we get that
\pi_{i}N\neq 0
for only finitely many
i
. Therefore it is sufficient to show that
\pi_{i}N
is finitely generated for each
i
. The induced long exact sequence in homotopy can be broken into short exact sequences of the form:

0\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow\pi_{i}N\longrightarrow Z\longrightarrow 0,

where
Z
is a submodule of the finitely generated
A
-module
\pi_{i}L
. Since
A
is Noetherian,
Z
is also finitely generated. The
A
-module
Y
is the image of the finitely generated
A
-module
\pi_{i}M
under an
A
-module homomorphism and therefore
Y
is also finitely generated. This proves that
\pi_{i}N
is finitely generated. We conclude that
\pi_{*}N
is a finitely generated graded
A
-module as desired.

Now we prove the other direction. Since dualizable
HA
-modules are closed under triangles, we get that an
HA
-module is dualizable if and only if one of its (de)suspensions is dualizable. Therefore, we can assume without loss of generality that
N
is connective. For a given
HA
-module
N
with finitely generated homotopy, let
h(N)
be the largest
i
for which
\pi_{i}N\neq 0
. We argue inductively on
h(N)
.

For the base case of our induction, we have
N=HM
, for some finitely generated
A
-module
M
. We already argued that
N
is a dualizable object in this situation.

Assume that every connective
A
-module
M
with
h\geq h(M)
and finitely generated homotopy is dualizable. Let
N
be an
HA
-module with finitely generated
\pi_{*}N
and
h(N)=h+1
. We need to show that
N
is dualizable.
Since
\pi_{0}N
is finitely generated, we have a map:

\bigvee_{j=1}^{m}HA\longrightarrow N,

that induces a surjective map on degree
0
homotopy where the domain denotes a finite coproduct.
Let
N^{\prime}
be defined by the cofiber sequence:

\bigvee_{j=1}^{m}HA\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow N^{\prime}.

Using the long exact sequence induced by this cofiber sequence, one observes that
\pi_{0}N^{\prime}=0
and:

\pi_{i}N^{\prime}=\pi_{i}N=0,

for every
i>h(N)=h+1
and
i<0
. Since
A
is Noetherian, every submodule of a finitely generated
A
-module is also finitely generated. This fact, together with the aforementioned long exact sequence implies that
\pi_{i}N^{\prime}
is finitely generated for every
i
. It follows by our induction hypothesis that
\Sigma^{-1}N^{\prime}
is dualizable because
h(\Sigma^{-1}N^{\prime})\leq h
. Therefore,
N^{\prime}
is also dualizable and it is generated by
HA
under triangles and retracts. The triangle above proves that
N
is also generated by
HA
under triangles and retracts. In other words,
N
is also dualizable.
∎

For the rest of this section, let
d
denote the global dimension of
A
. We say a spectrum

is

-connective
if
\pi_{i}E=0
for every

. Similarly, we say

is

if
\pi_{i}E=0
for every

. Before we start the proof of Lemmas

and

, we need to prove the following technical lemmas.

Lemma 5.4
.

If

is an

is an
-n

For this, we use the Ext spectral sequence of

to compute the mapping space defining

. The

-page of this spectral sequence is given by:

and it abuts to:

where

whenever

Since

for every

. Therefore the possible non-trivial entries contributing to the homotopy group in degree

. But in this situation, we have

. In other words,

.

Let

In this case, we use the Künneth spectral sequence in

to compute

In the first situation, there is a flat resolution of

6.
Coalgebras in Spectra

We have introduced the definition of topological coHochschild homology associated to an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra in any symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category. We are interested here in
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebras in the
\infty
-category of spectra
\mathsf{Sp}
or more generally in
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra in
R
-modules, where
R
is a commutative ring spectrum.

In symmetric monoidal model categories of spectra, such as symmetric spectra, an
\mathbb{S}
-coalgebra structure is quite a restrictive feature on a spectrum. However, unlike for algebras, the category of strictly coassociative and counital
\mathbb{S}
-coalgebras do not represent the
\mathbb{A}_{\infty}
-coalgebras in
\mathsf{Sp}
. See
[
PS19
]
and
[
Pér22b
]
. The main point is that the model categories only capture coalgebras that behave as the cocommutative
\mathbb{S}
-coalgebra
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X
with comultiplication induced by the diagonal:

X_{+}\longrightarrow(X\times X)_{+}\cong X_{+}\wedge X_{+},

where
X
is a space and
X_{+}
is the free pointed space on
X
. In particular, strictly coassociative and counital
\mathbb{S}
-coalgebras do not capture the Spanier-Whitehead duality between
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebras and
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebras in finite spectra of Corollary
3.13
.

Nonetheless, we show in this section that an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra structure on a spectrum
C
is also a restrictive feature. If
R
is a commutative ring spectrum, then an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebra in
R
-modules in
\mathsf{Sp}
is also an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebra in
\mathsf{Sp}
. In other words, we have that spectra in
\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}(\mathsf{Sp})
require less structure than spectra in
\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}(\mathsf{Mod}_{R}(\mathsf{Sp}))
.
This is not true for coalgebras. In fact, we can say that spectra in
\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}(\mathsf{Sp})
have a more restrictive structure than spectra in
\mathsf{CoAlg}_{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}(\mathsf{Mod}_{R}(\mathsf{Sp}))
. For instance, if
C
is an
R
-module endowed with an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra structure in
\mathsf{Sp}
, then
C
is an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra in
R
-modules in
\mathsf{Sp}
.

One of the main restrictive feature for an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-algebra
C
in
\mathsf{Sp}
is the requirement of a map
C\rightarrow\mathbb{S}
that is counital (up to higher homotopy).

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
C
be a connective
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra in the
\infty
-category of spectra. If
\pi_{0}C\neq 0
then the
p
-localization of
C
contains the
p
-local sphere spectrum
\mathbb{S}_{(p)}
as a retract in the stable homotopy category for every
p\gg 0
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra in
\mathsf{Sp}
, it is in particular a coassociative counital coalgebra up to homotopy: i.e. it is a strictly coassociative counital coalgebra in the homotopy category of
\mathsf{Sp}
.
Let
\Delta
denote the comultiplication map and
\epsilon
denote the counit map of
C
in the homotopy category of
\mathsf{Sp}
respectively. The following composite is homotopic to the identity map on
C
:

(6.1)

C\xrightarrow{\Delta}C\wedge C\xrightarrow{\epsilon\wedge id_{C}}\mathbb{S}\wedge C\simeq C,

where the smash product denotes the derived smash product. Since
C
is connective, by the Künneth spectral sequence
[
EKMM97
, IV.4.1]
we have:

\pi_{0}(C\wedge C)\cong\pi_{0}C\otimes\pi_{0}C\textup{\ and\ }\pi_{0}(\mathbb{S}\wedge C)\cong\pi_{0}\mathbb{S}\otimes\pi_{0}C.

We apply
\pi_{0}(-)
to (
6.1
) and due to the functoriality of the Künneth spectral sequence, we obtain the following composite:

\pi_{0}C\to\pi_{0}C\otimes\pi_{0}C\xrightarrow{\pi_{0}\epsilon\otimes\pi_{0}id_{C}}\mathbb{Z}\otimes\pi_{0}C\cong\pi_{0}C.

If
\pi_{0}\epsilon
is the trivial map, it follows that the composite above is trivial but this contradicts the fact that this composite is the identity map and that
\pi_{0}C\neq 0
. Therefore
\pi_{0}\epsilon
is non-trivial. Let
m
be the smallest positive integer in the image of
\pi_{0}\epsilon
and let

c\colon\thinspace\mathbb{S}\to C,

denote a map whose image under
\pi_{0}\epsilon
is
m
. If
m=1
, then this makes sure that
C
contains the sphere spectrum
\mathbb{S}
as a retract in the stable homotopy category through the composite:

\mathbb{S}\xrightarrow{c}C\xrightarrow{\epsilon}\mathbb{S}.

For
m>1
, let
p
be a prime greater than
m
. In this situation,
m
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
and therefore a unit in
\pi_{0}(\mathbb{S}_{(p)})
. Therefore, there is a map
m^{-1}\colon\thinspace\mathbb{S}_{(p)}\to\mathbb{S}_{(p)}
representing
m^{-1}
in
\pi_{0}(\mathbb{S}_{(p)})
. We obtain the following composite:

\mathbb{S}_{(p)}\xrightarrow{c}C_{(p)}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}\mathbb{S}_{(p)}\xrightarrow{m^{-1}}\mathbb{S}_{(p)},

which is the identity map of
\mathbb{S}_{(p)}
(in the homotopy category) as desired.
In particular, this implies that
\pi_{*}C_{(p)}
contains the
p
-local stable homotopy groups of spheres for every

.
∎

This recovers the cohomological version described in
[
CHV06
, Comments 3, Section I.1.5]
.

Example 6.6
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group. Then
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}G
is a ring spectrum. Its Spanier-Whitehead is the Thom spectrum
G^{-\tau}
of its stable normal bundle, just as in Example
6.5
. By Corollary
3.13
, we obtain that
G^{-\tau}
is an
\mathbb{S}
-coalgebra, induced by the group structure on

. By Theorem
4.1
, we obtain:

\mathsf{coTHH}(G^{-\tau})\simeq\left(\mathsf{THH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}G)\right)^{\vee}.

We also have that
\mathsf{THH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}G)\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathscr{L}BG
, and thus we obtain:

\mathsf{coTHH}(G^{-\tau})\simeq\left(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathscr{L}BG\right)^{\vee}.

If
G
is connected, then
BG
is simply connected and thus by our discussion in Example
6.5
, we obtain:

\mathsf{coTHH}(G^{-\tau})\simeq\left(\mathsf{coTHH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}BG)\right)^{\vee}.

In
[
BGH+18
, 5.7]
, the mod
p
-homology of
\mathsf{coTHH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}BG)
was computed for

equals one of the Lie groups
U(n)
,
SU(n)
,
Sp(n)
,
SO(2k)
,
G_{2}
,
F_{4}
,

,
E_{7}
and
E_{8}
.

Remark 6.7
.

Let
A\rightarrow B
be a map of commutative
{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}
-algebras in a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
. One might expect
B\otimes_{A}B
to be an
{\mathbb{A}_{\infty}}
-coalgebra in
B
-modules in
\mathcal{C}
due to the comultiplicaton map

B\otimes_{A}B\cong B\otimes_{A}A\otimes_{A}B\to B\otimes_{A}B\otimes_{A}B\cong\\
B\otimes_{A}(B\otimes_{B}B)\otimes_{A}B\cong(B\otimes_{A}B)\otimes_{B}(B\otimes_{A}B),

and the counit map

B\otimes_{A}B\to B

given by the multiplication on
B
. However, this does not work. This is due to the fact that the comultiplication map above is not a map of
B
-modules in general but of
B
-bimodules. The
B
-module structure on
B\otimes_{A}B
is given by the map

B\cong B\otimes_{A}A\to B\otimes_{A}B,

but the
B
-module structure of the right hand side is given by the one induced by the tensor product in
B
-modules. We believe that there is no general way of turning this comultiplication map into a map of
B
-modules.

7.
Computations

In this section, we apply Theorem
5.8
to construct interesting examples of coalgebras in module spectra and then we use Theorem
1.3
to compute the topological coHochschild homology groups of these coalgebras. Furthermore, all our topological coHochschild homology computations are algebraic. In other words, in each case, we compute
\mathsf{coTHH}^{HR}(C)
for some discrete ring
R
and
HR
-coalgebra
C
.

Each subsection below is devoted to the construction of a particular coalgebra and its coHochschild homology computation. In Section
7.1
, we show that
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}
is an
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra in a unique way and compute its coHochschild homology groups. Section
7.2
is devoted to the study of the Steenrod algebra spectrum as an
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra and in Section
7.3
, we construct an
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra structure on
\Omega H\mathbb{F}_{p}
; we compute the relevant coHochschild homology groups in each of these cases.

7.1.
CoHochschild Homology of the Dual Steenrod Algebra in
H\mathbb{Z}
-modules

Here, we study the dual Steenrod algebra spectrum
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}
in
H\mathbb{Z}
-modules and its coHochschild homology. We claim in Remark
6.7
that such objects, i.e. objects of the form
B\otimes_{A}B
for some map of
{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}
-ring spectra
A\to B
, do not carry a canonical
B
-coalgebra structure in general.
In contrast, we show below that
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}
carries a unique
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra structure. Subsequently, we compute the coHochschild homology of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}
with this
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra structure using Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 7.1
.

There is a unique
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra structure on
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Furthermore, this
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra structure lifts to a cocommutative
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra structure.

We postpone the proof of this theorem to the end of the subsection and start the computation of the coHochschild homology groups of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Note the theorem above allows us to consider
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}
as an
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra.

Theorem 7.2
.

The coHochschild homology groups of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}
are given by:

\mathsf{coTHH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}_{i}(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{F}_{p}&i=0\\
\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{F}_{p}&i=1\\
0&\textup{otherwise.}\\
\end{array}\right.

In other words, there is an equivalence of graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules:

(7.1)

\mathsf{coTHH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}_{*}(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(x_{1})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}[[t]]

where
\lvert t\rvert=0
and
\lvert x_{1}\rvert=1
.

By a Tor calculation, one obtains that:

\pi_{*}(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(y_{1})

where
\lvert y_{1}\rvert=1
, see
[
EKMM97
, Theorem 4.1 in IV.4]
. In particular,
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
1.3
. Indeed,
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a dualizable
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-module due to Example
3.9
. We obtain the following equivalence of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules:

\begin{split}\mathsf{coTHH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})\simeq\Big(\mathsf{THH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left((H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\vee}\right)\Big)^{\vee}.\end{split}

Therefore our question reduces to the calculation of the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-Hochschild homology groups of
(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\vee}
. Recall that dualization in
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules results in dualization of graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules at the level of homotopy groups which carries coproducts to products. Therefore Proposition
7.3
below together with the equivalence above provide Theorem
7.2
. Let
X
denote the
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\vee}
for the rest of this subsection.

Proposition 7.3
.

There is an isomorphism of graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules:

\mathsf{THH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}_{*}((H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\vee})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[y]\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}})

where
\lvert y\rvert=0
and
\lvert z_{-1}\rvert=-1
.

Proof.

By the Ext spectral sequence calculating the homotopy groups of mapping spectra
[
EKMM97
, IV.4.4.1]
, we have an equivalence of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules

\pi_{*}X\cong\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})

where
\lvert z_{-1}\rvert=-1
.
This is also an isomorphism of rings since there is a unique ring structure on the right hand side.

We show below in Proposition
7.4
that the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-DGA
X
is formal, i.e. it is quasi-isomorphic to an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-DGA with trivial differentials. Note that in the spectral sequence corresponding to a double complex with trivial vertical differentials, all the differentials are trivial on the
E^{2}
page and after. This shows that the differentials in the standard spectral sequence calculating the Hochschild homology groups of
X
are trivial after the first page.

For this spectral sequence, we have:

\begin{split}E^{2}_{s,t}=\mathsf{HH}^{\mathbb{F}_{p}}_{s,t}(\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}}),\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}}))=&\mathsf{Tor}^{\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}})^{op}}_{s,t}(\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}}),\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}}))\\
&\Longrightarrow\mathsf{THH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}_{s+t}(X).\end{split}

To calculate the
E^{2}
page of this spectral sequence, we use the automorphism of
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}})

given by:

z_{-1}\otimes 1\to z_{-1}\otimes 1\textup{\ and \ }1\otimes z_{-1}\to z_{-1}\otimes 1-1\otimes z_{-1}.

Precomposing with this automorphism, the action of the second factor of
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}})
on
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}})
becomes trivial, i.e. this action is the one obtained by the augmentation map of
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}})
. Using the Künneth formula
[
Bay21
, Equation (2)]
, we obtain the following:

\begin{split}E^{2}\cong&\mathsf{Tor}^{\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}})^{op}}(\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}}),\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}}))\\
\cong&\mathsf{Tor}^{\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}})}(\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}}),\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}}))\otimes\mathsf{Tor}^{\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}})}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})\\
\cong&\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})\otimes\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\sigma z_{-1}).\end{split}

Here:
\text{deg}(\sigma z_{-1})=(1,-1)
and
\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\sigma z_{-1})
denotes the divided power algebra on a single generator. There is an equivalence of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules
\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\sigma z_{-1})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[\sigma z_{-1}]
.

As mentioned before, all the differentials in this spectral sequence are trivial on the
E^{2}
page and after. Therefore, there is an isomorphism of graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules

\mathsf{THH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}_{*}(X)\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[y]\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}({z_{-1}})

where
\lvert y\rvert=0
.
∎

In order to finish the proof of Theorem
7.2
, we need to prove the following proposition. Recall that an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-DGA is said to be formal if it is quasi-isomorphic to an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-DGA with trivial differentials.

Proposition 7.4
.

Every
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-DGA
Z
with homology

H_{*}Z=\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})

is formal as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-DGA. In particular,
X=(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\vee}
is a formal
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-DGA.

Remark 7.5
.

In
[
Bay21
, proof of Theorem 1.6]
the first author shows that every
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-DGA with homology
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
is formal as a DGA. However, we need a slightly stronger result. We need to show that such
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-DGAs are formal as
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-DGAs.

We work on this problem in
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras and use the obstruction theory of Hopkins and Miller
[
Rez98
]
. This obstruction theory provides obstructions to lifting a map of monoids in the homotopy category of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules to a map of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras
[
JN14
, 4.5]
.

Proof of Proposition
7.4
.

Let
Z
also denote an
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra corresponding to the
Z
in Proposition
7.4
and let
T
denote the
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra corresponding to the formal DGA with homology
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
.

Since the homotopy category of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules is the category of graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules as a monoidal category via the functor
\pi_{*}-
, there is an isomorphism of monoids from
Z
to
T
in the homotopy category of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules. By Theorem 4.5 of
[
JN14
]
, the obstructions to lifting this map to a map of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras lie in the following André–Quillen cohomology groups for graded associative
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras
[
JN14
, Section 5.2.1]
:

\mathsf{Der}^{s+1}(\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1}),\Omega^{s}\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1}))\textup{\ for \ }s\geq 1.

Here,
\Omega^{n}
shifts down a graded module
n
times. By the identification of André–Quillen cohomology groups with Hochschild cohomology groups, one obtains that the obstructions lie in the following groups
[
JN14
, Section 5.2.1]
:

(7.2)

\mathsf{Ext}_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})^{op}}^{s+2}(\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1}),\Omega^{s}\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1}))\textup{\ for \ }s\geq 1.

By the discussion above, it is sufficient to show that the Ext groups in (
7.2
) are trivial.
Note that all the rings in sight are graded commutative and therefore we actually have
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1}){{}^{\mathsf{op}}}=\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
. There is an automorphism of

\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1}),

given by:

z_{-1}\otimes 1\to z_{-1}\otimes 1\textup{\ and \ }1\otimes z_{-1}\to z_{-1}\otimes 1-1\otimes z_{-1}.

We consider the obstruction groups in (
7.2
) after precomposing with this automorphism. This ensures that the action of the first factor in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
on
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
and
\Omega^{s}\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
are the canonical non-trivial actions and the action of the second factor on
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
and
\Omega^{s}\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
are the trivial actions given by the augmentation map of
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
.

Let

\varphi\colon\thinspace\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})

denote the map of rings given by the inclusion of the second factor. By the derived extension of scalars functor induced by
\varphi
, we obtain the first isomorphism below. The second isomorphism is obtained using the fact that
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
is a flat
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
-module through the action induced by
\varphi
:

\begin{split}&\mathsf{Ext}_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})}^{s+2}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\Omega^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{p}\oplus\Omega\mathbb{F}_{p}))\\
&\cong\mathsf{Ext}_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})}^{s+2}((\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1}))\otimes_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathbb{F}_{p},\Omega^{s}\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1}))\\
&\cong\mathsf{Ext}_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})}^{s+2}(\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1}),\Omega^{s}\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})).\\
\end{split}

Therefore it is sufficient to show that:

\mathsf{Ext}_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})}^{s+2}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\Omega^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{p}\oplus\Omega\mathbb{F}_{p}))=0\textup{\ for }s\geq 1.

We have the following free resolution of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
-modules:

\cdots\to\Omega^{2}\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})\to\Omega\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})\to\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})\to\mathbb{F}_{p}.

Applying
\mathsf{Hom}_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})}(-,\Omega^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{p}\oplus\Omega\mathbb{F}_{p}))
to this resolution gives a resolution which is given by the following in resolution degree
s+2
:

\mathsf{Hom}_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})}(\Omega^{s+2}\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1}),\Omega^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{p}\oplus\Omega\mathbb{F}_{p}))=0.

Therefore the groups in (
7.2
) containing the obstructions to the formality of
Z
are trivial.
∎

Remark 7.6
.

Our computation above shows that:

\Big(\mathsf{coTHH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C)\Big)^{\vee}\not\simeq\mathsf{THH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C^{\vee}).

Indeed we have that
\mathsf{coTHH}_{0}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})
is an uncountably infinite dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space, see (
7.1
). We also know that
\mathsf{THH}_{0}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}((H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\vee})
is a countably infinite dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space, see Proposition

7.3
. Note that dualization in
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules results in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dualization at the level of homotopy groups
[
EKMM97
, IV.4.1]
. In particular, the dual of
\mathsf{coTHH}_{0}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})
is again an uncountable dimensional vector space and therefore it is not isomorphic to
\mathsf{THH}_{0}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}((H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\vee})
.

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

To prove this theorem, we apply Theorem
5.8
for the case
A=\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
\mathcal{O}=\mathsf{Assoc}
.
Recall that
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules are uniquely determined by their homotopy groups; this follows by the Ext spectral sequence calculating mapping spectra
[
EKMM97
, IV.4.4.1]
. Therefore, our goal is to show that there is a unique
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra with homotopy groups given by:

\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(y_{1})\cong\pi_{*}(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}).

It follows by the Ext spectral sequence that the dualization functor in
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules results in graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-module dualization at the level of homotopy groups. In particular, we have:

\pi_{*}((H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\vee})\cong\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})

where
\lvert z_{-1}\rvert=-1
. Similarly, the dual of an
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra with homotopy
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
has homotopy groups given by
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(y_{1})
. Using this together with Theorem
5.8
, we obtain that the set of equivalence classes of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebras with homotopy groups
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(y_{1})
is in bijective correspondence with the set of equivalence classes of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras with homotopy groups
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
. Furthermore, observe that the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-module
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
has a unique ring structure on it.

Therefore to prove the first part of Theorem
7.1
, it is sufficient to show that there is a unique
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra with homotopy ring
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
. This is provided by Proposition
7.4
.
Indeed, this shows that
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}
is the dual of the formal
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-DGA with homology
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(z_{-1})
. Since this formal
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-DGA is commutative, we deduce that
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}
is the dual of a commutative
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra. Together with Theorem
5.8
, this shows that
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a cocommutative
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra.
∎

7.2.
CoHochschild Homology of the Steenrod Algebra Spectrum

In this section, we apply Theorem
1.3
to compute the coHochschild homology of the Steenrod algebra spectrum. Recall that

\pi_{*}(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge H\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\mathcal{A}_{*}

is the dual Steenrod algebra. Furthermore, we have:

{[H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}\simeq{[H\mathbb{F}_{p},{[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}]}_{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}\simeq{[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}},

where
[-,-]_{R}
denotes the internal hom in
R
-modules for a given commutative ring spectrum
R
. Recall that
{[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}
is the spectrum of cohomology operations on the cohomology theory defined by
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Indeed:

\mathcal{A}=\pi_{*}\left({[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}\right)

is the Steenrod algebra.
There is an
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra structure on
{[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}
given by Theorem
5.8
and the fact that it is the dual of the
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge H\mathbb{F}_{p}
. This induces the usual comultiplication on the Steenrod algebra
[
Sch12
, Section II.10.2]
.
We use Theorem
1.3
to compute the coHochschild homology of
{[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}
with this
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra structure. Let
(-)^{\vee}
denote the linear dual functor in
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules. There are equivalences of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras

\left({[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}\right)^{\vee}\simeq(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge H\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\vee\vee}\simeq H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge H\mathbb{F}_{p}

where the first equivalence follows by our definition of the
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coalgebra structure on
{[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}
and the second equality follows by Theorem
5.8
.
Using Theorem
1.3
, we obtain:

\mathsf{coTHH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}_{*}\left({[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}\right)\cong\left(\mathsf{THH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}_{*}(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge H\mathbb{F}_{p})\right)^{\vee},

where
(-)^{\vee}
here denotes the linear dual functor in graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules.
To compute the right hand side of this equality, we use the following equalities

\mathsf{THH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge H\mathbb{F}_{p})\simeq H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge\mathsf{THH}(H\mathbb{F}_{p})\simeq(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge H\mathbb{F}_{p})\wedge_{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathsf{THH}(H\mathbb{F}_{p})

and the Bökstedt periodicity
[
Bök87
]

\mathsf{THH}_{*}(H\mathbb{F}_{p})=\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{2}].

This shows that:

\mathsf{coTHH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}_{*}\left({[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}\right)\cong(\mathcal{A}_{*}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{2}])^{\vee},

where
(-)^{\vee}
on the right hand side denotes the dualization functor in graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules. Note that
(M^{\vee})_{i}=(M_{-i})^{\vee}
for every graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-module
M
. The right hand side of the isomorphism above is a finite dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-module at each degree and
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dualization is symmetric monoidal on finite dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector spaces, see Remark
3.23
. We therefore have the following isomorphisms of graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules:

(\mathcal{A}_{*}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{2}])^{\vee}\cong(\mathcal{A}_{*})^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{2}]^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{-2}],

where
\lvert x_{-2}\rvert=-2
. We obtain the following result.

Theorem 7.7
.

There is an equivalence of graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules:

\mathsf{coTHH}^{H\mathbb{F}_{p}}_{*}\left({[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{F}_{p}]}_{\mathbb{S}}\right)\cong\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{-2}],

where
\mathcal{A}
denotes the Steenrod algebra and
\lvert x_{-2}\rvert=-2
.

7.3.
An Interesting Coalgebra in
H\mathbb{Z}
-modules

Recall that dualizable
H\mathbb{Z}
-modules are precisely those that can be obtained from
H\mathbb{Z}
via finitely many cofiber sequences and retracts
[
Lur18
, 3.2.3]
. Therefore, the
H\mathbb{Z}
-algebra
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
is dualizable as an
H\mathbb{Z}
-module. This is due to the cofiber sequence

H\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\cdot p}H\mathbb{Z}\to H\mathbb{F}_{p}.

Thus
H\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\vee}
, the dual of
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
in
H\mathbb{Z}
-modules, is an
H\mathbb{Z}
-coalgebra, see Corollary
3.13
. Applying the dualization functor to the sequence above, one obtains a fiber sequence

\Sigma(H\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\vee})\leftarrow H\mathbb{Z}\xleftarrow{\cdot p}H\mathbb{Z}\xleftarrow{}H\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\vee},

which shows that
\Sigma(H\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\vee})=H\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Therefore we have an equivalence

H\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\vee}\simeq\Omega H\mathbb{F}_{p},

of
H\mathbb{Z}
-modules. We consider
\Omega H\mathbb{F}_{p}
as an
H\mathbb{Z}
-coalgebra through this equivalence.

Theorem 7.8
.

With the
H\mathbb{Z}
-coalgebra structure on
\Omega H\mathbb{F}_{p}
described above, the coHochschild homology of
\Omega H\mathbb{F}_{p}
is given by:

\mathsf{coTHH}_{*}^{H\mathbb{Z}}(\Omega H\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\Omega\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{-2}],

as a graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-module where
\lvert x_{-2}\rvert=-2
and
\Omega
on the right hand side denotes the functor that decreases the grading by 1.

Proof.

We compute the coHochschild homology of
\Omega H\mathbb{F}_{p}
using Theorem
1.3
. Since
H\mathbb{F}_{p}
is dualizable in
H\mathbb{Z}
-modules, we have the following equivalences of
H\mathbb{Z}
-algebras:

(\Omega H\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\vee}\simeq H\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\vee\vee}\simeq H\mathbb{F}_{p},

where the first equivalence follows by our definition of
\Omega H\mathbb{F}_{p}
as an
H\mathbb{Z}
-coalgebra and the second equivalence follows by Corollary
3.13
. Using Theorem
1.3
, we obtain:

\mathsf{coTHH}_{*}^{H\mathbb{Z}}(\Omega H\mathbb{F}_{p})=\pi_{*}((\mathsf{THH}^{H\mathbb{Z}}(H\mathbb{F}_{p}))^{\vee}).

There is an equivalence of graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules

\pi_{*}(\mathsf{THH}^{H\mathbb{Z}}(H\mathbb{F}_{p}))\cong\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(x_{2})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{2}].

Here:
\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(x_{2})
denotes the divided power algebra over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
on a single generator where
\lvert x_{2}\rvert=2
. Hochschild homology groups above are obtained via the Künneth spectral sequence calculating

\pi_{*}(H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{F}_{p}\wedge_{H\mathbb{Z}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}}H\mathbb{F}_{p}),

whose
E^{2}
page, as a bigraded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-module, is given by:

\mathsf{Tor}_{*,*}^{\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(y_{1})}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(x_{1,1})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{1,1}].

Since equivalence type of Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectra are uniquely determined by their homotopy groups, we have an equivalence of
H\mathbb{Z}
-modules:

\mathsf{THH}^{H\mathbb{Z}}(H\mathbb{F}_{p})\simeq\bigvee_{i=0}^{\infty}\Sigma^{2i}H\mathbb{F}_{p}.

Therefore:

\begin{split}\mathsf{THH}^{H\mathbb{Z}}(H\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\vee}\simeq&[\bigvee_{i=0}^{\infty}\Sigma^{2i}H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{Z}]\simeq\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\Omega^{2i}[H\mathbb{F}_{p},H\mathbb{Z}]\\
\simeq&\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\Omega^{2i}H\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\vee}\simeq\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\Omega^{2i}\Omega H\mathbb{F}_{p}.\end{split}

We obtain:

\mathsf{coTHH}_{*}^{H\mathbb{Z}}(\Omega H\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\Omega\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{-2}],

where
\lvert x_{-2}\rvert=-2
and
\Omega
denotes the functor that decreases the grading by 1.
∎

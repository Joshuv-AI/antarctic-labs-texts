Large-scale multiple testing of cross-covariance functions with applications to functional network models
Qin Fang, Qing Jiang, Xinghao Qiao

Abstract
The estimation of functional networks through functional covariance and graphical models have recently attracted increasing attention in settings with high dimensional functional data, where the number of functional variables p p is comparable to, and maybe larger than, the number of subjects. However, the existing methods all depend on regularization techniques, which make it unclear how the involved tuning parameters are related to the number of false edges. In this paper, we first reframe the functional covariance model estimation as a tuning-free problem of simultaneously testing p ⁡ ( p − 1 ) / 2 p(p-1)/2 hypotheses for cross-covariance functions, and introduce a novel multiple testing procedure. We then explore the multiple testing procedure under a general error-contamination framework and establish that our procedure can control false discoveries asymptotically. Additionally, we demonstrate that our proposed methods for two concrete examples: the functional covariance model for discretely observed functional data and, importantly, the more challenging functional graphical model, can be seamlessly integrated into the general error-contamination framework, and, with verifiable conditions, achieve theoretical guarantees on effective false discovery control. Finally, we showcase the superiority of our proposals through extensive simulations and brain connectivity analysis of two neuroimaging datasets.



Keywords:
Discretely observed functional data; False discovery control; Functional covariance model; Functional graphical model; High-dimensional functional data; Power.

1
Introduction

Recent advances in information technology have led to the growing prevalence of multivariate or even high-dimensional functional datasets across various applications. Examples include time-course gene expression data in genomics, and various types of brain imaging data in neuroscience, such as electrocorticography, magnetoencephalography and functional magnetic resonance imaging scans, among others. The brain signals are typically recorded in the form of a location-by-time matrix for each individual subject, where the rows and columns correspond to a set of brain regions and a number of observational time points spanning over minutes, respectively. To capture the non-stationary and dynamic patterns, recent proposals involve modeling brain signals as multivariate random functions, treating the time course data of each region as a random function
(
Zapata et al., 2022
;
Lee et al., 2023
;
Xue et al., 2023
, e.g.,)
. Such high-dimensional functional data can be represented as

{\bf X}_{i}(\cdot)=\{X_{i1}(\cdot),\dots,X_{ip}(\cdot)\}^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}
defined on a compact interval
{\cal U}
with marginal- and cross-covariance functions, which together form the covariance function matrix

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\cdot,\cdot)=\{\Sigma_{jk}(\cdot,\cdot)\}_{p\times p},~~~\Sigma_{jk}(u,v)=\text{Cov}\{X_{ij}(u),X_{ik}(v)\}~~\text{for}~~(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2}.

We observe
{\bf X}_{i}(\cdot)
for
i=1,\dots,n,
where the dimension
p
is large relative to, and maybe greater than the number of subjects
n.

Our motivation lies in the brain connectivity analysis based on brain imaging data. The literature primarily focuses on two types of multivariate functional data analysis approaches for estimating brain connectivity networks, consisting of
p
nodes.
The first method, proposed by
Fang et al. (2023)
, considers a functional covariance model depicting the marginal correlation information in
{\bf X}_{i}(\cdot).
This approach aims to identify the functional sparsity structure in
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\cdot,\cdot),
i.e., discovering edges
(j,k)
’s such that
\Sigma_{jk}(u,v)\neq 0
for some
(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2}.
By applying adaptive functional thresholding to the entries of the sample version of
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\cdot,\cdot),
they achieve estimation and support recovery consistencies.
The second method frames the network estimation as a functional graphical modelling problem
Zhu et al. (2016)
;
Qiao et al. (2019)
. This model characterizes the conditional dependence structure of
p
Gaussian random functions, i.e., nodes
j
and
k
are connected by an edge if and only if
X_{ij}(\cdot)
and
X_{ik}(\cdot)
are dependent, conditional on the remaining
p-2
functions. This line of work has witnessed numerous advancements, see, e.g.,
Li and Solea (2018)
;
Zapata et al. (2022)
;
Solea and Li (2022)
;
Lee et al. (2023)
and
Tsai et al. (2024)
.
Both types of models involve developing the regularized estimation of functional network structures that leverage the functional sparsity information.
However, the selection of practically reasonable regularization parameters poses a challenging task. While large regularization parameters result in sparse networks and may not be effective in discovering edges with small weights, small regularization parameters can produce excessive false edges, leading to high false discovery rates, as evidenced by simulation results in Section
5
.

In this paper, we reframe the functional covariance model estimation as a tuning-free problem of simultaneously testing
p(p-1)/2
hypotheses for cross-covariance functions

H_{0,jk}:\Sigma_{jk}(u,v)=0\text{ for any }(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2}~~\text{vs}~~H_{1,jk}:\Sigma_{jk}(u,v)\neq 0\text{ for some }(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2},

(1)

where
1\leq j<k\leq p
and we identify a significant edge between nodes
j
and
k
if and only if
H_{0,jk}
is rejected.
Compared to the multiple testing for the local covariance structures of high-dimensional vectors
Cai (2017)
, the infinite-dimensional nature of functional data introduces additional methodological and theoretical complexities that arise from constructing the test statistic under a functional norm and addressing various technical obstacles within an abstract Hilbert space. Furthermore, when handling the practical scenario for brain imaging data with each trajectory
X_{ij}(\cdot)
being observed over a densely sampled grid, nonparametric smoothing is frequently employed to obtain estimated curves
\widehat{X}_{ij}(\cdot),
which requires considering functional error-contaminated versions of
X_{ij}(\cdot)
satisfying:

\widehat{X}_{ij}(\cdot)=X_{ij}(\cdot)+e_{ij}(\cdot),~~i=1,\dots,n,~j=1,\dots,p.

(2)

Additionally, we present a novel procedure based on nodewise functional regressions that converts the functional graphical model estimation into the multiple testing task for the cross-covariance structures of functional regression errors.
As a result, we need to deal with estimated functional regression errors (i.e., functional residuals) instead of true ones, following a similar form as (
2
). In both scenarios, accounting for the functional errors, such as
e_{ij}(\cdot)
’s in (
2
), introduces an extra layer of complexity to our theoretical analysis.

Our paper aims to develop a general framework for large-scale multiple testing of cross-covariance functions from both methodological and theoretical perspectives. We begin by constructing a Hilbert–Schmidt-norm-based test statistic for each pair
j<k,
which is shown to have asymptotically the same limiting null distribution as an infinite mixture of chi-squares. We then employ normal quantile transformations for all test statistics, upon which a multiple testing procedure is proposed to account for the multiplicity and dependence among the transformed test statistics. Theoretically, we establish that our procedure can control false discoveries asymptotically under both fully observed and error-contaminated functional scenarios, and, furthermore, be applied to two concrete examples: the functional covariance model with discrete observations and the more challenging functional graphical model.
Specifically, we demonstrate the seamless integration of our proposed methods for both examples into the general error-contamination framework, and, with verifiable conditions, achieve theoretical guarantees on false discovery control. Empirically, we conduct simulations to showcase the uniform superiority of our proposals over competitors in terms of false discovery control and power for both fully and discretely observed functional data within both functional covariance and graphical models. We also apply our method to identify network structures using two brain imaging datasets, and observe scientifically interpretable patterns.

The main contributions of our paper are threefold.

•

First, we make the first attempt in the literature of functional data analysis and multiple testing to develop a general procedure with theoretical guarantees for the simultaneous testing of a large collection of hypotheses for the functional sparse covariance structures. Our approach is fully functional in the sense it does not rely on dimension reduction techniques, thereby avoiding any incurred information loss.

•

Second, we extend our method and theory to the more general error-contamination setting (
2
), encompassing the common practical scenario of discretely observed functional data as a special case. On the method front, our procedure remains valid by simply replacing each
X_{ij}(\cdot)
with its estimated surrogate
\widehat{X}_{ij}(\cdot).
Theoretically, we demonstrate that, under an additional condition, our proposal still ensures control over false discoveries. Such condition can be verified under mild circumstances for discretely observed functional data.

•

Third, we propose a novel method to formulate the functional graphical model estimation as a multiple testing problem for the cross-covariance structures between
p(p-1)/2
pairs of functional regression errors formed by respectively regressing each pair
X_{ij}(\cdot)
and
X_{ik}(\cdot)
(
1\leq j<k\leq p
) on the remaining
p-2
functional variables. Employing the penalized functional regression technique to estimate functional coefficients, we obtain
p(p-1)/2
pairs of functional residuals, which can be nicely integrated into a generalized version of our error-contamination framework. By deriving relevant convergence rates to validate the extra condition and applying our established theory, we show that such proposal combined with our multiple test procedure achieves effective false discovery control.

Our work lies in the intersection of high-dimensional functional data analysis and large-scale multiple testing.
In addition to aforementioned functional covariance and graphical models, various functional sparsity assumptions are commonly imposed on the model parameter space for high-dimensional functional data, and different regularized estimation procedures have been developed for the respective learning tasks including functional additive regressions
Fan et al. (2015)
;
Kong et al. (2016)
;
Luo and Qi (2017)
;
Chang et al. (2024)
, functional linear discriminant analysis
Xue et al. (2023)
and sparse functional principal components analysis
Hu and Yao (2022)
.
There is also a wealth of literature focused on the simultaneous testing of a large collection of hypotheses with false discovery control for the local covariance structures. Examples include, but are not limited to, studies on correlations
Cai and Liu (2016)
, conditional dependence between variables or subvectors in Gaussian graphical models
Liu (2013)
;
Xia, Cai and Cai (2018)
, regression coefficients over multiple responses
Xia, Cai and Li (2018)
, cross-covariance matrices between subvectors in multimodal integrative analysis
Xia et al. (2020)
and more complex dependence structures of multimodal imaging data
Chang et al. (2023)
.

2.1
Testing a cross-covariance function

Based on
n
i.i.d. functional observations
{\bf X}_{1}(\cdot),\dots,{\bf X}_{n}(\cdot),
we obtain the sample covariance function matrix
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}(\cdot,\cdot)=\{\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}(\cdot,\cdot)\}_{p\times p}
with its
(j,k)
th entry given by

\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}(u,v)=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{X_{ij}(u)-\widebar{X}_{j}(u)\}\{X_{ik}(v)-\widebar{X}_{k}(v)\}~\text{for}~(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2}~\text{and}~j,k\in[p]

(3)

and
\widebar{X}_{j}(\cdot)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{ij}(\cdot)
for
j\in[p].

We propose the following Hilbert–Schmidt-norm-based test statistic for testing
H_{0,jk}
with a given pair
j\neq k,
i.e.,

T_{n,jk}=n\|\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}\|_{{\cal S}}^{2}\,.

(4)

Remark 1

Note that the test statistic
T_{n,jk}
measures the discrepancy between the sample cross-covariance function
\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}(u,v)
and the true function using an
L_{2}
-type norm, which tends to be small under the null hypothesis
H_{0,jk}.
For functional data with local spikes, one may consider a supremum-norm-based test statistic, upon which our multiple testing procedure in Section
2.2
remains valid under the associated technical analysis. See more detailed discussions in Section
7
. By comparison, our
L_{2}
type-based test performs better when a (eventually not so strong) signal in the cross-covariance function is spread out over the entire domain
{\cal U}^{2},
making it well-suited for accommodating the brain imaging data.
See Figures
S1
and
S2
of the Supplementary Material for the supporting empirical evidence.

Denote the
j
th subject-effect function
Z_{ij}(\cdot)=X_{ij}(\cdot)-\mu_{j}(\cdot)
with the mean function
\mu_{j}(\cdot)=\mathbb{E}\{X_{ij}(\cdot)\}
for
j\in[p].

Denote
\Gamma_{jk}(u_{1},v_{1},u_{2},v_{2})=\text{Cov}\{Z_{j}(u_{1})Z_{k}(v_{1}),Z_{j}(u_{2})Z_{k}(v_{2})\}
for
(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in{\cal U}^{2}
, which can be shown to be the asymptotic covariance function of
\sqrt{n}\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}(u,v)
for
(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2}.
By performing an eigenanalysis of
\Gamma_{jk}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
, we obtain the sorted eigenvalues

\lambda_{jk1}\geq\lambda_{jk2}\geq\cdots>0
and the associated orthonormal eigenfunctions
\varphi_{jk1}(\cdot,\cdot),\varphi_{jk2}(\cdot,\cdot),\dots
satisfying

\iint\Gamma_{jk}(u_{1},v_{1},u_{2},v_{2})\varphi_{jkr}(u_{2},v_{2})\,{\rm d}u_{2}{\rm d}v_{2}=\lambda_{jkr}\varphi_{jkr}(u_{1},v_{1})~~\text{for}~~r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

(5)

We compute the sample estimator of
\Gamma_{jk}
by

\displaystyle\widehat{\Gamma}_{jk}(u_{1},v_{1},u_{2},v_{2})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{\widetilde{X}_{ij}(u_{1})\widetilde{X}_{ik}(v_{1})-\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}(u_{1},v_{1})\}\{\widetilde{X}_{ij}(u_{2})\widetilde{X}_{ik}(v_{2})-\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}(u_{2},v_{2})\}

with
\widetilde{X}_{ij}(\cdot)=X_{ij}(\cdot)-\widebar{X}_{j}(\cdot)
and then carry out an eigenanalysis of
\widehat{\Gamma}_{jk}
in the form of (
5
), which leads to the estimated eigenvalue/eigenfunction pairs
\{\hat{\lambda}_{jkr},\hat{\varphi}_{jkr}(\cdot,\cdot)\}_{r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}.

Before investigating the theoretical properties, we need some regularity conditions.

Condition 1

(i)

\max_{j\in[p]}\int\Sigma_{jj}(u,u)\,{\rm d}u=O(1);

(ii)
\sup_{u\in{\cal U}}\Sigma_{jj}(u,u)=O(1)
for any
j\in[p]
.

Condition 2

{\mathbb{E}}(\|Z_{1j}\|^{4})=O(1)
for any
j\in[p]
and
\sup_{(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\{Z_{1j}^{2}(u)Z_{1k}^{2}(v)\}=O(1)
for any
j,k\in[p].

Condition
1
(i) implies that
\int\Sigma_{jj}(u,u){\rm d}u=O(1)
for any
j\in[p],
which together with Conditions
1
(ii) and
2
are standard in functional data analysis literature, see, e.g., Chapter 10 of
Zhang (2013)
.
We are now ready to present the theorem regarding the limiting null distribution and the asymptotic power.

Theorem 1

Suppose that Conditions
1
and
2
hold, then the following two assertions hold.

(i)
Under the null hypothesis
H_{0,jk}
, we have that

T_{n,jk}\overset{d}{\rightarrow}T_{0,jk}
as
n\rightarrow\infty,

where

T_{0,jk}\overset{d}{=}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\hat{\lambda}_{jkr}A_{r}
with
\{A_{r}\}_{r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
being i.i.d.
\chi^{2}_{1}
random variables.

(ii)
Under the alternative hypothesis
H_{1,jk}
with
\|\Sigma_{jk}\|_{{\cal S}}>0
, we have that
{\mathbb{P}}\{T_{n,jk}\geq T_{0,jk}(\alpha)\}\rightarrow 1
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, where
T_{0,jk}(\alpha)
is the
100\alpha\%
upper percentile of
T_{0,jk}
.

Remark 2

Theorem
1
indicates that
T_{n,jk}
is asymptotically an infinite
\chi^{2}
-type mixture under the null hypothesis
H_{0,jk}.
Its distribution can be approximated by a noncentral chi-squared distribution with parameters determined by
\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\hat{\lambda}_{jkr},

\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\hat{\lambda}_{jkr}^{2},

\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\hat{\lambda}_{jkr}^{3}
and
\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\hat{\lambda}_{jkr}^{4}
, see, e.g.,
Liu et al. (2009)
. We will use this approximation in our numerical studies. It is noteworthy that, instead of calculating each individual eigenvalue
\hat{\lambda}_{jkr}
explicitly, it is more convenient to compute the aforementioned infinite sums. For example,
\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\hat{\lambda}_{jkr}
and
\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\hat{\lambda}_{jkr}^{2}
can be obtained through the discretization of functional data and the respective simple Riemann sum approximations to the integrals
\iint\widehat{\Gamma}_{jk}(u,v,u,v)\,{\rm d}u{\rm d}v
and
\iiiint\widehat{\Gamma}_{jk}(u_{1},v_{1},u_{2},v_{2})^{2}\,{\rm d}u_{1}{\rm d}v_{1}{\rm d}u_{2}{\rm d}v_{2}.
In this sense, our procedure is fully functional without relying on any dimension reduction techniques that may result in information loss.

2.2
Multiple testing of cross-covariance functions

To develop a general multiple testing procedure, we let
{\cal H}=\{(j,k):j,k\in[p],j<k\}
and reformulate (
1
) as simultaneously testing
Q=p(p-1)/2
hypotheses:

\displaystyle H_{0}^{(q)}:\Sigma_{j_{q}k_{q}}(u,v)=0\mbox{ for any }(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2}\mbox{ and }(j_{q},k_{q})\in{\cal H}~~\mbox{vs}~~H_{1}^{(q)}:H_{0}^{(q)}\mbox{ is not true}\,,

(6)

for
q\in[Q]
. The test statistic of the
q
th hypothesis test
H_{0}^{(q)}
is constructed as

T_{n}^{(q)}:=T_{n,j_{q}k_{q}}=n\|\widehat{\Sigma}_{j_{q}k_{q}}\|_{{\cal S}}^{2}~,~~q\in[Q].

(7)

Then we reject
H_{0}^{(q)}
when
T_{n}^{(q)}
takes some large values.
Let
{\cal H}_{0}=\{q\in[Q]:H_{0}^{(q)}~\textrm{is true}\}
be the set of all true null hypotheses with
Q_{0}=|{\cal H}_{0}|.

By Theorem
1
(i), we obtain that

{\mathbb{P}}\{T_{n}^{(q)}\leq x\}-{\mathbb{P}}\{T_{0}^{(q)}\leq x\}=o(1)\text{ for any }q\in{\cal H}_{0},

(8)

where
T_{0}^{(q)}\overset{d}{=}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\hat{\lambda}_{r}^{(q)}A_{r}
with

\hat{\lambda}_{r}^{(q)}:=\hat{\lambda}_{j_{q}k_{q}r}
for notational simplicity.
Then the p-value of
H_{0}^{(q)}
is given by
{\rm pv}^{(q)}={\mathbb{P}}\{T_{0}^{(q)}\geq T_{n}^{(q)}\}
, and its normal quantile transformation takes the form
V_{q}=\Phi^{-1}\{1-{\rm pv}^{(q)}\},
where
\Phi(\cdot)
is the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution. For any threshold level
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
H_{0}^{(q)}
is rejected if
V_{q}\geq t,
denote the total number of false positives by
R_{0}(t)=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}I(V_{q}\geq t)
and the total number of rejections by
R(t)=\sum_{q=1}^{Q}I(V_{q}\geq t)
. Then, the false discovery proportion (FDP) and the false discovery rate (FDR) are respectively defined as

\displaystyle{\rm FDP}(t)=\frac{R_{0}(t)}{R(t)\vee 1}~~~\textrm{and}~~~{\rm FDR}(t)=\mathbb{E}\{{\rm FDP}(t)\}\,.

Given a prespecified level
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the main target is to find the smallest
\hat{t}
such that
{\rm FDP}(\hat{t})\leq\alpha.

To achieve this, we first consider
{\rm FDP}(t),
the numerator of which needs to be estimated due to the unknown set
{\cal H}_{0}.
Using (
8
), we can approximate
{\mathbb{P}}(V_{q}\geq t)
as
1-\Phi(t)
for any
q\in\mathcal{H}_{0}.
An ideal estimate for
{\rm FDR}(t)
can be obtained by

[Q_{0}\{1-\Phi(t)\}]/\{1\vee\sum_{q=1}^{Q}I(V_{q}\geq t)\}
,
where
Q_{0}
is unknown in practice. Thus we propose to estimate
{\rm FDP}(t)
in a more conservative way by replacing
Q_{0}
with
Q:

\widehat{\rm FDP}(t)=\frac{Q\{1-\Phi(t)\}}{1\vee\sum_{q=1}^{Q}I(V_{q}\geq t)}.

(9)

We summarize the proposed multiple testing procedure in Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm 1

Multiple testing procedure

Step 1.
Calculate the test statistics
T_{n}^{(q)}
in (
7
) and its transformation
V_{q}
for all
q\in[Q]
.

Step 2.
Estimate the FDP by
\widehat{\rm FDP}(t)
in (
9
).

Step 3.
For a given
0<\alpha<1
, choose

\displaystyle\hat{t}=\inf\big\{0\leq t\leq(2\log Q-2\log\log Q)^{1/2}:\,\widehat{\rm FDP}(t)\leq\alpha\big\}\,.

If
\hat{t}
does not exist, set
\hat{t}=(2\log Q)^{1/2}
.

Step 4.
For
q\in[Q]
, reject
H_{0}^{(q)}
with
V_{q}\geq\hat{t}.

Before presenting the theoretical results, we impose some regularity conditions.

Condition 3

For each
j\in[p]
and
i\in[n],

Z_{ij}(\cdot)
is a mean-zero sub-Gaussian process, i.e., there exists some constant
\check{c}_{j}>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{H},

\mathbb{E}\big\{\exp(\langle x,Z_{ij}\rangle)\big\}\leq\exp\big\{2^{-1}\check{c}_{j}^{2}\iint x(u)\Sigma_{jj}(u,v)x(v)\rm{d}u{\rm d}v\big\}.

To accommodate the formulation in this section, for any
q\in\mathcal{H}_{0},
the Karhunen-Loève expansion allows us to represent

z_{iq}(u,v):=Z_{ij_{q}}(u)Z_{ik_{q}}(v)=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}a_{ir}^{(q)}\varphi_{r}^{(q)}(u,v),
where
\lambda_{r}^{(q)}:=\lambda_{j_{q}k_{q}r}
,
\varphi_{r}^{(q)}(u,v):=\varphi_{j_{q}k_{q}r}(u,v)
, and
a_{ir}^{(q)}=\iint z_{iq}(u,v)\varphi_{r}^{(q)}(u,v)\,{\rm d}u{\rm d}v.

Condition 4

For any
q\in\mathcal{H}_{0},

\lambda_{1}^{(q)}>\lambda_{2}^{(q)}>\cdots>0,

and there exist some constant
\eta_{1}>1
such that
\lambda_{r}^{(q)}\lesssim r^{-\eta_{1}}
for
r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

Condition
3
is an infinite-dimensional analog of the sub-Gaussian condition within Hilbert space, which is satisfied by Gaussian processes. This condition ensures that
z_{iq}(\cdot,\cdot)
exhibits sub-exponential behavior, as reflected in the associated basis coefficients
\{a_{ir}^{(q)}\}_{r\in{\mathbb{N}_{+}}}.
See Lemma
A1
of the Supplementary Material. These sub-exponentialities facilitate the Gaussian approximations of the proposed test statistics.
Condition
4
allows the polynomial decay of the upper bounds on eigenvalues with parameter
\eta_{1}
determining the decay rate, and can be used to control the truncation error rate
\sum_{r=M+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{r}^{(q)}\lesssim M^{-\eta_{1}+1}
for
M\rightarrow\infty.
Larger values of
\eta_{1}
correspond to faster decay rate and smaller truncation errors. It can be easily verified that Condition
4
is fulfilled when the eigenvalues for the covariance function of
Z_{ij_{q}}(\cdot)
decay polynomially
Kong et al. (2016)
;
Qiao et al. (2019)
.

To investigate the theoretical properties of the proposed multiple testing procedure, we need to give some regularity conditions on the dependency among the test statistics
\{T_{n}^{(q)}\}_{q=1}^{Q}
. Notice that
\zeta_{q}=\Phi^{-1}[F_{q}\{T_{n}^{(q)}\}]
follows the standard normal distribution, where
F_{q}(\cdot)
denotes the distribution of
T_{n}^{(q)}
. We use the correlation between two normal random variables
\zeta_{q}
and
\zeta_{q^{\prime}}
to characterize the dependence between test statistics
T_{n}^{(q)}
and
T_{n}^{(q^{\prime})}
. For some constant
\gamma>0
, define the set

\mathcal{S}_{q}(\gamma)=\{q^{\prime}\in[Q]:q^{\prime}\neq q,|{\rm Corr}(\zeta_{q},\,\zeta_{q^{\prime}})|\geq(\log Q)^{-2-\gamma}\}\,.

For a given
q\in[Q]
, the remaining
Q-1
test statistics

\{T_{n}^{(q^{\prime})}\}_{q^{\prime}\in[Q]/\{q\}}
can be categorized into two scenarios:
(i) for
q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{q}(\gamma)
,
T_{n}^{(q^{\prime})}
has a relatively strong dependence with
T_{n}^{(q)},

and (ii) for
q^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{S}_{q}(\gamma)
,
T_{n}^{(q^{\prime})}
has a quite weak dependence with
T_{n}^{(q)}
.
We establish the theoretical guarantee under these two scenarios separately using different technical tools.

Theorem 2

Suppose that Conditions
1
–
4
hold,
\max_{1\leq q\neq q^{\prime}\leq Q}|{\rm Corr}(\zeta_{q},\zeta_{q^{\prime}})|\leq c_{\zeta}
for some constant
c_{\zeta}\in(0,1),
and
\max_{q\in[Q]}|\mathcal{S}_{q}(\gamma)|=o(p^{2\nu})
for some constants
\gamma>0
and
0<\nu<(1-c_{\zeta})/(1+c_{\zeta})
. If

p\lesssim n^{\kappa}
for some constant
\kappa>0
, then
\limsup_{n,Q\rightarrow\infty}{\rm FDR}(\hat{t})\leq\alpha Q_{0}/Q
and
\lim_{n,Q\rightarrow\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\{{\rm FDP}(\hat{t})\leq\alpha Q_{0}/Q+\varkappa\}=1
for any
\varkappa>0
.

Theorem
2
shows that both FDP and FDR can be controlled below the level
\alpha Q_{0}/Q
asymptotically.
The conditions on
{\rm Corr}(\zeta_{q},\zeta_{q^{\prime}})
and
\mathcal{S}_{q}(\gamma)
are imposed to bound the variance of
\sum_{q\in{\cal H}_{0}}I(V_{q}\geq t)
in
{\rm FDP}(t),
see similar conditions in
Liu (2013)
and
Chang et al. (2023)
. Specifically, the condition
\max_{1\leq q\neq q^{\prime}\leq Q}|{\rm Corr}(\zeta_{q},\zeta_{q^{\prime}})|\leq c_{\zeta}
places a constraint on the strength of dependence between different
\zeta_{q}
and
\zeta_{q^{\prime}}
. The condition
\max_{q\in[Q]}|\mathcal{S}_{q}(\gamma)|=o(p^{2\nu})
controls the number of pairs of
(\zeta_{q},\zeta_{q^{\prime}})
with relatively strong dependencies.

3
Test with error-contaminated functional data

In practical applications,
{\bf X}_{i}(\cdot)
for
i\in[n]
are rarely fully observed. Instead, we estimate them using certain methods, resulting in estimated curves
\widehat{{\bf X}}_{i}(\cdot)=\{\widehat{X}_{i1}(\cdot),\ldots,\widehat{X}_{ip}(\cdot)\}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}},
denoted as error-contaminated functional data satisfying (
2
). This leads to functional errors
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}(\cdot)=\widehat{{\bf X}}_{i}(\cdot)-{\bf X}_{i}(\cdot)
with
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}(\cdot)=\{e_{i1}(u),\ldots,e_{ip}(\cdot)\}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}.
See below for two examples that are related to applications in Sections
4.1
and
4.2
, respectively.

•

When
\{{\bf X}_{i}(\cdot)\}
are discretely observed with errors, we can apply nonparametric smoothing methods to the observed data, thereby obtaining functional estimates
\widehat{{\bf X}}_{i}(\cdot).

•

When
\{{\bf X}_{i}(\cdot)\}
represent the functional regression errors in function-on-function linear regressions, we can estimate functional coefficients and thus obtain functional fitted values, resulting in functional residuals
\widehat{\bf X}_{i}(\cdot).

In the above examples, it is apparent that each
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}(\cdot)
may not be independent, and the properties of
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}(\cdot)
need to be investigated for specific models. This section is devoted to the corresponding tests for the cross-covariance functions
\{\Sigma_{jk}(u,v)\}_{1\leq j\neq k\leq p}
based on the estimated curves
\widehat{{\bf X}}_{i}(\cdot)
under some general conditions.

Analogous to Section
2.1
, we first consider the marginal testing problem (
1
) for error-contaminated functional data.
To this end, we replace each
{\bf X}_{i}(\cdot)
by its error-contaminated version
\widehat{\bf X}_{i}(\cdot)
in (
3
)–(
4
), and propose a new test statistic for
H_{0,jk}
for a given
1\leq j\neq k\leq p:

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{n,jk}=n\|\widetilde{\Sigma}_{jk}\|_{{\cal S}}^{2}\,,

(10)

where
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{jk}(u,v)=(n-1)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{\widehat{X}_{ij}(u)-{\widebar{\widehat{X}}}_{j}(u)\}\{\widehat{X}_{ik}(v)-\widebar{\widehat{X}}_{k}(v)\}
and
\widebar{\widehat{X}}_{j}(u)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{X}_{ij}(u).

To control the difference between
\widetilde{T}_{n,jk}
and
T_{n,jk}
specified in (
4
), we need the cross-covariance functions
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{Xe}(u,v)={\text{Cov}}\{{\bf X}(u),{\mathbf{e}}(v)\}=\{\Sigma_{jk}^{Xe}(u,v)\}_{j,k\in[p]}
and
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{ee}(u,v)={\text{Cov}}\{{\mathbf{e}}(u),{\mathbf{e}}(v)\}=\{\Sigma_{jk}^{ee}(u,v)\}_{j,k\in[p]},
whose sample versions are

\displaystyle\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{Xe}(u,v)=\{\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}^{Xe}(u,v)\}_{j,k\in[p]}=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{{\bf X}(u)-\widebar{\bf X}(u)\}\{{\mathbf{e}}(v)-\bar{\mathbf{e}}(v)\}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\,,

\displaystyle\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{ee}(u,v)=\{\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}^{ee}(u,v)\}_{j,k\in[p]}=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{{\mathbf{e}}(u)-\bar{\mathbf{e}}(u)\}\{{\mathbf{e}}(v)-\bar{\mathbf{e}}(v)\}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\,,

respectively.
Before presenting the asymptotic properties of the new test statistic
\widetilde{T}_{n,jk}
under the null and alternative hypotheses, we need the following condition, which will be verified by deriving the corresponding elementwise maximum rates of convergence for two applications in Section
4
.

Condition 5

There exist some constants
a_{1},a_{3}>1/2
and
a_{2},a_{4}>0
such that

\displaystyle\max_{j,k\in[p]}\|\widehat{\Sigma}^{Xe}_{jk}\|_{{\cal S}}=O_{\rm p}\big\{n^{-a_{1}}(\log p)^{a_{2}}\big\}~~{\rm and}~~\max_{j,k\in[p]}\|\widehat{\Sigma}^{ee}_{jk}\|_{{\cal S}}=O_{\rm p}\big\{n^{-a_{3}}(\log p)^{a_{4}}\big\}.

(11)

Theorem 3

Suppose that Conditions
1
,
2
,
5
hold, then the following two assertions hold.

(i)
Under the null hypothesis
H_{0,jk}
, we have that

\widetilde{T}_{n,jk}\overset{d}{\rightarrow}T_{0,jk}
as
n\rightarrow\infty,

where
T_{0,jk}\overset{d}{=}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\hat{\lambda}_{jkr}A_{r}
with
A_{r}\overset{i.i.d.}{\sim}\chi^{2}_{1}
.

(ii)
Under the alternative hypothesis

H_{1,jk}
with
\|\Sigma_{jk}\|_{{\cal S}}>0
, we have
{\mathbb{P}}\{\widetilde{T}_{n,jk}\geq T_{0,jk}(\alpha)\}\rightarrow 1
as
n\rightarrow\infty.

Analogous to Section
2.2
, we next consider the multiple testing problem (
6
) under the functional error-contamination setting. For each
q\in[Q]
, we propose the test statistic for the
q
th hypothesis testing as follows:

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{n}^{(q)}:=\widetilde{T}_{n,j_{q}k_{q}}=n\|\widetilde{\Sigma}_{j_{q}k_{q}}\|_{{\cal S}}^{2}\,.

By Theorem
3
(i), we have

{\mathbb{P}}\{\widetilde{T}_{n}^{(q)}\leq x\}-{\mathbb{P}}\{T_{0}^{(q)}\leq x\}=o(1)

for any
q\in{\cal H}_{0}
.
Let the resulting p-value of
H_{0}^{(q)}
be
\widetilde{\rm pv}^{(q)}={\mathbb{P}}\{T_{0}^{(q)}\geq\widetilde{T}_{n}^{(q)}\}
, its normal quantile transformation
\widetilde{V}_{q}=\Phi^{-1}\{1-\widetilde{\rm pv}^{(q)}\},
and

{t}
be the threshold level such that
H_{0}^{(q)}
is rejected if
\widetilde{V}_{q}\geq{t}
.
Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the ideal choice of
{t}
is the smallest
{t}
such that we would reject as many true positives as possible while controlling the false discovery proportion at the prescribed level
\alpha
. To achieve this, similar to (
9
) and Step 3 in Algorithm
1
, we let

\widetilde{{\rm FDP}}(t)=Q\{1-\Phi(t)\}/\{1\vee\sum_{q=1}^{Q}I(\widetilde{V}_{q}\geq t)\}
and

\tilde{t}=\inf\{0\leq t\leq(2\log Q-2\log\log Q)^{1/2}:\,\widetilde{\rm FDP}(t)\leq\alpha\}.

We then propose a new algorithm to implement the multiple testing procedure by replacing
T_{n}^{(q)},

\widehat{{\rm FDP}}(t),

\hat{t}
and
V_{q}
in Algorithm
1
with

\widetilde{T}_{n}^{(q)},

\widetilde{{\rm FDP}}(t),

\tilde{t}
and
\widetilde{V}_{q},
respectively.

To impose additional regularity conditions similar to those in Theorem
2
, we let
\widetilde{F}_{q}(\cdot)
be the distribution of
\widetilde{T}_{n}^{(q)},
and write

\widetilde{\zeta}_{q}=\Phi^{-1}[\widetilde{F}_{q}\{\widetilde{T}_{n}^{(q)}\}]
, which follows the standard normal distribution. Accordingly, we define

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{q}(\tilde{\gamma})=\{{q^{\prime}}\in[Q]:{q^{\prime}}\neq q,|{\rm Corr}(\widetilde{\zeta}_{q},\,\widetilde{\zeta}_{{q^{\prime}}})|\geq(\log Q)^{-2-\tilde{\gamma}}\}~\text{ for }\tilde{\gamma}>0~\text{and}~q\in[Q].

Similar to Theorem
2
established for fully observed functional data, the next theorem indicates that our proposed multiple testing procedure for error-contaminated functional data is guaranteed to control both FDR and FDP below the nominal level asymptotically.

Theorem 4

Suppose that Conditions
1
–
5
hold,
\max_{q,q^{\prime}\in[Q]}|{\rm Corr}(\tilde{\zeta}_{q},\tilde{\zeta}_{q^{\prime}})|\leq c_{\tilde{\zeta}}
for some constant
c_{\tilde{\zeta}}\in(0,1)
, and
\max_{q\in[Q]}|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{q}(\tilde{\gamma})|=o(p^{2\tilde{\nu}})
for some constants
\tilde{\gamma}>0
and
0<\tilde{\nu}<(1-c_{\tilde{\zeta}})/(1+c_{\tilde{\zeta}})
. If
p\lesssim n^{\tilde{\kappa}}
for some constant
\tilde{\kappa}>0
, then
\limsup_{n,Q\rightarrow\infty}{\rm FDR}({\tilde{t}})\leq\alpha Q_{0}/Q
and
\lim_{n,Q\rightarrow\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\{{\rm FDP}({\tilde{t}})\leq\alpha Q_{0}/Q+\widetilde{\varkappa}\}=1
for any
\widetilde{\varkappa}>0
.

Remark 3

Note that our proposed multiple testing procedure considers
Q=p(p-1)/2
hypotheses of cross-covariance functions induced from
p(p-1)/2
pairs of
\{\widehat{X}_{ij}(\cdot),\widehat{X}_{ik}(\cdot)\}
based on
p
estimated curves
\widehat{X}_{i1}(\cdot),\dots,\widehat{X}_{ip}(\cdot).
The theoretical guarantee of this framework can be easily extended to a more generalized multiple testing setting, where multiple hypotheses are induced from
Q=p(p-1)/2
pairs based on
p(p-1)/2
instead of
p
estimated curves. For example, in Section
4.2
, we transform the multiple testing problem of conditional dependence structures to that of the error cross-covariance patterns in (
15
), which is induced from

p(p-1)/2
pairs of functional residuals as specified in (
16
), i.e.
\{\hat{\varepsilon}_{i,jk}(\cdot),\hat{\varepsilon}_{i,kj}(\cdot)\}
for
1\leq j<k\leq p.
Not only does our proposal for the generalized multiple testing problem remain unchanged, but also, following similar arguments as those in the proofs of Theorems
2
and
4
, it can still achieve effective control of false discoveries asymptotically. Further details are available in the application to the functional graphical model in Section
4.2
.

4
Applications

4.1
Discretely observed functional data

For any
i\in[n]
and
j\in[p],
each
X_{ij}(\cdot)
is not directly observable in practice. Instead, it is observed with errors at
T_{ij}
random time points,
U_{ij1},\dots,U_{ijT_{ij}}\in{\cal U}.
Let
W_{ijt}
be the observed value of
X_{ij}(U_{ijt})
satisfying

W_{ijt}=X_{ij}(U_{ijt})+\varsigma_{ijt}\,,

(12)

where the errors
\varsigma_{ijt}
’s, independent of
X_{ij}(\cdot)
’s, are i.i.d. with
\mathbb{E}(\varsigma_{ijt})=0
and
{\rm Var}(\varsigma_{ijt})=\sigma_{j}^{2}<\infty.

The sampling frequency
T_{ij}
plays a vital role in the estimation as it may affect the choice of the estimation procedure. For densely sampled functional data with
T_{ij}
’s larger than some order of
n,
the conventional approach is to implement nonparametric smoothing to the observations from each subject, thus reconstructing each individual curve before subsequent analysis
Zhang and Chen (2007)
. For sparsely sampled functional data with bounded
T_{ij}
’s, pre-smoothing is no longer viable; it is standard practice to consider pooling data from all subjects to borrow strength across all observations
Zhang and Wang (2016)
.

Since brain signals in neuroimaging data of our interest are typically recorded at a dense set of points, we make use of the pre-smoothing to facilitate the presentation of methodology and theory. In what follows, denote
K_{h}(\cdot)=h^{-1}K(\cdot/h)
for a univariate kernel with bandwidth
h>0.
We first apply the frequently-adopted local linear smoothers to the observed data
\{(U_{ijt},W_{ijt}):t\in[T_{ij}]\}
for each
i\in[n]
and
j\in[p],
and thus can obtain the estimated curves
\widehat{X}_{ij}(u)=\hat{g}_{0ij},
where

(\hat{g}_{0ij},\hat{g}_{1ij})=\arg\min_{g_{0ij},g_{1ij}}\,\sum_{t=1}^{T_{ij}}\big\{W_{ijt}-g_{0ij}-g_{1ij}(U_{ijt}-u)\big\}^{2}K_{h_{ij}}(U_{ijt}-u).

For any
j\in[p],
individual functions across
i\in[n]
often admit similar smoothness properties and sometimes similar patterns, it is thus reasonable to use a common bandwidth
h_{j}
for all of them.
We then employ the multiple testing procedure in Section
3
to the estimated curves
\{\widehat{X}_{ij}(\cdot)\},
which leads to
e_{ij}(\cdot)=\widehat{X}_{ij}(\cdot)-X_{ij}(\cdot).

To provide the theoretical guarantee for such procedure based on Theorem
4
, we need to verify Condition
5
, i.e. specifying the convergence rates of
\max_{j,k\in[p]}\|\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}^{Xe}\|_{{\cal S}}
and
\max_{j,k\in[p]}\|\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}^{ee}\|_{{\cal S}}.
To this end, we impose the following regularity conditions.

Condition 6

For each
i\in[n],

j\in[p],t\in[T_{ij}]
,
\varsigma_{ijt}
is a sub-Gaussian random variable, i.e., there exists some constant
\tilde{c}>0
such that for all
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R},

\mathbb{E}\{\exp(\varsigma_{ijt}\tilde{x})\}\leq\exp(2^{-1}\tilde{c}^{2}\sigma_{j}^{2}\tilde{x}^{2}).

Condition 7

(i)
For any
j\in[p],
the average sampling frequency
\widebar{T}_{j}=(n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{ij}^{-1})^{-1}\rightarrow\infty
,
h_{j}\rightarrow 0
and
\widebar{T}_{j}h_{j}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty;

(ii)

h_{j}\asymp h
and
\widebar{T}_{j}\asymp T
for each
j
.

Condition 8

(i)
Let
\{U_{ijt};i\in[n],j\in[p],t\in[T_{ij}]\}
be i.i.d. copies of a random variable
U
defined on
{\cal U}

with density
f_{U}(\cdot)
satisfying
0<m_{f}\leq\inf_{u\in{\cal U}}f_{U}(u)\leq\sup_{u\in{\cal U}}f_{U}(u)\leq M_{f}<\infty
;

(ii)

X_{ij}(\cdot)
,
U_{ijt}
and
\varsigma_{ijt}
are independent for each
i,j,t.

Condition 9

(i)

The kernel
K(\cdot)
is a symmetric probability density function on compact support
[-1,1]
with
\int u^{2}K(u)\,{\rm d}u<\infty
and
\int K^{2}(u)\,{\rm d}u<\infty
.

(ii)

K(\cdot)
is Lipschitz continuous, i.e., there exists some positive constant
L
such that
|K(u)-K(v)|\leq L|u-v|
for any
u,v\in[-1,1]
.

Condition 10

(i)

\partial^{2}\mu_{j}(u)/\partial u^{2}
is uniformly bounded over
u\in{\cal U}
and
j\in[p]
.
(ii)
The covariance functions satisfy
\max_{j\in[p]}\sup_{u\in{\cal U}}|\Sigma_{jj}(u,u)|=O(1)
.

Condition
6
requires that the random errors are sub-Gaussian.
Conditions
7
–
10
are standard in the literature of nonparametric smoothing for functional data
Zhang and Chen (2007)
;
Zhang and Wang (2016)
adaptable to the multivariate setting. The following proposition indicates that Condition
5
holds for discretely observed functional data.

Proposition 1

Under Conditions
3
and
6
–
10
, we have

\max_{j,k\in[p]}\|\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}^{Xe}\|_{{\cal S}}=O_{\rm p}\big[\log(p\vee n)(Th)^{-1/2}+\{\log(p\vee n)\}^{1/2}h^{2}\big]

and

\max_{j,k\in[p]}\|\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}^{ee}\|_{{\cal S}}=O_{\rm p}\big\{\log(p\vee n)(Th)^{-1}+h^{4}\big\}
.

Remark 4

Recall that
p\lesssim n^{\tilde{\kappa}}
for some
\tilde{\kappa}>0
is required in Theorem
4
.
To facilitate further discussion, we consider a simplified common scenario where there exist three constants
\eta_{2},\eta_{3}
and
\eta_{4}>0
such that
T\asymp n^{\eta_{2}}
,
h\asymp n^{-\eta_{3}}
and
p\asymp n^{\eta_{4}}
.
Some calculations show that Condition

is satisfied by setting
a_{1}=\min\{(\eta_{2}-\eta_{3})/2,2\eta_{3}\}
,
a_{2}=1,

a_{3}=\min(\eta_{2}-\eta_{3},4\eta_{3})
and
a_{4}=1
provided that
\eta_{3}>1/4
and
\eta_{2}>\eta_{3}+1.

With verifiable Condition

under the possibly sub-optimal rates established in Proposition

and other conditions required in Theorem

, an application of Theorem

yields that our multiple testing procedure for discretely observed functional data ensures the effective control of both FDR and FDP asymptotically.

4.2
Functional graphical model

Suppose that we observe
p
-vector of functional data
{\bf Y}_{i}(\cdot)=\{Y_{i1}(\cdot),\ldots,Y_{ip}(\cdot)\}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}
for
i\in[n]
defined on
{\cal U}
. Assume that
{\bf Y}_{i}(\cdot)
follows a mean zero multivariate Gaussian process with covariance function matrix
\boldsymbol{\Xi}(u,v)=\text{Cov}\{{\bf Y}_{i}(u),{\bf Y}_{i}(v)\}
for
(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2}.
Let
{\bf Y}_{i,-j,-k}(\cdot)
be the subvector of
{\bf Y}_{i}(\cdot)
with
Y_{ij}(\cdot)
and
Y_{ik}(\cdot)
being removed. In this section, we formulate the estimation of functional graphical model depicting the conditional dependence structure among
p
components of
{\bf Y}_{i}(\cdot)
as simultaneous testing of
Q=p(p-1)/2
hypotheses,

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{0,jk}:Y_{ij}(\cdot)\perp\!\!\!\perp Y_{ik}(\cdot)\,|\,{\bf Y}_{i,-j,-k}(\cdot)~~~\mbox{vs}~~~\widetilde{H}_{1,jk}:Y_{ij}(\cdot)\not\perp\!\!\!\perp Y_{ik}(\cdot)\,|\,{\bf Y}_{i,-j,-k}(\cdot)

(13)

for
1\leq j<k\leq p
, and we identify a significant edge between nodes
j
and
k
if and only if
\widetilde{H}_{0,jk}
is rejected.

For each
1\leq j<k\leq p
, consider regressing each pair
Y_{ij}(\cdot)
and
Y_{ik}(\cdot)
on the remaining
p-2
functional variables, i.e.,

\displaystyle Y_{ij}(u)=\sum_{\ell\neq j,k}\int_{{\cal U}}Y_{i\ell}(v)\beta_{jk,\ell}(u,v)\,{\rm d}v+\varepsilon_{i,jk}(u)\,,~~u\in{\cal U}\,,

(14)

\displaystyle Y_{ik}(u)=\sum_{\ell\neq j,k}\int_{{\cal U}}Y_{i\ell}(v)\beta_{kj,\ell}(u,v)\,{\rm d}v+\varepsilon_{i,kj}(u)\,,~~u\in{\cal U}\,,

where
\varepsilon_{i,jk}(\cdot)
and
\varepsilon_{i,kj}(\cdot)
represent the functional regression errors, independent of
{\bf Y}_{i,-j,-k}(\cdot)
. Under such functional regression setup, we have
\text{Cov}\big\{Y_{ij}(u),Y_{ik}(v)\big|{\bf Y}_{i,-j-k}(\cdot)\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\big[Y_{ij}(u)-{\mathbb{E}}\{Y_{ij}(u)|{\bf Y}_{i,-j-k}(\cdot)\}\big]\big[Y_{ik}(v)-{\mathbb{E}}\{Y_{ik}(v)|{\bf Y}_{i,-j-k}(\cdot)\}\big]\Big|{\bf Y}_{i,-j-k}(\cdot)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big\{\varepsilon_{i,jk}(u)\varepsilon_{i,kj}(v)|{\bf Y}_{i,-j,-k}(\cdot)\big\},

which possesses the property that

\text{Cov}\big\{Y_{ij}(u),Y_{ik}(v)\big|{\bf Y}_{i,-j-k}(\cdot)\big\}=0
for any
(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2},
and hence

\Sigma_{jk}^{\varepsilon}(u,v)={\text{Cov}}\{\varepsilon_{i,jk}(u),\varepsilon_{i,kj}(v)\}=0

for any
(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2}
whenever
Y_{ij}(\cdot)\perp\!\!\!\perp Y_{ik}(\cdot)\,|\,{\bf Y}_{i,-j,-k}(\cdot).

Thus, the hypotheses of conditional independence in (
13
) reduces to

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{0}^{(q)}:\Sigma_{j_{q}k_{q}}^{\varepsilon}(u,v)=0~\mbox{for any }(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2}\mbox{ and }(j_{q},k_{q})\in{\cal H}~\mbox{vs}~\widetilde{H}_{1}^{(q)}:\widetilde{H}_{0}^{(q)}\mbox{ is not true}\,,

(15)

for
q\in[Q].

Let
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{jk}(\cdot,\cdot)=\{\hat{\beta}_{jk,\ell}(\cdot,\cdot)\}_{\ell\neq j,k}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}
be some consistent estimator of
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{jk}(\cdot,\cdot)=\{\beta_{jk,\ell}(\cdot,\cdot)\}_{\ell\neq j,k}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}
.
Define the functional residuals by

\hat{\varepsilon}_{i,jk}(u):=Y_{ij}(u)-\int_{{\cal U}}{\bf Y}_{i,-j,-k}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}(v)\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{jk}(u,v)\,{\rm d}v={\varepsilon}_{i,jk}(u)+\text{deviation},

(16)

which takes a similar form as (
2
) within the error-contamination framework.
Let the sample covariance functions between residuals be

\widetilde{\Sigma}_{jk}^{\varepsilon}(u,v)=(n-1)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{\hat{\varepsilon}_{i,jk}(u)-\bar{\hat{\varepsilon}}_{jk}(u)\}\{\hat{\varepsilon}_{i,kj}(v)-\bar{\hat{\varepsilon}}_{kj}(v)\}
with
\bar{\hat{\varepsilon}}_{jk}(u)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{\varepsilon}_{i,jk}(u)

for any
j,k\in[p]
. Building upon the methodology in Section
3
, for each
q\in[Q]
, we propose the test statistic for
\widetilde{H}_{0}^{(q)}
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{n}^{\varepsilon,(q)}=n\|\widetilde{\Sigma}_{j_{q}k_{q}}^{\varepsilon}\|_{{\cal S}}^{2}\,.

The above formulation then allows for the direct adaptation of the notation and the multiple testing procedure from Section
3
to the functional graphical model setting by substituting the true signal pairs
\{X_{ij}(\cdot),X_{ik}(\cdot)\}
with
\{\varepsilon_{i,jk}(\cdot),\varepsilon_{i,kj}(\cdot)\}
, and the estimated signal pairs
\{\widehat{X}_{ij}(\cdot),\widehat{X}_{ik}(\cdot)\}
with
\{\hat{\varepsilon}_{i,jk}(\cdot),\hat{\varepsilon}_{i,kj}(\cdot)\}
for any
1\leq j<k\leq p
. To save space, we annotate quantities related to the functional graphical model with a superscript
\varepsilon
. For notational consistency, we will continue to use FDP and FDR to denote the false discovery proportion and rate, respectively, where
{\rm FDP}(t)={R_{0}^{\varepsilon}(t)}/\{R^{\varepsilon}(t)\vee 1\}
in this example.

We then follow the proposed multiple testing procedure in Section
3
based on
\widetilde{T}_{n}^{\varepsilon,(q)}
. Similar to Section
4.1
, we need to verify Condition
5
by establishing the convergence rates in (
11
) within the functional graphical model framework.
Denote
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}(u,v)=(n-1)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{{\bf Y}_{i}(u)-\widebar{\bf Y}(u)\}\{{\bf Y}_{i}(v)-\widebar{\bf Y}(v)\}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}

7
Discussion

Our test statistic
T_{n,jk}
(or
\widetilde{T}_{n,jk}
) is constructed based on the
L_{2}
-norm of the difference between the estimated cross-covariance function
\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}(u,v)
(or
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{jk}(u,v)
) and the true one
\Sigma_{jk}(u,v)
. For functional data with local spikes, one may use the supremum-norm based test statistics,

{T}_{n,jk}’=n^{1/2}\sup_{(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2}}|\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}(u,v)|
and
\widetilde{T}_{n,jk}^{\prime}=n^{1/2}\sup_{(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2}}|\widetilde{\Sigma}_{jk}(u,v)|,

upon which our multiple testing procedures in Sections
2.2
and
3
remain valid under the associated technical analysis. We next provide a sketch for establishing the theoretical properties of the supremum-norm based test statistics. For fully observed functional data, under Conditions
1
–
2
and regular continuity condition for
Z_{ij}(u)
, we can use the central limit theorem for empirical process and continuous mapping theorem to obtain the asymptotic properties of
{T}_{n,jk}^{\prime}
under the null and alternative hypotheses. It is not difficult to know the limiting null distribution of
{T}_{n,jk}^{\prime}
is
\sup_{(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2}}|G_{jk}(u,v)|
, where
G_{jk}(u,v)
follows a mean-zero Gaussian process with covariance function
\Gamma_{jk}(u_{1},v_{1},u_{2},v_{2})
. As
\sup_{(u,v)\in{\cal U}^{2}}|G_{jk}(u,v)|
is usually intractable, a parametric bootstrap method could be employed to determine critical values, which would also be used to construct the multiple testing. To investigate the theoretical properties of the multiple testing, we could apply the partition technique to reducing the problem from supremum over
{\cal U}^{2}
to the maximum over a grid of pairs, and then adopt the idea of Gaussian approximation in
Chernozhukov et al. (2017)
and the theory of multiple testing in
Chang et al. (2023)
to derive the theoretical results.
Furthermore, with the aid of initial pre-smoothing, this procedure can be extended naturally to the densely observed functional scenario. Although similar technical tools could be used, we need to address the additional complexity that arises from the estimation errors in the pre-smoothing step.

\displaystyle~{\text{Cov}}\{z_{1jk}(u_{1},v_{1}),z_{1jk}(u_{2},v_{2})\}

\displaystyle~{\mathbb{E}}\{Z_{1j}(u_{1})Z_{1k}(v_{1})Z_{1j}(u_{2})Z_{1k}(v_{2})\}-\Sigma_{jk}(u_{1},v_{1})\Sigma_{jk}(u_{2},v_{2})\,.

\displaystyle~\{{\mathbb{E}}(\|Z_{1j}\|^{4})\}^{1/2}\{{\mathbb{E}}(\|Z_{1k}\|^{4})\}^{1/2}<\infty\,.

as

\displaystyle=

(S.4)

Notice that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{e}}_{0}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}[{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{{\bf S}}_{ij}(u)\}]^{-1}[\widehat{\bf R}_{ij}(u)-{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{\bf R}_{ij}(u)\}]

\displaystyle-{\mathbf{e}}_{0}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\{\widehat{{\bf S}}_{ij}(u)\}^{-1}[\widehat{\bf S}_{ij}(u)-{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{\bf S}_{ij}(u)\}][{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{{\bf S}}_{ij}(u)\}]^{-1}\widehat{\bf R}_{ij}(u),

leading to

\begin{split}&|\widehat{X}_{ij}(u)-Z_{ij}(u)-\widetilde{X}_{ij}(u)|\\
\leq&~\|{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{{\bf S}}_{ij}(u)\}\|_{\min}^{-1}\|\widehat{\bf R}_{ij}(u)-{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{\bf R}_{ij}(u)\}\|\\
&+\|\widehat{{\bf S}}_{ij}(u)\|_{\min}^{-1}\|\widehat{\bf S}_{ij}(u)-{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{\bf S}_{ij}(u)\}\|_{\text{F}}\|{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{{\bf S}}_{ij}(u)\}\|_{\min}^{-1}\|\widehat{\bf R}_{ij}(u)\|,\end{split}

(S.18)

where

\|{\bf B}\|_{\min}=\{\lambda_{\min}({\bf B}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}{\bf B})\}^{1/2}
for any matrix
{\bf B}=(B_{jk})_{p\times p}
.

For
m_{1},m_{2}=1,2
, let
\widehat{S}_{ijm_{1}m_{2}}(u)
be the
(m_{1},m_{2})
-th element of
\widehat{\bf S}_{ij}(u)
. By Conditions
7
–
9
, we have that for
q=2,3,\dots
and
s=0,1,2
, there exits a positive constant
c_{0}
such that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\Big|\Big(\frac{U_{ijt}-u}{h_{j}}\Big)^{s}K_{h_{j}}(U_{ijt}-u)\Big|^{q}\bigg\}\leq\int h_{j}^{-q}K^{q}\Big(\frac{t-u}{h_{j}}\Big)\Big|\frac{t-u}{h_{j}}\Big|^{sq}f_{U}(t){\rm d}t\leq c_{0}h^{1-q}.

Then we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T_{ij}}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\Big|\Big(\frac{U_{ijt}-u}{h_{j}}\Big)^{s}K_{h_{j}}(U_{ijt}-u)\Big|^{2}\bigg\}\leq c_{0}Th^{-1}.

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T_{ij}}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\Big|\Big(\frac{U_{ijt}-u}{h_{j}}\Big)^{s}K_{h_{j}}(U_{ijt}-u)\Big|^{q}\bigg\}\leq 2^{-1}q!c_{0}Th^{-1}h^{2-q}~~~\text{for }q\geq 3.

This, together with the Bernstein inequality (see also Theorem 2.10 and Corollary 2.11 of
Boucheron
et al. (2014)
), implies that
there exists some positive constant
\tilde{c}_{3}
(independent of
u
) such that for any
\delta>0
and
u\in{\cal U}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big\{\|\widehat{\bf S}_{ij}(u)-{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{\bf S}_{ij}(u)\}\|_{\text{F}}\geq\delta\big\}\leq 8\exp\bigg(-\frac{\tilde{c}_{3}Th\delta^{2}}{1+\delta}\bigg).

(S.19)

For
m=1,2
, let
\widehat{R}_{ijm}(u)
be the
m
-th element of
\widehat{{\bf R}}_{ij}(u)
. We will then show that there exists some positive constant
\tilde{c}_{4}
(independent of
u
) such that for any
\delta>0
and
u\in{\cal U}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big\{|\widehat{R}_{ijm}(u)-{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{R}_{ijm}(u)\}|\geq\delta\big\}\leq\tilde{c}_{4}\exp\bigg(-\frac{\tilde{c}_{4}Th\delta^{2}}{1+\delta}\bigg).

(S.20)

We focus on the case of
m=1
and the proof of
m=2
follows similarly. Let

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{ij11}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T_{ij}}\sum_{t=1}^{T_{ij}}K_{h_{j}}(U_{ijt}-u)\mu_{j}(U_{ijt}),

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{ij12}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T_{ij}}\sum_{t=1}^{T_{ij}}K_{h_{j}}(U_{ijt}-u)\varsigma_{ijt},

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{ij13}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T_{ij}}\sum_{t=1}^{T_{ij}}K_{h_{j}}(U_{ijt}-u)\{Z_{ij}(U_{ijt})-Z_{ij}(u)\}.

Thus,
\widehat{R}_{ij1}(u)-{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{R}_{ij1}(u)\}
can be decomposed into

\widehat{R}_{ij1}(u)-{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{R}_{ij1}(u)\}=\widehat{R}_{ij11}(u)-{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{R}_{ij11}(u)\}+\widehat{R}_{ij12}(u)+\widehat{R}_{ij13}(u)\,.

Similar to (
S.19
),
we obtain that there exists some positive constant
\tilde{c}_{5}
(independent of
u
) such that for any
\delta>0
and
u\in{\cal U}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big\{|\widehat{R}_{ij11}(u)-{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{R}_{ij11}(u)\}|\geq\delta\big\}\leq 2\exp\bigg(-\frac{\tilde{c}_{5}Th\delta^{2}}{1+\delta}\bigg).

(S.21)

Recall that
\varsigma_{ijt}
is a sub-Guassian random variable with

\mathbb{E}\{\exp(\varsigma_{ijt}\tilde{x})\}\leq\exp(2^{-1}\tilde{c}^{2}\sigma_{j}^{2}\tilde{x}^{2})

for all
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}
. By Proposition 2.5.2 of
Vershynin
(2018)
, for any positive integer
q
,
{\mathbb{E}}(|\varsigma_{ijt}|^{q})\leq c_{0}^{q}\sigma_{j}^{q}q^{q/2}
. Then by the fact that
q^{q/2}\leq c_{0}q!/2
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\Big|K_{h_{j}}(U_{ijt}-u)\varsigma_{ijt}\Big|^{q}\bigg\}\leq c_{0}^{q}h^{1-q}q!/2.

Thus, there exists some positive constant
\tilde{c}_{6}
(independent of
u
) such that for any
\delta>0
and
u\in{\cal U}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big\{|\widehat{R}_{ij12}(u)\}|\geq\delta\big\}\leq 2\exp\bigg(-\frac{\tilde{c}_{6}Th\delta^{2}}{1+\delta}\bigg).

(S.22)

Define the event
V_{j}=\{U_{ijt},t\in[T_{ij}],i\in[n]\}
. Recall that
Z_{ij}(\cdot)
is a mean-zero sub-Gaussian process. Similar to the previous proof, by Condition
10
, we have
{\mathbb{E}}\{|Z_{ij}(U_{ijt})|^{q}|V_{j}\}\leq c_{0}^{q}q!/2
.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\Big|K_{h_{j}}(U_{ijt}-u)\{Z_{ij}(U_{ijt})-Z_{ij}(u)\}\Big|^{q}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{q-1}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\Big|K_{h_{j}}(U_{ijt}-u)Z_{ij}(U_{ijt})\Big|^{q}\bigg\}+2^{q-1}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\Big|K_{h_{j}}(U_{ijt}-u)Z_{ij}(u)\Big|^{q}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{q-1}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\Big|K_{h_{j}}(U_{ijt}-u)\Big|^{q}{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{ij}(U_{ijt})|V_{j}]\Big|^{q}\bigg\}+2^{q-1}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\Big|K_{h_{j}}(U_{ijt}-u)Z_{ij}(u)\Big|^{q}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{0}^{q}h^{1-q}q!/2.

Then we have that there exists some positive constant
\tilde{c}_{7}
(independent of
u
) such that for any
\delta>0
and
u\in{\cal U}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big\{|\widehat{R}_{ij13}(u)\}|\geq\delta\big\}\leq 2\exp\bigg(-\frac{\tilde{c}_{7}Th\delta^{2}}{1+\delta}\bigg).

This, together with (
S.21
) and (
S.22
), implies that (
S.20
) holds. By applying the same techniques used in the proofs of the local and
\ell_{2}
norm concentration inequalities in Sections A.1.2 and A.1.3 of
Guo et al. (2023)
, we can show that there exist some positive constants
c_{3},c_{4}
such that for any
\delta>0
,

{\mathbb{P}}(\|\widehat{X}_{ij}-Z_{ij}-\widetilde{X}_{ij}\|\geq\delta)\leq c_{3}\exp(-c_{4}Th\delta^{2}).

For
M>0
with
1-c_{4}M^{2}<0
, we select
\delta=\log(p\vee n)^{1/2}(Th)^{-1/2}M\leq 1
. Then

{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\frac{\max_{i\in[n],j\in[p]}\|\widehat{X}_{ij}-Z_{ij}-\widetilde{X}_{ij}\|}{\log(p\vee n)^{1/2}(Th)^{-1/2}}\leq M\bigg\}\leq c_{3}\exp\{(1-c_{4}M^{2})\log(p\vee n)\}.

We hence complete the proof of (i) in Lemma
A3
.

Recall that
\widetilde{X}_{ij}(u)={\mathbf{e}}_{0}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}[{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{{\bf S}}_{ij}(u)\}]^{-1}{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{{\bf R}}_{ij}(u)\}
. Thus,

\widetilde{X}_{ij}(u)-\mu_{j}(u)={\mathbf{e}}_{0}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}[{\mathbb{E}}\{\widehat{{\bf S}}_{ij}(u)\}]^{-1}{\mathbb{E}}[\widehat{{\bf R}}_{ij}(u)-\widehat{\bf S}_{ij}(u)\{\mu_{j}(u),0\}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}].

By applying the same techniques used in the proof of Theorem 3 in
Guo et al. (2023)
, we complete the proof of Lemma
A3
.
\hfill\Box

B.4

Inequality
1
and its proof

Inequality 1

Let
\boldsymbol{{\cal F}}=({\cal F}_{1},\ldots,{\cal F}_{p})^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}
with each
{\cal F}_{i}\in\mathbb{S}
and
\boldsymbol{{\cal G}}=({\cal G}_{1},\ldots,{\cal G}_{p})^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}
with each
{\cal G}_{i}\in\mathbb{S}
. Then
\iint\{\int\boldsymbol{{\cal F}}(u,s)^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\boldsymbol{{\cal G}}(v,s)\,{\rm d}s\}^{2}{\rm d}u{\rm d}v\leq\|\boldsymbol{{\cal F}}\|_{{\cal S},\max}^{2}\|\boldsymbol{{\cal G}}\|_{{\cal S},1}^{2}
.

Proof.
By Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\iint\bigg\{\int\boldsymbol{{\cal F}}(u,s)^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\boldsymbol{{\cal G}}(v,s)\,{\rm d}s\bigg\}^{2}{\rm d}u{\rm d}v

\displaystyle~~~~~~=\iint\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{p}\int{\cal F}_{i}(u,s){\cal G}_{i}(v,s)\,{\rm d}s\bigg\}^{2}{\rm d}u{\rm d}v

\displaystyle~~~~~~=\iint{\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}\iint{\cal F}_{i}(u,s_{1}){\cal G}_{i}(v,s_{1}){\cal F}_{j}(u,s_{2}){\cal G}_{j}(v,s_{2})\,{\rm d}s_{1}{\rm d}s_{2}}{\rm d}u{\rm d}v

\displaystyle~~~~~~=\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}\iint\bigg\{\int{\cal F}_{i}(u,s_{1}){\cal F}_{j}(u,s_{2})\,{\rm d}u\bigg\}\bigg\{\int{\cal G}_{i}(v,s_{1}){\cal G}_{j}(v,s_{2})\,{\rm d}v\bigg\}\,{\rm d}s_{1}{\rm d}s_{2}

\displaystyle~~~~~~\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}\bigg[\iint\bigg\{\int{\cal F}_{i}(u,s_{1}){\cal F}_{j}(u,s_{2})\,{\rm d}u\bigg\}^{2}\,{\rm d}s_{1}{\rm d}s_{2}\iint\bigg\{\int{\cal G}_{i}(v,s_{1}){\cal G}_{j}(v,s_{2})\,{\rm d}v\bigg\}^{2}\,{\rm d}s_{1}{\rm d}s_{2}\bigg]^{1/2}

\displaystyle~~~~~~\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}\|{\cal F}_{i}\|_{{\cal S}}\|{\cal F}_{j}\|_{{\cal S}}\|{\cal G}_{i}\|_{{\cal S}}\|{\cal G}_{j}\|_{{\cal S}}=\big(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\|{\cal F}_{i}\|_{{\cal S}}\|{\cal G}_{i}\|_{{\cal S}}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle~~~~~~\leq\big(\max_{i}\|{\cal F}_{i}\|_{{\cal S}}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\|{\cal G}_{i}\|_{{\cal S}}\big)^{2}=\|\boldsymbol{{\cal F}}\|_{{\cal S},\max}^{2}\|\boldsymbol{{\cal G}}\|_{{\cal S},1}^{2}.

Hence, the proof is complete.

\hfill\Box

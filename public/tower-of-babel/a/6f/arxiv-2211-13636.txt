Ramification current, post-critical normality and stability of holomorphic endomorphisms of ℙ k \mathbb{P}^{k}
François Berteloot and Maxence Brevard

Abstract
. In the context of holomorphic families of endomorphisms of ℙ k \mathbb{P}^{k} , we prove that stability in the sense of [ BBD18 ] is equivalent to a summability condition for the post-critical mass and to the convergence of a suitably defined ramification current. This allows us to both simplify the approach of [ BBD18 ] and better relate stability to post-critical normality.

37F44 - 37F80 - 32H50 - 32U40.

1.
Introduction and results

A
holomorphic family of degree
d\geq 2
endomorphisms on
\mathbb{P}^{k}
, parametrized by a complex manifold
M
, is a holomorphic map
f:M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}\to M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
, of the form
(\lambda,z)\mapsto(\lambda,f_{\lambda}(z))
such that, as endomorphisms on
\mathbb{P}^{k}
, the maps
f_{\lambda}
have the same algebraic degree

d\geq 2
.

In dimension
k=1
, the dynamical stability of such families has been independently studied by
Mañé-Sad-Sullivan
[
MSS83
]
and Lyubich
[
Lju83
]
.
At the heart of their theory stands a characterization of global dynamical stability by a post-critical normality condition encoding the dynamical stability of the critical set.
By considering a cover of the parameter space, one may always assume that
the critical points are marked, that is to say that the critical set

C_{f}
of
f
is given by the graphs of
2d-2
holomorphic maps
c_{j}:M\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Under this mild assumption, this characterization can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f:M\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to M\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a holomorphic family of rational functions
with marked critical points
c_{j}
and let
J_{\lambda}
denote the Julia set of
f_{\lambda}
.
Then the two following assertions are equivalent:

i)

the Julia sets
J_{\lambda}
move holomorphically with
\lambda
,

ii)

the sequences
(f^{\circ n}\circ c_{j})_{n}
are normal.

The fact that the Julia sets
J_{\lambda}
move holomorphically with
\lambda
amounts to say that there exists a holomorphic lamination

\mathscr{L}
by graphs over
M
in
M\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
whose slices
{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}:={\mathscr{L}}\cap(\{\lambda\}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})
coincide with
J_{\lambda}
for every
\lambda\in M
;
in that case the family
f
is said to be
stable
.
More generally, the
stability locus

\stab(f)
of a family
f
is the set of parameters
\lambda\in M
admitting
a neighbourhood
D
such that the restricted family
f|_{D\times\mathbb{P}}
is stable.
An important corollary of the above theorem is the density of the stability locus in the parameter space.
Another one, due to DeMarco
[
deM03
]
, is that the Lyapunov exponents
L(\lambda)
of the maximal entropy measures of
f_{\lambda}
define a plurisubharmonic function on
M
which is pluriharmonic exactly on
\stab(f)
.

When studying stability of families of endomorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{k\geq 2}
,
basic one-dimensional tools are no longer efficient, and a different approach,
mainly based on pluripotential and ergodic techniques, has recently been
designed in
[
BBD18
]
. To describe it, we first briefly recall some basic facts and definitions.
For any parameter
\lambda\in M
, denote by
\mu_{\lambda}
the equilibrium measure of
f_{\lambda}
and by
J_{\lambda}:={\rm supp}(\mu_{\lambda})
its Julia set.
The space
\mathcal{O}(M,\mathbb{P}^{k})
of holomorphic maps from
M
to
\mathbb{P}^{k}
is endowed with the topology of local uniform convergence;
which yields a complete metric space

(\mathcal{O}(M,\mathbb{P}^{k}),d_{\textrm{luc}})
on which the family
f
induces a topological dynamical system
(\mathcal{O}(M,\mathbb{P}^{k}),\mathscr{F})
:

\forall\gamma\in\mathcal{O}(M,\mathbb{P}^{k}),\forall\lambda\in M\>:\>\mathscr{F}(\gamma)(\lambda):=(f_{\lambda}\circ\gamma)(\lambda).

The following (possibly empty)
\mathscr{F}
-invariant subset of
\mathcal{O}(M,\mathbb{P}^{k})
is also of importance

\mathscr{J}:=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{O}(M,\mathbb{P}^{k})\>:\>\gamma(\lambda)\in J_{\lambda}\text{ for every }\lambda\in M\}.

A key idea is to replace a holomorphic motion of Julia sets by a holomorphic motion of
the equilibrium measures, meaning by that the existence of a holomorphic lamination
\mathscr{L}
by graphs over
M
in
M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
whose
slices
{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}:={\mathscr{L}}\cap(\{\lambda\}\times\mathbb{P}^{k})
have full
\mu_{\lambda}
measure for every
\lambda\in M
.
Such a lamination can be further required to be induced by a relatively compact subset of
\mathscr{J}

on which
\mathscr{F}
induces a
d^{k}
to
1
map and, in that case, is called an
equilibrium lamination
.
This leads to the following definition of stability.

Definition 1.2
.

A family
f
is said to be
\mu
-stable
if it admits an equilibrium lamination. The
\mu
-
stability locus
of a family
f
, denoted
\Stab(f)
, is the set of parameters
\lambda\in M
for which there exists a neighbourhood
D
in
M
such that the restricted family
f|_{D\times\mathbb{P}^{k}}
is
\mu
-stable.

Although some post-critical normality conditions played a crucial role in the work
[
BBD18
]
,
they were not directly related to stability. We aim here to both introduce a new notion of post-critical normality and show that it
naturally implies
\mu
-stability.
As we shall explain in section
2
, this allows to simplify the overall scheme of proof of
[
BBD18
]
. To this purpose, we borrow the concept of
ramification current
from the work

[
DS03
]
, see also
[
DS10
]
, of Dinh and Sibony on the equidistribution of iterated preimages
for endomorphisms on
\mathbb{P}^{k}
and polynomial-like maps,
and adapt it to the setting of holomorphic families.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
C_{f}
denote the critical set of
f
. For every integer
n\geq 0
, we denote by
R_{n,f}
the closed positive current on
M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
defined
by

R_{n,f}:=d^{-kn}(f^{\circ n})_{*}[f(C_{f})]

where
[f(C_{f})]
denotes the current of integration on the analytic subset
f(C_{f})
.
The
formal ramification current

R_{f}
of
f
is defined by

R_{f}:=\sum_{n\geq 0}R_{n,f}=\sum_{n\geq 0}d^{-kn}(f^{\circ n})_{*}[f(C_{f})].

The
convergence domain

\Omega(R_{f})
of
R_{f}
is the subset of points in
M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}

which admit a neighbourhood
U
such that the series
\sum_{n\geq 0}\|\mathds{1}_{U}R_{n,f}\|
converges.

Let us stress that
R_{f}
induces a closed positive
(1,1)
-current on its convergence domain
\Omega(R_{f})
.

We can now state our main result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
f:M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}\to M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
be a holomorphic family of endomorphisms
of degree
d\geq 2
on
\mathbb{P}^{k}
and
\pi_{M}:M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}\to M
be the canonical projection.
Then:

\Omega(R_{f})=\pi_{M}^{-1}({\Stab}(f))=\pi_{M}(\Omega(R_{f}))\times\mathbb{P}^{k}.

As we shall see in the next section, it follows from Theorem
1.4
that
\lambda_{0}\in{\Stab}(f)
if and only if

\|(f^{\circ n})_{*}[C_{f}]\|_{U\times\mathbb{P}^{k}}=O(d^{(k-1)n})
for some neighbourhood
U
of
\lambda_{0}
in
M
.
This last condition already appeared in
[
BBD18
, Proposition 3.12(3)]
and, since for every
\lambda
,

\|{(f_{\lambda}^{\circ n})}_{*}[C_{f_{\lambda}}]\|_{\mathbb{P}^{k}}\sim_{n}d^{(k-1)n}
,
it can be interpreted as a post-critical normality statement.
In dimension
k=1
, one may indeed check that it is equivalent to the second assertion of Theorem
1.1

(see
[
BB18a
, Theorem 3.9, proof of
(5)\implies(6)
]
).

Notations:

The mass of a positive current
T
on some Borel measurable set
W
is denoted
\|T\|_{W}
or
\lVert\mathds{1}_{W}T\rVert
.
The set of all holomorphic maps from
U
to
V
is denoted
{\mathscr{O}}(U,V)
.
When
V=\mathbb{C}
, we simply note
\mathscr{O}(U)
. The euclidean
ball of radius
r
and centered at point
a
in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
is denoted
B(a,r)
.
The determinant of the jacobian matrix of an endormorphism
f:\mathbb{P}^{k}\to\mathbb{P}^{k}
(respectively a polynomial map
F:\mathbb{C}^{k}\to\mathbb{C}^{k}
)
will be denoted
{\rm Jac}f
(resp.
{\rm Jac}F
). The cardinality of a finite set
A
is denoted
|A|
.

2.
The role of theorem
1.4
in the stability theory

From now on,
f
is a holomorphic family of degree
d\geq 2
endomorphisms on
\mathbb{P}^{k}
, parametrized by a complex manifold
M
of complex dimension
m
.
We recall that the equilibrium measure
\mu_{\lambda}
of
f_{\lambda}
is a Monge-Ampère mass given by

\mu_{\lambda}=(dd^{c}_{z}g(\lambda,z)+\omega_{\mathbb{P}^{k}})^{k}
,
where
g
is a Green function (see
[
DS10
, Theorem 1.16]
), which is continuous on
M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
.
We denote by
L(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{P}^{k}}\ln|{\rm Jac}f_{\lambda}(z)|\;d\mu_{\lambda}(z)

the sum of Lyapunov exponents of
(f_{\lambda},\mu_{\lambda})
.
For every integer
n
and every parameter
\lambda
, we denote by
{\mathscr{R}}_{n}(\lambda)

the set of
n
-periodic repelling points of
f_{\lambda}
which belong to the Julia set of
f_{\lambda}
. We now introduce some weaker notions of stability.

Definition 2.1
.

A family
f
is said to be
weakly stable
(resp.
asymptotically weakly stable
) if,
for every compact subset
M_{0}\Subset M
, there exists a sequence of holomorphic laminations

({\mathscr{L}}_{n})_{\mathbb{N}}
by graphs over
M
in
M_{0}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
such that

({\mathscr{L}}_{n})_{\lambda}:={\mathscr{L}}_{n}\cap(\{\lambda\}\times\mathbb{P}^{k})={\mathscr{R}}_{n}(\lambda)

(resp.
({\mathscr{L}}_{n})_{\lambda}\subset{\mathscr{R}}_{n}(\lambda)
and
|({\mathscr{L}}_{n})_{\lambda}|\sim_{n}|{\mathscr{R}}_{n}(\lambda)|
) for every
\lambda\in M_{0}
.

The following result combines those of
[
BBD18
]
with Theorem
1.4
.
It both offers a much direct proof of some results of
[
BBD18
]
and completes them with a sharper characterization of stability in terms of post-critical normality.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f:M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}\to M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
be a holomorphic family of endomorphisms of degree
d\geq 2

on
\mathbb{P}^{k}
parametrized by a simply connected complex manifold
M
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

1)

L
is pluriharmonic on
M
,

2)

\|f^{\circ n}_{*}[C_{f}]\|_{U\times\mathbb{P}^{k}}=O(d^{(k-1)n})

for every
U\Subset M
,

3)

\pi_{M}(\Omega(R_{f}))=M
,

4)

f
is
\mu
-stable,

5)

f
is asymptotically weakly stable.

Let us stress that in
[
BBD18
]
the assertions
1
) and
2
) were shown to be equivalent to weak stability
in the family of all degree
d\geq 2
holomorphic endomorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{k}
or for any family
when
k=2
, while in
[
Bia19
]
, using further techniques, Bianchi proved the equivalence of assertions

1
),
4
),
5
) and a version of
2
) in the larger setting of holomorphic families of polynomial maps of large topological degree.

We now recall some fundamental tools from
[
BBD18
]
which will also be useful here.
Our main thread is that the stability properties are encoded in the Lyapunov function
L(\lambda)
,
from which they can be extracted by mean of the two following formulas:

\boldsymbol{dd^{c}L}
-formula
:

dd^{c}L=\pi_{M*}\left((dd^{c}_{\lambda,z}g(\lambda,z)+\omega_{\mathbb{P}^{k}})^{k}\wedge[C_{f}]\right)
.

Approximation formula
:

L(\lambda)=\lim_{n}d^{-kn}\sum_{z\in{\mathscr{R}}_{n}(\lambda)}\ln|{\rm Jac}f_{\lambda}(z)|
.

The first formula has been obtained by Bassanelli and Berteloot in
[
BaBe07
]
,
it generalizes similar formulas in dimension one due to Przytycki
[
Prz85
]
and Manning
[
Man84
]
for polynomials
and DeMarco
[
deM03
]
for rational functions. The second one was proved by Berteloot, Dupont and Molino in
[
BDM08
]
and a simplified proof,
avoiding difficulties due to the possible resonances between the Lyapunov exponents, has been given by Berteloot and Dupont in
[
BD19
]
.

We shall also need the notion of
acritical equilibrium web
whose existence, as shown in
[
BBD18
, Theorem 4.1]
,
implies that of an equilibrium lamination and, in principle, is much easier to prove.
The projections
p_{\lambda}:\mathcal{O}(M,\mathbb{P}^{k})\to\mathbb{P}^{k}
are defined by
p_{\lambda}(\gamma):=\gamma(\lambda)
.

Definition 2.3
.

An
equilibrium web for
f
on
M
is a Borel probability measure on the metric space
(\mathcal{O}(M,\mathbb{P}^{k}),d_{\textrm{luc}})
such that:

(1)

\mathscr{F}_{*}\mathscr{M}=\mathscr{M}
,

(2)

\forall\lambda\in M\>:\>(p_{\lambda})_{*}\mathscr{M}=\mu_{\lambda}
,

(3)

{\rm supp}(\mathscr{M})
is compact.

Set
\mathscr{J}_{s}:=\{\gamma\in\mathscr{J}\>:\>\Gamma_{\gamma}\cap(\cup_{m\geq 0}f^{-m}(\cup_{n\geq 0}f^{n}(C_{f})))\neq\emptyset\}
.
An equilibrium web
\mathscr{M}
is said
acritical
if
\mathscr{M}(\mathscr{J}_{s})=0
.

In
[
BBD18
]
an equilibrium web was also required to be supported on
\mathscr{J}
, this is actually a consequence of the
above simpler definition. Indeed, if
\gamma_{0}\notin\mathscr{J}
then there exists
\lambda_{0}\in M
for which

\gamma_{0}(\lambda_{0})\notin\textrm{supp}\;\mu_{\lambda_{0}}
and then, taking any neighbourhood
\mathcal{V}_{0}

of
\gamma_{0}
in
{\mathscr{O}}(M,\mathbb{P}^{k})
such that
p_{\mathcal{\lambda}_{0}}(\mathcal{V}_{0})\subset\mathbb{P}^{k}\setminus\textrm{supp}\;\mu_{\lambda_{0}}
, one gets
{\mathscr{M}}(\mathcal{V}_{0})\leq{\mathscr{M}}(p_{\lambda_{0}}^{-1}(p_{\lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{V}_{0})))=(p_{\lambda_{0}*}{\mathscr{M})(p_{\mathcal{\lambda}_{0}}(\mathcal{V}_{0}))=\mu_{\lambda_{0}}(p_{\lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{V}_{0}))=0}
.

We now show that Theorem
2.2
can be obtained from the following key lemma, whose proof
will be given in the next section.
Applying Theorem
2.2
to the restricted families
f|_{\Stab\times\mathbb{P}^{k}}

then easily leads to Theorem
1.4
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\lambda_{0}\in\pi_{M}(\Omega(R_{f}))
, then there exists a neighbourhood
D_{0}
of
\lambda_{0}

in
M
such that the restricted family
f|_{D_{0}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}}
admits an acritical equilibrium web.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

1)\Leftrightarrow 2).
This follows immediately from the following estimate which is a direct consequence of the
dd^{c}L
-formula
(see
[
BBD18
, Lemma 3.13]
).
There exists a positive constant
\alpha
, only depending on
k
and
m
,
such that

\|f^{n}_{*}[C_{f}]\|_{U\times\mathbb{P}^{k}}=\alpha\|dd^{c}L\|_{U}+O(d^{(k-1)n})

for every relatively compact open subset
U
of
M
.

2)\Rightarrow 3)
is obvious.

3)\Rightarrow 4).
By Lemma
2.4
, any parameter
\lambda_{0}\in M
has a neighbourhood
D_{0}

such that the restricted family
f|_{D_{0}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}}
admits an acritical equilibrium web
\mathscr{M}
.
Using Choquet’s theory, one shows that
f|_{D_{0}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}}
also admits an acritical web which is ergodic
(see
[
BBD18
, Proposition 2.4]
).
It then follows from
[
BBD18
, Theorem 4.1]
that
f|_{D_{0}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}}
admits an equilibrium lamination;
the proof of this theorem fully exploits the ergodicity of the dynamical system
({\mathscr{J}},{\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{M}})
.
Finally, since
M
is simply connected, the family

f
itself admits an equilibrium lamination
\mathscr{L}
by an analytic continuation argument.

4)\Rightarrow 5).
This has been proved by Bianchi in the wider context of holomorphic families of polynomial-like maps
of large topological degree (see
[
Bia19
, Theorem 4.11]
).
The proof consists on a generalization to the dynamical system
({\mathscr{J}},{\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{M}})
, where
\mathscr{M}

is an ergodic acriticalequilibrium web associated to the lamination
\mathscr{L}
, of the strategy developed
by Briend and Duval
[
BrDu99
]
to recover the equidistribution of the repelling cycles from the properties of the equilibrium measure.

5)\Rightarrow 1).
For every integer
n
we have, by assumption,
a finite collection
(\gamma_{j,n})_{1\leq j\leq N_{n}}
of holomorphic maps

\gamma_{j,n}:M\to\mathbb{P}^{k}
such that
\gamma_{j,n}(\lambda)\in{\mathscr{R}}_{n}(\lambda)
for every
\lambda\in M

and
N_{n}\sim d^{kn}
. The collection is invariant by the action of
\mathscr{F}
. We thus may write

d^{-kn}\sum_{z\in{\mathscr{R}}_{n}(\lambda)}\ln|{\rm Jac}f_{\lambda}(z)|=d^{-kn}\sum_{1\leq j\leq N_{n}}\ln|{\rm Jac}f_{\lambda}(\gamma_{j,n}(\lambda)|+d^{-kn}\sum_{z\in{\mathscr{R}^{\prime}}_{n}(\lambda)}\ln|{\rm Jac}f_{\lambda}(z)|,

where
{\mathscr{R}^{\prime}}_{n}(\lambda):={\mathscr{R}}_{n}(\lambda)\setminus\cup_{1\leq j\leq N_{n}}\{\gamma_{j,n}(\lambda)\}
.

The second term in the above sum is positive
since the cycles involved in
{\mathscr{R}^{\prime}}_{n}(\lambda)
are repelling, and it is bounded from above
by
K_{\lambda}d^{-kn}|{\mathscr{R}^{\prime}}_{n}(\lambda)|\leq K_{\lambda}(1-\frac{N_{n}}{d^{kn}})

where
K_{\lambda}
is a positive constants which depends continuously on
\lambda
.
Similarly the first term is locally uniformly bounded and, moreover, defines a pluriharmonic function on
M
.
By the approximation formula, the function
L
is thus
a limit in
L^{1}_{loc}(M)
of pluriharmonic functions.

To end this section we recall the role played by Misiurewicz parameters in the study of stability.
A parameter
\lambda_{0}\in M
is called
Misiurewicz
if some repelling periodic point
of
f_{\lambda_{0}}
belongs to the post-critical set of
f_{\lambda_{0}}
and if this configuration is not stable by small perturbations
(see
[
BBD18
, Definition 1.5]
for a precise statement).
Note that Misiurewicz parameters are basic examples of parameters where, in some sense, the post-critical normality fails.

The basic result about Misiurewicz parameters is the following,
its proof combines the
dd^{c}L
-formula with an asymptotic phase-parameter transfer (see
[
BBD18
, Proposition 3.7]
).

Proposition 2.5
.

If
dd^{c}L=0
on
M
then there are no Misiurewicz parameters on
M
.

This result has been used by several authors to construct holomorphic
families of endomorphisms for which the bifurcation locus
M\setminus\Stab(f)
has non empty interior
(see
[
BT17
]
,
[
Duj17
]
,
[
Taf21
]
,
[
Bie19
]
),
or for estimating the Hausdorff dimension of slices in the bifurcation locus
(see
[
BB18
]
). Let us also stress that,
using different techniques than in
[
BBD18
]
, Bianchi generalized Proposition
2.5
to the setting of holomorphic families of
polynomial-like maps of large topological degree (see
[
Bia19
, Theorem A]
).

It has also been shown that Misiurewicz parameters are dense in the bifurcation locus
(see
[
BBD18
]
Theorem 1.6) and thus, taking Proposition
2.5
into account,
that the the bifurcation locus is the closure of the subset of Misiurewicz parameters.
This result played an important role in the approach of
[
BBD18
]
and its proof is quite involved.
The proof of Theorem
2.2
presented here avoids these difficulties.

3.
Proof of Lemma
2.4

We set
Y:=f(C_{f})
.
Take
a:=(\lambda_{0},z_{0})\in\Omega(R_{f})
and let
U=:D\times B
be a neighbourhood of
a

in
M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
such that
\sum_{n\geq 0}\|\mathds{1}_{U}R_{n,f}\|<+\infty
. Using Baire’s theorem, one sees that
z_{0}
might be slightly moved in
B
so that

a\notin\cup_{n\geq 0}f^{\circ n}(Y).

Since the problem is local, we may shrink
D
(resp.
B
) so that it is contained in a local chart of
M
(resp.
\mathbb{P}^{k}
) and therefore
assume that
D\times B\subset\mathbb{C}^{m+k}
.
We then denote by
\mathscr{D}
the set of complex lines in
\mathbb{C}^{m+k}
passing
through the point
a
and, for each
\Delta\in{\mathscr{D}}
and every
\rho>0
, we denote by
\Delta_{\rho}

the euclidean disc lying on
\Delta
, centered at
a
and of radius
\rho
. We identify
\mathscr{D}
with

\mathbb{P}^{m+k-1}
and endow it with a probability measure
\mathscr{L}
induced by the Fubiny-Study metric
on
\mathbb{P}^{m+k-1}
.

From now on, we fix
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
and set
\delta:=\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}
.
We will construct an acritical equilibrium web for

f|_{D_{0}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}}
where
D_{0}
is some neighbourhood of
\lambda_{0}
contained in
D
.
We will proceed in five steps. The first three are directly inspired by the work of Dinh and Sibony on the equidistribution of iterated preimages
(see
[
DS03
, section 3.4]
or
[
DS10
, Section 1.4]
).

Step
1
: Constructing a large set of discs
on which
f^{\circ n}
admits almost
d^{kn}
inverse branches.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exist

r>0
and
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathscr{D}}
such that:

i)

B(a,r)\cap Y=\emptyset
,

ii)

{\mathscr{L}}({\mathscr{D}}^{\prime})>1-\delta
,

iii)

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\|R_{n,f}\wedge[\Delta_{r}]\|\leq\varepsilon\;\forall\Delta\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}
,

iv)

for every integer
n
and every
\Delta\in{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}
, there exists a set
\Gamma_{r}^{n}(\Delta)
of
inverses branches of
f^{\circ n}
above
\Delta_{r}
such that
|\Gamma_{r}^{n}(\Delta)|\geq(1-\varepsilon)d^{kn}
,

v)

{\mathscr{F}}(\Gamma_{r}^{n+1}(\Delta))\subset\Gamma_{r}^{n}(\Delta)

and
\gamma(\Delta_{r})\cap({C_{f}\cup}f(C_{f}))=\emptyset
for every integer
n
and every
\gamma\in\Gamma_{r}^{n}(\Delta)
.

Proof.

Take
r>0
small enough so that the closed euclidean ball
\overline{B(a,r)}
in
\mathbb{C}^{m+k}
is contained in
U
.
We shall use the following standard fact (see
[
DS10
, Lemma 1.53]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
S
be a positive closed
(1,1)
-current on
U
. Then there exist a family

\mathscr{D}^{\prime}\subset\mathscr{D}
with
\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{D}^{\prime})>1-\delta
and a constant
A_{\delta}>0
which is independant of
S
, such that the measures
S\wedge[\Delta_{r}]
are well-defined and
of mass
\leq A_{\delta}||S||
for every
\Delta
in
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
.

Applying Lemma
3.2
for
S=\mathds{1}_{U}R_{f}
, we get
A_{\delta}>0
and a family of lines
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}\subset\mathscr{D}

satisfying ii) and such that
\mathds{1}_{U}R_{f}\wedge[\Delta_{r}]
is well defined as a closed positive current with mass less than

A_{\delta}\cdot||\mathds{1}_{U}R_{f}||
for any
\Delta\in{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}
. Removing some
\mathscr{L}
-negligible subset from
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}

allows to assume that the currents
\mathds{1}_{U}R_{n,f}\wedge[\Delta_{r}]
are well defined for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
This forces the series
\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{U}R_{n,f}\wedge[\Delta_{r}]\right\rVert
to converge
for any line
\Delta\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
. Indeed, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
we have:

\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{U}R_{n,f}\wedge[\Delta_{r}]\right\rVert&=\|\sum_{n=0}^{N}\mathds{1}_{U}R_{n,f}\wedge[\Delta_{r}]\|\\
&\leq||\mathds{1}_{U}R_{f}\wedge[\Delta_{r}]||\leq A_{\delta}\cdot||\mathds{1}_{U}R_{f}||<+\infty.\end{split}

Let us set
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(N):=\{\Delta\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}\>:\>N(\Delta)\leq N\}
, where
N(\Delta)
is the smallest positive integer for which

\sum_{n\geq N(\Delta)}\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{U}R_{n,f}\wedge[\Delta_{r}]\right\rVert\leq\varepsilon
.
As the union
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}=\cup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(N)
is increasing,
we may assume, after replacing
{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}
by
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(N)
for
N
big enough, that

(3.1)

\forall\Delta\in{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}:\>\sum_{n\geq N}\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{U}R_{n,f}\wedge[\Delta_{r}]\right\rVert\leq\varepsilon.

Since
a\notin\cup_{n\leq N}f^{\circ n}(Y)
, we may reduce
r
so that

B(a,r)\cap\cup_{n\leq N}f^{\circ n}(Y)=\emptyset.

It follows that i) is satisfied and that
R_{n,f}\wedge[\Delta_{r}]=0
for any
n\leq N
.
Combining this with (
3.1
) yields iii).

Let us establish iv). Set
\varepsilon_{n}^{\Delta}:=||\mathds{\mathds{}}{1}_{U}R_{n,f}\wedge[\Delta_{r}]||
.
Note that
d^{kn}\varepsilon_{n}^{\Delta}
is the cardinality of the intersection of
f^{n}(Y)
with
\Delta_{r}
, counting multiplicities and that, in particular,
\varepsilon_{n}^{\Delta}=0
for
n\leq N
.
Given
\Delta\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
, we shall prove by induction on
n
that
f^{\circ n}
admits at least
\nu_{n}^{\Delta}

inverse branches which do not meet
Y
on
\Delta_{r}
where

(3.2)

\nu_{n}^{\Delta}\geq(1-\sum_{i=0}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}^{\Delta})d^{kn}.

The base case is covered by i). For the induction step, assume the hypothesis for
f^{\circ n}
.
As
f
realizes an unramified covering of degree
d^{k}
,
f^{n+1}
admits at least

\left(1-\sum_{i=0}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}^{\Delta}\right)d^{k(n+1)}
inverse branches above
\Delta_{r}
.
Among them, at most
d^{k(n+1)}\varepsilon_{n+1}^{\Delta}
do intersect
Y
, which leads to the desired property for
f^{\circ(n+1)}
.
Now iii) and (
3.2
) immediately yield the announced estimate
\nu_{n}^{\Delta}\geq(1-\varepsilon)d^{kn}
.

The assertion v) directly follows from the above construction.
∎

Step
2
: Estimating the number of points in
(f^{\circ n})^{-1}(a)
which belong to a lot of inverse branches
of the form
(f^{\circ n})^{-1}(\Delta_{r})
given by step 1.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
r>0
be given by Lemma
3.1
. Set
(f^{\circ n})^{-1}(a)=:\{a_{1}^{n},\cdots,a_{l_{n}}^{n}\}
and
let
I_{n}:{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}\to{\mathscr{P}}\{1,2,\cdots,l_{n}\}
be the map which associates to any
\Delta\in{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}

the subset of
s\in\{1,2,\cdots,l_{n}\}
such that there exist an inverse branch of
f^{\circ n}
defined on
\Delta_{r}
and passing through
a_{s}^{n}
.

Set
{\mathscr{D}}_{s}^{r,n}:=\{\Delta\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}\>:\>s\in I_{n}(\Delta)\}
and

{\mathscr{S}}_{\varepsilon,r,n}:=\{1\leq s\leq l_{n}\;\colon\;{\mathscr{L}}({\mathscr{D}}_{s}^{r,n})\geq 1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon}\}
, for
1\leq s\leq l_{n}
.
Then:

i)

(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})d^{kn}\leq|{\mathscr{S}}_{\varepsilon,r,n}|\leq d^{kn}
;

ii)

there exists
i_{n}:\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}\to\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n-1}
such that

f(a_{s}^{n})=a_{i_{n}(s)}^{n-1}
and
|i_{n}^{-1}(\{s\})|\leq d^{k}
, for any
s\in\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}
;

iii)

the sequence
(d^{-kn}|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}|)_{n}

converges.

Proof.

We first establish the following estimate:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{l_{n}}{\mathscr{L}}({\mathscr{D}}_{s}^{r,n})\geq(1-\delta)(1-\varepsilon)d^{kn}.

Let
\nu
be the counting measure on
\{1,2,\dots,l_{n}\}
and set
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}:=\nu\otimes\mathscr{L}
.
Consider the following subset
Q_{n}:=\{(s,\Delta)\>:\>\{s\}\in I_{n}(\Delta)\}
of
\{1,2,\dots,l_{n}\}\times\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
.
We compute
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}(Q_{n})
with two different partitions of
Q_{n}
.
The first one is based on the value of
s\in\{1,2,\dots,l_{n}\}
:

(3.4)

\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}(Q_{n})=\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}\left(\bigcup_{s=1}^{l_{n}}\{s\}\times{\mathscr{D}}_{s}^{r,n}\right)=\sum_{s=1}^{l_{n}}\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}\left(\{s\}\times{\mathscr{D}}_{s}^{r,n}\right)=\sum_{s=1}^{l_{n}}\mathscr{L}({\mathscr{D}}_{s}^{r,n}).

The second one is based on the value of
I_{n}(\Delta)\in{\mathscr{P}}\{1,2,\cdots,l_{n}\}
:

\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}(Q_{n})=\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}\left(\bigcup_{\mathscr{S}\in\mathscr{P}\{1,2,\dots,l_{n}\}}\mathscr{S}\times I_{n}^{-1}(\{\mathscr{S}\})\right)=\sum_{\mathscr{S}\in\mathscr{P}\{1,2,\dots,l_{n}\}}\nu(\mathscr{S})\cdot\mathscr{L}(I_{n}^{-1}(\{\mathscr{S}\})).

As soon as
|\mathscr{S}|<(1-\varepsilon)d^{kn}
, we get from Lemma
3.1
iv) that
I_{n}^{-1}(\{\mathscr{S}\})=\emptyset
. Then the last equality leads to

(3.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}(Q_{n})\geq(1-\varepsilon)d^{kn}\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{D}^{\prime}),

and (
3.3
) immediately follows from (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and Lemma
3.1
ii).

We can now end the proof of the Lemma.
Since
l_{n}\leq d^{kn}
, in order to prove i), it is sufficient to prove the first inequality,
namely that
(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})d^{kn}\leq|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}|
.
One has

\begin{split}\sum_{s=1}^{l_{n}}\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{D}_{s}^{r,n})&=\sum_{s\in\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}}\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{D}_{s}^{r,n})+\sum_{s\notin\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}}\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{D}_{s}^{r,n})\\
&\leq|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}|+(l_{n}-|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}|)(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})\\
&\leq 2\sqrt{\varepsilon}|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}|+(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})d^{kn},\end{split}

which, combined with (
3.3
), gives that

2\sqrt{\varepsilon}|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}|\geq\left[(1-\delta)(1-\varepsilon)-(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})\right]d^{kn}
.
Then, by our choice of
\delta
, we get:

|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}|\geq\left[1-\frac{\varepsilon+\delta(1-\varepsilon)}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\right]d^{kn}=(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})d^{kn},

which gives i).

Let us finally justify ii) and iii).
We define the map
i_{n}
on
\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}
by
f(a_{s}^{n})=a_{i_{n}(s)}^{n-1}
. It then follows from the assertion v) of Lemma
3.1
and the very definition of
\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}
that
i_{n}(\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n})\subset\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n-1}
. Since
f
is a ramified covering of degree
d^{k}
, one has
|i_{n}^{-1}(\{s\})|\leq d^{k}
.

Since
|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}|=\sum_{s\in\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n-1}}|i_{n}^{-1}(s)|\leq d^{k}|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n-1}|
, the sequence

(d^{-kn}|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}|)_{n}
is positive non-increasing and thus converges.
∎

Step
3:
Extension of the inverse branches to a ball
B(a,r)
.

We will prove here the existence
of at least
(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})d^{kn}
inverse branches of
f^{\circ n}
on an open ball
B(a,\tau r)\subset B(a,r)
centered at
a

in
\mathbb{C}^{m+k}
. To achieve this, we will combine Lemma
3.3
with the extension theorem of Sibony-Wong
(see
[
SW80
]
or
[
DS10
, Theorem 1.54]
) which we recall below.

Theorem 3.4
.

(Sibony-Wong)

Let
\mathscr{D}^{\prime\prime}\subset\mathscr{D}
be such that
\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{D}^{\prime\prime})\geq c
for a positive constant
c
and
let
\Sigma
denote the intersection of
\mathscr{D}^{\prime\prime}
with
B(a,r)
.
Then there exists
\tau\in(0,1)
, independant from
r
and
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
, such that any holomorphic function
h
on a neighbourhood of
\Sigma
can be extended
to a holomorphic function
\tilde{h}
on
B(a,\tau r)
.
Moreover, the extended function
\tilde{h}
enjoys the following estimate:
\sup_{B(a,\tau r)}|\tilde{h}|\leq\sup_{\Sigma}|h|
.

Our precise statement is as follows; we keep the notations introduced in Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.5
.

There exists
\tau\in]0,1[
such that to every
s\in{\mathscr{S}}_{\varepsilon,r,n}
is associated an inverse branch

\gamma_{s}^{n}:B(a,\tau r)\to\mathbb{C}^{m}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
of
f^{\circ n}
such that
\gamma_{s}^{n}(a)=a_{s}^{n}
. Moreover,

f\circ\gamma_{s}^{n}=\gamma_{i_{n}(s)}^{n-1}\text{ for every }s\in\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}
.
For every
\Delta\in{\mathscr{D}}_{s}^{r,n}=\{\Delta\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}\>:\>s\in I_{n}(\Delta)\})
,
we denote by
\gamma_{s,\Delta}^{n}
the inverse branch of
f^{\circ n}
above
\Delta_{r}
such that
\gamma_{s,\Delta}^{n}(a)=a_{s}^{n}
.
Observe that the holomorphic maps
\gamma_{s,\Delta}^{n}
and
\gamma_{s,\Delta^{\prime}}^{n}

are actually defined on neighbourhoods of
\Delta_{r}
and
\Delta^{\prime}_{r}
,
and coincide on some neighbourhood of
a
, for any pair of lines
\Delta
,
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}_{s}^{r,n}
.
By analytic continuation, we can therefore consider the branch
\gamma_{s}^{n}
as defined on a neighbourhood of
\cup_{\Delta\in\mathscr{D}_{s}^{r,n}}\Delta_{r}
.

By definition of
\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}
(see Lemma
3.3
),
one has
\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{D}_{s}^{r,n})\geq 1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon}
and we may therefore apply
Theorem
3.4
with
c=1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon}
and

\mathscr{D}^{\prime\prime}=\mathscr{D}_{s}^{r,n}
to the coordinate functions of
\gamma_{s}^{n}
.
In this way, one sees that each
\gamma_{s}^{n}
extends to an inverse branch of
f^{\circ n}
defined on
B(a,\tau r)
.

When
s\in\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}
, the map
f\circ\gamma_{s}^{n}
is an inverse branch of
f^{\circ(n-1)}
which is defined on
B(a,\tau r)
and
whose value at
a
is
f(a_{s}^{n})=a_{i_{n}(s)}^{n-1}
. It must therefore coincide with the branch
\gamma_{i_{n}(s)}^{n-1}
which,
by assertion ii) of Lemma
3.3
and the above construction, does exist.
∎

The following lemma is also a consequence of the Sibony-Wong extension theorem, we shall use it in the fifth (and last) step.

Lemma 3.6
.

Fix
\rho>0
. For
u\in{\mathscr{O}}(B(a,\rho))
we set

\mathscr{D}_{u}:=\{\Delta\in{\mathscr{D}}\;\colon\;0\notin u(\Delta_{\rho})\}
.
There exists
0<\tau^{\prime}<1
such that
0\notin u(B(a,\tau^{\prime}\rho))
as soon as
{\mathscr{L}}(\mathscr{D}_{u})>\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

If
u\in{\mathscr{O}}(B(a,\rho))
and
{\mathscr{L}}(\mathscr{D}_{u})>\frac{1}{2}

then
u(a)\neq 0
and thus, by Hurwitz lemma, the function

u
does not vanish on
\Delta_{\rho}
for every
\Delta\in\overline{\mathscr{D}_{u}}
.
This shows that
\mathscr{D}_{u}
is closed and that the function

h:=\frac{1}{u}
is holomorphic on some neighbourhood of
\Sigma_{u}:={\mathscr{D}}_{u}\cap B(a,\rho)
.
By Theorem
3.4
, this function extends to
\tilde{h}\in{\mathscr{O}}(B(a,\tau^{\prime}\rho))

where
0<\tau^{\prime}<1
neither depends on
u
or
\rho
.
Since
u\tilde{h}=uh=1
on
\cup_{\Delta\in{\mathscr{D}_{u}}}\Delta_{\tau^{\prime}\rho}

which has positive Lebesgue measure, one has
u\tilde{h}=1
, and therefore
u
does not vanish on
B(a,\tau^{\prime}\rho)
.∎

Step
4:
Construction of an equilibrium web.

We will use the collection of inverse branches
(\gamma_{s}^{n})_{n\geq 1,s\in{\mathscr{S}}_{\varepsilon,r,n}}

obtained in the former step to build an equilibrium web
\mathscr{M}

for the restricted family
f\lvert_{D_{0}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}}
and some neighbourhood
D_{0}
of
\lambda_{0}
.
The equidistribution of iterated preimages towards the measures
\mu_{\lambda}
,
as well as the ergodicity of these measures, will play an important role here.

Let
D_{0}\times B_{0}
be a neighbourhood of
a
contained in
B(a,\tau r)
, where
\tau>0
is given by Lemma
3.5
. For every
z\in B_{0}
,
we define a sequence of discrete measures
(m_{n}(z))_{n}
on
\mathcal{O}(D_{0},\mathbb{P}^{k})
, and their Cesàro means
({\mathscr{M}}^{n}(z))_{n}
, by

m^{n}(z):=d^{-kn}\sum_{s\in\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}}\delta_{\gamma_{s,z}^{n}}\;\quad\textrm{and}\quad\;\mathscr{M}^{n}(z):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=1}^{n}m^{r}(z),

where
\gamma_{s}^{n}(\lambda,z)=:(\lambda,\gamma_{s,z}^{n}(\lambda))
.
If
{\rm vol}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
B_{0}
, we then set

m^{n}:=\frac{1}{{\rm vol}(B_{0})}\int_{B_{0}}m^{n}(z)\;d{\rm vol}(z)\;\textrm{and}\;\mathscr{M}^{n}:=\frac{1}{{\rm vol}(B_{0})}\int_{B_{0}}{\mathscr{M}}^{n}(z)\;d{\rm vol}(z)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=1}^{n}m^{r}.

The equilibrium web
\mathscr{M}
will be obtained as a rescaled weak limit of
({\mathscr{M}}^{n})_{n}
. In the above definitions,
the averaging on
B_{0}
is devoted to make
z
avoid the exceptional set of
f_{\lambda}
for Lebesgue-almost every
z

and every fixed
\lambda
. The Cesàro means will allow us to get the
\mathscr{F}
-invariance of
\mathscr{M}
.
The compactness of the support of
\mathscr{M}
will be obtained from the following special case of a classical result of Ueda
(see
[
Ued98
, Theorem 2.1]
).

Lemma 3.7
.

The family of all inverse branches of
f^{\circ n}
on
B(a,\tau r)
, when
n
runs over
\mathbb{N}
,
is a normal family in
\mathcal{O}(B(a,\tau r),\mathbb{P}^{k})
. In particular, for any
\alpha>0
, we may reduce
r
so that
d_{\mathbb{P}^{k}}(\gamma_{s}^{n}(z),\gamma_{s}^{n}(a))\leq\alpha
for all points
z\in B(a,\tau r)
and all branches
\gamma_{s}^{n}
given by Lemma
3.5
.

We may now state the main result of this step.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
D_{0}\times B_{0}
be any neighbourhood of
a
contained in
B(a,\tau r)
.
There exists a positive measure
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}
on
\mathcal{O}(D_{0},\mathbb{P}^{k})

such that a subsequence of
(\mathscr{M}^{n})_{n}
converges to
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}

and the probability measure
\displaystyle\mathscr{M}:=\frac{\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}}{||\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}||}

is an equilibrium web for the restricted family

f|_{D_{0}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}}
.

Proof.

By definition we have

\begin{split}||\mathscr{M}^{n}||=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=1}^{n}||m^{r}||=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=1}^{n}\frac{1}{{\rm vol}(B_{0})}\int_{B_{0}}||m^{n}(z)||d{\rm vol}(z)=d^{-kn}|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}|,\end{split}

which, by the last assertion of Lemma
3.3
, yields the existence of
\alpha\in[0,1[
such that

\lim_{n}||\mathscr{M}^{n}||=1-\alpha.

Since the family of all inverse branches of
f^{\circ n}
above
B(a,\tau r)
, when
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
is a normal family in
\mathcal{O}(B(a,\tau r),\mathbb{P}^{k})
, due to Lemma
3.7
, the family of their restrictions to
D_{0}\times\{z\}
when
z\in B_{0}
is
normal as well. In other words, there exists a compact subset
\mathcal{K}
of the space
(\mathcal{O}(B(a,\tau r),\mathbb{P}^{k}),d_{\textrm{luc}})
such that

{\rm supp}(\mathscr{M}^{n})\subset\mathcal{K}
for all integers
n
.
By Banach-Alaoglu theorem, the sequence
(\mathscr{M}^{n})_{n}
admits a cluster value
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}
with support in
\mathcal{K}
.

Let us now show that
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}
is
\mathscr{F}
-invariant.
To this purpose, we first establish the following estimate:

(3.6)

a_{n}(z):=||m^{n-1}(z)-\mathscr{F}_{*}m^{n}(z)||\leq\frac{|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n-1}|}{d^{k(n-1)}}-\frac{|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}|}{d^{kn}}=:a_{n},\;\forall z\in B_{0}.

We observe that
\mathscr{F}(\gamma_{s,z}^{n})=\gamma_{i_{n}(s),z}^{n-1}
. Indeed, by Lemma
3.5
we have, for every
\lambda\in D_{0}
:

\mathscr{F}(\gamma_{s,z}^{n})(\lambda)=f_{\lambda}(\gamma_{s}^{n}(\lambda,z))=\Pi_{\mathbb{P}^{k}}\circ f\circ\gamma_{s}^{n}(\lambda,z)=\Pi_{\mathbb{P}^{k}}\circ\gamma_{i_{n}(s)}^{n-1}(\lambda,z)=\gamma_{i_{n}(s),z}^{n-1}(\lambda),

where
\Pi_{\mathbb{P}^{k}}:M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}\to\mathbb{P}^{k}
is the canonical projection.
Hence, by partitioning
\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}
on the values of
i_{n}(s)
, where the function
i_{n}
is defined in Lemma
3.3
, one gets

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}_{*}m^{n}(z)&=d^{-kn}\sum_{s\in\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}}\mathscr{F}_{*}(\delta_{\gamma_{s,z}^{n}})=d^{-kn}\sum_{s\in\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}}\mathcal{\delta}_{\gamma_{i_{n}(s),z}^{n-1}}=d^{-kn}\sum_{s\in\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,{n-1}}}|i_{n}^{-1}(s)|\delta_{\gamma_{s,z}^{n-1}},\end{split}

and thus
m^{n-1}(z)-\mathscr{F}_{*}m^{n}(z)=d^{-kn}\sum_{s\in\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,{n-1}}}(d^{k}-|i_{n}^{-1}(s)|)\delta_{\gamma_{s,z}^{n-1}}
. Then,
as
|i_{n}^{-1}(s)|\leq d^{k}
(see Lemma
3.3
), we obtain

a_{n}(z)\leq d^{-kn}\sum_{s\in\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,{n-1}}}d^{k}-|i_{n}^{-1}(s)|=d^{-k(n-1)}|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n-1}|-d^{-kn}\sum_{s\in\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,{n-1}}}|i_{n}^{-1}(s)|,

which is the desired inequality, since
\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,{n-1}}}|i_{n}^{-1}(s)|=|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}|
.

It immediately follows from (
3.6
) that

(3.7)

\displaystyle||m^{r-1}-\mathscr{F}_{*}m^{r}||\leq\frac{1}{{\rm vol}(B_{0})}\int_{B_{0}}a_{r}(z)d{\rm vol}(z)\leq a_{r}.

Now, since

\mathscr{M}^{n}-\mathscr{F}_{*}\mathscr{M}^{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=1}^{n}m^{r}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=1}^{n}\mathscr{F}_{*}m^{r}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=2}^{n}(m^{r-1}-\mathscr{F}_{*}m^{r})+\frac{m^{n}-\mathscr{F}_{*}m^{1}}{n},

one deduces from (
3.7
) that

\begin{split}||\mathscr{M}^{n}-\mathscr{F}_{*}\mathscr{M}^{n}||&\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=2}^{n}||m^{r-1}-\mathscr{F}_{*}m^{r}||+\frac{2}{n}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=2}^{n}a_{r}+\frac{2}{n}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,1}|}{d^{k}}-\frac{|\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,r,n}|}{d^{kn}}\right)+\frac{2}{n}\leq\frac{3}{n}.\end{split}

Since
\mathscr{F}_{*}
is continuous for the weak topology,
this proves that
\mathscr{F}_{*}\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}=\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}
.

Now
\mathscr{M}:=\frac{\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}}{1-\alpha}
is a compactly supported
\mathscr{F}
-invariant probability measure
on the metric space
(\mathcal{O}(B(a,\tau r),\mathbb{P}^{k}),d_{\textrm{luc}})
,
and it remains to prove that
(p_{\lambda})_{*}(\mathscr{M})=\mu_{\lambda}
for any
\lambda\in D_{0}
.
For
(\lambda,z)\in D_{0}\times B_{0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let us set
\mu_{\lambda}^{n}(z):=d^{-kn}(f_{\lambda}^{\circ n})^{*}\delta_{z}
.
Then, by definition, we have
0\leq(p_{\lambda})_{*}(\mathscr{M}^{n}(z))\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=1}^{n}\mu_{\lambda}^{r}(z)
for every
z\in B_{0}
and therefore:

(3.8)

\displaystyle 0\leq(p_{\lambda})_{*}(\mathscr{M}^{n})\leq\frac{1}{{\rm vol}(B_{0})}\int_{B_{0}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=1}^{n}\mu_{\lambda}^{r}(z)\;d{\rm vol}(z).

As the exceptional set of
f_{\lambda}
is a proper pluripolar subset of
\mathbb{P}^{k}

[
FS95
]
,
it follows from the equidistribution theorem
[
DS10
]
that

(\mu_{\lambda}^{n}(z))_{n}
is weakly converging to
\mu_{\lambda}
for almost every
z\in B_{0}
.
Then, by Lebesgue convergence Theorem, the right hand side of (

Abstract
. For a k k -uniform hypergraph F F we consider the parameter Θ ⁡ ( F ) \Theta(F) , the minimum size of a clique cover of the edge set of F F . We derive bounds on Θ ⁡ ( F ) \Theta(F) for F F belonging to various classes of hypergraphs.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a
k
-uniform hypergraph, a
set representation
of
G
on a set
T
is a system of subsets
\{S_{v}\subseteq T:\>v\in V(G)\}
with the property that

\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}\in G\text{ if and only if }\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{k}S_{v_{i}}\neq\emptyset.

The
representation number

\Theta(G)
of
G
is the smallest cardinality of a set
T
which admits a set representation.

We define the
clique covering number

\cc(G)
of a
k
-graph
G
as the minimum integer
m
such that there exist complete graphs
C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}
satisfying
G=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}C_{i}
, i.e., every edge is covered by a clique and no clique contains an edge that is not from
G
.
It is well known that the representation number
\Theta(G)
is equal to
\cc(G)
(see e.g.
[
8
,
9
]
or Section
7
for a proof). The parameter
\Theta
has been subject of
interest for a number of researchers (see e.g.
[
3
,
1
,
2
]
). In particular it was proved in
[
1
]
that
\Theta(G)\leq c_{1}d^{2}\log n
for any graph
G
on
n
vertices with maximum degree on its complement
\Delta(\overline{G})\leq d
. On the other hand, it was proved in
[
2
]
that there are graphs
G
with
\Delta(\overline{G})\leq d
and
\Theta(G)\geq c_{2}\frac{d^{2}}{\log d}\log n
.

Here we will be mainly interested in extending these results to
k
-uniform hypergraphs. Perhaps surprisingly, this turned out to be quite nontrivial, and our results in this direction are far from being optimal for large values of
k
.

Since most of our results regarding
\Theta(G)
will be formulated in terms of restrictions on the degree of the complement
\overline{G}
, we will introduce a parameter
\vartheta(G)=\Theta(\overline{G})
allowing for simpler formulation of results. Clearly,
\vartheta(G)
is the minimum cardinality of a set
T
such that there is a system
\{S_{v}\subseteq T:\>v\in V(G)\}
of sets with

\displaystyle\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}\in G\text{ if and only if }\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{k}S_{v_{i}}=\emptyset.

Alternatively, one can view
\vartheta(G)
as the minimum cover of the edges of the complement
\overline{G}
by independent sets of
G
. In what follows we will often use the latter definition instead of the former. Moreover, we say that
G
can be
t
-represented
if there exists a system of independent sets
\{I_{j}:\>1\leq j\leq t\}
covering the edges of
\overline{G}
.

2.
Results

Let
G
be a
k
-graph. For
S\subseteq V(G)
, let

\deg_{G}(S)=|\{e\in G:\>S\subseteq e\}|

be the degree of the set
S
in
G
and

\Delta_{i}(G)=\max\limits_{|S|=i,S\subseteq V(G)}\deg_{G}(S)

be the maximum degree of an
i
-tuple of vertices of
G
. (Consequently,
\Delta_{1}(G)=\Delta(G)
is just the usual maximum degree).

We say that a
k
-graph
G
is
d
-balanced
if
\Delta_{i}(G)\leq d^{\frac{k-i}{k-1}}
for all
1\leq i\leq k
. In particular, a
d
-balanced graph has maximum degree
d
. Our first result gives almost sharp bounds on
\vartheta(G)
for the family of
d
-balanced
k
-graphs. Let

\displaystyle b(n,d,k)=\max\{\vartheta(G):\>\text{$G$ is a $d$-balanced $k$-graph}\}

be the maximum value of
\vartheta(G)
over all
d
-balanced graphs with
n
vertices.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
n,d,k\geq 2
, there exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depending only on
k
such that

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}d^{\frac{k}{k-1}}}{\log d}\log\left(\frac{n^{k-1}}{d}\right)\leq b(n,d,k)\leq c_{2}d^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\log n

holds.

For the general family of bounded degree hypergraphs, we obtain the following bounds. Let
\vartheta(n,d,k)
be the maximum value of
\vartheta(G)
, where
G
is a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
\Delta(G)=\Delta_{1}(G)\leq d
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
n,d,k\geq 3
, there exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depending only on
k
such that

\displaystyle c_{1}\frac{d^{\frac{k}{k-1}}}{\log d}\log\left(\frac{n^{k-1}}{d}\right)\leq\vartheta(n,d,k)\leq c_{2}d^{\frac{k}{2}}\log n.

Moreover, if
k
is even the lower bound can be improved to

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}d^{2}}{\log d}\log\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)\leq\vartheta(n,d,k)

Note that since every
2
-graph
G
with
\Delta(G)\leq d
is
d
-balanced, then the bounds of
\vartheta(n,d,k)
provided in Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
are almost sharp for
k=2,3,4
and
d\ll n
. Finally, we address similar questions for some other classes of hypergraphs. For example, denoting
\vartheta_{s}(n,k)
as the maximum value of
\vartheta(G)
, where
G
runs over all
k
-uniform simple hypergraphs, we show the following:

Theorem 2.3
.

For
k\geq 3
and
n\geq n_{0}(k)
, there exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depending only on
k
such that

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}n^{\frac{k}{k-1}}}{(\log n)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}\leq\vartheta_{s}(n,k)\leq c_{2}n^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\log n

holds.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
3
we discuss the upper bounds of Theorem
2.1
and
2.2
, while in Section
4
we provide their respective lower bounds. Section
5
is devoted to the problem of representing special hypergraphs.

3.
Upper Bounds of Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2

We start with the upper bound of Theorem
2.1
. We are going to show that if
G
is a
d
-balanced
k
-graph, then
\vartheta(G)\leq 2^{k}k^{k+1}d^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\log n
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
n,d,k\geq 2
and
c=2^{k+2}k^{k+1}
. If
G
is a
d
-balanced
k
-graph on
n
vertices, then
\vartheta(G)\leq cd^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\log n
.

Proof.

We recall that
\vartheta(G)
is the minimum size of a cover of
\overline{G}
by independent sets of
G
. Consider
t=cd^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\log n
random subsets
W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{t}
of
V:=V(G)
, where each
W_{j}
is chosen by selecting vertices from
V
independently and uniformly with probability
p=\frac{1}{2kd^{1/(k-1)}}
. Our aim is to cover
\overline{G}
by modifying the sets
W_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq t
. For each
W_{j}
, construct an independent set as follows: For every edge
e
in
G[W_{j}]
delete all of its vertices from
W_{j}
. Let
I_{j}
be the set obtained after deleting vertices from every edge in
G[W_{j}]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle I_{j}=W_{j}\setminus\bigcup_{e\in G[W_{j}]}e.

Clearly,
I_{j}
is an independent set. We claim that
\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{t}\}
is a covering of
\overline{G}
with positive probability.

For a
k
-tuple
\overline{e}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\in\overline{G}
, we examine the probability that
\overline{e}\subseteq I_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq t
. To do that, note that the only reason why a vertex
x_{i}\in\overline{e}
could be deleted is if there exists an edge
e\in G[W_{j}]
such that
x_{i}\in e\cap\overline{e}
. That is,
\overline{e}\subseteq I_{j}
if
\overline{e}\subseteq W_{j}
and
e\cap\overline{e}=\emptyset
for all
e\in G[W_{j}]
. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\overline{e}\subseteq I_{j})\geq p^{k}\left(1-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}\Delta_{i}(G)p^{k-i}\binom{k}{i}\right)\geq p^{k}\left(1-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}\frac{\binom{k}{k-i}}{(2k)^{k-i}}\right)>\frac{p^{k}}{4}.

Consequently, since all
I_{j}
were chosen independently, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{t}(\overline{e}\not\subseteq I_{j})\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{t}\mathbb{P}\left(\overline{e}\not\subseteq I_{j}\right)<\left(1-\frac{p^{k}}{4}\right)^{t}\leq\exp\left(-\frac{p^{k}t}{4}\right)=\frac{1}{n^{k}}.

Since there are at most
\binom{n}{k}
non-edges in
\overline{G}
to cover, the probability that one remains uncovered is at most
\binom{n}{k}\frac{1}{n^{k}}<1
. Consequently, with positive probability all edges of
\overline{G}
are covered by
\bigcup_{j=1}^{t}I_{j}
.
∎

The proof of the upper bound of Theorem
2.2
follows similar lines.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
n,d,k\geq 3
,
\delta=\frac{1}{(k-1)2^{k+2}}
and
c=\frac{2k}{\delta^{k}}
. If
G
is a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
\Delta(G)\leq d
, then
\vartheta(G)\leq cd^{\frac{k}{2}}\log n
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we want to cover all the edges of
\overline{G}
with independent sets of
G
. To this end, we consider
t=cd^{\frac{k}{2}}\log n
random subsets
W_{1},\ldots,W_{t}
of
V
, where each
W_{j}
is chosen by selecting vertices from
V
independently and uniformly with probability
p=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{d}}
. Note that the sets
W_{1},\ldots,W_{t}
are not necessarily independent. Our aim is to modify those sets
W_{j}
to independent sets
I_{j}
such that
\bigcup_{j=1}^{t}I_{j}
covers every
k
-tuple in
\overline{G}
.

To this end, we consider the auxiliary
(k-1)
-graph
H
given by
V(H)=V
and

\displaystyle H=\left\{S\in V^{(k-1)}:\>\deg_{G}(S)\geq\sqrt{d}\right\},

i.e.,
H
is the
(k-1)
-graph where the edges are the
(k-1)
-tuples of large degree in
G
. The next proposition gives an upper bound on the maximum degree of
H
.

Proposition 3.3
.

\Delta(H)\leq(k-1)\sqrt{d}
.

Proof.

Assume the contrary and let
v
be a vertex with
\deg_{H}(v)>(k-1)\sqrt{d}
. Then by a counting argument we obtain that

\displaystyle\deg_{G}(v)=\frac{\sum_{v\in S\in V^{(k-1)}}\deg_{G}(S)}{k-1}\geq\frac{\deg_{H}(v)\sqrt{d}}{k-1}>d,

which contradicts the fact that
\Delta(G)\leq d
.
∎

Now we describe how to construct the independent sets
I_{j}
. For each
1\leq j\leq t
, we sequentially remove vertices from
W_{j}
satisfying the following cleaning strategy:

Cleaning Strategy

Let
X=W_{j}
. While there is an edge in
G[X]
perform one of the two operations:

Operation (i):

If there is an edge
g\in G[X]
containing precisely one
(k-1)
-tuple
S\in H
, then we remove the vertex not in
S
from
X
, i.e., the vertex in the singleton set
g\setminus S
.

Operation (ii):

Otherwise, if all the edges in
G[X]
contain either zero or more than one
(k-1)
-tuple from
H
, then we select an arbitrary edge
g\in G[X]
and delete an arbitrary vertex from it.

Set
I_{j}
to be the resulting
X
obtained after the process is over.

Clearly, the set
I_{j}
does not contain any edge from
G
and thus it is independent. It remains to show that with positive probability any edge from the complement
\overline{G}
is covered by
\bigcup_{j=1}^{t}I_{j}
.

For a
k
-tuple
\overline{e}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\in\overline{G}
, we want to estimate the probability that
\overline{e}\subseteq I_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq t
. The following lemma gives us a lower bound on the probability.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
\overline{e}\in\overline{G}
and
1\leq j\leq t
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\overline{e}\subseteq I_{j})\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{d}}\right)^{k}.

Proof.

In what follows, we will say that the set
X

crosses
the pair
(Y,Z)
if
X
has non empty intersection with both
Y
and
Z
. Set
\overline{e}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}
. We start by defining some auxiliary events.

For a set
S\subseteq\overline{e}
with
S\notin H
and
|S|\leq k-1
, let

\displaystyle A_{S}=\left\{\nexists R\subseteq W_{j}-\overline{e},\,R\cup S\in G\right\}

(1)

be the event that there is no edge
g\in G
crossing the pair
(\overline{e},W_{j}-\overline{e})
with
\overline{e}\cap g=S
. Similarly, for a set
S\subseteq\overline{e}
with
|S|\leq k-2
, let

\displaystyle B_{S}=\left\{\nexists R\subseteq W_{j}-\overline{e},\,R\cup S\in H\right\}

(2)

be the event stating that there is no
(k-1)
-tuple
h\in H
crossing the pair
(\overline{e},W_{j}-\overline{e})
with
\overline{e}\cap h=S
.

The next claim gives a lower bound on the probability of
\overline{e}
be covered by
I_{j}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

If
\overline{e}\in\overline{G}
is a
k
-tuple satisfying

(a)

For every
S\subseteq\overline{e}
with
|S|\leq k-1
and
S\notin H
, the event
A_{S}
holds,

(b)

For every
S\subseteq\overline{e}
with
|S|\leq k-2
, the event
B_{S}
holds,

(c)

\overline{e}\subseteq W_{j}
,

then
\overline{e}\subseteq I_{j}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\overline{e}\not\subseteq I_{j}
. This means that a vertex of
\overline{e}
was deleted while performing operations (i) and (ii), i.e., in our cleaning process we deleted a vertex from an edge
g\in G
with
g\cap\overline{e}\neq\emptyset
. Since (a) holds, we obtain that
g\cap\overline{e}
is an edge of
H
. Moreover, by (b), we have that
g\cap\overline{e}
is the only
(k-1)
-tuple of
g
in
H
. Therefore, we deleted a vertex of
g
in the operation (i). By the definition of operation (i), we obtain that the deleted vertex was in
g-\overline{e}
, which contradicts our assumption that the deleted vertex belongs to
\overline{e}
.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
3.5
, one can estimate the probability of
\overline{e}\subseteq I_{j}
by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\overline{e}\subseteq I_{j})\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\{\overline{e}\subseteq W_{j}\}\wedge\bigwedge_{S\in\binom{\overline{e}}{k-1}\setminus H}A_{S}\wedge\bigwedge_{S\in\binom{\overline{e}}{k-2}}B_{S}\right)=p^{k}\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge_{S\in\binom{\overline{e}}{k-1}\setminus H}A_{S}\wedge\bigwedge_{S\in\binom{\overline{e}}{k-2}}B_{S}\right).

(3)

To compute the probability of the intersection of events
A_{S}
and
B_{S}
, we will estimate the probability of the complementary events
A_{S}^{c}
and
B_{S}^{c}
. Set
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2^{k+2}}
and recall that
\delta=\frac{1}{(k-1)2^{k+2}}
. We split the computations into cases depending on the size of
S
:

Case 1:

|S|\leq k-2
.

By using the definitions of
A_{S}
in (
1
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{S}^{c})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigvee_{R\cup S\in G}\{R\subseteq W_{j}-\overline{e}\}\right)\leq\sum_{R\cup S\in G}\mathbb{P}(R\subseteq W_{j}-\overline{e})

\displaystyle\leq p^{k-|S|}\deg_{G}(S)\leq p^{2}d=\delta^{2}\leq\varepsilon.

(4)

Proposition
3.3
and the definition of
B_{S}
in (
2
) give us that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(B_{S}^{c})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigvee_{R\cup S\in H}\{R\subseteq W_{j}-\overline{e}\}\right)\leq\sum_{R\cup S\in H}\mathbb{P}(R\subseteq W_{j}-\overline{e})

\displaystyle\leq p^{k-1-|S|}\deg_{H}(S)\leq p\Delta(H)=(k-1)\delta=\varepsilon.

(5)

Case 2:

|S|=k-1
and
S\notin H
.

By the definition of
A_{S}
in (
1
) and by the fact that
S\notin H
implies that
\deg_{G}(S)\leq\sqrt{d}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{S}^{c})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigvee_{R\cup S\in G}\{R\subseteq W_{j}-\overline{e}\}\right)\leq\sum_{R\cup S\in G}\mathbb{P}(R\subseteq W_{j}-\overline{e})

\displaystyle\leq p^{k-|S|}\deg_{G}(S)\leq p\sqrt{d}=\delta\leq\varepsilon.

(6)

Finally, since there exist at most
2^{k}
choices of
S\subseteq\overline{e}
, by putting (
3
), (
4
), (
5
) and (
6
) together we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\overline{e}\subseteq I_{j})\geq p^{k}\left(1-\sum_{S\in\binom{\overline{e}}{k-1}\setminus H}\mathbb{P}(A_{S}^{c})-\sum_{S\in\binom{\overline{e}}{k-2}}\mathbb{P}(B_{S}^{c})\right)\geq p^{k}(1-2^{k+1}\varepsilon)\geq\frac{p^{k}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{d}}\right)^{k}.

∎

Now the remaining part of the proof of Proposition
3.2
is straightforward. By Lemma
3.4
, the probability that
\overline{e}
is not covered by
\bigcup_{j=1}^{t}I_{j}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{t}(\overline{e}\not\subseteq I_{j})\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{t}\mathbb{P}\left(\overline{e}\not\subseteq I_{j}\right)<\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{d}}\right)^{k}\right)^{t}\leq\exp\left(-\frac{t}{2}\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{d}}\right)^{k}\right)=\frac{1}{n^{k}}.

Since there are at most
\binom{n}{k}
non-edges in
\overline{G}
to cover, the probability that one remains uncovered is at most
\binom{n}{k}\frac{1}{n^{k}}<1
. Consequently, with positive probability all edges of
\overline{G}
are covered by
\bigcup_{j=1}^{t}I_{j}
.
∎

4.
Lower Bounds of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2

In this section, we prove the lower bound of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
. The next proposition shows the lower bound of Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
when
k
is odd.

Proposition 4.1
.

For
n,d,k\geq 2
, there exists a
d
-balanced
k
-graph
G
such that

\displaystyle\vartheta(G)\geq c\frac{d^{\frac{k}{k-1}}}{\log d}\log\left(\frac{n^{k-1}}{d}\right)

for a positive constant
c
depending only on
k
.

The proof of Proposition
4.1
follows from a counting argument using the next two auxiliary lemmas. Recall that a
t
-representation of a
k
-graph
H
is a system
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{j}\subseteq[n]:\>1\leq j\leq t\}
covering the edges of the complement
\overline{H}
. For integers
n,\alpha,t\geq 1
, define
f(n,\alpha,t)
as the number of all possible
t
-representations of graphs
H
with independence number
\alpha(H)\leq\alpha
(If some graph
H
does not admit a
t
-representation, then its contribution to the sum is zero).

Lemma 4.2
.

For
\alpha,t\geq 1
and sufficiently large
n
,
f(n,\alpha,t)\leq\left(2\binom{n}{\alpha}\right)^{t}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{t}\}
be a set system covering
\overline{H}
for some
H
. Since
I_{j}
is an independent set of
H
and
\alpha(H)\leq\alpha
, we obtain that
|I_{j}|\leq\alpha
. Hence, the number of ways to choose distinct systems
\mathcal{I}
is bounded by

\displaystyle f(n,\alpha,t)\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}\binom{n}{i}\right)^{t}\leq\left(2\binom{n}{\alpha}\right)^{t}.

∎

Given integers
m,d,k
, we define
\mathcal{F}_{m,d}^{(k)}
to be a family of
k
-partite
k
-graphs
F
on a fixed set of vertices
V=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
with
|V_{i}|=m
for every
1\leq i\leq k
satisfying:

i)

All edges
e\in F
are transversal to the partition
V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{k}
, i.e.,
|e\cap V_{i}|=1
for all
1\leq i\leq k
.

ii)

F
is linear.

iii)

\Delta(F)\leq d
.

Note that since
F
is linear, the maximum degree satisfies
\Delta(F)\leq d\leq|V_{i}|=m
. The following lemma gives a lower bound on the size of
\mathcal{F}_{m,d}^{(k)}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
k,d,m
be integers with
k\geq 3
and
m\geq m_{0}(k)
. Then,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}_{m,d}^{(k)}\right|\geq\left(\frac{m^{k-1}}{d}\right)^{\frac{md}{2k^{2}}}

holds.

Proof.

Set
s=\frac{dm}{2k^{2}}
. We will construct a graph
F
from
\mathcal{F}_{m,d}^{(k)}
by successively choosing edges
e_{1},\ldots,e_{s}
transversal to the partition
V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{k}
. Suppose that for
\ell<s
we already constructed
F_{\ell}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
satisfying:

i)

e_{i}
is transversal to
V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{k}
for all
1\leq i\leq\ell
.

ii)

F_{\ell}
is linear.

iii)

\Delta(F_{\ell})\leq d
.

Now we intend to add an edge
e_{\ell+1}
to construct the next graph
F_{\ell+1}=F_{\ell}\cup\{e_{\ell+1}\}
. For each
1\leq i\leq k
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V_{i}}\deg_{F_{\ell}}(x)=\ell<s=\frac{dm}{2k^{2}}.

Consequently, if
X_{i}=\{x\in V_{i}:\>\deg_{F_{\ell}}(x)=d\}
, then we have

\displaystyle|X_{i}|\leq\frac{m}{2k^{2}}.

(7)

To choose an edge
e_{\ell+1}
we need to satisfy
|e_{i}\cap e_{\ell+1}|\leq 1
for every
1\leq i\leq\ell
. Given
e_{i}
, there are at most
\binom{k}{2}m^{k-2}

k
-tuples
f\in V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{k}
such that
|e_{i}\cap f|\geq 2
. Therefore, since
d\leq m
, there are at most

\displaystyle\ell\binom{k}{2}m^{k-2}<s\binom{k}{2}m^{k-2}\leq\frac{dm^{k-1}}{4}\leq\frac{m^{k}}{4}

k
-tuples of
V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{k}
that violate condition ii) of
\mathcal{F}_{m,d}^{(k)}
. Since the addition of any
k
-tuple containing an element of
X_{i}

5.
Special hypergraphs

While for
k
-graphs of bounded degree with
k
large we still have a significant gap between lower and upper bounds, for Steiner systems one can obtain more precise bounds. Given
1<\ell<k<n
, a
partial Steiner
(n,k,\ell)
-system
is a
k
-graph
S\subseteq[n]^{(k)}
such that every
\ell
-tuple
P\in[n]^{(\ell)}
is contained in at most one edge of
S
. An
(n,k,\ell)
-system in which every
\ell
-tuple
P\in[n]^{(\ell)}
is contained in precisely one edge of
S
is called a
full Steiner
(n,k,\ell)
-system
. While it is easy to check the existence of partial
(n,k,\ell)
-system, the existence of full
(n,k,\ell)
-system for all admissible parameters
1<\ell<k
and
n\geq n_{0}(\ell,k)
was established only recently in
[
4
]
and
[
6
]
. In this section we are going to provide bounds for the representation number of partial and full Steiner
(n,k,\ell)
-systems for
\ell=2
.

For
1<\ell<k<n
, we define

\displaystyle s(n,k,\ell)=\max\{\vartheta(S):\>S\text{ is a partial $(n,k,\ell)$-system}\}

as the maximum value of
\vartheta(S)
, where
S
runs over all partial
(n,k,\ell)
-systems. Similarly, we define
s^{*}(n,k,\ell)
as the maximum value of
\vartheta(S)
, where
S
runs over all full
(n,k,\ell)
-systems.

Theorem 5.1
.

Given
k>1
, there exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depending only on
k
such that the following holds:

i)

For
n
sufficiently large,

\displaystyle c_{1}\frac{n^{\frac{k}{k-1}}}{(\log n)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}\leq s(n,k,2)\leq c_{2}n^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\log n

ii)

For infinitely many
n
,

\displaystyle c_{1}\frac{n^{\frac{k}{k-1}}}{(\log n)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}\leq s^{*}(n,k,2)\leq c_{2}n^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\log n

Note that Theorem
5.1
.i) is just a reformulation of Theorem
2.3
.
Before we give a proof of the theorem, we are going to introduce two useful results.

Let
K_{k}^{(\ell)}(m)
be an
m
-blowup of the complete
\ell
-graph with
k
vertices, i.e.,
K_{k}^{(\ell)}(m)
is the graph with vertex set
V=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
, where
|V_{i}|=m
for
1\leq i\leq k
, and is such that for every
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{\ell}\leq k
and
x_{i_{j}}\in V_{i_{j}}
the
\ell
-tuple
\{x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{\ell}}\}
is an edge. The
K_{k}^{(\ell)}
-decomposition of
K_{k}^{(\ell)}(m)
is a system of pairwise edge disjoint copies of
K_{k}^{(\ell)}
covering all edges of
K_{k}^{(\ell)}(m)
. Equivalently, one can view each such decomposition as a
k
-partite
k
-graph
F
with vertex partition
V(F)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
, where
|V_{i}|=m
for
1\leq i\leq k
, and such that for every
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{\ell}\leq k
and vertices
x_{i_{j}}\in V_{i_{j}}
there exists exactly one edge
f\in F
with
\{x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{\ell}}\}\subseteq f
.

In
[
7
]
, Keevash obtained strong bounds on the number of such decompositions.

Theorem 5.2

(Theorem 2.8,
[
7
]
)
.

The number of distinct
K_{k}^{(\ell)}
-decompositions of
K_{k}^{(\ell)}(m)
is given by

\displaystyle\left(\left(e^{1-\binom{k}{\ell}}+o(1)\right)m^{k-\ell}\right)^{m^{\ell}}

for sufficiently large
m
.

We will also need the following result on existence of
(n,k,\ell)
-systems with bounded independence number.

Theorem 5.3

(
[
5
]
)
.

For
k>\ell\geq 2
, there is a positive constant
c
depending only on
k
such that the following holds. If
m=q^{2}
for
q
sufficiently large prime power, then there exists a full Steiner
(m,k,2)
-system
S
with
\alpha(S)\leq cm^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}(\log m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}
.

Proof of Theorem

.

To establish the upper bound we first note that any full or partial

-system

is a

-balanced

. Also note that for

\displaystyle\deg(x)\leq\sum_{y\in[n]\setminus\{x\}}\deg(\{x,y\})\leq n-1,

and by the upper bound of Theorem

, there exists positive constant

\displaystyle\vartheta(S)\leq c_{2}\Delta_{1}(S)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\log n\leq c_{2}n^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\log n.

Next we prove the lower bounds of
s(n,k,2)
and

. We may assume without changing the asymptotics that
m=n/k=q^{2}
for
q
sufficiently large prime. By Theorem

there exists a full
(m,k,2)
-system
S
with
\alpha(S)\leq cm^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}(\log m)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}
. Consider
k

of

and let
V_{i}=V(S_{i})
for

. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the family of
k
-partite

-graphs

with vertex set

such that for every

and

such that

\mathcal{F}
is in one-to-one correspondence with the
K_{k}^{(2)}
-decompositions of

5.2
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|=\left(\left(e^{1-\binom{k}{2}}+o(1)\right)m^{k-2}\right)^{m^{2}},

(10)

for sufficiently large

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the family of graphs defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\{F\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}:\>F\in\mathcal{F}\}.

We observe that every
H\in\mathcal{H}
is a full
(n,k,2)
-system. Let
H=F\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}
for some

.

Set

and let

and by our assumption that every graph

\displaystyle 2^{t\alpha\log n}\geq\left(2\binom{n}{\alpha}\right)^{t}\geq|\mathcal{H}|=|\mathcal{F}|=\left(\left(e^{1-\binom{k}{2}}+o(1)\right)m^{k-2}\right)^{m^{2}}\geq 2^{c^{\prime}n^{2}\log n}

6.
Concluding Remarks

6.1.
Representation of sparse hypergraphs of high uniformity

In Sections
3
and
4
we determined upper and lower bounds for the family of
k
-graphs on
n
vertices of bounded degree
d
. In particular, if we define

\displaystyle f_{k}(d)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\vartheta(n,d,k)}{\log n},

then we established almost sharp bounds for
k=2,3,4
, i.e.,
\Omega\left(d^{2}/\log d\right)\leq f_{2}(d)\leq O(d^{2})
,
\Omega\left(d^{3/2}/\log d\right)\leq f_{3}(d)\leq O(d^{3/2})
and
\Omega\left(d^{2}/\log d\right)\leq f_{4}(d)\leq O(d^{2})
.

Unfortunately, for
k\geq 5
, the bounds we have at the moment are worse. More precisely,

\displaystyle\Omega\left(\frac{d^{2}}{\log d}\right)\leq f_{k}(d)\leq O\left(d^{k/2}\right)

for
k
even and

\displaystyle\Omega\left(\frac{d^{\frac{k}{k-1}}}{\log d}\right)\leq f_{k}(d)\leq O\left(d^{k/2}\right)

for
k
odd. It would be interesting to close the gap between the bounds for
f_{k}(d)
.

6.2.
Representation of
k
-partite
k
-graphs

Let
\mathcal{K}(n,d,k)
be the family of
k
-partite
k
-graphs
G
on
n
vertices with bounded maximum degree
\Delta(G)\leq d
. As in the previous case, it would be interesting to find good bounds on
\vartheta(G)
for
G\in\mathcal{K}(n,d,k)
. Let

\displaystyle g_{k}(d)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\max\{\vartheta(G):\>G\in\mathcal{K}(n,d,k)\}}{\log n}.

A similar counting argument as the one used in Section
4
yields that
g_{k}(d)=\Omega(d)
. For
k=2
, similar techniques as in Section
3
give the upper bound which is linear in
d
, i.e.,
g_{2}(d)=O(d)
. This leads us to believe that perhaps the same could be true for
k\geq 3
.

Conjecture 6.1
.

g_{k}(d)=O(d)
for
k\geq 3
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
F
be a
k
-graph for
k\geq 2
. Then
\Theta(F)=\cc(F)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{t}\}
be a system of cliques covering all edges of
F
. To each vertex
v\in V(F)
assign the set
S_{v}
of all cliques containing
v
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is a covering of the edges, if
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}\in F
, then there exists a clique
C_{i}
containing
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
. Thus
C_{i}\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}S_{v_{i}}
and hence
\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}S_{v_{i}}\neq\emptyset
. On the other hand, by construction,
S_{v_{j}}=\{C_{i}\in\mathcal{C}:\>v_{j}\in C_{i}\}
and therefore the set
\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}S_{v_{i}}
contains all cliques containing
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
. Consequently, if
\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{k}S_{v_{j}}=\emptyset
, then there is no clique containing
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
, which implies that
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}\notin F
. Hence, we proved that
\mathcal{C}
is a set representation of
F
and we obtain that

\displaystyle\Theta(F)\leq\text{cc}(F).

To prove the opposite inequality, let
V(F)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and let
t
be the an integer such that
F
admits a set representation
\{S_{v_{i}}\subseteq[t]:\>1\leq i\leq n\}
. For each
s\in[t]
, we define the set
C(s)=\{v_{i}:\>s\in S_{v_{i}}\}
. Since
s
belongs to the intersection of any
k
sets of the form
S_{v}
for
v\in C(s)
, we obtain that
F[C(s)]
is a clique. We claim that
\mathcal{F}=\left\{F[C(1)],\ldots,F[C(s)]\right\}
is a clique covering of
F
. Indeed, if
\{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}}\}\in F
is an edge of
F
, then
\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}S_{v_{i_{j}}}\neq\emptyset
. Let
s\in\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}S_{v_{i_{j}}}
. Then clearly
\{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}}\}\in F[C(s)]
. Now if
\{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}}\}\notin F
, then
\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}S_{v_{i_{j}}}=\emptyset
and consequently there is no
s
such that
\{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}}\}\subseteq C(s)
. Hence,
\mathcal{F}
is a clique covering and
\cc(F)\leq\Theta(F)
follows.
∎

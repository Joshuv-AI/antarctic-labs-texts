Abstract
. Let G G be a connected simply connected noncompact classical simple Lie group of Hermitian type. Then G G has unitary highest weight representations. The proof of the classification of unitary highest weight representations of G G given by Enright, Howe and Wallach is based on the Dirac inequality of Parthasarathy, Jantzen’s formula and Howe’s theory of dual pairs where one group in the pair is compact. In this paper we focus on the Dirac inequality which can be used to prove the classification in a more direct way.

Let

be the universal enveloping algebra of

\mathfrak{p}
and

with respect to the Killing form

and that it is

–module, and

is the spin module for

D^{2}=-(\text{Cas}_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes 1+\|\rho\|^{2})+(\text{Cas}_{\mathfrak{k}_{\Delta}}+\|\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}^{2}\|),

where

is a half sum of the compact positive roots. There are many applications of the Dirac operators in representation theory (see

[

The Dirac inequality is a very useful necessary condition for unitarity. More precisely, if a
(\mathfrak{g},K)
–module is unitary, than

. By the formula for

of

\|\tau+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}\|^{2}\geq\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}.

In

As we already said, it is difficult to determine the

Let

In Lemma

(w_{1}\mu-w_{2}\nu)^{+}

In the computations needed to prove the Dirac inequality, we will repeatedly use the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mu
and
\nu
be two
n
-tuples with strictly decreasing coordinates and let
\rho
be as in Table
1
.

(1) Suppose there are
u,v
,
1\leq u<v\leq n
, such that

\mu_{u}=\nu_{u},\dots,\mu_{v-1}=\nu_{v-1};\ \mu_{v}<\nu_{v}.

Let
\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}
be obtained from
\mu,\nu
by moving the
v
-th coordinate to the
u
-th place and shifting the coordinates in between to the right, i.e.,

\mu^{\prime}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{u-1},\mu_{v},\mu_{u},\dots,\mu_{v-1},\mu_{v+1},\dots,\mu_{n})

and likewise for
\nu^{\prime}
. Then

(2.3)

\|\mu+\rho\|^{2}-\|\nu+\rho\|^{2}>\|\mu^{\prime}+\rho\|^{2}-\|\nu^{\prime}+\rho\|^{2}.

(2) Suppose there are
u,v
,
1\leq u<v\leq n
, such that

\mu_{u}>\nu_{u};\ \mu_{u+1}=\nu_{u+1},\dots,\mu_{v}=\nu_{v}.

Let
\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}
be obtained from
\mu,\nu
by moving the
u
-th coordinate to the
v
-th place and shifting the coordinates in between to the left, i.e.,

\mu^{\prime}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{u-1},\mu_{u+1},\dots,\mu_{v},\mu_{u},\mu_{v+1},\dots,\mu_{n})

and likewise for
\nu^{\prime}
. Then

(2.4)

\|\mu+\rho\|^{2}-\|\nu+\rho\|^{2}>\|\mu^{\prime}+\rho\|^{2}-\|\nu^{\prime}+\rho\|^{2}.

(3) Let
\mu
be a
n
-tuple, such that for some
s,t\geq 1
and for some
u
between 1 and
n-s-t
,

\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{u},\underbrace{x+1}_{s},\underbrace{x}_{t},\mu_{u+s+t+1},\dots,\mu_{n}).

Let

\mu^{\prime}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{u},\underbrace{x}_{t},\underbrace{x+1}_{s},\mu_{u+s+t+1},\dots,\mu_{n}).

Then

\|\mu+\rho\|^{2}>\|\mu^{\prime}+\rho\|^{2}

Proof.

(1) The difference of the two sides of (
2.3
) is

[(\mu_{v}+\rho_{v})^{2}-(\nu_{v}+\rho_{v})^{2}]-[(\mu_{v}+\rho_{u})^{2}-(\nu_{v}+\rho_{u})^{2}]\\
=(\mu_{v}-\nu_{v})(\mu_{v}+\nu_{v}+2\rho_{v})-(\mu_{v}-\nu_{v})(\mu_{v}+\nu_{v}+2\rho_{u})\\
=(\mu_{v}-\nu_{v})(2\rho_{v}-2\rho_{u})=2(\nu_{v}-\mu_{v})(\rho_{u}-\rho_{v}).

Since
\nu_{v}>\mu_{v}
by assumption, and since
\rho_{u}>\rho_{v}
, the claim follows.

The proof of (2) is analogous to the proof of (1).

(3) We prove the required inequality by successively switching pairs
x+1,x
as follows. Let

\mu^{\prime\prime}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{u},x,\underbrace{x+1}_{s-1},\underbrace{x}_{t-1},x+1,\mu_{u+s+t+1},\dots,\mu_{n}).

Then we see, factoring the differences of squares, that

\|\mu+\rho\|^{2}-\|\mu^{\prime\prime}+\rho\|^{2}\\
=(x+1+\rho_{u+1})^{2}-(x+\rho_{u+1})^{2}+(x+\rho_{u+s+t})^{2}-(x+1+\rho_{u+s+t})^{2}\\
=(2x+1+2\rho_{u+1})-(2x+1+2\rho_{u+s+t})=2(\rho_{u+1}-\rho_{u+s+t})>0.

Now we continue with the next pair until we reach
\mu^{\prime}
. The claim follows.

3.
Dirac inequalities

3.1.
Dirac inequality for
\mathfrak{sp}(2n,\mathbb{R})

The basic Schmid
\mathfrak{k}
-submodules of
S(\mathfrak{p}^{-})
have lowest weights
-s_{i}
, where

s_{i}=(\underbrace{2,\dots,2}_{i},0,\dots,0),\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

The highest weight
(\mathfrak{g},K)
–modules have highest weight of the form
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
, where
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ i>j
.

In this case
\rho=(n,n-1,\ldots,2,1).
The basic necessary condition for unitarity is the Dirac inequality

(3.1)

\|(\lambda-s_{1})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}.

To understand this inequality better, let
q\leq r
be integers in
[1,n]
such that

(3.2)

\lambda=(\underbrace{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{1}}_{q},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-1,\dots,\lambda_{1}-1}_{r-q},\lambda_{r+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}),

with
\lambda_{1}-2\geq\lambda_{r+1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
.
Then

(\lambda-s_{1})^{+}=(\underbrace{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{1}}_{q-1},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-1,\dots,\lambda_{1}-1}_{r-q},\lambda_{1}-2,\lambda_{r+1},\dots,\lambda_{n})=\lambda-(\varepsilon_{q}+\varepsilon_{r}).

The inequality (
3.1
) now becomes equivalent to

\|\lambda+\rho-\gamma\|^{2}\geq\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}
, or to

2\langle\lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{q}+\varepsilon_{r}\rangle\leq\|\gamma\|^{2},

where
\gamma=\varepsilon_{q}+\varepsilon_{r}
.
If
q\neq r
, then
\lambda_{q}=\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{r}=\lambda_{1}-1
and
\|\gamma\|^{2}=2
, and the inequality becomes

(3.3)

\lambda_{1}\leq-n+\frac{r+q}{2}.

If
q=r
, then
\lambda_{q}=\lambda_{r}=\lambda_{1}
and
\|\gamma\|^{2}=4
, and the inequality is again (
3.3
).

Now we are going to see in which cases the Dirac inequality holds for
s_{i},\ i\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
.
We have

\displaystyle(\lambda-s_{i})^{+}=(\underbrace{\lambda_{1}}_{q-i},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-1}_{i},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-1}_{r-q-i},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-2}_{i},\lambda_{r+1},\dots,\lambda_{n})

\displaystyle\lambda=(\underbrace{\lambda_{1}}_{q-i},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}}_{i},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-1}_{r-q-i},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-1}_{i},\lambda_{r+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}),

so

(3.4)

\|(\lambda-s_{i})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2},

is equivalent to non-negativity of

\sum_{u=q-i+1}^{q}\left[(\lambda_{1}-1+\rho_{u})^{2}-(\lambda_{1}+\rho_{u})^{2}\right]+\sum_{v=r-i+1}^{r}\left[(\lambda_{1}-2+\rho_{v})^{2}-(\lambda_{1}-1+\rho_{v})^{2}\right],

where
\rho_{u}=n-u+1
,
\rho_{v}=n-v+1
.
Factoring the differences of squares this expression becomes

-\sum_{u=q-i+1}^{q}(2\lambda_{1}-1+2\rho_{u})-\sum_{v=r-i+1}^{r}(2\lambda_{1}-3+2\rho_{v})=\\
-i(2\lambda_{1}-1)-2[(n-q+i)+\dots+(n-q+1)]-i(2\lambda_{1}-3)-2[(n-r+i)+\dots+(n-r+1)]=\\
-4i\lambda_{1}+4i-2i(n-q)-2i(n-r)-4(i+\dots+1)=\\
-2i(2\lambda_{1}+2n-2-q-r)-2i(i+1).

Dividing by
(-2i)
, we see that (
3.4
) is equivalent to

2\lambda_{1}+2n-2-q-r+i+1\leq 0,

or

(3.5)

\lambda_{1}\leq-n+\frac{r+q-i+1}{2}.

Moreover, it is clear from the above argument that (
3.4
) holds strictly if and only if (
3.5
) holds strictly.
Note also that for
i=1
, (
3.5
) is exactly our basic inequality (
3.3
), while for
i=q
we get

\lambda_{1}\leq-n+\frac{r+1}{2}.

Let

\displaystyle(\lambda-s^{\prime})^{+}=(\underbrace{\lambda_{1}}_{q-j},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-1}_{r-q},\lambda_{1}-2b_{j},\dots,\lambda_{1}-2b_{k+1},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-2b_{1}+2}_{k},\lambda_{r+1},\dots,\lambda_{n})^{+},

where the first

By Lemma

(3.10)

\|\eta+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|\eta^{\prime}+\rho\|^{2},

where

\displaystyle\eta=(\underbrace{\lambda_{1}}_{q-j},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-1}_{r-q},\lambda_{1}-2b_{j},\dots,\lambda_{1}-2b_{k+1},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-2b_{1}}_{k},\lambda_{r+1},\dots,\lambda_{n});

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}=(\underbrace{\lambda_{1}}_{q-j},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-1}_{r-q},\lambda_{1}-2b_{j},\dots,\lambda_{1}-2b_{k+1},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-2b_{1}+2}_{k},\lambda_{r+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}).

In more detail, to see that (
3.9
) follows from (
3.10
),
we first use Lemma
2.2
(3) to move the coordinates
\lambda_{1}-2b_{1}
of
(\lambda-s)^{+}
to the left past those of
\lambda_{r+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
that are equal to
\lambda_{1}-2b_{1}+1
. Now the coordinates
\lambda_{1}-2b_{1}
of
(\lambda-s)^{+}
are positioned exactly above the coordinates
\lambda_{1}-2b_{1}+2
of
(\lambda-s^{\prime})^{+}
, and we can use Lemma

(1) to move these coordinates simultaneously to the left of those of
\lambda_{r+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
that are
\geq\lambda_{1}-2b_{1}+2
.

To prove (
3.10
), we compute factoring the differences of squares,

\|\eta+\rho\|^{2}-\|\eta^{\prime}+\rho\|^{2}=\sum_{j=r-k+1}^{r}[(\lambda_{1}-2b_{1}+\rho_{j})^{2}-(\lambda_{1}-2b_{1}+2+\rho_{j})^{2}]\\
=\sum_{j=r-k+1}^{r}(-2)(2\lambda_{1}-4b_{1}+2+2\rho_{j})\\
=-2k(2\lambda_{1}-4b_{1}+2)-4[(n-r+k)+\dots+(n-r+1)]\\
=-2k(2\lambda_{1}-4b_{1}+2+2n-2r+k+1).

This expression is positive if and only if

(3.11)

2\lambda_{1}+2n-4b_{1}-2r+k+3<0.

Since by assumption

\lambda_{1}<-n+\frac{r+q-i+1}{2},

and since
b_{1}\geq 2
, we see that (
3.11
) will follow if we prove

r+q-i+1-8-2r+k+3<0,

or

-r+q-i+k-4<0.

The last inequality is however obvious since
q\leq r
and
k\leq i
.

So we have proved (
3.10
), and as explained above, this finishes the proof of Theorem
3.6
(1).

We now prove Theorem
3.6
(2). If we take
i=q
in the already proved Theorem
3.6
(1), we see that (
3.4
) holds strictly for all
s
having at most
q

Assume now that
q<r
, and that

(3.12)

s=(2b_{1},\dots,2b_{i},0,\dots,0),\qquad b_{i}\geq 1,\quad q<i\leq r.

Let

and let
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda-s_{q})^{+}
, where

is the

th basic Schmid module. We claim that

(3.13)

\|(\lambda-s)^{+}+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|(\lambda^{\prime}-s^{\prime})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}

and that

(3.14)

\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}<\|\lambda^{\prime}+\rho\|^{2}.

If we prove these two inequalities, then it follows that

(3.15)

\|(\lambda-s)^{+}+\rho\|^{2}-\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}>\|(\lambda^{\prime}-s^{\prime})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}-\|\lambda^{\prime}+\rho\|^{2},

so the strict inequality (
3.7
) for
\lambda

3.4

and

.

Since

the analogue
r^{\prime}
of

for
\lambda^{\prime}

. Moreover,

\lambda^{\prime}_{1}=\lambda_{1}-1<\lambda_{1}<-n+\frac{r+1}{2}\leq-n+\frac{r^{\prime}+1}{2}.

Furthermore, the analogue
q^{\prime}
of
q
for
\lambda^{\prime}
satisfies

, and the number of nonzero coordinates of
s^{\prime}
is
\leq i\leq r^{\prime}
.
Also, the sum of coordinates of

, and as we keep reducing as above, the number of nonzero coordinates will also become smaller. So we can use induction and keep reducing

until we come to the situation

(2) for

3.12

To prove (

as follows. Let

\mu=\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda-s_{q})^{+},\qquad\nu=s^{\prime}=(s-s_{q})^{+}.

and this is exactly (
3.13

To prove (

), we compute

\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}-\|\lambda^{\prime}+\rho\|^{2}=\\
\sum_{j=1}^{q}(\lambda_{1}+\rho_{j})^{2}+\sum_{j=q+1}^{r}(\lambda_{1}-1+\rho_{j})^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{r-q}(\lambda_{1}-1+\rho_{j})^{2}-\sum_{j=r-q+1}^{r}(\lambda_{1}-2+\rho_{j})^{2}\\
=q\lambda_{1}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{q}(2\lambda_{1}\rho_{j}+\rho_{j}^{2})+(r-q)(\lambda_{1}-1)^{2}+\sum_{j=q+1}^{r}[2(\lambda_{1}-1)\rho_{j}+\rho_{j}^{2}]\\
-(r-q)(\lambda_{1}-1)^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{r-q}[2(\lambda_{1}-1)\rho_{j}+\rho_{j}^{2}]-q(\lambda_{1}-2)^{2}-\sum_{j=r-q+1}^{r}[2(\lambda_{1}-2)\rho_{j}+\rho_{j}^{2}]\\
=q[\lambda_{1}^{2}-(\lambda_{1}-2)^{2}]-2\sum_{j=q+1}^{r}\rho_{j}+2\sum_{j=1}^{r-q}\rho_{j}+4\sum_{j=r-q+1}^{r}\rho_{j}\\
=q(4\lambda_{1}-4)-2\sum_{j=q+1}^{r}\rho_{j}+2\sum_{j=1}^{r}\rho_{j}+2\sum_{j=r-q+1}^{r}\rho_{j}\\
=4q(\lambda_{1}-1)+2\sum_{j=1}^{q}\rho_{j}+2\sum_{j=r-q+1}^{r}\rho_{j}\\
=4q(\lambda_{1}-1)+[2q(n-q)+2(q+\dots+1)]+[2q(n-r)+2(q+\dots+1)]\\
=2q[(2\lambda_{1}-2)+(n-q)+(n-r)+(q+1)]=2q(2\lambda_{1}+2n-r-1).

Since
\lambda_{1}<-n+\frac{r+1}{2}
, the last expression is clearly
<0
, and this implies (
3.14
).
So we have proved Theorem
3.6
(2) for
s
as in (
3.12
).

Finally, suppose that

and that

(3.16)

s=(2b_{1},\dots,2b_{i},0,\dots,0),\qquad b_{i}\geq 1,\quad i>r.

Let

and let

. We claim that

\|(\lambda-s)^{+}+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|(\lambda^{\prime}-s^{\prime})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}

and that

These two equations imply that

\|(\lambda-s)^{+}+\rho\|^{2}-\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|(\lambda^{\prime}-s^{\prime})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}-\|\lambda^{\prime}+\rho\|^{2}.

Moreover, since
\lambda^{\prime}
has the same

as
\lambda
, and also
\lambda^{\prime}_{1}=\lambda_{1}
, we have
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}<-n+\frac{r^{\prime}+1}{2}
.
Now we can use induction and keep decreasing the last nonzero coordinate of

until we come to the situation

. In this case, we already proved the strict Dirac inequality. So it suffices to prove (
3.17
) and (
3.18
).

To prove (
3.17
), we use Lemma
2.1
.
Namely, we set
\mu=\lambda^{\prime}
and

, and choose

such that

. Then Lemma

for

,

\|(\lambda-2\varepsilon_{i}-s^{\prime})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|(\lambda^{\prime}-s^{\prime})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}.

This is equivalent to (

To prove (

So (

\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}\leq\|\lambda+\rho-(\varepsilon_{j}+\varepsilon_{k})\|^{2}=\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}-2\langle\lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{j}+\varepsilon_{k}\rangle+\|\varepsilon_{j}+\varepsilon_{k}\|^{2},

Since
\lambda_{j}
and

are both
\leq\lambda_{1}-2
, and since

3.2.
Dirac inequality for
\mathfrak{so}^{*}(2n)

The basic Schmid
\mathfrak{k}
-submodules of
S(\mathfrak{p}^{-})
have lowest weights
-s_{i}
, where

s_{i}=(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{2i},0,\dots,0),\qquad i=1,\dots,[n/2].

Moreover, all irreducible
\mathfrak{k}
-submodules of
S(\mathfrak{p}^{-})
have lowest weights
-s
, where

(3.19)

s=(b_{1},b_{1},b_{2},b_{2},\dots,b_{j},b_{j},0,\dots,0)

for some
j
,
1\leq j\leq[n/2]
, and some positive integers
b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\dots\geq b_{j}
.

The highest weight
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules have highest weights of the form

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n}),\qquad\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n},\qquad\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{Z},\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

In this case
\rho=(n-1,n-2,\ldots,1,0).

The basic necessary condition for unitarity is, as before, the Dirac inequality

(3.20)

\|(\lambda-s_{1})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}.

To make this inequality more precise, we as before write

(\lambda-s_{1})^{+}=\lambda-\gamma.

Then (
3.20
) becomes

\|\lambda-\gamma+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2},

and since
\|\gamma\|^{2}=2
, this is equivalent to

\langle\lambda+\rho,\gamma\rangle\leq 1.

There are two basic cases:

Case
\mathbf{1}
:
\mathbf{\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}}
.
Let
q\in[2,n]
be such that
\lambda_{2}=\dots=\lambda_{q}
and, in case
q<n
,
\lambda_{q}>\lambda_{q+1}
. Then
\gamma=\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{q}
, and since
\lambda_{q}=\lambda_{2}
, the basic inequality becomes

(3.21)

\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\leq-2n+q+2.

Case
\mathbb{2}
:
\mathbb{\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}}
.
Let
p\in[2,n]
be such that
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\dots=\lambda_{p}
and, in case
p<n
,
\lambda_{p}>\lambda_{p+1}
. Then
\gamma=\varepsilon_{p-1}+\varepsilon_{p}
, and since
\lambda_{p-1}=\lambda_{p}=\lambda_{1}
, the basic inequality becomes

(3.22)

\lambda_{1}\leq-n+p.

Theorem 3.23
.

Let
\lambda
be as in Case 1, and suppose that (
3.21
) holds strictly. Let
s\neq 0
be as in (
3.19
). Then

\|(\lambda-s)^{+}+\rho\|^{2}>\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}.

To prove the theorem, assume first that
2j\leq q
. Let
k\leq j
be the largest integer such that
b_{1}=\dots=b_{k}
. Let

s^{\prime}=(\underbrace{b_{1}-1,\dots,b_{1}-1}_{2k},b_{k+1},b_{k+1},\dots,b_{j},b_{j},0,\dots,0).

We claim that

\|(\lambda-s)^{+}+\rho\|^{2}>\|(\lambda-s^{\prime})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}.

The claim implies the theorem for
2j\leq q
by induction on
b_{1}+\dots+b_{j}
.

To prove the claim, we first note that
(\lambda-s)^{+}
and
(\lambda-s^{\prime})^{+}
both contain coordinates

\underbrace{\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{2}}_{q-2j},\lambda_{2}-b_{j},\dots,\lambda_{2}-b_{k+1},\lambda_{q+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}.

The remaining coordinates of
(\lambda-s)^{+}
are

\lambda_{1}-b_{1},\underbrace{\lambda_{2}-b_{1},\dots,\lambda_{2}-b_{1}}_{2k-1},

while the remaining coordinates of
(\lambda-s^{\prime})^{+}
are

\lambda_{1}-b_{1}+1,\underbrace{\lambda_{2}-b_{1}+1,\dots,\lambda_{2}-b_{1}+1}_{2k-1}.

Using Lemma
2.2
(1), we can move the equal coordinates to the right. We use that to move
\lambda_{q+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
all the way to the right, and to move those of

\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2}-b_{j},\dots,\lambda_{2}-b_{k+1}
that are left of
\lambda_{1}-b_{1}
to the right of
\lambda_{1}-b_{1}
. Note that

\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2}-b_{j},\dots,\lambda_{2}-b_{k+1}
are left of
\lambda_{2}-b_{1}
or
\lambda_{2}-b_{1}+1
and we leave them in this position.

Thus we conclude that it is enough to prove that

(3.24)

\|\mu+\rho\|^{2}>\|\nu+\rho\|^{2},

where

\displaystyle\mu=(\lambda_{1}-b_{1},\underbrace{\lambda_{2}}_{q-2j},\lambda_{2}-b_{j},\dots,\lambda_{2}-b_{k+1},\underbrace{\lambda_{2}-b_{1}}_{2k-1},\lambda_{q+1},\dots,\lambda_{n});

\displaystyle\nu=(\lambda_{1}-b_{1}+1,\underbrace{\lambda_{2}}_{q-2j},\lambda_{2}-b_{j},\dots,\lambda_{2}-b_{k+1},\underbrace{\lambda_{2}-b_{1}+1}_{2k-1},\lambda_{q+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}).

Proving (
3.24
) is equivalent to proving that the expression

[(\lambda_{1}-b_{1}+\rho_{1})^{2}-(\lambda_{1}-b_{1}+1+\rho_{1})^{2}]+[(\lambda_{2}-b_{1}+\rho_{q-2k+2})^{2}-(\lambda_{2}-b_{1}+1+\rho_{q-2k+2})^{2}]+\\
\dots+[(\lambda_{2}-b_{1}+\rho_{q})^{2}-(\lambda_{2}-b_{1}+1+\rho_{q})^{2}]

is positive. Factoring each difference of squares, we see this is equivalent to the expression

(2\lambda_{1}-2b_{1}+1+2\rho_{1})+(2\lambda_{2}-2b_{1}+1+2\rho_{q-2k+2})+\\
\dots+(2\lambda_{2}-2b_{1}+1+2\rho_{q})

being negative. Dividing by two and simplifying we see that we should prove

(3.25)

\lambda_{1}+(2k-1)\lambda_{2}-2kb_{1}+k+\rho_{1}+\rho_{q-2k+2}+\dots+\rho_{q}<0.

We compute

\rho_{q-2k+2}+\dots+\rho_{q}=(n-q+2k-2)+\dots+(n-q)=\\
(2k-1)(n-q)+1+2+\dots+(2k-2)=(2k-1)(n-q)+(k-1)(2k-1).

On the other hand, since (
3.21
) holds strictly for
\lambda
and since
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}
, we see that

\lambda_{1}+(2k-1)\lambda_{2}<k(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})<k(-2n+q+2).

Thus we see that to prove (
3.25
) it is enough to prove that

k(-2n+q+2)-2kb_{1}+k+n-1+(2k-1)(n-q)+(k-1)(2k-1)\leq 0.

Simplifying and taking into account that
b_{1}\geq 1
, we see that the last inequality follows if we prove

(q-2k)(-k+1)\leq 0,

but this is obvious since
2k\leq 2j\leq q
and since
k\geq 1
.

It remains to prove the theorem when
2j>q
. In that case we set

\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda-s_{j})^{+};\qquad s^{\prime}=s-s_{j}.

We are going to prove

(3.26)

\|(\lambda-s)^{+}+\rho\|^{2}-\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}>\|(\lambda^{\prime}-s^{\prime})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}-\|\lambda^{\prime}+\rho\|^{2}.

This implies that the statement of the theorem holds for
\lambda
and
s
if it holds for
\lambda^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime}
.
On the other hand, since
\lambda^{\prime}
starts with coordinates

\lambda_{1}-1,\underbrace{\lambda_{2}-1,\dots,\lambda_{2}-1}_{q-1},

we see that
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}<\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}^{\prime}<\lambda_{2}
and
q^{\prime}\geq q
, so (
3.21
) holds strictly for
\lambda^{\prime}
. Now if
2j^{\prime}\leq q^{\prime}
we already proved the theorem for
\lambda^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime}
, and if
2j^{\prime}>q^{\prime}
we note that
s^{\prime}
is shorter than
s
and so we can assume the theorem holds for
\lambda^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime}
by induction.

To prove (
3.26
), we first note that Lemma
2.1
immediately implies

\|(\lambda-s)^{+}+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|(\lambda^{\prime}-s^{\prime})^{+}+\rho\|^{2},

by setting
\mu=\lambda^{\prime}
and
\nu=s^{\prime}
, and picking

such that

.

\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}<\|\lambda^{\prime}+\rho\|^{2}.

The difference

(\lambda_{i}+\rho_{i})^{2}-(\lambda_{i}-1+\rho_{i})^{2}=2\lambda_{i}-1+2\rho_{i},

where

3.21

On the other hand,

So

\sum_{i=1}^{q}(2\lambda_{i}-1+2\rho_{i})<(-2nq+q^{2}+2q)-q+(2qn-q^{2}-q)=0.

We now turn to Case 2, i.e.,

\lambda=(\underbrace{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{1}}_{p},\lambda_{p+1},\dots,\lambda_{n})

. Besides the basic inequality (

), we also examine when

\langle\lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{p-2i+1}+\dots+\varepsilon_{p}\rangle=2i\lambda_{1}+2i(n-p)+(1+\dots+(2i-1))=2i(\lambda_{1}+n-p+i-\frac{1}{2}).

\lambda_{1}\leq-n+p-i+1.

In particular, for

Theorem 3.31
.

Let
\lambda
be in Case 2, i.e.,

\lambda=(\underbrace{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{1}}_{p},\lambda_{p+1},\dots,\lambda_{n})

(2)

\lambda_{1}<-n+p-\left[\frac{p}{2}\right]+1=-n+\left[\frac{p+1}{2}\right]+1,

then (

.

Proof.

(1) Let

be an integer such that

\lambda_{1}<-n+p-i+1,

) holds strictly for
\lambda
and

.

Let

. It is enough to prove

\|(\lambda-s)^{+}+\rho\|^{2}>\|(\lambda-s^{\prime})^{+}+\rho\|^{2};

the statement then follows by induction on

. (If

, then

, and (

We first note that
(\lambda-s)^{+}
contains coordinates

\underbrace{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{1}}_{p-2j},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-b_{i},\dots,\lambda_{1}-b_{k+1}}_{2j-2k},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-b_{1},\dots,\lambda_{1}-b_{1}}_{2k}

in that order, and then also
\lambda_{p+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
, which may be interlaced with these coordinates.
Similarly,
(\lambda-s^{\prime})^{+}
contains coordinates

\underbrace{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{1}}_{p-2j},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-b_{i},\dots,\lambda_{1}-b_{k+1}}_{2j-2k},\underbrace{\lambda_{1}-b_{1}+1,\dots,\lambda_{1}-b_{1}+1}_{2k}

in that order, and then also
\lambda_{p+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
, which may be interlaced with these coordinates.

Using Lemma
2.2
(1), we may assume that
\lambda_{p+1},\dots\lambda_{n}
are all the way to the right in both
(\lambda-s)^{+}
and
(\lambda-s^{\prime})^{+}
. Thus it suffices to show that

\sum_{r=p-2k+1}^{p}\left[(\lambda_{1}-b_{1}+\rho_{r})^{2}-(\lambda_{1}-b_{1}+1+\rho_{r})^{2}\right]>0.

By factoring differences of squares, this is equivalent to

(3.36)

(2) If

3.3.

\mathfrak{su}(p,q)
,

The basic necessary condition for unitarity is, as before, the Dirac inequality

\|(\lambda-s_{1})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}.

To understand this inequality better, let

\|\lambda+\rho-\gamma\|^{2}\geq\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}

\langle\lambda+\rho,\varepsilon_{p^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{n-q^{\prime}+1}\rangle\leq 1

\lambda_{p^{\prime}}=\lambda_{1}

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n}\leq-n+p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}.

As in the other cases,
we start by examining the condition on

3.42

3.43

\langle\lambda+\rho,t_{i}\rangle\leq i.

We note that

\lambda_{1}

k>\max(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
, and hence the proof of Theorem
3.47
is completed.
∎

3.4.
Dirac inequality for
\mathfrak{so}(2,2n-2)

The basic Schmid
\mathfrak{k}
-submodules of
S(\mathfrak{p}^{-})
have lowest weights
-s_{1}
or
-s_{2}
, where

s_{1}=(1,1,0,\ldots 0),\ s_{2}=(2,0,0,\ldots 0).

Moreover, all irreducible
\mathfrak{k}
-submodules of
S(\mathfrak{p}^{-})
have lowest weights
-s_{a,b}
, where

s_{a,b}=(2b+a,a,0,\ldots 0).

The highest weight
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules have highest weights of the form

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
is
\mathfrak{k}
-dominant if

\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{3}\geq\cdots\lambda_{n-1}\geq|\lambda_{n}|.

where components
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
satisfy

\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
for
2\leq i,j\leq n.

In this case
\rho=(n-1,n-2,\ldots,0)
.

The basic necessary condition for unitarity is, as before, the Dirac inequality

(3.72)

\|(\lambda-s_{1})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}.

The basic Dirac inequality for a Schmid module
s
is

(3.73)

\|(\lambda-s)^{+}+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}

This is equivalent to

(3.74)

2\langle\gamma\,|\,\lambda+\rho\rangle\leq\|\gamma\|^{2}

where
\gamma
is defined by
(\lambda-s_{a,b})^{+}=\lambda-\gamma.

Lemma 3.75
.

The basic Dirac inequality for
s=s_{1}
is given by

(3.76)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 0

\displaystyle\text{ for }\lambda=(\lambda_{1},0,\ldots,0)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 3/2-n

\displaystyle\text{ for }\lambda=(\lambda_{1},1/2,\ldots,\pm 1/2)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2+p-2n

\displaystyle\text{ for }\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{p},\ldots,\lambda_{n})

\displaystyle\text{ where }1\leq\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{p}>\lambda_{p+1}\text{ and }2\leq p\leq n.

Proof.

Case 1:

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},0,\ldots,0)

In this case we have

\displaystyle(\lambda-s_{1})^{+}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}-1,-1,0,\ldots,0)^{+}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}-1,1,0,\ldots,0)

\displaystyle=\lambda-(\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2})

which shows that
\gamma=\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}
and (
3.73
) reduces to

\lambda_{1}+n-1-(n-2)\leq 1

which is equivalent to
\lambda_{1}\leq 0.

Case 2:

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},1/2,\ldots,\pm 1/2)

In this case we have

\displaystyle(\lambda-s_{1})^{+}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}-1,-1/2,1/2,\ldots,1/2,\pm 1/2)^{+}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}-1,1/2,1/2,\ldots,1/2,\mp 1/2)

\displaystyle=\lambda-(\epsilon_{1}\pm\epsilon_{n})

Plugging
\gamma=\epsilon_{1}\pm\epsilon_{n}
into (
3.73
) we obtain
\lambda_{1}+n-1+1/2\leq 1
which gives

(3.77)

\lambda_{1}\leq 3/2-n.

Case 3:

1\leq\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{p}>\lambda_{p+1}

The basic Dirac inequality (
3.73
) for
s=s_{1}
has
\gamma=\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{p}
and

\displaystyle 2(\lambda_{1}+n-1+\lambda_{p}+n-p)

\displaystyle\leq 2

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2+p-2n

∎

We will refer to the first case as to the
scalar
case, the second case is the
spinor
case and the remaining one is the
general
case. In the scalar and spinor case we can actually prove Dirac inequalities directly.

Theorem 3.78

(Scalar case)
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},0,\ldots,0)
such that
\lambda_{1}<2-n
. Then the Dirac inequality (
3.73
) holds for any Schmid module
s
.

Proof.

The Dirac inequality for the second basic Schmid
s_{2}=2\epsilon_{1}
yields
\lambda_{1}\leq 2-n.

Now we have

\displaystyle(\lambda-s_{a,b})^{+}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}-2b-a,-a,0,\ldots,0)^{+}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}-2b-a,a,0,\ldots,0)

\displaystyle=\lambda-[(2b+a)\epsilon_{1}-a\epsilon_{2}]

which we will plug into the Dirac inequality (
3.73
)

\displaystyle 2[(2b+a)(\lambda_{1}+n-1)-a(n-2)]

\displaystyle\leq(2b+a)^{2}+a^{2}

\displaystyle(2b+a)(\lambda_{1}+n-1)-a(n-2)

\displaystyle\leq 2b^{2}+2ab+a^{2}

Using
\lambda_{1}\leq 2-n
we see that it is sufficient to prove that

\displaystyle(2b+a)(2-n+n-1)-a(n-2)

\displaystyle\leq 2b^{2}+2ab+a^{2}

\displaystyle 2b+(3-n)a

\displaystyle\leq 2b^{2}+2ab+a^{2}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq 2b(b-1)+2ab+a(a+n-3).

Since
a,b\geq 0
and
n\geq 4
we are done.
∎

Theorem 3.79

(Spinor case)
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},1/2,\ldots,\pm 1/2)
such that
\lambda_{1}\leq 3/2-n.
Then the Dirac inequality (
3.73
) holds for any Schmid module
s
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle(\lambda-s_{a,b})^{+}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}-2b-a,1/2-a,1/2,\ldots,\pm 1/2)^{+}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}-2b-a,a-1/2,1/2,\ldots,\mp 1/2)

\displaystyle=\lambda-[(2b+a)\epsilon_{1}-(a-1)\epsilon_{2}\pm\epsilon_{n}]

for
a\geq 1.

Dirac inequality for the case
a=0
reads

\displaystyle 2[2b(\lambda_{1}+n-1)]

\displaystyle\leq 4b^{2}

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+n-1

\displaystyle\leq b

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle\leq(b+1)-n

which is satisfied (strictly) whenever
b\geq 1
due to (
3.76
). In the general case we get

2[(2b+a)(\lambda_{1}+n-1)-(a-1)(1/2+n-2)+1/2]\leq(2b+a)^{2}+(a-1)^{2}+1

and using (
3.76
) it is sufficient to show

\displaystyle 2[(2b+a)(3/2-n+n-1)-(a-1)(1/2+n-2)+1/2]

\displaystyle\leq(2b+a)^{2}+(a-1)^{2}+1.

This is in turn equivalent to showing nonnegativity of

\displaystyle a^{2}+2ab+an-3a+2b^{2}-b-n+2

\displaystyle\geq 0

\displaystyle(a+b)^{2}+b(b-1)+a(n-3)+2-n

\displaystyle\geq 0

This is clearly decreasing in
b
and so we just need to prove that

a^{2}+(n-3)a-n+2\geq 0.

The roots of this quadratic polynomial are
1
and
2-n
which finishes this case.
∎

Lemma 3.80
.

Let
s_{a,b}=(2b+a,a,0,\ldots,0)
be a Schmid module with
a\geq 1
and
b\geq 0.
Let
\lambda
satisfy (
3.76
) in the case
\lambda_{2}\neq 0
and
\lambda_{1}\leq 2-n
in the scalar case. Then we have

(3.81)

\|(\lambda-s_{a,b+1})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}-\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|(\lambda-s_{a,b})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}-\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}.

Proof.

Since the weights differ only in the first coordinate, the difference of the left hand side and the right hand side is just the difference of squares on the first coordinate:

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}-2b-2-a+n-1)^{2}-(\lambda_{1}-2b-a+n-1)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 0

\displaystyle-2[2(\lambda_{1}-2b-a+n-1)-2]

\displaystyle\geq 0

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}-2b-a+n-1-1

\displaystyle\leq 0

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle\leq a+2b+2-n

For scalar
\lambda
we immediately get
\lambda_{1}\leq 2-n\leq a+2b+2-n.
In the spinorial case we get
\lambda_{1}\leq 3/2-n\leq a+2b+2-n.
In the remaining case we actually have
\lambda_{2}\geq 1
and so (
3.76
) implies
\lambda_{1}\leq 1+p-2n
which is indeed less than or equal to
a+2b-n.

∎

Theorem 3.82

(General case)
.

Let
\lambda
be as in case 3 and let (
3.76
) holds. Then the Dirac inequality (
3.73
) holds for any Schmid module
s
.

Proof.

Thanks to the previous lemma we only have to prove that for
a\geq 1
we have

\|(\lambda-s_{a,0})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}-\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}\geq 0,

with strict inequality if (
3.76
) is strict. It follows by induction from combining the following two inequalities

\displaystyle\|(\lambda-s_{a,0})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|(\lambda^{\prime}-s^{\prime})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}

\displaystyle\|\lambda^{\prime}+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}

where

\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda-s_{1})^{+}\quad s^{\prime}=s_{a,0}-s_{1}=s_{a-1,0}.

The first one immediately follows from the Lemma
2.1
for
\mu=(\lambda-s_{1})^{+}
and
\nu=(s_{a,0}-s_{1})^{+}
and the second one is the Dirac inequality for
s_{1}
which is nothing but (
3.76
).

What remains is to check that
\lambda^{\prime}
satisfies the Dirac inequality for
s^{\prime}=s_{a-1,0}.
It can happen that
\lambda^{\prime}
falls into the spinor or scalar case. For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},1,0,\ldots,0)
the inequality (
3.76
) takes the form
\lambda_{1}+1\leq 2+2-2n
which means that
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}=\lambda_{1}-1
satisfies
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}\leq 2-2n.
Looking back at the Theorem
3.78
we see that
\lambda^{\prime}
satisfies the Dirac inequality with any Schmid module. Analogously, for
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},3/2,1/2,\ldots,\pm 1/2)
we have
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{1}+3/2\leq 2+2-2n
which gives
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}=\lambda_{1}-1\leq 3/2-2n
which is below the unitarizability bound
3/2-n
for the spinor case.

In all other cases
\lambda^{\prime}
is of general type. If
p>2
, then
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}+\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=\lambda_{1}-1+\lambda_{2}
and using (
3.76
) and
p^{\prime}=p-1
we see that this is less than or equal to
2+p^{\prime}-2n.
Hence the Dirac inequality is satisfied by the induction hypothesis. For
p=2
we have similarly
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}+\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-2\leq 2+2-2n\leq 2+p^{\prime}-2n
since
p^{\prime}\geq 2.

∎

3.5.
Dirac inequality for
\mathfrak{so}(2,2n-1)

The basic Schmid
\mathfrak{k}
-submodules of
S(\mathfrak{p}^{-})
have lowest weights
-s_{1}
or
-s_{2}
, where

s_{1}=(1,1,0,\ldots 0),\ s_{2}=(2,0,0,\ldots 0).

Moreover, all irreducible
\mathfrak{k}
-submodules of
S(\mathfrak{p}^{-})
have lowest weights
-s_{a,b}
, where

s_{a,b}=(2b+a,a,0,\ldots 0).

The highest weight
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules have highest weights of the form

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
, where

\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{3}\geq\cdots\lambda_{n}\geq 0,

\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
2\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for all
2\leq i,j\leq n.

In this case
\rho=(n-1/2,n-3/2,\ldots,1/2)
.

The basic necessary condition for unitarity is, as before, the Dirac inequality

(3.83)

\|(\lambda-s_{1})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}.

The basic Dirac inequality for a Schmid module is

(3.84)

\|(\lambda-s)^{+}+\rho\|^{2}\geq\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}

This is equivalent to

(3.85)

2\langle\gamma\,|\,\lambda+\rho\rangle\leq\|\gamma\|^{2}

where
\gamma
is defined by
(\lambda-s)^{+}=\lambda-\gamma.

Lemma 3.86
.

The basic Dirac inequality for
s=s_{1}
is given by

(3.87)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 0

\displaystyle\text{ for }\lambda=(\lambda_{1},0,\ldots,0)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 1-n

\displaystyle\text{ for }\lambda=(\lambda_{1},1/2,\ldots,1/2)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 1+p-2n

\displaystyle\text{ for }\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{p},\ldots,\lambda_{n})

\displaystyle\text{ where }1\leq\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{p}>\lambda_{p+1}\text{ and }2\leq p\leq n.

Proof.

Case 1:

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},0,\ldots,0)

In this case we have

\displaystyle(\lambda-s_{1})^{+}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}-1,-1,0,\ldots,0)^{+}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}-1,1,0,\ldots,0)

\displaystyle=\lambda-(\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2})

which shows that
\gamma=\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}
and (
3.84
) reduces to

\lambda_{1}+n-1/2-(n-3/2)\leq 1

which is equivalent to
\lambda_{1}\leq 0.

Case 2:

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},1/2,\ldots,1/2)

In this case we have

\displaystyle(\lambda-s_{1})^{+}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}-1,-1/2,1/2,\ldots,1/2,1/2)^{+}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}-1,1/2,1/2,\ldots,1/2,1/2)

\displaystyle=\lambda-\epsilon_{1}

Plugging
\gamma=\epsilon_{1}
into (
3.84
) we obtain
2(\lambda_{1}+n-1/2)\leq 1
which gives

(3.88)

\lambda_{1}\leq 1-n.

Case 3:

1\leq\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{p}>\lambda_{p+1}

The basic Dirac inequality (
3.84
) for
s=s_{1}
has
\gamma=\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{p}
and

\displaystyle 2(\lambda_{1}+n-1/2+\lambda_{p}+n-p+1/2)

\displaystyle\leq 2

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 1+p-2n

∎

Theorem 3.89

(Scalar case)
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},0,\ldots,0)
such that
\lambda_{1}<3/2-n
. Then (
3.84
) holds for any Schmid module
s
.

Proof.

The Dirac inequality for the second basic Schmid yields
\lambda_{1}\leq 3/2-n.

General Schmid module has highest weight
s_{a,b}=(2b+a,a,0,\ldots 0)
and similarly as before we get that the Dirac inequality is equivalent to

(2b+a)(\lambda_{1}+n+1/2-1)-a(n+1/2-2)\leq 2b^{2}+2ab+a^{2}.

Using
\lambda_{1}\leq 3/2-n
we see that it is sufficient to prove that

\displaystyle(2b+a)(3/2-n+n+1/2-1)-a(n+1/2-2)

\displaystyle\leq 2b^{2}+2ab+a^{2}

\displaystyle 2b+a+(3/2-n)a

\displaystyle\leq 2b^{2}+2ab+a^{2}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq 2b(b-1)+2ab+a(a+n-5/2).

Since
a,b\geq 0
and
n\geq 3
we are done.
∎

.

\displaystyle\leq a+2b+3/2-n

Theorem 3.92

\lambda

\|(\lambda-s_{a,0})^{+}+\rho\|^{2}-\|\lambda+\rho\|^{2}\geq 0,

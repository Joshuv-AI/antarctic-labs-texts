Unions of lines in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n}
Joshua Zahl

Abstract
We prove a conjecture of D. Oberlin on the dimension of unions of lines in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . If d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 is an integer, 0 ≤ β ≤ 1 0\leq\beta\leq 1 , and L L is a set of lines in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} with Hausdorff dimension at least 2 ​ ( d − 1 ) + β 2(d-1)+\beta , then the union of the lines in L L has Hausdorff dimension at least d + β d+\beta . Our proof combines a refined version of the multilinear Kakeya theorem by Carbery and Valdimarsson with the multilinear → \to linear argument of Bourgain and Guth.



1
Introduction

This paper concerns the following conjecture of D. Oberlin. In what follows,
\dim
will always refer to Hausdorff dimension.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
1\leq d\leq n
be integers and let
\beta\in[0,1]
. Let
L
be a set of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
\dim L\geq 2(d-1)+\beta
. Then

\dim\bigcup_{\ell\in L}\ell\geq d+\beta.

(1.1)

The bound (
1.1
) is best-possible, since
L
could be the set of lines contained in a
\beta
-dimensional union of (parallel)
d
-dimensional affine subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In
[
9
]
, R. Oberlin proved the finite field analogue of Conjecture
1.1
, and this is also the first place where the conjecture appeared in print. In
[
7
]
, Héra, Keleti, and Máthé considered a variant of Conjecture
1.1
where
d=0
and
L
is replaced by a set of
k
-dimensional affine subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Finally in
[
6
]
, Héra posed a generalization of Conjecture
1.1
, where lines are replaced by
k
-dimensional affine subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

When
d=1
, Conjecture
1.1
was proved by Héra, Keleti, and Máthé
[
7
]
and when
d=n-1,
Conjecture
1.1
was proved by D. Oberlin
[
8
]
. In this paper, we will prove Conjecture
1.1
for the remaining values of
d
. As is typical for such problems, the conjecture will follow from the corresponding maximal function estimate, which we will state below. In what follows,
\mathcal{L}_{n}
is the set of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with the following metric: if
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{n}
, then
d(\ell,\ell^{\prime})=|x-x^{\prime}|+|u\wedge u^{\prime}|
. Here
u
(resp
u^{\prime}
) is a unit vector parallel to
\ell
;
x
(resp.
x^{\prime}
) is the unique point on
\ell
with
x\perp u
; and
|u\wedge u|
is the (unsigned) area of the parallelogram spanned by
u
and
u^{\prime}
. A
\delta
-tube is the
\delta
-neighborhood of a unit line segment in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and if
T
is a
\delta
-tube, then
\ell(T)
is the line coaxial with
T
. Finally, if
p\in[1,\infty]
, then
p^{\prime}
denotes the conjugate Lebesgue exponent.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists
C=C(n,\varepsilon)
so that the following holds. Let
1\leq d<n
be an integer, and let
0\leq\beta\leq 1
. Let
\mathbb{T}
be a set of
\delta
-tubes in
B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that for all balls
B\subset\mathcal{L}_{n}
of radius
r
, we have

\#\{T\in\mathbb{T}\colon\ell(T)\subset B\}\leq(r/\delta)^{2(d-1)+\beta}.

(1.2)

Then

\Big\|\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}\leq C\delta^{\frac{1-d}{p^{\prime}}-\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T|\Big)^{1/p},\quad p^{\prime}=d+\beta.

(1.3)

We prove Theorem
1.2
by combining the multilinear Kakeya theorem
[
1
,
5
]
with the multilinear to linear arguments developed by Bourgain and Guth in
[
2
]
; this is done in Section
2
. A secondary goal of this paper is provide a short and self-contained introduction to this type of argument. We prove Conjecture
1.1
by first using a result from
[
7
]
to reduce to the case where
L
is analytic and hence supports a Frostman measure, and then using a standard discretization argument; this is done in Section
3
.

2
The Bourgain-Guth multilinear
\to
linear argument

In
[
2
]
, Bourgain and Guth combined the multilinear restriction theorem of Bennett, Carbery, and Tao
[
1
]
(see also
[
5
]
) with induction on scales to prove new Fourier restriction estimates. In this section, we revisit those arguments and obtain variants that are adapted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

In the arguments that follow, we adopt the notation
A\lesssim B
to mean that there is a constant
C
(which may depend on the ambient dimension
n
, and possibly also the Lebesgue exponent
p
, if this is relevant) so that
A\leq CB
.

We will use the following version of the multilinear Kakeya theorem, which is Theorem 1 from
[
3
]
. In what follows,
|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{k}|
is the unsigned volume of the parallelepiped spanned by
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
2\leq k\leq n
and let
\mathbb{T}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{T}_{k}
be sets of
\delta
-tubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{1}}\ldots\sum_{T_{k}\in\mathbb{T}_{k}}\chi_{T_{1}}\cdots\chi_{T_{k}}|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{k}|\Big)^{\frac{1}{k}}\Big\|_{\frac{k}{k-1}}\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{\delta}\Big)^{\frac{n}{k}-1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Big(\sum_{T_{i}\in\mathbb{T}_{i}}|T_{i}|\Big)^{\frac{1}{k}},

(2.1)

where in the above expression,
u_{i}
is the direction of the tube
T_{i}
.

When proving Theorem
1.2
, Theorem
2.1
is helpful in the special case where, for a typical point
x
where
\big(\sum\chi_{T}\big)^{p}
is large, most
(d+1)
-tuples of tubes
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d+1}
that contain
x
satisfy
|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{d+1}|\sim 1
. Indeed, it is straightforward to verify that our desired bound (
1.3
) follows from Theorem
2.1
in this case. The Bourgain-Guth multilinear
\to
linear argument establishes a dichotomy between this situation and an opposite extreme, where most of the tubes containing a typical point
x
are contained in the thin neighborhood of a
d
-dimensional affine subspace.

The lemmas below will help make this dichotomy precise. In what follows, if
H
is a
k
-dimensional vector subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a unit vector, we write
|H\wedge u|
to mean
|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{k}\wedge u|
, where
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
are orthonormal vectors that span
H
; the value of
|H\wedge u|
is independent of the choice of
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
. In particular,
|H\wedge u|=0
if
u\in H
, and
|H\wedge u|=1
if
u\perp H
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
U\subset S^{n-1}
be a multiset of unit vectors, let
1\leq k\leq n
, and let
0\leq\rho\leq 1
. Then at least one of the following must hold.

A.

\#\big\{(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})\in U^{k}\colon|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{k}|\geq\rho^{k-1}\big\}\geq\frac{1}{2}(\#U)^{k}.

B.
There is a
(k-1)
-dimensional vector subspace
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
so that

\#\{u\in U\colon|H\wedge u|\leq\rho\}\geq 2^{-2k}(\#U).

In particular, we always have

(\#U)\lesssim\rho^{\frac{1-k}{k}}\Big(\sum_{u_{1}\in U}\ldots\sum_{u_{k}\in U}|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{k}|\Big)^{1/k}+\sup_{H\in\operatorname{Gr}(k-1,\mathbb{R}^{n})}\#\{u\in U\colon|H\wedge u|\leq\rho\}.

(2.2)

Proof.

When
k=1
there is nothing to prove, so we can assume
k\geq 2
. Suppose Option A does not hold. Let
N=\#U
and let
2\leq j\leq k
be the smallest index so that

\#\big\{(u_{1},\ldots,u_{j})\in U^{j}\colon|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{j}|<\rho^{j-1}\big\}\geq 2^{2j-2k-1}N^{j}.

(2.3)

(Such an index must exist, since the failure of Option A implies that (
2.3
) holds for
j=k
). For notational simplicity, we will suppose that
j>2
(the same argument works when
j=2
, though some care must be taken with indices). There are at least
2^{2j-2k-2}N^{j-1}
tuples
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{j-1})
for which

\#\big\{u\in U\colon|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{j-1}\wedge u|<\rho^{j-1}\big\}\geq 2^{2j-2k-2}N.

(2.4)

But by the minimality of
j
, at most
2^{2j-2k-3}N^{j-1}
of these tuples satisfy
|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{j-1}|<\rho^{j-2}
. We conclude that there exists a tuple
(u_{1},\ldots u_{j-1})
that satisfies (
2.4
), with
|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{j-1}|\geq\rho^{j-2}
. Let
W=\operatorname{span}\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{j-1}\}
. Then
|W\wedge u|\leq\rho
for each
u
in the set (
2.4
), i.e.

\#\{u\in U\colon|W\wedge u|\leq\rho\}\geq 2^{2j-2k-2}N\geq 2^{-2k}N.

The result now follows by selecting a
(k-1)
-dimensional vector subspace
H
containing
W
.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
U\subset S^{n-1}
be a multiset of unit vectors, let
2\leq k\leq n
, and let
0<\rho\leq 1
. Let
\{\tau\}
be a covering of
S^{n-1}
by spherical caps of diameter
\rho
, and suppose each
u\in S^{n-1}
is contained in at most
10^{n}
caps. Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then

(\#U)^{p}\lesssim\rho^{\frac{(1-k)p}{k}}\Big(\sum_{u_{1}\in U}\ldots\sum_{u_{k}\in U}|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{k}|\Big)^{p/k}+\rho^{(2-k)(p-1)}\sum_{\tau}\big(\#(U\cap\tau)\big)^{p}.

(2.5)

Proof.

Apply Lemma
2.2
. The first term in (
2.2
) corresponds to the first term in (
2.5
). For the second term, note that if
H
is a
(k-1)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
\lesssim\rho^{2-k}
of the caps from
\{\tau\}
intersect the set
\{u\in S^{n-1}\colon|H\wedge u|\leq\rho\}
(it is not important whether the supremum in (
2.2
) is realized). Next we use Hölder’s inequality, and finally we add the remaining caps to the RHS of (
2.5
).
∎

We record the following consequence of Corollary
2.3
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0<\delta\leq\rho\leq 1
, let
2\leq k\leq n
, and let
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
\{\tau\}
be a covering of
S^{n-1}
by spherical caps of diameter
\rho
that is at most
10^{n}
-overlapping. Let
\mathbb{T}
be a set of
\delta
-tubes in
B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\Big\|\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}\lesssim\rho^{\frac{1-k}{k}}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}\in\mathbb{T}}\chi_{T_{1}}\cdots\chi_{T_{k}}|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{k}|\Big)^{\frac{1}{k}}\Big\|_{p}+\rho^{\frac{2-k}{p^{\prime}}}\Big(\sum_{\tau}\Big\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}\\
\operatorname{dir}(T)\in\tau\end{subarray}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2.6)

Proof.

For each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we apply Corollary
2.3
to the multiset of unit vectors
\{\operatorname{dir}(T)\colon x\in T\}
and integrate.
∎

Observe that if
\delta<\rho/2
, then the final term in (
2.6
) can be further decomposed. For each cap
\tau
with center
u\in S^{n-1}
, we can cover
B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
by boundedly overlapping
\rho
-tubes that point in direction
u
. If
T\in\mathbb{T}
and
\operatorname{dir}(T)\in\tau
, then
T
will be contained in at least 1, and at most
\lesssim 1
tubes
T_{\rho}
of this type. Let
\mathbb{T}_{\rho}
denote the set of all such tubes, for all caps
\tau
. Then each
T\in\mathbb{T}
is contained in at least 1, and at most
\lesssim 1
tubes from
\mathbb{T}_{\rho}
. Thus we have

\sum_{\tau}\Big\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}\\
\operatorname{dir}(T)\in\tau\end{subarray}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{T_{\rho}\in\mathbb{T}_{\rho}}\Big\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}\\
T\subset T_{\rho}\end{subarray}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}^{p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

(2.7)

The advantage is that each term on the RHS of (
2.7
) has been localized to a
\rho
-tube.

We will use Theorem
2.1
to control the first term on the RHS of (
2.6
). We record the following computation.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
1\leq d<n
be integers, let
\beta\in[0,1]
, and let
\mathbb{T}
be a set of
\delta
-tubes in
B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with

\#\mathbb{T}\leq\delta^{2(1-d)-\beta}
. Then

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{T_{1},\ldots,T_{d+1}\in\mathbb{T}}\chi_{T_{1}}\cdots\chi_{T_{d+1}}|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{d+1}|\Big)^{\frac{1}{d+1}}\Big\|_{p}\lesssim\delta^{\frac{1-d}{p^{\prime}}}\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T|\Big)^{1/p},\quad p^{\prime}=d+\beta.

(2.8)

Proof.

Note that
p-\frac{d+1}{d}\geq 0
, with equality if
\beta=1
. We have

\begin{split}\int\Big(&\sum_{T_{1},\ldots,T_{d+1}\in\mathbb{T}}\chi_{T_{1}}\cdots\chi_{T_{d+1}}|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{d+1}|\Big)^{\frac{p}{d+1}}\\
&\leq(\#\mathbb{T})^{p-\frac{d+1}{d}}\int\Big(\sum_{T_{1},\ldots,T_{d+1}\in\mathbb{T}}\chi_{T_{1}}\cdots\chi_{T_{d+1}}|u_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge u_{d+1}|\Big)^{\frac{1}{d}}\\
&\lesssim\Big(\delta^{1-n}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T|\Big)^{p-\frac{d+1}{d}}\Big[\delta^{\frac{d+1-n}{d}}\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T|\Big)^{\frac{d+1}{d}}\Big]\\
&=\delta^{1+(1-n)(p-1)}\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T|\Big)^{p-1}\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T|\Big)\\
&\leq\delta^{1+(1-n)(p-1)}\Big(\delta^{n-1}\delta^{2(1-d)-\beta}\Big)^{p-1}\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T|\Big)\\
&=\delta^{\frac{p(1-d)}{p^{\prime}}}\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T|\Big).\end{split}

(2.9)

For the first inequality, we used the observation that the integrand is bounded by
(\#\mathbb{T})^{d+1}
. The second inequality used Theorem
2.1
. The next equality re-groups terms. The fifth line used the hypothesis on the cardinality of
\mathbb{T}
, while the final line simplifies the expression.
∎

Combining (
2.7
) and Lemma
2.5
, we obtain the following; recall that
\mathbb{T}_{\rho}
is a maximal collection of boundedly overlapping
\rho
-tubes.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
1\leq d<n
be integers and let
\beta\in[0,1]
. Let
0<\delta\leq\rho/2\leq 1
, and let
\mathbb{T}
be a set of
\delta
-tubes in
B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
\#\mathbb{T}\leq\delta^{2(1-d)-\beta}
. Then

\Big\|\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}\lesssim\rho^{\frac{-d}{d+1}}\delta^{\frac{1-d}{p^{\prime}}}\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T|\Big)^{1/p}+\rho^{\frac{1-d}{p^{\prime}}}\bigg(\sum_{T_{\rho}\in\mathbb{T}_{\rho}}\Big\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}\\
T\subset T_{\rho}\end{subarray}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},\quad p^{\prime}=d+\beta.

(2.10)

Using Inequality
2.10
, we can prove Theorem
1.2
using induction on scales.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

In the arguments that follow, we will choose the (small, positive) quantities
\rho
and
\delta_{0},
and then the (large) quantity
C
, in that order;
C
is the quantity appearing in (
1.3
).

We will prove the theorem by induction on
\delta
. By choosing
C
appropriately, we can suppose that (
1.3
) holds whenever
\delta\geq\delta_{0}
. Suppose now that
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
, and suppose furthermore that for all
\tilde{\delta}\in(\delta,1]
, we have that (
1.3
) has been established for all collections
\tilde{\mathbb{T}}
of
\tilde{\delta}
-tubes in
B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
that satisfy the analogue of (
1.2
) with
\tilde{\delta}
in place of
\delta
. Let
\mathbb{T}
be a set of
\delta
-tubes in
B(0,1)
that satisfy (
1.2
). Our goal is to establish (
1.3
) for this collection of tubes.

By Corollary
2.6
, we have

\Big\|\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim\rho^{\frac{-dp}{d+1}}\delta^{\frac{(1-d)p}{p^{\prime}}}\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T|\Big)+\rho^{\frac{(1-d)p}{p^{\prime}}}\sum_{T_{\rho}\in\mathbb{T}_{\rho}}\Big\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}\\
T\subset T_{\rho}\end{subarray}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}^{p}.

(2.11)

If
C=C(n,\varepsilon)
is chosen sufficiently large depending on
\rho
(which in turn, will ultimately only depend on
n
and
\varepsilon
) and the implicit constant in (
2.11
), then (
2.11
) may be re-written as

\Big\|\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}^{p}-\frac{1}{2}C^{p}\delta^{\frac{(1-d)p}{p^{\prime}}}\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T|\Big)\lesssim\rho^{\frac{(1-d)p}{p^{\prime}}}\sum_{T_{\rho}\in\mathbb{T}_{\rho}}\Big\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}\\
T\subset T_{\rho}\end{subarray}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}^{p}.

(2.12)

We must now bound the RHS of (
2.12
). Let
T_{\rho}\in\mathbb{T}_{\rho}
. Let
A
be the affine map that sends the coaxial line of
T_{\rho}
to the
e_{1}
-axis, and dilates
T_{\rho}
by a factor of
1/\rho
in each of the directions
e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}
. We can verify that the image of each of the tubes
\{T\in\mathbb{T}\colon T\subset T_{\rho}\}
under this transformation is comparable to a
\delta/\rho
-tube contained in
B(0,1)
, and that this set of
\delta/\rho
-tubes satisfies the analogue of (
1.2
) with
\delta/\rho
in place of
\delta
(the inequality (
1.2
) might be weakened by a constant factor, but this is harmless and can be remedied, for example, by partitioning the set of
\delta/\rho
-tubes into
\sim 1
families, applying the arguments below to each family in turn, and using the triangle inequality).

Since
\delta/\rho>\delta
, we can apply the induction hypothesis to conclude that (after undoing the re-scaling)

\Big\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}\\
T\subset T_{\rho}\end{subarray}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim C^{p}(\delta/\rho)^{\frac{(1-d)p}{p^{\prime}}-\varepsilon p}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}\\
T\subset T_{\rho}\end{subarray}}|T|\Big).

Thus

\begin{split}\rho^{\frac{(1-d)p}{p^{\prime}}}\sum_{T_{\rho}\in\mathbb{T}_{\rho}}\Big\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}\\
T\subset T_{\rho}\end{subarray}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}^{p}&\lesssim C^{p}\rho^{\frac{(1-d)p}{p^{\prime}}}(\delta/\rho)^{\frac{(1-d)p}{p^{\prime}}-\varepsilon p}\sum_{T_{\rho}\in\mathbb{T}_{\rho}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}\\
T\subset T_{\rho}\end{subarray}}|T|\Big)\\
&\lesssim C^{p}\rho^{\varepsilon p}\delta^{\frac{(1-d)p}{p^{\prime}}-\varepsilon p}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T|.\end{split}

(2.13)

Selecting
\rho
sufficiently small compared to
\varepsilon,

p
, and the implicit constants in (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) (recall that these constants depend only on
n
and
p
; crucially, they do not depend on
\rho
or
C
), we obtain (
1.3
), which closes the induction and completes the proof.
∎

3
Proof of Conjecture
1.1

We are now ready to prove Conjecture
1.1
. First, we will reduce the situation where
L
is analytic, and in particular supports a Frostman measure of the appropriate dimension. A reduction of this type first appeared in
[
7
]
; we briefly summarize those arguments here.

For convenience, we will suppose that
\beta>0
(so in particular
d<n
). This is harmless, since if
\beta=0
then we can replace
d
by
d-1
and
\beta
by 1. Next, note that it suffices to establish a variant of (
1.1
), where the RHS has been replaced by
d+\beta-\varepsilon
for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
.

Fix such an
\varepsilon
. Let
X=\bigcup_{\ell\in L}\ell
, and let
X_{1}\supset X
be a
G_{\delta}
set with
\dim X_{1}=\dim X
; the construction of such a set is explained, for example, in
[
4
]
. Since
\ell\subset X_{1}
for each
\ell\in L
, if we select
R
sufficiently large then the set
L_{1}=\{\ell\in L\colon|\ell\cap X_{1}\cap B(0,R)|>1\}
satisfies
\dim L_{1}\geq\dim L-\varepsilon/2.

Abusing notation, we will replace the set
X_{1}
and the lines in
L_{1}
and
L
with their images under the re-scaling
x\mapsto x/R
. We still have
\dim L_{1}\geq 2(d-1)+\beta-\varepsilon/2
, but now

L_{1}=\{\ell\in L\colon|\ell\cap B(0,1)|>R^{-1}\}.

(3.1)

The set
X_{2}=X_{1}\cap B(0,1)
is
G_{\delta}
and bounded, and
\ell\cap X_{2}
contains an interval of length
\geq 1/R
for each
\ell\in L_{1}.

Next, we recall the following special case of Lemma 3.1 from
[
7
]
. In the statement below,
A(n,1)
is the set of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and if
Z\subset\ell
, then
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\infty}(Z)=\inf\sum|J|
, where the infimum is taken over all coverings of
Z
by countable collections of open intervals
\{J\}
. Note that the analyticity of a set
M\subset A(1,n)
depends on the metric (or more generally, the topology), and the authors in
[
7
]
discuss their choice of metric in Remark 2.2. The metric on
\mathcal{L}_{n}\subset A(n,1)
described in Section
1
is compatible with that from
[
7
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be
G_{\delta}
and bounded, and let
c>0
. Then the set of lines

\big\{\ell\in A(n,1)\colon\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\infty}(\ell\cap Y)>c\big\}

is analytic.

Let
L_{2}=\{\ell\in A(n,1)\colon\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\infty}(\ell\cap X_{2})\geq 1/R\}
. Then
L_{2}
is analytic, and
L_{2}\supset L_{1}
(indeed,
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\infty}(\ell\cap X_{2})\geq 1/R
for each
\ell\in L_{1}
, since
\ell\cap X_{2}
contains an interval of length
\geq 1/R
), so
\dim L_{2}\geq 2(d-1)+\beta-\varepsilon/2
. At this point, we have replaced our original set
X=\bigcup_{\ell\in L}\ell
by a new set
X_{2}
whose Hausdorff dimension is no larger than that of
X
, and we have replaced the original set of lines
L
by a set
L_{2}
that is analytic (in the metric space
\mathcal{L}_{n}
), with
\dim L_{2}\geq 2(d-1)+\beta-\varepsilon/2
. In particular,
L_{2}
supports a Frostman probability measure
\mathbb{P}
that satisfies the following ball condition: There is a constant
C_{0}
so that for all
r>0
,

\mathbb{P}(B)\leq C_{0}r^{2(d-1)+\beta-\varepsilon}\quad\textrm{for all balls}\ B\subset\mathcal{L}_{n}\ \textrm{of radius}\ r.

(3.2)

To prove Conjecture
1.1
, it suffices to establish the estimate

\dim X_{2}\geq d+\beta-C_{1}\varepsilon,

(3.3)

for some constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(n)>0
; we will prove (
3.3
) with
C_{1}=7n
.

Armed with Theorem
1.2
, the proof of (
3.3
) is a straightforward discretization argument. Let
k_{0}
be a large integer that will be chosen below. Cover
X_{2}
by a union
\bigcup_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}B
, where
\mathcal{B}_{k}
is a collection of balls of radius
2^{-k}
, and
\sum_{k}2^{-k(\dim X_{2}+\varepsilon)}\#\mathcal{B}_{k}<\infty
.

For each line
\ell\in L_{2}
, there is an index
k=k(\ell)\geq k_{0}
so that

\Big|\ell\cap\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}B\Big|\geq\frac{1}{100Rk^{2}},

where
R
is the constant from (
3.1
). For each index
k
, define
L^{(k)}=\{\ell\in L_{2}\colon k(\ell)=k\}
. Then
\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}\mathbb{P}(L^{(k)})\geq 1,
so there is an index
k_{1}\geq k_{0}
with
\mathbb{P}(L^{(k_{1})})\geq\frac{1}{100k_{1}^{2}}
. Define
L_{3}=L^{(k_{1})}
, define
\delta=2^{-k_{1}}
, and define
E_{\delta}=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{k_{1}}}B
.

In what follows, we will write
A\lessapprox B
to mean
A\lesssim|\log\delta|^{C}B
, where
C
is an absolute constant (in practice we can take
C\leq 10
). In particular, we have
\mathbb{P}(L_{3})\gtrapprox 1
, and
|\ell\cap E_{\delta}|\gtrapprox 1
for each
\ell\in L_{3}
.

By dyadic pigeonholing, we can select a
2\delta
-separated set
L_{3}^{\delta}\subset L_{3}
and a number
A\geq C_{0}^{-1}\delta^{\varepsilon}
so that
(A^{-1}\delta^{2(1-d)-\beta})(\#L_{3}^{\delta})\gtrapprox 1
, and for each
\ell\in L_{3}^{\delta}
we have

A^{-1}\delta^{2(d-1)+\beta}\leq\mathbb{P}(B(\ell,\delta))<2A^{-1}\delta^{2(d-1)+\beta}.

(3.4)

Comparing (
3.2
) and (
3.4
), we see that if
B\subset\mathcal{L}_{n}
is a ball of radius
r\geq\delta
, then

\#(L_{3}^{\delta}\cap B)\leq C_{0}\delta^{-\varepsilon}A(r/\delta)^{2(d-1)+\beta}.

(3.5)

Let
L_{4}^{\delta}\subset L_{3}^{\delta}
be obtained by randomly and independently selecting each
\ell\in L_{3}^{\delta}
with probability
C_{0}^{-1}\delta^{2\varepsilon}A^{-1}
. We claim that if
\delta>0
is sufficiently small (depending on
\varepsilon
), then there exists such a random selection
L_{4}^{\delta}
with

\#L_{4}^{\delta}\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{0}^{-1}\delta^{2\varepsilon}A^{-1}\#L_{3}^{\delta}\geq\delta^{2(1-d)-\beta+4\varepsilon},

(3.6)

which satisfies the ball condition

\#(L_{4}^{\delta}\cap B)\leq(r/\delta)^{2(d-1)+\beta}\quad\textrm{for all balls}\ B\subset\mathcal{L}_{n}.

(3.7)

This is a standard random sampling argument. The key insight is that it suffices to establish (
3.7
) (with
(r/\delta)^{2(d-1)+\beta}
strengthened to
\frac{1}{2}(r/\delta)^{2(d-1)+\beta}
) for a boundedly-overlapping family of balls of radius
r
, for each dyadic
\delta\leq r\leq 1
. There are only about
\delta^{-n}
such balls, and the probability that (
3.7
) fails for any particular ball is exponentially small in
\delta
. The existence of an appropriate random sample then follows from the union bound. See e.g.
[
10
, Section 2]
for details.

Finally, for each
\ell\in L_{4}^{\delta}
, let
\tilde{\ell}\subset\ell
be a unit line segment in
B(0,1)
with
|\tilde{\ell}\cap E_{\delta}|\gtrapprox 1
, and let
T(\ell)
be the
\delta
-neighborhood of
\tilde{\ell}
. Let
\mathbb{T}=\{T(\ell)\colon\ell\in L_{4}^{\delta}\}
. Then
\mathbb{T}
is a collection of
\delta
-tubes in
B(0,1)
that satisfies (
1.2
), and
|T\cap E_{\delta}|\gtrapprox|T|
for each
T\in\mathbb{T}
. By (
3.6
) and Hölder’s inequality, have that for
p\in(1,\infty)
,

\delta^{n-1}\delta^{2(1-d)-\beta+4\varepsilon}\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T|\lessapprox\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T\cap E_{\delta}|\leq|E_{\delta}|^{1/p^{\prime}}\Big\|\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}\lesssim\delta^{(n-\dim X_{2}-\varepsilon)/p^{\prime}}\Big\|\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p},

i.e.

\Big\|\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}\gtrapprox\delta^{5\varepsilon}\delta^{n-2d+1-\beta}\delta^{-(n-\dim X_{2})/p^{\prime}}.

(3.8)

On the other hand, by Theorem
1.2
, if
p=\frac{d+\beta}{d+\beta-1}
then

\Big\|\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}\chi_{T}\Big\|_{p}\leq C\delta^{\frac{1-d}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{n+1-2d-\beta}{p}-\varepsilon}.

(3.9)

Comparing (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) and simplifying, we conclude that
\delta^{\frac{\dim X_{2}-d-\beta}{p^{\prime}}}\lessapprox\delta^{-6\varepsilon}
. Thus if
k_{0}
is selected sufficiently large (which forces
\delta
to be sufficiently small), we conclude that
\dim X_{2}\geq d+\beta-7(d+1)\varepsilon
, which is (
3.3
).

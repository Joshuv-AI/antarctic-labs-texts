Global existence and infinite time blow-up of classical solutions to chemotaxis systems of local sensing in higher dimensions
Kentaro Fujie, Takasi Senba

Abstract
This paper deals with the fully parabolic chemotaxis system of local sensing in higher dimensions. Despite the striking similarity between this system and the Keller–Segel system, we prove the absence of finite-time blow-up phenomenon in this system even in the supercritical case. It means that for any regular initial data, independently of the magnitude of mass, the classical solution exists globally in time in the higher dimensional setting. Moreover, for the exponential decaying motility case, it is established that solutions may blow up at infinite time for any magnitude of mass. In order to prove our theorem, we deal with some auxiliary identity as an evolution equation with a time dependent operator. In view of this new perspective, the direct consequence of the abstract theory is rich enough to establish global existence of the system. Key words: chemotaxis; Keller–Segel system; global existence AMS Classification: 35B44, 35K51, 35Q92, 92C17.

Introduction

Consider the following initial-boundary value problem for the fully parabolic system:

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta(\gamma(v)u)&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
v_{t}=\Delta v-v+u&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\partial_{\nu}u=\partial_{\nu}v=0\qquad&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\;\;v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\qquad&x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Assume to the contrary that
T_{\mathrm{max}}<\infty
.
Define the operator

A(t)\varphi:=-\gamma(v(t+\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}}))(\Delta\varphi-\varphi)

for
\varphi\in\{\psi\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\,|\,\partial_{\nu}\psi=0\ \mbox{on }\partial\Omega\}
with
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Since
\gamma(v)
is Hölder continuous (Lemma
3.1
),
the operator
-A(t)
generates analytic semigroup in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for any
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Denoting

\displaystyle W(s)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle w(s+\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}}),

\displaystyle F(s)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(1-\Delta)^{-1}(\gamma(v(s+\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}}))u(s+\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}}))

for
s\in[0,\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}})
,
we can check that

is a solution of the evolution equation

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}W+A(t)W=F\qquad t\in[0,\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}}),\\
W(0)=w(\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}}).\end{cases}

Since the elliptic regularity theorem implies
F\in C([0,\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}});L^{p}(\Omega))
for any
p\in(1,\infty)
,
in view of the abstract theory (
[
15
, Theorem 5.2.2]
),
the solution

can be represented by the integral equation:

W(t)=U(t,0)w(\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}})+\int_{0}^{t}U(t,s)F(s)\,ds\qquad t\in[0,\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}}),

where

is the fundamental solution.
Due to the fact that
w(\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}})

satisfy the Neumann boundary condition, we can apply the estimate of fundamental solutions (
[
15
, Theorem 5.2.1]

\displaystyle\|A(t)W(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|A(t)U(t,0)(A(0))^{-1}A(0)w(\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\int_{0}^{t}\|A(t)U(t,s)(A(s))^{-1}A(s)F(s)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(p)\|A(0)w(\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+C(p)\int_{0}^{t}\|A(s)F(s)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\,ds

with some
C(p)>0
.
By the definition of
W
, it follows

\displaystyle\|A(t)W(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\gamma(v(t+\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}}))(\Delta w(t+\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}})-w(t+\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}}))\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\gamma(v(t+\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}}))u(t+\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

We also obtain for
s\in[0,\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}})
,

\displaystyle A(s)F(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\gamma(v(s+\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}}))(\Delta-1)\left[(1-\Delta)^{-1}(\gamma(v(s+\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}}))u(s+\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}}))\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\gamma(v(s+\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}})))^{2}u(s+\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\mathrm{max}}).

Since
\gamma(v)
is bounded, for any
p\in(1,\infty)
it follows for

with some
C^{\prime}(p)>0
.
By the Gronwall inequality, for any

there exists some

\limsup_{t\to T_{\mathrm{max}}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C^{\prime\prime}(p).

We can pick up some

which contradicts the assumption

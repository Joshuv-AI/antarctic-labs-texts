Some Algebraic Questions about the Reed-Muller Code
Xiang-dong Hou

Abstract
. Let R q ​ ( r , n ) R_{q}(r,n) denote the r r th order Reed-Muller code of length q n q^{n} over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} . We consider two algebraic questions about the Reed-Muller code. Let H q ​ ( r , n ) = R q ​ ( r , n ) / R q ​ ( r − 1 , n ) H_{q}(r,n)=R_{q}(r,n)/R_{q}(r-1,n) . (1) When q = 2 q=2 , it is known that there is a “duality” between the actions of GL ​ ( n , 𝔽 2 ) \text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{2}) on H 2 ​ ( r , n ) H_{2}(r,n) and on H 2 ​ ( r ′ , n ) H_{2}(r^{\prime},n) , where r + r ′ = n r+r^{\prime}=n . The result is false for a general q q . However, we find that a slightly modified duality statement still holds when q q is a prime or r < char ​ 𝔽 q r<\text{char}\,\mathbb{F}_{q} . (2) Let ℱ ⁡ ( 𝔽 q n , 𝔽 q ) \mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q}) denote the 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -algebra of all functions from 𝔽 q n \mathbb{F}_{q}^{n} to 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} . It is known that when q q is a prime, the Reed-Muller codes { 0 } = R q ​ ( − 1 , n ) ⊂ R q ​ ( 0 , n ) ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ R q ​ ( n ⁡ ( q − 1 ) , n ) = ℱ ⁡ ( 𝔽 q n , 𝔽 q ) \{0\}=R_{q}(-1,n)\subset R_{q}(0,n)\subset\cdots\subset R_{q}(n(q-1),n)=\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q}) are the only AGL ​ ( n , 𝔽 q ) \text{AGL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q}) -submodules of ℱ ⁡ ( 𝔽 q n , 𝔽 q ) \mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q}) . In particular, H q ​ ( r , n ) H_{q}(r,n) is an irreducible GL ​ ( n , 𝔽 q ) \text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q}) -module when q q is a prime. 1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q})
denote the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra of all functions from
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Each such function is uniquely represented by a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
with
\deg_{X_{i}}f\leq q-1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
; polynomials of this form are called
reduced
. Each polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
is congruent to a reduced polynomial modulo the ideal
(X_{1}^{q}-X_{1},\dots,X_{n}^{q}-X_{n})
. For
0\leq r\leq n(q-1)
, the
r
th order
Reed-Muller code
of length
q^{n}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is defined to be

R_{q}(r,n)=\{f\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q}):\deg f\leq r\}.

In addition, we define
R_{q}(-1,n)=\{0\}
. There is a natural identification of
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q})
with
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{q^{n}}
: Each
f\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q})
is identified with its vector of values
(f(x))_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{q^{n}}
. Therefore,
R_{q}(r,n)
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{q^{n}}
and hence is a linear code of length
q^{n}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
; this is the context in which the Reed-Muller code was initially discovered with
q=2
.

The
affine linear group
of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is

\text{AGL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})=\Bigl\{\left[\begin{matrix}A&0\cr a&1\end{matrix}\right]:A\in\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q}),\ a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\Bigr\}<\text{GL}(n+1,\mathbb{F}_{q}).

The group
\text{AGL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
acts on
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q})
as follows: For
\sigma=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}A&0\cr a&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\text{AGL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
f\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q})
,

\sigma(f)=f((X_{1},\dots,X_{n})A+a),

that is,
\sigma(f)=f\circ\sigma
, where the
\sigma
in
f\circ\sigma
is treated as an affine transformation of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.
Under this action, the Reed-Muller codes
R_{q}(r,n)
become
\text{AGL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-modules. (In fact, except for the extreme cases
r\in\{-1,0,n(q-1)-1,n(q-1)\}
,
\text{AGL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
is the largest subgroup
G
of the permutation group on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
such that
R_{q}(r,n)
is
G
-invariant; in coding theoretic terms,
\text{AGL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
is the automorphism group of
R_{q}(r,n)
except for the extreme cases
[
3
]
.) Interesting algebraic questions arise about these
\text{AGL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-modules. The quotient module

(1.1)

H_{q}(r,n):=R_{q}(r,n)/R_{q}(r-1,n)

consists of reduced homogeneous polynomials of degree
r
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
. Translations
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\mapsto(X_{1}+a_{1},\dots,X_{n}+a_{n})
have no effect on
H_{q}(r,n)
. Hence, the
\text{AGL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-structure of
H_{q}(r,n)
induces a
\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-module structure. In this paper, we consider two separate questions about the module
H_{q}(r,n)
.

Let
\Omega_{q,n}=\{0,1,\dots,q-1\}^{n}
. For
{\boldsymbol{i}}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\Omega_{q,n}
, define
|{\boldsymbol{i}}|=i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}
,
\bar{\boldsymbol{i}}=(q-1-i_{1},\dots,q-1-i_{n})
,
X^{{\boldsymbol{i}}}=X_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{i_{n}}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q})
, and, for
0\leq r\leq n(q-1)
, define
\Omega_{q,n,r}=\{{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\Omega_{q,n}:|{\boldsymbol{i}}|=r\}
. Then
H_{q}(r,n)
has an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-basis
\{X^{\boldsymbol{i}}:{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}\}
, and the “dual” module
H_{q}(r^{\prime},n)
, where
r+r^{\prime}=n(q-1)
, has a “dual” basis
\{(-1)^{n}X^{\bar{\boldsymbol{i}}}:{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}\}
. Let
(\ )^{c}:H_{q}(r,n)\to H_{q}(r^{\prime},n)
be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-map sending
X^{{\boldsymbol{i}}}
to
(-1)^{n}X^{\bar{\boldsymbol{i}}}
. When
q=2
, it is known that
f,g\in H_{2}(r,n)
are GL-equivalent (i.e., in the same
\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{2})
-orbit) if and only if
f^{c},g^{c}\in H_{2}(r^{\prime},n)
are GL-equivalent
[
15
, §4]
. For a general
q
, this duality statement is not true; see Example
2.4
. However, we will prove in Theorem
2.2
that a slightly modified duality statement still holds when
q
is a prime or
r<\text{char}\,\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

When
q=p
is a prime, Mortimer
[
24
, Ch. 5]
proved that the Reed-Muller codes

\{0\}=R_{p}(-1,n)\subset R_{p}(0,n)\subset\cdots\subset R_{p}(n(p-1),n)=\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{p})

are the only
\text{AGL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{p})
-submodules of
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{p})
; also see
[
1
, §5.5]
. In particular,
H_{p}(r,n)
is an irreducible
\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{p})
-module. However, for a general
q
,
H_{q}(r,n)
is not necessarily irreducible and its submodules have not been determined previously. Our second main result (Theorem
3.9
) gives all
\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-submodules of
H_{q}(r,n)
. Moreover, we determine the factors in the composition series of
H_{q}(r,n)
. Consequently, we obtain a class of irreducible modular representations of
\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. There is a method for constructing all irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{q}\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-modules using Weyl modules. The factors of the composition series of
H_{q}(r,n)
, though accounting for a small portion of all irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{q}\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-modules, have the advantage that they are explicit and much easier to describe. More comments in this regard are given in Section 5.

The above questions and their solutions have practical applications in coding theory. The duality between
H_{q}(r,n)
and
H_{q}(r^{\prime},n)
, when it exists, allows people to study the homogeneous
q
-ary functions of degree
r^{\prime}
through the canonical homogeneous
q
-ary functions of degree
r
. The duality between
H_{2}(2,n)
and
H_{2}(n-2,n)
played an essential role in a simplified approach to the determination of the covering radius of
R_{2}(1,7)

[
14
,
25
]
. When
q=2
, the canonical forms in
H_{2}(3,n)
are known for
n\leq 9

[
7
,
8
,
15
]
. (Elements of
H_{2}(3,n)
are reduced binary cubic forms in
n
variables.) Recently, these results and the duality between
H_{2}(3,n)
and
H_{2}(n-3,n)
were used by Dougherty, Mauldin and Tiefenbruck
[
12
]
to study the covering radius of
R_{2}(n-4,n)
in
R_{2}(n-3,n)
. The
\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-submodules of
H_{q}(r,n)
correspond to the
\text{AGL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-submodules between
R_{q}(r-1,n)
and
R_{q}(r,n)
.
\text{AGL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-submodules of
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q})
are codes whose automorphism groups contain
\text{AGL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
; they belong to the class of affine invariant codes. For studies on other types of affine invariant codes, see
[
4
,
10
,
11
,
16
,
17
,
19
]
.
In general, codes with large automorphism groups facilitate effective decoding schemes such as permutation decoding
[
20
,
21
,
23
]
.

To simplify writing, we will allow a few harmless abuses of notation. When a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
is treated as an element of
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q})
, it is meant to be the reduced polynomial
f_{1}
such that
f\equiv f_{1}\pmod{(X_{1}^{q}-X_{1},\dots,X_{n}^{q}-X_{n})}
. When a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
of degree
\leq r
is treated as an element of
H_{q}(r,n)
, it is meant to be the coset
f_{1}+R_{q}(r-1,n)
, where
f_{1}
is the reduced polynomial of
f
.

2.
A Duality Theorem

Define an inner product
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
on
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q})
by

(2.1)

\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}}f(x)g(x),\qquad f,g\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q}).

For
\sigma\in\text{AGL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
f,g\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q})
, we have

(2.2)

\langle\sigma(f),g\rangle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}}f(\sigma(x))g(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}}f(y)g(\sigma^{-1}(y))=\langle f,\sigma^{-1}(g)\rangle.

Hence, when
\sigma
is treated as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear transformation of
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q})
, its adjoint with respect to the inner product
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
is
\sigma^{-1}
.
For
0\leq r,r^{\prime}\leq n(q-1)
with
r+r^{\prime}=n(q-1)
, the above inner product induces a well-defined non-degenerate
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-bilinear map
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle:H_{q}(r,n)\times H_{q}(r^{\prime},n)\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In fact, if
f_{1},f_{2}\in R_{q}(r,n)
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in R_{q}(r^{\prime},n)
are such that
f_{1}\equiv f_{2}\pmod{R_{q}(r-1,n)}
and
g_{1}\equiv g_{2}\pmod{R_{q}(r^{\prime}-1,n)}
, then it is easy to see that

\langle f_{1},g_{1}\rangle=\langle f_{2},g_{2}\rangle.

The map
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle:H_{q}(r,n)\times H_{q}(r^{\prime},n)\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
will be referred to as the
pairing
between
H_{q}(r,n)
and
H_{q}(r^{\prime},n)
. The module
H_{q}(r,n)
has an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-basis

\mathfrak{B}_{r}=\{X^{{\boldsymbol{i}}}:{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}\},

which is ordered by the lexicographic order on
\Omega_{q,n,r}
.
The dual basis of
\mathfrak{B}_{r}
in
H_{q}(r^{\prime},n)
with respect to the pairing
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
is

\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}_{r}=\{(-1)^{n}X^{\bar{\boldsymbol{i}}}:{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}\},

as one can easily see that for
{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{j}}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}
,

(2.3)

\langle X^{{\boldsymbol{i}}},(-1)^{n}X^{\bar{\boldsymbol{j}}}\rangle=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ {\boldsymbol{i}}={\boldsymbol{j}},\cr 0&\text{if}\ {\boldsymbol{i}}\neq{\boldsymbol{j}}.\end{cases}

For
A\in\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the action of
A
on
f\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q})
is

A(f)=f((X_{1},\dots,X_{n})A).

Let
\mathcal{A}_{r}(A)
denote the matrix of
A
(as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear transformation of
H_{q}(r,n)
) with respect to the basis
\mathfrak{B}_{r}
. Then by (
2.3
),
\mathcal{A}_{r}(A)=(\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{j}}}(A))_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{j}}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}}
, where

(2.4)

\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{j}}}(A)=\langle A(X^{{\boldsymbol{i}}}),(-1)^{n}X^{\bar{\boldsymbol{j}}}\rangle.

Let
A=(a_{ts})_{1\leq t,s\leq n}
and
{\boldsymbol{i}}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n}),{\boldsymbol{j}}=(j_{1},\dots,j_{n})\in\Omega_{q,n,r}
. Then

\displaystyle\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{j}}}(A)\,

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\sum_{x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}}(a_{11}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n1}x_{n})^{i_{1}}\cdots(a_{1n}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{nn}x_{n})^{i_{n}}

\displaystyle\kern 100.00015pt\cdot x_{1}^{q-1-j_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{q-1-j_{n}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}}\Bigl(\sum_{i_{11}+\cdots+i_{1n}=i_{1}}\binom{i_{1}}{i_{11},\dots,i_{1n}}(a_{11}x_{1})^{i_{11}}\cdots(a_{n1}x_{n})^{i_{1n}}\Bigr)\cdots

\displaystyle\kern 60.00009pt\Bigl(\sum_{i_{n1}+\cdots+i_{nn}=i_{n}}\binom{i_{n}}{i_{n1},\dots,i_{nn}}(a_{1n}x_{1})^{i_{n1}}\cdots(a_{nn}x_{n})^{i_{nn}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\kern 60.00009pt\cdot x_{1}^{q-1-j_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{q-1-j_{n}},

where

\binom{i_{s}}{i_{s1},\dots,i_{sn}}=\frac{i_{s}!}{i_{s1}!\cdots i_{sn}!}

is the multinomial coefficient. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{j}}}(A)\,

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i_{st})\cr\sum_{t}i_{st}=i_{s},\,1\leq s\leq n\end{subarray}}\binom{i_{1}}{i_{11},\dots,i_{1n}}\cdots\binom{i_{n}}{i_{n1},\dots,i_{nn}}\Bigl(\prod_{s,t}a_{ts}^{i_{st}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\kern 50.00008pt\cdot\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}}x_{1}^{i_{11}+\cdots+i_{n1}+q-1-j_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{i_{1n}+\cdots+i_{nn}+q-1-j_{n}}.

In the above,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}}x_{1}^{i_{11}+\cdots+i_{n1}+q-1-j_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{i_{1n}+\cdots+i_{nn}+q-1-j_{n}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(-1)^{n}&\text{if\ $\displaystyle\sum_{s}i_{st}+q-1-j_{t}$ is $>0$\ and\ $\equiv 0\ \text{mod}\;(q-1)$ for all $1\leq t\leq n$},\cr 0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Hence

\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{j}}}(A)=\sum_{(i_{st})}\binom{i_{1}}{i_{11},\dots,i_{1n}}\cdots\binom{i_{n}}{i_{n1},\dots,i_{nn}}\Bigl(\prod_{s,t}a_{ts}^{i_{st}}\Bigr),

where the sum is over all the matrices
(i_{st})
subject to the conditions

(2.5)

\begin{cases}i_{st}\geq 0\ \text{for all}\ 1\leq s,t\leq n,\vskip 3.99994pt\cr\displaystyle\sum_{t}i_{st}=i_{s}\ \text{for all}\ 1\leq s\leq n,\vskip 3.99994pt\cr\displaystyle\sum_{s}i_{st}\equiv j_{t}\ \text{mod}\;(q-1)\ \text{and}\ >j_{t}-q+1\ \text{for all}\ 1\leq t\leq n.\end{cases}

The first and third conditions in (
2.5
) imply that
\sum_{s}i_{st}\geq j_{t}
for all
1\leq t\leq n
. (Look at the cases
0\leq j_{t}<q-1
and
j_{t}=q-1
separately.)
Since
|{\boldsymbol{i}}|=|{\boldsymbol{j}}|
, the conditions
\sum_{t}i_{st}=i_{s}
(for all
s
) and
\sum_{s}i_{st}\geq j_{t}
(for all
t
) imply that
\sum_{s}i_{st}=j_{t}
for all
t
. Let

M({\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{j}})=\Bigl\{(i_{st})_{1\leq s,t\leq n}:\sum_{t}i_{st}=i_{s},\ 1\leq s\leq n;\ \sum_{s}i_{st}=j_{t},\ 1\leq t\leq n\Bigr\};

see Figure
1
. Then

(2.6)

\sigma_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{j}}}(A)=\sum_{(i_{st})\in M({\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{j}})}\binom{i_{1}}{i_{11},\dots,i_{1n}}\cdots\binom{i_{n}}{i_{n1},\dots,i_{nn}}\Bigl(\prod_{s,t}a_{ts}^{i_{st}}\Bigr).

For
{\boldsymbol{i}}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\Omega_{q,n,r}
, define
{\boldsymbol{i}}!:=i_{1}!\cdots i_{n}!
. Let
D_{r}
be the
\Omega_{q,n,r}\times\Omega_{q,n,r}
diagonal matrix whose
({\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{i}})
entry is
{\boldsymbol{i}}!
.

\binom{i_{1}}{j}X_{1}^{i_{1}-j}X_{2}^{j+i_{2}}\cdots X_{n}^{i_{n}}\in M
for all
1\leq j\leq i_{1}
. Choosing
j=p^{k}
gives
X_{1}^{i_{1}-p^{k}}X_{2}^{i_{2}+p^{k}}\cdots X_{n}^{i_{n}}\in M
. (Note that
\binom{i_{1}}{p^{k}}\neq 0
.)
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

If
f\in M
, then every monomial in
f
belongs to
M
.

Proof.

Use induction on
n
.

First we claim that if
X_{n}^{i}a(X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1})\in M
, where
a\in H_{q}(r-i,n-1)
, then all monomials in
X_{n}^{i}a
are in
M
. Let
M_{1}=\{b\in H_{q}(r-i,n-1):X_{n}^{i}b\in M\}
. Then
M_{1}
is a
\text{GL}(n-1,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-module and
a\in M_{1}
. By the induction hypothesis, all monomials in
a
are in
M_{1}
. Hence all monomials in
X_{n}^{i}a
are in
M
.

Let

f=X_{n}^{q-1}a_{q-1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1})+X_{n}^{q-2}a_{q-2}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1})+\cdots+a_{0}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1}).

By the above claim, it suffices to show that
X_{n}^{i}a_{i}\in M
for all
0\leq i\leq q-1
. Let
\gamma
be a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then

f(X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1},\gamma X_{n})-f(X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1},X_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{q-2}(\gamma^{i}-1)X_{n}^{i}a_{i}\in M.

By Lemma
3.1
,
X_{n}^{i}a_{i}\in M
for all
1\leq i\leq q-2
. Thus we also have
X_{n}^{q-1}a_{q-1}+a_{0}\in M
. Let

M_{2}=\{b\in H_{q}(r,n-1):X_{n}^{q-1}a+b\in M\ \text{for some}\ a\in H_{q}(r-(q-1),n-1)\}.

Then
M_{2}
is a
\text{GL}(n-1,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-module and
a_{0}\in M_{2}
. By the induction hypothesis, all monomials of
a_{0}
are in
M_{2}
, that is, for any monomial
X_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}
of
a_{0}
, there exists
a(X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1})\in H_{q}(r-(q-1),n-1)
such that
X_{n}^{q-1}a+X_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}\in M
. It suffices to show that
X_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}\in M
. (Then
a_{0}\in M
and hence
X_{n}^{q-1}a_{q-1}\in M
.) Without loss of generality, assume
i_{1}>0
. Write

i_{1}=c_{0}p^{0}+\cdots+c_{m-1}p^{m-1}

in base
p
expansion and assume that
c_{j}>0
for some
j
. Then

\displaystyle M\ni\,

\displaystyle\Bigl(X_{n}^{q-1}a_{q-1}((X_{1}+X_{n}),X_{2},\dots,X_{n-1})+(X_{1}+X_{n})^{i_{1}}X_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle-\Bigl(X_{n}^{q-1}a_{q-1}(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n-1})+X_{1}^{i_{1}}X_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle(X_{1}+X_{n})^{i_{1}}X_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}-X_{1}^{i_{1}}X_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{i_{1}}\binom{i_{1}}{k}X_{n}^{k}X_{1}^{i_{1}-k}X_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}.

(To see the first equality in the above, note that
X_{n}^{q}=X_{n}
in
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\mathbb{F}_{q})
, and hence any monomial
X_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{j_{n}}
with
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{n}=r
and
j_{n}\geq q
is 0 in
H_{q}(r,n)
.)
By Lemma
3.1
,
\binom{i_{1}}{k}X_{n}^{k}X_{1}^{i_{1}-k}X_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}\in M
for all
1\leq k\leq i_{1}
. (Note:
X_{1}
here plays the role of
X_{n}
in Lemma
3.1
.) Since
c_{j}>0
, by Lucas’s theorem,
\binom{i_{1}}{p^{j}}\neq 0
, whence
X_{n}^{p^{j}}X_{1}^{i_{1}-p^{j}}X_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}\in M
. Then by Lemma
3.2
,
X_{1}^{i_{1}}X_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}\in M
. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

M
is generated over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
by a set of monomials.

Proof.

This is a restatement of Lemma
3.3
.

For
{\boldsymbol{i}}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\Omega_{q,n,r}
, write

i_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}i_{jk}p^{k},\quad 0\leq i_{jk}\leq p-1,

and let

D({\boldsymbol{i}})=\left[\begin{matrix}i_{10}&\cdots&i_{1,m-1}\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr i_{n0}&\cdots&i_{n,m-1}\end{matrix}\right].

Define
T({\boldsymbol{i}})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
by

(3.1)

\displaystyle T({\boldsymbol{i}})\,

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{0}\sum_{j=1}^{n}i_{jk}p^{k},\,\sum_{k=0}^{1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}i_{jk}p^{k},\,\dots,\,\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}i_{jk}p^{k}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{matrix}1&\cdots&1\end{matrix}\right]D({\boldsymbol{i}})\left[\begin{matrix}p^{0}&p^{0}&\cdots&p^{0}\cr&p^{1}&\cdots&p^{1}\cr&&\ddots&\vdots\cr&&&p^{m-1}\end{matrix}\right].

In the above,

\left[\begin{matrix}1&\cdots&1\end{matrix}\right]D({\boldsymbol{i}})

Let
T({\boldsymbol{i}})=(t_{0},\dots,t_{m-1})

(3.2)

t_{k}+p^{k+1}\Bigl(\Bigl\lfloor\frac{i_{1}}{p^{k+1}}\Bigr\rfloor+\cdots+\Bigl\lfloor\frac{i_{n}}{p^{k+1}}\Bigr\rfloor\Bigr)=r,\quad 0\leq k\leq m-1,

and hence
t_{m-1}=r
and

t_{k}\equiv r\pmod{p^{k+1}},\quad 0\leq k\leq m-2.

The image set
T(\Omega_{q,n,r})
consists of
m
-tuples
(t_{0},\dots,t_{m-1})
satisfying the following conditions:

(3.3)

\begin{cases}t_{m-1}=r,\cr t_{k}\equiv r\pmod{p^{k+1}},\quad 0\leq k\leq m-2,\cr 0\leq t_{0}\leq n(p-1),\vskip 1.99997pt\cr 0\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{k}}(t_{k}-t_{k-1})\leq n(p-1),\quad 1\leq k\leq m-1.\end{cases}

For
{\boldsymbol{t}}=(t_{0},\dots,t_{m-1}),{\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}=(t_{0}^{\prime},\dots,t_{m-1}^{\prime})\in T(\Omega_{q,n,r})
, define
{\boldsymbol{t}}\leq{\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}
and
A\in\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Then in
H_{q}(r,n)
,

(3.4)

A(X^{\boldsymbol{i}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{j}}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}\cr T({\boldsymbol{j}})\leq T({\boldsymbol{i}})\end{subarray}}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{j}}}X^{{\boldsymbol{j}}},

where
\alpha_{\boldsymbol{j}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proof.

Let
{\boldsymbol{i}}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
. If
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})A=(\lambda X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})
, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, then
A(X^{\boldsymbol{i}})=\lambda^{i_{1}}X^{\boldsymbol{i}}
. If
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})A=(X_{2},X_{1},X_{3},\dots,X_{n})
, then
A(X^{\boldsymbol{i}})=X^{\boldsymbol{j}}
, where
{\boldsymbol{j}}=(i_{2},i_{1},i_{3},\dots,i_{n})\in\Omega_{q,n,r}
and
T({\boldsymbol{j}})=T({\boldsymbol{i}})
. It remains to consider the case
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})A=(X_{1}+X_{2},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})
. We have

\displaystyle A(X^{\boldsymbol{i}})\,

\displaystyle=(X_{1}+X_{2})^{i_{1}}X_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots X_{n}^{i_{n}}=\sum_{l}\binom{i_{1}}{l}X_{1}^{l}X_{2}^{i_{1}-l}X_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots X_{n}^{i_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}-(q-1)\leq l\leq i_{1}}\binom{i_{1}}{l}X_{1}^{l}X_{2}^{i_{1}+i_{2}-l}X_{3}^{i_{3}}\cdots X_{n}^{i_{n}}.

Fix
l
such that
i_{1}+i_{2}-(q-1)\leq l\leq i_{1}
and
\binom{i_{1}}{l}\neq 0
and let

{\boldsymbol{j}}=(l,i_{1}+i_{2}-l,i_{3},\dots,i_{n})\in\Omega_{q,n,r}.

We want to show that
T({\boldsymbol{j}})\leq T({\boldsymbol{i}})
. Let
T({\boldsymbol{i}})=(t_{0},\dots,t_{m-1})
and
T({\boldsymbol{j}})=(t_{0}^{\prime},\dots,t_{m-1}^{\prime})
. Let
0\leq k\leq m-1
. Write
i_{1}=ap^{k+1}+b
and
l=up^{k+1}+v
, where
a,b,u,v\in\mathbb{Z}
and
0\leq b,v<p^{k+1}
. Since
\binom{i_{1}}{l}\neq 0
, by Lucas’s theorem,
v\leq b
. Thus

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{l}{p^{k+1}}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{i_{1}+i_{2}-l}{p^{k+1}}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{i_{3}}{p^{k+1}}\right\rfloor+\cdots+\left\lfloor\frac{i_{n}}{p^{k+1}}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{up^{k+1}+v}{p^{k+1}}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{(a-u)p^{k+1}+b-v+i_{2}}{p^{k+1}}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{i_{3}}{p^{k+1}}\right\rfloor+\cdots+\left\lfloor\frac{i_{n}}{p^{k+1}}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle\geq u+a-u+\left\lfloor\frac{i_{2}}{p^{k+1}}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{i_{3}}{p^{k+1}}\right\rfloor+\cdots+\left\lfloor\frac{i_{n}}{p^{k+1}}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{i_{1}}{p^{k+1}}\right\rfloor+\cdots+\left\lfloor\frac{i_{n}}{p^{k+1}}\right\rfloor.

This means, in light of (
3.2
), that
t_{k}^{\prime}\leq t_{k}
. Therefore,
T({\boldsymbol{j}})\leq T({\boldsymbol{i}})
.
∎

For
{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}
, we describe an operation on the matrix
D({\boldsymbol{i}})
called
digit transfer
: Take two entries
i_{j_{1},k}
and
i_{j_{2},k}
in the same column with
i_{j_{1},k}>0
and
i_{j_{2},k}<p-1
. Replace
i_{j_{1},k}
with
i_{j_{1},k}-1
and
i_{j_{2},k}
with
i_{j_{2},k}+1
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
M
be a GL-submodule of
H_{q}(r,n)
such that
X^{\boldsymbol{i}}\in M
, where
{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}
. If
{\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}
is such that

can be obtained from

through a digit transfer, then

.

.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
M
be a GL-submodule of
H_{q}(r,n)
such that
X^{\boldsymbol{i}}\in M
, where
{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}
. Then
X^{{\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime}}\in M
for all
{\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}
with
T({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime})\leq T({\boldsymbol{i}})
.

Proof.

Let
{\boldsymbol{i}}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
and
{\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime}=(i_{1}^{\prime},\dots,i_{n}^{\prime})
and let

i_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}i_{jk}p^{k}\quad\text{and}\quad i_{j}^{\prime}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}i_{jk}^{\prime}p^{k}

be the base
p
expansions of
i_{j}
and
i_{j}^{\prime}
, respectively, that is,
D({\boldsymbol{i}})=(i_{jk})
and
D({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime})=(i_{jk}^{\prime})
.

1^{\circ}
First assume that
T({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime})=T({\boldsymbol{i}})
. Since
D({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime})
and
D({\boldsymbol{i}})
have the same column sums,
D({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime})
can be obtained from
D({\boldsymbol{i}})
through a finite number of digit transfers. Therefore, by Lemma
3.6
,
X^{{\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime}}\in M
.

2^{\circ}

Now assume that
T({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime})\lneqq T({\boldsymbol{i}})
. Using induction on the partial order
\leq
, it suffices to show that there exists
{\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime\prime}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}
such that
X^{{\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime\prime}}\in M
and
T({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime})\leq T({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime\prime})\lneqq T({\boldsymbol{i}})
.
Write
T({\boldsymbol{i}})=(t_{0},\dots,t_{m-1})
and
T({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime})=(t_{0}^{\prime},\dots,t_{m-1}^{\prime})
and assume that
t_{k}=t_{k}^{\prime}
for
0\leq k<l
but
t_{l}>t_{l}^{\prime}
.

Let

(s_{0},\dots,s_{m-1})=\left[\begin{matrix}1&\cdots&1\end{matrix}\right]D({\boldsymbol{i}})

and

(s_{0}^{\prime},\dots,s_{m-1}^{\prime})=\left[\begin{matrix}1&\cdots&1\end{matrix}\right]D({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime}).

We claim that
s_{l}\geq p
. Since
s_{l}p^{l}=t_{l}-t_{l-1}
and
s_{l}^{\prime}p^{l}=t_{l}^{\prime}-t_{l-1}^{\prime}
, we have
s_{l}-s_{l}^{\prime}=p^{-l}(t_{l}-t_{l}^{\prime})
. It follows that
s_{l}-s_{l}^{\prime}>0
and
s_{l}-s_{l}^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
since
t_{l}\equiv r\equiv t_{l}^{\prime}\pmod{p^{l+1}}
(by (
3.3
)). Thus
s_{l}\geq p
.

We claim that there is some
k
with
l<k\leq m-1
, such that

(3.5)

\left[\begin{matrix}i_{1k}\cr\vdots\cr i_{nk}\end{matrix}\right]\neq\left[\begin{matrix}p-1\cr\vdots\cr p-1\end{matrix}\right].

Otherwise,
s_{k}\geq s_{k}^{\prime}
for all
l<k\leq m-1
. Then

(3.6)

t_{m-1}=t_{l}+s_{l+1}p^{l+1}+\cdots+s_{m-1}p^{m-1}>t_{l}^{\prime}+s_{l+1}^{\prime}p^{l+1}+\cdots+s_{m-1}^{\prime}p^{m-1}=t_{m-1}^{\prime},

which is impossible since
t_{m-1}=t_{m-1}^{\prime}=r
. Let
u
be the smallest
k
(
l<k\leq m-1
) satisfying (
3.5
). Then, through digit transfers, we may write

\left[\begin{matrix}i_{1l}&\cdots&i_{1u}\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr i_{nl}&\cdots&i_{nu}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}p-1,&p-1&\cdots&p-1&i_{1u}\cr i_{2l}&p-1&\cdots&p-1&i_{2u}\cr\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\cr i_{nl}&p-1&\cdots&p-1&i_{nu}\end{matrix}\right],

where
i_{2l}>0
and
i_{1u}<p-1
. Similar to (
3.6
), we have
t_{k}>t_{k}^{\prime}
for
l\leq k<u
. Let

{\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime\prime}=(i_{1}+p^{l},i_{2}-p^{l},i_{3},\dots,i_{n})\in\Omega_{q,n,r}.

By Lemma
3.2
,
X^{{\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime\prime}}\in M
. Write
D({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime\prime})=(i_{jk}^{\prime\prime})
. Then
i_{jk}^{\prime\prime}=i_{jk}
for
0\leq k<l
and
u<k\leq m-1
. For
l\leq k\leq u
, we have

\left[\begin{matrix}i_{1l}^{\prime\prime}&\cdots&i_{1u}^{\prime\prime}\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr i_{nl}^{\prime\prime}&\cdots&i_{nu}^{\prime\prime}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}0&0&\cdots&0&i_{iu}+1\cr i_{2l}-1&p-1&\cdots&p-1&i_{2u}\cr\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\cr i_{nl}&p-1&\cdots&p-1&i_{nu}\end{matrix}\right].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}1&\cdots&1\end{matrix}\right]D({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=(s_{0},\dots,s_{l-1},s_{l}-p,s_{l+1}-(p-1),\dots,s_{u-1}-(p-1),s_{u}+1,s_{u+1},\dots,s_{m-1}).

Hence
T({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime\prime})=(t_{0}^{\prime\prime},\dots,t_{m}^{\prime\prime})
, where

t_{k}^{\prime\prime}=\begin{cases}t_{k}-(p^{l+1}+(p-1)p^{l+1}+\cdots+(p-1)p^{k})=t_{k}-p^{k+1}\quad\text{if}\ l\leq k<u,\cr t_{k}\quad\text{if}\ 0\leq k<l\ \text{or}\ u\leq k\leq m-1.\end{cases}

Clearly,
T({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime\prime})\lneqq T({\boldsymbol{i}})
.

l\leq k<u
,
t_{k}>t_{k}^{\prime}
and
t_{k}\equiv t_{k}^{\prime}\pmod{p^{k+1}}
, whence
t_{k}\geq t_{k}^{\prime}+p^{k+1}
. Therefore
t_{k}^{\prime\prime}\geq t_{k}^{\prime}
for all
k
, i.e.,
T({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime\prime})\geq T({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime})
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Definition 3.8
.

A subset
I\subset T(\Omega_{q,n,r})
is called an ideal of the partially ordered set
(T(\Omega_{q,n,r}),\leq)
if for
{\boldsymbol{t}},{\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}\in T(\Omega_{q,n,r})
with
{\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}\leq{\boldsymbol{t}}
,
{\boldsymbol{t}}\in I
implies
{\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}\in I
.

For each ideal
I
of
(T(\Omega_{q,n,r}),\leq)
, define

(3.7)

M(I)=\text{the $\mathbb{F}_{q}$-linear span of}\ \{X^{\boldsymbol{i}}:{\boldsymbol{i}}\in T^{-1}(I)\}.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be the set of all ideals of
(T(\Omega_{q,n,r}),\leq)
and
\mathcal{M}
be the set of all
\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-submodules of
H_{q}(r,n)
.
Combining several previous lemmas, we arrive at the following main result.

Theorem 3.9
.

The map

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Phi:&\mathcal{I}&\longrightarrow&\mathcal{M}\cr&I&\longmapsto&M(I)\end{array}

is a bijection.

Proof.

For each
M\in\mathcal{M}
, define

(3.8)

\Psi(M)=\{T({\boldsymbol{i}}):{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\Omega_{q,n,r},X^{\boldsymbol{i}}\in M\}.

By Lemma
3.7
,
\Psi(M)
is an ideal of
(T(\Omega_{q,n,r}),\leq)
. Hence we have a map
\Psi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{I}
. It remains to show that both
\Phi\circ\Psi
and
\Psi\circ\Phi
are identity maps.

Let
M\in\mathcal{M}
. If
{\boldsymbol{i}}\in T^{-1}(\Psi(M))
, then
T({\boldsymbol{i}})\in\Psi(M)
. By (
3.8
),
T({\boldsymbol{i}})=T({\boldsymbol{j}})
for some
{\boldsymbol{j}}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}
with
X^{\boldsymbol{j}}\in M
. By Lemma
3.7
,
X^{\boldsymbol{i}}\in M
. Since
M(\Psi(M))
is the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear span of
\{X^{\boldsymbol{i}}:{\boldsymbol{i}}\in T^{-1}(\Psi(M))\}
, we have
M(\Psi(M))\subset M
. On the other hand, if
X^{\boldsymbol{i}}
is a monomial in
M
, by (
3.8
),
T({\boldsymbol{i}})\in\Psi(M)
, i.e.,
{\boldsymbol{i}}\in T^{-1}(\Psi(M))
. Then by (
3.7
),
X^{\boldsymbol{i}}\in M(\Psi(M))
. Since
M
is generated over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
by a set of monomials (Corollary
3.4
), we have
M\subset M(\Psi(M))
. Therefore,
M(\Psi(M))=M
for all
M\in\mathcal{M}
, whence
\Phi\circ\Psi
is the identity map.

Let
I\in\mathcal{I}
. If
{\boldsymbol{t}}\in\Psi(M(I))
, then
{\boldsymbol{t}}=T({\boldsymbol{i}})
for some
{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\Omega_{q,n,r}
with
X^{\boldsymbol{i}}\in M(I)
. By (
3.7
),
{\boldsymbol{i}}\in T^{-1}(I)
. Thus
{\boldsymbol{t}}=T({\boldsymbol{i}})\in I
. So
\Psi((M(I))\subset I
. On the other hand, if
{\boldsymbol{t}}\in I
, choose
{\boldsymbol{i}}\in T^{-1}({\boldsymbol{t}})
. Then by (
3.7
),
X^{\boldsymbol{i}}\in M(I)
. By (
3.8
),
{\boldsymbol{t}}=T({\boldsymbol{i}})\in\Psi(M(I))
. So
I\subset\Psi(M(I))
. Therefore,
\Psi(M(I))=I
for all
I\in\mathcal{I}
, whence
\Psi\circ\Phi
is the identity map.
∎

Each ideal
I
of the partially ordered set
(T(\Omega_{q,n,r}),\leq)
is determined by its

which is the set of maximal elements in

. On the other hand, each subset

-submodules of

is equivalent to the enumeration of subsets of pairwise noncomparable elements of

. Let
\mathcal{B}
denote the set of all of subsets of pairwise noncomparable elements of
T(\Omega_{q,n,r})
. For

, the corresponding ideal of

and the corresponding GL-module

is the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span of all

such that

.

Example 3.10
.

Let
p=2

4.
Factors of the Composition Series of
H_{q}(r,n)

We follow the notation introduced in Section 3. In addition, for
I,I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}
, we write
I\subset_{\max}I^{\prime}
to mean that
I\subsetneq I^{\prime}
and there is no
I^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{I}
such that
I\subsetneq I^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq I^{\prime}
.

A composition series of
H_{q}(r,n)
is given by

(4.1)

M(I_{0})\subset M(I_{1})\subset\cdots\subset M(I_{N}),

where
I_{0},I_{1},\dots,I_{N}\in\mathcal{I}
are such that

(4.2)

\emptyset=I_{0}\subset_{\max}I_{1}\subset_{\max}\cdots\subset_{\max}I_{N}=T(\Omega_{q,n,r}).

It is clear that
I_{i}\subset_{\max}I_{i+1}
if and only if
I_{i}=I_{i+1}\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\}
for some maximal element
{\boldsymbol{t}}
in
I_{i+1}
. Therefore, the composition series (
4.1
) can be obtained as follows: First, let
I_{N}=T(\Omega_{q,n,r})
. Choose a maximal element
{\boldsymbol{t}}_{N}\in I_{N}
and let
I_{N-1}=I_{N}\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}_{N}\}
. Choose a maximal element
{\boldsymbol{t}}_{N-1}
in
I_{N-1}
and let
I_{N-2}=I_{N-1}\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}_{N-1}\}
. Continue this way until
I_{0}=\emptyset
. Clearly,
{\boldsymbol{t}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{t}}_{N-1},\dots,{\boldsymbol{t}}_{1}
is an enumeration of all elements in
T(\Omega_{n,q,r})
, whence
N=|T(\Omega_{q,n,r})|
. The factors of the composition series (
4.1
) are

M(I_{i})\,/\,M(I_{i-1})=M(I_{i})\,/\,M(I_{i}\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}_{i}\}),\quad 1\leq i\leq N.

The structure of the module
M(I_{i})\,/\,M(I_{i}\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}_{i}\})
depends only on
{\boldsymbol{t}}_{i}
but not on
I_{i}
. For
{\boldsymbol{t}}\in T(\Omega_{q,n,r})
, let

I({\boldsymbol{t}})=\{{\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}\in T(\Omega_{q,n,r}):{\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}\leq{\boldsymbol{t}}\}\in\mathcal{I}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
I\in\mathcal{I}
and
{\boldsymbol{t}}
be a maximal element of
I
. Then

M(I)\,/\,M(I\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\})\cong M(I({\boldsymbol{t}}))\,/\,M(I({\boldsymbol{t}})\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\}).

Proof.

Define a GL-module map

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\phi:&M(I({\boldsymbol{t}}))&\longrightarrow&M(I)\,/\,M(I\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\})\cr&f&\longmapsto&f+M(I\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\}).\cr\end{array}

Since
M(I)=M(I\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\})+M(I({\boldsymbol{t}}))
,
\phi
is onto. Since
I({\boldsymbol{t}})\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\}\subset I\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\}
, we have
M(I({\boldsymbol{t}})\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\})\subset M(I\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\})
, whence
M(I({\boldsymbol{t}})\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\})\subset\ker\phi
. Thus
\phi
induces an onto GL-module map

\bar{\phi}:M(I({\boldsymbol{t}}))\,/\,M(I({\boldsymbol{t}})\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\})\longrightarrow M(I)\,/\,M(I\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\}).

Since
I({\boldsymbol{t}})\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\}\subset_{\max}I({\boldsymbol{t}})
,
M(I({\boldsymbol{t}}))\,/\,M(I({\boldsymbol{t}})\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\})
is an irreducible GL-module. It follows that
\bar{\phi}
is an isomorphism.
∎

For
{\boldsymbol{t}}\in T(\Omega_{q,n,r})
, let

\mathfrak{M}({\boldsymbol{t}})=M(I({\boldsymbol{t}}))\,/\,M(I({\boldsymbol{t}})\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{t}}\}).

The structure of the GL-module
\mathfrak{M}({\boldsymbol{t}})
is easy to describe (with a little abuse of notation). It has a basis
\{X^{\boldsymbol{i}}:{\boldsymbol{i}}\in T^{-1}({\boldsymbol{t}})\}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. When
A\in\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
acts on
X^{\boldsymbol{i}}
, in the expansion (
3.4
) of
A(X^{\boldsymbol{i}})
, only the terms
\alpha_{\boldsymbol{j}}X^{\boldsymbol{j}}
with
{\boldsymbol{j}}\in T^{-1}({\boldsymbol{t}})
are kept. We have
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathfrak{M}({\boldsymbol{t}})=|T^{-1}({\boldsymbol{t}})|
, which can be made explicit.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{t}}=(t_{0},\dots,t_{m-1})\in T(\Omega_{q,n,r})
and

(4.3)

(s_{0},\dots,s_{m-1})=\Bigl(t_{0},\frac{t_{1}-t_{0}}{p},\dots,\frac{t_{m-1}-t_{m-2}}{p^{m-1}}\Bigr).

Then

(4.4)

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathfrak{M}({\boldsymbol{t}})=|T^{-1}({\boldsymbol{t}})|=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\Bigl(\sum_{0\leq k\leq s_{j}/p}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n-1+s_{j}-kp}{n-1}\Bigr).

Proof.

It is straightforward from (
4.3
) that

(4.5)

(t_{0},\dots,t_{m-1})=(s_{0},\dots,s_{m-1})\left[\begin{matrix}p^{0}&p^{0}&\cdots&p^{0}\cr&p^{1}&\cdots&p^{1}\cr&&\ddots&\vdots\cr&&&p^{m-1}\end{matrix}\right].

By (
3.1
),
{\boldsymbol{i}}\in T^{-1}({\boldsymbol{t}})
if and only if

\left[\begin{matrix}1&\cdots&1\end{matrix}\right]D({\boldsymbol{i}})\left[\begin{matrix}p^{0}&p^{0}&\cdots&p^{0}\cr&p^{1}&\cdots&p^{1}\cr&&\ddots&\vdots\cr&&&p^{m-1}\end{matrix}\right]=T({\boldsymbol{i}})=(t_{0},\dots,t_{m-1}).

In light of (
4.5
), this happens if and only if

(4.6)

\left[\begin{matrix}1&\cdots&1\end{matrix}\right]D({\boldsymbol{i}})=(s_{0},\dots,s_{m-1}).

Write

D({\boldsymbol{i}})=\left[\begin{matrix}i_{10}&\cdots&i_{1,m-1}\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr i_{n0}&\cdots&i_{n,m-1}\end{matrix}\right].

Then (
4.6
) is satisfied if and only if for all
0\leq j\leq m-1
,

(4.7)

i_{1j}+\cdots+i_{nj}=s_{j},\quad 0\leq i_{1j},\dots,i_{nj}\leq p-1.

The number of
(i_{1j},\dots,i_{nj})
satisfying (
4.7
), denoted by
N_{j}
, is the coefficient of
X^{s_{j}}
in
(1+X+\cdots+X^{p-1})^{n}
. We have

\displaystyle(1+X+\cdots+X^{p-1})^{n}=\Bigl(\frac{1-X^{p}}{1-X}\Bigr)^{n}=(1-X^{p})^{n}(1-X)^{-n}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum_{k}\binom{n}{k}(-X^{p})^{k}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{l}\binom{-n}{l}(-X)^{l}\Bigr)=\sum_{k,l}\binom{n}{k}\binom{-n}{l}(-1)^{k+l}X^{kp+l}.

Hence

\displaystyle N_{j}\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{kp+l=s_{j}}\binom{n}{k}\binom{-n}{l}(-1)^{k+l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{kp+l=s_{j}}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+l-1}{l}\kern 20.00003pt(\text{since}\ (-1)^{l}\binom{-n}{l}=\binom{n+l-1}{l}\,)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq n\cr s_{j}-kp\geq 0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+s_{j}-kp-1}{n-1}.

By (
3.3
) and (
4.3
),
s_{j}/p\leq n(p-1)/p<n
, whence
k\leq s_{j}/p
implies
k\leq n
. Therefore, the effective range for
k
in the above sum is
0\leq k\leq s_{j}/p
. Now,

|T^{-1}({\boldsymbol{t}})|=N_{0}\cdots N_{m-1}=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\Bigl(\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n-1+s_{j}-kp}{n-1}\Bigr).

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If
{\boldsymbol{t}},{\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}\in T(\Omega_{q,n,r})
are such that
\mathfrak{M}({\boldsymbol{t}})\cong\mathfrak{M}({\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime})
, then
{\boldsymbol{t}}={\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

If
r=n(q-1)
, then
{\boldsymbol{t}}={\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}=T({\boldsymbol{i}})
, where

D({\boldsymbol{i}})=\left[\begin{matrix}p-1&\cdots&p-1\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr p-1&\cdots&p-1\end{matrix}\right].

So we assume that
r<n(q-1)
.

We use induction on
n
. When
n=1
, let
{\boldsymbol{i}}\in T^{-1}({\boldsymbol{t}})
,
{\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime}\in T^{-1}({\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime})
, and write
D({\boldsymbol{i}})=(i_{10},\dots,i_{1,m-1})
,
D({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime})=(i_{10}^{\prime},\dots,i_{1,m-1}^{\prime})
, where
0\leq i_{1j},i_{1j}^{\prime}\leq p-1
,
0\leq j\leq m-1
. Then

i_{10}p^{0}+\cdots+i_{1,m-1}p^{m-1}=r=i_{10}^{\prime}p^{0}+\cdots+i_{1,m-1}^{\prime}p^{m-1},

whence
D({\boldsymbol{i}})=D({\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime})
. Therefore,
{\boldsymbol{i}}={\boldsymbol{i}}^{\prime}
, and hence
{\boldsymbol{t}}={\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}
.

Now assume
n>0
. Since
\mathfrak{M}({\boldsymbol{t}})
is generated over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
by
X^{\boldsymbol{i}}
,

Theorem 4.4
.

The composition factors of

\mathfrak{M}({\boldsymbol{t}})

5.
Irreducible Representations of
\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

The number of irreducible representations of
\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
equals the number of
p
-regular
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-conjugacy classes of
\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
(
[
5
,
26
]
). The
p
-regular
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-conjugacy classes of
\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
are precisely the conjugacy classes of the elements whose elementary divisors are irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Such conjugacy classes are
parametrized by monic polynomials of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with nonzero constant term. Therefore, the number of irreducible representations of
\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
equals
q^{n-1}(q-1)
.

When
n=2
, the irreducible representations of
\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
were determined by Brauer and Nesbitt
[
6
]
; also see Barthel and Livné
[
2
]
.

For an arbitrary
n
, James and Kerber
[
18
, Exercise 8.4]
outlined a method for constructing all irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{q}\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-modules using Weyl modules by emulating a construction of irreducible modules over a certain superalgebra by Cater and Lusztig
[
9
]
.
However, the outlined construction in
[
18
]
is not a straightforward adaptation of that of
[
9
]
; additional technical steps are needed to prove the claims in the construction of
[
18
]
. References do not seem to be immediately available and we plan to give a detailed account of the construction in a separate paper.
The irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{q}\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-modules constructed from Weyl modules are not entirely explicit. For example, their dimensions are not known.

The factors
\mathfrak{M}({\boldsymbol{t}})
of the composition series of
H_{q}(r,n)
that we constructed in Section 4 only account for a small portion of the irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{q}\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-modules. However, they are explicit, and in particular, their dimensions are known. The corresponding representations of these modules belong to the class of polynomial representations of the general linear group in the sense that the entries of their representation matrices are homogeneous polynomials in the entries of the elements of the general linear group. When
F
is an infinite field, the irreducible polynomial representations of
\text{GL}(n,F)
have been determined
[
13
]
. However, when
F
is finite, the knowledge of such representations is incomplete.

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we considered two separate questions about the AGL-module structure of the quotient
H_{q}(r,n)=R_{q}(r,n)/R_{q}(r-1,n)
of two consecutive Reed-Muller codes. In the first question, we proved a duality between
H_{q}(r,n)
and
H_{q}(r^{\prime},n)
, where
r+r^{\prime}=n(q-1)
, which generalizes the known result for
q=2
. The general duality is a useful tool for studying
q
-ary functions. In the second question, we determined all submodules of
H_{q}(r,n)
. This resolves a long-standing question about the affine invariant subcodes of the Reed-Muller code and provides an explicit family of irreducible representations of
\text{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_{q})
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Dynamics of radial threshold solutions for generalized energy-critical Hartree equation
Xuemei Li, Chenxi Liu, Xingdong Tang, Guixiang Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, we study long time dynamics of radial threshold solutions for the focusing, generalized energy-critical Hartree equation and classify all radial threshold solutions. The main arguments are the spectral theory of the linearized operator, the modulational analysis and the concentration compactness rigidity argument developed by T. Duyckaerts and F. Merle to classify all threshold solutions for the energy critical NLS and NLW in [ 18 , 19 ] , later by D. Li and X. Zhang in [ 43 , 44 ] in higher dimensions. The new ingredient here is to solve the nondegeneracy of positive bubble solutions with nonlocal structure in H ˙ 1 ​ ( ℝ N ) \dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) (i.e. the spectral assumption in [ 51 ] ) by the nondegeneracy result of positive bubble solution in L ∞ ​ ( ℝ N ) L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) in [ 45 ] and the Moser iteration method in [ 16 ] , which is related to the spectral analysis of the linearized operator with nonlocal structure, and plays a key role in the construction of the special threshold solutions, and the classification of all threshold solutions.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the focusing, generalized energy-critical Hartree equation

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+\left(I_{\lambda}*|u|^{p}\right)|u|^{p-2}u=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.1)

where
N\geq 3
,
0<\lambda<N
,
\lambda\leq 4
,
p=\frac{2N-\lambda}{N-2}\geq 2
and the Riesz-potential stands for

I_{\lambda}(x):=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\lambda}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{N-\lambda}{2})2^{N-\lambda}|x|^{\lambda}}.

The generalized Hartree equation can be considered as a classical limit of a field equation and it describes a quantum mechanical non-relativistic many-boson system interacting through a two body potential
V(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{\lambda}}
, see
[
28
]
. As for the mean-field limit of many-body quantum systems, we can refer to the work by Hepp in
[
32
]
, see also
[
9
,
10
,
26
,
28
,
61
]
. Lieb and Yau use it to develop the theory for stellar collapse in
[
49
]
.

The equation (
1.1
) enjoys several symmetries: If
u(t,x)
is a solution, then

(a)

so is
\delta^{\frac{N-2}{2}}u(\delta^{2}t,\delta x)
,
\delta>0
by scaling invariance;

(b)

so is
u(t+t_{0},x)
for
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
by time translation invariance;

(c)

so is
u(t,x+x_{0})
for
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
by spatial translation invariance;

(d)

so is
e^{i\theta_{0}}u(t,x)
,
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
by phase rotation invariance;

(e)

so is
\overline{u(-t,x)}
by time reversal.

The Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) was developed by A. K. Arora and S. Roudenko in
[
1
,
3
,
5
,
13
]
. Namely, if
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there
exists a unique solution defined on a maximal interval

I=\left(-T_{-}(u),T_{+}(u)\right)
and the energy

\displaystyle E(u(t)):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big|\nabla u(t,x)\big|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(I_{\lambda}*|u|^{p}\right)\big|u(t,x)\big|^{p}\ dx

(1.2)

is conserved by time translation invariance of equation (
1.1
). The exponent

p=\frac{2N-\lambda}{N-2}

is energy-critical exponent since the scaling transform

u(t,x)\rightarrow\delta^{\frac{N-2}{2}}u\left(\delta^{2}t,\delta x\right),\;\delta>0

makes the equation (
1.1
) and the energy
(
1.2
) invariant.

For long time behavior of solutions for (
1.1
), the ground state plays an important role, which is the solution of the elliptic equation with nonlocal potential

\displaystyle-\Delta W(x)=\left(I_{\lambda}*W^{p}\right)\;W^{p-1},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.3)

According to
[
17
]

[

[
47
,
48

\mathbb{R}^{N}
by using the stereographic projection and sharp Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality on the sphere in
[
45
]
. That is,

Proposition 1.1

(

\displaystyle\;C(N,\lambda)\big\|\nabla f\big\|^{2}_{2},

C(N,\lambda)=\left[(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}})^{N-\lambda}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\lambda}{2})}{\Gamma(N-\frac{\lambda}{2})}\big(\frac{\Gamma(N)}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}\big)^{\frac{N-\lambda}{N}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2p}}\cdot\left[\frac{N(N-2)}{4}2^{2/N}\pi^{\frac{N+1}{N}}\Gamma(\frac{N+1}{2})^{-\frac{2}{N}}\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}

(1.4)

3.
Existence of special threshold solutions

In this section, we follow the arguments in
[

[

to show the existence of special threshold solutions

and

in the energy space, we can also refer to

and a series of the elliptic iterations, and then obtain the special solution

3.1.
A family of approximate solutions converging to
W
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{R}
. There exists a sequence of functions

(\mathcal{Z}^{a}_{j})_{j\geq 1}
in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\mathcal{Z}^{a}_{1}=a\mathcal{Y}_{+}
and if

\displaystyle h^{a}_{k}(t,x):=\sum^{k}_{j=1}e^{-je_{0}t}\mathcal{Z}^{a}_{j},\;k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+},

(3.1)

then we have as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{k}:=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h^{a}_{k}+\mathcal{L}h^{a}_{k}-R(h^{a}_{k})=O(e^{-(k+1)e_{0}t})\quad\text{in }\;\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(3.2)

Remark 3.2
.

Let

\displaystyle U^{a}_{k}(t,x):=W(x)+h^{a}_{k}(t,x),

(3.3)

then as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, we have the high-order error estimate

\displaystyle\epsilon_{k}:=

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}U^{a}_{k}+\Delta U^{a}_{k}+[I_{\lambda}*|U^{a}_{k}|^{p}]|U^{a}_{k}|^{p-2}U^{a}_{k}=O(e^{-(k+1)e_{0}t})\quad\text{in }\;\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Sketch of proof.

Using the eigenfunctions of the real eigenvalues of the linearized operator, we can define
h^{a}_{1}:=e^{-e_{0}t}\mathcal{Z}^{a}_{1}
. By the definition of (
2.7
), we know that
R
has at
least the quadratic term, that is,
R\left(e^{-e_{0}t}\mathcal{Z}^{a}_{1}\right)=O(e^{-2e_{0}t})
, which yields (
3.2
) for
k=1
. we can use a series of the elliptic iterations to cancel low-order error term, which together with the induction argument implies the existence of
\mathcal{Z}^{a}_{j}
,
j=2,\ldots
.
∎

3.2.
Construction of special solutions near an approximate solution.

Now we show the
existence of special radial threshold solution which can be proved by the fixed point argument.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

. There exist

of

\displaystyle\big\|U^{a}(t)-W-ae^{-e_{0}t}\mathcal{Y}_{+}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq e^{-\frac{3}{2}e_{0}t}.

(3.5)

Sketch of proof.

The proof depends on the fixed point argument and Strichartz estimate in Lemma
2.1
. Let
e:=U^{a}-U^{a}_{k}=h^{a}-h^{a}_{k}
and it satisfies

\partial_{t}e+\mathcal{L}e=R(h^{a}_{k}+e)-R(h^{a}_{k})-\varepsilon_{k},

where

is defined in Remark
3.2
. This equation may be rewritten as

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}e+\Delta e=

\displaystyle Ve+iR(h^{a}_{k}+e)-iR(h^{a}_{k})-i\varepsilon_{k}.

Let us define the mapping

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{k}(e)(t):=-\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}\Big(iVe(s)-R\big(h^{a}_{k}(s)+e(s)\big)+R(h^{a}_{k}(s))+\varepsilon_{k}(s)\Big)ds.

Thus the existence of a solution

(

\displaystyle\forall\;t\geq t_{0},\quad e(t)=\mathcal{M}_{k}(e)(t)\text{ and }\|e\|_{l(t,+\infty)}\leq e^{-\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)e_{0}t}.

Let us fix

. Denote

\displaystyle B_{l}^{k}

\displaystyle:=\big\{e\in Z(t_{0},+\infty),\;\nabla e\in S(t_{0},+\infty);\;\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}e^{\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)e_{0}t}\|e\|_{l(t,+\infty)}\leq 1\big\}.

2.8

Finally, by the finite propagation of finite energy solutions, we can obtain that the analogue estimate

from the derivative estimate of the localized mass to that in
[
18

if

and
\psi=0
if

Then, we have

\displaystyle F_{R}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{R}Im\int U^{a}\nabla\bar{U}^{a}\cdot(\nabla\psi)(\frac{x}{R})dx=\frac{2}{R}Im\int W\nabla(\bar{U}^{a}-W)\cdot(\nabla\psi)(\frac{x}{R})dx

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{R}Im\int(U^{a}-W)\nabla W\cdot(\nabla\psi)(\frac{x}{R})dx+\frac{2}{R}Im\int(U^{a}-W)\nabla(\bar{U}^{a}-\bar{W})\cdot(\nabla\psi)(\frac{x}{R})dx

Appendix A
Nondegeneracy of energy solution of equation (
1.5
)

From Remark
1.5
(b) and (c) , it suffices to show

-regularity of

solution of equation (

16

to show this result in this appendix.

Recalling equation (

satisfies

(A.1)

Let
\beta\geq 1,\;T>0
, we define the cutoff function

\varphi(t)=\begin{cases}\beta T^{\beta-1}(t-T)+T^{\beta},&if\;t\geq T;\\
|t|^{\beta},&if\;-T\leq t\leq T;\\
\beta T^{\beta-1}(T-t)+T^{\beta},&if\;t\leq-T.\end{cases}

(A.2)

Since
\varphi
is convex and Lipschitz, we have

(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi(u)\leq\varphi^{\prime}(u)(-\Delta)^{s}u,\;s\in[0,1]

(A.3)

in the weak sense.
By the equation (
A.1
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta u)\varphi(u)\varphi^{\prime}(u)\;dx=

\displaystyle\,(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(I_{\lambda}*W^{p}\right)W^{p-2}u\varphi(u)\varphi^{\prime}(u)\;dx

\displaystyle\,\,+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[I_{\lambda}*(W^{p-1}u)\right]W^{p-1}\varphi(u)\varphi^{\prime}(u)\;dx.

Using (
A.3
) and integration by parts, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta u)\varphi(u)\varphi^{\prime}(u)\;dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}(\varphi^{\prime}(u))^{2}\;dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\varphi(u)\varphi^{\prime\prime}(u)\;dx.

By (
A.2
), we know that the last integral is nonnegative, therefore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}(\varphi^{\prime}(u))^{2}\;dx\leq

\displaystyle\,(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(I_{\lambda}*W^{p}\right)W^{p-2}|u||\varphi(u)\varphi^{\prime}(u)|\;dx

\displaystyle\,\,+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[I_{\lambda}*(W^{p-1}|u|)\right]W^{p-1}\varphi(u)|\varphi^{\prime}(u)|\;dx.

Applying the weak-Young inequality and noting that
W=c\left(\frac{t}{t^{2}+|x|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\;p>(N-2)/N
, we have

I_{\lambda}*W^{p}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Using that
\varphi(u)\varphi^{\prime}(u)\leq\beta u^{2\beta-1}
and the Hölder inequality, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(I_{\lambda}*W^{p}\right)W^{p-2}|u||\varphi(u)\varphi^{\prime}(u)|\;dx\leq C\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2\beta}.

Similarly, we can get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[I_{\lambda}*(W^{p-1}|u|)\right]W^{p-1}\varphi(u)|\varphi^{\prime}(u)|\;dx\leq C\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2\beta}.

Thus, by the Sobolev inequality, we have

\|\varphi(u)\|_{L^{2^{*}}}^{2}\leq\|\varphi(u)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}(\varphi^{\prime}(u))^{2}\;dx\leq C\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2\beta},

where

.
Let
T\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\beta}\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\leq C\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2\beta},

where C changing from line to line, but independent of
\beta
. Therefore,

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}\beta}}\leq(C\beta)^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}.

(A.4)

Because

, we know

by the Sobolev inequality. Therefore, we can take
\beta_{1}=\frac{N}{N-2}\geq 1
. From now on, we can follow exactly the iteration argument. That is, we define
\beta_{m+1},m\geq 1
, so that

2\beta_{m+1}=2^{*}\beta_{m}.

Thus,

\beta_{m+1}=\left(\frac{2^{*}}{2}\right)^{m}\beta_{1},

and replacing in equation (
A.4
), we know that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2\beta_{m+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\beta_{m+1}}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\beta_{m}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}\beta_{m}}}\leq\left(C\beta_{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2\beta_{m}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\beta_{m}}}.

we conclude that there exists a constant

Thus, we obtain

This together with the result in

51
,
67
]
.

We first show that there exists a constant
c
such that for any
radial real-valued
\dot{H}^{1}
-function
h_{1}\in\{W,\widetilde{W}\}^{\bot}
(in the sense of
\dot{H}^{1}
),

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(h_{1})\geq c\big\|h_{1}\big\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

Assume that the above inequality does not hold. Then there exists a
sequence of real-valued radial
\dot{H}^{1}
-functions
\{f_{n}\}_{n}
such
that

\displaystyle f_{n}\in H^{\bot},\quad\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\Phi_{1}(f_{n})=0,\quad\big\|f_{n}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=1.

(B.1)

Extracting a subsequence from
\{f_{n}\}
, we may assume that

\displaystyle f_{n}\rightharpoonup f_{*}\;\text{in}\;\dot{H}^{1}.

The weak convergence of
f_{n}\in H^{\bot}
to
f_{*}
implies that

f_{*}\in H^{\bot}
. In addition, by compactness, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}I_{\lambda}(x-y)W(y)^{p}W(x)^{p-2}|f_{n}(x)|^{2}\;dxdy

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}I_{\lambda}(x-y)W(y)^{p}W(x)^{p-2}|f_{*}(x)|^{2}\;dxdy,

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}I_{\lambda}(x-y)W(x)^{p-1}|f_{n}(x)|W(y)^{p-1}|f_{n}(y)|\;dxdy

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}I_{\lambda}(x-y)W(x)^{p-1}|f_{*}(x)|W(y)^{p-1}|f_{*}(y)|\;dxdy.

Thus by the Fatou lemma, (
B.1
) and Step 1, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\Phi_{1}(f_{*})\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\Phi_{1}(f_{n})=0.

f_{*}
is the solution to the following minimizing problem

\displaystyle 0=\min_{f\in\Omega\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}L_{+}f\cdot f\;dx}{\big\|f\big\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}}},\quad\Omega=\left\{f\in\dot{H}^{1}_{rad},(f,W)_{\dot{H}^{1}}=(f,\widetilde{W})_{\dot{H}^{1}}=0\right\}.

Thus for some Lagrange multipliers
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle L_{+}f_{*}=\lambda_{0}\Delta W+\lambda_{1}\Delta\widetilde{W}.

Note that
(W,\widetilde{W})_{\dot{H}^{1}}=0
and

L_{+}(\widetilde{W})=0
, we have

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f_{*}L_{+}(\widetilde{W})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(L_{+}f_{*})\widetilde{W}=\lambda_{1}\big\|\widetilde{W}\big\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}}\Longrightarrow\lambda_{1}=0.

Thus

\displaystyle L_{+}f_{*}=\lambda_{0}\Delta W=-\lambda_{0}\big(|x|^{-4}*W^{2}\big)W=\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}L_{+}W.

By
\text{Null}(L_{+})=\text{span}\{\widetilde{W}\}
in Lemma

2.12
, there exists
\mu_{1}
such that

\displaystyle f_{*}=\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}W+\mu_{1}\widetilde{W}.

Using
f_{*}\in H^{\bot}
, we get
\mu_{1}=0
and
\lambda_{0}=0
, therefore, we have

\displaystyle f_{*}=0,\;\;\text{and}\;\;f_{n}\rightharpoonup 0\;\text{in}\;\dot{H}^{1}.

Now, by compactness, we have

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}I_{\lambda}(x-y)W(y)^{p}W(x)^{p-2}|f_{n}(x)|^{2}\;dxdy\longrightarrow 0,

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}I_{\lambda}(x-y)W(x)^{p-1}|f_{n}(x)|W(y)^{p-1}|f_{n}(y)|\;dxdy\longrightarrow 0.

By
\Phi_{1}(f_{n})\rightarrow 0
in (
B.1
), we get
that

\displaystyle\big\|\nabla f_{n}\big\|_{2}\rightarrow 0,

which contradicts
\big\|f_{n}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=1
in
(
B.1
).

Using the same argument, we can show that there exists a constant

c
, such that for any real-valued radial
\dot{H}^{1}
-function
h_{2}\in\{W\}^{\bot}
, we have

\Phi_{2}(h_{2})\geq c\big\|h_{2}\big\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

This completes the proof. ∎

Appendix C
Spectral properties of the linearized operator

In this Appendix, we follow the argument in
[

,

,

to give the proof
of Proposition
2.14
.

C.1.
Existence, symmetry of the eigenfunctions

Note that

\overline{\mathcal{L}v}=-\mathcal{L}\overline{v},

so that if

e_{0}>0
is an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}
with the eigenfunction

\mathcal{Y}_{+}
,
-e_{0}
is also an eigenvalue with eigenfunction

\mathcal{Y}_{-}=\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{+}
.

Now we show the existence of
\mathcal{Y}_{+}
. Let
\mathcal{Y}_{1}=\mathrm{Re}\mathcal{Y}_{+},\mathcal{Y}_{2}=\mathrm{Im}\mathcal{Y}_{+}
, it suffices to
show that

-L_{-}\mathcal{Y}_{2}=\;e_{0}\mathcal{Y}_{1},\quad L_{+}\mathcal{Y}_{1}=\;e_{0}\mathcal{Y}_{2}.

(C.1)

From the proof of the coercivity property of
\Phi
on
H^{\bot}\cap\dot{H}^{1}_{rad}
in Proposition
2.13
, we know that
L_{-}
on

L^{2}
with domain
H^{2}
is self-adjoint and nonnegative, By Theorem
3.35 in
[
34
, Page 281]
, it has a unique square root
(L_{-})^{1/2}
with domain
H^{1}
.

Assume that there exists a function
f\in H^{4}_{rad}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}f=-e^{2}_{0}f_{1},\quad\mathcal{P}:=\big(L_{-}\big)^{1/2}\big(L_{+}\big)\big(L_{-}\big)^{1/2}.

Then taking

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{1}:=\big(L_{-}\big)^{1/2}f,\quad\mathcal{Y}_{2}:=\frac{1}{e_{0}}\big(L_{+}\big)\big(L_{-}\big)^{1/2}f

would yield a solution of (
C.1
), which implies the
existence of the radial
\mathcal{Y}_{\pm}
by the rotation
invariance of the operator
\mathcal{L}
.

It suffices to show that the operator
\mathcal{P}
on
L^{2}
with
domain
H^{4}_{rad}
has a strictly negative eigenvalue. Since

\mathcal{P}
is a relatively compact, self-adjoint, perturbation
of
\big(-\Delta\big)^{2}
, then by the Weyl theorem

[
33
,
34
]
, we know that

\displaystyle\sigma_{ess}(\mathcal{P})=[0,+\infty).

Thus we only need to show that
\mathcal{P}
has at least one
negative eigenvalue
-e^{2}_{0}
.

Lemma C.1
.

\displaystyle\sigma_{-}(\mathcal{P}):=\inf\{\big(\mathcal{P}f,f\big)_{L^{2}},f\in H^{4}_{rad},\big\|f\big\|_{L^{2}}=1\}<0.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{P}f,f\big)_{L^{2}}=\big(L_{+}F,F\big)_{L^{2}},\quad F:=\big(L_{-}\big)^{1/2}f.

Thus it suffices to find
F
such that there exists
g\in H^{4}_{rad}
,
F=(\Delta+V)g
and

\displaystyle\big(L_{+}F,F\big)_{L^{2}}<0,

(C.2)

where
V:=(I_{\lambda}*W^{p})W^{p-2}
.

We divide into two cases.

First assume that N=3, 4, in these cases
W\notin L^{2}
, we will use the localization method. Let
W_{a}:=\chi(x/a)W(x)
, where
\chi
is a smooth, radial positive function such that
\chi(r)=1
for
r\leq 1
and
\chi(r)=0
for
r\geq 2
. We first claim

\exists a>0,\;E_{a}:=\int L_{+}W_{a}W_{a}<0

(C.3)

Recall that
-\Delta W=(I_{\lambda}*W^{p})W^{p-1}
. Thus

\displaystyle L_{+}W_{a}=

\displaystyle-(p-2)(I_{\lambda}*W^{p})\chi(x/a)W^{p-1}-p\big[I_{\lambda}*(\chi(x/a)W^{p})\big]W^{p-1}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{a}\nabla\chi(x/a)\nabla W-\frac{1}{a^{2}}\Delta\chi(x/a)W.

Hence

\displaystyle\int L_{+}W_{a}W_{a}=

\displaystyle-(p-2)\int(I_{\lambda}*W^{p})\chi^{2}(x/a)W^{p}-p\int\big[I_{\lambda}*(\chi(x/a)W^{p})\big]W^{p}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{a}\int\nabla\chi(x/a)\nabla WW-\frac{1}{a^{2}}\int\Delta\chi(x/a)W^{2}.

Let
A:=\frac{2}{a}\int\nabla\chi(x/a)\nabla WW,\;B:=\frac{1}{a^{2}}\int\Delta\chi(x/a)W^{2}
. According to the explicit expression (
1.4
) of W,
W\leq C|x|^{-(N-2)}
and
\nabla W\leq C|x|^{-(N-1)}
at infinity, which gives
|(A)|+|(B)|\leq\frac{C}{a}
if
N=3
,
|(A)|+|(B)|\leq\frac{C}{a^{2}}
if
N=4
. Hence (
C.3
).

Let us fix
a
such that (
C.3
) holds. Recall that W is not in
L^{2}
. Thus
\Delta+V
is a self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}
, with domain
H^{2}
, and without eigenfunction. In particular the orthogonal of its range
R(\Delta+V)
is
0
, and thus
R(\Delta+V)
is dense in
L^{2}
. Let
\epsilon>0
, and consider
G_{\epsilon}\in H^{2}
such that

\big\|(\Delta+V)G_{\epsilon}-(\Delta+V-1)W_{a}\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq\epsilon.

Taking

F_{\epsilon}:=\big(L_{-}+1\big)^{-1}L_{-}G_{\epsilon},

we obtain
\big\|(\Delta+V-1)(F_{\epsilon}-W_{a})\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq\epsilon
which implies
\big\|F_{\epsilon}-W_{a}\big\|_{H^{2}}\leq\epsilon\big\|(\Delta+V-1)^{-1}\big\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}
. Hence for some constant
C_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}L_{+}F_{\epsilon}\cdot F_{\epsilon}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}L_{+}W_{a}\cdot W_{a}\right|\leq C_{0}\epsilon.

As a consequence of (
C.3
), we get (
C.2
) for
F=F_{\epsilon},\epsilon=\frac{-E_{a}}{2C_{0}}
, which shows the claim in the case
N=3,4
.

Assume now that
N\geq 5
, so that
W\in L^{2}
and more generally in all space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
In this case

\displaystyle\text{Ran}(L_{-})^{\bot}=\text{Null}(L_{-})=\text{span}\{W\},

Thus

\displaystyle\text{Ran}(L_{-})=\{f\in L^{2},(f,W)_{L^{2}}=0\}.

(C.4)

Note that
L_{+}
is a self-adjoint compact perturbation of
-\Delta

and

\displaystyle\big(L_{+}W,W\big)_{L^{2}}=-(2p-2)\int|\nabla W|^{2}\;dx<0,

which implies that
L_{+}
has a negative eigenvalue. Let
Z
be the
eigenfunction for this eigenvalue (it is radial by the minimax
principle). Note that
L_{+}\widetilde{W}=0
, then for any real
number
\alpha
, we have

\displaystyle E_{0}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}L_{+}(Z+\alpha\widetilde{W})\cdot(Z+\alpha\widetilde{W})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}L_{+}Z\cdot Z<0.

(C.5)

Note that

\big(\widetilde{W},W\big)_{L^{2}}\not=0,

we can choose the real number
\alpha_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\big(Z+\alpha_{1}\widetilde{W},W\big)_{L^{2}}=0,

which means that

\displaystyle\left((L_{-}+1)(Z+\alpha_{1}\widetilde{W}),W\right)_{L^{2}}=

\displaystyle\left(Z+\alpha_{1}\widetilde{W},(L_{-}+1)W\right)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(Z+\alpha_{1}\widetilde{W},W\big)_{L^{2}}=0.

By (
C.4
), for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a function

G_{\epsilon}\in H^{2}_{rad}
such that

\displaystyle\big\|L_{-}G_{\epsilon}-\big(L_{-}+1\big)(Z+\alpha_{1}\widetilde{W})\big\|_{L^{2}}<\epsilon.

Taking

F_{\epsilon}:=\big(L_{-}+1\big)^{-1}L_{-}G_{\epsilon},

we obtain that

\displaystyle\big\|\big(L_{-}+1\big)\big(F_{\epsilon}-(Z+\alpha_{1}\widetilde{W})\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq\epsilon,

which implies that

\displaystyle\big\|F_{\epsilon}-(Z+\alpha_{1}\widetilde{W})\big\|_{H^{2}}\leq\epsilon\big\|\big(L_{-}+1\big)^{-1}\big\|_{L^{2}\rightarrow L^{2}}.

Hence for some constant
C_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}L_{+}F_{\epsilon}\cdot F_{\epsilon}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}L_{+}(Z+\alpha_{1}\widetilde{W})\cdot(Z+\alpha_{1}\widetilde{W})\right|\leq C_{0}\epsilon.

By (
C.5
), we have (
C.2
) for
F=F_{\epsilon},\epsilon=-\frac{E_{0}}{2C_{0}}
. ∎

C.2.
Decay of the eigenfunctions at infinity

By the bootstrap argument,
we know that
\mathcal{Y}_{\pm}\in C^{\infty}\cap H^{\infty}
. In fact, we
have
\mathcal{Y}_{\pm}\in\mathcal{S}
. By (
C.1
), it
suffices to show that
\mathcal{Y}_{1}\in\mathcal{S}
. Note that
\mathcal{Y}_{1}

satisfies that

\displaystyle\left(e^{2}_{0}+\Delta^{2}\right)\mathcal{Y}_{1}=

\displaystyle-p\Delta\left(I_{\lambda}*(W^{p-1}\mathcal{Y}_{1})W^{p-1}\right)-p\left(I_{\lambda}*W^{p}\right)\left(I_{\lambda}*(W^{p-1}\mathcal{Y}_{1})\right)W^{2p-3}

\displaystyle-(p-1)\Delta\left((I_{\lambda}*W^{p})W^{p-2}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\right)-(p-1)\left((I_{\lambda}*W^{p})^{2}W^{2p-4}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle-(I_{\lambda}*W^{p})W^{p-2}\Delta\mathcal{Y}_{1}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left(e_{0}-\Delta\right)^{2}\mathcal{Y}_{1}=

\displaystyle-(p-1)\Delta\left((I_{\lambda}*W^{p})W^{p-2}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\right)-(p-1)\left((I_{\lambda}*W^{p})^{2}W^{2p-4}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle-p\Delta\left(I_{\lambda}*(W^{p-1}\mathcal{Y}_{1})W^{p-1}\right)-p\left(I_{\lambda}*W^{p}\right)\left(I_{\lambda}*(W^{p-1}\mathcal{Y}_{1})\right)W^{2p-3}

\displaystyle-(I_{\lambda}*W^{p})W^{p-2}\Delta\mathcal{Y}_{1}-2e_{0}\Delta\mathcal{Y}_{1}.

Because of the existence of the nonlocal interaction on the right
hand side, the decay estimate in

[
18
]
does not work. From the Bessel
potential theory in
[
62
]
, we know that the integral
kernel
G
of the operator
\left(e_{0}-\Delta\right)^{-2}
is

\displaystyle G(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\frac{e_{0}}{4\pi}\delta}e^{-\frac{\pi}{\delta}|x|^{2}}\delta^{\frac{-N+4}{2}}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}.

Hence we have

(1)

G(x)=\frac{|x|^{-N+4}}{\gamma(4)}+o(|x|^{-N+4}),\;|x|\rightarrow 0
;

(2)

there exists
c>0
such that

G(x)=o(e^{-c|x|}),\;|x|\rightarrow+\infty.

Then the conclusion follows by the analogue estimates in

[
25
,
39
]
.∎

1.
Introduction

In this paper we prove the validity of Schauder theory for a new class of variational integrals at nearly linear growth featuring multiple phases, under optimal assumptions quantifying the rate of nonuniform ellipticity. The model we have in mind is the log-multiphase energy, i.e.:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)\ni w\mapsto\mathcal{L}(w)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}H(x,Dw)\,{\rm d}x\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle H(x,z):=\lvert z\rvert\log(1+\lvert z\rvert)+a(x)\lvert z\rvert^{q}+b(x)\lvert z\rvert^{s},\end{array}

where
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
is an open set,
1<q,s<\infty
and
a(\cdot)
,
b(\cdot)
are nonnegative, bounded coefficients. The crucial feature of integrand
H(\cdot)
, that makes functional
\mathcal{L}(\cdot)
relevant under the regularity theory viewpoint, relies in the combination of its strong nonuniformly elliptic character with the lack of control over the zero-set of the modulating coefficients
a(\cdot)
,
b(\cdot)
. In fact, following the classification in
[
31
]
, the related pointwise ellipticity ratio

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\partial H(\cdot)}(x,z):=\frac{\mbox{highest eigenvalue of }\partial^{2}H(x,z)}{\mbox{lowest eigenvalue of }\partial^{2}H(x,z)}\lesssim 1+\log(1+\lvert z\rvert)

and the nonlocal one

(1.4)

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{\partial H(\cdot)}(z;B)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{$x\in B$}\\
\text{$B\Subset\Omega$ ball}\end{subarray}}\frac{\textnormal{highest eigenvalue of }\partial H(x,z)}{\textnormal{lowest eigenvalue of }\partial H(x,z)}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 1+\lVert a\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B)}\lvert z\rvert^{q-1}+\lVert b\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B)}\lvert z\rvert^{s-1},

both blow up on large values of the gradient variable. In particular, the behavior at infinity of the nonlocal ellipticity ratio suggests that, rather than chasing competition, each power-type phase interacts only with the superlinear, elliptic one. This aspect is reflected into the possibility of assigning moduli of continuity to
a(\cdot)
and
b(\cdot)
without imposing any quantitative relation among them, and no geometric information on the zero level sets of coefficients is required to gain maximal regularity for minima. The main result of this paper is indeed recorded in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u\in W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
be a local minimizer of functional (
1
), with modulating coefficients
a(\cdot)
,
b(\cdot)
satisfying

(1.5)

\displaystyle 0\leq a(\cdot)\in C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega),\qquad\quad 0\leq b(\cdot)\in C^{0,\beta}(\Omega),

for some
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
, and growth exponents
(q,s)
such that

(1.6)

1<q<1+\frac{\alpha}{n},\qquad\quad\quad 1<s<1+\frac{\beta}{n}

is verified. Then
Du
is locally Hölder continuous in
\Omega
. Moreover, given any ball
B_{r}\Subset\Omega
,
r\in(0,1]
, the Lipschitz bound

(1.7)

\displaystyle||Du||_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/2})}\leq c\left(\fint_{B_{r}}1+H(x,Du)\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\theta}+c,

holds true with
c\equiv c(n,q,s,||a||_{C^{0,\alpha}},||b||_{C^{0,\beta}})
and
\theta\equiv\theta(n,q,s,\alpha,\beta)>0
.

To keep the amount of technicalities involved in the proof at a reasonable level, Theorem
1.1
is stated for the model example (
1
), but, after very minor modifications, we are able to handle an arbitrarily large, finite number of phases exhibiting the same phenomenology displayed in (
1.5
)-(
1.6
).

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer, and
u\in W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
be a local minimizer of functional

(1.8)

\displaystyle W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)\ni w\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\lvert Dw\rvert\log(1+\lvert Dw\rvert)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}(x)\lvert Dw\rvert^{q_{i}}\,{\rm d}x,

with modulating coefficients
0\leq a_{i}(\cdot)\in C^{0,\alpha_{i}}(\Omega)
, for some
\alpha_{i}\in(0,1]
,
i\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
, and growth exponents

verifying

(1.9)

\displaystyle 1<q_{i}<1+\frac{\alpha_{i}}{n},

\mbox{for all}\ \ i\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
. Then

is locally Hölder continuous in
\Omega
, and a

-energy estimate analogous to (

first treated by De Filippis & Oh

modelling the mixture of fluids with different viscosities, first introduced by Zhikov

in the setting of homogenization or for studying possible occurrences of Lavrentiev phenomenon. A comprehensive regularity theory for double phase problems has been obtained by Baroni & Colombo & Mingione in a series of seminal papers

This approach strictly requires homogeneity of the various estimates eventually leading to the final iteration scheme, ingredients unavoidably missing for strongly nonuniformly elliptic functionals as our log-multiphase integrals. Actually, nonhomogeneity is a distinctive feature of genuine nonuniformly elliptic problems, thus classical perturbative methods are doomed to fail. The right perspective to adopt when dealing with the regularity for nonuniformly elliptic functionals is the one introduced by Marcellini in his foundational works on so-called
(p,q)
-nonuniformly elliptic variational integrals
[

. These are functionals of the type

which sufficiently smooth integrand

(p,q)
-nonuniform ellipticity conditions

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\lvert z\rvert^{p}\lesssim F(z)\lesssim 1+\lvert z\rvert^{q},\qquad\quad 1<p<q<\infty\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\lvert z\rvert^{p-2}\mathds{I}_{n\times n}\lesssim\partial^{2}F(z)\lesssim\lvert z\rvert^{q-2}\mathds{I}_{n\times n},\end{array}\right.

terminology justified by the fact that the only possible control on the pointwise ellipticity ratio is given by

\mathcal{R}_{\partial F(\cdot)}(z)\lesssim 1+\lvert z\rvert^{q-p}.

In a nutshell, Marcellini’s stategy consists in relating the growth of the ellipticity ratio to the amount of regularity allowed for minima: slowing down the rate of blow up of the ellipticity ratio by choosing

and

Our approach allows bypassing the ”separation of phases” trick usually employed when handling multiphase problems

2.1.
Notation

In this section we specify the notation adopted. We denote by
\Omega
an open, bounded subset of
\mathds{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, and we set

B_{r}\equiv B_{r}(x_{0})\coloneqq\{x\in\mathds{R}^{n}:|x-x_{0}|<r\},

where, unless differently specified, all the balls considered will have the same center. With
c
we denote a constant not necessarily the same in any two occurrences, the relevant dependence being emphasized. We
denote the inner hypercube of
B_{r}
by

Q_{\textnormal{inn}}\equiv Q_{\textnormal{inn}}(B_{r}).

It is the largest hypercube, contained in
B_{r}
, with sides parallel to the coordinate axes and concentric to
B_{r}
. The sidelength of
Q_{\textnormal{inn}}(B_{r})
is
2r/\sqrt{n}
. We adopt the usual definition of local minimizer.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
u\in W^{1,1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
is a local minimizer of
\mathcal{L}
if and only if
x\mapsto H(x,Du(x))\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
and

\int_{\text{supp}\ \varphi}H(x,Du(x))\ {\rm d}x\leq\int_{\text{supp}\ \varphi}H(x,Du(x)+D\varphi(x))\ {\rm d}x,

for any
\varphi\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)
with
\text{supp}\ \varphi\Subset\Omega
.

Let
\omega\in[0,1]
, for
z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
k\geq 1
, we define the quantity

{\ell_{\omega}}(z)\coloneqq\sqrt{\omega^{2}+|z|^{2}}.

Given a function
f:U\to\mathbb{R}
and a generic set
U
, we denote

(f-k)_{+}\coloneqq\max\{f-k,0\}\quad\text{and}\quad(f-k)_{-}\coloneqq\max\{k-f,0\},

with
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, we use the notation

||f||_{C^{0},\gamma}\coloneqq||f||_{L^{\infty}}+[f]_{0,\gamma,U},\quad[f]_{0,\gamma,U}\coloneqq\sup_{x,y\in U,x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\gamma}}.

Finally, if
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a measurable subset with bounded positive measure and
f
is a measurable map, we set

(f)_{U}\equiv\fint_{U}f(x)\,{\rm d}x\coloneqq\frac{1}{|U|}\int_{U}f(x)\,{\rm d}x.

is a Lebesgue point of
v
, then

\displaystyle v(x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq k_{0}+cL_{0}^{\frac{\chi}{\chi-1}}\left(\fint_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}(v-k_{0})^{2}_{+}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+cL^{\frac{1}{\chi-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{4}L_{j}P^{l_{j},\theta_{j}}_{2,\sigma_{j}}(f_{j},x_{0},2r_{0})

holds with
c\equiv c(n,\chi,\sigma_{j},\theta_{j},c_{*})
.

2.2.1.
Absence of Lavrentiev Gap

In this last part of preliminaries we give an important approximation result for the functional
\mathcal{L}
. We observe that the functional
{\mathcal{L}}
does not present Lavrentiev gap, namely: every function
w\in W^{1,1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
can be approximated, also in energy, by a sequence
w_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,\infty}
. This is stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
w\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
be a function such that
H(\cdot,Dw)\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega).
For every
B_{r}\Subset\Omega
,
r\in(0,1]
, there exists a sequence

\{w_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset W^{1,\infty}(B_{r})

such that
w_{\varepsilon}\to w
in
W^{1,1}(B_{r})
and

\mathcal{L}(w_{\varepsilon},B_{r})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(w,B_{r}).

For more details about Lavrentiev gap we refer to
[
1
,
3
,
44
]
. We remark that the proof closely follows that of
[
44
, Lemma 13]
and
[
33
, Section 5]
. The substantial difference lies in the term
|Dw|\log(1+|Dw|)
. For completeness we give an outline of the proof.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1]
be such that
B_{r+2\varepsilon_{0}}\Subset\Omega
. Then
w\in W^{1,1}(B_{r+2\varepsilon_{0}}).
For every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
we denote

{w}_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}w(y)\varepsilon^{-n}\phi\left(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\right)\,dy\coloneqq\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}w(y)\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\,dy,\quad\text{for all }x\in B_{r},

where
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1}(0))
,
\phi\geq 0
and
\int_{B_{1}(0)}\phi(x)\,{\rm d}x=1
. It follows that
{w}_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(B_{r})
and

(2.4)

{w}_{\varepsilon}\to w\quad\text{in }W^{1,1}(B_{r}).

Moreover, for all
x\in B_{r}
there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle|D{w}_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{n}}||Dw||_{L^{1}(B_{r+2\varepsilon_{0}})}.

Now, for all
x\in B_{r}
, we define

a_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\inf\{a(y):|x-y|\leq\varepsilon\},

b_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\inf\{b(y):|x-y|\leq\varepsilon\},

and

H_{\varepsilon}(x,z)\coloneqq|z|\log(1+|z|)+a_{\varepsilon}(x)|z|^{q}+b_{\varepsilon}(x)|z|^{s}.

By definition, we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(x,z)\leq H(y,z),\quad\text{for all }x\in B_{r},\text{ for all }y\in B_{\varepsilon}(x).

Moreover, if
|z|<\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{n}}
for some
c>0
, it holds

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(x,z)

\displaystyle=H(x,z)+(a_{\varepsilon}(x)-a(x))|z|^{q}+(b_{\varepsilon}(x)-b(x))|z|^{s}

\displaystyle\geq H(x,z)-c[a]_{0,\alpha,B_{r}}\varepsilon^{\alpha}|z|^{q-1}|z|-c[b]_{0,\beta,B_{r}}\varepsilon^{\beta}|z|^{s-1}|z|

\displaystyle\geq H(x,z)-c\varepsilon^{\alpha-n(q-1)}|z|-c\varepsilon^{\beta-n(s-1)}|z|

\displaystyle\geq H(x,z)-c|z|\,,

where for the last inequality we have used (
1.6
).
Then, for all
x\in B_{r}
, for all
|z|<\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{n}}
, it holds

(2.7)

H(x,z)\leq(1+c)(|z|+H_{\varepsilon}(x,z)),

with
c\equiv c(||Dw||_{L^{1}(B_{r+2\varepsilon_{0}})},[a]_{0,\alpha,B_{r}},[b]_{0,\beta,B_{r}})
. Now, we use the fact that
t\mapsto H_{\varepsilon}(x,t)
is convex, Jensen inequality and (
2.6
), to obtain

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(x,D{w}_{\varepsilon}(x))

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}H_{\varepsilon}(x,Dw(y))\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}H(y,Dw(y))\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\,dy

\displaystyle\eqcolon(H(\cdot,Dw(\cdot)))_{\varepsilon}(x).

Bearing in mind (
2.5
) and (
2.7
), we have

H(x,D{w}_{\varepsilon}(x))\leq c[|D{w}_{\varepsilon}(x)|+(H(\cdot,Dw(\cdot)))_{\varepsilon}(x)].

By (
2.4
) and

(H(\cdot,Dw(\cdot)))_{\varepsilon}(x)\to H(x,Dw(x))\quad\text{strongly in }L^{1}(B_{r})

we can use well-known variant of Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem to obtain that

H(x,D{w}_{\varepsilon}(x))\to H(x,Dw(x))\quad\text{strongly in }L^{1}(B_{r}).

This ends the proof.
∎

3.
L^{\infty}
-estimate for the frozen problem

In this section we construct densities
H{{}_{\omega,\sigma}}
representing a regularized version of

, in this way we transform the starting problem into an elliptical one. At this point, we consider the frozen integrand

and, using the fact that the coefficients

Let us start considering a ball

and two parameters

where

and

have been introduced in Section

. We observe that

. It is easy to check that, for every

and for every

, there exists a constant

such that

(3.2)

\langle\partial_{zz}H{{}_{\omega,\sigma}}(x,z)\xi,\xi\rangle\geq c\sigma\left[\ ({\ell_{\omega}}(z))^{q-2}+({\ell_{\omega}}(z))^{s-2}\right]|\xi|^{2},

(3.3)

where, for the last property, we have used the fact that

\displaystyle\partial_{z}H{{}_{\omega,\sigma}}(x,z)=\frac{z}{{\ell_{\omega}}(z)}\left[\log(1+{\ell_{\omega}}(z))+\frac{{\ell_{\omega}}(z)}{1+{\ell_{\omega}}(z)}\right]

\displaystyle+qa_{\sigma}(x)({\ell_{\omega}}(z))^{q-2}z

\displaystyle+sb_{\sigma}(x)({\ell_{\omega}}(z))^{s-2}z,

\displaystyle\partial_{zz}H{{}_{\omega,\sigma}}(x,z)=

\displaystyle\left[\mathds{I}_{n\times n}+\frac{z\otimes z}{({\ell_{\omega}}(z))^{2}}\right]\frac{\log(1+{\ell_{\omega}}(z))}{{\ell_{\omega}}(z)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mathds{I}_{n\times n}}{1+{\ell_{\omega}}(z)}+\frac{z\otimes z}{({\ell_{\omega}}(z))^{2}(1+{\ell_{\omega}}(z))^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left[|\xi|^{2}-\frac{|z\cdot\xi|^{2}}{({\ell_{\omega}}(z))^{2}}\right]\frac{\log(1+({\ell_{\omega}}(z)))}{{\ell_{\omega}}(z)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+{\ell_{\omega}}(z)}+\frac{|z\cdot\xi|^{2}}{({\ell_{\omega}}(z))^{2}(1+{\ell_{\omega}}(z))^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c}{(d_{2}-d_{1})^{n(1+\delta/2)}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}(1+H^{\iota}(Du_{0}))\,{\rm d}x\right)^{1+\frac{\delta}{2}},

with
c\equiv c(n,q,s,\delta)
; for the last inequality we used the minimality of

and Young’s inequality with the conjugate exponents

Now, applying Lemma

\displaystyle\tilde{E}^{\iota}(||Dv^{\iota}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{3r/4})})

\displaystyle\leq c\left[H^{\iota}(||Du_{0}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})})\right]^{1+\frac{\delta}{2}}+\bar{c}

\displaystyle\leq c\left[H^{\iota}(||Du_{0}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})})\right]^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\log(1+{\ell_{\omega}}(||Du_{0}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}))

\displaystyle\quad\times{\ell_{\omega}}(||Du_{0}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})})

4.
Main Theorem

This section is devoted to the proof of the main result of the present paper, that is Theorem
1.1
.
Assuming the existence of a minimizer
u\in W^{1,1}
of the functional (
1
), we show that
u
has locally Hölder continuous gradient by showing that it can be locally approximated, in energy, by more regular maps satisfying suitable uniform Lipschitz bounds. Once proven gradient boundedness, the amount of nonuniform ellipticity of (
1
) becomes immaterial and gradient Hölder continuity follows by more standard means.

&\frac{\delta_{1}}{2(\chi-1)}+1+(1+q)\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}-\frac{\delta_{2}}{4}<1\\
&\frac{\delta_{1}}{2(\chi-1)}+1+(1+s)\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}-\frac{\delta_{2}}{4}<1.\end{cases}

From now on, without loss of generality, we assume that

\displaystyle n>2\alpha,\quad n>2\beta,

indeed, since we have strict inequalities in (
1.6
), we can take
\alpha,\beta<1
.
By the choice of
\delta_{2}
in (
4.42
) we have

\max\left\{\frac{n(q-1+\delta_{2})}{\alpha},\frac{n(s-1+\delta_{2})}{\beta}\right\}<1,

so we can apply Lemma
2.4
with
\gamma=1
and obtain

\begin{cases}&||P^{q-1+\delta_{2},1}_{2,\alpha/2}(1+|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|,\cdot,(d_{2}-d_{1})/4)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{1}})}\\
&\qquad\leq c||1+|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|\ ||_{L^{1}(B_{R})}^{(q-1+\delta_{2})/2}\\
&||P^{s-1+\delta_{2},1}_{2,\beta/2}(1+|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|,\cdot,(d_{2}-d_{1})/4)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{1}})}\\
&\qquad\leq c||1+|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|\ ||_{L^{1}(B_{R})}^{(s-1+\delta_{2})/2}\\
&||P^{2(q-1+\delta_{2}),1}_{2,\alpha}(1+|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|,\cdot,(d_{2}-d_{1})/4)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{1}})}\\
&\qquad\leq c||1+|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|\ ||_{L^{1}(B_{R})}^{q-1+\delta_{2}}\\
&||P^{2(s-1+\delta_{2}),1}_{2,\beta}(1+|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|,\cdot,(d_{2}-d_{1})/4)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{1}})}\\
&\qquad\leq c||1+|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|\ ||_{L^{1}(B_{R})}^{s-1+\delta_{2}},\end{cases}

with
c\equiv c(n,q,s,\alpha,\beta)
. Then, (
4
) becomes

\displaystyle||\tilde{E}{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(\cdot,|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{1}})}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{c}{(d_{2}-d_{1})^{n/2}}||\tilde{E}{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(\cdot,|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|)||^{\frac{\delta_{1}\chi}{2(\chi-1)}+\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{2}})}\left(\int_{B_{R}}\tilde{E}{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(x,|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|)\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+c||\tilde{E}{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(\cdot,|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{2}})}^{\frac{\delta_{1}}{2(\chi-1)}+1-\delta_{2}}||1+|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|\ ||_{L^{1}(B_{R})}^{q-1+\delta_{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+c||\tilde{E}{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(\cdot,|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{2}})}^{\frac{\delta_{1}}{2(\chi-1)}+1-\delta_{2}}||1+|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|\ ||_{L^{1}(B_{R})}^{s-1+\delta_{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+c||\tilde{E}{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(\cdot,|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{2}})}^{\frac{\delta_{1}}{2(\chi-1)}+1+(1+q)\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}-\frac{\delta_{2}}{4}}||1+|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|\ ||_{L^{1}(B_{R})}^{q-1+\delta_{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+c||\tilde{E}{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(\cdot,|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{2}})}^{\frac{\delta_{1}}{2(\chi-1)}+1+(1+s)\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}-\frac{\delta_{2}}{4}}||1+|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|\ ||_{L^{1}(B_{R})}^{s-1+\delta_{2}}+c,

with
c\equiv c(n,q,s,||a||_{C^{0,\alpha}},||b||_{C^{0,\beta}})
. By (
4.44
) we can apply Young’s inequality to the above diplayed inequality, getting

\displaystyle||\tilde{E}{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(\cdot,|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{1}})}\leq\frac{1}{2}||\tilde{E}{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(\cdot,|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{2}})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{c}{(d_{2}-d_{1})^{n\theta}}||\tilde{E}{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(\cdot,|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|)||^{\theta}_{L^{1}(B_{R})}+||Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}||^{\theta}_{L^{1}(B_{R})}+c.

At this point we use Lemma
2.3
to obtain

\displaystyle||\tilde{E}{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(\cdot,|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d})}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{c}{(R-d)^{n\theta}}||\tilde{E}{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(\cdot,|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|)||^{\theta}_{L^{1}(B_{R})}+||Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}||^{\theta}_{L^{1}(B_{R})}+c,

where
c\equiv c(n,q,s,||a||_{C^{0,\alpha}},||b||_{C^{0,\beta}})
and
\theta\equiv\theta(n,q,s,\alpha,\beta)\geq 1
.

Step 7:
Convergence

We recall that, by very definition (
3.15
)

\tilde{E}{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(x,|z|)\leq 2(1+H{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(x,z)),\quad\text{for all }(x,z)\in B_{r}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Therefore, using the information in the last display together with (
3.1
) and (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle||\tilde{E}{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(\cdot,|Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}|)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{(R-d)^{n\theta}}\left(\int_{B_{R}}(1+H{{}_{\omega,\sigma_{\varepsilon}}}(x,Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}))\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\theta}+c

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{(R-d)^{n\theta}}\left(\mathcal{L}(u,B_{R})+o_{\varepsilon}(\omega)+o(\varepsilon)+|B_{R}|\right)^{\theta}+c,

with
c\equiv c(n,q,s,||a||_{C^{0,\alpha}},||b||_{C^{0,\beta}})
and
\theta\equiv\theta(n,q,s,\alpha,\beta)\geq 1
. Now, by (
3.15
) we have

(4.45)

\displaystyle||Du{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d})}\leq\frac{c}{(R-d)^{n\theta}}\left(\mathcal{L}(u,B_{R})+o_{\varepsilon}(\omega)+o(\varepsilon)+|B_{R}|\right)^{\theta}+c,

this means that, up to not relabelled subsequences, by (
4.6
),

u{{}_{\omega,\varepsilon}}\rightharpoonup^{*}u_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{in }W^{1,\infty}(B_{d}),

for every
\varepsilon>0
. Therefore, letting
\omega\to 0
, (
4.45
) implies

||Du_{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d})}\leq\frac{c}{(R-d)^{n\theta}}\left(\mathcal{L}(u,B_{R})+o(\varepsilon)+|B_{R}|\right)^{\theta}+c.

We now recall (
4.8
) to get

u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup^{*}u\quad\text{in }W^{1,\infty}(B_{d}).

Hence, we can conclude that

\displaystyle||Du||_{L^{\infty}(B_{d})}\leq\frac{c}{(R-d)^{n\theta}}\left(\mathcal{L}(u,B_{R})+|B_{R}|\right)^{\theta}+c,

with
c
and
\theta
as above. We note that the choices
d=r/2
and
R=r
are admissible, so (
1.7
) follows.

Step 8:

Du
is locally Hölder continuous.

The gradient local Hölder continuity now follows by a similar argument as the one used in Sections 5.9 and 5.10 of
[
33
]
.

The proof is complete.

Proof of Corollary
1.2

Corollary
1.2
directly comes from repeated applications of the arguments detailed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Remark 4.1
.

We observe that we have analyzed the multidimensional scalar case. But, since the integrand has the Uhlenbeck structure

H(x,z)=\tilde{H}(x,|z|),

it is possible to extend the result to functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with
N\geq 2
.

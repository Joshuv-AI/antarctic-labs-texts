An Exact Invariant for Relativistic Linear Harmonic Oscillator with Time-dependent Frequency
B. K. Shivamoggi and D. Reinhart

Abstract
In this paper we give an exact invariant for a relativistic linear harmonic oscillator with time-dependent frequency. This is accomplished, following Eliezer and Gray [ 8 ] , for the non-relativistic case, by associating a relativistic plane isotropic harmonic oscillator with this problem. This exact invariant reflects the conservation of angular momentum of the associated relativistic plane isotropic oscillator. Departures in the physical interpretations of this exact invariant caused by relativistic effects are pointed out.

2
Lewis’ Invariant for a Non-relativistic Linear Harmonic Oscillator with Time-dependent Frequency

Consider a non-relativistic linear harmonic oscillator with time-dependent frequency,

\frac{d^{2}x}{dt^{2}}+\omega^{2}(t)x=0.

(1)

An exact invariant for this problem was given by Lewis
[
1
]
,

I=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{x^{2}}{r^{2}}+(r\dot{x}-\dot{r}x)^{2}\right]=\text{const.}

(2)

where
r(t)
satisfies the nonlinear differential equation,

\ddot{r}+\omega^{2}(t)r-\frac{1}{r^{3}}=0

(3)

and the dots overhead denote derivatives with respect to
t
.

Eliezer and Gray
[
8
]
pointed out that one can associate a plane isotropic harmonic oscillator with the motion of a non-relativistic linear harmonic oscillator with time-dependent frequency.

One considers a time-dependent central force potential,

V=\frac{1}{2}\omega^{2}(t)r^{2}

(4)

with the Lagrangian,

L=\frac{1}{2}\left[\dot{r}^{2}+r^{2}\dot{\theta}^{2}-\omega^{2}(t)r^{2}\right]

(5a)

or

L=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\dot{x}_{1}^{2}-\omega^{2}x_{1}^{2}\right)+\left(\dot{x}_{2}^{2}-\omega^{2}x_{2}^{2}\right)\right]\equiv L_{1}+L_{2}.

(5b)

(
5b
) implies that this problem, in Cartesian coordinates, splits into two independent linear harmonic oscillators of the form,

L_{i}=\frac{1}{2}(\dot{x}_{i}^{2}-\omega^{2}(t)x_{i}^{2}),\hskip 14.45377pti=1,2.

(6)

On noting from (
5a
) that
L
is cyclic in
\theta
, the azimuthal component of the equation of motion gives the constancy of the angular momentum,

h=r^{2}\dot{\theta}=\text{const.}

(7)

The radial component of the equation of motion associated with (
5a
) is given by

\ddot{r}+r\omega^{2}-r\dot{\theta}^{2}=0

(8)

and on using (
7
), equation (
8
) becomes

\ddot{r}+\omega^{2}r-\frac{h^{2}}{r^{3}}=0.

(9)

On noting that,

\cos\theta=\frac{x_{1}}{r},\hskip 14.45377pt\sin\theta=\frac{\dot{r}x_{1}-\dot{x}_{1}r}{h}

(10)

we obtain

\left(\frac{hx_{1}}{r}\right)^{2}+\left(\dot{r}x_{1}-\dot{x}_{1}r\right)^{2}=h^{2}=\text{const.}

or

I=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{h^{2}x_{1}^{2}}{r^{2}}+\left(r\dot{x}_{1}-x_{1}\dot{r}\right)^{2}\right]=\text{const.}

(11)

Thus, the exact invariance of
I
for a linear harmonic oscillator with time-dependent frequency is merely a statement of the conservation of angular momentum of the associated plane isotropic oscillator.

For the case of time-independent frequency,
\omega=\text{const.}
, one solution of equation (
9
) is

r=\sqrt{\frac{h}{\omega}}=\text{const.}

(12)

Using (
12
), (
11
) yields

I=\frac{h}{2\omega}(\dot{x}_{1}^{2}+\omega^{2}x_{1}^{2})\equiv h\left(\frac{E}{\omega}\right)\equiv hJ.

(13)

So, for the case of the standard linear harmonic oscillator, with
\omega=\text{const.}
, the exact invariant is proportional to the action variable,
J
. Here,
E
is the total energy of the oscillator.

3
An Exact Invariant of a Relativistic Linear Harmonic Oscillator with Time-dependent Frequency

Consider a relativistic linear harmonic oscillator with time-dependent frequency (MacColl
[
9
]
, Moreau et al.
[
10
]
),

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{\dot{x}}{\left(1-\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\dot{x}^2}{c^2}$}}\right)^{1/2}}\right]+\omega^{2}(t)x=0

(14)

where,

\omega^{2}(t)\equiv\frac{k(t)}{m_{0}}

k(t)
being the time-dependent spring constant, and
m_{0}
the rest mass.

Since

\displaystyle\cos\theta=\frac{x_{1}}{r}

For the case of the time-independent frequency,
\omega=\text{const.}

r^{2}\left(r^{2}+\frac{h^{2}}{c^{2}}\right)=\frac{h^{2}}{\omega^{2}}

(24a)

H=c^{2}(\gamma-1)+\frac{1}{2}\omega^{2}x_{1}^{2}

\gamma\equiv\left(1-\frac{\dot{x}_{1}^{2}}{c^{2}}\right)^{-1/2}

\frac{\dot{x}_{1}^{2}}{c^{2}}=1-\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}

(27b)

(

I=\frac{h^{2}}{\omega^{2}r^{2}}\left[H-\frac{c^{2}}{2\gamma^{2}}(2\gamma+1)(\gamma-1)^{2}\right]

On using (

I=\frac{h}{\omega}\left[\frac{H-\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{c^2}{2 \gamma^2}$}}(2\gamma+1)(\gamma-1)^{2}}{-\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{h \omega}{2c^2}$}}+\sqrt{1+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{h^2 \omega^2}{4 c^4}$}}}}\right].

(

J\equiv H/\omega
, for the standard linear harmonic oscillator, with
\omega=\text{const.}

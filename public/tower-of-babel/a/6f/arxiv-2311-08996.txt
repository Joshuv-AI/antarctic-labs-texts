Channel Estimation for mmWave MIMO using sub-6 GHz Out-of-Band Information PubID: pubid: 979-8-3503-8532-8/24/$31.00 ©2024 IEEE
Faruk Pasic 1 , Markus Hofer 3 , Mariam Mussbah 1 2 , Sebastian Caban 1 , Stefan Schwarz 1 , Thomas Zemen 3 and Christoph F. Mecklenbräuker 1

Abstract
Future wireless MIMO ( MIMO ) communication systems will employ sub-6 GHz and MMW ( MMW ) frequency bands working cooperatively. Establishing a MIMO communication link usually relies on estimating CSI ( CSI ) which is difficult to acquire at MMW frequencies due to a low SNR ( SNR ). In this paper, we propose three novel methods to estimate MMW MIMO channels using out-of-band information obtained from the sub-6 GHz band. We compare the proposed channel estimation methods with a conventional one utilizing only in-band information. Simulation results show that the proposed methods outperform the conventional MMW channel estimation method in terms of achievable spectral efficiency, especially at low SNR and high K-factor.

Introduction

Today’s wireless communication systems operate mainly in the sub-6 GHz frequency bands.
Due to the spectrum shortage, sub-6 GHz bands can not keep up with the growing demand for high data rates.
Fortunately, significantly more bandwidth is available in
MMW
bands (10 GHz – 300 GHz), which enables high data rate transmissions
[
1
]
.
To provide a sufficient link margin, in most
MMW
systems, antenna arrays will be used at the transmitter and receiver side.
This creates many opportunities to apply
MIMO
communication techniques such as directional beamforming
[
2
]
.
Therefore,
MMW

MIMO
communication is a promising technology for next-generation wireless systems
[
3
]
.

Configuring
MMW
antenna arrays is challenging.
The main challenge in using
MMW
frequency bands is link establishment, which is done by designing the precoder and combiner
[
4
]
.
The precoder and combiner design usually relies on
CSI
, which is difficult to acquire at
MMW
frequencies due to low pre-beamforming
SNR
.
Hence, the performance of
CSI
estimation is directly related to the coverage capability of wireless systems, which is the main obstacle of
5G
(
5G
) and beyond systems employing
MMW
bands.

Many multi-band measurement campaigns in different environments have shown that
MMW
frequency bands have similar propagation characteristics as sub-6 GHz bands
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
Specifically, the multipath components were found to be similarly distributed in these two frequency bands.
Since
MMW
systems will likely be deployed in conjunction with sub-6 GHz systems
[
11
]
, the opportunity arises to use these two bands cooperatively to achieve a better system throughput.

So far, several beam-selection strategies to leverage sub-6 GHz out-of-band information as side information on
MMW
band have been proposed.
In
[
12
]
, the authors propose using spatial information extracted at sub-6 GHz to improve
MMW
compressed beam selection.
Furthermore, two approaches (non-parametric and parametric) to translate the lower frequency spatial correlation to the higher frequency have been proposed in
[
13
]
.
In
[
14
]
, the authors analyze the feasibility of using low-band channel information for coarse estimation of high-band beam directions.
The authors in
[
15
,
16
]
propose a deep learning-based beam selection algorithm for
MMW
bands, exploiting sub-6 GHz channel information.
However, the idea of exploiting sub-6 GHz out-of-band information for
MMW
channel estimation has not been investigated yet.

Contribution:

In this paper, we propose three novel pilot-aided channel estimation methods for
MMW

MIMO
systems based on out-of-band information.
We exploit the relationship between
LOS
(
LOS
) channel components across different frequency bands and estimate the
MMW
channel with the aid of adapted channel coefficients obtained in the sub-6 GHz band.
We evaluate the proposed methods through simulations in terms of
SE
(
SE
).

We consider a point-to-point multi-band
MIMO

MMW

The transmitter consists of
M_{\rm Tx}
sub-6 GHz and
M_{\rm Tx}

M_{\rm Rx}
sub-6 GHz and
M_{\rm Rx}

The sub-6 GHz and
MMW
parts of the system are arranged as a
ULA
(
ULA
) of dipole antennas, modeled as isotropic point sources (see
Fig.

1
).
We assume that the sub-6 GHz and
MMW

Both arrays have the same number of antenna elements mutually separated by 0.5
\,\lambda^{\left(\rm m\right)}
, where
\lambda^{\left(\rm m\right)}
represents the wavelength of the
MMW
system.
The small antenna spacing for the sub-6 GHz array can be achieved by a compact design, as proposed in
[
17
]
.
Furthermore, we assume perfect time and frequency synchronization at the receiver.
The transmitter and receiver are equipped with one
RF
(
RF
) chain per antenna, thereby allowing for fully digital beamforming for both sub-6 GHz and
MMW
systems
[
18
]
.

We consider an
OFDM
(
OFDM
) system with
N^{\left(\rm b\right)}

N^{\left(\rm b\right)}

QAM
(

) are mapped onto

The

.

\mathbf{H}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]

OFDM

II-A

Channel Model

We consider a frequency-selective Rician fading channel model defined as

\mathbf{H}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]=\sqrt{\eta^{\left(\rm b\right)}}\left(\mathrm{A_{\rm fs}^{\left(\rm b\right)}}\mathbf{H}_{\rm fs}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]+\mathrm{A_{\rm rp}^{\left(\rm b\right)}}\mathbf{H}_{\rm rp}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]\right)

(1)

where
\eta^{\left(\rm b\right)}
is the path loss coefficient,
\mathrm{A_{\rm fs}^{\left(\rm b\right)}}=\sqrt{K^{\left(\rm b\right)}/\left(1+K^{\left(\rm b\right)}\right)}
denotes the free-space scaling factor,
\mathrm{A_{\rm rp}^{\left(\rm b\right)}}=\sqrt{1/\left(1+K^{\left(\rm b\right)}\right)}
represents the Rayleigh-part scaling factor and
K^{\left(\rm b\right)}
denotes the Rician
K
-factor.
Based on the measurements presented in
[
21
]
, due to the less pronounced multipath components at
MMW
bands compared to sub-6 GHz bands, we assume that the
MMW

K
-factor
K^{\left(\rm m\right)}=10K^{\left(\rm s\right)}
. The deterministic free-space channel
\mathbf{H}_{\rm fs}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]
is defined by

\mathbf{H}_{\rm fs}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]=e^{-{\rm j}\frac{2\pi}{\lambda^{\left(\rm b\right)}}\mathbf{D}},

(2)

where

\mathbf{D}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{d}_{1,1}&\cdots&\mathrm{d}_{1,M_{\rm Tx}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathrm{d}_{M_{\rm Rx},1}&\cdots&\mathrm{d}_{M_{\rm Rx},M_{\rm Tx}}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{M_{\rm Rx}\times M_{\rm Tx}}

(3)

represents the distances between specific transmit and receive antenna elements and
\lambda^{\left(\rm b\right)}
denotes the wavelength.
The Rayleigh channel matrix
\mathbf{H}_{\rm rp}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]
consists of independent complex Gaussian random variables with the power of one.

The path loss coefficient for
MMW
bands is defined by

\eta^{\left(\rm m\right)}=\left(\frac{4\pi df_{\rm c}^{\left(\rm m\right)}}{c_{0}}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{4\pi d\sqrt{\alpha}f_{\rm c}^{\left(\rm s\right)}}{c_{0}}\right)^{2}=\alpha\eta^{\left(\rm s\right)},

(4)

where
d
denotes the distance between the transmitter and the receiver,
c_{0}
is the speed of light,
\eta^{\left(\rm s\right)}
represents the free space path loss for the sub-6 GHz band and
\sqrt{\alpha}=f_{\rm c}^{\left(\rm m\right)}/f_{\rm c}^{\left(\rm s\right)}
denotes the carrier frequency ratio between the sub-6 GHz band and the
MMW
band.
Furthermore, we assume the same transmit power
P_{\rm T}
for both frequency bands.
Since
MMW
systems tend to use larger bandwidth and can have a noise figure different to sub-6 GHz systems, we introduce
\beta=\frac{B^{\left(\rm m\right)}F^{\left(\rm m\right)}}{B^{\left(\rm s\right)}F^{\left(\rm s\right)}}
as the ratio of sub-6 GHz and
MMW
bandwidths and noise figures.
Then, the obtained pre-beamforming
MMW

SNR
for the single receive antenna element can be expressed by

\begin{split}\gamma^{\left(\rm m\right)}=&\frac{P_{\rm T}}{\eta^{\left(\rm m\right)}k_{\rm B}TB^{\left(\rm m\right)}F^{\left(\rm m\right)}}=\frac{P_{\rm T}}{\alpha\eta^{\left(\rm s\right)}k_{\rm B}T\beta B^{\left(\rm s\right)}F^{\left(\rm s\right)}}\\
=&\gamma^{\left(\rm s\right)}\frac{1}{\alpha\beta},\end{split}

(5)

where
k_{\rm B}
represents the Boltzmann constant and
T
denotes the receiver temperature.
A sub-6 GHz system with
\alpha\beta
times higher
SNR
has benefits.
These benefits will be highlighted below.

II-B

Link Establishment

Establishing a communication link consists of a training phase and data transmission.
The wireless channel is estimated at sub-6 GHz and
MMW
frequency bands during the training phase.
The channel estimates obtained at the sub-6 GHz and
MMW
bands are then utilized for data transmission at the
MMW
band.
We do not transmit data in the sub-6 GHz band.

II-B
1
Training Phase

During the training phase, each transmit antenna is assigned pilot symbols that are known at the receiver.
The pilot symbols
\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Tx}\times 1}
generated from the
QAM
alphabet are distributed over
N^{\left(\rm b\right)}
subcarriers so that each antenna occupies a certain number of non-overlapping subcarriers.
The pilot allocation at the
t
-th transmit antenna is given by

\mathrm{\phi}_{t}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]=\begin{cases}\mathrm{\phi}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n],\quad n\in\{t,t+M_{\rm Tx},\ldots,N^{\left(\rm b\right)}\}\\
0,\quad\quad\quad\text{else}\end{cases}.

(6)

A pilot allocation example for
M_{\rm Tx}=4
transmit antennas is shown in
Fig.

2
.

Fig. 2:
Pilot symbols at each antenna occupy a certain number of subcarriers such that they do not overlap in the frequency domain.

The input-output relationship for the training phase is given by

\mathbf{y}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]=\mathbf{H}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]+\mathbf{w}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n],

(7)

where the received signal is denoted by
\mathbf{y}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Rx}\times 1}
and the
AWGN
(
AWGN
) added at the receiver with the power of
\sigma_{w}^{2}
is denoted by
\mathbf{w}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]\in\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma_{w}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M_{\rm Rx}})
.
At the receiver, the
LS
(
LS
) estimates of the channel are obtained at pilot positions
n_{t}\in\{t,t+M_{\rm Tx},\ldots,N^{\left(\rm b\right)}\}
.
Further, linear interpolation is performed to estimate the channel at non-occupied subcarrier positions, resulting in an estimated channel
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Rx}\times M_{\rm Tx}}
.
Since the channel is estimated at the receiver, the
CSI
has to be fed back to help the transmitter select the best beamforming matrix.
Therefore, the estimated channel
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Rx}\times M_{\rm Tx}}
is sent to the transmitter, assuming perfect feedback.
Furthermore, we assume here that the channel in
Eq.

1
stays relatively stable over time (long coherence time), so the channel estimation and sending the estimated channel to the transmitter via feedback is only performed infrequently.

II-B
2
Data Transmission

For the data transmission phase, we consider only the mmWave system.
The estimated channels
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]
are processed using the three methods proposed in
Section

III
.
The resulting
MMW
channel estimate
\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]
is then utilized for achieving the highest performance of the
MIMO
channel by
SVD
(
SVD
) based unitary precoding and combining.
The compact-form
SVD
of the channel matrix
\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]
can be written as

\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]=\overline{\mathbf{Q}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\overline{\mathbf{\Sigma}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\left(\overline{\mathbf{F}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\left(\mathbf{P}^{\left(\rm m\right)}\right)^{1/2}\right)^{\rm H},

(8)

where the semi-unitary matrix
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Rx}\times{\ell_{\rm max}}}
denotes the matrix of left singular vectors and
\overline{\mathbf{F}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Tx}\times{\ell_{\rm max}}}
represents the matrix of right singular vectors.
The singular value matrix is given by

\overline{\mathbf{\Sigma}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]={\rm diag}\left(\overline{\sigma}_{(1)}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n],\ldots,\overline{\sigma}_{(\ell_{\rm max})}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\right),

(9)

where the
i
-th diagonal element
\overline{\sigma}_{(i)}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]
of the singular value matrix is equals the
i
-th largest singular value of
\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]
.
Assuming a full-rank channel, the maximum number of streams is
\ell_{\rm max}={\rm min}\left(M_{\rm Rx},M_{\rm Tx}\right)
.
The power loading matrix is given by

\mathbf{P}^{\left(\rm m\right)}={\rm diag}\left(p_{(1)}^{\left(\rm m\right)},\ldots,p_{(\ell_{\rm max})}^{\left(\rm m\right)}\right),

(10)

where
p_{(1)}^{\left(\rm m\right)}=p_{(2)}^{\left(\rm m\right)}=\ldots=p_{(\ell_{\rm max})}^{\left(\rm m\right)}=P_{\rm T}/\ell_{\rm max}>0
and the total transmit power constraint

\left\lVert\overline{\mathbf{F}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\left(\mathbf{P}^{\left(\rm m\right)}\right)^{1/2}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}=P_{\rm T}

(11)

is satisfied by the precoder.

The symbol vector to be transmitted is written as
\mathbf{x}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Tx}\times 1}
.
Prior to transmission over the wireless channel, the symbol vector
\mathbf{x}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]
is precoded with a precoding matrix
\overline{\mathbf{F}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]
.
At the receiver, combining is performed with a combining matrix
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]
.
With this notation, the input-output relationship for the data transmission phase is then given by

\begin{split}\mathbf{y}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]&=\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\right)^{\rm H}\mathbf{H}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\left(\mathbf{P}^{\left(\rm m\right)}\right)^{1/2}\overline{\mathbf{F}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\mathbf{x}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\\
&+\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\right)^{\rm H}\mathbf{w}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n],\end{split}

(12)

where the received signal is denoted by
\mathbf{y}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell_{\rm max}\times 1}
and the
AWGN
added at the
MMW
receiver is denoted by
\mathbf{w}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\in\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma_{w}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M_{\rm Rx}})
.

III
Channel Estimation Methods

Due to smaller propagation losses and smaller bandwidth, a sub-6 GHz system has an
\alpha\beta
times higher
SNR
compared to the corresponding
MMW
system given the same transmit power (see
Section

II-A
).
This higher
SNR
enables us to achieve more accurate channel estimation for the
MMW
system with the aid of the sub-6 GHz channel estimate.

We assume that the wavelengths
\lambda^{\left(\rm s\right)}
and
\lambda^{\left(\rm m\right)}
, as well as the effective distance
\mathbf{D}
between the transmitter and receiver are known at the receiver, for example, because they are at known positions.
To ensure that the sub-6 GHz channel estimate occupies the bandwidth
B^{\left(\rm m\right)}
of the
MMW
system, we first average the
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]
over subcarriers and use the obtained average value to extrapolate the
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]
in the frequency domain.
Next, we take the sub-6 GHz channel estimate and rotate its phase by
e^{{\rm j}2\pi\mathbf{D}\xi}
as follows

\begin{split}&\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]=\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]\odot e^{{\rm j}2\pi\mathbf{D}\xi}\\
&=\sqrt{\eta^{\left(\rm s\right)}}\left(\mathrm{A_{\rm fs}^{\left(\rm s\right)}}\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\rm fs}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]+\mathrm{A_{\rm rp}^{\left(\rm s\right)}}\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\rm rp}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]\right)\odot e^{{\rm j}2\pi\mathbf{D}\xi}\\
&=\sqrt{\eta^{\left(\rm s\right)}}\left(\mathrm{A_{\rm fs}^{\left(\rm s\right)}}\underbrace{\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\rm fs}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]\odot e^{{\rm j}2\pi\mathbf{D}\xi}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\rm fs}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]}+\mathrm{A_{\rm rp}^{\left(\rm s\right)}}\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\rm rp}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]\odot e^{{\rm j}2\pi\mathbf{D}\xi}\right)\\
&=\sqrt{\eta^{\left(\rm s\right)}}\left(\mathrm{A_{\rm fs}^{\left(\rm s\right)}}\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\rm fs}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]+\mathrm{A_{\rm rp}^{\left(\rm s\right)}}\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\rm rp}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]\odot e^{{\rm j}2\pi\mathbf{D}\xi}\right),\end{split}

(13)

where
\xi=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\left(\rm s\right)}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{\left(\rm m\right)}}
.
Now, the deterministic free-space component of the obtained sub-6 GHz channel estimate
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]
matches the corresponding one of the
MMW
channel estimate
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]
.
Introducing the scaling factors
\mathrm{A_{\rm fs}^{\left(\rm s\right)}}
and
\mathrm{A_{\rm rp}^{\left(\rm s\right)}}
, the
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]
can be rewritten as

\begin{split}\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]=&\sqrt{\eta^{\left(\rm s\right)}}\sqrt{\frac{0.1K^{\left(\rm m\right)}}{1+0.1K^{\left(\rm m\right)}}}\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\rm fs}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\\
+&\sqrt{\eta^{\left(\rm s\right)}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{1+0.1K^{\left(\rm m\right)}}}\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\rm rp}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]\odot e^{{\rm j}2\pi\mathbf{D}\xi}.\end{split}

(14)

As the
MMW

K
-factor
K^{\left(\rm m\right)}
increases, the amplitude of the stochastic Rayleigh component decreases and the phase of the
MMW
channel can be estimated more accurately.

III-A

Translating

The most straightforward approach to utilize out-of-band information for establishing the
MMW
link is only to use the phase-rotated sub-6 GHz channel estimate
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]
, as follows

\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]=\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n].

(15)

In its basic implementation, there is no need for any additional calculations, except the phase rotation in
Eq.

13
.
This method performs well for high
K
-factors, where the stochastic Rayleigh component is negligible.
For low
K
-factors, where the stochastic component dominates the deterministic one, there is no benefit to be gained from using this method.

III-B

Averaging

To enhance performance for lower
K
-factors, we propose a method that incorporates the phase-rotated sub-6 GHz channel estimate
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]
along with the
MMW
channel estimate
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n].

The resulting channel estimate is then given by the average

\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]=\frac{\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]+\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]}{2}.

(16)

At moderate
K
-factors, this method yields better results on average than the translating method.
However, this method involves one more implementation step compared to the translating method.
In cases of low and high
K
-factors, there is still room for improvement by assigning weights different to 0.5 to
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]
in
Eq.

16
.

III-C

Weighting

Performance improvement is achieved through weighting the sub-6 GHz
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm s\right)}[n]
and
MMW

\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]
channel estimates by a weighting factor
W\left(K^{\left({\rm m}\right)},\gamma^{\left({\rm m}\right)}\right)\in\left[0,1\right]
that is a function of the
MMW

K
-factor
K^{\left({\rm m}\right)}
and the
MMW

SNR

\gamma^{\left(\rm m\right)}
:

\begin{split}\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]&=W\left(K^{\left({\rm m}\right)},\gamma^{\left(\rm m\right)}\right)\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm s}\right)}[n]\\
&+\left(1-W\left(K^{\left({\rm m}\right)},\gamma^{\left(\rm m\right)}\right)\right)\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\end{split}

(17)

Since the wireless channel is changing, it is important to select an optimum
W\left(K^{\left({\rm m}\right)},\gamma^{\left({\rm m}\right)}\right)
for each specific combination of
K^{\left({\rm m}\right)}
and
\gamma^{\left(\rm m\right)}
.
This ensures that the method is optimal for different wireless channel conditions.

Fig. 3:
For the TDL-A 4
\times
4
MIMO
channel, the proposed weighting method achieves up to 80% higher

. The small vertical bars within the circular markers indicate the 95% confidence intervals.

During the training phase, we determine and store
W\left(K^{\left({\rm m}\right)},\gamma^{\left({\rm m}\right)}\right)
for a set of
K^{\left({\rm m}\right)}
and
\gamma^{\left(\rm m\right)}
in a lookup table, see for example
Table

II
.
To calculate
W
, we assume perfect knowledge of the
MMW
channel
\mathbf{H}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]
, the
\gamma^{\left({\rm m}\right)}
and
K^{\left({\rm m}\right)}
by the transmitter and receiver.
Next, we find
W
that results in the minimum mean channel estimation error

W\left(K^{\left({\rm m}\right)},\gamma^{\left({\rm m}\right)}\right)=\operatorname*{argmin}_{W}E\Bigl\{W\left(K^{\left({\rm m}\right)},\gamma^{\left({\rm m}\right)}\right)\Bigr\},

(18)

where the mean channel estimation error is defined by

\begin{split}E&\Bigl\{W\left(K^{\left({\rm m}\right)},\gamma^{\left({\rm m}\right)}\right)\Bigr\}=\\
&=\frac{1}{LN^{\left(\rm m\right)}}\sum_{l=1}^{L}\sum_{n=1}^{N^{\left(\rm m\right)}}\left\lVert\mathbf{H}_{l}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]-\overline{\mathbf{H}}_{l}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\right\rVert_{F}^{2}.\end{split}

(19)

In
Eq.

19
,
\mathbf{H}_{l}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]
denotes the actual channel coefficient and
\overline{\mathbf{H}}_{l}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]
is the estimated channel coefficient for a specific channel realization
l
.
Results are averaged over
L=1000
different channel realizations.
The lookup table needs to be available at the transmitter and receiver.
During data transmission, the previously calculated
W\left(K^{\left({\rm m}\right)},\gamma^{\left(\rm m\right)}\right)
is taken from the lookup table and used to combine sub-6 GHz and
MMW
channel estimates.

Compared to the translating method and the averaging method, implementing the weighting method is significantly more demanding.
It involves an additional phase for populating the lookup table, and requires the lookup table,
\gamma^{\left(\rm m\right)}
and
K^{\left({\rm m}\right)}
to be known at the transmitter and receiver.

We consider the achievable

The achievable

\mathrm{SE}=\frac{1}{N^{\left(\rm m\right)}}\sum_{n=1}^{N^{\left(\rm m\right)}}\log_{2}\left(1+\mathrm{SINR}[n]\right)

(20)

with

\mathrm{SINR}[n]=\frac{\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu=1\\
\nu=\mu\end{subarray}}^{\ell_{\rm max}}\left|\overline{\mathrm{G}}_{\rm\mu,\nu}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\right|^{2}}{\sum\limits_{\mu=1}^{\ell_{\rm max}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu=1\\
\nu\neq\mu\end{subarray}}^{\ell_{\rm max}}\left|\overline{\mathrm{G}}_{\rm\mu,\nu}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\right|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2}\left\lVert\overline{\mathbf{Q}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\right\rVert^{2}_{F}}.

(21)

(22)

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose three novel channel estimation methods for
MMW

MIMO
systems based on using sub-6 GHz out-of-band information.
The
SNR
and
K
-factor affect the performance of the proposed channel estimation methods to a great extent.
The proposed methods lead to a significant increase in the achievable
SE
in the low-
SNR
regime, while the gains are negligible in the high-
SNR
regime.
The translating method performs well at high
K
-factors.
The averaging method performs well at moderate
K
-factor.
The weighting method is better (high
K
-factor) or at least as good (low
K
-factor) as the conventional channel estimation method using only the
MMW
band.

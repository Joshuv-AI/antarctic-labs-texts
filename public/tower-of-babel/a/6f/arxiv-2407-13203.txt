Minimal hypersurfaces in 𝕊 5 \mathbb{S}^{5} with constant scalar curvature and zero Gauss curvature are totally geodesic
Qing Cui

Abstract
. We show that a closed minimal hypersurface in 𝕊 5 \mathbb{S}^{5} with constant scalar curvature and zero Gauss curvature is totally geodesic.



1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
with second fundamental form
A
. In 1968, Simons
[
29
]
derived the famous Simons’ identity:

(1)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}=\left\lvert\nabla A\right\rvert^{2}+\left(n-\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\right)\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2},

where
\nabla A
is the covariant derivation of
A
. Therefore, if
M
is closed and assume
0\leq\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\leq n
, then the divergence theorem gives
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\equiv 0
or
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\equiv n
.
This pinching phenomenon has attracted many mathematicians’ attention. Chern-do Carmo-Kobayashi
[
9
]
and Lawson
[
19
]
independently characterized the case of
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\equiv n
: the Clifford hypersurfaces are the only minimal hypersurfces in
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
with
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\equiv n
. Assume
R_{M}
is the scalar curvature of
M
. By the Gauss equation, one has

\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}=n(n-1)+R_{M}
. Therefore,
M
has constant scalar curvature iff
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
is constant. The authors
[
9
]
proposed the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1
.

Let
M^{n}
be a closed immersed minimal hypersurface of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
with constant scalar curvature
R_{M}
. Then for each
n
, the set of all possible values for
R_{M}
is discrete.

Now this conjecture is known as
Chern conjecture
.
In 1983, Peng and Terng
[
26
,
27
]
made the first breakthrough towards Chern Conjecture, they showed:

If
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}>n
, then
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}>n+\frac{1}{12n}
. Moreover, for
n=3
,
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\geq 6
if
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}>3
.

In 1993, Chang
[
7
]
completed the proof of Chern Conjecture for
n=3
. After that,
Yang-Cheng
[
36
]
and
Suh-Yang
[
30
]
improved the constant from
\frac{1}{12n}
to
\frac{3n}{7}
. Based on known examples, mathematicians believe this constant should be
n
, that is, if
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\equiv const.>n
, then
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\geq 2n
(this is usually called
the second pinching conjecture
). Up to now, even the second pinching conjecture is still open for higher dimension.

Note that, in Simons’ pinching result,
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
is not required to be constant. Thus, many mathematicians believe the second pinching conjecture still holds without the assumption that
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
is constant. In this case, Peng-Terng
[
26
,
27
]
showed that there does exist a pinching phenomenon if
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}>n
and
n\leq 5
.
Later, Cheng-Ishikawa
[
8
]
, Wei-Xu
[
34
]
and Zhang
[
37
]
promoted it to
n\leq 8
.
Finally, Ding-Xin
[
16
]
proved that if
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}>n
then
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}>n+\frac{n}{23}
for all dimension
n
. After that, Xu-Xu
[
35
]
and Lei-Xu-Xu
[
20
]
improved the pinching constant
\frac{n}{23}
to
\frac{n}{18}
.

Since all the known minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
with constant scalar curvature are isoparametric (equivalently to have constant principal curvatures), mathematicians proposed the following
strong version of Chern conjecture
:

Conjecture 2
.

A closed immersed minimal hypersurface
M^{n}
of
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
with constant scalar curvature must be isoparametric.

Actually, if
M
is a minimal isoparametric hypersurface in
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
, according to Münzner’s result
[
25
]
,
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
can only take values in the set
\left\{0,n,2n,3n,5n\right\}
. It is worth pointing out that,
isoparametric hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
are completely classified until recently (see
[
6
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
17
,
18
,
23
,
24
]
). When
n=3
, Chang
[
7
]
gave an affirmative answer to Conjecture
2
.
When
n=4
,
Deng-Gu-Wei
[
15
]
proved Conjecture
2
with an additional assumption that
M^{4}
is Willmore. Note that in this case,
M
is Willmore iff
f_{3}\equiv 0
(see
[
22
]
), where
f_{r}
denotes the
r
-th power sum of the principal curvatures for each positive integer
r
. With assumption that
R_{M}\geq 0
,
f_{3}
and the number of distinct principal curvatures
g
are constant, Tang and Yang
[
31
]
proved Conjecture
2
. Recently, Li
[
21
]
showed Conjecture
2
is true
if
f_{3}
is constant satisfies
f_{3}^{2}\leq 72
and the Gauss curvature
\mathcal{K}
satisfies
\mathcal{K}\leq 1
.
For higher dimensional case, with assumptions that the Gauss curvature
\mathcal{K}
is constant and
M
has three pairwise distinct principal curvatures everywhere, de Almeida-Brito-Scherfner-Weiss
[
14
]
showed that Conjecture
2
is true. Recently, Tang-Wei-Yan
[
32
]
and Tang-Yan
[
33
]
showed Conjecture
2
is true if
f_{r}
is constant for each
k=1,\cdots,n-1
and
R_{M}\geq 0
. Their results strongly support Conjecture
2
and generalized de Almeida-Brito’s results in
[
13
]
.

In this paper, we focus on the case of dimension
n=4
and
M
has vanishing Gauss curvature. In this case, a minimal isoparametric hypersurface must be totally geodesic (see Lemma
3
in Section 3). Therefore, based on the Conjecture
2
, a natural question is: Is a closed minimal hypersurface with constant scalar curvature and zero Gauss curvature totally geodesic? We give an affirmative answer to this question as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Let
M
be a closed minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{S}^{5}
with constant scalar curvature and zero Gauss curvature, then
M
is totally geodesic.

We emphasize that without the assumption of constant scalar curvature, there are many non-totally geodesic minimal hypersurfaces in a unit sphere with zero Gauss curvature, cf.
[
28
]
.

This paper is arranged as follows. In Section 2, we set notations and review some known formulas and results. In Section 3, we give the proof of Theorem
1
.

2.
Preliminary

In this section, we will fix some notations and list several known formulas.

Let
M
be a minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{S}^{5}
with second fundamental form
A
. We choose a local orthonormal frame
\left\{e_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{5}
in
\mathbb{S}^{5}
such that
e_{1},\cdots,e_{4}
are tangent to
M
. Let
\left\{\omega^{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{5}
be the dual frame of
\left\{e_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{5}
. Then
A
can be written as
A=\sum_{i,j=1}^{4}h_{ij}\omega^{i}\otimes\omega^{j}
with
h_{ij}=h_{ji}
.
Unless otherwise specified, throughout this paper, the summation is always from 1 to 4.
The minimality of
M
implies
0=H=\dfrac{1}{4}\sum_{i}h_{ii}
.
Set

\displaystyle F_{,i}=\nabla_{i}F,\ \ F_{,ij}=\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}F,\ \ h_{ijk}=\nabla_{k}h_{ij}\ \text{and}\ h_{ijkl}=\nabla_{l}\nabla_{k}h_{ij},

where
\nabla_{j}
is the covariant differentiation operator with respect to
e_{j}
.
The Gauss equation, the Codazzi equation and the Ricci formulas are given by (cf.
[
9
]
)

(2)

\displaystyle R_{ijkl}=

\displaystyle\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}+h_{ik}h_{jl}-h_{il}h_{jk}.

(3)

\displaystyle h_{ijk}=

\displaystyle h_{ikj}.

(4)

\displaystyle h_{ijkl}-h_{ijlk}=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}h_{im}R_{mjkl}+\sum_{m}h_{mj}R_{mikl}.

From the Gauss equation (
2
), it follows that

R_{M}=\sum_{i,j}R_{ijij}=12-\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}.

Therefore,
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
is constant iff the scalar curvature is constant.
If
M
has constant scalar curvature, Simons’ identity (
1
) implies

(5)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla A\right\rvert^{2}=\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}-4\right).

Assume
A
has principal curvature
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{4}
at some point.
Combining (
2
) with (
4
) gives

(6)

\displaystyle h_{ijkl}-h_{ijlk}=\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)\left(1+\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\right)\left(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}\right).

For each positive integer
k
, denote by

(7)

\displaystyle f_{k}={\rm tr}A^{k},\quad\sigma_{k}=\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}.

Although
f_{k}
and
\sigma_{k}
are defined by using the principal curvatures, they are actually globally well defined smooth functions.
A direct computation gives,

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&f_{1}=\sigma_{1}=4H=0,\\
&f_{2}=\sigma_{1}^{2}-2\sigma_{2}=\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2},\\
&f_{3}=\sigma_{1}^{3}-3\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}+3\sigma_{3}=3\sigma_{3},\\
&f_{4}=\sigma_{1}^{4}-4\sigma_{1}^{2}\sigma_{2}+4\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}+2\sigma_{2}^{2}-4\sigma_{4}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}-4\mathcal{K},\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{K}=\sigma_{4}=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}

is the Gauss curvature (also called Gauss-Kronecker curvature). Note that

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\equiv 0\ \ \text{iff}\ \ f_{4}\equiv\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}.

The following identities are due to Peng and Terng
[
26
]
,

(10)

\displaystyle\Delta f_{3}=

\displaystyle 3\left(4-\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\right)f_{3}+6\mathscr{C},

(11)

\displaystyle\Delta f_{4}=

\displaystyle 4\left(4-\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\right)f_{4}+4\left(2\mathscr{A}+\mathscr{B}\right),

where

(12)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}=\sum_{i,j,k}\lambda_{i}^{2}h_{ijk}^{2},\quad\mathscr{B}=\sum_{i,j,k}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}h_{ijk}^{2},\quad\mathscr{C}=\sum_{i,j,k}\lambda_{i}h_{ijk}^{2}.

3.
Proof of the main theorem

The following result is due to Cartan
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2

(cf.
[
3
, page 84]
)
.

Let
M
be an isoparametric hypersurface in
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
. Denote by
g
the number of distinct principal curvatures, and by
\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{g}
the distinct principal curvatures with corresponding multiplicities
m_{1},\cdots,m_{g}
. Then for each fixed
i=1,\cdots,g
,

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1,\ j\neq i}^{g}m_{j}\dfrac{1+\mu_{i}\mu_{j}}{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}}=0.

Although isopermetric hypersurfaces are completely classified, we can give a direct proof of the following result by Cartan’s fundamental formula.

Lemma 3
.

Assume
M
is a minimal isoparametric hypersurface in
\mathbb{S}^{5}
with zero Gauss curvature, then
M
is totally geodesic.

Proof.

Assume
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\lambda_{4}
are the principal curvatures of
M
. By our assumption,

\displaystyle 0=4H=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\lambda_{i},\quad 0=\mathcal{K}=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}.

Suppose
M
is not totally geodesic, we can assume, without loss of generality,
\lambda_{2}=0
. Then the principal curvatures are

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}=0\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\lambda_{4}>0.

There are three possibilities:

•

\lambda_{3}=0
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle g=3,\mu_{1}=\lambda_{1},\mu_{2}=0,\mu_{3}=\lambda_{4}=-\lambda_{1},m_{1}=1,m_{2}=2,m_{3}=1.

Therefore, for
i=1
in (
13
), we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{2}{\lambda_{1}}+\dfrac{1-\lambda_{1}^{2}}{2\lambda_{1}}=0,

which implies
\lambda_{1}=-\sqrt{5}
. Consequently,
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}=\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{4}^{2}=10
. However, from Münzner’s result
[
25
]
,
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}=(g-1)n=2\times 4=8
, we get a contradiction.

•

\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{4}>0
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle g=3,\mu_{1}=\lambda_{1},\mu_{2}=0,\mu_{3}=\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{4}=-\dfrac{1}{2}\lambda_{1},m_{1}=1,m_{2}=1,m_{3}=2.

Therefore, for
i=2
in (
13
), we obtain,

\displaystyle 0=\dfrac{1}{0-\lambda_{1}}+2\dfrac{1}{0-\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}\right)}=\dfrac{3}{\lambda_{1}},

which is impossible.

•

0<\lambda_{3}<\lambda_{4}
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle g=4,\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i},m_{i}=1,i=1,\cdots,4.

Therefore, for
i=2
in (
13
), we obtain,

\displaystyle 0=\dfrac{1}{0-\lambda_{1}}+\dfrac{1}{0-\lambda_{3}}+\dfrac{1}{0-\lambda_{4}}=-\dfrac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{4}+\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}},

which implies
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{4}+\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}=0
. Since
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}=0,
we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}^{2}+\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}+\lambda_{4}^{2}=0,

which is impossible for
\lambda_{4}>\lambda_{3}>0
.

Therefore,
M
must be totally geodesic.
∎

We also need an elementary algebraic lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Assume
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
a\leq b\leq c
and
a+b+c=0
, then

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}\right)^{3/2}\leq a^{3}+b^{3}+c^{3}\leq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}\right)^{3/2},

and

\displaystyle a^{3}+b^{3}+c^{3}=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}\right)^{3/2}\ \ \text{iff}\ \ b=c=-\dfrac{a}{2},

\displaystyle a^{3}+b^{3}+c^{3}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}\right)^{3/2}\ \ \text{iff}\ \ a=b=-\dfrac{c}{2},

\displaystyle a^{3}+b^{3}+c^{3}=

\displaystyle 0\ \ \text{iff}\ \ b=0\ \ \text{and}\ \ a=-c.

Lemma 5
.

Let
M
be a closed minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{S}^{5}
with constant scalar curvature and zero Gauss-Kronecker curvature. If the smallest (or largest) principal curvature
\lambda_{1}
has multiplicity two at some point
p
, then
M
is totally geodesic.

Proof.

Assume
M
is not totally geodesic, but the multiplicity of the smallest principal curvature
\lambda_{1}
is two at
p
.
Since
\mathcal{K}=0
, then at
p
we can assume the principal curvatures are

(14)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=-\lambda<0=\lambda_{3}<\lambda_{4}=2\lambda>0.

At the point
p
, by Lemma
4
,
f_{3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{3}
attains
f_{3}
’s maximum. Therefore at
p
, we have for each
k

(15)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&H_{,k}=0;\\
&\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\right)_{,k}=0;\\
&\left(f_{3}\right)_{,k}=0;\\
&\left(f_{4}\right)_{,k}=0,\end{cases}\ \ \text{which yields}\ \ \begin{cases}&\sum_{i}h_{iik}=0,\\
&\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}h_{iik}=0,\\
&\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}h_{iik}=0,\\
&\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{3}h_{iik}=0,\end{cases}

Consider that the value of principal curvatures (
14
), we have

\displaystyle h_{11k}+h_{22k}=0,\ \ h_{44k}=h_{33k}=0.

We have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla A\right\rvert^{2}=

\displaystyle 4\left(h_{111}^{2}+h_{112}^{2}\right)+6\left(h_{113}^{2}+h_{114}^{2}+h_{123}^{2}+h_{124}^{2}+h_{134}^{2}+h_{234}^{2}\right).

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}\lambda_{i}^{2}h_{ijk}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\sum_{j,k}\left(h_{1jk}^{2}+h_{2jk}^{2}+4h_{4jk}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{6}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left[4\left(h_{111}^{2}+h_{112}^{2}+h_{113}^{2}+h_{123}^{2}\right)+10\left(h_{234}^{2}+h_{134}^{2}\right)+12\left(h_{114}^{2}+h_{124}^{2}\right)\right].

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}h_{ijk}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\sum_{k}\left(h_{11k}^{2}+h_{22k}^{2}+2h_{12k}^{2}-4h_{14k}^{2}-4h_{24k}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\left[2\left(h_{113}^{2}+h_{123}^{2}\right)+4\left(h_{111}^{2}+h_{112}^{2}-h_{134}^{2}-h_{234}^{2}\right)-6\left(h_{114}^{2}+h_{124}^{2}\right)\right].

Therefore, combined the above formula with (
11
), we have,

\displaystyle 3\left[4\left(h_{111}^{2}+h_{112}^{2}\right)+6\left(h_{113}^{2}+h_{114}^{2}+h_{123}^{2}+h_{124}^{2}+h_{134}^{2}+h_{234}^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3\left\lvert\nabla A\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}-4\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(2\mathscr{A}+\mathscr{B}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 12\left(h_{111}^{2}+h_{112}^{2}\right)+10\left(h_{113}^{2}+h_{123}^{2}\right)+16\left(h_{234}^{2}+h_{134}^{2}\right)+18\left(h_{114}^{2}+h_{124}^{2}\right),

which implies

\displaystyle 0=8\left(h_{113}^{2}+h_{123}^{2}\right)+2\left(h_{134}^{2}+h_{234}^{2}\right).

Consequently,

\displaystyle h_{113}=h_{123}=h_{134}=h_{234}=0.

Moreover, since
H,\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
and
f_{4}
are all constants,
we have, for all
1\leq k,l\leq 4
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&H_{,kl}=0,\\
&\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\right)_{,kl}=0,\\
&\left(f_{4}\right)_{,kl}=0.\end{cases}

A direct computation yields,

(16)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\sum_{i}h_{iikl}=0,\\
&\sum_{i,j}\left(h_{ijk}h_{ijl}+h_{ij}h_{ijkl}\right)=0,\\
&\sum_{i}\left(\lambda_{i}^{3}h_{iikl}\right)+\sum_{i,j}\left(2\lambda_{i}^{2}h_{ijk}h_{ijl}+\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}h_{ijk}h_{ijl}\right)=0.\end{cases}

Note that, at the point
p
, we have (
14
)
and

\displaystyle h_{11k}+h_{22k}=0,h_{33k}=h_{44k}=0,h_{113}=h_{123}=h_{134}=h_{234}=0.

Therefore, for
k=l=3
, (
16
) becomes,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&h_{1133}+h_{2233}+h_{3333}+h_{4433}=0,\\
&-\lambda h_{1133}-\lambda h_{2233}+2\lambda h_{4433}=0,\\
&-\lambda^{3}h_{1133}-\lambda^{3}h_{2233}+8\lambda^{3}h_{4433}=0,\end{cases}

which implies
h_{4433}=0
.
While for
k=l=4
, (
16
) becomes,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&h_{1144}+h_{2244}+h_{3344}+h_{4444}=0,\\
&-\lambda h_{1144}-\lambda h_{2244}+2\lambda h_{4444}=-2\left(h_{114}^{2}+h_{124}^{2}\right),\\
&-\lambda^{3}h_{1144}-\lambda^{3}h_{2244}+8\lambda^{3}h_{4444}=-6\lambda^{2}\left(h_{114}^{2}+h_{124}^{2}\right),\end{cases}

which implies
h_{3344}=0
. Consequently,

\displaystyle h_{3344}-h_{4433}=0.

However, (
6
) and (
14
) yield

\displaystyle h_{3344}-h_{4433}=-2\lambda<0.

We get a contradiction.
∎

In dimension four, the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula is quite special. In our case, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 6
.

Assume
M
is a closed minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{S}^{5}
with second fundamental form
A
, Euler number
\chi(M)
, and
f_{4}
is defined by (
7
). Then the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula becomes

(17)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\dfrac{3}{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}-3f_{4}-2\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+12\right)=16\pi^{2}\chi\left(M\right).

Proof.

For a closed 4-dimensional manifold, the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula (see
[
2
]
or
[
4
]
) reads

(18)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\frac{R_{M}^{2}}{3}-\left\lvert\rm Ric\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{\left\lvert W\right\rvert^{2}}{2}\right)=16\pi^{2}\chi(M),

where
R_{M},Ric,W
are the scalar curvature, Ricci tensor, Weyl curvature tensor of
M
respectively, which can be calculated as follows (see
[
1
, p.117]
),

\displaystyle R_{M}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}R_{ijij},

\displaystyle\left\lvert{\rm Ric}\right\rvert^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}R_{ij}^{2}=:\sum_{i,j}\left(\sum_{k}R_{ikjk}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\left\lvert W\right\rvert^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,l}W_{ijkl}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,l}\left[R_{ijkl}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left(R_{ik}\delta_{jl}-R_{il}\delta_{jk}+R_{jl}\delta_{ik}-R_{jk}\delta_{il}\right)+\dfrac{R_{M}}{6}\left(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}\right)\right]^{2}.

Then combined the above formulas and Gauss equation (
2
), a direct computation yields

\displaystyle R_{M}=

\displaystyle 12-\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2},

\displaystyle\left\lvert Ric\right\rvert^{2}=

\displaystyle 36-6\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}+f_{4},

\displaystyle\left\lvert W\right\rvert^{2}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{7}{3}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}-4f_{4}.

Substituting the above formulas into Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula (
17
), we obtain (
18
).
∎

Remark.

Since for
n\geq 4
, locally conformally flat is equivalent to
W\equiv 0
. Therefore, by the proof of the above lemma, a minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{S}^{5}
is locally conformally flat iff
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}=\dfrac{12}{7}f_{4}
. By a direct computation, the trace-free Ricci tensor
\mathring{Ric}=Ric-\dfrac{R_{M}}{4}Id
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathring{Ric}\right\rvert^{2}=f_{4}-\dfrac{1}{4}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}.

Hence,
M
is Einstein iff
\mathring{Ric}\equiv 0
, i.e.,
4f_{4}=\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}
.

We also need a result due to Cheng-Yang.

Lemma 7

(
[
36
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Let
M^{4}
be a closed minimal hypersurface of
S^{5}
with constant scalar curvature and constant
f_{4}
. If
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}>4
, then
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\geq\frac{20}{3}
.

Remark.
Cheng-Yang actually proved the above lemma for general dimension
n
and with the assumption that
f_{3}
is constant instead of
f_{4}
is constant. The key ingredients of their proof are equalities (
10
) and (
11
). When
f_{4}
is constant, we can change a little in the proof of
[
36
, Lemma 3.1]
, such that

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle 3\left(4-\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\right)f_{3}+6\mathscr{C},\ \ \text{at a point}\ \ x_{0}\in M,

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle 4\left(4-\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\right)f_{4}+4\left(2\mathscr{A}+\mathscr{B}\right),\ \ \text{at all points in }\ M.

Consider at
x_{0}
, we can get the same result as
[
36
, Lemma 3.1]
, and consequently we can obtain Lemma
7
. Cheng-Yang also mentioned this lemma in
[
36
, Remark 1]
.

Now we can give the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Suppose
M
is not totally geodesic, then from Lemma
5
, we know the smallest principal curvature
\lambda_{1}
can not has multiplicity two at any point. Since
\mathcal{K}=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}=0
,
\lambda_{1}
can not has multiplicity 3 at any point either. Therefore,
\lambda_{1}
always has multiplicity one. Consequently, the unit eigenvectors of

\lambda_{1}
form a nowhere vanishing vector field on
M
, which implies
\chi(M)=0
by the Poincaré-Hopf theorem. Combined this fact with
\mathcal{K}\equiv 0
and relation (
9
), the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula (
17
) is reduced to

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}-6\right)=0,

which implies
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}=6
.

On the other hand, since
M
has constant scalar curvature Simons’ identity implies
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\geq 4
. However,
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}=4
iff
M
is a Clifford hypersurface (
[
9
,
19
]
), which is impossible since the Clifford hypersurfaces have non-zero Gauss curvature. Thus, we have
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}>4
. Notice that,
\mathcal{K}=0
implies
f_{4}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}
is constant. Consequently, we have
\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}\geq\frac{20}{3}
from Lemma
7
. We get a contradiction.
∎

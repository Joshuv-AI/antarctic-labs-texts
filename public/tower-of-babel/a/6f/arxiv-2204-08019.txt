Abstract
Given a local ring ( R , 𝔪 ) (R,\mathfrak{m}) and an elliptic curve E ⁡ ( R / 𝔪 ) E(R/\mathfrak{m}) , we define its elliptic loop as the points of ℙ 2 ​ ( R ) \mathbb{P}^{2}(R) projecting to E E under the canonical modulo- 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} reduction, endowed with an operation that extends the curve’s addition. While its subset of points satisfying the curve’s Weierstrass equation is a group, this larger object is proved to be a power-associative abelian algebraic loop, which is seldom completely associative. When an elliptic loop has no points of order 3 3 , its affine part is obtained as a stratification of a one-parameter family of elliptic curves defined over R R , which we call layers . Stronger associativity properties are established when 𝔪 e \mathfrak{m}^{e} vanishes for small values of e ∈ ℤ e\in\mathbb{Z} . When the underlying ring is R = ℤ / p e ​ ℤ R=\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z} , the infinity part of an elliptic loop is generated by two elements, the group structure of layers may be established and the points with the same projection and same order possess a geometric description.

Introduction

Elliptic curves have been proving to be a field of intense research and fruitful applications.
They were born as smooth plane projective cubics with (at least) a rational point defined over a field, but they have been generalized over arbitrary base schemes as proper smooth curves with geometrically connected genus-one fibers, with a prescribed zero section.

Their main interest resides in the possibility of endowing these objects with an abelian group structure
[
9
, Theorem 2.1.2]
, so that they constitute the abelian varieties of minimal positive dimension.
When the underlying structure is a ring with prescribed properties, these objects may be embedded in a projective plane and their addition law may be explicitly determined
[
10
]
.
Among the several research lines offered by these objects, their lifting has played a crucial role both from theoretical
[
13
]
and applied
[
17
,
20
]
purview.

In the literature, lifts of elliptic curves over fields are usually investigated under several strong (classical) assumptions, including: lifting to integral domains, having a prescribed characteristic and enjoying certain levels of topological separation (usually, Hausdorff).
With such requirements, the resulting lifts may still be proven to be abelian varieties
[
13
]
.
Moreover, there are typically multiple lifts of a given curve, which are considered individually.
Apart from the canonical lift
[
5
]
, the others are often indistinguishable and their interplay has not been deeply investigated.

In this work, we relax these assumptions by investigating large objects made of all the projective points reducing to a given base curve.
They are globally non-associative, yet they contain the usual lifts as associative sections.
This way, we are not selecting special lifts of the base curve, but we are considering all of them at once.

Precisely, we study the projective points over a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
with
6\in R^{*}
that project to an elliptic curve of odd order defined by a short Weierstrass model over
R/\mathfrak{m}
, namely we define the set

\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(R)=\{(X:Y:Z)\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(R)\ |\ X^{3}+AXZ^{2}+BZ^{3}-Y^{2}Z\in\mathfrak{m}\}.

We call these objects
elliptic loops
, as we prove they are power-associative abelian algebraic loops (Proposition
3.6
and Proposition
5.3
).
This result follows by establishing a sufficient criterion to ensure triples associativity in terms of the rank of a prescribed matrix (Theorem
5.2
).

Elliptic loops are not the first occurrence of quasigroups arising from operations between the points of cubics
[
6
]
.
Elliptic curves may also be obtained as sections of cubic hypersurfaces, which were proved to be commutative Moufang loops
[
11
]
that are not necessarily groups
[
8
]
.
The loops defined in the present paper have weaker associativity properties, since they do not need to satisfy the Moufang identities.
However, they are usually larger objects that appear to be natural candidates for investigating the lifts of the given curve, as they are proved to have a notably ordered geometric structure.

The affine part of elliptic loops is completely described in terms of a new family of elliptic curves, which are defined over
R
by non-invertible linear combinations of the Weierstrass polynomial and its Hessian polynomial, namely for every
t\in\mathfrak{m}
by the equation

x^{3}+Axz^{2}+Bz^{3}-y^{2}z-t(3Ax^{2}z+3xy^{2}+9Bxz^{2}-A^{2}z^{3})=0.

Those projective cubics are again abelian varieties, which we call
layers
since in absence of points of order
3
they are proved to stratify the affine part of
\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(R)
(Proposition
6.3
).
For
t=0
, this construction agrees with the standard definition of elliptic curves over the ring
R
.

The infinity part of
\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(R)
exhibits a different behavior: layers may have a non-trivial intersection at infinity when
R
has zero-divisors (Proposition
7.3
).
Moreover, layers do not necessarily cover the whole infinity part: if the base ring is Hausdorff with a discrete valuation
\mathrm{v}
, we show that layers never contain non-zero points at infinity
(X:1:Z)
with
\mathrm{v}(Z)\leq\mathrm{v}(X)
(Lemma
7.2
).

If the base ring is Hausdorff and
\mathfrak{m}
has a non-zero integer generator (e.g. a Cohen ring), the loop at infinity behaves similarly to the additive group of
\mathfrak{m}
(Proposition
7.1
).
When
R
is a discrete valued ring, this is consistent with the known structure of formal groups constructed over large powers of
\mathfrak{m}
, which can be made explicit via the discrete logarithm map
[
19
, Theorem IV.6.4]
.

When
\mathfrak{m}^{\mathcal{N}}\neq\langle 0\rangle
for small values of
\mathcal{N}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we can explicitly exhibit triples of non-associative points of elliptic loops (Section
8.1
).
Conversely, if
\mathfrak{m}^{\mathcal{N}}=\langle 0\rangle
for small values of
\mathcal{N}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, then special families of triples may be proved associative (Section
8.2
).

When
R
is Hausdorff and
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=\langle 0\rangle
, the points with any given finite torsion lying over the same base point
P
may be obtained via the additive action of a group at infinity (Theorem
9.1
), therefore they are proved to be projectively collinear, and if
\mathfrak{m}
is a principal
\mathbb{Z}
-module, then they precisely are all the points of such line projecting to
P
(Proposition
9.2
).

When the underlying ring is
\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z}
, the infinity part of elliptic loops is generated (as a loop) by the points
(p:1:0)
and
(0:1:p)
, which have both order
p^{e-1}
(Theorem
10.1
).
These generators play different roles with respect to elliptic curves defined over the same ring, as the group
\langle(0:1:p)\rangle
is proved to be a forbidden locus for layers (Lemma
10.2
).
Moreover, there are several other groups at infinity of the same order, namely the infinity part of layers, which is also proved to be a cyclic group of order
p^{e-1}
(Proposition
10.3
).
As a consequence, if we let
E
be the projected elliptic curve over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, every layer is isomorphic to either
\mathbb{Z}/p^{e-1}\mathbb{Z}\times E
or to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z}
.

2
Preliminaries

In this paper,
R
is always a local commutative ring with unity and
6\in R^{*}
, and its maximal ideal is denoted by
\mathfrak{m}
.
We let
\pi:R\to R/\mathfrak{m}
be the canonical projection into the residue field, and to simplify the notation we also denote every componentwise or coefficientwise projection by
\pi
, i.e. for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
we write

\pi\big((X_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\big)=\big(\pi(X_{i})\big)_{1\leq i\leq n}\in(R/\mathfrak{m})^{n}\quad{\rm and}\quad\pi\left(\sum_{d=0}^{n}f_{d}x^{d}\right)=\sum_{d=0}^{n}\pi(f_{d})x^{d}\in(R/\mathfrak{m})[x].

Given some elements
u_{1},\dots,u_{s}
of a ring
\mathcal{R}
, we denote by
\langle u_{1},\dots,u_{s}\rangle
the ideal they generate in
\mathcal{R}
.
In the context of the present paper,
\mathcal{R}
will be either the local ring
R
or a polynomial ring with coefficients in
R
.

A tuple in
(X_{0},\dots,X_{n})\in R^{n+1}
is called
primitive
if
\langle X_{0},\dots,X_{n}\rangle=R
, which for local rings is equivalent to having at least one entry
X_{i}
not in
\mathfrak{m}
.
The group of units
R^{*}=R\setminus\mathfrak{m}
acts on the primitive
(n+1)
-tuples by componentwise multiplication, and its quotient by this action is the
projective
n
-space
over
R
, denoted by
\mathbb{P}^{n}(R)
.
The orbit of an element
(X_{0},\dots,X_{n})
is denoted by
(X_{0}:\dots:X_{n})
.
We say that a point is
affine
if its last coordinate does not belong to
\mathfrak{m}
, otherwise we say it is
at infinity
.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
A\in M_{n\times m}(R)
.
For every integer
1\leq t\leq\min\{n,m\}
, the
t-minor ideal

I_{t}(A)
is defined as the ideal generated by the
t
-th minor determinants of
A
, namely the ideal generated by all the determinants of the
t\times t
submatrices of
A
.
By convention we set
I_{0}(A)=R
, and for every
t>\min\{n,m\}
we set
I_{t}(A)=\langle 0\rangle
.
We also recall
[
3
, Chapter 4]
that the rank of
A
is defined by

\textnormal{rk}\,A=\max_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\{I_{t}(A)\neq\langle 0\rangle\}.

This notion of rank over rings provides us with a practical way of testing if two projective points are equal
[
15
, Lemma 6]
:

(X_{0}:\dots:X_{n})=(Y_{0}:\dots:Y_{n})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}(R)\quad\iff\quad\textnormal{rk}\,\begin{bmatrix}X_{0}&\dots&X_{n}\\
Y_{0}&\dots&Y_{n}\end{bmatrix}=1.

Given a homogeneous polynomial
F\in R[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
, its evaluation on a given projective point
P=(X_{0}:\dots:X_{n})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}(R)
is unique modulo
R^{*}
, and we equivalently denote it by
F(P)
or
F(X_{0},\dots,X_{n})
.

In this work, we only consider the
projective plane

\mathbb{P}^{2}(R)
.
Irreducible projective plane cubics with a rational point are called
elliptic curves
.
Since
6\in R^{*}
, it is well-known
[
19
, Section III.1]
that, up to a change of coordinates, they all arise from zero-sets of short Weierstrass polynomials, i.e. there are
A,B\in R
such that

\Delta_{A,B}=-(4A^{3}+27B^{2})\in R^{*},

and such that the elliptic curve is given by

E_{A,B}(R)=\{(X:Y:Z)\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(R)\ |\ X^{3}+AXZ^{2}+BZ^{3}-Y^{2}Z=0\}.

Lenstra has proved
[
10
, Section 3]
that these objects have a group structure, which arises from a combination of different addition laws
[
2
, Section 4]
.
Whenever the projected curve
E_{A,B}(R/\mathfrak{m})
has no
2
-torsion points, this operation may be entirely described by the addition law corresponding to
(0:1:0)
.
Since this is a slight assumption in the computational practice (e.g. for ordinary curves employed in cryptographic applications), we assume that it is always the case.
We recall here for convenience the explicit formulation of this addition law, with the minor correction pointed out in
[
23
, Section 2.11]
:

(X_{1}:Y_{1}:Z_{1})+_{(0:1:0)}(X_{2}:Y_{2}:Z_{2})=(T_{1}:T_{2}:T_{3}),

where

\displaystyle T_{1}=\

\displaystyle Y_{1}Y_{2}(X_{1}Y_{2}+X_{2}Y_{1})-AX_{1}X_{2}(Y_{1}Z_{2}+Y_{2}Z_{1})-A(X_{1}Y_{2}+X_{2}Y_{1})(X_{1}Z_{2}+X_{2}Z_{1})

\displaystyle-3B(X_{1}Y_{2}+X_{2}Y_{1})Z_{1}Z_{2}-3B(X_{1}Z_{2}+X_{2}Z_{1})(Y_{1}Z_{2}+Y_{2}Z_{1})+A^{2}(Y_{1}Z_{2}+Y_{2}Z_{1})Z_{1}Z_{2},

\displaystyle T_{2}=\

\displaystyle Y_{1}^{2}Y_{2}^{2}+3AX_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2}+9BX_{1}X_{2}(X_{1}Z_{2}+X_{2}Z_{1})-A^{2}X_{1}Z_{2}(X_{1}Z_{2}+2X_{2}Z_{1})

\displaystyle-A^{2}X_{2}Z_{1}(2X_{1}Z_{2}+X_{2}Z_{1})-3ABZ_{1}Z_{2}(X_{1}Z_{2}+X_{2}Z_{1})-(A^{3}+9B^{2})Z_{1}^{2}Z_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle T_{3}=\

\displaystyle 3X_{1}X_{2}(X_{1}Y_{2}+X_{2}Y_{1})+Y_{1}Y_{2}(Y_{1}Z_{2}+Y_{2}Z_{1})+A(X_{1}Y_{2}+X_{2}Y_{1})Z_{1}Z_{2}

\displaystyle+A(X_{1}Z_{2}+X_{2}Z_{1})(Y_{1}Z_{2}+Y_{2}Z_{1})+3B(Y_{1}Z_{2}+Y_{2}Z_{1})Z_{1}Z_{2}.

The following lemma provides us with a clean and computationally efficient way of evaluating the above addition law.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{3},Q_{4}\in R[X_{i},Y_{i},Z_{i}]_{i\in\{1,2\}}
be the homogeneous polynomials of bidegree
(1,1)
defined by

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=-AX_{1}Z_{2}-AX_{2}Z_{1}-3BZ_{1}Z_{2}+Y_{1}Y_{2},

\displaystyle Q_{2}=A^{2}Z_{1}Z_{2}-AX_{1}X_{2}-3BX_{1}Z_{2}-3BX_{2}Z_{1},

\displaystyle Q_{3}

\displaystyle=AZ_{1}Z_{2}+3X_{1}X_{2},

\displaystyle Q_{4}=AX_{1}Z_{2}+AX_{2}Z_{1}+3BZ_{1}Z_{2}+Y_{1}Y_{2}.

By defining

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=(X_{1}Y_{2}+X_{2}Y_{1})Q_{1}+(Z_{1}Y_{2}+Z_{2}Y_{1})Q_{2},

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=Q_{1}Q_{4}-Q_{2}Q_{3},

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=(X_{1}Y_{2}+X_{2}Y_{1})Q_{3}+(Z_{1}Y_{2}+Z_{2}Y_{1})Q_{4},

we have

(T_{1}:T_{2}:T_{3})=(X_{1}:Y_{1}:Z_{1})+_{(0:1:0)}(X_{2}:Y_{2}:Z_{2}).

Proof.

Straightforward computation.
∎

2.1
Discrete valued ring

Let
\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
be the ordered additive monoid obtained from
(\mathbb{Z},+,\leq)
by adding the element
\infty
, such that for every
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
it satisfies

\infty+\gamma=\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad\infty\geq\gamma.

When the map

\mathrm{v}:R\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\},\quad X\mapsto\mathrm{v}(X)=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\{X\in\mathfrak{m}^{i}\},

satisfies, for every
X,Y\in R
,

\mathrm{v}(XY)=\mathrm{v}(X)+\mathrm{v}(Y),

it is called the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic valuation
of
R
, and it turns this ring into a
discrete valued ring
, which corresponds to the classical
discrete valuation ring
(DVR) when
R
is a domain.
Sometimes
\mathrm{v}
is required to be surjective
[
12
]
, but we remark that this is not the case here, as we are mainly interested in proper subsets of
\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
.

It is easy to see that
R^{*}=\{X\in R\ |\ \mathrm{v}(X)=0\}
, and
\mathfrak{m}=\{X\in R\ |\ \mathrm{v}(X)\geq 1\}
.
Moreover, for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
the ideal
\mathfrak{m}^{i}
is
\mathrm{v}
-closed, namely

X\in\mathfrak{m}^{i},\ \mathrm{v}(Y)\geq\mathrm{v}(X)\implies Y\in\mathfrak{m}^{i}.

Finally, for any integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote the valuation of
n
-times the unity of
R
by
\mathrm{v}(n)=\mathrm{v}(n\cdot 1_{R})
.

2.2
Hausdorff rings

Many results about points at infinity will require the considered ring
R
to be
Hausdorff
, namely its
\mathfrak{m}
-topology is Hausdorff, i.e.

\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\mathfrak{m}^{i}=\langle 0\rangle.

For Hausdorff rings we may define the
nilpotency
of
\mathfrak{m}
as

\mathcal{N}=\inf_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}\big\{\mathfrak{m}^{i}=\langle 0\rangle\big\}.

We remark that a Hausdorff ring may have
\mathfrak{m}^{i}\neq\langle 0\rangle
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, e.g. if
R=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the ring of
p
-adic integers. In such cases, we have
\mathcal{N}=\inf\emptyset=\infty
.

In Hausdorff rings, the inclusion sequence

R=\mathfrak{m}^{0}\supset\mathfrak{m}\supset\dots\supset\mathfrak{m}^{\mathcal{N}}=\langle 0\rangle

is strictly decreasing until it stabilizes to
\langle 0\rangle
.

If
R
is a Hausdorff discrete valued ring, then
0
is the unique element of maximal (infinite) valuation.
There are many examples of such rings.
As an instance, when the maximal ideal is principal
\mathfrak{m}=\langle\mathcal{\mu}\rangle
, we call its generator
\mathcal{\mu}
the
uniformizer
of
R
, and it is well known that the following are equivalent.

•

R
is Noetherian,

•

R
is Hausdorff,

•

either
R
is a DVR, or there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
such that the ideals of
R
are precisely
\{\mathfrak{m}^{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq n}
.

The ring
R
is a DVR precisely when
\mathcal{N}=\infty
.
If
\mathcal{N}
is finite, it coincides with the
n
of the above equivalence.
Moreover,
R
is a field precisely when
\mathcal{N}=1
, and in this case the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic valuation of
R
is the trivial valuation, namely for every
X\neq 0
we have
\mathrm{v}(X)=0
.
When this is not the case, for every non-zero element
X\in R
there are uniquely determined
u\in R^{*}
and
e_{X}\in\{0,\dots,\mathcal{N}-1\}
such that

X=u\mathcal{\mu}^{e_{X}}.

In this case, the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic valuation of
R
is the map
\mathrm{v}(X)=e_{X}
.

2.3
Finite rings

When the ring
R
is local and finite, we have convenient enumerative properties
[
7
]
, which we recall here:
\mathfrak{m}
is a nilpotent ideal, the residue field is a finite field
R/\mathfrak{m}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}
for some prime power
q=p^{t}
, and every
\mathfrak{m}^{i}/\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space, so there is a positive
e\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that

|R|=q^{e},\quad|\mathfrak{m}|=q^{e-1}.

One can explicitly count the points in projective spaces by observing that affine elements may be assumed to have the last entry equal to
1
, while the infinity part of
\mathbb{P}^{n}(R)
may be realized by
|\mathfrak{m}|
copies of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(R)
. Hence, we inductively have

|\mathbb{P}^{n}(R)|=\sum_{i=0}^{n}|R|^{n-i}|\mathfrak{m}|^{i}=q^{en}\left(1+\frac{1}{q}+\dots+\frac{1}{q^{n}}\right)=\frac{q^{en+1}-(q^{n})^{e-1}}{q-1}.

2.4
Non-associative structures

We recall some basic definitions and results about non-associative algebraic structures
[
18
]
.

A
magma

\mathcal{M}
is a set equipped with a (closed) binary operation
+:\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
.

A
quasigroup

\mathcal{Q}
is a magma satisfying the
Latin square property
, i.e.

\forall P,Q\in\mathcal{Q}\ \exists!\ X,Y\in\mathcal{Q}\ :\ \begin{cases}P+X=Q,\\
Y+P=Q.\end{cases}

A
loop

\mathcal{L}
is a quasigroup with
identity
, i.e. there is
\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{L}
such that

\forall P\in\mathcal{L}\ :\ P+\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}+P=P.

A magma (resp. quasigroup, loop) is called
abelian
if its operation
+
is commutative.

An abelian loop
\mathcal{L}
is called

•

alternative loop
, if
\forall P,Q\in\mathcal{L}\ :\ P+(P+Q)=(P+P)+Q
,

•

Jordan loop
, if
\forall P,Q\in\mathcal{L}\ :\ (P+P)+(P+Q)=P+\big(Q+(P+P)\big)
,

•

Moufang loop
(or
Bol loop
), if
\forall P,Q,R\in\mathcal{L}\ :\ \big(P+(Q+R)\big)+R=\big((P+R)+R\big)+Q
,

•

power-associative loop
, if the subloop generated by any element is associative,

•

diassociative loop
, if the subloop generated by any subset of size at most two is associative.

Finally, we observe that diassociative clearly implies power-associative, while it can be shown that Moufang implies both alternative and dissociative.

3
Elliptic Loops

Definition 3.1

(Elliptic Loop)
.

Let
A,B\in R
be elements defining an elliptic curve
E_{A,B}(R/\mathfrak{m})
without points of even order.
We define its
Elliptic Loop
as the set

\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(R)=\{(X:Y:Z)\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(R)\ |\ X^{3}+AXZ^{2}+BZ^{3}-Y^{2}Z\in\mathfrak{m}\},

endowed with the operation
+_{(0:1:0)}
, simply referred to as
+
.
When the coefficients and the underlying ring are understood, we simply denote it by
\mathcal{L}
.
The polynomial
x^{3}+Axz^{2}+Bz^{3}-y^{2}z\in R[x,y,z]
is referred to as the
defining polynomial of

\mathcal{L}
.

Remark 3.2
.

The operation
+
always depends on the considered
A,B\in R
.
Moreover, as recalled in the previous section, the absence of points of order
2
in
E(R/\mathfrak{m})
guarantees that the operation
+
is always well-defined between points of
\mathcal{L}
.

Elliptic loops canonically project on their underlying elliptic curve via
\pi
, and it is easy to see that when
R
is finite this is a
|\mathfrak{m}|^{2}
-covering of the curve.
We define their
affine
(resp.
infinity
)
points

\mathcal{L}^{a}
(resp.
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
) as those projecting to affine points of
E_{A,B}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
(resp. to
\mathcal{O}
), namely

\mathcal{L}^{a}=\{(X:Y:Z)\in\mathcal{L}\ |\ Z\not\in\mathfrak{m}\},\quad\mathcal{L}^{\infty}=\{(X:1:Z)\}_{X,Z\in\mathfrak{m}}.

We remark that, as a set,
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}=\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O})
is independent of the considered curve parameters
A,B\in R
, so it is common to all the elliptic loops over the same ring
R
, but its operation depends on the coefficients of the underlying curve.

The elliptic loop constructed over
E=E_{A,B}(R/\mathfrak{m})
contains (set-theoretically) the points of every possible lift of
E
over
R
.
The next lemma shows that its affine part is precisely made of the affine points of classical elliptic curves over
R
that project on
E
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(R)
be an elliptic loop and
P\in\mathcal{L}^{a}
be one of its affine points.
For every
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}
there exists
\beta\in\mathfrak{m}
such that
P\in E_{A+\alpha,B+\beta}(R)
.

Proof.

Since
P
is affine, we may assume
P=(X:Y:1)
.
For every
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}
, we define

\beta=Y^{2}-X^{3}-(A+\alpha)X-B.

We immediately verify that
P
satisfies the Weierstrass equation of
E_{A+\alpha,B+\beta}(R)
. Since
\alpha X\in\mathfrak{m}
and by definition of elliptic loop
Y^{2}-X^{3}-AX-B\in\mathfrak{m}
, then also
\beta\in\mathfrak{m}
.
∎

Unlike the affine component, the infinity part
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
usually contains more points than those arising from elliptic curves over
R
, such as
\{(0:1:\alpha)\}_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}\setminus\{\mathcal{O}\}}
.

We now show that the name is well-given, i.e.
\mathcal{L}
is always an algebraic loop.
We begin by proving that it is a set with a well-defined commutative binary operation, namely an abelian magma.

Proposition 3.4
.

Every elliptic loop
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(R)
is an abelian magma with identity
\mathcal{O}=(0:1:0)
.
Moreover, every element
(X:Y:Z)\in\mathcal{L}
has a unique inverse

-(X:Y:Z)=(X:-Y:Z).

Proof.

The addition law
+
is symmetric in the two addenda, hence it is commutative.
Moreover, it is defined by polynomial relations in their entries, hence it commutes with
\pi
, so for every
P_{1},P_{2}\in\mathcal{L}
we have

\pi(P_{1}+P_{2})=\pi(P_{1})+\pi(P_{2})\in E_{A,B}(R/\mathfrak{m}).

This shows that
P_{1}+P_{2}\in\mathcal{L}
, hence
+
is a well-defined binary operation on
\mathcal{L}
.

To show that
\mathcal{O}
acts as identity, we explicitly compute

(X:Y:Z)+(0:1:0)=(XY:Y^{2}:YZ).

Since
E(R/\mathfrak{m})
has no points of order
2
, the latter needs to be a proper projective point, hence we have
Y\not\in\mathfrak{m}
and we conclude
(XY:Y^{2}:YZ)=(X:Y:Z)\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(R)
.

To show that
(X:-Y:Z)
is an inverse of
(X:Y:Z)
, we compute

(X:Y:Z)+(X:-Y:Z)=(0:3AX^{4}+18BX^{3}Z-6A^{2}X^{2}Z^{2}-6ABXZ^{3}+Y^{4}+(-A^{3}-9B^{2})Z^{4}:0),

and since the operation is well-defined, the result equals
\mathcal{O}
.

As for the uniqueness part, let
P_{1}=(X_{1}:Y_{1}:Z_{1})
and
P_{2}=(X_{2}:Y_{2}:Z_{2})
be two points of
\mathcal{L}
such that

(T_{1}:T_{2}:T_{3})=P_{1}+P_{2}=\mathcal{O}\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(R).

We observe that, as polynomials in
R[X_{i},Y_{i},Z_{i}]_{i\in\{1,2\}}
, we have

(X_{1}Y_{2}+X_{2}Y_{1})T_{2}\in\langle T_{1},T_{3}\rangle,\quad(Y_{1}Z_{2}+Y_{2}Z_{1})T_{2}\in\langle T_{1},T_{3}\rangle.

hence
(T_{1}:T_{2}:T_{3})=\mathcal{O}
implies
X_{1}Y_{2}+X_{2}Y_{1}=Y_{1}Z_{2}+Y_{2}Z_{1}=0
. Since the base curve has odd order, we have
Y_{1},Y_{2}\not\in\mathfrak{m}
, hence we conclude

(X_{2}:Y_{2}:Z_{2})=\left(-\frac{Y_{1}}{Y_{2}}X_{2}:-\frac{Y_{1}}{Y_{2}}Y_{2}:-\frac{Y_{1}}{Y_{2}}Z_{2}\right)=(X_{1}:-Y_{1}:Z_{1}),

i.e. the unique inverse of
(X_{1}:Y_{1}:Z_{1})
is
(X_{1}:-Y_{1}:Z_{1})
.
∎

If elliptic loops were associative, they would be groups.
Although this is almost never the case (Section
8.1
), a weak form of associativity always holds.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
P,Q\in\mathcal{L}
be two points of an elliptic loop. Then

P+(-P+Q)=Q.

Proof.

Let
Q=(Q_{1}:Q_{2}:Q_{3})
. We can directly compute by means of an algebraic calculator

(T_{1}:T_{2}:T_{3})=P+(-P+Q),

and we symbolically verify that

I_{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}T_{1}&T_{2}&T_{3}\\
Q_{1}&Q_{2}&Q_{3}\end{bmatrix}\right)=\langle 0\rangle,

which means
(T_{1}:T_{2}:T_{3})=Q\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(R)
.
∎

From Lemma
3.5
we can show that elliptic loops satisfy the Latin square property, so they are quasigroups with identity, i.e. algebraic loops.

Proposition 3.6
.

Every elliptic loop
\mathcal{L}
is an abelian algebraic loop.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.4
we know that
\mathcal{L}
is an abelian magma with identity, hence it is sufficient to show that for every
P,Q\in\mathcal{L}
there is a unique
R\in\mathcal{L}
such that
P+R=Q
.
By Lemma
3.5
a solution is
R=Q-P
, and it is unique because if
R_{2}\in\mathcal{L}
satisfies the same equation, by the same lemma we have

R_{2}=-P+(P+R_{2})=-P+Q,

which implies
R=R_{2}
.
∎

One may check that even for relatively simple rings (such as
R=\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z}
, see Section
10
) elliptic loops need not be alternative loops, Jordan loops or diassociative loops, hence neither Moufang loops.
However, in Section
5
we will prove that they are necessarily power-associative loops.

4
The Hessian of an elliptic curve

Given a projective degree-
d
hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(R)
, we may construct its Hessian as the hypersurface of degree
(d-2)(n+1)
defined by the vanishing of its Hessian matrix.
We refer to
[
4
]
for a general treatment of such surfaces.
In this work we are primarily interested in the Hessian polynomial, i.e. the polynomial defining the Hessian hypersurface: given a homogeneous degree-
d
polynomial
F\in R[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
, its
Hessian
(polynomial) is defined by

\mathcal{H}_{F}=\det(\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}F)_{0\leq i,j\leq n}\in R[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}].

It is easy to see that the surfaces defined by a polynomial and its Hessian lie in the same projective space precisely in the following cases:

•

d=3,n=2
: the curves defined by
F
and
\mathcal{H}_{F}
are plane projective cubics.

•

d=4,n=1
: they are quartics defined on a projective line.

Here we only consider the first case: the Hessian polynomial of a short Weierstrass polynomial
F=x^{3}+Axz^{2}+Bz^{3}-y^{2}z\in R[x,y,z]
is

\mathcal{H}_{F}=-8(3Ax^{2}z+3xy^{2}+9Bxz^{2}-A^{2}z^{3})\in R[x,y,z].

As we are interested in the zeroes of
\mathcal{H}_{F}
over rings with
2\in R^{*}
, we can neglect the factor
-8
.
We also recall that the Hessian of an elliptic curve of non-zero
j
-invariant defines another elliptic (hence non-singular) curve
[
4
, Proposition 5.11]
.

Regardless of their smoothness, the curves defined by
F
and
\mathcal{H}_{F}
over
R
lie in the same ambient space, so we may investigate their intersection properties.
In particular, the following lemma extends
[
19
, Exercise III.3.9]
, and it shows that they may intersect only on lifts of
3
-torsion points.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{L}
be a point of the elliptic loop with defining polynomial
F
.
Then

3\pi(P)=\mathcal{O}\quad\iff\quad\mathcal{H}_{F}(P)\in\mathfrak{m}.

Proof.

We formally compute the polynomials
D_{1},D_{2},D_{3},T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}\in R[x,y,z]
such that

(D_{1}:D_{2}:D_{3})=(x:y:z)+(x:y:z),\quad(T_{1}:T_{2}:T_{3})=(x:y:z)+(D_{1}:D_{2}:D_{3}).

We straightforwardly verify that

T_{2}\mathcal{H}_{F}\in\langle F,D_{2}z+D_{3}y,D_{1}z-D_{3}x,D_{1}y+D_{2}x\rangle.

If
3\pi(P)=\mathcal{O}
, then
T_{2}(P)\in R^{*}
and
\pi(2P)=\pi(-P)\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(R/\mathfrak{m})
, hence for each
P=(X:Y:Z)
we have

I_{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}D_{1}(P)&D_{2}(P)&D_{3}(P)\\
X&-Y&Z\end{bmatrix}\right)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}.

Moreover, since
P\in\mathcal{L}
then
F(P)\in\mathfrak{m}
, therefore we conclude
\mathcal{H}_{F}(P)\in\mathfrak{m}
.

On the other side, we symbolically verify the ideal inclusion

\langle T_{1},T_{3}\rangle\subseteq\langle F,\mathcal{H}_{F}\rangle.

Since
F(P)\in\mathfrak{m}
by definition of
\mathcal{L}
, then
\mathcal{H}_{F}(P)\in\mathfrak{m}
implies
T_{1}(P),T_{3}(P)\in\mathfrak{m}
, from which we conclude that
3\pi(P)=\mathcal{O}
.
∎

We now observe that the addition law
+
respects linear combinations of
F
and
\mathcal{H}_{F}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
F
be the defining polynomial of an elliptic loop
\mathcal{L}
, and
\alpha,\beta\in R
.
For every
P_{1},P_{2}\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(R)
such that

(\alpha F+\beta\mathcal{H}_{F})(P_{1})=0=(\alpha F+\beta\mathcal{H}_{F})(P_{2}),

we have

(\alpha F+\beta\mathcal{H}_{F})(P_{1}+P_{2})=0.

Proof.

We define
T_{\alpha,\beta}=\alpha F+\beta\mathcal{H}_{F}\in R[x,y,z]
and we symbolically verify that in the polynomial ring
R[x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}]_{i\in\{1,2\}}
we have

T_{\alpha,\beta}\big((x_{1}:y_{1}:z_{1})+(x_{2}:y_{2}:z_{2})\big)\in\langle T_{\alpha,\beta}(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1}),T_{\alpha,\beta}(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})\rangle.

(1)

Thus, for every pair of points
P_{1},P_{2}\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(R)
such that
T_{\alpha,\beta}(P_{1})=0=T_{\alpha,\beta}(P_{2})
, we always have
T_{\alpha,\beta}(P_{1}+P_{2})=0
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Lemma
4.2
does not ensure that
P_{1}+P_{2}
is a proper projective point. As an instance, if the elliptic curve defined by
F
has
2
-torsion points, this operation may produce points with all entries in
\mathfrak{m}
.
However, this lemma may be extended to other addition laws defined in
[
2
]
, which may also be proved to projectively agree with a few more assumptions
[
21
]
.
This way, this result holds also if the elliptic curve defined by
F
has points of even order.

5
Power-Associativity

Even for non-associative loops, one may define a notion of
multiple
for a given point
P
recursively: for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we set

0P=\mathcal{O},\quad(-n)P=n(-P),\quad(n+1)P=nP+P.

We denote the set of multiples of a given point
P
by

\langle P\rangle=\{nP\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}.

If the loop operation is associative when restricted to the multiples of every given point, then we say that the loop is
power-associative
.
Equivalently, the multiples of an element in power-associative loops form a group, i.e. for every
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

(n+m)P=nP+mP.

Those loops are computationally friendly, since fast-multiplication techniques may be applied to evaluate
nP
in at most
O(\log n)
point additions.

In this section, we prove that elliptic loops are power-associative.
To show that, we employ the following objects.

Definition 5.1

(Associativity matrix)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an elliptic loop.
Given
n
points
P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\in\mathcal{L}
, we define their
associativity matrix
as

\mathbb{A}(P_{1},\dots,P_{n})=\begin{bmatrix}F(P_{1})&\dots&F(P_{n})\\
\mathcal{H}_{F}(P_{1})&\dots&\mathcal{H}_{F}(P_{n})\end{bmatrix}\in M_{2\times n}(R).

The following theorem shows that a sub-maximal rank of the associativity matrix is a sufficient condition for establishing points associativity.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}\in\mathcal{L}
be points of an elliptic loop.

(i)

We have

\textnormal{rk}\,\mathbb{A}(P_{1},P_{2}+P_{3})\leq\textnormal{rk}\,\mathbb{A}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}).

(ii)

If
\textnormal{rk}\,\mathbb{A}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})\leq 1
, then the triple is associative, i.e.

(P_{1}+P_{2})+P_{3}=P_{1}+(P_{2}+P_{3})
.

Proof.

Let
F
be the defining polynomial of
\mathcal{L}
.

(
i
)

It is sufficient to show that for
i\in\{1,2\}
we have
I_{i}\big(\mathbb{A}(P_{1},P_{2}+P_{3})\big)\subseteq I_{i}\big(\mathbb{A}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})\big)
.

Since the
1
-minor ideal of a matrix is the ideal generated by its entries, the case
i=1
follows from the inclusion (
1
) of Lemma
4.2
, by considering
T_{1,0}
and
T_{0,1}
.
As for the
2
-minors, we formally compute in
R[x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}]_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}
the sum

T=(x_{2}:y_{2}:z_{2})+(x_{3}:y_{3}:z_{3}),

and we explicitly verify that

F(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})\mathcal{H}_{F}(T)-F(T)\mathcal{H}_{F}(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})\in I_{2}\Big(\mathbb{A}\big((x_{1}:y_{1}:z_{1}),(x_{2}:y_{2}:z_{2}),(x_{3}:y_{3}:z_{3})\big)\Big).

We observe that
\big(F(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})\mathcal{H}_{F}(T)-F(T)\mathcal{H}_{F}(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})\big)(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})
is the unique generator of
I_{2}\big(\mathbb{A}(P_{1},P_{2}+P_{3})\big)
, from which the inclusion between the
2
-minor ideals follows.

(

⁢
i
i

)

We explicitly compute
S_{1},S_{2},S_{3},T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}\in R[x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}]_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}
such that

\displaystyle(S_{1}:S_{2}:S_{3})

\displaystyle=\big((x_{1}:y_{1}:z_{1})+(x_{2}:y_{2}:z_{2})\big)+(x_{3}:y_{3}:z_{3}),

\displaystyle(T_{1}:T_{2}:T_{3})

\displaystyle=(x_{1}:y_{1}:z_{1})+\big((x_{2}:y_{2}:z_{2})+(x_{3}:y_{3}:z_{3})\big).

We straightforwardly verify that

I_{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}S_{1}&S_{2}&S_{3}\\
T_{1}&T_{2}&T_{3}\end{bmatrix}\right)\subseteq I_{2}\Big(\mathbb{A}\big((x_{1}:y_{1}:z_{1}),(x_{2}:y_{2}:z_{2}),(x_{3}:y_{3}:z_{3})\big)\Big),

hence when
\textnormal{rk}\,\mathbb{A}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})\leq 1
we have
I_{2}\big(\mathbb{A}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})\big)=\langle 0\rangle
, so we conclude

\displaystyle(P_{1}+P_{2})+P_{3}

\displaystyle=\big(S_{1}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}):S_{2}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}):S_{3}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})\big)

\displaystyle=\big(T_{1}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}):T_{2}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}):T_{3}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})\big)=P_{1}+(P_{2}+P_{3}),

namely the addition law on
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
is associative.
∎

By means of Theorem
5.2
, we show that every elliptic loop is power-associative.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{L}
be a point of an elliptic loop. Then
\langle P\rangle
is a group.

Proof.

Let
F
be the defining polynomial of
\mathcal{L}
. By part

(

⁢
i
i

)

of Theorem
5.2
it is sufficient to show that for every
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\textnormal{rk}\,\begin{bmatrix}F(mP)&F(nP)\\
\mathcal{H}_{F}(mP)&\mathcal{H}_{F}(nP)\end{bmatrix}\leq 1.

(2)

Since
F(\mathcal{O})=\mathcal{H}_{F}(\mathcal{O})=0
,
F(P)=F(-P)
and
\mathcal{H}_{F}(P)=\mathcal{H}_{F}(-P)
, we may assume without losing of generality that
n\geq m\geq 1
.
Moreover, we observe that the case
m=n
is trivial, since

I_{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}F(nP)&F(nP)\\
\mathcal{H}_{F}(nP)&\mathcal{H}_{F}(nP)\end{bmatrix}\right)=\langle F(nP)\mathcal{H}_{F}(nP)-\mathcal{H}_{F}(nP)F(nP)\rangle=\langle 0\rangle.

(3)

We prove by extended induction on
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
that the inequality (
2
) holds for every
1\leq m\leq n
.

[n=1]
: There is only one possible
m
, that is
m=n=1
.

[1,\dots,n-1\to n]
: By part

(
i
)

of Theorem
5.2
we have

\textnormal{rk}\,\begin{bmatrix}F(mP)&F(nP)\\
\mathcal{H}_{F}(mP)&\mathcal{H}_{F}(nP)\end{bmatrix}\leq\textnormal{rk}\,\begin{bmatrix}F(mP)&F\big((n-1)P\big)&F(P)\\
\mathcal{H}_{F}(mP)&\mathcal{H}_{F}\big((n-1)P\big)&\mathcal{H}_{F}(P)\end{bmatrix}.

When
m=n
the inequality (
2
) holds by (
3
), while if
m<n
then by inductive hypothesis

{\rm rk}\begin{bmatrix}F(mP)&F\big((n-1)P\big)\\
\mathcal{H}_{F}(mP)&\mathcal{H}_{F}\big((n-1)P\big)\end{bmatrix}\leq 1,\ {\rm rk}\begin{bmatrix}F\big((n-1)P\big)&F(P)\\
\mathcal{H}_{F}\big((n-1)P\big)&\mathcal{H}_{F}(P)\end{bmatrix}\leq 1,\ {\rm rk}\begin{bmatrix}F(mP)&F(P)\\
\mathcal{H}_{F}(mP)&\mathcal{H}_{F}(P)\end{bmatrix}\leq 1.

Thus, we obtain

\textnormal{rk}\,\begin{bmatrix}F(mP)&F\big((n-1)P\big)&F(P)\\
\mathcal{H}_{F}(mP)&\mathcal{H}_{F}\big((n-1)P\big)&\mathcal{H}_{F}(P)\end{bmatrix}\leq 1,

which concludes the inductive step.
∎

6
Layers

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an elliptic loop with defining polynomial
F
. For every
t\in\mathfrak{m}
, we define the
t
-
layer
of
\mathcal{L}
as

L_{t}=\{P\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(R)\ |\ (F-t\mathcal{H}_{F})(P)=0\}.

By definition
t\in\mathfrak{m}
, then
L_{t}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
.
Moreover, the elliptic curve
E_{A,B}(R)
is the
0
-layer of
\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(R)
, hence there is a projective abelian variety lying inside such a loop.
The following proposition shows that this actually holds for every layer.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an elliptic loop and
t\in\mathfrak{m}
. Then
L_{t}
is a subloop of
\mathcal{L}
, which is a group with the addition law of
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

The addition law of
\mathcal{L}
is closed on
L_{t}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
by Lemma
4.2
, and they have the same unity
\mathcal{O}\in L_{t}
, so we only need to show that this operation is associative on
L_{t}
.
Let
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}\in L_{t}
, then for every
i\in\{1,2,3\}
we have

F(P_{i})=t\mathcal{H}_{F}(P_{i}).

Thus, we have

\mathbb{A}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})=\begin{bmatrix}t\mathcal{H}_{F}(P_{1})&t\mathcal{H}_{F}(P_{2})&t\mathcal{H}_{F}(P_{3})\\
\mathcal{H}_{F}(P_{1})&\mathcal{H}_{F}(P_{2})&\mathcal{H}_{F}(P_{3})\end{bmatrix},

which has vanishing
2
-minors. Hence,
\textnormal{rk}\,\mathbb{A}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})\leq 1
, so
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
associate by part

(

⁢
i
i

)

of Theorem
5.2
.
∎

We denote the affine (resp. infinity) points of
L_{t}
by

L_{t}^{a}=L_{t}\cap\mathcal{L}^{a},\quad L_{t}^{\infty}=L_{t}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}.

As shown by the following proposition, in absence of
3
-torsion points, the affine part of an elliptic loop is stratified by the affine parts of its layers.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an elliptic loop without points of order
3
.
Then
\mathcal{L}^{a}
is given by the disjoint union

\mathcal{L}^{a}=\bigsqcup_{t\in\mathfrak{m}}L_{t}^{a}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{L}
does not have points of order
3
, by Lemma
4.1
for every
P\in\mathcal{L}^{a}
we have
\mathcal{H}_{F}(P)\in R^{*}
.
Therefore, we may define

n_{P}=F(P)\big(\mathcal{H}_{F}(P)\big)^{-1}\in\mathfrak{m},

so we have
P\in L_{n_{P}}
.
As for the disjointness, we observe that
P\in L_{s}\cap L_{t}
implies

(s-t)\mathcal{H}_{F}(P)=0,

but
\mathcal{H}_{F}(P)\in R^{*}
, so we conclude
s=t
.
∎

We observe that for Weierstrass polynomials defining ordinary curves of cryptographic interest, the absence of
3
-torsion points is a standard assumption.

The group structure of layers
L_{t}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
depends on the exact sequence of groups

0\to L_{t}^{\infty}\to L_{t}\xrightarrow{\pi}E_{A,B}(R/\mathfrak{m})\to 0,

(4)

which generalizes the usual reduction sequence of elliptic curves
[
19
, Proposition VII.2.1]
.
Determining the group structure of
L_{t}^{\infty}
may be challenging, depending on the properties of the underlying ring.
For instance, in Section
10
we prove that this group is cyclic if the underlying ring is
\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z}
, but this result heavily relies on the special properties of this ring.

7
The infinity part

In this section, we outline some general properties of the infinity parts of elliptic loops.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an elliptic loop and
(X_{1}:1:Z_{1}),(X_{2}:1:Z_{2})\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
be two of its points adding to

(X:1:Z)=(X_{1}:1:Z_{1})+(X_{2}:1:Z_{2}).

If
e\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
is such that
X_{1},Z_{1},X_{2},Z_{2}\in\mathfrak{m}^{e}
, then the following hold.

(i)

We have

X\equiv X_{1}+X_{2}\bmod\mathfrak{m}^{3e},\quad Z\equiv Z_{1}+Z_{2}\bmod\mathfrak{m}^{3e}.

(ii)

For every
f\geq e
and
\delta_{X},\delta_{Z}\in\mathfrak{m}^{f}
, if

(X_{m}:1:Z_{m})=(X_{1}+\delta_{X}:1:Z_{1}+\delta_{Z})+(X_{2}:1:Z_{2}),

then

X_{m}\equiv X+\delta_{X}\bmod\mathfrak{m}^{f+2e},\quad Z_{m}\equiv Z+\delta_{Z}\bmod\mathfrak{m}^{f+2e}.

(iii)

If
R
is a discrete valued ring and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is an integer with
\mathrm{v}(n)\leq 2e
, then for every
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
the
n^{f}
-th multiple

(X_{f}:1:Z_{f})=n^{f}(X_{1}:1:Z_{1}),

satisfies

X_{f}\equiv n^{f}X_{1}\bmod\mathfrak{m}^{3e+(f-1)\mathrm{v}(n)},\quad Z_{f}\equiv n^{f}Z_{1}\bmod\mathfrak{m}^{3e+(f-1)\mathrm{v}(n)}.

(iv)

If

Lemma 7.2
.

Let

\mathrm{v}(Z)>\mathrm{v}(X).

Proof.

Since

\mathrm{v}(Z)\geq\min\{\mathrm{v}(X^{3}),\mathrm{v}(AXZ^{2}),\mathrm{v}(BZ^{3}),\mathrm{v}(3AtX^{2}Z),\mathrm{v}(3tX),\mathrm{v}(9BtXZ^{2}),\mathrm{v}(A^{2}tZ^{3})\}.

If

, since

is Hausdorff either

or
\mathrm{v}(Z)>\mathrm{v}(X)
.
If

\mathrm{v}(Z)\geq\min\{3\mathrm{v}(X),\mathrm{v}(t)+\mathrm{v}(3X)\},

which implies
\mathrm{v}(Z)>\mathrm{v}(X)
.
∎

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
R
be a Hausdorff discrete valued ring, and let
\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(R)
be an elliptic loop. Then

\bigcap_{t\in\mathfrak{m}}L_{t}^{\infty}=\begin{cases}\{\mathcal{O}\}&\text{if }\mathcal{N}\in\{1,\infty\},\\
\{(X:1:0)\}_{X\in\mathfrak{m}^{\mathcal{N}-1}}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The case
\mathcal{N}=1
is trivial, since when
R
is a field there is only one layer
L_{0}=E_{A,B}(R)
, for which
\mathcal{O}
is the unique point at infinity.

When
R
is not a field, the chain
\{\mathfrak{m}^{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
cannot stabilize at
\mathfrak{m}\neq\langle 0\rangle
, hence there is an element
t\in\mathfrak{m}\setminus\mathfrak{m}^{2}

An infinity point

that belongs to both

and

\begin{cases}F(P)=t\mathcal{H}_{F}(P),\\

t\mathcal{H}_{F}(P)=0,\end{cases}\implies\begin{cases}Z=X^{3}+AXZ^{2}+BZ^{3},\\
3tX=tAZ^{3}-3tAX^{2}Z-9tBXZ^{2}.\end{cases}

By Lemma

\mathrm{v}(Z)\geq\mathrm{v}(X)

\displaystyle\mathrm{v}(tX)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{v}(tAZ^{3}-3tAX^{2}Z-9tBXZ^{2})\geq\min\{\mathrm{v}(tAZ^{3}),\mathrm{v}(3tAX^{2}Z),\mathrm{v}(9tBXZ^{2}),\mathcal{N}\}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\mathrm{v}(tX)+\mathrm{v}(AZ^{2}),\mathrm{v}(tX)+\mathrm{v}(AXZ),\mathrm{v}(tX)+\mathrm{v}(BZ^{2}),\mathcal{N}\}.

This is only possible if

, i.e.

If
\mathcal{N}=\infty

\mathrm{v}(X)=\infty

If
\mathcal{N}
is finite, then

, i.e.

By Lemma

, which implies

On the other side, it is easy to see that if

8
More on associativity

In this section, we present special results about associativity with additional hypotheses on the base ring.
First, we show that complete associativity may almost never occur among all the points of an elliptic loop.
Afterward, we show that weak forms of associativity hold if the underlying ring has small nilpotency.

8.1
Non-group guarantee

Here we certify that elliptic loops are never groups if
\mathcal{N}\geq 3
, or if
\mathcal{N}\geq 2
and they have at least one point of order coprime to
3
.
We require a technical condition on the base ring, namely it needs to have at least one element whose power ideals do not immediately stabilize. This is a very broad assumption for general rings, but we remark that it is never the case if
R
is a field.
This is not surprising, as elliptic loops over fields agree with their underlying elliptic curves, which are associative.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
R
be a ring with an element
p\in R
such that
p^{2}\not\in\langle p^{3}\rangle
, let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(R)
be an elliptic loop and
P\in\mathcal{L}^{a}
.
Then

\big(P+(p:1:p)\big)+(0:1:p)\neq P+\big((p:1:p)+(0:1:p)\big).

Proof.

Let
P=(X:Y:1)
, and compute

(S_{1}:S_{2}:S_{3})=\big(P+(p:1:p)\big)+(0:1:p),\quad(T_{1}:T_{2}:T_{3})=P+\big((p:1:p)+(0:1:p)\big).

Let us assume by contradiction that they are equal, then
S_{i}T_{j}-S_{j}T_{i}=0
for every
1\leq i\neq j\leq 3
, hence the ideal

I=\langle p^{3},S_{1}T_{2}-S_{2}T_{1},S_{1}T_{3}-S_{3}T_{1},S_{2}T_{3}-S_{3}T_{2}\rangle

is equal to
\langle p^{3}\rangle
. However, we can symbolically verify that

2(4A^{3}+27B^{2})^{2}p^{2}Y^{3}\in I.

Since the three quantities
2
,
\Delta_{A,B}=4A^{3}+27B^{2}
and
Y
are invertible in
R
by definition of
\mathcal{L}
, it follows that
p^{2}\in\langle p^{3}\rangle
, contradicting the hypothesis.
∎

R

8.2
Low nilpotency

We now examine associativity properties that hold over Hausdorff rings with low values of
\mathcal{N}

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
R
be a Hausdorff ring with
\mathcal{N}\leq 5
and
\mathcal{L}
be an elliptic loop.
Then
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
is an abelian group.

Proof.

Given three points
\{(X_{i}:1:Z_{i})\}_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
, we explicitly compute

\displaystyle(S_{1}:S_{2}:S_{3})

\displaystyle=\big((X_{1}:1:Z_{1})+(X_{2}:1:Z_{2})\big)+(X_{3}:1:Z_{3}),

\displaystyle(T_{1}:T_{2}:T_{3})

\displaystyle=(X_{1}:1:Z_{1})+\big((X_{2}:1:Z_{2})+(X_{3}:1:Z_{3})\big),

and we verify that

I_{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}S_{1}&S_{2}&S_{3}\\
T_{1}&T_{2}&T_{3}\end{bmatrix}\right)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{5},

which concludes the proof since
\mathcal{N}\leq 5
implies
\mathfrak{m}^{5}=\langle 0\rangle
.
∎

By virtue of Lemma

is the best result we can achieve on
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
in terms of

For even smaller values of

, this group is isomorphic to two copies of the additive group of

Let

be a Hausdorff ring with
\mathcal{N}\leq 3
and
\mathcal{L}
be an elliptic loop. Then

\mathcal{L}^{\infty}\to(\mathfrak{m},+)^{2},\quad(X:1:Z)\to(X,Z),

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{m}^{3}=\langle 0\rangle
, it immediately follows by Proposition
7.1
-

(
i
)

.
∎

We now provide a family of weak associativity results, which only hold for
\mathcal{N}\leq 2

Lemma 8.6

Let

be an elliptic loop.
For every

We formally compute

Lemma 8.7

Let

be a Hausdorff ring with
\mathcal{N}\leq 2
and
\mathcal{L}
be an elliptic loop.
For every

(P+R_{1})-(Q+R_{2})=(P-Q)+(R_{1}-R_{2}).

Proof.

By hypothesis there are

such that if

, then

By computing

(S_{1}:S_{2}:S_{3})=(P+R_{1})-(Q+R_{2}),\quad(T_{1}:T_{2}:T_{3})=(P-Q)+(R_{1}-R_{2}),

we straightforwardly verify that

from which the thesis follows by observing that
\langle m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},X_{1},Z_{1},X_{2},Z_{2}\rangle^{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}=\langle 0\rangle

Lemma 8.8

Let
R
be a Hausdorff ring with
\mathcal{N}\leq 2
and
\mathcal{L}
be an elliptic loop.
For every

\pi(P)=\pi(Q)=\pi(R)

(P+Q)-R=P+(Q-R).

Proof.

By hypothesis there are

such that if

We compute

(S_{1}:S_{2}:S_{3})=(P+Q)-R,\quad(T_{1}:T_{2}:T_{3})=P+(Q-R),

and we verify that

I_{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}S_{1}&S_{2}&S_{3}\\
T_{1}&T_{2}&T_{3}\end{bmatrix}\right)\subseteq\langle m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},m_{4},m_{5},m_{6}\rangle^{2},

and since
\langle m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},X_{1},Z_{1},X_{2},Z_{2}\rangle^{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}=\langle 0\rangle
, then the triple
P,Q,-R
is associative.
∎

Remark 8.9
.

In the setting of Lemma
8.8
we can avoid useless parentheses: as the result is independent of the association order, we simply denote it by
P+Q-R
.

Proposition 8.10
.

Let
R
be a Hausdorff ring with
\mathcal{N}\leq 2
and
\mathcal{L}
be an elliptic loop.
For every
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{3}\in\mathcal{L}
such that
\pi(P_{1})=\pi(P_{2})=\pi(P_{3})
and
\pi(Q_{1})=\pi(Q_{2})=\pi(Q_{3})
, we have

(P_{1}+P_{2}-P_{3})+(Q_{1}+Q_{2}-Q_{3})=(P_{1}+Q_{1})+(P_{2}+Q_{2})-(P_{3}+Q_{3}).

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{L}
has no even-order points, we may assume
P_{1}=(X_{1}:1:Z_{2})
,
Q_{1}=(X_{2}:1:Z_{2})
and we can consider
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},m_{4},m_{5},m_{6},m_{7},m_{8}\in\mathfrak{m}
such that

\displaystyle P_{2}=(X_{1}+m_{1}:1:Z_{1}+m_{2}),

\displaystyle P_{3}=(X_{1}+m_{3}:1:Z_{1}+m_{4}),

\displaystyle Q_{2}=(X_{2}+m_{5}:1:Z_{2}+m_{6}),

\displaystyle Q_{3}=(X_{2}+m_{7}:1:Z_{2}+m_{8}).

We formally compute

(S_{1}:S_{2}:S_{3})=(P_{1}+P_{2}-P_{3})+(Q_{1}+Q_{2}-Q_{3}),\quad(T_{1}:T_{2}:T_{3})=(P_{1}+Q_{1})+(P_{2}+Q_{2})-(P_{3}+Q_{3}).

An intensive computation shows that

I_{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}S_{1}&S_{2}&S_{3}\\
T_{1}&T_{2}&T_{3}\end{bmatrix}\right)\subseteq\langle m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},m_{4},m_{5},m_{6},m_{7},m_{8}\rangle^{2},

which concludes the proof since
\langle m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},m_{4},m_{5},m_{6},m_{7},m_{8}\rangle^{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}=\langle 0\rangle
.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
8.10
, we observe that scalar multiplication is distributive on the associative triples of Lemma
8.8
.

Lemma 8.11
.

Let
R
be a Hausdorff ring with
\mathcal{N}\leq 2
and
\mathcal{L}
be an elliptic loop.
For every triple
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}\in\mathcal{L}
such that
\pi(P_{1})=\pi(P_{2})=\pi(P_{3})
and every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

m(P_{1}+P_{2}-P_{3})=mP_{1}+mP_{2}-mP_{3}.

Proof.

We prove it by induction on
m
.

[
m=1
] There is nothing to prove.

[
m\to m+1
] By inductive hypothesis we have

(m+1)(P_{1}+P_{2}-P_{3})=(P_{1}+P_{2}-P_{3})+(mP_{1}+mP_{2}-mP_{3}).

Since
\pi(mP_{1})=\pi(mP_{2})=\pi(mP_{2})=m\pi(P_{1})
, then the thesis follows by Proposition
8.10
.
∎

9
Finite order in the same fiber

In this section, we employ the results of Section
8.2
for characterizing the torsion elements of elliptic loops when
\mathcal{N}\leq 2
.

For every integer
q\in\mathbb{Z}
and elliptic loop
\mathcal{L}
, we denote the points of
\mathcal{L}
annihilated by the multiplication-by-
q
map as

\mathcal{L}_{q}=\{P\in\mathcal{L}\ |\ qP=\mathcal{O}\}.

In other terms, the set
\mathcal{L}_{q}
is made of the points of
\mathcal{L}
whose order is finite and divides
q
.
Moreover, given a point
P\in\mathcal{L}_{q}
, we denote the points of
\mathcal{L}_{q}
lying over
\pi(P)
by

\mathcal{L}_{q/P}=\{Q\in\mathcal{L}_{q}\ |\ \pi(Q)=\pi(P)\},

and we denote the set of their differences by

\mathcal{D}_{q/P}=\{P_{1}-P_{2}\}_{P_{1},P_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{q/P}}.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
R
be a Hausdorff ring with
\mathcal{N}\leq 2
and
\mathcal{L}
be an elliptic loop.
For every
q\in\mathbb{Z}
and
P\in\mathcal{L}
we have that
\mathcal{D}_{q/P}
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
, and

\mathcal{L}_{q/P}=P+\mathcal{D}_{q/P}.

Proof.

By Lemma
8.4
we know that
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
is an abelian group, which clearly contains
\mathcal{D}_{q/P}
.
Moreover, by Lemma
8.7
, for every
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},P_{4}\in\mathcal{L}_{q/P}
we have

(P_{1}-P_{2})+(P_{3}-P_{4})=(P_{1}+P_{3}-P_{4})-P_{2},

and
P_{1}+P_{3}-P_{4}\in\mathcal{L}_{q/P}
by Lemma
8.11
.
Hence the addition law is closed on
\mathcal{D}_{q/P}
, and since
\mathcal{O}=P_{1}-P_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{q/P}
and
-(P_{1}-P_{2})=P_{2}-P_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{q/P}
, then
\mathcal{D}_{q/P}
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
.

The inclusion
\mathcal{L}_{q/P}\subseteq P+\mathcal{D}_{q/P}
is clear, since every
Q\in\mathcal{L}_{q/P}
may be written as
Q=P+(Q-P)
by Lemma
3.5
.
On the other side, for every
P_{1},P_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{q/P}
, by Lemma
8.11
we have

q(P+P_{1}-P_{2})=qP+qP_{1}-qP_{2}=0,

and since
\pi
is linear, also
\pi(P+P_{1}-P_{2})=\pi(P)
.
Thus, we conclude
P+P_{1}-P_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{q/P}
.
∎

Proposition 9.2
.

Let
R
be a Hausdorff ring with
\mathcal{N}\leq 2
and
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(R)
be an elliptic loop.
For every
q\in\mathbb{Z}
and
P\in\mathcal{L}
, if
\mathcal{D}_{q/P}
is cyclic then there is a projective line
L\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}(R)
such that

\mathcal{L}_{q/P}\subseteq L.

Moreover, if also
\mathfrak{m}
is a principal
\mathbb{Z}
-module, then there is a projective line
L^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}(R)
such that

\mathcal{L}_{q/P}=L^{\prime}\cap\pi^{-1}\big(\pi(P)\big).

Proof.

Let
(X:1:Z)\in\mathcal{L}_{q/P}
and
(m_{x}:1:m_{z})\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
be a generator of
\mathcal{D}_{q/P}
.
By Lemma
8.5
and Theorem
9.1
, there is
(m_{x}:1:m_{z})\in\mathcal{D}_{q/P}
such that

\mathcal{L}_{q/P}=\{(X:1:Z)+(km_{x}:1:km_{z})\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}.

By defining

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=A^{2}Z^{2}m_{z}-AX^{2}m_{z}-2AXZm_{x}-6BXZm_{z}-3BZ^{2}m_{x}+m_{x},

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=2AXZm_{z}+AZ^{2}m_{x}+3BZ^{2}m_{z}+3X^{2}m_{x}+m_{z}.

we can straightforwardly verify that, since
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=\langle 0\rangle
, then

(X:1:Z)+(km_{x}:1:km_{z})=(X+k\alpha:1:Z+k\beta).

Hence, all the points of
\mathcal{L}_{q/P}
belong to the projective line
L
defined by

-\beta x+(\beta X-\alpha Z)y+\alpha z=0.

As for the moreover part, we notice that both
\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{m}
, hence if
\mathfrak{m}=\mathcal{\mu}\mathbb{Z}
, then we can write
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}\mathcal{\mu},\ \beta=\beta^{\prime}\mathcal{\mu}
and check that again we have

-\beta^{\prime}x+(\beta^{\prime}X-\alpha^{\prime}Z)y+\alpha^{\prime}z=0,

so
\mathcal{L}_{q/P}
is contained in the line
L^{\prime}
defined by the above equation (and in
\pi^{-1}\big(\pi(P)\big)
by definition).
On the other side, for every
Q\in\pi^{-1}\big(\pi(P)\big)
there are
s_{x},s_{z}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
Q=(X+s_{x}\mathcal{\mu}:1:Z+s_{z}\mathcal{\mu})
.
If also
Q\in L^{\prime}
, then

0=-\beta^{\prime}(X+s_{x}\mathcal{\mu})+(\beta^{\prime}X-\alpha^{\prime}Z)+\alpha^{\prime}(Z+s_{z}\mathcal{\mu})=-\beta^{\prime}s_{x}\mathcal{\mu}+\alpha^{\prime}s_{z}\mathcal{\mu}.

If
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{m}
, then
Q=P\in\mathcal{L}_{q/P}
.
Otherwise, one of them is invertible, without losing of generality
\alpha^{\prime}\in R^{*}
.
In this case we have

X+s_{x}\mathcal{\mu}=X+\frac{s_{x}}{\alpha^{\prime}}\alpha^{\prime}\mathcal{\mu}=X+\frac{s_{x}}{\alpha^{\prime}}\alpha,\quad Z+s_{z}\mathcal{\mu}=Z+\frac{s_{x}}{\alpha^{\prime}}\beta^{\prime}\mathcal{\mu}=Z+\frac{s_{x}}{\alpha^{\prime}}\beta.

Hence, by defining
k=\frac{s_{x}}{\alpha^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we conclude

Q=(X+k\alpha:1:Z+k\beta)=(X:1:Z)+k(m_{x}:1:m_{z})\in\mathcal{L}_{q/P},

which proves the second inclusion.
∎

10
The
\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z}
-case

In this section, we consider an integer prime
p
and a positive integer
e
, and we examine elliptic loops over the ring
R=\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z}
, whose maximal ideal is
\mathfrak{m}=\langle p\rangle
.
The
\mathfrak{m}
-adic discrete valuation of this ring is usually referred to as the
p
-adic valuation, and it is denoted by
\mathrm{v}_{p}
.

An elliptic loop
\mathcal{L}
over this ring projects to an elliptic curve
E
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, whose possible group structures are known
[
14
,
22
]
, although they may be difficult to be effectively computed.

We denote by
q=|E|
the size of the underlying curve.
In this setting,
\pi
is a
p^{2(e-1)}
-covering, thus we have

|\mathcal{L}^{\infty}|=p^{2(e-1)},\quad|\mathcal{L}^{a}|=(q-1)p^{2(e-1)}.

By Proposition
3.6
and
5.3
we know that such
\mathcal{L}
is a power-associative abelian algebraic loop, while by Lemma
8.1
and
8.2
we also know that it is not a group if one of the following conditions hold:

(\text{A})\ e\geq 3\quad(\text{B})\ e\geq 2\text{ and }p\geq 17.

In fact, when
p\geq 17
the base curve always has a point of order other than
3
, since it needs to have at least
10
elements by the Hasse Theorem
[
23
, Theorem 4.2]
and its group of points has rank at most
2

[
23
, Theorem 4.1]
.

By testing every existent elliptic curve defined over a prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
(with
p\leq 17
) with a group of points isomorphic to either
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
, one may verify
[
21
]
that there are precisely
6
elliptic loops over local quotients of
\mathbb{Z}
that are actual groups, namely

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{4,2}(\mathbb{Z}/25\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathcal{L}_{4,3}(\mathbb{Z}/25\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/15\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0,4}(\mathbb{Z}/49\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/21\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0,2}(\mathbb{Z}/49\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbb{Z}/21\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/21\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0,3}(\mathbb{Z}/169\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathcal{L}_{0,10}(\mathbb{Z}/169\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbb{Z}/39\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/39\mathbb{Z}.

We also know that
\mathcal{L}^{a}
is (set-theoretically) made of all the affine points of elliptic curves lifting
E(\mathbb{F}_{p})
(Lemma
3.3
), and when
3\nmid q
it is stratified by the layers of
\mathcal{L}
(Proposition
6.3
).

Over these special rings, the results of Section
7
lead to a complete characterization of the infinity part
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
.

Theorem 10.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z})
be an elliptic loop. Then

(i)

\langle(p:1:0)\rangle\cap\langle(0:1:p)\rangle=\{\mathcal{O}\}
.

(ii)

|\langle(p:1:0)\rangle|=|\langle(0:1:p)\rangle|=p^{e-1}
.

(iii)

For every
P\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
there are uniquely determined integers
0\leq\alpha,\beta\leq p^{e-1}-1
, such that

P=\alpha(p:1:0)+\beta(0:1:p).

Proof.

(
i
)

By Proposition
7.1
-

(

⁢
i
v

)

(X:1:Z)=\alpha(p:1:0)

Z\equiv 0\mod p^{3\mathrm{v}_{p}(X)-1}\implies X=Z=0\text{ or }\mathrm{v}_{p}(Z)>\mathrm{v}_{p}(X).

Similarly, if the same point may also be written as
\beta(0:1:p)
, then

X\equiv 0\mod p^{3\mathrm{v}_{p}(Z)-1}\implies X=Z=0\text{ or }\mathrm{v}_{p}(X)>\mathrm{v}_{p}(Z).

The unique point satisfying both the above conditions is
(X:1:Z)=\mathcal{O}
.

(

⁢
i
i

By Proposition
7.1
-

(

⁢
i

)

we know that if

hence the lowest possible

such that

is
\alpha=p^{e-1}
, which is therefore the order of the group
\langle(p:1:0)\rangle
.
In the same way, we prove the analogous result on
\langle(0:1:p)\rangle

(

⁢
i

i

The case

\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(\mathbb{F}_{p})=E_{A,B}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
has a unique point at infinity, hence we may assume
e\geq 2

Since
|\mathcal{L}^{\infty}|=|\ker\pi|=p^{2(e-1)}
, it is sufficient to prove that for every
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and every

we have

\alpha_{1}(p:1:0)+\beta_{1}(0:1:p)=\alpha_{2}(p:1:0)+\beta_{2}(0:1:p)\implies\begin{cases}\alpha_{1}\equiv\alpha_{2}\bmod p^{\epsilon-1},\\
\beta_{1}\equiv\beta_{2}\bmod p^{\epsilon-1}.\end{cases}

We prove it by induction on
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
.

[

] It follows from Proposition

-

(

)

.

[
\epsilon\to\epsilon+1
] By inductive hypotheses there are
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
such that we have
\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{1}+\alpha p^{\epsilon-1}
and
\beta_{2}=\beta_{1}+\beta p^{\epsilon-1}
.
Since
\mathcal{L}
is power-associative, we can write

\alpha_{2}(p:1:0)+\beta_{2}(0:1:p)=\big(\alpha_{1}(p:1:0)+\alpha p^{\epsilon-1}(p:1:0)\big)+\big(\beta_{1}(0:1:p)+\beta p^{\epsilon-1}(0:1:p)\big).

Moreover, if we define

(\delta_{1}:1:\delta_{2})=\alpha p^{\epsilon-1}(p:1:0),\quad(\delta_{3}:1:\delta_{4})=\beta p^{\epsilon-1}(0:1:p),

by Proposition
7.1

\delta_{1}\equiv\alpha p^{\epsilon}\bmod p^{\epsilon+1},\quad\delta_{2}\equiv\delta_{3}\equiv 0\bmod p^{\epsilon+1},\quad\delta_{4}\equiv\beta p^{\epsilon}\bmod p^{\epsilon+1}.

Furthermore, by defining

(X_{\alpha}:1:Z_{\alpha})=\alpha_{1}(p:1:0),\ (X_{\beta}:1:Z_{\beta})=\beta_{1}(0:1:p),\ (X:1:Z)=(X_{\alpha}:1:Z_{\alpha})+(X_{\beta}:1:Z_{\beta})

and

(X_{1}:1:Z_{1})=(X_{\alpha}:1:Z_{\alpha})+(\delta_{1}:1:\delta_{2}),\quad(X_{2}:1:Z_{2})=(X_{\beta}:1:Z_{\beta})+(\delta_{3}:1:\delta_{4}),

Thus,
\alpha_{2}(p:1:0)+\beta_{2}(0:1:p)

and

\alpha_{1}\equiv\alpha_{2}\bmod p^{\epsilon}
and

Theorem
10.1
shows that
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
may be set theoretically thought of as

\mathcal{L}^{\infty}\longleftrightarrow\langle(p:1:0)\rangle\times\langle(0:1:p)\rangle.

However, we remark that the above correspondence does not preserve the addition law, as the sum in
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
is not necessarily the product-sum of these subloops, unless
e\leq 5
(Lemma
8.4
).

The next lemma shows that
\langle(0:1:p)\rangle
has a trivial intersection with every layer.

Lemma 10.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z})
be an elliptic loop. For every
t\in p\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z}
, we have

L_{t}\cap\langle(0:1:p)\rangle=\{\mathcal{O}\}.

Proof.

By Proposition
7.1
every non-zero multiple
(X:1:Z)=\alpha(0:1:p)
satisfies

X\equiv 0\bmod p^{\mathrm{v}_{p}(\alpha)+2},\quad Z\equiv\alpha p\bmod p^{\mathrm{v}_{p}(\alpha)+2}.

Hence, we have
\mathrm{v}_{p}(X)\geq\mathrm{v}_{p}(Z)
.
If also
(X:1:Z)\in L_{t}
, then by Lemma
7.2
we conclude
(X:1:Z)=\mathcal{O}
.
∎

Not only the infinity part of the loop may be characterized, but also the infinity part of its layers can be established.
Indeed, all the
p^{e-1}
layers
L_{t}
of
\mathcal{L}
have a cyclic infinity part.

Proposition 10.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z})
be an elliptic loop. For every
t\in p\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z}
there is a unique
Z_{t}\in p\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z}
such that

L_{t}^{\infty}=\langle(p:1:Z_{t})\rangle.

Proof.

We consider the polynomial
F-t\mathcal{H}_{F}(p,1,z)\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
.
Since
F-t\mathcal{H}_{F}(p,1,z)(p,1,0)\equiv 0\bmod p
and its derivative in
z=0
is constantly
-1
, by the Hensel Lemma there is a unique
Z_{t}\in p\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z}
such that
(p:1:Z_{t})\in L_{t}
.
Since
\pi|_{L_{t}}
is a
p^{e-1}
-covering of the base curve, it is sufficient to show that the order of
(p:1:Z_{t})
is
p^{e-1}
.
For a given
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
, if we define

(X_{\alpha}:1:Z_{\alpha})=\alpha(p:1:Z_{t}),

then by proposition
7.1
-

(

⁢
i
v

)

we have

X_{\alpha}\equiv\alpha p\bmod p^{\mathrm{v}_{p}(\alpha)+2},\quad Z_{\alpha}\equiv\alpha Z_{t}\bmod p^{\mathrm{v}_{p}(\alpha)+2}.

Thus, the minimal positive
\alpha
that makes
(X_{\alpha}:1:Z_{\alpha})=\mathcal{O}
is
\alpha=p^{e-1}
.
∎

When the size
q
of the underling curve is coprime to
p
, the short exact sequence (
4
) splits, thus Proposition
10.3
provides the group structure of layers:

L_{t}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p^{e-1}\mathbb{Z}\times E_{A,B}(\mathbb{F}_{p}).

We also remark that the entry
Z_{t}
in the generator of
L_{t}^{\infty}
may be explicitly computed by truncating to the correct exponent of the classical series
[
19
, Chapter IV]
.
This result extends the group classification of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}

[
15
, Theorem 17]
to every layer of
\mathcal{L}
.

Finally, we observe that over such rings, the group
\mathcal{D}_{q/P}
of Section
9
is almost always cyclic, hence Proposition
9.2
applies: there is a projective line that locates the points lying over the same base point that have
q
-torsion.

Proposition 10.4
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{A,B}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})
be an elliptic loop, and let
P\in\mathcal{L}
be a point of order
q
, such that
\gcd(3p,q)=1
. Then

\begin{cases}\mathcal{D}_{q/P}=\{\mathcal{O}\}&\text{if }P\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty},\\
\mathcal{D}_{q/P}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}&\text{if }P\in\mathcal{L}^{a}.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Theorem
9.1
we know that
\mathcal{D}_{q/P}
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
, hence by Theorem
10.1
it is a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.

If
P\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
, then its order is divisible by both
p
and
q
, hence
P=\mathcal{O}
.

Conversely, if
P\in\mathcal{L}^{a}
then
\pi(P)\in E_{A,B}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is an affine point of order coprime to
3
.
Since
\gcd(p,q)=1
, by Proposition
10.3
all the layers
L_{t}
of
\mathcal{L}
have the same structure, namely

L_{t}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\times E_{A,B}(\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Hence,
\mathcal{L}_{q/P}
intersects the every layer
L_{t}
in precisely one point
P_{t}
, i.e. the point corresponding to
\big(0,\pi(P)\big)
under the above group isomorphism.
Different layers never intersect over points with order coprime to
3
, as proved in Proposition
6.3
.
Therefore, all the
P_{t}
’s are different, and the group

\mathcal{D}_{q/P}=\{P_{t}-P_{s}\}_{s,t\in p\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}}

contains at least
p
different points, but it cannot have size
p^{2}
since some elements are repeated (e.g.
P_{t}-P_{t}=P_{s}-P_{s}
).
Thus, we must have
|\mathcal{D}_{q/P}|=p
.
∎

Conclusions and open problems

We presented the construction of a new family of power-associative abelian loops with a canonical projection on an elliptic curve
E
defined over the residue field of a given local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
.

When
\mathfrak{m}=\langle 0\rangle
(i.e.
R
is a field), the results of this paper coincide with known results of elliptic curves over fields, but when
\mathfrak{m}
is non-trivial this construction simultaneously encodes all the lifts of the base curve.
In particular, layers appear to correspond 1-1 to elliptic curves over
R
with a canonical projection on
E
, but the identification of the element
t\in\mathfrak{m}
that gives rise to the layer
L_{t}
corresponding to the canonical lift of
E
is still open.

Determining the structure of elliptic loops over rings different from
\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z}
is also considered a challenging research line, as it is deeply connected to determining the structure of the formal group associated with elliptic curves over local rings.
Besides, we identified large associative structures over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z}
, but it is still only conjectured that layers and groups of size
p^{e-1}
at infinity are the largest possible subgroups of an elliptic loop.

Finally, the investigation of the abelian varieties admitting a loop sup-structure is open, as well as the few cases excluded from the present paper (
6\in\mathfrak{m}
or points with order not coprime to
6
).

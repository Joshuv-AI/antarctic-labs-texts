Optimality conditions at infinity for nonsmooth minimax programming
NGUYEN VAN TUYEN 1, KWAN DEOK BAE 2, DO SANG KIM 3

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to study of optimality conditions at infinity in nonsmooth minimax programming problems and applications. By means of the limiting subdifferential and normal cone at infinity, we dirive necessary and sufficient optimality conditions of Karush–Kuhn–Tucker type for nonsmooth minimax programming problems with constraint. The obtained results are applied to a nonsmooth vector optimization problem.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in optimality conditions at infinity for the following constrained minimax optimization problem

\min_{x\in\mathcal{F}}\max_{1\leq i\leq m}f_{i}(x),

where the feasible set
\mathcal{F}
is defined by

\mathcal{F}:=\{x\in\Omega\;|\;g_{j}(x)\leq 0,j=1,\ldots,p\},

\Omega
is a nonempty and closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f_{i}
and
g_{j}
are lower semicontinuous functions.

Minmax optimization problems have many applications in various areas such as Chebyshev’s theory of best approximation
[
8
]
, game theory
[
1
,
4
,
9
,
11
,
23
,
24
]
, vector optimization
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
12
,
13
]
, … and recently in machine learning
[
14
,
15
,
21
]
.

The study of optimality conditions is one of the most attractive topics in optimization and plays a crucial role in both the theory and practice of optimization problems. The classical Fermat’s rule (or stationary principle) says that the derivative of a differentiable function vanishes at a point where the function attains its minimum. The nonsmooth version of Fermat’s rule states that if a lower semicontinuous function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
reaches its infimum at some point
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,f
, then
0\in\partial^{*}f(\bar{x})
, where
\partial^{*}
is a subdifferential of classical type; see, for example,
[
17
,
18
,
20
,
22
]
.

In contrast with the above case there are many others to be found in which the functions are bounded from below but do not have a global minimum, for example, the function
f(x)=e^{x}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence, Fermat’s rule cannot be applied for such cases. However, we can see that if a bounded from below function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
does not attain its infimum, then there exists a sequence
x_{k}
tending to infinity such that
f(x_{k})
tends to its infimum. A natural question arises:
What kind of necessary optimality conditions can we derive in these cases?

Very recently, Nguyen and Pham
[
19
]
proposed the concepts of
Clarke’s
tangent and normal cones, subgradients, Lipschitz continuity, …,
at infinity
and then they derived necessary optimality conditions for optimization problems in scenarios where objective functions are bounded from below but do not attain its minimum. Shortly thereafter, Kim, Nguyen and Pham
[
16
]
introduced the notions of
limiting
normal cones
at infinity
to unbounded sets as well as
limiting
and
singular
subdifferentials
at infinity
for extended real-valued functions. With the aid of these notions, the authors have succeeded in deriving necessary optimality conditions
at infinity
and studying weak sharp minima properties
at infinity
as well as stability results for optimization problems. It should be noted here that the concepts of Clarke and limiting subdifferentials at infinity are different and when a function is
Lipschitz at infinity
, then its limiting subdifferential is strictly smaller than the Clarke subdifferential at infinity; see
[
16
, Proposition 5.6]
.

To the best of our knowledge, so far there have been no papers investigating optimality conditions at infinity for minimax optimization problems. This motivates us to study optimality conditions for such problems by using the limiting subdifferential and normal cone at infinity.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains some basic definitions from variational analysis and several auxiliary results. In particular, we present necessary and sufficient at infinity for optimality for a general optimization problem as well as derive a result for computing subdifferentials at infinity of maximum functions. In Section
3
, we present necessary optimality conditions of Karush–Kuhn–Tucker type at infinity for minimax optimization problems. A sufficient condition at infinity for the nonemptiness and compactness of the solution set of such problems is also given in this section. The application of the obtained results to study optimality conditions at infinity for vector optimization problems is established in Section
4
. In the last section, we give several concluding remarks.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Normal cones and subdifferentials

Let us recall some notions related to generalized differentiation from
[
17
,
18
,
22
]
. Throughout the paper, we deal with the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the usual scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and the corresponding norm
\|\cdot\|
. The closed ball centered at
x
with radius
r
is denoted by
\mathbb{B}_{r}(x)
; when
x
is the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we write
\mathbb{B}_{r}
instead of
\mathbb{B}_{r}(x)
. The topological interior, the convex hull, and the positive hull of a set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are denoted, respectively, by
\mathrm{int}\,D
,
\mathrm{co}\,D
, and
\mathrm{pos}\,D
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a set-valued mapping. The
domain
and the
graph
of
F
are given, respectively, by

{\rm dom}\,F=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid F(x)\not=\emptyset\}

and

{\rm gph}\,F=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid y\in F(x)\}.

We say that
F
is
proper
if
\mbox{\rm dom}\,F\not=\emptyset.
The limiting construction

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}F(x):=\bigg\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid\exists x_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x},y_{k}\rightarrow y\ \mbox{with}\ y_{k}\in F(x_{k}),\forall k=1,2,....\bigg\}

is known as the
Painlevé–Kuratowski outer/upper limit
of
F
at
\bar{x}
.

Definition 2.1

(see
[
17
,
18
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\bar{x}\in\Omega
.

(i)

The
regular/Fréchet normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{N}(\bar{x},\Omega)=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\limsup\limits_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0\right\},

where
x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}
means that
x\rightarrow\bar{x}
and
x\in\Omega
.

(ii)

The
limiting/Mordukhovich normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is given by

\displaystyle N(\bar{x},\Omega)=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}\widehat{N}(x,\Omega).

We put
\widehat{N}(\bar{x},\Omega)=N(\bar{x},\Omega)=\emptyset
if
\bar{x}\not\in\Omega
.

Clearly, one always has

\displaystyle\widehat{N}(x,\Omega)\subset N(x,\Omega),\ \ \ \forall x\in\Omega.

Consider a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
with the
effective domain

\mbox{dom}\,f:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid f(x)<+\infty\}

and the
epigraph

{\rm{epi}}\,f:=\{(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\mid\alpha\geq f(x)\}.

We call
f
a
proper
function if its
\mathrm{dom}f
is a nonempty set.

Definition 2.2

(see
[
17
,
18
]
)
.

Consider a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and a point
\bar{x}\in{\rm dom}f
.

(i)

The
regular/Fréchet subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}
is

\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x}):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(v,-1)\in\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{epi}f}(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))\}.

(ii)

The
limiting/Mordukhovich subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}
is

\partial f(\bar{x}):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(v,-1)\in{N}_{\mathrm{epi}f}(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))\},

and the
limiting/Mordukhovich singular subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}
is

\partial^{\infty}f(\bar{x}):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\exists v_{k}\in\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x}),\lambda_{k}\downarrow 0,\lambda_{k}v_{k}\to v\}.

It is well-known that

\partial f(\bar{x})=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\xrightarrow{f}\bar{x}}\widehat{\partial}f(x)\supseteq\widehat{\partial}f(x),

where
x\xrightarrow{f}\bar{x}
means that
x\to\bar{x}
and
f(x)\to f(\bar{x})
. In
particular, if
f
is a convex function, then the subdifferentials
\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x})
and
\partial f(\bar{x})

coincide with the subdifferential in the sense of convex analysis.

For the singular subdifferential, we have

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f(\bar{x})\subseteq\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(v,0)\in N_{\mathrm{epi}f}(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))\},

see, for example,
[
22
]
. This relationship holds with equality whenever
f
is lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) at
\bar{x}
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the
indicator function

\delta_{\Omega}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
by

\delta_{\Omega}(x):=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{ if }x\in\Omega,\\
+\infty&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

It holds that
\partial\delta_{\Omega}(x)=\partial^{\infty}\delta_{\Omega}(x)=N_{\Omega}(x)
for any
x\in\Omega.

Some calculus rules for the limiting/Mordukhovich and singular subdifferentials used later are collected in the following lemmas (see
[
17
,
18
,
22
]
).

Lemma 2.1

(Fermat rule, see
[
17
, Proposition 1.114]
)
.

If a proper function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
has a local minimum at
\bar{x},
then
0\in\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x})\subset\partial f(\bar{x}).

Lemma 2.2

(see
[
18
, Theorem 4.10]
)
.

Let
f_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,
i=1,\dots,m
with
m\geq 2
, be Lipschitz around
\bar{x}
. Then the subdifferential of maximum function holds

\partial(\max f_{i})(\bar{x})\subseteq\left\{\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})}\lambda_{i}\partial f_{i}(\bar{x})\mid\lambda_{i}\geq 0,\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})}\lambda_{i}=1\right\},

where
I(\bar{x}):=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\mid f_{i}(\bar{x})=(\max f_{i})(\bar{x})\}
.

We now recall the Ekeland variational principle (see
[
10
]
).

Lemma 2.3

(Ekeland variational principle)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper l.s.c. function and bounded from below. Let
\epsilon>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be given such that

\displaystyle f(x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)+\epsilon.

Then, for any
\lambda>0
there is a point
x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the following conditions

(i)

f(x_{1})\leq f(x_{0}),

(ii)

\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|\leq\lambda,
and

(iii)

f(x_{1})\leq f(x)+\dfrac{\epsilon}{\lambda}\|x-x_{1}\|
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

2.2.
Normal cones and subdifferentials at infinity

Let
\Omega
be a
locally closed
subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e., for any
x\in\Omega
there is a neighborhood
U
of
x
for
which
\Omega\cap U
is closed. Let
I
be a nonempty subset of
\{1,\ldots,n\}.
Consider the projection
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{\#I},x:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto(x_{i})_{i\in I}
and assume that the set
\pi(\Omega)
is unbounded. In what follows, the notation
\pi(x)\xrightarrow{\Omega}\infty
means that
x\in\Omega
and
\|\pi(x)\|\to\infty
.

Definition 2.3

(see
[
16
]
)
.

The
normal cone to the set
\Omega
at infinity (with respect to the index set
I
)
is defined by

\displaystyle N_{\Omega}(\infty_{I})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{\pi(x)\xrightarrow{\Omega}\infty}\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(x).

When
I=\{1,\ldots,n\},
we write
N_{\Omega}(\infty)
instead of
N_{\Omega}(\infty_{I}).

The following result gives the intersection rule for
normals at infinity.

Proposition 2.1

(see
[
16
, Proposition 3.7]
)
.

Let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
be locally closed subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the normal qualification condition at infinity

\displaystyle N_{\Omega_{1}}(\infty_{I})\cap\big(-N_{\Omega_{2}}(\infty_{I})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{0\}.

Then we have the inclusion

\displaystyle N_{\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}}(\infty_{I})

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle N_{\Omega_{1}}(\infty_{I})+N_{\Omega_{2}}(\infty_{I}).

Now let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a lower semicontinuous function and let
I:=\{1,\ldots,n\}\subset\{1,\ldots,n,n+1\}.
To avoid triviality, we will assume that
f
is
proper at infinity
in the sense that the set
\mathrm{dom}f
is unbounded.

Definition 2.4

(see
[
16
]
)
.

The
limiting/Mordukhovich and the singular subdifferentials
of
f
at infinity are defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle\partial f(\infty)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ (u,-1)\in\mathcal{N}\},

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ (u,0)\in\mathcal{N}\},

where
\mathcal{N}:=\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty}{N}_{\textrm{epi}f}(x,f(x)).

The following result gives limiting representations of limiting and singular subgradients at infinity.

Proposition 2.2

(see
[
16
, Proposition 4.4]
)
.

The following relationships hold

\displaystyle\partial f(\infty)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty}\partial f(x),

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty,r\downarrow 0}r\partial f(x),

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)

\displaystyle\supseteq

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\infty}\partial^{\infty}f(x).

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an unbounded and closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then by Proposition
2.2
and
[
18
, Proposition 1.19]
, we have

\partial\delta_{\Omega}(\infty)=\partial^{\infty}\delta_{\Omega}(\infty)=N_{\Omega}(\infty).

We now recall the notion of the Lipschitzness at infinity for l.s.c. functions (see
[
16
,
19
]
).

Definition 2.5
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a l.s.c function. We say that
f
is
Lipschitz at infinity
if there exist constants
L>0
and
R>0
such that

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\|x-x^{\prime}\|\ \ \textrm{ for all }\ \ x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R}.

The following result gives a characterization for the Lipschitzness at infinity of l.s.c. functions.

Proposition 2.3

(see
[
16
, Proposition 5.2]
)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a l.s.c. function. Then
f
is Lipschitz at infinity if and only if
\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)=\{0\}.
In this case,
\partial f(\infty)
is nonempty compact.

The next result presents sum rules for both basic
and singular subdifferentials at infinity.

Proposition 2.4

(cf.
[
16
, Proposition 4.9]
)
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be l.s.c. functions such that the following subdifferential qualification condition at infinity

(1)

\left[u_{1}+\ldots+u_{m}=0,u_{i}\in\partial^{\infty}f_{i}(\infty)\right]\Rightarrow u_{i}=0,i=1,\ldots,m,

is valid. Then one has the inclusions

\displaystyle\partial(f_{1}+\ldots+f_{m})(\infty)

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\partial f_{1}(\infty)+\ldots+\partial f_{m}(\infty),

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}(f_{1}+\ldots+f_{m})(\infty)

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}f_{1}(\infty)+\ldots+\partial^{\infty}f_{m}(\infty).

Remark 2.2
.

Clearly, by Proposition
2.3
, the subdifferential qualification condition (
1
) holds at infinity if functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
, except, possibly, one are Lipschitz at infinity.

We now present a necessary optimality condition at infinity for a general optimization problem of the form

(P
0
)

\min_{x\in\Omega}f(x),

where
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is a l.s.c. function and
\Omega
is a nonempty and closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 2.1

(Fermat rule, cf.
[
16
, Theorem 6.1]
)
.

Assume that the following conditions hold:

(i)

\mathrm{dom}f\cap\Omega
is unbounded.

(ii)

\partial^{\infty}f(\infty)\cap\big(-N_{\Omega}(\infty)\big)=\{0\}.

(iii)

f
is bounded from below on
\Omega,
i.e.,
f_{*}:=\inf_{x\in\Omega}f(x)
is finite.

If there exists a sequence
x_{k}\xrightarrow{\Omega}\infty
such that
f(x_{k})\to f_{*}
, then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\partial f(\infty)+N_{\Omega}(\infty).

Proof.

We first consider the case that
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
N_{\Omega}(\infty)=\{0\}.
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle f_{*}\leq f(x_{k})\leq f_{*}+\left(f(x_{k})-f_{*}+\frac{1}{k}\right).

Clearly,
\epsilon_{k}:=f(x_{k})-f_{*}+\frac{1}{k}>0
and
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Put
\lambda_{k}:=\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}}
, then by the Ekeland variational principle (Lemma
2.3
), there exists
x^{\prime}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
k>0
such that

\displaystyle\|x_{k}-x^{\prime}_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda_{k},

\displaystyle f(x^{\prime}_{k})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(x)+\lambda_{k}\|x-x^{\prime}_{k}\|\quad\textrm{for all }\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The first inequality and the fact that
x_{k}\to\infty
imply that the sequence
x^{\prime}_{k}\penalty\ \to\penalty\ \infty
. While the second inequality says that
x^{\prime}_{k}
is a global minimizer of the function
\varphi(\cdot):=f(\cdot)+\lambda_{k}\|\cdot-x^{\prime}_{k}\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By the Fermat rule (Lemma
2.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\partial\left(f(\cdot)+\lambda_{k}\|\cdot-x^{\prime}_{k}\|\right)(x^{\prime}_{k}).

By the Lipschitz property of the function
\|\cdot-x^{\prime}_{k}\|
and the sum rule (see
[
18
, Theorem 2.19]
), we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\in\partial f(x^{\prime}_{k})+\lambda_{k}\partial(\|\cdot-x^{\prime}_{k}\|)(x^{\prime}_{k})

\displaystyle=\partial f(x^{\prime}_{k})+\lambda_{k}\mathbb{B}

due to
\partial(\|\cdot-x^{\prime}_{k}\|)(x^{\prime}_{k})=\mathbb{B}
. Hence,

0\in\partial f(x^{\prime}_{k})+\lambda_{k}\mathbb{B},

and so there is
{u}_{k}\in\partial f(x^{\prime}_{k})
such that
\|{u}_{k}\|\leq\lambda_{k}
. Since
\lambda_{k}\to 0
, by letting
k\to\infty
and applying Proposition
2.2
, we obtain
0\in\partial f(\infty).

We now consider the case that
\Omega
is an arbitrary subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We have

\displaystyle f_{*}=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(f+\delta_{\Omega}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\Omega}f(x)\ >\ -\infty,

where
\delta_{\Omega}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
stands for the indicator function of the set
\Omega.
Clearly,
f(x_{k})+\delta_{\Omega}(x_{k})\to f_{*}
. Therefore,
0\in\partial(f+\delta_{\Omega})(\infty)
(by the argument employed in the first case). This, together with Proposition
2.4
and the assumption (ii), yields
0\in\partial f(\infty)+N_{\Omega}(\infty).

∎

The following theorem gives a sufficient condition at infinity for the nonemptiness and compactness of (
P0
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that condition (ii) of Theorem
2.1
is satisfied. If
f
is bounded from below on
\Omega
and

(2)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\notin

\displaystyle\partial f(\infty)+N_{\Omega}(\infty),

then the global solution set of (
P0
), denoted by
\mathrm{sol}\eqref{problem-0}
, is nonempty and compact.

Proof.

Since
f
is bounded from below on
\Omega,

f_{*}:=\inf_{x\in\Omega}f(x)
is finite. By the definition of infimum, there exists a sequence
x_{k}\in\Omega
such that
f(x_{k})\to f_{*}
as
k\to\infty
. If the condition (
2
) holds, then by Theorem
2.1
the sequence
x_{k}
is bounded. By passing to a subsequence if necessary we may assume that
x_{k}
converges to some
\bar{x}
. Since
\Omega
is closed,
\bar{x}\in\Omega
. By the lower semicontinuity of
f
, we have

f(\bar{x})\leq\liminf_{x\to\bar{x}}f(x)\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}f(x_{k})=f_{*}.

This implies that
f(\bar{x})=f_{*}
, or, equivalently,
\bar{x}
is a global minimum of
f
on
\Omega
. This means that
\mathrm{sol}\eqref{problem-0}
is nonempty. Furthermore, we see that

\mathrm{sol}\eqref{problem-0}=\{x\in\Omega\;|\;f(x)\leq f_{*}\}=\{x\in\Omega\;|\;f(x)=f_{*}\}.

This set is closed due to the lower semicontinuity of
f
and the closedness of
\Omega
. By Theorem
2.1
and (
2
),
\mathrm{sol}\eqref{problem-0}
is bounded and so it is compact. The proof is complete.
∎

2.3.
Subdifferential of maximum functions at infinity

In this subsection, we proceed with evaluating limiting and singular subdifferentials at infinity of maxima of finitely many functions.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be Lipschitz at infinity functions. Then we have

(3)

\displaystyle\partial(\max f_{i})(\infty)

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathrm{co}\,\left\{\partial f_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial f_{m}(\infty)\right\},

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}(\max f_{i})(\infty)

\displaystyle=\{0\}.

Proof.

Since
f_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, are Lipschitz at infinity, it is easy to check that
(\max f_{i})
is also Lipschitz at infinity. So by Proposition
2.3
, one has
\partial^{\infty}(\max f_{i})(\infty)=\{0\}
. We now take any
z\in\partial(\max f_{i})(\infty)
, then there exist sequences
x_{k}\to\infty
and
z_{k}\in\partial(\max f_{i})(x_{k})
such that
z_{k}\to z
as
k\to\infty
. Since
f_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, are Lipschitz at infinity, we can assume that these functions are locally Lipschitz around
x_{k}
with the same modulus
L>0
for all
k
large enough. Hence,
\|u\|\leq L
for all
u\in\partial f_{1}(x_{k})
,
i=1,\ldots,m
and
k
large enough. By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\partial(\max f_{i})(x_{k})\subseteq\mathrm{co}\,\left\{\partial f_{1}(x_{k}),\ldots,\partial f_{m}(x_{k})\right\}.

Hence, there exist
\lambda^{i}_{k}\geq 0
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, with
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda^{i}_{k}=1
and
u^{i}_{k}\in\partial f_{i}(x_{k})
,
i=1,\ldots,m
such that
z_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda^{i}_{k}u^{i}_{k}
. By passing to a subsequence if necessary we may assume that
\lambda^{i}_{k}\to\lambda_{i}
and
u^{i}_{k}\to u_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, as
k\to\infty
. Hence,
u_{i}\in\partial^{\infty}f_{i}(\infty)
,
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}=1
and
z=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}u_{i}
. This means that
z\in\mathrm{co}\,\left\{\partial f_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial f_{m}(\infty)\right\}
and thus (
3
) holds. The proof is complete.
∎

3.
Optimality conditions at infinity

Let
\Omega
be a nonempty and closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
I:=\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
J:=\{1,\ldots,p\}
. Let us consider a minimax programming problem of the following form

(P)

\min_{x\in\mathcal{F}}\max_{i\in I}f_{i}(x),

where the feasible set
\mathcal{F}
is defined by

(4)

\mathcal{F}:=\{x\in\Omega\;|\;g_{j}(x)\leq 0,j\in J\}

and
f_{i},i\in I
, and
g_{j},j\in J
, are l.s.c functions.
Denote
f:=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
,
g:=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{p})
and
\varphi:=\max_{i\in I}f_{i}
.

Hereafter
, we assume that
\mathcal{F}
is nonempty and
unbounded set
,
f
and
g
are
Lipschitz at infinity
, and the maxima function
\varphi
is
bounded from below
on
\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
the constraint qualification condition
(CQ) holds at infinity if there do not exist
\beta_{j}\geq 0
,
j\in J
, such that
\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}>0
and

(5)

0\in\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}\partial g_{j}(\infty)+N_{\Omega}(\infty).

The following result gives a necessary optimality condition of Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) type at infinity to problem (
P
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that the condition (CQ) holds at infinity. If there is a sequence
x_{k}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\infty
such that
\varphi(x_{k})\to\varphi_{*}:=\inf_{x\in\mathcal{F}}\varphi(x)
, then there exist
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
and
\beta:=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}
such that

(6)

\displaystyle 0\in\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\partial f_{i}(\infty)+\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}\partial g_{j}(\infty)+N_{\Omega}(\infty).

Proof.

Since
f_{i}
,
i\in I
, are Lipschitz at infinity,
\varphi
is also Lipschitz at infinity. Hence, all assumptions of Theorem
2.1
are satisfied. Then, one has

(7)

0\in\partial\varphi(\infty)+N_{\mathcal{F}}(\infty).

By Theorem
2.3
, we have

(8)

\partial\varphi(\infty)\subseteq\mathrm{co}\,\left\{\partial f_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial f_{m}(\infty)\right\}.

On the other hand, by letting

C:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;|\;g_{j}(x)\leq 0,j\in J\},

then one has
\mathcal{F}=C\cap\Omega
.

We first claim that

(9)

N_{C}(\infty)\subset\mathrm{pos}\,\{\partial g_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial g_{p}(\infty)\},

where

\mathrm{pos}\,\{\partial g_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial g_{p}(\infty)\}:=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{p}\beta_{j}v_{j}\,|\,\beta_{j}\geq 0,v_{j}\in\partial g_{j}(\infty),j=1,\ldots,p\right\}.

Indeed, take any
u\in N_{C}(\infty)
. Clearly,
u=0\in\mathrm{pos}\,\{\partial g_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial g_{p}(\infty)\}
. So we consider the case that
u\neq 0
. Then there exist sequence
x_{k}\xrightarrow{C}\infty
and
u_{k}\in N_{C}(x_{k})
such that
u_{k}\to u
as
k\to\infty
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
u_{k}\neq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
g_{j}
,
j\in J
, are Lipschitz at infinity and
x_{k}\xrightarrow{C}\infty
, we may assume that
g_{j}
,
j\in J
, are locally Lipschitz with the same modulus
L>0
at
x_{k}
for all
k
large enough. Then by
[
17
, Corollary 4.36]
one has

N_{C}(x_{k})\subset\mathrm{pos}\,\{\partial g_{1}(x_{k}),\ldots,\partial g_{p}(x_{k})\}

for all
k
large enough, i.e., there exist
\beta^{j}_{k}\geq 0
and
v^{j}_{k}\in\partial g_{j}(x_{k})
,
j\in J
, such that

(10)

u_{k}=\sum_{j\in J}\beta^{j}_{k}v^{j}_{k}.

Since
u_{k}\neq 0
, one has
\beta_{k}:=\sum_{j\in J}\beta^{j}_{k}>0
. Moreover, by the locally Lipschitz property at
x_{k}
of
g_{j}
,
j\in J
,

\|v^{j}_{k}\|\leq L
for all
j\in J
and
k
large enough. Dividing two sides of (
10
) by
\beta_{k}
we obtain

(11)

\dfrac{u_{k}}{\beta_{k}}=\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\beta^{j}_{k}}{\beta_{k}}v^{j}_{k}.

Since sequences
\frac{\beta^{j}_{k}}{\beta_{k}}
and
v^{j}_{k}
are bounded, by passing to a subsequence if necessary we can assume that
\frac{\beta^{j}_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\to\beta_{j}\geq 0
and
v^{j}_{k}\to v_{j}\in\partial g_{j}(\infty)
.

If the sequence
\beta_{k}
is unbounded, then
\frac{u_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\to 0
. Since
\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\beta^{j}_{k}}{\beta_{k}}=1
, one has
\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}=1
. Now by letting
k\to\infty
in (
11
) we obtain

0=\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}v_{j},

contrary to (
5
). Thus the sequence
\beta_{k}
is bounded. By passing to a subsequence if necessary we can assume that
\beta_{k}\to\beta\geq 0
. If
\beta=0
, then it follows from (
10
) that
u=\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}=0
, a contradiction. So
\beta>0
. Letting
k\to\infty
in (
11
) we obtain

u=\beta\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}v_{j}\in\mathrm{pos}\,\{\partial g_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial g_{p}(\infty)\},

as required.

We now show that
N_{C}(\infty)\cap(-N_{\Omega}(\infty))=\{0\}
and hence by Proposition
2.1
and (
9
) we have

\displaystyle N_{\mathcal{F}}(\infty)

\displaystyle\subset N_{C}(\infty)+N_{\Omega}(\infty)

(12)

\displaystyle\subset\mathrm{pos}\,\{\partial g_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial g_{p}(\infty)\}+N_{\Omega}(\infty).

Indeed, let
u\in N_{C}(\infty)\cap(-N_{\Omega}(\infty))
. Then by (
9
), there exist
\beta_{j}\geq 0
and
v_{j}\in\partial g_{j}(\infty)
,
j\in J
, such that
u=\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}v_{j}
. Hence,

0=\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}v_{j}-u.

Since the condition (CQ) holds at infinity, we deduce that
\beta_{j}=0
,
j\in J
, or, equivalently,
u=0
, as required.

Combining (
7
), (
8
) and (
12
) we obtain

0\in\mathrm{co}\,\left\{\partial f_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial f_{m}(\infty)\right\}+\mathrm{pos}\,\{\partial g_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial g_{p}(\infty)\}+N_{\Omega}(\infty).

This means that there exist
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
and
\beta:=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}
satisfying (
6
). The proof is complete.
∎

Let us see a simple example to illustrate the above theorem.

Example 3.1
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},g\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be functions defined respectively by
f_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{|x|+1}
,
f_{2}(x)=0
,
g(x)=x
and let
\Omega=\mathbb{R}
. Is is easy to see that
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
and
g
are Lipschitz at infinity,
\partial f_{1}(\infty)=\partial f_{2}(\infty)=0
,
\partial g(\infty)=1
and
N_{\Omega}(\infty)=\{0\}
. Hence, the condition (CQ) holds at infinity. Furthermore,

\varphi(x)=\max\{f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x)\}=\frac{1}{|x|+1}\ \ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

Clearly,
\varphi_{*}=\inf_{x\in\mathcal{F}}\varphi(x)=0
and
\varphi
does not attain its infimum on
\mathcal{F}
. Then by Theorem
3.1
, there exist
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
and
\beta\geq 0
such that

0\in\alpha_{1}\partial f_{1}(\infty)+\alpha_{2}\partial f_{2}(\infty)+\beta\partial g(\infty).

The following theorem gives sufficient conditions for the nonempty and compactness of the global solution set of (
P
). This result is a direct consequence of Theorems
2.2
and
3.1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

If the condition (CQ) holds at infinity and

0\notin\mathrm{co}\,\left\{\partial f_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial f_{m}(\infty)\right\}+\mathrm{pos}\,\{\partial g_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial g_{p}(\infty)\}+N_{\Omega}(\infty),

then the global solution set of (
P
) is nonempty and compact.

4.
Applications to vector optimization problems

In this section, we consider the following vector optimization problem:

(VP)

\mathrm{Min}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}}\,\{f(x)\;|\;x\in\mathcal{F}\},

where the feasible set
\mathcal{F}
is defined by (
4
) and
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
is the nonnegative orthant of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and given by

\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}:=\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\;|\;x_{i}\geq 0,i\in I\}.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
y\in\mbox{\rm cl}\,f(\mathcal{F})
. We say that

(i)

y
is a
weak Pareto (optimal) value
of (
VP
) if

f(\mathcal{F})\cap(y-\mathrm{int}\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+})=\emptyset.

The set of all weak Pareto values of (
VP
) is denoted by
\mathrm{val}^{w}\,\eqref{VOP}
.

(ii)

y
is a
Pareto (optimal) value
of (
VP
) if

f(\mathcal{F})\cap(y-\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\})=\emptyset.

The set of all Pareto values of (
VP
) is denoted by
\mathrm{val}\,\eqref{VOP}
.

(iii)

A point
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{F}
is said to be a
weak Pareto solution
(resp.,
Pareto solution
) of problem (
VP
) if
f(\bar{x})
is a weak Pareto value (resp. Pareto value) of (
VP
). The set of all weak Pareto solutions (resp., Pareto solutions) of (
VP
) is denoted by
\mathrm{sol}^{w}\,\eqref{VOP}
(resp.,
\mathrm{sol}\,\eqref{VOP}
).

Remark 4.1
.

By definition, it is clear that

\mathrm{val}\,\eqref{VOP}\subset\mathrm{val}^{w}\,\eqref{VOP}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathrm{sol}\,\eqref{VOP}\subset\mathrm{sol}^{w}\,\eqref{VOP}.

The following theorem presents a necessary optimality condition of KKT type for a weak Pareto value at infinity of problem (
VP
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\bar{y}\in\mathrm{val}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
. Assume that the condition (CQ) holds at infinity. If there is a sequence
x_{k}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\infty
such that
f(x_{k})\to\bar{y}
, then there exist
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
and
\beta:=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}
such that

(13)

\displaystyle 0\in\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\partial f_{i}(\infty)+\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}\partial g_{j}(\infty)+N_{\Omega}(\infty).

Proof.

Assume that
\bar{y}=(\bar{y}_{1},\ldots,\bar{y}_{m})\in\mathrm{val}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
. For each
i\in I
let
\hat{f}_{i}
be the function defined by

\hat{f}_{i}(x)=f_{i}(x)-\bar{y}_{i},\ \ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Clearly, functions
\hat{f}_{i}
,
i\in I
, are Lipschitz at infinity. We now consider the following minimax programming problem:

(14)

\min_{x\in\mathcal{F}}\max_{i\in I}\hat{f}_{i}(x).

For each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, put
\widehat{\varphi}(x):=\max_{i\in I}\hat{f}_{i}(x)
.
We will show that
\widehat{\varphi}
is bounded from below and zero is the optimal value of problem (
14
). To do this, let us first prove that

(15)

\widehat{\varphi}(x)\geq 0\ \ \ \forall x\in\mathcal{F}.

Indeed, if (
15
) does not hold, then there exists
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
\hat{\varphi}(x^{\prime})<0
. This means that

f_{i}(x^{\prime})-\bar{y}_{i}<0,\ \ \forall i\in I,

or, equivalently,

f(x^{\prime})\in(\bar{y}-\mathrm{int}\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}),

which contradicts the fact that
\bar{y}
is a weak Pareto value of (
VP
). Now since
f(x_{k})\to\bar{y}
, one has
f_{i}(x_{k})-y_{i}\to 0
,
i\in I
, as
k\to\infty
. Combining this with the following estimate

0\leq\widehat{\varphi}(x_{k})\leq\sum_{i\in I}|f_{i}(x_{k})-\bar{y}_{i}|

we obtain
\widehat{\varphi}(x_{k})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Hence, zero is the optimal value of problem (
14
).

By Theorem
3.1
, there exist
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
and
\beta:=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}
such that

(16)

\displaystyle 0\in\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\partial\hat{f}_{i}(\infty)+\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}\partial g_{j}(\infty)+N_{\Omega}(\infty).

Clearly, condition (
16
) implies (
13
) and we thus complete the proof of the theorem.
∎

The next result gives a sufficient condition for the nonemptiness and compactness of the weak Pareto solution set of (
VP
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that
\mathrm{val}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
is nonempty. If the condition (CQ) holds at infinity and

(17)

0\notin\mathrm{co}\,\left\{\partial f_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial f_{m}(\infty)\right\}+\mathrm{pos}\,\{\partial g_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial g_{p}(\infty)\}+N_{\Omega}(\infty),

then
\mathrm{sol}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
is nonempty and compact.

Proof.

We first claim that
\mathrm{sol}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
is nonempty. Indeed, let
\bar{y}\in\mathrm{val}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
. Then there exists a sequence
x_{k}\in{\mathcal{F}}
such that
f(x_{k})\to\bar{y}
. By assumptions of the theorem and Theorem
4.1
, the sequence
x_{k}
is bounded and so we may assume that
x_{k}
converges to some
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{F}
. By the lower semicontinuity of functions
f_{i}
at
\bar{x}
, we have

f_{i}(\bar{x})\leq\liminf_{x\to\bar{x}}f_{i}(x)\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{i}(x_{k})=\bar{y}_{i}\ \ \forall i\in I.

This means that
f(\bar{x})\in\bar{y}-\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
. We claim that
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{sol}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
. This means that
\mathrm{sol}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
is nonempty. Indeed, if otherwise, then there exists
x\in\mathcal{F}
such that
f(x)\in f(\bar{x})-\mathrm{int}\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
. This implies that

\displaystyle f(x)\in f(\bar{x})-\mathrm{int}\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}

\displaystyle\subset(\bar{y}-\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+})-\mathrm{int}\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}

\displaystyle\subset\bar{y}-\mathrm{int}\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+},

which contradicts the fact that
\bar{y}\in\mathrm{val}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
.

Now we will prove that
\mathrm{sol}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
is bounded. Suppose on the contrary there exists a sequence
x_{k}
in
\mathrm{sol}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
such that
x_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Using similar arguments applied in the proof of Theorem
4.1
, for each
k
we have
x_{k}\in\mathrm{argmin}_{\mathcal{F}}\,\max_{i\in I}(f_{i}(x)-f_{i}(x_{k}))
. By
[
18
, Corollary 6.6]
, for
k
large enough there exist
\alpha^{i}_{k}\geq 0
,
u^{i}_{k}\in\partial f_{i}(x_{k})
,
i\in I
,
\beta^{j}_{k}\geq 0
,
v^{j}_{k}\in\partial g_{j}(x_{k})
,
j\in J
, and
w_{k}\in N_{\Omega}(x_{k})
such that
\sum_{i\in I}\alpha^{i}_{k}+\sum_{j\in J}\beta^{j}_{k}=1
and

(18)

\sum_{i\in I}\alpha^{i}_{k}u^{i}_{k}+\sum_{j\in J}\beta^{j}_{k}v^{j}_{k}+w_{k}=0.

By the Lipschitz at infinity property of
f
and
g
, sequences
u^{i}_{k}
and
v^{j}_{k}
are bounded for all
k
large enough. By passing to a subsequence if necessary we can assume that
\alpha^{i}_{k}\to\alpha_{i}
,
\beta^{j}_{k}\to\beta_{j}
,
u^{i}_{k}\to u_{i}\in\partial f_{i}(\infty)
,
v^{j}_{k}\to v_{j}\in\partial g_{j}(\infty)
such that
\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}=1
. By (
18
), the sequence
w_{k}
also converges to some
w\in N_{\Omega}(\infty)
.
Hence,

(19)

\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}u_{i}+\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}v_{j}+w=0.

If
\alpha_{i}=0
for all
i\in I
, then by (
19
),
0\in\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}\partial g_{j}(\infty)+N_{\Omega}(\infty)
, where
\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{j}=1
, which contradicts to the fact that the condition (CQ) holds at infinity. Hence,
\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}>0
and so

0\in\mathrm{co}\,\left\{\partial f_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial f_{m}(\infty)\right\}+\mathrm{pos}\,\{\partial g_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial g_{p}(\infty)\}+N_{\Omega}(\infty),

contrary to (
17
), as required.

To finish the proof, we need to show that
\mathrm{sol}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
is closed and so this set is compact. To proceed, let
x_{k}
be a sequence in
\mathrm{sol}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
that converges to some
\bar{x}
. We claim that
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{sol}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
and so
\mathrm{sol}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
is closed. Indeed, if otherwise,
\bar{x}\notin\mathrm{sol}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
, then by definition there exist
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
such that

f(x^{\prime})\in f(\bar{x})-\mathrm{int}\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+},

or, equivalently,

(20)

f_{i}(x^{\prime})<f_{i}(\bar{x})\ \ \ \forall i\in I.

Now by the lower semicontinuity of
f_{i}
at
\bar{x}
, we have

f_{i}(\bar{x})\leq\liminf_{x\to\bar{x}}f_{i}(x)\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{i}(x_{k})\ \ \ \forall i\in I.

This and (
20
) imply that for all
k
large enough

f_{i}(x^{\prime})<f_{i}(x_{k})\ \ \ \forall i\in I.

Hence,

f(x^{\prime})\in f(x_{k})-\mathrm{int}\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\ \ \text{for all}\ \ k\ \ \text{large enough},

which contradicts to the fact that
x_{k}\in\mathrm{sol}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
. The proof is complete.
∎

We finish this section by presenting a result that gives a sufficient condition for the nonemptiness and boundedness of the Pareto solution set of (
VP
).

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that
\mathrm{val}\eqref{VOP}
is nonempty. If the condition (CQ) holds at infinity and

0\notin\mathrm{co}\,\left\{\partial f_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial f_{m}(\infty)\right\}+\mathrm{pos}\,\{\partial g_{1}(\infty),\ldots,\partial g_{p}(\infty)\}+N_{\Omega}(\infty),

then
\mathrm{sol}\eqref{VOP}
is nonempty and bounded.

Proof.

Let
\bar{y}\in\mathrm{val}\eqref{VOP}
. Then, by a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
4.2
, there exists a sequence
x_{k}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
f(x_{k})\to\bar{y}
,
x_{k}
converges to some
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{F}
and
f(\bar{x})\in\bar{y}-\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
. We show that
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{sol}\eqref{VOP}
, i.e.,
\mathrm{sol}\eqref{VOP}
is nonempty. Indeed, if otherwise, there is a point
x\in\mathcal{F}
satisfying
f(x)\in f(\bar{x})-\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
. Hence,

\displaystyle f(x)\in f(\bar{x})-\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\}

\displaystyle\subset(\bar{y}-\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+})-\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\}

\displaystyle\subset\bar{y}-\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\},

which contradicts the fact that
\bar{y}\in\mathrm{val}\eqref{VOP}
. The bounded of
\mathrm{sol}\eqref{VOP}
follows directly from Theorem
4.2
and the fact that
\mathrm{sol}\eqref{VOP}\subset\mathrm{sol}^{w}\eqref{VOP}
. ∎

5.
Conclusions

In this paper, necessary and sufficient optimality condition at infinity for nonsmooth minimax programming problems are investigated. The tools used here are the limiting subdifferential and normal cone at infinity. The obtained results are then applied to nonsmooth vector optimization problems. Regarding to Theorem
4.3
, the closedness of
\mathrm{sol}\eqref{VOP}
is still unclear and we leave here as an open question for future research.

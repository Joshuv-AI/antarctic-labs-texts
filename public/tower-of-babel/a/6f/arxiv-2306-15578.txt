Fourier Analysis on 𝕋 m × ℝ n \T^{m}\times\R^{n} and applications to global hypoellipticity
André Pedroso Kowacs

Abstract
. This article presents a convenient approach to Fourier analysis for the investigation of functions and distributions on 𝕋 m × ℝ n \T^{m}\times\R^{n} . Our approach involves the utilization of a mixed Fourier transform, incorporating both partial Fourier series on the torus on the first m m variables and partial Fourier transform in Euclidean space on the remaining variables. By examining the behaviour of the mixed Fourier coefficients, we achieve a comprehensive characterization of the spaces of rapidly decreasing smooth functions and distributions in this context. Additionally, we apply our results to derive necessary and sufficient conditions for the global hypoellipticity of a class first-order differential operators defined on 𝕋 1 × ℝ \T^{1}\times\R , including all constant coefficient first-order differential operators.

2.
Partial Fourier Analysis

Let
m,n\in\N
. We denote by
\T^{m}\times\R^{n}
the product of
\T^{m}=(\R/2\pi\Z)^{m}
, the
m
-dimensional torus and the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space
\R^{n}
. We also denote by
\mathcal{S}(\R^{n})
the Schwartz space on
\R^{n}
, and by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\R^{n})
its topological dual, also known as the space of tempered distributions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\G)
. Define its partial Fourier transform by

\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(f)(t,\xi)=\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\vcentcolon=\int_{\R^{n}}f(t,x)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}dx,

for a.e.
(t,\xi)\in\G
, and

\mathcal{F}_{\T^{m}}(f)(k,x)=\widehat{f}(k,x)\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{m}}f(t,x)e^{-ik\cdot t}dt,

for all
k\in\Z^{m}
and a.e.
x\in\R^{n}
.
Also define its mixed partial Fourier transform by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\T^{m}}(\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(f))(k,\xi)=\ftil(k,\xi)

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{m}}\int_{\R^{n}}f(t,x)e^{-i(k\cdot t+x\cdot\xi)}dxdt,

for all
k\in\Z^{m}
and a.e.
\xi\in\R^{n}
.
Note that by Fubini’s Theorem, these are all well defined and moreover
\widehat{f}(\cdot,\xi)\in L^{1}(\T^{m})
, for every
\xi\in\R^{n}
and
\widehat{f}(k,\cdot),\ftil(k,\cdot)\in L^{1}(\R^{n})
for every
k\in\Z^{m}
. Furthermore, the following inequalities hold

|\widehat{f}(t,\xi)|\leq\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\R^{n})},

for almost every
t\in\T^{m}
,

|\widehat{f}(k,x)|\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\|f(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{1}(\T^{m})},

for almost every
x\in\R^{n}
,

|\ftil(k,\xi)|\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\T^{m}\times\R^{n})},

for almost every
(k,\xi)\in\Z^{m}\times\R^{n}
.

In order to extend this definition to the appropriate set of distributions, we need to consider an appropriate space of test functions. While
\mathcal{S}(\R^{n})
and
C^{\infty}(\T^{m})
are adequate spaces of test functions in
\R^{n}
and
\T^{m}
, respectively, for
\G
we need a space of test functions which decay fast enough in
x
uniformly in
t
. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}{(\G)}
. For each multi-index
\alpha\in\N^{m}_{0},\,\beta,\gamma\in\N^{n}_{0}
, the real number

\|f\|_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\vcentcolon=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,x)\in\T^{m}\end{subarray}\times\R^{n}}|x^{\gamma}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}f(t,x)|.

Define
\mathcal{S}(\G)
to be the set of all smooth functions on
\G
which satisfy
\|f\|_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}<+\infty
for every multi-index
\alpha\in\N_{0}^{m},\,\beta,\gamma\in\N_{0}^{n}

with the topology induced by the countable family of (semi)norms:

p_{N}(f)\vcentcolon=\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+|\gamma|\leq N}\|f\|_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma},\qquad N\in\N_{0}.

This provides
\mathcal{S}(\G)
with a Fréchet space structure. With this in mind, we define
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
to be its dual space, that is, the space of all linear functionals
u:\mathcal{S}(\G)\to\mathbb{C}
such that there exist
N=N_{u}>0,C=C_{u}>0
such that

|\langle u,f\rangle|\leq Cp_{N}(f),

for every
f\in\mathcal{S}(\G).

Remark 1
.

Note that every continuous function
u:\T^{m}\times\R^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
which satisfies

|u(t,x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{2})^{N/2},

for some
C,N>0
and all
(t,x)\in\G
induces an element of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
by

f\mapsto\int_{\G}u(t,x)f(t,x)dtdx.

Remark 2
.

It is clear that
C^{\infty}(\T^{m})\otimes\mathcal{S}(\R^{n})\subset\s
, however, note that this inclusion is strict, that is

\{f\in C^{\infty}(\G)|f(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}(\R^{n}),\,\forall t\in\T^{m}\}\supsetneq\mathcal{S}(\G).

To see why that is the case, consider that the function
f:\T^{1}\times\R\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\displaystyle f(t,x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tan(t)\exp\left(\frac{-1}{1-\left(x-\tan(t)\right)^{2}}\right),\quad&&\text{ for }|x-\tan(t)|<1,\,t\neq k\frac{\pi}{2},\,k\in\Z,\\
&0,&&\text{ otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

Although smooth and compactly supported in
\R
, for each
t\in\T
, it is not in
\mathcal{S}(\G)
. Indeed, as

\sup_{x\in\R}|f(t,x)|=\tan(t)e^{-1}
for each
t\in(0,2\pi)\backslash\{\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi}{2}\}
, we have that
\sup_{t\in\T^{1}}\sup_{x\in\R}|f(t,x)|=+\infty
.

Next we fully characterize
\mathcal{S}(\G)
by the decay of the partial Fourier transforms of its elements.

Proposition 2.1
.

A function
f\in C^{\infty}(\G)\cap L^{1}(\T^{m}\times\R^{n})
is in
\mathcal{S}(\G)
if and only if for every
\beta,\in\N_{0}^{n}
,
N>0
, there exists
C_{N,\beta}>0
, such that

(2.1)

{}|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|\leq C_{N,\beta}(1+|k|^{2})^{-N/2}(1+|x|^{2})^{-N/2},

for every
k\in\Z^{m},

x\in\R^{n}
.

Proof.

First, suppose
f\in\mathcal{S}(\G)
. Note that integration by parts yields
\mathcal{F}_{\T^{m}}(\partial_{t_{j}}f)(k,x)=(ik_{j})\widehat{f}(k,x)
, for every
j=1,\dots,m
. Let
\beta,
and
N
be as stated. Then

\mathcal{F}_{\T^{m}}\left((1-\Delta_{t})^{N/2}(1+|x|^{2})^{N/2}\partial_{x}^{\beta}f\right)(k,x)=(1+|k|^{2})^{N/2}(1+|x|^{2})^{N/2}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x),

so

\displaystyle|(1+|k|^{2})^{N/2}(1+|x|^{2})^{N/2}

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|\leq

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{\T^{m}}|\left((1-\Delta_{t})^{N/2}(1+|x|^{2})^{N/2}\partial_{x}^{\beta}f\right)(t,x)e^{-ik\cdot t}|dt

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\T^{m}\\
x\in\R^{n}\end{subarray}}|\left((1-\Delta_{t})^{N/2}(1+|x|^{2})^{N/2}\partial_{x}^{\beta}f\right)(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq{C}_{N,\beta}<+\infty,

for some
{C}_{N,\beta}>0
and every
k\in\Z^{m},

x\in\R^{n}
, and so inequality (
2.1
) follows. Conversely, suppose (
2.1
) holds. Then these inequalities imply that the series

g(t,x)\vcentcolon=\sum_{k\in\Z^{m}}\widehat{f}(k,x)e^{ik\cdot t}

converges absolutely and uniformly in
x
. Moreover, for each multi-index
\alpha\in\N_{0}^{m}
,
\beta,\gamma\in\N_{0}^{n}
, the series

\sum_{k\in\Z^{m}}(ik)^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)e^{ik\cdot t}

converges absolutely and uniformly in
x
, therefore
g\in C^{\infty}(\G)
and
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}{g}(t,x)
is given by the series above. Moreover, the usual Fourier inversion theorem implies that
g=f
point-wise in
x
. Also, note that for every
\alpha\in\N_{0}^{m}
,
\beta,\gamma\in\N_{0}^{n}
we have that

\displaystyle|x^{\gamma}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}{f}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\Z^{m}}|k^{\alpha}x^{\gamma}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\Z^{m}}|k|^{|\alpha|}C_{|\alpha|+\gamma+m+1,\beta}(1+|k|^{2})^{-(|\alpha|+m+1)/2}|x|^{\gamma}(1+|x|^{2})^{-(|\alpha|+m+1)/2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}<+\infty,

for some
\tilde{C}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
, for each
(t,x)\in\G
, so that
f\in\mathcal{S}(\G)
as claimed.
∎

With this result in mind, given a sequence of functions
\{{g}(k,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}(\R^{n})\}_{k\in\Z^{m}}
which satisfies inequalities (
2.1
), we define its inverse partial Fourier transform by

\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\T^{m}}(g)(t,x)=\widecheck{g}(t,x)\vcentcolon=\sum_{k\in\Z^{m}}g(k,x)e^{ik\cdot t},\,(t,x)\in\G.

This way, from the proof of last proposition, we have that

\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\T^{m}}(\mathcal{F}_{\T^{m}}(f))(t,x)=f(t,x),\,\forall f\in\mathcal{S}(\G).

Proposition 2.2
.

A function
f\in C^{\infty}(\G)\cap L^{1}(\T^{m}\times\R^{n})
is in
\mathcal{S}(\G)
if and only if for every
\alpha\in\N_{0}^{m},\,\beta\in\N_{0}^{n}
,
N>0
, there exists
C_{N,\alpha,\beta}>0
, such that

(2.2)

|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)|\leq C_{N,\alpha,\beta}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-N/2},

for every
t\in\T^{m}
,
\xi\in\R^{n}
.

Proof.

First, suppose
f\in\mathcal{S}(\G)
. Note that integration by parts and Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem yield
\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(\partial_{x_{j}}[(-ix_{l})f])(t,\xi)=(i\xi_{j})\partial_{\xi_{l}}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)
, for every
j,l=1,\dots,n
. Now let
\alpha,\beta
and
N
be as stated. Then

\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}\left((1-\Delta_{x})^{N/2}[(-ix)^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}f]\right)(t,\xi)=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{N/2}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(t,\xi),

so

\displaystyle|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{N/2}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\R^{n}}\left|(1-\Delta_{x})^{N/2}[(-ix)^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}f(t,x)]e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\T^{m}\\
x\in\R^{n}\end{subarray}}\left|(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{n+1}{2}}(1-\Delta_{x})^{N/2}[(-ix)^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}f(t,x)]\right|

\displaystyle\times\int_{\R^{n}}(1+|x|^{2})^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{N,\alpha,\beta}<+\infty,

for some
C_{N,\alpha,\beta}>0
and for every
t\in\T^{m},\,\xi\in\R^{n}
,
and inequality (
2.2
) follows. Conversely, suppose inequality (
2.2
) holds. This implies that for each
\alpha\in\N_{0}^{m},\,\beta,\gamma\in\N_{0}^{n}
there exists
C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}>0
,
such that

|\xi^{\gamma}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{n+1}{2}},

for every
t\in\T^{m}
,
\xi\in\R^{n}
. Therefore, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, it follows that the function

h(t,x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\R^{n}}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}d\xi

is smooth on
\G
. Moreover, by the usual Fourier inversion theorem, we have that
h=f
point-wise in
t
. Finally, notice that for every
\alpha\in\N_{0}^{m},\,\beta,\gamma\in\N_{0}^{n}
, we have that

\displaystyle|x^{\gamma}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}{f}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\R^{n}}|\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}\xi^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)|d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\R^{n}}|\xi|^{\beta}\tilde{C}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-(|\beta|+n+1)/2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}<+\infty,

for some
\tilde{C}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma},\tilde{C}^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}>0
, for each
(t,x)\in\G
, so that
f\in\mathcal{S}(\G)
.
∎

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\G)
. Then
f\in\mathcal{S}(\T^{m}\times\R^{n})\iff\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(f)\in\mathcal{S}(\G)
.

With this result in mind, for
g\in\mathcal{S}(\G)
, we define its inverse partial Fourier transform by

\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\R^{n}}(g)(t,x)=\widecheck{g}(t,x)\vcentcolon=\int_{\R^{n}}g(t,\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}d\xi,\,(t,x)\in\G.

This way, from the proof of last proposition, we have that:

\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\R^{n}}(\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(f))(t,x)=f(t,x),\,\forall f\in\mathcal{S}(\G).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\G)\cap L^{1}(\G)
. Then
f\in\mathcal{S}(\G)
if and only if for each
\beta\in\N_{0}^{n}
,
N>0
there exists
C_{N,\beta}>0
such that

(2.3)

|\xi^{\gamma}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\ftil(k,\xi)|\leq C(1+|k|^{2})^{-N/2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-N/2},

for every
k\in\Z^{m}
,
\xi\in\R^{n}
.

Proof.

Suppose first
f\in\mathcal{S}(\G)
. Notice that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(\mathcal{F}_{\T^{m}}((1-\Delta_{x})^{N/2}((-ix)^{\beta}(1-\Delta_{t})^{N/2}f))))(k,\xi)=

\displaystyle=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{N/2}(1+|k|^{2})^{N/2}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\ftil(k,\xi).

Therefore

\displaystyle(1+|\xi|^{2})^{N/2}(1+|k|^{2})^{N/2}|\xi^{\gamma}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\ftil(k,\xi)|=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\left|\int_{\R^{n}}\int_{\T^{m}}(1-\Delta_{x})^{N/2}((-ix)^{\beta}(1-\Delta_{t})^{N/2}f(t,x)e^{-i(k\cdot t+x\cdot\xi)}dtdx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\T^{m}\\
x\in\R^{n}\end{subarray}}|(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{n+1}{2}}(1-\Delta_{x})^{N/2}\partial_{x}^{\gamma}((-ix)^{\beta}(1-\Delta_{t})^{N/2}f(t,x)|

\displaystyle\times\int_{\R^{n}}\int_{\T^{m}}(1+|x|^{2})^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}dtdx<+\infty,

for every
k\in\Z^{m}
,
\xi\in\R^{n}
,
so inequality (
2.3
) follows. Conversely, suppose (
2.3
) holds.
This means that for each
\alpha\in\N_{0}^{m}
,
\beta,\gamma\in\N_{0}^{n}
there exists
C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}>0
such that

|k^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}\ftil(k,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(1+|k|^{2})^{-\frac{m+n+1}{2}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{m+n+1}{2}},

for every
k\in\Z^{m}
,
\xi\in\R^{n}
. Therefore, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, it follows that the function

h_{\alpha}(k,x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\R^{n}}(ik)^{\alpha}\ftil(k,\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}d\xi

is smooth in
x\in\R^{n}
, for each
k\in\Z^{m}
. Moreover,
\exists K_{\alpha}>0
such that

|h_{\alpha}(k,x)|\leq K_{\alpha}(1+|k|^{2})^{-\frac{m+n+1}{2}},

for each
x\in\R^{n}.
This means that the function

g(t,x)=\sum_{k\in\Z^{m}}h_{0}(k,x)e^{ik\cdot t}=\sum_{k\in\Z^{m}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\R^{n}}\ftil(k,\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}d\xi e^{ik\cdot t}

is smooth for
(t,x)\in\G
. Finally, we see point-wise in
k
and then in

, that by the usual Fourier inversion theorem
\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(h_{0})(k,\xi)=\ftil(k,\xi)
and
\mathcal{F}_{\T^{m}}(g)(k,x)=h_{0}(k,x)
, so that
f=g
.
∎

We then extend the definition of the partial Fourier transforms to a more general setting, by considering the “tempered” distributions of
\G
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
. We define its partial Fourier transforms by

\langle\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(u)(t,\xi),f(t,\xi)\rangle=\langle u(t,\xi),\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(f)(t,\xi)\rangle,

for every
\,f\in\mathcal{S}(\G)
, and

\mathcal{F}_{\T^{m}}(u)(k,\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\R^{n}),

acting by

\langle\mathcal{F}_{\T^{m}}(u)(k,x),f(x)\rangle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\langle u(t,x),e^{-ik\cdot t}f(x)\rangle,

for every
f\in\mathcal{S}(\R^{n})
.
We also define its mixed partial Fourier transform by

\mathcal{F}_{\T^{m}}(\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(u))(k,\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\R^{n}),

acting by

\left\langle\mathcal{F}_{\T^{m}}(\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(u))(k,\xi),f(\xi)\right\rangle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\langle u(t,\xi),e^{-ik\cdot t}\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(f)(\xi)\rangle,

for every
f\in\mathcal{S}(\R^{n})
.
For
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
we also define

\langle\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}^{-1}u(t,x),f(t,x)\rangle=\langle u(t,x),\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}^{-1}f(t,x)\rangle.

Notice that for
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\T^{m}\times\R^{n})
we have that
\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}u,\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}^{-1}u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
. We will also sometimes denote the partial Fourier transforms of
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\T^{m}\times\R^{n})
by
\widehat{u}
.

Note that by Corollary
2.1
and the inclusion
C^{\infty}(\T^{m})\otimes\mathcal{S}(\R^{n})\subset\mathcal{S}(\G)
, these distributions are all well defined. Additionally, it is easy to see that this definition is consistent with Definition
2.1
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
. Then there exist
C,N>0
such that

(2.4)

|\langle\widehat{u}(k,x),f(x)\rangle|\leq C\tilde{p}_{N}(f)(1+|k|^{2})^{N/2},

where
\tilde{p}_{N}(f)=\sum_{|\alpha|+|\gamma|\leq N}\|x^{\gamma}\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{\infty}
, for every
f\in\mathcal{S}(\R^{n})
,
k\in\Z^{m}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(\R^{n})
. Then from definition, there exist
C^{\prime},N>0
such that

\displaystyle|\langle\widehat{u}(k,x),f(x)\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle u,e^{ik\cdot t}f(x)\rangle|\leq C^{\prime}p_{N}(e^{ik\cdot t}f(x))

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}C_{N}(1+|k|^{2})^{N/2}\tilde{p}_{N}(f),

for some
C>0
and for every
k\in\Z^{m}
.
∎

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(u_{j})_{j\in\N}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
such that, for each
\theta\in\mathcal{S}(\G)
,
(\langle u_{j},\theta\rangle)_{j\in\N}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{C}
. Then there exists a unique
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
such that
u=\lim_{j\to\infty}u_{j}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof in [Theorem 2.9 in
[
12
]
]. Define

\langle u,\phi\rangle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\langle u_{j},\phi\rangle\in\mathbb{C},

for each
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\G)
. Clearly,, the mapping
\phi\mapsto\langle u,\phi\rangle
islinear; therefore, itonly remains to prove that it is continuous. Suppose
\phi_{l}\to 0
in
\s
as
l\to\infty
. Since for each
\psi\in\s
, the sequence
(\langle u_{j},\psi\rangle)_{j\in\N}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{C}
, it is bounded, say by
0<C_{\psi}<+\infty
. As
\s
is a Fréchet space and
\mathbb{C}
is a Banach space,
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in\N}
is equicontinuous on the unit ball, by the uniform boundness principle. Therefore, there exist
K,N>0
such that

|\langle u_{j},\psi\rangle|\leq K,

for each
\psi\in\s
satisfying
p_{N}(\psi)\leq 1
. Let
\epsilon>0
and set

\psi_{l}=\frac{2K}{\epsilon}\phi_{l},\,l\in\N.

Then
\psi_{l}\to 0
in
\s
as well, and so
\exists l_{0}\in\N
such that
p_{N}(\psi_{l})\leq 1
for all
l\geq l_{0}
. Therefore, for each
l\geq l_{0}
we have that

|\langle u_{j},\phi_{l}\rangle|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2},

for each
j\in\N
. Also, since
\langle u,\phi_{l}\rangle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\langle u_{j},\phi_{l}\rangle
, for each
l\geq l_{0}
, there exists
j_{l}\in\N
such that

|\langle u,\phi_{l}\rangle-\langle u_{j_{l}},\phi_{l}\rangle|<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Therefore, we conclude that for
l\geq l_{0}
we have that

|\langle u,\phi_{l}\rangle|\leq|\langle u,\phi_{l}\rangle-\langle u_{j_{l}},\phi_{l}\rangle|+|\langle u_{j_{l}},\phi_{l}\rangle|<\epsilon,

so that
\langle u,\phi_{l}\rangle\to 0
and
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\{{u}(k,\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\R^{n})\}_{k\in\Z^{m}}
be a sequence of tempered distributions which satisfy inequalities (
2.4
). Then there exists a unique
\widecheck{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
such that

\langle\widecheck{u},f\rangle=(2\pi)^{m}\sum_{k\in\Z^{m}}\langle{u}(k,x),\widehat{f}(-k,x)\rangle=\sum_{k\in\Z^{m}}\langle{u}(k,x)e^{ik\cdot t},f(t,x)\rangle,

so that we define

\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\T^{m}}({u})\vcentcolon=\widecheck{{u}}.

Proof.

For each
j\in\N
, define
S_{j}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
by

\displaystyle\langle S_{j}(t,x),f(t,x)\rangle\

\displaystyle=\sum_{|k|\leq j}\langle\widehat{u}(k,x)e^{ik\cdot t},f(t,x)\rangle=\sum_{|k|\leq j}\int_{\T^{m}}\langle\widehat{u}(k,x),f(t,x)\rangle e^{ik\cdot t}dt

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{m}\sum_{|k|\leq j}\langle\widehat{u}(k,x),\widehat{f}(-k,x)\rangle,

for every
f\in\s
.
Note that for each
k\in\Z^{m}
, inequality (
2.4
) implies that

\displaystyle|\langle{u}(k,x),\widehat{f}(-k,x)\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\tilde{p}_{N}\left(\widehat{f}(-k,\cdot)\right)(1+|k|^{2})^{N/2}

for some
\tilde{C}
.
Therefore, for every
j,l\in\N
we have that

\displaystyle|\langle S_{j+l},f\rangle-\langle S_{j},f\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|k|>j}^{j+l}|\langle{u}(k,x),\widehat{f}(-k,x)\rangle|\leq\sum_{|k|>j}^{j+l}\tilde{C}\tilde{p}_{N}\left(\widehat{f}(-k,\cdot)\right)(1+|k|^{2})^{N/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|k|>j}^{\infty}\tilde{C}\tilde{p}_{N}\left(\widehat{f}(-k,\cdot)\right)(1+|k|^{2})^{N/2}.

As
f\in\s
, there exists
C_{f}>0
such that

\tilde{p}_{N}\left(\widehat{f}(-k,\cdot)\right)\leq C_{f}(1+|k|^{2})^{-\frac{N+m+1}{2}},

for every
k\in\Z^{m}
, by the same argument used in the proof of Proposition
2.1
. This implies

|\langle S_{j+l},f\rangle-\langle S_{j},f\rangle|\leq\tilde{C}C_{f}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|k|>j\\
k\in\Z^{m}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}(1+|k|^{2})^{-\frac{m+1}{2}}\right)\to 0

as
j\to+\infty
, as the series above is convergent for each
j\in\N
. This means that the sequence
(\langle S_{j},f\rangle)_{j\in\N}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{C}
, for each
f\in\s
, so that it follows from Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle\widecheck{u}=(2\pi)^{m}\sum_{k\in\Z^{m}}{u}(k,x)e^{ik\cdot t}=\lim_{j\to\infty}S_{j}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G).

∎

Note using both Propositions
2.4
and
2.5
, for each
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
we have that

\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\T^{m}}(\mathcal{F}_{\T^{m}}(u))=u.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
u:\G\to\mathbb{C}
be such that there exist
C,N>0
such that

(2.5)

|{u}(t,\xi)|\leq C(1+|\xi|^{2})^{N/2},

for each
(t,\xi)\in\G
. Then there exists a unique
\widecheck{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
given by

\displaystyle\langle\widecheck{u},f\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\T^{m}}\int_{\R^{n}}\int_{\R^{n}}u(t,\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}f(t,x)d\xi dxdt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\T^{m}}\int_{\R^{n}}u(t,\xi)\widehat{f}(t,-\xi)d\xi dt,

for each
f\in\mathcal{S}(\G)
and we define
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\R^{n}}({u})\vcentcolon=\widecheck{u}.

Proof.

Let
u
be as stated. Then inequality (
2.5
) implies the integrals above are well defined and finite, so it is clear that the map
f\mapsto\langle\widecheck{u},f\rangle
is linear. It only remains to prove that it is continuous. Hence, let
f_{j}\to 0
in
\s
. Then
p_{M}(f_{j})\to 0
for each
M>0
. But then Proposition
2.1
and its proof imply that
p_{M}(\widehat{f_{j}})\to 0
for each
M>0
. This means that

\displaystyle|\langle\widecheck{u},f_{j}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\T^{m}}\int_{\R^{n}}|u(t,\xi)|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{N+n+1}{2}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{N+n+1}{2}}|\widehat{f_{j}}(t,-\xi)|d\xi dt

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{m-n}p_{N+n+1}\left(\widehat{f_{j}}\right)C\int_{\R^{n}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}d\xi\to 0

as
j\to+\infty
, for some
C>0
. It follows that
\widecheck{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
.
∎

Notice that for
u
as in the previous proposition, we have that

\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\R^{n}}(u))=u

in the sense of distributions.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\{{u}(k,\cdot):\R^{n}\to\mathbb{{C}}\}_{k\in\Z^{m}}
be a sequence of functions such that
\exists N,C>0
, such that:

(2.6)

|u(k,\xi)|\leq C(1+|k|^{2})^{N/2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{N/2}

for all

. Then
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\R^{n}}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\T^{m}}(u))
is well defined and belongs to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{F}_{\T^{m}}(\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\R^{n}}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\T^{m}}(u))))=u
in the sense of distributions.

Proof.

Note that if
u
is as stated, then there exist
C^{\prime},N^{\prime}>0
such that:

\left|\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\T^{m}}\int_{\R^{n}}u(k,\xi)\widehat{f}(-\xi)d\xi dt\right|\leq C^{\prime}\tilde{p}_{N^{\prime}}(f)(1+|k|^{2})^{N^{\prime}/2}

for each

, so that the claim follows from Propositions
2.5
and
2.6
.
∎

It is worth noting that as in
\mathcal{S}(\R^{n})
, for

, we may define as usual
gu
and
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}u
, where

is a suitable function (as with polynomial growth in

, uniform in

, along with all of its derivatives) and

. It is also easy to see that in

the following equalities also hold

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\T^{m}}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u)(k,x)=(ik)^{\alpha}\widehat{u}(k,x),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}(\partial_{x}^{\beta}u)(k,x)=(i\xi)^{\beta}\widehat{u}(t,\xi),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\R^{n}}((-ix)^{\gamma}u)(t,\xi)=\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}\widehat{u}(t,\xi).

3.
Application on regularity of Differential Operators

The previous results allow the study of regularity differential operators on
\G
as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
L:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
be a differential operator. We say
L
is Schwartz globally hypoelliptic (SGH) if whenever
Lu=f\in\s
for some
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)
, this implies
u\in\s
.

As an example, consider the differential operator
L=\partial_{t}+\partial_{x}
on
\T^{1}\times\R
. Then clearly
L
is not SGH since
v(t,x)\equiv 1\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\G)\backslash\s
satisifies
Lv=0\in\s
.

3.1.
Constant Coefficient case

The next theorem provides necessary and sufficient conditions for a class of constant coefficient differential operator acting on on
\T^{1}\times\R
to be SGH.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
L
be a constant coefficient differential operator on
\T^{1}\times\R
, given by

(3.1)

L=p(\partial_{x})+q(\partial_{t}),

where
p
and
q
are non-constant polynomials in one variable, and
p
or
q
have real coefficients. Then
L
is SGH if and only if

(3.2)

Z=\left\{p(\xi)+q(k)=0|(k,\xi)\in\Z\times\R\right\}=\emptyset.

Proof.

First suppose there exists
(k_{0},\xi_{0})\in Z
. Then the function

v(t,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{i(k_{0}t+\xi_{0}x)}

satisfies
v\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\T^{1}\times\R)\backslash\mathcal{S}(\T^{1}\times\R)
and
Lv=0\in\mathcal{S}(\T^{1}\times\R)
, so that
L
is not SGH.

Suppose now
Z
is empty. We will assume that
q
has real coefficients, the proof of the other case is analogous.
We claim that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
|p(\xi)+q(k)|\geq\varepsilon
, for every
(k,\xi)\in\Z\times\R
. Indeed, if
Z
is empty, then either
\Real(p(\xi))\neq 0
, for all
\xi\in\R
, or whenever
\Real(p(\xi))=0
, we have that
\Imag(p(\xi))+q(k)\neq 0
. If
\Real(p(\xi))\neq 0
, since polynomials in one variable are closed maps, we have that there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\Real(p(\xi))\geq\epsilon
and so

|p(\xi)+q(k)|\geq|\Real(p(\xi))|\geq\epsilon.

On the other hand, if
\Real(p(\xi))=0
for some
\xi\in\R
, then by the fundamental theorem of algebra, there are at most
\deg(p)
values for
\xi
where this occurs, say at
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
. Since
\Imag(p(\xi_{j}))+q(k)\neq 0
, for
j=1,\dots,n
, and
q
is also a closed map, we have that for each
j=1,\dots,n
there exists
\epsilon_{j}
such that

(3.3)

|\Imag(p(\xi_{j}))+q(k)|\geq\epsilon_{j},

for every
k\in\Z
and for every
j=1,\dots,n
. Take
\epsilon=\min\{\epsilon_{j},\,j=1,\dots,n\}
. Since
\Imag(p(\xi))
is continuous, there exists
\delta>0
such that whenever
|\xi-\xi_{j}|<\delta
we have that

(3.4)

|p(\xi)-p(\xi_{j})|<\epsilon/2.

Moreover, since
\Real(p(\xi))
is a closed map, we have that there exists
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
such that

(3.5)

|\Real(p(\xi)|>\epsilon^{\prime},

whenever
\xi\in\R\backslash\cup_{j=1}^{n}(\xi_{j}-\delta,\xi_{j}+\delta)
.
Therefore, if
|\xi-\xi_{j}|<\delta
we have that

\displaystyle|p(\xi)+q(k)|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}(|\Real(p(\xi))|+(|\Imag(p(\xi_{j})+q(k)|-|\Imag(p(\xi_{j}))-\Imag(p(\xi))|))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon-\epsilon/2)=\epsilon/4,

for every
k\in\Z
, by (
3.3
) and (
3.4
). On the other hand, if
|\xi-\xi_{j}|\geq\delta
, for every
j=1,\dots,n
, we have that

|p(\xi)+q(k)|\geq|\Real(p(\xi))|>\epsilon^{\prime},

for every
k\in\Z
, by (
3.5
). Taking
\varepsilon=\min\{\epsilon/4,\epsilon^{\prime}\}
yields the claim. Now if
Lu=f\in\mathcal{S}(\g)
, then since
p(\xi)+q(k)
never vanishes we have that

(3.6)

\util(k,\xi)=\frac{\ftil(k,\xi)}{i(p(\xi)+q(k))}.

Therefore by the Leibniz rule for differentiation, and the fact that
|p(\xi)+q(k)|\geq\varepsilon
for every
(k,\xi)\in\Z\times\R
, for every
\gamma,\beta\in\N
we have that

|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\util(k,\xi)|\leq K_{\beta}\max_{\gamma\leq m-1,\,\beta^{\prime}\leq\beta}|\xi^{\gamma}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta^{\prime}}\ftil(k,\xi)|,

for some
K_{\beta}>0
and
m=\deg(p)
, so that
f\in\mathcal{S}(\T^{1}\times\R)\implies u\in\mathcal{S}(\T^{1}\times\R)
and
L
is SGH.

∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
L
be a first order constant coefficients linear differential operator on
\T^{1}\times\R
, so that

L=c_{1}\partial_{t}+c_{2}\partial_{x}+c_{3},

for some
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\in\mathbb{C}
,
c_{1}\neq 0
or
c_{2}\neq 0
. Then
L
is Schwartz globally hypoelliptic if and only if

Z\vcentcolon=\{(k,\xi)\in\Z\times\R|c_{1}k+c_{2}\xi-ic_{3}=0\}=\emptyset.

Proof.

Indeed, if
c_{i}\neq 0
,
i=1
or
i=2
, then
L
is SGH if and only if
\frac{1}{c_{i}}L
is SGH, and we can apply the previous theorem to
\frac{1}{c_{i}}L
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
L
be a differential operator on
\T^{1}\times\R
given by

L=\partial_{t}+(a+ib)\partial_{x}+q,

where
a,b\in\R,\,q\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
L
is globally hypoelliptic if and only if:

•

b\neq 0
and
a\frac{\Real(q)}{b}+\Imag(q)\not\in\Z
or

•

b=0
and
\Real(q)\neq 0
or

•

b=\Real(q)=0=a
and
\Imag(q)\not\in\Z
.

Also let
\tilde{L}
be a differential operator on
\T^{1}\times\R
given by

\tilde{L}=(a+ib)\partial_{t}+\partial_{x}+q,

where
a,b\in\R,\,q\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
\tilde{L}
is globally hypoelliptic if and only if:

•

b\neq 0
and
\frac{\Real(q)}{b}\not\in\Z
or

•

b=0
and
\Real(q)\neq 0
.

Note that in this last case, the global hypoellipticity of
\tilde{L}
does not depend on the value of
a
, nor
\Imag(q)
.

Example 3.1
.

Let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be the differential operators on
\T^{1}\times\R
given by

L_{1}=\partial_{t}+(a+bi)\partial_{x}+q,

and

L_{2}=(a+bi)\partial_{t}+\partial_{x}+q,

where
a,b,\Real(q),\Imag(q)\in\Z
,
b\neq 0
. Then by Corollary
3.2
we have that
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are globally hypoelliptic if and only if
b
does not divide
\Real(q)
.

3.2.
Variable Coefficients - Real case

In this section, we prove that a differential operator given by

L=\partial_{t}+a(t)\partial_{x}+q(t),

where
a,q\in C^{\infty}(\T^{1})
and
a
is real valued, is Schwartz globally hypoelliptic in
\T^{1}\times\R
if and only in
L_{0}=\partial_{t}+a_{0}\partial_{x}+q_{0}
is Schwartz globally hypoelliptic, where

a_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}a(t)dt\quad\text{ and }\quad q_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}q(t)dt.

The idea is to find an operator
\Psi:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\T^{1}\times\R)\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\T^{1}\times\R)
which preserves
\mathcal{S}(\g)
and conjugates
L
with
L_{0}
.

First, we deal with the function
a
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
a\in C^{\infty}(\T^{1})
,
a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
be as before. Then the operator
\Psi_{a}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\T^{1}\times\R)\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\T^{1}\times\R)
given by

\displaystyle(\Psi_{a}u)(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\R}e^{i\xi A(t)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)e^{ix\xi}d\xi

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\R}^{-1}\left\{e^{i\xi A(t)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)\right\},

where

A(t)=\int_{0}^{t}a(s)ds-a_{0}t,

is well defined and defines an automorphism over
\mathcal{S}(\T^{1}\times\R)
.

Proof.

Using that
A\in C^{\infty}(\T^{1})
is real valued and the torus is compact, a simple calculation shows that
\Psi_{a}
is well defined. Also, clearly
\Psi_{a}
is invertible with
\Psi_{a}^{-1}
given by

\displaystyle\Psi_{a}^{-1}u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\R}e^{-i\xi A(t)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)e^{ix\xi}d\xi

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\R}^{-1}\left\{e^{-i\xi A(t)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)\right\}.

Now let
u\in\mathcal{S}(\T^{1}\times\R)
, then for each
\gamma,\beta\in\N_{0}
we have that

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{\Psi_{a}u}(t,\xi)|

\displaystyle=|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\left\{e^{i\xi A(t)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)\right\}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\beta^{\prime}\leq\beta}C_{\beta^{\prime}}|\xi^{\beta^{\prime}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)|,

for some
C_{\beta^{\prime}}>0
, so that by Proposition (
2.2
) we have that
\Psi_{a}u\in\mathcal{S}(\T^{1}\times\R)
. A similar argument works for
\Psi_{a}^{-1}
, so that
\Psi_{a}
is an automporphism of
\mathcal{S}(\T^{1}\times\R)
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
a
,
\Psi_{a}
and
L
be as before. Then we have that

L_{a_{0}}\circ\Psi_{a}=\Psi_{a}\circ L_{a},

where

L_{a_{0}}=\partial_{t}+a_{0}\partial_{x}+q(t).

Proof.

Note that, for
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\g)
, we have that

L_{a_{0}}\circ\Psi_{a}u=\partial_{t}\Psi_{a}u+a_{0}\partial_{x}\Psi_{a}u+q(t)\Psi_{a}u.

Taking the partial Fourier transform in
\R
yields

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{\Psi_{a}u}(t,\xi)+i\xi

\displaystyle a_{0}\widehat{\Psi_{a}u}(t,\xi)+q(t)\widehat{\Psi_{a}u}(t,\xi)

(3.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\{e^{i\xi A(t)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)\right\}+i\xi a_{0}e^{i\xi A(t)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)+q(t)e^{i\xi A(t)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi).

But as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\{e^{i\xi A(t)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)\right\}=i\xi(a(t)-a_{0})e^{i\xi A(t)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)+e^{i\xi A(t)}\partial_{t}\widehat{u}(t,\xi),

equation (
3.7
) implies that

\displaystyle\widehat{L_{a_{0}}\circ\Psi_{a}u}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=e^{i\xi A(t)}(\partial_{t}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)+i\xi a(t)\widehat{u}(t,\xi)+q(t)\widehat{u}(t,\xi))

\displaystyle=\widehat{\Psi_{a}\circ Lu}(t,\xi),

from which the claim follows.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
q\in C^{\infty}(\T^{1})
,
q_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
be as before. Then the operator
\Psi_{q}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\g)\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\g)
given by

(\Psi_{q}u)(t,x)=e^{Q(t)}{u}(t,x),

where

Q(t)=\int_{0}^{t}q(s)ds-q_{0}t,

is well defined and an automorphism of
\mathcal{S}(\g)
.

Proof.

This follows from the definitions and compacity of the torus.
∎

The next proposition is a simple calculation, and its proof is left to the reader.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
q
,
\Psi_{q}
,
L_{a_{0}}
and
L_{0}
be as before. We then have that

L_{0}\circ\Psi_{q}=\Psi_{q}\circ L_{a_{0}}.

Finally we obtain the necessary conjugation and equivalence, as follows.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\Psi_{a},\Psi_{q}
be as before. Then

L_{0}\circ\Psi=L\circ\Psi

and

L\circ\Psi^{-1}=\Psi^{-1}\circ L_{0},

where
\Psi=\Psi_{a}\circ\Psi_{q}
. Therefore
L
is Schwartz globally hypoelliptic if and only if
L_{0}
is Schwartz globally hypoelliptic.

Proof.

The equalities above are an immediate consequence of the previous propositions. The proof of the second claim is as follows: note that if
L
is SGH and
L_{0}u=f\in\mathcal{S}(\g)
, then
L\circ\Psi^{-1}u=\Psi^{-1}\circ L_{0}u=\Psi^{-1}f\in\mathcal{S}(\g)
so that
\Psi^{-1}u\in\mathcal{S}(\g)\implies u\in\mathcal{S}(\g)
and so

is SGH. On the other hand, if

, then

, so that

and so

is SGH.
∎

.

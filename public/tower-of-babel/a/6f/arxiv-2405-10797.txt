K-stability of special Gushel–Mukai manifolds
Yuchen Liu, Linsheng Wang

Abstract




Abstract.
Gushel–Mukai manifolds are specific families of
n
-dimensional Fano manifolds of Picard rank
1
and index
n-2
, where
3\leq n\leq 6
. A Gushel–Mukai
n
-fold is either ordinary,
i.e.
a hyperquadric section of a quintic Del Pezzo
(n+1)
-fold, or special,
i.e.
it admits a double cover over the quintic Del Pezzo
n
-fold branched along an ordinary Gushel–Mukai
(n-1)
-fold.
In this paper, we prove that a general special Gushel–Mukai
n
-fold is K-stable for every
3\leq n\leq 6
. Furthermore, we give a description of the first and last walls of the K-moduli of the pair
(M,cQ)
, where
M
is the quintic Del Pezzo fourfold (or fivefold) and
Q
is an ordinary Gushel–Mukai threefold (or fourfold). Finally, we compute
\delta
-invariants of quintic Del Pezzo fourfolds and fivefolds that were shown to be K-unstable by K. Fujita and show that they admit Kähler–Ricci solitons.

Keywords.
K-stability, Gushel–Mukai manifolds, quintic Del Pezzo manifolds

1. Introduction

The theory of K-stability, first introduced by Tian
[
Tia97
]
and later reformulated algebraically by Donaldson
[
Don02
]
, is an algebraic stability theory that characterizes the existence of canonical metrics on Fano varieties. According to the Yau–Tian–Donaldson correspondence, see
[
CDS15
,
Tia15
,
Ber16
,
BBJ21
,
LTW22
,
Li22
,
LXZ22
]
, a Fano variety is K-polystable if and only if it admits a Kähler–Einstein metric. Thus it is an important problem to check K-stability for explicit Fano manifolds. In dimension
2
, K-stability of Del Pezzo surfaces was completely determined in
[
Tia90
]
. For Fano threefolds, this problem was largely solved; see
e.g.

[
LX19
,
AZ23
,
ACC+23
]
. In higher dimensions, much progress has been made for Fano hypersurfaces; see
e.g.

[
Che01
,
Fuj19
,
AZ22
,
AZ23
,
Liu22
]
. Despite these developments, it remains a challenge to determine K-stability for most Fano manifolds in higher dimensions.

In this paper, we study the K-stability of Gushel–Mukai manifolds which are specific families of
n
-dimensional Fano manifolds of Picard rank
1
and index
n-2
, where
3\leq n\leq 6
.
Let us recall relevant notions. Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional Fano manifold with Picard group generated by an ample divisor
H
. The
index
of
X
is the positive integer
r
such that
-K_{X}\sim rH
. The self-intersection number
d:=(H^{n})
is called the
degree
of
X
.
For
r\geq n
, Kobayashi and Ochiai proved in
[
KO73
]
that
X
is isomorphic to either

{\mathbb P}^{n}
(where
r=n+1
) or a smooth quadric hypersurface in
{\mathbb P}^{n+1}
(where
r=n
).
Fano manifolds with index
r=n-1
, known as
Del Pezzo manifolds
, were classified by T. Fujita and Iskovskikh from 1977 to 1988; see for example
[
Fuj90
]
.
Fano manifolds with index
r=n-2
were studied by Gushel
[
Gus83
]
and Mukai
[
Muk89
]
. It was proved by Mukai that there are only
10
possibilities for the degree
d
. For the case
d=10
, the corresponding Fano manifolds are called
Gushel–Mukai
(
GM
)
manifolds
. See
[
Deb20
]
for a comprehensive survey on Gushel–Mukai manifolds.

There are two types of GM manifolds:
ordinary GM
n
-folds

Q_{n}
are quadratic sections of quintic Del Pezzo
(n+1)
-folds
M_{n+1}
(which are smooth
(n+1)
-dimensional linear sections of
\mathrm{Gr}(2,5)
);
special GM
n
-folds

X_{n}
are double covers of quintic Del Pezzo
n
-folds
M_{n}
branched along an ordinary GM
(n-1)
-fold
Q_{n-1}
. For
3\leq n\leq 6
, GM
n
-folds are Fano manifolds. It was proved by
[
AZ23
, Theorem 5.1]
that all the smooth GM threefolds are K-stable.

Our first main result proves the K-stability for a general member of special GM manifolds.

Theorem 1.1
.

A general special GM manifold of dimension
4
,
5
or
6
is K-stable.

Since the parameter space of the special GM
n
-folds is an irreducible closed subvariety of the parameter space of the GM
n
-folds, which is also irreducible (see Section
2
for details), we have the following corollary by the openness of K-stability; see
[
BL22
,
BLX22
,
LXZ22
]
.

Corollary 1.2
.

A general GM manifold of dimension
4
,
5
or
6
is K-stable.

Recall that a special GM
n
-fold
X_{n}
admits a double cover
X_{n}\to M_{n}
branched along an ordinary GM
(n-1)
-fold
Q_{n-1}
(see Section
2
). By
[
LZ22a
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
Zhu21
]
, we know that the K-polystability of
X_{n}
is equivalent to the K-polystability of the log Fano pair
(M_{n},\frac{1}{2}Q_{n-1})
. Hence it is natural to first study the K-stability of quintic Del Pezzo manifolds
M_{n}
.

The quintic Del Pezzo surfaces and threefolds are K-polystable, which were proved by
[
Tia90
]
and
[
CS09
]
, respectively. However, the quintic Del Pezzo fourfolds and fivefolds are K-unstable. They are among the first examples of K-unstable Fano manifolds with Picard number
1
found by K. Fujita
[
Fuj17
]
. He found a
2
-plane
S\subseteq M_{4}\subseteq M_{5}
(which is of “non-vertex type”, see Section
3.1
for details) such that
\beta(E_{S})<0
, where
E_{S}
is the exceptional divisor of the blowup of
M_{4}
or
M_{5}
along
S
. We further show the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

The delta invariant of
M_{4}
is
\delta(M_{4})=\frac{25}{27}
, which is minimized by
E_{S}
.

However, the delta invariant of
M_{5}
is not minimized by
E_{S}
(where
\frac{A_{M_{5}}(E_{S})}{S(E_{S})}=\frac{45}{46}
, see Section
4.1
for details). There exists a
3
-space
W={\mathbb P}^{3}\subseteq M_{5}
such that
\frac{A_{M_{5}}(E_{W})}{S(E_{W})}=\frac{15}{16}<\frac{45}{46}
, where
E_{W}
is the exceptional divisor of the blowup of
M_{5}
along
W
. We show the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

The delta invariant of
M_{5}
is
\delta(M_{5})=\frac{15}{16}
, which is minimized by
E_{W}
.

Next, we study the wall-crossing problem of the K-moduli spaces of
(M_{n},cQ_{n-1})
for
n=4
or
5
. Recall that for any Fano manifold
X
and divisor
D\in|-rK_{X}|
, where
r>0
is a rational number such that
rK_{X}
is Cartier, the
K-semistable domain
of
(X,D)
is defined by

\Kss(X,D)=\left\{c\in\left[0,{\operatorname{min}}\left\{1,r^{-1}\right\}\right]\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}(X,cD)\text{ is K-semistable}\right\}.

We determine the K-semistable domains of
(M_{4},Q_{3})
and
(M_{5},Q_{4})
. This is an essential step in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For general
Q_{3}\in|{\mathcal{O}}_{M_{4}}(2)|
and
Q_{4}\in|{\mathcal{O}}_{M_{5}}(2)|
, we have

\Kss(M_{4},Q_{3})=\left[\frac{1}{9},\frac{7}{8}\right],\quad\Kss(M_{5},Q_{4})=\left[\frac{1}{8},\frac{4}{5}\right].

The last walls are obtained by computing beta invariants at
\ord_{Q_{3}}
and
\ord_{Q_{4}}
. However, the first walls are more mysterious. To overcome the difficulty, we construct special quadric sections
Q_{3,0}
on
M_{4}
and
Q_{4,0}
on
M_{5}
to obtain K-polystable pairs; see Section
5
for the definition of
Q_{3,0}
and
Q_{4,0}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

The log Fano pairs
(M_{4},\frac{1}{9}Q_{3,0})
and
(M_{5},\frac{1}{8}Q_{4,0})
are K-polystable.

With a similar strategy, we may consider the weighted K-stability instead of the K-stability of log Fano pairs. For the soliton candidates
\xi_{0}
on
M_{4}
and
\eta_{0}
on
M_{5}
(see Section
6
), we have the following.

Theorem 1.7
.

The Fano pairs
(M_{4},\xi_{0})
and
(M_{5},\eta_{0})
are weighted K-polystable.

Hence by
[
HL23
,
BLXZ23
]
, we get the existence of Kähler–Ricci solitons on quintic Del Pezzo manifolds, which answers a question asked in
[
Ino19
,
Del22
]
.

Corollary 1.8
.

Any quintic Del Pezzo fourfold or fivefold admits a Kähler–Ricci soliton.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the definitions of quintic Del Pezzo manifolds and GM manifolds. Next, we give a detailed description of the quintic Del Pezzo fourfolds and fivefolds, and compute their delta invariants in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively. We study the wall-crossing problem of the K-moduli spaces of
(M_{4},cQ_{3})
and
(M_{5},cQ_{4})
in Section
5
. With a similar strategy, we show that
M_{4}
and
M_{5}
both admit Kähler–Ricci solitons in Section
6
. Finally, we prove the main theorem in Section
7
, that is, general special GM manifolds are K-stable.

2. Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.

2.1. Gushel–Mukai manifolds and quintic Del Pezzo manifolds

We recall the definitions of GM manifolds and quintic Del Pezzo manifolds in this subsection.

For any
{\mathbb C}
-vector space
W
, the projectivization
{\mathbb P}(W)
for
W
parameterizes the
1
-dimensional subspaces of
W
.
Let
V_{5}
be a
{\mathbb C}
-vector space of dimension
5
. Let
\mathrm{Gr}=\mathrm{Gr}(2,V_{5})\subseteq{\mathbb P}(\wedge^{2}V_{5})=:{\mathbb P}
be the Grassmannian of
2
-dimensional subspaces in
V_{5}
, and
{\rm CGr}\subseteq{\mathbb P}({\mathbb C}\oplus\wedge^{2}V_{5})=:\tilde{{\mathbb P}}
be the projective cone over
\mathrm{Gr}
with vertex
v={\mathbb P}({\mathbb C})
. We have the natural projection map
\pi:\tilde{{\mathbb P}}\setminus\{v\}\to{\mathbb P}
, which restricts to
\pi_{\mathrm{Gr}}\colon{\rm CGr}\setminus\{v\}\to\mathrm{Gr}
. For any
3\leq n\leq 6
, we define

\displaystyle X_{n}

\displaystyle={\rm CGr}\cap H_{6-n}\cap Q,

\displaystyle M_{n}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Gr}\cap H^{\prime}_{6-n},

\displaystyle Q_{n-1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Gr}\cap H^{\prime}_{6-n}\cap Q^{\prime},

where
H_{6-n}\subseteq\tilde{{\mathbb P}}
,
H^{\prime}_{6-n}\subseteq{\mathbb P}
are linear subspaces of codimension
6-n
, and
Q\subseteq\tilde{{\mathbb P}}
,
Q^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathbb P}
are quadric hypersurfaces.

It is not difficult to show that
X_{n}
is a Fano manifold of dimension
n
with
\Pic(X_{n})=\langle H\rangle
, where
H={\mathcal{O}}_{\tilde{{\mathbb P}}}(1)|_{X_{n}}
. We also have
(-K_{X_{n}})=(n-2)H
and
H^{n}=10
.
On the other hand, we see that
M_{n}
is a Fano manifold of dimension
n
with
\Pic(M_{n})=\langle H\rangle
, where
H={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb P}}(1)|_{M_{n}}
. We also have
(-K_{M_{n}})=(n-1)H
and
H^{n}=5
.
We have the following classification results due to Mukai
[
Muk89
]
and T. Fujita
[
Fuj81
]
, respectively.

Definition-Theorem 2.1

(
cf.

[
Muk89
]
)
.

Let
X_{n}
be a Fano manifold of dimension
n
with Picard number
1
, index
n-2
and degree
10
. Then
n\in\{3,4,5,6\}
, and there exist a codimension
(6-n)
subspace
H_{6-n}\subseteq\tilde{{\mathbb P}}
and a quadric hypersurface
Q\subseteq\tilde{{\mathbb P}}
such that

\displaystyle X_{n}={\rm CGr}\cap H_{6-n}\cap Q.

The manifolds
X_{n}
are called
Gushel–Mukai
(
GM
)
manifolds
. There are two different types of
X_{n}
:

(1)

If
v\in H_{6-n}
, then we have
H^{\prime}_{6-n}=H_{6-n}\cap{\mathbb P}\subseteq\tilde{{\mathbb P}}
, and
\pi
induces a double cover
X_{n}\to M_{n}
branched along some
Q_{n-1}\subseteq H^{\prime}_{6-n}
. We say that
X_{n}
is a
special Gushel–Mukai manifold
.

(2)

If
v\notin H_{6-n}
, then
n\leq 5
and
\pi
maps
H_{6-n}\subseteq\tilde{{\mathbb P}}
isomorphically to
H^{\prime}_{5-n}=\pi(H_{6-n})\subseteq{\mathbb P}
, hence maps
X_{n}
isomorphically to
Q_{n}=\pi(X_{n})\subseteq H^{\prime}_{5-n}
. We say that
X_{n}
is an
ordinary Gushel–Mukai manifold
.

Note that if
n=6
, then every Gushel–Mukai manifold is special.

Definition-Theorem 2.2

(
cf.

[
Fuj81
]
)
.

Let
M_{n}
be a Fano manifold of dimension
n
with Picard number
1
, index
n-1
and degree
5
. Then
M_{n}
is a linear section of
\mathrm{Gr}(2,V_{5})\subseteq{\mathbb P}(\wedge^{2}V_{5})
.
The manifolds
M_{n}
are called
quintic Del Pezzo manifolds
.

Remark 2.3
.

(1)

If
n=1
or
2
, then the above definition extends verbatim to smooth GM curves and GM surfaces. By
[
DK18
]
, a smooth GM curve is precisely a Clifford general smooth curve of genus
6
, and a smooth GM surface is precisely a Brill–Noether general smooth polarized K3 surface of degree
10
.

(2)

By the discussions above, we know that for any special GM
n
-fold
X_{n}
, there exists a double cover
\pi\colon X_{n}\to M_{n}
branched along an ordinary GM
(n-1)
-fold
Q_{n-1}
.
On the other hand, the parameter space of all the GM
n
-folds is irreducible and contains the parameter space of the special GM
n
-folds as an irreducible subvariety.

By
[
Fuj81
]
, we know that the quintic Del Pezzo manifolds are unique.

Theorem 2.4

(
cf.

[
Fuj81
]
)
.

For any
2\leq n\leq 6
, all the quintic Del Pezzo
n
-folds are isomorphic to each other.

The quintic Del Pezzo fourfolds and fivefolds are among the first examples of K-unstable Fano manifolds with Picard number
1
found by K. Fujita
[
Fuj17
]
.

Theorem 2.5

(
cf.

[
Fuj17
]
)
.

The quintic Del Pezzo fourfolds and fivefolds are K-unstable.

On the other hand, by
[
Tia90
]
and
[
CS09
]
, we know that the quintic Del Pezzo surface
M_{2}
and threefold
M_{3}
are K-polystable. Since
M_{6}=\mathrm{Gr}(2,V_{5})
is a homogeneous space, it is K-polystable; see for example
[
ACC+23
, Theorems 1.2.5 and 1.4.7]
.

The following result summarizes fundamental properties of the moduli spaces of smooth GM manifolds.

Theorem 2.6

(
cf.

[
DK20
,
Deb20
]
)
.

For any
3\leq n\leq 6
, there exists a quasi-projective irreducible coarse moduli space
\mathbf{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{GM}}
parameterizing smooth GM
n
-folds. Moreover,
\dim\mathbf{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{GM}}=25-\frac{1}{2}(6-n)(5-n)
. The moduli space
\mathbf{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{GM}}
is the union of an open subvariety
\mathbf{M}_{n,\mathrm{ord}}^{\mathrm{GM}}
and an irreducible closed subvariety
\mathbf{M}_{n,\mathrm{spe}}^{\mathrm{GM}}
, complement to each other, that parameterize ordinary and special GM
n
-folds, respectively. Moreover, the following properties hold:

(1)

\mathbf{M}_{n,\mathrm{ord}}^{\mathrm{GM}}=\emptyset
if and only if
n=6
.

(2)

\mathbf{M}_{n,\mathrm{spe}}^{\mathrm{GM}}\cong\mathbf{M}_{n-1,\mathrm{ord}}^{\mathrm{GM}}
for
4\leq n\leq 6
.

(3)

\mathbf{M}_{3,\mathrm{spe}}^{\mathrm{GM}}
is isomorphic to the moduli space
\mathbf{M}_{2,\mathrm{ord,ss}}^{\mathrm{GM}}
parameterizing strongly smooth GM surfaces; see
[
DK18
, Definition 3.15]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
X_{n}\to M_{n}
be the double cover in Remark
2.3
(
2
), and let
Q_{n-1}\subseteq M_{n}
be the branched divisor. Then there exists a morphism
\Aut(X_{n})\to\Aut(M_{n},Q_{n-1})
with kernel
{\mathbb Z}/2{\mathbb Z}.

Proof.

Let
L
be the ample generator of
\Pic(X_{n})\cong{\mathbb Z}
. Then the morphism
\phi_{|L|}\colon X_{n}\to|L|
has image
M_{n}
and is branched along some
Q_{n-1}\subseteq M_{n}
. For any
g\in\Aut(X_{n})
,
g^{*}L
is also the ample generator of
\Pic(X_{n})
, that is,
g^{*}L\cong L
. It induces an action
\tilde{g}\colon|L|\to|L|
preserving
M_{n}
and
Q_{n-1}
. If
\tilde{g}={\mathrm{id}}
, then
g
is either
{\mathrm{id}}
or an involution. We get a homomorphism
\Aut(X_{n})\to\Aut(M_{n},Q_{n-1})
, whose kernel is
{\mathbb Z}/2{\mathbb Z}
.
∎

2.2. K-stability

We recall the basic notions in K-stability theory in this subsection.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log Fano pair,
l_{0}\in{\mathbb N}
be a positive integer such that
l_{0}(K_{X}+\Delta)
is Cartier, and
R=R(X,\Delta)=\oplus_{m\in l_{0}{\mathbb N}}R_{m}
be the anti-canonical ring of
(X,\Delta)
, where
R_{m}=H^{0}(X,-m(K_{X}+\Delta))
. By choosing an admissible flag over
X
(see
[
LM09
, Equation (1.1)]
), we get an
Okounkov body

\mathbf{O}=\mathbf{O}(-(K_{X}+\Delta))
of the ample
{\mathbb Q}
-divisor
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
.
A
filtration

{\mathcal{F}}
on
R
is a collection of subspaces
{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}R_{m}\subseteq R_{m}
for each
\lambda\in{\mathbb R}
and
m\geq l_{0}{\mathbb N}
such that

•

(
Decreasing
)
{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}R_{m}\supseteq{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m}
for
\lambda\leq\lambda^{\prime}
,

•

(
Left-continuous
)
{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}R_{m}={\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda-\epsilon}R_{m}
for
0<\epsilon\ll 1
,

•

(
Bounded
)
{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}R_{m}=R_{m}
for
\lambda\ll 0
and
{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}R_{m}=0
for
\lambda\gg 0
,

•

(
Multiplicative
)
{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}R_{m}\cdot{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m+m^{\prime}}
.

The filtration
{\mathcal{F}}
is called
linearly bounded
if there is a constant
C>0
such that
{\mathcal{F}}^{mC}R_{m}=0
for all
m
.
For example, any valuation
v
on
X
defines a filtration
{\mathcal{F}}_{v}
on
R
,

{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{\lambda}R_{m}:=\{s\in R_{m}\mid v(s)\geq\lambda\}.

If the log discrepancy satisfies
A_{X,\Delta}(v)<+\infty
, then
{\mathcal{F}}_{v}
is linearly bounded; see
[
JM12
]
.

Definition 2.8

(Concave transforms)
.

For any linearly bounded filtration
{\mathcal{F}}
on
R
and
t\in{\mathbb R}
, we have a graded linear subseries
{\mathcal{F}}^{(t)}R_{\bullet}\subseteq R_{\bullet}
defined by
({\mathcal{F}}^{(t)}R)_{m}={\mathcal{F}}^{mt}R_{m}
(see
[
BJ20
, Section 2.4]
), which induces a descending family of Okounkov bodies
\mathbf{O}^{(t)}=\mathbf{O}({\mathcal{F}}^{(t)}R_{\bullet})\subseteq\mathbf{O}
. By
[
BJ20
, Section 2.5]
, we define the
concave transform

G_{\mathcal{F}}\colon\mathbf{O}\to{\mathbb R}
of
{\mathcal{F}}
by

\displaystyle G_{\mathcal{F}}(y)={\operatorname{sup}}\left\{t\in{\mathbb R}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}y\in\mathbf{O}^{(t)}\right\}.

If
y\in\text{int}(\mathbf{O})_{\mathbb Q}=\text{int}(\mathbf{O})\cap{\mathbb Q}^{n}
, then we have

\displaystyle G_{\mathcal{F}}(y)={\operatorname{sup}}\left\{\frac{\ord_{\mathcal{F}}(s)}{m}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\frac{\mathfrak{v}(s)}{m}=\gamma,\exists m\in l_{0}{\mathbb N},\,\exists s\in R_{m}\right\},

where
\mathfrak{v}\colon K(X)\to{\mathbb Z}^{n}
is the faithful valuation in the definition of the Okounkov body
\mathbf{O}
.

We define the
S
-invariant
of
{\mathcal{F}}
by

\displaystyle S(-(K_{X}+\Delta);{\mathcal{F}})=\frac{1}{\mathbf{v}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbf{O}}Gd\rho,

where
\mathbf{v}=\vol(\mathbf{O})
is the volume of the Okounkov body
\mathbf{O}
and
d\rho
is the Lebesgue measure.
Moreover, for any valuation
v
on
X
, we define the
Fujita–Li invariant

\FL(v)
(or
\beta
-invariant
\beta(v)
) by

\displaystyle\FL(v)=A_{X,\Delta}(v)-S(-(K_{X}+\Delta);{\mathcal{F}}_{v}).

We also define the
\delta
-invariant
of
(X,\Delta)
by

\displaystyle\delta(X,\Delta)=\mathop{{\operatorname{inf}}}_{v}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{S(-(K_{X}+\Delta);{\mathcal{F}}_{v})},

where the infimum runs over all the non-trivial valuations
v
over
X
.

Theorem 2.9

(Fujita–Li’s valuative criterion)
.

A log Fano pair
(X,\Delta)
is K-stable
(
resp. K-semistable
)
if and only if  we have
\FL(v)>0

(
resp.
\FL(v)\geq 0)
for any valuation
v
over
X
.

We now consider a Fano pair
(X,\Delta)
with a torus
{\mathbb T}={\mathbb G}_{m}^{r}
-action. Then the Cartier divisors
-m(K_{X}+\Delta)

4.1. The automorphism group and delta invariant of a quintic Del Pezzo fivefold

Remark 5.3
.

The theorem says that if
\delta(V,\Delta_{V})>1
and
\delta(X,\Delta)>\frac{r(n+1)}{n(r+1)}
, then

is K-stable for any

Proof.

For any

such that the pair

is log Fano, we denote by

the graded linear series of
-K_{X}-\Delta-cV
and by

the refinement of

by

If

is a closed point, then
[

implies that

\displaystyle\delta_{x}(X,\Delta+cV)\geq{\operatorname{min}}\left\{\frac{(1-c)(n+1)}{r+1-c},\delta_{x}(V,\Delta_{V};W^{V}_{\bullet\bullet})\right\}.

Since

\displaystyle=\left((-K_{X}-\Delta-cV)-S(-K_{X}-\Delta-cV;V)V\right)|_{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{n(r+1-c)}{r(n+1)}(-K_{V}-\Delta_{V}).

(5.2)

\displaystyle\delta_{x}(X,\Delta+cV)\geq{\operatorname{min}}\left\{\frac{(1-c)(n+1)}{r+1-c},\frac{r(n+1)}{n(r+1-c)}\delta_{x}(V,\Delta_{V})\right\}.

\displaystyle\delta_{x}(X,\Delta+cV)

\displaystyle=\mathop{{\operatorname{inf}}}_{C_{X}(v)\ni x}\frac{A_{X,\Delta+cV}(v)}{S(-K_{X}-\Delta-cV;v)}

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{r+1-c}\delta(X,\Delta)>1

We get (

7. K-stability of general special Gushel–Mukai manifolds

In this section, we prove the main theorem of this paper, that is, general special GM manifolds are K-stable.
Let
X
be a GM
n
-fold and
M\subseteq{\mathbb P}^{n+3}
be the quintic Del Pezzo
n
-fold. When
X
is special, there is a double cover
p\colon X\to M
branched along a smooth quadric divisor
Q
which is an ordinary GM
({n-1})
-fold. We have
p^{*}(K_{M}+\frac{1}{2}Q)=K_{X}
. By
[
LZ22a
, Theorem 1.2]
, we need to test the K-stability of
(M,\frac{1}{2}Q)
.

We first recall some basic results about the projective cone over a log Fano pair. Let
(V,\Delta_{V})
be an
(n-1)
-dimensional log Fano pair such that
L=-\frac{1}{r}(K_{V}+\Delta_{V})
is an ample Cartier divisor for some rational number
r\in(0,n]
.
We define the projective cone over
V
with polarization
L
by

Y=\overline{{\mathcal{C}}}(V,L):=\Proj\left(\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{m\geq 0}\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{0\leq\lambda\leq m}H^{0}(V,(m-\lambda)L)s^{\lambda}\right),

which is the union of the affine cone
{\mathcal{C}}=\Spec\big(\oplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}(V,mL)\big)
and a divisor
V_{\infty}\cong V
at infinity. Let
\Delta_{Y}
be closure of
\Delta_{V}\times{\mathbb C}^{*}
in
Y
. Then
-(K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y})
is Cartier and

-(K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y})\sim_{\mathbb Q}(1+r)V_{\infty}.

By
[
LX20
, Proposition 5.3]
or Theorem
5.2
, we have the following.

Proposition 7.1
.

If
(V,\Delta_{V})
is K-semistable, then
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+(1-\frac{r}{n})V_{\infty})
is K-semistable.

We will use the following result about degeneration to the normal cone. See for example
[
LZ22b
, Lemma 2.12]
and
[
ZZ22
, Lemma 5.3]
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
X
be a Fano variety of dimension
n
, and
V
be a prime divisor on
X
such that
-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb Q}(1+r)V
with
0<r\leq n
. Then
(X,V)
specially degenerates to
(Y,V_{\infty})
, where
Y=\overline{{\mathcal{C}}}(V,H)
and
H=V|_{V}
.
In particular, if
V
is K-semistable, then
(X,(1-\frac{r}{n})V)
is K-semistable.

Now we prove the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 7.3
.

General special GM-manifolds are K-stable.

Proof.

It was proved by
[
AZ22
]
that smooth GM threefolds are all K-stable. We need to prove that there exists a K-stable special GM
n
-fold for
n=4,5,6
. Then by the openness of K-stability, we are done.
We first prove that general special GM
n
-folds are K-semistable for
n=4,5,6
. It suffices to find a smooth quadric divisor
Q_{n-1}\subseteq M_{n}
such that
(M_{n},\frac{1}{2}Q_{n-1})
is K-semistable.

For
n=4
, note that
Q
is a GM threefold, which is K-stable by
[
AZ23
, Theorem C]
. Since
-K_{M}\sim_{\mathbb Q}\frac{3}{2}Q
, we have
r=\frac{1}{2}
and
1-\frac{r}{n}=\frac{7}{8}=:c_{\operatorname{max}}
. By Proposition
7.2
, we conclude that
(M,\frac{7}{8}Q)
is K-semistable. On the other hand, we consider a smooth hyperplane section
H
of
M
. Then
H\cong M_{3}
, which is K-polystable by
[
ACC+23
, Example 3.4.1]
. BY the same method, since
-K_{M}=3H
, we have
r=2
,
1-\frac{r}{n}=\frac{1}{2}
. Hence
(M,\frac{1}{2}H)
is K-semistable. One can construct a family that deforms
(M,\frac{1}{2}H)
to
(M,\frac{1}{4}Q)
for some smooth quadric divisor
Q
of
M
. By the openness of K-semistability, we conclude that there exists a smooth quadric divisor
{\tilde{Q}}
on
M
such that
(M,\frac{1}{4}{\tilde{Q}})
is K-semistable. Finally, by interpolation of K-stability (see
[
ADL24
, Proposition 2.13]
), we see that
(M,c{\tilde{Q}})
is K-semistable for any
\frac{1}{4}\leq c\leq\frac{7}{8}
. In particular,
(M,\frac{1}{2}{\tilde{Q}})
is K-semistable.

For
n=5
, we consider the pair
(M=M_{5},\frac{1}{2}(H+H^{\prime}))
, where
M=M_{5}
and
H
,
H^{\prime}
are two distinct smooth hyperplane sections of
M
with smooth intersection
I=H\cap H^{\prime}
. So
H
and
H^{\prime}
are
M_{4}
and
I\cong M_{3}
. By Proposition
7.2
, this pair can specially degenerate to
\big(\overline{{\mathcal{C}}}(H,{\mathcal{O}}_{H}(1)),\frac{1}{2}(H_{\infty}+{\mathcal{E}})\big)
, where
{\mathcal{E}}=\overline{{\mathcal{C}}}(I,{\mathcal{O}}_{I}(1))
. Applying Proposition
7.1
to
(H,\frac{1}{2}I)
and
r=\frac{5}{2}
, since
{\mathcal{O}}_{H}(1)\sim_{\mathbb Q}-\frac{1}{r}(K_{H}+\frac{1}{2}I)
, we see that
\big(\overline{{\mathcal{C}}}(H,{\mathcal{O}}_{H}(1)),\frac{1}{2}(H_{\infty}+{\mathcal{E}})\big)
is K-semistable.
Then by the openness of K-semistability,
(M,\frac{1}{2}(H+H^{\prime}))
is K-semistable. Similarly to the proof of the
n=4
case, we can deform
(M,\frac{1}{2}(H+H^{\prime}))
to some
(M,\frac{1}{2}Q)
, and we get the K-semistability of
(M,\frac{1}{2}{\tilde{Q}})
for some smooth quadric divisor
{\tilde{Q}}
.

We also consider the degeneration induced by some
Q\subseteq M_{5}
which is an ordinary GM fourfold. This will be useful in the proof of K-stability. In the
n=4
case, we already have a K-semistable special GM fourfold. By the openness of K-semistability, there exists a K-semistable ordinary GM fourfold
Q
. Since
-K_{M}\sim 2Q
, we have
r=1
and
c_{\operatorname{max}}=\frac{4}{5}
. Then by Proposition
7.2
, we see that
(M,\frac{4}{5}Q)
is K-semistable.
By interpolation we have that
(M,cQ)
is K-semistable for any
\frac{1}{2}\leq c\leq\frac{4}{5}
.

For
n=6
, we already have a K-polystable base
M=M_{6}=\mathrm{Gr}(2,5)
. The same argument as in the previous paragraph shows that
(M,\frac{3}{4}Q)
is K-semistable for some
Q
. By interpolation, we see that
(M,cQ)
is K-semistable for any
0\leq c\leq\frac{3}{4}
. In particular,
(M,\frac{1}{2}Q)
is K-semistable.

Next, we prove that general special GM
n
-folds are K-stable for
n=4,5,6
. We give a proof by induction on
n
using Theorem
5.2
.

Since any ordinary GM threefold
Q_{3}\subseteq M_{4}
is K-stable and
\delta(M_{4})=\frac{25}{27}>\frac{5}{12}
, we see that
(M_{4},cQ_{3})
is K-stable for any
0\ll c<\frac{7}{8}
by Remark
5.3
. By interpolation,
(M_{4},cQ_{3})
is K-stable for any
\frac{1}{4}<c<\frac{7}{8}
(this holds for any
\frac{1}{9}<c<\frac{7}{8}
and general
Q_{3}\subseteq M_{4}
by Theorem
5.5
). In particular,
(M_{4},\frac{1}{2}Q_{3})
is K-stable, and
\Aut(M_{4},Q_{3})
is finite. Let
X_{4}\to(M_{4},\frac{1}{2}Q_{3})
be the double cover in Remark
2.3
(
2
). Then
\Aut(X_{4})
is finite by Lemma
2.7
. Hence
X_{4}
is K-stable by
[
LZ22a
, Theorem 1.2]
.

By the openness of K-stability, we see that a general
Q_{4}\subseteq M_{5}
is K-stable. Since
\delta(M_{5})=\frac{15}{16}>\frac{3}{5}
, we see that
(M_{5},cQ_{4})
is K-stable for any
0\ll c<\frac{4}{5}
and general
Q_{4}
by Remark
5.3
, hence for any
\frac{1}{8}<c<\frac{4}{5}
and general
Q_{4}
by Theorem
5.6
using interpolation. In particular,
(M_{5},\frac{1}{2}Q_{4})
is K-stable and
\Aut(M_{5},Q_{4})
is finite. Let
X_{5}\to(M_{5},\frac{1}{2}Q_{4})
be the corresponding double cover. Then
\Aut(X_{5})
is finite by Lemma
2.7
, and
X_{5}
is K-stable by
[
LZ22a
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Also by the openness of K-stability, we get a K-stable ordinary GM fivefold
Q_{5}\subseteq M_{6}
. With the same argument, we conclude that
(M_{6},cQ_{5})
is K-stable for any
0<c<\frac{3}{4}
. In particular,
(M_{6},cQ_{5})
is K-stable with finite automorphism group. Hence its double cover
X_{6}
has finite automorphism group by Lemma
2.7
and is K-stable by
[
LZ22a
, Theorem 1.2]
.

We conclude by the openness of K-stability that general special GM-manifolds are K-stable.
∎

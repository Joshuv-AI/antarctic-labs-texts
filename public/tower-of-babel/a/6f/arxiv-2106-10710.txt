A New Signal Representation Using Complex Conjugate Pair Sums
Shaik Basheeruddin Shah, Vijay Kumar Chakka, and Arikatla Satyanarayana Reddy

Abstract
This letter introduces a real valued summation known as Complex Conjugate Pair Sum (CCPS) . The space spanned by CCPS and its one circular downshift is called Complex Conjugate Subspace (CCS) . For a given positive integer N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 , there exists φ ⁡ ( N ) 2 \frac{\varphi(N)}{2} CCPSs forming φ ⁡ ( N ) 2 \frac{\varphi(N)}{2} CCSs, where φ ⁡ ( N ) \varphi(N) is the Euler’s totient function . We prove that these CCSs are mutually orthogonal and their direct sum form a φ ⁡ ( N ) \varphi(N) dimensional subspace s N s_{N} of ℂ N \mathbb{C}^{N} . We propose that any signal of finite length N N is represented as a linear combination of elements from a special basis of s d s_{d} , for each divisor d d of N N . This defines a new transform named as Complex Conjugate Periodic Transform (CCPT) . Later, we compared CCPT with DFT (Discrete Fourier Transform) and RPT (Ramanujan Periodic Transform). It is shown that, using CCPT we can estimate the period, hidden periods and frequency information of a signal. Whereas, RPT does not provide the frequency information. For a complex valued input signal, CCPT offers computational benefit over DFT. A CCPT dictionary based method is proposed to extract non-divisor period information.

Complex Conjugate Pair Sum

In DFT, an
N
-finite length signal is represented by using the following complex exponential basis,

\footnotesize{s_{N,k}}(n)=e^{\frac{j2{\pi}kn}{N}},\hskip 8.50012pt0\ {\leq}\ n,k\ {\leq}\ {N-1}.\normalsize

(1)

For a given
k

{s_{N,k}}(n)

P_{k}

Hence

Let

(k,N)=\frac{N}{d},

i.e.,

\#H_{d}

H_{d}=\{k\in\mathbb{N}{\mid}0\leq k\leq N-1,(k,N)=\frac{N}{d}\}.
Here it is easy to see that the sets

for each

form a partition of
\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}.
And the set

is equivalent to the set
\{x\in\mathbb{N}{\mid}0{\leq}x{\leq}{d-1},(x,d)=1\}.
As a consequence, we have
\#H_{d}=\varphi(d)
and
\sum_{d|N}\varphi(d)=N
.
From this discussion the following result is trivial.

Theorem 1:
The number of sequences in
\{s_{N,k}(n){\mid}

0{\leq}k{\leq}N-1\}
, having period exactly equal to
d
is
\varphi(d)
, where

.

For every

there exists a complex conjugate sequence

as
(k,N)=(N-k,N)
.
These two sequences form a complex conjugate pair. So, for
N{\geq}3
, there are
\frac{\varphi(N)}{2}
complex conjugate pair sequences, having period exactly equal to
N
.
If
N=1
(or)
2
, the sequence

itself is a complex conjugate, because
\varphi(1)=\varphi(2)=1
.
Given any

, the Complex Conjugate Pair Sum (CCPS) is defined as,

\displaystyle c_{N,k}(n)

\displaystyle=M\big(s_{N,k}(n)+s_{N,N-k}(n)\big)=2Mcos\Big(\frac{2{\pi}{k}n}{N}\Big).

(2)

where
\footnotesize M=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},&\text{if}\ N=1\ \text{(or)}\ 2\\
1,&\text{if }\ {N{\geq}3}\end{cases}\normalsize
,
k\in

A_{N}=\{a\in

\mathbb{N}{\mid}1{\leq}a{\leq}\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor,

(a,N)=1\}
if

and
k=1

Notice that CCPSs are real, even symmetric
(
c_{N,k}(n)=c_{N,k}(N-n)
)
and periodic with period

.
Using the orthogonality between the complex exponentials, one can easily verify the following theorem.

Theorem 2: Orthogonality:
Any two CCPSs
and their circular shifts are orthogonal to each other,
i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N-1}c_{N_{1},k_{1}}(n-{l_{1}})c_{N_{2},k_{2}}(n-{l_{2}})

(3)

\displaystyle=2N{M^{2}}cos\Bigg(\frac{2{\pi}{k_{1}}({l_{1}}-{l_{2}})}{N_{1}}\Bigg)\delta({N_{1}}-{N_{2}})\delta({k_{1}}-{k_{2}}),

where

,
{l_{1}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and

A New Basis For Complex Conjugate Subspaces

For each

\footnotesize\mathbf{D_{N,k}}=\begin{bmatrix}c_{N,k}(0)&c_{N,k}(N-1)&\dots&c_{N,k}(1)\\
c_{N,k}(1)&c_{N,k}(0)&\dots&c_{N,k}(2)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
c_{N,k}(N-1)&c_{N,k}(N-2)&\dots&c_{N,k}(0)\end{bmatrix}.\normalsize

(4)

Here each column in
\mathbf{D_{N,k}}
is a circular downshift of the previous column.
Using (
2
),

\displaystyle\mathbf{D_{N,k}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}e^{\frac{j2{\pi}k(0)}{N}}+e^{\frac{-j2{\pi}k(0)}{N}}&\dots&e^{\frac{j2{\pi}k(1)}{N}}+e^{\frac{-j2{\pi}k(1)}{N}}\\

\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\

(5)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B^{H}},

where

\footnotesize\mathbf{B^{H}}=\begin{bmatrix}e^{\frac{-j2{\pi}{k}(0)}{N}}&e^{\frac{-j2{\pi}{k}(1)}{N}}&\dots&e^{\frac{-j2{\pi}{k}(N-1)}{N}}\\
e^{\frac{j2{\pi}{k}(0)}{N}}&e^{\frac{j2{\pi}{k}(1)}{N}}&\dots&e^{j\frac{2{\pi}{k}(N-1)}{N}}\end{bmatrix}_{2\times N}.\normalsize

(6)

This property of decomposing

into

is known as

[

]
and columns of

are orthogonal

]

Further, it is easy to check that first two columns of

From (
5
), the column space of
\mathbf{D_{N,k}}
(denoted as

[
13
]
, which consists of
p_{i}
periodic signals having a particular frequency

In
[

]

\scriptsize x_{N,k}(n)=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{1}{\beta_{lk}c_{N,k}(n-l)}.\normalsize

(7)

Since

As CCPSs and its circular shifts are mutually orthogonal, whenever

In summary, for a given

VI
Conclusion

In this work, the problem of a finite length signal representation is solved by using the signals belongs to CCSs.
Later, we have shown that the proposed transformation (CCPT) allows us to get both divisor and non-divisor period information of a signal.
We compared the proposed method with DFT and RPT for a given complex (or) real valued input sequence.
Although this letter introduces CCPT,
there are many other properties that require a detailed analysis.
We will aspire to work on this in the near future.

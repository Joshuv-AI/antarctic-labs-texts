On Lebesgue points of entropy solutions to the eikonal equation
Xavier Lamy, Elio Marconi

Abstract
We consider entropy solutions to the eikonal equation | ∇ u | = 1 |\nabla u|=1 in two space dimensions. These solutions are motivated by a class of variational problems and fail in general to have bounded variation. Nevertheless they share with BV functions, several of their fine properties: we show in particular that the set of non-Lebesgue points has co-dimension at least one.

Introduction

We consider an open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
m\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
a solution of the eikonal equation

\displaystyle|m|=1\text{ a.e., and }\nabla\cdot m=0\text{ in }\Omega.

(1)

We are interested in particular in solutions that arise as limits as
\varepsilon\to 0
of vector fields
m_{\varepsilon}
with equi-bounded energy
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}F_{\varepsilon}(m_{\varepsilon},\Omega)<\infty
, where

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(m;\Omega)=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla m|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}(1-|m|^{2})^{2},

(2)

\displaystyle m\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\qquad\nabla\cdot m=0,

are the functionals introduced by Aviles and Giga in
[
4
]
.
We refer to the introduction of
[
10
]
for a description of several physical applications.

The notion of entropy, borrowed from the field of conservation laws, plays a fundamental role in the study of the singular limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
of these functionals.
We say that a compactly supported function
\Phi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
is an
entropy
for (
1
) if for every open set
U
and every smooth
m:U\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
solving
\nabla\cdot m=0
and
|m|=1
it holds
\nabla\cdot\Phi(m)=0
.
It is shown in
[
2
,
7
]
that functions with equi-bounded energy as
\varepsilon\to 0
are pre-compact in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and any limit is an entropy solution of (
1
): namely for every entropy
\Phi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
the distribution
\nabla\cdot\Phi(m)
is a finite Radon measure.
Remarkably, the same class of entropy solutions to (
1
) contains the asymptotic domain of other families of functionals: see
[
1
,
16
]
for two micromagnetics models.

It is shown in
[
8
]
that
m
is an entropy solution if and only if the following kinetic equation (introduced in
[
9
]
) is satisfied:

\displaystyle e^{is}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathbf{1}_{m(x)\cdot e^{is}>0}=\partial_{s}\sigma,\qquad\sigma\in\mathcal{M}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}).

(3)

We denote by
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
the entropy dissipation measure given by

\displaystyle\nu(A)=|\sigma|(A\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}),\qquad A\subset\Omega.

(4)

It is known
[
6
]
that
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e. point
x\in\Omega
at which
\nu(B_{r}(x))/r\to 0
as
r\to 0^{+}
is a vanishing mean oscillation (VMO) point of
m
, that is,

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{r}(x)}\left|m-\fint_{B_{r}(x)}m\right|\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }r\to 0^{+}.

It is conjectured in
[
6
,
Conjecture 1
(b’)]
that

\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e. such point is in fact
a Lebesgue point.
Our main result states that this conjecture is true under the additional assumption that
\nu(B_{r}(x))/r
decays algebraically to 0.

Theorem 1
.

Let
m\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be an entropy solution (
3
) of the eikonal equation (
1
).
Then
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
such that
\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\nu(B_{r}(x))/r^{1+a}=0

for some
a>0
is a Lebesgue point of
m
.
In particular, the set of non Lebesgue points of
m
has Hausdorff dimension at most 1.

Analogs of Theorem
1
have been obtained previously in
[
12
]
for Burgers’ equation, and in
[
15
]
for general scalar conservations laws.
To prove Theorem
1

we follow the scheme laid out in
[
15
]
,
where it is shown that oscillations
of averages
\fint_{B_{r}(x)}u
of the solution
u
are controlled by the
entropy dissipation.
This, together with the VMO property, implies the Lebesgue point property.
However, a key feature for the argument of
[
15
]

is that the solution
u
takes values in the ordered set
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Here our solution
m
takes values in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
and adapting the argument of
[
15
]
is not enough to conclude (see Proposition
3
).
Our proof of Theorem
1

relies instead on the following dichotomy:
either the oscillations of
\fint_{B_{r}(x)}m
are
controlled by the entropy dissipation
\nu
,
or
m
takes very different values in large subsets of
B_{R}(x)
–
this second alternative is ruled out by the VMO property.
That dichotomy is made quantitative in the next statement.

Proposition 2
.

Assume
B_{1}\subset\Omega
. Let
r\in(0,1/2)
and

\displaystyle h=h(r)

\displaystyle=\max_{x_{1},x_{2}\in\overline{B}_{2r}}\left|\fint_{B_{r}(x_{1})}m-\fint_{B_{r}(x_{2})}m\right|.

(5)

There exist absolute constants
c,\delta>0
such that, if

\displaystyle R=\frac{32r}{\delta h^{2}}\leq 1,

then either

\displaystyle\nu(B_{R})\geq c\,h^{11}r,

(6)

or there exist
s_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\displaystyle\left|B_{R}\cap\left\{m\cdot e^{is}\geq-\frac{1}{2}\right\}\right|\geq c\,R^{2}\quad\text{ for }\dist(s,\{s_{0},s_{0}+\pi\})\leq\pi/4.

(7)

Here and in the rest of the article, we denote by
|A|
the Lebesgue measure of a measurable set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Theorem
1
is a rather direct consequence of Proposition
2
, as we explain now.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
x\in\Omega
be a VMO point of
m
such that
\nu(B_{r}(x))/r^{1+a}\to 0
for some
a>0
.
Translating and rescaling we assume without loss of generality that
x=0
and
B_{1}\subset\Omega
.
We claim that
h(r)=\mathcal{O}(r^{b})
for
b=a/(10+2a)>0
.
This, together with
the fact that
0
is a VMO point of
m
,
implies that
0
is a Lebesgue point (see
[
15
, Lemma 4.6]
).
To prove that
h(r)=\mathcal{O}(r^{b})
we argue by contradiction and assume that
h(r)/r^{b}\to\infty
along a sequence
r\to 0^{+}
.
Then, along the same sequence,

\displaystyle R=\frac{32r}{\delta h^{2}}=\frac{32}{\delta}r^{1-2b}\left(\frac{r^{b}}{h}\right)^{2}\to 0\quad\text{because }b<\frac{1}{2},

and

\displaystyle\frac{R^{1+a}}{h^{8}r}=\frac{32^{1+a}}{\delta^{1+a}}\left(\frac{r^{b}}{h}\right)^{10+2a}\to 0.

Therefore, applying Proposition
2
along the sequence
R\to 0
, the condition (
6
) cannot be satisfied because
\nu(B_{R})/R^{1+a}\to 0
, so we have (
7
). This contradicts the VMO property: for all small enough
R
, the projection
z_{R}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
of
\fint_{B_{R}}m
onto
\mathbb{S}^{1}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left|B_{R}\cap\{|m-z_{R}|\geq\pi/12\}\right|\leq\frac{c}{2}R^{2}.

(8)

But one can choose
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\dist(s,\{s_{0},s_{0}+\pi\})\leq\pi/4
and

\displaystyle z\cdot e^{is}\geq-\frac{1}{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|z-z_{R}|\geq\pi/12,

for any
z\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
(if
z_{R}=e^{is_{R}}
, any
s\in[s_{R}+3\pi/4,s_{R}+5\pi/4]
has that property).
According to (
7
) this implies

|B_{R}\cap\{|m-z_{R}|\geq\pi/12\}|\geq cR^{2}
,
in contradiction with (
8
).
Hence we have proved that
x
is a Lebesgue point.
The estimate on the Hausdorff dimension of non Lebesgue points follows via a covering argument (see e.g.
[
3
, Theorem 2.56]
).
∎

The proof of Proposition
2
has two main ingredients.
The first ingredient consists in adapting the arguments of
[
15
]
to prove a
dichotomy similar to Proposition
2
,
but where the second option (
7
) is replaced by a statement which is not strong enough to conclude.

Proposition 3
.

Let
r\in(0,1/2)
and
h
be as in Proposition
2
.
There exist absolute constants
c,\delta>0
such that, if

R=32r/(\delta h^{2})\leq 1
,
then we have either
\nu(B_{R})\geq ch^{11}r
, or

\displaystyle\left|B_{R/2}\cap\left\{m\cdot m_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2}\right\}\right|\geq chr^{2}\quad\text{and }\quad\left|B_{R/2}\cap\left\{m\cdot m_{0}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\right\}\right|\geq chr^{2},

(9)

for some
m_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

The main idea behind the argument in
[
15
]
is that a large value of
h
implies the existence of a configuration which would be impossible in the absence of entropy dissipation. In the presence of dissipation, such configuration provides a lower bound on the dissipation, and there is no dichotomy.
Here instead, not all configurations created by large values of
h
can be ruled out in the absence of dissipation:
in particular the vortex solution
m(x)=x^{\perp}/|x|
has zero dissipation but the values of
h(r)
around the origin are not vanishing.
This is reflected in the second alternative (
9
) of the dichotomy.

The second ingredient in our proof of Proposition
2
consists in using the methods developed in
[
14
,
13
,
5
,
11
]
in order to pass from (
9
) to (
7
).

Proposition 4
.

Let
m_{0}=e^{is_{0}}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, and
R>0
such that
B_{R}\subset\Omega
. Then we have either

\displaystyle\nu(B_{R})\geq\frac{c}{R}\min(|X_{+}|,|X_{-}|),\qquad X_{\pm}=B_{R/2}\cap\{\pm m\cdot m_{0}\geq 1/2\},

(10)

or
\nu(B_{R})\geq cR
, or (
7
), for some absolute constant
c>0
.

Proposition
2
follows readily from Proposition
3
and Proposition
4
.
Thanks to Proposition
3
, we know indeed that either
\nu(B_{R})\geq ch^{11}r
, in which case we are done, or estimate (
9
) is valid.
But according to Proposition
4
, if (
9
) is satisfied, then we have either
\nu(B_{R})\geq chr^{2}/R\geq ch^{11}r
, or
\nu(B_{R})\geq cR\geq ch^{11}r
, or (
7
).
In all cases, Proposition
2
is verified.

The proofs of Proposition
3
and Proposition
4
are presented in
Section
2
and Section
3
.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}e^{is}\rho_{1}(s)\,ds-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}e^{is}\rho_{2}(s)\,ds\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}|\rho_{1}(s)-\rho_{2}(s)|\,ds

\displaystyle\inf_{I_{\delta h^{2}}(s)}\rho_{1}-\sup_{I_{\delta h^{2}}(s)}\rho_{2}\geq\frac{h}{4\pi}.

\displaystyle\inf_{I_{\delta h^{2}/4}(s_{0}+\pi/2)}\rho_{1}-\sup_{I_{\delta h^{2}/4}(s_{0}+\pi/2+\delta h^{2})}\rho_{2}\geq\frac{h}{4\pi},

As

\displaystyle\essinf_{I_{\delta h^{2}/4}(s_{0}+\pi/2)}\rho_{1}+\essinf_{I_{\delta h^{2}/4}(s_{0}-\pi/2+\delta h^{2})}\rho_{2}\geq 1+\frac{h}{4\pi},

(12)

\displaystyle\essinf_{I_{\delta h^{2}/4}(s_{0}+\pi/2+\delta h^{2})}\rho_{1}+\essinf_{I_{\delta h^{2}/4}(s_{0}-\pi/2)}\rho_{2}\geq 1+\frac{h}{4\pi}.

Thanks to the techniques in

The main idea is that (

\displaystyle{\mathrm{FT}}(E,t)=\left\{(x,s)\in\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}\colon(x-te^{is},s)\in E\right\}.

Lemma 5
.

Let
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\rho>0
such that
B_{\rho+|t|}\subset\Omega
.
For all
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(B_{\rho}\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}\phi(x,s)\left(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathrm{FT}}(E_{m},t)}-\mathbf{1}_{E_{m}}\right)\,dxds\leq\left(|t|\,\|\partial_{s}\phi\|_{\infty}+t^{2}\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{\infty}\right)\nu(B_{\rho+|t|}).

Proof of Lemma
5
.

Define
\chi,\chi^{{\mathrm{FT}}}\colon[-|t|,|t|]\times\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

\displaystyle\chi(\tau,x,s)=\mathbf{1}_{(x,s)\in E_{m}},\qquad\chi^{{\mathrm{FT}}}(\tau,x,s)=\mathbf{1}_{(x,s)\in{\mathrm{FT}}(E_{m},\tau)}=\chi(x-\tau e^{is},s).

so we have, in the sense of distributions,

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\chi+e^{is}\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}\sigma(x,s),\qquad\partial_{\tau}\chi^{{\mathrm{FT}}}+e^{is}\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi^{{\mathrm{FT}}}=0.

Setting
\hat{\chi}=\chi^{{\mathrm{FT}}}-\chi
, and
\psi(\tau,x,s)=\phi(x+e^{is}(t-\tau),s)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\left[\psi\hat{\chi}\right]+e^{is}\cdot\nabla_{x}\left[\psi\hat{\chi}\right]=-\psi\partial_{s}\sigma.

Integrating with respect to

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}\psi\hat{\chi}\,dxds=\int_{\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}\partial_{s}\psi\,d\sigma(x,s).

Integrating this from

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}\phi(x,s)\left(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathrm{FT}}(E_{m},t)}-\mathbf{1}_{E_{m}}\right)\,dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}\partial_{s}\psi d\sigma\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq|t|\,\|\partial_{s}\psi\|_{\infty}|\nu|(B_{\rho+|t|}\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}).

Noting that

\displaystyle|A_{1}^{{\mathrm{FT}}}|+|A_{2}^{{\mathrm{FT}}}|\geq 2c|B_{r}|\left(1+\frac{h}{4\pi}\right)-64\frac{c^{2}r^{2}}{\delta h^{2}}\geq 2c|B_{r}|\left(1+\frac{h}{8\pi}\right),

(17)

by the choice
c=\delta h^{3}/128\pi
.
Next we consider two cases, depending on whether
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
satisfy a similar inequality.

Case 1.
Assume first that

\displaystyle|A_{1}|+|A_{2}|\geq 2c|B_{r}|\left(1+\frac{h}{16\pi}\right),

then

\displaystyle|\pi_{x}(A_{1})|+|\pi_{x}(A_{2})|\geq|B_{r}|\left(1+\frac{h}{16\pi}\right).

Moreover, since
\pi_{x}(A_{1})\cup\pi_{x}(A_{2})\subset B_{r}(\hat{z})
, it follows that
A:=\pi_{x}(A_{1})\cap\pi_{x}(A_{2})
satisfies
|A|\geq h|B_{r}|/16
.
By construction, we have

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\Big\{x\in B_{r}(\hat{z})\colon\exists s_{1}\in I_{c}(s_{0}+\pi/2),\,s_{2}\in I_{c}(s_{0}-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta h^{2}),

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0001ptm(x)\cdot e^{is_{1}}>0\text{ and }m(x)\cdot e^{is_{2}}>0\Big\}

\displaystyle\subset B_{r}(\hat{z})\cap\{m\cdot e^{is_{0}}\geq\cos(2\delta h^{2})\},

so this implies

\displaystyle\left|B_{R/2}\cap\left\{m\cdot m_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2}\right\}\right|\gtrsim hr^{2}.

(18)

Case 2.
Assume now that

\displaystyle|A_{1}|+|A_{2}|<2c|B_{r}|\left(1+\frac{h}{16\pi}\right).

Then using (
17
) we obtain

\displaystyle|A_{1}^{{\mathrm{FT}}}|-|A_{1}|+|A_{2}^{{\mathrm{FT}}}|-|A_{2}|>2c|B_{r}|\frac{h}{16\pi},

so either
|A_{1}^{{\mathrm{FT}}}|-|A_{1}|
or
|A_{2}^{{\mathrm{FT}}}|-|A_{2}|
is larger than half the right-hand side. We consider without loss of generality only the first case:

\displaystyle|A_{1}^{{\mathrm{FT}}}|-|A_{1}|>|B_{r}|\frac{ch}{16\pi}.

This implies a lower bound on the entropy dissipation
\nu(B_{R})
thanks to Lemma
5
.
Specifically, we apply Lemma
5
to
t=t_{1}
and
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{2r(\hat{z})}\times I_{2c}(s_{0}+\pi/2))
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{x\in B_{r}(\hat{z})}\mathbf{1}_{s\in I_{c}(s_{0}+\pi/2)}\leq\phi(x,s)\leq\mathbf{1}_{x\in B_{(1+\varepsilon)r}(\hat{z})}\mathbf{1}_{s\in I_{(1+\varepsilon)c}(s_{0}+\pi/2)},

and
|\partial_{s}\phi|\leq 2/(\varepsilon c)
,
|\nabla_{x}\phi|\leq 2/(\varepsilon r)
.
We choose
\varepsilon=h/192\pi
to ensure

\displaystyle\left|\left(B_{(1+\varepsilon)r}(\hat{z})\times I_{(1+\varepsilon)c}\right)\setminus\left(B_{r}(\hat{z})\times I_{c}\right)\right|\leq\frac{ch}{32\pi}|B_{r}|.

Since
|t_{1}|\leq 8r/(\delta h^{2})
and
B_{2r+|t_{1}|}(\hat{z})\subset B_{R}
, we deduce
\nu(B_{R})\gtrsim\delta^{3}h^{11}r\gtrsim h^{11}r
.

Similarly, using (
13
) we have two cases: either

\displaystyle\left|B_{R/2}\cap\left\{m\cdot m_{0}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\right\}\right|\gtrsim hr^{2},

(19)

or
\nu(B_{R})\gtrsim h^{11}r
.
So gathering all cases, we see that either both (
18
) and (
19
) are satisfied, or
\nu(B_{R})\gtrsim h^{11}r
,
which is exactly the dichotomy of Proposition
3
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4

In order to prove Proposition
4
, we briefly recall from
[
13
]
the notion of
Lagrangian representation
of an entropy solution
m
of the eikonal equation.
In
[
13
,
14
]
the second author shows the existence of a finite non-negative Radon measure
\omega
on the set of curves

\displaystyle\Gamma=\Big\{(\gamma,t^{-}_{\gamma},t^{+}_{\gamma})\colon

\displaystyle 0\leq t^{-}_{\gamma}\leq t^{+}_{\gamma}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\gamma=(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{s})\in{\mathrm{BV}}((t^{-}_{\gamma},t^{+}_{\gamma});\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}),

\displaystyle\gamma_{x}\text{ is Lipschitz}\Big\},

with the following three properties:

•

for every
t\in(0,1)
, the pushforward of
\omega
, restricted to the section

\Gamma(t)=\{(\gamma,t^{-}_{\gamma},t^{+}_{\gamma})\in\Gamma\colon t_{\gamma}^{-}<t<t_{\gamma}^{+}\}
, by the evaluation map
e_{t}\colon\gamma\mapsto\gamma(t)
(a right-continuous representative of
\gamma_{s}
is always considered), is uniform on the ‘epigraph’
E_{m}=\{m(x)\cdot e^{is}>0\}
, that is,

(e_{t})_{\sharp}\left[\omega\lfloor\Gamma(t)\right]=\mathbf{1}_{m(x)\cdot e^{is}>0}\,dx\,ds;

(20)

•

the measure
\omega
is concentrated on curves
(\gamma,t^{-}_{\gamma},t^{+}_{\gamma})\in\Gamma
solving the characteristic equation,

\dot{\gamma}_{x}(t)=e^{i\gamma_{s}(t)}\qquad\text{for a.e. }t\in(t^{-}_{\gamma},t^{+}_{\gamma});

(21)

•

the entropy dissipation measure (
4
) disintegrates along the Lagrangian curves as

\nu(A)=\int_{\Gamma}\mu_{\gamma}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(A))\,d\omega(\gamma)\qquad\text{for all measurable }A\subset\Omega,

(22)

where
\mu_{\gamma}=|D_{t}\gamma_{s}|
, with the convention that a jump of
\gamma_{s}
from
s^{-}
to
s^{+}
at time
t_{0}\in(t_{\gamma}^{-},t_{\gamma}^{+})
contributes
\dist_{{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}(s^{-},s^{+})\delta_{t=t_{0}}
to the jump part of
\mu_{\gamma}
(see
[
13
, Proposition 2.5]
).

Moreover, the Lagrangian property (
20
) implies that
\omega
is concentrated on curves
\gamma
such that
\gamma_{x}(t)
is a Lebesgue point of
m
with
m(\gamma_{x}(t))\cdot e^{i\gamma_{s}(t^{+})}>0
, for a.e.
t\in(0,1)

[
13
, Lemma 2.7]
.
We denote by
\Gamma_{g}\subset\Gamma
the full-measure subset of Lagrangian curves which satisfy that property together with the characteristic equation (
21
).

The proof of Proposition
4
is based on two main tools.
The first, Lemma
6
, is a dichotomy stating that either Lagrangian curves passing through a given set create a lot of dissipation, or one can find an almost-straight Lagrangian curve passing through that set.
The second (
[
11
, Lemma 5.2]
, a slightly more precise version of
[
13
, Lemma 3.1]
, itself adapted from
[
14
, Lemma 22]
) is another dichotomy: given an almost-straight Lagrangian curve, either the density of points at which
m
lies in the semi-circle indicated by the
s
-component of that curve is high, or a lot of dissipation must be created.
The succession of these two dichotomies is reflected in the three alternatives in the conclusion of Proposition
4
.
We first state and prove the first tool, and then proceed to the proof of Proposition
4
.

Lemma 6
.

For any
R>0
such that
B_{R}\subset\Omega
, any measurable set
A\subset B_{R}\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}
, and any
\eta\in(0,1)
, we have either

\displaystyle\nu(B_{R})\gtrsim\frac{\eta}{R}\left|\{(x,s)\in A\colon m(x)\cdot e^{is}>0\}\right|,

(23)

or there exists a curve
\gamma\in\Gamma_{g}
and a connected component
J
of
\gamma_{x}^{-1}(B_{R})
such that

\displaystyle J\cap\gamma^{-1}(A)\neq\emptyset\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{\gamma}(J)\leq\eta.

Proof of Lemma
6
.

Assume that the second alternative of Lemma
6
is not verified: for every curve
\gamma\in\Gamma_{g}
and every connected component
J
of
\gamma_{x}^{-1}(B_{R})
intersecting
\gamma^{-1}(A)
, we have
\mu_{\gamma}(J)>\eta
.
Then we claim that

\displaystyle\mu_{\gamma}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(B_{R}))

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{\eta}{R}T(\gamma),\qquad T(\gamma)=\left|\{t\in(t_{\gamma}^{-},t_{\gamma}^{+})\colon\gamma(t)\in A\}\right|,

(24)

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{g}
.
To prove (
24
), denote by
J_{k}=(t_{k}^{-},t_{k}^{+})
the connected components of
\gamma_{x}^{-1}(B_{R})
which intersect
\gamma^{-1}(A)
.
We show next that
\mu_{\gamma}(J_{k})\gtrsim\eta|J_{k}|/R
for all
k
.
On the one hand, if
|J_{k}|\leq 4R
then
\mu_{\gamma}(J_{k})\gtrsim\eta|J_{k}|/R
because
\mu_{\gamma}(J_{k})>\eta
by assumption.
On the other hand, from the characteristic equation (
21
)
and the definition of
\mu_{\gamma}=|D_{t}\gamma_{s}|
, we have the inequality

\displaystyle|\gamma_{x}(t_{2})-\gamma_{x}(t_{1})-e^{i\gamma_{s}(t_{1})}(t_{2}-t_{1})|\leq\mu_{\gamma}([t_{1},t_{2}])|t_{2}-t_{1}|,

and we deduce that in any
any interval
J\subset(0,1)
such that
\gamma_{x}(J)\subset B_{R}
and
|J|\geq 4R
, we must have
\mu_{\gamma}(J)\geq 1/2
.
Therefore, if
|J_{k}|\geq 4R
, cutting
J_{k}
in disjoint subintervals of length between
4R
and
8R
, we obtain that
\mu_{\gamma}(J_{k})\gtrsim|J_{k}|/R\geq\eta|J_{k}|/R
.
So we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\gamma}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(B_{R}))\geq\sum_{k}\mu_{\gamma}(J_{k})\gtrsim\frac{\eta}{R}\sum_{k}|J_{k}|,

which implies (
24
) since
\gamma^{-1}(A)\subset\bigcup_{k}J_{k}
.
From (
24
) and the fact that
\omega(\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{g})=0
we infer

\displaystyle\nu(B_{R})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\mu_{\gamma}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(B_{R}))\,d\omega(\gamma)\gtrsim\frac{\eta}{R}\int_{\Gamma}T(\gamma)\,d\omega(\gamma),

where the first equality comes from the disintegration (
22
).
Making use of the Lagrangian property (
20
)
to rewrite the last expression,
we see that it is precisely equal to the right-hand side of (
23
), which concludes the proof of Lemma
6
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4
.

We recall that
m_{0}=e^{is_{0}}
and the sets
X_{\pm}
are defined by

\displaystyle X_{\pm}=B_{R/2}\cap\{\pm m\cdot e^{is_{0}}\geq 1/2\}.

For any
\hat{s}\in[s_{0}-\pi/4,s_{0}+\pi/4]
, we apply Lemma
6
to

, where

\eta\in(0,\pi/12)

\displaystyle\left|\{(x,s)\in A(\hat{s})\colon m(x)\cdot e^{is}>0\}\right|\geq\eta|X_{+}|.

So we have either
\nu(B_{R})\gtrsim\eta^{2}|X_{+}|/R

\gamma\in\Gamma_{g}
and a connected component
J
of
\gamma_{x}^{-1}(B_{R})
intersecting
A(\hat{s})
such that
\mu_{\gamma}(J)<\eta
. In that second case, applying

11

we deduce that either
\nu(B_{R})\gtrsim\eta^{3}R

|B_{R}\cap\{m\cdot e^{i\hat{s}}\geq-2\eta\}|\gtrsim\eta R^{2}

We fix
\eta=1/4

\hat{s}\in[s_{0}-\pi/4,s_{0}+\pi/4]
, we have

\displaystyle\nu(B_{R})\gtrsim\frac{|X_{+}|}{R},\quad\text{ or }\nu(B_{R})\gtrsim R,\quad\text{ or }\left|B_{R}\cap\{m\cdot e^{i\hat{s}}\geq-1/2\}\right|\gtrsim R^{2}.

Similarly, for all

\displaystyle\nu(B_{R})\gtrsim\frac{|X_{-}|}{R},\quad\text{ or }\nu(B_{R})\gtrsim R,\quad\text{ or }\left|B_{R}\cap\{m\cdot e^{i\hat{s}}\geq-1/2\}\right|\gtrsim R^{2}.

We conclude that we have either (

\displaystyle\left|B_{R}\cap\{m\cdot e^{is}\geq-1/2\}\right|\gtrsim R^{2},

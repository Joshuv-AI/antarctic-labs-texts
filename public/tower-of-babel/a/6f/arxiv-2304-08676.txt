Trajectories of astroparticles in pseudo-Finsler spacetime with the most general modified dispersion
Jie Zhu, Bo-Qiang Ma

Abstract
Finsler geometry is a natural and fundamental generalization of Riemann geometry, and is a tool to research Lorentz invariance violation. We find the connection between the most general modified dispersion relation and a pseudo-Finsler structure, and then we calculate the arrival time delay of astroparticles with different modified dispersion relations in the framework of Finsler geometry. The result suggests that the time delay is irrelevant with the exact form of the modified dispersion relation. If the modified term becomes 0 when E = p E=p , there is no arrival time difference, otherwise the time delays only depend on the Lorentz violation scale and the order at which the Lorentz invariance breaks.

II
A Brief Introduction to Finsler Geometry

Finsler geometry
textbook
is a natural and fundamental generalization of Riemann geometry.
For a manifold
M
, denoted by
T_{x}M
the tangent space at
x\in M
, and by
TM
the tangent bundle of
M
. Each element of
TM
has the form
(x,y)
, where
x\in M
and
y\in T_{x}M
. The natural projection
\pi:TM\rightarrow M
is given by
\pi(x,y)\equiv x
.
A Finsler structure, or Finsler norm of
M
is a function

F:TM\rightarrow[0,\infty)

(6)

with the following properties:
(i) Regularity:
\mathrm{F}
is
C^{\infty}
on the entire slit tangent bundle
TM\backslash 0
.
(ii) Positive homogeneity :
F(x,\lambda y)=\lambda F(x,y)
for all
\lambda>0
.
(iii) Strong convexity: The
n\times n
Hessian matrix

g_{ij}\equiv\left(\frac{1}{2}F^{2}\right)_{y^{i}y^{j}}

(7)

is positive-definite at every point of
TM\backslash 0
, where we have used the notation ()
{}_{y^{i}}=

\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}()
.
Finsler geometry has its genesis in integrals of the form

\int_{s}^{r}F\left(x^{1},\cdots,x^{n};\frac{dx^{1}}{d\tau},\cdots,\frac{dx^{n}}{d\tau}\right)d\tau,

(8)

and its geometric meaning is the distance between two points in the Finsler manifold through a certain path.
Given a manifold
M
and a Finsler structure
F
on
TM
, the pair
(M,F)
is called as a Finsler manifold. It is obvious that the Finsler structure
F
is a function of
\left(x^{i},y^{i}\right)
. In the case of
g_{ij}
depending on
x^{i}
only, the Finsler manifold reduces to Riemannian manifold.

To describe the “1 + 3” spacetime, instead of Finsler geometry we turn to pseudo-Finsler geometry.
A pseudo-Finsler metric is said to be locally Minkowskian if at every point there is a local coordinate system, such that
F=F(y)
is independent of the position
x
.
For a massive particle propagating in 1+3 spacetime, its action can be expressed as

S=m\int F\left(x^{\mu},y^{\mu}\right)d\tau,

(9)

where
m
is the mass of the particle, and
y^{\mu}=\frac{dx^{\mu}}{d\tau}
is the 4-speed of the particle.
And thus the Lagrangian of the particle is

L=mF\left(x^{\mu},y^{\mu}\right).

(10)

In this work we focus on the geodesic equation of Finsler geometry. The geodesic equation for the Finsler manifold is given as
textbook

\frac{d^{2}x^{\mu}}{d\tau^{2}}+2G^{\mu}=0,

(11)

where

G^{\mu}=\frac{1}{4}g^{\mu\nu}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F^{2}}{\partial x^{\lambda}\partial y^{\nu}}y^{\lambda}-\frac{\partial F^{2}}{\partial x^{\nu}}\right)

(12)

is called the geodesic spray coefficient. Obviously if

is a Riemann metric, then

G^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\nu\lambda}^{\mu}y^{\nu}y^{\lambda},

(13)

where
\gamma_{\nu\lambda}^{\mu}
is the Riemann Christoffel symbol. We can also see that if
F
is locally Minkowskian, then
G^{\mu}=0
, and the geodesic equation (
11
) is actually
\frac{d^{2}x^{\mu}}{d\tau^{2}}=0
.

III
Pseudo-Finsler Structure of Particles Subject to General Forms of Lorentz Violation

To simplify the discussion, here we introduce the concept of homogeneous function
hf

f(\lambda\vec{x})=\lambda^{n}f(\vec{x}),

A slightly more general form of homogeneity is called positive homogeneity, by requiring only that the above identities hold for
\lambda>0

Euler’s homogeneous function theorem asserts that the positively homogeneous functions of degree

are exactly the solution of a specific partial differential equation

nf(\vec{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}f(\vec{x}).

(15)

We can easily see that a locally Minkowskian Finsler norm

is a 1-order homogeneous function.

According to the result of our previous work

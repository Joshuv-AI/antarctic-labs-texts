Friezes of cluster algebras of geometric type
Antoine de Saint Germain, Min Huang, Jiang-Hua Lu

Abstract
. For a cluster algebra 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} over ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} of geometric type, a frieze of 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} is defined to be a ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} -algebra homomorphism from 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} to ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} that takes positive integer values on all cluster variables and all frozen variables. We present some basic facts on friezes, including frieze testing criteria, the notion of frieze points when 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} is finitely generated, and pullbacks of friezes under certain ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} -algebra homomorphisms. When the cluster algebra 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} is acyclic, we define frieze patterns associated to acyclic seeds of 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} , generalizing the frieze patterns with coefficients of type A A studied by J. Propp and by M. Cuntz, T. Holm, and P. Jørgensen, and we give a sufficient condition for such frieze patterns to be equivalent to friezes. For the special cases when 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} has an acyclic seed with either trivial coefficients, principal coefficients, or what we call the BFZ coefficients (named after A. Berenstein, S. Fomin, and A. Zelevinsky), we identify frieze points of 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} both geometrically as certain positive integral points in explicitly described affine varieties and Lie theoretically (in the finite case) in terms of reduced double Bruhat cells and generalized minors on the associated semi-simple Lie groups. Furthermore, extending the gliding symmetry of the classical Coxeter frieze patterns of type A A , we determine the symmetry of frieze patterns of any finite type with arbitrary coefficients.

Introduction and main results

1.1.
Introduction

The notion of
frieze patterns
was first introduced by Coxeter
[
Cox71
]

and further studied by Conway and Coxeter
[
CC73a
,
CC73b
]
. Motivated by the recently discovered connections of Coxeter’s frieze patterns with cluster algebras
[
CC06
]
, Assem, Reutenauer, and Smith introduced in
[
ARS10
]
frieze patterns associated to an arbitrary symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix (called
frieze sequences
in
[
KS11
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

[
ARS10
,
\lx@sectionsign
3]

For an
r\times r
symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix
A=(a_{i,j})
,
a
frieze pattern associated to
A
is
a map
f:[1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}

satisfying

f(i,m)\,f(i,m+1)=1+\prod_{j=i+1}^{r}f(j,m)^{-a_{j,i}}\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}f(j,m+1)^{-a_{j,i}}

(1.1)

for every
(i,m)\in[1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.
Here and for the rest of the paper,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
(resp.
{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
) denotes the set of all non-negative (resp. positive) integers, and
[1,r]=\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
.

\diamond

The relations in (
1.1
) are recursive with respect to the total order on
[1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
given by

(i,m)<(i^{\prime},m^{\prime})\hskip 14.45377pt\mbox{iff}\hskip 14.45377ptm<m^{\prime}\;\;\mbox{or}\;\;m=m^{\prime}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;i<i^{\prime},

(1.2)

and a frieze pattern is uniquely determined by its initial values
\{f(i,0):i\in[1,r]\}
.
Frieze patterns associated to the standard Cartan matrix of type

are precisely Coxeter’s frieze patterns.
It was shown recently by Gunawan and Muller
[

]
that
the numbers of frieze patterns associated to Cartan matrices of finite type are finite, and their exact or conjectured numbers can be found in

[

,
FP16
]
.
Other generalizations of Coxeter’s frieze patterns include
frieze patterns with coefficients of type

by Propp
[

]
and by

[

]
(see Example

),
and
SL_{k}
-friezes for

(see
[
BFG+21
,
Cun17
]
).
We refer to
[
MG15
]
for a survey on the history and generalizations of Coxeter’s frieze patterns and their relations to other fields such as linear difference equations
and integrable systems.

Motivated by frieze patterns, the following definition has
recently emerged (see
[
Fon14
,
FP16
,
GS20
]
and more particularly
[
GM22
]
for cluster algebras with trivial coefficients).

Definition 1.2
.

Let

be a
{\mathbb{K}}
-cluster algebra of geometric type (see Definition

). A

-algebra homomorphism

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{K}}
.

\diamond

r\times r
symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix
A=(a_{i,j})
, and consider the

B_{\scriptstyle A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&-a_{1,2}&\cdots&-a_{1,r}\\
a_{2,1}&0&\cdots&-a_{2,r}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vskip 5.78172pta_{r,1}&a_{r,2}&\cdots&0\end{array}\right).

(1.3)

Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(B_{\scriptstyle A})
be the cluster algebra with trivial coefficients that has

\{u(i,0):i\in[1,r]\}

as an initial cluster and

as the initial mutation matrix (see
\lx@sectionsign

). Applying the

[

and
[

one obtains cluster variables

satisfying mutation relations (see
\lx@sectionsign

u(i,m)\,u(i,m+1)=1+\prod_{j=i+1}^{r}u(j,m)^{-a_{j,i}}\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}u(j,m+1)^{-a_{j,i}},\hskip 14.45377pt(i,m)\in[1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}.

(1.4)

Compairing (

) and (

The fact that this is indeed the case has been proved in

Definition

1.2.
Basics on friezes

We present in

\lx@sectionsign
3
some basic concepts on friezes.

The Positive Laurent Phenomenon for cluster algebras of geometric type, proved in
[
FZ03
, Proposition 11.2]
and
[
GHKK18
, Theorem 4.10]
,
ensures the existence of a special class of friezes, called

unitary friezes
, for any cluster algebra. See Lemma-Definition
3.3
.

When
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}
is finitely generated,
we consider the affine variety
V={\rm Spec}({\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}})
,
and we call
v\in V
a
frieze point
if all the extended cluster variables in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}

take positive integer values at
v
. Regarding each extended cluster of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}
as a coordinate system on
V
, frieze points are then exactly those

v\in V
that have
positive integral coordinates
in
every
such coordinate system.
Existence of non-unitary friezes (see
[
FP16
]
for examples) is thus a rather remarkable fact from this geometric point of view.

As a cluster algebra typically has infinitely many cluster variables, it is natural to ask for frieze testing criteria
using a small number of extended cluster variables, which is
desirable even when the cluster algebra is of finite type. We give in
Lemma
3.5
examples of such testing criteria which cover a
rich class of polynomial cluster algebras coming from Lie theory such as coordinate rings on Schubert cells

[
GLS11
]
and double Bott-Samelson cells
[
SW21
]
. See Example
3.7
.

For a cluster algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
of geometric type, let
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
be the unital sub-semiring of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
generated by all the extended cluster variables of

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
In
\lx@sectionsign
4
, we introduce the category
{\bf Frieze}
, whose objects are cluster algebras
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
of geometric type with
frozen variables non-invertible,
and whose morphisms are ring homomorphisms
\psi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
with
Property
{\mathfrak{F}}
, in the sense
that

\psi({\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{+})\subset({\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime})^{+}

(see Definition
4.1
). Regarding
{\mathbb{Z}}
as an object in
{\bf Frieze}
, i.e., the cluster algebra of rank
0
and with trivial coefficients,
a frieze of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is nothing but a morphism from
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
to
{\mathbb{Z}}
in
Frieze
, and friezes have well-defined pullbacks under morphisms in

{\bf Frieze}
. Our main examples of morphisms in
{\bf Frieze}
include

(1) (Lemma
4.4
) the inclusion map
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
, where
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is obtained from
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
by

freezing
a subset of cluster variables in a cluster of

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
;

(2) (Proposition
4.5
) ring homomorphisms
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
, where
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
is of finite type and is obtained from
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
by
deleting

a subset of cluster variables in a cluster of

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
;

(3) (Corollary
4.12
and Example
4.13
) certain cluster algebra quasi-homomorphisms
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
in the sense of Fraser
[
Fra16
]
;

(4) (Theorem
4.16
) all universal coefficient specializations for cluster algebras of finite type.

1.3.
Friezes and frieze patterns of acyclic cluster algebras

In
\lx@sectionsign
5
we turn our attention to friezes of
acyclic
cluster algebras
(of geometric type), i.e. cluster algebras that have at least one acyclic
seed
[
BFZ05
, Definition 1.14]
. Recall (see
[
BFZ05
, p.36]

the extended mutation matrix of an acyclic seed is, up to simultaneous re-labelling of rows and columns, of the form

\widetilde{B}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}B_{\scriptstyle A}\\

) for some generalized symmetrizable Cartan matrix

P\in M_{l,r}({\mathbb{Z}})

.
Without loss of generality, we thus fix throughout
\lx@sectionsign
5
an extended mutation matrix
(B_{\scriptstyle A}\backslash\!\!\backslash P)
and consider the seed pattern
{\mathcal{S}}(B_{\scriptstyle A}\backslash\!\!\backslash P)=\{\Sigma_{t}=({\bf u}_{t},{\bf p},\widetilde{B}_{t}):t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}\}

with
\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}}=(B_{\scriptstyle A}\backslash\!\!\backslash P)

({\mathbb{T}}_{r},t_{0})
a rooted
r
-regular tree. Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(B_{\scriptstyle A}\backslash\!\!\backslash P,\emptyset)
be the corresponding cluster algebra over
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\bf p}]
(see
\lx@sectionsign
2.1
for more detail).
Consider the

of

whose vertices are labelled

\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r-1}}{{-\!\!\!-}}t(r,\!-2)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{-\!\!\!-}}t(1,\!-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{-\!\!\!-}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r-1}}{{-\!\!\!-}}t(r,\!-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{-\!\!\!-}}t(1,\!0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{-\!\!\!-}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r-1}}{{-\!\!\!-}}t(r,\!0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{-\!\!\!-}}t(1,\!1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{-\!\!\!-}}\cdots

(see e.g.
[

\lx@sectionsign

For
(i,m)\in[1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}}

Definition 1.3
.

A
frieze pattern associated
to
A
with coefficient matrix
P
is a map

f:\;\;([1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}})\cup\{p_{1}\ldots,p_{l}\}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}

satisfying, for all

f(i,m)\,f(i,m+1)=f({\bf p})^{[p(i,m)]_{+}}+f({\bf p})^{[-p(i,m)]_{+}}\prod_{j=i+1}^{r}f(j,m)^{-a_{ji}}\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}f(j,m+1)^{-a_{ji}}.

\diamond

Let
{\mathfrak{X}}(B_{\scriptstyle A}\backslash\!\!\backslash P)
be the set of all cluster variables of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(B_{\scriptstyle A}\backslash\!\!\backslash P,\emptyset)
. We refer to the assignment

[1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{X}}(B_{\scriptstyle A}\backslash\!\!\backslash P),\;\;(i,m)\longmapsto u(i,m)

as the
generic frieze pattern
associated to
A
with coefficient matrix
P
or associated to
(B_{\scriptstyle A}\backslash\!\!\backslash P)
.
Every frieze
h:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(B_{\scriptstyle A}\backslash\!\!\backslash P,\emptyset)\to{\mathbb{Z}}
thus gives rise to a frieze pattern
f
via

f(i,m)=h(u(i,m)),\hskip 14.45377pt(i,m)\in[1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}}.

If
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(B_{\scriptstyle A}\backslash\!\!\backslash P,\emptyset)
is generated over
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\bf p}]
by a subset of

\{u(i,m):\,(i,m)\in[1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}}\}
,
for example if

is of finite type,
we show in

that the converse holds, i.e. that every frieze pattern associated to
(B_{\scriptstyle A}\backslash\!\!\backslash P)
is obtained from a frieze of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(B_{\scriptstyle A}\backslash\!\!\backslash P,\emptyset)
via evaluation.

The set of vectors
\{p(i,m):i\in[1,r],m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
is completely determined
by

. We prove in Proposition

that for every

, the map

is a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{l}
-valued
cluster-additive function associated to

in the sense of Ringel

]
, and that all

-valued cluster-additive functions associated to

In Example

, we show that the

studied by Cuntz, Holm, and Jørgensen

are special cases of Definition

, where the cluster algebra is of type

and the extended mutation matrix
(B_{\scriptstyle A}\backslash\!\!\backslash P)
comes from a shell triangulation of an

-gon.

Frieze points in examples

In

2.2.
Tropicalization

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a seed pattern of geometric type with extended rank
(r,l)
and frozen variables
{\bf p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{l})
.
Using any cluster
{\bf u}
of

{\bf u}

of the seed pattern

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a unique semi-field homomorphism

\mathbb{F}_{>0}({\mathcal{S}})\to\mathbb{P},\quad Z\mapsto Z|_{\mathbb{P}},

which sends all cluster variables of
{\mathcal{S}}
to
1\in\mathbb{P}
and
p_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{>0}({\mathcal{S}})
to
p_{j}\in\mathbb{P}
for each
j\in[1,l]
.

Proof.

We prove existence by constructing a semi-field homomorphism with the prescribed properties. Fix any cluster
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{r})
of
{\mathcal{S}}
and consider the semi-field homomorphism

\mathbb{F}_{>0}({\mathcal{S}})\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}},\;\;Z\longmapsto Z|_{{\bf u}\to{\bf 1},\mathbb{P}},

that sends
u_{i}\to 1\in\mathbb{P}
for each
i\in[1,r]
and
p_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{>0}({\bf u},{\bf p})
to
p_{j}\in{\mathbb{P}}
for each
j\in[1,l]
. Let
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
be such that
{\bf u}
is the cluster of
{\mathcal{S}}
at
t_{0}
, and for
t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
, let

\Sigma_{t}=({\bf u}_{t}=(u_{t,1},\ldots,u_{t,r}),\;{\bf p},\;\widetilde{B}_{t})

be the seed of
{\mathcal{S}}
at

. We
prove by induction on the distance of

that

t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
and

.
The claim holds for

by definition.
Suppose that the claim holds for

and consider an edge

u_{t^{\prime},k}=\frac{1}{u_{t,k}}\left({\bf u}_{t}^{[B_{t,k}]_{+}}{\bf p}^{[P_{t,k}]_{+}}+{\bf u}_{t}^{[-B_{t,k}]_{+}}{\bf p}^{[-P_{t,k}]_{+}}\right).

.

By Lemma
2.2
one has

u|_{\mathbb{P}}=1
for every
u\in{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{S}})
, which
is a stronger statement than
u
being polynomial in
{\bf p}
.

\diamond

Recall that a
cluster monomial
of
{\mathcal{S}}
is an element of
\mathbb{F}({\mathcal{S}})
of the form
{\bf u}_{t}^{\alpha}
for some
t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
and

\alpha\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})^{r}
. By Theorem
2.6
, all cluster monomials of
{\mathcal{S}}
are in

\mathcal{U}^{+}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)\subset\mathcal{U}^{+}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})
for any
{\rm inv}\subset{\bf p}
.
Recall that
{\mathcal{S}}
is said to be of
finite type
if it has finitely many cluster variables
[
FZ03
]
. The following Proposition
2.8
will
be used in
\lx@sectionsign
4.2
.

Proposition 2.8
.

If
{\mathcal{S}}
is of finite type, then for any
{\rm inv}\subset{\bf p}
, every
Z\in\mathcal{U}^{+}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})
is a unique finite linear combination of
cluster monomials with coefficients in
{\mathbb{A}}^{+}_{\rm inv}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}[{\bf p}][p_{j}^{-1}:j\in{\rm inv}]
.

Proof.

When
{\rm inv}={\bf p}
, the statement is equivalent to the fact that cluster monomials form a basis of
\mathcal{U}^{+}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}})
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}[{\bf p}^{\pm 1}]
(see, e.g.,
[
FT17
, Theorem 10.2]
or

[
Hua19
, Corollary 3.7]
).

Let
{\rm inv}\subset{\bf p}
be arbitrary, and let
Z\in\mathcal{U}^{+}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})\subset\mathcal{U}^{+}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}})
. Then
Z
is a unique
sum

Z=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}M_{i},

where each
c_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}[{\bf p}^{\pm 1}]
and
M_{i}
a cluster monomial of
{\mathcal{S}}
.
Let

be such that

. By Corollary

,
M_{i}|_{{\rm trop}(p_{j})}=1
for every
i\in[1,m]
. Thus

Z|_{{\rm trop}(p_{j})}=\oplus_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}|_{{\rm trop}(p_{j})},

where
\oplus
is the addition in the semi-field
{\rm trop}(p_{j})
.
Writing

with
\delta_{j}(Z)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
it follows from

that

.
Thus every

must be polynomial in

. Since

is arbitrary,

for every

.

Q.E.D.

3.
Friezes, frieze testing criteria, and frieze points

3.1.
Friezes

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a seed pattern of geometric type, and
recall from Definition
2.1
the
{\mathbb{K}}
-cluster algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})
associated to
{\mathcal{S}}
for any
{\rm inv}\subset{\rm Froz}({\mathcal{S}})
, where
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{Z}}
or is a field of characteristic
0
. Recall also that

\widetilde{{\mathfrak{X}}}({\mathcal{S}})
denotes the set of all extended cluster variables of
{\mathcal{S}}
. The following definition follows
[
Fon14
,
FP16
,
GS20
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

1) By a
frieze
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)
we mean any ring homomorphism

h:{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)\to{\mathbb{Z}}
that takes positive integral values on every

u\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{X}}}({\mathcal{S}})
;

2) For a field
{\mathbb{K}}
of characteristic
0
, a
frieze
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{K}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})
is any
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra homomorphism

h:{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})\to{\mathbb{K}}
that takes positive integral values on every
u\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{X}}}({\mathcal{S}})
;

3) Any frieze as in 1) or 2) is called an
{\mathcal{S}}
-frieze.

\diamond

Remark 3.2
.

It is clear from the definitions that every
{\mathcal{S}}
-frieze restricts to, and is determined by, a frieze of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)
.
The freedom of allowing arbitrary fields
{\mathbb{K}}
of characteristics
0
, especially for
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{Q}}
or
{\mathbb{C}}
, makes it easier to
consider friezes when
{\rm inv}\neq\emptyset
(see

\lx@sectionsign
3.2
) and
renders possible geometrical considerations (see
\lx@sectionsign
3.3
).

\diamond

The Positive Laurent Phenomenon for cluster algebras of geometric type in Theorem
2.6

ensures the existence of a special class of
{\mathcal{S}}
-friezes.

Lemma-Definition 3.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a seed pattern of geometric type with extended rank
(r,l)
, and let

\widetilde{{\bf u}}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r},p_{1},\ldots,p_{l})
be any extended cluster in
{\mathcal{S}}
. For any
{{\bf m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{l})\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{>0})^{l}
,
one has a unique frieze
h_{\widetilde{{\bf u}},{{\bf m}}}:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)\to{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

h_{\widetilde{{\bf u}},{{\bf m}}}(u_{i})=1,\;\;\;i\in[1,r]\hskip 14.45377pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.45377pth_{\widetilde{{\bf u}},{{\bf m}}}(p_{j})=m_{j},\,\;\;j\in[1,l].

(3.1)

Friezes obtained this way are said to be unitary.

Proof.

Let
T_{\widetilde{{\bf u}}}={\mathbb{Z}}[u_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,u_{r}^{\pm 1},p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}]
and

T_{\widetilde{{\bf u}}}^{+}={\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}[u_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,u_{r}^{\pm 1},p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}]
.
Then each
{{\bf m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{l})\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{>0})^{l}
gives rise to a

{\mathbb{Z}}
-algebra homomorphism
h_{\widetilde{{\bf u}},{{\bf m}}}:T_{\widetilde{{\bf u}}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
via (
3.1
).
By Theorem
2.6
,

\widetilde{{\mathfrak{X}}}({\mathcal{S}})\subset T_{\widetilde{{\bf u}}}^{+}
, so
h_{\widetilde{{\bf u}},{{\bf m}}}
restricts to a frieze of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)
.

Q.E.D.

3.2.
Frieze testing criteria

Let again
{\mathcal{S}}
be a seed pattern with frozen variables
{\bf p}
.

Definition 3.4
.

A subset
{\mathcal{T}}
of
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{X}}}({\mathcal{S}})

is called an
{\mathcal{S}}
-frieze testing set
if for any
{\rm inv}\subset{\bf p}
, a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra homomorphism

h:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})\to{\mathbb{Q}}
is a frieze if and only if
h(u)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
for every
u\in{\mathcal{T}}
.

\diamond

In examples, when testing an
h:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})\to{\mathbb{Q}}
using a subset
{\mathcal{T}}\subset\widetilde{{\mathfrak{X}}}({\mathcal{S}})
,
we often can modify the condition that
h(u)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
for every
u\in{\mathcal{T}}
by dividing
{\mathcal{T}}
into
{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{T}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{T}}_{2}

and imposing integrality of
h
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{1}
and positivity of
h
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{2}
.
We refer to all such conditions as
frieze testing criteria
.
We illustrate this by the following simple observation in a special case.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)
is generated over
{\mathbb{Z}}
by a subset
{\mathcal{Z}}
of
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{X}}}({\mathcal{S}})

and let

(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r+l})
be any extended cluster of
{\mathcal{S}}
. Then for any
{\rm inv}\subset{\bf p}
, a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra homomorphism

h:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})\to{\mathbb{Q}}
is a frieze if and only if the following two conditions hold:

1)
h(z)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for every
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
, and

2)
h(u_{j})>0
for every
j\in[1,r+l]
.

Proof.

Let
h:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})\to{\mathbb{Q}}
be a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra homomorphism satisfying 1) and 2).
Let
u\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{X}}}({\mathcal{S}})
.
Condition 1) implies that
h(u)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and Condition 2) implies that
h(u)>0
by the
positivity of the Laurent expression of
u
in
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r+l}
. Thus
h
is a frieze.

Q.E.D.

Remark 3.6
.

If

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)
is finitely generated as a ring, it is generated by
a finite subset of
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{X}}}({\mathcal{S}})
, and Lemma
3.5
yields a finite
{\mathcal{S}}
-frieze testing set.

\diamond

.

A particular case of Lemma
3.5
is when
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)
is the polynomial ring of a subset

\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}
of
{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{S}})
.
A systematic class of such examples comes from the theory of symmetric Poisson CGL extensions developed by K. Goodearl and M. Yakimov
[
GY17
,
GY18
]
.
For a rich class of examples from Lie theory, see
[
EL21
,
GLS11
,
GSW20
]
.

\diamond

3.3.
Frieze points in cluster varieties

For cluster algebras that are finitely generated, we take a geometrical view point on their friezes via the notion of cluster varieties.

Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional irreducible rational affine variety over
{\mathbb{C}}
, let
{\mathbb{C}}(V)
be the field of rational functions on
V
, and let

{\mathbb{C}}[V]\subset{\mathbb{C}}(V)
be the algebra of regular functions on
V
. The following definition of cluster structures on
V
follows
[
GSV10
, Proposition 3.37]
.

Definition 3.8
.

1) A
cluster structure on
V
is a seed pattern
{\mathcal{S}}
in
{\mathbb{C}}(V)
such that

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})={\mathbb{C}}[V]

for some
{\rm inv}\subset{\rm Froz}({\mathcal{S}})
.
A seed
\Sigma
in
{\mathbb{C}}(V)
is said to
define a cluster structure on
V
if it is a seed in a seed pattern that is a cluster structure on
V
;

2) For a cluster structure
{\mathcal{S}}
on
V
, by an
{\mathcal{S}}
-frieze point in
V
we mean a point
v\in V
such that all extended cluster variables in
{\mathcal{S}}

take positive integral values at
v
.

\diamond

For a cluster structure
{\mathcal{S}}
on
V
, the subset
{\rm inv}
of
{\rm Froz}({\mathcal{S}})
such that
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})={\mathbb{C}}[V]

is necessarily unique, as we now explain.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a seed pattern in
{\mathbb{C}}(V)
.

1) If there exists
{\rm inv}\subset{\rm Froz}({\mathcal{S}})
such that
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})={\mathbb{C}}[V]
, then

{\rm Froz}({\mathcal{S}})\subset{\mathbb{C}}[V]
and

{\rm inv}={\rm inv}(V)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\{p\in{\rm Froz}({\mathcal{S}}):p\;\mbox{vanishes nowhere on}\;V\}.

2) The seed pattern
{\mathcal{S}}
is a cluster structure on
V
if and only if

{\rm Froz}({\mathcal{S}})\subset{\mathbb{C}}[V]\hskip 14.45377pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.45377pt{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv}(V))={\mathbb{C}}[V].

Proof.

1) Let
{\rm inv}\subset{\rm Froz}({\mathcal{S}})
be such that
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})={\mathbb{C}}[V]
. By definition

{\rm Froz}({\mathcal{S}})\cap\{p^{-1}:p\in{\rm inv}\}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv}).

Thus
{\rm Froz}({\mathcal{S}})\subset{\mathbb{C}}[V]
and
{\rm inv}\subset{\rm inv}(V)
.
To show that
{\rm inv}(V)\subset{\rm inv}
, let

\widetilde{{\bf u}}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r},p_{1},\ldots,p_{l})

be any extended cluster in
{\mathcal{S}}
, and assume without
loss of generality that
{\rm inv}=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{a}\}
for some
1\leq a\leq l
. By the Laurent phenomenon,

{\mathbb{C}}[V]={\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv})\subset{\mathbb{C}}[u_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,u_{r}^{\pm 1},p_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,p_{a}^{\pm 1},p_{a+1},\ldots,p_{l}].

(3.2)

If
j\in[1,l]
is such that
p_{j}\in{\rm inv}(V)
, then
p_{j}^{-1}\in{\mathbb{C}}[V]
. It follows that

p_{j}\in{\rm inv}
. This shows that
{\rm inv}={\rm inv}(V)
.
2) follows trivially from 1).

Q.E.D.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a cluster structure on
V
with
{\rm Froz}({\mathcal{S}})=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}\}
, and let

{\rm inv}(V)=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{a}\}
for some
0\leq a\leq l
. For any extended cluster

\widetilde{{\bf u}}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r},p_{1},\ldots,p_{l})
in
{\mathcal{S}}
, the inclusion in (
3.2
)
gives rise to a biregular isomorphism

\rho_{\widetilde{\bf u}}:\;\;({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{r+a}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{l-a}\longrightarrow V_{\widetilde{\bf u}}:=\{v\in V:u_{1}(v)\cdots u_{r}(v)p_{1}(v)\cdots p_{a}(v)\neq 0\}\subset V,

whose inverse is given by

\widetilde{\bf u}:\;\;V_{\widetilde{\bf u}}\longrightarrow({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{r+a}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{l-a},\;\;v\longmapsto(u_{1}(v),\ldots,u_{r}(v),p_{1}(v),\ldots,p_{l}(v)).

We call the above map
\widetilde{{\bf u}}

the
{\mathcal{S}}
-cluster coordinate chart
on
V
defined by
\widetilde{{\bf u}}
.
By definition, we then have the following description of
{\mathcal{S}}
-frieze points.

Lemma 3.10
.

Given a cluster structure
{\mathcal{S}}
on
V
, the
{{\mathcal{S}}}
-frieze points in
V
are precisely all those
v\in V
that
have positive integral coordinates in every

{\mathcal{S}}
-cluster coordinate chart in
V
.

Remark 3.11
.

When
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)

is finitely generated, we can identify
{\mathcal{S}}
-friezes with

{\mathcal{S}}
-frieze points in
V={\rm Spec}({\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv}))
for any
{\rm inv}\subset{\rm Froz}({\mathcal{S}})
. This is
especially helpful if we have good geometrical models for
{\rm Spec}({\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{S}},{\rm inv}))
for some choices of

{\rm inv}
.

\diamond

4.
The category
{\bf Frieze}
and pullbacks of friezes

4.1.
Property
{\mathfrak{F}}
and the category
{\bf Frieze}

For a seed pattern

{\mathcal{S}}
of geometric type,
recall again that

\widetilde{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{S}})
denotes the set of all extended cluster variables of
{\mathcal{S}}
. Let

{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{+}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)

be the semi-ring (with
1
but without
0
) generated by
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{X}}}({\mathcal{S}})
.

Definition 4.1
.

For two seed patterns
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
of geometric type, a ring homomorphism

\psi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},\emptyset)
is said to have
Property
\mathfrak{F}
if

\psi\left(\widetilde{{\mathfrak{X}}}({\mathcal{S}})\right)\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{+}({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},\emptyset),\hskip 14.45377pt\mbox{equivalently}\hskip 14.45377pt\psi\left({\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{+}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)\right)\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{+}({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},\emptyset).

(4.1)

\diamond

Property
{\mathfrak{F}}
is clearly preserved under compositions of ring homomorphisms.

Definition 4.2
.

By
{\bf Frieze}
we mean the category, whose objects are
{\mathbb{Z}}
-cluster algebras of geometric type in which frozen variables are not invertible,
and whose morphisms are ring homomorphisms with Property
{\mathfrak{F}}
.

\diamond

We also regard
{\mathbb{Z}}
as an object in

{\bf Frieze}
, i.e., the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-cluster algebra of rank
0
with trivial coefficients.
A frieze as in Definition
3.1
is nothing but a morphism to
{\mathbb{Z}}
in the category
{\bf Frieze}
.
For two objects
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
and

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
in

{\bf Frieze}
, a morphism from
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
to
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
in
{\bf Frieze}
can then be regarded as an
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
-valued frieze of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We make the following explicit statement for future reference.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\psi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},\emptyset)
is a ring homomorphism
with Property
{\mathfrak{F}}
,
then for every frieze

h:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},\,\emptyset)\to{\mathbb{Z}}
, the composition

h\circ\psi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\,\emptyset)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}

is again a frieze.

In the context of Lemma
4.3
, we will call
h\circ\psi
the

pullback of the frieze
h
by
\psi
.

Recall that
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{S}})
denotes the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-cluster algebra defined by
{\mathcal{S}}
with all frozen variables invertible.
Extending Definition
4.1
, we say that a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra homomorphism

\psi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{S}})\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime})

has
Property
\mathfrak{F}
if it induces a ring homomorphism
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},\emptyset)

with Property
\mathfrak{F}
.
We now give examples of ring (algebra) homomorphisms between cluster algebras that have Property
{\mathfrak{F}}
.

4.2.
Freezing and deletion of cluster variables

Consider a seed pattern

{\mathcal{S}}=\{\Sigma_{t}=({\bf u}_{t}=(u_{t,1},\ldots,u_{t,r}),\,{\bf p},\,\widetilde{B}_{t}):t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}\}

of geometric type with extended rank
(r,l)
. Fix
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}

and write
{\bf u}_{t_{0}}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})
.

For an integer
0\leq s<r
, consider first a seed pattern

{\mathcal{S}}^{\scriptstyle\diamond}=\{\Sigma^{\scriptstyle\diamond}_{t}=({\bf u}^{\scriptstyle\diamond}_{t},\,{\bf p}^{\scriptstyle\diamond},\,\widetilde{B}^{\scriptstyle\diamond}_{t}):t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{s}\}

(4.2)

of extended rank
(s,\,r-s+l)
obtained by
freezing

r-s
cluster variables of
\Sigma_{t_{0}}
, which, for simplicity, are assumed to be

u_{s+1},\ldots,u_{r}
. More precisely, identifying
{\mathbb{T}}_{s}
with the sub-graph of

{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
generated by
t_{0}
and all the edges labeled by elements in
[1,s]
, we have
\mathbb{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\scriptstyle\diamond})=\mathbb{F}({\mathcal{S}})={\mathbb{Q}}({\bf u}_{t_{0}},{\bf p})
,

{\bf u}^{\scriptstyle\diamond}_{t_{0}}=(u_{1},\,\ldots,\,u_{s}),\hskip 14.45377pt{\bf p}^{\scriptstyle\diamond}=(u_{s+1},\,\ldots,\,u_{r},\,p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}),

and
\widetilde{B}^{\scriptstyle\diamond}_{t_{0}}
is obtained from
\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}}
by deleting the
columns
of
\widetilde{{B}}_{t_{0}}

Then
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{X}}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\scriptstyle\diamond})\subset\widetilde{{\mathfrak{X}}}({\mathcal{S}})
and

is a sub-ring of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)
. Consequently, we have

Lemma 4.4
.

With notation as above, the inclusion

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\scriptstyle\diamond},\emptyset)\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)

has Property
{\mathfrak{F}}
.

Again for an integer
0\leq s<r
, we consider next the seed pattern

{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}=\{\Sigma^{\dagger}_{t}=({\bf u}^{\dagger}_{t},\,{\bf p}^{\dagger},\,\widetilde{B}^{\dagger}_{t}):t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{s}\}

of extended rank
(s,l)
obtained by
deleting

r-s
cluster variables of
\Sigma_{t_{0}}
, again assumed to be

u_{s+1},\ldots,u_{r}
. More precisely, the mutation matrix
\widetilde{B}^{\dagger}_{t_{0}}
is obtained from

\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}}
by deleting the
rows and columns
of
\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}}
indexed by
j\in[s+1,r]
, and we write the
extended cluster of
\Sigma^{\dagger}_{t_{0}}
as

{\bf u}^{\dagger}_{t_{0}}=(u^{\dagger}_{1},\,\ldots,\,u^{\dagger}_{s}),\hskip 14.45377pt{\bf p}^{\dagger}=(p^{\dagger}_{1},\,\ldots,\,p^{\dagger}_{l}).

Let
\psi:{\mathbb{Z}}[u_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,u_{r}^{\pm 1},p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}]\to{\mathbb{Z}}[(u_{1}^{\dagger})^{\pm 1},\ldots,(u_{s}^{\dagger})^{\pm 1},p_{1}^{\dagger},\ldots,p_{l}^{\dagger}]
be the ring homomorphism
given by

\psi(u_{i})=u^{\dagger}_{i},\hskip 14.45377pt\psi(u_{j})=1,\hskip 14.45377pt\psi(p_{j^{\prime}})=p^{\dagger}_{j^{\prime}},\hskip 14.45377pti\in[1,s],\,j\in[s+1,r],\,j^{\prime}\in[1,l].

Let
{\mathcal{S}}^{\scriptstyle\diamond}
be the seed pattern obtained from
{\mathcal{S}}
by freezing the variables
u_{s+1},\ldots,u_{r}
as in (
4.2
).
Then
\psi
restricts to a ring homomorphism

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\scriptstyle\diamond},\emptyset)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger},\emptyset)

which induces a bijection
from
{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\scriptstyle\diamond})
to
{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger})

In particular, if
{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}
is of finite type, so is
{\mathcal{S}}^{\scriptstyle\diamond}
. The following proposition
is adapted from
[
GM22
, Proposition 2.9]
to include cluster algebras with coefficients.

Proposition 4.5
.

With notation as above, if
{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}
is of finite type, then the ring homomorphism

\psi:{\mathbb{Z}}[u_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,u_{r}^{\pm 1},p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}]\to{\mathbb{Z}}[(u_{1}^{\dagger})^{\pm 1},\ldots,(u_{s}^{\dagger})^{\pm 1},p_{1}^{\dagger},\ldots,p_{l}^{\dagger}]

restricts to a ring homomorphism

\psi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}},\emptyset)\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger},\emptyset)
that has Property
{\mathfrak{F}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\psi({\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{S}}))\subset{\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger},\emptyset)
.

Consider again the seed pattern
{\mathcal{S}}^{\scriptstyle\diamond}
in
\mathbb{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\scriptstyle\diamond})=\mathbb{F}({\mathcal{S}})
given in (
4.2
) and let

{\rm inv}^{\scriptstyle\diamond}=(u_{s+1},\ldots,u_{r})\subset{\bf p}^{\scriptstyle\diamond}=(u_{s+1},\ldots,u_{r},p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}).

In the notation of
\lx@sectionsign
2.3
, we then have

\mathcal{U}^{+}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\scriptstyle\diamond},{\rm inv}^{\scriptstyle\diamond})=\bigcap_{t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{s}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}[{\bf u}_{t}^{\pm 1},{\bf p}].

By the positive
Laurent phenomenon for seed patterns of geometric type in Theorem
2.6
,

{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{S}})\subset\bigcap_{t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}[{\bf u}_{t}^{\pm 1},{\bf p}]\subset\mathcal{U}^{+}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\scriptstyle\diamond},{\rm inv}^{\scriptstyle\diamond}).

By Proposition
2.8
applied to
\mathcal{U}^{+}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\scriptstyle\diamond},{\rm inv}^{\scriptstyle\diamond})
, every

is a unique sum

u=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}M_{i}^{\scriptstyle\diamond},

where each
c_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}[u_{s+1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,u_{r}^{\pm 1},{\bf p}]
,
c_{i}\neq 0
, and
M_{i}^{\scriptstyle\diamond}
a cluster monomial of
{\mathcal{S}}^{\scriptstyle\diamond}
.
Thus

\psi(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\psi(c_{i})\psi(M_{i}^{\scriptstyle\diamond})\in{\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger},\emptyset).

Q.E.D.

4.3.
Homomorphisms of coefficient rescaling type

For an arbitrary seed pattern
{\mathcal{S}}
of geometric type with extended rank
(r,l)
and frozen variables
{\bf p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{l})
,
we say that two non-zero elements
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}({\mathcal{S}})
are
proportional
if
there exists
v\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{l}
such that
z=z^{\prime}{\bf p}^{v}
.

Consider now
two seed patterns
{\mathcal{S}}
and
\overline{{\mathcal{S}}}

of geometric type with respective extended ranks
(r,l)
and
(r,\overline{l})
and frozen variables
{\bf p}
and
\overline{{\bf p}}
.
Recall again that
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{S}})
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\overline{{\mathcal{S}}})
are the respective
cluster algebras in which all frozen variables are invertible.

Definition 4.7
.

A
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra homomorphism
\psi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{S}})\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\overline{{\mathcal{S}}})
is said to be of
coefficient rescaling type
if there
exists
E\in M_{\overline{l},l}({\mathbb{Z}})
such that
\psi({\bf p})=\overline{{\bf p}}^{E}
, and if for every
u\in{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{S}})
, there exists
\overline{u}\in{\mathfrak{X}}({\overline{{\mathcal{S}}}})
such that

\psi(u)
and
\overline{u}
are proportional.
In such a case, for every

u\in{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{S}})
, the cluster variable
\overline{u}\in{\mathfrak{X}}({\overline{{\mathcal{S}}}})
is uniquely determined by
u
and

\psi
via

\overline{u}=\frac{\psi(u)}{\psi(u)|_{\overline{\mathbb{P}}}}.

Consequently one has the well-defined map

{\mathfrak{X}}_{\psi}:\;\;{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{S}})\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{X}}(\overline{{\mathcal{S}}}),\;\;u\longmapsto\overline{u}.

For

u\in{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{S}})
, the unique
R_{u}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\overline{l}}
such that

\psi(u)|_{\overline{\mathbb{P}}}=\overline{{\bf p}}^{R_{u}}

is called the
coefficient rescaling vector
of
u
. We say that
\psi
rescales
u

trivially
if
R_{u}=0
.

\diamond

Recall that for an integral matrix
M
, we write
M\geq 0
if all of its entries are non-negative.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\psi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{S}})\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\overline{{\mathcal{S}}})
be a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra homomorphism of coefficient rescaling type such that

\psi({\bf p})=\overline{{\bf p}}^{E}
, where
E\in M_{\overline{l},l}({\mathbb{Z}})
.

1)
\psi
has Property
{\mathfrak{F}}

if and only if
E\geq 0
and
R_{u}\geq 0
for every
u\in{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{S}})
;

2) If
E\geq 0
and if there exists a cluster
{\bf u}
of
{\mathcal{S}}
such that
R_{u}=0
for every cluster variable in
{\bf u}
,
then
R_{u}\geq 0
for every
u\in{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{S}})
, and thus
\psi
has Property
{\mathfrak{F}}
.

Proof.

1) follows from the definitions.
To prove 2),
write
\psi({\bf u})=\overline{{\bf u}}
(not assuming that
\overline{{\bf u}}
is a cluster in
\overline{{\mathcal{S}}}
).
Let
u\in{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{S}})
be arbitrary and write

u=U({\bf u},{\bf p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}[{\bf u}^{\pm 1},{\bf p}]
. Then

\psi(u)=U(\overline{{\bf u}},\overline{{\bf p}}^{E}).

Since
E\geq 0
,
\psi(u)
is polynomial in
\overline{{\bf p}}
. As
\overline{{\bf p}}^{R_{u}}=\psi(u)|_{\overline{\mathbb{P}}}
, we have

R_{u}\geq 0
.

Q.E.D.

(4.5)

\diamond

of

Proposition 4.10

1) For any

Proof.

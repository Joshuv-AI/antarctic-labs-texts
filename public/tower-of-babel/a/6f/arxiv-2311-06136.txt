On polynomials of small range sum
Gergely Kiss, Ádám Markó, Zoltán Lóránt Nagy, Gábor Somlai

Abstract
In order to reprove an old result of Rédei’s on the number of directions determined by a set of cardinality p p in 𝔽 p 2 \mathbb{F}_{p}^{2} , Somlai proved that the non-constant polynomials over the field 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} whose range sums are equal to p p are of degree at least p − 1 2 \frac{p-1}{2} . Here the summand in the range sum are considered as integers from the interval [ 0 , p − 1 ] [0,p-1] . In this paper we characterise all of these polynomials having degree exactly p − 1 2 \frac{p-1}{2} , if p p is large enough. As a consequence, for the same set of primes we re-establish the characterisation of sets with few determined directions due to Lovász and Schrijver using discrete Fourier analysis.



1
Introduction

Given a set
S
of
q
points in the affine plane
\mathrm{AG}(2,q)
, where
q
is a prime power. The classical direction problem, investigated first by Rédei
[
13
]
, aims to determine the possible size of the direction set of
S
, defined as
D=\{(a_{i}-a_{j})/(b_{i}-b_{j}):(a_{i},b_{i}),(a_{j},b_{j})\in S\times S,~i\neq j\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}\cup\{\infty\}
.
Rédei
[
13
]
proved that if
S
is a set of size
p
in the affine plane
\mathrm{AG}(2,p)
, where
p
is a prime, then
S
is either a line or it determines at least
\frac{p+1}{2}
directions. This result was extended by Megyesi excluding

\frac{p+1}{2}
, namely a set of size
p
is either a line or determines at least

\frac{p+3}{2}
directions.
The proofs rely on the usage of Rédei’s polynomials, which have become a useful tool in finite geometry.
Later, the exact same result was obtained by Dress, Klin and Muzychuk
[
3
]
who exploited this fact to prove an old theorem of Burnside describing transitive permutation groups of degree
p
.

Sets determining exactly
\frac{p+3}{2}
directions exist and they were explicitly described by Lovász and Schrijver
[
12
]
. They proved that up to an affine transformation there is a unique set of this sort.
Gács
[
5
]
proved that there is another gap in the number of directions so a set of size
p
in
\mathrm{AG}(2,p)
is either a line or the set determined by Lovász and Schrijver or determines at least
\lfloor 2\frac{p-1}{3}\rfloor+1
directions.

A new method to handle this set of problems arises in
[
14
]
, where projection polynomials (defined in the next section) are used.
The concept of projection polynomials appeared in a paper of Kiss and Somlai
[
8
]
.
The idea of identifying the elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with integers is also used in a similar context in a paper of De Beule, Demeyer, Mattheus, and Sziklai
[
2
]
, where
lifting functions were introduced to treat polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
as functions from
\mathbb{F}_{p}
to
\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}\subset\mathbb{N}
.
This approach connects the direction problem to a natural general problem.

Problem 1.1
.

Given a multiset
M
of size
p
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, determine the minimum degree of a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with range
M
.

Recall that over any finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, any
function can be uniquely represented by a polynomial of degree at most
q-1
, as
x^{q}=x
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The degree of such a polynomial is called the reduced degree of the polynomial (function). Thus for
q=p
prime, the minimum (reduced) degree of a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with range
M
is at most
p-1
.
It is easy to observe on the other hand (see also Lemma
2.1
in Section 2) that the reduced degree of a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
must be
p-1
unless the sum of the elements in the range is divisible by
p
.
Thus in terms of the sum of the elements, the first interesting question to study is when the sum of the elements, considered as elements from
[0,p-1]
, is equal to
p
. Throughout the paper, we use the term
range sum
for the sum of the admitted values of a polynomial with multiplicity, evaluated over all elements of a given prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, where the admitted values are considered as elements from
[0,p-1]\subset\mathbb{Z}
, i.e.,
P:=\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
.
It was proved in
[
14
]
that if the range sum of a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is
p
, then either the polynomial is the constant
1
or its degree is at least
\frac{p-1}{2}
.

Notice that there are polynomials of degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
whose value sum is
p
.
The uniqueness of these polynomials is also asked in
[
14
]
. We answer this question by proving some sort of uniqueness.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
p>7408848
is a prime.
Up to affine transformations (
x\to ax+b
, where
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
a\neq 0
), there are two polynomials
P
of degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}P(x)=p
. These are the following.

(i)

x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1
,

(ii)

\frac{p+1}{2}\left(x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1\right)
.

For sake of completeness, the set of polynomials satisfying the conditions of the theorem are the following.

(i)

\pm(x-a)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1
,

(ii)

\frac{p+1}{2}\left(\pm(x-a)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1\right),

where
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

We believe that the assumption on prime
p
is only a technicality, and the result stands without it.

For further results on the connection between the multiset
M
and the degree of the corresponding polynomial we refer to
[
1
]
(for the case when
M
consists of
0
s and
1
s),
[
6
]
(for the case when the degree is at least
p-2
) and
[
11
]
(for the case when the degree is at least
p-t
for some
t>p/2
).

Next we highlight the connection between the degrees of polynomials and the number of determined directions.
The following proposition was proved in
[
8
]
.

Proposition 1.3
.

If the projection polynomial of a set
S\subset\mathrm{AG}(2,p)
is of degree
d
, then
S
determines at least
d+2
directions if
p-2\geq d\geq 1
.

The proof of this result uses Rédei’s polynomial as a core tool. Since the investigation of this paper heavily relies on Proposition
1.3
we cannot avoid the usage of the original approach. As it is observed in
[
14
]
, by combining the result on the sum of values in
[
14
]
and the one on the degrees of the projection polynomials and the number of directions in
[
8
]
we obtain a new proof for the Rédei-Megyesi result on the number of directions determined by a set of size
p
in
\mathrm{AG}(2,p)
.

Notice that the special case of
p
-element sets of the result in
[
8
]
, mentioned in the previous paragraph, can be derived from an earlier result of Gács
[
4
]
. The projection polynomial in direction
m
of a subset
S=\{(a_{i},b_{i})\colon i=0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
can be written as follows:

F_{m}(t):=p-\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(b_{i}-ma_{i}-t)^{p-1}.

The coefficient of
t^{j}
is equal to
(-1)^{j}\binom{p-1}{j}\sum_{i}(b_{i}-ma_{i})^{p-1-j}
.

Putting
a_{i}=x
and
b_{i}=f(x)
we
define
the polynomials
g_{a}
(
1\leq a\leq p-1
) as in
[
4
, Lemma 2.4]
, i.e.,

g_{j}(m):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}(f(x)+mx)^{j}.

Clearly
\deg(g_{j})\leq j-1
, since the leading coefficient cancels out in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let
D
denote the number of directions determined by the set
S
. If
-m
is a not determined direction of
S
, then
f(x)+mx
is a bijection and hence the polynomials
g_{j}(m)=\sum_{u\in F_{p}}u^{a}=0
. Thus if
1\leq j\leq p-D
, then
g_{j}
has more root then its degree, so
g_{j}
is identically zero. Hence
\deg F_{m}\leq(p-1)-(p-D+1)=D-2
, which implies Proposition
1.3
for sets of size
p
.

Similar arguments were applied in
[
7
]
concerning the direction problem when the order of the field is
p^{2}
.

It is natural to hope that the characterisation of polynomials of Theorem
1.2
would also give us back the descriptive result of Lovász and Schrijver
[
12
]
for large enough primes, and indeed as a Fourier analytic application of Theorem
1.2
we obtain a new proof for the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume
p>9
is a prime which satisfies Theorem
1.2
. Then up to an affine transformation, there is a unique set in
\mathrm{AG}(2,p)
of cardinality
p
determining exactly
\frac{p+3}{2}
directions. All of these sets are affine equivalent to

\{(0,x):x^{(p-1)/2}=1\}\cup\{(x,0):x^{(p-1)/2}=1\}\cup\{(0,0)\}.

The previous presentation is borrowed from Szőnyi
[
17
]
due to its simplicity. Let us remark that the above result was reproved and extended by Gács
[
4
]
.
As an additional benefit we provide an explicit description of the Lovász-Schrijver construction based on projection polynomials listed in Theorem
1.2
.

At the end of this section, we outline the proof of our results while presenting the organisation of the paper.
In Section 2, we summarize the basic facts concerning polynomials over finite fields and set the main notation of the paper. The core of the proof of our main result, Theorem
1.2
, is carried out in Section 3.
First we show that polynomials of degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
with range sum
p
are fully reducible and have distinct roots. Then we prove a list of identities involving the leading coefficient (
\mathrm{LC}
, for brief) of the polynomial, the roots and the elements of the range. Using affine transformation, we may suppose that the
\mathrm{LC}
is positive and is at most
\frac{p-1}{2}
. From the identities, we will deduce that the leading coefficient cannot take a value greater than
1
and smaller than
\frac{p-1}{2}
. Here we distinguish between three cases depending on the value of the
\mathrm{LC}
, and use several tricks beside a deeper result which is related to the Hasse-Weil bound. At the end of this section, we handle the cases
\mathrm{LC}(P)=1
and
\mathrm{LC}(P)=\frac{p-1}{2}
separately and show the uniqueness of the polynomial in both cases, up to affine transformation.
Next we present the proof of the aforementioned result concerning the sum of Legendre symbols evaluated on translates of a set in Section 4. This might be of independent interest as well. Finally, for large enough primes we derive the Lovász-Schrijver theorem from our result in Section 5 and pose some open problems in Section 6.

2
Basic ideas and notation

Let
p
denote a prime number.
A multiset
M
of size
p
is called the range of a polynomial
P
if
M=\{P(x):x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
as a multiset. In this case, the elements of the range of
P
are identified with the corresponding representatives from
\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}\subset\mathbb{Z}
.

For general finite fields
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
q
being a prime power, any function over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can uniquely be represented by a reduced degree polynomial
P(x)
of degree at most
q-1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
P(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}a_{k}x^{k}
is a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}x^{t}P(x)=-a_{q-t-1}

holds over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, for any
t\in[0,q-1].

Proof.

Observe that
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}x^{t}=0
if
0\leq t\leq q-2
and
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}x^{q-1}=-1
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Suppose that
P
is a reduced degree polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
the degree of
P
is smaller than
q-t
iff
\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}x^{d}P(x)=0
holds over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, for all
0\leq d\leq t-1
.

To put it differently, for every non-negative integer
\gamma
we have

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}x^{\gamma}P(x)\neq 0\Rightarrow\deg P\geq q-1-\gamma.

and

\deg P\geq q-1-\gamma\Rightarrow\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}x^{\delta}P(x)\neq 0\mbox{ \ for \ some \ }0\leq\delta\leq\gamma.

For a pair of sets
A
and
B
,
A+B
denotes the Minkowski-sum of the sets, i.e.,
x\in A+B
iff
x
is of form
a+b
for some
a\in A
and
b\in B
. If
B=\{b\}
is a singleton, we might omit the brackets for brevity.

Let
S
be a set in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
which does not contain a line, and let
m
be an element of
\mathbb{F}_{p}\cup\infty
. Then the function
f_{m}
is defined as follows. Let
f_{m}(i)
be the number of elements of
S
on the line
y=mx+i
. If
m=\infty
, then we take the line as
x=i
. Now
f_{m}
can be viewed as a function from
\mathbb{F}_{p}
to
\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
. Thus we obtain a polynomial which we call the
m
’th projection polynomial.

3
Proof of the main result

Let us assume that
P
is a polynomial whose range sum is
p
. Recall that range sum refers to the sum of the admitted values of the polynomial
P
with multiplicity, evaluated over all elements of a given prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, where the admitted values are considered as elements from
\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
. In sequel we denote this by
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}P(x)=p
.
We have already seen that
P
is either at least of degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
or constant. The main goal of this section is to characterise those
P
that are of degree exactly
\frac{p-1}{2}
. As a consequence of this section we establish the description of the polynomials given in Theorem
1.2
.

3.1
Roots of a polynomial of degree
\frac{p-1}{2}

The main purpose of this section is to prove that
P
is fully reducible.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
P
be a polynomial of degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
with
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}P(x)=p
. Then
P
is fully reducible. Moreover,
P
has
\frac{p-1}{2}
different roots.

For a function
P
, we denote by
supp(P)
the support of
P
.
In order to prove Proposition
3.1
we prove the following.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}P(x)=p
and
|\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}:P(y)=0\}|\leq i<p-1
. Then
\deg P\geq p-1-i
. In another words, the conditions imply
\deg P\geq|supp(P)|-1
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume
P(0)=0
.
Let
0=a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{j}
(
j\leq i
) denote the roots of
P
. Since the sum of the values of
P
is
p
we can see that if
j>1
, then there is
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
P(x)>1
. Now we build a multiset
S
of the elements of

\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The weight of
S
at
x
is
P(x)-1
if
P(x)\geq 1
and
0
otherwise.
The total weight of
S
is clearly
j
.

Let us calculate
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}x^{k}P(x)
, where
k\leq j
. We will compare this sum with

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}x^{k}
, which is congruent to zero except if
k=p-1
.

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}x^{k}P(x)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}x^{k}-\sum_{l=1}^{j}a_{l}^{k}+\sum_{b\in S}b^{k},

(1)

where the numbers appearing in
S
are counted with multiplicity
in the last sum. Instead we use
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{j}
, where
b_{l}
’s are not necessarily different.

Assume now indirectly that
P
is of degree less than
p-1-j
. Then we have
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}x^{k}P(x)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for
k\leq j
. By equation (
1
) we have
\sum_{l=1}^{j}a_{l}^{k}\equiv\sum_{l=1}^{j}b_{l}^{k}\pmod{p}
if
k\leq j
since
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}x^{k}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for
k\leq j<p-1
.

Then by Newton’s identities we have that the elementary symmetric polynomials of both
a_{l}
’s and
b_{l}
’s are the same. Since
a_{1}=0
we have
\sigma_{j}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{j})=0
. This implies that
\sigma_{j}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{j})=0
. Then
b_{l^{\prime}}=0
for some
1\leq l^{\prime}\leq j
. This leads to a contradiction since
P(b_{l^{\prime}})>1
while
P(0)=0
. Notice that the fact that the symmetric polynomials of
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
are the same implies that these numbers are the same. ∎

In particular the argument presented below shows that the proof of Proposition
3.2
essentially becomes trivial when
j\geq\frac{p-1}{2}
.

As a corollary of Proposition
3.2
we obtain a proof for Proposition
3.1
.

3.2
Identities for roots and values with multiplicities

We denote by
c
the leading coefficient of
P
. Let
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{\frac{p-1}{2}}
denote the roots of
P
and as in the proof of Proposition
3.2
let
\{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k}\}
be a multiset, where the weight of
x\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
is
P(x)-1
if
P(x)\geq 1
and
0
otherwise. Since
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}P(x)=p
we have
k=\frac{p-1}{2}
.

It follows from equation (
1
) that

\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}a_{l}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\equiv\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}b_{l}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+c\pmod{p}

(2)

Clearly
\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)\equiv a^{\frac{p-1}{2}}
for every
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Thus we obtain.

\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{a_{l}}{p}\right)\equiv\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{b_{l}}{p}\right)+c\pmod{p}

(3)

It is clear that
-\frac{p-1}{2}\leq\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{x_{l}}{p}\right)\leq\frac{p-1}{2}
for every
(x_{l})_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}
.
Thus we obtain that

\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{a_{l}}{p}\right)=\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{b_{l}}{p}\right)+r,

(4)

where
r=c
or
r=c-p
.

Using a suitable change of variables (
x\to x-\gamma
,
r\to r_{\gamma}
) of the polynomial
P
we obtain

S_{\gamma}:=\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{a_{l}-\gamma}{p}\right)=\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{b_{l}-\gamma}{p}\right)+r_{\gamma}.

(5)

The following is just a technical statement.

Lemma 3.3
.

\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\left(\frac{x_{l}-\gamma}{p}\right)=0
for every
(x_{l})_{l=1}^{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
.

Proof.

Since half of the nonzero elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
are quadratic residues we have

\sum_{\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{\delta}{p}\right)=0
. Then changing the order of the summations gives the result.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
r_{\gamma}
denote the number defined in equation (
5
). Then

•

m_{c-p}:=|\left\{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\colon r_{\gamma}=c-p\right\}|=c
,

•

m_{c}:=|\left\{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\colon r_{\gamma}=c\right\}|=p-c
.

Proof.

Lemma
3.3
gives that
m_{c}c+m_{c-p}(c-p)=0
. The result follows from
m_{c}+m_{c-p}=p
.
∎

3.3
Constraints on the leading coefficient

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
3.5
.

Let
k
denote an absolute constant that satisfies

\#\bigg\{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\colon\bigg\lvert\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{a_{l}-\gamma}{p}\right)\bigg\lvert\geq\frac{p}{7}\bigg\}\leq k.

(6)

The framework of
Proposition
4.6
guarantees that for any fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
inequality (
6
) holds if
p>p_{0}(k)
for some
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
depending only on
k
. For our purpose
k=16
is suitable, when
p_{0}=7408848
, but the following arguments work for any fixed
k\geq 16
and large enough
p
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
p>p_{0}
and
P
be a polynomial of degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
with
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}P(x)=p
. Then
\mathrm{LC}(P)=1,p-1,\frac{p-1}{2}
or
\frac{p+1}{2}
.

We will prove this theorem using a case by case analysis.
Let us first observe that if
\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)=-1
, then
\mathrm{LC}(P(ax))=p-\mathrm{LC}(P(x))

since the degree of
P
is equal to
\frac{p-1}{2}
. Thus we may assume
1\leq\mathrm{LC}(P)\leq\frac{p-1}{2}
.
From now on, the leading coefficient of
P
will be denoted by
c
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\frac{p-1}{2}>c>\frac{p-1}{2}-\frac{p}{7}
.
If
p>p_{0}
, then
\mathrm{LC}(P)\neq c
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma^{+}
denote the elements
\gamma
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that

\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\bigg(\frac{a_{l}-\gamma}{p}\bigg)>-\frac{p}{7}~~\mbox{ and }~r_{\gamma}=c-p.

It does follow from Corollary
3.4
and Proposition
4.6
that

c-k\leq\lvert\Gamma^{+}\lvert\leq c.

For every
\gamma\in\Gamma^{+}
we have that
\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{a_{l}-\gamma}{p}\right)=\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{b_{l}-\gamma}{p}\right)+(c-p)
. Using

\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{a_{l}-\gamma}{p}\right)\geq-\frac{p}{7}

we obtain

\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\bigg(\frac{b_{l}-\gamma}{p}\bigg)\geq\frac{p+1}{2}-\frac{p}{7}>\frac{5}{14}p.

(7)

This means

\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{+}}\left(\frac{b_{l}-\gamma}{p}\right)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{+}}\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\bigg(\frac{b_{l}-\gamma}{p}\bigg)\geq\frac{5}{14}\lvert\Gamma^{+}\lvert p.

(8)

Let
b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
for which
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{+}}\left(\frac{b^{\prime}-\gamma}{p}\right)

is maximal.
Let
t\in\mathbb{Q}
such that

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{+}}\bigg(\frac{b^{\prime}-\gamma}{p}\bigg)=\frac{p}{4}+t.

(9)

We claim that for every
b\neq b^{\prime}

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{+}}\bigg(\frac{b-\gamma}{p}\bigg)\leq\frac{p}{4}-t+3.

(10)

Indeed, let
g:=|\Gamma^{+}|
and let
g_{+}
and
g_{-}
denote the number of elements
\gamma
of
\Gamma^{+}
such that
\left(\frac{b^{\prime}-\gamma}{p}\right)=1
and

3.4
Uniqueness

In the previous subsections we proved that if

4
Intersections of translates of the set of quadratic residues

In this section we prove the previously used combinatorial statement that
if, generally speaking, a system of sets over a given ground set is given which has a relatively rigid intersection pattern, meaning that their
k
-wise intersections are bounded from above via their sizes, then for any large enough set over the ground set we can find a representative from the set system which intersects it in a small subset.

This idea will be applied to a system of translates of the quadratic residues over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
We begin with a lemma that is used several times in the previous sections. The lemma relies on the knowledge of the number of solutions of any quadratic equations, which in particular,
gives the parameters
(p,\frac{p-1}{2},\frac{p-5}{4},\frac{p-1}{4})

p\equiv 1\pmod{4}

Let

is a non-square and
\omega(0)=0.

The lemma below is due to Szőnyi and is proven via the estimation of sums of multiplicative characters.

Lemma 4.2

(Szőnyi,
[
16
]
)
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be polynomials for which no partial product of form
\prod\limits_{i\in I,I\subseteq[1,m]}f_{i}(x)
can be written as a
constant multiple of a square of a polynomial.

Suppose that
2^{m-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\deg(f_{i})<{\sqrt{q}-1}
. Let
\textbf{v}\in\{+1,-1\}^{m}
and
N(\textbf{v})
denote the number of elements
y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
for which
\omega(f_{i}(y))=v_{i}
for every
i=1,\ldots,m
. Then

|N(\textbf{v})-\frac{q}{2^{m}}|<\sum_{i=1}^{m}\deg(f_{i})\frac{\sqrt{q}+1}{2}.

We apply the result to linear polynomials
f_{i}(x)=x+r_{i}
. Let
Q
denote the non-zero square elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then the lemma states that we can estimate not only the intersection of translates
\bigcap_{i}(Q-r_{i})
but we can take the complement of each translate independently from each other.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
A_{i}
be the square elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
translated by pairwise distinct
r_{i}
for
i=1\ldots t
, where
t<0.5\log p
. Then

\left|\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigcap_{j\in\overline{I}}\overline{A_{j}}\right|<\frac{p}{2^{t}}+t\frac{\sqrt{p}+1}{2}.

Proof.

Direct consequence of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Note that the former statement is suggested as an Exercise in the handbook of Lidl and Niederreiter
[
10
]
, see Ex. 5.64.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{t}
and
B
be finite sets. Then

\min\limits_{i\in[t]}|A_{i}\cap B|\leq\frac{1}{t}\left(\sum\limits_{r\geq\hat{r}}\left|\bigcup\limits_{|I|\geq{r}}\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigcap_{j\in\overline{I}}\overline{A_{j}}\right|+(\hat{r}-1)|B|\right),

provided that

\left|\bigcup\limits_{|I|\geq\hat{r}}\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigcap_{j\in\overline{I}}\overline{A_{j}}\right|\leq|B|.

Proof.

We apply the identity below,

\sum_{i=1}^{t}|A_{i}\cap B|=\sum\limits_{I\subseteq[t]}\left|\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigcap_{j\in\overline{I}}\overline{A_{j}}\cap B\right|\cdot|I|

which relies on the fact that each of

\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigcap_{j\in\overline{I}}\overline{A_{j}}\cap B

appears
I
times in the sum of the left hand side, notable exactly for all
A_{i}\cap B
where
i\in I
; while

A_{k}=\bigcup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\ni k\\
I\subseteq[t]\end{subarray}}\bigcap\limits_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigcap\limits_{j\in\overline{I}}\overline{A_{j}}.

In order to bound the sum from above, we introduce another summation.

\sum_{i=1}^{t}|A_{i}\cap B|=\sum\limits_{I\subseteq[t]}\left|\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigcap_{j\in\overline{I}}\overline{A_{j}}\cap B\right|\cdot|I|=\sum_{r=1}^{t}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|\geq r\\
{I\subseteq[t]}\end{subarray}}\left|\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigcap_{j\in\overline{I}}\overline{A_{j}}\cap B\right|

Now we use the simple bound
|H\cap B|\leq\min\{|H|,|B|\}
for
H=\bigcup\limits_{|I|\geq r}\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigcap_{j\in\overline{I}}\overline{A_{j}}
for every
r
.

Suppose that

|\bigcup\limits_{|I|\geq\hat{r}}\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigcap_{j\in\overline{I}}\overline{A_{j}}|\leq|B|\leq|\bigcup\limits_{|I|\geq\hat{r}-1}\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigcap_{j\in\overline{I}}\overline{A_{j}}|

Then

\sum_{i=1}^{t}|A_{i}\cap B|\leq\sum\limits_{r\geq\hat{r}}\left|\bigcup\limits_{|I|\geq{r}}\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigcap_{j\in\overline{I}}\overline{A_{j}}\right|+(\hat{r}-1)|B|.

(17)

This in turn implies the statement of the lemma.∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Taking
t=8
,
\hat{r}=5
in Lemma
4.4
, we get

\min\limits_{i\in[8]}|A_{i}\cap B|\leq\frac{1}{8}\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{3}\binom{8}{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{2}\binom{8}{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{1}\binom{8}{i}+1\right)\left(\frac{p}{2^{8}}+8\frac{\sqrt{p}+1}{2}\right)+4\frac{p-1}{2}\right),

provided that
p>7408848
.

Proof.

We only have to verify that

\left|\bigcup\limits_{|I|\geq\hat{r}}\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigcap_{j\in\overline{I}}\overline{A_{j}}\right|\leq|B|

holds for an arbitrary set
B
of size
\frac{p-1}{2}
. This is equivalent to check that

(\sum_{i=0}^{3}\binom{8}{i})\left(\frac{p}{2^{8}}+8\frac{\sqrt{p}+1}{2}\right)<\frac{p-1}{2}
.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
B=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{(p-1)/2}\}
denote a subset of the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, where
p>7408848
. Then

\#\bigg\{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\colon\bigg\lvert\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{l}-\gamma}{p}\right)\bigg\lvert\geq\frac{p}{7}\bigg\}\leq 16.

Proof.

We first show that

\#\bigg\{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\colon\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{l}-\gamma}{p}\right)\geq\frac{p}{7}\bigg\}\leq 8

holds.

Let
Q
denote the square elements in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Then observe that the sum of the Legendre symbols measure the intersection of the translate of
Q
with
B
. More precisely,

\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{l}-\gamma}{p}\right)\geq\mu{(p-1)}\Leftrightarrow|(Q+\gamma)\cap B|\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu\right)\frac{p-1}{2}

holds if
-\gamma
is not a quadratic residue and

\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{l}-\gamma}{p}\right)\geq\mu{(p-1)}+1\Leftrightarrow|(Q+\gamma)\cap B|\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu\right)\frac{p-1}{2}

holds if
-\gamma
is a quadratic residue.

Hence what we aim for is to apply Corollary
4.5
and show that the minimum is smaller than
(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{7})\frac{p-1}{2}=\frac{9}{14}\frac{p-1}{2}
. If we simplify the expression we get

\min\limits_{i\in[8]}|A_{i}\cap B|\leq\frac{1}{8}\left(140\left(\frac{p}{2^{8}}+8\frac{\sqrt{p}+1}{2}\right)+2({p-1})\right),

and it is easy to verify that this will be smaller than
\frac{9}{14}\frac{p-1}{2}
provided that
p>26910
.

The inequality

\#\bigg\{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\colon\sum_{l=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{l}-\gamma}{p}\right)\leq-\frac{p}{7}\bigg\}\leq 8

follows similarly, which in turn proves the statement.
∎

5
Lovász-Schrijver construction

Lovász and Schrijver showed
[
12
]
that up to affine transformations (elements of
AGL(2,p)
) there is only one set in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
determining exactly
\frac{p+3}{2}
directions. We mention that
the characterisation of this set also follows from Rédei’s characterisation of the solutions of his Problem II for
q=p

[
13
]
.
Such a set can be described due to Szőnyi
[
17
]
as follows.

Let
\{(0,x)\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
and
\{(x,0)\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
be identified with
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Then we may also identify
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
with

\{(0,x)\colon 0\neq x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
and
\{(x,0)\colon 0\neq x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
.
Let

L:=\left\{(0,x)\colon\left(\frac{x}{p}\right)=1\right\}\bigcup\left\{(x,0)\colon\left(\frac{x}{p}\right)=1\right\}\bigcup\left\{(0,0)\right\}.

It is clear that
|L|=p
and it determines exactly
\frac{p+3}{2}
directions. Notice that the projection polynomials determined by
L
are described in Theorem
1.2
. Up to affine transformations there are two polynomials listed in Theorem
1.2
. We may see that both types appear as projection polynomials of
L
. Moreover, the ones listed in
Theorem
1.2

(ii)
appears exactly twice.
Since
L
is equidistributed in the set of parallel lines not determining a direction of
L
we may see that there are exactly
(p-1)-\frac{p+3}{2}
projection polynomials that are constant. The remaining projection polynomials are of type
1.2

(i)

The purpose of this section is to show that for large enough primes this result follows from Theorem
1.2
.
In order to do so we have to introduce the basic notion and some technique of the discrete Fourier transformation. We will also reformulate it in terms of the projection polynomials.

A character of finite abelian group is a homomorphism to the multiplicative group of the complex numbers.
For a function
f\colon\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
and a character
\xi
let
\hat{f}(\xi)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}}f(x)\overline{\xi(x)}
. Then
\hat{f}
is called the Fourier transform of
f
. The set of characters of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
will be denoted by
\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}}
. Any character of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
can be written as
\xi_{v}(x)=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{p}\langle x,v\rangle}
, where
\langle x,v\rangle
denotes the standard
\pmod{p}
scalar product of the vectors
v,x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
(where
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}}
are identified).
Thus the set of characters can be endowed with a group structure that is isomorphic to the original group
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
. This identification also assigns to each nontrivial representation a direction in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
.

The Plancherel formula says that

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}}|f(x)|^{2}=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}.

(18)

In particular, for the characteristic function
1_{S}
of the set
S
we have

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}}|1_{S}(x)|^{2}=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}}}|\widehat{1_{S}}(\xi)|^{2}.

We will apply this for a set
S
of cardinality
p
determining exactly
\frac{p+3}{2}
directions. Clearly,
\sum|1_{S}(x)|^{2}=p
.

It is easy to see that if
\xi
is not the trivial representation, then

\hat{1}_{S}(\xi)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}m(i)\sigma^{i},

(19)

where
m
denotes an affine transformation of the projection polynomial corresponding to
\xi
and
\sigma
is a primitive
p
’th root of unity.

Let
m
be a projection polynomial of a set of cardinality
p
.
It follows from Proposition
1.3
that
m
is either the constant
1
polynomial or of degree at most
\frac{p-1}{2}=\frac{p+3}{2}-2
. On the other hand
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
implies that if
m
is not constant, then
m
is of degree at least
\frac{p-1}{2}
so we obtain that
m
is either constant or of degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
.
The polynomials of degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
whose range sum is
p
are described in Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
m
denote an affine transformation of the projection polynomial corresponding to
\xi
.

1.

If
m
is constant, then
\hat{1}_{S}(\xi)=0
.

2.

If the leading coefficient of
m
is
\pm 1
, then
|\hat{1}_{S}(\xi)|=\sqrt{p}
.

3.

If the leading coefficient of
m
is
\pm\frac{p-1}{2}
, then
\frac{p}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{p}}{2}\leq|\hat{1}_{S}(\xi)|\leq\frac{p}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{p}}{2}
.

Proof.

1.

Follows trivially from the fact that the sum of the
n
’th roots of unity is 0 for every positive integer
n
.

2.

It is well-known
[
9
]
that the Gauss sum
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}e^{\frac{2\pi i}{p}x^{2}}
is of absolute value
\sqrt{p}
.

As we have seen in equation (
19
),
\hat{1}_{S}(\xi)=\sum m(i)\sigma^{i}
. If the leading coefficient of
m
is
\pm 1
, then
m(x)
can be obtained from
x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1
by a change of variable
x\to ax+b
.

Now
m(x)=x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1
is 0 at the quadratic non-residues, 2 at the quadratic residues and 1 at 0. This means that

\hat{1}_{S}(\xi)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}m(i)\sigma^{i}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}e^{\frac{2\pi i}{p}x^{2}}

so

|\hat{1}_{S}(\xi)|=\sqrt{p}.

Now
\sum m(ai+b)\sigma^{i}
can also be expressed as

\sum m(i)\sigma^{a^{\prime}i+b^{\prime}}
for some
a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*},b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The effect by the translation by
b^{\prime}
is just a multiplication with a number of absolute value
1
. Further
\sigma^{x}\to\sigma^{a^{\prime}x}
can be obtained using a Galois automorphism in
Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\sigma)|\mathbb{Q})
. This is an abelian group containing the complex conjugation so each automorphism preserves the absolute value of the sum, finishing the proof of this case.

3.

As we have seen in the previous case we only have to verify it when
m(x)=\frac{p+1}{2}(x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1)
.

Now treating the values of
m(x)
as nonnegative rational numbers in
\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
we obtain that

m(x)=\frac{p+1}{2}\left(x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1\right)=\frac{p}{2}1_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(\frac{x}{p}\right)+1\right).

∎

Using Lemma
5.1
we are able to estimate
the right hand side of equation (
18
) for
f=1_{S}
.

If
\xi
is the trivial representation, then
\hat{f}(\xi)=p
so
|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}=p^{2}
.

If the polynomial
m
in equation (
19
) is constant, then
\hat{f}(\xi)=0
. Since
|S|=p
we have that in this case, each line orthogonal to
\xi
(the representations of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
are identified with elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
so they are considered vectors in this vector space) contains exactly
1
element of
S
so
S
does not determine the direction orthogonal to
\xi
.
We have exactly
(p+1)-\frac{p+3}{2}=\frac{p-1}{2}
directions not determined by
S
so we have exactly

(p-1)\frac{p-1}{2}
roots of
\hat{1}_{S}
, the representations corresponding to these directions.

Let
M
denote the number of directions such that the corresponding projection polynomial
m
is of leading coefficient
\pm 1
. Note that the projection polynomial towards a direction is only determined up to an element of
AGL(1,p)
but these transformations can only change the leading coefficient of
m
up to a
\pm 1
factor.

Then it does follow from equation (
18
) and by Lemma
5.1
that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{p^{2}}\left(p^{2}+(p-1)\left(\frac{p-1}{2}0+M\sqrt{p}^{2}+\left(\frac{p+3}{2}-M\right)\left(\frac{p}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{p}}{2}\right)^{2}\right)\right)\leq\\
&p=\sum|\widehat{1}_{S}(\xi)|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{p^{2}}\left(p^{2}+(p-1)\left(\frac{p-1}{2}0+M\sqrt{p}^{2}+\left(\frac{p+3}{2}-M\right)\left(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{p}}{2}\right)^{2}\right)\right).\end{split}

From the lower bound we immediately get that
\frac{p+3}{2}-M\leq 2
if
p>9
. If
\frac{p+3}{2}-M=0
, then
p^{2}+(p-1)\frac{p+3}{2}p=p^{3}
, which holds only for
p=3
. If
\frac{p+3}{2}-M=1
, then the reduced equation satisfies that

\left(\frac{p}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{p}}{2}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{(p-1)p}{2}\leq\left(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{p}}{2}\right)^{2}.

Here we get that the lower bound holds if
p>2
, but the upper bound holds only for
3\leq p
.
These implies that
\frac{p+3}{2}-M=2
if
p
large enough
(p>9)
.

It remains to translate this calculation on the Fourier coefficients to a combinatorial result. We have obtained that there are two directions when the corresponding projection polynomials
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are of degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
with leading coefficient
\pm\frac{p-1}{2}
.
Then it follows from Theorem
1.2

that there is an
y_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
for
i=1,2
such that
m_{i}(y_{i})=\frac{p+1}{2}
(and
m_{i}(y_{i}+z)=0\mbox{ or }1

depending only on whether
z\neq 0
is a quadratic residue or not).

It does simply mean that there are two non-parallel lines, each of them containing
\frac{p+1}{2}
elements of
S
.
Then the intersection of these lines must belong to
S
since
|S|=p
, moreover there can not be further points of
S
outside the two lines in view.
Note that the nonzero elements of the support of
\frac{p+1}{2}(x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1)

and

\frac{p+1}{2}(-x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1)
are the set of quadratic residues and the set of quadratic nonresidues, respectively. This simply means that the we have two parallel classes of lines and on both of these classes,
p+1
points lie on the lines indexed by
0
. The remaining points are found on the lines indexed by quadratic residues or quadratic nonresidues.

Thus, after applying a suitable identifications of these two lines with
\mathbb{F}_{p}
we may assume that the point in the intersection of the lines coincide with
0
on both lines and the remaining points of
S
can be identified with the quadratic residues or quadratic nonresidues, just as it is described in Theorem
1.4
.

Note that the previous argument shows that if
p>9
holds for the prime
p
and the statement of Theorem
1.2
holds, then for the same prime
p
we can derive the uniqueness result of Lovász and Schrijver.∎

Further, note that Szőnyi
[
15
]
classified the sets contained the union of two lines, with minimal possible number of directions. This result could also have been applied to finish the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

6
Concluding remarks and open problems

In this paper we characterised those polynomials
P
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
for which

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}P(x)=p
, and
\deg(P)
is minimal; provided that
p
is large enough. We strongly believe that the latter condition is only technical, and one can get rid of it.

Several natural questions arise here. The first is concerning stability.

Problem 6.1
.

Suppose that
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}P(x)=p
for a polynomial
P
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, but
P
is not an affine translate of either
x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1

or
\frac{p+1}{2}\left(x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1\right)
. Does this imply
\deg(P)\geq C\cdot(p-1)
for some constant
C>\frac{1}{2}
, independent of
p
?

We conjecture that for
p
large enough, this holds even for
C=\frac{2}{3}
. This would imply the result of Gács concerning the number of determined directions
[
5
]
.

The next question is concerning further results on small range sums.

Problem 6.2
.

Fix a positive integer
k>1
.
Suppose that
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}P(x)=k\cdot p
for a polynomial
P
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Is it true that apart from finitely many primes
p
, either
P
is an affine translate of
c\cdot(x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+1)+d
for some constants
c,d
, or
\deg(P)>\frac{p-1}{2}
holds?

Notice that the range sum of the polynomials
(x-1)(x-2)+1,(x-1)(x-2)+2\in\mathbb{F}_{7}
is
14
. Moreover, if
p=5
, then the non-constant polynomials of degree less than
\frac{p-1}{2}
are linear. These are permutation polynomials so the range sum is
10
in this case. These examples show that we may only expect an affirmative answer for the question raised in Problem
6.2
when
p
is large enough.

Finally, we are interested in general in the relation between
h(p)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}P(x)
and

\min\deg\{P:\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}P(x)=h(p)\mbox{ and }P\neq 1\}.

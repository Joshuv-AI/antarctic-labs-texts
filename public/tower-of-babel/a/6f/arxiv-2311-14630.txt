SYK Model in a Non-Gaussian disorder ensemble and emergent Coleman’s mechanism
Ido Ben-Dayan, Pankaj Chaturvedi

Abstract
We consider the case of the SYK model with non-gaussian disorder in the large N N limit. After obtaining the effective action, we derive the density of states and the free energy of the modified theory. We show that the non-gaussian disorder corresponds to a non-local Liouville theory, and non-minimally coupled 2D gravity action. It also provides a nice realization of Colemania - Coleman’s idea from the 80s of generating a small Cosmological Constant. Finally, we also calculate out of time order correlation functions (OTOC) for the model.

SYK models have also found connections with random matrix theories (RMT) and their classifications. Motivated by the above dualities, in this work we have considered the SYK model with interacting Majorana fermions where the values of the coupling constants are now picked from a non-Gaussian distribution. We will call the model considered here as non-Gaussian SYK throughout the manuscript. The idea behind considering such a model is that different distributions of couplings one must recover the usual SYK results in the large N limit with
\mathcal{O}\left(1/N\right)
or higher corrections that might differ for each such distribution. Moreover, for such models it is expected that different distributions of couplings will give rise to different 2D gravity theories at low energy, generating a whole family of such theories that can be analyzed at the quantum level. The specific model we consider here, is inspired by the p-spin glass model with non-Gaussian, correlated disorder studied in
[
46
]
and the SYK model containing flavored complex fermions with non-Gaussian disorder averaging over the random couplings studied in
[
47
]
. The construction of such models is motivated by the Gaussian universality of the random tensor model which was first established in
[
45
]
. In random tensor model, the elements of a random tensor of rank D are often assumed to be independent and identically distributed complex random variables. The authors in
[
45
]
showed that in the large N limit the distribution of
N^{D}
elements of a random tensor of rank D converges to that of a Gaussian tensor model. They also established that if one considers a perturbed Gaussian distribution for the elements of a random tensor then in the same large N limit it again reduces to a Gaussian distribution with the covariance now being dependent on the nature of the perturbation.

It is clear from the above discussion that introducing non-Gaussian disorder averaging has interesting consequences on the properties of the SYK model. In this work we have explored several interesting constructs like the partition function, time ordered and out of time ordered correlation functions, and the free energy of the non-Gaussian SYK model. The main observations of this study can be summarized as follows:

1.

In the large N limit we have obtained the disorder averaged bilocal effective action for the non-Gaussian SYK model. It is observed that this non-Gaussian disorder averaging for the SYK model with interactions of order-q results in a modification of the variance of the Gaussian distribution of couplings to the leading order in N. One also observes the emergence of an
\mathcal{O}\left(1/N^{q-2}\right)
non-local term in the large N effective action. Similar observation was also made in the case of the SYK model containing flavored complex fermions with non-Gaussian disorder averaging in
[
47
]
.

2.

In the large N and large q limit (where q is the order of interactions) the effective action for the non-Gaussian SYK model is described by a nonlocal Liouville field theory for the fluctuations around the free fermion Green’s function. The two dimensional euclidean Liouville field theory contains a cosmological term and a subdominant nonlocal term. This theory is very similar to the Eisntein gravity with positive cosmological constant and a nonlocal term considered by Veneziano
[
48
]
in a four dimensional setting. The idea of considering such a nonlocal euclidean quantum gravity theory with wormholes solutions was inspired by Coleman in
[
49
]
. Using such a theory he further argued that if the wormholes exist, they have the effect of making the cosmological constant small. Polchinski showed that the method works only at space-time dimensions
D=2\,(\text{mod}\,4)

[
50
]
, which is the case at hand. Hence, using the approach adopted in
[
48
]
we show that one can also have a large volume and a small cosmological contant solution to the two dimensional nonlocal euclidean Liouville field theory corresponding to the non-Gaussian SYK model, providing a nice realization of Colemania.

3.

In the low temperature regime, we have also computed various physical quantities pertaining to the non-Gaussian SYK model namely: the thermal partition function, density of states, time and out of time ordered correlation functions and the Lyapunov exponent. The results indicate that all of these quantities to the leading order in the large N expansion matches with the usual SYK model with Gaussian disorder averaging. We also show that the non-Gaussianity of the SYK model does not effect the Lyapunov exponent obtained from its four-point OTOC indicating that the model is still maximally chaotic.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we introduce the SYK model with non-Gaussian disorder and in the large N limit, and obtain the bilocal effective action in Euclidean time. In this section we also outline the procedure for computing the thermal partition and correlation function for the non-Gaussian SYK model which involves using a Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation. In section 3, in the large N and large q limit we derive the effective action for the non-Gaussian SYK model which is described by a nonlocal Liouville field theory. We also compute the free energy for the non-Gaussian SYK model in this regime and elucidate on the possible Coleman mechanism for nonlocal Liouville field theory. In section 4, we derive the nonlocal Schwarzian theory corresponding to the non-Gaussian SYK model in the low temperature regime. Here we also compute the four-point OTOC and the corresponding Lyapunov exponent for the non local Schwarzian theory corresponding to the non-Gaussian SYK model. We then conclude,
and discuss the implications of introducing non-Gaussian disorder in the SYK model and possible open problems.

2
SYK model with non-Gaussian disorder

As discussed in the previous section the theory of interest here is the one-dimensional SYK model with N Majorana fermions described by the following Euclidean action

\displaystyle S(\psi)=\int d\tau\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi_{i}\partial_{\tau}\psi_{i}+\frac{(i)^{q/2}}{q!}\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}^{N}J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}\psi_{i_{1}}\psi_{i_{2}}\cdots\psi_{i_{q}}\right),

(2.1)

where the label set
\{{i_{a}\}}:=\{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{q}\}
such that
\{1\leqslant i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{q}\leqslant N\}
, and
q
(an even number) is the order of the interaction term involving q-fermions at a time. Here, a factor of
i
is necessary to make the Hamiltonian hermitian when
q=2\mod{(4)}
. It may also be seen that the SYK model is not time reversal symmetric for odd
q/2
. Thus we restrict ourselves to the case when
q
is a multiple of four as these models have time reversal symmetric the interactions which are dominant at low energy. The rank q tensor
J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}
in the SYK action plays the role of a coupling constant. Each of the components of the rank q tensor is defined conventionally as a Gaussian random variable whose values are picked from the distribution

\displaystyle P(J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}})=\sqrt{\frac{N^{q-1}}{2\pi\;\sigma^{2}}}\exp{\left(-N^{q-1}\frac{J^{2}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)},

(2.2)

having zero mean and the variance

\displaystyle\left\langle J^{2}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}\right\rangle=\frac{J^{2}\;(q-1)!}{N^{q-1}}=\frac{2^{q-1}\;(q-1)!}{q\;N^{q-1}}\mathcal{J}^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{N^{q-1}},

(2.3)

characterized by the dimension one parameter
J
which is the same for all the components. The SYK model described here is subjected to a quenched disorder which implies that the free energy is obtained as an average over the couplings. The disorder averaging can be defined in the following way

\displaystyle\left\langle f(J,\psi)\right\rangle_{J}=\frac{\int\prod\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}dJ_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}\;f(J,\psi)\;\exp{\left(-N^{q-1}\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}^{N}\frac{J^{2}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)}}{\int\prod\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}dJ_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}\;\exp{\left(-N^{q-1}\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}^{N}\frac{J^{2}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)}},

(2.4)

where
f(J,\psi)
is an arbitrary function of the couplings and the fermionic fields.

Given the Gaussian disorder averaging in (
2.4
), one may ask whether it is possible to consider an SYK model with more general disorder averaging. For this, we consider that the random couplings
J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}
take values according to a more general distribution which may be given as

\displaystyle P(J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}})

\displaystyle=C\exp{\left[-N^{q-1}\left(\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}^{N}\frac{J^{2}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}}{2\sigma^{2}}+V(J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}})\right)\right]},

\displaystyle V(J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda}{4N\sigma^{4}}\left(\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\},\{k_{a}\}}^{N}J^{2}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}J^{2}_{k_{1}k_{2}\cdots k_{q}}\right),

(2.5)

where
V(J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}})
denotes the deviation from a Gaussian distribution. Notice that now the probability distribution is a complicated non-gaussian multivariate function, rather than a product of independent gaussians. The division by
\sigma^{4}
in the expression of
V(J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}})
is done to make the quartic term scale independent and hence
\lambda
is a dimensionless parameter here. Moreover, the division of the coupling constant
\lambda
by
N
is done to make the quartic interaction strength comparable to the quadratic term in the action at large
N
. Such a division also results in a coupling constant
\lambda
that can in principle be large and is independent of
N
. The case of an SYK model for complex fermions with flavor studied in
[
47
]
considers a coupling constant
\lambda
. The results here agree with their analysis if we take their
\lambda\rightarrow\lambda/N
and consider SYK model with Majorana fermions. The disorder averaging in (
2.4
) can now be modified as follows

\displaystyle\left\langle f(J,\psi)\right\rangle_{J}=\frac{\int\prod\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}dJ_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}\;f(J,\psi)\;\exp{\left[-N^{q-1}\left(\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}^{N}\frac{J^{2}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}}{2\sigma^{2}}+\frac{\lambda}{N}\left(\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}^{N}\frac{J^{2}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\right]}}{\int\prod\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}dJ_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}\;\exp{\left[-N^{q-1}\left(\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}^{N}\frac{J^{2}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}}{2\sigma^{2}}+\frac{\lambda}{N}\left(\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}^{N}\frac{J^{2}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\right]}},

(2.6)

and on using the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation it can be further written down as

\displaystyle\left\langle f(J,\psi)\right\rangle_{J}=\frac{\int\mathcal{D}J\;dM\;f(J,\psi)\;\exp{\left[-\left(\frac{M^{2}}{2}+N^{q-1}\left(1+i\left(\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}\right)^{1/2}M\right)\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}^{N}\frac{J^{2}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\right]}}{\int\mathcal{D}J\;dM\;\exp{\left[-\left(\frac{M^{2}}{2}+N^{q-1}\left(1+i\left(\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}\right)^{1/2}M\right)\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}^{N}\frac{J^{2}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\right]}}.

(2.7)

where
M
is some auxiliary parameter. It is to be noted that in (
2.7
) integration over each
M
contributes a
\sqrt{2\pi}
factor which cancels with the same factor coming from the denominator. The main focus of giving the formulation in (
2.7
) for a disorder averaging is to evaluate the free energy
F(J)
of the SYK model. This is usually achieved by employing the replica trick

\displaystyle F(J)=-\frac{1}{\beta}\left\langle\ln{Z(J)}\right\rangle_{J}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\lim_{n\to 0}\frac{\left\langle Z^{n}(J)\right\rangle_{J}-1}{n},

(2.8)

where

\displaystyle Z^{n}(J)=\int\prod\limits_{i}d\psi^{\alpha}_{i}\;\exp{\left[-\int d\tau\;\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\partial_{\tau}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}+\frac{(i)^{q/2}}{q!}\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}^{N}J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i_{1}}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i_{2}}\cdots\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i_{q}}\right)\right]},

(2.9)

and
\alpha=\{1,\cdots,n\}
is the replica index. The relation in (
2.8
) further implies that one only needs to evaluate the quantity
\left\langle Z^{n}(J)\right\rangle_{J}
and then take the replica limit to evaluate the disorder averaged free energy. Thus considering (
2.7
), the disorder average of the replicated partition function in (
2.9
) can be written as

\displaystyle\left\langle Z^{n}(J)\right\rangle_{J}

\displaystyle=\int\;\mathcal{D}J\;\mathcal{D}\psi\;dM\;\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\partial_{\tau}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}-\frac{M^{2}}{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\left(N^{q-1}\left(1+i\left(\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}\right)^{1/2}M\right)\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}^{N}\frac{J^{2}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.-\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}^{N}\left(\frac{(i)^{q/2}}{q!}\int d\tau\;\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i_{1}}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i_{2}}\cdots\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i_{q}}\right)J_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}\right)\right],

(2.10)

Having obtained the replica partition function in (
2.10
), one can now perform the disorder average to obtain the following

\displaystyle\left\langle Z^{n}(J)\right\rangle_{J}=

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}\psi\;dM\;\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\pi N^{1-q}\sigma^{2}}{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}}\;M\right)}}\right)^{\binom{N}{q}}\exp\left[n\left(-\frac{1}{2n}\int d\tau\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\partial_{\tau}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}-\frac{M^{2}}{2}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\frac{1}{2(q!)^{2}}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\{i_{a}\}}^{N}\frac{\left(\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i_{1}}(\tau)\cdots\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i_{q}}(\tau)\right)\left(\sum\limits_{\beta=1}^{n}\psi^{(\beta)}_{i_{1}}(\tau^{\prime})\cdots\psi^{(\beta)}_{i_{q}}(\tau^{\prime})\right)}{N^{q-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}}\;\frac{M}{\sigma^{2}}\right)}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2\pi\sigma^{2}}{N^{q-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\binom{N}{q}}\int\mathcal{D}\psi\;dM\;\exp\left[\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\partial_{\tau}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.-\frac{M^{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\binom{N}{q}\ln{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}}\;M\right)}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\frac{N\sigma^{2}}{2~q!\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}}M\right)}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}(\tau)\psi^{(\beta)}_{i}(\tau^{\prime})\right)^{q}\right)\right],

(2.11)

where from the last line in the above expression it is clear that the replica partition function can not be further simplified and one has to consider certain approximations which we describe next.

Replica partition function in small

Using the formal expansion

\displaystyle\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}}\;M\right)^{-1}=\left(1+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\left(i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}}\;M\right)^{k}\right),

(2.12)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}}M\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}\left(i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}}\;M\right)^{k},

(2.13)

and
\binom{N}{q}\simeq N^{q}
, one can write (
2.11
) as

\displaystyle\left\langle Z^{n}(J)\right\rangle_{J}=

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}\psi\;dM\;\exp\left[\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\partial_{\tau}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}-\frac{M^{2}}{2}+\frac{N^{q}}{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}\left(i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}}\;M\right)^{k}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\frac{N\sigma^{2}}{2\;q!}\left(1+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\left(i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}}\;M\right)^{k}\right)\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}(\tau)\psi^{(\beta)}_{i}(\tau^{\prime})\right)^{q}\right)\right].

(2.14)

Considering the small
\lambda
limit and keeping everything to the order
\lambda^{1/2}
in the above expression for
\left\langle Z^{n}(J)\right\rangle_{J}
, one arrives at the following in the large N limit

\displaystyle\left\langle Z^{n}(J)\right\rangle_{J}

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}\psi\;dM\;\exp\left[\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\partial_{\tau}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}-\frac{M^{2}}{2}-i\;\frac{N^{q}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}}M\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+N\frac{2^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q^{2}}\left(1-i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}}\;M\right)\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}(\tau)\psi^{(\beta)}_{i}(\tau^{\prime})\right)^{q}\right)\right],

(2.15)

where in the above expression we have used (
2.3
) to replaced
\sigma
in terms of
\mathcal{J}
. One can integrate out the auxiliary field M in (
2.15
) to obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle Z^{n}(J)\right\rangle_{J}

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}\psi\;\exp\left[\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\partial_{\tau}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+N\frac{2^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q^{2}}\left(1-\lambda\right)\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}(\tau)\psi^{(\beta)}_{i}(\tau^{\prime})\right)^{q}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.-\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-2}}\frac{4^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{4}}{q^{4}}\left(\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}(\tau)\psi^{(\beta)}_{i}(\tau^{\prime})\right)^{q}\right)\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\left(\int\int d\overline{\tau}d\overline{\tau}^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\mu,\nu}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\mu)}_{i}(\overline{\tau})\psi^{(\nu)}_{i}(\overline{\tau}^{\prime})\right)^{q}\right)\right)\right].

(2.16)

Replica partition function in finite
\lambda
and Large N approximation:

In the above paragraph we showed that one can approximate the replica partition function in the small
\lambda
and large N limit. However, it is also possible to consider a finite
\lambda
and a large N expansion. To see this we begin by considering
\left\langle Z^{n}(J)\right\rangle_{J}
in (
2.11
) and expand it in Large N limit while keeping
\lambda
fixed as

\displaystyle\left\langle Z^{n}(J)\right\rangle_{J}=

\displaystyle\left(2\pi N^{1-q}\sigma^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}N^{q}}\int\mathcal{D}\psi\;dM\;\exp\left[\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\partial_{\tau}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.-\frac{M^{2}}{2}-iM\sqrt{\frac{\lambda N^{q}}{2}}-\frac{\lambda}{2}M^{2}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\frac{i^{q}N\sigma^{2}}{2~q!}\left(1-iM\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{N^{q}}}\right)\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}(\tau)\psi^{(\beta)}_{i}(\tau^{\prime})\right)^{q}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-3q/2})\right)\right].

(2.17)

Once again integrating out the auxiliary field M from the above action, one is left with the following

\displaystyle\left\langle Z^{n}(J)\right\rangle_{J}

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}\psi\;\exp\left[\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\partial_{\tau}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+N\frac{2^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q^{2}\left(1+\lambda\right)}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}(\tau)\psi^{(\beta)}_{i}(\tau^{\prime})\right)^{q}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.-\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-2}\left(1+\lambda\right)}\frac{4^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{4}}{q^{4}}\left(\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}(\tau)\psi^{(\beta)}_{i}(\tau^{\prime})\right)^{q}\right)\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\left(\int\int d\overline{\tau}d\overline{\tau}^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\mu,\nu}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\mu)}_{i}(\overline{\tau})\psi^{(\nu)}_{i}(\overline{\tau}^{\prime})\right)^{q}\right)\right)\right].

(2.18)

where in the above expression for
\left\langle Z^{n}(J)\right\rangle_{J}
we have used (

as before. It is instructive to note that on taking small

2.1
Effective action in the Large N limit

In this section we present the large N effective action
(I_{eff})
for the SYK model which involves integrating out the fermions in (
2.18
) through the introduction of bilocal fields
\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
and
G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
which are related to the Majorana fermionic fields of the SYK model. In order to achieve this one has to first define the bilocal field

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{\alpha,\beta}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}(\tau)\psi^{(\beta)}_{i}(\tau^{\prime}),

(2.19)

and then introduce the Lagrange multiplier field
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\alpha,\beta}
which enforces the constraint given above. To enforce the constraint (
2.19
) we include in the action (
2.18
), the following identity

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}\widetilde{\Sigma}\;\mathcal{D}\widetilde{G}\;\exp\left[-\frac{N}{2}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}^{n}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\;\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\alpha,\beta}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\left(\widetilde{G}_{\alpha,\beta}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})-\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}(\tau)\psi^{(\beta)}_{i}(\tau^{\prime})\right)\right]=1,

(2.20)

which leads to the following expression for the replica partition function

\displaystyle\left\langle Z^{n}(J)\right\rangle_{J}=

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}\widetilde{\Sigma}\;\mathcal{D}\widetilde{G}\left(\int\mathcal{D}\psi\exp\left[\left(-\frac{1}{2\;N}\int d\tau\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\partial_{\tau}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\left.+\frac{2^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q^{2}\left(1+\lambda\right)}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}^{n}\widetilde{G}_{\alpha,\beta}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{q}\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\left.-\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-1}\left(1+\lambda\right)}\frac{4^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{4}}{q^{4}}\left(\int\int d\overline{\tau}d\overline{\tau}^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\mu,\nu}^{n}\widetilde{G}_{\mu,\nu}(\overline{\tau},\overline{\tau}^{\prime})^{q}\right)\left(\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}^{n}\widetilde{G}_{\alpha,\beta}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{q}\right)\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\left.-\frac{1}{2}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}^{n}\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\alpha,\beta}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\left(\widetilde{G}_{\alpha,\beta}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})-\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{(\alpha)}_{i}(\tau)\psi^{(\beta)}_{i}(\tau^{\prime})\right)\right)\right]\right)^{N},

(2.21)

such that on introducing the rescalings
\mathcal{J}\to n^{-\frac{1}{2q}}\mathcal{J}
and
N\to n^{\frac{1}{q}}N
in the replica partition function above, it reduces to the following

In the large N limit the integrals over

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\alpha,\beta}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle G_{\alpha,\beta}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left(\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}-\Sigma_{\alpha,\beta}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\right)^{-1}.

(2.26)

\Sigma_{\alpha,\beta}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
and

\displaystyle I^{(n)}[\Sigma,G]

\displaystyle=n~N\left(-\frac{1}{2}\ln\det\left(\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}-\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{2}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})-\frac{2^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q^{2}\left(1+\lambda\right)}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{q}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-1}\left(1+\lambda\right)}\frac{4^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{4}}{q^{4}}\left(\int\int d\overline{\tau}d\overline{\tau}^{\prime}G(\overline{\tau},\overline{\tau}^{\prime})^{q}\right)\left(\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{q}\right)\right),

(2.27)

Using (
2.8
), it is now trivial to take the replica limit

\displaystyle-\left\langle\ln Z(J)\right\rangle_{J}=-\lim_{n\to 0}\frac{\left\langle e^{(-n\;I_{eff}(J))}\right\rangle-1}{n}\simeq I_{eff}(J)

(2.28)

where

\displaystyle\frac{I_{eff}(J,\lambda)}{N}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr\,}\ln\left(\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}-\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle-\frac{2^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q^{2}\left(1+\lambda\right)}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{q}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-1}\left(1+\lambda\right)}\frac{4^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{4}}{q^{4}}\left(\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{q}\right)\left(\int\int d\overline{\tau}d\overline{\tau}^{\prime}G(\overline{\tau},\overline{\tau}^{\prime})^{q}\right),

(2.29)

such that the Schwinger Dyson equations in (
2.25
) and (

) now become

\displaystyle\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{q-1}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q\left(1+\lambda\right)}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{q-1}-\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-1}\left(1+\lambda\right)}\;\frac{4^{q-1}\mathcal{J}^{4}}{q^{3}}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{q-1}\left(\int\int d\overline{\tau}d\overline{\tau}^{\prime}G(\overline{\tau},\overline{\tau}^{\prime})^{q}\right),

(2.30)

\displaystyle G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left(\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}-\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\right)^{-1},

(2.31)

for the effective action
I_{eff}
given above. To this end, we would like to emphasize that we have kept the subdominant
\mathcal{J}^{4}
term in the effective action (
2.29

N

\lambda
limit. As one can see, the non-Gaussian disorder has two effects. First it renormalizes the bare propagator
G
such that
\mathcal{J}^{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{J}^{2}/(1+\lambda)
and second, it induces a non-local term in the action suppressed by powers of
N^{q-1}
in the large N limit.

2.2
Partition and Correlations functions

In this subsection, we outline the prescription for calculating the correlation functions in the SYK model with non-Gaussian disorder. For this, we will first focus on computing the thermal partition function using a particular method and then show how this method can be used to evaluate the general n-point correlation function.

Thermal Partition function

In order to evaluate the thermal partition function for the non-Gaussian SYK model
[
51
]
, we start with the large
N
effective action for the SYK model with Gaussian disorder, i.e. we set
\lambda=0
in (
2.29
) to obtain the following

\displaystyle\frac{I_{eff}(J)}{N}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr\,}\ln\left(\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}-\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\left(\int d\tau^{\prime}\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{2^{q-1}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q^{2}}\int d\tau^{\prime}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{q}\right),

(2.32)

where one can identify the interaction Hamiltonian for the SYK model with Gaussian disorder as

\displaystyle H_{0}=-N\left(\frac{2^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q^{2}}\int d\tau^{\prime}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{q}\right).

(2.33)

Given the form of the interaction Hamiltonian for the SYK model above, we write the interaction Hamiltonian for the case of non-Gaussian disorder in (
2.29
) as

\displaystyle H=-N\left(\frac{2^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q^{2}(1+\lambda)}\int d\tau^{\prime}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{q}\right),

(2.34)

where it can be observed that the deformation parameter only rescales the coupling
\mathcal{J}
of the SYK model. Moreover, the total interaction term in (
2.29
) can also be written in terms of
H
defined in (
2.34
) as

\displaystyle f(\lambda,H)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q^{2}\left(1+\lambda\right)}\int d\tau^{\prime}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{q}+\frac{\lambda\beta}{N^{q-1}\left(1+\lambda\right)}\frac{4^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{4}}{q^{4}}\left(\int d\tau^{\prime}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{q}\right)\left(\int d\overline{\tau}^{\prime}G(\overline{\tau},\overline{\tau}^{\prime})^{q}\right),

\displaystyle=\frac{H}{N}+\frac{\lambda\beta}{N^{q-1}}\left(1+\lambda\right)\left(\frac{H}{N}\right)^{2},

(2.35)

as
G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
is considered to be a function of the difference of its arguments which is why there is a factor of
\beta
present in the second term of the above expression. Now using a Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation one can write the thermal partition function for the SYK model with non-Gaussian disorder as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\lambda,\beta)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr\,}{\left(e^{-\beta f(\lambda,H)}\right)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}dE\;e^{-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}-\beta\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)E}\;\rho(E),

(2.36)

It is known that in the low temperature regime
(\beta\to\infty)
, the dynamics of the SYK model is described by a Schwarzian theory
[
20
,
51
]
. In this regime, we understand the bulk dual for the SYK model is described by JT gravity in
AdS_{2}
with Dirichlet conditions for the dilaton and the metric at the AdS boundary
[
20
,
21
]
. The partition function and the density of states for the SYK model in the small temperature regime can be given as

\displaystyle Z^{syk}_{0}(\beta)=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\pi\beta^{3}}}e^{\frac{\pi^{2}}{\beta}},\quad\rho_{0}(E)=\frac{\sinh{\left(2\pi\sqrt{E}\right)}}{4\pi^{2}},

(2.37)

Now on substituting the density of the states for the SYK model with Gaussian disorder,
\rho(E)=\rho_{0}(E)
defined in (
2.37
) in the relation (
2.36
), one can evaluate the partition function for the non-Gaussian SYK model as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\lambda,\beta)=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{32\pi^{2}\beta^{3}}}\;\exp{\left[-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{\beta\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)}-\log{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)}\right]},

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{32\pi^{2}\beta^{3}}}\;\left(1-i\left(1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{\beta}\right)\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}-\frac{\left(2\beta^{2}+4\pi^{2}\beta+\pi^{4}\right)\lambda(1+\lambda)}{\beta^{2}N^{q-1}}\widetilde{M}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\mathcal{O}\left(N^{-\frac{3(q-1)}{2}}\right)\right)\exp{\left[-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{\beta}\right]},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\pi\beta^{3}}}\left(1-\frac{\left(2\beta^{2}+4\pi^{2}\beta+\pi^{4}\right)\lambda(1+\lambda)}{\beta^{2}N^{q-1}}\right)\exp{\left[\frac{\pi^{2}}{\beta}\right]},

(2.38)

where in the second line we have performed a large N expansion to approximate the partition function. In order to determine the density of states for the non-Gaussian SYK model we first write the partition function in (
2.36

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\lambda,\beta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\overline{E}\;\frac{e^{-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}-\beta\overline{E}}}{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)}\;\rho\left(\frac{\overline{E}}{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)}\right),

(2.39)

where in deriving the second line we have considered the transformation

\displaystyle E\to\frac{\overline{E}}{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)}.

(2.40)

As
\overline{E}
is just an integration variable, one can replace it by
E

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\lambda,\beta)

where the modified density of states
\rho_{\lambda}(E)

\displaystyle\rho_{\lambda}(E)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\;e^{-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}-\log{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)}}\;\rho\left(\frac{E}{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)}\right).

(2.42)

\rho(E)=\rho_{0}(E)

\displaystyle\rho_{\lambda}(E)

which shows that the non-Gaussian model starts with a similar density of states at low energy, but the density decays exponentially at higher energies compared to the Gaussian SYK as shown in the figure (

The procedure described above for computing the low-temperature partition function for the non-Gaussian SYK model shows that one has to introduce an auxiliary field through a Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation. This transformation relates the physical quantities in the Gaussian SYK model to the corresponding physical quantities in the non-Gaussian SYK model. As we will elucidate later, implementing a Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation will be our method for obtaining the correlation functions and other relevant physical quantities pertaining to the non-Gaussian SYK model.

Correlation Functions:

Before we focus on the computation of the correlation functions for the non-Gaussian SYK model it is important to understand how they are evaluated for the case of Gaussian disorder. For this, we first consider an operator
\mathcal{O}
made up of the product of Majorana fermion bilinears for the usual SYK model. The finite temperature expectation value of such an operator can be given as

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{O}\right\rangle_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dE\;e^{-\beta E}\mathcal{O}(E),\quad\mathcal{O}(E)=\left\langle E\left|\mathcal{O}\right|E\right\rangle,

(2.44)

where the vacuum expectation value of the operator
\mathcal{O}
is simply given by
\left\langle 0\left|\mathcal{O}\right|0\right\rangle
. We have defined the finite temperature expectation value of the operator in the sense that when
\mathcal{O}
is an identity operator its expectation value is just the thermal partition function.

The finite temperature expectation value of the operator
\mathcal{O}
can now be evaluated for the SYK with the non-Gaussian disorder as

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{O}\right\rangle_{\lambda,\beta}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dE\;e^{-\beta f(\lambda,E)}\mathcal{O}(E),

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dE\;e^{-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}-\beta\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)E}\mathcal{O}(E),

(2.45)

where the function
f(\lambda,H)
is defined in (
2.35
) and in writing the last line in the above expression we have used a Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation similar to the case of the thermal partition discussed in (
2.36
) before. Here it is to be noted that the thermal expectation value of the operator
\left\langle\mathcal{O}\right\rangle_{\lambda,\beta}
with the subscript
\lambda
denotes its value for the non-Gaussian SYK model. From (
2.45
) it is trivial to see that as
\lambda\to 0
, the expectation value of the operator goes to the one that corresponds to the case of the Gaussian SYK model in (
2.44
). Now in order to relate the expectation values of the operator
\mathcal{O}
in the two SYK models with different disorder averaging we consider the following transformation (
2.40
) which reduces (
2.45
) to the following

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{O}\right\rangle_{\lambda,\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\overline{E}\;\frac{e^{-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}-\beta\overline{E}}}{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)}\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\overline{E}}{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)}\right),

(2.46)

which can be further written as

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{O}\right\rangle_{\lambda,\beta}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dE\;e^{-\beta E}\;\mathcal{O}(E)_{\lambda},

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(E)_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\;\frac{e^{-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}}}{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)}\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{E}{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)}\right),

(2.47)

where we have removed the bar over the energy as it is only an integration variable. Here it is to be noted that
\mathcal{O}(E)_{\lambda}
stands for the expectation value of the operator for the energy eigenstate
\left|E\right\rangle
in the case of the non-Gaussian SYK model. This is related to
\mathcal{O}(E)
through the relation (
2.47
), where
\mathcal{O}(E)
is the expectation value of the operator for the energy eigenstate
\left|E\right\rangle
in the case of the Gaussian SYK model. Thus, the relation in (
2.47
) gives us a dictionary to relate the thermal correlation functions in the two SYK models with different disorder averaging.

Now in order to demonstrate the computation of a general n-point correlation function of the operator
\mathcal{O}
, we begin by considering the simple case of the vacuum one-point function of the same operator. It can be given as

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{O}(\tau)\right\rangle_{\lambda,0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dE\;e^{-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}-\tau\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)E}\mathcal{O}(E),

(2.48)

where we have replaced
\beta
by
\tau
in (
2.45
) assuming that
\tau>0
and the ground state energy is zero. In a similar way, the n-point vacuum correlator for the non-Gaussian SYK model can be obtained

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{O}(\tau_{1})\mathcal{O}(\tau_{2})\cdots\mathcal{O}(\tau_{n-1})\mathcal{O}(0)\right\rangle_{\lambda,0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\;e^{-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\prod\limits_{i}^{n-1}dE_{i}\left(\left\langle 0\left|\mathcal{O}(\tau_{1})\right|E_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle E_{1}\left|\mathcal{O}(\tau_{2})\right|E_{2}\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left\langle E_{2}\left|\mathcal{O}(\tau_{3})\right|E_{3}\right\rangle\cdots\left\langle E_{n-1}\left|\mathcal{O}(0)\right|0\right\rangle\right)\exp{\left[-\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-2}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i+1})E_{i}+\tau_{n-1}E_{n-1}\right)\right]},

(2.49)

whereas the finite temperature n-point correlation function can be given as

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{O}(\tau_{1})\mathcal{O}(\tau_{2})\cdots\mathcal{O}(\tau_{n-1})\mathcal{O}(\tau_{n})\right\rangle_{\lambda,\beta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\;e^{-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\prod\limits_{i}^{n-1}dE_{i}\left(\left\langle E_{1}\left|\mathcal{O}(\tau_{1})\right|E_{2}\right\rangle\left\langle E_{2}\left|\mathcal{O}(\tau_{2})\right|E_{3}\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left\langle E_{3}\left|\mathcal{O}(\tau_{3})\right|E_{4}\right\rangle\cdots\left\langle E_{n-1}\left|\mathcal{O}(\tau_{n})\right|E_{n}\right\rangle\right)\exp{\left[-\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}E_{i}\right)\right]},

(2.50)

where we identified,
\tau_{i}-\tau_{i+1}=\beta_{i}
for
i=\{1,2,\cdots,n-1\}
,
\tau_{1}=\beta_{1}
and
\tau_{n}=\beta_{n}
. To this end, it should be noted that the expectation values and the energy eigenstates in the right-hand side of the expressions (
2.48
) to (
2.50
) correspond to the Gaussian SYK model.

Having given the general prescription for evaluating the n-point correlation function we now consider correlators in one-dimensional conformal field theory as an example. For instance, the two-point zero temperature correlator in this case can be given as

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{O}(\tau)\mathcal{O}(0)\right\rangle_{0,0}=\frac{1}{\tau^{2\Delta}}=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dE\;e^{-\tau E}\left|\left\langle 0\left|\mathcal{O}\right|E\right\rangle\right|^{2},\quad\left|\left\langle 0\left|\mathcal{O}\right|E\right\rangle\right|^{2}=\frac{E^{2\Delta-1}}{\Gamma{(2\Delta)}}

(2.51)

where
\Delta
is the scaling dimension of the operator. Plugging the value of
\left|\left\langle 0\left|\mathcal{O}\right|E\right\rangle\right|^{2}
from above in (
2.49
) for
n=2
and
E_{1}=E
, one gets the two-point function in the modified conformal field theory with non zero
\lambda
as

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{O}(\tau)\mathcal{O}(0)\right\rangle_{\lambda,0}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\;\frac{\exp{\left[-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}\right]}}{\tau^{2\Delta}(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M})^{2\Delta}},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau^{2\Delta}}\left(1-2\Delta(2\Delta+1)(\lambda+1)\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-1}}+\mathcal{O}\left(N^{-\frac{3(q-1)}{2}}\right)\right)

(2.52)

where we have obtained the above result by first integrating over the energy eigenstates and then integrating over the auxiliary variable
\widetilde{M}
in the large N approximation.

We can now use the above formulation to determine the correlation functions for the non-Gaussian SYK model. For this, we first consider the Schwinger-Dyson equations
[
20
]
in frequency space for the Gaussian SYK model,

\displaystyle G^{syk}(i\omega)=\frac{1}{-i\omega-\Sigma^{syk}(i\omega)},\quad\Sigma^{syk}(i\omega)=\frac{2^{q-1}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q}G^{syk}(i\omega)^{q-1},

(2.53)

which can be solved analytically for
q=2
case giving the thermal two-point function
G^{syk}(i\omega)
as

\displaystyle G^{syk}(i\omega)=-\frac{2}{i\omega+i\operatorname{sgn}{(\omega)}\sqrt{4\mathcal{J}^{2}+\omega^{2}}},\quad\Sigma^{syk}(i\omega)=-\frac{i}{2}\left(\omega+\operatorname{sgn}{(\omega)}\sqrt{4\mathcal{J}^{2}+\omega^{2}}\right)

(2.54)

where
\omega=\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{\beta}
is the Matsubara frequency. The above two-point function
G^{syk}(i\omega)
can also be written in terms of energy
E=i\omega
, as

\displaystyle G^{syk}(E)=-\left(\frac{2}{E+i\sqrt{4\mathcal{J}^{2}-E^{2}}}\right),

(2.55)

such that the density of states can now be computed from the above two point function as

\displaystyle\rho^{syk}(E)={\rm Im\,}(G^{syk}(E))=\frac{1}{\mathcal{J}}\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{E}{2\mathcal{J}}\right)^{2}},

(2.56)

which clearly follows the Wigner’s Semi-circle law when plotted against the energy
[
52
]
.

The thermal two-point function in (
2.55
) can now be used to deduce the thermal two-point function for the non-Gaussian SYK model. For this, we first identify
G^{syk}(E)=\mathcal{O}(E)
and then on using the relation in (
2.47
) one can obtain the corresponding quantity in the case of non-Gaussian SYK as

\displaystyle G(E)_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\;\frac{e^{-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}}}{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)}G^{syk}\left(\frac{E}{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)}\right),

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\;\exp\left[-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}-i\left(1+\frac{iE}{\sqrt{4\mathcal{J}^{2}-E^{2}}}\right)\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N}}\;\widetilde{M}\right.

\displaystyle\left.~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right]G^{syk}(E),

(2.57)

such that after integrating over the auxiliary field
\widetilde{M}
one gets

\displaystyle G(E)_{\lambda}=-\left(\frac{2}{E+i\sqrt{4\mathcal{J}^{2}-E^{2}}}\right)-\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N}\left(\frac{\left(E-i\sqrt{4\mathcal{J}^{2}-E^{2}}\right)^{2}}{\left(4\mathcal{J}^{2}-E^{2}\right)\left(E+i\sqrt{4\mathcal{J}^{2}-E^{2}}\right)}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)

(2.58)

and the density of states corresponding to the above two-point function can now be given as

\displaystyle\rho_{\lambda}(E)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{J}}\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{E}{2\mathcal{J}}\right)^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N}\frac{\left(\mathcal{J}^{2}-E^{2}\right)}{\mathcal{J}^{2}\sqrt{4\mathcal{J}^{2}-E^{2}}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)\right)

(2.59)

which shows the deviation of order
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)
and higher from the semi-circle law obtained in (
2.56
) for the case of the Gaussian SYK model. This deviation from the semi-circle law for the density of states corresponding to
q=2
non-Gaussian SYK model is depicted in figure (
2
) which shows that for fixed
\mathcal{J}
and
N
, one gets a more squashed semi-circular behaviour for the density of states as the parameter
\lambda
is increased.

Figure 2:
Plot of density of states
\rho_{\lambda}(E)
(for
q=2
case) with respect to the energy E for different values of
\lambda=\left\{0,3,5,7\right\}
represented by the blue, orange, green, and red curves respectively. We have fixed the parameters
N=1000
and
\mathcal{J}=1
for all values of
\lambda
.

As demonstrated for the
q=2
case, if the usual Gaussian SYK n-point correlation functions are known in the frequency/energy representation then using the above procedure one can easily determine the n-point correlation functions for the non-Gaussian SYK model.

3
Large q approximation and relation to Liouville field theory

In order to study the thermodynamical properties of the SYK model one has to evaluate the free energy. It can be determined in the large N limit if one has the exact solution for the fields
\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
and
G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
satisfying the Schwinger-Dyson equations described in the previous section. However, solving the Schwinger Dyson equations in (
2.30
) and (
2.31
) analytically for
\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
and
G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
is a bit nontrivial and one requires some approximations. One such approximation is the large q limit which helps considerably in solving the Schwinger-Dyson equations for the SYK model being considered here
[
20
]
. In order to consider this limit we first begin by noting that in the frequency space, the free fermion Green’s function can be given as

\displaystyle G_{0}(\omega_{n})=-\frac{1}{i\omega_{n}},~~\omega_{n}=\frac{2\pi}{\beta}(n+1/2),

(3.1)

where
\omega_{n}
is the Matsubara frequency. It is also possible to write the dispersionless free fermion Green’s function in the
\tau
space as

G_{0}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sgn}(\tau-\tau^{\prime}).

(3.2)

such that in the large q limit one can recast the full Green’s function
G(\tau)
as follows,

G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=G_{0}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\left[1+\frac{1}{q}g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\right].

(3.3)

Given the large q expansion of Green’s function above, one can in principle determine an effective action for the field
g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
which is the
\mathcal{O}(1/q)
correction. For this, it may be noted that the saddle point self-energy in (
2.30
) also scales with
1/q
so that one can expand the determinant term in (
2.29
) as follows

\displaystyle{\rm Tr\,}\ln(\partial_{\tau}-\Sigma)={\rm Tr\,}\ln(G_{0}^{-1})-{\rm Tr\,}\left(G_{0}*\Sigma\right)-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr\,}\left(G_{0}*\Sigma*G_{0}*\Sigma\right)+\cdots,

(3.4)

where

\displaystyle G_{0}^{-1}(\omega)\equiv\partial_{\tau},\quad{\rm Tr\,}\left(G_{0}*\Sigma\right)=\int d\tau^{\prime\prime}G_{0}(\tau,\tau^{\prime\prime})\Sigma(\tau^{\prime\prime},\tau^{\prime})

(3.5)

Substituting (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) in (
2.29
) and dropping the constant term
{\rm Tr\,}\ln(G_{0}^{-1})
one can get the following form of the effective action in the large q limit

\displaystyle\frac{I^{(q)}_{eff}(J)}{N}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{4}{\rm Tr\,}\left(G_{0}*\Sigma*G_{0}*\Sigma\right)+\frac{1}{2q}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})G_{0}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}}{4q^{2}(1+\lambda)}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}e^{g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})}+\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-1}(1+\lambda)}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{4}}{16q^{4}}\left(\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}e^{g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})}\right)\left(\int\int d\overline{\tau}d\overline{\tau}^{\prime}e^{g(\overline{\tau},\overline{\tau}^{\prime})}\right).

(3.6)

Now on integrating out
\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
from the above action one can obtain an effective action for the
g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
field. For this, we define the following field

\displaystyle\Phi(\tau,\tau^{\prime})={\rm Tr\,}\left(G_{0}*\Sigma\right)=\int d\tau^{\prime\prime}G_{0}(\tau,\tau^{\prime\prime})\Sigma(\tau^{\prime\prime},\tau^{\prime}),\quad\partial_{\tau}\Phi(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime}),

(3.7)

which on substituting in (
3.6
) gives us the following expression

\displaystyle\frac{I^{(q)}_{eff}(J)}{N}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{4}{\rm Tr\,}\left(\Phi*\Phi\right)+\frac{1}{2q}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\partial_{\tau}\Phi(\tau,\tau^{\prime})G_{0}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}}{4q^{2}(1+\lambda)}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}e^{g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-1}(1+\lambda)}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{4}}{16q^{4}}\left(\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}e^{g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})}\right)\left(\int\int d\overline{\tau}d\overline{\tau}^{\prime}e^{g(\overline{\tau},\overline{\tau}^{\prime})}\right),

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{4}{\rm Tr\,}\left(\Phi*\Phi\right)-\frac{1}{2q}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\Phi(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}\left(G_{0}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}}{4q^{2}(1+\lambda)}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}e^{g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-1}(1+\lambda)}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{4}}{16q^{4}}\left(\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}e^{g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})}\right)\left(\int\int d\overline{\tau}d\overline{\tau}^{\prime}e^{g(\overline{\tau},\overline{\tau}^{\prime})}\right),

(3.8)

where in writing the second line we have used integration by parts in the second term. Integrating over the field
\Phi(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
one can obtain the Liouville effective action for the field
g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
as follows

\displaystyle\frac{I^{(q)}_{eff}(J)}{N}\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16q^{2}}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\partial_{\tau}g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})-\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}}{4q^{2}(1+\lambda)}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}e^{g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-1}(1+\lambda)}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{4}}{16q^{4}}\left(\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}e^{g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})}\right)\left(\int\int d\overline{\tau}d\overline{\tau}^{\prime}e^{g(\overline{\tau},\overline{\tau}^{\prime})}\right),

(3.9)

where in writing the above effective action from (
3.8
) we have substituted the explicit value of
G_{0}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=1/2\operatorname{sgn}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})
, such that it is only valid in the regime
\tau>\tau^{\prime}
, for the integrals. From the effective action in (
3.9
) one can obtain an equation of motion for the field
g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
as

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=-\frac{2\mathcal{J}^{2}}{1+\lambda}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2N^{q-1}}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q^{2}}\int\int d\overline{\tau}d\overline{\tau}^{\prime}e^{g(\overline{\tau},\overline{\tau}^{\prime})}\right)e^{g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})},

(3.10)

where we will be interested in general solutions that are time translation invariant. This implies that the field
g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
is a function of the difference of its arguments.

Liouville field Theory:

Here we first define the following coordinates

\displaystyle\nu=\tau^{\prime}-\tau,

\displaystyle\sigma=\tau^{\prime}+\tau,

(3.11)

which then helps us to write the effective action in (
3.9
) as

\displaystyle\frac{I^{(q)}_{eff}(J)}{N}\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8q^{2}}\int\int d\nu d\sigma\left(-\left(\partial_{\nu}g(\nu,\sigma)\right)^{2}+\left(\partial_{\sigma}g(\nu,\sigma)\right)^{2}-\frac{4\mathcal{J}^{2}}{(1+\lambda)}e^{g(\nu,\sigma)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-1}(1+\lambda)}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{4}}{2q^{2}}e^{g(\nu,\sigma)}\left(\int\int d\overline{\nu}d\overline{\sigma}^{\prime}e^{g(\overline{\nu},\overline{\sigma})}\right)\right),

(3.12)

and the corresponding equations of motion as

\displaystyle-\partial^{2}_{\nu}g(\nu,\sigma)+\partial^{2}_{\sigma}g(\nu,\sigma)=-\frac{2\mathcal{J}^{2}}{1+\lambda}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2N^{q-1}}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q^{2}}\int\int d\overline{\nu}d\overline{\sigma}^{\prime}e^{g(\overline{\nu},\overline{\sigma})}\right)e^{g(\nu,\sigma)}.

(3.13)

The above effective action for the non-Gaussian SYK model can now be considered as a two dimensional Liouville field theory. The dynamics or the quantization of this Liouville field theory depends only on the field
g(\nu,\sigma)
and it can be considered as the Weyl factor to the naive flat metric appearing in the kinetic term of the action
[
53
]
. Thus, one can motivate the following Euclidean two-dimensional anti-de-Sitter metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}=e^{g(\nu,\sigma)}(d\nu^{2}-d\sigma^{2}),

(3.14)

or the Euclidean de-Sitter metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}=e^{g(\nu,\sigma)}(-d\nu^{2}+d\sigma^{2}),

(3.15)

for
g(\nu,\sigma)
satisfying the equations of motion in (
3.13
)
[
20
,
53
]
. As elucidated earlier, we are working with the Euclidean action (
3.12
) which does not indicate which metric to prefer. Thus, one may choose any one of the Euclidean de-Sitter or the anti-de-Sitter metrics described above. This ambiguity is also due to the fact that in two dimensions de-Sitter and anti-de-Sitter spaces have the same isometries
and the same topology. However, here we first choose the anti-de-Sitter metric (
3.14
) for which one can compute the Ricci scalar as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=-e^{-g(\nu,\sigma)}\left(\partial^{2}_{\nu}g(\nu,\sigma)-\partial^{2}_{\sigma}g(\nu,\sigma)\right).

(3.16)

Using the above form of the two-dimensional Ricci scalar one can recast the effective action in (
3.12
) in the following form

\displaystyle\frac{I^{(q)}_{eff}(J)}{N}\simeq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{16q^{2}}\int\int d\nu d\sigma\sqrt{-\widetilde{g}}\left(\ln{\left(-\widetilde{g}(\nu,\sigma)\right)}\mathcal{R}(\nu,\sigma)+\frac{4\mathcal{J}^{2}}{(1+\lambda)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-1}(1+\lambda)}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{4}}{q^{2}}\left(\int\int d\overline{\nu}d\overline{\sigma}^{\prime}\sqrt{-\widetilde{g}}\right)\right),

(3.17)

where we have defined,
\widetilde{g}=-e^{2g(\nu,\sigma)}
as the determinant of the corresponding AdS metric in (
3.14
). In a similar way when one considers the de-Sitter metric (
3.15
) for which the Ricci scalar is given as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=-e^{-g(\nu,\sigma)}\left(-\partial^{2}_{\nu}g(\nu,\sigma)+\partial^{2}_{\sigma}g(\nu,\sigma)\right),

(3.18)

using which one can now recast the effective action in (
3.12
) as

\displaystyle\frac{I^{(q)}_{eff}(J)}{N}\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16q^{2}}\int\int d\nu d\sigma\sqrt{-\widetilde{g}}\left(\ln{\left(-\widetilde{g}(\nu,\sigma)\right)}\mathcal{R}(\nu,\sigma)-\frac{4\mathcal{J}^{2}}{(1+\lambda)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-1}(1+\lambda)}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{4}}{q^{2}}\left(\int\int d\overline{\nu}d\overline{\sigma}^{\prime}\sqrt{-\widetilde{g}}\right)\right),

(3.19)

In the large q and N limit, the above effective action can then be used to compute the free energy and other thermodynamical properties of the non-Gaussian SYK model which we address in the next subsection.

3.1
Free Energy in the large q limit

In order to determine the free energy, we begin by considering the partition function for the effective action in (
3.12
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{J},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int dg\;\exp{\left(-\frac{I^{(q)}_{eff}(J)}{N}\right)},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\;\exp{\left(-\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{2}}{2}\right)}\;\int dg\;\exp{\left[\frac{1}{8q^{2}}\left(-\Theta_{1}+\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\frac{4\mathcal{J}^{2}}{\left(1+\lambda\right)}\Theta_{0}\right)\right]},

(3.20)

where

\displaystyle\Theta_{0}=\int\int d\nu d\sigma\;e^{g\left(\nu,\sigma\right)},\quad\Theta_{1}=\int\int d\nu d\sigma\left(-\left(\partial_{\nu}g(\nu,\sigma)\right)^{2}+\left(\partial_{\sigma}g(\nu,\sigma)\right)^{2}\right).

(3.21)

One can further simplify the partition function by integrating out the
g
field using a saddle point approximation as was done in
[
48
]
. For this, we first consider the equation of motion for the following action

\displaystyle I_{eff}=-\frac{1}{8q^{2}}\left(-\Theta_{1}+\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\frac{2\mathcal{J}^{2}}{\left(1+\lambda\right)}\Theta_{0}\right),

(3.22)

which is given as

\displaystyle-\partial^{2}_{\nu}g(\nu,\sigma)+\partial^{2}_{\sigma}g(\nu,\sigma)=-\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\frac{4\mathcal{J}^{2}}{\left(1+\lambda\right)}\;e^{g\left(\nu,\sigma\right)},

(3.23)

We are interested in finding a solution to the above equation of motion. For this, we consider the ansatz that the field
g(\nu,\sigma)=g(\nu)
is a function of the
\nu\in[0,\beta]
variable only. The integration over the
\sigma
variable is also performed over the
[0,\beta]
interval. This gives us the following

\displaystyle I_{eff}=-\frac{\beta}{8q^{2}}\int d\nu\left(\left(\partial_{\nu}g(\nu)\right)^{2}+\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\frac{4\mathcal{J}^{2}}{\left(1+\lambda\right)}e^{g(\nu)}\right),

(3.24)

where the equation of motion now takes the form

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{\nu}g(\nu)=\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\frac{2\mathcal{J}^{2}}{\left(1+\lambda\right)}\;e^{g\left(\nu\right)},

(3.25)

For the boundary conditions
g(0)=g(\beta)=0
, The solution to the above equation of motion can be given as

\displaystyle g(\nu)=2\log{\left[\frac{\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}{\cos\left(\pi\alpha\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{|\nu|}{\beta}\right)\right)}\right]},\quad\pi^{2}\alpha^{2}=\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}\beta^{2}}{\left(1+\lambda\right)}\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right).

(3.26)

where the parameter
\alpha
ranges from zero to one as
\beta\mathcal{J}
ranges from zero to infinity. The parameter
\mathcal{J}
is dimensionful and remains fixed as q tends towards larger values.

Now in order to determine the on-shell value of the effective action
I_{eff}
, we consider its derivative with respect to
\mathcal{J}
while keeping the parameters
q,\beta,\lambda
and
\widetilde{M}
fixed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}\partial_{\mathcal{J}}I_{eff}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{1+\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\tan{\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}}\partial_{\alpha}I_{eff},

\displaystyle=-\frac{\beta}{2q^{2}}\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}}{\left(1+\lambda\right)}\int d\nu\;e^{g(\nu)}=-\frac{\beta}{4q^{2}}(g^{\prime}(\beta)-g^{\prime}(0)),

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi\alpha}{q^{2}}\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right),

(3.27)

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}I_{eff}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi}{q^{2}}\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\tan{\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}\right),

(3.28)

where in writing the above expressions it may be noted that we have used the second relation in (
3.26
) to turn the
\mathcal{J}
derivative into
\alpha
derivative. One can now determine
I_{eff}
by simply integrating (
3.28
) with respect
\alpha
which gives us

\displaystyle I_{eff}=-\frac{1}{2}\log{2}-\frac{\pi\alpha}{q^{2}}\left(\tan{\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}-\frac{\pi\alpha}{4}\right),

(3.29)

where the integration constant can be fixed using the fact that for
\mathcal{J}\to 0
, it is simply the log of the total dimension of the Hilbert space.

Using the expression for
I_{eff}
in (
3.20
) renders the following form for the partition function in the finite temperature regime

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{J},\lambda)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\;e^{-\mathcal{I}(\widetilde{M})},

(3.30)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\widetilde{M})

\displaystyle=\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\log{2}-\frac{\pi\;\alpha\left(\widetilde{M}\right)}{q^{2}}\left(\tan{\left(\frac{\pi\;\alpha\left(\widetilde{M}\right)}{2}\right)}-\frac{\pi\;\alpha\left(\widetilde{M}\right)}{4}\right),

(3.31)

and
\alpha
is considered to be a function of the auxiliary variable
\widetilde{M}
using the constraint relation in (
3.26
). In order to determine the saddle point value for the auxiliary variable
\widetilde{M}
we consider the zeros of the following

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(\widetilde{M})=\widetilde{M}+\frac{i\pi\alpha\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}}{2q^{2}\left(1+i\widetilde{M}\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\right)},

(3.32)

which is trivial to obtain by determining
\alpha^{\prime}(\widetilde{M})
from the constraint relation in (
3.26
) and then using the same to replace
\mathcal{J}
in terms of
\alpha
and
\widetilde{M}
. Setting
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(\widetilde{M})
to zero gives the following value for the auxiliary field

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{0}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{\frac{8\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}}\left(1\pm\sqrt{1+\frac{4\pi\alpha\lambda(1+\lambda)\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}{q^{2}N^{q-1}}}\right).

(3.33)

which when combined with the constraint in (
3.26
) gives us

\displaystyle\beta{\cal J}=\frac{\pi\alpha\sqrt{2(1+\lambda)}}{\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\sqrt{1\mp\sqrt{1+\frac{4\pi\alpha\lambda(1+\lambda)\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}{q^{2}N^{q-1}}}}}.

(3.34)

such that for the solution of
\widetilde{M}
with the positive sign we get the corresponding negative sign in the above expression.

The partition function (
3.30
) can now be evaluated using a saddle point approximation around the solution (
3.33
) with the constraint (
3.34
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{J},\lambda)\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{I}^{\prime\prime}(\widetilde{M}_{0})}}e^{-\mathcal{I}(\widetilde{M}_{0})},

(3.35)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\widetilde{M}_{0})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\log{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}\alpha^{2}}{4q^{2}}-\frac{\pi\alpha}{q^{2}}\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{N^{q-1}}{16\lambda(1+\lambda)}\left(1-\sqrt{1+\frac{4\pi\alpha\lambda(1+\lambda)\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}{q^{2}N^{q-1}}}\right)^{2},

(3.36)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\prime\prime}(\widetilde{M}_{0})

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\pi\alpha\lambda(1+\lambda)\sec^{2}\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)(\pi\alpha\cos(\pi\alpha)-\sin(\pi\alpha))}{N^{q-1}\left(1+\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\right)\left(1+\sqrt{1+\frac{4\pi\alpha\lambda(1+\lambda)\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}{q^{2}N^{q-1}}}\right)^{2}}.

(3.37)

which we have evaluated for the constraint in (
3.33
) for the negative sign. The free energy can now be determined from (
3.35
) as

\displaystyle-\beta F=\log{\mathcal{Z}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi^{2}\alpha^{2}}{4q^{2}}+\frac{\pi\alpha}{q^{2}}\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)-\frac{N^{q-1}}{16\lambda(1+\lambda)}\left(1-\sqrt{1+\frac{4\pi\alpha\lambda(1+\lambda)\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}{q^{2}N^{q-1}}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\log\left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\pi\alpha\lambda(1+\lambda)\sec^{2}\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)(\pi\alpha\cos(\pi\alpha)-\sin(\pi\alpha))}{2N^{q-1}\left(1+\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\right)\left(1+\sqrt{1+\frac{4\pi\alpha\lambda(1+\lambda)\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}{q^{2}N^{q-1}}}\right)^{2}}\right],

(3.38)

for the modified constraint in (
3.34
) with the positive sign. Moreover, in the large
N
limit we can expand the free energy as

\displaystyle-\beta F=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log{2}+\frac{\pi\alpha}{q^{2}}\left(\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)-\frac{\pi\alpha}{4}\right)+\frac{\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}\alpha^{2}}{4q^{4}}\tan^{2}\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{\pi\alpha}{2q^{2}}\frac{\pi\alpha\cos(\pi\alpha)-\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\left(2+\pi\alpha\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\right)}\right)+\mathcal{O}(1/N^{2q-2})

(3.39)

where the first two terms yield the free energy for the SYK model with Gaussian disorder. The other terms in the large N expansion of the free energy represent the
\lambda

\mathcal{O}(1/N^{2q-2})
corrections signaling deviations away from Gaussianity.

3.2
Coleman Mechanism for a small cosmological constant

The effective action obtained in (
3.19
) for the case of Euclidean de-Sitter metric is surprisingly reminiscent of the non-local toy model considered by Veneziano in
[
48
]
. This model was used to provide a new mechanism for suppressing the cosmological constant which is related to Coleman’s
[
49
]
idea of considering the effects of the wormhole solutions in Euclidean quantum gravity. It is well known in the literature that a two-site coupled SYK model possesses a wormhole solution that violates the null energy condition
[
17
]
. Thus one may propose a Coleman like mechanism for two-site coupled SYK model whose low energy physics is captured by the bimetric JT gravity
[
17
]
. However, in the present case, we observed that on considering non-Gaussian disorder in the SYK model one obtains a non-local action as in (
3.12
). This effective action may then be used to formulate a mechanism for suppressing the cosmological constant. This suppression may work here since as shown in
[
50
]
,
d=2\,(\text{mod}\,4)
is required which is the case here.

To elucidate the given mechanism for the case of the non-Gaussian SYK model, we begin by considering the effective action in (
3.12
) which after performing integration over
\sigma\in[0,\beta]
coordinate for the ansatz
g(\nu,\sigma)=g(\nu)
, can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{I^{(q)}_{eff}(J)}{N}\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{8q^{2}}\int d\nu\left(-\left(\partial_{\nu}g(\nu)\right)^{2}-\frac{4\mathcal{J}^{2}}{(1+\lambda)}e^{g(\nu)}-\frac{\lambda\beta}{N^{q-1}(1+\lambda)}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{4}}{2q^{2}}e^{g(\nu)}\int d\overline{\nu}e^{g(\overline{\nu})}\right),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\beta\Lambda_{0}\theta_{0}+\beta\Lambda_{1}\theta_{1}+\frac{\beta^{2}C}{2}\theta_{0}^{2},

(3.40)

where in writing the second line in the above expression, we have identified the following

\displaystyle\theta_{0}

\displaystyle=\int d\nu e^{g(\nu)},\quad\theta_{1}=-\int d\nu\left(\partial_{\nu}g(\nu)\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}}{2q^{2}(1+\lambda)},\quad\Lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{8q^{2}},\quad C=\frac{\lambda}{N^{q-1}(1+\lambda)}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{4}}{8q^{4}},

(3.41)

and have considered that the field,
g(\nu,\sigma)=g(\nu)
is a function of the
\nu
variable only. The constants
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{1}
are positive definite whereas
C
is a very small positive constant. For the de-Sitter geometry, using integration by parts the parameter
\theta_{1}
is related to the Ricci scalar in (
3.18
) in the following way

\displaystyle\theta_{1}=\int d\nu\;g(\nu)\partial^{2}_{\nu}g(\nu)=\int d\nu\sqrt{-\widetilde{g}}\ln{\left(-\widetilde{g}(\nu)\right)}\mathcal{R}(\nu)

(3.42)

where
\widetilde{g}=-e^{2g(\nu)}
, is the determinant of the corresponding Euclidean de-Sitter metric. The nonlocal effective action in (
3.40
) is very similar to the one studied in
[
48
]
with the only difference being that in our case we are restricted to two dimensions only. For this effective action, one can write down the following partition function

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{J},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int dg\;\exp{\left(-\frac{I^{(q)}_{eff}(J)}{N}\right)},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi C}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\;\exp{\left(-\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{2}}{2C}\right)}\;\int dg\;\exp{\left[\beta\left((\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M})\theta_{0}-\Lambda_{1}\theta_{1}\right)\right]},

(3.43)

which is a bit different from the partition function considered in (
3.20
) where the integration contour over the auxiliary variable
\widetilde{M}
runs along the real axis instead of the imaginary. The above partition function can be evaluated by integrating out the
g
field using a saddle point approximation for which we first consider the effective action

\displaystyle I_{eff}(\widetilde{M})=-\beta(\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M})\theta_{0}+\beta\Lambda_{1}\theta_{1}

(3.44)

whose corresponding equation of motion is further given by

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{\nu}g(\nu)=\left(\frac{\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M}}{2\Lambda_{1}}\right)e^{g(\nu)}

(3.45)

For the boundary conditions
g(0)=g(\beta)=0
, once again the solution to the above equation of motion can be given as

\displaystyle g(\nu)=2\log{\left[\frac{\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}{\cos\left(\pi\alpha\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{|\nu|}{\beta}\right)\right)}\right]},

(3.46)

for the constraint

\displaystyle\frac{\pi\alpha}{\sec\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}=\beta\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M}}{4\Lambda_{1}}},\quad\Lambda_{eff}=\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M}

(3.47)

where the parameter
\alpha
ranges from zero to one as the quantity under square root ranges from zero to infinity. Given the solution for
g(\nu)
above, one can determine the on-shell value of the effective action
\widetilde{I}(\widetilde{M})
by considering its derivative with respect to
\Lambda_{eff}
while keeping the parameters
\Lambda_{1}
and
\widetilde{M}
fixed as

\displaystyle 2\Lambda_{eff}\partial_{\Lambda_{eff}}I_{eff}(\widetilde{M})

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{1+\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\tan{\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}}\partial_{\alpha}\widetilde{I}(\widetilde{M}),

\displaystyle=-2\beta\left(\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M}\right)\int d\nu\;e^{g(\nu)}=-4\beta\Lambda_{1}(g^{\prime}(\beta)-g^{\prime}(0)),

\displaystyle=-16\pi\Lambda_{1}\alpha\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right),

(3.48)

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}I_{eff}(\widetilde{M})

\displaystyle=-16\pi\Lambda_{1}\tan\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\tan{\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}\right),

(3.49)

where in writing the above expressions it may be noted that we have used the relation in (
3.47
) to turn the
\Lambda_{0}
derivative into
\alpha
derivative. Now the on-shell value of
I_{eff}(\widetilde{M})
can be determined by simply integrating (
3.49
) with respect to
\alpha
which gives us

\displaystyle I_{eff}(\widetilde{M})=-\frac{1}{2}\log{2}-16\pi\Lambda_{1}\alpha\left(\tan{\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}-\frac{\pi\alpha}{4}\right),

(3.50)

which is different from the on-shell action obtained previously in (
3.29
) with different parametrizations. We now explore the low temperature
(\beta\to\infty)
limit of the given effective action. In this limit where
\alpha\to 1
, one can use the constraint in (
3.47
) to obtain the following

\displaystyle\alpha=1-\frac{2}{\beta\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M}}{4\Lambda_{1}}}}+\frac{4}{\beta^{2}\left(\frac{\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M}}{4\Lambda_{1}}\right)}-\frac{(24+\pi^{2})}{3\beta^{3}\left(\frac{\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M}}{4\Lambda_{1}}\right)^{3/2}}+\cdots,

(3.51)

The effective action in (
3.50
) is valid for any non-zero finite temperature. However, using (
3.51
) one can determine the expression for the effective action in the low temperature regime as

\displaystyle I_{eff}(\widetilde{M})=-\left(\frac{1}{2}\log{2}-4\pi^{2}\Lambda_{1}\right)-8\beta\sqrt{\Lambda_{1}\left(\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M}\right)}-\frac{16\pi^{2}\Lambda_{1}^{3/2}}{\beta\sqrt{\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M}}},

(3.52)

which gives us the partition function in (
3.43
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{J},\lambda)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi C}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\;e^{-\mathcal{I}(\widetilde{M})},

(3.53)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\widetilde{M})=\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2C}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\log{2}-4\pi^{2}\Lambda_{1}\right)-8\beta\sqrt{\Lambda_{1}\left(\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M}\right)}-\frac{16\pi^{2}\Lambda_{1}^{3/2}}{\beta\sqrt{\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M}}}.

(3.54)

The saddle point value for the auxiliary variable
\widetilde{M}
can be determined from the zeros of the following

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(\widetilde{M})=\frac{\widetilde{M}}{C}-i\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda_{1}}{\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M}}}\left(4\beta-\frac{8\pi^{2}}{\beta}\left(\frac{\Lambda_{1}}{\Lambda_{0}+i\widetilde{M}}\right)\right),

(3.55)

where it is not clear which value of
\widetilde{M}
obtained from equating the above to zero gives the physical solutions. For this, we first consider the case when
\widetilde{M}
is small which gives us

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(\widetilde{M})\simeq\frac{\widetilde{M}}{C}-i\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda_{1}}{\Lambda_{0}}}\left(4\beta-\frac{8\pi^{2}\Lambda_{1}}{\beta\Lambda_{0}}\right),

(3.56)

and on imposing
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(\widetilde{M})=0
, one obtains the following saddle point value of the auxiliary variable

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{0}=i4\beta C\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda_{1}}{\Lambda_{0}}}\left(1-\frac{2\pi^{2}\Lambda_{1}}{\beta^{2}\Lambda_{0}}\right)\simeq i\frac{\lambda\beta\mathcal{J}^{3}}{4q^{4}N^{q-1}\sqrt{\lambda+1}},

(3.57)

This saddle point corresponds to small
\widetilde{M}
and a small volume universe which can be approximated as

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}=\int\limits_{0}^{\beta}d\nu e^{g(\nu)}\simeq 4\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda_{1}}{\Lambda_{0}}}=2\frac{\sqrt{1+\lambda}}{\mathcal{J}}.

(3.58)

and in a similar way, one can also approximate
\mathcal{I}(\widetilde{M})
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\widetilde{M}_{0})\simeq-\frac{\log(2)}{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{2q^{2}}-\frac{2\beta\mathcal{J}}{q^{2}\sqrt{1+\lambda}}-\frac{\pi^{2}\sqrt{1+\lambda}}{\beta\mathcal{J}q^{2}}+\frac{\lambda\left(\pi^{2}(1+\lambda)-2\beta^{2}\mathcal{J}^{2}\right)^{2}}{16\beta^{2}\mathcal{J}^{4}q^{4}N^{q-1}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2q-2}}\right)

(3.59)

which suggests that as the volume
\mathcal{V}\propto 1/\mathcal{J}\to\infty
the action
\mathcal{I}(\widetilde{M}_{0})
also diverges implying that this saddle point does not correspond to a stable two-dimensional universe.

The second saddle point corresponds to the small effective cosmological constant
\Lambda_{eff}=\Lambda_{0}+\widetilde{M}
and a large volume universe,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{eff}=4\Lambda_{1}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}C}{\beta\Lambda_{0}}\right)^{2/3}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\pi^{4}\lambda^{2}\mathcal{J}^{4}}{2N^{2q-2}q^{10}\beta^{2}}\right)^{1/3}\ll\Lambda_{0},\quad\mathcal{V}\simeq 2\left(\frac{\beta\Lambda_{0}}{\pi^{2}C}\right)^{1/3}+\frac{\left(\pi^{2}-24\right)\Lambda_{0}}{3\pi^{2}\beta C},

(3.60)

Notice that
\beta
is related to the size of the Universe, as it is coming from the integration over the
\sigma
coordinate. As a result, the suppression of the cosmological constant is coming from several factors namely, the large volume as
\beta\rightarrow\infty
, the large
N\gg 1
limit and large values of the parameter
q
which has positive exponent in the expression of
\Lambda_{eff}
in (
3.60
). We also compute the free energy as

\displaystyle-\beta F=\log{\mathcal{Z}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{2}}{C}-8\Lambda_{1}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{\beta}-\frac{\pi^{4/3}\sqrt[3]{\Lambda_{0}}}{\beta^{4/3}\sqrt[3]{C}}\right)-\log\left(\frac{3\beta^{4/3}\Lambda_{0}^{5/3}}{8\pi^{4/3}C^{5/3}\Lambda_{1}}\right)\right)

(3.61)

which on using the relations in (
3.41
) can be shown to remain finite as
\beta\to\infty
, implying that the second saddle is the physical one. This suppression of the cosmological constant for the non-local gravitational effective action arising from the large-
q
description of the non-Gaussian SYK model, is in stark parallel with the case of non-local gravitational action considered in
[
48
]
to demonstrate the Coleman-Veneziano mechanism for a small cosmological constant.

. It may be observed that the source term including
s(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
, explicitly breaks the conformal invariance. It is this source term that governs the dynamics of the reparameterization modes
\phi(\tau)
as it lifts the degeneracy of the solutions for the action (
4.2
) without the kinetic term and ensures that there is a single saddle. We now wish to consider the effective action for the fluctuations around the conformal solutions in (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) given before. For this one can expand the field
G
around the conformal solution as follows

\displaystyle G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=G_{c}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})+g(\tau,\tau^{\prime}).

(4.14)

Using the above expansion, the action in (
4.13
) can be written to the second order in
g
as,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{S}}{N}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\;G_{c}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})s(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})-\frac{1}{2}\int\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\;g(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})s(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})+\mathcal{S}_{c}[G_{c}]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}\;g(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\;\mathcal{K}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2};\tau_{3},\tau_{4})\;g(\tau_{3},\tau_{4}),

(4.15)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{c}[G]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr\,}\ln\left(G\right)+\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}-\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\frac{2^{q-2}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q^{2}(1+\lambda)}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{q},

(4.16)

and the kernel
\mathcal{K}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2};\tau_{3},\tau_{4})
is simply defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2};\tau_{3},\tau_{4})=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{2}\mathcal{S}_{c}(G_{c})}{\delta G_{c}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\;\delta G_{c}(\tau_{3},\tau_{4})},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{c}^{-1}(\tau_{1},\tau_{3})G_{c}^{-1}(\tau_{2},\tau_{4})

\displaystyle+\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\frac{2^{q-1}(q-1)\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q(1+\lambda)}G_{c}^{q-2}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\delta(\tau_{1},\tau_{3})\delta(\tau_{2},\tau_{4}),

(4.17)

where
G_{c}^{-1}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=-\Sigma_{c}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
, which is trivial to observe from the equations of motion in (
4.5
) in the low-temperature approximation.

Varying the action (
4.15
) with respect to
g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
shows that it satisfies the following equation of motion

\displaystyle\int d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}\;{\cal K}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2};\tau_{3},\tau_{4})\;g(\tau_{3},\tau_{4})=s(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}),

(4.18)

and one can use this as an identity to evaluate the first term in (
3.6
) which breaks the reparametrization symmetry of the action explicitly. From the form of the kernel above it may be seen that it transforms under a scaling as
\mathcal{K}\sim|\tau|^{-4+4/q}
. This may prompt one to consider that
g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
has the form
g(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\propto\operatorname{sgn}{(\tau_{12})|\tau_{12}|^{-\frac{4}{q}}}
,
which contradicts with the desired
\delta^{\prime}
-source of (
4.18
). This clearly implies that the
\delta^{\prime}
-source can only be matched at the non-perturbative level as argued in
[
54
,
55
]
. In order to rectify this one can consider an ansatz for the first order perturbative field
g(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
as

\displaystyle g(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})=\mathcal{B}\frac{\operatorname{sgn}{(\tau_{12})}}{|\tau_{12}|^{\frac{2}{q}+2h}}

(4.19)

where
h
is a positive definite parameter and
\mathcal{B}
is a normalization constant that is fixed numerically as demonstrated in
[
55
]
. With such an ansatz for the
g(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
field one can now evaluate the left hand side of the (
4.18
). For the first term in the expression of
{\cal K}
, the integral on the left hand side in (
4.18
) is given as

\displaystyle\int d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}G_{c}^{-1}(\tau_{1},\tau_{3})G_{c}^{-1}(\tau_{2},\tau_{4})g(\tau_{3},\tau_{4})

\displaystyle=\left[\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\frac{2^{q-1}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q(1+\lambda)}\right]^{2}b^{2q-2}\int d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}\frac{{\rm sgn}(\tau_{13})\,{\rm sgn}(\tau_{24})\,{\rm sgn}(\tau_{34})}{|\tau_{13}|^{2\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\,|\tau_{24}|^{2\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\,|\tau_{34}|^{2\left(h+\frac{1}{q}\right)}}

(4.20)

which can be further evaluated by using the following integral

\displaystyle\int d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}\ \frac{{\rm sgn}(\tau_{13})\,{\rm sgn}(\tau_{24})\,{\rm sgn}(\tau_{34})}{|\tau_{13}|^{2\Delta}\,|\tau_{24}|^{2\Delta}\,|\tau_{34}|^{2\alpha}}=

\displaystyle\,-\pi^{2}\left[\frac{\sin(2\pi\alpha)\,+\,2\sin(2\pi(\alpha+\Delta))\,+\,\sin(2\pi(\alpha+2\Delta))}{\sin(2\pi\alpha)\sin(2\pi\Delta)\sin(2\pi(\alpha+\Delta))\sin(2\pi(\alpha+2\Delta))}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{\Big[\sin(2\pi\Delta)+\sin(2\pi(\alpha+\Delta))\Big]\Gamma(1-2\Delta)}{\Gamma(2\alpha)\Gamma(2\Delta)\Gamma(3-2\alpha-4\Delta)}\frac{{\rm sgn}(\tau_{12})}{|\tau_{12}|^{2\alpha+4\Delta-2}}\,.

(4.21)

for
\Delta=1-1/q
and
\alpha=h+1/q
as given in
[
54
,
55
]
. Thus the full results for the integral in (
4.18
) can be written as

\displaystyle s(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})=

\displaystyle\int d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}\;{\cal K}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2};\tau_{3},\tau_{4})\;g(\tau_{3},\tau_{4}),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\;2^{q-1}b^{q-2}\mathcal{B}\;\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\;\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}(q-1)}{q(1+\lambda)}\;\gamma(h,q)\;\frac{\operatorname{sgn}{(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})}}{|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{2-\frac{2}{q}+2h}}\right),

(4.22)

where

\displaystyle\gamma(h,q)=1-\frac{\pi\Gamma\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)\csc{\left[\pi\left(h+\frac{1}{q}\right)\right]}\sec{\left[\pi\left(h-\frac{1}{q}\right)\right]}}{q\;\Gamma\left(3-\frac{2}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(2h+\frac{2}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{2}{q}-2h-1\right)},

(4.23)

and it may be noted that the function
\gamma(h,q)
vanishes for
h=1/2
. Expanding the function
\gamma(h,q)
around
h=1/2
one can approximate it as

\displaystyle\gamma(h,q)=\frac{6q\;\overline{\gamma}}{(q-1)b^{q-1}}\left(h-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(h-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\right),\quad\overline{\gamma}=\frac{(q-1)b^{q-1}}{6q}\gamma^{\prime}\left(h=\frac{1}{2},q\right),

(4.24)

where
\gamma^{\prime}
is the derivative of
\gamma
function in (
4.23
) with respect to h. Thus expanding the right hand side of (
4.22
) around
h=1/2
and using (
4.24
), the source
s(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
can now be given as

\displaystyle s(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\simeq

\displaystyle\left(2^{q-1}6\;q\;b^{-1}\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\;\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\;\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q(1+\lambda)}\frac{\operatorname{sgn}{(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})}}{|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{2-\frac{2}{q}+2h}}\right)\left(h-\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(h-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\right),

(4.25)

which implies that in the limit
h\to 1/2
, the source vanishes and the condition in (
4.18
) becomes homogeneous. In this sense, the source
s(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
is said to be off-shell regularized slightly away from the
h=1/2
value.

Plugging back the form of the source (
4.25
) obtained above in the action (
3.6
) and then taking the limit
h\to 1/2
, one can see that the second and fourth terms vanish trivially in its expression. In this limit, the action
S
can now be given as

\displaystyle\frac{S}{N}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{c}[G_{c}]-\frac{1}{2}\lim_{h\to\frac{1}{2}}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\;G_{c}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\left(2^{q-1}6\;q\;b^{-1}\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\;\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\;\right.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q(1+\lambda)}\;\frac{\operatorname{sgn}{(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})}}{|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{2-\frac{2}{q}+2h}}\left(h-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(h-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\right)\right),

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{S}_{c}[G_{c}]-2^{q-1}6\;q\;b^{-1}\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\;\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q(1+\lambda)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~\left(\lim_{h\to\frac{1}{2}}\left(h-\frac{1}{2}\right)\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\;\frac{\operatorname{sgn}{(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})}}{|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{2+2h}}\right),

(4.26)

where the only nontrivial contribution comes from the second term in the above expression. As elucidated earlier, the above action remains invariant under the transformation
\tau\to\phi(\tau)
where
\phi(\tau)\in SL(2,R)
. These symmetry modes can be uplifted to become dynamical variables using the Faddeev-Popov method suggested in
[
54
,
55
]
which at the end gives us the following partition function corresponding to the
\phi(\tau)
modes

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\lambda,\mathcal{J})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\exp{\left[-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}-\frac{S}{N}\right]},

(4.27)

where

\displaystyle\frac{S}{N}=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{S}_{c}[G^{\phi}_{c}]-2^{q-1}6\;q\;b^{-1}\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\;\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q(1+\lambda)}

\displaystyle\left(\lim_{h\to\frac{1}{2}}\left(h-\frac{1}{2}\right)\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\;\left(\frac{\sqrt{\left|\phi(\tau_{1})\phi(\tau_{2})\right|}}{|\phi(\tau_{1})-\phi(\tau_{2})|}\right)^{2+2h}\operatorname{sgn}{(\phi(\tau_{1})-\phi(\tau_{2}))}\right),

(4.28)

for which we have made a change to the integration variable from
\tau\to\phi(\tau)
in the action (
4.26
). It was shown in
[
54
,
55
]
that the second term in (
4.28
) actually gives the Schwarzian action as the integral under parenthesis has a simple pole at
h=1/2
which exactly cancels out with the
(h-1/2)
factor present in the source
s(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
giving a finite result. Thus following
[
54
,
55
]
we have

\displaystyle\frac{S}{N}=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{S}_{c}[G^{\phi}_{c}]-\frac{\mathcal{B}\overline{\gamma}}{2\sqrt{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\frac{2^{q-1}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q(1+\lambda)}}}\int d\tau\{\phi(\tau),\tau\},

(4.29)

where the second term

\displaystyle\int d\tau\{\phi(\tau),\tau\}=\int d\tau\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\tau)}{\phi^{\prime}(\tau)}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(\tau)}{\phi^{\prime}(\tau)}\right)^{2}\right),

(4.30)

in the above expression is the zero temperature Schwarzian action and the prime denotes the derivative with respect to the
\tau
-coordinate. For a small but finite temperature case, using the following transformation

\displaystyle\phi(\tau)\to\tan{\left(\frac{\pi\phi(\tau)}{\beta}\right)},

(4.31)

one can also obtain the finite temperature version of the action in (
4.29
) as

\displaystyle\frac{S}{N}=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{S}_{c}[G^{\phi}_{c}]-\frac{\mathcal{B}\overline{\gamma}}{2\sqrt{\left(1+i\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(1+\lambda)}{N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\frac{2^{q-1}\mathcal{J}^{2}}{q(1+\lambda)}}}\left(\int\limits_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\left\{\tan{\left[\frac{\pi\phi(\tau)}{\beta}\right]},\tau\right\}\right).

(4.32)

In order to determine the Schwarzian action for the SYK model with non-Gaussian disorder we evaluate the partition function in (
4.27
) for the action (
4.29
) given above,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\lambda,\mathcal{J})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\exp{\left[-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}-\frac{S}{N}+\mathcal{S}_{c}[G^{\phi}_{c}]\right]},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\widetilde{M}\exp\left[-\frac{\widetilde{M}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\sqrt{\;q(1+\lambda)}}{\mathcal{J}\sqrt{2^{q+1}}}\left(1-i\sqrt{\frac{\lambda(1+\lambda)}{2N^{q-1}}}\;\widetilde{M}\right)\right.

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\left.\int d\tau\{\phi(\tau),\tau\}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N^{q-1}}\right)\right],

(4.33)

where in the second line we have made a large
N
expansion in order to approximate the integral. Thus on integrating over the auxiliary field
\widetilde{M}
one arrives at the following effective action

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{I}_{sch}}{N}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\sqrt{\;q(1+\lambda)}}{\mathcal{J}\sqrt{2^{q+1}}}\int d\tau\{\phi(\tau),\tau\}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda\;q\;\mathcal{B}^{2}\;\overline{\gamma}^{2}(1+\lambda)^{2}}{2^{q+3}\mathcal{J}^{2}N^{q-1}}\int\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\{\phi(\tau),\tau\}\{\phi(\tau^{\prime}),\tau^{\prime}\},

(4.34)

for the non-Gaussian SYK model in the zero temperature regime. However, in the near-infrared (small but finite temperature) regime, one can use the transformation in (
4.31
) to obtain the finite temperature version of the above action.

4.1
Out of time order correlators (OTOC) and Chaos

An important aspect of the Schwarzian theory is its maximally chaotic behavior
[
20
]
which is usually captured by the out-of-time order correlation function. To see this we first focus on the following partition function for the Gaussian SYK (GS) model in the near-infrared regime

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{GS}=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\mu(\phi)}{SL(2,R)}\;\exp\left[\frac{\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\sqrt{q}}{\mathcal{J}\sqrt{2^{q+1}}}\int d\tau\left\{\tan{\left[\frac{\pi\phi(\tau)}{\beta}\right]},\tau\right\}\right],

(4.35)

where
d\mu(\phi)
is the appropriate measure for the reparametrization modes
\phi(\tau)\in SL(2,R)
. The effective action corresponding to the above partition function can now be given as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{I}^{GS}}{N}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\sqrt{q}}{\mathcal{J}\sqrt{2^{q+1}}}\int\limits_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\left(\left\{\phi(\tau),\tau\right\}+\frac{2\pi^{2}\phi^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\right),

(4.36)

which is obtained by first taking the transformation (
4.31
) and then setting
\lambda=0
in (
4.34
). The mode
\phi(\tau)
satisfies the periodic boundary conditions
\phi(\tau+\beta)=\phi(\tau)
. The periodic boundary condition helps us to write the Schwarzian action in (
4.36
) as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{I}^{GS}}{N}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\sqrt{q}}{\mathcal{J}\sqrt{2^{q+1}}}\int\limits_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(\tau)}{\phi^{\prime}(\tau)}\right)^{2}+\frac{2\pi^{2}\phi^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\right),

(4.37)

We now use the following expansion

\displaystyle\phi(\tau)=\tau+\epsilon(\tau),\quad\epsilon(\tau+\beta)=\epsilon(\tau),

(4.38)

which gives the action in (
4.37
) to the leading order in
\epsilon(\tau)
as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{I}_{\epsilon}^{GS}}{N}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\sqrt{q}}{\mathcal{J}\sqrt{2^{q+1}}}\int\limits_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\left(\frac{4\pi^{2}\epsilon^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}}{\beta^{2}}-\epsilon^{\prime\prime}(\tau)^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\right),

(4.39)

where in writing above we have dropped off a constant term appearing in the above action. Rescaling the time coordinate as
\tau\to 2\pi\tau/\beta
, the above effective action can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{I}_{\epsilon}^{GS}}{N}

\displaystyle=-\frac{8\pi^{3}}{\beta^{3}}\frac{\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\sqrt{q}}{\mathcal{J}\sqrt{2^{q+1}}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}d\tau\left(\epsilon^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}-\epsilon^{\prime\prime}(\tau)^{2}\right),

(4.40)

whose corresponding equation of motion can be given by

\displaystyle\epsilon^{(4)}(\tau)+\epsilon^{\prime\prime}(\tau)=0.

(4.41)

The solutions to the above equation of motion for the periodic boundary condition in (
4.38
) are given by
\epsilon(\tau)=e^{in\tau}
, with
n=\left\{0,\pm 1\right\}
. Moreover, the two-point function for the
\epsilon(\tau)
field can now be obtained as

\displaystyle G^{GS}_{\epsilon}(\tau,0)=\left\langle\epsilon(\tau)\epsilon(0)\right\rangle_{GS}=\frac{\beta^{3}}{8\pi^{3}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\sqrt{q}}{\mathcal{J}\sqrt{2^{q+1}}}\right)^{-1}\sum\limits_{\left|n\right|\geqslant 2}\frac{e^{in\tau}}{n^{2}(n^{2}-1)},

(4.42)

which has poles at
n=\left\{0,\pm 1\right\}
, corresponding to the unbroken
SL(2,R)
gauge symmetry and so we should not sum over these modes. The sum in the above propagator can be evaluated as a contour integral where the contour is taken such that it only encircles the poles at
n=\left\{0,\pm 1\right\}
as shown in the figure. This gives us a Matsubara integral for the frequencies
\omega=in
, as follows

\displaystyle\left\langle\epsilon(\tau)\epsilon(0)\right\rangle_{GS}=\frac{\beta^{3}}{8\pi^{3}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\sqrt{q}}{\mathcal{J}\sqrt{2^{q+1}}}\right)^{-1}\int\limits_{\mathcal{C}}d\omega\;\frac{e^{\omega\tau}}{e^{2\pi\omega}-1}\left(\frac{1}{\omega^{2}(\omega^{2}+1)}\right),

(4.43)

which in turn can be evaluated as a sum of contour integrals around the poles
\omega=\left\{0,\pm i\right\}
as

\displaystyle\left\langle\epsilon(\tau)\epsilon(0)\right\rangle_{GS}=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{3}}{16\pi^{3}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\sqrt{q}}{\mathcal{J}\sqrt{2^{q+1}}}\right)^{-1}\left(-5\cos\left(\frac{2\pi\tau}{\beta}\right)+\frac{4\pi^{2}\tau^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(2\pi\sin\left(\frac{2\pi\tau}{\beta}\right)+\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3}-2\right)-\frac{4\left(\pi\tau\sin\left(\frac{2\pi\tau}{\beta}\right)+\pi^{2}\tau\right)}{\beta}\right).

(4.44)

Having determined the
\epsilon
-propagator, we now move to evaluate the two and four-point functions for the operators
V(\tau)
and
W(\tau)
with conformal dimensions
\Delta_{v}
and
\Delta_{w}
which are made up of the product of
Majorana fermion bilinears for the usual Gaussian SYK model. First, we consider the thermal two-point function for the field
V(\phi)
which can be obtained from (
4.9
) as

\displaystyle\left\langle V(\tau_{1})V(\tau_{2})\right\rangle_{GS}

\displaystyle=b\left[\frac{\pi}{\beta\sin\left(\frac{\pi\tau_{12}}{\beta}\right)}\right]^{2\Delta_{V}},

(4.45)

then on using the transformation
\tau\to\phi(\tau)
, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle V(\phi_{1})V(\phi_{2})\right\rangle_{GS}

\displaystyle=b\left[\frac{\pi\sqrt{\phi^{\prime}(\tau_{1})\phi^{\prime}(\tau_{2})}}{\beta\sin\left(\frac{\pi\left(\phi(\tau_{1})-\phi(\tau_{2})\right)}{\beta}\right)}\right]^{2\Delta_{V}},\quad\beta>\tau_{12}>0

\displaystyle\simeq\left\langle V(\tau_{1})V(\tau_{2})\right\rangle_{GS}\left(1+\frac{\delta\left\langle V(\tau_{1})V(\tau_{2})\right\rangle_{GS}}{\left\langle V(\tau_{1})V(\tau_{2})\right\rangle_{GS}}\right),

(4.46)

where
\phi_{i}=\phi(\tau_{i})
,
\tau_{ij}=(\tau_{i}-\tau_{j})
, and

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\left\langle V(\tau_{1})V(\tau_{2})\right\rangle_{GS}}{\left\langle V(\tau_{1})V(\tau_{2})\right\rangle_{GS}}=\Delta_{V}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}(\tau_{1})+\epsilon^{\prime}(\tau_{2})-\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\;\left(\frac{\epsilon(\tau_{1})-\epsilon(\tau_{2})}{\tan\left(\frac{\pi\tau_{12}}{\beta}\right)}\right)\right).

(4.47)

In the second line of (
4.46
) considering
\phi(\tau)=\tau+\epsilon(\tau)
, we have expanded the two-point function to the linear order in
\epsilon
. In a similar way as above, one can also obtain the thermal two-point function for the
W(\phi)
field. In the two-point function for the field
V
, we now substitute
\epsilon(\tau)=e^{i\frac{2\pi n\tau}{\beta}}
which gives us

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\left\langle V(\tau_{1})V(\tau_{2})\right\rangle_{GS}}{\left\langle V(\tau_{1})V(\tau_{2})\right\rangle_{GS}}

\displaystyle=i\frac{4\pi\Delta_{V}}{\beta}\operatorname{sgn}{(n)}\;e^{i\frac{\pi n(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})}{\beta}}\left(\left|n\right|\cos{\left(\frac{\left|n\right|\pi\tau_{12}}{\beta}\right)}-\frac{\sin{\left(\frac{\left|n\right|\pi\tau_{12}}{\beta}\right)}}{\tan{\left(\frac{\pi\tau_{12}}{\beta}\right)}}\right),

(4.48)

which vanishes for the modes corresponding to
n=\left\{0,\pm 1\right\}
. As we will see later that this relation will be important in determining the four-point function for the
V
and
W
fields.

We next consider the four-point function for the fields
V
and
W
which in the large N limit can be given as

\displaystyle\left\langle V(\tau_{1})V(\tau_{2})W(\tau_{3})W(\tau_{4})\right\rangle_{GS}\simeq

\displaystyle\left\langle V(\tau_{1})V(\tau_{2})\right\rangle_{GS}\left\langle W(\tau_{3})W(\tau_{4})\right\rangle_{GS}\begin{cases}\left(1+N\mathcal{F}^{TO}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4})\right),&0\leqslant\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\tau_{3}<\tau_{4}\leqslant\beta\\
\pm\left(1+N\mathcal{F}^{OTO}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4})\right),&0\leqslant\tau_{1}<\tau_{3}<\tau_{2}<\tau_{4}\leqslant\beta\end{cases},

(4.49)

where the superscript
TO
stands for the time-ordered four-point function and the superscript
OTO
stands for the out-of-time ordered four-point function. The leading correction to the four-point function
\mathcal{F}
denotes the connected part of the four-point function
[
20
,
36
]
. In the time-ordered case the function
\mathcal{F}^{TO}
can be given as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{TO}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\frac{\delta\left\langle V(\tau_{1})V(\tau_{2})\right\rangle_{GS}}{\left\langle V(\tau_{1})V(\tau_{2})\right\rangle_{GS}}\right)\left(\frac{\delta\left\langle W(\tau_{3})W(\tau_{3})\right\rangle_{GS}}{\left\langle W(\tau_{3})W(\tau_{4})\right\rangle_{GS}}\right)\right\rangle,

\displaystyle=\frac{2\Delta_{V}\Delta_{W}}{\pi}\left(\frac{\mathcal{B}\;\overline{\gamma}\sqrt{q}}{\mathcal{J}\sqrt{2^{q+1}}}\right)^{-1}\sum\limits_{\left|n\right|\geqslant 2}\frac{e^{i\frac{\pi n\xi_{+}}{\beta}}}{n^{2}(n^{2}-1)}\left(\left|n\right|\cos{\left(\frac{\left|n\right|\pi\xi}{\beta}\right)}-\frac{\sin{\left(\frac{\left|n\right|\pi\xi}{\beta}\right)}}{\tan{\left(\frac{\pi\xi}{\beta}\right)}}\right)

5
Summary and Discussion

The study of the effects of non-Gaussian quenched disorder on the SYK model with Majorana fermions within the framework of large N expansion reveals intriguing insights into the behavior of this complex system. The non-Gaussian disorder averaging induces modification of the variance of the Gaussian distribution of couplings to the leading order in N. This also gives rise to a subdominant non-local term in the large N effective action. These findings are also observed in the case of the SYK model with flavored complex fermions, suggesting a broader applicability of introducing non-Gaussian disorder. Exploring the physical quantities in the low-temperature regime emphasizes that, despite its non-Gaussian nature, the model retains considerable similarity to the Gaussian SYK model at leading order in N. A particularly notable result is the insensitivity of the Lyapunov exponent to non-Gaussianity, underscoring the model’s robust chaotic behavior. In the large N and large q limit one uncovers a connection between the non-Gaussian SYK model and a non-local Liouville field theory. This invokes intriguing parallels to ideas proposed by Veneziano and Coleman in the context of nonlocal Euclidean quantum gravity theory with wormholes solutions. The presence of wormhole solutions suggests that the cosmological constant can be tuned to be very small which we have demonstrated for the non-local Liouville field theory dual to the non-Gaussian SYK model. Overall, this investigation demonstrates the intricate interplay between non-Gaussian SYK model, two-dimensional non local Euclidean quantum gravity, and its wormhole solutions which further expands our comprehension of such complex quantum systems and their gravity duals.

One can also investigate several future directions. First, one may consider investigating the Coleman’s mechanism in the case of two coupled SYK model discussed in
[
17
]
. It would also be interesting to explore the same mechanism for a large number of coupled SYK models that might describe a two-dimensional multiverse. One can then use this multiverse model to explain the observed small cosmological constant. Finally, our 2D gravity action is classical. In
[
53
]
it was shown that quantum fluctuations in the double scaling limit of SYK modify the geometry. It would be interesting to repeat such a calculation in the non-Gaussian disorder setting we suggested. We leave these interesting avenues for future works.

Functional Integral approach to time-dependent heat exchange in open quantum systems: general method and applications
M Carrega, P Solinas, A Braggio, M Sassetti, and U Weiss

Abstract
We establish the path integral approach for the time-dependent heat exchange of an externally driven quantum system coupled to a thermal reservoir. We derive the relevant influence functional and present an exact formal expression for the moment generating functional which carries all statistical properties of the heat exchange process for general linear dissipation. The general method is applied to the time-dependent average heat transfer in the dissipative two-state system. We show that the heat can be written as a convolution integral which involves the population and coherence correlation functions of the two-state system and additional correlations due to a polarization of the reservoir. The corresponding expression can be solved in the weak-damping limit both for white noise and for quantum mechanical coloured noise. The implications of pure quantum effects are discussed. Altogether a complete description of the dynamics of the average heat transfer ranging from the classical regime down to zero temperature is achieved.

A complementary approach is to measure the energy, or heat, dissipated because of interactions between the system and an environment. In this case, it is essential to study the degrees of freedom of the environment itself. In this direction, a promising experimental proposal was put forward in Ref.

We will approach the issue with functional integral techniques.
In such a formalism, all quantum effects of the environment on a microscopic system, which are
related to energy transfer, can be included in a specific influence functional of the coordinates of the system only.
Within the requirements of a linear environment all conceivable quantum noise sources may be accounted for

The central problem of this study is to develop a general formalism for
finding all statistical features of the energy transfer between the system and the environment.
In order to investigate the properties and advantages of this
approach, and to map out potential of the method we will apply it to a specific quantum systems.

As in the context of dissipative quantum dynamics

the influence functional does not depend on the particular quantum system under consideration. In addition, this

2
Model and general settings

We consider a quantum mechanical particle of mass
M
, position
q
and momentum
p
moving in a time-dependent potential
V(q,t)
. We assume that the particle is coupled to a thermal bath, modelled as a set of
N
independent harmonic oscillators
[
39
,
22
,
23
,
40
]
.
The Hamiltonians of the system S and reservoir R are

\displaystyle H_{\rm S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p^{2}}{2M}+{V(q,t)}\,,

(1)

\displaystyle H_{\rm R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\Big[\frac{p_{\alpha}^{2}}{2m_{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{2}m_{\alpha}\omega^{2}_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}^{2}\Big]~,

(2)

where
x_{\alpha}
and
p_{\alpha}
are position and momentum of the harmonic oscillator
\alpha
, respectively.
The system-reservoir coupling is chosen to be bi-linear in the positions variables of system and reservoir.
It reads

H_{\rm I}=-q\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}c_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\frac{c_{\alpha}^{2}}{m_{\alpha}\omega_{\alpha}^{2}}q^{2}~,

(3)

with
c_{\alpha}
the interaction strength.
The second term in
H_{\rm I}
has been added to compensate renormalization of the potential
V(q)
caused by the bi-linear coupling term in
H_{\rm I}

[
22
,
23
]
.
All effects of the reservoir on the system induced by the coupling
H_{\rm I}

are captured by the spectral density of the coupling

J(\omega)=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\frac{c_{\alpha}^{2}}{m_{\alpha}\omega_{\alpha}}\delta(\omega-\omega_{\alpha})~.

(4)

Being interested in the continuum limit
N\to\infty
, we consider smooth
spectral densities of the coupling with power-law behaviour at low frequencies and spectral cut-off, e.g., exponential cutoff, at high frequencies,

J(\omega)=M\gamma_{s}\omega\left(\frac{\omega}{\tilde{\omega}}\right)^{s-1}e^{-\omega/\omega_{\rm c}}~.

(5)

Here,
\gamma_{s}
is the coupling constant,
\tilde{\omega}
a reference frequency and
\omega_{\rm c}
the
high-frequency cut-off.
The important case of Ohmic damping is represented by
s=1
with
M\gamma_{1}\equiv\eta
being the viscosity coefficient. The regime
s>1
(
0<s<1
) describes super- (sub-) Ohmic dissipation
[
41
,
22
,
34
,
37
]
.
In the sequel we consistently use units in which
k_{\rm B}=\hbar=1
.

Our goal is to determine the amount and the statistics of the exchanged energy
between the system and the reservoir in a time interval
t
(hereafter we put the initial time
t_{0}=0
).
This information may be obtained by performing a projective measurement of the energy of the environment both at the beginning and at the end of the time interval.
Different kinds of the preparation of the initial state are feasible.
In the following, we consider the density matrix of the composite system at time zero in factorized form
[
22
]
,

\rho(0)=\rho_{\rm S}(0)\otimes\rho_{\rm R}(0)\,.

(6)

We assume that
\rho_{\rm R}(0)
is the canonical density of the unperturbed reservoir,

\rho_{\rm R}(0)=\frac{1}{Z_{\rm R}}e^{-H_{\rm R}/T}\,,

(7)

where
Z_{\rm R}
is its partition function.
Then
H_{\rm R}
commutes with
\rho(0)
, and thus the initial measurement
of the heat of the reservoir does not affect the dynamics.
With this assumption, we can formulate the conditional probability
P(Q,t)
for the output of the two measures and, therefore, for the dissipated heat
Q

[
19
]
.

The central quantity of interest, which includes the entire statistics of the heat transfer process,
is the characteristic function
G_{\nu}(t)
defined as Fourier transform of the probability distribution
P(Q,t)
,

G_{\nu}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm d}Q\,P(Q,t)\,{e}^{i\nu Q}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\nu)^{n}}{n!}\langle\,Q^{n}(t)\,\rangle\,.

(8)

Because of the second equality, the function
G_{\nu}(t)
is referred to as the moment generating function (MGF).

The
n
-th derivative of the MGF at
\nu=0
gives the
n
th moment,

\langle Q^{n}(t)\rangle=\left.(-i)^{n}\frac{d^{n}}{d\nu^{n}}G_{\nu}(t)\right|_{\nu=0}\,.

(9)

The first moment,
n=1
, yields the average heat transferred to the reservoir
[
19
]
,

\langle Q(t)\rangle=\left.-i\frac{dG_{\nu}(t)}{d\nu}\right|_{\nu=0}~.

(10)

A major advantage of using the MGF is that it can be written as the
trace of a generalized density operator. Thus, known techniques can be applied to solve the dynamics.
Following Ref.
[
19
]
, we may write

G_{\nu}(t)={\rm Tr}[e^{i\nu H_{\rm R}}U(t,0)e^{-i\nu H_{\rm R}}\rho(0)U^{\dagger}(t,0)]\,,

(11)

where the trace is over all degrees of freedom and
U(t,0)
is the unitary evolution operator of the composite system,
i.e., the corresponding generator is the total Hamiltonian
H(t)=H_{\rm S}(t)+H_{\rm R}+H_{\rm I}
.

In Ref.
[
19
]
the MGF has been studied within the weak-coupling Born-Markov approximation
by solving a master equation associated with a generalized density matrix of the total system.
In this work we will advance significantly by investigating the MGF
G_{\nu}(t)
from a quite different perspective. In essence, we shall derive a path integral formulation for the characteristic function
G_{\nu}(t)
which may serve as a basis for the study of all statistical moments of the heat transfer process, as specified in Eq. (
9
).

We do not specify a priori the particular form of the initial state
\rho_{\rm S}(0)
. The only assumption we make for convenience is that the system-bath complex is initially in a product state and that the
system-bath interaction is switched on at time
t=0^{+}
. However, the approach envisaged here can easily be generalized to other scenarios of the initial preparation.

3
Path integral representation of the characteristic generating function
G_{\nu}(t)

In this section we take the route of obtaining a closed formal expression for the characteristic function
G_{\nu}(t)
.
It is advantageous to carry out the reduction of the dynamics of the system-plus-reservoir entity to the dynamics of the system alone within the functional integral description
[
22
,
23
]
, a technique introduced by Feynman and Vernon already in 1963
[
42
]
.
The present problem, however, is substantially different because of the additional operator terms
{e}^{i\nu H_{\rm R}}
and

{e}^{-i\nu H_{\rm R}}
in the original expression (
11
).

The coordinate representation of Eq. (
11
) reads

G_{\nu}(t)=\int d{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f}\int dq_{f}\mathinner{\langle{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f},q_{f}}|}e^{i\nu H_{\rm R}}U(t,0){\rho}^{(\nu)}(0)U^{\dagger}(t,0)\mathinner{|{{\bm{x}}_{f}^{\prime},q_{f}}\rangle}\,,

(12)

where the
N
-component vector
{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f}
stands for the bath coordinates
(x^{\prime}_{1f},....,x^{\prime}_{Nf})
,
and
q_{f}
represents a given position of the particle.
For convenience, the factor
{e}^{-i\nu H_{\rm R}}
in Eq. (
11
) is included in the initial density matrix of the reservoir

{\rho}^{(\nu)}(0)=\rho_{\rm S}(0)\otimes{\rho}^{(\nu)}_{\rm R}(0)

(13)

with the shifted canonical density operator of the reservoir

{\rho}^{(\nu)}_{\rm R}(0)=\frac{1}{Z_{\rm R}}{\rm e}^{-H_{\rm R}/T-i\,\nu H_{\rm R}}\,.

(14)

Using completeness of the position eigenstates we can rewrite
G_{\nu}(t)
as

\displaystyle G_{\nu}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d{\bm{x}}_{f}d{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f}d{\bm{x}}_{i}d{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{i}\int dq_{f}dq_{i}dq^{\prime}_{i}

(15)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\langle{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f}}|{{e}^{i\nu H_{R}}}|{{\bm{x}}_{f}}\rangle K({\bm{x}}_{f},q_{f},t;{\bm{x}}_{i},q_{i},0)\langle{{\bm{x}}_{i}q_{i}}|{{\rho^{(\nu)}}(0)}|{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{i}q^{\prime}_{i}}\rangle K^{*}({\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f},q_{f},t;{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{i},q^{\prime}_{i},0)~.

Here,

K({\bm{x}}_{f},q_{f},t;{\bm{x}}_{i},q_{i},0)\equiv\langle{{\bm{x}}_{f},q_{f}}|{U(t,0)}|{{\bm{x}}_{i},q_{i}}\rangle

(16)

is the probability amplitude for the transition from the initial state
i
to the final state
f
according to evolution with
U(t,0)
.
The path integral representation of the propagator is
[
22
,
42
,
23
]

K({\bm{x}}_{f},q_{f},t;{\bm{x}}_{i},q_{i},0)=\int\limits_{q(0)=q_{i}}^{q(t)=q_{f}}\!\!\!\!\!\mathcal{D}q\,{e}^{iS_{\rm S}[q]}\int\limits_{{\bm{x}}(0)={\bm{x}}_{i}}^{{\bm{x}}(t)={\bm{x}}_{f}}\!\!\!\!\!\mathcal{D}{\bm{x}}\,{e}^{i(S_{\rm R}[{\bm{x}}]+S_{\rm I}[q,{\bm{x}}])}\,.

(17)

Here,
S_{\rm S}[q]
is the action of the system,
S_{\rm R}[{\bm{x}}]
the action of the reservoir, and

S_{\rm I}[q,{\bm{x}}]
is the action associated with the interaction
H_{\rm I}[q,{\bm{x}}]
. With the
factorizing form (
13
) of
{\rho}^{(\nu)}(0)
one then obtains

G_{\nu}(t)=\int dq_{f}dq_{i}dq^{\prime}_{i}{\mathinner{\langle{q_{i}}|{\rho_{\rm S}(0)}|{q^{\prime}_{i}}\rangle}}J^{(\nu)}(q_{f},t;q_{i},q^{\prime}_{i},0)\,.

(18)

The propagating function
J^{(\nu)}
describes the time evolution of the RDM
resulting from the generalized density operator given in Eq. (
11
) upon integrating out the reservoir. It reads

J^{(\nu)}(q_{f},t;q_{i},q^{\prime}_{i},0)=\int\limits_{q(0)=q_{i}}^{q(t)=q_{f}}\!\!\mathcal{D}q\,e^{iS_{\rm S}[q]}\int\limits_{q^{\prime}(0)=q^{\prime}_{i}}^{q^{\prime}(t)=q_{f}}\!\!\!\!\mathcal{D}q^{\prime}\,e^{-iS_{\rm S}[q^{\prime}]}\mathcal{F}^{(\nu)}[q,q^{\prime}]\,.

(19)

For
\mathcal{F}^{(\nu)}[q,q^{\prime}]=1
,
J^{(\nu)}(q_{f},t;q_{i},q^{\prime}_{i},0)
describes propagation of the RDM in the absence of the system-reservoir interaction
H_{\rm I}
. This illustrates that the functional
\mathcal{F}^{(\nu)}[q,q^{\prime}]
is the influence functional
for the characteristic function
G_{\nu}(t)
. This functional carries all effects of the system-reservoir coupling
both on the dynamics of the reduced system and on the heat exchange process with the environment. The still formal expression reads

\mathcal{F}^{(\nu)}[q,q^{\prime}]=\int d{\bm{x}}_{f}d{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f}d{\bm{x}}_{i}d{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{i}{\mathinner{\langle{{\bm{x}}_{i}}|{{\rho}^{(\nu)}_{\rm R}(0)}|{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{i}}\rangle}}{\mathinner{\langle{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f}}|{e^{i\nu H_{\rm R}}}|{{\bm{x}}_{f}}\rangle}}F[q,{\bm{x}}_{f},{\bm{x}}_{i}]F^{*}[q^{\prime},{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f},{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{i}]\,.

(20)

Here the amplitude

F[q,{\bm{x}}_{f},{\bm{x}}_{i}]=\int\limits_{{\bm{x}}(0)={\bm{x}}_{i}}^{{\bm{x}}(t)={\bm{x}}_{f}}\!\!\mathcal{D}{\bm{x}}\,{e}^{i\,(S_{\rm R}[{\bm{x}}]+S_{\rm I}[q,{\bm{x}}])}

(21)

is the propagator of the bath in presence of the system-bath coupling
H_{\rm I}
given in Eq. (
3
).
In the limit
\nu\to 0
, the matrix element
{\mathinner{\langle{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f}}|{e^{i\nu H_{\rm R}}}|{{\bm{x}}_{f}}\rangle}}
would
reduce to
\prod_{\alpha}\delta(x^{\prime}_{\alpha f}-x_{\alpha f})
and hence
\mathcal{F}^{(\nu)}[q,q^{\prime}]
to the standard Feynman-Vernon
influence functional
\mathcal{F}^{(\nu=0)}[q,q^{\prime}]=\mathcal{F}_{\rm FV}[q,q^{\prime}]
.

The expression in Eq. (
18

\rho_{\rm S}(t)
reported in
[
22
]
in the context of quantum dissipative system. However, the substantial difference is

\mathinner{\langle{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f}}|}e^{i\nu H_{\rm R}}\mathinner{|{{\bm{x}}_{f}}\rangle}

\mathcal{F}^{(\nu)}[q,q^{\prime}]

\rho_{{\rm R}}^{(\nu)}
which entail independent integrations of the end/initial point
{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f/i}
and
{\bm{x}}_{f/i}

It is convenient to express the influence functional (
20
) in terms of the linear combinations

\eta(\tau)=[q(\tau)+q^{\prime}(\tau)]/q_{0}\,,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\xi(\tau)=[q(\tau)-q^{\prime}(\tau)]/q_{0}\,.

(22)

Here,
q_{0}
is a characteristic length of the system S, which is introduced to render
the paths
\eta(\tau)
and
\xi(\tau)
dimensionless.
The path
\eta(\tau)
, usually referred to as quasi-classical, measures propagation of the system along the diagonal of the density matrix, whereas the path
\xi(\tau)
measures off-diagonal excursions, i.e., quantum fluctuations.

In order to evaluate Eq. (
20
) we follow the standard approach
[
22
,
34
]
of gaussian path integral integrations.
The details of the calculation are presented in
A
.
With the resulting expressions (
81

94
) – (

\mathcal{F}^{(\nu)}[\eta,\xi]={e}^{-(\,\Phi^{(0)}_{\rm loc}[\eta,\xi]+\Phi^{(0)}_{\rm cor}[\eta,\xi]\,)}\;{\rm e}^{i\,\Delta\Phi^{(\nu)}[\eta,\xi]}~.

(23)

The influence actions of the first exponential factor are

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Phi^{(0)}_{\rm loc}[\eta,\xi]&=&{\displaystyle\;i\,\frac{\mu q_{0}^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\!dt^{\prime}\,\xi(t^{\prime})\eta(t^{\prime})\quad\;\;\mbox{with}\quad\;\;\mu=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!d\omega\,\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega}\,,}\\[11.38109pt]
\Phi^{(0)}_{\rm cor}[\eta,\xi]&=&{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\!\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\!\!dt^{\prime\prime}\,\xi(t^{\prime})[L^{\prime}(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})\xi(t^{\prime\prime})+i\,L^{\prime\prime}(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})\eta(t^{\prime\prime})]\,.}\end{array}

The local action
\Phi^{(0)}_{\rm loc}[\eta,\xi]
originates from the potential counterterm in the interaction (
3
). The nonlocal term
\Phi^{(0)}_{\rm cor}[\eta,\xi]
is the standard Feynman-Vernon influence action functional which governs decoherence and relaxation of the reduced system

The real part of
\Phi^{(0)}_{\rm cor}[\eta,\xi]
describes time-correlations of the quantum fluctuations, and
the imaginary part describes time-correlations between quasiclassical propagation and
subsequent off-diagonal excursions. The kernel
L(\tau)
is the equilibrium time-correlation function of the
collective bath mode
{\cal E}(t)=q_{0}\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}(t)
,

L(\tau)=L^{\prime}(\tau)+iL^{\prime\prime}(\tau)=\frac{q_{0}^{2}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!d\omega\,J(\omega)\Big[\,\coth\Big(\frac{\omega}{2T}\Big)\cos\omega\tau-i\sin\omega\tau\,\Big]\,.

(25)

The imaginary part

is proportional to the time derivative of the friction kernel

M\gamma(\tau)
in the associated classical equation of motion (

is the inertial mass of the system),

\Theta(\tau)\,L^{\prime\prime}(\tau)=\frac{Mq_{0}^{2}}{2}\,\dot{\gamma}(\tau)\,.

(26)

The second exponential factor in Eq. (

\displaystyle\Delta\Phi^{(\nu)}[\eta,\xi]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\!dt^{\prime}\!\!\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\!\!dt^{\prime\prime}\!\left\{\big[\eta(t^{\prime})\eta(t^{\prime\prime})-\xi(t^{\prime})\xi(t^{\prime\prime})\big]L^{(\nu)}_{1}(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})\right.

(27)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\left.+\;\big[\eta(t^{\prime})\xi(t^{\prime\prime})-\xi(t^{\prime})\eta(t^{\prime\prime})\big]L^{(\nu)}_{2}(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})\right\}\,.

carries in addition to the time-correlations of the aforementioned types also time-correlations between quasiclassical propagation at different time

\begin{array}[]{rcl}L^{(\nu)}_{1}(\tau)&=&\;{\displaystyle\frac{q_{0}^{2}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!d\omega\,\frac{\sin(\nu\,\omega/2)\sinh[(\omega/T+i\,\nu\,\omega)/2]}{\sinh[\omega/(2T)]}\,J(\omega)\cos(\omega\tau)}\,,\\[14.22636pt]

&&\qquad\qquad\quad\left.+\,i\,\big[\eta(t^{\prime})\xi(t^{\prime\prime})-\xi(t^{\prime})\eta(t^{\prime\prime})\big]\dot{L}^{\prime}(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})\right\}\,,\end{array}

3
) for

\Delta\Phi^{(\nu)}[\eta,\xi]
and
\Phi^{(1)}[\eta,\xi]
, respectively,
are the main results of this section. They hold for general linear dissipation with
any type of memory-friction.

We conclude this section with a short additional consideration. Firstly, we see that the term
in (
3
) which describes time correlations between quasiclassical paths
yields a contribution to
\langle Q(t)\rangle
even if
the system is frozen in its initial position.
The constant path
q(\tau)=q^{\prime}(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}\eta_{i}q_{0}
yields the contribution

\langle\delta Q(t)\rangle=\int d\eta_{i}\,p(\eta_{i})\langle\delta Q_{i}(t)\rangle\qquad\mbox{with}\qquad\langle\delta Q_{i}(t)\rangle=\Phi^{(1)}[\eta_{i},0]\,.

(31)

Here
p(\eta_{i})=\mathinner{\langle{\eta_{i}}|}\rho_{\rm S}(0)\mathinner{|{\eta_{i}}\rangle}
is the occupation probability
of the initial state
\eta_{i}
,
upon using the relation (
26
), we can relate the heat portion
\langle\delta Q_{i}(t)\rangle
to the
friction kernel
M\gamma(t)
,

\langle\delta Q_{i}(t)\rangle=\frac{\eta_{i}^{2}q_{0}^{2}}{4}M\,\big[\,\gamma(0^{+})-\gamma(t)\,\big]\,.

(32)

Remarkably, the heat portion (
31
) generally occurs when the system dynamics is slow
on the scale
1/\omega_{\rm c}
.

Evidently, for the thermal initial state (
7
) of the uncoupled reservoir, the heat contribution

\langle\delta Q_{i}(t)\rangle
depends on the particular initial state
\eta_{i}
of the system. In fact, when the coupling

H_{\rm I}[\eta_{i}q_{0}/2,{\bm{x}}]
is switched on at time zero, the thermal state of the uncoupled
reservoir relaxes to a shifted canonical state.
The initial slip (
31
) on the time scale
1/\omega_{\rm c}
represents the respective heat transfer.
If we had calculated the functional
\Phi^{(1)}[\eta,\xi]
for a canonical initial state with
shifted oscillator positions,
x_{\alpha}-\bar{\eta}q_{0}c_{\alpha}/(2m_{\alpha}\omega_{\alpha}^{2})
, and had subtracted
the irrelevant constant polarization energy
(\bar{\eta}q_{0})^{2}\,\mu/8
, the heat contribution would be

\langle\delta Q_{i}(t)\rangle=\frac{q_{0}^{2}}{4}\,\eta_{i}(\eta_{i}-\bar{\eta})M\,\big[\,\gamma(0^{+})-\gamma(t)\,\big]\,.

(33)

With the choice
\bar{\eta}=\eta_{i}
the initial slip term (
33
) would be absent. The relevant shifted thermal initial state can be arranged, e.g., by trapping the system at times
t<0
in the particular state

\eta(t)=\eta_{i}
and releasing the constraint at time zero. Keeping this in mind, we disregard the
initial slip contribution in the sequel.

4
Application to the spin-boson model: the average heat transfer

Consider a particle moving in a double well potential and in contact with its environment and assume that the thermal energy is small compared to the energy splitting in the individual wells. Then only
the two lowest states become relevant to the dynamics.
This scenario is conveniently described by the spin-boson model. It is of great interest in different fields
ranging from nuclear magnetic systems to
quantum optics
[
34
,
24
,
25
,
35
]
.

We now apply the method developed in the previous section to the driven
spin-boson model
[
22
,
34
,
35
]
. Here our interest is focused
on the transferred average heat and on the heat power.
The Hamiltonian of the two-state system (TSS) in pseudo-spin representation is

H_{\rm S}=-\frac{\Delta}{2}\sigma_{x}-\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\sigma_{z}~.

(34)

The state basis is formed by the two localized states
\mathinner{|{R}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|{L}\rangle}
of the double well, which are eigenstates of
\sigma_{z}
with eigenvalues
+1
and
-1
, respectively. The position operator is
q=q_{0}\sigma_{z}/2
and has
eigenvalues
\pm q_{0}/2
being associated with the minima of the double-well potential
V(q)
. The tunneling operator

\sigma_{x}=\mathinner{|{R}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{L}|}-\mathinner{|{L}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{R}|}
transfers the particle between the two wells with tunneling amplitude
\Delta
, while

\epsilon(t)=\epsilon_{0}+\epsilon_{1}(t)

(35)

describes an externally applied bias with a constant part
\epsilon_{0}
, and a driving term
\epsilon_{1}(t)
.

The MGF expression in Eq. (
29
) takes for the TSS in the
\{\xi,\eta\}
-representation the form

\qquad G_{\nu}(t)=\sum_{\eta_{f}=\pm 1}\sum_{\eta_{i}=\pm 1}{\mathinner{\langle{\eta_{i}}|{\rho_{\rm S}(0)}|{\eta_{i}}\rangle}}\int_{\eta(0)=\eta_{i}}^{\eta(t)=\eta_{f}}\mathcal{D}\eta\int_{\xi(0)=0}^{\xi(t)=0}\mathcal{D}\xi\,\,{e}^{i\,S_{\rm S}[\eta,\xi]}\,{e}^{-\Phi^{(0)}_{\rm cor}[\eta,\xi]}\,{e}^{i\,\Delta\Phi^{(\nu)}[\eta,\xi]}\,.

(36)

The corresponding expression for the transfer of heat reads

\qquad\langle Q(t)\rangle=\sum_{\eta_{f}=\pm 1}\sum_{\eta_{i}=\pm 1}{\mathinner{\langle{\eta_{i}}|{\rho_{\rm S}(0)}|{\eta_{i}}\rangle}}\int_{\eta(0)=\eta_{i}}^{\eta(t)=\eta_{f}}\mathcal{D}\eta\int_{\xi(0)=0}^{\xi(t)=0}\mathcal{D}\xi\,\Phi^{(1)}[\eta,\xi]\,\,{e}^{i\,S_{\rm S}[\eta,\xi]}\,{e}^{-\Phi^{(0)}_{\rm cor}[\eta,\xi]}\,.

(37)

Here we have utilized that the local action
\Phi^{(0)}_{\rm loc}[\eta,\xi]

defined in Eq. (
24
) is zero for the TSS.

4.1
Exact formal solution for the average heat

The double path sum in Eq. (
36
) can be visualized as a path sum for a single path that successively visits the four states of the RDM, i.e., the two diagonal or sojourn states labelled by
\eta=\pm 1
,
and the two off-diagonal or blip states labelled by
\xi=\pm 1
. Starting out from a diagonal state of the RDM,
the path dwells in sojourn
j
during time interval
s_{j}=t_{2j+1}-t_{2j}
, and in a blip during time interval

\tau_{j}=t_{2j}-t_{2j-1}
, where
t_{j}
is the flip time. The piecewise constant paths

\eta(\tau)
- and
\xi(\tau)
with
2n
flips are given by

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\eta^{(n)}(\tau)&=&{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\eta_{j}\big[\,\Theta(\tau-t_{2j})-\Theta(\tau-t_{2j+1})\,\big]\,,}\\[14.22636pt]
\xi^{(n)}(\tau)&=&{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}\big[\,\Theta(\tau-t_{2j-1})-\Theta(\tau-t_{2j})\,\big]\,,}\end{array}

(38)

where
t_{0}=0
and
t_{2n+1}=t
. Here the initial state of the RDM is
\eta_{i}=\eta_{0}
and the final state is

\eta_{f}=\eta_{n}
.
The RDM at initial time
t=0
is given by the initial populations

p_{L}=\frac{1}{2}(1+p_{0})
and
p_{R}=\frac{1}{2}(1-p_{0})
of the left and right well. We have

-1\leq p_{0}\leq 1
and

{\mathinner{\langle{\eta_{0}}|{\rho_{\rm S}(0)}|{\eta_{0}}\rangle}}=\frac{1}{2}(1-p_{0})\,\delta_{\eta_{0},1}+\frac{1}{2}(1+p_{0})\,\delta_{\eta_{0},-1}\,.

(39)

The path sum in the expressions (
36
) results in a series in the
number of flip pairs, and in each term the summation of all possible
arrangements in the time-ordered alternating series of sojourns and blips,

\int\!\mathcal{D}\eta\int\!\mathcal{D}\xi\cdots\to\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\mathcal{D}_{2n}\{t_{j}\}\!\sum_{\{\xi_{j}=\pm 1\}}\sum_{\{\eta_{j}=\pm 1\}}\cdots\,.

(40)

Here the integration symbol
\int\mathcal{D}_{2n}\{t_{j}\}
is a short form of the
2n
time-ordered integrations,

\int_{0}^{t}\!\mathcal{D}_{2n}\{t_{j}\}=\int_{0}^{t}\!\!dt_{2n}\!\int_{0}^{t_{2n}}\!\!\!dt_{2n-1}....\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\!\!\!dt_{1}\,.

(41)

Consider first the weight factors of the paths resulting from the internal dynamics of the
TSS.
The weight to switch per unit time from the diagonal state
\eta_{j}
to the directly following off-diagonal state

\xi_{j+1}
is
-i\,\eta_{j}\,\xi_{j+1}\,\Delta/2
, and the weight to switch per unit time
from the offdiagonal state
\xi_{j+1}
to the directly following diagonal state
\eta_{j+1}
is

-i\,\xi_{j+1}\,\eta_{j+1}\,\Delta/2
. Thus, the aggregated weight of
2n
flips depends only on the initial and
final sojourn state,

(-1)^{n}(\Delta^{2}/4)^{n}\,\eta_{0}\,\eta_{n}\,.

(42)

The weight to stay in a sojourn is unity, whereas the weight to stay
in the blip
\xi_{j}
during time interval
t_{2j}-t_{2j-1}
is
\exp[\,i\xi_{j}\int_{t_{2j-1}}^{t_{2j}}dt^{\prime}\epsilon(t^{\prime})\,]
.
Accordingly, the contributions of
n
blips are accumulated in the bias weight factor

B_{n}=e^{i\,\varphi_{n}},\qquad\varphi_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}\,\Big[\epsilon_{0}\tau_{j}+\int_{t_{2j-1}}^{t_{2j}}\!\!\!d\tau\,\epsilon_{1}(\tau)\Big]~.

(43)

For the piecewise constant path (
38
) in the influence action
\Phi_{\rm cor}^{(0)}[\eta,\xi]
the time correlations
among the
\{\eta_{i}\}
- and
\{\xi_{j}\}
- labels or charges
are expressed in terms of the second integral of the function
L(\tau)
,

W(\tau)=W^{\prime}(\tau)+i\,W^{\prime\prime}(\tau)=\frac{q_{0}^{2}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!d\omega\,\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}\Big[\coth\Big(\frac{\omega}{2T}\Big)[\,1-\cos(\omega\tau)\,]+i\,\sin(\omega\tau)\,\Big]\,.

(44)

The resulting correlation factor for
2n
flips is
[
34
,
43
,
44
]

\displaystyle\Big(e^{-\Phi^{(0)}_{\rm cor}[\eta,\xi]}\Big)_{n}=G_{n}H_{n}\,,

\displaystyle G_{n}=\exp{\Big\{-\sum_{j=1}^{n}W^{\prime}_{2j,2j-1}\Big\}}\;\exp{\Big\{-\sum_{j=2}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\xi_{j}\Lambda_{j,k}\xi_{k}\Big\}}\,,

(45)

\displaystyle H_{n}=\exp{\Big\{i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\xi_{j}X_{j,k}\eta_{k}\Big\}}=\exp\Big\{i\,\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\phi_{nk}\eta_{k}\Big\}\,,

where

\phi_{n,k}=\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}\xi_{j}X_{j,k}\;.

(46)

The first exponential factor of
G_{n}
represents the intrablip correlations, and the second exponential factor
the interblip correlations of the n blips. With the short form
W_{j,k}=W(t_{j}-t_{k})

the interactions between all sojourns and all subsequent blips are in the phase factor
H_{n}
.
In the second form,
H_{n}
is written in terms of the entire blip correlations
\phi_{n,k}
with sojourn
k
.
The interactions between blip
k
and subsequent blip
j
, and the interactions between
sojourn
k
and subsequent blip
j
are written as

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Lambda_{j,k}&=&\!\!\!W^{\prime}_{2j,2k-1}+W^{\prime}_{2j-1,2k}-W^{\prime}_{2j,2k}-W^{\prime}_{2j-1,2k-1}\,,\\[8.53581pt]
X_{j,k}&=&\!\!\!W^{\prime\prime}_{2j,2k+1}+W^{\prime\prime}_{2j-1,2k}-W^{\prime\prime}_{2j,2k}-W^{\prime\prime}_{2j-1,2k+1}\,.\end{array}

(47)

With these individual terms the average heat takes the form

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\langle Q(t)\rangle}&=&{\displaystyle\sum_{\eta_{0}=\pm 1}{\mathinner{\langle{\eta_{0}}|{\rho_{\rm S}(0)}|{\eta_{0}}\rangle}}\,\eta_{0}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{-\Delta^{2}}{4}\Big)^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}_{2n}\{t_{j}\}}\\[11.38109pt]
&&\qquad{\displaystyle\times\sum_{\{\xi_{j}=\pm 1\}}B_{n}G_{n}\sum_{\{\eta_{j}=\pm 1\}^{\prime\prime}}H_{n}\sum_{\eta_{n}=\pm 1}\eta_{n}\,\Phi^{(1)}[\eta^{(n)},\xi^{(n)}]\,.}\end{array}

(48)

Here the double prime in
\{\eta_{j}=\pm 1\}^{\prime\prime}
indicates summation over the internal sojourn states

\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{n-1}
.

For the piecewise constant paths (
38
) the functional
(\ref{eq:Phi1})
describes time correlations between sojourns and blips with any time-ordering. By analogy with the relation of the kernels in the actions
(
3
) and (
24
),
the time correlations between the
\{\eta_{i}\}
- and
\{\xi_{j}\}
-charges in the action

\Phi^{(1)}[\eta^{(n)},\xi^{(n)}]
are specified by the derivative of the function
W(\tau)
defined in Eq. (
44
).
The imaginary part
\dot{W}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)
is connected with the damping kernel according to

\dot{W}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)=\frac{Mq_{0}^{2}}{2}\gamma(\tau)\,.

(49)

Interestingly enough, the summation over the final sojourn states
\eta_{n}=\pm 1
in the expression (
48
)
cancels, by reason of the extra factor
\eta_{n}
, all correlations of blips and sojourns in
\Phi^{(1)}
except the
correlations with the two final sojourns states. Summation of the leftover correlations yields

\sum_{\eta_{n}=\pm 1}\eta_{n}\Phi^{(1)}[\eta^{(n)},\xi^{(n)}]=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{U}_{n,k}(t)\,\eta_{k}+i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{V}_{n,j}(t)\,\xi_{j}\,.

(50)

Here
\mathcal{U}_{n,k}(t)
represents the correlations of sojourn
k
with sojourn
n
and

\mathcal{V}_{n,j}(t)
the correlations of blip
j
with sojourn
n
. Explicit dependence on the end time
t

is indicated for later purpose. We obtain analogous to the forms (
47
) the expressions

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\mathcal{U}}_{n,k}(t)&=&\dot{W}^{\prime\prime}(t-t_{2k+1})+\dot{W}^{\prime\prime}(t_{2n}-t_{2k})-\dot{W}^{\prime\prime}(t-t_{2k})-\dot{W}^{\prime\prime}(t_{2n}-t_{2k+1})\,,\\[11.38109pt]
{\mathcal{V}}_{n,k}(t)&=&\dot{W}^{\prime}(t-t_{2k-1})+\dot{W}^{\prime}(t_{2n}-t_{2k})-\dot{W}^{\prime}(t-t_{2k})-\dot{W}^{\prime}(t_{2n}-t_{2k-1})\,.\end{array}

(51)

Next, we add up the contributions of the
2n-1
intermediate sojourns. Further, we utilize that
in Eq. (
48
) the contributions of the blip states which are odd under
the substitutions
\{\xi_{j}\}\to\{-\xi_{j}\}
cancel each other out.
The resulting expression for
{\langle Q(t)\rangle}
is conveniently divided into the
{\cal U}
- and

{\cal V}
-contributions,
\langle Q(t)\rangle_{1}
and
\langle Q(t)\rangle_{2}
,
and these into those which are symmetric (s) and antisymmetric (a) under bias inversion (
\epsilon\to-\epsilon
).
We readily find

\langle Q(t)\rangle=\langle Q(t)\rangle_{1}+\langle Q(t)\rangle_{2}\,,

(52)

where

\qquad{\langle Q(t)\rangle}_{r}=\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{-\Delta^{2}}{2}\Big)^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}_{2n}\{t_{j}\}\sum_{\{\xi_{j}=\pm 1\}}\!\bigg(\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\cos(\phi_{n,k})\bigg)\,G_{n}\,\left[\,{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{(r,{\rm s})}(t)+{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{(r,{\rm a})}(t)\,\right]\,,

(53)

and where
r=1,\,2
. The time correlations carried by the kernel
\dot{W}(\tau)
are in the functions

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{(1,{\rm s})}(t)&=&{\displaystyle\cos(\varphi_{n})\Big[\,\mathcal{U}_{n,0}(t)-\tan(\phi_{n,0})\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\tan(\phi_{n,\ell})\,\mathcal{U}_{n,\ell}(t)\,\Big]\,,}\\[11.38109pt]
{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{(1,{\rm a})}(t)&=&{\displaystyle p_{0}\sin(\varphi_{n})\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\tan(\phi_{n,\ell})\,\mathcal{U}_{n,\ell}(t)\,,}\\[11.38109pt]
{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{(2,{\rm s})}(t)&=&-\,{\displaystyle\cos(\varphi_{n})\tan(\phi_{n,0})\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathcal{V}_{n,\ell}(t)\,\xi_{\ell}\,,}\\[11.38109pt]
{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{(2,{\rm a})}(t)&=&{\displaystyle p_{0}\sin(\varphi_{n})\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathcal{V}_{n,\ell}(t)\,\xi_{\ell}\,.}\end{array}

(54)

The expression (
52
) with (
53
) and (
54
)
holds for linear dissipation with arbitrary memory-friction.

4.2
The Ohmic case

Of particular importance is the case of Ohmic dissipation, which is the limit
s\to 1
in the spectral density of Eq. (
5
). In the Ohmic universality limit
\omega_{\rm c}\to\infty
we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}W(\tau)&=&{\displaystyle 2K\ln\Big[\frac{\omega_{\rm c}}{\pi T}\sinh\big(\pi T|\tau|\big)\Big]+i\,\pi K{\rm sign}(\tau)\,,}\\[11.38109pt]
\dot{W}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)&=&{\displaystyle\frac{Mq_{0}^{2}}{2}\gamma(\tau)=2\pi K\delta(\tau)\,,}\end{array}

(55)

where
K
is a dimensionless damping strength,
K=M\gamma_{1}q_{0}^{2}/(2\pi)
.

Because the kernel
{\dot{W}}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)
is memoryless,
the sojourn-sojourn correlation
{\cal U}_{n,\ell}(t)
is restricted to the nearest-neighbour term
\ell=n-1
in which
the intermediate sojourn has length zero,

{\cal U}_{n,n-1}=-2\pi K\,\delta(t_{2n}-t_{2n-1})\,.

(56)

Observing that a blip of zero length does not interact with other blips, the
\xi
-summation in
Eq. (
53
) cancels all sojourn-sojourn correlations except those of the first with the second.
Thus we have
{\cal U}_{n,n-1}=\delta_{n,1}\,{\cal U}_{1,0}
.
In addition, the odd term
{\mathcal{R}}_{1}^{(1,{\rm a})}(t)
is zero because the single
blip left over has zero length, thus readily

\langle Q(t)\rangle_{1}=\frac{\pi K}{2}\,\delta^{2}\,t\,.

(57)

Here
\delta=\Delta\,{e}^{-W_{\rm adia}/2}
is a renormalized transition amplitude which takes into account the polarization cloud of the bath modes
in the adiabatic limit. We have
W_{\rm adia}=2K\ln(\omega_{\rm c}/\Delta_{\rm r})
and hence

\delta=\Delta_{\rm r}\equiv\Delta(\Delta/\omega_{\rm c})^{K/(1-K)}
in the regime

T\ll\Delta
, and
W_{\rm adia}=2K\ln[\omega_{\rm c}/(2\pi T)]
and hence

\delta=\Delta_{\rm r}(2\pi T/\Delta_{\rm r})^{K}
in the regime

T\gg\Delta
.

Consider next the temperature-dependent term
\langle Q(t)\rangle_{2}
. We see from the imaginary part of the
expression (
55
) that the blip-sojourn correlations
X_{j,k}
are restricted to the
nearest-neighbour term,

X_{j,k}=\pi K\,\delta_{j,k+1}
. Thus we have
\phi_{n,k}=\pi K\xi_{k+1}
. With this the expression
(
53
) with (
54
) takes the form

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\langle Q(t)\rangle}_{2}&=&{\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{-\Delta^{2}}{2}\Big)^{n}\cos(\pi K)^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}_{2n}\{t_{j}\}\sum_{\{\xi_{j}=\pm 1\}}\!G_{n}}\\[14.22636pt]
&&{\displaystyle\quad\times\;\Big(-\cos(\varphi_{n})\tan(\pi K)\,\xi_{1}+p_{0}\sin(\varphi_{n})\Big)\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}{\cal V}_{n,\ell}(t)\,\xi_{\ell}}\,.\end{array}

(58)

At this point it is expedient to turn to the average heat power
\langle P(t)\rangle
,
which is the time derivative of the heat,
\langle P(t)\rangle={\langle\dot{Q}(t)\rangle}
.
Since
{\cal V}_{n,\ell}(t=t_{2n})=0
, the derivative of
{\langle Q(t)\rangle}_{2}
is restricted to the derivative of
{\cal V}_{n,\ell}(t=t_{2n})
in Eq. (
58
). Observing that
\ddot{W}^{\prime}(\tau)
coincides with
L^{\prime}(\tau)
given in Eq. (
25
), we readily obtain

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\langle P(t)\rangle}&=&{\displaystyle\frac{\pi K}{2}\delta^{2}\,-\,\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{-\Delta^{2}}{2}\Big)^{n}\cos(\pi K)^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}_{2n}\{t_{j}\}\sum_{\{\xi_{j}=\pm 1\}}\!G_{n}}\\[14.22636pt]
&\times&{\displaystyle\Big(\cos(\varphi_{n})\tan(\pi K)\,\xi_{1}-p_{0}\sin(\varphi_{n})\Big)\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\big[\,L^{\prime}(t-t_{2\ell-1})-L^{\prime}(t-t_{2\ell})\,\big]\,\xi_{\ell}}\,.\end{array}

(59)

This form is the exact formal path sum expression for the average heat power of the two-state system
in the Ohmic scaling limit. Apart from the last factor, the expression represents the dynamics of the RDM of the TSS under all the time-correlations carried by the intra- and inter-blip correlation factor
G_{n}
. The residual factor with the time correlations of all intermediate flip times with the final time
t
is specific to the mean of the heat power.
The expression (
59
) may be used to calculate the time dependence of the heat power for any
time-dependent driving of the system according to Eq. (
35
).

To sound out validity and potential of the presented method, we now study
the expression (
59
) for a constant bias
\epsilon_{0}
. First, we see that in the term of
\Delta^{2n}

the blip
\xi_{\ell}
, which may take any position in the sequence of
n
blips, plays a particular role.
In the weak damping limit, we may safely disregard the interblip correlations
of the preceding and the subsequent blips with the blip
\ell
,
\Lambda_{j,\ell}=0
and
\Lambda_{\ell,k}=0
,
as well as all interblip correlations across this blip, i.e.,
\Lambda_{j,k}=0
, where
j<\ell
and
k>\ell
. Then
the series (
59
) can be expressed in terms of a convolution which involves in the time segment the population correlation function
\langle\sigma_{z}(\tau)\rangle

[
40
]

and the coherence correlation function
\langle\sigma_{x}(\tau)\rangle
of the TSS
[
44
,
43
,
22
]
. The resulting expression is

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\langle P(t)\rangle&=&{\displaystyle\frac{\pi K}{2}\delta^{2}-\frac{\Delta}{2}\left\{\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\,\Big(\,\langle\sigma_{x}(t-\tau)\rangle_{\rm s}\,\langle\sigma_{z}(\tau)\rangle_{\rm s}-\langle\sigma_{z}(t-\tau)\rangle_{\rm a}\,\langle\sigma_{x}(\tau)\rangle_{\rm a}\right.}\\[11.38109pt]
&&\qquad\;\;{\displaystyle-\,p_{0}\,\big[\langle\sigma_{x}(t-\tau)\rangle_{\rm a}\,\langle\sigma_{z}(\tau)\rangle_{\rm s}-\langle\sigma_{z}(t-\tau)\rangle_{\rm s}\,\langle\sigma_{x}(\tau)\rangle_{\rm a}\,\big]\,\Big)\,L^{\prime}(\tau)}\\[11.38109pt]
&&{\displaystyle\qquad\;\;+\,\left.\tan(\pi K)\,\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\,\big[\,1-\langle\sigma_{z}(\tau)\rangle_{\rm s}\,\big]\,L^{\prime}(\tau)\right\}\,.}\end{array}

(60)

Here the components which are symmetric under bias inversion
\epsilon_{0}\to-\epsilon_{0}
have index s, and the respective antisymmetric components have index a. The initial conditions are

\langle\sigma_{z}(0)\rangle_{\rm a}=\langle\sigma_{x}(0)\rangle_{\rm s}=\langle\sigma_{x}(0)\rangle_{\rm a}=0

and
\langle\sigma_{z}(0)\rangle_{\rm s}=1
.

In the further analysis of the average heat power
\langle P(t)\rangle
we can thus rely on analytic results for the
nonequilibrium correlation functions
\langle\sigma_{z}(t)\rangle
and
\langle\sigma_{x}(t)\rangle
reported
in the recent literature
[
40
,
41
,
22
]
.

To conclude this subsection, we consider as a preparatory work for the subsequent studies
the total heat transferred from the TSS to the reservoir in the weak damping regime. Writing the
localized states
\mathinner{|{L}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|{R}\rangle}
as linear combinations of the ground state

\mathinner{|{g}\rangle}
and excited state
\mathinner{|{e}\rangle}
of
H_{\rm S}
, and assuming initial preparation according to Eq. (
39
)
and thermal occupation of the states
\mathinner{|{g}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|{e}\rangle}
at time infinity, we obtain

Q_{\infty}\equiv\langle Q(t\to\infty)\rangle=\langle E\rangle_{\rm ini}-\langle E\rangle_{\rm eq}=\frac{p_{0}\,\epsilon_{0}}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{\delta^{2}+\epsilon_{0}^{2}}}{2}\tanh\Big(\frac{\sqrt{\delta^{2}+\epsilon_{0}^{2}}}{2T}\Big)\,~,

(61)

where
\langle E\rangle_{{\rm ini}}
and
\langle E\rangle_{{\rm eq}}
indicate the initial and equilibrium energy of the system respectively.

4.3
Markov regime

In the temperature regime
T\gg\sqrt{\delta^{2}+\epsilon_{0}^{2}}
,
the power spectrum

5
Conclusions

In this paper we established the functional integral description for the time-dependent heat exchange
of a quantum system coupled to a thermal reservoir. We presented the exact formal solution for the
moment generating functional which carries all statistical features of the heat exchange process for general linear dissipation.
We derived an exact formal expression for the transferred heat and applied this formalism to the dissipative two-state system.
We showed that the difference between the dynamics of the heat transfer and the dynamics of the reduced density matrix (RDM) is an additional time-nonlocal correlation function which correlates intermediate states of the RDM
with the final state.

To investigate the potential of the presented method,
we calculated the dynamics of the average heat power and average heat in analytic form for weak Ohmic dissipation both in the Markovian regime relevant at high temperatures and in the non-Markovian quantum noise regime holding
when temperature is of the order of the level splitting
\Omega
or lower.
In the latter regime, the heat is represented by a convolution integral
which involves the population and coherence correlation functions of the dissipative TSS and the polarization correlation function of the reservoir. We find that the heat transfer receives contributions both from singularities
related to the system dynamics and from Matsubara singularities resulting from the system-reservoir correlations.
The latter yield significant contributions in the quantum noise regime while they are absent in the Markovian regime.

Altogether we have achieved a complete description of the dynamics of the heat transfer for weak damping
ranging from the classical regime down to zero temperature

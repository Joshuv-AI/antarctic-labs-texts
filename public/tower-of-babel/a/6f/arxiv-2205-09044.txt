Normalized image of a vector by an infinite product of nonnegative matrices
Alain Thomas

Abstract
. To prove that a measure, linearly representable by means of a finite set of nonnegative matrices ℳ \mathcal{M} , has the weak-Gibbs property, one check the uniform convergence (on ℳ ℕ \mathcal{M}^{\mathbb{N}} ) of the sequence of vectors A 1 ⋯ A n c ∥ A 1 ⋯ A n c ∥ \frac{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}c}{\|A_{1}\cdots A_{n}c\|} ( c c positive column-vector). The main theorem gives a sufficient condition for this sequence to converge pointwise. This theorem generalizes the Birkhoff contraction method because it can be used even if the matrices have many zero entries. We also look at the convergence of the sequence of matrices A 1 ⋯ A n ∥ A 1 ⋯ A n ∥ \frac{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}{\|A_{1}\cdots A_{n}\|} . The measures defined by Bernoulli convolution are in certain cases linearly representable; we give two example of weak-Gibbs Bernoullt convolutions, by using the Birkhoff contraction coefficient for the first and the theorem for the second. Furthermore we explicit the relationship between the notions of Bernoulli convolution, fundamental curves and lattice two-scale difference equations.

Introduction

By Carathéodory’s extension theorem one can define as follows a probability measure
\nu
on the Borel algebra of the set
\Omega_{a}:=\{0,1,\dots,a-1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Given some nonnegative row-vectors
r_{0},\dots,r_{a-1}
(i.e. all their entries are nonegative), some square nonnegative matrices
M_{0},\dots,M_{a-1}

(i.e. all the entries of

\sum_{i}r_{i}c=1

\sum_{i}M_{i}c=c

we set

(1)

\begin{array}[]{l}\nu([\omega_{1}\dots\omega_{n}]):=r_{\omega_{1}}M_{\omega_{2}}\cdots M_{\omega_{n}}c\\
\text{for any cylinder-set }[\omega_{1}\dots\omega_{n}]:=\{\xi\in\Omega_{a}\;;\;\xi_{1}=\omega_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}=\omega_{n}\}.\end{array}

The measure defined in (

[

, or ”sofic”, ”hidden Markov chain”, ”function of Markov chain”, ”probabilistic function of Markov chain” (

,

,

,

]

[

Remark 0.1

The definition (

\mu
by the classical formula
\mu[\omega_{1}\dots\omega_{n}]:=p_{\omega_{1}}p_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}\cdots p_{\omega_{n-1},\omega_{n}}

\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\

Remark 0.2

To prove that a linearly representable measure has the weak-Gibbs property (see

[

The Markov probability measures are a special case of weak-Gibbs measures: the potential involved in Definition

(i) is
\phi(\omega)=\log p_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}
.

For any sequence of nonnegative
d\times d
matrices

we use the notation

\textstyle P_{n}:=A_{1}\cdots A_{n}\text{ and }P_{m,n}:=A_{m+1}\cdots A_{n}\ (n>m\geq 0),P_{0}=P_{n,n}:=I_{d}.

It seems that in many cases the sequence of vectors
\frac{P_{n}c}{\|P_{n}c\|}
converges while the sequence of matrices
\frac{P_{n}}{\|P_{n}\|}
diverges (§1 and §4 respectively). The convergence of
\frac{P_{n}c}{\|P_{n}c\|}
can be proved by different methods. We cannot use the known results about the infinite products of matrices
[
35
,
6
,
27
,
40
,
41
, …]
for the following reason: the sequence
P_{n}
either diverges or converges to

if the set
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}:=\{M\;;\;A_{n}=M\text{ for infinitely many }n\}
is finite and if the elements of this set do not have a common left-eigenvector for the eigenvalue 1. This result is a direct consequence of
[
16
, Theorem 1]
. So, if the matrices
A_{n}
belong to a finite set, we are restricted to suppose that the matrices of
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
have a common left-eigenvector for the eigenvalue 1, to write
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{P_{n}c}{\|P_{n}c\|}=\frac{Ac}{\|Ac\|}
if

exists.

The Birkhoff contraction coefficient computed in
[

are positive, implying the convergence of the sequence
\frac{P_{n}c}{\|P_{n}c\|}

have many null entries, it is appropriate to seek another explanation for the convergence of

\textstyle P_{n}=P_{s_{1}}P_{s_{1},s_{2}}\cdots P_{s_{k-1},s_{k}}P_{s_{k},n}\ (s_{k+1}\leq n<s_{k+2}),

Rank one property, and the sequence

We use the following notations:

u_{1},\dots,u_{d}
are the canonical
d
-dimensional column-vectors, and
u:=\sum_{i}u_{i}
;

\|X\|
is the sum of the absolute values of the entries, in the matrix or vector
X
; in this paper, the choice of the norm does not matter because, given two norms
\mathcal{N}_{1}
and
\mathcal{N}_{2}
, if a sequence of the form
\frac{x_{n}}{\mathcal{N}_{1}(x_{n})}
converges to a limit
\ell
, then the sequence
\frac{x_{n}}{\mathcal{N}_{2}(x_{n})}
converges to
\frac{\ell}{\mathcal{N}_{2}(\ell)}

the shift

is defined for any sequence

1.1.
Sufficient condition for the sequence
\frac{P_{n}c}{\|P_{n}c\|}
to converge

One can always chose an increasing sequence
(s_{k})_{\in\mathbb{N}}
for
\frac{P_{s_{k}}}{\|P_{s_{k}}\|}
to converge but, when one makes the product
P_{s_{k}}P_{s_{k},n}=P_{n}
, the dominant columns (in norm) of
P_{s_{k}}
may disappear if the rows of
P_{s_{k},n}
with same indices as them are null, or if they are small (when
k\to\infty
) with respect to
\|P_{s_{k},n}\|
. So we make two hypotheses in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of nonnegative
d\times d
matrices such that
\forall n\ P_{n}\neq 0
, and suppose there exists a limit-point
P^{(s)}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{P_{s_{k}}}{\|P_{s_{k}}\|}
such that

(H_{1})

\textstyle\inf\big\{\frac{\|P_{n}\|}{\|P_{s_{k}}\|\|P_{s_{k},n}\|}\;;\;k,n\text{ such that }s_{k+1}\leq n<s_{k+2}\big\}>0
;

(H_{2})
setting
J=\{j\;;\;P^{(s)}u_{j}\neq 0\}
, there exists a limit-point
Q=(q_{i,j})_{i,j}
of the sequence
k\mapsto\frac{P_{s_{k},s_{k+1}}}{\|P_{s_{k},s_{k+1}}\|}
such that

\textstyle\forall j,j^{\prime}\in J\ \exists i\in J\ q_{i,j}q_{i,j^{\prime}}\neq 0.

Then the sequence
\frac{P_{n}c}{\|P_{n}c\|}
converges for any positive column-vector
c
, and more generally for any
c\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\{v\text{ nonnegative}\;;\;\inf_{n}\frac{\|P_{n}v\|}{\|P_{n}\|}>0\}
. The limit does not depend on
c
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
P^{(s)}
satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, and let
\varepsilon_{k}:=\big\|\frac{P_{s_{k}}}{\|P_{s_{k}}\|}-P^{(s)}\big\|
. Using the classical inequality
\big\|\frac{x}{\|x\|}-\frac{y}{\|y\|}\big\|\leq\frac{2\|x-y\|}{\|x\|}
,

(2)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Big\|\frac{P_{n}c}{\|P_{n}c\|}-\frac{P^{(s)}P_{s_{k},n}c}{\|P^{(s)}P_{s_{k},n}c\|}\Big\|&=&\Big\|\frac{P_{n}c/\|P_{s_{k}}\|}{\|P_{n}c\|/\|P_{s_{k}}\|}-\frac{P^{(s)}P_{s_{k},n}c}{\|P^{(s)}P_{s_{k},n}c\|}\Big\|\\
&\leq&\frac{2\big\|P_{n}c/\|P_{s_{k}}\|-P^{(s)}P_{s_{k},n}c\big\|}{\|P_{n}c\|/\|P_{s_{k}}\|}\\
&\leq&\frac{2\varepsilon_{k}\|P_{s_{k},n}c\|}{\|P_{n}c\|/\|P_{s_{k}}\|}.\end{array}

The r.h.s. tends to 0 in consequence of the hypotheses
c\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
(H_{1})
. To simplify the l.h.s. we prove below that
P^{(s)}
has rank 1.

Using the set
J
and the matrix
Q
defined in
(H_{2})
,
P^{(s)}Q
is a limit-point of
\frac{P_{s_{k+1}}}{\|P_{s_{k}}\|\|P_{s_{k},s_{k+1}}\|}
and, using
(H_{1})
,

(3)

P^{(s)}Q\neq 0\text{ and }\frac{P^{(s)}Q}{\|P^{(s)}Q\|}=P^{(s)}.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the convex hull of the normalized columns of
P^{(s)}
, that is, the vectors
c_{j}=\frac{P^{(s)}u_{j}}{\|P^{(s)}u_{j}\|}
for
j\in J
, and suppose that
\mathcal{C}
has two distinct extreme points
v,v^{\prime}
. From the definition of the extreme points,
v
cannot be a positive linear combinations of distinct elements of
\mathcal{C}
, hence there exists
j\in J
such that
v=c_{j}
. By (
3
),
c_{j}
is collinear to
\sum_{i\in J}q_{i,j}c_{i}
. Since
c_{j}
cannot be a positive
linear combination of different vectors
c_{i}
, and since the
c_{i}
have norm 1,
c_{j}=c_{i}
for any
i
such that
q_{i,j}\neq 0
.

Similarly, there exist
j^{\prime}\in J
such that
v=c_{j^{\prime}}
, and
c_{j^{\prime}}=c_{i^{\prime}}
for any
i^{\prime}
such that
q_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\neq 0
. Using the index
i
of the hypothesis
(H_{2})
,
v=v^{\prime}=c_{i}
, a contradiction.

By a theorem of Minkowski (see for instance Theorem 0.4 of

http://www.mat.unimi.it/users/libor/AnConvessa/ext.pdf),
\mathcal{C}
is the convex hull of its extreme points. Hence
\mathcal{C}
has only one element
v
, and there exists a row-vector
r
such that
P^{(s)}=vr
.

The l.h.s. of (
2
) is
\big\|\frac{P_{n}c}{\|P_{n}c\|}-\frac{v}{\|v\|}\big\|
, so
\frac{P_{n}c}{\|P_{n}c\|}
converges to
\frac{v}{\|v\|}
.∎

Counterexample 1.2
.

Suppose that

with

(resp.

(resp.

). Given a positive column-vector

1.2.
Approximation by a matrix of rank 1, and a counterexample

We expect that in general, if the matrices
A_{n}
are nonnegative there exist a sequence of row-vectors
(r_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and a constant column-vector
v
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\frac{P_{n}}{\|P_{n}\|}-vr_{n}\big)=0
.

More generally, any sequence of matrices (not necessarily a sequence of product-matrices) satisfies the following straightforward equivalences:

Proposition 1.3
.

(i) For any sequence
(M_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of complex-valued
d\times d
matrices we have the following equivalences, where
\delta_{1}(n)\geq\delta_{2}(n)\geq\dots
are the singular values of
M_{n}
:

\begin{array}[]{l}\exists v_{n}\text{ (column-vector)},r_{n}\text{ (row-vector)}\ \lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\frac{M_{n}}{\|M_{n}\|}-v_{n}r_{n}\big)=0\\
\Leftrightarrow\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\delta_{2}(n)}{\delta_{1}(n)}=0\\
\Leftrightarrow\text{any limit-point of }\frac{M_{n}}{\|M_{n}\|}\text{ has rank }1.\end{array}

If it holds,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\det\frac{M_{n}}{\|M_{n}\|}=0
.

(ii) We have the following equivalence if the matrices
M_{n}
are nonnegative:

\begin{array}[]{l}\text{There exists }v\text{ (constant) and }r_{n}\text{ such that }\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\frac{M_{n}}{\|M_{n}\|}-vr_{n}\big)=0\\
\Leftrightarrow\frac{M_{n}c}{\|M_{n}c\|}\text{ converges to a limit independent of }c>0\text{ when }n\to\infty.\end{array}

The vector
v
of the l.h.s. is collinear to
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{M_{n}c}{\|M_{n}c\|}
and to the limit of the first left singular vector of
M_{n}
.

Proof.

(i)We use the singular value decomposition
M_{n}=S_{n}\Delta_{n}{{}^{t}\overline{T_{n}}}
. If
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\delta_{2}(n)}{\delta_{1}(n)}=0
, since
S_{n}
and
T_{n}
are unitary we deduce the estimation
\frac{M_{n}}{\|M_{n}\|}=v_{n}r_{n}+o(1)
with
v_{n}=\frac{\delta_{1}(n)}{\|M_{n}\|}S_{n}u_{1}
and
r_{n}={{}^{t}u_{1}}{{}^{t}\overline{T_{n}}}
.

On the other hand if
\frac{\delta_{2}(n)}{\delta_{1}(n)}
does not converge to
0
, there exists an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
for the sequences
\frac{\delta_{2}(n_{k})}{\delta_{1}(n_{k})},\frac{M_{n_{k}}}{\|M_{n_{k}}\|},S_{n_{k}}
and
T_{n_{k}}
to have some limits
\delta
(nonnull),
M,S
and
T
when
k\to\infty
. Hence the diagonal matrix
\frac{\Delta_{n_{k}}}{\|M_{n_{k}}\|}
has a limit
\Delta
of rank at least 2. The matrix
M=S\Delta{{}^{t}\overline{T}}
has rank at least 2, hence there cannot exist a column-vector
v_{n}
and a row-vector
r_{n}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\frac{M_{n}}{\|M_{n}\|}-v_{n}r_{n}\Big)=0
.

This last assertion is equivalent to ”
\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\big\|\frac{M_{n}}{\|M_{n}\|}-u\big\|
converges to 0 when
n\to\infty
”, where
\mathcal{U}
is the compact set of the
d\times d
matrices of norm 1 and rank 1. It is equivalent to ”any limit-point of
\frac{M_{n}}{\|M_{n}\|}
has rank 1”.

The equality
\lim_{n\to\infty}\det\frac{M_{n}}{\|M_{n}\|}=0
follows from
\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\frac{M_{n}}{\|M_{n}\|}-v_{n}r_{n}\big)=0
, by using the uniform continuity of the determinant on the compact set of the
d\times d
matrices of norm 1.

(ii) Suppose

\textstyle\big\|\frac{M_{n}u_{j}}{\|M_{n}u_{j}\|}-c\big\|\leq\frac{2\varepsilon\|M_{n}\|}{\|M_{n}u_{j}\|}+\varepsilon\text{ and }\big\|\frac{M_{n}u_{j}}{\|M_{n}\|}-\frac{\|M_{n}u_{j}\|}{\|M_{n}\|}c\big\|\leq 3\varepsilon.

Remark 1.5

About the singular values of a product of triangular matrices: let

For any

,
\#I=k,\ \sigma

2.1.

The ”two-scale difference equations”

\textstyle f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}c_{n}f(kx-n)\text{ for any }x\in\mathbb{R},\text{ where }c_{n}\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and }1<k\in\mathbb{R}

[

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

B_{i}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}c_{i}&c_{i-1}&\dots&c_{i-q}\\

Proof.

A function

4.
Divergence of the sequence of matrices
\frac{P_{n}}{\|P_{n}\|}

We first point out that, if the matrices
A_{n}
are positive and if the sequence
\frac{A_{n}}{\|A_{n}\|}
converges to a positive matrix, then the sequence
\frac{P_{n}}{\|P_{n}\|}
converges to
a rank-one matrix. For this we apply
[
27
, Theorem 1.1]
to the matrices
B_{n}=\frac{{}^{t}{A_{n}}}{\|A_{n}\|}
: the sequence
\frac{B_{n}\cdots B_{1}}{\|B_{n}\cdots B_{1}\|}
converges to a rank-one matrix, hence by transposition the sequence
\frac{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}{\|^{t}{A_{n}}\cdots^{t}{A_{1}}\|}
converges to a rank-one matrix. We deduce that
\frac{\|A_{1}\cdots A_{n}\|}{\|^{t}{A_{n}}\cdots^{t}{A_{1}}\|}
converges to a positive number hence
\frac{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}{\|A_{1}\cdots A_{n}\|}
converges to a rank-one matrix.

Theorem
4.1
below can be seen as a partial converse of this result: for any sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of complex-valued matrices such that
\frac{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}{\|A_{1}\cdots A_{n}\|}
converges, either the sequence
\frac{A_{n}}{\|A_{n}\|}
converges, or this sequence has several limit-points with a common left-eigenvector (the proof is similar to the one of Elsner and Friedland in
[
16
, Theorem 1]
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of
d\times d
matrices on
\mathbb{C}
, and suppose that the product-matrix
P_{n}=A_{1}\cdots A_{n}
is nonnull for any
n
. The sequence
\frac{P_{n}}{\|P_{n}\|}
diverges if the sequence
\frac{A_{n}}{\|A_{n}\|}
diverges and if its limit-points do not have a common left-eigenvector.

Proof.

We prove this assertion by contraposition. We first prove that:

Lemma 4.2
.

If the sequence
\frac{P_{n}}{\|P_{n}\|}
converges, there exist a row-vector
r\neq 0
and a sequence of positive reals
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

(17)

\textstyle\lim_{n\to\infty}r\big(\frac{A_{n}}{\|A_{n}\|}-\lambda_{n}I_{d}\big)=0.

Proof.

(i) Let
\lambda_{n}:=\frac{\|P_{n}\|}{\|P_{n-1}\|\cdot\|A_{n}\|}
. The bounded sequence
\lambda_{n}
satisfies

\textstyle\lambda_{n}\Big(\frac{P_{n}}{\|P_{n}\|}-\frac{P_{n-1}}{\|P_{n-1}\|}\Big)=\frac{P_{n-1}}{\|P_{n-1}\|}\Big(\frac{A_{n}}{\|A_{n}\|}-\lambda_{n}I_{d}\Big)

hence, if the sequence
\frac{P_{n}}{\|P_{n}\|}
converges to a matrix
P
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{P_{n-1}}{\|P_{n-1}\|}\big(\frac{A_{n}}{\|A_{n}\|}-\lambda_{n}I_{d}\big)=0
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}P\big(\frac{A_{n}}{\|A_{n}\|}-\lambda_{n}I_{d}\big)=0
. Since
P
has norm
1
, it has at least one non-null row
r
and (
17
) holds.∎

If the sequence
\frac{P_{n}}{\|P_{n}\|}
converges the relation (
17
) implies, by the second triangular inequality,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\big\|r\frac{A_{n}}{\|A_{n}\|}\big\|-\lambda_{n}\|r\|\big)=0
. So for any limit-point
A=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{A_{n_{k}}}{\|A_{n_{k}}\|}
, the sequence
k\mapsto\lambda_{n_{k}}
converges to
\lambda=\frac{\|rA\|}{\|r\|}
and, by (
17
),
r
is a left-eigenvector of
A
. Either the sequence
\frac{A_{n}}{\|A_{n}\|}
converges, or it has several limit-points with
r
as common left-eigenvector.∎

To prove that the sequence
\frac{P_{n}}{\|P_{n}\|}
diverges with probability 1, the probability we use can be any infinite product of a non-atomic Borel probability measure
p
with support
\mathbb{C}
. This means that the probability for the
(i,j)^{\rm th}
entry of
A_{n}
to belong to the Borel set
B_{n,i,j}
for any
n,i,j
, is
\prod_{n,i,j}p(B_{n,i,j})
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let

be a non-atomic Borel probability measure with support
\mathbb{C}
, and
p^{*}
the product-probability on
\mathcal{M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})^{\mathbb{N}}

Proof.

We have to prove that the following sets have probability

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{E}_{0}&:=&\{(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\;;\;\exists n,\ P_{n}=0\},\\
\mathcal{E}&:=&\big\{(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\;;\;\text{the limit-points of }\frac{A_{n}}{\|A_{n}\|}\text{ have at least one common}\\
&&\text{left-eigenvector}\big\}.\end{array}

\mathcal{E}_{0}
is a subset of
\{(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\;;\;\exists n\ \det A_{n}=0\}

\mathcal{E}_{d}:=\{M\in\mathcal{M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})\;;\;\det M=0\}
has probability

has probability
0

of a matrix

(18)

To prove that

T^{(0)}:=\frac{2}{d(d+1)}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0&\dots&0\\
1&1&\dots&0\\

Definition A.2
.

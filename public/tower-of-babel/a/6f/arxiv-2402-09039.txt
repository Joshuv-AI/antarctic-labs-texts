Morse index stability for Yang-Mills connections
Mario Gauvrit, Paul Laurain

Abstract
We prove stability results of the Morse index plus nullity of Yang-Mills connections in dimension 4 under weak convergence. Precisely we establish that the sum of the Morse indices and the nullity of a bounded sequence of Yang-Mills connections is asymptotically bounded above by the sum of the Morse index and the nullity of the weak limit and the bubbles while the Morse indices are asymptotically bounded below by the sum of the Morse index of the weak limit and the bubbles.

\mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}F_{A}=0.

One consequence of the
\epsilon
-regularity result is that sequence of Yang-Mills a connection must satisfies a concentration-compactness phenomena. More precisely, locally either the energy doesn’t concentrate and then, up to gauge, the sequence of connections strongly converges to a limit connection, or the energy concentrate and we must rescale the sequence of connections around the concentration point to get a non-trivial weak limit, i.e a Yang-Mills connection over

One natural question once the quantization of the energy is achieved is to understand how are linked the indices of the sequence of connections and its bubble-tree limits, see section 2 for a definition of the index of a Yang-Mills connection. The Morse index stability for sequences of Yang-Mills connections is of particular interest, motivated by constructing Yang-Mills connections in dimension 4 by min-max procedures. Additionally, the theory of Yang-Mills connections in dimension 4 has many similarities with the theory of harmonic maps in dimension 2. In a recent work
[
5
]
, F. Da Lio, M. Gianocca, and T. Rivière developed a new method to show upper semi-continuity results in geometric analysis, which they applied to conformally invariant Lagrangians in dimension 2 (that include harmonic maps). Their theory was then extended to the the setting of Willmore immersions
[
17
]
and biharmonic maps
[
16
]

Compared to the lower semi-continuity Morse index estimates, precise pointwise estimates of the sequence of solutions in regions of loss of compactness, the so-called
neck regions

Second variation : computation and finiteness of the Morse index

We start by remind the first variations of the Yang-Mills functional in our framework.

Proposition 2.1
.

For every connection
A\in\mathfrak{U}_{G}(M)
, for all
a\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(M,T^{*}M\otimes\mathfrak{g})
,

\mathrm{D}\mathcal{YM}_{A}(a)=\langle F_{A},\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle.

(3)

Proof.

Let us show that we have, for
t\in\mathbf{R}
,

F_{A+ta}=F_{A}+t\mathrm{d}_{A}a+t^{2}a\wedge a\text{ in }\mathrm{L}^{2}.

(4)

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(M,\Lambda^{2}M\otimes\mathfrak{g})
then

\displaystyle\langle F_{A+ta},\phi\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\mathrm{d}^{*}\phi,A+ta\rangle_{h}+\langle\phi,(A+ta)\wedge(A+ta)\rangle_{h}\mathrm{vol}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\mathrm{d}^{*}\phi,A\rangle_{h}+\langle\phi,A\wedge A\rangle_{h}\mathrm{vol}_{h}+t\int_{M}\langle\mathrm{d}^{*}\phi,a\rangle_{h}+\langle\phi,A\wedge a+A\wedge a\rangle_{h}\mathrm{vol}_{h}

\displaystyle+t^{2}\int_{M}\langle\phi,a\wedge a\rangle_{h}\mathrm{vol}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle F_{A},\phi\rangle+t\langle\phi,da+A\wedge a+a\wedge a\rangle+t^{2}\langle\phi,a\wedge a\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\phi,F_{A}\rangle+t\langle\phi,\mathrm{d}_{A}a\rangle+t^{2}\langle\phi,a\wedge a\rangle.

Hence we have the desired equality in distributional sense. Moreover we remark that all right-hand side term are in
\mathrm{L}^{2}
, in particular
\mathrm{d}_{A}a
is in
\mathrm{L}^{2}
since by the improved Sobolev embedding, see lecture 32 of
[
29
]
,
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{4,2}
and by Holder inequality on Lorentz spaces, see section 1.4 of
[
9
]
,
\mathrm{L}^{4,2}.\mathrm{L}^{4,\infty}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{2,2}=\mathrm{L}^{2}
. The proposition follows directly from (
4
).
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

If
A\in\mathfrak{U}_{G}(M)
is a Yang-Mills connection, for all
a,b\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(M,T^{*}M\otimes\mathfrak{g})
,

\mathrm{D}^{2}\mathcal{YM}_{A}(a,b)=\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}a,\mathrm{d}_{A}b\rangle+\langle F_{A},[a,b]\rangle=\langle L_{A}a,b\rangle

(5)

where

L_{A}a=\mathrm{d}_{A}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\star[\star F_{A},a].

Proof.

From

\mathrm{d}_{A+a}b=\mathrm{d}_{A}b+[a,b],

F_{A+a}-F_{A}=\mathrm{d}_{A}a+a\wedge a,

and the embedding
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{4,2}
, we deduce, by substituting in (
3
) :

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}\mathcal{YM}_{A+a}(b)

\displaystyle=\langle F_{A}+\mathrm{d}_{A}a+a\wedge a,\mathrm{d}_{A}b+[a,b]\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\mathcal{YM}_{A}(b)+\langle F_{A},[a,b]\rangle+\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}a,\mathrm{d}_{A}b\rangle+\mathrm{O}\left(\|a\|^{2}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,2}}\|b\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,2}}\right)

which implies

\mathrm{D}^{2}\mathcal{YM}_{A}(a,b)=\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}a,\mathrm{d}_{A}b\rangle+\langle F_{A},[a,b]\rangle.

Furthermore

\displaystyle\tr([a,b]\wedge\star F_{A})=\tr([b,a]\wedge\star F_{A})

\displaystyle=\tr\left((b\wedge a+a\wedge b)\wedge\star F_{A}\right)

\displaystyle=\tr\left(b\wedge(a\wedge\star F_{A})\right)+\tr\left(a\wedge(b\wedge\star F_{A})\right)

\displaystyle=\tr\left(b\wedge(a\wedge\star F_{A})\right)-\tr\left((b\wedge\star F_{A})\wedge a\right)

\displaystyle=\tr(b\wedge[a,\star F_{A}])

\displaystyle=-\tr(b\wedge[\star F_{A},a])

\displaystyle=\tr(b\wedge\star\star[\star F_{A},a])

and we obtain the following identity

\langle F_{A},[a,b]\rangle=\int_{M}\tr([a,b]\wedge\star F_{A})=\int_{M}\tr(b\wedge\star\star[\star F_{A},a])=\langle b,\star[\star F_{A},a]\rangle.

Therefore the second variation has the following expression:

\mathrm{D}^{2}\mathcal{YM}_{A}(a,b)=\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}_{A}a+\star[\star F_{A},a],b\rangle.

∎

Definition 2.3
.

Q_{A}
is the quadratic form defined by

Q_{A}(a):=\mathrm{D}^{2}\mathcal{YM}_{A}(a,a)=\langle L_{A}a,a\rangle.

Remark
.

The functional
\mathcal{YM}
is gauge invariant, so is its second derivative, namely

Q_{A^{g}}(g^{-1}ag)=Q_{A}(a)\text{ for all }g\in\mathrm{W}^{1,(4,\infty)}.

This comes from the pointwise equality :

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}_{A^{g}}\left(g^{-1}ag\right)&=d(g^{-1}ag)+[g^{-1}dg+g^{-1}Ag,g^{-1}ag]\\
&=\cancel{dg^{-1}\wedge ag}+g^{-1}dag\cancel{-g^{-1}a\wedge dg}+\cancel{g^{-1}dg\wedge g^{-1}ag}\\
&+g^{-1}A\wedge ag+\cancel{g^{-1}a\wedge dg}+g^{-1}a\wedge Ag\\
&=g^{-1}(da+{A,a})g\\
&=g^{-1}\left(\mathrm{d}_{A}a\right)g.\end{split}

(6)

A consequence of the gauge invariance is that
Q_{A}
has a huge kernel, as shown in the following result :

Proposition 2.4
.

\Imag{\mathrm{d}_{A}}\subset\ker Q_{A}

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(M,\mathfrak{g})
,we introduce
g_{t}=\exp(t\phi)
(well-defined and
G
-valued if

is small enough). Since
A^{g_{t}}
is a Yang-Mills connection, for all

\mathrm{D}\mathcal{YM}_{A^{g_{t}}}(a)=0,

which implies, by differentiating with respect to

and evaluating at

\mathrm{D}^{2}\mathcal{YM}_{A}\left(\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}A^{g_{t}}\right)_{|t=0},a\right)=0.

Furthermore, since
g_{t}=\mathrm{id}+t\phi+o(t)
and
\mathrm{d}g_{t}=t\mathrm{d}\phi+o(t)
, we get

\displaystyle A^{g_{t}}=g_{t}^{-1}Ag_{t}+g_{t}^{-1}\mathrm{d}g_{t}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}-t\phi+o(t))A(\mathrm{id}+t\phi+o(t))+(\mathrm{id}+o(1))(t\mathrm{d}\phi+o(t))

\displaystyle=A+t\left([A,\phi]+\mathrm{d}\phi\right)+o(t)

\displaystyle=A+t\,\mathrm{d}_{A}\phi+o(t).

Hence
\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}A^{g_{t}}\right)_{|t=0}=\mathrm{d}_{A}\phi
and

\mathrm{D}^{2}\mathcal{YM}_{A}(\mathrm{d}_{A}\phi,a)=0

i.e.

\Imag\mathrm{d}_{A}\subset\ker Q_{A}
.

Recall the index of a quadratic form

\ind q=\sup\left\{\dim W\mid q_{|W}<0\right\}.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{A}a:=\left(\mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}\mathrm{d}_{A}+\mathrm{d}_{A}\mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}\right)a+\star[\star F_{A},a]
and
\mathcal{Q}_{A}(a)=\langle\mathcal{L}_{A}a,a\rangle
. The index and kernel of
Q_{A}
can be expressed from those of

as follows :

\displaystyle\ind Q_{A}

\displaystyle=\ind{\mathcal{Q}_{A}}

\displaystyle\ker Q_{A}

\displaystyle=\ker{\mathcal{Q}_{A}}\oplus\Imag\mathrm{d}_{A}

Since
\mathcal{Q}_{A}(a)=\|\mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}a\|^{2}+Q_{A}(a)

(7)

),(

) and (

).

For the second point, we already have

, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{A}(a)

\displaystyle\geqslant\Lambda\int_{M}|a|^{2}_{h}\mathrm{vol}_{h}+\int_{M}\langle F_{A},a\wedge a\rangle_{h}\mathrm{vol}_{h}

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{M}\left(\Lambda-C|F_{A}|_{h}\right)|a|^{2}_{h}\mathrm{vol}_{h}.

We conclude that if
W
a subspace on which
\mathcal{Q}_{A}
is negative-definite,
W\cap\left(\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}\right)^{\perp}=\{0\}
. Consequently,
\dim W\leqslant\mathrm{codim}\left(\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}\right)^{\perp}=\dim\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}<+\infty
so
\ind\mathcal{Q}_{A}<+\infty
. A similar argument can be applied to prove that
\ker\mathcal{Q}_{A}
is also finite dimensional.
∎

3
-
Contribution of the necks to the second variation

3.1
-
Positive contribution of the necks

In order to study the behavior of the second variation in the neck regions, the method of Da Lio-Gianocca-Rivière requires sharp pointwise estimates, in contrast to the one coming from the
\varepsilon
-regularity result. Application of
\epsilon
-regularity, see theorem
1.2
, gives: for all
R>r>0
and for all Yang-Mills connection
A
on
\mathrm{B}_{2R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r/2}
, if
E=\|F_{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{2R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r/2})}\leqslant\varepsilon
for
x\in\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}
,

|F_{A}(x)|\leqslant C\frac{E}{|x|^{2}}.

(10)

We will explain in the following section why such estimates is not enough in the context of Yang-Mills connections. In other frameworks
[
5
,
17
,
16
]
, refined estimated were obtained using improved energy quantization in Lorentz’s spaces. Even if
\mathrm{L}^{2,1}
-energy quantization for Yang-Mills connection is known by the authors
[
8
]
, such estimates can be obtained independently: the following result was from proved in
[
22
]
in the flat case and the technical details to extend it to the general case were studied in
[
10
, theorem I.1]
. For the sake of clarity, we will state the results of this section in the Euclidean framework. They remain true on small enough geodesic balls of a Riemaniann manifold.

Theorem 3.1
.

There exist
C,\varepsilon>0
, such that, for all
R>r>0
and for all Yang-Mills connection
A
on
\mathrm{B}_{2R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r/2}
, if
E=\|F_{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{2R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r/2})}\leqslant\varepsilon
then for
x\in\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}
,

|F_{A}(x)|\leqslant C\frac{E}{|x|^{2}}\left(\left(\frac{|x|}{R}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{r}{|x|}\right)^{2}\right)

(11)

Let
R>r>0
. In the following, we will write
\omega_{R,r}(x):=\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\left(\left(\frac{|x|}{R}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{r}{|x|}\right)^{2}\right)
. before to extend this estimate to the connection itself, we give a lemma that allow to cut-off a
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}
-connection in the neck region.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}_{G}^{1,2}(\mathrm{B}_{R})
with
0<R<1
, there exists
C>0
, such that for
0<r<R
there exists
\chi:\mathrm{B}_{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
such that
\chi\equiv 1
on
\mathrm{B}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{B}_{r}
and
\mathrm{supp}\chi\subset\mathrm{B}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{B}_{r/2}
, such that

\|F_{\chi A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{R})}\leq\|F_{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{B}_{r/2})}+C\|A\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(\mathrm{B}_{r}\setminus\mathrm{B}_{r/2})}

Proof.

Let
\chi
a cut-off as in the theorem, with
\|d\chi\|_{\infty}\leq 4/r
, then

\displaystyle F_{\chi A}

\displaystyle=d\chi A+\chi dA+\chi^{2}A\wedge A

\displaystyle=\chi^{2}F_{A}+(\chi-\chi^{2})dA+d\chi A

Then we have

\|F_{\chi A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{R})}\leq\|F_{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{B}_{r/2})}+\|dA\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{r}\setminus\mathrm{B}_{r/2})}+\frac{4}{r}\|A\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{r}\setminus\mathrm{B}_{r/2})}

But, by Cauchy-Schwartz,

\|A\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{r}\setminus\mathrm{B}_{r/2})}\leq Cr\|A\|_{\mathrm{L}^{4}(\mathrm{B}_{r}\setminus\mathrm{B}_{r/2})}

which achieves the proof thanks Sobolev’s embedding.
∎

From the
\varepsilon
-regularity estimate and theorem
11
, we deduce the following result:

Corollary 3.3
.

There exists
C,\varepsilon
, such that, for all
R>r>0
and for all connection
A\in\mathfrak{U}_{G}(\mathrm{B}_{4R})
satisfying the Yang-Mills equation on
\mathrm{B}_{2R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r/2}
, if
E=\|F_{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{4R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r/8})}\leqslant\varepsilon
, there exists a gauge
g\in\mathrm{W}^{1,(4,\infty)}(\mathrm{B}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{B}_{r})
in which, for all
x\in\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}
,

|F_{A}(x)|+|A^{g}(x)|^{2}\leqslant CE\omega_{R,r}(x).

(12)

Proof.

According to lemma
A.6
, there exists
\sigma\in\mathrm{W}^{1,(4,\infty)}(\mathrm{B}_{2R},G)
such that
A^{\sigma}
is
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}
on
\mathrm{B}_{2R}
. Moreover, if
\varepsilon
is small enough, then we can assume
\|A^{\sigma}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(\mathrm{B}_{r/2}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r/4})}\leqslant C\varepsilon
. We can use a cutoff function
\chi
as in lemma
3.2
changing
r
by
r/2
, we get

\displaystyle\|F_{\chi A^{\sigma}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{2R})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|F_{A^{\sigma}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{2R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r/4})}+C\|A^{\sigma}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(\mathrm{B}_{r/2}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r/4})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\varepsilon

Having
\varepsilon
small enough, we can therefore assume that
\|F_{\chi A^{\sigma}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{2R})}
is less than the
\varepsilon_{G}
of theorem
1.1
. We deduce the existence of
\tilde{g}\in\mathrm{W}^{1,(4,\infty)}(\mathrm{B}_{2R},G)
which is a Coulomb gauge for
A^{\sigma}
on
\mathrm{B}_{2R}\setminus\mathrm{B}_{r/2}
, meaning that
g:=\sigma\tilde{g}
is a Coulomb gauge for
A
. Since for all
x\in\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}
,
\mathrm{B}_{\rho/2}(x)\subset\mathrm{B}_{2\rho}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\rho}\subset\mathrm{B}_{2R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r/2}
where
\rho=2|x|/3
, the
\varepsilon
-regularity estimates from theorem
10
give

|x||A^{g}(x)|\leqslant C\frac{\rho}{2}\|A^{g}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathrm{B}_{\rho/4}(x))}\leqslant C\|F_{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{\rho/2}(x))}\leqslant C\|F_{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{2\rho}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\rho})}.

Using the energy estimate of theorem
11
, we get

\|F_{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{2\rho}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\rho})}\leqslant CE|\mathrm{B}_{2\rho}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\rho}|^{1/2}\sup_{y\in\mathrm{B}_{2\rho}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\rho}}\omega_{R,r}(y)\leqslant CE|x|^{2}\omega_{R,r}(x).

Finally, since
\omega_{R,r}(x)\leqslant 2/|x|^{2}
,

|A^{g}(x)|^{2}\leqslant\frac{C}{|x|^{2}}\|F_{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{2\rho}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\rho})}^{2}\leqslant CE|x|^{2}\omega_{R,r}(x)\times\omega_{R,r}(x)\leqslant CE\omega_{R,r}(x).

∎

Furthermore, we have the following inequalities:

Proposition 3.4
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
R>r>0
, for all
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r},\mathfrak{g})
-valued
1

\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}|a|^{2}\omega_{R,r}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant C\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}|\mathrm{d}a|^{2}+|\mathrm{d}^{*}a|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

(13)

Proof.

It suffices to combine the inequalities from proposition
A.13
and lemma
A.14
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

There exist
c_{0},\varepsilon>0
, such that, for all
R>r>0
and for all connection
A\in\mathfrak{U}_{G}(\mathrm{B}_{4R})
satisfying the Yang-Mills equation on
\mathrm{B}_{2R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r/2}
if
E=\|F_{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{4R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r/8})}\leqslant\varepsilon
then for all
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r},\mathfrak{g})
-valued
1
- form
a
,

\mathcal{Q}_{A}(a):=\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}\left(|\mathrm{d}_{A}a|^{2}+|\mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}a|^{2}+\langle F_{A},[a,a]\rangle\right)\mathrm{d}x\geqslant c_{0}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}|a|^{2}\omega_{R,r}\mathrm{d}x.

(14)

Proof.

Using the fact that, for any gauge
g
,
\mathrm{d}_{A^{g}}a^{g}=\mathrm{d}a^{g}+[A^{g},a^{g}]
and
\mathrm{d}_{A^{g}}^{*}a^{g}=\mathrm{d}^{*}a^{g}-\star[A^{g},\star a^{g}]
, where
a^{g}:=g^{-1}ag
, we get :

\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}|\mathrm{d}a^{g}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}2\left(|\mathrm{d}_{A^{g}}a^{g}|^{2}+|[A^{g},a^{g}]|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x

and according to (
6
),

\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}|\mathrm{d}a^{g}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}2\left(|\mathrm{d}_{A}a|^{2}+|[A^{g},a^{g}]|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x

and similarly

\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}|\mathrm{d}^{*}a^{g}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}2\left(|\mathrm{d}_{A}^{*}a|^{2}+\left|\star[A^{g},\star a^{g}]\right|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x.

We apply this with the gauge from corollary
3.3
and combine it with proposition
3.4
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{A}(a)

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(|\mathrm{d}a^{g}|^{2}+|\mathrm{d}^{*}a^{g}|^{2}\right)-C\left(|A^{g}|^{2}+|F_{A}|\right)|a^{g}|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geqslant\left(\frac{1}{2C}-CE\right)\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}|a^{g}|^{2}\omega_{R,r}\mathrm{d}x=\left(\frac{1}{2C}-CE\right)\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}|a|^{2}\omega_{R,r}\mathrm{d}x.

We complete the proof by taking
\displaystyle\varepsilon\leqslant\frac{1}{4C^{2}}
and
\displaystyle c_{0}=\frac{1}{4C}
.
∎

3.2
-
The need for sharp neck estimates

The goal of this section is to show that, even in the more favorable setting of Yang-Mills connections, the
\varepsilon
-regularity estimates have to be refined. We will use the following weighted Poincaré inequality:

Proposition 3.6
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
R>r>0
and for all
\mathrm{W}_{0}^{1,2}(\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r},\mathfrak{g})
-valued 1-form
a
:

\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}\frac{|a|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant C\|\nabla a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}\right)}^{2}.

(15)

Proof.

See proposition
A.12
.
∎

As a consequence (with the same proof as theorem
3.5
), for all
\mathrm{W}_{0}^{1,2}(\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r},\mathfrak{g})
-valued 1-form
a
,

\mathcal{Q}_{A}(a)\geqslant c\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}\frac{|a|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x,

which is a first result of positive contribution of the necks to the second derivative. Wanting to diagonalize this quadratic form, we would need compactness in the embedding coming from (
15
) (see lemmas
A.15
and
A.17
). However the embedding
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\mathrm{B}_{1})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{1},\mathrm{d}x/|x|^{2})
is not compact : fix a non trivial
a\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(\mathrm{B}_{1},T^{*}\mathrm{B}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g})
with compact support and introduce, for
\varepsilon\in]0;1]
,
a_{\varepsilon}=\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}a
where
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)=x/\varepsilon
. Since
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
is conformal in
\mathrm{B}_{1}
, we

\int_{\mathrm{B}_{\varepsilon}}\left(|\mathrm{d}a_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\mathrm{d}^{*}a_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1}}\left(|\mathrm{d}a|^{2}+|\mathrm{d}^{*}a|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x,

\int_{\mathrm{B}_{\varepsilon}}|a_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathrm{B}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}|a|^{2}\circ\varphi_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}x=\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1}}|a|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

and

\int_{\mathrm{B}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|a_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathrm{B}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|a|^{2}\circ\varphi_{\varepsilon}}{|x/\varepsilon|^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\varepsilon^{4}}=\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1}}\frac{|a|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x.

In particular,
(a_{\varepsilon})
is bounded in
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}
. Furthermore

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|a_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1}}\frac{|a|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x.

(16)

Since
a_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0

a.e.
, if a sequence
(\varepsilon_{n})
converging to zero was such that

a_{\varepsilon_{n}}\rightarrow a_{0}
in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{1},\mathrm{d}x/|x|^{2})
then Fatou lemma would imply

\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1}}\frac{|a_{0}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1}}\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}|a_{0}-a_{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x|^{2}}\leqslant\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1}}|a_{0}-a_{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x|^{2}}=0

i.e.

a_{0}=0

a.e.
This is a contradiction with (
16
). Thus,
(a_{\varepsilon})
has no converging subsequence in

\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{1},\mathrm{d}x/|x|^{2})
.

4
-
Morse index semi-continuity

This section is dedicated to the study of the Morse index stability for Yang-Mills connections. To simplify notations and clarify the exposition, we consider the case where there is only one bubble. It is easy to deduce the general case by splitting the manifold into neck regions, bubbles and thick part and to treat each neck region independently as in the case of single bubble. Hence we will only be considering the following special case of theorem
1.3
: a sequence of Yang-Mills connections
(A_{k})
on a closed 4-manifold
M
which converges into
\mathfrak{C}_{G,\mathrm{loc}}^{\infty}(M\setminus\{p\})
to
A_{\infty}\in\mathfrak{U}_{G}(M)
a Yang-Mills connection and there exists
p_{k}\rightarrow p
and
\lambda_{k}\rightarrow 0
such that
\phi_{k}^{*}A_{k}
converges into
\mathfrak{C}_{G,\mathrm{loc}}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{4})
to
\widehat{A}_{\infty}\in\mathfrak{U}_{G}(M)
where
\phi_{k}(x)=p_{k}+\lambda_{k}x
in local coordinates and
\widehat{A}_{\infty}=\pi_{*}\tilde{A}_{\infty}
where
\tilde{A}_{\infty}\in\mathfrak{U}_{G}(S^{4})
is a Yang-Mills connections and

is the stereographic projection.

Recall that for all
k\in\mathbf{N}
and
a\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(M,T^{*}M\otimes\mathfrak{g})

Q_{A_{k}}(a):=\mathrm{D}^{2}\mathcal{YM}_{A_{k}}(a,a)=\int_{M}|\mathrm{d}_{A_{k}}a|_{h}^{2}+\langle F_{A_{k}},[a,a]\rangle_{h}\mathrm{vol}_{h}.

(17)

The Morse index of
\mathcal{YM}
at

is defined by:

\mathrm{ind}_{\mathcal{YM}}(A_{k}):=\ind Q_{A_{k}}=\sup\{\dim W\mid Q_{\left.A_{k}\right|W}<0\}.

A first stability result is the lower semi-continuity one:

Theorem 4.1
.

For
k
large enough,

\mathrm{ind}_{\mathcal{YM}}(A_{k})\geqslant\mathrm{ind}_{\mathcal{YM}}(A_{\infty})+\mathrm{ind}_{\mathcal{YM}}(\widehat{A}_{\infty}).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
a\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(M,T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{g})
and assume there exists
\eta>0
such that

•

,

•

•

.

Consider a finite open cover

According to theorem
A.3
, we can always assume that
(A_{k})
is bounded in
\mathrm{L}^{4,\infty}(M)
, which imply that, for all
\alpha
,
(g_{k}^{\alpha})
is bounded in
\mathrm{W}^{1,(4,\infty)}

a.e. on

.

\displaystyle\underset{k\to+\infty}{\rightarrow}Q_{A_{\infty}}(a_{\eta}),

\displaystyle\underset{k\to+\infty}{\rightarrow}Q_{\widehat{A}_{\infty}}(\widehat{a}_{\eta}),

and using the fact that

Using

There exists
C>0
such that for all
R>r>0
, for all
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}\left(\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}\right)
-valued
1
-form
a
,

\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}\frac{|a|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant C\|\nabla a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}\right)}^{2}

Proof.

Using
\mathrm{L}^{4,2}\cdot\mathrm{L}^{4,\infty}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{2}
and
\dot{\mathrm{W}}_{0}^{1,2}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{4,2}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}\frac{|a|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x=\left\|\frac{1}{|x|}a\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|\frac{1}{|x|}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{4,\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{4})}\|a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{4,2}\left(\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left\|\frac{1}{|x|}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{4,\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{4})}\|\nabla a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}\right)}^{2}.

We conclude thanks to

\left\|\frac{1}{|x|}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{4,\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{4})}=\sup_{t>0}t|\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{4},|x|^{-1}>t\}|^{1/4}=\sup_{t>0}t\left|\mathrm{B}_{1/t}\right|^{1/4}=\left|\mathrm{B}_{1}\right|^{1/4}<+\infty.

∎

Proposition A.13
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
R>r>0
, for all
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}\left(\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}\right)
-valued
1
-form
a

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}|a|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant CR^{2}\|\nabla a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}\right)}^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}\frac{|a|^{2}}{|x|^{4}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{r^{2}}\|\nabla a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}\right)}^{2}.

Proof.

The first inequality is true for
R=1
and
r\in]0,1[
(using Poincaré inequality), then we apply it to

m_{R}^{\ast}a\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}\left(\mathrm{B}_{1}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r/R}\right)
where
m_{R}:x\mapsto Rx
).

We prove the second inequality using the conformal invariance of the right-hand side and the first one : introduce
b=\varphi^{\ast}a
(where
\varphi:x\mapsto x/|x|^{2}
is the inversion), we have

b\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}\left(\mathrm{B}_{1/r}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{1/R}\right)
so

\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1/r}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{1/R}}|b|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\frac{C}{r^{2}}\|\nabla a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}\right)}^{2}.

Then, since
\varphi^{\ast}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{|x|^{8}}\mathrm{d}x
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1/r}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{1/R}}|b|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1/r}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{1/R}}\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\right)^{2}|a|^{2}\circ\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1/r}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{1/R}}\varphi^{\ast}\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{4}}|a|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{r}}\frac{|a|^{2}}{|x|^{4}}\mathrm{d}x.

∎

Diagonalization of quadratic forms

A.3.1
Spectral theory results

Lemma A.15
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},D(\mathcal{L}))
a self-adjoint operator, bounded below, on a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
. Denote by
q
the corresponding closed quadratic form with domain
\mathcal{Q}
. Assume that
\mathcal{L}
has compact resolvent and denote by
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
an Hilbert basis of eigenvectors, with corresponding eigenvalues
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
(in ascending order). Then

\mathcal{Q}=\left\{x\in\mathfrak{H};\sum_{n\in\mathbf{N}}|\lambda_{n}||\langle e_{n},x\rangle|^{2}<+\infty\right\}

and if
x\in\mathcal{Q}
,
q(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbf{N}}\lambda_{n}|\langle x,e_{n}\rangle|^{2}
.
Furthermore,

\mathcal{Q}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\leqslant 0}\ker(\mathcal{L}-\lambda)\oplus\overline{\bigoplus_{\lambda>0}\ker(\mathcal{L}-\lambda)}

where
\mathcal{Q}
is equipped with the Hilbert norm associated with the quadratic form
\mathcal{N}:x\mapsto q(x)+(1-\lambda_{0})\|x\|^{2}
.

Remark
.

The hypothesis
\mathcal{L}
has compact resolvent is equivalent to the compactness of the embedding
(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{N})\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{H}
.

Proof.

Since
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
,
\sum_{n\in\mathbf{N}}|\lambda_{n}||\langle e_{n},x\rangle|^{2}<+\infty
if and only if

\sum_{n\in\mathbf{N}}\lambda_{n}|\langle e_{n},x\rangle|^{2}
is convergent.

By definition of
\mathcal{Q}
(see
[
14
, théorème 3.10]
),
\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}

is a Hilbert norm on
\mathcal{Q}
and there is a continuous embedding
\mathcal{Q}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{H}
(since
\|x\|^{2}\leqslant\mathcal{N}(x)
).

For all
x\in\mathfrak{H}
,
x=\sum_{n\in\mathbf{N}}\langle e_{n},x\rangle e_{n}
. Introduce
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}=\left\{x\in\mathfrak{H};\sum_{n\in\mathbf{N}}|\lambda_{n}||\langle e_{n},x\rangle|^{2}<+\infty\right\}
. Assume
\sum_{n\in\mathbf{N}}|\lambda_{n}||\langle e_{n},x\rangle|^{2}<+\infty
. Then, writing
x_{N}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\langle e_{n},x\rangle e_{n}
, we have
x_{N}\in\mathcal{Q}
,
x_{N}\rightarrow x
in

\mathfrak{H}
and

q(x_{N+p}-x_{N})=\sum_{n=N}^{N+p}\lambda_{n}|\langle e_{n},x\rangle|^{2}

so
(x_{N})
is a Cauchy sequence for
\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}
. We deduce
x\in\mathcal{Q}
and
q(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbf{N}}\lambda_{n}|\langle e_{n},x\rangle|^{2}
. Therefore
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{Q}
.

If
{x\in\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}}^{\bot_{\mathcal{N}}}
then for all
n\in\mathbf{N}
,
x
is orthogonal (for
\mathcal{N}
) to
e_{n}

i.e.

(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{0}+1)|\langle e_{n},x\rangle|^{2}=0

so
\langle e_{n},x\rangle=0
and we conclude
x=0
. It follows that

\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
is dense in
\mathcal{Q}
for the norm
\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}
. Let’s show that it is also closed. If
(p_{k})_{k\in\mathbf{N}}
is a sequence of elements of
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
and
x\in\mathcal{Q}
are such that
\mathcal{N}(p_{k}-x)\rightarrow 0
then, we have in particular

\|p_{k}-x\|^{2}\leqslant q(p_{k}-x)+(1-\lambda_{0})\|p_{k}-x\|^{2}\underset{k\rightarrow+\infty}{\rightarrow}0

hence
p_{k}\rightarrow x
in
\mathfrak{H}
and
\mathcal{N}(p_{k})\rightarrow\mathcal{N}(x)
so
q(p_{k})\rightarrow q(x)
. Additionally, for
N
large enough,

q(p_{k})=\sum_{n\in\mathbf{N}}\lambda_{n}|\langle e_{n},p_{k}\rangle|^{2}\geqslant\sum_{n=0}^{N}\lambda_{n}|\langle e_{n},p_{k}\rangle|^{2}

so

q(x)=\lim q(p_{k})\geqslant\lim\sum_{n=0}^{N}\lambda_{n}|\langle e_{n},p_{k}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\lambda_{n}|\langle e_{n},x\rangle|^{2}

and having
N
go to
+\infty
, we conclude that
x\in\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma A.16
.

With the notations of the previous lemma, we have

\dim\left(\bigoplus_{\lambda<0}\ker(\mathcal{L}-\lambda)\right)=\mathrm{ind}q,

\ker\mathcal{L}=\ker q.

Furthermore,

\mathrm{ind}q+\dim\ker q=\inf\{\mathrm{codim}W\mid q_{|W}>0\}<+\infty.

In particular, if
F
is a subspace on which
q
is negative semi-definite then
\dim F\leqslant\mathrm{ind}q+\dim\ker q
.

Remark
.

Let
F,W
two subspaces of a vector space
E
. Assume that
F\cap W=\{0\}
. Then
\dim F\leqslant\mathrm{codim}W
. Indeed, the restriction to
F
of the projection
E\rightarrow E/W
is one-to-one.

Proof.

Firstly,
\ker q=\ker\mathcal{L}
and on
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda<0}\ker(\mathcal{L}-\lambda)
,
q
is negative-definite so

\dim\left(\bigoplus_{\lambda<0}\ker(\mathcal{L}-\lambda)\right)\leqslant\mathrm{ind}q.

Let
W
a subspace of
\mathcal{Q}
such that
q_{|W}<0
. Then
W\cap\overline{\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda\geqslant 0}\ker(\mathcal{L}-\lambda)}=\{0\}
: if
w\in W\cap\overline{\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda\geqslant 0}\ker(\mathcal{L}-\lambda)}
, we can write
w=v+z
with
v\bot z
,
v\in\ker q
et
q(z)\geqslant 0
so

q(w)=q(z)\geqslant\lambda_{0}\|z\|^{2}

and we deduce
w=0
. Thus,

\dim W\leqslant\mathrm{codim}\left(\overline{\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda\geqslant 0}\ker(\mathcal{L}-\lambda)}\right)=\dim\left(\bigoplus_{\lambda<0}\ker(\mathcal{L}-\lambda)\right)

which concludes the proof of the first equality.

Let
W
a subspace of
\mathcal{Q}
such that
q_{|W}>0
. Then
W\cap\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda\leqslant 0}\ker(\mathcal{L}-\lambda)=\{0\}
and

\mathrm{codim}W\geqslant\dim\left(\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda\leqslant 0}\ker(\mathcal{L}-\lambda)\right)=\ind q+\dim\ker q
. Furthermore
q
is positive-definite on

W=\overline{\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda>0}\ker(\mathcal{L}-\lambda)}
and
\mathrm{codim}W=\ind q+\dim\ker q
, which proves the third equality.

If a subspace
F
is such that
q_{|F}<0
, then for all subspace
W
such that
q_{|W}>0
we have
W\cap F=\{0\}
so
\dim F\leqslant\mathrm{codim}W
. The last statement follows.
∎

A.3.2
A diagonalisability condition

Lemma A.17
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{4},h)
a closed Riemannian manifold,
E\to\mathcal{M}
a
G
-bundle and
\nabla
a Yang-Mills connection. Consider a weight function
\omega:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}_{+}
such that
\omega
and
1/\omega
are in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.
Define
\mathfrak{H}=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M},T^{*}M\otimes\mathfrak{g})
equipped with the inner product associated to the norm

\|a\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\omega}}=\int_{M}|a|^{2}_{h}\omega\mathrm{vol}_{h},

and
W:=\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(\mathcal{M},T^{*}M\otimes\mathfrak{g})
. Fix a bounded operator
B:\mathfrak{H}\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}
and assume that
B
is symmetric for the usuel
\mathrm{L}^{2}
inner product.

The operator
\omega^{-1}(\Delta_{\nabla}+B)
on
\mathfrak{H}
with domain
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g})
has a unique self-adjoint extension with domain is a subset of
W
. Furthermore, this extension has compact resolvent and is bounded below.

Remark
.

Since
\omega
and
1/\omega
are in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
, the
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\omega}
norm and the usual
\mathrm{L}^{2}
norm are equivalent on
\mathfrak{H}
.

Proof.

It is enough to show this result with
B=0
and then that
\omega^{-1}B(\omega^{-1}\Delta_{\nabla}+1)^{-1}
is a compact operator of
\mathfrak{H}
(see
[
14
, corollaries 3.19, 5.24, theorem 5.31]
).

Introduce
(\mathcal{L}^{\min},D(\mathcal{L}^{\min}))=\left(\omega^{-1}\Delta_{\nabla},\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g})\right)
and
Q

the corresponding quadratic form. If
a\in D(\mathcal{L}^{\min})
,

Q(a)=\langle a,\mathcal{L}^{\min}a\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\omega}}\geqslant 0

so
Q
is bounded below and closed on
W
. We define
(\mathcal{L},D(\mathcal{L}))
to be the Friedrichs extension of
\mathcal{L}^{\min}
.
\mathcal{L}
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator and we have

D(\mathcal{L})=\left\{a\in W\mid\Delta_{\nabla}a\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\right\}=\mathrm{W}^{2,2}(\mathcal{M},T^{*}M\otimes\mathfrak{g}).

Since the embedding
W\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{H}
is compact by Rellich-Kondrachov theorem,
\mathcal{L}
has compact resolvent.

To conclude, since
\omega^{-1}B:\mathfrak{H}\to\mathfrak{H}
is bounded and
(\omega^{-1}\Delta_{\nabla}+1)^{-1}:\mathfrak{H}\to\mathfrak{H}
is compact,
\omega^{-1}B(\omega^{-1}\Delta_{\nabla}+1)^{-1}:\mathfrak{H}\to\mathfrak{H}
is compact, which concludes the proof.
∎

Global conformal blocks via Shadow formalism
Vladimir Belavin, J. Ramos Cabezas

Abstract
We study 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{sl}_{2} and 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \mathfrak{sl}_{3} global conformal blocks on a sphere and a torus, using the shadow formalism. These blocks arise in the context of Virasoro and 𝒲 3 \mathcal{W}_{3} conformal field theories in the large central charge limit. In the 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{sl}_{2} case, we demonstrate that the shadow formalism yields the known expressions in terms of conformal partial waves. Then, we extend this approach to the 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \mathfrak{sl}_{3} case and show that it allows to build simple integral representations for 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \mathfrak{sl}_{3} global blocks. We demonstrate this construction on two examples: the four-point block on the sphere and the one-point torus block.

2.1
Basics of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
Shadows formalism

Basic concepts

Let us introduce the shadow operator of the primary field
\varphi_{\Delta}(z,\bar{z})
. Taking into account (
2.3
), the shadow operator is defined as

\tilde{\varphi}_{\Delta^{*}}(z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}w\frac{\varphi_{\Delta}(w,\bar{w})}{|z-w|^{4(1-\Delta)}},

(2.9)

where
d^{2}w=dwd\bar{w}
represents integration over the complex plane, and
\Delta^{*}
is the holomorphic and antiholomorphic conformal dimension of the shadow operator (which is also a primary field)

\Delta^{*}=1-\Delta,

(2.10)

and
\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}
is a normalization coefficient

\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}=\pi^{2}\frac{\Gamma(2\Delta-1)\Gamma(1-2\Delta)}{\Gamma(2-2\Delta)\Gamma(2\Delta)}.

(2.11)

Below, we will show that the shadow operator has the property

\langle\tilde{\varphi}_{\Delta^{*}}(z,\bar{z})\varphi_{\Delta}(w,\bar{w})\rangle=\delta^{2}(z-w),

(2.12)

where the two-dimensional delta function
\delta^{2}(z-w)
is defined according to
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(z,\bar{z})\delta^{2}(z-w)d^{2}z=f(w,\bar{w})
. We define the “projector” operator

\Pi_{\Delta}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}z\varphi_{\Delta}(z,\bar{z})\ket{0}\bra{0}\tilde{\varphi}_{\Delta^{*}}(z,\bar{z}).

(2.13)

It satisfies the property

\Pi_{\Delta_{m}}\Pi_{\Delta_{n}}=\delta_{mn}\Pi_{\Delta_{m}}.

(2.14)

For the operator

\mathcal{P}=\sum_{\Delta\in\mathcal{D}}\Pi_{\Delta},

(2.15)

where
\mathcal{D}
is the domain of admissible conformal dimensions for the considered CFT, one can show that

\mathcal{P}\ket{h}=\ket{h},\quad\text{where $\ket{h}=\varphi_{h}(0,0)\ket{0}$}.

(2.16)

This property follows directly from the definition (
2.13
,
2.15
). By writing explicitly (
2.15
), we have

\mathcal{P}\ket{h}=\sum_{\Delta\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}z\varphi_{\Delta}(z,\bar{z})\ket{0}\bra{0}\tilde{\varphi}_{\Delta^{*}}(z,\bar{z})\varphi_{h}(0,0)\ket{0}.

(2.17)

Because of (
2.12
), we have

\bra{0}\tilde{\varphi}_{\Delta^{*}}(z,\bar{z})\varphi_{h}(0,0)\ket{0}=\begin{cases}\delta_{h,\Delta}\delta^{2}(z),\\
0,\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.18)

Hence

\mathcal{P}\ket{h}=\sum_{\Delta\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}z\varphi_{\Delta}(z,\bar{z})\ket{0}(\delta_{h,\Delta}\delta^{2}(z))=\varphi_{h}(0,0)\ket{0}=\ket{h}.

(2.19)

We notice that since
\tilde{\varphi}_{\Delta^{*}}(z,\bar{z})
is a primary field, one could include an extra contribution to the two-point correlation function

\bra{0}\tilde{\varphi}_{\Delta^{*}}(z,\bar{z})\varphi_{h}(0,0)\ket{0}=\begin{cases}\delta_{h,\Delta}\delta^{2}(z),\\
\delta_{h,1-\Delta}\frac{1}{z^{2(1-\Delta)}\bar{z}^{2(1-\Delta)}},\\
0,\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.20)

However, this modification does not substantially change (
2.19
).
Indeed, assuming (
2.20
), we have

\begin{split}&\mathcal{P}\ket{h}=\sum_{\Delta\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}z\varphi_{\Delta}(z,\bar{z})\ket{0}\bra{0}\tilde{\varphi}_{\Delta^{*}}(z,\bar{z})\varphi_{h}(0,0)\ket{0}=\\
&\sum_{\Delta\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}z\varphi_{\Delta}(z,\bar{z})\ket{0}(\delta_{h,\Delta}\delta^{2}(z)+\delta_{1-\Delta,h}\frac{1}{z^{2(1-\Delta)}\bar{z}^{2(1-\Delta)}})=\\
&\ket{h}+\int d^{2}z\frac{\varphi_{1-h}(z,\bar{z})}{z^{2h}\bar{z}^{2h}}\ket{0}=\ket{h}+\mathcal{N}_{1-h}\tilde{\varphi}_{h}(0,0)\ket{0}=(1+\mathcal{N}_{1-h})\ket{h}\propto\ket{h},\end{split}

(2.21)

where in the third line we used that
\tilde{\varphi}_{h}
can be expressed in terms of its shadow field
\varphi_{h^{*}}
. The result (
2.21
) is essentially the same as (
2.19
).

Delta function

The relation (
2.12
) can be established as follows. By inserting the shadow operator (
2.9
) into the lhs of (
2.12
), and writing it for
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
coordinates, we have

\langle\tilde{\varphi}_{\Delta^{*}}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\varphi_{\Delta}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}}\int d^{2}z\frac{\langle\varphi_{\Delta}(z,\bar{z})\varphi_{\Delta}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\rangle}{|z-z_{2}|^{4(1-\Delta)}}=\delta^{2}(z_{1}-z_{2}).

(2.22)

Since
\langle\varphi_{\Delta}(z,\bar{z})\varphi_{\Delta}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\rangle=\frac{1}{|z-z_{1}|^{4\Delta}}

2.22
) becomes

\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}}\int d^{2}z\frac{1}{|z-z_{2}|^{4(1-\Delta)}|z-z_{1}|^{4\Delta}}=\delta^{2}(z_{1}-z_{2}).

(2.23)

By using the parametrization

,

, eq. (
2.23
) can be written more generally as

\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}}\int d^{2}z\frac{1}{(z-z_{1})^{h_{1}}(z-z_{2})^{h_{2}}}\frac{1}{(\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{1})^{h_{1}}(\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{2})^{h_{2}}}=\delta^{2}(z_{1}-z_{2}),

(2.24)

where
\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}
is expressed in terms of

as

\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}=\frac{\pi^{2}\Gamma({1-h_{1}})\Gamma({1-h_{2}})}{\Gamma({h_{1}})\Gamma({h_{2}})}.

(2.25)

Let us prove (
2.23
) and show that
\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}
is given by (
2.11
) or (
2.25
). For this purpose, we write (
2.23
) in Cartesian coordinates, and use the above parametrization
h_{1},h_{2}
, then (
2.23
) becomes

\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}}\int d^{2}x\frac{1}{(x-x_{1})^{2h_{1}}(x-x_{2})^{2h_{2}}}=\delta^{2}(x_{1}-x_{2}).

(2.26)

By using the relation

\frac{\Gamma(\Delta)}{x^{2\Delta}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt_{1}t_{1}^{\Delta-1}e^{-t_{1}x^{2}},

(2.27)

we rewrite (
2.26
) as follows

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}}\int d^{2}x\frac{1}{(x-x_{1})^{2h_{1}}(x-x_{2})^{2h_{2}}}=\\
&\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}\Gamma({h_{1}})\Gamma({h_{2}})}\int d^{2}x\int_{0}^{\infty}dt_{1}dt_{2}t_{1}^{h_{1}-1}t_{2}^{h_{2}-1}e^{-t_{1}(x-x_{1})^{2}-t_{2}(x-x_{2})^{2}}.\end{split}

(2.28)

After some transformations and integration over
x
, one can convert this integral to

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}}\int d^{2}x\frac{1}{(x-x_{1})^{2h_{1}}(x-x_{2})^{2h_{2}}}=\\
&\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}\Gamma({h_{1}})\Gamma({h_{2}})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt_{1}dt_{2}}{t_{1}+t_{2}}t_{1}^{h_{1}-1}t_{2}^{h_{2}-1}e^{-\frac{t_{1}t_{2}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}{t_{1}+t_{2}}}.\end{split}

(2.29)

For the exponent, we use the Fourier transform

e^{-\alpha x^{2}}=\int d^{2}k\left(e^{-\frac{k^{2}}{4\alpha}}\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\right)e^{-ik\cdot x}.

(2.30)

By multiplying the rhs of (
2.26
) by
e^{ik\cdot(x_{1}-x_{2})}
and performing the two-dimensional integral over the variable
(x_{1}-x_{2})
, we obtain 1. Therefore, by multiplying both sides of (
2.29
) and integrating over
(x_{1}-x_{2})
, we must obtain the same result. Applying this reasoning, we obtain from (
2.29
)

\frac{\pi^{2}}{\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}\Gamma({h_{1}})\Gamma({h_{2}})}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt_{1}t_{1}^{h_{1}-1}e^{-\frac{k^{2}}{4t_{1}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt_{2}t_{2}^{h_{2}-1}e^{-\frac{k^{2}}{4t_{2}}}=1.

(2.31)

This implies that

\frac{\pi^{2}\Gamma({1-h_{1}})\Gamma({1-h_{2}})}{\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}\Gamma({h_{1}})\Gamma({h_{2}})}=1\;,

(2.32)

where we used that
h_{1}+h_{2}=2
. Hence

\mathcal{N}_{\Delta}=\frac{\pi^{2}\Gamma({1-h_{1}})\Gamma({1-h_{2}})}{\Gamma({h_{1}})\Gamma({h_{2}})},

(2.33)

in accordance with (
2.11
) and (
2.25

\langle\varphi_{\Delta_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\varphi_{\Delta_{2}}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\varphi_{\Delta_{3}}(z_{3},\bar{z}_{3})\rangle=C_{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}\Delta_{3}}|V_{\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3}}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})|^{2},

(2.38)

where
z_{ij}=z_{i}-z_{j}

\bra{0}\tilde{\varphi}_{\tilde{\Delta}^{*}}(z,\bar{z})\varphi_{\Delta_{3}}(z_{3},\bar{z}_{3})\varphi_{\Delta_{4}}(z_{4},\bar{z}_{4})\rangle

|V_{1-\tilde{\Delta},\Delta_{3},\Delta_{4}}(z,z_{3},z_{4})|^{2}

2.36

G^{s}_{\mathbf{\Delta}_{4}}(\mathbf{z}_{4},\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{4})=\sum_{\tilde{\Delta}\in\mathcal{D}}B(\mathbf{\Delta}_{4},\tilde{\Delta})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}z|V_{\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\tilde{\Delta}}(z_{1},z_{2},z)|^{2}|V_{1-\tilde{\Delta},\Delta_{3},\Delta_{4}}(z,z_{3},z_{4})|^{2},

(2.39)

where

is proportional to
C_{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}\tilde{\Delta}}C_{\tilde{\Delta}\Delta_{3}\Delta_{4}}
. The object on the rhs of (

4.1
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
invariant functions

The
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
algebra can be represented in the Chevalley basis, which includes two Cartan elements (
h^{1},h^{2}
), along with creation and annihilation generators (
e^{1},e^{2},e^{3}
) and (
f^{1},f^{2},f^{3}
), respectively. These elements satisfy the following relations

\begin{split}&[f^{1},f^{2}]=-f^{3},\quad[e^{1},e^{2}]=e^{3},\quad[e^{1},f^{1}]=h^{1},\\
&[e^{2},f^{2}]=h^{2},\quad[e^{3},f^{3}]=h^{1}+h^{2},\quad[e^{1},f^{3}]=-f^{2},\\
&[e^{2},f^{3}]=f^{1},\quad[e^{3},f^{1}]=-e^{2},\quad[e^{3},f^{2}]=e^{1},\\
\end{split}

(4.1)

with all other commutators being zero. The generators in (
4.1
) can be expressed as linear combinations of the generators in (
3.5
). The generators (
2.7
) correspond to the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
transformation for the variable
z
(representing the physical coordinates of the fields). To represent the generators of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
in terms of differential operators, we introduce three-component isospin variables
Z

Z=(w,x,y).

(4.2)

For a given vector
j=rw_{1}+sw_{2}
, the generators (
4.1
) can be constructed as differential operators acting on monomials of the form
x^{a}y^{b}w^{c}
for
a+c\leq r
,
b\leq s
, as follows

\begin{split}&D_{(j,Z)}(h^{1})=2x\partial_{x}+r-y\partial_{y}+w\partial_{w},\\
&D_{(j,z)}(h^{2})=2y\partial_{y}+s-x\partial_{x}+w\partial_{w},\\
&D_{(j,Z)}(e^{1})=x^{2}\partial_{x}+rx+(w-xy)\partial_{y}+xw\partial_{w},\\
&D_{(j,Z)}(e^{2})=y^{2}\partial_{y}+sy-w\partial_{x},\\
&D_{(j,Z)}(e^{3})=w^{2}\partial_{w}+s(w-xy)+rw+xw\partial_{x}+y(w-xy)\partial_{y},\\
&\partial_{(j,Z)}(f^{1})=-\partial_{x},\\
&D_{(j,Z)}(f^{2})=-\partial_{y}-x\partial_{w},\\
&D_{(j,Z)}(f^{3})=-\partial_{w}.\end{split}

(4.3)

To construct
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
invariant functions, we define the following notations:

\begin{split}&j_{i}=r_{i}w_{1}+s_{i}w_{2}=(r_{i},s_{i}),\quad j_{i}^{w}=(s_{i},r_{i}),\quad j_{i}^{*}=(2-s_{i},2-r_{i}),\\
&Z_{i}=(w_{i},x_{i},y_{i}),\\
&\rho_{ij}=y_{i}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)-\left(w_{i}-w_{j}\right),\\
&\chi_{ijk}=y_{i}w_{j}-w_{i}y_{j}+y_{i}y_{j}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)-y_{i}w_{k}+w_{i}y_{k}+\\
&+y_{i}y_{k}\left(x_{k}-x_{i}\right)-w_{j}y_{k}+y_{j}w_{k}+y_{j}y_{k}\left(x_{j}-x_{k}\right),\\
&\sigma_{ijk}=x_{i}w_{j}-w_{i}x_{j}-x_{i}w_{k}+w_{i}x_{k}-w_{j}x_{k}+x_{j}w_{k},\end{split}

(4.4)

where
j_{i}^{*}
and
j_{i}^{w}
correspond to the maximal Weyl transformation and Dynkin automorphism of the spin
j_{i}
, respectively. An
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}

n

\xi(j_{i}|Z_{i})

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{(j_{i},Z_{i})}(t^{a})\right)\xi(j_{i}|Z_{i})=0,\quad\text{for any $t^{a}\in\{h^{i},e^{i},f^{i}\}$}.

(4.5)

The function
\xi

\begin{split}&\text{if for some $k$ }\quad(j,w_{k})=0,\quad\text{then},\\
&d^{(k)}_{Z_{k}}\xi(j_{i}|Z_{i})=0,\end{split}

(4.6)

where

d^{(1)}_{Z}=\partial_{x}+y\partial_{w},\quad d^{(2)}_{Z}=\partial_{y}.

(4.7)

For two spins

and
j_{2}

\xi(j_{1},j_{2}|Z_{1},Z_{2})=\begin{cases}\rho_{21}^{-r_{1}}\rho_{12}^{-s_{1}},\quad\text{for $j_{1}=j_{2}^{w}$},\\
0,\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(4.8)

For general representations

5
Conclusions

In this work, we have studied global conformal blocks using shadow formalism. Our study focused on
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
global CBs, which arise in the large central charge limit of Virasoro and
\mathcal{W}_{3}
conformal field theories. In sections
2.2
and
2.3
, we examined the representation of sphere and torus
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
global CBs through the shadow formalism. While our primary focus was on the torus topology, we also discussed the spherical case. For
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, our discussion built upon ideas from previous works, we introduced an operator (
2.15
) that acts as a resolution of identity. This allows us to express the CBs in terms of conformal partial waves. We showed that the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
global four-point sphere and one-point torus CBs can be computed using shadow formalism. The construction is quite universal and can be easily generalized to higher multi-point global torus or spherical conformal blocks.

In section
4
, we investigated
\mathcal{W}_{3}
CFT and generalized the shadow formalism to this case; this enables the computation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
global CBs. The formalism was then applied to both spherical and toroidal CBs in sections
4.3
and
4.4
. We verified that this approach yields the established expression (
4.37
) for the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
global four-point sphere CBs. In Section
4.4
, we obtained an integral representation for the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
global one-point torus CB (
4.38
), resulting in the explicit expression (
4.51
). This expression is in complete agreement with the known perturbative expressions (
3.25
). The expression we obtained for
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
global one-point torus CB represents the main result of the present study.

It is interesting to investigate the possibility of constructing
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
global higher multi-point torus CBs involving different OPE channels. In the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
case,
(n+2)
-point sphere CBs correlate with
n
-point torus CBs. It would be interesting to find out whether a similar connection exists in the case of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
. Another direction for generalization is the development of the shadow formalism for general
\mathcal{W}_{N}
two-dimensional CFTs or supersymmetric ones. As demonstrated in the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
cases, such generalization requires the analysis of invariant functions related to the corresponding algebras.

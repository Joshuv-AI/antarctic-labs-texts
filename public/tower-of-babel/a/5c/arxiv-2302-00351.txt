Stable maps to Looijenga pairs built from the plane
Michel van Garrel

Abstract
. Choosing a normal crossings anticanonical divisor of ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} leads to four log Calabi–Yau surfaces, three of which are Looijenga pairs. In this survey article, I describe how to count rational curves in these that intersect each component of the divisor in one point of maximal tangency.



1.
Introduction

A log Calabi–Yau surface
(Y,D)
is a surface
Y
with a choice of normal crossings divisor
D
such that the complement is Calabi–Yau.
(Y,D)
is a Looijenga pair if in addition,
D
has a dimension 0 stratum. Starting with
Y=\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
D
is of degree 3 and there are 4 ways of building a log Calabi–Yau surface:
D=D^{\rm toric}
, the toric divisor given as a union of 3 lines;
D=D_{1}+D_{2}
, the union of a line
D_{1}
and a conic
D_{2}
not tangent to
D_{1}
;
D=D_{3}
a nodal cubic; and
D=E
a smooth cubic. All but the last are Looijenga pairs.

A surprisingly intricate question is to study the geometry of

2.
Genus 0 log Gromov-Witten invariants of maximal tangency

For each Looijenga pair
(\mathbb{P}^{2},D)
, endow
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with the divisorial log structure determined by
D
. The log structure is used as a combinatorial tool to impose tangency conditions along the components of
D
. For a map

(2.1)

f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}

this is achieved by requiring that the pullback map on log structures at marked points is given by multiplication by the tangency imposed at that point. Compactifying the space of maps as in (
2.1
),

[
34
,
19
,
3
]
construct the moduli space
\overline{\mmm}^{\log}_{m}(\mathbb{P}^{2},D,d)
of genus 0 maximally tangent basic stable log maps with
m
marked points, and its virtual fundamental class

[\overline{\mmm}^{\log}_{m}(\mathbb{P}^{2},D,d)]^{\rm vir}\in\hhh_{2(l+m-1)}(\overline{\mmm}^{\log}_{m}(\mathbb{P}^{2},D,d)).

I suppress in the notation the
l
marked points that carry a tangency and keep track of the
m
interior points that do not carry a tangency condition. The notion of basicness/minimality selects a universal log structure needed for algebraicity and compactness of the moduli space.

For
j=1,\dots,m
, the moduli space admits evaluation maps at the
j
th marked points

\operatorname{ev}_{j}:\overline{\mmm}^{\log}_{m}(\mathbb{P}^{2},D,d)\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}.

Imposing passing through a fixed point in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
corresponds to capping the virtual fundamental class with
\operatorname{ev}_{j}^{*}([{\rm pt}])
. Doing this
l-1
times leads to the invariant

N_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{2},D):=\int_{[\overline{\mmm}^{\log}_{l-1}(\mathbb{P}^{2},D,d)]^{\rm vir}}\,\prod_{j=1}^{l-1}\operatorname{ev}_{j}^{*}([{\rm pt}]).

Other insertions may be imposed. For example, for the class toric varieties for which each toric divisor is nef,
[
12
]
computes all invariants with point and psi classes.

Shalika germs for tamely ramified elements in G ​ L n GL_{n}
Oscar Kivinen, Cheng-Chiang Tsai

Abstract
. Degenerating the action of the elliptic Hall algebra on the Fock space, we give a combinatorial formula for the Shalika germs of tamely ramified regular semisimple elements γ \gamma of G ​ L n GL_{n} over a nonarchimedean local field. As a byproduct, we compute the weight polynomials of affine Springer fibers in type A and orbital integrals of tamely ramified regular semisimple elements. We conjecture that the Shalika germs of γ \gamma correspond to residues of torus localization weights of a certain quasi-coherent sheaf ℱ γ \mathcal{F}_{\gamma} on the Hilbert scheme of points on 𝔸 2 \mathbb{A}^{2} , thereby finding a geometric interpretation for them. As corollaries, we obtain the polynomiality in q q of point-counts of compactified Jacobians of planar curves, as well as a virtual version of the Cherednik-Danilenko conjecture on their Betti numbers. Our results also provide further evidence for the ORS conjecture relating compactified Jacobians and HOMFLY-PT invariants of algebraic knots.

Introduction

Shalika germs are a family of functions on a neighborhood of the identity in an algebraic group
G
defined over a nonarchimedean field
F
. They were introduced in
[
68
]
and further studied in for example
[
44
,
64
,
18
,
74
]
. We refer to
[
42
]
for a survey. More precisely, given
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G(F))
an Iwahori bi-invariant function on
G
, the
Shalika germ expansion

[
18
,
68
]
states that

Proposition 1.1
.

I_{\gamma}(f)=\sum_{\mathbb{O}\in G(0)}\Gamma_{\mathbb{O}}(\gamma)I_{\mathbb{O}}(f)

for any
\gamma\in U
, a neighborhood of the identity in
G(F)
. Here
G(0)
is the set of unipotent orbits in
G(F)
,
I_{\gamma},I_{\mathbb{O}}
are orbital integrals and
\Gamma_{\mathbb{O}}:U\to{\mathbb{C}}
are functions called the Shalika germs.

See Corollary
2.7
for a more precise version we will use.
Computing the Shalika germs for a given group
G
is an important, in general open problem, which refines formulas for regular semisimple orbital integrals as well as character values of supercuspidal representations
[
54
]
. In the present paper, we find a new mathematical incarnation of the Shalika germs of tamely ramified elements in
GL_{n}(F)
in terms of the Hilbert scheme of points on
\Hilb^{n}(\mathbb{A}^{2})
. From now on,
G=GL_{n}
and
\gamma
is tamely ramified, meaning
F(\gamma)
(the algebra
F[x]/f_{\gamma}
where
f_{\gamma}
is the minimal polynomial of
\gamma
) is a product of tamely ramified extensions of
F.

In
[
75
]
, Waldspurger gives a rather complicated inductive formula for (almost, see Theorem
2.19
and Section
6
) the Shalika germs of
\gamma
in this case, built inductively from those of smaller depth elements in
GL_{n^{\prime}}
,
n^{\prime}|n
. Here depth is meant in the sense of Moy-Prasad theory, see Definition
2.45
. The algorithm in
[
75
]
rests on a clever choice of test functions and a version of ”Kazhdan’s lemma”, using which one can bootstrap computation of the germs to what is essentially just linear algebra. A similar idea is used again by Waldspurger in
[
76
]
and also by the second author in
[
72
]
based on a lemma of Kim-Murnaghan
[
40
]
to obtain less precise results for general groups. Waldspurger mentions similar strategies due to Kazhdan, Henniart, and others. The issue is that the resulting linear algebra is usually cumbersome to carry out and many steps of the algorithm have no obvious conceptual meaning. As noted by Waldspurger in
[
75
]
:

L’auteur est
convaincu qu’il existe une bonne combinatoire, moins naïve que celle utilisée ici, qui
devrait permettre de calculer les germes.
- J.-L. Waldspurger
[
75
]

Our method of computation of the germs will use Waldspurger’s techniques from
[
74
,
75
]
. Notably we replace, or rather extend, the PSH-algebra calculations in
loc. cit.
by the
elliptic Hall algebra
. This greatly clarifies the resulting combinatorics, giving a formula which is essentially computable ’’by hand”

The elliptic Hall algebra (EHA) has appeared in many guises related to automorphic forms, starting with the original article of Burban and Schiffmann
[
10
]
. For us, it appears through a ”shuffle algebra” action on the algebra of symmetric functions in infinitely many variables
[
66
,
57
]
. More precisely, much of this paper is concerned with a symmetric function we denote by
\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}
attached to any tamely ramified regular semisimple element in
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}(F)
. This symmetric function can be constructed using the action of the elliptic Hall algebra on the Fock space, as explained in Sections
5
and
6
. We have called
\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}
”the master symmetric function” for lack of a better name. When
\gamma
is totally ramified,
\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}
essentially equals another symmetric function which we denote
\mathbf{f}_{\vec{p},\vec{q}}
. A version of this latter function has appeared before in the knot theory literature;
\mathbf{f}_{\vec{p},\vec{q}}
is the
t\to 1
limit of a symmetric function implicitly appearing in the definition of the
superpolynomial
for an iterated torus knot in the work of Cherednik–Danilenko
[
12
,
30
]
. A precise definition is given in Sections
4
,
5
.

In the papers
[
74
,
75
]
, Waldspurger already essentially introduces the symmetric function we call
\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}
, but mostly as a bookkeeping tool which turns out to be helpful because of the relation to calculations in the Hall algebra of
GL_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, under its incarnation as Zelevinsky’s PSH-algebra
[
77
]
. Apparently, the relationship of Shalika germs to this Hall algebra was first suggested to Waldspurger by B. Srinivasan. We note that the papers
[
74
,
75
]
a priori employ the group
GL_{n}(F)
instead of
the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}(F)
; their connection is straightforward and is explained in Section
2
.

We make the case that the master symmetric function (in its ”degenerate” and ”deformed” incarnations) is a completely natural object and arises as a vector in the Fock space representation of the elliptic Hall algebra. By the results of
[
6
,
67
]
the elliptic Hall algebra is the decategorification of a form of induction-restriction functors for a coherent realization of affine character sheaves, and one expects this to be reflected on the constructible side of Langlands duality. Indeed, in an appropriate sense each
\gamma
gives rise to
G(F)
-equivariant constructible sheaf on
\mathfrak{g}(F)
by taking the extension by zero of the constant sheaf on the conjugacy class and one may view our induction as some shadow of yet-to-be-defined induction-restriction functors for affine character sheaves of this sort (meaning that we impose no singular support condition). While we only work with the elements
\gamma
themselves, it would be compelling to make the induction more precise on the level of affine character sheaves.

On the decategorified level,
the appearance of the Fock space representation on the harmonic analysis side seems to stem from the natural ”cyclotomic” quotient map from the affine Hecke algebra down to the finite one in type A. Note that these maps respect parabolic induction. This is exactly the same phenomenon as in the skein-theoretic version
[
53
]
.

1.1.
The formulas

Let us now state our main results in some detail. Let
G=GL_{n}
,
\mathfrak{g}=\Lie(G)=\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}
. Let
F
be a complete discrete valuation field, with
\mathcal{O}
its ring of integers,
\mathfrak{m}_{F}
the maximal ideal, and
k:=\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}_{F}
the residue field. We endow
F
and any of its finite extension with the standard valuation. Let
\gamma\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O})
be a regular semisimple element that lives in a maximal torus that splits over a tamely ramified extension. Let
F(\gamma)
be the commutative algebra generated by
\gamma
(in the algebra of matrices). For the sake of simplicity, in the introduction we will assume that
\gamma
is inertially elliptic, which means
F(\gamma)/F
is a totally ramified degree
n
extension. We refer to Remark
1.7
for (the reference to) the general case.

Let
u
be a uniformizer of
F(\gamma)
such that
u^{n}\in F

[
43
, Proposition II.5.12]
. It might be inspiring to think of the example when
F=k((t))
and
u
is of the form

(1.1)

u=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&t\\
1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&1&\ddots&0&0\\
0&0&\ddots&0&0\\
0&\cdots&0&1&0\end{pmatrix}

so that
u^{n}=t
. In general, that
F(\gamma)
is a complete DVF guarantees that we can always write

(1.2)

\gamma=\sum_{r\in\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}a_{r}u^{nr},

where
a_{i}\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}
. Consider the set of root valuations

RV(\gamma):=\left\{r\in\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\;|\;r\not\in\mathrm{span}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\{1,\,r^{\prime}\in\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\;|\;r^{\prime}<r,\;a_{r^{\prime}}\not=0\}\right\}.

The set
RV(\gamma)
is finite; in fact
RV(\gamma)=\{\mathrm{val}_{F}(\sigma(\gamma)-\gamma)\;|\;\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(F^{sep}/F)\}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Write
\{r_{1},r_{2},\cdots,r_{k}\}=RV(\gamma)
where
r_{1}>\cdots>r_{k}
. Let
r_{k}=m_{k}/n_{k}
be the reduced expression, and inductively
r_{i}=m_{i}/n_{i}n_{i+1}...n_{k}
where
m_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
,
n_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
are coprime. We call
(m_{1},n_{1}),\ldots,(m_{k},n_{k})
the
Puiseux pairs
of
\gamma
.

Define another sequence of pairs of integers
(p_{1},q_{1}),\ldots,(p_{k},q_{k})
related to the previous one by
p_{i}=n_{i}
,
q_{i}=m_{i}-m_{i+1}n_{i}
. We call
(p_{1},q_{1}),\ldots,(p_{k},q_{k})
the
Newton pairs
of
\gamma
. We will write
(\vec{p},\vec{q})
for this sequence.

Remark 1.3
.

If we adapt the slight abuse of notation that
t^{m/n}:=u^{m}
, then a typical example of
\gamma
with given Newton pairs (or root valuations, say) is given by:

\gamma=t^{q_{k}/p_{k}}(a_{k}^{\prime}+t^{q_{k-1}/p_{k}p_{k-1}}(a_{k-1}^{\prime}+\cdots(a_{2}^{\prime}+a_{1}^{\prime}t^{q_{1}/p_{1}\ldots p_{k}}))\cdots)

where
a_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}
are arbitrary.

Remark 1.4
.

Suppose that
F={\mathbb{C}}((t))
, and that (
1.2
) defines a convergent Taylor series over
{\mathbb{C}}
. Then the Puiseux (equivalently, Newton) pairs
determine the
topological type
of the singularity
\{\cchar(\gamma)=0\}
cut out by the characteristic polynomial of
\gamma
. Recall that this is by definition the knot in
S^{3}
determined by intersecting
\{\cchar(\gamma)=0\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
with a small three-sphere centered at the origin. Conversely, if we impose the condition
q_{k}\geq p_{k}
, the topological type uniquely determines the Puiseux pairs.
We note that our conventions for relating Puiseux data and the topological type are opposite to the conventions used in e.g.
[
21
]
.

Example 1.5
.

Let
F=k((t))
,
n=4
and

\gamma=u^{6}+u^{7}=\begin{pmatrix}0&t^{2}&t^{2}&0\\
0&0&t^{2}&t^{2}\\
t&0&0&t^{2}\\
t&t&0&0\end{pmatrix}

as in (
1.1
). Then
r_{1}=7/4,r_{2}=3/2
and

\cchar(\gamma)=x^{4}-2t^{3}x^{2}-4t^{5}x-t^{7}+t^{6}

The Puiseux pairs are
(m_{1},n_{1})=(7,2),(m_{2},n_{2})=(3,2)
and the Newton pairs are
(p_{1},q_{1})=(2,1),(p_{2},q_{2})=(2,3)
. The link is the ”
(2,13)
-cable of the trefoil”.
This example features also for example in
[
21

.

As explained in Section
4
, for every pair of coprime integers
(e,d)
one may define a homomorphism from the ring of symmetric functions over
{\mathbb{Q}}(q)
to itself
\varphi_{d/e}:{\mathrm{Sym}}_{q}\to{\mathrm{Sym}}_{q}
by letting

(1.3)

\varphi_{d/e}(e_{k})=E_{m,n,k}:=\sum_{\pi\in\mathbb{D}_{kd,ke}}q^{\area(\pi)}e_{\pi}

where
\mathbb{D}_{kd,ke}
is the set of Dyck paths in
(kd\times ke)
rectangle below the diagonal; see Proposition

Starting from the Newton pairs of
\gamma
, we define the master symmetric function
\mathbf{f}_{\vec{p},\vec{q}}
for

as follows:

\mathbf{f}_{\vec{p},\vec{q}}=\varphi_{q_{d}/p_{d}}(\cdots\varphi_{q_{1}/p_{1}}(e_{1}))\cdots)

Denote by
\mathbf{f}_{\vec{p},\vec{q}}|_{q\mapsto q^{-1}}
the image of
\mathbf{f}_{\vec{p},\vec{q}}
under the involution on

sending

to

. This simply means changing

to

in Eq. (

and

(see §

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
k
be a finite field so that
F
is a non-archimedean local field. Let
\gamma\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O})
be inertially elliptic as before. For any partition

(resp.

(resp.

\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\Gamma_{\lambda^{t}}(\gamma)\widetilde{h}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\Gamma_{\lambda^{t}}(1+\gamma)\widetilde{h}_{\lambda}=q^{\Xi(\gamma)}\omega\mathbf{f}_{\vec{p},\vec{q}}|_{q\mapsto q^{-1}}

More precisely, it is explained in Section

\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}
in Definition

.
Similarly, one gets what we call ”Steinberg germs” from Theorem

\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\Gamma_{\lambda^{t}}(\gamma)\widetilde{h}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\Gamma_{\lambda}^{St}(\gamma)e_{\lambda}

Remark 1.7
.

So far, we have been restricting to the case where
F(\gamma)/F
is totally tamely ramified. In the case where our extension is tamely ramified but not necessarily totally ramified, the above theorem also works with appropriate modifications, as explained in Section

to account for intermediate unramified extensions.

If
\gamma\in\mathfrak{g}(F)
is regular semisimple but not necessarily compact, the Theorem also holds via the homogeneity properties for Shalika germs, cf. Remark

.

Finally, if
\gamma
is not elliptic but still regular semisimple and belongs to a Levi

, we take
\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}
to be the product of the master symmetric functions of the factors, see Definition

. When
F=k((t))
, this is uniquely determined by the Puiseux series of the branches of the spectral curve (which correspond to the different blocks of
\gamma
).

On the harmonic analysis side, the recursive definition of
\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}
essentially boils down to writing

\gamma^{\leq}:=\sum_{r\leq r_{k}}a_{r}u^{nr},\;\gamma^{>}:=\sum_{r>r_{k}}a_{r}u^{n(r-r_{k})}

in terms of (

), so that we have
\gamma=\gamma^{\leq}+u^{nr_{k}}\gamma^{>}
.
The centralizer of
\gamma^{\leq}
in

is isomorphic to some

for a tamely and totally ramified extension

of degree

with
n^{\prime}:=n/e
; in fact
p_{k}
is the (minimal) denominator of

and
F^{\prime}=F(u^{nr_{k}})
.
The Shalika germs for
\gamma
are then computed from those of
\gamma^{>}
, viewing
\gamma^{>}
as an element in
\mathfrak{gl}_{n^{\prime}}(F^{\prime})
. In detail, this is slightly more involved as we need to pass between the group
GL_{n}
and the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}
to use the results of
[

. Details are explained in Section

As we show in Theorem

, the Dyck path recursion for
\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}
can be translated to the following fairly complicated recursive formula for the Shalika germs using compositions, see Section

Theorem 1.8
.

The transition matrix between the Shalika germs for
\gamma
and
\gamma^{>}
, indexed by
\lambda\vdash n
and
\lambda^{\prime}\vdash n/e
respectively, is given by

M_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}^{d/e}=\left(c_{\lambda^{\prime}}q^{s}\sum_{\mu\vdash n/e}\frac{|S_{\lambda^{\prime}}\cap C_{\mu}|}{b_{\mu}\lambda^{\prime}!}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}(\sum_{\alpha\vDash e\mu_{i}}\mathrm{wt}_{d/e}(\alpha)_{q\mapsto q^{-1}}q^{-n(\alpha^{t})}\widetilde{h}_{\alpha})\right)\Bigg|_{\widetilde{h}_{\lambda}}

where
e:=p_{k}
,

,
and
|_{\widetilde{h}_{\lambda}}
means we pick the coefficient of
\widetilde{h}_{\lambda}
. Here
\mathrm{wt}(\alpha)_{d/e}
is as in Eq. (
5.11
), and
|S_{\lambda^{\prime}}\cap C_{\mu}|
is the number of elements in the symmetric group

which belong to both the Young subgroup
S_{\lambda^{\prime}}
and have cycle type

. We also use the normalizing constants

c_{\lambda^{\prime}}=(1-q)^{n^{\prime}}[\lambda^{\prime}]_{q}!,\;b_{\mu}=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}(1-q^{\mu_{i}})

Remark 1.9
.

We do not have a good theoretical understanding of the meaning of the transition matrix
M^{d/e}
, but give some combinatorial stipulations about its structure in Section

. If one is only interested in the actual values of the Shalika germs, or computing specific entries of these transition matrices, these are easy to compute using the included computer program.

Finally, we explain how our results recover combinatorial formulas for many ”simple” orbital integrals. For any partition
\lambda\vdash n
, let
\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}\subset G(\mathcal{O})
be the standard parahoric subgroup consisting of elements whose reduction in
G(k)
is block-wise lower triangular with block sizes
\lambda
. In particular
\mathbf{I}:=\mathbf{P}_{(1^{n})}
is our standard Iwahori subgroup. Let
{\bf 1}_{\lambda}\in C^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O}))
be the characteristic function of
\Lie\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}
, the associated parahoric subalgebra, divided by the measure of
\Lie\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}
. With this normalization and the normalization of measures introduced in Section
2
, the integrals of
{\bf 1}_{\lambda}
over the orbit of
\gamma
give exactly the point counts of affine Springer fibers in the case
F={\mathbb{F}}_{q}((t))
(or their weight polynomials when we are coming from characteristic zero via reduction).

The values of these integrals are obtained easily from the master symmetric function by pairing it with the complete homogeneous symmetric functions:

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
\lambda\vdash n
be a partition. Then

I_{\gamma}({\bf 1}_{\lambda})=\langle\mathbf{f}_{\gamma},h_{\lambda}\rangle=|{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\gamma}^{\lambda}(k)/\Lambda_{\gamma}(k)|

where we pair using the usual Hall inner product and
{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\gamma}^{\lambda}
is the affine Springer fiber of
\gamma
in the partial affine flag variety
G(F)/\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}
, see Section
8
. See also Section
2
for the normalizations of measures we are using.

The following is more or less obvious from Theorem
1.10
and has been a folklore conjecture for quite long. We give details in Section
8.2
.

Corollary 1.11
.

The point-counts of (local) compactified Jacobians of plane curves over a finite field are polynomials in
q
and only depend on the Newton-Puiseux pairs of
\gamma
. In addition, they are polynomials with nonnegative integer coefficients. Similarly, the weight polynomials of local compactified Jacobians over
{\mathbb{C}}
are nonnegative polynomials in
q
.

Proof.

Theorem
1.10
combined with Proposition
8.10
implies that

\langle\mathbf{f}_{\gamma},h_{\lambda}\rangle

is the number of points of the projective variety
{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\gamma}^{\lambda}/\Lambda
. In the case over
{\mathbb{C}}
, we reduce to this case after spreading out and modding out by
q
outside a finite set of primes.

It is well known that
\langle h_{\mu},h_{\lambda}\rangle
counts the number of certain nonnegative integer matrices with row sums
\mu
and column sums
\lambda
and in particular this count is a nonnegative integer. Since
\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}=\sum\sigma_{\lambda}(\gamma)h_{\lambda}
with
\sigma_{\lambda}(\gamma)\in{\mathbb{N}}[q]
by Corollary
6.22
, we get

|X_{\gamma}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|\in{\mathbb{N}}[q]

as desired.
∎

We note that the element
\gamma
does not have to be elliptic for this to hold. Indeed, as explained in Section
2
the point-counts on regular semisimple affine Springer fibers can be reduced to those of elliptic elements. The above is also in line with the expectation that all local compactified Jacobians (for elliptic
\gamma
, say) are paved by affines.

Our final main result follows from our method using the elliptic Hall algebra. The detailed statement is formulated and proved in Section
6.2
. In Section
9
we connect this Theorem to the geometry of Hilbert schemes of points on
\mathbb{A}^{2}
.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
\gamma
be elliptic and tamely ramified. The master symmetric function
\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}
admits a canonical
t
-deformation
\widehat{\mathbf{f}}_{\gamma}
which admits a
t
-deformed version of the Shalika germ expansion:

\widehat{\mathbf{f}}_{\gamma}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\lambda^{t}}(\gamma)\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}

where
\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}
are the modified Macdonald polynomials.

We now remark on further generalizations of our results. It is unlikely the methods in this paper will yield results for other groups, in that the elliptic Hall algebra seems to be confined to work with
G=GL_{n}
only. There are also a number of geometric simplifications in this case for the affine Springer fibers. It is however interesting to ponder what part of the theory carries through to other
G
.

Another direction of generalization is to the wildly ramified elements. In particular, it would be interesting to know whether there is a finite algorithm that determines the Shalika germs when
F(\gamma)
is arbitrarily ramified and
F
is an arbitrary local field.

Finally, it seems likely our methods also inform us about the geometry of the mixed characteristic affine Springer fibers. More precisely, while the comparison to plane curves fails in mixed characteristic, the geometric interpretation of orbital integrals from
[
26
,
46
,
39
]
still goes through but the geometry is now replaced by the Witt vector affine flag varieties of
[
79
]
and the affine Springer fibers therein. For example, Proposition
8.2
still goes through in mixed characteristic. This is presumably the case for the rest of Section
8
as well, but some technical groundwork seems to be needed.

Theorem 2.3

For any compact subset

Remark 2.5

2.5.
Moy-Prasad theory

Introducing some Moy-Prasad theory will be convenient for the statement of our results – we will do it in this section. However, this section may be skipped on a first reading, and is not essential for any of our proofs. We will only sketch this for

For any point the Bruhat-Tits building of

, denoted

, as well as a real number

, Moy and Prasad define subspaces

(and also

When

, for generic

the hyperspecial

.

There are analogous notions for the group

(in our chosen coordinates) we get the hyperspecial and if

is generic, say in the fundamental alcove, we get the ”standard” Iwahori.

of

(respectively,

5.2.

The

Corollary 6.22
.

Let
\gamma\in GL_{n}(\mathcal{O})

(\vec{p},\vec{q})

Consider the master symmetric function

is as defined in Definition

.

Proof.

By assumption
\gamma
lies in the ring of integers of the totally and tamely ramified extension
F(\gamma)/F
. Dividing
\gamma
by an element in
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
, we may assume

is topologically unipotent, i.e.
\gamma\equiv 1(\operatorname{mod}\mathfrak{m}_{F(\gamma)})
. The resulting

is of the type Lemma
6.11

6.17

Write
\gamma=\eta(1+\delta\gamma^{\prime})
as in Lemma
6.11
. By Theorem
6.21

6.18
we see that the master symmetric functions of

\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}=q^{\frac{(dn^{\prime}-1)(en^{\prime}-1)+n^{\prime}-1}{2}}(-1)^{n-n^{\prime}}\omega\varphi_{d/e}|_{q\mapsto q^{-1}}\omega^{-1}((-1)^{n-n^{\prime}}\tau_{1}(\mathbf{f}_{\gamma^{\prime}}))

where

are as defined after Lemma

.

First, we note the overall sign cancels.
Second, to see that the power of

6.3.
The formula for Shalika germs

In this section, we will state and prove the main combinatorial formula for Shalika germs.
Let

\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\Gamma_{\lambda^{t}}(\gamma)\widetilde{h}_{\lambda}

be the Shalika expansion of the master symmetric function for
\gamma\in\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}(F)
that is elliptic, topologically nilpotent and tamely ramified.

In the notation of Theorem
6.27
, the ”cabling process” passing from
\mathbf{f}_{\gamma_{i-1}}
to
\mathbf{f}_{\gamma_{i}}
with new Newton exponents
(p_{i},q_{i})
essentially expands
\mathbf{f}_{\gamma_{i-1}}
in the
\{h_{\lambda}\}
, replacing each
h_{\lambda}
by
H^{-}_{q_{i},p_{i},\lambda}
. If
f_{i}>1
, we also need to precompose with
\tau_{f_{i}}
, which is essentially the ”slope
0/f_{i}
” case of this process.

On the level of the Shalika expansions themselves, we treat these two cases separately, similar as they are, so that each step of the algorithm in Theorem
6.27
is broken into two steps.
It is clear from Definition
6.6
that the transition matrix for Shalika germs is exactly the matrix for the operator
\omega\varphi_{q_{i}/p_{i}}|_{q\mapsto q^{-1}}\omega
or
\tau_{f_{i}}
written in the bases
\{\widetilde{h}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\vdash n}
and
\{\widetilde{h}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\}_{\lambda^{\prime}\vdash n/e}

In either case, we denote this matrix by

(6.8)

M^{d/e}=\{M^{d/e}_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\}

with the convention that
M^{0/e}
corresponds to the operator
\tau_{e}
.

Theorem 6.35
.

Let
e\geq 1
and
d=0
or
d\geq 1
with
(e,d)=1
.
Considering the symmetric group of permutations on
n/e
letters, denote by

is the number of permutations simultaneously lying in the Young/parabolic subgroup
S_{\lambda^{\prime}}:=S_{\lambda^{\prime}_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{\lambda^{\prime}_{\ell}}
and the conjugacy class
C_{\mu}
of permutations with cycle type
\mu
.

When
d\geq 1
, the transition matrix
M^{d/e}
of Shalika germs
has a combinatorial description as follows:

M_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}^{d/e}=\left(c_{\lambda^{\prime}}q^{s}\sum_{\mu\vdash n/e}\frac{|S_{\lambda^{\prime}}\cap C_{\mu}|}{b_{\mu}\lambda^{\prime}!}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}(\sum_{\alpha\vDash e\mu_{i}}\mathrm{wt}_{d/e}(\alpha)_{q\mapsto q^{-1}}q^{-n(\alpha^{t})}\widetilde{h}_{\alpha})\right)\Bigg|_{\widetilde{h}_{\lambda}}

where

is as in Eq. (

,

and

is defined in Eq. (
5.11
). When
d=0
the transition matrix is given by

(6.10)

M_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}^{0/e}=\left(c_{\lambda^{\prime}}\sum_{\mu\vdash n/e}\frac{|S_{\lambda^{\prime}}\cap C_{\mu}|}{b_{\mu}\lambda^{\prime}!}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}(\sum_{\alpha\vDash e\mu_{i}}\mathrm{wt}_{0/e}(\alpha)\widetilde{h}_{\alpha})\right)\Bigg|_{\widetilde{h}_{\lambda}}

where

is still defined by Eq. (
5.11
) but with
S_{0/e}(i):=0
for all

Proof.

Essentially, we need to compute the slope ”

\widetilde{h}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
, i.e. expand them in the

and replace each

\omega
and invert

, as well as multiply the result by

. In the case

.

We first notice that by
[
20
]
the
untransformed

h_{\lambda^{\prime}}=\sum_{\mu}\frac{|S_{\lambda^{\prime}}\cap C_{\mu}|}{\lambda^{\prime}!}p_{\mu}

where

\widetilde{h}_{\lambda^{\prime}}=c_{\lambda^{\prime}}\sum_{\mu\vdash n^{\prime}}\frac{|S_{\lambda^{\prime}}\cap C_{\mu}|}{\lambda^{\prime}!b_{\mu}}p_{\mu}

and

Let now

.

We first note that the matrix elements of

in the
\widetilde{h}_{\lambda}
-basis are the same as those of
\varphi_{d/e}
in the

6.3.1.
Integrality properties

In this section, we conjecture a different combinatorial approach to the (renormalized) Shalika germs.
While it may not seem obvious from the previous formulas, we have the following.

Proposition 6.38
.

The symmetric functions
h_{\lambda}
expand with
{\mathbb{Z}}[q]
-coefficients in the basis
h_{\lambda}\left[\frac{X}{1-q}\right]
and the symmetric functions
\widetilde{h}_{\lambda}
expand with
{\mathbb{Z}}[q]
-coefficients in the basis

Proof.

The bases

{\mathbb{Z}}[q]

\lambda
and column sums
\mu
, with only a single nonzero entry in each row (see e.g.
[
77
, 3.17.]
). It is also integral by e.g.
[
32
, p. 52]
. The second statement follows from a similar argument.
∎

From the above and the fact that the transition matrix

in the

-basis is integral (we use the notation

to denote the conjugation to the appropriate basis, similar to
[
20
]
) by Theorem

, we get
that the coefficients of

in the basis

The

We have

\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}
up to a sign by multiplying

in the basis

∎

Definition 8.1

Recall that

divided by the measure of

[

Let us also note the following application to components of affine Springer fibers.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathfrak{g}(F)
be compact and regular semisimple.
Then the number of geometric components of

stable under

Proof.

By
[

of the integral of
{\bf 1}_{11\cdots 1}
along the orbit of
\gamma

by pairing it with

\mathbf{f}_{\gamma}
is formed by multiplying the master symmetric functions for the blocks of
\gamma

the smallest power in the Dyck germs is

For the general case where the construction

involves the operator

is designed so that for any homogeneous symmetric function

\langle h_{\lambda},\tau_{f}(\mathbf{f})\rangle=\begin{cases}\langle h_{\lambda/f},\mathbf{f}\rangle,&\text{ if $\lambda$ is divisible by $f$}\\

In particular, we may reduce these cases to the computation above.

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
\gamma
be inertially elliptic. Then the smallest power of
q
appearing in the coefficients of
\mathbf{f}_{\vec{p},\vec{q}}=\sum_{\lambda}\sigma_{\lambda}(\gamma)e_{\lambda}
is
1=q^{0}
and it only appears in front of the smallest partition in dominance order for which
\sigma_{\lambda}(\gamma)\neq 0
. In addition, it appears with coefficient
1
.

Proof.

We will prove this by induction. For one Puiseux pair it is clear, as there is always a Dyck path with area
0
(for example in Example
6.4
it is the rightmost Dyck path).
Suppose the statement holds for Newton pairs
(p_{1},q_{1}),\ldots,(p_{i-1},q_{i-1})

\varphi_{q_{i}/p_{i}}(e_{\lambda})=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\left(\sum_{\pi\in\mathbb{D}_{\lambda_{j}q_{i},\lambda_{j}p_{i}}}q^{area(\pi)}e_{\pi}\right)

\mathbf{f}_{(p_{1},q_{1}),\ldots,(p_{i-1},q_{i-1})}

There is a universal homeomorphism

\prod_{c\in\text{Sing}(C)}{\mathrm{Sp}}_{c}/\Lambda_{c}\to\overline{\Pic}(C)/\Lambda

If

Let

\left|\prod_{c\in\text{Sing}(C)}{\mathrm{Sp}}_{c}(k^{\prime})/\Lambda_{c}\right|=\left|\overline{\Pic}(C)(k^{\prime})/\Lambda\right|

.

The number of points on

A standard spreading-out argument, combined with

Let

P_{X}(q)=\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i}q^{j}\dim\gr_{W}^{j}H^{i}(\overline{\Pic}(C))

Yet another corollary of Corollary

8.3.
Orbital integrals

Let us finally comment on possible other applications of our results, as the explicit computation of orbital integrals bears on many problems in number theory and automorphic forms.

For example, in
[
70
]
Shin and Templier prove an equidistribution theorem for ”families” of automorphic L-functions (for any
G
). Their main result
[
70
, Theorem 1.3.]
rests on an explicit, residue-characteristic independent bound for the size of orbital integrals derived by Kottwitz from the Shalika germ expansion. For
G=GL_{n}
, our methods should be applicable to give sharper bounds and as they remark, possible improvements on their analytic results. It would be interesting to see more analytic applications of our results.

In his Beyond Endoscopy -proposal
[
45
]
, Langlands computes global orbital integrals for
GL(2)
using ”elementary” methods. In the thesis of Espinosa Lara
[
47
]
, which builds on work of Altug
[
1
]
, the corresponding local orbital integrals are computed and compared via a product formula to Langlands’ results. In Altug’s work analysis of orbital integrals is used to ”isolate” the contribution of the trivial representation to a certain trace formula Langlands introduces.

A priori, as suspected by Arthur in
[
2
]
, it should be possible to use an explicit computation of the local orbital integrals (which is where our results come in) to have similar results for
GL_{n}
. It would be interesting to see how the possible application to Beyond Endoscopy plays out.

9.
Hilbert schemes of points

In this section we give a, frankly tentative, conjectural geometric expression for the Shalika germs of
\gamma\in\mathfrak{g}(F)
in terms of the Hilbert scheme of points
on
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{K}
. We consider these Hilbert schemes over a field
K
, which is algebraically closed of characteristic zero. We also take
K
as the field of
coefficients for the Borel-Moore homology of the (generalized) affine Springer fibers we will be considering.

Additionally, the reader may want to restrict to the
case where
F=k((t))
is a function field, so that the BM homology considered is the étale cohomology of the Verdier dualizing complex. We believe that with appropriate definitions, everything in this section can be made to work over a mixed-characteristic
F
. To conform with much of the Hilbert scheme literature, the reader
can also think of
F={\mathbb{C}}((t))
and work in singular BM homology for the associated analytic space. We note that the Shalika germs of
\gamma
cannot be defined in terms
of harmonic analysis in this case, but do essentially exist as coefficients in the expansion of the combinatorially defined master symmetric function

\mathbf{f}_{(\vec{f},\vec{p},\vec{q})}=\mathbf{f}_{\vec{p},\vec{q}}
from Eq. (
6.6
) – we take this as a definition.

9.1.
The Fock space and Hilbert schemes on
\mathbb{A}^{2}

Let
\Hilb^{n}(\mathbb{A}^{2})

n

\mathbb{A}^{2}

[
32
]
.
There is a natural action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{2}

\mathbb{A}^{2}

(
[

]

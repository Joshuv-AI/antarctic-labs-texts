On New Thue Colouring Concepts of Certain Graphs
Johan Kok, Erika Škrabul’áková, Naduvath Sudev

Abstract
Let G G be a simple undirected graph of order n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 and let φ \varphi be a proper vertex colouring of G G . The Thue colouring of a graph is a colouring such that the sequence of vertex colours of any path of even and finite length in G G is non-repetitive. The change in the Thue number, π ⁡ ( G ) \pi(G) , as edges are iteratively removed from a graph G G is studied. Clearly π ⁡ ( G − e ) ≤ π ⁡ ( G ) , e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) \pi(G-e)\leq\pi(G),~e\in E(G) . As the edges are removed iteratively the resultant values of π \pi are used to construct a Thue sequence for the graph G G . The notion of the τ \tau -index denoted, τ ⁡ ( G ) \tau(G) , of a graph G G is introduced as well. τ ⁡ ( G ) \tau(G) serves as a measure for the efficiency of edge deletion to reduce the Thue chromatic number of a graph G G .

Thue chromatic number, Thue number, Thue sequence,
\tau
-index.

Thue Sequence of a Graph

In this section, we first discuss the change in the Thue number,
\pi(G)
, as edges are iteratively removed from a graph
G
. By definition, we have
\pi(K_{1})=1
and if
G=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}G_{i}
, where each
G_{i}
is a simple connected graph, then
\pi(G)=\max\{\pi(G_{i}):1\leq i\leq\ell\}
. Clearly,
\pi(G-e)\leq\pi(G),~e\in E(G)
. Label the edges of
G,~e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},\ldots,e_{\epsilon}
, where
\epsilon
is the number of edges in
G
. Then, we can define a Thue sequence as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G=G^{\ast}_{0}
and
G^{\ast}_{i}=G^{*}_{i-1}-e_{j},e_{j}\in E(G_{i-1})
, then the sequence
(\pi(G^{*}_{i}))_{1=1}^{\epsilon}
is called a
Thue sequence
of
G
.

The following proposition provides a relation between Thue numbers of a graph
G
and that of a graph obtained by joining some of their non-adjacent vertices.

Proposition 2.1
.

For a graph
G
, which is not complete, of order
n\geq 3
we have
\pi(G)\leq\pi(G+uv);u,v\in V(G)
.

Proof.

Recall that
\pi(P_{3})=2
and
\pi(C_{3})=3
(see
[
13
,
15
]
). Hence, the result holds for non-complete graphs on
3
vertices. Now, assume that the results holds for all non-complete graphs
G
on
4\leq n\leq k
vertices. Consider the non-complete graph
H=G\bigcup K_{1}
on
k+1
vertices. Now, by the induction hypothesis, the result
\pi(G)=\pi(G\cup K_{1})\leq\pi((G+uv)\cup K_{1})
holds. Hence, we need only to check the cases by adding the edges
uv_{i}
, where
u
is the vertex of
K_{1}
and
v_{i}\in V(G)
. Add the edge
uv_{i}
. Clearly, by re-colouring, we have either
\pi(G+uv_{i})=\pi(G)
or
\pi(G+uv_{i})=\pi(G)+1
. By immediate induction, the second equation holds for any number of edges
uv_{i},1\leq i\leq k
, added to
G
to obtain a new graph
G^{\prime}
. If
G^{\prime}
is not complete, then again by induction, it follows that
\pi(G^{\prime})\leq\pi(G^{\prime}+uv_{j})
for all possible
v_{j}\in V(G^{\prime})
. Hence, the result holds for all graphs, that are not complete, on
k+1
vertices. Then, the result follows by induction.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

For graph
G
of order
n\geq 1
we have
\pi(G-e)\leq\pi(G)
for all
e\in E(G)
.

Proof.

The result follows by similar reasoning used in the proof of Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Note that if a graph
G
is a Hamiltonian graph, then
\pi(G)\geq 3
. Label the edges of
G
in injective manner by
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},\ldots,e_{\epsilon}
. Also, let
G=G^{\ast}_{0}
and
G^{\ast}_{i}=G^{\ast}_{i-1}-e_{j},~e_{j}\in E(G_{i-1})
, then the sequence
(\pi(G^{\ast}_{i})_{1=1}^{\epsilon}
is called a
Thue sequence
of the edge deletion sequence. There are
\epsilon!
such edge deletion sequences for
G
. Let
s_{\pi,j}
, for all
1\leq j\leq\epsilon!
denote the edge deletion sequences.

Definition 2.2
.

For an edge deletion sequence
(s_{\pi,j}),j\in\{1,2,3\ldots,\epsilon!\}
, define the
\tau
-index
by
\tau(s_{\pi,j})=\frac{1}{\epsilon+1}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\epsilon}\pi(G^{\ast}_{i})
.

Invoking the above definition, we can say that the
\tau
-index
of a graph
G
is the efficiency of edge deletion to reduce the Thue chromatic number of
G
. Hence, the
\tau
-index of a graph
G
is defined as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

For a graph
G
the
\tau
-index of
G,~\tau(G)=\min\{\tau(s_{\pi,j}):\forall s_{\pi,j},1\leq j\leq\epsilon\,!\}
.

Illustration 1
.

Consider the cycle
C_{9}
and label the vertices by
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},\ldots,v_{9}
in a clockwise manner and the edges by
e_{i}=v_{i}v_{i+1}
in the sense that
v_{n+1}=v_{1}
. Consider the edge deletion sequence
s_{\pi,1}=(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},\ldots,e_{9})
. Deleting the edges sequentially results in the Thue sequence
(4,3,3,3,3,3,3,2,2,1)
. Hence,
\tau(s_{\pi,1})=\frac{27}{10}
.

Now, consider the edge deletion sequence
s_{\pi,2}=(e_{3},e_{6},e_{2},e_{4},e_{6},e_{8},e_{1},e_{5},e_{7},e_{9})
. The corresponding Thue sequence is given by
(4,3,3,2,2,2,2,2,2,1)
. Hence,
\tau(s_{\pi,2})=\frac{23}{10}
. Over all
s_{\pi,j},~1\leq j\leq 9!
we find
\tau(C_{9})=\frac{23}{10}
.

Corollary 2.3
.

For a graph
G,~\pi(G)=1
if and only if
E(G)=\emptyset
. That is, the Thue chromatic number of a graph
G,
not necessary connected, is
1
if and only if it is an edgeless graph.

Proof.

Let
E(G)=\emptyset
. The result is trivially true for
K_{1}
. For an edgeless graph on
n\geq 2
vertices the colouring
\varphi:v_{i}\mapsto c_{1},\forall~v_{i}\in V(G)
is a proper minimum vertex colouring of
G
. Also, since no distinct vertices of
G
are reachable,
\pi(G)=\max\{\underbrace{\pi(K_{1}),\pi(K_{1}),\ldots\pi(K_{1})}_{n-entries}\}
and hence the result holds.

Now, let
\pi(G)=1
and assume that
G
has at least one edge say,
uv
. Since a Thue colouring is a proper vertex colouring
\varphi
of
G
, the chromatic number
\chi(G)\geq 2
and hence
\pi(G)\geq 2
. This is a contradiction to the hypothesis and therefore
G
is edgeless.
∎

Since
0!=1
the edgeless graph has the default edge deletion sequence
s_{\pi,\emptyset}
. For a trivial graph
G
, it follows from Definitions
2.2
and
2.3
that
\tau(s_{\pi,\emptyset})=\tau(G)=1
.

Theorem 2.4
.

For all graphs
G
with
\epsilon\geq 1
, we have
\frac{3}{2}\leq\min\{\tau(G)\}<2
.

Proof.

First, note that
\tau(P_{2})=\frac{3}{2}
and hence we have
\min\{\tau(G)\}\geq\frac{3}{2}
.
Also, note that the unique Thue sequence of the star
K_{1,n}
is
(\underbrace{2,2,2,\ldots,2}_{n-entries},1)
. Since
\pi(G^{\ast}_{i})\in\mathbb{N},~1\leq i\leq\epsilon(G)
, no other Thue sequence of any other graph
G
with
n

\tau(K_{1,n})
. Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2n+1}{n+1}=2
, the result
\frac{3}{2}\leq\min\{\tau(G)\}<2
follows.
∎

Observe that for all graphs
G
with
\epsilon(G)\geq 2
the inequality
\tau(S_{1,n})<\tau(G)
holds.

2.1
\tau
-Index of Paths and Cycles

From
[
13
]
it is known that
\pi(P_{1})=1,~\pi(P_{2})=\pi(P_{3})=2
and
\pi(P_{n})=3
for
n\geq 4
. Hence, the most efficient way to reduce the Thue number of paths through edge deletion is to delete edges which create
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
components. For paths
P_{n},~n\geq 4
, the following result holds.

Proposition 2.5
.

For a path
P_{n},~n\geq 4
, the
\tau
-index is given by

\tau(P_{n})=\begin{cases}\frac{7n-6}{3n};&n=3t,~t\in\mathbb{N},\\
\frac{7n-5}{3n};&n=3t-1,~t\in\mathbb{N},\\
\frac{7n-4}{3n};&n=3t-2,~t\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Consider a path
P_{n},~n\geq 4
. Label the vertices and the edges of the path from left to right,
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},\ldots,v_{n}
and
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},\ldots,e_{n-1}
, respectively.

Let
n=3t,t\in\mathbb{N}
. First, consider the partial edge deletion sequence
s_{\pi,1}=(e_{3},e_{6},e_{9},\ldots,e_{3(t-2)})
. Deleting these edges sequentially results in the partial Thue sequence
(\underbrace{3,3,3,\ldots,3}_{(t-1)-entries})
. Hence, the partial sum is equal to
3(t-1)=\frac{3n}{3}-3
.

Now, consider the partial edge deletion sequence
s_{\pi,2}=(e_{1},e_{4},e_{7},\ldots,e_{3(t-1)})
. Deleting these edges sequentially results in the partial Thue sequence
(\underbrace{2,2,2,\ldots,2}_{2t-entries})
. The corresponding partial sum is equal to
4t=\frac{4n}{3}
. So far, we have deleted
(t-2)+2t=3t-2
edges. Deleting the last edge adds the
partial sum
, by
1
. From Definition
2.2
, it follows that
\tau(P_{n})=\frac{1}{n}(\frac{3n}{3}-3+\frac{4n}{3}+1)=\frac{7n-6}{3}
.

The other cases will also follow by a similar counting technique.
∎

The result of Proposition
2.5
can be re-written as
\tau(P_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{7(n+i)}{3}-2(i+1)\right),~i\in\{0,1,2\}
and
n=3t-i,~t\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathbb{C}=\{5,7,9,10,14,17\}
. From
[
6
]
, we have
\pi(C_{n})=4,~n\in\mathbb{C}
, else
\pi(C_{n})=3
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Consider a cycle
C_{n},n\geq 4
, then

\tau(C_{n})=\begin{cases}\frac{7n-i+6}{3(n+1)};&i\in\{0,1,2\}~~\text{and}~~n=3t-i,~t\in\mathbb{N},n\in\mathbb{C},\\
\frac{7n-i+3}{3(n+1)};&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Observe that
C_{n}-e,~e\in V(C_{n})
is the path
P_{n}
, and hence the result follows immediately.
∎

Observe that for the corresponding line graphs,
\tau(L(P_{n}))=\tau(P_{n-1})
and
\tau(L(C_{n}))=\tau(C_{n})
. In respect of non-repetitive edge colouring, the
Thue chromatic index
or
Thue index
for brevity, denoted
\pi^{\prime}(G)
is defined in (see
[
1
]
) as a minimal, proper, non-repetitive edge colouring. Since edge colouring of the edges of a graph

is equivalent to vertex colouring of the vertices of the line graph

\pi(G),\pi^{\prime}(G)

2.2
\tau
-Index of Complete Graphs

It is known that
\pi(K_{n\geq 1})=n
, (see
[
13
]
). We now study the
\tau
-index of complete graphs
K_{n},~n\geq 3
. The basis for the proof of the next result through induction, can be illustrated as follows. Consider the complete graph
K_{3}
and label the edges
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
. Deleting these edges sequentially results in the Thue sequence
(3,2,2,1)
. Extend
K_{3}
to
K_{4}
and remove the same edges sequentially. The partial Thue sequence is now given by
(4,3,3,2)
and the resultant graph is
K_{1,3}
. Hence, on deleting
2
additional edges the partial Thue sequence becomes
(4,3,3,2,2,2)
. On deleting the last edge, we get the complete Thue sequence
(4,3,3,2,2,2,1)
.

Proposition 2.7
.

For
n\geq 2
, the
\tau
-index of a complete graph
K_{n}
is

\tau(K_{n})=\frac{2}{n(n-1)}(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-2}(i+1)(n-i)+1)
.

Proof.

We proceed by mathematical induction. The result trivially holds for
K_{2}
and
K_{3}
. Now, assume that it holds for
K_{t},~2\leq t\leq m
. Hence, the Thue sequence of
K_{m}
is given by
(m,m-1,m-1,m-2,m-2,m-2,\ldots,\underbrace{(m-\ell),(m-\ell)\ldots,(m-\ell)}_{(\ell+1)-entries},\\
\ldots,\underbrace{2,2,2,\ldots,2}_{(m-1)-entries},1)
.

Now, consider the complete graph
K_{m+1}
and delete the edges of the subgraph
K_{m}
as before and then delete the edges of the resultant graph
K_{1,m}
. Then, the Thue sequence of
K_{m+1}
is given by
(m+1,m,m,(m-1),(m-1),(m-1),\ldots,\underbrace{(m-\ell+1),(m-\ell+1)\ldots,(m-\ell+1)}_{(\ell+1)-entries},\ldots,\underbrace{3,3,3,\ldots,3}_{(m-1)-entries},2,\underbrace{2,2,2\ldots,2}_{(m-1)-entries},1)
. This sequence can be written as
(q_{i}:(i+1)(n-i),0\leq i\leq n-2)\cup(1)
. As
\epsilon(K_{m+1})=\frac{m(m+1)}{2}
, we have

\tau(K_{m+1})=\frac{2}{m(m+1)}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m-1}(i+1)((m+1)-i)+1)
.

Hence, the result follows by induction.
∎

3
Thue Colouring of Linear Jaco Graphs

As a generalisation of certain types of Jaco graphs, the notion of a linear Jaco graph has been introduced in
[
12
]
as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

[
12
]
Let
f(x)=mx+c;x\in\mathbb{N},~m,c\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The family of infinite linear Jaco graphs, denoted by
\{J_{\infty}(f(x)):f(x)=mx+c;x\in\mathbb{N}
and
m,c\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
, is defined by
V(J_{\infty}(f(x)))=\{v_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\},~A(J_{\infty}(f(x)))\subseteq\{(v_{i},v_{j}):i,j\in\mathbb{N},i<j\}
and
(v_{i},v_{j})\in A(J_{\infty}(f(x)))
if and only if
(f(i)+i)-d^{-}(v_{i})\geq j
.

Definition 3.2
.

[
12
]
The family of finite linear Jaco graphs denoted by
\{J_{n}(f(x)):f(x)=mx+c;x\in\mathbb{N}
and
m,c\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
is defined by
V(J_{n}(f(x)))=\{v_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N},i\leq n\},~A(J_{n}(f(x)))\subseteq\{(v_{i},v_{j}):i,j\in\mathbb{N},i<j\leq n\}
and
(v_{i},v_{j})\in A(J_{n}(f(x)))
if and only if
(f(i)+i)-d^{-}(v_{i})\geq j
.

A vertex which attains the degree
\Delta(J_{n}(f(x)))
is called a
Jaconian vertex
and the set of Jaconian vertices of the Jaco graph
J_{n}(f(x))
is called the
Jaconian set
of
J_{n}(f(x))
and is denoted by
\mathbb{J}(J_{n}(x))
or simply by
\mathbb{J}_{n}(x)
for brevity, if the context is clear. The lowest numbered (subscripted) Jaconian vertex is called the
prime Jaconian vertex
of a Jaco Graph.

If
v_{i}
is the prime Jaconian vertex of a Jaco Graph
J_{n}(1)
, the complete subgraph on
vertices
v_{i+1},v_{i+2},\ldots,v_{n}
is called the
Hope subgraph
or
Hope graph
of a Jaco Graph and is denoted by
\mathbb{H}(J_{n}(x))
or
\mathbb{H}_{n}(x)
for brevity.

We refer to the corresponding underlying graph of
J_{n}(f(x))
, denoted
J^{\ast}_{n}(f(x))
, as a Jaco graph as well. We recall a result from
[
12
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
12
]
For
m=0
and
c\geq 0
, two special classes of disconnected linear Jaco graphs exist. For
c=0
the Jaco graph
J_{n}(0)
is a null graph (edgeless graph) on
n
vertices. For
c>0
, the Jaco graph
J_{n}(c)=\bigcup\limits_{\lfloor\frac{n}{c+1}\rfloor-copies}K_{c+1}\bigcup K_{n-(c+1)\cdot\lfloor\frac{n}{c+1}\rfloor}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
m=0
and
c\geq 0
, then
\pi(J^{\ast}_{n\geq 1}(c))\in\{1,2,3,\ldots,c+1\}
.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.1
, it follows that if
c=0
then
J^{\ast}_{n\geq 1}(0)
is an edgeless graph. Hence,
\pi(J^{\ast}_{n\geq 1}(0))=1
. If

then
J^{\ast}_{n\geq 1}(c)
is the union of complete graphs of which a largest component is
K_{c+1}
if and only if

. Hence,
\pi(J^{\ast}_{n\geq c+1}(c))=c+1
. For

thus,
\pi(J^{\ast}_{1\leq t\leq c}(c))=t

\pi(J^{\ast}_{n\geq 1}(c))\in\{1,2,3,\ldots,c+1\}
.
∎

[

Case 3:
Let
\ell=\pi(J^{\ast}_{k}(x))=\Delta(J^{\ast}_{k}(x))+1
;
\Delta(J^{\ast}_{k+1}(x))=\Delta(J^{\ast}_{k}(x))+1
. Since
\varphi^{\prime}:v_{k+1}\mapsto c_{\ell+1}
might not allow a minimum Thue colouring,
\pi(J^{\ast}_{k+1}(x))\leq\Delta(J^{\ast}_{k+1}(x))+1
.

Case 4:
Let
\ell=\pi(J^{\ast}_{k}(x))=\Delta(J^{\ast}_{k}(x))+1
;
\Delta(J^{\ast}_{k+1}(x))=\Delta(J^{\ast}_{k}(x))
. The smallest Jaco graph for which we have this case is
J^{\ast}_{5}(x)
and
J^{\ast}_{6}(x)
. For
\varphi^{\prime}:v_{1}\mapsto c_{1},\varphi^{\prime}:v_{2}\mapsto c_{2},\varphi^{\prime}:v_{3}\mapsto c_{1},\varphi^{\prime}:v_{4}\mapsto c_{3},\varphi^{\prime}:v_{5}\mapsto c_{4},\varphi^{\prime}:v_{6}\mapsto c_{1}
, a minimum Thue colouring is allowed and
\pi(J^{\ast}_{6}(x))\leq\Delta(J^{\ast}_{6}(x))+1
. Extend
\varphi^{\prime}
to
J^{\ast}_{k+1}(x)
with
\varphi^{\prime}:v_{k+1}\mapsto c_{1}
. Without loss of generality, assume that there exists a path beginning at vertex
v_{k+1}
which represents at least a two block repetitive sequence say,
(c_{1},c_{j},c_{k},c_{m},\ldots,c_{t},c_{1},c_{j},c_{k},c_{m},\ldots,c_{t})
, then
(c_{j},c_{k},c_{m},\ldots,c_{t},c_{j},c_{k},c_{m},\ldots,c_{t})
corresponds to a two block repetitive sequence in
J^{\ast}_{k}(x)
which is a contradiction. Equally so, if a two block repetitive sequence
(c_{j},c_{k},c_{m},\ldots,c_{t},c_{1},c_{j},c_{k},c_{m},\ldots,c_{t},c_{1})
, then
(c_{j},c_{k},c_{m},\ldots,c_{t},c_{j},c_{k},c_{m},\ldots,c_{t})
corresponds to a two block repetitive sequence in
J^{\ast}_{k}(x)
, which is a contradiction. Hence, by induction, it follows that no such repetitive sequence of any number of blocks exists.

Therefore,
\Delta(J^{\ast}_{n}(x))\leq\pi(J^{\ast}_{n}(x))\leq\Delta(J^{\ast}_{n}(x))+1
.
∎

We strongly believed that a generalisation of Theorem
3.4
is possible as provided in the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1
.

For the Jaco graph
J^{\ast}_{n}(f(x)),~f(x)=mx+c,x\in\mathbb{N},~m\geq 2,~c\geq 1
, we have
\pi(J^{\ast}_{n}(f(x)))\leq\Delta(J^{\ast}_{n}(f(x)))+\delta(J^{\ast}_{n}(f(x)))
.

4
On Thue Distance, Thue Diameter, Thue Radius and Thue Reach

A Thue colouring of a graph
G
has the property that it is a proper vertex colouring for which any path in
G
is a non-repetitive sequence of colours. Therefore, the property of minimality
per se
is not embedded in the concept of a Thue colouring. Hence, a rainbow colouring is a Thue colouring, but not a minimum Thue colouring. We call a minimum Thue colouring of
G
a
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring of
G
.

Hence, if
G
and
\pi(G)
are known, and a finite number
t
colours may be chosen from then,
\binom{t}{\pi(G)}
sets of colours are available to choose from. Call these sets,
\mathfrak{T}
-sets. For any
\mathfrak{T}
-set different mappings
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{k}
exist, all of which will ensure a minimum Thue colouring of
G
. So for a chosen
\mathfrak{T}
-set it is true that up to equivalence the many other
\mathfrak{T}
-sets become irrelevant. Consider a graph
G
of order
n
and a corresponding
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring.

Definition 4.1
.

For a graph
G
with
\pi(G)<n
and a colour
c_{i}
, the
Thue colour distance
, denoted by
\pi_{d}(c_{i})
, is defined to be
\pi_{d}(c_{i})=\min\{d_{G}(u,v):u,v\in V(G),\varphi(u)=\varphi(v)=c_{i}\}
.

Definition 4.2
.

For a graph
G
with
\pi(G)<n
, the
Thue radius
of a graph
G
, denoted by
\pi_{r}(G)
, is defined to be
\pi_{r}(G)=\min\{\pi_{d}(c_{i}):\forall\varphi,~\forall\pi_{d}(c_{i})>0\}
.

Definition 4.3
.

For a graph
G
with
\pi(G)<n
and a colour
c_{i}
, the
Thue colour diameter
, denoted by
\pi_{D}(c_{i})
, and is defined as
\pi_{D}(c_{i})=\max\{d_{G}(u,v):u,v\in V(G),\varphi(u)=\varphi(v)=c_{i}\}
.

Definition 4.4
.

For a graph
G
with
\pi(G)<n
, the
Thue reach
of a graph
G
, denoted by
\pi_{R}(G)
, is defined to be
\pi_{R}(G)=\max\{\pi_{D}(c_{i})\}
, for all
\varphi
and for all
\pi_{D}(c_{i})>0\}
.

In view of the property of inherent adjacency, we can define the default value
\pi_{d}(c_{i})=\pi_{D}(c_{i})=0
for a rainbow colouring. Obviously,
\pi_{d}(c_{i})\neq 1
and hence
\pi_{D}(c_{i})\neq 1
.

Illustration 2
.

To ensure a
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring of
P_{4}
, let the colours to be
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}
. There are many possible colourings (
\varphi
’s). Six correspond to
\pi_{d}(c_{i})>0
for any
i\in\{1,2,3\}
.

(i)

\varphi(v_{1})=c_{1},\varphi(v_{2})=c_{2},\varphi(v_{3})=c_{1},\varphi(v_{4})=c_{3}
.

(ii)

\varphi(v_{1})=c_{1},\varphi(v_{2})=c_{3},\varphi(v_{3})=c_{1},\varphi(v_{4})=c_{2}
.

(iii)

\varphi(v_{1})=c_{2},\varphi(v_{2})=c_{1},\varphi(v_{3})=c_{2},\varphi(v_{4})=c_{3}
.

(iv)

\varphi(v_{1})=c_{2},\varphi(v_{2})=c_{3},\varphi(v_{3})=c_{2},\varphi(v_{4})=c_{1}
.

(v)

\varphi(v_{1})=c_{3},\varphi(v_{2})=c_{1},\varphi(v_{3})=c_{3},\varphi(v_{4})=c_{2}
.

(vi)

\varphi(v_{1})=c_{3},\varphi(v_{2})=c_{2},\varphi(v_{3})=c_{3},\varphi(v_{4})=c_{1}
.

For (i),
\pi_{d}(c_{1})=2,~\pi_{d}(c_{2})=0,~\pi_{d}(c_{3})=0
. Hence
\pi_{r}(P_{4})=2
. Similarly, for (ii), (iii), (iv), (v), (vi),
\pi_{r}(G)
is constant regardless of
\varphi
.

Similarly, to ensure the same
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring of
P_{4}
determines
\pi_{R}(P_{4})
, consider the following.

(i)

\varphi(v_{1})=c_{1},\varphi(v_{2})=c_{2},\varphi(v_{3})=c_{3},\varphi(v_{4})=c_{1}
.

(ii)

\varphi(v_{1})=c_{1},\varphi(v_{2})=c_{3},\varphi(v_{3})=c_{2},\varphi(v_{4})=c_{1}
.

(iii)

\varphi(v_{1})=c_{2},\varphi(v_{2})=c_{1},\varphi(v_{3})=c_{3},\varphi(v_{4})=c_{2}
.

(iv)

\varphi(v_{1})=c_{2},\varphi(v_{2})=c_{3},\varphi(v_{3})=c_{1},\varphi(v_{4})=c_{2}
.

(v)

\varphi(v_{1})=c_{3},\varphi(v_{2})=c_{1},\varphi(v_{3})=c_{2},\varphi(v_{4})=c_{3}
.

(vi)

\varphi(v_{1})=c_{3},\varphi(v_{2})=c_{2},\varphi(v_{3})=c_{1},\varphi(v_{4})=c_{3}
.

For (i),
\pi_{D}(c_{1})=3,~\pi_{D}(c_{2})=0,~\pi_{D}(c_{3})=0
, hence
\pi_{R}(P_{4})=3
. Similarly, for (ii), (iii), (iv), (v), (vi),
\pi_{R}(G)
is constant regardless of
\varphi
.
∎

These concepts can find application if different colours represent different technologies to be installed at the points of a network and there is a requirement that a set of a particular technology should be minimum distance or maximum distance apart. For example, certain communication technology is such that the efficiency deteriorates if units are to far apart and a maximum distance criteria is set. On the other hand, for certain nuclear technology a minimum distance criteria is set. The next lemma is perhaps obvious but useful for further results.

Lemma 4.1
.

A graph
G
of order
n\geq 3
is a non-complete graph if and only if a subset of three vertices say,
\{u,v,w\}
exists such that the induced subgraph
\langle u,v,w\rangle\neq C_{3}
.

Proof.

Since a disconnected graph cannot be complete the result holds for disconnected graphs. Assume that
G
is connected.

First, assume that there exist three vertices
v,u,w
such that
\langle v,u,w\rangle\neq C_{3}
then at least
vu\notin E(G)
or
vw\notin E(G)
or
uw\notin E(G)
so
G
is not complete.

Conversely, assume that
G
is a non-complete graph. For any two vertices
v,w
for which the edge
vw\notin E(G)
, consider the shortest path
P=vu_{1}u_{2}u_{3}\ldots u_{k}w
. If
w=u_{2}
, then
\langle v,u_{1},u_{2}\rangle\neq C_{3}
and the result follows. However if
u_{2}\neq w
, then the edge
vu_{2}\notin E(G)
else the path
P^{\prime}=vu_{2}u_{3}\ldots w
is a shortest path which is a contradiction. Hence, the induced subgraph
\langle v,u_{1},u_{2}\rangle\neq C_{3}
.
∎

In view of the above results, the existence of Thue colouring for graphs, that are not complete, is discussed in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

For a graph
G\neq K_{n},~\pi_{r}(G)=2
. In other words, for a graph
G\neq K_{n}
there exists a
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring
\varphi
such that, vertices
u,v
exist such that
\varphi(u)=\varphi(v)
and
d_{G}(u,v)=2
.

Proof.

Consider a graph
G\neq K_{n}
and a corresponding
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring
\varphi:V(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{C},~\mathcal{C}=\{c_{i}:1\leq i\leq\pi(G)\}
of
G
. Since
G
is connected, it has
|V(G)|\geq 3
and at least one subgraph,
P_{3}
(see Lemma
4.1
). Let
P_{3}=v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}
. If
\varphi(v_{1})=\varphi(v_{3})
the result follows. Else, assume without loss of generality that
\varphi(v_{1})=c_{1},~\varphi(v_{2})=c_{2},~\varphi(v_{3})=c_{3}
. Consider the graph
G-v_{2}
. Hence, if need be, through colour index rotation it is possible to Thue colour (not necessary minimum) the vertices of
G-v_{2}
such that
\varphi(v_{1})=c_{1}
and
\varphi(v_{3})=c_{1}
and
\varphi:v_{j}\mapsto c_{k},~c_{k}\in\{c_{i}:1\leq i\leq\pi(G)\},~j\neq 1,3
. This allows a
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring of
G
such that
\pi_{d}(c_{1})=\min\{d_{G}(u,v):u,v\in V(G),\varphi(u)=\varphi(v)=c_{1}\}=d_{G}(v_{1},v_{3})=2
. Therefore,
\pi_{r}(G)=2
.
∎

It can be noted that the above theorem can be generelised for any graph
G
as
\pi_{r}(G)\in\{0,2\}
.

4.1
On
\pi_{R}(G)

Observe that for any non-complete graph
G,~\pi_{R}(G)\leq diam(G)
. It then follows that
\pi_{R}(G+e)\leq\pi_{R}(G)
. Except for
K_{n}
for which
\pi_{R}(K_{n})=0<1=diam(K_{n})
no other graphs have been found as yet for which inequality holds. Hence, it is strongly suspected that
\pi_{R}(G)=diam(G)
. A proof of the latter still escapes us. We shall determine
\pi_{R}(G)
for some classes of graphs.

Proposition 4.3
.

(i)

For
n\geq 3
, we have
\pi_{R}(P_{n})=diam(P_{n})=n-1
.

(ii)

For
n\geq 4
, we have
\pi_{R}(C_{n})=diam(C_{n})=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.

(iii)

For a star
S_{n+1},~n\geq 2
, we have
\pi_{R}(S_{n+1})=diam(S_{n+1})=2
.

Proof.

Part-(i):
Without loss of generality let
P_{n}=v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}\ldots v_{n}
and let
\varphi(v_{1})=c_{1}
and
c_{i}\in\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}
. Up to equivalence the path
P_{3}
has a unique
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring,
\varphi(v_{1})=c_{1},\varphi(v_{2})=c_{2},\varphi(v_{3})=c_{1}
hence,
\pi_{R}(P_{3})=diam(P_{3})=2=3-1
. Assume that the result holds for
P_{n},~3\leq n\leq k
. It implies that
\varphi(v_{k})=c_{1}
in
P_{k}
. Consider a
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring of the path
P_{k-1}
such that
\varphi(v_{k-1})=c_{1}
which is possible by the induction assumption. It implies that
\varphi(v_{k-2})\in\{c_{2},c_{3}\}
. Colour
\varphi(v_{k+1})=c_{1}
and
\varphi(v_{k})\neq\varphi(v_{k-2})
. Hence, a
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring of
P_{k+1}
exists such that
\pi_{R}(P_{k+1})=diam(P_{k+1})=k
. Therefore, the result is settled through induction.

Part (ii)(a):
Consider two copies of path
P_{n},~n\geq 3
and label them
P^{\prime}_{n},~P^{\prime\prime}_{n}
respectively. Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}=\{c_{1},c^{\prime}_{2},c^{\prime}_{3}\}
allow a
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring of
P^{\prime}_{n},~P^{\prime\prime}_{n}
respectively such that the four end vertices carry the colour
c_{1}
. From Part (i) the latter is always possible. Merge the end vertices pairwise (one each from a different path) to obtain the cycle
C_{2(n-1)}
. Certainly the cycle has been Thue coloured. Recolour the remaining vertices from say
P^{\prime\prime}_{n}
to obtain a
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring for
C_{2(n-1)}
which is always possible. It follows that
\pi_{D}(c_{1})=n-1
which is a maximum over all colours. Hence,
\pi_{R}(C_{2(n-1)})=diam(C_{2(n-1)})=\frac{2(n-1)}{2}=\lfloor\frac{2(n-1)}{2}\rfloor=n-1
.

Part (ii)(b):
Consider two copies of path
P_{n},~n\geq 3
and label them
P^{\prime}_{n},~P^{\prime\prime}_{n}
respectively. Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}=\{c_{1},c^{\prime}_{2},c^{\prime}_{3}\}
allow a
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring of
P^{\prime}_{n},~P^{\prime\prime}_{n}
respectively such that three end vertices carry the colour
c_{1}
and one end vertex from say
P^{\prime\prime}_{n}
carry the colour
c^{\prime}_{2}
. Merge one end vertex from
P^{\prime}_{n}
and one from
P^{\prime\prime}_{n}
which carries the colour
c_{1}
. Add an edge to join the remaining two end vertices. Clearly the cycle
C_{2n-1}
has been Thue coloured. Recolour the remaining vertices of say
P^{\prime\prime}_{n}
to obtain a
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring for
C_{2n-1}
. It follows that
\pi_{D}(c_{1})=n-1
which is a maximum over all colours. Hence,
\pi_{R}(C_{2n-1})=diam(C_{2n-1})=\lfloor\frac{2n-1}{2}\rfloor
.

Finally, since any cycle
C_{n},~n\geq 4
can be constructed from either Parts (ii)(a) or (ii)(b), the result follows.

Part (iii): For
n=2
the star
S_{2+1}\simeq P_{3}
so the result follows from Part (i). Consider
n\geq 3
. Since it trivially holds for
S_{3+1}
the result follows through immediate induction.
∎

4.2
On Subdivisions of a Graph

For a graph
G
of order
n
and vertices
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},\ldots,v_{n}
, a
k
-subdivision is obtained by inserting
k
additional vertices
u_{\ell},~1\leq\ell\leq k
to each edge
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
. In other words each edge
v_{i}v_{j}
is replaced by the path
v_{i}u_{1}u_{1}u_{3}\ldots u_{k}v_{j}
. Denote the subdivision of
G
by
G_{k}
.

The second subdivision is called the
cycle subdivision
. A distinct pair of vertices
u,w\in V(C_{m})
with
d_{C_{m}}(u,w)=diam(C_{m})
, is called
diameter vertices
of the cycle
C_{m}
. A cycle subdivision of a graph
G
is the graph obtained by inserting a cycle
C_{m}
into each edge with the edges
v_{i}u
and
v_{j}w
, replacing the edge
v_{i}v_{j}
and vertices
u,w
are diameter vertices of
C_{m}
. Denote a cycle subdivision of
G
by
G_{\circ m}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

For a graph
G
we have

(i)

\pi(G_{k})\leq\pi(G)+1
, for
k\geq 3
.

(ii)

\pi(G_{\circ m})\leq\pi(G)+2
, for
m\in\{5,7,9,10,14,17\}
else,
\pi(G_{\circ m})\leq\pi(G)+1
.

Proof.

Part (i):
Let a
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring of
G
be allowed by
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\ldots,c_{\pi(G)}\}
and let a
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring of
P_{k},~k\geq 3
be allowed by
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\{c^{\prime}_{1},c^{\prime}_{2},c^{\prime}_{3}\}
such that
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\emptyset
. Consider the subdivision
G_{k}
with the initial colouring
\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. For each edge
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
recolour the corresponding vertices of the path which carry the colour
\varphi^{\prime}(u_{1})
to the colour
\varphi(v_{j})
. Similarly recolour the corresponding vertices of the path which carry the colour
\varphi^{\prime}(u_{k})
to the colour
\varphi(v_{i})
. Clearly the new colouring is a Thue colouring, perhaps not minimum. Exactly one new colour
c^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
has been added to the initial Thue colouring of
G
to Thue colour
G_{k}
. Therefore, for
k\geq 3,~\pi(G_{k})\leq\pi(G)+1
.

Part (ii):
The second part can also be proved using the similar reasoning as in Part (i).
∎

Note that in the construction of the cycle subdivision there is no necessity to link the diameter vertices. The results holds for any distinct pair of vertices
u_{i},u_{j}\in V(C_{m})
if
d_{C_{m}}(u_{i},u_{j})\geq 2
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Consider paths
P_{t},~t\in\{3,4,5,\ldots,k\}
and insert any path
P_{t}
into an edge of
G
. The varied subdivision is denoted
G_{k^{\ast}}
. For
k\geq 3
we have
\pi(G_{k^{\ast}})\leq\pi(G)+1
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Consider cycles
C_{t},~t\in\{3,4,5,\ldots,m\}
and insert any cycle
C_{t}
into an edge of
G
. The varied subdivision is denoted
G_{\circ m^{\ast}}
. For
m\in\{5,7,9,10,14,17\}
we have
\pi(G_{\circ m^{\ast}})\leq\pi(G)+2
else,
\pi(G_{\circ m^{\ast}})\leq\pi(G)+1
.

The above leads to a general result. We begin by defining the
H
-subdivision of a given graph
G
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let the graph
H
have at least one distinct pair of vertices
u,w
such that
d_{H}(u,w)\geq 2
. The
H
-subdivision
of a graph
G
, denoted
G_{H}
, is the graph obtained by inserting the graph
H
into each edge
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
with the edges
v_{i}u
and
v_{j}w
replacing the edge
v_{i}v_{j}
.

The following theorem establishes the relation between the Thue chromatic numbers of the graphs
G
and
H
and the
H
-subdivision
G_{H}
of
G
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
H
be a graph with at least two distinct vertices
u,w,~d_{H}(u,w)\geq 2
. Then, for a graph
G
we have
\pi(G_{H})\leq\pi(G)+\pi(H)-1
.

Proof.

Let a
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring of
G
be allowed by
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\ldots,c_{\pi(G)}\}
and let a
\mathfrak{T}
-colouring of
H
be allowed by
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\{c^{\prime}_{1},c^{\prime}_{2},c^{\prime}_{3}\ldots,c_{\pi(H)}\}
such that
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\emptyset
. Consider the
H
-subdivision
G_{H}
with the initial colouring
\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. For each edge
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
recolour the corresponding vertices of H which carry the colour
\varphi^{\prime}(u)
to the colour
\varphi(v_{j})
. Similarly recolour the corresponding vertices of
H
which carry the colour
\varphi^{\prime}(w)
to the colour
\varphi(v_{i})
. Clearly the new colouring is a Thue colouring, perhaps not minimum. Thus far, at most two new colours
c^{\prime}_{\ell},c^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
have been removed from the initial Thue colouring of
G_{H}
. Since this new Thue colouring might not be minimum,
\pi(G_{H})\leq\pi(G)+\pi(H)-1
.
∎

5
Conclusion

So far, we have discussed the Thue colouring of certain graph classes and studied certain parameters of certain graphs, with respect to this type of graph colouring. There are so many open problems in this for further investigations.

Since a closed formula for the number of edges of
J^{\ast}_{n}(f(x))
is not known yet, a closed formula for
\tau(J^{\ast}_{n}(f(x)))
also remains open. Using the results on determining the number of edges, provided in
[
12
]
,
\tau
-index can be determined recursively. The factor
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\epsilon(J^{\ast}_{n}(x))}\pi(J^{\ast}_{n,_{i}})
is edge dependent as well. Hence, it is desirable to seek a recursive formula for the aforesaid factor, in the absence of a closed formula for the number of edges of a Jaco graph. It is believed that a clear understanding of the Thue number and Thue sequence of caterpillar graphs will be the foundation for finding such a recursive formula. Other Thue related invariants in respect of Jaco graphs deserve intensive further investigations.

Certainly the new notions such as Thue sequence,
\tau
-index, Thue distance, Thue diameter, Thue radius and Thue reach, also offer a wide scope of further studies.

Some Generalized Multi-sum Chu-Vandermonde Identities
M.J. Kronenburg

Abstract
Some generalized multi-sum Chu-Vandermonde identities are presented and proved, generalizing some known multi-sum Chu-Vandermonde identities from literature and adding some quadratic and cubic examples of these identities. Some other closely related identities are provided. The identities are proved with the integral representation method using complex residues, a method also used in some earlier papers.



Keywords
: binomial coefficient, combinatorial identities.

A_{p,q}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{p}a_{i}^{q}

(1.1)

(1.2)

(1.9)

The following generalized multi-sum Chu-Vandermonde identities are proved.

For integer
m\geq 1
and
n\geq 0
:

\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle k_1=0\ \cdots\ k_m=0}{\scriptstyle\sum_{i=1}^m k_i = n}}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}=\binom{A_{0}-C_{0}}{n-C_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{c_{i}}

(1.12)

\begin{split}&\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle k_1=0\ \cdots\ k_m=0}{\scriptstyle\sum_{i=1}^m k_i = n}}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}\right]\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}k_{i}\\
&=\binom{A_{0}-C_{0}}{n-C_{0}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{c_{i}}\right]\frac{(n-C_{0})A_{1}+(A_{0}-n)C_{1}}{A_{0}-C_{0}}\\
\end{split}

(1.13)

\begin{split}&\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle k_1=0\ \cdots\ k_m=0}{\scriptstyle\sum_{i=1}^m k_i = n}}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}\right](\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}k_{i})^{2}=\binom{A_{0}-C_{0}}{n-C_{0}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{c_{i}}\right]\\
&\cdot\frac{(n-C_{0})[(n-C_{0}-1)A_{1}^{2}+(A_{0}-n)(A_{2}-C_{2}+2A_{1}C_{1})]+(A_{0}-n)(A_{0}-n-1)C_{1}^{2}}{(A_{0}-C_{0})(A_{0}-C_{0}-1)}\\
\end{split}

(1.14)

\begin{split}&\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle k_1=0\ \cdots\ k_m=0}{\scriptstyle\sum_{i=1}^m k_i = n}}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}\right]|\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}k_{i}|^{2}\\
&=\binom{A_{0}-C_{0}}{n-C_{0}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{c_{i}}\right]\frac{1}{(A_{0}-C_{0})(A_{0}-C_{0}-1)}\\
&\qquad\cdot\{(n-C_{0})[(n-C_{0}-1)|A_{1}|^{2}+(A_{0}-n)(A_{\textrm{\rm abs}}-C_{\textrm{\rm abs}}+A_{1}\overline{C_{1}}+\overline{A_{1}}C_{1})]\\
&\qquad\quad+(A_{0}-n)(A_{0}-n-1)|C_{1}|^{2}\}\\
\end{split}

(1.15)

\begin{split}&\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle k_1=0\ \cdots\ k_m=0}{\scriptstyle\sum_{i=1}^m k_i = n}}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}\right](\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}k_{i})(\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}k_{i})\\
&=\binom{A_{0}-C_{0}}{n-C_{0}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{c_{i}}\right]\frac{1}{(A_{0}-C_{0})(A_{0}-C_{0}-1)}\\
&\quad\cdot\{(n-C_{0})[(n-C_{0}-1)A_{1}A_{1}^{*}+(A_{0}-n)(A_{1,1}^{*}-C_{1,1}^{*}+A_{1}C_{1}^{*}+A_{1}^{*}C_{1})]\\
&\quad\quad+(A_{0}-n)(A_{0}-n-1)C_{1}C_{1}^{*}\}\\
\end{split}

(1.16)

\begin{split}&\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle k_1=0\ \cdots\ k_m=0}{\scriptstyle\sum_{i=1}^m k_i = n}}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}\right]\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}k_{i}^{2}=\binom{A_{0}-C_{0}}{n-C_{0}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{c_{i}}\right]\\
&\cdot\frac{(n-C_{0})[(n-C_{0}-1)A_{1,2}+(A_{0}-n)(A_{1}-C_{1}+2S_{1,1})]+(A_{0}-n)(A_{0}-n-1)C_{1,2}}{(A_{0}-C_{0})(A_{0}-C_{0}-1)}\\
\end{split}

(1.17)

\begin{split}&\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle k_1=0\ \cdots\ k_m=0}{\scriptstyle\sum_{i=1}^m k_i = n}}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}\right](\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}k_{i})^{3}\\

Proof of the Multi-sum Chu-Vandermonde

Identities

For the proof of the multi-sum identities, the order of summation is changed:

\begin{split}&\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle k_1=0\ \cdots\ k_m=0}{\scriptstyle\sum_{i=1}^m k_i = n}}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}\right]\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\scriptstyle i_{1}=0\ \cdots\ i_{s}=0}^{m\ \ \cdots\ \ m}\prod_{j=1}^{s}w_{i_{j}}k_{i_{j}}^{p_{j}}\\
&=\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\scriptstyle i_{1}=0\ \cdots\ i_{s}=0}^{m\ \ \cdots\ \ m}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{s}w_{i_{j}}\right]\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle k_1=0\ \cdots\ k_m=0}{\scriptstyle\sum_{i=1}^m k_i = n}}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}\right]\prod_{j=1}^{s}k_{i_{j}}^{p_{j}}\\
\end{split}

(3.1)

The powers
p_{j}
in the right side must be constant in the second multiple summation over the
k_{i}
,
which means that the indices

A product of sums in the summand is therefore splitted into multiple

(\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}k_{i})(\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}k_{i})=\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle p=1\ q=1}{\scriptstyle p\neq q}}^{m\ \ \ m}x_{p}y_{q}k_{p}k_{q}+\sum_{p=1}^{m}x_{p}y_{p}k_{p}^{2}

(3.2)

\begin{split}&(\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}k_{i})(\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}k_{i})(\sum_{i=1}^{m}z_{i}k_{i})=\mathop{\sum\sum\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle p=1\ q=1\ r=1}{\scriptstyle p\neq q\neq r,\ p\neq r}}^{m\ \ \ m\ \ \ m}x_{p}y_{q}z_{r}k_{p}k_{q}k_{r}\\
&+\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle p=1\ q=1}{\scriptstyle p\neq q}}^{m\ \ \ m}(x_{p}y_{q}z_{q}+y_{p}x_{q}z_{q}+z_{p}x_{q}y_{q})k_{p}k_{q}^{2}+\sum_{p=1}^{m}x_{p}y_{p}z_{p}k_{p}^{3}\\

Theorem 3.1
.

For integer

\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle k_1=0\ \cdots\ k_m=0}{\scriptstyle\sum_{i=1}^m k_i = n}}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}=\binom{A_{0}-C_{0}}{n-C_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{c_{i}}

(3.6)

Proof.

The trinomial revision identity
[
5

10

is applied:

\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}=\binom{a_{i}-c_{i}}{k_{i}-c_{i}}\binom{a_{i}}{c_{i}}

(3.7)

Because this expression is zero when

,
in the multiple summation it can be assumed that
0\leq c_{i}\leq a_{i}

and that

The restriction on the

k_{m}=n-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}k_{i}

(3.8)

reducing an

-fold sum.
The infinite geometric series

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}w^{k}=\frac{1}{1-w}

(3.9)

The upper bounds

\begin{split}&\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle k_1=0\ \cdots\ k_m=0}{\scriptstyle\sum_{i=1}^m k_i = n}}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}\\

\end{split}

Theorem 3.2
.

For integer
m\geq 1
and
1\leq p\leq m
:

\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle k_1=0\ \cdots\ k_m=0}{\scriptstyle\sum_{i=1}^m k_i = n}}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}\right]k_{p}=\binom{A_{0}-C_{0}}{n-C_{0}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{c_{i}}\right]\frac{(n-C_{0})a_{p}+(A_{0}-n)c_{p}}{A_{0}-C_{0}}

(3.12)

Proof.

The following modified infinite geometric series
[
9
]
is used:

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}kw^{k}=\frac{w}{(1-w)^{2}}

(3.13)

The theorem is first proved for
1\leq p<m
:

\begin{split}&\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\textrm{\rm Res}_{w_{i}}\right]\frac{1}{w_{m}^{n}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1+w_{i})^{a_{i}-c_{i}}w_{i}^{c_{i}-1}\right]\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\scriptstyle k_{1}=0\cdots k_{m-1}=0}^{\infty\ \ \cdots\ \ \infty}k_{p}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\left(\frac{w_{m}}{w_{i}}\right)^{k_{i}}\\
&=\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\textrm{\rm Res}_{w_{i}}\right]\frac{1}{w_{m}^{n}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1+w_{i})^{a_{i}-c_{i}}w_{i}^{c_{i}-1}\right]\frac{\frac{\textstyle w_{m}}{\textstyle w_{p}}}{(1-\frac{\textstyle w_{m}}{\textstyle w_{p}})^{2}}\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle i=1}{\scriptstyle i\neq p}}^{m-1}\frac{1}{1-\frac{\textstyle w_{m}}{\textstyle w_{i}}}\\
&=\textrm{\rm Res}_{w_{m}}\frac{(1+w_{m})^{a_{m}-c_{m}}}{w_{m}^{n-c_{m}}}\textrm{\rm Res}_{w_{p}}\frac{(1+w_{p})^{a_{p}-c_{p}}w_{p}^{c_{p}}}{(w_{p}-w_{m})^{2}}\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\scriptstyle i=1}{\scriptstyle i\neq p}}^{m-1}\textrm{\rm Res}_{w_{i}}\frac{(1+w_{i})^{a_{i}-c_{i}}w_{i}^{c_{i}}}{w_{i}-w_{m}}\\

\end{split}

(3.14)

The residue of
w_{p}
is evaluated with (
3.5
) using the product
rule for the derivative:

w_{m}

[

,
10
]

\begin{split}&\textrm{\rm Res}_{w_{m}}[(a_{p}-c_{p})\frac{(1+w_{m})^{\sum_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}-c_{i})-1}}{w_{m}^{n-\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}}}+c_{p}\frac{(1+w_{m})^{\sum_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}-c_{i})}}{w_{m}^{n-\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}+1}}]\\

and using for complex

[

Theorem 4.1
.

For integer
m\geq 1
:

\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\scriptstyle k_{1}=0\ \cdots\ k_{m}=0}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}=2^{A_{0}-C_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{c_{i}}

(4.1)

Proof.

The multiple sum of the product on the left side is a product of sums:

\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\scriptstyle k_{1}=0\ \cdots\ k_{m}=0}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{k_{i}=0}^{a_{i}}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}

(4.2)

The trinomial revision identity
[
5
,
10
]
is applied as in theorem
3.1
:

\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}=\binom{a_{i}-c_{i}}{k_{i}-c_{i}}\binom{a_{i}}{c_{i}}

(4.3)

Because this expression is zero when
c_{i}>a_{i}
,
in the individual sums it can be assumed that
0\leq c_{i}\leq a_{i}
.
Then each sum can be evaluated as follows, where the upper bound
a
can be
replaced by
\infty
because the summand is zero when
k>a
:

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=0}^{a}\binom{a-c}{k-c}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\textrm{\rm Res}_{w}\frac{(1+w)^{a-c}}{w^{k-c+1}}=\textrm{\rm Res}_{w}(1+w)^{a-c}w^{c-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\frac{1}{w})^{k}\\
&=\textrm{\rm Res}_{w}\frac{(1+w)^{a-c}w^{c-1}}{1-1/w}=\textrm{\rm Res}_{w}\frac{(1+w)^{a-c}w^{c}}{w-1}=2^{a-c}\\
\end{split}

(4.4)

The product of these individual sums gives the theorem.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

For integer
m\geq 1
and

:

\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}_{\scriptstyle k_{1}=0\ \cdots\ k_{m}=0}^{a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ a_{m}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{k_{i}}{c_{i}}\right]k_{p}=2^{A_{0}-C_{0}-1}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{a_{i}}{c_{i}}\right](a_{p}+c_{p})

(4.5)

Proof.

The following modified infinite geometric series
[
9
]
is used:

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}kw^{k}=\frac{w}{(1-w)^{2}}

(4.6)

One of the individual sums of the product becomes:

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=0}^{a}\binom{a-c}{k-c}k=\sum_{k=0}^{a}k\textrm{\rm Res}_{w}\frac{(1+w)^{a-c}}{w^{k-c+1}}=\textrm{\rm Res}_{w}(1+w)^{a-c}w^{c-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k(\frac{1}{w})^{k}\\
&=\textrm{\rm Res}_{w}\frac{(1+w)^{a-c}w^{c-1}}{w(1-1/w)^{2}}=\textrm{\rm Res}_{w}\frac{(1+w)^{a-c}w^{c}}{(w-1)^{2}}=(a-c)2^{a-c-1}+c2^{a-c}\\
&=2^{a-c-1}(a-c+2c)=2^{a-c-1}(a+c)\\
\end{split}

(4.7)

The other individual sums of the product remain identical,
so the product becomes the theorem.
∎

The other individual sums of the product remain identical,

5
A Proof of Residue Identity
3.5

Theorem 5.1
.

When a complex function
f(z)
has a pole of order
m
at
z=z_{p}
,
then the residue of this function at
z=z_{p}
is
[
7
]
:

\textrm{\rm Res}_{z=z_{p}}f(z)=\frac{1}{(m-1)!}D_{z}^{m-1}[(z-z_{p})^{m}f(z)]|_{z=z_{p}}

(5.1)

where
D_{z}^{n}f(z)|_{z=z_{p}}
is the
n
-th derivative of
f(z)
at
z=z_{p}
.

Proof.

When a complex function
f(z)
has a pole of order
m
at
z=z_{p}
,
then the residue of
f(z)
at
z=z_{p}
can be defined as the
coefficient
a_{-1}
in the Laurent series expansion of
f(z)
at
z=z_{p}
,
where
a_{-m}\neq 0

[
7
,
12
]
:

f(z)=\sum_{k=-m}^{\infty}a_{k}(z-z_{p})^{k}

(5.2)

From this follows:

(z-z_{p})^{m}f(z)=\sum_{k=-m}^{\infty}a_{k}(z-z_{p})^{m+k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k-m}(z-z_{p})^{k}

(5.3)

For integer
k\geq 0
:

D_{z}^{n}(z-z_{p})^{k}=\begin{cases}(z-z_{p})^{k-n}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(k-j)&\text{if $k\geq n$}\\
0&\text{if $k<n$}\\
\end{cases}

(5.4)

The
n
-th derivative of
(z-z_{p})^{m}f(z)
at
z=z_{p}
becomes,
by differentiating the infinite power series term by term
[
6
]
,
and using
0^{0}=1

[
5
]
, where
\delta_{k,n}
is the Kronecker delta:

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{n!}D_{z}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k-m}(z-z_{p})^{k}|_{z=z_{p}}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k-m}D_{z}^{n}(z-z_{p})^{k}|_{z=z_{p}}\\
&=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}a_{k-m}(z-z_{p})^{k-n}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(k-j)|_{z=z_{p}}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}a_{k-m}0^{k-n}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(k-j)\\
&=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}a_{k-m}\delta_{k,n}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(k-j)=\frac{1}{n!}a_{n-m}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-j)=\frac{1}{n!}a_{n-m}n!=a_{n-m}\\
\end{split}

(5.5)

Taking
n=m-1
the theorem is proved.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

When a complex function
f(z)
does not have a pole at
z=z_{p}
, then:

\textrm{\rm Res}_{z=z_{p}}\frac{f(z)}{(z-z_{p})^{m}}=\frac{1}{(m-1)!}D_{z}^{m-1}f(z)|_{z=z_{p}}

(5.6)

Proof.

Because
f(z)
does not have a pole at
z=z_{p}
,
the power series expansion of
f(z)
at
z=z_{p}
is the Taylor series expansion:

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(z-z_{p})^{k}

(5.7)

and therefore:

\frac{f(z)}{(z-z_{p})^{m}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(z-z_{p})^{k-m}=\sum_{k=-m}^{\infty}a_{m+k}(z-z_{p})^{k}=\sum_{k=-m}^{\infty}b_{k}(z-z_{p})^{k}

(5.8)

where
b_{k}=a_{m+k}
. The residue of this function at
z=z_{p}
is
b_{-1}=a_{m-1}
,
and using the same method as in the previous theorem:

\frac{1}{n!}D_{z}^{n}f(z)|_{z=z_{p}}=\frac{1}{n!}D_{z}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(z-z_{p})^{k}|_{z=z_{p}}=a_{n}

(5.9)

Taking
n=m-1
the theorem is proved.
∎

When in the last theorem
a_{0}\neq 0
, the last two theorems are equivalent.

Solving high dimensional FBSDE with deep signature techniques with application to nonlinear options pricing
Hui Sun, Feng bao

Abstract
We report two methods for solving FBSDEs of path dependent types of high dimensions. Inspired by the work from [ 5 ] , [ 6 ] and [ 19 ] , we propose a deep learning framework for solving such problems using path signatures as underlying features. Our two methods (forward/backward) demonstrate comparable/better accuracy and efficiency compared to the state of the art [ 1 ] , [ 2 ] and [ 3 ] . More importantly, leveraging the techniques developed in [ 23 ] we are able to solve the problem of high dimension which is an limitation in [ 1 ] and [ 2 ] . We also provide convergence proof for both methods with the proof of the backward methods inspired from [ 26 ] in the Markovian case.

The classical decoupled forward-backward SDE takes the following form

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX_{t}=b(t,X_{t})dt+\sigma(t,X_{t})dW_{t}\\

2.1
A quick introduction to Signature methods

In this section, we give a brief introduction to the path signature. We first provide definition of the signature of a path and then present a few examples which is then followed by the log-signature. We comment that the dimension of signature of a path increase exponentially with the dimension of the underlying state process. Log-signature on the other hand, will effectively reduce the dimension without losing the information content of the path signature. We then introduce the Chen’s identity which is needed for the efficiency of numerical computation of signatures. Lastly, we introduce the Universal nonlinearity of proposition which shows the approximation power of functions using signature as features to the path dependent functions.

Definition 2.1

(Definition A.1.

\displaystyle Sig(X)_{a,b}=

\displaystyle\Big(\underset{{a<t_{1}<...<t_{k}<b}}{\int...\int}dX_{t_{1}}\otimes...\otimes dX_{t_{k}}\Big)_{k\geq 0}

Assumption 1
.

Let

3.

4.

\displaystyle|f(t,x,y_{1},z_{1})-f(t,x,y_{2},z_{2})|^{2}\leq K_{y}|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}+K_{z}|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}

We comment that those assumptions are standard for the existence of the BSDE except for

We want to identify the solution of the BSDE with the solution of a Semi-linear PDE according to the non-linear Feynman-Kac formula. Hence, we make the following assumption.

Assumption 2

Assume that the following PDE has a classical solution

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)+\mathcal{L}u(t,x)+f(t,x,u,\sigma^{T}\partial_{x}u)=0,\ (t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
u(T,x)=g(x),\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{cases}

(3.1)

Then under such assumption, one can write

\displaystyle Y_{t}=u(t,X_{t}),\ \ Z_{t}=\sigma^{T}(X_{t})D_{x}u(t,X_{t})

In all the analysis that follows, we will take

3.2.1
Forward Algorithm

Lemma 3.3
.

Under Assumption 1 and Assumption 2, further assume that
Z_{t}=\sigma^{T}(X_{t})D_{x}u(t,X_{t})
is Lipschitz uniformly in
t
, then there exist
Z^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}(\cdot)
for different
0\leq n\leq N-1
such that the following is true

\displaystyle\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}[\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}|Z_{t}-Z^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}dt]\leq C\Delta t

(3.3)

Proof.

Note that we have the following sequence of inequalities

\displaystyle\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}[\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}|Z_{t}-Z^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}dt]

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}[\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}|Z_{t}-Z_{t_{n}}|^{2}+|Z_{t_{n}}-Z^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}dt]

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t+4\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}[\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}|Z_{t_{n}}(X_{t_{n}})-Z_{t_{n}}(X^{N}_{t_{n}})|^{2}+|Z_{t_{n}}(X^{N}_{t_{n}})-Z^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}dt]

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t+4\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}[\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}|Z_{t_{n}}(X^{N}_{t_{n}})-Z^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}dt]

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t

(3.4)

The last term can be made arbitrarily small by the universal non-linearity of the path signature.
∎

Next, we state and prove the main theorem of the algorithm.

Theorem 3.4
.

Under Assumption 1 and Assumption 2 then there exist
Z^{N,Sig}_{n}(\cdot)
for different
0\leq n\leq N-1
such that the following is true

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{t_{n}\leq t\leq t_{n+1}}|Y_{t}-Y^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}]\leq C\Delta t

(3.5)

Proof.

Take the difference between the following set of equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Y_{t_{n+1}}=Y_{t_{n}}-\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}f(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t})dt+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}Z_{t}dW_{t}\\
Y^{N,Sig}_{t_{n+1}}=Y^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}-\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}f(t_{n},X^{N}_{t_{n}},Y^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}},Z^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}})dt+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}Z^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}dW_{t}\end{cases}

(3.6)

and we obtain the following

\displaystyle\Delta Y_{n+1}=\Delta Y_{n}-\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}(\Delta f_{n})dt+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}(Z_{t}-Z^{N,Sig}_{n})dW_{t}

(3.7)

Denote
\Delta\bar{Y}^{N,Sig}_{n}:=Y_{t_{n}}-Y^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}

By squaring both sides and using Young’s inequality with epsilon, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\Delta\bar{Y}^{N,Sig}_{n+1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\frac{\Delta t}{\epsilon})\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[|\Delta\bar{Y}^{N,Sig}_{n}+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}(Z_{t}-Z^{N,Sig}_{n})dW_{t}|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}]]+(1+\frac{\epsilon}{\Delta t})\mathbb{E}[\Big(-\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\Delta f_{n}dt\Big)^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq(1+\frac{\Delta t}{\epsilon})\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[|\Delta\bar{Y}^{N,Sig}_{n}|^{2}+|\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}(Z_{t}-Z^{N,Sig}_{n})dW_{t}|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}]]+2(1+\frac{\epsilon}{\Delta t})\Big(\mathbb{E}[\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}|\Delta f_{n}|^{2}dt]\Delta t\Big)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\frac{\Delta t}{\epsilon})\mathbb{E}[|\Delta\bar{Y}^{N,Sig}_{n}|^{2}]+C(\Delta t^{2}+\mathbb{E}[\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}|Z_{t}-Z^{N,Sig}_{n}|^{2}dt]+\mathbb{E}[|\Delta\bar{Y}^{N,Sig}_{n}|^{2}]\Delta t)

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}[\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}|Z_{t}-Z^{N,Sig}_{n}|^{2}]dt

\displaystyle\leq(1+\frac{\Delta t}{\epsilon})\mathbb{E}[|\Delta\bar{Y}^{N,Sig}_{n}|^{2}]+C(\Delta t^{2}+\mathbb{E}[\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}|Z_{t}-Z^{N,Sig}_{n}|^{2}]dt+\mathbb{E}[\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}|\Delta Z^{N}_{n}|^{2}]dt)

Then, by Discrete Gronwall inequality, by Lemma
3.3
and Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t_{n}}-Y^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}]\leq C\Delta t

(3.8)

Then together with Lemma
3.1
, the claim is proved.
∎

3.2.2
Backward Algorithm

Since the convergence takes the variance of
Y^{N,Sig}_{0}
as the loss function, we first prove an a-priori estimate for the loss.

Now we state the main thereom regarding the convergence of the backward scheme.
We also provide a lower bound for
\text{Var}(Y^{N,Sig}_{0})

Theorem 3.5
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle\text{Var}(Y^{N,Sig}_{0})>(1-\epsilon-\frac{2TK_{z}}{\epsilon})\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]-C\Delta t-\frac{4T^{2}K_{y}}{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}[\max_{0\leq n\leq N-1}|Y_{t_{n}}-Y^{N}_{t_{n}}|^{2}]

(3.9)

Proof.

Note that the true solution
Y_{0}
is deterministic and that
\text{Var}(Y^{N,Sig}_{0})=\text{Var}(Y_{0}-Y^{N,Sig}_{0})
,
we have the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\text{Var}(Y^{N,Sig}_{0})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\Delta g_{n}-\int^{T}_{0}\Delta f_{n}ds+\int^{T}_{0}\Delta Z_{n}dW_{t}-\mathbb{E}[\Delta g_{n}-\int^{T}_{0}\Delta f_{n}ds]\big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]+2\mathbb{E}\Big[\int^{T}_{t}\Delta Z_{n}dW_{t}(\Delta g_{n}-\int^{T}_{0}\Delta f_{n}dt-\mathbb{E}[\Delta g_{n}-\int^{T}_{0}\Delta f_{n}dt])\Big]

\displaystyle\geq(1-\epsilon)\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}[(\Delta g_{n}-\int^{T}_{0}\Delta f_{n}dt-\mathbb{E}[\Delta g_{n}+\int^{T}_{0}\Delta f_{n}dt])^{2}]

(3.10)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\epsilon)\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}[(\Delta g_{n}-\int^{T}_{0}\Delta f_{n}dt)^{2}]

\displaystyle\geq(1-\epsilon)\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]-\frac{2}{\epsilon}(\mathbb{E}[|\Delta g_{n}|^{2}]+T\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta f_{n}|^{2}dt])

\displaystyle\geq(1-\epsilon)\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]-C\Delta t-\frac{2}{\epsilon}(C\Delta t+T\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}K_{y}|\Delta Y_{n}|^{2}+K_{z}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt])

\displaystyle\geq(1-\epsilon-\frac{2TK_{z}}{\epsilon})\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]-C\Delta t-\frac{2TK_{y}}{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|Y_{t}-Y_{t_{n}}+Y_{t_{n}}-Y^{N}_{t_{n}}|^{2}dt]

\displaystyle\geq(1-\epsilon-\frac{2TK_{z}}{\epsilon})\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]-C\Delta t-\frac{4T^{2}K_{y}}{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}[\max_{0\leq n\leq N-1}|Y_{t_{n}}-Y^{N}_{t_{n}}|^{2}]

(3.11)

where in the inequalities we used the fact that
Var(X)\leq\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]
and the second inequality that
2ab\geq-\epsilon a^{2}-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}b^{2}
. In the second to last inequality, we used Theorem
3.1
.
∎

We state the following main theorem about the convergence of the backward algorithm.

Theorem 3.6
.

Under Assumption 1 and Assumption 2, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}-Y^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}]+\int^{T}_{0}\mathbb{E}[|Z_{t}-Z^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}dt]\leq C(\Delta t+\text{Var}(Y^{N,Sig}_{0}))

(3.12)

Proof.

Take the difference between the exact solution and the numerical solution, we have the following inequality:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t_{n}}-Y^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|]

\displaystyle\leq 3\Big(\mathbb{E}[|\Delta g|^{2}]+T\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta f_{n}|^{2}dt+\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 3C\Delta t+C\Delta t+3T\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}K_{y}|\Delta Y_{n}|^{2}+K_{z}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]+3\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t+3(1+TK_{y})\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]+3TK_{y}\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|Y_{t}-Y_{t_{n}}+Y_{t_{n}}-Y^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}dt]

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t+3(1+TK_{y})\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]+6T^{2}K_{y}\max_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t_{n}}-Y^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}]

(3.13)

This shows that

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t_{n}}-Y^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\Delta t+3(1+TK_{y})\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]}{1-6T^{2}K_{y}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t+\frac{3(1+TK_{y})}{1-6T^{2}K_{y}}\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]

(3.14)

Using Theorem 4.4 ((
3.11

\displaystyle\text{Var}(Y^{N,Sig}_{0})

\displaystyle\geq(1-\epsilon-\frac{2TK_{z}}{\epsilon})\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]-C\Delta t-\frac{4T^{2}K_{y}}{\epsilon}\big(C\Delta t+\frac{3(1+TK_{y})}{1-6T^{2}K_{y}}\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]\big)

\displaystyle\geq\Big((1-\epsilon-\frac{2TK_{z}}{\epsilon})+\frac{4T^{2}K_{y}}{\epsilon}\frac{3(1+TK_{y})}{1-6T^{2}K_{y}}\Big)\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]-C\Delta t

Then take

(3.16)

together with (

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}-Y^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}]+\int^{T}_{0}\mathbb{E}[|Z_{t}-Z^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}dt]\leq C(\Delta t+\text{Var}(Y^{N,Sig}_{0}))

(3.17)

Theorem 3.7
.

Under Assumption 1 and Assumption 2, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\text{Var}(Y^{N,Sig}_{0})\leq C\Big(\Delta t+\sum_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\mathbb{E}[|Z_{t_{n}}(X^{N}_{n})-Z^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}]\Delta t\Big)

Proof.

Again, note that the true solution

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t+C\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Y_{n}|^{2}+|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt]+2E[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta Z_{n}|^{2}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 2E[\int^{T}_{0}|Z_{t}-Z_{t_{n}}|^{2}+|Z_{t_{n}}-Z^{N,Sig}_{t_{n}}|^{2}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[d^{2}_{\infty}(X_{t},X_{t_{n}})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\sup_{t_{n}\leq t\leq t_{n+1}}|X_{t}-X_{t_{n}}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t

(3.30)

As such, by using similar argument, one can show that the non-Markovian version of Theorem
3.4
and Theorem
3.8
also hold.

4.1
Lookback Options

Consider the Blackscholes seting where the stock price follows the following dynamics

\displaystyle dX_{t}=rX_{t}dt+\sigma X_{t}dW_{t}

(4.1)

The terminal payoff is defined to be

\displaystyle g(X_{\cdot\wedge T})=X_{T}-\inf_{0\leq t\leq T}X_{t}

(4.2)

The option price is defined to be

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\exp(-r(T-t))\mathbb{E}[g(X_{\cdot\wedge T})|\mathcal{F}_{t}]

(4.3)

Then
e^{-rt}Y_{t}=\mathbb{E}[e^{-rT}g(X_{\cdot\wedge T})|\mathcal{F}_{t}]
is a martingale. Meaning that

\displaystyle d(e^{-rt}Y_{t})=Z^{\prime}_{t}dW_{t}

(4.4)

for some square integrable process
Z^{\prime}_{t}
, then one immediately has

\displaystyle dY_{t}=rY_{t}dt+Z_{t}dW_{t}

(4.5)

where
Z_{t}:=e^{rt}Z^{\prime}_{t}
.
The solution
Y_{t}
has the following analytic formula

\displaystyle Y_{t}=X_{t}\Phi(p_{1})-m_{t}e^{-r(T-t)}\Phi(p_{2})-X_{t}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2r}\big(\Phi(-p_{1})-e^{-r(T-t)}(\frac{m_{t}}{yt})^{2r/\sigma^{2}}\Phi(-p_{3})\big)

(4.6)

where

m_{t}:=\inf_{0\leq u\leq t}X_{t},p_{1}=\frac{\log(X_{t}/m_{t})+(r+\sigma^{2}/2)(T-t)}{\sigma\sqrt{T-t}},p_{2}=p_{1}-\sigma\sqrt{T-t},p_{3}=p_{1}-\frac{2r}{\sigma}(\sqrt{T-t})

For the model specification, similar to
[
1
]
, we use
\tilde{N}=2000
and
N=20
. We use the truncated signature method with level
m=3
.
We take
x_{0}=1,\sigma=1,r=0.01,T=1
. Since in
[
1

it is demonstrated that the method therein is state of the art, we present comparison between results obtained using methods in
[
1
]
and our forward/backward methods in table

For the forward algorithm (method 1), we take batch size to be 100. And we use the Adam optimizer with learning rate
10^{-3}
. since
N=20
, we use 20 individual neural network to approximate
Z_{t_{n}}
for each
0\leq n\leq N-1
. We use fully connected feedforward neural networks with 2 hidden layers each of 64 neurons.
It can be observed from Figure

that our forward algorithm produces good result compared to the exact solution: the blue curve is very close to the red dashline.

For the backward algorithm (method), we note that due to the designed model methodology, it is desirable to use a larger batch size: we are minimizing the variance of the mini-batches and use the mean of the batch samples as the sample estimate for
Y_{0}
. We observe that for this method, convergence happens fast but with values typically fluctuating about a fixed level. This is due to the fact that

•

We are not training the model on a fixed amount of trajectories of
\{X_{\tilde{t}_{j}}\}^{M}_{0\leq j\leq\tilde{N}}
where M is a fixed amount (say 10000). Instead, to overcome over-fitting, we generate different batch samples for each iteration. Hence, one trajectory of sample path may leads to high variance.

•

We minimize the variance of the sample and use the mean of the sample as estimation for
Y_{0}

where the

]

]

On the analytic side for benchmark purposes, we comment that:

i.

The American option price should be higher than the European price due to its flexibility of being able to exercise early.

ii.

By Jensen’s inequality, using the current parameters

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{-rT}\Big(\sum^{d}_{i=1}\frac{1}{dT}\int^{T}_{0}X^{i}_{t}dt-K\Big)^{+}]\geq e^{-rT}\Big(\mathbb{E}[\frac{1}{dT}\sum^{d}_{i=1}\int^{T}_{0}X^{i}_{t}dt-K]\Big)^{+}=2.42

iii.

Again, we can show that the price of the Ameransian option price is
decreasing

\displaystyle e^{-rT}\Big(\frac{1}{2dT}\sum^{2d}_{i=1}\int^{T}_{0}X^{i}_{t}dt-K\Big)^{+}\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{-rT}\Big(\frac{1}{dT}\sum^{d}_{i=1}\int^{T}_{0}X^{i}_{t}dt-K\Big)^{+}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-rT}\Big(\frac{1}{dT}\sum^{2d}_{i=d+1}\int^{T}_{0}X^{i}_{t}dt-K\Big)^{+}

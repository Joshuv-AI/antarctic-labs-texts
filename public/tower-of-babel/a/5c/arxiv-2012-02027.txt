CF + \text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} excitation in the interstellar medium
Benjamin Desrousseaux, François Lique, Javier R. Goicoechea, Ernesto Quintas-Sánchez, Richard Dawes

Abstract
The detection of CF + \text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} in interstellar clouds potentially allows astronomers to infer the elemental fluorine abundance and the ionization fraction in ultraviolet-illuminated molecular gas. Because local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) conditions are hardly fulfilled in the interstellar medium (ISM), the accurate determination of the CF + \text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} abundance requires one to model its non-LTE excitation via both radiative and collisional processes. Here, we report quantum calculations of rate coefficients for the rotational excitation of CF + \text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} in collisions with para- and ortho- H 2 \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} (for temperatures up to 150 K). As an application, we present non-LTE excitation models that reveal population inversion in physical conditions typical of ISM photodissociation regions (PDRs). We successfully applied these models to fit the CF + \text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} emission lines previously observed toward the Orion Bar and Horsehead PDRs. The radiative transfer models achieved with these new rate coefficients allow the use of CF + \text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} as a powerful probe to study molecular clouds exposed to strong stellar radiation fields.



1
Introduction

Although fluorine is one of the most reactive species in the interstellar medium (ISM;
Neufeld et al. 2005
;
Neufeld & Wolfire 2009
), the chemistry of the most abundant F-bearing molecules, HF and
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
, can be accurately described by a few chemical reactions that depend on the amount of F atoms, H
2
molecules, and C
+
ions
(
Neufeld et al. 2005
)
.

\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
is produced in ISM regions bathed by stellar ultraviolet (UV) photons able to ionize carbon atoms in diffuse interstellar clouds or at the illuminated surfaces of dense molecular clouds (so-called photodissociation regions; PDRs).
The simplicity of the fluorine chemical network results in a great sensitivity of astrophysical models to molecular data (reactive rate coefficients).

Interstellar
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
was first detected in the Orion Bar PDR by
Neufeld et al. 2006
.
Since then, this molecular ion has been widely observed in other PDRs and diffuse clouds of the Milky Way and beyond
(
Neufeld et al. 2006
;
Kalenskii &
Johansson 2010a
;
Kalenskii &
Johansson 2010b
;
Guzmán et al. 2012a
;
Liszt et al. 2014
;
Liszt et al. 2015
;
Muller et al. 2016
;
Nagy et al. 2013
;
Nagy et al. 2017
)
.
In these environments, the
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
abundance is a powerful proxy for
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
(closely related to the ionization fraction) and for F atoms, which are both difficult to observe
(
Guzmán et al. 2012a
)
.
Because local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) conditions are hardly fulfilled in
the ISM

(
Roueff & Lique 2013
)
, the competition between radiative and collisional processes has to be taken into account in the molecular line modeling in order to derive an accurate
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
abundance and gas physical conditions.
Collisional data for collisions between
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and the most abundant species in the ISM (usually atomic and molecular hydrogen) are then essential.
Collisional rate coefficients with
He
, as a template for
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, were first computed by
Ajili & Hammami 2013
and recently updated by
Denis-Alpizar et al. 2018
.
However, it is well established
(
Roueff & Lique 2013
)
that
He
data are a bad surrogate for
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in case of collisions with an ion.

Denis-Alpizar & Rubayo-Soneira 2019
provided the first proper study of the
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
–
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
system.
They treated the rotational relaxation of
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
by para-
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
using a reduced-dimensional potential energy surface (PES) averaged over
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
rotation.
The use of an averaged PES, however, only allows one to consider collisions of
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
with a spherical
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, neglecting anisotropy effects due to the
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
rotation.

\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
is mostly detected in warm molecular gas, where the
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
ortho-to-para ratio is found to be large, therefore obtaining rate coefficients for collisional excitation with ortho-
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is crucial and cannot be achieved considering a spherical structure-less
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.

In order to overcome this limitation,
Desrousseaux et al. 2019
recently presented a new highly accurate four-dimensional (4D) PES, by which the calculation of rate coefficients for collisional excitation of
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
by both para- and ortho-
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is made possible.
In that work, preliminary cross-sections at low collisional energy (up to 150 cm
-1
) were presented for both para- and ortho-
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
collisions.
In this paper, we describe further results using this recently computed
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
–
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
4D PES
(
Desrousseaux et al. 2019
)
to carry out scattering calculations of rate coefficients for the collisional excitation of
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
by
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
We also used the new collisional data to simulate the excitation of
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
in the ISM.
We demonstrate that
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
exhibits population inversion at typical physical conditions of molecular clouds, and we show that
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
is a powerful tracer of the gas characteristics in diffuse interstellar clouds and illuminated surfaces of dense molecular clouds.

The
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
–
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
PES was computed employing electronic structure data from coupled-cluster theory extrapolated to the complete basis set (CBS) limit and constructed using the recently released software-package
autosurf

(
Quintas-Sánchez & Dawes 2019
)
.
On this PES, a global minimum of
-1230.2
cm
-1
is found for a planar structure with the two monomers almost parallel (see
Desrousseaux et al. 2019
for more details).
This large energy well-depth, typical for ion-molecule collisional systems, makes employing state-of-the-art methods to compute the cross-sections very challenging, and, to the best of our knowledge, only four molecular ions (
\text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}

(
Kłos & Lique 2011
)
,
\text{HCO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}

(
Massó & Wiesenfeld 2014
)
,
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}

(
Walker et al. 2016
)
, and
\text{SH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}

(
Dagdigian 2019b
;
Dagdigian 2019a
)
) among the 38 detected in the ISM have been studied in collision with nonspherical
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.

2
Scattering calculations

The
molscat
nonreactive scattering code (Hutson & Green 1994) was used to carry out close-coupling calculations of the rotational (de)excitation cross-sections for collisions between
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and both para- and ortho-
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
The cross-sections were computed using the quantum time-independent close-coupling (CC) approach and the hybrid log-derivative/Airy propagator implemented in the
molscat
code.
In the following, molecule-related parameters are labeled with subscripts 1 and 2, referring to the
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
molecules, respectively.

The expansion over angular functions of the potential was performed as described by
Green 1975
:

V(R,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\varphi)=\sum_{l_{1},l_{2},l}v_{l_{1},l_{2},l}(R)A_{l_{1},l_{2},l}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\varphi),

(1)

where
A_{l_{1},l_{2},l}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\varphi)
is constructed from coupled spherical functions
Y_{l_{i},m_{i}}(\theta_{i},\varphi)
and the rotational angular momenta of
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
The potential was expanded including
0\leq l_{1}\leq 24
for the
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
molecule, and
0\leq l_{2}\leq 6
for the
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
molecule.

The two molecules were considered as rigid rotors.
The molecular constants of
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
were set at the value given by
Cazzoli et al. 2010
:
B_{e}=1.720912
cm
-1
,
\alpha_{e}=0.0189
cm
-1
, and
D_{e}=63\times 10^{-6}
cm
-1
.
The molecular constants of
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
were set as
(
Herzberg & Huber 1979
)

B_{e}=60.853
cm
-1
,
\alpha_{e}=3.062
cm
-1
, and
D_{e}=4.71\times 10^{-2}
cm
-1
.

Cross-sections were obtained for the first 22 rotational levels of
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
(
0\leq j_{1}\leq 21
).
As already discussed in
Desrousseaux et al. 2019
, convergence of these cross-sections was ensured by including the 33 lowest rotational levels of
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and only the lowest rotational levels of para-
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
j_{2}=0
) and ortho-
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
j_{2}=1
) in the basis set.

At each collisional energy, the maximum value of the total angular momentum

was automatically determined by the

1\times 10^{-4}
Å
2
, going up to

at the highest energies.

The determination of the thermal rate coefficients was achieved by averaging the cross-sections
\sigma_{\alpha\rightarrow\beta}
over the collisional energy (
E_{c}

k_{\alpha\rightarrow\beta}(T)=\left(\frac{8}{\pi\mu k_{B}^{3}T^{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma_{\alpha\rightarrow\beta}\ E_{c}e^{-\frac{E_{c}}{k_{\text{B}}T}}dE_{c},

(2)

where
\mu
is the reduced mass of the colliding system and
k_{B}
is the Boltzmann’s
constant.

\alpha
and
\beta,
respectively, designate the initial and final rotational states of the
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
molecule.
Collisional energies up to 1500 cm
-1
were explored in order to allow the determination of the rate coefficients for collisions between
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and both para- and ortho-
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

4
Summary

In summary, we computed rate coefficients for inelastic collisions of
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
by both para- and ortho-
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
for temperatures up to 150 K and rotational transitions between the first 22 levels of the
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
molecule (
0\leq j_{1}\leq 21
).
We used these new rate coefficients in non-LTE excitation and radiative transfer calculations that reveal inversion population and weak maser
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
emission at gas physical conditions corresponding to those in regions where this molecule can be observed.
Finally, we were able to constrain gas density at the surface of the Horsehead and Orion Bar PDRs by reproducing their observed
\text{CF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
line intensities.

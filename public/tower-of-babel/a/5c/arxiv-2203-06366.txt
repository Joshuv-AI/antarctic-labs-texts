1.
Introduction

To any strongly continuous orthogonal representation
(U_{t})
of
\mathbb{R}
on a real Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
with
dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})\geqslant 2
, Hiai in
[
Hi03
]
constructed the
q
-deformed Araki-Woods von Neumann algebras
(
hereafter abbreviated as
q
-Araki-Woods algebras
)
for
-1<q<1
. These are
W^{\ast}
-algebras usually arising from non-tracial representations of the
q
-commutation relations (a Yang-Baxter deformation of the canonical commutation relations), thereby yielding an interpolation between the Bosonic and Fermionic statistics. Hiai’s functor is a fusion of Shlyakhtenko’s free CAR functor
[
S97
]

(
associated
W^{*}
-algebras are called free Araki-Woods factors
)
and the
q
-Gaussian functor of Bo
\overset{.}{\text{z}}
ejko-Speicher
(
associated
W^{*}
-algebras are called Bo
\overset{.}{\text{z}}
ejko-Speicher factors
)

(
see
[
BS91
]
)
. All of these constructions are generalizations of Voiculescu’s
C^{*}
-free Gaussian functor, which is the central object of study in free probability
[
VDN92
]
. Note that when
q=0
, i.e. the Yang-Baxter deformation is trivial
(
free case
)
, Hiai’s functor reduces to Shlyakhtenko’s functor. However, when
(U_{t})
is trivial, Hiai’s functor reduces to the
q
-Gaussian functor. Further, when
q=0
and
(U_{t})
is trivial, one obtains Voiculescu’s functor.

The
q
-Araki-Woods algebras are quite complicated objects. Structural properties of the Bo
\overset{.}{\text{z}}
ejko-Speicher factors have been studied in
[
A11
,
BS91
,
BKS97
,
D14
,
R05
,
N04
,
Sn04
,
S04
,
S09
]
, and those of the free Araki-Woods factors have been studied in
[
H09
,
HR11
,
BHV15
]
; though, this list is by no means complete. On the contrary, very little is known about the
q
-Araki-Woods algebras
(
see
[
N04
,
N06
,
W21
]
)
. In fact, the simplest question regarding its factoriality is not known in full generality. This is because, unlike the case when
q=0
, there is very little room to perform meaningful calculations with the standard generators of these algebras owing to the complicated nature of the scalar product
(
of the GNS space
)
and the interference of the modular group. The case when
dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})=2
is the hardest and most notorious.

This paper attempts the factoriality problem of the
q
-Araki-Woods algebras. Before describing our results, though, we note the past efforts in this direction.

The factoriality of the Bo
\overset{.}{\text{z}}
ejko-Speicher algebras was not a single-handed attempt. In
[
BKS97
]
, the factoriality was established by Bo
\overset{.}{\text{z}}
ejko, Kümmerer and Speicher when
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
is infinite. By making careful estimates of norms of certain operators on the
q
-deformed full Fock space, Śniady established the factoriality when
dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
is finite but greater than a constant depending on
q
. It was finally settled in
[
R05
]
by showing that any standard generator of the Bo
\overset{.}{\text{z}}
ejko-Speicher algebra generates a strongly mixing
(
see
[
CFM
]
for defn.
)
MASA in the ambient algebra. Therefore, the center of the algebra gets arrested in two orthogonal
(
with respect to the vacuum state
)
MASAs and is thus reduced to scalars. By using freeness and modular data, Shlyakhtenko established the factoriality of the free Araki-Woods algebras in
[
S97
]
.

Hiai established the factoriality of the
q
-Araki-Woods algebras in
[
Hi03
]
in the case when the dimension of the almost periodic part of
(U_{t})
is infinite or
(U_{t})
is weakly mixing, by showing that the centralizer of the vacuum state has trivial relative commutant
(
irreducible inclusion
)
. Unfortunately, there is a gap in the proof of
[
Hi03
, Thm. 3.2]
. To be precise, Hiai’s proof holds only in the case when the set of eigenvalues of the analytic generator of
(U_{t})
has a limit point in
\mathbb{R}
other than
0
. Without this assumption, the conclusion ‘
\varphi(y^{*}x)=0
’ in the last equation in
[
Hi03
, Thm. 3.2]
would fail, and hence, the final statement cannot be concluded. Using the theory of free monotone transport
(
see
[
Ne15
, Thm. 4.5]
)
, Nelson proved that when
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
is finite-dimensional, the
q
-Araki-Woods algebras are isomorphic to the free Araki-Woods factors for sufficiently small values of
\left|q\right|
; in particular, they are factors, in this case.

[
BM
]
closely followed
[
R05
]
. In
[
BM
]
, the authors observe that a non-zero vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
fixed by
(U_{t})
enables the construction of an orthonormal basis of analytic vectors
(
of the GNS space
)
which behaves well as long as one only considers its interaction with elements of the algebra
vN(s_{q}(\xi))
, where
s_{q}(\xi)
is the standard self-adjoint generator of the
q
-Araki-Woods algebra corresponding to
\xi
. This allows exploiting the ideas in
[
R05
]
to show that
vN(s_{q}(\xi))
is a strongly mixing MASA living inside the centralizer of the vacuum state. Using this, the factoriality of the
q
-Araki-Woods algebras was shown to be true if
(U_{t})
is not ergodic or has a non-zero weakly mixing component. Irreducibility of the centralizer was also obtained when
(U_{t})
is not ergodic, and the almost periodic component is at least two-dimensional. At the same time, a proof of the same statement on factoriality was obtained independently by Skalski and Wang
[
SW
]
; note that the phenomenon of mixing is implicit in this proof too.

When
(U_{t})
is ergodic and almost periodic, the above ideas involving MASAs freeze. This is because the standard self-adjoint generators no longer generate abelian von Neumann subalgebras with appropriate conditional expectations or operator-valued weights. In fact, it is far worse that these algebras are quasi split
[
BM2
]
and therefore admit very large relative commutants.

Given the previous attempts, we are left to deal with the case when
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{R}}
, where
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{R}}
is a real Hilbert space
(
could be
0)
, and
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is reducing subspace for
(U_{t})
with associated sub representation being ergodic. As discussed before, we cannot rely on MASAs anymore, but our strategy is to leverage with a bit of ‘mixing’. The analysis is split into two cases.

When
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{R}}=0
, denote
\mathcal{A}
to be the algebra generated by the centralizer and the commutant of the ambient algebra. The novelty of this approach is to identify a suitable subspace inside the GNS space of the centralizer which possesses ‘sufficient mixing’
(
the associated subspace in
[
BM
,
R05
]
was the GNS space of a MASA
)
, and use an appropriate orthonormal basis of that subspace to track a vector
\xi
, so that the cyclic subspace
\mathcal{A}\xi
fully captures the ‘size’ of the relative commutant of the centralizer. However, there is a payoff. Since we are working with a ‘mixing subspace’, we lose algebraic techniques (most importantly cannot locate unitaries) and depend on norm estimates of operators involving creation and annihilation operators. This forces us a bargain with the characteristic parameter that defines the two-dimensional representation, but our results remain valid for all
q\neq 0
.

When
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{R}}\neq 0
, a similar idea with slight modification works. This time, the increment in dimension allows us to choose unitaries from an orthogonal subalgebra and frees us from any bargain with the characteristic parameter as was in the previous case, and the conclusions are no longer subject to any constraints.

The key to the factoriality is Lemma
3.12
in which ‘some mixing’ is analyzed to control the relative commutant of the centralizer. The main results of this paper are summarized as follows:

Theorem
: Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{R}}
, where
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{R}}
is a real Hilbert space
(
could be
0)
, and let
\mathbb{R}^{2}
be reducing subspace for
(U_{t})
with associated sub representation being ergodic. Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
be the parameter that defines
(U_{t})
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then, the following holds:

(1)

if
dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})=2
and
\lambda
is small (depending on
q
), then the centralizer of the
q
-quasi free
(
vacuum
)
state is irreducible if
q\neq 0
;

(2)

if
dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})\geqslant 3
, the
q
-Araki-Woods algebras are factors for all
-1<q<1
;

(3)

if the almost periodic part of
(U_{t})
is sufficiently large, then the centralizer of the
q
-quasi free state is irreducible for all
-1<q<1
.

This paper is organized as follows. In §
2
, we lay out all the technical prerequisites that are needed to address the problem. The technical lemmas that will be used to deal with factoriality is divided into two groups under §
3
; the case when
dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})=2
and
(U_{t})
is ergodic appears in §
3.1
and that for all cases
(dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})\geqslant 2)
appears in §
3.2
. The main theorems on factoriality appear in §
4
. Irreducibility of the centralizer is discussed in §
5
.

Preliminaries

]

2.1.
Hiai’s Construction

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
be a real Hilbert space with
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})\geqslant 2
and let
(U_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be a strongly continuous orthogonal representation of
\mathbb{R}
on

\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
denote the
complexification of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Denote the inner product and norm on

\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}
and

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}
respectively. Identify
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
in

\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
by
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes 1
. Thus,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}+i\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
, and as a real Hilbert space the inner product of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
is given by
\Re\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}
. Consider the bounded anti-linear
(
complex
(
left
)
conjugation
)
operator
\mathcal{J}:\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
given by
\mathcal{J}(\xi+i\eta)=\xi-i\eta
,
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
, and note that
\mathcal{J}\xi=\xi

for
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}=\overline{\langle\eta,\xi\rangle}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}=\langle\eta,\mathcal{J}\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}},\text{ for all }\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}},\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}.

By abuse of notation we denote the linear extension of
(U_{t})
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
by the same notation, which is again a strongly continuous one-parameter group of unitaries in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
A
denote the analytic generator of
(U_{t})
. Then
A
is positive, nonsingular and self-adjoint. Note that
\mathcal{J}A=A^{-1}\mathcal{J}
.

Introduce a new inner product on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
by
\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{U}=\langle\frac{2}{1+A^{-1}}\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}
,
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
, and let
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{U}
denote the associated norm on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the complex Hilbert space obtained by completing
(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{U})
. The inner product and norm of
\mathcal{H}
will respectively be denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{U}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{U}
as well. Then,
(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}})\ni\xi\overset{\imath}{\mapsto}\xi\in(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{U})\subseteq(\mathcal{H},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{U})
, is an isometric embedding of the real Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
in
\mathcal{H}

(
in the sense of
[
S97
]
)
. With abuse of notation, we will identify
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
with its image
\iota(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
. Then,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\cap i\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}=\{0\}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}+i\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}

(
see pp. 332
[
S97
]
)
.

Note that
(U_{t})
extends to a strongly continuous unitary representation
(\widetilde{U}_{t})
of
\mathbb{R}
on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\widetilde{A}
be the analytic generator associated to
(\widetilde{U}_{t})
, which is obviously an extension of
A
. Any eigenvector of
\widetilde{A}
is an eigenvector of
A
corresponding to the same eigenvalue
[
BM
, Prop. 2.1]
.
Since the spectral data of
A
and
\widetilde{A}

(
and hence of
(U_{t})
and
(\widetilde{U}_{t}))
are essentially the same
(
see
[
BM
, §2]
for details
)
, and
\widetilde{U}_{t},\widetilde{A}
are respectively extensions of
U_{t},A
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, so we would now write
\widetilde{A}=A
and
\widetilde{U}_{t}=U_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

For
q\in(-1,1)
and for the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, consider the associated

q
-Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
introduced in
[
BS91
]
.
\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
is constructed as follows. Let
\Omega
be a distinguished unit vector in
\mathbb{C}
usually referred to as the vacuum vector. Denote
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 0}=\mathbb{C}\Omega
, and, for
n\geqslant 1
, let
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}=\text{ span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n}:\xi_{i}\in\mathcal{H}\text{ for }1\leqslant i\leqslant n\}
denote the algebraic tensor products. Let
\mathcal{F}_{fin}(\mathcal{H})=\text{ span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}:n\geqslant 0\}
. For
n,m\geqslant 0
and
f=\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
,
g=\zeta_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\zeta_{m}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m}
, the association

(1)

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{q}=\delta_{m,n}\sum_{\pi\in S_{n}}q^{i(\pi)}\langle\xi_{1},\zeta_{\pi(1)}\rangle_{U}\cdots\langle\xi_{n},\zeta_{\pi(n)}\rangle_{U},

where
i(\pi)
denotes the number of inversions of the permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
, defines a positive definite sesquilinear form on
\mathcal{F}_{fin}(\mathcal{H})
and the
q
-Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
is the completion of
\mathcal{F}_{fin}(\mathcal{H})
with respect to the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{q}
induced by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{q}
. Denote
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{q}n}:=\overline{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{q}}
,
n\geqslant 0
. Note that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{q}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{U}
on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{q}1}=\mathcal{H}
.

For
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
, the left
q
-creation and
q
-annihilation operators on
\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
are respectively defined by:

(2)

\displaystyle c_{q}(\xi)\Omega=\xi,

\displaystyle c_{q}(\xi)(\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n})=\xi\otimes\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n},

\displaystyle\text{and},

\displaystyle c_{q}(\xi)^{*}\Omega=0,

\displaystyle c_{q}(\xi)^{*}(\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{q^{i-1}}\langle\xi,\xi_{i}\rangle_{U}\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{i-1}\otimes\xi_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n},

where
\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{q}n}
for
n\geqslant 1
.
The operators
c_{q}(\xi)
and
c_{q}(\xi)^{*}
are bounded on
\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
and they are
adjoints of each other. Moreover,

(3)

\left\|c_{q}(\xi)\right\|=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-q}}\left\|\xi\right\|_{U},\quad&\text{ if }0\leqslant q<1;\\
\left\|\xi\right\|_{U},\quad&\text{ if }-1<q\leqslant 0.\end{cases}

Moreover, they satisfy the following
q
-commutation relation:

(4)

\displaystyle c_{q}(\xi)^{*}c_{q}(\zeta)-qc_{q}(\zeta)c_{q}(\xi)^{*}=\langle\xi,\zeta\rangle_{U}1,\text{ for all }\xi,\zeta\in\mathcal{H}.

.

,

\displaystyle c_{q}

\displaystyle(\xi)^{*}\bigg((\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n})\otimes(\eta_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta_{m})\bigg)

\displaystyle=\bigg(c_{q}(\xi)^{*}(\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n})\bigg)\otimes(\eta_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta_{m})

\displaystyle\indent\indent\indent\indent\indent+q^{n}(\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n})\otimes\bigg(c_{q}(\xi)^{*}(\eta_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta_{m})\bigg).

Following

Hi03
,

]
, consider the

-algebra

\Gamma_{q}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{t}):=C^{*}\{s_{q}(\xi):\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\}
and the von Neumann algebra

\Gamma_{q}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{t})^{\prime\prime}
, where

\displaystyle s_{q}(\xi)=c_{q}(\xi)+c_{q}(\xi)^{*},\text{ }\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}.

\Gamma_{q}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{t})^{\prime\prime}
is known as the

-
deformed Araki-Woods von Neumann algebra

2.2.
Modular Theory

Most of what follows in §
2.2
and §
2.3
is taken from
[
S97
,
Hi03
]
. We need to have a convenient description of the commutant and centralizer of
\Gamma_{q}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{t})^{\prime\prime}

(
which has been recorded in the case

in
[
S97
]
and a similar collection of operators in the commutant has been identified in
[
Hi03
]
)
. Thus, we need to record some facts related to the modular theory of the
q
-quasi free state
\varphi_{q,U}
. Let
J_{\varphi_{q,U}}
and
\Delta_{\varphi_{q,U}}
respectively denote the modular conjugation and modular operator associated to
\varphi_{q,U}
and let
S_{\varphi_{q,U}}=J_{\varphi_{q,U}}\Delta_{\varphi_{q,U}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(5)

\displaystyle J_{\varphi_{q,U}}(\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n})=A^{-1/2}\xi_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes A^{-1/2}\xi_{1},\text{ }\forall\text{ }\xi_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\cap\mathfrak{D}(A^{-{\frac{1}{2}}});

\displaystyle\Delta_{\varphi_{q,U}}(\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n})=A^{-1}\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes A^{-1}\xi_{n},\text{ }\forall\text{ }\xi_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\cap\mathfrak{D}(A^{-1});

\displaystyle S_{\varphi_{q,U}}(\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n})=\xi_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{1},\text{ }\forall\text{ }\xi_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}.

The modular automorphism group
(\sigma_{t}^{\varphi_{q,U}})
of
\varphi_{q,U}
is given by
\sigma_{-t}^{\varphi_{q,U}}=\text{Ad}(\mathcal{F}(U_{t}))
, where
\mathcal{F}(U_{t})=id\oplus\oplus_{n\geqslant 1}U_{t}^{\otimes_{q}n}
, for all

. In particular,

(6)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\varphi_{q,U}}_{-t}(s_{q}(\xi))=s_{q}(U_{t}\xi),\text{ for all }\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}.

To reduce notation, the complex
(
left
)
conjugation
\mathcal{J}
associated to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\subset\mathcal{H}
will be denoted
by
\overline{\xi+i\eta}=\xi-i\eta

\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
. It corresponds with

.

2.3.
Commutant

Now we proceed to describe the commutant of
\Gamma_{q}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{t})^{\prime\prime}
. Consider the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}=\{\xi\in\mathcal{H}:\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{U}\in\mathbb{R}\text{ for all }\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\}.

Then
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
is a real subspace. Note that
\overline{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}+i\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}}=\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}\cap i\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}=\{0\}
. It is easy to check that
A^{-1/2}\zeta\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
for all
\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}(A^{-{\frac{1}{2}}})\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Now for
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
, define the right creation operator
c_{q,r}(\xi)
on
\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
by

(7)

\displaystyle c_{q,r}(\xi)\Omega=\xi,

\displaystyle c_{q,r}(\xi)(\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n})=\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n}\otimes\xi,\text{ }\xi_{i}\in\mathcal{H},n\geqslant 1.

Clearly,
c_{q,r}(\xi)=\jmath c_{q}(\xi){\jmath}^{*}
, where
\jmath:\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})

(1-q^{2m+1})(1-q^{2m+2})\geqslant 1

Now, consider the following operator

Also note that, for each fixed

Lemma 3.6
.

The sequence
S_{n}
converges in norm to

\displaystyle{S_{\infty}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}(1-q)^{k}\lambda^{k/2}c_{q}(e)^{*k}Tc_{q}(\overline{e})^{k*}}.

Moreover, if
q>0
and
\lambda<(1+\frac{C_{q}}{d_{\infty}})^{-2}
or if
q<0
and
\lambda<(1+\frac{C_{q}^{2}D(q)(1-q)^{2}}{d_{\infty}})^{-2}
, the operator

is invertible.

Proof.

Let
B(q)=\sqrt{C_{q}D(q)}
. First note that the series defining
S_{\infty}
converges absolutely. Indeed by Lemma
3.3

(i)
, we have

\displaystyle\|(1-q)^{k/2}c_{q}(e)^{k}\|\leqslant\lambda^{-k/4},\quad\|(1-q)^{k/2}c_{q}(\overline{e})^{k}\|\leqslant\lambda^{k/4},\quad\text{ when }0\leqslant q<1;

\displaystyle\|(1-q)^{k/2}c_{q}(e)^{k}\|\leqslant B(q)\lambda^{-k/4},\,\,\|(1-q)^{k/2}c_{q}(\overline{e})^{k}\|\leqslant B(q)\lambda^{k/4},\text{ when }-1<q<0;

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|c_{k}(1-q)^{k}\lambda^{k/2}c_{q}(e)^{*k}Tc_{q}(\overline{e})^{k*}\|\leqslant\begin{cases}C_{q}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\lambda}^{k/2}<\infty,&\text{ if }q\geqslant 0;\\
C_{q}^{2}D(q)(1-q)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\lambda}^{k/2}<\infty,&\text{ if }q<0.\end{cases}

We note the following:

\displaystyle S_{n}

\displaystyle=(1-q)^{n}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}}{c_{q}(e)}^{*n}W(e^{n})

\displaystyle=(1-q)^{n}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}}{c_{q}(e)}^{*n}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(\begin{matrix}n\\

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(\begin{matrix}n\\

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right)_{q}(1-q)^{k}\lambda^{\frac{k}{2}}{c_{q}(e)}^{*k}T_{n-k}c_{q}(\bar{e})^{*k}\quad(\text{Lemma \ref{Tn}}).

We have just seen in Lemma

that

is norm convergent to

(and bounded by

if

1-q

). Thus, the general term in

converges to the general term of
S_{\infty}
in norm and one concludes the convergence in the statement with the dominated convergence theorem as
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{k/2}<\infty
.

We also have
S_{\infty}=T(1+T^{-1}V)

(
use Lemma
3.4
)
, where

\|V\|\leqslant\begin{cases}C_{q}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\lambda}^{k/2},\quad&\text{ if }q>0;\\
C_{q}^{2}D(q)(1-q)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\lambda}^{k/2},\quad&\text{ if }q<0.\end{cases}

The rest follows by ensuring that

. We know that

if

and

. Thus,

is invertible if

Lemma 3.9
.

The sequence

Proof.

We rely on Lemma

\displaystyle\|(1-q)^{n}W({\bar{e}}^{n}e^{n})\|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=0}^{n}C_{q}^{4}|q|^{(n-k)l}\sqrt{d_{l}d_{n-l}d_{k}d_{n-k}}\lambda^{(k-l)/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=0}^{n}C_{q}^{6}|q|^{(n-k)l}\lambda^{(k-l)/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{q}^{6}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\lambda^{j/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant k,l\leqslant n\\
k-l=j\end{subarray}}|q|^{(n-k)l}.

If we denote by

\displaystyle\|(1-q)^{n}W({\bar{e}}^{n}e^{n})\|

This allows to conclude as

Lemma 3.11

\displaystyle(1-q)^{n}c_{q}(\eta_{t})^{*}\bar{e}^{n+a_{f}}e^{n+\alpha_{f}}\chi_{f}=q^{2n+a_{f}+\alpha_{f}}(1-q)^{n}\bar{e}^{n+a_{f}}e^{n+\alpha_{f}}c_{q}(\eta_{t})^{*}\chi_{f}\rightarrow 0.

The next lemma is the key to factoriality and irreducible centralizers.

Lemma 3.12

The

The next lemma will be used particularly when

Lemma 3.13
.

Suppose
\text{dim}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})\geqslant 3
with
(U_{t})
as in the set up. Let

\eta\perp\bar{e},e

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{q}
. Further, let
\{u_{n}\}

\xi=d_{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}^{2}(1-q)^{k}\lambda^{k/2}{\bar{e}}^{k}e^{k}

Proof.

Firstly, note that

is a diffuse abelian von Neumann algebra
[
BM
, §4]
. Thus, the desired sequence
\{u_{n}\}
exists as in the statement.

By Lemma
3.12
, the series defining
\xi

\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{q}
.
Further,
\{(1-q)^{k}W(\bar{e}^{k}e^{k})\}

is convergent in norm as

. Using Thm.

, it follows that

\displaystyle W(\xi)=d_{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}^{2}(1-q)^{k}\lambda^{k/2}W(\bar{e}^{k}e^{k})\in M_{q}^{\varphi}.

Therefore, it is sufficient to show that

converges to

in the

as

for each

. Further, the boundedness of the sequence entails that it is sufficient to verify
the convergence with vectors of the form

and

When

[

[

As

4.
Factoriality

This is the main section of this paper. Here we establish the factoriality of
M_{q}
.
The set up of §
3.2
will be in force in this section
. As informed earlier, we will have to compromise with the parameter
\lambda
to assert the factoriality of
M_{q}
when
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})=2
. In this case though, we will directly deduce that
M_{q}^{\varphi}
is irreducible. When
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})\geqslant 3
,
M_{q}
is a factor
regardless
of the value of the parameter
\lambda
defining
(U_{t})
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and

is irreducible will not be direct and will be constrained. When

,

is the free Araki-Woods factor
[

]
and there is nothing to prove in this case.

As an outcome of the results in §
3
, we have the following:

Lemma 4.1
.

With
\xi
as in Lemma
3.12
, let

be the orthogonal projection onto
[M_{q}^{\varphi}M_{q}^{\prime}\xi]^{\perp}
. Then,

is trivial if and only if

. In particular,

and
M_{q}
are factors if and only if

Proof.

It is clear that
P\in\mathcal{(}M_{q}^{\varphi})^{\prime}\cap M_{q}
. Suppose that
(M_{q}^{\varphi})^{\prime}\cap M_{q}=\mathbb{C}1
. Then,
P=0
as
\xi\neq 0
and
M_{q}^{\varphi}\text{ and }M_{q}
are factors.

Conversely, by Lemma
3.12
it follows that
T_{\xi}\in M_{q}^{\varphi}\vee M_{q}^{\prime}
. Let

so that

. In particular,

. Then, for any

and

.
If
P=0
, this means that
x(\eta)=\lambda\eta
for all
\eta\in\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
and thus

.

\Box

Now we are in position to state the first theorem on factoriality.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

. If

is ergodic then

(1+\frac{(C_{q}(1-q))^{2}D(q)}{d_{\infty}})^{-2},\quad&\text{if }q<0.\end{cases}

Consider

. Then, we have

and Eq. (

\displaystyle 0=\langle P(\xi),\,\Omega\rangle_{q}

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\lambda^{n/2}(1-q)^{n}\langle PW(e^{n})^{*}W(e^{n})\Omega,\,\Omega\rangle_{q}

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\lambda^{n/2}(1-q)^{n}\langle W(e^{n})^{*}W(e^{n})P\Omega,\,P\Omega\rangle_{q}

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\lambda^{n/2}(1-q)^{n}\|W(e^{n})P(\Omega)\|_{q}^{2}.

Thus,

.

\Box

Now, we assume that
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{R}}

c>0
is a scalar. It follows that
[M_{q}^{\varphi}M_{q}^{\prime}\xi]=\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
. Thanks to Lemma
4.1
, the argument is complete.

Now it remains to establish the claim. The proof of the claim is identical for both
M_{q}^{(2)}
and
M_{q}
. So we prove it for
M_{q}
and work with the sub representation
(\mathbb{R}_{1}^{2},{U_{t}}_{\upharpoonleft\mathbb{R}_{1}^{2}})
as in §
3.2
.

Recall that a factor
M
equipped with a faithful normal state
\psi
is of type
\rm{I}
if and only if,
M\ni x\mapsto\Delta_{\psi}^{\frac{1}{4}}x\Omega_{\psi}\in L^{2}(M,\psi)
is a compact embedding
(
see
[
BDL
, Cor. 2.9]
or
[
DM
, Thm. 7.13]
)
.
Note that
\{(1-q)^{n}W({\bar{e}}^{n}e^{n})\}_{n\geqslant 1}\subseteq M_{q}^{\varphi}
is bounded from Lemma
3.9
. However,
\Delta^{\frac{1}{4}}(1-q)^{n}W({\bar{e}}^{n}e^{n})\Omega=(1-q)^{n}{\bar{e}}^{n}e^{n}
, and this sequence has no converging subsequence in
\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
. Therefore, the symmetric embedding of
M_{q}
in
\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H})
is not compact proving the claim.

\Box

Remark 5.2
.

(1)

If
dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{ap})=4
and
(U_{t})
is ergodic, then upon assuming that the parameter
\lambda\in(0,1)
which defines a two-dimensional sub representation is small in the sense of Thm.
4.2
, one can still conclude that
(M_{q}^{\varphi})^{\prime}\cap M_{q}=\mathbb{C}1

(
when
q\neq 0)
.

(2)

In all cases where
(M_{q}^{\varphi})^{\prime}\cap M_{q}=\mathbb{C}1
, the
S
-invariant of Connes can be directly deduced from the modular theory of the vacuum state and is exactly as in
[
BM
, Thm. 8.2]
.

(3)

Note that the last part of the proof of Thm.
5.1
along with Thm. 5.2 of
[
DM2
]
says that if
\mathcal{H}^{ap}\neq 0
, then
M_{q}^{\varphi}
cannot be discrete
(
direct sum of matrix algebras
)
.

(4)

Suppose that
dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})=4
and
(U_{t})
is ergodic. In this case if
\zeta\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
be such that
\left\|\zeta\right\|_{q}=1
, then
M_{\zeta}=vN(s_{q}(\zeta))\subseteq M_{q}
is a quasi-split inclusion
(
see
[
BM2
, Thm. 4.6]
)
. Since
M_{q}
is a factor
(
Thm.
4.3
)
,
M_{\zeta}\otimes 1\subseteq M_{q}\otimes\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{K})
is a split inclusion
(
see
[
BM2
, Thm. 3.8]
)
, where
\dim(\mathcal{K})=\aleph_{0}
. It follows that there is no normal conditional expectation from
M_{q}
onto
M_{\zeta}
. This forces that
M_{q}
cannot be a
\rm{II}_{1}
factor, for if it were there would have been a normal conditional expectation from
M_{q}
onto
M_{\zeta}
preserving the canonical trace.

In view of Rem.
5.2
, we are still not in position to compute the
S
-invariant when
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})=4
and
(U_{t})
is ergodic. Therefore, we conclude this section by proving that
M_{q}
is never semifinite.

Theorem 5.3
.

M_{q}
is not semifinite. In particular, if
dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})=4
then
M_{q}
is a type
\rm{III}
factor.

Proof.

If
(U_{t})
has a fixed (non-zero) vector or a non-trivial weakly mixing component, the statement follows from
[
BM
]
.
Therefore, we assume that
(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{t})
is as in the set up of §
3.1
.

We claim that
(\sigma_{t}^{\varphi})
cannot be inner. Suppose there exists a one-parameter group of unitaries
(u_{t})\subseteq M_{q}^{\varphi}
such that
\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)=u_{t}xu_{t}^{*}
for all
x\in M_{q}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then, by Lemma
3.12
, we have
u_{t}T_{\xi}=T_{\xi}u_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, there exists scalars
z_{t}
with
\left|z_{t}\right|=1
such that
u_{t}\xi=z_{t}\xi
for all
t
. Replacing
u_{t}
by
\overline{z_{t}}u_{t}
, we may assume that
z_{t}=1
for all
t
. Then,
u_{t}W(\xi)=W(\xi)
for all
t
.

Let
\mathcal{K}=Ker(W(\xi))
and
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
. Then,
{u_{t}}_{\upharpoonleft\mathcal{L}}=1_{\mathcal{L}}
. Put
w_{n}=\left|W\big((\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}(1-q)^{\frac{1}{2}}e)^{n}\big)\right|^{2}
. By the proof of Thm.
3.12
, we have

\displaystyle W(\xi)=w.o.t.\text{-}\lim_{n}w_{n}.

Hence,
\mathcal{K}=\{\zeta\in\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H}):w_{n}\zeta\overset{w}{\rightarrow}0\}
and it follows that
W(e)\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{K}

(
use Lemma
3.2
)
. Thus, decomposing
\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{F}_{q}(\mathcal{H}))
with respect
\mathcal{K}\oplus\mathcal{L}
, one has the form

\displaystyle W(e)=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\
0&w\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }u_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{t}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\forall\,t.

Since
u_{t}W(e)u_{t}^{*}=\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(W(e))=e^{it\log\lambda}W(e)
for all
t

(
use Eq. (
5
)
)
, so choosing
t
such that
t\log\lambda\not\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
it follows that
w=0
. Thus,

Ran(W(e))\subseteq\mathcal{K}
.

This is false as
e\not\in\mathcal{K}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle W(\xi)

\displaystyle=w.o.t.\text{-}\lim_{n}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}}(1-q)^{n}W(\bar{e})^{n}W(e)^{n}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-q)W(\bar{e})W(\xi)W(e).

Consequently,
\xi=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-q)W(\bar{e})W(\xi)e\neq 0
. This completes the proof.

\Box

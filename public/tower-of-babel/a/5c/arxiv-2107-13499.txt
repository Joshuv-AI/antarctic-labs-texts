Boundary slopes for the Markov ordering on relatively prime pairs
Jonah Gaster

Abstract
. Following McShane, we employ the stable norm on the homology of the modular torus to investigate the Markov ordering on the set of relatively prime integer pairs ( q , p ) (q,p) with q ≥ p ≥ 0 q\geq p\geq 0 . Our main theorem is a characterization of slopes along which the Markov ordering is monotone with respect to q q , confirming conjectures of Lee-Li-Rabideau-Schiffler that refine conjectures of Aigner. The main tool is an explicit computation of the slopes at the corners of the stable norm ball for the modular torus.



1.
Introduction

The set of
Markov numbers

\mathcal{M}=\{1,2,5,\ldots\}
is the set of positive integers that appear in a Markov triple, i.e. a solution
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
to the cubic

(1.1)

x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=3xyz\penalty\ .

The
Markov Uniquness Conjecture
(MUC), apparently due to Frobenius, asserts that each positive Markov triple is determined by its largest entry
[
Aig15
,
CF89
,
Fro13
,
Mar79
]
.

The Markov numbers admit an interpretation as a system of labels on the set
\mathcal{H}
of horoball regions in the complement of a rooted planar trivalent tree.
That is, there is a surjective map
\lambda_{\mathcal{M}}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{M}
, whose injectivity is equivalent to MUC
[
Aig15
, p. 54]
.
There is another well-known natural parametrization of
\mathcal{H}
via Farey fractions
\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}:\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]
, so one may obtain the Markov numbers as
m_{p/q}=\lambda_{\mathcal{M}}\circ\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(p/q)
. See
Figure 3
and
Figure 3
for an illustration of
\lambda_{\mathcal{M}}
and
\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}
.

It will be convenient to record this map as follows: let
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
indicate the set of relatively prime pairs
(q,p)
with
q\geq p\geq 0
.
The map
(q,p)\mapsto m_{p/q}
records the Markov label associated to the horoball with label
p/q
. MUC predicts that this map is in fact a bijection; because
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{N}
, one would obtain a somewhat mysterious ordering of
\mathcal{Q}
.
We will refer to this ordering as the ‘Markov ordering’, denoted
\prec_{\mathcal{M}}
. (For the careful reader who prefers not to assume MUC,
\prec_{\mathcal{M}}
is a priori only a strict partial order, with potentially nontrivial mutually incomparable subsets for horoballs with common Markov labels.)
See
Figure 3
for an illustration.

Initiating a study of
\prec_{\mathcal{M}}
, Aigner offered some conjectural statements suggested by numerical experiment
[
Aig15
]
: assuming the following pairs are relatively prime,

(i)

(q,p)\prec_{\mathcal{M}}(q^{\prime},p)
when
q^{\prime}>q
,

(ii)

(q,p)\prec_{\mathcal{M}}(q,p^{\prime})
when
p^{\prime}>p
, and

(iii)

(q,p)\prec_{\mathcal{M}}(q^{\prime},p^{\prime})
when
q^{\prime}>q
and
p+q=p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}
.

These three statements were subsequently proven by several authors using the theory of cluster algebras and continued fractions
[
Rab18
,
RS20
,
LLRS20
,
LPTV21
]
.
Shortly thereafter, Greg McShane gave a unified proof of Aigner’s conjectures
[
McS21
]
by exploiting hyperbolic geometry and the so-called
stable norm
, a norm on the homology of the modular torus induced by the hyperbolic length function
[
MR95a
]
.

There is a well-known geometric interpretation of

Recently, McShane has shown how to use this viewpoint to prove Aigner’s conjectures, relying on a certain convexity induced by the hyperbolic length function.
The stable norm

Our computation of the extremal slopes above is convenient when phrased in terms of Fock’s function
\Psi
.
Vladimir Fock introduced the function
\Psi:[0,1/2]\to\mathbb{R}
whose value at rationals is given by

(1.2)

\Psi(p/q)=\frac{\cosh^{-1}\left(\frac{3}{2}m_{T\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)}\right)}{q}\penalty\ ,

where
T\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)=\frac{p}{q-p}
.
Suitably interpreted,
\Psi(p/q)
is the ratio of (one-half of) the hyperbolic length to an ‘arithmetic height’ for a simple closed geodesic corresponding to the homology class
T(q,p)=(q-p,p)
.
Fock showed that
\Psi
is a continuous, convex, and decreasing function
[
Foc97
]
.

2.
Hyperbolic geometry of Markov triples on the modular torus

The modular torus
X
(also called the
equianharmonic torus
in the literature) can be obtained as the quotient of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
by the commutator subgroup of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
. The holonomy group of
X
admits a lift to
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
(in fact, such a lift exists in much greater generality
[
Cul86
]
), so one may speak of the ‘trace of a simple closed geodesic on
X
’.
For punctured tori, any such choice of lift will give rise to a representation sending the cusp to an element of trace
-2
, from which one can deduce using the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-trace identity that triples of simple closed curves pairwise intersecting once have traces that satisfy a Markov-like equation.
A lift exists so that all simple closed curves have positive integral traces, and one finds that the set of traces of simple closed geodesics on
X
, in the chosen lift, will be precisely
3\mathcal{M}
. See
[
Gol03
]
for details.
We indicate the trace of the simple closed geodesic
\gamma
on
X
by
3m_{\gamma}
, for
m_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{M}
.

In order to compute derivatives for
\Psi
, we recall some aspects of simple closed geodesics on
X
.
The following is a topological exercise.
See
[
McS04
]
for detail.

2.1
.

Any collection of three simple closed geodesics pairwise intersecting once on
X
have
two complementary triangles, exchanged by the elliptic involution, whose vertices are the three Weierstrass points of
X
and whose side lengths are the half-lengths of the three geodesics.

In order to compute derivatives of Fock’s function, it will be important to keep track of the behavior of hyperbolic lengths under Dehn twisting. We indicate the mapping class obtained via the right Dehn twist about the simple closed curve
\gamma
by
\tau_{\gamma}
, and we indicate the half-length of
\gamma
on
X
by
\ell(\gamma)
.

2.2
.

Suppose that
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\gamma
are a trio of simple closed geodesics on
X
that pairwise intersect once,
and suppose that a triangular complementary component sees
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
in counterclockwise order, as in
Figure 5
. Then the half-length function
\ell
satisfies

(2.1)

\displaystyle\ell\left(\tau_{\gamma}^{k}(\beta)\right)

\displaystyle=(k+1)\ell(\gamma)+\log\left(\frac{3}{2}m_{\alpha}-\frac{9m_{\gamma}m_{\alpha}-6m_{\beta}}{2\sqrt{9m_{\gamma}^{2}-4}}\right)+O\left(e^{-2k\ell(\gamma)}\right)

as

. (The implied constant in the last term depends on
\alpha
,
\beta

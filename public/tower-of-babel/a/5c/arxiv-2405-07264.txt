Information Rates Over Multi-View Channels
V. Arvind Rameshwar and Nir Weinberger

Abstract
We investigate the fundamental limits of reliable communication over multi-view channels, in which the channel output is comprised of a large number of independent noisy views of a transmitted symbol. We consider first the setting of multi-view discrete memoryless channels and then extend our results to general multi-view channels (using multi-letter formulas). We argue that the channel capacity and dispersion of such multi-view channels converge exponentially fast in the number of views to the entropy and varentropy of the input distribution, respectively. We identify the exact rate of convergence as the smallest Chernoff information between two conditional distributions of the output, conditioned on unequal inputs. For the special case of the deletion channel, we compute upper bounds on this Chernoff information. Finally, we present a new channel model we term the Poisson approximation channel — of possible independent interest — whose capacity closely approximates the capacity of the multi-view binary symmetric channel for any fixed number of views.



I
Introduction

Consider a communication setting, in which the decoder receives multiple, potentially noisy, views of a single transmitted sequence, which is sent via independent transmissions through a stochastic noise channel. Clearly, multiple noisy views (or multiple draws) of a transmitted sequence can only improve the error probabilities of decoding, as compared to decoding a single received sequence. Viewed differently, it is also clear that the Shannon capacity of the
multi-view
channel, as described above, is at least as large as the capacity of the standard single-view channel.

In this paper, we consider first the setting where the noisy channel is a discrete memoryless channel (DMC) and then extend our results to general multi-letter channels. Multi-view DMCs were first studied in the classic work by Levenshtein
[
1
]
on sequence reconstruction. Among several other results,
[
1
]
presented bounds on the probability of error under ML decoding and characterized the number of noisy views required to guarantee recovery of the transmitted sequence, with
decaying error probability
. These results are determined by the minimum Chernoff information between conditional distributions of outputs of the DMC, conditioned on unequal inputs. Levenshtein’s result on the number of noisy views required for reconstruction, however, depends crucially on the assumption that the error probability decays with the blocklength, and leaves open the question of characterizing the rates achievable over DMCs with fixed error probability, using finitely many channel uses. Furthermore, the proof strategy in
[
1
]
resists extension to channels, such as those that model synchronization errors, wherein the lengths of the input and output sequences could be different.

In this work, we characterize exactly the rate of convergence of the information rate and the channel dispersion of general multi-view channels, under arbitrary input distributions, to the input entropy and the varentropy, respectively, when the number of views is large. As our main result, we show that the rates of (exponential) convergence are again determined by the minimum Chernoff information between conditional distributions, as above. This result does not assume any additional structure on the channel such as symmetry, or binary alphabets (though require the input and output alphabet to be finite)

Evidently, in this DNA-storage model, each molecule is possibly read many times, and so the multi-view mutual information between the molecule sequence and its multiple output reads plays a pivotal role in determining the capacity of this storage system. Indeed, it was shown in

Previous works considered the mutual information for a fixed number of views. In this non-asymptotic setting, the capacity of a multi-view binary symmetric channel (BSC) was identified and an explicit expression was provided in

As an additional contribution, we also explore the non-asymptotic capacity of the so-called

Such a technique also allows us to obtain simple bounds on the non-asymptotic capacities of general binary-input memoryless symmetric (BIMS) channels.

III
Main Results for DMCs

We now formalize the problem for the case the noisy channel is a DMC, and present our main results. Later on, in Section
V
we will extend Theorem
III.1
here to general multi-letter channels. Consider a DMC
W
with finite input and output alphabets
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, respectively, specified by the channel law
\{P_{Y|X}(y|x)=W(y|x):\ x\in\mathcal{X},y\in\mathcal{Y}\}
. Let
X\sim P_{X}
be the input to the channel. Our setting of interest is when
d\geq 1
noisy outputs are obtained by independently passing the input through the DMC. Let these outputs be denoted by
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}
; their conditional distribution obeys

P(Y^{d}=y^{d}\mid X=x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}W(y_{i}|x).

(5)

We call a DMC with a channel law as above as the
d
-view
DMC
W^{(d)}
. We assume further that
|\mathcal{X}|
and
|\mathcal{Y}|
do not depend on
d
.

Now, given any DMC
W
, we denote its capacity by
[
18
]

C(W)=\max_{P_{X}}I(X;Y)=\max_{P_{X}}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{W(Y|X)}{\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P(x)W(Y|x)}\right].

(6)

Hence, the capacity of
W^{(d)}
is given by
C^{(d)}:=\max_{P_{X}}I^{(d)},
where
I^{(d)}:=I(X;Y^{d})
. Further, for any input distribution
P_{X}
, the channel dispersion of the
d
-view DMC
W
is given by

V^{(d)}:=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\iota(X;Y^{d})-I(X;Y^{d})\right)^{2}\right],

(7)

where

\iota(x;y^{d}):=\log\frac{P(y^{d}|x)}{P(y^{d})}

(8)

is the
information density
.
We are interested in characterizing the behaviour of
C^{(d)}
(or more generally, of
I^{(d)}
) and
V^{(d)}
when
d
is large. From operational considerations, we can argue that
I^{(d)}
and
V^{(d)}
are non-decreasing in
d
. More formally, for
I^{(d)}
, the data processing inequality
[
17
, Thm. 2.8.1]
guarantees that
I^{(d)}
is non-decreasing in
d
. Furthermore, it is reasonable to expect that
I^{(d)}
and
V^{(d)}
converge to
\mathsf{H}(X)
and
\mathsf{V}(X)
, respectively, since for very large

, the “uncertainty” introduced by the channel is expected to drop to

. In this work, we confirm these convergence results by showing that the
I^{(d)}
and
V^{(d)}
in fact converge to their limiting values exponentially quickly in
d
, and explicitly identify the rate of convergence.

To build some intuition why the convergence is exponentially fast in
d
, at least for
I^{(d)}
, consider the simple example of the
d
-view BSC
(p)
, which we denote by BSC
{}^{(d)}(p)
(here,
p<1/2
is the crossover probability of the BSC), with a uniform input distribution. At the decoder end, we construct a simple “data processor” that takes the (random) outputs
Y^{d}\in\{0,1\}^{d}
and computes their majority
M\in\{0,1\}
. Intuitively, the more the number of noisy output sequences (or views) that are available to the decoder, the better the estimate that results from majority decoding. More precisely, we have

\displaystyle I^{(d)}

\displaystyle=I(X;Y^{d})

(9)

\displaystyle\geq I(X;M)=\log 2-h_{b}(\Pr[X\neq M]),

(10)

where the inequality holds by the data processing inequality and the last equality holds by symmetry. Now, observe that since
p<1/2
, we have by the Chernoff bound (see
[
19
, Example 1.6.2]
) that

\displaystyle\Pr[X\neq M]

\displaystyle\leq\text{exp}(-d\cdot D(\text{Ber}(1/2)||\text{Ber}(p)))

(11)

\displaystyle=\text{exp}(-d\cdot Z(p)),

(12)

where
Z(p)=2\sqrt{p(1-p)}
is the Bhattacharya parameter for the BSC
(p)
. Therefore, for large enough

h_{b}(\Pr[X\neq M])\leq-2\Pr[X\neq M]\cdot\log\Pr[X\neq M].

Hence, for sufficiently large

to

Our main theorem is given below.

Theorem III.1
.

We have that

I^{(d)}=\mathsf{H}(X)-\textnormal{exp}\left({-d\rho+\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\right),\

(14)

and

\left\lvert V^{(d)}-\mathsf{V}(X)\right\rvert=\textnormal{exp}\left({-d\rho+\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\right),

\rho=\min_{x,x^{\prime}:x\neq x^{\prime}}\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|x^{\prime}}).

(16)

Note that the theorem above captures the dependence of the speed of convergence of mutual information and dispersion to their limits in terms of both the number of views

IV
Proof Sketch of Main Results

In this section, we provide a sketch of our strategy for proving Theorem
III.1
for a DMC
W
with an aribitrary distribution
P_{X}
. To this end, we shall first consider the special case when
W
is a BIMS channel, with a uniform input distribution, and provide a simple proof for the convergence of the mutual information
I^{(d)}
to the input entropy. We then provide a brief sketch of the proof of Theorem
III.1
for general DMCs, which makes use of well-known results from large-deviations theory. The full proof, owing to its length, is presented in Section
VII
.

IV-A

Convergence of Mutual Information for BIMS Channels

To build intuition, we present a simple proof for the case when the DMC
W
is a BIMS channel, with
P_{X}=\text{Ber}(1/2)
. The choice of the input distribution being uniform assumes relevance since the capacity of a BIMS channel is achieved by uniform inputs (see
[
20
, Problem 4.8]
).

The following bounds on the conditional entropy of the input to a BIMS channel given the output, which are drawn from
[
26
, Prop. 2.8]
, will prove useful. Recall the definition of the Bhattacharya parameter of a BIMS channel
W
with output alphabet
\mathcal{Y}
given by
Z_{b}(W):=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\sqrt{P_{Y|0}(y)P_{Y|1}(y)}
.

Lemma IV.1
.

Given a BIMS channel
\overline{W}
with input
\overline{X}\sim P_{X}
, where
P_{X}=\Ber(1/2)
, and output
\overline{Y}
, we have that

Z_{b}(\overline{W})^{2}\leq\mathsf{H}(\overline{X}\mid\overline{Y})\leq\log(1+Z_{b}(\overline{W})).

(34)

We thus obtain the following simple corollary.

Corollary IV.1
.

Given a BIMS channel
W
with input
X\sim P_{X}
, where
P_{X}=\Ber(1/2)
, we have that for the
d
-view DMC
W^{(d)}
,

Z_{b}({W})^{2d}\leq\mathsf{H}({X}\mid{Y^{d}})\leq Z_{b}({W})^{d}.

(35)

Proof.

We first observe that when
W
is a BIMS channel, then so is the
d
-view DMC
W^{(d)}
. To see why this is true, note that corresponding to a BIMS channel
W
, there exists a permutation
\pi:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Y}
such that

W(y\mid 0)=W(\pi(y)\mid 1),

(36)

with
\pi
being an involution, i.e.,
\pi^{-1}=\pi
. Now, for the
d
-view DMC
W^{(d)}
, we define the permutation
\pi^{(d)}:\mathcal{Y}^{d}\to\mathcal{Y}^{d}
such that

\pi^{(d)}(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{d})=(\pi(y_{1}),\pi(y_{2}),\ldots,\pi(y_{d})),

(37)

for
y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}\in\mathcal{Y}
. Clearly, we have that
\pi^{(d)}
is an involution, with

W^{(d)}((y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})\mid 0)=W(\pi^{(d)}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})\mid 1),

(38)

from (
5
). This hence implies that
W^{(d)}
is also a BIMS channels.

Now, the Bhattacharya parameter

\displaystyle Z_{b}(W^{(d)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{y^{d}\in\mathcal{Y}^{d}}\sqrt{P_{Y|0}(y^{d}|0)P_{Y|1}(y^{d}|1)}

(39)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y^{d}\in\mathcal{Y}^{d}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\sqrt{P_{Y|0}(y_{i}|0)P_{Y|1}(y_{i}|1)}

(40)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{y_{i}\in\mathcal{Y}}\sqrt{P_{Y|0}(y_{i}|0)P_{Y|1}(y_{i}|1)}

(41)

\displaystyle=Z_{b}(W)^{d}.

(42)

The proof is then completed by appealing to Lemma
IV.1
and noting that
\log(1+z)\leq z
, for all
z>-1
.
∎

Moreover, we have the following bound on
\rho(I)
for general binary-input DMCs and for arbitrary input distributions
P_{X}
.

Lemma IV.2
.

Given a binary-input DMC
W
with input
X\sim P_{X}
, where
P_{X}
is arbitrary, we have that

\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})\geq e^{-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y\mid X=0},P_{Y\mid X=1})+\Theta(\log d)}.

(43)

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{1}{P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(X\mid Y^{d})}\right].

(44)

Further, recall that in a binary Bayesian hypothesis testing setting (see
[
17
, Ch. 11]
) with prior
P_{X}

and hypotheses
\{P_{Y\mid 0},P_{Y\mid 1}\}
, we have that the probability of correct decision is given by

\mathbb{E}\left[P(X\mid Y^{d})\right]=1-e^{-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y\mid X=0},P_{Y\mid X=1})+\Theta(\log d)}.

(45)

Hence, we see that for large enough
d
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{1}{P(X\mid Y^{d})}\right]

(46)

\displaystyle\geq-\log\left(1-e^{-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y\mid X=0},P_{Y\mid X=1})+\Theta(\log d)}\right)

(47)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y\mid X=0},P_{Y\mid X=1})+\Theta(\log d)},

(48)

where the first inequality follows from Jensen’s inequality and the second from the fact that
\log(1+z)\leq z
, for
z>-1
. Also, the
\Theta(\log d)
term in the exponent in (
47
) is due either to the Laplace method (see
[
27
, Sec. 4.2]
) or to the results of
[
28
, Thm. 2.1 and Corollary 1]
.

∎

The above ingredients suffice to afford a proof of Theorem
III.1
for the special case of BIMS channels with a uniform input distribution.

Proof of Thm.
III.1
for BIMS channels with uniform inputs.

For the case when
W
is a BIMS channel, we have that
\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|0},P_{Y|1})=-\log Z_{b}(W)
(see the remark following the statement of Theorem
III.1
). Hence, when the input distribution is uniform, we have from Corollary
IV.1
, we have that
\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})\leq e^{-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|0},P_{Y|1})}
. The proof concludes by appealing then to Lemma
IV.2
, which shows that

\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})\geq e^{-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|0},P_{Y|1})+\Theta(\log d)}.

(49)

Hence, overall, we have that

\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})=e^{-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|0},P_{Y|1})+\Theta(\log d)}.

(50)

∎

It is possible, with some effort, to prove Theorem
III.1
for
I^{(d)}
for general DMCs with arbitrary input distributions with the aid of
[
1
, Lemma 9]
(see also
[
2
, Thm. 5]
) and an extension of Lemma
IV.2
. However, the analysis of dispersion requires more involved techniques. Owing to the length of the proofs, we present a detailed proof for
I^{(d)}
that introduces our techniques, in Section
VII-A
, and then extend them to
V^{(d)}
, in Section
VII-B
.

We now sketch a broad outline of the proof strategy for the convergence of mutual information to the input entropy. We first express the conditional entropy
\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})
as

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{1}{P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(X\mid Y^{d})}\right]

(51)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P_{X}(x)\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{1}{P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(x\mid Y^{d})}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right].

(52)

We then fix an
x\in\mathcal{X}
and focus on the inner term in the summation above. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{1}{P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(x\mid Y^{d})}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\log\frac{1}{P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(x\mid Y^{d})}\geq t\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]\mathrm{d}t.

(53)

Our approach is to obtain (exponentially tight) bounds on the probability
\mathbb{P}\left[-\log P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(x\mid Y^{d})\geq t\lvert X=x\right]
, using techniques from large-deviations theory—Sanov’s theorem, in particular. We show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{1}{P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(x\mid Y^{d})}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]=\text{exp}\left({-d\cdot\min_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\right).

(54)

This then allows us to use Laplace’s method (e.g., see
[
27
, Sec. 4.2]
) and the independence of the input distribution
P_{X}
on
d
to conclude that

\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,\tilde{x}:\tilde{x}\neq x\end{subarray}}\text{exp}\left({-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\right),

(55)

and hence that

I^{(d)}=\mathsf{H}(X)-\textnormal{exp}\left({-d\rho+\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\right).

(56)

Interestingly, our proof strategy allows us to obtain insights into the “higher-order” terms in the exponent of
\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})
and their dependence on both
d
and the size of the input alphabet
|\mathcal{X}|
. The proof for the convergence of the channel dispersion to the input varentropy is similar, and relies on the results obtained during the derivation of the convergence of the mutual information to the input entropy.

In the next section, we extend Theorem
III.1
to general
multi-letter
channels, which includes as a special case, synchronization channels such as the deletion channel. We then derive explicit, analytical bounds on the rates of convergence of the mutual information and dispersion of the deletion channel to their limits.

V
Convergence for Multi-Letter Channels and Some Examples

Recall that Theorem
III.1
presented the convergence of mutual information and channel dispersion for
d
-view DMCs
W^{(d)}
, where the channel
W
is specified by
single-letter
transition probabilities
\{W(y|x):\ x\in\mathcal{X},y\in\mathcal{Y}\}
. In this section, we first extend the statement of this theorem and its proof to multi-letter channels. Consider a multi-letter channel
W_{n,\nu}
, for any
n,\nu\geq 0
that are independent of
d
, specified by a channel law
\{W(y^{\nu}|x^{n})=P_{Y^{\nu}|X^{n}}(y^{\nu}|x^{n}):\ x^{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n},y^{\nu}\in\mathcal{Y}^{\nu}\}
. Here, the input alphabet is
\mathcal{X}^{n}
and the output alphabet is
\mathcal{Y}^{\nu}
. We work with the
d
-view multi-letter channel
W_{n,\nu}^{(d)}
defined by the channel law

P(Y^{d\nu}=y^{d\nu}\mid X^{n}=x^{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{d}W_{n,\nu}(y^{\nu}|x^{n}).

(57)

Recall here our assumption that
|\mathcal{X}|
and
|\mathcal{Y}|
do not depend on
d
. Similar to the earlier definition, we let

I_{n,\nu}^{(d)}:=I(X^{n};Y^{d\nu})

(58)

to be the mutual information rate over
W_{n,\nu}^{(d)}
. Here, we assume as before that the input distribution
P_{X^{n}}
is arbitrary, but fixed; in particular, it is
d
-independent. Likewise, let the channel dispersion be

V_{n,\nu}^{(d)}:=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\iota(X^{n};Y^{d\nu})-I(X^{n};Y^{d\nu})\right)^{2}\right],

(59)

where
\iota(x;y^{d}):=\log\frac{P(y^{d\nu}|x^{n})}{P(y^{d\nu})}
.
The following corollary of Theorem
III.1
holds:

Corollary V.1
.

We have that

I_{n,\nu}^{(d)}=\mathsf{H}(X^{n})-\textnormal{exp}\left({-d\rho_{n,\nu}+\Theta(\log d)+\Theta(n\cdot\log|\mathcal{X}|)}\right),

(60)

and

\left\lvert V_{n,\nu}^{(d)}-\mathsf{V}(X^{n})\right\rvert=\textnormal{exp}\left({-d\rho_{n,\nu}+\Theta(\log d)+\Theta(n\cdot\log|\mathcal{X}|)}\right),

(61)

where

\rho_{n,\nu}:=\min_{x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n}:x^{n}\neq\tilde{x}^{n}}\mathsf{C}(P_{Y^{\nu}|x^{n}},P_{Y^{\nu}|\tilde{x}^{n}}).

(62)

Note that the
\Theta(n\cdot\log|\mathcal{X}|)
terms above arise due to the fact that the input alphabet size is now
|\mathcal{X}|^{n}
, instead of
|\mathcal{X}|
, as in Theorem
III.1
. Observe also, from the earlier proofs, that only the
\Theta(\log|\mathcal{X}|)
term gets scaled by a factor of
n
and not the
\Theta(\log d)
term.

Now, consider the special case when
\nu=n
and the channel
W_{n,n}
corresponds to an
n
-letter DMC; more precisely, suppose that the multi-letter channel law obeys

W_{n,n}(y^{n}|x^{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}W(y_{i}|x_{i}).

(63)

Now, for the
single-letter
channel
W
as above, let
\rho=\min_{x,\tilde{x}:x\neq\tilde{x}}\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})
. We then have the following corollary:

Corollary V.2
.

For an
n
-letter DMC
W_{n,n}
,

I_{n,n}^{(d)}=\mathsf{H}(X^{n})-\textnormal{exp}\left({-dn\rho+\Theta(\log d)+n\cdot\Theta(\log|\mathcal{X}|)}\right),\

(64)

and

\left\lvert V_{n,n}^{(d)}-\mathsf{V}(X^{n})\right\rvert=\textnormal{exp}\left({-dn\rho+\Theta(\log d)+n\cdot\Theta(\log|\mathcal{X}|)}\right).

(65)

Proof.

The proof will follow from Corollary
V.1
if we can show that
\rho_{n,n}=n\rho
, for an
n
-letter DMC
W_{n,n}
. To this end, let
u,v\in\mathcal{X}
be such that
\rho=\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|u},P_{Y|v})
. Further, observe that for any two inputs
x^{n}\neq\tilde{x}^{n}
, we have
\mathsf{C}(P_{Y^{n}|x^{n}},P_{Y^{n}|\tilde{x}^{n}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x_{i}},P_{Y|\tilde{x}_{i}})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\rho_{n,n}

\displaystyle=\min_{x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n}:x^{n}\neq\tilde{x}^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x_{i}},P_{Y|\tilde{x}_{i}})

(66)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\min_{x_{i},\tilde{x}_{i}:x_{i}\neq\tilde{x}_{i}}\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x_{i}},P_{Y|\tilde{x}_{i}})=n\rho,

(67)

where equality in the second step is obtained when
x^{n}=(u,u,\ldots,u)
and
\tilde{x}^{n}=(v,v,\ldots,v)
.
∎

While Corollary
V.1
treats
n
and
\nu
as fixed constants, one can generalize the definitions of the
d
-view mutual information and channel dispersion to the setting where
\nu
is a
random variable
too. In particular, let
I_{n}^{(d)}
and
V_{n}^{(d)}
be defined the same way as in (
58
) and (
59
), respectively, but where the expectations are taken with respect to the randomness in the length
\nu
of the outputs too. Furthermore, let

f_{\lambda}(x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n}):=\sum_{m\geq 0}\Pr[\nu=m]\sum_{y^{m}}P^{1-\lambda}(y^{m}|x^{n})P^{\lambda}(y^{m}|\tilde{x}^{n}).

(68)

We then define

\rho_{n}:=\min_{x^{n}\neq\tilde{x}^{n}}\max_{\lambda\in[0,1]}-\log f_{\lambda}(x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n}).

(69)

In what follows, we present some estimates of
\rho_{n}
for the deletion channel, where the length
\nu\leq n
of the outputs is random.

V-A

Upper Bounds on
\rho_{n}
for the Deletion Channel

In this subsection, we consider the multi-letter deletion channel, which is an important example of a synchronization channel
[
3
]
. Recall that for
\delta\in(0,1)
, the deletion channel
\text{Del}(\delta)
deletes each input bit
x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, with probability
\delta
.

For Del
(\delta)
, for any fixed
x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n}
with
x^{n}\neq\tilde{x}^{n}
, and for any fixed
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n})=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\delta^{n-m}(1-\delta)^{m}\sum_{y^{m}}N^{1-\lambda}(x^{n}\to y^{m})\cdot N^{\lambda}(\tilde{x}^{n}\to y^{m}),

(70)

where
N(u^{n}\to v^{m})
, for
u^{n}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
,
v^{m}\in\{0,1\}^{m}
, is the number of occurrences of
v^{m}
as a (potentially non-contiguous) subsequence in
u^{n}
.

In what follows, we present three upper bounds on
\rho_{n}
for Del
(\delta)
, and via these upper bounds, arrive at some interesting properties of
\rho_{n}
and comparisons with the rates
\rho_{n,n}
of the BEC
(\delta)
and BSC
(\delta)
. The upper bounds are obtained by fixing specific sequences
x^{n}
,
\tilde{x}^{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}
and using
\max_{\lambda\in[0,1]}-\log f_{\lambda}(x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n})
, evaluated at these chosen sequences, as an upper bound on
\rho_{n}
. The first of our upper bounds is naïve and will be subsumed by the later bounds.

Lemma V.1
.

For
\Del(\delta)
, we have that

\rho_{n}\leq-n\log{\delta}
.

Proof.

Consider
any
fixed
x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n}
with
x^{n}\neq\tilde{x}^{n}
. For any
\lambda\in[0,1]
,
f_{\lambda}(x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n})\geq\delta^{n}
, from (
70
), since the empty string occurs exactly once as a subsequence of both
x^{n}
and
\tilde{x}^{n}
. Hence, we have that

\rho_{n}=\min_{x^{n}\neq\tilde{x}^{n}}\max_{\lambda\in[0,1]}-\log f_{\lambda}(x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n})\leq-n\log{\delta}.

(71)

∎

As expected, from the above lemma and from Corollary
V.2
, we see that the rate
\rho_{n}
for the deletion channel is
smaller
than the rate
\rho_{n,n}
for the
n
-letter erasure channel with erasure probability
\delta
. Indeed, recall that for the BEC
(\delta)
, we have that
\rho=-\log\delta
, and hence, by Corollary
V.2
,
\rho_{n,n}=-n\log\delta
. Note also that by an application of the data processing inequality, it follows that the capacity of
\Del(\delta)
is at most the capacity of the BEC
(\delta)
, for
\delta\in[0,1]

[
3
, Sec. 5.2]
.

The bound that follows improves on this naïve upper bound, for a certain range of
\delta
values, and for large enough
n
. For simplicity, in the second part of the next lemma, we assume that
n
is even; the case of odd
n
can be handled with minor modifications to the exisiting proof.

Lemma V.2
.

For
\Del(\delta)
, we have that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\rho_{n}=0
, if
\delta>1/2
.

Proof.

Fix
\delta>1/2
. We pick
x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n}
to be, respectively, the alternating sequences
01010\ldots
and
10101\ldots
, each having length
n
, for the purposes of this lemma.

Now, for any
y^{m}\in\{0,1\}^{m}
, with
m\leq n
, we see that
N(x^{n}\to y^{m})=N(\tilde{x}^{n}\to y^{m})
. Furthermore,
N(x^{n}\to y^{m})>0
iff
w(y^{m})\in[\max\{0,m-n/2\},n/2]
; we then call
y^{m}
“admissible”. For such admissible
y^{m}
, we have that

N(x^{n}\to y^{m})=\binom{n/2}{w(y^{m})}\cdot\binom{n/2}{m-w(y^{m})}=N(\tilde{x}^{n}\to y^{m}).

(72)

The above expression is obtained by choosing
w(y^{m})
locations among the
n/2

1
s in
x^{n}
(resp.
\tilde{x}^{n}
) as the locations of the
1
s in
y^{m}
, and likewise,
n-w(y^{m})
locations among the
n/2

0
s in
x^{n}
(resp.
\tilde{x}^{n}
) as the locations of the
0
s in
y^{m}
.
Furthermore, in the above expression, we implicitly assume that the binomial coefficient
\binom{k}{t}=0
if
t<0
or
t>k
.

Thus, for
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\delta^{n-m}(1-\delta)^{m}\sum_{y^{m}\text{ admissible}}\binom{n/2}{w(y^{m})}\binom{n/2}{m-w(y^{m})}

(73)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\delta^{n-m}(1-\delta)^{m}\sum_{w=\max\{m-n/2,0\}}^{\min\{m,n/2\}}\binom{m}{w}\binom{n/2}{w}\binom{n/2}{m-w}.

(74)

Let us call the inner summation above as
\beta(m)
. Now, note that

\beta(m)=\begin{cases}\sum_{w=0}^{m}\binom{m}{w}\binom{n/2}{w}\binom{n/2}{m-w},\ \text{if }m\leq n/2,\\
\sum_{w=m-n/2}^{n/2}\binom{m}{w}\binom{n/2}{w}\binom{n/2}{m-w},\ \text{if }m>n/2.\end{cases}

(75)

After some algebra, we can write

\beta(m)=\begin{cases}\sum_{w=0}^{m}\binom{m}{w}\binom{n/2}{w}\binom{n/2}{m-w},\ \text{if }m\leq n/2,\\
\sum_{w=0}^{n-m}\binom{m}{n/2-w}\binom{n/2}{w}\binom{n/2}{m-(n/2-w)},\ \text{if }m>n/2.\end{cases}

(76)

Let

\alpha_{1}(m):=\sum_{w=0}^{m}\binom{m}{w}\binom{n/2}{w}\binom{n/2}{m-w}

(77)

and

\alpha_{2}(m):=\sum_{w=0}^{n-m}\binom{m}{n/2-w}\binom{n/2}{w}\binom{n/2}{m-(n/2-w)}.

(78)

Consider
\alpha_{1}(m)
, for the case when
m\leq n/2
. We then have that

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(m)

\displaystyle\geq\min_{0\leq\omega\leq m}\binom{n/2}{m-\omega}\cdot\sum_{w=0}^{m}\binom{m}{w}\binom{n/2}{w}

(79)

\displaystyle=\binom{n/2}{0}\binom{n/2+m}{m}=\binom{n/2+m}{m},

(80)

where the first equality follows from the Chu-Vandermonde identity (see, e.g.,
[
29
, p. 42]
).
Likewise, consider
\alpha_{2}(m)
, for the case when
m\geq n/2
. Note that

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}(m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w=0}^{n-m}\binom{m}{n/2-w}\binom{n/2}{w}\binom{n/2}{m-(n/2-w)}

(81)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w=0}^{n-m}\binom{m}{n/2-w}\binom{n/2}{w}\binom{n/2}{n-m-w}

(82)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{w=0}^{n-m}\binom{n/2}{w}\binom{n/2}{n-m-w}=\binom{n}{m},

(83)

where the last equality above again uses the Chu-Vandermonde identity. Thus, we obtain that

\beta(m)\geq\begin{cases}\binom{n/2+m}{m},\ \text{if }m\leq n/2,\\
\binom{n}{m},\ \text{if }m>n/2.\end{cases}

(84)

Plugging this back into (
74
), we see that

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{m=0}^{n/2}\delta^{n-m}(1-\delta)^{m}\cdot\binom{n/2+m}{m}+\sum_{m=n/2+1}^{n}\delta^{n-m}(1-\delta)^{m}\cdot\binom{n}{m}.

(85)

Call the first summation above as
\gamma_{1}
and the second as
\gamma_{2}
. Now, observe that
\gamma_{2}
is precisely the probability
\Pr[L>n/2]
, for a binomial random variable
L\sim\text{Bin}(n,\delta)
. It is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
19
, Example 1.6.2]
) that for
\delta>1/2
, this probability is at least

1-\text{exp}(-n\cdot D(\text{Ber}(1/2)||\text{Ber}(\delta)))=1-Z(\delta)^{n},

(86)

where
Z(\delta)=2\sqrt{\delta(1-\delta)}
is the Bhattacharya parameter of the BSC
(\delta)
.
Furthermore, note that

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle\geq(\delta(1-\delta))^{n/2}\cdot\binom{n}{n/2}

(87)

\displaystyle=(\delta(1-\delta))^{n/2}\cdot 2^{n-O(\log n)}

(88)

\displaystyle=Z(\delta)^{n}\cdot 2^{-O(\log n)}.

(89)

where the first equality follows from
[
30
, Eq. (5.38)]
.

Hence, we obtain that for
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n})\geq 1-Z(\delta)^{n}\cdot(1-2^{-O(\log n)}).

(90)

Using the previous inequality, we obtain that when
\delta>1/2
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\rho_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max_{\lambda\in[0,1]}-\log f_{\lambda}(x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n})

(91)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n}\max\{0,-\log(1-Z(\delta)^{n}\cdot(1-2^{-O(\log n)}))\}

(92)

\displaystyle=0,

(93)

where the last inequality uses the fact that for
\lambda\in\{0,1\}
, we have
f_{\lambda}(x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n})=1
.
∎

Next, we show that for all
\delta\in(0,1)
(and hence for
\delta\leq 1/2
too), the rate of convergence
\rho_{n}
can in fact be bounded from above by a constant, for large enough
n
. Before we do so, we state and prove a useful lemma.

Lemma V.3
.

For
\Del(\delta)
, where
\delta\in(0,1)
, we have that

\rho_{n}\leq\max_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\lambda\log n-\log\mathbb{E}_{L\sim\text{Bin}(n,\delta)}\left[L^{\lambda}\right].

(94)

Proof.

As before, we pick specific sequences
x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n}
and seek to upper bound
\rho_{n,\nu}
by
\max_{\lambda\in[0,1]}-\log f_{\lambda}(x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n})
, for this choice of
x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n}
. Here, we choose
x^{n}=0^{n}
and
\tilde{x}^{n}=0^{n-1}1
.

Clearly, for any
m\leq n
, we have that
y^{m}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
is a subsequence of
x^{n}
iff
y^{m}=0^{m}
. For
y^{m}=0^{m}
, hence, it follows that

N(x^{n}\to y^{m})=\binom{n}{m}\ \text{and}\ N(\tilde{x}^{n}\to y^{m})=\binom{n-1}{m}.

(95)

Thus, for
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\delta^{n-m}(1-\delta)^{m}\cdot\binom{n}{m}^{1-\lambda}\cdot\binom{n-1}{m}^{\lambda}

(96)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\delta^{n-m}(1-\delta)^{m}\cdot\binom{n}{m}\cdot\left(\frac{n-m}{n}\right)^{\lambda}

(97)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{L\sim\text{Bin}(n,1-\delta)}\left[\left(1-\frac{L}{n}\right)^{\lambda}\right]

(98)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{\lambda}}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{L\sim\text{Bin}(n,\delta)}\left[{L}^{\lambda}\right],

(99)

and the claim follows by noting that
f_{\lambda}(x^{n},\tilde{x}^{n})=1
, for
\lambda\in\{0,1\}
.
∎

We next use Lemma
V.3
to show that for large enough
n
,
\rho_{n}
can be bounded from above by a
constant
. We thus obtain the following corollary:

Corollary V.3
.

For
\Del(\delta)
, where
\delta\in(0,1)
, we have that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\rho_{n}\leq-\log\delta
.

Proof.

The proof proceeds by obtaining estimates of the fractional moments of a binomial random variable used in Lemma
V.3
. Let
\beta,\eta>0
be small real numbers. We then have that for
L\sim\text{Bin}(n,\delta)
, and
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[L^{\lambda}]

\displaystyle\geq(n(\delta-\beta))^{\lambda}\cdot\Pr[L\geq n(\delta-\beta)]

(100)

\displaystyle\geq(n(\delta-\beta))^{\lambda}\cdot(1-\text{exp}(-n\cdot D(\text{Ber}(\delta-\beta)||\text{Ber}(\delta))))

(101)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\eta)\cdot(n(\delta-\beta))^{\lambda},

(102)

for
n
large enough.

Plugging (
102
) into Lemma
V.3
, we obtain that for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\rho_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\lambda\log n-\log\mathbb{E}_{L\sim\text{Bin}(n,\delta)}\left[L^{\lambda}\right]

(103)

\displaystyle\leq\max\bigg\{0,\max_{\lambda\in(0,1)}\lambda\log n-\lambda\log(n(\delta-\beta))-\log(1-\eta)\bigg\}

(104)

\displaystyle=\max\bigg\{0,\max_{\lambda\in(0,1)}-\lambda\log(\delta-\beta)-\log(1-\eta)\bigg\}

(105)

\displaystyle\leq-(1-\zeta)\log\delta,

(106)

for small
\zeta>0
. Thus, taking
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
on both sides of the inequality above and letting
\zeta\downarrow 0
, we obtain the statement of the corollary.
∎

Hence, putting together Lemma
V.2
and Corollary
V.3
, we see that for the deletion channel Del
(\delta)
, the rate
\rho_{n}
, for large enough
n
, is bounded above by a fixed constant, for
\delta\leq 1/2
, and by an arbitrarily small constant, for
\delta>1/2
.

In the next section, we take a detour from the asymptotic regime of large
d
considered in the rest of the paper, and seek to obtain tight, non-asymptotic bounds on the capacity of a special multi-view DMC, which holds for all values of
d
.

VI
Poisson Approximation Channel

In this section, we consider the special case of the
d
-view BSC
(p)
, denoted by
\text{BSC}^{(d)}(p)
. Recall that the capacity of this channel equals the capacity of the binomial channel
\mathsf{Bin}_{d}(p)
, which is given in (
26
). Our objective is to show that the Poisson approximation channel
\mathsf{Poi}_{d}(p)
introduced in Section
III
has capacity that is a very good lower bound on the capacity of
\text{BSC}^{(d)}(p)
, via the proof of Theorem
III.3
. Before we proceed with the proof, we state and prove a simple lemma about the concavity of the mutual information
I^{(d)}=I(X;Y^{d})
, for any
d
-view DMC
W^{(d)}
and input distribution
P_{X}
.

Lemma VI.1
.

For any
d
-view DMC
W^{(d)}
and input distribution
P_{X}
, we have that
I^{(d)}
is concave over the positive integers, i.e.,

I^{(d)}-I^{(d-1)}\geq I^{(d+1)}-I^{(d)},

(107)

for all
d\geq 1
.

Proof.

We first rewrite what we intend to prove differently: since
I^{(m)}=\mathsf{H}(X)+\mathsf{H}(Y^{m})-\mathsf{H}(X,Y^{m})
, for any
m\geq 0
, with
I^{(0)}:=0
, we would like to show that

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}(Y^{d})-

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}(Y^{d-1})+\mathsf{H}(X,Y^{d-1})-\mathsf{H}(X,Y^{d})

\displaystyle\geq\mathsf{H}(Y^{d+1})-\mathsf{H}(Y^{d})+\mathsf{H}(X,Y^{d})-\mathsf{H}(X,Y^{d+1}).

(108)

However, observe that for any
m\geq 0
,
\mathsf{H}(X,Y^{m+1})-\mathsf{H}(X,Y^{m})=\mathsf{H}(Y_{m+1}|X,Y^{m})=\mathsf{H}(Y_{m+1}|X)
, by the channel law of
W^{(d)}
. Hence, (
108
) reduces to showing that

\mathsf{H}(Y^{d})-\mathsf{H}(Y^{d-1})\geq\mathsf{H}(Y^{d+1})-\mathsf{H}(Y^{d}).

(109)

Now, by the submodularity of entropy
[
24
, Thm. 1.6]
, we have that

\mathsf{H}(Y^{d-1},Y_{d+1})-\mathsf{H}(Y^{d-1})\geq\mathsf{H}(Y^{d+1})-\mathsf{H}(Y^{d}).

(110)

The above implies (
109
), since the distributions of
(Y^{d-1},Y_{d+1})
and
Y^{d}
are equal, by the stationarity of the DMC
W^{(d)}
.
∎

For clarity, we first write down the capacity
C(\mathsf{Poi}_{d}(p))
of the Poisson approximation channel. As argued in Section
III
, the capacity is achieved when the inputs
X\sim P_{X}=\text{Ber}(1/2)
, yielding

\displaystyle C(\mathsf{Poi}_{d}(p))

\displaystyle=\mathsf{H}(X)-\mathsf{H}(X|R_{1},R_{2})

(111)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1}\geq 0,\\
r_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{e^{-dp}(dp)^{r_{1}}}{r_{1}!}\cdot\frac{e^{-d(1-p)}(d(1-p))^{r_{2}}}{r_{2}!}\cdot\log\frac{p^{r_{1}}(1-p)^{r_{2}}}{p^{r_{1}}(1-p)^{r_{2}}+p^{r_{2}}(1-p)^{r_{1}}}.

(112)

We now prove Theorem
III.3
.

Proof of Thm. 3.

Consider the mutual information between the input and outputs of the
\mathsf{Poi}_{d}(p)
channel when
P_{X}
is uniform over the inputs. Since the number of outputs
N=R_{1}+R_{2}
is known to the decoder, we have that

\displaystyle C(\mathsf{Poi}_{d}(p))

\displaystyle=I(X;R_{1},R_{2})

(113)

\displaystyle=I(X;R_{1},R_{2}\mid N)

(114)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[N=n]\cdot I(X;R_{1},R_{2}\mid N=n).

(115)

Now, observe that since the sum of a Poi
(dp)
random variable and a Poi
(d(1-p))
random variable is distributed according to Poi
(d)
, we have by symmetry that
N\sim\text{Poi}(d)
. Further, it is easy to check that the conditional distribution
P_{R_{1},R_{2}\mid N=n}
obeys
P_{R_{1},R_{2}\mid N=n}(r_{1},r_{2}\mid n)=\binom{n}{r_{1}}p^{r_{1}}(1-p)^{n-r_{1}}
, for all
r_{1},r_{2}\geq 0
such that
r_{1}+r_{2}=n
. Hence, we have that
I(X;R_{1},R_{2}\mid N=n)=C(\mathsf{Bin}_{n}(p))
.

Thus, substituting in (
115
), we get that

C(\mathsf{Poi}_{d}(p))=\mathbb{E}_{N\sim\text{Poi}(d)}\left[C(\mathsf{Bin}_{N}(p))\right].

(116)

Next, using the above pleasing expression, we show that
C(\mathsf{Poi}_{d}(p))
is indeed a lower bound on
C(\mathsf{Bin}_{d}(p))
. From Lemma
VI.1
we have that
C(\mathsf{Bin}_{n}(p))
is concave over the positive integers. Therefore, applying Jensen’s inequality, we get that

\displaystyle C(\mathsf{Poi}_{d}(p))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{N\sim\text{Poi}(d)}\left[C(\mathsf{Bin}_{N}(p))\right]

(117)

\displaystyle\leq C(\mathsf{Bin}_{\mathbb{E}N}(p))=C(\mathsf{Bin}_{d}(p)).

(118)

Furthermore, we have that

\displaystyle C(\mathsf{Bin}_{d}(p))-C(\mathsf{Poi}_{d}(p))

\displaystyle=C(\mathsf{Bin}_{d}(p))-\mathbb{E}_{N\sim\text{Poi}(d)}\left[C(\mathsf{Bin}_{N}(p))\right]

(119)

\displaystyle\leq C(\mathsf{Bin}_{d}(p))-1+\mathbb{E}_{N\sim\text{Poi}(d)}[Z(p)^{N}]

(120)

\displaystyle\leq\text{exp}(-d(1-Z(p)))-Z(p)^{2d},

(121)

where the two inequalities make use of Corollary
IV.1
and the last inequality uses the well-known expression for the moment-generating function of a Poisson random variable.
∎

Remark
.

For the binomial channel
\overline{\mathsf{Bin}}_{n}
, whose input is a real number in
[0,1]
, with
n
output trials, that is considered in
[
31
]
too, a suitable Poisson approximation channel
\overline{\mathsf{Poi}}_{n}
can be designed. This channel takes as input
X\in[0,1]
and outputs the pair
(R_{1},R_{2})
such that
P_{R_{1}|X}=\text{Poi}(nX)
and
P_{R_{2}|X}=\text{Poi}(n(1-X))
. For
\overline{\mathsf{Poi}}_{n}
, as above, we will have that
C(\overline{\mathsf{Poi}}_{n})=\mathbb{E}_{N\sim\text{Poi}(n)}\left[C(\overline{\mathsf{Bin}}_{N})\right]
. However, the proof of the concavity of the capacity
C(\overline{\mathsf{Bin}}_{n})
as in Lemma
VI.1
will not hold, owing to the failure of the conditional independence assumption that is crucially employed in the proof. We are hence unable to obtain a straightforward lower bound on
C(\overline{\mathsf{Bin}}_{n})
via
C(\overline{\mathsf{Poi}}_{n})
, using the previous techniques.

In this section, we provide a detailed proof of our main result, Theorem
III.1
.

VII-A

Convergence of Mutual Information for General DMCs

Given a general DMC
W
with input alphabet
\mathcal{X}
(not necessarily binary) and output alphabet
\mathcal{Y}
, consider the
d
-view DMC
W^{(d)}
. Let the input to
W^{(d)}
be
X
, with input distribution being
P_{X}
, and let its outputs be
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}\in\mathcal{Y}^{d}
. The joint distribution of the inputs and outputs is such that
P_{X,Y^{d}}(x,y^{d})=P_{X}(x)\prod_{i=1}^{d}W(y_{i}|x)
. Much like the proof of Corollary
IV.1
, the inequality

\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})\leq Z_{g}(W)^{d},

(122)

can be shown to hold (see
[
26
, Prop. 4.8]
), where

Z_{g}(W):=\sum_{x\neq x^{\prime}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\sqrt{P(x)W(y|x)P(x^{\prime})W(y|x^{\prime})}

(123)

is a scaled version of the Bhattacharya parameter for general DMCs. This then results in
\rho(I)\geq-\log Z_{g}(W)
, thereby showing that for well-behaved DMCs, the rate of convergence of
I^{(d)}
to
\mathsf{H}(X)
is exponential in
d
. We shall now take up the question of the exact speed of this convergence.

To this end, we will be next interested in evaluating, up to exponential tightness, the behaviour of

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{1}{P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(X\mid Y^{d})}\right]

(124)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P_{X}(x)\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{1}{P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(x\mid Y^{d})}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right].

(125)

Now, fix an
x\in\mathcal{X}
and focus on the inner term in (
125
). Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{1}{P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(x\mid Y^{d})}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\log\frac{1}{P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(x\mid Y^{d})}\geq t\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]\mathrm{d}t.

(126)

The inner probability, to wit,

p_{x}(t):=\mathbb{P}\left[-\log P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(x\mid Y^{d})\geq t\lvert X=x\right]

(127)

is bounded as follows:

Lemma VII.1
.

We have that

\frac{p_{x}(t)}{(|{\cal X}|-1)}\leq\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{e^{-(t-\log(|{\cal X}|-1))}}{1-e^{-t}}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]

(128)

and

p_{x}(t)\geq\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{e^{-t}}{1-e^{-t}}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right].

(129)

Proof.

First, observe that

\displaystyle p_{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{P_{XY^{d}}(x,Y^{d})}{P_{Y^{d}}(Y^{d})}\leq e^{-t}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]

(130)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)\leq e^{-t}\sum_{\tilde{x}}P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})\Big\lvert X=x\right]

(131)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{\sum_{\tilde{x}\neq x}P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{e^{-t}}{1-e^{-t}}\Big\lvert X=x\right].

(132)

We first prove the upper bound in the lemma. The above probability obeys

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{\sum_{\tilde{x}\neq x}P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{e^{-t}}{1-e^{-t}}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{(|{\cal X}|-1)\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{e^{-t}}{1-e^{-t}}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]

(133)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{|{\cal X}|-1}\cdot\min_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{e^{-t}}{1-e^{-t}}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]

(134)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{|{\cal X}|-1}\cdot\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{e^{-t}}{1-e^{-t}}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]

(135)

\displaystyle\leq(|{\cal X}|-1)\cdot\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{|{\cal X}|-1}\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{e^{-t}}{1-e^{-t}}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right],

(136)

thereby showing the upper bound. Further, for any
\tilde{x}
, we have that

\displaystyle p_{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{\sum_{\tilde{x}\neq x}P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{e^{-t}}{1-e^{-t}}\mid X=x\right]

(137)

\displaystyle\geq\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{e^{-t}}{1-e^{-t}}\mid X=x\right],

(138)

thereby proving the lower bound.
∎

Since we are interested mainly in the behaviour of
\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})
up to first order in the exponent
d
, it suffices for us to understand the behaviour of
p_{x}(t)
upto first order in the exponent
d
with the expectation that we will accrue an additional
\Theta(\log d)
term in the exponent, in the final expression (see Eq. (
126
) above and
[
27
, Sec. 4.2]
for intuition). To this end, following Lemma
VII.1
, for fixed
z\in[0,\infty)
and
c>0
, we shall focus on the term

\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z):=\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{ce^{-z}}{1-e^{-z}}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right],

(139)

with the reasoning that the rate of convergence of
\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})
will be dictated by the smallest rate of
\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)
, as
z
increases from
0
to
\infty
.

Before we proceed, we require some more notation. Let us denote the log-likelihood ratio by

L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{y}:=\log\frac{P_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)}{P_{Y\mid X}(y\mid\tilde{x})}.

(140)

For a given
y^{d}\in{\cal Y}^{d}
and for
b\in\mathcal{Y}
, let

\hat{Q}_{y^{d}}(b):=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathds{1}\{y_{i}=b\}
be the empirical frequency of the letter
b
in any output sequence. Further, we define

L_{x,\tilde{x}}(\hat{Q}):=\sum_{b\in{\cal Y}}\hat{Q}(b)L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{b}.

(141)

Finally, let

v_{x,\tilde{x}}\equiv v_{x,\tilde{x}}(z):=\frac{1}{d}\left(\log\left(\frac{ce^{-z}}{1-e^{-z}}\right)-\log\frac{P(x)}{P(\tilde{x})}\right)

(142)

and let
z(v_{x,\tilde{x}}):=\log\left(1+\frac{cP(\tilde{x})}{P(x)}\cdot e^{-dv_{x,\tilde{x}}}\right)
be its inverse function.

Proposition VII.1
.

We have that for any fixed
\delta>0
,

1.

If
v_{x,\tilde{x}}\geq L_{x,\tilde{x}}({P_{Y|x}})+\delta
, then
\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z(v_{x,\tilde{x}}))=\Theta(1)
.

2.

If
v_{x,\tilde{x}}\leq L_{x,\tilde{x}}({P_{Y|x}})-\delta
, then
\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z(v_{x,\tilde{x}}))=e^{-dE(v_{x,\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d)}
, where

\displaystyle E(v_{x,\tilde{x}}):=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\max\limits_{\lambda\geq 0}\bigg[-\log\left(\sum_{y}P(y|x)^{1-\lambda}P(y|x^{\prime})^{\lambda}\right)-\lambda v_{x,\tilde{x}}\bigg],\ \text{if }v_{x,\tilde{x}}\geq\min_{b\in\mathcal{Y}}L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{b},\\
\infty,\ \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(143)

Proof.

Observe that for any
z\in[0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{ce^{-z}}{1-e^{-z}}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]

(144)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\log\frac{P_{Y\mid X}(Y_{i}\mid x)}{P_{Y\mid X}(Y_{i}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\log\left(\frac{ce^{-z}}{1-e^{-z}}\right)-\log\frac{P(x)}{P(\tilde{x})}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]

(145)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{Y_{i}}\leq\log\left(\frac{ce^{-z}}{1-e^{-z}}\right)-\log\frac{P(x)}{P(\tilde{x})}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]

(146)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\hat{Q}}\left[L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{Y^{d}}\right]\leq v_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)\Big\lvert X=x\right].

(147)

Writing this differently, we have that for any
v_{x,\tilde{x}}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z(v_{x,\tilde{x}}))=\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\hat{Q}}\left[L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{Y^{d}}\right]\leq v_{x,\tilde{x}}\Big\lvert X=x\right].

(148)

Consider first the case when
v_{x,\tilde{x}}\geq L_{x,\tilde{x}}({P_{Y|x}})+\delta
. Note that
L_{x,\tilde{x}}({P_{Y|x}})=D(P_{Y|x}||P_{Y|\tilde{x}})>0
. We split this case into two subcases.

•

If
v_{x,\tilde{x}}\geq\max_{b\in{\cal Y}}L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{b}
then
\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z(v_{x,\tilde{x}}))=1
.

•

Otherwise, consider the case where
L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y\mid x})+\delta\leq v_{x,\tilde{x}}\leq\max_{b\in{\cal Y}}L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{b}
. Now, observe that

\displaystyle 1\geq\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(

\displaystyle z(v_{x,\tilde{x}}))\geq\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\hat{Q}}\left[L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{Y^{d}}\right]\leq L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y\mid x})+\delta\Big\lvert X=x\right].

(149)

Furthermore, by a simple application of Markov’s inequality, we get that since
L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y\mid x})=\mathbb{E}_{Y^{d}\sim P_{Y^{d}|x}}[L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{Y^{d}}]
, the following inequality holds:

\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\hat{Q}}\left[L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{Y^{d}}\right]\leq L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y\mid x})+\delta\Big\lvert X=x\right]\geq\frac{\delta}{L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y\mid x})+\delta}.

(150)

Hence, we have that
1\geq\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z(v_{x,\tilde{x}}))\geq\frac{\delta}{L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y\mid x})+\delta}
.

Hence, overall, if
v_{x,\tilde{x}}\geq L_{x,\tilde{x}}({P_{Y|x}})+\delta
, we get that
\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z(v_{x,\tilde{x}}))=\Theta(1)
.

Next, consider the case when
v_{x,\tilde{x}}\leq L_{x,\tilde{x}}({P_{Y|x}})-\delta
. We again split this case into two subcases.

•

If
v_{x,\tilde{x}}\leq\min_{b\in{\cal Y}}L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{b}
, then
\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z(v_{x,\tilde{x}}))=0
.

•

If
\min_{b\in{\cal Y}}L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{b}\leq v_{x,\tilde{x}}\leq L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y\mid X=x})
,
then this is a large-deviations event. The probability
\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z(v_{x,\tilde{x}}))
thus decays
exponentially in
d
, i.e.,
\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z(v_{x,\tilde{x}}))=e^{-dE_{1}(v_{x,\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d)}
, for some
E_{1}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
, where by Sanov’s theorem (see
[
17
, Sec. 11.4]
), we have that

E_{1}(v_{x,\tilde{x}})=\min_{Q\colon L_{x,\tilde{x}}(Q)\leq v_{x,\tilde{x}}}D(Q\mid\mid P_{Y\mid X=x}).

(151)

We now aim to characterize this function
E_{1}
.

From convex duality (see
[
32
, Sec. 5.2]
), we obtain that

E_{1}(v_{x,\tilde{x}})=\max_{\lambda\geq 0}\min_{Q}D(Q\mid\mid P_{Y\mid X=x})+\lambda(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(Q)-v_{x,\tilde{x}}).

(152)

First, let us solve the inner minimization. Fix
\lambda\geq 0
. Observe that the term
f(\lambda,Q):=D(Q\mid\mid P_{Y\mid X=x})+\lambda L_{x,\tilde{x}}(Q)
obeys

\displaystyle f(\lambda,Q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y}Q(y)\left(\log\frac{Q(y)}{P_{Y\mid X=x}(y)}+\lambda L_{x,\tilde{x}}(y)\right)

(153)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{y}Q(y)\left(\log\frac{P_{Y\mid X=x}(y)}{Q(y)e^{\lambda L_{x,\tilde{x}}(y)}}\right)

(154)

\displaystyle\geq-\log\sum_{y}\frac{P_{Y\mid X=x}(y)}{e^{\lambda L_{x,\tilde{x}}(y)}},

(155)

using Jensen’s inequality. Also note that equality holds when

Q(y)=Q^{*}(y)=\frac{1}{Z_{x,\tilde{x}}(\lambda)}P_{Y\mid X=x}(y)e^{-\lambda L_{x,\tilde{x}}(y)},

(156)

where
Z_{x,\tilde{x}}(\lambda):=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}P_{Y\mid X=x}(y)e^{-\lambda L_{x,\tilde{x}}(y)}
is the partition function (normalization constant). Substituting
Q^{*}
back into the function results

\displaystyle f(\lambda,Q^{*})

\displaystyle=-\log Z_{x,\tilde{x}}(\lambda)-\lambda v_{x,\tilde{x}}

(157)

\displaystyle=-\log\left(\sum_{y}P_{Y\mid X=x}(y)e^{-\lambda L_{x,\tilde{x}}(y)}\right)-\lambda v_{x,\tilde{x}}

(158)

\displaystyle=-\log\left(\sum_{y}P_{Y\mid X=x}^{1-\lambda}(y\mid x)P_{Y\mid X}^{\lambda}(y\mid\tilde{x})\right)-\lambda v_{x,\tilde{x}}.

(159)

Plugging back into (
152
), we get that

\displaystyle E_{1}(v_{x,\tilde{x}})

\displaystyle=\max_{\lambda\geq 0}\Bigg[-\log\left(\sum_{y}P_{Y\mid X=x}^{1-\lambda}(y\mid x)P_{Y\mid X}^{\lambda}(y\mid\tilde{x})\right)-\lambda v_{x,\tilde{x}}\Bigg].

(160)

Note that by setting
E(v_{x,\tilde{x}})=E_{1}(v_{x,\tilde{x}})
when
\min_{b\in{\cal Y}}L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{b}\leq v_{x,\tilde{x}}\leq L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y\mid X=x})
and
E(v_{x,\tilde{x}})=\infty
when
v_{x,\tilde{x}}\leq\min_{b\in{\cal Y}}L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{b}
, we obtain a tight characterization of the rate of exponential decay of
\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z(v_{x,\tilde{x}}))
, in these ranges of
v_{x,\tilde{x}}
.
∎

We now state a prove a lemma that will allow us to identify the exact rate of exponential decay of
\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})
.

Lemma VII.2
.

We have that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)\mathrm{d}z=e^{\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\cdot e^{-d\cdot\min_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})}.

(161)

Proof.

Consider an integral of the form

\overline{I}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)\mathrm{d}z.

(162)

First, observe that

\displaystyle\overline{I}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)\mathrm{d}z

(163)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{X}|^{2}\cdot\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)\mathrm{d}z

(164)

\displaystyle=e^{\Theta(\log|\mathcal{X}|)}\cdot\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)\mathrm{d}z.

(165)

Further,

\displaystyle\overline{I}

\displaystyle\geq\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)\mathrm{d}z.

(166)

Hence, overall, we have that
\overline{I}=\text{exp}(\Theta(\log|\mathcal{X}|))\cdot\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)\mathrm{d}z
. We next focus on the integral

I=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)\mathrm{d}z,

(167)

for a fixed
x,\tilde{x}
.

By the definition

v_{x,\tilde{x}}=v_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)=\frac{1}{d}\left(\log\left(\frac{ce^{-z}}{1-e^{-z}}\right)-\log\frac{P(x)}{P(x^{\prime})}\right),

(168)

and from Prop.
VII.1
, we see that the integral
I
can be split into two integrals for its evaluation: integral
I_{1}
, when

z\leq\log\left(1+\frac{cP(\tilde{x})}{P(x)}\cdot e^{-d(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})+\delta)}\right):=u(d),

(169)

or equivalently, when
v_{x,\tilde{x}}\geq L_{x,\tilde{x}}({P_{Y|x}})+\delta
, and integral
I_{2}
, when

z\geq\log\left(1+\frac{cP(\tilde{x})}{P(x)}\cdot e^{-d(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})-\delta)}\right):=\ell(d),

(170)

or equivalently, when
v_{x,\tilde{x}}\leq L_{x,\tilde{x}}({P_{Y|x}})-\delta
, with
I=I_{1}+I_{2}
. Here, we pick
\delta<L_{x,\tilde{x}}({P_{Y|x}})
.

Consider first the integral
I_{1}
. We have that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{z\leq u(d)}\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)\mathrm{d}z

(171)

\displaystyle=\Theta(1)\cdot u(d).

(172)

Now, we claim that
u(d)=e^{-d(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})+\delta)}
. To see this, let
\tilde{c}:=\frac{cP(\tilde{x})}{P(x)}
and recall that
\frac{t}{1+t}\leq\log(1+t)\leq t
, for
t>-1
. Hence, we have that

u(d)\leq\tilde{c}e^{-d(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})+\delta)},

(173)

and

u(d)\geq\frac{\tilde{c}e^{-d(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})+\delta)}}{1+\tilde{c}e^{-d(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})+\delta)}}\geq\frac{\tilde{c}}{2}\cdot e^{-d(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})+\delta)},

(174)

since
L_{x,\tilde{x}}({P_{Y|x}})=D(P_{Y|x}||P_{Y|\tilde{x}})>0
.
Hence, overall, we have that for any fixed
\delta>0
,

I_{1}=e^{-d(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})+\delta)+\Theta(1)}.

(175)

Taking the limit as
\delta\downarrow 0
, we get that

I_{1}=e^{-dL_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})+\Theta(1)}=e^{-d\cdot D(P_{Y|x}||P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(1)}.

(176)

Consider now the integral
I_{2}
. Note that from case 2) in Prop.
VII.1
, when
z\geq\log\left(1+\tilde{c}e^{-d\cdot\min_{b}L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{b}}\right):=U(d)
, we have that
I_{2}=0
. Hence, we can write

I_{2}=\int_{\ell(d)\leq z\leq U(d)}\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)\mathrm{d}z,

(177)

where, for this interval
\ell(d)\leq z\leq U(d)
of interest,

\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)=e^{-d{E}(v_{x,\tilde{x}}(z))+\Theta(\log d)},

(178)

with

{E}(v_{x,\tilde{x}}(z))=\max_{\lambda\geq 0}\left[-\log\left(\sum_{y}P(y|x)^{1-\lambda}P(y|x^{\prime})^{\lambda}\right)-\lambda v_{x,\tilde{x}}\right],

(179)

from Prop.
VII.1
.

It is clear that
E(v_{x,\tilde{x}}(z))
increases as
v_{x,\tilde{x}}
decreases. In the integral
I_{2}
, we thus make the change of variables

v=\frac{1}{d}\left(\log\left(\frac{ce^{-z}}{1-e^{-z}}\right)-\log\frac{P(x)}{P(x^{\prime})}\right),

(180)

with
\mathrm{d}z=\frac{-dce^{-dv}}{1+ce^{-dv}}\mathrm{d}v
. Thus,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=dc\int^{L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})-\delta}_{v=\min_{b}L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{b}}e^{-dE(v)}\cdot\frac{e^{-dv}}{1+ce^{-dv}}\mathrm{d}v

(181)

\displaystyle=dc\int^{L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})-\delta}_{v=0}e^{-dE(v)}\cdot\frac{e^{-dv}}{1+ce^{-dv}}\mathrm{d}v+dc\int^{0}_{v=\min_{b}L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{b}}e^{-dE(v)}\cdot\frac{e^{-dv}}{1+ce^{-dv}}\mathrm{d}v.

(182)

Let us call the first integral in the expansion above as
I_{2,a}
and the second as
I_{2,b}
(note that
\min_{b}L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{b}\leq 0
). Now, observe that

\displaystyle I_{2,a}

\displaystyle=dc\int^{L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})-\delta}_{v=0}e^{-dE(v)}\cdot{e^{-dv+\Theta(\log d)}}dv

(183)

\displaystyle=\text{exp}(-d(E(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x}))+L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})-\delta)+\Theta(\log d)).

(184)

Here, in the first eqauility, we argue as previously that

\frac{e^{-dv}}{1+ce^{-dv}}=e^{-dv+\Theta(1)}.

(185)

Furthermore, the second equation follows from Laplace’s method (e.g., see
[
27
, Sec. 4.2]
), using the fact that both
e^{-dE(v)}
and
e^{-dv}
increase as
v
increases. Taking
\delta\downarrow 0
, we obtain that

I_{2,a}=e^{-d(E(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x}))+L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x}))+\Theta(\log d)}.

(186)

Moreover,

\displaystyle I_{2,b}

\displaystyle=e^{\Theta(\log d)}\cdot\int^{0}_{v=\min_{b}L_{x,\tilde{x}}^{b}}e^{-dE(v)}\mathrm{d}v

(187)

\displaystyle=e^{-dE(0)+\Theta(\log d)}=e^{-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d)}.

(188)

Here, for the first equality, we use the fact that when
v\leq 0
,

\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{e^{-dv}}{1+ce^{-dv}}\leq\frac{1}{c},

(189)

and hence
\frac{e^{-dv}}{1+ce^{-dv}}=\Theta(1)
.
The second equality holds again by Laplace’s method, using the fact that
e^{-dE(v)}
increases with
v
.

Putting everything together, in order to nail down the exponential behaviour of integral
I
, we need to reconcile Equations (
176
), (
186
), and (
188
), to understand which among
I_{1}
,
I_{2,a}
, and
I_{2,b}
, is the largest. First, observe that since

\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})\leq D(P_{Y|x}||P_{Y|\tilde{x}}),

(190)

we have that
I_{2,b}
dominates
I_{1}
.

Therefore, the exponential behaviour of integral
I
is governed by the rate

\overline{E}=\min\left\{E(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x}))+L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x}),\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})\right\}.

(191)

Now note that the maximum over
\lambda\geq 0
in the expressions for
E
and
\mathsf{C}
can be rewritten as a maximum over
\lambda\in[0,1]
(owing to the concavity of
-\log\left(\sum_{y}P(y|x)^{1-\lambda}P(y|x^{\prime})^{\lambda}\right)
and the fact that it equals
0
at
\lambda=0,1
). We then get that

E(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x}))+L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})\geq\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}}),

(192)

implying that
\overline{E}=\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})
. Therefore,

I=e^{-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d)},

(193)

and hence that

\overline{I}=\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\text{exp}({-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d)+\Theta(\log|\mathcal{X}|)}).

(194)

∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
III.1
for the convergence of the mutual information.

Proof of Thm.
III.1
for mutual information.

Recall from Lemma
VII.1
that

p_{x}(t)\leq(|\mathcal{X}|-1)\cdot\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(t),

(195)

with the constant
c
in the definition of
\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(t)
taken to be
|\mathcal{X}|-1
. Thus, from Lemma
VII.2
and (
126
), we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{1}{P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(x\mid Y^{d})}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{x}(t)\mathrm{d}t

(196)

\displaystyle\leq\text{exp}\left({-d\cdot\min_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\right),

(197)

where the inequality holds by the Laplace method
[
27
, Sec. 4.2]
.
Similarly, since

p_{x}(t)\geq\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(t),

(198)

with the constant
c
in the definition of
\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}(t)
taken to be
1
, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{1}{P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(x\mid Y^{d})}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]

\displaystyle\geq\text{exp}\left({-d\cdot\min_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\right).

(199)

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{1}{P_{X\mid Y^{d}}(x\mid Y^{d})}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]

\displaystyle=\text{exp}\left({-d\cdot\min_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\right).

(200)

Plugging back in (
125
), we obtain that

\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,\tilde{x}:\tilde{x}\neq x\end{subarray}}\text{exp}\left({-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\right),

(201)

yielding the required result.
∎

VII-B

Convergence of Channel Dispersion of DMCs

We now shift our attention to proving the convergence of the dispersion
V^{(d)}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\iota(X;Y^{d})-I(X;Y^{d})\right)^{2}\right]
, to the varentropy
\mathsf{V}(X)
of the input distribution
P_{X}
, for general DMCs
W
.

First, we write

\displaystyle V^{(d)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\log\frac{P(X|Y^{d})}{P(X)}-\left(\mathsf{H}(X)-\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})\right)\right)^{2}\right]

(202)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\log\frac{1}{P(X)}-\mathsf{H}(X)\right)^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\log P(X|Y^{d})+\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ +2\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[(\log P(X|Y^{d})+\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d}))\cdot\left(\log\frac{1}{P(X)}-\mathsf{H}(X)\right)\right]

(203)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{V}(X)+\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\log P(X|Y^{d})\right)^{2}\right]-\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})^{2}\right)+\theta_{d},

(204)

where we use the fact that
\mathbb{E}\left[-\log P(X|Y^{d})\right]=\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})
, and the cross-term
\theta_{d}
is

2\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[(\log P(X|Y^{d})+\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d}))\cdot\left(\log\frac{1}{P(X)}-\mathsf{H}(X)\right)\right].

(205)

Further, observe that when
P_{X}=\text{Unif}(\mathcal{X})
, the cross term vanishes, since for any
x\in\mathcal{X}
, we have that
\log\frac{1}{P(x)}=\log|\mathcal{X}|=\mathsf{H}(X)
. Our proof strategy hence is to first characterize (up to exponential tightness) the behaviour of
\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\log P(X|Y^{d})\right)^{2}\right]
and then handle the cross-term
\theta_{d}
.

Lemma VII.3
.

We have that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\log P(X|Y^{d})\right)^{2}\right]=e^{\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\cdot\max_{x,\tilde{x}:\tilde{x}\neq x}e^{-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})}.

(206)

Proof.

This proof is entirely analogous to the proof of convergence of
I^{(d)}
to
\mathsf{H}(X)
, for general DMCs, in Section
VII-A
, and we only highlight the key steps.

We first consider the term
\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\log P(X|Y^{d})\right)^{2}\right]
. As in (
125
), we first expand this term as:

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\log P(X|Y^{d})\right)^{2}\right]=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P(x)\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\log P(X|Y^{d})\right)^{2}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right],

(207)

where for any
x\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\log P(x|Y^{d})\right)^{2}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{t=0}^{\infty}\Pr\left[\left(\log P(x|Y^{d})\right)^{2}\geq t\Bigg\lvert X=x\right]\mathrm{d}t

(208)

\displaystyle=\int_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\log P(x|Y^{d})\geq\sqrt{t}\Big\lvert X=x\right]\mathrm{d}t+\int_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\log P(x|Y^{d})\leq-\sqrt{t}\Big\lvert X=x\right]\mathrm{d}t

(209)

\displaystyle=\int_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\log P(x|Y^{d})\leq-\sqrt{t}\Big\lvert X=x\right]\mathrm{d}t.

(210)

Here, the last equality holds since
P(x|Y^{d})\leq 1
, for any
Y^{d}\in\mathcal{Y}^{d}
. As earlier, our task reduces to understanding the exponential behaviour of

q_{x}(t):=\Pr\left[\log P(x|Y^{d})\leq-\sqrt{t}\Big\lvert X=x\right].

(211)

Exactly as in Lemma
VII.1
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{q_{x}(t)}{(|{\cal X}|-1)}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{e^{-(\sqrt{t}-\log(|{\cal X}|-1))}}{1-e^{-\sqrt{t}}}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right],

(212)

and

q_{x}(t)\geq\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{e^{-\sqrt{t}}}{1-e^{-\sqrt{t}}}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right].

(213)

Consider, for a fixed
c>0
and
z\in[0,\infty)
, the expression

\Delta_{x,\tilde{x}}(z):=\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{P(x)P(Y^{d}\mid x)}{P(\tilde{x})P(Y^{d}\mid\tilde{x})}\leq\frac{ce^{-\sqrt{z}}}{1-e^{-\sqrt{z}}}\Bigg\lvert X=x\right].

(214)

As argued in the case of the mutual information, the rate of exponential decay of
\Delta_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)
, as a function of
z
, dictates the rate of exponential decay of
q_{x}(t)
, and hence of
\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\log P(X|Y^{d})\right)^{2}\right]
. Once again, letting

v_{x,\tilde{x}}=v_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)=\frac{1}{d}\left(\log\left(\frac{ce^{-z}}{1-e^{-z}}\right)-\log\frac{P(x)}{P(x^{\prime})}\right)

(215)

and setting
(z(v_{x,\tilde{x}}))^{1/2}:=\log\left(1+\frac{cP(\tilde{x})}{P(x)\cdot}e^{-dv_{x,\tilde{x}}}\right)
, we obtain a result similar to Proposition
VII.1
, with the only modification being the replacement of
\Gamma_{x,\tilde{x}}
by
\Delta_{x,\tilde{x}}
.

Next, as in Lemma
VII.2
, consider the integral

\overline{I}^{V}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\Delta_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)\mathrm{d}z.

(216)

We claim that

\overline{I}^{V}=e^{\Theta(\log d)+\Theta(\log|\mathcal{X}|)}\cdot e^{-d\min_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})}.

(217)

To obtain this, we argue as in the proof of Lemma
VII.2
that
\overline{I}^{V}=e^{\Theta(\log|\mathcal{X}|)}\cdot\max_{\tilde{x}\neq x}I^{V}
, where

I^{V}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Delta_{x,\tilde{x}}(z)\mathrm{d}z,

(218)

for a fixed
x,\tilde{x}
.

Now, we split the evaluation of
I^{V}
into two integrals: integral
I_{1}^{V}
, when

z\leq\left(\log\left(1+\frac{cP(\tilde{x})}{P(x)}\cdot e^{-d(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})+\delta)}\right)\right)^{2},

(219)

and integral
I_{2}^{V}
, when

z\geq\left(\log\left(1+\frac{cP(\tilde{x})}{P(x)}\cdot e^{-d(L_{x,\tilde{x}}(P_{Y|x})-\delta)}\right)\right)^{2},

(220)

with
I^{V}=I_{1}^{V}+I_{2}^{V}
. By arguments analogous to those in the proof of Lemma
VII.2
, we obtain that

I_{1}^{V}=e^{-2d\cdot D(P_{Y|x}||P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d)},

(221)

and

I_{2}^{V}=e^{-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d)}.

(222)

Thus, we have that

I^{V}=e^{-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d)},

(223)

and hence that

\overline{I}^{V}=\text{exp}\left({-d\cdot\min_{\tilde{x}\neq x}\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\right).

(224)

Putting everything together, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\log P(X|Y^{d})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\max_{x,\tilde{x}:\tilde{x}\neq x}\text{exp}\left({-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\right).

(225)

∎

Lemma VII.4
.

We have that

\theta_{d}=e^{\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\cdot\max_{x,\tilde{x}:\tilde{x}\neq x}e^{-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})}.

(226)

Proof.

Recall the cross term

\theta_{d}:=2\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[(\log P(X|Y^{d})+\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d}))\cdot\left(\log\frac{1}{P(X)}-\mathsf{H}(X)\right)\right].

(227)

We then write

\displaystyle\theta_{d}(x)

\displaystyle:=\left(\log\frac{1}{P(x)}-\mathsf{H}(X)\right)\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\log P(x|Y^{d})\Big\lvert X=x\right]+\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})\right),

(228)

with
\theta_{d}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P(x)\theta_{d}(x)
. From the capacity analysis, we know that (see (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\log P(x|Y^{d})\Big\lvert X=x\right]

\displaystyle=-\max_{x^{\prime}\neq x}\text{exp}\left({-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\right).

(229)

From our previous analysis, we know that

\mathsf{H}(X|Y^{d})=\max_{x,x^{\prime}:x^{\prime}\neq x}\text{exp}\left({-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\right).

(230)

Hence,

\displaystyle\theta_{d}

\displaystyle=e^{\Theta(\log d)}\cdot\theta_{d}(x)=\max_{x,x^{\prime}:x\neq x^{\prime}}\text{exp}\left({-d\cdot\mathsf{C}(P_{Y|x},P_{Y|\tilde{x}})+\Theta(\log d|\mathcal{X}|)}\right).

(231)

VIII
Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, we considered the behaviour of the information rate and channel dispersion of multi-view noisy channels, with arbitrary, but fixed, input distributions, in the regime where the number of views is very large. In the first part of the paper, we considered multi-view discrete memoryless channels (DMCs), and showed that the information rate and the channel dispersion converge exponentially quickly to the input entropy and varentropy, respectively, with an input distribution-independent rate that equals the smallest Chernoff information between two conditional distributions of the output given different inputs. We then extended these results to general multi-letter channels, and computed explicit upper bounds on the Chernoff information above for the deletion channel. Finally, for the special case of the multi-view binary symmetric channel, we also presented a close, non-asymptotic approximation to its capacity, via the capacity of the so-called Poisson approximation channel.

One interesting direction for future work would be the extension of the techniques and results in this paper to other multi-view channels with memory and general synchronization channels (see
[
33
]
and
[
3
, Sec. VII]
).

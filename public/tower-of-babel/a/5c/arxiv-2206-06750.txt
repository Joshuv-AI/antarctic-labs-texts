Light trajectory and shadow shape in the rotating naked singularity
Vishva Patel, Divya Tahelyani, Ashok B. Joshi, Dipanjan Dey, Pankaj S. Joshi

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the light trajectories and shadow properties in the rotating version of null naked singularity (NNS) spacetime which is derived using the Newman- Janis algorithm without complexification method. We discuss some of the geometrical properties and causal structure of Rotating Naked Singularity (RNS) spacetime. The gravitational lensing in a rotating naked singularity is analyzed, and the results are compared to those of a Kerr black hole. In the case of a Kerr black hole, the photon sphere exists for both prograde and retrograde photon orbits, whereas for RNS, the photon sphere exists only for retrograde photon orbits. As a result, the naked singularity projects an arc-shaped shadow that differs from the contour-shaped shadow cast by a Kerr black hole. Key words: Black hole, Naked singularity, Shadow, Newman-Janis Algorithm.

Newman-Janis Algorithm without Complexification

To apply the Newman- Janis algorithm without complexification, let us consider a general form of spherically symmetric and static spacetime, in which, the
g_{tt}(r)
and
g_{rr}(r)
are inversely proportional to each other. The spacetime metric is,

ds^{2}=-A(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{B(r)}+C(r)d\Omega^{2}.

(1)

where,

(2)

and in that null coordinates, the spacetime metric (

ds^{2}=-A(r)du^{2}-2\sqrt{\frac{A(r)}{B(r)}}dudr+C(r)d\Omega^{2}.

(3)

One can write the null tetrads of the above spacetime metric as,

\displaystyle l^{\mu}=\delta^{\mu}_{1}\,,

(4)

\displaystyle n^{\mu}=\sqrt{\frac{B(r)}{A(r)}}\delta^{\mu}_{0}-\frac{1}{2}B(r)\delta^{\mu}_{1}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle m^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2C(r)}}\left(\delta^{\mu}_{2}+\frac{i}{\sin\theta}\delta^{\mu}_{3}\right)

(6)

The complexification procedure of the NJA extrapolates the function (i.e.,

\{u,r,\theta,\Phi\}

(7)

\displaystyle d\Phi=d\phi-\chi(r)dr

(8)

where we get the BLC transformation function

\displaystyle\chi(r)=\frac{a}{CB+a^{2}},

(9)

\displaystyle\lambda(r)=\frac{K+a^{2}}{CB+a^{2}},

(10)

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2f}{\rho^{2}}\right)dt^{2}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta}dr^{2}+\rho^{2}d\theta^{2}+\frac{\Sigma\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}d\phi^{2}

\displaystyle-\,\frac{4af\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}dtd\phi.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

(11)

Where,

\displaystyle\rho^{2}=K(r)+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\,,

(12)

\displaystyle f=\frac{K(r)-C(r)B(r)}{2}\,,

(13)

\displaystyle\Delta=C(r)B(r)+a^{2}\,,

(14)

\displaystyle\Sigma=\big(K(r)+a^{2}\big)^{2}-a^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta.

(15)

The rotating solution (

Rotating compact object

III.2
Kerr Black Hole Spacetime

In the Boyer- Lindquist coordinates
(t,r,\theta,\phi)
, the line element of a stationary, axisymmeric, and rotating Kerr spacetime is given by Eq. (
17
) with,

\displaystyle a=\frac{J}{M}\,,

(32)

\displaystyle f=Mr\,,

(33)

\displaystyle\rho^{2}=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\,,

(34)

\displaystyle\Delta=r^{2}-2Mr+a^{2}\,,

(35)

\displaystyle\Sigma=(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-a^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta\,.

(36)

The spin parameter

has the dimensions of length in geometrized units. As discussed above, the positions of horizons in Kerr geometry can be determined using the condition
\Delta(r)=0
, which gives

r_{\pm}=M\pm\sqrt{M^{2}-a^{2}}\,.

(37)

The surface at

represents a Cauchy horizon, while the surface at

represents the event horizon. In the extreme case

, both the horizons coincide at

. For

, the horizons become imaginary, and the singularity in the Kerr black hole becomes naked.

IV
LIGHT TRAJECTORIES IN ROTATING NAKED SINGULARITY AND KERR BLACK HOLE

A general rotating spacetime in the Boyer-Lindquist co-ordinates can be given by,

ds^{2}=-g_{tt}dt^{2}+g_{rr}dr^{2}+g_{\theta\theta}d\theta^{2}+g_{\phi\phi}d\phi^{2}-2g_{t\phi}dtd\phi\,.

(38)

In order to obtain the shadow of these compact objects, it is required to study the null geodesics in the spacetimes. The null geodesics can be derived from the Lagrangian given by

2\mathcal{L}=g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu}\,,

(39)

where the overdot represents the differentiation with respect to the affine parameter (
\lambda
) along the light geodesic. For the line element given in Eq. (
38
), Eq. (
39
) becomes

2\mathcal{L}=-g_{tt}\dot{t}^{2}+g_{rr}\dot{r}^{2}+g_{\theta\theta}\dot{\theta}^{2}+g_{\phi\phi}\dot{\phi}^{2}-2g_{t\phi}\dot{t}\dot{\phi}\,.

(40)

Setting
\theta=\pi/2
for equatorial plane in RNS (Eq. (
17
)) and Kerr black hole (Eq. (
36
)) metric, the above equation yields,

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{L}_{RNS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(1+\frac{M}{r})^{2}}\dot{t}^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{\Delta}\dot{r}^{2}+\frac{\left(a^{2}+r^{2}\right)^{2}-a^{2}\Delta}{r^{2}}\dot{\phi}^{2}-\frac{2aM(M+2r)}{(M+r)^{2}}\dot{t}\dot{\phi}\,,

(41)

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{L}_{Kerr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)\dot{t}^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{\Delta}\dot{r}^{2}+\left(r^{2}+a^{2}+\frac{2Ma^{2}}{r}\right)\dot{\phi}^{2}-\frac{4aM}{r}\dot{t}\dot{\phi}\,.

(42)

Since the Lagrangian is independent of

and

\displaystyle p_{t}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{t}}=-g_{tt}\dot{t}-g_{t\phi}\dot{\phi}=-\epsilon\,,

(43)

\displaystyle p_{\phi}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\phi}}=-g_{t\phi}\dot{t}+g_{\phi\phi}\dot{\phi}=L\,,

(44)

where

and

are, respectively, the conserved energy and angular momentum (about the axis of symmetry) of the photon evaluated at spatial infinity. The

(45)

Solving Eq. (

(47)

Four velocity

(48)

The shape of the photon orbit can be described by how

\frac{d\phi}{dr}=\frac{(Lg_{tt}+\epsilon g_{t\phi})\sqrt{g_{rr}}}{\sqrt{(g_{tt}g_{\phi\phi}+g^{2}_{t\phi})(-g_{tt}L^{2}-2\epsilon Lg_{t\phi}+\epsilon^{2}g_{\phi\phi})}}

(49)

For the simplicity, we define a new variable in above equation such that

\frac{d^{2}u}{d{\phi}^{2}}=\frac{2}{r^{3}}\left(\frac{dr}{d\phi}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{dr}{d\phi}\right)^{2}\,.

(50)

We can construct the orbit equation in the RNS and Kerr spacetimes by employing the corresponding expression of

VI
Discussion and Conclusion

In this paper, we carry out a comparative study of the properties of the lightlike geodesics and shadow in the rotating naked singularity (RNS) and Kerr black hole spacetimes. First, we obtain the stationary and axisymmetric rotating null singularity spacetime by applying the Newman-Janis algorithm without the complexification process from the static and spherically symmetric null naked singularity (NNS) spacetime. The orbit equation for null geodesics is then derived in order to plot the light trajectories around RNS and Kerr black holes. From the Hamilton-Jacobi formalism, we obtain the separable solution of radial
(R(r))
and azimuthal
(\Theta(\theta))
parts of the effective potential. Using the conditions of unstable circular orbits, we have studied the equatorial prograde and retrograde photon orbits.
The following are the outcomes of this paper:

•

In the Kerr black hole, there are two horizons and two stationary limit surfaces. However, no such horizons or stationary limit surfaces are present in the RNS spacetime. Therefore, the ergoregion does not exist in an RNS.

•

We have studied the properties like nature of singularity, light trajectory, and shadow shape due to the photon sphere of RNS. As per the given spacetime in
Joshi2020
, the static null naked singularity spacetime contains the null like singularity. Whereas, due to the inclusion of non-zero spin, the RNS spacetime has the time like singularity. Furthermore, there is no photon sphere for static null singularity
Joshi2020
. In the case of RNS spacetime, however, a photon sphere exists for the retrograde motion of the null geodesics.

•

In Fig. (
2(b)
), we can see that there are two radii of the photon sphere in the equatorial plane of the Kerr black hole. One radius corresponds to the prograde motion of light geodesics, which has a positive value of
\xi
when
\eta
= 0. Another radius is for a retrograde motion of light, where the value of
\xi
is negative when
\eta
= 0. Besides that, for an RNS in Fig. (
2(a)
), there exists only one photon sphere in the equatorial plane, and that is for retrograde motion of light geodesics. As a result, in the case of an RNS, all equatorial light rays travelling in prograde motion will be scattered by the central singularity. While for the retrograde light orbits, light geodesics with impact parameters smaller than the critical impact parameter will fall into the singularity. This can be understood from the behavior of light trajectories shown in Fig. (
3
).

•

We know that the rotating gravitational sources drag the surrounding spacetime. This dragging effect can be observed from the deflection of light geodesics in Fig. (
3
). From Fig. (
3(b)
), it is apparent that the dragging effect is largest near the outer event horizon of the Kerr black hole, causing the retrograde light orbits to change their direction of motion in the direction of the spin of the Kerr black hole. Whereas for RNS spacetime, the dragging effect is most significant around the ring singularity, as seen in Fig. (
3
).

•

The RNS casts an arc shape shadow which can be seen in Fig. (
4(a)
). That is because of the particular occurrence of the photon sphere around RNS. The shadow in Fig. (
4(b)
) is cast by Kerr black hole. In both cases, shadow shape is shown for different spin parameters indicated by blue to magenta color lines are formed for the spin parameters 0.9 to 0.1, respectively. The shadow cast by RNS is almost half of that of the Kerr black hole. With the increasing spin, the shadow size in RNS increases, whereas in Kerr black hole, the deformation in the shadow shape increases.

In this work, we restricted ourselves to examining the shadow shape due to the presence of the photon sphere in RNS spacetime. In general, one may show that in the absence of photon sphere, the shadow can be cast by a rotating compact object due to the existence of the ring singularity.

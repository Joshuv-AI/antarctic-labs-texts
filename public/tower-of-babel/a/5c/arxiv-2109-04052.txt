Abstract
. We discuss the continuum limit of discrete Dirac operators on the square lattice in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} as the mesh size tends to zero. To this end, we propose the most natural and simplest embedding of ℓ 2 ​ ( ℤ h d ) \ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{d}) into L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) , which enables us to compare the discrete Dirac operators with the continuum Dirac operators in the same Hilbert space L 2 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) 2 L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2} . In particular, we prove that the discrete Dirac operators converge to the continuum Dirac operators in the strong resolvent sense. Potentials are assumed to be bounded and uniformly continuous functions on ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} and allowed to be complex matrix-valued. We also prove that the discrete Dirac operators do not converge to the continuum Dirac operators in the norm resolvent sense. This is closely related to the observation that the Liouville theorem does not hold in discrete complex analysis.

1.
Introduction

This paper is concerned with
the discrete Dirac operator
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}+V_{h}
defined by

(1.1)

{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}+V_{h}=\begin{pmatrix}m&i\partial_{1,h}^{*}+\partial_{2,h}^{*}\\
-i\partial_{1,h}+\partial_{2,h}&-m\end{pmatrix}+V_{h}\text{ \; in \;}\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2},

which is a discrete
analogue of the two-dimensional Dirac operator defined by

(1.2)

{\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V=-i\sigma_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}-i\sigma_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}+m\sigma_{3}+V(x)\;\text{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2},

where
m\geq 0
and
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
,
\sigma_{3}
are the Pauli matrices,

(1.3)

\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},

and
V
is a complex matrix valued function.
For the definition of
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2}
,
see (
2.1
) in section
2
;
the finite difference operators
\partial_{j,h}
and
\partial_{j,h}^{*}
(
j\in\{1,\,2\}
)
are defined in (
2.3
)
and (
2.4
) in section
2
,
respectively;
for
V_{h}
,
see (
5.3
) in section
5
.

We remark that both operators
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}
in
(
1.1
) and
{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
in (
1.2
) possess supersymmetry structure
(see
[
28
, Chapter 5]
,
[
29
, Chapter 3]
).
The discrete Dirac operator (
1.1
) can be rewritten
in a form analogous to (
1.2
),

{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}=-i\sigma_{1}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{1,h}&0\\
0&-\partial_{1,h}^{*}\end{pmatrix}-i\sigma_{2}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{2,h}&0\\
0&-\partial_{2,h}^{*}\end{pmatrix}+m\sigma_{3}+V_{h}.

It is widely recognized that 2D Dirac operators, especially in the massless case,
have been the object of extensive research in the context of graphene since
its discovery in 2004, see
[
5
]
or
[
21
]
for an exposition.
In particular, we would like to mention the work

[
20
]
, which reported that electron transport in graphene is essentially governed by a massless Dirac equation
and that a variety
of unusual phenomena are characteristic of two-dimensional Dirac fermions.
These are the main reasons why we focus on the two-dimensional case,
although
it is apparent that the methods and ideas to be developed below in the present paper
are directly applicable to the one-dimensional and the three-dimensional cases.
The discussions in these two cases will appear elsewhere.

It is natural to make an attempt to show that the discrete operator (
1.1
)
converges to the continuum operator (
1.2
)
as the mesh size
h
of the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2}
tends to
0
.
However, there is a difficulty
in that these two operators work in completely different Hilbert spaces.
For example, it is not immediately obvious how one can make sense of the expression

({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}+V_{h})-({\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V)
.
For this reason,
it is necessary to embed
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2}

onto an appropriate subspace of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}
.

In this paper, we propose a simple and natural
embedding of

\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})

into
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by assigning to each element in

\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})

a step function in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
:

(1.4)

[J_{h}f](x):=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}f(hn)\,{\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}\!(x)\qquad(f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d}))

where
{\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}
is a characteristic function of the set

I_{n,h}:=\{x\,|\,hn_{j}\leq x_{j}<h(n_{j}+1),\ j\in\{1,\cdots,d\}\}

(see subsection
2.1
).
We find it is important that
the discrete Fourier transform can be naturally defined for
J_{h}f
(see subsection
2.2
).
Also, the use of
step functions is desirable
from the point of view of numerical analysis.
This idea of embedding

\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})

into
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

induces a subspace

L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
With this embedding, one can naturally define the difference operators

\partial_{j,h}
and
\partial_{j,h}^{*}
in

L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
,
the subspace of step functions of the form (
1.4
)
(cf. subsection
2.1
),
and hence the discrete Dirac operators
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2}
.
For the reasons mentioned here,
the discrete Dirac operators

{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2}

are the exact counterparts of
the discrete Dirac operators
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}

in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2}
, so we can identify these two operators.
In other words,
we are able to
regard the discrete Dirac operators
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}+V_{h}
as
an operator acting in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}

with domain
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})^{2}
,
and able to compare the discrete Dirac operators
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}+V_{h}
with
the continuum Dirac operators
{\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V

in the same Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}
.
The purpose of the present paper is to show,
with the embedding operator
defined by (
1.4
),
that the resolvents of the discrete Dirac operators
(
1.1
)
converge to the continuum
Dirac operators (
1.2
) in the strong resolvent sense as the mesh
size
h
tends to
0

(see Theorem
4.2
in section
4
and
Theorem
5.1
in section
5
).
In addition, we show that the discrete operator
\mathbb{D}_{m,h}
does not
converge to the continuum operator
\mathbb{D}_{m}
in the norm resolvent sense (see Theorem
4.3

in section
4
).
As a motivation for the proof of the latter theorem, we observe that the Liouville theorem does not hold in discrete
complex analysis (see Remark
4.4
in section
4
).

In connection with the embedding operator (
1.4
),
we would like to mention the works by
[
7
]
and
[
19
]
, in which
the embedding operators are defined by

(1.5)

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\rho\big((x-hn)/h\big)f(hn)\qquad(f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})),

with
\rho
a smooth and (possibly rapidly) decreasing function.
However, in this paper, we do not adopt this type of
embedding of
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})

into
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, because of the following three
reasons.
Firstly, the embedding operator (
1.5
) depends on the choice of the function
\rho
.
Secondly, the embedded
functions defined by (
1.5
) are smooth, and no longer
discrete objects. In fact, the discrete Fourier transform is not applicable to smooth functions.
Thirdly,
the difference operators working on smooth functions
can be regarded as a mixture of discreteness and continuum, and
may not
be regarded as the exact counterparts of difference operators in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})
.
On the other hand,
as was pointed out in
[
7
]
,
it is inevitable to introduce the embedding operator
(
1.5
)
and a modification of the discrete Dirac operators in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2}

if one would like to show
the norm resolvent convergence.

We should like to remark that if one replaces the function
\rho
with the characteristic
function
{\chi}_{{}_{I_{0,1}}}

(i.e.,
{\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}
with
n=0
,
h=1
)
then the embedding operator (
1.5
) coincides with
our embedding operator
(
1.4
).

When we apply our idea to the discrete Dirac operator (
1.1
)
to discuss the continuum limit
as the mesh size
h
tends to
0
,
we require a convergence theorem for the orthogonal projection
P_{h}

onto
the closed subspace

L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2}
of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}
.
Also, we
require a discrete Fourier transform on

L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})
(which is
essentially the same,
but not identical to the Fourier series with coefficients in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})
),
and need to prove a convergence theorem for the
discrete Fourier transform as
h\to 0
.
Indeed, we will establish both convergence theorems
in the strong topology of

L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Precise descriptions are given in subsection

2.2
and section
3
.
With these strong convergence theorems, we can prove that the resolvents of the discrete Dirac operator
(
1.1
) strongly converge to those
of the Dirac operator (
1.2
)
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}
.

2.
Embedding of
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
into
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and discrete Fourier transform in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

In applications to the Dirac operator (
1.1
), the underlying Hilbert space is
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2}:=\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
,
which consists of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
-valued functions on the
2
-dimensional lattice.
In this section, we focus on the space of complex-valued functions on the
d
-dimensional lattice,

\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
, and related spaces of functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
; the results naturally extend to the corresponding spaces of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
-valued functions.

The
d
-dimensional square lattice with the mesh size
h>0
is denoted by

{\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d}:=\{\,hn\,\big|\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\}.

The Hilbert space

(2.1)

\displaystyle{}\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d}):=\big\{\,f\,\big|\,f:{\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d}\to\mathbb{C},\;\;\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|f(hn)|^{2}<\infty\,\big\},

has the standard inner product

(2.2)

\big(f,\,g\big)_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}f(hn)\,\overline{g(hn)}.

For
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
, define

(2.3)

[\partial_{j,h}f](hn):=\frac{1}{h}\Big\{f(h(n+e_{j}))-f(hn)\Big\}\quad(j\in\{1,\dots,d\}),

where
e_{1}=(1,\,0,\,\dots,0),\dots,e_{d}=(0,\,\dots,0,\,1)
.
The adjoint of
\partial_{j,h}
is
given by

(2.4)

[\partial_{j,h}^{*}f](hn):=\frac{1}{h}\Big\{f(h(n-e_{j}))-f(hn)\Big\}\quad(j\in\{1,\dots,d\}),

so

(2.5)

\big(\partial_{j,h}f,\,g\big)_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})}=\big(f,\,\partial_{j,h}^{*}g\big)_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})},\quad\forall f,g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d}).

2.1.
Embedding of
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
into
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

We introduce an embedding of
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
into
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by assigning
to
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
the step function

(2.6)

[J_{h}f](x):=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}f(hn)\,{\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}\!(x)\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

where
{\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}
is a characteristic function of the set

(2.7)

I_{n,h}:=\{x\,|\,hn_{j}\leq x_{j}<h(n_{j}+1),\ j\in\{1,\cdots,d\}\};

clearly,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}{\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}\equiv 1
.
Note that

(2.8)

\|J_{h}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=h^{d/2}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})},

so
h^{-d/2}J_{h}
is an isometry from
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
into
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Since

\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
is a Hilbert space,
the image
J_{h}\big(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})\big)
is
a closed subspace of the Hilbert space

L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
We thus have an orthogonal decomposition

(2.9)

L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})=L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})\oplus L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})^{\perp},\qquad L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d}):=J_{h}\big(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})\big).

(The notation
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
already appeared in
[
14
]
, but there it essentially denotes

\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
; for details, see
[
14
, Subsection 2.1]
.)
The orthogonal projection
P_{h}
of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
onto
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})

can be described, for general
\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, as

P_{h}\varphi=J_{h}\tilde{\varphi}
, where

(2.10)

\tilde{\varphi}(hn)=\frac{1}{h^{d}}\int_{I_{n,h}}\varphi(x)\,dx\qquad(n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

Remark 2.1
.

The following example illustrates the action of the projection
P_{h}
. Let
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be
defined by
\varphi(x)=0\ (|x|\geq 2h)
,
\varphi(x)=x+2h\ (-2h<x<-h)
,

\varphi(x)=h\ (|x|\leq h)
,
and
\varphi(x)=-x+2h\ (h<x<2h)
.
This function can be decomposed as
\varphi=f_{h}+g_{h}
with

f_{h}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h&\mbox{if}\ -h\leq x<h,\\
\frac{h}{2}&\mbox{if}\ -2h\leq x<-h\ \mbox{or}\ h\leq x<2h,\\
0&\mbox{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and

g_{h}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x+\frac{3h}{2}&\mbox{if}\ -2h\leq x<-h,\\
-x+\frac{3h}{2}&\mbox{if}\ h\leq x<2h,\\
0&\ \mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

It is easy to see that
f_{h}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h})
and

g_{h}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h})^{\bot}
, so

P_{h}\varphi=f_{h}
.

As the above embedding gives a one-to-one relationship between the elements of
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
and of
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
,
we can define the finite difference operators
\partial_{j,h}
and
\partial_{j,h}^{*}
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
by
applying them, as defined in (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), to the corresponding
element of
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
, such that

(2.11)

\partial_{j,h}J_{h}[f]:=J_{h}[\partial_{j,h}f]\qquad\partial_{j,h}^{*}J_{h}[f]:=J_{h}[\partial_{j,h}^{*}f]\qquad(f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})).

Then we again have

(2.12)

\big(\partial_{j,h}f,\,g\big)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})}=\big(f,\,\partial_{j,h}^{*}g\big)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})}\qquad(f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})).

in analogy to (
2.5
).

2.2.
Discrete Fourier transform

Let

(2.13)

{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d}=[-\pi/h,\,\pi/h]^{d}=\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mid|\xi|_{\infty}\leq\pi/h\},

where

|\xi|_{\infty}=\max\{|\xi_{1}|,\dots,|\xi_{d}|\}
.
(Although we use a notation alluding to the interpretation, natural in the following, of this set as a flat

d
-dimensional torus of side length
2\pi/h
, we emphasize that it is a bounded interval in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.)
As the functions

e_{n}(\xi)=\left(\frac{h}{2\pi}\right)^{d/2}\,e^{-ihn\cdot\xi}\qquad(\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d};n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d})

form an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d})
, any function
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
serves as a collection of Fourier
coefficients for a
d
-dimensional Fourier series in
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d})
,

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}f(hn)\,e_{n}(\xi)\qquad(\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d}).

In view of the bijection between
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
and
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
, this motivates the following definition of
a
discrete Fourier transform

{\mathcal{F}}_{h}:L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})\rightarrow L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d})
,

\displaystyle[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}J_{h}f](\xi)

\displaystyle:=h^{d/2}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}f(hn)e_{n}(\xi)

(2.14)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}e^{-ihn\cdot\xi}f(hn)\,{\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}(x)\,dx\quad(\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d})

for
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
.
By Parseval’s identity for the orthonormal basis
\{e_{n}\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\}
,

(2.15)

\|{\mathcal{F}}_{h}J_{h}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d})}^{2}=h^{d}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|f(hn)|^{2}=h^{d}\,\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})}^{2}=\|J_{h}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})}^{2}

for any
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
, so
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
is a unitary operator.

Its inverse is the operator

{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}:L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d})\rightarrow L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
,

(2.16)

[{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}u](x)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\Big\{\!(2\pi)^{-d/2}\!\int_{{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d}}\!e^{ihn\cdot\xi}\,u(\xi)\,d\xi\Big\}{\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}\!(x)\quad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

for
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d})
.

By direct computations, we have

(2.17)

[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}(\partial_{j,h}f)](\xi)=\frac{1}{h}(e^{ih\xi_{j}}-1)[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}f](\xi)\qquad(f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d}))

and

(2.18)

[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}(\partial_{j,h}^{*}f)](\xi)=\frac{1}{h}(e^{-ih\xi_{j}}-1)[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}f](\xi)\qquad(f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})).

Extending functions by 0 outside
{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d}
, the space
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d})
naturally forms a closed subspace of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
;
it is the range of
the orthogonal projection
Q_{1\!/h}
defined as the operator of multiplication with the characteristic function of
{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d}
.

Using the projections
P_{h}\in\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

Q_{\!1\!/h}\in\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
, we can
extend
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
and its inverse
{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}

to become elements of
\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))

by setting

(2.19)

{\mathcal{F}}_{h}:=Q_{\!1\!/h}\,{\mathcal{F}}_{h}P_{h},\quad{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}:=P_{h}{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}Q_{\!1\!/h}.

Here
\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
denotes the Banach space of all bounded linear operators in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
equipped with the uniform operator topology.
Note that
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
is a partial isometry from
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

with the initial set
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})

and
the final set
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d})
, and that
{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}

is a partial isometry from
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

with the initial set
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d})

and
the final set
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
.
Clearly,

(2.20)

{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}{\mathcal{F}}_{h}=P_{h},\qquad{\mathcal{F}}_{h}{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}=Q_{\!1\!/h},

and

(2.21)

P_{h}^{\perp}{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}=\mathbf{0},\quad Q_{\!1\!/h}^{\perp}{\mathcal{F}}_{h}=\mathbf{0}.

3.
Convergence of discrete Fourier transforms

As in the previous section, we will work in
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions on
d
dimensional Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing functions,
we define

(3.1)

\varphi_{h}(x)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\varphi(hn){\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}\!(x).

It is clear that
\varphi_{h}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
and
P_{h}\varphi_{h}=\varphi_{h}
. However, we emphasize that

P_{h}\varphi\not=\varphi_{h}
in general.
In fact, for each
h>0
one can easily choose a function

\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that

(\varphi_{h},\,\varphi-\varphi_{h})_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\not=0
.

Remark 3.1
.

As was noted in (
2.8
), we have

\|\varphi_{h}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=h^{d/2}\|\varphi_{h}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(i)

There exists a constant
C_{\!\varphi k}
, depending only on

\varphi
and
k
, such that

(3.2)

|\varphi_{h}(x)-\varphi(x)|\leq C_{\!\varphi k}\langle x\rangle^{-k}h\qquad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,0<h<\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}),

where
\langle x\rangle=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}
.
In particular,
\varphi_{h}
is rapidly decreasing, i.e.,
|\varphi_{h}(x)|\leq C_{\varphi k}\langle x\rangle^{-k}
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(ii)

There exists a constant
C_{\!\varphi}
, depending only on

\varphi
, such that

(3.3)

\|\varphi_{h}-\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{\!\varphi}h\qquad(0<h<\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}).

Proof.

We prove statement (i); then statement (ii) follows as a straightforward consequence, taking
k>d/2
.
Let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Then, for
x\in I_{n,h}
,

\varphi(hn)-\varphi(x)=(hn-x)\cdot\!\int_{0}^{1}\big(\nabla\varphi\big)(t(hn-x)+x)\,dt.

Consequently,

\displaystyle|\varphi(hn)-\varphi(x)|\,\langle x\rangle^{k}

\displaystyle\leq|hn-x|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\langle x\rangle^{k}}{\langle t(hn-x)+x\rangle^{k}}\big|\big(\nabla\varphi\big)(t(hn-x)+x)\big|\langle t(hn-x)+x\rangle^{k}dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|(\nabla\varphi)(y)\right|\langle y\rangle^{k}\right)\,|hn-x|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\langle x\rangle^{k}}{\langle t(hn-x)+x\rangle^{k}}dt.

Now if
|x|\geq 2\sqrt{d}h
, then

|t(hn-x)+x|\geq|x|-|hn-x|\geq|x|-\sqrt{d}h\geq\frac{|x|}{2},

so

\langle t(hn-x)+x\rangle\geq\sqrt{1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}=\sqrt{1+\frac{\langle x\rangle^{2}-1}{4}}\geq\frac{\langle x\rangle}{2};

if
|x|<2\sqrt{d}h
, then (trivially)
\langle t(hn-x)+x\rangle\geq 1
and

\langle x\rangle<\sqrt{1+4dh^{2}}<\sqrt{5}
.
In either case,

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\langle x\rangle^{k}}{\langle t(hn-x)+x\rangle^{k}}\,dt\leq\sqrt{5}^{k},

and, noting that
|hn-x|\leq\sqrt{d}h
, the inequality (
3.2
) follows.

\blacksquare

The statement of Lemma
3.1
(i) has the following immediate consequence.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
There exists a constant
C_{\!\varphi k}
, depending only on

\varphi
and
k
, such that

(3.4)

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|\varphi(hn)|\,{\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}(x)\leq|\varphi(x)|+C_{\!\varphi k}\,h\langle x\rangle^{-k}\qquad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d})

for
0<h<1/\sqrt{d}
.

In what follows, we shall use the notation

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{h}^{\,\rm step}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

\displaystyle=\{\;\varphi_{h}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\varphi(hn){\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}\;|\;\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\,\},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{0+}^{\,\rm step}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{h>0}\mathcal{S}_{h}^{\,\rm step}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

Note that, unlike
\mathcal{S}_{h}^{\,\rm step}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the set of functions
\mathcal{S}_{0+}^{\,\rm step}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is not a vector space.
As we shall see in Lemma
3.4
in subsection
3.1
,
each
\varphi_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{0+}^{\,\rm step}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

allows an explicit expression of its Fourier transform
in a certain sense.

The following lemma is a direct consequence of Lemma
3.1
(ii).

Lemma 3.2
.

\mathcal{S}_{0+}^{\,\rm step}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a dense subset of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

We can now prove the strong convergence of the orthogonal projectors
P_{h}
and
Q_{1/h}
to the identity.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
\|P_{h}u-u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\to 0
and
\|Q_{1/h}u-u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\to 0
as
h\to 0
.

Proof.

Since
\|P_{h}\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}=1
for all
h>0
and

\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is dense in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
it is sufficient to prove
that
for
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\|P_{h}\varphi-\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\to 0
as
h\to 0
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and, for each
h>0
, let
\varphi_{h}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{d})
be the step function defined in (
3.1
).
Then
P_{h}\varphi_{h}=\varphi_{h}
, so

\displaystyle\|P_{h}\varphi-\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{h}\varphi-P_{h}\varphi_{h}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\|\varphi_{h}-\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|P_{h}\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}+1\right)\,\|\varphi_{h}-\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\rightarrow 0\qquad(h\rightarrow 0)

by Lemma
3.1
(ii).

Furthermore, for any
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

\|Q_{1/h}u-u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d}}|u|^{2}\rightarrow 0\qquad(h\rightarrow 0).

\blacksquare

Remark 3.2
.

It is clear from the proof of Lemma
3.3
that
the projection
Q_{1/h}
does not
converge to the identity operator in the operator norm as
h\rightarrow 0
.
The same is true for the projection
P_{h}
.
Indeed, for any
h>0
there is a function
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1
and
\int_{I_{n,h}}\varphi=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
e.g.

\varphi(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}c_{n}\chi_{I_{n,h}}(x)\prod_{j=1}^{d}(x_{j}-h(n_{j}+\frac{1}{2}))\qquad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{d})

with suitable

(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
.
Then, using (
2.10
),
we find

\|P_{h}\varphi-\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|0-\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1,

so
\|P_{h}-I\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\geq 1
for any
h>0
.

3.1.
Convergence of
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
and
{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}

As usual, the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and its inverse
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
arise by extension
of the integral operators

(3.7)

[\mathcal{F}\varphi](\xi)=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\varphi(x)\,dx\qquad(\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d};\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))

and

(3.8)

[\overline{\mathcal{F}}\varphi](x)=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\varphi(\xi)\,d\xi\qquad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d};\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})),

respectively.
We emphasize that we can compare
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
with
\mathcal{F}
and
{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}
with
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
on

L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, as we have extended the discrete Fourier transform and its inverse to all of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

in (
2.19
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\varphi_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{0+}^{\,\rm step}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Then

[\mathcal{F}\varphi_{h}](\xi)=\Big\{(2\pi)^{-d/2}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\varphi(hn)\,h^{d}e^{-ihn\cdot\xi}\Big\}\,\displaystyle{\prod_{j=1}^{d}}a(h\xi_{j})\qquad(\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

where

a(\theta)=\dfrac{1-e^{-i\theta}}{i\theta}\;\;\;(\theta\in\mathbb{R}).

Proof.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and
h>0
.
A direct computation shows that

(3.9)

\int_{I_{\!n\!,\!\,h}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}dx=\prod_{j=1}^{d}e^{-ihn_{j}\xi_{j}}\frac{e^{-ih\xi_{j}}-1}{-i\xi_{j}}=h^{d}e^{-ihn\cdot\xi}\prod_{j=1}^{d}a(h\xi_{j}).

We then have

(3.10)

\begin{split}[\mathcal{F}\varphi_{h}](\xi)&=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\varphi(hn)\int_{I_{\!n\!,\!\,h}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}dx\\
&=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\varphi(hn)\,h^{d}e^{-ihn\cdot\xi}\prod_{j=1}^{d}a(h\xi_{j}).\\
\end{split}

This completes the proof.
\blacksquare

An immediate consequence of (
2.14
) and Lemma
3.4
is the following
corollary, which we expect will be useful from the view point of numerical analysis
of discrete approximations of Fourier transform.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\varphi_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{0+}^{\,\rm step}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Then

(3.11)

[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}\varphi_{h}-\mathcal{F}\varphi_{h}](\xi)=\Big(1-\prod_{j=1}^{d}a(h\xi_{j})\Big)[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}\varphi_{h}](\xi)\qquad(\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d}).

Remark 3.3
.

Note that
a(\theta)\to 1
as
\theta\to 0
, and that
|a(\theta)|\leq 1
for all
\theta
.

Remark 3.4
.

One can deduce that
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}\varphi
converges locally in
L^{2}
to
\mathcal{F}\varphi
for any
\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

in the following manner.

Since
\|{\mathcal{F}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}=1
for all
h>0
, and
since
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is dense in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
it is sufficient to prove the local convergence in
L^{2}
for
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
let
\varphi_{h}
be given by (
3.1
).
Then, by (
2.14
) and (
3.4
),

(3.12)

\begin{split}|[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}\varphi_{h}](\xi)|&=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}e^{-ihn\cdot\xi}\varphi(hn)\,{\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}\!(x)\,dx\Big|\\
&\leq(2\pi)^{-d/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|\varphi(hn)|\,{\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}\!(x)\,dx\\
&\leq(2\pi)^{-d/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(|\varphi(x)|+{\rm const}_{\!\varphi}\,\langle x\rangle^{-d-1}\big)\,dx=:C_{\varphi}<\infty.\end{split}

Taken together with Corollary
3.2
, this estimate implies that

\|[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}-\mathcal{F}]\varphi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)}\leq C_{\varphi}\sup_{\xi\in K}\Big|1-\prod_{j=1}^{d}a(h\xi_{j})\Big|\to 0\quad\text{ as }h\to 0

for any compact subset
K
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
The decomposition

(3.13)

[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}-\mathcal{F}]\varphi={\mathcal{F}}_{h}(\varphi-\varphi_{h})+[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}-\mathcal{F}]\varphi_{h}+\mathcal{F}(\varphi_{h}-\varphi),

together with Lemma
3.1
(ii),
gives the local convergence in
L^{2}
. However, Lemma
3.6
below
shows a stronger convergence of
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
.

We close section
3
with the (fairly straightforward) proofs of convergences of

{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
respectively.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\|{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}u-\overline{\mathcal{F}}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\to 0
as
h\to 0
.

Proof.

Since
\|{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}=1
for all
h>0
, and
since
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
it is sufficient to prove
the assertion for
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Let
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and choose
h_{*}>0
so that

{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d}\supset\text{supp}[u]
for all
h\in(0,\,h_{*})
.
Then we have, by (
2.16
) and by the fact that
{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1\!/h}^{d}\supset\text{supp}[u]
,

\displaystyle{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}u

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\Big\{\!(2\pi)^{-d/2}\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\!e^{ihn\cdot\xi}\,u(\xi)\,d\xi\Big\}{\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}\!

(3.14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\varphi(hn){\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}\!

for all
h\in(0,\,h_{*})
, where we set
\varphi:=\overline{\mathcal{F}}u
. This equality implies that

(3.15)

|{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}u(x)-\overline{\mathcal{F}}u(x)|^{2}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|\varphi(hn)-\varphi(x)|^{2}{\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}\!(x)\qquad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

In view of the fact that
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, it follows from (
3.15
) and
Lemma
3.1
(i) that

\|{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}u-\overline{\mathcal{F}}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\to 0
as
h\to 0
.

\blacksquare

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

(3.16)

\|\mathcal{F}_{h}\varphi-\mathcal{F}\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\to 0\;\mbox{ as }\;h\to 0.

Proof.

We first prove that for any
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

(3.17)

\|[\mathcal{F}_{h}-\mathcal{F}\,]\,\overline{\mathcal{F}}Q_{1/h}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\to 0\;\mbox{ as }\;h\to 0.

In fact, we see that the left hand side of (
3.17
) is equal to

(3.18)

\|\mathcal{F}_{h}\overline{\mathcal{F}}Q_{1/h}u-Q_{1/h}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

which is bounded by

(3.19)

\|\overline{\mathcal{F}}Q_{1/h}u-{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Here we have used the fact that
Q_{\!1\!/h}={\mathcal{F}}_{h}{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}
(recall (
2.20
),
and the fact that

\|{\mathcal{F}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}=1
.
Since

Q_{1/h}u=(Q_{1/h}u-u)+u,

(
3.19
) is estimated by

\|Q_{1/h}u-u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\|\overline{\mathcal{F}}u-{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
.
The fact (
3.17
) now follows from Lemmas
3.5
and
3.3
.

We next prove (
3.16
).
For
\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we put
u=\mathcal{F}\varphi
. We decompose

\varphi=\overline{\mathcal{F}}Q_{1/h}u+\overline{\mathcal{F}}(u-Q_{1/h}u).

We then see that

(3.20)

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{F}_{h}\varphi-\mathcal{F}\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}&\leq\|(\mathcal{F}_{h}-\mathcal{F})\overline{\mathcal{F}}Q_{1/h}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\\
&\qquad+\|(\mathcal{F}_{h}-\mathcal{F})\overline{\mathcal{F}}(u-Q_{1/h}u)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.\end{split}

Then it is clear that
(
3.16
) follows from (
3.17
), the fact that

(3.21)

\|({\mathcal{F}}_{h}-\mathcal{F})\overline{\mathcal{F}}\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq 2,

and Lemma
3.3
.
\blacksquare

4.
Resolvent convergences of
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}

The continuum Dirac operator we shall consider in this section
is

(4.1)

{\mathbb{D}}_{m}=-i\sigma_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}-i\sigma_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}+m\sigma_{3}\;\text{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2},

where
m\geq 0
and

(4.2)

\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

It is well-known that
{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
is a self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}

with domain
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}
,
the Sobolev space of order
1
of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
-valued functions.

The discrete Dirac operator
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}

we shall consider is

(4.3)

{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}=\begin{pmatrix}m&i\partial_{1,h}^{*}+\partial_{2,h}^{*}\\
-i\partial_{1,h}+\partial_{2,h}&-m\end{pmatrix}\text{ in }L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2},

where difference operators
\partial_{j,h}
and
\partial_{j,h}^{*}
(
j\in\{1,\,2\}
) are as defined in (
2.11
).
It is evident that
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}
is a bounded self-adjoint operator in

L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2}
.
We mention in passing that (
4.3
) is a Dirac operator with supersymmetry
in the abstract sense (see
[
28
, Chapter 5]
).
It can be rewritten
in the form

{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}=-i\sigma_{1}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{1,h}&0\\
0&-\partial_{1,h}^{*}\end{pmatrix}-i\sigma_{2}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{2,h}&0\\
0&-\partial_{2,h}^{*}\end{pmatrix}+m\sigma_{3},

which is comparable with (
1.2
).

In accordance with the decomposition (
2.9
) of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
we can compare
{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
and
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}

in the same Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}
, where
\mathbf{0}_{h}
is the null operator on
(L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{2})^{2})^{\bot}
.
In particular, we investigate the difference

(4.4)

({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\quad\mbox{as }h\to 0.

We define

\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}:=\mathcal{F}{\mathbb{D}}_{m}\overline{\mathcal{F}}
, which is the operator of multiplication with the matrix-valued function

(4.5)

\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}m&\xi_{1}-i\xi_{2}\\
\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2}&-m\end{pmatrix}\quad(\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2})

in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}
.

With help of (
2.17
), (
2.18
) and (
4.3
), we also define

\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}:={\mathcal{F}}_{h}{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}
,
where we abbreviate
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}:={\mathcal{F}}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}\in\mathbf{B}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2},L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2})^{2}\big)

and
{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}:={\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}\in\mathbf{B}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2})^{2},L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2}\big)
.
The operator
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}\in\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2})^{2})
is the operator of multiplication with the matrix-valued function

(4.6)

\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}m&\dfrac{i(e^{-ih\xi_{1}}-1)+(e^{-ih\xi_{2}}-1)}{h}\\
\dfrac{-i(e^{ih\xi_{1}}-1)+(e^{ih\xi_{2}}-1)}{h}&-m\end{pmatrix}

(\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2})
.
With the notation of (
2.19
), we see that

(4.7)

{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}={\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}){\mathcal{F}}_{h}\;\mbox{ in }L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}.

Therefore, the difference in (
4.4
) can be written as

(4.8)

\begin{split}&({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\\
&\quad={\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}\big((\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}){\mathcal{F}}_{h}-\overline{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\mathcal{F}.\end{split}

To investigate the matrices
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)
and
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)
, we start by noting the following.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
m\geq 0
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
, and assume that either
m>0
or
\zeta\not=0
.
Then, with the unitary matrix

(4.9)

U_{m}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,}\sqrt{\mu_{m}(\zeta)^{2}+m\mu_{m}(\zeta)}}\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{m}(\zeta)+m&-\overline{\zeta}\\
\zeta&\mu_{m}(\zeta)+m\end{pmatrix},

where
\mu_{m}(\zeta)=\sqrt{|\zeta|^{2}+m^{2}}
, we have

(4.10)

U_{m}(\zeta)^{*}\!\begin{pmatrix}m&\overline{\zeta}\,\\
\zeta&-m\,\end{pmatrix}U_{m}(\zeta)=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{m}(\zeta)&0\,\\
0&-\mu_{m}(\zeta)\,\end{pmatrix}.

Note that the columns of the matrix on the right hand side of (
4.9
) are the eigenvectors of the matrix

(4.11)

\begin{pmatrix}m&\overline{\zeta}\,\\
\zeta&-m\,\end{pmatrix}.

Applying Lemma
4.1
to the matrix
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)
in (
4.5
) with
\zeta=\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2}
, we see that
the eigenvalues of the matrix
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)
are given by

(4.12)

\pm\lambda_{m}(\xi):=\pm\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}\,},

and that the unitary transformation defined by

U_{m}(\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2})^{*}[\mathcal{F}\varphi](\xi)

(\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2})
brings
the Dirac operator
{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
in (
1.2
) into the form of
the operator of multiplication with the diagonal matrix-valued function

(4.13)

\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{m}(\xi)&0\,\\
0&-\lambda_{m}(\xi)\,\end{pmatrix}

in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}
.
This unitary transformation is a two-dimensional
version of the Foldy-Wouthuysen-Tani transformation;
see
[
10
]
,
[
28
, Section 1.4]
,
[
3
, Section2.1]
.
As is well-known,
one can infer from (
4.13
) that the Dirac operator
{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
is absolutely continuous and that its
spectrum is given by

\sigma({\mathbb{D}}_{m})=(-\infty,\,-m]\cup[m,\,\infty)
.

In a similar manner,
applying Lemma
4.1
to the matrix
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)
in (
4.6
)
with

(4.14)

\zeta=\dfrac{-i(e^{ih\xi_{1}}-1)+(e^{ih\xi_{2}}-1)}{h},

we see that
the eigenvalues of the matrix
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)
are given by

(4.15)

\pm\lambda_{m,h}(\xi):=\pm\sqrt{h^{-2}\omega(h\xi)+m^{2}},

where

(4.16)

\begin{split}\omega(\xi)&=4+2\sin(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})-2(\sin\xi_{1}+\cos\xi_{1})+2(\sin\xi_{2}-\cos\xi_{2})\\
&=2(1-\cos\xi_{1})(1+\sin\xi_{2})+2(1-\cos\xi_{2})(1-\sin\xi_{1}).\end{split}

Then the unitary transformation defined by

U_{m}(\zeta)^{*}[{\mathcal{F}}_{h}f](\xi)

{(f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2})}
, with
\zeta
as specified in
4.14
, brings
the discrete Dirac operator
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}
in (
4.3
) into the form of
the operator of multiplication with the diagonal matrix-valued function

(4.17)

\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{m,h}(\xi)&0\,\\
0&-\lambda_{m,h}(\xi)\,\end{pmatrix}

in
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2})^{2}
.

Remark 4.1
.

It is interesting that the Laplacian on the hexagonal lattice considered in
[
14
]
has a
similar form to the discrete Dirac operator (
4.3
) in the
massless case
m=0
. As a result, a function similar to
\omega(\xi)
appears in

[
14
, subsection 8.1]
.

Simple calculations show that for
\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{1}

\begin{split}&\nabla\omega(\xi)=0\Longleftrightarrow\\
&\quad\xi\in\left\{(0,\,0),\,\Big(\frac{\pi}{2},\,-\frac{\pi}{2}\Big),\,\Big(\!-\!\dfrac{3\pi}{4},\,\dfrac{3\pi}{4}\Big),\,\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{4},\,-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\Big),\,\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{4},\,\dfrac{3\pi}{4}\Big),\,\Big(\!-\!\dfrac{3\pi}{4},\,-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\Big)\right\},\end{split}

and that the function

\omega:{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
has the following properties:

(1)

\omega
attains its minimum value
0
at
(0,\,0)
and at

\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{2},\,-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\Big)
;

(2)

\omega
attains its unique maximum
6+4\sqrt{2}
at
\Big(\!-\!\dfrac{3\pi}{4},\,\dfrac{3\pi}{4}\Big)
;

(3)

The saddle points of
\omega
are
\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{4},\,-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\Big),\,\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{4},\,\dfrac{3\pi}{4}\Big),\,\Big(\!-\!\dfrac{3\pi}{4},\,-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\Big)
.

Summing up, we have shown the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

The discrete Dirac operator
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}
is a bounded self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2}

with
purely absolutely continuous spectrum

\sigma({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h})=\Big[\!-\!\sqrt{h^{-2}(6+4\sqrt{2})+m^{2}},\,-m\Big]\cup\Big[m,\,\!\sqrt{h^{-2}(6+4\sqrt{2})+m^{2}}\,\Big].

Remark 4.2
.

As was mentioned in the introduction, some spectral properties of
2D and 3D discrete Dirac operators
with a fixed mesh size were already discussed in
[
23
, Theorem 2.1]
, where
the statement of Theorem
4.1
is shown in the special case
h=1
.

To prove the convergence theorems on
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}
,
we need to examine the function
\omega
in more detail.
It follows from (
4.16
) that

(4.18)

0\leq\omega(\xi)\leq 2|\xi|^{2}\quad\;\;(\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2})

and that

(4.19)

\omega(\xi)\geq\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{8}|\xi|^{2}\quad\;\;(|\xi|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\pi}{4})

(Obviously, the number
(2-\sqrt{2})/8
can be replaced by any smaller positive constant, but we fix this value for the sake of definiteness.)
Also, it follows from (
4.16
) that, setting
\alpha=\left(\frac{\pi}{2},-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
,

(4.20)

\begin{split}\omega(\alpha+\xi)&=4-2\sin(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})+2(\sin\xi_{1}-\cos\xi_{1})-2(\sin\xi_{2}+\cos\xi_{2})\\
&=2(1-\cos\xi_{2})(1+\sin\xi_{1})+2(1-\cos\xi_{1})(1-\sin\xi_{2}),\end{split}

which, together with (
4.18
), implies that

(4.21)

0\leq\omega(\alpha+\xi)\leq 2|\xi|^{2}\quad\;\;(\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2})

and that

(4.22)

\omega(\alpha+\xi)\geq\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{8}|\xi|^{2}\quad\;\;(|\xi|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\pi}{4}).

Now, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\pi^{2}/128)

we divide
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{1}
into two disjoint subsets,

(4.23)

\begin{split}&\hskip 80.0pt{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{1}=E(\varepsilon)\cup F(\varepsilon),\\
&E(\varepsilon):=\big\{\,\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{1}\,\big|\,\omega(\xi)\geq\varepsilon\,\big\},\;\;F(\varepsilon):=\big\{\,\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{1}\,\big|\,\omega(\xi)<\varepsilon\,\big\}.\end{split}

In view of (
4.18
), (
4.19
),(
4.21
) and (
4.22
),
one can see that
F(\varepsilon)
consists of two disjoint components

F_{0}(\varepsilon)
and
F_{1}(\varepsilon)

satisfying

(4.24)

B\Big(0,\,\sqrt{\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}}\,\Big)\subset F_{0}(\varepsilon)\subset B(0,\,4\sqrt{\varepsilon}),

and

(4.25)

B\Big(\alpha,\,\sqrt{\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}}\,\Big)\subset F_{1}(\varepsilon)\subset B(\alpha,\,4\sqrt{\varepsilon}),

where
B(a,\,r)=\big\{\,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,\big|\,|a-\xi|<r\,\big\}
is the ball
with center
a
and radius
r>0
.
Hence we have a disjoint decomposition of
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{1}
:

(4.26)

{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{1}=E(\varepsilon)\cup F_{0}(\varepsilon)\cup F_{1}(\varepsilon).

Accordingly, we have
a disjoint decomposition of
{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2}
:

(4.27)

{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2}=E(\varepsilon,1/h)\cup F_{0}(\varepsilon,1/h)\cup F_{1}(\varepsilon,1/h),

where

(4.28)

\begin{split}&E(\varepsilon,1/h):=\big\{\,\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2}\,\big|\,h\xi\in E(\varepsilon)\,\big\},\\
&F_{j}(\varepsilon,1/h):=\big\{\,\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2}\,\big|\,h\xi\in F_{j}(\varepsilon)\,\big\}\qquad(j=0,\,1).\end{split}

In the following proposition, we refer to the decomposition
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}=L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2})^{2}\oplus L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2})^{2}
; remember that
Q_{1/h}
, the operator of multiplication with
\chi_{{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2}}
, is the orthogonal projection
onto the first direct summand.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. Then, for any
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}
,

(4.29)

\lim_{h\to 0}\|\big[(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\!\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\big]u\,\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}}=0.

The proof of Proposition
4.1
can be found after the proof of Lemma
4.5
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
h>0
. Then we have

(4.30)

\big\|\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)\big\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}\leq\dfrac{\,h}{2}|\xi|^{2}\qquad(\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2}),

where
\|M\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}
denotes the operator norm of a
2\times 2
matrix
M
as
a linear operator in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.

Proof.

By using the inequality

(4.31)

|e^{i\theta}-1-i\theta|\leq\frac{\,\theta^{2}}{2}\qquad(\theta\in\mathbb{R}),

we see that

(4.32)

\begin{split}&\Big|\dfrac{-i(e^{ih\xi_{1}}-1)+(e^{ih\xi_{2}}-1)}{h}-(\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2})\Big|\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{h}\Big\{\frac{\,(h\xi_{1})^{2}}{2}+\frac{\,(h\xi_{2})^{2}}{2}\Big\}=\frac{\,h}{2}|\xi|^{2}\qquad(\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}),\end{split}

which, together with (
4.5
) and (
4.6
),
implies the lemma.

\blacksquare

Since
h|\xi|\leq\sqrt{2}\pi
for
\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2}
, the above lemma immediately gives the following estimate.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
h>0
. Then we have

(4.33)

\big\|\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)\big\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}\leq\dfrac{\,\sqrt{2}\pi}{2}|\xi|\qquad(\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2}).

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, there exists a constant
C_{z}
such that

(4.34)

\|(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}\leq C_{z}\big(|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}+|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|^{2}\big)^{-1/2}\qquad(\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}).

Proof.

We first note that the matrix
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-z
is unitarily equivalent to the matrix

(4.35)

\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{m}(\xi)-z&0\,\\
0&-\lambda_{m}(\xi)-z\,\end{pmatrix},

as was discussed after Lemma
4.1
.
Hence it is clear that

(4.36)

\|(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}=\max\left\{\frac{1}{|\lambda_{m}(\xi)-z|},\frac{1}{|-\lambda_{m}(\xi)-z|}\right\}.

Now let
z_{0}=i\mathop{\mathfrak{Im}}z
. Then

\frac{|z_{0}-t|^{2}}{|z-t|^{2}}=\frac{(\mathop{\mathfrak{Im}}z)^{2}+t^{2}}{(\mathop{\mathfrak{Im}}z)^{2}+(t-\mathop{\mathfrak{Re}}z)^{2}}

is a continuous function of
t\in\mathbb{R}
that tends to
1
as
t\to\pm\infty
.
It is therefore bounded, so there exists a constant
C_{z}
such that

|z_{0}-t|\leq C_{z}\,|z-t|
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, which together with (
4.36
) gives (
4.34
).

\blacksquare

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then
for any
h\in\Big(0,\,\dfrac{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{2|\mathfrak{Re}\,z|}\Big)
we have

(4.37)

\|(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}\leq\min\Big(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|},\,\frac{\,2h\,}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,}\Big)\qquad\big(\xi\in E(\varepsilon,1/h)\big).

Proof.

As a consequence of the spectral theorem for self-adjoint operators,

(4.38)

\|(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}\leq\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|}\qquad(\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2})

for any
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore, it is sufficient to prove the inequality

(4.39)

\|(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}\leq\frac{\,2h\,}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,}\qquad(\xi\in E(\varepsilon,1/h)).

To this end,
we note the fact that the matrix
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-z
is unitarily equivalent to the matrix

(4.40)

\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{m,h}(\xi)-z&0\,\\
0&-\lambda_{m,h}(\xi)-z\,\end{pmatrix},

as was discussed after Lemma
4.1
.
By the reverse triangle inequality,

(4.41)

\begin{split}|\lambda_{m,h}(\xi)\pm z|&>\sqrt{h^{-2}\,\omega(h\xi)+m^{2}\,}-|\mathfrak{Re}\,z|\\
&\quad>h^{-1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}-|\mathfrak{Re}\,z|\qquad(\mbox{for }\xi\in E(\varepsilon,1/h)).\end{split}

If
h
satisfies the inequality
0<h<\sqrt{\varepsilon}/(2|\mathfrak{Re}\,z|)
,
then
the inequality (
4.39
) follows from (
4.41
).

\blacksquare

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Let
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|\left((\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\right)u\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2}}\left|\left((\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-z)^{-1}-(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-z)^{-1}\right)u(\xi)\right|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus{\mathbb{T}}_{\!1/h}^{2}}\left|(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-z)^{-1}\,u(\xi)\right|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}^{2}d\xi.

As the operator norm of
(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-z)^{-1}
can be estimated by
|\mathop{\mathfrak{Im}}z|^{-1}
, the second
integral tends to
0
as
h\to 0
.
Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\pi^{2}/128)
.
In view of (
4.27
), we divide the first integral into three terms,

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{E(\varepsilon,1/h)}+\int_{F_{0}(\varepsilon,1/h)}+\int_{F_{1}(\varepsilon,1/h)}\Big)\big|\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-z)^{-1}-(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-z)^{-1}\}u(\xi)\big|_{{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}}^{2}\,d\xi

(4.42)

\displaystyle\qquad=:I(\varepsilon,h)+I\!I_{0}(\varepsilon,h)+I\!I_{1}(\varepsilon,h).

Since

(4.43)

\begin{split}&(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-z)^{-1}-(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-z)^{-1}\\
&\quad=\big(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-z)^{-1}(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)\big)(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-z)^{-1},\end{split}

we get

(4.44)

\begin{split}I(\varepsilon,h)&\leq\int_{E(\varepsilon,1/h)}\|\big(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}\\
&\quad\times\|\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}\,\|\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}\,\big|u(\xi)\big|_{{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}}^{2}\,d\xi\\
&\leq\int_{E(\varepsilon,1/h)}\Big\{\min\Big(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|},\,\frac{\,2h\,}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,}\Big)\Big\}^{2}\\
&\qquad\times\Big(\dfrac{\,\sqrt{2}\pi}{2}|\xi|\Big)^{2}C_{z}^{2}\big(|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}+|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|^{2}\big)^{-1}\big|u(\xi)\big|_{{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}}^{2}\,d\xi\\
&\leq\Big\{\min\Big(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|},\,\frac{\,2h\,}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,}\Big)\Big\}^{2}\Big(\dfrac{\,\sqrt{2}\pi}{2}\Big)^{2}C_{z}^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\big|u(\xi)\big|_{{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}}^{2}\,d\xi,\end{split}

where we have used Lemmas
4.3
,
4.4

(with the same constant
C_{z}
) and

4.5
.
It follows from (
4.44
)
that

(4.45)

\lim_{h\to 0}I(\varepsilon,h)=0.

The second term in (
4
) can be estimated in a similar manner:

(4.46)

\begin{split}I\!I_{0}(\varepsilon,h)&\leq\int_{F_{0}(\varepsilon,1/h)}\|\big(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}\\
&\quad\times\|\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}\,\|\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}(\xi)-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}\,\big|u(\xi)\big|_{{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}}^{2}\,d\xi\\

where we have used (
4.38
), Lemmas
4.2
and
4.4
.
If

, then by (
4.24
) we see that

\Big(\dfrac{h}{2}|\xi|^{2}\Big)^{2}C_{z}^{2}\big(|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}+|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|^{2}\big)^{-1}<4\varepsilon C_{z}^{2}.

\limsup_{h\to 0}I\!I_{0}(\varepsilon,h)\leq\Big(\frac{2C_{z}}{|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|}\,\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\Big)^{\!2}\,\varepsilon.

Also, the third term in (

(4.49)

\begin{split}I\!I_{1}(\varepsilon,h)&\leq\int_{F_{1}(\varepsilon,1/h)}\|\big(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}(\xi)-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}^{2}\\

where we have used (

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

. Then

(4.53)

\mathop{\hbox{\rm s-lim}}_{h\to 0}\big\{({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\!\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}\big\}=({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\;\mbox{ in }\,L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}
.
It follows from (
4.8
) that

\displaystyle\big\{({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\big\}\varphi

\displaystyle\quad={\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}\big\}{\mathcal{F}}_{h}\varphi-\overline{\mathcal{F}}(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\mathcal{F}\varphi

(4.54)

\displaystyle\quad={\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}\big\}({\mathcal{F}}_{h}-\mathcal{F})\varphi

(4.55)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+({\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}-\overline{\mathcal{F}})\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}\big\}\mathcal{F}\varphi

(4.56)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\overline{\mathcal{F}}\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\big\}\mathcal{F}\varphi.

The

\displaystyle\|(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2})}\|({\mathcal{F}}_{h}-\mathcal{F})\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|}\Big)\|({\mathcal{F}}_{h}-\mathcal{F})\varphi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}}

which, by Lemma
3.6
, tends to
0
as

.

The term in (

(4.59)

\displaystyle({\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}-\overline{\mathcal{F}})\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}\big\}\mathcal{F}\varphi

(4.60)

\displaystyle=({\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}-\overline{\mathcal{F}})\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}\big\}\mathcal{F}\varphi

(4.61)

\displaystyle\quad+({\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{h}-\overline{\mathcal{F}})\big\{(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-(\widehat{\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\big\}\mathcal{F}\varphi,

where, by Lemma
3.5
, the

) tends
to

as

,
and
the

) is bounded by

(4.62)

as

.
Therefore, the

Finally,

4.1
immediately implies that

Remark 4.3
.

In view of the strong convergence of the orthogonal projectors
P_{h}
(see Lemma
3.3
), the strong limits of

(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-z)^{-1}
and of
(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}
are the same.

We conclude this section by proving that
({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-z)^{-1}
does not converge in the operator norm
sense to
({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}
as
h\rightarrow 0
.
We would like to mention that the proof of Theorem
4.3
below is based on the idea demonstrated in
[
31
]
and
[
30
]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. Then

(4.63)

\begin{split}\liminf_{h\to 0}&\|({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-z)^{-1}-({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\,\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2})}\\
&\qquad\geq\max\Big(\frac{1}{|m-z|},\frac{1}{|m+z|}\Big).\end{split}

Proof.

For
h>0
, consider the function

u_{h}=\left(\begin{matrix}y_{h}\\
0\end{matrix}\right)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}
, where

(4.64)

y_{h}(x)=\sqrt{h}\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{2h}(x_{1}-x_{2})}\,e^{-h(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})}\qquad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Then
\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}
and

(4.65)

(\mathcal{F}y_{h})(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{h}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{4h}[(\xi_{1}-\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}+(\xi_{2}+\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}]}\qquad(\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Further, by (
4.5
) we find that

(4.66)

(\mathbb{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}u_{h}=\overline{\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{m^{2}-z^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}m+z&\xi_{1}-i\xi_{2}\\
\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2}&-m+z\end{matrix}\right)\,\left(\begin{matrix}\mathcal{F}y_{h}\\
0\end{matrix}\right),

and, as the Fourier transform is an isometry on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we conclude that

(4.67)

\begin{split}\|(\mathbb{D}_{m}&-z)^{-1}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}^{2}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|m+z|^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}}{|m^{2}-z^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}\,|^{2}}\,\frac{1}{4h}\,e^{-[(\xi_{1}-\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}+(\xi_{2}+\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}]/2h}\,d\xi\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{C_{z}}{|(m^{2}-z^{2})+(\eta_{1}+\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}+(\eta_{2}-\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}|}\,\frac{1}{4h}\,e^{-(\eta_{1}^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2})/2h}\,d\eta.\end{split}

Here we have used the fact that

\frac{|m+z|^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}}{|m^{2}-z^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}\,|}\to 1\quad\mbox{ as }\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\to\infty,

and the fact that
|m^{2}-z^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}\,|\geq c_{z}>0
for
\forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
In fact, we have

|m^{2}-z^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}\,|\geq\begin{cases}|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|^{2}&\mbox{if }z\mbox{ is pure imaginary},\\
2|(\mathfrak{Re}\,z)(\mathfrak{Im}\,z)|&\mbox{if }z\mbox{ is not pure imaginary}.\end{cases}

The second integral in (
4.67
) can be written in the form

(4.68)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{C_{z}}{|(m^{2}-z^{2})+(\sqrt{h}\,\eta_{1}+\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}+(\sqrt{h}\,\eta_{2}-\frac{\pi}{2h})^{2}|}\,\frac{1}{4}\,e^{-(\eta_{1}^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2})/2}\,d\eta.

The integrand in (
4.68
)
tends to 0 pointwise as
h\rightarrow 0
and is bounded above by the function

\frac{C_{z}}{|m^{2}-z^{2}|}\,\frac{1}{4}\,e^{-(\eta_{1}^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2})/2}
,
so by the dominated convergence theorem

(4.69)

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\|(\mathbb{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}=0.

Now, in order to apply the discrete Dirac operator, we project
u_{h}
into
L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{2})^{2}
.
We see that

P_{h}y_{h}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\widetilde{y}_{h}(hn){\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}
, where

\widetilde{y}_{h}(hn)=\sqrt{h}\left(\int_{n_{1}}^{n_{1}+1}e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}t}\,e^{-h^{3}t^{2}}\,dt\right)\!\!\left(\int_{n_{2}}^{n_{2}+1}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}t}\,e^{-h^{3}t^{2}}\,dt\right)\quad(n=(n_{1},\,n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2})

by (
2.10
).
Integration by parts
and the formula

\int_{n}^{n+1}2at\,e^{-at^{2}}\,dt=e^{-an^{2}}-e^{-a(n+1)^{2}}\qquad(n\in\mathbb{Z})

show
that

\displaystyle\int_{n}^{n+1}e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}t}\,e^{-h^{3}t^{2}}\,dt\qquad(n\in\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{2}{i\pi}\,\left(e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}(n+1)}\,e^{-h^{3}(n+1)^{2}}-e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}n}\,e^{-h^{3}n^{2}}\right)\pm\frac{2}{i\pi}\int_{n}^{n+1}e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}t}\,2h^{3}t\,e^{-h^{3}t^{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\,(1\pm i)\,e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}n}\,e^{-h^{3}n^{2}}\pm\frac{4h^{3}}{i\pi}\int_{n}^{n+1}\left(e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}t}-e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}(n+1)}\right)\,t\,e^{-h^{3}t^{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle=:\frac{2}{\pi}\,(1\pm i)\,e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}n}\,e^{-h^{3}n^{2}}\pm\frac{4h^{3}}{i\pi}K^{\pm}(n,\,h).

Hence

(4.70)

P_{h}u_{h}=\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}(y_{h})_{h}+R_{h}\\
0\end{matrix}\right)=\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}(u_{h})_{h}+\left(\begin{matrix}R_{h}\\
0\end{matrix}\right),

where we use the notation of (
3.1
) for
(y_{h})_{h}
,
(u_{h})_{h}
and set

R_{h}:=R_{h,a}+R_{h,b}+R_{h,c}

with

\displaystyle R_{h,a}(n,h)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{h}\frac{8h^{3}}{\pi^{2}}\,(i-1)\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}n_{1}}e^{-h^{3}n_{1}^{2}}K^{-}(n_{2},h)

\displaystyle R_{h,b}(n,h)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{h}\frac{8h^{3}}{\pi^{2}}\,(i+1)\,e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}n_{2}}e^{-h^{3}n_{2}^{2}}K^{+}(n_{1},h)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2})

\displaystyle R_{h,c}(n,h)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{h}\frac{16h^{6}}{\pi^{2}}K^{+}(n_{1},h)K^{-}(n_{2},h).

Using the asymptotics

(4.71)

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\beta k^{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta}}+O(1),\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{2}\,e^{-\beta k^{2}}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4\beta^{3/2}}+O(\beta^{-1})\quad(\beta\rightarrow 0),

we find

(4.72)

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-h^{3}n^{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{h^{3}}}+O(1)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle\big|h^{\frac{3}{2}}K^{\pm}(n,h)\big|^{2}\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(2\int_{n}^{n+1}h^{\frac{3}{2}}|t|\,e^{-(h^{3/2}t)^{2}/2}\,e^{-h^{3}t^{2}/2}\,dt\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{e}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{n}^{n+1}e^{-h^{3}t^{2}/2}\,dt\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{e}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-h^{3}n^{2}}=\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}}{e}\,h^{-\frac{3}{2}}+O(1)\quad(h\rightarrow 0),

where we have used the inequality
h^{\frac{3}{2}}|t|\,e^{-(h^{3/2}t)^{2}/2}\leq 1/\sqrt{e}
in the second inequality.
Hence, bearing in mind (
2.8
),

(4.73)

\|R_{h,a}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{2})}^{2},\|R_{h,b}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\leq h^{3}\,\frac{256}{\sqrt{2}\pi^{3}e}+O(h^{9/2})

and

\|R_{h,c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{2})}^{2}=O(h^{6})
, which gives

\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow 0}\|R_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{2})}=0
, while

(4.74)

\left\|\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}(y_{h})_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{2})}=\frac{8}{\sqrt{2}\pi^{3/2}}+o(1)\qquad(h\rightarrow 0).

Now, applying the discrete Dirac operator to
(u_{h})_{h}=\left(\begin{matrix}(y_{h})_{h}\\
0\end{matrix}\right)
, we find

(4.75)

(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}-z)(u_{h})_{h}=(m-z)(u_{h})_{h}+\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
(-i\partial_{1,h}+\partial_{2,h})(y_{h})_{h}\end{matrix}\right),

where

\displaystyle(-i\partial_{1,h}

\displaystyle+\partial_{2,h})(y_{h})_{h}(hn)=(-i\partial_{1,h}+\partial_{2,h})\sqrt{h}\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(n_{1}-n_{2})}e^{-h^{3}(n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}\,\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(n_{1}-n_{2})}e^{-h^{3}(n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2})}\left((e^{-h^{3}(2n_{1}+1)}-1)-i(e^{-h^{3}(2n_{2}+1)}-1)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}\,\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(n_{1}-n_{2})}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left((e^{-h^{3}(n_{1}+1)^{2}}-e^{-h^{3}n_{1}^{2}})e^{-h^{3}n_{2}^{2}}-ie^{-h^{3}n_{1}^{2}}(e^{-h^{3}(n_{2}+1)^{2}}-e^{-h^{3}n_{2}^{2}})\right)

\displaystyle=:\widetilde{R}_{h}(n,h)\quad(n=(n_{1},\,n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}).

Then, using the formula

\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|e^{-h^{3}(n_{1}+1)^{2}}-e^{-h^{3}n_{1}^{2}}\big|^{2}=2\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{\infty}\big|e^{-h^{3}(n_{1}+1)^{2}}-e^{-h^{3}n_{1}^{2}}\big|^{2}

and
the estimate

(4.76)

\big|e^{-h^{3}(2n_{1}+1)}-1\big|=\Big|\int_{0}^{2n_{1}+1}(-h^{3})\,e^{-h^{3}s}\,ds\Big|\leq h^{3}\,(2n_{1}+1)\qquad(n_{1}\geq 0),

we obtain for

\widetilde{R}_{h,a}(n,h):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}\,\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}n_{1}}(e^{-h^{3}(n_{1}+1)^{2}}-e^{-h^{3}n_{1}^{2}})e^{-h^{3}n_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{R}_{h,a}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq h^{7}\,\frac{64}{\pi^{4}}\left(2\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{\infty}(2n_{1}+1)^{2}\,e^{-2h^{3}n_{1}^{2}}\right)\left(\sum_{n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-2h^{3}n_{2}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq h^{7}\,\frac{64}{\pi^{4}}\left(2\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{\infty}e^{-2h^{3}n_{1}^{2}}+16\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{\infty}n_{1}^{2}\,e^{-2h^{3}n_{1}^{2}}\right)\left(\sum_{n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-2h^{3}n_{2}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=h\,\frac{64}{\pi^{3}}+o(h)\qquad(h\rightarrow 0)

and similarly for

\widetilde{R}_{h,b}(n,h):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}\,\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\,e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}n_{2}}e^{-h^{3}n_{1}^{2}}(e^{-h^{3}(n_{2}+1)^{2}}-e^{-h^{3}n_{2}^{2}})
,
so

(4.77)

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\|\widetilde{R}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{2})}=0.

(4.78)

(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-z)^{-1}=(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\oplus\left(-\frac{1}{z}\right).

Now we observe that

(4.79)

(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}-z)P_{h}u_{h}=\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}(m-z)\,(u_{h})_{h}+\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
\widetilde{R}_{h}\end{matrix}\right)+(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}-z)\left(\begin{matrix}R_{h}\\
0\end{matrix}\right),

so,
applying (
4.70
) to the
(u_{h})_{h}
on the right hand side of (
4.79
)
and multiplying the both sides of (
4.79
) by

\frac{1}{m-z}(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}
,
we infer that

\displaystyle(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}P_{h}u_{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-z}\,P_{h}u_{h}-\frac{1}{m-z}\,(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
\widetilde{R}_{h}\end{matrix}\right)-\frac{1}{m-z}\left(\begin{matrix}R_{h}\\
0\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\left(\begin{matrix}R_{h}\\
0\end{matrix}\right).

As
\|(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{2})^{2})}\leq 1/|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|
,
it follows that

\displaystyle\|(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}P_{h}u_{h}-\frac{1}{m-z}\,P_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\;\,\leq\frac{8}{|m-z|\,|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|\,\pi^{2}}\,\|\widetilde{R}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{2})}+\left(\frac{1}{|m-z|}+\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{Im}\,z|}\right)\|R_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{2})}\rightarrow 0\;\,(h\rightarrow 0).

\displaystyle\|u_{h}

\displaystyle\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}\,\|(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-z)^{-1}-(\mathbb{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2})}

\displaystyle\geq\|(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-z)^{-1}u_{h}-(\mathbb{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\|(\mathbb{D}_{m,h}-z)^{-1}P_{h}u_{h}-z^{-1}(1-P_{h})u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}-\|(\mathbb{D}_{m}-z)^{-1}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\|(\mathbb{D}_{m_{h}}-z)^{-1}P_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}^{2}+|z^{-1}|\,\|(1-P_{h})u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}^{2}}+o(1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{|m-z|}\,\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}+o(1)\qquad(h\rightarrow 0).

(4.80)

\liminf_{h\to 0}\|({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-z)^{-1}-({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\,\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2})}\geq\frac{1}{|m-z|}.

If we replace
u_{h}=\left(\begin{matrix}y_{h}\\

with

y_{h}\end{matrix}\right)

\liminf_{h\to 0}\|({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}-z)^{-1}-({\mathbb{D}}_{m}-z)^{-1}\,\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2})}\geq\frac{1}{|m+z|}.

We now arrived at the inequality (
4.63
).

\blacksquare

Remark 4.4
.

The lack of norm resolvent convergence shown in Theorem
4.3
is closely related to the fact that, unlike
the continuous Dirac operator, the discrete Dirac operator does not control the gradient: while a simple calculation
shows that

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}\leq\|\mathbb{D}_{m}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}
for all
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}
,
there is no constant
C>0
such that
\sqrt{\|\partial_{1,h}u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{2})^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{2,h}u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{2})^{2}}^{2}}\leq C\|\mathbb{D}_{m,h}u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{2})^{2}}
.
This, in turn, is connected to the fact that the Liouville theorem does not hold in discrete complex analysis. Indeed,
the function

y:\mathbb{Z}+i\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}+i\mathbb{Z}
,

y(n_{1}+in_{2})=i^{n_{1}-n_{2}}
satisfies the discrete Cauchy-Riemann equation

(\partial_{1,1}+i\partial_{2,1})y=0

in the whole lattice of Gaussian integers (and thus is ‘monodiffric’, see
[
13
]
,
[
18
]
) and is
bounded, but not constant.

The functions
y_{h}
in the proof of Theorem
4.3
arise from this function
y
by a natural extension to
all of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\cong\mathbb{C}
, scaling to the lattice with spacing
h
and multiplication with a suitable
Gaussian to place the functions into Schwartz space.

5.
Strong resolvent convergence of
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}+V_{h}

In this section, we shall discuss the continuum Dirac operators

(5.1)

{\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V=-i\sigma_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}-i\sigma_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}+m\sigma_{3}+V(x)\;\text{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2},

where
V
is a complex
2\times 2
matrix-valued potential. More precisely, we make the following

Assumption (V).

V:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}
is a matrix-valued function each element of which is a bounded and
uniformly continuous function.

Remark 5.1
.

It is apparent that electro-magnetic Dirac operators

\sigma\cdot(-i\nabla-a(x))+m\beta+q(x)

can
be written in the form (
5.1
). Indeed, one can take
V
to be

-\sigma\cdot a(x)+q(x)
.

We note that
V
can be decomposed into its Hermitian and skew-Hermitian parts,

V=V_{\mathfrak{R}}+iV_{\mathfrak{I}}
, where

(5.2)

V_{\mathfrak{R}}(x)=\dfrac{V(x)+V(x)^{*}}{2},\qquad iV_{\mathfrak{I}}(x)=\dfrac{V(x)-V(x)^{*}}{2}\qquad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}).

It is evident that under the assumption (V), the operator
V
of multiplication with the matrix-valued function
V

is a bounded operator in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}
and that
the operator
{\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V
is well-defined.
In particular, if
V_{\mathfrak{I}}=0
,
then
{\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V
is a self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}

with domain
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}
.

In analogy to (
5.1
), we consider the discrete Dirac operator
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}+V_{h}
in

L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2}
, where
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}
is the operator introduced
in (
4.3
) and
V_{h}
is the operator of multiplication by

(5.3)

V_{h}(x)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}V(hn){\chi}_{{}_{I_{n,h}}}\!(x)\qquad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}).

It is clear that
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}+V_{h}
is a bounded operator in

L^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{h}^{2})^{2}
.
In the same manner as in (
5.2
), we split
V_{h}=V_{\mathfrak{R},h}+iV_{\mathfrak{I},h}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Under the assumption (V),

(i)

both the spectra
\sigma({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}+V_{h})
and

\sigma({\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V)
are subsets of the strip

\big\{\,z\in\mathbb{C}\,\big|\,|{\mathfrak{Im}}\,z|\leq\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\|V_{\mathfrak{I}}(x)\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}\,\big\};

(ii)

for
z
with
|{\mathfrak{Im}}\,z|>\displaystyle{\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}}\|V_{\mathfrak{I}}(x)\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}

(5.4)

\mathop{\hbox{\rm s-lim}}_{h\to 0}\big\{({\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}+V_{h}-z)^{-1}\!\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}\big\}=({\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V-z)^{-1}\;\mbox{ in }\,L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}.

Note that in the self-adjoint case
V_{\mathfrak{I}}=0
,
(
5.4
) holds for

z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
.

We prepare the proof of Theorem
5.1
by providing the following auxiliary statements.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that (V) holds. Then

\sigma({\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V)\subset\big\{\,z\in\mathbb{C}\,\big|\,|{\mathfrak{Im}}\,z|\leq\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\|V_{\mathfrak{I}}(x)\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}\,\big\}.

Moreover,

(5.5)

({\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V-z)^{-1}=({\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V_{\mathfrak{R}}-z)^{-1}\big\{I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}}+iV_{\mathfrak{I}}\,({\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V_{\mathfrak{R}}-z)^{-1}\big\}^{-1}

for all
z
with
|{\mathfrak{Im}}\,z|>\displaystyle{\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}}\|V_{\mathfrak{I}}(x)\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}
.

Proof.

Let

|{\mathfrak{Im}}\,z|>\displaystyle{\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}}\|V_{\mathfrak{I}}(x)\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}
.
Since
{\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V_{\mathfrak{R}}
is self-adjoint,
it follows that
z\in\rho({\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V_{\mathfrak{R}})
,
the resolvent set of
{\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V_{\mathfrak{R}}
. This enables us to write

(5.6)

{\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V-z=\big\{I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+iV_{\mathfrak{I}}\,({\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V_{\mathfrak{R}}-z)^{-1}\big\}({\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V_{\mathfrak{R}}-z).

Since

\|V_{\mathfrak{I}}\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2})}\leq{\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}}\|V_{\mathfrak{I}}(x)\|_{\mathbf{B}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}
,
we see that

\|V_{\mathfrak{I}}({\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V_{\mathfrak{R}}-z)^{-1}\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2})}<1,

so the operator on the right hand side of
(
5.6
) is invertible in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})^{2}
,
and therefore
z\in\rho({\mathbb{D}}_{m}+V)
.

\blacksquare

In the same manner as in the proof of Lemma
5.1
, one can prove a similar statement for

{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}+V_{h}
. Recall that
{\mathbb{D}}_{m,h}+V_{h}

Remark 5.2
.

As shown in Lemma
5.3
,
V_{h}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{h}
converges to
V
strongly, but not in the
operator norm unless
V\equiv 0
.
Indeed, if
V\not\equiv 0
, then there is some open subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, some
v\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
with

\|v\|_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}=1
and a constant
C>0
such that
\|V(x)v\|_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\geq
for all
x\in\Omega
.
Let
h>0
be so small that for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
I_{n,h}\subset\Omega
, and set

\varphi(x)=\chi_{I_{n,h}}(x)\prod_{j=1}^{2}(x_{j}-h(n+\frac{1}{2}))\,v\qquad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Then

\displaystyle\|(V_{h}\oplus 0

\displaystyle-V)\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|V_{h}P_{h}\varphi-V\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|V\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\int_{I_{n,h}}\|V(x)v\|_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{2}|x_{j}-h(n_{j}+\frac{1}{2})|^{2}\,dx\Big\}^{1/2}\geq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Therefore
\|V_{h}\oplus 0-V\|_{\mathbf{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\geq C>0
for all sufficiently small

.

This remark shows that even at the level of the potential operator

slightly counterintuitive, as

norm to

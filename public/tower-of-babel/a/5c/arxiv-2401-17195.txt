The point scatterer approximation for wave dynamics
Andrea Mantile, Andrea Posilicano

Abstract
. Given an open, bounded and connected set Ω ⊂ ℝ 3 \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3} and its rescaling Ω ε \Omega_{\varepsilon} of size ε ≪ 1 \varepsilon\ll 1 , we consider the solutions of the Cauchy problem for the inhomogeneous wave equation ( ε − 2 ​ χ Ω ε + χ ℝ 3 \ Ω ε ) ​ ∂ t ​ t u = Δ ​ u + f (\varepsilon^{-2}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}+\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}})\partial_{tt}u=\Delta u+f with initial data and source supported outside Ω ε \Omega_{\varepsilon} ; here, χ S \chi_{S} denotes the characteristic function of a set S S . We provide the first-order ε \varepsilon -corrections with respect to the solutions of the inhomogeneous free wave equation and give space-time estimates on the remainders in the L ∞ ​ ( ( 0 , 1 / ε τ ) , L 2 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) ) L^{\infty}((0,1/\varepsilon^{\tau}),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})) -norm. Such corrections are explicitly expressed in terms of the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the Newton potential operator in L 2 ​ ( Ω ) L^{2}(\Omega) and provide an effective dynamics describing a legitimate point scatterer approximation in the time domain. 1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be open, bounded and connected,
y_{0}\in\Omega
and let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be a small-scale
parameter. Denoting with
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
the rescaled domain

(1.1)

\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=\left\{y=y_{0}+\varepsilon(x-y_{0})\,,\ x\in\Omega\right\},

we introduce the contrast function

\varepsilon^{-2}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}+\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\,,

modeling a sharp discontinuity of a medium across the interface
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. The perturbed wave equation

(1.2)

(\varepsilon^{-2}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}+\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}})\partial_{tt}u=\Delta u\,,

describes the interaction between a scalar wave and a small inhomogeneity
having high contrast with respect to an homogeneous background. Here, the
relative speed of propagation inside
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is given by the
number
\varepsilon
; hence, small values of
\varepsilon
correspond to a
local regime of small relative speed of propagation. We are interested in
the asymptotic behavior of the solutions of the Cauchy problem for (
1.2
) as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.

Under the time-harmonic assumption
u(t,x)=e^{it\omega}u_{\omega}(x)
, the
corresponding stationary equation writes as

(1.3)

\big(\Delta+(\varepsilon^{-2}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}+\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}})\,\omega^{2}\big)u_{\omega}=0\,.

It is known that, depending on the incident frequency
\omega
, the
scattering solutions
u^{sc}_{\omega}
of (
1.3
) may
concentrate around
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
. Namely,
specific values of
\omega
, referred to as
resonant frequencies
, are
associated to a scattering enhancement localized at
y_{0}
. At these
specific frequencies, the dominant part of the scattered field assumes the
form

(1.4)

u_{\omega}^{sc}(x)\sim u_{\omega}^{in}(y_{0})\,\Lambda_{\Omega}(\omega)\,\frac{e^{-i\omega|x-y_{0}|}}{4\pi|x-y_{0}|}\,,

where
\Lambda_{\Omega}(\omega)
is a scattering coefficient depending on
the physical parameters. Such asymptotic, provided in
[
3
]
under
far-field approximations, describes a diffusion from a
point scatterer

placed at
y_{0}
.

The point scatterer model, introduced by Foldy in
[
10
]
and further
developed by Lax,
[
19
]
, consists in using a (
1.4
)-like formula as an heuristic approximation for the stationary scattering
from a small obstacles placed at
y_{0}
. Such an approximation is commonly
adopted to describe scattering from small Dirichlet obstacles. An
interpretation, discussed in
[
14
]
, shows that the Foldy-Lax
description of a point scatterer corresponds to modeling the interaction
between the wave and the scatterer in terms of a point perturbation of the
Laplacian, a class of singular perturbations studied in
[
1
]
, with a
frequency-dependent scattering length. It is worth to remark that such model has no direct counterpart in the time domain setting. However, since the point scatterer approximation
provides an effective limit for the asymptotic regime at specific
frequencies, the asymptotic analysis of the wave dynamics generated by
solutions of (
1.2
) at small scale may suggests a physically coherent
definition of a point scatterer in the time domain.

The analysis of the stationary problem (
1.3
) in the small
scale limit
\varepsilon\searrow 0
enlightens the emergency of a discrete
set of
scattering resonances
at resonant frequencies
\omega_{k}

related to the inverse spectral points of the
Newton potential operator

N_{0}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)\,,\qquad N_{0}u(x):=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(y)\,dy}{|x-y|}

by
\omega_{k}=\lambda_{k}^{-1/2}
, where
\lambda_{k}\in\sigma_{d}(N_{0})
, see
[
3
]
,
[
8
]
. This suggests that
the generator of the dynamics may have spectral resonances at the energies
\lambda_{k}^{-1}
. Such a spectral problem is considered in
[
20
]
, where this picture is validated and precise estimates for the imaginary
parts of resonances are provided. In particular, under suitable assumptions
on
\Omega
, it is shown that at each
\lambda_{k}\in\sigma_{d}(N_{0})

there corresponds a unique resonance of
-(\varepsilon^{2}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}+\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}})\Delta
converging to
\lambda_{k}^{-1}
with imaginary part of order
\varepsilon
.

According to the theory of second-order Cauchy problems with self-adjoint
generators, the solutions of (
1.2
) express in terms of time
propagators which are related to the inverse Laplace transform of the
resolvent operator
(-(\varepsilon^{2}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}+\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}})\Delta+z^{2})^{-1}
, see, e.g.,
[
6
, Sec I.3.14]
. Hence, the relevant properties of
the dynamical system are encoded in the spectral profile of
(\varepsilon^{2}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}+\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}})\Delta
, including eigenvalues and
resonances. In view of the results from
[
20
]
, we expect that in
the small-scale regime, the wave dynamics (
1.2
) may be governed by a
(possibly finite) number of resonant states whose survival time, defined by
the imaginary part of spectral resonances, is of order
1/\varepsilon
. In
this connection, the role of resonances on the asymptotic dynamics may be
relevant on a large time-scale.

In this work we focus on a direct asymptotic analysis of the dynamics
generated by solutions of the Cauchy problem for the wave equation (
1.2

). The main results concern both the homogeneous case, considered in Theorem

5.1
, and the non-homogeneous case, in Theorem
5.2
. In Theorem

5.3
a model for the effective dynamics in the small-scale limit is
provided in both cases. We next present an overview of the outcomes for the
homogeneous Cauchy problem; more detailed results on such a case as well on
the one in presence of a source term are provided in the aforementioned
theorems.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
and
u_{\text{{free}}}
denote the classical
solutions of the Cauchy problems (here and in the following
t\geq 0
)

\begin{cases}(\varepsilon^{-2}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}+\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}})\partial_{tt}u_{\varepsilon}(t)=\Delta u_{\varepsilon}(t)\,,\\
u_{\varepsilon}(0)=\phi\,,\\
\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(0)=\psi\,,\end{cases}\qquad\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}u_{\text{{free}}}(t)=\Delta u_{\text{{free}}}(t)\,,\\
u_{\text{{free}}}(0)=\phi\,,\\
\partial_{t}u_{\text{{free}}}(0)=\psi\,.\end{cases}

Assume that
\phi,\psi\in H^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
are supported outside
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and define
h:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

depending on these Cauchy data by

h(t):=\int_{|y-y_{0}|<t}\frac{\Delta^{2}\phi(y)}{4\pi|y-y_{0}|}\,dy+\frac{1}{4\pi t}\int_{|x-y_{0}|=t}\Delta\psi(x)\,d\sigma_{t}(y)\,.

Notice that, by Kirchhoff’s formula,
h(t)=\Delta u_{\text{{free}}}(t,y_{0})
. Then, for any
\tau\in(0,\frac{1}{11})
there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
small enough such that the expansion

(1.5)

u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\,u_{\text{{free}}}(t,x)+\varepsilon(\varepsilon^{2}-1)\,H(t-|x-y_{0}|)\,\frac{q(t-|x-y_{0}|)}{4\pi|x-y_{0}|}+{r}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,,

holds for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and the remainder
allows the estimate

(1.6)

\sup_{0<t\leq 1/\varepsilon^{\tau}}\|{r}_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\,o(\varepsilon)\big(\|\Delta^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|\Delta^{3}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\big)\,.

Here,
H
is the Heaviside function and
q(t):=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}q_{k}(t)
, with each
q_{k}(t)
solving the Cauchy problem

(1.7)

\begin{cases}\ddot{q}_{k}(t)=-\lambda_{k}^{-1}\,\big(q_{k}(t)-|\langle e_{k},1\rangle|^{2}\,h(t)\big)\,,\\
q_{k}(0)=\dot{q}_{k}(0)=0\,,\end{cases}

where
e_{k}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is an eigenfunction of
N_{0}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
.

The effective dynamics given by

(1.8)

u_{\varepsilon,\text{{eff}}}(t,x)=\,u_{\text{{free}}}(t,x)+\varepsilon(\varepsilon^{2}-1)\,H(t-|x-y_{0}|)\,\frac{q(t-|x-y_{0}|)}{4\pi|x-y_{0}|}

is the resulting point scatterer approximation of the propagation, since it
solves the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}u_{\varepsilon,\text{{eff}}}(t)=\Delta u_{\varepsilon,\text{{eff}}}(t)+\varepsilon(\varepsilon^{2}-1)\,q(t)\,\delta_{y_{0}}\\
u_{\varepsilon,\text{{eff}}}(0)=\phi\\
\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon,\text{{eff}}}(0)=\psi\,,\end{cases}

where
\delta_{y_{0}}
denotes the Dirac delta distribution supported at the
point
y_{0}
. The spherical wave component of
u_{\varepsilon,\text{{eff}}}
has the space profile of a Green function centered at
y_{0}

modulated by a function whose evolution in time is determined by the Cauchy
data through the solution of (
1.7
).

It is worth to remark that (
1.5
) holds for the specific class of
H^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
Cauchy data with support away from
y_{0}
. This
condition is needed to control the high-energy contribution to the inverse
Laplace transform involved in the computation of the dynamics. It is unclear
if, for less regular data which may concentrate their
L^{2}
-mass close to
y_{0}
, a different asymptotic may occur.

Since the solution
t\mapsto u_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)
defines a continuous
flow in the Sobolev space
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and since (
1.8
) is smooth outside
y_{0}
but
neither bounded nor continuous there, the small remainder
{r}_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)
must compensate such loss of regularity close to
y_{0}
. It is worth to remark that this is not in contradiction with the
estimates (
1.6
), however, such circumstance prevents to control
the errors on a more regular Sobolev scale.

Although (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) does not explicitly involve resonant
states, the resonant energies
\lambda_{k}^{-1}
appears in the definition
of the modulation functions
q_{k}(t)
. This expansion holds on a large time
scale which is, however, shorter than the estimated resonances life-time. We
expect that (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) can be recasted in terms of
quasi-bound states. Let us notice in turns that, representing the quantum
and the classical-waves dynamics for point perturbations of the Laplacian in
terms of resonant states presents well-known technical asperities (involving
a careful estimate of the contributions to the time-propagator from energies
close to resonances) and very few general results have been provided in this
sense, see
[
2
]
.

The small-scale expansions and the effective model presented in this work
are new. In particular, the asymptotic formulae resulting from our analysis
are given with global-in-space estimates of the errors on a large time
scale. Such formulae provide, in our opinion, a useful contribution in
applications involving time domain data. Let us recall, at this concern,
that the interaction of classical waves with
micro-resonators
is
attracting the increasing interest of different branches of applied physics,
where the manipulation of the dynamics at micro-scales has several relevant
applications. For instance, these resonant-scattering phenomena have been
used for the design of acoustic and electromagnetic metamaterials, or in the
realization of contrast agents for various imaging strategies. Moreover,
they show to have a potential impact in classical and quantum information
processing. A vast literature has been devoted to these topics, see
[
11
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
24
]
to cite a few.

While the small-
\varepsilon
asymptotic of the stationary scattering
problem (
1.3
) has been well understood, only partial results
have been provided so far for the corresponding dynamics. An attempt to derive a Foldy-Lax model in the time domain has been recently
discussed in the two-dimensional case using Dirichlet discs of small size, see
[
16
]
. As regards the model equation (
1.3
), a possible approach to time-domain expansions was argued in
[
3
]
relying on the previous works
[
4
]
and
[
5
, Appendix B]
. This, however, would produce an expansion only valid after truncating the high-frequency components of the fields. To the best of our knowledge, none explicit formula has been produced within such an approach, neither the relation with the asymptotic expansion of the full time-domain solution has been discussed so far.
In
[
23
]
, the equation (
1.2
), excited by a source term but with
trivial Cauchy data, have been considered in the perspective of using
dielectric micro-resonators as contrast agents for imaging in the time
domain. The authors use known estimates in the Laplace-transform domain to
get the asymptotic expansion of the solution on a finite time interval with
a point-wise estimate of the error holding far from the scatterer location,
while, it deserves noting, none effective limit model has been discussed. In the limit of their validity, the interesting asymptotic
expansions provided in
[
23
]
are coherent with the ones provided
here, enlightening the role of the Newton eigenvalues in the construction of
the dynamics.

3.
The model operator and the corresponding wave equation

The wave equation (
1.2
) re-writes as

\partial_{tt}u=A(\varepsilon)u\,,

where the linear operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is defined by

A(\varepsilon):H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,,\qquad A(\varepsilon):=(\varepsilon^{2}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}+\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}}})\Delta\,.

It corresponds to an additive perturbation
of the Laplacian:

(3.1)

\begin{array}[c]{ccc}A(\varepsilon)=\Delta-T(\varepsilon)\,,&&T(\varepsilon):=(1-\varepsilon^{2})\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\Delta\,.\end{array}

Let us set

R_{z}:=(-\Delta+z)^{-1}\,,\qquad z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash(-\infty,0]\,.

We introduce the Hilbert space

L_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};(\varepsilon^{-2}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}+\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}}})dx)\,;

the corresponding scalar product and norm are denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\varepsilon}
and

\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon}
respectively; By the inequalities

(3.2)

\|u\|\leq\|u\|_{\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\|u\|\,,

one gets the equivalence, holding in the Banach space sense,

(3.3)

L_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\simeq L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,.

Remark 3.1
.

Notice that, if
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is supported outside
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, then
\|u\|_{\varepsilon}=\|u\|
. Therefore, using
\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon}
to also denote the norm in
{\mathscr{B}}(L_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, one has, for any
L\in{\mathscr{B}}(L_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and for any
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
supported outside
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
,

\|Lu\|\leq\|L\|_{\varepsilon}\|u\|\,.

Theorem 3.2
.

A(\varepsilon)
is a closed operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and a non-positive self-adjoint operator in
L_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
; it has resolvent

(3.4)

R_{z}(\varepsilon):=(-A(\varepsilon)+z)^{-1}=R_{z}-K_{z}(\varepsilon)\,,\qquad z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash(-\infty,0]\,,

where

K_{z}(\varepsilon):=R_{z}(1+T(\varepsilon)R_{z})^{-1}T(\varepsilon)R_{z}\,,\qquad z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash(-\infty,0]\,.

Proof.

Since both
(\varepsilon^{2}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}+\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}}})
and
(\varepsilon^{-2}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}+\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}}})
are bounded and
\Delta:H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is closed,
A(\varepsilon)=(\varepsilon^{2}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}+\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}}})\Delta
is closed as well; furthermore, by

\langle u,A(\varepsilon)u\rangle_{\varepsilon}=\langle u,\Delta u\rangle\leq 0\,,

one gets
A(\varepsilon)\leq 0
and
\sigma(A(\varepsilon))\subseteq(\infty,0]
.

Let
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash(-\infty,0]
such that
\text{Re}(z)\geq 0
; the identity

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|^{2}=\,

\displaystyle\left\|(-\Delta+z)R_{z}u\right\|^{2}=\left\|-\Delta R_{z}u\right\|^{2}+|z|^{2}\left\|R_{z}u\right\|^{2}+2\text{Re}(z)\langle-\Delta R_{z}u,R_{z}u\rangle

implies

\left\|-\Delta R_{z}\right\|\leq 1\,.

By (
3.1
), this gives

(3.5)

\left\|T(\varepsilon)R_{z}\right\|\leq(1-\varepsilon^{2})\left\|\Delta R_{z}\right\|\leq(1-\varepsilon^{2})<1\,.

Hence,

(3.6)

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}(-1)^{n}(T(\varepsilon)R_{z})^{n}=(1+T(\varepsilon)R_{z})^{-1}\,,

where the Neumann series on the left converges in
{{\mathscr{B}}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
for any
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash(-\infty,0]
such that
\text{Re}(z)\geq 0
.
Furthermore, for such a
z
, by simple algebraic manipulations, one gets

(3.7)

R_{z}(1+T(\varepsilon)R_{z})^{-1}=R_{z}-R_{z}(1+T(\varepsilon)R_{z})^{-1}T(\varepsilon)R_{z}\,,

and

(-A(\varepsilon)+z)R_{z}(1+T(\varepsilon)R_{z})^{-1}={\mathbb{1}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\,,

R_{z}(1+T(\varepsilon)R_{z})^{-1}(-A(\varepsilon)+z)={\mathbb{1}}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\,.

By the equivalence (
3.3
),
A(\varepsilon)
is closed as an operator in
L_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
; furthermore,
A(\varepsilon)
is symmetric in
L_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Hence, by

\text{\rm ran}(-A(\varepsilon)\pm i)=\text{\rm dom}(R_{z}(1+T(\varepsilon)R_{\pm i})^{-1})=L_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,,

A(\varepsilon)
is self-adjoint as an operator in
L_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Finally, by
[
7
, Theorem 2.19 and Remark 2.20]
, the resolvent formula (
3.7
) extends to
\varrho(A(\varepsilon))\cap\varrho(\Delta)={\mathbb{C}}\backslash(-\infty,0]
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

One can show that
\sigma(A(\varepsilon))=\sigma_{ac}(A(\varepsilon))=(-\infty,0]
. However, for the purposes of this work, it suffices to know, by
A(\varepsilon)\leq 0
, that
\sigma(A(\varepsilon))\subseteq(-\infty,0]
.

Corollary 3.4
.

For any
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash(-\infty,0]
, for any integer
n\geq 1
and for any
u\in H^{2n}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
supported outside
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, one has

K_{z}(\varepsilon)u=\frac{1}{z^{n}}\,K_{z}(\varepsilon)\Delta^{n}u\,.

Proof.

For any self-adjoint operator
A
and for any
z\in\varrho(A)\cap{\mathbb{C}}\backslash\{0\}
, one has

(-A+z)^{-1}=\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{z}\,(-A+z)^{-1}A\,.

Iterating, one gets

(3.8)

(-A+z)^{-1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{z^{k}}\,A^{k-1}+\frac{1}{z^{n}}\,(-A+z)^{-1}A^{n}\,.

By
A(\varepsilon)u=\Delta u
for any
u
supported outside
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, by (
3.4
) and by applying (
3.8
) to both
A=\Delta
and
A=A(\varepsilon)
, one gets, whenever
u\in H^{2n}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\displaystyle R_{z}(\varepsilon)u=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{z^{k}}\,A(\varepsilon)^{k-1}u+\frac{1}{z^{n}}\,R_{z}(\varepsilon)A(\varepsilon)^{n}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{z^{k}}\,\Delta^{k-1}u+\frac{1}{z^{n}}\,R_{z}\Delta^{n}u-\frac{1}{z^{n}}\,K_{z}(\varepsilon)\Delta^{n}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,R_{z}u-\frac{1}{z^{n}}\,K_{z}(\varepsilon)\Delta^{n}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,R_{z}(\varepsilon)u+K_{z}(\varepsilon)u-\frac{1}{z^{n}}\,K_{z}(\varepsilon)\Delta^{n}u\,.

∎

Given a non-positive self-adjoint operator
A
in a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, we consider the Cauchy problem for the corresponding wave equation, i.e.,

(3.9)

\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}u(t)=Au(t)\\
u(0)=\phi\\
\partial_{t}u(0)=\psi\,,\end{cases}

where both the data
\phi
and
\psi
are in
{\mathcal{H}}
. We say that
u\in{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};{\mathcal{H}})
is a mild solution of (
3.9
) whenever

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}u(r)\,dr\,ds\equiv\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)u(s)\,ds\,\in\text{\rm dom}(A)

and

u(t)=\phi+t\psi+A\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)u(s)\,ds

for any
t\geq 0
.
By
[
6
, Proposition 3.14.4, Corollary 3.14.8 and Example 3.14.16]
, the unique mild solution of (
3.9
) is given by

(3.10)

u(t)=\cos(t(-A)^{1/2})\,\phi+(-A)^{-1/2}\sin(t(-A)^{1/2})\,\psi\,,

where the sine and cosine operator functions are defined through the functional calculus for the self-adjoint
A
. By
[
12
, Theorem 7.4 in Chapter 2, Section 8]
, if
\phi\in\text{\rm dom}(A)
and
\psi\in\text{\rm dom}((-A)^{1/2})
, then
u
in (
3.10
) is a classical solution, i.e.,

u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},{\mathcal{H}})\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\text{\rm dom}((-A)^{1/2})\cap{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\text{\rm dom}(A))\,.

The Laplace transforms of the sine and the cosine functions give the relations

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda\,t}\sin(tx)\,dt=x(x^{2}+\lambda^{2})^{-1}\,,\qquad\lambda>0\,,

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda\,t}\cos(tx))\,dt=\lambda(x^{2}+\lambda^{2})^{-1}\,,\qquad\lambda>0\,.

Thus,
by functional calculus, by the inversion of the Laplace-Stieltjes transform of Lipschitz,
{\mathscr{B}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
-valued, functions

(see

), one gets

\displaystyle(-A)^{-1/2}\sin(t(-A)^{1/2})\psi

\displaystyle\,=\lim_{\gamma\nearrow\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\gamma}^{c+i\gamma}e^{tz}(-A+z^{2})^{-1}\psi\,dz\,,\qquad\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

and

\displaystyle(\cos(t(-A)^{1/2})-1)\phi

\displaystyle\,=\lim_{\gamma\nearrow\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c^{\prime}-i\gamma}^{c^{\prime}+i\gamma}e^{tz}(-A+z^{2})^{-1}A\phi\ \frac{dz}{z}\,,\qquad\phi\in\text{\rm dom}(A)\,,

where

and

are arbitrary.

By applying these results to both

and
A=\Delta
one gets the following

Remark 3.6
.

By
|\Omega_{\varepsilon}|\searrow 0
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
, for any
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
one has

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\|A(\varepsilon)u-\Delta u\|^{2}=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\,(1-\varepsilon^{2})^{2}\!\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\Delta u(x)|^{2}\,dx=0\,.

Therefore, by
[
17
, Theorems 2.25 and 2.29]
,
A(\varepsilon)
converges to the free Laplacian in norm resolvent sense, i.e., for any
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash(-\infty,0]
, there holds

(3.12)

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\left\|(-A(\varepsilon)+z)^{-1}-(-\Delta+z)^{-1}\right\|=0\,.

If the sine and the cosine operator function of
A(\varepsilon)
had norms in
\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
, then (
3.12
) would imply the convergence of the solutions of the Cauchy problem for the wave equation for
A(\varepsilon)
to the ones for
\Delta
, and that would be true for generic initial data (see
[
12
, Theorem 8.6 in Section 8, Chapter 2]
). However, by (
3.2
), these operator norms behave like
1/\varepsilon
, and so, the convergence of the dynamics generated by
A(\varepsilon)
to the free one is not guaranteed. As we will prove in the following sections, thanks to Lemma
3.5
, convergence to the free case surely holds whenever the initial data are supported outside
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.

4.
Operator estimates

In the following, we use the
identification

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\equiv L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon})\oplus L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\,,

provided by the unitary map
u\mapsto 1_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}}u\oplus 1_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
.
Here and below, given the measurable domain

we denote by

the bounded linear operator given by the restriction to

; then, the adjoint
1^{*}_{D}:L^{2}(D)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
corresponds to the extension by zero. Notice that

In such a framework, the free resolvent rewrites as the operator block matrix

(4.1)

R_{z}=\begin{bmatrix}1_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}}}R_{z}1^{*}_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}}}&1_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{c}}R_{z}1^{*}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\\

By the identity

T(\varepsilon)R_{z}=(1-\varepsilon^{2})\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\Delta R_{z}=-(1-\varepsilon^{2})1_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(1-zR_{z})\,,

making use of (
4.1

\displaystyle 1+T(\varepsilon)R_{z}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}-(1-\varepsilon^{2})\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}-z\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
1_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}R_{z}1^{*}_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}}}&1_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}R_{z}1^{*}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle\left.=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
(1-\varepsilon^{2})z1_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}R_{z}1^{*}_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}}}&\varepsilon^{2}+(1-\varepsilon^{2})z1_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}R_{z}1^{*}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\end{bmatrix}\,.\right.

Hence, for any

4.1.
The Newton potential operator of
\Omega

Now, we introduce the not negative, symmetric operator
N_{0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
defined by

N_{0}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)\,,\qquad N_{0}u(x):=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(y)\,dy}{|x-y|}\,.

By Sobolev’s inequality (see, e.g.,
[
22
, Appendix 2 to I.1]
)

\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\chi_{\Omega}(x)\chi_{\Omega}(y)\,\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{2}}\lesssim\|\chi_{\Omega}\|^{2}_{L^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

and so
N_{0}
is Hilbert-Schmidt and hence compact (see, e.g.,
[
22
, Theorem A.28]
).
Since
-\Delta N_{0}u=u
, one gets
\ker(N_{0})=\{0\}
.
By the spectral theory of compact symmetric operators (see, e.g.,
[
15
, Section 6]
),
\sigma_{d}(N_{0})=\sigma(N_{0})\backslash\{0\}
and the orthonormal
sequence
\{e_{k}\}_{1}^{+\infty}
of eigenvectors corresponding to the discrete spectrum is an orthonormal base of
\ker(N_{0})^{\perp}=L^{2}(\Omega)
. We denote by
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{1}^{+\infty}
, the set of eigenvalues (counting multiplicities) indexed in decreasing order. One has
\lambda_{1}=\|N_{0}\|
and
\lambda_{k}\searrow 0
. Furthermore, for any
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and for any bounded measurable function
f:[\,0,\|N_{0}\|\,]\to{\mathbb{C}}
,

(4.8)

f(N_{0})u=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}f(\lambda_{k})\langle e_{k},u\rangle e_{k}\,.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
z=c+i\gamma
,
c>0
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,
1+z^{2}N_{0}
has a bounded inverse and

\|(1+z^{2}N_{0})^{-1}\|\leq\begin{cases}1&|\gamma|\leq c\\
{|z|^{2}}{(2c|\gamma|\,)^{-1}}&|\gamma|>c\end{cases}\quad.

Proof.

By
\bar{z}^{2}=(c^{2}-\gamma^{2})-i2c\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash(-\infty,0]
and by
\sigma(-N_{0})\subset\left[-\|N_{0}\|,0\,\right]
, one gets
z^{-2}=|z|^{-4}\bar{z}^{2}\in\varrho(-N_{0})
. Hence,

(1+z^{2}N_{0})={z^{2}}\,(N_{0}+z^{-2})

has a bounded inverse and, by the functional calculus for
N_{0}
,

\|(1+z^{2}N_{0})^{-1}\|=\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(N_{0})}\,\frac{1}{|1+z^{2}\lambda|}\leq\sup_{0\leq\lambda\leq\|N_{0}\|}\,\frac{1}{|1+z^{2}\lambda|}=\left(\inf_{0\leq\lambda\leq\|N_{0}\|}\,|1+z^{2}\lambda|\right)^{-1}\,.

The quadratic polynomial

p(\lambda):=|1+z^{2}\lambda|^{2}=\big(1+(c^{2}-\gamma^{2})\lambda\big)^{2}+4c^{2}\gamma^{2}\lambda^{2}=(\gamma^{2}+c^{2})^{2}\lambda^{2}-2(\gamma^{2}-c^{2})\lambda+1

has minimum at

where it assumes the value

Therefore,

\inf_{0\leq\lambda\leq\|N_{0}\|}\,p(\lambda)=\begin{cases}p(0)&\lambda_{\circ}<0\\

p(\|N_{0}\|)&\lambda_{\circ}>\|N_{0}\|\,\,.\end{cases}

However, since

p(\lambda_{\circ})&\lambda_{\circ}\geq 0\end{cases}=\begin{cases}1&|\gamma|\leq c\\
4c^{2}\gamma^{2}|z|^{-4}&|\gamma|>c\ .\end{cases}

∎

Proof.

1)

\displaystyle\,\varepsilon^{1/2}\,\frac{e^{tc}}{c^{1/2}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{c}\frac{d\gamma}{(c^{2}+\gamma^{2})^{n}}+\int_{0}^{c}\frac{d\gamma}{(c^{2}+\gamma^{2})^{n+1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int_{c}^{c_{1}(c/\varepsilon)^{1/4}}\bigg(\frac{c}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}+\frac{c^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}+1\bigg)\,\frac{d\gamma}{(c^{2}+\gamma^{2})^{n-1}}\bigg)\|\Delta^{n+1}\psi\|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\varepsilon^{1/2}\,\frac{e^{tc}}{c^{1/2}}\bigg(\frac{1}{c^{2n-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\gamma}{(1+\gamma^{2})^{n}}+\frac{1}{c^{2n+1}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\gamma}{(1+\gamma^{2})^{n+1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{c^{2n-1}}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{c^{2}\gamma^{2}}+\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}+1\bigg)\,\frac{d\gamma}{(1+\gamma^{2})^{2n-1}}\bigg)\|\Delta^{n+1}\psi\|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\varepsilon^{1/2}\,\frac{e^{tc}}{c^{1/2}}\bigg(\frac{1}{c^{2n-1}}+\frac{1}{c^{2n+1}}\bigg)\|\Delta^{n+1}\psi\|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\varepsilon^{1/2}\,\frac{e^{tc}}{c^{2n+3/2}}\ \|\Delta^{n+1}\psi\|

∎

Lemma 6.2
.

\|I_{2}(t,\varepsilon)\|\lesssim\varepsilon^{1/2}\,\frac{e^{tc}}{c^{2n+1}}\ \|\Delta^{n+1}\psi\|\,.

Proof.

By Lemmata
4.4
,
4.5
,
4.6
and
4.7
(with
s=5/4
),

\displaystyle\|I_{2}(t,\varepsilon)\|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,e^{tc}\int_{0}^{c_{1}(c/\varepsilon)^{1/4}}\!\!\!\|G_{c+i\gamma}(\varepsilon)\|^{2}\|\Lambda_{c+i\gamma}(\varepsilon)\|\,\frac{d\gamma}{(c^{2}+\gamma^{2})^{n}}\ \|\Delta^{n+1}\psi\|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\varepsilon^{1/2}\,e^{tc}\bigg(\int_{0}^{c}\bigg(1+\frac{1}{c^{2}+\gamma^{2}}\bigg)^{\!\!5/4}\!\!\frac{c^{2}(1+c)}{(c^{2}+\gamma^{2})^{3/4}}\,\frac{d\gamma}{(c^{2}+\gamma^{2})^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\varepsilon^{1/2}\,e^{tc}\bigg(\int_{0}^{c}\bigg(1+\frac{1}{c^{2}+\gamma^{2}}\bigg)^{\!\!5/4}\frac{c^{2\,}d\gamma}{(c^{2}+\gamma^{2})^{n+3/4}}

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\varphi\in H^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be supported outside
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. Then

\int_{|x|<t}\!\!\left(\frac{(t-|x|)^{2}}{2\lambda}-1+\cos\bigg(\frac{t-|x|}{\lambda^{1/2}}\bigg)\right){\Delta^{3}\varphi(x)}\,\frac{dx}{|x|}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{|x|<t}\!\!\left(1-\cos\bigg(\frac{t-|x|}{\lambda^{1/2}}\bigg)\right)\Delta^{2}\varphi(x)\,\frac{dx}{|x|}

and

\displaystyle\int_{|x|<t}\left(\frac{(t-|x|)^{3}}{6\lambda^{3/2}}-\frac{t-|x|}{\lambda^{1/2}}+\sin\bigg(\frac{t-|x|}{\lambda^{1/2}}\right)\bigg){\Delta^{3}\varphi(x)}\,\frac{dx}{|x|}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{|x|<t}\sin\bigg(\frac{t-|x|}{\lambda^{1/2}}\bigg)\Delta\varphi(x)\,\frac{dx}{|x|}\,.

Proof.

For any
g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and for any
x\not=0
, one has

\nabla\left(\frac{g(|x|)}{|x|}\right)=\left(g^{\prime}(|x|)-\,\frac{g(|x|)}{|x|}\right)\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\,,\qquad\Delta\left(\frac{g(|x|)}{|x|}\right)=\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(|x|)}{|x|}\,.

Hence, by Green’s formula and by
0\notin\text{supp}(\varphi)\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
,

\displaystyle\int_{|x|<t}\frac{g(|x|)}{|x|}\,{\Delta^{n}\varphi(x)}\,dx=\int_{|x|<t}\Delta\left(\frac{g(|x|)}{|x|}\right)\,\Delta^{n-1}\varphi(x)\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{g(t)}{t^{2}}\int_{|x|=t}x\!\cdot\!\nabla\Delta^{n-1}\varphi(x)\,d\sigma_{t}(x)-\frac{1}{t}\int_{|x|=t}x\!\cdot\!\nabla\left(\frac{g(|x|)}{|x|}\right)\Delta^{n-1}\varphi(x)\,d\sigma_{t}(x)

(6.1)

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|<t}\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(|x|)}{|x|}\,\Delta^{n-1}\varphi(x)\,dx+\frac{g(t)}{t^{2}}\int_{|x|=t}x\!\cdot\!\nabla\Delta^{n-1}\varphi(x)\,d\sigma_{t}(x)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{t}\left(g^{\prime}(t)-\,\frac{g(t)}{t}\right)\int_{|x|=t}\Delta^{n-1}\varphi(x)\,d\sigma_{t}(x)\,.

Taking
g(s)=\frac{(t-s)^{2}}{2\lambda}-1+\cos\left(\frac{t-s}{\lambda^{1/2}}\right)
, one gets, by (
6
) and by
g(t)=g^{\prime}(t)=0

\displaystyle\int_{|x|<t}\left(\frac{(t-|x|)^{2}}{2\lambda}-1+\cos\bigg(\frac{t-|x|}{\lambda^{1/2}}\bigg)\right){\Delta^{3}\varphi(x)}\,\frac{dx}{|x|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{|x|<t}\left(1-\cos\bigg(\frac{t-|x|}{\lambda^{1/2}}\bigg)\right)\Delta^{2}\varphi(x)\,\frac{dx}{|x|}\,.

Taking
g(s)=\frac{(t-s)^{3}}{6\lambda^{3/2}}-\frac{t-s}{\lambda^{1/2}}+\sin\left(\frac{t-s}{\lambda^{1/2}}\right)
, one gets, by using (
6
) twice and by
g(t)=g^{\prime}(t)=g^{\prime\prime}(t)=g^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)=0
,

\displaystyle\int_{|x|<t}\left(\frac{(t-|x|)^{3}}{6\lambda^{3/2}}-\frac{t-|x|}{\lambda^{1/2}}+\sin\bigg(\frac{t-|x|}{\lambda^{1/2}}\bigg)\right){\Delta^{3}\varphi(x)}\,\frac{dx}{|x|}

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|<t|}\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(|x|)}{|x|}\,\Delta^{2}\varphi(x)\,dx=\int_{|x|<t|}\frac{g^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(|x|)}{|x|}\,\Delta\varphi(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{|x|<t}\sin\bigg(\frac{t-|x|}{\lambda^{1/2}}\bigg)\Delta\varphi(x)\,\frac{dx}{|x|}\,.

∎

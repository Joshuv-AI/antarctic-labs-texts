Abstract
. Let 𝕜 \Bbbk be a field of characteristic zero and let 𝕎 n = Der ⁡ ( 𝕜 ⁡ [ x 1 , ⋯ , x n ] ) \mathbb{W}_{n}=\Der(\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]) be the n th n^{\text{th}} general Cartan type Lie algebra. In this paper, we study Lie subalgebras L L of 𝕎 n \mathbb{W}_{n} of maximal Gelfand–Kirillov (GK) dimension, that is, with GKdim ⁡ ( L ) = n \GKdim(L)=n . For n = 1 n=1 , we completely classify such L L , proving a conjecture of the second author. As a corollary, we obtain a new proof that 𝕎 1 \mathbb{W}_{1} satisfies the Dixmier conjecture, in other words, End ⁡ ( 𝕎 1 ) ∖ { 0 } = Aut ⁡ ( 𝕎 1 ) \End(\mathbb{W}_{1})\setminus\{0\}=\Aut(\mathbb{W}_{1}) , a result first shown by Du. For arbitrary n n , we show that if L L is a GK-dimension n n subalgebra of 𝕎 n \mathbb{W}_{n} , then U ⁡ ( L ) U(L) is not (left or right) noetherian.

Introduction

Throughout,
\Bbbk
denotes a field of characteristic zero. All vector spaces are
\Bbbk
-vector spaces. For brevity, we say that a
\Bbbk
-algebra is
noetherian
if it is left and right noetherian.

The
Lie algebras of general Cartan type
(also known as
Cartan type
W
Lie algebras
), are denoted
\mathbb{W}_{n}=\Der(\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}])
for
n\geq 1
. For simplicity of notation, we write
\mathbb{W}_{1}=\Der(\Bbbk[t])=\Bbbk[t]\partial
, where
\partial=\frac{d}{dt}
. Subalgebras of these Lie algebras form an important class of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras, which includes, for example, all the other Cartan type Lie algebras. In this paper, we study Lie subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
of maximal Gelfand–Kirillov (GK) dimension, in other words, subalgebras of GK-dimension
n
.

We begin by studying subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
of maximal GK-dimension (equivalently, infinite-dimensional subalgebras). Letting
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\Bbbk
, some examples of such subalgebras include:

(1)

f\mathbb{W}_{1}
, known as
submodule-subalgebras
, because they are also
\Bbbk[t]
-submodules of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
under the natural action of
\Bbbk[t]
. These have finite codimension in
\mathbb{W}_{1}
.

(2)

L(f)=\mathbb{W}_{1}\cap\Der(\Bbbk[f])
, where this intersection is taken in
\Der(\Bbbk(t))=\Bbbk(t)\partial
upon identifying
\Der(\Bbbk[f])=\frac{1}{f^{\prime}}\Bbbk[f]\partial\subseteq\Bbbk(t)\partial
. These are not
\Bbbk[t]
-submodules of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
, but they are still
\Bbbk[f]
-submodules. If
\deg(f)\geq 2
, then
L(f)
has infinite codimension in
\mathbb{W}_{1}
.

(3)

\Bbbk[f]
-submodules of
L(f)
. Note that this generalises (
1
) and (
2
).

We show that, up to a finite-dimensional vector space, this is an exhaustive list of infinite-dimensional subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
.

Theorem 0.1

(Theorem
1.8
)
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
. Then there exist
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\Bbbk
and a
\Bbbk[f]
-submodule
L^{\prime}
of
L(f)
such that

L^{\prime}\subseteq L\subseteq L(f).

In particular,
L
has finite codimension in
L(f)
.

This establishes
[
Buz23
, Conjecture 5.1]
. We remark that the case where
L
has finite codimension in
\mathbb{W}_{1}
is due to Petukhov and Sierra
[
PS23
, Proposition 3.2.7]
.

The inclusion
L\subseteq L(f)
is
[
Buz23
, Theorem 5.3]
, where
f
is chosen in a “canonical” way: it is the generator of the
field of ratios

F(L)=\Bbbk(f)
(see Definition
1.10
). To prove Theorem
0.1
, we show that
L
has finite codimension in
L(f)
, which is enough to construct
L^{\prime}\subseteq L
. That
L
has finite codimension in
L(f)
is shown by exploiting Hensel’s lemma to write an element
g\partial\in L
as
g\partial=s^{d}\partial_{s}
, where
s=t+\text{lower degree terms}\in\Bbbk((t^{-1}))
and
\partial_{s}=\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}\partial
. We then prove that every element of
L
can be written as a (possibly infinite) linear combination of terms of the form
s^{n}\partial_{s}
, from which we deduce that
L
has finite codimension in
L(f)
.

We then present some applications of Theorem
0.1
. First, we consider the noetherianity of universal enveloping algebras of subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
. The question of whether it is possible for an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra to have a noetherian universal enveloping algebra is long-standing, having first appeared fifty years ago in Amayo and Stewart’s book on infinite-dimensional Lie algebras
[
AS74
, Question 27]
.

While it is widely believed that enveloping algebras of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras are never noetherian
[
SW14
, Conjecture 0.1]
, there are very few examples whose non-noetherianity is known. One of the most significant milestones since the question was posed by Amayo and Stewart was Sierra and Walton’s proof that
U(\mathbb{W}_{1})
is not noetherian
[
SW14
, Theorem 0.5]
.

As a consequence of the classification of subalgebras from Theorem
0.1
, it follows that an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
is isomorphic to a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
of finite codimension. Enveloping algebras of finite codimension subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
are known to be non-noetherian
[
Buz23
]
. This immediately implies that enveloping algebras of infinite-dimensional subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
are not noetherian, confirming
[
Buz23
, Conjecture 4.1]
.

Corollary 0.2

(Corollary
3.2
)
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
. Then
U(L)
is not noetherian.

Another consequence of Theorem
0.1
is that
\mathbb{W}_{1}
satisfies the Dixmier conjecture. While this had previously been established in
[
Du04
]
, we provide a more conceptual proof by showing that
\mathbb{W}_{1}
does not have any proper infinite-dimensional simple subalgebras.

Corollary 0.3

(Corollary
3.4
)
.

The Lie algebra
\mathbb{W}_{1}
satisfies the Dixmier conjecture. In other words,
\End(\mathbb{W}_{1})\setminus\{0\}=\Aut(\mathbb{W}_{1})
.

This (re)proves the one-variable case of
[
Zha98
, Conjecture 1]
, which says that the Dixmier conjecture holds for all
\mathbb{W}_{n}
. Zhao’s conjecture, which is open for two or more variables, is exceptionally difficult and has deep implications; Zhao himself proved that it implies the Jacobian conjecture
[
Zha98
, Theorem 4.1]
.

The last part of the paper is devoted to extending the non-noetherianity result of Corollary
0.2
to maximal dimensional subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
for arbitrary
n
.

Theorem 0.4

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
L
be a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
of GK-dimension
n
. Then
U(L)
is not noetherian.

The proof of Theorem
0.4
is achieved by constructing subalgebras
L_{ij}
of
L
such that
L_{ij}\supseteq L_{i,j+1}
for all
i,j
, and studying the dimensions of successive quotients

d_{ij}(L)\coloneqq\dim_{\Bbbk}(L_{ij}/L_{i,j+1}).

One of the key steps is deducing that if
\GKdim(L)=n
and
d_{0,0}(L)=\infty
, then
U(L)
is not noetherian (see Corollary
4.11
). We then show that (possibly after changing variables), if
\GKdim(L)=n
then we must have
d_{0,0}(L)=\infty
, allowing us to deduce that
U(L)
is not noetherian.

1.
Preliminaries

We begin by recalling the definition of a derivation of an associative algebra, and defining the Lie algebras of interest in this paper.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
A
be a
\Bbbk
-algebra. A
derivation
of
A
is a
\Bbbk
-linear map
D\colon A\to A
such that
D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b)
for all
a,b\in A
. We denote the set of all derivations of
A
by
\Der(A)
.

For
n\geq 1
, we write

\mathbb{W}_{n}=\Der(\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}])=\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\partial_{1}+\cdots+\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\partial_{n},

where
\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
denotes differentiation with respect to
x_{i}
. The Lie algebra
\mathbb{W}_{1}
is the
one-sided Witt algebra
. To simplify notation, we write
\mathbb{W}_{1}=\Bbbk[t]\partial
, where
\partial=\frac{d}{dt}
.

In this section, we classify infinite-dimensional subalgebras of the one-sided Witt algebra. The Lie bracket of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
is given by

[f\partial,g\partial]=(fg^{\prime}-f^{\prime}g)\partial

for
f,g\in\Bbbk[t]
. It has basis
\{e_{n}=t^{n+1}\partial\mid n\geq-1\}
such that
[e_{n},e_{m}]=(m-n)e_{n+m}
. We will also consider the Lie algebras
\Bbbk(t)\partial
and
\Bbbk((t^{-1}))\partial
with the obvious Lie brackets, and view
\mathbb{W}_{1}
as a subalgebra of both of these.

We now introduce some noteworthy subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
. There is a natural action of
\Bbbk[t]
on
\mathbb{W}_{1}
, and the following subalgebras are precisely the subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
that are also
\Bbbk[t]
-submodules.

Definition 1.2
.

A
submodule-subalgebra
of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
which is also a
\Bbbk[t]
-submodule. They are of the form

f\mathbb{W}_{1}=\{g\partial\in\mathbb{W}_{1}\mid f\text{ divides }g\},

for
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
.

Note that submodule-subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
have finite codimension in
\mathbb{W}_{1}
: we have
\dim_{\Bbbk}(\mathbb{W}_{1}/f\mathbb{W}_{1})=\deg(f)
. Subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
of finite codimension were classified in
[
PS23
]
: they are “very close” to being submodule-subalgebras. Although the result in
[
PS23
]
considers subalgebras of the
Witt algebra

W=\Der(\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}])
, the analogous result is true for subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
with a nearly identical proof.

Proposition 1.3

(
[
PS23
, Proposition 3.2.7]
)
.

Let
L
be a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
of finite codimension. Then there exist
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

f^{n}\mathbb{W}_{1}\subseteq L\subseteq f\mathbb{W}_{1}.

Therefore, it remains to classify subalgebras of infinite codimension. In
[
Buz23
]
, a conjectural classification was given, which we prove in the present paper. In order to state the classification, we need to introduce notation for some subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
.

Notation 1.4
.

For
f,g\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
such that
f^{\prime}g\in\Bbbk[f]
, we write
L(f,g)=\Bbbk[f]g\partial
.

Letting
g_{f}
be the unique monic polynomial of minimal degree such that
f^{\prime}g_{f}\in\Bbbk[f]
, we write
L(f)
instead of
L(f,g_{f})
. We write
h_{f}\in\Bbbk[t]
for the polynomial such that
f^{\prime}g_{f}=h_{f}(f)
.

Remark 1.5
.

(1)

By
[
Buz23
, Proposition 4.13]
,
L(f,g)\subseteq L(f)
for all
f,g\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
f^{\prime}g\in\Bbbk[f]
.

(2)

We could define
L(f,g)=\Bbbk[f]g\partial
for any pair of polynomials
f,g\in\Bbbk[t]
. However, this is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
if and only if
f^{\prime}g\in\Bbbk[f]

[
Buz23
, Lemma 4.12]
.

The Lie algebras
L(f,g)
seem much more mysterious than submodule-subalgebras, so we briefly explain their origin. The condition that
f^{\prime}g\in\Bbbk[f]
implies that
g\partial
is a derivation of
\Bbbk[f]
. Indeed, if
f^{\prime}g=h(f)
, where
h\in\Bbbk[t]
, then

g\partial(p(f))=f^{\prime}gp^{\prime}(f)=h(f)p^{\prime}(f)\in\Bbbk[f],

where
p\in\Bbbk[t]
. Hence,
g\partial=h(f)\partial_{f}
as an element of
\Der(\Bbbk[f])=\Bbbk[f]\partial_{f}
, where
\partial_{f}
denotes differentiation with respect to
f
. Consequently, we introduce the following notation.

Notation 1.6
.

For
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\Bbbk
, we write
\mathbb{W}[f]=\Der(\Bbbk[f])
. Submodule-subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}[f]
are denoted by
g(f)\mathbb{W}[f]=g(f)\Bbbk[f]\partial_{f}=\{h(f)\partial\mid g\text{ divides }h\}
(these are
\Bbbk[f]
-submodules of
\mathbb{W}[f]
).

Clearly,
\mathbb{W}[f]\cong\mathbb{W}_{1}
and
h(f)\mathbb{W}[f]\cong h\mathbb{W}_{1}
as Lie algebras. By the above discussion,
L(f,g)
can be viewed as the submodule-subalgebra
h(f)\mathbb{W}[f]
of
\mathbb{W}[f]
, where
h\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
f^{\prime}g=h(f)
. We therefore immediately see that
L(f,g)\cong h\mathbb{W}_{1}
.

The Lie algebra
\mathbb{W}[f]
can be naturally viewed as a subalgebra of
\Bbbk(t)\partial
by identifying
\partial_{f}
with
\frac{1}{f^{\prime}}\partial
, which is the unique extension of
\partial_{f}
to a derivation of
\Bbbk(t)
. In other words, we make the identification
\mathbb{W}[f]=\frac{1}{f^{\prime}}\Bbbk[f]\partial\subseteq\Bbbk(t)\partial
. Now,
L(f)\subseteq\mathbb{W}_{1}\cap\mathbb{W}[f]
, viewing this as an intersection in
\Bbbk(t)\partial
. It is therefore natural to ask whether
L(f)=\mathbb{W}_{1}\cap\mathbb{W}[f]
. The following result answers this question positively.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\Bbbk
. Then
L(f)=\mathbb{W}_{1}\cap\mathbb{W}[f]
.

Proof.

As noted above,
L(f)\subseteq\mathbb{W}_{1}\cap\mathbb{W}[f]
, so it remains to show that
\mathbb{W}_{1}\cap\mathbb{W}[f]\subseteq L(f)
.

Let
u\in\mathbb{W}_{1}\cap\mathbb{W}[f]\setminus\{0\}
. Since
u\in\mathbb{W}[f]=\frac{1}{f^{\prime}}\Bbbk[f]\partial
, there exists
h\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
u=\frac{h(f)}{f^{\prime}}\partial
. Similarly,
u
being an element of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
implies that
u=g\partial
for some
g\in\Bbbk[t]
. Therefore, we have
f^{\prime}g=h(f)\in\Bbbk[f]
, and thus
u=g\partial\in L(f,g)\subseteq L(f)
, where this last inclusion is from Remark
1.5
.
∎

We now state the classification of subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
first conjectured in
[
Buz23
]
.

Theorem 1.8

(cf.
[
Buz23
, Conjecture 5.1]
)
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
. Then there exist
f,g\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
f^{\prime}g\in\Bbbk[f]
and

L(f,g)\subseteq L\subseteq L(f).

In particular,
L
has finite codimension in
L(f)
.

Furthermore,
f
are
g
are unique under the following assumptions:

•

f
is monic and
f(0)=0
;

•

g
is monic and has minimal degree such that
L(f,g)\subseteq L
.

In other words, if
\widetilde{f}
and
\widetilde{g}
are polynomials such that
L(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g})\subseteq L\subseteq L(\widetilde{f})
, then
\widetilde{f}=\alpha f+\beta
and
\widetilde{g}=p(f)g
, for some
\alpha,\beta\in\Bbbk
with
\alpha\neq 0
and
p\in\Bbbk[t]
. In particular,
L(\widetilde{f})=L(f)
and
L(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g})\subseteq L(f,g)
.

As noted in
[
Buz23
]
, in order to prove Theorem
1.8
, it suffices to prove that if
L
is an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
, then there exists a polynomial
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
such that
L
has finite codimension in
L(f)
. For completeness, we give a proof of this fact.

Lemma 1.9
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
and let
L
be a subalgebra of
L(f)
of finite codimension. Then there exists a polynomial
g\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
such that
L(f,g)\subseteq L
.

Proof.

Let
h\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
f^{\prime}g_{f}=h(f)
. There is an isomorphism

\displaystyle\varphi\colon L(f)

\displaystyle\to h\mathbb{W}_{1}

\displaystyle p(f)g_{f}\partial

\displaystyle\mapsto p(t)h\partial,

for
p\in\Bbbk[t]
. Now,
\varphi(L)
is a subalgebra of
h\mathbb{W}_{1}
of finite codimension, so by Proposition
1.3
, there exists
p\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
ph\mathbb{W}_{1}\subseteq\varphi(L)
. Let
g=p(f)g_{f}
. Note that
f^{\prime}g\in\Bbbk[f]
and
\varphi^{-1}(ph\mathbb{W}_{1})=L(f,g)
, which immediately implies that
L(f,g)\subseteq L
.
∎

For the next result, we will need the following notation.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
L
be a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
. The
set of ratios
of
L
is

R(L)=\left\{\frac{p}{q}\in\Bbbk(t)\mid p\partial,q\partial\in L,q\neq 0\right\}.

The
field of ratios
of
L
, denoted
F(L)
, is the subfield of
\Bbbk(t)
generated by
R(L)
.

The following result from
[
Buz23
]
will be crucial in our proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Proposition 1.11

(
[
Buz23
, Theorem 5.3]
)
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
. Then there exists a polynomial
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
such that
F(L)=\Bbbk(f)
and
L\subseteq L(f)
.

By Lemma
1.9
and Proposition
1.11
, it suffices to prove that if
L
is an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
and
F(L)=\Bbbk(f)
, where
f\in\Bbbk[t]
, then
L
has finite codimension in
L(f)
. In order to prove this, we will analyse the degrees of elements of
L
.

Notation 1.12
.

For
f\in\Bbbk[t]
, we let the
degree
of
f\partial\in\mathbb{W}_{1}
be
\deg(f\partial)=\deg(f)-1
. For a subalgebra
L
of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
, we write
d(L)=\gcd\{\deg(u)\mid u\in L\}
.

The following lemma shows that an infinite-dimensional subalgebra
L\subseteq\mathbb{W}_{1}
contains elements whose degrees are arbitrarily large multiples of
d(L)
.

Lemma 1.13

(
[
Buz23
, Lemma 4.7]
)
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
and let
d=d(L)
. Then there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
m\geq n
, there is an element
u_{m}\in L
such that
\deg(u_{m})=md
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
and let
f\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
F(L)=\Bbbk(f)
and
L\subseteq L(f)
, which exists by Proposition
1.11
. It is clear that
d(L(f))=\deg(f)
. By Lemma
1.13
, if
\deg(f)=d(L)
then
L
has finite codimension in
L(f)
. Therefore, in order to prove Theorem
1.8
, it suffices to prove that
\deg(f)=d(L)
. The next section is devoted to proving this fact.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.8

As noted above, the goal of this section is to prove the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
and let
f\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
F(L)=\Bbbk(f)
and
L\subseteq L(f)
. Then
\deg(f)=d(L)
.

The next result is an application of Hensel’s lemma which shows that any element
u\in\mathbb{W}_{1}
can be written as
u=s^{d}\partial_{s}
for some
s\in\Bbbk((t^{-1}))
of degree 1, where
d=\deg(u)+1
. Note that, as before, we make the identification
\partial_{s}=\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}\partial
.

This will be used as follows: if
L
is an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
, then we can choose
u\in L\setminus\{0\}
and write it as
u=s^{d}\partial_{s}
, where
s
and
d
are as above. It will then follow that all elements of
L
are spanned by elements of the form
s^{kd(L)+1}\partial_{s}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. From this, we deduce Proposition
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
g\in\Bbbk[t]
is monic of degree
d\geq 1
, then there exists
s\in\Bbbk((t^{-1}))
with
s=t+{\rm lower~degree~terms}
such that
g\partial=s^{d}\partial_{s}=\frac{s^{d}}{s^{\prime}}\partial
.

Proof.

The proof is achieved by Hensel’s lemma. We claim that for all
n\geq 0
, there exist
s_{n}\in\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]
such that
s_{i+1}=s_{i}+O(t^{-i})
and
s_{i}^{d}\partial_{s_{i}}=(g+O(t^{d-i-1}))\partial
for
i\geq 0
, or equivalently,
s_{i}^{d}/s_{i}^{\prime}=g+O(t^{d-i-1})
. We prove this by induction. We let
s_{0}=t
. Then
s_{0}^{d}\partial_{s_{0}}=(g+O(t^{d-1}))\partial
, so we have the base case.

Now suppose we have constructed
s_{0},\cdots,s_{n}
. Then
s_{n}^{d}/s_{n}^{\prime}=(g+O(t^{d-n-1}))
, so, noting that
s_{n}^{\prime}=1+O(t^{-1})
,

(2.2.1)

s_{n}^{d}=s_{n}^{\prime}(g+\alpha t^{d-n-1})+O(t^{d-n-2})

for some
\alpha\in\Bbbk
. We let
s_{n+1}=s_{n}+ct^{-n}
, where
c\in\Bbbk
is a constant to be determined later. Then

s_{n+1}^{d}=(s_{n}+ct^{-n})^{d}=s_{n}^{d}+cdt^{d-n-1}+O(t^{d-n-2}).

By (
2.2.1
), we have
s_{n+1}^{d}=s_{n}^{\prime}(g+\alpha t^{d-n-1})+cdt^{d-n-1}+O(t^{d-n-2})
. Since
s_{n}^{\prime}=1+O(t^{-1})
, it follows that

s_{n+1}^{d}=s_{n}^{\prime}(g+(\alpha+cd)t^{d-n-1})+O(t^{d-n-2}).

Noting that
s_{n}^{\prime}=s_{n+1}^{\prime}+cnt^{-n-1}
, we get

\displaystyle s_{n+1}^{d}

\displaystyle=(s_{n+1}^{\prime}+cnt^{-n-1})(g+(\alpha+cd)t^{d-n-1})+O(t^{d-n-2})

\displaystyle=s_{n+1}^{\prime}(g+(\alpha+c(n+d))t^{d-n-1}+O(t^{d-n-2})),

where we used that
g=t^{d}+O(t^{d-1})
and
s_{n+1}^{\prime}=1+O(t^{-1})
. Therefore, taking
c=-\frac{\alpha}{n+d}
, we see that
s_{n+1}^{d}/s_{n+1}^{\prime}=g+O(t^{d-n-2})
, as required.
∎

Notice that the
s
we have constructed is of the form

t+{\rm lower~degree~terms}
. In particular,
\Bbbk((t^{-1}))=\Bbbk((s^{-1}))
and so
\Bbbk((s^{-1}))\partial_{s}=\Bbbk((t^{-1}))\partial
. Moreover,
\mathbb{W}_{1}
is a subalgebra of
\Bbbk((t^{-1}))\partial
, so we can work in the Lie algebra
\Bbbk((s^{-1}))\partial_{s}
. We will consider the following subalgebras of
\Bbbk((s^{-1}))\partial_{s}
, which we refer to as
Veronese subalgebras
.

Notation 2.3
.

Let
s\in\Bbbk((t^{-1}))
such that
s=t+{\rm lower~degree~terms}
and let
d\geq 1
. We let
V_{d}(s)=\Bbbk((s^{-d}))s\partial_{s}
. If
s=t
, we simply write
V_{d}
instead of
V_{d}(t)
.

The Veronese subalgebra
V_{d}
for
d\geq 1
is simply the subalgebra of
\Bbbk((t^{-1}))\partial
whose elements are of the form
\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n}\alpha_{k}e_{kd}
, where
\alpha_{k}\in\Bbbk
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. In other words, when we write elements of
V_{d}
as (possibly infinite) linear combinations of the elements
e_{n}
, the only terms that appear are those whose indices are multiples of
d
.

Similarly, the Lie algebras
V_{d}(s)
consist of elements of the form
\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n}\alpha_{k}s^{kd+1}\partial_{s}
, where
\alpha_{k}\in\Bbbk
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, which is precisely what we get from
V_{d}
under the change of variables
t\mapsto s
.

Given an infinite-dimensional subalgebra
L\subseteq\mathbb{W}_{1}
, we would like to show that
L\subseteq V_{d(L)}(s)
for some suitable choice of
s\in\Bbbk((t^{-1}))
, which would immediately imply that
F(L)\subseteq\Bbbk((s^{-d(L)}))
. Letting
f\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
F(L)=\Bbbk(f)
, it would then follow that
\deg(f)=d(L)
.

The following result gives useful restrictions for Lie algebras
L
not contained in
V_{d(L)}
. When we write
e_{n}+\cdots
, we mean
e_{n}+{\rm lower~degree~terms}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
L
be a subalgebra of
\Bbbk((t^{-1}))\partial
and let
d=d(L)
. Suppose
L
is not contained in
V_{d}
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be minimal such that there is an element

a=e_{n}+\cdots+\alpha e_{n-k}+\cdots\in L,

where
n=rd
for some
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
d
does not divide
k
, and
\alpha\in\Bbbk\setminus\{0\}
. Let
b\in L
be a monic element such that
m=\deg(b)\neq n
, and let
\beta\in\Bbbk
be the coefficient of
e_{m-k}
in
b
(so

, where

for some

). Then
(m+k)\alpha=(n+k)\beta
. In particular,
\beta\neq 0
.

Proof.

Step 1.
We begin by proving this when
m=2n
, i.e.

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=e_{n}+\cdots+\alpha e_{n-k}+\cdots,

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=e_{2n}+\cdots+\beta e_{2n-k}+\cdots.

By taking Lie brackets of

with
b
, we get two different elements of degree
5n
in

, as follows:

\displaystyle[a,b]

\displaystyle=ne_{3n}+\cdots+((n+k)\alpha+(n-k)\beta)e_{3n-k}+\cdots

\displaystyle[a,[a,b]]

\displaystyle=2n^{2}e_{4n}+\cdots+((4n^{2}+2kn-k^{2})\alpha+(2n^{2}-3kn+k^{2})\beta)e_{4n-k}+\cdots

\displaystyle[a,[a,[a,b]]]

\displaystyle=6n^{3}e_{5n}+\cdots

\displaystyle\quad+((18n^{3}+4kn^{2}-5k^{2}n+k^{3})\alpha+(6n^{3}-11kn^{2}+6k^{2}n-k^{3})\beta)e_{5n-k}+\cdots

\displaystyle[b,[a,b]]

\displaystyle=n^{2}e_{5n}+\cdots+((n^{2}-k^{2})\alpha+(2n^{2}-kn+k^{2})\beta)e_{5n-k}+\cdots,

where we used the minimality of

to ensure there are no other contributions to the coefficients. Let

. We see that

(recall that

. Hence, we have

(18n^{3}+4kn^{2}-5k^{2}n+k^{3})\alpha+(6n^{3}-11kn^{2}+6k^{2}n-k^{3})\beta=6n((n^{2}-k^{2})\alpha+(2n^{2}-kn+k^{2})\beta),

and therefore

(2.4.1)

(12n^{3}+4kn^{2}+k^{2}n+k^{3})\alpha=(6n^{3}+5kn^{2}+k^{3})\beta.

We have

\displaystyle 12n^{3}+4kn^{2}+k^{2}n+k^{3}

\displaystyle=(2n+k)(6n^{2}-kn+k^{2}),

\displaystyle 6n^{3}+5kn^{2}+k^{3}

\displaystyle=(n+k)(6n^{2}-kn+k^{2}).

We claim that

. Note that
-1\leq n-k<n
. If

, then

. Since

does not divide

. Therefore, the only other possibility is

. In this case, we have

. This proves the claim.

Dividing (
2.4.1
) by
(6n^{2}-kn+k^{2})
, we get

(2n+k)\alpha=(n+k)\beta.

Step 2.
We now prove the result for a general

. We have

\displaystyle[a,b]

\displaystyle=(m-n)e_{n+m}+\cdots+((m-n+k)\alpha+(m-n-k)\beta)e_{n+m-k}+\cdots

\displaystyle[a,[a,b]]

\displaystyle=m(m-n)e_{2n+m}+\cdots

\displaystyle+((2m^{2}-2nm+km-k^{2})\alpha+(m^{2}-nm+kn-2km+k^{2})\beta)e_{2n+m-k}+\cdots

\displaystyle[b,[a,[a,b]]]

\displaystyle=2nm(m-n)e_{2(n+m)}+\cdots

\displaystyle+(4nm^{2}-4n^{2}m-2km^{2}+4knm-2k^{2}n-k^{2}m+k^{3})\alpha e_{2(n+m)-k}

\displaystyle+(4nm^{2}-4n^{2}m+2kn^{2}-4knm+k^{2}n+2k^{2}m-k^{3})\beta e_{2(n+m)-k}+\cdots.

Let
a^{\prime}=\frac{1}{m-n}[a,b]\in L
, and let
b^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2nm(m-n)}[b,[a,[a,b]]]\in L
. Letting
\alpha^{\prime}
be the coefficient of
e_{n+m-k}
in
a^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}
be the coefficient of

in

, we see that

(2(n+m)+k)\alpha^{\prime}=(n+m+k)\beta^{\prime},

by Step 1. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\frac{(2n+2m+k)(m-n+k)}{m-n}\alpha+\frac{(2n+2m+k)(m-n-k)}{m-n}\beta

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt=\frac{(n+m+k)(4nm^{2}-4n^{2}m-2km^{2}+4knm-2k^{2}n-k^{2}m+k^{3})}{2nm(m-n)}\alpha

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\quad+\frac{(n+m+k)(4nm^{2}-4n^{2}m+2kn^{2}-4knm+k^{2}n+2k^{2}m-k^{3})}{2nm(m-n)}\beta,

and hence

\displaystyle(2km^{3}+2kn^{2}m+2k^{2}n^{2}+3

\displaystyle k^{2}m^{2}+k^{2}nm+k^{3}n-k^{4})\alpha

(2.4.2)

\displaystyle=(2kn^{3}+2knm^{2}+3k^{2}n^{2}+2k^{2}m^{2}+k^{2}nm+k^{3}m-k^{4})\beta.

Note that

\displaystyle 2km^{3}+2kn^{2}m+2k^{2}n^{2}+3k^{2}m^{2}+k^{2}nm

\displaystyle+k^{3}n-k^{4}

\displaystyle=k(2n^{2}+2m^{2}+kn+km-k^{2})(m+k),

\displaystyle 2kn^{3}+2knm^{2}+3k^{2}n^{2}+2k^{2}m^{2}+k^{2}nm

\displaystyle+k^{3}m-k^{4}

\displaystyle=k(2n^{2}+2m^{2}+kn+km-k^{2})(n+k).

Since
n\neq m
, we must have that
k<n
or
k<m
, and therefore

. Hence, we have

2n^{2}+2m^{2}+kn+km-k^{2}=2n^{2}+2m^{2}+(n+m-k)k>0.

Dividing (
2
) by

, we conclude that

(m+k)\alpha=(n+k)\beta,

as required.
∎

As an immediate consequence of the proposition above, we get a surprising criterion for a subalgebra
L\subseteq\mathbb{W}_{1}
to be contained in
V_{d(L)}(s)
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\Bbbk((t^{-1}))\partial
, let
s\in\Bbbk((t^{-1}))
such that
s=t+{\rm lower~degree~terms}
, and write
d=d(L)
. Then
L\cap V_{d}(s)\neq 0
if and only if
L\subseteq V_{d}(s)
.

Proof.

Suppose
L
is not contained in
V_{d}(s)
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be minimal such that there is an element

a=s^{n+1}\partial_{s}+\cdots+\alpha s^{n-k+1}\partial_{s}+\cdots\in L,

where
n=rd
for some
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
d
does not divide
k
, and
\alpha\in\Bbbk\setminus\{0\}
. Upon changing variables
t\mapsto s
, Proposition
2.4
implies that no nonzero element of
L
can be contained in
V_{d}(s)
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Combining Lemma
2.2
and Corollary
2.5
, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
and let
d=d(L)
. Then there exists
s\in\Bbbk((t^{-1}))
such that
s=t+{\rm lower~degree~terms}
and
L\subseteq V_{d}(s)
.

Proof.

Let
u\in L\setminus\{0\}
. By Lemma
2.2
, there exists
s\in\Bbbk((t^{-1}))
such that
s=t+{\rm lower~degree~terms}
and
u=s^{n}\partial_{s}
, where
n=\deg(u)+1
. Now,
d
divides
\deg(u)=n-1
, so
n=kd+1
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore,
u=s^{kd+1}\partial_{s}\in V_{d}(s)
, and thus
L\cap V_{d}(s)\neq 0
. By Corollary
2.5
, we have
L\subseteq V_{d}(s)
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
2.1
.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Let
d=d(L)
and let
f\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
F(L)=\Bbbk(f)
. Note that
\deg(f)\leq d
: by Lemma
1.13
, there exist elements
p\partial,q\partial\in L\setminus\{0\}
such that
\deg(p)=\deg(q)+d
. Now,
\frac{p}{q}\in\Bbbk(f)
, which would be impossible if
\deg(f)>d
. Therefore, it suffices to prove that
\deg(f)\geq d
.

By Corollary
2.6
, there exists
s=t+\cdots\in\Bbbk((t^{-1}))
such that
L\subseteq V_{d}(s)=\Bbbk((s^{-d}))s\partial_{s}
. Therefore,
F(L)\subseteq\Bbbk((s^{-d}))
, so
f\in\Bbbk((s^{-d}))
.

Note that the valuation on
\Bbbk((t^{-1}))
given by taking the order of the pole at
\infty
has the property that every element of
\Bbbk((s^{-d}))
has valuation a multiple of
d
. On the other hand, this valuation applied to
f
is just the degree of
f
, so
f
has degree a multiple of
d
. Hence,
\deg(f)\geq d
, which completes the proof.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.8
now follows.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]
be such that
F(L)=\Bbbk(f)
, which exists by Proposition
1.11
. Certainly, we can choose
f
to be monic and
f(0)=0
, since
\Bbbk(f)=\Bbbk(\alpha f+\beta)
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Bbbk
with
\alpha\neq 0
. As noted at the end of Section
1
, to prove that
L
has finite codimension in
L(f)
, it suffices to prove that
\deg(f)=d(L)
. This is Proposition
2.1
.

We now prove that
f
is unique. Suppose
\widetilde{f}\in\Bbbk[t]
is such that
L
has finite codimension in
L(\widetilde{f})
. It must be the case that
F(L)\subseteq\Bbbk(\widetilde{f})
. By Lemma
1.9
, there exists
\widetilde{g}\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
such that
L(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g})\subseteq L
, and thus
\Bbbk(\widetilde{f})\subseteq F(L)
. We conclude that

\Bbbk(f)=F(L)=\Bbbk(\widetilde{f}).

This equality implies that the map

\displaystyle\varphi\colon\Bbbk(f)

\displaystyle\to\Bbbk(\widetilde{f})=\Bbbk(f)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\widetilde{f}

is an automorphism. Therefore,
\varphi
is a Möbius transformation, so there exist
a,b,c,d\in\Bbbk
with
ab-cd\neq 0
such that

\widetilde{f}=\varphi(f)=\frac{af+b}{cf+d}.

But
f
and
\widetilde{f}
are non-constant polynomials, so it must be the case that
c=0
. Letting
\alpha=\frac{a}{d}
and
\beta=\frac{b}{d}
, we see that
\widetilde{f}=\alpha f+\beta
, which proves uniqueness of
f
.

Now let
g\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
be a monic polynomial of minimal degree such that
f^{\prime}g\in\Bbbk[f]
and
L(f,g)\subseteq L
(which exists by Lemma
1.9
), and suppose
\widetilde{g}
is another polynomial such that
f^{\prime}\widetilde{g}\in\Bbbk[f]
and
L(f,\widetilde{g})\subseteq L
. Let
q,\widetilde{q}\in\Bbbk[t]
such that

f^{\prime}g=q(f),\quad f^{\prime}\widetilde{g}=\widetilde{q}(f),

and let
r=\gcd(q,\widetilde{q})
. By Bézout’s lemma, there exist
u,v\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
uq+v\widetilde{q}=r
. We have

r(f)=(u(f)q(f)+v(f)\widetilde{q}(f))=(u(f)g+v(f)\widetilde{g})f^{\prime}.

Letting
h=\frac{r(f)}{f^{\prime}}=u(f)g+v(f)\widetilde{g}
, we see that
f^{\prime}h\in\Bbbk[f]
and

L(f,h)=\Bbbk[f]h\partial=\Bbbk[f](u(f)g+v(f)\widetilde{g})\partial\subseteq\Bbbk[f]g\partial+\Bbbk[f]\widetilde{g}\partial=L(f,g)+L(f,\widetilde{g})\subseteq L.

By minimality of
\deg(g)
, it must be the case that
\deg(h)\geq\deg(g)
, which implies that
\deg(r)\geq\deg(q)
. But
r=\gcd(q,\widetilde{q})
, so it follows that
r=q
. Therefore,
q
divides
\widetilde{q}
, so
\widetilde{q}=pq
for some
p\in\Bbbk[t]
. Thus,

\widetilde{g}=\frac{\widetilde{q}(f)}{f^{\prime}}=\frac{p(f)q(f)}{f^{\prime}}=p(f)g

and the uniqueness of
g
follows.
∎

3.
Consequences of Theorem
1.8

In this section, we collect some consequences of Theorem
1.8
. The first result follows from Theorem
1.8
and the observation that for all
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\Bbbk
, we have
L(f)\cong h_{f}\mathbb{W}_{1}
.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
. Then
L
is isomorphic to a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
of finite codimension.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.8
, there exists
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\Bbbk
such that
L
has finite codimension in
L(f)
. Since
L(f)
is isomorphic to a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
of finite codimension, so is
L
.
∎

One of the main questions in
[
Buz23
]
is whether
U(L)
is noetherian if
L
is an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
. Note that when we say
noetherian
, we mean left and right noetherian.

The ring
U(L)
was already known to be non-noetherian if
L
is a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
of finite codimension, but the general case was still open. Thanks to Corollary
3.1
, the general case follows immediately.

Corollary 3.2

(cf.
[
Buz23
, Conjecture 4.1]
)
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
. Then
U(L)
is not noetherian.

Proof.

By
[
SW14
, Theorem 0.5]
,
U(\mathbb{W}_{1})
is not noetherian. Now,
L
is isomorphic to a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
of finite codimension by Corollary
3.1
, so
[
Buz23
, Proposition 2.1]
implies that
U(L)
is not noetherian.
∎

We finish the section by considering endomorphisms of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
. Zhao conjectured that the Lie algebras
\mathbb{W}_{n}
satisfy the Dixmier conjecture for
n\geq 1

[
Zha98
, Conjecture 1]
, in other words, that nonzero endomorphisms of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
are automorphisms. The one-variable case was proved by Du
[
Du04
]
, but the question remains open for two or more variables. This is an extremely difficult question with deep consequences: Zhao showed that his
n
-variable conjecture implies the
n
-dimensional Jacobian conjecture
[
Zha98
, Theorem 4.1]
. In turn, the
2n
-dimensional Jacobian conjecture implies the Dixmier conjecture for the
n^{\text{th}}
Weyl algebra
[
Tsu05
,
BKK07
]
, so Zhao’s conjecture for all
n
also implies the Dixmier conjecture for Weyl algebras.

We give an alternative and more conceptual proof of Du’s result, which follows by showing that proper infinite-dimensional subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
are not simple.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional simple Lie subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
. Then
L=\mathbb{W}_{1}
.

Proof.

Note that
L
cannot be contained in a Lie algebra isomorphic to
f\mathbb{W}_{1}
for any
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\Bbbk
: this is because
f\mathbb{W}_{1}
does not contain any simple subalgebras. An easy way to see this is that nonzero subalgebras of
f\mathbb{W}_{1}
are never perfect, since
[f\mathbb{W}_{1},f\mathbb{W}_{1}]=f^{2}\mathbb{W}_{1}
.

Now, Theorem
1.8
implies that
L
has finite codimension in
L(f)
for some
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\Bbbk
. Let
g_{f},h_{f}\in\Bbbk[t]
be as in Notation
1.4
, so that
f^{\prime}g_{f}=h_{f}(f)\in\Bbbk[f]
. If
\deg(f)>1
, then
\deg(f^{\prime}g_{f})\geq 1
, so
h_{f}
is non-constant. In this case,
L(f)\cong h_{f}\mathbb{W}_{1}
with
h_{f}\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\Bbbk
, which contradicts the first paragraph. Therefore,
\deg(f)=1
, so
L(f)=\mathbb{W}_{1}
, and thus
L
has finite codimension in
\mathbb{W}_{1}
.

By Proposition
1.3
, there exist
g\in\Bbbk[t]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

g^{n}\mathbb{W}_{1}\subseteq L\subseteq g\mathbb{W}_{1}.

By the first paragraph, we see that
g
must be constant, so
L=\mathbb{W}_{1}
.
∎

The computation of endomorphisms of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
now follows easily.

Corollary 3.4
.

The Lie algebra
\mathbb{W}_{1}
satisfies the Dixmier conjecture. In other words,
\End(\mathbb{W}_{1})\setminus\{0\}=\Aut(\mathbb{W}_{1})
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\End(\mathbb{W}_{1})\setminus\{0\}
. Since
\mathbb{W}_{1}
is a simple Lie algebra,
\varphi
must be injective. By Proposition
3.3
,
\varphi
must also be surjective.
∎

It would be interesting to see if a similar study of subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{2}
yields any information about Zhao’s conjecture for two variables. In turn, this would give information about the two-dimensional Jacobian conjecture and the Dixmier conjecture for the first Weyl algebra.

4.
Subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{n}

In this section, we generalise Corollary
3.2
to subalgebras of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
of Gelfand–Kirillov (GK) dimension
n
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
L
be a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
of GK-dimension
n
. Then
U(L)
is not noetherian.

Remark 4.2
.

We note that
\mathbb{W}_{n}
is itself a finitely generated Lie algebra of GK-dimension
n
, so the constraint that
L
has GK-dimension
n
simply says
L
is in some sense a “large” subalgebra.

The key to the proof of Theorem
4.1
is to introduce subalgebras
L_{ij}
of
L
and consider the dimensions of successive quotients
L_{ij}/L_{i,j+1}
. Most of the results in this section are devoted to reducing to the case where almost all of these dimensions are finite. We will then conclude the proof by showing that this is impossible if
L
has GK-dimension
n
.

Notation 4.3
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
L
be a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
. For
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
j=0,\cdots,n-1
, we let

L_{ij}=L\cap\left(\sum_{k\leq j}x_{1}^{i+1}\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\partial_{k}+\sum_{k>j}x_{1}^{i}\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\partial_{k}\right).

We will take
L_{i,n}=L_{i+1,0}
. We also write
d_{ij}(L)=\dim_{\Bbbk}(L_{ij}/L_{i,j+1})
.

Note that, for a subalgebra
L
of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
, we have
L_{ij}\supseteq L_{i,j+1}
for
j=0,\cdots,d-2
, and
L_{i,n-1}\supseteq L_{i+1,0}
. We will ultimately have to apply a change of variables (i.e. an automorphism of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
) to ensure that we can obtain the conditions needed for the proof of Theorem
4.1
to work, but this will only be done at the end.

We will use the following lemma extensively to greatly simplify some proofs. In particular, in order to prove that the enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra
L
is not noetherian, it suffices to prove this for a subalgebra of
L
.

Lemma 4.4

(
[
SW14
, Lemma 1.7]
)
.

Let
L_{1}
be a Lie algebra and let
L_{2}
be a Lie subalgebra of
L_{1}
. If
U(L_{1})
is noetherian then
U(L_{2})
is also noetherian.

The next result shows that Lie algebras with infinite-dimensional abelianisations have non-noetherian enveloping algebras.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra. If
[L,L]
has infinite codimension in
L
then
U(L)
is not noetherian.

Proof.

The abelianisation
L^{\ab}=L/[L,L]
is infinite-dimensional and abelian, so
U(L^{\ab})
is not noetherian, since it is isomorphic to a polynomial ring in infinitely many variables. The natural map
U(L)\to U(L^{\ab})
is surjective, so
U(L)
is also not noetherian.
∎

In some sense, Lemma
4.5
says that Lie algebras that are “almost abelian” have non-noetherian enveloping algebras. The Lie algebras
\mathbb{W}_{n}
have many such subalgebras for
n\geq 2
, but this is not the case for
\mathbb{W}_{1}
. This gives some indication as to why the proof of Theorem
4.1
is so different depending on whether
n=1
or
n\geq 2
.

Using Lemma
4.5
, we can now reduce to the case where
d_{ij}(L)<\infty
for
i\geq 1
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
L
be a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
such that
d_{ij}(L)=\infty
for some
i\geq 1
and
j\in\{0,\cdots,n-1\}
. Then
U(L)
is not noetherian.

Proof.

We claim that
[L_{ij},L_{ij}]\subseteq L_{i,j+1}
. Let

u=\sum_{k\leq j}x_{1}^{i+1}u_{k}\partial_{k}+\sum_{k>j}x_{1}^{i}u_{k}\partial_{k},\quad v=\sum_{\ell\leq j}x_{1}^{i+1}v_{\ell}\partial_{\ell}+\sum_{\ell>j}x_{1}^{i}v_{\ell}\partial_{\ell}\in L_{ij},

where
u_{k},v_{\ell}\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
. Write
w_{k\ell}=[x_{1}^{i_{k}}u_{k}\partial_{k},x_{1}^{i_{\ell}}v_{\ell}\partial_{\ell}]
, where
i_{m}=i+1
if
m\leq j
or
i_{m}=i
if
m>j
, so that
[u,v]=\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}w_{k\ell}
. We will prove the claim by showing that
w_{k\ell}
is an element of

(\mathbb{W}_{2})_{i,j+1}=\sum_{k\leq j+1}x_{1}^{i+1}\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\partial_{k}+\sum_{k>j+1}x_{1}^{i}\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\partial_{k}

for all
k,\ell
. We will use the following identity:

(4.6.1)

[x_{1}^{p}f\partial_{k},x_{1}^{q}g\partial_{\ell}]=x_{1}^{p+q-1}\Big((x_{1}f\partial_{k}(g)+\delta_{1,k}qfg)\partial_{\ell}\\
-(x_{1}\partial_{\ell}(f)g+\delta_{1,\ell}pfg)\partial_{k}\Big),

for
f,g\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
and
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
. By (
4.6.1
), the only nontrivial case is when
i=1,j=0
, and at least one of
k
and
\ell
is 1. First, if exactly one of
k
and
\ell
is 1, say
k\geq 2
and
\ell=1
, then (
4.6.1
) implies that

w_{k,1}=[x_{1}u_{k}\partial_{k},x_{1}v_{1}\partial_{1}]=x_{1}^{2}u_{k}\partial_{k}(v_{1})\partial_{1}-x_{1}(x_{1}\partial_{1}(u_{k})v_{1}+u_{k}v_{1})\partial_{k}\in(\mathbb{W}_{2})_{1,1}.

If
k=\ell=1
, then

w_{1,1}=[x_{1}u_{1}\partial_{1},x_{1}v_{1}\partial_{1}]=x_{1}^{2}(u_{1}\partial_{1}(v_{1})-\partial_{1}(u_{1})v_{1})\partial_{1}\in(\mathbb{W}_{2})_{1,1}.

This proves the claim.

It follows that the abelianisation
L_{ij}^{\ab}=L_{ij}/[L_{ij},L_{ij}]
is infinite-dimensional, since
d_{ij}(L)=\infty
, so
U(L_{ij})
is not noetherian by Lemma
4.5
. The result now follows by Lemma
4.4
.
∎

We now focus on reducing to
d_{0,0}(L)
also being finite. This will be achieved by showing that
U(L)
can only be noetherian if either
d_{0,0}(L)<\infty
or
L_{2,0}=0
. We then use the GK-dimension assumption to prove that
L_{2,0}
is always nonzero.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
L
be a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
such that
d_{0,0}(L)=\infty
and
L_{k,0}/L_{k+1,0}\neq 0
for some
k\geq 2
. Then
U(L)
is not noetherian.

Proof.

Assume, for a contradiction, that
U(L)
is noetherian. The Lie bracket gives a bilinear map
\tau\colon L/L_{0,1}\times L_{k,0}/L_{k+1,0}\to L_{k-1,0}/L_{k,0}
. We emphasise that we are only regarding these as quotients of vector spaces, since
L_{0,1}
is not a Lie ideal of
L
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\coloneqq N_{L}(L_{k,0})=\{\Phi\in L\mid[\Phi,L_{k,0}]\subseteq L_{k,0}\}
be the normaliser of
L_{k,0}
in
L
, and let
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
be the image of
\mathcal{A}
in
L/L_{0,1}
. We claim that
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
is infinite-dimensional. Note that we may assume that
L_{k,0}/L_{k+1,0}
and
L_{k-1,0}/L_{k,0}
are finite-dimensional, by Lemma
4.6
. Let
w_{1},\cdots,w_{m}
be a basis for
L_{k,0}/L_{k+1,0}
. For
i=1,\cdots,m
, define
\tau_{i}\colon L/L_{0,1}\to L_{k-1,0}/L_{k,0}
by
\tau_{i}(v)=\tau(v,w_{i})
, where
v\in L/L_{0,1}
. Since
L_{k-1,0}/L_{k,0}
is finite-dimensional, it follows that
\ker(\tau_{i})
has finite codimension in
L/L_{0,1}
. Therefore,
\overline{\mathcal{A}}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\ker(\tau_{i})
is infinite-dimensional, as claimed.

Let
u=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\partial_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
u\notin L_{0,1}
, in other words,
u_{1}\notin x_{1}\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
. Let
v=x_{1}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\partial_{i}\in L_{k,0}\setminus L_{k+1,0}
, which exists by assumption. By definition of
\mathcal{A}
, we have
[u,v]\in L_{k,0}
. Expanding
[u,v]
, we get

\displaystyle[u,v]

\displaystyle=\left[u_{1}\partial_{1},x_{1}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\partial_{i}\right]+\left[\sum_{i=2}^{n}u_{i}\partial_{i},x_{1}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\partial_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=kx_{1}^{k-1}u_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\partial_{i}+x_{1}^{k}\left(u_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{1}(v_{i})\partial_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\partial_{i}(u_{1})\partial_{1}+\left[\sum_{i=2}^{n}u_{i}\partial_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\partial_{i}\right]\right).

Since
[u,v]\in L_{k,0}\subseteq x_{1}^{k}\mathbb{W}_{n}
, we must have
kx_{1}^{k-1}u_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\partial_{i}\in x_{1}^{k}\mathbb{W}_{n}
. We have
u_{1}\notin x_{1}\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
by assumption, and therefore
v_{i}\in x_{1}\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
. This is a contradiction, since
v\notin L_{k+1,0}
.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
L
be a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
such that
d_{0,0}(L)=\infty
and
L_{2,0}\neq 0
. Then
U(L)
is not noetherian.

Proof.

Assume, for a contradiction, that
U(L)
is noetherian. Proposition
4.7
implies that
L_{i,0}/L_{i+1,0}=0
for all
i\geq 2
, in other words,
L_{i,0}=L_{2,0}
for all
i\geq 2
. By Krull’s intersection theorem,

L_{2,0}=\bigcap_{i\geq 2}L_{i,0}=0,

a contradiction.
∎

Note that, until now, all the results hold for any subalgebra
L\subseteq\mathbb{W}_{n}
. The results that follow are specific to subalgebras of GK-dimension
n
. We first need some notation.

Notation 4.9
.

Let
V
be a subspace of a Lie algebra
L
. We write
C_{0}(V)=V
and

C_{N}(V)=[C_{N-1}(V),V]=\spn\{[u,v]\mid u\in C_{N-1}(V),v\in V\},

for
N\geq 1
.

We now prove that
L_{2,0}\neq 0
if
\GKdim(L)=n
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
L
be a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
of GK-dimension
n
. Then
\dim_{\Bbbk}(L_{2,0})=\infty
.

Proof.

Since
\GKdim(L)=n
, there is a finitely generated subalgebra
L^{\prime}
of
L
of GK-dimension greater than
n-1
. We let
u_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}u_{ij}\partial_{j}
for
i=1,\cdots,m
be a set of generators of
L^{\prime}
. Letting

N=\max\{\deg(u_{ij})\mid 1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n\}

and
V=\spn\{u_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq m\}
, we have

C_{k}(V)\subseteq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\leq Nk}\partial_{j},

where
\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\leq\ell}
denotes the space of polynomials of degree at most
\ell
. It is well-known that
\dim_{\Bbbk}(\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\leq\ell})=\binom{n+\ell}{n}
.

We claim that
\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\leq Nk}\cap(x_{1}^{2})
has codimension
\binom{Nk+n-1}{n-1}+\binom{Nk+n-2}{n-1}=O(k^{n-1})
in
\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\leq Nk}
. To see this, note that

\frac{\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\leq Nk}}{\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\leq Nk}\cap(x_{1}^{2})}\cong\Bbbk[x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\leq Nk}\oplus x_{1}\Bbbk[x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\leq Nk-1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\dim_{\Bbbk}\left(\frac{\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\leq Nk}}{\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\leq Nk}\cap(x_{1}^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\Bbbk}(\Bbbk[x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\leq Nk})+\dim_{\Bbbk}(\Bbbk[x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\leq Nk-1})

\displaystyle=\binom{Nk+n-1}{n-1}+\binom{Nk+n-2}{n-1},

as claimed. By the assumption on the GK-dimension of
L^{\prime}
, there is some
\varepsilon>0
such that
\dim_{\Bbbk}(C_{k}(V))\geq k^{n-1+\varepsilon}
for
k\gg 0
. Since
\dim_{\Bbbk}(\frac{C_{k}(V)}{C_{k}(V)\cap x_{1}^{2}\mathbb{W}_{n}})=O(k^{n-1})
, we must have
\dim_{\Bbbk}(L^{\prime}_{2,0})=\infty
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Combining Corollary
4.8
and Proposition
4.10
, we immediately deduce the following.

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
L
be a subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
of GK-dimension
n
. If
d_{0,0}(L)=\infty
then
U(L)
is not noetherian. ∎

Thanks to the above results, we have reduced to the case where
d_{ij}(L)
is finite for all
i\geq 1
and
j\in\{0,\cdots,n-1\}
, and
(i,j)=(0,0)
. Note that the same is true if we change variables; in other words, if
\varphi\in\Aut(\mathbb{W}_{n})
, we may assume that
d_{ij}(\varphi(L))<\infty
for all
i\geq 1
and
j\in\{0,\cdots,n-1\}
, and
(i,j)=(0,0)
. We now prove Theorem
4.1
by showing that no such subalgebra
L\subseteq\mathbb{W}_{n}
with
\GKdim(L)=n
exists.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

For
n=1
, this is Corollary
3.2
, so we assume that
n\geq 2
.

Assume, for a contradiction, that
U(L)
is noetherian. Note that Corollary
4.11
applies for all choices of generators of
\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
. In particular, letting
y_{1}=x_{1}-\lambda
and
y_{i}=x_{i}
for
i\geq 2
, the quotient

L_{\lambda}\coloneqq\frac{L}{L\cap\left(y_{1}\Bbbk[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]\partial_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\Bbbk[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]\partial_{j}\right)},

which generalises
L_{0,0}/L_{0,1}=L_{\lambda=0}
, is finite-dimensional for all
\lambda\in\Bbbk
. If
\Bbbk
is uncountable, then it is immediate that there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
and an infinite subset
U\subseteq\Bbbk
such that
\dim_{\Bbbk}(L_{\lambda})\leq N
for all
\lambda\in U
. However, if
\Bbbk
is countable we require a different argument.

Let
\overline{L}=L\otimes_{\Bbbk}\Bbbk(t)
, viewed as a Lie algebra over
\Bbbk(t)
. This is a Lie subalgebra of

\mathbb{W}_{n}^{\Bbbk(t)}=\Der_{\Bbbk(t)}(\Bbbk(t)[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]),

where
\Der_{\Bbbk(t)}
denotes
\Bbbk(t)
-linear derivations. Since
U(L)
is assumed to be noetherian, it follows that
U_{\Bbbk(t)}(\overline{L})\cong U(L)\otimes_{\Bbbk}\Bbbk(t)
is noetherian, by Hilbert’s basis theorem, where
U_{\Bbbk(t)}(\overline{L})
denotes the universal enveloping algebra of
\overline{L}
as a Lie algebra over
\Bbbk(t)
.

The results above only assumed that
\Bbbk
is a field of characteristic zero, so they all apply to
\overline{L}
and
U_{\Bbbk(t)}(\overline{L})
. Therefore, letting
y_{1}=x_{1}-\chi(t)
and
y_{i}=x_{i}
for
i\geq 2
, the quotient

\overline{L}_{\chi(t)}\coloneqq\frac{\overline{L}}{\overline{L}\cap\left(y_{1}\Bbbk(t)[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]\partial_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\Bbbk(t)[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]\partial_{j}\right)}

is a finite-dimensional vector space over
\Bbbk(t)
for all
\chi(t)\in\Bbbk(t)
, similarly to above. In particular,
\overline{L}_{t}
is finite-dimensional. Let
N=\dim_{\Bbbk(t)}(\overline{L}_{t})
.

Let

X=\overline{L}\cap\left((x_{1}-t)\Bbbk(t)[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\partial_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\Bbbk(t)[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\partial_{j}\right),

so that
\overline{L}_{t}=\overline{L}/X
. Note that
X
must be finitely generated: if not, then there exists an infinite strictly ascending chain of (finitely generated) subalgebras of
X
, which would imply that
U_{\Bbbk(t)}(X)
is not noetherian by
[
AS74
, Proposition 11.1.2]
, a contradiction to Lemma
4.4
. Let

a_{i}=(x_{1}-t)a_{i,1}\partial_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}a_{ij}\partial_{j}

with
i=1,\cdots,\ell
be a set of generators of
X
, where
a_{ij}\in\Bbbk(t)[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
.

Pick elements
b_{i}=c_{i}\otimes 1\in\overline{L}
for
i=1,\cdots,N
, where
c_{i}\in L
, such that

(4.11.1)

\overline{L}=\Bbbk(t)b_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\Bbbk(t)b_{N}\oplus X.

Let
\overline{w}_{1}=w_{1}\otimes 1,\cdots,\overline{w}_{r}=w_{r}\otimes 1
be generators of
\overline{L}
, where
w_{i}\in L
. The equality (
4.11.1
) can be witnessed as follows:

(1)

For each
i=1,\cdots,r
, we have an expression

(4.11.2)

\overline{w}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{ij}(t)b_{j}+(x_{1}-t)f_{i,1}\partial_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}f_{ij}\partial_{j},

for some
\alpha_{ij}(t)\in\Bbbk(t)
and
f_{ij}\in\Bbbk(t)[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
.

(2)

For each
i,j
,

(4.11.3)

[b_{i},b_{j}]=\sum_{s=1}^{N}\beta_{ijs}(t)b_{s}+(x_{1}-t)g_{i,j,1}\partial_{1}+\sum_{s=2}^{n}g_{ijs}\partial_{s},

for some
\beta_{ijs}(t)\in\Bbbk(t)
and
g_{ijs}\in\Bbbk(t)[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
.

(3)

For each
i,j
,

(4.11.4)

[a_{i},b_{j}]=\sum_{s=1}^{N}\gamma_{ijs}(t)b_{s}+(x_{1}-t)h_{i,j,1}\partial_{1}+\sum_{s=2}^{n}h_{ijs}\partial_{s},

for some
\gamma_{ijs}\in\Bbbk(t)
and
h_{ijs}\in\Bbbk(t)[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
.

Indeed, (
4.11.2
) is saying that
\Bbbk(t)b_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\Bbbk(t)b_{N}\oplus X
contains a set of generators of
\overline{L}
, while (
4.11.3
) and (
4.11.4
) imply that
\Bbbk(t)b_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\Bbbk(t)b_{N}\oplus X
is closed under the Lie bracket of
\mathbb{W}_{n}^{\Bbbk(t)}
, giving the desired equality. Now, there are only finitely many polynomials
\alpha_{ij}(t),\beta_{ijs}(t),\gamma_{ijs}(t),a_{ij},f_{ij},g_{ijs},h_{ijs}
, so there are only finitely many poles of the coefficients of these polynomials.

In particular, (
4.11.2
) implies that for all but finitely many specializations
t=\lambda
with
\lambda\in\Bbbk
, we have a relation:

w_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{ij}(\lambda)c_{j}+(x_{1}-\lambda)\widetilde{f}_{i,1}\partial_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\widetilde{f}_{ij}\partial_{j},

where
\widetilde{f}_{ij}
denotes
f_{ij}
after we substitute
t=\lambda
into all the coefficients. This implies that a set of generators of
L
is contained in
\Bbbk c_{1}+\cdots+\Bbbk c_{N}+\widetilde{X}_{\lambda}
, where

\widetilde{X}_{\lambda}=L\cap\left((x_{1}-\lambda)\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\partial_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\partial_{j}\right).

Similarly, we can get relations corresponding to (
4.11.3
) and (
4.11.4
), which tells us that
\Bbbk c_{1}+\cdots+\Bbbk c_{N}+\widetilde{X}_{\lambda}
is closed under the Lie bracket of
\mathbb{W}_{n}
. Therefore,

L=\Bbbk c_{1}+\cdots+\Bbbk c_{N}+\widetilde{X}_{\lambda},

which means that
\dim_{\Bbbk}(L_{\lambda})\leq N
for all but finitely many
\lambda\in\Bbbk
.

In other words, even when
\Bbbk
is countable, there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
and an infinite subset
U\subseteq\Bbbk
such that
\dim_{\Bbbk}(L_{\lambda})\leq N
for all
\lambda\in U
.

We claim that
\dim_{\Bbbk}(F_{1})\leq N
, where

F_{1}=\spn\{f\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\mid f\partial_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}g_{j}\partial_{j}\in L\text{ for some }g_{j}\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\}.

Assume, for a contradiction, that there exist
N+1
elements
u_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}u_{ij}\partial_{j}\in L
such that
u_{i,1}
are linearly independent for
i=1,\cdots,N+1
. Write
v_{i}=u_{i,1}
for
i=1,\cdots,N+1
. Since
\dim_{\Bbbk}(L_{\lambda})\leq N
for
\lambda\in U
, we must have that
v_{1},\cdots,v_{N+1}
are linearly dependent modulo
x_{1}-\lambda
for all
\lambda\in U
. Write

v_{i}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n-1}}P_{\mathbf{k}}^{(i)}(x_{1})x_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{k_{n}},

where
\mathbf{k}=(k_{2},\cdots,k_{n})
and
P_{\mathbf{k}}^{(i)}(x_{1})\in\Bbbk[x_{1}]
. Let

I=\{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{n-1}\mid P_{\mathbf{k}}^{(i)}(x_{1})\neq 0\text{ for some }i\in\{1,\cdots,N+1\}\}.

For
i\in\{1,\cdots,N+1\}
and
\mathbf{k}\in I
, let
c_{i,\mathbf{k}}=P_{\mathbf{k}}^{(i)}(x_{1})
. Let
C=(c_{i,\mathbf{k}})\in M_{(N+1)\times|I|}(\Bbbk[x_{1}])
be a matrix with
N+1
rows, and columns labelled by elements of
I
. Since
v_{1},\cdots,v_{N+1}
are linearly independent, we have
|I|\geq N+1
and there is some
(N+1)\times(N+1)
nonzero minor
P\in\Bbbk[x_{1}]
of
C
. On the other hand,
P\in(x_{1}-\lambda)\Bbbk[x_{1}]
for all
\lambda\in U
, since
v_{1}(\lambda,x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}),\cdots,v_{N+1}(\lambda,x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})
are linearly dependent. But then

P\in\bigcap_{\lambda\in U}(x_{1}-\lambda)\Bbbk[x_{1}]=0,

a contradiction.

Let

F_{i}=\spn\{f\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\mid f\partial_{i}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}g_{j}\partial_{j}\in L\text{ for some }g_{j}\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\}.

We have shown that
F_{1}
is finite-dimensional. Since we can do this for all variables, we see that
F_{i}
is finite-dimensional for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
, and therefore
L
is finite-dimensional, a contradiction.
∎

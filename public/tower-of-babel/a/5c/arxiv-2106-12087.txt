Generalized eigenvalues of the Perron-Frobenius operators of symbolic dynamical systems
Hayato Chiba, Masahiro Ikeda, and, Isao Ishikawa

Abstract
The generalized spectral theory is an effective approach to analyze a linear operator on a Hilbert space ℋ \mathcal{H} with a continuous spectrum. The generalized spectrum is computed via analytic continuations of the resolvent operators using a dense locally convex subspace X X of ℋ \mathcal{H} and its dual space X ′ X^{\prime} . The three topological spaces X ⊂ ℋ ⊂ X ′ X\subset\mathcal{H}\subset X^{\prime} is called the rigged Hilbert space or the Gelfand triplet. In this paper, the generalized spectra of the Perron-Frobenius operators of the one-sided and two-sided shifts of finite types (symbolic dynamical systems) are determined. A one-sided subshift of finite type which is conjugate to the multiplication with the golden ration on [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] modulo 1 1 is also considered. A new construction of the Gelfand triplet for the generalized spectrum of symbolic dynamical systems is proposed by means of an algebraic procedure. The asymptotic formula of the iteration of Perron-Frobenius operators is also given. The iteration converges to the mixing state whose rate of convergence is determined by the generalized spectrum.

Introduction

The spectrum of a linear operator on a topological vector space is
an important objective in any area of mathematics.
If an underlying space is a finite dimensional space, the spectrum set consists of eigenvalues, while on an infinite dimensional space, the spectrum set may include a continuous and a residual spectra.
From eigenvalues, a lot of information of a linear operator is obtained by using, such as eigenvectors, projections to eigenspaces and the residue theorem.
However, a continuous and a residual spectra are far from tractable because how to treat them depends on the problem at hand and a standard method has not been established yet.

The generalized spectral theory based on a rigged Hilbert space is one of the methods to handle problems related to a continuous and a residual spectra.

Let
T
be a linear operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
(\lambda-T)^{-1}
its resolvent operator.
The resolvent set
\rho(T)\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is defined such that
(\lambda-T)^{-1}
is a continuous operator on
\mathcal{H}
when
\lambda\in\rho(T)
.
The spectrum set is defined as its complement:
\sigma(T)={\mathbb{C}}\backslash\rho(T)
.
It is well known that the resolvent
(\lambda-T)^{-1}
is holomorphic with respect to
\lambda

on the resolvent set
\rho(T)
, while is singular on
\sigma(T)
.
A basic idea of the generalized spectral theory is that by controlling the topology of the underlying vector space well,
a continuous and a residual spectra disappear, and generalized eigenvalues may appear instead, which play a similar role to the usual eigenvalues.

Specifically, let
X
be a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}
whose topology is stronger than that of
\mathcal{H}
,
and
X^{\prime}
its dual space. Then, the three topological vector spaces

\displaystyle X\subset\mathcal{H}\subset X^{\prime}

is called the rigged Hilbert space or the Gelfand triplet.
In the generalized spectral theory, the domain of the resolvent
(\lambda-T)^{-1}
is restricted to
X
and the range is
extended to
X^{\prime}
.
By regarding the resolvent as the operator from
X
into
X^{\prime}
, under a suitable assumption,
it has an analytic continuation from the set
\rho(T)
into the spectrum set
\sigma(T)
.
Then, the region on which
(\lambda-T)^{-1}
is holomorphic becomes a nontrivial Riemann surface.
The analytic continuation of the resolvent is called the generalized resolvent.
The set of singularities of the analytic continuation of
(\lambda-T)^{-1}
on the Riemann surface is called the generalized spectrum set.
A point of the generalized spectrum set is called the generalized eigenvalue if a corresponding eigenvector exists in the dual space
X^{\prime}
.
Since the generalized eigenvalue lies on a Riemann surface and the associated eigenvector
is not an element of
\mathcal{H}
,
we can investigate phenomena that cannot be captured by the usual spectrum set based on the
Hilbert space theory.
See Section 2 for the detail.

2
The generalized spectral theory

Let
X
be a locally convex Hausdorff topological vector space over
{\mathbb{C}}
and
X^{\prime}
its dual space.

X^{\prime}
is the set of continuous anti-linear functionals on
X
.
For
\mu\in X^{\prime}
and
f\in X
,
\mu(f)
is denoted by

\langle\mu\,|\,f\rangle
.
For any
a,b\in{\mathbb{C}},\,f,g\in X
and
\mu,\xi\in X^{\prime}
, the equalities

\displaystyle\langle\mu\,|\,af+bg\rangle=\overline{a}\langle\mu\,|\,f\rangle+\overline{b}\langle\mu\,|\,g\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle a\mu+b\xi\,|\,f\rangle=a\langle\mu\,|\,f\rangle+b\langle\xi\,|\,f\rangle,

hold.
The dual space
X^{\prime}
is equipped with the weak dual topology (weak * topology) ;
A sequence
\{\mu_{j}\}\subset X^{\prime}
is said to be weakly convergent to
\mu\in X^{\prime}

if
\langle\mu_{j}\,|\,f\rangle\to\langle\mu\,|\,f\rangle
as
j\rightarrow\infty
for each
f\in X
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space with the inner product
(\cdot\,,\,\cdot)
such that
X
is a dense subspace of

\mathcal{H}
.
Since a Hilbert space is isomorphic to its dual space, we obtain
\mathcal{H}\subset X^{\prime}
through
\mathcal{H}\simeq\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
The isomorphism
\mathcal{H}\simeq\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is defined so that
\langle g\,|\,f\rangle=(g,f)

when
g\in\mathcal{H}
; that is, the dual pair is compatible with the inner product.

Definition 2.1
.

If a locally convex Hausdorff topological vector space
X
is a dense subspace of
a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and the topology of
X
is stronger than that of
\mathcal{H}
, the triplet

X\subset\mathcal{H}\subset X^{\prime}

(2.1)

is called the
rigged Hilbert space
or the
Gelfand triplet
.

In this case,
\mathcal{H}
is a dense subspace of the dual space

X^{\prime}
and the topology of
\mathcal{H}
is stronger than that of
X^{\prime}
.

Let
T
be a linear operator densely defined on
\mathcal{H}
.
The (Hilbert) adjoint
T^{*}
of
T
is defined through
(Tf,g)=(f,T^{*}g)
as usual.
If
T^{*}
preserves
X
and is continuous on
X
, the dual operator
T^{\times}:X^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}
of
T^{*}|_{X}
defined through

\displaystyle\langle T^{\times}\mu\,|\,f\rangle=\langle\mu\,|\,T^{*}f\rangle,\quad f\in X,\,\mu\in X^{\prime}

is also continuous on
X^{\prime}
with respect to the weak dual topology.
We can show the equality
T^{\times}g=Tg
for any
g\in X
, which implies that
T^{\times}
is an extension of
T
.
To define a generalized spectrum set of
T
, we further assume that
X
is a quasi-complete barreled space
(see Tréves
[
27
, Definition 34.1, Definition 33.1]
for the definitions), which is used to justify an integration of

X^{\prime}
-valued complex functions
[
10
]
.
Any Fréchet spaces, Banach spaces and Montel spaces satisfy this condition.

Let
\rho(T)
be a resolvent set of
T
; the resolvent operator
R_{\lambda}=(\lambda-T)^{-1}

is a continuous operator on
\mathcal{H}
when
\lambda\in\rho(T)

and is holomorphic on
\rho(T)
.
The set
\sigma(T):={\mathbb{C}}\backslash\rho(T)
is called the spectrum set of
T
,
on which the resolvent operator is not
defined as a continuous operator on
\mathcal{H}
.
For typical Perron-Frobenius operators considered in this paper, both of point spectra
(eigenvalues) and the continuous spectrum lie on the unit disk.
For an isolated eigenvalue
\lambda
, the Riesz projection to its eigenspace is given by

\displaystyle\Pi_{\lambda}[\phi]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C}(z-T)^{-1}\phi dz,\quad\phi\in\mathcal{H},

where
C
is a positively oriented closed curve, which encircles an eigenvalue
\lambda

but no other spectrum.
The eigenspace of
\lambda
is given by the image of
\Pi_{\lambda}
as long as it is a finite dimensional space.

However, the continuous spectrum has no corresponding eigenvectors that makes many problems difficult. To overcome this difficulty caused by the continuous spectrum, the generalized spectral theory has been developed.
For a suitable choice of a Gelfand triplet (depending on the problem at hand),
it is shown that if the resolvent
(\lambda-T)^{-1}
is regarded as an operator from
X
into
X^{\prime}

(i.e. the domain is restricted to
X
and the range is extended to
X^{\prime}
),
it has an analytic continuation beyond the continuous spectrum, which is called the generalized resolvent operator.
The region, on which the resolvent
(\lambda-T)^{-1}
is holomorphic, becomes a nontrivial Riemann surface and the continuous spectrum becomes its branch cut

If the continuous spectrum is a curve, the edge point could be a branch point of the Riemann surface. For example, the continuous spectrum of the Laplace operator on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is the negative real axis and the edge point (the origin) becomes a branch point of the Riemann surface of the resolvent of type

(resp. logarithmic) when

.
.
If the generalized resolvent has a singularity on the Riemann surface,
it is called the generalized spectrum.
It is also called the generalized eigenvalue, which plays a similar role to a usual eigenvalue, if the associated eigenvector exists in the dual space
X^{\prime}
.
The Riesz projection for the isolated generalized eigenvalue is same as above but it is an operator from
X
into
X^{\prime}
because so is the generalized resolvent.
We define the generalized eigenspace, which is a subspace of
X^{\prime}
, as the image of the Riesz projection and the generalized eigenvectors are the elements of the generalized eigenspace.
It is known that there exists an element
\mu
in the image of the generalized Riesz projection satisfying (
[
10
]
Theorem 3.5)

\displaystyle T^{\times}\mu=\lambda\mu,\quad\mu\in X^{\prime}.

That is, a generalized eigenvector is a true eigenvector of the dual operator.
Remark that not all the eigenvalues of the dual operator

is a
generalized eigenvalue in the sense of the generalized spectral theory.
Usually, the spectrum set of

is too large and eigenspaces
are often infinite dimensional, so that it is not suitable to investigate the properties of

.
On the other hand, the number of the generalized eigenvalues is at most countable and their generalized eigenspaces are finite dimensional under a mild assumption (
[
10

For the Perron-Frobenius operator
V_{T}
considered in this paper,
its generalized resolvent
(\lambda-V_{T})^{-1}:X\to X^{\prime}
has an analytic continuation from the outside
of the unit circle to the inside beyond the continuous spectrum on the unit circle.
Then, we will find infinitely many generalized eigenvalues inside the unit circle.

Calculation of the generalized eigenvalues.

We compute the generalized eigenvalues for

.
First, we specify a test space

to determine a rigged Hilbert space.
Let

Proposition 3.3

Proof.

Via the isomorphism (

In particular,

has a stronger topology than

∎

We note that

is a Montel space. Hence, the rigged Hilbert space

is well-defined.
Then,

and

Proposition 3.4
.

For

V_{S}X_{n}\subset X_{n}.

Moreover, the representation matrix of

associated to the basis

is an upper triangular matrix whose diagonal components are

.
In particular,

induces a continuous linear operator on

Proof.

It follows from Proposition

.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

For each

in

Proof.

For any

in

.
Since eigenvectors of distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent and the dimension of

is

is

∎

We denote by

Theorem 3.6
.

(1) The resolvent

has an analytic continuation
to the inside of the unit circle as an operator from

into
X^{\prime}
.

(2)

\displaystyle V_{S}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}2^{-i}\overline{\langle\Phi_{i}^{\prime}\,|\,f\rangle}\Phi_{i},\quad f\in X,

(3.13)

\displaystyle U_{S}^{\times}\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}2^{-i}\langle\mu\,|\,\Phi_{i}\rangle\Phi_{i}^{\prime},\quad\mu\in X^{\prime}.

Proof.

(1) For

\displaystyle\left((\lambda-V_{S})^{-1}f,g\right)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left((1-V_{S}/\lambda)^{-1}f,g\right).

∎

As a corollary of Theorem

converges to the average of

.

The notation is as in Theorem

Let

.
Then, we have

.

It follows from the following formula:

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{+}}\Phi_{n}{\rm d}\mu_{+}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{+}}\Phi_{n}\Phi_{0}{\rm d}\mu_{+}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{+}}\Phi_{n}U_{S}[\Phi_{0}]{\rm d}\mu_{+}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{+}}V_{S}[\Phi_{n}]\Phi_{0}{\rm d}\mu_{+}

∎

Remark 3.8
.

Antoniou and Tasaki
[
5
]
computed the generalized eigenvalues for the Rényi map.
They prove that the generalized eigenvalues of Perron-Frobenius operator associated to 2-adic Rényi map are
1,2^{-1},2^{-2},\dots
, and the corresponding eigenvectors are
Bernoulli polynomials

\{B_{n}(x)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, which are defined as coefficients of the following Taylor expansion:

\frac{te^{xt}}{e^{t}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{n}(x)}{n!}t^{n}.

Theorem
3.6
is compatible with their result through the isomorphism
h^{*}
of (
3.8
). Namely, the Bernoulli polynomial

is identical with
\Phi_{n}
.
In fact, the Bernoulli polynomial satisfies (a special case of) Raabe’s multiplication theorem
[
23
]
:

\frac{1}{2}B_{n}(x)+\frac{1}{2}B_{n}(x+1/2)=2^{-n}B(2x).

We can prove that the left hand side coincides with
(V_{T}B_{n})(2x)
, and thus, Raabe’s multiplication theorem means that the Bernoulli polynomial
B_{n}
is an eigenvector of
V_{T}
corresponding to
2^{-n}
.
Since the test space
X
in
L^{2}(\Sigma_{+},\mu_{+})
corresponds to the space of polynomials in
L^{2}([0,1],{\rm d}{x})
via
h^{*}
,
by the same argument of the proof of Proposition
3.4
,
B_{n}
is characterized as a unique eigenvector with respect to

in the space of polynomials, and thus
h^{*}B_{n}=\Phi_{n}
.

.
∎

\displaystyle\hskip 50.0pt\times\overline{g(\omega)}{\rm d}\mu_{+}(\omega).

\displaystyle(f_{+},U_{L+}g_{+})_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{+})}\cdot(f_{-},V_{R-}g_{-})_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{-})}

\displaystyle(f_{+}\otimes f_{-},(U_{L+}g_{+})\otimes(V_{R-}g_{-}))_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}

Lemma 4.12
.

(1)
Q_{1}^{\times}\Phi_{m}\otimes\Psi^{\prime}_{n}\in P(\Sigma_{+})\otimes P(\Sigma_{-})^{\prime}\subset X_{+}^{\prime}

(2)
\langle Q_{1}^{\times}\Phi_{m}\otimes\Psi^{\prime}_{n}\,|\,\Phi^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}\otimes\Psi_{n^{\prime}}\rangle=\langle\Psi^{\prime}_{n}\,|\,V_{L-}(-1)^{\omega_{0}}\Psi_{n^{\prime}}\rangle\cdot\overline{\langle\Phi^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}\,|\,V_{L+}(-1)^{\omega_{1}}\Phi_{m}\rangle}

Proof.

(1) By a similar calculation to the proof of Lemma

\displaystyle Q_{1}^{\times}\Phi_{m}\otimes\Psi^{\prime}_{n}

\displaystyle(V_{L+}(-1)^{\omega_{1}}\Phi_{m})(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\cdots)\otimes\bigl((-1)^{\omega_{0}}U_{R-}\bigr)^{\times}\Psi^{\prime}_{n}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\Phi_{m}(0,\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\cdots)-\Phi_{m}(1,\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\cdots)\right)\otimes\bigl((-1)^{\omega_{0}}U_{R-}\bigr)^{\times}\Psi^{\prime}_{n}.

\displaystyle h(0,\omega_{1},\cdots)^{m}-h(1,\omega_{1},\cdots)^{m}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{0}{2}+\frac{\omega_{1}}{2^{2}}+\cdots\right)^{m}-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\omega_{1}}{2^{2}}+\cdots\right)^{m}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{m}}h^{m}-\frac{1}{2^{m}}\left(1+h\right)^{m}.

(2) Due to the statement (1), the bilinear form

\displaystyle\langle Q_{1}^{\times}\Phi_{m}\otimes\Psi^{\prime}_{n}\,|\,\Phi^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}\otimes\Psi_{n^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle V_{L+}(-1)^{\omega_{1}}\Phi_{m}\otimes\bigl((-1)^{\omega_{0}}U_{R-}\bigr)^{\times}\Psi^{\prime}_{n}\,|\,\Phi^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}\otimes\Psi_{n^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle\bigl((-1)^{\omega_{0}}U_{R-}\bigr)^{\times}\Psi^{\prime}_{n}\,|\,\Psi_{n^{\prime}}\rangle\cdot\overline{\langle\Phi^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}\,|\,V_{L+}(-1)^{\omega_{1}}\Phi_{m}\rangle}

\displaystyle\langle\Psi^{\prime}_{n}\,|\,U^{*}_{R-}(-1)^{\omega_{0}}\Psi_{n^{\prime}}\rangle\cdot\overline{\langle\Phi^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}\,|\,V_{L+}(-1)^{\omega_{1}}\Phi_{m}\rangle}

\displaystyle\langle\Psi^{\prime}_{n}\,|\,V_{R-}(-1)^{\omega_{0}}\Psi_{n^{\prime}}\rangle\cdot\overline{\langle\Phi^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}\,|\,V_{L+}(-1)^{\omega_{1}}\Phi_{m}\rangle}.

Theorem 4.14
.

For any
0\leq\varepsilon\leq 1
,

(1) The resolvent
(\lambda-V_{L}(\varepsilon))^{-1}
has an analytic continuation
to the inside of the unit circle as an operator from
X_{-}
into
X_{+}^{\prime}
.
The generalized eigenvalues with respect to the Gelfand triplet
X_{-}\subset L^{2}(\Sigma)\subset X_{+}^{\prime}
are
2^{-k},\,\,k=0,1,2,\cdots
.

(2) The generalized eigenspace of the generalized eigenvalue
2^{-k}
is a
k+1
dimensional space.
When
\varepsilon\neq 0
,

\displaystyle(\text{algebraic multiplicity})=k+1,\quad(\text{geometric multiplicity})=1.

(3) The statements (1) and (2) hold for the Perron-Frobenius operator
V_{L}
of the two-sided shift.

Proof.

(1) has been already obtained. If (2) is true, (3) is trivial because
V_{L}=V_{L}(1)
. Let us prove the statement (2).

Let
W_{k}(\varepsilon)\subset X_{+}^{\prime}
be the generalized eigenspace associated with the generalized eigenvalue

\lambda=2^{-k}
.
This is the image of the generalized Riesz projection (Section 2)

\displaystyle W_{k}(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\mathrm{Im}\oint_{C_{k}}(z-V_{L}(\varepsilon))^{-1}dz,

where
C_{k}
is a positively oriented closed curve, which encircles an eigenvalue
2^{-k}

but no other eigenvalues.
When
\varepsilon=0
(i.e.
V_{L}(\varepsilon)=Q_{0}
),
W_{k}(0)
is a
k+1
dimensional space
given by
W_{k}(0)=\mathrm{span}\{\Phi_{i}\otimes\Psi^{\prime}_{j}\}_{i+j=k}

as was shown in Theorem
4.10
.
Since the generalized eigenvalues are independent of
\varepsilon
, there are no eigenvalues which cross the integral pass
C_{k}
as
\varepsilon
varies.
This means that
\mathrm{dim}\,W_{k}(\varepsilon)=k+1\,(=\text{the algebraic multiplicity})
even when
\varepsilon\neq 0
.
Since
\lambda=2^{-k}
is a pole of
(\lambda-V_{L}(\varepsilon))^{-1}
of order
k+1
,
the geometric multiplicity is one.
∎

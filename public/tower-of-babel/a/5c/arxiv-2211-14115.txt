Inverse Feasibility in Over-the-Air Federated Learning
Tomasz Piotrowski, Rafail Ismayilov, Matthias Frey, Renato L.G. Cavalcante

Abstract
We introduce the concept of inverse feasibility for linear forward models as a tool to enhance OTA ( OTA ) FL ( FL ) algorithms. Inverse feasibility is defined as an upper bound on the condition number of the forward operator as a function of its parameters. We analyze an existing OTA FL model using this definition, identify areas for improvement, and propose a new OTA FL model. Numerical experiments illustrate the main implications of the theoretical results. The proposed framework, which is based on inverse problem theory, can potentially complement existing notions of security and privacy by providing additional desirable characteristics to networks.

I
Introduction

In recent years, wireless solutions have been increasingly applied to
FL
because of their potential to distribute models efficiently, particularly in mobile environments. However, the physical properties of wireless channels present many challenges for
FL
, including scalability, privacy, and security considerations.

In particular, with traditional digital transmission schemes, interference caused by concurrent transmission of multiple users poses difficulties related to scalability, especially in dense networks operating in low frequency bands, which typically operate in interference limited regimes. To address this challenge, analogue
OTA
transmission schemes have been proposed as a scalable alternative to traditional digital communication technologies in
FL
. In
OTA
, the superposition property of signals in the wireless medium is exploited to fuse the processes of communication and computation, which alleviates the need for complex scheduling and interference mitigation mechanisms. The scalability gains of OTA schemes are specially pronounced if a receiver has to compute a function of data distributed across a large network, which is a common scenario in
FL
approaches. For example, during the learning stage of traditional
FL
algorithms based on neural networks, a server may not be interested in obtaining gradients of the local cost functions of each node, but only in computing the average of all local gradients.

OTA

FL
may seem to improve security and privacy because individual nodes’ data are not easily accessible (all signals are mixed in the wireless medium), and reconstructing information intended for the server depends on the propagation environment of malicious nodes (because
OTA
is an analogue transmission scheme). However, we emphasize that there are no inherent guarantees of privacy and security. For this reason, researchers have been introducing many concepts related to privacy and security in
OTA

FL

[

,

,

]
. However, any security guarantee necessarily relies on additional assumptions, such as a computationally bounded adversary, a secret key only known to legitimate parties, or a bound on the attacker’s channel strength. Therefore, a communication system that offers multiple security guarantees based on different assumptions can enhance resilience in the event that any individual assumption fails.

Against this backdrop, we propose a novel concept called inverse feasibility, which complements existing notions of security and privacy. Drawing on inverse problem theory, inverse feasibility leverages the fact that, during training, neural networks in
OTA

FL
typically have to compute the average of a huge number of parameters distributed across nodes within a dense network. We assume that nodes apply possibly lossy compression schemes to local parameters prior to transmission, as proposed in
[
10
,
11
]
, because of the limited bandwidth and high communication costs of wireless systems. As a result, the legitimate server needs to solve an ill-posed inverse problem to reconstruct the true average of parameters from the received compressed data. The core concept of inverse feasibility is that the difficulty of solving this inverse problem should be upper-bounded by a known function of the
OTA

FL
model’s parameters. We note that the proposed concept allows to evaluate this difficulty for the existing
OTA

FL
models and also to introduce novel
OTA

FL
models with improved inverse feasibility.
Building on this, the second main contribution of this study is the derivation of a new
OTA

FL
compression scheme that enhances an existing
OTA

FL
system proposed in
[
10
,
11
]
with respect to our definition of inverse feasibility.

\bm{g}(\theta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\tiny\mbox{df.}}}}{{=}}(g_{1}(\theta),\dots,g_{M}(\theta))\in\mathbb{R}^{Md}.

Inverse Feasibility in Over-the-Air Federated Learning: Definition and Examples

III-A

Definition of Inverse Feasibility

In the federated learning task under consideration, the objective of the server is to reconstruct the average of gradients, defined as

\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni\bar{g}^{sp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\tiny\mbox{df.}}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}g_{m}^{sp}=\frac{1}{M}\underbrace{[I_{d},\dots,I_{d}]}_{M}\bm{g}^{sp},

(5)

by solving an inverse problem
from the compressed received signal
y
in (
1
). Following a standard assumption in the literature, we assume that
\bar{g}^{sp}
is sparse (see, e.g.,
[
10
, Assumption 3]
), which is often justified by the empirical evidence that, in some learning tasks, the sparsity pattern of
g_{m}^{sp}
for
m=1,\ldots,M
in
\bm{g}^{sp}
is common across the
M
active nodes in the network.

In view of (
3
)-(
4
), and recalling that the condition number of a linear operator is a classical tool for assessing the difficulty of solving inverse problems (see, e.g.,
[
12
,
13
]
and references therein), we posit that a desirable condition for the estimation of any function of
\bm{g}^{sp}
, such as the arithmetic mean in (
5
), is that the condition number of the linear operator
\bm{L}(\underline{p})
should be close to one. The concept of inverse feasibility we propose is based on this assumption. Formally, we define inverse feasibility as follows:

Definition 1

(inverse feasibility)
.

We call an
FL
model of the form in (
3
) at least
F
-inverse feasible for a given function
F\colon\underline{P}\to[1,\infty]
if

(\forall\underline{p}\in\underline{P})~\operatorname{cond}(\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 0.5mu\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 1.0mu\mathbb{L}$\crcr}}(\underline{p}))\leq F(\underline{p}),\quad a.s.,

(6)

where the condition number of
\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 0.5mu\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 1.0mu\mathbb{L}$\crcr}}(\underline{p})
is a random variable defined in the following standard way:

\operatorname{cond}\left(\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 0.5mu\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 1.0mu\mathbb{L}$\crcr}}(\underline{p})\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\tiny\mbox{df.}}}}{{=}}\sigma_{1}\left(\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 0.5mu\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 1.0mu\mathbb{L}$\crcr}}(\underline{p})\right)/\sigma_{s}\left(\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 0.5mu\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 1.0mu\mathbb{L}$\crcr}}(\underline{p})\right)\geq 1,

(7)

where
\sigma_{1}\left(\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 0.5mu\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 1.0mu\mathbb{L}$\crcr}}(\underline{p})\right)
and
\sigma_{s}\left(\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 0.5mu\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 1.0mu\mathbb{L}$\crcr}}(\underline{p})\right)
are the largest and the smallest singular values of
\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 0.5mu\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 1.0mu\mathbb{L}$\crcr}}(\underline{p})
, respectively. (We assume that, in practical settings, we have
\sigma_{s}\left(\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 0.5mu\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 1.0mu\mathbb{L}$\crcr}}(\underline{p})\right)>0
almost surely.)

Remark 1
.

The concept of inverse feasibility introduced in Definition
1
enables us to provide weaker notions of inverse feasibility, such as
F
-inverse feasibility in the mean sense,

(\forall\underline{p}\in\underline{P})~\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{cond}(\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 0.5mu\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 1.0mu\mathbb{L}$\crcr}}(\underline{p}))]\leq F(\underline{p}),

(8)

and
F
-inverse feasibility with probability of at least
r\in[0,1]
,

(\forall\underline{p}\in\underline{P})~\mathbb{P}[\operatorname{cond}(\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 0.5mu\mathbb{L}$\cr$\mkern 1.0mu\mathbb{L}$\crcr}}(\underline{p}))\leq F(\underline{p})]\geq r.

(9)

We will use the concept of
F
-inverse feasibility with probability for the model to be proposed in Example
1
in Section
III-B
below. For brevity, the concept of
F
-inverse feasibility in the mean sense is not used in this letter.

Ideally, for well-behaved
OTA

FL
model, there should exist a function
F
taking values close to one for every possible
\underline{p}\in\underline{P}
at the server. We also emphasize that the concept of inverse feasibility provides us with a means of quantifying a desirable property of
OTA

FL
models, but it does not replace existing concepts of security and privacy in wireless systems.

IV-B

Parameter Estimation

We now present a method for estimating the average of sparse gradients
\bar{g}^{sp}
in (
5
) using the compressed signal
y
received at the server. Our objective is to demonstrate that Definition
1

\bar{g}^{sp}
from

In more detail, the estimation of
\bar{g}^{sp}
in (
1

11

\underset{\widehat{\bar{g}}^{sp}}{\text{minimize }}\left[\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|y-Y\widehat{\bar{g}}^{sp}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\beta\left\|\widehat{\bar{g}}^{sp}\right\|_{1}\right],

(16)

\bar{g}^{sp}
in (
5

4

\bm{g}\mapsto\bm{L}(\underline{p})(1_{M}\otimes I_{d})(\bm{g}^{sp}/M)

In more detail, in (

V
Conclusion and final remarks

We have introduced the concept of inverse feasibility for general linear forward models, and we demonstrated its usefulness in OTA FL applications. The notion of inverse feasibility is rooted in the idea that, in inverse problems, linear forward operators should have a low condition number to ensure good reconstruction performance. We illustrated the validity of this assumption in an OTA FL problem focused on reconstructing the average of gradients. One potential application is to provide FL systems with additional guarantees against attacks. More precisely, by designing FL systems to guarantee that eavesdroppers have a high condition number of the forward operator, we expect to equip the system with an additional level of protection that complements existing notions of security and privacy. Finally, our results have the potential to be extended to more general nonlinear forward models, opening up opportunities for further research in this area.

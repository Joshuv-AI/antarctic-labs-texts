Abstract
. We consider the initial-value problem for the one-dimensional, time-dependent wave equation with positive, Lipschitz continuous coefficients, which are constant outside a bounded region. Under the assumption of compact support of the initial data, we prove that the local energy decays exponentially fast in time, and provide the explicit constant to which the solution converges for large times. We give explicit estimates of the rate of this exponential decay by two different techniques. The first one is based on the definition of a modified, weighted local energy, with suitably constructed weights. The second one is based on the integral formulation of the problem and, under a more restrictive assumption on the variation of the coefficients, allows us to obtain improved decay rates.



Keywords
wave equation with variable coefficients, local energy decay, long-time asymptotics

35L05, 35L10, 35B40

1.
Introduction

The paper is dedicated to the investigation of the time-decay of the solution of the following initial-value problem

(1)

\begin{cases}\beta\left(x\right)\partial_{t}^{2}u\left(x,t\right)-\partial_{x}\left(\alpha\left(x\right)\partial_{x}u\left(x,t\right)\right)=0,&x\in\mathbb{R},\>t>0,\\
u\left(x,0\right)=u_{0}\left(x\right),\hskip 10.00002pt\partial_{t}u\left(x,0\right)=u_{1}\left(x\right),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

where the coefficients
\alpha
,
\beta
are positive,
Lipschitz, and constant outside a bounded domain,
and the initial data
u_{0}
,
u_{1}
are compactly supported.
Studying such time-decay is important for practical applications
and it shall be used in our follow-up paper
[
2
]
.

Most relevant works, such as the recent
ones
[
10
]
and
[
3
]
, are focussed on proving the
decay in time of the solution derivatives in
L^{2}
-norm taken over
bounded sets. Such local energy decay is usually obtained in spatial
dimensions
d\geq 2
. Another recent paper
[
4
]
contains
a time-decay result in the one-dimensional case under certain restrictions on
\alpha
,
\beta
and localisation of the initial data
u_{0}
,
u_{1}
.

It is the purpose of the present work to improve such
L^{2}
-estimates
of the solution derivatives by showing that, in case of compactly
supported initial data, the decay of the solution in time is
exponential rather than algebraic. We also prove
that, in this case, an auxiliary condition on
\alpha
,
\beta
used in
[
4
]
can be avoided.
Moreover, we generalise the result to prove the decay not only of derivatives but also of the solution function itself. Namely, we conclude that the solution converges exponentially fast in time to a constant, which is explicitly computable in terms of
\alpha
,
\beta
and
u_{1}
.

Let us denote
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:x\geq 0\right\}

and start with a well-posedness result for
problem (
1
).

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\in W^{1,\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
and
\beta\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)

with
\alpha\left(x\right)\geq\alpha_{\min}>0
and
\beta\left(x\right)\geq\beta_{\min}>0

for
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore,
let
u_{0}\in H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)

and
u_{1}\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
. Then, problem (
1
) has
a unique solution
u\in C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)\cap C\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)
, with the continuous dependence on the initial data, according to the following estimate

(2)

\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}+\left\|\partial_{t}u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}\leq C\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}\right),\hskip 10.00002pt0\leq t\leq T,

valid for any
T>0
, with some constant
C=C\left(T,\alpha,\beta\right)>0
.

Moreover, if
u_{0}\in H^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
and
u_{1}\in H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
,
then we have
u\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)\cap C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)\cap C\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},H^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)
.

For the long-time analysis, we shall now make more restrictive assumptions.

Assumption 1.2
.

Let
\alpha
,
\beta\in W^{1,\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)

be real-valued functions such that
\alpha(x)\geq\alpha_{\min}
,
\beta(x)\geq\beta_{\min}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\alpha(x)\equiv\alpha_{0}
,
\beta(x)\equiv\beta_{0}

for
x\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\Omega_{in}
, with some
open bounded interval

\Omega_{in}\subset\mathbb{R}
and constants
\alpha_{\min}
,
\beta_{\min}
,
\alpha_{0}
,
\beta_{0}>0
.

Assumption 1.3
.

Suppose that
u_{0}\in H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
,
u_{1}\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
are real-valued functions with the
supports
\mathrm{supp}\,u_{0}
,
\mathrm{supp}\,u_{1}\subset\Omega_{0}
for some
open bounded interval

\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}
.

Our main results are given by the following two theorems.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\Omega_{in}
are as in Assumption
1.2
, and
u_{0}
,
u_{1}
,
\Omega_{0}
are as in Assumption
1.3
.
Then, for any
open bounded interval

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
, the solution of (
1
) obeys the decay estimate

(3)

\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)-u_{\infty}\right\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}+\left\|\partial_{t}u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq Ce^{-\Lambda t},\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0,

for some constant
C=C\left(u_{0},u_{1},\alpha,\beta,\left|\Omega_{0}\right|,\left|\Omega_{in}\right|\right){>0}
,
with
\left|\Omega_{0}\right|
,
\left|\Omega_{in}\right|
denoting the Lebesgue measure of the sets
\Omega_{0}
,
\Omega_{in}
, respectively, and

(4)

u_{\infty}:=\frac{1}{2\left(\alpha_{0}\beta_{0}\right)^{1/2}}\int_{\Omega_{0}}u_{1}\left(x\right)\beta\left(x\right)dx,\hskip 10.00002pt{\Lambda:=\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2t_{0}}e^{-2\gamma_{0}}\left(1-4\gamma_{0}e^{-4\gamma_{0}}\right)^{1/2}},

(5)

\gamma_{0}:=\frac{t_{0}}{4}\left\|\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\left(\alpha\beta\right)^{1/2}}+\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta^{3}}\right)^{1/2}\beta^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{in}\right)},\hskip 10.00002ptt_{0}:=\int_{\Omega_{in}}\left(\frac{\beta\left(x\right)}{\alpha\left(x\right)}\right)^{1/2}dx.

We remark that the steady state
u_{\infty}
given in
(
4
) generalises the one resulting from the d’Alembert
formula for constant
\alpha
and
\beta
, and for
u_{0}
,
u_{1}
with compact
support:

u_{\infty}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{0}}{\alpha_{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{1}(x)\,dx.

Note that even though the decay (
3
) is exponential, the decay rate
\Lambda

might be be rather small in case of significantly varying coefficients
\alpha
,
\beta
or large
\Omega_{in}
. In particular, the estimate for the decay rate given by the second equation of (
4
) is exponentially small for large values of
\gamma_{0}
.
Therefore, an alternative estimate is provided by the following
theorem, which employs a different idea and yields a better decay
rate, but in return requires an
assumption on the variation of
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\Omega_{in}
are as in Assumption

1.2
,
u_{0}
,
u_{1}
,
\Omega_{0}
are as in Assumption

1.3
, and
t_{0}
is
defined in (
5
).
Moreover, suppose
that

(6)

b_{0}:=\left\|\alpha\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{in}\right)}^{3/2}\left\|\beta\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{in}\right)}^{1/2}\left\|\left(\frac{1}{\left(\alpha\beta\right)^{1/2}}\right)^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega_{in}\right)}^{2}<\frac{1}{t_{0}}.

Then, for the solution of (
1
), estimate (
3
) holds true, and the decay rate is given by

(7)

\Lambda=\frac{1}{2t_{0}}\left|\log\left(b_{0}t_{0}\right)\right|.

Remark 1.6
.

Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
are valid
also for complex-valued
u_{0}
,
u_{1}
in Assumption
1.3
.
Indeed, due to the linearity of problem (
1
) and
the real-valuedness of
\alpha
,
\beta
, both theorems could be
applied separately to the real and imaginary parts of the initial data.

Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
are to be compared with the following recent result from
[
4
]
.

Proposition 1.7
.

[
4
, Thm. 1.2 (for
d=1
)]

Assume that
\alpha\left(x\right)\equiv\alpha_{0}
and that
\beta
satisfies Assumption
1.2
. Moreover, suppose that
\beta
is such that

(8)

\eta:=\left\|\beta\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{in}\right)}^{1/2}\left\|\left(\frac{1}{\beta^{1/2}}\right)^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{in}\right)}{\left|\Omega_{in}\right|}<1.

Then, for any bounded
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
, the solution of (
1
) satisfies

(9)

\left\|\partial_{x}u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+\left\|\partial_{t}u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq\frac{C}{t^{\left(1-\eta\right)/2}},\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq t_{1},

for some constant
C>0
and sufficiently large
t_{1}>0
.

Note that, when the initial data are compactly supported, the
algebraic decay in (
9
) appears to be sub-optimal compared to both
Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
, which
show the exponential decay of the solution derivatives. As observed
in
[
4
, Rem. 1.2]
, the smallness assumption
(
8
) on
\eta
measuring the relative perturbation of
the coefficients
is essential in the proof there,
but it is not needed in our proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For the wave equation (
1
) with
constant

coefficients and compactly supported initial data, the solution
converges on all bounded domains
\Omega
to the constant
u_{\infty}

in
finite time
. However, for variable coefficients, convergence in finite time does not hold and exponential
decay is the generic scenario: as an example, consider
(
1
) with
\alpha(x)\equiv\alpha_{0}
in

\mathbb{R}
and
\beta
Lipschitz with
\beta(x)=\beta_{1}
on
a closed subinterval of some open set
\Omega_{in}
, and
\beta(x)=\beta_{0}
in

\mathbb{R}\setminus\Omega_{in}
. A localised, travelling wave packet starting within
\Omega_{in}
gets partly reflected and partly transmitted. Hence, its local norm
\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)-u_{\infty}\right\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega_{in}\right)}
decays by a constant factor at each of these reflections, giving rise to an exponential decay.
On the other hand, we note that, for non-compactly supported initial data, the decay of the derivatives of the solution in time is generally related to the decay of the initial data at infinity. This can be easily seen already for the constant-coefficient case: the solution furnished explicitly by the d’Alembert formula decays to some
u_{\infty}
only algebraically fast if the assumed decay at infinity of
u_{0}
or
u_{1}
is algebraic.

,

,

4

1.1
, Theorem
1.4
, and Theorem
1.5
,
respectively. Some conclusions are drawn in Section

, and

1.4
are

2.
Proof of Proposition
1.1

One standard approach to prove
the well-posedness of problem (
1
) is
to construct the solution
u
, for coefficients
\alpha
and
\beta

sufficiently regular, by a Galerkin approximation (e.g. see
[
5
, Sect. 7.2]
, or
[
9
, Par. 11.2]
). Here, we shall, however,
use semigroup theory tools, since they require lower regularity assumptions
on
\alpha
and
\beta
. Although
this approach is quite standard (e.g.
see
[
8
, Par. 2]
,
[
11
, Par. 3]
, or

[
9
, Sect. 12.3.2]
), we
still outline
it, adapting to our particular setting.

With the notation

\phi\left(x,t\right):=\left(u\left(x,t\right),v\left(x,t\right)\right)^{T},\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002ptv\left(x,t\right):=\partial_{t}u\left(x,t\right),

A=A\left(x\right):=i\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
\frac{1}{\beta\left(x\right)}\partial_{x}\left(\alpha\left(x\right){\partial_{x}\,\cdot}\,\right)-\frac{1}{\beta\left(x\right)}&0\end{array}\right),\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002ptC=C\left(x\right):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
\frac{1}{\beta\left(x\right)}&0\end{array}\right),

problem (
1
) is equivalent to the first order linear system

(10)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\phi\left(x,t\right)={\left(-iA\left(x\right)+C\left(x\right)\right)}\phi\left(x,t\right),&x\in\mathbb{R},\hskip 10.00002ptt>0,\\
\phi\left(x,0\right)=\phi_{0}\left(x\right):=\left(u_{0}\left(x\right),u_{1}\left(x\right)\right)^{T},&x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

We split the proof into 2 steps.

Step 1. Functional setting.

We set

L^{2}_{\beta}\left(\mathbb{R}\right):=L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R};\beta\left(x\right)dx\right)
. Due to the assumption
\beta
,
1/\beta\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, the space
{L^{2}_{\beta}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}

actually consists of the same functions as
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.
We define the operator
B^{2}:=-\frac{1}{\beta\left(x\right)}\partial_{x}\left(\alpha\left(x\right){\partial_{x}\,\cdot\,}\right)+\frac{1}{\beta\left(x\right)}

in
{L^{2}_{\beta}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}
.
Since
\alpha
,
\beta
are assumed to be positive and bounded away from zero, the operator
B^{2}
is strictly positive.
We claim that this operator is self-adjoint with its (maximal) domain
being
\Dom B^{2}=H^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
. Indeed, the
symmetry of the operator
B^{2}
is evident, and
w\in H^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
implies
B^{2}w\in L^{2}_{\beta}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.
By
[
12
, Lem 2.3]
, it
remains to verify its surjectivity on
{L^{2}_{\beta}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}

or, equivalently, on

L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.
The latter follows
from the fact that, for
\alpha\in W^{1,\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
,
the operator
\widetilde{B}^{2}:=-\partial_{x}\left(\alpha\left(x\right){\partial_{x}\,\cdot\,}\right)+1

from
H^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
into
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)

is strictly positive, and hence invertible.

Next, we define in
{L^{2}_{\beta}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}

the positive square
root
B:=\sqrt{B^{2}}
, whose
(maximal) domain is
\Dom B=H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
. In fact,
for any
w\in H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|Bw\right\|_{{L^{2}_{\beta}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle Bw,Bw\right\rangle_{{L^{2}_{\beta}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(B^{2}w\right)\left(x\right)w\left(x\right)\beta\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\alpha\left(x\right)\left(w^{\prime}\left(x\right)\right)^{2}+w^{2}\left(x\right)\right]dx,

which, due to positivity and boundedness of
\alpha
, shows the
equivalence between
\left\|B\,\cdot\,\right\|_{{L^{2}_{\beta}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}}
and

\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}
.

With this preparation, we can introduce the Hilbert space
X:=H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)

equipped with the
inner product
\langle\phi,\widetilde{\phi}\rangle_{X}:=\langle Bu,B\widetilde{u}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\beta}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}+\langle v,\widetilde{v}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\beta}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}
.
Note that we have
\phi_{0}\in X
.

Step 2. Evolution semigroup.

Observe that the matrix operator
A
is symmetric in

X
, since for any
\phi
,
\widetilde{\phi}\in H^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle A\phi,\widetilde{\phi}\right\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle=i\left\langle Bv,B\widetilde{u}\right\rangle_{{L^{2}_{{\beta}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}}+i\left\langle-B^{2}u,\widetilde{v}\right\rangle_{{L^{2}_{{\beta}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}}

\displaystyle=i\left\langle v,B^{2}\widetilde{u}\right\rangle_{{L^{2}_{{\beta}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}}-i\left\langle Bu,B\tilde{v}\right\rangle_{{L^{2}_{{\beta}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}}=\left\langle\phi,A\widetilde{\phi}\right\rangle_{X}.

In fact,
A
is self-adjoint in
X
with (maximal) domain
\Dom A=H^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.
The latter statement follows from the maximality of
\Dom B^{2}=H^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.

By Stone’s theorem
[
7
, Ch. 1 Thm. 10.8]
, the operator

-iA
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup of unitary operators on
X
,
and the operator
C
is a bounded perturbation. Therefore, using
results on the perturbation of semigroups
[
7
, Sect. 3.1 Thm. 1.1]
,
the operator
-iA+C
, which defines problem (
10
), generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup of bounded
operators on
X
.
Consequently, we have the following two solution concepts for
problem (
10
), and thus for problem (
1
), depending
on the regularity of
\phi_{0}=\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)^{T}
(see

[
7
, p. 105]
for this discussion):

i)

For
\phi_{0}\in X
,
problem (
10
) has a unique mild solution, i.e.
\phi\in C\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},X\right)
,
and hence

u\in C\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)\cap C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)
,

v=\partial_{t}u\in C\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)

ii)

For
\phi_{0}\in\text{Dom }A,

problem (
10
) has a unique classical
solution (in the semigroup sense), i.e.
\phi\in C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},X\right)\cap C\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},\text{Dom }A\right)
,
and hence
u\in C\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},H^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)\cap C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)
,

v=\partial_{t}u\in C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)
.

Finally, estimate (
2
) is an automatic consequence of the semigroup approach due to
[
7
, Sect. 1.2 Thm. 2.2]
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Without loss of generality, let us take

\Omega=\Omega_{in}=\Omega_{0}=\left(0,x_{0}\right)
for some

x_{0}>0
. This is possible since the decay in
the region of interest
\Omega
can be deduced by enlarging it (if necessary) to an interval
containing both
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{in}
; moreover, as the
problem is posed on the entire real line, we can choose the origin at the left end of the resulting interval.

While the result of this theorem is stated for the mild solution

(11)

u\in C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)\cap C\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right),

its proof requires
the regularity of classical solutions established in
Proposition
1.1
.
Hence we approximate the initial data
(u_{0},\,u_{1})^{T}\in X{:=H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}
by a sequence
of functions
(u_{0}^{n},\,u_{1}^{n})^{T}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
with support in
\Omega_{0}

and converging in
X
to
(u_{0},\,u_{1})^{T}
. Due to Proposition
1.1
, each approximate initial condition
(u_{0}^{n},\,u_{1}^{n})
gives rise to a classical solution

(12)

u^{n}\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)\cap C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)\cap C\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},H^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right).

By the linearity of problem (
1
) and bound (
2
), these classical solutions converge uniformly to the
mild solution
u
on any finite time interval
[0,T]
:

(13)

u^{n}\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>u\quad\mbox{in }C^{1}([0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))\cap C([0,T],H^{1}(\mathbb{R})).

We recall that the notions of classical and mild solutions are discussed in
[
7
, p. 105]
.

The proof is performed in 3 steps.
In Step 1,
for any fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider
problem (
1
) for initial data
(u_{0}^{n},\,u_{1}^{n})\in H^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
and classical solution
u^{n}
as
in (
12
). We
transform (
1
) into an auxiliary
problem, for which we construct a weighted, local energy
functional that would admit
an exponential decay under
certain conditions on weight functions.
In Step 2,
we construct these weight functions such that the rate of decay of the energy functional can be estimated explicitly.
In Step 3,
we use the local energy decay and the convergence in (
13
)
to prove that the mild solution
u
of (
1
), for large
times, converges
to a constant uniformly on
\left[0,x_{0}\right]
. Moreover,
we identify this constant as
u_{\infty}
defined in (
4
).

Step 1. Auxiliary local energy functional.

As anticipated, here and in Step 2, we
establish the exponential-in-time decay of any classical solution
u^{n}
, i.e. for fixed index
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let us perform the
change of variable

(14)

x\mapsto y\left(x\right):=\dfrac{1}{t_{0}}\int_{0}^{x}\sqrt{\beta\left(\xi\right)/\alpha\left(\xi\right)}d\xi,\hskip 10.00002ptx\left(s\right)=y^{-1}\left(s\right),

(15)

t\mapsto\tau\left(t\right):=t/t_{0},\hskip 10.00002ptt\left(\tau\right)=t_{0}\tau,\hskip 10.00002ptt_{0}:=\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\sqrt{\beta\left(\xi\right)/\alpha\left(\xi\right)}d\xi,

and denote
v\left(y,\tau\right):={u^{n}}\left(x\left(y\right),t_{0}\tau\right)
,
v_{0}\left(y\right):={u^{n}_{0}}\left(x\left(y\right)\right)
,

v_{1}\left(y\right):=t_{0}\,{u^{n}_{1}}\left(x\left(y\right)\right)
,

(16)

\rho\left(y\right):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\left(x\left(y\right)\right)\beta\left(x\left(y\right)\right)}},\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma\left(y\right):=\frac{\rho^{\prime}\left(y\right)}{\rho\left(y\right)},\hskip 10.00002pt\rho_{0}:=\rho\left(0\right)=\rho\left(1\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{0}\beta_{0}}}.

Due to Assumption
1.2

W^{2,\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, and hence

given by (

v\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)\cap C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right)\cap C\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},H^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right).

We thus arrive at a problem equivalent to (
1

(18)

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}^{2}v\left(y,\tau\right)-\partial_{y}^{2}v\left(y,\tau\right)+\gamma\left(y\right)\partial_{y}v\left(y,\tau\right)=0,&y\in\mathbb{R},\hskip 10.00002pt\tau>0,\\
v\left(y,0\right)=v_{0}\left(y\right),\hskip 10.00002pt\partial_{\tau}v\left(y,0\right)=v_{1}\left(y\right),&y\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

where
\gamma\left(y\right)\equiv 0
for

and
\text{supp }v_{0}
,
\text{supp
}v_{1}\subseteq(0,1)
.

Motivated by the form of the global energy introduced in
[
6
]
for the wave equation in (

we consider the local energy functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(\tau\right):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\left(\partial_{\tau}v\left(y,\tau\right)\right)^{2}+\left(\partial_{y}v\left(y,\tau\right)\right)^{2}\right]\frac{dy}{\rho\left(y\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\left(\partial_{\tau}v\left(y,\tau\right)+\partial_{y}v\left(y,\tau\right)\right)^{2}+\left(\partial_{\tau}v\left(y,\tau\right)-\partial_{y}v\left(y,\tau\right)\right)^{2}\right]\frac{dy}{\rho\left(y\right)}>0.

This quantity is decaying since, by direct calculations employing
integration by parts, using (

) and the wave

), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{loc}\left(\tau\right)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[\partial_{\tau}v\left(y,\tau\right)\partial^{2}_{\tau}v\left(y,\tau\right)+\partial_{y}v\left(y,\tau\right){\partial_{y}\partial_{\tau}}v\left(y,\tau\right)\right]\frac{dy}{\rho\left(y\right)}

(20)

\displaystyle{=}

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{1}\left[\partial_{\tau}v\left(y,\tau\right)\left(\partial^{2}_{\tau}v\left(y,\tau\right)-\partial_{y}^{2}v\left(y,\tau\right)+\frac{\rho^{\prime}\left(y\right)}{\rho\left(y\right)}\partial_{y}v\left(y,\tau\right)\right)\right]\frac{dy}{\rho\left(y\right)}}

\displaystyle{+\left[\partial_{y}v\left(1,\tau\right)\partial_{\tau}v\left(1,\tau\right)-\partial_{y}v\left(0,\tau\right)\partial_{\tau}v\left(0,\tau\right)\right]\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{y}v\left(1,\tau\right)\partial_{\tau}v\left(1,\tau\right)-\partial_{y}v\left(0,\tau\right)\partial_{\tau}v\left(0,\tau\right)\right]\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\leq 0.

Note that we have used here the regularity of a classical

(well-defined for any

\left.\left(\partial_{y}-\partial_{\tau}\right)v\left(y,\tau\right)\right|_{y=0}=0=\left.\left(\partial_{y}+\partial_{\tau}\right)v\left(y,\tau\right)\right|_{y=1},

which are due to the support properties of the functions

,

and
\gamma
.

We note, in passing, that the global energy, defined in the same way as (

, is conserved.

Even though the conventional local energy functional (

and

To this effect, we consider now a modified local energy functional in the spirit of

where
\phi_{1}\left(y\right)
,
\phi_{2}\left(y\right)
are some
strictly positive weight functions on
\left[0,1\right]
, which are yet
to be chosen.

Similarly to (
3
), we differentiate under the integral sign to obtain (suppressing the arguments of all functions for the sake of brevity)

(23)

\displaystyle E_{loc}^{\prime}\left(\tau\right)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[\phi_{1}\left(\partial_{\tau}v+\partial_{y}v\right)\left(\partial_{\tau}^{2}v+{\partial_{y}\partial_{\tau}}v\right)+\phi_{2}\left(\partial_{\tau}v-\partial_{y}v\right)\left(\partial_{\tau}^{2}v-{\partial_{y}\partial_{\tau}}v\right)\right]\frac{dy}{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[\phi_{1}\left(\partial_{\tau}v+\partial_{y}v\right)\left(\partial_{y}^{2}v-\gamma\partial_{y}v+{\partial_{y}\partial_{\tau}}v\right)+\phi_{2}\left(\partial_{\tau}v-\partial_{y}v\right)\left(\partial_{y}^{2}v-\gamma\partial_{y}v-{\partial_{y}\partial_{\tau}}v\right)\right]\frac{dy}{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\phi_{1}\partial_{y}\frac{\left(\partial_{\tau}v+\partial_{y}v\right)^{2}}{\rho}-\phi_{2}\partial_{y}\frac{\left(\partial_{\tau}v-\partial_{y}v\right)^{2}}{\rho}+\left(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\right)\gamma\frac{\left(\partial_{\tau}v\right)^{2}-\left(\partial_{y}v\right)^{2}}{\rho}\right]dy,

\gamma

Integrating (

),
we obtain

\displaystyle E_{loc}^{\prime}\left(\tau\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left[-\phi_{1}^{\prime}\left(y\right)\left(\partial_{\tau}v\left(y,\tau\right)+\partial_{y}v\left(y,\tau\right)\right)^{2}+\phi_{2}^{\prime}\left(y\right)\left(\partial_{\tau}v\left(y,\tau\right)-\partial_{y}v\left(y,\tau\right)\right)^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(\phi_{1}\left(y\right)-\phi_{2}\left(y\right)\right)\gamma\left(y\right)\left(\left(\partial_{\tau}v\left(y,\tau\right)\right)^{2}-\left(\partial_{y}v\left(y,\tau\right)\right)^{2}\right)\right]\frac{dy}{\rho\left(y\right)}

\displaystyle-\left[\phi_{2}\left(1\right)\left(\partial_{y}v\left(1,\tau\right)\right)^{2}+\phi_{1}\left(0\right)\left(\partial_{y}v\left(0,\tau\right)\right)^{2}\right]\frac{2}{\rho_{0}}.

We estimate

\displaystyle\left(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\right)\gamma\left(\left(\partial_{\tau}v\right)^{2}-\left(\partial_{y}v\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\right)\gamma\left(\partial_{\tau}v+\partial_{y}v\right)\left(\partial_{\tau}v-\partial_{y}v\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\right|\left|\gamma\right|\left(\left(\partial_{\tau}v+\partial_{y}v\right)^{2}+\left(\partial_{\tau}v-\partial_{y}v\right)^{2}\right),

and therefore, since

, we arrive at

\displaystyle E_{loc}^{\prime}\left(\tau\right)\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\left(\phi_{1}^{\prime}\left(y\right)-\frac{\left|\gamma\left(y\right)\right|}{2}\left|\phi_{1}\left(y\right)-\phi_{2}\left(y\right)\right|\right)\left(\partial_{\tau}v\left(y,\tau\right)+\partial_{y}v\left(y,\tau\right)\right)^{2}\right.

(24)

\displaystyle\left.+\left(-\phi_{2}^{\prime}\left(y\right)-\frac{\left|\gamma\left(y\right)\right|}{2}\left|\phi_{1}\left(y\right)-\phi_{2}\left(y\right)\right|\right)\left(\partial_{\tau}v\left(y,\tau\right)-\partial_{y}v\left(y,\tau\right)\right)^{2}\right]\frac{dy}{\rho\left(y\right)}.

We aim to obtain
an estimate of the form
E_{loc}^{\prime}\left(\tau\right)\leq-\lambda_{0}E_{loc}\left(\tau\right)
with some
\lambda_{0}>0
,
and explore the possibility of choosing

,

Step 2. Construction of the weights
\phi_{1}
,
\phi_{2}
.

Motivated by (
24

\phi_{1}\left(y\right)
,
\phi_{2}\left(y\right)>0

)
that satisfy, on

, the following differential inequalities

(25)

\begin{cases}\phi_{1}^{\prime}\left(y\right)-\frac{\left|\gamma\left(y\right)\right|}{2}\left|\phi_{1}\left(y\right)-\phi_{2}\left(y\right)\right|\geq\lambda\phi_{1}\left(y\right),\hskip 10.00002pty\in\left(0,1\right),\\
-\phi_{2}^{\prime}\left(y\right)-\frac{\left|\gamma\left(y\right)\right|}{2}\left|\phi_{1}\left(y\right)-\phi_{2}\left(y\right)\right|\geq\lambda\phi_{2}\left(y\right),\hskip 10.00002pty\in\left(0,1\right),\\
\phi_{1}\left(0\right)=\phi_{2}\left(0\right)=1,\end{cases}

for some constant
\lambda>0
to be chosen. To this end,
we consider functions
\varphi_{1}\left(y\right)
,
\varphi_{2}\left(y\right)>0

\begin{cases}\varphi_{1}^{\prime}\left(y\right)-\gamma_{0}\left(\varphi_{1}\left(y\right)-\varphi_{2}\left(y\right)\right)=\lambda\varphi_{1}\left(y\right),\hskip 10.00002pty\in\left(0,1\right),\\

\varphi_{1}\left(0\right)=\varphi_{2}\left(0\right)=1,\end{cases}

where
\gamma_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\gamma\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,1\right)}
.

It is easy to see that any solution of (
26

25
), provided that
\varphi_{1}\left(y\right)
,
\varphi_{2}\left(y\right)>0
for all

0\leq\varphi_{1}\left(y\right)-\varphi_{2}\left(y\right)=\left|\varphi_{1}\left(y\right)-\varphi_{2}\left(y\right)\right|,\hskip 10.00002pty\in\left[0,1\right].

Summing both ODEs of (

e^{-2\gamma_{0}y}
, and integrating on
(0,y)
,
we obtain

\varphi_{1}\left(y\right)-\varphi_{2}\left(y\right)=\lambda\int_{0}^{y}e^{2\gamma_{0}\left(y-\xi\right)}\left[\varphi_{1}\left(\xi\right)+\varphi_{2}\left(\xi\right)\right]d\xi,\hskip 10.00002pty\in\left[0,1\right],

and hence positivity of the integrand directly implies (
27

Note that it is possible to consider more general versions of (
25
) (and hence (
26
)), with equations involving different parameters
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
on the right-hand sides, and initial conditions given by a positive constant different from
1
. However, it can be shown that no advantage could be gained from such generalisations.

We shall now estimate the largest possible value
\lambda>0
(or, more precisely, its supremum) such that
\varphi_{1}
,
\varphi_{2}>0
in
\left[0,1\right]

According to the first equation in (

for

\varphi_{2}\left(y\right)
decreases monotonically in
\left[0,1\right]
starting from the value
\varphi_{2}\left(0\right)=1
.
Therefore, to guarantee the positivity of
\varphi_{2}\left(y\right)
in
\left[0,1\right]
,

\varphi_{2}\left(1\right)>0

We can write the solution of (

\varphi_{1}\left(y\right)=\frac{e^{\gamma_{0}y}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\gamma_{0}^{2}}}\left[\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\gamma_{0}^{2}}\cosh\left(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\gamma_{0}^{2}}y\right)+\left(\lambda-\gamma_{0}\right)\sinh\left(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\gamma_{0}^{2}}y\right)\right],

\varphi_{2}\left(y\right)=\frac{e^{\gamma_{0}y}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\gamma_{0}^{2}}}\left[\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\gamma_{0}^{2}}\cosh\left(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\gamma_{0}^{2}}y\right)-\left(\lambda+\gamma_{0}\right)\sinh\left(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\gamma_{0}^{2}}y\right)\right],

Then, the above requirement

\tanh\left({\sqrt{\gamma_{0}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}\right)<\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{0}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}{\gamma_{0}+\lambda}.

To determine the interval of admissible values of

\tanh\left({\sqrt{\gamma_{0}^{2}+\lambda^{2}_{*}}}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{0}^{2}+\lambda^{2}_{*}}}{\gamma_{0}+\lambda_{*}},

\lambda_{*}(\gamma_{0})
on

\lambda_{*}

in the Appendix (inequality (

The function

While the parameter choice

,
\varphi_{2}
.

.

{\lambda_{0}(\gamma_{0}):=\gamma_{0}e^{-2\gamma_{0}}\sqrt{1-4\gamma_{0}e^{-4\gamma_{0}}}}

Consequently, we obtain from (

Step 3. Convergence of the solution and identification of the constant

The local energy functional (

decay of
\left\|\partial_{t}u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(0,x_{0}\right)}

,

Using the regularity of

u\left(x,t\right)=u\left(0,t\right)+\int_{0}^{x}\partial_{x}u\left(s,t\right)ds=u\left(x_{0},t\right)-\int_{x}^{x_{0}}\partial_{x}u\left(s,t\right)ds,

\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)-u\left(0,t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(0,x_{0}\right)}^{2},\,\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)-u\left(x_{0},t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(0,x_{0}\right)}^{2}\leq x_{0}^{2}\left\|\partial_{x}u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(0,x_{0}\right)}^{2}.

Let us now integrate the wave equation in (

Using the outflow boundary conditions (

\left.\left(\partial_{x}-\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{0}}{\alpha_{0}}}\partial_{t}\right)u\left(x,t\right)\right|_{x=0}=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\left.\left(\partial_{x}+\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{0}}{\alpha_{0}}}\partial_{t}\right)u\left(x,t\right)\right|_{x=x_{0}}=0,

,

From (

\displaystyle u\left(x,t\right)-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\alpha_{0}\beta_{0}}}\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\beta\left(s\right)u_{1}\left(s\right)ds=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[u\left(x,t\right)-u\left(0,t\right)\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[u\left(x,t\right)-u\left(x_{0},t\right)\right]

(38)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\alpha_{0}\beta_{0}}}\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\beta\left(s\right)\partial_{t}u\left(s,t\right)ds

for all

For the first two terms on the right-hand side

\left\|u\left(x,t\right)-u\left(0,t\right)\right\|_{H^{1}\left(0,x_{0}\right)}^{2}=\left\|u\left(x,t\right)-u\left(0,t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(0,x_{0}\right)}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{x}u\left(x,t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(0,x_{0}\right)}^{2},

\displaystyle\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)-u_{\infty}\right\|_{H^{1}\left(0,x_{0}\right)}\leq

Proof of Theorem

.

1.4
.

), we shall focus on its

19
) and estimate the quantities that enter it.
Since we shall not

work here directly with the mild solution

.
We consider again equation (

) in the

(y,\tau)

).
By treating the third term on the left-hand side as a (known)

v\left(y,\tau\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left[v_{0}\left(y+\tau\right)+v_{0}\left(y-\tau\right)\right]+\frac{1}{2}\int_{y-\tau}^{y+\tau}v_{1}\left(z\right)dz-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{y-s}^{y+s}\gamma\left(z\right)\partial_{y}v\left(z,\tau-s\right)dzds.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{y}v\left(y,\tau\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[v_{0}^{\prime}\left(y+\tau\right)+v_{0}^{\prime}\left(y-\tau\right)\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[v_{1}\left(y+\tau\right)-v_{1}\left(y-\tau\right)\right]

(40)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left[\gamma\left(y-s\right)\partial_{y}v\left(y-s,\tau-s\right)-\gamma\left(y+s\right)\partial_{y}v\left(y+s,\tau-s\right)\right]ds,

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}v\left(y,\tau\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[v_{0}^{\prime}\left(y+\tau\right)-v_{0}^{\prime}\left(y-\tau\right)\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[v_{1}\left(y+\tau\right)+v_{1}\left(y-\tau\right)\right]

(41)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left[\gamma\left(y-s\right)\partial_{y}v\left(y-s,\tau-s\right)+\gamma\left(y+s\right)\partial_{y}v\left(y+s,\tau-s\right)\right]ds.

Identity (

) becomes evident
by making a change of variable
s\mapsto\tau-s
in the

39
)
before and after the differentiation with respect to
\tau

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{y-s}^{y+s}\gamma\left(z\right)\partial_{y}v\left(z,\tau-s\right)dzds=\partial_{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{y-\tau+s}^{y+\tau-s}\gamma\left(z\right)\partial_{y}v\left(z,s\right)dzds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\left[\gamma\left(y+\tau-s\right)\partial_{y}v\left(y+\tau-s,s\right)+\gamma\left(y-\tau+s\right)\partial_{y}v\left(y-\tau+s,s\right)\right]ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\left[\gamma\left(y+s\right)\partial_{y}v\left(y+s,\tau-s\right)+\gamma\left(y-s\right)\partial_{y}v\left(y-s,\tau-s\right)\right]ds.

Note that, for
\tau\geq 1
, the
terms in (
40
) and (
41
)
that do not contain the integral
vanish
for all
y
in
\left(0,1\right)
.
To estimate
\mathcal{E}_{loc}
for
\tau\geq 1
, we
insert (
40
) and (
41
) in the
definition of
\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(\tau\right)
in (
19
) and use that
\textrm{supp }\gamma\subset\left(0,1\right)
:

(42)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(\tau\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\left(\int_{y}^{1}\gamma\left(r\right)\partial_{y}v\left(r,\tau+y-r\right)dr\right)^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(\int_{0}^{y}\gamma\left(r\right)\partial_{y}v\left(r,\tau-y+r\right)dr\right)^{2}\right]\frac{dy}{\rho\left(y\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\int_{y}^{1}\rho\left(\tilde{r}\right)\gamma^{2}\left(\tilde{r}\right)d\tilde{r}\int_{y}^{1}\left(\partial_{y}v\left(r,\tau+y-r\right)\right)^{2}\frac{dr}{\rho\left(r\right)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{0}^{y}\rho\left(\tilde{r}\right)\gamma^{2}\left(\tilde{r}\right)d\tilde{r}\int_{0}^{y}\left(\partial_{y}v\left(r,\tau-y+r\right)\right)^{2}\frac{dr}{\rho\left(r\right)}\right]\frac{dy}{\rho\left(y\right)}.

Denoting

b_{0}:=\left\|\alpha\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,x_{0}\right)}^{3/2}\left\|\beta\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,x_{0}\right)}^{1/2}\left\|\left(1/\left(\alpha\beta\right)^{1/2}\right)^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(0,x_{0}\right)}^{2},

as in (
6
), we have

\max\left\{\sup_{y\in\left(0,1\right)}\frac{1}{\rho\left(y\right)}\int_{0}^{y}\rho\left(s\right)\gamma^{2}\left(s\right)ds,\sup_{y\in\left(0,1\right)}\frac{1}{\rho\left(y\right)}\int_{y}^{1}\rho\left(s\right)\gamma^{2}\left(s\right)ds\right\}\leq b_{0}t_{0}.

Therefore, we continue with (
42

(43)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(\tau\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b_{0}t_{0}}{4}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{y}\left(\partial_{y}v\left(r,\tau-y+r\right)\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\rho\left(r\right)}drdy\right.

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\qquad\quad\left.+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{y}^{1}\left(\partial_{y}v\left(r,\tau+y-r\right)\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\rho\left(r\right)}drdy\right].

In the two terms on the right-hand side
of (

), the integration involves the values of
\partial_{y}v
on two right-angled triangles with vertices
\left\{\left(0,\tau-1\right),\left(0,\tau\right),\left(1,\tau\right)\right\}
and
\left\{\left(1,\tau-1\right),\left(0,\tau\right),\left(1,\tau\right)\right\}
. These triangles are parametrised by the sets of lines parallel to their hypotenuses, which are the two characteristic families of the wave equation in (
18
). We now change the integration variables to have parametrisation of these triangles by the sets of horizontal lines. Consequently, for the first integral, denoting
\sigma:=\tau-y+r
, we have that

\sigma\in(\tau-1,\tau)
and

. Similarly, for
the second integral, denoting
\sigma:=\tau+y-r
, we have that

\sigma\in(\tau-1,\tau)
and
r\in(\tau-\sigma,1)
. Therefore, rewriting

) as integrals in the variables

\sigma
and

, we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{y}\left(\partial_{y}v\left(r,\tau-y+r\right)\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\rho\left(r\right)}drdy+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{y}^{1}\left(\partial_{y}v\left(r,\tau+y-r\right)\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\rho\left(r\right)}drdy\\
&\qquad=\int_{\tau-1}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{\sigma-\tau+1}\left(\partial_{y}v\left(r,\sigma\right)\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\rho\left(r\right)}drd\sigma+\int_{\tau-1}^{\tau}\int_{\tau-\sigma}^{1}\left(\partial_{y}v\left(r,\sigma\right)\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\rho\left(r\right)}drd\sigma.\end{split}

With this identity, taking into account the positivity of the
integrands, we obtain from (
43
) that

(44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(\tau\right)\leq\frac{b_{0}t_{0}}{2}\int_{\tau-1}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\partial_{y}v\left(r,\sigma\right)\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\rho\left(r\right)}drd\sigma\leq b_{0}t_{0}\int_{\tau-1}^{\tau}\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(\sigma\right)d\sigma.

Since
\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(\tau\right)
is a positive non-increasing
function (recall (
3
)), it follows
from (
44
) that

\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(\tau\right)\leq b_{0}t_{0}\,\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(\tau-1\right),\hskip 10.00002pt\tau\geq 1.

Iterating this inequality
\left\lfloor\tau\right\rfloor

times (where
\left\lfloor X\right\rfloor
denotes the integer part
of
X
), and using the assumption
b_{0}t_{0}<1
, we obtain, for
\tau\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(\tau\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(\lfloor\tau\rfloor\right)\leq(b_{0}t_{0})^{2}\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(\lfloor\tau\rfloor-2\right)\leq\ldots\leq{(b_{0}t_{0})^{\lfloor\tau\rfloor}\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(0\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(0\right)e^{\lfloor\tau\rfloor\log(b_{0}t_{0})}\leq\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(0\right)e^{(\tau-1)\log(b_{0}t_{0})}=\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(0\right)\frac{e^{\tau\log(b_{0}t_{0})}}{b_{0}t_{0}},

which, due to

\log(b_{0}t_{0})<0
, shows an exponential decay in
\tau
.

When scaling back to the original variables
(x,t)
we obtain

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{loc}\left(t\right):=\mathcal{E}_{loc}\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}\right)\leq\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{loc}\left(0\right)\frac{e^{\frac{t}{t_{0}}\log(b_{0}t_{0})}}{b_{0}t_{0}},\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq t_{0}.

Step 3 of the proof of Theorem
1.4
can then be repeated (with a slight modification
of the multiplicative constants), yielding the exponential convergence (with rate (
7
))
of the solution to the constant
u_{\infty}
.

We note that, even though the last bound was proved for
t\geq t_{0}
, it can be extended to
t\geq 0
merely at the expense of enlarging the constant in front of the time-decaying exponential factor. This is due to the well-posedness result for the wave equation on a finite time-interval, see Proposition
1.1
and, in particular, (
2
).
∎

5.
Conclusion

For the homogeneous wave equation in
\mathbb{R}
with
compactly supported initial data, in the case of constant coefficients,
the solution converges to the
steady state in finite time. However, exponential
convergence is the generic scenario in the case of
variable coefficients.

We proved two different results for
this convergence in local energy. The first one employs
a multiplier technique, constructing appropriate weight functions
to define a modified local energy that satisfies a first order
differential inequality. This allowed us to prove
the exponential decay.
Using the solution balance over space-time rectangles, we were
able to identify the constant steady state.
The second result improves the first one, under an additional
assumption on the variation of the coefficients.
After rescaling the equation, its
integral form allowed us to estimate
the local energy by iterating in time, leading to an
exponential decay with improved rates.

Our results complement those of algebraic time-decay
presented in
[
4
]
, where
the initial data were assumed to be
sufficiently localised but not compactly supported. In that case, exponential decay is generally not expected.

Lemma A.1
.

For any fixed
\gamma_{0}>0
, the equation

(45)

{\tanh\left({\sqrt{\gamma_{0}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{0}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}{\gamma_{0}+\lambda},\quad\lambda>0,}

has a unique solution
\lambda=\lambda_{*}
.
Moreover, for
\lambda>0
, the strict inequality (
30
)
is satisfied if and only if
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
.

Proof.

Since the right-hand side of (
45
), for
\lambda>0
, is smaller than 1, equation (
45
) is equivalent to

(46)

f_{\gamma_{0}}(\tilde{\lambda}):=\gamma_{0}\sqrt{1+\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}-\artanh\left({\frac{\sqrt{1+\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}}{1+\tilde{\lambda}}}\right)=0,

where
\tilde{\lambda}:=\lambda/\gamma_{0}
. We have

f_{\gamma_{0}}^{\prime}(\tilde{\lambda})=\frac{2\gamma_{0}\tilde{\lambda}^{2}-\tilde{\lambda}+1}{2\tilde{\lambda}\sqrt{1+\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}},\qquad\lim_{\tilde{\lambda}\to+\infty}f^{\prime}_{\gamma_{0}}(\tilde{\lambda})=\gamma_{0}>0.

Hence
\lim_{\tilde{\lambda}\to+\infty}f_{\gamma_{0}}(\tilde{\lambda})=+\infty

and, moreover,
\lim_{\tilde{\lambda}\to 0^{+}}f_{\gamma_{0}}(\tilde{\lambda})=-\infty
.

Case 1:

For
\gamma_{0}\geq 1/8
, we have

f^{\prime}_{\gamma_{0}}(\tilde{\lambda})\geq 0
for any
\tilde{\lambda}>0
.
Hence,
f_{\gamma_{0}}
is strictly increasing and
f_{\gamma_{0}}
has a
unique zero at some
\tilde{\lambda}_{*}>0
and

f_{\gamma_{0}}(\tilde{\lambda})<0
for

\tilde{\lambda}\in(0,\tilde{\lambda}_{*})
.

Case 2:

For
\gamma_{0}\in(0,1/8)
, we consider
f_{\gamma_{0}}(\tilde{\lambda}_{1})
,
where
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}:=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-8\gamma_{0}}}{4\gamma_{0}}
is the
first zero of
f_{\gamma_{0}}^{\prime}
.
Define

g(\gamma_{0}):=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-8\gamma_{0}}}{4\gamma_{0}}
. From

g^{\prime}(\gamma_{0})>0
for all
\gamma_{0}\in(0,1/8)
,

\lim_{\gamma_{0}\to 0^{+}}g(\gamma_{0})=1
, and
g(1/8)=2
, we deduce that

g
is strictly increasing and
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}=g(\gamma_{0})\in(1,2)
for all

\gamma_{0}\in(0,1/8)
. Since

\artanh\left({\frac{\sqrt{1+\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}}{1+\tilde{\lambda}}}\right)

is an increasing function of
\tilde{\lambda}
in
(1,2)
, we obtain
from (
46
) that

f_{\gamma_{0}}(\tilde{\lambda}_{1})\leq\frac{\sqrt{1+4}}{8}-\artanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)\simeq-0.6019
.
This
shows that
f_{\gamma_{0}}(\tilde{\lambda}_{1})<0

for
\gamma_{0}\in(0,1/8)
. Hence,
f_{\gamma_{0}}
also has a unique zero

\tilde{\lambda}_{*}{>\tilde{\lambda}_{2}}>0
in this case, where

\tilde{\lambda}_{2}:=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-8\gamma_{0}}}{4\gamma_{0}}
is the
second zero of
f_{\gamma_{0}}^{\prime}
.

This proves unique solvability of equation (
45
). Moreover,
the reasoning above also shows that, for any
\gamma_{0}>0
, there is
a unique
\lambda_{*}=\lambda_{*}\left(\gamma_{0}\right)>0
such that, for
\lambda>0
, the following equivalences hold true:

\displaystyle\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})\quad

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad f_{\gamma_{0}}\left(\lambda/\gamma_{0}\right)<0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sqrt{\gamma_{0}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}-\artanh\left({\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{0}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}{\gamma_{0}^{2}+\lambda}}\right)<0

Here, the last equivalence is due to the strict monotonicity of the
\tanh
function. The second part of the statement thus follows.

∎

Lemma A.2
.

For any
a>0
and

(47)

1<q<\sqrt{1+\dfrac{e^{4a}+4a-e^{2a}\sqrt{e^{4a}+8a}}{8a^{2}}},

we have

(48)

\frac{q}{\tanh\left(aq\right)}>1+\sqrt{q^{2}-1}.

Proof.

The proof is split into two steps.

Step 1.
We shall first analyse for which values
q_{0}\geq 1
the following auxiliary inequality holds:

(49)

\left(1+2a\right)e^{-2a}-2ae^{-2a}q_{0}^{2}\geq\sqrt{q_{0}^{2}-1}.

we rewrite (
49
)

(50)

1+2a-2aQ\geq e^{2a}\sqrt{Q-1}.

This is equivalent to

(51)

4a^{2}Q^{2}-\left(4a\left(1+2a\right)+e^{4a}\right)Q+\left(1+2a\right)^{2}+e^{4a}\geq 0,

as long as both sides of (

) are nonnegative, i.e. for
Q\in\left[1,\frac{1+2a}{2a}\right]
.

The left-hand side of (
51
) is a quadratic expression in

which is positive
for
Q\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\left[Q_{1},Q_{2}\right]
, with

\displaystyle Q_{1}:=Q_{0}-\frac{e^{2a}\sqrt{e^{4a}+8a}}{8a^{2}},\hskip 10.00002ptQ_{2}:=Q_{0}+\frac{e^{2a}\sqrt{e^{4a}+8a}}{8a^{2}},

\displaystyle Q_{0}:=1+\frac{e^{4a}+4a}{8a^{2}}.

It can be checked easily that
Q_{1}\in\left(1,\frac{1+2a}{2a}\right)
,
and therefore any
Q\in\left[1,Q_{1}\right]
satisfies (

).
Consequently,
(

) is satisfied if and only if

Step 2.
We observe that since

\displaystyle q_{0}\left[1+e^{-2a}\left(1+2a-2aq_{0}\right)\right]

\displaystyle 1+\left(1+2a\right)e^{-2a}-2ae^{-2a}q_{0}^{2}.

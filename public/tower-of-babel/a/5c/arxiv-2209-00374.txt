Spin contributions to the gravitational-waveform modes for spin-aligned binaries at the 3.5PN order
Quentin Henry, Sylvain Marsat, Mohammed Khalil

Abstract
We complete the post-Newtonian (PN) prediction at the 3.5PN order for the spin contributions to the gravitational waveforms emitted by inspiraling compact binaries, in the case of quasi-circular, equatorial orbits, where both spins are aligned with the orbital angular momentum. Using results from the multipolar post-Minkowskian wave generation formalism, we extend previous works that derived the dynamics and gravitational-wave energy flux and phasing, by computing the full waveform decomposed in spin-weighted spherical harmonics. This new calculation requires the computation of multipolar moments of higher multipolar order, new quadratic-in-spin contributions to the hereditary tail terms entering at the 3.5PN order, as well as other non-linear interactions between moments. When specialized to the test-mass limit, our results are equivalent to those obtained in the literature for the waveform emitted by a test-mass in equatorial, circular orbits around a Kerr black hole. We also compute the factorized modes for use in effective-one-body waveform models, correcting the 2.5PN nonspinning and 3PN quadratic-in-spin terms in the (2,1) mode used in current models.

(1)

II
Summary of the formalism

II.1
Definitions and conventions

In this paper, we compute the amplitude modes of the GW emitted by a binary system of spinning compact objects in the inspiral phase. We restrict ourselves to the non-precessing case which implies that, as for the non-spinning case, the motion of the system remains planar. Let us define the radiative coordinate system
X^{\mu}=(cT,\bm{X})
, in which
R=|\bm{X}|
is the distance of the observer from the source and
\bm{N}=\bm{X}/R
is the direction of propagation of the GW. We denote retarded time
T_{R}=T-R/c
, where
c
is the speed of light in vacuum. We define the spatial unitary basis
(\bm{e}_{X},\bm{e}_{Y},\bm{e}_{Z})
such that the orbital plane lies within the
(\bm{e}_{X},\bm{e}_{Y})
plane and
\bm{N}
in the
(\bm{e}_{Y},\bm{e}_{Z})
plane. The two GW polarizations
h_{+}
and
h_{\times}
, defined in Eq. (
5
), propagate in the plane orthogonal to the direction of propagation
\bm{N}
. It is convenient to introduce the orthonormal triad
(\bm{P},\bm{Q},\bm{N})
by choosing
\bm{P}=\bm{e}_{X}
and
\bm{Q}=\bm{N}\times\bm{P}
.

We use the following conventions henceforth:
\mathcal{O}(n)
means
\mathcal{O}(1/c^{n})
, i.e. represents a contribution of the order
(n/2)
PN at least. Greek indices denote spacetime coordinates, i.e.
\mu=0,1,2,3
, while Latin indices are used for spatial coordinates, i.e.
i=1,2,3
. We use the multi-index notation
L=i_{1}\dots i_{\ell}
. Symmetrization and anti-symmetrization are represented by, respectively, parenthesis and brackets around indices while the symmetric trace-free (STF) projection is denoted by
\langle\rangle
. We adopt the signature
(-,+,+,+)
and keep explicit both Newton’s constant
G
and the speed of light
c
, unless explicitly specified. Finally the covariant derivative along the worldline is written as
\mathrm{D}/(c\,\mathrm{D}\tau)=u^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}
, where
u^{\mu}
is the four-velocity of the particle.

The problem is parametrized using the following notations: we use the coordinate-time
t
parametrization. The quantities
m_{A}
,
\bm{y}_{A}
,
\bm{v}_{A}=\mathrm{d}\bm{y}_{A}/\mathrm{d}t
and
\bm{a}_{A}=\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{y}_{A}/\mathrm{d}t^{2}
refer respectively to the mass, position, velocity and acceleration of body
A=1,2
. The notation SO refers to the spin-orbit interaction, SS to the quadratic-in-spin interaction and finally SSS to the cubic-in-spin terms.

II.2
Spherical harmonics decomposition

The Einstein field equations can be exactly written, by imposing the harmonic gauge condition
\partial_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}=0
, as

\Box h^{\mu\nu}=\frac{16\pi G}{c^{4}}\tau^{\mu\nu},

(2)

where
\Box
is the flat d’Alembertian operator defined with respect to the inverse Minkowski metric
\eta^{\mu\nu}
,
h^{\mu\nu}
is the deviation to the gothic metric
h^{\mu\nu}=\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}-\eta^{\mu\nu}
,
g=\text{det}(g_{\mu\nu})
is the determinant of the metric. Finally,

is the stress-energy pseudo tensor

\tau^{\mu\nu}=|g|T^{\mu\nu}+\frac{c^{4}}{16\pi G}\,\Lambda^{\mu\nu}\,,

(3)

where
T^{\mu\nu}
is the stress-energy tensor and
\Lambda^{\mu\nu}
is a function of derivatives of at least quadratic terms in the perturbed metric
h^{\mu\nu}
. Its expression is given in Eq. (24) of Ref.
Blanchet 2014
.

In the coordinate system

, the transverse-traceless (TT) projection

can be, at leading order in

and

\displaystyle h_{ij}^{\text{TT}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4G}{c^{2}R}\,\mathcal{P}_{ijkl}(\bm{N})\sum^{+\infty}_{\ell=2}\frac{1}{c^{\ell}\ell!}\left\{N_{L-2}\,U_{klL-2}(T_{R})-\frac{2\ell}{c(\ell+1)}\,N_{aL-2}\,\varepsilon_{ab(k}\,V_{l)bL-2}(T_{R})\right\}\,,

(4)

where we introduced the TT projection operator
\mathcal{P}_{ijkl}=\mathcal{P}_{i(k}\mathcal{P}_{l)j}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{P}_{ij}\mathcal{P}_{kl}
, with
\mathcal{P}_{ij}=\delta_{ij}-N_{i}N_{j}
the projector orthogonal to the unit direction
\bm{N}
. We can define the usual polarization waveforms in the orthonormal triad
(\bm{P},\bm{Q},\bm{N})
as

\displaystyle h_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(P_{i}P_{j}-Q_{i}Q_{j})h_{ij}^{\text{TT}},

(5a)

\displaystyle h_{\times}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(P_{i}Q_{j}+Q_{i}P_{j})h_{ij}^{\text{TT}}.

(5b)

We can then decompose the quantity

characterize the direction of propagation

in the coordinate system

and

(7)

where

III
Computation of the radiative multipole moments

In this paper, we use the PN-MPM formalism, see Ref.
Blanchet 2014
for a review. Within this approach, the procedure to derive the radiative multipole moments is well-known. For completeness, we give a summarized version of the method, recalling the different steps: i) we first compute the stress-energy tensor from the effective skeletonized action modeling spinning particles in Sec.
III.1
; ii) in Sec.
III.2
, we decompose the spatial and temporal indices of the stress-energy tensor to compute the source densities; iii) in Sec.
III.4
, we compute the PN metric parametrized by a set of potentials; iv) in Sec.
III.5
, we derive the source multipole moments defined in Sec.
III.3
; v) we then express the source moments in the CM frame for aligned spins and quasi-circular orbits in Sec.
III.6
; vi) finally, we compute the non-linear effects to deduce the radiative multipole moments in Sec.
III.7
.

III.1
Effective skeletonized action of spinning particles

In this section, we start by recalling the method of the effective action approach to the spin-induced effects. We follow here the presentation of
Bohé et al. 2015
;
Marsat 2015
, but note that a more general formalism in the language of effective field theories can be found in Ref.
Levi and Steinhoff 2015a
. Here, we drop the particle’s label
A
and set
c=1
for more clarity.

The matter action is constructed on the worldline of the individual body
z^{\mu}
. The affine parameter along this worldline is called
\tau
and will be associated after variation of the action to the proper time of the body. We define the usual 4-velocity of the particle
u^{\mu}=\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}/\mathrm{d}\tau
such that
u^{\mu}u_{\mu}=-1
. Attaching an orthonormal tetrad
\epsilon_{A}^{\phantom{A}\mu}
to the moving body, we also introduce antisymmetric rotation coefficients
\Omega^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{A\mu}D\epsilon_{A}^{\phantom{A}\nu}/d\tau
representing the rotational degrees of freedom. The matter action then reads symbolically

S_{\text{M}}=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\int\mathrm{d}\tau\,L_{\text{M}}[u^{\mu},\Omega^{\mu\nu},g_{\mu\nu},R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},\nabla_{\lambda}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}]\frac{\delta^{(4)}(x^{\mu}-z^{\mu}(\tau))}{\sqrt{-g}},

(9)

where the matter Lagrangian includes finite-size effects in the form of couplings to the Riemann tensor and its derivative, necessary to represent spin-induced effects up to the cubic order in spin. We also introduce the following quantities

\displaystyle p_{\mu}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\partial L}{\partial u^{\mu}},\qquad\qquad S_{\mu\nu}\equiv 2\frac{\partial L}{\partial\Omega^{\mu\nu}},

\displaystyle J^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle\equiv-6\frac{\partial L}{\partial R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}},\qquad J^{\lambda\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\equiv-12\frac{\partial L}{\partial\nabla_{\lambda}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}},

(10)

that represent the couplings entering the action. The quantities
p^{\mu}
and
S^{\mu\nu}
are the linear momentum and the antisymmetric spin tensor. The moments
J^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
and
J^{\lambda\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
are Dixon-type moments
Dixon 1964
;
Dixon 1973
;
Dixon 1979
representing the quadrupole and octupole, assumed to be purely spin-induced in our case (no other finite-size effects such as tidal effects). The general form of the stress-energy tensor will be expressed in terms of these quantities in the following section.

Varying the action with respect to the worldline and rotational degrees of freedom (together with a scalarity condition for the Lagrangian, see (2.8) in Ref.
Marsat 2015
) yields evolution equations for the 4-momentum as well as the spin tensor:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{D}p_{\mu}}{\mathrm{D}\tau}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}u^{\nu}S^{\rho\sigma}-\frac{1}{6}J^{\lambda\nu\rho\sigma}\nabla_{\mu}R_{\lambda\nu\rho\sigma}-\frac{1}{12}J^{\tau\lambda\nu\rho\sigma}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\tau}R_{\lambda\nu\rho\sigma}+\mathcal{O}(S^{4})\,,

(11a)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{D}S^{\mu\nu}}{\mathrm{D}\tau}

\displaystyle=2p^{[\mu}u^{\nu]}+\frac{4}{3}R^{[\mu}_{\phantom{\mu}\lambda\rho\sigma}J^{\nu]\lambda\rho\sigma}+\frac{2}{3}\nabla^{\lambda}R^{[\mu}_{\phantom{\mu}\tau\rho\sigma}J_{\lambda}^{\phantom{\lambda}\nu]\tau\rho\sigma}+\frac{1}{6}\nabla^{[\mu}R_{\lambda\tau\rho\sigma}J^{\nu]\lambda\tau\rho\sigma}+\mathcal{O}(S^{4})\,.

(11b)

However, the introduction of the spin tensor adds 3 degrees of freedom to the problem. In order to close the system of equations describing the dynamics, we impose a spin supplementary condition (SSC). In particular we choose to impose the Tulczyjew-Dixon SSC
Tulczyjew 1959
;
Dixon 1979
which reads

S^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}=0\,.

(12)

Note that the mass
p_{\mu}p^{\mu}=-\tilde{m}^{2}
is not conserved at
\mathcal{O}(S^{2})
. Together with the equations of motion, the SSC allows us to find a conserved mass, that we use henceforth in this paper,

m\equiv\tilde{m}-\frac{1}{6}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}J^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,,

(13)

and to relate
p^{\mu}
and the 4-velocity as

\displaystyle p^{\mu}

\displaystyle=mu^{\mu}+\frac{1}{6}u^{\mu}R_{\rho\lambda\mu\nu}J^{\rho\lambda\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2m}S^{\mu\nu}R_{\nu\lambda\rho\sigma}u^{\lambda}S^{\rho\sigma}+\frac{4}{3}R^{[\mu}_{\phantom{\mu}\lambda\rho\sigma}J^{\nu]\lambda\rho\sigma}u_{\nu}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{3}u_{\nu}\nabla^{\lambda}R^{[\mu}_{\phantom{[\nu}\tau\rho\sigma}J_{\lambda}^{\phantom{\lambda}\nu]\tau\rho\sigma}+\frac{1}{6}u_{\nu}\nabla^{[\mu}R_{\lambda\tau\rho\sigma}J^{\nu]\lambda\tau\rho\sigma}-\frac{1}{6m}S^{\mu\nu}J^{\lambda\tau\rho\sigma}\nabla_{\nu}R_{\lambda\tau\rho\sigma}+\mathcal{O}(S^{4})\,.

(14)

The norm of the spin tensor

s^{2}\equiv\frac{1}{2}S_{\mu\nu}S^{\mu\nu},

(15)

is conserved at the spin-cubic order that we consider:
\mathrm{d}s/\mathrm{d}\tau=0
.

Imposing that the quadrupole and octupole moments are purely spin-induced with the appropriate symmetries, we find

\displaystyle J^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{3\kappa}{m}u^{[\mu}S^{\nu]\lambda}S_{\lambda}^{\phantom{\lambda}[\rho}u^{\sigma]}\,,

(16a)

\displaystyle J^{\lambda\mu\nu\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{4m^{2}}\left[\Theta^{\lambda[\mu}u^{\nu]}S^{\rho\sigma}+\Theta^{\lambda[\rho}u^{\sigma]}S^{\mu\nu}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\;-\Theta^{\lambda[\mu}S^{\nu][\rho}u^{\sigma]}-\Theta^{\lambda[\rho}S^{\sigma][\mu}u^{\nu]}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\;\left.-S^{\lambda[\mu}\Theta^{\nu][\rho}u^{\sigma]}-S^{\lambda[\rho}\Theta^{\sigma][\mu}u^{\nu]}\right]\,,

(16b)

where we used the notation
\Theta^{\mu\nu}=S^{\mu\lambda}S^{\nu}_{\phantom{\nu}\lambda}
, with quadrupolar and octupolar parameters
\kappa
,
\lambda
normalized to unity for a Kerr black hole.

With the SSC (
12
), one can construct a spin vector
S^{\mu}
from the spin tensor
S^{\mu\nu}
, that also has a conserved norm by construction. They are linked using the orthogonal unit vector
p^{\mu}/\tilde{m}
(the mass correction (
13
) is irrelevant at our PN order in spin terms) through the relations

\displaystyle S^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\frac{p_{\rho}}{\tilde{m}}S_{\sigma}\,,

(17a)

\displaystyle S^{\mu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\frac{p_{\nu}}{\tilde{m}}S_{\rho\sigma}\,,

(17b)

where
\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
is the Levi-Civita tensor. One can further construct a 3-dimensional spin vector with conserved Euclidean norm
\bm{S}
(see Section II. C in Ref.
Bohé et al. 2015
), which we use in our final results.

For the two-body problem, the equations of motion (EOM) of the individual bodies as well as the precession evolution of the spins have been derived in harmonic coordinates in Refs.
Bohé et al. 2013a
;
Bohé et al. 2015
;
Marsat 2015

(31)

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle=-\sin\phi(t)\,\bm{e}_{X}+\cos\phi(t)\,\bm{e}_{Y}\,,

\displaystyle=\bm{e}_{Z}\,,

(32c)

III.7
Non-linear contributions to the radiative moments

Once the source multipoles are known in the CM, we follow rigorously the procedure in Ref.
Blanchet et al. 2008
to compute the radiative moments. Without precession, there are no additional technicalities and the problem is equivalent to point-particle.

To link the radiative to the source moments, one has to introduce the canonical moments
(M_{L},S_{L})
which are related to the set of source and gauge moments
(I_{L},J_{L},W_{L},X_{L},Y_{L},Z_{L})
(see
Blanchet et al. 2008
for an account of the procedure). The computation of the canonical moments is detailed in Sec.
III.7.1
. Once the canonical moments are known, we can deduce the radiative moments through the following relations

\displaystyle U_{L}

\displaystyle=M_{L}^{(\ell)}+(\text{non-linear terms}),

(35a)

\displaystyle V_{L}

\displaystyle=S_{L}^{(\ell)}+(\text{non-linear terms}),

(35b)

where the non-linear terms are at least of order
\mathcal{O}(3)
, so for radiative multipoles that are required at a low order, their contribution vanish. They are composed of three different types of contributions: the instantaneous terms, the tail terms and the memory terms. The computation of these different terms are detailed below.

III.7.1
Link between source and canonical moments

The relations between the canonical and source moments take the form

\displaystyle M_{L}

\displaystyle=I_{L}+\delta I_{L}\,,

(36a)

\displaystyle S_{L}

\displaystyle=J_{L}+\delta J_{L}\,,

(36b)

where
\delta I_{L}
and
\delta J_{L}
are non-linear corrections made of products of source and gauge moments, and starting at the 2.5PN order. The full expressions of
\delta I_{L}
and
\delta J_{L}
up to the 3.5PN order are displayed in Sec. III. B. of Ref.
Faye et al. 2015
. Recently, the expression for
\delta I_{ij}
has been derived up to the 4PN order
Blanchet et al. 2022
. For the spin contributions, we have explicitly

\displaystyle\left[\delta I_{ij}\right]_{S}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(8)\,,

(37a)

\displaystyle\left[\delta I_{ijk}\right]_{S}

\displaystyle={12G\over c^{5}}\left[I_{\langle ij}Y_{k\rangle}^{(1)}\right]_{S}+\mathcal{O}(8)\,,

(37b)

\displaystyle\left[\delta J_{ij}\right]_{S}

\displaystyle={2G\over c^{5}}\left[\epsilon_{ab\langle i}\left(-I^{(3)}_{j\rangle b}W_{a}-2I_{j\rangle b}Y_{a}^{(2)}+I_{j\rangle b}^{(1)}Y_{a}^{(1)}\right)+3J_{\langle i}W_{j\rangle}^{(1)}\right]_{S}+\mathcal{O}(8)\,,

(37c)

where the spin parts of
W_{i}
,
Y_{i}
and
J_{i}
for aligned spins are given by
[J_{i}]_{S}=S_{\ell}\ell^{i}/c

[W_{i}]_{S}=\nu r\Sigma_{\ell}\lambda^{i}/4c
and
[Y_{i}]_{S}=\nu r\omega\Sigma_{\ell}n^{i}/4c

. After obtaining their expression, one has to perform a time differentiation to obtain the linear part of the radiative moments.

III.7.3
Tail terms

The tails correspond to time integrals over the past of the source. Their expressions are known for each
\ell
. For the spin effects at the order considered, we only need to compute the following ones

\displaystyle\left[U_{ij}^{\rm tail}\right]_{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{2G\mathcal{M}}{c^{3}}\int^{+\infty}_{0}\mathrm{d}\tau\left[\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2b}\right)+\frac{11}{12}\right]\left[M^{(4)}_{ij}(T_{R}-\tau)\right]_{S}\,,

(39a)

\displaystyle\left[U_{ijk}^{\rm tail}\right]_{S}

\displaystyle={2G\mathcal{M}\over c^{3}}\int^{+\infty}_{0}\mathrm{d}\tau\left[\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2b}\right)+{97\over 60}\right]\left[M^{(5)}_{ijk}(T_{R}-\tau)\right]_{S}\,,

(39b)

\displaystyle\left[V_{ij}^{\rm tail}\right]_{S}

\displaystyle={2G\mathcal{M}\over c^{3}}\int^{+\infty}_{0}\mathrm{d}\tau\left[\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2b}\right)+{7\over 6}\right]\left[S^{(4)}_{ij}(T_{R}-\tau)\right]_{S}\,,

(39c)

\displaystyle\left[V_{ijk}^{\rm tail}\right]_{S}

\displaystyle={2G\mathcal{M}\over c^{3}}\int^{+\infty}_{0}\mathrm{d}\tau\left[\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2b}\right)+{5\over 3}\right]\left[S^{(5)}_{ijk}(T_{R}-\tau)\right]_{S}\,,

(39d)

\displaystyle\left[V_{ijkl}^{\rm tail}\right]_{S}

\displaystyle={2G\mathcal{M}\over c^{3}}\int^{+\infty}_{0}\mathrm{d}\tau\left[\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{2b}\right)+{119\over 60}\right]\left[S^{(6)}_{ijkl}(T_{R}-\tau)\right]_{S}\,,

(39e)

where we introduced an arbitrary time-scale constant

. The mass monopole
\mathcal{M}
, or ADM mass, differs from the total constant mass

through the relation
\mathcal{M}=M+\bar{E}/c^{2}
where
\bar{E}
is the conservative binding energy of the system. This implies that the spin contributions in
\mathcal{M}
start at
\mathcal{O}(5)
and thus do not need to be taken into account in the computation of the tail terms as they are of higher order.

To compute the tail integrals, we consider the aligned-spin case. There are no precession effects to consider here and the evolution of the dynamics of the binary is qualitatively the same as for the usual quasi-circular orbits, with the aligned conserved norm spins acting simply as constant vectors. This is to be contrasted with the more general case of binaries on quasi-circular but precessing orbits (as defined for instance in Ref.
Arun et al. 2009
), where one must solve analytically the dynamics consistently with the order at which the analysis is carried, to be able to compute these integrals. The idea to compute these integrals in the non-precessing case is to project the moments in the spatial basis
(\bm{e}_{X},\bm{e}_{Y},\bm{e}_{Z})
defined in Sec.
II.1
using the relations (
32
) and assuming that the separation
r
is constant over time
3
3

3

It has been shown
Blanchet and Schäfer 1993
;
Arun et al. 2008
that for the tail integrals, such an approximation is valid because the remote past of the source is negligible when compared to its recent past. On this time scale,
r
does not have the time to vary significantly and can be assumed constant.
. By doing so, we find one-dimensional integrals of the type
\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\ln(y)e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha y}
that are computable analytically
Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 1980
.

III.7.4
Memory terms

The memory terms, as well as the tail terms, are called hereditary effects in the sense that they are integrals over the past of the source. They are integrals of quadratic interactions of canonical moments and only appear in the mass-type multipoles
Favata 2009
. In our case, only the mass octupole moment contains a spin contribution to the memory terms according to

\left[U_{ijk}^{\rm mem}\right]_{S}=-{4G\over 5c^{5}}\int^{+\infty}_{0}\mathrm{d}\tau\left[\epsilon_{ab\langle i}\mathrm{M}^{(3)}_{j\underline{a}}(T_{R}-\tau)\mathrm{S}^{(3)}_{k\rangle b}(T_{R}-\tau)\right]_{S}+\mathcal{O}(7)\,.

(40)

For the mass quadrupole, the spin contribution to the memory effects are of order
\mathcal{O}(8)
and thus do not need to be considered.

The computation of the memory terms differ from the one of the tails because one cannot assume that the separation
r
is constant. We have to take into account the radiation reaction. As for the tails, we project the values of the moments on the basis
(\bm{e}_{X},\bm{e}_{Y},\bm{e}_{Z})
. By doing so, we encounter integrals of the type

\int_{-\infty}^{T_{R}}\mathrm{d}\tau\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}n\phi(\tau)}}{r^{p}(\tau)},

(41)

where
n
is a non-zero integer,
p
is a half-integer, and
\phi
is the phase variable defined in Sec.
III.6
, which satisfies
\dot{\phi}=\omega
. As we can see, if we assumed the separation to be constant over time, this integral would diverge. At leading order, the separation scales as
r(\tau)\sim(-\tau)^{1/4}
and the phase as
\phi(\tau)\sim(-\tau)^{5/8}
, which allows to compute this integral, as detailed in Refs.
Arun et al. 2008
;
Blanchet et al. 2008
.

One can also encounter integrals of this type with
n=0
, which can be computed analytically as well. However, the main difference from the case
n\neq 0
is that they induce a factor
c^{5}
. This means that their contribution is of order 2.5PN lower than the initial order of the memory interaction. Thus, when using the PN-MPM formalism, we cannot be consistent to the 3.5PN order for these terms since it requires the knowledge of the general memory interactions at higher orders. Fortunately, these integrals only contribute to the modes for
m=0
, which we do not derive in this paper. More details about these peculiar modes are given in Sec.
IV.1
.

V
Summary

We computed the spin contributions to the spherical-harmonics modes of the GW polarizations to the 3.5PN order, for non-precessing spins in quasi-circular orbits.
We used the PN-MPM formalism to tackle the computation of the radiative multipole moments, which were required to a higher multipolar order than what was known in the literature.
We also derived the spin contributions to the hereditary tail terms, as well as other non-linear interactions between the moments.

Our results include all spin terms, i.e., all SO, SS and SSS terms to that order. We wrote the waveform modes in two forms: in the conventional PN-expanded form, as well as a factorized form convenient for the EOB approach.
The factorized modes we obtained are in agreement with Refs.
Pan et al. 2011a
;
Cotesta et al. 2018
except for the three terms
\mathcal{O}(v^{6}\chi^{2}\nu^{2})
in the (2,1) mode used in
SEOBNRv4HM
. As stated in Ref.
Cotesta et al. 2018
, these terms came from private communications and are now corrected. We also corrected a NS term in the quantity
\delta_{21}
.

The results derived in this paper can be useful in improving analytical waveform models. Interestingly, preliminary implementation of some of our results in an EOB waveform model showed a good improvement when compared to numerical relativity
Pri
.

Future work will focus on relaxing the non-precessing approximation as well as the quasi-circular-orbits condition, which is important to improve waveform models for eccentric orbits and precessing spins.

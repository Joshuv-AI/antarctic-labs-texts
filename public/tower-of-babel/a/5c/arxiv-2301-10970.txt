On the choice of weights in aggregate compositional data analysis
Choulakian V, Université de Moncton, Canada, De Tibeiro J, Université de Moncton, Canada, Sarnacchiaro P, The University of Naples Federico II, Italy

Abstract
Résumé In this paper, we distinguish between two kinds of compositional data sets : elementary and aggregate. This fact will help us to decide the choice of the weights to use in log interaction analysis of aggregate compositional vectors. We show that in the aggregate case, the underlying given data form a paired data sets composed of responses and qualitative covariates ; this fact helps us to propose two approaches for analysis-visualization of data named log interaction of aggregates and aggregate of log interactions. Furthermore, we also show the first-order approximation of log interaction of a cell for different choices of the row and column weights. Key words : elementary and aggregate compositional vectors ; log interaction of aggregates ; aggregate of log interactions ; taxicab singular value decomposition ; row and column weights. AMS 2010 subject classifications : 62H25, 62H30

Introduction

We start by comparing the structure of two compositional data sets, accessible
on the internet, which have been analyzed-visualized by centered clr
transformation, that we name log interaction approach.

First, the archeological CUPS compositional data set
\mathbf{X}=(x_{ij}),
of

Second, the FOOD compositional data set

, for

considered by Choulakian et al. (2023).

The CUPS and FOOD data sets are compositional in nature, but of different
structure : this is the main topic of this paper that we want to highlight and
discuss. Additionally, our discussion will lead further on to the choice of
the weights used in an analysis.

k

The choice of weights

Benzécri (1973, Vol.1, p. 31-32) emphasized the importance of the row and
column weights or metrics in multidimensional data analysis. Choulakian et al.
(2023) present a review of literature concerning the choice of weights in log
interaction analysis. We have three cases :

a) Consider the elementary compositional data set
\mathbf{X}=(x_{ij})
.
Aitchison (1983,1986,1994,1997) used the uniform weights
w_{i}^{I}=1/I
,

w_{j}^{J}=1/J
; and this has been followed up. Its first-order approximation
is, by Lemma 1b,

\lambda_{ij}^{IJ}\approx\widetilde{\lambda}_{ij}^{IJ}=(IJp_{ij}^{IJ}-Ip_{i+}^{IJ}-Jp_{+j}^{IJ}+1).

b) Consider the aggregate compositional data set
\mathbf{T}=(t_{kj})
. We
suggest the use of the following weights
w_{k}^{K}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}z_{ik}/\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{k=1}^{K}z_{ik}

=z_{+k}/z_{++}
and
w_{j}^{J}=1/J,

essentially for the following two reasons. First, by looking at (1), we see
that the sample size of each category
k

taken into account. For example, the number of males observed is
117,
while
the number of females observed is
49

\mathbf{A}=(a_{kj})=\mathbf{Z}^{{}^{\prime}}\Lambda^{IJ}/I;
note that here
\mathbf{D}_{I}=(1/I=1/166=z_{i+}/z_{++}=Q/(QI)

because the effect of the sample

interactions in the category

Conclusion

We conclude by summarizing the topics discussed in this paper.

First, we emphasize the fact that the inclusion of the row and column weights
in logarithmic approach of analyzing-visualizing a compositional data set
renders it quite versatile.

Second, we distinguished between elementary (
X
) and aggregate
compositional data sets (
T) ; T
is built from the paired data sets
(
X
,
Z
), where
X
is the response data set and

Z
the indicator matrix with elements (0,1) of
Q
qualitative covariates.

Third, we proposed to analyze-visualize the paired data sets (
X
,

Z
) by two approaches named, aggregate compositional data (
T
)
and aggregate log interactions (
A
).

Fourth, we presented the first-order approximation to the log interaction of a
cell under different weighting schemes ; and we think these can be used in case
the original data contain zero cells. This needs further study.

Appendix 1: An overview of taxicab singular value decomposition

Consider a matrix
\mathbf{X}
of size
I\times J
and
rank(\mathbf{X})=k
.
Taxicab singular value decomposition (TSVD) of
X
is a
decomposition similar to SVD of
X
, see Choulakian (2006, 2016).

For a vector
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{(}u_{i}),
its taxicab or L
1
norm is

\left|\left|\mathbf{u}\right|\right|_{1}=\sum_{i}|u_{i}|,
the Euclidean or L
2
norm is
\left|\left|\mathbf{u}\right|\right|_{2}=(\sum_{i}|u_{i}|^{2})^{1/2}
and the

∞

\left|\left|\mathbf{u}\right|\right|_{\infty}=\max_{i}\ |u_{i}|
.

In TSVD the calculation of the dispersion measures
(\delta_{\alpha})
,
principal axes (
\mathbf{u}_{\alpha},\mathbf{v}_{\alpha})
and principal
scores
(\mathbf{a}_{\alpha},\mathbf{b}_{\alpha})
for
\alpha=1,...,k
is
done in a stepwise manner. We put
\mathbf{X}_{1}=\mathbf{X}=(x_{ij})
and

\mathbf{X_{\alpha}}
be the residual matrix at the
\alpha
-th iteration for

\alpha=1,...,k
.

The variational definitions of the TSVD at the
\alpha
-th iteration are

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathbf{u\in\mathbb{R}}^{J}}\frac{\left|\left|\mathbf{X_{\alpha}u}\right|\right|_{1}}{\left|\left|\mathbf{u}\right|\right|_{\infty}}=\max_{\mathbf{v\in\mathbb{R}}^{I}}\ \frac{\left|\left|\mathbf{X_{\alpha}^{\prime}v}\right|\right|_{1}}{\left|\left|\mathbf{v}\right|\right|_{\infty}}=\max_{\mathbf{u\in\mathbb{R}}^{J},\mathbf{v\in\mathbb{R}}^{I}}\frac{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\mathbf{X_{\alpha}u}}{\left|\left|\mathbf{u}\right|\right|_{\infty}\left|\left|\mathbf{v}\right|\right|_{\infty}},

(A1)

\displaystyle=\max\ ||\mathbf{X_{\alpha}u||}_{1}\ \ \text{subject to }\mathbf{u}\in\left\{-1,+1\right\}^{J},

\displaystyle=\max\ ||\mathbf{X_{\alpha}^{\prime}v||}_{1}\ \ \text{subject to
}\mathbf{v}\in\left\{-1,+1\right\}^{I},

\displaystyle=\max\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\mathbf{X_{\alpha}u}\text{ \ subject to
\ }\mathbf{u}\in\left\{-1,+1\right\}^{J},\mathbf{v}\in\left\{-1,+1\right\}^{I}.

The
\alpha
-th principal axes are

\mathbf{u}_{\alpha}\ =\arg\max_{\mathbf{u}\in\left\{-1,+1\right\}^{J}}\left|\left|\mathbf{X_{\alpha}u}\right|\right|_{1}\text{
\ \ and \ \ }\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}\ =\arg\max_{\mathbf{v}\in\left\{-1,+1\right\}^{I}}\left|\left|\mathbf{X_{\alpha}v}\right|\right|_{1}\text{,}

(A2)

and the
\alpha
-th principal projections of the rows and the columns are

\mathbf{a}_{\alpha}=\mathbf{X_{\alpha}u}_{\alpha}\text{ \ and \ }\mathbf{b}_{\alpha}=\mathbf{X_{\alpha}^{\prime}v}_{\alpha}.

(A3)

Furthermore, the following relations are also useful

\mathbf{u}_{\alpha}=sign(\mathbf{b}_{\alpha})\text{ \ and \ }\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}=sign(\mathbf{a}_{\alpha}),

(A4)

where
sign(.)
is the coordinatewise sign function,
sign(x)=1
if
x>0,

and
sign(x)=-1
if
x\leq 0.

The
\alpha
-th taxicab dispersion measure
\delta_{\alpha}
can be
represented in many different ways

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}\

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\mathbf{X_{\alpha}u}_{\alpha}\right|\right|_{1}=\left|\left|\mathbf{a}_{\alpha}\right|\right|_{1}=\mathbf{a}_{\alpha}^{\prime}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha},

(A5)

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\mathbf{X_{\alpha}^{\prime}v}_{\alpha}\right|\right|_{1}=\left|\left|\mathbf{b}_{\alpha}\right|\right|_{1}=\mathbf{b}_{\alpha}^{\prime}\mathbf{u}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}{}^{\prime}\mathbf{X_{\alpha}u}_{\alpha}=sign(\mathbf{a}_{\alpha})^{\prime}\mathbf{X_{\alpha}}sign(\mathbf{b}_{\alpha}).

The
(\alpha+1)
-th residual matrix is

\mathbf{X_{\alpha+1}}=\mathbf{X_{\alpha}-a}_{\alpha}\mathbf{b}_{\alpha}^{\prime}/\delta_{\alpha}.

(A6)

An interpretation of the term
\mathbf{a}_{\alpha}\mathbf{b}_{\alpha}^{\prime}/\delta_{\alpha}

approximation of the residual matrix
\mathbf{X_{\alpha}}
, in the sense of
the taxicab matrix norm (A1).

Thus TSVD of
\mathbf{X}
corresponds to the bilinear decomposition

x_{ij}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k}a_{\alpha}(i)b_{\alpha}(j)/\delta_{\alpha},

(A7)

a decomposition similar to SVD, but where the vectors

for
\alpha=1,...,k

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{\alpha}^{\prime}\mathbf{v}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{a}_{\alpha}^{\prime}sign(\mathbf{a}_{\beta})

(A8)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{b}_{\alpha}^{\prime}\mathbf{u}_{\beta}=\mathbf{b}_{\alpha}^{\prime}sign(\mathbf{b}_{\beta})

\displaystyle=0\text{ for }\alpha\geq\beta+1.

Appendix 3: QSR index

Let

QSR_{\alpha}=\frac{\delta_{\alpha}}{\sum_{(i,j)}|X_{\alpha}(i,j)|},

which we will interpret as a new intrinsic measure of quality of the signs of
the residuals in the residual matrix
\mathbf{X}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha=1,...,k
.

Let
S\cup\overline{S}=I
be the optimal principal axis partition of
I
, and
similarly
T\cup\overline{T}=J
be the optimal principal axis partition of

J,
such that
S=\left\{i:a_{\alpha}(i)>0\right\}=

\left\{i:v_{\alpha}(i)>0\right\}
and
T=\left\{j:b_{\alpha}(j)>0\right\}=\left\{j:u_{\alpha}(j)>0\right\}
by (A4). Thus the data set is divided
into 4 quadrants. Based on the equations (A15), we define a new index
quantifying the quality of the signs of the residuals in each quadrant of the

\alpha
th residual matrix
\mathbf{X}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha=1,...,k
to be

\displaystyle QSR_{\alpha}(E,F)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{(i,j)\in E\times F}v_{\alpha}(i)X_{\alpha}(i,j)u_{\alpha}(j)}{\sum_{(i,j)\in E\times F}|X_{\alpha}(i,j)|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{\alpha}/4}{\sum_{(i,j)\in E\times F}|X_{\alpha}(i,j)|}\text{
for }(E,F)=(\overline{S},\overline{T})\text{ or }(S,T)

\displaystyle=\frac{-\delta_{\alpha}/4}{\sum_{(i,j)\in E\times F}|X_{\alpha}(i,j)|}\text{ for }(E,F)=(S,\overline{T})\text{ or }(\overline{S},T)

Sometimes we express it also in %.

We have the following easily proved

Lemma
: a)
-1\leq QSR_{\alpha}(E,F)\leq 1;
furthermore,

QSR_{\alpha}=1
if and only if
QSR_{\alpha}(S,T)=QSR_{\alpha}(\overline{S},\overline{T})=-QSR_{\alpha}(S,\overline{T})=-QSR_{\alpha}(\overline{S},T)=1,
for
\alpha=1,...,k-1.

b) For
\alpha=k,

QSR_{\alpha}=1.\vskip 12.0pt plus 4.0pt minus 4.0pt

The interpretation of
QSR_{\alpha}(E,F)=\pm 1
is that in the quadrant

E\times F
the residuals have one sign ; and this is a signal for very
influential cells or columns or rows ; for an example see Choulakian
(2021).

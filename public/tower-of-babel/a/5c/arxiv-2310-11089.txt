Abstract
. We consider normal almost contact structures on a Riemannian manifold and, through their associated sections of an ad-hoc twistor bundle, study their harmonicity, as sections or as maps. We rewrite these harmonicity equations in terms of the curvature tensor and find conditions relating the harmonicity of the almost contact metric and almost complex structures of the total and base spaces of the Morimoto fibration. We apply these results to homogeneous principal circle bundles over generalised flag manifolds, in particular Aloff-Wallach spaces, to mass-produce harmonic almost contact metric structures.

1.
Introduction

Though there can be a wealth of almost contact structures on an odd-dimensional manifold, few tools exist to select a better one within this multitude.

In presence of a Riemannian metric, compatible almost contact structures can be interpreted as reductions of the orthonormal frame bundle and, as such, are in one-one correspondence with sections of an ad-hoc twistor bundle. Fitting this homogeneous fibre bundle with a Sasaki-like metric allows one to unfold the programme of harmonic map theory: construct a functional, characterize critical points, study the associated flow, determine stable maps. The objective is to compare almost contact structures among themselves, so critical points must be understood with respect to vertical variations.

The first steps were achieved in
[
VW1
]
and an almost contact metric structure (acms)
(\theta,\xi,\eta)
on a Riemannian manifold
(M^{2n+1},g)
is harmonic if (we refer to Section 2 for notations and precise definitions):

(HSE1)

[\bar{\nabla}^{*}\bar{\nabla}\bar{J},\bar{J}]=0,

and

(HSE2)

\nabla^{*}\nabla\xi=|\nabla\xi|^{2}\xi-(1/2)\bar{J}\circ\trace(\bar{\nabla}\bar{J}\otimes\nabla\xi),

where
\bar{J}
(resp.
\bar{\nabla}
) is the restriction of
\theta
to the horizontal distribution
\xi^{\perp}
(resp. the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
) and
\nabla^{*}\nabla=-\trace\nabla^{2}
.

A supplementary condition appears when further requiring the section to be a critical point of the energy for all possible variations, i.e. a harmonic map:

(HME)

\sum_{i=1}^{2n+1}\Bigl(g(R(E_{i},X),\bar{J}(\overline{\nabla}_{E_{i}}\bar{J}))+4g(R(E_{i},X)\xi,\nabla_{E_{i}}\xi)\Bigr)=0,

for any tangent vector field
X
and an orthonormal frame
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,2n+1}
.

Here a comparison should be drawn with its even-dimensional counterpart, as the harmonicity equation for almost Hermitian structures can be read as the limiting case of a Cartesian product with a circle and a parallel Reeb vector field. Only (
HSE1
) remains and harmonic almost Hermitian structures are described by the commutation of
J
and
\nabla^{*}\nabla J

[
Woo
]
. A similar remark applies for the harmonic map condition (
HME
).

Having the harmonic section equations (
HSE1
) and (
HSE2
), the next task is to determine classes of acms which are harmonic or, failing that, conditions so they become harmonic. The first part was done in
[
VW1
,
VW2
]
and
[
LV
]
, and this article investigates the particular case of normal almost contact structures (nacs), that is acms whose cone construction is a Hermitian manifold.

After rewriting, in Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
of Section
3
, the harmonic section equations for nacs in terms of the curvature tensor, echoing some results in
[
Woo
]
, we apply, in Section
4
these properties to the Morimoto fibration to obtain a condition linking the harmonicity of the normal almost contact structures on the total space of this circle bundle and that of the Hermitian structure on the base. It involves the rough Laplacian of the Reeb vector field, the divergence of the tensor
\theta
and lives on the horizontal distribution.

This relationship then leads in Section
6
to an
extensive study of harmonic nacs on homogeneous principal circle
bundles
{\widetilde{M}}
over a generalised flag manifold
M
, see
Theorems
6.15
and
6.16
. Specifically, to each
Hermitian metric on
M
one associates a (canonical) normal acms on

{\widetilde{M}}

automatic and the acms is harmonic, as a section or map, if and
only if so is the Hermitian structure on
M
. The construction of
this acms relies on the classical characterisation of invariant
Hermitian metrics on
M

convenience of the reader, we recall in Section

The article closes with an explicit description of harmonic (map) normal almost contact
metric structures on Aloff-Wallach spaces
{\widetilde{M}}_{k,\ell}
for all pairs of integers
(k,l)\neq(0,0)
.

Note that we adopt the Einstein convention on the summation of repeated
indices and the following sign for the curvature
tensor:
R(X,Y)=[\nabla_{X},\nabla_{Y}]-\nabla_{[X,Y]}
.

2.
Normal almost contact metric structures

An
almost contact structure
on an odd-dimensional
differentiable manifold
M^{2n+1}
is a reduction of the
structure group of its tangent bundle to
\operatorname{U}(n)\times 1
. More
concretely, this is equivalent to the existence of a field of
endomorphisms
\theta
of the tangent space, a vector field
\xi

and a one-form
\eta
related by

\eta(\xi)=1,\quad\theta^{2}=-\mathrm{Id}+\eta\otimes\xi.

Then, necessarily
\theta\xi=0
and
\eta\circ\theta=0
.
A Riemannian metric
g
on
M^{2n+1}
is
compatible
if it satisfies

g(\theta X,\theta Y)=g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y),

for all vector fields
X
and
Y
tangent to
M^{2n+1}
, and such a
metric can always be constructed from the data

(\theta,\xi,\eta)
. We will only consider almost contact
structures compatible with the Riemannian metric. This approach
is initially due to Gray
[
Gra
]
and a good treatment can be
found in
[
Bla
]
.

One can also lift the almost contact structure to the Cartesian
line-product
\tilde{M}=M^{2n+1}\times\mathbb{R}
and construct an
almost complex structure
\tilde{J}
by

\tilde{J}(X+f\partial_{t})=\theta X-f\xi+\eta(X)\partial_{t},

for
X\in{\mathcal{X}}(M)
and
\partial_{t}
the canonical unit vector field
tangent to
\mathbb{R}
. When
\tilde{J}
is integrable, the almost contact
structure will be called
normal
, which can be characterized
by the equation

N_{\theta}+2\textrm{d}\eta\otimes\xi=0,

where
N_{\theta}
is the Nijenhuis tensor

N_{\theta}(X,Y)=\theta^{2}[X,Y]+[\theta X,\theta Y]-\theta[\theta X,Y]-\theta[X,\theta Y].

An immediate effect of this condition is that the vector field

\xi
, while not necessarily Killing, must be with geodesic integral
lines and preserve the field of endomorphisms
\theta
,
i.e.
\nabla_{\xi}\theta=0
(cf.
[
Bla
]
).

To prepare the computations of the next section, we derive an
alternative characterization of normal almost contact metric
structures. Proposition
2.1
is quite probably a well-known result but, with no reference available, a proof is included for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(M^{2n+1},g)
be a Riemannian manifold equipped with an
almost contact metric structure
(\theta,\xi,\eta)
. Then this
structure is normal if and only if

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\theta)(Y)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{\theta X}\theta)(\theta Y)-\eta(Y)\nabla_{\theta X}\xi,

for any vector fields
X
and
Y
on
M^{2n+1}
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\nabla}
denote the covariant derivative of

\tilde{M}
and
\nabla
the covariant derivative of
M
, on
the factor
\mathbb{R}
derivation will be noted by
D
and

\partial_{t}
will be the unit vector field. Given two vector fields

X
and
Y
in
T_{x}M
and functions
F
and
G
of
t
, we
consider the vector fields
(X+F\partial_{t})
and
(Y+G\partial_{t})

tangent to
\tilde{M}=M\times\mathbb{R}
and re-write the
integrability condition of
\tilde{J}

(cf.
[
BW
, Prop. 7.1.3]
)

(2.1)

(\tilde{\nabla}_{\tilde{J}(X+F\partial_{t})}\tilde{J})(\tilde{J}(Y+G\partial_{t}))=(\tilde{\nabla}_{(X+F\partial_{t})}\tilde{J})(Y+G\partial_{t}).

For the sake of simplicity, we will evaluate all expressions at a
given point
x\in M
and assume that, around this point, we
locally extended the vector
Y\in T_{x}M
such that
\nabla Y(x)=0
.

On the one hand

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{\tilde{J}(X,F\partial_{t})}\tilde{J}\right)\left(\tilde{J}(Y+G\partial_{t})\right)

\displaystyle=-\tilde{\nabla}_{(\theta X-F\xi+\eta(X)\partial_{t})}(Y+G\partial_{t})-\tilde{J}(\tilde{\nabla}_{(\theta X-F\xi+\eta(X)\partial_{t})}(\theta Y-G\xi+\eta(Y)\partial_{t}))

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{\theta X}Y+F\nabla_{\xi}Y-\theta(\nabla_{\theta X}\theta Y)+G\theta(\nabla_{\theta X}\xi)+F\theta(\nabla_{\xi}\theta Y)-FG\theta(\nabla_{\xi}\xi)

\displaystyle+\left((\theta X)(\eta(Y))-F\xi((\eta(Y))\right)\xi+\left(F\eta(\nabla_{\xi}\theta Y)-\eta((\nabla_{\theta X}\theta Y)\right)\partial_{t},

while, on the other hand, by definition of
\tilde{J}
,

\displaystyle(\tilde{\nabla}_{(X+F\partial_{t})}\tilde{J})(Y+G\partial_{t})

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{X}\theta)(Y)-G\nabla_{X}\xi+g(\nabla_{X}\xi,Y)\partial_{t}.

Equating these two computations by (
2.1
) and using
different values for
F
and
G
, yields, for the
M
-component,

(2.2)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\theta)(Y)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{\theta X}\theta)(\theta Y)-\eta(Y)\nabla_{\theta X}\xi

(2.3)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\xi}Y+\theta(\nabla_{\xi}\theta Y)-\xi(\eta(Y))\xi

(2.4)

\displaystyle-\nabla_{X}\xi

\displaystyle=\theta(\nabla_{\theta X}\xi)

(2.5)

\displaystyle\theta(\nabla_{\xi}\xi)

\displaystyle=0,

and for the
\mathbb{R}
-component

(2.6)

\displaystyle 0=g(\nabla_{\xi}\theta Y,\xi),

(2.7)

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}\xi,Y)=-g(\nabla_{\theta X}\theta Y,\xi).

Choosing the right type of vector fields, it is easily proved that
Equation (
2.2
) implies (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and
(
2.5
), and also Equations (
2.6
) and (
2.7
),
so the normality of an almost contact metric structure is merely
equivalent to (
2.2
).
∎

From the proof of Proposition
2.1
, we easily obtain a
series of equations we will repeatedly use.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
(M^{2n+1},g)
be a Riemannian manifold equipped with a
normal almost contact metric structure
(\theta,\xi,\eta)
,
then we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}\xi,Y)+\eta(\nabla_{\theta X}\theta Y)

\displaystyle=0,

(2.9)

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{\xi}\theta Y,\xi)

\displaystyle=0,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\xi}Y+\theta(\nabla_{\xi}\theta Y)-\xi\bigl(\eta(Y)\bigr)\xi

\displaystyle=0,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\xi+\theta(\nabla_{\theta X}\xi)

\displaystyle=0,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\xi}\xi

\displaystyle=0,

for any vector fields
X
and
Y
on
M^{2n+1}
.

A consequence of Proposition
2.1
is that, on the
complement to the
\xi
-direction, the field of endomorphisms

\theta
behaves like an integrable complex structure.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
(M^{2n+1},g)
be a Riemannian manifold equipped with a
normal almost contact metric structure

(\theta,\xi,\eta)
. Denote by
\mathcal{F}
the contact sub-bundle,
i.e. the distribution orthogonal to
\xi
, by
\bar{J}
the
restriction of
\theta
to
\mathcal{F}
and by
\bar{\nabla}
the
connection induced on
\mathcal{F}
by
\nabla
. Then

(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}X}\bar{J})(\bar{J}Y)=(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\bar{J})(Y),

for any
X
and
Y
in
\mathcal{F}
.

3.
Curvature equations of harmonicity

In the more favourable cases, the two equations characterizing
the harmonicity of an almost contact metric structure can be expressed
in terms of the curvature tensor of
(M,g)
. This is what we
establish for normal almost contact metric structures in this section,
starting with a preliminary series of technical lemmas on a
Riemannian manifold
(M^{2n+1},g)
equipped with a normal almost
contact metric structure
(\theta,\xi,\eta)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
E
be a horizontal vector field, that is a section of the
horizontal sub-bundle
\mathcal{F}
, such that, at some point

x\in M^{2n+1}
,
(\bar{\nabla}E)(x)=0
. Then, at
x
,

[E,\bar{J}E]=(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(E)+2g(E,\nabla_{\theta E}\xi)\xi.

Proof.

If
E
is a horizontal vector field then so is
\bar{J}E
and, evaluating at
x
,

\displaystyle[E,\bar{J}E]

\displaystyle=\bar{\nabla}_{E}(\bar{J}E)+g(\nabla_{E}(\bar{J}E),\xi)\xi-\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}E-g(\nabla_{\bar{J}E}E,\xi)\xi

\displaystyle=(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(E)+g(\nabla_{E}{\theta E}-\nabla_{\theta E}E,\xi)\xi

\displaystyle=(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(E)+g((\nabla_{E}\theta)(E)-\nabla_{\theta E}E,\xi)\xi.

By Proposition
2.1
,
g((\nabla_{E}\theta)(E),\xi)=g((\nabla_{\theta E}\theta)(\theta E),\xi)=-g(\nabla_{\theta E}E,\xi)
.
Then,

\displaystyle[E,\bar{J}E]

\displaystyle=(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(E)-2g((\nabla_{\theta E}E,\xi)\xi=(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(E)+2g(E,\nabla_{\theta E}\xi)\xi,

since
\xi
is vertical.
∎

To compute the first harmonic section equation, we need to
express the commutator of
\bar{J}
and its Laplacian.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
E
be a section of
\mathcal{F}
, such that, at some point

x\in M^{2n+1}
,
(\bar{\nabla}E)(x)=0
. Then, at
x
,

[\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J},\bar{J}]=-2[\bar{R}(E,\bar{J}E),\bar{J}]-2\bar{\nabla}_{(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(E)}\bar{J}-[\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J},\bar{J}],

where
\bar{R}
is the curvature tensor of the contact sub-bundle

\mathcal{F}
equipped with the connection
\bar{\nabla}
.

Proof.

Let
E
be a section of
\mathcal{F}
and
X
a horizontal vector
extended locally such that
\bar{\nabla}X=0
at the point

x\in M
where we evaluate all expressions. By the Leibniz
rule, we have

(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X)=\bar{\nabla}_{E}\big((\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X)\big)-(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})((\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(X)).

Using Corollary
2.3
and Lemma
3.1
, the
first term on the right-hand side may be expressed in terms of
the curvature tensor
\bar{R}
as follows

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{E}\big((\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X)\big)=-\bar{\nabla}_{E}\big((\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})(X)\big)=-\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}(\bar{J}X)+\bar{\nabla}_{E}(\bar{J}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}X)

\displaystyle=-\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}(\bar{J}X)+\bar{J}(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}X)

\displaystyle=-\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}(\bar{J}X)+\bar{J}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}X-\bar{\nabla}_{[E,\bar{J}E]}(\bar{J}X)+\bar{J}\bar{\nabla}_{[E,\bar{J}E]}X-[\bar{R}(E,\bar{J}E),\bar{J}](X)

\displaystyle=-[\bar{R}(E,\bar{J}E),\bar{J}](X)-\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}(\bar{J}X)+\bar{J}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}X-\bar{\nabla}_{(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(E)}(\bar{J}X)-2g(E,\nabla_{\bar{J}E}\xi)\bar{\nabla}_{\xi}(\bar{J}X)

\displaystyle\phantom{xxx}+\bar{J}(\bar{\nabla}_{(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(E)}X)+2g(E,\nabla_{\bar{J}E}\xi)\bar{J}(\bar{\nabla}_{\xi}X)

\displaystyle=-[\bar{R}(E,\bar{J}E),\bar{J}](X)-\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}(\bar{J}X)+\bar{J}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}X-\big(\bar{\nabla}_{(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(E)}\bar{J}\big)(X),

since
\bar{\nabla}_{\xi}\bar{J}=0
. Now

\displaystyle-\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}(\bar{J}X)+\bar{J}(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}X)

\displaystyle=-\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}((\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(X))-\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}(\bar{J}(\bar{\nabla}_{E}X))+\bar{J}(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}X)

\displaystyle=-\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}((\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(X))=-\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}((\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X))

\displaystyle=-(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X)-(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}(\bar{J}X))

\displaystyle=-(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X)-(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})((\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})(X)),

because of the way we choose to extend the vector
X
.
Therefore

(3.1)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{E}\big((\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X)\big)

\displaystyle=-[\bar{R}(E,\bar{J}E),\bar{J}](X)-\big(\bar{\nabla}_{(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(E)}\bar{J}\big)(X)

\displaystyle-(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X)-(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})\circ(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})(X).

By definition of the covariant derivative of
\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J}

\bar{\nabla}_{E}((\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X))=(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X)+(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})((\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(X)),

hence, using Equation (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X)

\displaystyle=-(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})((\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(X))+\bar{\nabla}_{E}((\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X))

\displaystyle=-(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})\circ(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(X)-[\bar{R}(E,\bar{J}E),\bar{J}](X)-(\bar{\nabla}_{(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(E)}\bar{J})(X)

\displaystyle-(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X)-(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})\circ(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})(X)

(3.2)

\displaystyle=-2(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})\circ(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(X)-[\bar{R}(E,\bar{J}E),\bar{J}](X)-(\bar{\nabla}_{(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(E)}\bar{J})(X)

\displaystyle-(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X),

since, by Corollary
2.3
,

(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})\circ(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})(X)=(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})\circ(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(X)
.
To compute the second term of
[\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J},\bar{J}](X)
we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(\bar{J}^{2}X)

\displaystyle=-2\bar{J}\circ(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})\circ(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(X)-[\bar{R}(E,\bar{J}E),\bar{J}](\bar{J}X)

\displaystyle+\bar{J}(\bar{\nabla}_{(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(E)}\bar{J})(X)+(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})(X),

then

\displaystyle-\bar{J}(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(X)

\displaystyle=2(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})\circ(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(X)-[\bar{R}(E,\bar{J}E),\bar{J}](X)

\displaystyle-(\bar{\nabla}_{(\bar{\nabla}_{E}\bar{J})(E)}\bar{J})(X)+\bar{J}(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}E}\bar{J})(X).

Summing up this last equation with (
3.2
), we obtain the result.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{F_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,2n}
be an orthonormal frame on the
horizontal sub-bundle
\mathcal{F}
, such that, at the point

x\in M^{2n+1}
where we evaluate all our expressions,

(\bar{\nabla}F_{i})(x)=0
. Define the Lee vector field by

\bar{\delta}\bar{J}=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}(\bar{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\bar{J})(F_{i})
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}F_{i}}(\bar{J}F_{i})}\bar{J}=\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}\bar{\delta}\bar{J}}\bar{J}.

Proof.

Combining the Leibniz rule, Corollary
2.3
and

\bar{\nabla}\bar{J}\circ\bar{J}=-\bar{J}\circ\bar{\nabla}\bar{J}
, yields

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}F_{i}}(\bar{J}F_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\bar{J})(F_{i})=-\sum_{i=1}^{2n}(\bar{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\bar{J})(\bar{J}F_{i})=\bar{J}\bar{\delta}\bar{J},

as required.
∎

We can now give the first harmonicity condition for a normal structure.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(M^{2n+1},g)
be a Riemannian manifold equipped with a
normal almost contact metric structure
(\theta,\xi,\eta)
.
The first harmonic section equation (
HSE1
) for this
structure can be written

\sum_{i=1}^{2n}[\bar{R}(F_{i},\bar{J}F_{i}),\bar{J}]=-2\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{\delta}\bar{J}}\bar{J},

where
\{F_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,2n}
is an orthonormal frame on the
horizontal sub-bundle
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Assume, without loss of generality, that the
orthonormal frame
\{F_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,2n}
satisfies

\bar{\nabla}F_{i}(x)=0
, for all
i
, at the point we evaluate
at. First observe that

-\bar{\nabla}^{*}\bar{\nabla}\bar{J}=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}(\bar{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\bar{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\bar{J}-\bar{\nabla}_{\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{i}}\bar{J})+\bar{\nabla}_{\xi}\bar{\nabla}_{\xi}\bar{J}-\bar{\nabla}_{\nabla_{\xi}\xi}\bar{J}=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\bar{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\bar{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\bar{J}

since
\nabla F_{i}
is in the
\xi
-direction,
\bar{\nabla}_{\xi}\bar{J}=0
and
\nabla_{\xi}\xi=0
.
Working now with the orthonormal frame
\{\bar{J}F_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,2n}
,
-\bar{\nabla}^{*}\bar{\nabla}\bar{J}=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\bar{J}-\bar{\nabla}_{\nabla_{\bar{J}F_{i}}(\bar{J}F_{i})}\bar{J}-\bar{\nabla}^{2}_{\xi,\xi}\bar{J}
, thus

-\bar{\nabla}^{*}\bar{\nabla}\bar{J}=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\bar{J}-\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}F_{i}}(\bar{J}F_{i})}\bar{J}-g(\nabla_{\bar{J}F_{i}}(\bar{J}F_{i}),\xi)\bar{\nabla}_{\xi}\bar{J}=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\bar{J}-\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}\bar{\delta}\bar{J}}\bar{J},

by the previous lemma. Then,

-[\bar{\nabla}^{*}\bar{\nabla}\bar{J},\bar{J}](X)=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}[\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\bar{J},\bar{J}](X)-[\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}\bar{\delta}\bar{J}}\bar{J},\bar{J}](X)

\displaystyle-[\bar{\nabla}^{*}\bar{\nabla}\bar{J},\bar{J}](X)=-2\sum_{i=1}^{2n}[\bar{R}(F_{i},\bar{J}F_{i}),\bar{J}](X)-2(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{\delta}\bar{J}}\bar{J})(X)+[\bar{\nabla}^{*}\bar{\nabla}\bar{J},\bar{J}](X)-[\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}\bar{\delta}\bar{J}}\bar{J},\bar{J}](X).

[\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}\bar{\delta}\bar{J}}\bar{J},\bar{J}](X)=(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}\bar{\delta}\bar{J}}\bar{J})(\bar{J}X)-\bar{J}(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{J}\bar{\delta}\bar{J}}\bar{J})(X)=2(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{\delta}\bar{J}}\bar{J})(X)

Therefore

[\bar{\nabla}^{*}\bar{\nabla}\bar{J},\bar{J}](X)=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}[\bar{R}(F_{i},\bar{J}F_{i}),\bar{J}](X)+2(\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{\delta}\bar{J}}\bar{J})(X)

and the theorem follows.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(M^{2n+1},g)
be a Riemannian
manifold equipped with a normal almost contact metric structure

(\theta,\xi,\eta)
. Then the second harmonic section
equation (
HSE2
) for the structure
(\theta,\xi,\eta)
is

\nabla^{*}\nabla\xi-|\nabla\xi|^{2}\xi=(1/2)\sum_{i=1}^{2n}[R(F_{i},\theta F_{i}),\theta](\xi)+(\nabla_{\delta\theta}\theta)(\xi),

where
\{F_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,2n}
is an orthonormal frame on the
horizontal sub-bundle
\mathcal{F}
and
\delta\theta=\trace_{g}\nabla\theta
.

Proof.

Notice that

\delta\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}(\nabla_{F_{i}}\theta)(F_{i})

(since, by Proposition
2.1
,

(\nabla_{\xi}\theta)(\xi)=0
). Equation (
HSE2
) can be
written

(\nabla^{*}\nabla\xi)^{\mathcal{F}}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\bar{J}\circ(\bar{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\bar{J})(\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\theta\circ(\nabla_{F_{i}}\theta)(\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi).

Working with an orthonormal frame
\{F_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,2n}
as in
Theorem
3.4
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2n}(\nabla_{F_{i}}\theta)(\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\nabla_{F_{i}}(\theta(\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi))-\theta(\nabla_{F_{i}}\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\nabla_{F_{i}}\nabla_{\theta F_{i}}\xi+\theta(\nabla^{*}\nabla\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}R(F_{i},\theta F_{i})\xi+\nabla_{\theta F_{i}}\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi+\nabla_{[F_{i},\theta F_{i}]}\xi+\theta(\nabla^{*}\nabla\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}R(F_{i},\theta F_{i})\xi-\nabla_{\theta F_{i}}(\theta\nabla_{\theta F_{i}}\xi)+\nabla_{[F_{i},\theta F_{i}]}\xi+\theta(\nabla^{*}\nabla\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}R(F_{i},\theta F_{i})\xi-(\nabla_{\theta F_{i}}\theta)(\nabla_{\theta F_{i}}\xi)-\theta(\nabla_{\theta F_{i}}\nabla_{\theta F_{i}}\xi)+\nabla_{[F_{i},\theta F_{i}]}\xi+\theta(\nabla^{*}\nabla\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}R(F_{i},\theta F_{i})\xi-(\nabla_{\theta F_{i}}\theta)(\nabla_{\theta F_{i}}\xi)-\theta(\nabla_{\nabla_{\theta F_{i}}(\theta F_{i})}\xi)+\nabla_{[F_{i},\theta F_{i}]}\xi+2\theta(\nabla^{*}\nabla\xi).

Notice that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\theta(\nabla_{\nabla_{\theta F_{i}}(\theta F_{i})}\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\nabla_{\theta(\nabla_{\theta F_{i}}\theta)(F_{i})}\xi=-\nabla_{\delta\theta}\xi,

by Lemma
3.3
, the geodesic integral curves of
\xi

and the general property (since

(\nabla_{X}\theta)(\theta Y)+\theta(\nabla_{X}\theta)(Y)=g(\nabla_{X}\xi,Y)\xi+g(Y,\xi)\nabla_{X}\xi,

for any

and
Y
in
{\mathcal{X}}(M)
.
Similarly, with Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\nabla_{[F_{i},\theta F_{i}]}\xi

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\bar{\delta}\bar{J}+2\sum_{i=1}^{2n}g(F_{i},\nabla_{\theta F_{i}}\xi)\xi}\xi=\nabla_{\bar{\delta}\bar{J}}\xi=\nabla_{\delta\theta}\xi.

Thus

\sum_{i=1}^{2n}(\nabla_{F_{i}}\theta)(\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi)=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2n}R(F_{i},\theta F_{i})\xi+\theta(\nabla^{*}\nabla\xi)+\nabla_{\delta\theta}\xi

and
\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\theta\circ(\nabla_{F_{i}}\theta)(\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi)

Then the second harmonic equation (

Since
-\theta(\nabla_{\delta\theta}\xi)=(\nabla_{\delta\theta}\theta)(\xi)
and

-\theta R(F_{i},\theta F_{i})\xi=[R(F_{i},\theta F_{i}),\theta](\xi)

∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(M^{2n+1},g)
be a Riemannian manifold equipped with a
normal almost contact metric structure
(\theta,\xi,\eta)
. If
the vector field
\xi
is harmonic, as unit section, then the
almost contact metric structure
(\theta,\xi,\eta)
is a harmonic
section if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{2n}[R(F_{i},\theta F_{i}),\theta]=-2\nabla_{\delta\theta}\theta.

Proof.

If
\xi
is harmonic then the second harmonic equation simplifies to

0=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}[R(F_{i},\theta F_{i}),\theta](\xi)+2(\nabla_{\delta\theta}\theta)(\xi).

The link between the
\mathcal{F}
-component of the curvature tensor of

(M^{2n+1},g)
and the curvature of the connection
\bar{\nabla}

was established in
[
Thes
]
:

\bar{R}(X,Y)=R^{\mathcal{F}}(X,Y)+r(\nabla_{X}\xi,\nabla_{Y}\xi),

where
r
denotes the curvature tensor of the unit sphere,
i.e.
r(X,Y)Z=g(Y,Z)X-g(X,Z)Y
.
If one proves that

(3.4)

\sum_{i=1}^{2n}[r(\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi,\nabla_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\xi),\bar{J}]=0,

then the first harmonic section equation could be rewritten as

(3.5)

\sum_{i=1}^{2n}[R(F_{i},\theta F_{i}),\theta]-g([R(F_{i},\theta F_{i}),\theta],\xi)\xi=-2\nabla_{\delta\theta}\theta+2g(\nabla_{\delta\theta}\theta,\xi)\xi,

which, applied to

, gives the first harmonic section equation

\sum_{i=1}^{2n}[R^{\mathcal{F}}(F_{i},\theta F_{i}),\theta](X)=-2(\bar{\nabla}_{\delta\bar{J}}\bar{J})(X),

which is the
\mathcal{F}
-part of

\sum_{i=1}^{2n}[R(F_{i},\theta F_{i}),\theta]=-2\nabla_{\delta\theta}\theta
.
When evaluated on
\xi
, Equation (
3.5
) becomes

\sum_{i=1}^{2n}[R(F_{i},\theta F_{i}),\theta](\xi)-g([R(F_{i},\theta F_{i}),\theta](\xi),\xi)\xi=-2(\nabla_{\delta\theta}\theta)(\xi)+2g((\nabla_{\delta\theta}\theta)(\xi),\xi)\xi

which is the second harmonic section equation

\sum_{i=1}^{2n}[R(F_{i},\theta F_{i}),\theta](\xi)=-2(\nabla_{\delta\theta}\theta)(\xi),

i.e. the
\xi

\sum_{i=1}^{2n}[R(F_{i},\theta F_{i}),\theta]=-2\nabla_{\delta\theta}\theta
.
To show that (
3.4
) is indeed valid, we will only need
Proposition
2.1
and the definition of the tensor

. Let

be a horizontal vector field, then

\displaystyle r(\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi,\nabla_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\xi)(\bar{J}V)

\displaystyle=g(\nabla_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\xi,\bar{J}V)\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi-g(\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi,\bar{J}V)\nabla_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\xi

\displaystyle=-g(\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi,V)\bar{J}\nabla_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\xi+g(\nabla_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\xi,V)\bar{J}\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi

\displaystyle=\bar{J}\big(r((\nabla_{F_{i}}\xi),\nabla_{\bar{J}F_{i}}\xi)V\big),

hence the conclusion.
∎

4.
The Morimoto submersion

This section rewrites the equations of Theorems

and
3.5
for the total space of a circle bundle, in order to relate the harmonicity of its normal almost
contact metric structure with the harmonicity of the Hermitian structure on the base. To ensure the normality of the structure on the total space, we use Morimoto’s version of the Boothby-Wang fibration and extend
[

]

is a complex manifold, in
[

h_{\phi}\in{\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}}

{\langle{h_{\phi}}\mid{h}\rangle}=\phi(h)
for all
h\in{\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}}
.
We have
{\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}}=\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}_{\beta\in\mathsf{B}}{\mathbb{R}}h_{\beta}
if
\mathsf{B}
is a basis of
\mathsf{R}
.
For each
\lambda\in\mathsf{R}
one can choose a root vector

E_{\lambda}\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{\lambda}_{\mathbb{R}}
such that

{\mathfrak{g}}^{\lambda}_{\mathbb{R}}={\mathbb{R}}E_{\lambda}
,

[E_{\lambda},E_{-\lambda}]=h_{\lambda}\in{\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}}
; hence,

[h_{\lambda},E_{\mu}]=\mu(h_{\lambda})E_{\mu}={\langle{h_{\mu}}\mid{h_{\lambda}}\rangle}E_{\mu}
for all
\mu\in\mathsf{R}
. One has
{\langle{E_{\lambda}}\mid{E_{-\lambda}}\rangle}=1
and

{\langle{E_{\lambda}}\mid{E_{\mu}}\rangle}=0
if
\mu\neq-\lambda
.

One defines a compact Cartan subalgebra by

{\mathfrak{t}}=i{\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}}=\bigoplus_{\beta\in\mathsf{B}}{\mathbb{R}}ih_{\beta}
.
If
{\mathfrak{l}}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}
is a subspace we set
{\mathfrak{l}}_{\mathbb{R}}=\{x\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{R}}:ix\in{\mathfrak{l}}\}
, thus
{\mathfrak{l}}=i{\mathfrak{l}}_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Set, for
\lambda\in\mathsf{R}
:

(5.1)

X_{\lambda}={\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}}(E_{\lambda}-E_{-\lambda}),\quad Y_{\lambda}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(E_{\lambda}+E_{-\lambda}).

Thus, for all
H\in{\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}}
:

[X_{\lambda},Y_{\lambda}]=ih_{\lambda},\quad[iH,X_{\lambda}]=\lambda(H)Y_{\lambda},\quad[iH,Y_{\lambda}]=-\lambda(H)X_{\lambda}.

Notice that

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\langle{X_{\lambda}}\mid{Y_{\lambda}}\rangle}=0,\ \;{\langle{X_{\lambda}}\mid{X_{\lambda}}\rangle}={\langle{Y_{\lambda}}\mid{Y_{\lambda}}\rangle}=-1,

(5.3)

\displaystyle{\langle{Y_{\lambda}}\mid{Y_{\mu}}\rangle}={\langle{X_{\lambda}}\mid{X_{\mu}}\rangle}={\langle{X_{\lambda}}\mid{Y_{\mu}}\rangle}=0\ \;\text{for all $\lambda\neq\mu$ in $\mathsf{R}^{+}$}.

Set
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\lambda}={\mathfrak{m}}_{-\lambda}={\mathbb{R}}X_{\lambda}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathbb{R}}Y_{\lambda}
and
{\mathfrak{n}}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathsf{R}^{+}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\lambda}
.
The
{\mathfrak{t}}
-modules
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\lambda}
are pairwise non isomorphic
irreducible
{\mathfrak{t}}
-modules. One has

[{\mathfrak{t}},{\mathfrak{m}}_{\lambda}]\subset{\mathfrak{m}}_{\lambda}
,

[{\mathfrak{m}}_{\lambda},{\mathfrak{m}}_{\lambda}]={\mathbb{R}}ih_{\lambda}
and, if
\lambda\neq\pm\mu
:

(5.4)

[{\mathfrak{m}}_{\lambda},{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mu}]\subset{\mathfrak{m}}_{\lambda+\mu}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\lambda-\mu}

with the convention that
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\nu}=0
when
\nu\notin\mathsf{R}
, see
[
WG
, Lemma 9.2]
.
Under this notation, the Lie subalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{t}}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathfrak{n}}

is a compact real form of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
We denote by
G
and
T
the closed connected Lie subgroups of

G_{\mathbb{C}}
with Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}
. Hence,
G
is compact
and
T
is a maximal torus of
G
.

One obtains a Euclidean scalar product on
{\mathfrak{g}}
by setting,
cf.
[
Bou
, Chapitre 9, § 1, p. 16]
:

B(U,V)=-{\langle{U}\mid{V}\rangle}\ \;\text{for all $U,V\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

The orthogonality with respect to
B
, or
{\langle{}\mid{}\rangle}
, is
denoted by
\perp
and if
\mathfrak{c}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
is a subspace such that

\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{a}\perp\mathfrak{b}
we denote by

x_{\mathfrak{a}}
the orthogonal projection of
x\in\mathfrak{c}

onto
\mathfrak{a}
.
By (
5.2
),
B(X_{\lambda},X_{\lambda})=B(Y_{\lambda},Y_{\lambda})=1
,
B(X_{\lambda},Y_{\lambda})=0
.
Therefore
\{X_{\lambda},Y_{\lambda}\}
is an orthonormal basis
(for
B
) of
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\lambda}
and, by (
5.3
),

\bigl((X_{\lambda},Y_{\lambda})\,;\lambda\in\mathsf{R}^{+}\bigr)

is an orthonormal basis of
{\mathfrak{n}}
. One verifies that

{\langle{h_{\alpha}}\mid{E_{\lambda}}\rangle}=0
, hence

B(h_{\alpha},X_{\lambda})=B(h_{\alpha},Y_{\lambda})=0
and

B({\mathfrak{t}},{\mathfrak{n}})=B({\mathfrak{m}}_{\lambda},{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mu})=0
, if

\lambda\neq\pm\mu
.

A
generalised flag manifold
is a homogeneous space

M=G/K
where
K
is the centraliser of a torus
C\subset G
;
we may assume that
C\subset T
is the connected component of
the centre of
K
. When
C=T
, we obtain the full flag
manifold
G/T
. The group
K
is compact and connected; we set

{\mathfrak{k}}=\operatorname{Lie}(K)
, which is a reductive Lie algebra. We mostly
follow
[
Arv
,
WG
]
for notation and results on generalised
flag manifolds and we will assume that
C
is non-trivial, i.e.
K\neq G
.

Let
{\mathfrak{z}}=\operatorname{Lie}(C)
be the centre of
{\mathfrak{k}}
. Since

{\mathfrak{t}}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
one can find a root subsystem

\mathsf{P}\subset\mathsf{R}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
such that,
setting
{\mathfrak{n}}^{\mathsf{P}}={\sum_{\beta\in\mathsf{P}}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\beta}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}^{\mathsf{P}}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathsf{P}}{\mathbb{R}}\,ih_{\lambda}
, one has
{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{z}}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathfrak{t}}^{\mathsf{P}}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}={\mathfrak{t}}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathfrak{n}}^{\mathsf{P}}
.
Set:

\mathsf{Q}=\mathsf{R}{\smallsetminus}\mathsf{P},\qquad{\mathfrak{m}}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathsf{Q}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha}.

Then

B({\mathfrak{k}},{\mathfrak{m}})=0
and
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{k}}\perp{\mathfrak{m}}
is the
reductive decomposition of
{\mathfrak{g}}
associated to
K
, i.e. to the
homogeneous space
G/K
.
Let
\varpi:\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\to\mathfrak{z}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
be
the restriction map. The elements of
R_{T}=\varpi(\mathsf{R})=\varpi(\mathsf{Q})

parameterise the irreducible summands of
the
K
-module
{\mathfrak{m}}
as follows, cf.
[
Arv
, Theorem 7.3]
. If

\gamma\in R_{T}\subset{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
, set:

(5.5)

\varpi^{-1}(\gamma)=\{\alpha\in\mathsf{Q}\,:\,\alpha_{\mid{\mathfrak{z}}}=\gamma\}\subset\mathsf{Q}.

The
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathbb{C}}
-module
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{C}}
decomposes as the
sum of the pairwise non isomorphic irreducible modules

{\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma}^{\mathbb{C}}=\sum_{\alpha\in\varpi^{-1}(\gamma)}{\mathbb{C}}E_{\alpha}
,
i.e.
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in R_{T}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma}^{\mathbb{C}}
.
Choose a set of positive roots

\mathsf{R}^{+}
and define for
\gamma\in R_{T}
:

{\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma}={\mathfrak{m}}_{-\gamma}=({\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma}^{\mathbb{C}}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{-\gamma}^{\mathbb{C}})\cap{\mathfrak{g}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\varpi^{-1}(\gamma)\cap\mathsf{R}^{+}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha}.

Then, the
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma}
are
nonequivalent irreducible
K
-modules. In order to write
\sum_{\gamma}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma}
as a direct sum, for each
class in
R_{T}/\{\pm 1\}
we choose a representative element

\gamma\in R_{T}
and we denote by
R_{T}^{+}
the set of these
representative elements. Observe that

\mathsf{Q}\cap\mathsf{R}^{+}=\bigsqcup_{\gamma\in R_{T}^{+}}(\varpi^{-1}(\gamma)\cap\mathsf{R}^{+}),\quad{\mathfrak{m}}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in R_{T}^{+}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathsf{Q}\cap\mathsf{R}^{+}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha}.

Notice that
\{(X_{\alpha},Y_{\alpha})\,:\,{\alpha\in\varpi^{-1}(\gamma)\cap\mathsf{R}^{+}}\}
is an orthonormal basis (for
B
) of

{\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma}
and that
B({\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma},{\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma^{\prime}})=0
if
\gamma\neq\gamma^{\prime}
.

We say that
J\in\operatorname{End}({\mathfrak{m}})
is an invariant complex structure
on
{\mathfrak{m}}
if
J^{2}=-{\mathrm{id}}
and
\Ad(k)J=J
for all
k\in K
. We
extend
J
to
{\mathfrak{g}}
by
J=0
on
{\mathfrak{k}}
. Recall that if
z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
one
defines the Kostant-Kirillov-Souriau (KKS for short) form

\omega_{z}
by

\omega_{z}(A,B)=B(z,[A,B])\ \;\text{for all $A,B\in{\mathfrak{g}}$.}

Notice that if
v\in{\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}}
one has

\omega_{iv}(X_{\lambda},Y_{\lambda})=\lambda(v)
since

B(iv,ih_{\lambda})={\langle{v}\mid{h_{\lambda}}\rangle}=\lambda(v)
. The
proof of the next classical proposition is left to the reader.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
J
be an invariant complex structure on
{\mathfrak{m}}
and

z\in{\mathfrak{z}}
.

(1)
For all
\gamma\in R_{T}
, there exists
\epsilon(\gamma)=\pm 1
such that if
\lambda\in\varpi^{-1}(\gamma)
one has

JX_{\lambda}=\epsilon(\gamma)Y_{\lambda}
and

JY_{\lambda}=-\epsilon(\gamma)X_{\lambda}
.

(2)
The form
\omega_{z}
is
J
-invariant and
K
-invariant, i.e.,

\text{$\omega_{z}(JA,JB)=\omega_{z}(A,B)=\omega_{z}(\Ad(k)A,\Ad(k)B)$ for all $A,B\in{\mathfrak{g}}$ and $k\in K$}.

Moreover,
\omega_{z}(A,B)=B(z,[A,B]_{\mathfrak{t}})=B(z,[A,B]_{\mathfrak{z}})
and

\omega_{z}(A,B)=\omega_{z}(A_{\mathfrak{m}},B_{\mathfrak{m}})=\omega_{z}(JA_{\mathfrak{m}},JB_{\mathfrak{m}}).

(3)
Suppose that
z^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{z}}
and
\omega_{z}=\omega_{z^{\prime}}

on
{\mathfrak{m}}\times{\mathfrak{m}}
, then
z=z^{\prime}
.

The two next theorems summarise results proved in

[
Arv
, Propositions 7.5, 7.6, 7.7]
and
[
WG
, Theorem 4.7,
Proposition 9.3, Theorem 9.4]
. Let
e\in G
be the identity
and
{p_{0}}=eK\in M
; recall that
T_{p_{0}}M
identifies
with
{\mathfrak{m}}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

(1)
Any
G
-invariant almost complex structure
J
on

M=G/K
is determined by an
\Ad(K)
-invariant complex
structure
J_{\mathfrak{m}}
on
{\mathfrak{m}}
, defined on each
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma}={\mathfrak{m}}_{-\gamma}
,
\gamma\in R_{T}^{+}
, by
\epsilon(\gamma)=\pm 1
such that
J_{\mathfrak{m}}X_{\lambda}=\epsilon(\gamma)Y_{\lambda}
,
J_{\mathfrak{m}}Y_{\lambda}=-\epsilon(\gamma)X_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\varpi^{-1}(\gamma)\cap\mathsf{R}^{+}
.

(2)
Any
G
-invariant metric
\kappa
on
M
is
compatible with any invariant almost complex structure. It is determined
by an
\Ad(K)
-invariant scalar product
{\kappa_{\mathfrak{m}}}
on
{\mathfrak{m}}

defined on each
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma}
,
\gamma\in R_{T}^{+}
, by
\kappa_{{\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma}}=\kappa_{\gamma}B
for some
\kappa_{\gamma}>0
, hence,
{\kappa_{\mathfrak{m}}}(X_{\lambda},X_{\lambda})={\kappa_{\mathfrak{m}}}(Y_{\lambda},Y_{\lambda})=\kappa_{\gamma}
for all
\lambda\in\varpi^{-1}(\gamma)\cap\mathsf{R}^{+}
.

Set

{\mathfrak{z}}_{{\mathrm{reg}}}={\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathbb{R}}{\smallsetminus}\bigl(\bigcup_{\gamma\in R_{T}}\{\gamma=0\}\bigr)
and define a Weyl chamber
to be a connected component of
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathrm{reg}}
.
The invariant orderings
\mathsf{Q}^{+}
, as defined in
[
Arv
, Chapter 7,
§7]
, are in bijection with the
Weyl chambers in
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathbb{R}}
: if
\mathsf{C}
is a
Weyl chamber, pick
z\in\mathsf{C}
; then

\mathsf{Q}^{+}=\{\alpha\in\mathsf{Q}\,:\,\alpha(z)>0\}
gives an
invariant ordering. Furthermore, one defines an

\Ad(K)
-invariant complex structure
J_{\mathfrak{m}}
on
{\mathfrak{m}}
by:

(5.6)

J_{\mathfrak{m}}X_{\lambda}=Y_{\lambda},\quad J_{\mathfrak{m}}Y_{\lambda}=-X_{\lambda},\quad\text{for all $\lambda\in\mathsf{Q}^{+}$}.

From an invariant ordering
\mathsf{Q}^{+}
one can deduce a set of
positive roots
\mathsf{R}^{+}=\mathsf{P}^{+}\sqcup\mathsf{Q}^{+}
where

\mathsf{P}^{+}
is a set of positive roots in
\mathsf{P}
. When
J_{\mathfrak{m}}

is a complex structure associated to an invariant ordering we
will choose the set of positive roots
\mathsf{R}^{+}
in this way.
Notice that, in this case, one may take

R_{T}^{+}=\varpi(\mathsf{Q}^{+})
and if
\gamma\in R_{T}^{+}
, one has

\varpi^{-1}(\gamma)=\{\alpha\in\mathsf{Q}^{+}:\varpi(\alpha)=\gamma\}
, cf. (
5.5
) for the definition.

Theorem 5.3
.

Retain the notation of Theorem
5.2
.

(1)
There is a bijection between the following sets:

(i)

G
-invariant complex structures on
M
;

(ii)

\Ad(K)
-invariant complex structures
J_{\mathfrak{m}}
on
{\mathfrak{m}}
as
in (
5.6
) defined by a choice of an invariant
ordering
\mathsf{Q}^{+}
, i.e. a Weyl chamber
{\mathsf{C}}
.

(2)
Let
J
be an invariant complex structure on
M
and
\kappa

be a
G
-invariant metric on
M
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

(\kappa,J)
is Kähler;

(ii)

for all
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in R_{T}^{+}=\varpi(\mathsf{Q}^{+})
such that
\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}\in R_{T}^{+}
, one has
\kappa_{\gamma}+\kappa_{\gamma^{\prime}}=\kappa_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}
;

(iii)

the Kähler form
\Omega_{\mathfrak{m}}(U,V)={\kappa_{\mathfrak{m}}}(J_{\mathfrak{m}}U,V)

is equal to the KKS form
{\omega_{ih}}_{\mid{\mathfrak{m}}\times{\mathfrak{m}}}
for some

in the
Weyl chamber defined by
\mathsf{Q}^{+}
.

(3)
The metric
\kappa
on
M
is Kähler-Einstein
if and only if
\Omega_{\mathfrak{m}}=c{\omega_{ih_{\rho}}}
where
\rho={\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathsf{Q}^{+}}\alpha
and

is
a positive real constant.

6.
Homogeneous principal bundles over a generalised flag manifold

Let
M=G/K
,
K=Z_{G}(C)
, be a generalised flag manifold as in
section
5
.
Choose a sub-torus
\widetilde{C}\subset C
of codimension
one. Set
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{z}}}=\operatorname{Lie}(\widetilde{C})
and pick a unit vector

X_{0}\in{\mathfrak{z}}
, i.e.
B(X_{0},X_{0})=1
, such that
B(X_{0},{\widetilde{\mathfrak{z}}})=0
,
hence
{\mathfrak{z}}={\mathbb{R}}X_{0}\perp{\widetilde{\mathfrak{z}}}
. Then, if

{\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}}={\widetilde{\mathfrak{z}}}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}[{\mathfrak{k}},{\mathfrak{k}}]
we get
{\mathfrak{k}}={\mathbb{R}}X_{0}\perp{\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}}

and
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}}
is the orthogonal of
X_{0}
in
{\mathfrak{k}}
.
One has
Y_{0}=-iX_{0}\in{\mathfrak{z}}_{\mathbb{R}}

and
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{z}}}={\mathfrak{z}}\cap{\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}}
.
The algebra
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}}
is reductive and

{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}}={\widetilde{\mathfrak{z}}}\perp{\mathfrak{t}}^{\mathsf{P}}
is a Cartan subalgebra of

{\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}}
.
Let
{\widetilde{K}}
be the
closed connected subgroup of
G
such that
\operatorname{Lie}({\widetilde{K}})={\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}}
,
then
S=K/{\widetilde{K}}\cong C/\widetilde{C}\cong S^{1}
and
we obtain a
G
-homogeneous principal
S
-bundle:

\pi:{\widetilde{M}}=G/{\widetilde{K}}\twoheadrightarrow M=G/K.

From
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{k}}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathfrak{m}}={\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathbb{R}}X_{0}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathfrak{m}}
one
deduces that the
{\widetilde{K}}
-invariant decomposition associated to the
reductive homogeneous space
{\widetilde{M}}=G/{\widetilde{K}}
is:

{\mathfrak{g}}={\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}}\perp{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}\ \;\text{with ${\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}={\mathbb{R}}X_{0}\perp{\mathfrak{m}}$
(hence $[{\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}},{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}]\subset{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}$)}.

Set
{\tilde{p}_{0}}=e{\widetilde{K}}
. One can identify
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}
with
T_{\tilde{p}_{0}}{\widetilde{M}}

and the differential

d\pi({\tilde{p}_{0}}):T_{\tilde{p}_{0}}{\widetilde{M}}\twoheadrightarrow T_{p_{0}}M
with
the orthogonal projection
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathfrak{m}}
. Recall that there is a
bijection between
G
-invariant tensors on
{\widetilde{M}}
and

\Ad({\widetilde{K}})
-invariant tensors on
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}
, cf.
[
CS
]
. We will
emphasise this bijection in a few cases which enable us to
construct invariant structures on
{\widetilde{M}}
.

Let
g\in G
, we denote by
\mathtt{L}_{g}:{\widetilde{M}}\to{\widetilde{M}}
, or

\mathtt{L}_{g}:M\to M
, the left translation by
g
,
i.e.
x\mapsto gx
. If
{\mathcal{O}}\subset{\widetilde{M}}
is open and

Z\in{\mathcal{X}}({\mathcal{O}})
, the vector field

g_{*}Z\in{\mathcal{X}}(g{\mathcal{O}})
is defined by
g_{*}Z_{gq}=d\mathtt{L}_{g}(q).Z_{q}
for all
q\in{\mathcal{O}}
.
A vector field
V\in{\mathcal{X}}({\widetilde{M}})
is said to be left invariant
if
g_{*}V=V
for all
g\in G
, that is
V_{gq}=d\mathtt{L}_{g}(q).V_{q}
for all
g\in G
and
q\in{\widetilde{M}}
.
We denote by
{\mathcal{X}}({\widetilde{M}})^{G}
the vector space of left invariant
vector fields.
Suppose that
V\in{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}
centralises
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}}
. The one parameter
subgroup
\exp({\mathbb{R}}V)
acts by right translation on
{\widetilde{M}}
,
and one defines the vector field
V^{\#}
on
{\widetilde{M}}
by:

V^{\#}_{q}=\frac{d}{dt}_{\big|t=0}\left(qe^{tV}\right)\ \;\text{for $q\in{\widetilde{M}}$.}

It is easy to see that
V^{\#}
is left invariant. The next
(well known) lemma recalls that we can obtain
{\mathcal{X}}({\widetilde{M}})^{G}

through that construction.

Lemma 6.1
.

There exists a linear bijection
f
between
{\mathcal{X}}({\widetilde{M}})^{G}

and

{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}^{\widetilde{K}}=\{V\in{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}\,:\,\Ad(h)V=V\ \,\text{for all $h\in{\widetilde{K}}$}\}=\{V\in{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}\,:\,[{\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}},V]=0\}
.
One has
f(X)=X_{{\tilde{p}_{0}}}
and
f^{-1}(V)=V^{\#}
.

Using the previous lemma one can prove the following
proposition. Since we will not use this result, the proof is left
to the interested reader.

Proposition 6.2
.

The space
{\mathcal{X}}({\widetilde{M}})^{G}
is equal to
{\mathbb{R}}X_{0}^{\#}
unless

{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}}=i\operatorname{Ker}(\alpha)
for an
\alpha\in\mathsf{Q}
such that,
setting
\delta=\varpi(\alpha)
, the
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}}_{\mathbb{C}}
-module

{\mathfrak{m}}_{\delta}^{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{C}}E_{\alpha}
is of dimension one. In this
case one has

{\mathcal{X}}({\widetilde{M}})^{G}={\mathbb{R}}X_{0}^{\#}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathbb{R}}X_{\alpha}^{\#}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathbb{R}}Y_{\alpha}^{\#}
and

ih_{\alpha}=\pm\sqrt{{\langle{h_{\alpha}}\mid{h_{\alpha}}\rangle}}X_{0}\in{\mathfrak{z}}
.

Definition 6.3
.

Set
\xi=X_{0}^{\#}
.
We say that
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}}
is
generic
if
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}^{\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}}={\mathbb{R}}X_{0}
,
i.e.
{\mathcal{X}}({\widetilde{M}})^{G}={\mathbb{R}}\xi
.

Notice that, by the previous proposition,
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{k}}}

is generic except in a finite number of cases.

We now study some invariant vector fields which are important in
the construction of harmonic normal almost contact metric
structures on
{\widetilde{M}}
, cf. Theorem
4.6
.
Endow
{\widetilde{M}}
with a
G
-invariant metric
{\widetilde{\kappa}}
, i.e.:

{\widetilde{\kappa}}_{gp}(d\mathtt{L}_{g}(p).U_{p},d\mathtt{L}_{g}(p).V_{p})={\widetilde{\kappa}}_{p}(U_{p},V_{p})\ \,\text{for all $U,V\in{\mathcal{X}}({\widetilde{M}})$.}

Since
{\widetilde{M}}
is a reductive homogeneous space,
{\widetilde{\kappa}}
is
uniquely determined by an
\Ad({\widetilde{K}})
-invariant Euclidean product

{\widetilde{\kappa}}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}
on
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}
. Denote by
{\widetilde{\nabla}}
the Levi-Civita
connection associated to
{\widetilde{\kappa}}
. Then
{\widetilde{\nabla}}
is

G
-invariant, that is to say:

g_{*}{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{X}Y={\widetilde{\nabla}}_{g_{*}X}g_{*}Y\ \;\text{for all $g\in G$
and $X,Y\in{\mathcal{X}}({\widetilde{M}})$.}

There is a bijection between the space of
G
-invariant
connections on
{\widetilde{M}}
and the space of bilinear maps
\Lambda:{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}\times{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}\to{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}
which are
{\widetilde{K}}
-invariant,
i.e.
\Lambda(\Ad(h)U,\Ad(h)V)=\Lambda(U,V)
for
U,V\in{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}
and
h\in{\widetilde{K}}
(see
[
Nom
]

and
[
KN
]
for the general theory).
We quickly recall, for further use, the construction of a connection
\nabla

starting from
\Lambda
as given in
[
Nom
]
. Firstly, if

{\widetilde{\pi}}:G\twoheadrightarrow{\widetilde{M}}=G/{\widetilde{K}}
is the natural projection, there
exists an open neighbourhood
N
of

such that
{\widetilde{\pi}}:N\to{\widetilde{N}}={\widetilde{\pi}}(N)
is a diffeomorphism.
Then, for
X\in{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}
one can define
X^{*}\in{\mathcal{X}}({\widetilde{N}})
by
setting
X^{*}_{k{\tilde{p}_{0}}}=d\mathtt{L}_{k}({\tilde{p}_{0}}).X
for all
k\in N
.
The connection
\nabla
is completely determined on
{\widetilde{N}}
by

\nabla_{X^{*}}Y^{*}=\Lambda(X,Y)^{*}
for
X,Y\in{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}
.
This local construction then furnishes the invariant connection
\nabla
on
the manifold
{\widetilde{M}}
. In particular, if
{\mathcal{O}}
is an open set
of
{\widetilde{M}}
, one has on the translated open set
g{\mathcal{O}}
:

(6.1)

g_{*}\nabla_{X}Y=\nabla_{g_{*}X}g_{*}Y\ \;\,\text{for all $X,Y\in{\mathcal{X}}({\mathcal{O}})$}.

By
[
Nom
, Theorem 13.1]
or

[
KN
, Theorem 3.3 & p. 202]
the map
\Lambda
corresponding to the Levi-Civita connection

{\widetilde{\nabla}}
associated to the chosen invariant metric
{\widetilde{\kappa}}

Since

The computation in the different cases is straightforward; we

(1)

Theorem 6.20

The map
\xi:{\widetilde{M}}\to U{\widetilde{M}}
is a harmonic map for any
g-natural metric on

.

Proof.

We have shown in Theorem
6.15

{\widetilde{\nabla}}^{*}{\widetilde{\nabla}}\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}\xi
, therefore, by
Theorem
6.18
(1),
\xi
is a harmonic unit vector
field. Since
\xi
is Killing, by Theorem

(2) & (3), it
suffices to prove that
S(\xi)=0
. Furthermore, by invariance
of
S(\xi)
it is enough to show that
S(\xi)
vanishes on the
open subset
{\widetilde{N}}
used in Lemma
6.13
and we can apply
the formulas of that lemma. We calculate
S(\xi)
in the
orthonormal frame
\{\xi,(F_{\alpha},\Phi_{\alpha})_{\alpha}\}
. Since
{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{\xi}\xi=0
the vector field
S(\xi)
is sum of
terms of the form

\widetilde{R}({\widetilde{\nabla}}_{F_{\alpha}}\xi,\xi)F_{\alpha}+\widetilde{R}({\widetilde{\nabla}}_{\Phi_{\alpha}}\xi,\xi)\Phi_{\alpha}
. From
the definition we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}({\widetilde{\nabla}}_{F_{\alpha}}\xi,\xi)F_{\alpha}

\displaystyle={\widetilde{\nabla}}^{2}_{{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{F_{\alpha}}\xi,\xi}F_{\alpha}-{\widetilde{\nabla}}^{2}_{\xi,{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{F_{\alpha}}\xi}F_{\alpha}

\displaystyle={\widetilde{\nabla}}_{{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{F_{\alpha}}\xi}({\widetilde{\nabla}}_{\xi}F_{\alpha})-{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{F_{\alpha}}\xi}\xi}F_{\alpha}-\bigl\{{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{\xi}({\widetilde{\nabla}}_{{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{F_{\alpha}}\xi}F_{\alpha})-{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{\xi}({\widetilde{\nabla}}_{F\alpha}\xi)}F_{\alpha}\bigr\}.

Recall (with the notation of Lemma
6.13
) that

{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{\xi}{F_{\alpha}}=(c_{\gamma}-\frac{a_{\gamma}}{2})\Phi_{\alpha}
,
{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{F_{\alpha}}\xi=-\frac{a_{\gamma}}{2}\Phi_{\alpha}
,
{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{\xi}{\Phi_{\alpha}}=\frac{a_{\gamma}}{2}F_{\alpha}
and
{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{\xi}{\Phi_{\alpha}}=-(c_{\gamma}-\frac{a_{\gamma}}{2})F_{\alpha}
. Therefore,
{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{F_{\alpha}}F_{\alpha}=0

implies that:

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}({\widetilde{\nabla}}_{F_{\alpha}}\xi,\xi)F_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{\gamma}}{2}{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{\Phi_{\alpha}}\xi}F_{\alpha}+\frac{a_{\gamma}}{2}\bigl\{{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{\xi}({\widetilde{\nabla}}_{\Phi_{\alpha}}F_{\alpha})-{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{\xi}\Phi_{\alpha}}F_{\alpha}\bigr\}

A similar calculation gives

. Thus

manifold, i.e.
K=T
is a maximal torus. In this setting the
notation simplifies since we have:
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{t}}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathfrak{m}}
,
{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathfrak{n}}=\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}_{\alpha\in\mathsf{R}^{+}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha}
,
{\mathfrak{z}}={\mathfrak{t}}
,

\mathsf{P}=\emptyset
,
\mathsf{Q}=\mathsf{R}
,
\varpi={\mathrm{id}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{R}}}
and
R_{T}=\mathsf{R}
.
Therefore the irreducible
K
-modules
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma}
are the
irreducible
T
-modules

{\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha}={\mathbb{R}}X_{\alpha}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathbb{R}}Y_{\alpha}
. The choice of an
invariant ordering is the choice of a set of positive roots

\mathsf{R}^{+}
, i.e. the choice of a Weyl chamber
{\mathsf{C}}
in
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{R}}
. The

G
-invariant complex structure
J
on
M
associated to

\mathsf{R}^{+}
is defined by
J_{\mathfrak{m}}X_{\alpha}=Y_{\alpha}
,

J_{\mathfrak{m}}Y_{\alpha}=-X_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\mathsf{R}^{+}
. Let

\kappa
be a
G
-invariant metric on
M
, then
the Euclidean product
\kappa_{\mathfrak{m}}
is given by
\kappa_{\mathfrak{m}}=\kappa_{\alpha}B
on
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\mathsf{R}^{+}
. The metric
\kappa
is
Kähler if and only if
\kappa_{\alpha}+\kappa_{\beta}=\kappa_{\alpha+\beta}
if
\alpha+\beta\in\mathsf{R}^{+}
; equivalently, the
Kähler form
\Omega^{\kappa}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is equal to the KKS form

\omega_{ih_{\kappa}}
with
h_{\kappa}\in{\mathsf{C}}
.
Choose a sub-torus
{\widetilde{T}}
of
T
such that

S=T/{\widetilde{T}}\cong S^{1}
, let
X_{0}=iY_{0}\in{\mathfrak{t}}
(with

Y_{0}\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{R}}
) be such that
B(X_{0},X_{0})=1
and

{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathbb{R}}X_{0}\perp{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}}
. Then

\pi:{\widetilde{M}}=G/{\widetilde{T}}\twoheadrightarrow M=G/T
is a principal
S
-bundle.
Recall that a normal almost contact metric
structure
{(\theta,\xi,\eta,{\widetilde{\kappa}})}
on
{\widetilde{M}}
is constructed from: a
Hermitian metric
(\kappa,J)
, the connection
\eta
such
that
\eta_{\tilde{p}_{0}}(U)=B(X_{0},U)
, the metric

{\widetilde{\kappa}}=\pi^{*}\kappa+\eta\otimes\eta
and the
endomorphism
\theta
lifting
J
to the horizontal bundle.

We give an example of this construction in the case:

G=\operatorname{SU}(3)\subset G_{\mathbb{C}}=\operatorname{SL}(3,{\mathbb{C}}),\quad{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{su}}(3)\subset{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathfrak{sl}}(3,{\mathbb{C}}).

The split real form of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
is
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{R}}={\mathfrak{sl}}(3,{\mathbb{R}})
and we
choose the
G_{\mathbb{C}}
-invariant bilinear form

{\langle{A}\mid{B}\rangle}=\trace(AB)
on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
. The split Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{R}}
consists of the traceless diagonal real matrices, which will
be written
\operatorname{diag}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]
with
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}=0
. The
compact Cartan subalgebra is

{\mathfrak{t}}=i{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{R}}=\{i\operatorname{diag}[a,b,c]\,:\,\operatorname{diag}[a,b,c]\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{R}}\}.

For
i=1,2,3
, let
\varepsilon_{i}\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
be the linear form
which maps
\operatorname{diag}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]
to
x_{i}
. Set
\alpha_{j}=\varepsilon_{j}-\varepsilon_{j+1}
for
j=1,2
and
\alpha_{3}=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{3}
. Then
one has

\mathsf{R}=\{\pm\alpha_{1},\pm\alpha_{2},\pm\alpha_{3}\}=\{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j},\ 1\leq i\neq j\leq 3\}
.
For

\lambda=\varepsilon_{k}-\varepsilon_{\ell}
the element
E_{\lambda}

is the unit matrix
E_{k\ell}
. Hence,

X_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(E_{k\ell}-E_{\ell k}),\quad Y_{\lambda}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(E_{k\ell}+E_{\ell k}).

The algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
decomposes as the orthogonal direct sum
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{t}}\perp{\mathfrak{m}}
. One has

{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathfrak{m}}_{\pm\alpha_{1}}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\pm\alpha_{2}}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\pm\alpha_{3}}
, where

{\mathfrak{m}}_{\pm\alpha_{i}}={\mathbb{R}}X_{\pm\alpha_{i}}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathbb{R}}Y_{\pm\alpha_{i}}
.
Any
T
-invariant scalar product
\kappa_{\mathfrak{m}}
on
{\mathfrak{m}}
is
defined by
{(\kappa_{\mathfrak{m}})}_{\mid{\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha_{j}}}=\kappa_{\alpha_{j}}B

with
\kappa_{\alpha_{j}}>0
.
Finally, recall that a
T
-invariant complex structure
J_{\mathfrak{m}}
on
{\mathfrak{m}}

is determined by
J_{\mathfrak{m}}X_{\alpha_{j}}=\epsilon(\alpha_{j})Y_{\alpha_{j}}
,

J_{\mathfrak{m}}Y_{\alpha_{j}}=-\epsilon(\alpha_{j})X_{\alpha_{j}}
with

\epsilon(\alpha_{j})=\pm 1
for
j=1,2,3
.

We fix two integers
k,\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
(k,\ell)\neq(0,0)
and set
\Gamma=\sqrt{k^{2}+\ell^{2}+k\ell}
.
Define an element of
{\mathfrak{t}}
by X
{}_{-1}=\frac{i}{\Gamma\sqrt{2}}\operatorname{diag}[k,\ell,-(k+\ell)]
.
Set
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{k,\ell}={\mathbb{R}}X_{-1}
,
{\widetilde{T}}=T_{k,\ell}=\exp({\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}})\subset T
.
Therefore,
{\widetilde{T}}
is the one dimensional sub-torus of
T
such that

{\widetilde{T}}=T_{k,\ell}=\{\operatorname{diag}[e^{itk},e^{it\ell},e^{-it(k+\ell)}]\,:\,t\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
.
Let
X_{0}\in{\mathfrak{t}}{\smallsetminus}\{0\}
such that
B(X_{-1},X_{0})=0
,
B(X_{0},X_{0})=1
:

X_{0}=\frac{-i}{\Gamma\sqrt{6}}\operatorname{diag}[2\ell+k,-(\ell+2k),-\ell+k]=iY_{0},\quad Y_{0}=\frac{-1}{\Gamma\sqrt{6}}\operatorname{diag}[2\ell+k,-(\ell+2k),-\ell+k].

Notice that
(X_{-1},X_{0})
is an orthonormal basis, for
B
, of

{\mathfrak{t}}=\mathfrak{t}_{k,\ell}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathbb{R}}X_{0}={\mathbb{R}}X_{-1}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathbb{R}}X_{0}
.
The group
S=T/{\widetilde{T}}
is a one dimensional torus
such that
\operatorname{Lie}(S)={\mathfrak{s}}={\mathfrak{t}}/{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}}={\mathbb{R}}A
, with
A=[X_{0}+{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}}]
.
One can therefore define a principal
S
-bundle

\pi:{\widetilde{M}}=G/{T}_{k,\ell}\twoheadrightarrow M=G/T.

This homogeneous bundle is an
Aloff-Wallach
space

[
AW
]
and is denoted by
{\widetilde{M}}_{k,\ell}
.
Recall that we set

{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}={\mathbb{R}}X_{0}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathbb{R}}X_{0}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha_{1}}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha_{2}}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha_{3}}
,
and that
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}}
is a
{\widetilde{T}}
-module under the adjoint action.
One easily sees, by computing

\lambda(X_{-1})
for
\lambda\in\mathsf{R}
, that:

i{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}}=\operatorname{Ker}(\alpha_{1})\iff k=\ell,\ i{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}}=\operatorname{Ker}(\alpha_{2})\iff 2\ell+k=0,\ i{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}}=\operatorname{Ker}(\alpha_{3})\iff k+2\ell=0.

Then,
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}}
is generic if and only if
k\neq\ell
,
2\ell+k\neq 0
or
2k+\ell\neq 0
. Proposition
6.2

becomes in this example:

Proposition 6.21
.

The space of
G
-invariant vector fields

{\mathcal{X}}(\widetilde{M}_{k,\ell})^{G}
is equal to:

(1)

{\mathbb{R}}\xi
when
k\neq\ell
,
2\ell+k\neq 0
or
2k+\ell\neq 0
;

(2)

{\mathbb{R}}\xi\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathbb{R}}X_{\alpha_{1}}^{\#}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}Y_{\alpha_{1}}^{\#}
if
k=\ell
;

(3)

{\mathbb{R}}\xi\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathbb{R}}X_{\alpha_{2}}^{\#}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}Y_{\alpha_{2}}^{\#}
if
2\ell+k=0
;

(4)

{\mathbb{R}}\xi\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}{\mathbb{R}}X_{\alpha_{3}}^{\#}\mathbin{\boldsymbol{\oplus}}Y_{\alpha_{3}}^{\#}
if
2k+\ell=0
.

We now choose the complex structure
J
on
M
associated to the
basis
\mathsf{B}=\{\alpha=\alpha_{3},\,\beta=-\alpha_{2}\}
of

\mathsf{R}
, hence

\mathsf{R}^{+}=\{\alpha=\alpha_{3},\,\beta=-\alpha_{2},\,\gamma=\alpha+\beta=\alpha_{1}\}

and the corresponding Weyl chamber is
{\mathsf{C}}=\{\operatorname{diag}[a,b,c]\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{R}}:a>c>b\}
.
Then, we have

(X_{\alpha}=X_{\alpha_{3}},\,Y_{\alpha}=Y_{\alpha_{3}}),\ (X_{\beta}=-X_{\alpha_{2}},\,Y_{\beta}=Y_{\alpha_{2}}),\ (X_{\gamma}=X_{\alpha_{1}},\,Y_{\gamma}=Y_{\alpha_{1}}),

and
J_{\mathfrak{m}}X_{\lambda}=Y_{\lambda}
,
J_{\mathfrak{m}}Y_{\lambda}=-X_{\lambda}

for
\lambda=\alpha,\beta,\gamma
.
Moreover, under the choice of
Y_{0}
made above:

\alpha(Y_{0})=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\Gamma\sqrt{2}}\ell,\quad\beta(Y_{0})=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\Gamma\sqrt{2}}k,\quad\gamma(Y_{0})=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\Gamma\sqrt{2}}(\ell+k).

Thus,
-Y_{0}\in{\mathsf{C}}
if and only if
k>0
and
\ell>0
.
Notice that
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha}={\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha_{3}}
,

{\mathfrak{m}}_{\beta}={\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha_{2}}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\gamma}={\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha_{1}}
. Let
(\kappa,J)
be a Hermitian metric on
M

determined by a
T
-invariant Euclidean product
\kappa_{\mathfrak{m}}
as
above, i.e. by a triple

(\kappa_{\alpha},\kappa_{\beta},\kappa_{\gamma})
of positive
scalars. Then,
(\kappa,J)
is Kähler if and only if
\kappa_{\alpha}+\kappa_{\beta}=\kappa_{\gamma}
; equivalently, for all
U,V\in{\mathfrak{m}}
,

\kappa_{\mathfrak{m}}(U,V)=\omega_{ih_{\kappa}}(U,J_{\mathfrak{m}}V)
with
h_{\kappa}\in{\mathsf{C}}
and in this case

\kappa_{\alpha}=\alpha(h_{\kappa}),\kappa_{\beta}=\beta(h_{\kappa})

(thus
\kappa_{\gamma}=\gamma(h_{\kappa})
).

Recall that from
J,X_{0},\kappa
we can construct an almost contact metric
structure
\sigma={(\theta,\xi,\eta,{\widetilde{\kappa}})}
on

{\widetilde{M}}_{k,\ell}=G/T_{k,\ell}
. Theorem
6.16
yields:

Theorem 6.22
.

Let
J
be as above and choose a Kähler metric
(\kappa,J)
on

M
. Then, the normal almost contact metric
structure structure
\sigma
on
{\widetilde{M}}_{k,\ell}
is a harmonic map.

By Theorem
6.16
,
\sigma
is c-Sasakian if and only if there
exists
c>0
such that
-Y_{0}=ch_{\kappa}
,
i.e.
c\kappa_{\alpha}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\Gamma\sqrt{2}}\ell

and
c\kappa_{\beta}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\Gamma\sqrt{2}}k
. Therefore, it suffices to pick
h_{\kappa}\in{\mathsf{C}}
such
that
h_{\kappa}\notin-{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{*}Y_{0}
to get that
\sigma
is not
c-Sasakian. In particular, if
k
or
\ell
is
<0
, the
structure
\sigma
is not c-Sasakian. Moreover, the metric

\kappa
is Kähler-Einstein if and only if
h_{\kappa}
is a multiple
of
h_{\rho}=h_{\gamma}=\operatorname{diag}[1,-1,0]
. Therefore, for this
choice of
\kappa
, the structure
\sigma
is c-Sasakian when

-Y_{0}=c\operatorname{diag}[1,-1,0]
, which is equivalent to
k=\ell
and

\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\Gamma\sqrt{2}}\ell=c>0
. Thus, if

k\neq\ell
or
k=\ell<0
the structure
\sigma
is not
Sasakian with
\kappa
Kähler-Einstein.

For completeness, we give the expression of the vector fields

,
{\widetilde{\nabla}}^{*}{\widetilde{\nabla}}\xi
obtained in

\delta\theta=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\Gamma\sqrt{2}}\Bigl(\frac{\ell}{\kappa_{\alpha}}+\frac{k}{\kappa_{\beta}}+\frac{(\ell+k)}{\kappa_{\gamma}}\Bigr)\xi,\quad{\widetilde{\nabla}}^{*}{\widetilde{\nabla}}\xi=\frac{3}{4\Gamma^{2}}\Bigl(\frac{\ell^{2}}{\kappa_{\alpha}^{2}}+\frac{k^{2}}{\kappa_{\beta}^{2}}+\frac{(\ell+k)^{2}}{\kappa_{\gamma}^{2}}\Bigr)\xi.

Norm inequalities related to Heinz and Heron operator means Thanks: Data sharing not applicable to this article as no datasets were generated or analyzed during the current study.
A. G. Ghazanfari

Abstract
. In this article we present some new comparisons between the Heinz and Heron operator means, which improve some recent results known from the literature. We derive some refinements of these inequalities for unitarily invariant norms with the help of the contractive maps.

47A30, 47A63, 15A45.

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Let
M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})
be the space of
m\times n
complex matrices and
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})=M_{n,n}(\mathbb{C})
. Let
|||.|||
denote any unitarily invariant
norm on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. So,

|||UAV|||=|||A|||
for all
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and for all unitary matrices
U,V\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. The Hilbert-Schmidt and trace class norm
of
A=[a_{ij}]\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
are denoted by

\|A\|_{2}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{j}^{2}(A)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\left\|A\right\|_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{j}(A)

where
s_{1}(A)\geq s_{2}(A)\geq...\geq s_{n}(A)
are the singular values of
A
, which are the eigenvalues of the positive semidefinite
matrix
\mid A\mid=(A^{*}A)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, arranged in decreasing order and repeated according to multiplicity.
For Hermitian matrices
A,B\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, we write that
A\geqslant 0

if
A
is positive semidefinite,
A>0
if
A
is positive definite, and
A\geqslant B
if
A-B\geqslant 0
.

Let
A,B,X\in M_{n}
,
A,B
are positive definite,
The matrix version of the Heinz means are defined by

H_{\nu}(A,X,B)=\frac{A^{\nu}XB^{1-\nu}+A^{1-\nu}XB^{\nu}}{2},

and the matrix version of the Heron means are defined by

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(A,X,B)=(1-\alpha)A^{\frac{1}{2}}XB^{\frac{1}{2}}+\alpha\left(\frac{AX+XB}{2}\right).

In 1998, Zhan
[
11
]
proved that if

\frac{1}{4}\leq\nu\leq\frac{3}{4}
and
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},\infty)
, then

\displaystyle|||A^{\nu}XB^{1-\nu}+A^{1-\nu}XB^{\nu}|||\leq\frac{2}{2+t}|||AX+XB+tA^{\frac{1}{2}}XB^{\frac{1}{2}}|||,

(1.1)

for
-2<t\leq 2
.

Kaur and Singh
[
4
]
have shown that if

\frac{1}{4}\leq\nu\leq\frac{3}{4}
and
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},\infty)
, then

|||H_{\nu}(A,X,B)|||\leq\left|\left|\left|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(A,X,B)\right|\right|\right|.

(1.2)

Obviously, if
A,B,X\in M_{n}
, such that
A,B
are positive definite, then for
\frac{1}{4}\leq\nu\leq\frac{3}{4}

and
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},\infty)
, and any unitarily invariant norm
|||.|||
, the following inequalities hold

|||A^{\frac{1}{2}}XB^{\frac{1}{2}}|||\leq|||H_{\nu}(A,X,B)|||\leq\left|\left|\left|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(A,X,B)\right|\right|\right|.

(1.3)

I. Ali, H. Yang and A. shakoor
[
1
]
gave a refinement of the inequality (
1.3
) as follows:

\displaystyle|||H_{\nu}(A,X,B)|||\leq(4r_{0}-1)|||A^{\frac{1}{2}}XB^{\frac{1}{2}}|||+2(1-2r_{0})\left|\left|\left|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(A,X,B)\right|\right|\right|.

where
\frac{1}{4}\leq\nu\leq\frac{3}{4}
,
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},\infty)
and
r_{0}=\min\{\nu,1-\nu\}
.

Heretofore the inequalities discussed above are proved in the setting of matrices.
Kapil and Singh in
[
3
]
, using the contractive maps proved that the relation (
1.2
) holds for positive definite operators
on a complex Hilbert space
H
.
They also proved that if
A,B
and
X
are bounded linear operators on
H
and
A,B
are
positive definite operators and
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},\infty)
, then

\left|\left|\left|A^{\frac{1}{2}}XB^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|\right|\right|\leq\left|\left|\left|\int_{0}^{1}A^{\nu}XB^{1-\nu}d\nu\right|\right|\right|\leq\left|\left|\left|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(A,X,B)\right|\right|\right|,

(1.4)

the function
|||\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}|||
is increasing for
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},\infty)
and

|||\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}|||\leq|||\mathcal{F}_{\frac{1}{2}}|||
for
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
.

Heinz and Heron inequalities involving unitarily invariant
norms have received renewed attention in recent years and a remarkable variety of refinements
and generalizations have been found (see, for example,
[
2
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
).

The aim of this paper is to obtain refinements or generalizations of the above mentioned inequalities in the setting of operators
for unitarily invariant norms.

Main results

Let
B(H)
denote the set of all bounded linear operators on a complex

To reach inequalities for bounded self-adjoint operators on Hilbert space, we shall use
the following monotonicity property for operator functions:

If
X\in B(H)
is self adjoint with a spectrum

on an interval containing

f(t)\geq g(t),~t\in Sp(X)\Rightarrow~f(X)\geq g(X).

(2.1)

For more details about this property, the reader is referred to

[

and
R_{Y}(T)=TY
. Since
L_{X}
and

e^{L_{X}+R_{Y}}(T)=e^{X}Te^{Y}.

Let
U
be an invertible positive operator in

such that
U=e^{V}
.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
A,B
be two invertible positive operators in

. To simplify computations,
we denote

and
B
by

and

where

and

.

Proposition 1
.

Let
0\leq s_{2}\leq s_{1}
and
r,r^{\prime}\leq\frac{s_{1}+s_{2}}{2}
or
s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq 0
and
r,r^{\prime}\geq\frac{s_{1}+s_{2}}{2}
, then the following
operator maps are contractive,

(1)

\frac{(1+t)\cosh(rD)}{\cosh(s_{1}D)+t\cosh(s_{2}D)}
, for
-1<t\leq 1
.

(2)

\frac{\alpha\cosh(rD)+(1-\alpha)\cosh(r^{\prime}D)}{\beta\cosh(s_{1}D)+(1-\beta)\cosh(s_{2}D)}
, for
0\leq\alpha\leq 1,~\frac{1}{2}\leq\beta
.

(3)

\frac{(1+t)\sinh(rD)}{r(\sinh(s_{1}D)+t\sinh(s_{2}D))}
, for
-1<t\leq 1
and
|s_{1}+s_{2}|\geq 2
.

(4)

\frac{\frac{\alpha}{r}\sinh(rD)+\frac{1-\alpha}{r^{\prime}}\sinh(r^{\prime}D)}{\beta\sinh(s_{1}D)+(1-\beta)\sinh(s_{2}D)}
, for
0\leq\alpha\leq 1,~\frac{1}{2}\leq\beta
and
|s_{1}+s_{2}|\geq 2
.

Proof.

(1)

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\frac{(1+t)\cosh(rD)}{\cosh(s_{1}D)+t\cosh(s_{2}D)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+t)\cosh(rD)}{\cosh(s_{1}D)+\cosh(s_{2}D)}\left(1-\frac{(1-t)\cosh(s_{2}D)}{\cosh(s_{1}D)+\cosh(s_{2}D)}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+t}{2}\frac{2\cosh(rD)}{\cosh(s_{1}D)+\cosh(s_{2}D)}\times\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1-t}{2}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{2\cosh(s_{2}D)}{\cosh(s_{1}D)+\cosh(s_{2}D)}\right)^{n}.

For each
T\in B(H)
, using part (2) of Corollary 2.5 of
[
3
]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\left|\left|\frac{(1+t)\cosh(rD)}{\cosh(s_{1}D)+t\cosh(s_{2}D)}T\right|\right|\right|\leq\frac{1+t}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1-t}{2}\right)^{n}|||T|||=|||T|||.

(2)

To prove part (2), we write

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\cosh(rD)+(1-\alpha)\cosh(r^{\prime}D)}{\beta\cosh(s_{1}D)+(1-\beta)\cosh(s_{2}D)}

\displaystyle=\alpha\frac{\cosh(rD)}{\beta\cosh(s_{1}D)+(1-\beta)\cosh(s_{2}D)}+(1-\alpha)\frac{\cosh(r^{\prime}D)}{\beta\cosh(s_{1}D)+(1-\beta)\cosh(s_{2}D)}

Now, using part (1), we obtain

\left|\left|\left|\frac{\cosh(rD)}{\beta\cosh(s_{1}D)+(1-\beta)\cosh(s_{2}D)}T\right|\right|\right|\\
=\left|\left|\left|\frac{\frac{1}{\beta}\cosh(rD)}{\cosh(s_{1}D)+(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)\cosh(s_{2}D)}T\right|\right|\right|\leq 1.

Similarly,

\left|\left|\left|\frac{\cosh(r^{\prime}D)}{\beta\cosh(s_{1}D)+(1-\beta)\cosh(s_{2}D)}T\right|\right|\right|\leq 1.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\left|\left|\left|\frac{\alpha\cosh(rD)+(1-\alpha)\cosh(r^{\prime}D)}{\beta\cosh(s_{1}D)+(1-\beta)\cosh(s_{2}D)}T\right|\right|\right|\leq 1.

A simple calculation shows that

\displaystyle\frac{(1+t)\sinh(rD)}{r(\sinh(s_{1}D)+t\sinh(s_{2}D))}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+t}{s_{1}+s_{2}}\frac{(s_{1}+s_{2})\sinh(rD)}{r(\sinh(s_{1}D)+\sinh(s_{2}D))}\left(1-\frac{(1-t)\sinh(s_{2}D)}{\sinh(s_{1}D)+\sinh(s_{2}D)}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+t}{s_{1}+s_{2}}\frac{(s_{1}+s_{2})\sinh(rD)}{r(\sinh(s_{1}D)+\sinh(s_{2}D))}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2s_{2}}{s_{1}+s_{2}}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{1-t}{2}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{(s_{1}+s_{2})\sinh(s_{2}D)}{s_{2}(\sinh(s_{1}D)+\sinh(s_{2}D))}\right)^{n}.

Using part (3) of Corollary 2.5 in
[
3
]
, and
|\frac{2s_{2}}{s_{1}+s_{2}}|\leq 1
, we deduce for each
T\in B(H)

\displaystyle\left|\left|\left|\frac{(1+t)\sinh(rD)}{r(\sinh(s_{1}D)+t\sinh(s_{2}D))}T\right|\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+t}{|s_{1}+s_{2}|}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1-t}{2}\right)^{n}|||T|||

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{|s_{1}+s_{2}|}|||T|||\leq|||T|||.

The proof of (4) is similar to (2) and we omitted it.
∎

Theorem 1
.

Let
A,X,B\in B(H)
and
A,B
be two positive definite operators.
Let
\frac{1}{2}\leq\beta\leq 1
and
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}

(1)

If
\frac{3}{8}\leq\nu\leq\frac{5}{8}

\displaystyle|||H_{\nu}(A,X,B)|||

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left|\left|(1-\beta)A^{\frac{1}{2}}XB^{\frac{1}{2}}+\beta H_{\frac{1}{4}}(A,X,B)\right|\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left|\left|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(A,X,B)\right|\right|\right|.

(2.2)

(2)

If
\frac{5}{16}\leq\nu\leq\frac{11}{16}

\displaystyle|||H_{\nu}(A,X,B)|||

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left|\left|(1-\beta)H_{\frac{3}{8}}(A,X,B)+\beta H_{\frac{1}{4}}(A,X,B)\right|\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left|\left|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(A,X,B)\right|\right|\right|.

(2.3)

Proof.

Put
T=A^{\frac{1}{2}}XB^{\frac{1}{2}}
, a calculus computation shows that

\displaystyle A^{\nu}XB^{1-\nu}+A^{1-\nu}XB^{\nu}=A^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}A^{\frac{1}{2}}XB^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{\frac{1}{2}-\nu}+A^{\frac{1}{2}-\nu}A^{\frac{1}{2}}XB^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=A^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}TB^{\frac{1}{2}-\nu}+A^{\frac{1}{2}-\nu}TB^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{(2\nu-1)X_{1}}Te^{(1-2\nu)Y_{1}}+e^{(1-2\nu)X_{1}}Te^{(2\nu-1)Y_{1}}

\displaystyle=e^{(2\nu-1)D}T+Te^{-(2\nu-1)D}=2\cosh((2\nu-1)D)T.

Hence, we can write the operator on the left hand side of the first inequality in (
(1)
) as

\displaystyle\frac{A^{\nu}XB^{1-\nu}+A^{1-\nu}XB^{\nu}}{2}=\cosh((2\nu-1)D)T

(2.4)

and the operator on the right hand side of the first inequality in (
(1)
) as

\displaystyle(1-\beta)A^{\frac{1}{2}}XB^{\frac{1}{2}}+\beta\frac{A^{\frac{1}{4}}XB^{\frac{3}{4}}+A^{\frac{3}{4}}XB^{\frac{1}{4}}}{2}=\left((1-\beta)+\beta\cosh\left(\frac{1}{2}D\right)\right)T.

Considering

\displaystyle\frac{\cosh((2\nu-1)D)}{(1-\beta)+\beta\cosh(\frac{1}{2}D)}=\frac{\frac{1}{\beta}\cosh((2\nu-1)D)}{\frac{(1-\beta)}{\beta}+\cosh(\frac{1}{2}D)}

we put

|t|\leq 1\Leftrightarrow\beta\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
Therefore, part (1) of Proposition
1
proves the first inequality in (
(1)
).

\left|\left|\left|(1-\beta)A^{\frac{1}{2}}XB^{\frac{1}{2}}+\beta H_{\frac{1}{4}}(A,X,B)\right|\right|\right|\\
\leq(1-\beta)|||A^{\frac{1}{2}}XB^{\frac{1}{2}}|||+\beta|||H_{\frac{1}{4}}(A,X,B)|||\\
\leq(1-\beta)|||\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(A,X,B)|||+\beta|||\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(A,X,B)|||.

(2.5)

Hence the proof of part (1) is completed.

Using the same arguments, we can show the operator on
the right hand side of the first inequality in (
(2)
) as

\displaystyle(1-\beta)\frac{A^{\frac{3}{8}}XB^{\frac{5}{8}}+A^{\frac{5}{8}}XB^{\frac{3}{8}}}{2}+\beta\frac{A^{\frac{1}{4}}XB^{\frac{3}{4}}+A^{\frac{3}{4}}XB^{\frac{1}{4}}}{2}

\displaystyle=\left((1-\beta)\cosh\left(\frac{1}{4}D\right)+\beta\cosh\left(\frac{1}{2}D\right)\right)T.

(2.6)

By part (1) of Proposition
1

\frac{\cosh((2\nu-1)D)}{(1-\beta)\cosh\left(\frac{1}{4}D\right)+\beta\cosh\left(\frac{1}{2}D\right)}

(2)
) holds. The Similar to relation (
2.5
),
the second inequality in (
(2)
) is proved.
Hence the proof is completed.

Theorem 2

Theorem 3
.

Let
A,X,B\in B(H)
and

be two positive definite operators. If

(2.9)

Proof.

By the relation (
(2)

\displaystyle H_{\nu}(A,X,B)=\cosh((2\nu-1)D)T.

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}\cosh\left(\frac{3}{4}D\right)+\frac{1}{2}\cosh\left(\frac{1}{4}D\right)\right)T,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}A^{\nu}XB^{1-\nu}d\nu=\int_{0}^{1}\exp((2\nu-1)D)Td\nu=(D^{-1}\sinh D)T,

(2.10)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}H_{\frac{1}{4}}(A,X,B)+\frac{1}{2}F_{\frac{1}{2}}(A,X,B)=\left(\frac{1}{2}\cosh\left(\frac{1}{2}D\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1+\cosh(D)}{2}\right)\right)T.

We get the first inequality in (
3

By part (3) of the Proposition 2.3 in

[

\displaystyle\frac{2\sinh D}{D\left(\cosh\left(\frac{1}{2}D\right)+\frac{1+\cosh(D)}{2}\right)}=\frac{2\sinh D}{D\left(\cosh\left(\frac{1}{2}D\right)+\cosh^{2}(\frac{1}{2}D)\right)}

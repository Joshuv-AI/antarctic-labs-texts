Global stabilization of the cubic defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation on the torus
Kévin Le Balc’h, Jérémy Martin

Abstract
In this article, we prove the (uniform) global exponential stabilization of the cubic defocusing Schrödinger equation on the torus ( ℝ / 2 ​ π ​ ℤ ) d (\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{d} , for d = 1 d=1 , 2 2 or 3 3 , with a linear damping localized in a subset of the torus satisfying some geometrical assumptions. In particular, this answers an open question of Dehman, Gérard, Lebeau from 2006. Our approach is based on three ingredients. First, we prove the well-posedness of the closed-loop system in Bourgain spaces. Secondly, we derive new Carleman estimates for the nonlinear equation by directly including the cubic term in the conjugated operator. Thirdly, by conjugating with energy estimates and Morawetz multipliers method, we then deduce quantitative observability estimates leading to the uniform exponential decay of the total energy of the system. As a corollary of the global stabilization result, we obtain an upper bound of the minimal time of the global null-controllability of the nonlinear equation by using a stabilization procedure and a local null-controllability result.

Introduction

1.1
Control of Schrödinger equations on compact manifolds

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a compact smooth connected boundaryless Riemannian
d
-dimensional manifold, for
d\in\{1,2,3\}
and
\Delta_{g}
be the Laplace Beltrami operator on
\mathcal{M}
associated to the metric

\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{T}^{d}=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{d}

We are interested in the cubic defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}u=-\Delta_{g}u+|u|^{2}u&\text{ in }(0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{M},\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}&\text{ in }\mathcal{M}.\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

This equation arises naturally in nonlinear optics, as a model of wave propagation in fiber optics. The function
u(t,x)\in\mathbb{C}
represents a wave and the nonlinear Schrödinger equation describes the propagation of the wave through a nonlinear medium. In this context, the metric
g
can be interpreted as an inhomogeneity of the optical index.

Formally, in (
1.1
), two quantities are conserved. First, by multiplying by
\overline{u}
the equation (
1.1
) and by taking the imaginary part, we observe that the
L^{2}
-energy is conserved, i.e.

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx\right)=0\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\geq 0.

(1.2)

Secondly, by multiplying by
\partial_{t}\overline{u}
the equation (
1.1
) and by taking the real part, we also observe that the nonlinear-energy (or
H^{1}
-energy) is conserved, i.e.

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u(t,x)|^{4}dx\right)=0\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\geq 0.

(1.3)

Concerning the (global) well-posedness of (
1.1
) for initial data in

, in the
1

[
Caz03
, Corollary 3.5.2]
. However, this strategy fails in the
d
-dimensional case (

), see for instance
[

, Corollary 3.5.2]

, one needs to use Strichartz-type estimates. For

, we have that (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in

for every

[
Bou93
]

[

[
BGT05a
]

We first introduce the controlled linear Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}u=-\Delta_{g}u+h1_{\omega}&\text{ in }(0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{M},\\

In (

Controllability for the linear Schrödinger equation has been started to be strongly investigated in the 1990’s. We recall the definitions of controllability and its dual notion, called observability.

Let
s\geq 0
and
T>0
.

The equation (
1.4
) is
exactly controllable
in
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
at time

if for every
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
and
u_{1}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
, there exists
h\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathcal{M}))
such that the mild solution
u
of (
1.4
) belongs to
C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathcal{M}))
and satisfies
u(T,\cdot)=u_{1}
.

The linear Schrödinger equation is
observable
in

at time

if there exists a constant

such that for every

\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{-s}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert e^{it\Delta}u_{0}1_{\omega}\right\rVert_{H^{-s}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}dt
.

The
Hilbert Uniqueness Method
(H.U.M.) relates the two previous notions, see for instance
[

. The controlled linear Schrödinger equation (
1.4

at time

In this direction, one of the most breakthrough result is from
[
Leb92
]
that guarantees that the so-called Geometric Control Condition (GCC) for the wave equation equation is sufficient for the exact controllability of the Schrödinger equation in any time
T>0
. The proof of this result is based on microlocal analysis. The GCC can be, roughly, formulated as follows, the subdomain
\omega
is said to
satisfy the GCC in time
T>0
if and only if all rays of Geometric Optics that propagate inside the domain reach the control set
\omega
in time less than

. A particular case of this result was proved previously by
[
Mac94
]
by multiplier techniques. One can also see
[
LTZ04
]
for the obtention of such results by using Carleman estimates. In
[
Phu01
]
, the author establishes the connections between the heat, wave and Schrödinger equations through suitable integral transformations called Fourier-Bros-Iagonitzer (FBI) transformations. This allows him to get, for instance, estimates on the cost of approximate controllability for the Schrödinger equation when the GCC is not satisfied and also on the dependence of the size of the control with respect to the control time. For the sphere
S^{d}
, by using explicit quasimodes that concentrate on the equator, one can prove that the GCC is necessary and sufficient for the observability. On the other hand, there a number of results showing that, in some situations in which the GCC is not fulfilled in any time, one can still achieve very satisfactory results for the Schrödinger equation. For instance, any nonempty open subset of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is sufficient for the observability then for the exact controllability of the Schrödinger equation, see
[
Jaf90
]
,
[
KL05
]
by using Ingham’s estimates or
[
AM14
]
by using semi-classical measures. Note that it has been recently extended to any nontrivial measurable subset of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
in
[
BZ19
]
with the crucial use of dispersive properties of the Schrödinger equation. For the unit disk

with Dirichlet boundary conditions, explicit eigenfunctions concentrate near the boundary so one can prove that the observability holds if and only if
\omega
contains a (small) part of the boundary

[
ALM16
]

]

Comments.
Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
differ from the existing literature. Indeed, Theorem
1.1
states the (uniform) global exponential stabilization of (
1.6
) by the feedback
h=-ia(x)u
in the energy space while Theorem
1.2
states the global null-controllability of (
1.6
) with an explicit estimate of the (possible) minimal time in function of the size of the initial data in the energy space. Up to our knowledge, they are the first results in this direction for nonlinear Schrödinger equations. The key point is hidden in the exponential decay (
1.12
) where the constants
C>0
,
\gamma>0
do not depend on the initial data, but only on the geometry of the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and the observation set
\omega
. In particular, this estimate answers by the affirmative the open problem stated in
[
DGL06
, Remark 1]
.

For proving Theorem
1.1
, we develop a new method in comparison to the above mentioned references. Our strategy is based on new quantitative observability inequalities for the cubic defocusing Schrödinger equation with the internal damping (
1.11
). The first ingredient for obtaining such inequalities is a new Carleman estimates for
(
1.11
). Instead of seeing
|u|^{2}u
as
V(t,x)u
with
V
a time/space-dependent potential then performing a Carleman estimate in a linear Schrödinger-type equation, we directly include the cubic semilinearity in the symmetric part of the Carleman conjugated operator. The internal linear damping
-ia(x)u
is then treated as a (local) source term that can be absorbed in the Carleman estimates. First, note that such a strategy only enables us to treat defocusing cases, that is strongly in contrast with
[
Lau10a
]
where the author can manage to deal with focusing cases. Second, one cannot handle internal linear dampings like
a(x)(1-\Delta)^{-1}a(x)\partial_{t}u
or
-ia(x)(-\Delta)^{1/2}u
that are nonlocal. The second ingredient is a combinaison of energy estimates and Morawetz multipliers method for obtaining the exponential decay of the energy of the solution to the damped equation. As a consequence, we completely bypass the classical use of propagation of compactness-regularity for tackling such a question. We strongly believe that such a method can have other applications to the problem of exponential stabilization of partial differential equations, for instance for semilinear wave equations as considered in
[
DLZ03
]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is a corollary of the global exponential stabilization from Theorem
1.1
and local controllability results from
[
Lau10a
, Theorem 3.2]
in
1
-d,
[
DGL06
, Proof of Theorem 2]
in
2
-d,
[
Lau10b
, Theorem 0.3]
in
3
-d.

In comparison to what is known for the linear case, where an arbitrary open set of the torus

Finally, concerning the null-controllability adressed in Theorem

2.1
Some properties of
X^{s,b}
spaces

The goal of this part is to state well-posedness results for (
1.11
) in Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. As said previously, it is an adaptation of the idea introduced in
[
Bou93
]
, see for instance
[
Bou99
, Chapter 5]
for a detailed account of these techniques.

In all the following, we use the notation

\langle x\rangle=\sqrt{1+x^{2}}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we equip the Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with the norm

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle|k|\rangle^{2s}|\hat{u}(k)|^{2}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}).

(2.2)

For
s,b\in\mathbb{R}
, the Bourgain space is equipped with the norm

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X^{s,b}}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle|k|\rangle^{2s}\langle\tau+|k|^{2}\rangle^{2b}|\hat{\hat{u}}(\tau,k)|^{2}d\tau=\left\lVert u^{\sharp}\right\rVert_{H^{b}(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}^{2},

(2.3)

where
u^{\sharp}(t)=e^{-it\Delta}u(t)
and
\hat{\hat{u}}(\tau,k)
denotes the Fourier transform with respect to the time variable and the spatial variable.

For
T>0
, the restricted Bourgain space
X_{T}^{s,b}
is the associated restriction space with the norm

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X_{T}^{s,b}}=\inf\{\left\lVert\tilde{u}\right\rVert_{X^{s,b}}\ ;\ \tilde{u}=u\ \text{in}\ (0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\}.

(2.4)

More generally, for
I
an interval in
\mathbb{R}
, one can define
X_{I}^{s,b}
the associated restriction space.

The following properties of the Bourgain spaces are straightforward.

\bullet
The Bourgain spaces
X^{s,b}
and
X_{T}^{s,b}
are Hilbert spaces.

\bullet
If
s_{1}\leq s_{2}
,
b_{1}\leq b_{2}
,
X^{s_{2},b_{2}}
is continously embedded in
X^{s_{1},b_{1}}
.

\bullet
For
b>1/2
,
X_{T}^{s,b}
is continously embedded in
C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
.

\bullet
For every
s_{1}<s_{2}
,
b_{1}<b_{2}
,
X_{T}^{s_{2},b_{2}}
is compactly embedded in
X_{T}^{s_{1},b_{1}}
.

\bullet
The dual of
X_{T}^{s,b}
is
X_{T}^{-s,-b}
.

\bullet
For
\theta\in(0,1)
, the complex interpolation space
(X^{s_{1},b_{1}},X^{s_{2},b_{2}})_{\theta}
is
X^{(1-\theta)s_{1}+\theta s_{2},(1-\theta)b_{1}+\theta b_{2}}
.

\bullet
If
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
b\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
0<T_{1}<T_{2}
,
u_{1}\in X^{s,b}_{(0,T_{1})}
and
u_{2}\in X^{s,b}_{(T_{1},T_{2})}
with
u_{1}(T_{1})=u_{2}(T_{1})
, then the function
u
defined by
u(t,\cdot)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{1}(t,\cdot),&t\in[0,T_{1}]\\
u_{2}(t,\cdot),&t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]\end{array}\right.
belongs to
X^{s,b}_{(0,T_{2})}
.

The following result studies the stability of the Bourgain spaces with respect to multiplication operators.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
b\in[-1,1]
,
T>0
. The following linear mappings

\displaystyle\Phi:u\in X^{s,b}\mapsto\varphi(t)u\in X^{s,b},\hskip 20.00003pt\Phi_{T}:u\in X_{T}^{s,b}\mapsto\varphi(t)u\in X_{T}^{s,b},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Psi:u\in X^{s,b}\mapsto\psi(x)u\in X^{s-|b|,b},\hskip 20.00003pt\Psi_{T}:u\in X_{T}^{s,b}\mapsto\psi(x)u\in X_{T}^{s-|b|,b},

(2.6)

are continuous.

Proof.

We only prove the first parts of (
2.5
) and (
2.6
).

By using the commutation of
e^{-it\Delta}
with
\varphi(t)
, we have

\left\|\varphi u\right\|_{X^{s,b}}=\left\|e^{-it\Delta}[\varphi(t)u]\right\|_{H_{t}^{b}(H_{x}^{s})}=\left\|\varphi u^{\#}\right\|_{H_{t}^{b}(H_{x}^{s})}\leq C\left\|u^{\#}\right\|_{H_{t}^{b}(H_{x}^{s})}\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}},

(2.7)

which concludes the proof of (
2.5
).

For (
2.6
), we first treat the two cases
b=0
and
b=1
.

For
b=0
,
X^{s,0}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{s})
and the result is obvious.

For
b=1
, we have
u\in X^{s,1}
if and only if
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{s})\textnormal{ and }i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{s})
, with the norm

\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{X^{s,1}}=\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{s})}+\left\|i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{s})}.

Then, we have, by using that the commutator
[\psi,\Delta]
is an operator of order
1
in space,

\displaystyle\left\|\psi(x)u\right\|^{2}_{X^{s-1,1}}=\left\|\psi u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{s-1})}+\left\|i\partial_{t}(\psi u)+\Delta(\psi u)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{s-1})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{s-1})}+\left\|\psi\left(i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u\right)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{s-1})}\right.\left.+\left\|\left[\psi,\Delta\right]u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{s-1})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{s-1})}+\left\|i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{s-1})}+\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{s})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{X^{s,1}},

that concludes the proof in the case
b=1
.

We finally conclude by interpolation and duality.
∎

F:\left|\begin{array}[]{lll}H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\times L^{2}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))&\to&X_{T}^{s,b}\\
(u_{0},g)&\mapsto&u,\end{array}\right.

(2.18)

is Lipschitz on every bounded subset.

Proof.

In Proposition
2.3
, we fix
s_{2}=s
and

. This gives us the existence of

such that (

) and (

) hold.

First, we notice that if

then

.

Now, let us fix

such that

and
b+b^{\prime}\leq 1
.

Step 1: Local existence in

.

We consider the functional

\Phi(u)(t)=e^{it\Delta}u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}[|u|^{2}u-ia(x)u+g](\tau)d\tau.

(2.19)

Thanks to a Banach fixed-point argument, we will prove that
\Phi
admits a unique fixed point in
X_{T}^{s,b}
provided that

is small enough.

Let

be such that

on

. Then we have

\left\lVert\psi(t)e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\right\rVert_{X^{s,b}}=\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{b}(\mathbb{R})}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

(2.20)

Therefore, for

, we have

\left\lVert e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\right\rVert_{X_{T}^{s,b}}\leq C\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

(2.21)

The estimate (
2.8
) from Lemma

then implies that

\left\lVert\psi(t/T)\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}F(\tau)d\tau\right\rVert_{X_{T}^{s,b}}\leq CT^{1-b-b^{\prime}}\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{X_{T}^{s,-b^{\prime}}}.

(2.22)

Then, by using the trilinear estimate (
2.9
) from Lemma
2.3
the multiplication estimate (
2.6
) from Lemma
2.1
and Bourgains spaces embeddings, we get

\displaystyle\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}[|u|^{2}u-ia(x)u+g](\tau)d\tau\right\rVert_{X_{T}^{s,b}}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{1-b-b^{\prime}}\left\lVert|u|^{2}u-ia(x)u+g\right\rVert_{X_{T}^{s,-b^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{1-b-b^{\prime}}\left(\left\lVert|u|^{2}u\right\rVert_{X^{s,-b^{\prime}}}+\left\lVert a(x)u\right\rVert_{X_{T}^{s,-b^{\prime}}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{X_{T}^{s,-b^{\prime}}}\right)

Similarly, by using this time the trilinear estimate (

) from Lemma

to itself, then admits a unique fixed-point. Moreover, we have uniqueness in the class

Let us take here

in

,

such that

\lim_{t\to T_{2}}\left\lVert u_{2}\right\rVert_{X_{t}^{s,b}}=+\infty\ \text{and}\ \left\lVert u_{2}\right\rVert_{X_{t}^{1,b}}\leq C\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\in[T_{2}-\delta,T_{2}).

(2.26)

By using the local existence in

i.e. Step 1 (for

) and gluing of solutions, we then get that there exists

Then by using (

) on

. Step 2 i.e. propagation of regularity then implies the global existence in

The local Lipschitz estimate on the flow is a straightforward consequence of (

3
Carleman estimate on the nonlinear equation

The goal of this part is to obtain a Carleman estimate for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
2.1
) and to deduce from it an observability inequality. In order to do this, we closely follow the approach of
[
MOR08
]
for establishing Carleman estimates for linear Schrödinger equation. We want to highlight the fact that the main difference is the presence of the cubic defocusing nonlinearity
-|u|^{2}u
, that we include in our operator. Note that such a strategy has been proposed for instance in the context of dissipative nonlinear parabolic equations in
[
BGO09
]
but up to our knowledge, this strategy seems to be new in the context of nonlinear Schrödinger equation.

3.1

Let us define
\omega_{1}\subset\subset\omega_{0}\subset\subset\omega
such that

(3.2)

First, we have the following easy lemma.

.

There exists

(3.3)

4.2.1
First step: study of the linear system.

Before studying the local controllability of nonlinear equation, let us consider the linear system

T=\gamma^{-1}\ln R+\gamma^{-1}\ln\left(\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)

\|u(T)\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq\sqrt{E(u(T))}\leq Ce^{-\gamma T}R\leq\varepsilon^{2}.

On the other hand, thanks to Proposition
4.3
, there exists a control
h_{2}\in\mathcal{C}([0,1],H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
supported in
[0,1]\times\overline{\omega}
such that the solution
\tilde{u}
of (
1.6
) started from
u(T)
satisfies
\tilde{u}(1,\cdot)=0
.

To conclude, it suffices to define the control by
h(t,\cdot)=h_{1}(t,\cdot)
on
[0,T]
and
h(t,\cdot)=h_{2}(t-T,\cdot)
on
[T,T+1]
. With this choice of control, the solution
u
of (
1.6
) satisfies
u(T+1,\cdot)=0
and
T+1
is a controllability time independent on
u_{0}
.
In particular,
\tau(R)\leq T+1\leq\tilde{C}\ln(R+1)

where
\tilde{C}
is a positive constant depending on
\gamma
and
C
.

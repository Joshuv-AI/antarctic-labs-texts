Enumeration of Dyck paths with air pockets
Jean-Luc Baril, Sergey Kirgizov, Rémi Maréchal, Vincent Vajnovszki

Abstract
We introduce and study the new combinatorial class of Dyck paths with air pockets. We exhibit a bijection with the peakless Motzkin paths which transports several pattern statistics and give bivariate generating functions for the distribution of patterns as peaks, returns and pyramids. Then, we deduce the popularities and asymptotic expectations of these patterns and point out a link between the popularity of pyramids and a special kind of closed smooth self-overlapping curves, a subset of Fibonacci meanders. A similar study is conducted for non-decreasing Dyck paths with air pockets.



Keywords:
Dyck path, pattern distribution/popularity, Fibonacci meander

U=(1,1)
(resp.

x
-axis,

Banderier and Wallner

In this paper, we introduce and study the paths with air pockets corresponding to a queue evolution with partial reset operations that cannot be consecutive. These paths can also be viewed as airplane flights, sometimes showcasing turbulences that are
known as

, where consecutive turbulences are considered to be one. More formally, a
Dyck path with air pockets
is a nonempty lattice path in the first quadrant of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
starting at the origin, ending on the

-axis, and consisting of up-steps

and down-steps

be the set of

\mathcal{A}_{0}=\mathcal{A}_{1}=\varnothing

\mathcal{A}=\bigcup_{n\geqslant 2}\mathcal{A}_{n}

Bijection with peakless Motzkin paths

In this section we exhibit a constructive bijection between
n
-length Dyck paths with air pockets and
(n-1)
-length peakless Motzkin paths, i.e. lattice paths in the first quarter plane starting at the origin, ending at
(n-1,0)

U
,
D

and having no occurrence of
UD
.
Moreover, we show how our bijection transports some pattern based statistics. We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{n}
the set of peakless Motzkin paths of length
n

\mathcal{M}=\bigcup_{n\geqslant 1}\mathcal{M}_{n}

Definition 1

We recursively define the map

from
\mathcal{A}
to

as follows. For

\psi(\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{llr}F&\text{if }\alpha=UD,&(i)\\
U\psi(\beta)D&\text{if }\alpha=\beta UD\text{ with }\beta\in\mathcal{A},&(ii)\\
\psi(\alpha^{\flat})F&\text{if }\alpha\in\mathcal{P},&(iii)\\
\psi(\gamma^{\flat})U\psi(\beta)D&\text{if }\alpha=\beta\gamma\text{ with }\beta\in\mathcal{A}\text{ and }\gamma\in\mathcal{P}.&(iv)\end{array}\right.

Notice that each factor in the above decomposition is nonempty, and that
\psi
maps nonempty objects to nonempty ones. Due to the recursive definition, the image by
\psi
of a
n
-length Dyck path with air pockets is a peakless Motzkin path of length
n-1
. For instance, the images of

,

,

Theorem 2
.

For all
n\geqslant 2
, the map
\psi
induces a bijection between
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
.

Proof.

It is well known that the cardinality of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is given by the
n
-th term of generalized Catalan number (see
A004148
in
[
22
]
). So it suffices (see observation after relation (
1
)) to prove the injectivity of
\psi
. We proceed by induction on
n
. The case
n=2
is obvious since
\mathcal{A}_{2}=\{UD\}
and
\mathcal{M}_{1}=\{F\}
.
For all
k\leqslant n
, we assume that
\psi
is an injection from
\mathcal{A}_{k}
to
\mathcal{M}_{k-1}
, and we prove the result for

According to Definition
1

\alpha
and

\mathcal{A}_{n+1}

\psi(\alpha)=\psi(\beta)
, then
\alpha
and
\beta

is injective and so bijective.
∎

Notice that the mirror of a Dyck path with air pockets is a Łukasiewicz path avoiding flat steps and two consecutive up-steps. Since there is a bijection between Łukasiewicz paths and plane trees (see

Theorem 4
.

Let
A(x,y,z)=\sum_{n,k,\ell\geqslant 0}a_{n,k,\ell}x^{n}y^{k}z^{\ell}
be the generating function (g.f.) where

is the number of paths in
\mathcal{A}_{n}
having

and

Proof.

Due to the first return decomposition, any Dyck path with air pockets has one of the following forms:
(i)

(ii)

(iii)

(\alpha UD)^{\sharp}\gamma
with
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
,
(iv)

\alpha^{\sharp}\gamma
with
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
not being
UD
nor
\beta UD
(

), where
\gamma\in\mathcal{A}\cup\{\varepsilon\}
. These four cases are disjoint and cover

and

,

A=\left(\underbrace{\vphantom{xy\left(A-x^{2}yz-x^{2}y^{2}zA\right)}x^{2}yz}_{(i)}+\underbrace{\vphantom{xy\left(A-x^{2}yz-x^{2}y^{2}zA\right)}x^{3}y^{2}}_{(ii)}+\underbrace{\vphantom{xy\left(A-x^{2}yz-x^{2}y^{2}zA\right)}x^{3}y^{2}A}_{(iii)}+\underbrace{xy\left(A-x^{2}yz(1+A)\right)}_{(iv)}\right)(1+A),

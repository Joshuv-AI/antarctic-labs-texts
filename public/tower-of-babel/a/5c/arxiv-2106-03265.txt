Introduction

In time frequency analysis, when one needs to analyse modulation or Wiener amalgam spaces with super-exponential weights, or more generally, to study
ultradistributions of quasi-analytical classes, kernels of the form
e^{s(1+|x|^{2})^{q/2}},q\geq 1
,
s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
are involved. Our aim is to show that it is possible to study convolution with such kernel beyond a very general theory we have developed in
[
17
]
.

The problem of when a distribution in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
admits a
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
-convolution with the Gaussian
e^{s|x|^{2}}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, was studied in
[
21
]
and the distributions that do satisfy this property were characterised by their growth behaviour. Later, the same problem was also solved in the setting of non-quasianalytic ultradistributions in
[
15
]
and applied in extending the Anti-Wick quantisation to more general symbol classes. Our aim in this article is to find conditions on the Gelfand-Shilov ultradistributions in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which are sufficient for the existence of convolution with the function
x\mapsto e^{s\langle x\rangle^{q}}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
,
q_{1}>q\geq 1
, where, as standard,
\langle x\rangle=(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}
(of course, when
q=2
,
e^{s\langle x\rangle^{2}}
is just a constant multiple of the Gaussian). Here
\{M_{p}\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Gevrey type sequence, bounded from below by
\{p!\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
, which controls the growth of the derivatives of the test functions.

The definition of the convolution of
S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
we employ is the following: we say
S
is convolvable with
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
if for every test function
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
(\varphi*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})S\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(the ultradistributional analogue of the Schwartz space
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
) and
S*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as

\langle S*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}},\varphi\rangle=\langle(\varphi*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})S,1\rangle

(see Subsection
1.1
for the reasons why this is indeed a well defined element of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
). When
q_{1}\leq 1
, this reduces to the definition of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}
-convolution of ultradistributions, see
[
17
, Definition 5.7 and Theorem 5.8]
(see also
[
6
,
14
,
9
,
4
]
) and is analogous to one of the equivalent formulations of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
-convolution in the distributional setting (see
[
19
,
20
]
; see also
[
1
,
5
,
12
,
13
]
).

When studying the convolution with the Gaussian, one of the key ingredients employed in
[
21
]
and
[
15
]
is the fact that
e^{s|x-t|^{2}}
splits into a product of two parts:
e^{s|x|^{2}}
and
e^{-2sxt+s|t|^{2}}
. However, when
q\neq 2
this is no longer the case, and thus we can not apply the same ideas from
[
21
]
and
[
15
]
. In fact, a significant part of Section
2
is devoted to finding good bounds on the growth of the derivatives of
e^{s\langle x\rangle^{q}}
before we provide sufficient conditions in Theorem
2.6
. It is important to emphasise that, when
q=2
, these are analogous to the ones given in the distributional setting as well as in the non-quasianalytic ultradistributional setting (see the remark at the very end of Section
2
).

In Section
3
we give also necessary conditions for the existence of the convolution with
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
, when
s>0
. When
q=1
they turn out to be the same as the sufficient conditions, and thus providing a characterisation of the ultradistributions in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which are convolvable with
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle}
,
s>0
.

The main results of the paper can be summarised as follows (see Theorem
2.6
and Corollaries
3.5
,
3.6
and
3.7
):

Let
q_{1}>q\geq 1
,
s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
and
S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{p!\}}_{\{p!^{1/{q_{1}}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

1. If

e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}e^{k\langle\cdot\rangle^{(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)}}S\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{p!\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,k\geq 0,

then the
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{p!\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}
-convolution of
S
and
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
exists.

2. When
q=1
, the
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{p!\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}
-convolution of
S
and
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle}
,
s>0
, exists if and only if
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle}S\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{p!\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

3. Let

S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{p!^{2-1/q}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If the

\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{p!^{2-1/q}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}
-convolution of
S
and
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
,
s>0
, exists then the
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{p!^{2-1/q}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}
-convolution of
S
and
e^{s^{\prime}\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
also exists for all
s^{\prime}<s
,
s^{\prime}\neq 0
.

Note that the Beurling ultradistribution spaces give even more flexibility in the analysis of these problems, so most of the results (with appropriate changes) can be easily transferred to the ultradistribution spaces of Beurling class.

1.
Preliminaries

Let
\{M_{p}\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of positive numbers satisfying
M_{0}=M_{1}=1
. Unless stated otherwise, we will always assume the following conditions on
\{M_{p}\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
(cf.
[
10
]
):

(M.1)

M_{p}^{2}\leq M_{p-1}M_{p+1}
,
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
;

(M.2)

\displaystyle M_{p}\leq c_{0}H^{p}\min_{0\leq q\leq p}\{M_{p-q}M_{q}\}
,
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
, for some
c_{0},H\geq 1
;

(M.5)
there exists
q>0
such that
M_{p}^{q}
is strongly
non-quasianalytic (see
[
10
]
), i.e., there exists
c_{0}\geq 1
such that
\sum_{j=p+1}^{\infty}{M_{j-1}^{q}}/{M_{j}^{q}}\leq c_{0}p{M_{p}^{q}}/{M_{p+1}^{q}}
,
\forall p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
;

(M.6)

p!\subset M_{p}
, i.e. there exist
C,L>0
such that
p!\leq CL^{p}M_{p}
,
\forall p\in\mathbb{N}
.

For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we set
M_{\alpha}=M_{|\alpha|}
. Note that the Gevrey sequences
\{p!^{\kappa}\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
, with
\kappa\geq 1
, satisfy all of the above conditions.

Let
q>0
. For every
h>0
, we denote by
\mathcal{S}^{M_{p},h}_{p!^{1/q},h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the Banach space of all
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for which the following norm is finite

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}h^{|\alpha|}\|e^{h|\cdot|^{q}}\partial^{\alpha}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}/M_{\alpha}<\infty.

Recall, the Gelfand-Shilov test space
\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
h\rightarrow 0^{+}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{S}^{M_{p},h}_{p!^{1/q},h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Its strong dual
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the space of Gelfand-Shilov ultradistributions; we refer to
[
2
,
8
,
17
]
for its properties. Next, for each
h>0
, let
\mathcal{D}^{M_{p},h}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the Banach space of all
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}h^{|\alpha|}\|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}/M_{\alpha}<\infty
, and define

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
h\rightarrow 0^{+}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{D}^{M_{p},h}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{d});

it is a complete barrelled
(DF)
-space (see
[
7
, Section 4.3]
). Fix
q>0
and denote by
\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{\{M_{p}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the closure of
\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in
\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the strong dual of
\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{\{M_{p}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. As
\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q^{\prime}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with
q^{\prime}>q
, is continuously and densely injected into
\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{\{M_{p}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the closure of
\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q^{\prime}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in
\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for any
q^{\prime}>0
. We mention several important properties of the space
\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{\{M_{p}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and refer to
[
7
, Section 4.3]
for the complete account. First,
\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{\{M_{p}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a complete barrelled
(DF)
-space and thus
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is an
(F)
-space. The strong dual of the latter is topologically isomorphic to
\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(i.e.
\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the strong bidual of
\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{\{M_{p}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
). Denote by
\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty},c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the space
\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
equipped with the topology of compact convex circled convergence from the duality
\langle\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rangle
. Then
\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty},c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a complete space whose topology is weaker then the original one,
\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{\{M_{p}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is continuously and densely injected into
\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty},c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the strong dual of
\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty},c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is topologically isomorphic to
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see
[
17
, Section 5]
).

1.1.
Convolution of Gelfand-Shilov ultradistributions

Let
0<q\leq 1
and
S,T\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}
-convolution of
S
and
T
is said to exists if
(S\otimes T)\varphi^{\Delta}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\varphi^{\Delta}(x,y)=\varphi(x+y)
. When this is the case,
S*T\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as

\displaystyle\langle S*T,\varphi\rangle={}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\langle(S\otimes T)\varphi^{\Delta},1\rangle_{\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty},c}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})},\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
1
denotes the constant one function; see the comments after
[
17
, Definition 5.7]
for the reason why
S*T
is indeed a continuous functional on
\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Furthermore, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
17
, Theorem 5.8 and Remark 5.9]

Let
0<q\leq 1
and
S,T\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

the
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}
-convolution of
S
and
T
exists;

(ii)

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
(\varphi*\check{T})S\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

(iii)

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
(\varphi*\check{S})T\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Furthermore, when the
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q}\}}
-convolution of
S
and
T
exists,

\displaystyle\langle S*T,\varphi\rangle={}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\langle(\varphi*\check{T})S,1\rangle_{\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty},c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}={}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\langle(\varphi*\check{S})T,1\rangle_{\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty},c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(1.1)

Our goal is to study the convolvability with
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
,
q\geq 1
. We will adopt Theorem
1.1

(ii)
as a definition for the existence of the convolution with
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
. More precisely:

Definition 1.2
.

Let
q_{1}>q\geq 1
and
s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
. The ultradistribution

S\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
admits a
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}
-convolution with the function
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
if
(\varphi\ast e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})S\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for all

\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. When this is the case,
S*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by

\displaystyle\langle S\ast e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}},\varphi\rangle:={}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\langle(\varphi*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})S,1\rangle_{\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty},c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(1.2)

To see that (
1.2
) indeed defines a continuous functional on
\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it is enough to prove that the mapping

\displaystyle\varphi\mapsto(\varphi*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})S,\quad\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(1.3)

is continuous (as
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the strong dual of
\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty},c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
; see the comments before this section). Clearly
\varphi\mapsto(\varphi*e^{s\langle x\rangle^{q}})S
,
\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, is continuous and thus has a closed graph. As
(\varphi\ast e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})S\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, its graph is closed in
\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Now the Ptak closed graph theorem
[
18

implies that (

) is continuous (
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Ptak space since it is an
(F)
-space; see
[
18
, Section 4.8, p. 162]
).

Remark 1.3
.

Let
q_{1}>q\geq 1
and
s>0
. If
S
is a measurable function which satisfies
e^{k\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}S\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\forall k>0
, then the ordinary convolution of
e^{k\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
and

exists. It is straightforward to verify that the
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}
-convolution also exists and they are the same. Similarly, when

S
is a measurable function such that
e^{-\varepsilon\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}S\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

, then both the ordinary and the
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}
-convolution of
S
and
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
exist and they coincide.

2.
Sufficient conditions for the convolution with
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
,
q\geq 1
,
s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}

The aim of this section is to give a sufficient condition for an ultradistribution
S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to be
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}
-convolvable with
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
, where
q_{1}>q\geq 1
and
s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
.

Before we start, we recall the following multidimensional variant of the Faà di Bruno formula.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
3
, Corollary 2.10]
)
Let
|\alpha|=n\geq 1
and
h(x_{1},...,x_{d})=f(g(x_{1},...,x_{d}))
with
g\in\mathcal{C}^{n}
in a neighbourhood of
x^{0}
and
f\in\mathcal{C}^{n}
in a neighbourhood of
y^{0}=g(x^{0})
. Then

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}h(x^{0})=\sum_{r=1}^{n}f^{(r)}(y^{0})\sum_{p(\alpha,r)}\alpha!\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\left(\partial^{\alpha^{(j)}}g(x^{0})\right)^{k_{j}}}{k_{j}!\left(\alpha^{(j)}!\right)^{k_{j}}},

where

\displaystyle p(\alpha,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{\left(k_{1},...,k_{n};\alpha^{(1)},...,\alpha^{(n)}\right)\Big|\,\mbox{for some}\,1\leq s\leq n,k_{j}=0\mbox{ and }\alpha^{(j)}=0

\displaystyle\mbox{for }1\leq j\leq n-s;\,k_{j}>0\mbox{ for }n-s+1\leq j\leq n;\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle 0\prec\alpha^{(n-s+1)}\prec...\prec\alpha^{(n)}\mbox{ are such that}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}=r,\,\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}\alpha^{(j)}=\alpha\Big\}.

In the above formula, the convention
0^{0}=1
is used. The relation
\prec
used in this proposition is defined in the following way (cf.
[
3
]
). We say that
\beta\prec\alpha
when one of the following holds:

(i)

|\beta|<|\alpha|
;

(ii)

|\beta|=|\alpha|
and
\beta_{1}<\alpha_{1}
;

(iii)

|\beta|=|\alpha|
,
\beta_{1}=\alpha_{1},...,\beta_{k}=\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k+1}<\alpha_{k+1}
for some
1\leq k<d
.

We need precise estimates for the growth of the derivatives of
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
. For this purpose we first give a bound for the derivatives of
\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For every
q\in\mathbb{R}
there exists
C\geq 1
such that

|\partial^{\alpha}\langle x\rangle^{q}|\leq C^{|\alpha|}\alpha!\langle x\rangle^{q-|\alpha|},\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}.

(2.1)

Proof.

We apply the Faá di Bruno formula (Proposition
2.1
) with
f(\rho)=\rho^{q}
and
g(x)=\langle x\rangle
to infer, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
,

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}\langle x\rangle^{q}|\leq\sum_{m=1}^{|\alpha|}\langle x\rangle^{q-m}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}|q-j|\right)\alpha!\sum_{p(\alpha,m)}\prod_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\frac{|\partial^{\alpha^{(j)}}\langle x\rangle|^{k_{j}}}{k_{j}!(\alpha^{(j)}!)^{k_{j}}}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}|q-j|\leq\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(|q|+j)\leq(\lfloor|q|\rfloor+m)!\leq 2^{|q|+m}\lfloor|q|\rfloor!m!.

Recall the bound (see
[
11
,
(0.1.1)
, p. 10]
)

|\partial^{\beta}\langle x\rangle|\leq 2^{|\beta|+1}|\beta|!\langle x\rangle^{1-|\beta|}\leq 2(2d)^{|\beta|}\beta!\langle x\rangle^{1-|\beta|},\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}.

(2.2)

Hence, we deduce

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}\langle x\rangle^{q}|\leq 2^{|q|}(2d)^{|\alpha|}\lfloor|q|\rfloor!\alpha!\langle x\rangle^{q-|\alpha|}\sum_{m=1}^{|\alpha|}4^{m}m!\sum_{p(\alpha,m)}\prod_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\frac{1}{k_{j}!}.

Now,
[
16
, Lemma 7.4]
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{|\alpha|}m!\sum_{p(\alpha,m)}\prod_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\frac{1}{k_{j}!}\leq 2^{|\alpha|(d+1)}.

(2.3)

Plugging this in the above estimate, we deduce (
2.1
).
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
. Then for every
q>0
there exists
C\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}e^{s\langle x\rangle^{q}}|\leq C^{|\alpha|}\alpha!e^{s\langle x\rangle^{q}}\sum_{m=1}^{|\alpha|}\frac{\langle x\rangle^{qm-|\alpha|}}{m!},\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\backslash\{0\}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
. We apply the Faá di Bruno formula (Proposition
2.1
) with
f(\rho)=e^{s\rho}
and
g(x)=\langle x\rangle^{q}
together with Lemma
2.2
to infer (
C^{\prime}_{q}\geq 1
is the constant from this lemma)

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}e^{s\langle x\rangle^{q}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha!\sum_{m=1}^{|\alpha|}|s|^{m}e^{s\langle x\rangle^{q}}\sum_{p(\alpha,m)}\prod_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\frac{C^{\prime|\alpha^{(j)}|k_{j}}_{q}\langle x\rangle^{qk_{j}-|\alpha^{(j)}|k_{j}}}{k_{j}!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha!((|s|+1)C^{\prime}_{q})^{|\alpha|}e^{s\langle x\rangle^{q}}\sum_{m=1}^{|\alpha|}\langle x\rangle^{qm-|\alpha|}\sum_{p(\alpha,m)}\prod_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\frac{1}{k_{j}!}.

Now, the claim in the lemma follows from (
2.3
).
∎

As an immediate consequence we have the following result.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
. Then for every
q>0
there exists
C\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}e^{s\langle x\rangle^{q}}|\leq C^{|\alpha|}\alpha!e^{s\langle x\rangle^{q}+\langle x\rangle^{q-1}},\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}.

Before we prove the main result of this section, we need a technical lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

For all
x,t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
q\geq 1
,
s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, the following inequality holds true:

\displaystyle s\langle x-t\rangle^{q}-s\langle x\rangle^{q}\leq q2^{q-1}|s||t|\langle x\rangle^{q-1}+q2^{q-1}|s||t|\langle t\rangle^{q-1}.

(2.4)

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\geq 0
. By the mean value theorem, there exists
\lambda^{\prime}
between
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
such that

\displaystyle s\langle\lambda_{1}\rangle^{q}-s\langle\lambda_{2}\rangle^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle sq(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\lambda^{\prime}\langle\lambda^{\prime}\rangle^{q-2}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q|s||\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}|\langle\max\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}\rangle^{q-1}.

(2.6)

We apply (
2.6
) with
\lambda_{1}=|x-t|
and
\lambda_{2}=|x|
. If
|x|\geq|x-t|
, we infer

\displaystyle s\langle x-t\rangle^{q}-s\langle x\rangle^{q}\leq q|s||t|\langle x\rangle^{q-1}.

If
|x|\leq|x-t|
, then (
2.6
) together with the inequality
\langle x-t\rangle^{q-1}\leq 2^{q-1}\langle x\rangle^{q-1}+2^{q-1}\langle t\rangle^{q-1}
gives (
2.4
).
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
q_{1}>q\geq 1
,
s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
and
S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/{q_{1}}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If

\displaystyle e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}e^{k\langle\cdot\rangle^{(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)}}S\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,k\geq 0,

(2.7)

then the
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}
-convolution of
S
and
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
exists.

Remark 2.7
.

Notice that
e^{k\langle\cdot\rangle^{(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)}}
is a multiplier for
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
because
(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)<q<q_{1}
and Corollary
2.4
.

Remark 2.8
.

When
q>1
, (
2.7
) is equivalent to the following seemingly weaker condition

\displaystyle e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}e^{k\langle\cdot\rangle^{(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)}}S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{(q_{1}-1)/(q_{1}(q-1))}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,k\geq 0.

(2.8)

To see this, denote
q^{\prime}=(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)<q_{1}
and assume that (
2.8
) holds. As

\displaystyle e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}e^{k\langle\cdot\rangle^{q^{\prime}}}S=e^{-\langle\cdot\rangle^{q^{\prime}}}(e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}e^{(k+1)\langle\cdot\rangle^{q^{\prime}}}S),

and
e^{-\langle\cdot\rangle^{q^{\prime}}}\in\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q^{\prime}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(because of Corollary
2.4
) it is enough to prove that for every
\psi\in\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q^{\prime}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
T\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q^{\prime}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\psi T\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
\varphi\mapsto\psi\varphi
,
\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q^{\prime}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, is continuous, the mapping
\varphi\mapsto\langle T,\psi\varphi\rangle
,
\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{\{M_{p}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, is continuous, and
\psi T
coincides with it on the dense subspace
\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q^{\prime}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of
\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{\{M_{p}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Consequently,
\psi T\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be arbitrary but fixed. There exists
C,\eta>0
such that
|\varphi(x)|\leq Ce^{-\eta\langle x\rangle^{q_{1}}}
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Corollary
2.4
implies

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}\varphi*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}(x)|\leq CC_{1}^{|\alpha|}\alpha!e^{2^{q}\langle x\rangle^{q-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\eta\langle t\rangle^{q_{1}}}e^{s\langle x-t\rangle^{q}}e^{2^{q}\langle t\rangle^{q-1}}dt.

Denote
\lambda=(\eta/(4^{q}q|s|))^{1/(q-1)}
when
q>1
and
\lambda=1
when
q=1
. Lemma
2.5
gives

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}\varphi*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}(x)|\leq CC_{1}^{|\alpha|}\alpha!e^{2^{q}\langle x\rangle^{q-1}}e^{s\langle x\rangle^{q}}(I_{1}+I_{2}),

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|t|\leq(|x|/\lambda)^{(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}}e^{-\eta\langle t\rangle^{q_{1}}}e^{q2^{q-1}|s||t|\langle x\rangle^{q-1}}e^{q2^{q-1}|s|\langle t\rangle^{q}}e^{2^{q}\langle t\rangle^{q-1}}dt,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|t|\geq(|x|/\lambda)^{(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}}e^{-\eta\langle t\rangle^{q_{1}}}e^{q2^{q-1}|s||t|\langle x\rangle^{q-1}}e^{q2^{q-1}|s|\langle t\rangle^{q}}e^{2^{q}\langle t\rangle^{q-1}}dt.

Notice that

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq e^{q2^{q-1}|s|\lambda^{-(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}\langle x\rangle^{(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)}}\|e^{-\eta\langle\cdot\rangle^{q_{1}}}e^{q2^{q-1}|s|\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}e^{2^{q}\langle\cdot\rangle^{q-1}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

To estimate
I_{2}
, let
|t|\geq(|x|/\lambda)^{(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}
. When
q>1
, we infer

\displaystyle|t|\langle x\rangle^{q-1}\leq 2^{q}|t|+2^{q}|t||x|^{q-1}\leq 2^{q}|t|+2^{q}\lambda^{q-1}|t|^{q_{1}},

which implies

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\|e^{-(\eta/2)\langle\cdot\rangle^{q_{1}}}e^{q2^{2q-1}|s|\langle\cdot\rangle}e^{q2^{q-1}|s|\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}e^{2^{q}\langle\cdot\rangle^{q-1}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

When
q=1
, one immediately deduces
I_{2}\leq e^{2}\|e^{-\eta\langle\cdot\rangle^{q_{1}}}e^{2|s|\langle\cdot\rangle}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. Consequently, there exists
c_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}\varphi*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}(x)|\leq C_{2}C_{1}^{|\alpha|}\alpha!e^{s\langle x\rangle^{q}}e^{c_{1}\langle x\rangle^{(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)}},\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}.

Corollary
2.4
now verifies that
e^{-s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}e^{-(c_{1}+1)\langle\cdot\rangle^{(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)}}(\varphi*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})\in\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which, in turn, gives

\displaystyle(\varphi*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})S=\left(e^{-s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}e^{-(c_{1}+1)\langle\cdot\rangle^{(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)}}(\varphi*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})\right)\left(e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}e^{(c_{1}+1)\langle\cdot\rangle^{(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)}}S\right)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

∎

Remark 2.9
.

Notice that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
h\rightarrow 0^{+}\end{subarray}}\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{M_{p},h}_{p!^{1/q_{1}},h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{M_{p},h}_{p!^{1/q_{1}},h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the Banach space of all
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}h^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{|\beta|}\partial^{\alpha}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}/(M_{\alpha}\beta!^{1/q_{1}})<\infty.

Thus, if
\{M_{p}\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the non-quasianalyticity condition
\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}M_{p}^{-1/p}<\infty
, one can consider the case when
q_{1}=\infty
as well; in fact
\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{0}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is exactly
\mathcal{D}^{\{M_{p}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, i.e. the space of compactly supported ultradifferentiable functions of class
\{M_{p}\}
(see
[
10
]
). In this case (and when
\{M_{p}\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
additionally satisfies the condition
(M.3)
from
[
10
]
), when
q=2
, the condition (
2.7
), which boils down to
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{2}}e^{k\langle\cdot\rangle}S\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\forall k\geq 0
, is known to be both necessary and sufficient for the existence of the
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}
-convolution with the Gaussian
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{2}}
; see
[
15
, Theorem 4.3
(a)
]
. (See also
[
21
]
for the necessary and sufficient conditions for existence of convolution with the Gaussian
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{2}}
in the setting of Schwartz distributions.)

3.
Necessary conditions for the convolution with
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
,
q\geq 1
,
s>0

We now turn our attention to finding necessary conditions for the existence of the
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}
-convolution with
e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
. We start with two technical results.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
q_{1}>q\geq 1
,
s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
and
g
be a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which satisfies the following: there exist
\eta_{1},\eta_{2},C^{\prime}_{1},C^{\prime}_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{1}e^{-\eta_{1}\langle x\rangle^{q_{1}}}\leq g(x)\leq C^{\prime}_{2}e^{-\eta_{2}\langle x\rangle^{q_{1}}},\quad\mbox{a.e.}

(3.1)

Then for every
k>0
there exists
c,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle(g(\varepsilon\,\cdot)*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})(x)\geq ce^{s\langle x\rangle^{q}}e^{k\langle x\rangle^{(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)}},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

Notice first that when
q=1
the claim immediately follows from

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle-\langle t\rangle\leq\langle x-t\rangle\leq\langle x\rangle+\langle t\rangle,\quad\mbox{for all }\,\,x,t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

So, throughout the rest of the proof we assume
q>1
; additionally, we denote by
B(x,r)
the closed ball with centre at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and radius
r>0
. Assume first
s>0
and let
k>0
be arbitrary but fixed. Pick
k_{1}\geq 1+4(k+1)/(qs)
and set
\varepsilon=1/(4^{q_{1}}(\eta_{1}+1)k_{1})
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
|x|\geq 2
, be arbitrary but fixed. For every
t\in B(-k_{1}|x|^{-1+(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}x,1)
, we infer

\displaystyle|x-t|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|x+k_{1}|x|^{-1+(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}x|-|k_{1}|x|^{-1+(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}x+t|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|x|+k_{1}|x|^{(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}-1\geq|x|+(k_{1}-1)|x|^{(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}.

We apply the equality (
2.5
) with
\lambda_{1}=|x-t|
,
\lambda_{2}=|x|
. Because of the above,
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}
and thus
\lambda^{\prime}\langle\lambda^{\prime}\rangle^{q-2}\geq\langle x\rangle^{q-1}/2
. We infer

\displaystyle s\langle x-t\rangle^{q}\geq s\langle x\rangle^{q}+(qs(k_{1}-1)/2)|x|^{(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}\langle x\rangle^{q-1}\geq s\langle x\rangle^{q}+(k+1)\langle x\rangle^{(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)},

(3.2)

for all
t\in B(-k_{1}|x|^{-1+(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}x,1)
. On the other hand, with
t
as before, we have

\displaystyle\eta_{1}\langle\varepsilon t\rangle^{q_{1}}\leq 2^{q_{1}}\eta_{1}\langle\varepsilon\rangle^{q_{1}}+2^{q_{1}}\eta_{1}\langle\varepsilon k_{1}|x|^{(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}\rangle^{q_{1}}\leq 8^{q_{1}}\eta_{1}+\langle x\rangle^{(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)}.

Thus, denoting by
\omega_{d}
the measure of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we deduce

\displaystyle(g(\varepsilon\,\cdot)*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{1}\int_{B(-k_{1}|x|^{-1+(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}x,1)}e^{-\eta_{1}\langle\varepsilon t\rangle^{q_{1}}}e^{s\langle x-t\rangle^{q}}dt

(3.3)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\omega_{d}C^{\prime}_{1}e^{-8^{q_{1}}\eta_{1}}e^{s\langle x\rangle^{q}}e^{k\langle x\rangle^{(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)}}.

Hence, the above holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
|x|\geq 2
. As
g(\varepsilon\,\cdot)*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
is positive and continuous, this holds on all of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as well possibly with a different constant in place of
\omega_{d}C^{\prime}_{1}e^{-8^{q_{1}}\eta_{1}}
.

Assume now
s<0
. Let
k>0
be arbitrary but fixed. Pick
k_{1}\geq 1+4^{q}(k+1)/(q|s|)
and set
\varepsilon=1/(4^{q_{1}}(\eta_{1}+1)k_{1})
. There exists
c^{\prime}\geq 4
such that
k_{1}|x|^{-1+(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}\leq 1/2
, for all
|x|\geq c^{\prime}
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
|x|\geq c^{\prime}
, be arbitrary but fixed. For
t\in B(k_{1}|x|^{-1+(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}x,1)
we infer

\displaystyle|x-t|\leq|x|(1-k_{1}|x|^{-1+(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)})+1=|x|-k_{1}|x|^{(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}+1,

and thus
|x|\geq|x-t|+(k_{1}-1)|x|^{(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}
. Furthermore

\displaystyle|x-t|\geq|x|(1-k_{1}|x|^{-1+(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)})-1\geq|x|/2-1\geq|x|/4

(the very last inequality follows from
|x|\geq c^{\prime}\geq 4
). We apply (
2.5
) with
\lambda_{1}=|x-t|
and
\lambda_{2}=|x|
. Because of the above,
\lambda^{\prime}\langle\lambda^{\prime}\rangle^{q-2}\geq\langle x-t\rangle^{q-1}/2
and consequently

\displaystyle s\langle x-t\rangle^{q}-s\langle x\rangle^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q|s|(|x|-|x-t|)\lambda^{\prime}\langle\lambda^{\prime}\rangle^{q-2}\geq q|s|((k_{1}-1)/2)|x|^{(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}\langle x/4\rangle^{q-1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(k+1)\langle x\rangle^{(q-1)q_{1}/(q_{1}-1)},

for all
t\in B(k_{1}|x|^{-1+(q-1)/(q_{1}-1)}x,1)
. Now, similarly as above, one deduces the validity of (
3.3
) for all
|x|\geq c^{\prime}
. As
g(\varepsilon\,\cdot)*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
is positive and continuous this implies the claim in the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
q_{1}>q\geq 1
,
s>0
and
g
be a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which satisfies the following:

\displaystyle\mbox{there exists}\,\,\,C^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}>0\,\,\,\mbox{such that}\,\,\,0\leq g(x)\leq C^{\prime}e^{-\eta^{\prime}\langle x\rangle^{q_{1}}},\quad\mbox{a.e.}

\displaystyle\mathrm{measure}(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}|\,g(x)>0\})>0.

(i)

There exists
C>1
such that

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}(g*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})(x)|\leq C^{|\alpha|+1}\alpha!\langle x\rangle^{(q-1)|\alpha|}(g*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})(x),\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}.

(ii)

There exists
C>1
such that

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}(1/(g*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})(x))|\leq C^{|\alpha|+1}\alpha!\langle x\rangle^{(q-1)|\alpha|}/(g*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})(x),\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}.

Proof.

The condition on
g
, as well as the fact that
s>0
, imply

\displaystyle(g*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(t)e^{s\langle x-t\rangle^{q}}dt\geq\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}>0,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

To prove
(i)
we apply Lemma
2.3
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
to infer

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}(g*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})(x)|\leq C^{|\alpha|}\alpha!(I_{1}+I_{2}),\quad\mbox{where}

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int_{|t|\leq|x|}g(t)e^{s\langle x-t\rangle^{q}}\sum_{m=1}^{|\alpha|}\frac{\langle x-t\rangle^{qm-|\alpha|}}{m!}dt,\quad I_{2}=\int_{|t|\geq|x|}g(t)e^{s\langle x-t\rangle^{q}}\sum_{m=1}^{|\alpha|}\frac{\langle x-t\rangle^{qm-|\alpha|}}{m!}dt.

When
|t|\leq|x|
,

\displaystyle\langle x-t\rangle^{qm-|\alpha|}\leq\langle x-t\rangle^{(q-1)|\alpha|}\leq 2^{(q-1)|\alpha|}\langle x\rangle^{(q-1)|\alpha|};

whence
I_{1}\leq e2^{(q-1)|\alpha|}\langle x\rangle^{(q-1)|\alpha|}(g*e^{\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})(x)
. To estimate
I_{2}
, we proceed as follows

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\int_{|t|\geq|x|}g(t)e^{s\langle x-t\rangle^{q}}e^{\langle x-t\rangle^{q-1}}dt\leq\int_{|t|\geq|x|}g(t)e^{s(2\langle t\rangle)^{q}}e^{(2\langle t\rangle)^{q-1}}dt\leq\|ge^{s2^{q}\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}e^{2^{q-1}\langle\cdot\rangle^{q-1}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Now,
(i)
follows from the fact that
g*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle}
is bounded from below by a positive constant.

To prove
(ii)
we apply the Faá di Bruno formula (Proposition
2.1
) to the composition of the functions
\rho\mapsto 1/\rho
with
x\mapsto(g*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})(x)
. The first part of the lemma gives

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}(1/(g*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})(x))|\leq\alpha!\sum_{m=1}^{|\alpha|}\frac{C^{|\alpha|+m}m!\langle x\rangle^{(q-1)|\alpha|}}{(g*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})(x)}\sum_{p(\alpha,m)}\prod_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\frac{1}{k_{j}!}.

Now, the bound in
(ii)
follows from (
2.3
).
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Under the assumptions of the above lemma, by applying Corollary
2.4
, it is straightforward to verify that

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}(g*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})(x)|\leq C^{|\alpha|+1}\alpha!e^{s^{\prime}\langle x\rangle^{q}},\quad\mbox{for all},\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d},

for some
C>1
,
s^{\prime}>s
; i.e.
g*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}}
is a multiplier for
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Now we are ready to prove the main results of this section; recall that
p!^{\kappa}\subset M_{p}
means: there exist
C,L>0
such that
p!^{\kappa}\leq CL^{p}M_{p}
,
\forall p\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
q_{1}>q\geq 1
,
s>0
and
g
a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which satisfies (
3.1
).

(i)

Assume
p!^{2-1/q}\subset M_{p}
. If
S\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{\{p!^{1/q_{1}}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies
(g(\varepsilon\,\cdot)*e^{s\langle\cdot\rangle^{q}})S\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime\{M_{p}\}}_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

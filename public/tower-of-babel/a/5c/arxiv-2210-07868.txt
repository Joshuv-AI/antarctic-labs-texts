Dead ends and rationality of complete growth series
Pierre Alderic Bagnoud, Corentin Bodart

Abstract
We are interested in the ℕ ​ G \mathbb{N}G -rationality and ℕ ​ G \mathbb{N}G -algebraicity of the complete growth series of finitely generated groups. It is shown that dead ends of arbitrarily large depths form an obstruction to ℕ ​ G \mathbb{N}G -rationality. In the case of the 3 3 -dimensional Heisenberg group H 3 ​ ( ℤ ) H_{3}(\mathbb{Z}) , we prove that the complete series is not ℕ ​ G \mathbb{N}G -algebraic for any generating set. Dead ends are also used to show that complete growth series of higher Heisenberg groups are not ℕ ​ G \mathbb{N}G -rational for specific generating sets. Using a more general version of this obstruction, we prove that complete growth series of some lamplighter groups are not ℕ ​ G \mathbb{N}G -rational either. This work provides the first examples of groups exhibiting a difference between rationality of standard growth series, and rationality of complete growth series.



The idea of geometric group theory is to see a group
G
as a geometric space: fix a finite symmetric generating set
S
, and endow
G
with the word metric. Once this is done, we may be interested in the growth of the group, specifically the sequence
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
counting elements
g\in G
at distance
\left\|g\right\|_{S}=n
of the identity. This sequence can be looked at under a “coarse” lens, and turned into a quasi-isometry invariant. In another direction, we may study finer properties of the sequence. This is usually done through the
“numerical” growth series
of
G

G_{\mathrm{num}}(s)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\cdot s^{n}\in\mathbb{N}[[s]].

In this paper, we will be interested in an even richer sequence, namely
A_{n}=\sum_{\left\|g\right\|=n}g
, seen as elements of the group semiring
\mathbb{N}G
. Just as in the standard case, the sequence
(A_{n})_{n}
gives rise to a growth series: the
complete growth series
of
G

G(s)=\sum_{n\geq 0}A_{n}\cdot s^{n}\in\mathbb{N}G[[s]].

Note that both constructions are strongly related. Indeed, there exists an
augmentation map

\epsilon\colon\mathbb{N}G\to\mathbb{N}
, defined as the morphism sending all group elements to
1
. This morphism naturally extends into a morphism
\epsilon\colon\mathbb{N}G[[s]]\to\mathbb{N}[[s]]
. Observe that
\epsilon(A_{n})=a_{n}
hence
\epsilon(G(s))=G_{\mathrm{num}}(s)
.

A series with coefficients in a semiring
R
is
R
-rational
if it satisfies a certain system of linear equations (see §
1
for a proper definition). Similarly, the series is
R
-algebraic
if it satisfies a certain system of polynomial equations.

The interest of rationality for numerical growth series is that their coefficients are relatively easy to compute: they satisfy a linear recurrence equation. Moreover precise asymptotics are known. Similarly, knowing that the complete growth series of a group is
\mathbb{Z}G
-rational says that the list of elements of length
n
satisfies some linear recurrence relations. In some sense, being
\mathbb{N}G
-rational is even computationally better. (See Remark
1.10
for a discussion.)

Proving complete growth series of groups are
\mathbb{N}G
-rational (and sometimes computing them) has been central since their introduction. Indeed, the two thesis of Liardet and Nagnibeda which introduced complete growth series contains strong positive results. On the one hand Liardet builds on the work of Klarner and Benson. He proved the following:

Theorem

(Liardet
[
14
]
)

The complete growth series of a virtually abelian group is
\mathbb{N}G

On the other hand Grigorchuk and Nagnibeda extended results of Cannon and Gromov on rationality of numerical growth series of Gromov hyperbolic groups:

Theorem

(Grigorchuk–Nagnibeda
[
11
]
)

The complete growth series of a Gromov hyperbolic group is
\mathbb{N}G
-rational for any finite generating set.

Let us also point to

15
]
for rationality of complete growth series of Coxeter groups w.r.t. Coxeter generating sets, and
[
1
]
for stability of rationality w.r.t. diverse group constructions.

It should be noted our methods are not able to distinguish between

Let us summarize how those results are obtained: The obstruction to
\mathbb{N}G
-rationality relies on existence of dead ends of arbitrarily large depths (aka
deep pockets
) - or related “saddle elements” - in the group
(G,S)
of interest. Informally, a dead end of depth
D
is an element
g\in G
so that no longer element can be found in a ball of radius
D
around
g
. The existence of groups with dead ends of arbitrarily large depth is not trivial, and was open for several years, until the first examples were given by
[
7
]
. Here is a version of this obstruction:

Theorem 1

(Corollary
2.7
)
.

Let
G
be an infinite group and
S
a finite generating set. If
(G,S)
has dead ends of arbitrarily large depth, then its complete growth series is not
\mathbb{N}G
-rational.

This will apply to a large array of groups, in particular to the discrete Heisenberg group
H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})=\left\langle x,y\mid[x,y]\text{ central}\right\rangle
. The growth of
H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
has been under a lot of attention, culminating in results of Duchin and Shapiro
[
10
]
showing that its numerical growth series is rational (indeed
\mathbb{N}
-rational) w.r.t. any symmetric generating set. On the other hand, deep pockets were already exhibited by Warshall
[
24
]
. This already implies that complete growth series for
H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
is not
\mathbb{N}G
-rational for all generating set. We extend Warshall’s results showing that
all
large commutators are
almost
dead ends of large depths, more precisely

Theorem 2

(Theorem
3.13
(a))
.

Fix
S
a finite symmetric generating set for
H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
. There exist constants
C,M
such that, for all
D\gg 1
and
n\geq C\cdot D^{4}
, the element
g=[x,y]^{n}
satisfies

\forall h\in H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})\text{ such that }\left\|h\right\|_{S}\leq D,\hskip 20.00003pt\left\|gh\right\|_{S}\leq\left\|g\right\|_{S}+M.

With additional arguments from Carnot-Caratheodory geometry we prove

Theorem 3

(Theorem
5.3
)
.

The complete growth series of the Heisenberg group
H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
is not
\mathbb{N}G
-algebraic for all finite symmetric generating set.

In the case of higher Heisenberg groups
H_{2n+1}(\mathbb{Z})
, Stoll
[
22
]
has shown that the numerical growth series of
H_{5}(\mathbb{Z})
w.r.t. the standard generating set is transcendental (i.e., not
\mathbb{Z}
-algebraic), while for all

the growth series of

is
\mathbb{N}
-rational w.r.t. better-behaved “cubical” generating sets. On the other side, we establish a version of Theorem

(b); see §

w.r.t. any cubical-like generating set is not
\mathbb{N}G
-rational. Work in progress of the second author shows that complete growth series of
2
-step nilpotent groups are never
\mathbb{N}G
-rational, unless the group is virtually abelian.

Other groups of interest are wreath products
L\wr Q
. We will only be interested in generating set of the form
S_{L}\sqcup S_{Q}
. Deep pockets can be found in any group
L\wr\mathbb{Z}
where

)

7
]
. This result was recently extended to wreath products

with

, and even any group and at least one generating set

[

. However, as soon as the lamp group

), or for specific (non-abelian) base group

), the group

doesn’t have deep pockets. This led us to introduce “saddle elements” (see §2 for a definition), which then allowed us to extend our techniques to some groups without dead ends. In particular

Theorem 4

(Theorem

)
.

Let
L
be a non-trivial group and
S_{L}
a symmetric generating set. The complete growth series of the wreath product

is not
\mathbb{N}G
-rational.

This is to be compared with

Theorem

(Bartholdi
[
2
, Theorem C.3]
)
.

Let
L
be a group and
S_{L}
a symmetric generating set. Suppose that the complete growth series of the pair

\mathbb{N}G

For instance both results hold for

1
Rationality and Algebraicity

In this section, we recall the definition of
R
-rational and
R
-algebraic formal series, where
R
is a fixed semiring. In what follows, we will consider
R=\mathbb{N}G
, other relevant cases being
R=\mathbb{N},\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}G
. We then proceed with alternate characterizations. See
[
8
,
19
]
for a complete treatment.

Definition 1.1

(Proper systems)
.

An
algebraic system
over
R[s]
is a system of equations

X_{i}=P_{i}(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for }i=1,2,\ldots,n

where
P_{i}\in R[s]\left\langle X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}\right\rangle
are polynomials with (a priori) non-commutative coefficients and variables, i.e., finite sums of monomials
r_{0}X_{i_{1}}r_{1}X_{i_{2}}r_{2}\ldots X_{i_{d}}r_{d}\cdot s^{k}
with
d,k\geq 0
integers,
1\leq i_{j}\leq n
and
r_{j}\in R
. A system is
proper
if it contains

•

no constant term (i.e., no monomial with
d=k=0
);

•

no monomial with
d=1
and
k=0
.

A system is
linear
if furthermore all monomials satisfy
d=0
, or
d=1
and
r_{1}=1_{R}
.

Definition 1.2
.

A formal series
S=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,a_{n}\cdot s^{n}\in R[[s]]
is
proper
(or
quasiregular
) if its constant coefficient is zero, that is
a_{0}=0
.

The main motivation for those two definitions is the following result:

Theorem 1.3

(See
[
19
, Theorem IV.1.1]
)
.

Every proper algebraic system admits a unique solution
(S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{n})\in R[[s]]^{n}
consisting of proper formal series.

Finally, we say a proper series is
R
-rational (resp.
R
-algebraic) if it is solution to a proper linear (resp. algebraic system). More generally

Definition 1.4

(
R
-rational and
R
-algebraic series)
.

A formal series
S\in R[[s]]
is
R
-rational
(resp.
R
-algebraic
) if
S-a_{0}
is the first component of a proper solution
(S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{n})\in R[[s]]
to a proper linear (resp. algebraic) system over
R[s]
.

Let us give another less technical definition of rational series over non-commutative semirings. We first need the notion of quasi-inverse:

Definition 1.5
.

Given a
proper
series
S(s)\in R[[s]]
, we define its
quasi-inverse
as

S(s)^{*}=\sum_{n\geq 0}S(s)^{n},

where
S(s)^{n}
is the multiplication of
S(s)
with itself
n
times and
S(s)^{0}=1
.

The name quasi-inverse comes from the “equality”
(1-S)S^{*}=1
. This (linear) equation shows that, if
S
itself is rational, then
S^{*}
is rational too. Reciprocally, all rational series can be obtained from polynomials using addition, multiplication and quasi-inversion:

Theorem 1.6

(See
[
19
, Theorems II.1.2 and II.1.4]
)
.

The set of
R
-rational series is the smallest semiring containing
R[s]
and closed under quasi-inversion (for proper series).

Rational and algebraic series can also be characterized in terms of automata and languages.

Definition 1.7
.

An
R[s]
-automaton
is a finite oriented graph
A=(V,E)
with an edge labeling
p\colon E\to R[s]
. A series
S(s)\in R[[s]]
is
recognized by the automaton

A
if there exist two initial and terminal vertices
I,T\in V
such that

S(s)=\sum_{\gamma\in P(I,T)}p(\gamma),

where
P(I,T)
is the set of all oriented paths from
I
to
T
, and
p(e_{1}\ldots e_{\ell}):=p(e_{1})\ldots p(e_{\ell})
.

Theorem 1.8

(Kleene-Schützenberger
[
20
]
, see also
[
8
, Theorem 3.2.5]
)
.

A formal series is
R
-rational if and only if it is recognized by an
R[s]
-automaton.

Remark 1.9
.

(a)

In case
R=\mathbb{N}G
, we may
and will
assume that all labels are of the form
p(e)=w(e)\cdot s^{l(e)}
for some
w(e)\in G
and
l(e)\in\mathbb{N}
, at the cost of allowing multiple edges between vertices.

(b)

If furthermore
S(s)
is the complete growth series, then
\|w(\gamma)\|=l(\gamma)
holds for all path
\gamma\in P(I,T)
(with the obvious notations)

Remark 1.10
.

The computational advantages of
\mathbb{N}G
-rational series over
\mathbb{Z}G
-rational series are made clearer through the formalism of
R[s]
-automata. Given an
\mathbb{N}G[s]
-automaton for a complete growth series, we can pick a path at random, compute the associated term
g\cdot s^{l}
, and get a certificate that the element
g
has length
l
. In contrast, if the automaton had labels in
\mathbb{Z}G[s]
, the term
g\cdot s^{l}
might cancel out with other terms, so that we need to compute all terms with the same exponent
s^{l}
before having a certificate
g
has indeed length
l
.

Theorem
1.8
admits a generalization linking
R
-algebraic series and weighted pushdown automata (see
[
8
, Section 7.5.3]
). For complete growth series, this can be reformulated as

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
G
be a group and
S
a generating set. The associated complete growth series is
\mathbb{N}G
-algebraic if and only if there exists an unambiguous context-free language
\mathcal{L}\subset(G\times\mathbb{N})^{*}
such that the evaluation map
\ev\colon\mathcal{L}\to G\times\mathbb{N}
is injective and its image is given by

\ev(\mathcal{L})=\{(g,\left\|g\right\|_{S}):g\in G\}.

2
Dead ends and saddle elements

Let us recall the notion of dead ends introduced in
[
3
]
, and introduce some related notions:

Definition 2.1
.

Consider
(G,\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|)
a group endowed with a left-invariant metric (e.g., a finitely generated group endowed with a word metric, or a nilpotent Lie group endowed with a Carnot-Cartheodory metric) and let
D>0
and
M\geq 0
be real numbers. An element
g\in G
is

•

a
dead end of depth
at least
D
if

\forall h\in G\text{ such that }\left\|h\right\|\leq D,\hskip 10.00002pt\left\|gh\right\|\leq\left\|g\right\|.

•

an
almost dead end of depth
at least
D
and
margin
M
if

\forall h\in G\text{ such that }\left\|h\right\|\leq D,\hskip 10.00002pt\left\|gh\right\|\leq\left\|g\right\|+M.

•

a
saddle element of depth
at least
D
if

\forall h\in G\text{ such that }\left\|h\right\|\leq D,\hskip 10.00002pt\left\|gh\right\|=\|gh^{-1}\|.

We say that
(G,\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|)
has
deep pockets
if it possesses dead ends of arbitrarily large depths.

Remark 2.2
.

Finite (and more generally compact) groups have deep pockets: element of maximal length are dead ends of infinite depth. The notion of deep pockets gets way more interesting when looking at non-bounded groups, as all elements have finite depth.

To the best of our knowledge, the notion of almost dead ends is new, even though almost dead ends appear implicitly in Warshall’s work
[
25
,
24
]
. The main interest of almost dead ends is that the existence of almost dead ends of large depth is preserved by rough isometries.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X,Y
be two metric spaces. A function
f\colon X\to Y
is a
rough isometry
(or
(1,K)
-quasi-isometry) if there exists some constant
K\geq 0
such that

•

for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, we have

d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})-K\leq d_{Y}\big(f(x),f(x^{\prime})\big)\leq d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})+K.

•

for all
y\in Y
, there exists
x\in X
such that
d_{Y}\big(y,f(x)\big)\leq K
.

Remark.
Even though this might not be obvious from the definition, this defines an equivalence relation on metric spaces, in particular it is symmetric.

Lemma 2.4

(Compare with
[
25
, Proposition 7]
)
.

Let
(G,d_{G})
and
(H,d_{H})
be two roughly isometric metric group. Suppose that there exists some
M\geq 0
such that
G
contains almost dead ends of arbitrarily large depth and fixed margin
M
, then the same is true in
H
.

Proof.

Let
f\colon G\to H
be a rough isometry. Up to translation we may suppose that
f(e_{G})=e_{H}
. Let
g\in G
be an almost dead end of margin
M
and depth
D
. We prove that
f(g)
is an almost dead end. For any
h\in H
s.t.
\|h\|\leq D-2K
, there exists
x\in G
s.t.
d\big(f(x),f(g)h\big)\leq K
hence

d(g,x)\leq d\big(f(g),f(x)\big)+K\leq d\big(f(g),f(g)h\big)+d\big(f(g)h,f(x)\big)+K\leq D.

By hypothesis
g
is an almost dead end of depth
D
so that
\left\|x\right\|\leq\left\|g\right\|+M
hence

\left\|f(g)h\right\|\leq\left\|f(x)\right\|+d\big(f(x),f(g)h\big)\leq\big(\!\left\|x\right\|+K\big)+K\leq\left\|g\right\|+M+2K\leq\left\|f(g)\right\|+M+3K.

This means that
f(g)
is an almost dead end of margin
M+3K
(which is fixed) and depth
D-2K
(which can be made arbitrarily large for well chosen
g\in G
).
∎

Usually we should think of the situation where the depth
D
is way larger than the margin
M
. In case of integer-valued metric and
D\gg M
, the following key lemma ensures that we can “promote” almost dead ends into genuine dead ends of comparatively large depth.

Lemma 2.5

(Warshall
[
25
, Proposition 6]
)
.

Let
X
be a metric space,
f\colon X\to\mathbb{N}
a function, and
D,M\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that there exists
x\in X
such that

f(x^{\prime})<f(x)+M\text{ for all }x^{\prime}\in B_{D}(x).

Then there exists
x^{\prime}\in B_{D}(x)
such that
f
reaches a maximum on
B_{\frac{D}{M}}(x^{\prime})
at
x^{\prime}
.

Let us now relate dead ends with the rationality of complete growth series

Theorem 2.6

(Pumping lemma +
\varepsilon
)
.

Let
G
be a group and
S
a finite generating set. Suppose that the complete growth series of
(G,S)
is
\mathbb{N}G
-rational. Then there exists
D>0
such that all but finitely many
g\in G
can be written as
g=uv
with
v\neq e
,
\left\|v\right\|\leq D
and

\left\|uv^{n}\right\|=\left\|u\right\|+n\tau\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for all }n\geq 0

for a certain
\tau\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Moreover

\tau=\tau(v)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\left\|v^{n}\right\|}{n}

is the
translation number
of
v
.

Proof.

As the complete growth series is
\mathbb{N}G
-rational, it is recognized by an automaton
(V,E)
. We take
D=2\left|V\right|\max_{e\in E}\left\|w(e)\right\|
(with

. Note that

∎

3.6
A parte - Dead ends in direct products

Proposition 3.14
.

Consider
G=\prod_{i}G_{i}

(
i.e.,
Z
trivial above
)

.

(a)

Suppose one of the pairs

is infinite and has deep pockets, then so does
(G,S_{\mathrm{cube-like}})
.

(b)

Suppose one of the pairs
(G_{j},S_{j})
doesn’t have deep pockets, then neither does
(G,S_{\mathrm{stnd-like}})
.

Proof.

(a) Fix some depth
D
. Let
g_{j}\in G_{j}
be a dead end of depth
\geq D
and length
\left\|g_{j}\right\|_{S_{j}}\geq D
. Consider the element
g=(e,\ldots,e,g_{j},e,\ldots,e)\in\prod_{i}G_{i}
. For any element
h\in\prod_{i}G_{i}
satisfying

\left\|h\right\|_{\mathrm{cube-like}}\leq D,

i.e.,
\left\|h_{i}\right\|_{S_{i}}\leq D
for all

, we have

\left\|gh\right\|_{\mathrm{cube-like}}=\max\big\{\!\left\|h_{1}\right\|_{S_{1}},\ldots,\left\|g_{j}h_{j}\right\|_{S_{j}},\ldots,\left\|h_{m}\right\|_{S_{m}}\!\big\}\leq\left\|g_{j}\right\|_{S_{j}}=\left\|g\right\|_{\mathrm{cube-like}}.

This means that
g
is a dead end of depth
\geq D
.

(b) As
(G_{j},S_{j})
doesn’t have any deep pocket, there exists some
D
such that
(G_{j},S_{j})
doesn’t have any dead end of depth
\geq D
. We show that the same holds in
(\prod_{i}G_{i},S_{\mathrm{stnd-like}})
. Consider any
g\in\prod_{i}G_{i}
. As
g_{j}\in G_{j}
isn’t a dead end of depth

, there exists
h_{j}\in G_{j}
with
\left\|h_{j}\right\|_{S_{j}}\leq D
and
\left\|g_{j}h_{j}\right\|_{S_{j}}>\left\|g_{j}\right\|_{S_{j}}
. We define

\displaystyle\left\|h\right\|_{\mathrm{stnd-like}}

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{i}\left\|h_{i}\right\|_{S_{i}}=0+\ldots+0+\left\|h_{j}\right\|_{S_{j}}+0+\ldots+0\leq D,

\displaystyle\left\|gh\right\|_{\mathrm{stnd-like}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left\|g_{i}h_{i}\right\|_{S_{i}}=\sum_{i}\left\|g_{i}\right\|_{S_{i}}+\big(\!\left\|g_{j}h_{j}\right\|_{S_{j}}-\left\|g_{j}\right\|_{S_{j}}\!\big)>\left\|g\right\|_{\mathrm{stnd-like}}.

4
Lamplighters and wreath products

Let us start with a classical result for lengths in wreath products:

Proposition 4.1
.

Consider
G=L\wr Q
, endowed with the standard generating set
S=S_{L}\cup S_{Q}
. Elements

can be identified with pairs
(\Phi,q)
with

\left\|g\right\|_{\mathrm{std}}=\sum_{p\in Q}\left\|\Phi(p)\right\|_{S_{L}}+TS(e_{Q};\,\supp(\Phi);\,q),\vskip-2.84526pt

where

is the length of the shortest path in the Cayley graph
Cay(Q,S_{Q})
starting at
x
, going through
S
in some order, and ending at
y
.

Using this formula, it was shown in
[
7
]
that many of those groups have deep pockets:

Theorem 4.2

(Theorem 6.1 in
[
7
]
)
.

Consider
G=L\wr\mathbb{Z}
with
L
non-trivial, endowed with the standard generating set
S=S_{L}\cup\{t^{\pm}\}
. Suppose that
L
has dead ends of arbitrary depth w.r.t.
S_{L}
(for instance if
L
is finite), then so does
G
with respect to
S
.

Note that the condition on
(L,S_{L})
is indeed important, as wreath products like
\mathbb{Z}\wr\mathbb{Z}
do not have dead ends (at least w.r.t. the standard generating set). However, the question of
\mathbb{N}G
-rationality of their complete growth series is still settled by the following proposition:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G=L\wr\mathbb{Z}
with
L
non-trivial. Consider the standard generating set
S=S_{L}\cup\{t^{\pm}\}
with
S_{L}
symmetric. There exists a sequence of distinct elements
(g_{d})
satisfying

\forall h\in G\text{ such that }\left\|h\right\|_{S}\leq d,\hskip 10.00002pt\left\|g_{d}h\right\|_{S}=\|g_{d}h^{-1}\|_{S}.

As a corollary, the associated complete growth series in not
\mathbb{N}G
-rational.

Proof.

Let
\ell\in L\setminus\{e_{L}\}
and define

\Psi_{d}(q)=\begin{cases}\ell&\text{if }q=\pm d,\\
e_{L}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We consider
g_{d}=(\Psi_{d},0)
i.e., the element with only lamps in a non-trivial state on site
\pm d
, and the lamplighter guy back at
0
. Consider
h=(\Phi,q)\in L\wr\mathbb{Z}
with
\left\|h\right\|_{S}\leq d
. We have
h^{-1}=(\Phi(-q+\,\cdot\,)^{-1},-q)
. Note that
\|h^{-1}\|_{S}=\left\|h\right\|_{S}\leq d
so that

\supp\Phi\cap\supp\Psi_{d}=\emptyset=\supp\Phi(-q+\,\cdot\,)^{-1}\cap\supp\Psi_{d}.

It follows that
\left\|g_{d}h\right\|_{S}
can be easily computed:

\displaystyle\left\|g_{d}h\right\|_{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\|\Psi_{d}(p)\right\|_{S_{L}}+\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\|\Phi(p)\right\|_{S_{L}}+TS(0;\,B(0,d);\,q)

\displaystyle=2\left\|\ell\right\|_{S_{L}}+\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\|\Phi(p)\right\|_{S_{L}}+2d-\left|q\right|.

(We use that any path going through
\pm d
must go through the entire interval.) Similarly

\displaystyle\left\|g_{d}h^{-1}\right\|_{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\|\Psi_{d}(p)\right\|_{S_{L}}+\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\|\Phi(-q+p)^{-1}\right\|_{S_{L}}+TS(0;\,B(0,d);\,-q)

\displaystyle=2\left\|\ell\right\|_{S_{L}}+\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\|\Phi(p)^{-1}\right\|_{S_{L}}+2d-\left|q\right|

Compare both formula, recalling that
S_{L}
is symmetric: we have
\left\|g_{d}h\right\|_{S}=\left\|g_{d}h^{-1}\right\|_{S}
.
∎

so that

\displaystyle p\left\|\delta_{n}h_{2}\right\|_{\mathsf{CC}}

\displaystyle\sim\alpha+np(\lambda_{j}\tau_{j}+\ldots+\lambda_{k}\tau_{k})

\displaystyle p\left\|\delta_{m}h_{1}\cdot\delta_{n}h_{2}\right\|_{\mathsf{CC}}

\displaystyle\sim\alpha+p\big(m(\lambda_{1}\tau_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{j-1}\tau_{j-1})+n(\lambda_{j}\tau_{j}+\ldots+\lambda_{k}\tau_{k})\big)

and finally
\left\|\delta_{m}h_{1}\cdot\delta_{n}h_{2}\right\|_{\mathsf{CC}}=\left\|\delta_{m}h_{1}\right\|_{\mathsf{CC}}+\left\|\delta_{n}h_{2}\right\|_{\mathsf{CC}}
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
(hence all
m,n\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
).

(c-ii) Suppose that we have two elements
h_{1},h_{2}\neq e
such that
\hat{h}_{1}+\hat{h}_{2}=\mathbf{0}
and
\left\|\delta_{m}h_{1}\cdot\delta_{n}h_{2}\right\|_{\mathsf{CC}}=\left\|\delta_{m}h_{1}\right\|_{\mathsf{CC}}+\left\|\delta_{n}h_{2}\right\|_{\mathsf{CC}}
for all
m,n\geq 0
. This means that, for any
\mathsf{CC}
-geodesics
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
representing
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
, the concatenation
\delta_{m}\gamma_{1}\cdot\delta_{n}\gamma_{2}
is a
\mathsf{CC}
-geodesic for
\delta_{m}h_{1}\cdot\delta_{n}h_{2}
.

Let us first consider
m=n=1
. We know that
h_{1}h_{2}
is a commutator, so any geodesic has to follow a rescaled isoperimetrix. Suppose wlog that
\left\|h_{1}\right\|_{\mathsf{CC}}\geq\left\|h_{2}\right\|_{\mathsf{CC}}
, then any geodesic
\gamma_{1}
for
h_{1}
has to cover at least half the perimeter of the isoperimetrix.

•

If either the isoperimetrix has
\geq 6
sides or
\left\|h_{1}\right\|_{\mathsf{CC}}>\left\|h_{2}\right\|_{\mathsf{CC}}
, then
\gamma_{1}
has to cover two corners of the isoperimetrix, hence the scale of the isoperimetrix followed by any geodesic continuation of
\gamma_{1}
is fixed, and
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}
is the maximal geodesic continuation. In particular the longer curve
\gamma_{1}\cdot\delta_{2}\gamma_{2}
cannot be a geodesic continuation.

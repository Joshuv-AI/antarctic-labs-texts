Maximizing one Laplace eigenvalue on n-dimensional manifolds
Romain Petrides

Abstract
. We prove existence and regularity results for the problem of maximization of one Laplace eigenvalue with respect to metrics of same volume lying in a conformal class of a Riemannian manifold of dimension n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 .



Let
M
be a smooth compact manifold of dimension
n
. Given a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
, we denote the sequence of eigenvalues associated to the Laplace operator
\Delta_{g}=-div_{g}\nabla

0=\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}(g)\leq\lambda_{2}(g)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}(g)\leq\cdots\to+\infty\text{ as }k\to+\infty.

The eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
is associated to constant functions and if
M
is connected, the other ones are positive, depending on
g
. We will focus on the following natural scale invariant functional

\bar{\lambda}_{k}(g)=\lambda_{k}(g)V_{g}(M)^{\frac{2}{n}},

where
V_{g}(M)
is the volume of
g
on
M
. We let
[g]=\{e^{2u}g;u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(M\right)\}
be a given conformal class of metrics. In the current paper, we aim at studying the following variational problem

\Lambda_{k}(M,[g])=\sup_{\tilde{g}\in[g]}\lambda_{k}(\tilde{g})V_{\tilde{g}}(M)^{\frac{2}{n}}=\sup_{\tilde{g}\in[g]}\bar{\lambda}_{k}(\tilde{g}).

Studying the maximization of
\bar{\lambda}_{k}
is more relevant than the minimization because the well-known examples of Cheeger dumbells prove
\inf_{[g]}\bar{\lambda}_{k}=0
. Moreover, while it is standard in geometric analysis to restrict a functional with respect to metrics to a conformal class (e.g the Yamabe problem, problems for Q-curvature etc), there are deep reasons to do it in the context of spectral geometry:

so that the limit equation is the equation of weak harmonic maps, known to be smooth and stongly harmonic, so that the limiting measure is smooth.

However, establishing existence and regularity of metrics achieving

\lambda_{k}(\tilde{g})=\min_{E\in\mathcal{G}_{k+1}\left(\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)\right)}\max_{\varphi\in E\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{M}\left|\nabla\varphi\right|_{g}^{2}e^{(n-2)u}dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\varphi^{2}e^{nu}dv_{g}}

so that the possible degenerescence of maximizing sequence of conformal factors

-div_{g}\left(e^{\left(n-2\right)u_{\varepsilon}}\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right)=\lambda_{k}(e^{2u_{\varepsilon}}g)e^{nu_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon},

but also at the left-hand side, allowing to loose the elliptic properties of the operator

This boundedness was often used to initiate compactness arguments on the sequence of maximizing metric associated to an ”almost critical” system of equations approaching the system of equations of a harmonic map into a sphere. Indeed, the number of equations in the system is automatically uniformly bounded for

In the following result, we overcome these problems by establishing a natural generalization of the maximization results for
\Lambda_{k}(M,[g])
in dimension
2

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
(M,[g])
be a compact connected manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
endowed with a conformal class and
k\geq 1
. If

\Lambda_{k}(M,[g])>\Lambda_{k}(\widetilde{M},[\widetilde{g}])

for any
(\widetilde{M},[\widetilde{g}])=(M,[g])\sqcup\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},[h]\right)\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},[h]\right)
or
(\widetilde{M},[\widetilde{g}])=\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},[h]\right)\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},[h]\right)
where
h
is the round metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, then for some
\alpha>0
, there is a non-negative factor
f\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}\left(M\right)\cap\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M\setminus Z)
where
Z=\{z\in M;f(z)=0\}
such that

\lambda_{k}(fg)\left(\int_{M}f^{\frac{n}{2}}dv_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}=\Lambda_{k}(M,[g])

Moreover,
f=\frac{\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{2}}{\lambda_{k}(fg)}
, where
\Phi:M\to\mathbb{S}^{p}
is some
n
-harmonic map into a sphere, whose coordinate functions are eigenfunctions with respect to
\lambda_{k}(fg)
.

[
Pet14a
]

1.
The variational approach

1.1.
Splitting the conformal factor into two densities

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
. We consider
\alpha
and
\beta
two non-negative functions, as weights for the following eigenvalue problem:

\lambda_{k}(\alpha,\beta)=\inf_{E\subset\mathcal{G}{k+1}\left(\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(M\right)\right)}\sup_{\phi\in E\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{M}\left|\nabla\phi\right|_{g}^{2}\alpha dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\phi^{2}\beta dv_{g}}

Notice that if
\beta=\alpha^{\frac{n}{n-2}}
is a positive function,
\lambda_{k}(\alpha,\beta)
is nothing but the
k
-th Laplace eigenvalue associated to the metric
\beta^{\frac{2}{n}}g
. Thanks to this remark, a natural functional invariant by dilatation to consider is

\bar{\lambda}_{k}(\alpha,\beta)=\lambda_{k}(f,h)\frac{\int_{M}\beta dv_{g}}{\left(\int_{M}\alpha^{\frac{n}{n-2}}dv_{g}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}}.

If the functions
\alpha
and
\beta
are smooth positive, by compact embeddings of the natural weighted
L^{2}
and
W^{1,2}
spaces involved in this problem there is existence of eigenfunctions. Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be such that
\int_{M}\beta dv_{g}=1
and
\int_{M}\alpha^{\frac{n}{2}}dv_{g}=1
. The subdifferential of
-\bar{\lambda}_{k}
at
(\alpha,\beta)
(see
[
PT22
]
for definition and computations of the subdifferential) satisfies

(1.1)

-\partial\left(-\bar{\lambda}_{k}\right)(\alpha,\beta)\subset co\left\{\left(\left|\nabla\phi\right|_{g}^{2}-\bar{\lambda}_{k}\alpha^{\frac{2}{n-2}},\bar{\lambda}_{k}\left(1-\phi^{2}\right)\right);\phi\in E_{k}(\alpha,\beta),\int_{M}\phi^{2}\beta dv_{g}=1\right\}

where
\bar{\lambda}_{k}=\bar{\lambda}_{k}(\alpha,\beta)
. Notice that as a convention, we compute the subdifferential of
-\bar{\lambda}_{k}
because the subdifferential is well adapted for minimization of functionals. If
(\alpha,\beta)
is critical, we have by definition that
0\in\partial\bar{\lambda}_{k}(\alpha,\beta)
. Then, there are eigenfunctions
\Phi=(\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{p})
such that

1.3.
Existence of Palais-Smale sequences for the maximization problem

If we take
c=\sup_{X}\bar{\lambda}_{k}<+\infty
, proposition
1.1
implies that for any sequence
\varepsilon\to 0
and
\delta_{\varepsilon}\to 0
we have a sequence of

such that

Now, up to classical convolutions, pairs of smooth positive functions are dense in

, so that we have a sequence of pairs smooth positive functions

such that

\bar{\lambda}_{k}(\alpha_{\varepsilon},e^{nu_{\varepsilon}})>c-\varepsilon\text{ and }\left|\partial\bar{\lambda}_{k}(\alpha_{\varepsilon},e^{nu_{\varepsilon}})\right|<\delta_{\varepsilon}

the first condition gives that the pair

is a maximizing sequence. The second condition can be rewritten as

\forall(\dot{\alpha},\dot{\beta})\in\tilde{X},\exists\Psi\in-\partial\left(-\bar{\lambda}_{k}\right)(\alpha_{\varepsilon},e^{nu_{\varepsilon}});\left\langle(\dot{\alpha},\dot{\beta}),\Psi\right\rangle\leq\delta_{\varepsilon}.

We denote

where
\lambda_{\varepsilon}=\bar{\lambda}_{k}(\alpha_{\varepsilon},e^{nu_{\varepsilon}})
.

\begin{split}\forall(\dot{\alpha},\dot{\beta})\in\tilde{X},-\partial\left(-\bar{\lambda}_{k}\right)(\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2-n}{2}}Q_{\varepsilon},e^{nu_{\varepsilon}}),\left\langle(\dot{\alpha},\dot{\beta}),(\psi_{1}-\delta_{\varepsilon},\psi_{2}-\delta_{\varepsilon})\right\rangle\leq 0\end{split}

meaning that

\left(-\partial\left(-\bar{\lambda}_{k}\right)(\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2-n}{2}}Q_{\varepsilon},e^{nu_{\varepsilon}})-\{(\delta_{\varepsilon},\delta_{\varepsilon})\}\right)\cap\{(a,b)\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(M);a\leq 0,b\leq 0\}\neq\emptyset.

Indeed, if not, we use the classical Hahn-Banach theorem to separate these two spaces (the first one is compact, the second one is closed in
\mathcal{C}^{0}\times\mathcal{C}^{0}
) by a linear form
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
(where
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are Radon measures, belonging to the dual space of continuous functions) satisfying

\begin{split}\forall(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\in-\partial\left(-\bar{\lambda}_{k}\right)(\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2-n}{2}}Q_{\varepsilon},e^{nu_{\varepsilon}}),\int_{M}\left(\psi_{1}d\mu_{1}+\psi_{2}d\mu_{2}\right)>0\end{split}

and

\forall(a,b)\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(M);a\leq 0,b\leq 0,\int_{M}(ad\mu_{1}+bd\mu_{2})\leq 0

The second condition implies that
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are non negative Radon measures. Up to a renormalization, we assume that

and we obtain a contradiction. Therefore we obtain the following Palais Smale condition: there is a map
\Phi_{\varepsilon}=(\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon},\cdots,\phi_{p_{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon})
such that

-div_{g}\left(\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2-n}{2}}Q_{\varepsilon}\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right)=\lambda_{\varepsilon}e^{nu_{\varepsilon}}\Phi_{\varepsilon}

and

\int_{M}e^{nu_{\varepsilon}}dA_{g}=\int_{M}\left|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}e^{nu_{\varepsilon}}dA_{g}=\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{M}Q_{\varepsilon}\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|_{g}^{2}dA_{g}=\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{M}Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n}{n-2}}=1

satisfying

\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\leq Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2}{n-2}}+\delta_{\varepsilon}
and

\left|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\geq 1-\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}}{\lambda_{\varepsilon}}

where
\delta_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

=&\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n}{2}}\int_{M}e^{2u_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{1-\omega_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right)dv_{g}+O\left(\delta_{\varepsilon}^{2}\right)=O\left(\delta_{\varepsilon}\right)\\
\end{split}

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, where we crucially used again
\int_{\Sigma}e^{2u_{\varepsilon}}\omega_{\varepsilon}^{2}=\int_{\Sigma}e^{2u_{\varepsilon}}=1
. We then obtain (
2.2
). Now, let’s prove (
2.3
). We define
A_{\varepsilon}
by

A_{\varepsilon}^{2}=Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2}{n-2}}+\delta_{\varepsilon}-\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}

A_{\varepsilon}
is a non-negative function and we know that

\int_{M}\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}_{g}Q_{\varepsilon}dv_{g}=\int_{M}Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n}{n-2}}dv_{g}=1.

Therefore multiplying
A_{\varepsilon}^{2}
by
Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n}{n-2}}
and integrating, we obtain

\int_{M}A_{\varepsilon}^{2}Q_{\varepsilon}dv_{g}=O\left(\delta_{\varepsilon}\right)\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0.

Now, we also obtain that

\int_{M}A_{\varepsilon}^{2}\left(\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+A_{\varepsilon}^{2}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}=\int_{M}A_{\varepsilon}^{2}\left(Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2}{n-2}}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\leq c_{n}\left(\int_{M}A_{\varepsilon}^{2}Q_{\varepsilon}+\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\int_{M}A_{\varepsilon}^{2}\right)

for a constant
c_{n}
depending only on the dimension so that by a minoration of the left-hand term we obtain

\int_{M}A_{\varepsilon}^{2}\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|_{g}^{2}+\int_{M}A_{\varepsilon}^{n}=O\left(\delta_{\varepsilon}+\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\int_{M}A_{\varepsilon}^{2}\right)

and in particular, we deduce

\int_{M}A_{\varepsilon}^{n}=O\left(\delta_{\varepsilon}\right)

Now, writing

Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{q}{n-2}}-\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{\frac{q}{2}}=Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{q}{n-2}}-\left(Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2}{n-2}}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}+\left(A_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}-\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{\frac{q}{2}}

we obtain that

\int_{M}\left(Q_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{q}{n-2}}-\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{\frac{q}{2}}\right)f_{\varepsilon}=\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-q}}}\left(O\left(\delta_{\varepsilon}\right)+O\left(\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2}{n}}\right)\right)

which completes the proof of the claim.
∎

From the previous claim and assumptions on the Palais-Smale sequence, we already deduce a global
W^{1,q}
convergence of
\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}
for any
q<n
.

Claim 2.3
.

Up to a subsequence, there is a map
\Phi_{0}:M\to\mathbb{S}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\left|\Phi_{0}\right|^{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}\left(\phi_{0}^{i}\right)^{2}=_{a.e}1\text{ and }\int_{M}\left|\nabla\Phi_{0}\right|_{g}^{n}dv_{g}\leq_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup\int_{M}\left|\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|_{g}^{n}dv_{g}

where
\left|\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|_{g}^{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}\left|\nabla\frac{\phi_{\varepsilon}^{i}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|_{g}^{2}
and such that for any
1\leq p<+\infty
,
1\leq q<n
,

\int_{M}\left(\left|\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}-\Phi_{0}\right|^{p}+\left|\nabla\left(\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}-\Phi_{0}\right)\right|_{g}^{q}\right)dv_{g}\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

We use Claim
3.3
in the appendix where we extend
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
by
\phi_{\varepsilon}^{i}=0
for
i\geq p_{\varepsilon}+2
. We have that

-div_{g}\left(\left|\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|^{n-2}\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right)=\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n}{2}}e^{nu_{\varepsilon}}\Phi_{\varepsilon}-div_{g}\left(\left|\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|^{n-2}\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}-Q_{\varepsilon}\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right)

We notice that
A_{\varepsilon}=\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n}{2}}e^{nu_{\varepsilon}}\Phi_{\varepsilon}
satisfies
\left(\left|A_{\varepsilon}\right|\right)_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
L^{1}
and it remains to prove that

B_{\varepsilon}=-div_{g}\left(\left|\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|^{n-2}\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}-Q_{\varepsilon}\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right)

satisfies
\|B_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{-1,n}}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Let
\eta:M\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
be such that

\int_{M}\left(\left|\eta\right|^{n}+\left|\nabla\eta\right|_{g}^{n}\right)dv_{g}<+\infty.

We have by an integration by parts that

\begin{split}\int_{M}B_{\varepsilon}.\eta dv_{g}=&\int_{M}\nabla\eta\left(\left|\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|^{n-2}\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}-Q_{\varepsilon}\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right)\\
=&\int_{M}\left(\left|\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|^{n-2}-\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{n-2}\right)\nabla\eta\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}+\int_{M}\left(\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{n-2}-Q_{\varepsilon}\right)\nabla\eta\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\\
&+\int_{M}Q_{\varepsilon}\nabla\eta.\nabla\left(\Phi_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right)dv_{g}\end{split}

The second right-hand term satisfies by (
2.3
) and then a Hölder inequality

\left|\int_{M}\left(\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{n-2}-Q_{\varepsilon}\right)\nabla\eta\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|\leq O\left(\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2}{n}}\right)\|\left|\nabla\eta\right|\|_{L^{n}}.

The third right-hand term satisfies by a Hölder inequality, then (
2.2
), and then another Hölder inequality,

\left|\int_{M}Q_{\varepsilon}\nabla\eta.\nabla\left(\Phi_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right)dv_{g}\right|\leq O\left(\delta_{\varepsilon}\right)\|\left|\nabla\eta\right|\|_{L^{n}}.

we write the first right-hand term as

\left|\int_{M}\left(\left|\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|^{n-2}-\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{n-2}\right)\nabla\eta\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|\leq c_{n}\int_{M}\left|\nabla\left(\Phi_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right)\right|_{g}\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|_{g}^{n-2}\left|\nabla\eta\right|_{g}

for a constant
c_{n}
depending only on the dimension
n
where we used that

\left|\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|_{g}^{2}\leq\frac{\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|_{g}^{2}}{1-\delta_{\varepsilon}}\leq 2\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|_{g}^{2}

for
\varepsilon
small enough. Then, we have

\begin{split}\int_{M}\left|\nabla\left(\Phi_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right)\right|_{g}\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|_{g}^{n-2}\left|\nabla\eta\right|_{g}=&\int_{M}\left|\nabla\left(\Phi_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right)\right|_{g}\left(\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|_{g}^{n-2}-Q_{\varepsilon}\right)\left|\nabla\eta\right|_{g}\\
&+\int_{M}\left|\nabla\left(\Phi_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right)\right|_{g}Q_{\varepsilon}\left|\nabla\eta\right|_{g}\end{split}

and as before, we apply (
2.3
) and then a Hölder inequality for the first term and Hölder inequalities and (
2.2
) for the second term to obtain

\left|\int_{M}\left(\left|\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|^{n-2}-\left|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{n-2}\right)\nabla\eta\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|\leq O\left(\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2}{n}}\right)\|\left|\nabla\eta\right|\|_{L^{n}}

and gathering all the previous inequalities gives the Claim.
∎

In the next subsections, we aim at proving that the strong convergence holds in a better space, in order to pass to the limit on the sequence of equations satisfied by
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
.

2.3.1.
Selection of bad points

In the following, we perform local regularity estimates on

For

2.3.2.
Non concentration of the energy

Far from bad points, the energy cannot have concentration points:

Claim 2.5
.

Let

, then

(2.4)

\begin{split}\lim_{r\to 0}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}e^{nu_{\varepsilon}}&=\lim_{r\to 0}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}Q_{\varepsilon}\left|\nabla\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right|^{2}\\

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}Q_{\varepsilon}\left|\nabla\left(\Phi_{\varepsilon}-\Psi_{\varepsilon}\right)\right|^{2}_{g}\leq&2\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}Q_{\varepsilon}\left|\nabla\left(\Phi_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\right)\right|^{2}_{g}+2\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}P_{\varepsilon}\left|\nabla\left(\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}}-\Psi_{\varepsilon}\right)\right|^{2}_{g}\\

The first left-hand term converges to

The proof of (

∎

.

The limiting measure has a

2.4.
A compact embedding for the weighted Sobolev spaces associated to the limiting metrics

In this section, we prove Theorem
0.2
.
Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a non-negative continuous function and we denote
Z
its zero set. We set
L^{2}(f.g)
the set of measurable functions
u:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\|u\|_{L^{2}(f.g)}:=\int_{M}u^{2}f^{\frac{n}{2}}dv_{g}<+\infty,

We set
W^{1,2}(f.g)
the completion of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(M\right)
with respect to the semi-norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(f.g)}=\int_{M}u^{2}f^{\frac{n}{2}}dv_{g}+\int_{M}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}f^{\frac{n-2}{2}}dv_{g}

We aim at proving that for
f=\frac{\left|\nabla\Phi_{0}\right|_{g}^{2}}{\lambda}
given by Theorem
0.1
, the embedding
W^{1,2}(f.g)\to L^{2}(f.g)
is compact. For that purpose, we will prove the following local Sobolev embedding:

Claim 2.8
.

There is
\kappa_{0}>1
, there is
r_{0}>0
and
C_{0}>0
such that for any
x\in M
and any
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}\left(\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)\right)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2\kappa_{0}}f^{\frac{n}{2}}dv_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}}}\leq C_{0}r^{2-\frac{n(\kappa_{0}-1)}{\kappa_{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla u\right|_{g}^{2}f^{\frac{n-2}{2}}dv_{g}.

Proof.

Using a classical Sobolev inequality, we have a universal constant
C_{0}
such that

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2\kappa_{0}}f^{\frac{n}{2}}dv_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}}}\leq&C_{0}r^{2-\frac{n(\kappa_{0}-1)}{\kappa_{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\left(uf^{\frac{n}{4\kappa_{0}}}\right)\right|_{g}^{2}dv_{g}\\
\leq&2C_{0}r^{2-\frac{n(\kappa_{0}-1)}{\kappa_{0}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla u\right|_{g}^{2}f^{\frac{n}{2\kappa_{0}}}dv_{g}+\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla f^{\frac{n}{4\kappa_{0}}}\right|_{g}^{2}u^{2}\right)\end{split}

We aim at estimating the right-hand term, noticing that
f
is nothing but the limit of
B:=\left|\nabla\Psi_{\tau}\right|^{2}_{g}
as
(\tau,m)\to(0,+\infty)
, where
\Psi_{\tau}:\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)\to\mathbb{S}^{m}
is a
(\tau,n)
-harmonic map, if we choose
r\leq\frac{r_{\star}}{2}
, where

r_{\star}=\sup\left\{r>0,\sup_{x}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(x)}\left(f+\tau\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{n,g}}{2}\right\}.

We recall that
\Psi_{\tau}
satisfies by Claim
3.1

\mu^{n-2}B\left(\kappa_{g}+B\right)\geq-div_{g}\left(a^{\tau}\nabla\mu^{n}\right)+\left|\nabla^{2}\Psi\right|\mu^{n-2}+\frac{n-2}{4}\mu^{n-4}\left|\nabla B\right|^{2}

where
\mu=\left(B+\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and

a_{i,j}^{\tau}=\frac{1}{n}\left(\delta_{i,j}+(n-2)\frac{\nabla_{i}\Psi.\nabla_{j}\Psi}{B+\tau}\right).

We integrate this equation over
u^{2}\mu^{-\nu_{0}}
for
\nu_{0}=\frac{n(\kappa_{0}-1)}{\kappa_{0}}
and we get

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2}\left(a\left(\nabla\mu^{-\nu_{0}}\nabla\left(\mu^{n}\right)\right)+\frac{n-2}{4}\mu^{n-4-\nu_{0}}\left|\nabla B\right|^{2}_{g}+\left|\nabla^{2}\Psi\right|^{2}_{g}\mu^{n-2-\nu_{0}}\right)\\
\leq\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2}B\mu^{n-2-\nu_{0}}(B+k_{g})-\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}2u\mu^{-\nu_{0}}a\left(\nabla u\nabla\left(\mu^{n}\right)\right)\end{split}

Knowing that
\frac{1}{n}\left|X\right|^{2}\leq a_{i,j}X^{i}X^{j}\leq\frac{n-1}{n}\left|X\right|^{2}
, and the following computations

\nabla\mu^{-\nu_{0}}\nabla\left(\mu^{n}\right)=-\nu_{0}n\mu^{n-2-\nu_{0}}\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\text{ and }\left|\nabla B\right|^{2}=4\mu^{2}\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}

we obtain that

\begin{split}&\left(n-2-(n-1)\nu_{0}\right)\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2}\mu^{n-2-\nu_{0}}\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}_{g}+\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2}\left|\nabla^{2}\Psi\right|^{2}_{g}\mu^{n-2-\nu_{0}}\\
\leq&(n-1)\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}2\left|u\nabla u\right|\mu^{n-1-\nu_{0}}\left|\nabla\mu\right|+\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2}\mu^{n-\nu_{0}}(B+k_{g})\\
\leq&(n-1)\left(\theta^{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2}\mu^{n-2-\nu_{0}}\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}_{g}+\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\mu^{n-\nu_{0}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}_{g}\right)\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2}\mu^{n-\nu_{0}}(B+k_{g})\end{split}

so that knowing that
\left|\nabla^{2}\Psi\right|^{2}_{g}\geq\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}_{g}
(by a Hölder inequality)

\begin{split}\left(1-\nu_{0}-\theta^{2}\right)&\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2}\mu^{n-2-\nu_{0}}\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}_{g}\\
\leq&\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\mu^{n-\nu_{0}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}_{g}+\frac{1}{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2}\mu^{n-\nu_{0}}(B+k_{g})\end{split}

and choosing
\nu_{0}<1
and
\theta^{2}=\frac{1-\nu_{0}}{2}
, we obtain such that

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2}\left|\nabla\mu^{\frac{n-\nu_{0}}{2}}\right|^{2}_{g}\leq\frac{4}{(1-\nu_{0})^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\mu^{n-\nu_{0}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}_{g}+\frac{2}{(1-\nu_{0})(n-1)}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2}\mu^{n-\nu_{0}}(B+k_{g})

and passing to the limit as
\tau\to 0
, we have that
\mu^{2}\to f
as
\tau\to 0
and noticing that
n-\nu_{0}=\frac{n}{\kappa_{0}}
we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2}\left|\nabla f^{\frac{n}{4\kappa_{0}}}\right|^{2}_{g}\leq\frac{4}{(1-\nu_{0})^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}f^{\frac{n}{2\kappa_{0}}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}_{g}+\frac{2}{(1-\nu_{0})(n-1)}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2}f^{\frac{n}{2\kappa_{0}}}(f+k_{g})

so that there is a constant
c:=c(n,1-\nu_{0})
such that

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2\kappa_{0}}f^{\frac{n}{2}}dv_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}}}\leq&2C_{0}r^{2-\frac{n(\kappa_{0}-1)}{\kappa_{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla u\right|_{g}^{2}f^{\frac{n}{2\kappa_{0}}}(1+c)dv_{g}\\
&+2C_{0}r^{2-\frac{n(\kappa_{0}-1)}{\kappa_{0}}}c\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2}f^{\frac{n}{2\kappa_{0}}}(f+k_{g})dv_{g}\\
\leq&2C_{0}r^{2-\frac{n(\kappa_{0}-1)}{\kappa_{0}}}A^{1-\frac{n(\kappa_{0}-1)}{2\kappa_{0}}}\left(1+c\right)\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla u\right|_{g}^{2}f^{\frac{n-2}{2}}dv_{g}\\
&+2C_{0}c_{g}r^{2}c\left(A+k_{g}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}u^{2\kappa_{0}}f^{\frac{n}{2}}dv_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}}}\end{split}

noticing that
f
is bounded by a constant
A
in
M
and applying a Hölder inequality for the last inequality. Letting
r>0
small enough so that
2C_{0}c_{g}r^{2}c\left(A+k_{g}\right)<\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain the expected Sobolev inequality.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

In the classical Sobolev inequality, the optimal constant
2\kappa_{0}
is
\frac{2n}{n-2}
.
Our Caccioppoli type estimate involved in the proof of the Sobolev inequality seems to be optimal regarding other similar regularity results on the
n
-harmonic equation (e.g
[
Sar22
]
). In this case, we only obtain
2\kappa_{0}<\frac{2n}{n-1}
.
It would be interesting to improve
\nu_{0}>0
so that

f^{\frac{n-\nu_{0}}{4}}
is a
H^{1}
function.

Now, it is clear that the embedding
W^{1,2}(f.g)\to L^{2}(f.g)
is compact. Indeed, first, any sequence of functions
(u_{k})
bounded in
W^{1,2}(f.g)
has to be bounded in
L^{2\kappa_{0}}(f.g)
(up to take a partition of unity to globalize the previous Sobolev inequality). We also know that up to a subsequence, it converges weakly to
u\in W^{1,2}(f.g)
. Moreover, using the classical compact embedding
W^{1,2}(g)\to L^{2}(g)
on any compact subset of
M\setminus Z
where
Z=\{x\in M;f(x)=0\}
, we deduce that up to a subsequence, the sequence
(u_{k})
converges almost everywhere to
u
with respect to the measure
f^{\frac{n}{2}}dv_{g}
. These two properties prove that up to a subsequence
(u_{k})
converges strongly in
L^{2}(f.g)
.

2.5.
Conclusion

Let’s prove Theorem
0.4
. By section
1
, there is a Palais-Smale sequence for the maximization problem. We denote
\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{n}{2}}=\lambda_{k}(Q_{\varepsilon},e^{nu_{\varepsilon}})
. We apply proposition
2.1
to this sequence. Theorem
0.4
then follows from upper semi-continuity of
\bar{\lambda}_{k}
. Indeed, let
\theta_{0},\cdots,\theta_{k}
be the
(k+1)
first eigenfunctions of the limiting manifold endowed with generalized metrics
(\widetilde{M},f.g)=(M,f_{0}.g)\sqcup\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},f_{1}.h\right)\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},f_{t}.h\right)
if

or
(\widetilde{M},f.g)=\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},f_{1}.h\right)\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},f_{t}.h\right)
if
f_{0}=0
, where
f_{0}dv_{g}=\tilde{\mu}_{0}
and
f_{i}dv_{h}=\pi^{\star}\tilde{\mu}_{i}
(where
\pi:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\cup\{\infty\}
is a stereographic projection) and
f_{i}
are
\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}
functions. We use
\theta_{0},\cdots,\theta_{k}
as test functions in the variational characterization of
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
in the following way: we set

\tilde{\theta}_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\eta_{0}(x)\theta_{i}(x)+\eta_{1}\left(\frac{x-q_{1}^{\varepsilon}}{\alpha_{1}^{\varepsilon}}\right)\theta_{i}\circ\pi^{-1}\left(\frac{x-q_{1}^{\varepsilon}}{\alpha_{1}^{\varepsilon}}\right)+\cdots+\eta_{t}\left(\frac{x-q_{t}^{\varepsilon}}{\alpha_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\right)\theta_{i}\circ\pi^{-1}\left(\frac{x-q_{t}^{\varepsilon}}{\alpha_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\right)

where points
q_{i}^{\varepsilon}
and scales
\alpha_{i}^{\varepsilon}
are given by Claim
2.1
and we define
\eta_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{i}(\rho)\right)
as cut-off functions such that
\eta_{i}=1
in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{i}(\sqrt{\rho})\right)
and

\int_{M}\left|\nabla\eta_{0}\right|_{g}^{n}\leq\frac{C}{\ln{\frac{1}{\rho}}}\text{ and }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla\eta_{i}\right|_{g}^{n}\leq\frac{C}{\ln{\frac{1}{\rho}}}\text{ if }i\geq 1

where if we denote
p_{1}^{i},\cdots,p_{1}^{s_{i}}
the rescaled bad points,

\Omega_{0}(\rho)=\Sigma\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{s_{0}}\mathbb{B}_{\rho}\left(p_{i}^{j}\right)=\text{ and }\Omega_{i}(\rho)=\mathbb{B}_{\frac{1}{\rho}}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{s_{i}}\mathbb{B}_{\rho}\left(p_{i}^{j}\right)\text{ if }i\geq 1.

Then, knowing that
Q_{\varepsilon}\left(q_{i}^{\varepsilon}+\alpha_{i}^{\varepsilon}x\right)\to f^{\frac{n-2}{2}}

\lambda_{\varepsilon}\leq\max_{\varphi\in\left\langle\tilde{\theta}_{0}^{\varepsilon},\cdots,\tilde{\theta}_{k}^{\varepsilon}\right\rangle}\frac{\int_{M}\left|\nabla\varphi\right|_{g}^{2}\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2-n}{2}}Q_{\varepsilon}dv_{g}}{\int_{M}\varphi^{2}e^{nu_{\varepsilon}}dv_{g}}\leq\lambda_{k}\left(\tilde{M},f.h\right)+o(1)+c\left(\frac{C}{\ln{\frac{1}{\rho}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

as

. Letting

3.

n

3.1.
A priori estimates for
n
-harmonic maps from a
n
-manifold into spheres

We say that
\Psi:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{p}
is a
\tau
-approximated
n
-harmonic map if it is the limit as
\tau\to 0
of a sequence of maps
\Psi_{\tau}:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{p}
which are solutions of the following minimization problems

\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}\left(\left|\nabla\varphi\right|_{g}^{2}+\tau\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}dv_{g};\varphi:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{p}\text{ and }\varphi=\Psi\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}\right\}

It satisfies the following Euler-Lagrange equation

-div_{g}\left(\left(\left|\nabla\Psi_{\tau}\right|_{g}^{2}+\tau\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\nabla\Psi_{\tau}\right)=\left(\left|\nabla\Psi_{\tau}\right|_{g}^{2}+\tau\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left|\nabla\Psi_{\tau}\right|_{g}^{2}\Psi_{\tau}

and we have that
\Psi_{\tau}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{B}^{n}\right)
[
HL87
]
[
Str94
]
. We say that
\Psi_{\tau}
is a
(\tau,n)
-harmonic map. For instance, we will deduce from Proposition
3.4
and
[
Str94
]
that any
n
-harmonic map into a sphere is locally a
\tau
-approximated
n
-harmonic map. In particular, we deduce a local uniqueness of the harmonic replacement of
n
-harmonic maps into a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{p}
. We also have the following proposition:

Proposition 3.1
.

There is a constant
\varepsilon_{0}
and a constant
C_{n}
such that for any
p\geq 2
and any
\tau
-approximated
n
-harmonic map
\Psi:\mathbb{B}_{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{p}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}\left|\nabla\Psi\right|_{g}^{n}\leq\varepsilon_{0}

we have for any ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{r}(p)\subset\mathbb{B}_{n}

r^{n}\left\|\nabla\Psi\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}\left(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{\frac{r}{2}}(p)\right)}^{n}\leq C_{n}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\Psi\right|_{g}^{n}dv_{g}

In order to prove Proposition
3.1
, we first prove that a
(\tau,n)
-harmonic map satisfies the following

Claim 3.1
.

For any
p\geq 2
and any
(\tau,n)
-harmonic map
\Psi_{\tau}:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{p}
, there is
(a_{i,j}^{\tau})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
such that for any
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\frac{1}{n}\left|X\right|^{2}\leq a_{i,j}^{\tau}X^{i}X^{j}\leq\frac{n-1}{n}\left|X\right|^{2}\text{ and }\forall i,j,\left|a_{i,j}^{\tau}\right|\leq 1

and such that

u\left(\kappa_{g}+\left|\nabla\Psi\right|_{g}^{2}\right)\geq P\left|\nabla\Psi\right|^{2}\left(\kappa_{g}+\left|\nabla\Psi\right|_{g}^{2}\right)\geq-div_{g}\left(a^{\tau}\nabla u\right)+\left|\nabla^{2}\Psi\right|P+\frac{n-2}{4}\mu^{n-4}\left|\nabla B\right|^{2}

where
B=\left|\nabla\Psi_{\tau}\right|_{g}^{2}
,
\mu=\left(B+\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
,
P=\mu^{n-2}
,

u=\mu^{n}
and
\kappa_{g}
is a constant such that
Ric_{g}\geq-\kappa_{g}g
.

Proof.

During the proof we set
\Psi=\Psi_{\tau}
,
u=u_{\tau}
and
B=\left|\nabla\Psi\right|_{g}^{2}
and
P=\left(B+\tau\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}
so that
u=PB
. We have that

\begin{split}&\nabla\Psi.\Delta\left(P\nabla\Psi\right)\\
=&\sum_{i}\left(-\nabla_{i}\left(\nabla\Psi.\nabla_{i}\left(P\nabla\Psi\right)\right)+\nabla_{i}\nabla\Psi.\nabla_{i}\left(P\nabla\Psi\right)\right)\\
=&-\sum_{i}\left(\nabla_{i}\left(\left|\nabla\Psi\right|^{2}\nabla_{i}P\right)+\nabla_{i}\left(\nabla_{i}\nabla\Psi.\nabla\Psi P\right)\right)\\
&+\sum_{i}\left(P\nabla_{i}\nabla\Psi.\nabla_{i}\nabla\Psi+\nabla_{i}P\nabla_{i}\nabla\Psi.\nabla\Psi\right)\\
=&-\sum_{i}\nabla_{i}\left(\left(\frac{n-2}{2}B\left(B+\tau\right)^{\frac{n-4}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}P\right)\nabla_{i}B\right)\\
&+\left|\nabla^{2}\Psi\right|P+\frac{n-2}{4}\left(B+\tau\right)^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\left|\nabla B\right|^{2}\\
\end{split}

-harmonic map
\Psi

the Laplacian acting on forms, so that

\begin{split}&\left\langle d\Psi,\Delta_{g}\left(Pd\Psi\right)\right\rangle_{g}=-\left\langle d\Psi,\left(d_{g}^{\star}d_{g}+d_{g}d_{g}^{\star}\right)\left(Pd\Psi\right)\right\rangle_{g}\\
=&-\left\langle d\Psi,d_{g}^{\star}\left(dP\wedge d\Psi\right)\right\rangle_{g}+\left\langle d\Psi,d\left(P\left|\nabla\Psi\right|_{g}^{2}\Psi\right)\right\rangle_{g}+P\left\langle d\Psi,\star\left((\star F)\wedge d\Psi)\right)\right\rangle_{g}\\

where we used for the second equality that

function, it satisfies the a priori estimate on the gradient for
x\in\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)

\left|\nabla\Phi(x)\right|_{g}^{2}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{B}_{2r}(p)}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|^{n}_{g}dv_{g}\leq C\delta_{n,g}

We have for
x\in\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)
that

\left|\phi_{1}(0)-\phi_{1}(x)\right|^{2}\leq r^{2}C_{g}\left|\nabla\Phi(z)\right|_{g}^{2}\leq C_{g}C\delta_{n,g}

so that taking
\delta_{n,g}
small enough, and up to a rotation of coordinates so that
\phi_{1}(0)=1
we can assume that

\forall x\in\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)\left|\phi_{1}(x)\right|\geq\frac{3}{4}.

Moreover, up to reduce
\delta_{n,g}
, and for
\tau
small enough
\Psi_{\tau}
has to satisfy

\forall x\in\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)\left|\left(\psi_{\tau,m}\right)_{1}(x)\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}

thanks to the same trick as in the proof of Step 1 of Claim
2.7
based on Courant-Lebesgue lemma and Morrey-Sobolev embeddings.

Step 2:
We set
\tilde{\Phi}_{m}=\frac{\Phi_{m}}{\left|\Phi_{m}\right|}
where
\Phi_{m}=(\phi_{0},\cdots,\phi_{m},0,\cdots)
. We denote
\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
the minimizer of

\Psi\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left(\left|\nabla\Psi\right|_{g}^{2}+\tau\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}dv_{g}

under the
W^{1,n}
maps
\Psi:M\to\mathbb{S}^{m}
such that
\left|\Psi\right|^{2}=_{a.e}1
and
\Psi=\tilde{\Phi}_{m}
on
\partial\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)
. we also set

\Psi_{\tau,m}=\left|\Phi_{m}\right|\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}+\Phi-\Phi_{m}\text{ so that }\Psi_{\tau,m}-\Phi=\left|\Phi_{m}\right|\left(\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}-\tilde{\Phi}_{m}\right).

We have:

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\left(\Phi-\Psi_{\tau,m}\right)\right|_{g}^{2}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n-2}-\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\Psi_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n-2}-\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n}\right)\\
=2\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n-2}\nabla\Phi.\nabla\left(\Phi-\Psi_{\tau,m}\right)=2\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n}\Phi.\left(\Phi-\Psi_{\tau,m}\right)\\
=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n}\left|\Phi-\Psi_{\tau,m}\right|^{2}\end{split}

and we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\left(\Phi-\Psi_{\tau,m}\right)-\nabla\ln\phi_{1}\left(\Phi-\Psi_{\tau,m}\right)\right|_{g}^{2}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n-2}\\
=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\left(\Phi-\Psi_{\tau,m}\right)\right|_{g}^{2}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n-2}-\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n}\left|\Phi-\Psi_{\tau,m}\right|^{2}\end{split}

so that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\left(\Phi-\Psi_{\tau,m}\right)-\nabla\ln\phi_{1}\left(\Phi-\Psi_{\tau,m}\right)\right|_{g}^{2}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n-2}=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\Psi_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n-2}\\
-\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n}\\
=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n-2}\left|\Phi_{m}\right|^{2}\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}-\left|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{m}\right|_{g}^{2}\right)\end{split}

Moreover, we have by similar computations that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}&\left|\nabla\left(\tilde{\Phi}_{m}-\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right)-\nabla\ln\left(\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right)_{1}\left(\tilde{\Phi}_{m}-\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right)\right|_{g}^{2}\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}+\tau\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{m}\right|_{g}^{2}\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}+\tau\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}-\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}+\tau\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\\
\end{split}

and we obtain

\begin{split}A:=&\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\left(\tilde{\Phi}_{m}-\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right)-\nabla\ln\left(\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right)_{1}\left(\tilde{\Phi}_{m}-\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right)\right|_{g}^{2}\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}+\tau\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\left(\Phi-\Psi_{\tau,m}\right)-\nabla\ln\phi_{1}\left(\Phi-\Psi_{\tau,m}\right)\right|_{g}^{2}\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n-2}\textdiv\\
\leq&\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}-\left|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{m}\right|_{g}^{2}\right)\left(\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n-2}\left|\Phi_{m}\right|^{2}-\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}+\tau\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\right)\\
\end{split}

so that splitting the right-hand side into three terms, we have

\begin{split}A\leq&\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}-\left|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{m}\right|_{g}^{2}\right)\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{m}\right|_{g}^{n-2}-\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{n-2}\right)\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}-\left|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{m}\right|_{g}^{2}\right)\left(\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n-2}\left|\Phi_{m}\right|^{2}-\left|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{m}\right|_{g}^{n-2}\right)\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}-\left|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{m}\right|_{g}^{2}\right)\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{n-2}-\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}+\tau\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\right)\end{split}

and since the first right-hand term is non positive, we obtain

\begin{split}A\leq&\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}+\left|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{m}\right|_{g}^{2}\right)\left|\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{n-2}\left|\Phi_{m}\right|^{2}-\left|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{m}\right|_{g}^{n-2}\right|\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}+\left|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{m}\right|_{g}^{2}\right)\left|\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{n-2}-\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}\right|_{g}^{2}+\tau\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\right|.\end{split}

Knowing that

\left|\nabla\Phi\right|^{2}=\left|\Phi_{m}\right|^{2}\left|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{m}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla\left|\Phi_{m}\right|\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla\left(\Phi-\Phi_{m}\right)\right|_{g}^{2}

and that
\Phi_{m}
and
\nabla\Phi_{m}
converge a.e to
\Phi
and
\nabla\Phi
, it is clear by the dominated convergence theorem that the first integral converges to
0
as
m\to+\infty
. Thanks to the a priori estimates of Claim
3.1
, up to a subsequence,
\Psi
is the limit as
(\tau,m)\to(0,+\infty)
of
\tilde{\Psi}_{\tau,m}
. We easily obtain that
\Psi
is also the limit of
\Psi_{\tau,m}
and that the second integral in the previous inequality converges to
0
as
(\tau,m)\to(0,+\infty)
. Moreover, if we set

Z=\{x\in\mathbb{B}_{r}(p);\left|\nabla\Psi\right|_{g}^{2}+\left|\nabla\Phi\right|_{g}^{2}=0\},

we have

\left|\nabla\left(\Phi-\Psi\right)-\nabla\ln\psi_{1}\left(\Phi-\Psi\right)\right|_{g}^{2}+\left|\nabla\left(\Phi-\Psi\right)-\nabla\ln\phi_{1}\left(\Phi-\Psi\right)\right|_{g}^{2}=0\text{ in }\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)\setminus Z.

so that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\left(\Phi-\Psi\right)\right|_{g}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)\setminus Z}\left|\nabla\left(\Phi-\Psi\right)\right|_{g}^{2}\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)\setminus Z}\left(\left|\nabla\ln\psi_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla\ln\phi_{1}\right|^{2}\right)\left|\Phi-\Psi\right|^{2}\\
\leq 2C\delta_{n,g}^{\frac{2}{n}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\frac{\left|\Phi-\Psi\right|^{2}}{\left(r-\left|x\right|\right)^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}\delta_{n,g}^{\frac{2}{n}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)}\left|\nabla\left(\Phi-\Psi\right)\right|_{g}^{2}\end{split}

where the second inequality comes from classical weak estimates on the gradient when it satisfies a
L^{\infty}

\varepsilon
-regularity property and the third one comes from a classical Hardy inequality. Letting
\delta_{n,g}
be small enough gives that
\Phi=\Psi
.
∎

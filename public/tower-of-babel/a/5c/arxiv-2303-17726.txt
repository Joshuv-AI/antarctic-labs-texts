On difference equations with ‘ B B ’-type solitons on three dimensional lattice.
Sergey M. Sergeev

Abstract
In this paper we discuss an example of classical integrable equation with rather unusual ‘B’-type Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (KP) soliton hierarchy.

1
Zero curvature problem: the Euler angles.

Let
\boldsymbol{g}
be an element of group
SO(3)
. It has the unique decomposition into a sequence of simple rotations called the Euler decomposition,

\boldsymbol{g}\;=\;\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\cos\phi&-\sin\phi&0\\
\sin\phi&\cos\phi&0\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right)}_{\displaystyle X_{12}(\phi)}\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\cos\theta&0&-\sin\theta\\
0&1&0\\
\sin\theta&0&\cos\theta\end{array}\right)}_{\displaystyle X_{13}(\theta)}\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&\cos\psi&-\sin\psi\\
0&\sin\psi&\cos\psi\end{array}\right)}_{\displaystyle X_{23}(\psi)}

(1.1)

where
\phi,\theta,\psi
are the Euler angles.

The same element
\boldsymbol{g}
can be parametrised by another triple of Euler angles corresponding to the opposite sequence of matrices
X_{\alpha\beta}
,

\boldsymbol{g}\;=\;X_{12}(\phi)X_{13}(\theta)X_{23}(\psi)\;=\;X_{23}(\psi^{\prime})X_{13}(\theta^{\prime})X_{12}(\phi^{\prime})\;.

(1.2)

Equation (
1.2
) thus defines uniquely the map

R\;:\;\;\phi,\theta,\psi\;\to\;\phi^{\prime},\theta^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}\;.

(1.3)

Since (
1.2
) can be seen as an auxiliary linear problem in
3d
, the map defines a classical lattice integrable model in
3d
.

Remarks.
Equation (
1.2
) is a particular case of Korepanov
3d
linear problem
[
1
,
2
]
. Euler angles decomposition as an example of a scalar solution to the Functional Tetrahedron equation was observed in
[
3
,
4
]
. See also
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
for the quantisation of the Korepanov model. Quantum reductions were studied in
[
8
]
. The aim of this paper is to discuss the result of reduction of the classical Korepanov’s general case to the particular case of Euler angles.

2
Lattice equation of motion.

Let
\vartheta\;:\;\mathbb{Z}^{2}\;\to\;\mathbb{C}
be the scalar field variable on cubic lattice. Define the lattice shift notations by

\vartheta\;=\;\vartheta(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})\;,\quad\vartheta_{1}\;=\;\vartheta(n_{1}+1,n_{2},n_{3})\;,\quad\textrm{etc.}

(2.1)

Equation (
1.2
) provides the lattice equation of motion by means of identification

\cos^{2}\psi\;=\;\eta_{1}^{2}\,\frac{\vartheta\vartheta_{23}}{\vartheta_{2}\vartheta_{3}}\;,\quad\cos^{2}\theta^{\prime}\;=\;\eta_{2}^{2}\,\frac{\vartheta\vartheta_{13}}{\vartheta_{1}\vartheta_{3}}\;,\quad\cos^{2}\phi\;=\;\eta_{3}^{2}\,\frac{\vartheta\vartheta_{12}}{\vartheta_{1}\vartheta_{2}}\;,

(2.2)

and

\cos^{2}\psi^{\prime}\;=\;\eta_{1}^{2}\,\frac{\vartheta_{1}\vartheta_{123}}{\vartheta_{12}\vartheta_{13}}\;,\quad\cos^{2}\theta\;=\;\eta_{2}^{2}\,\frac{\vartheta_{2}\vartheta_{123}}{\vartheta_{12}\vartheta_{13}}\;,\quad\cos^{2}\phi^{\prime}\;=\;\eta_{3}^{2}\,\frac{\vartheta_{3}\vartheta_{123}}{\vartheta_{13}\vartheta_{23}}\;.

(2.3)

Here
\eta_{k}
are constant coefficients.
The single non-trivial relation in (
1.2
) in new the lattice variables is

\eta_{1}^{2}\eta_{2}^{2}\eta_{3}^{2}\,\vartheta\vartheta_{123}\;=\;\eta_{1}^{2}\,\vartheta_{1}\vartheta_{23}\,+\,\eta_{2}^{2}\,\vartheta_{2}\vartheta_{13}\,+\,\eta_{3}^{2}\,\vartheta_{3}\vartheta_{12}\;-\;2\;\frac{\vartheta_{1}\vartheta_{2}\vartheta_{3}-H}{\vartheta}\;,

(2.4)

where

H^{2}\;=\;(\vartheta_{1}\vartheta_{2}\,-\,\eta_{3}^{2}\vartheta\vartheta_{12})(\vartheta_{1}\vartheta_{3}\,-\,\eta_{2}^{2}\vartheta\vartheta_{13})(\vartheta_{2}\vartheta_{3}\,-\,\eta_{1}^{2}\vartheta\vartheta_{23})\;.

(2.5)

3
‘AKP’, ‘BKP’ and ‘KDV’ solitons.

In this section we remind the definition of “rational
\Theta
-functions”.

Rational “
\Theta
-function” of KP type ‘A’ with “soliton modes”

(F,x,y),\quad(F^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),\quad\dots

(3.1)

is given by

\Theta([F,F^{\prime},\dots])\;=\;1\;+\;F\;+\;F^{\prime}\;+\;F\,F^{\prime}\,S_{A}\;+\;\cdots

(3.2)

where

S_{A}\;=\;\frac{(x-x^{\prime})(y-x^{\prime})}{(x-y^{\prime})(y-x^{\prime})}\;.

(3.3)

Expression (
3.2
) is written for two soliton modes. The general expression is uniquely defined by the principle

For a
g
-soliton
\Theta
-function with soliton amplitudes
F,F^{\prime},\dots,F^{(g-1)}
,

\Theta\;\to\;\Theta^{\prime}\;=\;\partial\Theta_{g}/\partial F^{(g-1)}

(3.4)

is a
(g-1)
-soliton
\Theta
-function with properly re-defined amplitudes.

In what follows, define

E(x,y)\;=\;\frac{x-y}{x+y}\;,

(3.5)

and the exponents

\omega_{k}\;=\;\frac{E(x,p_{k})}{E(y,p_{k})}\;,\quad\omega_{k}^{\prime}\;=\;\frac{E(x^{\prime},p_{k})}{E(y^{\prime},p_{k})}\;,\quad\textrm{etc.}

(3.6)

These exponents play important role in soliton solutions of difference equations. In particular, it is convenient to define the shifts of amplitudes by

\Theta_{k}\;=\;\Theta([F\omega_{k},F^{\prime}\omega_{k}^{\prime},\dots])\;.

(3.7)

Consider next a
(2g)
-soliton
\Theta
-function with the modes

(F,x,y)\;,\;\;(F^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\;,\;\;\dots\;\;(F^{(2g-1)},x^{(2g-1)},y^{(2g-1)})\;.

(3.8)

The key observation is that

\omega_{k}(x,y)\;=\;\omega_{k}(x^{(g)},y^{(g)})\;,\quad\omega_{k}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\;=\;\omega_{k}(x^{(g+1)},y^{(g+1)})\;,\quad\textrm{etc.}

(3.9)

if

x^{(g)}\;=\;-y\quad\textrm{and}\quad y^{(g)}\;=\;-x\;,\quad x^{(g+1)}\;=\;-y^{\prime}\quad\textrm{and}\quad y^{(g+1)}\;=\;-x^{\prime}\;,\quad\textrm{etc.}

(3.10)

Let us denote such situation by the collection of pairs

(F,\tilde{F},x,y),\quad(F^{\prime},\tilde{F}^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\;,\quad\dots\;,

(3.11)

where
\tilde{F}
stands for
F^{(g)}
, etc.
Three remarkable factorisation happens in this case. The first one is

Statement 3.1
.

For the pairs of the amplitudes chosen as

\Theta\;=\;\Theta([\left(\frac{2x}{(x+y)}f,\frac{2y}{(x+y)}f\right),\left(\frac{2x^{\prime}}{(x^{\prime}+y^{\prime})}f^{\prime},\frac{2y^{\prime}}{(x^{\prime}+y^{\prime})}f^{\prime}\right)\dots])\;,

(3.12)

the
\Theta
-function factorises into the complete square,

\Theta\;=\;\tau^{2}\;,

(3.13)

where
\tau
is the ‘B’-type theta-function,

\tau([f,f^{\prime},\dots])\;=\;1\;+\;f\;+\;f^{\prime}\;+\;f\,f^{\prime}\,S_{B}\;+\;\cdots\;

(3.14)

with

S_{B}\;=\;\frac{E(x,x^{\prime})E(y,y^{\prime})}{E(x,y^{\prime})E(y,x^{\prime})}\;.

(3.15)

Another example of the factorisation is

Statement 3.2
.

If the pairs of the amplitudes are chosen as

\Theta\;=\;\Theta([\left(\frac{2x}{(x+y)}\frac{(y+p_{k})}{(x+p_{k})}f,\frac{2y}{(x+y)}\frac{(x-p_{k})}{(y-p_{k})}f\right),\cdots])\;,

(3.16)

ane has

\Theta\;=\;\tau\tau_{k}\;,

(3.17)

where

\tau_{k}\;=\;\tau([f\omega_{k},f^{\prime}\omega_{k}^{\prime},\dots])\;,

(3.18)

cf. with (
3.7
).

An example of application of such factorisation will be given in the Appendix A.

The third example of complete square factorisation will be given in the next section.

The KDV rational

(3.19)

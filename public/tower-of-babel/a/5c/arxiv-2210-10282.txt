On eigenvalue problems involving the critical Hardy potential and Sobolev type inequalities with logarithmic weights in two dimensions
Megumi Sano, Futoshi Takahashi

Abstract
We consider the two-dimensional eigenvalue problem for the Laplacian with the Neumann boundary condition involving the critical Hardy potential. We prove the existence of the second eigenfunction and study its asymptotic behavior around the origin. A key tool is the Sobolev type inequality with a logarithmic weight, which is shown in this paper as an application of the weighted nonlinear potential theory.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth bounded domain with
0\in\overline{\Omega}
.
For simplicity, we assume
\sup_{x\in\Omega}|x|=1
without loss of generality.
Let
a\geq 1
.
In this paper, we consider the following linear eigenvalue problem

\displaystyle(N)\,\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda\frac{u}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\quad&\text{in}\,\,\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0\quad&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

here
\nu
denotes the unit outer normal vector to
\partial\Omega
.
The problem stems from the critical Hardy inequality on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

for functions in the Sobolev space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
: for any
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, it holds that

\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx.

(1)

Moreover, the constant
\frac{1}{4}
on the left-hand side is best possible and is not attained.
We recall that the Sobolev space
H^{1}(\Omega)
is a set of functions
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that its distributional gradient
\nabla u
is also in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

H^{1}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space with an inner product
(u,v)_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+uv)\,dx
, and

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is a closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=(u,u)_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{1/2}
.
For the inequality (
1
), we refer the readers to
[
11
]
,
[
15
]
,
[

]

[

]
and the references there in.

In a higher dimensional case, we know the subcritical Hardy inequality for functions in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
:

H_{N}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

holds for any
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, here
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
, with
0\in\Omega
.
The constant
H_{N}=\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}
is optimal and is never attained by a non zero function in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
In
[
6
]
, Chabrowski, Peral and Ruf consider the linear eigenvalue problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda\frac{u}{|x|^{2}}\quad&\text{in}\,\,\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},N\geq 3,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0\quad&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Clearly, the first eigenvalue is
\lambda=0
and constant functions are the first eigenfunctions.
To seek the nontrivial solution in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, the authors in
[
6
]
introduce the minimization problem

\displaystyle\lambda_{H}=\inf\left\{\,\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx}\,\,\middle|\,\,u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\},\,\int_{\Omega}\frac{u}{|x|^{2}}\,dx=0\,\right\},

and prove that if
\lambda_{H}<H_{N}
, then
\lambda_{H}
is attained and the minimizer corresponds the second eigenfunction of the above eigenvalue problem. Also the authors obtain several examples of domains such that the condition
\lambda_{H}<H_{N}
holds true.
Especially, they establish the existence of the second eigenfunction on balls in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 7
.
Moreover, they study the asymptotic behavior of the second eigenfunctions around the origin in the case
0\in\Omega
.
To obtain the asymptotic estimate of the second eigenfunction near the origin, they use De Giorgi-Nash-Moser type procedure and the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality
[
4
]
.

The aim of this paper is to extend the results in
[
6
]
to the two-dimensional problem (N).
Let
a\geq 1
and
0\in\overline{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
We consider the minimization problem

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}=\inf\left\{\,\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx}\,\,\middle|\,\,u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\},\,\int_{\Omega}\frac{u}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx=0\,\right\}.

(2)

We show

for

, see (

We seek for sufficient conditions to assure the existence of minimizers, which yiedls the second eigenfunction of the problem (N).
Our sufficient condition claims that if

and

is attained by a non trivial function in

We also study the asymptotic behavior near the origin of the second eigenfunctions.
We remark that, unlike

Furthermore, since our Hardy potential involves the logarithmic weights,

Therefore, we need to establish the Sobolev type inequality with logarithmic weights.
Combining this inequality with the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser procedure, we obtain the expected asymptotic behavior of the second eigenfunctions.

The critical Hardy type inequalities for
H^{1}(\Omega)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth bounded domain with
0\in\overline{\Omega}
and
\sup_{x\in\Omega}|x|=1
.
In §

, we prove the following Hardy-Sobolev type inequality with logarithmic weights.

Theorem 1
.

Let
a>1
,
p\geq 2
,
B<1
,
A\geq 1+\frac{p}{2}(1-B)
.
Then there exists a positive constant
C_{p,A,B}
such that the inequality

\displaystyle C_{p,A,B}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{2}(\log\frac{a}{|x|})^{A}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{B}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

(3)

holds for any
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Note that if we set

,

,

1

Also we remark that if

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}(x)=\begin{cases}\lambda^{-\frac{1-B}{2}}u\left(\left(\frac{|x|}{a}\right)^{\lambda-1}x\right)\,\,&\text{for}\,\,x\in B_{a^{\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}}},\\
0&\text{for}\,\,x\in B_{1}\setminus B_{a^{\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}}}.\end{cases}

When

, (

) does not have this scale invariance and by letting

,
we can easily show that the inequality does not hold when

.

The proof of Theorem
1
is postponed to §

We follow the argument of the proof of
[

\displaystyle C_{p,A,B}\left(\int_{B^{0}_{r}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{A}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}

(II)

\displaystyle=\left(\log\frac{a}{\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|h(x_{1})|^{2}-2x_{2}h(x_{1})}}\right)^{B}

for any

Assume

For any

(10)

Proof.

(I)
Assume that

.
Then there exists
\delta>0
such that

Let

on

Then, for any

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\delta}}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\delta}}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx

From the critical Hardy inequality (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(4+\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+C\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dx,

(II)
Assume that

Proposition 2

Assume that

,

From Proposition
1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(u-\int_{\Omega}\frac{ug}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx\right)^{2}\,\frac{dx}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}

Therefore, it is enough to show that there exists a positive constant

such that the inequality

\displaystyle\left\|\,u-\int_{\Omega}\frac{ug}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx\,\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\,\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\frac{\|\nabla u_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\left\|\,u_{m}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{m}\,g}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx\,\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\to 0\quad(m\to\infty).

3
Existence of the second eigenfunction

Recall the minimization problem (
2
):

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{A}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}\,\,\middle|\,\,\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx=1,\int_{\Omega}\frac{u}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx=0\,\right\}.

Main goal in this section is the following existence result.

Theorem 2
.

Assume
0\in\overline{\Omega}
and
a>1
.
If
\lambda_{a}<\frac{1}{4}
, then
\lambda_{a}
is attained.

Proof.

Let
\{u_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}
be a minimizing sequence for
\lambda_{a}
.
Thus, we have
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\to\lambda_{a}
as
m\to\infty
,
\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{m}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx=0
,
and
\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{m}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx=1
for each
m
.
Since
\frac{1}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\geq\min\left\{\frac{1}{(\log a)^{2}},\,\frac{e^{2}}{a^{2}}\right\}
for any
x\in\Omega
,
we have that
\{u_{m}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Thus we may assume that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&u_{m}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{in }H^{1}(\Omega),\\
&u_{m}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{in }L^{2}\left(\Omega,\frac{dx}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\right),\\
&u_{m}\to u\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega.\end{cases}

Therefore, we see that
\int_{\Omega}\frac{u}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx=0

and

0\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{m}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx=1.

We claim that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx=1.

(13)

If we show (
13
), then we have

\lambda_{a}\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx=\lambda_{a}

which implies that
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is a minimizer of
\lambda_{a}
.

First, we show that
u\not\equiv 0
. Assume the contrary that
u\equiv 0
.
Then by the compactness of the embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
, we see
\int_{\Omega}u_{m}^{2}\,dx\to 0
.
Thus by the critical Hardy inequality for
H^{1}(\Omega)
(
10
), we have

\displaystyle 1=\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{m}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(4+\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx+o(1)

\displaystyle\leq(4+\varepsilon)(\lambda_{a}+o(1))+o(1).

Letting
m\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have
1\leq 4\lambda_{a}
, which contradicts the assumption
\lambda_{a}<\frac{1}{4}
.
Therefore,
u\not\equiv 0
.
Let us assume by contradiction that (
13
) does not hold and
\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx<1
.
Since

u_{m}-u\rightharpoonup 0\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega)$}\quad\text{and}\quad\text{in $L^{2}\left(\Omega,\frac{dx}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\right)$},

we see
\int_{\Omega}|u_{m}-u|^{2}\,dx=o(1)
and by (
10
),
again we have

\displaystyle 0<1-\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{m}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{m}-u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx+o(1)

\displaystyle\leq(4+\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u_{m}-u)|^{2}\,dx+C\int_{\Omega}|u_{m}-u|^{2}\,dx+o(1)

\displaystyle\leq(4+\varepsilon)\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\right)+o(1)

\displaystyle=(4+\varepsilon)\lambda_{a}-(4+\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+o(1)

as
m\to\infty
.
Also since
\int_{\Omega}\frac{u}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx=0
,
we have
\lambda_{a}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

by the definition of
\lambda_{a}
.
Therefore letting
m\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle 1-\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx\leq(4+\varepsilon)\lambda_{a}\left(1-\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx\right),

which implies that
1\leq(4+\varepsilon)\lambda_{a}
.
Again, letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have a contradiction to the assumption of
\lambda_{a}
.
Therefore, we have (
13
).

The proof is now complete.
∎

In the end of this section, we give two examples of the domain which satisfies
\lambda_{a}<\frac{1}{4}

We say that the domain
\Omega
satisfies condition (A) if there exist
\delta
,
\theta_{*}
,
\theta^{*}>0
,

Proposition 3
.

If
\Omega
satisfies condition (A), then there exists a positive constant
C
such that
\lambda_{a}\leq C\log a
for
a>1
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{\delta}
be a smooth cut-off function which satisfies
\varphi_{\delta}(r)=1
for
r\in\left[1-\frac{\delta}{2},1\right]
,

\varphi_{\delta}(r)=0
for
r\in[0,1-\delta],0\leq\varphi_{\delta}\leq 1
, and
|\nabla\varphi_{\delta}|\leq C\delta^{-1}
.
Consider the following test function.

\displaystyle\phi_{\delta}(x)=\phi_{\delta}(r,\theta)=\begin{cases}r\varphi_{\delta}(r)\sin\left(\frac{2\pi(\theta-\theta_{*})}{\theta^{*}-\theta_{*}}\right)\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\theta\in[\theta_{*},\theta^{*}]\\
0&\text{if}\,\,\theta\not\in[\theta_{*},\theta^{*}]\end{cases}

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi_{\delta}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx=\left(\int_{1-\delta}^{1}\frac{\varphi_{\delta}(r)}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dr\right)\left(\int_{\theta_{*}}^{\theta^{*}}\sin\left(\frac{2\pi(\theta-\theta_{*})}{\theta^{*}-\theta_{*}}\right)\,d\theta\right)=0

Applying
\phi_{\delta}
to
\lambda_{a}
implies that

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{1-\delta}^{1}\int_{\theta_{*}}^{\theta^{*}}\left[\left|(r\varphi_{\delta})^{\prime}\right|^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi(\theta-\theta_{*})}{\theta^{*}-\theta_{*}}\right)+\varphi_{\delta}^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\theta^{*}-\theta_{*}}\right)^{2}\cos^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi(\theta-\theta_{*})}{\theta^{*}-\theta_{*}}\right)\right]\,r\,drd\theta}{\left(\int_{1-\delta/2}^{1}\frac{r}{\left(\log\frac{a}{r}\right)^{2}}\,dr\right)\left(\int_{\theta_{*}}^{\theta^{*}}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi(\theta-\theta_{*})}{\theta^{*}-\theta_{*}}\right)\,d\theta\right)}.

Since

\displaystyle\int_{1-\delta/2}^{1}\frac{r}{\left(\log\frac{a}{r}\right)^{2}}\,dr\geq\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{\log a}^{\log a-\log\left(1-\delta/2\right)}\frac{dt}{t^{2}}=O\left((\log a)^{-1}\right)

as
a\to 1
, we have
\lambda_{a}\leq C\log a
for
a>1
.
∎

For the next example, we say that a pair
(a,L)
is
admissible
if
a>1
,
0<L<1
, and

8\pi<\int_{B_{L}}\frac{1}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx.

holds.

Lemma 2
.

The set of admissible pairs
(a,L)
is non-empty.

Proof.

Indeed, since
\frac{1}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\in L^{1}(B_{1})
for any
a>1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{L\to 1-0}\int_{B_{L}}\frac{1}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{1}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx.

So it is enough to show that the following integral

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\frac{1}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx=2\pi\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r}{\left(\log\frac{a}{r}\right)^{2}}dr=2\pi a^{2}\int_{\log a}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-2t}}{t^{2}}dt

is large enough for some
a>1
and
0<L<1
.
Since

\displaystyle 2\pi a^{2}\int_{\log a}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-2t}}{t^{2}}dt

\displaystyle=2\pi a^{2}\left\{\left[\left(-\frac{1}{t}\right)e^{-2t}\right]_{\log a}^{\infty}-2\int_{\log a}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-2t}}{t}dt\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\log a}-4\pi a^{2}\int_{\log a}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-2t}}{t}dt,

we have

\displaystyle 4\pi a^{2}\int_{\log a}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-2t}}{t}dt

\displaystyle=4\pi a^{2}\left(\int_{\log a}^{\log(a+1)}\frac{e^{-2t}}{t}dt+\int_{\log(a+1)}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-2t}}{t}dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4\pi a^{2}\left(\int_{\log a}^{\log(a+1)}\frac{e^{-2\log a}}{t}dt+\int_{\log(a+1)}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-2t}}{\log(a+1)}dt\right)

\displaystyle=4\pi a^{2}\left(a^{-2}\int_{\log a}^{\log(a+1)}\frac{1}{t}dt+\frac{1}{\log(a+1)}\int_{\log(a+1)}^{\infty}e^{-2t}dt\right)

\displaystyle=4\pi a^{2}\left(a^{-2}\left(\log\log(a+1)-\log\log a\right)+\frac{1}{\log(a+1)}\frac{e^{-2\log(a+1)}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=4\pi\log\log(a+1)-4\pi\log\log a+\frac{2\pi}{\log(a+1)}\frac{a^{2}}{(a+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=-4\pi\log\log a+O(1)\quad(\text{as}\ a\to 1+0).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\frac{1}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\log a}-4\pi a^{2}\int_{\log a}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-2t}}{t}dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2\pi}{\log a}-\left(-4\pi\log\log a+O(1)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\log a}+4\pi\log\log a+O(1)\to+\infty\quad(a\to 1+0).

Then, there exists
a_{0}>1
such that

\int_{B_{1}}\frac{1}{\left(\log\frac{a_{0}}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx>16\pi
.
Therefore, there exists
L_{0}<1
such that

\int_{B_{L_{0}}}\frac{1}{\left(\log\frac{a_{0}}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx>8\pi
.
∎

Let

.

Proposition 4
.

\Omega
satisfies the condition

Proof.

Set

.
Note that

satisfies that
\int_{\Omega}\frac{u}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx=0
.
Testing
\lambda_{a}(\Omega)
by

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx=(\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2})|\Omega|.

From the symmetry, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx\geq\int_{B_{L}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx+\alpha_{2}^{2}\int_{B_{L}}\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx+2\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\int_{B_{L}}\frac{x_{1}x_{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}}{2}\int_{B_{L}}\frac{1}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx}\leq\frac{(\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2})|\Omega|}{\frac{\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}}{2}\int_{B_{L}}\frac{1}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx}=\frac{2|\Omega|}{\int_{B_{L}}\frac{1}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\pi}{\int_{B_{L}}\frac{1}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\,dx}<\frac{1}{4}

since
|\Omega|<|B_{1}|=\pi
and
(a,L)
is admissible.
Thus we have
\lambda_{a}(\Omega)<1/4
.
∎

,
we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{j+1}}}\frac{v^{2}v_{\ell}^{2s_{j+1}-2}}{|x|^{2}(\log\frac{a}{|x|})^{A}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2s_{j+1}}}\leq\left(\frac{Cs_{j}}{(\rho_{0}-\rho_{0}^{2})^{2}\rho_{0}^{2j}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2s_{j}}}\left(\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{j}}}\frac{v^{2}v^{2s_{j}-2}_{\ell}}{|x|^{2}(\log\frac{a}{|x|})^{A}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2s_{j}}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{j+1}}}v^{2}v_{\ell}^{2s_{j+1}-2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2s_{j+1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{Cs_{j}}{\rho_{0}^{2j}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2s_{j}}}\left(\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{j}}}v^{2}v^{2s_{j}-2}_{\ell}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2s_{j}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sqrt{C}\right)^{\sum_{k=0}^{j}s_{k}^{-1}}\rho_{0}^{-\sum_{k=0}^{j}ks_{k}^{-1}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{j}s_{k}^{\frac{1}{2s_{k}}}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r_{0}}}\frac{v^{2}v^{2s_{0}-2}_{\ell}}{|x|^{2}(\log\frac{a}{|x|})^{A}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2s_{0}}}

which implies that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap B_{\rho_{0}})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}\|v_{\ell}\|_{L^{2s_{j+1}}(\Omega\cap B_{\rho_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sqrt{C}\right)^{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}s_{k}^{-1}}\rho_{0}^{-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}ks_{k}^{-1}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}s_{k}^{\frac{1}{2s_{k}}}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since the infinite sums and the infinite product on the right-hand side of the above inequality are finite,
v\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap B_{\rho_{0}})
.
The desired result follows.
∎

5
The Robin boundary conditions

As in the former sections, we can consider the minimization problem

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}^{R}:=\inf\left\{\,\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\beta u^{2}dS}{\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}(\log\frac{a}{|x|})^{2}}\,dx}\,\,\middle|\,\,u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}\,\right\},

where
\beta
is a continuous function on
\partial\Omega
and
a>1
.
Then
\lambda_{a}^{R}
is the first (smallest) eigenvalue of the following linear eigenvalue problem with Robin boundary conditions

\displaystyle(R)\,\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda\frac{u}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2}}\quad&\text{in}\,\,\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta u=0\quad&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Clearly we have
\lambda_{a}^{R}\leq\frac{1}{4}
for any
\beta\in C(\partial\Omega)
by using
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
and the inequality (
1
).
In this section we prove some results about
\lambda_{a}^{R}
.
Since the arguments we use are similar to those in §
3
and §
4
,
we will omit the most proofs here.

First, we assume that
\beta
is a non-negative continuous function.

Theorem 4
.

(
\lambda_{a}^{R}
with a non-negative coefficient
\beta
)
Assume
0\in\overline{\Omega}
.
Let
a>1
,
\beta\geq 0
,
\beta\not\equiv 0
.
Then
\lambda_{a}^{R}>0
.
Furthermore, if
\lambda^{R}_{a}<\frac{1}{4}
, then
\lambda^{R}_{a}
is attained.

Remark 2
.

Let
\Omega=B_{R}(0)
.
Using the test function
u\equiv 1
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}^{R}\leq\frac{\int_{\partial\Omega}\beta\,dS}{\int_{\Omega}\frac{dx}{|x|^{2}(\log\frac{a}{|x|})^{2}}}\leq\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}R\log\frac{a}{R}.

Therefore,
\lambda^{R}_{a}<\frac{1}{4}
if
\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}R\log\frac{a}{R}<\frac{1}{4}
holds.

Proof.

First we show that
\lambda_{a}^{R}>0
. Obviously
\lambda_{a}^{R}\geq 0
.
Assume that
\lambda_{a}^{R}=0
.
Then there exists a sequence
\{u_{m}\}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\beta u_{m}^{2}\,dS\to 0\quad(m\to\infty),\quad\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{m}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}(\log\frac{a}{|x|})^{2}}\,dx=1\quad(\forall m\in\mathbb{N}).

Since
\beta\geq 0
, we have
\|\nabla u_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=o(1)
as
m\to\infty
and moreover
\left(\frac{1}{\log a}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{m}^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{m}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}(\log\frac{a}{|x|})^{2}}\,dx=1
.
Thus
\{u_{m}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\Omega)
and we may assume that
u_{m}\rightharpoonup u
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
for some
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,
u_{m}\to u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
,
since the embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
is compact.
Since
\nabla u_{m}\to 0
in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have
\nabla u=0
, that is
u
is a constant.
On the other hand,
\int_{\partial\Omega}\beta u_{m}^{2}dS=o(1)
implies
\int_{\partial\Omega}\beta u^{2}dS=0
, which is impossible if
u
is a non zero constant.
Thus we have
u\equiv 0
and
u_{m}\to 0
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
strongly.
However this contradicts
\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{m}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}(\log\frac{a}{|x|})^{2}}\,dx=1
and the critical Hardy inequality (
10
) for
u_{m}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Hence
\lambda_{a}^{R}>0
.

The attainability of
\lambda_{a}^{R}
can be shown by a minor modification of the proof of Theorem
2
.
We omit it here.
∎

In

In the case where

Recall the following Theorem.

Theorem A
.

(
[
1
]
Theorem 7.1)
Let
p>2
. Assume that
\omega
belongs to Muckenhoupt
A_{2}
-class,
g\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

Then the following two assertions are equivalent to each other:

\displaystyle(1)\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\,r>0}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}g(x)\,dx\right)J[\omega](x,r)^{\frac{p}{2}}<\infty,

\displaystyle(2)\,\text{There exists a constant}\,C>0\,\text{such that for any}\,f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\,\omega\,dx),

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\|I_{1}*f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\,g(x)\,dx)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\,\omega\,dx)}.

Here,
I_{s}(x)=\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2-s}{2}\right)}{2^{s}\pi\,\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)}|x|^{-(2-s)}

\displaystyle J[\omega](x,r)=\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\pi t^{2}}\left(\int_{B_{t}(x)}\frac{dy}{\omega(y)}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}

Proof of Theorem

Let

(I)

In this case, we can apply a theory of generalized rearrangement of functions (
[

20
]

Set

\displaystyle f(x)=\begin{cases}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{-B}\quad&\text{if}\,\,|x|\leq 1,\\
\left(\log a\right)^{-B}&\text{if}\,\,|x|\geq 1.\end{cases}

Since

is admissible. Thanks to zero extension, it is enough to show the inequality (
3
) for

.
Now, we define the rearrangement function
\mathcal{R}_{f}[u]
of

with respect to
f
as follows:
For

,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{f}[u](x)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{f}[u](|x|)=\sup\{\,t\geq 0\,|\,\,\mu_{f}[u](t)>\mu_{f}\left(B_{|x|}\right)\,\},

\displaystyle\mu_{f}(A)

\displaystyle=\int_{A}f(x)\,dx,

\displaystyle\mu_{f}[u](t)

\displaystyle=\mu_{f}\left(\,\{\,|u|>t\,\}\,\right)=\int_{\{\,|u|>t\,\}}f(x)\,dx.

In the case where

,

coincides with the well-known rearrangement

Given

such that

Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{A}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{\tilde{a}}{|x|}\right)^{A-B}}f(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|\,\mathcal{R}_{f}[u]\,|^{p}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{\tilde{a}}{|x|}\right)^{A-B}}f(x)\,dx\leq C\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|\,\mathcal{R}_{f}[u]\,|^{p}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{A}}\,dx,

where the second inequality comes from the Hardy-Littlewood type inequality, see e.g.
[
10
]
Proposition 4.4.
On the other hand, the Pólya-Szegö type inequality, see e.g.
[
10

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{B}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{f(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla\mathcal{R}_{f}[u]\,|^{2}}{f(x)}\,dx=\int_{B_{1}}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{B}|\nabla\mathcal{R}_{f}[u]\,|^{2}\,dx.

Therefore it is enough to show the inequality (

Here, we recall the Hardy type inequality with logarithmic weigts (see e.g.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1-B}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{2-B}}\,dx\leq\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{-B}}\,dx\quad(u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1}))

(17)

\displaystyle|u(x)|\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{1-B}}\,\left(\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{B}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|}\right)^{\frac{1-B}{2}}\,\,(u\in C_{c,{\rm rad}}^{\infty}(B_{1}))

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{2}(\log\frac{a}{|x|})^{A}}\,dx

Lemma 5
.

Let

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{\alpha}\,dy\leq CR^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{R}\right)^{\alpha}.

By a change of variables, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{\alpha}\,dy=2\pi\int_{0}^{R}\left(\log\frac{a}{s}\right)^{\alpha}s\,ds=2\pi a^{2}\int_{\log\frac{a}{R}}^{\infty}t^{\alpha}e^{-2t}dt.

\displaystyle 2\pi a^{2}\int_{\log\frac{a}{R}}^{\infty}t^{\alpha}e^{-2t}dt

\displaystyle=2\pi a^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{R}\right)^{\alpha}\left[\frac{e^{-2t}}{-2}\right]_{\log\frac{a}{R}}^{\infty}=\pi R^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{R}\right)^{\alpha}.

Next, we consider the case where

\displaystyle 2\pi a^{2}\int_{\log\frac{a}{R}}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-\beta}\cdot t^{\beta}e^{-2t}dt

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi a^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{R}\right)^{\beta}e^{-\beta}\int_{\log\frac{a}{R}}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-\beta}dt

\displaystyle=2\pi a^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{R}\right)^{\beta}\left(\frac{a}{R}\right)^{-2}\left[\frac{t^{\alpha-\beta+1}}{\alpha-\beta+1}\right]_{\log\frac{a}{R}}^{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{2\log\frac{a}{R}-(\alpha+1)}R^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{R}\right)^{\alpha+1}\leq\frac{2\pi}{1-\alpha}R^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{R}\right)^{\alpha},

where we have used

Proof of Lemma

A_{2}
-class, we show

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},r>0}S(x,r)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},r>0}\frac{1}{\pi^{2}r^{4}}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}\omega(y)\,dy\right)\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}\omega(y)^{-1}\,dy\right)<\infty.

(22)

Note that we are in the case
0<B<1
.
According to the value of
|x|
, we divide the proof into three parts.

(I)
The case where
x=0
.

First, we assume that
r\leq 1
. From Lemma
5
, we have

\displaystyle S(0,r)=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}r^{4}}\left(\int_{B_{r}(0)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{B}\,dy\right)\left(\int_{B_{r}(0)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{-B}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}r^{4}}\left(CR^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{R}\right)^{B}\right)\left(CR^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{R}\right)^{-B}\right)\leq C.

Next, we assume that
r>1
. Then we have

\displaystyle S(0,r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}r^{4}}\left(\int_{B_{1}(0)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{B}\,dy+\int_{B_{r}(0)\setminus B_{1}(0)}\left(\log a\right)^{B}|y|^{\gamma}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{B_{1}(0)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{-B}\,dy+\int_{B_{r}(0)\setminus B_{1}(0)}\left(\log a\right)^{-B}|y|^{-\gamma}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{4}}\left(1+r^{2+\gamma}\right)\left(1+r^{2-\gamma}\right)\leq\frac{C}{r^{2-\gamma}}+C<\infty

since
0<\gamma<2
.

(II)
The case where
0<|x|\leq 1
.

Let
M>0
be a large constant such that
M>\frac{1}{a-1}
.
We divide the prof into four parts.

(II-a)

The case where
0<r\leq\min\left\{\frac{|x|}{M},\,1-|x|\right\}
.

Then we see that
y\in B_{r}(x)\subset B_{|x|+r}(0)\setminus B_{|x|-r}(0)\subset B_{1}
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle S(x,r)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}r^{4}}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|-r}\right)^{B}\,dy\right)\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|+r}\right)^{-B}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\log\frac{a}{|x|}+\log\frac{|x|}{|x|-r}}{\log\frac{a}{|x|}+\log\frac{|x|}{|x|+r}}\right)^{B}\leq\left(\frac{\log\frac{a}{|x|}+\log\frac{M}{M-1}}{\log\frac{a}{|x|}+\log\frac{M}{M+1}}\right)^{B}<\infty

for any
x\in B_{1}(0)\setminus\{0\}
.
Here we have used
\frac{|x|}{|x|-r}\leq\frac{M}{M-1}
and
\frac{|x|}{|x|+r}\geq\frac{M}{M+1}
for
r\leq\frac{|x|}{M}
.

(II-b)

The case where
1-|x|\leq r\leq\frac{|x|}{M}
.

In this case, since
0<|x|\leq 1
, we have
0\leq r\leq\frac{1}{M}
and
\frac{M}{M+1}\leq|x|\leq 1
.
Then we see that
y\in B_{r}(x)\subset B_{|x|+r}(0)\setminus B_{|x|-r}(0)
and

|x|+r\leq\frac{M+1}{M}
,
|x|-r\geq\frac{M-1}{M+1}
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle S(x,r)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}r^{4}}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap B_{1}(0)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|-r}\right)^{B}\,dy+\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap B_{1}(0)^{c}}\left(\log a\right)^{B}(|x|+r)^{\gamma}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap B_{1}(0)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|+r}\right)^{-B}\,dy+\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap B_{1}(0)^{c}}\left(\log a\right)^{-B}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left(\log\frac{M+1}{M-1}a\right)^{B}+(\log a)^{B}\left(\frac{M+1}{M}\right)^{\gamma}\right)\left(\left(\log\frac{M}{M+1}a\right)^{-B}+(\log a)^{-B}\right)<\infty.

(II-c)
The case where
\frac{|x|}{M}\leq r\leq 1-|x|
.

In this case,
B_{r}(x)\subset B_{|x|+r}(0)\subset B_{1}(0)
. From Lemma
5
, we have

\displaystyle S(x,r)\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}r^{4}}\left(\int_{B_{|x|+r}(0)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{B}\,dy\right)\left(\int_{B_{|x|+r}(0)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{-B}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{4}}(|x|+r)^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|+r}\right)^{B}\cdot(|x|+r)^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|+r}\right)^{-B}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{|x|+r}{r}\right)^{4}\leq C\left(\frac{Mr+r}{r}\right)^{4}<\infty.

(II-d)
The case where
r\geq\max\left\{\frac{|x|}{M},\,1-|x|\right\}
.

Then we see that
r\geq\frac{1}{M+1}
is away from
0
and
|x|+r\leq(M+1)r
.
Therefore, by Lemma
5
, we have

\displaystyle S(x,r)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}r^{4}}\left(\int_{B_{1}(0)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{B}\,dy+\int_{B_{|x|+r}(0)\setminus B_{1}(0)}\left(\log a\right)^{B}|y|^{\gamma}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{B_{1}(0)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{-B}\,dy+\int_{B_{|x|+r}(0)\setminus B_{1}(0)}\left(\log a\right)^{-B}|y|^{-\gamma}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{4}}\left((\log a)^{B}+(\log a)^{B}\int_{0}^{(M+1)r}s^{\gamma+1}ds\right)\cdot\left((\log a)^{-B}+(\log a)^{-B}\int_{0}^{(M+1)r}s^{-\gamma+1}ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{4}}\left(1+r^{\gamma+2}\right)\left(1+r^{2-\gamma}\right)<\infty

since
0<\gamma<2
.

(III)
The case where
|x|>1
.

Again, we divide the proof into four cases.

(III-a)
The case where
0<r\leq\min\left\{\frac{|x|}{M},\,|x|-1\right\}
.

Then we see that
B_{r}(x)\cap B_{1}=\emptyset
.
Since
B_{|x|-r}(0)\subset B_{r}(x)\subset B_{|x|+r}(0)
, we have

\displaystyle S(x,r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}r^{4}}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}\left(\log a\right)^{B}|y|^{\gamma}\,dy\right)\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}\left(\log a\right)^{-B}|y|^{-\gamma}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{|x|+r}{|x|-r}\right)^{\gamma}=\left(1+\frac{2}{\frac{|x|}{r}-1}\right)^{\gamma}\leq\left(1+\frac{2}{M-1}\right)^{\gamma}<\infty.

(III-b)
The case where
|x|-1\leq r\leq\frac{|x|}{M}
.

The proof is the same as it in the case (II-b). We omit the proof.

(III-c)
The case where
\frac{|x|}{M}\leq r\leq|x|-1
.

Then we see that
B_{r}(x)\cap B_{1}=\emptyset
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle S(x,r)\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}r^{4}}\left(\int_{B_{|x|+r}(0)}|y|^{\gamma}\,dy\right)\left(\int_{B_{|x|+r}(0)}|y|^{-\gamma}\,dy\right)\leq C\left(\frac{r+|x|}{r}\right)^{4}=C\left(1+\frac{1}{M}\right)<\infty.

(III-d)
The case where
r\geq\max\left\{\frac{|x|}{M},\,|x|-1\right\}
.

The proof is the same as it in the case (II-d). We omit the proof.

Finally, we obtain (
22
), which ends the proof of Lemma
3
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4
:
Set

\displaystyle T(x,r)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap B_{1}(0)}g(y)\,dy=\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap B_{1}(0)}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{A}},

\displaystyle J(x,r)

\displaystyle=J[\omega](x,r)=\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\pi t^{2}}\left(\int_{B_{t}(x)}\frac{dy}{\omega(y)}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t},

where
g
and
\omega
are defined in (
19
) and (
20
).
Our goal is to show

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\,r>0}T(x,r)J(x,r)^{\frac{p}{2}}<\infty.

(23)

According to the value of
r>0
, we divide the proof into two parts.

(I)
The case where
0<r\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

(I-a)
The case where
r\leq\frac{|x|}{2}
and
|x|<\frac{2}{3}
.

Then we see that
\frac{|x|}{2}<|x|-r<|y|<|x|+r<\frac{3}{2}|x|<1
for
y\in B_{r}(x)
.
We fix
\tilde{a}>1
large enough so that the function
|y|^{-2}\left(\log\frac{\tilde{a}}{|y|}\right)^{-A}
is radially decreasing on
B_{1}
.
Then using
\frac{|x|}{2}<|y|
, we have

\displaystyle T(x,r)\leq C\int_{B_{r}(x)}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}\left(\log\frac{\tilde{a}}{|y|}\right)^{A}}\leq\frac{Cr^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{2\tilde{a}}{|x|}\right)^{A}}.

Set

\displaystyle J(x,r)

\displaystyle=\int_{r}^{\frac{|x|}{2}}\frac{1}{\pi t^{2}}\left(\int_{B_{t}(x)}\frac{dy}{\omega(y)}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}+\int_{\frac{|x|}{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\pi t^{2}}\left(\int_{B_{t}(x)}\frac{dy}{\omega(y)}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=:J_{1}(x,r)+J_{2}(x).

For
J_{1}
, since
\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{-B}\leq\left(\log\frac{2a}{3|x|}\right)^{-B}
for
|y|<\frac{3}{2}|x|
, we have

\displaystyle J_{1}(x,r)

\displaystyle=\int_{r}^{\frac{|x|}{2}}\frac{1}{\pi t^{2}}\left(\int_{B_{t}(x)}\frac{dy}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{B}}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}\leq\left(\log\frac{2a}{3|x|}\right)^{-B}\int_{r}^{\frac{|x|}{2}}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\left(\log\frac{2a}{3|x|}\right)^{-B}\log\frac{|x|}{2r}.

(24)

For
J_{2}
, note that
B_{t}(x)\subset B_{1}(0)
if
0<t<\frac{1}{3}
, since
|x|<\frac{2}{3}
.
Also note that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{dy}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{B}}\leq 2\pi(\log a)^{-B}<\infty,\quad\text{for }a>1,B>0,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)\cap B_{1}(0)^{c}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{\gamma}\left(\log a\right)^{B}}\leq(\log a)^{-B}\int_{1}^{R}s^{1-\gamma}\,ds\quad\text{for any }R>1.

Thus we have

\displaystyle J_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{|x|}{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\pi t^{2}}\left(\int_{B_{t}(x)\cap B_{1}(0)}\frac{dy}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{B}}+\int_{B_{t}(x)\cap B_{1}(0)^{c}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{\gamma}\left(\log a\right)^{B}}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\frac{|x|}{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\frac{1}{\pi t^{2}}\left(\int_{B_{t}(x)}\frac{dy}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{B}}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle+\int_{\frac{1}{3}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\pi t^{2}}\left(\int_{B_{t}(x)\cap B_{1}(0)}\frac{dy}{\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{B}}+\int_{B_{t+|x|}(0)\cap B_{1}(0)^{c}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{\gamma}\left(\log a\right)^{B}}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\log\frac{2a}{3|x|}\right)^{-B}\int_{\frac{|x|}{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\,\frac{dt}{t}+C(\log a)^{-B}\int_{\frac{1}{3}}^{\infty}\left(1+\int_{1}^{|x|+t}s^{1-\gamma}\,ds\right)\frac{dt}{t^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\log\frac{2a}{3|x|}\right)^{1-B}+C

(25)

since
0<B<1
and
0<\gamma<2
.
Combining (
6
) and (
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle T(x,r)J(x,r)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{p-2}{2}}T(x,r)\left(J_{1}(x,r)^{\frac{p}{2}}+J_{2}(x,r)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cr^{2}\left(\log\frac{|x|}{2r}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{2\tilde{a}}{|x|}\right)^{A}\left(\log\frac{2a}{3|x|}\right)^{\frac{Bp}{2}}}+\frac{Cr^{2}\left(\log\frac{2a}{3|x|}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}(1-B)}}{|x|^{2}\left(\log\frac{2\tilde{a}}{|x|}\right)^{A}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{r}{|x|}\right)^{2}\left(\log\frac{|x|}{2r}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+C,

where the last inequality follows from the assumption
A\geq 1+\frac{p}{2}(1-B)\geq\frac{p}{2}(1-B)
in Theorem
1
.
In the case (I-a), we have
\frac{r}{|x|}<1/2
.
Since the function
t\mapsto t^{2}(\log\frac{1}{2t})^{p/2}
is bounded for
0<t<1/2
, we have the result (
23
) in this case.

(I-b)
The case where
r\leq\frac{|x|}{2}
and
\frac{2}{3}<|x|<1
.

If
\frac{1}{3}<r
, then we easily show that
T(x,r)J(x,r)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq C
for some
C>0
independent of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
r>\frac{1}{3}
.
Therefore, we assume that
r\leq\frac{1}{3}
.
Since
\frac{1}{3}<|x|-r\leq|x|-t<|x|+t\leq|x|+r<\frac{4}{3}
for
t\in[r,\frac{1}{3}]
,
we have, as in the former case,

\displaystyle T(x,r)

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{2},

\displaystyle J(x,r)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{r}^{\frac{1}{3}}2\left(\log a\right)^{-B}\,\frac{dt}{t}+C(\log a)^{-B}\int_{\frac{1}{3}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\pi t^{2}}\left(1+\int_{1}^{1+t}s^{1-\gamma}\,ds\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\log\frac{1}{3r}+1\right).

Thus we obtain
T(x,r)J(x,r)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq Cr^{2}\left(\log\frac{1}{3r}+1\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}<\infty
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
0<r\leq\frac{1}{3}
.

(I-c)
The case where
r\leq\frac{|x|}{2}
and
|x|\geq 1
.

If
r\leq|x|-1
, then
T(x,r)=0
.
Therefore, we assume that
r>|x|-1
. Then we see that
|x|+t<r+1+t\leq\frac{3}{2}+t
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle T(x,r)

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{2},

\displaystyle J(x,r)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{r}^{\frac{1}{2}}2\left(\log a\right)^{-B}\,\frac{dt}{t}+C(\log a)^{-B}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\pi t^{2}}\left(1+\int_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}+t}s^{1-\gamma}\,ds\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\log\frac{1}{2r}+1\right).

Thus we obtain
T(x,r)J(x,r)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq Cr^{2}\left(\log\frac{1}{2r}+1\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}<\infty
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
0<r\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

(I-d)
The case where
\frac{|x|}{2}<r
.

We may assume that
r<\frac{1}{3}
. Then we see that
B_{r}(x)\subset B_{3r}(0)\subset B_{1}(0)
. First, we consider the case where
|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}
. From Lemma
5
, we have

\displaystyle T(x,r)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{3r}(0)}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{A}}=\frac{2\pi}{A-1}\left(\log\frac{a}{3r}\right)^{1-A},

\displaystyle J(x,r)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{r}^{1-|x|}\frac{1}{\pi t^{2}}\int_{B_{t+|x|}(0)}\left(\log\frac{a}{|y|}\right)^{-B}\,\frac{dt}{t}+C\int_{1-|x|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\pi t^{2}}\left(1+\int_{1}^{|x|+t}s^{1-\gamma}\,ds\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{r}^{1-|x|}\left(\frac{t+|x|}{t}\right)^{2}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|+t}\right)^{-B}\,\frac{dt}{t}+C\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\pi t^{2}}\left(1+(t+1)^{2-\gamma}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{r}^{1-|x|}3^{3}\left(\log\frac{a}{|x|+t}\right)^{-B}\,\frac{dt}{t+|x|}+C\leq C\left(\left(\log\frac{a}{r}\right)^{1-B}+1\right),

Thus we have

T(x,r)J(x,r)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq C\left(\log\frac{a}{3r}\right)^{1-A}\left(\left(\log\frac{a}{r}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}(1-B)}+1\right).

The last expression is finite since
A\geq 1+\frac{p}{2}(1-B)
.
We can also show the case where
\frac{1}{2}<|x|<1
in the same way as above, so we omit the proof.

(II)
The case where
r\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

If
|x|<2
, then we can easily see that
T(x,r)
and
J(x,r)
are finite. Also, if
r<|x|-1
, then
T(x,r)=0
. Therefore, we assume that
|x|\geq 2
and
r\geq|x|-1
.
In this case, we can also show in the same way as (I-c) that
T(x,r)
and
J(x,r)
are finite.
Therefore, we omit the proof.

Finally, we obtain (
23
), which ends the proof of Lemma
4
.
∎

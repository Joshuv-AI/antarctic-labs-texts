Quotient gradings and the intrinsic fundamental group
Yuval Ginosar, Ofir Schnabel

Abstract
. Quotient grading classes are essential participants in the computation of the intrinsic fundamental group π 1 ​ ( 𝒜 ) \pi_{1}(\mathcal{A}) of an algebra 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . In order to study quotient gradings of a finite-dimensional semisimple complex algebra 𝒜 \mathcal{A} it is sufficient to understand the quotient gradings of twisted group algebra gradings. We establish the graded structure of such quotients using Mackey’s obstruction class. Then, for matrix algebras 𝒜 = M n ​ ( ℂ ) \mathcal{A}=M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}) we tie up the concepts of braces, group-theoretic Lagrangians and elementary crossed products. We also manage to compute the intrinsic fundamental group of the diagonal algebras 𝒜 = ℂ 4 \mathcal{A}={\mathbb{C}}^{4} and 𝒜 = ℂ 5 \mathcal{A}={\mathbb{C}}^{5} .

Since it was firstly suggested by

and Definition

Our concern in this paper are the fundamental groups of finite-dimensional semisimple associative algebras
\mathcal{A}
.
In this regard, to-date

is fully in hand only

A

A

(1.2)

A

Due to this inclusion, the homogeneous

A

is a pair

If

A graded homomorphism

is a

if

is an algebra isomorphism and the group homomorphism

A quotient-morphism
(\psi,\phi)
between two graded algebras

1.3
) is a

is a

of (

Quotients of graded algebras can be given just in terms of a quotient of

However, if a TGA grading class
[{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}G]
of a complex matrix algebra does admit an elementary crossed product quotient grading class,
then this elementary crossed product class is maximal (not necessarily unique) among the elementary quotient grading classes of
[{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}G]
(see Corollary
5.7
). We therefore ask

Question 1.4
.

Let
G
be a CT group, and let
[\alpha]\in H^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{*})
be non-degenerate. For which normal subgroups
N\lhd G
(if at all)
is the quotient
[{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{${\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}G$}\left/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{$N$}\right.}]
an elementary crossed product grading class?

An answer to Question
1.4
involves the group-theoretical concept of a Lagrangian subgroup hereby explained. Let
[\alpha]\in H^{2}(G,{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
be a cohomology class of a finite group
G
.
A subgroup
H<G
is called
[\alpha]
-isotropic
if
[\alpha]
is trivial when restricted to
H
.
If, additionally,
[\alpha]
is non-degenerate and
|H|^{2}=|G|
, we say that
H
is a
Lagrangian
with respect to
[\alpha]
.
The following theorem answers Question
1.4
.

Theorem C
.

A quotient
[{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{${\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}G$}\left/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{$N$}\right.}]
of a TGA grading class
[{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}G]
of a simple complex algebra is an elementary crossed product grading class
if and only if the normal subgroup
N\lhd G
is an abelian Lagrangian with respect to the non-degenerate cohomology class
[\alpha]\in H^{2}(G,{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
.

As opposed to symplectic linear forms, non-degenerate cohomology classes do not necessarily admit normal Lagrangians as shown in
[
7
, §1]
.
Thus, by Theorem C there exist maximal elementary quotients of TGA grading classes which are not elementary crossed product gradings.
However, if a CT group
G
is either abelian or nilpotent of order which is free of eighth powers then any non-degenerate
[\alpha]\in H^{2}(G,{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
admits a normal Lagrangian

[
7
, Theorem 1.9]
.
By Theorem C, the corresponding simple twisted group algebras over these two families of CT groups admit elementary crossed product quotient classes, answering Question
1.4
.
For the first one of these families, namely of the abelian CT groups, the following result suggests a solution to Problem
1.3
in terms of elementary crossed product grading classes.

Theorem D
.

Let
A
be an abelian group of CT whose order is
n^{2}
and let
[\alpha]\in H^{2}(A,{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
be non-degenerate.

(1)

An elementary quotient grading class in
\text{Grad}(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))
is maximal in
[{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}A]_{{\mathbb{C}}}
if and only if it is an elementary crossed product grading class.

(2)

There is a unique maximal class in
\left[{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}A\right]_{{\mathbb{C}}}\subseteq\text{Grad}(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))

if and only if
A
is elementary abelian.

For the following remark, recall that (see §
2.1
) for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the group
A=C_{n}\times C_{n}
admits a non-degenerate cohomology class
[\alpha]\in H^{2}(A,{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
.

Remark E
.

In
[
10
, Proposition 4.15]
it is claimed that for every
n
the grading classes

[1({\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}C_{n}\times C_{n})]
and
[\tilde{x}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})]
of
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})

possess a

maximal common quotient grading class (graded by the cyclic group

), namely the elementary crossed product grading class
\left[\left(\sum_{h\in C_{n}}h\right)({\mathbb{C}})\right]
.
However, the proof of maximality of this class is not
precise. Moreover, by Theorem D for the abelian group

, the uniqueness part is true if and only if

is square-free.
Nevertheless, the computation of
\pi_{1}(M_{p}(\mathbb{C}))
in
[
10
]
is unaffected for primes
p
.

It is natural to pose a converse to Question
1.4
.

Question 1.5
.

Given a group
H
of order
n
,
does there exist a CT group
G
(of order

) and a non-degenerate

[\mathbb{C}^{\alpha}G

A highly non-trivial theorem says that CT groups are solvable
[

,
30

.
Consequently, a necessary condition for

It turns out that solvability of

A finite group

Identification of an IYB group

It was firstly introduced in

[

By

The answer to Question

yields an alternative characterization of braces as follows.

Theorem F
.

A group

of order
n
is IYB if and only if its corresponding elementary crossed-product grading class
\left[\sum_{h\in H}h(\mathbb{C})\right]
is a quotient of
some TGA grading class of
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Lastly, the intrinsic fundamental group of the diagonal algebra
{\mathbb{C}}^{i}
is
computed in
[

]
for

. The case

turns out to be incorrect. We fix

for

.

The intrinsic fundamental groups of the complex diagonal algebras of ranks 4 and 5 are

(1)

\pi_{1}({\mathbb{C}}^{4})\cong H_{4}\times C_{6}
, where
H_{4}
is the central extension

\quad 1\rightarrow\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\{\pm 1\}}}{{C_{2}}}\rightarrow H_{4}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta_{4}}}{{\rightarrow}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\langle a\rangle}}{{C_{2}}}*\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\langle b\rangle}}{{C_{2}}}\rightarrow 1

determined by
\bar{a}^{2}=\bar{b}^{2}=-1
for a choice of

2.
Preliminaries

We begin this section recording a group-theoretical result that is useful in §
3
. For a subgroup
G<\Gamma

let
[\Gamma:G]=\{\gamma G\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be the corresponding set of left cosets.
Any
\gamma\in\Gamma
gives rise to a one-to-one correspondence

\displaystyle\sigma_{\gamma}:\begin{array}[]{ccc}[\Gamma:G]&\rightarrow&[\Gamma:G]\\
\gamma^{\prime}G&\mapsto&\gamma\cdot\gamma^{\prime}G\end{array},

and hence determines a permutation in
\text{Sym}_{[\Gamma:G]}
. The following result can easily be verified and appears in many textbooks.

Lemma 2.1
.

With the above notation, the map

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Gamma&\rightarrow&\text{Sym}_{[\Gamma:G]}\\
\gamma&\mapsto&\sigma_{\gamma}\end{array}

is a group homomorphism determining a transitive left action of
\Gamma
on
[\Gamma:G]
.

2.1.
Ablian groups of central type and their non-degenerate classes

Let
A
be a finite abelian group. The second cohomology group
H^{2}(A,{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
is easily described as follows.
A function
\chi:A\times A\to{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
is called an
alternating bi-character
(see
[
18
, P. xx]
) if for every
\sigma,\tau,\eta\in A

(1)

\chi(\sigma\tau,\eta)=\chi(\sigma,\eta)\chi(\tau,\eta),

(2)

\chi(\sigma,\tau\eta)=\chi(\sigma,\tau)\chi(\sigma,\eta)
, and

(3)

\chi(\sigma,\sigma)=1
.

The set of alternating bi-characters of
A
is an abelian group itself with the pointwise multiplication.
Then, any 2-cocycle
\alpha\in Z^{2}(A,{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
determines an alternating bi-character

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha}:\begin{array}[]{rcl}A\times A&\to&{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\\
(\sigma,\tau)&\mapsto&\alpha(\sigma,\tau)\cdot\alpha(\tau,\sigma)^{-1}\end{array},

such that the rule
[\alpha]\mapsto\chi_{\alpha}
is a well-defined isomorphism between
H^{2}(A,{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
and the group of alternating bi-characters of
A
.
Explicitly, if
\{u_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\in A}
is a basis of invertible homogeneous elements of
{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}A
as in (
1.10
) then

(2.10)

\chi_{\alpha}(\sigma,\tau)=u_{\sigma}\cdot u_{\tau}\cdot u_{\sigma}^{-1}\cdot u_{\tau}^{-1}=[u_{\sigma},u_{\tau}],

that is the multiplicative commutator of the invertible elements
u_{\sigma}
and
u_{\tau}
for every
\sigma,\tau\in A
.
The following well-known theorem characterizes non-degeneracy in terms of alternating bi-characters.

Theorem 2.2
.

(see e.g.
[
25
, §3.1]
)
Let
A
be abelian group. Then
[\alpha]\in H^{2}(A,{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
is non-degenerate if and only if there exists a subgroup (which depends on
[\alpha]
)

(2.11)

A_{1}=\langle x_{1}\rangle\times\langle x_{2}\rangle\times\ldots\times\langle x_{r}\rangle\cong C_{n_{1}}\times C_{n_{2}}\times\ldots\times C_{n_{r}}

of
A
,
and an embedding of abelian groups
\phi:A_{1}\to A
, such that
A=A_{1}\times\phi(A_{1})
and whose corresponding alternating bi-character (
2.1
) is

\chi_{\alpha}:\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{l_{i}}\cdot\phi(x_{i})^{l^{\prime}_{i}},\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{m_{i}}\cdot\phi(x_{i})^{m^{\prime}_{i}}\right)\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{r}\zeta_{i}^{l_{i}\cdot m^{\prime}_{i}-l^{\prime}_{i}\cdot m_{i}},

where
\zeta_{i}
is a primitive
n_{i}
-th root of unity for every
1\leq i\leq r
.

In particular, for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
the group
C_{n}\times C_{n}
is of CT (see Remark E).
It is not hard to verify that in this case any choice of a cyclic subgroup
A_{1}<A
of order
n
is good for the decomposition in Theorem
2.2
.
The following is another immediate consequence of Theorem
2.2
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
B\subseteq\{1,\cdots,r\}
be any subset.
Then with the notation (
2.11
), the restriction of
[\alpha]
to
\langle\{x_{i}\}_{i\in B}\rangle\times\phi(\langle\{x_{i}\}_{i\in B}\rangle)
is non-degenerate as well.

2.2.
Graded modules, graded endomorphisms and induction

A left (right) module of a
\Gamma
-graded complex algebra (
1.1
) is
graded
if it affords a linear decomposition

(2.12)

\mathcal{W}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathcal{W}_{\gamma}

which respects the grading (
1.1
), that is
for every
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma

(2.13)

\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}}\mathcal{W}_{\gamma_{2}}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\gamma_{1}\cdot\gamma_{2}}(\text{respectively, }\mathcal{W}_{\gamma_{1}}\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\gamma_{1}\cdot\gamma_{2}}).

Let (
2.12
) be a graded left module over a graded algebra (
1.1
).
A
{\mathbb{C}}
-endomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{W}
is
left (right) homogeneous of degree
\gamma
if

\varphi(\mathcal{W}_{\gamma^{\prime}})\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\gamma\cdot\gamma^{\prime}}

(respectively,
\varphi(\mathcal{W}_{\gamma^{\prime}})\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\gamma^{\prime}\cdot\gamma}
) for every
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
.
The
{\mathbb{C}}
-space of left (right) homogeneous
\mathcal{W}
-endomorphisms of degree
\gamma\in\Gamma
is denoted by End
{}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{\textmd{l}(\gamma)}(\mathcal{W})

(respectively, End
{}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{\textmd{r}(\gamma)}(\mathcal{W})
).
The direct sums of these homogeneous subspaces of End
{}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{W})

are
\Gamma
-graded algebras denoted by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\text{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{\text{l}(\Gamma)}(\mathcal{W}):=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\text{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{\textmd{l}(\gamma)}(\mathcal{W}),\text{ and }\\
\text{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{\text{r}(\Gamma)}(\mathcal{W}):=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\text{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{\textmd{r}(\gamma)}(\mathcal{W}).\end{array}

The algebra of right graded endomorphisms
\text{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{\text{r}(\Gamma)}(\mathcal{W})
contains an important
\Gamma
-graded subalgebra

(2.17)

\text{End}^{\text{r}(\Gamma)}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})\subseteq\text{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{\text{r}(\Gamma)}(\mathcal{W})

of right graded endomorphisms of
\mathcal{W}
, which intertwine with its left
\mathcal{A}
-action. Then
\mathcal{W}
can be regarded as a
\Gamma
-graded

(\mathcal{A},\text{End}^{\text{r}(\Gamma)}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W}))
-bimodule.

If
\mathcal{W}
is finite-dimensional, or more generally, if supp
{}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{W})
is finite,
then both graded algebras (
2.2
) coincide with the ungraded endomorphism algebra
\text{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{W})
(see
[
18
, P. 2]
).

With the above notation, here is a description of induced gradings and their support in more details.
Let
V
be an
r
-dimensional vector space. As usual, this space is regarded as an End
{}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(V)
-module.
Let (
1.6
) be a
\Gamma
-grading of
V
. Can the group
\Gamma
grade the algebra End
{}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(V)
in such a way that
V
is a left graded module over it?
Indeed, the
\Gamma
-graded space (
1.6
) well-determines a character (
1.9
).
Let

(2.18)

(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{r})\in\Gamma^{r}

be any
r
-tuple of group elements, such that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\gamma_{i}
is equal to the character
x\in\mathbb{N}[\Gamma]

(evidently, the tuple can be ordered in few ways). Then there exists a base
\mathcal{E}:=\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{r}\}
of
V
such that
e_{i}\in V_{\gamma_{i}}
for every
i=1,\cdots,r
.

Let
E_{i,j}
be the elementary matrix whose
(i,j)
-th entry is 1, whereas all its other entries are 0. Considering this matrix as a
V
-endomorphism under
the choice of the base
\mathcal{E}
, then

\displaystyle E_{i,j}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}V_{\gamma_{j}}&\to&V_{\gamma_{i}},\text{ and }\\
V_{\gamma_{l}}&\to&0,\text{ if }l\neq j.\end{array}\right.

Impose now the demand that
V
should be a
\Gamma
-graded left module over End
{}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(V)
. Then from (
2.2
) it follows that
E_{i,j}

must be homogeneous of degree
\gamma_{i}\cdot\gamma_{j}^{-1}
.
Then under this
\Gamma
-grading the elementary matrices form a
{\mathbb{C}}
-basis of
M_{r}({\mathbb{C}})
consisting of homogeneous elements. Thus, the elementary
\Gamma
-grading

\mathcal{G}_{M_{r}({\mathbb{C}})}:\begin{array}[]{ccl}M_{r}({\mathbb{C}})&=&\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left(M_{r}({\mathbb{C}})\right)_{\gamma},\ \ \text{ where }\\
\left(M_{r}({\mathbb{C}})\right)_{\gamma}&=&\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\left\{E_{i,j}|\ \ \gamma=\gamma_{i}\cdot\gamma_{j}^{-1}\right\}\end{array}

admits

as a graded left module, as well as

More generally, let

\mathcal{A}^{r}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{A}_{i}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}e_{i}\cdot\mathcal{A}

be a free (right)
\mathcal{A}
-module of rank
r
with basis
e_{1},\cdots,e_{r}
.
As customary, the rule

(2.22)

E_{i,j}\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}}a:\ \ \ e_{l}\cdot a^{\prime}\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}e_{i}\cdot a\cdot a^{\prime},&\text{ if }l=j\\

furnishes

with a left
M_{r}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}}\mathcal{A}
-module structure. Clearly, right multiplication in the algebra
\mathcal{A}
intertwines with the left action (
2.22
).
Suppose now that
\mathcal{A}
possesses a (
1.1
), and let (
2.18
) be an
r
-tuple. Define the following two
\Gamma
-gradings.

(1)

Grade
\mathcal{A}^{r}
by letting

be homogeneous of degree
\gamma_{j}\cdot\gamma
.

(2)

Grade

by letting

be homogeneous of degree
\gamma_{i}\cdot\gamma\cdot\gamma_{j}^{-1}
.

Then these two gradings are compatible, that is
\mathcal{A}^{r}
is a
\Gamma
-graded left module over

The
\Gamma

is

) of

It can then be verified that for every

In particular, the support (see (

)) of the

is given by

\text{supp}_{\Gamma}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}n_{\gamma}\gamma(\mathcal{G_{A}})\right)=\left\{\gamma_{1}\cdot\gamma_{2}\cdot\gamma_{3}^{-1}|\ \gamma_{2}\in\text{supp}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{G_{A}}),\ \ n_{\gamma_{1}},n_{\gamma_{3}}>0\right\}.

Since

grades the algebra

, where

Graded induction respects the product in the semiring
{\mathbb{N}}[\Gamma]
as follows

\left[x_{1}(x_{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}))\right]=\left[x_{1}\cdot x_{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}})\right],\ \ \forall x_{1},x_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}[\Gamma].

For later use we recall that given a subgroup
G<\Gamma
, the

-coset
(see
[

, that is

. The corresponding quotient set is denoted

2.3.
Gradings of semisimple algebra and their quotients

Definition 2.4
.

A
graded ideal
of a group-graded algebra (
1.1
) is a graded submodule of the two-sided free module
\mathcal{A}.

In other words, it is a two-sided ideal
I
of
\mathcal{A}
satisfying

I=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}I\cap\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}.

A graded algebra is
graded-simple
if it admits no
non-trivial graded ideals.

The following is an important observation.

Theorem 2.5
.

(see e.g.
[
15
, Theorem 2.3’]
,
[
32
, §A.
1
]
)
Any group-grading of a semisimple finite-dimensional algebra admits a decomposition as a direct sum of graded-simple algebras.

TGA gradings, as well as gradings which are induced from such gradings, are natural
examples of
G
-simple gradings. The converse is also true due to

Theorem 2.6
.

[
4
, Theorem 5.1]
,
[
5
, Theorem 3]

Any
\Gamma
-simple grading class of a finite-dimensional complex algebra is of the form
[x({\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}G)]
, i.e. it is induced by some

from a TGA grading
{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}G
for some subgroup
G<\Gamma
and
\alpha\in Z^{2}(G,{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
.

The
elementary
part
x\in{\mathbb{N}}[\Gamma]
and the
fine
part
{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}G
of the simple grading in Theorem
2.6
are
determined up to a certain equivalence relation in
[
18
, Corollary 2.22]
(see also
[
25
, Theorem 2.20]
).

By Theorem
2.5
and Theorem
2.6
, any group grading of a complex
semisimple algebra is obtained from TGA gradings by direct
sums and inductions.
More explicitly, any grading class
[\mathcal{G_{A}}]
of a finite-dimensional semisimple complex algebra
\mathcal{A}
by a group
\Gamma
is a direct sum of, say,
l
simply-graded classes,
each of which are induced from TGA grading classes.
That is, for every

there exist

(1)

a subgroup

supporting the fine part of the

-th summand,

(2)

a 2-cocycle

and

(3)

an element

, responsible for the elementary part of the
i
-th summand,

decomposing
[\mathcal{G_{A}}]
as (
1.12
).

Let (
1.12
) be a
\Gamma
-grading class of a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra
\mathcal{A}
.
Let

be any normal subgroup.
By Theorem

, the corresponding quotient class
[{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{$\mathcal{G_{A}}$}\left/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{$N$}\right.}]
is a direct sum of quotients of its graded simple summands.
Furthermore, since induction commutes with quotient morphisms

, then
[{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{$\mathcal{G_{A}}$}\left/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{$N$}\right.}]
admits the decomposition (

).
Here

are just

(see (

)).
It should be remarked that the

) are no longer graded simple, and may themselves be decomposable into simply-graded constituents.

2.4.
Crossed products, the inertia group and the obstruction class

A graded algebra (

3.
On equi-dimensional gradings

Definition 3.1
.

A
\Gamma
-graded algebra (
1.1
) is

equi-dimensional
if

dim
{}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}})=
dim
{}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{2}})
for every

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in{\Gamma}
. A
\Gamma
-grading class is equi-dimensional if so are its members.

Crossed product grading classes, including TGA grading classes, are equi-dimensional. Furthermore, it is
easy to verify that the family of equi-dimensional grading classes is closed to quotients. Therefore, this property is helpful in the proof of Theorem A.
Here is a sufficient and necessary condition for a graded class induced from an equi-dimensional graded class to be equi-dimensional itself.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group, let
G<\Gamma
be a subgroup of finite index and let
[\mathcal{G_{A}}]
be an equi-dimensional
G
-graded class of an algebra
\mathcal{A}
.
Then the induced
\Gamma
-grading class

\left[\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}n_{\gamma}\gamma(\mathcal{G_{A}})\right]
is equi-dimensional if and only if

m_{\gamma_{1}G}=m_{\gamma_{2}G},\ \ \forall\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma,

where
m_{\gamma G}:=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\gamma G}n_{\gamma^{\prime}}
for the left coset
\gamma G\in[\Gamma:G]
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the corresponding matrix algebra over
\mathcal{A}
endowed with the induced grading
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}n_{\gamma}\gamma(\mathcal{G_{A}})
.
We compute dim
{}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{B}_{\gamma_{0}})
for
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
.
Let
d:=
dim
{}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
be the common dimension of all components of
\mathcal{A}
.
Then by (
2.23
), we have

\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{B}_{\gamma_{0}})=\sum n_{\gamma_{1}}\cdot d\cdot n_{\gamma_{2}},

where the sum is over all expressions of the form

(3.1)

\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{1}\cdot g\cdot\gamma_{2}^{-1},\ \ g\in G,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma.

With the notation (
2
) notice that expressions of the form (
3.1
) satisfy

\gamma_{1}\cdot g\in\sigma_{\gamma_{0}}(\gamma_{2}G).

Hence, the contribution to dim
{}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{B}_{\gamma_{0}})
for a fixed
\gamma_{2}
is

(3.2)

\sum_{\gamma\in\sigma_{\gamma_{0}}(\gamma_{2}G)}n_{\gamma}\cdot d\cdot n_{\gamma_{2}}.

Notice that the number (
3.2
) does not depend on the representative
\gamma_{2}
in its left coset. Therefore, the contribution of this entire coset
\gamma_{2}G

to dim
{}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{B}_{\gamma_{0}})
is

(3.3)

\sum_{\gamma\in\sigma_{\gamma_{0}}(\gamma_{2}G)}n_{\gamma}\cdot d\cdot\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\gamma_{2}G}n_{\gamma^{\prime}}=m_{\sigma_{\gamma_{0}}(\gamma_{2}G)}\cdot d\cdot m_{\gamma_{2}G}.

Summing (
3.3
) over all the cosets in
[\Gamma:G]
we have

(3.4)

\text{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{B}_{\gamma_{0}})=d\cdot\sum_{\gamma G\in[\Gamma:G]}m_{\gamma G}\cdot m_{\sigma_{\gamma_{0}}(\gamma G)}.

From (
3.4
) it is clear that if
m_{\gamma_{1}G}=m_{\gamma_{2}G}(=m
, say) for all
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
, then

\text{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{B}_{\gamma_{0}})=d\cdot m^{2}\cdot|[\Gamma:G]|,\ \ \forall\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma.

In particular, the grading on
\mathcal{B}
is equi-dimensional, proving the “if” direction.

Note that
\sigma_{e}=
Id
[Γ:G]
for the identity element
e\in\Gamma
. Hence by (
3.4
) we have

(3.5)

\text{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{B}_{e})=d\cdot\sum_{\gamma G\in[\Gamma:G]}m_{\gamma G}^{2}=d\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{\gamma G\in[\Gamma:G]}m^{2}_{\gamma G}}\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{\gamma G\in[\Gamma:G]}m^{2}_{\gamma G}}.

By reordering the summation under the square root on the right hand side of (
3.5
) with respect to the permutation
\sigma_{\gamma_{0}}
for any
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
we have

(3.6)

\text{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{B}_{e})=d\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{\gamma G\in[\Gamma:G]}m^{2}_{\gamma G}}\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{\gamma G\in[\Gamma:G]}m^{2}_{\sigma_{\gamma_{0}}(\gamma G)}},\ \ \ \forall\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma.

In particular, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the dimension (
3.4
) does not exceed the dimension (
3.6
), i.e. (compare with
[
24
, Theorem B]
)

(3.7)

\text{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{B}_{\gamma_{0}})\leq\text{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{B}_{e}),\ \ \ \forall\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma.

Now, assume that the grading on
\mathcal{B}
is equi-dimensional. In particular, the dimensions in (
3.4
) and (
3.6
) are equal for every
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
, and so

d\cdot\sum_{\gamma G\in[\Gamma:G]}m_{\gamma G}\cdot m_{\sigma_{\gamma_{0}}(\gamma G)}=d\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{\gamma G\in[\Gamma:G]}m^{2}_{\gamma G}}\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{\gamma G\in[\Gamma:G]}m^{2}_{\sigma_{\gamma_{0}}(\gamma G)}},\ \ \ \forall\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma.

Consequently, the equality condition in the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that for every
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
, the
|[\Gamma:G]|
-tuples

\left(m_{\gamma G}\right)_{\gamma G\in[\Gamma:G]}\text{ and }(m_{\sigma_{\gamma_{0}}(\gamma G)})_{\gamma G\in[\Gamma:G]}

are
\mathbb{Q}
-dependent. Since these are tuples of non-negative integers that defer by a permutation, we conclude from their linear dependence that they are equal. Finally, since the

\Gamma
-action (
2.1
) on

[\Gamma:G]
is transitive, these tuples are constant, that is
m_{\gamma_{1}G}=m_{\gamma_{2}G}
for any

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
. This proves the“only if” part.
∎

With the notation of Theorem
3.2
choose a transversal set
\mathcal{T}
of
G
in
\Gamma
. Then the theorem says that the induced
\Gamma
-grading is equi-dimensional if and only if
the element responsible for the induction can be given independently of
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
as

(3.8)

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}n_{\gamma}=m_{\gamma_{0}G}\cdot\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}t\in{\mathbb{N}}[\Gamma].

As mentioned in the beginning of this section, the equi-dimension property is closed to quotients. On the other hand,
by Theorem
2.5
, a semisimple finite-dimensional graded algebra decomposes into a direct sum of graded-simple algebras.
The following claim merges these two facts.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let (
1.1
) be an equi-dimensional
\Gamma
-grading of a semisimple finite-dimensional algebra
\mathcal{A}
and let
N\lhd\Gamma
.
Then every simply-graded summand of the quotient
{\raisebox{1.02228pt}{$\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}$}\left/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{$N$}\right.}
is equi-dimensional over

Proof.

Let
\Lambda_{\Gamma/N}
be a simply-graded summand of the quotient
{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{$\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}$}\left/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{$N$}\right.}
. We make use either of
[
24
, Theorem B]
, or of (

)
(noticing that by Theorem

Finally, the equi-dimensional property of the entire sum yields an equality in (

) for every
g\in\Gamma/N
running over all direct summands of
{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{$\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}$}\left/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{$N$}\right.}

∎

Returning to the setup of Theorem A, let

By equations (

With the above notation

Considering the graded basis (

\omega_{\mathcal{G}}([M])\in H^{2}({G/N},{\mathbb{C}}^{*})

{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\cong}}\bigoplus_{[M]\in\Omega}\text{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\omega_{[M]}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{G}}(M)}({{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}G}{\otimes}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}N}M),

Proof of (1).

Let

5.1.
Lagrangians

We borrow the bilinear form notation to the group-theoretic forms determined by 2-cocycles as in
[
7
]
.

Definition 5.8
.

[
7
]

A subgroup
N<G
is a
Lagrangian
with respect to the non-degenerate cohomology class
[\alpha]\in H^{2}(G,{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
if

(1)

N
is isotropic with respect to
[\alpha]
, that is res
{}^{G}_{N}([\alpha])=[1]
, and

|N|=|G/N|=\sqrt{|G|}
.

Alternatively, by,
[

]
(see also
[
27
, Lemma 1.2(i)]
), condition (1) in Definition

can be replaced by

(1^{\prime})
There is a 1-dimensional irreducible

-module.

If

is a non-degenerate class of an abelian group

it is not hard to verify that both

The following result fits
[

, Main Theorem]
to nilpotent groups.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
[\alpha]\in H^{2}(N,{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
, where

is a finite nilpotent group. Then there exists an
\alpha
-isotropic subgroup

with

In particular, if

is non-degenerate, then it admits a Lagrangian
[
7

Proof.

Since
N
is nilpotent, it is a direct product
N=\prod_{p}N_{p}
of its

-Sylow subgroups.
For every prime

be

-isotropic of minimal index in the

-Sylow subgroup

.
The direct product

is a subgroup of

Moreover, since the subgroups

are

.
By
[

the r.h.s. of (

-modules.
For a finite nilpotent group this g.c.d. is the minimum of such dimensions (see

-isotropic subgroup

The second part of the claim follows from the fact that

is non-degenerate, then the unique element

satisfies

. Therefore, the

-isotropic subgroup

as above is of index

and so is a Lagrangian.

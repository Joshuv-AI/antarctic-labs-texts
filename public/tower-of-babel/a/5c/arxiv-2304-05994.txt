Gravitational form factors of nuclei in the Skyrme model
Alberto García Martín-Caro, Miguel Huidobro, Yoshitaka Hatta

Abstract
We compute the gravitational form factor D ⁡ ( t ) D(t) of various nuclei in the generalized Skyrme model where nuclei are described as solitonic field configurations each with a definite baryon number B B . We separately discuss the cases B = 1 B=1 (nucleons), B = 2 B=2 (deuteron), B = 3 B=3 (helium-3 and tritium) and extrapolate to larger B B -values. Configurations with B > 1 B>1 are in general not spherically symmetric, and we demonstrate how group theory helps to extract the form factor. Numerical results are presented for the configurations with B = 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 , 32 , 108 B=1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,32,108 . We find that the B B -dependence is consistent with a power-law D ⁡ ( 0 ) ∝ B β D(0)\propto B^{\beta} with β = 1.7 ∼ 1.8 \beta=1.7\sim 1.8 . Other gravitational form factors can be calculated in the same framework, and we show the result for the J ⁡ ( t ) J(t) form factor associated with angular momentum for the B = 3 B=3 solution.

I
Introduction

The gravitational form factors (GFFs) are form factors associated with the QCD energy momentum tensor
T_{\mu\nu}

Kobzarev and Okun 1962
;
Pagels 1966
. Much like the electromagnetic form factors, they contain a wealth of information about the structure of the nucleons, or more generally hadrons and nuclei. However, unlike the latter which have enjoyed more than 70 years of continual theoretical and experimental efforts, it was not until recently that GFFs started to attract community-wide attention
Polyakov and Schweitzer 2018
. A major catalyst is the Electron-Ion Collider (EIC) project
Abdul Khalek et al. 2022
poised to uncover the mass and spin structure of the nucleons and nuclei. Since the energy momentum tensor encodes mechanical properties such as mass, angular momentum, internal forces and their distribution, the study of GFFs is perfectly aligned with the core missions of the EIC. In a sense, GFFs are more ‘quintessentially QCD’ than the electromagnetic form factors since they can be defined without reference to quark electric charges, and are directly sensitive to the gluonic degrees of freedom.

Among the various GFFs, of particular interest is the
D(t)
form factor related to the ‘pressure’ distribution inside hadrons and nuclei
Polyakov and Schweitzer 2018
. Its value at vanishing momentum transfer
D(t=0)
, often referred to as the D-term, is a fundamental constant similar to the magnetic moment. While the value is presently unknown, even for the nucleons, in principle the form factor
D(t)
can be experimentally accessed in Deeply Virtual Compton Scattering
Teryaev 2005
;
Anikin and Teryaev 2007
and near-threshold quarkonium photo- and electro-production in electron-nucleon scattering

Hatta and Yang 2018
;
Boussarie and Hatta 2020
;
Hatta and Strikman 2021
;
Sun et al. 2021
;
Guo et al. 2021
;
Kharzeev 2021
;
Mamo and Zahed 2022
. See
Burkert et al. 2018
;
Duran et al. 2023
;
Adhikari et al. 2023
for recent experimental efforts. The same methods could be used to measure the
D(t)
form factor of light nuclei at the EIC.

In this paper, we compute
D(t)
for a number of nuclei in the Skyrme model
Skyrme 1961
. In this model, the nucleon is realized as a finite-energy solitonic configuration of meson fields with a nontrivial topological number identified with the baryon number
B=1
. The model has been successful in explaining the low energy properties of the nucleon
Adkins et al. 1983
;
Schechter and Weigel 1999
. Subsequently it has been extended to describe light nuclei, such as the deuteron (
B=2
)
Verbaarschot et al. 1987
;
Braaten and Carson 1988
, the helium-3 or the tritium (
B=3
)
Carson 1991a
and so on. At the moment, solutions up to
B=108
are known
Feist et al. 2013
;
Gudnason and Halcrow 2022
and they have been even extrapolated to infinite crystals (
B=\infty
)
Klebanov 1985
;
Castillejo et al. 1989
to discuss the properties of nuclear matter
Adam et al. 2022
and neutron stars
Adam et al. 2020
;
Lee et al. 2022
. Moreover, low-lying excited states of various nuclei have been systematically studied
Walhout 1992
;
Manko et al. 2007
;
Bjarke Gudnason and Halcrow 2018
. Concerning form factors, the electromagnetic and axial form factors have been calculated for the
B=2

Braaten and Carson 1989
and
B=3

Carson 1991b
solutions, as well as for larger-
B
solutions with zero isospin
Karliner et al. 2016
. However, the application of the model to GFFs has been so far limited to the
B=1
sector (nucleons and their excited states)
Cebulla et al. 2007
;
Perevalova et al. 2016
;
Kim and Sun 2021
which is relatively tractable due to the spherical symmetry of the classical solution. In fact, all the known Skyrmion solutions with
B>1
are not spherically symmetric. Yet, each solution possesses a (discrete) symmetry group, and techniques from group theory can greatly facilitate the extraction of form factors as demonstrated by Carson
Carson 1991b
in the context of the electromagnetic form factors.
We shall adapt this method to the computation of GFFs using the state-of-the-art numerical Skyrmion solutions.

It should be mentioned that the form factor
D(t)
for nuclei has been evaluated in the liquid drop model
Polyakov 2003
, the Walecka model
Guzey and Siddikov 2006
and from the nonrelativistic nuclear spectral functions
Liuti and Taneja 2005
. We shall study the dependence of the D-term on the baryon number and compare the result with these previous works.

II
Skyrmions in the generalized Skyrme model

The generalized Skyrme model that we will consider is given by the following Lagrangian density,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm SK}=-\frac{f_{\pi}^{2}}{16}\Tr\{L_{\mu}L^{\mu}\}+\frac{1}{32e^{2}}\Tr\{[L_{\mu},L_{\nu}]^{2}\}-\lambda^{2}\pi^{4}B_{\mu}B^{\mu}+\frac{m_{\pi}^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}{8}\Tr\{U-\bm{1}\},

(1)

where we use the metric convention

(2)

Although only the first two terms (namely, the quadratic and quartic terms in

1

Such a term has recently proven to be crucial for an accurate description of the high density equation of state of neutron stars, allowing to reach sufficiently high maximum masses and sound velocities above the conformal limit

;
Adam et al. 2023

We will consider static solutions of the Skyrme field, and for numerical purposes we adopt the usual Skyrme units of energy and length,

E_{s}=\frac{3\pi^{2}f_{\pi}}{e},\quad x_{s}=\frac{1}{f_{\pi}e},

(4)

The static energy functional in these units becomes

\displaystyle E=\frac{1}{24\pi^{2}}\int d^{3}x

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{1}{2}\Tr\left\{L_{i}^{2}\right\}-\frac{1}{4}\Tr\left\{\left[L_{i},L_{j}\right]^{2}\right\}+8\lambda^{2}\pi^{4}f^{2}_{\pi}e^{4}({B}^{0})^{2}+\left(\frac{m_{\pi}}{f_{\pi}e}\right)^{2}\Tr\{\bm{1}-U\}\right]

\displaystyle\left[(\partial_{i}\phi_{\alpha})^{2}+\left(\partial_{i}\phi_{\alpha}\partial_{j}\phi_{\beta}-\partial_{i}\phi_{\beta}\partial_{j}\phi_{\alpha}\right)^{2}+c_{6}\left(\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\phi_{\alpha}\partial_{1}\phi_{\beta}\partial_{2}\phi_{\gamma}\partial_{3}\phi_{\delta}\right)^{2}+c_{0}(1-\sigma)\right],

(5)

The energy momentum tensor of the model can be obtained via the Hilbert prescription, which for our sign convention is given by

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Tr{-2L_\mu L_\nu+\eta_{\mu\nu}L_\rho L^\rho}-\frac{1}{4}\Tr{-4[L_\mu,L_\rho] [L_\nu,L^\rho]+\eta_{\mu\nu}[L_\rho,L_\sigma]^2}

We shall be mainly interested in the

\displaystyle\Tr{-L_i L_j}

II.1
Quantization

Nucleons and nuclei are described within the Skyrme model as classical solitonic configurations through the identification of the Skyrmion topological charge and the baryon number of nuclear states. However, other quantum numbers such as the spin and isospin of quantum nuclear states are not described at the classical level. Hence, a quantization of the Skyrmion field is needed in order to take into account the relevant quantum numbers. This is done in the semi-classical approach by promoting the zero modes of the soliton to dynamical degrees of freedom.

To do so, we introduce the rotational and iso-rotational degrees of freedom through a pair of time-dependent
SU(2)
transformations of the classical (static) solitonic solution,
representing the iso-rotation and the spatial rotation zero modes, respectively, as well as a time dependent vector
\vec{X}(t)
representing the translational zero modes:

U(t,\bm{x})=A(t)U_{0}(R_{B}(t)(\bm{x}-\bm{X}(t)))A^{\dagger}(t)

(10)

where
R^{ij}_{B}=\tfrac{1}{2}\Tr{\tau^i B\tau^jB^\dagger}\in SO(3)
is the corresponding rotation matrix in space.
A(t),B(t)\in SU(2)

and
{\bm{X}}(t)
together form the
collective coordinates
of the soliton.

III
The D term

In this section, we introduce the gravitational form factors and outline our strategy to compute the
D(t)
form factor for the
B=1
(nucleons),
B=2
(deuteron) and
B=3
(helium-3 and tritium) solutions. These three examples are representative of different situations one encounters in the computations of
D(t)
in the Skyrme model. Despite the differences, however, under certain assumptions we shall arrive at the same formula in all cases. This motivates us to extrapolate our discussion to solutions with arbitrary values of
B
. The actual computation of the form factor is carried out in the next section.

III.1
B=1
: the nucleons

We start with the nucleons (proton and neutron) with
B=1
.
The off-forward (i.e., nonzero momentum transfer) nucleon matrix element of the QCD energy momentum tensor can be parameterized as

\langle p^{\prime}|T_{\mu\nu}|p\rangle=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big[\gamma_{(\mu}P_{\nu)}A(t)+\frac{iP_{(\mu}\sigma_{\nu)\rho}q^{\rho}}{2M_{N}}B(t)+\frac{q_{\mu}q_{\nu}-\eta_{\mu\nu}q^{2}}{4M_{N}}D(t)\Big]u(p)

(22)

where
P_{\mu}=(p_{\mu}+p^{\prime}_{\mu})/2
and
q=p^{\prime}-p
is the momentum transfer with
t=q^{2}
.
A_{(\mu}B_{\nu)}=\frac{A_{\mu}B_{\nu}+A_{\nu}B_{\mu}}{2}
denotes symmetrization.
u(p)
is the nucleon spinor normalized as
\bar{u}(p)u(p)=2M_{N}
with
M_{N}
being the mass of the nucleon. The same formula applies to all the spin-1/2 nuclei with trivial changes. The gravitational form factors
A,B,D
are scalar, renormalization-group invariant functions of
t
. In the zero momentum transfer limit, i.e.
t=0
, the values of
A(t=0)
and
B(t=0)
are constrained to
1
and
0
due to momentum and angular momentum conservation, respectively. However, the so-called D-term
D=D(t=0)
is not constrained by any symmetry, and this is our main object of interest.

The
D(t)
form factor can be isolated by working in the Breit frame (
{\bm{p}}^{\prime}=-{\bm{p}}={\bm{q}}/2
,
t=-\bm{q}^{2}
) and taking the spatial
i,j=1,2,3

\langle p^{\prime}|T_{ij}|p\rangle=2P^{0}\frac{D(t)}{4M_{N}}(q_{i}q_{j}-\bm{q}^{2}\delta_{ij})\approx\frac{D(t)}{2}(q_{i}q_{j}-\bm{q}^{2}\delta_{ij}),

(23)

where we used the nonrelativistic approximation in the last expression. Classically, in the Skyrme model,
D(t)
can be obtained by simply Fourier-transforming the classical energy momentum tensor
T^{cl}_{ij}
(
7
). For spherically symmetric configurations such as the
B=1

T^{cl}_{ij}({\bm{x}})=\quantity(\frac{x_ix_j}{{x^2}}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij})s(x)+p(x)\delta_{ij}.

(24)

(Below we shall write
|\bm{x}|=x
and
|\bm{q}|=q

It then follows that

\displaystyle D(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-6M_{N}\int d^{3}x\left(x^{i}x^{j}-\frac{1}{3}\delta^{ij}x^{2}\right)\frac{j_{2}(qx)}{(qx)^{2}}T^{cl}_{ij}({\bm{x}}).

(25)

In particular,

\displaystyle D(t=0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2M_{N}}{5}\int d^{3}x\left(x_{i}x_{j}-\frac{1}{3}x^{2}\delta_{ij}\right)T^{cl}_{ij}({\bm{x}})

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4M_{N}}{15}\int d^{3}x\,x^{2}s(x).

We see that the D-term is related to the distribution of shear forces inside the nucleon, parametrized in the spherically symmetric case by the function
s(x)
.
It is interesting to note that in the so-called BPS Skyrme model, a solution that saturates the Bogomolny bound exists

From physical point of view, the passing of a gravitational wave causes a volume preserving deformation of the nucleon, which is precisely a symmetry in the BPS model
Adam et al. 2010

III.2
B=2
: the deuteron

Next we consider the
B=2
sector, the ‘deuteron’, with spin
J=1
. For spin-1 nuclei, there are in general six independent gravitational form factors
Holstein 2006
related to the fact that the nuclear wavefunction is not spherically symmetric. In particular, it is well-known that the deuteron wavefunction has quadrupole deformation. In terms of the energy momentum tensor matrix element, this is most clearly shown by the following multipole expansion in the Breit frame in the nonrelativistic limit
Polyakov and Sun 2019

\displaystyle\langle p^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}|T_{ij}|p\sigma\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(q_{i}q_{j}-\delta_{ij}q^{2}){\cal D}_{1}(t)\epsilon^{*}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\cdot\epsilon_{\sigma}+\left(q_{j}q_{k}Q_{ik}+q_{i}q_{k}Q_{jk}-q^{2}Q_{ij}-\delta_{ij}q_{k}q_{l}Q_{kl}\right)_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}{\cal D}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2M_{D}^{2}}(q_{i}q_{j}-\delta_{ij}q^{2})q_{k}q_{l}Q_{kl,\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}{\cal D}_{3}(t)+\cdots

(32)

where
M_{D}
is the deuteron mass.
\epsilon_{\sigma}
are the polarization vectors of the deuteron with spin
\sigma=\pm 1,0
measured along the
x^{3}
-direction (not helicity).
Q_{ij,\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}\equiv\langle\sigma^{\prime}|\hat{Q}_{ij}|\sigma\rangle
is the matrix element of the quadrupole operator

\hat{Q}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left(J_{i}J_{j}+J_{j}J_{i}-\frac{4}{3}\delta_{ij}\right),

(33)

where
J_{i}
is the spin-1 operator with matrix elements
\langle\sigma^{\prime}|J_{i}|\sigma\rangle=-i\epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{\sigma^{\prime}j}^{*}\epsilon_{\sigma k}
.
While the quadrupole part is of interest in its own right (see recent extractions for the
\rho
-meson
Freese and Cloët 2019
;
Pefkou et al. 2022
), in this work we focus on the monopole part
{\cal D}_{1}
. The

\sum_{\sigma}^{\pm,0}Q_{ij,\sigma\sigma}=0.

(34)

Restricting ourselves to this simpler situation, we write

\frac{1}{3}\sum_{\sigma}\langle p^{\prime}\sigma|T_{ij}|p\sigma\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(q_{i}q_{j}-\delta_{ij}q^{2})D(t),

(35)

where we renamed

to emphasize the correspondence with (
23

Turning now to the Skryme model, we recall that the classical
B=2
solution has toroidal symmetry

Braaten and Carson 1988
and is mainly characterized by a c-number quadrupole tensor
Q_{ij}

Polyakov and Sun 2019

\displaystyle T^{cl}_{ij}\approx Y_{2}^{ij}s(x)+p(x)\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2s^{\prime}(x)\left(Q_{ik}Y_{2}^{kj}+Q_{jk}Y_{2}^{ki}-\delta_{ij}Q_{ab}Y_{2}^{ab}\right)+p^{\prime}(x)Q_{ij}

(36)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{M_{D}^{2}}Q^{kl}\partial_{k}\partial_{l}\left(p^{\prime\prime}(x)\delta^{ij}+s^{\prime\prime}(x)Y_{2}^{ij}\right),

where we abbreviated
Y_{2}^{ij}=\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{x^{2}}-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{3}
. In principle,
Q_{ij}
can be numerically extracted from a given Skyrmion configuration. But in parallel with the spin-averaging procedure above, we eliminate it by forming the moment (
25
) and using the integrals

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\Omega Y_{2}^{ij}=0,\qquad\int d^{2}\Omega Y_{2}^{ij}\left(Q_{ik}Y_{2}^{kj}+Q_{jk}Y_{2}^{ki}-\delta_{ij}Q_{ab}Y_{2}^{ab}\right)=0,

\displaystyle Q^{kl}\int d^{2}\Omega Y_{2}^{ij}\partial_{k}\partial_{l}\left(s^{\prime\prime}(x)Y_{2}^{ij}\right)=0,

(37)

where we used the traceless property
Q_{ii}=0

D(t)
form factor as defined in (

25

M_{N}\to M_{D}
even if the classical solution is not spherical.

III.3
B=3
and beyond

Next we turn to the
B=3
sector relevant to the helium-3 nucleus
(^{3}
He) and the tritium
(^{3}
H). Since they have spin
1/2

B=3

Carson 1991a
(see
fig.

In order to answer this question, we need some elements of group theory. The tetrahedral group
T_{d}

Tinkham 1954
consists of 24 discrete transformations such as a 120
o
rotation around one of the four vertices of a tetrahedron.
It turns out that this symmetry imposes strong constraints on various moments of classical configurations
Carson 1991b
. For example, consider the following integral

A_{ab}=\int d^{3}xT^{cl}_{ab}(\bm{x})j_{0}(qx),

(38)

which appears in the
l=0
partial wave in (
29
). Let
g
be an element of the tetrahedral group. Changing variables as
{\bm{x}}\to g{\bm{x}}
, we find

A_{ab}=g^{T}_{ai}g^{T}_{bj}\int d^{3}x^{\prime}T^{\prime cl}_{ij}(\bm{x}^{\prime})j_{0}(qx^{\prime})=g^{T}_{ai}g^{T}_{bj}A_{ij}

(39)

where we used
T^{\prime cl}_{ij}=T^{cl}_{ij}
due to symmetry. This means that
A_{ab}\propto\delta_{ab}
. Mathematically, the integral transforms as the singlet
(A_{1})
representation contained in the product of two vector representations (
T_{1}
) of the tetrahedral group

T_{1}\times T_{1}=A_{1}+E+T_{1}+T_{2},

(40)

along with the two-dimensional (
E
) and axial vector (
T_{2}
) representations
Tinkham 1954
.

Consider, then, the transformation properties of the tensor
T_{abkl}
defined in (
30
) under the tetrahedral group. Since the trace part of the energy momentum tensor
T_{ab}^{cl}
does not play a role,
T_{abkl}
can be viewed as the product of two symmetric and traceless tensors formed by
T_{1}
vectors

(T_{1}\times T_{1})\times(T_{1}\times T_{1}),

(41)

where
(T_{1}\times T_{1})=E+T_{1}
denotes the traceless part.
We are interested in the components that transform as the irreducible, singlet representation
A_{1}
. There are two such structures
3
3

3

One coming from
E\times E=A_{1}+A_{2}+E

T_{1}\times T_{1}=A_{1}+E+T_{1}+T_{2}
. The cross term
E\times T_{1}=T_{1}+T_{2}
does not contribute to the trivial representation.
which can be rearranged in the form
Carson 1991b

\displaystyle T_{abkl}=\frac{1}{10}\left(\delta_{ak}\delta_{bl}+\delta_{al}\delta_{bk}-\frac{2}{3}\delta_{ab}\delta_{kl}\right)T_{cdcd}+C_{abkl}.

(42)

The first structure is the same as before (
31
). The second tensor

is symmetric and traceless in any pair of indices

(see
Carson 1991b
for the details). Plugging (

\displaystyle\left.\bra{\bm{q}/2}T_{ij}(-R{\bm{X}})\ket{-\bm{q}/2}\right|_{l=2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(q_{i}q_{j}-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{3}q^{2}\right)\frac{T_{cdcd}}{10}

(43)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(q_{c}q_{d}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{cd}q^{2}\right)R_{ia}^{T}(B)R_{jb}^{T}(B)R_{ck}^{T}(B)R_{dl}^{T}(B)C_{ab,kl}.

The operator in the second term is totally symmetric and traceless in the four indices
i,j,c,d
. It is thus a spin-4 operator whose matrix element between spin-1/2 states vanishes. The first term then leads to the same formula (
25
) for the
D
-form factor. We now appreciate the effect of the rotation matrix
R
in the present calculation. Had we neglected
R

R_{ia}^{T}\to\delta_{ia}
etc. in (

q_{i}
, in contradiction with the unique tensor structure (
23
) for a spin-1/2 nucleus. By introducing
R

The above argument can be broadly generalized. Each Skyrmion solution possesses a symmetry group
Bjarke Gudnason and Halcrow 2018
. For
B\geq 3
, the group consists of discrete symmetry transformations and this puts strong constraints on the possible tensor structures of the integral (
30
). For example, the
B=4
solution (the helium-4 nucleus, or the ‘alpha’ particle) has cubic symmetry
Braaten et al. 1990
. The associated octahedral group
O_{h}
has irreducible representations which correspond to those of the tetrahedral group
A_{1}\to A_{1g}
,
T_{1}\to T_{1u}
, etc. We can then immediately conclude that the
D
-form factor is again given by (
25
).
4
4

4

For a spinless nucleus such as the helium-4, there are only two GFFs which we parameterize as

\langle p^{\prime}|T_{\mu\nu}|p\rangle=2A(t)P_{\mu}P_{\nu}+\frac{D(t)}{2}(q_{\mu}q_{\nu}-\eta_{\mu\nu}q^{2}).

(44)

A similar argument can be repeated for larger-
B
solutions. (Spin-averaging is understood for spin
\geq 1
nuclei.) We thus use (
25
), with
M_{N}
replaced by nuclear masses, as a working definition for all nuclei in the Skyrme model.

IV
Angular momentum form factor
J(t)

Although our main interest in this paper is the
D(t)
-form factor, our approach can be straightforwardly generalized to the other gravitational form factors. As an example, let us consider the form factor

J(t)=\frac{1}{2}(A(t)+B(t)),

(45)

for spin-
1/2
nuclei where
A(t),B(t)
are defined in (
22
). Physically,
J(t)
represents the form factor associated with the total angular momentum of the system. The forward value
J(0)=1/2
is constrained by angular momentum conservation. In the Skyrme model,
J(t)
has been computed for the
B=1
solutions
Cebulla et al. 2007
;
Kim and Sun 2021
. Here, for the first time, we compute it for the
B=3
(helium-3, tritium) solution.

In the Breit frame, the
J
-form factor appears in the following components of the energy momentum tensor matrix element (
22
)

\displaystyle\frac{\bra{p^{\prime}, s^{\prime}}T_{00}(0)\ket{ p, s}}{2P^{0}}=M\left[A(t)-\frac{t}{4M^{2}}\left[A(t)-2J(t)+D(t)\right]\right]\delta_{ss^{\prime}}

(46)

\displaystyle\frac{\bra{p^{\prime}, s^{\prime}}T_{0i}(0)\ket{ p, s}}{2P^{0}}=-J(t)\,i\epsilon_{ijk}\frac{\tau^{j}_{s^{\prime}s}}{2}q^{k}.

(47)

Note that the mixed (timelike-spacelike) components vanish in the classical limit which corresponds to a static configuration. Once the quantization of the Skyrmion is taken into account, we find a nonzero, operator-valued result

T_{0i}=I_{ij}a^{j}-J_{ij}b^{j},

(48)

where

\displaystyle I_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{24\pi^{2}}\quantity[ \Tr{L_{i} T_{j}}+\Tr{\left[L_{k}, L_{i}\right]\left[L_{k}, T_{j}\right]}],

(49)

\displaystyle J_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24\pi^{2}}\epsilon_{jkl}x^{k}\quantity[ \Tr{L_{i} L_{l}}+\Tr{\left[L_{a}, L_{i}\right]\left[L_{a}, L_{l}\right]}].

(50)

Both of the above tensor densities transform as the product of a vector and an axial vector, i.e., they belong to the
T_{2}\times T_{1}
representation which does not contain an irreducible trivial component
A_{1}
.
Therefore, their contribution must vanish at first order (
l=0
) in the partial wave expansion (
29
). At the next order
l=1
, we find

\displaystyle\left.\langle\bm{q}/2|T_{0i}(-R\bm{X})|-\bm{q}/2\rangle\right|_{l=1}=

\displaystyle\,R_{ij}^{T}(B)\int d^{3}x\exp{i\bm{q}\cdot R^T(B)\bm{x}}T_{0j}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,iR_{ij}^{T}(B)R_{kl}^{T}(B)q^{k}\int d^{3}x\frac{3j_{1}(qx)}{qx}\quantity[I_{jm}a^m-J_{jm}b^m]x^{l}.

(51)

Defining the currents

I_{ijk}(q)=\int d^{3}x\frac{3j_{1}(qx)}{qx}I_{ij}x^{k},\qquad J_{ijk}(q)=\int d^{3}x\frac{3j_{1}(qx)}{qx}J_{ij}x^{k},

(52)

we can identify their irreducible component that transforms as
A_{1}\in T_{2}\times T_{1}\times T_{1}
, which is totally antisymmetric in the three indices
Carson 1991b
:

I_{ijk}=\epsilon_{ijk}\mathcal{I}(q),\qquad J_{ijk}=\epsilon_{ijk}\mathcal{J}(q),

(53)

where

\mathcal{I}(q)=\frac{1}{3!}\epsilon^{abc}I_{abc}(q),\qquad\mathcal{J}(q)=\frac{1}{3!}\epsilon^{abc}J_{abc}(q).

(54)

Substituting (
53
) into (
51
), we get

\displaystyle\langle\bm{q}/2|T_{0i}(-R\bm{X})|-\bm{q}/2\rangle=

\displaystyle\,iR_{ij}^{T}(B)R_{kl}^{T}(B)q^{k}\epsilon_{jml}\quantity[\mathcal{I}(q)a^m-\mathcal{J}(q)b^m]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,i\epsilon_{ijk}q^{k}R_{jm}^{T}(B)\quantity[\mathcal{I}(q)a^m-\mathcal{J}(q)b^m].

(55)

At this point, we need to invert the relations in (
18

This is a complicated task in general, but for the specific
B=3
solution at hand,
the tensors defined in

14

U_{ij}=u\delta_{ij},V_{ij}=v\delta_{ij},W_{ij}=w\delta_{ij}

Carson 1991b

a^{m}=\frac{vK^{m}+wL^{m}}{uv-w^{2}},\qquad b^{m}=\frac{wK^{m}+uL^{m}}{uv-w^{2}},

(56)

and we may write

R_{lm}^{T}(B)\quantity[\mathcal{I}(q)a^m-\mathcal{J}(q)b^m]=\frac{1}{uv-w^{2}}\quantity{\qty[\mathcal{I}(q)(w-v)+\mathcal{J}(q)(w-u)]R^T_{lm}(B)L^m+\qty[v\mathcal{I}(q)-w\mathcal{J}(q)] R_{lm}(B)M^m},

(59)

V
Numerical results

In order to compute the gravitational form factors of light nuclei, we start by generating the static energy-minimizing classical field configuration corresponding to each of the topological sectors. We do this by first constructing a numerical ansatz using the rational map approximation
Manko et al. 2007
, and allow this initial configuration to relax to the true minimizer via gradient descent.
We remark that this is an increasingly difficult task for values of the topological charge
B\gtrsim 8
, not only due to the increase of the solution’s mean radius, which requires a numerical simulation on a lattice with an increasing number of points, but also because of the flatness of the field configuration landscape, which in general presents multiple local minima with very similar energies but different symmetries and shapes, as recently shown by Gudnason and Halcrow
Gudnason and Halcrow 2022
. This fact points towards the failure of the rigid quantization approximation—in which quantum effects do not modify the classical shape of the Skyrmions—for higher baryon charges in general. We have, nevertheless, constructed also solutions with large baryon charge and cubic symmetry, namely, the
B=32=4\times 2^{3}
and
B=108=4\times 3^{3}
Skyrmions, which are expected to not be affected by spin nor isospin quantum effects in their ground state, and are considered to be the minimum energy configurations due to their high degree of symmetry. These solutions are illustrated in

VI
Conclusions

In this paper, we have calculated one of the gravitational form factors, the
D(t)
-form factor, for nuclei with baryon numbers
B=1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,32,108
in the Skyrme model. While the electromagnetic and axial form factors have been previously computed for light nuclei in this model
Braaten and Carson 1989
;
Carson 1991b
, its application to nuclear GFFs is new. Despite different patterns of nuclear deformation, the monopole
D(t)
-form factor is given by the single formula (
25
) which can be readily evaluated for a given classical configuration. Our approach can be generalized to other GFFs and we have presented a concrete example, the angular momentum form factor
J(t)
, for the
B=3
solution.

There are a number of future directions. In this work we neglected the
{\cal O}(J^{2},I^{2})
terms in (
28
) invoking the large-
N_{c}
approximation. However, these quantum corrections are necessary to classify different isobars and study the detailed spin and isospin effects for individual nuclei and their excited states. For the
B=1
solution, they have been included in
Kim and Sun 2021
to compute the GFFs of the
\Delta
-resonances. Additional complications arise for
B>1

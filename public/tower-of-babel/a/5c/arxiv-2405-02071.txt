Spacelike initial data for black hole stability
Allen Juntao Fang, Jérémie Szeftel, Arthur Touati

Abstract
. We construct initial data suitable for the Kerr stability conjecture, that is, solutions to the constraint equations on a spacelike hypersurface with boundary entering the black hole horizon that are arbitrarily decaying perturbations of a Kerr initial data set. This results from a more general perturbative construction on any asymptotically flat initial data set with r − 1 r^{-1} fall-off and the topology of ℝ 3 ∖ { r < 1 } {\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{r<1\} enjoying some analyticity near and at the boundary. In particular, we design a suitable mixed boundary condition for the elliptic operator of the conformal method in order to exclude the Killing initial data sets (KIDS).



1.
Introduction

The Kerr family of
stationary black holes is generally believed to
describe the black holes observed in astrophysics. An important question regarding
whether or not this belief is substantiated is the stability property
of this family. In this article, we construct initial data for the Kerr stability problem.

1.4.
Construction of initial data sets

There is a rich literature on the constraint equations (
1.2
) and we refer the reader for example to
[
Car21
]
for a detailed review. The main two approaches to produce solutions to these equations are the conformal method and the gluing method. First introduced in
[
Lic44
]
, the conformal method proposes a conformal ansatz for
(g,k)
and transforms the quasi-linear underdetermined system (
1.2
) into a semi-linear elliptic system for a scalar function and a vector field. This approach has been successful in constructing many interesting solutions to the constraint equations, including those with large mean curvature or with apparent horizons
[
AL22
,
DIMM14
,
HNT09
,
HM15
,
HMT18
,
Max05
,
Max09
]
. However these results fall short of constructing perturbations of a given black hole satisfying (
1.3
). This is due to the fact that these constructions are typically done within the range of invertibility of the relevant elliptic operators resulting in perturbations that decay like
r^{-\delta}
for
0<\delta<1
, falling short
4
4

4

For instance, applying Theorem 1.17 from
[
Bar86
]
could improve the decay of the solutions but only modulo harmonic polynomials.
of the decay rates assumed in the aforementioned stability results mentioned right after Theorem
1.2
.

The gluing method, first introduced in
[
Cor00
]
and later extended in
[
CS06
,
CD03
]
, consists of gluing any asymptotically flat solution of (
1.2
) to an exact Kerr initial data set outside a far away annulus. However, critically, these constructions, viewed as a perturbation of the background black hole metric, are compactly supported, whereas we aim to construct non-compactly supported initial data.

Remark 1.3
.

Gluing results use the Kerr initial data, viewed as a 10-parameter family, to saturate the 10-dimensional space of linear obstructions coming from the symmetries of Minkowski. Such families are called admissible families in
[
CS06
]
(see also
[
CH20
]
) and reference families in
[
CD03
]
. Theorem
1.2
exhibits infinitely many such admissible or reference families with the same asymptotics as Kerr at main order. As a result, one should be able to glue asymptotically flat initial data to arbitrarily decaying perturbations of Kerr.

Remark 1.4
.

In Theorem 8.9 of
[
CD03
]
, initial data coinciding to arbitrary order with Simon-Beig approximate stationary vacuum spacetimes (see
[
SB83
]
) are constructed. This gives particular examples of initial data sets close to a given Kerr with arbitrary fall-off. Given that they are parametrized by the formal stationary solutions of
[
SB83
]
, they form a very thin subset of the solutions constructed in this paper. Indeed, consider as an analogy the scalar wave equation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{1+3}
with data
(\psi_{0},\psi_{1})
: in that context, the equivalent of our solutions is the whole space
H^{2}_{-q-\delta}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\times H^{1}_{-q-\delta-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
while the equivalent of the solutions constructed in Theorem 8.9 of
[
CD03
]
is the subspace
\left\{\text{$\Delta\psi_{0}=0$ outside of a ball}\right\}\times\{\psi_{1}=0\}
.

The authors have previously constructed and parametrized initial data perturbations of Minkowski space with arbitrary decay
[
FST24
]
. While the overall methodology is similar, there are two key differences between the current paper and
[
FST24
]
. We first recall that in
[
FST24
]
the Killing initial data sets (KIDS) presented a fundamental, linear obstacle to the construction. Since black hole spacetimes have fewer Killing vectorfields than Minkowski, there are fewer KIDS to deal with in the present paper than in
[
FST24
]
. In addition, motivated by the stability of Minkowski, initial data were constructed in
[
FST24
]
for a manifold without boundary. On the other hand, in the present paper, initial data are constructed for a manifold with boundary, reflecting the presence of an event horizon. The presence of the boundary adds flexibility, as the elliptic problem to be solved does not itself impose boundary conditions. This will play a crucial role in the ensuing proof, where we take advantage of this freedom.

1.5.
Sketch of proof

We now give a brief sketch of the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Rather than considering initial data
(\Sigma,g,k)
it will be convenient to instead consider initial data triplets
(\Sigma,g,\pi)
, where
\pi=k-(\mathrm{tr}_{g}k)g
will be referred to as the
reduced second fundamental form
of
\Sigma
.

Using the conformal method, we consider as an ansatz
(g,\pi)
of the form

(1.4)

\displaystyle(g,\pi)=\left((1+\widecheck{u})^{4}\left(g_{m,a}+\widecheck{g}\right),(1+\widecheck{u})^{2}\left(\pi_{m,a}+\widecheck{\pi}+L_{g_{m,a}}\widecheck{X}\right)\right),

where
L_{g_{m,a}}
is a modified Lie derivative (see
(
3.2
)), and where
(\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi})
are the ”seed” of
the construction and enjoy smallness and decay satisfying

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{k=0,1}\left(r^{k}|\nabla^{k}\widecheck{g}|+r^{k+1}|\nabla^{k}\widecheck{\pi}|\right)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{(1+r)^{q+\delta}}.

Remark 1.5
.

The conformal ansatz (
1.4
) does not correspond to the most standard conformal formulation of the constraint, which usually makes use of the conformal Killing operator to distinguish between the tracefree and trace part of the second fundamental form. Here, our goal is to study the perturbative regime for the constraint equations, a setting where it suffices to consider the less precise ansatz of
[
CS06
]
.

The constraint equations (
1.2
) for
(g,\pi)
then become the following system of PDEs on
\Sigma

(1.6)

\displaystyle P\left(\widecheck{u},\widecheck{X}\right)=D\Phi[g_{m,a},\pi_{m,a}](\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi})+\text{non-linear terms},

where
P
is an elliptic operator depending only on
(g_{m,a},\pi_{m,a})
, and
D\Phi[g_{m,a},\pi_{m,a}]
denotes the linearization of the constraint operator at
(g_{m,a},\pi_{m,a})
.
Theorem

1.2
then asks whether there exists a solution
\left(\widecheck{u},\widecheck{X}\right)
to (
1.6
) satisfying

(1.7)

\displaystyle|\widecheck{u}|+r\left|\nabla\widecheck{X}\right|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{(1+r)^{q+\delta}}.

We remark that applying the conformal method does not impose boundary conditions on
\partial\Sigma
for the solutions
\left(\widecheck{u},\widecheck{X}\right)
to (
1.6
), which will play a critical role as mentioned above.

To address this question, we study the behavior of the elliptic operator
P
on weighted Sobolev spaces

Using qualitative properties of elliptic operators to understand the decay of their solutions is a classical method, and we illustrate the basic idea behind with toy model of the scalar Laplacian on Euclidean space before addressing the full problem at hand. The mapping properties of the Laplacian

(1.8)

\Delta:H^{2}_{\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\longrightarrow L^{2}_{\delta-2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

are well studied
[
Bar86
,
McO79
]
, and in particular, it is known that the Laplacian as defined in (
1.8
) is a Fredholm operator, if
\delta\notin\mathbb{Z}
. This implies, via the Fredholm alternative, that provided
h
is orthogonal to the kernel of
\Delta^{*}
, then
\Delta\varphi=h
is solvable. Thus, given some
h\in L^{2}_{\delta-2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, finding a perturbation
\tilde{h}
such that
\Delta\varphi=h+\tilde{h}
is solvable in
H^{2}_{\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
reduces to solving for
\tilde{h}
such that

(1.9)

\left\langle h+\tilde{h},w\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=0,\quad\text{for all }w\in\ker(\Delta^{*}).

In this case of the Laplacian, the kernel of
\Delta^{*}
can be explicitly described and it consists just of harmonic polynomials. Thus, a suitable perturbation
\tilde{h}
can be explicitly solved for. In particular, this toy model emphasizes the fact that a full characterization of the cokernel of the elliptic operator under consideration is crucial.

We now return to giving a sketch of the proof in our setting. It can be verified that
P
is also a Fredholm operator acting between weighted Sobolev spaces. However, characterizing the kernel of
P^{*}
is more delicate. We characterize the kernel of
P^{*}
as perturbations of the harmonic polynomials. The critical tool will be a good choice of mixed boundary condition on
\partial\Sigma
of the form

(1.10)

\displaystyle(B_{\nu}+F)\left(\widecheck{u},\widecheck{X}\right)=0,

such that the operator
(P,B_{\nu}+F)
acting on
H^{2}_{-\delta}(\Sigma)
for
0<\delta<1
will be injective, and where
B_{\nu}
is a Neumann type boundary operator defined on
\partial\Sigma
. We will also require that
F
vanishes identically on an open subset of
\partial\Sigma
and is the identity on another open subset. This condition on
F
is not necessary to characterize the kernel of
P^{*}
but will play a key role in eliminating certain obstructions in what follows.

Remark 1.6
.

As opposed to the boundary condition considered for instance in
[
Max05
]
, our boundary condition (
1.10
) has no geometric interpretation. In addition to ensuring solvability of our elliptic problem with appropriately decaying solutions, it will also allow us to exclude the KIDS, as we will see below.

To ensure the orthogonality conditions necessary for
P
to be invertible, we will modify the ansatz
(
1.4

2-tensors
(\breve{g},\breve{\pi})
such that the elliptic system
reads

(1.11)

\displaystyle P\left(\widecheck{u},\widecheck{X}\right)=D\Phi[g_{m,a},\pi_{m,a}]\left((\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi})+(\breve{g},\breve{\pi})\right)+\text{non-linear terms}.

The correctors
(\breve{g},\breve{\pi})
will then be chosen in a
4q^{2}
dimensional space such as to ensure the following analogue of (
1.9

(1.12)

\displaystyle\left\langle(\breve{g},\breve{\pi}),D\Phi[g_{m,a},\pi_{m,a}]^{*}(\mathbb{W})\right\rangle=-\left\langle D\Phi[g_{m,a},\pi_{m,a}](\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi}),\mathbb{W}\right\rangle+\text{non-linear terms}

for all
\mathbb{W}
in the cokernel of
(P,B_{\nu}+F)
. The
linearization of the system in (
1.12
) is solvable if

D\Phi[g_{m,a},\pi_{m,a}]^{*}
acts injectively on the cokernel of

(P,B_{\nu}+F)
restricted to the support of

(\breve{g},\breve{\pi})
. Now, since the Kerr initial data

(g_{m,a},\pi_{m,a})
can be proved to be analytic near and at the

can also be proved
to be analytic near and at the boundary (see Lemma

for the precise statement). Therefore, they cannot
satisfy the boundary condition (
1.10
) since
we assumed that
F
vanishes in an open set of
\partial\Sigma
and is the identity on another one. This shows how
D\Phi[g_{m,a},\pi_{m,a}]^{*}
acts
injectively on the cokernel of
(P,B_{\nu}+F)
(see Lemma
5.6
for the details).

The above procedure illustrates how a careful choice of
F
allows us to simultaneously
describe the mapping properties of
P
and also to ensure that we can
appropriately solve for the correctors
(\breve{g},\breve{\pi})
by
eliminating the KIDS, i.e. the elements of
\mathrm{ker}(D\Phi[g_{m,a},\pi_{m,a}]^{*})

(
1.12

resolution of the non-linear system (
1.11
).

Remark 1.7
.

In fact, the only properties of the Kerr initial data set we use are its analyticity near and at the boundary, and the fact that it solves (
1.2

1.2

We recall the expression of the ingoing principal null frame

8

\displaystyle u^{5}{\mathcal{H}}\left(u^{4}\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p}),u^{2}\left(\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})+L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-8\Delta_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}u+u{\mathcal{H}}\left(\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p}),\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})+L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X\right)

and

\displaystyle u^{2}{\mathcal{M}}\left(u^{4}\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p}),u^{2}\left(\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})+L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X\right)\right)_{i}

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathrm{div}_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}\left(\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})+L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X\right)_{i}+2u^{-1}\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})^{j\ell}\left(2\left(\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})+L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X\right)_{i\ell}\partial_{j}u-\left(\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})+L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X\right)_{j\ell}\partial_{i}u\right).

On the one hand, thanks to (
2.2
) and (
2.4
), we can compute

D{\mathcal{H}}[\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p}),\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})](0,L_{\hat{g}}X)
and rewrite the
equation

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}\left(u^{4}\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p}),u^{2}\left(\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})+L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X\right)\right)=0

as

\displaystyle-8\Delta_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}u-2\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})^{ij}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})_{ij}

\displaystyle+\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{div}_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X

\displaystyle=-{\mathcal{H}}(\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p}),\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))

\displaystyle\quad-(u-1){\mathcal{H}}(\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p}),\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))-uQ_{\mathcal{H}}[\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p}),\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})](0,L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X)

\displaystyle\quad-(u-1)\left(-2\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})^{ij}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})_{ij}+\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{div}_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X\right).

Using now the definition (
5.1
), (
2.2
) and
{\mathcal{H}}(\bar{g},\bar{\pi})=0
, we rewrite this equation as

\displaystyle-8\Delta_{\bar{g}}u-2\bar{\pi}^{ij}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\bar{g}_{ij}+\mathrm{tr}_{\bar{g}}\bar{\pi}\mathrm{div}_{\bar{g}}X

\displaystyle=-D{\mathcal{H}}[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}](\widecheck{g}+\breve{g},\widecheck{\pi}+\breve{\pi})

\displaystyle\quad-Q_{\mathcal{H}}[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}](\widecheck{g}+\breve{g},\widecheck{\pi}+\breve{\pi})

\displaystyle\quad-(u-1)\left(D{\mathcal{H}}[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}](\widecheck{g}+\breve{g},\widecheck{\pi}+\breve{\pi})+Q_{\mathcal{H}}[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}](\widecheck{g}+\breve{g},\widecheck{\pi}+\breve{\pi})\right)

\displaystyle\quad-uQ_{\mathcal{H}}[\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p}),\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})](0,L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X)

\displaystyle\quad-(u-1)\left(-2\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})^{ij}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})_{ij}+\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{div}_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X\right)

\displaystyle\quad+8\Delta_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}u+2\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})^{ij}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})_{ij}-\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{div}_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X

\displaystyle\quad-8\Delta_{\bar{g}}u-2\bar{\pi}^{ij}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\bar{g}_{ij}+\mathrm{tr}_{\bar{g}}\bar{\pi}\mathrm{div}_{\bar{g}}X.

On the other hand, thanks to (
2.3
) and (
2.5
), as well as (
5.1
) and
\mathrm{div}_{\bar{g}}\bar{\pi}=0
, we rewrite the equation

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}\left(u^{4}\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p}),u^{2}\left(\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})+L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X\right)\right)_{i}=0

as

\displaystyle-2\mathrm{div}_{\bar{g}}L_{\bar{g}}X_{i}-8\bar{g}^{j\ell}\bar{\pi}_{i\ell}\partial_{j}u+4\mathrm{tr}_{\bar{g}}\bar{\pi}\partial_{i}u

\displaystyle=2D{\mathcal{M}}[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}](\widecheck{g}+\breve{g},\widecheck{\pi}+\breve{\pi})_{i}+2Q_{\mathcal{M}}[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}](\widecheck{g}+\breve{g},\widecheck{\pi}+\breve{\pi})_{i}

\displaystyle\quad+8u^{-1}\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})^{j\ell}L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X_{i\ell}\partial_{j}u-4u^{-1}\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X\partial_{i}u

\displaystyle\quad+8(u^{-1}-1)\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})^{j\ell}\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})_{i\ell}\partial_{j}u-4(u^{-1}-1)\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\partial_{i}u

\displaystyle\quad+2\mathrm{div}_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}X_{i}+8\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})^{j\ell}\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})_{i\ell}\partial_{j}u-4\mathrm{tr}_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\partial_{i}u

\displaystyle\quad-2\mathrm{div}_{\bar{g}}L_{\bar{g}}X_{i}-8\bar{g}^{j\ell}\bar{\pi}_{i\ell}\partial_{j}u+4\mathrm{tr}_{\bar{g}}\bar{\pi}\partial_{i}u.

Applying the above formula to
u=1+\widecheck{u}
and

concludes
the proof of the lemma.
∎

5.2.
Estimating the remainder

We start by estimating the remainder term in (
5.3
), i.e.

{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)
. For the sake of clarity, we introduce
the schematic notations

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}=\{\nabla\bar{g},\bar{\pi}\},\quad\widecheck{\Gamma}=\{\nabla\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi}\},\quad\breve{\Gamma}=\{\nabla\breve{g},\breve{\pi}\},\quad\hat{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{p})=\{\nabla\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p}),\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\}.

Since
(\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi})
,
\mathfrak{p}
and
\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}
will be small in appropriate spaces, we only write down quadratic terms
when manipulating
{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)
since cubic and higher order
terms are smaller and enjoy more decay and thus are easier
to treat. Note also that, from now on,
\widecheck{g}
and
\widecheck{\pi}
are fixed and are assumed to satisfy (
3.1
) for some
\varepsilon>0
that will be fixed in the proofs of Lemmas
5.9
and
5.12
.

Thanks to its expression given in Lemma
5.1
and the various schematic expressions of Lemma
2.5
we find

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{R}}_{1}\left(\mathfrak{p}\right)+{\mathcal{R}}_{2}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)+{\mathcal{R}}_{3}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)+\text{higher-order terms}

where we have schematically

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\left(\mathfrak{p}\right)

\displaystyle=(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g})\nabla\left(\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right)+\bar{\Gamma}(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g})\left(\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right)+\left(\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right)^{2}+\bar{\Gamma}^{2}(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g})^{2},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{2}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})\left(\nabla\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)^{2}+\hat{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{p})\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\left(\nabla\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}+\hat{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{p})\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{3}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)

\displaystyle=(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g})\nabla^{2}\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}+\left((\widecheck{g}+\breve{g})\bar{\Gamma}+\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right)\nabla\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\nabla\left(\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right)+(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g})\nabla\bar{\Gamma}+\bar{\Gamma}\left(\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right)+\bar{\Gamma}^{2}(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g})\right)\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}.

Note that we have omitted undifferentiated components of
\bar{g}
since they are
O(1)
. We now explain the origin of the three remainders above:

•

{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\left(\mathfrak{p}\right)
collects quadratic terms in
(\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi})+\mathfrak{p}
coming from the linearization of
\Phi\left(\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p}),\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\right)
around
(\bar{g},\bar{\pi})
, i.e.
Q_{\mathcal{H}}[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}](\widecheck{g}+\breve{g},\widecheck{\pi}+\breve{\pi})
and
Q_{\mathcal{M}}[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}](\widecheck{g}+\breve{g},\widecheck{\pi}+\breve{\pi})
for which we use (
2.6
)-(
2.7
).

•

{\mathcal{R}}_{2}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)
collects quadratic terms in
\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}
produced after having extracted from
\Phi(g,\pi)=0
the linear system for
\mathbb{U}
(recall the definition (
5.2
)), for instance
4(1+\widecheck{u})^{-1}\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})^{j\ell}\left(2L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}\widecheck{X}_{i\ell}\partial_{j}\widecheck{u}-L_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}\widecheck{X}_{j\ell}\partial_{i}\widecheck{u}\right)
which is of the form
\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})\left(\nabla\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)^{2}
.

•

{\mathcal{R}}_{3}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)
collects quadratic cross terms in
\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}
and
(\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi})+\mathfrak{p}
obtained when relating the linear operators defined with
(\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p}),\hat{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}))
applied to
\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}
with the ones defined with
(\bar{g},\bar{\pi})
, for instance
8\left(\Delta_{\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})}-\Delta_{\bar{g}}\right)\widecheck{u}
which is of the form
(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g})\nabla^{2}\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}+(\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma})\nabla\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}
.

In the lemmas below and in the rest of the article, all the implicit constants hidden with the symbol
\lesssim
depend on the size of the support of
\breve{g}
and
\breve{\pi}
, which is not an issue since this support will be fixed in Definition
5.5
below.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
. Assume

(5.4)

\displaystyle\left\|\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right\|_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\varepsilon,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\left\|\breve{g}\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Sigma)}+\left\|\breve{\pi}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq D_{0}\varepsilon,

for some
C_{0},D_{0}>0
. We have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(C_{0},D_{0})\varepsilon^{2},

(5.7)

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right),\mathbb{W}_{j,\ell,{\alpha}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim C(C_{0},D_{0})\varepsilon^{2}.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathfrak{p}=(\breve{g},\breve{\pi})
is compactly supported and
that its support will be fixed in the whole proof so that when
estimating terms containing either
\breve{g}
or
\breve{\pi}
we can
drop the weights at infinity. Moreover, it suffices to estimate

{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\left(\mathfrak{p}\right)
,
{\mathcal{R}}_{2}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)
and

{\mathcal{R}}_{3}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)
since the higher order terms in

{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)
enjoy better decay and are smaller. We
start with
{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\left(\mathfrak{p}\right)
:

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\left(\mathfrak{p}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g})\nabla\left(\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}+\left\|(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g})\left(\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\left(\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right)^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}+\left\|(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g})^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta+2}(\Sigma)},

where we used asymptotic flatness of
(\bar{g},\bar{\pi})
from
Definition
2.1
, which implies

|\bar{\Gamma}|\lesssim(1+r)^{-2}
. Using now the continuous bilinear
embeddings from Lemma
2.4
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\left(\mathfrak{p}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\widecheck{g}+\breve{g}\right\|_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}\left(\left\|\nabla\left(\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}+\left\|\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right\|_{H^{1}_{-q-\delta-1}(\Sigma)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right\|_{H^{1}_{-q-\delta-1}(\Sigma)}^{2}+\left\|\widecheck{g}+\breve{g}\right\|_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}^{2}.

Using now (
3.1
) and (
5.5
)
this gives

(5.8)

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\left(\mathfrak{p}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(D_{0})\varepsilon^{2}.

We now estimate
{\mathcal{R}}_{2}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)
:

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{R}}_{2}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+C(D_{0})\varepsilon)\left(\left\|\left(\nabla\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}+\left\|\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\nabla\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}+\left\|\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta+2}(\Sigma)}\right),

where we used (
5.1
), asymptotic flatness,
(
3.1
) and (
5.5
) which
imply
\hat{g}(\mathfrak{p})=1+O\left(r^{-1}\right)+O\left(\varepsilon C(D_{0})r^{-q-\delta}\right)
and

\hat{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{p})=O\left(r^{-2}\right)+\varepsilon C(D_{0})\tilde{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{p})
where
\tilde{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{p})\in H^{1}_{-q-\delta-1}(\Sigma)
(for this last term we thus need to use a multiplication property from Lemma
2.4
). The
assumption (
5.4
) and the continuous bilinear
embeddings from Lemma
2.4
then imply

(5.9)

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{R}}_{2}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(C_{0},D_{0})\varepsilon^{2}.

We finally estimate
{\mathcal{R}}_{3}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)
:

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{R}}_{3}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g})\nabla^{2}\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}+\left\|\left(\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right)\nabla\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}+\left\|\left(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g}\right)\nabla\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\nabla\left(\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right)\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}+\left\|(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g})\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta+1}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\left(\widecheck{\Gamma}+\breve{\Gamma}\right)\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}+\left\|(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g})\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta+2}(\Sigma)},

where we used asymptotic flatness to write
\bar{\Gamma}=O\left(r^{-2}\right)
and

\nabla\bar{\Gamma}=O\left(r^{-3}\right)
. As above the continuous bilinear
embeddings from Lemma
2.4
together with (

), (

) and (

) imply

(5.10)

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{R}}_{3}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(C_{0},D_{0})\varepsilon^{2}.

5.8
), (
5.9
) and (

)

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\widecheck{\mathbb{U}},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}
satisfying (

(5.13)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{2}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)-{\mathcal{R}}_{2}\left(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\left(\breve{\Gamma}-\breve{\Gamma}^{\prime}\right)\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}+\hat{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}-\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right)\right)\left(\hat{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{p})\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}+\hat{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right).

(5.15)

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{R}}_{2}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)-{\mathcal{R}}_{2}\left(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim{}

Finally, we have

(5.16)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{3}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)-{\mathcal{R}}_{3}\left(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right)={}

\displaystyle(\breve{g}-\breve{g}^{\prime})\nabla^{2}\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}+(\widecheck{g}+\breve{g}^{\prime})\nabla^{2}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}-\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right)

Remark 5.7

\displaystyle=-\left\langle D\Phi[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}](\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi}),\mathbb{W}_{j,\ell,{\alpha}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}-\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right),\mathbb{W}_{j,\ell,{\alpha}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}.

solves

(5.45)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Psi^{(c)}(\mathfrak{p}),D\Phi[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}]^{*}(\mathbb{W}_{j,\ell,{\alpha}})\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle D\Phi[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}](\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi}),\mathbb{W}_{j,\ell,{\alpha}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}-\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right),\mathbb{W}_{j,\ell,{\alpha}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}

for all
1\leq j\leq q
,
-(j-1)\leq\ell\leq j-1
and
{\alpha}=0,1,2,3
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Assume that
\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}
satisfies (
5.42
). If
D_{0}
is
large enough and if
\varepsilon
is small enough compared to
D_{0}^{-1}
and

C_{0}^{-1}
, then the corrector map
\Psi^{(c)}
is a well-defined
contraction.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in B_{\mathcal{Z}_{q}}(0,D_{0}\varepsilon)
. Thanks to Lemma
5.6
there exists a unique
\Psi^{(c)}(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{Z}_{q}

satisfying (
5.45
) for all
1\leq j\leq q
,

-(j-1)\leq\ell\leq j-1
and
{\alpha}=0,1,2,3
satisfying moreover

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi^{(c)}(\mathfrak{p})\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Sigma)\times W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\sup_{j,\ell,{\alpha}}\left|\left\langle D\Phi[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}](\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi}),\mathbb{W}_{j,\ell,{\alpha}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}+\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right),\mathbb{W}_{j,\ell,{\alpha}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\right|.

We estimate both terms on the right-hand side. First since
j\leq q
and
\delta>0
we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle D\Phi[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}](\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi}),\mathbb{W}_{j,\ell,{\alpha}}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|D\Phi[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}](\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi})\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\widecheck{g}\right\|_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}+\left\|\widecheck{\pi}\right\|_{H^{1}_{-q-\delta-1}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon

where we have used (
2.4
), (
2.5
), asymptotic flatness and
(
3.1
). For the second term in the estimate for

\Psi^{(c)}(\mathfrak{p})
we use (
5.7
). We obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi^{(c)}(\mathfrak{p})\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Sigma)\times W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\varepsilon\left(1+C(C_{0},D_{0})\varepsilon\right).

Taking
D_{0}
large enough and
\varepsilon
small enough compared to

D_{0}^{-1}
and
C_{0}^{-1}
shows that

\Psi^{(c)}(\mathfrak{p})\in B_{\mathcal{Z}_{q}}(0,D_{0}\varepsilon)
and thus that the
corrector map
\Psi^{(c)}
is well-defined.

Next let

\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\in B_{\mathcal{Z}_{q}}(0,D_{0}\varepsilon)
. Taking the difference of the
relations defining
\Psi^{(c)}(\mathfrak{p})
and
\Psi^{(c)}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})
and using
Lemma
5.6
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi^{(c)}(\mathfrak{p})-\Psi^{(c)}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Sigma)\times W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{j,\ell,{\alpha}}\left|\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)-{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right),\mathbb{W}_{j,\ell,{\alpha}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim C(C_{0},D_{0})\varepsilon\left\|\mathfrak{p}-\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Sigma)\times W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma)},

where we have used (
5.12
). Taking
\varepsilon
small enough compared to
D_{0}^{-1}
and
C_{0}^{-1}
shows that
\Psi^{(c)}
is a contraction.
∎

From now on,
D_{0}>0
is fixed according to Lemma
5.9
.

Corollary 5.10
.

Assume that
\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}
satisfies (
5.42
). If
\varepsilon
is
small enough compared to
C_{0}^{-1}
, there exists a unique

\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\in\mathcal{Z}_{q}
such that
(
5.43
) holds. Moreover we have

(5.46)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Sigma)\times W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\varepsilon

and

(5.47)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)-\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right)\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Sigma)\times W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma)}\lesssim C(C_{0})\varepsilon\left\|\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}-\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right\|_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)},

if
\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}
and
\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}
both satisfy (
5.42
).

Proof.

The proof of the first part of the corollary follows from Lemma

5.9
and the Banach fixed point theorem. For the
proof of (
5.47
) we take the difference of the
relations defining
\mathfrak{p}\vcentcolon=\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)
and

\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right)
together with Lemma

5.6
and (
5.12
) to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{p}-\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Sigma)\times W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{j,\ell,{\alpha}}\left|\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)-{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right),\mathbb{W}_{j,\ell,{\alpha}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim C(C_{0})\varepsilon\bigg(\left\|\mathfrak{p}-\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Sigma)\times W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma)}+\left\|\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}-\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right\|_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}\bigg).

By taking
\varepsilon
small enough compared to
C_{0}^{-1}
we can absorb

C(C_{0})\varepsilon\left\|\mathfrak{p}-\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Sigma)\times W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma)}
by the LHS and obtain (
5.47
).
∎

5.4.
Solving the elliptic system

In this section we conclude the proof of Theorem
3.1
by
solving the elliptic system for
\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}
with the corrector

\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)
constructed in Section
5.3
. We want to solve

(5.48)

\left\{\begin{aligned} P\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)&=D\Phi[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}]\left((\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi})+\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\right)+{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right),\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right),\quad\text{on $\Sigma$,}\\
(B_{\nu}+F_{\delta})\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)&=0,\quad\text{on $\partial\Sigma$.}\end{aligned}\right.

Definition 5.11
.

Let
C_{0}>0
. The
solution map

\Psi^{(s)}
is defined by

\displaystyle\Psi^{(s)}:B_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}(0,C_{0}\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow B_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}(0,C_{0}\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\Psi^{(s)}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)

such that
\Psi^{(s)}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)
solves

(5.49)

\left\{\begin{aligned} P\left(\Psi^{(s)}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\right)&=D\Phi[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}]\left((\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi})+\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\right)+{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right),\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right),\quad\text{on $\Sigma$,}\\
(B_{\nu}+F_{\delta})\left(\Psi^{(s)}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\right)&=0,\quad\text{on $\partial\Sigma$.}\end{aligned}\right.

Lemma 5.12
.

If
C_{0}
is large enough and
\varepsilon
is small enough compared to

C_{0}^{-1}
, the solution map
\Psi^{(s)}
is a well-defined
contraction.

Proof.

Let
\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\in B_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}(0,C_{0}\varepsilon)
. First note
that if
\varepsilon
is small enough compared to
C_{0}^{-1}
then Corollary

5.10
applies and the corrector

\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)
exists and satisfy the bounds of Corollary

5.10
. By construction, this corrector is such that

\displaystyle\left\langle D\Phi[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}]\left((\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi})+\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\right)+{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right),\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right),\mathbb{W}_{j,\ell,{\alpha}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle=0,

for all
1\leq j\leq q
,
-(j-1)\leq\ell\leq j-1
and
{\alpha}=0,1,2,3
.
Moreover, using (
2.4
), (
2.5
), asymptotic flatness, (
3.1
), (
5.6
) and (
5.46
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|D\Phi[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}]\left((\widecheck{g},\widecheck{\pi})+\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\right)+{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right),\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon(1+C(C_{0})\varepsilon).

Therefore, the third point in Proposition
4.15
implies
that there exists a unique
\Psi^{(s)}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)

solving (
5.49
) with the bound

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi^{(s)}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\right\|_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\varepsilon(1+C(C_{0})\varepsilon).

Choosing first
C_{0}
large enough and then
\varepsilon
small enough compared to

C_{0}^{-1}
implies that

\Psi^{(s)}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)\in B_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}(0,C_{0}\varepsilon)

and thus that the solution map
\Psi^{(s)}
is well-defined.

Next
let

\widecheck{\mathbb{U}},\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\in B_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}(0,C_{0}\varepsilon)
. We subtract the two systems
defining
\Psi^{(s)}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)
and

\Psi^{(s)}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right)
together with Proposition

4.15
and (
5.11
) to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi^{(s)}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)-\Psi^{(s)}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right)\right\|_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|D\Phi[\bar{g},\bar{\pi}]\left(\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)-\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right),\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)-{\mathcal{R}}\left(\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right),\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{-q-\delta-2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+C(C_{0})\varepsilon)\left\|\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)-\mathfrak{p}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right)\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Sigma)\times W^{1,\infty}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\quad+C(C_{0})\varepsilon\left\|\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}-\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right\|_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}.

We now use (
5.47
) to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi^{(s)}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right)-\Psi^{(s)}\left(\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right)\right\|_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(C_{0})\varepsilon\left\|\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}-\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}^{\prime}\right\|_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}.

Taking
\varepsilon
small enough compared to
C_{0}^{-1}
shows that
\Psi^{(s)}
is a contraction.
∎

In view of Lemma
5.12
, the Banach fixed point theorem implies that if
\varepsilon
is small
enough then there exists a unique solution
\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}
to the system
(
5.3
) with the bound

\displaystyle\left\|\widecheck{\mathbb{U}}\right\|_{H^{2}_{-q-\delta}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\varepsilon.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

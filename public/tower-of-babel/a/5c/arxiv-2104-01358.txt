Abstract
We study the semantics of an untyped lambda-calculus equipped with operators representing read and write operations from and to a global store. We adopt the monadic approach to model side-effects and treat read and write as algebraic operations over a monad. We introduce operational and denotational semantics and a type assignment system of intersection types and prove that types are invariant under the reduction and expansion of term and state configurations. Finally, we characterize convergent terms via their typings, establishing the adequacy of the denotational semantics w.r.t. the operational semantics.

Introduction

The problem of integrating non-functional aspects into functional programming languages goes back to the early days of functional programming;
nowadays, even procedural and object-oriented languages embody more and more features from declarative languages, renewing interest and
motivations in the investigation of higher-order effectful computations.

Focusing on side-effects, the methods used in the early studies to treat the store,
e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
and
[
4
]
, are essentially additive:
update and dereferentiation primitives are added to a (often typed)
\lambda
-calculus, possibly with constructs to dynamically create and initialize
new locations.
Then, a posteriori, tools to reason about such calculi are built either by extending the type discipline or by means of denotational and operational semantics, or both. Although our main concern here is the study of an imperative
\lambda
-calculus, we are not following the same path of previous treatments
of this topic in the literature, rather we insist on the construction of the type assignment system from first principles and denotational semantics of effectuful calculi in general, as in the companion paper
[
5
]
; differently than in our previous work we now consider the operational semantics of the imperative calculus.

A turning point in the study of computational effects has been Moggi’s idea to model effectful computations as morphisms of the Kleisli category of a monad in
[
6
]
, starting a very rich thread in investigating programming language foundations based on categorical semantics which is still flourishing.
The methodological advantage is that we have a uniform and abstract
way of speaking of various kinds of effects, and the definition of equational logics to reason about them. At the same time computational effects
including side effects can be safely embodied into purely functional languages without disrupting their declarative nature, as shown by
Wadler’s
[
7
,
8
]
together with a long series of papers
by the same author and others.

Initiating with
[
9
]
, we have approached the issue of modelling effects in a computational

\lambda
-calculus using domain logic and intersection types. Since
[
10
]
, intersection types have been understood
as a type theoretic tool to construct
\lambda
-models, which has systematically related to domain theory in
[
11
]
.
As such, intersection types can serve as an intermediate layer between the abstract denotational semantics and
the concrete syntax of the calculus, which we want to extend to the case of Moggi’s computational calculi.

The idea is to model untyped computational
\lambda
-calculi into domains satisfying the equation
D=D\xrightarrow{}TD
, clearly
reminiscent of Scott’s
D=D\xrightarrow{}D
reflexive object, where
T
is a monad (see
[
12
]
, §5 and Section
5
below). Since intersection
types denote compact points in such a domain, we can recover from the domain theoretic definition of the monad
all the information needed to build a sound and complete type assignment system.

The study in
[
9
]
was confined to a generic monad represented by an uninterpreted symbol
T
in the type syntax,
without considering algebraic operators. In the
present paper, we address the issue of modelling an imperative
\lambda
-calculus equipped with a global store, and
read and write operations. In this case, the monad

is instantiated to a variant of the state monad in
[
6

[

operations

and
\textit{set}_{\ell}

proposed in
[
14

, chap. 3. We call
\lambda_{imp}
the resulting calculus.

By solving the domain equation
D=D\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{S}D
we get a model of
\lambda_{imp}
, whose theory includes
the convertibility relation induced by the reduction and the monadic laws. Such a model, when
constructed in the category of
\omega
-algebraic lattices provides the basis to define an intersection
type system, which is an instance of the generic one in
[
9

into the details of the process for obtaining such a system from the semantics, which has been treated in

[
5

An untyped imperative
\lambda
-calculus

Imperative extensions of the
\lambda

based on the call-by-value
\lambda
-calculus, enriched with constructs for reading and writing to
the store. Aiming at exploring the semantics of side effects in computational calculi, where
“impure” functions are modelled by pure ones sending values to computations in the sense of

[

]

\lambda_{\scalebox{.4}{\copyright}}

from

]

we add syntax denoting algebraic effect operations à la Plotkin and Power

Let
\textbf{L}=\{\ell_{0},\ell_{1},\ldots\}
be a denumerable set of abstract

[
14
]

\textit{get}_{\ell}
and
\textit{set}_{\ell}
, obtaining:

Definition 2.1

(Term syntax)

\begin{array}[]{rrcll}\textit{Val}~\ni&V,W&::=&x\mid\lambda x.M\\
\textit{Com}~\ni&M,N&::=&[V]\mid M\star V\\
&&\mid&\textit{get}_{\ell}(\lambda x.M)\mid\textit{set}_{\ell}(V,M)&(\ell\in\textbf{L})\\
\end{array}

As for
\lambda_{\scalebox{.4}{\copyright}}
, terms are of either sorts
Val
and
Com
, representing values and computations, respectively.
The new constructs are
\textit{get}_{\ell}(\lambda x.M)
and
\textit{set}_{\ell}(V,M)
.
The variable

is bound in
\lambda x.M
and
\textit{get}_{\ell}(\lambda x.M)
;
terms are identified up to renaming of bound variables so that the capture avoiding substitution
M[V/x]
is always well defined;

FV(M)
denotes the set of free variables in
M

We call
\textit{Val}^{\,0}
the subset of closed
V\in\textit{Val}
; similarly for
\textit{Com}^{0}
.

With respect to the syntax of the “imperative
\lambda
-calculus” in
[
14
]
,
we do not have the
let
construct, nor the application
VW
among values. The justification is the same as for the computational core, indeed.
These constructs are definable:

\mathsf{let}\,x\!\coloneqq\!M\,\mathsf{in}\,N\;\equiv\;M\star(\lambda x.N)\qquad VW\;\equiv\;[W]\star V

where
\equiv
is syntactic identity.
In general, application among computations can be encoded by
MN\equiv M\star(\lambda z.N\star z)
, where
z
is fresh.

In a sugared notation from functional programming languages, we could have written:

\mathsf{let}\,x\!\coloneqq\!\;!\ell\,\mathsf{in}\,M~\equiv~\textit{get}_{\ell}(\lambda x.M)\qquad\ell:=V;M~\equiv~\textit{set}_{\ell}(V,M)

representing location dereferentiation and assignment. Observe that we do not consider locations as
values; consequently they cannot be dynamically created like with the
ref
operator from ML, nor
is it possible to model aliasing. On the other hand, since the calculus is untyped, “strong updates” are allowed. In fact, when evaluating
\textit{set}_{\ell}(V,M)
,
the value
V
to which the location
\ell
is updated bears no relation
to the previous value, say
W
, of
\ell
in the store: indeed, in our type assignment system
W
and
V
may well have completely different types. This will be, of course, a major challenge when designing the type assignment
system in the next sections.

Operational semantics

We define the operational semantics of our calculus
via a reduction relation
(M,s)\rightarrow(N,t)
, where
M,N\in\textit{Com}
and
s,t

are
store

Definition 3.1

(Store and Lookup terms)

Let
V
vary over
Val
; then define:

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\textit{Store}\ni s&::=&\textit{emp}\mid\textit{upd}_{\ell}(u,s)\\[2.84526pt]
\textit{Lkp}\ni u&::=&V\mid\textit{lkp}_{\ell}(s)&\ell\in\textit{dom}(s)\\
\\
\textit{dom}(\textit{emp})&=&\emptyset\\[2.84526pt]
\textit{dom}(\textit{upd}_{\ell}(u,s))&=&\{\ell\}\cup\textit{dom}(s)\end{array}

Store terms represent finite mappings from
L
to
Val
.

emp
is the
empty store
, that is the everywhere undefined map;

\textit{upd}_{\ell}(V,s)
is the
update
of
s
, representing the same map as
s
,
but for
\ell
where it holds
V
.

To compute the value of the store
s
at location
\ell
we add the expressions

\textit{lkp}_{\ell}(s)\in\textit{Lkp}
, whose intended meaning is the
lookup
(partial) function searching
the value of
\ell
in the store
s
; more precisely
\textit{lkp}_{\ell}(s)
picks the value
V
from the leftmost
outermost occurrence in
s
of a subterm of the shape
\textit{upd}_{\ell}(V,s^{\prime})
, if any.

To avoid dealing with undefined expressions like
\textit{lkp}_{\ell}(\textit{emp})
,
we have asked that
\ell\in\textit{dom}(s)
for the lookup expression
\textit{lkp}_{\ell}(s)
to be well-formed. Then
the meaning of store and lookup terms can be defined axiomatically as follows.

Definition 3.2

(Store and Lookup Axioms)

The axioms of the algebra of store terms and well-formed lookup expressions are the following:

1.

\textit{lkp}_{\ell}(\textit{upd}_{\ell}(u,s))=u

2.

\textit{lkp}_{\ell}(\textit{upd}_{\ell^{\prime}}(u,s))=\textit{lkp}_{\ell}(s)
if
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}

3.

\textit{upd}_{\ell}(\textit{lkp}_{\ell}(s),s)=s

4.

\textit{upd}_{\ell}(U,\textit{upd}_{\ell}(W,s))=\textit{upd}_{\ell}(U,s)

5.

\textit{upd}_{\ell}(U,\textit{upd}_{\ell^{\prime}}(W,s))=\textit{upd}_{\ell^{\prime}}(W,\textit{upd}_{\ell}(U,s))
if
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}

We write
\vdash s=t
to stress that the equality
s=t
has been derived from the above axioms using reflexivity,
symmetry, transitivity, and congruence of equational logic.

The equalities in Definition
3.2
are folklore for terms representing the store in the literature:
see e.g.
[
24
]

the same as those ones for global state in
[

since we have not found any good reference to establish the properties we shall use in the subsequent sections,
we devote the next paragraphs to the study of this theory.
The main results are Theorem

The reduction relation
.
A
configuration
is a pair
(M,s)
, with
M\in\textit{Com}
and
s\in\textit{Store}
;
then the
one-step reduction
is the binary relation over configurations inductively defined by the rules in Figure
1
.

The reduction relation is deterministic, reflecting the strictly sequential nature of evaluation for programming languages with
side-effects. A configuration of the shape
([V],t)
is irreducible, and it is the result of
the evaluation of
(M,s)
whenever
(M,s)\rightarrow^{*}([V],t)
, where
\rightarrow^{*}
is the reflexive and
transitive closure of
\rightarrow
. Infinite reductions exist; consider the term:

\Omega_{c}\equiv[(\lambda x.[x]\star x)]\star(\lambda x.[x]\star x)

which is such that
(\Omega_{c},s)\rightarrow(\Omega_{c},s)
, for any
s

Definition 4.8

(Convergence)

For

and

1.

(M,s)\Downarrow~\iff~\exists V,t.~(M,s)\Downarrow(V,t)

M\Downarrow~\iff~\forall s\in\textit{Store}^{0}.\,(M,s)\Downarrow

25

5
Denotational semantics

In this section, we construct a model
D_{\infty}^{\mathbb{S}}
to interpret terms of the
\lambda_{imp}
calculus. We first define precisely the
partiality and state monad

\mathbb{S}

that is essential to model stores; then we recall Plotkin and Power’s
generalised algebraic operators
over a monad, by adapting their definition
the to the present setting. By solving the equation
D=D\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{S}\,D
in a suitable category of domains

\omega
-algebraic lattices (see the references below), we get a domain
D_{\infty}^{\mathbb{S}}

such that we can interpret values and computations in
D_{\infty}^{\mathbb{S}}
and

\mathbb{S}\,D_{\infty}^{\mathbb{S}}

7
to show the soundness of the type system. A more detailed treatment of the denotational semantics and of the filter-model construction for
\lambda_{imp}
can be found in
[
5
]
.

Recall that a concrete category
{\cal C}
is essentially a set-like category; more precisely it is equipped with a faithful functor to the category of sets. If it is a cartesian closed category, shortly a ccc (see e.g.
[
18
, chap. 3]

and
[
16
, sec. 4]
), then products can be seen as sets of pairs and exponents as sets of functions.

Definition 5.1

(Computational Monad

§3)

Let

be a concrete cartesian closed category. A functional computational monad, henceforth just a monad
over
{\cal C}
is a triple
(T,\mbox{\it unit}_{\,},\star)
where

is a map over the objects of
{\cal C}

\begin{array}[]{c@{\hspace{2cm}}c}\mbox{\it unit}_{A}:A\xrightarrow{}TA\hfil\hskip 56.9055pt&\star_{A,B}:TA\times(TB)^{A}\xrightarrow{}TB\end{array}

such that, writing
\star_{A,B}
as an infix operator and omitting subscripts:

\begin{array}[]{l@{\hspace{0.5cm}}rcl@{\hspace{0.5cm}}l}\textit{Left unit}:\hfil\hskip 14.22636pt&\mbox{\it unit}_{\,}\,a\star f&=&f\,a\hfil\hskip 14.22636pt\\

\mathop{\hbox to0.0pt{$\lambda$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu\raisebox{1.18399pt}{$\lambda$}}d.(f\,d\star g)

6
Intersection Type Assignment System

Intersection types have been introduced in the ’80 as an extension of Curry’s simple type assignment system to the untyped
\lambda
-calculus, and
have been developed into a large family of systems, with applications to various
\lambda
-calculi, including call-by-value and linear ones.
Prominent results concern the characterization of normalizing and strongly normalizing as well as solvable terms, namely reducing to a head normal form.
It is then natural to think of intersection types as a tool to characterize convergent terms in
\lambda_{imp}
-calculus.

We extend the system in
[
9
]
to type stores and operations
\textit{get}_{\ell}
and
\textit{set}_{\ell}
,
by specializing the generic monad
T
to the state and partiality monad.

Types and subtyping
.
An intersection type theory
\textit{Th}_{A}
over a language of types
\mathcal{L}_{A}
is an axiomatic presentation of a preorder
\leq_{A}
over
\mathcal{L}_{A}

turning the latter set into an inf-semilattice with top.

Definition 6.1

An intersection type theory, shortly itt, is a pair
\textit{Th}_{A}=(\mathcal{L}_{A},\leq_{A})
where
\mathcal{L}_{A}
, the language of

\textit{Th}_{A}
, is a countable set of type expressions closed under
\wedge
, and
\omega_{A}\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
is a special constant;
\leq_{A}

is a pre-order over
\mathcal{L}_{A}
closed under the following rules:

\begin{array}[]{c@{\hspace{1.5cm}}c@{\hspace{1.5cm}}c@{\hspace{1.5cm}}c@{\hspace{1.5cm}}c}\alpha\leq_{A}\omega_{A}\hfil\hskip 42.67912pt&\alpha\wedge\beta\leq_{A}\alpha\hfil\hskip 42.67912pt&\alpha\wedge\beta\leq_{A}\beta\hfil\hskip 42.67912pt&{\vbox{\hbox spread0.0pt{\hskip 0.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\penalty\alpha\leq_{A}\alpha^{\prime}\quad\beta\leq_{A}\beta^{\prime}$}\hskip 0.0pt}\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\vrule height=2.75002pt,depth=-2.24998pt,width=73.85652pt\hbox{}}}\hbox{\kern 3.53477pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\alpha\wedge\beta\leq_{A}\alpha^{\prime}\wedge\beta^{\prime}$}}}}\hfil\hskip 42.67912pt\end{array}

From the operational semantics, and especially Proposition
4.6
, and from the denotational semantics in the previous section, it is clear that we have four kinds of entities to type:
the values
V
and
\textit{lkp}_{\ell}(s)
, the store terms
s
, the computations
M
, and the configurations
(M,s)
. Therefore, we define four sorts of types.

Definition 6.2

We define four sorts of types by mutual induction as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rlll@{\hspace{0.5cm}}lllll}\mathcal{L}_{D}~\ni&\delta&::=&\delta\xrightarrow{}\tau\mid\delta\wedge\delta^{\prime}\mid\omega_{D}\hfil\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{Value Types}\\[5.69054pt]
\mathcal{L}_{S}~\ni&\sigma&::=&\langle\ell:\delta\rangle\mid\sigma\wedge\sigma^{\prime}\mid\omega_{S}\hfil\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{Store Types}\\[5.69054pt]
\mathcal{L}_{C}~\ni&\kappa&::=&\delta\times\sigma\mid\kappa\wedge\kappa^{\prime}\mid\omega_{C}\hfil\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{Computation Types}\\[5.69054pt]
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{S}D}~\ni&\tau&::=&\sigma\xrightarrow{}\kappa\mid\tau\wedge\tau^{\prime}\mid\omega_{\mathbb{S}D}\hfil\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{Configuration Types}\end{array}

We assume that
\wedge
and
\times
take precedence over
\xrightarrow{}
and that
\xrightarrow{}
associates to the right so that

\delta\xrightarrow{}\tau\wedge\tau^{\prime}
reads as
\delta\xrightarrow{}(\tau\wedge\tau^{\prime})
and

\delta^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}\sigma^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}\delta^{\prime\prime}\times\sigma^{\prime\prime}
reads as
\delta^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}(\sigma^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}(\delta^{\prime\prime}\times\sigma^{\prime\prime}))
.

Since (closed) values are abstractions, types in
\mathcal{L}_{D}
are arrow types from value (as the calculus is call-by-value)
to computation types. To the arrows, we add atoms plus intersections and a universal type for values, as we do for all type sorts.

Store types
\mathcal{L}_{S}
associate value types to locations.
As observed after Proposition
4.6
, computations are functions from stores to results, hence they are typed by arrow types

\sigma\xrightarrow{}\kappa
where
\kappa
is the type of results. Results are pairs of values and store terms so that types in

\mathcal{L}_{C}
have the form
\delta\times\sigma
and the intersection thereof, plus a universal type
\omega_{C}
which is the type of all results and
the undefined one, that is the “result” of a diverging reduction.

Similarly to the system in
[
9
]
, we introduce a subtyping relation
\leq_{A}
for each sort
\mathcal{L}_{A}
, which is in the style
of the system in
[
10
]
. Subtyping is a form of implication which, if types are interpreted as the extension of predicates over terms, is subset inclusion.
Such a choice has advantages and disadvantages: with subtyping, we can assign to terms just arrow,
\langle\ell:\delta\rangle

(a sort of record type) or product types, disregarding the cases of intersection and of
the trivial types
\omega
in the type inference rules in Figure
3
,
that otherwise jeopardize their form. Moreover, intersection types with subtyping do correspond to basic open subsets
of domain theoretic models, providing a “logical semantics” to the calculus in the sense of
[
11
]
.
A drawback is that the type system is not syntax-directed, making proofs more elaborate. This is partly remedied by means of
the Generation Lemma
8.3
.

Definition 6.3

(Subtyping)

For each sort
A=D,S,C,\mathbb{S}D
, we define the itt
\textit{Th}_{A}=(\mathcal{L}_{A},\leq_{A})
as the least one such that:

1.

\omega_{D}\leq_{D}\omega_{D}\xrightarrow{}\omega_{\mathbb{S}D}

2.

(\delta\xrightarrow{}\tau)\wedge(\delta\xrightarrow{}\tau^{\prime})\leq_{D}\delta\xrightarrow{}(\tau\wedge\tau^{\prime})

3.

\langle\ell:\delta\rangle\wedge\langle\ell:\delta^{\prime}\rangle\leq_{S}\langle\ell:\delta\wedge\delta^{\prime}\rangle

4.

(\delta\times\sigma)\wedge(\delta^{\prime}\times\sigma^{\prime})\leq_{C}(\delta\wedge\delta^{\prime})\times(\sigma\wedge\sigma^{\prime})

5.

\omega_{\mathbb{S}D}\leq_{\mathbb{S}D}\omega_{S}\xrightarrow{}\omega_{C}

6.

(\sigma\xrightarrow{}\kappa)\wedge(\sigma\xrightarrow{}\kappa^{\prime})\leq_{\mathbb{S}D}\sigma\xrightarrow{}(\kappa\wedge\kappa^{\prime})

and that are closed under the rules:

\begin{array}[]{c@{\hspace{1cm}}c}{\vbox{\hbox spread0.0pt{\hskip 0.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\penalty\delta^{\prime}\leq_{D}\delta\quad\tau\leq_{\mathbb{S}D}\tau^{\prime}$}\hskip 0.0pt}\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\vrule height=2.75002pt,depth=-2.24998pt,width=70.50063pt\hbox{}}}\hbox{\kern 8.18594pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\delta\xrightarrow{}\tau\leq_{D}\delta^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}\tau^{\prime}$}}}}\hfil\hskip 28.45274pt&{\vbox{\hbox spread0.0pt{\hskip 0.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\penalty\delta\leq_{D}\delta^{\prime}\quad\sigma\leq_{S}\sigma^{\prime}$}\hskip 0.0pt}\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\vrule height=2.75002pt,depth=-2.24998pt,width=70.08614pt\hbox{}}}\hbox{\kern 2.32713pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\delta\times\sigma\leq_{C}\delta^{\prime}\times\sigma^{\prime}$}}}}\par\\[17.07164pt]
{\vbox{\hbox spread0.0pt{\hskip 0.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\penalty\sigma^{\prime}\leq_{S}\sigma\quad\kappa\leq_{C}\kappa^{\prime}$}\hskip 0.0pt}\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\vrule height=2.75002pt,depth=-2.24998pt,width=69.41492pt\hbox{}}}\hbox{\kern 2.77502pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\sigma\xrightarrow{}\kappa\leq_{\mathbb{S}D}\sigma^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}\kappa^{\prime}$}}}}\hfil\hskip 28.45274pt&{\vbox{\hbox spread0.0pt{\hskip 0.0pt\hbox{\kern 18.1585pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\penalty\delta\leq_{D}\delta^{\prime}$}}\hskip 0.0pt}\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\vrule height=2.75002pt,depth=-2.24998pt,width=65.82071pt\hbox{}}}\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\langle\ell:\delta\rangle\leq_{S}\langle\ell:\delta^{\prime}\rangle$}}}}\par\end{array}

We avoid subscripts in the
\omega
and the
\leq
whenever clear from the context.
It is easily seen that all axioms of the shape
\varphi\leq\varphi^{\prime}
imply their reverse
\varphi^{\prime}\leq\varphi
; when this is the case we write

\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}
and say that such types are
equivalent
.
We use the abbreviations
\bigwedge_{i\in I}\varphi_{i}\equiv\varphi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\varphi_{n}
if
I=\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
and
\bigwedge_{i\in\emptyset}\varphi_{i}\equiv\omega
.

We comment on some equalities and inequalities derivable from Definition
6.3
. Obviously, the intersection
\wedge
is
idempotent, commutative and associative, and
\varphi\wedge\omega=\varphi
.

The arrow types in
\textit{Th}_{D}
and
\textit{Th}_{\mathbb{S}D}
are treated in the same way as in
[
10
]
for ordinary intersection types.
In particular, we have that
\delta\xrightarrow{}\omega_{\mathbb{S}D}=\omega_{D}
for all
\delta\in\mathcal{L}_{D}
, which implies
\omega_{D}\xrightarrow{}\omega_{\mathbb{S}D}=\omega_{D}
;
a similar remark applies to
\textit{Th}_{\mathbb{S}D}
.

Types
\langle\ell:\delta\rangle\in\mathcal{L}_{S}
are covariant in
\delta
w.r.t.
\leq_{D}
, therefore by
6.3
.
3
,

\langle\ell:\delta\rangle\wedge\langle\ell:\delta^{\prime}\rangle\leq\langle\ell:\delta\wedge\delta^{\prime}\rangle\leq\langle\ell:\delta\rangle\wedge\langle\ell:\delta^{\prime}\rangle

hence they are equivalent.
Notice that
\omega_{S}\not\leq\langle\ell:\omega_{D}\rangle
hence the inequality
\langle\ell:\omega_{D}\rangle<\omega_{S}
, which holds in any itt, is strict. The reason
for distinguishing among these types will be apparent in the definition of the type system; see also the remark below.

Product types
\delta\times\sigma\in\mathcal{L}_{C}
are covariant in both
\delta
and
\sigma

6.3
.
4
implies

(\delta\times\sigma)\wedge(\delta^{\prime}\times\sigma^{\prime})=(\delta\wedge\delta^{\prime})\times(\sigma\wedge\sigma^{\prime})
.

Remark 6.4

In comparison to
[

]
, the key difference is that in the cited paper there was no distinction among

\omega_{S}
and
\langle\ell:\omega_{D}\rangle
since they were equated in the theory.
The present change essentially amounts to dropping such an equation. This reflects at the level of types
that the semantics of a store

must be different than the semantics
of some

for example when

The following definition will be useful in technical development, and more precisely in the side condition of rule
(\textit{set})
of the typing system.

Definition 6.8

(Type Assignment System)

\varphi

With respect to the pre-existing literature on imperative and effectful
\lambda
-calculi,
let us mention Pfenning’s work
[
29
]
, which is further discussed in

[
30
]
. In Pfenning’s and others’ paper, intersection types are added to the
Hindley-Milner type system for ML to enhance type expressivity and strengthen polymorphism. However, the resulting system is unsound, which forces the restriction
of intersection types to values and the loss of certain subtyping inequations which are crucial in any type system inspired to
[
10
]
, including the present one. The issue is due to reference types in ML, where the type of a location is its “intrinsic” type in the sense of Reynolds
[
31
]
.
In contrast, our store types are predicates over the stores, namely “extrinsic” types,
telling what are the meanings of the values associated to the locations in the store.
Indeed our system enjoys type invariance under reduction and expansion,
which does not hold for Pfenning’s system.

A further line of research which seems relevant to us concerns
the type and effect systems, which have
been introduced in
[
32
]
and pursued
in
[
33
]
.
In the insightful paper
[
34
]
a type and effect judgement
\Gamma\vdash e:A,\varepsilon
is translated into
and ordinary typing
\Gamma\vdash e:T^{\varepsilon}A
, where
T
is a monad.
This has fostered the application of type systems with monadic types to static analysis for code transformation in
[
35
,
36
]
,
but also raised the question of the semantics of the types
T^{\varepsilon}A
.

In the papers by Benton and others, the semantics of monadic types with effect decorations is given in terms of PERs that are preserved by
read and write operations. Such semantics validates equations that do hold under assumptions about the effects of the equated programs;
e.g. only pure terms, neither depending on the store nor causing any mutation, can be evaluated in arbitrary order,
or repeated occurrences of the same pure code can be replaced by a reference where the value of the code is stored after computing it just once.

Such properties are nicely reflected in our types: if
\lambda x.M
has type
\delta\xrightarrow{}\sigma\xrightarrow{}\delta^{\prime}\times\sigma
, and
\textit{dom}(\sigma)

includes all the
\ell
occurring in
M
, then we know that the function represented by
\lambda x.M
is pure, as
\sigma
is non-trivial and
\lambda x.M
leaves the store unchanged; similarly if

M:\sigma\xrightarrow{}\delta\times\sigma
and
N:\sigma\xrightarrow{}\delta^{\prime}\times\sigma
then for any
P:\sigma\xrightarrow{}\kappa
both

M;N;P
and
N;M;P
will have the same types, and hence the same behaviour. In general, this suggests how to encode regions with store types
in our system.

As we wrote in
[
5
]

also called quantitative types, where
\tau\neq\tau\wedge\tau

The type system we have treated here is instead a classical intersection type system namely idempotent;
in the time elapsed between
[
20

and the present writing, a quantitative type system inspired to the present one
has been introduced in
[
38

for a call-by-value
\lambda

Finally, in
[
39

the authors have explored how intersection type systems can be used in the wider case of algebraic operations of
various kind, and in connection with abstract relational reasoning, hence going beyond the particular case of side-effects.

11
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented a type assignment system to study the semantics of an imperative computational
\lambda
-calculus equipped
with global store and algebraic operators.
The system defines the semantics of the untyped calculus, and we obtained a type-theoretic characterization of convergence.

We see the present work as a case study, albeit relevant in itself, toward a type-theoretic analysis
of effectful
\lambda
-calculi based on Moggi’s idea of modelling effects by means of monads.

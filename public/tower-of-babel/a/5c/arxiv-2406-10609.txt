Karim Johannes Becher, Fatma Kader Bingöl

Abstract
. We provide upper bounds on the u u -invariant for skew-hermitian forms over a quaternion algebra with its canonical involution in terms of the u u -invariant of the base field F F of characteristic different from 2 2 when 𝖨 3 ​ F = 0 \mathsf{I}^{3}F=0 . 1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a field of characteristic different from
2
. We denote by
\mathsf{I}^{3}F
the third power of the fundamental ideal in the Witt ring of
F
.
R. Elman and T.Y. Lam
[
12
]
proved that non-degenerate quadratic forms over
F
are classified by dimension, discriminant and the Clifford invariant precisely when
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
(see
Proposition

3.11
).
Under the same condition that
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
,
E. Bayer-Fluckiger and R. Parimala
[
2
]
obtained analogous classification results for hermitian forms over
F
-algebras with orthogonal involution.
From this D. Lewis and J.-P. Tignol
[
19
]
derived classification results for algebras with involution over such fields.
They showed that discriminant and Clifford algebra suffice to classify
F
-algebras with orthogonal involution up to conjugation when
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
.

Let us say that the field
F

satisfies
\mathsf{I}^{3}=0
if
F
has characteristic different from
2
and
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
.
Some of the most classical types of fields where quadratic form theory was first developed have the property
\mathsf{I}^{3}=0
:
p
-adic fields, non-real number fields and function fields of complex surfaces.
Those fields present a rich theory of quadratic forms and linear algebraic groups.
Often a result was proven first for, say,
p
-adic fields and non-real number fields and later extended to fields with
\mathsf{I}^{3}=0
.

A remarkable feature concerning quadratic forms over fields which is not controlled by the property
\mathsf{I}^{3}=0
is given by the
u
-invariant.
Recall that a quadratic form over
F
is
isotropic
if it has a non-trivial solution, otherwise it is
anisotropic
.
The
u
-invariant of
F
, denoted by
u(F)
, is the supremum in
\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}

on the dimensions of anisotropic quadratic forms over
F
.

The classical examples of fields with
\mathsf{I}^{3}=0
all have
u
-invariant
1,2
or
4
.
A. Merkurjev
[
22
]
constructed examples of fields that show that any even positive integer is the
u
-invariant of some field satisfying
\mathsf{I}^{3}=0
. The value
\infty
can be realised as well.

In this article, we want to add a new aspect to the study of fields with
\mathsf{I}^{3}=0
by considering the possible degrees of central simple algebras over
F
carrying an anisotropic orthogonal involution.
General upper bounds on the degrees in terms of the
u
-invariant of
F
have been obtained,
however with no specific attention to fields with
\mathsf{I}^{3}=0
.
In our study we make in particular use of the discriminant and the Clifford algebra of an orthogonal involution on a central simple algebra over
F
. They can be interpreted as cohomological invariants, and in low degrees they can be used to determine whether an involution is isotropic.

We view the involutions on a central simple algebra as the adjoint involutions of hermitian or skew-hermitian forms.
In
Section

2
, we revisit this connection. In
Section

3
, we discuss two classical invariants of orthogonal involutions, namely the discriminant and the Clifford invariant. We further make some remarks related to those invariants for the case of a field with
\mathsf{I}^{3}=0
, including a new elementary proof, in this special case, of Merkurjev’s theorem that the
2
-torsion part of the Brauer group is generated by classes of quaternion algebras.
In
Section

5
, we obtain new upper bounds on the orthogonal
u
-invariant of a central simple algebra when
\mathsf{I}^{3}=0
.
In
Theorem

5.4
we show that, given a field
F
satisfying
\mathsf{I}^{3}=0

and a central simple algebra
A
over
F
of index
2
and of degree exceeding
4\lceil\frac{u(F)}{4}\rceil+6
, every orthogonal involution on
A
is isotropic.
In
Corollary

5.8
we reformulate these results in terms of the orthogonal
u
-invariant of an algebra with involution introduced in
[
20
]
.
When
u(F)\geqslant 6
, this bound is better than the bounds that can be obtained from the results in
[
20
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
26
]
; see
Remark

5.9
.

In
Section

6
and
Section

7
we take a closer look on the case where
u(F)=4
.
In this situation, a central simple
F
-algebra admits an orthogonal involution if and only if it is Brauer equivalent to an
F
-quaternion algebra.
In
Theorem

6.7
, we construct from a quadratic field extension
K/F
with
u(K)=6
an anisotropic
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution of index
2
and degree
8
of discriminant given by
K/F
.
In
Theorem

7.1
, we derive a characterisation of the existence of a quadratic field extension
K/F
with
u(K)=6
in terms of the presence of an anisotropic
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution of degree
10
.
This allows us to conclude that our bounds from
Theorem

5.4
and
Corollary

5.8
are optimal when
u(F)=4
; see
Example

7.2
.
We achieve this by constructing algebras with an anisotropic orthogonal involution in degrees
8
and
10
over such fields.
Furthermore, the characterisations obtained in
Theorem

6.7
and
Theorem

7.1
show that the study of questions on isotropy of involutions can be used to study problems relevant to the isotropy of quadratic forms over field extensions which a priori do not involve involutions.

Our main references are
[
16
]
for the theory of algebras with involution,
[
15
]
for the theory of hermitian forms and
[
10
]
for the theory of quadratic forms.

We write
\mathbb{N}
for the set of natural numbers including zero and
\mathbb{N}^{+}=\mathbb{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.

2.
Involutions and hermitian forms

Let
A
be a central simple
F
-algebra.
The
degree, index and exponent of
A
are denoted by
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}A
, respectively.
Given another central simple
F
-algebra
B
, we write
A\sim B
to indicate that
A
and
B
are Brauer equivalent.
By
[
25
, Chap. 8, Corollary 1.6]
, we have
A\simeq\mathbb{M}_{s}(D)
for a unique
F
-division algebra
D
and
s=\frac{\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}{A}}{\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}{A}}
, and we say that
A
is
split
if
D=F
, or equivalently, if
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=1
.
By
[
25
, Chap. 8, Theorem 1.8]
, every finitely generated
A
-right module
V
decomposes into a direct sum of simple
A
-right modules.
The number of simple components in a decomposition of
V
as a direct sum of simple
A
-right modules is called the
rank of

V
and denoted by
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}V
.
It follows that
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}V=\frac{\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{F}V}{s\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{F}D}
.
In particular, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}A=s
, and if
A
is a division algebra, then
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}V
is the dimension of
V
as an
A
-right vector space.

An
F
-involution on

A
is an anti-automorphism
\sigma:A\to A
such that
\sigma^{2}=\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{A}
and
\sigma|_{F}=\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{F}
.
If
A
admits an
F
-involution, then
A\otimes_{F}A
is split.
If
A
is split, that is

A\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{F}V
for some finite-dimensional
F
-vector space
V
,
then by
[
16
, Chap. I, Theorem, p. 1]
, the
F
-involutions on
A
are in one-to-one correspondence with the non-degenerate symmetric or alternating
F
-bilinear forms on
V
up to a scalar factor in
F^{\times}
:
The correspondence is given by
b\mapsto\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{b}
where
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{b}
is the
adjoint involution
on
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{F}V
of a non-degenerate symmetric or alternating
F
-bilinear form
b
on
V
, which is determined by the rule

b(v,f(w))=b(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{b}(f)(v),w)\quad\text{for all}\,v,w\in V\,\text{and all}\,f\in\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{F}V\,.

If
\sigma
is an
F
-involution on
A
and
K/F
is a splitting field of
A
, whereby

A\otimes_{F}K\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K}V
for some finite-dimensional
K
-vector space
V
, then the
K
-involution
\sigma_{K}=\sigma\otimes\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{K}
on
A\otimes_{F}K
corresponds under such an isomorphism to the adjoint involution of a non-degenerate symmetric or alternating
K
-bilinear form on
V
, and we call
\sigma

symplectic
if that form is alternating and
orthogonal
otherwise.
The nature of the associated bilinear form depends neither on the choice of the splitting field
K/F
nor on the isomorphism; see
[
16
, Proposition 2.6]
.
The property of
\sigma
of being orthogonal or symplectic is called the
type of
\sigma
.

Since the adjoint involution determines the bilinear form uniquely up to a factor in
F^{\times}
, orthogonal involutions on the split algebra
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{F}V
are in one-to-one correspondence with non-degenerate symmetric bilinear forms on
V
up to scaling.
Moreover, any non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on
V
is given as the polar form
b_{\varphi}:V\times V\to F,(x,y)\mapsto\frac{1}{2}(\varphi(x+y)-\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))

of a unique non-degenerate (regular) quadratic form
\varphi
defined on
V
,
and we write
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{\varphi}
for the corresponding involution
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{b_{\varphi}}
.
In what follows, we will use this correspondence of orthogonal involutions on
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{F}V
with non-degenerate quadratic forms up to scaling.

A pair
(A,\sigma)
consisting of a central simple
F
-algebra
A
and an (
orthogonal
, resp.
symplectic
)
F
-involution
\sigma
on
A
will be simply called
an
F
-algebra with
(
orthogonal
, resp.
symplectic
)
involution
.

Let be given an
F
-algebra with involution
(B,\gamma)
.
Let
V
be a finitely generated
B
-right module and let
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
.
A bi-additive map
h:V\times V\to B
is called an
\varepsilon
-
hermitian form over

(B,\gamma)
if it satisfies the following:

•

h(v\alpha,w\beta)=\gamma(\alpha)h(v,w)\beta
for all
v,w\in V
and
\alpha,\beta\in B
,

•

h(w,v)=\varepsilon\gamma(h(v,w))
for all
v,w\in V
.

We also simply call
h

hermitian
if
\varepsilon=1
and
skew-hermitian
if
\varepsilon=-1
.
For an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form
h
over
(B,\gamma)
, we denote by
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h
the rank of the underlying
B
-right module and call it the
rank of
h
.

A hermitian or skew-hermitian form over
(B,\gamma)
, defined on the
B
-right module
V
, is called
isotropic
if
h(v,v)=0
holds for some
v\in V\smallsetminus\{0\}
, otherwise it is called
anisotropic
.

Let
h:V\times V\to B
be an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(B,\gamma)
.
Consider a
B
-right submodule
U
of
V
. The restriction of
h
to
U\times U
is an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(B,\gamma)
, and we denote it by
h|_{U}
. Such a form
h|_{U}
is called a
subform of

h
.
We define

\begin{split}U^{\perp}&=\{v\in V\mid h(v,u)=0\quad\forall u\in U\}\\
&=\{v\in V\mid h(u,v)=0\quad\forall u\in U\}.\end{split}

The
\varepsilon
-hermitian form
h
over
(B,\gamma)
is called
non-degenerate
if
V^{\perp}=\{0\}
,
that is, if the only vector
v\in V
such that
h(v,w)=0
holds for all
w\in V
is the vector
v=0
.
Note that any anisotropic
\varepsilon
-hermitian form is non-degenerate.
A submodule
U
of
V
is called
totally isotropic
if
h|_{U}=0
, or equivalently, if
U\subseteq U^{\perp}
.

The
\varepsilon
-hermitian form
h
is called
hyperbolic
if it is non-degenerate and there exists a totally isotropic
B
-submodule
U
of
V
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}U=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h
, or equivalently, such that
U=U^{\perp}
.

We refer to
[
15
, Chap. I, §2.2 & §3.4]
for the basic concepts of isometry (
\simeq
) and orthogonal sum (
\perp
) for
\varepsilon
-hermitian forms.
We set

\operatorname{\mathsf{Sym}}^{\varepsilon}(B,\gamma)=\{\alpha\in B\mid\gamma(\alpha)=\varepsilon\alpha\}\,,

which is an
F
-linear subspace of
B
. We also write
\operatorname{\mathsf{Skew}}(B,\gamma)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Sym}}^{-1}(B,\gamma)
.
We say that the
\varepsilon
-hermitian form
h

represents
the element
\alpha\in B
, if there exists
v\in V
such that
\alpha=h(v,v)
; note that this implies that
\alpha\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Sym}}^{\varepsilon}(B,\gamma)
.

For
\alpha\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Sym}}^{\varepsilon}(B,\gamma)
, we denote by
\langle\alpha\rangle
the
\varepsilon
-hermitian form
B\times B\to B
given by
(v,w)\to\gamma(v)\alpha w
.
Given
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Sym}}^{\varepsilon}(B,\gamma)
, we denote by
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
the
\varepsilon
-hermitian form obtained as the orthogonal sum
\langle\alpha_{1}\rangle\perp\ldots\perp\langle\alpha_{n}\rangle
over
(B,\gamma)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
B
is a division algebra and
h
is a hyperbolic
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(B,\gamma)
. Then every subform
h^{\prime}
of
h
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h^{\prime}>\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h
is isotropic.

Proof.

Let
V
be the
B
-right vector space on which
h
is defined.
Since
h
is hyperbolic, there exists a totally isotropic
B
-subspace
W
of
V
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{B}W=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{B}V
.
Let now
U
be a
B
-right subspace of
V
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{B}U>\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{B}V
. In view of the dimensions, we have that
W\cap U\neq\{0\}
.
As
h|_{W}=0
, we deduce that
h|_{U}
is isotropic.
∎

Up to isometry, there is a unique hyperbolic
\varepsilon
-hermitian form of rank
2
, and we denote this form by
\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}
; see
[
15
, Chap. I, §3.5]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
B
is a division algebra. Let
h
be a non-degenerate isotropic
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(B,\gamma)
.
Then
h
admits a subform isometric to
\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}
.
In particular
h
represents every element of
\operatorname{\mathsf{Sym}}^{\varepsilon}(B,\gamma)
.

Proof.

See
[
15
, Corollary 3.7.4]
for fact that
h
admits
\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}
as a subform. For any
\alpha\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Sym}}^{\varepsilon}(B,\gamma)\smallsetminus\{0\}
, we have that the
\varepsilon
-hermitian form
\langle\alpha,-\alpha\rangle
over
(B,\gamma)
is non-degenerate, isotropic and of rank
2
, and consequently isometric to
\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}
, whereby in particular
\alpha
is represented by
\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}
.
∎

Let
V
be a finitely generated
B
-right module.
Let
h:V\times V\to B
be a non-degenerate hermitian or skew-hermitian form over
(B,\gamma)
.
One associates to
h
an involution
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}
on
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{B}V
determined by the rule

h(v,f(w))=h(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}(f)(v),w)\quad\text{for all}\,v,w\in V\,\text{and all}\,f\in\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{B}V.

We call
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}
the
adjoint involution of

h
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Any
F
-involution
\sigma
on
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{B}V
is the adjoint involution
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}
of some non-degenerate hermitian or skew-hermitian form
h
over
(B,\gamma)
, which is unique up to a factor in
F^{\times}
.
The involutions
\sigma
and
\gamma
have the same type if
h
is hermitian, and they have different types if
h
is skew-hermitian.

Proof.

See
[
16
, Theorem 4.2]
.
∎

We denote by
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{B}(h)
the
F
-algebra with involution
(\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{B}V,\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h})
.

Example 2.4
.

For
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and
\alpha\in B^{\times}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Sym}}^{\varepsilon}(B,\gamma)
, we have

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{B}(\langle\alpha\rangle)\simeq(B,\operatorname{\mathsf{Int}}(\alpha^{-1})\circ\gamma).

Let
(A,\sigma)
be an
F
-algebra with involution.
We say that
(A,\sigma)
, or simply
\sigma
, is
isotropic
if there exists some
a\in A\smallsetminus\{0\}
such that
\sigma(a)a=0
, and
anisotropic
otherwise.
If there exists some
e\in A
with
e^{2}=e
and
\sigma(e)=1-e
, then we call
(A,\sigma)
, or simply
\sigma
,

hyperbolic
.
In particular, any hyperbolic involution is isotropic.
Note that, if
A
is a division algebra, then any involution on
A
is anisotropic.

The following statement allows us to switch swiftly back and forth between isotropy for a hermitian or skew-hermitian form and for its adjoint involution. We will often use it without explicit reference.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(A,\sigma)
be an
F
-algebra with involution.
Let
D
be a central
F
-division algebra Brauer equivalent to
A
and
\gamma
an involution on
D
.
Let
V
be a finite-dimensional
D
-right vector space such that
A\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{D}V
and

h:V\times V\to D
a non-degenerate
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(D,\gamma)
such that
\sigma=\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}
.
Then
\sigma
is isotropic (respectively hyperbolic) if and only if
h
is isotropic (respectively hyperbolic).

Proof.

See
[
1
, Corollary 1.8 and Theorem 2.1]
.
∎

Let
(A,\sigma),(A_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(A_{2},\sigma_{2})
be
F
-algebras with involution.
Following
[
24
]
, we say that
(A,\sigma)
is an
orthogonal sum of
(A_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(A_{2},\sigma_{2})
and we write

(A,\sigma)\in(A_{1},\sigma_{1})\boxplus(A_{2},\sigma_{2})

if there exist an
F
-division algebra with involution
(D,\gamma)
,
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and two non-degenerate
\varepsilon
-hermitian forms
h_{i}:V_{i}\times V_{i}\to D
over
(D,\gamma)
such that
(A_{i},\sigma_{i})\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{D}(h_{i})
for
i=1,2
and
(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{D}(h_{1}\perp h_{2})
.
In particular, we have that
A,A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are all Brauer equivalent to
D
and the involutions
\sigma,\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
have the same type.
Note that, in general, the
F
-algebra with involution
(A,\sigma)
is not uniquely determined:
For any
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in F^{\times}
, the
F
-algebra with involution
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{D}(\lambda_{1}h_{1}\perp\lambda_{2}h_{2})
is also an orthogonal sum of
(A_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(A_{2},\sigma_{2})
, but it is not necessarily isomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{D}(h_{1}\perp h_{2})
.

3.
Cohomological invariants

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and let
A
be a central simple
F
-algebra with
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A=2n
.
Let
\sigma
be an orthogonal involution on
A
.
For any
a,b\in A^{\times}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Skew}}(A,\sigma)
, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{A}(a){F}^{\times 2}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{A}(b){F}^{\times 2}
, by
[
16
, Proposition 7.1]
.
The
discriminant of

\sigma
is defined as the class

e_{1}(\sigma)=(-1)^{n}\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{A}(a)F^{\times 2}\mbox{ in }F^{\times}/F^{\times 2}

for an arbitrary
a\in A^{\times}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Skew}}(A,\sigma)
.
We say that
\sigma
has
trivial discriminant
if
e_{1}(\sigma)
is the trivial square-class in
F^{\times}/F^{\times 2}
.

By an
F
-quaternion algebra
we mean a central simple
F
-algebra of degree
2
.
Any
F
-quaternion algebra
Q
carries a unique symplectic involution, called the
canonical involution of
Q
, and we will denote it by
\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q}
.
For
a,b\in{F}^{\times}
, we denote by
(a,b)_{F}
the
F
-quaternion algebra generated by two elements
i
and
j
satisfying the relations
i^{2}=a,j^{2}=b\mbox{ and }ij+ji=0
.
Any
F
-quaternion algebra is isomorphic to
(a,b)_{F}
for certain
a,b\in{F}^{\times}
.

For later use we make the following observation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
Q
be an
F
-quaternion algebra. Let
h
be a non-degenerate skew-hermitian form over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
.
Assume that
h
represents an element
\alpha\in Q^{\times}
with
-\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q}(\alpha)\in F^{\times 2}
.
Then
Q
is split and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h)
is isotropic.

Proof.

Since
\alpha
is represented by
h
, we deduce that
\alpha
is a pure quaternion in
Q
.
Hence
-\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q}(\alpha)=-\alpha\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q}(\alpha)=\alpha^{2}
.
It follows by our assumption that there exists some
c\in F^{\times}
such that
\alpha^{2}=c^{2}
.
Hence
(\alpha-c)(\alpha+c)=0
in
Q
. Since
\alpha\not\in F
, this implies that
Q
is split.

Let now
V
be the underlying
Q
-right module of
h
and let
u\in V
be such that
h(u,u)=\alpha
.
Set
v=u(\alpha+c)
.
Note that
\alpha\in{Q}^{\times}\smallsetminus F
and therefore
h(u,v)=\alpha(\alpha+c)\neq 0
.
In particular
v\in V\smallsetminus\{0\}
.
As

h(v,v)=h(u(\alpha+c),v)=\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q}(\alpha+c)h(u,v)=(c-\alpha)\alpha(\alpha+c)=\alpha(c^{2}-\alpha^{2})=0\,,

we obtain that
h
is isotropic.
Hence
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}
is isotropic, by
Proposition

2.5
.

Lemma 3.8

Non-degenerate quadratic forms over

In particular, if
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0

Proof.

See e.g.
[

We denote by
\operatorname{\mathsf{Br}}_{2}(F)
the

-torsion subgroup of the Brauer group

. Note that
[Q]\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Br}}_{2}(F)

-quaternion algebra

This very deep theorem turns out to have a far more elementary proof for fields with

4.
Isotropy over a quadratic field extension

Consider a quadratic field extension
K/F
.
For a non-degenerate quadratic form
\varphi
defined over
F
, the anisotropic part of the quadratic form
\varphi_{K}
obtained by extending scalars to
K
is itself defined over
F
(see
[
10
, Example 29.2]
).
In this section, we show that the same phenomenon appears for non-degenerate skew-hermitian forms over a quaternion division algebra with its canonical involution.
This will be presented by a distinction of two cases, according to whether the given quaternion algebra remains a division algebra or splits by scalar extension to
K
.

Let
(B,\gamma)
be an
F
-algebra with involution,
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}

and
h:V\times V\to B
an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(B,\gamma)
, where
V
is a finitely generated
B
-right module.
Consider a field extension
K/F
.
By scalar extension from
F
to
K
, we obtain an
\varepsilon
-hermitian form
h_{K}:V\otimes_{F}K\times V\otimes_{F}K\to B\otimes_{F}K
over
(B\otimes_{F}K,\gamma_{K})
given by
h_{K}(v\otimes\alpha,w\otimes\beta)=h(v,w)\otimes\alpha\beta
for
v,w\in V,\alpha,\beta\in K
.

The following fact is well-known, see e.g. the proof of
[
25
, Chapter 10, Theorem 3.2]
. For convenience we include a proof.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
Q
be an
F
-quaternion division algebra and let
K/F
be a quadratic field extension such that

Q_{K}
is split.
Fix
\alpha\in Q\smallsetminus F
with
\alpha^{2}\in F^{\times}
and
K\simeq_{F}F(\alpha)
.
Let
h
be a non-degenerate skew-hermitian form over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
.
There exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in F^{\times}
and a non-degenerate skew-hermitian form
h^{\prime}
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
such that
h^{\prime}_{K}
is anisotropic and

h\simeq\langle\alpha\lambda_{1},\ldots,\alpha\lambda_{n}\rangle\perp h^{\prime}\,.

Moreover, given any such decomposition, the form
\langle\alpha\lambda_{1},\ldots,\alpha\lambda_{n}\rangle_{K}
is hyperbolic and
e_{1}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{\langle\alpha\lambda_{1},\ldots,\alpha\lambda_{n}\rangle})=(-\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q}(\alpha))^{n}{F}^{\times 2}

Proof.

If
h_{K}
is anisotropic, then we may simply take
n=0
and
h^{\prime}=h
. Assume therefore now that
h_{K}
is isotropic.
We identify
K
with the subfield
F(\alpha)
of
Q
.
It follows by the Skolem-Noether Theorem that
Q=K\oplus\beta K
for some
\beta\in Q\smallsetminus F
with
\beta^{2}\in F^{\times}
and
\beta\alpha=-\alpha\beta
.
Let
V
denote the
Q
-right vector space on which
h
is defined.
Consider the two projections
\pi_{0},\pi_{1}:Q\to K
determined by the decomposition
Q=K\oplus\beta K
, that is such that
z=\pi_{0}(z)+\beta\pi_{1}(z)
for any
z\in Q
.
Set
h_{0}=\pi_{0}\circ h
and
h_{1}=\pi_{1}\circ h
.
Then
h_{0}
is a non-degenerate skew-hermitian form over
(K,\iota)
, where
\iota
is the non-trivial
F
-autormorphism of
K
, and
h_{1}
is a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form over
K
.

Any
\lambda\in K
induces a
K
-linear map
r_{\lambda}:V\to V,v\mapsto v\lambda
.
Now the map
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{Q}V\times K\to\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K}V
,
(f,\lambda)\mapsto f\circ r_{\lambda}
induces a homomorphism of
F
-vector spaces
\varphi:\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{Q}V\otimes_{F}K\to\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K}V
such that
\varphi(f\otimes\lambda)=f\circ r_{\lambda}
for all
f\in\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{Q}V
and all
\lambda\in K
.
Clearly
\varphi
is a
K
-algebra homomorphism.
Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{Q}V\otimes_{F}K
is simple and
\varphi(\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{V}\otimes 1_{K})=\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{V}\neq 0
, it follows that
\varphi
is injective.
We have

\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{Q}V\otimes_{F}K=\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{Q}V=\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}_{Q}V\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}_{K}V=\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K}V.

It follows that
\varphi
is also surjective.

Consider
f\in\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{Q}V
,
\lambda\in K
and
v,w\in V
.
As
h(v,f(w))=h(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}(f)(v),w)
, we have
h_{1}(v,f(w))=h_{1}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}(f)(v),w)
.
Since
h_{1}
is
K
-bilinear, we obtain that

\begin{split}h_{1}((\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}(f)\circ r_{\lambda})(v),w)&=h_{1}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}(f)(v\lambda),w)=h_{1}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}(f)(v)\lambda,w)\\
&=h_{1}(v,f(w)\lambda)=h_{1}(v,f(w\lambda))=h_{1}(v,(f\circ r_{\lambda})(w)).\end{split}

This shows that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h_{1}}(f\circ r_{\lambda})=\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}(f)\circ r_{\lambda}
for any
\lambda\in K
and
f\in\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{Q}V
.
This implies that
\varphi
is an isomorphism of
K
-algebras with involution. Hence

(\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{K}V,\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}h_{1})\simeq(\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{Q}V,\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}h)_{K}.

Since
h_{K}
is isotropic, it follows that
h_{1}
is isotropic. Fix
v_{1}\in V\smallsetminus\{0\}
such that
h_{1}(v_{1},v_{1})=0
.
Hence
h(v_{1},v_{1})=h_{0}(v_{1},v_{1})
. Since
h_{0}
is a skew-hermitian form over
(K,\iota)
, we get that
h(v_{1},v_{1})=\lambda\alpha
for some
\lambda\in F
.
If
\lambda\neq 0
, then we set
\lambda_{1}=\lambda
and otherwise we set
\lambda_{1}=1
.
In view of
Lemma

2.2
, in either case,
\alpha\lambda_{1}\in{Q}^{\times}
and
\alpha\lambda_{1}
is represented by
h
.
By
[
15
, Lemma 3.6.2]
, we get that
h\simeq\langle\alpha\lambda_{1}\rangle\perp\tilde{h}
for some non-degenerate skew-hermitian form
\tilde{h}
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
.
If
\tilde{h}_{K}
is anisotropic, then we set
h^{\prime}=\tilde{h}
, and otherwise, we repeat the process with
\tilde{h}
in the place of
h
.
This proves the first part of the statement.

By
Lemma

3.1
, for every
\lambda\in{F}^{\times}
, the
K
-algebra with involution
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(\langle\alpha\lambda\rangle)_{K}
is hyperbolic.
It follows by
Proposition

2.5
that
\langle\alpha\lambda_{1},\ldots,\alpha\lambda_{n}\rangle_{K}
is hyperbolic.
Lemma

3.8
yields that
e_{1}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{\langle\alpha\lambda_{1},\ldots,\alpha\lambda_{n}\rangle})=(-1)^{n}(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n})^{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q}(\alpha)^{n}{F}^{\times 2}=(-\operatorname{\mathsf{Nrd}}_{Q}(\alpha))^{n}{F}^{\times 2}
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Assume that
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
.
Let
Q
be an
F
-quaternion division algebra and let
h
be an anisotropic skew-hermitian form over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
.
Let
K/F
be a quadratic field extension such that
Q_{K}
is split.
Then
h\simeq h^{\prime}\perp h^{\prime\prime}
for some skew-hermitian forms
h^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime\prime}
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
such that
h^{\prime}_{K}
is anisotropic,
h^{\prime\prime}_{K}
is hyperbolic and
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h^{\prime\prime}\leqslant 2
.

Proof.

By
Proposition

4.1
, there exist two skew-hermitian forms
h^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime\prime}
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
such that
h^{\prime}_{K}
is anisotropic,
h^{\prime\prime}_{K}
is hyperbolic and
h\simeq h^{\prime}\perp h^{\prime\prime}
.
As
h
is anisotropic, so is
h^{\prime\prime}
.
Since
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
, and
(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h^{\prime\prime}))_{K}
is split and hyperbolic,
it follows by
[
8
, Proposition 4.1]
that
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h^{\prime\prime})\leqslant 4
, whereby
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h^{\prime\prime}\leqslant 2
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(D,\gamma)
be an
F
-division algebra with involution.
Let
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and
h
a non-degenerate
\varepsilon
-hermitian form over
(D,\gamma)
.
Let
K/F
be a quadratic field extension such that
D_{K}
is a division algebra. Then

h\simeq h_{1}\perp\ldots\perp h_{n}\perp h^{\prime}

for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\varepsilon
-hermitian forms
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}
over
(D,\gamma)
of rank
2
such that
(h_{i})_{K}
is hyperbolic for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
and some
\varepsilon
-hermitian form
h^{\prime}
over
(D,\gamma)
such that
h^{\prime}_{K}
is anisotropic.

Proof.

In view of
Lemma

2.2
, it is enough to prove the statement for the anisotropic part of
h
.
We may therefore assume that
h
is anisotropic.

If
h_{K}
is anisotropic, then we take
n=0
and
h^{\prime}=h
. Assume now that
h_{K}
is isotropic.
Let
V
be the
D
-right vector space on which
h
is defined and fix
\alpha\in K\smallsetminus F
.
As
h_{K}
is isotropic and
K=F(\alpha)=F\oplus F\alpha
, there exist
u,v\in V
not both zero such that
h_{K}(u+v\alpha,u+v\alpha)=0
.
Denote by
W
the subspace of
V
spanned by
u
and
v
and let
W^{\perp}
denote its orthogonal complement with respect to
h
.
Since
h
is anisotropic,
h|_{W}
is a non-degenerate subform of
h
.
Since
D_{K}
is a division algebra and
(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h|_{W}})_{K}
is isotropic, we deduce that
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{D}W=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{D_{K}}W_{K}=2
.
Set
h_{1}=h|_{W}
. Then
V=W\oplus W^{\perp}
and
h\simeq h_{1}\perp h_{1}^{\prime}
with
h_{1}^{\prime}=h|_{W^{\perp}}
.
As
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{1}=2
and
(h_{1})_{K}
is isotropic, we get that
(h_{1})_{K}
is hyperbolic.
Repeating this process with
h_{1}^{\prime}
in the place of
h
, we will finally reach a decomposition of
h
as desired.
∎

5.
Orthogonal
u
-invariant of quaternion algebras

In this section, we obtain an upper bound on the rank of anisotropic skew-hermitian forms defined over a quaternion algebra with canonical involution in terms of the
u
-invariant when
\mathsf{I}^{3}=0
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(A,\sigma)
be an
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A>u(F)
.
If
A
is split, then
\sigma
is isotropic. In particular, if
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A
is odd, then
\sigma
is isotropic.

Proof.

Since
A
carries an orthogonal involution, by
[
16
, Corollary 2.8]
,
A
is split when
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A
is odd.

Assume now that
A
is split. Then
(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\varphi)
for some non-degenerate quadratic form
\varphi
over
F
.
As
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}\varphi=\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A>u(F)
, we have that
\varphi
is isotropic. Therefore
(A,\sigma)
is isotropic.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
.
Let
(A,\sigma)
be an
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution of trivial discriminant with
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A\leqslant 2
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A\equiv 0\operatorname{\,\mathsf{mod}}\,4
.
If
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A>u(F)+2
, then
(A,\sigma)
is isotropic.

Proof.

If
A
is split, then the statement follows by
Proposition

5.1
.
Assume now that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=2
. Hence,
A\sim Q
for an
F
-quaternion division algebra
Q
.
By
Theorem

3.4
, we have
C(A,\sigma)\simeq C^{+}\times C^{-}
for some central simple
F
-algebras
C^{+}
and
C^{-}
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}C^{+},\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}C^{-}\leqslant 2
and
C^{+}\otimes_{F}C^{-}\sim Q
.

It follows by
Proposition

3.13
that
[C^{+}]=e_{2}(q^{+})
and
[C^{-}]=e_{2}(q^{-})
for two even-dimensional non-degenerate quadratic forms
q^{+}
and
q^{-}
over
F
of trivial discriminant.
Recall that
e_{2}
vanishes for forms in
\mathsf{I}^{3}F
. Hence
Proposition

3.7

(c)
yields that
e_{2}(q^{+})
and
e_{2}(q^{-})
depend on the forms
q^{+}
and
q^{-}
, respectively, only up to Witt equivalence and similarity.
We may thus choose
q^{+}
and
q^{-}
in such way that
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}q^{+}=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}q^{-}\leqslant u(F)
and both
q^{+}
and
q^{-}
represent
1
.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}q^{+}=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}q^{-}=2m
.
Let
n_{Q}
denote the norm form of
Q
, which is a
2
-fold Pfister form over
F
. Let
n^{\prime}_{Q}
denote the
3
-dimensional subform such that

n_{Q}=\langle 1\rangle\perp n_{Q}^{\prime}
.
We write
q^{+}=\langle 1\rangle\perp q^{+}_{0}
and
q^{-}=\langle 1\rangle\perp q^{-}_{0}
for some non-degenerate quadratic forms
q_{0}^{+}
,
q_{0}^{-}
over
F
.
Note that
q_{0}^{+}\perp-q^{-}\perp-n^{\prime}_{Q}
has trivial discriminant and

e_{2}(q_{0}^{+}\perp-q^{-}\perp-n^{\prime}_{Q})=e_{2}(q^{+})+e_{2}(q^{-})+e_{2}(n_{Q})=[C^{+}]+[C^{-}]+[Q]=0.

Since
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
, we obtain by
Proposition

3.11
that
q^{+}_{0}\perp-n^{\prime}_{Q}
is Witt equivalent to
q^{-}
.
Since further
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}(q^{+}_{0}\perp-n^{\prime}_{Q})=2m+2>2m=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}q^{-}
, we obtain that
q^{+}_{0}\perp-n^{\prime}_{Q}
is isotropic.
Hence
q^{+}_{0}
and
n^{\prime}_{Q}
represent some element
a\in F^{\times}
in common.
We set
K=F(\sqrt{-a})
. It follows that
(n_{Q})_{K}
and
q^{+}_{K}
are isotropic.
In particular,
Q_{K}
is split.
Since
A\sim Q
, we obtain that
(A,\sigma)_{K}\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\varphi)
for some non-degenerate quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}\varphi=\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A
.
Furthermore, we have that
e_{2}(\varphi)=e_{2}(\sigma_{K})=[C^{+}_{K}]=e_{2}(q^{+}_{K})
.
Since
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
, we also have
\mathsf{I}^{3}K=0
, by
[
10
, Corollary 35.8]
.
We conclude by
Proposition

3.11
that
\varphi
is Witt equivalent to
q^{+}_{K}
.

Assume now that
\sigma
is anisotropic.
Then
(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h)
for some anisotropic skew-hermitian form
h
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A
.
Since
Q_{K}
is split, it follows by
Corollary

4.2
that
h\simeq h_{0}\perp h_{1}
for two non-degenerate skew-hermitian forms
h_{0},h_{1}
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{0}\leqslant 2
,
(h_{0})_{K}
is hyperbolic, and
(h_{1})_{K}
is anisotropic.
We obtain that

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\varphi)\simeq(A,\sigma)_{K}\simeq(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h_{0}\perp h_{1}))_{K}

and that
\varphi\simeq\psi\perp(\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{0})\times\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}
for some anisotropic quadratic form
\psi
over
K
.
Hence
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}\varphi=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}\psi+2\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{0}\leqslant\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}\psi+4
.
Recall that
q^{+}_{K}
is isotropic and Witt equivalent to
\varphi
, hence also to
\psi
.
Therefore

\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}\psi\leqslant\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}q^{+}_{K}-2=2m-2.

We conclude that
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A\leqslant 2m+2\leqslant u(F)+2
.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume that
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
and
u(F)<\infty
. Let
Q
be an
F
-quaternion division algebra and
m=\lceil\frac{1}{4}u(F)\rceil
.
Let
h
be a non-degenerate skew-hermitian form over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
. Then the following hold:

(a)

If
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h=2n
for some integer
n>m
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}
has trivial discriminant, then
h\simeq(n-m)\times\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}\perp h^{\prime}
for some non-degenerate skew hermitian form
h^{\prime}
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
.

(b)

If
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h\geqslant 2m+2
,
then
h\simeq h_{0}\perp h_{1}
for two non-degenerate skew-hermitian forms
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{0}=2m
and
e_{1}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h_{0}})
is trivial.

(c)

If
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h\geqslant 2m+4
, then
h
is isotropic.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h=2n+1
for some integer
n\geqslant m
, then to show
(b)
or
(c)
, we may replace
h
by a non-degenerate subform of rank
2n
.
Hence we may assume throughout without loss of generality that
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h=2n
for an integer
n\geqslant m
.

Assume first that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h}
has trivial discriminant.
Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h)
is an
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution of trivial discriminant.
If
n>m
, then it follows by
Proposition

5.2
that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h)
is isotropic, whereby
h
is isotropic and hence contains
\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}
as a subform, by
Lemma

2.2
.
An
(n-m)
-fold application of this argument establishes
(a)
.

To show
(b)
and
(c)
, we fix
d\in F^{\times}
with
e_{1}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h})=dF^{\times 2}
.
Using
Lemma

3.9
, we take a non-degenerate skew-hermitian form
h^{\prime\prime}
of rank
2
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
with
e_{1}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h^{\prime\prime}})=dF^{\times 2}
.
We consider the non-degenerate skew-hermitian form
h^{\prime}=h\perp h^{\prime\prime}
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
.
Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h^{\prime}=2n+2
, and by
Proposition

3.7

(b)
,
e_{1}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h^{\prime}})
is trivial.
Hence, we obtain by
(a)
that

h^{\prime}\simeq(n+1-m)\times\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}\perp h_{0}

for a non-degenerate skew-hermitian form
h_{0}
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{0}=2m
.
As
e_{1}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h^{\prime}})
is trivial, we obtain by
Proposition

3.7

(b)
that
e_{1}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h_{0}})
is trivial.
Set
h_{1}=-h^{\prime\prime}
. Then

h\perp 2\times\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}\simeq h^{\prime}\perp h_{1}\simeq(n+1-m)\times\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}\perp h_{0}\perp h_{1}\,.

Assume now that
n\geqslant m+1
.
Using Witt Cancellation
[
15
, Corollary 6.4.2]
, we get that

h\simeq(n-1-m)\times\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}\perp h_{0}\perp h_{1}.

This establishes
(b)
. Furthermore, if

, then

is isotropic, and this shows
(c)
.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume that
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
. Let
(A,\sigma)
be an
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution with
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A\leqslant 2
.
If
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A>4\lceil\frac{u(F)}{4}\rceil+6

(A,\sigma)

Proof.

If
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=1
, then

5.1
implies that

is isotropic. Assume now that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=2
.
Then
A
is Brauer equivalent to an
F
-quaternion division algebra

.
We have
(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h)
for some non-degenerate skew-hermitian form

over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A
.
Thus
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h>2\lceil\frac{u(F)}{4}\rceil+3
, and it follows by

5.3
that

is isotropic.
Hence

is isotropic.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Assume that
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
. Let
(A,\sigma)
be an
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A\leqslant 2
.
Assume that
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A=4\lceil\frac{u(F)}{4}\rceil+4
.
Let

be such that

and set

.
Assume that

. Then
(A,\sigma)
is isotropic.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=1
, then the statement follows by
Proposition

5.1
.
Assume that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=2
. Then
A
is Brauer equivalent to an
F
-quaternion division algebra
Q
.
If
d\in{F}^{\times 2}
, then
Proposition

5.2
implies that
(A,\sigma)
is isotropic.
So we may assume that

, whereby

is a quadratic field extension.

Set

. Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A=4n\geqslant u(K)+4
.
Since
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
, it follows by
[
10
, Theorem 34.22]
that
\mathsf{I}^{3}K=0
.
As
\sigma_{K}
has trivial discriminant, it follows by
Proposition

5.2
that
\sigma_{K}
is isotropic.

We fix a non-degenerate skew-hermitian form
h
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h=2n
such that
(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h)
. Then

is isotropic.
We claim that
h\simeq h_{0}\perp h_{1}
for some non-degenerate skew-hermitian forms
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{0}=2
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{1}=2n-2

is split, this follows by

, using that

Proposition

4.3
.
Since

is a division algebra,
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{0}=2

We now set

.
It follows that

,
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A_{1}=4n-4

It follows by

3.7

(b)

By

3.10

(A_{0},\sigma_{0})\simeq(H,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{H})\otimes_{F}(H^{\prime},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{H^{\prime}})

.

Then

is hyperbolic, it follows by
Proposition

3.5
that one of
H_{K}
and
H^{\prime}_{K}
is split.
We may assume without loss of generality that
H_{K}
is split.
Hence
H\simeq(d,\lambda)_{F}
for some
\lambda\in F^{\times}
.
We consider the
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h_{1}\perp-\lambda h_{1})\in(A_{1},\sigma_{1})\boxplus(A_{1},\sigma_{1})\,.

Its degree is
8n-8
, and by
Proposition

3.7

(b)
, its discriminant is trivial.
Since
h_{1}\perp-\lambda h_{1}=\langle 1,-\lambda\rangle\otimes h_{1}
and
e_{1}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h_{1}})=d{F}^{\times 2}
, we conclude that
e_{2}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h_{1}\perp-\lambda h_{1}})=[(d,\lambda)]
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Br}}(F)/\langle[Q]\rangle
.
We set
h^{\prime}=h_{0}\perp h_{1}\perp-\lambda h_{1}
and note that
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h^{\prime}=4n-2
.
We consider the
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h^{\prime})\in(A_{0},\sigma_{0})\boxplus(A_{1},\sigma_{1})\boxplus(A_{1},\sigma_{1}).

By
Proposition

3.7
, we have that
e_{1}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h^{\prime}})
is trivial and that

e_{2}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h^{\prime}})=e_{2}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h_{0}})+e_{2}(\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h_{1}\perp-\lambda h_{1}})=[H]+[H]=0\quad\text{in}\,\operatorname{\mathsf{Br}}(F)/\langle[Q]\rangle.

Since
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
, it follows by
Proposition

3.14
that
h^{\prime}
is hyperbolic.
Then, since
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(h_{0}\perp h_{1})=2n>\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h^{\prime}
, we conclude by
Proposition

2.1
that
h_{0}\perp h_{1}
is isotropic.
Therefore
h
is isotropic. Hence
\sigma
is isotropic.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Assume that
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
u(K)\leqslant 4k
for every field extension
K/F
with
[K:F]\leqslant 2
.
Let
A
be a central simple
F
-algebra with
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A\leqslant 2
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A>4k+2
.
Then every orthogonal involution on
A
is isotropic.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=1
, then the statement follows by
Proposition

5.1
.
Assume now that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=2
. Then
A
is Brauer equivalent to an
F
-quaternion division algebra
Q
.
Let
\sigma
be an orthogonal involution on
A
.
Then
(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h)
for a non-degenerate skew-hermitian form
h
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A>2k+1
.
If
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A>4\lceil\frac{u(F)}{4}\rceil+6
, then it follows by
Theorem

5.4
that
\sigma
is isotropic.
Assume now that
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A\leqslant 4\lceil\frac{u(F)}{4}\rceil+6
.
Since we have
u(F)\leqslant 4k
, we obtain that
4k+2<\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A\leqslant 4\lceil\frac{u(F)}{4}\rceil+6\leqslant 4k+6
.
In particular
\lceil\frac{u(F)}{4}\rceil=k
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h\in\{2k+2,2k+3\}
.
We choose a non-degenerate subform
h^{\prime}
of
h
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h^{\prime}=2k+2
and consider the
F
-algebra
(A^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h^{\prime})
.
We fix
d\in{F}^{\times}
such that
e_{1}(\sigma^{\prime})=d{F}^{\times 2}
and set
K=F(\sqrt{d})
.
Then
u(K)\leqslant 4k
, and it follows by
Proposition

5.5
that
\sigma^{\prime}
is isotropic.
We obtain that
h^{\prime}
is isotropic, and hence so is
h
. Therefore
\sigma
is isotropic.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Assume that
u(F)\leqslant 6
.
Let
(A,\sigma)
be an
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution such that
\sigma
is anisotropic and
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A\leqslant 2
.
Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A\leqslant 10
.

Proof.

Consider a quadratic field extension
K/F
.
We have
u(K)\leqslant\frac{3}{2}u(F)\leqslant 9
, by
[
11
, Theorem 4.3]
.
Since
u(F)\leqslant 6
, in particular all
3
-fold Pfister forms are hyperbolic and thus
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
.
Hence also
\mathsf{I}^{3}K=0
, by
[
10
, Theorem 34.22]
.
Then
u(K)
is even, by
[

, Proposition 36.3]
. Therefore

.

This argument shows that
u(K)\leqslant 8
for every field extension
K/F
with

.
We conclude by
Theorem

5.6
that
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A\leqslant 10
.
∎

Let us reformulate the main results of this section in terms of the orthogonal
u
-invariant.
Consider a central simple
F
-algebra
B
that carries an orthogonal involution
\gamma^{\prime}
.
The
orthogonal
u
-invariant of

B
, denoted by
u^{+}(B)
, is defined as follows:

\mbox{$u^{+}(B)=\sup\{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h\mid h\,\text{anisotropic hermitian form over}\,(B,\gamma^{\prime})\}$}.

This definition does not depend on the particular choice of
\gamma^{\prime}
; see
[
20
, Proposition 2.2]
.
For
B=F
, we obtain that
u^{+}(F)=u(F)
, as non-degenerate hermitian forms with respect to the orthogonal involution
\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{F}
on
F
are given by the polar forms of non-degenerate quadratic forms.

Taking instead of an orthogonal involution a symplectic involution
\gamma
on
B
, provided that such an involution exists, we can also express
u^{+}(B)
in terms of skew-hermitian forms over
(B,\gamma)
:

u^{+}(B)

\displaystyle\mbox{$=\sup\{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h\mid h\,\text{anisotropic skew-hermitian form over}\,(B,\gamma)\}$}.

We will now use this latter description of
u^{+}(B)
in the special situation where
B
is an
F
-quaternion division algebra, by taking
\gamma=\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{B}
.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
Q
be an
F
-quaternion division algebra.

(a)

If
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
, then
u^{+}(Q)\leqslant 2\lceil\frac{u(F)}{4}\rceil+3
.

(b)

If
u(F)\leqslant 6
, then
u^{+}(Q)\leqslant 5
.

Proof.

Part
(a)
follows directly by
Proposition

5.3
, and
(b)
is clear by
Corollary

5.7
.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

Let
Q
be an
F
-quaternion division algebra. By
[
20
, Corollary 3.4]
, the bound
u^{+}(Q)\leqslant\frac{5}{4}u(F)
holds without any condition on
\mathsf{I}^{3}F
.
This covers part
(a)
from
Corollary

5.8
in the case where
u(F)=4
.
When
\mathsf{I}^{3}F=0
and
u(F)\geqslant 6
, then the bounds from
Corollary

5.8
are better than what can be obtained from
[
20
]
.

6.
Anisotropic orthogonal involutions in degree
8

In this section, we provide examples of anisotropic orthogonal involutions on a central simple algebra of degree
8
over a field with
u
-invariant
4
.
The discriminant of such an involution is necessarily non-trivial, in view of
Proposition

5.2
.
We first collect some well-known facts, mostly coming from
[
11
]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Assume that
u(F)\leqslant 4
. Let
A
be a central simple
F
-algebra with
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}A\leqslant 2
.
Then
A\sim Q
for an
F
-quaternion algebra
Q
.

Proof.

It follows by
Proposition

3.13
that

A\sim C(\varphi)
for an even-dimensional non-degenerate quadratic form
\varphi
over
F
of trivial discriminant.
Furthermore, as
u(F)\leqslant 4
, we may choose
\varphi
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}\varphi=4
.
Then
C(\varphi)\simeq\mathbb{M}_{2}(Q)
for an
F
-quaternion algebra
Q
.
We obtain that
A\sim C(\varphi)\sim Q
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume that
u(F)=6
. Then there exists an
F
-biquaternion division algebra.

Proof.

By
[
11
, Proposition 1.4]
, there exists some
6
-dimensional anisotropic quadratic form
\varphi
over
F
of trivial discriminant.
By
[
16
, Section 16.A]
, we obtain that
C(\varphi)\simeq\mathbb{M}_{2}(B)
for an
F
-biquaternion division algebra
B
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
K/F
be a quadratic field extension and
\varphi
a non-degenerate quadratic form over
K
.
Then
\varphi\simeq\vartheta_{K}\perp\psi
for a non-degenerate quadratic form
\vartheta
over
F
and a non-degenerate quadratic form
\psi
over
K
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}\psi\leqslant\frac{1}{2}u(F)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{char}F\neq 2
we can reason with the polar form of
\varphi
.
The statement now follows by choosing an
F
-linear map
s:K\to F
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ker}}s=F
and then applying
[
10
, Proposition 34.1]
.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Assume that
u(F)\leqslant 4
. Let
K/F
be a quadratic field extension. Then either
u(K)\leqslant 4
or
u(K)=6
.
Furthermore, any central simple
K
-algebra of exponent at most
2
is Brauer equivalent to
(a,b)_{K}\otimes_{K}(c,x)_{K}
for some
a,b,c\in F^{\times}
and
x\in K^{\times}
.

Proof.

Since
u(F)\leqslant 4
, it follows by
[
11
, Theorem 4.3]
that
u(K)\leqslant 6
.
Since
u(K)\neq 5
by
[
10
, Corollary 36.4]
, we conclude that
u(K)\leqslant 4
or
u(K)=6
.

Let
B
be a central simple
K
-algebra with
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B\leqslant 2
.
It follows by
Proposition

3.13
that
B\sim C(\varphi)
for some even-dimensional non-degenerate quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
of trivial discriminant, and since
u(K)\leqslant 6
, we may choose
\varphi
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}\varphi=6
.
Since
u(F)\leqslant 4
, by
Lemma

6.3
we may write
\varphi\simeq\vartheta_{K}\perp\psi
with a non-degenerate
4
-dimensional quadratic form
\vartheta
over
F
and a non-degenerate
2
-dimensional quadratic form
\psi
over
K
.
We choose elements
u,v,w,t\in{F}^{\times}
such that
\vartheta\simeq\langle u,v,w,t\rangle
.
Let
y\in{K}^{\times}
be an element represented by
\psi
.
As
\varphi
has trivial discriminant, we obtain that
\varphi\simeq\langle u,v,w,t,y,-tuvwy\rangle
.
Using
[
14
, Proposition 6.4.7 and Corollary 6.2.7]
, one finds that
C(\varphi)\simeq(-uv,-vw)_{K}\otimes_{K}(uvwt,-ty)_{K}\otimes_{K}\mathbb{M}_{2}(K)
.
Now we set
a=-uv
,
b=-vw
,
c=uvwt
and
x=-ty
, whereby
a,b,c\in{F}^{\times}
,
x\in{K}^{\times}
and
B\sim C(\varphi)\sim(a,b)_{K}\otimes_{K}(c,x)_{K}
.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
d\in F^{\times}\smallsetminus F^{\times 2}
and
K=F(\sqrt{d})
. Assume that
u(F)\leqslant 4
and
u(K)=6
.
Then there exists an anisotropic
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution
(A,\sigma)
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A=8
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=2
and
e_{1}(\sigma)=dF^{\times 2}
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

6.2
, there exists a
K
-biquaternion division algebra
B
.
By
Proposition

6.4
, there exist
a,b,c\in{F}^{\times}
and
x\in{K}^{\times}
such that

B\simeq(a,b)_{K}\otimes_{K}(c,x)_{K}\,.

We set
H=(a,b)_{F}
and
Q=(c,x)_{K}
.
Let
\iota
be the non-trivial
F
-automorphism of
K
and
{}^{\iota}Q
the
K
-quaternion algebra obtained by letting
K
act on
Q
via
\iota
.
Now
[
16
, Section 15.B]
provides an
F
-biquaternion algebra with orthogonal involution
(C,\sigma_{1})
such that
(C,\sigma_{1})_{K}\simeq(Q\otimes_{K}\!{{}^{\iota}}Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q}\otimes\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{\,{}^{\iota}Q})
,
e_{1}(\sigma_{1})=dF^{\times 2}
and
C\simeq\mathbb{M}_{2}(Q^{\prime})
for
Q^{\prime}=(c,\mathsf{N}_{K/F}(x))_{F}
, where
\mathsf{N}_{K/F}(x)
is the norm of
x
with respect to
K/F
.

We claim that
Q^{\prime}
is a division algebra.
Suppose for the sake of a contradiction that
Q^{\prime}
is split.
Then
\mathsf{N}_{K/F}(x)
is represented by the quadratic form
\langle 1,-c\rangle
over
F
.
It follows by
[
13
, Norm Principle 2.13]
that
x=ey
for some
y\in{K}^{\times}
which is represented by
\langle 1,-c\rangle
over
K
and some
e\in{F}^{\times}
.
Then
Q\simeq(c,e)_{K}
and
B\simeq H_{K}\otimes_{K}Q=(a,b)_{K}\otimes_{K}(c,e)_{K}
.
Since
B
is a division algebra, we conclude that
(a,b)_{F}\otimes_{F}(c,e)_{F}
is a division algebra.
By
Proposition

6.1
, this contradicts the hypothesis that
u(F)\leqslant 4
. Hence
Q^{\prime}
is a division algebra.

Since
u(F)\leqslant 4
, it follows by
Proposition

6.1
that
H\otimes_{F}Q^{\prime}\sim H^{\prime}
for an
F
-quaternion algebra
H^{\prime}
.
Then
H\otimes_{F}H^{\prime}\sim Q^{\prime}\sim C
. As
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}H\otimes_{F}H^{\prime}=4=\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}C
, we obtain that
H\otimes_{F}H^{\prime}\simeq C
.
We fix an orthogonal involution
\sigma_{0}
on
C
such that
(H\otimes_{F}H^{\prime},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{H}\otimes\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{H^{\prime}})\simeq(C,\sigma_{0})
.
Then
e_{1}(\sigma_{0})
is trivial, by
Proposition

3.10
, and
e_{2}(\sigma_{0})=[H]
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Br}}(F)/\langle[Q^{\prime}]\rangle
, by
Example

3.3
.
For
i=0,1
, we have that
(C,\sigma_{i})\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q^{\prime}}(h_{i})
for some non-degenerate skew-hermitian form
h_{i}
over
(Q^{\prime},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q^{\prime}})
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{i}=2
.

We consider now the
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution

(A,\sigma)\,\,=\,\,\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q^{\prime}}(h_{0}\perp h_{1})\,.

Note that
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A=8
and
A\sim Q^{\prime}
. In particular
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=2
.
Since
e_{1}(\sigma_{0})
is trivial and
(A,\sigma)\in(C,\sigma_{0})\boxplus(C,\sigma_{1})
, we obtain by
Proposition

3.7

(b)
that
e_{1}(\sigma)=e_{1}(\sigma_{1})=dF^{\times 2}
.

We claim that
(A,\sigma)
is anisotropic. Establishing this will finish the proof.
Suppose on the contrary that
(A,\sigma)
is isotropic.
Then, by
Proposition

3.2
, there exists a quadratic field extension
L/F
such that
(C,\sigma_{i})_{L}
is split and isotropic for
i=0,1
.
Since
(H_{L}\otimes^{\vphantom{M}}_{L}H^{\prime}_{L},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{H_{L}}\otimes\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{H^{\prime}_{L}})\simeq(C,\sigma_{0})_{L}
, which is split and isotropic,
it follows by
Proposition

3.5
that
H_{L}
and
H^{\prime}_{L}
are split.
Since furthermore
(Q_{KL},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q_{KL}})\otimes_{L}(^{\iota}Q_{KL},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{{}^{\iota}Q_{KL}})\simeq(C,\sigma_{1})_{KL}
, which is isotropic,
Proposition

3.5
yields that
Q_{KL}
is split.
In particular
B_{KL}
is split. Hence
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B\leqslant[KL:K]\leqslant 2
.
This contradicts that
B
is a division algebra.
∎

Example 6.6
.

By
[
18
]
, there exists a field
F
with
\mathsf{char}F\neq 2
and
u(F)=4
having a quadratic field extension
K/F
with
u(K)=6
.
(See
[
5
, Example 4.5]
for another construction of such an example.)
It follows by
Proposition

6.5
that there exists an
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution
(A,\sigma)
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=2
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A=8
and such that
\sigma
is anisotropic.

In the following, we give a necessary and sufficient condition for a field with
u
-invariant
4
to admit a quadratic field extension with
u
-invariant
6
.

Theorem 6.7
.

Assume that
u(F)\leqslant 4
. Let
d\in{F}^{\times}\smallsetminus{F}^{\times 2}
and

.
If
u(K)=6
, then there exists an anisotropic
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution
(A,\sigma)
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A=8
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=2
and

.
Otherwise

, and every
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution of degree
8
of discriminant

is isotropic.

Proof.

By
Proposition

6.4
, we have
u(K)\leqslant 4
or
u(K)=6
.
If
u(K)\leqslant 4
, then it follows by
Proposition

5.5
that every
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution of degree
8
and discriminant
d{F}^{\times 2}
is isotropic.
If
u(K)=6
, then by
Proposition

6.5
there exists an anisotropic
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution of degree
8
, index
2
and discriminant
d{F}^{\times 2}
.
∎

7.
Anisotropic orthogonal involutions in degree
10

In this section, we show that the bounds obtained in
Theorem

5.4
and
Corollary

5.8
are optimal when
u(F)=4
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Assume that
u(F)\leqslant 4
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

Every
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution of degree
10
with trivial discriminant is isotropic.

(2)

Every central simple
F
-algebra of exponent
4
has index
4
.

(3)

For every central
F
-division algebra
B
of degree
4
and every
F
-quaternion algebra
H
, there exists a quadratic field extension
K/F
such that
H_{K}
is split and
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B_{K}=2
.

Proof.

(1\Rightarrow 3)
Consider a central
F
-division algebra
B
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}B=4
and an
F
-quaternion algebra
H
.
Since
B
is not Brauer equivalent to any
F
-quaternion algebra, it follows by
Proposition

6.1
that
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B>2
.
Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B
divides
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}B
, we conclude that
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B=4
. Hence
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B^{\otimes 2}=2
.
Since
u(F)\leqslant 4
, it follows by
Proposition

6.1
that there exist
F
-quaternion algebras
Q
and
H^{\prime}
such that
B^{\otimes 2}\sim Q
and
H\otimes_{F}Q\sim H^{\prime}
.
As
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B=4
, we have that
Q
is an
F
-division algebra.
We set

(A_{0},\sigma_{0})=(H,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{H})\otimes(H^{\prime},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{H^{\prime}})

and observe that
A_{0}\sim Q
and
\sigma_{0}
is an orthogonal involution of trivial discriminant.
Let
\operatorname{\mathsf{sw}}
denote the switch involution on
B\times B^{\operatorname{\mathsf{op}}}
. This is a unitary involution.
As explained in
[
16
, Section 15.D]
, the construction of the discriminant algebra of
(B\times B^{\operatorname{\mathsf{op}}},\operatorname{\mathsf{sw}})
and of its induced canonical involution
provides us with an
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution
(A_{1},\sigma_{1})
of degree
6
and trivial discriminant having the property that

C(A_{1},\sigma_{1})\simeq B\times B^{\operatorname{\mathsf{op}}}\,.

By
Theorem

3.4
, we obtain that
A_{1}\sim B^{\otimes 2}\sim Q
.
As
A_{0}\sim A_{1}\sim Q
, there exist two non-degenerate skew-hermitian forms
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
such that

\hskip 71.13188pt(A_{i},\sigma_{i})\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h_{i})\quad\mbox{ for }i=0,1\,.

We obtain that
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A_{0}=2
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A_{1}=3
.
We now consider the
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution

(A,\sigma)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h_{0}\perp h_{1})\,.

We have
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A=10
,
A\sim Q
and
(A,\sigma)\in(A_{0},\sigma_{0})\boxplus(A_{1},\sigma_{1})
, and we compute by
Proposition

3.7

(b)
that
\sigma
has trivial discriminant.

Assume now that Condition
(1)
holds. Then
(A,\sigma)
is isotropic.
It follows by
Proposition

3.2
that there exists a quadratic field extension
K/F
such that
(A_{0},\sigma_{0})_{K}
and
(A_{1},\sigma_{1})_{K}
are both split and isotropic.
We obtain that
e_{2}((\sigma_{1})_{K})=[B_{K}]
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Br}}(K)
, the
K
-quaternion algebras
H_{K},H^{\prime}_{K},Q_{K}
are all split and
(A_{1},\sigma_{1})_{K}\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}(\varphi)
for some non-degenerate, isotropic
6
-dimensional quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
of trivial discriminant.
As
[B_{K}]=e_{2}((\sigma_{1})_{K})=e_{2}(\varphi)
and
\varphi
is isotropic, we obtain that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B_{K}\leqslant 2
.
Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B=4
and
[K:F]=2
, we conclude that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B_{K}=2
.
Hence we have found a quadratic field extension
K/F
such that
H_{K}
is split and
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B_{K}=2
.

(3\Rightarrow 2)
Consider a central simple
F
-algebra
B
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B=4
.
Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B\geqslant 4
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B^{\otimes 2}=2
.
Since
u(F)\leqslant 4
, we obtain by
Proposition

6.1
that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B^{\otimes 2}=2
.
Hence there exists a quadratic field extension
K/F
such that
B^{\otimes 2}_{K}
is split.
Since
u(F)\leqslant 4
, it follows by
Proposition

6.4
that there exist an
F
-quaternion algebra
H
and a
K
-quaternion algebra
Q
such that

B_{K}\sim H_{K}\otimes_{K}Q
.
Let
B^{\prime}

F

.
Since

, we obtain that
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B^{\prime}=\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B=4
.
Furthermore

, whereby
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B^{\prime}\leqslant[K:F]\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}Q\leqslant 4=\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B^{\prime}
.
We conclude that
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}B^{\prime}=\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B^{\prime}=\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B^{\prime}=4
.

Assume now that
(3)
holds.
Then there exists a quadratic field extension
L/F
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B^{\prime}_{L}=2
and

Then

and in particular

.
We obtain that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B\leqslant[L:F]\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B_{L}\leqslant 4=\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B
, whereby
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}B=\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B=4
.

(2\Rightarrow 1)
Let
(A,\sigma)
be an

As

and
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A=10
, we get that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A\leqslant 2
.
If

\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A>u(F)
, it follows by

that
(A,\sigma)
is isotropic.
Assume now that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=2
.
Let
Q
denote the
F
-quaternion division algebra which is Brauer equivalent to

.
We have that

(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h)

for a non-degenerate skew-hermitian form
h
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h=5
.
By

5.3

for two non-degenerate skew-hermitian forms

and

over

with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{0}=2
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{1}=3

\operatorname{\mathsf{ad}}_{h_{0}}
has trivial discriminant.
For

Then
\sigma_{0}
has trivial discriminant, and since
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A_{0}=\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}Q\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h_{0}=4
, we get by

3.10
that

(A_{0},\sigma_{0})\simeq(H,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{H})\otimes(H^{\prime},\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{H^{\prime}})

for two
F
-quaternion algebras

and

.
It follows that
H\otimes_{F}H^{\prime}\sim A_{0}\sim Q
. Furthermore,
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A_{1}=6
and
A_{1}\sim Q
.
Since
\sigma
and
\sigma_{0}
have trivial discriminant, so has
\sigma_{1}
, in view of

3.7

(b)
.
Hence, it follows by

3.4
that
C(A_{1},\sigma_{1})\simeq B\times B^{\operatorname{\mathsf{op}}}
for some central simple
F

.

.
Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}H\leqslant 2

\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B\otimes_{F}H=\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}B=4

Assume now that

The following example shows that the upper bounds obtained in
Theorem

5.4
and
Corollary

5.8
are optimal in the case where
u(F)=4
.

Example 7.2
.

By
[
5
, Example 4.4]
, there exist a field
F
with
\mathsf{char}F\neq 2
and
u(F)=4
and a central
F
-division algebra
D
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}D=4
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}D=8
.
Given such a field
F
, it follows by
Theorem

7.1
that there exists an anisotropic
F
-algebra with orthogonal involution
(A,\sigma)
of degree
10
with trivial discriminant.
In view of
Theorem

5.4
, this is the maximal possible degree for a central simple
F
-algebra carrying an anisotropic orthogonal involution when
u(F)=4
.

The presence of an
F
-involution on
A
implies that
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}A\leqslant 2
. Since
u(F)=4
, it follows that
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A\leqslant 2
.
As
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A>u(F)
and
A
carries an anisotropic orthogonal involution,
A
cannot be split.
Hence
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A=2
, whereby
A
is Brauer equivalent to an
F
-quaternion division algebra
Q
.
We have
(A,\sigma)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Ad}}_{Q}(h)
for a non-degenerate skew-hermitian form
h
over
(Q,\operatorname{\mathsf{can}}_{Q})
.
Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}A=5
, and since
\sigma
is anisotropic, it follows
that
h
is anisotropic.
In particular,
u^{+}(Q)\geqslant\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}h=5
.
Hence the upper bound
5
for
u^{+}(Q)
obtained by
Corollary

5.8
when
u(F)=4
is attained in this example.

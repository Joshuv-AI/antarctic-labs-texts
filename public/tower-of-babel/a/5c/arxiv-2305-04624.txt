Spectral properties of the Rhaly operator on weighted null sequence spaces and associated operator ideals
Arnab Patra, Jyoti Rani, Sanjay Kumar Mahto

Abstract
. In this article, a comprehensive study is made on the continuity, compactness, and spectrum of the lower triangular terraced matrix, introduced by H. C. Rhaly, Jr. [Houston J. Math. 15(1): 137-146, 1989], acting on the weighted null sequence spaces with bounded, strictly positive weights. Several spectral subdivisions such as point spectrum, residual spectrum, and continuous spectrum are also discussed. In addition, a new class of operator ideal χ c 0 ​ ( r ) ( s ) \chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)} associated to the Rhaly operator on weighted c 0 c_{0} space is defined using the concept of s s -number and it is proved that under certain condition, χ c 0 ​ ( r ) ( s ) \chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)} forms a quasi-Banach closed operator ideal.

2.
Preliminaries and Notations

Throughout the article, all the infinite sequences and matrices are indexed by the set of natural numbers
\mathbb{N}.
Let
T:X\rightarrow Y
be a bounded linear operator where
X
and
Y
are complex Banach spaces. The range space of
T
and null space of
T
are denoted by
R(T)
and
N(T)
respectively. The Banach-adjoint of
T
, denoted by
T^{*}
, is a bounded linear operator
T^{*}:Y^{*}\rightarrow X^{*}
which is defined by

(T^{*}\phi)(x)=\phi(Tx)\ \ \ \mbox{for all}\ \phi\in Y^{*}\ \mbox{and}\ x\in X,

where
X^{*}
and
Y^{*}
are the dual spaces of
X
and
Y
respectively.
\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
and
\mathcal{K}(X,Y)
denote the space of all bounded linear operators and the ideal of all compact operators form
X
into
Y
. If
X=Y,
then
\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
and
\mathcal{K}(X,Y)
are denoted by
\mathcal{B}(X)
and
\mathcal{K}(X)
respectively. For any
T\in\mathcal{B}(X),
the operator norm is denoted by
\|T\|
and the norm
\|.\|_{\infty}
denotes the supremum norm on the sequence space
c_{0}
. For any operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(X),
the resolvent set of
T
is the set of all complex numbers
\lambda
for which the operator
T-\lambda I
has a bounded inverse in
X
where
I
is the identity operator in
X.
The resolvent set of
T
is denoted by
\rho(T,X).
The complement of the resolvent set in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
is called the spectrum of
T
and it is denoted by
\sigma(T,X)
. The set of points
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
for which
N(T-\lambda I)\neq\{0\}
is called the point spectrum of
T
and it is denoted by
\sigma_{p}(T,X)
. The set of points
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
for which
N(T-\lambda I)=\{0\},
and
\overline{R(T-\lambda I)}=X
but
{R(T-\lambda I)}\neq X
is called the continuous spectrum of
T
and denoted by
\sigma_{c}(T,X),
and the set of points
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
for which
N(T-\lambda I)=\{0\}
and
\overline{R(T-\lambda I)}\neq X
is called the residual spectrum of
T
and it is denoted by
\sigma_{r}(T,X)
. The three sets
\sigma_{p}(T,X),\sigma_{c}(T,X),\sigma_{r}(T,X)
are disjoint and their union is the whole spectrum
\sigma(T,X).

Let
\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}
denotes the space of all complex sequences. For an infinite positive real sequence
r=\{r_{k}\},
the weighted null sequence space
c_{0}(r)
is defined as

c_{0}(r)=\{\{x_{k}\}\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}:\lim_{k\to\infty}r_{k}x_{k}=0\}

equipped with the norm
\|x\|_{r}=\sup_{k}|x_{k}|r_{k}
. If
D_{r}
is the diagonal matrix with
i
-th diagonal entry
r_{i}
then,
D_{r}
is an isometric isomorphism from
c_{0}(r)
to
c_{0}
since
x=\{x_{k}\}\in c_{0}(r)
implies
D_{r}x=\{x_{k}r_{k}\}\in c_{0}
, and
\|x\|_{r}=\|D_{r}x\|_{\infty}.
Hence
\|x\|_{r}
defines a norm on
c_{0}(r)
and
(c_{0}(r),\|x\|_{r})
is a Banach space under this norm. The dual of
c_{0}(r)
is linearly isometric with the weighted sequence space
\ell_{1}(r^{-1})
where
r^{-1}=\{\frac{1}{r_{n}}\}.
Also it must be noted that if
\inf_{k}r_{k}>0
then
c_{0}(r)=c_{0}
and the norms are equivalent. Therefore we are interested in the case
\inf_{k}r_{k}=0.

We record the following lemmas which are useful in this sequel.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
22
]

The matrix
A=(a_{nk})
gives rise to a bounded linear operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(c_{0})
if and only if the following conditions hold,

(i)

the rows of
A
are in
\ell_{1}
and their
\ell_{1}
norms are bounded,

(ii)

the columns of
A
are in
c_{0}
.

The operator norm of
T
is given by the supremum of the
\ell_{1}
norms of the rows.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
6
, Theorem 1]

For two weight vectors
r=\{r_{n}\}
,
s=\{s_{n}\}
and an infinite matrix
A=(a_{nk})
, the matrix
A\in\mathcal{B}(c_{0}(r),c_{0}(s))
if and only if the following conditions hold,

(i)

\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}s_{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|\frac{a_{nk}}{r_{k}}|<\infty,

(ii)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}s_{k}a_{nk}=0\ \forall\ k\in\mathbb{N}.

Then the norm of
A
is given by
\|A\|_{r,s}=\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}s_{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|\frac{a_{nk}}{r_{k}}|
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
12
, p. 59]

The bounded linear operator
T:X\rightarrow Y
has dense range if and only if
T^{*}
is one to one.

3.
Boundedness and compactness of
R_{a}

Let
r=\{r_{n}\}
and
s=\{s_{n}\}
are two bounded, strictly positive weight vectors for the sequence spaces
c_{0}(r)
and
c_{0}(s)
respectively. For any operator
T:c_{0}(r)\to c_{0}(s)
the operator norm is denoted by
\|T\|_{r,s}
and if
T
is defined from
c_{0}(s)
to
c_{0}(s)
then
\|T\|_{r,s}=\|T\|_{s}
. Following theorem is an immediate application of Lemma
2.2
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The Rhaly operator
R_{a}:c_{0}(r)\to c_{0}(s)
is bounded if and only if

\left\{s_{n}a_{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{k}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell_{\infty},

and in this case,
\left\lVert R_{a}\right\rVert_{r,s}=\sup\limits_{n}s_{n}\left|a_{n}\right|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{k}}.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.2
that the Rhaly operator
R_{a}
is a bounded linear operator from
c_{0}(r)
to
c_{0}(s)
if and only if

\sup\limits_{n}s_{n}\left|a_{n}\right|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{k}}<\infty\ \mbox{and}\ \lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}s_{n}=0.

If
\sup\limits_{n}s_{n}\left|a_{n}\right|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{k}}<\infty
then there exists a positive real number
M
such that,

s_{n}\left|a_{n}\right|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{k}}<M\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.1)

Since
r=\{r_{k}\}
is a bounded sequence of strictly positive real numbers, there exists a real number
M_{1}>0
such that
\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{k}}>n\frac{1}{M_{1}}.

Finally from (
3.1
) it follows that

0<s_{n}\left|a_{n}\right|<\frac{MM_{1}}{n}\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},

and consequently
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}s_{n}=0.
Hence
\sup_{n}s_{n}\left|a_{n}\right|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{k}}<\infty
implies
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}s_{n}=0.
Also the expression of
\left\lVert R_{a}\right\rVert_{r,s}
is a direct consequence of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

The Rhaly operator
R_{a}:c_{0}(r)\to c_{0}(s)
is compact if and only if

\left\{s_{n}a_{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{k}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in c_{0}.

Proof.

Let us consider the linear operator
T_{r,s}:\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}
defined as

T_{r,s}(x)=\left\{a_{n}s_{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x_{k}}{r_{k}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.

Then
D_{s}R_{a}=T_{r,s}D_{r}
and consequently
R_{a}:c_{0}(r)\to c_{0}(s)
is a compact map if and only if
T_{r,s}\in\mathcal{K}(c_{0}).

Let
\left\{a_{n}s_{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{k}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in c_{0}
. Define a sequence of operators
\{T_{r,s}^{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
where
T_{r,s}^{(k)}:c_{0}\rightarrow c_{0}
and

T_{r,s}^{(k)}(x)=\left\{a_{1}s_{1}\frac{x_{1}}{r_{1}},a_{2}s_{2}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{r_{1}}+\frac{x_{2}}{r_{2}}\right),\cdots,a_{k}s_{k}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\frac{x_{i}}{r_{i}},0,0,\cdots\right\}.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
the operator
T_{r,s}^{(k)}
is a finite rank operator. Now

\left\lVert(T_{r,s}-T_{r,s}^{(k)})x\right\rVert_{\infty}=\sup\limits_{n>k}\left|a_{n}\right|s_{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}}{r_{i}}\leq\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}\sup\limits_{n>k}\left|a_{n}\right|s_{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{i}}.

Hence

\left\lVert T_{r,s}-T_{r,s}^{(k)}\right\rVert\leq\sup\limits_{n>k}\left|a_{n}\right|s_{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{i}}.

Since
a_{n}s_{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{i}}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, by taking
k\rightarrow\infty
on both side of the above relation we get

\left\lVert T_{r,s}-T_{r,s}^{(k)}\right\rVert\rightarrow 0.

Hence the sequence of operators
\{T_{r,s}^{(k)}\}
converges to
T_{r,s}
under the operator norm, and this implies
T_{r,s}\in\mathcal{K}(c_{0})
.

For the converse part we adopt similar approach as in Proposition 2.2
[
4
]
. Let
R_{a}:c_{0}(r)\rightarrow c_{0}(s)
is a compact operator. This implies
T_{r,s}\in\mathcal{K}(c_{0}).
This also implies
T_{r,s}(B_{c_{0}}[0,1])
is a relatively compact set where
B_{c_{0}}[0,1]
denotes the closed unit ball in
c_{0}.
Therefore using the result of
[
9
, p. 15]
there exists a sequence
y=\{y_{n}\}\in c_{0}
such that

\left|(T_{r,s}(x))_{n}\right|\leq\left|y_{n}\right|\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},\ x\in B_{c_{0}}[0,1].

Since
y\in c_{0},
for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that,

\left|(T_{r,s}(x))_{n}\right|<\epsilon\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n>n_{0}.

Let us consider for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the sequence
\{u^{(n)}\}
with
\{u_{k}^{(n)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in B_{c_{0}}[0,1]
such that for
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\},

u_{k}^{(n)}=1
and
0
otherwise. Then

\left|(T_{r,s}(u^{(n)}))_{n}\right|=s_{n}\left|a_{n}\right|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{k}}<\epsilon\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n>n_{0}.

Hence
\left\{a_{n}s_{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{k}}\right\}_{n}\in c_{0}
and this proves the theorem.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Few important remarks on the above result are mentioned below.

(a)

If
r_{n}=s_{n}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
then both the weighted sequence spaces
c_{0}(r)
and
c_{0}(s)
reduce to the classical sequence space
c_{0}.
In this setting, Theorem
3.1
yields, the Rhaly operator
R_{a}:c_{0}\rightarrow c_{0}
is bounded if and only if
\{na_{n}\}\in\ell_{\infty}.
This result is proved by Leibowitz
[
14
]
in Proposition 3.2.

(b)

Since
|a_{n}|s_{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{s_{k}}\geq|a_{n}|,\ n\in\mathbb{N},
a necessary condition for
R_{a}\in\mathcal{B}(c_{0}(s))
and
R_{a}\in\mathcal{K}(c_{0}(s))
is that
a_{n}\in\ell_{\infty}
and
a_{n}\in c_{0}
respectively.

Let
s=\{s_{n}\}
be a decreasing, strictly positive sequence of real numbers. Then

s_{n}\left|a_{n}\right|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{s_{k}}\leq n\left|a_{n}\right|.

Therefore
R_{a}\in\mathcal{B}(c_{0}(s))
if
\{na_{n}\}\in\ell_{\infty}
and
R_{a}\in\mathcal{K}(c_{0}(s))
if
\{na_{n}\}\in c_{0}.
Also let
\{e_{n}\}
be a sequence in
c_{0}(r)
such that the
n
-th entry is one and all other entries are zero. Then for
n\in\mathbb{N},

\left\lVert R_{a}e_{n}\right\rVert_{s}=\sup\limits_{k\geq n}s_{k}\left|a_{k}\right|\geq a_{n}s_{n}=\left|a_{n}\right|\left\lVert e_{n}\right\rVert_{s}.

This gives the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
s=\{s_{n}\}
be a bounded, decreasing, strictly positive sequence of real numbers. Then
R_{a}\in\mathcal{B}(c_{0}(s))
if
\{na_{n}\}\in\ell_{\infty}
and
R_{a}\in\mathcal{K}(c_{0}(s))
if
\{na_{n}\}\in c_{0}.
Also

\sup\limits_{n}\left|a_{n}\right|\leq\left\lVert R_{a}\right\rVert_{s}\leq\sup\limits_{n}n\left|a_{n}\right|.

The following example implies the above corollary is merely a sufficient condition.

Example 3.5
.

Let for
n\in\mathbb{N},

a_{n}=\frac{1}{\log(n+1)}
and
s_{n}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}.
Then
s_{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{s_{k}}=\frac{2^{n+1}-2}{2^{n}},

n\in\mathbb{N}.
Hence

a_{n}s_{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{s_{k}}=\frac{2^{n+1}-2}{2^{n}\log(n+1)}\to 0\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Here,
\{s_{n}\}
is a bounded strictly decreasing sequence and
\{na_{n}\}
is unbounded but
R_{a}\in\mathcal{K}(c_{0}(s)).

4.
Spectral properties of
R_{a}

From now onwards, let
\{a_{n}\}
is a sequence of positive real numbers such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}na_{n}
exists finitely and equal to
\chi\neq 0
and
S=\{a_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. The following lemma (
[
29
, Lemma 2.7]
,
[
19
, Lemma 7]
) is useful in this sequel.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus S
and
\alpha=\Re(\frac{1}{\lambda}).
Then the following relation holds

\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left|1-\frac{a_{k}}{\lambda}\right|\simeq\frac{1}{n^{\alpha\chi}},

where the notation
a_{n}\simeq b_{n}
means the sequences
\left\{\frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}\right\}
and
\left\{\frac{b_{n}}{a_{n}}\right\}
are bounded and for any
z\in\mathbb{C},

\Re(z)
denotes the real part of
z.

Using the similar concept as
[
19
, Lemma 7]
, we prove a slightly modified result.

Lemma 4.2
.

For a fixed
m\in\mathbb{N},
let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{a_{k}:k=m+1,m+2,\cdots\}
and
\alpha=\Re(\frac{1}{\lambda}).
Then the following relation holds

\prod\limits_{k=m+1}^{n}\left|1-\frac{a_{k}}{\lambda}\right|\simeq\frac{1}{n^{\alpha\chi}}.

Proof.

Let
\frac{1}{\lambda}=\alpha+i\beta
where
\alpha,

\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}na_{n}=\chi
implies
a_{n}\simeq\frac{\chi}{n}.
Imitating the calculations of Lemma 7 in
[
19
]
and using the fact that
e^{x}\geq 1+x
for all
x\in\mathbb{R},
we have the following relation

\prod\limits_{k=m+1}^{n}\left|1-\frac{a_{k}}{\lambda}\right|\leq\mathcal{O}(1)\exp\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{n}\left(-\frac{\chi}{k}\alpha+\frac{(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2})}{2}\frac{\chi^{2}}{k^{2}}\right).

(4.1)

Now we use the following inequality

\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}\geq\int\limits_{m}^{n}\frac{1}{x+1}dx=\log(n+1)-\log(m+1)\geq\log n-\log(m+1).

Therefore from (
4.1
) we deduce that

\prod\limits_{k=m+1}^{n}\left|1-\frac{a_{k}}{\lambda}\right|\leq\frac{\mathcal{O}(1)}{n^{\chi\alpha}}.

Also note that

\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}\leq\int\limits_{m-1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{x+1}dx=\log n-\log m.

Using the above inequality, in a similar way it can be proved that

\prod\limits_{k=m+1}^{n}\left|1-\frac{a_{k}}{\lambda}\right|^{-1}\leq\mathcal{O}(1)n^{\chi\alpha}.

This proves the result.
∎

Next we derive the point spectrum of
R_{a}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
s=\{s_{n}\}
be a bounded, strictly positive sequence such that
R_{a}\in\mathcal{B}(c_{0}(s)).
Then

\sigma_{p}(R_{a},c_{0}(s))=\{\lambda\in S:\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}a_{n}s_{n}n^{{\alpha\chi}}=0\}

where
\alpha=\Re({\frac{1}{\lambda}})=\frac{1}{\lambda}.

Proof.

Let us consider
R_{a}x=\lambda x
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and non-zero
x.
This gives the system of equations

\left.\begin{aligned} a_{1}x_{1}&=&\lambda x_{1}\\
a_{2}(x_{1}+x_{2})&=&\lambda x_{2}\\
&\vdots&\\
a_{k}(x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{k})&=&\lambda x_{k}\\
&\vdots&\end{aligned}\right\}.

(4.2)

If
\lambda\notin S
then from the above equations it follows that
x_{k}=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}.
Hence
\sigma_{p}(R_{a},c_{0}(s))\subseteq S.
. Any element
\lambda
of
S
belongs to
\sigma_{p}(R_{a},c_{0}(s))
if and only if
R_{a}x=\lambda x
holds for some non-zero
x=\{x_{n}\}\in c_{0}(s).
Simplifying
R_{a}x=\lambda x
we get,

x_{n}=\prod\limits_{j=m+1}^{n}\left(\frac{\lambda a_{j-1}^{-1}}{\lambda a_{j}^{-1}-1}\right)x_{m},\ n=m+1,m+2,\cdots,

(4.3)

where
x_{m}
is the first non-zero entry of
\{x_{n}\}.
Now we rearrange the right hand side of the above equation as follows;

\displaystyle x_{n}=\prod\limits_{j=m+1}^{n}\left(\frac{\lambda a_{j-1}^{-1}}{\lambda a_{j}^{-1}-1}\right)x_{m}=\prod\limits_{j=m+1}^{n}\left(\frac{a_{j}a_{j-1}^{-1}}{1-\frac{a_{j}}{\lambda}}\right)x_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod\limits_{j=m+1}^{n}a_{j}a_{j-1}^{-1}}{\prod\limits_{j=m+1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{a_{j}}{\lambda}\right)}x_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{a_{n}}{a_{m}}}{\prod\limits_{j=m+1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{a_{j}}{\lambda}\right)}x_{m}.

From Lemma
4.2
it can be deduced that
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(R_{a},c_{0}(s))
if and only if

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}a_{n}s_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}=0,

where
\alpha=\Re({\frac{1}{\lambda}})=\frac{1}{\lambda}.

∎

Remark 4.4
.

Here we mention few important observations related to the point spectrum.

(i)

Since the weight vector
s=\{s_{n}\}
is a bounded sequence, and
a_{n}\simeq\frac{\chi}{n},
for any
\lambda\in S
satisfies
\lambda>\chi
we have

a_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}\simeq\chi n^{\alpha\chi-1}

where
\alpha=\Re({\frac{1}{\lambda}})=\frac{1}{\lambda}.
This proves that
a_{n}s_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and consequently
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(R_{a},c_{0}(s)).
In this regard we have the following inclusion relation

\{\lambda\in S:\lambda>\chi\}\subseteq\sigma_{p}(R_{a},c_{0}(s)).

(ii)

Let
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(R_{a},c_{0}(s)).
Then for all
\mu\in S
such that
\lambda\leq\mu
the following relation holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

a_{n}s_{n}n^{\frac{\chi}{\mu}}\leq a_{n}s_{n}n^{\frac{\chi}{\lambda}}.

This shows that
\mu\in\sigma_{p}(R_{a},c_{0}(s))
for all
\lambda\leq\mu.

(iii)

If
a_{n}=\frac{1}{n}
and
s_{n}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
then
\chi=1
. In this case
a_{n}s_{n}n^{\frac{\chi}{a_{k}}}
does not converge to zero for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and hence
\sigma_{p}(R_{a},c_{0}(s))=\emptyset.
This reflects the case of the point spectrum of the Cesàro matrix over
c_{0}
space which is obtained by Reade
[
19
, Theorem 1]
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
s=\{s_{n}\}
be a bounded strictly positive sequence such that
R_{a}\in\mathcal{B}(c_{0}(s))
. Then the following statements hold

(i)

0\notin\sigma_{p}(R_{a}^{*},c_{0}(s)^{*}),

(ii)

S\subseteq\sigma_{p}(R_{a}^{*},c_{0}(s)^{*}),

(iii)

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{S}.
Then
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(R_{a}^{*},c_{0}(s)^{*})
if and only if

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}}<\infty,

where
\alpha=\Re(\frac{1}{\lambda}).

Proof.

(i) Let the relation
R_{a}^{*}x=0
holds for some
x.
This implies
\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}a_{k}x_{k}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and eventually we have
x_{n}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
This shows that
0\notin\sigma_{p}(R_{a}^{*},c_{0}(s)^{*}).

(ii) Consider the equation
R_{a}^{*}x=\lambda x
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.
This gives the following system

\left.\begin{aligned} a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+a_{3}x_{3}+a_{4}x_{4}+\cdots&=&\lambda x_{1}\\
a_{2}x_{2}+a_{3}x_{3}+a_{4}x_{4}+\cdots&=&\lambda x_{2}\\
a_{3}x_{3}+a_{4}x_{4}+\cdots&=&\lambda x_{3}\\
&\vdots&\end{aligned}\right\}.

(4.4)

From the above system it follows that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\geq 2

x_{n}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(1-\frac{a_{j}}{\lambda}\right)x_{1}.

(4.5)

If
\lambda=a_{l}
for some
l\in\mathbb{N}
then from the above equation, it follows

x_{l+1}=x_{l+2}=\cdots=0.

Hence
\{x_{k}\}\in\ell_{1}(s^{-1}).
It follows that
S\subseteq\sigma_{p}(R_{a}^{*},c_{0}(s)^{*}).

(iii) Now let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{S}
. Then
R_{a}^{*}x=\lambda x
implies the equation (
4.5
) for
\{x_{n}\}.
Then using Lemma
4.1
we get for
n\in\mathbb{N},

|x_{n}|=\left|\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(1-\frac{a_{j}}{\lambda}\right)x_{1}\right|\simeq\frac{1}{n^{\alpha\chi}}\left|x_{1}\right|,

where
\alpha=\Re(\frac{1}{\lambda}).
Hence
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(R_{a}^{*},c_{0}(s)^{*})
if and only if
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}}<\infty.

∎

Remark 4.6
.

The following facts are used to obtain the next lemma. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and a fixed
\chi>0
the following statements hold

(i)

\left|\lambda-\frac{\chi}{2}\right|<\frac{\chi}{2}\mbox{ if and only if }\ \Re\left({\frac{1}{\lambda}}\right)>\frac{1}{\chi},

(ii)

\left|\lambda-\frac{\chi}{2}\right|=\frac{\chi}{2}\mbox{ if and only if }\ \Re\left({\frac{1}{\lambda}}\right)=\frac{1}{\chi},

(iii)

\left|\lambda-\frac{\chi}{2}\right|>\frac{\chi}{2}\mbox{ if and only if }\ \Re\left({\frac{1}{\lambda}}\right)<\frac{1}{\chi}.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
s=\{s_{n}\}
be a bounded strictly positive sequence such that
R_{a}\in\mathcal{B}(c_{0}(s))
. Then

\sigma_{p}(R_{a}^{*},c_{0}(s)^{*})\subseteq\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\left|\lambda-\frac{\chi}{2}\right|<\frac{\chi}{2}\right\}\cup S.

Proof.

It is already proved that
a_{n}\in\sigma_{p}(R_{a}^{*},c_{0}(s)^{*})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Let
\lambda\notin S
such that
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(R_{a}^{*},c_{0}(s)^{*}).
Then from Theorem
4.5
it follows that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}}<\infty,
where
\alpha=\Re(\frac{1}{\lambda}).
Now

\frac{1}{s_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}}\geq||s||_{\infty}^{-1}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha\chi}}.

holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence

\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(R_{a}^{*},c_{0}(s)^{*})\Rightarrow\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha\chi}}<\infty\Rightarrow\alpha\chi>1,

and consequently
\lambda
satisfies
\left|\lambda-\frac{\chi}{2}\right|<\frac{\chi}{2}.
This proves the lemma.
∎

The next result provides an estimation of the spectrum of the operator
R_{a}.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
s=\{s_{n}\}
be a bounded, decreasing sequence of positive real numbers such that
R_{a}\in\mathcal{B}(c_{0}(s))
, then

\sigma(R_{a},c_{0}(s))\subseteq\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\bar{S}:\left|\lambda-\frac{\chi}{2}\right|\leq\frac{\chi}{2}\right\}\cup\bar{S}.

Proof.

From the inclusion relation
(ii)
in Theorem
4.5
we have,
S\subseteq\sigma_{p}(R_{a}^{*},c_{0}(s)^{*})
. Also it is known that for a Banach space
X
,
\sigma_{p}(T^{*},X^{*})\subseteq\sigma(T,X).
Therefore we have
S\subseteq\sigma(R_{a},c_{0}(s))
with the fact that
\sigma(R_{a},c_{0}(s))
is a closed subset of
\mathbb{C}.
Hence
\overline{S}\subset\sigma(R_{a},c_{0}(s))
. Again for
\lambda\notin\overline{S}
the inverse
(R_{a}-\lambda I)^{-1}
exists and has the matrix representation

(R_{a}-\lambda I)^{-1}=(b_{nk})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{a_{n}-\lambda},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ n=k\\
&\frac{-a_{n}}{\lambda^{2}\prod\limits_{j=k}^{n}(1-\frac{a_{j}}{\lambda})},\ \ \ 1\leq k<n\\
&0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

(4.6)

From Lemma
2.2
it follows that
(R_{a}-\lambda I)^{-1}=(b_{nk})\in\mathcal{B}(c_{0}(s))
if and only if both the conditions
\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}s_{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|b_{nk}|}{s_{k}}<\infty,
and
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}s_{n}|b_{nk}|=0,\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}
are satisfied. Let
\lambda\in\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\bar{S}:\left|\lambda-\frac{\chi}{2}\right|>\frac{\chi}{2}\right\}
. Then we have for
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle s_{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|b_{nk}|}{s_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{|b_{nk}|}{s_{k}}+|b_{nn}|

(4.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{a_{n}}{\left|\lambda^{2}\prod\limits_{j=k}^{n}(1-\frac{a_{j}}{\lambda})\right|s_{k}}+\left|\frac{1}{a_{n}-\lambda}\right|.

Also, since
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n^{a}}{(n+1)^{a}}=1
for all real
a
, Lemma
4.2
yields

\frac{D_{1}}{(n+1)^{\alpha\chi}}\leq\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left|1-\frac{a_{k}}{\lambda}\right|\leq\frac{D_{2}}{(n+1)^{\alpha\chi}},

(4.8)

for some positive constants
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
and Lemma
4.2
and equation (
4.8
) together imply

M_{1}a_{n}\frac{n^{\alpha\chi}}{k^{\alpha\chi}}\leq\frac{a_{n}}{\prod\limits_{j=k}^{n}\left|1-\frac{a_{j}}{\lambda}\right|}\leq M_{2}a_{n}\frac{n^{\alpha\chi}}{k^{\alpha\chi}},

(4.9)

for some positive constants
M_{1}
and
M_{2}.
Therefore using the relation (
4.9
) in the equation (
4.7
) we obtain for
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle s_{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|b_{nk}|}{s_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{2}\frac{s_{n}}{|\lambda|^{2}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{a_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}}{s_{k}k^{\alpha\chi}}+\frac{1}{d_{\lambda}},

where
d_{\lambda}=\mbox{dist }(\lambda,\bar{S}).
Therefore we have

\sup_{n}a_{n}s_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{s_{k}k^{\alpha\chi}}<\infty\Rightarrow\sup\limits_{n}s_{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|b_{nk}|}{s_{k}}<\infty.

As
\lambda\in\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\bar{S}:\left|\lambda-\frac{\chi}{2}\right|>\frac{\chi}{2}\right\}
,
this is equivalent to
\alpha\chi<1.
In this case for
n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle a_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{s_{n}}{s_{k}k^{\alpha\chi}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(na_{n})n^{\alpha\chi-1}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k^{\alpha\chi}}\leq D_{1}n^{\alpha\chi-1}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k^{\alpha\chi}}

(4.10)

for some positive constant
D_{1}
as
\{na_{n}\}
is convergent sequence and
\{s_{n}\}
is decreasing. Also

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k^{\alpha\chi}}\leq\int\limits_{0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{x^{\alpha\chi}}dx=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0+}\int\limits_{\epsilon}^{n-1}\frac{1}{x^{\alpha\chi}}dx=\frac{(n-1)^{1-\alpha\chi}}{1-\alpha\chi}.

The above inequality and (
4.10
) together imply

\sup_{n}a_{n}s_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{s_{k}k^{\alpha\chi}}<\infty.

Also from (
4.9
) we have for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
and large
n

0\leq s_{n}|b_{nk}|=\frac{s_{n}a_{n}}{\left|\lambda^{2}\prod\limits_{j=k}^{n}(1-\frac{a_{j}}{\lambda})\right|}\leq M_{2}a_{n}s_{n}\frac{n^{\alpha\chi}}{|\lambda|^{2}k^{\alpha\chi}}.

Hence
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}s_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}=0\Rightarrow\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}|b_{nk}|=0.

Again

0\leq a_{n}s_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}=(na_{n})s_{n}n^{\alpha\chi-1}\leq D_{2}n^{\alpha\chi-1}

holds for some positive constant
D_{2}
as
\{na_{n}\}
and
\{s_{n}\}
are bounded. This also proves that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}a_{n}s_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}=0.
Hence
(R_{a}-\lambda I)^{-1}
exists and
(R_{a}-\lambda I)^{-1}\in\mathcal{B}(c_{0}(s))
and consequently
\lambda\in\rho(R_{a},c_{0}(s)).
Finally we have

\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\bar{S}:\left|\lambda-\frac{\chi}{2}\right|>\frac{\chi}{2}\right\}\subseteq\rho(R_{a},c_{0}(s)).

This proves the result.

∎

Now we summarize all the result on the spectrum and fine spectrum of the Rhaly operator over the sequence space
c_{0}(s).
Consider the following sets

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\lambda\in S:\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}a_{n}s_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}=0\right\},

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(S\cup\{0\}):\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}n^{\alpha\chi}}<\infty\right\},

where
\alpha=\Re(\frac{1}{\lambda}).
In this setting we have the following result.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\{s_{n}\}
be a bounded and strictly positive sequence such that
R_{a}\in\mathcal{B}(c_{0}(s))
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}na_{n}=\chi\neq 0
then the following statements hold

(i)

\sigma_{p}(R_{a},c_{0}(s))=A_{1},

(ii)

\sigma_{p}(R_{a}^{*},c_{0}(s)^{*})=A_{2}\cup S,

(iii)

\sigma_{r}(R_{a},c_{0}(s))=(A_{2}\cup S)\setminus A_{1}=A_{2}\cup(S\setminus A_{1}).

In addition, if the sequence
\{s_{n}\}
is a decreasing sequence then

(iv)

\sigma(R_{a},c_{0}(s))\subseteq\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\bar{S}:\left|\lambda-\frac{\chi}{2}\right|\leq\frac{\chi}{2}\right\}\cup\bar{S},

(v)

\{0\}\subseteq\sigma_{c}(R_{a},c_{0}(s))\subseteq(\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\bar{S}:\left|\lambda-\frac{\chi}{2}\right|\leq\frac{\chi}{2}\right\}\cup\bar{S})\setminus(A_{2}\cup S).

Proof.

The relations (i) and (ii) are direct consequences of Theorem
4.3
and Theorem
4.5
respectively. Also from lemma
2.3
it follows that
\sigma_{r}(R_{a},c_{0}(s))=\sigma_{p}(R_{a}^{*},c_{0}(s)^{*})\setminus\sigma_{p}(R_{a},c_{0}(s))
which gives the expression for
\sigma_{r}(R_{a},c_{0}(s))
in (iii). The relation (iv) follows from Theorem
4.8
. The first inclusion relation in (v) follows from Theorem
4.3
and Theorem
4.5
(i) and the other inclusion relation in (v) follows from the fact that
\sigma_{p}(R_{a},c_{0}(s)),

\sigma_{r}(R_{a},c_{0}(s)),
and
\sigma_{c}(R_{a},c_{0}(s))
forms a partition of
\sigma(R_{a},c_{0}(s)).

∎

5.
Operator Ideal

The theory of operator ideals holds a distinctive place in functional analysis because of its numerous applications in spectrum theory, Banach space geometry, the theory of eigenvalue distributions, etc. Many researchers have developed classes of operator ideals that result from operators acting over sequence spaces
[
15
,
16
,
23
]
. In this section, we introduce a new class of operator ideal with the help of
s
-number sequences and discussed of its properties. Let
X
,
Y
,
Z
,
X_{0}
and
Y_{0}
are the Banach spaces,
\mathcal{B}
denotes the class of all bounded linear operators between any pair of Banach spaces,
X^{\prime}
denotes the dual of
X
,
x^{\prime}
denotes the continuous linear functional on
X
. The sequences
r=\{r_{n}\}
,
t=\{t_{n}\}
and
u=\{u_{n}\}
are the weight vectors corresponding to sequence spaces
c_{0}(r)
,
c_{0}(t)
and
c_{0}(u)
respectively. Let
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
and
y\in Y
then the mapping
(x^{\prime}\otimes y):X\to Y
is defined by

(x^{\prime}\otimes y)(x)=x^{\prime}(x)y,~x\in X
. Before proceeding, we list certain known definitions and results which are necessary for our results.

Definition 5.1
.

[
18
, p.79]

A map
s:\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
which assigns every operator
\phi\in\mathcal{B}
to a non-negative sequence
\{s_{n}(\phi
)} is called a
s
-number sequence if it satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\|\phi\|=s_{1}(\phi)\geq s_{2}(\phi)\geq\cdots\geq 0
for
\phi\in\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
,

(ii)

s_{m+n-1}(\phi+\psi)\leq s_{m}(\phi)+s_{n}(\psi)
for
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
,

(iii)

s_{n}(\zeta\phi\eta)\leq\|\zeta\|s_{n}(\phi)\|\eta\|
for
\zeta\in\mathcal{B}(Y,Y_{0})
,
\phi\in\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
,
\eta\in\mathcal{B}(X_{0},X)
,

(iv)

If rank
(\phi)<n
, then
s_{n}(\phi)=0
,

(v)

s_{n}(I_{2}:\ell_{2}^{(i)}\to\ell_{2}^{(i)})=1
, where
I_{2}
denotes the identity operator on the
i
-dimensional Hilbert space
\ell_{2}^{(i)}
.

We call
s_{n}(\phi)
the
n
-th
s
-number of the operator
\phi
.

Definition 5.2
.

[
18
, p.25]

For a subset
\mathcal{A}(X,Y)
of
\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
, the class
\mathcal{A}=\cup_{X,Y}\mathcal{A}(X,Y)
is said to be an operator ideal if each component
\mathcal{A}(X,Y)
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

x^{\prime}\otimes y\in\mathcal{A}(X,Y)
for
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
and
y\in Y
,

(ii)

\phi+\psi\in\mathcal{A}(X,Y)
for
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{A}(X,Y)
,

(iii)

\zeta\phi\eta\in\mathcal{A}(X_{0},Y_{0})
for
\eta\in\mathcal{B}(X_{0},X)
,
\phi\in\mathcal{A}(X,Y)
,
\zeta\in\mathcal{B}(Y,Y_{0})
.

Definition 5.3
.

[
18
, p.25]

A function
\Gamma:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
which assigns to every operator
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
a non-negative number
\Gamma(\phi)
is called a quasi-norm on the operator ideal
\mathcal{A}
if it satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\Gamma(x^{\prime}\otimes y)=\|x^{\prime}\|\|y\|
for
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
and
y\in Y
,

(ii)

\Gamma(\phi+\psi)\leq c(\Gamma(\phi)+\Gamma(\psi))
for
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{A}(X,Y)
where
c\geq 1
is a constant,

(iii)

\Gamma(\zeta\phi\eta)\leq\|\zeta\|\Gamma(\phi)\|\eta\|
for
\eta\in\mathcal{B}(X_{0},X),\phi\in\mathcal{A}(X,Y),\zeta\in\mathcal{B}(Y,Y_{0})
.

Definition 5.4
.

[
18
, p.81]

An s-number
s
is called multiplicative if

s_{m+n-1}(\phi\psi)\leq s_{m}(\phi)s_{n}(\psi)~~\text{for}~~\psi\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)~~\text{and}~~\phi\in\mathcal{L}(Y,Z).

Definition 5.5
.

[
18
, p.26]

An operator ideal
\mathcal{A}
is closed if all components
\mathcal{A}(X,Y)
are closed linear subsets of
\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
.

The following lemma is useful in this context.

Lemma 5.6
.

[
18
, p.80]

If
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
then
|s_{i}(\phi)-s_{i}(\psi)|\leq\|\phi-\psi\|,~~i\in\mathbb{N}.

Now we define sequence space
\chi_{c_{0}(r)}
associated to the Rhaly operator
R_{a}
as follows

\chi_{c_{0}(r)}=\{v\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}:R_{a}(v)\in c_{0}(r)\}.

Thus

\chi_{c_{0}(r)}=\left\{v\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}:\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{i}v_{j}\right)r_{i}=0\right\}.

An operator
\phi\in\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
is called s-type
\chi_{c_{0}(r)}
operator if

\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right)r_{i}=0

We denote the class of all s-type
\chi_{c_{0}(r)}
operators by
\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}
i.e.,

\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}=\left\{\phi\in\mathcal{B}:\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right)r_{i}=0\right\}.

If
\phi\in\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
then

\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X,Y)=\left\{\phi\in\mathcal{B}(X,Y):\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right)r_{i}=0\right\}.

Lemma 5.7
.

If
r_{n}\leq t_{n}
for all
n
then
\chi_{c_{0}(t)}^{(s)}\subseteq\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\phi\in\chi_{c_{0}(t)}^{(s)}
then

\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right)t_{i}=0

Now,

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right)r_{i}\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right)t_{i}=0

Thus
\phi\in\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}
.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

The following statements hold,

(i)

\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}
is an operator ideal if
\{a_{n}\}\in c_{0}(r)
,

(ii)

\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}
is a closed operator ideal if
\{na_{n}\}\in c_{0}(r)
.

Proof.

(i)
Let
X
and
Y
are any two Banach spaces.
Let
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
and
y\in Y
. Then
x^{\prime}\otimes y
is a rank one operator so
s_{i}(x^{\prime}\otimes y)=0
for all
i\geq 2
. Consider,

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(x^{\prime}\otimes y)\right)r_{i}=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}s_{1}(x^{\prime}\otimes y)r_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x^{\prime}\otimes y\|\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}r_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Thus,
(x^{\prime}\otimes y)\in\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X,Y).

Suppose
\phi,\psi\in\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X,Y)
. By using properties of
s
-number, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi+\psi)\right)r_{i}=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{2j-1}(\phi+\psi)+\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{2j}(\phi+\psi)\right)\right)r_{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{2j-1}(\phi+\psi)+\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{2j-1}(\phi+\psi)\right)\right)r_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\lim_{l\to\infty}a_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{2j-1}(\phi+\psi)\right)r_{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2a_{i}\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}\left(s_{j}(\phi)+s_{j}(\psi)\right)\right)r_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right)r_{i}+2\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}a_{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\psi)\right)r_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

This implies,
\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi+\psi)\right)r_{i}=0
. Thus,
\phi+\psi\in\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X,Y)
.

Suppose
\zeta\in\mathcal{B}(Y,Y_{0}),\eta\in\mathcal{B}(X_{0},X)
and
\phi\in\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X,Y)
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\zeta\phi\eta)\right)r_{i}=\|\zeta\|\|\eta\|\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right)r_{i}=0.

Thus,
\zeta\phi\eta\in\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X_{0},Y_{0})
. Hence
\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}
is an operator ideal.

(ii) Let
\{\phi_{k}\}
be any sequence in
\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X,Y)
converging to
\phi\in\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
with respect to the operator norm. Given
\epsilon>0
, we can fix
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\|\phi-\phi_{k}\|\leq\epsilon~~~~\text{for}~k\geq k_{0}.

Now,

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right)r_{i}=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi-\phi_{k}+\phi_{k})\right)r_{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{1}(\phi-\phi_{k})\right)r_{i}+\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi_{k})\right)r_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\phi-\phi_{k}\|\lim_{i\to\infty}ia_{i}r_{i}+\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi_{k})\right)r_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Hence,
\phi\in\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X,Y)
as
\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right)r_{i}=0
.
The required result is proved.

∎

Define a mapping
Q^{(s)}:\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
where
\mathbb{R}^{+}
is the set of all positive real numbers, by

Q^{(s)}(\phi)=\sup_{i}\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right|r_{i}~~\text{where $\phi\in\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}$}.

The next result proves that under certain condition
Q^{(s)}
forms a quasi norm on
\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}
.

Theorem 5.9
.

If
\sup_{i}|a_{i}|r_{i}=1
then
the mapping
Q^{(s)}
is a quasi norm on the set
\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}
and the operator ideal
\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}
is complete under the quasi-norm
Q^{(s)}
.

Proof.

Let
X
and
Y
are any two Banach spaces and
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
,
y\in Y
. Now

\displaystyle Q^{(s)}(x^{\prime}\otimes y)=

\displaystyle\sup_{i}\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(x^{\prime}\otimes y)\right|r_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{i}\left|a_{i}s_{1}(x^{\prime}\otimes y)\right|r_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x^{\prime}\otimes y\|\sup_{i}a_{i}r_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x^{\prime}\otimes y\|.

Clearly
\|x^{\prime}\otimes y\|=\|x^{\prime}\|\|y\|
, so
Q^{(s)}(x^{\prime}\otimes y)=\|x^{\prime}\|\|y\|
.

Suppose
\phi,\psi\in\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X,Y)
and consider

\displaystyle Q^{(s)}(\phi+\psi)=

\displaystyle\sup_{i}\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi+\psi)\right|r_{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sup_{i}\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}(s_{j}(\phi)+s_{j}(\psi))\right|r_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sup_{i}\left|a_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right)r_{i}+a_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\psi)\right)r_{i}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sup_{i}\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right|r_{i}+2\sup_{i}\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\psi)\right|r_{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(Q^{(s)}(\phi)+Q^{(s)}(\psi)\right).

Thus,

Q^{(s)}(\phi+\psi)\leq 2\left(Q^{(s)}(\phi)+Q^{(s)}(\psi)\right).

Let
\phi\in\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X,Y)
and
\zeta\in\mathcal{B}(Y,Y_{0})
,
\eta\in\mathcal{B}(X_{0},X)
.

\displaystyle Q^{(s)}(\zeta\phi\eta)=

\displaystyle\sup_{i}\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\zeta\phi\eta)\right|r_{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\zeta\|\|\eta\|\sup_{i}\left|a_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right)\right|r_{i}.

This implies,

Q^{(s)}(\zeta\phi\eta)\leq\|\zeta\|Q^{(s)}(\phi)\|\eta\|.

Hence,
Q^{(s)}
is quasi-norm on operator ideal
\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}
.

Also, suppose
\phi\in\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X,Y)
. We have

\displaystyle Q^{(s)}(\phi)=

\displaystyle\sup_{i}\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right|r_{i}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sup_{i}s_{1}(\phi)a_{i}r_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\phi\|\sup_{i}a_{i}r_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\phi\|.

This implies,

\|\phi\|\leq Q^{(s)}(\phi)\text{~for $\phi\in\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X,Y).$}

(5.1)

∎

Let
\{\phi_{n}\}
be a Cauchy sequence in
\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X,Y)
. Then for
\epsilon>0
there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

Q^{(s)}(\phi_{n}-\phi_{m})<\epsilon~~\text{for all}~~n,m\geq k.

(5.2)

By using equation (
5.1
), we have

\|\phi_{n}-\phi_{m}\|<\epsilon~~\text{for all}~~n,m\geq k.

This implies
\{\phi_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
.
Since
\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
is a Banach space, there exists
\phi\in\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
such that
\phi_{n}\to\phi
as
n\to\infty
. Using Lemma
5.6
, we have

\left|s_{i}(\phi_{n}-\phi_{m})-s_{i}(\phi-\phi_{m})\right|\leq\|\phi_{n}-\phi\|,~~i\in\mathbb{N}.

As
\phi_{n}\to\phi
, taking
n\to\infty
and keeping
m
fixed we have

s_{i}(\phi_{n}-\phi_{m})\to s_{i}(\phi-\phi_{m}),~~i\in\mathbb{N}.

(5.3)

Now, we shall show that
\phi_{n}\to\phi
as
n\to\infty
in
\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X,Y)
. Again, from equation (
5.2
), we have

\sup_{i}\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi_{n}-\phi_{m})\right|r_{i}<\epsilon~~\text{for all}~~m\geq k.

Keeping
m
fixed and letting
n\to\infty
, we obtain on using equation (
5.3
)

\sup_{i}\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi-\phi_{m})\right|r_{i}<\epsilon~~\text{for all}~~n,m\geq k.

(5.4)

which gives
Q^{(s)}(\phi-\phi_{m})<\epsilon
for all
m\geq k
. This implies
\phi_{m}\to\phi
under the quasi norm
Q^{(s)}
. Again,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)=\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{2j-1}(\phi)+\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{2j}(\phi)\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{2j-1}(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{2j-1}(\phi-\phi_{m}+\phi_{m})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi-\phi_{m})+\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi_{m})\right).

Thus,

\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\leq 2\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi-\phi_{m})+\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi_{m})\right).

(5.5)

The above inequality implies that

\displaystyle\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right|r_{i}\leq

\displaystyle 2\left|a_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi-\phi_{m})+\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi_{m})\right)\right|r_{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi-\phi_{m})\right|r_{i}+2\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi_{m})\right|r_{i}.

By using relation (
5.4

\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right|r_{i}\leq 2\epsilon+2\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi_{m})\right|r_{i}~~\forall m\geq k.

Taking limit on both sides, we obtain that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right|r_{i}\leq 2\epsilon+2\lim_{i\to\infty}\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi_{m})\right|r_{i}

Then,

\lim_{i\to\infty}\left|a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\phi)\right|r_{i}\leq 2\epsilon.

Thus,
\phi\in\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}(X,Y)
, and
this implies,
\chi_{c_{0}(r)}^{(s)}
is complete under quasi-norm

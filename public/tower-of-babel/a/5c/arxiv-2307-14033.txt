Abstract
Given a graph G G and assuming that some vertices of G G are infected, the r r -neighbor bootstrap percolation rule makes an uninfected vertex v v infected if v v has at least r r infected neighbors. The r r -percolation number, m ⁡ ( G , r ) m(G,r) , of G G is the minimum cardinality of a set of initially infected vertices in G G such that after continuously performing the r r -neighbor bootstrap percolation rule each vertex of G G eventually becomes infected. In this paper, we consider the 3 3 -bootstrap percolation number of grids with fixed widths. If G G is the cartesian product P 3 □ P m P_{3}\mathbin{\Box}P_{m} of two paths of orders 3 3 and m m , we prove that m ⁡ ( G , 3 ) = 3 2 ​ ( m + 1 ) − 1 m(G,3)=\frac{3}{2}(m+1)-1 , when m m is odd, and m ⁡ ( G , 3 ) = 3 2 ​ m + 1 m(G,3)=\frac{3}{2}m+1 , when m m is even. Moreover if G G is the cartesian product P 5 □ P m P_{5}\mathbin{\Box}P_{m} , we prove that m ⁡ ( G , 3 ) = 2 ​ m + 2 m(G,3)=2m+2 , when m m is odd, and m ⁡ ( G , 3 ) = 2 ​ m + 3 m(G,3)=2m+3 , when m m is even. If G G is the cartesian product P 4 □ P m P_{4}\mathbin{\Box}P_{m} , we prove that m ⁡ ( G , 3 ) m(G,3) takes on one of two possible values, namely m ⁡ ( G , 3 ) = ⌊ 5 ​ ( m + 1 ) 3 ⌋ + 1 m(G,3)=\lfloor\frac{5(m+1)}{3}\rfloor+1 or m ⁡ ( G , 3 ) = ⌊ 5 ​ ( m + 1 ) 3 ⌋ + 2 m(G,3)=\lfloor\frac{5(m+1)}{3}\rfloor+2 .

Our aim in this paper is to study

Claim 11

The following properties hold.

(a)

|S\cap V_{1}|\geq 3
and

(b)

and

in

. Since the set

contains at least one of every two adjacent boundary vertices, we note that

|S\cap V_{1}|\geq 3
. By symmetry,
|S\cap V_{m}|\geq 3
. Thus, Property (a) holds. Property (b) follows from Corollary
3.3
(b).

To prove Property (c), consider the set

for some

where
2\leq i\leq m-1

3.3
(a),
|S\cap\{a_{i},a_{i+1}\}|\geq 1
and
|S\cap\{d_{i},d_{i+1}\}|\geq 1

Q_{1}=G[\{b_{i},b_{i+1},c_{i},c_{i+1}\}]
, then

, and so the subgraphs

is a

. These observations imply that

To prove Property (d), consider the set

|S\cap(V_{2}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{4})|\geq 5
. Hence, we may assume that

contains at most three boundary vertices in
V_{2}\cup V_{3}\cup V_{4}
, for otherwise the desired lower bound holds.

Suppose that
S
contains exactly three boundary vertices in
V_{2}\cup V_{3}\cup V_{4}
. By symmetry, we may assume that
\{a_{2},a_{4},d_{2}\}\subset S
or
\{a_{2},a_{4},d_{3}\}\subset S
. Suppose that
\{a_{2},a_{4},d_{2}\}\subset S
. Let

,

, and so

. At least two vertices in

are needed for this purpose, implying that
|S\cap(V_{2}\cup V_{3}\cup V_{4})|\geq 5
, as desired. Suppose next that
\{a_{2},a_{4},d_{3}\}\subset S
. Let
Q_{5}=G[\{d_{2},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4},d_{4}\}]
,
Q_{6}=G[\{d_{2},c_{2},b_{2},b_{3},a_{3}\}]
, and
Q_{7}=G[\{b_{3},b_{4},c_{3},c_{4}\}]
. Since
Q_{5}
and
Q_{6}
are path joining two boundary vertices of

and since
Q_{7}=C_{4}
, the subgraphs
Q_{5}
,
Q_{6}
and
Q_{7}
are all
3
-forbidden subgraphs of

, and so
S
must contain at least one vertex from each of
Q_{5}
,
Q_{6}
and
Q_{7}

are needed for this purpose, implying once again that

, as desired.

Hence, we may assume that

contains at most two boundary vertices in

contains at least one vertex among every two adjacent boundary vertices, this implies that

|S\cap V_{2}|\geq 1
and

Claim 12

If
m\equiv 0\,({\rm mod\,}\,3)
, then
m(G,3)\geq\lfloor\frac{5(m+1)}{3}\rfloor+1
.

Proof.
Suppose that
m\equiv 0\,({\rm mod\,}\,3)
. By Claim
11
we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}m(G,3)=|S|&=&\displaystyle{|S\cap V_{1}|+|S\cap V_{2}|+|S\cap V_{m}|+\sum_{i=3}^{m-1}|S\cap V_{i}|}\\
&\geq&\displaystyle{3+1+3+\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{m-3}{3}}|S\cap(V_{3i}\cup V_{3i+1}\cup V_{3i+2})|}\\
&\geq&3+1+3+\frac{m-3}{3}\times 5\\
&=&\frac{5}{3}(m+1)+\frac{1}{3}\\
&=&\lfloor\frac{5(m+1)}{3}\rfloor+1,\end{array}

noting that in this case
m\equiv 0\,({\rm mod\,}\,3)
.
(
\Box
)

.

)

Claim 14

m\equiv 2\,({\rm mod\,}\,3)

4
Open problems

As shown in Theorem
2.3
, for

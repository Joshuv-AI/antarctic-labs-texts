Full instability of boundary layers with the Navier boundary condition
Lorenzo Quarisa, José L. Rodrigo

Abstract
We consider the problem of the stability of the Navier-Stokes equations in 𝕋 × ℝ + \mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}_{+} near shear flows which are linearly unstable for the Euler equation. In [ 4 ] , the authors prove an L ∞ L^{\infty} instability result for the no-slip boundary condition which also denies the validity of the Prandtl boundary layer expansion. In this paper, we generalise this result to a Navier slip boundary condition with viscosity dependent slip length: ∂ y u = ν − γ ​ u \partial_{y}u=\nu^{-\gamma}u at y = 0 y=0 , where γ > 1 / 2 \gamma>1/2 . This range includes the physical slip rate γ = 1 \gamma=1 .

1
Introduction

We consider the following problem for the Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Let
\mathbf{u}=(u(t,x,y),v(t,x,y))
be a smooth flow. Then the Navier-Stokes equations with a viscosity-dependent Navier boundary condition and forcing
\mathbf{f}
may be written as

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla p={\nu}\Delta\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{f}&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+};\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+};\\
\partial_{y}u=\nu^{-\gamma}u&y=0;\\
v=0&y=0.\end{cases}

(1.1)

Let
\mathbf{U}_{0}=(U_{0}(y),0)
be a smooth shear flow (we will also refer to its first component
U_{0}
as
shear flow
). We can then consider the linearized Euler equations around
\mathbf{U}_{0}
:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}_{0}+(\mathbf{U}_{0}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla p=0&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+};\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+};\\
v=0&y=0.\end{cases}

(1.2)

The shear flow
\mathbf{U}_{0}
is called
linearly unstable
for the Euler equations when (
1.2
) admits a solution which is exponentially growing in time.

With this boundary condition, the Prandtl expansion, which states that solutions to (
1.1
) may be approximated by the sum of an inviscid solution to the Euler equations and a
boundary layer
term,

\mathbf{u}^{\nu}(t,x,y)\sim\mathbf{u}^{E}(t,x,y)+\nu^{\max\left\{1/2-\gamma;0\right\}}\mathbf{u}^{b}\left(t,x,\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}}\right),\qquad\text{ as }\nu\to 0^{+},

(1.3)

is expected to display stronger stability properties. For instance, Iftimie and Sueur
[
5
]
proved that the above expansion is valid in energy spaces when
\gamma=0
(viscosity-independent case). More recently in
[
12
]
, the authors exhibit an analogous result for
\gamma\in(0,1/2]
with initial condition in the Gevrey class
(2\gamma)^{-1}
, which is simply the analytic class in the case
\gamma=1/2
.

The goal of this paper is to extend the nonlinear
L^{\infty}
instability result by Grenier and Nguyen
[
4
]
near a shear flow
\mathbf{U}_{0}
which is linearly unstable for the Euler equations, to the case of the Navier boundary condition (
1.1
)
3
-(
1.1
)
4
. This leads to order one instability of the Prandtl boundary layer expansion for all
\gamma\geq 1/2
. This improves and extends the instability result by Paddick
[
7
]
for
\gamma=1/2
, as well as the authors’ previous result from
[
8
]
for
\gamma\geq 1/2
. This range is particularly interesting as it includes the physically relevant case
\gamma=1
, which corresponds to the slip rate predicted by Navier
[
6
]
.

As the boundary condition depends on the viscosity through a power of
\nu
, we must consider a viscosity-dependent family of shear flows

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}U^{\nu}(t,y)=\partial_{yy}U^{\nu}(t,y)&t\geq 0,y\geq 0;\\
\partial_{y}U^{\nu}(t,0)=\nu^{1/2-\gamma}U^{\nu}(t,0)&t\geq 0;\\
U^{\nu}(0,y)=U_{0}(y)&y\geq 0.\end{cases}

(1.4)

Note that
y\mapsto\mathbf{U}^{\nu}(t,y/\sqrt{\nu}):=(U^{\nu}(t,y/\sqrt{\nu}),0)
then satisfies the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) with
\mathbf{f}=0
.

We will assume the following conditions on the linearly unstable shear flow
U_{0}
. It must be smooth, and for some
\eta_{\infty}>0
it must satisfy:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|U_{0}^{(k)}(y)|\leq C_{k}e^{-\eta_{\infty}y}&k\geq 1;\\
U_{0}^{(k)}(0)=0&k\geq 0;\\
\sup_{y>0}\frac{y^{k}}{(1+y)^{k}}|U_{0}^{(k)}(y)|\leq C^{k+1}k!&k\geq 0,\text{for some }C>0.\end{cases}

(1.5)

The second condition (
1.5
)
2
is required in order for the map
\nu\mapsto U^{\nu}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)-U_{0}(y)
to be continuous in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for
t>0
, and corresponds to satisfying the compatibility conditions at all orders at
(t,y)=(0,0)
. It is stronger than the assumption needed in the no-slip case, due to the dependence on the viscosity of the boundary condition (see the Appendix). The third condition (
1.5
)
3
replaces analyticity, thanks to the weight
y^{k}(1+y)^{-k}
which allows the flow to be non-analytic at the origin.

This is our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\gamma>1/2
. For
\nu>0
, let
\mathbf{U}^{\nu}=(U^{\nu},0)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be a family of shear flows constructed in (
1.4
) and with
U_{0}
linearly unstable for Euler and satisfying (
1.5
). Then for any
s\geq 0,N_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
there exists a family of solutions
\mathbf{u}^{\nu}=(u^{\nu},v^{\nu})
to (
1.1
), with a forcing
\mathbf{f}^{\nu}
and a
constant
\sigma>0
and times
\tilde{T}^{\nu}\searrow 0
such that for all
\nu>0
,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}^{\nu}(0,x,y)-\mathbf{U}_{0}\left(y/\sqrt{\nu}\right)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\nu^{N_{0}};

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{f}^{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{\nu}];H^{s})}\leq\nu^{N_{0}};

(1.7)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}^{\nu}(\tilde{T}^{\nu},x,y)-\mathbf{U}^{\nu}(\tilde{T}^{\nu},y/\sqrt{\nu})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq\sigma.

(1.8)

The result only denies the validity of the Prandtl expansion (
1.3
) for
\gamma>1/2
, so there is no contradiction with
[
5
]
. It is an improvement to the main result from
[
8
]
, where the instability in (
1.8
) was proven with an additional factor of
\nu^{\vartheta}
, for some
0\leq\vartheta\leq 1/4
depending on the value of
\gamma
.

The proof of the result follows the same scheme as
[
4
]
. We start by applying the isotropic rescaling

(t,x,y)\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}(t,x,y),

(1.9)

where we keep the notation from the previous variables. As a result, (
1.1
) is transformed into (replacing
\sqrt{\nu}\mathbf{f}
with
\mathbf{f}
for simplicity of notation):

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla p={\sqrt{\nu}}\Delta\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{f}&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+};\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+};\\
\partial_{y}u=\nu^{1/2-\gamma}u&y=0;\\
v=0&y=0.\end{cases}

(1.10)

We construct the solutions
\mathbf{u}^{\nu}
to (
1.10
) as a sum in the form

\mathbf{u}^{\nu}(t,x,y)=\mathbf{U}^{\nu}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)+\sum_{n\geq 1}\nu^{nN_{s}}e^{n\imaginary c_{1}t}\mathbf{u}^{n}(t,x,y),\qquad N_{s}=N_{0}+\max\left\{0;3s/4-7/8\right\}.

(1.11)

This construction implies that at time
t=0
, in the original coordinates we have
\|\mathbf{u}^{\nu}(0,x,y)-\mathbf{U}_{0}(y/\sqrt{\nu})\|_{H^{s}}\sim\nu^{N_{0}}
(see Section
4
for the details, including the derivation of
N_{s}
). It follows the same principles of the construction used in
[
8
]
and originally conceived in
[
1
]
. In these papers, one truncates the series in (
1.11
), obtaining an approximate solution
\mathbf{u}^{\textup{app}}
, considering the remaining terms as a forcing
\mathbf{R}^{\textup{app}}.
The forcing satisfies

\|\mathbf{R}^{\textup{app}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\nu^{\vartheta\left(1+\frac{M+1}{2^{\Lambda}N_{0}}\right)},

(1.12)

for
t\leq\tilde{T}^{\nu}
and for some
\vartheta\in[0,1/4]
depending only on
\gamma
(see
[
8
]
for the exact definition). Finally, using Gronwall’s lemma one can show that there exists an exact solution
\mathbf{u}^{\nu}
with
\|\mathbf{u}^{\nu}(t)-\mathbf{u}^{\textup{app}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq\nu^{\vartheta}
for all
t\leq\tilde{T}^{\nu}
, which produces the instability. Whilst the instability produced by
Theorem 1.1
acquires some forcings
\mathbf{f}^{\nu}
, their scale is much smaller than (
1.12
), as expressed by (
1.7
). In particular, the forcings (
1.12
) do not even vanish as
\nu\to 0^{+}
at the time where the instability reaches order one. As a consequence, the result of this chapter is indeed stronger than
Theorem 1.1
, despite the presence of forcing terms.

To achieve the improved instability result, instead of truncating the series and then bounding the difference with an exact solution using energy estimates, which causes a loss of instability order, we prove the convergence of the series by utilizing
generator functions
. Generator functions, which were introduced in
[
4
]
, act as uniform space-time norms encompassing derivatives of all orders. In order to preserve the required boundary conditions, the terms
\mathbf{u}^{n}
are obtained by progressively solving
Orr-Sommerfeld equations
. These arise from
the linearized Navier-Stokes equations around
\mathbf{U}_{0}
:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}_{0}+(\mathbf{U}_{0}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla p=\sqrt{\nu}\Delta\mathbf{u}&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+};\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+};\\
\partial_{y}u=\nu^{1/2-\gamma}u&y=0;\\
v=0&y=0;\end{cases}

(1.13)

when we assume that the solution is of the form

(u,v)(t,x,y)=\nabla^{\perp}\left(\phi(y)e^{i\alpha(x-ct)}\right),

(1.14)

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
represents the (spatial) frequency, and
c\in\mathbb{C}
represents the time dependence (which will be a growth if
\imaginary c>0
).
In particular the first term,
\mathbf{u}^{1}
, is obtained from a non-trivial solution of the Orr-Sommerfeld equation without forcing,

\begin{cases}(U_{0}-c)(\partial_{yy}-\alpha^{2})\phi-U_{0}^{\prime\prime}\phi-\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{i\alpha}(\partial_{yy}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\phi=0;\\
\phi(0)=0;\\
\phi^{\prime}(0)=\nu^{\gamma-1/2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(0).\end{cases}

(1.15)

The existence of such a solution for certain values of
c
with
\imaginary c>0
near the so-called Rayleigh eigenvalues, is rigorously proven in our previous paper
[
8
]
. In the aforementioned paper, we also posed the question of whether an Orr-Sommerfeld eigenmode, namely a non-trivial solution to (
1.15
), belongs to the image. We derived an integral criterion for this property to hold, but were unable to reach a conclusion. As a result, we are going to assume that any Orr-Sommerfeld eigenmode does not belong to the image, in line with
[
2
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the necessary background on the Rayleigh and Orr-Sommerfeld equation with the Navier boundary condition, which was developed in
[
9
]
. We also define the pseudo-inverse for the Orr-Sommerfeld operator together with the appropriate boundary condition, extending the theory built in
[
4
]
. In Section
3
we recall the definition of generator functions from
[
4
]
and prove that the pseudo-inverse operator is bounded with respect to them. Finally, in Section
4
we construct the unstable family of solutions
\mathbf{u}^{\nu}
to (
1.1
) starting from an unstable solution to the Orr-Sommerfeld equation and prove our main result
Theorem 1.1
.

2
The Orr-Sommerfeld equation: Green function and eigenvalues

In this Section, we recall a few results from our previous paper
[
9
]
on the adjoint Orr-Sommerfeld equation. Whilst most of these results were only stated for the adjoint Orr-Sommerfeld operator, the treatment of the original operator is similar, and we can apply the same techniques. Furthermore, in
[
9
]
we required the adjoint operator to deduce some results which will be applied in this paper. We remind that in this paper the scaling of the viscosity is different due to the application of the isotropic rescaling (
1.9
). As a result, in the momentum equation the viscosity appears as
\sqrt{\nu}
rather than
\nu
, and in the boundary condition
\nu^{\gamma}
is transformed into
\nu^{\gamma-1/2}
(compare (
1.1
) to (
1.10
)).

For each frequency
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
, we introduce the following operators acting on one-variable functions with sufficient regularity:

\nabla_{\alpha}:=(\partial_{y},i\alpha),\qquad\Delta_{\alpha}:=\partial_{yy}-\alpha^{2}=\nabla_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{\alpha}.

(2.1)

Let
c\in\mathbb{C}
with
\imaginary c>0
, and let
U_{0}
be a smooth shear flow on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying the decay assumption (
1.5
)
1
.
Consider the Rayleigh equation
[
10
]

\begin{cases}\operatorname{Ray}_{c}\phi:=(U_{0}-c)\Delta_{\alpha}\phi-U_{0}^{\prime\prime}\phi=0;\\
\phi(0)=0;\end{cases}

(2.2)

on the domain

\mathcal{D}=\left\{\phi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{C}):\phi(0)=0\right\},

though
\operatorname{Ray}_{c}\phi
makes sense for any
\phi
with sufficient regularity. The Rayleigh equation is of course obtained from the linearized Euler equations (
1.2
) by applying the ansatz (
1.14
), so that
\alpha
represents the spatial frequency and
c
the time dependence.

For each value of
\alpha
and
c
, the equation (
2.2
)
1
admits two
fundamental solutions

\phi_{c}^{-}
and
\phi_{c}^{+}
, with

\partial_{y}^{j}\phi_{c}^{\pm}(y)\sim(\pm|\alpha|)^{j}e^{\pm|\alpha|y},\qquad y\to+\infty.

If we linearize the Navier-Stokes equations instead of Euler we obtain the Orr-Sommerfeld equation
[
11
]
coupled with the viscosity-dependent Navier boundary condition, which is given as

\begin{cases}\operatorname{Orr}_{c,\nu}\phi:=(U_{0}-c)\Delta_{\alpha}\phi-U_{0}^{\prime\prime}\phi-\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{i\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}^{2}\phi=0;\\
\phi(0)=0;\\
\phi^{\prime}(0)=\nu^{\gamma-1/2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(0).\end{cases}

(2.3)

We can define the above operator on the domain

\mathcal{D}_{\nu}:=\left\{\phi\in H^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{C}):\phi^{\prime}(0)=0,\;\phi^{\prime}(0)=\nu^{\gamma-1/2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)\right\},

(2.4)

though of course
\operatorname{Orr}_{c,\nu}\phi
makes sense for any function with sufficient regularity. The Orr-Sommerfeld equation is obtained from the linearized Navier-Stokes equations when we apply the ansatz (
1.14
).
To establish the necessary properties of the image of the Orr-Sommerfeld operator, it is useful to consider its adjoint, obtained through integration by parts:

\operatorname{Orr}^{*}_{c,\nu}\phi=(U_{0}-\bar{c})\Delta_{\alpha}\phi+2U_{0}^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}+\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{i\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}^{2}\phi,

(2.5)

whose domain in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
contains
\mathcal{D}_{\nu}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For each
N\geq 1
there exist four linearly independent approximate solutions of the Orr-Sommerfeld equation
\phi^{s,\pm}_{N}
and
\phi^{f,\pm}_{N}
such that

\partial_{y}^{j}\phi^{s,\pm}_{N}(y)\sim\partial_{y}^{j}\phi_{c}^{\pm}(y)+O(\sqrt{\nu}),\qquad\partial_{y}^{j}\phi^{f,\pm}_{N}(y)\sim\partial_{y}^{j}e^{\pm\int\mu\,\mathrm{d}y}+O(\sqrt{\nu}),\qquad\nu\to 0^{+},\;j\in\mathbb{N},

(2.6)

where

\mu=\mu(\alpha,c,\nu,y)=|\alpha|^{1/2}\nu^{-1/4}\sqrt{\alpha\sqrt{\nu}+i(U_{0}(y)-c)},

(2.7)

satisfying

|\operatorname{Orr}_{c,\nu}\phi^{k,\pm}_{N}(y)|\leq D_{N}\nu^{N+1}|\phi^{k,\pm}_{N}(y)|,\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}_{+},k=s,f.

Remark 2.2
.

1.

Unfortunately, the constant
D_{N}
may grow extremely quickly as
N\to\infty
, as it depends on higher order derivatives of
U_{0}
,
so we have been unable to obtain the convergence to exact solutions as
N\to\infty
.

2.

The labels
s
and
f
for the approximate solutions constructed above refer to the
slow
or
fast
respectively behavior at infinity. Indeed when
\nu
is small,
\mu
will become much larger than
\alpha
.

The same result of Proposition
2.1
holds true for the adjoint Orr-Sommerfeld equation, except
\mu
is replaced by its complex conjugate

\mu^{*}=|\alpha|^{1/2}\nu^{-1/4}\sqrt{\alpha\sqrt{\nu}-i(U_{0}(y)-\bar{c})}.

The resulting approximate solutions are denoted as
\phi_{N}^{*,k,\pm}
,
k=s,f
, and they satisfy the same estimates. In fact, to first order approximation as
\nu\to 0
, we have

\phi^{*,s,\pm}_{N}=\frac{\bar{\phi}_{N}^{s,\pm}}{U_{0}-\bar{c}},\qquad\phi_{N}^{*,f,\pm}=\overline{\phi^{f,\pm}_{N}}.

By adapting the procedure used in our previous paper
[
9
]
for the adjoint Orr-Sommerfeld operator, which is inspired from the work of Grenier and Nguyen
[
2
]
, we can construct an approximate interior Green function
G_{c,\nu}^{I}
for the Orr-Sommerfeld equation in the form

G_{c,\nu,N}^{I}(x,y)=\begin{cases}a^{s,+}(x)\phi^{s,+}_{N}(y)+a^{f,+}(x)\phi^{f,+}_{N}(y)&y<x\\
a^{s,-}(x)\phi^{s,-}_{N}(y)+a^{f,-}(x)\phi^{f,-}_{N}(y)&y>x.\end{cases}

As long as we do not require any boundary conditions for
G_{c,\nu}^{I}
, we can always choose the coefficients
a^{s,\pm},a^{f,\pm}

so that

\operatorname{Orr}_{c,\nu}\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{c,\nu,N}^{I}(x,y)\psi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\psi(y)+O(D_{N}\nu^{N}),

(2.8)

for all
\psi\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and such that the above integral converges for all
y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Finally, we can correct
G_{c,\nu,N}^{I}
to an
exact
interior Green function
G_{c,\nu}^{I}
, by setting

G_{c,\nu}^{I}(x,y):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\delta_{x=y}(x,y)\star-\operatorname{Orr}_{c,\nu}G_{c,\nu,N}^{I}(x,y)\star\right)^{n}G_{c,\nu,N}^{I}(x,y),

where the above series converges in
L^{\infty}
with its first three derivatives in
y
as long as
\nu
is small enough. In particular, we obtain that
G_{c,\nu}^{I}
is of class
C^{2}
and with a locally bounded third derivative in
y
. Here,
exact
means that
G_{c,\nu}^{I}
satisfies (
2.8
) without the
O(D_{N}\nu^{N})
term or any other remainder.

Furthermore, one can use
G_{c,\nu}^{I}
to construct four
exact
fundamental solutions
\phi^{s,\pm}_{c,\nu},\phi^{f,\pm}_{c,\nu}
for the Orr-Sommerfeld equation, such that

\phi^{k,\pm}_{c,\nu}=\phi^{k,\pm}_{N}+O(D_{N}\nu^{N}),\qquad\text{ as }\nu\to 0^{+},\quad k=s,f.

Definition 2.3
.

Fix
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
.
A pair
(c,\nu)
is called an
eigenvalue
for the Orr-Sommerfeld operator
\operatorname{Orr}_{c,\nu}
if and only if there exists
0\neq\phi_{c,\nu}\in\ker\operatorname{Orr}_{c,\nu}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\nu}
. In this case,
\phi_{c,\nu}
is called an
eigenmode
.

Let us introduce the quantities

\mathcal{O}_{\gamma,c_{0}}(\nu):=\lim_{c\to c_{0}}\frac{\partial_{y}\phi^{s,-}_{c,\nu}(0)-\nu^{\gamma-1/2}\partial_{yy}\phi^{s,-}_{c,\nu}(0)}{\partial_{y}\phi^{f,-}_{c,\nu}(0)-\nu^{\gamma-1/2}\partial_{yy}\phi^{f,-}_{c,\nu}(0)},\qquad\mathcal{O}^{*}_{\gamma,c_{0}}(\nu):=\lim_{c\to c_{0}}\frac{\partial_{y}\phi^{*,s,-}_{c,\nu}(0)-\nu^{\gamma-1/2}\partial_{yy}\phi^{*,s,-}_{c,\nu}(0)}{\partial_{y}\phi^{*,f,-}_{c,\nu}(0)-\nu^{\gamma-1/2}\partial_{yy}\phi^{*,f,-}_{c,\nu}(0)}.

In
[
9
]
we have proven that these quantities are well-defined, even if
c_{0}
is a Rayleigh eigenvalue, and continuous in
c
and
\nu\geq 0
.
As
\nu\to 0^{+}
, they behave as follows. From (
2.6
), as well as Lemma 2.10 from
[
9
]
and the definition of
\mu
we know that

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\phi^{s,-}_{c,\nu}(0)

\displaystyle\sim C_{1}\alpha,\quad\partial_{yy}\phi^{s,-}_{c,\nu}(0)\sim C_{2}(1+\alpha^{2}),

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\phi^{f,-}_{c,\nu}(0)

\displaystyle\sim-|\alpha|^{1/2}\nu^{-1/4}\sqrt{-ic},\quad\partial_{yy}\phi^{f,-}_{c,\nu}(0)\sim|\alpha|\nu^{-1/2}\sqrt{-ic},

for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
, with
C_{1}\neq 0
.
As a result,

\mathcal{O}_{\gamma,c_{0}}(\nu)\sim|\alpha|^{1/2}\nu^{1/4}\frac{C_{1}-C_{2}\alpha\nu^{\gamma-1/2}}{\sqrt{-ic}(-1-|\alpha|\nu^{\gamma-3/4})}\;\text{ as }\nu\to 0^{+}.

(2.9)

The same estimate holds of course for
\mathcal{O}_{\gamma,c_{0}}^{*}(\nu)
, except with different values of the constants
C_{1},C_{2}
and
\sqrt{-ic}
being replaced by
\sqrt{i\bar{c}}.

Now recall the following result, proven by the authors in
[
9
]
. This will be a key ingredient in constructing the instability from the main result.

Proposition 2.4
.

A pair
(c,\nu)
is an eigenvalue for the Orr-Sommerfeld operator if and only if it is an eigenvalue for the adjoint Orr-Sommerfeld operator. Moreover, if
c_{0}
is a Rayleigh eigenvalue then there exists a
\kappa>0
such that for
\nu>0
small enough there exist
\kappa
Orr-Sommerfeld eigenvalues
(c_{\nu}^{1},\nu),\dots,(c_{\nu}^{\kappa},\nu)
(counted with their multiplicity) with

|c_{\nu}^{j}-c_{0}|^{\kappa}\sim C|\mathcal{O}_{\gamma,c_{0}}(\nu)|,\qquad j=1,\dots,\kappa,\qquad\nu\to 0^{+},

(2.10)

for some
C>0
. In particular, if
\phi_{c_{\nu},\nu}
denotes one such eigenmode, then it is of the form

\phi_{c_{\nu},\nu}\sim\phi_{c_{\nu},\nu}^{s,-}+C\mathcal{O}_{\gamma,c_{0}}(\nu)\phi^{*,f,-}_{c_{\nu},\nu}\sim\phi_{c_{\nu}}^{-}+O(\mathcal{O}_{\gamma,c_{0}}(\nu))\sim\phi_{c_{0}}^{-}+O(\mathcal{O}_{\gamma,c_{0}}(\nu)),

(2.11)

whereas for the adjoint eigenmode
\phi^{*}_{c,\nu}
we have

\phi^{*}_{c_{\nu},\nu}\sim\phi_{c_{\nu},\nu}^{*,s,-}+C\mathcal{O}_{\gamma,c_{0}}(\nu)\phi^{*,f,-}_{c_{\nu},\nu}\sim\frac{\bar{\phi}_{c_{\nu}}^{-}}{U_{0}-\bar{c}_{\nu}}+O(\mathcal{O}_{\gamma,c_{0}}(\nu))\sim\frac{\bar{\phi}_{c_{0}}^{-}}{U_{0}-\bar{c}_{0}}+O(\mathcal{O}_{\gamma,c_{0}}(\nu)),

(2.12)

as
\nu\to 0^{+}
.

Let
(c,\nu)
be an Orr-Sommerfeld eigenvalue, and let
\phi_{c,\nu}
and
\phi^{*}_{c,\nu}
be an Orr-Sommerfeld eigenmode and adjoint Orr-Sommerfeld eigenmode respectively. We know that the subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R_{+}})
generated by
\phi^{*}_{c,\nu}
is
\left\langle\phi^{*}_{c,\nu}\right\rangle=\ker\operatorname{Orr}^{*}_{c,\nu}\subset\imaginary\operatorname{Orr}_{c,\nu}^{\perp}
. Therefore
\imaginary\operatorname{Orr}_{c,\nu}
has at least codimension one in

, and hence the Orr-Sommerfeld operator is not surjective. Moreover, we also have

\phi^{*}_{c,\nu}\notin(\ker\operatorname{Orr}^{*}_{c,\nu})^{\perp}\implies\phi^{*}_{c,\nu}\notin\imaginary\operatorname{Orr}_{c,\nu}.

In other words, an adjoint Orr-Sommerfeld eigenmode cannot belong to the image of the Orr-Sommerfeld operator. Unfortunately, this is not enough to conclude the same result for an Orr-Sommerfeld eigenmode.

3
Generator functions

To construct the instability, we will carry out our analysis using the so-called generator functions. We will use the same generator functions which were employed to construct the instability for the no-slip case in
[
2
]
. We summarize the construction here, focusing on some aspects which are of particular relevance to this paper.

For a function
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\delta\geq 0
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
, define the norm

\|g\|_{\ell,\delta}:=\begin{cases}\sup_{y\geq 0}\varphi(y)^{\ell}|g(y)|\left(\delta^{-1}e^{-y/\delta}+1\right)^{-1}&\delta>0,\\
\sup_{y\geq 0}\varphi(y)^{\ell}|g(y)|&\delta=0;\end{cases}\qquad\varphi(y):=\frac{y}{1+y}.

Now define the weight function

w_{\ell,\delta}(y):=\varphi(y)^{\ell}\left(\delta^{-1}e^{-y/\delta}+1\right)^{-1},\qquad y\geq 0.

Then
w_{\ell,\delta}
is increasing in
y\geq 0
and
w_{\ell,\delta}(0)=0
for all
\ell>0
.

For
\ell=0
, we have
w_{\ell,\delta}=\left(\delta^{-1}e^{-y/\delta}+1\right)^{-1}\in\left[\frac{1}{1+\delta^{-1}},1\right].
Therefore, the
\|\cdot\|_{0,\delta}
norms control the
L^{\infty}
norm. However, the
\|\cdot\|_{\ell,\delta}
norms do not control the
L^{\infty}
norms of any derivatives, since for
\ell\geq 1
,
\varphi(y)
appears with a positive exponent and
\varphi(0)=0
. Another property is that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and for any function
g
,

\|g\|_{\ell+k,\delta}=\|\varphi^{k}w_{\ell,\delta}g\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|w_{\ell,\delta}g\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|g\|_{\ell,\delta}.

(3.1)

As for the role of
\delta
, we have the following relation between the
\|\cdot\|_{\ell,\delta}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\ell,0}
norms:

\frac{1}{1+\delta^{-1}}\|\cdot\|_{\ell,0}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\ell,\delta}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\ell,0}.

(3.2)

In other words, the
\|\cdot\|_{\ell,\delta}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\ell,0}
norms are equivalent for any fixed
\ell
. However, in our application we will interpret
\delta
as

\delta=\gamma_{0}\nu^{1/4},

(3.3)

for some
\gamma_{0}>0
. We will simplify the notation by writing (
3.3
) as
\delta\propto\nu^{1/4}
from now on. A consequence of (
3.3
) is that the mapping between the
\|\cdot\|_{\ell,\delta}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\ell,0}
norms is not uniformly bounded as
\nu\to 0
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
g=g(y):\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function. We define the
one-variable generator function
as

\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}g(z_{2}):=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\|\partial_{y}^{\ell}g\|_{\ell,\delta}\frac{z_{2}^{\ell}}{\ell!}.

(3.4)

Let now
f=f(x,y):\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be a two-variable function, smooth in
x
and analytic in
y>0
. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
f_{\alpha}(y)
be the Fourier transform in
x
of
f
. For
\delta,z_{1},z_{2}\geq 0
we can define the
two-variable generator function
as

\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}f(z_{1},z_{2}):=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}e^{z_{1}|\alpha|}\|\partial_{y}^{\ell}f_{\alpha}\|_{\ell,\delta}\frac{z_{2}^{\ell}}{\ell!},\qquad\forall\delta\geq 0.

(3.5)

Remark 3.2
.

Following the notation of Definition
3.1
:

1.

From (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) it follows that

\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}f(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{z_{1}|\alpha|}\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}\left(z_{2}\mapsto f(z_{1},z_{2})\right).

2.

The maps
z_{1}\mapsto\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}f(z_{1},z_{2})
,
z_{2}\mapsto\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}f(z_{1},z_{2})
and
z_{2}\mapsto\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}g(z_{2})
are all increasing.

3.

From (
3.2
) we deduce that in the two-variable case we have
\operatorname{Gen}_{0}f(z_{1},z_{2})<\infty
if and only if
\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}f(z_{1},z_{2})<\infty
for any (and all)
\delta>0
, and similarly in the one-variable case.

4.

If
\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}f<\infty
for some
\delta\geq 0
, then
f
is analytic in
y
except possibly for
y=0
. In addition, there is a limit on how the norms of derivatives of
f
can grow. For the above series to converge, one needs a constant
C>0
such that

\|\partial_{y}^{\ell}f\|_{\ell,\delta}\leq C^{\ell}\ell!\;.

We refer to
[
4
]
, Section 3 for a more detailed discussion on the general properties of the generator functions.

Remark 3.3
.

An important characteristic of generator functions is their behavior with respect to
boundary layer
-type functions, which results from the
\varphi^{\ell}
factor in the
\|\cdot\|_{\ell,\delta}
norms. Let
\delta>0
, and let
f
be smooth and rapidly decaying. Then

\|\partial_{y}^{\ell}\left(f(y/\delta)\right)\|_{\ell,\delta}\leq\delta^{-\ell}\sup_{y\geq 0}y^{\ell}|(\partial_{y}^{\ell}f)(y/\delta)|=\sup_{y\geq 0}y^{\ell}|f(y)|.

(3.6)

This shows that the generator function
\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}
of

can be bounded by a constant independent from
\delta
. More generally, by (
3.2
), this extends to functions of the type
y\mapsto g(y)+f(y/\delta)
, where
g
and
f
are smooth and rapidly decaying. We call these
boundary layer
functions.

In the following Proposition, we establish that the projection operator defined in (

Let

Proof.

Let

be defined as above. Then the series

converge in the sense of generator functions if
\tau=\nu^{N_{s}}e^{\imaginary c_{0}t}
is smaller than some positive constant independent from
\nu
.

Proof.

We just need to connect the estimates (
4.15
) and (
4.16
). To do this, we apply Corollary
3.7
(for
\alpha\neq 0
) and Proposition
3.8
(for
\alpha=0
) to (
4.11
), recalling that
\nabla_{\alpha}\psi_{\alpha}^{n}=((\psi_{\alpha}^{n})^{\prime},i\psi_{\alpha}^{n})=\mathbf{u}_{\alpha}^{n}
and
\Delta_{\alpha}\psi_{\alpha}^{n}=\omega_{\alpha}^{n}
by definition. For
j=1,\dots,n
, let
G^{j}:=\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{u}^{j},\omega^{j})
following the notation from Lemma
4.3
. Then for
n\geq 2
, by Lemma
4.3
we have

G^{n}\leq C\mathcal{A}(\omega^{n})\leq\frac{C_{0}}{n}\mathcal{A}\left(\sum_{1\leq j\leq n-1}\mathbf{u}^{j}\cdot\nabla\omega^{n-j}\right)\leq\frac{C}{n}\sum_{1\leq j\leq n-1}\left(G^{j}\left(\partial_{z_{1}}+\partial_{z_{2}}\right)G^{n-j}\right),

from which it follows that the series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}G^{n}(z_{1},z_{2})\tau^{n-1}

converges for
\tau,z_{1},z_{2}
small enough.

Now recall that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}(u^{j}\partial_{x}\omega^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Gen}_{0}(u^{j})\partial_{z_{1}}\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}(\omega^{k});

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}(v^{j}\partial_{y}\omega^{k})

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\operatorname{Gen}_{0}(v^{j})+\partial_{z_{1}}\operatorname{Gen}_{0}(u^{j})\right)\partial_{z_{2}}\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}(\omega^{k}).

As a result, for some
C>0
we have

\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}(u^{j}\cdot\nabla\omega^{k})\leq(C+1)G^{j}G^{k}.

Therefore,

\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g^{n}\tau^{n}\right)\leq\tau^{\nu^{-1/4}}\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g^{n}\tau^{n-\nu^{-1/4}}\right)\leq\tau^{\nu^{-1/4}}(C+1)C^{2}\sum_{n>\nu^{-1/4}}^{\infty}\tau^{n-\nu^{-1/4}}\leq C\tau^{\nu^{-1/4}}.

We conclude that the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau^{n}g^{n}
converges in the space of functions with
\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}<\infty
for
z_{1},z_{2}
small enough.
∎

4.1
Time dependent shear flow

We now complete the construction started in the previous subsection by considering the time and viscosity dependence of the shear flows. This generates some higher order terms in
\nu
and
t
.

By Proposition 2.1 and Proposition 2.2 from
[
8
]
,
S\mathbf{w}
is smooth and bounded, uniformly in
\nu
and
t\geq 0
in all spatial
H^{s}
norms. For this, the assumption that
U_{0}^{(k)}(0)=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
is necessary. In the no-slip case, it suffices to assume that
U_{0}^{(2k)}(0)=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
; in both cases, this corresponds to enforcing the compatibility conditions for all orders (see the Appendix).

Recall that the shear flows
U^{\nu}
satisfy the heat equation (
1.4
). For each integer
M>0
we can then write, for
\gamma>1/2,
the following Taylor expansion near
\sqrt{\nu}t=0
:

U^{\nu}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)=U_{0}(y)+\sum_{k=1}^{M}(\nu^{1/2}t)^{k}U_{0}^{(2k)}(y)+R_{U}^{\nu}(t,y),\qquad R_{U}^{\nu}(t,y)=O((\nu^{1/2}t)^{M+1}).

(4.19)

Recall that
U^{\nu}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)
, and hence
R_{U}^{\nu}(t,y)
, is bounded in any
H^{s}
norm, uniformly as
\nu\to 0^{+}
. This follows from Proposition 2.1 and 2.2 in
[
8
]
. We will keep the value of
M>0
from (
4.19
) fixed until the end of the Section.

Consider the vorticity formulation of the Navier-Stokes equations:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\omega+(\mathbf{U}_{0}\cdot\nabla)\omega+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)U_{0}^{\prime}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\omega-\sqrt{\nu}\Delta\omega=\nu^{2^{-\Lambda}}S\omega+g;\\
v=0&y=0;\\
\omega=\nu^{1/2-\gamma}\partial_{y}\omega&y=0;\end{cases}

(4.20)

where

S\omega=-\frac{U^{\nu}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)-U_{0}(y)}{\nu^{2^{-\Lambda}}}\partial_{x}\omega-(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\frac{\partial_{y}U^{\nu}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)-U_{0}^{\prime}(y)}{\nu^{2^{-\Lambda}}}.

We are looking for a solution of the form

\psi(t,x,y)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{p=0}^{2M}\sum_{q=0}^{M}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n\land\nu^{-1/4}}\tau^{n}t^{p}\nu^{q2^{-\Lambda}}e^{i\alpha(x-\real c_{1}t)}\psi_{\alpha}^{n,p,q}(y),

(4.21)

where
\mathbf{u}^{\nu}:=\nabla^{\perp}\psi
is such that
\mathbf{u}^{\nu}(t,x,y)+\mathbf{U}^{\nu}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)
solves the Navier-Stokes equations with the Navier boundary condition (
1.10
), or equivalently
\omega:=\Delta\psi
satisfies (
4.20
). This will be the family of solutions displaying the instability of our main result
Theorem 1.1
.

Substituting (
4.21
) into the Navier-Stokes equations and equating terms with the same order

t^{p}\nu^{q2^{-\Lambda}}e^{n\imaginary c_{0}t}
, we obtain the equations

i\alpha\operatorname{Orr}_{c_{n,\alpha},\nu}(\psi_{\alpha}^{n,p,q})+(p+1)\psi_{\alpha}^{n,p+1,q}=Q^{n,p,q}+L^{n,p,q},

(4.22)

for
n\geq 1,0\leq q\leq M,0\leq p\leq 2M
where

\displaystyle Q^{n,p,q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha^{\prime}|\leq n\land\nu^{-1/4}}\sum_{1\leq j\leq n-1}\sum_{0\leq k\leq p}\sum_{0\leq l\leq q}(\mathbf{u}_{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,k,l}\cdot\nabla_{\alpha^{\prime}})\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{n-j,p-k,q-l};

\displaystyle L^{n,p,q}

\displaystyle=i\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{M}U_{0}^{(2k)}\omega_{\alpha}^{n,p-k,q-2^{\Lambda-1}k}-i\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{M}\psi_{\alpha}^{n,p-k,q-2^{\Lambda-1}k}U_{0}^{(2k+2)},

and
c
is defined as in (
4.12
). However, the terms with powers strictly higher than
M
in
t
or
\nu^{2^{-\Lambda}}
(including the remainder from (
4.19
)) will not appear in any of the equations (
4.22
), and will instead end up in a remainder:

\displaystyle R^{n}_{\alpha}(t,y):

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha^{\prime}|\leq n\land\nu^{1/4}}\sum_{1\leq j\leq n-1}\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}<2M}(\star)+i\alpha R^{\nu}_{U}(t,y)\partial_{x}\omega_{\alpha}-i\alpha\psi_{\alpha}\partial_{yy}R^{\nu}_{U}(t,y),

\displaystyle(\star)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{q_{1}+q_{2}>M}^{q_{1},q_{2}\leq M}{\nu}^{(q_{1}+q_{2})2^{-\Lambda}}t^{p_{1}+p_{2}}(\mathbf{u}_{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,p_{1},q_{1}}\cdot\nabla_{\alpha^{\prime}})\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{n-j,p_{2},q_{2}}&|\alpha|\leq n\land\nu^{-1/4};\\
\displaystyle\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}\leq M}{\nu}^{(q_{1}+q_{2})2^{-\Lambda}}t^{p_{1}+p_{2}}(\mathbf{u}_{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}^{j,p_{1},q_{1}}\cdot\nabla_{\alpha^{\prime}})\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{n-j,p_{2},q_{2}}&\nu^{-1/4}<|\alpha|\leq n\land 2\nu^{-1/4}.\end{cases}

As usual, we define

R^{n}(t,x,y)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n\land 2\nu^{-1/4}}e^{i\alpha(x-\real c_{0}t)}R^{n}_{\alpha}(t,y),\qquad R=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau^{n}R^{n},

(4.23)

so that the forcing
g
from (
4.20
) is given by
g=\Delta R
.

Notice that while we could truncate the sum for
p
and
q
in our ansatz (
4.21
), we cannot do so for
n
. Indeed, while
\tau
can be made arbitrarily small, it cannot be asymptotically smaller than
\nu^{N_{s}}
. On the other hand, taking
M>2^{\Lambda}N_{s}
, we obtain a factor of
\nu^{N_{s}}
in the terms above. As for
p
, we shall prove that if
\psi_{\alpha}^{n,p,q}\neq 0
then
p\leq 2q
. Hence all terms with
p>2M
will vanish. Moreover, if
q_{1}+q_{2}\leq M
, then
p_{1}+p_{2}<2(q_{1}+q_{2})\leq 2M
, so those terms will not appear in the remainder.

Throughout this discussion we assume, by simplicity, that
\alpha_{0}
is the only integer such that
c_{0}
is a Rayleigh eigenmode, and that
c_{n,\alpha}
(as defined in (
4.12
) is not an eigenmode for any
n>1
.

To find all the terms
\psi_{\alpha}^{n,p,q}
, we first claim that the following ansatz is consistent with equation (
4.22
):

\psi_{\alpha}^{n,p,q}=0\qquad\forall p>2q.

To see why this is the correct range, notice that the terms in
Q^{n,p,q}
are products of terms with indexes
(j,k,l)
and
(n-j,p-k,q-l)
, with
1\leq j\leq n-1
. Now supposing these terms satisfy the above claim, we can deduce that
Q^{n,p,q}=0
for
p>2q
. Indeed, the non-zero products must satisfy both
k\leq 2l
and

2q-k<p-k\leq 2(q-l)\iff k>2l,

which is impossible.

As for
L^{n,p,q}
, it contains terms with indexes
(n,p-k,q-2^{\Lambda-1}k)
for
k\geq 1
. Suppose these lower order terms satisfy the claim. Then for
p>2q
, the non-zero terms will satisfy

2q-k<p-k\leq 2(q-2^{\Lambda-1}k)\iff k<0,

which is impossible. In conclusion,
Q^{n,p,q}=L^{n,p,q}=0
in the specified range if the claim is true for lower order terms in at least one between
n
and
q
. This implies that we can prove the claim by induction over
(n,q)
, once we have dealt with the base cases
q=0
and
n=1
.

•

q=0
. In this case,
L^{n,p,0}=0
by construction. Moreover,
Q^{n,p,0}
consists of terms with indexes
(n^{\prime},p^{\prime},0)
where
n^{\prime}<n
. For
n=1
, in particular we have
Q^{1,p,0}=0
. We can then set
\psi_{\alpha_{0}}^{1,1,0}=0
because
\operatorname{Orr}_{c,\nu}\psi_{\alpha_{0}}^{1,0,0}=0
. Suppose now the claim is true for
n^{\prime}\leq n
. Then
Q^{n,p,0}=0
for all
p>0
. Hence the claim is consistent for all
p>0
, recalling that for
n>1
the Orr-Sommerfeld operator is invertible.

•

n=1
. In this case,
Q^{1,p,q}=0
by construction. Moreover,
L^{1,p,q}
consists of terms with indexes
(1,p-k,q-k)
, for
k\geq 1
. The case
q=0
has been treated above, so suppose the claim is true for
q^{\prime}<q
,
q\geq 1
. Let
p\geq 2q
. Then the non-zero terms of
L^{1,p,q}
must satisfy
2q-k\leq p-k\leq 2(q-2^{\Lambda-1}k)
, but this is impossible for
k\geq 1
. Thus
L^{1,p,q}=0
, and the equation is consistent. For
p=2q-1\geq 1
, we get
L^{1,2q-1,q}\neq 0
(choosing
k=1
). If
\alpha\neq\alpha_{0}
, since the Orr-Sommerfeld operator is invertible, the equation can then be subsequently solved for
\psi^{1,p,q}
for all
p\leq 2q-1
. Otherwise if
\alpha=\alpha_{0}
, the Orr-Sommerfeld operator is non-invertible, so we define

\psi^{1,2q-1,q}:=\tilde{\operatorname{Orr}}_{c_{n,\alpha},\nu}^{-1}(L^{1,2q-1,q})

and, if
\phi\mapsto\lambda_{c,\nu}(\phi)
denotes the quantity defined in (
2.22
), then

2\psi^{1,2q,q}:=\lambda_{c,\nu}(L^{1,2q-1,q})\lambda_{c,\nu}(\phi_{c_{1},\nu})^{-1}\phi_{c_{1},\nu},

where
\phi_{c_{1},\nu}
is the Orr-Sommerfeld eigenmode. Since then
\operatorname{Orr}_{c_{n,\alpha},\nu}\psi^{1,2q,q}=0
, this is consistent with
L^{1,2q,q}=0
. The terms for
p<2q-1
may be derived by progressively solving Orr-Sommerfeld equations, using the pseudo-inverse. Proposition
3.4
will allow us to control the size of the solutions each time the pseudo-inverse is applied.

Remark 4.5
.

The case
n=1
above is where we have finally used the assumption that
\phi_{c,\nu}\notin\imaginary\operatorname{Orr}_{c,\nu}
.
It is also where the condition
p>2q
for the ansatz
\psi_{\alpha}^{n,p,q}=0
becomes necessary. If we used
p>q
for the ansatz instead, then we would have to make an exception for the case
\alpha=\pm\alpha_{0}
, since evidently e.g.
\psi_{\alpha_{0}}^{1,2,1}\neq 0
. However, it also follows that
\psi_{2\alpha_{0}}^{2,2,1}\neq 0
, even though
2\alpha_{0}\neq\pm\alpha_{0}
and
p=2>1=q
. The inconsistency caused by this cascade effect to higher order terms can only be avoided with the condition
p>2q
.

Let us now show that all the modes can be actually derived. We have already shown this for
q=0
and
n=1
. We proceed by induction over
(n,q)
. Suppose we have constructed
\psi_{\alpha}^{n^{\prime},p,q^{\prime}}
for all
n^{\prime}\leq n,q^{\prime}\leq q
and for all
p
. We construct
\psi_{\alpha}^{n+1,p,q^{\prime}}
for
q^{\prime}\leq q+1
and
\psi_{\alpha}^{n^{\prime},p,q+1}
for
n^{\prime}\leq n+1
. In the first case, we proceed by induction over
q^{\prime}
. We have already constructed all the
\psi_{\alpha}^{n+1,p,0}
. Suppose we have constructed
\psi_{\alpha}^{n+1,p,q^{\prime}}
for
q^{\prime}<q_{0}
. For
p>2q_{0}
we know that
\psi_{\alpha}^{n+1,p,q_{0}}=0
.
Q^{n+1,p,q_{0}}
and
L^{n+1,p,q_{0}}
contain terms which are lower order in
n
or
q_{0}
respectively, so these all vanish by inductive assumption. Hence the equations in this range reduce to
0=0
. Now consider
p=2q_{0}
. Then
Q^{n,p,q}+L^{n,p,q}\neq 0
and we can define
\psi_{\alpha}^{n,p,q}:=\operatorname{Orr}_{c,\nu}^{-1}(Q^{n,p,q}+L^{n,p,q})
, recalling that

and hence the Orr-Sommerfeld operator is invertible. For
\psi_{\alpha}^{n^{\prime},p,q+1}
the procedure is the same. This completes the induction.

4.1.1
Bounds

We now derive bounds for the generator functions of the solutions. Recall that the sums for
p
and
q
are truncated to
2M
and
M
respectively, where
M
is a fixed quantity. Therefore, the bounds need only be uniform in
n
, not
p
or
q
. We use
C_{p,q}
to indicate a constant that may depend on
p
and
q
but not on
n
. We define

\psi^{n,p,q}(t,x,y):=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\nu^{-1/4}\land n}e^{i\alpha(x-\real c_{1}t)}\psi_{\alpha}^{n,p,q}(y),\quad\mathbf{u}^{n,p,q}:=\nabla^{\perp}\psi^{n,p,q};\quad\omega^{n,p,q}:=\Delta\psi^{n,p,q},

\displaystyle G^{n,p,q}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{u}^{n,p,q},\omega^{n,p,q})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Gen}_{0}(u^{n,p,q})+\operatorname{Gen}_{0}(v^{n,p,q})+\partial_{z_{1}}\operatorname{Gen}_{0}(u^{n,p,q})+\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}(\omega^{n,p,q})+\partial_{z_{1}}\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}(\omega^{n,p,q})+\partial_{z_{2}}\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}(\omega^{n,p,q}).

Notice that, by equation (
4.22
) as well as Remark
3.3
(recall that
\delta\propto\nu^{1/4}
), each
G^{n,p,q}
is continuous in
\nu
, converging to a non-zero quantity as
\nu\to 0^{+}
if
p\leq 2q
.

We wish to prove the following result.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let

G^{n}:=\sum_{q=0}^{M}\sum_{p=0}^{2q}t^{p}\nu^{q2^{-\Lambda}}G^{n,p,q}.

(4.24)

Then the series of positive real numbers

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau^{n-1}G^{n}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau^{n-1}R^{n}

(4.25)

converge for all
\tau<C_{\infty}
, for some
C_{\infty}>0
independent from
\nu
. Furthermore, the value of each sum is continuous in
\nu
.

Define

G_{N}:=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\tau^{n-1}G^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{q=0}^{M}\sum_{p=0}^{2q}\tau^{n-1}t^{p}\nu^{q2^{-\Lambda}}G^{n,p,q}.

Suppose we can prove that there exists a constant
C_{M}
, continuous in
\nu
and such that

\partial_{\tau}G_{N}\leq C_{M}G_{N}\left(\partial_{z_{1}}+\partial_{z_{2}}\right)G_{N}.

(4.26)

From this, proceeding as in
[
4
]
, Section 6.3, we can then deduce that for
\tau
small enough, we have
G_{N}\leq C_{M}
for some
C_{M}>0
independent from
N
, from which we conclude. Let
n\geq 1
. For each
p,q
by (
4.22
), Proposition
3.4
(in the case

) and Proposition

we have, for some constants

,

(4.27)

Notice that for
p=2q
the term containing

disappears. We claim the inequality

G^{n,p,q}\leq\frac{C_{p}}{n}\sum_{p^{\prime}=p}^{2q}\left(\sum_{1\leq j\leq n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{q}\sum_{l=0}^{p^{\prime}}G^{j,k,l}(\partial_{z_{1}}+\partial_{z_{2}})G^{n-j,q-k,p^{\prime}-l}+\sum_{k=1}^{M}C_{U,k}G^{n,p^{\prime}-k,q-2^{\Lambda-1}k}\right).

(4.28)

Proof.

We proceed by reverse induction on
p
, starting from
p=2q
. In the base case
p=2q
, the term
G^{n,p+1,q}
from (
4.27
) disappears, as we have set
\psi_{\alpha}^{n,p,q}=0
for all
\alpha
and for all
p>2q
(see Section
4.1
). Therefore, (
4.28
) holds with
C_{p}=C_{2q}=1
.

Now, suppose (
4.28
) holds for all
p^{\prime}
such that
p<p^{\prime}\leq 2q
, where
p<2q
. Then

\displaystyle nG^{n,p,q}

\displaystyle\leq(p+1)C_{p+1}\sum_{p^{\prime}=p+1}^{2q}\left(\sum_{1\leq j\leq n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{q}\sum_{l=0}^{p^{\prime}}G^{j,k,l}(\partial_{z_{1}}+\partial_{z_{2}})G^{n-j,q-k,p^{\prime}-l}+\sum_{k=1}^{M}C_{U,k}G^{n,p^{\prime}-k,q-2^{\Lambda-1}k}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq j\leq n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{q}\sum_{l=0}^{p}G^{j,k,l}(\partial_{z_{1}}+\partial_{z_{2}})G^{n-j,q-k,p-l}+\sum_{k=1}^{M}C_{U,k}G^{n,p-k,q-2^{\Lambda-1}k}

\displaystyle\leq\left((p+1)C_{p+1}+1\right)\sum_{p^{\prime}=p}^{2q}\left(\sum_{1\leq j\leq n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{q}\sum_{l=0}^{p^{\prime}}G^{j,l,l}(\partial_{z_{1}}+\partial_{z_{2}})G^{n-j,q-k,p^{\prime}-l}+\sum_{k=1}^{M}C_{U,k}G^{n,p^{\prime}-k,q-2^{\Lambda-1}k}\right).

∎

We can now multiply both sides of (
4.28
) by
t^{p}\nu^{q2^{-\Lambda}}
and sum over
p=0
to
p=2q
, and
q=0
to
q=M
. Note that, for any given positive real numbers
a_{p^{\prime},q}
, we have

\sum_{p=0}^{2q}C_{p}\sum_{p^{\prime}=p}^{2q}a_{p^{\prime},q}=\sum_{p^{\prime}=0}^{2q}a_{p^{\prime},q}\sum_{p=0}^{p^{\prime}}C_{p}\leq\left(\sum_{p=0}^{2M}C_{p}\right)\left(\sum_{p=0}^{2q}a_{p,q}\right).

Therefore, letting

, we obtain for all

,

\displaystyle G^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{q=0}^{M}\sum_{p=0}^{2q}\sum_{l=0}^{q}\sum_{k=0}^{p}t^{k}\nu^{l2^{-\Lambda}}G^{j,k,l}t^{p-k}\nu^{(q-l)2^{-\Lambda}}(\partial_{z_{1}}+\partial_{z_{2}})G^{n-j,p-k,q-l}

\displaystyle+C\sum_{q=0}^{M}\sum_{p=0}^{2q}\sum_{k=1}^{M}t^{k}\nu^{k/2}C_{U,k}t^{p-k}\nu^{q2^{-\Lambda}-k/2}G^{n,p-k,q-2^{\Lambda-1}k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}G^{j}(\partial_{z_{1}}+\partial_{z_{2}})G^{n-j}+M\sum_{k=1}^{M}C_{U,k}t^{k}\nu^{2^{-\Lambda}k}\,G^{n}.

For
\nu
small enough the second sum above can be absorbed in the left-hand side, and the proof then carries on as in the time-independent case.

In conclusion, we have that the series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{p,q=0}^{M}\tau^{n}t^{p}\nu^{q2^{-\Lambda}}G^{n,p,q}

converges to some positive real number, and hence the solution defined by (
4.21
) converges in the space of smooth functions with finite generator functions. In particular, if
1/C_{\infty}
is the radius of convergence of the series (
4.25
), then
G^{n}\leq 2C_{\infty}^{n}
. Hence the series (
4.21
) defines a solution
U_{0}
to (
4.20
), leaving an error defined by (
4.23
). We need to prove that the series defining
R
also converges for
\tau
small enough. We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gen}_{\delta}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}R^{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{1\leq j\leq n-1}\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\leq 2M}\sum_{q_{1}+q_{2}>M}^{q_{1},q_{2}\leq M}(\nu^{2^{-\Lambda}(q_{1}+q_{2})}t^{p_{1}+p_{2}})G^{j,p_{1},q_{1}}G^{n-j,p_{2},q_{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=\nu^{-1/4}}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{1\leq j\leq n-1}\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\leq 2M}\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}\leq M}(\nu^{2^{-\Lambda}(q_{1}+q_{2})}t^{p_{1}+p_{2}})G^{j,p_{1},q_{1}}G^{n-j,p_{2},q_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\leq 2M}\sum_{q_{1}+q_{2}>M}^{q_{1},q_{2}\leq M}(\nu^{2^{-\Lambda}(q_{1}+q_{2})}t^{p_{1}+p_{2}})\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\tau^{j}G^{j,p_{1},q_{1}}\sum_{n=j+1}^{N}\tau^{n-j}G^{n-j,p_{2},q_{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\leq 2M}\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}\leq M}(\nu^{2^{-\Lambda}(q_{1}+q_{2})}t^{p_{1}+p_{2}})\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\tau^{j}G^{j,p_{1},q_{1}}\sum_{n>j\land\nu^{-1/4}}^{N}\tau^{n-j}G^{n-j,p_{2},q_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\leq 2M}\sum_{q_{1}+q_{2}>M}^{q_{1},q_{2}\leq M}(\nu^{2^{-\Lambda}(q_{1}+q_{2})}t^{p_{1}+p_{2}})G_{N}^{p_{1},q_{1}}G_{N}^{p_{2},q_{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{p_{1}\leq 2M}\sum_{q_{1}\leq M}\left(\nu^{2^{-\Lambda}(q_{1}+q_{2})}t^{p_{1}+p_{2}}\right)G_{N}^{p_{1},q_{1}}\tau^{\nu^{-1/4}}\sum_{n>\nu^{-1/4}}\sum_{p_{2}\leq 2M}\sum_{q_{2}\leq M}\tau^{n-\nu^{-1/4}}G^{n,p_{2},q_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}\left(\nu^{2^{-\Lambda}}t^{2}\right)^{q_{1}+q_{2}}+C_{M}\tau^{\nu^{-1/4}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C_{M}}\left(\nu^{2^{-\Lambda}}\log\nu^{-2}\right)^{M}+C_{M}\tau^{\nu^{-1/4}}.

Thus for
\tau
small enough the series defining

converges to a smooth function with

. Moreover, for

large enough, the quantity above is smaller than

4.3
Conclusion

Let us return to the original variables in which
Theorem 1.1
is stated, by reverting the isotropic rescaling (
1.9
). With a notation abuse, we still denote with
\mathbf{u}^{\nu}
the approximate solution constructed in Section
4.1
, even if it is now expressed in terms of the original variables. Recall that for all
t\geq 0
, by the normalization condition (
4.2
) we have

\|\mathbf{u}^{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=1
for all
t\geq 0
,
where
\mathbf{u}^{1}
was introduced in (
4.1
).
Thus, at
t=0
, since
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau^{n-1}\mathbf{u}^{n}
converges in
H^{s}
we can approximate

\|\mathbf{u}^{\nu}(0,x,y)-\mathbf{U}_{0}(y/\sqrt{\nu})\|_{H^{s}}\leq\nu^{N_{s}}\|\psi^{1}\|_{H^{s+1}}\leq\nu^{N_{s}+7/8-3s/4}\leq\nu^{N_{0}},

by definition of
N_{s}
. This establishes (
1.6
).

It remains to determine the
L^{\infty}
instability of
\mathbf{u}^{\nu}
at a time
\tilde{T}^{\nu}\to 0^{+}
as
\nu\to 0^{+}
. Let
T_{0}>0
and let
\tilde{T}^{\nu}:=\sqrt{\nu}\left(-N_{s}\frac{\log\nu}{\imaginary c_{0}}-T_{0}\right)
, so that
\tau(\tilde{T}^{\nu})=\nu^{N_{s}}e^{\imaginary c_{0}\tilde{T}^{\nu}/\sqrt{\nu}}=e^{-\imaginary c_{0}T_{0}}
. Since the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau^{n-1}\|{\mathbf{u}}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}
converges for

, we know that
\|\mathbf{u}^{n}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\mathbf{u}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
, for all

. We can choose

large enough so that
\tau^{2}/(1-\tau)\leq 1/(2C)
. Furthermore, for
\nu
small enough, we will have
\tilde{T}^{\nu}>0
.

We then have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}^{\nu}(\tilde{T}^{\nu})-U^{\nu}(\tilde{T}^{\nu})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq\tau(\tilde{T}^{\nu})\|\mathbf{u}^{1}(\tilde{T}^{\nu})\|_{L^{\infty}}-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\tau^{n}(\tilde{T}^{\nu})\|\mathbf{u}^{n}(\tilde{T}^{\nu})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq e^{-\imaginary c_{0}T_{0}}\left(1-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\tau^{n-1}(\tilde{T}^{\nu})\|\mathbf{u}^{n}(\tilde{T}^{\nu})\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-\imaginary c_{0}T_{0}}\left(1-C\frac{\tau^{2}}{1-\tau}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}e^{-\imaginary c_{0}T_{0}}=:\sigma>0.

This completes the proof of

, where in the original variables the instability time is given by

.

Appendix A
Necessity of the assumption on the derivatives of the shear flow at the origin

Let
U_{0}
be the shear flow around which we have constructed the instability as per
Theorem 1.1
. Both in this paper and our previous paper,
[
8
]
, we have made the rather strong assumption that all the derivatives of
U_{0}
vanish at the origin:
U_{0}^{(k)}(0)=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

This assumption is necessary in order to ensure that the terms

{U^{\nu}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)-U_{0}(y)}
, and thus the forcing
S\mathbf{w}
defined in (
4.6
), are bounded in
H^{s}
(and vanish as
\nu\to 0^{+}
) for all
s\geq 0
. It corresponds to enforcing the compatibility condition of all orders for the corresponding heat equation (
1.4
). This is a sufficient and necessary for the solution to be regular up to the boundary, specifically up to the point
(t,y)=(0,0)
. In this case, the boundary condition and initial condition tell us that, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

0=\left.\partial_{t}^{k}\left(\partial_{y}U^{\nu}-\nu^{1/2-\gamma}U^{\nu}\right)\right|_{(0,0)}=\left.\left(\partial_{y}^{2k+1}U^{\nu}-\nu^{1/2-\gamma}\partial_{y}^{2k}U^{\nu}\right)\right|_{(0,0)}=U_{0}^{(2k+1)}(0)-\nu^{1/2-\gamma}U_{0}^{(2k)}(0).

As this must be true for all
\nu>0
, we must require all derivatives of
U_{0}
to vanish at
y=0
. In
[
2
]
and
[
7
]
, the compatibility conditions do not involve the viscosity, so one only needs to require
U_{0}^{(2k)}(0)=0
and
U_{0}^{(2k+1)}(0)=U_{0}^{(2k)}(0)
respectively, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. These weaker assumptions have the advantage that they still admit analytic flows. For instance,
U_{0}(y)=ye^{-y^{2}}
satisfies the no-slip compatibility conditions
U_{0}^{(2k)}(0)=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, as well as the exponential decay property.

Let
\gamma>1/2
, and let
U_{0}
be smooth and satisfying (
1.5
)
1
as well as
U_{0}^{(2k)}(0)=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, but
U_{0}^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
(e.g.
U_{0}(y)=ye^{-y^{2}}
). We can take the odd extension of
U_{0}
to
\mathbb{R}
, which will be smooth over
\mathbb{R}
and which we will keep calling
U_{0}
. The solution
U^{\nu}
to (
1.4
) is given by

U^{\nu}(t,y)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}K(t,y-x)u_{0}^{\nu}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad t\in[0,T],

where

\displaystyle u_{0}^{\nu}(y)

\displaystyle=U_{0}(y)+\chi_{(-\infty,0]}V^{\gamma}(y,\nu),

\displaystyle V^{\nu,\gamma}(y)

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{-y}e^{\nu^{1/2-\gamma}(x+y)}U_{0}^{\prime}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=2\int_{0}^{-y}e^{-\nu^{1/2-\gamma}x}U_{0}^{\prime}(-x-y)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Note that
V^{\nu,\gamma}(y)
decays rapidly as
y\to-\infty
, reflecting the rate of decay of
U_{0}^{\prime}
. For instance, with
U_{0}(y)=ye^{-y^{2}}
and
\gamma=1
we can explicitly compute

V^{\nu,\gamma}(y)=\frac{1}{2\nu}\left(-2e^{-y^{2}}\sqrt{\nu}\left(-1+e^{y(y+\nu^{-1/2})}+2\sqrt{\nu}y\right)+\sqrt{\pi}e^{-\frac{1}{4\nu}+\frac{y}{\nu}}\left(-\textup{Erf}\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\nu}}\right)+\textup{Erf}\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\nu}}+y\right)\right)\right).

In general, for all
k\geq 1
and
y\leq 0
, through integration by parts we obtain

(V^{\nu,\gamma})^{(k)}(y)=2(-1)^{k}\int_{0}^{-y}e^{\nu^{1/2-\gamma}x}U_{0}^{(k+1)}(-x-y)\,\mathrm{d}x+2e^{\nu^{1/2-\gamma}y}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}U_{0}^{(j)}(0)\nu^{(j-k)(\gamma-1/2)}.

(A.1)

As a result, as
\nu\to 0^{+}
we have

\|(V^{\nu,\gamma})^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}(-\infty,0)}\sim C_{U_{0}^{(k+1)}}\nu^{\gamma-1/2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}|U_{0}^{(j)}(0)|\nu^{(j-k+1/2)(\gamma-1/2)},

for some constants
C_{U_{0}^{(j)}}>0
,
j=1,\dots,k+1
which only depend on
U_{0}^{(j)}
.

Therefore, for
k\geq 2
we have

\lim_{\nu\to 0^{+}}\|(V^{\nu,\gamma})^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}(-\infty,0)}=0\iff U_{0}^{(j)}(0)=0\;\forall j=1,\dots,k-1\iff(V^{\nu,\gamma})^{(k)}(0)=2(-1)^{k}U_{0}^{(k)}(0)\;\forall\nu>0.

Moreover, if the above condition does not hold for some
k\geq 2
, then
\lim_{\nu\to 0^{+}}\|(V^{\nu,\gamma})^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}}=\infty.

On the other hand, we always have
\lim_{\nu\to 0^{+}}\|V^{\nu,\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}=0
and
\lim_{\nu\to 0^{+}}\|(V^{\nu,\gamma})^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}=0,
as well as
V^{\nu,\gamma}(0)=0
,
(V^{\nu,\gamma})^{\prime}(0)=2U_{0}^{\prime}(0)
. In our case, since
U_{0}^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
, we conclude that
\lim_{\nu\to 0^{+}}\|(V^{\nu,\gamma})^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}}=\infty
if and only if
k\geq 2
.

Now let
U^{0}
be the solution to the heat equation with no-slip boundary condition and initial condition
U_{0}
. Thanks to the compatibility condition
U_{0}^{(2k)}(0)=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we know that

\lim_{\nu\to 0^{+}}\|U^{0}(t)-U_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s})}=0,\qquad\forall s\geq 0,t\geq 0.

Therefore, in order to study the behavior of
U^{\nu}-U_{0}
as
\nu\to 0^{+}
it is equivalent to consider

\lim_{\nu\to 0^{+}}\|U^{\nu}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)-U^{0}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s})}.

We have

U^{\nu}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)-U^{0}(y,\sqrt{\nu}t)=K(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)\star_{y}(u_{0}^{\nu}(y)-U_{0}(y))=\int_{-\infty}^{0}K(\sqrt{\nu}t,y-x)V^{\nu,\gamma}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Thus, for
k=0
and
k=1
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\nu\to 0^{+}}\|\partial_{y}^{k}U^{\nu}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)-\partial_{y}^{k}U^{0}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}\leq\lim_{\nu\to 0^{+}}\|(V^{\nu,\gamma})^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}}=0.

However, if we take the second derivative, then

\partial_{y}^{2}(U^{\nu}-U^{0})(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}K(\sqrt{\nu}t,y-x)(V^{\nu,\gamma})^{\prime\prime}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x-K(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)(V^{\nu,\gamma})^{\prime}(0).

(A.2)

In the above relation, the first term may be seen as the solution to the heat equation over
\mathbb{R}
with initial condition
(V^{\nu,\gamma})^{\prime\prime}\chi_{(-\infty,0]}.
As
\nu\to 0^{+}
we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}^{2}(U^{\nu}-U^{0})(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}\geq\left|\frac{1}{(8\pi\sqrt{\nu}t)^{1/4}}|U_{0}^{\prime}(0)|-\|(V^{\nu,\gamma})^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}}-o(1)\right|\geq\left|\frac{\nu^{1/8}}{t^{1/4}}-1-o(1)\right|\frac{2}{\nu^{1/4}}.

This shows that the difference between the second derivatives of the slip and the no-slip solutions is unbounded in the energy norm as
\nu\to 0^{+}
for any time
t>0
. Since the latter converges to the second derivative of the initial condition
U_{0}^{\prime\prime}
, we deduce that

\lim_{\nu\to 0^{+}}\|\partial_{y}^{2}U^{\nu}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)-U_{0}^{\prime\prime}(y)\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}=\infty.

More generally, if the compatibility conditions are not satisfied for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then the
(k+1)
-th spatial derivative of
U^{\nu}(t,y)
(and thus of
U^{\nu}(t,y)-U_{0}(y)
) will be discontinuous at
(t,y)=(0,0)
. In fact, the energy of
\partial_{y}U^{\nu}-U_{0}^{\prime}
, which is zero at
t=0
, will shoot up instantly to accomodate for the gap between the boundary and initial conditions. Therefore,
\lim_{\nu\to 0^{+}}\|U^{\nu}(\sqrt{\nu}t,y)-U_{0}(y)\|_{H^{k}}\neq 0
. This shows that our assumption that
U_{0}^{(k)}(0)=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
is necessary if we want to follow the approximate construction used in
[
4
]
. However, it does not imply that it is a necessary assumption for the statement contained in
Theorem 1.1
, as a different strategy for the proof could still eliminate this assumption.

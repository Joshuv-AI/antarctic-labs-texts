Introduction

Everywhere in the paper
p
is a prime number. If
K
is a field
we denote by
K_{sep}
its separable closure and set

\Gamma_{K}=\mathrm{Gal}(K_{sep}/K)
.

0.1.
Motivation

We say that
K
is cdvf if it is a complete discrete
valuation field with finite residue field of characteristic
p
.
Then the structure of the group

\Gamma_{K}
, cf.
[
19
,
30
]
, can be explicitly described.
As a matter of fact, it depends only on
\mathrm{char}K
and
the torsion subgroup of

K^{*}
.
In particular, it is very weak invariant of
K
. Note that
in the case of algebraic number fields
E
the situation is quite
opposite. Here by the Neukirch-Uchida theorem, the functor

E\mapsto\mathrm{Gal}(E_{sep}/E)
is
fully faithful
[
28
]
. In other words,
\Gamma_{E}
is an absolute
invariant of
the field
E
.

For a cdvf
K
, consider the decreasing filtration by normal
ramification subgroups
\{\Gamma_{K}^{(v)}\}_{v\geqslant 0}
in
\Gamma_{K}
,
[
29
]
.
This (ramification) filtration allows us to recover arithmetic invariants
(say, discriminants, differentes, etc)
of subextensions
L
of
K
in
K_{sep}
. The importance of
ramification filtration
has been never doubted because this is
highly non-trivial number theoretic interpretation of the
fundamental geometric concept of ramification.
However, during very long time the structure of ramification
subgroups
\Gamma_{K}^{(v)}
was known just modulo the subgroup of
second commutators of
\Gamma_{K}
. (In other words, we knew only the images of

\Gamma_{K}^{(v)}
in the maximal abelian quotient

\Gamma_{K}^{ab}
of
\Gamma_{K}

given by local class field theory.)
Only in
[
27
]
Mochizuki
proved that in the mixed characteristic case the group

\Gamma_{K}
together with additional structure
given by its ramification filtration behaves
like a Galois group of algebraic number field. In other words, if

K
is a cdvf of characteristic 0 then
the functor

K\mapsto(\Gamma_{K},\{\Gamma_{K}^{(v)}\}_{v\geqslant 0})
is fully faithful.
This gave the
first formal confirmation of the importance of ramification theory in the
number theoretic setting.
As a result, the ramification filtration should be not anymore
treated just as
a technical concept,
it is a strict invariant of
K
and, as such,
requires very careful and
detailed study. Notice that Mochizuki’s proof is a spectacular application of the theory of

p
-adic representations; at the same time his proof is highly non-efficient.

It is well-known that the group structure of

\Gamma_{K}
can be easily recovered
from its maximal
pro-
p
-quotient
\Gamma_{K}(p)=\mathrm{Gal}(K(p)/K)
, where

K(p)
is the maximal
p
-extension of
K
in
K_{sep}
, cf.
[
19
]
.
This group is provided with induced ramification filtration

\{\Gamma_{K}(p)^{(v)}\}_{v\geqslant 0}
.
In

[
1
,
2
,
3
]
the author
obtained first explicit description (at substantially non-abelian level)
of the subgroups
\Gamma_{K}(p)^{(v)}
modulo the subgroup

C_{p}(\Gamma_{K}(p))

of commutators of order
\geqslant p
in
\Gamma_{K}(p)
.
This approach systematically used the equivalence of
the categories of
p
-groups and
Lie
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebras with nilpotent class
<p
. It is based on
new techniques which allowed us
to work with ramification properties in terms of the appropriate Lie algebras. These results already found the following applications.

0.1.1.
Mixed characteristic case

In
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
we used the Fontaine-Wintenberger
field-of-norms functor
[
34
]

to extend the approach from
[
1
,
2
,
3
]

to the case of cdvf’s
K
of mixed characteristic
under the assumption that
K
contains non-trivial

p
-th roots of unity. In particular, suppose that

K
contains a primitive
p^{M}
-th root of unity,
M\geqslant 1
, and let

K(s,M)
be the maximal
p
-extension of
K
in
K(p)
with the
Galois group of period
p^{M}
and nilpotent class
s<p
. Then we obtained explicit value for the number
v(s,M)
(the last jump) such that
\mathrm{Gal}(K(s,M)/K)^{(v)}=e
iff
v>v(s,M)
.

0.1.2.
Local analogue of the Grothendieck Conjecture

In
[
4
,
7
]
we obtained by essentially different method
quite efficient generalisation of Mochizuki’s result
[
27
]

in the context of
p
-extensions for cdvf
K
of any characteristic.
Namely, if
K
is a cdvf then the functor
K\mapsto(\Gamma_{K}(p),\{\Gamma_{K}(p)^{(v)}\}_{v\geqslant 0})
is fully faithful.
If
p\geqslant 3
the proof of the equicharacteristic case
in
[
4
]
uses explicit description of the ramification subgroups

\Gamma_{K}(p)^{(v)}
modulo third commutators

C_{3}(\Gamma_{K}(p))\supset C_{p}(\Gamma_{K}(p))
. Then
the mixed chracteristic case is deduced via
application of the field-of-norms functor.
In the case
p=2
our approach
does not work because modulo

C_{p}(\Gamma_{K}(p))=C_{2}(\Gamma_{K}(2))
we are just
in the abelian situation where the ramification filtration
carries no essential information.
However, in
[
7
]
we studied the images of ramification filtration
in the Magnus algebra associated with
\Gamma_{K}(2)
modulo
subgroup
C_{3}(\Gamma_{K}(2))
of third commutators. This new
techniques allowed us to prove the case
p=2
in the both
(equicharacteristic and mixed characteristic) cases. Our method even worked in
the case of higher local fields, cf.
[
5
,
6
]
.

0.1.3.
“Small”
p
-adic representations

Suppose
V
is a finite
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module and
\pi:\Gamma_{K}\longrightarrow GL_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(V)

is a group homomorphism. Then we have a finite field extension

K_{\pi}:=K_{sep}^{\mathrm{Ker}\pi}
of
K
and arithmetic properties
of
K_{\pi}/K
depend on the images

\pi(\Gamma_{K}^{(v)})=\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\pi}/K)^{(v)}\subset GL_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(V)

of subgroups
\Gamma_{K}^{(v)}
of
\Gamma_{K}
.

Question.
How to describe the subgroups

\pi(\Gamma_{K}^{(v)})\subset GL_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(V)
?

In
[
13
]
we introduced the concept of “small”
p
-adic
representation. By definition,
\pi
is
small
if for the wild inertia subgroup

\tilde{I}_{K}
of
\Gamma_{K}
it holds

\pi(\tilde{I}_{K})=\exp(L)
, where
L
is a Lie subalgebra in

\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}V
such that
L^{p}=0
. We proved that if

\mathrm{char}K=p
and

\pi
is
small
then all images
\pi(\Gamma_{K}^{(v)})\subset GL_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(V)
can be
uniquely recovered from explicitly constructed
differential form on Fontaine’s

\varphi
-module
\mathcal{M}

associated with
\pi
. The construction of such differential form
is purely geometric. It is related to the canonical connection

\nabla
on
\mathcal{M}
and its preimages
\varphi^{*N}(\nabla)
on

\mathcal{M}\otimes_{K}K_{rad}
,
where
K_{rad}
is the radical closure of
K
in
K_{sep}
. In particular, this construction
gives quite unexpected relation of the
number-theoretic ramification theory
to a very popular theory of
\mathcal{D}
-modules, lifts of Frobenius, etc.

0.1.4.
Non-abelian explicit reciprocity laws (ERL)

Suppose that
V
is a finite cyclic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module.
If
V=(\mathbb{Z}/p^{M+1})v^{*}
then

\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\pi}/K)\subset(\mathbb{Z}/p^{M+1})^{\times}
and

\pi
factors as

\Gamma_{K}\longrightarrow\Gamma_{K}^{ab}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\pi}/K)\subset(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}/p^{M}\,.

Since
K_{\pi}/K
is abelian all
\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\pi}/K)^{(v)}
come from

\Gamma_{K}^{ab(v)}
which are well-known by local
class field theory. Then the corresponding
images of
\Gamma_{K}^{(v)}
can be found via one-or-another
ERL for the Hilbert symbol.
For example, suppose

K=k((t_{0}))
contains a
primitive
p^{M}
-th root of unity and
[K_{\pi}:K]=p^{M}

then
K_{\pi}=K(\root p^{M} \of{v_{\pi}})
,

v_{\pi}\in K^{*}
.
So the images
\pi(\Gamma_{K}^{(v)})
can be calculated via calculations
with the
M
-th Hilbert symbols
(u,v_{\pi})
,
where
u\equiv 1\,\mathrm{mod}\,t_{0}^{v}
.
The study of different ERL’s for the Hilbert symbol (and its analogs)
is one of most popular
areas of Algebraic Number Theory. It has very rich history
[
26
]

started with Kummer’s work in 1859. All known ERL’s have
more-or-less similar structure: the answer appears
as a residue of a suitable explicitly constructed differential form.
As example, we can refer to
fundamental contributions of
Shafarevich
[
31
]
, Artin-Hasse
[
14
]
,
Iwasawa
[
21
]
, Brückner-Vostokov
[
16
,
33
]
,
Witt
[
18
]
, Coates-Wiles
[
17
]
, et al.
If
V
from above Subsection

0.1.3
is not cyclic then one-or-another
non-abelian version of the Hilbert
symbol and the corresponding ERL’s could be used to
study a similar question in full generality.
Such symbol should use as essential ingredients nilpotent versions of
local class field theory and appropriate versions of
Kummer or Artin-Schreier theory.
The idea of creating nilpotent class field theory was very popular
for a very long time, e.g.
[
31
,
23
,
22
]
. Recently,
F. Laubie
[
24
]

obtained such theory by
generalizing the approach from
[
23
]
. It is based on
the idea of iteration of Lubin-Tate theories via the
field-of-norms functor. This theory posseses
many nice properties but is not adjusted for obtaining
(even very well-known) explicit information about
involved Galois groups. A nilpotent analog of the Artin-Schreier theory was constructed
by the author in
[
1
,
2
]
; it allowed us to obtain
new results about ramification filtration in
[
1
,
3
]
. Introduction of
nilpotent analogue of Hilbert symbol could be stated now as the question about linking
the Laubie
and our theories. This will allow us to benefit from
functorial properties of the first
and explicit constructions of the second theories.
Notice that the case of “small” representations
[
13
]

gives a positive feeling about existence of ERL’s in the
non-abelian context.

0.1.5.
Geometrical approach to ramification filtration

The above
applications not only illustrate a fundamental role of explicit results
from
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
but also rise the question about their
geometric origin and more structural approach.
The proofs in
[
1
]
are reasonably technical but their
modulo
p^{M}
-version in
[
2
,
3
]
relies on heavy computations with
enveloping algebras.
When working with Lie algebras of nilpotent class
<p
the most efficient and clear approach should use one-or-another
deformation theory. Such theory was recently constructed in the case of
Galois groups of period
p
in
[
12
]
and this gave us a geometrical
approach to
[
1
]
.
In this paper we construct more general deformation theory which adjusted to
the modulo
p^{M}
situation. This theory
not just gives the results of
[
2
,
3
]
but also
considerably simplifies the approach from
[
12
]
.
We expect that this
new techniques could be applied to develop the modulo
p^{M}
-case
of results obtained in the mixed characteristic
case in
[
9
,
10
]
.
Note that in
[
12
]
we gave also an explicit description of arbitrarily long
segments of ramification filtration (not just of
a single ramification subgroup) in the case of
Galois groups of period
p
.
This description admits a straight generalisation to the modulo

p^{M}
-situation. As a result, we have a substantial control
on explicit information about arithmetical structure of
Galois groups of cdvf’s. We also expect the existence of other
fundamental relations between Arithmetic and Geometry of cdvf’s.

0.2.
The content of the paper

Everywhere in the paper
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
are fixed.
For
s\in\mathbb{N}
and a topological
group
G
, we denote by
C_{s}(G)
the closure of the subgroup of

G
generated by commutators of order
\geqslant s
.
If
L
is a toplogical
Lie algebra then
C_{s}(L)
is the closure of the ideal generated by
commutators of order
\geqslant s
.
For any topological
A
-modules
M
and
B
, we use the notation

M_{B}:=M\hat{\otimes}_{A}B
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a cdvf and
let
\mathcal{K}_{<p,M}
be a maximal
p
-extension of
\mathcal{K}

with the Galois group

\mathrm{Gal}(\mathcal{K}_{<p,M}/\mathcal{K}):=\mathcal{G}_{<p,M}

of nilpotence class
<p
and exponent
p^{M}
.
If
L
is a Lie
\mathbb{Z}/p^{M}
-algebra then
G(L)
is the
group obtained from the elements of

L
via the Campbell-Hausdorff composition law
\circ
, cf. Sect.
1.2
.

Consider a decreasing filtration by ramification
subgroups in upper numbering

\{\mathcal{G}^{(v)}_{<p,M}\}_{v\geqslant 0}
of
\mathcal{G}_{<p,M}
.
In
[
12
]
we constructed a deformation theory which
allowed us to retrieve (in essentially geometrical way)
the results from
[
1
]
.
In this paper, we obtain a
generalisation (and also a considerable
simplification)
of our approach from
[
12
]
to the case of
Galois groups
\mathcal{G}_{<p,M}
with arbitrary exponent
p^{M}
.
We also explain how this theory can be applied to recover the structure of
the ramification subgroups
\mathcal{G}_{<p,M}^{(v)}
.
Our approach could be overviewed as follows.

a) Let
\mathcal{K}=k((t_{0}))
, where
k\simeq\mathbb{F}_{p^{N_{0}}}
. Then we can use the uniformiser
t_{0}

to define the lifts modulo

p^{M}
,
O_{M}(\mathcal{K})=W_{M}(k)((t))\subset W_{M}(\mathcal{K})
and

O_{M}(\mathcal{K}_{sep})=W_{M}(\sigma^{M-1}\mathcal{K}_{sep})[t]\subset W_{M}(\mathcal{K}_{sep})
. Here
W_{M}
is
the functor of Witt vectors of length
M
and

t=[t_{0}]\in W_{M}(\mathcal{K})
is the Teichmuller representative
of
t_{0}
. This allows us to apply the nilpotent version of
Artin-Shreier theory, cf.
[
2
]
, to fix an
identification
\eta^{0}:\mathcal{G}_{<p,M}\simeq G(\mathcal{L})
, where
\mathcal{L}
is
explicitly constructed pro-finite
Lie
\mathbb{Z}/p^{M}
-algebra. The identification
\eta^{0}

appears via choosing
a suitable
e\in\mathcal{L}_{O_{M}(\mathcal{K})}
, taking

f\in\mathcal{L}_{O_{M}(\mathcal{K}_{sep})}
such that
\sigma(f)=e\circ f
and setting
for any

\tau\in\mathcal{G}_{<p,M}
,
\eta^{0}(\tau)=(-f)\circ\tau(f)\in G(\mathcal{L})
.
This provides us also with
“ramification” ideals
\mathcal{L}^{(v)}
in
\mathcal{L}
such that

\eta^{0}(\mathcal{G}_{<p,M}^{(v)})=\mathcal{L}^{(v)}
.

b) The extension of scalars
\mathcal{L}_{W(k)}

appears with the set of explicit
generators
\{D_{an}\ |\ \mathrm{gcd}(a,p)=1,n\in\mathbb{Z}/N_{0}\}\cup\{D_{0}\}
.
We use them to define a decreasing filtration of ideals

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(1)\supset\mathcal{L}(2)\supset\ldots\supset\mathcal{L}(p)
and provide

\bar{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{L}(p)
with induced filtration

\bar{\mathcal{L}}(w)
,
1\leqslant w\leqslant p
.
The algebra
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
is considerably smaller than
\mathcal{L}
;
it is generated
over
W(k)
by all
D_{an}
with
0\leqslant a<(p-1)v_{0}
.
However,
we prove that
\mathcal{L}(p)\subset\mathcal{L}^{(v_{0})}
and, as a result,
it will be sufficient to
describe the image
\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(v_{0})}
of
\mathcal{L}^{(v_{0})}
in
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
.

c) Our next step is a construction of a new
Lie
\mathbb{Z}/p^{M}
-algebra
\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{{\dagger}}

together with projection
\bar{\mathcal{V}}:\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{{\dagger}}\to\bar{\mathcal{L}}
. As a
matter of fact, there is a splitting of Lie algebras

\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{{\dagger}}=\mathrm{Ker}\bar{\mathcal{V}}\oplus\bar{\mathcal{L}}
.
The construction of
\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{{\dagger}}
depends on a construction of a
special system of coefficients inside
{O_{M}(\mathcal{K})}
, cf.
Sect.
2
.
We just mention that this system specifies explicitly the
powers of
t
which depend on
a choice of a sufficiently large
N^{*}\in\mathbb{N}
and a sufficiently close to

v_{0}
rational number
r^{*}<v_{0}
. More precisely, if
q:=p^{N^{*}}
then

r^{*}(q-1):=b^{*}
is a natural number prime to
p
, and
there are no ramification breaks of the ramification
filtration
\mathcal{G}^{(v)}_{<p,M}

inside the interval
(r^{*},v_{0})
.
(The ramification breaks of the filtration
\mathcal{G}^{(v)}_{<p,M}

are discrete because
they are discrete for abelian extensions and

\mathcal{G}_{<p,M}
has finite nilpotence class.) Then the
admissible exponents
t^{-\iota}
,
\iota\in\mathfrak{A}^{0}
, appear
in the form

\iota=qp^{M-1}A-b^{*}U\in\mathfrak{A}^{0}
, where
A
is a finite sum of various

p^{n}a
with
n\geqslant 0

and
a\in[0,(p-1)v_{0})
, and
U
is a finite sum of
u_{j}p^{m_{j}}
with

u_{j},m_{j}\geqslant 0
and
1\leqslant\sum_{j}u_{j}<p
.
One of properties of such exponents
\iota\in\mathfrak{A}^{0}
is that

\mathrm{ch}(\iota):=\sum_{j}u_{j}
is an invariant of
\iota
. In these terms,
working with the Lie algebra

\bar{\mathcal{L}}
inside
\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{{\dagger}}
is related
to
t
-exponents with
\mathrm{ch}(\iota)=0
, and working
with
\mathrm{Ker}\bar{\mathcal{V}}\subset\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{{\dagger}}
corresponds to
t-exponents with
\mathrm{ch}(\iota)\geqslant 1
.

d) Let us come back to the identification

\eta^{0}:\mathcal{G}_{<p,M}\simeq G(\mathcal{L})
from a)
determined (up to conjugation)
by a choice of a suitable
e\in\mathcal{L}_{O_{M}(\mathcal{K})}
. Then
the image
\bar{e}
of
e
under the projection

\mathcal{L}_{O_{M}(\mathcal{K})}\to\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{O_{M}(\mathcal{K})}
determines the epimorphic map

\bar{\eta}^{0}:{\mathcal{G}}_{<p,M}\to G(\bar{\mathcal{L}})
.
On the other hand, choose an analog
\bar{e}^{{\dagger}}
of
\bar{e}
for
\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{{\dagger}}

which is compatible with
\bar{e}
, i.e.
such that
(\bar{\mathcal{V}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\bar{e}^{{\dagger}}=\bar{e}
.
This will allow us
to obtain a unique lift

\bar{\eta}^{{\dagger}}:\mathcal{G}_{<p,M}\to G(\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{{\dagger}})
of

\bar{\eta}^{0}
such that

\bar{\mathcal{V}}\bar{\eta}^{{\dagger}}=\bar{\eta}^{0}
.

e) Note that
\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{{\dagger}}
also could be
provided with a decreasing filtration of length

p
such that the projection

\bar{\mathcal{V}}:\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{{\dagger}}\to\bar{\mathcal{L}}
is
epimorphism of filtered Lie algebras. As a matter
of fact, the filtration on
\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{{\dagger}}

is defined in terms of invariant
\mathrm{ch}(\iota)\in[0,p-1]
.
This allows us to define the
action
\Omega
of the elements
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{M}

on the system of coefficients
\mathfrak{A}^{0}

by the correspondences

t^{-qp^{M-1}a}\mapsto t^{-qp^{M-1}a}E(t^{b^{*}})^{a\gamma p^{M-1}}
,
where
E
is the Artin-Hasse exponential.

f) We use the action
\Omega
to obtain a deformation of
the element
\bar{e}^{{\dagger}}
to
\Omega({\bar{e}}^{{\dagger}})\in\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{{\dagger}}_{O_{M}(\mathcal{K})}
. This element determines the deformation

\Omega(\bar{\eta}^{{\dagger}})
of
\bar{\eta}^{{\dagger}}
which
is given by a unipotent automorphism
A^{{\dagger}}
of
the Lie algebra
\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{{\dagger}}
. Equivalently,
it appears from a
differentiation
B^{{\dagger}}
of
\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{{\dagger}}
such that

A^{{\dagger}}=\widetilde{\exp}B^{{\dagger}}
(this is the truncated exponential).
With this notation, our main result states that
the ramification ideal

\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(v_{0})}
appears as

\bar{\mathcal{V}}B^{{\dagger}}(\mathrm{Ker}{\bar{\mathcal{V}}})
.

In the remaining part of the paper we are doing explicit
calculations with specially chosen element
\bar{e}
to
find explicit generators of
\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(v_{0})}

in the style of paper

[
3
]

1.2.
Ideals
\mathcal{L}(w)
,
w\geqslant 1

Recall that we
fixed
v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}

Definition
.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
, let
U(s,m)
be
the minimal value of

1/p^{i_{1}}+\ldots+1/p^{i_{s}}
, where all

i_{j}
run over
\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
and satisfy the condition

i_{1}+\dots+i_{s}=m
. By definition, set for any
m\geqslant 0
,
U(0,m)=0
.

Note that directly from definitions it follows that:

–
U(s,0)=s
and for
s\geqslant 1
,
U(s,m)>0
;

– if
s^{\prime}>s
then for any
m\geqslant 1
,
U(s^{\prime},m)>U(s,m)
;

– if
m^{\prime}>m
and
s\geqslant 1
then

U(s,m^{\prime})<U(s,m)
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
s,s^{\prime},m,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
. Then:

a)

U(s,m)+U(s^{\prime},m^{\prime})\geqslant U(s+s^{\prime},m+m^{\prime})\,.

b)
For
s\geqslant 1
, let

m=su+s_{1}
, where
0\leqslant s_{1}<s
.
Then

U(s,m)=s_{0}/p^{u}+s_{1}/p^{u+1}\,\text{with }s_{0}=s-s_{1}\,.

In particular,
u,s_{0},s_{1}
are uniquely determined by
m
and
s
.

c)
For any
m^{0},s,s^{\prime}\geqslant 0
,

\min\{U(s,m)+U(s^{\prime},m^{\prime})\ |\ m+m^{\prime}=m^{0}\}=U(s+s^{\prime},m^{0})\,.

Proof.

a) is obvious.

b) Suppose
m_{1},\dots,m_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
,

m_{1}+\ldots+m_{s}=m
and

U(s,m)=1/p^{m_{1}}+\cdots+1/p^{m_{s}}\,.

If for some indices
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant s

it holds that
m_{i}\geqslant m_{j}+2

then we obtain the contradiction by the use of the obvious inequality

1/p^{m_{i}-1}+1/p^{m_{j}+1}<1/p^{m_{i}}+1/p^{m_{j}}\,.

Therefore, the minimal value
U(s,m)
can appear
only if there is

u\in\mathbb{N}
such that among all numbers
m_{i}
,

1\leqslant j\leqslant s
, there are

s_{0}>0
of those which are equal to
u
and all
remaining
s_{1}=s-s_{0}

numbers are equal to
u+1
.
It remains to note that

m=m_{1}+\dots+m_{s}=us_{0}+(u+1)s_{1}=su+s_{1}\,.

c) Suppose
U(s,m)+U(s^{\prime},m^{\prime})
is the minimal value of
the LHS. We can assume that

s,s^{\prime}\geqslant 1
. Let
m=si+s_{1}
and
m^{\prime}=s^{\prime}i^{\prime}+s_{1}^{\prime}
,
where
0\leqslant s_{1}<s
and

0\leqslant s_{1}^{\prime}<s^{\prime}
. Then for
s_{0}=s-s_{1}
and
s_{0}^{\prime}=s^{\prime}-s_{1}^{\prime}
,

U(s,m)=s_{0}/p^{i}+s_{1}/p^{i+1}
and

U(s^{\prime},m^{\prime})=s_{0}^{\prime}/p^{i^{\prime}}+s_{1}^{\prime}/p^{i^{\prime}+1}
.

c_{1}
) Suppose
i=i^{\prime}
. Then
U(s,m)+U(s^{\prime},m^{\prime})=U(s+s^{\prime},m+m^{\prime})

and our statement is proved.

c_{2}
) Suppose
i>i^{\prime}
.

c_{21}
) Let
s_{1}\neq 0
and
s_{1}^{\prime}\neq s^{\prime}-1
. In this case

m-1=si+(s_{1}-1)

and
m^{\prime}=s^{\prime}i^{\prime}+(s_{1}^{\prime}+1)
imply that

(1.2)

U(s,m-1)+U(s^{\prime},m^{\prime}+1)<U(s,m)+U(s^{\prime},m^{\prime})

because
U(s,m-1)=U(s,m)+(1/p^{i}-1/p^{i+1})
and

U(s^{\prime},m^{\prime}+1)=U(s^{\prime},m^{\prime})-(1/p^{i^{\prime}}-1/p^{i^{\prime}+1})
.

c_{22}
) Let
s_{1}=0
but
s_{1}^{\prime}\neq s^{\prime}-1
. In this case

m-1=s(i-1)+(s-1)
and

m+1=s^{\prime}i^{\prime}+(s^{\prime}_{1}+1)
imply that either we have again
the contradiction (
1.2
)
or
i=i^{\prime}+1
. But in the later case,

m+m^{\prime}=(s+s^{\prime})i^{\prime}+s+s_{1}^{\prime}
and

U(s+s^{\prime},m+m^{\prime})=\frac{s^{\prime}}{p^{i^{\prime}}}+\frac{s+s_{1}^{\prime}}{p^{i^{\prime}+1}}=U(s,m)+U(s^{\prime},m^{\prime})\,.

c_{23}
) Let
s_{1}\neq 0
but
s_{1}^{\prime}-1=s^{\prime}
. Here
m-1=si+s_{1}-1

and
m+1=(s^{\prime}+1)i^{\prime}
. Then

U(s,m-1)=U(s,m)+(1/p^{i}-1/p^{i+1})
and

U(s^{\prime},m^{\prime}+1)=U(s^{\prime},m^{\prime})-(1/p^{i^{\prime}}-1/p^{i^{\prime}+1})
.
Then again we obtain contradiction (
1.2
) because
i>i^{\prime}
.

c_{24}
) Let
s_{1}=0
and
s_{1}^{\prime}=s^{\prime}-1
. Here

m-1=s(i-1)+s-1
and
m^{\prime}+1=s^{\prime}(i^{\prime}+1)
.
We obtain either contradiction (
1.2
) or
i-1=i^{\prime}
.
In the later case

m+m^{\prime}=s^{\prime}i^{\prime}+s+s^{\prime}-1
and
U(s+s^{\prime},m+m^{\prime})=U(s,m)+U(s^{\prime},m^{\prime})
.
∎

, i.e.

with

,

and

-digits.
Prove that the corresponding condition

Kohn-Luttinger Superconductivity and Inter-Valley Coherence in Rhombohedral Trilayer Graphene
Yi-Zhuang You, Ashvin Vishwanath

Abstract
Motivated by recent experiments on ABC-stacked rhombohedral trilayer graphene (RTG) which observed spin-valley symmetry-breaking and superconductivity, we study instabilities of the RTG metallic state to symmetry breaking orders. We find that interactions select the inter-valley coherent order (IVC) as the preferred ordering channel over a wide range, whose theoretically determined phase boundaries agree well with experiments on both the hole and electron doped sides. The Fermi surfaces near van Hove singularities admit partial nesting between valleys, which promotes both inter-valley superconductivity and IVC fluctuations. We investigate the interplay between these fluctuations and the Hunds (intervalley spin) interaction using a renormalization group approach. For antiferromagnetic Hund’s coupling, intervalley pairing appears in the spin-singlet channel with enhanced T c T_{c} , that scales with the dimensionless coupling g g as T c ∼ exp ( − 1 / g ) T_{c}\sim\exp(-1/\sqrt{g}) , compared to the standard exp ( − 1 / g ) \exp(-1/g) scaling. In its simplest form, this scenario assumes a sign change in the Hund’s coupling on increasing hole doping. On the other hand, the calculation incorporates breaking of the independent spin rotations between valleys from the start, and strongly selects spin singlet over spin triplet pairing, and naturally occurs in proximity to the IVC, consistent with observations.

A prominent feature of the RTG band structure is the presence of van Hove singularities in both the conduction and valence bands, where the DOS diverges due to Lifshitz transitions of the Fermi surface topology, as demonstrated in Fig.
2
(a). On the hole-doped side (in the valence band), the van Hove singularity features an interwoven circular and triangular Fermi surface, whose intersection points are where the larger annular Fermi surface pinches off into smaller Fermi pockets. On the electron-doped side (in the conduction band), the van Hove singularity features a three-leaf clover-shaped Fermi surface when the three smaller pockets are about to merge. These particular shapes of Fermi surfaces at van Hove singularities are well-nested between
K
and
K^{\prime}
valleys, as illustrated in Fig.
2
(b,c). Under repulsive interactions, the inter-valley nesting leads to a strong instability towards the inter-valley coherence (IVC) order, described by the order parameter
c_{{\bm{k}},K}^{\dagger}c_{{\bm{k}},K^{\prime}}
, which is energetically favored as it gaps out the nested part of the Fermi surface and reduces the DOS at the Fermi level. Furthermore, the time-reversal symmetry ensures that band dispersions around the two valleys are related by
\epsilon_{K^{\prime}}({\bm{k}})=\epsilon_{K}(-{\bm{k}})
, which admits perfect inter-valley pairing of electrons, as illustrated in Fig.
2
(d,e). If the interaction is renormalized to attractive, the system will likely develop inter-valley superconductivity in the
s
-wave channel which can fully gap the Fermi surface.

Superconductivity.
— It is recently discovered in experiment
Zhou et al. 2021b
that superconducting (SC) phases emerge at low temperature within the Fermi liquid (FL) phase, and are closely adjacent to the boundary of the spin-valley symmetry breaking phase (which we identified as the IVC phase). However, signatures of enhanced scattering from the underlying pairing-glue modes is not observed above the SC transition temperature
Zhou et al. 2021b
, which points to the Kohn-Luttinger mechanism
Kohn and Luttinger 1965
;
Raghu et al. 2010
;
Maiti and Chubukov 2013
;
Kagan et al. 2015
;
Chubukov and Kivelson 2017

for superconductivity based on the intrinsic instability of the FL. To explore this possibility, we perform the patch renormalization group (RG) analysis
Furukawa et al. 1998
;
Raghu and Kivelson 2011
;
Isobe et al. 2018
;
Nandkishore et al. 2012
;
Park et al. 2021
;
Betouras
that tracks the RG flow of interactions at one-loop level. We will focus on the van Hove singularity in a single band, described by
H_{0}=\sum_{{\bm{k}},\xi}c_{{\bm{k}},\xi}^{\dagger}\epsilon_{\xi}({\bm{k}})c_{{\bm{k}},\xi}

\epsilon_{\xi}({\bm{k}})

1

\epsilon_{\xi}({\bm{k}})=\xi\pi_{{\bm{k}}}+\mu_{{\bm{k}}}

\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}J_{H}&=-J_{H}((1+d_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}})J_{H}+(1-d_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}})V),\\
\end{split}

Although the

Discussion.
— Before closing, let us highlight some issues raised by experiments. In the vicinity of SC1 we have assumed antiferromagnetic spin coupling
J_{H}<0
,
Zhou et al. 2021b

. The same sign of
J_{H}
is observed in other graphene structures, e.g. monolayers and bilayers
Maher et al. 2013
;
Maher et al. 2013
. However, the spin-split Fermi surfaces at smaller hole doping points to
J_{H}>0
, implying that it changes sign with doping, as illustrate in Fig.
1
(b). Clarifying this evolution will require a microscopic calculation of
J_{H}
, an important future task. Across the
J_{H}=0
line, the IVC phase can be split into the spin-singlet (
J_{H}<0
) and spin-triplet (
J_{H}>0
) sub-phases, see Fig.
1
A different mechanism for IVC fluctuation superconductivity was discussed in
You and Vishwanath 2019
, even when SO(4) symmetry is preserved. There, topological superconductivity was obtained and the details of that mechanism are rather different from the one we explored here. We propose that the spin unpolarized PIP phase could correspond to an IVC phase. However, our RPA approach does not capture the Stoner instability of spin or valley polarized states, which is another competitive valley-spin symmetry breaking order near the van Hove singularity. We will leave the competition between IVC and Stoner ferromagnetism for future investigation.

Next, we note that the observation of a Pauli limiting field in SC1 is significant, particularly if we assume that the in-plane magnetic field couples entirely through the Zeeman effect. As a side remark, although it is natural to ascribe the observed Pauli limiting behavior to the Zeeman coupling, we note that potentially, an in-plane field could also lead to an orbital suppression of superconductivity, due to finite layer separation.
Future experiments probing in-plane field anisotropy, expected for the orbital but not for the Zeeman coupling, should help isolate the relevant coupling. At present, the absence of any in-plane field effect on the putative triplet superconductor SC2, and the observation of the Pauli limit in SC1, suggests pure Zeeman coupling.) Under this assumption, the Pauli limiting behavior strongly suggests that anisotropy terms like
J_{H}
play a significant role in superconductivity. If instead pairing was primarily determined by
\mathrm{SO}(4)
symmetric interactions, then one would expect both singlet and triplet superconductors to be nearly degenerate. The
\mathrm{SO}(4)
breaking anisotropies would lead only to small splittings in
T_{c}
implying that on destroying the spin-singlet superconductor with Zeeman field, a spin-triplet superconductor, with slightly reduced
T_{c}
would be established beyond the Pauli limit. Indeed, such behavior is observed in a moiré material of mirror-symmetric twisted trilayer graphene
Cao et al. 2021
, but is not observed in the RTG SC1 phase. This indicates a potentially different origin for superconductivity here compared to moiré graphene. Our pairing mechanism emphasizes the role of
J_{H}
from the start, in contrast to those that rely predominantly on
\mathrm{SO}(4)
symmetric interactions, either electronic or those based on acoustic phonons. The Hund’s coupling
J_{H}
plays an indispensable role to allow the inter-valley density interaction
V
to change sign under RG, which enables the
s
-wave SC to emerge even under a repulsive bare interaction. A weak coupling
J_{H}
also flows strong under RG, which explains the absence of spin-triplet SC phase above the Pauli limit, even if the
\mathrm{SO}(4)
symmetry is only weakly broken at the bare level.

Finally, it is interesting to compare and contrast magic-angle graphene (bilayer and mirror-symmetric trilayer) and RTG. On one hand, differences include the fact that the transition temperatures in the former are larger than the latter by over an order of magnitude despite comparable electronic densities, and show signatures of strong coupling superconductivity
Cao et al. 2018b
;
Yankowitz et al. 2019a
;
Lu et al. 2019
;
Park et al. 2021
;
Hao et al. 2021
along with strange metal normal state behavior
Cao et al. 2020
;
Polshyn et al. 2019
;
Jaoui et al. 2021
. The width in doping supporting superconductivity is also dramatically different in the two cases. However, we note that the proximity of isospin order and superconductivity is a common feature in both. Comparing these two remarkable superconductors will be a useful future exercise.

Summary.
— We combine the mean-field/RPA approach and the patch RG analysis to investigate the IVC and inter-valley SC orders in RTG systems. The particular Fermi surface topology at the van Hove singularity enables a good inter-valley nesting, which promotes both the IVC and SC ordering under local electronic interactions. The IVC picture nicely reproduces the general shape of the phase diagram and explains the doubling of quantum oscillation frequency. It also leaves room for cascades of secondary ordered phases when the system is further doped away from the van Hove singularity. The diverging density of state near the van Hove singularity also strongly renormalizes the interaction in the FL phase, which, particularly in the presence of an inter-valley antiferromagnetic
J_{H}<0
coupling, causes the inter-valley density interaction to become attractive at low-energy, favoring the inter-valley spin-singlet
s
-wave SC with an enhanced critical temperature
T_{c}
compared to the standard BCS formula. The RG flow can also amplify the effect of the inter-valley Hunds interaction, leading to a significant splitting of spin-singlet and spin-triplet SC states, even if the
\mathrm{SO}(4)
breaking anisotropy is weak at the lattice scale.

\times

\times

\begin{split}&V_{1234}=V_{5678}=-U,\\
&V_{1256}=V_{3478}=-V+J_{H},\\
&V_{1278}=V_{3456}=-V-J_{H},\\
&V_{1357}=V_{1458}=V_{2367}=V_{2468}=V_{1368}=V_{1476}=V_{2385}=V_{2457}=J_{H}.\end{split}

y^{\prime}=\Pi_{{\bm{0}}}^{\text{pp}}
and the nesting parameters are defined as

d_{1}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}}}{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{{\bm{0}}}^{\text{pp}}},d_{2}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{{\bm{0}}}^{\text{ph}}}{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{{\bm{0}}}^{\text{pp}}},d_{3}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{pp}}}{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{{\bm{0}}}^{\text{pp}}}.

(31)

We first need to change the RG time from
y^{\prime}=\Pi_{{\bm{0}}}^{\text{pp}}
to
y=\Pi_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{pp}}
and rewrite the RG equation in terms of our nesting parameters in Eq. (
25
) (which amounts to replacing
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}=(1/d_{\bm{0}}^{\text{pp}})\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}
,
d_{1}=d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}}/d_{\bm{0}}^{\text{pp}}
,
d_{2}=0
,
d_{3}=1/d_{\bm{0}}^{\text{pp}}
),

\begin{split}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}g_{1}&=-2d_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}}g_{1}(g_{1}-g_{2})-2g_{1}g_{2},\\
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}g_{2}&=d_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}}(g_{2}^{2}+g_{3}^{2})-(g_{1}^{2}+g_{2}^{2}),\\
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}g_{3}&=-2d_{\bm{0}}^{\text{pp}}g_{3}g_{4}-2d_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}}g_{3}(g_{1}-2g_{2}),\\
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}g_{4}&=-d_{\bm{0}}^{\text{pp}}(g_{3}^{2}+g_{4}^{2}).\\
\end{split}

(32)

Then by comparing with Eq. (
6
), we identify the couplings in Eq. (
29
) as

g_{1}=J_{H},g_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(V+J_{H}),g_{3}=0,g_{4}=\frac{1}{2}U,

(33)

then the RG equation becomes

\begin{split}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}J_{H}&=-d_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}}J_{H}(J_{H}-V)-J_{H}(J_{H}+V),\\
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\tfrac{1}{2}(V+J_{H})&=d_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}}\tfrac{1}{4}(V+J_{H})^{2}-(J_{H}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}(V+J_{H})^{2}),\\
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}(\tfrac{1}{2}U)&=-d_{\bm{0}}^{\text{pp}}(\tfrac{1}{4}U^{2}).\\
\end{split}

(34)

After some simple algebra, it is straightforward to show that the RG equation is consistent with our derivation.

A.2
Other Form of Hund’s Interaction

Another form
You and Vishwanath 2019
of Hund’s interaction in terms of IVC operators
{\bm{I}}=c_{K}^{\dagger}{\bm{\sigma}}c_{K^{\prime}}
, (Ref.
Chatterjee et al. 2021
points out that this has a better justified microscopic origin in the present problem). In terms of the IVC Hund’s coupling, the total interaction will be reparametrized as

H_{\text{int}}=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{x}}\;\frac{U}{2}(n_{K}^{2}+n_{K^{\prime}}^{2})+\tilde{V}n_{K}n_{K^{\prime}}-\tilde{J}_{H}{\bm{I}}^{\dagger}\cdot{\bm{I}}.

(35)

We have assume the limit that the interaction is very local, such that there is no momentum dependence of the coupling coefficients. In this limit, we can use the following operator identity

{\bm{I}}^{\dagger}\cdot{\bm{I}}=-\frac{3}{2}n_{K}n_{K^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}{\bm{S}}_{K}\cdot{\bm{S}}_{K^{\prime}}

(36)

to connect Eq. (
35
) and Eq. (
6
), and establish the following linear relations between couplings
(V,J_{H})
and
(\tilde{V},\tilde{J}_{H})
,

\left[\begin{matrix}V\\
J_{H}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}1&3/2\\
0&1/2\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\tilde{V}\\
\tilde{J}_{H}\end{matrix}\right].

(37)

Therefore the RG equations

\begin{split}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}U&=-\tfrac{1}{2}d_{{\bm{0}}}^{\text{pp}}U^{2},\\
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}V&=-\tfrac{1}{2}(1-d_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}})(V^{2}+3J_{H}^{2}),\\
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}J_{H}&=-J_{H}((1+d_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}})J_{H}+(1-d_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}})V),\\
\end{split}

(38)

can also be written as

\begin{split}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}U&=-\tfrac{1}{2}d_{{\bm{0}}}^{\text{pp}}U^{2},\\
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\tilde{V}&=-\tfrac{1}{2}(1-d_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}})\tilde{V}^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}\tilde{J}_{H}^{2},\\
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\tilde{J}_{H}&=-\tilde{J}_{H}((2-d_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}})\tilde{J}_{H}+(1-d_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}})\tilde{V}),\\
\end{split}

(39)

The RG equation for
U
is not affected by the reparameterization, and is decoupled. The RG flow in the
(V,J_{H})
or
(\tilde{V},\tilde{J}_{H})
planes are compared in Fig.
7
.

). (b) RG flow in

A.3
RG Fixed Rays and Oder Parameter Scaling

We will focus on the
(V,J_{H})
basis, and discuss the RG fixed rays of the flow equation Eq. (
38
). By solving the RG equation, the asymptotic behavior near
y\to y_{c}
is expected to be

V=\frac{\gamma_{V}}{y_{c}-y},J_{H}=\frac{\gamma_{J_{H}}}{y_{c}-y}.

(40)

Substitute Eq. (
40
) to Eq. (
38
), one can determine the coefficients
\gamma_{V}
and
\gamma_{J_{H}}
by the following algebraic equations

\begin{split}\gamma_{V}&=\tfrac{1}{2}(1-d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}})(\gamma_{V}^{2}+3\gamma_{J_{H}}^{2}),\\
\gamma_{J_{H}}&=-\gamma_{J_{H}}((1+d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}})\gamma_{J_{H}}+(1-d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}})\gamma_{V}).\end{split}

(41)

The solutions are:

•

Gaussian fixed point

\gamma_{V}=\gamma_{J}=0;

(42)

•

\mathrm{SO}(4)
fixed ray

\gamma_{V}=-\frac{2}{1-d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}}},\gamma_{J_{H}}=0;

(43)

•

\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\text{s}}^{\text{singlet}}
fixed ray

\gamma_{V}=-\frac{1}{1-d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}}}\left(1-\frac{1+d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}}}{2\sqrt{1-d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}}+(d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}})^{2}}}\right),\gamma_{J_{H}}=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1-d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}}+(d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}})^{2}}};

(44)

•

\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\text{s}}^{\text{triplet}}
fixed ray

\gamma_{V}=-\frac{1}{1-d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}}}\left(1+\frac{1+d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}}}{2\sqrt{1-d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}}+(d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}})^{2}}}\right),\gamma_{J_{H}}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1-d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}}+(d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}})^{2}}}.

(45)

To study the scaling of fermion bilinear order parameters along the RG flow, one can consider deforming the Hamiltonian with infinitesimal fermion bilinear terms
H=H_{0}+\delta H
,

\delta H=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}x\sum_{a,b}X_{ab}\chi_{a}\chi_{b},

(46)

where
X
is an antisymmetric imaginary matrix representing the fermion bilinear order. The correction of
X
is given by

X_{ab}(E)=X_{ab}-V_{abc^{\prime}d^{\prime}}\Pi_{c^{\prime}d^{\prime}a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}(E)X_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}+\cdots
,
which leads to the RG equation

\mathrm{d}X_{ab}=-V_{abc^{\prime}d^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\Pi_{c^{\prime}d^{\prime}a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}X_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}=-V_{abc^{\prime}d^{\prime}}(d_{{\bm{0}}}^{\text{pp}}Q^{\bm{++}}_{c^{\prime}d^{\prime}a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}+Q^{\bm{-+}}_{c^{\prime}d^{\prime}a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}+d_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\text{ph}}Q^{\bm{--}}_{c^{\prime}d^{\prime}a^{\prime}b^{\prime}})X_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}.

(47)

Along the eigen direction (labeled by
\Phi
) of the RG flow, we have

\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}X_{\Phi}=V_{\Phi}X_{\Phi},

(48)

where
V_{\Phi}
denotes the eigen value (which depends on
V_{abcd}
linearly). Near the critical point
y\to y_{c}
,
V_{\Phi}
diverges as
\gamma_{\Phi}/(y_{c}-y)
, then the RG equation implies

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}X_{\Phi}=\frac{\gamma_{\Phi}}{y_{c}-y}X_{\Phi},

(49)

whose solution is

X_{\Phi}(y)\propto(y_{c}-y)^{-\gamma_{\Phi}}.

(50)

Then the susceptibility diverges near
y_{c}
as

\chi_{\Phi}(y)=\frac{X_{\alpha}(y)}{X_{\Phi}(0)}\sim(y_{c}-y)^{-\gamma_{\Phi}}.

(51)

Therefore, a large positive exponent
\gamma_{\Phi}
indicates a stronger instability in the eigen channel of the fermion bilinear ordering. In the following, we will apply this general framework to different bilinear orders.

•

Inter-Valley superconductivity
\Delta^{\mu}=c_{K}^{\intercal}\mathrm{i}\sigma^{2}\sigma^{\mu}c_{K^{\prime}}
. The RG equation reads

\begin{split}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\Delta^{0}&=-2(3J_{H}+V)\Delta^{0},\\
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}{\bm{\Delta}}&=2(J_{H}-V){\bm{\Delta}},\\
\end{split}

(52)

therefore
\gamma_{\Delta^{0}}=-2(3\gamma_{J_{H}}+\gamma_{V})
and
\gamma_{{\bm{\Delta}}}=2(\gamma_{J_{H}}-\gamma_{V})
.

•

Inter-Valley coherence
I^{\mu}=c_{K}^{\dagger}\sigma^{\mu}c_{K^{\prime}}
. The RG equation reads

\begin{split}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}I^{0}&=-2d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}}(3J_{H}-V)I^{0},\\
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}{\bm{I}}&=2d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}}(J_{H}+V){\bm{I}},\\
\end{split}

(53)

therefore
\gamma_{\Delta^{0}}=-2d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}}(3\gamma_{J_{H}}-\gamma_{V})
and
\gamma_{{\bm{\Delta}}}=2d_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{ph}}(\gamma_{J_{H}}+\gamma_{V})
.

•

Intra-Valley superconductivity
\Delta_{\xi}=c_{\xi}^{\intercal}\mathrm{i}\sigma^{2}c_{\xi}
. The RG equation reads

\begin{split}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\Delta_{\xi}&=-2d_{\bm{0}}^{\text{pp}}U\Delta_{\xi},\end{split}

(54)

therefore
\gamma_{\Delta_{\xi}}=-2d_{\bm{0}}^{\text{pp}}\gamma_{U}
.

•

Valley charge density
n_{v}=n_{K}-n_{K^{\prime}}
. The RG equation is trivial
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}n_{v}=0
, so
\gamma_{n_{v}}=0
.

•

Valley charge density
{\bm{S}}_{\xi}=c_{\xi}^{\dagger}{\bm{\sigma}}c_{\xi}
. The RG equation is trivial
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}{\bm{S}}_{\xi}=0
, so
\gamma_{{\bm{S}}_{\xi}}=0
.

2
Exact solution

In this paper, we will consider the
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric potential
V(x)=A[\sech(\lambda x)+i\tanh(\lambda x)]
, then the one-dimension Schrödinger equation write as

\displaystyle[\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+E-V(x)]\psi(x)=0\,.

(1)

Define
z=ie^{\lambda x}
, which transform the variable
x\in(-\infty,\infty)
to a new variable
z\in(0,\infty)
,
and introduce a function
u(z)
, define as
\psi(z)=(-z)^{p}u(z)
, by means of this substitution, we can rewrite the Schrödinger equation as

\displaystyle z(1-z)u^{\prime\prime}(z)+[1+2p-(1+2p)z]u^{\prime}(z)+\frac{E-iA+p^{2}\lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}u(z)=0\,.

(2)

If and only if
E+iA+p^{2}\lambda^{2}=0
, Namely

\displaystyle p=\pm\sqrt{-iA-E}/\lambda\,.

(3)

We can write the solution by comparing with hypergeometric equation

\displaystyle z(1-z)u^{\prime\prime}(z)+[\gamma-(\alpha+\beta+1)z]u^{\prime}(z)-\alpha\beta u(z)=0\,.

(4)

After simple calculation, we have

\displaystyle\alpha=p\pm\sqrt{iA-E}/\lambda\equiv p\pm q\,,\qquad\beta=2p-\alpha\,,\qquad\gamma=2p+1\,.

(5)

Because of the commutative symmetry of
\alpha
and
\beta
, without losing generality, we can choose
\alpha=p-q
.
From the hypergeometric functions theory, the wave function is given by

\displaystyle\psi(z)

\displaystyle=(-z)^{p}[c_{1}F(\alpha,\beta,\gamma;z)+c_{2}(-z)^{1-\gamma}F(\alpha-\gamma+1,\beta-\gamma+1,2-\gamma;z)]\,.

(6)

Where
c_{1},c_{2}
are two arbitrary combination coefficients.
Due to the positive and negative sign of
p
cause the exchange of left and right travelling waves of wave function.
In this we can choose
p=\sqrt{-iA-E}/\lambda
, we assume
\lambda>0
for simplify.

3
Scattering states

Considering the parametrization

\displaystyle\sqrt{E+iA}=k_{R}+ik_{I}=k\,.\,\qquad\sqrt{E-iA}=k^{\prime}_{R}+ik^{\prime}_{I}=k^{\prime}

(7)

and assuming that
E,A
are real numbers, the relations

\displaystyle E=k_{R}^{2}-k_{I}^{2}={k^{\prime}}_{R}^{2}-{k^{\prime}}_{I}^{2}\,\qquad A=2k_{R}k_{I}=-2{k^{\prime}}_{R}{k^{\prime}}_{I}\,.

(8)

So we have
k^{2}=[(k^{\prime})^{2}]^{*}
, which implies that
k^{\prime}=\pm k^{*}
.

For
A>0
, choice
k^{\prime}=k^{*}
this mean
k_{R}=k^{\prime}_{R}>0
and
k_{I}=-k^{\prime}_{I}>0
(For
A<0
the roles of
k
and
k^{\prime}
are exchanged).
Let us analyse the asymptotic behaviour of solutions of (
6
) for
x\rightarrow-\infty
(or
z\rightarrow 0
), we have

\displaystyle\psi(x)_{-\infty}\sim c_{1}(-z)^{p}+c_{2}(-z)^{-p}=(-i)^{p}c_{1}e^{ikx}+(-i)^{-p}c_{2}e^{-ikx}\,.

(9)

So we have

\displaystyle a_{1-}=(-i)^{p}\,\qquad a_{2-}=0\,\qquad b_{1-}=0\,\qquad b_{2-}=(-i)^{-p},\,.

(10)

where the notation can be found in
[
22
]
or see appendix
A
.

And the asymptotic behaviour of the wave function
\psi(x)
for
x\rightarrow\infty
(or
z\rightarrow\infty
) is given

\displaystyle\psi(x)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=[c_{1}\frac{\Gamma(1+2p)\Gamma(-2q)}{\Gamma(p-q)\Gamma(1+p-q)}+c_{2}\frac{\Gamma(1-2p)\Gamma(-2q)}{\Gamma(-p-q)\Gamma(1-p-q)}](-z)^{-q}

\displaystyle+[c_{1}\frac{\Gamma(1+2p)\Gamma(2q)}{\Gamma(p+q)\Gamma(1+p+q)}+c_{2}\frac{\Gamma(1-2p)\Gamma(2q)}{\Gamma(-p+q)\Gamma(1-p+q)}](-z)^{q}\,.

(11)

where we used the formula

\displaystyle F(\alpha,\beta,\gamma;z)=\frac{\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(\beta-\alpha)}{\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(\gamma-\alpha)}(-z)^{-\alpha}F(\alpha,\alpha-\gamma+1,\alpha-\beta+1;1/z)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(\alpha-\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\gamma-\beta)}(-z)^{-\beta}F(\beta,\beta-\gamma+1,\beta-\alpha+1;1/z)\,.

(12)

We have similar notation, which are expressed

\displaystyle b_{1+}

\displaystyle=(-i)^{-q}\frac{\Gamma(1+2p)\Gamma(-2q)}{\Gamma(p-q)\Gamma(1+p-q)}\,,\qquad b_{2+}=(-i)^{-q}\frac{\Gamma(1-2p)\Gamma(-2q)}{\Gamma(-p-q)\Gamma(1-p-q)}

\displaystyle a_{1+}

\displaystyle=(-i)^{-q}\frac{\Gamma(1+2p)\Gamma(2q)}{\Gamma(p+q)\Gamma(1+p+q)}\,,\qquad a_{2+}=(-i)^{-q}\frac{\Gamma(1-2p)\Gamma(2q)}{\Gamma(-p+q)\Gamma(1-p+q)}\,.

(13)

From Eq. (
10
) and Eq. (
13
), we have the transmission and reflection coefficients for a wave coming from the right are

\displaystyle R_{R\rightarrow L}=(-i)^{2q}\frac{\Gamma(-p-q)\Gamma(1-p-q)\Gamma(2q)}{\Gamma(-2q)\Gamma(-p+q)\Gamma(1-p+q)}\,,

\displaystyle T_{R\rightarrow L}=(-i)^{q-p}\frac{\Gamma(-p-q)\Gamma(1-p-q)}{\Gamma(1-2p)\Gamma(-2q)}\,.

(14)

The transmission and reflection coefficients for a wave coming from the left are also given

\displaystyle R_{L\rightarrow R}=-e^{ip\pi}\frac{\Gamma(1+2p)\Gamma(-p-q)\Gamma(1-p-q)}{\Gamma(p-q)\Gamma(1+p-q)\Gamma(1-2p)}\,,

\displaystyle T_{L\rightarrow R}={\frac{p}{q}}T_{R\rightarrow L}\,.

(15)

we see that
R_{R\rightarrow L}\neq R_{L\rightarrow R}
, this reflects the handedness of the problem. As the imaginary part of potential asymptotically non-vanishing, that have

T_{L\rightarrow R}={\frac{p}{q}}T_{R\rightarrow L}
.

Here we point out for
A>0
, if let
k^{\prime}=-k^{*}
this mean
k_{R}=-k^{\prime}_{R}>0
and
k_{I}=k^{\prime}_{I}>0
(For
A<0
the roles of
k
and
k^{\prime}
are exchanged).
The corresponding transmission and reflection coefficients is expressed as

\displaystyle R_{R\rightarrow L}=e^{i\pi q}\frac{\Gamma(-p+q)\Gamma(1-p+q)\Gamma(-2q)}{\Gamma(2q)\Gamma(-p-q)\Gamma(1-p-q)}\,,

\displaystyle T_{R\rightarrow L}=e^{i\pi(p+q)/2}\frac{\Gamma(-p+q)\Gamma(1-p+q)}{\Gamma(1-2p)\Gamma(2q)}\,,

\displaystyle R_{L\rightarrow R}=-e^{ip\pi}\frac{\Gamma(1+2p)\Gamma(-p+q)\Gamma(1-p+q)}{\Gamma(p+q)\Gamma(1+p+q)\Gamma(1-2p)}\,,

\displaystyle T_{L\rightarrow R}=-{\frac{p}{q}}T_{R\rightarrow L}\,.

(16)

We can see that we just need to change
q
to
-q
in Eq. (
16
), the expression will return to the case of
k^{\prime}=-k^{*}
.
If we exchange p and q in transmission and reflection coefficients,
which will correspond to the situation when
A<0
.

4
Bound states

Let us try to find the solutions of the eigenvalue problem associated to this potential, One way to obtain those solutions is to analyze the behavior of the eigenfunctions in the limits
x\rightarrow-\infty
. For
A>0
, without loss of generality, we choose
\text{Re}p<0
, this corresponds to the case of
k_{I}=-k^{\prime}_{I}>0
,
it is required that eigenfunctions square integrable, so we have
\psi(x)\sim z^{-p}F(\alpha-\gamma+1,\beta-\gamma+1,2-\gamma;z)
. Similarly, when
x\rightarrow\infty
, see Eq. (
11
), if
\text{Re}q>0
we obtain bound states only when
-p+q=-n,n=1,2,3,\cdots
, but in this case we have
-p+q>0
, mean
n<0
. If
\text{Re}q<0
we obtain bound states only when
-p-q=-n,n=1,2,3,\cdots
, but in this case we have
-p-q>0
, mean
n<0
, both of these are inconsistent with
n
being a positive integer. If we choose
\text{Re}p>0
, this corresponds to the case of
k_{I}=-k^{\prime}_{I}<0
, we will have bound states conditions
p+q=-n,n=1,2,3,\cdots
for
\text{Re}q>0
, or
p-q=-n,n=1,2,3,\cdots
for
\text{Re}q<0
,we also found these cases are inconsistent with
n
being a positive integer.

By the way, for
A<0
, corresponds to exchanging the momenta
k
and
k^{\prime}
, also corresponds to exchanging the
p
and
q
. Choose
\text{Re}q<0
, we have
\psi(x)\sim z^{-q}F(\alpha-\gamma+1,\beta-\gamma+1,2-\gamma;z)
with
x\rightarrow-\infty
. When
x\rightarrow\infty
, if
\text{Re}p>0
we obtain bound states only when
-q+p=-n,n=1,2,3,\cdots
, but in this case
-q+p>0
, mean
n<0
. If
\text{Re}p<0
we have bound states only when
-p-q=-n,n=1,2,3,\cdots
, but in this case
-p-q>0
, mean
n<0
, both of these are inconsistent with
n
being a positive integer. If we choose
\text{Re}q>0
, this corresponds to the case of
k_{I}=-k^{\prime}_{I}<0
, we will have bound states conditions
p+q=-n,n=1,2,3,\cdots
for
\text{Re}p>0
, or
q-p=-n,n=1,2,3,\cdots
for
\text{Re}p<0
,we also found these cases are inconsistent with
n
being a positive integer. So we have conclusion that there is no bound state solution for potential
V(x)=A[\sech(\lambda x)+i\tanh(\lambda x)]
.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we consider to solve Schrödinger equations with the potential
V(x)=A[\sech(\lambda x)+i\tanh(\lambda x)]
,
and calculated the transmission and reflection coefficients of this potential.
For this
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric potential, the wave numbers are complex and the asymptotic wave numbers in
x\rightarrow\infty
is different in
x\rightarrow-\infty
, cause the transmission coefficient changes a complex phase factor.
The handedness effect was found by changing the direction of the incoming wave, where the reflection coefficient changes.
By the way, we also can consider the
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric potential
V(x)=A[\sech(\lambda x)-i\tanh(\lambda x)]
,
that only need redefine
z=-ie^{\lambda x}
, and change the
iA
to
-iA
in the solutions of (
6
).
Obviously, there is no bound state for this potential.

Appendix A
Transmission and reflection coefficients

Consider the time independent Schrödinger equation satisfied by

\displaystyle H\psi(x)=[-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V(x)]\psi(x)=k^{2}\psi(x)\,.

(17)

With the asymptotic states (finite-range local potential) can be expressed at
x\rightarrow\pm\infty
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{-}e^{ikx}+B_{-}e^{-ikx},&x\rightarrow-\infty\\
A_{+}e^{ikx}+B_{+}e^{-ikx},&x\rightarrow-\infty\end{array}\right.\end{split}

Schrödinger equation admits a general solution written as two linear independent solutions,
F_{1}(x)
and
F_{2}(x)
,
with non-zero Wronskian, whose asymptotic expressions as:

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\pm\infty}F_{1}=a_{1\pm}e^{ikx}+b_{1\pm}e^{-ikx}\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\pm\infty}F_{2}=a_{2\pm}e^{ikx}+b_{2\pm}e^{-ikx}\,.

(18)

which related to the asymptotic amplitudes
A_{\pm}
and
B_{\pm}

\displaystyle A_{\pm}=\alpha a_{1\pm}+\beta a_{2\pm}\,,

\displaystyle B_{\pm}=\alpha b_{1\pm}+\beta b_{2\pm}\,.

(19)

when the wave moving from left to right, the transmission and reflection coefficients are written as

\displaystyle T_{L\rightarrow R}={\frac{a_{2+}b_{1+}-a_{1+}b_{2+}}{a_{2-}b_{1+}-a_{1-}b_{2+}}}\,,

\displaystyle R_{L\rightarrow R}={\frac{b_{1+}b_{2-}-b_{1-}b_{2+}}{a_{2-}b_{1+}-a_{1-}b_{2+}}}\,.

(20)

when the wave moving from right to left, the transmission and reflection coefficients are written as

\displaystyle T_{R\rightarrow L}={\frac{a_{2-}b_{1-}-a_{1-}b_{2-}}{a_{2-}b_{1+}-a_{1-}b_{2+}}}\,,

\displaystyle R_{L\rightarrow R}={\frac{a_{1+}a_{2-}-a_{1-}a_{2+}}{a_{2-}b_{1+}-a_{1-}b_{2+}}}\,.

(21)

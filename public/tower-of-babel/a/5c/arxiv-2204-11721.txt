Comment on the “Significance of Electromagnetic Potentials in the Quantum Theory”
Siddhant Das

Abstract
A missing angle-dependent prefactor in the wave functions for half-integer magnetic flux obtained in [Y. Aharonov and D. Bohm, Phys. Rev. 115 , 485 (1959) ] is supplied, without which the result is not single-valued. No physical conclusions of the paper are affected.



In their celebrated 1959 paper
Aharonov and Bohm 1959
, Aharonov and Bohm (hereafter AB) considered the motion of an electron of mass
m
and charge
-\kern 1.00006pte
in the field of an infinitely long solenoid of vanishing radius, and governed by the (Coulomb gauge) Hamiltonian

H=\frac{1}{2m}\!\left(-\kern 1.00006pti\hbar\kern 1.00006pt\boldsymbol{\gradient}+\frac{e\Phi}{2\kern 1.00006pt\pi r}\kern 1.00006pt{\hat{\mathbf{\bm{\theta}\mkern 3.0mu}}\mkern-3.0mu}{}\right)^{\!2}\!,

(1)

where
\smash{(r,\theta)}
denote plane-polar coordinates, and
\Phi
is the magnetic flux linked with the solenoid. The axis of the solenoid defines the
z
-axis. Exploiting the translational symmetry of the problem, AB factor out the motion along the
z
direction, obtaining a complete set of eigenfunctions
F_{\alpha}(kr,\theta)
of
H
with eigenvalue
\smash{E=\hbar^{2}k^{2}/(2m)}
. Here,

F_{\alpha}(r,\theta)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-\kern 1.00006pti)^{|n+\alpha|}J_{|n+\alpha|}(r)\kern 1.00006pte^{in\theta},

(2)

where
J_{\nu}(\cdot)
is Bessel’s function of the first kind and

\alpha=-\kern 1.00006pte\Phi/(2\kern 1.00006pt\pi\hbar)

is the nondimensionalized magnetic flux. The propagator for this problem, i.e., the matrix element of the time-evolution operator
\kern 1.00006pt\matrixelement{\vb{r}^\prime}{\exp(-\sm itH/\hbar)}{\vb{r}}
, is directly expressible in terms of
F_{\alpha}
:

K\big(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime};t\big)=\frac{1}{2\kern 1.00006pt\pi i\kern-1.00006pt\tau}\exp[\frac{i}{2\tau}\big(r^{2}+r^{\prime\kern 0.81949pt2}\big)]\kern 1.00006ptF_{\alpha}\!\left(\frac{rr^{\prime}}{\tau},\theta-\theta^{\prime}\right)\!,

(3)

where
\smash{\tau=\hbar\kern 1.00006ptt/m}

(
Kretzschmar 1965
, Eq. (7))
. Therefore, it is useful to evaluate (
2
) in closed form, say, for special values of
\alpha
.

In
(
Aharonov and Bohm 1959
, p. 490)
AB noticed that (
2
) can be summed explicitly for integer and half-integer
\alpha
s. In the latter case they present without derivation the result
(
Aharonov and Bohm 1959
, Eq. (23))

F_{n+1/2}(r,\theta)=\sqrt{\frac{i}{2}}\,\,e^{-\kern 0.81949pti\theta/2-\kern 0.81949ptir\cos\theta}\!\int_{0}^{\sqrt{r\kern 0.81949pt(1+\cos\theta)}}\!\!\!\!dz~e^{iz^{2}},

(4)

which may be related to the error function
\text{erf}(\cdot)
. However, this expression is
not
single-valued, as

F_{n+1/2}(r,\theta+2\kern 1.00006pt\pi)\neq F_{n+1/2}(r,\theta).

Thus (
4
) cannot be a special case of (
2
) (which is single-valued regardless of
\alpha
). Furthermore, the right-hand side of (
4
) does not depend on
n
, the integer-part of
\alpha
. This seems suspicious since
H
depends on
\Phi
(which is
n
-dependent via the definition of
\alpha
), while its eigenfunction (
4
) appears to be independent of
n
.

In what follows, we evaluate the infinite series (
2
) for half-integer
\alpha
s via Laplace transforms, obtaining a result that mainly differs from (
4
) by a
\theta
-dependent prefactor,
viz.
,

\smash{\text{sgn}\big(\kern-1.00006pt\cos(\theta/2)\big)\kern 1.00006pte^{-\kern 0.81949ptin\theta}}
. This missing factor correctly restores single-valuedness of AB’s result as well as its anticipated
n
-dependence.

First, let
n\mapsto n-\lfloor\alpha\rfloor
in (
2
), where
\lfloor\alpha\rfloor
denotes the greatest integer
\leq\alpha
, to obtain

F_{\alpha}(r,\theta)=e^{-\kern 0.81949pti\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\theta}\!\!\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-\kern 1.00006pti)^{|n+\{\alpha\}|}J_{|n+\{\alpha\}|}(r)\kern 1.00006pte^{in\theta}.

(5)

Here,
\smash{\{\alpha\}=\alpha-\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}
is the fractional part of
\alpha
(
\smash{0\leq\{\alpha\}<1}
for any
\alpha
).

For
\smash{\{\alpha\}=0}
, i.e., integer
\alpha
, (
5
) can be evaluated in closed form. For this, note that
\smash{J_{-n}(r)=(-1)^{n}\kern 1.00006ptJ_{n}(r)}
, and

(-\kern 1.00006pti)^{|n|}=(-\kern 1.00006pti)^{n}\begin{cases}\,1,&n\geq 0\\
\,(-1)^{n},&n<0\end{cases},

for any integer
n
. Thus, invoking the Jacobi-Anger expansion
(
Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 2007
, Sec. 8.511.4.)
, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}(r,\theta)=e^{-\kern 0.81949pti\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\theta}\!\!\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\!i^{n}J_{n}(r)\kern 1.00006pte^{in(\theta-\pi)}=e^{-\kern 0.81949pti(\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\theta+r\cos\theta)}\!,

(6)

in agreement with AB after identifying
r\cos\theta=x
, see
(
Aharonov and Bohm 1959
, p. 490)
.

It remains to consider
\smash{\{\alpha\}>0}
only, for which

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}(r,\theta)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{template1}}{=}e^{-\kern 0.81949pti\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\theta}\!\left[\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-\kern 1.00006pti)^{n+\{\alpha\}}J_{n+\{\alpha\}}(r)\kern 1.00006pte^{in\theta}\right.

\displaystyle\kern 56.9055pt\left.+\sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1}(-\kern 1.00006pti)^{-n-\{\alpha\}}J_{-n-\{\alpha\}}(r)\kern 1.00006pte^{in\theta}\right]

(letting
n\mapsto-1-n
in the second sum)

\displaystyle=e^{-\kern 0.81949pti\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\theta}\Big[(-\kern 1.00006pti)^{\{\alpha\}}f_{\{\alpha\}}\big(r,\theta\big)

\displaystyle\kern 48.36958pt+e^{-\kern 0.81949pti\theta}(-\kern 1.00006pti)^{1-\{\alpha\}}f_{1-\{\alpha\}}\big(r,-\kern 1.00006pt\theta\big)\Big],

(7)

where we have introduced the function

f_{\epsilon}(r,\theta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-\kern 1.00006pti)^{n}J_{n+\epsilon}(r)\kern 1.00006pte^{in\theta}.

(8)

Consider its Laplace transform w.r.t.
r
, given by

\mathcal{L}\kern 1.00006pt\big[f_{\epsilon}(r,\theta)\big]=\frac{\big(s+\sqrt{1+s^{2}}\,\big)^{1-\epsilon}}{\sqrt{1+s^{2}}\,\big(s+\sqrt{1+s^{2}}+ie^{i\theta}\big)},

(9)

\smash{\real[s]>0}
. It follows from exploiting the linearity of the Laplace transform, the identity
(
Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 2007
, Sec. 17.13.103.)

\mathcal{L}\kern 1.00006pt\big[J_{\nu}(r)\big]=\frac{\big(s+\sqrt{1+s^{2}}\big)^{-\kern 0.81949pt\nu}}{\sqrt{1+s^{2}}},~\nu>-1,~\real[s]>0,

(10)

and finally the geometric series formula
\sum_{n\geq 0}z^{n}=(1-z)^{-1}
for
|z|<1
. For half-integer flux,
\smash{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor=n}
and
\smash{\{\alpha\}=1/2}
, the Laplace transform of (
Comment on the “Significance of Electromagnetic Potentials in the Quantum Theory”
) reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\kern 1.00006pt\big[F_{n+1/2}(r,\theta)\big]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-\kern 1.00006pti}\,e^{-\kern 0.81949ptin\theta}\kern 1.00006pt\mathcal{L}\kern 1.00006pt\big[f_{1/2}(r,\theta)\big]+\sqrt{-\kern 1.00006pti}\,e^{-\kern 0.81949pti(n+1)\theta}\kern 1.00006pt\mathcal{L}\kern 1.00006pt\big[f_{1/2}(r,-\kern 1.00006pt\theta)\big]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{lapf}}{=}\sqrt{-\kern 1.00006pti}\,e^{-\kern 0.81949ptin\theta}\frac{\sqrt{s+\sqrt{1+s^{2}}}}{\sqrt{1+s^{2}}}\left[\frac{\big(1+e^{-\kern 0.81949pti\theta}\big)\big(s+\sqrt{1+s^{2}}+i\big)}{\big(s+\sqrt{1+s^{2}}+ie^{i\theta}\big)\big(s+\sqrt{1+s^{2}}+ie^{-\kern 0.81949pti\theta}\big)}\right]\!.

(11)

Following careful simplifications, this reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\kern 1.00006pt\big[F_{n+1/2}(r,\theta)\big]=\frac{1}{2}\kern 1.00006pt\sqrt{-\kern 1.00006pti}\,e^{-\kern 0.81949ptin\theta}\big(1+e^{-\kern 0.81949pti\theta}\big)\frac{s+\sqrt{1+s^{2}}+i}{\sqrt{1+s^{2}}\,\sqrt{s+\sqrt{1+s^{2}}}}\cdot\frac{1}{s+i\cos\theta}.

(12)

Now, in order to invert the Laplace transform, note that

\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left[\frac{1}{s+i\cos\theta}\right]=e^{-\kern 0.81949ptir\cos\theta}\quad\text{\cite[cite]{(\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{GH}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}, Sec.\ 17.13.7.)}},

(13)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left[\frac{s+\sqrt{1+s^{2}}+i}{\sqrt{1+s^{2}}\,\sqrt{s+\sqrt{1+s^{2}}}}\right]=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left[\frac{\sqrt{s+\sqrt{1+s^{2}}}}{\sqrt{1+s^{2}}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+i\,\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+s^{2}}\,\sqrt{s+\sqrt{1+s^{2}}}}\right]=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi r}}\,e^{ir}\!,

(14)

given

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left[\frac{\big(s+\sqrt{1+s^{2}}\kern 1.00006pt\big)^{\pm 1/2}}{\sqrt{1+s^{2}}}\right]\overset{\eqref{Lap}}{=}J_{\mp 1/2}(r)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi r}}\,\matrixquantity{\lx@physics@matrix\displaystyle\cos\\[-3pt]\displaystyle\sin\endlx@physics@matrix}\kern 1.00006pt(r).

(15)

Applying the Laplace-convolution (or Faltung) theorem,

\mathcal{L}^{-1}[f(s)\kern 1.00006ptg(s)]=\mathcal{L}^{-1}[f(s)]*\mathcal{L}^{-1}[g(s)]\kern 1.00006pt,

to (
12
), we are thus led to

F_{n+1/2}(r,\theta)=\frac{e^{-\kern 0.81949ptin\theta}}{\sqrt{2\kern 1.00006pt\pi i}}\kern 1.00006pt\big(1+e^{-\kern 0.81949pti\theta}\big)\int_{0}^{r}\frac{dr^{\prime}}{\sqrt{r^{\prime}}}\,e^{ir^{\prime}}\!e^{-\kern 0.81949pti(r-r^{\prime})\cos\theta}\!.

(16)

Observe that (
16
) vanishes at
\smash{\theta=\pi}
due to the factor in parenthesis. But for
\smash{\theta\neq\pi}
, it follows that
\smash{1+\cos\theta>0}
, hence substituting
\smash{z=\sqrt{r^{\prime}(1+\cos\theta)}}
in the above integral yields

\displaystyle F_{n+1/2}(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi i}}\,\frac{1+e^{-\kern 0.81949pti\theta}}{\sqrt{1+\cos\theta}}\,e^{-\kern 0.81949ptin\theta-\kern 0.81949ptir\cos\theta}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\!\int_{0}^{\sqrt{r\kern 0.81949pt(1+\cos\theta)}}\!\!\!\!\!\!dz~e^{iz^{2}}\!.

(17)

To complete the calculation, note that
1+e^{-\kern 0.81949pti\theta}=2\kern 1.00006pte^{-\kern 0.81949pti\theta/2}\cos(\theta/2),
and
\smash{\sqrt{1+\cos\theta}=\sqrt{2}\,|\cos(\theta/2)|}
, which together imply the final result:

\displaystyle F_{n+1/2}(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi i}}\,\text{sgn}\!\left(\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\right)e^{-\kern 0.81949pti(n+1/2)\theta-\kern 0.81949ptir\cos\theta}

\displaystyle\kern 71.13188pt\times\!\int_{0}^{\sqrt{r\kern 0.81949pt(1+\cos\theta)}}\!\!\!\!\!\!dz~e^{iz^{2}}\!,

(18)

where
\text{sgn}(\kern 1.00006pt\cdot\kern 1.00006pt)
is the signum function. Clearly, (
Comment on the “Significance of Electromagnetic Potentials in the Quantum Theory”
) is a near duplicate of (
4
), modulo
\theta
-dependent factors that had hitherto escaped notice. These insure that (
Comment on the “Significance of Electromagnetic Potentials in the Quantum Theory”
) is single-valued, unlike the function defined by (
4
).

In any case, we emphasize that none of AB’s
physical
conclusions rely on (
4
), and are therefore unaffected. This brief comment is meant in no way to denigrate AB’s discovery, whose significance can hardly be overemphasised.

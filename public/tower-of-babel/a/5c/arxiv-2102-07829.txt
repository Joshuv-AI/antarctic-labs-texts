A transmission problem for waves under time-varying delay and nonlinear weight
Carlos A. S. Nonato, Carlos A. Raposo, Waldemar D. Bastos

Abstract
. This manuscript focus on in the transmission problem for one dimensional waves with nonlinear weights on the frictional damping and time-varying delay. We prove global existence of solutions using Kato’s variable norm technique and we show the exponential stability by the energy method with the construction of a suitable Lyapunov functional.

Primary 35L20; 35B40; Secondary 93D15

1.
Introduction

In this paper we investigate global existence and decay properties of solutions for a transmission problem for waves with nonlinear weights and time-varying delay. We consider the following system

\begin{gathered}u_{tt}(x,t)-au_{xx}(x,t)+\mu_{1}(t)u_{t}(x,t)+\mu_{2}(t)u_{t}(x,t-\tau(t))=0\,\,\mbox{in }\Omega\times]0,\infty[,\\
v_{tt}(x,t)-bv_{xx}(x,t)=0\,\,\mbox{in }]L_{1},L_{2}[\times]0,\infty[,\end{gathered}

(1.1)

where
0<L_{1}<L_{2}<L_{3}
,
\Omega=]0,L_{1}[\cup]L_{2},L_{3}[
and
a
,
b
are positive constants.

The system (
1.1
) is subjected to the transmission conditions

\begin{gathered}u(L_{i},t)=v(L_{i},t),\quad i=1,2\\
au_{x}(L_{i},t)=bv_{x}(L_{i},t),\quad i=1,2,\end{gathered}

(1.2)

the boundary conditions

u(0,t)=u(L_{3},t)=0

(1.3)

and initial conditions

\begin{gathered}u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x)\quad\mbox{on }\Omega,\\
u_{t}(x,t-\tau(0))=f_{0}(x,t-\tau(0))\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times]0,\tau(0)[,\\
v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\quad v_{t}(x,0)=v_{1}(x)\quad\mbox{on }]L_{1},L_{2}[,\end{gathered}

(1.4)

where the initial datum
\left(u_{0},u_{1},v_{0},v_{1},f_{0}\right)
belongs to a suitable Sobolev space.

Here
0<\tau(t)
is the time-varying delay and
\mu_{1}(t)
and
\mu_{2}(t)
are nonlinear weights acting on the frictional damping. As in
[
20
]
, we assume that

\tau(t)\in W^{2,\infty}([0,T]),\quad\forall T>0

(1.5)

and that there exist positive constants
\tau_{0}
,
\tau_{1}
and
d
satisfying

0<\tau_{0}\leq\tau(t)\leq\tau_{1},\,\,\,\tau^{\prime}(t)\leq d<1,\quad\forall t>0.

(1.6)

We are interested in proving the exponential stability for the problem (
1.1
)-(
1.4
). In order to obtain this, we will assume that

\max\{1,\frac{a}{b}\}<\frac{L_{1}+L_{3}-L_{2}}{2(L_{2}-L_{1})}.

(1.7)

As described in
[
6
]
, the assumption (
1.7
) gives the relationship between the boundary regions and the transmission permitted. It can be also seen as a restriction on the wave speeds of the two equations and the damped part of the domain. It is known that for Timoshenko systems
[
25
]
and Bresse systems
[
3
]
the wave speeds always control the decay rate of the solution. It is an interesting open question to investigate the behavior of the solution when (
1.7
) is not satisfied.

Time delay is the property of a physical system by which the response to an applied force is delayed in its effect, and the central question is that delays source can destabilize a system that is asymptotically stable in the absence of delays, see
[
8
,
7
,
9
,
26
]
.

Transmission problems are closely related to the design of material components, attracting considerable attention in recent years, e.g., in the analysis of damping mechanisms in the metallurgical industry or smart materials technology, see
[
4
,
24
]
and the references therein. Studies of fluid structure interaction and the added mass effect, also known as virtual mass effect, hydrodynamic mass, and hydroelastic vibration of structures, started with H. Lamb
[
14
]
who investigated the vibrations of a thin elastic circular plate in contact with water. Experimental study of impact on composite plates with fluid-structure interaction was investigated in
[
13
]
. From the mathematical point of view a transmission problem for wave propagation consists on a hyperbolic equation for which the corresponding elliptic operator has discontinuous coefficients.

We consider the wave propagation over bodies consisting of two physically different materials, one purely elastic and another subject to frictional damping. The type of wave propagation generated by mixed materials originates a transmission (or diffraction) problem.

To the best of our knowledge, the first contribution in literature for transmission problem with a time delay
was given by A. Benseghir in
[
6
]
. More precisely, in
[
6
]
the transmission problem

\begin{gathered}u_{tt}-au_{xx}+\mu_{1}u_{t}(x,t)+\mu_{2}u_{t}(x,t-\tau)=0,\,\,\mbox{in }\Omega\times]0,\infty[,\\
v_{tt}-bv_{xx}=0,\,\,\mbox{in }]L_{1},L_{2}[\times]0,\infty[.\end{gathered}

(1.8)

with constant weights
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
and time delay
\tau>0
was studied. Under an appropriate assumption on the weights of the two feedbacks (
\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}
), it was proved the well-posedness of the system and, under condition (
1.7
), it was established an exponential decay result.

The result in
[
6
]
were improved by S. Zitouni et al.
[
27
]
. There, the authors considered the problem with a time-varying delay

of the form

\begin{gathered}u_{tt}-au_{xx}+\mu_{1}u_{t}(x,t)+\mu_{2}u_{t}(x,t-\tau(t))=0,\,\,\mbox{in }\Omega\times]0,\infty[,\\
v_{tt}-bv_{xx}=0,\,\,\mbox{in }]L_{1},L_{2}[\times]0,\infty[\end{gathered}

(1.9)

and proved the global existence and exponential stability under suitable assumptions on the delay term and assumption (
1.7
).
Without delay, systems (
1.8
), (
1.9
) has been investigated in
[
5
]
.

The transmission problem with history and delay was considered by G. Li et al.,
[
17
]
where the equations were expressed as

\begin{gathered}u_{tt}\!-\!au_{xx}\!+\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!g(s)u_{xx}(x,t-s)ds\!+\!\mu_{1}u_{t}(x,t)\!+\!\mu_{2}u_{t}(x,t-\tau)=0,\,\mbox{in }\Omega\times]0,\infty[,\\
v_{tt}\!-\!bv_{xx}=0,\,\mbox{in }]L_{1},L_{2}[\times]0,\infty[.\end{gathered}

(1.10)

Under suitable assumptions on the delay term and on the function

, the authors proved an exponential stability result for two cases. In the first case, they considered
\mu_{2}<\mu_{1}
and for second case, they assumed that
\mu_{2}=\mu_{1}
.

S. Zitouni et al.,

In

was proved existence and the uniqueness of the solution by using the semigroup theory and the exponential stability of the solution by the energy method for the following problem

\begin{gathered}u_{tt}\!-\!au_{xx}\!+\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!g(s)u_{xx}(x,t\!-\!s)ds\!+\!\mu_{1}u_{t}(x,t)\!+\!\mu_{2}u_{t}(x,t-\tau(t))\!=\!0,\,\mbox{in }\Omega\times]0,\infty[,\\
v_{tt}\!-\!bv_{xx}\!=\!0,\,\mbox{in }]L_{1},L_{2}[\times]0,\infty[.\end{gathered}

(1.11)

Stability to localized viscoelastic transmission problem was considered by Muñoz Rivera et al.,
[
19
]
where they considered

\begin{gathered}\rho\phi_{tt}+\sigma_{x}=0,\\
\sigma(x,t)=\alpha(x)\varphi_{x}-k(x)\varphi_{xt}-\beta(x)\varphi_{xt}=0.\end{gathered}

(1.12)

In
[
19
]
the authors investigated the effect of the positions of the dissipative mechanisms on a bar with three component
]0,L_{0}[,\,]L_{0},L_{1}[,\,]L_{1},L[
, and showed that the system is exponentially stable if and only if the viscous component is not in the center of the bar. In other case, they showed the lack of exponential stability, and that the solutions still decay but just polynomially to zero.

The case of time-varying delay has already been considered in other works, such as
[
22
,
12
,
16
,
28
]
. Wave equations with time-varying delay and nonlinear weights was considered in the recent work of Barros et al.,
[

where was studied the equation given by

u_{tt}-u_{xx}+\mu_{1}(t)u_{t}+\mu_{2}(t)u_{t}(x,t-\tau(t))=0,\,\,\mbox{in }]0,L[\times]0,+\infty[.

(1.13)

Under proper conditions on nonlinear weights
\mu_{1}(t),\mu_{2}(t)
and
\tau(t)
, authors proved global existence and an estimate for the decay rate of the energy.

In the present work we improve the results in
[
27
]
where, for constant weights
\mu_{1}(t)=\mu_{1}
,
\mu_{2}(t)=\mu_{2}
and under adequate assumptions regarding the weight and time-varying delay, was proved the well posedness and singularity of solutions by using the semigroup theory. Authors also showed exponential stability by introducing an appropriate Lyapunov functional.

Here we consider a transmission problem with nonlinear weights and time-varying delay, which is the main characteristic of this work. Although there are some works on laminated beam and on Timoshenko system with delay, all of them consider constant weights, i.e.,
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are constants. To the best of our knowledge, there is no result for these systems with nonlinear weights. Moreover, since the weights are nonlinear, a difficulty comes in: the operator is nonautonomous. This makes hard the use semigroup theory to study well-posedness. To overcome it we use the Kato’s variable norm technique together with semigroup theory to show that the system is well-posed.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In section
2
we introduce some notations and prove the dissipative property for the energy of the system. In the section
3
, by using Kato’s variable norm technique and under some restriction on the non-linear weights and the time-varying delay, the system is shown to be well-posed. In section
4
, we present the result of exponential stability by energy methods, and by using suitable sophisticated estimates for multipliers to construct an appropriated Lyapunov functional.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

We start by setting the following hypothesis:

(H1)

\mu_{1}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow]0,+\infty[
is a non-increasing function of class
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
satisfying

\left|\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{1}(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right|\leq M_{1},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

(2.1)

where
M_{1}>0
is a constant.

(H2)

\mu_{2}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a function of class
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, which is not necessarily positive or monotone, such that

\displaystyle\left|\mu_{2}(t)\right|\leq\beta\mu_{1}(t),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left|\mu^{\prime}_{2}(t)\right|\leq M_{2}\mu_{1}(t),

(2.3)

for some
0<\beta<\sqrt{1-d}
and
M_{2}>0
.

As in Nicaise and Pignotti
[
20
]
we introduce the new variable

z(x,\rho,t)=u_{t}(x,t-\tau(t)\rho),\quad(x,\rho)\in\Omega\times]0,1[,\;t>0.

(2.4)

It is easily verified that the new variable satisfies

\tau(t)z_{t}(x,\rho,t)+(1-\tau^{\prime}(t)\rho)z_{\rho}(x,\rho,t)=0

and the problem (
1.1
) is equivalent to

\begin{gathered}u_{tt}(x,t)-au_{xx}(x,t)+\mu_{1}(t)u_{t}(x,t)+\mu_{2}(t)z(x,1,t)=0\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times]0,\infty[,\\
v_{tt}(x,t)-bv_{xx}(x,t)=0\quad\mbox{in }]L_{1},L_{2}[\times]0,\infty[,\\
\tau(t)z_{t}(x,\rho,t)+(1-\tau^{\prime}(t)\rho)z_{\rho}(x,\rho,t)=0\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times]0,1[\times]0,\infty[.\end{gathered}

(2.5)

This system is subject to the transmission conditions

\begin{gathered}u(L_{i},t)=v(L_{i},t),\quad i=1,2,\\
au_{x}(L_{i},t)=bv_{x}(L_{i},t),\quad i=1,2,\end{gathered}

(2.6)

the boundary conditions

u(0,t)=u(L_{3},t)=0

(2.7)

and the initial conditions

\begin{gathered}u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x)\quad\mbox{on }\Omega,\\
v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\quad v_{t}(x,0)=v_{1}(x)\quad\mbox{on }\Omega,\\
z(x,\rho,0)=u_{t}(x,-\tau(0)\rho)=f_{0}(x,-\tau(0)\rho),\quad(x,\rho)\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times]0,1[.\end{gathered}

(2.8)

For any regular solution of (
2.5
), we define the energy as

\displaystyle E_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(|u_{t}(x,t)|^{2}+a|u_{x}(x,t)|^{2}\right)\,dx,

\displaystyle E_{2}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\left(|v_{t}(x,t)|^{2}+b|v_{x}(x,t)|^{2}\right)\,dx.

The total energy is defined by

E(t)=E_{1}(t)+E_{2}(t)+\frac{\xi(t)\tau(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}(x,\rho,t)\,d\rho\,dx,

(2.9)

where

\xi(t)=\bar{\xi}\mu_{1}(t)

(2.10)

is a non-increasing function of class
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
\bar{\xi}
be a positive constant such that

\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{1-d}}<\bar{\xi}<2-\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{1-d}}.

(2.11)

Our first result states that the energy is a non-increasing function.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(u,v,z)
be a solution to the system (
2.5
)-(
2.8

) satisfies

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq-\mu_{1}(t)\left(1-\frac{\bar{\xi}}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2\sqrt{1-d}}\right)\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,dx

(2.12)

\displaystyle\quad-\mu_{1}(t)\left(\frac{\bar{\xi}(1-\tau^{\prime}(t))}{2}-\frac{\beta\sqrt{1-d}}{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}z_{1}^{2}(x,\rho,t)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Proof.

Multiplying the first and second equations of (
2.5
) by
u_{t}(x,t)
and
v_{t}(x,t)
, integrating on
\Omega
and
]L_{1},L_{2}[
respectively and using integration by parts, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\left(u_{t}^{2}+au_{x}^{2}\right)\,dx=

\displaystyle-\mu_{1}(t)\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,dx-\mu_{2}(t)\int_{\Omega}z(x,1,t)u_{t}\,dx+a\left[u_{x}u_{t}\right]_{\partial\Omega},

(2.13)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\left(v_{t}^{2}+bv_{x}^{2}\right)\,dx=b\left[v_{x}v_{t}\right]_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}.

(2.14)

Now multiplying the third equation of (

) by
\xi(t)z(x,\rho,t)
and integrating on

\displaystyle\tau(t)\xi(t)\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}z_{t}(x,\rho,t)z(x,\rho,t)\,d\rho\,dx=-\frac{\xi(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\tau^{\prime}(t)\rho)\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}(z(x,\rho,t))^{2}\,d\rho\,dx.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\xi(t)\tau(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}(x,\rho,t)\,d\rho\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}(z^{2}(x,0,t)-z^{2}(x,1,t))\,dx

(2.15)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\xi(t)\tau^{\prime}(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}(x,1,t)\,d\rho\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\xi^{\prime}(t)\tau(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}(x,\rho,t)\,d\rho\,dx.

From (
2.9
), (
2.13
), (
2.14
), (
2.15
) and using the conditions (

) and (

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,dx-\frac{\xi(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}z^{2}(x,1,t)\,dx+\frac{\xi(t)\tau^{\prime}(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}z^{2}(x,1,t)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\xi^{\prime}(t)\tau(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}(x,\rho,t)\,d\rho\,dx-\mu_{1}(t)\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,dx-\mu_{2}(t)\int_{\Omega}z(x,1,t)u_{t}\,dx.

Due to Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(t)\int_{\Omega}z(x,1,t)u_{t}\,dx\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\mu_{2}(t)\right|}{2\sqrt{1-d}}\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,dx+\frac{\left|\mu_{2}(t)\right|\sqrt{1-d}}{2}\int_{\Omega}z^{2}(x,1,t)\,dx.

(2.17)

Inserting (
2.17

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq-\left(\mu_{1}(t)-\frac{\xi(t)}{2}-\frac{\left|\mu_{2}(t)\right|}{2\sqrt{1-d}}\right)\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;-\left(\frac{\xi(t)}{2}-\frac{\xi(t)\tau^{\prime}(t)}{2}-\frac{\left|\mu_{2}(t)\right|\sqrt{1-d}}{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}z^{2}(x,1,t)\,dx

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\frac{\xi^{\prime}(t)\tau(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}(x,\rho,t)\,d\rho\,dx

\displaystyle\leq-\mu_{1}(t)\left(1-\frac{\bar{\xi}}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2\sqrt{1-d}}\right)\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;-\mu_{1}(t)\left(\frac{\bar{\xi}(1-\tau^{\prime}(t))}{2}-\frac{\beta\sqrt{1-d}}{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}z^{2}(x,1,t)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 0.

3.
Global solution

In this section, our goal is to prove existence and uniqueness of solutions to the system (
2.5
) - (
2.8
). This is the content of Theorem

.

We begin by introducing the vector function
U=(u,v,\varphi,\psi,z)^{T}
, where
\varphi(x,t)=u_{t}(x,t)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}U_{t}-\mathcal{A}(t)U=0,\\

\mathcal{A}(t)\,U=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\varphi(x,t)\\
\psi(x,t)\\
au_{xx}(x,t)-\mu_{1}(t)\varphi(x,t)-\mu_{2}(t)z(x,1,t)\\
bv_{xx}(x,t)\\
-\frac{1-\tau^{\prime}(t)\rho}{\tau(t)}z_{\rho}(x,\rho,t)\end{array}\right).

(3.2)

Now, taking into account the conditions (
1.2
)-(

15

17

\displaystyle X_{*}=

\displaystyle\{(u,v)\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(]L_{1},L_{2}[)/u(0)=u(L_{3})=0,u(L_{i})=v(L_{i}),au_{x}(L_{i})=bv_{x}(L_{i}),i=1,2\}.

We define the phase space as

\mathcal{H}=X_{*}\times L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(]L_{1},L_{2}[)\times L^{2}(\Omega\times]0,1[)

\displaystyle\langle U,\hat{U}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{\Omega}\left(\varphi\hat{\varphi}+au_{x}\hat{u}_{x}\right)\,dx+\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\left(\psi\hat{\psi}+bv_{x}\hat{v}_{x}\right)\,dx+\xi(t)\tau(t)\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}z\hat{z}\,d\rho\,dx,

for

and

The domain

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{A}(t))=\{

(3.5)

]
,

]

]

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that

(i)

is dense subset of
\mathcal{H}
,

(ii)

(

(iii)

Theorem 3.2

[Global solution]

Our goal is then to check the above assumptions for problem (

First, we show that

The proof we will follow method used in

be orthogonal to all elements of

, namely

\displaystyle 0=\langle U,\hat{U}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\varphi\hat{\varphi}+au_{x}\hat{u}_{x}\right)\,dx+\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\left(\psi\hat{\psi}+bv_{x}\hat{v}_{x}\right)\,dx+\xi(t)\tau(t)\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}z\hat{z}\,d\rho\,dx,

for

We first take

and

U=(0,0,0,0,z)^{T}\in D(\mathcal{A}(0))
and therefore, from (
3.6
), we deduce that

\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}z\hat{z}\,d\rho\,dx=0.

Since
C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\Omega\times]0,1[\right)

, then, it follows then that
\hat{z}=0
. Similarly, let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, then
U=(0,0,\varphi,0,0)^{T}\in D(\mathcal{A}(0))
, which implies from (

) that

\int_{\Omega}\varphi\hat{\varphi}\,dx=0.

So, as above, it follows that

. In the same way, by taking

and by density of

.

Finally, for
(u,v)\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\Omega\times]L_{1},L_{2}[\right)
(then

Since

is dense in

, we deduce that
\left(\hat{u}_{x},\hat{v}_{x}\right)=(0,0)
because
\left(\hat{u},\hat{v}\right)\in X_{*}
.

We consequently have

D(\mathcal{A}(0))\,\,\text{is dense in }\mathcal{H}.

(3.7)

Now, we show that the operator
\mathcal{A}(t)
generates a

semigroup in
\mathcal{H}
for a fixed

.

We calculate
\langle\mathcal{A}(t)U,U\rangle_{t}
for a fixed
t
. Take
U=(u,v,\varphi,\psi,z)^{T}\in D(\mathcal{A}(t))
. Then

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}(t)U,U\rangle_{t}=

\displaystyle-\mu_{1}(t)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\,dx-\mu_{2}(t)\int_{\Omega}z(x,1)\varphi\,dx-\frac{\xi(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\tau^{\prime}(t)\rho)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\rho}z^{2}(x,\rho)\,d\rho\,dx.

Since

\left(1-\tau^{\prime}(t)\rho\right)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\rho}z^{2}(x,\rho)=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\left(\left(1-\tau^{\prime}(t)\rho\right)z^{2}(x,\rho)\right)+\tau^{\prime}(t)z^{2}(x,\rho),

we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(1-\tau^{\prime}(t)\rho\right)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\rho}z^{2}(x,\rho)\,d\rho=

\displaystyle\left(1-\tau^{\prime}(t)\right)z^{2}(x,1)-z^{2}(x,0)+\tau^{\prime}(t)\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}(x,\rho)\,d\rho.

Whereupon

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}(t)U,U\rangle_{t}=

\displaystyle-\mu_{1}(t)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\,dx-\mu_{2}(t)\int_{\Omega}z(x,1)\varphi\,dx+\frac{\xi(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle-\frac{\xi(t)(1-\tau^{\prime}(t))}{2}\int_{\Omega}z^{2}(x,1)\,dx-\frac{\xi(t)\tau^{\prime}(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}(x,\rho)\,d\rho\,dx.

Therefore, from (
2.17
), we deduce

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}(t)U,U\rangle_{t}\leq

\displaystyle-\mu_{1}(t)\left(1-\frac{\bar{\xi}}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2\sqrt{1-d}}\right)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle-\mu_{1}(t)\left(\frac{\bar{\xi}(1-\tau^{\prime}(t))}{2}-\frac{\beta\sqrt{1-d}}{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}z^{2}(x,1)\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{\xi(t)|\tau^{\prime}(t)|}{2\tau(t)}\tau(t)\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}(x,\rho)\,d\rho\,dx.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}(t)U,U\rangle_{t}\leq

\displaystyle-\mu_{1}(t)\left(1-\frac{\bar{\xi}}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2\sqrt{1-d}}\right)\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle-\mu_{1}(t)\left(\frac{\bar{\xi}(1-\tau^{\prime}(t))}{2}-\frac{\beta\sqrt{1-d}}{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}z^{2}(x,1)\,dx

\displaystyle+\kappa(t)\langle U,U\rangle_{t},

where

From (
2.12
) we conclude that

\langle\mathcal{A}(t)U,U\rangle_{t}-\kappa(t)\langle U,U\rangle_{t}\leq 0,

(3.8)

which means that operator
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(t)=\mathcal{A}(t)-\kappa(t)I
is dissipative.

which is solution of

that is, the entries of

\displaystyle\lambda u-\varphi=f_{1},

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lambda v-\psi=f_{2},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\lambda\varphi-au_{xx}+\mu_{1}(t)\varphi+\mu_{2}(t)z(x,1)=f_{3},

(3.11)

\displaystyle\lambda\psi-bv_{xx}=f_{4},

(3.12)

\displaystyle\lambda z+\frac{1-\tau^{\prime}(t)\rho}{\tau(t)}z_{\rho}=f_{5}.

(3.13)

Suppose that we have found
u
and
v
with the appropriated regularity. Therefore, from (
3.9
) and (

) we have

\displaystyle\varphi=\lambda u-f_{1},

(3.14)

\displaystyle\psi=\lambda v-f_{2}.

(3.15)

It is clear that

and
\psi\in H^{1}(]L_{1},L_{2}[)
. Furthermore, if
\tau^{\prime}(t)\neq 0
, following the same approach as in

Otherwise,

\displaystyle z(x,1)

\displaystyle=\varphi e^{\sigma(1,t)}+\tau(t)e^{\sigma(1,t)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f_{5}(x,s)}{1-s\tau^{\prime}(s)}e^{-\sigma(s,t)}\,ds

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda u-f_{1}\right)e^{\sigma(1,t)}+\tau(t)e^{\sigma(1,t)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f_{5}(x,s)}{1-s\tau^{\prime}(s)}e^{-\sigma(s,t)}\,ds

\displaystyle g_{1}:=

\displaystyle f_{3}+\lambda f_{1}+\mu_{1}(t)f_{1}+\mu_{2}(t)f_{1}e^{\sigma(1,t)}

\displaystyle-\mu_{2}(t)\tau(t)e^{\sigma(1,t)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f_{5}(x,s)}{1-s\tau^{\prime}(s)}e^{-\sigma(s,t)}\,ds,

In order to solve (

It is easy to verify that

for (

and

. Again as
\kappa(t)>0
, this prove that

\lambda I-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(t)=\left(\lambda+\kappa(t)\right)I-\mathcal{A}(t)\ \text{is surjective}

for any

and

.

To complete the proof of (iii), it suffices to show that

\dfrac{\|\Phi\|_{t}}{\|\Phi\|_{s}}\leq e^{\frac{c}{2\tau_{0}}|t-s|},\quad t,s\in[0,T],

(3.21)

where
\Phi=(u,v,\varphi,\psi,z)^{T}
,

is a positive constant and
\|\cdot\|
is the norm associated the inner product (
3.3
). For all

, we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{t}^{2}-\|\Phi\|_{s}^{2}e^{\frac{c}{\tau_{0}}|t-s|}=

\displaystyle\left(1-e^{\frac{c}{\tau_{0}}|t-s|}\right)\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(\varphi^{2}+au_{x}^{2}\right)dx+\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\left(\psi^{2}+bv_{x}^{2}\right)dx\right]

\displaystyle+\left(\xi(t)\tau(t)-\xi(s)\tau(s)e^{\frac{c}{\tau_{0}}|t-s|}\right)\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}(x,\rho)\,d\rho\,dx.

It is clear that

. Now we will prove

for some

. In order to do this , first observe that

for some

. Since
\xi

Using (
1.5

3.21
) and therefore
(iii)
follows.

Moreover, as
\kappa^{\prime}(t)=\frac{\tau^{\prime}(t)\tau^{\prime\prime}(t)}{2\tau(t)\sqrt{1+\tau^{\prime}(t)^{2}}}-\frac{\tau^{\prime}(t)\sqrt{1+\tau^{\prime}(t)^{2}}}{2\tau(t)^{2}}
is bounded on

0\\
-\mu_{1}^{\prime}(t)\varphi-\mu_{2}^{\prime}(t)z(\cdot,1)\\
0\\

by (
1.5
) and (
2.11
). Thus

where

into

Then, (

.
The requested solution of (

because

\displaystyle=\kappa(t)e^{\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)\,ds}\tilde{U}(t)+e^{\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)\,ds}\tilde{U}_{t}(t)

4.
Exponential stability

This section is dedicated to study of the asymptotic behavior. The main goal of this section is to study the stability of solutions to the system (
2.5
)-(
2.8
). This is the content of Theorem
4.4
where we show that the solution of problem (
2.5
)-(
2.8
) is exponentially stable. Our effort consists in building a suitable Lyapunov functional by the energy method. For this goal we present several technical lemmas.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(u,v,z)
be a solution of (
2.5
)-(
2.8
), then for any
\varepsilon_{1}>0
and
c_{1}
is the Poincaré’s constant, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)\leq

\displaystyle-\left(a-\mu_{1}^{2}(0)c_{1}^{2}\varepsilon_{1}\right)\int_{\Omega}u_{x}^{2}\,dx-b\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}v_{x}^{2}\,dx

(4.1)

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{1}}\right)\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,dx+\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}v_{t}^{2}\,dx+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\varepsilon_{1}}\int_{\Omega}z^{2}(x,1,t)\,dx,

where

\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)=\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}\,dx+\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}vv_{t}\,dx.

(4.2)

Proof.

Differentiating
\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)
and using (
2.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,dx-a\int_{\Omega}u_{x}^{2}\,dx-\mu_{1}(t)\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}\,dx-\mu_{2}(t)\int_{\Omega}uz(x,1,t)\,dx+\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}v_{t}^{2}\,dx-b\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}v_{x}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,dx-a\int_{\Omega}u_{x}^{2}\,dx+\left|\mu_{1}(t)\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}\,dx\right|+\left|\mu_{2}(t)\int_{\Omega}uz(x,1,t)\,dx\right|+\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}v_{t}^{2}\,dx-b\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}v_{x}^{2}\,dx.

From hypothesis (H1) and (H2), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,dx-a\int_{\Omega}u_{x}^{2}\,dx+\mu_{1}(0)\left|\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}\,dx\right|

(4.3)

\displaystyle+\beta\mu_{1}(0)\left|\int_{\Omega}uz(x,1,t)\,dx\right|+\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}v_{t}^{2}\,dx-b\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}v_{x}^{2}\,dx.

By using the conditions (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle u^{2}(x,t)=\left(\int_{0}^{x}u_{x}(s,t)\,ds\right)^{2}\leq L_{1}\int_{0}^{L_{1}}u_{x}^{2}(x,t)\,dx,\quad x\in[0,L_{1}],

\displaystyle u^{2}(x,t)\leq(L_{3}-L_{2})\int_{L_{2}}^{L_{3}}u_{x}^{2}(x,t)\,dx,\quad x\in[L_{2},L_{3}],

which imply the following Poincaré’s inequality

\int_{\Omega}u^{2}(x,t)\,dx\leq c_{1}^{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{x}^{2}\,dx,\quad x\in\Omega,

(4.4)

where
c_{1}=\max\{L_{1},L_{3}-L_{2}\}
is the Poincaré’s constant. Using Young’s inequality and (
4.4
), we have

\mu_{1}(0)\left|\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}\,dx\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}\mu_{1}^{2}(0)c_{1}^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{x}^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{1}}\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,dx

(4.5)

and

\beta\mu_{1}(0)\left|\int_{\Omega}uz(x,1,t)\,dx\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}\mu_{1}^{2}(0)c_{1}^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{x}^{2}\,dx+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\varepsilon_{1}}\int_{\Omega}z^{2}(x,1,t)\,dx.

(4.6)

Substituting (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) in (
4.3
) we conclude the lemma.
∎

Now, inspired by
[

]
, we introduce the functional

q(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}x-\dfrac{L_{1}}{2},&x\in[0,L_{1}],\\
\\
\dfrac{L_{2}-L_{3}-L_{1}}{2(L_{2}-L_{1})}(x-L_{1})+\dfrac{L_{1}}{2},&x\in[L_{1},L_{2}],\\
\\
x-\dfrac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{2},&x\in[L_{2},L_{3}].\end{array}\right.

It is easy to see that

We have the following result.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(u,v,z)
be a solution of (
2.5
)-(
2.8

\varepsilon_{2}>0

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{2}}\right)\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,dx+\left(\frac{a}{2}+M^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}(0)\varepsilon_{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}u_{x}^{2}\,dx+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\varepsilon_{2}}\int_{\Omega}z^{2}(x,1,t)\,dx

(4.8)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\left[L_{1}u_{t}^{2}(L_{1},t)+(L_{3}-L_{2})u_{t}^{2}(L_{2},t)\right]-\frac{a}{4}\left[L_{1}u_{x}^{2}(L_{1},t)+(L_{3}-L_{2})u_{x}^{2}(L_{2},t)\right],

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}_{3}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{L_{2}-L_{3}-L_{1}}{4(L_{2}-L_{1})}\left(\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}v_{t}^{2}\,dx+b\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}v_{x}^{2}\,dx\right)+\frac{1}{4}\left[L_{1}v_{t}^{2}(L_{1},t)+(L_{3}-L_{2})v_{t}^{2}(L_{2},t)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{b}{4}\left[L_{1}v_{x}^{2}(L_{1},t)+(L_{3}-L_{2})v_{x}^{2}(L_{2},t)\right],

\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)=-\int_{\Omega}q(x)u_{x}u_{t}\,dx\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{I}_{3}(t)=-\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}q(x)v_{x}v_{t}\,dx.

(4.10)

Proof.

\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}q(x)u_{xt}u_{t}\,dx-a\int_{\Omega}q(x)u_{xx}u_{x}\,dx

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)

By the same argument, taking the derivative of
\mathcal{I}_{3}(t)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}q^{\prime}(x)v_{t}^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\left[q(x)v_{t}^{2}\right]_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}+\frac{b}{2}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}q^{\prime}(x)v_{x}^{2}\,dx-\frac{b}{2}\left[q(x)v_{x}^{2}\right]_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{2}-L_{3}-L_{1}}{4(L_{2}-L_{1})}\left(\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}v_{t}^{2}\,dx+b\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}v_{x}^{2}\,dx\right)+\frac{1}{4}\left[L_{1}v_{t}^{2}(L_{1},t)+(L_{3}-L_{2})v_{t}^{2}(L_{2},t)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{b}{4}\left[L_{1}v_{x}^{2}(L_{1},t)+(L_{3}-L_{2})v_{x}^{2}(L_{2},t)\right]

Hence, the proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Proof.

Let us define the Lyapunov functional

\mathcal{L}(t)=NE(t)(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{3}N_{i}\mathcal{I}_{i}(t)+\mathcal{J}(t),

(4.16)

where

,

(4.17)

It follows from the transmission conditions (
2.6
) that

a^{2}u_{x}^{2}(L_{i},t)=b^{2}v_{x}^{2}(L_{i},t),\quad i=1,2.

(4.18)

Using the estimates (
4.1
), (
4.8
), (
4.9
), (
4.13
), (
4.17
) and the equation (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{L}(t)\leq

\displaystyle-\left[KN-\left(1+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{1}}\right)N_{1}-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{2}}\right)N_{2}-\bar{\xi}\right]\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,dx

(4.19)

\displaystyle-\left(KN-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\varepsilon_{1}}N_{1}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\varepsilon_{2}}N_{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}z^{2}(x,1,t)\,dx

\displaystyle-\left[\left(a-\mu_{1}^{2}(0)c_{1}^{2}\varepsilon_{1}\right)N_{1}-\left(\frac{a}{2}+M^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}(0)\varepsilon_{2}\right)N_{2}\right]\int_{\Omega}u_{x}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle-\left[N_{1}-\frac{L_{2}-L_{3}-L_{1}}{4(L_{2}-L_{1})}N_{3}\right]b\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}v_{x}^{2}\,dx

), we can always find real constants

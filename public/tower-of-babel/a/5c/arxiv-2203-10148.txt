PiTSBiCG: Parallel in Time Stable Bi-Conjugate Gradient Algorithm
Mohamed Kamel RIAHI

Abstract
This paper presents a new algorithm for the parallel in time (PiT) numerical simulation of time dependent partial/ordinary differential equations. We propose a reliable alternative to the well know parareal in time algorithm, by formulating the parallel in time problem algebraically and solve it using an adapted Bi-Conjugate gradient stabilized method. The proposed Parallel in time Stable Bi-Conjugate algorithm (PiTSBiCG) has a great potential in stabilizing the parallel resolution for a variety of problems. In this work, we describe the mathematical approach to the new algorithm and provide numerical evidences that show its superiority to the standard parareal method.

The parareal algorithm benefits from a predictor-corrector scheme to gain acceleration in a shorter time compared to serial computation. In such scheme the prediction is performed by a computationally cheap coarse solver
\mathcal{G}_{\triangle}
while the correction is performed using an accurate high ordered but computationally expensive fine solver
\mathcal{F}_{\triangle}

[0,T]

\lambda_{N}^{k}

2

\mathcal{G}_{\triangle}

\mathcal{F}_{\triangle}
. The method decomposes the time domain in the sense that it allows to divide the propagation of the fine solver over
[0,T]
into propagation on sub-intervals that can be run concurrently and simultaneously on several processors.

2
Parallel in time settings

The main goal behind time parallelization algorithms is to be able to solve a given time dependent problem over a predefined set of sub-intervals. Ideally, one should be able to solve only on these sub-intervals. Unfortunately, and because the sequential nature of the time-evolution problem, this is not possible unless at least these sub-intervals inter-communicate information.

For a positive time
T>0
and a bounded domain
\Omega
with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
, we consider the following Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}y(t,x)-\mathcal{A}(t,y(t,x))=f(t,x)&\hbox{ in }[0,T]\times\Omega\\
y(0,x)=y_{0}.&\hbox{ at }\{0\}\times\Omega\end{cases}

(1)

for which
\mathcal{A}(t,\bullet(t,x))
stands for a differential linear operator, and

is a given source term. The Cauchy problem (
1
) is supplemented with appropriate boundary condition at
\partial\Omega
. It is assumed that (
1
) is well posed and has a unique solution on its computational domain. Therefore, it generates a semi-group of evolution that we shall denote by
\mathcal{F}_{\triangle}
, in such a way
y(t+\Delta t,x)=\mathcal{F}_{\triangle}(y(t,x))
represents the solution at time
t+\Delta t
from a given initial condition

at time

.
Without loss of generality, we assume that the time interval is equality split into

[T_{n},T_{n+1}]

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}y_{n}(t,x)-\mathcal{A}(t,y_{n}(t,x))=f_{n}(t,x)&\hbox{ in }[T_{n},T_{n+1}]\times\Omega\\
y_{n}(T_{n},x)=\lambda_{n}&\hbox{ on }\{T_{n}\}\times\Omega.\end{cases}

(2)

For a given initial condition,
\lambda_{n}
, and source term
f_{n}=f_{|_{[T_{n},T_{n+1}]}}

We, indeed, propose new and robust time parallel algorithm that outperform the parareal algorithm. The new method has also the capability of being applied to any evolution equation, as it is the case for the parareal method. On the other hand, our algorithm performs well with large sub-domains compared to the parareal method.

PiTSBiCG method

The approach we follow in developing our numerical algorithm is based on the nature of the block-non-symmetric linear system (
4
). It is well-known in the computational linear algebra literature (see for instance
[
10
]
) that the most appropriate and efficient linear solvers are BiCG/QMR and their enhanced variant such as GMRes. Our focus goes for the BiCG which uses the Lanczos Biorthogonalization procedure, and process the solution through projection upon the following Krylov vector space

{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{m}=\left\{v_{1},\mathbfcal{F}\cdot v_{1},\cdots,\mathbfcal{F}^{m-1}\cdot v_{1}\right\},

and orthogonal to

{\bf\mathcal{L}}_{m}=\left\{w_{1},(\mathbfcal{F}^{T})\cdot w_{1},\cdots,(\mathbfcal{F}^{T})^{m-1}\cdot w_{1}\right\},

It is worth mentioning that, minimal residual algorithms, such as QMR and GMRES, that are based on matrix algebra factorization, are not suitable to solve (
4
) as of the operators-block-structure of the problem. In the solution process of (
4
) we only consider the matrix-by-vector product., where the operator block structures are neither assembled nor stored. Furthermore, algorithms that do not use factorization techniques can, actually, be used to solve the parallel in time problem (
4

[
11

, or with the use of the BiCG method. Nonetheless, the CGS algorithm which is designed to avoid evaluating the transpose of the linear system my also face accuracy challenges as such in the matrix linear algebra, where the residual may present unacceptable high variations hence affects the outcome in the iterative process, where substantial buildup of rounding error may often be observed. The BiCGStab combines residual formulas from both BiCG and CGS and has been shown to be effective in both classical and block versions
[
13

. The later observation encouraged us to consider such algorithm for the time parallel settings.

5
Conclusion

This paper presented a novel parallel in time algorithm based on simple investigation of the equivalent algebraic structure of the initial condition system of equations. We showed that our method outperforms the well-known parareal method and presented several numerical examples to support our claim.

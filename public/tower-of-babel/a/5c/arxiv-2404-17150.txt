Abstract
Reliability assessment of interconnection networks is critical to the design and maintenance of multiprocessor systems. The ( n , k ) (n,k) -enhanced hypercube Q n , k Q_{n,k} , as a variation of the hypercube Q n Q_{n} , was proposed by Tzeng and Wei in 1991. As an extension of traditional edge-connectivity, h h -extra edge-connectivity of a connected graph G , G, λ h ​ ( G ) , \lambda_{h}(G), is an essential parameter for evaluating the reliability of interconnection networks. This article intends to study the h h -extra edge-connectivity of the ( n , 2 ) (n,2) -enhanced hypercube Q n , 2 Q_{n,2} . Suppose that the link malfunction of an interconnection network Q n , 2 Q_{n,2} does not isolate any subnetwork with no more than h − 1 h-1 processors, the minimum number of these possible faulty links concentrates on a constant 2 n − 1 2^{n-1} for each integer ⌈ 11 × 2 n − 1 48 ⌉ ≤ h ≤ 2 n − 1 \lceil\frac{11\times 2^{n-1}}{48}\rceil\leq h\leq 2^{n-1} and n ≥ 9 n\geq 9 . That is, for about 77.083 % 77.083\% of values where h ≤ 2 n − 1 , h\leq 2^{n-1}, the corresponding h h -extra edge-connectivity of Q n , 2 Q_{n,2} , λ h ​ ( Q n , 2 ) \lambda_{h}(Q_{n,2}) , presents a concentration phenomenon. Moreover, the lower and upper bounds of h h mentioned above are both tight.

FQ_{n}
, first proposed by El-Amawy and Latifi
[
4
]
, is a graph obtained from the hypercube
Q_{n}
by adding an edge between every pair of vertices
x_{n}x_{n-1}\cdots x_{1}
and
\overline{x_{n}}~\overline{x_{n-1}}\cdots\overline{x_{1}}
, where
\overline{x_{i}}=1-x_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. The
FQ_{n}
is to add complementary edges between two
(n-1)
-dimensional sub-cubes. Motivated by this, by adding
k
-complementary edges between two paired
(n-k)
-dimensional sub-cubes, in 1991, Tzeng and Wei
[
19
]
introduced the
(n,k)
-enhanced hypercube
Q_{n,k}
.

Definition 2.2

For two integers
n
and
k
with
1\leq k\leq n-1
, the
(n,k)
-enhanced hypercube, denoted by
Q_{n,k},
is defined to be a graph with the vertex set
V\left(Q_{n,k}\right)=\{x_{n}x_{n-1}\cdots x_{2}x_{1}:x_{i}\in\{0,1\},1\leq i\leq n\}
. Two vertices
x=x_{n}x_{n-1}\cdots x_{2}x_{1}
and
y=y_{n}y_{n-1}\cdots y_{2}y_{1}
are adjacent if
y
satisfies one of the following two conditions:

(1)
y=x_{n}x_{n-1}\cdots{x}_{i+1}\bar{x}_{i}{x}_{i-1}\cdots x_{2}x_{1}
for
1\leq i\leq n,
where
\bar{x}_{i}=1-x_{i}
or

(2)
y=x_{n}x_{n-1}\cdots\bar{x}_{n-k+1}\bar{x}_{n-k}\cdots\bar{x}_{2}\bar{x}_{1}
.

Note that
Q_{n,1}
is the
n
-dimensional folded hypercube
FQ_{n}
. The
(n,2)
-enhanced hypercube
Q_{n,2}
is obtained from the hypercube
Q_{n}
by adding
2
-complementary edges between two pairs of vertices
x=x_{n}x_{n-1}\cdots x_{2}x_{1}
and
y=x_{n}\bar{x}_{n-1}\cdots\bar{x}_{2}\bar{x}_{1}
in two
(n-1)
-dimensional sub-cubes.

Lemma 2.7

Let

Proof 2.8

Note that
m_{2}=m-m_{1}=2^{t_{a+1}}+2^{t_{a+2}}+\cdots+2^{t_{s}}=\sum_{i=a+1}^{s}2^{t_{i}}=\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{s-a-1}2^{t_{i+a+1}}

strongly depends on the binary decomposition of

and the domain of

, it can be divided into the following two cases according to its two different forms.

(a)

For

, by Lemma
2.4
, it can be obtained

\textstyle{ex_{m_{1}}(Q_{n,2})=\sum_{i=0}^{a}t_{i}2^{t_{i}}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{a}2i2^{t_{i}}}

\textstyle{ex_{m_{2}}(Q_{n,2})=\sum_{i=0}^{s-a-1}t_{i+a+1}2^{t_{i+a+1}}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{s-a-1}2i2^{t_{i+a+1}}}.

Note that

\displaystyle ex_{m}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle=\textstyle{\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{s}t_{i}2^{t_{i}}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{s}2i2^{t_{i}}}

\displaystyle=\textstyle{(\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{a}t_{i}2^{t_{i}}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{s-a-1}t_{i+a+1}2^{t_{i+a+1}})}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\textstyle{+(\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{a}2i2^{t_{i}}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{s-a-1}2(a+1+i)2^{t_{i+a+1}})}

\displaystyle=\textstyle{ex_{m_{1}}(Q_{n,2})+ex_{m_{2}}(Q_{n,2})+2\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{s-a-1}(a+1)2^{t_{i+a+1}}}

For

\textstyle{ex_{m_{1}}(Q_{n,2})=\sum_{i=0}^{a}t_{i}2^{t_{i}}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{a}2i2^{t_{i}}+2m_{1}-2^{n-1}}

\textstyle{ex_{m_{2}}(Q_{n,2})=\sum_{i=0}^{s-a-1}t_{i+a+1}2^{t_{i+a+1}}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{s-a-1}2i2^{t_{i+a+1}}}.

\displaystyle ex_{m}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle=\textstyle{\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{s}t_{i}2^{t_{i}}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{s}2i2^{t_{i}}+2m-2^{n-1}}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\textstyle{+(\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{a}2i2^{t_{i}}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{s-a-1}2(a+1+i)2^{t_{i+a+1}})+2m-2^{n-1}}

Let
f=0
if
n
is even, and

if
n

To deal with the interval
\left\lceil\frac{11\times 2^{n-1}}{48}\right\rceil\leq m\leq 2^{n-1}

\textstyle{\left\lceil\frac{11\times 2^{n-1}}{48}\right\rceil=m_{n,1}<m_{n,2}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<m_{n,r}<m_{n,r+1}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<m_{n,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1}=2^{n-1}.}

This interval is divided into
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1

2^{n-3}&\text{ if }r=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-2;(g)\\
2^{n-1}&\text{ if }r=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1,(h)\end{array}\right.

for
r=1,2,\cdots,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1.
By calculation, it can be obtained that

\textstyle{\left\lceil\frac{11\times 2^{n-1}}{48}\right\rceil=m_{n,1}=\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{2}2^{n-4-i}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-5}2^{n-8-2i}+2^{1-f}}.

Lemma 3.1

[
21
]
Let

and

be three integers,

.

Lemma 3.2

Let
n,r
be two integers,
n\geq 9,

r=1,2,\cdots,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1
. Then
\xi_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})=2^{n-1}
.

Proof 3.3

According to different expressions of
m_{n,r}
, the proof will be divided into four cases.

Case 1
. For
1\leq r\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-4
,
m_{n,r}=\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{2}2^{n-4-i}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-4-r}2^{n-8-2i}+2^{2r-1-f},
by Lemma
2.4
and formula (
5
), it can be obtained that

\displaystyle\xi_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle=(n+1)m_{n,r}-ex_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle=\textstyle{(n+1)\left[\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{2}2^{n-4-i}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-4-r}2^{n-8-2i}+2^{2r-1-f}\right]}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\textstyle{-\Big\{\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{2}\left[(n-4-i)2^{n-4-i}+2i2^{n-4-i})\right]}

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+\textstyle{\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-4-r}\left[(n-8-2i)2^{n-8-2i}+2(3+i)2^{n-8-2i}\right]}

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+\textstyle{\left[(2r-1-f)2^{2r-1-f}+2(\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-r)2^{2r-1-f}\right]}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\textstyle{(n+1-n+4-i)\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{2}2^{n-4-i}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\textstyle{+(n+1-n+2)\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-4-r}2^{n-8-2i}+(n+2+f-2\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil)2^{2r-1-f}}

\displaystyle=\textstyle{(5-i)\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{2}2^{n-4-i}+3\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-4-r}2^{n-8-2i}+2^{2r-f}}

\displaystyle=5\cdot 2^{n-4}+4\cdot 2^{n-5}+4\cdot 2^{n-6}-2^{2r-f}+2^{2r-f}=3\cdot 2^{n-3}+2^{n-3}=2^{n-1}.

Case 2
. For
r=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-3,m_{n,r}=\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{3}2^{n-4-i}
, by Lemma
2.4
and the formula (
5
),

\displaystyle\xi_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle=(n+1)m_{n,r}-ex_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle=\textstyle{(n+1)\sum_{i=0}^{3}(n-4-i)2^{n-4-i}-\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{3}(n-4-i)2^{n-4-i}-\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{3}2i2^{n-4-i}}

\displaystyle=\textstyle{(5-i)\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{3}2^{n-4-i}=5\times 2^{n-4}+4\times 2^{n-5}+3\times 2^{n-6}+2\times 2^{n-7}=2^{n-1}}.

Case 3
. For
r=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-2,m_{n,r}=2^{n-3},
by Lemma
2.4
and the formula (
5
), it is not difficult to see
that

\xi_{2^{n-3}}(Q_{n,2})=(n+1)\times 2^{n-3}-(n-3)\times 2^{n-3}=2^{n-1}.

Case 4
. For
r=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1,m_{n,r}=2^{n-1},
by the formula (
5
) and Lemma
2.4
, then

\xi_{2^{n-1}}(Q_{n,2})=(n+1)\times 2^{n-1}-[(n-1)\times 2^{n-1}+2\times 2^{n-1}-2^{n-1}]=2^{n-1}.

From the above four cases, it can conclude that
\xi_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})=2^{n-1}
for
r=1,2,{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\cdots},\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1
. The proof is completed.

Lemma 3.4

Given two integers
n\geq 9,\left\lceil\frac{11\times 2^{n-1}}{48}\right\rceil\leq m\leq 2^{n-1},
there exists a positive integer
r,
satisfying
m_{n,r}<m<m_{n,r+1}
.

\displaystyle\xi_{m}\left(Q_{n,2}\right)>\xi_{m_{n,r}}\left(Q_{n,2}\right)

\displaystyle=\xi_{m_{n,r+1}}\left(Q_{n,2}\right)=\cdots=\xi_{m_{n,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1}}\left(Q_{n,2}\right)

\displaystyle=\xi_{2^{n-1}}(Q_{n,2})=\xi_{\left\lceil\frac{11\times 2^{n-1}}{48}\right\rceil}(Q_{n,2})=2^{n-1}.

Proof 3.5

According to different expressions of
ex_{m}(Q_{n,2})
, the proof will be divided into two cases.

Case 1.

\left\lceil\frac{11\times 2^{n-1}}{48}\right\rceil<m\leq 2^{n-2}
.

One can check that
m_{n,r+1}-m_{n,r}=2^{2r-1-f}
for
1\leq r\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-3
. By Lemma
3.2
,

\xi_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})=\xi_{2^{n-1}}(Q_{n,2})=2^{n-1}

for
1\leq r\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1
. Let
m=m_{n,r}+p
, where

\displaystyle m_{n,r}

\displaystyle=\textstyle{\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{2}2^{n-4-i}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-4-r}2^{n-8-2i}+2^{2r-1-f}}

\displaystyle\textstyle{\text{for $1\leq r<\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-4$}},

\displaystyle m_{n,r}

\displaystyle=\textstyle{\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{3}2^{n-4-i}}

\displaystyle\textstyle{\text{for $r=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-3$}},

0\leq p<2^{2r-1-f},p=\sum_{i=0}^{s}2^{t^{\prime}_{i}}<m_{n,r+1}-m_{n,r},2r-1-f>t^{\prime}_{0}>t^{\prime}_{1}>\cdot\cdot\cdot>t^{\prime}_{s}
. By the equation (
5
) and Lemma
2.7
, one can deduce that

\displaystyle\xi_{m}(Q_{n,2})-\xi_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle\quad=(n+1)(m_{n,r}+p)-ex_{m_{n,r}+p}(Q_{n,2})-(n+1)m_{n,r}+ex_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle\quad=(n+1)p-ex_{m_{n,r}+p}(Q_{n,2})+ex_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})\quad\textrm{(Lemma~\ref{s3})}

\displaystyle\quad=\textstyle{(n+1)p-ex_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})-ex_{p}(Q_{n,2})-2(\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-r+1)p+ex_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})}\quad\textrm{(Lemma~\ref{s1}(2))}

\displaystyle\quad=(2r-1-f)p-ex_{p}(Q_{n,2})=(2r-1-f)p-ex_{p}(Q_{2r-1-f})=\xi_{p}(Q_{2r-1-f}).

For
p<2^{2r-1-f}<2^{n-1},
the
value of
ex_{p}(Q_{n})
is uniquely determined by the binary representation of
p
. Therefore,
ex_{p}(Q_{n})=ex_{p}(Q_{2r-1-f})
. By Lemma
2.3
,

ex_{p}(Q_{2r-1-f})=2\lvert E(Q_{2r-1-f}[L_{p}^{2r-1-f}])\rvert.

[L_{p}^{2r-1-f},\overline{L_{p}^{2r-1-f}}]_{Q_{2r-1-f}}
be an
edge cut of
Q_{2r-1-f}
. Since
Q_{2r-1-f}
is connected graph, and if
one deletes the edge cut
[L_{p}^{2r-1-f},\overline{L_{p}^{2r-1-f}}]_{Q_{2r-1-f}},
two induced subgraphs
Q_{2r-1-f}[L_{p}^{2r-1-f}]
and
Q_{2r-1-f}[\overline{L_{p}^{2r-1-f}}]

are connected. So the edge cut
[L_{p}^{2r-1-f},\overline{L_{p}^{2r-1-f}}]_{Q_{2r-1-f}}
of
Q_{2r-1-f}
does exist. By Lemma
2.5
, it is sufficient to show that
ex_{p}(Q_{2r-1-f})\leq(2r-1-f)p,
and
\xi_{m}(Q_{n,2})-\xi_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})=(2r-1-f)p-ex_{p}\left(Q_{2r-1-f}\right)>0
.

If
r=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-2
, then
m_{n,r}=2^{n-3}
. There exists a
positive integer
p^{\prime}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}2^{t^{\prime}_{i}},
satisfying
0\leq p^{\prime}<2^{n-3}
,
m=2^{n-3}+p^{\prime}
and
n-3>t^{\prime}_{0}>t^{\prime}_{1}>\cdot\cdot\cdot>t^{\prime}_{s}
. The proof of
\xi_{m}(Q_{n,2})>\xi_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})
is the same as the above proof of
\xi_{m}(Q_{n,2})>\xi_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2}).

Case 2
.
2^{n-2}<m\leq 2^{n-1}
.

If
r=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-2
, then
m_{n,r}=2^{n-3}
. There exists a
positive integer
m^{\prime\prime}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}2^{t^{\prime}_{i}},
satisfying
0\leq m^{\prime\prime}<2^{n-2}
,
m=m_{n,r}+2^{n-3}+m^{\prime\prime}=2^{n-2}+m^{\prime\prime}
and
n-2>t^{\prime}_{0}>t^{\prime}_{1}>\cdot\cdot\cdot>t^{\prime}_{s}
. By the equation (
5
) and Lemma
2.7
,

\displaystyle\xi_{m}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle-\xi_{2^{n-3}}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle=\xi_{m}(Q_{n,2})-\xi_{2^{n-2}}(Q_{n,2})+\xi_{2^{n-2}}(Q_{n,2})-\xi_{2^{n-3}}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle=(n+1)(2^{n-2}+m^{\prime\prime})-(n+1)2^{n-2}-(ex_{m}(Q_{n,2})-ex_{2^{n-2}}(Q_{n,2}))+2^{n-2}

\displaystyle=(n+1)m^{\prime\prime}-(ex_{2^{n-2}+m^{\prime\prime}}(Q_{n})+2m^{\prime\prime})+ex_{2^{n-2}}(Q_{n})+2^{n-2}~~(\textrm{Lemma~\ref{s3}})

\displaystyle=(n+1)m^{\prime\prime}-ex_{2^{n-2}}(Q_{n})-ex_{m^{\prime\prime}}(Q_{n})-4m^{\prime\prime}+ex_{2^{n-2}}(Q_{n})+2^{n-2}

\displaystyle=(n+1)m^{\prime\prime}-ex_{m^{\prime\prime}}\left(Q_{n}\right)-4m^{\prime\prime}+2^{n-2}=(n-3)m^{\prime\prime}-ex_{m^{\prime\prime}}\left(Q_{n}\right)+2^{n-2}

\displaystyle=(n-3)m^{\prime\prime}-ex_{m^{\prime\prime}}\left(Q_{n-3}\right)+2^{n-2}=\xi_{m^{\prime\prime}}(Q_{n-3})+2^{n-2}>0.

For
0<m^{\prime\prime}\leq 2^{n-2},
the
value of
ex_{m^{\prime\prime}}(Q_{n})
is uniquely determined by the binary representation of
m^{\prime\prime}
. Thus,
ex_{m^{\prime\prime}}(Q_{n})=ex_{m^{\prime\prime}}(Q_{n-3})
. By Lemma
2.5
,
(n-3)m^{\prime\prime}-ex_{m}^{\prime\prime}\left(Q_{n-3}\right)>0
for
0<m^{\prime\prime}\leq 2^{n-2}.
Thus,
\xi_{m}(Q_{n,2})>\xi_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})
.

Combining the above two cases,
\xi_{m}(Q_{n,2})>\xi_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})=\xi_{m_{n,r+1}}(Q_{n,2})=\xi_{\left\lceil\frac{11\times 2^{n-1}}{48}\right\rceil}(Q_{n,2})=2^{n-1}
for
1\leq r\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-2
. So the proof is completed.

4
The
h
-extra edge-connectivity of
Q_{n,2}
concentrates on
2^{n-1}

for
\left\lceil\frac{11\times 2^{n-1}}{48}\right\rceil\leq h\leq 2^{n-1}

The proof of Theorem

1.1

(a):

Given each integer
h
, for
\left\lceil\frac{11\times 2^{n-1}}{48}\right\rceil\leq h\leq m_{n,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1}=2^{n-1}
, there exists an integer
r,1\leq r\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1,
satisfying
m_{n,r}\leq h\leq m_{n,r+1}
.
By Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
3.4
,

\lambda_{h}(Q_{n,2})=\min\{\xi_{m}(Q_{n,2}):m_{n,r}\leq h\leq m<m_{n,r+1}\}=\xi_{m_{n,r}}(Q_{n,2})

for
r=1,2,\cdots,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1
. So
for any
\left\lceil\frac{11\times 2^{n-1}}{48}\right\rceil\leq h\leq 2^{n-1}
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}\left(Q_{n,2}\right)

\displaystyle=\min\{\xi_{m}(Q_{n,2}):h\leq m\leq 2^{n-1}\}\qquad\textrm{(Lemma~\ref{s5})}

\displaystyle=\min\{\xi_{m}(Q_{n,2}):h\leq m\leq m_{n,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1}\}\qquad\textrm{(Lemmas~\ref{s10} and~\ref{s15})}

\displaystyle=\xi_{2^{n-1}}(Q_{n,2})\qquad\textrm{(Lemmas~\ref{s9} and~\ref{s10})}

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}.

The proof of Theorem

1.1
(b):

If
h=m_{n,r}
or
h=m_{n,r+1},
by Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
3.2

\lambda_{h}(Q_{n,2})=\xi_{h}(Q_{n,2})=2^{n-1}
. If
m_{n,r}<h<m_{n,r+1}
, by Lemma
3.4
,
\xi_{h}(Q_{n,2})>\xi_{m_{n,r+1}}(Q_{n,2})
, by Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle=\min\{\xi_{m}(Q_{n,2}):h\leq m\leq m_{n,r+1}\}

\displaystyle=\xi_{m_{n,r+1}}(Q_{n,2})=\xi_{m_{n,r+2}}(Q_{n,2})=\cdot\cdot\cdot=\xi_{m_{n,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1}}(Q_{n,2})=2^{n-1}.

So, one can get
\lambda_{h}(Q_{n,2})=\xi_{h}(Q_{n,2})=2^{n-1}
for
h=m_{n,r}
or
h=m_{n,r+1},1\leq r<\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1
.

The proof is completed.

Remark 4.1

For
\left\lceil\frac{11\times 2^{n-1}}{48}\right\rceil\leq h\leq 2^{n-1}
and
n\geq 9
, the lower and upper bounds of
h
of
\lambda_{h}(Q_{n,2})
in the above Theorem
1.1
are both tight.

(1) In fact, if
n
is even, then

\displaystyle m_{n,1}

\displaystyle=\textstyle{\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{2}2^{n-4-i}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-5}2^{n-8-2i}+2},

\displaystyle m_{n,1}-1

\displaystyle=\textstyle{\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{2}2^{n-4-i}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-5}2^{n-8-2i}+1}.

By Lemma
2.6
,
ex_{m_{n,1}}(Q_{n,2})=ex_{m_{n,1}-1}(Q_{n,2})+n
. So,

\xi_{m_{n,1}}(Q_{n,2})-\xi_{m_{n,1}-1}(Q_{n,2})=(n+1)m_{n,1}-ex_{m_{n,1}}(Q_{n,2})-(n+1)(m_{n,1}-1)+ex_{m_{n,1}-1}(Q_{n,2})=1.

Note that

\displaystyle\lambda_{m_{n,1}-1}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle=\min\{\xi_{h}(Q_{n,2}):m_{n,1}-1\leq h\leq m_{n,1}\}=\xi_{m_{n,1}-1}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-1<2^{n-1}=\lambda_{m_{n,1}}(Q_{n,2})=\xi_{m_{n,1}}(Q_{n,2}).

If
n
is odd, then

\displaystyle m_{n,1}

\displaystyle=\textstyle{\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{2}2^{n-4-i}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-5}2^{n-8-2i}+1},

\displaystyle m_{n,1}-1

\displaystyle=\textstyle{\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{2}2^{n-4-i}+\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-5}2^{n-8-2i}}.

By Lemma
2.6
,
ex_{m_{n,1}}(Q_{n,2})=ex_{m_{n,1}-1}(Q_{n,2})+n-1
. So,

\xi_{m_{n,1}}(Q_{n,2})-\xi_{m_{n,1}-1}(Q_{n,2})=(n+1)m_{n,1}-ex_{m_{n,1}}(Q_{n,2})-(n+1)(m_{n,1}-1)+ex_{m_{n,1}-1}(Q_{n,2})=2.

Similarly, it can be seen that

\displaystyle\lambda_{m_{n,1}-1}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle=\min\{\xi_{m}(Q_{n,2}):m_{n,1}-1\leq m\leq m_{n,1}\}=\xi_{m_{n,1}-1}(Q_{n,2})

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-2<2^{n-1}=\lambda_{m_{n,1}}(Q_{n,2})=\xi_{m_{n,1}}(Q_{n,2}).

Therefore, the lower bound is sharp.

(2) As
|V(Q_{n,2})|=2^{n},
by the definition of
h
-extra edge-connectivity, there are at least two components with at least
h
vertices. So, the upper bound of the above interval is
2^{n-1}
. Therefore, the upper bound is sharp.

5
Conclusion

It is well known that the
h
-extra edge-connectivity is an important indicator for measuring the fault tolerance and reliability of interconnection networks. This paper shows that the
h
-extra
edge-connectivity of
(n,2)
-enhanced hypercubes
Q_{n,2}
presents a concentration phenomenon in the subinterval

\textstyle{\left\lceil\frac{11\times 2^{n-1}}{48}\right\rceil\leq h\leq 2^{n-1}}

for
n\geq 9
. For approximately
77.083\%
values of
h\leq 2^{n-1}
, the minimum number of link malfunctions is
2^{n-1}
, and these link malfunctions
disconnect
(n,2)
-enhanced hypercube
Q_{n,2}
and keep each resulting connected subnetworks with at least
h
processors. Our results provide a more accurate measure for evaluating the reliability and availability of large-scale
Q_{n,2}
networks. In order to completely solve the
h
-extra edge-connectivity of the remaining intervals, we will further investigate an algorithm to determine the exact value and the optimality of the
h
-extra edge-connectivity of
Q_{n,2}
for each integer
h\leq 2^{n-1}
in the future. Additionally, for the general network
Q_{n,k}
, we propose to design an algorithm to determine the exact value and the optimality of
\lambda_{h}(Q_{n,k})
.

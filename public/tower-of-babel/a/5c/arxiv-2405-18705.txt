A simple inverse power method for balanced graph cut Thanks: Submitted to the editors on August 8, 2026.
Sihong Shao, Chuan Yang

Abstract
The existing inverse power ( 𝐈𝐏 \mathbf{IP} ) method for solving the balanced graph cut lacks local convergence and its inner subproblem requires a nonsmooth convex solver. To address these issues, we develop a simple inverse power ( 𝐒𝐈𝐏 \mathbf{SIP} ) method using a novel equivalent continuous formulation of the balanced graph cut, and its inner subproblem allows an explicit analytic solution, which is the biggest advantage over 𝐈𝐏 \mathbf{IP} and constitutes the main reason why we call it simple . By fully exploiting the closed-form of the inner subproblem solution, we design a boundary-detected subgradient selection with which 𝐒𝐈𝐏 \mathbf{SIP} is proved to be locally converged. We show that 𝐒𝐈𝐏 \mathbf{SIP} is also applicable to a new ternary valued θ \theta -balanced cut which reduces to the balanced cut when θ = 1 \theta=1 . When 𝐒𝐈𝐏 \mathbf{SIP} reaches its local optimum, we seamlessly transfer to solve the θ \theta -balanced cut within exactly the same iteration algorithm framework and thus obtain 𝐒𝐈𝐏 \mathbf{SIP} - 𝐩𝐞𝐫𝐭𝐮𝐫𝐛 \mathbf{perturb} — an efficient local breakout improvement of 𝐒𝐈𝐏 \mathbf{SIP} , which transforms some “partitioned” vertices back to the “un-partitioned” ones through the adjustable θ \theta . Numerical experiments on G-set for Cheeger cut and Sparsest cut demonstrate that 𝐒𝐈𝐏 \mathbf{SIP} is significantly faster than 𝐈𝐏 \mathbf{IP} while maintaining approximate solutions of comparable quality, and 𝐒𝐈𝐏 \mathbf{SIP} - 𝐩𝐞𝐫𝐭𝐮𝐫𝐛 \mathbf{perturb} outperforms Gurobi in terms of both computational cost and solution quality.

Introduction

We consider the following balanced cut problem defined on an undirected graph
G=(V,E,w,\mu)

h(G)=\min\limits_{S\subset V,S\not\in\{\emptyset,V\}}\frac{|E(S,S^{c})|}{\min\{\mathrm{vol}(S),\mathrm{vol}(S^{c})\}},

(1)

where
V=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
gives the vertex set,

\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{n})
with
\mu_{i}
being a positive weight on
i\in V
,

\mathrm{vol}(S)=\sum_{i\in S}\mu_{i}

S\subset V
,

E

W=(w_{ij})_{n\times n}
with
w_{ij}
being a positive weight on
\{i,j\}\in E
and vanishing on
\{i,j\}\notin E

E(S,T)
collects the set of the edges across
S\subset V
and
T\subset V
with the amount of
|E(S,T)|=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(S,T)}w_{ij}
,

S^{c}=V\backslash S
denotes the complement of
S
in
V
and
(S,S^{c})
presents a binary cut of
G
.
Two typical examples of Eq. (
1

\mu_{i}=d_{i}
holds for
\forall\,i\in V
with

being the degree of the

[
Alon1986
]
, and Sparest cut where
\mu_{i}=1
holds for
\forall\,i\in V

[
arora2009expander
]
. Both problems are NP-hard
[
hochbaum2013polynomial
]
and have various real-world applications in the fields of clustering
[
bresson2013multiclass
,
szlam2010total
,
HeinBuhler2010
]
, community detection
[
van2016local
]
, and computer vision
[
shi2000normalized
]

[
lang2004flow
]
, the breakout tabu search
[
lu2019stagnation
]
and the hybrid evolutionary algorithm
[
lu2020hybrid
]
. Recently, developing efficient algorithms with continuous flavor, as an alternative, has also attracted more and more attention
[
HeinBuhler2010
,
szlam2010total
,
Chang2015
]
and this work falls into such category.

The first well-known continuous equivalent formulation of the balanced cut problem (
1

[
Chung1997
,
chang2021lovasz
]

h(G)=\min\limits_{\text{nonconstant~}\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{I(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})}{N(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})},

(2)

where

I(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}w_{ij}|x_{i}-x_{j}|,\quad N(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})=\min_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{i\in V}\mu_{i}|x_{i}-c|.

Formally applying a Lagrange multiplier technique equipped with the subgradient into the above continuous equivalent formulation yields the so-called
1
-Laplacian eigenproblem and the resulting second smallest eigenvalue happens to be
h(G)

[
Chang2015
]
.
Accordingly, a three-step inverse power (
\mathbf{IP}
) method starting from an initial data

[
HeinBuhler2010
,
Chang2015
]

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$x$}^{k+1}=\mathop{\argmin}\limits_{\|\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\|_{1}\leq 1}\{I(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})-r^{k}\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}^{k}\rangle\},

(3a)

\displaystyle r^{k+1}=I(\mbox{\boldmath$x$}^{k+1})/N(\mbox{\boldmath$x$}^{k+1}),

(3b)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$v$}^{k+1}\in\partial N(\mbox{\boldmath$x$}^{k+1}),

(3c)

was proposed to approximate
h(G)
after introducing a subgradient relaxation to the equivalent two-step Dinkelbach iteration for Eq. (
2
)
[
dinkelbach1967nonlinear
]

\mathbb{R}^{n}

\|\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\|_{1}=\sum_{i\in V}|x_{i}|

However, solving the inner subproblem (

]
or to use extra optimization solvers (e.g. CVX
[

]
). To compensate this, we first propose an alternative equivalent continuous formulation for the balanced cut problem (
1

h(G)=\min\limits_{\text{nonconstant~}\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{e\|\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\|_{\infty}-I^{+}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})}{N(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})},

(4)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$x$}^{k+1}=\mathop{\argmin}\limits_{\|\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\|_{1}\leq 1}\{\|\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\|_{\infty}-\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\mbox{\boldmath$s$}^{k}\rangle\},

\displaystyle r^{k+1}=B(\mbox{\boldmath$x$}^{k+1}),

1.
Introduction

In this paper we obtain Lipschitz type estimates for functions of pairs of not necessarily commuting maximal dissipative operators
in the Schatten–von Neumann norm
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}
with
1\leq p\leq 2
.
Similar results for not necessarily commuting and not necessarily bounded self-adjoint operators were obtained in the paper
[
AP8
]
.
The case of commuting dissipative operators was considered by the authors in
[
AP7
]
.

Let
(L,M)
be a pair of not necessarily commuting maximal dissipative operators.
Let us define functions
f(L,M)
of these operators in the case when the function
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
belongs to the Haagerup tensor product

{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}
, where

{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}H^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})\cap{\rm C}({\mathbb{R}}).

Recall that the Haagerup tensor product
{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}

consists of functions
f
of two variables that admit a representation in the form

\displaystyle f(x,y)=\sum_{n}{\varphi}_{n}(x)\psi_{n}(y),

(1.1)

where
{\varphi}_{n}\in{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}
,
\psi_{n}\in{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}
and

\displaystyle\left(\left\|\sum_{n}|{\varphi}_{n}(x)|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\left\|\sum_{n}|\psi_{n}(x)|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

(1.2)

The norm of
f
in
{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}\!\otimes_{\rm h}{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}

is, by definition, the infimum of the left-hand side of
(
1.2
) over all representations of
f
in the form
(
1.1
).

Let
f\in\!{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}\otimes_{\rm h}\!{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}
. Then we can define the function
f
of the pair

(L,M)
of not necessarily commuting maximal dissipative operators by

\displaystyle f(L,M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}}f(x,y)\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{L}(x)\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{M}(y),

(1.3)

where
{\mathscr{E}}_{L}
and
{\mathscr{E}}_{M}
are the semi-spectral measures of
L
and
M
. We refer the reader to
[
AP6
]
and
[
AP7
]
where the semi-spectral measure of a maximal dissipative operator is defined and integrals with respect to semi-spectral measures are discussed.

Note that for
f\in\!{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}\otimes_{\rm h}\!{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}
, the operator
f(L,M)
is bounded and

\|f(L,M)\|\leq\|f\|_{{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}\otimes_{\rm h}\!{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}}.

Recall that for a function
f
in
{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}\otimes_{\rm h}\!{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}
, the integral
on the right-hand side of (
1.3
) can be defined, for example, by

f(L,M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{n}{\varphi}_{n}(L)\psi_{n}(M).

The series converges in the weak operator topology and the sum does not depend on the choice of a representation (
1.1
).

In this paper we define the operator
f(L,M)
for a broader class of functions than

{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}\otimes_{\rm h}\!{\rm C}_{{\rm A},\infty}
, however, in this case the operator
f(L,M)
is not necessarily bounded anymore.

Recall that it was shown in
[
ANP
]
that for pairs
(A_{1},B_{1})
and
(A_{2},B_{2})

of not necessarily commuting bounded self-adjoint operators

A
and
B
and a function
f
in the

homogeneous
Besov space
B_{\infty,1}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
the functions
f(A_{1},B_{1})
and
f(A_{2},B_{2})
of the operators
(A_{1},B_{1})
and
fA_{2},B_{2})

are defined and the following estimate

\displaystyle\|f(A_{1},B_{1})-f(A_{2},B_{2})\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}\leq\operatorname{const}\|f\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{1}}\max\big\{\|A_{1}-B_{1}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}},\|A_{2}-B_{2}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}\big\}.

(1.4)

holds for all
p\in[1,2]
.
In the same paper
[
ANP
]
it was shown that for
p>2

(for the operator norm
p=\infty
) the inequality (
1.4
) cannot hold for all
bounded self-adjoint operators.

The main result of this article is a version of inequality (
1.4
) for dissipative operators and for functions

in the
inhomogeneous
analytic Besov class
(\Be_{\infty,1}^{1})_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.
In other words, we are going to prove that for

, the following inequality holds:

\|f(L_{1},B_{1})-f(L_{2},M_{2})\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}\leq\operatorname{const}\|f\|_{\Be_{\infty,1}^{1}}\max\big\{\|L_{1}-M_{1}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}},\|L_{2}-M_{2}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}\big\}

for all

and arbitrary dissipative operators
L_{1}
,
M_{1}
,
L_{2}
and

.

As in
[
ANP
]
an important role here will be played by triple operator integrals.
A brief introduction ti triple operator integrals is given in §
3
.
In §
4
we define functions of pairs of not necessarily commuting maximal dissipative operators. In §

we obtain for this functional calculus Lipschitz type estimates

,

Finally, we give in §

,

\Be_{\infty,1}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and

.

2.
Besov spaces

In this paper we deal with the homogeneous Besov class
B_{\infty,1}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, with inhomogeneous Besov class
\Be_{\infty,1}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
with the inhomogeneous analytic Besov class

(\Be_{\infty,1}^{1})_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.
We refer the reader to the book
[
Pee
]
, as well as to the papers
[
ANP
]
and
[
AP4
]
for additional information on Besov classes
B_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Here we give a definition only in the case when
p=\infty
,
q=s=1
and
d=2
.
Let
w
be an infinitely differentiable function on the real line
{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\displaystyle w\geq 0,\quad\operatorname{supp}w\subset\left[\frac{1}{2},2\right],\quad\mbox{and}\quad w(t)=1-w\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\quad\mbox{for}\quad t\in[1,2].

(2.1)

With each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we associate the function
W_{n}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that

\big({\mathscr{F}}W_{n}\big)(x)=w\left(\frac{\|x\|_{2}}{2^{n}}\right),\quad n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2}),\quad\|x\|_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})^{1/2},

where
{\mathscr{F}}
denotes the
Fourier transform
defined on
L^{1}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)
by

\big({\mathscr{F}}f\big)(t)=\!\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f(x)e^{-{\rm i}(x,t)}\,dx,\!\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2}),\quad t=(t_{1},t_{2}),\!\quad(x,t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}x_{1}t_{1}+x_{2}t_{2}.

Clearly,

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}({\mathscr{F}}W_{n})(t)=1,\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}.

With each tempered distribution
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)

we associate the sequence of functions
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
,

\displaystyle f_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f*W_{n}.

(2.2)

The formal series

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{n}

is called a Littlewood–Payley expansion of
f
.
The series does not have to converge to
f
.

First we define the homogeneous Besov class
\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)

as the space of tempered distributions
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{n}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty.

(2.3)

In accordance with this definition the space
\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
contains
all polynomials and
\|f\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}=0
for every polynomial
f
. Moreover,
a distribution
f
is determined uniquely by the sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
up to a polynomial. It is easy to see that the series
\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}
converges in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.
However, the series
\sum_{n<0}f_{n}
in general can diverge. Clearly, the series

\displaystyle\sum_{n<0}\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x_{1}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{n<0}\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x_{2}}

(2.4)

converge uniformly on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
for an arbitrary
f
in
\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.
Now we are going to say that a function
f
belongs to the
(reduced) homogeneous Besov class
B^{1}_{\infty,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,

if (
2.3
) holds and

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x_{j}},\quad j=1,\,2.

In this case the function
f
is determined by the sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}

uniquely up to a constant, and a polynomial
g
belongs to
B^{1}_{\infty,1}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)

if and only if it is a constant.

In contrast with our previous papers we are going to use in this paper the inhomogeneous Besov space

\Be_{\infty,1}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. We define the function
W^{[0]}
by

\big({\mathscr{F}}W^{[0]}\big)(t)=1-\sum_{n\geq 1}({\mathscr{F}}W_{n})(t),\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

It is easy to see that

({\mathscr{F}}W^{[0]}\big)(t)=1,\quad\mbox{if}\quad\|t\|_{2}\leq 1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\operatorname{supp}{\mathscr{F}}W^{[0]}\subset\big\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:~\|t\|_{2}\leq 2\big\}.

We say that a tempered distribution
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

belongs to the
inhomogeneous Besov space

\Be_{\infty,1}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
if

\displaystyle f^{[0]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f*W^{[0]}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{n\geq 1}2^{n}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty.

(2.5)

The norm of a function
f
in
\Be_{\infty,1}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
can be defined as

\|f\|_{\Be_{\infty,1}^{1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\big\|f^{[0]}\big\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sum_{n\geq 1}2^{n}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

It is easy to see that if
f\in\Be_{\infty,1}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, then

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}=\frac{\partial f^{[0]}}{\partial x_{j}}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x_{j}},\quad j=1,\,2.

Moreover, the series converges uniformly. Clearly,
\Be_{\infty,1}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

is dense in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

We define now the inhomogeneous
analytic
Besov class
(\Be_{\infty,1}^{1})_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
by

(\Be_{\infty,1}^{1})_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\{f\in\Be_{\infty,1}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}):\operatorname{supp}{\mathscr{F}}f\subset[0,+\infty)^{2}\}.

The analytic Besov class
(\Be_{\infty,1}^{1})_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
is a subspace of the Besov class
\Be_{\infty,1}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

We would like to mention that our notation slightly differs form the standard one
(see e.g.,
[
Pee
]
).
However, we believe that our notation is more convenient.

It is well known and it is easy to prove that
\Be_{\infty,1}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})=B^{1}_{\infty,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\bigcap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

With each positive number
\sigma
we associate two subspaces

{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\Big\{f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}):~\operatorname{supp}{\mathscr{F}}f\subset\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:~\|t\|_{2}\leq\sigma\}\Big\}.

and

({\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}^{\infty})_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\Big\{f\in{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}):\operatorname{supp}{\mathscr{F}}f\subset[0,+\infty)^{2}\Big\}.

Clearly,
({\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}^{\infty})_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\subset{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\subset{\rm C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Note that
f_{n}\in{\mathscr{E}}_{2^{n+1}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
f_{n}
is defined in (
2.2
).
In particular, if
f\in(\Be_{\infty,1}^{1})_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, then
f_{n}\in({\mathscr{E}}_{2^{n+1}}^{\infty})_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

and
f^{[0]}\in({\mathscr{E}}_{2}^{\infty})_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, where
f^{[0]}
is defined in (
2.5
).

3.
Triple operator integrals and Haagerup-like tensor products

A triple operator integral – is an expression of the form

\displaystyle\int\limits_{{\mathscr{X}}_{1}}\int\limits_{{\mathscr{X}}_{2}}\int\limits_{{\mathscr{X}}_{3}}\Psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\,dE_{1}(x_{1})T\,dE_{2}(x_{2})R\,dE_{3}(x_{3}),

(3.1)

where
\Psi
is a bounded measurable function on
{\mathscr{X}}_{1}\times{\mathscr{X}}_{2}\times{\mathscr{X}}_{3}
;

E_{1}
,
E_{2}
and
E_{3}
are spectral measures on Hilbert space, and

T
and
R
are bounded linear operators. Such triple operator integrals
can be defined under certain conditions on
\Psi
,
T
and
R
.

In
[
Pe3
]
triple operator integrals of the form (
3.1
) were defined for arbitrary bounded linear operators
T
and
R
and for functions
\Psi
in the integral projective tensor product
L^{\infty}(E_{1})\!\otimes_{\rm i}\!L^{\infty}(E_{2})\!\otimes_{\rm i}\!L^{\infty}(E_{3})
of the spaces
L^{\infty}(E_{1})
,

L^{\infty}(E_{2})
and
L^{\infty}(E_{3})
. In this case the following inequality holds:

\left\|\iiint\Psi\,dE_{1}T\,dE_{2})R\,dE_{3}\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{r}}\leq\|\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}\otimes_{\rm i}L^{\infty}\otimes_{\rm i}L^{\infty}}\|T\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}\|R\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{q}},\quad T\in{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p},~R\in{\boldsymbol{S}}_{q},

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\quad\mbox{under the assumption that}\quad\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\leq 1.

Then triple operator integrals were defined in
[
JTT
]

for the Haagerup tensor products
of the spaces
L^{\infty}
.

The Haagerup tensor product

L^{\infty}(E_{1})\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}(E_{2})\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}(E_{3})

can be defined as the space of functions
\Psi
of the form

\displaystyle\Psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\sum_{j,k\geq 0}\alpha_{j}(x_{1}){\beta}_{jk}(x_{2})\gamma_{k}(x_{3}),

(3.2)

where
\alpha_{j}
,
{\beta}_{jk}
and
\gamma_{k}
are measurable functions such that

\displaystyle\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}\in L_{E_{1}}^{\infty}(\ell^{2}),\quad\{{\beta}_{jk}\}_{j,k\geq 0}\in L_{E_{2}}^{\infty}({\mathcal{B}}),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\in L_{E_{3}}^{\infty}(\ell^{2}),

(3.3)

where
{\mathcal{B}}
denotes the space of matrices that induce bounded linear operators
\ell^{2}
equipped with the operator norm.

The norm of
\Psi
in the space
L^{\infty}\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}

is defined as the infimum of the set of numbers of the form

\|\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\ell^{2})}\|\{{\beta}_{jk}\}_{j,k\geq 0}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{B}})}\|\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\ell^{2})}

over all representations of
\Psi
in the form (
3.2
) with families

\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
,

\{{\beta}_{jk}\}_{j,k\geq 0}
adn
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
satisfying (
3.3
).

Let
\Psi\in L^{\infty}\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}
and let
(
3.2
) and (
3.3
) be satisfied. Then the triple operator integral
(
3.1
) is defined by

\displaystyle\iiint\Psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle\,dE_{1}(x_{1})T\,dE_{2}(x_{2})R\,dE_{3}(x_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k\geq 0}\left(\int\alpha_{j}\,dE_{1}\right)T\left(\int{\beta}_{jk}\,dE_{2}\right)R\left(\int\gamma_{k}\,dE_{3}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{M,N\to\infty}~\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{M}\left(\int\alpha_{j}\,dE_{1}\right)T\left(\int{\beta}_{jk}\,dE_{2}\right)R\left(\int\gamma_{k}\,dE_{3}\right).

(3.4)

The limit in (
3
) exists in the weak operator topology and does not depend on the choice of a representation (
3.2
), it determines a bounded linear operator and

\displaystyle\left\|\iiint\Psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\,dE_{1}(x_{1})T\,dE_{2}(x_{2})R\,dE_{3}(x_{3})\right\|\leq\|\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}}\|T\|\cdot\|R\|

(3.5)

(see
[
ANP
]
and
[
AP5
]
).

It was proved in
[
ANP
]
(see also
[
AP5
]
) that if

T\in{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p},\quad R\in{\boldsymbol{S}}_{q},\quad p,\,q\in[2,\infty]\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Psi\in L^{\infty}\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty},

then the triple operator integral (
3.1
) belongs to
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{r}
,
1/r=1/p+1/q
, and

\displaystyle\left\|\iiint\!\Psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\,dE_{1}(x_{1})T\,dE_{2}(x_{2})R\,dE_{3}(x_{3})\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{r}}\!\!\!\leq\|\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}}\|T\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}\|R\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{q}}.

(3.6)

Note that for
p=\infty
we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
the operator norm.

It turns out that Lipschitz type estimates for functions of pairs of noncommuting operators depend on estimates of triple integral operators with integrands in
Haagerup-like tensor products of the first and the second kind
of
L^{\infty}
spaces. Such tensor products were introduced in
[
ANP
]
.

Definition 1.
A function
\Psi
of three variables is said to belong to
the
Haagerup-like tensor product

L^{\infty}(E_{1})\otimes_{\rm h}L^{\infty}(E_{2})\otimes^{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}(E_{3})
of the first kind

if it admits a representation of the form

\displaystyle\Psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\sum_{j,k\geq 0}\alpha_{j}(x_{1}){\beta}_{k}(x_{2})\gamma_{jk}(x_{3}),\quad x_{j}\in{\mathscr{X}}_{j},

(3.7)

with
\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j\geq 0},~\{{\beta}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\in L^{\infty}(\ell^{2})
and

\{\gamma_{jk}\}_{j,k\geq 0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B})
. As usual,

\|\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}\otimes^{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\inf\big\|\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\ell^{2})}\big\|\{{\beta}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\ell^{2})}\big\|\{\gamma_{jk}\}_{j,k\geq 0}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B})},

where the infimum is taken over all representations of the form (
3.7
).

We define now triple operator integrals with integrand in the tensor product

L^{\infty}(E_{1})\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}(E_{2})\!\otimes^{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}(E_{3})
.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
. For a function
\Psi
in
L^{\infty}(E_{1})\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}(E_{2})\otimes^{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}(E_{3})
, for a bounded linear operator
R
and for an operator
T
of class
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}
, we define the triple operator integral

\displaystyle W=\iint\!\!\upintop\nolimits\Psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\,dE_{1}(x_{1})T\,dE_{2}(x_{2})R\,dE_{3}(x_{3})

(3.8)

as the linear functional on the space
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p^{\prime}}
,
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
(on the space of compact operators in the case
p=1
):

\displaystyle Q\mapsto\operatorname{trace}\left(\left(\iiint\Psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\,dE_{2}(x_{2})R\,dE_{3}(x_{3})Q\,dE_{1}(x_{1})\right)T\right).

(3.9)

Clearly, the triple operator integral in (

) is well defined because the function

(x_{2},x_{3},x_{1})\mapsto\Psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

belongs to the Haagerup tensor product
L^{\infty}(E_{2})\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}(E_{3})\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}(E_{1})
.
It follows easily from (
3.6
) that

\displaystyle\|W\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}\leq\|\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}\otimes^{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}}\|T\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}\|R\|,\quad 1\leq p\leq 2.

(3.10)

We also need triple operator integrals in the case when

T
is a bounded linear operator and
R\in{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}
,
1\leq p\leq 2
.

Definition 2.
A function
\Psi
is said to belong to the Haagerup-like tensor product

L^{\infty}(E_{1})\!\otimes^{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}(E_{2})\!\otimes_{\rm h}\!L^{\infty}(E_{3})
of the second kind if
\Psi
admits a representation of the form

\displaystyle\Psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\sum_{j,k\geq 0}\alpha_{jk}(x_{1}){\beta}_{j}(x_{2})\gamma_{k}(x_{3}),

\displaystyle=\left(\upintop\nolimits\!\!\!\iint\frac{f(x,y_{1})-f(x,y_{2})}{y_{1}-y_{2}}\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{L}(x)\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{M_{1}}(y_{1})(M_{1}-M_{2})\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{M_{2}}(y_{2})\right)(I-{\rm i}M_{2})^{-1}

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\upintop\nolimits\!\!\!\iint\frac{f(x,y_{1})-f(x,y_{2})}{y_{1}-y_{2}}\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{L}(x)\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{M_{1}}(y_{1})(M_{1}(\varepsilon)-M_{2}(\varepsilon))\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{M_{2}}(y_{2})\right)(I-{\rm i}M_{2})^{-1}\\
=\!\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\upintop\nolimits\!\!\!\iint\!\frac{f(x,y_{1})\!-\!f(x,y_{2})}{y_{1}-y_{2}}\!\left(\!\frac{y_{1}}{1\!-\!{\rm i}\varepsilon y_{1}}\!-\!\frac{y_{2}}{1\!-\!{\rm i}\varepsilon y_{2}}\!\right)\!d{\mathscr{E}}_{L}(x)d{\mathscr{E}}_{M_{1}}(y_{1})d{\mathscr{E}}_{M_{2}}(y_{2})\right)\!(I-{\rm i}M_{2})^{\!-1}\\

=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\upintop\nolimits\!\!\!\iint\frac{f(x,y_{1})}{(1-{\rm i}\varepsilon y_{1})(1-{\rm i}\varepsilon y_{2})(1-{\rm i}y_{2})}\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{L}(x)\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{M_{1}}(y_{1})\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{M_{2}}(y_{2})\\
-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\upintop\nolimits\!\!\!\iint\frac{f(x,y_{2})}{(1-{\rm i}\varepsilon y_{1})(1-{\rm i}\varepsilon y_{2})(1-{\rm i}y_{2})}\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{L}(x)\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{M_{1}}(y_{1})\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{M_{2}}(y_{2})=X_{\varepsilon}-Y_{\varepsilon},

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}X_{\varepsilon}\\
=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\iint\frac{f(x,y_{1})}{1-{\rm i}y_{1}}\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{L}(x)\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{M_{1}}(y_{1})\right)(I-{\rm i}\varepsilon M_{1})^{-1}(I-{\rm i}M_{1})(I-{\rm i}M_{2})^{-1}(I-{\rm i}\varepsilon M_{2})^{-1}\\

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}Y_{\varepsilon}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\iint\frac{f(x,y_{2})}{1-{\rm i}y_{2}}\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{L}(x)(I-{\rm i}\varepsilon M_{1})^{-1}\,d{\mathscr{E}}_{M_{2}}(y_{2})\right)(I-{\rm i}\varepsilon M_{2})^{-1}

in the strong operator topology. Thus, we have proved that

in the strong operator topology.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let

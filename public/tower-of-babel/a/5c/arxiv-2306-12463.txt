Abstract




Abstract

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of
r
-uniform hypergraphs, and let
H
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph.
Then
H
is called
\mathcal{F}
-free if it does not contain any member of

\mathcal{F}
as a subhypergraph. The Turán number of
\mathcal{F}
, denoted by
ex_{r}(n,\mathcal{F})
, is the maximum number of hyperedges in an
\mathcal{F}
-free
n
-vertex
r
-uniform hypergraph.
Our current results are motivated by earlier results on Turán numbers of star forests and hypergraph star forests.
In particular, Lidický, Liu and Palmer [Electron. J. Combin. 20 (2013)] determined the Turán number
ex(n,F)
of a star forest
F
for sufficiently large
n
.
Recently, Khormali and Palmer [European. J. Combin. 102 (2022) 103506] generalized the above result to three different well-studied hypergraph settings, but restricted to the case that all stars in the hypergraph star forests are identical. We further generalize these results to general hypergraph star forests.

Key Words
: Turán number; Berge hypergraph; star forest

Introduction

The results in this paper deal with Turán numbers of hypergraph star forests. Our work is motivated by recent results by different groups of researchers, and aims at generalizing their results. Before we can give more details, we need to introduce and recall some essential terminology and notation.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of
r
-uniform hypergraphs. An
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
is called
\mathcal{F}
-free if it does not contain any
member of
\mathcal{F}
as its subhypergraph. Note that here we mean subhypergraph, and not necessarily induced subhypergraph.
The Turán number of
\mathcal{F}
, denoted by
ex_{r}(n,\mathcal{F})
, is the maximum number of hyperedges in an
r
-uniform
n
-vertex
\mathcal{F}
-free hypergraph. When
r=2
, instead of
writing
ex_{2}(n,\mathcal{G})
we use
ex(n,\mathcal{G})
for a family of graphs
\mathcal{G}
.
Determining the Turán numbers for specific graphs and graph classes is a classic and well-studied area within graph theory. This started already back in 1907, when Mantel
[
28
]
determined the Turán number of a triangle
K_{3}
. The term Turán number reflects the fact that in 1941, Turán
[
35
]
generalized Mantel’s result by his seminal theorem that settles the problem of determining the Turán number of a complete graph
K_{k}
for general
k
. Since then, there have appeared many papers and monographs on this type of problems. One of the most fundamental results within this area of research is the Erdős-Stone-Simonovits theorem, which gives the following asymptotic result for all
k
-chromatic graphs
F
:

ex(n,F)=\bigg(1-\frac{1}{k-1}\bigg)\frac{n^{2}}{2}+o(n^{2}).

Here
k
denotes the chromatic number of
F
,
i.e.
, the smallest number of colors needed to assign one color to each vertex of
F
in such a way that adjacent vertices receive different colors.
Note that the above formula only gives
ex(n,F)=o(n^{2})
for a bipartite (2-chromatic) graph
F
. Determining Turán numbers for bipartite graphs remains an active area of research. We refer to the survey of Füredi and Simonovits
[
12
]
for an extensive history.

In this paper, we focus on sparse bipartite graphs, namely star forests, and their hypergraph counterparts.
Fix a positive integer
\ell
. Let
S_{\ell}
denote the star with
\ell
edges,
i.e.
, the complete bipartite graph
K_{1,\ell}
.
A star forest is a graph consisting of a number of vertex-disjoint stars. Let
G
and
H
be two vertex-disjoint graphs. The union of
G
and
H
, denoted
by
G\cup H
, is the graph with vertex set
V(G)\cup V(H)
and edge set
E(G)\cup E(H)
. Denote by

k\cdot G
the graph composed of
k
pairwise vertex-disjoint copies of the graph
G
.

In 2013, Lidický, Liu and Palmer
[
26
]
determined the Turán number of a star forest for sufficiently large
n
.

Theorem 1.1

(Lidický, Liu and Palmer,
[
26
]
)
.

Let
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{k}
be
k
positive integers. For
n
sufficiently large,

ex\bigg(n,\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}S_{d_{i}}\bigg)=\mathop{max}\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}\Bigg\{(i-1)(n-i+1)+\binom{i-1}{2}+\left\lfloor\frac{d_{i}-1}{2}(n-i+1)\right\rfloor\Bigg\}.

We come back to this later, but first continue with a discussion on hypergraph analogues of Turán type extremal problems.
Determining Turán numbers for hypergraphs is another direction which has attracted a lot of attention.
One of the issues that we meet is to give a suitable and reasonable generalization of graph structures to hypergraph structures.
It is natural to generalize the notion of a complete graph to a complete hypergraph in the following way.
Since the edge set of a complete graph
K_{k}
on
k
vertices is the set of all possible 2-element subsets of these
k
vertices, the set of hyperedges of a
complete
r
-uniform
k
-vertex hypergraph is the set of all possible
r
-element subsets of these
k
vertices.
The difficulty of determining the Turán numbers for hypergraphs is reflected by the fact that we still do not
know the Turán number of
K^{3}_{4}
, the complete 3-uniform 4-vertex hypergraph.

In this paper, we aim to determine Turán numbers for analogues of star forests in the hypergraph setting.
For this, we will in fact consider three different hypergraph settings that are known from literature. The first hypergraph setting we consider is based on the notion of expansion.

Fix a graph
F
and an integer
r\geq 2
. The expansion of
F
is the
r
-uniform hypergraph
F^{+}
constructed by adding
r-2
new distinct vertices to each edge of
F
, where all added new vertices are distinct. When
r=2
, the expansion
F^{+}
is exactly the original graph
F
. There already exist several papers involving Turán numbers for various expansions (See,
e.g.
,
[
2
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
29
,
31
]
).
The case with
F^{+}=S^{+}_{\ell}
has a considerable history. See the survey
[
30
]
for an overview.

In 1977, Duke and Erdős
[
4
]
proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(Duke and Erdős,
[
4
]
)
.

Fix integers
\ell\geq 2
and
r\geq 3
. Then there exists a constant
c(r)
such that for sufficiently large
n
,

ex_{r}(n,S^{+}_{\ell})\leq c(r)\ell(\ell-1)n^{r-2}.

We can construct an extremal hypergraph with size
ex_{r}(n,S^{+}_{\ell})
of order
n^{r-2}
as follows.
Take an
r
-uniform hypergraph all hyperedges of which contain a fixed pair of vertices.

For presenting the next result due to Erdős
[
5
]
, let
M_{k}
be a matching of size
k
,
i.e.
,
a set of
k
disjoint edges. We can view
M_{k}
as a star forest each star of which is
S_{1}
, an edge. The following well-known theorem
gives the Turán number of the expansion of
M_{k}
, when the number of vertices is sufficiently large.

Theorem 1.3

(Erdős,
[
5
]
)
.

Fix integers
k\geq 1
and
r\geq 2
. Then for sufficiently large
n
,

ex_{r}(n,M^{+}_{k})=\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-k+1}{r}.

In a recently published paper, Khormali and Palmer
[
21
]
generalized the above result to the expansion
k\cdot S^{+}_{\ell}
of a star forest
k\cdot S_{\ell}
composed
of
k
copies of the star
S_{\ell}
.

Theorem 1.4

(Khormali and Palmer,
[
21
]
)
.

Fix integers
\ell,k\geq 1
and
r\geq 2
. Then for sufficiently large
n
,

ex_{r}(n,k\cdot S^{+}_{\ell})=\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-k+1}{r}+ex_{r}(n-k+1,S^{+}_{\ell}).

In our first new main result, we generalize the above result by obtaining the Turán number of
the expansion of a general star forest. We postpone our proof of the next result to Section
2
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Fix integers
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{k}
,
k\geq 1
and
r\geq 2
. Then for
sufficiently large
n
,

ex_{r}\bigg(n,\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}}\bigg)=\mathop{max}\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}\Bigg\{\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-i+1}{r}+ex_{r}\Big(n-i+1,S^{+}_{d_{i}}\Big)\Bigg\}.

For our next main result, we turn to another hypergraph setting, which is based on the notion of a linear hypergraph.
An
r
-uniform hypergraph is called linear if every pair of hyperedges of it shares at most one vertex.
It is easy to see that each expansion is a linear hypergraph. Hence, we can consider corresponding problems among linear hypergraphs. Let
F
be a linear
r
-uniform hypergraph.
Then the linear Turán number of
F
, denoted by
ex^{lin}_{r}(n,F)
, is the maximum number of hyperedges in an
r
-uniform
n
-vertex
F
-free linear hypergraph.
A seminal result in this context settles the problem of determining the linear Turán number
ex^{lin}_{3}(n,C^{+}_{3})
of
the expansion of
C_{3}
, which is equivalent to the famous (6,3)-problem.
Motivated by this result, more and more papers on this topic are starting to emerge (See,
e.g.
,
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
13
,
15
,
18
,
19
,
25
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
).

In 2022, Khormali and Palmer
[
21
]
determined the linear Turán number of
k\cdot S^{+}_{l}
asymptotically for sufficiently large
n
.

Theorem 1.6

(Khormali and Palmer,
[
21
]
)
.

Fix integers
\ell,k\geq 1
and
r\geq 2
. Then for sufficiently large
n
,

ex^{lin}_{r}(n,k\cdot S^{+}_{\ell})\leq\bigg(\frac{\ell-1}{r}+\frac{k-1}{r-1}\bigg)(n-k+1)+\frac{\binom{k-1}{2}}{\binom{r}{2}}.

Moreover, this bound is sharp asymptotically.

In our second main result, we determine the linear Turán number of a general hypergraph star forest asymptotically for sufficiently large
n
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Fix integers
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{k}
,
k\geq 1
and
r\geq 2
. Then for sufficiently large
n
,

ex^{lin}_{r}\bigg(n,\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}}\bigg)\leq\mathop{max}\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{d_{i}-1}{r}+\frac{i-1}{r-1}\bigg)(n-i+1)+\frac{\binom{i-1}{2}}{\binom{r}{2}}\bigg\}.

Moreover, this bound is sharp asymptotically.

We postpone our proof of the above result to Section
3
.
In the following, we turn to a third hypergraph setting, as defined by Gerbner and Palmer
[
16
]
. Let
F
be a graph. An
r
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is called
a Berge-
F
if there is an injection
f:V(F)\rightarrow V(\mathcal{H})

and a bijection
f^{\prime}:E(F)\rightarrow E(\mathcal{H})
such that for every edge
uv\in E(F)
we have
\{f(u),f(v)\}\subseteq f^{\prime}(uv)
. For a fixed graph
F
, we can
observe that there are many different hypergraphs that are a Berge-
F
, and that
a fixed hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
can be a Berge-
F
for more than one graph
F
. For a short survey of extremal results for Berge-
F
-free hypergraphs see Subsection 5.2.2 in
[
17
]
.

In 2022, by considering distinct values of
r,k,\ell
in the following two results,
Khormali and Palmer
[
21
]
determined the Turán number of a Berge-
k\cdot S_{\ell}

asymptotically for sufficiently large
n
.

Theorem 1.8

(Khormali and Palmer,
[
21
]
)
.

Fix integers
\ell,k\geq 2
and
r\geq\ell+k-1
. Then for sufficiently large
n
,

ex_{r}(n,\mbox{Berge-}k\cdot S_{\ell})\leq\frac{\ell-1}{r-k+1}(n-k+1).

Moreover, this bound is sharp whenever
r-k+1
divides
n-k+1
.

Theorem 1.9

(Khormali and Palmer,
[
21
]
)
.

Fix integers
\ell,k\geq 2
and
r\leq\ell+k-2
. Then for sufficiently large
n
,

ex_{r}(n,\mbox{Berge-}k\cdot S_{\ell})\leq\bigg(\binom{\ell+k-1}{r}-\binom{k-1}{r}\bigg)\left\lceil\frac{n-k+1}{\ell}\right\rceil+\binom{k-1}{r}.

Moreover, this bound is sharp whenever
\ell
divides
n-k+1
.

With our final two main results, we generalize
the above results to more general Berge-star forests. We postpone our proofs to Section
4
and
5
, respectively.

Theorem 1.10
.

Fix integers
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{k}\geq 2
,
k\geq 2
and
r\geq d_{1}+k-1
. Then
for sufficiently large
n
,

ex_{r}\bigg(n,\mbox{Berge-}\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}S_{d_{i}}\bigg)\leq\mathop{max}\limits_{1\leq s\leq k-1}\bigg\{\frac{d_{s+1}-1}{r-s}(n-s)\bigg\}.

Moreover, this bound is sharp whenever
r-s
divides
n-s
for
1\leq s\leq k-1
.

Theorem 1.11
.

Fix integers
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{k}\geq 2
,
k\geq 2
and
r\leq d_{1}+k-2
. Then
for sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle ex_{r}\bigg(n,\mbox{Berge-}\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}S_{d_{i}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{max}\bigg\{\mathop{max}\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}\bigg\{\bigg(\binom{d_{i}+i-1}{r}-\binom{i-1}{r}\bigg)\left\lceil\frac{n-i+1}{d_{i}}\right\rceil+\binom{i-1}{r},

\displaystyle\frac{d_{i}-1}{r-i+1}(n-i+1)\bigg\},\mathop{max}\limits_{1\leq c<k-1}\bigg\{\frac{\mathop{max}\Big\{\binom{d_{c}+c}{r-1},d_{c}+c\Big\}}{r-c}(n-c)\bigg\}\bigg\}.

Moreover, this bound is sharp whenever
d_{i}
and
r-i+1
divide
n-i+1
for
1\leq i\leq k
.

The remainder of this paper is devoted to the proofs of our new contributions. We complete the paper by a short concluding section.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.5

By Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, the theorem holds for
r=2
or
d_{1}=1
. Thus,
it suffices to show that the theorem holds when
r\geq 3
and
d_{1}\geq 2
.

For the lower bound, we can construct an extremal hypergraph
H
as follows. Fix
i
such that
1\leq i\leq k
, and let
A
and
B
be vertex sets of
i-1
and
n-i+1
vertices, respectively. Firstly, we embed an
S^{+}_{d_{i}}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraph with
ex_{r}(n-i+1,S^{+}_{d_{i}})
hyperedges into
B
. Next, we add every
r
-set that is incident to
A
to our hypergraph. Since
B
contains no copy of
S^{+}_{d_{i}}
, each
S^{+}_{d_{j}}
with
1\leq j\leq i
must contain at least one vertex of
A
.
Therefore,
H
is
\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}}
-free. Thus, we can choose
i
such that the number of hyperedges in
H

attains the value in the statement of the theorem.

In the following, we consider the upper bound. Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
r
-uniform
n
-vertex hypergraph with

|E(\mathcal{H})|>\mathop{max}\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}\Bigg\{\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-i+1}{r}+ex_{r}\Big(n-i+1,S^{+}_{d_{i}}\Big)\Bigg\}.

We need to show that
\mathcal{H}
is not
\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}}
-free. We proceed by induction on
k
. For
k=1
, since
|E(\mathcal{H})|>ex_{r}(n,S^{+}_{d_{1}})
we have
\mathcal{H}
is not
S^{+}_{d_{1}}
-free. Let
k>1
and assume the statement holds for
k-1
. In the following, we discuss two cases based on the maximum degree
\Delta(\mathcal{H})
of
\mathcal{H}
.

Case 1:

\Delta(\mathcal{H})<\frac{1}{\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}((r-1)d_{i}+1)}\bigg(\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-k+1}{r}\bigg).

Assume that
\mathcal{H}
contains a copy of
\cup_{i=1}^{t}S^{+}_{d_{i}}
such that
t
is maximal.
We claim that
t\geq k
. Otherwise, there are at most
\cup_{i=1}^{k-1}((r-1)d_{i}+1)
vertices spanned by

\cup_{i=1}^{t}S^{+}_{d_{i}}
. After removing these vertices and the incident hyperedges we have at least

\displaystyle|E(\mathcal{H})|

\displaystyle-\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}((r-1)d_{i}+1)\cdot\Delta(\mathcal{H})>|E(\mathcal{H})|-\bigg(\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-k+1}{r}\bigg)

\displaystyle>\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-k+1}{r}+ex_{r}\Big(n-k+1,S^{+}_{d_{k}}\Big)-\bigg(\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-k+1}{r}\bigg)

\displaystyle=ex_{r}\Big(n-k+1,S^{+}_{d_{k}}\Big)

hyperedges. Therefore, there is a copy of
S^{+}_{d_{k}}
that is vertex-disjoint from

\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{t}S^{+}_{d_{i}}
. This violates the maximality of
t
, a contradiction.

Case 2.

\Delta(\mathcal{H})\geq\frac{1}{\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}((r-1)d_{i}+1)}\bigg(\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-k+1}{r}\bigg).

Let
u
be a vertex of maximum degree. Since
d(u)\leq\binom{n-1}{r-1}
,

\displaystyle|E(\mathcal{H})|-d(u)

\displaystyle>\mathop{max}\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}\bigg\{\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-i+1}{r}+ex_{r}\Big(n-i+1,S^{+}_{d_{i}}\Big)\bigg\}-\binom{n-1}{r-1}

\displaystyle=\mathop{max}\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}\bigg\{\binom{n-1}{r}-\binom{n-(i-1)}{r}+ex_{r}\Big(n-(i-1),S^{+}_{d_{i}}\Big)\bigg\}

\displaystyle\overset{d^{\prime}_{j}=d_{j+1}}{\geq}\mathop{max}\limits_{1\leq j\leq k-1}\bigg\{\binom{n-1}{r}-\binom{n-j}{r}+ex_{r}\Big(n-j,S^{+}_{d^{\prime}_{j}}\Big)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=ex_{r}\bigg(n-1,\bigcup\limits_{i=2}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}}\bigg).

Therefore, we can consider a copy of
\bigcup\limits_{i=2}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}}
(that we indicate as
\bigcup\limits_{i=2}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}}
in the sequel)
after removing
u
from
\mathcal{H}
, and apply induction on
the resulting hypergraph.

Next we will show that there is a copy of
S^{+}_{d_{1}}
with center
u
that is vertex-disjoint from

\bigcup\limits_{i=2}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}}
. Observe that
u
and any vertex
v\in V(\bigcup\limits_{i=2}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}})
are contained in at most
\binom{n-2}{r-2}
common hyperedges. Therefore the number of hyperedges containing
u
and a vertex of

\bigcup\limits_{i=2}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}}
is at most

\bigcup\limits_{i=2}^{k}((r-1)d_{i}+1)\binom{n-2}{r-2}=O(n^{r-2}).

Note that
d(u)=\Omega(n^{r-1})
. Therefore if we remove the hyperedges incident to

\bigcup\limits_{i=2}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}}
, there still are
\Omega(n^{r-1})
hyperedges incident to
u
. As
r\geq 3
and
d_{1}\geq 2
, we may apply Theorem
1.2
to these hyperedges to get a copy of
S^{+}_{d_{1}}
that is vertex-disjoint from

\bigcup\limits_{i=2}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}}
,
i.e.
,
\mathcal{H}
contains a copy of
\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}}
, a contradiction.

Thus, the theorem holds. ∎

3
Proof of Theorem
1.7

Before proving Theorem
1.7
, we would like to introduce an average degree lemma obtained by Khormali and Palmer
[
21
]
that will be used in
our proof.

Lemma 3.1

(Average Degree Lemma,
[
21
]
)
.

Fix integers
d
and
\Delta
and a constant
0\leq\varepsilon<1
. If
\mathcal{H}
is a hypergraph with average degree at least
d-\varepsilon
and maximum degree at most
\Delta
, then the number of vertices in
\mathcal{H}
of degree less than
d
is at most

\frac{\Delta-d+\varepsilon}{\Delta-d+1}n.

In particular, the number of vertices in
\mathcal{H}
of degree at least
d
is
\Omega(n)
.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.7
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
r
-uniform
n
-vertex linear hypergraph that is
\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}}
-free. Let
A
be the vertex set
each vertex of which has degree at least some fixed large enough constant
D=D(d_{1},k,r)
. If
|A|\geq k
, then we can greedily embed a copy of
\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{i}}
into
\mathcal{H}
. Thus we have
|A|\leq k-1
.

After removing the vertices of
A
and the hyperedges incident to them from
\mathcal{H}
, we
obtain an
r
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. The maximum degree in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is less than
D
. Let
|A|=i-1
. Therefore
1\leq i\leq k
. If the average degree in
\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
is at least
d_{i}-\varepsilon
for any
0<\varepsilon<1
, then by
Lemma
3.1
we have
\Omega(n)
vertices of degree at least
d_{i}
in
\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
.
In this case we can greedily embed a copy of
\bigcup\limits_{t=i}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{t}}
into
\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
.
Then, together with
\bigcup\limits_{t=1}^{i-1}S^{+}_{d_{t}}
consisting of hyperedges incident to
A
, we
obtain a copy of
\bigcup\limits_{t=1}^{k}S^{+}_{d_{t}}
in
\mathcal{H}
, a contradiction.

Therefore, the average degree in
\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
is at most
d_{i}-1
. Thus we have

|E(\mathcal{H^{\prime}})|\leq\frac{d_{i}-1}{r}(n-|A|).

Let
B
be the vertex set of
\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
,
i.e.
,
B=V(\mathcal{H})\setminus A
. Denote by
E(A,B)
the hyperedge set of
\mathcal{H}
that contain at least one vertex of
A
and one vertex of
B
. To count the size of
E(A,B)
, we first count the number of pairs
(h,\{u,v\})
where
h
is a hyperedge of
\mathcal{H}
,
u\in A\cap h
and
v\in B\cap h
.
Let
h\in E(A,B)
. Then we have
|A\cap h|
choices for
u
and
|B\cap h|
choices for
v
. Thus the number of pairs
(h,\{u,v\})
is

\sum\limits_{h\in E(A,B)}|A\cap h||B\cap h|\geq\sum\limits_{h\in E(A,B)}(r-1)=|E(A,B)|(r-1).

On the other hand, for a fixed
u
and
v
there is at most one hyperedge containing them, as
\mathcal{H}
is linear. Thus, the number of pairs
(h,\{u,v\})
is at most
|A|(n-|A|)
.

Combining these two
estimates
for the number of pairs
(h,\{u,v\})
, and solving for
|E(A,B)|
gives

|E(A,B)|\leq\frac{|A|}{r-1}(n-|A|).

Furthermore, the maximum number of hyperedges contained completely in
A
is at most

Just like the construction of the

Denote by

The Cartesian product of two sets

Note that if
\mathcal{H}
is
r
-uniform and
\mathcal{G}

-uniform, then the hyperedges in

. We claim that if two hypergraphs

we have that among the remaining hyperedges there is a Berge-
S_{\cup_{1\leq j\leq k-1}(d_{j}+1)+d_{k}}
, denoted by
\mathcal{S}
, with center
u
. Thus
\mathcal{S}
is hyperedge-disjoint from the Berge-
\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}S_{d_{j}}
. However, the skeleton of both of these subhypergraphs may intersect. At most
\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}(d_{j}+1)
vertices of the skeleton of
\mathcal{S}

are shared with the skeleton of the Berge-
\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}S_{d_{j}}
. Therefore, there is a Berge-
S_{d_{k}}
whose skeleton is disjoint from the skeleton of the Berge-
\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}S_{d_{j}}
. In particular, we have a Berge-
\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}}
, a contradiction.
∎

Next we distinguish two cases.

Case 1.
c=k-1
.

We will discuss two subcases.

Subcase 1.1.
r\leq d_{k}+k-2
.

We claim that each vertex of
B
has degree at most
\binom{d_{k}+k-2}{r-1}
. Otherwise, by Lemma
4.2
we have that there is a Berge-
S_{d_{k}+k-1}
with center
x
which has degree greater than
\binom{d_{k}+k-2}{r-1}
. The skeleton of this Berge-
S_{d_{k}+k-1}
uses at most
k-1
vertices from
A
, so there remains a Berge-
S_{d_{k}}
, denoted by
\mathcal{S}
, whose skeleton is contained in
B
. As
D
is large enough, we may construct
k-1
more pairwise hyperedge-disjoint copies of a Berge-
S_{d_{1}}
(with pairwise vertex-disjoint skeletons) that are hyperedge-disjoint from
\mathcal{S}
and whose skeletons are vertex-disjoint
from the skeleton of
\mathcal{S}
. Thus, we have a copy of a Berge-
\bigcup\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}S_{d_{i}}
in
\mathcal{H}
, a contradiction.

Now we compare
\mathcal{H}
with
\mathcal{H}_{k}
. Firstly, by definition we have
|E(\mathcal{H})|\geq|E(\mathcal{H}^{*})|\geq|E(\mathcal{H}_{k})|
. Suppose that there are two vertices
y\in A
and
y^{*}\in A^{*}_{k}
such that
d_{\mathcal{H}}(y)>d_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}(y^{*})
.
For each
1\leq j\leq r-1
, we define

E^{y}_{j}=\{e\setminus A|y\in e\in E(\mathcal{H}),|e\setminus A|=j\}

and

E^{y^{*}}_{j}=\{f\setminus A^{*}|y^{*}\in f\in E(\mathcal{H}_{k}),|f\setminus A^{*}|=j\}.

It is easy to see that the members of
E^{y}_{j}
have multiplicity at most
\binom{k-2}{r-1-j}
and those in
E^{y^{*}}_{j}

have multiplicity exactly
\binom{k-2}{r-1-j}
.

If
k-1\geq r-1
, then by the construction of
\mathcal{H}_{k}
we have that each vertex of
B^{*}_{k}
is in a hyperedge with each subset of
A^{*}_{k}
of size
r-1
. This implies that
|E^{y^{*}}_{1}|\geq|E^{y}_{1}|
. When
k-1<r-1
, then
E^{y^{*}}_{1}=E^{y}_{1}=\emptyset
. Therefore, since
d_{\mathcal{H}}(y)\geq d_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}(y^{*})
, we have that
|E^{y}_{j}|>|E^{y^{*}}_{j}|
for some
j\geq 2
. Let
\mathcal{G}
be the
j
-uniform hypergraph resulting from deleting all repeated hyperedges in this
E^{y}_{j}
. Thus, the number of hyperedges in the
j
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{G}
can be calculated as

\displaystyle|E(\mathcal{G})|

\displaystyle\geq\binom{k-2}{r-1-j}^{-1}|E^{y}_{j}|

\displaystyle>\binom{k-2}{r-1-j}^{-1}|E^{y^{*}}_{j}|

\displaystyle=\binom{k-2}{r-1-j}^{-1}\binom{k-2}{r-1-j}\frac{n-k+1}{d_{k}}\binom{d_{k}}{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-k+1}{d_{k}}\binom{d_{k}}{j}

\displaystyle\geq ex_{j}(n-k+1,\mbox{Berge-}S_{d_{k}}).

Therefore, there is a
j
-uniform Berge-
S_{d_{k}}
in
\mathcal{G}
on the vertices of
B
. As each hyperedge of
\mathcal{G}
is contained in a hyperedge of
\mathcal{H}
, this corresponds to a Berge-
S_{d_{k}}
in
\mathcal{H}
whose skeleton is contained in
B
. As before, the degree condition on the vertices in
A
guarantees the existence of a Berge-
\bigcup\limits_{1\leq j\leq k-1}S_{d_{j}}

that together with this Berge-
S_{d_{k}}
forms a Berge-
\bigcup\limits_{1\leq j\leq k}S_{d_{j}}
in
\mathcal{H}
, a contradiction.

Therefore, every vertex
y\in A
has degree at most that of the vertices in
A^{*}_{k}
in the construction
\mathcal{H}_{k}
. Every vertex in
B
has degree at most

\binom{d_{k}+k-2}{r-1}
while every vertex in
B^{*}_{k}
has degree exactly
\binom{d_{k}+k-2}{r-1}
.
Thus,
|E(\mathcal{H})|\leq|E(\mathcal{H}_{k})|\leq|E(\mathcal{H}^{*})|
.

Subcase 1.2.
r\geq d_{k}+k-1
.

We claim that each vertex of
B
has degree at most
d_{k}-1
.
Suppose that there is a vertex
v
in
B
of degree at least
d_{k}
. Let us remove vertices from the hyperedges incident to
v
so that the resulting hyperedges are disjoint from
A
and of size exactly
d_{k}
. This is possible as
r\geq d_{k}+k-1
. By Lemma
4.1
, there is a Berge-
S_{d_{k}}
with center
v
among these
d_{k}
-uniform hyperedges. The skeleton of this Berge-
S_{d_{k}}
is necessarily contained in
B
. It is easy to see that this Berge-
S_{d_{k}}
corresponds to a Berge-
S_{d_{k}}
, denoted by
\mathcal{S}
, in
\mathcal{H}
whose skeleton is contained in
B
.
Since the degrees of the vertices in
A
are large enough, we can construct a Berge-
\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}S_{d_{j}}
whose hyperedges and skeleton are disjoint from those of
\mathcal{S}
. Therefore
\mathcal{H}
contains a Berge-
\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}}
, a contradiction.
Thus we have

\displaystyle r|E(\mathcal{H})|

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{u\in A}d(u)+\sum\limits_{v\in B}d(v)

\displaystyle\leq(k-1)|E(\mathcal{H})|+(d_{k}-1)(n-k+1).

Solving for
|E(\mathcal{H})|
gives

|E(\mathcal{H})|\leq\frac{d_{k}-1}{r-k+1}(n-k+1)\leq E(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}).

Case 2.
c<k-1
.

If
r\leq d_{c}+c
, then we claim that
d(B)\leq\binom{d_{c}+c}{r-1}
, where
d(B)
is the average degree of the vertices in
B
. Otherwise, we have
d(B)\geq\binom{d_{c}+c}{r-1}+\varepsilon
for a constant
0<\varepsilon<1
. Let
s
be the number of vertices of degree at most
\binom{d_{c}+c}{r-1}
in
B
. Recall that the vertices in
B
have degree at most
D
. Thus, the sum of degrees in
B
is at most
\binom{d_{c}+c}{r-1}s+D(n-c-s)
. Combining these estimates and solving for
s
gives

s\leq\frac{D-\binom{d_{c}+c}{r-1}-\varepsilon}{D-\binom{d_{c}+c}{r-1}}(n-c)=(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})(n-c)

for some
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
not depending on
n
. Therefore, the number of vertices of degree greater than
\binom{d_{c}+c}{r-1}
is
\varepsilon^{\prime}n=\Omega(n)
.

Let us call a pair of vertices
u,v\in B

far
if they do not share a common neighbor in
B
(they may still have a common neighbor in
A
). Since the vertices of
B
have constant maximum degree we can find a subset
B^{\prime}
of size
\Omega(n)
such that all vertices have degree greater than
\binom{d_{c}+c}{r-1}
and all pairs of vertices are far.

For each vertex
u\in B^{\prime}
, by Lemma
4.2
we have that there is a Berge-
S_{d_{c}+c+1}
with center
u
whose skeleton is disjoint from
A
. Since any two vertices of
B^{\prime}
do not share a common neighbor in
B
, we have a collection of hyperedge-disjoint copies of a Berge-
S_{d_{c}}
. As
n
is large enough, we can find a Berge-
\bigcup\limits_{c+1\leq j\leq k}S_{d_{j}}
whose skeleton is disjoint from
A
. And since the degrees of the vertices in
A
are large enough, we can construct a Berge-
\bigcup\limits_{1\leq j\leq c}S_{d_{j}}
whose hyperedges and skeleton are disjoint from those of Berge-
\bigcup\limits_{c+1\leq j\leq k}S_{d_{j}}
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{H}
contains a Berge-
\bigcup\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}S_{d_{i}}
, a contradiction.

Observe that
\sum\limits_{x\in A}d(x)\leq c|E(\mathcal{H})|
and

r|E(\mathcal{H})|=\sum\limits_{x\in B}d(x)+\sum\limits_{x\in A}d(x)\leq d(B)(n-c)+c|E(\mathcal{H})|.

Solving for
|E(\mathcal{H})|
gives

|E(\mathcal{H})|\leq\frac{\binom{d_{c}+c}{r-1}}{r-c}(n-c).

Since
c<k-1
,

|E(\mathcal{H})|\leq\mathop{max}\limits_{1\leq c<k-1}\bigg\{\frac{\binom{d_{c}+c}{r-1}}{r-c}(n-c)\bigg\}.

Similarly, if
r\geq d_{c}+c+1
then we have
d(B)\leq d_{c}+c
. In this case, we have that

|E(\mathcal{H})|\leq\mathop{max}\limits_{1\leq c<k-1}\bigg\{\frac{d_{c}+c}{r-c}(n-c)\bigg\}.

∎

6
Concluding Remarks

We complete this paper with two consequences of our results for
generalized Turán numbers.
Given two graphs
H
and
F
, the generalized Turán number of
F
,
denoted by
ex(n,H,F)
,
is defined as the maximum number of copies of a subgraph
H
in an
n
-vertex
F
-free graph.
These generalized Turán numbers
have been studied by different groups of researchers for
many types of graphs.
We refer the interested readers to
[
1
,
20
,
27
,
36
,
37
,
38
]

for more details.

Let
G
be an

n
-vertex

F
-free graph. We define an
r
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with the same vertex set as
G
,
such that
an
r
-set in
\mathcal{H}
is a hyperedge if and only if it is the vertex set of a
K_{r}
in
G
. Note that
this implies that

\mathcal{H}
contains no Berge-
F
if
G
is
F
-free. Thus,

ex(n,K_{r},F)\leq ex_{r}(n,\mbox{Berge-}F).

(1)

For the case that

r\geq d_{1}+k-1
, by Theorem
1.10
and (
1
) we
obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 6.1
.

Fix integers
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\ldots\geq d_{k}\geq 2
and
k\geq 2
. If
r\geq d_{1}+k-1
, then for
n
large enough,

ex\bigg(n,K_{r},\mbox{Berge-}\bigcup\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}S_{d_{i}}\bigg)\leq\mathop{max}\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}\bigg\{\frac{d_{i}-1}{r-i+1}(n-i+1)\bigg\}.

Similarly, for the case that

r\leq d_{1}+k-1
, by Theorem
1.11
and (
1
) we
obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 6.2
.

Fix integers
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\ldots\geq d_{k}\geq 2
and
k\geq 2
. If
r\leq d_{1}+k-2
, then for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle ex\bigg(n,K_{r},\mbox{Berge-}\bigcup\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}S_{d_{i}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{max}\bigg\{\mathop{max}\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}\bigg\{\bigg(\binom{d_{i}+i-1}{r}-\binom{i-1}{r}\bigg)\left\lceil\frac{n-i+1}{d_{i}}\right\rceil+\binom{i-1}{r},

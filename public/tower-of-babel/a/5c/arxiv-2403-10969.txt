Existence of strongly nonlocal sets of three states in any N N -partite system
Zong-Xing Xiong 1 , Mao-Sheng Li 2 1 Institute of Quantum Computing and Software, School of Computer Science and Engineering, Sun Yat-Sen University, Guangzhou 510006, China 2 School of Mathematics, South China University of Technology, Guangzhou 510641, China

Abstract
The notion of strong nonlocality, which refers to local irreducibility of a set of orthogonal multipartite quantum states across each bipartition of the subsystems, was put forward by Halder et al. in [Phys. Rev. Lett. 122, 040403 (2019)]. Here, we show the existence of three orthogonal quantum states in ( ℂ 2 ) ⊗ N (\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes N} that cannot be perfectly distinguished locally across any bipartition of the subsystems. Specifically, all these three states are genuinely entangled, among which two are the N N -qubit GHZ pairs. Since any three locally indistinguishable states are always locally irreducible, the three N N -partite orthogonal states we present are strongly nonlocal. Thus, the caridnality of strongly nonlocal sets here is dramatically smaller than all known ones.

Introduction.
In 1999, C.H.Bennett (and his co-workers) introduced a new form of quantum nonlocality, which serves to explore the fundamental question about locally accessing global information encoded in a set of quantum states
Bennett99

\mathbb{C}^{3}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{3}
that cannot be perfectly distinguished by the separated subsystems, provided only local operations and classical communications (LOCC) are available by them. He named this phenomenon “quantume nonlocality without entanglement”, as opposed to the stereotype of many people that to manifest nonlocality, existence of entanglement is always required. Since then, such form of distinguishability-based nonlocality has been studied extensively (see

Although entanglement is not necessary for this form of nonlocality, lots of evidence have been found showing that entangled states are generally more difficult to be distinguished locally. For example, it has been shown by Horodecki et al. that if a set of full orthogonal basis in an arbitrary two-partite system is locally distinguishable, then they must be all product states
Horodecki03

Hayashi06

With that said, entanglement is to a large extent responsible for local indistinguishability, thought there also exist exceptions on some occasions

Local irreducibility.
In
Halder19
, Halder et al. put forward the notion of “local irreducibility”. A set of orthogonal multipartite quantum states are called locally irreducible if it is impossible to eleminate one or more states from the whole set, with restriction that only orthogonality–preserving local measurements (OPLM) are allowed. By definition, a locally irreducible set of multipartite quantum states must be locally indistinguishable, while the opposite is generally not true.

Genuine nonlocality and strong nonlocality.
A set of orthogonal multipartite quantum states are called genuinely nonlocal if they cannot be locally distinguished (perfectly) through any bipartition of the subsystems. It is immediate that genuine nonlocality implies local indistinguishability due to definition. In the literature, several techniques have been proposed for detecting genuinely nonlocal sets of different types of multipartite quantum states

Genuinely (strongly) nonlocal sets of three states.
Owing to Lemma 1 and also the definition of strong nonlocality, any genuinely nonlocal set of three states must also be strongly nonlocal. Since any two orthogonal states are always locally distinguishable, they cannot be genuinely nonlocal. It’s then natural to consider the existence of three genuinely nonlocal states in multipartite systems. Actually, this seems so improbable that few of us would ever be curious about it. By definition, genuine nonlocality is much stronger than local indistinguishability, especially when a large number of subsystems are considered. In the scenarios of large
N

Conclusion.
In this work, we show the existence of genuinely nonlocal sets of three orthogonal quantum states in any
N
-partite system. Since any three locally indistinguishable states are always locally irreducible, the three states we present are also strongly nonlocal.

The notion of strong nonlocality was put forward by Halder et al. in
Halder19
. There, the authors also questioned about the the existence of strong nonlocality with genuine multipartite entanglement. The works
Wang21
,
Hu24
had already answered this question positively, yet the constructions there require far more states than here. The strongly nonlocal sets we present are the first examples in systems
(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes N}
and their caridnality is dramatically smaller than the known ones. Unlike locally indistinguishable sets, however, the supersets of a locally irreducible set may not be locally irreducible. Therefore, it’s natural to question further: do strongly nonlocal sets of
d=4,5,\cdots
states exist in
N
-partite systems?

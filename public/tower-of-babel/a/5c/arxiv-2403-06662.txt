A PDE Framework of Consensus-Based Optimization for Objectives with Multiple Global Minimizers
Massimo Fornasier, Lukang Sun

Abstract
Consensus-based optimization (CBO) is an agent-based derivative-free method for non-smooth global optimization that has been introduced in 2017 [ 40 ] , leveraging a surprising interplay between stochastic exploration and Laplace’s principle. In addition to its versatility and effectiveness in handling high-dimensional, non-convex, and non-smooth optimization problems, this approach lends itself well to theoretical analysis. Indeed, its dynamics is governed by a degenerate nonlinear Fokker–Planck equation, whose large time behavior explains the convergence of the method. Recent results [ 10 , 23 ] provide guarantees of convergence under the restrictive assumption of unique global minimizer for the objective function. In this work, we propose a novel and simple variation of CBO to tackle non-convex optimization problems with multiple global minimizers. Despite the simplicity of this new model, its analysis is particularly challenging because of its nonlinearity and nonlocal nature. We prove existence of solutions of the corresponding nonlinear Fokker–Planck equation and we show exponential concentration in time to the set of minimizers made of multiple smooth, convex, and compact components. Our proofs require combining several ingredients, such as delicate geometrical arguments, new variants of a quantitative Laplace principle, ad hoc regularizations and approximations, and regularity theory for parabolic equations. Ultimately, this result suggests that the corresponding CBO algorithm, formulated as an Euler–Maruyama discretization of the underlying empirical stochastic process, tends to converge to multiple global minimizers.

Introduction

Computing efficiently minimal points of a potentially non-convex and non-smooth objective functions is a highly relevant problem across diverse applications spanning applied mathematics, science, technology, engineering, and machine learning. The global optimization task can be succinctly defined as:

(\arg)\min_{v\in\Omega}f(v).

(1)

Here, we aim to find the minimal values
f(v)
with the variable
v
within the state space
\Omega
, a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
d
may in some case be notably large. Below we are interested in unconstrained optimizations only, for which
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. While this problem is straightforward to pose, solving it can be extremely daunting when dealing with general non-convex objective functions, as there are no clear directional or geometrical principles to guide the search for global minima. The difficulty of this task is further compounded when we attempt to address it using methods reliant solely on local information, such as gradient descent techniques. This is due to the abundance of local minima resulting from the non-convex nature of the problem. To address these limitations of local higher-order methods, various approaches have been explored in computer science and engineering. In recent years, inspiration has been drawn from the concept of “swarm intelligence”, which is rooted in physical or biological models and involves interacting particle or agent systems. Among these methods, gradient-free techniques, which rely solely on evaluations of the objective function, have proven to be sensible strategies in scenarios with low regularity or when the computation of gradients is prohibitively expensive.

Relevance of Consensus-Based Optimization in applications.

Besides these theoretical results, CBO has been used successfully with state-of-the-art performances in several high-dimensional benchmark applications and we recall here a few of them:
[

applies CBO for a phase retrieval problem, robust subspace detection, and the robust computation of eigenfaces;

Variations of the CBO methods have also been put forward to address global optimization problems with constraints. In cases of equality-constrained problems, it is handled by ensuring that the dynamics stay within the feasible manifold at all times, as done in

Objectives with multiple minimizers and contribution of this work.

All the results, applications, and extensions reported above assume explicitly or implicitly that the objective function
f
to be optimized possesses a unique minimizer. Indeed, when
f
has multiple global minimizers, the original CBO
dynamic (
4
) may have pathological behaviors as it is not designed to converge to multiple minimizers. The pathological cases may be rare, however they can occur, for example: let
d=1,f(v):=(v-1)^{2}\zeta_{v\geq 0}+(v+1)^{2}\zeta_{v\leq 0},\rho_{0}=\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, where
\zeta
is the characteristic function, then we know
1
and
-1
are the two global minimizers of
f
and

for any
t\geq 0
, this means if
\overline{V}_{t}
starts from
0
, then it will always stay at
0
, however
0
is not a global minimizer of
f
. Moreover, given the fact that in (
4

In order to cope with optimizations with multiple minimizers recently a variation of CBO was proposed in
[

]
to allow for multiple consensus points, whose equation reads

Paper Structure.

The main results of this work are separated into two cases:

1.

is compact, convex and has smooth boundary;

2.

The first case is certainly simpler and is included in the second case, however it encapsulates the key insights for the analysis, so once the readers understand this simpler case, it will not be as hard for them to follow the more technical derivation of the second case. We will discuss the two cases separately in Section

Existence of solutions in the simple case

The next lemma shows that
V^{*}(v)
is close to the global minimizer of the function
A(\cdot,v)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under Assumption
2.1
and Assumption
2.3
, let
\vartheta\geq-\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{f}\mkern 1.5mu\underline{\mkern-1.5mu\phantom{f}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu$}f
, then we have

A(w,v)-A(V^{*}(v),v)\geq\left\|w-V^{*}(v)\right\|_{2}^{2}-2(\frac{2\varkappa}{\ell^{2}})^{\frac{1}{p-1}},\quad\text{ for all }w,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(28)

Proof.

For simplicity, we denote
a:=V^{*}(v)
.
Let
\left\{e_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{d}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we will assume
v-V^{*}(v)=te_{d}
for some
t\geq 0
and denote
w=\sum_{i=1}^{d}w_{i}e_{i}
for any
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so we know
\left<v-V^{*}(v),w\right>=(v_{d}-V_{d}^{*}(v))w_{d}=tw_{d}
.
When
w_{d}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle A(a+w,v)-A(a,v)

(29)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varkappa}(f(a+w)-f(a))(f(v)+\vartheta)+\left\|a+w-v\right\|_{2}^{2}-\left\|a-v\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\varkappa}(\ell\operatorname{dist}(a+w,{\mathscr{V}}^{*})^{p})(\ell\left\|v-a\right\|_{2}^{p}+\vartheta+\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{f}\mkern 1.5mu\underline{\mkern-1.5mu\phantom{f}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu$}f)+\left\|w\right\|_{2}^{2}-2|a_{d}-v_{d}|w_{d}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|w\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\ell^{2}}{\varkappa}\left\|v-a\right\|_{2}^{p}w_{d}^{p}-2|a_{d}-v_{d}|w_{d}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|w\right\|_{2}^{2}-2(\frac{2\varkappa}{\ell^{2}})^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

in the above, the second inequality is due to
\operatorname{dist}(a+w,{\mathscr{V}}^{*})\geq w_{d}
which can be derived by the convexity of
{\mathscr{V}}^{*}
, the choice of
e_{d}
and
\vartheta+\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{f}\mkern 1.5mu\underline{\mkern-1.5mu\phantom{f}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu$}f\geq 0
; the last inequality is due to the following argument: when
\left\|a-v\right\|_{2}w_{d}\leq({2\varkappa}/{\ell^{2}})^{1/(p-1)}
, we have

\frac{\ell^{2}}{\varkappa}\left\|v-a\right\|_{2}^{p}w_{d}^{p}-2\left\|a-v\right\|_{2}w_{d}\geq-2\left\|a-v\right\|_{2}w_{d}\geq-2(\frac{2\varkappa}{\ell^{2}})^{\frac{1}{p-1}};

(30)

when
\left\|a-v\right\|_{2}w_{d}>({2\varkappa}/{\ell^{2}})^{1/(p-1)},

we have

\frac{\ell^{2}}{\varkappa}\left\|v-a\right\|_{2}^{p}w_{d}^{p}-2\left\|a-v\right\|_{2}w_{d}=\left\|a-v\right\|_{2}w_{d}\left(\frac{\ell^{2}}{\varkappa}\left\|v-a\right\|_{2}^{p-1}w_{d}^{p-1}-2\right)>0.

(31)

When
w_{d}<0
, since we already have
f(a+w)-f(a)\geq 0
, so we obtain

\displaystyle A(a+w,v)-A(a,v)

\displaystyle\geq\left\|a+w-v\right\|_{2}^{2}-\left\|a-v\right\|_{2}^{2}

(32)

\displaystyle=\left\|w\right\|_{2}^{2}-2|a_{d}-v_{d}|w_{d}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|w\right\|_{2}^{2}.

So all in all, for any
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

A(w,v)-A(a,v)\geq\left\|w-a\right\|_{2}^{2}-2(\frac{2\varkappa}{\ell^{2}})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

(33)

∎

By the above lemma, the global minimizer of
A(\cdot,v)
is in
B_{r}(V^{*}(v))
with
r=\sqrt{2(2\varkappa/\ell^{2})^{1/(p-1)}}
, so to have
r
small, we need to set
\varkappa
small depending on
\ell
.

The next lemma shows the Lipschitz continuity of the map

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Under Assumption
2.2
and assuming that
\int\left\|w\right\|_{2}^{4}d\rho(w)\leq K
, we have

\left\|v_{\alpha}(\rho,v)-v_{\alpha}(\rho,w)\right\|_{2}\leq C_{3}(\alpha,R,K,L,\varkappa)\left\|v-w\right\|_{2},

(34)

for any
v,w\in B_{R}(0)
, where
C_{3}(\alpha,R,K,L,\varkappa)
means a constant that only depends on
\alpha,R,K,L,\varkappa
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{f}\mkern 1.5mu\underline{\mkern-1.5mu\phantom{f}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu$}f=0
(since replacing
f(u)
by
f(u)-\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{f}\mkern 1.5mu\underline{\mkern-1.5mu\phantom{f}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu$}f
in
A(u,v)
won’t change
v_{\alpha}(\rho,v)
) and
A(u,v):=\frac{1}{\varkappa}f(u)(f(v)+\vartheta)+\left\|v-u\right\|_{2}^{2}
to be non-negative. We have

\displaystyle\left\|v_{\alpha}(\rho,v)-v_{\alpha}(\rho,w)\right\|_{2}

(35)

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\int ue^{-\alpha A(u,v)}d\rho(u)}{\int e^{-\alpha A(u,v)}d\rho(u)}-\frac{\int ue^{-\alpha A(u,w)}d\rho(u)}{\int e^{-\alpha A(u,w)}d\rho(u)}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{{\int e^{-\alpha A(u,v)}d\rho(u)}}\left\|{\int ue^{-\alpha A(u,v)}d\rho(u)}-{\int ue^{-\alpha A(u,w)}d\rho(u)}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{{\int e^{-\alpha A(u,v)}d\rho(u)}{\int e^{-\alpha A(u,w)}d\rho(u)}}

\displaystyle\times\left\|{\int ue^{-\alpha A(u,w)}d\rho(u)}\left[{\int e^{-\alpha A(u,v)}d\rho(u)}-{\int e^{-\alpha A(u,w)}d\rho(u)}\right]\right\|_{2}.

Since

\frac{1}{{\int e^{-\alpha A(u,v)}d\rho(u)}}\leq e^{\alpha\int A(u,v)d\rho(u)}\leq C_{1}(\alpha,R,K,L,\varkappa)

(36)

and

\displaystyle|{e^{-\alpha A(u,v)}-e^{-\alpha A(u,w)}}|

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{e^{-\alpha A(u,v)},e^{-\alpha A(u,w)}\right\}\alpha|{A(u,v)-A(u,w)}|

(37)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(\alpha,R,L,\varkappa)(1+\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2})\left\|v-w\right\|_{2},

in (
37
), the first inequality is due to
|e^{x}-e^{y}|\leq\max\{e^{x},e^{y}\}|x-y|,

the second one is due to

\displaystyle|A(u,v)-A(u,w)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varkappa}f(u)\left(f(v)-f(w)\right)+\left<2u-v-w,w-v\right>

(38)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L^{2}}{\varkappa}(1+\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2})(\left\|v\right\|_{2}+\left\|w\right\|_{2}+1)\left\|v-w\right\|_{2}+\left\|2u-v-w\right\|_{2}\left\|w-v\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(\alpha,R,L,\varkappa)(1+\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2})\left\|v-w\right\|_{2}.

So, combining (
35
), (
36
), (
37
), and the assumption that
\int\left\|w\right\|_{2}^{4}d\rho(w)\leq K
, we finally have

\left\|v_{\alpha}(\rho,v)-v_{\alpha}(\rho,w)\right\|_{2}\leq C_{3}(\alpha,R,K,L,\varkappa)\left\|v-w\right\|_{2}.

(39)

∎

To prove the existence of a strong solution of the SDE (
25
), we are going to use an approximation procedure based on suitable projections onto compact sets. To do this, we first define the projection map onto a centered ball of radius
R>0
as

\mathcal{P}_{R}\left(v\right):=\begin{cases}v,&\text{if $\left\|v\right\|_{2}\leq R,$}\\
R\frac{v}{\left\|v\right\|_{2}},&\text{if $\left\|v\right\|_{2}>R.$}\end{cases}

(40)

With this projection map, we can prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

Under Assumption
2.2
,
the following SDE

dV^{R}_{t}=-\lambda(V^{R}_{t}-v_{\alpha}(\rho^{R}_{t},\mathcal{P}_{R}(V^{R}_{t}))dt+\sigma\left\|V^{R}_{t}-v_{\alpha}(\rho^{R}_{t},\mathcal{P}_{R}(V^{R}_{t})\right\|_{2}^{2}dB_{t},\quad R\in(0,\infty),

(41)

where
\rho_{t}^{R}
is the distribution of
V_{t}^{R}
and
\rho_{0}^{R}=\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, has a unique strong solution and
\rho_{t}^{R}
satisfies the following Fokker–Planck equation in the weak sense,

{\partial_{t}}\rho_{t}^{R}=\lambda\operatorname{div}\left(\left(v-v_{\alpha}(\rho_{t}^{R},\mathcal{P}_{R}(v))\right)\rho_{t}^{R}\right)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta\left(\left\|v-v_{\alpha}(\rho_{t}^{R},\mathcal{P}_{R}(v))\right\|_{2}\rho_{t}^{R}\right).

(42)

Proof.

The proof is an adaptation from the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
and it is based on the application of Leray–Schauder fixed point theorem; we only provide a sketch here. We first define the mapping

\mathcal{T}_{R}:\mathcal{C}([0,T^{*}]\times\overline{B_{R}(0)},\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{C}([0,T^{*}]\times\overline{B_{R}(0)},\mathbb{R}^{d});\quad u\mapsto v_{\alpha}(\rho_{\cdot}^{R,u},\cdot),~v\in B_{R}(0),

(43)

where
\rho_{t}^{R,u}
is the distribution of
V_{t}^{R,u}
, which satisfies the following SDE

dV^{R,u}_{t}=-\lambda(V^{R}_{t}-u(t,\mathcal{P}_{R}(V_{t}^{R,u})))dt+\sigma\left\|V^{R}_{t}-u(t,\mathcal{P}_{R}(V_{t}^{R,u}))\right\|_{2}dB_{t}.

(44)

Below we intend to show that
\mathcal{T}_{R}
has a fixed point, hence the existence of solutions for (
41
) and (
42
).
For that, we first show that
\mathcal{T}_{R}
is a compact mapping.
By standard arguments (see
[
1
, Chapter 7]
), for any
p\geq 1
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|V_{t}^{R,u}\right\|_{2}^{p}\right)\leq C_{4}(1+\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|V_{0}^{R,u}\right\|_{2}^{p}\right)),

(45)

for some constant
C_{4}>0
depending on

. By Itô isometry, for any

, we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|V_{t}^{R,u}-V_{s}^{R,u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)\leq C_{5}\left\|t-s\right\|_{2},

(46)

here

depends on
\norm{u}_{\infty},\lambda,\sigma,T^{*}
, then apply
[
10
, Lemma 3.2]
, we have

\left\|v_{\alpha}(\rho_{s}^{R,u},\mathcal{P}_{R}(v))-v_{\alpha}(\rho_{t}^{R,u},\mathcal{P}_{R}(v))\right\|_{2}\leq C_{6}W_{2}(\rho_{t}^{R,u},\rho_{s}^{R,u})\leq C_{6}C_{5}\left\|t-s\right\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}},

(47)

for some constant

that depends on

and

with exponent

. Due to Lemma
3.2
,

is Lipschitz continuous (and thus is also Hölder continuous with exponent

), and so the compactness of

can be provided by the compact embedding
\mathcal{C}^{0,1/2}\left([0,T^{*}]\times\overline{B_{R}(0)},\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\hookrightarrow

\mathcal{C}\left([0,T^{*}]\times\overline{B_{R}(0)},\mathbb{R}^{d}\right).

Then, we show that the set

, for some uniform constant

; then by the Leray–Schauder fixed point theorem, we can conclude the existence part of this proof. Towards this, when

In the above, the first condition is due to

, the second condition is due to Assumption

.

Lemma 3.4
.

Under Assumption
2.2
and Assumption

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\left\|v-V^{*}(v)\right\|_{2}^{2}d\rho^{R}_{t}(v)

\displaystyle\leq-(\lambda-2\sigma^{2}d)\int\left\|v-V^{*}(v)\right\|_{2}^{2}d\rho^{R}_{t}(v)

(49)

\displaystyle+(\lambda+2\sigma^{2}d)\int\left\|V^{*}(v)-v_{\alpha}(\rho_{t}^{R},\mathcal{P}_{R}(v))\right\|_{2}^{2}d\rho^{R}_{t}(v).

Proof.

For ease of notation, we will omit
R
and
\mathcal{P}_{R}
in the proof, also in the proof
v,V^{*}(v)
are row vectors.
Apply Itô formula to the function
\left\|v-V^{*}(v)\right\|_{2}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\left\|v-V^{*}(v)\right\|_{2}^{2}d\rho_{t}(v)

\displaystyle={-\lambda\int\left<\nabla\left\|v-V^{*}(v)\right\|_{2}^{2},v-v_{\alpha}(\rho_{t},v)\right>d\rho_{t}(v)}

(50)

\displaystyle+{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\int\Delta\left\|v-V^{*}(v)\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|v-v_{\alpha}(\rho_{t},v)\right\|_{2}^{2}d\rho_{t}(v)}

\displaystyle=-2\lambda\int(v-v_{\alpha}(\rho_{t},v))(I-\nabla V^{*}(v))(v-V^{*}(v))^{\top}d\rho_{t}(v)

\displaystyle+{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\int\Delta\left\|v-V^{*}(v)\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|v-v_{\alpha}(\rho_{t},v)\right\|_{2}^{2}d\rho_{t}(v)}

\displaystyle=-2\lambda\underbrace{\int(v-V^{*}(v))(I-\nabla V^{*}(v))(v-V^{*}(v))^{\top}d\rho_{t}(v)}_{I}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\underbrace{\int\Delta\left\|v-V^{*}(v)\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|v-v_{\alpha}(\rho_{t},v)\right\|_{2}^{2}d\rho_{t}(v)}_{II}

\displaystyle-\int(v-V^{*}(v))(I-\nabla V^{*}(v))(v-V^{*}(v))^{\top}d\rho_{t}(v)

\displaystyle=-\int\left\|v-V^{*}(v)\right\|_{2}^{2}d\rho_{t}(v),

(53)

as a basis of

Lemma 4.2

\displaystyle{\partial_{t}}\rho_{t}^{\kappa,R,i}

\displaystyle=\lambda\operatorname{div}\left(\chi_{i,t}(v)\rho_{t}^{\kappa,R,i}\right)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta\left(({\left\|\chi_{i,t}(v)\right\|_{2}+\kappa})^{2}\rho_{t}^{\kappa,R,i}\right),

(129)

(130)

\varphi_{\epsilon}

\Phi_{i}(v)=v,

\lim_{i\to\infty}\chi_{i,t}(v)=v-v_{\alpha}(\rho_{t}^{\kappa,R},\mathcal{P}_{R}(v))

\chi_{i,t}

Next, we will show that

\left\|v-V^{*}(v)\right\|_{2}^{2}-\left\|w-V^{*}(w)\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{18}(1+\left\|w\right\|_{2}+\left\|v\right\|_{2})\left\|v-w\right\|_{2},

II\leq\frac{(q+g_{2,r})^{\nu}}{\eta}+\frac{e^{-\alpha(q-\beta)}}{\rho(B_{r}(v^{*}_{2}))}\int_{\mathcal{Z}_{2}}\left\|w-v_{2}^{*}\right\|_{2}d\rho(w),

(210)

for any
r>0,q>\beta
, where
g_{2,r}:=\sup_{w\in B_{r}(v^{*}_{2})}g(w)-g(v^{*}_{2})
.

Combine term
I
and
II
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|v_{\alpha}(\rho)-v^{*}_{2}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|v^{*}_{1}-v_{2}^{*}\right\|_{2}+\frac{(q+g_{1,r})^{\nu}}{\eta}+\frac{(q+g_{2,r})^{\nu}}{\eta}

(211)

\displaystyle+\frac{e^{-\alpha(q-\beta)}}{\min\{\rho(B_{r}(v_{1}^{*})),\rho(B_{r}(v^{*}_{2}))\}}\int\left\|w-v_{2}^{*}\right\|_{2}d\rho(w)

\displaystyle+\left\|v^{*}_{1}-v_{2}^{*}\right\|_{2}\frac{e^{-\alpha(q-\beta)}}{\min\{\rho(B_{r}(v_{1}^{*})),\rho(B_{r}(v^{*}_{2}))\}},

finally, using

\displaystyle\min\{\rho(B_{r}(v^{*}_{1}),\rho(B_{r}(v^{*}_{2})\}

\displaystyle\geq{\inf_{w\in\{v^{*}_{1},v^{*}_{2}\}}\int_{B_{r}(w)}\phi_{r}^{\tau}(u-w)d\rho(u)},

(212)

we proved the lemma.
∎

On the continuity of the law on sets.

The next lemma shows that
\rho_{t}^{\kappa}(B_{R}(0)\cap(\partial\mathcal{Z}_{1}+B_{r}(0)))
is continuous in terms of
t
, then by a further analysis, we can find
r
small enough such that
\rho_{t}^{\kappa}(B_{R}(0)\cap(\partial\mathcal{Z}_{1}+B_{r}(0)))\leq\epsilon^{\prime}
, for any
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
T^{*},\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, we can choose
r
small enough, depending on
\operatorname{diam}({\mathscr{V}}^{*}),\lambda,\sigma,d,

R,T^{*},\epsilon^{\prime},\rho_{0}
,
\varkappa
small enough depending on
\lambda,\sigma,d,\ell,

\alpha
large enough depending on
\lambda,\sigma,d,T^{*},L,\kappa,\varkappa,

\operatorname{diam}({\mathscr{V}}^{*}),\epsilon^{\prime}
such that
\rho_{t}^{\kappa}(B_{R}(0)\cap(\partial\mathcal{Z}_{1}+B_{r}(0)))\leq\epsilon^{\prime}
, for any
t\in[0,T^{*}]
.

Proof.

We can find a smooth function
\phi_{r}(v)
(see Remark
5.4
) which will vanish outside of
(\partial\mathcal{Z}_{1}+B_{2r}(0))\cap B_{2R}(0)
and has range
[C_{\mathrm{s}},C_{\mathrm{h}}]
(
C_{\mathrm{h}}\geq C_{\mathrm{s}}>0
) inside of
(\partial\mathcal{Z}_{1}+B_{r}(0))\cap B_{R}(0)
independent of
r>0
, then we have
\rho^{\kappa}_{t}((\partial\mathcal{Z}_{1}+B_{r}(0))\cap B_{R}(0))\leq C_{21}\int\phi_{r}(v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{t}(v)
for some constant
C_{21}>0
independent of
r>0
. So to prove the lemma, it is enough to show that for any
\epsilon
, we have
\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*}]}\int\phi_{r}(v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{t}(v)\leq\epsilon
for
r
small enough.

Firstly, we have that
(\left\|v-v_{\alpha}(\rho^{\kappa}_{t},v)\right\|_{2}+\kappa)^{\frac{d}{d+1}}\rho^{\kappa}_{t}(v)
is
absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, see
[
4
, Theorem 6.3.1]
. Moreover, since

(\left\|v-v_{\alpha}(\rho^{\kappa}_{t},v)\right\|_{2}+\kappa)^{\frac{d}{d+1}}\geq\kappa^{\frac{d}{d+1}}>0
, we have that

\rho^{\kappa}_{t}
is absolutely continuous for any
t\in[0,T^{*}]
, and so by monotone convergence theorem and the properties that
\phi_{r}(v)
is monotone increasing in terms of
r
,
\phi_{0}(v)=0
(see Remark
5.4
), we have for any
t\in[0,T^{*}]
that
\int\phi_{r}(v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{t}(v)\to 0
as
r\to 0
.

Secondly, define
r_{\epsilon}^{*}(t):=\sup\left\{r>0:\int\phi_{r}(v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{t}(v)\leq\epsilon\right\}
, then we have
r_{\epsilon}^{*}(t)>0
for any
t\in[0,T^{*}]
. In the following, we want to show
r_{\epsilon}^{*}(t)\geq r_{\epsilon}>0
for all
t\in[0,T^{*}]
, for some constant
r_{\epsilon}>0
. If not, without loss of generality, there will be a increasing sequence
\left\{t_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, such that
\lim_{i\to\infty}t_{i}=t_{0}\in[0,T^{*}]
and
\lim_{i\to\infty}r^{*}_{\epsilon}(t_{i})=0
. For simplicity, we will denote
r_{0}:=r^{*}_{\epsilon}(t_{0})
and we know
r_{0}>0
.

We have for any
r,t
that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\phi_{r}(v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{t}(v)

\displaystyle=\int-\lambda\left<v-v_{\alpha}(\rho^{\kappa}_{t},v),\nabla\phi_{r}(v)\right>+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\left\|v-v_{\alpha}(\rho^{\kappa}_{t},v)\right\|_{2}^{2}\Delta\phi_{r}(v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{t}(v)

(213)

\displaystyle\in[-C_{22},C_{22}],

for some
C_{22}\in(0,\infty)
depending on
r,R,d,\lambda,\sigma
, since
\phi_{r}(v)
vanishes outside
B_{2r}(0)
, and inside
B_{2R}(0)
we have
\nabla\phi_{r}(v)
and
\Delta\phi_{r}(v)
bounded. Based on the above equation, we have

\int\phi_{r_{0}}(v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{t_{i}}(v)\leq\int\phi_{r_{0}}(v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{t_{0}}(v)+C_{22}\left(t_{0}-t_{i}\right)=\epsilon+C_{22}\left(t_{0}-t_{i}\right).

(214)

On the other hand, we have for any
\theta>0
that

\displaystyle\int\phi_{r_{0}-\theta}(v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{t_{i}}(v)=\int\phi_{r_{0}}(v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{t_{i}}(v)+\underbrace{\int\left[\phi_{r_{0}-\theta}(v)-\phi_{r_{0}}(v)\right]d\rho^{\kappa}_{t_{i}}(v)}_{(I)},

(215)

while we know that the term
(I)
is strictly negative, because
\phi_{r}
is monotonically increasing with respect to
r
.
By Lemma
3.12
we have

\rho^{\kappa}_{t}(B_{\dot{r}}(v))\geq e^{-q^{\prime}T^{*}}\int\phi_{\dot{r}}^{\tau}(z-v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{0}(z)>0,

(216)

for any
t\in[0,T^{*}]
and
\dot{r}>0,v\in(\partial\mathcal{Z}_{1}\pm(2r-\frac{\theta}{2}))\cap B_{R}(0)
, here we use
\partial\mathcal{Z}_{1}\pm(2r-\frac{\theta}{2})
to denote the set
\cup_{v\in\partial\mathcal{Z}_{1}}v\pm(2r-\frac{\theta}{2}))N(v)
where
N
is the Gauss map. The constant
q^{\prime}
is from Lemma
3.12
.

Then due to
\phi_{r_{0}-\theta}(v)-\phi_{r_{0}}(v)\in[-C_{23},-C_{24}],\quad\forall v\in(\partial\mathcal{Z}_{1}\pm(2r-\frac{\theta}{2}))\cap B_{R/2}(0)
, where
C_{23},C_{24}>0
only depend on
r_{0},\theta
, we have
(I)\leq-C_{25}e^{-q^{\prime}T^{*}}\int\phi_{\frac{\theta}{4}}^{\tau}(z-v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{0}(z)<0
for any
v^{\prime}\in(\partial\mathcal{Z}_{1}\pm(2r-\frac{\theta}{2}))\cap B_{R/2}(0)
, where
C_{25}
depends on
r_{0},\theta
. And so we have

\displaystyle\int\phi_{r_{0}-\theta}(v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{t_{i}}(v)

\displaystyle\leq\int\phi_{r_{0}}(v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{t_{i}}(v)-C_{25}e^{-q^{\prime}T^{*}}\int\phi_{\frac{\theta}{4}}^{\tau}(z-v^{\prime})d\rho^{\kappa}_{0}(z)

(217)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon+C_{22}\left(t_{0}-t_{i}\right)-C_{25}e^{-q^{\prime}T^{*}}\int\phi_{\frac{\theta}{4}}^{\tau}(z-v^{\prime})d\rho^{\kappa}_{0}(z),

for any
v^{\prime}\in(\partial\mathcal{Z}_{1}\pm(2r-\frac{\theta}{2}))\cap B_{R/2}(0)
.

Thirdly, based on the above analysis, we can choose
\theta=\frac{1}{2}r_{0}
, and
i_{0}
large enough so that for any
i\geq i_{0}
, we have
C_{22}\left(t_{0}-t_{i}\right)-C_{25}e^{-q^{\prime}T^{*}}\int\phi_{\frac{\theta}{4}}^{\tau}(z-v^{\prime})d\rho^{\kappa}_{0}(z)<0
and
r^{*}_{\epsilon}(t_{i})\leq\frac{1}{3}r_{0}
. However in this case, we have

\int\phi_{\frac{r_{0}}{2}}(v)d\rho^{\kappa}_{t_{i}}(v)<\epsilon,

(218)

for any
i\geq i_{0}
, and this means
r^{*}_{\epsilon}(t_{i})>\frac{1}{2}r_{0}
when
i\geq i_{0}
, which is a contradiction. So we have proved that
r_{\epsilon}>0
for any
t\in[0,T^{*}]
.

Lastly, we want to show
r_{\epsilon}
will not vanish when
\alpha\to\infty
. Since
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}v_{\alpha}(\rho,v)=V^{*}(v)
, for almost every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\rho
with
\operatorname{supp}(\rho)=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the Fokker–Planck equation will converge to

{\partial_{t}}\rho_{t}=\lambda\operatorname{div}\left((v-V^{*}(v))\rho_{t}\right)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta\left((\left\|v-V^{*}(v)\right\|_{2}+\kappa)^{2}\rho_{t}\right),

(219)

and
\rho_{t}
is absolutely continuous (also due to
[
4
, Theorem 6.3.1]
); hence, in view of the arguments above for this
\rho_{t}
, we have
r_{\epsilon}>0
when
\alpha=\infty
, and therefore
r_{\epsilon}
cannot vanish as
\alpha\to\infty
.

∎

On the convergence of the fully discrete finite particle iterations (
5
).

We conclude this Appendix here with the estimates that explain the rationale of the convergence of the fully discrete finite particle algorithm (
5
). In the following
\overline{V}_{t}^{i}
represent i.i.d. realizations of a solution of (
15
) for
i=1,\dots,N
, and
V_{t}^{i}
satisfies

d{V}^{i}_{t}=-\lambda\left(V^{i}_{t}-v_{\alpha}(\hat{\rho}_{t}^{N},{V}^{i}_{t})\right)dt+\sigma\left(\|V^{i}_{t}-v_{\alpha}(\widehat{\rho}^{N}_{t},{V}^{i}_{t})\|_{2}+\kappa\right)dB_{t}^{i},\quad i=1,\dots,N.

(220)

Denote
V_{t}:=((V_{t}^{1})^{\top},\ldots,(V_{t}^{N})^{\top})^{\top}
, then the above emprical SDE can be written in a compact way

dV_{t}=G(V_{t})dt+H(V_{t})dB_{t},

(221)

where
G=((G^{1})^{\top},\ldots,(G^{N})^{\top})^{\top},H=\operatorname{Diag}(H^{1},\ldots,H^{N})
and
G(V_{t})^{i}=-\lambda(V_{t}^{i}-v_{\alpha}(\hat{\rho}^{N}_{t},V_{t}^{i}),

H^{i}(V_{t}):=\sigma\left(\|V^{i}_{t}-v_{\alpha}(\widehat{\rho}^{N}_{t},{V}^{i}_{t})\|_{2}+\kappa\right)I_{d}
. Then we can show
H,G
are locally Lipschitz and
H,G
grow at most linearly,
since
v_{\alpha}(\hat{\rho}^{N}_{t},V_{t}^{i})
is a convex combination between
\{V_{t}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}

and so
\left\|v_{\alpha}(\hat{\rho}^{N}_{t},V_{t}^{i})\right\|_{2}\leq\max_{i\in[N]}\{\left\|V_{t}^{i}\right\|_{2}\}\leq\left\|V_{t}\right\|_{2}
. Hence, we have that the above empirical SDE has a unique strong solution.

Once again we consider the set of bounded processes

\Omega_{M}=\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\max\left\{\left\|{V_{t}^{i}}\right\|_{2}^{4},\left\|{\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{V}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\phantom{V}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu$}V_{t}^{i}}\right\|_{2}^{4}\right\}\leq M\right\},

(222)

for any
M>0
, as we did in (
8
).
In analogy to the chain of inequalities (
9
), we should study instead
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|F(V_{K}^{i})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)
, where
F(v)=v-V^{*}(v)
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|F(V_{K}^{i})\right\|_{2}^{2}\Bigg|\Omega_{M}\right)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|F(\overline{}{V}_{T^{*}}^{i})\right\|_{2}^{2}\Bigg|\Omega_{M}\right)}_{I}

(223)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|F({V}_{T^{*}}^{i})\right\|_{2}^{2}\Bigg|\Omega_{M}\right)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|F(\overline{V}_{T^{*}}^{i})\right\|_{2}^{2}\Bigg|\Omega_{M}\right)}_{II}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|F({V}_{K}^{i})\right\|_{2}^{2}\Bigg|\Omega_{M}\right)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|F({V}_{T^{*}}^{i})\right\|_{2}^{2}\Bigg|\Omega_{M}\right)}_{III},

where
K\Delta t=T^{*}
.
We have
I\leq\epsilon
by the main result of this paper, Theorem
1.1
. From the estimate (
144
) and the analysis of (
145
), we know that, for any transport coupling
\pi
between
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
, we have

\Big|\int\left\|F(v)\right\|_{2}^{2}d\rho_{1}(v)-\int\left\|F(w)\right\|_{2}^{2}d\rho_{2}(w)\Big|\leq C\sqrt{\iint\left\|v-w\right\|_{2}^{2}d\pi(v,w)},

(224)

so for term
II
, we obtain

II\leq C\mathbb{E}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\left\|\overline{V}_{T^{*}}^{i}-V_{T^{*}}^{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}}}\Bigg|\Omega_{M}\right)\leq C\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left({\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\left\|\overline{V}_{T^{*}}^{i}-V_{T^{*}}^{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}}}\Bigg|\Omega_{M}\right)}\leq C_{\rm MFA}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}},

(225)

where the last inequality is due to a similar analysis as in
[
23
, Proposition 3.11]
and
C_{\rm MFA}>0
may depend exponentially on
M>0
.

Similarly, and using standard results from
[
41
]
, for term
III
, we have

\displaystyle III\leq C\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\left\|{V}_{{K}}^{i}-V_{T^{*}}^{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}}\Bigg|\Omega_{M}\right)}\leq C_{\rm NA}{(\Delta t)^{s}},

(226)

for some index
s>0
. Then by using the same arguments as in (
10
), we conclude

\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|V_{K}^{i}-V^{*}(V_{K}^{i})\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\Bigg)\geq 1-\left[\varepsilon^{-1}(C_{\mathrm{NA}}\Delta t+C_{\mathrm{MFA}}N^{-1}+\epsilon)-\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{M}^{c})\right].

(227)

Unfortunately, a uniform bound with respect to
N
of the moments of the solution of (
220
) remains elusive, because for
v_{\alpha}(\rho,v)
in estimates like (
48
) the constant
b_{2}
depends on
\|v\|_{2}^{2}
(compare also
[
10
, Lemma 3.3]
). This gap does not allow us yet to provide an explicit estimate of

.

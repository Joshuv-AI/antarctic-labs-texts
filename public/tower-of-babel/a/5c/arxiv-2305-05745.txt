Abstract
Given two jointly distributed random variables ( X , Y ) (X,Y) , a functional representation of X X is a random variable Z Z independent of Y Y , and a deterministic function g ⁡ ( ⋅ , ⋅ ) g(\cdot,\cdot) such that X = g ⁡ ( Y , Z ) X=g(Y,Z) . The problem of finding a minimum entropy functional representation is known to be equivalent to the problem of finding a minimum entropy coupling where, given a collection of probability distributions P 1 , … , P m P_{1},\dots,P_{m} , the goal is to find a coupling X 1 , … , X m X_{1},\dots,X_{m} ( OPEN X i ∼ P i ) X_{i}\sim P_{i}) with the smallest entropy H α ​ ( X 1 , … , X m ) H_{\alpha}(X_{1},\dots,X_{m}) . This paper presents a new information spectrum converse, and applies it to obtain direct lower bounds on minimum entropy in both problems. The new results improve on all known lower bounds, including previous lower bounds based on the concept of majorization. In particular, the presented proofs leverage both - the information spectrum and the majorization - perspectives on minimum entropy couplings and functional representations.

I
Introduction

I-A

Functional Representation Lemma

The Functional Representation Lemma (FRL)
[
1
]
states that it is possible to decompose a random variable
X
into an arbitrarily correlated random variable
Y
and a residual random variable
Z
, independent of
Y
. More precisely, given any pair of random variables
(X,Y)\sim P_{XY}
, there exists a random variable
Z
, independent of
Y
such that
X
is a deterministic function of
(Y,Z)
. FRL has been widely used in proving various results in multi-user information theory
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
, privacy and secrecy
[
5
]
, and entropic causal inference
[
6
,
7
]
.

Several properties of functional representations have been studied in the literature. One such property of functional representations is the mutual information
I(X;Z)
. Bounds on
I(X;Z)
have been studied in
[
4
]
in the form of a strong functional representation lemma. This problem has also been studied in
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
with applications to one-shot channel simulation with unlimited common randomness. It has been applied to prove the result on constrained min-max remote prediction
[
10
]
and to prove the asymptotic achievability in Gelfand-Pinsker’s Theorem
[
11
]
. A closely related line of work is the setting of privacy funnel function
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
. Here, the goal is to maximize
I(X;Z)
while enforcing the Markov chain
Y\leftrightarrow X\leftrightarrow Z
. The motivation is to have perfect privacy for the information
Y
while realizing maximum information about
X
, see
[
5
,
15
,
16
]
for connections with FRL.

Another important property, which is the focus of this paper, is the entropy of the residual random variable
Z
. The minimization of
H(Z)
has been applied in identifying the direction of causality
[
6
,
7
]
, and the compression length in a variable and fixed length secure compression
[
5
,
17
]
. It could also be applied to studying a noisy communication channel
[
18
]
, if the goal is to minimize the entropy of the noise source. Lower entropy of
Z
also means that less auxiliary randomness is needed to construct it from
(X,Y)
. This is of prime importance as randomness does not come for free and various expensive methods have been designed to generate it in practical systems
[
19
,
20
]
.

I-B

Minimum Entropy Coupling

The minimum entropy coupling problem aims to find the joint distribution
(P_{X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{m}})
with minimum joint Rényi entropy
H_{\alpha}(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{m})
, given the
m
marginal distributions
X_{1}\sim P_{1},X_{2}\sim P_{2},\dots,X_{m}\sim P_{m}
(see Definition
2
). This problem has been widely studied in
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
and is closely related to the functional representation lemma. It has been shown in
[
7
,
24
]
that finding the minimum entropy of
Z
is the same as solving the minimum entropy coupling problem for the marginal distributions
\{P_{X|Y=y}\}_{y\in{\cal Y}}
(see Lemma
1
and Appendix for details). Thus, the lower bounds on
H(Z)
provided in this paper are also lower bounds on the joint entropy in the minimum entropy coupling problem.

Additional scenarios where the problem of minimum entropy coupling arises include the entropic causal inference
[
7
,
6
]
, dimension reduction
[
25
]
, contingency tables and transportation polytopes in
[
26
,
27
]
and randomness generation
[
19
,
20
]
. The computation of the minimum entropy coupling was shown to be NP-hard in
[
22
]
and
[
25
]
. In
[
21
]
, it is shown that any coupling has the joint entropy at least
H(\land_{i=1}^{m}P_{i})
, where
\land
denotes the greatest lower bound with respect to the majorization of probability distributions. A polynomial-time approximation algorithm for the construction of coupling within
\left\lceil{\log m}\right\rceil
bits from
H(\land_{i=1}^{m}P_{i})
is also provided in
[
21
]
. Li
[
24
]
improved upon the result of
[
21
]
by providing construction of a coupling with joint entropy being within
2-2^{2-m}
bits from the
H(\land_{i=1}^{m}P_{i})
. Recently, Compton
[
28
]
showed that the greedy coupling algorithm provided in
[
6
]
is always within
\log_{2}(e)
bits from
H(\land_{i=1}^{m}P_{i})
which further improves upon the result in
[
24
]
for
m>2
.

In this paper, we focus on lower bounds on the Rényi entropy
H_{\alpha}(Z)
(or equivalently
H_{\alpha}(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{m})
) for every
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
, and prove a new converse in terms of the information spectrum of
Z
. This converse improves upon the information spectrum converse in
[
5
]
and the majorization converse in
[
21
]
for Rényi entropy of any order
\alpha
. Similar results have also been recently studied in a parallel work by Compton et. al in
[
29
]
(see Remark
1
for details).

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
II
, we state our notation, formulate the problem, and review the known lower bounds. We present our main results in Section
III
. In Section
IV
, we compare all the lower bounds and make concluding remarks. Finally, we postpone some of the proofs to Section
V
and the Appendix.

II
Preliminaries

II-A

Notations

We denote the probability mass function (PMF) of a random variable using a capital letter, say
P
, while the probability of an event is denoted using the bold-face letter
\mathbb{P}
and the expectation of a random variable is denoted with
\mathbb{E}
. Given a random variable
X
, its support (and sets in general) is denoted by
{\cal X}
, while a realization is denoted by lower case letter, for example,
x\in{\cal X}
. The information of a random variable
X
is

\displaystyle\imath_{X}(x):=\log\frac{1}{P_{X}(x)}\quad\forall x\in{\cal X},

(1)

where all logarithms have base two unless specified otherwise. The bold-face
\mathbb{F}_{X}
denotes the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of
X
. With some abuse of notation, we use
\mathbb{F}_{\imath_{X}}
to denote the CDF of the information
\imath_{X}(X)
of
X
;
\mathbb{F}_{\imath_{X}}
is also known as
the information spectrum
of
X
. Recall that the Shannon entropy of a random variable
X
can be written as

\displaystyle H(X)=\mathbb{E}\left[\imath_{X}(X)\right].

(2)

Similarly, the Rényi entropy of
X
can be written as

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left(\mathbb{E}\left[2^{(1-\alpha)\imath_{X}(X)}\right]\right)

(3)

for
\alpha\in[0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
and
H_{\alpha}(X):=H(X)
for
\alpha=1
. Given a random variable
X
with PMF
P_{X}
we also use the notation
H(P_{X})
and
H_{\alpha}(P_{X})
to denote
H(X)
and
H_{\alpha}(X)
.

Given two PMFs
P=(p_{1},p_{2},\cdots)
and
Q=(q_{1},q_{2},\cdots)
with countably infinite supports, let the probability masses for both be given in non-increasing order, that is
p_{1}\geq p_{2}\dots
and
q_{1}\geq q_{2}\dots
. Then, we say that the distribution
P
majorizes
Q
, or
Q\preceq_{m}P
, if

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}q_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}

(4)

for all
k\in\{1,2,\dots\}
.
In the case that
P
and
Q
have finite supports with different cardinalities, we apply the same definition by padding both the PMFs with zeros.

II-B

Problem Formulation

Consider two jointly distributed random variables
(X,Y)\sim P_{XY}
taking values in a countable set
{\cal X}
and a finite set
{\cal Y}
, respectively. Recall that the Functional Representation Lemma
[
1
,
5
,
4
]
says that there exists a random variable
Z
taking values in some set
{\cal Z}
, such that

1.

X
is a deterministic function of
Y
and
Z
i.e.,

\displaystyle H(X|Y,Z)=0

(5)

2.

Y
and
Z
are independent of each other i.e.,

\displaystyle I(Y;Z)=0.

(6)

In other words,
X
can be represented as a deterministic function of two independent random variables
Y
and
Z
.

Definition 1

(Minimum Entropy Functional Representation)
.

The minimum Rényi entropy of functional representation of
(X,Y)\sim P_{XY}
is

\displaystyle{H}^{\star}_{\alpha}(P_{XY}):=\inf_{P_{Z|XY}\colon\begin{subarray}{c}H(X|Y,Z)=0,\\
I(Y;Z)=0\end{subarray}}H_{\alpha}(Z)

(7)

for
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
.

The coupling of
m
PMFs
P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{m}
refers to a joint distribution of
P_{X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{m}}
subject to the constraints
X_{i}\sim P_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
.

Definition 2

(Minimum Entropy Coupling)
.

The minimum Rényi entropy of a coupling of a set of PMFs is

\displaystyle H^{\ast}_{\alpha}(P_{1},\dots

\displaystyle,P_{m})\!:=\!\inf_{P_{X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{m}}\colon{X_{i}}\sim P_{i}}H_{\alpha}(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})

(8)

for
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
(X,Y)\sim P_{XY}
be jointly distributed random variables with
{\cal Y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\}
. Then

\displaystyle{H}^{\star}_{\alpha}(P_{XY})=H^{\ast}_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y=y_{1}},\dots,P_{X|Y=y_{m}}).

(9)

That is, the problem of finding the functional representation with the minimum entropy is a minimum entropy coupling problem.

Proof.

The lemma is proved in
[
6
,
24
]
. For the sake of completeness, the proof is presented in the Appendix.
∎

We state all results in terms of the functional representation conditions for the remainder of the paper. However, in light of Lemma
1
, we see that the same results also apply to the minimum entropy coupling problem.

II-C

Prior Work

It is shown in
[
21
]
that

\displaystyle{H}^{\star}_{\alpha}(P_{XY})\geq H_{\alpha}(\land_{y\in{\cal Y}}P_{X|Y=y})

(10)

where
\land
represents the greatest lower bound with respect to majorization,
\preceq_{m}
, of the set
\{P_{X|Y=y}\}_{y\in{\cal Y}}
. For details, refer to
[
30
,
21
]
. Likewise,
[
5
,
21
]
independently presented the following lower bound for
\alpha=1
,

\displaystyle H^{\star}_{\alpha}(P_{XY})\geq\sup_{y\in{\cal Y}}H_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y=y}).

(11)

Although the general case is not addressed in
[
5
,
21
]
, this lower bound could readily be extended to Rényi Entropy of order
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
, see Section
III
and Appendix for details.

The following information spectrum converse is essentially shown in
[
5
]
. Let
Z
be any random variable satisfying (
5
) and (
6
). Then, for any real valued
t\in[0,\infty)
and
\tau>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[{\imath_{Z}(Z)\!>t}]\geq

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in{\cal Y}}\mathbb{P}[\imath_{X|Y}(X|Y)\!>t\!+\!\tau|Y\!=\!y]\!-\!\exp(-\tau).

(12)

Our main contribution in this work is to strengthen (
12
) by removing the dependence on
\tau
, as well as to leverage this new information spectrum converse in order to get direct lower bounds on
{H}^{\star}_{\alpha}(P_{XY})
.

III
Main Results

III-A

Information Spectrum Converse

Theorem 1
.

Let
(X,Y)
be jointly distributed random variables supported on countable
\cal X
and countable
\cal Y
. Consider any random variable
Z
that satisfies (
5
) and (
6
). Then, for any real-valued
t\in[0,\infty)
we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\imath_{Z}(Z)>t]\geq\sup_{y\in{\cal Y}}\mathbb{P}[\imath_{X|Y}(X|Y)>t|Y=y].

(13)

The proof of Theorem
1
is given in Section
V
. In this paper, we focus on countable
\cal X
and finite
\cal Y
, though the results of Theorem
1
could be extended to a more general setting.

In order to connect Theorem
1
to the majorization lower bound (
10
), we propose a similar definition with the ordering based on the information spectrum.

Definition 3

(
\preceq_{\imath}
)
.

Consider two PMFs
P
and
Q
with countably infinite supports. We write
Q\preceq_{\imath}P
, if the information spectrum of
Q
lower bounds the information spectrum of
P
. In other words, let
U\sim Q
and
V\sim P
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\imath_{U}(U)\leq t\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\imath_{V}(V)\leq t\right]

(14)

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\imath_{U}(U)>t\right]\geq\mathbb{P}\left[\imath_{V}(V)>t\right]

(15)

for all
t\in[0,\infty)
.

The following lemmas connect the above definition to the definition of majorization.

Lemma 2
.

Given two PMFs
Q
and
P
we have that

\displaystyle Q\preceq_{\imath}P\Rightarrow Q\preceq_{m}P

(16)

where
\preceq_{m}
denotes majorization.

The proof of Lemma
2
is based on standard induction arguments, see Appendix for details. (See also
[
31
]
, where this result was independently proven.)

Note that in general, the reverse is not true: it is possible to have
Q\preceq_{m}P
but not
Q\preceq_{\imath}P
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\{P_{1},\dots,P_{m}\}
be a finite collection of PMFs supported on finite
\{{\cal S}_{1},\dots,{\cal S}_{m}\}
and let

\displaystyle{\cal F}=\{Q\colon Q\preceq_{\imath}P_{i}\quad\forall i\in\{1,\dots,m\}\}.

(17)

There exists
Q^{\ast}\in{\cal F}
such that
Q\preceq_{m}Q^{\ast}
for all
Q\in{\cal F}
. In addition,
Q^{\ast}
could be computed using a greedy algorithm with complexity linear in
\sum_{i=1}^{m}|{\cal S}_{i}|
. This result could also be extended for a countable set
\{{\cal S}_{1},{\cal S}_{2},\dots\}
. We leave the complete details for future work.

The idea behind the greedy construction of
Q^{\ast}
is as follows. We construct
Q^{\ast}=(q^{\ast}_{1},q^{\ast}_{2},\dots)
with
q^{\ast}_{1}\geq q^{\ast}_{2}\dots
in a greedy way. First, assign

\displaystyle q^{\ast}_{1}=\min_{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}}\left\{\max_{s\in{\cal S}_{i}}P_{i}(s)\right\}.

(18)

This is the maximum value we can assign to
q^{\ast}_{1}
while satisfying the information spectrum constraint defined by
\cal F
. We continue with this strategy: at each step
k
we assign the maximum possible value to
q^{\ast}_{k}
until the total probability of all
q^{\ast}_{k}
reaches one. The fact that this distribution majorizes all other
Q\in{\cal F}
is proven using a standard induction argument. Likewise, the number of steps the algorithm needs to take is linear in
\sum_{i=1}^{m}|{\cal S}_{i}|
, since the constraints defined by
\cal F
is a step function with at most steps
\sum_{i=1}^{m}|{\cal S}_{i}|
, see the Appendix.

III-B

Lower bounds on Shannon and Rényi Entropy

While Theorem
1
does not provide direct lower bounds on
H^{\star}_{\alpha}(P_{XY})
, the next two corollaries could be used to compute such lower bounds.

Corollary 1
.

Let
(X,Y)\sim P_{XY}
be jointly distributed random variables supported on countable
\cal X
and countable
\cal Y
. Then

\displaystyle H^{\star}_{\alpha}(P_{XY})\geq K_{\alpha}(P_{XY})

(19)

for any
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
, where

\displaystyle K_{\alpha}(P_{XY})

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left[1+\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{\alpha}(t)dt\right],&\text{if }\alpha\in[0,1)\\
&\text{or }\alpha\in(1,\infty)\\
\int_{0}^{\infty}G(t)dt,&\alpha=1\end{cases}

(20)

\displaystyle\mbox{ with }G(t)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{y\in{\cal Y}}\mathbb{P}[\imath_{X|Y}(X|Y)>t|Y=y]

(21)

\displaystyle\mbox{ and }J_{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle:=\ln 2(1-\alpha)G(t)2^{(1-\alpha)t}.

(22)

Proof.

Theorem
1
could be restated as

\displaystyle 1-\mathbb{F}_{\imath_{Z}}(t)\geq G(t).

(23)

Recall that for any non-negative random variable
V
, its expectation could be related to its CDF via

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[V]=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\mathbb{F}_{V}(t))dt,

(24)

see, for example
[
32
]
. Since
\imath_{Z}(Z)\geq 0
, we have that

\displaystyle H(Z)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\imath_{Z}(Z)]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(1-\mathbb{F}_{\imath_{Z}}(t)\Big)\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}G(t)dt,

(25)

where (
25
) follows from (
23
). Equation (
19
) is proved similarly for
\alpha\in[0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
, see Appendix for details.
∎

We remark that Corollary
1
is different from all other lower bounds in this paper in that it does not involve computing the entropy of a random variable. For example, we compute the expectation of a random variable with CDF
1-G(t)
in order to obtain a lower bound on Shannon entropy (
\alpha=1
). In general, there may not be an information random variable with this CDF. The next corollary incorporates this additional constraint in its application of Theorem
1
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
(X,Y)
be jointly distributed random variables supported on finite
\cal X
and finite
\cal Y
. Further, let the parameter
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
. Then,

\displaystyle H^{\star}_{\alpha}(P_{XY})\geq\inf_{Q\in{\cal F}}H_{\alpha}(Q)=H_{\alpha}(Q^{\ast})

(26)

where
{\cal F}=\{Q\colon Q\preceq_{\imath}P_{X|Y=y}\forall y\in{\cal Y}\}
and
Q^{\ast}\in{\cal F}
is as given in Lemma
3
.

Proof.

The first inequality is a restatement of Theorem
1
using the relation
\preceq_{\imath}
. According to Lemma
3
,
Q^{\ast}
majorizes every distribution in
\cal F
. Rényi Entropy is Schur concave
[
33
]
with respect to majorization: that is, if
Q\preceq_{m}P
then
H_{\alpha}(Q)\geq H_{\alpha}(P)
. Thus,
Q^{\ast}
achieves the infimum over
\cal F
.
∎

Remark 1
.

Additionally, Note that for
\alpha=1
, these result have also been shown in a parallel work
[
29
]
using ‘profile method’. Our Corollary
1
and Corollary
2
are equivalent to Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
5.5
in
[
29
]
, respectively. The authors also showed that these results also hold for a concave function
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that
F(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},...,p_{n}):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(p_{i})
, where
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a non-negative and concave function satisfying
f(0)=0
.

IV
Comparison of Lower Bounds

IV-A

Analytical Comparison

The following theorem compares the lower bounds in Corollary
1
and
2
to (
10
) and (
11
).

Theorem 2
.

Let
(X,Y)
be jointly distributed random variables supported on finite
\cal X
and finite
\cal Y
. Let

\displaystyle{\cal F}=\{Q\colon Q\preceq_{\imath}P_{X|Y=y}\quad\forall y\in{\cal Y}\},

(27)

with
Q^{\ast}\in{\cal F}
as in Lemma
3
. Let
K_{\alpha}(P_{XY})
be as in Corollary
1
. We make the following statements for all
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
:

1.

In general, we have that

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(Q^{\ast})

\displaystyle\geq K_{\alpha}(P_{XY})\geq\sup_{y\in{\cal Y}}H_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y=y})

(28)

and,

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(Q^{\ast})

\displaystyle\geq H_{\alpha}(\land_{y\in{\cal Y}}P_{X|Y=y})

(29)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{y\in{\cal Y}}H_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y=y}).

(30)

2.

If there exists
y\in{\cal Y}
such that
P_{X|Y=y}\preceq_{\imath}P_{X|Y=\hat{y}}
for all
{\hat{y}}\in{\cal Y}
, then

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(Q^{\ast})

\displaystyle=K_{\alpha}(P_{XY})

(31)

\displaystyle=H_{\alpha}(\land_{y\in{\cal Y}}P_{X|Y=y})

(32)

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in{\cal Y}}H_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y=y}).

(33)

3.

If there exists
y\in{\cal Y}
such that
P_{X|Y=y}\preceq_{m}P_{X|Y=\hat{y}}
for all
{\hat{y}}\in{\cal Y}
, then

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(\land_{y\in{\cal Y}}P_{X|Y=y})=\sup_{y\in{\cal Y}}H_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y=y}).

(34)

In particular, in light of (
28
), this implies that

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(Q^{\ast})

\displaystyle\geq K_{\alpha}(P_{XY})\geq H_{\alpha}(\land_{y\in{\cal Y}}P_{X|Y=y}).

(35)

Proof.

To see (
29
), note that for all
y\in{\cal Y}
we have the following relation

\displaystyle Q^{\ast}\preceq_{m}\land_{y\in{\cal Y}}P_{X|Y=y}\preceq_{m}P_{X|Y=y}.

(36)

The relations holds by Lemma
2
together with
Q^{\ast}\in\cal F
, and the fact that
\land_{y\in{\cal Y}}P_{X|Y=y}
is the greatest lower bound with respect to majorization. Therefore, (
29
) holds by Schur concavity of Rényi entropy. As already observed in
[
21
]
, (
30
) and (
34
) hold by the same application of majorization and Schur concavity.

Let
U\sim Q^{\ast}
. We state the rest of the proof for the
\alpha=1
case. The other cases follow analogously.
For (
28
), we have that

\displaystyle H(Q^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[\imath_{U}(U)>t]dt

(37)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{y\in{\cal Y}}\mathbb{P}[\imath_{X|Y}(X|Y)>t|Y=y]dt

(38)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{y\in{\cal Y}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[\imath_{X|Y}(X|Y)>t|Y=y]dt

(39)

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in{\cal Y}}H(P_{X|Y=y})

(40)

where (
38
) follows since
Q^{\ast}\in{\cal F}
, and (
37
) and (
40
) follow from (
24
). If there exists
y\in{\cal Y}
such that
P_{X|Y=y}\preceq_{\imath}P_{X|Y=\hat{y}}
for all
{\hat{y}}\in{\cal Y}
then
P_{X|Y=y}
will have the same information spectrum as
Q^{\ast}
, and (
38
) and (
39
) become equalities. This shows that
H_{\alpha}(Q^{\ast})=K_{\alpha}(P_{XY})=\sup_{y\in{\cal Y}}H_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y=y})
.
Finally, (
32
) holds by (
34
) and Lemma
2
.
∎

IV-B

Numerical Comparison

As we see in Theorem
2
, the lower bound in Corollary
2
outperforms all other lower bounds. It is not clear how Corollary
1
compares to (
10

Theorem
1
.

Let’s begin by fixing a
y\in{\cal Y}
and a real-valued
t\in[0,\infty)
.
Now, let us define

\displaystyle{\cal X}_{y}

\displaystyle:=\{x\in{\cal X}:{P}_{X|Y}(x|y)>0\}

(47)

\displaystyle\mbox{ and }{\cal X}_{y,t}

\displaystyle:=\{x\in{\cal X}_{y}:{P}_{X|Y}(x|y)<2^{-t}\}

(48)

\displaystyle=\{x\in{\cal X}_{y}:t<{\imath}_{X|Y}(x|y)<\infty\}.

(49)

Define also

\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\{z\in{\cal Z}:{P}_{Z}(z)<2^{-t}\}=\{z\in{\cal Z}:{\imath}_{Z}(z)>t\}.

(50)

Recall that
X
is a deterministic function of
Y
and
Z
i.e., for some
g:{\cal Y}\times{\cal Z}\rightarrow{\cal X}
, we have
X=g(Y,Z)
. Further, define
{\cal Z}_{y}(x):=\{z\in{\cal Z}:g(y,z)=x\}
and observe that

\displaystyle P_{X|YZ}(x|y,z)=\begin{cases}1&,\mbox{ if }z\in{\cal Z}_{y}(x)\\
0&,\mbox{ if }z\not\in{\cal Z}_{y}(x)\end{cases}

(51)

i.e., for
y\in{\cal Y}
fixed as above, every element of
{\cal X}_{y}
maps to a disjoint subset of
{\cal Z}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle{P}_{X|Y}(x|y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in{\cal Z}}P_{X|YZ}(x|y,z){P}_{Z|Y}(z|y)

(52)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in{\cal Z}_{y}(x)}P_{X|YZ}(x|y,z){P}_{Z|Y}(z|y)

(53)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\sum_{z\in{\cal Z}_{y}(x)}{P}_{Z|Y}(z|y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}\sum_{z\in{\cal Z}_{y}(x)}{P}_{Z}(z).

(54)

Eq. (
52
) follows by the law of total probability. Eq. (
53
) and (
54
)(a) follow from
51
, while (
54
)(b) follows from the fact that
Y
and
Z
are independent. Putting everything together,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle[\imath_{X|Y}(X|Y)>t|Y=y]=\sum_{x\in{\cal X}_{y,t}}{P}_{X|Y}(x|y)

(55)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\sum_{x\in{\cal X}_{y,t}}\sum_{z\in{\cal Z}_{y}(x)}{P}_{Z}(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}\sum_{z\in\bigcup_{x\in{\cal X}_{y,t}}{\cal Z}_{y}(x)}{P}_{Z}(z)

(56)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\leq}}\sum_{z\in{\cal Z}_{t}}{P}_{Z}(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{=}}\mathbb{P}[\imath_{Z}(Z)>t].

(57)

Eq. (
56
)(a) follows from (
54
), Eq (
56
)(b) follows since every element of
{\cal X}_{y}
maps to a disjoint subset of
{\cal Z}
, Eq. (
57
) follows from noting that
\forall

z\in\bigcup_{x\in{\cal X}_{y,t}}{\cal Z}_{y}(x)
, we have
P_{Z}(z)<2^{-t}
and therefore
\bigcup_{x\in{\cal X}_{y,t}}{\cal Z}_{y}(x)\subseteq{\cal Z}_{t}
.

Thus, we have shown that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\imath_{Z}(Z)>t\right]\geq\mathbb{P}\left[\imath_{X|Y}(X|Y)>t|Y=y\right].

(58)

Since (
58
) holds for
\forall

y\in{\cal Y}
, it holds for the supremum over
{\cal Y}
.

Lemma
1
.

Let the set
{\cal Y}:=\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},\cdots,y_{|{\cal Y}|}\}
. Recall that
X
is a deterministic function of
Y
and
Z
. Thus,

\displaystyle X=g(Y,Z).

For a fixed
y\in{\cal Y}
, we define the random variable
X_{y}
as

\displaystyle X_{y}

\displaystyle:=g(y,Z)

\displaystyle=g_{y}(Z)

for some function
g_{y}:{\cal Z}\rightarrow{\cal X}
.

Further, note that the random variable
X_{y}
is distributed according to
P_{X|Y=y}
. Now,

\displaystyle H(Z,g_{y_{1}}(Z),g_{y_{2}}(Z),\cdots,g_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}}(Z))

\displaystyle\hskip-142.26378pt=H(g_{y_{1}}(Z),g_{y_{2}}(Z),\cdots,g_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}}(Z))

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt+H(Z|g_{y_{1}}(Z),g_{y_{2}}(Z),\cdots,g_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}}(Z)).

(59)

Also,

\displaystyle H(Z,g_{y_{1}}(Z),g_{y_{2}}(Z),\cdots,g_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}}(Z))

\displaystyle\hskip-142.26378pt=H(Z)+H(g_{y_{1}}(Z),g_{y_{2}}(Z),\cdots,g_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}}(Z)|Z)

(60)

\displaystyle\hskip-142.26378pt=H(Z).

(61)

From (
59
) and (
61
), we have

\displaystyle H(Z)

\displaystyle\geq H(g_{y_{1}}(Z),g_{y_{2}}(Z),\cdots,g_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}}(Z))

\displaystyle=H(X_{y_{1}},X_{y_{2}},\cdots,X_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}})

(62)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{P_{X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{m}}\colon{X_{i}}\sim P_{i}}H(X_{y_{1}},X_{y_{2}},\cdots,X_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}}).

(63)

Note that Eq. (
62
) holds for every random variable
Z
satisfying (
5
) and (
6
), and for every joint distribution of the random variables
(X_{y_{1}},X_{y_{2}},\cdots,X_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}})
. Therefore, the best possible lower bound on
H(Z)
is obtained by minimizing the joint entropy
H(X_{y_{1}},X_{y_{2}},\cdots,X_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}})
, which is equivalent to solving the minimum entropy coupling problem given the random variables
X_{y_{1}},X_{y_{2}},\cdots,X_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}}
distributed according to
P_{X|Y=y_{1}},P_{X|Y=y_{2}},\cdots,P_{X|Y=y_{|{\cal Y}|}}
, respectively.

Remark 2
.

The above proof can be easily extended for the Rényi entropy of order
\alpha\in[0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
.

Let
Z,g_{y_{1}}(Z),g_{y_{2}}(Z),\cdots,g_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}}
be random variables as above, then

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(Z,g_{y_{1}}(Z),g_{y_{2}}(Z),\cdots,g_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}}(Z))

\displaystyle=H_{\alpha}(Z)

\displaystyle\geq H_{\alpha}(g_{y_{1}}(Z),g_{y_{2}}(Z),\cdots,g_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}}).

(64)

Note that Equation
64
holds from noting that for any two random variables
X
and
Y
, the following holds
H_{\alpha}(X,Y)\geq H_{\alpha}(X)
and
H_{\alpha}(X,Y)\geq H_{\alpha}(Y)
. Further, if
Y=f(X)
then
H_{\alpha}(X,Y)=H_{\alpha}(X)\geq H_{\alpha}(Y)
.

Thus, we have the following result from (
64
):

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(Z)\geq\inf_{P_{X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{m}}\colon{X_{i}}\sim P_{i}}H_{\alpha}(X_{y_{1}},X_{y_{2}},\cdots,X_{y_{|{\cal Y}|}}).

(65)

Lemma

Define

\displaystyle t_{i}=\log\frac{1}{q_{i}}\mbox{ and }s_{i}=\log\frac{1}{p_{i}},

(66)

and note that
t_{1},t_{2},\dots
and
s_{1},s_{2},\dots
are both increasing sequences. We prove the claim by induction.

For the base case, suppose
p_{1}<q_{1}
and note that this implies that
s_{1}>t_{1}
. Then

\displaystyle q_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\imath_{U}(U)\leq t_{1}\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\imath_{V}(V)\leq t_{1}\right]=0

(67)

which is a contradiction. Therefore
p_{1}\geq q_{1}
.

For the inductive step, assume that for some
k

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}q_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}.

(68)

If
p_{k+1}\geq q_{k+1}
, then we are done. Else, for the case
p_{k+1}<q_{k+1}
, we note that
s_{k+1}>t_{k+1}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}q_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\imath_{U}(U)\leq t_{k+1}\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\imath_{V}(V)\leq t_{k+1}\right]

(70)

This completes the proof.

Lemma
3
.

Define

\displaystyle G(t):=\max_{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}}\mathbb{P}[\imath_{S_{i}}(S_{i})>t]

(71)

where
S_{i}\sim P_{i}
. Note
G(t)
is an increasing step function that goes from zero to one in at most
\sum_{i=1}^{m}|{\cal S}_{i}|
steps. Also note that every
Q\in{\cal F}
satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}q_{i}\leq G\left(\log\frac{1}{q_{k}}\right)

(72)

where, again, we assume
q_{1}\geq q_{2}\geq q_{3}\dots
.

We construct
Q^{\ast}=(q^{\ast}_{1},q^{\ast}_{2},q^{\ast}_{3},\dots)
with
q^{\ast}_{1}\geq q^{\ast}_{2}\geq q^{\ast}_{3}\dots
in a greedy way. First, assign

\displaystyle q^{\ast}_{1}=\min_{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}}\left\{\max_{s\in{\cal S}_{i}}P_{i}(s)\right\}.

(73)

This is the maximum value we can assign to
q^{\ast}_{1}
while satisfying the information spectrum constraint (
72
). We continue with this strategy: at each step
k
we assign the maximum possible value to
q^{\ast}_{k}
until the total probability of all
q^{\ast}_{k}
reaches one. We claim that the algorithm will terminate in at most
2\sum_{i=1}^{m}|{\cal S}_{i}|
iterations. Indeed, for every ‘step’ in
G(t)
, the algorithm can assign at most two probability values to
Q^{\ast}
before it needs to start assigning smaller values and move on to the next ‘step’.

We also claim that the resulting distribution
Q^{\ast}
will majorize all other distributions that satisfy the information spectrum constraint (
72
). To this end, let
Q\in{\cal F}
be arbitrary such distribution. We show that
Q\preceq_{m}Q^{\ast}
inductively. For the base case, we have that
q^{\ast}_{1}\geq q_{1}
since by construction we assigned the maximum possible value to
q^{\ast}_{1}
.

For the inductive step, assume that for some integer
k
we have that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}q_{k}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}q^{\ast}_{k}
. If
q^{\ast}_{k+1}\geq q_{k+1}
, we are done. Otherwise, define

\displaystyle t_{k+1}=\log\frac{1}{q^{\ast}_{k+1}}\mbox{ and }s_{k+1}=\log\frac{1}{q_{k+1}},

(74)

and note that
s_{k+1}<t_{k+1}
and
G(s_{k+1})\leq G(t_{k+1})
. Let

\displaystyle g_{k+1}=G(s_{k+1})-\sum_{i=1}^{k}q^{\ast}_{i}

(75)

and note that
g_{k+1}<q_{k+1}
. This is a consequence of the greedy construction; if this was not the case, we would assign
q_{k+1}
to
q^{\ast}_{k+1}
. Also,
q^{\ast}_{k+1}\geq g_{k+1}
, again by the property of the greedy construction. This is because we could always assign the value of
g_{k+1}
to
q^{\ast}_{k+1}
without violating the information spectrum constraint. Thus we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}q^{\ast}_{i}+q^{\ast}_{k+1}\geq G(s_{k+1})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}q_{i}

(76)

and this completes the proof.

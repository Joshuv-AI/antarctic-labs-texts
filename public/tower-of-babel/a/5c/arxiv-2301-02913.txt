Observational constraints of diffusive dark-fluid cosmology
A. Abebe

Abstract
In this manuscript, the background and perturbed cosmic dynamics have been investigated using an interacting dark-fluid model by assuming energy exchange between dark matter and dark energy through a diffusion mechanism. We solve the background expansion history for the late-time Universe and derive the full set of the evolution equations of the matter density contrast, δ ⁡ ( z ) \delta(z) and redshift space distortion, f ​ σ 8 ​ ( z ) f\sigma_{8}(z) using the 1 + 3 1+3 -covariant formalism. We then seek to constrain the best-fit cosmological parameters: h h , Ω m \Omega_{m} , r d r_{d} , M M , σ 8 \sigma_{8} and the interaction term Q m Q_{m} through the MCMC simulations with cosmologica datasets. Using the joint datasets of the Hubble parameter measurements from cosmic chronometers (CC), Baryon Acoustic Oscillations (BAO) from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), and SNIa distance moduli from Pantheon+ SH0ES, redshift space distortion (RSD) from large-scale galaxy surveys, a detailed statistical analysis of the work is made. To evaluate the dark fluid model’s viability in describing late-time cosmic dynamics, the numerical results of background cosmological parameters are presented. The results show that the dark fluid behaves like Chaplygin gas (CG) that drives cosmic acceleration when Q m Q_{m} is negative, while for positive Q m Q_{m} , it exhibits characteristics of a quintessence-like phase. From the perturbation evolution equations, the numerical results of δ ⁡ ( z ) \delta(z) and f ​ σ 8 ​ ( z ) f\sigma_{8}(z) were used to explore structure growth. A comparison of the H 0 H_{0} and S 8 S_{8} values has been made of the Λ \Lambda CDM and diffusive models across recent cosmological surveys to show the possibility of alleviating the cosmological tensions, though the detailed analysis is beyond the scope of the current study.

It is often normalized to
\delta(z=0)=1
and is governed by a differential equation involving the Hubble parameter and matter density. This factor shows how initial density perturbations grow over time due to gravity, influencing the formation of large-scale structures. In a dark energy-dominated Universe, the growth slows due to accelerated expansion. The growth factor is crucial for modeling galaxy formation and comparing theoretical predictions with observations, such as those from the CMB and galaxy surveys. Additionally, the growth rate
f(z)
, related to
D(z)
, measures structure growth and is used in observational probes like redshift-space distortions,
{f}\sigma_{8}
. The growth rate
f(z)
, as obtained from the density contrast
\mathcal{\delta}_{m}
, yields
[
64
]

f\equiv\frac{{\rm d}\ln{{{\delta}}_{m}}}{{\rm d}\ln{a}}=-(1+z)\frac{\delta^{\prime}_{m}(z)}{\delta_{m}(z)}\;.

(2.37)

Therefore, the growth factor is fundamental in understanding the dynamical evolution of the Universe’s structures.
Thus, by substituting the definition of (
2.37
) into the quasi-static second-order evolution equation (
2.33
), for the matter-dominated Universe, the evolution of the growth rate is governed by the following expression:

\displaystyle(1+z)f^{\prime}=f^{2}-\left((1+z)\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}-2+\frac{Q_{m}}{\Omega_{m}}\frac{1}{h(1+z)}\right)f-\frac{1}{h^{2}}\bigg(\frac{Q^{2}_{m}}{\Omega^{2}_{m}}(1+z)^{6}

\displaystyle-\frac{2Q_{m}}{\Omega_{m}}(1+z)^{3}h-\frac{3\Omega_{m}}{2}(1+z)^{3}\bigg)\;.

(2.38)

For the case of
Q_{m}=0
, Eq. (
2.4
) exactly reduces to the
\Lambda
CDM result which is given by

\displaystyle(1+z)f^{\prime}=f^{2}-\left((1+z)\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}-2\right)f-\frac{3\Omega_{m}}{2h^{2}}(1+z)^{3}\;.

A combination of the linear growth rate
f(z)
with the root-mean-square normalization of the matter power spectrum
\sigma_{8}
within the radius sphere
8h^{-1}
Mpc, yields the redshift-space distortion
f\sigma_{8}

[
65
]
as

\displaystyle f\sigma_{8}(z)=-(1+z)\sigma_{8}\frac{\ln\delta_{m}(z)}{\delta_{m}(z)}\;.

(2.40)

Note that all the above background and perturbation evolution equations are reduced to the
\Lambda
CDM model for the close of vanishing interaction term,
Q_{m}=0
.

3.5
Statistical analysis

The Bayesian/Schwarz Information Criterion (BIC), and Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) are used in our statistical analysis to evaluate the suitability of diffusive models compared to
\Lambda
CDM. As broadly described in the work
[
92
,
93
,
94
]
, we consider the statistical computations BIC and AIC to determine if the diffusive model should be “accepted" or “rejected" in comparison to
\Lambda
CDM. We use the
\Lambda
CDM as the “accepted" model for comparison to support our claims using the
\rm{AIC}
and
\rm{BIC}
criteria. By using these standards, we can determine if the diffusive model will be accepted or rejected.
The following relations are used for calculating the
\rm{AIC}
and
\rm{BIC}
values in the
\Lambda
CDM and diffusive models:
\rm{AIC}=\chi^{2}+2K,~\text{and}~\rm{BIC}=\chi^{2}+K\log(N_{i}),
where
\chi^{2}
is computed using the model’s Gaussian likelihood function
\mathcal{L}(\hat{\theta}|\text{data})
, the number of free parameters for that specific model is
K
. At the same time,
N_{i}
is the number of data points for the
i^{th}
dataset. Consequently, by defining the AIC Bayes factor,

\Delta\rm{\rm{AIC}}=\rm{AIC}_{\rm{\rm{Diffusive}}}-\rm{\rm{AIC}}_{\it\Lambda\rm{CDM}}\;,

where
\rm\Delta AIC\leq 2
indicates that the proposed theoretical model holds a substantial observational support for the fitted data,
4\leq{\rm\Delta AIC}\leq 7
indicates less observational support, and finally
\Delta\rm{AIC}\geq 10
indicates no observational support as stated in
[
93
]
.
The BIC Bayes factor can also be expressed as follows:

\Delta\rm{BIC}\equiv 2\ln\rm{BIC}=-(\rm{BIC}_{\it i}-\rm{BIC}_{j})\;,

where
2\ln\rm{BIC}
is the BIC Bayes factor comparing model (i) against model (j). In this case, (i) stands for the
\Lambda
CDM model, and (j) for the diffusive model. The following is a ranking of the evidence against
\Lambda
CDM, i.e., in favor of the diffusive model, based on the categorization in
[
93
]
is negligible if
0\leq 2\ln\rm{BIC}\leq 2
, positive if
2\leq\Delta\rm{BIC}\leq 6
, strong if
6\leq\Delta\rm{BIC}\leq 10
, and extremely strong if
\Delta\rm{BIC}>10
.

4
Conclusion

In the current manuscript, the implications of the diffusive model for late-time cosmology and the evolution of cosmic structures in which dark matter and dark energy interact via energy exchange have been investigated. In this paradigm, we assume that the sign of the interaction component,
Q_{m}
, determines the direction of energy transfer between dark matter and dark energy. A negative interaction term
-Q_{m}
implies the transfer of energy from dark matter to dark energy, allowing the diffusive component to serve as an effective dark energy. This energy transfer causes cosmic acceleration while limiting the growth of structures, whereas a positive interaction term has the reverse effect.

To provide a comprehensive understanding, the background evolution and perturbation equations within the diffusive model have been derived, and the best-fit values of cosmological parameters are obtained through MCMC simulations, with the results summarized in Table
2
. In parameter constraints analysis, the values of
\Omega_{m}
,
h
,
r_{d}
,
M
,
\sigma_{8}
and the interaction term
Q_{m}
have been found using combined data sets from the CC, DESI, Pantheon + SH0ES and RSD data sets. From the constrained best-fit values (see Table
2
), we notice that the interaction term has a negative sign across all observational data. The background evolution results given, particularly the diagnostic diagram of the state finder, show that the diffusive model behaves like a CG. This indicates that the diffusive dark fluid cosmology is a promising model as an alternative to dark energy as a driving force for late-time cosmic acceleration due to the exchanging of energy through a diffusive mechanism from the dark matter component to dark energy. This investigation is also supported by the work presented in
[
95
]
. We go over the work by taking into consideration the positive sign of
Q_{m}
to understand the full picture of the model’s dynamics where the energy flow is from dark energy to dark matter. In such an assumption, the diffusive model behaves as a quintessence phase of cosmology. By considering both cases

The investigated parameters, such as

Q_{m}
highlights two very different paths for the Universe’s expansion quintessence and CG model, each with distinct implications for the nature of dark energy. From the result, we noticed that the diffusive model proves more accurate than
\Lambda

We also extended the work to investigate further the scalar perturbations leading to structure formation. We demonstrated the growth of matter density contrast

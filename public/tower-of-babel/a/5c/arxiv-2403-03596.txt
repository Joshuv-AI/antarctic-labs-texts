Motives on pro-algebraic stacks
Can Yaylali

Abstract
We define an ∞ \infty -category of rational motives for inverse limits of algebraic stacks, so-called pro-algebraic stacks. We show that it admits a 6 6 -functor formalism for certain classes of morphisms. On pro-schemes, we show that this 6-functor formalism is in the sense of Liu-Zheng. This theory yields an approach to the theory of motives for non-representable algebraic stacks and non-finite type morphisms.

Introduction

Motivation:
Barsotti–Tate groups have been central to many studies in arithmetic geometry. Consider the stack
BT
of Barsotti–Tate groups over
\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Geometrically,
BT
is difficult to understand, as it is not an algebraic stack. However, we can express
BT
as a pro-system of algebraic stacks,
\textup{BT}\simeq\lim_{n\geq 0}\textup{BT}_{n}
, where
\textup{BT}_{n}
denotes the algebraic stack of
n
-truncated Barsotti–Tate groups. Given the importance of
BT
, it is natural to ask about its “motive” in
\textup{DM}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{Q})
. Since
BT
is not algebraic, this question does not a priori make sense, though one can formally define

M(\textup{BT})\coloneqq\mathop{\textup{colim}}_{n\geq 0}M(\textup{BT}_{n}).

Alternatively, we could formally extend
\textup{DM}^{!}_{\mathbb{Q}}
to arbitrary prestacks and define
M(\textup{BT})
as the
!
-pull/push of the unit. However, there is no general reason why these two definitions should agree. This issue is often referred to as continuity. If
S=\lim_{i}S_{i}
is a projective system of noetherian finite-dimensional schemes with affine transition maps, then

M(S)\cong\mathop{\textup{colim}}_{i}M(S_{i})

provided that
S
is also noetherian of finite dimension
[
CD19
, Thm. 14.3.1]
.

Returning to
BT
, both of the above definitions seem reasonable. However, the formal extension
\textup{DM}^{!}_{\mathbb{Q}}
does not come with the six-functor formalism one would typically expect in a theory of motives. For computations, such a formalism would be very useful. This becomes clearer when considering the stack
Disp
of so-called displays
[
Lau13
]
. This stack also admits a presentation
\textup{Disp}\simeq\lim_{n\geq 0}\textup{Disp}_{n}
as a pro-system of algebraic stacks over
\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, and we have a map of pro-systems of algebraic stacks
\phi\colon\textup{BT}\to\textup{Disp}

[
Lau13
]
. This morphism is level-wise smooth and a universal homeomorphism. The motive of each
\textup{Disp}_{n}
is in fact Tate (cf. Theorem
4.16
), and hence easier to understand than the motive of each
\textup{BT}_{n}
. To transfer information from
Disp
to
BT
, a definition of the motive of a pro-system of algebraic stacks should be compatible with morphisms, at least those of pro-(locally of finite type).

In this article, we will investigate such situations more closely. To be more precise, we will study the category of rational motives on pro-systems of algebraic stacks
X\simeq\lim_{i}X_{i}
defined via
\mathop{\textup{colim}}_{i}\textup{DM}(X_{i})
, and investigate its functorial properties.

Motives on algebraic stacks:
We can define
\textup{DM}(F)
, the DG-category of motives for any prestack
F\colon\textup{(Ring)}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}
(here
\mathbb{S}
denotes the
\infty
-category of Kan-complexes) via right Kan extension along the Yoneda embedding
[
RS20
]
. In this way, we can define
\textup{DM}(X)
for any algebraic stack locally of finite type over a field
K
for rational motives. Since rational motives satisfy étale descent, the
\infty
-category of rational motives on an algebraic stack
X
with smooth atlas
\tilde{X}
can be computed via the formula

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{DM}(X)\coloneqq\lim(\textup{DM}(\tilde{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{DM}(\tilde{X}\times_{X}\tilde{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots)}

[
RS20
]
. The problem with these definitions is that the computation of the motive of a limit
\lim_{i}X_{i}
as above is not straightforward. To circumvent this problem, we will work with an alternative definition and show that in classical situations our definition agrees with the usual one.

Motives on pro-algebraic stacks:

Let us fix a filtered
\infty
-category
{\mathcal{I}}
.

Definition 1
.

Let
K
be a field and
x\colon{\mathcal{I}}\rightarrow\textup{PreStk}_{K}^{\op}
be an inverse system of prestacks. Assume that each
X_{i}\coloneqq x(i)
is representable by an algebraic stack locally of finite type over

. Then we call the tuple

a
pro-
{\mathcal{I}}
-algebraic stack
.

We call
(x,X)

tame
, if
x_{ij}
is smooth for all
i\to j\in{\mathcal{I}}
.

Note that there is an obvious notion of morphisms of pro-
{\mathcal{I}}
-algebraic stacks.

Definition 2
.

Let
(x,X)
be a pro-
{\mathcal{I}}
-algebraic stack. Then we define the
\infty
-category of rational motives on
(x,X)
to be

\textup{DM}(x,X)\coloneqq\mathop{\textup{colim}}_{i\in{\mathcal{I}},x^{*}}\textup{DM}(X_{i}),

where the colimit is computed in
\textup{DGCat}_{\textup{cont}}\coloneqq\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbb{Q})}(\Pr^{L}_{\mathrm{st}})
.

With this construction, we will obtain a full
6
-functor formalism on pro-
{\mathcal{I}}
-algebraic stacks, which is not provided by the right Kan extended version. The caveat is that this
6
-functor formalism does not exist for all morphisms of pro-
{\mathcal{I}}
-algebraic stacks. We have to make restrictions depending if
{\mathcal{I}}\simeq\mathbb{N}
, the poset
(\mathbb{N},\leq)
viewed as an
\infty
-category, or not. We will explain this in the main text of this article.

To keep this introduction light, we will only state a summarized version of the existence of a (motivic)
6
-functor formalism and refer for a precise version to the main text.

Theorem 3

(
2.9
)
.

The
\infty
-category
\textup{DM}(x,X)
is closed symmetric monoidal, compactly generated and the assignment
(x,X)\mapsto\textup{DM}(x,X)
is contravariantly functorial in
(x,X)
.

Let

be a morphisms of tame pro-
\mathbb{N}
-algebraic stacks that is levelwise locally of finite type. Then

is part of a
6
-functor formalism if we assume that the exchange map
f_{n+1!}x_{n}^{!}\rightarrow y_{n}^{!}f_{n!}
induced by the diagram is

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{n+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{n+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{n+1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{n}}

{\mathcal{I}}

The assumption on the base change map is satisfied, for example, if

Disp

The definition via

Theorem 4

(

)
.

The pullback along the truncation map
\textup{Disp}\rightarrow\textup{Disp}_{1}
induces a fully faithful embedding.

\textup{DM}(\textup{Disp}_{1})\hookrightarrow\textup{DM}(\textup{Disp})

In particular, we have an equivalence of motives

M(\textup{Disp})\simeq M(\textup{Disp}_{1})

inside
\textup{DM}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
and moreover it is contained in the full stable cocomplete subcategory of
\textup{DM}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
generated by Tate motives.

As a consequence, working with pro-algebraic stacks, we see that
H^{*,*}(BT,\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{*,*}(\textup{Disp}_{1},\mathbb{Q})
(see Corollary

Generalization:

Since most arguments in the proof of Theorem
3
apply in a more general context, we will frequently work with compactly generated
6
-functor formalisms. Although our primary interest lies in rational motives, we will formulate the necessary statements as generally as possible. This broader perspective encompasses both compactly generated pullback formalisms
[
DG22
]
and
6
-functor formalisms in the sense of Liu–Zheng
[
LZ17
]
. The advantage of this generality is that the key computations remain valid in a wider range of settings, such as pro-systems of ind-schemes. The étale derived category of such systems, for instance, is considered by Bouthier, Kazhdan and Varshavsky
[

]
.

2
Motives on pro-algebraic stacks

In this article, we aim to extend the six-functor formalism to limits of algebraic stacks, such as the stack of Barsotti–Tate groups. This stack can be realized as a limit of its truncations. We develop a general theory for inverse systems of algebraic stacks, allowing for non-representable transition maps.

Before introducing our main constructions and definitions, we recall the theory of rational motives on algebraic stacks. Following the construction of Richarz–Scholbach, we obtain the DG-category of rational motives on prestacks as defined in
[
RS20
, Def. 2.2.1]
. We will later specialize this to our specific context. In what follows, we omit some details and refer the reader to
loc. cit.
for an explicit construction.

Let
\textup{AffSch}^{\textup{ft}}_{S}
denote the category of affine schemes of finite type over
S
. Since
\textup{AffSch}^{\textup{ft}}_{S}
is essentially small, we replace it with a small skeleton consisting of the relevant objects for our purposes. We write
\textup{AffSch}^{\kappa}_{S}
for the pro-
\kappa
-completion of
\textup{AffSch}^{\textup{ft}}_{S}
, which is again small by
[
KS06
, Lem. 6.1.2]
.

A prestack over
S
is a functor from
\textup{AffSch}^{\kappa}_{S}
to
\infty
-groupoids. The
\infty
-category of prestacks over
S
is denoted by
\textup{PreStk}_{S}
.

The functor
\textup{DM}\colon X\mapsto\textup{DM}(X)
denotes the functor of Beilinson motives on finite type
S
-schemes. We extend
DM
to prestacks as follows. First, we perform a left Kan extension of
DM
from
\textup{AffSch}^{\textup{ft}}_{S}
to
\textup{AffSch}^{\kappa}_{S}
, followed by a right Kan extension along the Yoneda embedding
\textup{AffSch}^{\kappa}_{S}\hookrightarrow\textup{PreStk}_{S}
. The transition maps in this process are given by
*
-pullbacks. This yields a functor

\textup{DM}^{*}\colon\textup{PreStk}_{S}^{\op}\to\textup{DGCat}_{\textup{cont}}.

If we instead use
!
-pullbacks in place of
*
-pullbacks, we denote the resulting functor by
\textup{DM}^{!}
. However, when restricted to schemes locally of finite type over
S
, these two constructions are equivalent. This equivalence follows from the fact that, in our setting,
DM
satisfies h-descent on schemes
[
CD19
]
. Consequently, both
\textup{DM}^{!}
and
\textup{DM}^{*}
can be computed via descent. Indeed, schemes locally of finite type admit open covers by affine schemes of finite type, and
!
- and
*
-pullbacks agree up to shift and twist. The same reasoning shows that
\textup{DM}^{*}(X)\simeq\textup{DM}^{!}(X)
for any algebraic space, since the diagonal of an algebraic space is representable by a scheme.

Using étale descent, we may glue the six-functor formalism of
DM
to the restriction of
\textup{DM}^{*}
to algebraic spaces, as in
[
Kha19
]
, or equivalently via the DESCENT algorithm of Liu–Zheng
[
LZ17
]
. Similar constructions appear in
[
RS20
]
, where ind-algebraic stacks and ind-schemes are treated.

We may now apply the same arguments to glue the six-functor formalism to the restriction of
\textup{DM}^{*}
to algebraic stacks locally of finite type over
S
. As before, the existence of a smooth cover implies that
\textup{DM}^{!}(X)\simeq\textup{DM}^{*}(X)
for any such stack
X
.

To summarize, there is (up to equivalence) no distinction between working with
\textup{DM}^{!}
and
\textup{DM}^{*}
. Furthermore, we obtain a complete six-functor formalism in the sense of Liu–Zheng for algebraic stacks locally of finite type over
S
, i.e., the functor
\textup{DM}^{*}
extends to a symmetric monoidal functor

\textup{DM}^{*}\colon\textup{Corr}({\mathrm{Stk}^{\textup{lft}}_{S}})_{\textup{lft}}\to\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\omega}).

To simplify notation, we will henceforth write
DM
for the extension
\textup{DM}^{*}
. We retain the notation
\textup{DM}^{!}
for the
!
-variant.

Proposition 2.13
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{I}}

{\mathcal{I}}
-algebraic stack. Then the natural map

Proof.

For the proof we will use two immediate facts. The canonical map

is by construction monoidal and preserves colimits. Moreover, to show that

is an equivalence, we may work with the right adjoints

\textup{DM}(X)\rightarrow\lim_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}^{\op},x_{*}}\textup{DM}(X_{i})

(this follows from Lemma
2.10
applied to

).
By assumption
X_{0}
admits a smooth cover by affine finite type
S
-schemes

, for some set

and

. As
\textbullet X_{i}\rightarrow X_{0}
is affine for all

, the pullback

is also a smooth cover by affines. The same holds after passage to limits, i.e. for

the projection

is an effective epimorphism
[
Sta25
, 01YZ]

[

\lim_{\Delta}\textup{DM}(\check{C}_{\bullet}(\coprod_{k\in K}U_{i}/X))\simeq\textup{DM}(X)

\displaystyle\textup{DM}(X)\simeq\lim_{\Delta}\textup{DM}(\check{C}_{\bullet}(\coprod_{k\in K}U_{i}/X))

\displaystyle\simeq\lim_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}^{\op},x_{*}}\lim_{\Delta}\textup{DM}(\check{C}_{\bullet}(\coprod_{k\in K}U_{i,k}/X_{i}))

Definition 3.1

By Lemma

f_{L*}1_{(x,X_{L})}\simeq f_{L*}x_{L/K}^{*}1_{X}\simeq q_{L/K}^{*}f_{*}1_{X}.

If

Next, we claim that
\Gal(L/K)
acts on
h_{*}1_{(x,X_{L})}

\Gal(E/K)
on
h_{*}1_{(x,X_{L})}
for any finite Galois extension
E/K

\Gal(L/K)
as a limit of such.

Remark 4.21
.

Let
E/K
be a finite Galois extension and
\varphi\in\Aut_{K}(E)
. And
M\in\textup{DM}(q,\Spec(L))
. Then
\varphi
induces an automorphism

g_{*}M\rightarrow g_{*}M

via the following.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}_{E}^{L}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{K}^{L}
denote the subcategory of finite Galois extensions over
E
. Then we can restrict
q
along this (filtered) subcategory and we denote the induced pro-
{\mathcal{G}}_{E}^{L}
-algebraic stack by
(q_{|E},\Spec(L))
. The
K
-automorphism
\varphi
induces via base change a cartesian automorphism
\varphi\colon(q_{|E},\Spec(L))\rightarrow(q_{|E},\Spec(L))
. The
*
-pushforward along
\varphi
yields a functor

\varphi_{*}\colon\textup{DM}(q,\Spec(L))\rightarrow\textup{DM}(q,\Spec(L)).

By construction
g_{*}\varphi_{*}\simeq g_{*}
, yielding the desired endomorphism above.

This endomorphism by construction induces an action of
\Gal(L/K)
on
g_{*}M
, i.e. a map
B\Gal(L/K)\rightarrow\textup{DM}(K)
.

Remark 4.22
.

Combining the above, we have

\mathop{\textup{colim}}_{E\in{\mathcal{G}}_{K}^{L}}h_{*}1_{X}^{\Gal(E/K)}\simeq\mathop{\textup{colim}}_{E\in{\mathcal{G}}_{K}^{L}}\mathop{\textup{colim}}_{F\in{\mathcal{G}}_{E}^{L}}q_{F/K*}f_{F*}1_{X_{F}}^{\Gal(E/K)}\simeq f_{*}1_{X},

where we use
[
Ayo07
, Lem. 2.1.166]
in the first equivalence. In particular, for a
X
smooth
K
-scheme, we have

A^{n}(X,m)_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq A^{n}(X_{L},m)^{\Gal(L/K)}_{\mathbb{Q}}

by Example
4.7
. This recovers the computation on the level of cycles
[
Bro18
, Lem. 1.3.6]
.

There exists a notion of continuous homotopy fixed points on so-called discrete
G
-spectra
[
BD10
]
. The object

\mathop{\textup{colim}}_{E\in{\mathcal{G}}_{K}^{L}}h_{*}1_{X}^{\Gal(E/K)}
can be seen as an analog of continuous homotopy fixed points under the action of the absolute Galois group. By construction the action of
\Gal(L/K)
is stabilized on an open and closed normal subgroup after passage to a large enough field extension.

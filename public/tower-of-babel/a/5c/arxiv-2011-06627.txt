An extension of the Siegel-Walfisz theorem
Andreas Weingartner

Abstract
. We extend the Siegel-Walfisz theorem to a family of integer sequences that are characterized by constraints on the size of the prime factors. Besides prime powers, this family includes smooth numbers, almost primes and practical numbers.

Introduction

Let
\pi(x,q,a)
denote the number of primes up to
x
that are congruent to
a\bmod q

and let
\pi(x)
be the number of all primes up to
x

The Siegel-Walfisz theorem shows that, for any fixed
A>0
, we have

\pi(x,q,a)=\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(q)}+O_{A}\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}\right),

(1)

uniformly for
2\leq q\leq(\log x)^{A}
and
\gcd(a,q)=1
.
Thus, the primes are close to evenly distributed among the
\varphi(q)
residue classes that are
coprime to the modulus
q
.

We will generalize (
1
) to a
family of integer sequences that arise as follows.
Let
\theta
be an arithmetic function that satisfies

\theta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\},\quad\theta(1)\geq 2,\quad\theta(n)\geq P^{+}(n)\quad(n\geq 2),

where

denotes the largest prime factor of
n

be the set of positive integers containing

If

Theorem 1
.

Assume
\theta
satisfies (
2
). Let
A>0
be fixed. Uniformly for
2\leq q\leq(\log x)^{A}
,
a\in\mathbb{Z}
with

\gcd(a,q)=1
, we have

B(x,q,a):=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)\atop n\equiv a\bmod q}1=O_{A}\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}\right)+\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)\atop\gcd(n,q)=1}1.

The implied constant only depends on
A
and not on the choice of
\theta
.

For the problem of smooth numbers in arithmetic progressions,
Harper
[
2
]
establishes asymptotic equidistribution among the residue classes
in a larger domain than is implied by Theorem
1
.
For almost primes (
\omega(n)\leq k
) in arithmetic progressions, Friedlander
[
1
]
(
k=2
) and
Knafo
[
3
, Thm. 6]
(
k\geq 2
) give estimates for the corresponding sums with weights
\Lambda_{k}(n)
,
the generalized von Mangoldt function.

Unlike with primes, the distribution of integers in
\mathcal{B}
among residue classes
a\bmod q
with
\gcd(a,q)>1

is not immediately obvious in general. If
\theta
satisfies
\theta(n)\leq\theta(mn)
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we
will show that Theorem
1
implies uniform distribution among all the residue classes that share the same greatest common divisor with the modulus.

Corollary 1
.

Assume
\theta
satisfies (
2
) and
\theta(n)\leq\theta(mn)
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
A>0
be fixed. Uniformly for
2\leq q\leq(\log x)^{A}
,
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
d:=\gcd(a,q)
, we have

B(x,q,a)=O_{A}\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}\right)+\frac{1}{\varphi(q/d)}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)\atop\gcd(n,q)=d}1.

The implied constant only depends on
A
and not on the choice of
\theta
.

Define

\mathcal{B}_{q}(x)=\mathcal{B}_{\theta,q}(x)=\{n\in\mathcal{B}_{\theta}(x),q|n\},\quad B_{q}(x)=B_{\theta,q}(x)=|\mathcal{B}_{q}(x)|.

The inclusion-exclusion principle allows us to rewrite the right-hand side of Corollary
1
as follows.

Corollary 2
.

With the assumptions of Corollary
1
we have

B(x,q,a)=O_{A}\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}\right)+\frac{1}{\varphi(q/d)}\sum_{m|q/d}\mu(m)B_{dm}(x),

where
d=\gcd(a,q)
.

One way to understand
B_{q}(x)
, and thus the right-hand side of Corollary

is to consider a modified version of
\theta
, denoted by
\theta_{q}
,
which we define as follows.
Let

, where
q_{1}\leq\ldots\leq q_{k}
are the prime factors of
q

If
\theta(n)<q_{1}
then
\theta_{q}(n)=\theta(n)
. Otherwise,

\theta_{q}(n)=\theta(nq_{1}\cdots q_{j})

is as large as possible, subject to

q_{i}\leq\theta(nq_{1}\cdots q_{i-1})\quad(1\leq i\leq j).

Lemma 1
.

Assume (
2
) and
\theta(n)\leq\theta(mn)
for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. We have

B_{\theta_{q}}(x/q)-R(x,q)\leq B_{\theta,q}(x)\leq B_{\theta_{q}}(x/q),

where

R(x,q)=|\{n\leq x/q:\theta(n)<P^{+}(q)\}|.

Since
\theta(n)\geq P^{+}(n)
, we always have

R(x,q)\leq\Psi(x,q):=|\{n\leq x:P^{+}(n)\leq q\}|\ll_{A}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}},

(3)

provided

, by
[

. If
\theta(n)\geq n
, then

.

With Lemma

, the known asymptotic results for

under various conditions on
\theta(n)
(see
[

),

Since the implied constants in the error terms of these estimates depend on

.
We only take a closer look at the case when

,
which includes the practical numbers as well as the

.

If

,

B(x)=\frac{c_{\theta}x}{\log x}\left(1+O_{\theta}\left(\frac{1}{\log x}\right)\right),

B_{\theta,q}(x)=\frac{c_{\theta_{q}}x/q}{\log x/q}\left(1+O_{\theta,q}\left(\frac{1}{\log x/q}\right)\right)=\frac{c_{q}x}{\log x}\left(1+O_{\theta,q}\left(\frac{1}{\log x}\right)\right),

c_{q}:=c_{\theta_{q}}/q
. Thus, Corollary
2

and (

yield the following estimate for
B(x,q,a)
.

Corollary 3
.

Assume (
2
),
n\leq\theta(n)\ll\sigma(n)
and
\theta(n)\leq\theta(nm)\ll m^{O(1)}\theta(n)
for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
a\in\mathbb{Z}
and

, we have

B(x,q,a)=\frac{c_{q,a}x}{\log x}+O_{\theta,q}\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right),

where

c_{q,a}=\frac{1}{\varphi(q/d)}\sum_{m|q/d}\mu(m)c_{dm},\quad d=\gcd(a,q),

c_{dm}:=c_{dm,0}=\frac{c_{\theta_{dm}}}{dm},\quad c_{\theta_{dm}}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{B_{\theta_{dm}}(x)}{x/\log x}.

Moreover,
c_{q,a}=0
if and only if
B(x,q,a)\leq 1
for all

.

Margenstern
[

showed that the number of practical

says that if an arithmetic progression contains more than one practical number

Proof of Theorem
1

Throughout this section,
\chi
stands for a Dirichlet character modulo
q
and
\chi_{0}
denotes the principal character.
We write
s=\sigma+i\tau
, where
\sigma=\re(s)
and
\tau=\im(s)
.

Lemma 2
.

We have

\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{1}{n(\log P^{+}(n))^{2}}\ll 1.

Proof.

The sum equals

\sum_{p\geq 2}\frac{1}{p(\log p)^{2}}\sum_{P^{+}(m)\leq p}\frac{1}{m}=\sum_{p\geq 2}\frac{1}{p(\log p)^{2}}\prod_{q\leq p}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{-1}\ll\sum_{p\geq 2}\frac{1}{p\log p}\ll 1.

∎

Lemma 3
.

For
\gcd(a,q)=1
we have

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)\atop n\equiv a\bmod q}1=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)\atop\gcd(n,q)=1}1+\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}\overline{\chi}(a)\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)}\chi(n).

Proof.

This follows from summing over
n\in\mathcal{B}(x)
the orthogonality relation for Dirichlet characters
[
6
, Eq. 4.27]
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

[
6
, Sec. 11.3.1, Ex. 5]

Let
A>0
be fixed and
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
. For
q\leq(\log x)^{A}
we have

\sum_{p\leq x}\chi(p)\ll_{A}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}.

Lemma 5
.

Let
A>0
be fixed and
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
. For
q\leq(\log x)^{A}
we have

\sum_{p>x}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\ll_{A}\frac{|\tau|+1}{(\log x)^{A}}\qquad(\sigma\geq 1).

Proof.

If
1\leq\sigma\leq 2
, this follows from Lemma
4
and partial summation. If
\sigma>2
, it is obvious.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Assume
\theta
satisfies (
2
).
Let
\chi
be any Dirichlet character. For
\sigma>1
we have

L_{\chi}(s):=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\chi(m)}{m^{s}}=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{s}}\prod_{p>\theta(n)}\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}.

Proof.

Each
m\geq 1
factors uniquely as
m=nr
, with
n\in\mathcal{B}
and

P^{-}(r)>\theta(n)
if
r>1
, where
P^{-}(r)
denotes the smallest prime factor of
r
.
∎

Lemma 7
.

Let
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
and
T\geq 2
. For
|\tau|\leq T
and
\sigma\geq 0
,

L_{\chi}(s)\ll(Tq)^{\max(0,(1-\sigma)/2)}\log(Tq).

Proof.

When
0\leq\sigma\leq 1
, this is
[
4
, Eq. (3)]
. If
\sigma\geq 1
, this follows from
[

, Thm. II.8.6]

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Assume
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
. We have

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)}\chi(n)=\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)-\sum_{n\leq x\atop n\notin\mathcal{B}(x)}\chi(n)=O(q)-S(x),

say. Theorem
1
follows from Lemma
3
if we can show
S(x)\ll x/(\log x)^{A}
.
With
T=(\log x)^{A+1}
and
\kappa=1+1/\log x
, Perron’s formula
[
11
, Cor. II.2.2.1]
yields

S(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\kappa-iT}^{\kappa+iT}F(s)x^{s}\frac{ds}{s}+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}\right),

(8)

where, for
\sigma>1
,

F(s):=\sum_{m\notin\mathcal{B}}\frac{\chi(m)}{m^{s}}=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{s}}\left(\prod_{p>\theta(n)}\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}-1\right),

by Lemma
6
.
Let
F_{1}(s)
(resp.
F_{2}(s)
) denote the contribution to the last sum from
n
with
\theta(n)\leq N
(resp.
\theta(n)>N
),
where
N
is given by
\log N=T^{1/\lambda}
and
\lambda>0
is constant.

We first estimate the contribution of
F_{2}(s)
to the integral in (
8
).
For any fixed
\mu\geq\lambda
and all
n
with
\theta(n)>N
, we have

q\leq T\leq T^{\mu/\lambda}=(\log N)^{\mu}<(\log\theta(n))^{\mu}.

If
|\tau|\leq T
, Lemma
5
yields

\prod_{p>\theta(n)}\!\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}\!\!\!=\exp\left(\sum_{p>\theta(n)}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\theta(n)}\right)\right)\!=1+O\left(\frac{|\tau|+1}{(\log\theta(n))^{\mu}}\right).

Thus,

F_{2}(s)\ll\sum_{\theta(n)>N}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\frac{|\tau|+1}{(\log\theta(n))^{\mu}}\ll\frac{|\tau|+1}{(\log N)^{\mu-2}},

by (
2
) and Lemma
2
. With
\mu=2\lambda+2
, the contribution of
F_{2}(s)
to (
8
) is

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\kappa-iT}^{\kappa+iT}F_{2}(s)x^{s}\frac{ds}{s}\ll\frac{xT}{(\log N)^{\mu-2}}=\frac{x}{T^{(\mu-2)/\lambda-1}}=\frac{x}{T}=\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A+1}}.

To estimate the contribution of
F_{1}(s)
to (
8
), we rewrite
F_{1}(s)
as

F_{1}(s)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}\atop\theta(n)\leq N}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{s}}\left(L_{\chi}(s)\prod_{p\leq\theta(n)}\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\right)-1\right).

This formula, initially valid for
\sigma>1
, gives the analytic continuation of
F_{1}(s)
to
the half-plane
\sigma>0
, since

\prod_{p\leq\theta(n)}\left|1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\right|\leq 2^{\theta(n)}\leq 2^{N}

and

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}\atop\theta(n)\leq N}\frac{|\chi(n)|}{n^{\sigma}}\leq\sum_{P^{+}(n)\leq N}\frac{1}{n^{\sigma}}=\prod_{p\leq N}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\sigma}}\right)^{-1}.

We replace the vertical line segment, with endpoints

\kappa-iT
and
\kappa+iT
, by the three line segments with endpoints
\kappa-iT
,
\alpha-iT
,
\alpha+iT
,
and
\kappa+iT
, where
\alpha=1-1/(\log x)^{\delta}
and
\delta=(A+1)/\lambda
.
On each of these three line segments, we have
\alpha\leq\sigma\leq\kappa
and
|\tau|\leq T
, so that

\prod_{p\leq\theta(n)}\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\right)\ll\exp\left(\sum_{p\leq\theta(n)}\frac{1}{p^{\alpha}}\right)\leq\exp\left(\sum_{p\leq N}\frac{N^{1-\alpha}}{p}\right),

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}\atop\theta(n)\leq N}\frac{|\chi(n)|}{n^{\sigma}}\leq\sum_{P^{+}(n)\leq N}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}=\prod_{p\leq N}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\alpha}}\right)^{-1}\ll\exp\left(\sum_{p\leq N}\frac{N^{1-\alpha}}{p}\right),

\exp\left(\sum_{p\leq N}\frac{N^{1-\alpha}}{p}\right)=\exp\bigl(N^{1-\alpha}(\log\log N+O(1))\bigr)\ll(\log x)^{e(A+1)/\lambda}

and

L_{\chi}(s)\ll(Tq)^{1/(\log x)^{\delta}}\log(Tq)\ll\log\log x,

by Lemma
7
.
With
\lambda=6(A+1)
, we get

F_{1}(s)\ll(\log x)^{2e(A+1)/\lambda}\log\log x\ll\log x.

Since
\delta=(A+1)/\lambda=1/6
, the contribution from the vertical segment is

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\alpha-iT}^{\alpha+iT}F_{1}(s)x^{s}\frac{ds}{s}\ll x^{\alpha}(\log x)(\log T)\ll_{A}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}.

The contribution from the two horizontal segments can be estimated as

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\alpha+iT}^{\kappa+iT}F_{1}(s)x^{s}\frac{ds}{s}\ll\frac{x\log x}{T}=\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Assume (
2
) and

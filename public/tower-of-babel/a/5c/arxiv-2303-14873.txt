Strong attractors for the nonclassical diffusion equation with fading memory in time-dependent spaces


Abstract
In this paper, we discuss the long-time behavior of solutions to the nonclassical diffusion equation with fading memory when the nonlinear term f f fulfills the polynomial growth of arbitrary order and the external force g ​ ( x ) ∈ L 2 ​ ( Ω ) g(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega) . In the framework of time-dependent spaces, we verify the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions by the Galerkin method, then we obtain the existence of the time-dependent global attractor 𝒜 = { A t } t ∈ ℝ \mathscr{A}=\{A_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}} in ℳ t 1 \mathcal{M}_{t}^{1} .

In this article, we investigate the following nonclassical diffusion equation with fading memory

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}-\varepsilon(t)\Delta{u_{t}}-\Delta{u}-\int_{0}^{\infty}k(s)\Delta{u(t-s)}\,ds+f(u)=g(x),\ &&\text{in }\Omega\times(\tau,+\infty),\\
&u(x,t)=0,&&x\in\partial\Omega,\ t\in\mathbb{R},\\
&u(x,t)=u_{\tau}(x,t),&&x\in\Omega,\ t\leq\tau,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. For any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
u_{\tau}:\Omega\times(-\infty,\tau]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a given function and
g(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is an external force. We make some presumptions for function
\varepsilon(t)
, nonlinear term
f
and the memory term.

(\mathbf{H}_{1})
: Assume that
\varepsilon(\cdot)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is a decreasing bounded function satisfying

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\varepsilon(t)=0.

(1.2)

Especially, there exists a constant
L>0
, such that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}[\lvert\varepsilon(t)\rvert+\lvert\varepsilon^{\prime}(t)\rvert]\leq L.

(1.3)

(\mathbf{H}_{2})
: Assume that the nonlinear term
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\ f(0)=0
and fulfills the polynomial growth of arbitrary order

\beta_{1}\lvert s\rvert^{p}-\gamma_{1}\leq f(s)s\leq\beta_{2}\lvert s\rvert^{p}+\gamma_{2},\ p\geq 2,

(1.4)

and meets the dissipation condition

f^{\prime}(s)\geq-l,

(1.5)

where
\beta_{i},\gamma_{i}(i=1,2)
and
l
are positive constants.

Let
F(s)=\int_{\tau}^{s}f(r)\,dr
, it’s known from (
1.4
) that there are positive constants
\widetilde{\beta_{i}},\widetilde{\gamma_{i}}(i=1,2)
such that

\widetilde{\beta_{1}}\lvert s\rvert^{p}-\widetilde{\gamma_{1}}\leq F(s)\leq\widetilde{\beta_{2}}\lvert s\rvert^{p}+\widetilde{\gamma_{2}}.

(1.6)

(\mathbf{H}_{3})
: Assume that for any
s>0
,
k(\cdot)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\ k(s)\geq 0,\ k^{\prime}(s)\leq 0
, and
k(\infty)=\lim\limits_{s\to\infty}k(s)=0
.
Besides, we assume that the function
\mu(s)=-k^{\prime}(s)
and satisfies

\mu\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\ \mu(s)\geq 0,\ \mu^{\prime}(s)\leq 0,\ \forall s\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

(1.7)

\mu^{\prime}(s)+\delta\mu(s)\leq 0,\ \forall s\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

(1.8)

where
\delta
is a positive constant.

Aifantis
[
1
]
first proposed the classical diffusion equation in 1980, later he realized that the conductive medium’s properties would also have an effect on the equation. As a result, he established the nonclassical diffusion equation by taking into account the conductive medium’s viscosity, elasticity, pressure and other factors.

The viscoelasticity of the conductive medium is taken into account by the form of a memory term in Eq.(
1.1
), which is an extension of the nonclassical diffusion equation used in fluid mechanics, solid mechanics and heat conduction theory (see
[
1
,
6
,
10
]
). Under the influence of the convolution of function
\Delta u
and memory kernel
k(\cdot)
, the pace of energy dissipation of the dynamical system corresponding to Eq.(
1.1
) is faster than the nonclassical diffusion equation without fading memory.

When
\varepsilon(t)
is a positive constant independent of
t
, some conclusions have been achieved on the dynamical behavior of the solutions to Eq.(
1.1
) under different assumptions. For autonomous systems, Wang et al
[
19
]
investigated the existence and regularity of global attractors for Eq.(
1.1
) in weak topological space and strong topological space when nonlinearity meets the critical condition. Wang and Wang
[
18
]
considered the existence of trajectory and global attractors for Eq.(
1.1
) by applying the method presented by Chepyzhov and Miranville
[
2
,
3
]
. Ma et al
[
8
]
discussed the global attractors for Eq.(
1.1
) in an unbounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
when nonlinearity is critical. Zhang et al
[
21
]
added term
\lambda u
to Eq.(
1.1
) and proved the existence of global attractors in the space
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
when the nonlinearity satisfies arbitrary polynomial growth. Qin and Dong
[
12
]
verified the strong global attractors for Eq.(
1.1
) in
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
by the condition
(C)
. For non-autonomous systems, Wang and Zhong
[
20
]
developed the results in
[
19
]
and further obtained the existence of uniform attractors. Wang et al
[
16
]
explored the long-time dynamical behavior of Eq.(
1.1
) in strong topological space when nonlinearity is critical. Li and Wang
[
7
]
obtained the existence and topological structure of a compact uniform attractor for Eq.(
1.1
) with subcritical nonlinearity by means of the technique of asymptotic regularity estimate.

However, handling the Eq.(
1.1
) becomes more challenging if
\varepsilon(t)
is a bounded function that decreases monotonically. This is due to the fact that the classical semigroup theory is no longer entirely applicable, despite the external force
g
being independent of
t
. The dynamical system generated by Eq.(
1.1
) becomes non-autonomous case owing to
\varepsilon(t)
. To this end, Plinio et al
[
9
]
defined the solution operator as a family of maps acting on a time-dependent family of spaces
X_{t}
, in which the norm depends entirely on time
t
, and proposed the concept of time-dependent global attractors. Thereafter some scholars launched new researches based on the notation of time-dependent attractors. For example, Zhu et al
[
22
]
showed the existence of time-dependent global attractors in
\mathcal{H}_{t}
for a class of nonclassical reaction–diffusion equations with the forcing term
g(x)\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
and the nonlinearity
f
satisfying the polynomial growth of arbitrary
p-1(p\geq 2)
order. Tang et al
[
15
]
gained the well-posedness of Eq.(
1.1
) by using the nonclassical method of Faedo-Galerkin and analytical techniques.

As far as we are aware, no one has ever study the strong attractors for the nonclassical diffusion equation with fading memory in time-dependent spaces when the nonlinear term
f
fulfills the polynomial growth of arbitrary order. Hence, we shall investigate this matter. In order to solve the aforementioned issue, we must overcome some difficulties. On one hand, the existence of term
-\Delta{u_{t}}
means that we cannot get higher regularity of the solution. Thus we cannot use the compact Sobolev embedding to verify the asymptotic compactness of the process. On the other hand, since Eq.(
1.1
) contains the function
\varepsilon(t)
and the memory term, we need to build up a time-dependent space and introduce a new variable as done in
[
5
]
, then the solution space becomes more complicated. In order to overcome these difficulties, we apply the contractive function method. Finally we can establish the existence of the time-dependent attractor in strong topological space.

In Section 2, we introduce some notions and preliminaries. In Section3, we show the definition of strong solution to Eq.(
1.1
), and proved the existence and uniqueness of the strong solution by the Galerkin method under assumptions
(\mathbf{H}_{1})
-
(\mathbf{H}_{3})
. In Section4, we obatin the existence of an absorbing set and establish the existence of the strong attractor in time-dependent spaces.

\eta^{t}(x,s)=\int_{0}^{s}u(x,t-r)\,dr,\ s\geq 0,

&\eta^{t}_{t}=-\eta^{t}_{s}+u,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(2.3)

with initial boundary conditions

(2.4)

Additionally, we suppose the existence of two positive constants

3
Existence and uniqueness of strong solutions

We begin by showing the definition of a strong solution to problem (
2.3
)-(
2.4
).

Definition 3.1
.

For any
T>\tau
, let
I=[\tau,T]
, a binary form
z=(u,\eta^{t})\in\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}
is said to be a strong solution to problem (
2.3
)-(
2.4
) in the time interval
I
, with the initial value
z_{\tau}=(u_{\tau},\eta^{\tau})\in\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{1}
, if
z
satisfies

u\in C(I;\mathcal{H}_{t}^{1}),\ \eta^{t}\in C(I;L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H_{2})),

\eta^{t}_{t}+\eta^{t}_{s}\in L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H_{1}))\cap L^{2}(I;L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H_{2})),

and for all
\upsilon\in H_{1},\ \phi\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H_{1})
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\langle u_{t},\upsilon\rangle+\varepsilon(t)\langle\nabla u_{t},\nabla\upsilon\rangle+\langle\nabla u,\nabla\upsilon\rangle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(s)\langle\nabla{\eta^{t}(s),\nabla\upsilon\rangle}ds+\langle f(u),\upsilon\rangle=\langle g,\upsilon\rangle,\\
&\langle\eta^{t}_{t}+\eta^{t}_{s},\varphi\rangle_{\mu,1}=\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{\mu,1},\end{aligned}\right.

hold
a.e.\ t\in I.

We may now state the first important theory in this paper.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that assumptions
(\mathbf{H}_{1})
-
(\mathbf{H}_{3})
hold. For any initial data

is continuous with respect to the initial data. That is, let

)-(

Proof.

Proof of existence of strong solution needs the following three steps.

\mathbf{Step1:}
First we choose the base functions
\{w_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
in
H_{2}
with
w_{j}
being the eigenfunctions of the Laplacian operator subject to the Dirichlet boundary condition

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta w_{j}=\lambda_{j}w_{j},\\
&w_{j}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
satisfy
0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\cdots\leq\lambda_{j}\leq\cdots\cdots,\ \lambda_{j}\to\infty\ as\ j\to\infty.

We also normalize
w_{j}
, then
\{w_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
become an orthogonal basis in
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{1}
. At the same time, we choose an orthogonal basis
\{\zeta_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
of
L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H_{2})
.

Now we use the Faedo-Galerkin method to find the approximate solution. Let
m
be a given positive integer and

u_{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{im}(t)w_{i},\ \eta_{m}^{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{im}(t)\zeta_{i},

which satisfy the following indentities

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{mt}-\varepsilon(t)\Delta{u_{mt}}-\Delta{u_{m}}-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(s)\Delta{\eta^{t}_{m}(s)}ds+f(u_{m})=g_{m}(x),\\
&\eta^{t}_{mt}=-\eta^{t}_{ms}+u_{m},\\
&u_{m}(x,t)|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\ \eta^{t}_{m}(x,s)|_{\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}=0,\ t\geq\tau,\\
&u_{m}(x,\tau)=P_{m}u_{\tau},\ \eta^{\tau}_{m}(x,s)=Q_{m}\eta_{\tau},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

where
P_{m},\ Q_{m}
are projections on subspaces
Span\{w_{1},\cdots,w_{m}\}\subset\mathcal{H}_{t}^{1},\ Span\{\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{m}\}\subset L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H_{2})
respectively and
g_{m}(x)=P_{m}g(x)
.

The fundamental existence theory of Ordinary Differential Equations states that there exists a unique solution
z_{m}=(u_{m},\eta_{m}^{t})
to Eq.(
3.1
) on
[\tau,T]
.

\mathbf{Step2:}
We now try to get the priori estimates for the approximate solution
z_{m}
obtained in the previous step. In order to proceed with the proof, we introduce the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

(
[
15
]
)
Suppose that
(\mathbf{H}_{1})-(\mathbf{H}_{3})
hold, then for any
t\in I
, we have the following estimates

\|u_{m}\|^{2}+\varepsilon(t)\|\nabla u_{m}\|^{2}+\|\eta^{t}_{m}\|^{2}_{\mu,1}+\int_{\tau}^{T}\left(\|\nabla u_{m}(r)\|^{2}+\|\eta^{r}_{m}\|^{2}_{\mu,1}+\|u_{m}(r)\|^{p}_{p}\right)dr\leq\mathcal{K}_{1},

(3.2)

\|\nabla u_{m}\|^{2}+\|u_{m}\|^{p}_{p}\leq\mathcal{K}_{2},

(3.3)

\int_{\tau}^{T}\left(\|u_{mt}(r)\|^{2}+\varepsilon(t)\|\nabla u_{mt}(r)\|^{2}\right)dr\leq\mathcal{K}_{3},

(3.4)

where
\mathcal{K}_{1},\mathcal{K}_{2},\mathcal{K}_{3}
are positive constants only depending on
T
.

In fact, multiplying both sides of equation in (
3.1
) by
-\Delta u_{m}
and integrating it over
\Omega
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left(\|\nabla u_{m}\|^{2}+\varepsilon(t)\|\Delta u_{m}\|^{2}+\|\eta^{t}_{m}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}\right)+(2-\varepsilon^{\prime}(t))\|\Delta u_{m}\|^{2}+2\langle\eta^{t}_{m},\eta^{t}_{ms}\rangle_{\mu,2}

\displaystyle=-2\langle f(u_{m}),-\Delta u_{m}\rangle+2\langle g_{m},-\Delta u_{m}\rangle.

(3.5)

Using (
1.5
) and (
3.3
), we are able to determine that

\displaystyle-2\langle f(u_{m}),-\Delta u_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\langle f^{\prime}(u_{m})\nabla u_{m},\nabla u_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2l\|\nabla u_{m}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2l\mathcal{K}_{2}.

(3.6)

By use of Young’s inequality, we have

2\langle g_{m},-\Delta u_{m}\rangle\leq\|g_{m}\|^{2}+\|\Delta u_{m}\|^{2}.

(3.7)

Substituting (
3
), (
3.7
) into (
3
) and by Lemma
2.9
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left(\|\nabla u_{m}\|^{2}+\varepsilon(t)\|\Delta u_{m}\|^{2}+\|\eta^{t}_{m}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}\right)+(1-\varepsilon^{\prime}(t))\|\Delta u_{m}\|^{2}+\rho\|\eta_{m}^{t}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}

\displaystyle\leq 2l\mathcal{K}_{2}+\|g_{m}\|^{2}.

(3.8)

Since
H_{2}\hookrightarrow H_{1}
, we can write
\widetilde{\lambda}\|\nabla u_{m}\|^{2}\leq\|\Delta u_{m}\|^{2}
, then (
3
) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left(\|\nabla u_{m}\|^{2}+\varepsilon(t)\|\Delta u_{m}\|^{2}+\|\eta^{t}_{m}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}\right)+\frac{\widetilde{\lambda}}{2}\|\nabla u_{m}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u_{m}\|^{2}+\rho\|\eta_{m}^{t}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}

\displaystyle\leq 2l\mathcal{K}_{2}+\|g_{m}\|^{2}.

(3.9)

Set
\alpha=\min\left\{\frac{\widetilde{\lambda}}{2},\frac{1}{2L},\rho\right\}
, we then deduce from (
3
) that

\frac{d}{dt}\|z_{m}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}^{1}_{t}}+\alpha\|z_{m}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}^{1}_{t}}\leq 2l\mathcal{K}_{2}+\|g_{m}\|^{2}.

(3.10)

Applying the
Gronwall
lemma on
[\tau,t]
, we obtain

\|z_{m}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}}\leq e^{-\alpha(t-\tau)}\|z_{m}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{1}}+\mathfrak{C}_{0}.

(3.11)

where
\mathfrak{C}_{0}=\frac{1}{\alpha}(1-e^{-\alpha t})(2l\mathcal{K}_{2}+\|g_{m}\|^{2})
.

Therefore,
\{z_{m}\}_{m\geq 1}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(I;\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1})
, that is,
u_{m}
,
\eta_{m}^{t}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(I;\mathcal{H}_{t}^{1})
,
L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H_{2}))
respectively.

In addition, from assumption
(\mathbf{H}_{1})
and (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left(\|\nabla u_{m}\|^{2}+\varepsilon(t)\|\Delta u_{m}\|^{2}+\|\eta^{t}_{m}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}\right)+\|\Delta u_{m}\|^{2}+\rho\|\eta_{m}^{t}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}

\displaystyle\leq 2l\mathcal{K}_{2}+\|g_{m}\|^{2}.

(3.12)

Integrating from
\tau
to
T
on both sides of (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{T}\left(\|\Delta u_{m}(r)\|^{2}+\rho\|\eta_{m}^{r}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}\right)dr

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{m}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{1}}+(2l\mathcal{K}_{2}+\|g_{m}\|^{2})(T-\tau)

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{m}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{1}}+\mathfrak{C}_{1},

(3.13)

where
\mathfrak{C}_{1}=(2l\mathcal{K}_{2}+\|g_{m}\|^{2})(T-\tau)
.

Therefore,
\Delta u_{m}
,
\eta_{m}^{t}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))
,
L^{2}(I;L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H_{2}))
respectively.

We choose
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
, then in value of (
1.4
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|f(u_{m}(r))|^{q}dxdr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\beta_{2}|u_{m}|^{p-1}+\gamma_{2}\right)^{q}dxdr

\displaystyle\leq C_{q,\beta_{2}}\int_{\tau}^{T}\|u_{m}(r)\|^{p}_{p}dr+C_{q,\gamma_{2}}|\Omega|(T-\tau).

(3.14)

Hence,
f(u_{m})
is uniformly bounded in
L^{q}(I;L^{q}(\Omega))
.

\mathbf{Step3:}
According to the findings in step2, we are aware that there is a subsequence of
z_{m}=(u_{m},\eta_{m}^{t})
, still denoted by
z_{m}
suach that

\displaystyle u_{m}\rightharpoonup u\ weakly\ star\ in\ L^{\infty}(I;\mathcal{H}_{t}^{1}),

\displaystyle u_{m}\rightharpoonup u\ weakly\ in\ L^{2}(I;H_{2}),

\displaystyle u_{mt}\rightharpoonup u_{t}\ weakly\ in\ L^{2}(I;\mathcal{H}_{t}),

\displaystyle\eta_{m}^{t}\rightharpoonup\eta^{t}\ weakly\ star\ in\ L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H_{2})),

\displaystyle\eta_{m}^{t}\rightharpoonup\eta^{t}\ weakly\ in\ L^{2}(I;L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H_{2})),

\displaystyle f(u_{m})\rightharpoonup\mathcal{X}\ weakly\ in\ L^{q}(I;L^{q}(\Omega)).

Taking limit for Eq.(
3.1
), we find that
z=(u,\eta^{t})
is a solution to problem (
2.3
)-(
2.4
) satisfying

z\in L^{\infty}(I;\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}).

By Lemma
2.10
and the compact embedding
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t}
, we are able to acquire
u_{m}\longrightarrow u\in C(I;\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\gamma})(0<\gamma<1)
.
Then Lemma
2.11
allows us to reach the following conclusion

u\in C(I;\mathcal{H}_{t}^{1})\ for\ all\ t>\tau.

Indeed, it follows from the continuity of
f
that
f(u_{m})\longrightarrow f(u)
, a.e. in
\Omega\times I
, then we know
\mathcal{X}=f(u)
.

Finally, the continuity of
\eta^{t}
can be proved by the same method as in
[
21
]
, that is,
\eta^{t}\in C(I;L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H_{2}))
.

So far, we complete the proof of the existence of strong solution.

Proof of uniqueness of strong solution is the following.

Indeed, let
z_{i}(\tau)\in\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{1}(i=1,2)
be two initial values and
z_{i}(t)
be the corresponding solution to problem (
2.3
)-(
2.4
). We define
\bar{z}(t)=z_{1}(t)-z_{2}(t)=(u_{1}-u_{2},\eta^{t}_{1}-\eta^{t}_{2})=(\bar{u},\bar{\eta}^{t})
. Obviously, the following equation can be obtained

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\bar{u}_{t}-\varepsilon(t)\Delta{\bar{u}_{t}}-\Delta{\bar{u}}-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(s)\Delta{\bar{\eta}^{t}(s)}ds+f(u_{1})-f(u_{2})=0,\\
&\bar{\eta}^{t}_{t}=-\bar{\eta}^{t}_{s}+\bar{u},\\
&\bar{u}(x,t)|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\ \bar{\eta}^{t}(x,s)|_{\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}=0,\ t\geq\tau,\\
&\bar{u}(x,\tau)=u_{1}(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau),\ \bar{\eta}^{\tau}(x,s)=\eta_{1}^{\tau}-\eta_{2}^{\tau},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.15)

Multiplying both sides of equation in (
3.15
) by
-\Delta\bar{u}
and integrating it from
\Omega
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left(\|\nabla\bar{u}\|^{2}+\varepsilon(t)\|\Delta\bar{u}\|^{2}+\|\bar{\eta}^{t}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}\right)+(2-\varepsilon^{\prime}(t))\|\Delta\bar{u}\|^{2}+\rho\|\bar{\eta}^{t}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}

\displaystyle\leq-2\langle f(u_{1})-f(u_{2}),-\Delta\bar{u}\rangle.

(3.16)

In light of (
1.5
), we know

\displaystyle-2\langle f(u_{1})-f(u_{2}),-\Delta\bar{u}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-2\langle f^{\prime}(\theta u_{1}+(1-\theta)u_{2})\bar{u},-\Delta\bar{u}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2l\|\nabla\bar{u}\|^{2}.

(3.17)

Using
\varepsilon^{\prime}(t)<0
and (
3
), (
3
) can be written as

\frac{d}{dt}\|\bar{z}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}}\leq 2l\|\nabla\bar{u}\|^{2}\leq 2l\|\bar{z}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}}.

Applying the
Gronwall
lemma on
[\tau,t]
, it’s possible to draw the conclusion

\|\bar{z}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}}\leq e^{2l(t-\tau)}\|\bar{z}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{1}},

which proves the uniqueness of strong solutions.

Thus, we complete the proof of Theorem
3.2

∎

4
Time-dependent global attractors for strong solutions

According to Theroy
3.2
, we can define a continuous process
\{U(t,\tau)\}_{t\geq\tau}
generated by Eqs.(
2.3
)-(
2.4
) as

U(t,\tau):\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1},\ t\geq\tau\in\mathbb{R},

which acts as

U(t,\tau)z_{\tau}=U(t,\tau)(u_{\tau},\eta^{\tau})=z(t)=(u,\eta^{t}).

4.1
Absorbing sets in
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
(\mathbf{H}_{1})-(\mathbf{H}_{3})
hold. If
z(t)
is a strong solution to Eqs.(
2.3
)-(
2.4
) with initial value
z_{\tau}\in\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{1}
, then there exist a constant
Q\geq 0
such that

\|U(t,\tau)z_{\tau}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}}\leq e^{-\alpha(t-\tau)}\|z_{\tau}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{1}}+Q,\ \forall\ t\geq\tau.

Proof.

Multiplying both sides of equation in (
2.3
) by
-\Delta u
and repeating the arguments used in the proof of (
3.11
), we easily get the result.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
(\mathbf{H}_{1})-(\mathbf{H}_{3})
hold,
z(t)
is a strong solution to Eqs.(
2.3
)-(
2.4
) with initial value
z_{\tau}\in\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{1}
. There exists a constant
R_{0}>0
, such that the family
\mathscr{B}=\{\mathbb{B}_{t}(R_{0})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a time-dependent absorbing set for the process
U(t,\tau)
.

Proof.

Let
R_{0}^{2}=2Q+1
, for any initial value
z_{\tau}\in\mathbb{B}_{\tau}(R)
, it follows from Lemma
4.1
that

\|U(t,\tau)z_{\tau}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}}\leq e^{-\alpha(t-\tau)}R^{2}+Q.

Choosing
t_{0}=max\{0,\frac{1}{\alpha}\ln\frac{R^{2}}{1+Q}\}
, then for all
t\geq t_{0}+\tau
, we have

\|U(t,\tau)z_{\tau}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}}\leq 1+Q+Q=2Q+1=R_{0}^{2}.

(4.1)

Letting
\mathscr{B}=\{\mathbb{B}_{t}(R_{0})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, then

U(t,\tau)\mathbb{B}_{\tau}(R)\subset\mathbb{B}_{t}(R_{0}),\ \forall t-\tau\geq t_{0}.

∎

4.2
Time-dependent attractors

Based on the above discussion, we give the most important conclusion of this article.

Theorem 4.3
.

Under the same assumptions as in Lemma
4.2
, the process
\{U(t,\tau)\}_{t\geq\tau}
generated by Eqs.(
2.3
)-(
2.4
) is pullback asymptotic compact in
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}
.

Proof.

Let
z_{m}=(u_{m},\eta_{m}^{t}),\ z_{n}=(u_{n},\eta_{n}^{t})
be the corresponding two solutions to Eqs.(
2.3
)-(
2.4
) with initial data
z_{m}(\tau),\ z_{n}(\tau)
, set
\hat{z}=z_{m}-z_{n}=(u_{m}-u_{n},\eta_{m}^{t}-\eta_{n}^{t})=(\kappa,\zeta^{t})
, then we infer from Eqs.(
2.3
)-(
2.4
) that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\kappa_{t}-\varepsilon(t)\Delta{\kappa_{t}}-\Delta{\kappa}-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(s)\Delta{\zeta^{t}(s)}ds+f(u_{m})-f(u_{n})=0,\\
&\zeta^{t}_{t}=-\zeta^{t}_{s}+\kappa,\\
&\kappa(x,t)|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\ \zeta^{t}(x,s)|_{\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}=0,\ t\geq\tau,\\
&\kappa(x,\tau)=u_{m}(\tau)-u_{n}(\tau),\ \zeta^{\tau}(x,s)=\eta_{m}^{\tau}-\eta_{n}^{\tau}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.2)

Multiplying both sides of equation in (
4.2
) by
-\Delta\kappa
and integrating it from
\Omega
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left(\|\nabla\kappa\|^{2}+\varepsilon(t)\|\Delta\kappa\|^{2}+\|\zeta^{t}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}\right)+(2-\varepsilon^{\prime}(t))\|\Delta\kappa\|^{2}+\rho\|\zeta^{t}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}

\displaystyle\leq-2\langle f(u_{m})-f(u_{n}),-\Delta\kappa\rangle.

(4.3)

In view of (
1.5
), we know

\displaystyle-2\langle f(u_{m})-f(u_{n}),-\Delta\kappa\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-2\langle f^{\prime}(\theta u_{m}+(1-\theta)u_{n})\kappa,-\Delta\kappa\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2l\|\nabla\kappa\|^{2}.

(4.4)

Due to the embedding
H_{2}\hookrightarrow H_{1}
, we can write
\widetilde{\lambda}\|\nabla\kappa\|^{2}\leq\|\Delta\kappa\|^{2}
, then (
4.2
) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left(\|\nabla\kappa\|^{2}+\varepsilon(t)\|\Delta\kappa\|^{2}+\|\zeta^{t}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}\right)+\widetilde{\lambda}\|\nabla\kappa\|^{2}+\|\Delta\kappa\|^{2}+\rho\|\zeta_{m}^{t}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}

\displaystyle\leq 2l\|\nabla\kappa\|^{2}.

(4.5)

Setting
\alpha=\min\left\{\widetilde{\lambda},\frac{1}{L},\rho\right\}
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\|\hat{z}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}^{1}_{t}}+\alpha\|\hat{z}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}^{1}_{t}}\leq 2l\|\nabla\kappa\|^{2}.

(4.6)

Applying the
Gronwall
lemma on
[\tau,t]
, we obtain

\|\hat{z}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}}\leq e^{-\alpha(t-\tau)}\|\hat{z}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{1}}+2le^{-\alpha t}\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{\alpha r}\|\nabla\kappa(r)\|^{2}dr,

(4.7)

\|U(t,\tau)z_{m}(\tau)-U(t,\tau)z_{n}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}}\leq e^{-\alpha(t-\tau)}\|z_{m}(\tau)-z_{n}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{1}}+2l\int_{\tau}^{T}\|\nabla u_{m}(r)-\nabla u_{n}(r)\|^{2}dr.

(4.8)

On one hand, we set
\varPsi_{\tau}^{t}(z_{m},z_{n})=2l\int_{\tau}^{T}\|\nabla u_{m}(r)-\nabla u_{n}(r)\|^{2}dr
,
due to the compact embedding
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t}
, the boundness of
u_{n}
in
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{1}
and
u_{n}\in C(I;\mathcal{H}_{t}^{1})
, we easily know there is a subsequence
u_{nk}
of
u_{n}
, which is convergent in
\mathcal{H}_{t}
, that is,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{l\to\infty}\int_{\tau}^{T}\|u_{nk}(r)-u_{nl}(r)\|^{2}_{1}dr=0.

Thus,
\varPsi_{\tau}^{t}(z_{m},z_{n})
is a contrative function.

On the other hand, for any
\epsilon>0
, and any fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
, set
\tau_{0}=t-\frac{1}{\alpha}\ln\frac{\|z_{m}(\tau)-z_{n}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{1}}}{\epsilon^{2}}
, we are able to show without much difficulty that the process
\{U(t,\tau)\}_{t\geq\tau}
is pullback asymptotically compact in
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}
based on Lemma
2.13
.

Finally, combining Lemma
2.12
and Lemma
4.2
, we know that the process
\{U(t,\tau)\}_{t\geq\tau}
has a time-dependent global attractor for strong solutions in
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{1}
.
∎

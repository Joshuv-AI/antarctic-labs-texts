APPROXIMATION BY DOUBLE SECOND TYPE DELAYED ARITHMETIC MEAN OF PERIODIC FUNCTIONS IN H P ( ω , ω ) H_{P}^{(\omega,\omega)} SPACE
Xh. Z. Krasniqi, P. Kórus, B. Szal

Abstract
. In this paper, we give a degree of approximation of a function in the space H p ( ω , ω ) H_{p}^{(\omega,\omega)} by using the second type double delayed arithmetic means of its Fourier series. Such degree of approximation is expressed via two functions of moduli of continuity type. To obtain one more general result, we used the even-type double delayed arithmetic means of Fourier series as well.

40G05; 41A25; 42A10.

1.
Concise historical background and motivation

On one hand, the approximation of
2\pi
-periodic and integrable functions by their Fourier series in the Hölder metric has been studied widely and consistently in many papers. Das at al. studied the degree of approximation of functions by matrix means of their Fourier series in the generalized Hölder metric
[
1
]
, generalizing many previous known results. Again, Das at al.
[
2
]
studied the rate of convergence problem of Fourier series in a new Banach space of functions conceived as a generalization of the spaces introduced by Prössdorf
[
18
]
and Leindler
[
10
]
. Afterwards, Nayak at al.
[
13
]
studied the rate of convergence problem of the Fourier series by delayed arithmetic mean in the generalized Hölder metric space which was earlier introduced in
[
2
]
and obtaining a sharper estimate of Jackson’s order which is the main objective of their result. Moreover, Deǧer
[
3
]
determined the degree of approximation of functions by matrix means of their Fourier series in the same space of functions introduced in
[
2
]
. In particular, he extended some results of Leindler
[
11
]
and some other results by weakening the monotonicity conditions in the results obtained by Singh and Sonker
[
16
]
for some classes of numerical sequences introduced by Mohapatra and Szal
[
12
]
. Leindler’s results obtained in
[
11
]
, are generalized in
[
8
]
by first author of the present paper, for functions from a Banach space, mainly using the generalized Nörlund and Riesz means. Very recently, Kim
[
6
]
presented a generalized result of a particular case of a result obtained previously in
[
13
]
. In Kim’s result is treated the degree of approximation of functions in the same generalized Hölder metric, but using the so-called even-type delayed arithmetic mean of Fourier series.

On the other hand, results on approximation of bivariate integrable functions and
2\pi
-periodic in each variable by their double Fourier series in the Hölder metric, the interested reader can find in
[
4
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
14
]
. In all results reported in these papers, we came across to the degree of approximation of functions by various means of their double Fourier series and in which the quantity of the form
\mathcal{O}{(\log n)}
appears. Involving such quantity produces a degree of approximation which is not of Jackson’s order. This weakness motivated us to consider some means of double Fourier series which will overshoots it. Whence, we are going to investigate the degree of approximation of bivariate integrable functions and
2\pi
-periodic in each variable by their double Fourier series in the generalized Hölder metric, by motivation of removing the quantities of the form
\mathcal{O}{(\log n)}
and obtaining the degree of approximation of Jackson’s order, which is the aim of the paper.

2.
Introduction and preliminaries

By
L_{p}(T^{2})
,
p\geq 1
, we denote the space of all functions
f(x,y)

integrable with
p
-power on
T^{2}:=(0,2\pi)\times(0,2\pi)
, and with norm

\|f\|_{p}:=\left(\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(x,y)|^{p}dxdy\right)^{1/p}.

If
f\in L_{p}(T^{2})
,
p\geq 1
, then
\omega_{i}
,
(i=1,2)
are considered as
moduli of continuity if
\omega_{i}
are two positive non-decreasing
continuous functions on
[0,2\pi]
with properties

(i)

\omega_{i}(0)=0
,

(ii)

\omega_{i}(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})\leq\omega_{i}(\delta_{1})+\omega_{i}(\delta_{2})
,

(iii)

\omega_{i}(\lambda\delta)\leq(\lambda+1)\omega_{i}(\delta)
,
\lambda\geq 0
.

We define the space
H_{p}^{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})}
by

H_{p}^{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})}:=\left\{f\in L^{p}(T^{2}),p\geq 1:A(f;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})<\infty\right\},

where

A(f;\omega_{1},\omega_{2}):=\sup_{t_{1}\neq 0,\,\,t_{2}\neq 0}\frac{\|f(x+t_{1},y+t_{2})-f(x,y)\|_{p}}{\omega_{1}(|t_{1}|)+\omega_{2}(|t_{2}|)}

and the norm in the space
H_{p}^{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})}
is defined by

\|f\|_{p}^{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})}:=\|f\|_{p}+A(f;\omega_{1},\omega_{2}).

If
\omega_{1}
,
\omega_{2}
,
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are moduli of continuity so that
the two-variable function
\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{1})+\omega_{2}(t_{2})}{v_{1}(t_{1})+v_{2}(t_{2})}
has a maximum
M
on
T^{2}
, then it is easy to see that

\|f\|_{p}^{(v_{1},v_{2})}\leq\max\left(1,M\right)\|f\|_{p}^{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})},

which shows that in this case, for the given spaces
H_{p}^{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})}
and
H_{p}^{(v_{1},v_{2})}
we have

H_{p}^{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})}\subseteq H_{p}^{(v_{1},v_{2})}\subseteq L_{p}\quad(p\geq 1).

We write

\Omega_{p}(\delta_{1},\delta_{2};f):=\sup_{0\leq h_{1}\leq\delta_{1};0\leq h_{2}\leq\delta_{2}}\|f(x+h_{1},y+h_{2})-f(x,y)\|_{p}

for the integral modulus of continuity of
f(x,y)
, and whenever

\|f(x+t_{1},y+t_{2})-f(x,y)\|_{p}=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega_{1}(|t_{1}|)+\omega_{2}(|t_{2}|)\right)

we write
f\in\text{Lip}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},p)
, that is

\text{Lip}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},p)=\left\{f\in L_{p}(T^{2}):\|f(x+t_{1},y+t_{2})-f(x,y)\|_{p}=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega_{1}(|t_{1}|)+\omega_{2}(|t_{2}|)\right)\right\}.

Clearly, for
\omega_{1}(t_{1})=\mathcal{O}\left(t_{1}^{\alpha}\right)
and
\omega_{2}(t_{2})=\mathcal{O}\left(t_{2}^{\beta}\right)
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
,
0<\beta\leq 1
, the class
\text{Lip}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},p)
reduces to the
class
\text{Lip}(\alpha,\beta,p)
, that is

\text{Lip}(\alpha,\beta,p)=\left\{f\in L^{p}(T^{2}):\|f(x+t_{1},y+t_{2})-f(x,y)\|_{p}=\mathcal{O}\left(t_{1}^{\alpha}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(t_{2}^{\beta}\right)\right\}.

Then for
1\geq\alpha\geq\gamma\geq 0
and
1\geq\beta\geq\delta\geq 0
, by noting
\frac{t_{1}^{\alpha}+t_{2}^{\beta}}{t_{1}^{\gamma}+t_{2}^{\delta}}
is bounded on
T^{2}
, we have

\text{Lip}(\alpha,\beta,p)\subseteq\text{Lip}(\gamma,\delta,p)\subseteq L_{p}\quad(p\geq 1).

If
f(x,y)
is a continuous function periodic in both variables with period
2\pi
and
p=\infty
, then the class
\text{Lip}(\alpha,\beta,p)
reduces to
the Hölder class
\text{H}_{(\alpha,\beta)}
(also called Lipschitz
class), that is

\text{H}_{(\alpha,\beta)}=\left\{f:|f(x+t_{1},y+t_{2})-f(x,y)|=\mathcal{O}\left(t_{1}^{\alpha}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(t_{2}^{\beta}\right)\right\}.

It is verified that
\text{H}_{(\alpha,\beta)}
is a Banach space (see
[
4
]
) with the norm
\|f\|_{\alpha,\beta}
defined by

\|f\|_{\alpha,\beta}=\|f\|_{C}+\sup_{t_{1}\neq 0,\,\,t_{2}\neq 0}\frac{|f(x+t_{1},y+t_{2})-f(x,y)|}{|t_{1}|^{\alpha}+|t_{2}|^{\beta}},

where

\|f\|_{C}=\sup_{(x,y)\in T^{2}}|f(x,y)|.

Let
f(x,y)\in L^{p}(T^{2})
be a
2\pi
-periodic function with respect to each
variable, with its Fourier series

\displaystyle f(x,y)\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{k,\ell}(a_{k,\ell}\cos kx\cos\ell y+b_{k,\ell}\sin kx\cos\ell y

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+c_{k,\ell}\cos kx\sin\ell y+d_{k,\ell}\sin kx\sin\ell y)

at the point
(x,y)
, where

\displaystyle\lambda_{k,\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\frac{1}{4},&\mbox{if}&k=\ell=0,\\
\frac{1}{2},&\mbox{if}&k=0,\,\ell>0;\,\,\,\mbox{or}\,\,\,k>0,\,\ell=0,\\
1,&\mbox{if}&k,\,\ell>0;\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle a_{k,\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(u,v)\cos ku\cos\ell v\,dudv,

\displaystyle b_{k,\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(u,v)\sin ku\cos\ell v\,dudv,

\displaystyle c_{k,\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(u,v)\cos ku\sin\ell v\,dudv,

\displaystyle d_{k,\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(u,v)\sin ku\sin\ell v\,dudv,\quad k,\,\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},

and whose partial sums are

\displaystyle S_{n,m}(f;x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\lambda_{k,\ell}(a_{k,\ell}\cos kx\cos\ell y+b_{k,\ell}\sin kx\cos\ell y

\displaystyle\hskip 108.12054pt+c_{k,\ell}\cos kx\sin\ell y+d_{k,\ell}\sin kx\sin\ell y),\quad n,m\geq 0.

To reveal our intention, we recall some other notations and notions.

Let
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}u_{k,\ell}
be an infinite
double series with its sequence of arithmetic mean
\{\sigma_{m,n}\}
, where

\sigma_{m,n}=\frac{1}{(m+1)(n+1)}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}U_{k,\ell}

and
U_{k,\ell}:=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}u_{i,j}.

We define the
Double Delayed Arithmetic Mean

\sigma_{m,k;n,\ell}

by (see
[
5
]
):

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{m,k;n,\ell}:=&\left(1+\frac{m}{k}\right)\left(1+\frac{n}{\ell}\right)\sigma_{m+k-1,n+\ell-1}-\left(1+\frac{m}{k}\right)\frac{n}{\ell}\sigma_{m+k-1,n-1}\\
&-\frac{m}{k}\left(1+\frac{n}{\ell}\right)\sigma_{m-1,n+\ell-1}+\frac{mn}{k\ell}\sigma_{m-1,n-1},\end{split}

where
k
and
\ell
are positive integers.

If
k
tends to
\infty
with
m
in such a way that
\frac{m}{k}
is
bounded, and
\ell
tends to
\infty
with
n
in such a way that
\frac{n}{\ell}
is also bounded, then
\sigma_{m,k;n,\ell}
defines a method of
summability which is at least as strong as the well-known
(C,1,1)

summablity. This means that if
\sigma_{m,n}\to\mu
, then
\sigma_{m,k;n,\ell}\to\mu
as well. This important fact follows from (
2.1
) if we set
\sigma_{m,n}=\mu+\xi_{m,n}
, where
\xi_{m,n}\to 0
as
m,n\to\infty
. Introducing this mean we expect to be useful in
applications, particularly in approximating of
2\pi
periodic functions in
two variables.

We note that for
k=\ell=1
we obtain
\sigma_{m,1;n,1}=U_{m,n}
, while for
m=n=0
we get
\sigma_{0,k;0,\ell}=\sigma_{k-1,\ell-1}
. Moreover, for
k=m

and
\ell=n
, we get

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sigma_{m,m;n,n}=4\sigma_{2m-1,2n-1}-2\sigma_{2m-1,n-1}-2\sigma_{m-1,2n-1}+\sigma_{m-1,n-1}

that we name as the
first type
Double Delayed Arithmetic Mean.

However, in this research paper we will take
k=2m
and
\ell=2n
in the
Double Delayed Arithmetic Mean
\sigma_{m,k;n,\ell}
to obtain

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sigma_{m,2m;n,2n}=\frac{1}{4}\left(9\sigma_{3m-1,3n-1}-3\sigma_{3m-1,n-1}-3\sigma_{m-1,3n-1}+\sigma_{m-1,n-1}\right).

We name these particular sums as the
second type
Double Delayed
Arithmetic Mean.

By
\sigma_{m,n}(f;x,y)
and
\sigma_{m,2m;n,2n}(f;x,y)
we denote the
arithmetic mean and the second type Double Delayed Arithmetic Mean for
S_{k,\ell}(f;x,y)
, respectively. It is well-known (see e.g.
[
7

F_{m,n}(t_{1},t_{2}):=\frac{4}{(m+1)(n+1)}\left(\frac{\sin\frac{(m+1)t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{(n+1)t_{2}}{2}}{4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}}\right)^{2},

which we will use it in its equivalent form

(2.4)

F_{m,n}(t_{1},t_{2}):=\frac{1}{(m+1)(n+1)}\frac{(1-\cos(m+1)t_{1})(1-\cos(n+1)t_{2})}{\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}},

while the arithmetic mean
\sigma_{m,n}(f;x,y)
is

(2.5)

\sigma_{m,n}(f;x,y)=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}h_{x,y}(t_{1},t_{2})F_{m,n}(t_{1},t_{2})\,dt_{1}dt_{2},

where

Furthermore, using (
2.4
) successively, we have

\displaystyle F_{3m-1,3n-1}(t_{1},t_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\cos(3mt_{1}))(1-\cos(3nt_{2}))}{9mn\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}},

\displaystyle F_{3m-1,n-1}(t_{1},t_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\cos(3mt_{1}))(1-\cos(nt_{2}))}{3mn\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}},

\displaystyle F_{m-1,3n-1}(t_{1},t_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\cos(mt_{1}))(1-\cos(3nt_{2}))}{3mn\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}},

\displaystyle F_{m-1,n-1}(t_{1},t_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\cos(mt_{1}))(1-\cos(nt_{2}))}{mn\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}.

Thus, we can write

(2.6)

\begin{split}F_{m,2m;n,2n}(t_{1},t_{2}):=&\frac{1}{4}\left(9F_{3m-1,3n-1}(t_{1},t_{2})-3F_{3m-1,n-1}(t_{1},t_{2})\right.\\
&\quad\left.-3F_{m-1,3n-1}(t_{1},t_{2})+F_{m-1,n-1}(t_{1},t_{2})\right)\\
=&\frac{1}{4mn\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}\left((1-\cos(3mt_{1}))(1-\cos(3nt_{2}))\right.\\

where

Therefore, using (

\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})-H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})-H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})+H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})\|_{p}=\mathcal{O}\left((v_{1}(|z_{1}|)+(v_{1}(|z_{2}|))\left(\frac{\omega_{1}(h_{1})}{v_{1}(h_{1})}+\frac{\omega_{1}(h_{2})}{v_{1}(h_{2})}\right)\right)
.

Proof.

Part (i). We calculate as

\begin{split}\|\phi_{x,y}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}&\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\{\|f(x+t_{1},y+t_{2})-f(x,y)\|_{p}+\|f(x,y)-f(x-t_{1},y+t_{2})\|_{p}\right.\\
&\quad+\left.\|f(x,y)-f(x+t_{1},y-t_{2})\|_{p}+\|f(x,y)-f(x-t_{1},y-t_{2})\|_{p}\right\}\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega_{1}(t_{1})+\omega_{2}(t_{2})\right).\end{split}

Part (ii). Since
f\in\text{Lip}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},p)
, we have

\begin{split}\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}&=\|\phi_{x+z_{1},y+z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})-\phi_{x,y}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\{\|f(x+z_{1}+t_{1},y+z_{2}+t_{2})-f(x+z_{1},y+z_{2})\|_{p}\right.\\
&\quad+\|f(x+z_{1}-t_{1},y+z_{2}+t_{2})-f(x+z_{1},y+z_{2})\|_{p}\\
&\quad+\|f(x+z_{1}+t_{1},y+z_{2}-t_{2})-f(x+z_{1},y+z_{2})\|_{p}\\
&\quad+\|f(x+z_{1}-t_{1},y+z_{2}-t_{2})-f(x+z_{1},y+z_{2})\|_{p}\\
&\quad+\|f(x+t_{1},y+t_{2})-f(x,y)\|_{p}\\
&\quad+\|f(x-t_{1},y+t_{2})-f(x,y)\|_{p}\\
&\quad+\|f(x+t_{1},y-t_{2})-f(x,y)\|_{p}\\
&\quad+\left.\|f(x-t_{1},y-t_{2})-f(x,y)\|_{p}\right\}\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega_{1}(t_{1})+\omega_{2}(t_{2})\right).\end{split}

For the second part, a similar reasoning yields

(3.1)

\begin{split}\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}&=\|\phi_{x+z_{1},y+z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})-\phi_{x,y}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\{\|f(x+z_{1}+t_{1},y+z_{2}+t_{2})-f(x+t_{1},y+t_{2})\|_{p}\right.\\
&\quad+\|f(x+z_{1}-t_{1},y+z_{1}+t_{2})-f(x-t_{1},y+t_{2})\|_{p}\\
&\quad+\|f(x+z_{1}+t_{1},y+z_{2}-t_{2})-f(x+t_{1},y-t_{2})\|_{p}\\
&\quad+\left.\|f(x+z_{1}-t_{1},y+z_{2}-t_{2})-f(x-t_{1},y-t_{2})\|_{p}\right\}\\
&\quad+\|f(x+z_{1},y+z_{2})-f(x,y)\|_{p}\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega_{1}(|z_{1}|)+\omega_{2}(|z_{2}|)\right).\end{split}

The other relations can be verified in a very same way. We omit their
proofs.

Part (iii). Using part (ii) and the fact
v_{1}(t_{1})
is non-decreasing, in
case of
t_{1}\leq|z_{1}|
and
t_{2}\leq|z_{2}|
, we get

\begin{split}\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}&=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega_{1}(t_{1})+\omega_{1}(t_{2})\right)\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(v_{1}(t_{1})\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{1})}{v_{1}(t_{1})}+v_{1}(t_{2})\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{2})}{v_{1}(t_{2})}\right)\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(v_{1}(|z_{1}|)\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{1})}{v_{1}(t_{1})}+v_{1}(|z_{2}|)\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{2})}{v_{1}(t_{2})}\right).\end{split}

Since
\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{1})}{v_{1}(t_{1})}
is non-decreasing, and we have the
second part of (ii), then for
t_{1}\geq|z_{1}|
and
t_{2}\geq|z_{2}|
, we also
obtain

\begin{split}\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}&=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega_{1}(|z_{1}|)+\omega_{1}(|z_{2}|)\right)\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(v_{1}(|z_{1}|)\frac{\omega_{1}(|z_{1}|)}{v_{1}(|z_{1}|)}+v_{1}(|z_{2}|)\frac{\omega_{1}(|z_{2}|)}{v_{1}(|z_{2}|)}\right)\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(v_{1}(|z_{1}|)\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{1})}{v_{1}(t_{1})}+v_{1}(|z_{2}|)\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{2})}{v_{1}(t_{2})}\right).\end{split}

For
t_{1}\leq|z_{1}|
and
t_{2}\geq|z_{2}|
, consider two possibilities. If
|z_{1}|\geq t_{2}
, then

\begin{split}\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}&=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega_{1}(t_{1})+\omega_{1}(t_{2})\right)\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(v_{1}(t_{1})\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{1})}{v_{1}(t_{1})}+v_{1}(t_{2})\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{2})}{v_{1}(t_{2})}\right)\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(v_{1}(|z_{1}|)\left(\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{1})}{v_{1}(t_{1})}+\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{2})}{v_{1}(t_{2})}\right)\right).\end{split}

If
t_{2}\geq|z_{1}|
, then

\begin{split}\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}&=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega_{1}(|z_{1}|)+\omega_{1}(|z_{2}|)\right)\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(v_{1}(|z_{1}|)\frac{\omega_{1}(|z_{1}|)}{v_{1}(|z_{1}|)}+v_{1}(|z_{2}|)\frac{\omega_{1}(|z_{2}|)}{v_{1}(|z_{2}|)}\right)\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left((v_{1}(|z_{1}|)+v_{1}(|z_{2}|))\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{2})}{v_{1}(t_{2})}\right).\end{split}

For
t_{1}\geq|z_{1}|
and
t_{2}\leq|z_{2}|
, we can get

\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}=\mathcal{O}\left(v_{1}(|z_{2}|)\left(\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{1})}{v_{1}(t_{1})}+\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{2})}{v_{1}(t_{2})}\right)\right)

in case of

, and

\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}=\mathcal{O}\left((v_{1}(|z_{1}|)+v_{1}(|z_{2}|))\frac{\omega_{1}(t_{1})}{v_{1}(t_{1})}\right)

in case of

Part (iv). We have

\begin{split}&\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})-H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}\\
&\leq\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}+\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}\\

and

\begin{split}&\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})-H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})\|_{p}\\
&\leq\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}+\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})\|_{p}\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega_{1}(|z_{1}|)+\omega_{2}(|z_{2}|)\right),\end{split}

furthermore

\begin{split}\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})-&H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})-H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})\\
&\quad+H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})\|_{p}\\
&\leq\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})-H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}\\
&\quad+\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})-H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})\|_{p}\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega_{1}(h_{1})+\omega_{1}(h_{2})\right)\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})&-H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})-H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})\\

&\leq\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}+\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}\\
&\quad+\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})\|_{p}+\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})\|_{p}\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega_{1}(|z_{1}|)+\omega_{2}(|z_{2}|)\right).\end{split}

From analogous estimations, we can obtain part (iv) by considering the four

;

4.
Main Results

We prove the following statement.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\omega
and
v
be moduli of continuity so that
\frac{\omega(t)}{v(t)}
is non-decreasing in
t
. If
f\in H_{p}^{(\omega,\omega)}
,
p\geq 1
, then

\|\sigma_{m,2m;n,2n}(f)-f\|_{p}^{(v,v)}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\omega(h_{1})}{v(h_{1})}+\frac{\omega(h_{2})}{v(h_{2})}\right),

where
h_{1}=\frac{\pi}{m}
,
h_{2}=\frac{\pi}{n}
for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Using the equality

(4.1)

\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{\sin 2mt_{1}\sin mt_{1}\sin 2nt_{2}\sin nt_{2}}{\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}=\frac{mn\pi^{2}}{4}

we have

(4.2)

\begin{split}D_{m,n}(x,y)&=\sigma_{m,2m;n,2n}(f;x,y)-f(x,y)\\
&=\frac{4}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\phi_{x,y}(t_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}\phi_{x,y}(t_{1},t_{2})=&\frac{1}{4}\big[f(x+t_{1},y+t_{2})+f(x-t_{1},y+t_{2})\\
&\quad+f(x+t_{1},y-t_{2})+f(x-t_{1},y-t_{2})-4f(x,y)\big]\end{split}

and

S(t_{1},t_{2})=\sin 2mt_{1}\sin mt_{1}\sin 2nt_{2}\sin nt_{2}.

By definition, we have

(4.3)

\|D_{m,n}\|_{p}^{(v,v)}:=\|D_{m,n}\|_{p}+\sup_{z_{1}\neq 0,\,\,z_{2}\neq 0}\frac{\|D_{m,n}(x+z_{1},y+z_{2})-D_{m,n}(x,y)\|_{p}}{v(|z_{1}|)+v(|z_{2}|)}.

Now, we can write

(4.4)

\begin{split}&D_{m,n}(x+z_{1},y+z_{2})-D_{m,n}(x,y)\\
&=\frac{4}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left[\phi_{x+z_{1},y+z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})-\phi_{x,y}(t_{1},t_{2})\right]\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&=\frac{4}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&=\frac{4}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{4}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{h_{1}}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{4}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{h_{1}}\int_{h_{2}}^{\pi}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{4}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{h_{1}}^{\pi}\int_{h_{2}}^{\pi}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}:=\sum_{r=1}^{4}J_{r},\end{split}

Using Lemma
3.1
for
p\geq 1
, by the estimate

\left|\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}\right|=\mathcal{O}\left(m^{2}n^{2}\right),\quad 0<t_{1}\leq\pi,0<t_{2}\leq\pi,

and Lemma
3.2
, we have

(4.5)

\begin{split}\|J_{1}\|_{p}&\leq\frac{4}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}\left|\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}\right|dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\left(v(|z_{1}|)+v(|z_{2}|)\right)mn\int_{0}^{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}\left(\frac{\omega(t_{1})}{v(t_{1})}+\frac{\omega(t_{2})}{v(t_{2})}\right)dt_{1}dt_{2}\right)\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\left(v(|z_{1}|)+v(|z_{2}|)\right)mn\left(\frac{\omega(h_{1})}{v(h_{1})}+\frac{\omega(h_{2})}{v(h_{2})}\right)\int_{0}^{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\right)\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\left(v(|z_{1}|)+v(|z_{2}|)\right)\left(\frac{\omega(h_{1})}{v(h_{1})}+\frac{\omega(h_{2})}{v(h_{2})}\right)\right).\end{split}

The quantity
J_{2}
can be written as follows:

(4.6)

\begin{split}J_{2}&=\frac{4}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{h_{1}}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\sin 2mt_{1}\sin mt_{1}\\
&\quad\times\frac{\sin 2nt_{2}\sin nt_{2}}{\left(2\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\left(2\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\right)^{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{1}^{2}}\right)dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{4}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{h_{1}}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{t_{1}^{2}\left(2\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}:=J_{21}+J_{22}.\end{split}

Since the function

\frac{1}{\left(2\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\right)^{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{1}^{2}}

is bounded for
0<t_{1}\leq\pi
and

\left|\frac{\sin 2nt_{2}\sin nt_{2}}{\left(2\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}\right|=\mathcal{O}\left(n^{2}\right),\quad 0<t_{2}\leq\pi,

then by Lemma
3.1
with
p\geq 1
and Lemma
3.2
, we get

(4.7)

\begin{split}\|J_{21}\|_{p}&=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)\int_{h_{1}}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}\|H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{p}dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\left(v(|z_{1}|)+v(|z_{2}|)\right)\frac{n}{m}\int_{h_{1}}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}\left(\frac{\omega(t_{1})}{v(t_{1})}+\frac{\omega(t_{2})}{v(t_{2})}\right)dt_{1}dt_{2}\right)\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\left(v(|z_{1}|)+v(|z_{2}|)\right)\frac{n}{m}\left(\frac{\omega(\pi)}{v(\pi)}+\frac{\omega(h_{2})}{v(h_{2})}\right)\int_{h_{1}}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\right)\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{m}\left(v(|z_{1}|)+v(|z_{2}|)\right)\left(\frac{\omega(\pi)}{v(\pi)}+\frac{\omega(h_{2})}{v(h_{2})}\right)\right).\end{split}

It is clear that for
m\in\mathbb{N}

\frac{1}{m}\omega\left(\pi\right)\leq 2\omega\left(\frac{\pi}{m}\right),

whence

\left\|J_{21}\right\|_{p}=O\left(\left(v(|z_{1}|)+v(|z_{2}|)\right)\left(\frac{\omega(h_{1})}{v(h_{1})}+\frac{\omega(h_{2})}{v(h_{2})}\right)\right).

Further, substituting
t_{1}+h_{1}
in place of
t_{1}
in

(4.8)

J_{22}=\frac{4}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{h_{1}}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{t_{1}^{2}\left(2\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}

we obtain

(4.9)

J_{22}=-\frac{4}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi-h_{1}}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{(t_{1}+h_{1})^{2}\left(2\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2},

Hence, from (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) we get

\begin{split}J_{22}&=\frac{2}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{h_{1}}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{t_{1}^{2}\left(2\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&\quad-\frac{2}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi-h_{1}}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{(t_{1}+h_{1})^{2}\left(2\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&=\frac{2}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{h_{1}}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{t_{1}^{2}\left(2\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&\quad-\frac{2}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{(t_{1}+h_{1})^{2}\left(2\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&\quad-\frac{2}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{h_{1}}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{(t_{1}+h_{1})^{2}\left(2\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{2}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{\pi-h_{1}}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{(t_{1}+h_{1})^{2}\left(2\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\end{split}

(4.10)

\begin{split}&=\frac{2}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{h_{1}}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}\left(\frac{H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})}{t_{1}^{2}}-\frac{H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})}{(t_{1}+h_{1})^{2}}\right)\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{\left(2\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&\quad-\frac{2}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{(t_{1}+h_{1})^{2}\left(2\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{2}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{\pi-h_{1}}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{h_{2}}H_{x,z_{1},y,z_{2}}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2})\frac{S(t_{1},t_{2})}{(t_{1}+h_{1})^{2}\left(2\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}:=\sum_{s=1}^{3}J_{22}^{(s)}.\end{split}

Using the inequalities
\frac{2}{\pi}\beta\leq\sin\beta
for
\beta\in(0,\pi/2)
,
\sin\beta\leq\beta
for
\beta\in(0,\pi)
, Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2

H_{p;\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}^{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})}:=\left\{f\in W(L^{p}((-\pi,\pi)^{2});\beta_{1},\beta_{2}),p\geq 1:A(f;\omega_{1},\omega_{2};\beta_{1},\beta_{2})<\infty\right\},

where

A(f;\omega_{1},\omega_{2};\beta_{1},\beta_{2}):=\sup_{t_{1}\neq 0,\,\,t_{2}\neq 0}\frac{\|f(x+t_{1},y+t_{2})-f(x,y)\|_{p;\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}}{\omega_{1}(|t_{1}|)+\omega_{2}(|t_{2}|)}

and the norm in the space
H_{p;\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}^{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})}
is defined by

\|f\|_{p;\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}^{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})}:=\|f\|_{p;\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}+A(f;\omega_{1},\omega_{2};\beta_{1},\beta_{2}).

We take
k=rm
and
\ell=qn
in (
2.1
), to obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{m,rm;n,qn}:=

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{r}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{q}\right)\sigma_{m(r+1)-1,n(q+1)-1}-\left(1+\frac{1}{r}\right)\frac{1}{q}\sigma_{m(r+1)-1,n-1}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\left(1+\frac{1}{q}\right)\sigma_{m-1,n(q+1)-1}+\frac{1}{rq}\sigma_{m-1,n-1},

where
r,q\in\{2,4,6,\dots\}
.

Hence, we name the mean
\sigma_{m,rm;n,qn}(f;x,y)
as
Double Even-Type Delayed Arithmetic Mean
for
S_{k,\ell}(f;x,y)
, which can be represented in its integral form

\sigma_{m,rm;n,qn}(f;x,y)=\frac{1}{mn\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}h_{x,y}(t_{1},t_{2})L_{m,rm;n,qn}(t_{1},t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2},

where

L_{m,rm;n,qn}(t_{1},t_{2}):=\frac{4}{rq}\frac{\sin\frac{\left(r+2\right)mt_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{rmt_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{\left(q+2\right)nt_{2}}{2}\sin\frac{qnt_{2}}{2}}{\left(4\sin\frac{t_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{t_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}.

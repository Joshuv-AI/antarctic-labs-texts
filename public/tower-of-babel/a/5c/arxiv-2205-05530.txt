A

For two distinct vertices

2
The lower stiffness matrix

We start with the following explicit description of
L^{-}(G,p)
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
p:V\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Let
e_{1},e_{2}\in E
. Then,

L^{-}(G,p)(e_{1},e_{2})=\begin{cases}2&\text{ if }e_{1}=e_{2}=\{i,j\}\text{ and }p(i)\neq p(j),\\
\cos(\theta(e_{1},e_{2}))&\text{ if }|e_{1}\cap e_{2}|=1,\\
0&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

where, for
e_{1}=\{i,j\}
and
e_{2}=\{i,k\}
,

\theta(e_{1},e_{2})
is the angle between
d_{ij}
and
d_{ik}
; that is,
\cos(\theta(e_{1},e_{2}))=d_{ij}\cdot d_{ik}
(note that, by convention, if
d_{ij}=0
or
d_{ik}=0
, then
\cos(\theta(e_{1},e_{2}))=0
).

Proof.

For convenience, we identify the vertex set of
G
with the set
[n]
. Let
e_{1}=\{i,j\}
and
e_{2}=\{k,l\}
, where
i<j
and
k<l
. Let
\{1_{i}\}_{i\in V}
be the standard basis for
{\mathbb{R}}^{|V|}
, and
\{1_{e}\}_{e\in E}
be the standard basis for
{\mathbb{R}}^{|E|}
.
Then,

R(G,p)1_{e_{1}}=(1_{i}-1_{j})\otimes d_{ij},

and similarly

R(G,p)1_{e_{2}}=(1_{k}-1_{l})\otimes d_{kl}.

Note that

L^{-}(G,p)(e_{1},e_{2})=(R(G,p)1_{e_{1}})^{t}(R(G,p)1_{e_{2}})=((1_{i}-1_{j})\cdot(1_{k}-1_{l}))(d_{ij}\cdot d_{kl}).

If
e_{1}=e_{2}
, we obtain

L^{-}(G,p)(e_{1},e_{2})=\|1_{i}-1_{j}\|^{2}\cdot\|d_{ij}\|^{2}=\begin{cases}2&\text{ if }p(i)\neq p(j),\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

If
e_{1}\cap e_{2}=\emptyset
, then

L^{-}(G,p)(e_{1},e_{2})=((1_{i}-1_{j})\cdot(1_{k}-1_{l}))(d_{ij}\cdot d_{kl})=0\cdot(d_{ij}\cdot d_{kl})=0.

Finally, assume
|e_{1}\cap e_{2}|=1
. Then, either
i=k
, or
i=l
, or
j=k
or
j=l
. If
i=k
, then

L^{-}(G,p)(e_{1},e_{2})=1\cdot(d_{ij}\cdot d_{il})=\cos(\theta(e_{1},e_{2})).

If
i=l
, then

L^{-}(G,p)(e_{1},e_{2})=(-1)\cdot(d_{ij}\cdot d_{ki})=d_{ij}\cdot d_{ik}=\cos(\theta(e_{1},e_{2})).

The other two cases follow similarly.
∎

2.1
Interlacing of spectra

We will use the following special case of Cauchy’s interlacing theorem:

Theorem 2.2

(See e.g.
[
3
]
)
.

Let
A
be a real symmetric matrix of size
n\times n
and
B
a principal submatrix of
A
of size
(n-1)\times(n-1)
. Let
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{n}
be the eigenvalues of
A
and
\mu^{\prime}_{1}\geq\mu^{\prime}_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\mu^{\prime}_{n-1}
be the eigenvalues of
B
. Then, for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, we have

\mu_{i}\geq\mu^{\prime}_{i}\geq\mu_{i+1}.

We obtain the following interlacing result, generalizing a known result for graph Laplacians (see e.g.
[
6
, Theorem 13.6.2]
).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with
|V|=n
, and let
p:V\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Let
e\in E
, and let
G\setminus e=(V,E\setminus\{e\})
. Let
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{dn}
be the eigenvalues of
L(G,p)
and
\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda^{\prime}_{dn}
be the eigenvalues of
L(G\setminus e,p)
. Let
\lambda_{0}=0
. Then, we have

\lambda_{i-1}\leq\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\leq\lambda_{i},

for all
1\leq i\leq dn
.

Proof.

Let
\mu^{\prime}_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu^{\prime}_{|E|}
be the eigenvalues of
L^{-}(G,p)
and
\mu_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{|E|-1}
be the eigenvalues of
L^{-}(G\setminus e,p)
.

Note that
L^{-}(G\setminus\{e\},p)
is a principal submatrix of
L^{-}(G,p)
, therefore by Theorem
2.2
, we have

\mu_{i}\geq\mu^{\prime}_{i}\geq\mu_{i+1}

for
i=1,\ldots,|E|-1
.

For
i>|E|-1
, let
\mu^{\prime}_{i}=0
, and for
i>|E|
, let
\mu_{i}=0
. Then, we have

\mu_{i}\geq\mu^{\prime}_{i}\geq\mu_{i+1}

for all
i
.
Since
L(G,p)
and
L^{-}(G,p)
have the same positive eigenvalues, we have for all
i=1,\ldots,dn

\lambda_{dn+1-i}=\mu_{i},

and similarly

\lambda^{\prime}_{dn+1-i}=\mu^{\prime}_{i}.

Therefore, we have

\lambda_{dn+1-i}\geq\lambda^{\prime}_{dn+1-i}\geq\lambda_{dn-i}

for all
i=1,\ldots,dn
(using
\lambda_{0}=0
).
So, for
j=1,\ldots,dn
, we obtain

\lambda_{j-1}\leq\lambda^{\prime}_{j}\leq\lambda_{j},

as wanted.
∎

As an application of Theorem
2.3
, we show that restricting attention to maps
p:V\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
that are embeddings (i.e injective) does not affect the
d
-dimensional algebraic connectivity of a graph
G=(V,E)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G=(V,E)
and
d\geq 1
. Then,

a_{d}(G)=\sup\left\{\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G,p))\middle|\,p:V\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,p\text{ is injective}\right\}.

Proof.

Let

\tilde{a}_{d}(G)=\sup\left\{\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G,p))\middle|\,p:V\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,p\text{ is injective}\right\}.

Clearly,
\tilde{a}_{d}(G)\leq a_{d}(G)
.
In the other direction, let
p:V\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. We will show that for any
\epsilon>0
there exists a
p^{\prime}:V\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
p^{\prime}
is injective and

\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G,p^{\prime}))>\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G,p))-\epsilon.

Let
E^{\prime}=\{\{u,v\}\in E:\,p(u)\neq p(v)\}
and let

G^{\prime}=(V,E^{\prime})
. Note that
L(G,p)=L(G^{\prime},p)
, and in particular
\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G,p))=\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G^{\prime},p))
.

By Lemma
2.1
, for
p^{\prime}
in a neighborhood of
p
, the entries of the lower stiffness matrix
L^{-}(G^{\prime},p^{\prime})
are continuous functions of
p^{\prime}
. Therefore, the spectral gap
\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G^{\prime},p^{\prime}))
is also continuous in
p^{\prime}
(as a root of the characteristic polynomial of
L^{-}(G^{\prime},p^{\prime}))
. That is, there exists
\delta>0
such that if
\|p(u)-p^{\prime}(u)\|<\delta
for all
u\in V
, then

\left|\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G^{\prime},p^{\prime}))-\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G^{\prime},p))\right|<\epsilon.

Now, let
p^{\prime}:V\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be an embedding satisfying
\|p(u)-p^{\prime}(u)\|<\delta
for all
u\in V
. Then, we have

\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G^{\prime},p^{\prime}))>\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G^{\prime},p))-\epsilon=\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G,p))-\epsilon.

Finally, by Theorem
2.3
, we obtain

\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G,p^{\prime}))\geq\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G^{\prime},p^{\prime}))>\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(G,p))-\epsilon.

Thus,
\tilde{a}_{d}(G)\geq a_{d}(G)
, as wanted.
∎

4
Spectra of Turán graphs

T(n,d+1)
and
T(n,2d)

Recall that
T(n,r)
is the complete balanced
r
-partite graph with
n
vertices. For
r=d+1
, we denote by
q^{\triangle}:[n]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the mapping that maps the vertices of each of the
d+1
parts of
T(n,d+1)
to one of the vertices of the regular
d
-dimensional simplex. Similarly, we denote by
q^{\diamond}:[n]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the mapping that maps the vertices of each of the
2d
parts of
T(n,2d)
to one of the vertices of the regular
d
-dimensional crosspolytope.
In this section we determine the spectra of
L(T(n,d+1),q^{\triangle})
and
L(T(n,2d),q^{\diamond})
.
In fact, in each case we provide a basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{E}
consisting of eigenvectors of the corresponding lower stiffness matrix.

As a consequence, we obtain a lower bound on the
d
-dimensional algebraic connectivity of
K_{n}
(Theorem
1.5
).

We begin with the following result of Jordán and Tanigawa:

Lemma 4.1

(
[
7
, Lemma 4.3]
)
.

Let
p:[n]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. If
p
is not constant, then the largest eigenvalue of
L(K_{n},p)
is
n
.

In
[
7
]
it was shown that
p
itself (when considered as a vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{dn}
) is an eigenvector of
L(K_{n},p)
with eigenvalue
n
. The corresponding eigenvector for
L^{-}(K_{n},p)
is
\phi=R(G,p)^{t}p
, which satisfies

\phi(\{i,j\})=\|p(i)-p(j)\|

for each
i\neq j\in[n]
.

The following result shows that for mappings
p
satisfying certain “spherical symmetry”,
n/2
is an eigenvalue of
L(K_{n},p)
of high multiplicity:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
p:[n]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\|p(i)\|=1
for all
i\in[n]
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}p(i)=0
. Assume that the image of
p
is of size at least
3
. Then,
n/2
is an eigenvalue of
L(K_{n},p)
with multiplicity at least
n-1
.

Proof.

Since the non-zero eigenvalues of
L(K_{n},p)
and
L^{-}(K_{n},p)
are the same, it is enough to look at
L^{-}(K_{n},p)
.

Let
f\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}=0
.
For every
i\neq j\in[n]
, let
\ell_{ij}=\|p(i)-p(j)\|
. Let
E=E(K_{n})=\{\{i,j\}:\,1\leq i<j\leq n\}
, and let
\phi_{f}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{E}
be defined by

\phi_{f}(\{i,j\})=(f_{i}+f_{j})\ell_{ij}.

We will show that
\phi_{f}
is an eigenvector of
L^{-}(K_{n},p)
with eigenvalue
n/2
.

For
i,j,k\in[n]
, let
\theta_{ijk}
be the angle between
p(i)-p(j)
and
p(k)-p(j)
.
By the law of cosines, we have

\ell_{ij}\ell_{jk}\cos(\theta_{ijk})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\ell_{ij}^{2}+\ell_{jk}^{2}-\ell_{ik}^{2}\right).

Let
I^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{E\times E}
be a diagonal matrix with elements
I^{\prime}_{e,e}=1
if
e=\{i,j\}
where
p(i)\neq p(j)
and
I^{\prime}_{e,e}=0
otherwise.
Let
A=L^{-}(K_{n},p)-2I^{\prime}
.
Let
e=\{i,j\}\in E
. First, assume that
p(i)\neq p(j)
.
Then, by Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle A\phi_{f}(e)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq i,j}\left(\cos(\theta_{ijk})\phi_{f}(\{j,k\})+\cos(\theta_{jik})\phi_{f}(\{i,k\})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq i,j}\left(\ell_{jk}\cos(\theta_{ijk})(f_{j}+f_{k})+\ell_{ik}\cos(\theta_{jik})(f_{i}+f_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq i,j}\left(\frac{\ell_{ij}^{2}+\ell_{jk}^{2}-\ell_{ik}^{2}}{2\ell_{ij}}(f_{j}+f_{k})+\frac{\ell_{ij}^{2}+\ell_{ik}^{2}-\ell_{jk}^{2}}{2\ell_{ij}}(f_{i}+f_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq i,j}\ell_{ij}f_{k}+\sum_{k\neq i,j}(f_{i}+f_{j})\frac{\ell_{ij}}{2}+\frac{f_{j}-f_{i}}{2\ell_{ij}}\sum_{k\neq i,j}(\ell_{jk}^{2}-\ell_{ik}^{2}).

Note that, since
\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}=0
, we have

\sum_{k\neq i,j}f_{k}=-(f_{i}+f_{j}).

Also, since
\|p(x)\|=1
for all
x\in[n]
, for all
x,y\in[n]
we have

\ell_{xy}^{2}=\|p(x)\|^{2}+\|p(y)\|^{2}-2p(x)\cdot p(y)=2-2p(x)\cdot p(y).

So, since
\sum_{k=1}^{n}p(k)=0
, we obtain

\sum_{k\neq i,j}(\ell_{jk}^{2}-\ell_{ik}^{2})=2(p(i)-p(j))\cdot\sum_{k\neq i,j}p(k)\\
=2(p(j)-p(i))\cdot(p(i)+p(j))=2(\|p(j)\|^{2}-\|p(i)\|^{2})=0.

Therefore, we obtain

A\phi_{f}(e)=-(f_{i}+f_{j})\ell_{ij}+\frac{n-2}{2}(f_{i}+f_{j})\ell_{ij}=\frac{n-4}{2}\phi_{f}(e).

So,
L^{-}(K_{n},p)\phi_{f}(e)=(n/2)\phi_{f}(e)
.
Now, assume
p(i)=p(j)
. Note that
\phi_{f}(e)=(f_{i}+f_{j})\ell_{ij}=0
. Then, since
\cos(\theta_{ijk})=0
and
\cos(\theta_{jik})=0
for all
k\neq i,j
, we obtain

A\phi_{f}(e)=\sum_{k\neq i,j}\left(\cos(\theta_{ijk})\phi_{f}(\{j,k\})+\cos(\theta_{jik})\phi_{f}(\{i,k\}\right)=0,

and therefore
L^{-}(K_{n},p)\phi_{f}(e)=0=(n/2)\phi_{f}(e)
.
Thus,
\phi_{f}
is an eigenvector of
L^{-}(K_{n},p)
with eigenvalue
n/2
.

Finally, we will show that the dimension of the subspace

U=\left\{\phi_{f}:\,f\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}=0\right\}

is
n-1
. Indeed, let
W=\{f\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}=0\}
. Clearly
\dim(W)=n-1
. We have
U=\Phi(W)
, where
\Phi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|E|\times n}
is defined by

\Phi(e,i)=\begin{cases}\ell_{ij}&\text{ if }e=\{i,j\}\text{ for some }j\in[n],\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let
g\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
\Phi g=0
. Let
i\in[n]
. Since the image of
p
is of size at least
3
, there exist
j,k\in[n]
such that
p(i),p(j),p(k)
are pairwise distinct. Then, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(\Phi g)_{\{i,j\}}=(g_{i}+g_{j})\ell_{ij},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(\Phi g)_{\{i,k\}}=(g_{i}+g_{k})\ell_{ik},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(\Phi g)_{\{j,k\}}=(g_{j}+g_{k})\ell_{jk}.

We obtain
g_{i}=-g_{j}=g_{k}=-g_{i}
.
That is,
g_{i}=0
. Therefore,
g=0
. Thus,
\Phi
has linearly independent columns, and so
\dim(U)=\dim(\Phi(W))=\dim(W)=n-1
, as wanted.

Hence,
n/2
has multiplicity at least
n-1
as an eigenvalue of
L^{-}(K_{n},p)
(and thus also as an eigenvalue of
L(K_{n},p)
).
∎

In the case

\|p(i)\|=1

is

\left\{0^{(3)},\frac{n}{2}^{(2n-4)},n^{(1)}\right\}.

Proposition

extends Zhu’s result

, which states that the same conclusion holds under the more restrictive assumption that

maps

to the roots of unity of order

attaining the supremum

. For completeness, we include a proof, following the proof of the special case sketched by Zhu in
[

]

Proof.

Let
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
be defined by

x=(1,0,1,0,\ldots,1,0)^{t},\ \ y=(0,1,0,1,\ldots,0,1)^{t}.

For
i\in[n]
, let
p_{x}(i),p_{y}(i)
be the two coordinates of
p(i)
, and let

p^{\perp}(i)=(-p_{y}(i),p_{x}(i))^{t}.

Define
r\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
by

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=(p_{x}^{\perp}(1),p_{y}^{\perp}(1),p_{x}^{\perp}(2),p_{y}^{\perp}(2),\ldots,p_{x}^{\perp}(n),p_{y}^{\perp}(n))^{t}

\displaystyle=(-p_{y}(1),p_{x}(1),-p_{y}(2),p_{x}(2),\ldots,-p_{y}(n),p_{x}(n))^{t}.

It is easy to check (using the fact that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}p(i)=0
) that
x,y,r
belong to the kernel of
L(K_{n},p)
, and moreover, form an orthogonal set.

We identify the mapping
p
with the vector

p=(p_{x}(1),p_{y}(1),\ldots,p_{x}(n),p_{y}(n))^{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}.

By Lemma
4.1
,
p
is an eigenvector of
L(K_{n},p)
with eigenvalue
n
.

We will show that

L(K_{n},p)=\frac{n}{2}I+\frac{1}{2}(pp^{t}-xx^{t}-yy^{t}-rr^{t}).

(1)

Then, it immediately follows that every vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}
orthogonal to
p,x,y
and
r
is an eigenvector of
L(K_{n},p)
with eigenvalue
n/2
, as wanted.

We can write
L(K_{n},p)
as a
n\times n
block matrix (see
[
7
, Section 4.4]
), formed by
2\times 2
blocks

[L(K_{n},p)]_{i,j}=-d_{ij}d_{ij}^{t},

for
i\neq j\in[n]
, and

[L(K_{n},p)]_{i,i}=\sum_{j\in[n]\setminus\{i\}}d_{ij}d_{ij}^{t}

for
i\in[n]
.
It is then easy to check that proving (
1
) is equivalent to showing that, for all
i\in[n]
,

\sum_{j\in[n]\setminus\{i\}}d_{ij}d_{ij}^{t}=\frac{1}{2}p(i)p(i)^{t}-\frac{1}{2}p^{\perp}(i)(p^{\perp}(i))^{t}+\frac{n-1}{2}I,

(2)

and for all
i\neq j\in[n]

-d_{ij}d_{ij}^{t}=\frac{1}{2}p(i)p(j)^{t}-\frac{1}{2}p^{\perp}(i)(p^{\perp}(j))^{t}-\frac{1}{2}I.

(3)

First, note that (
2
) follows from (

). Indeed, let

. By (

(and similarly,

), we obtain

\sum_{j\in[n]\setminus\{i\}}d_{ij}d_{ij}^{t}=\sum_{j\in[n]\setminus\{i\}}\left(\frac{1}{2}I-\frac{1}{2}p(i)p(j)^{t}+\frac{1}{2}p^{\perp}(i)(p^{\perp}(j))^{t}\right)\\
=\frac{n-1}{2}I-\frac{1}{2}p(i)\left(\sum_{j\in[n]\setminus\{i\}}p(j)^{t}\right)+\frac{1}{2}p^{\perp}(i)\left(\sum_{j\in[n]\setminus\{i\}}p^{\perp}(j)^{t}\right)\\
=\frac{n-1}{2}I+\frac{1}{2}p(i)p(i)^{t}-\frac{1}{2}p^{\perp}(i)p^{\perp}(i)^{t}.

So, we are left to show that (
3
) holds for all
i\neq j\in[n]
.

Let
i\neq j\in[n]
, and let

We will show that

. We denote the four coordinates of

by
M_{xx}
,
M_{xy}
,
M_{yx}
and
M_{yy}
. Since
\|p(i)\|=\|p(j)\|=1
, we have

\|p(i)-p(j)\|^{2}=2(1-p(i)\cdot p(j)),

and therefore

d_{ij}d_{ij}^{t}=\frac{(p(i)-p(j))(p(i)-p(j))^{t}}{2(1-p(i)\cdot p(j))}.

Using this and the fact that
p_{x}(i)^{2}+p_{y}(i)^{2}=p_{x}(j)^{2}+p_{y}(j)^{2}=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle M_{xx}=\frac{(p_{x}(i)-p_{x}(j))(p_{x}(i)-p_{x}(j))}{2(1-p_{x}(i)p_{x}(j)-p_{y}(i)p_{y}(j))}+\frac{1}{2}p_{x}(i)p_{x}(j)-\frac{1}{2}p_{y}(i)p_{y}(j)-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(1-p_{x}(i)p_{x}(j)-p_{y}(i)p_{y}(j))}\big(p_{x}(i)^{2}-2p_{x}(i)p_{x}(j)+p_{x}(j)^{2}+p_{x}(i)p_{x}(j)

\displaystyle-p_{y}(i)p_{y}(j)+p_{y}(i)^{2}p_{y}(j)^{2}-p_{x}(i)^{2}p_{x}(j)^{2}-1+p_{x}(i)p_{x}(j)+p_{y}(i)p_{y}(j)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{x}(i)^{2}+p_{x}(j)^{2}-p_{x}(i)^{2}p_{x}(j)^{2}-1+(1-p_{x}(i)^{2})(1-p_{x}(j)^{2})}{2(1-p_{x}(i)p_{x}(j)-p_{y}(i)p_{y}(j))}=0.

Similarly,
M_{yy}=0
. Finally, we have

\displaystyle M_{xy}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p_{x}(i)-p_{x}(j))(p_{y}(i)-p_{y}(j))}{2(1-p_{x}(i)p_{x}(j)-p_{y}(i)p_{y}(j))}+\frac{1}{2}p_{x}(i)p_{y}(j)+\frac{1}{2}p_{y}(i)p_{x}(j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(1-p_{x}(i)p_{x}(j)-p_{y}(i)p_{y}(j))}\big(p_{x}(i)p_{y}(i)-p_{y}(i)p_{x}(j)-p_{x}(i)p_{y}(j)

\displaystyle+p_{x}(j)p_{y}(j)+p_{x}(i)p_{y}(j)+p_{y}(i)p_{x}(j)-p_{x}(i)^{2}p_{x}(j)p_{y}(j)

\displaystyle-p_{x}(i)p_{y}(i)p_{x}(j)^{2}-p_{x}(i)p_{y}(i)p_{y}(j)^{2}-p_{y}(i)^{2}p_{x}(j)p_{y}(j)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{x}(i)p_{y}(i)+p_{x}(j)p_{y}(j)-p_{x}(j)p_{y}(j)-p_{x}(i)p_{y}(i)}{2(1-p_{x}(i)p_{x}(j)-p_{y}(i)p_{y}(j))}=0,

and similarly

∎

|e\cap e^{\prime}|=1
.

Also, note that for all
v\in V
, we have
\sum_{e\in E:\,v\in e}\Psi_{ijk}(e)=0
.

Moreover, let
e=\{u,v\}\in\supp(\Psi_{ijk})
. Assume
u\in V_{i,1}
and
v\in V_{j,1}
(the other cases are analyzed similarly). Then, using (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E:\,|e\cap e^{\prime}|=1}\cos(\theta(e,e^{\prime}))\Psi_{ijk}(e^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in V_{i,1}\setminus\{u\}}1+\sum_{w\in V_{j,1}\setminus\{v\}}1-\sum_{w\in V_{k,1}\cup V_{k,-1}}\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2d}-2=\left(\frac{n}{2d}-2\right)\Psi_{ijk}(e).

Therefore, by Lemma
4.5
,
\Psi_{ijk}
is an eigenvector of
L^{-}
with eigenvalue
n/(2d)
.

For each
j\in[d]
, fix
i(j)\in[d]\setminus\{j\}
. Let

I_{j}=\{(i(j),j,k):\,k\in[d]\setminus\{j,i(j)\}\}

and
I=\cup_{j\in[d]}I_{j}
. Note that
|I|=d(d-2)
. We will show that
\{\Psi_{ijk}\}_{(i,j,k)\in I}
is a linearly independent set.

First, note that the vectors
\{\psi_{ij}\}_{i\neq j\in[d]}
form an orthogonal set. Indeed, let
i,j,k,s\in[d]
such that
i\neq j
and
k\neq s
. If
\{i,j\}\neq\{k,s\}
then
\psi_{ij}
and
\psi_{ks}
have disjoint supports, and therefore
\psi_{ij}\cdot\psi_{ks}=0
. Otherwise, if
i=s
and
j=k

we obtain

\psi_{ij}\cdot\psi_{ji}=\sum_{e\in E(V_{i,1},V_{j,1})}1\cdot 1+\sum_{e\in E(V_{i,1},V_{j,-1})}(-1)\cdot 1\\
+\sum_{e\in E(V_{i,-1},V_{j,1})}1\cdot(-1)+\sum_{e\in E(V_{i,-1},V_{j,-1})}(-1)\cdot(-1)=0.

Hence, for
\{i,j,k\}\in\binom{[d]}{3}
and
\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime},k^{\prime}\}\in\binom{[d]}{3}
such that
j\neq j^{\prime}
, we have
\Psi_{ijk}\cdot\Psi_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}=0
.
Therefore, we are left to show that for all
j\in[d]
,
\{\Psi_{ijk}\}_{(i,j,k)\in I_{j}}
is linearly independent. But this follows from the fact that for every
(i,j,k)\in I_{j}
there is an edge in the support of
\Psi_{ijk}
that is unique to
\Psi_{ijk}
(for example, any edge
\{u,v\}
where
u\in V_{j,1}
and
v\in V_{k,1}
).

We now complete the set
\{\Psi_{ijk}\}_{(i,j,k)\in I}
to an eigenbasis of
n/2d
.
Let
i\neq j\in[d]
and
x\in\{1,-1\}
. Let
u\neq v\in V_{i,x}
.

Define
f_{j}^{u,v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{E}
by

f_{j}^{u,v}=\sum_{w\in V_{j,1}}(1_{\{u,w\}}-1_{\{v,w\}})+\sum_{w\in V_{j,-1}}(-1_{\{u,w\}}+1_{\{v,w\}}).

Let
e\notin\supp(f_{j}^{u,v})
.
If
e\in E(V_{i,x},V_{j,1})\cup E(V_{i,x},V_{j,-1})
, then
\cos(\theta(e,e^{\prime}))=1
for all
e^{\prime}\in\supp(f_{j}^{u,v})
such that
|e\cap e^{\prime}|=1
. If
e\in E(V_{i,1},V_{i,-1})\cup E(V_{j,1},V_{j,-1})
, then
\cos(\theta(e,e^{\prime}))=1/\sqrt{2}
for all
e^{\prime}\in\supp(f_{j}^{u,v})
such that
|e\cap e^{\prime}|=1
. If
e\in E(V_{i,-x},V_{j,1})\cup E(V_{i,-x},V_{j,-1})
, then
\cos(\theta(e,e^{\prime}))=0
for all
e^{\prime}\in\supp(f_{j}^{u,v})
such that
|e\cap e^{\prime}|=1
. Otherwise,
\cos(\theta(e,e^{\prime}))=1/2
for all
e^{\prime}\in\supp(f_{j}^{u,v})
such that
|e\cap e^{\prime}|=1
.

In addition, it is easy to check that for every
w\in V
,
\sum_{e\in E,\\
w\in e}f^{u,v}_{j}(e)=0
.

Finally, let
e=\{a,b\}\in\supp(f_{j}^{u,v})
. Assume
a=u
and
b\in V_{j,1}
(the other cases are similar). Then, by (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E:\,|e\cap e^{\prime}|=1}\cos(\theta(e,e^{\prime}))f_{j}^{u,v}(e^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in V_{j,1}\setminus\{b\}}\cos(\theta_{buw})-\sum_{w\in V_{j,-1}}\cos(\theta_{buw})-\cos(\theta_{ubv})

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in V_{j}\setminus\{b\}}1-\sum_{w\in V_{k}}0-1=\frac{n}{2d}-2=\left(\frac{n}{2d}-2\right)f_{j}^{u,v}(e).

Therefore, by Lemma
4.5
,
f_{j}^{u,v}
is an eigenvector of
L^{-}
with eigenvalue
n/(2d)
.

For each
i\in[d]
and
x\in\{1,-1\}
, fix some
u(i,x)\in V_{i,x}
.
For
j\in[d]\setminus\{i\}
, let

J_{i,x,j}=\{(u(i,x),v,j):\,u(i,x)\neq v\in V_{i,x}\}.

and let
J=\cup_{i\in[d]}\cup_{x\in\{1,-1\}}\cup_{j\in[d]\setminus\{i\}}J_{i,x,j}
. Note that
|J|=2d(d-1)(n/(2d)-1)
.

We will show that
\{f_{j}^{u,v}\}_{(u,v,j)\in J}
are linearly independent.

Let
u\neq v\in V_{i,x}
and
u^{\prime}\neq v^{\prime}\in V_{i^{\prime},x^{\prime}}
. Let
j\neq i
and
j^{\prime}\neq i^{\prime}
.
Assume
(i,x,j)\neq(i^{\prime},x^{\prime},j^{\prime})
.
We will show that
f_{j}^{u,v}
and
f_{j^{\prime}}^{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}
are orthogonal. Indeed, the supports of the two vectors are disjoint unless
i^{\prime}=j
and
j^{\prime}=i
. But it is easy to check that also in this case we have
f_{j}^{u,v}\cdot f_{j^{\prime}}^{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}=0
.

Therefore, it is enough to show that for every
i\neq j\in[d]
and
x\in\{1,-1\}
,
\{f_{j}^{u,v}\}_{(u,v,j)\in J_{i,x,j}}
is linearly independent. But again, this follows from the fact that for every
(u,v,j)\in J_{i,x,j}
there is an edge in the support of
f_{j}^{u,v}
that is unique to it (for example, the edge
\{v,w\}
for any
w\in V_{j,1}
).

Finally, note that
\psi_{ij}\cdot f_{k}^{u,v}=0
for every
i\neq j\in[d]
and
(u,v,k)\in J
. Therefore,
\Psi_{ijk}\cdot f_{m}^{u,v}=0
for all
(i,j,k)\in I
and
(u,v,m)\in J
. Thus, the eigenvectors
\{\Psi_{ijk}\}_{(i,j,k)\in I}\cup\{f_{j}^{u,v}\}_{(u,v,j)\in J}
form a linearly independent set.

So,
n/(2d)
is an eigenvalue of
L^{-}
with multiplicity at least

d(d-2)+2d(d-1)(n/(2d)-1)=n(d-1)-d^{2},

as wanted.
∎

5
The
n
largest eigenvalues of the complete graph
K_{n}

We proceed to establish a lower bound for the sum of the largest
n
eigenvalues of
L(K_{n},p)
for all embeddings
p:[n]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. As a corollary, we derive an upper bound for
a_{d}(K_{n})
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
p:[n]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be injective. Then,

\sum_{j=(d-1)n+1}^{dn}\lambda_{j}(L(K_{n},p))\geq\frac{n^{2}}{3}+n\,.

See
1.6

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
p:V\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be injective. We compute the trace of
L(K_{n},p)

in two ways. By Lemma
2.1
, all the diagonal entries of
L^{-}(K_{n},p)
equal
2
, since
p
is injective. Thus, we have

\mathrm{Tr}(L(K_{n},p))=\mathrm{Tr}(L^{-}(K_{n},p))=2\binom{n}{2}=n^{2}-n\,.

(6)

On the other hand, let
\lambda=\lambda_{\binom{d+1}{2}+1}(L(K_{n},p))
. We deduce from Lemma
5.1
that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(L(K_{n},p))=

\displaystyle~\sum_{j=1}^{dn}\lambda_{j}(L(K_{n},p))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=\binom{d+1}{2}+1}^{(d-1)n}\lambda_{j}(L(K_{n},p))+\sum_{j=(d-1)n+1}^{dn}\lambda_{j}(L(K_{n},p))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\left((d-1)n-\binom{d+1}{2}\right)\lambda+\frac{n^{2}}{3}+n.

(7)

By combining (
6
) and (
7
), we derive that

\lambda\leq\frac{2n^{2}/3-2n}{(d-1)n-\binom{d+1}{2}}.

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2n^{2}/3-2n}{(d-1)n-\binom{d+1}{2}}-\frac{2n}{3(d-1)}=\frac{n(d(d+1)-6(d-1))}{3(d-1)\left((d-1)n-\binom{d+1}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n(d(d+1)-6(d-1))}{3n(d-1)^{2}}=\frac{d(d+1)-6(d-1)}{3(d-1)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{3}.

Therefore, we obtain

\lambda\leq\frac{2n}{3(d-1)}+\frac{1}{3}.

Thus, by Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

a_{d}(K_{n})\leq\frac{2n}{3(d-1)}+\frac{1}{3},

as claimed.
∎

To prove Lemma
5.1
we will need the following theorem due to Ky Fan:

Theorem 5.2

(Ky Fan
[
4
, Thm. 1]
)
.

Let
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times m}
be a symmetric matrix, and let
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{m}
be its eigenvalues. Then, for every
k\leq m
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}=\max\left\{\text{Tr}(AP):\,P\in\mathcal{P}_{k}\right\}

where
\mathcal{P}_{k}
consists of all orthogonal projection matrices into
k
-dimensional subspaces of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

Let
E=\binom{[n]}{2}
. For
i\in[n]
, let
v^{(i)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{E}
be defined by

v^{(i)}_{e}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }i\in e,\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

We claim that the

E\times E
symmetric matrix
P
defined by

P_{e,e^{\prime}}=\left\{\begin{matrix}\frac{2}{n-1}&e=e^{\prime}\\[6.0pt]
\frac{n-3}{(n-1)(n-2)}&|e\cap e^{\prime}|=1\\[6.0pt]
\frac{-1}{\binom{n-1}{2}}&e\cap e^{\prime}=\emptyset\end{matrix}\right.\,,

is the orthogonal projection on the subspace spanned by
\{v^{(i)}:\,i\in[n]\}
. Indeed, if
e=\{i,j\}
, then the
e
-th row of
P
is equal to

P_{e,\cdot}=\frac{1}{n-1}\left(v^{(i)}+v^{(j)}-\frac{1}{n-2}\sum_{k\neq i,j}v^{(k)}\right)\,.

Therefore,
Pw=0
for every
w
that is orthogonal to all the vectors
v^{(i)},i\in[n]
. In addition, a straightforward computation shows that
Pv^{(i)}=v^{(i)}
for every
i\in[n]
since
(v^{(i)})^{t}v^{(j)}=1
if
i\neq j
and
\|v^{(i)}\|^{2}=n-1
.

We apply theorem
5.2
for
L^{-}:=L^{-}(K_{n},p)

and
P
to find that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=(d-1)n+1}^{dn}\lambda_{i}(L(K_{n},p))=\sum_{i=\binom{n-1}{2}}^{\binom{n}{2}}\lambda_{i}(L^{-})\geq

\displaystyle~\mathrm{Tr}(L^{-}P)=\sum_{e,e^{\prime}}P_{e,e^{\prime}}L^{-}_{e,e^{\prime}}.

Recall the precise description of
L^{-}
in Lemma
2.1
. Since
p
is injective, the contribution of the diagonal terms
e=e^{\prime}
to the summation is

\sum_{e\in E}P_{e,e}L^{-}_{e,e}=\binom{n}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{n-1}\cdot 2=2n\,.

(8)

In addition, since
L^{-}_{e,e^{\prime}}=0
if
e\cap e^{\prime}=\emptyset
, the contribution of the non-diagonal terms is

\sum_{e\neq e^{\prime}}P_{e,e^{\prime}}L^{-}_{e,e^{\prime}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\sum_{j^{\prime}\neq i,j}\frac{n-3}{(n-1)(n-2)}L^{-}_{\{i,j\},\{i,j^{\prime}\}}\,.

(9)

Note that all the
\binom{n}{3}
triples
\{i,j,j^{\prime}\}
of vertices satisfy

L^{-}_{\{i,j\},\{i,j^{\prime}\}}+L^{-}_{\{j,i\},\{j,j^{\prime}\}}+L^{-}_{\{j^{\prime},i\},\{j^{\prime},j\}}\geq 1.

Indeed, since
p
is injective, this is the sum of the cosines of the angles in the (possibly flat) triangle
p(i),p(j),p(j^{\prime})
— which is bounded from below by
1
. In addition, note that each term
L^{-}_{\{i,j\},\{i,j^{\prime}\}}
appears twice in (
9
) since
j,j^{\prime}
are ordered. Consequently,

\sum_{e\neq e^{\prime}}P_{e,e^{\prime}}L^{-}_{e,e^{\prime}}\geq\binom{n}{3}\cdot\frac{2(n-3)}{(n-1)(n-2)}=\frac{n^{2}}{3}-n\,.

(10)

Joining (
8
) and (
10
), we obtain

\sum_{i=(d-1)n+1}^{dn}\lambda_{i}(L(K_{n},p))\geq\frac{n^{2}}{3}+n\,.

∎

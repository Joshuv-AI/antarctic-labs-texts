1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{N}_{+}
denotes the set of non-negative integers and the set of positive integers, respectively. Let
A\subset\mathbb{N}_{+}
be given and take
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By an
A
-partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
with parts in
A
, of a non-negative integer
n
, we mean a representation of
n
in the form

n=\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{k},

where
\lambda_{i}\in A
. The representations of
n
differing only in order of the terms are counted as one. By
p_{A}(n)
we denote the number of all
A
-partitions of
n
. We also use the standard convention
p_{A}(0)=1
. The famous partition function
p(n):=p_{\mathbb{N}_{+}}(n)
was introduced by Euler. However, a true explosion of works dedicated to the sequence
(p(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
was initiated by Ramanujan and his collaborations with Hardy. The literature concerning various arithmetic properties of
p(n)
and others partition functions associated with specific subsets of
\mathbb{N}_{+}
is enormous. The standard reference covering various aspects of the theory partitions is the monograph of Andrews
[
1
]
.

The common starting point in investigations of the sequence
(p_{A}(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is the ordinary generating function

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{A}(n)x^{n}=\prod_{a\in A}\frac{1}{1-x^{a}}.

The above identity can be seen twofold: as a identity in the formal power series ring and also as a equality of two analytic functions (in the disc
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\;|z|<1\}
) represented by corresponding sides.

For given
A\subset\mathbb{N}_{+}
let us write

F_{A}(t,x)=\prod_{a\in A}\frac{1}{1-tx^{a}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{A,n}(t)x^{n}

and observe that the polynomial
f_{A,n}(t)
is a natural generalization of the number
p_{A}(n)=f_{A,n}(1)
which counts the number of
A
-partitions of the number
n
.

Let us write

f_{A,n}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{A}(i,n)t^{i},

and note that
c_{A}(i,n)
is the number of
A
-partitions of
n
with exactly
i
parts. In particular
f_{A,n}(1)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{A}(i,n)
is the number of
A
-partitions of
A
. In the sequel, we will call the polynomial
f_{A,n}(t)
as a
n
-th
A
-partition polynomial.

In a recent paper
[
3
]
the case of
A
-partitions polynomials for
A=\{2^{i}:\;i\in\mathbb{N}\}
were investigated. Among many properties of the
A
-partition polynomials, the authors proved that for
k=0,1
, the expression
(-1)^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}i^{k}c_{A}(i,n)
is non-negative. Moreover, the conjecture was stated that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
this expression is non-negative. This suggest the following general question.

Question 1.1
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. What can be said about the sign behaviour of the sequence

S_{A,k}(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}i^{k}c_{A}(i,n)?

In particular, under which conditions on
A
we have that
(-1)^{n}S_{A,k}(n)\geq 0
for given
k
and all but finitely many
n
?

Let us also note that the value
S_{A,0}(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}c_{A}(i,n)
has a nice combinatorial interpretation as the difference between the number of partitions of
n
with even number of parts and odd number of parts. One can think that in general the sign behaviour of
S_{A,0}(n)
should be alternating, that means that
(-1)^{n}S_{A,0}(n)\geq 0
. The aim of this paper is to investigate this expectation and prove several results which in many cases allow the proof of the non-negativity of
(-1)^{n}S_{A,k}(n)
for a broad class of sets
A
and all
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
. However, as we will see this is not true in general. Our investigations fits in various studies concerning behaviour of various counting objects involving parts of partitions (see
[
2
]
).

Let us describe the content of the paper in some details. In Section
2
we collect some basic observations concerning the sequence
(S_{A,k}(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. In particular, by investigating so called
\delta
operator we introduce two family of polynomials
p_{A,n}(t),q_{A,n}(t)
closely related to the logarithmic derivative of the generating function of
A
-partition polynomials and obtain recurrence relations satisfied by terms of the double sequence
(S_{A,k}(n))_{k,n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
In Section
3
we obtain several results which allow the proof of nonnegativity of
S_{A,k}(n)
for each
k
and
n
and several sets
A
. In particular, if
A
contains only odd numbers then
(-1)^{n}S_{A,k}(n)\geq 0
. The same statement is true for the set
A=\mathbb{N}_{+}
and many others. Finally, in the last section we state several problems and conjectures which appeared during our investigations and we were unable to solve. We hope that this collection of problems will stimulate further activity in this area.

2.
Basic observations

In order to investigate the sign behaviour of the sequence
(S_{A,k}(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
is fixed, we consider the differential operator
\delta:\;\mathbb{R}[t]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}[t]
defined in the following way: for
f\in\mathbb{R}[t]
we put

\delta(f(t))=t\frac{df(t)}{dt}.

For
k=0
we put
\delta^{(0)}(f)=f
and for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
we define by induction a
k
-th power of the operator
\delta
as
\delta^{(k)}(f)
, where

\delta^{(k)}(f)=\delta(\delta^{(k-1)}(f)).

The basic properties of the operator
\delta
can be summarized in the following well known

Lemma 2.1
.

(1)

\delta
is linear operator, i.e., for all
u,v\in\mathbb{R},f,g\in\mathbb{R}[t]
we have
\delta(uf+vg)=u\delta(f)+v\delta(g)
;

(2)

if
f\in\mathbb{R}[t]\setminus\mathbb{R}
then
\operatorname{deg}\delta(f)=\operatorname{deg}f
;

(3)

for
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\delta^{(k)}(fg)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\delta^{(i)}(f)\delta^{(k-i)}(g).

(4)

if
f(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}t^{i}\in\mathbb{R}[t]
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
is given then

\delta^{(n)}(f(t))=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}i^{n}t^{i}

Proof.

The first two properties are easy consequences of the definition of the operator
\delta
. The proof of the third property is just the application of the Leibnitz rule together with induction on
k
. The last property follows from the linearity of the operator
\delta
and the equality
\delta^{(k)}(t^{i})=i^{k}t^{i}
. We omit the simple details.
∎

As an immediate consequence of the fourth property of the operator
\delta
we get the equality

S_{A,k}(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}i^{k}c_{A}(i,n)=\delta^{(k)}(f_{A,n}(t))\mid_{t=-1}.

In order to simplify the notation for a given
A\subset\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
we write

A(n):=\{d\in A:\;d|n\},

i.e., the set
A(n)
contains those divisors of
n
which are lying in
A
.

We prove certain identities which will be useful in our investigations and allow to express action of
\delta
operator on
A
-partition polynomial in terms of
A
-partitions polynomials and a related sequence of polynomials. More precisely, we prove the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{A,i}(t)f_{A,n-i}(t)

\displaystyle=tf_{A,n}^{\prime}(t)=\delta(f_{A,n}(t)),

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{A,i}(t)f_{A,n-i}(t)

\displaystyle=nf_{A,n}(t),

\displaystyle tq^{\prime}_{A,n}(t)

\displaystyle=np_{A,n}(t),

where

p_{A,n}(t)=\sum_{a\in A(n)}t^{n/a},

and

q_{A,n}(t)=\sum_{a\in A(n)}at^{n/a}.

Proof.

Applying logarithmic differentiation with respect to the variable
t
, we get the following expansion

\frac{t}{F_{A}(t,x)}\frac{\partial F_{A}(t,x)}{\partial t}=t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\log F_{A}(t,x))=\sum_{a\in A}\frac{tx^{a}}{1-tx^{a}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{A,n}(t)x^{n}.

Thus, the first identity is a consequence of the equality

t\frac{\partial F_{A}(t,x)}{\partial t}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{A,n}(t)x^{n}\right)F_{A}(t,x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{A,i}(n)f_{A,n-i}(t)\right)x^{n}

by a comparison of coefficients near
x^{n}
on far ends.

Similarly, applying logarithmic differentiation with respect to the variable
x
, we get

\frac{x}{F_{A}(t,x)}\frac{\partial F_{A}(t,x)}{\partial x}=x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(\log F_{A}(t,x))=\sum_{a\in A}\frac{atx^{a}}{1-tx^{a}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q_{A,n}(t)x^{n},

and thus, form the equality

x\frac{\partial F_{A}(t,x)}{\partial x}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q_{A,n}(t)x^{n}\right)F_{A}(t,x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{A,i}(n)f_{A,n-i}(t)\right)x^{n}

and comparison of coefficients, we get the second identity form the statement.

The third identity is a direct consequence of the from of the polynomials
p_{A,n},q_{A,n}
.

∎

Remark 2.3
.

Let us note that the polynomial
p_{A,n}(t)
can be seen as a polynomial generalization of the number of
A
-divisors function
\tau_{A}(n)=\sum_{d\in A(n)}1=p_{A,n}(1)
. Similarly, the polynomial
q_{A,n}(t)
can be seen as a polynomial analogue of the sum of
A
-divisors function
\sigma_{A}(n)=\sum_{d\in A(n)}d=q_{A,n}(1)
.

As an application of the above result we prove a double recurrence relation satisfied by the sequence
(S_{A,k}(n))_{k,n}
. More precisely, we have the following.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We have the following identities

S_{A,k+1}(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\delta^{(j)}(p_{A,i}(t))\mid_{t=-1}S_{A,k-j}(n-i).

and

\delta^{(k)}(p_{A,n}(t))=\sum_{a\in A(n)}\left(\frac{n}{a}\right)^{k}t^{\frac{n}{a}}.

Proof.

As a consequence of the first identity from Lemma
2.2
we get the following chain of identities

\displaystyle S_{A,k+1}(n)

\displaystyle=\delta^{(k)}(\delta(f_{A,n}(t)))=\delta^{(k)}(tf_{A,n}^{\prime}(t))\mid_{t=-1}

\displaystyle=\delta^{(k)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{A,i}(t)f_{A,n-i}(t)\right)\mid_{t=-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta^{(k)}(p_{A,i}(t)f_{A,n-i}(t))\mid_{t=-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\delta^{(j)}(p_{A,i}(t))\mid_{t=-1}\delta^{(k-j)}(f_{A,n-i}(t))\mid_{t=-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\delta^{(j)}(p_{A,i}(t))\mid_{t=-1}S_{A,k-j}(n-i)

and hence the result.

The second identity from the statement is a simple consequence of the last property from Lemma
2.1
.

∎

3.
Results

In this section we prove several results which allow to prove non-negativity of
(-1)^{n}S_{A,k}(n)
for a broad class of sets
A
. Moreover, we show that the sequence
(\operatorname{sign}(S_{A,k}(n)))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
A=\{a,b\},a<b,\gcd(a,b)=1
, is periodic of period
2a(b-a)
.

We start with the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A_{1},A_{2}\subset\mathbb{N}_{+}
and suppose that
A_{1}\cap A_{2}=\emptyset
. If for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
(-1)^{n}S_{A_{i},k}(n)\geq 0
for
i=1,2
, then
(-1)^{n}S_{A_{1}\cup A_{2},k}(n)\geq 0
.

Proof.

We are interesting in the value of

S_{A_{1}\cup A_{2},k}(n)=\delta^{(k)}(f_{A_{1}\cup A_{2},n}(t))\mid_{t=-1}.

Because
A_{1}\cap A_{2}=\emptyset
we have the identity
F_{A_{1}\cup A_{2}}(t,x)=F_{A_{1}}(t,x)F_{A_{2}}(t,x)
. As a consequence we deduce that

f_{A_{1}\cup A_{2},n}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}f_{A_{1},i}(t)f_{A_{2},n-i}(t).

Thus, from Lemma
2.1
we obtain

\delta^{(k)}(f_{A_{1}\cup A_{2},n}(t))=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\delta^{(j)}(f_{A_{1},i}(t))\delta^{(k-j)}(f_{A_{2},n-i}(t)).

Finally, we get

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}S_{A_{1}\cup A_{2},k}(n)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\delta^{(k)}(f_{A_{1}\cup A_{2},n}(t))\mid_{t=-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\{(-1)^{i}S_{A_{1},j}(i)\}\{(-1)^{n-i}S_{A_{2},k-j}(n-i)\}\geq 0

and from our assumptions we get the result.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\}
be a possible infinite family, i.e., we allow
m=+\infty
, of pairwise disjoint subsets of
\mathbb{N}_{+}
and suppose that for each
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
we have
(-1)^{n}S_{A_{i},k}(n)\geq 0
for
i=1,\ldots,m
. If
A=\cup_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
, then for each
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
we have
(-1)^{n}S_{A,k}(n)\geq 0
.

Proof.

If
m<+\infty
then the statement follows from the simple induction on
m
using Lemma
3.1
with
A=A_{1}\cup\ldots\cup A_{m-1},B=A_{m}
. We omit the simple details.

If
m=+\infty
we proceed as follows. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Because the family
\mathcal{A}
is infinite and contains pairwise-disjoint sets there is an integer
s
such that
n<\operatorname{min}A_{i}
for each
i>s
. Because for
(-1)^{n}S_{A_{i},k}(n)\geq 0
for each
i\leq s
the same is true for the set
\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}A_{i}
. Next, due to the fact that
n
can not be represented as a sum of elements from the set
\bigcup_{i=m+1}^{\infty}A_{i}
we get that
(-1)^{n}S_{B,k}(n)\geq 0
- and hence the result.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A\subset 2\mathbb{N}+1
. Then, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
(-1)^{n}S_{A,k}(n)\geq 0
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The generating function for the sequence
(S_{A,0}(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is

F_{A}(-1,x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{A,0}(n)x^{n}=\prod_{a\in A}\frac{1}{1+x^{a}}=\prod_{a\in A}\frac{1}{1-(-x)^{a}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}p_{A}(n)x^{n},

i.e.,
S_{A,0}(n)=(-1)^{n}p_{A}(n)
and our statement holds for
k=0
. Let us suppose that our statement is true up to fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We prove it for
k+1
. For
j\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
we have that

\delta^{(j)}(p_{A,n}(t))\mid_{t=-1}=\sum_{a\in A(n)}\left(\frac{n}{a}\right)^{j}(-1)^{\frac{n}{a}}=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}-\sum_{a\in A(n)}\left(\frac{n}{a}\right)^{j},&n\equiv 1\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu2)\\
\sum_{a\in A(n)}\left(\frac{n}{a}\right)^{j},&n\equiv 0\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu2).\end{array}\end{cases}

Thus, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
(-1)^{n}\delta^{(j)}(p_{A,n}(t))\mid_{t=-1}\geq 0
. To get the statement for
k+1
we use Corollary
2.4
and note that

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}S_{A,k+1}

\displaystyle(n)=(-1)^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\delta^{(j)}(p_{A,i}(t))\mid_{t=-1}S_{A,k-j}(n-i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\{(-1)^{i}\delta^{(j)}(p_{A,i}(t))\mid_{t=-1}\}\{(-1)^{n-i}S_{A,k-j}(n-i)\}\geq 0.

The first expression in curly parentheses is non-negative due to our observation above and the second expression in curly parentheses is non-negative from our induction hypothesis. Hence, the statement is true for
k+1
and our theorem is proved.

∎

We generalize the above result and prove the following:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A\subset 2\mathbb{N}+1,m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
{\bf p}=(p_{i})_{i\leq m}
be an increasing possibly infinite sequence of non-negative integers with
p_{0}=0
, i.e., we allow
m=+\infty
. Let us put

B=\bigcup_{j=0}^{m}2^{p_{j}}A.

Then for each
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
(-1)^{n}S_{B,k}(n)\geq 0
.

Proof.

First let us note that

B(n)=\{b\in B:\;b|n\}=\left\{b\in\bigcup_{j=0}^{m}2^{p_{j}}A:\;b|n\right\}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{m}A(n/2^{p_{j}}).

Although we allow
m=+\infty
, for any given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the sum on the right side is finite. Indeed, if
p_{j}>\nu_{2}(n)
then
A(n/2^{p_{j}})=\emptyset
. To get the result we consider two cases:
m<+\infty
and
m=+\infty
.

Suppose that
m<+\infty
. Let us define
q_{i}=p_{i+1}-p_{i}
. From our assumption on the sequence
{\bf p}
we know that
q_{i}\geq 1
for
i=0,\ldots,m-1
.

We note that the sign of
S_{B,0}(n)=f_{B,n}(-1)
is the sign of the
n
-th coefficient of

\displaystyle\prod_{a\in B}\frac{1}{1+x^{a}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{a\in A}\prod_{j=0}^{m}\frac{1}{1+x^{2^{p_{j}}a}}=\prod_{a\in A}\prod_{j=0}^{m}\frac{1-x^{2^{p_{j}}a}}{1-x^{2^{p_{j}+1}a}}=\prod_{a\in A}\left(\frac{1}{1-x^{2^{p_{m}+1}a}}\right)F(x),

where

\displaystyle F(x)=

\displaystyle\prod_{a\in A}\left(\left(1-x^{2^{p_{0}}a}\right)\left(\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{1-x^{2^{p_{i+1}}a}}{1-x^{2^{p_{i}+1}a}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\prod_{a\in A}\left(\left(1-x^{2^{p_{0}}a}\right)\left(\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}\prod_{j=1}^{q_{i}-1}\left(1+x^{2^{p_{i}+j}a}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\prod_{a\in A}\left(\left(1+(-x)^{2^{p_{0}}a}\right)\left(\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}\prod_{j=1}^{q_{i}-1}\left(1+(-x)^{2^{p_{i}+j}a}\right)\right)\right).

Now let us note that
F(-x)
is a generating function for the partition function, say
pd_{A}({\bf p},n)
, which counts partitions of
n
into distinct parts from the set

\bigcup_{i=0}^{k}\bigcup_{j=1}^{q_{i}-1}2^{p_{i}+j}A.

Consequently we get that

\displaystyle\prod_{a\in B}\frac{1}{1+x^{a}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{A}(n)x^{2^{p_{k}+1}a}\right)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}pd_{A}({\bf p},n)x^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2^{p_{k}+1}\rfloor}(-1)^{n-2^{p_{k}+1}i}p_{A}(i)pd_{A}({\bf p},n-2^{p_{k}+1}i)\right)x^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2^{p_{k}+1}\rfloor}p_{A}(i)pd_{A}({\bf p},n-2^{p_{k}+1}i)\right)x^{n}

and it is clear that
(-1)^{n}S_{B,0}(n)\geq 0
.

Next observe that for
n
odd we have

\displaystyle\delta^{(i)}(p_{B,n}(-1))=\sum_{a\in B(n)}\left(\frac{n}{a}\right)^{i}(-1)^{\frac{n}{a}}.

and this sum is smaller than zero because
(-1)^{n/a}=-1
. If
n
is even, i.e.,.
n=2^{\nu_{2}(n)}m
, where
\nu_{2}(n)
is the highest power of 2 which divides
n
, then we can group elements of
B_{2^{\nu_{2}(n)}m}
into
M+1
-tuples
(2^{p_{0}},a,2^{p_{1}}a,\ldots,2^{p_{M}}a)
with
a
odd and

M=\operatorname{max}\{i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}:\;p_{i}\leq\nu_{2}(n)\}.

We thus have

\displaystyle\delta^{(i)}(p_{B,n}(-1))

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in A(m)}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{M}\left(\frac{n}{2^{p_{s}}a}\right)^{i}(-1)^{\frac{n}{2^{p_{s}}a}}\right).

It is clear that for any given
n
, the only possible value of
s\in\{0,\ldots,M\}
for which
\frac{n}{2^{p_{s}}a}
may be an odd integer is
s=M
and additionally the equality
p_{M}=\nu_{2}(n)
need to be satisfied. We thus get that for each
i,n\in\mathbb{N}
we have the inequality
(-1)^{n}\delta^{(i)}(p_{B,n}(t))\mid_{t=-1}\geq 0
.

We are ready to finish the proof that
(-1)^{n}S_{B,k}(n)\geq 0
. We proceed by induction on
k
. We already proved that the statement is true for
k=0
. Let us assume that it holds for
j=0,1,\ldots,k
. Therefore,

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}S_{B,k+1}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\{(-1)^{i}\delta^{(j)}(p_{B,i}(t))\mid_{t=-1}\}\{(-1)^{n-i}S_{B,k-j}(n-i)\}.

As we observed, the first expression in the curly bracket is non-negative. The second is non-negative because of our induction assumption, and hence we get the result.

To get the result for
m=+\infty
we need the following observation. For a given
A\subset\mathbb{N}_{+}
and given
n\in\mathbb{N}
we put

U(n)=\{a\in A:\;a\leq n\}=A\cap[1,n].

Let us observe that for a given
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
n\leq N
we have the identity

f_{A,n}(t)=f_{U(N),n}(t).

In consequence, for a given
k\in\mathbb{N},n\leq N
, we have the equality
S_{A,k}(n)=S_{U(N),k}(n)
.

As a consequence of the above property we see that if
m=+\infty
and
n
is given, there is a
s\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that
n<2^{p_{i}}
for
i\geq s
. Indeed, the sequence
{\bf p}
is increasing. Hence, if we put
{\bf p}^{\prime}=(p_{i})_{i\leq s}
then we have the equality

f_{A,n}(t)=f_{U(2^{p_{s}}),n}(t)

and hance we can apply our statement for the finite set
{\bf p}^{\prime}
and get the required result.

∎

The combination of Lemma
3.3
and Theorem
3.4
is a very useful tool which can be used to prove sign alternating property of the sequence
(S_{A,k}(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for several sets
A
. We start with the following.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and put
A=\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
. Then, for each
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
we have
(-1)^{n}S_{A,k}(n)\geq 0
.

Proof.

To get the result it is enough to observe the equality of sets

A=\{1,\ldots,m\}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\lceil m/2\rceil}(2i-1)\{1,2,\ldots,2^{\lfloor\log_{2}(m/(2i-1))\rfloor}\}

and apply Theorem
3.4
together with Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
A=\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Then for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
(-1)^{n}S_{A,k}(n)\geq 0
.

Proof.

To get the result it is enough to note the equality

\mathbb{N}_{+}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}(2\mathbb{N}+1)2^{i}

and apply Theorem
3.4
with
p_{i}=i
and
A=2\mathbb{N}+1
-the set of odd numbers.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
be odd or be a power of two. Let us put
B=\{m^{i}:\;i\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Then for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
(-1)^{n}S_{B,k}(n)\geq 0
.

Proof.

If
m
is odd then we directly apply Lemma
3.3
. If
m=2^{s}
for
s\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
then we apply the decomposition into pairwise disjoint sets

B=\bigcup_{j=0}^{\infty}\{1\}2^{js}.

and apply Theorem
3.4
with
p_{j}=js
for
j=1,2\ldots
and
A=\{1\}
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Let us note that if
m=2
then we get that the
[
3
, Conjecture 5.1]
is true.

Our results shows that the alternating behaviour of the signs of the sums
S_{A,k}(n)
is quite typical. This may suggest that for all sets
A
, each
k\in\mathbb{N}
and sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have
(-1)^{n}S_{A,k}(n)\geq 0
. This is clearly not the case. We prove the following.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N},a,b\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and take
A=\{a,b\}
with
a<b
and
(a,b)=1
. If
k=0
then the sequence
(\operatorname{sign}(S_{A,k}(n)))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is eventually periodic of period
2ab
.

If
k>0
then the sequence
(\operatorname{sign}(S_{A,k}(n)))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is eventually periodic of period
2a(b-a)
.

Proof.

Let us recall that
S_{A,k}(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}i^{k}c_{A}(i,n)
, where
c_{A}(i,n)
is the number of
A
-partitions of
n
with exactly
i
-parts. Because we have only two elements so we can calculate
c_{A}(i,n)
directly as the number of non-negative solutions
(x,y)
of the linear system of equations:

(1)

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}xa+yb&=n\\
x+y&=i\end{array}\end{cases}

The necessary condition for solvability of (
1
) is the condition
ia\leq n\leq ib
. Equivalently we have
\frac{n}{b}\leq i\leq\frac{n}{a}.
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=xa+yb=(i-y)a+yb=y(b-a)+ia,

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{n-ia}{b-a}.

Consequently, the solution exists if and only if
i\equiv n/a\equiv r(n)\mod(b-a)
and
\frac{n}{b}\leq i\leq\frac{n}{a}
. Note that, the congruence
ai\equiv n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mub-a)
has always a solutions under due to the fact that
\gcd(a,b-a)=1|n
. Thus, we get the formula

c_{A}(i,n)=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\textrm{if $i\in[n/b,n/a]$ and $i\equiv r(n)\mod(b-a)$}\\
0&\textrm{in the remaining cases}\end{array}.\end{cases}

From our reasoning we obtain the expression for
f_{A,n}(t)
, i.e.,

f_{A,n}(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[n/b,n/a]\\
j\equiv r(n)\mod(b-a)\end{subarray}}t^{j}

and

\delta^{(k)}(f_{A,n})(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[n/b,n/a]\\
j\equiv r(n)\mod(b-a)\end{subarray}}j^{k}t^{j}

First let’s consider the case
k>0.
Assume that
n
is big enough such that the segment
[n/b,n/a]
contains at least
3(b-a)
elements.
Now substitute
-1
for
t.
If
b-a
is even then the parity of
j
is the same as the parity of
r(n)
so every term
j^{k}(-1)^{j}
has the same sign. In particular, the sign of the whole sum is the same as the sign of the last element in the sum. If
b-a
is odd then we obtain an alternating sum where every next term is bigger then the previous one, so the sign of this sum will be also the sign of the last element in the sum. Hence

\operatorname{sign}(\delta^{(k)}(f_{A,n})(-1))=\operatorname{sign}(j^{k}(-1)^{j})=\operatorname{sign}((-1)^{j}),

where
j
is the biggest number in the set
[n/b,n/a]
satisfying the condition
j\equiv r(n)\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu(b-a))
.
Now if we add
a(b-a)
to
n
then
n/a
will increase by
b-a
and
r(n)
will not change. So our maximal
j
will be now our previous maximal
j
plus
b-a
and
from our reasoning it is clear that the period of our sum is a divisor of the number
2a(b-a).

For
k=0
looking at the last term is not sufficient, because if two divides
\#(\mathbb{Z}\cap[n/a,n/b])
then our sum can be zero. But that is the only difference. As in
k>0
case, if
b-a
is even then the parity of our summand
j
is the same and the sign of the whole sum is the same as the sign of the last element in the sum. If
b-a
is odd and
2|\#(\mathbb{Z}\cap[n/a,n/b])
then our sum is zero. Otherwise it has the same sign as the last element. Now, if we add
2ab
to
n
then the number
r(n)
, the parity of
\#(\mathbb{Z}\cap[n/a,n/b])
, and the parity of its last integer element stay the same. Thus we get that the period of our sign sequence is a divisor of the number
2ab
.
∎

4.
Some problems and conjectures

In this section we collect some problems and conjectures which appeared during our investigations.

Conjecture 4.1
.

For any given
k\in\mathbb{N}
and finite
A\subset\mathbb{N}_{+}
with
\gcd(A)=1
, the sequence
(S_{A,k}(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is eventually periodic.

Let us consider the set
A=\{1,2,6\}
. One can show that the sequence of signs of
S_{A,k}(n)
is eventually periodic with period 4 for
k=0,1
. More precisely,

\displaystyle(-1)^{\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor}S_{0,A}(n)>0\quad\mbox{for}\quad n\geq 3,

\displaystyle(-1)^{\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor}S_{1,A}(n)>0\quad\mbox{for}\quad n\geq 8.

This can be easily proved from the explicit expressions for the sequences
(S_{A,i}(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for
i=0,1
. Indeed, if
i=0
, then
S_{A,0}(n)=f_{A,n}(-1)
is just the
n
-th coefficient in the power series expansion of the rational function
F_{A}(-1,x)=1/(1+x)(1+x^{2})(1+x^{6})
. Similarly, if
i=1
we need to investigate coefficients of the power series expansion of the rational function

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{A,1}(n)x^{n}=\partial_{t}F(t,x)\mid_{t=-1}=-\frac{x\left(3x^{6}+2x^{5}-x^{4}-x^{3}+x^{2}+x+1\right)}{(1+x)^{2}(1+x^{2})(1+x^{6})^{2}}.

However, one can observe that
(-1)^{n}S_{A,k}(n)>0
for
k\geq 2
and
n\gg 0
. This and related numerical observations suggest the following

Problem 4.2
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{N}_{+}
and suppose that for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
(-1)^{n}S_{A,k}(n)\geq 0
for
n\gg 0
. Then for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
we have
(-1)^{n}S_{A,k+i}(n)\geq 0
for
n\gg 0
.

During our investigations we obtained several results concerning periodic behaviour of the sequence of signs of the sequence
(S_{A,k}(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. It is an interesting question whether we can find a set
A\subset\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that the sign behaviour of the corresponding sequence (for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
) is not periodic.

Conjecture 4.3
.

Let
A=\{3n+1:\;n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. The sequence
(\operatorname{sign}(f_{A,n}(-1)))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not periodic.

We expect that the following is true

Conjecture 4.4
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
and take
U_{m}=\{i!:\;i=1,\ldots,m\}
. Then, for each
k\in\{0,\ldots,m-2\}

(-1)^{\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor}S_{U_{m},k}(n)\geq 0\quad\mbox{for}\quad n\gg 0,

i.e., the sequence of signs of the sequence
(S_{U_{m},k}(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is eventually periodic of period 4. Moreover, if
k\geq m-1
then
(-1)^{n}S_{U_{m},k}(n)\geq 0
for
n\gg 0
.

If the above conjecture is true then clearly implies that if
A=\{i!:\;i\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\}
, then for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence
(\operatorname{sign}(S_{A,0}(n)))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is eventually periodic of period 4.

Problem 4.5
.

Explain the behaviour of
(\operatorname{sign}(S_{A,k}(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for
A=\{m+1,\ldots,m+k\}
, where
k
is fixed.

Numerical calculations suggest that for
k\geq 3
the behaviour is alternating.

One can also go one step further and note that
S_{A,k}(n)
is just the value of the polynomial
\delta^{(k)}(f_{A,n}(t))
at the simplest root of unity
t=-1
. This suggest to investigate the following general

Problem 4.6
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
and
\zeta_{d}
be the
d
-th primitive root of unity. Let us write

\delta^{(k)}(f_{A,n}(t))\mid_{t=\zeta_{d}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\varphi(d)}u^{(d)}_{A,k}(i,n)\zeta_{d}^{i}.

What can be said about the sign behaviour of the family of sequences
(u^{(d)}_{A,k}(i,n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
i=0,1,\ldots,n-1
are fixed? Especially interesting is the case of
k=0
.

Numerical calculations suggest that we can not expect periodic behaviour even in the basic case when
A=\mathbb{N}_{+}
. In fact, based on our calculations we expect that there are arbitrary long sequences of consecutive positive and negative values of
u^{(4)}_{A,0}(i,n)
for
t=\zeta_{4}
, i.e.,
\zeta_{4}^{2}+1=0
,
i=0,1
. On the other hand, if
A=\{2^{i}:\;i\in\mathbb{N}\}
then in
[
3
, Corollary 4.2]
the authors proved, among other things, that

\sum_{i=0}^{4n+2}(-1)^{i}u^{(4)}_{A,0}(2i,8n+4)=\sum_{i=0}^{4n+1}(-1)^{i}u^{(4)}_{A,0}(2i+1,8n+4)=0,

which is a consequence of the vanishing of the value at
t=\zeta_{4}
of
8n+4
-th
A
-partition polynomial. Thus, a deeper study of Problem
4.6
may reveals interesting combinatorial identities.

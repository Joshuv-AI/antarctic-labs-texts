Barrett–Johnson inequalities for totally nonnegative matrices
Mark Skandera, Daniel Soskin

Abstract
. Given a matrix A A , let A I , J A_{I,J} denote the submatrix of A A determined by rows I I and columns J J . Fischer’s Inequalities state that for each n × n n\times n Hermitian positive semidefinite matrix A A , and each subset I I of { 1 , … , n } \{1,\dotsc,n\} and its complement I c I^{c} , we have det ( A ) ≤ det ( A I , I ) ​ det ( A I c , I c ) \det(A)\leq\det(A_{I,I})\det(A_{I^{c},I^{c}}) . Barrett and Johnson (Linear Multilinear Algebra 34, 1993) extended these to state inequalities for sums of products of principal minors whose orders are given by nonincreasing integer sequences ( λ 1 , … , λ r ) (\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{r}) , ( μ 1 , … , μ s ) (\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{s}) summing to n n . Specifically, if λ 1 + ⋯ + λ i ≤ μ 1 + ⋯ + μ i \lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{i}\leq\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{i} for all i i , then λ 1 ! ⋯ λ r ! ∑ ( I 1 , … , I r ) det ( A I 1 , I 1 ) ⋯ det ( A I r , I r ) ≥ μ 1 ! ⋯ μ s ! ∑ ( J 1 , … , J s ) det ( A J 1 , J 1 ) ⋯ det ( A J s , J s ) , \lambda_{1}!\cdots\lambda_{r}!\negthickspace\negthickspace\sum_{(I_{1},\dotsc,I_{r})}\negthickspace\negthickspace\det(A_{I_{1},I_{1}})\cdots\det(A_{I_{r},I_{r}})~\geq~\mu_{1}!\cdots\mu_{s}!\negthickspace\negthickspace\sum_{(J_{1},\dotsc,J_{s})}\negthickspace\negthickspace\det(A_{J_{1},J_{1}})\cdots\det(A_{J_{s},J_{s}}), where sums are over sequences of disjoint subsets of { 1 , … , n } \{1,\dotsc,n\} satisfying | I k | = λ k |I_{k}|=\lambda_{k} , | J k | = μ k |J_{k}|=\mu_{k} . We show that these inequalities hold for totally nonnegative matrices as well.



1.
Introduction

A matrix
A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C})
is called
Hermitian

if it satisfies
A^{*}=A
where
*
denotes conjugate transpose.
Such a matrix is called
Hermitian positive semi-definite
(HPSD) if we have
x^{*}Ax\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

For
A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{R})
, the Hermitian property reduces to symmetry
A^{\negthickspace\top\negthickspace}=A
, and the positive semidefinite (PSD) property reduces to
x^{\negthickspace\top\negthickspace}Ax\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hermitian matrices are fundamental to quantum mechanics since they describe operators with real eigenvalues. Positive definite (semi-definite) matrices are one of the matrix analogs of positive (nonnegative) numbers, and correspond to inner products (nonnegative quadratic forms). They appear in many fields such as geometry, numerical analysis, optimization, quantum physics, and statistics. See
[
2
]
and
[
26
]
.

Another matrix analog of nonnegative numbers is the
class of
totally nonnegative
(TNN) matrices,
those matrices in

\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{R})

in which each minor (determinant of a square submatrix) is
nonnegative. Initially, total nonnegativity arose in three different areas. It was studied by Gantmacher and Krein in oscillations of vibrating systems, by Schoenberg in applications to analysis of real roots of polynomials and spline functions, and by Karlin in integral equations and statistics. TNN matrices appear in many other areas of mathematics. (See, e.g.,
[
8
]
.)

Like TNN matrices, HPSD matrices have a well-known characterization in terms of matrix minors. Given an
n\times n
matrix
A=(a_{i,j})

and subsets
I,J\subseteq[n]:=\{1,\dotsc,n\}
,
define the submatrix
A_{I,J}=(a_{i,j})_{i\in I,j\in J}
, and define the set
I^{c}:=[n]\smallsetminus I
.
While TNN matrices are characterized by the inequalities

(1.1)

\det(A_{I,J})\geq 0,\qquad\text{for all $I,J\subseteq[n]$ with }|I|=|J|,

HPSD matrices are characterized by the equalities

(1.2)

\displaystyle a_{j,i}^{*}

\displaystyle=a_{i,j},

\displaystyle\text{for all $i,j\in[n]$},

\displaystyle\det(A_{I,I})

\displaystyle\geq 0,

\displaystyle\text{for all $I\subseteq[n]$}.

From the inequalities in (
1.1
) – (
1.2
), one can deduce many
inequalities satisfied by polynomials in the entries of
n\times n
HPSD, PSD, and/or TNN matrices.
The following inequalities hold for
HPSD matrices and TNN matrices.
Hadamard
[
13
]
showed that for
A
HPSD we have

(1.3)

\det(A)\leq a_{1,1}\cdots a_{n,n},

and Koteljanskii
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
showed that this holds for
A
TNN as well.
Marcus
[
21
]
proved a permanental analog

(1.4)

\mathrm{per}(A)\geq a_{1,1}\cdots a_{n,n}

for
A
HPSD, and this analog clearly holds for
A
TNN as well.
Fischer
[
11
]
strengthened (
1.3
) by showing that for all
I\subseteq[n]
we have

(1.5)

\det(A)\leq\det(A_{I,I})\det(A_{I^{c},I^{c}}),

and Ky Fan showed that this holds for
A
TNN as well (unpublished; see
[
5
]
).
Lieb
[
18
]
proved a permanental analog

(1.6)

\mathrm{per}(A)\geq\mathrm{per}(A_{I,I})\;\mathrm{per}(A_{I^{c},I^{c}}),

for
A
HPSD, and this analog holds for
A
TNN because every term in the expansion of the product on the right-hand side of (
1.6
) also appears on the left-hand side.
Koteljanskii
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
strengthened (
1.5
) further by proving that for all
I,J\subseteq[n]
we have

(1.7)

\det(A_{I\cup J,I\cup J})\det(A_{I\cap J,I\cap J})\leq\det(A_{I,I})\det(A_{J,J})

for
A
belonging to a class of matrices including HPSD and TNN matrices.
Strengthening (
1.3
) somewhat differently, Schur
[
27
]
proved that
for each
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-character
\chi
, the inequality

(1.8)

\det(A)\leq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi}(A)}{\chi(e)}

holds for
A
HPSD, where
\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi}
is the corresponding immanant (defined in §
3
). Stembridge
[
32
, Cor. 3.4]
showed that this holds for
A
TNN as well.

Other inequalities hold for
TNN matrices, and are not known to hold for HPSD matrices.
For instance Charles Johnson showed that the permanental analog of (
1.8
),

(1.9)

\mathrm{per}(A)\geq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi}(A)}{\chi(e)},

holds for TNN matrices (unpublished; see
[
33
, p. 1088]
). The validity of this inequality for HPSD matrices was conjectured by Lieb
[
18
]
, is still open, and is known as
the
permanental dominance conjecture
.
Extending the results (
1.5
) and (
1.7
), Fallat, Gekhtman, and Johnson
[
9
]

and the first author
[
28
]
characterized all
8
-tuples

(I,J,K,L,I^{\prime},J^{\prime},K^{\prime},L^{\prime})
of subsets of
[n]

for which we have the inequality

(1.10)

\det(A_{I,I^{\prime}})\det(A_{J,J^{\prime}})\leq\det(A_{K,K^{\prime}})\det(A_{L,L^{\prime}})

whenever
A
is TNN.
(See
[
29
]
for progress on products of three or more minors.)
Drake, Gerrish, and the first author
[
7
]
proved a similar result for products of
n
matrix entries.
In particular, the inequality

(1.11)

a_{1,v_{1}}\negthinspace\cdots{a}_{n,v_{n}}\geq a_{1,w_{1}}\negthinspace\cdots{a}_{n,w_{n}}

holds for all TNN
A
if and only if
v\leq w
in the Bruhat order on
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. (See, e.g.,
[
3
]
.)
Other linear combinations of the monomials in (
1.11
) which produce valid inequalities for TNN matrices are related to Lusztig’s work on canonical bases of quantum groups
[
20
]
and are called
Kazhdan-Lusztig immanants
in
[
25
]
.
Writing these as

\{\mathrm{Imm}_{w}(A)\,|\,w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\}
, we have

(1.12)

\mathrm{Imm}_{w}(A)\geq 0

for all
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and all TNN
A
.
(See
[
25
]
for definitions.)
We will define and use special cases of these,
called
Temperley-Lieb immanants
in
[
24
]
, in Section
4
.

Still other inequalities hold for HPSD matrices and are not known to hold for TNN matrices.
Heyfron
[
14
]
extended (
1.8
) and proved special cases of the permanental dominance conjecture (
1.9
) by considering irreducible
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-characters
indexed by ”hook” shapes
\lambda=(k,1,\dotsc,1)
. (See Sections
2
–
3
for definitions.)
In particular, we have

(1.13)

\mathrm{per}(A)=\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{n}}(A)}{\chi^{n}(e)}\geq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{n-1,1}}(A)}{\chi^{n-1,1}(e)}\geq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{n-2,1,1}}(A)}{\chi^{n-2,1,1}(e)}\geq\cdots\geq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{1,\cdots,1}}(A)}{\chi^{1,\cdots,1}(e)}=\det(A)

for
A
HPSD. (See
[
23
]
for more results of this form.)
Borcea and Brändén showed that averages
of the products of pairs of minors appearing in (
1.5
) satisfy the log-concavity inequalities
[
4
, Cor. 3.1 (b)]

(1.14)

\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[n]\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}\frac{\det(A_{I,I})\det(A_{I^{c},I^{c}})}{\displaystyle{\binom{n}{k}}}\negthickspace\right)^{\negthickspace\negthickspace 2}\geq\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[n]\\
|I|=k+1\end{subarray}}\negthickspace\negthickspace\frac{\det(A_{I,I})\det(A_{I^{c},I^{c}})}{\displaystyle{\binom{n}{k+1}}}\negthickspace\right)\negthickspace\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[n]\\
|I|=k-1\end{subarray}}\negthickspace\negthickspace\frac{\det(A_{I,I})\det(A_{I^{c},I^{c}})}{\displaystyle{\binom{n}{k-1}}}\negthickspace\right)

for all
A
HPSD and
k=1,\dotsc,n-1
.
They also showed that these averages
satisfy the Maclaurin-type inequalities
[
4
, Cor. 3.1 (c)]

(1.15)

\left(\frac{\displaystyle{\sum}_{|I|=k+1}\det(A_{I,I})\det(A_{I^{c},I^{c}})}{\displaystyle{\binom{n}{k+1}}\det(A)}\right)^{\negthickspace k}\negthinspace\geq\left(\frac{\displaystyle{\sum}_{|I|=k}\det(A_{I,I})\det(A_{I^{c},I^{c}})}{\displaystyle{\binom{n}{k}}\det(A)}\right)^{k+1}

for all nonsingular
A
HPSD and
k=1,\dotsc,n-1
.

Nearly belonging to the HPSD list above are
the Barrett–Johnson Inequalities
[
1
]
for (real) PSD matrices. Given nonnegative integer sequences

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{r})
,
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{s})
summing to
n
, we have

(1.16)

\lambda_{1}!\cdots\lambda_{r}!\negthickspace\negthickspace\sum_{(I_{1},\dotsc,I_{r})}\negthickspace\negthickspace\det(A_{I_{1},I_{1}})\cdots\det(A_{I_{r},I_{r}})~\geq~\mu_{1}!\cdots\mu_{s}!\negthickspace\negthickspace\sum_{(J_{1},\dotsc,J_{s})}\negthickspace\negthickspace\det(A_{J_{1},J_{1}})\cdots\det(A_{J_{s},J_{s}}),

if and only if
\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{i}\leq\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{i}
for all
i
,
where the sums are over
sequences of disjoint subsets of
[n]
having cardinalities
\lambda
,
\mu
, respectively.
We will show that these inequalities also hold for TNN matrices.

In Section
2
, we review basic facts about the symmetric group, its traces (class functions), and symmetric functions.
In Section
3
we apply these ideas to form certain polynomials called
immanants
in the entries of
n\times n
matrices, and we discuss the evaluation of these at TNN matrices.
In Section
4
, we discuss the Temperley–Lieb algebra, related immanants,
and their relationship to products of matrix minors.
In Section
5
, we present our main result
that the Barrett–Johnson inequalities hold for
all
n\times n
TNN matrices.

2.
The symmetric group, its traces, and symmetric functions

The
symmetric group algebra

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
is
generated over
\mathbb{C}
by
s_{1},\dotsc,s_{n-1}
, subject to relations

\displaystyle s_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=e

\displaystyle\text{for $i=1,\dotsc,n-1$},

\displaystyle s_{i}s_{j}s_{i}

\displaystyle=s_{j}s_{i}s_{j}

\displaystyle\text{for $|i-j|=1$},

\displaystyle s_{i}s_{j}

\displaystyle=s_{j}s_{i}

\displaystyle\text{for $|i-j|\geq 2$}.

We define the
one-line notation

w_{1}\cdots w_{n}
of
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
by
letting any expression for
w
act on the word
1\cdots n
,
where each generator
s_{j}=s_{[j,j+1]}
acts on an
n
-letter word by
swapping the letters in positions
j
and
j+1
, i.e.,

s_{j}\circ v_{1}\cdots v_{n}=v_{1}\cdots v_{j-1}v_{j+1}v_{j}v_{j+2}\cdots v_{n}
. Whenever
s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{\ell}}
is a reduced (short as possible) expression for
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we call
\ell
the
length

of
w
and write
\ell=\ell(w)
. It is known that
\ell(w)
is equal
to
\textsc{inv}(w)
, the number of inversions
in

w_{1}\cdots w_{n}
.
It is also known that conjugacy classes in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
are precisely the set of permutations that have the same cycle type. We name cycle type by integer
partitions of
n
, the
weakly decreasing positive integer sequences

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{\ell})
satisfying

\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}=n
.
The
\ell=\ell(\lambda)
components of
\lambda
are called its
parts
,
and we let
|\lambda|=n
and
\lambda\vdash n
denote that

\lambda
is a partition of
n
.
Given
\lambda\vdash n
,
we define the
transpose
partition

\lambda^{\negthickspace\top\negthickspace}=(\lambda^{\negthickspace\top\negthickspace}_{1},\dotsc,\lambda^{\negthickspace\top\negthickspace}_{\lambda_{1}})

by

\lambda^{\negthickspace\top\negthickspace}_{i}=\#\{j\,|\,\lambda_{j}\geq i\}.

Sometimes it is convenient to name a partition with exponential notation,
omitting parentheses and commas, so that

4^{2}1^{6}:=(4,4,1,1,1,1,1,1)
. Sometimes it is convenient to partially order partitions of
n
by the
majorization order
,

(2.1)

\lambda\preceq\mu\quad\text{iff}\quad\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{i}\leq\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{i}

for all
i
. It is known that if
\lambda
is covered by
\mu
in this partial order then
\lambda
,
\mu
have equal parts except in two positions
i<j
where we have

(2.2)

\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i}+1,\qquad\mu_{j}=\lambda_{j}-1.

Let
\Lambda
be the ring of symmetric functions in

x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc)
having integer coefficients, and
let
\Lambda_{n}
be the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of homogeneous functions
of degree
n
.
This submodule has rank equal to the number of partitions of
n
.
Five standard bases of
\Lambda_{n}
consist of the monomial
\{m_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\vdash n\}
,
elementary
\{e_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\vdash n\}
,
(complete) homogenous
\{h_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\vdash n\}
,
power sum
\{p_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\vdash n\}
,
and Schur
\{s_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\vdash n\}

symmetric functions.
(See, e.g.,
[
30
, Ch. 7]
for definitions.)

The change-of-basis matrix relating

\{e_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\vdash n\}
and

\{m_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\vdash n\}
is given by the equations

(2.3)

e_{\lambda}=\sum_{\mu\preceq\lambda^{\negthickspace\top\negthickspace}}M_{\lambda,\mu}m_{\mu},

where
M_{\lambda,\mu}
equals the number of column-strict Young tableaux
of shape
\lambda^{\negthickspace\top\negthickspace}
and content
\mu
. That is,
M_{\lambda,\mu}
is the number of histograms having
\lambda_{i}
boxes in column
i
for all
i
, filled with
\mu_{1}
ones,
\mu_{2}
twos, etc., with column entries strictly increasing from bottom to top.
For example we have

e_{221}=m_{32}+2m_{311}+5m_{221}+12m_{2111}+30m_{11111},

and the coefficients
M_{221,32}=1
of
m_{32}
,

M_{221,311}=2
of
m_{311}
,

M_{221,221}=5
of
m_{221}

3.
Immanants and totally nonnegative polynomials

Each of the inequalities stated in Section

]

Proposition 4.1
.

Proof.

This is clear if

Lemma 4.2

Let

of cardinality

\alpha(\tau,\kappa)+\beta(\tau,\kappa)=\alpha(\tau,\kappa^{\prime})+\beta(\tau,\kappa^{\prime}).

(n-j)-j=n-2j.

By this lemma, we may write

Extracting the coefficients

Proposition 4.3
.

Each immanant of the form

\mathrm{Imm}_{f}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\subseteq[n]\\

.

Theorem 4.4

For

\det(x_{I,I})\det(x_{I^{c},I^{c}})=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{B}_{n}}b_{\tau}\mathrm{Imm}_{\tau}(x),

By (

Theorem 4.5
.

For

The combinatorial description follows immediately from Theorem

Combining (

For

Let

5.
Main results

Fischer’s inequalities (
1.5
) naturally lead to the questions of how products

(5.1)

\det(A_{I,I})\det(A_{I^{c},I^{c}})

of complementary pairs of minors compare to one another, and of whether a greater cardinality difference
|I^{c}|-|I|
tends to make the product (
5.1
) greater or smaller.
This second question led Barrett and Johnson
[
1
]
to consider the average value of such products when cardinalities are fixed,

(5.2)

\frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[n]\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}\det(A_{I,I})\det(A_{I^{c},I^{c}}).

They found that for PSD matrices, a smaller cardinality difference makes the average product of minors greater
[
1
, Thm. 1]
.
We give two proofs that the same is true for TNN matrices.

Theorem 5.1
.

For all TNN matrices A and for
k=0,\dotsc,\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\rfloor-1,
we have

(5.3)

\frac{\displaystyle{\sum}_{|I|=k}\det(A_{I,I})\det(A_{I^{c},I^{c}})}{\displaystyle{\binom{n}{k}}}\leq\frac{\displaystyle{\sum}_{|I|=k+1}\det(A_{I,I})\det(A_{I^{c},I^{c}})}{\displaystyle{\binom{n}{k+1}}}.

First proof.

By (
3.2
) it is equivalent to show that the polynomial

(5.4)

\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{n-k-1,k+1}}(x)}{\displaystyle{\binom{n}{k+1}}}-\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{n-k,k}}(x)}{\displaystyle{\binom{n}{k}}}

is totally nonnegative. By Theorem
4.5
, this difference belongs to the span of the Temperley-Lieb immanants. In particular, we may multiply the difference by
n!/(k!(n-k-1)!)
to obtain

(5.5)

(k+1)\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{n-k-1,k+1}}(x)-(n-k)\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{n-k,k}}(x)=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{B}_{n}}c_{\tau}\mathrm{Imm}_{\tau}(x),

where

(5.6)

c_{\tau}=(k+1)d_{k+1,\tau}-(n-k)d_{k,\tau}.

and
d_{k+1,\tau}
,
d_{k,\tau}
are defined in terms of proper colorings of
\tau
as in Theorem
4.5

We claim that
c_{\tau}\geq 0
.
Suppose we have a proper coloring of
\tau
in which
k
vertices on the left are white. Let
\alpha=\alpha(\tau,k)
and
\beta=\beta(\tau,k)
be the numbers of right-unbalanced and left-unbalanced subgraphs of
\tau
respectively, as in
4.10
.

Let
d
be the number of cycles defined as in Theorem
4.5
. Then by Theorem
4.5
we have that (
5.6
) equals

(5.7)

\displaystyle(k+1)d_{k+1,\tau}-(n-k)d_{k,\tau}

\displaystyle=(k+1)2^{d-\alpha-\beta}\binom{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha-1}-(n-k)2^{d-\alpha-\beta}\binom{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha}

\displaystyle=2^{d-\alpha-\beta}\left((k+1)\binom{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha-1}-(n-k)\binom{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{d-\alpha-\beta}\frac{(\alpha+\beta)!}{(\alpha-1)!\beta!}\left(\frac{k+1}{\beta+1}-\frac{n-k}{\alpha}\right).

Then substituting
\alpha=n-2k+\beta
(see formula
4.8
), the final difference of fractions (
5.7
) becomes

\frac{(k+1)(n-2k+\beta)-(n-k)(\beta+1)}{(\beta+1)(n-2k+\beta)}.

This denominator is clearly positive. The numerator is
(n-2k-1)(k-\beta)
,
which is nonnegative because of the bounds on
k
and the definition of
\beta
.
∎

Second proof.

Again we show that the polynomial (
5.4
) is TNN.
Multiplying the polynomial by

\tfrac{n!}{k!(n-k-1)!}
and expanding it in the monomial immanant
basis of the trace immanant space, we have

(5.8)

(n-k)\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{(k,n-k)}}(x)-(k+1)\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{(k+1,n-k-1)}}(x)=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}c_{\mu}\mathrm{Imm}_{\phi^{\mu}}(x)

where the integers
\{c_{\mu}\,|\,\mu\vdash n\}
satisfy

(5.9)

(n-k)e_{(k,n-k)}-(k+1)e_{(k+1,n-k-1)}=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}c_{\mu}m_{\mu}.

We claim that
c_{\mu}\geq 0
for all relevant partitions
\mu
.
To see this, consider the special case of (
2.3
)
for two-part partitions,

e_{(k,n-k)}=\sum_{a=0}^{n-k}M_{(k,n-k),2^{a}1^{n-2a}}m_{2^{a}1^{n-2a}}.

It follows that each partition
\mu
appearing with nonzero coefficient in (
5.8
)
has the form
\mu=2^{a}1^{n-2a}
.

Since each column-strict Young tableaux of shape
(k,n-k)
and content

2^{a}1^{n-2a}
contains the letters
1,1,2,2,\dotsc,a,a
in rows

1,\dotsc,a
, it is completely determined by the subset of
the
letters
\{a+1,\dotsc,n-a\}

appearing in rows
a+1,\dotsc,n-k
of column
2
.
Thus there are
\tbinom{n-2a}{n-k-a}=\tbinom{n-2a}{k-a}
such tableaux.
It follows that in the right-hand side of (
5.9
),
the coefficient of
m_{2^{a}1^{n-2a}}
is

c_{2^{a}1^{n-2a}}=\begin{cases}(n-k)\binom{n-2a}{k-a}&\text{if $a=n-k$,}\\
(n-k)\binom{n-2a}{k-a}-(k+1)\binom{n-2a}{k-a+1}&\text{if $0\leq a\leq n-k-1$.}\end{cases}

This last expression is equal to

\frac{(n-2a)!}{(k-a)!(n-k-a-1)!}\bigg(\frac{n-k}{n-k-a}-\frac{k+1}{k-a+1}\bigg)\\
=\frac{(n-2a)!}{(k-a)!(n-k-a-1)!}\bigg(\frac{2k+1-n}{(n-k-a)(k-a+1)}\bigg),

which is positive, since

a<n-k\leq\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\rfloor\leq k\leq n
.

Now by Theorem
4.6

we have that
(
5.8
)
is a totally nonnegative polynomial, and that
(
5.4
) is as well.
∎

Consideration of the average value of products of two complentary minors (
5.2
) naturally led Barrett and Johnson to consider average values of products of arbitrarily many minors having fixed cardinality sequences
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{r})\vdash n
,

(5.10)

\frac{1}{\binom{n}{\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{r}}}\sum_{(I_{1},\dotsc,I_{r})}\det(A_{I_{1},I_{1}})\cdots\det(A_{I_{r},I_{r}}),

where the sum is over ordered set partitions of
[n]
of type
\lambda
and the multinomial coefficient
\binom{n}{\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{r}}=\tfrac{n!}{\lambda_{1}!\cdots\lambda_{r}!}
is the number of such ordered set partitions. They found that for PSD matrices, partitions which appear lower in the majorization order lead to greater average products
[
1
, Thm. 2]
. We show that the same is true for TNN matrices.

Theorem 5.2
.

Fix
n>0
and partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{r})\vdash n
,

\mu=(\mu_{1}\dotsc,\mu_{s})\vdash n
with
\lambda\preceq\mu
.
For all TNN matrices we have

(5.11)

\lambda_{1}!\cdots\lambda_{r}!\negthickspace\negthickspace\sum_{(I_{1},\dotsc,I_{r})}\negthickspace\negthickspace\det(A_{I_{1},I_{1}})\cdots\det(A_{I_{r},I_{r}})~\geq~\mu_{1}!\cdots\mu_{s}!\negthickspace\negthickspace\sum_{(J_{1},\dotsc,J_{s})}\negthickspace\negthickspace\det(A_{J_{1},J_{1}})\cdots\det(A_{J_{s},J_{s}}),

Proof.

By (
2.2
) it suffices to consider
\lambda
,
\mu
having equal parts except

\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i}+1,\qquad\mu_{j}=\lambda_{j}-1

for some
i<j
. (Thus we may assume
s=r
and will allow
\mu_{s}=0
.)
Let
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dotsc,\nu_{r-2})
be the partition of
n-\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}
consisting of all other parts.

As in the proofs of Theorem
5.1
, we observe that each sum of products of minors is an induced sign character immanant (
3.2
). Thus the inequality (
5.11
) is equivalent to the total nonnegativity of the polynomial

(5.12)

\lambda_{1}!\cdots\lambda_{r}!\;\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{\lambda}}(x)-\mu_{1}!\cdots\mu_{r}!\;\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{\mu}}(x).

Dividing this by
\nu_{1}!\cdots\nu_{r-2}!\lambda_{i}!\mu_{j}!
and collecting terms, we obtain

\begin{gathered}\lambda_{j}\negthickspace\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq[n]\\
|J|=|\nu|\end{subarray}}\negthickspace\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{\nu}}(x_{J,J})\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})}}(x_{J^{c},J^{c}})-\mu_{i}\negthickspace\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq[n]\\
|J|=|\nu|\end{subarray}}\negthickspace\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{\nu}}(x_{J,J})\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{(\lambda_{i}+1,\lambda_{j}-1)}}(x_{J^{c},J^{c}})\\

|J|=|\nu|\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{\nu}}(x_{J,J})\left(\lambda_{j}\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})}}(x_{J^{c},J^{c}})-\mu_{i}\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{(\lambda_{i}+1,\lambda_{j}-1)}}(x_{J^{c},J^{c}})\right).\end{gathered}

By Theorem
5.1
, or more precisely (
5.5
),
this polynomial is TNN and so is (
5.12
).
∎

Quantum de Finetti Theorems as Categorical Limits, and Limits of State Spaces of C*-algebras
Sam Staton Ned Summers

Abstract
De Finetti theorems tell us that if we expect the likelihood of outcomes to be independent of their order, then these sequences of outcomes could be equivalently generated by drawing an experiment at random from a distribution, and repeating it over and over. In particular, the quantum de Finetti theorem says that exchangeable sequences of quantum states are always represented by distributions over a single state produced over and over. The main result of this paper is that this quantum de Finetti construction has a universal property as a categorical limit. This allows us to pass canonically between categorical treatments of finite dimensional quantum theory and the infinite dimensional. The treatment here is through understanding properties of (co)limits with respect to the contravariant functor which takes a C*-algebra describing a physical system to its convex, compact space of states, and through discussion of the Radon probability monad. We also show that the same categorical analysis also justifies a continuous de Finetti theorem for classical probability.

2.1
Rudiments of Probability Theory

We begin by recalling some measure-theoretic probability theory.

Definition 2.1

(Probability Measure)
.

For a set
X
,
a
\sigma
-algebra for
X
is a collection of subsets of
X
,
\Sigma_{X}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
, which contains
X
and is closed under countable unions and complements.

A measurable space
is a pair
(X,\Sigma_{X})
of a set
X
and a
\sigma
-algebra
\Sigma_{X}
on
X
.

In what follows, we are almost exclusively concerned with measures on topological spaces. The
Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathit{Borel}\left(X\right)
on a topological space
X
is the smallest
\sigma
-algebra generated by the open sets of
X
.
Additionally, when we refer to finite or countable sets, we consider them as measurable spaces with the
\sigma
-algebra of all possible subsets.

A measurable function
between measurable spaces
(X,\Sigma_{X})
and
(Y,\Sigma_{Y})
is a function
f\colon X\to Y
such that for any
S\in\Sigma_{Y}
, we have
f^{-1}\left(S\right)\in\Sigma_{X}
. Continuous functions between topological spaces
(X,\mathit{Borel}\left(X\right))\to(Y,\mathit{Borel}\left(Y\right))
are measurable, though not all measurable functions are continuous.

A probability measure on a measurable space
(X,\Sigma_{X})
is a function
\mu\colon\Sigma_{X}\to[0,1]
such that
\mu(X)=1
and for a disjoint countable collection of sets
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in N}\subset\Sigma_{X}
,
\mu\left(\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}U_{i}\right)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mu(U_{i})
. Given a probability measure
\mu\colon\Sigma_{X}\to[0,1]
and a measurable function
f\colon X\to Y
, the
pushforward of
\mu
by
f
, a probability measure on
Y
, is denoted
f_{\ast}\mu
and given by
f_{\ast}\mu(S):=\mu\left(f^{-1}(S)\right)
for measurable
S\in\Sigma_{Y}
.

All our topological spaces will be compact, Hausdorff spaces, so we will only use measures that behave well with the compactness of the space. Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space.
A Radon probability measure on
X
,
\mu\colon\mathit{Borel}\left(X\right)\to[0,1]
, is a probability measure on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra which is
inner regular
: for any measurable
S\subseteq X
,
\mu(S)=\sup_{K\subseteq S}\mu(K)
where
K
varies over all compact subsets. For any continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
, a Radon probability measure
\mu
induces an integral

\int_{x\in X}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\in\mathbb{C}
, so that we can regard
\mu
as a map from the set (in fact, space) of continuous functions on
X
to
\mathbb{C}
(see Thm.
2.13
, §
2.5
).

2.2
Kolmogorov Extension Theorem and Hewitt-Savage de Finetti Theorem

Kolmogorov’s extension theorem connects measures on infinite product spaces with measures on finite truncations. Recall that for a set of topological spaces
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, their product has underlying set
\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}
and has topology generated by the cylinder sets
V[U_{i_{1}},\dots,U_{i_{n}}]=\left\{(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}\,\middle|\,x_{i_{k}}\in U_{i_{k}}\text{ for }1\leq k\leq n\right\}
, varying over all finite subsets
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\}\subset I
and open sets
U_{i_{k}}\subset X_{i_{k}}
.

Theorem 2.2

(Kolmogorov Extension Theorem (e.g.
[
22
]
))
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space. Let
X^{\mathbb{N}}
be the countable product of copies of
X
. For each finite
N\subset\mathbb{N}
, let
\mu_{N}
be a Radon probability measure on
X^{N}
with the product topology, with the condition that if we take finite subsets
M\subset N\subset\mathbb{N}
,
\mu_{M}
is the pushforward of
\mu_{N}
by the projection
X^{N}\to X^{M}
. Then there exists a unique probability measure
\mu
on
X^{\mathbb{N}}
such that, for any finite
N\subset\mathbb{N}
,
\mu_{N}
is the pushforward of
\mu
by the projection
X^{\mathbb{N}}\to X^{N}
. Further, this measure is itself Radon.

From here we can now define an exchangeable measure.

Definition 2.3

(Exchangeable Measure)
.

Let
X
be a measurable space. Let
\mu
be a measure on
X^{\mathbb{N}}
. For each permutation
\sigma\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, there is an isomorphism

\displaystyle\eta_{\sigma}\colon X^{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\to X^{\mathbb{N}}\qquad\eta_{\sigma}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dots)=(x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},x_{\sigma^{-1}(2)},x_{\sigma^{-1}(3)},\dots).

\mu
is called
exchangeable
if, for every permutation
\sigma\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
which fixes all but a finite number of elements, we have that
(\eta_{\sigma})_{\ast}\mu=\mu
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space. We define
\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)
to be the set of all
Radon probability measures on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
X
, made into a compact Hausdorff space with the topology generated by the open sets
\{\mu\in\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)\colon\int_{x\in X}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\in U\}
for
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}
open,
f\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
continuous.

Given a Radon measure
\mu
on
X
, by Kolmogorov extension, there is a Radon measure
\tilde{\mu}
on
X^{\mathbb{N}}
defined on the basis of cylinder set opens by
\tilde{\mu}\left(V\left[U_{n_{1}},\dots,U_{n_{k}}\right]\right)=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mu\left(U_{n_{i}}\right)
for
U_{n_{i}}\subset X
open. This is because for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is a unique Radon measure on
X^{n}
, denoted by
\mu^{n}
, which has
\mu^{n}(U_{1}\times\dots\times U_{n})=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mu\left(U_{i}\right)
for
U_{i}\in\mathit{Borel}\left(X\right)
.

Theorem 2.4

(Hewitt-Savage de Finetti Theorem
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{R}\left(X^{\mathbb{N}}\right)
be an exchangeable Radon probability measure on the countably infinite product of copies of
X
, with the Borel
\sigma
-algebra. Then there exists a Radon probability measure
\nu
on
\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)
(i.e.
\nu\in\mathcal{R}\left(\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)\right)
) such that, for all measurable
U\subseteq X^{\mathbb{N}}
,

\mu(U)=\int_{p\in\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)}\tilde{p}(U)\,\mathrm{d}\nu.

Example 2.5
.

Supposing that we are modelling coin flips, so
X=\{H,T\}
, then
\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)\cong[0,1]
, and the de Finetti result says that an exchangeable distribution on sequences of
H
and
T
can only come from picking from bag of coins with bias distributed according to some distribution on

and then flipping the coin you have picked over and over forever.

2.3
Rudiments of
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras and Gelfand duality

Definition 2.6

(C*-Algebra)
.

An
algebra

V
(over
\mathbb{C}
) is a vector space
V
over
\mathbb{C}
equipped with a binary operation of
multiplication
,
\cdot\colon V\times V\to V
, which is bilinear. If this multiplication is commutative, then
V
is a
commutative algebra
.
We will assume that all algebras are
unital
, i.e. have a multiplicative unit.

A
Banach algebra
is an algebra
V
equipped with a norm
\|\cdot\|
such that
V
is complete with respect to
\|\cdot\|
and for all
x,y\in V
,
\|x\cdot y\|\leq\|x\|\|y\|
.
A
∗
-algebra
is a algebra
V
that is equipped with an involution: a function
(-)^{\ast}\colon V\to V
that is is self-inverse, a multiplication antihomomorphism (i.e. it reverses multiplication) and is conjugate linear.

A bounded linear map between
∗
-algebras which preserves multiplication, the unit and involution is called a
∗
-homomorphism
.

A
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra

\mathcal{A}
is a Banach
∗
-algebra such that for all
x\in\mathcal{A}
,

\|x^{\ast}x\|=\|x\|^{2}
.

We write
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Mult}}
for the category which has as its objects
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras and
∗
-homomorphisms as its morphisms. It has as a full subcategory
\mathbf{c}\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Mult}}
of commutative
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras.

Example 2.7
.

For any Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
over
\mathbb{C}
, we denote the space of all bounded linear operators
\phi\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
by
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is the prototypical example of a
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra. The generally non-commutative multiplication is given by composition of operators, the unit is the identity map and involution is taking the adjoint of a map. The norm is the operator norm.

Theorem 2.8

(e.g.
[
18
]
, C.12)
.

Every
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra is isomorphic to a sub-algebra of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
for some
\mathcal{H}
.

Example 2.9

(Commutative
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras)
.

One important example of a
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra is the space
C(X)=\{\psi\colon X\to\mathbb{C}\,|\,\psi\text{ is continuous}\}
for some compact Hausdorff space
X
, equipped with the (topological) supremum norm:
\|f\|=\sup_{x\in X}|f(x)|<\infty
. It is an algebra with multiplication and involution defined pointwise, and is commutative. The unit is the constant map to
1
.

This extends to a duality between commutative
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras and the category
\mathbf{CH}
of compact Hausdorff spaces and continuous maps.

Theorem 2.10

(Gelfand Duality)
.

The functor
C\left(-\right)=\mathbf{Top}(-,\mathbb{C})\colon\mathbf{CH}\to\left(\mathbf{c}\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Mult}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}
, which acts on morphisms by
C\left(f\colon X\to Y\right)\colon\phi\mapsto\phi\circ f
, is an equivalence of categories.

2.4
Positivity, Probabilistic Gelfand Duality, and the Radon Monad

Definition 2.11

(Positivity in
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras)
.

For a
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, an element
x\in\mathcal{A}
is called
positive
if there is some
y\in\mathcal{A}
such that
x=y^{\ast}y
.

In
\mathbb{C}
, these are exactly the elements of the non-negative real line
\mathbb{R}_{0\leq}
. In
\mathcal{B}(H)
, for some Hilbert space
H
, these are exactly the operators
\phi\colon H\to H
such that for all
v\in H
,
\left\langle v\,\middle|\,\phi v\right\rangle\geq 0
. In
C(X)
, for some compact Hausdorff space
X
, these are the functions whose images lie exclusively in
\mathbb{R}_{0\leq}
.

A linear map between
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras,
f\colon\mathcal{A}_{1}\to\mathcal{A}_{2}
, is called
positive
if, for all
x\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
,
f(x^{\ast}x)\geq 0
. In other words, it takes positive elements of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
to positive elements of
\mathcal{A}_{2}
. If both the domain and codomain have a unit, the map is called
unital
if it takes the unit of the domain to the unit of the codomain.

We will refer to the category of
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras with positive, unital maps between them as
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}
. It has
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Mult}}
as a subcategory.

Jacobs and Furber
[
10
]
extended Gelfand duality stochastically: the addition of positive maps which are not multiplicative (i.e. not
∗
-homomorphisms) is equivalent to adding stochastic maps to
\mathbf{CH}
.

Definition 2.12

(Radon Monad, e.g.
[
10
]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space, and let
\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)
be the space of all Radon probability measures on
X
as for Thm.
2.4
above. With this topology,
\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)
is both compact and Hausdorff.

We regard
\mathcal{R}
as a monad on the category
\mathbf{CH}
of compact Hausdorff spaces and continuous maps. The functor part is given by pushforward: let
f\colon X\to Y
be a morphism in
\mathbf{CH}
; then
\mathcal{R}\left(f\right)(\mu):=f_{\ast}(\mu)
.
The unit of the monad takes
x\in X
to the Dirac measure
\delta_{x}\in\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)
, the distribution supported entirely at
x
. The multiplication is a form of marginalization, or averaging:

\displaystyle\mathit{mult}:\mathcal{R}^{2}\left(X\right)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)\qquad\mathit{mult}(\phi)(U):=\int_{\mu\in\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)}\mu(U)\,\mathrm{d}\phi(\mu)

Theorem 2.13

(Probabilistic Gelfand Duality,
[
10
]
)
.

The functor
C\left(-\right)\colon\mathcal{K}l\left(\mathcal{R}\right)\to\left(\mathbf{c}\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}
, which acts on morphisms by
C\left(f\colon X\to\mathcal{R}\left(Y\right)\right)\colon\phi\mapsto\int\phi\,\mathrm{d}f(-)
, is an equivalence of categories between the Kleisli category of the Radon monad and the opposite of the category of commutative
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras and positive unital maps.

2.5
States, State Spaces, and Convex Spaces

A particularly important class of positive, unital maps are states:

Definition 2.14
.

Positive, unital maps from a
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathbb{C}
are called
states on
\mathcal{A}
.

Under the probabilistic Gelfand duality (Thm.
2.13
), for any compact Hausdorff space
X
we have the correspondence (as sets)

\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}(C(X),\mathbb{C})\ \ \cong\ \ \mathcal{K}l\left(\mathcal{R}\right)\left(\left\{\ast\right\},X\right)\ \ =\ \ \mathbf{CH}\left(\left\{\ast\right\},\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)\right)\ \ \cong\ \ \mathcal{R}\left(X\right).

In fact, all of the objects in this correspondence have structures as convex, compact, Hausdorff spaces and are isomorphic as such. Thus it is meaningful to consider states on
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras as a generalisation of classical probability distributions.

Example 2.15

(Density Matrices are States on a
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra)
.

Density matrices in quantum theory are given by operators
\rho\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, for a finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, with
\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho\right)=1
such that for all
v\in\mathcal{H}
,
\left\langle v|\rho v\right\rangle\geq 0
. For each such
\rho
, we may define a linear map
s_{\rho}\colon\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{C}
by
s_{\rho}(a)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho a\right)
. The trace condition says this map is unital. Further, in the finite dimensional case, where we can form a eigenvalue decomposition
\rho=\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\left|v_{i}\middle\rangle\middle\langle v_{i}\right|
, then

s_{\rho}(a^{\ast}a)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho a^{\ast}a\right)=\textstyle\sum_{i}p_{i}\left\langle v_{i}\,\middle|\,a^{\ast}av_{i}\right\rangle=\sum_{i}p_{i}\left\langle av_{i}\,\middle|\,av_{i}\right\rangle=\sum_{i}p_{i}\|av_{i}\|\geq 0.

So
s_{\rho}
is a state on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. In fact, in this finite dimensional case, all states on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
are of this form.

So we can see
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra states generalise both classical and quantum probability.

Definition 2.16

(State Space of a
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra.
The state space of
\mathcal{A}
, denoted by
S\left(\mathcal{A}\right):=\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}(\mathcal{A},\mathbb{C})
, is the set of all states on
\mathcal{A}
equipped with the coarsest topology such that for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
, the evaluation function
\mathrm{ev}_{a}\colon S\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\to\mathbb{C}
which takes
\rho\mapsto\rho(a)
is continuous. The topology is generated by the sets
\mathrm{ev}^{-1}_{a}(\Omega)
for
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
open.
S\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
has the additional properties of always being Hausdorff and compact.
Moreover,

S\left(-\right)
extends to a functor
\left(\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{CH}
via
S\left(f\right)=-\circ f
.

In fact
S\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
also has an obvious convex structure where the convex combination
\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2}
for
\lambda\in[0,1]
and
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in S\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is evaluated pointwise using the addition of
\mathbb{C}
. We now recall how this convex structure is functorial.

Definition 2.17
.

For our purposes, a
compact convex space
is a pair
(V,X)
where
V
is a convex, compact subset of a locally convex, Hausdorff topological vector space
X
. The category
\mathbf{ConvCH}
has as objects compact convex spaces
(V,X)
, and morphisms are affine, continuous maps between the convex subsets
V
(only).

Theorem 2.18

(
[
10
]
)
.

The state space functor
S\colon\left(\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{CH}
factors through
\mathbf{ConvCH}
. Moreover the resulting functor
S\colon\left(\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{ConvCH}
is full and faithful.

In other words,
\left(\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}
is isomorphic to a full subcategory of
\mathbf{ConvCH}
(characterized in
[
2
]
).

Theorem 2.19

(
[
10
]
)
.

The category of Eilenberg-Moore algebras of the Radon monad,
\mathcal{E}m\left(\mathcal{R}\right)
(Def.
2.12
), is equivalent to the category
\mathbf{ConvCH}
(Def.
2.17
).

This realises the forgetful functor
U\colon\mathbf{ConvCH}\to\mathbf{CH}
as the right adjoint of the monadic adjunction
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\dashv U
for
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\colon\mathbf{CH}\to\mathbf{ConvCH}
taking a space
X
to the convex, compact Hausdorff space
\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)
.

Note then that all the objects of
\mathcal{E}m\left(\mathcal{R}\right)
are either spaces of probability measures (the free algebras, equivalently objects of
\mathcal{K}l\left(\mathcal{R}\right)
, Thm.
2.13
), or they are (category-theoretical) “quotients” of these spaces (the non-free algebras). The transition from classical to quantum probability has as a crucial part the fact that some convex mixtures of outcomes to an experiment are equivalent (for example, different decompositions of mixed states into pure states).

2.6
Tensor products and complete positivity

Recall that the tensor product of Hilbert spaces,
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes_{H}\mathcal{H}_{2}
, is the completion of the algebraic tensor product
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}
under the inner product norm.

Definition 2.20

(Spatial Tensor Product of
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
be
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras, with representations
\pi_{i}\colon\mathcal{A}_{i}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{i})
(Thm.
2.8
).
Then the map
\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\colon\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{2}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes_{H}\mathcal{H}_{2})
given by
(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2})(A_{1}\otimes A_{2})=\pi_{1}(A_{1})\otimes\pi_{2}(A_{2})
is a faithful representation of the vector space
\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{2}
and thus we can use it to give a norm to that space,
\|a\|_{\ast}=\|(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2})(a)\|
, which is independent of the choice of representations (
\pi_{i}
).
The spatial tensor product

\mathcal{A}_{1}\mathop{\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}}\mathcal{A}_{2}
is the completion with respect to this norm.

There are other tensor products definable on
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras, though this is the smallest. If a
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
is finite dimensional or commutative, then all possible
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-norms on
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
are equivalent.

Not all positive maps are physical, and now that we have defined a tensor product on
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras, we are able to define those that are.

Definition 2.21

(Completely Positive)
.

A linear map between
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras
\phi\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is called
completely positive
if, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the map

\phi\otimes 1_{n}\colon\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\to\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

is positive.

All positive maps to or from commutative spaces are completely positive.

Example 2.22
.

Let
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}
be finite dimensional Hilbert spaces. Then a completely positive and unital map
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
induces a function

S(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K}))\to S(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
between the corresponding spaces of density matrices (Ex.
2.15
); these are exactly the quantum channels (e.g.
[
21
]
).

As we noticed a few times before, much of the seemingly infinite behaviour in different formulations of the De Finetti theorem is, in fact, the result of behaviour which happens on all possible finite truncations of a process, with requirements of consistency between them.

Given a
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, we can define
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes n}:=\underbrace{\mathcal{A}\mathop{\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}}\dots\mathop{\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}}\mathcal{A}}_{n\text{ times}}
.
For
n\leq m
, there is an isometric
∗
-homomorphism embedding of
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras

\displaystyle\iota_{nm}\colon\mathcal{A}^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes m}\qquad\textstyle\iota_{nm}\Big(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\Big)\ :=\ \bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=n+1}^{m}1_{\mathcal{A}}.

2.7
The Quantum Kolmogorov Extension and de Finetti Theorems

Theorem 2.24

(Quantum Kolmogorov Extension Theorem
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\{\rho_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence with
\rho_{n}\in S\left(\mathcal{A}^{\otimes n}\right)
such that, for all
n\leq m
,
\rho_{n}=\rho_{m}\circ\iota_{nm}
. There is a unique state
\rho\in S(\mathcal{A}^{\otimes\infty})
such that
\rho_{n}=\rho\circ\psi_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
(with
\psi_{n}
as in (
6
)).

Definition 2.25

(Exchangeable State)
.

Given a permutation
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
, we may define an associated map permuting the spaces of the n-fold tensor product
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle\eta_{\sigma}\colon\mathcal{A}^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes n}\qquad\eta_{\sigma}(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n})\ :=\ a_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}

A state
\rho_{n}\in S\left(\mathcal{A}^{\otimes n}\right)
is said to be
symmetric
if, for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
,
\rho_{n}=\rho_{n}\circ\eta_{\sigma}
. A state
\rho\colon\mathcal{A}^{\otimes\infty}\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be
exchangeable
if, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\rho_{n}=\rho\circ\psi_{n}
is symmetric. Note again that we are only concerned with permutations on a finite number of factors.

The space of exchangeable states of
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes\infty}
, denoted by
\mathcal{I}\left(\mathcal{A}\right):=\{\rho\in S(\mathcal{A}^{\otimes\infty})\,|\,\rho\text{ is exchangeable}\}
is convex, compact and Hausdorff, so in
\mathbf{ConvCH}
.

Theorem 2.26

(Størmer’s Quantum de Finetti Theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra. Then there is a bi-continuous, affine bijection
\mathcal{I}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\cong\mathcal{R}\left(S\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\right)
. In other words, the exchangeable state space of
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes\infty}
is isomorphic to
S\left(C\left(S\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\right)\right)\cong\mathcal{R}\left(S\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\right)
in
\mathbf{ConvCH}
.

Remark
.

To be explicit about this isomorphism we note that given states
\rho_{1}\in S\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}\right),\rho_{2}\in S\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\right)
, we can form a unique state
\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\in S\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}\mathop{\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}}\mathcal{A}_{2}\right)
with the property that
\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})=\rho_{1}(a_{1})\rho_{2}(a_{2})
. Then, for
\rho\in S\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
, we define

\rho^{\otimes n}:=\underbrace{\rho\otimes\dots\otimes\rho}_{n\text{ times}}\in S\left(\mathcal{A}^{\otimes n}\right).

There is a state on
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes\infty}
,
\rho^{\otimes\infty}\in S\left(\mathcal{A}^{\otimes\infty}\right)
, via theorem
2.24
from the sequence
\left\{\rho^{\otimes n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

This isomorphism then is given by
-\circ\Phi\colon S\left(C\left(S\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\right)\right)\to\mathcal{I}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
where
\Phi\colon\mathcal{A}^{\otimes\infty}\to C(S\left(\mathcal{A}\right))
is defined as
\Phi(a)(\rho)=\rho^{\otimes\infty}(a)
.

(Co)limits and the State Space Functor

We now build on the prior work in Section

to characterize the categorical limits in the categories of positive unital maps between
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras (

Lemma 3.1
.

The state space functor
S\colon\left(\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{ConvCH}
(Thm.
2.18
) preserves and reflects limits.

That is to say that, given a diagram of
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras
\mathcal{A}_{-}\colon\mathcal{J}\to\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}
, a cocone
\{\mathcal{A}_{j}\to\mathcal{B}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
is a colimit if and only if the corresponding cone
\{S\left(\mathcal{B}\right)\to S\left(\mathcal{A}_{j}\right)\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
of the diagram
\left(\mathcal{J}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\left(\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}\overset{S}{\to}\mathcal{E}m\left(\mathcal{R}\right)
is a limit.

Proof.

Since
S
is full and faithful (Thm.
2.18
), it must reflect limits and colimits.

There are monadic forgetful functors from

and from

(Thm.
2.19
,
[

, VI.9]
). They create limits. The composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}m\left(\mathcal{R}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{CH}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Set}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U^{\prime}}

is just the hom-functor
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}(-,\mathbb{C})\colon\left(\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
. Thus, supposing a colimit exists in
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}
, it is preserved via
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}(-,\mathbb{C})
into a limit in

\mathbf{CH}
and then creates another in
\mathcal{E}m\left(\mathcal{R}\right)\cong\mathbf{ConvCH}
. Thus

preserves the original colimit(/limit in
\left(\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}
).

\mathbf{ConvCH}
are constructed

in the following way:

Lemma 3.2

(Pointwise limits are limits in
\mathbf{ConvCH}
)
.

Consider a diagram
\mathbf{W}_{-}\colon\mathcal{J}\to\mathbf{ConvCH}
. That is, consider a collection
\{\mathbf{W}_{i}\}_{i\in J}

{\{\xi_{k}\colon\mathbf{W}_{i}\to\mathbf{W}_{j}\}_{k\in\mathcal{J}(i,j)}}

•

is a cone over the diagram: for all
\xi_{k}\colon\mathbf{W}_{i}\to\mathbf{W}_{j}
in the diagram,
\omega_{i}=\omega_{j}\circ\xi_{k}
.

•

\xi_{k}\colon\mathbf{W}_{j}\to\mathbf{W}_{i}

with

\mathbf{W}

\mathbf{CH}
and
\mathbf{ConvCH}
.

, a cone of the diagram of

s, that is a collection of maps
\left\{f_{i}\colon A\to W_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
gives a unique map

by, for each

be the element of

corresponding to the collection
\{f_{i}(a)\in\mathbf{W}_{i}\}
under the pointwise limit property. Thus,
W
is the limit in
\mathbf{Set}
of the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{J}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{ConvCH}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{CH}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Set}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{W}_{-}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U^{\prime}}

Since
\mathbf{CH}
is monadic over
\mathbf{Set}
, then
\mathbf{W}
, regarded as a compact Hausdorff space, is created as the limit in
\mathbf{CH}
and similarly by monadicity of
\mathbf{ConvCH}
over
\mathbf{CH}
, it is created as the limit in
\mathbf{ConvCH}
.
∎

Informally, what this lemma tells us is that non-categorical, state-based treatments of limiting behaviours have been categorical all along. Looking at these things pointwise is fine because the structural properties take care of themselves.

Lemma 4.2

(Quantum de Finetti Theorem as a Categorical Limit For Positive Maps)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra. Let
\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{inj}}
be the category of the finite sets
\{1,\dots,n\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and injections between them. We define an
\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{inj}}
-indexed diagram in
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{Pos}}
by taking
\{1,\dots,n\}
to
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes n}
and, for
n\leq m
and an injection
\tau\colon\{1,\dots,n\}\hookrightarrow\{1,\dots,m\}

\eta_{\tau}\colon\mathcal{A}^{\otimes n}\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes m}\\

by taking
A_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes A_{n}
to the element

The colimit of this diagram in

In other words, the space of exchangeable sequences of states is a limit of a diagram and is affinely isomorphic to the space of Radon probability measures on

Proof.

Størmer’s proof of Thm.
2.26

[

]
gives a bi-continuous, affine bijection
\mathcal{I}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\cong\mathcal{R}\left(S\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\right)

is isomorphic to
\mathcal{R}\left(S\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\right)
in
\mathbf{ConvCH}
. All that is necessary then is to show that
\mathcal{I}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\subset S\left(\mathcal{A}^{\otimes\infty}\right)
, with the morphisms
\rho\mapsto\rho\circ\psi_{n}
(with

We do this using Lma.
3.2
.

Via Thm.
2.18

\{\eta_{\tau}\colon\mathcal{A}^{\otimes n}\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes m}\}
of C

in
\mathbf{ConvCH}
.
Since any

is symmetric we get that
\rho\mapsto\rho\circ\psi_{n}
is a cone for
\{S(\eta_{\tau})\}
.

Any collection of states

for all

and

That

Theorem 4.3

(Quantum de Finetti Theorem as a Categorical Limit For Quantum Channels)
.

The colimit of the diagram (

) in

6
Aside on the Hewitt-Savage de Finetti Theorem as a Categorical Limit

As an aside, we note that de Finetti theorem for classical probability (Thm.
2.4
) now also arises as a categorical limit. We express it first in the category
\mathbf{ConvCH}
of compact convex spaces, but then state it in terms of the Kleisli category of the Radon monad to show the similarity with the previous result in
[
17
]
.

Theorem 6.1

(Categorical Hewitt-Savage De Finetti Theorem)
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space. Consider the diagram
\left(\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{inj}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{ConvCH}
which takes
\{1,\dots,n\}
to
\mathcal{R}\left(X^{n}\right)
and an injective function
\tau\colon\{1,\dots,n\}\to\{1,\dots,m\}
to
\zeta_{\tau}\colon\mathcal{R}\left(X^{m}\right)\to\mathcal{R}\left(X^{n}\right)
, defined by
\zeta_{\tau}(\mu)(A)=\mu(\tilde{A}_{\tau})
for

\tilde{A}_{\tau}:=\left\{\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\right)\in X^{m}\,|\,\left(x_{\tau(1)},x_{\tau(2)},\dots,x_{\tau(n)}\right)\in A\right\}.

The limit of this diagram is the space of exchangeable measures on
X^{\mathbb{N}}
, and is isomorphic to
\mathcal{R}\left(\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)\right)
, where the maps
\mathcal{R}\left(\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)\right)\to\mathcal{R}\left(X^{n}\right)
take a measure
\Phi
on
\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)
to the measure

A\in\mathit{Borel}\left(X^{n}\right)\mapsto\int_{\mu\in\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)}\underbrace{\mu\times\dots\times\mu}_{n\text{ times}}(A)\,\mathrm{d}\Phi.

Proof.

This follows from instantiating Theorem
4.3
with the commutative
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
C\left(X\right)
and noting that
C\left(X\right)\mathop{\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}}C\left(Y\right)\cong C\left(X\times Y\right)
.
∎

To emphasise the connection with
[
17
]
, we write
X\rightsquigarrow Y
for a Kleisli morphism
X\to\mathcal{R}(Y)
.

Corollary 6.2

(Categorical de Finetti Theorem in
\mathcal{K}l\left(\mathcal{R}\right)
)
.

For some
X\in\mathbf{CH}
, consider the diagram
\left(\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{inj}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{K}l\left(\mathcal{R}\right)
into the Kleisli category of the Radon monad, which takes
\{1,\dots,n\}
to
X^{n}
and each injection
\tau\colon\{1,\dots,n\}\to\{1,\dots,m\}
to the Kleisli-map
\eta_{\tau}\colon X^{m}\rightsquigarrow X^{n}
given by

\eta_{\tau}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\delta_{\left(x_{\tau(1)},x_{\tau(2)},\dots,x_{\tau(n)}\right)}

This diagram has limit
\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)
in
\mathcal{K}l\left(\mathcal{R}\right)
, with maps
\mathrm{iid}_{n}\colon\mathcal{R}\left(X\right)\rightsquigarrow X^{n}
given, for measurable
A\subset X^{n}
, by

\mathrm{iid}_{n}(\mu)(A)=(\underbrace{\mu\times\dots\times\mu}_{n\text{ times}})(A).

Proof.

The limit in theorem
6.1
is reflected into
\mathcal{K}l\left(\mathcal{R}\right)
, as this category is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{E}m\left(\mathcal{R}\right)\cong\mathbf{ConvCH}
and the limit itself is a free algebra.
∎

The notation
\mathrm{iid}_{n}
arises because
\underbrace{\mu\times\dots\times\mu}_{n\text{ times}}
describes independent and identical distributions
\mu
on
n
copies of
X
.

7
Concluding remarks

We have shown that the quantum de Finetti theorem amounts to a categorical limit for a diagram
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes-}:\left(\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{inj}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\left(\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{CPU}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}
(Theorem
4.3
).
This puts the quantum de Finetti theorem, a cornerstone of quantum Bayesianism and a starting point for quantum tomography, in the setting of categorical and diagrammatic quantum theory. We have focused on
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-algebras, but the set-up is relevant more broadly. Recall that an
affine monoidal category
is a symmetric monoidal category with a terminal unit, and these are argued to be causal models of quantum theory (e.g.
[
6
]
). Now,
\left(\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{inj}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}
is the
free
affine monoidal category on one generator (e.g.
[
20
]
), and so
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes-}:\left(\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{inj}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\left(\mathbf{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{CPU}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}
is canonical for
\mathcal{A}
. So we can consider de Finetti limits in other models of quantum theory, perhaps making a bridge to the test space analysis of
[
3
]
.

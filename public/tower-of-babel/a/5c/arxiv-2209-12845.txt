For a real number
k
, define

\pi_{k}(x)=\sum_{p\leq x}p^{k},\qquad\psi_{k}(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}\Lambda(n)n^{k},

where the first sum runs over prime numbers and
\Lambda(n)=\log p
if
n=p^{m}
(with
m\geq 1
) is a prime power, and
\Lambda(n)=0
otherwise. Then
\pi_{0}(x)=\pi(x)
, the number of primes less than or equal to
x
, and
\psi_{0}(x)=\psi(x)
is the Chebyshev function (
Note:
There are other uses of the notation
\psi_{k}
in the literature that are different from the present one). As usual, we use the notation
f(x)=\Omega_{\pm}(g(x))
to indicate that there exists a constant
c>0
such that
f(x)>cg(x)
for a sequence of
x\to\infty
and
f(x)<-cg(x)
for a sequence of
x\to\infty
. Our goal is to prove the following:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\theta_{0}
be the supremum of the real parts of the zeros of
\zeta(s)
, and let
\epsilon>0
. As
x\to\infty
,

\pi_{k}(x)-\pi(x^{k+1})=\begin{cases}\Omega_{\pm}(x^{\theta_{0}+k-\epsilon})\quad\text{ if }k>0,\\
\Omega_{\pm}(x^{(k+1)(\theta_{0}-\epsilon)})\quad\text{ if }-1<k<0\text{ and }\theta_{0}<1.\end{cases}

If the Riemann Hypothesis is true,

\pi_{k}(x)-\pi(x^{k+1})=\begin{cases}\Omega_{\pm}\left(\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\log\log\log x}{\log x}\right)\quad\text{ if }k>0,\\
\Omega_{\pm}\left(\frac{x^{\frac{k+1}{2}}\log\log\log x}{\log x}\right)\quad\text{ if }k<0.\end{cases}

We are not able to treat the case where
\theta_{0}=1
and
k<0
.

A paper of J. Gerard and the author
[
2
]
showed that
\pi_{k}(x)
is asymptotic to
\pi(x^{k+1})

(see also
[
4
]
and
[
8
]
)
and proved the first half of the theorem. Unfortunately, this proof was based on an incorrect formula.
When the correct formula is used, the proof in
[
2
]
is valid only when the Riemann Hypothesis is false. The main work of the present paper uses a technique of Littlewood to establish the second half of the theorem, namely under the assumption that the Riemann Hypothesis is true.
The second half implies the first half when
\theta_{0}=1/2
.

In
[

]
, a heuristic explanation was given for why

In Sections 2 and 3 of the present paper, the following more quantitative results are proved. The methods in these sections

Theorem 2

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\pi_{k}(t)-\pi(t^{k+1})}{t^{k+2}}dt=\frac{-1}{k+1}\log(k+1)\quad\begin{cases}<0\text{ if }k>0,\\
>0\text{ if }-1<k<0.\end{cases}

Proof of Theorem 1

Proof.

When
k<0
and
\theta_{0}<1
, the result was proved in
[
2
, p. 174]
.
It was also proved that

\pi_{k}(x)-\pi(x^{k+1})=-E(x^{k+1})+x^{k}E(x)-k\int_{2}^{x}t^{k-1}E(t)dt+O(1),

where
E(x)=\pi(x)-\text{li}(x)
. When the Riemann Hypothesis holds, the estimate
E(y)=O(y^{1/2}\log y)
(see
[
6
, Theorem 13.1]
), combined with Littlewood’s oscillation result (see below, or
[
6
, p. 479]
),
yields the stronger statement given in the second half of the theorem:

\displaystyle\pi_{k}(x)-\pi(x^{k+1})

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\pm}\left(\frac{x^{\frac{k+1}{2}}\log\log\log x}{\log x}\right)+x^{k}O\left(x^{1/2}\log x\right)

\displaystyle+O\left(\int_{2}^{x}t^{k-1}t^{1/2}\log tdt\right)+O(1)

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\pm}\left(\frac{x^{\frac{k+1}{2}}\log\log\log x}{\log x}\right),

since
(k+1)/2>k+\frac{1}{2}
when
k<0
.

In the above, we have used the following well-known lemma. Since we also use it several times in the following, we state it explicitly.

Lemma 1
.

Let
r
be a real number (positive or negative) and let
\ell>-1
. Then

\int_{2}^{x}t^{\ell}\log^{r}t\,dt=\frac{1}{\ell+1}x^{\ell+1}\log^{r}x+O\left(x^{\ell+1}\log^{r-1}x\right)

as
x\to\infty
.

Proof.

\int_{2}^{x}t^{\ell}\log^{r}t\,dt=\frac{1}{\ell+1}x^{\ell+1}\log^{r}x+O(1)-\frac{1}{\ell+1}\int_{2}^{x}rt^{\ell}\log^{r-1}t\,dt.

When
\ell>0
and
t
is sufficiently large,
t^{\ell}\log^{r-1}t
is increasing, so

\int_{2}^{x}t^{\ell}\log^{r-1}t\,dt\leq x^{\ell+1}\log^{r-1}x,

for large
x
.

Now suppose
-1<\ell\leq 0
. Choose
0<\alpha<1
and choose
\epsilon
with
0<\epsilon<(\ell+1)(1-\alpha)/\alpha
. Then
\alpha(\ell+1+\epsilon)<\ell+1
, so
when
x
is sufficiently large,

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{x}t^{\ell}\log^{r-1}t\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{2}^{x^{\alpha}}t^{\ell}\log^{r-1}t\,dt+\int_{x^{\alpha}}^{x}t^{\ell}\log^{r-1}t\,dt

\displaystyle=O\left(\int_{2}^{x^{\alpha}}t^{\ell+\epsilon}dt\right)+O\left((\log^{r-1}x)\int_{x^{\alpha}}^{x}t^{\ell}dt\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(x^{\alpha(\ell+1+\epsilon)}\right)+O\left(x^{\ell+1}\log^{r-1}x\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(x^{\ell+1}\log^{r-1}x\right).

∎

Let

\displaystyle=\pi_{k}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\pi_{2k}(x^{1/2})+\frac{1}{3}\pi_{3k}(x^{1/3})+\frac{1}{4}\pi_{4k}(x^{1/4})+\cdots

Lemma 2
.

Let
k>-1
. Then

\Pi_{k}(x)=\pi_{k}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\pi_{2k}(x^{1/2})+O(x^{k+\frac{1}{3}})+O(\log x)

and

\Pi_{k}(x)=\pi_{k}(x)+O\left(\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{\log x}\right)+O(\log x).

Proof.

There are
O(\log x)
positive integers
\ell
such that
x^{1/\ell}\geq 2
, so the sum in (
1
) has
O(\log x)
nonzero terms.
When
k\geq 0
, we have

\pi_{k}(y)\leq\pi(y)y^{k}\leq 1.3\frac{y^{k+1}}{\log y}

(the first inequality is the trivial estimate and the second is in
[
7
]
).
Therefore,

\frac{1}{3}\pi_{3k}(x^{1/3})+\frac{1}{4}\pi_{4k}(x^{1/4})+\cdots=O\left(\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{3}}}{\log x}\log x\right)=O(x^{k+\frac{1}{3}}).

Now suppose
-1<k<0
. Let
n
be the largest positive integer such that
nk\geq-1
. Since
(n+1)k<-1
, we have
\pi_{mk}(y)\leq\pi_{(n+1)k}(y)=O(1)
for all

, and at most
O(\log x)
terms in (
1
) are nonzero.
Moreover,
\pi_{2k}(y)\sim\pi(y^{2k+1})
, from which it follows that

\displaystyle\Pi_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\pi_{k}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\pi_{2k}(x^{1/2})

\displaystyle+\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{3}}}{(3k+1)\log(x)}+o\left(\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{3}}}{(3k+1)\log(x)}\right)

\displaystyle+\cdots+\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{n}}}{(nk+1)\log(x)}+o\left(\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{n}}}{(nk+1)\log(x)}\right)+O(\log x)

\displaystyle=\pi_{k}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\pi_{2k}(x^{1/2})+O\left(\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{3}}}{\log x}\right)+O(\log x),

as desired (the implied constants depend on
k
, but not on
x
).

The second equality of the lemma follows from the first equality and the fact that
\pi_{2k}(y)\sim\pi(y^{2k+1})
.
∎

In

Let

\int_{2}^{x}\psi_{k}(t)\,dt=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s-k)}{\zeta(s-k)}\frac{x^{s+1}ds}{s(s+1)}.

The proof is identical to the proof in

Lemma 4
.

Assume RH. Let

A=\text{Res}_{s=0}\left(\frac{\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s-k)}{\zeta(s-k)}}{s(s+1)}\right),\qquad B=\text{Res}_{s=-1}\left(\frac{\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s-k)}{\zeta(s-k)}}{s(s+1)}\right).

If
k>-1
, then

\int_{2}^{x}\psi_{k}(t)\,dt=\frac{x^{k+2}}{(k+1)(k+2)}-\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho+k+1}}{(\rho+k)(\rho+k+1)}-Ax+B+O(x^{k-1})

as
x\to\infty
. The sum is over the zeros
\rho
of
\zeta(s)
with
\text{Re}(\rho)=1/2
, counted with multiplicity.

Proof.

The proof proceeds by moving the line of integration to the left. The details are the same as in
[
3
, pp. 73-74]
, where the proof is given when
k=0
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Assume RH and let
k>-1
. Then

\int_{2}^{x}\psi_{k}(t)dt=\sum_{n\leq x}(x-n)\Lambda(n)n^{k}=\frac{x^{k+2}}{(k+1)(k+2)}+O(x^{k+\frac{3}{2}})+O(x).

Proof.

The first equality is proved by integration by parts:

\int_{2}^{x}\psi_{k}(t)dt=x\psi_{k}(x)-\int_{2-}^{x}t\,d\psi_{k}(t)=x\sum_{n\leq x}\Lambda(n)n^{k}-\sum_{n\leq x}n^{k+1}\Lambda(n),

which yields the result.

The second equality follows from Lemma
4
and the absolute convergence of
\sum 1/\rho^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Assume RH and let
k>-1
. Then

\pi_{k}(x)-\pi(x^{k+1})=\frac{\psi_{k}(x)-\frac{x^{k+1}}{k+1}}{\log x}+O(x^{\frac{k+1}{2}}\log x)+O\left(\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{\log x}\right).

Proof.

We first prove the lemma with
\Pi_{k}(x)
in place of
\pi_{k}(x)
:

\displaystyle\Pi_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{2-}^{x}\frac{d\psi_{k}(t)}{\log t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi_{k}(x)}{\log x}+\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\psi_{k}(t)-\frac{t^{k+1}}{k+1}}{t\log^{2}t}dt+\frac{1}{k+1}\int_{2}^{x}\frac{t^{k}}{\log^{2}t}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi_{k}(x)}{\log x}+I_{1}+I_{2}.

Integration by parts yields

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{n\leq x}(x-n)\Lambda(n)n^{k}-\frac{x^{k+2}}{(k+1)(k+2)}}{x\log^{2}x}+O(1)

\displaystyle+\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\sum_{n\leq t}(t-n)\Lambda(n)n^{k}-\frac{t^{k+2}}{(k+1)(k+2)}}{t^{2}\log^{2}t}\left(1+\frac{2}{\log t}\right)dt

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{\log^{2}x}\right)+O(1)+\int_{2}^{x}\frac{O(t^{k+\frac{3}{2}})+O(t)}{t^{2}\log^{2}t}dt\quad(\text{by Lemma \ref{Lemma4}})

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{\log^{2}x}\right)+O(1).

Integration by parts also yields

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k+1}\int_{2}^{x}\frac{t^{k+1}}{t\log^{2}t}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{-x^{k+1}}{(k+1)\log x}+\int_{2}^{x}\frac{t^{k}}{\log t}dt+O(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{-x^{k+1}}{(k+1)\log x}+\int_{2}^{x^{k+1}}\frac{du}{\log u}+O(1)\quad\text{ (substitute $u=t^{k+1}$)}

\displaystyle=\frac{-x^{k+1}}{(k+1)\log x}+\pi(x^{k+1})+O\left(x^{\frac{k+1}{2}}\log x\right).

In the last equality, we have used the fact
[
6
, Theorem 13.1]
that the Riemann Hypothesis implies
\pi(y)=Li(y)+O(y^{1/2}\log y)
. Putting everything together yields

(3)

\Pi_{k}(x)=\frac{\psi_{k}(x)-\frac{x^{k+1}}{k+1}}{\log x}+\pi(x^{k+1})+O(x^{\frac{k+1}{2}}\log x)+O\left(\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{\log^{2}x}\right).

Since Lemma
2
tells us that

\Pi_{k}(x)=\pi_{k}(x)+O\left(\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{\log x}\right)+O(\log x),

the result of the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma

translates oscillations of
\psi_{k}(x)

The following lemma allows us to look at

Lemma 7

Assume RH and let

. Then, uniformly for

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(e^{\delta}-e^{-\delta})x}\int_{e^{-\delta}x}^{e^{\delta}x}\left(\psi_{k}(u)-\frac{u^{k+1}}{k+1}\right)du

\displaystyle=-2x^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\gamma>0}\frac{\sin(\gamma\delta)}{\gamma\delta}\frac{\sin(\gamma\log x)}{\gamma}+O(x^{\frac{1}{2}+k}).

The proof follows from Lemma
4
, as in the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 15.9]
. ∎

Finally, the proof of Theorem 1 can now be completed using a Diophantine approximation argument, as in the proof of

Proof of Theorem 2

Proof.

Recall that

\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p}=\log\log x+B+o(1),

where
B=0.261497\dots
is a constant
[
7
]
.

The substitution
u=t^{k+1}
yields

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{x}\frac{\pi(t^{k+1})}{t^{k+2}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k+1}\int_{1}^{x^{k+1}}\frac{\pi(u)}{u^{2}}du

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{k+1}\frac{\pi(x^{k+1})}{x^{k+1}}+\frac{1}{k+1}\int_{1}^{x^{k+1}}\frac{1}{u}d\pi(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{k+1}\frac{\pi(x^{k+1})}{x^{k+1}}+\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{p\leq x^{k+1}}\frac{1}{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k+1}\log\log(x^{k+1})+\frac{B}{k+1}+o(1).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{x}\frac{\pi_{k}(t)}{t^{k+2}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{k+1}\frac{\pi_{k}(x)}{x^{k+1}}+\frac{1}{k+1}\int_{1}^{x}\frac{1}{t^{k+1}}d\pi_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{k+1}\frac{\pi_{k}(x)}{x^{k+1}}+\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p^{k+1}}p^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k+1}\log\log(x)+\frac{B}{k+1}+o(1).

Therefore,

\int_{1}^{x}\frac{\pi_{k}(t)-\pi(t^{k+1})}{t^{k+2}}dt=\frac{-1}{k+1}\log(k+1)+o(1).

This yields the theorem.∎

Proof of Theorem 3

Proof.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis is true.
We need the following technical result:

Lemma 8
.

Assume RH and let
k>0
. Then

\left|\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\psi_{k}(t)-\frac{t^{k+1}}{(k+1)}}{\log t}dt\right|<0.04621\frac{x^{k+\frac{3}{2}}}{\log x}

for all sufficiently large

.

Proof.

From
[
1
, Corollary 1]
, with the standard notation
\rho=\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma
for zeros of

in the critical strip, we know that

\sum_{\rho}\left|\frac{1}{(\rho+k)(\rho+k+1)}\right|<\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}<0.04620999.

Let

. Integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{x}\frac{D(t)}{\log t}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{2}^{x}D(t)dt}{\log x}+\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\int_{2}^{t}D(u)du}{t\log^{2}t}dt

\displaystyle<0.04620999\frac{x^{k+\frac{3}{2}}}{\log x}+0.04620999\int_{2}^{x}\frac{t^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{\log^{2}t}dt+o(x)+o(x^{k-1})

\displaystyle(\text{from Lemma \ref{Lemma3}})

\displaystyle<0.04621\frac{x^{k+\frac{3}{2}}}{\log x}

when

is large.
∎

We know that

\Pi_{k}(x)=\pi_{k}(x)+\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)\log x}+o\left(\frac{x^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{\log x}\right),

which implies that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{x}\left(\Pi_{k}(t)-\pi_{k}(t)\right)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{2}^{x}\frac{t^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)\log t}+o\left(\int_{2}^{x}\frac{t^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{\log t}\,dt\right)

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\frac{x^{k+\frac{3}{2}}}{(k+\frac{3}{2})(2k+1)\log x}

as
x\to\infty
.
From Lemma

and (

\int_{1}^{x}\left(\Pi_{k}(t)-\pi(t^{k+1})\right)dt\leq(0.04621+o(1))\frac{x^{k+\frac{3}{2}}}{(k+\frac{3}{2})\log x}

for

sufficiently large.
Therefore,

\int_{1}^{x}\left(\pi_{k}(t)-\pi(t^{k+1})\right)dt\leq\left(0.04621-\frac{1}{2k+1}+o(1)\right)\frac{x^{k+\frac{3}{2}}}{(k+\frac{3}{2})\log x}<0

.

Now suppose the Riemann Hypothesis is false. Then

Let

.
Let

. Then

\displaystyle G(s):=

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{F(t)-t^{\theta_{0}+k+1-\epsilon}}{t^{s+2}}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{-F(t)}{(s+1)t^{s+1}}\right|_{1}^{\infty}+\frac{1}{s+1}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\Pi_{k}(t)-\Pi_{0}(t^{k+1})}{t^{s+1}}dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\theta_{0}+k-s-\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s(s+1)}\left(\log\zeta(s-k)-\log\zeta(\frac{s}{k+1})\right)+\frac{1}{\theta_{0}+k-s-\epsilon}.

The last line represents a function that is analytic for all real numbers

Let

x\to\infty
with

\int_{1}^{x}\left(\Pi_{k}(t)-\Pi_{0}(t^{k+1})\right)dt>x^{\theta_{0}+k+1-\epsilon}.

We now need to change from
\Pi_{k}
to
\pi_{k}
. Since
\Pi_{k}(t)=\pi_{k}(t)+O(t^{k+\frac{1}{2}}/\log x)
and
\Pi_{0}(t^{k+1})=\pi(t^{k+1})+O(t^{\frac{k+1}{2}}/\log t)
,

\left|\int_{1}^{x}\left(\Pi_{k}(t)-\Pi_{0}(t^{k+1})\right)dt-\int_{1}^{x}\left(\pi_{k}(t)-\pi_{0}(t^{k+1})\right)dt\right|=O(x^{k+\frac{3}{2}}/\log x).

Since
\theta_{0}+k+1-\epsilon>k+\frac{3}{2}
, we find that there exists a sequence of
x\to\infty
such that

\int_{1}^{x}\left(\pi_{k}(t)-\pi_{0}(t^{k+1})\right)dt>0.

∎

We note that the proof of “(2)
\implies
(1)” in Theorem 3 works for all
k>0
.

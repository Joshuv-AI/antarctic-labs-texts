Detector array for the H 7 {}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 7$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H} nucleus multi-neutron decay study Массив детекторов для исследования многонейтронного распада ядра H 7 {}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 7$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}


Abstract
На пучке радиоактивных ядер сепаратора ACCULINNA-2 Лаборатории ядерных реакций им. Г. Н. Флёрова создаётся установка, предназначенная для детального изучения пятительного распада ядер Н 7 {}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 7$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Н} , образующихся в результате реакции передачи протона от налетающего ядра He 8 {}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 8$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He} на ядро мишени Н 2 {}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 2$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Н} . В настоящей статье даётся описание устройства из 100 пластических сцинтилляторов ВС-404, предназначенного для регистрации нейтронов, телескопа Si детекторов ядер отдачи He 3 {}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He} , а также комплекта детекторов для Δ ​ E \Delta E - E E -TOF регистрации ядер H 3 {}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H} , вылетающих из мишени при распаде H 7 {}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 7$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H} . Показаны результаты моделирования методом Монте-Карло, давшего ожидаемые значения энергии и параметры траекторий всех частиц. Приведены результаты полученных оценок светимости обсуждаемых экспериментов. Setup fitting the requirements for the detailed study of the five-body decay of the H 7 {}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 7$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H} nucleus obtained as a result of the proton transfer from the He 8 {}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 8$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He} projectiles to the deuterium target nuclei is being built at the radioactive beam line of ACCULINNA-2 separator in the G.N. Flerov Laboratory of Nuclear Reactions. Described here is the assembly of 100 BC-404 plastic scintillators, intended for neutron detection, the annular Si detector telescope for the isotope[3]He recoils, and the detector array providing the Δ ​ E \Delta E - E E -TOF registration of H 3 {}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H} nuclei emitted at the H 7 {}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 7$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H} decay. Results obtained by the Monte Carlo simulations made for the energy values and flight passes of all these particles are given together with the luminosity expected for the discussed experiments.

Setup with the 75 mm thick BC-404 scintillators

Presented is the modification of the detector array intended for the study of
nuclei undergoing decay with the multi-neutron emission.
The modification assumes the use of the assembly of 100 BC-404 scintillators
which are at the disposal of ACCULINNA group.
The front view of a single scintillator is shown in Figure
1

In its cross-section, the 75 mm thick plastic is the hexagon inscribed in
the circle with diameter 100 mm and it is enclosed in some 1-mm thick cover.
Consequently, 95.5% area of a compact assembly made with the use of these
plastics will be covered by the BC-404 scintillators.

Triggering array

The suggested choice for the detector array, giving trigger signals produced by
the
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He}
recoil nuclei emitted in the

{{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 2$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}}({{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 8$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He}},{{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He}}){{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 7$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}}
reaction,
is to place at position 2a (see Figure
2
) a detector telescope
made of a pair of annular Si detectors.
Each detector is segmented in 64 rings and 64 sectors and has the 28-mm central
hole and sensitive area with the 32 mm inner diameter and 125 mm outer diameter.
The 0.140 mm thick front detector is the source of trigger signals generated
from the recoil
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He}
nuclei.
The second detector will be 1.0 mm thick.

Energy range 9–22 MeV is inherent to the
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He}
recoils emitted in the

{{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 2$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}}({{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 8$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He}},{{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He}}){{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 7$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}}
reaction populating the
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 7$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}
nucleus excitation spectrum in a range of 0–9 MeV
above the
{{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}}+4n
decay threshold. Such recoils with energy

>14
MeV pass through the 0.140 mm front Si detector and are stopped in the
second, 1 mm thick Si detector.
These nuclei will be identified by the
\Delta E-E
method against the
background made by the other recoil nuclei
(
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 2$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}
,
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}
,
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 4$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He}
,
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 6$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}
e, etc.)
which are emitted from the target.
The 9–14 MeV
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He}

will be safely discriminated from the
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}

the target with similar energy, because for these

recoil nuclei
the
\Delta E-E

As for the
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He}

{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 4$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He}

\Delta E

the whole group of the

6
Cross-talk and pile-up caused losses

The Neutron Wall composition made with the 75-mm thick plastics results that
less than 5% of the total number of detected 3-fold and 4-fold neutron events
are spoiled by the occurrence of such events when a single neutron is detected,
due to re-scattering, in two adjacent scintillators (the so-called cross-talk).
Time difference
<1
ns between the two neutron signals distinguishes these
cross-talks from the overwhelming part of events obtained with two

{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 7$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}
decay neutrons detected in adjacent scintillators.
Throwing out all events looking as cross-talks will not result in any noticeable
loss in the luminosity of the experiment.
The loss occurring from this ejection in the real collected data will be less
than 2%.

Probability to have two neutrons hitting a single plastic makes about 8% when
four neutrons emitted at the
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 7$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}
decay arrive at the Neutron Wall.
More than one-half of such pile-up events will be stored with the complete data
set, characterizing the
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He}
recoil, and with four
(3 neutrons and
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}
) of the five
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 7$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}
decay products.
About 4% of pile-up events are possible when three neutrons, emitted at
the
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 7$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}
decay, arrive at the Neutron Wall.
Therefore, conclusion is made that the neutron pile-up results in a 4%
reduction of detection efficiency as compared with the data given in the
last row of Table
3
.

In summary, taking into account the cross-talk rejection and the losses caused
due to pile-ups one should take luminosity estimates reduced by 6% as compared
with the numbers presented in Table
4
.

In addition, we expect a small background contribution from gamma rays.
The main gamma sources could be materials 1, 2a, 2b and 3
(Figure
2
).
In this case, the time of flight to the Neutron Wall will be less than

\sim 9
ns, which is far from the typical time of flights corresponded to
neutrons, see Figure
8
.

The given above estimations of cross-talk and detection efficiency,
made for the Neutron Wall looking as a very compact assembly of BC-404 plastics,
should be checked experimentally in a wide energy range of neutron energy
15–45 MeV.

reaction,
induced by the
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{H}
beam obtained at ACCULINNA-2 with energy
25–60 MeV and intensity
10^{6}\penalty\ \text{s}^{-1}

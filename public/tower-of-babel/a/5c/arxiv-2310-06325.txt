Long-time behavior for the Kirchhoff diffusion problem with magnetic fractional Laplace operator Volume: 00
Jiabin Zuo, Juliana Honda Lopes, Vicentiu D. Rădulescu

Abstract
We consider a Kirchhoff-type diffusion problem driven by the magnetic fractional Laplace operator. The main result in this paper establishes that infinite time blow-up cannot occur for the problem. The proof is based on the potential well method, in relationship with energy and Nehari functionals.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n>2s
) be a bounded
domain with smooth boundary.
In this paper we study the following Kirchhoff-type diffusion problem

\begin{cases}u_{t}+M\left(\|u\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2}\right)(-\Delta)_{A}^{s}u=f(|u|)u,&\mbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u(x,t)=0,&\mbox{ in }(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&\mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{cases}

(1)

Given
s\in(0,1)
and
A\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define the magnetic fractional Laplace operator defined
(-\Delta)_{A}^{s}

by

(-\Delta)_{A}^{s}u(x,t)=2\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x,\varepsilon)}\frac{u(x,t)-e^{i(x-y)\cdot A(\frac{x+y}{2})}u(y,t)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\ dy,

(2)

for

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C})
. This differential operator is weighted by a Kirchhoff-type function
M:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
(see
[
7
]
), satisfying
(M1)
-
(M2)
below. When
A\equiv 0
in (
2
), then we have the usual fractional Laplacian differential operator denoted by
(-\Delta)^{s}
. Such differential operator was studied in the context of problems in quantum mechanics and of the motion of chains or arrays of particles connected by elastic springs, as well as in the context of problems of unusual diffusion processes in turbulent fluids and of mass transport in fractured media. We refer to
[
1
]
(Lévy processes),
[
3
]
(nonlocal diffusions, drifts and games),
[
2
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
16
,
17
]
for
other classes of nonlocal operators. In all the aforementioned works, the authors deal with Schrödinger
operators with magnetic fields. For instance,
[
10
]
establish the existence of nontrivial solutions to a parametric fractional Schrödinger equation in the case of critical or supercritical nonlinearity.
Next, the operator
(-\Delta)_{A}^{s}
(see (
2
))
was introduced in
[
4
]
, as a
fractional counterpart of the magnetic Laplacian
(\nabla-iA)^{2}
,
where
A:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a

L_{loc}^{\infty}
-vector potential. Zuo & Lopes
[
19
]
established the existence of weak solutions to problem (
1
). The strategy is based on the potential well method, hence they obtain global in time
solutions and blow-up in finite time solutions. Here, we show that the global in time solutions to (
1
) can not blow-up in infinite time. We also mention
[
18
]
(non-local parabolic
equation in a bounded convex domain),

[
4
]
(for the equation
(-\Delta)_{A}^{s}u+u=|u|^{p-2}u
posed in

\mathbb{R}^{3}
),
[
11
]
(fractional Choquard equation), and
[
15
]
(system of Kirchhoff type equations).

2
Mathematical background and hypotheses

The right framework for the analysis of equation (
1
) is the function space
X_{0,A}
(hence
H_{A}^{s}(\Omega)
) defined as follows. For an open and bounded
set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n>2s
), let
|\Omega|
be the
measure of the set
\Omega
. By
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})
we mean the Lebesgue
space of complex valued functions with norm

\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
and inner product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. For
p=2
,
s\in(0,1)
and
A\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we consider the magnetic Gagliardo
semi-norm defined by

[u]_{H^{s}_{A}(\Omega)}^{2}:=\int\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x,t)-e^{i(x-y)\cdot A\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)}u(y,t)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\ dx\ dy.

Hence we consider the space
H_{A}^{s}(\Omega)
of functions
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})
with
[u]_{H^{s}_{A}(\Omega)}<\infty
and furnished with the norm

\|u\|_{H^{s}_{A}(\Omega)}:=(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+[u]^{2}_{H^{s}_{A}(\Omega)})^{\frac{1}{2}}.
Referring to
[
5
,
6
]
, we define

X_{0,A}:=\{u\in H_{A}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u=0\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\},

with the real
scalar product (see
[
4
]
) given as

\displaystyle\langle u,v\rangle_{X_{0,A}}:=\mathcal{R}\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\frac{\left(u(x,t)-e^{i(x-y)\cdot A\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)}u(y,t)\right)\overline{\left(v(x,t)-e^{i(x-y)\cdot A\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)}v(y,t)\right)}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\ dx\ dy,

where, for every
z\in\mathbb{C}
, by
\mathcal{R}z
we mean the real
part of
z
and by
\overline{z}
its complex conjugate. This scalar
product induces the following norm

\|u\|_{X_{0,A}}:=\left(\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\frac{|u(x,t)-e^{i(x-y)\cdot A\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)}u(y,t)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\ dx\ dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We will use
\xrightarrow{w}
and
\to
to denote weak and strong convergences, respectively.
Our hypotheses on problem (
1
) are the following:

(F)

f\in C^{1}([0,\infty))
, and we can find
C>0
and
\gamma\geq p
,
for
p\in(2,2_{s}^{\ast})
with
2_{s}^{\ast}=\frac{2n}{n-2s}
, such
that

\begin{array}[]{ll}|F(u)|\leq Cu^{p},f(u)u^{2}\leq pCu^{p},\mbox{ for }u\geq 0,\\
0<\gamma F(u)\leq f(u)u^{2},u^{2}(uf^{\prime}(u)-(p-2)f(u))\geq 0\mbox{ for }u>0,\end{array}

(3)

where
F(u):=\int_{0}^{u}f(\tau)\tau\ d\tau
.

Further, the Kirchhoff
function
M:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is as follows:

(M1)

it is a continuous function and
there exist constants
m_{0}>0
and

\theta\in(1,\frac{2_{s}^{\ast}}{2})
such that

M(u)\geq m_{0}u^{\theta-1}
for all
u\in[0,\infty)
;

(M2)

there exists a constant

\mu\in(1,\frac{2_{s}^{\ast}}{2})
such that

\mu\mathscr{M}(u)\geq M(u)u
for all
u\in[0,\infty)
,
where

\mathscr{M}(u):=\int_{0}^{u}M(\tau)\ d\tau.

Here,
\theta
and
\mu
satisfy
the condition:
(P)
2\max\{\theta,\mu\}<p<2_{s}^{\ast}
.

We consider the
C^{1}
-functional related to problem (
1
) and
defined by

J(u):=\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{M}(\|u\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})-\int_{\Omega}F(|u|)\ dx.

(4)

We have

\langle J^{\prime}(u),\phi\rangle=M(\|u\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})\langle u,\phi\rangle_{X_{0,A}}-\mathcal{R}\int_{\Omega}f(|u|)u\overline{\phi}\ dx,

for any
\phi\in X_{0,A}
, and we introduce the Nehari functional for (
1
) given by

I(u):=\langle J^{\prime}(u),u\rangle.

Hence, we can consider the non-negative value
d
(i.e., mountain pass level) given as
d=\inf_{u\in X_{0,A}\setminus\{0\}\,:\,I(u)=0}J(u).

According to Sattinger
[
14
]
and Payne & Sattinger
[
13
]
we know that if the initial energy
J(u_{0})
is less than the mountain pass level
d
, then the solution to problem (
1
) exists globally if it begins in the stable set

\mathcal{W}=\{u\in X_{0,A}:I(u)>0,\,J(u)<d\}\cup\{0\},

and fails to exist globally if it starts from the unstable set
\mathcal{U}=\{u\in X_{0,A}:I(u)<0,\,J(u)<d\}.

The functionals
I(\cdot)
and
J(\cdot)
are energy functionals of the stationary problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}M(\|u\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})(-\Delta)_{A}^{s}u=f(|u|)u,&\mbox{ \text{in} }\Omega,\\
u(x)=0,&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\end{array}\right.

and we recall that
u\in X_{0,A}(\Omega)
is a solution to
(
2
) (i.e., stationary solution to problem
(
1
)) if
\langle J^{\prime}(u),\phi\rangle=0
, namely

M(\|u\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})\langle u,\phi\rangle_{X_{0,A}}-\mathcal{R}\int_{\Omega}f(|u|)u\overline{\phi}\ dx=0,

for all
\phi\in X_{0,A}(\Omega)
, see also
[
19
, Definition 1]
. So,
u\in X_{0,A}(\Omega)
solves
(
1
) on
(0,T)
for
T>0
if
u\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,\infty;X_{0,A})\cap C(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))
with
u_{t}\in L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}u_{t}\overline{\phi}\ dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}M(\|u\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})\langle u,\phi\rangle_{X_{0,A}}\,dt

\displaystyle-\mathcal{R}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}f(|u|)u\overline{\phi}\ dx\,dt=0\mbox{ for all $\phi\in X_{0,A}(\Omega).$}

Remark 2.1
.

We note that
[
19
]
established the existence of a weak solution
u\in X_{0,A}(\Omega)
to problem (
1
), based on hypotheses (F) and (M1) (see
[
19
, Theorem 2.1]
for the precise requirements). Imposing also (M2), they obtained that weak solutions blow-up in a finite time, provided that the initial energy is negative (see
[
19
, Theorem 2.2]
). Further, problem (
1
) has a weak solution for all
T>0
(namely, a global solution) such that
u\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,\infty;X_{0,A})\cap C(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))
with
u_{t}\in L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))
, provided that
u_{0}\in\mathcal{W}
(see
[
19
, Theorem 2.3]
).

3
Main result

Let
u=u(t)
be solution of (
1
) with initial data

u_{0}\in X_{0,A}
, then by
T=T(u_{0})
we denote the maximal existence time of
u=u(t)
given as

(i)

T=\infty
if
u(t)\in X_{0,A}
for
t\in[0,\infty)
;

(ii)

T=t_{\max}\,(>0)
if
u(t)\in X_{0,A}
for
t\in[0,t_{\max})
,
u(t_{\max})\not\in X_{0,A}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that hypotheses (F), (M1), (M2)
and (P) hold and
p\in(2,2_{s}^{\ast})

(n>2s)
. Let
u=u(t)
be a
solution of (
1
) with
u_{0}\in X_{0,A}
such
that
J(u_{0})\leq d
and
I(u_{0})>0
. If the maximal existence time
is
T=\infty
, then there exists an increasing sequence

\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
with
t_{k}\rightarrow\infty
as

k\rightarrow\infty
, such that
u(t_{k})
converges to a stationary
solution
v\in X_{0,A}(\Omega)
of problem (
1
), that is

u(t_{k})\rightarrow v
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

We show global in time solutions to (
1
) can not blow-up in infinite time, by arguing in three steps.

Step 1: Existence of an increasing sequence
\{t_{k}\}
.

Let

u=u(t)
be a solution of problem (
1
) with
u_{0}\in X_{0,A}
and maximal existence time
T=\infty
. Now,
[
19
, Theorem
2.3]
gives us

u\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,\infty;X_{0,A})\cap C(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))
,
u_{t}\in L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega)).

Multiplying equation (
1
) by
\phi\in X_{0,A}
and integrating
over
\Omega
, we get

\displaystyle(u^{\prime}(t),\phi)=-M(\|u\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})\langle u(t),\phi\rangle_{X_{0,A}}+\mathcal{R}\int_{\Omega}f(|u(t)|)u(t)\overline{\phi}\ dx.

(8)

By
[
19

is non-increasing with respect to

\displaystyle 0\leq J(u(t))\leq J(u_{0}),

(9)

where we use a

that there exists a time

such that
J(u(t_{0}))<0

(
4

0>J(u(t_{0}))=\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{M}(\|u(t_{0})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})-\int_{\Omega}F(|u(t_{0})|)\ dx.
It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{M}(\|u(t_{0})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})<\int_{\Omega}F(|u(t_{0})|)\ dx.

(10)

Combining the Nehari functional
I(\cdot)
, together with assumptions (
3
), (M2), (P) and inequality
(
10
), we conclude that

\displaystyle I(u(t_{0}))=

\displaystyle M(\|u(t_{0})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})\|u(t_{0})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2}-\mathcal{R}\int_{\Omega}f(|u(t_{0})|)|u(t_{0})|^{2}\ dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu\mathscr{M}(\|u(t_{0})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})-\gamma\int_{\Omega}F(|u(t_{0})|)\ dx<\mu\mathscr{M}(\|u(t_{0})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})-\frac{\gamma}{2}\mathscr{M}(\|u(t_{0})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\mu-\frac{p}{2})\mathscr{M}(\|u(t_{0})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})<0\quad\mbox{(recall that $\gamma\geq p$)}.

So, at time

we have
J(u(t_{0}))<0
and

,
hence
u(t_{0})
is in the so-called unstable set
\mathcal{U}=\{u\in X_{0,A}:I(u)<0,\,J(u)<d\mbox{ ($d>0$})\}
. By
[
19
, Theorem
2.4]
,
u(t)
blows-up in a finite time,
which contradicts

T=\infty
.
Since
J(u(t))
is non-increasing with respect to
t
, by
(
9
) we can find
c
with

0\leq c\leq J(u_{0})
and such that
J(u(t))\rightarrow c
as

t\rightarrow\infty
. Passing to the limit as
t\rightarrow\infty
in

\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{\prime}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\ ds+J(u)=J(u_{0}),

then we get

c=J(u_{0})-\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u^{\prime}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\ ds.

It follows that we can find an increasing sequence

\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
with
t_{k}\rightarrow\infty
as

k\rightarrow\infty
satisfying

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|u^{\prime}(t_{k})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

(11)

Step 2: Convergence of
\{u(t_{k})\}
to a function
v\in X_{0,A}
.

Equation (
8
) leads to the following

\langle J^{\prime}(u(t)),\phi\rangle=M(\|u(t)\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})\langle u(t),\phi\rangle_{X_{0,A}}-\mathcal{R}\int_{\Omega}f(|u(t)|)u(t)\overline{\phi}\ dx=(-u^{\prime}(t),\phi)\mbox{ for all $\phi\in X_{0,A}$.}

Combining the Schwartz inequality, the
definition of the first eigenvalue

of

(see
[

) and the limit in (

\displaystyle\|J^{\prime}(u(t_{k}))\|_{X_{0,A}^{\prime}}=\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi\in X_{0,A}}}{{\|\phi\|_{X_{0,A}}=1}}}\langle J^{\prime}(u(t_{k})),\phi\rangle=\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi\in X_{0,A}}}{{\|\phi\|_{X_{0,A}}=1}}}(-u^{\prime}(t_{k}),\phi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi\in X_{0,A}}}{{\|\phi\|_{X_{0,A}}=1}}}\|u^{\prime}(t_{k})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|u^{\prime}(t_{k})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\sup_{\phi\in X_{0,A}\setminus\{0\}}\left(\frac{\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|\phi\|_{X_{0,A}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u^{\prime}(t_{k})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left(\displaystyle\inf_{\phi\in X_{0,A}\setminus\{0\}}\left(\frac{\|\phi\|_{X_{0,A}}}{\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\right)\right)^{-1}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}\|u^{\prime}(t_{k})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0

(12)

as
k\rightarrow\infty
. As usual, by

we denote the dual space of
X_{0,A}
, hence we can find
c_{1}>0
,
independent of the index

, such that

\displaystyle\|J^{\prime}(u(t_{k}))\|_{X_{0,A}^{\prime}}\leq c_{1},\ k=1,2,\cdots.

(13)

For the Nehari energy functional

, the bound from above in (
13
) and the Young inequality lead to

|I(u(t_{k}))|\leq|\langle J^{\prime}(u(t_{k})),u(t_{k})\rangle|\leq\|J^{\prime}(u(t_{k}))\|_{X_{0,A}^{\prime}}\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}\leq c_{1}\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}\leq c_{2}+\frac{(p-2\mu)m_{0}}{4p\mu}\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2\theta}.

Using
J(\cdot)
and
I(\cdot)
, together with (

) and (M1), we
obtain

\displaystyle J(u(t_{k}))=\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{M}\left(\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2}\right)-\int_{\Omega}F(|u(t_{k})|)\ dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mu}M(\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2}-\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{\Omega}f(|u(t_{k})|)|u(t_{k})|^{2}\ dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mu}M(\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2}+\frac{1}{p}I(u(t_{k}))-\frac{1}{p}M(\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2\mu)}{2p\mu}M(\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2}-c_{2}-\frac{(p-2\mu)}{4p\mu}m_{0}\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2\theta}

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2\mu)}{2p\mu}m_{0}\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2\theta}-\frac{(p-2\mu)}{4p\mu}m_{0}\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2\theta}-c_{2}.

\displaystyle\|u(t_{k})\|_{X_{0,A}}\leq\Big[\frac{(J(u_{0})+c_{2})4p\mu}{(p-2\mu)m_{0}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2\theta}},\quad k=1,2,\cdots.

(14)

From (
14
) and the fact that the embedding

X_{0,A}\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})

(see

\displaystyle u_{k}\xrightarrow{w}v\mbox{ in }X_{0,A}\mbox{ as

\displaystyle u_{k}\rightarrow v\mbox{ in }L^{q}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})\mbox{ for all }q\in[1,2_{s}^{\ast})\mbox{ as }k\rightarrow\infty.

By (

), there exist a subsequence, still denoted by

\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, and a function

for all

\displaystyle u_{k}(x)\rightarrow v(x)\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega\mbox{ as

\displaystyle\mbox{for all }k,|u_{k}(x)|\leq w(x)\mbox{ a.e. in

(18)

Step 3:
v

Let us prove that for all

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\int_{\Omega}f(|u_{k}|)u_{k}\overline{\phi}\ dx\to\mathcal{R}\int_{\Omega}f(|v|)v\overline{\phi}\ dx\quad\mbox{as $k\rightarrow\infty$.}

, we get

for a.e.

\displaystyle\langle J^{\prime}(u_{k}),\phi\rangle=M(\|u_{k}\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})\langle u_{k},\phi\rangle-\mathcal{R}\int_{\Omega}f(|u_{k}|)u_{k}\overline{\phi}\ dx,

and

. We show that

M(\|u_{k}\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})\rightarrow M(\|v\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})
and

strongly in
X_{0,A}
(see
[
12
]
).
Since
M
is continuous, (
14

M(\|u_{k}\|_{X_{0,A}}^{2})\leq c
for all

, hence

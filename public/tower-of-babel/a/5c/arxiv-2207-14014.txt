Generalized Matter Parities from Finite Modular Symmetries Preprint: EPHOU-22-014 KYUSHU-HET-245
Tatsuo Kobayashi 1, Satsuki Nishimura 2, Hajime Otsuka 2, Morimitsu Tanimoto 3 , Kei Yamamoto 4

Abstract
We classify a supersymmetric extension of the Standard Model by discrete symmetries originating from finite modular symmetries Γ N \Gamma_{N} . Since all the couplings in supersymmetric theories of finite modular symmetries Γ N \Gamma_{N} are described by holomorphic modular forms with even modular weights, renormalizable and non-renormalizable operators such as baryon- and/or lepton-number violating operators are severely constrained. From the modular transformation of matter multiplets with modular weight 1 / M 1/M , we find ℤ 2 ​ M \mathbb{Z}_{2M} symmetries, including the generalized baryon and lepton parities, R R -parity, ℤ 3 \mathbb{Z}_{3} baryon triality and ℤ 6 \mathbb{Z}_{6} proton hexality. Such ℤ 2 ​ M \mathbb{Z}_{2M} symmetries are enlarged to ℤ 2 ​ M ⋊ ℤ 2 CP \mathbb{Z}_{2M}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\text{CP}} symmetries together with the CP transformation.

2.1
Finite modular symmetries

The
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
modular group is defined by linear fraction transformations of the complex
structure modulus
\tau
in an upper half complex plane i.e.,
{\rm Im}(\tau)>0
:

\displaystyle\tau\rightarrow\gamma\tau=\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}

(2.1)

with
\{a,b,c,d\}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
ad-bc=1
.
The generators of
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
group are given by
S
and
T
:

\displaystyle S\,:\,\tau\rightarrow-\frac{1}{\tau}\,,\qquad T\,:\,\tau\rightarrow\tau+1\,,

(2.2)

satisfying

\displaystyle S^{2}=-\mathbb{I}\,,\qquad(ST)^{3}=\mathbb{I}\,.

(2.3)

By introducing the principal congruence subgroup of level
N
with
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
:

\displaystyle\Gamma(N)=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in SL(2,\mathbb{Z})\biggl|\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}1\,\,({\rm mod}\,N)&0\,\,({\rm mod}\,N)\\
0\,\,({\rm mod}\,N)&1\,\,({\rm mod}\,N)\end{bmatrix}\right\},

(2.4)

one can define a quotient group

\displaystyle\Gamma_{N}^{\prime}\equiv SL(2,\mathbb{Z})/\Gamma(N)=\langle S,T|S^{4}=(ST)^{3}=T^{N}=\mathbb{I},\quad S^{2}T=TS^{2}\rangle\,.

(2.5)

Since
\gamma
and
-\gamma
lead to the same modular transformation for
\tau
,
the transformation group of
\tau
is isomorphic to
PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})=SL(2,\mathbb{Z})/\{\pm\mathbb{I}\}
. Then, we introduce
\bar{\Gamma}(N)\equiv\Gamma(N)/\{\pm\mathbb{I}\}

for
N=1,2
, whereas
\bar{\Gamma}(N)=\Gamma(N)
for
N>2
due to the fact that
-\mathbb{I}
is not the element of
\Gamma(N)
with
N>2
.
In a similar way, the quotient group is also defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{N}\equiv PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})/\bar{\Gamma}(N)=\langle S,T|S^{2}=(ST)^{3}=T^{N}=\mathbb{I}\rangle\,,

(2.6)

which includes finite modular groups such as
\Gamma_{2}\simeq S_{3}
,
\Gamma_{3}\simeq A_{4}
,

\Gamma_{4}\simeq S_{4}
and
\Gamma_{5}\simeq A_{5}

deAdelhartToorop:2011re
.
These finite modular symmetries are widely used as flavor symmetries of quarks and leptons (see for Refs.
Altarelli:2010gt
;
Ishimori:2010au
;
Ishimori:2012zz
;
Hernandez:2012ra
;
King:2013eh
;
King:2014nza
;
Tanimoto:2015nfa
;
King:2017guk
;
Petcov:2017ggy
;
Feruglio:2019ybq
;
Kobayashi:2022moq
).
Note that
\Gamma^{\prime}_{N}
corresponds to the double cover of
\Gamma_{N}
.

To construct the modular symmetric action, let us introduce holomorphic modular forms
f(\tau)_{i}

of positive integer weight
k
for
\Gamma(N)
.
Their transformations under
\Gamma(N)
are given by

\displaystyle f_{i}(\gamma\tau)=(c\tau+d)^{k}\rho_{ij}(\gamma)f_{j}(\tau)\,,

(2.7)

for any
\gamma\in\Gamma(N)
. Here,
\rho
is a unitary representation of
\Gamma^{\prime}_{N}

since it obeys

\displaystyle\rho(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1})=\rho(\gamma_{2})\rho(\gamma_{1})\,,\qquad\rho(h)=\mathbb{I}\,,

(2.8)

for
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
and
h\in\Gamma(N)
.
Taking into account
f_{i}(S^{2}\tau)=f_{i}(\tau)
for
\gamma=S^{2}=-\mathbb{I}
,
the transformation of modular forms

\displaystyle f_{i}(S^{2}\tau)=(-1)^{k}\rho_{ij}(S^{2})f_{j}(\tau)

(2.9)

gives rise to the constraint:
(-1)^{k}\rho(S^{2})=\mathbb{I}
.
Therefore, there exist two possibilities
Liu:2019khw
:

(i)

k=
even,
\rho(S^{2})=\mathbb{I}
, i.e.,
\rho\in\Gamma_{N}
,

(ii)

k=
odd,
\rho(S^{2})=-\mathbb{I}
, i.e.,
\rho\in\Gamma_{N}^{\prime}
,

where it is notable that
S^{2}
is the element of
PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})
.
It turns out that the modular forms of even (odd) weight and level
N
transform in representations of
\Gamma_{N}
(
\Gamma_{N}^{\prime}
).
Throughout this paper, we focus on
\Gamma_{N}
rather than its double cover
\Gamma_{N}^{\prime}
.

We are now ready to write down the modular invariant action in the framework of
{\cal N}=1

global SUSY.
When we denote
\Phi_{I}
a set of chiral supermultiplets, the matter Kähler potential is given by

\displaystyle K=\sum_{I}\frac{|\Phi_{I}|^{2}}{(i(\bar{\tau}-\tau))^{k_{I}}},

(2.10)

where
k_{I}
is a modular weight of
\Phi_{I}
.
The above Kähler potential is invariant under the following modular transformations:

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\rightarrow\gamma\tau=\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi_{I}

\displaystyle\rightarrow(c\tau+d)^{-k_{I}}\rho_{I}(\gamma)\Phi_{I}\,,

(2.11)

with
\gamma\in PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})
,
where
\rho_{I}(\gamma)
is the unitary representation of
\Gamma_{N}
.
Such a Kähler potential, including the modulus kinetic term, will be determined by an ultraviolet physics such as the string theory.
In the framework of the string theory, the kinetic term of modulus and matter multiplets are derived from the higher-dimensional Einstein-Hilbert term and kinetic terms of higher-dimensional fermions as well as bosons, respectively. For instance, in Type IIB magnetized D-brane models, the modulus
\tau

will correspond to the complex structure modulus associated with the torus.
Magnetic fluxes on the torus induce moduli-dependent wavefunctions of
degenerate chiral zero-modes, and their low-energy effective field theory such as Yukawa couplings and

Kobayashi:2018rad
;
Kobayashi:2018bff
;

;
Kikuchi:2020frp
;
Kikuchi:2020nxn
;

The higher-dimensional operators of matter fields will be naturally suppressed with respect to the compactification scale. (See, Ref.
Kikuchi:2022txy
, for the modular flavor models from a higher-dimensional point of view.) In this paper, we analyze the matter action in the framework of
{\cal N}=1

By contrast, the matter superpotential

\displaystyle W=\sum_{n}Y_{I_{1}...I_{n}}(\tau)\Phi_{I_{1}}...\Phi_{I_{n}}

is modular invariant when the
n

\displaystyle Y_{I_{1}...I_{n}}(\tau)\rightarrow Y_{I_{1}...I_{n}}(\gamma\tau)=(c\tau+d)^{k_{Y}}\rho_{Y}(\gamma)Y_{I_{1}...I_{n}}(\tau)

(2.13)

with

Note that in the
\Gamma_{N}

2.2
Discrete symmetries

Before going into the classification of baryon- and/or lepton-number violating operators, we revisit the modular transformations of matter multiplets (

\displaystyle\Phi_{I}

\displaystyle\rightarrow(-1)^{-k_{I}}\rho_{I}(S^{2})\Phi_{I}=(-1)^{-k_{I}}\Phi_{I}\,,

where we employ
\rho(S^{2})=\mathbb{I}
, i.e.,
\rho\in\Gamma_{N}

It indicates that there exists a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

k_{I}

When the modular weight

For example, heterotic string theory on toroidal

Also, Type IIB string theory with magnetized D-branes leads to

;

2.3
Baryon- and/or lepton-number violating operators

In this section, we analyze the modular symmetric superpotential in the MSSM:

\displaystyle W=y^{u}_{ij}Q_{i}H_{u}\bar{U}_{j}+y^{d}_{ij}Q_{i}H_{d}\bar{D}_{j}+y^{\ell}_{ij}L_{i}H_{d}\bar{E}_{j}+y^{n}_{ij}L_{i}H_{u}N_{j}+m_{ij}^{n}N_{i}N_{j}+\mu H_{u}H_{d}\,,

\Gamma_{N}

Specifically, we represent the modular weights of matter multiplets, Yukawa couplings, Majorana neutrino mass, and

\displaystyle\{k_{Q},\,k_{U},\,k_{D},\,k_{L},\,k_{E},\,k_{N},\,k_{H_{u}},\,k_{H_{d}},\,k^{u}_{y},\,k^{d}_{y},\,k^{\ell}_{y},\,k^{n}_{y},\,k^{m},\,k_{\mu}\}\,,

Even if we allow fractional modular weights,
an existence of the Majorana mass term and
\mu
-term requires

\displaystyle k_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\qquad k_{H_{u}}+k_{H_{d}}=2h\in 2\mathbb{Z}

(5.1)

with
h
being an integer.
Since the modular weights are multiples of
1/M
,
the modular weights of Higgs fields are described by

\displaystyle(k_{H_{u}}\,,\,k_{H_{d}})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}({\rm Integer}\,,\,{\rm Integer})\\
\left(\cfrac{Mh\pm n}{M}\,,\,\cfrac{Mh\mp n}{M}\right)\quad(\text{mod}\,2M)\end{array}\right.

with
n=1,2,\cdots,M-1
.
Furthermore, the MSSM superpotential (
2.15
) requires

\displaystyle k_{U}+k_{H_{u}}=\text{even}\,,\quad k_{D}+k_{H_{d}}=\text{even}\,,\quad k_{L}+k_{N}+k_{H_{u}}=\text{even}\,,\quad k_{L}+k_{E}+k_{H_{d}}=\text{even}\,,

(5.4)

to be invariant under
\Gamma_{N}
modular symmetries in the basis of
k_{Q}=0
.
The first two equations yield

\displaystyle k_{U}=k_{H_{d}}+\text{even}\,,\qquad k_{D}=k_{H_{u}}+\text{even}\,,

(5.5)

by using Eq. (
5.1
).

Then, possible assignments of modular weights are classified into three cases:

(I)

(k_{H_{u}},k_{H_{d}})=({\rm Integer},{\rm Integer})

In this case, all the matters have integer modular weights from (
5.4
), and it reduces to the scenario discussed in Sec.
2
.
Thus, one can always consider the MSSM with
R
-parity by finite modular symmetries.

(II)

k_{N}=\text{even}
and
(k_{H_{u}},k_{H_{d}})=\left(\frac{Mh\pm n}{M},\frac{Mh\mp n}{M}\right)\quad(\text{mod}\,2M)

In this case, the last two equations in Eq. (
5.4
) reduce to be

\displaystyle k_{L}=k_{H_{d}}+\text{even}\,,\qquad k_{E}=k_{H_{u}}-k_{H_{d}}+\text{even}=\pm\frac{2n}{M}+\text{even}\,.

(5.6)

To summarize, we arrive at

\displaystyle\begin{split}k_{U}=k_{H_{d}}+\text{even}\,,\quad k_{D}=k_{H_{u}}+\text{even}\,,\quad k_{L}=k_{H_{d}}+\text{even}\,,\quad k_{E}=\pm\frac{2n}{M}+\text{even}\,,\end{split}

(5.7)

where
k_{E}
becomes an integer in the
M=2
case.
Thus, it turns out that the following baryon- and/or lepton-number violating operators are described by holomorphic modular forms with even modular weights:

\displaystyle\{LH_{u},\,LL\bar{E},\,LQ\bar{D},Q\bar{U}\bar{E}H_{d},\,LH_{u}LH_{u},\,LH_{u}H_{d}H_{u},\,\bar{U}\bar{D}^{\ast}\bar{E},\,H_{u}^{\ast}H_{d}\bar{E},\,Q\bar{U}L^{\ast}\}\,,

(5.8)

whereas modular weights of other operators
\{\bar{U}\bar{D}\bar{D},\,QQQL,\,\bar{U}\bar{U}\bar{D}\bar{E},\,QQQH_{d},\,QQ\bar{D}^{\ast}\}
are given by

\displaystyle\{k_{H_{u}},\,k_{H_{d}},\,k_{H_{u}},\,k_{H_{d}},\,-k_{H_{u}}\}\,,

(5.9)

up to even integers, respectively.
It is notable that
\{k_{H_{u}},k_{H_{d}}\}
are fractional numbers.
In this way, the operators in Eq. (
5.9
) are not allowed in the modular invariant theories, as summarized in Table
4
.
It indicates the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
baryon triality in the MSSM with
\Gamma_{N}
modular symmetries for an arbitrary value of
M
with
M>1
.

(III)

k_{N}=\text{odd}
and
(k_{H_{u}},k_{H_{d}})=\left(\frac{Mh\pm n}{M},\frac{Mh\mp n}{M}\right)\quad(\text{mod}\,2M)

In this case, the last two equations in Eq. (
5.4
) reduce to be

\displaystyle k_{L}+k_{H_{u}}=\text{odd}\,,\qquad k_{E}=k_{H_{u}}-k_{H_{d}}+\text{odd}=\pm\frac{2n}{M}+\text{odd}\,.

(5.10)

As a result, we arrive at

\displaystyle k_{U}=k_{H_{d}}+\text{even}\,,\quad k_{D}=k_{H_{u}}+\text{even}\,,\quad k_{L}=-k_{H_{u}}+\text{odd}\,,\quad k_{E}=\pm\frac{2n}{M}+\text{odd}\,.

(5.11)

Thus, we find that only the operators:

\displaystyle\{{\rm Yukawa},\,H_{u}H_{d},\,LH_{u}LH_{u}\}

(5.12)

are allowed due to the consequence of holomorphic modular forms with even modular weights,
but other baryon- and/or lepton-number violating operators are prohibited.
Indeed, modular weights of these operators are given by

\displaystyle\{\text{odd},\,\text{odd},\,\text{odd},\,k_{H_{u}},\,-k_{H_{u}},\,k_{H_{u}},\,k_{H_{d}},\,\text{odd},\,\text{odd}\}\,,

(5.13)

for
\{LH_{u},\,LL\bar{E},\,LQ\bar{D},\,\bar{U}\bar{D}\bar{D},\,QQQL,\,\bar{U}\bar{U}\bar{D}\bar{E},\,QQQH_{d},\,Q\bar{U}\bar{E}H_{d},\,LH_{u}H_{d}H_{u}\}
and

\displaystyle\{\text{odd},\,\text{odd},\,\text{odd},\,-k_{H_{u}}\}\,,

(5.14)

for
\{\bar{U}\bar{D}^{\ast}\bar{E},\,H_{u}^{\ast}H_{d}\bar{E},\,Q\bar{U}L^{\ast},\,QQ\bar{D}^{\ast}\}
,
up to even integers, respectively. It is notable that
\{k_{H_{u}},k_{H_{d}}\}
are fractional numbers.
We summarize the existence of these operators in the modular invariant theories in Table

6
Conclusions and Discussions

We systematically studied the baryon- and/or lepton-number violating operators in the modular invariant SUSY models.
Since the modular symmetry will be regarded as the stringy symmetry, it is important to
study the remnant of the modular symmetry in the low-energy effective action.

We revealed that the finite modular symmetries incorporate the generalized matter parities.
In particular, the odd (even) modular weights of quarks/leptons (Higgs) induce the
R
-parity in the MSSM, SUSY
SU(5)
and
SO(10)
GUTs with
\Gamma_{N}
modular symmetries, and fractional modular weights lead to the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
baryon triality and the
\mathbb{Z}_{6}
proton hexality.
It is known that the finite modular symmetries include non-Abelian discrete flavor symmetries which have been utilized to explain the structure of fermion masses and mixing angles
in both the bottom-up and top-down approaches.
Our results indicate that modular symmetries treat the flavor symmetry and generalized matter parities in a unified matter.
Although we focused on
S^{2}
transformation in the modular symmetry, the full
\Gamma_{N}
symmetry further constrain
the possible operators in the modular invariant SUSY models.
For instance,
[LH_{u}]_{F}
is not modular invariant if
L
and
H_{u}
are
triplet and singlet of
\Gamma_{3}
, respectively. However, they are model independent as demonstrated in explicit models in Secs.
3
and
4
. Furthermore, since the
F
-term of modulus chiral superfield has even modular weight
Kikuchi:2022pkd
, the generalized matter parities still control the low-energy effective action below the SUSY-breaking scale.

It is interesting to study the flavor physics and dark matter physics in terms of generalized matter parities from finite modular symmetries.
We will leave the more detailed discussion to future work.

Finally, we comment on possible extensions of our analysis.

•

The existence of generalized matter parities is based on the fact that Yukawa couplings and higher-dimensional operators are described by holomorphic modular forms of even modular weight. If we discuss the double covering of finite modular groups, such a structure will be lost, but some discrete symmetries remain in the low-energy effective action, depending on the modular weights of matter fields.
We hope to report on the whole picture of the double covering in the future.

•

We focus on the holomorphic modular transformation of the modulus
\tau
, which parameterizes the modular group.
When considering the anti-holomorphic modular transformation (
\tau\rightarrow-\bar{\tau}
), that is the CP transformation
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\rm CP}
, the modular group is enlarged to the generalized modular group such as
GL(2,\mathbb{Z})\simeq SL(2,\mathbb{Z})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\rm CP}

Baur:2019kwi
;
Novichkov:2019sqv
which is generalized to multimoduli cases
Ishiguro:2021ccl
.
Since the generalized matter parities are embedded in the subgroup of
SL(2,\mathbb{Z)}
,
the generalized matter parities
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
are also enhanced to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\rm CP}
together with the CP transformation.

•

In this paper, we deal with the discrete symmetry arising from the automorphy factor
(c\tau+d)^{-k_{I}}
in Eq. (
2.11
), which appears in a generic moduli space of
\tau
.
However, there also exist discrete symmetries at
fixed points in the moduli space of
\tau

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
at
\tau=i,\,i\infty
and
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
at
\tau=\omega
with
\omega=\frac{-1+\sqrt{3}}{2}

Ishiguro:2020tmo

When we assign family-independent
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
charges for matter multiplets around these fixed points, one would realize the
R
-parity or baryon triality along the line of our analysis. It is interesting to apply these analysis on the phenomenology of modular flavor models around fixed points

Certain binary minimal codes constructed using simplicial complexes
Vidya Sagar ID and Ritumoni Sarma ID

Abstract
In this manuscript, we work over the non-chain ring ℛ = 𝔽 2 ​ [ u ] / ⟨ u 3 − u ⟩ \mathcal{R}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[u]/\langle u^{3}-u\rangle . Let m ∈ ℕ m\in\mathbb{N} and let L , M , N ⊆ [ m ] := { 1 , 2 , … , m } L,M,N\subseteq[m]:=\{1,2,\dots,m\} . For X ⊆ [ m ] X\subseteq[m] , define Δ X := { v ∈ 𝔽 2 m : Supp ​ ( v ) ⊆ X } \Delta_{X}:=\{v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}:\textnormal{Supp}(v)\subseteq X\} and D := ( 1 + u 2 ) ​ D 1 + u 2 ​ D 2 + ( u + u 2 ) ​ D 3 D:=(1+u^{2})D_{1}+u^{2}D_{2}+(u+u^{2})D_{3} , an ordered finite multiset consisting of elements from ℛ m \mathcal{R}^{m} , where D 1 ∈ { Δ L , Δ L c } , D 2 ∈ { Δ M , Δ M c } , D 3 ∈ { Δ N , Δ N c } D_{1}\in\{\Delta_{L},\Delta_{L}^{c}\},D_{2}\in\{\Delta_{M},\Delta_{M}^{c}\},D_{3}\in\{\Delta_{N},\Delta_{N}^{c}\} . The linear code C D C_{D} over ℛ \mathcal{R} defined by { ( v ⋅ d ) d ∈ D : v ∈ ℛ m } \{\big(v\cdot d\big)_{d\in D}:v\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\} is studied for each D D . Further, we also consider simplicial complexes with two maximal elements in the above work. We study their binary Gray images and the binary subfield-like codes corresponding to a certain 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} -functional of ℛ \mathcal{R} . Sufficient conditions for these binary linear codes to be minimal and self-orthogonal are obtained in each case. Besides, we produce an infinite family of optimal codes with respect to the Griesmer bound. Most of the codes obtained in this manuscript are few-weight codes. INTRODUCTION

This manuscript studies the linear code
C_{D}=\{(v\cdot d)_{d\in D}:v\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\}
over
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[u]/\langle u^{3}-u\rangle
, where
D\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{m}
is an ordered finite multiset. Ding et al. in
[
8
]
first defined the above construction of
C_{D}
in order to generalize the Mattson-Solomon transform for cyclic codes. And due to this construction of linear codes, simplicial complex got huge attention in algebraic coding theory. By using simplicial complexes, several interesting linear codes have been constructed in the recent past. The authors in
[
13
]
studied linear codes by selecting certain defining sets derived using simplicial complexes and obtained a class of binary optimal linear codes. Zhu et al. in
[
36
]
studied linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
and obtained minimal optimal codes. Yansheng et al. in
[
34
]
studied linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
and produced two infinite classes of optimal codes; motivated by this work, the authors in
[
20
]
constructed linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{8}
using simplicial complexes and produced three infinite families of optimal codes. They also discussed sufficient conditions for minimality of these codes by using Ashikhmin-Barg condition (see
[
1
]
). Recently, the authors in
[
19
]
generalized the work of
[
20
]
for finite fields of characteristic
2
by using LFSR sequences. The weight distribution (see
[
12
]
) of a linear code contains crucial information regarding error-detecting as well as error-correcting capacity of the code, and it allows the computation of the error probability of error-detection and -correction with respect to some algorithms
[
15
]
. Few-weight codes are useful because of their connection with strongly regular graphs, difference sets and finite geometry
[
3
,
7
]
. The minimum Hamming distance of linear codes are well known for their importance in determining error-correcting capacity. Due to this reason, finding optimal linear codes has become one of the central topics for researchers. In
[
13
]
, the authors showed how optimal codes can be utilized for the construction of secret sharing schemes with nice access structures following the framework discussed in
[
35
]
.

The study of minimal codes is an active research topic nowadays because of its applications in secure two-party computation and secret sharing schemes
[
4
,
5
,
23
,
35
]
. This special class of codes is also important as these can be decoded by using the minimum distance decoding rule
[
1
]
. Normally, it is difficult to identify all the minimal codewords of a given code even over a finite field of characteristic
2
. Many researchers have studied linear codes over a single or mixed alphabet using simplicial complexes and produced minimal and optimal codes (see
[
14
,
24
,
26
,
25
,
16
,
27
,
28
,
17
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
) in the recent past.

Motivated by the research in
[
28
]
, a natural endeavor is to explore the construction of linear codes over other finite rings using simplicial complexes and obtain minimal and optimal codes. We, in this manuscript, construct linear codes over
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[u]/\langle u^{3}-u\rangle
whose defining sets are obtained from certain simplicial complexes generated by one and two maximal elements, and find their Lee-weight distributions. We study their binary Gray images and their binary subfield-like codes corresponding to certain
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-functional of
\mathcal{R}
. An infinite family of optimal codes are produced with respect to the Griesmer bound. Sufficient conditions for these binary codes to be minimal and self-orthogonal are established. We obtain the parameters and the weight distributions of the 1st order Reed-Muller codes of length
2^{m-1}
, where
m>1
, as the parameters and the weight distributions of certain subfield-like codes. We give a table that shows that our codes include several binary optimal codes with new parameters.

The remaining sections of this manuscript are arranged as follows. Preliminaries are presented in the next section. Section
3
studies the construction of linear codes using simplicial complexes generated by one and two maximal elements. In Section
4
, we obtain Gray images of linear codes constructed using simplicial complexes generated by a single maximal element in the previous section and find their minimality and self-orthogonality conditions. Section
5
studies certain binary subfield-like codes from codes considered in Section
3
and finds conditions for the optimality, minimality, and self-orthogonality of these codes. In Section
6
, we compare our binary codes with the recent research in this direction. In this section, we also present a table that shows that our codes include several optimal codes with new parameters. Section
7
concludes this manuscript.

Definitions and Preliminaries

Throughout the manuscript,
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[u]/\langle u^{3}-u\rangle
, where
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is the binary field. Observe that
\mathcal{R}
is a non-chain principal ideal ring. The group of units is
U(\mathcal{R})=\{1,1+u+u^{2}\}
and the set of non-units is
\mathcal{R}\setminus U(\mathcal{R})=\{0,u,u^{2},u+u^{2},1+u,1+u^{2}\}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
29
]

\mathcal{R}
can be written as
(1+u^{2})\mathbb{F}_{2}+u^{2}\mathbb{F}_{2}+(u+u^{2})\mathbb{F}_{2}
uniquely.

Let
\Phi:\mathcal{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}^{3}
be the
Gray map
given by

\Phi(a+ub+u^{2}d)=(a+b,b+d,d).

(2.1)

This extends to
\Phi:\mathcal{R}^{m}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}^{3m}
where
\Phi(r+us+u^{2}t)=(r+s,s+t,t)
.

Definition 2.2
.

An
\mathcal{R}
-submodule
C
of
\mathcal{R}^{m}
is a
linear code
over
\mathcal{R}
and
m
is its length.

Let
v,w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. Then the
Hamming weight
of
v
, denoted by
wt_{H}(v)
, is the number of nonzero entries in
v
. The
Hamming distance
between
v
and
w
is
d_{H}(v,w)=wt_{H}(v-w)
.

Let
x=\alpha+u\beta+u^{2}\gamma
,
y=\alpha^{\prime}+u\beta^{\prime}+u^{2}\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
, where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. Then the
Lee weight
of
x
is
wt_{Lee}(x)=wt_{H}(\Phi(x))=wt_{H}(\alpha+\beta)+wt_{H}(\beta+\gamma)+wt_{H}(\gamma)
. The
Lee distance
between
x
and
y
is
d_{L}(x,y)=wt_{Lee}(x-y)
. Thus, the map
\Phi
is an isometry. Note that the image of a linear code over
\mathcal{R}
under the Gray map
\Phi
is a binary linear code. Suppose
C
is a linear code over
\mathcal{R}
of length
n
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
A_{i}
be the cardinality of the set that contains all codewords of
C
having Lee weight
i
,
0\leq i\leq 3n
. Then the homogeneous polynomial in two variables

Lee_{C}(X,Y)=\sum_{c\in C}X^{3n-wt_{Lee}(c)}Y^{wt_{Lee}(c)}

is called the
Lee weight enumerator
of
C
and the string
(1,A_{1},\dots,A_{3n})
is called the
Lee weight distribution
of
C
. Similarly, we can define the Hamming weight enumerator and the Hamming weight distribution for a linear code over a finite field. In addition, if the total number of
i\geq 1
such that
A_{i}\neq 0
is
l
, then
C
is called an
l
-
weight linear code
. Every
1
-weight linear code is an equidistant linear code. In
[
2
]
, Bonisoli characterized all equidistant linear codes over finite fields.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
2
]
(Bonisoli)

Suppose
C
is an equidistant linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
C
is equivalent to the
r
-fold replication of a simplex code, possibly with added
0
-coordinates.

An
[n,k,d]
-linear code
C
is called
distance optimal
if there exists no
[n,k,d+1]
-linear code (see
[
12
]
). Next we recall the Griesmer bound.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
11
]

(Griesmer Bound)
If
C
is an
[n,k,d]
-linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then we have

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\left\lceil\frac{d}{q^{i}}\right\rceil\leq n,

(2.2)

where
\lceil\cdot\rceil
denotes the ceiling function.

A linear code is called a
Griesmer code
if equality holds in Equation (
2.2
). Note that every Griesmer code is distance optimal, but the converse needs not be true.

3
Construction of linear codes over the ring
\mathcal{R}

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we shall write
[m]
to denote the set
\{1,2,\dots,m\}
.
For any
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
D=\{d_{1}<d_{2}<\dots<d_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{m}
be an ordered multiset of cardinality
n
. Define
[
12
]
,

C_{D}=\{(v\cdot d_{1},v\cdot d_{2},\dots,v\cdot d_{n}):v\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\},

(3.1)

where
x\cdot y=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}y_{i}
for
x,y\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
. Then
C_{D}
forms a linear code of length
|D|
over
\mathcal{R}
. The ordered set
D
is called the
defining set
of
C_{D}
. Note that on changing the ordering of
D
we will get a code which is permutation equivalent to
C_{D}
. If
D
is chosen appropriately,
C_{D}
may possess good parameters. For
w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, the set
\textnormal{Supp}(w)=\{i\in[m]:w_{i}=1\}
is called the
support
of
w
. Note that the Hamming weight of
w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
is
wt_{H}(w)=|\textnormal{Supp}(w)|
. For
v,w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, one says that
v
covers
w
if
\textnormal{Supp}(w)\subseteq\textnormal{Supp}(v)
. If
v
covers
w
, we write
w\preceq v
.

Consider the map
\psi:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\longrightarrow 2^{[m]}
is defined as
\psi(w)=\textnormal{Supp}(w)
, where
2^{[m]}
denotes the power set of
[m]
. Note that
\psi
is a bijective map. Now onwards, we will write
w
instead of Supp(
w
) whenever we require.

Definition 3.1
.

A subset
\Delta
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
is called a
simplicial complex
if
v\in\Delta,w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
and
w\preceq v

\implies

w\in\Delta
. An element
v\in\Delta
is called a
maximal element of

\Delta
if for
w\in\Delta
,
v\preceq w

\implies

v=w
. A simplicial complex can have more than one maximal elements.

3.1
Using simplicial complexes having one maximal element

In this subsection, we study linear codes over
\mathcal{R}
using simplicial complexes generated by one maximal element.

Let
L\subseteq[m]
. The
simplicial complex generated by

L
is denoted by
\Delta_{L}
and is defined as

\Delta_{L}=\{w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}|\textnormal{ Supp}(w)\subseteq L\}.

(3.2)

Note that
\psi^{-1}(L)
is the only maximal element of
\Delta_{L}
and
|\Delta_{L}|=|2^{L}|=2^{|L|}
.

Given a subset
P
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, define the polynomial (referred to as an
m-variable generating function
, see
[
6
]
)
\mathcal{H}_{P}(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{m})
by

\mathcal{H}_{P}(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{m})=\sum\limits_{v\in P}\prod_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}^{v_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{m}],

(3.3)

where
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})
and
\mathbb{Z}
denotes the ring of integers.

We recall a lemma from
[
6
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

[
6
]

Suppose
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
is a simplicial complex and
\mathcal{F}
consists of its maximal elements. Then

\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{m})=\sum\limits_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq\mathcal{F}}(-1)^{|S|+1}\prod_{i\in\cap S}(1+y_{i}),

(3.4)

where
\cap S=\bigcap\limits_{F\in S}\textnormal{Supp}(F)
. In particular, we have

|\Delta|=\sum\limits_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq\mathcal{F}}(-1)^{|S|+1}2^{|\cap S|}.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and let
D_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m},1\leq i\leq 3
. Assume that
D=(1+u^{2})D_{1}+u^{2}D_{2}+(u+u^{2})D_{3}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{m}
.

Observe that
c_{D}:\mathcal{R}^{m}\longrightarrow C_{D}
defined by

c_{D}(v)=\big(v\cdot d\big)_{d\in D}

(3.5)

is an epimorphism of
\mathcal{R}
-modules. Assume that
x=(1+u^{2})\alpha+u^{2}\beta+(u+u^{2})\gamma\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
and

, where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
and
t_{i}\in D_{i},1\leq i\leq 3
. Then the Lee weight of
c_{D}(x)
is

\begin{split}wt_{Lee}(c_{D}(x))=&~wt_{Lee}\big(\big(\big((1+u^{2})\alpha+u^{2}\beta+(u+u^{2})\gamma\big)\cdot\big((1+u^{2})t_{1}+u^{2}t_{2}+(u+u^{2})t_{3}\big)\big)_{t_{i}\in D_{i}}\big)\\

&~+wt_{H}\big(\big(\alpha t_{1}+(\beta+\gamma)t_{2}+\beta t_{3}\big)_{t_{i}\in D_{i}}\big).\end{split}

Now if

&+\sum\limits_{t_{2}\in D_{2}}(-1)^{(\beta+\gamma)t_{2}}\sum\limits_{t_{3}\in D_{3}}(-1)^{\beta t_{3}}\big].\end{split}

Note that, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
and

This extends to a map from

\tau\big((x\cdot d)_{d\in D}\big)=\big(\tau(x\cdot d)\big)_{d\in D}

Remark 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{1+u^{2}<u^{2}<u+u^{2}\}
. This is an ordered basis of the vector space
\mathcal{R}
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. For

be the left multiplication map, that is,

l_{x}(y)=xy.

(5.3)

Then

is an
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-linear transformation. Define the map
\eta:\mathcal{R}\longrightarrow M_{3\times 3}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
by
\eta(x)=[l_{x}]_{\mathcal{B}}
, the matrix representation of

with respect to

. Then,

\displaystyle\eta(0)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\eta(1)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\eta(u)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&1&1\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\eta(u^{2})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\eta(1+u)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\eta(1+u^{2})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\eta(u+u^{2})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\eta(1+u+u^{2})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\

\mathbb{F}_{2}
-linear map

(5.4)

Let

Proof.
We prove part (
2
) and other parts can be proved similarly.

Observe, the length of
\tau(C_{D})
is
|D|=(2^{m}-2^{|L|})2^{|M|+|N|}
.
Suppose
x=(1+u^{2})\alpha+u^{2}\beta+(u+u^{2})\gamma\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
and
D=(1+u^{2})\Delta_{L}^{\textnormal{c}}+u^{2}\Delta_{M}+(u+u^{2})\Delta_{N}
. Then by Equation (
3.7
), Equation (
5.7
) and Lemma
3.4
, we have

\begin{split}wt_{H}\big(\tau(c_{D}(x))\big)=&\frac{1}{2}|D|-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d_{1}\in\Delta_{L}^{\textnormal{c}}}(-1)^{\alpha d_{1}}\sum_{d_{2}\in\Delta_{M}}(1)\sum_{d_{3}\in\Delta_{N}}(1)\\
=&(2^{m}-2^{|L|})2^{|M|+|N|-1}-\big(2^{m}\delta_{0,\alpha}-2^{|L|}\chi(\alpha|L)\big)2^{|M|+|N|-1}.\end{split}

Here we discuss the following cases.

1.

If
\alpha=0
then
wt_{H}\big(\tau(c_{D}(x))\big)=0
.

In this case,
\#\alpha=1,\#\beta=2^{m},\#\gamma=2^{m}
. Therefore,
Z_{0}=2^{2m}
.

2.

If
\alpha\neq 0
then
wt_{H}\big(\tau(c_{D}(x))\big)=(2^{m}-2^{|L|})2^{|M|+|N|-1}+2^{|L|+|M|+|N|-1}\chi(\alpha|L)
.

(a)

If
\chi(\alpha|L)=0
then
wt_{H}\big(\tau(c_{D}(x))\big)=(2^{m}-2^{|L|})2^{|M|+|N|-1}=j_{1}
.
In this case, by using Lemma
3.3
, we get

\#\alpha=(2^{|L|}-1)\times 2^{m-|L|}
,

\#\beta=2^{m}
,

\#\gamma=2^{m}
.

Therefore,
Z_{j_{1}}=(2^{|L|}-1)\times 2^{3m-|L|}
.

(b)

If
\chi(\alpha|L)=1
then
wt_{H}\big(\tau(c_{D}(x))\big)=2^{m+|M|+|N|-1}=j_{2}
.
In this case, by using Lemma
3.3
, we get

\#\alpha=(2^{m-|L|}-1)
,

\#\beta=2^{m}
,

\#\gamma=2^{m}
.

Therefore,
Z_{j_{2}}=2^{2m}(2^{m-|L|}-1)
.

Note that
T:\mathcal{R}^{m}\longrightarrow\tau(C_{D})\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{|D|}
is a surjective homomorphism and
|\ker(T)|=|\{x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}:\tau(x\cdot d)=0~\forall~d\in D\}|=Z_{0}=2^{2m}
. By the first isomorphism theorem, we have
|\tau(C_{D})|=\frac{|\mathcal{R}^{m}|}{|\ker(T)|}=2^{m}
. Hence,
\dim(\tau(C_{D}))=m
.

Note that, the minimum and maximum Hamming weight of nonzero codewords of
\tau(C_{D})
are
w_{0}=(2^{m}-2^{|L|})2^{|M|+|N|-1}
and
w_{\infty}=2^{m+|M|+|N|-1}
, respectively. Now we get

\frac{w_{0}}{w_{\infty}}=\frac{(2^{m}-2^{|L|})2^{|M|+|N|-1}}{2^{m+|M|+|N|-1}}>\frac{1}{2}\iff|L|\leq m-2
.

Hence, by Lemma
4.1
,
\tau(C_{D})
is minimal provided
|L|\leq m-2
.

(
2a
) Let
1\leq|L|+|M|+|N|\leq m
and
\theta_{1}=2^{|M|+|N|}-1
. The parameters of the code
\tau(C_{D})
are
n=(2^{m}-2^{|L|})2^{|M|+|N|}
,
k=m
and
d=(2^{m}-2^{|L|})2^{|M|+|N|-1}
. We get,

\begin{split}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\left\lceil\frac{2^{m+|M|+|N|-1}-2^{|L|+|M|+|N|-1}}{2^{i}}\right\rceil=&\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{2^{m+|M|+|N|-1}}{2^{i}}-\sum_{i=0}^{|L|+|M|+|N|-1}\left\lfloor\frac{2^{|L|+|M|+|N|-1}}{2^{i}}\right\rfloor-\\
&~~\sum_{i=|L|+|M|+|N|}^{m-1}\left\lfloor\frac{2^{|L|+|M|+|N|-1}}{2^{i}}\right\rfloor\\
=&~(2^{m+|M|+|N|}-2^{|M|+|N|})-(2^{|L|+|M|+|N|}-1)-0\\
=&~n-\theta_{1}.\end{split}

Therefore,
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\lceil\frac{d}{2^{i}}\rceil\leq n\iff\theta_{1}\geq 0
.

On the other hand,

\begin{split}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\left\lceil\frac{2^{m+|M|+|N|-1}-2^{|L|+|M|+|N|-1}+1}{2^{i}}\right\rceil=&\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{2^{m+|M|+|N|-1}}{2^{i}}\\
&~~-\sum_{i=0}^{|L|+|M|+|N|-1}\left\lfloor\frac{2^{|L|+|M|+|N|-1}}{2^{i}}-\frac{1}{2^{i}}\right\rfloor\\
&~~-\sum_{i=|L|+|M|+|N|}^{m-1}\left\lfloor\frac{2^{|L|+|M|+|N|-1}}{2^{i}}-\frac{1}{2^{i}}\right\rfloor\\
=&~(2^{m+|M|+|N|}-2^{|M|+|N|})-\{(2^{|L|+|M|+|N|}-1)\\
&~~-(|L|+|M|+|N|)\}-0\\
=&~n-\theta_{1}+|L|+|M|+|N|.\end{split}

Therefore,
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\lceil\frac{d+1}{2^{i}}\rceil>n\iff\theta_{1}<|L|+|M|+|N|
.

This completes the proof of (
2a
).

Part (
2b
) can be proved using the same argument.

7
Conclusion

In this manuscript, certain linear codes over the ring
\mathbb{F}_{2}[u]/\langle u^{3}-u\rangle
are studied with the help of simplicial complexes generated by one and two maximal elements. Under very mild conditions, their Gray images and their binary subfield-like codes corresponding to a certain
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-functional of
\mathcal{R}
, are minimal codes. Most of the binary codes here are few-weight codes. The binary codes in this manuscript are self-orthogonal under certain sufficient conditions. Besides, we obtain an infinite family of optimal codes with respect to the Griesmer bound.

In future, one can study linear codes over the ring
\mathbb{F}_{2}[u]/\langle u^{t}-u\rangle
for any
t\geq 4
using simplicial complexes by devising an elegant method and obtain linear codes with nice parameters.

Polynomial representation of additive cyclic codes and new quantum codes
Reza Dastbasteh and Khalil Shivji

Abstract
. We give a polynomial representation for additive cyclic codes over 𝔽 p 2 \mathbb{F}_{p^{2}} . This representation will be applied to uniquely present each additive cyclic code by at most two generator polynomials. We determine the generator polynomials of all different additive cyclic codes. A minimum distance lower bound for additive cyclic codes will also be provided using linear cyclic codes over 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} . We classify all the symplectic self-dual, self-orthogonal, and nearly self-orthogonal additive cyclic codes over 𝔽 p 2 \mathbb{F}_{p^{2}} . Finally, we present ten record-breaking binary quantum codes after applying a quantum construction to self-orthogonal and nearly self-orthogonal additive cyclic codes over 𝔽 4 \mathbb{F}_{4} .



Keywords:
additive cyclic codes, quantum code, self-orthogonal codes, self-dual codes

1.
Introduction

Quantum error-correcting codes, or simply quantum codes, are used in quantum computation to protect quantum information from corruption by noise (decoherence). A general framework of quantum codes is provided in
[
9
,
13
]
. Throughout this paper,
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
is the finite field of
p^{2}
elements, where
p
is a prime number. The parameters of a quantum code over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
that encodes
k
logical qubits to
n
physical qubits and has minimum distance
d
is denoted by
[[n,k,d]]_{p}
.
An important family of quantum codes with many similar properties as classical block codes is the family of quantum stabilizer codes. In particular, quantum stabilizer codes are constructed using additive codes which are self-orthogonal with respect to a certain symplectic inner product. Several constructions of quantum stabilizer codes from various classical codes are given in
[
18
]
.
An interesting modification of the original definition of quantum stabilizer codes is by relaxing its self-orthogonality constraint
[
5
,
19
]
. This method enables us to construct good quantum codes using not necessarily self-orthogonal additive codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
. Previously, this modification was applied for the construction of new quantum codes from different families of linear codes
[
6
,
10
,
20
]
.

Additive cyclic codes are of interest due to their rich algebraic properties and application in the construction of quantum codes.
There have been several works in the literature toward the classification of additive cyclic codes for different applications
[
1
,
4
,
7
,
16
,
17
,
21
]
, and also due to their connection to other families of block codes such as quasi-cyclic codes
[
15
]
.
In
[
16
]
, a canonical decomposition of additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
was introduced using certain finite field extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{4}
. This decomposition was applied to determine self-orthogonal and self-dual additive cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
with respect to the trace inner product. In
[
3
]
, it was shown that each additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
of length
n
can be generated by
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-span of at most two polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{4}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
and their cyclic shifts. Moreover, a criterion for the self-orthogonality of such codes with respect to the trace inner product was provided.
Another interesting construction for a subclass of additive cyclic code, namely twisted codes, was provided in
[
1
]
. This construction is analogous to the way linear cyclic codes are constructed. In spite of many useful properties of twisted codes, all additive cyclic codes cannot be described using the theory of additive twisted codes.

In this work, we first give a canonical representation of all
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-additive cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
using at most two generator polynomials. Our representation is more computationally friendly than the canonical representation of
[
16
]
. This representation allows us to give a minimum distance lower bound for additive cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
using the minimum distance of linear cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Moreover, we provide a unique set of generator polynomials for each additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. This representation of generator polynomials will be used to characterize all self-orthogonal and self-dual additive cyclic codes with respect to the symplectic inner product.
We also determine the generator polynomials of the symplectic dual of a given additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, and compute nearly the self-orthogonality of each additive cyclic code using only its generator polynomials. This allows us to apply the nearly self-orthogonal construction of quantum codes developed in
[
5
,
19
]
.
In particular, we provide a list of eleven record-breaking binary quantum codes after applying the mentioned quantum construction to nearly self-orthogonal additive cyclic codes. Furthermore, applying secondary constructions to our new quantum codes produce many more record-breaking binary codes. Note that such new quantum codes cannot be constructed using self-orthogonal additive cyclic codes of the same length.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
briefly recalls the essential terminologies used in this work.
Section
3
gives a canonical representation of additive cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. In fact, we follow a module theory approach to decompose each additive cyclic code using its polynomial representation in

\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
.
In Section
4
, we compute the symplectic dual of each additive cyclic code. We provide the necessary and sufficient conditions for an additive cyclic code to be self-orthogonal, self-dual, or nearly self-orthogonality with respect to the symplectic inner product.
Finally, in Section
5
, we present the parameters of our record-breaking quantum codes.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\omega
be a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. Then the set
\{1,\omega\}
forms a basis for
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Let
a+b\omega
and
a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{n}
, where
a,a^{\prime},b,b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
. The
symplectic inner product
of
a+b\omega
and
a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\omega
is defined by

\langle a+b\omega,a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\omega\rangle_{s}=a^{\prime}\cdot b-a\cdot b^{\prime}.

(2.1)

An
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear subspace
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{n}
is called a length
n

additive code
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. We denote the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dimension of an additive code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
with
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C)
.
Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{n}
be an additive code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C)=k
. Then we call
C
an
(n,p^{k})
code.
The set

C^{{\bot}_{s}}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle_{s}=0
for all
y\in C\}
.

is called the
symplectic dual
of
C
. One can easily see that
C^{{\bot}_{s}}
is an
(n,p^{2n-k})
additive code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. The code
C
is called
self-orthogonal
(respectively
self-dual
) if
C\subseteq C^{\bot_{s}}
(respectively if
C=C^{\bot_{s}}
). For each
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{n}
, we denote the number of non-zero coordinates of
x
by
{\rm wt}(x)
. Moreover, the minimum weight among non-zero vectors of an additive code
C
is denoted by
d(C)
.
The connection between quantum stabilizer codes and classical additive codes was initially formulated by the independent works of Calderbank, Rains, Shor, and Sloane
[
3
]
and Gottesman
[
11
]
.
A non-binary version of this connection is provided below.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
18
, Corollary 16]

Let
C
be an
(n,p^{n-k})
additive code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. Then there exists an
[[n,k,d]]_{p}
quantum stabilizer code if
C
is symplectic self-orthogonal, where
d=\min\{{\rm wt}(x):x\in C{{}^{\bot}_{s}}\setminus C\}
if
k>0
and
d=\min\{{\rm wt}(x):x\in C\}
if
k=0
.

The quantum code of Theorem
2.1
is called
pure
if
d=d(C^{\bot_{s}})
. There are several secondary constructions of quantum code. A short list of such constructions is provided below.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
18
, Section XV]

Let
C
be an
[[n,k,d]]_{p}
quantum code.

(1)

If
k>0
, then an
[[n+1,k,d]]_{p}
quantum code exists.

(2)

If
C
is pure and
n,d\geq 2
, then an
[[n-1,k+1,d-1]]_{p}
pure quantum code exists.

(3)

If
k>1
, then there exists an
[[n,k-1,d]]_{p}
quantum code.

3.
Additive cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}

Throughout this section, we assume that
n
is a positive integer such that
(n,p)=1
and
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}=\{\alpha+\beta\omega:\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
, where
\omega
is a root of a degree two irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
In this section, we provide a canonical representation of additive cyclic codes over the field
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. In particular, we give a unique representation of each additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
using at most two generator polynomials. Moreover, we determine the generator polynomials of all different additive cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.
In particular, each additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
is a linear combination of cyclic shifts of its generator polynomials.
Such representation is also suitable for practical computations of additive cyclic codes, especially using Magma computer algebra system
[
2
]
.
More particularly, there exists a built-in function in Magma which forms additive cyclic codes generated by two given generator polynomials. At the end of this section, we give a minimum distance lower bound for the minimum distance of additive cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
using the minimum distance of linear cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Definition 3.1
.

An
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subspace
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{n}
is called an
additive cyclic code
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, if for every
(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1})\in C
, the vector
(a_{n-1},a_{0},\dots,a_{n-2})
is also a codeword of
C
.

We will use the following concepts of module theory frequently in this section, and for more details one, for example, can see
[
8
, Chapter 12]
. Let
R
be a principal ideal domain and
M
be an
R
-module. The
annihilator
of
M
is an ideal of
R
defined by
\{r\in R:rm=0\ \text{for any}\ m\in M\}
. An element
m\in M
is called a
torsion element
, if there exists
0\neq r\in R
such that
rm=0
. The module
M
is called a
torsion module
if all of its elements are torsion.
The following theorem, known as the primary decomposition theorem of modules, plays an important role in our representation of additive cyclic codes.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
8
, Chapter
12
, Theorem
7
]

Let
R
be a principal ideal domain and
M
be a torsion
R
-module with the annihilator
\langle a\rangle\neq 0
. Let
a=u\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}^{a_{i}}
, where
u
is a unit and
p_{i}
is a prime element for each
1\leq i\leq n
. Then we can decompose
M
as a direct sum of its submodules in the form

M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}N_{i},

(3.1)

where
N_{i}=\{x\in M:xp_{i}^{a_{i}}=0\}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
.

Each element
(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{n}
can be represented uniquely as a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
in the form
\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}x^{i}
.
One can easily verify that, under this correspondence, a length
n
additive cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
-submodule of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
.

Notation 3.3
.

Let
f
and
g\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
. We fix the following notations for the rest of this paper.

(1)

The ideal generated by
f
in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
is denoted by
\langle f\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]}
. Equivalently it is the
{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]}
-submodule of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
generated by the polynomial
f
.

(2)

The
{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
-submodule of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
generated by the polynomial
g
is denoted by
\langle g\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
.

A straightforward computation shows that the annihilator of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
-module is the ideal
\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
. Moreover, we can decompose
x^{n}-1
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
as
x^{n}-1=\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{s}f_{i}(x)
, where each
f_{i}(x)
is an irreducible polynomial corresponding to a
p
-cyclotomic coset modulo
n
. Next, we apply Theorem
3.2
to
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
.
It is straightforward to see that

\ \mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}N_{i},

(3.2)

where
N_{i}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]}
for each
1\leq i\leq s
.
We call a non-zero length
n
additive cyclic code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}

irreducible
if for any additive cyclic code
D\subseteq C
, then
D=\{0\}
or
D=C
.
The next lemma shows that each
N_{i}
can be decomposed as a direct sum of two irreducible additive cyclic codes. We determine the generator polynomial of all irreducible additive cyclic codes inside
N_{i}
and provide other useful information about additive cyclic codes inside each
N_{i}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f(x)
be an irreducible divisor of
x^{n}-1
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
with
\deg(f)=k
and
N=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]}
.

(1)

Let
0\neq r(x)\in N
, then the set
L=\{r(x),xr(x),\ldots,x^{k-1}r(x)\}
forms a basis for
\langle r(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
vector space.

(2)

Let
0\neq C\subsetneq N
be an additive cyclic code.
The code
C
has
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dimension
k
and
C=\langle r(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
for any
0\neq r(x)\in C
.

(3)

The additive cyclic code
N
can be decomposed as

N=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}\oplus\langle\omega((x^{n}-1)/f(x))\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}.

Moreover,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(N)=2k
and
N
is linear over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.

(4)

The number of irreducible additive cyclic codes inside
N
is
2^{k}+1
. In particular, the following set gives all the different generator polynomials of such additive cyclic codes.

A=\{\big((x^{n}-1)/f(x)\big)\big(\omega+g(x)\big):g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x],\deg(g(x))<k\}\cup\{(x^{n}-1)/f(x)\}.

(3.3)

Proof.

(1)
Obviously
L\subseteq\langle r(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
. Suppose, on the contrary, that
L
is linearly dependent over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Hence we can find a polynomial
0\neq s(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
of degree less than
k
such that
r(x)s(x)\equiv 0\pmod{x^{n}-1}
. Since
(x^{n}-1)/f(x)\mid r(x)
and
f(x)
is irreducible, we conclude that
f(x)\mid s(x)
. However, it is a contradiction with the fact that
\deg(s(x))<k
.
This shows that
L
is linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Note that the set
L\cup\{x^{k}r(x)\}
is linearly dependent over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
as this new set generates
f(x)r(x)\equiv 0\pmod{x^{n}-1}
. In a similar fashion, one can show that
\{x^{i}r(x)\}
for
k<i<n-1
can be written as a linear combination of elements of
L
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Therefore,
L
forms a basis for
\langle r(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
.

(2)
Let
0\neq r(x)\in C
. Suppose in contrary that
\langle r(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}\subsetneq C
. Then there exists a polynomial
s(x)\in C
such that
s(x)\not\in\langle r(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
. Note that
\langle r(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}\cap\langle s(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}=\{0\}
as otherwise, by part (1), for any polynomial
a(x)
in the intersection, we have

\langle r(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}=\langle a(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}=\langle s(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]},

which is a contradiction.
Thus
C=\langle r(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
and has dimension
k
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

(3)
It is easy to see that
\langle(x^{n}-1)/f(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}\cap\langle\omega((x^{n}-1)/f(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}=\{0\}
and

N=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}\oplus\langle\omega((x^{n}-1)/f(x))\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}.

Hence
N
has dimension
2k
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The linearity part follows immediately from the structure of its generator polynomials.

(4)
In order to find an additive cyclic code with
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dimension
k
, we need to choose a nonzero polynomial
r(x)\in N
to be its generator. Also, any non-zero elements of
\langle r(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
generates the same code. Hence the number of additive cyclic codes with one non-zero generator inside
N
is
\frac{2^{2k}-1}{2^{k}-1}=2^{k}+1
.

Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two
k
-dimensional additive cyclic codes inside
N
. If
C_{1}\cap C_{2}\neq\{0\}
, then
C_{1}=C_{2}
by part
(1)
. Equivalently, if
C_{1}+C_{2}=N
, then
C_{1}\cap C_{2}=\{0\}
.
Now we show that different elements of the set
A
generate different codes. Let
g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
such that
\deg(g(x))<k
.
Clearly the additive cyclic code
C_{1}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f(x),((x^{n}-1)/f(x))(g(x)+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
contains
(x^{n}-1)/f(x)
and
\omega(x^{n}-1)/f(x)
. Therefore
C_{1}=N
. So
\langle(x^{n}-1)/f(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
and
\langle((x^{n}-1)/f(x))(g(x)+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
are different additive cyclic codes.

Let
g_{1}(x)
and
g_{2}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
be two different polynomials of degree less than
k
. The code
C=\langle((x^{n}-1)/f(x))(\omega+g_{1}(x)),((x^{n}-1)/f(x))(\omega+g_{2}(x))\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
contains
(x^{n}-1)/f(x)
and
\omega(x^{n}-1)/f(x)
. It is mainly because

\langle\big((x^{n}-1)/f(x)\big)(g_{1}(x)-g_{2}(x))\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}.

Thus
C=N
. This implies that the additive cyclic codes
\langle((x^{n}-1)/f(x))(\omega+g_{1}(x))\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
and
\langle((x^{n}-1)/f(x))(\omega+g_{2}(x))\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
are different.
This proves that the set
A
contains all the different generators of irreducible additive cyclic codes inside
N
.
∎

As we mentioned in part
(1)
of Lemma
3.4
, each additive cyclic code inside
\langle(x^{n}-1)/f(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]}
can have many different generator polynomials.
Through the next remark, we fix a canonical representation for each additive cyclic code inside
N
.

Remark 3.5
.

For each additive code
0\neq C\subsetneq\langle(x^{n}-1)/f(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]}
, we fix its generator polynomial inside the set
A
, introduced in
(\ref{one generator form})
, to be “the” generator polynomial of
C
.
Similarly, the additive cyclic code
C^{\prime}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]}
can be generated by the polynomials
(x^{n}-1)/f(x)
and
\omega((x^{n}-1)/f(x))
. We call them “the” generator polynomials of
C^{\prime}
.

This representation helps to uniquely identify each additive cyclic code inside
N
and avoid considering the same code more than once.
Next, we use the result of Lemma
3.4
and characterize all the additive cyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. Recall that
x^{n}-1=\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{s}f_{i}(x)
, where
f_{i}(x)
is an irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
for each
1\leq i\leq s
and
N_{i}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
C
be a length
n
additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. Then

(i)

we can decompose the code
C
as
C=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}C_{i}
, where each
C_{i}
is an additive cyclic code inside
N_{i}
.

(ii)

we have
C=\langle g(x)+\omega k(x),\omega h(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
, where

(a)

g(x)+\omega k(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}g_{i}(x)+\omega k_{i}(x)
,

(b)

h(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}h_{i}(x)
,

(c)

and
C_{i}
has the generator polynomial(s)
g_{i}(x)+\omega k_{i}(x)
and
\omega h_{i}(x)
selected as discussed in Remark
3.5
.

(iii)

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}(\deg(f_{i})\times\#
of non-zero generators of
C_{i}
).

Proof.

(i) As we mentioned in
(\ref{equation1})
, the following decomposition holds

\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}N_{i}.

So we can express
C
as
C=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}C_{i}
, where each
C_{i}
is an additive cyclic codes inside
N_{i}
.

(ii) We show that the additive cyclic codes
C=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}C_{i}
and
\langle g(x)+\omega k(x),\omega h(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
are the same. First note that
g(x)+\omega k(x),\omega h(x)\in C
and thus
\langle g(x)+\omega k(x),\omega h(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}\subseteq C
.
Let
1\leq i\leq s
be a fixed integer. Since
\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\mid g_{i}(x),k_{i}(x),h_{i}(x)
and

\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)g_{j}(x)\equiv\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)k_{j}(x)\equiv\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)h_{j}(x)\equiv 0\pmod{x^{n}-1}

for any
j\neq i
,
we have

\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\big(g(x)+\omega k(x)\big)\equiv\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\big(g_{i}(x)+\omega k_{i}(x)\big)\pmod{x^{n}-1}

and

\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\omega h(x)\equiv\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\omega h_{i}(x)\pmod{x^{n}-1}.

Moreover, we have

C_{i}=\langle g_{i}(x)+\omega k_{i}(x),\omega h_{i}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}=\langle\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\big(g(x)+\omega k(x)\big),\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\omega h(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}.

Thus

C_{i}\subseteq\langle g(x)+\omega k(x),\omega h(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}.

This show that
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}C_{i}\subseteq\langle g(x)+\omega k(x),\omega h(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
and completes the proof.

(iii) Note that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C_{i})
. Moreover, by Lemmas
3.4
,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C_{i})=0
,
k_{i}
, or
2k_{i}
if
C_{i}=0
,
C_{i}
is generated by one generator polynomial, or
C_{i}
has two generator polynomials, respectively. Combining these facts with the result of part (i) completes this proof.
∎

Through the next corollary, we characterize all the length
n
irreducible additive cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
C
be an additive cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. Then
C
is irreducible if and only if
C=\langle r(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
for some
0\neq r(x)\in N_{i}
and
1\leq i\leq s
. Moreover, there are
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}(2^{\deg(f_{i})}+1)
many different irreducible additive cyclic codes.

Proof.

Let
C=\langle r(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
for some
0\neq r(x)\in N_{i}
and
1\leq i\leq s
.
The result of part (1) in Lemma
3.4
shows that
C
is irreducible. Conversely, let
C
be an irreducible additive cyclic code. Then by part (i) of Theorem
3.6
we have
C=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}C_{i}
. Since
C
is irreducible, we have
C=C_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq s
. Moreover, since
N_{j}
is not irreducible by Lemma
3.4

part (3), we conclude that
C=\langle r(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
for some
0\neq r(x)\in N_{j}
.

Inside each
N_{i}
, there are
2^{\deg(f_{i})}+1
many different one generator additive cyclic codes. Hence the total number of irreducible codes is
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}(2^{\deg(f_{i})}+1)
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Henceforth, we always represent each additive cyclic code with its generator polynomials
g(x)+\omega k(x)
and
\omega h(x)
introduced in part (ii) of Theorem
3.6
. Moreover, the way we generate these polynomials is unique, and therefore each additive cyclic code has a unique set of generators.

From now on, we call
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
-linear cyclic codes simply linear cyclic codes.
Let
C=\langle g(x)+\omega k(x),\omega h(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
be a length
n
additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.
Note that Theorem
3.6
and part (3) of Lemma
3.4
imply that
C
is linear if and only if
g(x)=h(x)
and
k(x)=0
.
Hence linear cyclic codes can be easily distinguished from non-linear cyclic codes.

Next, we provide a minimum distance bound for additive cyclic codes using linear cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. In general, the minimum distance computation for linear codes is faster than the additive codes.
Hence the following result can speed up the minimum distance computation for additive cyclic codes. We denote the minimum distance of a code
C
with
d(C)
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
C=\langle g(x)+\omega k(x),\omega h(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
be a length
n
additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.
Let
G(x)=\frac{x^{n}-1}{\gcd(x^{n}-1,g(x))}
, and let
S(x)
be the generator polynomial of the intersection of the length
n
linear cyclic code generated by
k(x)
and the linear cyclic code generated by
h(x)
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Suppose that
D_{1}
,
D_{2}
,
D_{3}
, and
D_{3}
are the length
n
linear cyclic codes
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
generated by
g(x)
,
\gcd(k(x),h(x))
,
\gcd(G(x)k(x),h(x))
, and
\frac{g(x)S(x)}{\gcd(x^{n}-1,k(x))}
, respectively. Then

\min\{d(D_{3}),d(D_{4}),\max\{d(D_{1}),d(D_{2})\}\}\leq d(C).

(3.4)

Proof.

Only the following three types of codewords may appear in the code
C
.

T_{1}=\{a(x)\in C:\ 0\neq a(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]\},

T_{2}=\{\omega b(x)\in C:\ 0\neq b(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]\},

T_{3}=\{a(x)+\omega b(x)\in C:\ 0\neq a(x),0\neq b(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]\}.

We bound the minimum distance of
C
by considering the minimum distance in each of these sets. Let
f(x)\in T_{1}
. Then we can write it as
f(x)=a_{1}(x)(g(x)+\omega k(x))+b_{1}(x)\omega h(x)
for some
a_{1}(x),b_{1}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
. Hence
f(x)=a_{1}(x)g(x)
and
a_{1}(x)k(x)+b_{1}(x)h(x)\equiv 0\pmod{x^{n}-1}
. This implies that
a_{1}(x)k(x)
is an element of the length
n
linear cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
generated by
S(x)
. Hence
\frac{S(x)}{\gcd(x^{n}-1,k(x))}\mid a_{1}(x)
. In other words,
f(x)=a(x)g(x)\in D_{4}
.

Next, let
\omega f_{1}(x)\in T_{2}
. Then
\omega f_{1}(x)=a_{1}(x)(g(x)+\omega k(x))+b_{1}(x)\omega h(x)
for some
a_{1}(x),b_{1}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
. Then
a_{1}(x)g(x)\equiv 0\pmod{x^{n}-1}
or equivalently
G(x)\mid a_{1}(x)
. This implies that
f_{1}(x)=a_{1}(x)k(x)+b_{1}(x)h(x)
.
Therefore,
f_{1}(x)\in D_{3}
.

Finally, let
a(x)+\omega b(x)\in T_{3}
. Then
a(x)+\omega b(x)=l(x)(g(x)+\omega k(x))+m(x)\omega h(x)
for some
l(x),m(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
. Hence
a(x)\in D_{1}
and
b(x)\in D_{2}
. This implies that
{\rm wt}(a(x)+\omega b(x))\geq\max\{d(D_{1}),d(D_{2})\}
.
∎

Note that if
D_{i}=0
for any value
1\leq i\leq 4
, then we simply discard this code in the minimum distance lower bound of (
3.4
). For instance if
D_{1}=0
, then the minimum distance lower bound of (
3.4
) becomes

\min\{d(D_{3}),d(D_{4}),d(D_{2})\}\leq d(C).

The following corollary gives a modification of this result to additive cyclic codes, which are generated by only one generator. In this result, the cyclic codes
C_{i}
are obtained from
D_{i}
after substituting
h(x)
with
0
in Theorem
3.9
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. However, the code
C_{4}
is obtained differently by considering a more direct observation.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
C=\langle g(x)+\omega k(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
be a length
n
additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. Let
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
C_{3}
, and
C_{4}
be the length
n
linear cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
generated by polynomials
g(x)
,
k(x)
,
\frac{x^{n}-1}{\gcd(x^{n}-1,g(x))}k(x)
, and
\frac{x^{n}-1}{\gcd(x^{n}-1,k(x))}g(x)
, respectively. Then

\min\{d(C_{3}),d(C_{4}),\max\{d(C_{1}),d(C_{2})\}\}\leq d(C).

(3.5)

Proof.

As we mentioned above, the code
C_{i}
all are obtained after applying the condition
h(x)=0
in the structure of the codes
D_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
in Theorem
3.9
. Since the code
D_{4}
in Theorem
3.9
is applied to bound the minimum weight of the set

T_{1}=\{a(x)\in C:\ 0\neq a(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]\},

we compute the minimum weight of
T_{1}
directly in this proof.
Let
f(x)\in T_{1}
. Then we can write it as
f(x)=a(x)(g(x)+\omega k(x))
for some
a(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
. Hence
f(x)=a(x)g(x)
and
a(x)k(x)\equiv 0\pmod{x^{n}-1}
. This implies that
\frac{x^{n}-1}{\gcd(x^{n}-1,k(x))}\mid a(x)
. Hence
\frac{x^{n}-1}{\gcd(x^{n}-1,k(x))}g(x)\mid a(x)
and we have
f(x)\in C_{4}
.
∎

Next, we consider the restriction of the mentioned minimum distance bound to linear cyclic codes with the generator polynomials
g(x)+\omega k(x)
and

, where

.

.

Let

4.
Symplectic inner product and dual of additive cyclic codes

In this section, we determine generator polynomials of the symplectic dual of a given additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. Moreover, we give the generator polynomials of all self-orthogonal and self-dual codes. We also measure how close is a given additive cyclic code from being symplectic self-orthogonal.
Recall that
p
is a prime number and
n
is a positive integer coprime to
p
. Moreover, elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
are represented by
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}=\{\alpha+\beta\omega:\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
,
where
\omega
is a root of a degree 2 irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Recall that in (
2.1
) we defined the symplectic inner product of two elements in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{n}
.
We define the symplectic inner product of two polynomials analogously. In particular, for
c(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(a_{i}+\omega b_{i})x^{i}
and
c^{\prime}(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(a^{\prime}_{i}+\omega b^{\prime}_{i})x^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
, we define

c(x)*c^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(a_{i}b^{\prime}_{i}-a^{\prime}_{i}b_{i}).

Here we use a different notation for the symplectic inner product to differentiate between the vectors and polynomials as different objects.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
c(x)=g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x)
and
c^{\prime}(x)=g_{1}^{\prime}(x)+\omega g_{2}^{\prime}(x)
be two polynomials of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
, where
g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x),g_{1}^{\prime}(x),g_{2}^{\prime}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
.
Then
c(x)*c^{\prime}(x)
is the constant term of
g_{1}(x)g_{2}^{\prime}(x^{-1})-g_{2}(x)g_{1}^{\prime}(x^{-1})\pmod{x^{n}-1}
. A similar argument shows that
c(x)*x^{i}c^{\prime}(x)
is the coefficient of
x^{i}
in
g_{1}(x)g_{2}^{\prime}(x^{-1})-g_{2}(x)g_{1}^{\prime}(x^{-1})\pmod{x^{n}-1}
. Thus if
g_{1}(x)g_{2}^{\prime}(x^{-1})-g_{2}(x)g_{1}^{\prime}(x^{-1})\equiv 0\pmod{x^{n}-1}
, then the code generated by
c^{\prime}(x)
lies in the symplectic dual of the code generated by
c(x)
. We use this property very frequently through this section.

One can easily verify that the symplectic dual of an additive cyclic code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
is also an additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. Recall that by Theorem
3.6
part (ii), each additive cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
can be represented uniquely as
C=\langle g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x),h(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
, where
g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x),h(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
.
Our next theorem gives a criterion for the self-orthogonality of additive cyclic codes. The proof is very similar to that of
[
3
, Theorem 14 part c]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
C=\langle g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x),h(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
be a length
n
additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. The code
C
is self-orthogonal if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

g_{2}(x)h(x^{-1})\equiv 0\pmod{x^{n}-1}
,

(2)

g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x^{-1})\equiv g_{2}(x)g_{1}(x^{-1})\pmod{x^{n}-1}
.

Proof.

\Rightarrow:
Suppose that
C
is self-orthogonal. For each
0\leq i\leq n-1
, the inner product of
g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x)
and
x^{i}h(x)
is the coefficient of
x^{i}
in
-g_{2}(x)h(x^{-1})\pmod{x^{n}-1}
. Since
C
is self-orthogonal, we have
g_{2}(x)h(x^{-1})\equiv 0\pmod{x^{n}-1}
.
Moreover,
\big(x^{i}(g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x))\big)\ast\big(g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x)\big)
is the coefficient of
x^{i}
in
g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x^{-1})-g_{2}(x)g_{1}(x^{-1})\pmod{x^{n}-1}
. Hence, for each
0\leq i\leq n-1
, the coefficient of
x^{i}
in
g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x^{-1})-g_{2}(x)g_{1}(x^{-1})
is zero. Thus
g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x^{-1})\equiv g_{2}(x)g_{1}(x^{-1})\pmod{x^{n}-1}
.

\Leftarrow:
Conversely, the fact that
g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x^{-1})\equiv g_{2}(x)g_{1}(x^{-1})\pmod{x^{n}-1}
implies that all the vectors inside
\langle g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
are self-orthogonal. Moreover, since
g_{2}(x)h(x^{-1})\equiv 0\pmod{x^{n}-1}
, we conclude that
h(x)
is orthogonal to all the cyclic shifts of
g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x)
. Finally
h(x)\ast x^{i}h(x)=0
for each
0\leq i\leq n-1
. So
\langle g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x),h(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
is a symplectic self-orthogonal code.
∎

Recall that
x^{n}-1=\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{s}f_{i}(x)
, where each
f_{i}(x)
is an irreducible polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
.
Moreover, as we mentioned earlier in
(\ref{equation1})
, we have
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}N_{i}
, where
N_{i}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]}
. Let

\alpha
be a primitive
n
-th root of unity in a finite filed extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
We denote the
p
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
n
by
Z_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq s
in the way that
f_{i}(x)=\displaystyle\prod_{a\in Z_{i}}(x-\alpha^{i})
. This gives a one-to-one correspondence between the sets
N_{i}
and all the
p
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
n
.
Our first goal in this section is to find the symplectic dual of a given additive cyclic code. In order to achieve this goal, we need a few preliminary results. In the next lemma, we find the symplectic dual of each
N_{i}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
1\leq i\leq s
and
C=N_{i}
. Then
C^{\bot_{s}}=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{s}N_{k}
,
where
Z_{j}=-Z_{i}
.

Proof.

First note that by Lemma
3.4
part (3) we have
C=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x),\omega((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x))\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
.
If
Z_{k}\neq-Z_{i}
, then
f_{i}(x)\mid(x^{n}-1)/f_{k}(x^{-1})
and
f_{k}(x)\mid(x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x^{-1})
. So
we have

•

\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{k}(x^{-1})\big)\equiv 0\pmod{x^{n}-1}
and

•

\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{k}(x)\big)\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x^{-1})\big)\equiv 0\pmod{x^{n}-1}
.

Hence the symplectic inner product of each element of
N_{i}
and each element of
N_{k}
is zero by definition. This proves that
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{s}N_{k}\subseteq C^{\bot_{s}}
.
Note that both of
N_{i}
and
N_{j}
have
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dimension
2\deg(f_{i})
. Now, the facts that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C)+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C^{\bot_{s}})=2n
and
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C)=2\deg(f_{i})
implies the other inclusion.
∎

Next, we find the symplectic dual of each irreducible additive cyclic code inside
N_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq s
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
C\subsetneq N_{i}
be a non-zero additive cyclic code for some
1\leq i\leq s
. Then

C^{\bot_{s}}=(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{s}N_{k})\bigoplus\langle g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]},

(4.1)

where
Z_{j}=-Z_{i}
and

g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x)=\begin{cases}((x^{n}-1)/f_{j}(x))(s(x^{-1})+\omega)&\text{if}\ C=\langle\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\big(\omega+s(x)\big)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}\\
(x^{n}-1)/f_{j}(x)&\text{if}\ C=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}\\
\end{cases}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.3
, one can see that
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{s}N_{k}\subseteq C^{\bot_{s}}
. Note that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\langle g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C)
. So it is sufficient to show that
C
is orthogonal to
g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x)
and all its cyclic shifts. We prove the latter statement in two steps.
First suppose that
C=\langle\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\big(\omega+s(x)\big)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
for some
s(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
. To show that the codes
C
and
\langle g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
are orthogonal, we apply Remark
4.1
. In particular,

\begin{split}\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)s(x)&g_{2}(x^{-1})-\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)g_{1}(x^{-1})\equiv\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)s(x)\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\\
&-\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)s(x)\equiv 0\pmod{x^{n}-1}.\end{split}

Next, suppose that
C=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
. Then

\begin{split}\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)g_{2}(x^{-1})-\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)g_{1}(x^{-1})&\equiv\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)0-0\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{j}(x)\big)s(x)\\
&\equiv 0\pmod{x^{n}-1}.\end{split}

This shows that the code
C
is orthogonal to the additive cyclic code generated by
g_{1}(x)+\omega g_{2}(x)
and completes the proof.
∎

Note that as we showed in Lemma
4.4
, when
C=\langle\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\big(\omega+s(x)\big)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
, its symplectic inner product contains the code
C^{\prime}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{j}(x))(s(x^{-1})+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
. The code
C^{\prime}
is not in one of the forms given in Lemma
3.4
part (4). In order to express the code
C^{\prime}
using the standard notation introduced in
3.4
part (4), we choose its generator to be
g(x)=(x^{n}-1)/f_{j}(x))(t(x)+\omega)
, where
t(x)\equiv s(x^{-1})\pmod{f_{j}(x)}
. Now it is easy to see that
g(x)
belongs to the set
A
introduced in Lemma
3.4
part (4) and
C^{\prime}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{j}(x))(t(x)+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
.

Next, we combine the results of the previous two lemmas and the result of Theorem
3.6
to determine generator polynomials of the symplectic dual for any additive cyclic code.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
C
be a length
n
additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
such that
C=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}C_{i}
, where
C_{i}
is an additive cyclic codes inside
N_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq s
.
Then
C^{\bot_{s}}=\langle\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}g_{i}(x)+\omega k_{i}(x),\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\omega h_{i}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
, where for each
1\leq i\leq s
we have
Z_{j}=-Z_{i}
and

•

g_{i}(x)=k_{i}(x)=h_{i}(x)=0
if
C_{j}=N_{j}
,

•

g_{i}(x)=h_{i}(x)=(x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)
and
k_{i}(x)=0
if
C_{j}=0
,

•

g_{i}(x)+\omega k_{i}(x)=\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\big(\omega+t_{i}(x)\big)
and
h_{i}(x)=0
if
C_{j}=\langle\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{j}(x)\big)\big(\omega+s_{j}(x)\big)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
and
t_{i}(x)\equiv s_{j}(x^{-1})\pmod{f_{j}(x)}
,

•

g_{i}(x)=(x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)
and
k_{i}(x)=h_{i}(x)=0
if
C_{j}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{j}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
.

Proof.

We apply Lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
to prove the statement. If
C_{j}=N_{j}
, then
C^{\bot_{s}}\cap N_{i}=\{0\}
by Lemma
4.3
. Moreover, by the same lemma, if
C_{j}=0
, then
N_{i}\subseteq C^{\bot_{s}}
. This proves the first two bullets.
Finally, Lemma
4.4
implies that

•

\langle\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\big)\big(\omega+t_{i}(x)\big)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}\subseteq C^{\bot_{s}}
if
C_{j}=\langle\big((x^{n}-1)/f_{j}(x)\big)\big(\omega+s_{j}(x)\big)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
, and

•

\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}\subseteq C^{\bot_{s}}
if
C_{j}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{i}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
.

This proves the statements of the last two bullets.
∎

To determine self-orthogonal and self-dual additive cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, we need more information about irreducible factors of
x^{n}-1
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Let
Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{r}
and
Z_{1}^{\prime},-Z_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,Z_{t}^{\prime},-Z_{t}^{\prime}
be all the
p
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
n
, where
Z_{i}=-Z_{i}
and
r+2t=s
.
Each
Z_{i}
is in correspondence to an irreducible polynomial
f_{i}(x)
and
(Z_{j}^{\prime},-Z_{j}^{\prime})
are in correspondence with an irreducible pair of polynomials
(f_{j1}(x),f_{j2}(x))
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Therefore, we can rewrite the irreducible decomposition of
x^{n}-1
as

x^{n}-1=\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}(x)\prod_{j=1}^{t}f_{j1}(x)f_{j2}(x).

We use the above representation of cyclotomic cosets in the upcoming results. Next, we classify self-orthogonal and self-dual additive codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
C
be a length
n
additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
such that
C=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}C_{i}
, where
C_{i}
is an additive cyclic codes inside
N_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq s
. Then
C
is symplectic self-orthogonal if and only if

(1)

for all
1\leq k\leq r
only one of the following holds.

(a)

C_{k}=0
.

(b)

C_{k}=\langle((x^{n}-1)/f_{k}(x))(s(x)+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
, where
f_{k}\mid s(x^{-1})-s(x)
.

(c)

C_{k}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{k}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
.

(2)

for all
1\leq j\leq t
only one of the following holds.

(a)

C_{j1}=0
or
C_{j2}=0
.

(b)

C_{j1}=\langle((x^{n}-1)/f_{j1}(x))(s(x)+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
and
C_{j2}=\langle((x^{n}-1)/f_{j2}(x))(s(x^{-1})+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
.

(c)

C_{j1}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{j1}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
and
C_{j2}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{j2}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
.

Proof.

First, let
1\leq k\leq r
.
By Lemma
4.3
, if
C_{k}=N_{k}
, then
C^{\bot_{s}}\cap N_{k}=\{0\}
. So
C_{k}
cannot have two generator polynomials.
Moreover, by Lemma
4.4
, if
0\neq C_{k}=\langle((x^{n}-1)/f_{k}(x))(s(x)+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
, then
\langle((x^{n}-1)/f_{k}(x))(s(x^{-1})+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}\subseteq C^{\bot_{s}}
. Thus
C_{k}
is self-orthogonal if and only if

C_{k}=\langle((x^{n}-1)/f_{k}(x))(s(x)+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}=\langle((x^{n}-1)/f_{k}(x))(s(x^{-1})+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}.

Note that the above equality holds if and only if
f_{k}\mid s(x^{-1})-s(x)
.
Thus
C_{k}
is self-orthogonal if and only if one of the conditions of Part (1) follows.

Next, let
1\leq j\leq t
. By Lemma
4.3
, if
C_{j1}=N_{j1}
, then
C^{\bot_{s}}\cap N_{j2}=\{0\}
. So if one of
C_{j1}
or
C_{j2}
has two generator polynomials, the other code should be zero. Moreover, the same lemma shows that if
C_{j1}=0
or
C_{j2}=0
, then
C_{j1}+C_{j2}
is self-orthogonal.
So we concentrate only on the case when both
C_{j1}
and
C_{j2}
have exactly one non-zero generator.
By Lemma
4.4
, if
C_{j1}=\langle((x^{n}-1)/f_{j1}(x))(s(x)+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
, then

C^{\bot_{s}}\cap N_{j2}=\langle((x^{n}-1)/f_{j2}(x))(s(x^{-1})+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}.

In this case, the code
C_{j1}\oplus C_{j2}
is self-orthogonal if and only if condition (2)(b) is satisfied. Condition (2)(c) follows similarly by applying Lemma
4.4
.
∎

Next, we use the above conditions to characterize all the symplectic self-dual additive cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.

.

C
be a length

additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
such that
C=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}C_{i}
, where
C_{i}
is an additive cyclic codes inside
N_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq s
. Then
C
is symplectic self-dual if and only if

(1)

for all
1\leq k\leq r
only one of the following holds.

(a)

C_{k}=\langle((x^{n}-1)/f_{k}(x))(s(x)+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
where
f_{k}\mid s(x^{-1})-s(x)
.

(b)

C_{k}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{k}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
.

(2)

for all
1\leq j\leq t
only one of the following holds.

(a)

C_{j1}=0
and
C_{j2}=N_{j2}
.

(b)

C_{j2}=0
and
C_{j1}=N_{j1}
.

(c)

C_{j1}=\langle((x^{n}-1)/f_{j1}(x))(s(x)+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
and

Note that all the self-dual additive cyclic codes over

∎

Our next goal is to compute the parameter
e=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C)-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C\cap C^{\bot_{s}})
for all additive cyclic codes. The parameter

determines how close an additive cyclic code
C
is from being self-orthogonal. This parameter plays an important role in the quantum construction that we are applying in the next section.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
C
be a length
n
additive cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
such that
C=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}C_{i}
, where
C_{i}
is an additive cyclic codes inside
N_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq s
. Let

(1)

B_{1}=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{t_{1}}\}\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
such that
C_{\alpha_{l}}=N_{\alpha_{l}}
for all
1\leq l\leq t_{1}
,

(2)

B_{2}=\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{t_{2}}\}\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
such that
C_{\beta_{l}}=\langle((x^{n}-1)/f_{\beta_{l}}(x))(s_{\beta_{l}}(x)+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
and

for all

,

(3)

B_{3}=\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\ldots,\gamma_{t_{3}}\}\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,t\}
such that one of
C_{\gamma_{l}1}
and
C_{\gamma_{l}2}
is generated by two polynomials and the other one has only one generator polynomial for all
1\leq l\leq t_{3}
,

(4)

B_{4}=\{\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\ldots,\kappa_{t_{4}}\}\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,t\}
such that both of
C_{\kappa_{l}1}
and
C_{\kappa_{l}2}
are generated by two polynomials for all
1\leq l\leq t_{4}
,

(5)

B_{5}=\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{t_{5}}\}\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,t\}
such that both of
C_{\sigma_{l}1}
and
C_{\sigma_{l}2}
are generated by one polynomial for all
1\leq l\leq t_{5}
and

(a)

if
C_{\sigma_{l}1}=\langle((x^{n}-1)/f_{\sigma_{l}1}(x))(s_{\sigma_{l}}(x)+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
, then
C_{\sigma_{l}2}\neq\langle((x^{n}-1)/f_{\sigma_{l}2}(x))(s_{\sigma_{l}}(x^{-1})+\omega)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
.

(b)

if
C_{\sigma_{l}1}=\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{\sigma_{l}1}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
, then
C_{\sigma_{l}2}\neq\langle(x^{n}-1)/f_{\sigma_{l}2}(x))\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]}
.

Then

e=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C)-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C\cap C^{\bot_{s}})=\sum_{l=1}^{t_{1}}2|Z_{\alpha_{l}}|+\sum_{l=1}^{t_{2}}|Z_{\beta_{l}}|+\sum_{l=1}^{t_{3}}2|Z_{\gamma_{l}}|+\sum_{l=1}^{t_{4}}4|Z_{\kappa_{l}}|+\sum_{l=1}^{t_{5}}2|Z_{\sigma_{l}}|.

(4.2)

Proof.

By Theorem
4.6
, an additive cyclic code is not symplectic self-orthogonal if and only if at least one of the sets
B_{1}-B_{5}
is non-empty. Next, we consider all scenarios (1)-(5) independently.

(1)

Let
j\in B_{1}
. In this case,
C^{\bot_{s}}\cap C_{j}=\{0\}
which implies that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C_{j})-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C_{j}\cap C^{\bot_{s}})=2|Z_{j}|
.

(2)

Let
j\in B_{2}
. In this case,
C^{\bot_{s}}\cap C_{j}=\{0\}
which implies that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C_{j})-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C_{j}\cap C^{\bot_{s}})=|Z_{j}|
.

(3)

Let
j\in B_{3}
. Without loss of generality we assume that
C_{j1}=N_{j1}
and
C_{j2}
is an irreducible subcode of
N_{j2}
.
In this case, the intersection
C^{\bot_{s}}\cap(C_{j1}\oplus C_{j2})
is an irreducible subcode of
N_{j1}
which implies that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C_{j})-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}((C_{j1}\oplus C_{j2})\cap C^{\bot_{s}})=3|Z_{j}|-|Z_{j}|=2|Z_{j}|
.

(4)

Let
j\in B_{4}
.
In this case,
C^{\bot_{s}}\cap(C_{j1}\oplus C_{j2})=\{0\}
which implies that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C_{j})-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}((C_{j1}\oplus C_{j2})\cap C^{\bot_{s}})=4|Z_{j}|
.

(5)

Let
j\in B_{5}
. In both parts (a) and (b),
C^{\bot_{s}}\cap(C_{j1}\oplus C_{j2})=\{0\}
which implies that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C_{j})-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}((C_{j1}\oplus C_{j2})\cap C^{\bot_{s}})=2|Z_{j}|
.

Now, the result follows by combining the above observations.

∎

Note that the case (2) of Theorem
4.8
never happens for
C_{i}
with
\deg(f_{i}(x))=1
. Moreover,
for each
1\leq i\leq r
, the cyclotomic coset
Z_{i}
either is a singleton or it has an even size. This is mainly because for each
0\neq a\in Z_{i}
, if
a\equiv-a\pmod{n}
, then
n\mid 2a
, which implies that
n
is even. Hence in this case
p\neq 2
(we assumed that
\gcd(n,p)=1
) and
Z_{i}=\{a\}
.
Therefore, if
Z_{i}
satisfies the case (2) of Theorem
4.8
and
|Z_{i}|>1
, then for any
a\in Z_{i}
, we have
-a\in Z_{i}
and
-a\not\equiv a\pmod{n}
. This implies that
|Z_{i}|
is an even integer.
This fact and the formula in
(\ref{e computation})
imply that the nearly self-orthogonality parameter
e
of an additive cyclic code is always an even integer. Next, we classify additive cyclic codes with small values of
e
. First, we need the following preliminary result.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
p
be a prime number and
\gcd(n,p)=1
for some positive number
n
.

(i)

If
\gcd(n,p-1)=d
, then there are
d
singleton
p
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
n
and all of their coset leaders are
\{k\frac{n}{d}:0\leq k\leq d-1\}
.

(ii)

If
\gcd(n,p-1)=d
and
\gcd(n,p^{2}-1)=d^{\prime}
. Then there are
\frac{d^{\prime}-d}{2}

p
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
n
of size two.

Proof.

(i) The proof easily follows from the fact that
\{a\}
is a singleton coset if and only if
a\equiv pa\pmod{n}
or equivalently if and only if
a(p-1)\equiv 0\pmod{n}
. By elementary number theory, if
\gcd(n,p-1)=d
, then the latter equation has
d
solutions in the forms
\{k\frac{n}{d}:0\leq k\leq d-1\}
.

(ii) A
p
-cyclotomic coset modulo
n
containing
a
has size two if and only if
a\equiv p^{2}a\pmod{n}
and
a\not\equiv pa\pmod{n}
. So we get
d^{\prime}
candidate for the size two cosets by solving
a\equiv p^{2}a\pmod{n}
. Moreover, each singleton cyclotomic coset is formed by a solution of the latter equation. Note also that the
p
-cyclotomic coset of size two containing
a
and
pa
is counted twice in our previous observation. Hence there are

\frac{d^{\prime}-d}{2}
many different cosets.
∎

For example, for an odd
n
, the only singleton
p
-cyclotomic coset modulo
n
is
\{0\}
when
p=2
or
p=3
. If
n
is even, then
\{\frac{n}{2}\}
and
\{0\}
are the only singleton cyclotomic cosets for
p=3
. The next theorem classifies all the additive cyclic codes with
e=2
. Note that the case
e=0
happens if an additive cyclic code is symplectic self-orthogonal, and this case was characterized in Theorem
4.6
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
C=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}C_{i}
be an additive cyclic code of length

over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. Then

if and only if all

satisfy the conditions of Theorem

except one which is in correspondence to

(1)

a singleton coset and satisfies condition
(1)
of Theorem
4.8
,

(2)

a size two coset and satisfies condition
(2)
of Theorem
4.8
.

Proof.

The result follows from considering the formula
(\ref{e computation})
and considering all conditions of Theorem

∎

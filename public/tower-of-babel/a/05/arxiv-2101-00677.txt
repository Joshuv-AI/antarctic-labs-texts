Adjoint operations in twist-products of lattices
Ivan Chajda, Helmut Länger

Abstract
Given an integral commutative residuated lattice 𝐋 = ( L , ∨ , ∧ ) \mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge) , its full twist-product ( L 2 , ⊔ , ⊓ ) (L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap) can be endowed with two binary operations ⊙ \odot and ⇒ \Rightarrow introduced formerly by M. Busaniche and R. Cignoli as well as by C. Tsinakis and A. M. Wille such that it becomes a commutative residuated lattice. For every a ∈ L a\in L we define a certain subset P a ​ ( 𝐋 ) P_{a}(\mathbf{L}) of L 2 L^{2} . We characterize when P a ​ ( 𝐋 ) P_{a}(\mathbf{L}) is a sublattice of the full twist-product ( L 2 , ⊔ , ⊓ ) (L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap) . In this case P a ​ ( 𝐋 ) P_{a}(\mathbf{L}) together with some natural antitone involution ′ becomes a pseudo-Kleene lattice. If 𝐋 \mathbf{L} is distributive then ( P a ( 𝐋 ) , ⊔ , ⊓ , ) ′ (P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap,{}^{\prime}) becomes a Kleene lattice. We present sufficient conditions for P a ​ ( 𝐋 ) P_{a}(\mathbf{L}) being a subalgebra of ( L 2 , ⊔ , ⊓ , ⊙ , ⇒ ) (L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap,\odot,\Rightarrow) and thus for ⊙ \odot and ⇒ \Rightarrow being a pair of adjoint operations on P a ​ ( 𝐋 ) P_{a}(\mathbf{L}) . Finally, we introduce another pair ⊙ \odot and ⇒ \Rightarrow of adjoint operations on the full twist-product of a bounded commutative residuated lattice such that the resulting algebra is a bounded commutative residuated lattice satisfying the double negation law and we investigate when P a ​ ( 𝐋 ) P_{a}(\mathbf{L}) is closed under these new operations ⊙ \odot and ⇒ \Rightarrow .

1
Introduction

Kleene lattices were introduced by J. A. Kalman (
[
7
]
) (under a different name) as a special kind of De Morgan lattices which serve as an algebraic axiomatization of a certain propositional logic satisfying the double negation law but not necessary the excluded middle law. If the underlying lattice is not distributive such lattices are called pseudo-Kleene (see e.g.
[
4
]
). It is a question if certain binary operations can be introduced in a Kleene or pseudo-Kleene lattice such that they form an adjoint pair. To solve this problem, we apply an approach using the full twist-product construction and another construction extending a distributive lattice into a Kleene one.

Having a residuated lattice
(L,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
, M. Busaniche and R. Cignoli (
[
3
]
) as well as C. Tsinakis and A. M. Wille (
[
8
]
) introduced binary operations
\odot
and
\Rightarrow
on the full twist-product
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap)
to be converted into a residuated lattice
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap,\odot,\Rightarrow,(1,1))
. It is known that if
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
is a distributive lattice with an antitone involution,
a\in L
and
P_{a}(\mathbf{L}):=\{(x,y)\in L^{2}\mid x\wedge y\leq a\leq x\vee y\}
then
(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
is a Kleene lattice. If
\mathbf{L}
is not distributive then the situation is different.

Our aim is to combine both of these approaches and hence ask for several questions as follows:

•

When is
(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap)
a sublattice of the full twist-product
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap)
, also in the case of a non-distributive lattice
\mathbf{L}
?

•

When is
P_{a}(\mathbf{L})
closed under operations
\odot
and
\Rightarrow
mentioned above?

•

When can
P_{a}(\mathbf{L})
be equipped with these operations forming an adjoint pair?

•

Can we define the operations
\odot
and
\Rightarrow
in a way different from that of
[
3
]
or
[
8
]
to obtain an integral residuated lattice on the full twist-product
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap)
?

We answer these question in our paper by giving sufficient and, in some cases, also necessary conditions under which we get a positive solution. Moreover, we present examples showing how our constructions work.

2
Preliminaries

We recall several concepts which will be used throughout the paper. Moreover, we recall some results already published on which our present study is based.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
be a poset. An
antitone involution
on
\mathbf{P}
is a unary operation
′
on
P
satisfying

(i)

x\leq y
implies
y^{\prime}\leq x^{\prime}
,

(ii)

x^{\prime\prime}\approx x

for all
x,y\in P
. A distributive lattice having an antitone involution is called a
De Morgan lattice
or a
De Morgan algebra
.

Definition 2.1
.

A commutative residuated lattice is an algebra
(L,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
of type
(2,2,2,2,0)
such that

(i)

(L,\vee,\wedge)
is a lattice,

(ii)

(L,\cdot,1)
is a commutative monoid,

(iii)

for all
x,y,z\in L
,
x\cdot y\leq z
is equivalent to
x\leq y\rightarrow z

(
adjointness property
)
.

(L,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
is called integral if
1
is the top element of the lattice
(L,\vee,\wedge)
. A commutative residuated lattice with
0
is an algebra
(L,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,0,1)
of type
(2,2,2,2,0,0)
such that
(L,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
is a commutative residuated lattice and
0
is the bottom element of
(L,\vee,\wedge)
. Let
(L,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,0,1)
be a commutative residuated lattice with
0
. Define
x^{\prime}:=x\rightarrow 0
for all
x\in L
.
(L,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,0,1)
is

•

called a bounded commutative residuated lattice if
1
is the top element of
(L,\vee,\wedge)
,

•

said to satisfy the double negation law if it satisfies the identity
x^{\prime\prime}\approx x
, i.e.

(x\rightarrow 0)\rightarrow 0\approx x
.

We say that the operations
\cdot
and
\rightarrow
form an
adjoint pair
if they satisfy the adjointness (iii) of Definition
2.1
.

The following properties of integral commutative residuated lattices are well-known (cf. e.g.
[
2
]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(L,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
be an integral commutative residuated lattice.

Then the following hold for all
x,y,z\in L
:

(i)

x\leq y
implies
x\cdot z\leq y\cdot z
,

(ii)

x\cdot y\leq x,y
,

(iii)

1\rightarrow x\approx x
,

(iv)

x\leq y\rightarrow x
,

(v)

x\rightarrow y=1
if and only if
x\leq y
,

(vi)

x\leq y
implies
y\rightarrow z\leq x\rightarrow z
,

(vii)

x\leq y
implies
z\rightarrow x\leq z\rightarrow y
,

(viii)

x\rightarrow(y\wedge z)\approx(x\rightarrow y)\wedge(x\rightarrow z)
,

(ix)

(x\cdot y)\rightarrow z\approx x\rightarrow(y\rightarrow z)
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge)
be a lattice. By the
full twist-product
of
\mathbf{L}
is meant the lattice
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap)
where
\sqcup
and
\sqcap
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle(x,y)\sqcup(z,v)

\displaystyle:=(x\vee z,y\wedge v),

\displaystyle(x,y)\sqcap(z,v)

\displaystyle:=(x\wedge z,y\vee v)

for all
(x,y),(z,v)\in L^{2}
. Hence
(x,y)\leq(z,v)
if and only if both
x\leq z
and
v\leq y
. Assume now that
(L,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
is an integral commutative residuated lattice. In Theorem 3.1 in
[
3
]
which is a particular case of Corollary 3.6 in
[
8
]
, Busaniche and Cignoli introduced two additional binary operations
\odot
and
\Rightarrow
on its full twist-product
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap)
as follows:

\displaystyle(x,y)\odot(z,v):=(x\cdot z,(x\rightarrow v)\wedge(z\rightarrow y)),

(1)

\displaystyle(x,y)\Rightarrow(z,v):=((x\rightarrow z)\wedge(v\rightarrow y),x\cdot v)

(2)

for all
(x,y),(z,v)\in L^{2}
. They showed that
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap,\odot,\Rightarrow,(1,1))
is again a commutative residuated lattice, i.e.
\odot
and
\Rightarrow
form an adjoint pair. For the convenience of the reader we provide a proof since it is not explicitly contained in
[
3
]
and
[
8
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
be an integral commutative residuated lattice and
\odot
and
\Rightarrow
defined by
(
1
)
and
(
2
)
, respectively. Then
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap,\odot,\Rightarrow,(1,1))
is a commutative residuated lattice.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c,d,e,f\in L
.

(i)

It is easy to see that
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap)
is a lattice.

(ii)

We prove that
(L^{2},\odot,(1,1))
is a commutative monoid. Because of (iii), (v), (viii) and (ix) of Proposition
2.2
we have

\displaystyle(x,y)\odot(z,v)

\displaystyle\approx(x\cdot z,(x\rightarrow v)\wedge(z\rightarrow y))\approx(z\cdot x,(z\rightarrow y)\wedge(x\rightarrow v))\approx

\displaystyle\approx(z,v)\odot(x,y),

\displaystyle((x,y)\odot(z,v))\odot(t,w)

\displaystyle\approx(x\cdot z,(x\rightarrow v)\wedge(z\rightarrow y))\odot(t,w)\approx

\displaystyle\approx((x\cdot z)\cdot t,((x\cdot z)\rightarrow w)\wedge(t\rightarrow((x\rightarrow v)\wedge(z\rightarrow y))))\approx

\displaystyle\approx(x\cdot(z\cdot t),((x\cdot z)\rightarrow w)\wedge(t\rightarrow(x\rightarrow v))\wedge(t\rightarrow(z\rightarrow y)))\approx

\displaystyle\approx(x\cdot(z\cdot t),(x\rightarrow(z\rightarrow w))\wedge(x\rightarrow(t\rightarrow v))\wedge((z\cdot t)\rightarrow y))\approx

\displaystyle\approx(x\cdot(z\cdot t),(a\rightarrow((z\rightarrow w)\wedge(t\rightarrow v)))\wedge((z\cdot t)\rightarrow y))\approx

\displaystyle\approx(x,y)\odot(z\cdot t,(z\rightarrow w)\wedge(t\rightarrow v)\approx

\displaystyle\approx(x,y)\odot((z,v)\odot(t,w)),

\displaystyle(x,y)\odot(1,1)

\displaystyle\approx(x\cdot 1,(x\rightarrow 1)\wedge(1\rightarrow y))\approx(x,1\wedge y)\approx(x,y).

(iii)

Now we prove the adjointness property. The following are equivalent:

\displaystyle(a,b)\odot(c,d)\leq(e,f),

\displaystyle(a\cdot c,(a\rightarrow d)\wedge(c\rightarrow b))\leq(e,f),

\displaystyle a\cdot c\leq e\text{ and }f\leq(a\rightarrow d)\wedge(c\rightarrow b),

\displaystyle a\cdot c\leq e,f\leq a\rightarrow d\text{ and }f\leq c\rightarrow b,

\displaystyle a\leq c\rightarrow e,a\leq f\rightarrow d\text{ and }c\cdot f\leq b,

\displaystyle a\leq(c\rightarrow e)\wedge(f\rightarrow d)\text{ and }c\cdot f\leq b,

\displaystyle(a,b)\leq((c\rightarrow e)\wedge(f\rightarrow d),c\cdot f),

\displaystyle(a,b)\leq(c,d)\Rightarrow(e,f).

∎

It is worth noticing that the operations
\odot
and
\Rightarrow
defined above are not independent. Namely one can be expressed by the other by using the antitone involution
′
defined by
(x,y)^{\prime}:=(y,x)
. Namely,

\displaystyle(x,y)\odot(z,v)

\displaystyle\approx(x\cdot z,(x\rightarrow v)\wedge(z\rightarrow y)\approx((x\rightarrow v)\wedge(z\rightarrow y),x\cdot z)^{\prime}\approx

\displaystyle\approx((x,y)\Rightarrow(v,z))^{\prime}\approx((x,y)\Rightarrow(z,v)^{\prime})^{\prime},

\displaystyle(x,y)\Rightarrow(z,v)

\displaystyle\approx((x,y)\Rightarrow(z,v)^{\prime\prime})^{\prime\prime}\approx((x,y)\odot(z,v)^{\prime})^{\prime}.

Moreover, note that the residuated lattice
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap,\odot,\Rightarrow,(1,1))
as defined above is not integral since the top element
(1,0)
of the full twist-product is different from the neutral element
(1,1)
of the monoid
(L^{2},\odot,(1,1))
.

The following concept was introduced in
[
4
]
.

A
pseudo-Kleene lattice
is an algebra
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
of type
(2,2,1)
such that the following hold for all
x,y\in L
:

(i)

\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge)
is a lattice,

(ii)

′
is an antitone involution on
(L,\leq)
,

(iii)

x\wedge x^{\prime}\leq y\vee y^{\prime}
.

(Here and in the rest of the paper
\leq
denotes the induced order of the lattice
\mathbf{L}
.) If, moreover,
\mathbf{L}
is distributive then
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
is called a
Kleene lattice
.

3
A construction of pseudo-Kleene lattices in the full twist-product

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge)
be a lattice and
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap)
its full twist-product. It is easy to see that
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap)
is distributive if and only if so is
\mathbf{L}
. The following construction was introduced for distributive lattices in
[
6
]
and generalized for posets by the authors in
[
5
]
: Let
a\in L
and consider the following subset of
L^{2}
:

P_{a}(\mathbf{L}):=\{(x,y)\in L^{2}\mid x\wedge y\leq a\leq x\vee y\}.

Since our paper
[
5
]
is devoted to posets and not to lattices, we are going to show that if
(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap)
is a sublattice of
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap)
then
(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap,{}^{\prime})
where the unary operation
′
on
P_{a}(\mathbf{L})
is defined by
(x,y)^{\prime}:=(y,x)
for all
(x,y)\in P_{a}(\mathbf{L})
is a pseudo-Kleene lattice.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge)
be a lattice and
a\in L
, assume that
(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap)
is a sublattice of
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap)
and put
(x,y)^{\prime}:=(y,x)
for all
(x,y)\in L^{2}
. Then

(i)

(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap,{}^{\prime})
is a pseudo-Kleene lattice,

(ii)

the mapping
x\mapsto(x,a)
is an embedding of
\mathbf{L}
into
(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap)
,

(iii)

(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap)
is distributive if and only if so is
\mathbf{L}
.

Proof.

Let
(b,c),(d,e)\in P_{a}(\mathbf{L})
and
f,g\in L
.

(i)

The following are equivalent:

\displaystyle(b,c)\leq(d,e),

\displaystyle b\leq d\text{ and }e\leq c,

\displaystyle e\leq c\text{ and }b\leq d,

\displaystyle(e,d)\leq(c,b),

\displaystyle(d,e)^{\prime}\leq(b,c)^{\prime}.

Further, we have
(b,c)^{\prime\prime}=(c,b)^{\prime}=(b,c)
. Thus
′
is an antitone involution on
(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap)
. Moreover,

\displaystyle(b,c)\sqcap(b,c)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(b,c)\sqcap(c,b)=(b\wedge c,c\vee b)\leq(a,a)\leq(d\vee e,e\wedge d)=

\displaystyle=(d,e)\sqcup(e,d)=(d,e)\sqcup(d,e)^{\prime}

proving that
(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap,{}^{\prime})
is a pseudo-Kleene lattice.

(ii)

Since we have
(f,a)\leq(g,a)
if and only if
f\leq g
, it is evident.

(iii)

This can be easily checked.

∎

In general,
(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap)
need not be a sublattice of
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap)
.

.

Consider the lattice

We can give a necessary and sufficient condition for
(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap)
being a sublattice of
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge)
be a lattice and
a\in L
. Then
(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap)
is a sublattice of
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap)
if and only if the following condition holds for all
x,y,z,v\in L
:

\displaystyle(x\wedge y)\vee(z\wedge v)\leq a\leq(x\vee y)\wedge(z\vee v)\text{ implies}

\displaystyle((x\vee z)\wedge y\wedge v)\vee(x\wedge z\wedge(y\vee v))\leq a\leq(x\vee z\vee(y\wedge v))\wedge((x\wedge z)\vee y\vee v).

Proof.

Let
b,c,d,e\in L
.

The following are equivalent:

\displaystyle(b,c),(d,e)\in P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),

\displaystyle b\wedge c\leq a\leq b\vee c\text{ and }d\wedge e\leq a\leq d\vee e,

\displaystyle(b\wedge c)\vee(d\wedge e)\leq a\leq(b\vee c)\wedge(d\vee e).

Moreover, the following are equivalent:

\displaystyle(b,c)\sqcup(d,e)\in P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),

\displaystyle(b\vee d,c\wedge e)\in P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),

\displaystyle(b\vee d)\wedge c\wedge e\leq a\leq b\vee d\vee(c\wedge e).

Finally, the following are equivalent:

\displaystyle(b,c)\sqcap(d,e)\in P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),

\displaystyle(b\wedge d,c\vee e)\in P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),

\displaystyle b\wedge d\wedge(c\vee e)\leq a\leq(b\wedge d)\vee c\vee e.

∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge)
be a distributive lattice and
a\in L
. Then
(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,

\sqcap)
is a sublattice of
(L^{2},\sqcup,\sqcap)
and
(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap,{}^{\prime})
where the antitone involution is given by
(x,y)^{\prime}:=(y,x)
for all

is a Kleene lattice.

Proof.

If

and

(b\wedge c)\vee(d\wedge e)\leq a\leq(b\vee c)\wedge(d\vee e)

\displaystyle((b\vee d)\wedge c\wedge e)\vee(b\wedge d\wedge(c\vee e))

\displaystyle=(b\wedge c\wedge e)\vee(d\wedge c\wedge e)\vee(b\wedge d\wedge c)\vee(b\wedge d\wedge e)\leq

\displaystyle\leq(b\wedge c)\vee(d\wedge e)\vee(b\wedge c)\vee(d\wedge e)\leq a\leq

\displaystyle\leq(b\vee c)\wedge(d\vee e)\wedge(b\vee c)\wedge(d\vee e)\leq

\displaystyle\leq(b\vee d\vee c)\wedge(b\vee d\vee e)\wedge(b\vee c\vee e)\wedge(d\vee c\vee e)\leq

\displaystyle\leq(b\vee d\vee(c\wedge e))\wedge((b\wedge d)\vee c\vee e).

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge)
be a lattice and

(i)

\wedge

(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap)

Let
b,c\in L
.

(i) and (ii) are equivalent since

\Rightarrow
(iii):

\vee

a<b,c

(b,c)\in P_{a}(\mathbf{L})

(b,c)\parallel(a,a)

is
\wedge

\displaystyle=(b\vee d,c\wedge e)\geq(a,a),

\displaystyle(b,c)\sqcap(d,e)

\displaystyle=(b\wedge d,c\vee e)\leq(a,a),

(b\wedge b^{\prime},a\vee a^{\prime})=(b,a)\sqcap(b^{\prime},a^{\prime})=(b,a)\sqcap(b,a)^{\prime}\leq(c,a)\sqcup(c,a)^{\prime}=(c,a)\sqcup(c^{\prime},a^{\prime})=(c\vee c^{\prime},a\wedge a^{\prime}),

i.e.
b\wedge b^{\prime}\leq c\vee c^{\prime}
showing that
\mathbf{L}
is a pseudo-Kleene lattice. Conversely, assume
\mathbf{L}
to be a pseudo-Kleene lattice. Then
d\wedge e\leq a\leq d\vee e
whence
d^{\prime}\wedge e^{\prime}\leq a^{\prime}\leq d^{\prime}\vee e^{\prime}
, i.e.
d^{\prime}\wedge e^{\prime}\leq a\leq d^{\prime}\vee e^{\prime}
which shows
(d,e)^{\prime}=(d^{\prime},e^{\prime})\in P_{a}(\mathbf{L})
. Hence
P_{a}(\mathbf{L})
is closed with respect to
′
. Finally, we have

\displaystyle(d,e)\sqcap(d,e)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(d,e)\sqcap(d^{\prime},e^{\prime})=(d\wedge d^{\prime},e\vee e^{\prime})\leq(f\vee f^{\prime},g\wedge g^{\prime})=(f,g)\sqcup(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})=

\displaystyle=(f,g)\sqcup(f,g)^{\prime}

showing that
(P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),\sqcup,\sqcap,{}^{\prime})
is a pseudo-Kleene lattice.
∎

Our next aim is to show when
P_{a}(\mathbf{L})
is closed under the operation
\odot
defined by (
5
). We prove the following.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,{}^{\prime},1)
be a commutative residuated lattice with an antitone involution, let
a\in L
be idempotent with respect to
\cdot
,
\vee
-irreducible and
\wedge
-irreducible, assume
a^{\prime}\cdot a^{\prime}=a^{\prime}
and define
\odot
by
(
5
)
. Then
P_{a}(\mathbf{L})
is closed with respect to
\odot
.

Proof.

Let
(b,c),(d,e)\in P_{a}(\mathbf{L})
. We have

(x,y)\odot(z,v)\approx(x\cdot z,(y^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime})^{\prime})\approx(z\cdot x,(v^{\prime}\cdot y^{\prime})^{\prime})\approx(z,v)\odot(x,y).

According to Theorem
3.6
,

P_{a}(\mathbf{L})=\{(x,y)\in L^{2}\mid(x,y)\text{ is comparable with }(a,a)\}.

In the following we often use (i) and (ii) of Proposition
2.2
.

(i)

Assume
(b,c)\leq(a,a)
.

We have
b\cdot d\leq b\leq a
and every one of the following statements implies the next one:

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\leq c,

\displaystyle c^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq a^{\prime},

\displaystyle c^{\prime}\cdot e^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq a^{\prime},

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\leq(c^{\prime}\cdot e^{\prime})^{\prime}.

This shows
(b,c)\odot(d,e)=(b\cdot d,(c^{\prime}\cdot e^{\prime})^{\prime})\leq(a,a)
.

(ii)

Assume
(d,e)\leq(a,a)
.

Then
(b,c)\odot(d,e)=(d,e)\odot(b,c)\leq(a,a)
.

(iii)

Assume
(a,a)\leq(b,c),(d,e)
.

Then
c,e\leq a
and hence
a^{\prime}\leq c^{\prime},e^{\prime}
whence
a^{\prime}=a^{\prime}\cdot a^{\prime}\leq c^{\prime}\cdot a^{\prime}\leq c^{\prime}\cdot e^{\prime}
from which we conclude
(c^{\prime}\cdot e^{\prime})^{\prime}\leq a
. Because of
a\leq b,d
we have
a=a\cdot a\leq b\cdot a\leq b\cdot d
. Together we obtain
(a,a)\leq(b\cdot d,(c^{\prime}\cdot e^{\prime})^{\prime})=(b,c)\odot(d,e)
.

∎

Unfortunately,
P_{a}(\mathbf{L})
is not closed under

defined by (C2) provided

in non-trivial, i.e. if it has more than one element.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
(L,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a bounded commutative residuated lattice with an antitone involution and
a\in L
and put
(x,y)\Rightarrow(z,v):=(x\rightarrow z,(y^{\prime}\rightarrow v^{\prime})^{\prime})
for all
(x,y),(z,v)\in L^{2}
. Then
P_{a}(\mathbf{L})
is closed with respect to
\Rightarrow
if and only if
|L|=1
.

Proof.

Assume
P_{a}(\mathbf{L})
to be closed with respect to
\Rightarrow
. Since
(0,a),(a,0),(a,1),(1,a)\in P_{a}(\mathbf{L})
we have

\displaystyle(0,0)

\displaystyle=(0,1^{\prime})=(1\rightarrow 0,(a^{\prime}\rightarrow a^{\prime})^{\prime})=(1,a)\Rightarrow(0,a)\in P_{a}(\mathbf{L}),

\displaystyle(1,1)

\displaystyle=(1,0^{\prime})=(1,(1\rightarrow 0)^{\prime})=(a\rightarrow a,(0^{\prime}\rightarrow 1^{\prime})^{\prime})=(a,0)\Rightarrow(a,1)\in P_{a}(\mathbf{L})

whence

and therefore

, i.e.

.

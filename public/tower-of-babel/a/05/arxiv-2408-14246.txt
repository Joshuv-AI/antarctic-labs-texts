Abstract




Abstract
We study the local properties of positive solutions of the equation
-\Delta u+ae^{bu}=m\left|\nabla u\right|^{q}
in a punctured domain
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
where
m,a,b
are positive parameters and
q>1
. We study particularly the existence of solutions with an isolated singularity and the local behaviour of such singular solutions.

35J61, 34B16
.

.
elliptic equations; a priori estimates; isolated singularities.

1
Introduction

The following type of nonlinear equation

-\Delta u+f(u)-g(|\nabla u|)=0

(1.1
)

in a
N
dimensional domain when
f
and
g
are nondecreasing continuous functions is an interesting model for studying the interaction between the diffusion, the reaction and the absorption
when the last two quantities are of a totally different nature. The most interesting case correspond to the case where the two reaction functions
f
and
g
increasing in their respective variable. In its full generality the problem is difficult to handle, but the model cases where
f(u)=u^{p}
if
N\geq 3

-\Delta u+u^{p}-m|\nabla u|^{q}=0.

(1.2
)

has already been studied in
[
5
]
. The results therein shows the rich interaction of the phenomena coming eiter from the interaction of the diffusion and the absorption, the diffusion and the reaction or the reaction and the absorption. A variant of this equation is the Chipot-Weissler equation

-\Delta u-u^{p}+m|\nabla u|^{q}=0,

(1.3
)

which presents also interesting interaction between the pairing of the three terms. This has been studied in
[
3
]
and
[
6
]
. For this last equation the main difficulty is to obtain a priori estimates of positive solutions near an isolated singularity. These estimates have been obtained in

[
3
]
and
[
6
]
.
The aim of the present paper is to study a 2-dimensional problem of type
(\ref{I-2})
where naturally the power absorption is replaced by an exponential absorption. Our model equation is

{{\mathcal{E}}}(u):=-\Delta u+ae^{bu}-m|\nabla u|^{q}=0

(1.4
)

in
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
where
B_{1}
is the unit disk of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
m,a,b
are positive parameters and
q>1
. For
(\ref{I-2})
and
(\ref{I-0})
the a priori estimate near an isolated singularity is easy to obtain, the difficulty lies in the full description of singularities and the classifications of singular solutions . Three particular equations are naturally associated to equation
(\ref{I-1})
:

the Emden’s equation

-\Delta u+ae^{bu}=0,

(1.5
)

the Ricatti equation

-\Delta u-m|\nabla u|^{q}=0,

(1.6
)

and the eikonal equation

ae^{bu}-m|\nabla u|^{q}=0.

(1.7
)

The predominance of a specific equation of this type depends on the value of
q
, with critical value
q=2
. When
1<q<2
the singularities are similar to the one of Emden’s equation, an equation which has been completely described in
[
18
]
. We recall the main result therein:

Vázquez theorem
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded smooth domain containing
0
and
a,b>0
. Then for any
\phi\in C^{1}(\partial\Omega)
and
\gamma\in[0,\frac{2}{b}]
there exists a unique function
v_{\gamma}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\})
such that
e^{bv_{\gamma}}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying

-\Delta v_{\gamma}+ae^{bv_{\gamma}}=2\pi\gamma\delta_{0}\quad\text{in }{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\prime}(\Omega).

(1.8
)

coinciding with
\phi
on
\partial\Omega
. Furthermore

\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{v_{\gamma}(x)}{-\ln|x|}=\gamma.

(1.9
)

The condition
0\leq\gamma\leq\frac{2}{b}
is also a necessary condition for the solvability of
(\ref{I-Va})

When
q=2
, the change of unknown
u=\frac{1}{m}\ln v
transform
(\ref{I-1})
into

-\Delta v+mav^{\frac{b}{m}+1}=0,

(1.10
)

an equation which has been thoroughly studied (e.g.
[
11
]
,
[
20
]
) and thus we will omit it. When
q>2
the dominent equation is the eikonal one. There is a particular explicit solution which plays a key role in the studies of singularities

U(|x|)=\frac{q}{b}\left(\frac{m}{a}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\ln\left(\frac{q}{b|x|}\right).

(1.11
)

We summarise below our three main results

Theorem 1
Let
m,a,b>0
and
1<q<2
. If
u\in C^{1}(\overline{B}_{1}\setminus\{0\})
is a positive function satisfying
(\ref{I-1})
. Then there exists
\gamma\in[0,\frac{2}{b}]
such that

\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{u(x)}{-\ln|x|}=\gamma.

(1.12
)

Furthermore
e^{bu}
and
|\nabla u|^{q}
are integrable in
B_{1}
and there holds

-\Delta u+ae^{bu}-m|\nabla u|^{q}=2\pi\delta_{0}\quad\text{in }{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\prime}(B_{1}).

(1.13
)

If
\gamma=0
,
u
can be extended as a
C^{1}(\overline{B}_{1})
function.

Conversely we prove

Theorem 2
Under the assumptions on
m,a,b,q
of Theorem 1, for any
\gamma\in(0,\frac{2}{b}]
and
\phi\in C^{1}(\partial B_{1})
there exists a unique function
u\in C^{1}(\overline{B}_{1}\setminus\{0\})
satisfying
(\ref{I-9})
and coinciding with
\phi
on
\partial B_{1}
. Furthermore
e^{bu}
and
|\nabla u|^{q}
are integrable
in
B_{1}
and
(\ref{I-9})
holds.

When
q>2
, the classification of the solutions is totally different and present some similarity with the study of isolated singularities of Lane-Emden equation

-\Delta u-u^{p}=0,

(1.14
)

in the supercritical case
N\geq 3
and
p>\frac{N}{N-2}
(see
[
14
]
). We prove the following characterisation of singular solutions,

Theorem 3
Let
m,a,b>0
and
q>2
. If
u\in C^{1}(\overline{B}_{1}\setminus\{0\})
is a positive solution of
(\ref{I-1})
, then

(i) either

\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{u(x)}{-\ln|x|}=\frac{q}{b},

(1.15
)

(ii) or
u
can be extended to
\overline{B}_{1}
as a Hölder continuous function.

2
A priori estimates

The next estimates are valid in any dimension

Proposition 2.1

Let
m,a,b>0
and
q>1
. Assume that for
x_{0}\in B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
0<R<|x_{0}|

u
is a solution of
(\ref{I-1})
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. Then there exists

\Lambda=\Lambda(m,q,N,a,b)>0
such that

(i) If
1<q<2
,

u(x_{0})\leq\frac{2}{b}\ln\frac{1}{|x_{0}|}+\Lambda.

(2.1
)

(ii) If
q>2

u(x_{0})\leq\frac{q}{b}\ln\frac{1}{|x_{0}|}+\tilde{\Lambda}.

(2.2
)

Proof.
Let
R^{\prime}<R
. We set for
\lambda,\mu>0
,

\psi(x)=\lambda\ln(1/(R^{\prime 2}-|x-x_{0}|^{2}))+\mu=\lambda\ln(1/(R^{\prime 2}-r^{2}))+\mu,

with
r=|x-x_{0}|
. Then

ae^{b\psi}=\frac{ae^{b\mu}}{(R^{\prime 2}-r^{2})^{b\lambda}},

|\nabla\psi(x)|=|\psi_{r}(r)|=\frac{2\lambda r}{R^{\prime 2}-r^{2}},

|\nabla\psi(x)|^{q}=\frac{2^{q}\lambda^{q}r^{q}}{(R^{\prime 2}-r^{2})^{q}},

-\Delta\psi(x)=-2\lambda\frac{NR^{\prime 2}-(N-2)r^{2}}{(R^{\prime 2}-r^{2})^{2}}.

Hence there holds in
B_{R^{\prime}}(x_{0})
,

{{\mathcal{E}}}(\psi)=\frac{ae^{b\mu}}{(R^{\prime 2}-r^{2})^{b\lambda}}-2\lambda\frac{NR^{\prime 2}-(N-2)r^{2}}{(R^{\prime 2}-r^{2})^{2}}-\frac{m2^{q}\lambda^{q}r^{q}}{(R^{\prime 2}-r^{2})^{q}}.

We have
\psi>u
in a neighbourhood of
\partial B_{R^{\prime}}(x_{0})
. We encounter two cases

(i)
1<q\leq 2
. Then we take
b\lambda=2
and get

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle{{\mathcal{E}}}(\psi)=(R^{\prime 2}-r^{2})^{-2}\left[ae^{b\mu}-\frac{4}{b}\left(NR^{\prime 2}-(N-2)r^{2}\right)-\frac{4^{q}mr^{q}}{b^{q}}\left(R^{\prime 2}-r^{2}\right)^{2-q}\right]\\[8.53581pt]
\phantom{\displaystyle{{\mathcal{E}}}(\psi)}\displaystyle\geq(R^{\prime 2}-r^{2})^{-2}\left[ae^{b\mu}-\Lambda^{*}\max\{R^{\prime 2},R^{\prime 4-q}\}\right]\\[8.53581pt]
\phantom{\displaystyle{{\mathcal{E}}}(\psi)}\displaystyle\geq(R^{\prime 2}-r^{2})^{-2}\left[ae^{b\mu}-\Lambda^{*}R^{\prime 2}\right],\end{array}

(2.3
)

where
\Lambda^{*}=\Lambda^{*}(N,q,m,b)>0
. We choose

\mu=\frac{2}{b}\ln R^{\prime}+\frac{1}{b}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda^{*}}{a}\right)

(2.4
)

and derive that
{{\mathcal{E}}}(\psi)\geq 0
in
B_{R^{\prime}}(x_{0})
. Hence
u\leq\psi
therein. This implies

u(x_{0})\leq\psi(x_{0}):=\frac{2}{b}\ln\frac{1}{R^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{b}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda^{*}}{a}\right).

(2.5
)

Letting
R^{\prime}\uparrow R=|x_{0}|
yields
(\ref{I-11})
.

(ii)
q>2
. Then we take
b\lambda=q
. Hence

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle{{\mathcal{E}}}(\psi)=(R^{2}-r^{2})^{-q}\left[ae^{b\mu}-\frac{q}{b}\left(NR^{2}-(N-2)r^{2}\right)\left(R^{2}-r^{2}\right)^{q-2}-\frac{(2q)^{q}mr^{q}}{b^{q}}\right]\\[8.53581pt]
\phantom{\displaystyle{{\mathcal{E}}}(\psi)}\displaystyle\geq(R^{2}-r^{2})^{-q}\left[ae^{b\mu}-\tilde{\Lambda}^{*}R^{q}\right]\end{array}

(2.6
)

where
\tilde{\Lambda}^{*}=\tilde{\Lambda}^{*}(N,q,m,a,b)>0
. We take

\mu=\frac{q}{b}\ln R+\frac{1}{b}\ln\left(\frac{\tilde{\Lambda}^{*}}{a}\right),

(2.7
)

and derive as in case (i)

u(x_{0})\leq\psi(x_{0}):=\frac{q}{b}\ln\left(\frac{1}{R^{\prime}}\right)+\frac{1}{b}\ln\left(\frac{\tilde{\Lambda}^{*}}{a}\right).

(2.8
)

Letting
R^{\prime}\uparrow R
we obtain
(\ref{I-12})
.
\square

Lemma 2.2

Let
m,a,b>0
and
0<\gamma\leq\frac{2}{b}
. If
v_{\gamma}\in C^{1}(\overline{B}_{1}\setminus\{0\})
is the solution
(\ref{I-Va})
which vanishes on
\partial B_{1}

1- If
0\leq\gamma<\frac{2}{b}
, there exists

v_{\gamma}(x)=\gamma\ln\frac{1}{|x|}-\phi^{*}(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in\overline{B}_{1}\setminus\{0\}.

(2.9
)

2- If
\gamma=\frac{2}{b}
, then for all
x\in\overline{B}_{1}\setminus\{0\}
we have

\frac{2}{b}\left(\ln\frac{1}{|x|}-\ln(1-\ln|x|)\right)+\kappa_{2}\leq v_{\frac{2}{b}}(x)\leq\frac{2}{b}\left(\ln\frac{1}{|x|}-\ln(1-\ln|x|)\right)+\kappa_{1}

(2.10
)

where

\kappa_{1}=\max\left\{0,\frac{1}{b}\ln\frac{2}{ab}\right\}\quad\text{and }\;\kappa_{2}=\min\left\{0,\frac{1}{b}\ln\frac{2}{ab}\right\}.

(2.11
)

In particular

v_{\frac{2}{b}}(x)=\frac{2}{b}\left(\ln\frac{1}{|x|}-\ln(1-\ln|x|)\right),

(2.12
)

if
ab=2
.

Proof.
Let
\gamma\in(0,\frac{2}{b}]
and
\phi_{1}
be the first eigenfunction of
-\Delta
in
W^{12}_{0}(B_{1})
, with corresponding eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}>0
and supremum equal to
1
(at
r=0
). For
\theta>0
and
A>0
, we set

h_{A}(x)=\gamma\ln\frac{1}{|x|}-A|x|^{\theta}\phi_{1}(x)=\gamma\ln\frac{1}{r}-Ar^{\theta}\phi_{1}(r)\quad\text{with }r=|x|.

(2.13
)

Then there holds in
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(B_{1})
,

-\Delta h_{A}(r)=2\pi\gamma\delta_{0}+A\theta^{2}r^{\theta-2}\phi_{1}(r)-A\lambda_{1}r^{\theta}\phi_{1}(r)+2A\theta r^{\theta-1}\phi^{\prime}_{1}(r),

thus

\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta h_{A}+ae^{bh_{A}}=2\pi\gamma\delta_{0}+r^{\theta-2}\left(A\theta^{2}\phi_{1}(r)-A\lambda_{1}r^{2}\phi_{1}(r)+2A\theta r\phi^{\prime}_{1}(r)+ar^{2-\theta-b\gamma}e^{-bAr^{\theta}\phi_{1}(r)}\right).\end{array}

Step 1: the case
b\gamma<2

(i)
Estimate from above.

Take
\theta=2-b\gamma
. Since

ae^{-bAr^{2-b\gamma}\phi_{1}(r)}\geq a-abAr^{2-b\gamma}\phi_{1}(r),

we derive that for all
0<|x|<1
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta h_{A}+ae^{bh_{A}}=r^{-b\gamma}\left(A(2-b\gamma)^{2}\phi_{1}(r)-A\lambda_{1}r^{2}\phi_{1}(r)+2A(2-b\gamma)r\phi^{\prime}_{1}(r)+ae^{-bAr^{2-b\gamma}\phi_{1}(r)}\right)\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{-\Delta h_{A}+ae^{bh_{A}}}\geq r^{-b\gamma}\left(A(2-b\gamma)^{2}\phi_{1}(r)-A\lambda_{1}r^{2}\phi_{1}(r)+2A(2-b\gamma)r\phi^{\prime}_{1}(r)+a-abAr^{2-b\gamma}\phi_{1}(r)\right).\end{array}

Then there exists
A_{0}>0
such that for
A\leq A_{0}
we have

-\Delta h_{A}+ae^{bh_{A}}\geq 2\gamma\pi\delta_{0}\quad\text{in }{\bf D}^{\prime}(B_{1}),

(2.14
)

and
A_{0}
can be taken independent of
\gamma
. We derive by the comparison principle that

v_{\gamma}(x)\leq\gamma\ln\frac{1}{|x|}-A|x|^{2-b\gamma}\phi_{1}(x)\quad\text{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\}.

(2.15
)

(ii)
Estimate from below.

We give an explicit expression for
v_{\gamma}
. We already know that
v_{\gamma}(x)<\gamma\ln\frac{1}{|x|}
. If we write

v_{\gamma}(x)=\gamma\ln\frac{1}{|x|}+\phi^{*}(x),

(2.16
)

then
\phi^{*}
vanishes on
\partial B_{1}
and satisfies

-\Delta\phi^{*}+ar^{-b\gamma}e^{b\phi^{*}}=0.

The convex functional

J(\phi)=\int_{B_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\frac{a}{b|x|^{b\gamma}}e^{b\phi}\right)dx

is well defined on the closed subset
K_{-}
of
W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{1})
of nonpositive functions and it satisfies

J(\phi)\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}dx.

Hence it achieves its minimum in
K_{-}
at
\phi^{*}
. There holds

-\Delta(\gamma\ln\tfrac{1}{|x|}-A|x|^{2-b\gamma})=A\theta^{2}|x|^{-b\gamma}

and
\phi^{*}
vanishes on
\partial B_{1}
. Since
|x|^{2-b\gamma}\in L^{1+\epsilon}(B_{1})
,
\phi^{*}\in W^{2,1+\epsilon}(B_{1})\subset C^{0,\frac{2\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}}(\overline{B}_{1})
.
Consequently

v_{\gamma}(x)=\gamma\ln\frac{1}{|x|}+\phi^{*}(x)\quad\text{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\},

(2.17
)

and

v_{\gamma}(x)-\gamma\ln\frac{1}{|x|}\to\phi^{*}(0)<0\quad\text{as }x\to 0.

(2.18
)

Step 2: the case
b\gamma=2
.
For
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
we set

\psi_{\kappa}(r)=\frac{2}{b}\left(\ln\frac{1}{r}-\ln(1-\ln r)\right)+\kappa.

Then
\psi_{\kappa}(1)=\kappa
and

-\Delta\psi_{\kappa}+ae^{b\psi_{\kappa}}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(-\frac{2}{b(1-\ln r)^{2}}+\frac{ae^{b\kappa}}{(1-\ln r)^{2}}\right).

(2.19
)

Since

\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{2}(1-\ln|x|)^{2}}=2\pi\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dr}{r(1-\ln r)^{2}}=2\pi

the function
e^{b\psi_{\kappa}}
is integrable in
B_{1}
and
\psi_{\kappa}
satisfies in
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(B_{1})

-\Delta\psi_{\kappa}+ae^{b\psi_{\kappa}}=\left(ae^{b\kappa}-\frac{2}{b}\right)\frac{1}{|x|^{2}(1-\ln|x|)^{2}}+\frac{4\pi}{b}\delta_{0}.

(2.20
)

Hence
\psi_{\kappa}
is a supersolution nonnegative on
\partial B_{1}
if we choose
\kappa=\kappa_{1}
where

\kappa_{1}=\max\left\{0,\frac{1}{b}\ln\frac{2}{ab}\right\}

(2.21
)

It is a subsolution if we take
\kappa=\kappa_{2}
where

\kappa_{2}=\min\left\{0,\frac{1}{b}\ln\frac{2}{ab}\right\}.

(2.22
)

Therefore

\frac{2}{b}\left(\ln\frac{1}{|x|}-\ln(1-\ln|x|)\right)+\kappa_{2}\leq v_{\frac{2}{b}}(x)\leq\frac{2}{b}\left(\ln\frac{1}{|x|}-\ln(1-\ln|x|)\right)+\kappa_{1},

(2.23
)

with equality if

Lemma 3.2

Let

Clearly

-u^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{r}u^{\prime}-m|u^{\prime}|^{q}\leq 0,

then

\left(\frac{1}{1-q}|u^{\prime}|^{1-q}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{1}{r}|u^{\prime}|^{1-q}\leq m,

1-(ii) Convergence and sharp gradient estimates.

Let

be the average of

on

. Then

\ell

\int_{S^{1}}w^{*}d\theta=0

(3.36
)

Combining this estimate with the fact that
\bar{w}(t)\to\ell

\lim_{t\to-\infty}\left\|w(t,.)-\ell\right\|_{L^{2}(S^{1})}=0.

(3.37
)

Let

{\mathcal{T}}[w]=\bigcup_{t\leq 0}\{(w(t,.),w_{t})\}

be the negative trajectory of
(w,w_{t})
. It is relatively compact in

in

and thus

in
C^{1}(S^{1})
. In order to

(\ref{W*})
with respect to

-w^{*}_{ttt}-w^{*}_{t\theta\theta}+e^{(2-b\gamma)t}\tilde{H}^{*}+e^{(2-q)t}\tilde{G}^{*}=0

)

where

and
\tilde{G}^{*}=(2-q)G^{*}-G^{*}_{t}

where

it implies again that

\left\|w_{t}^{*}(t,.)\right\|_{L^{2}(S^{1})}\leq C^{\prime}e^{\beta t}.

(3.39

By
(\ref{WT})

\bar{w}_{t}(t)\to 0
as

. This implies

\lim_{t\to-\infty}\left\|w_{t}(t,.)\right\|_{L^{2}(S^{1})}=0.

(3.40
)

As in the case of the function

.

-\Delta z+m^{2}\theta z^{2}\leq\frac{K}{|x|^{2q}}+\frac{C_{\theta}m^{2}}{2}+\frac{2m}{\delta^{2}}\frac{|\nabla z|^{2}}{z}\quad\text{in }B_{1}\setminus B_{\epsilon}.

(4.32
)

If
\epsilon<|x|<1-\epsilon
we apply
[
1
, Lemma 3.1]
in
B_{|x|-\epsilon}(x)
with
q=2
and derive

z(x):=|\nabla w_{\epsilon}(x)|^{2}\leq\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}}{|x|^{q}}+\frac{\tilde{C}_{2}}{(|x|-\epsilon)^{2}}

(4.33
)

with
\tilde{C}_{j}>0
independent of
\epsilon
. We now define
\epsilon^{*}>\epsilon
by the identity

\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}}{\epsilon^{*\,q}}+\frac{\tilde{C}_{2}}{(\epsilon^{*}-\epsilon)^{2}}=\left(\frac{C^{*}}{\epsilon}\right)^{2}

(4.34
)

where
C^{*}
is defined at
(\ref{E26-3})
(such a
\epsilon^{*}
exists by the mean value theorem). The value of
\epsilon^{*}
cannot be explicited but

\tilde{C}_{1}^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{C^{*}}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\leq\epsilon^{*}\leq\tilde{C}_{1}^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{C^{*}}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}(1+o(1))\quad\text{as }\epsilon\to 0.

(4.35
)

As a consequence the function
w_{\epsilon}
is also a solution of

\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta w+ae^{bw}-m|\nabla w|^{q}=0&\text{in }B_{1}\setminus\overline{B}_{\epsilon^{*}}\\[2.84526pt]
\phantom{-\Delta+ae^{bw}-m|\nabla w|^{q}}w=0&\text{on }\partial B_{1},\end{array}

(4.36
)

and thus it satisfies the upper estimate obtained in Proposition
4.6
,

|\nabla w_{\epsilon}(x)|\leq\frac{C^{*}}{|x|-\epsilon^{*}}\quad\text{for all }x\in B_{1}\setminus\overline{B}_{\epsilon^{*}}.

(4.37
)

Step 3: End of the proof.
It follows from
(\ref{E26-12})
that for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
small enough,
w_{\epsilon}
and
\nabla w_{\epsilon}
are uniformly bounded in

B_{1}\setminus\overline{B}_{2\epsilon^{*}}
. By the standard elliptic equations regularity theory,
w_{\epsilon}
remains bounded in
C^{2,\alpha}(K)
for any
K\subset B_{1}\setminus\overline{B}_{3\epsilon^{*}}
. Using a diagonal sequence, there exists
\{\epsilon_{n}\}
converging to
0
and
\tilde{u}\in C^{2}(\overline{B}_{1}\setminus\{0\})
such that
\{w_{\epsilon_{n}}\}
converges to

\tilde{u}
in the
C^{2}_{loc}
-topology of
\overline{B}_{1}\setminus\{0\}
. Therefore
\tilde{u}
is a solution of
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
which vanishes on
\partial B_{1}
and satisfies
(u-\mu)_{+}\leq\tilde{u}\leq u
jointly with the gradient estimate
(\ref{E26-2+})
.
\square

Proposition 4.8

Let
q>2
and
u\in C^{1}(B_{1}\setminus\{0\})\cap C(\overline{B_{1}}\setminus\{0\})
is a nonnegative solutions of
(\ref{E5})
in
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
which vanishes on

\partial B_{1}
and satisfies

\lim_{x\to 0}u(x)=\infty.

(4.38
)

Then
u
is radially symmetric.

Proof.
The proof is an easy variant of the moving plane method as it is introduced in
[
13
]
(see also
[
16
, Theorem 8.2.2]
). The only difference is the assumption that
u
satisfies
(\ref{E26-1})
which actually allows the reflection through the singular point. The sketch of the proof is as follows. We set
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
and, if
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
B^{\lambda}=\{x\in B_{1}:x_{1}>\lambda\}
,
T^{\lambda}=\{x\in B_{1}:x_{1}=\lambda\}
and

C^{\lambda}=\partial B_{1}\cap\overline{B^{\lambda}}
. Put
x_{\lambda}=(2\lambda-x_{1},x_{2})

and
u_{\lambda}(x)=u(x_{\lambda})
. If
w=u_{\lambda}-u
, there holds

-\Delta w(x)+A(x)w(x)-B(x).\nabla w(x)=0\quad\text{in }B^{\lambda}

(4.39
)

where

A(x)=ab\int_{0}^{1}e^{b(tu_{\lambda}(x)+(1-t)u(x)}dt

and

B(x)=mq\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla\left(tu_{\lambda}(x)+(1-t)u(x)\right)|^{q-2}\nabla\left(tu_{\lambda}(x)+(1-t)u(x)\right)dt

The two functions
A
and
B
are Hölder continuous in
B^{\lambda}
for
\lambda>0
. Since
u>0
in
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
and vanishes on
\partial B_{1}
, the outward normal derivative
\frac{\partial u}{\partial{\bf n}}
is negative on
\partial B_{1}
. For
\lambda\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
the function
w
satisfies
(\ref{E26-2++})
in
B^{\lambda}
. It vanishes on
T^{\lambda}
and is positive on
C^{\lambda}
except at the two end-points
belonging to
\partial B_{1}\cap T^{\lambda}
. In fact, if we assume now that there exists
x^{*}\in\overline{B^{\lambda}}
where
w
achieves a negative minimum, then
x^{*}\in B^{\lambda}
and because
w
is
C^{2}
,

\nabla w(x^{*})=0
and
-\Delta w(x^{*})\leq 0
. Since

-\Delta w(x^{*})+A(x^{*})w(x^{*})-B(x^{*}).\nabla w(x^{*})=0

and
A(x^{*})>0
we have a contradiction. If
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
the function
w
is not continuous in whole
\overline{B_{\lambda}}
, but since
u
satisfies
(\ref{E26-1})
, the function

w
tends to infinity when
x\to(2\lambda,0)
(this point is the reflected image of
(0,0)
by the symmetry
(x\mapsto x_{\lambda})
). Hence the minimum of
w
in
\overline{B_{\lambda}}
coincides with the minimum in
\overline{B^{\lambda}}\setminus B_{\delta}((2\lambda,0))
. Since
w
is
C^{2}
therein, this implies that it cannot achieve a negative minimum. As a consequence
w\geq 0
in

B^{0}_{+}:=B_{1}\cap\{x=(x_{1},x_{2}):x_{1}>0\}
, and by continuity this inequality holds also in
\overline{B}_{1}\cap\{x=(x_{1},x_{2}):x_{1}\geq 0\}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
. This implies in particular

u_{x_{1}}(0,x_{2})\geq 0
. Starting the reflection process from the point
(-1,0)
we obtain in the same way
u_{x_{1}}(0,x_{2})\leq 0
. Hence
u_{x_{1}}(0,x_{2})=0
for all
x_{2}\in(-1,1)
. Because the equation is invariant by rotation we can replace
the axis
(0,x_{2})
by any axis through
(0,0)
. Consequently we have
u_{\theta}(\theta,r)=0
in polar coordinates, which is the claim.
\square

Proof of Theorem 3
. It follows from Proposition
4.2
, Proposition
4.3
, Proposition
4.5
, Lemma
4.7
and Proposition
4.8
.
\square

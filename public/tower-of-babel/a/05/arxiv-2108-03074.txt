Adaptive finite element method for an elliptic optimal control problem with integral state constraints Thanks: The first author’s work is supported by CSIR Extramural Research Grant (Grant No. 25(0297)/19/EMR-II)
Kamana Porwal, Pratibha Shakya

Abstract
. In this article, we develop a posteriori error analysis of a nonconforming finite element method for a linear quadratic elliptic distributed optimal control problem with two different set of constraints, namely (i) integral state constraint and integral control constraint (ii) integral state constraint and pointwise control constraints. In the analysis, we have taken the approach of reducing the state-control constrained minimization problem into a state minimization problem obtained by eliminating the control variable. The reliability and efficiency of a posteriori error estimator are discussed. Numerical results are reported to illustrate the behavior of the error estimator.



Key words.

Elliptic optimal control problem, Fourth order variational inequality, Integral state constraints, Adaptive finite element method

Introduction

Optimal control problems (OCPs) play an important role in various applications in physics, mechanics and other engineering sciences. For the theoretical and numerical development of the OCPs, we refer to
[

,

,

[

and Geveci
[
25

The authors of
[

[
30

have introduced the variational discretization method, therein the error estimates are obtained by exploiting the relationship between the state and adjoint state. The numerical approximation of the elliptic optimal control problems with control variable from measure spaces can be found in
[
16
,
17

.

There have been abundant research on the adaptive finite method for OCPs governed by differential equations in last few decades. The use of adaptive techniques based on a posteriori error estimation is well accepted in the context of finite element discretization of partial differential equations
[
2
,
56
]
. In this direction, the pioneer work has been made by Liu and Yan
[
43
]
for residual based a posteriori error estimates, and Becker et al.
[
4

for dual-weighted goal oriented adaptivity for optimal control problems. In
[
36

the authors have proved that the sequence of adaptively generated discrete solutions converge to the true solutions of OCPs. Recently, Gong and Yan
[
27

have presented a rigorous proof for convergence and quasi-optimality of adaptive finite element method for an OCP with pointwise control constraints by means of variational discretization technique. In
[
57
]
, Wolfmayr has derived functional type a posteriori error estimates for elliptic optimal control problems with control constraints. The authors of
[
54
]
have studied the finite element approximation of OCPs governed by elliptic equations with measure data, therein they have
derived both a priori and a posteriori error bounds for the state and control variables. We refer to the reference section for other notable works on the adaptive finite element methods for OCPs with control constraints.

[

[
32

A priori analysis of OCPs with integral state constraint is discussed in
[
46
,
59
,
60

52

[

[
20

[
59

In this article, we use a different approach to analyze adaptive finite element method for OCPs with integral state constraints. This approach avoids the use of the first order optimality conditions and therein the state and control constrained OCP can be reformulated into purely state constrained optimization problem.
The optimal state is then obtained by solving a fourth order variational inequality. The main intent of this article is to derive a reliable and efficient a posteriori error estimator of a non-conforming finite element method for the elliptic distributed optimal control problem with two different set of constraints, namely (i) with integral state constraint and integral control constraint (ii) with integral state constraint and pointwise control constraints.
We refer to
[
11
,
9
,
45
,
12
]
, for the numerical analysis of constrained OCP based on the approach of reduction to purely state constrained optimization problem.
Recently in
[
10
]
, the authors have used
C^{0}
interior penalty method for an elliptic state-constrained optimal control problem with Neumann boundary conditions and derived a priori and a posteriori error estimates.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a convex polygonal domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. For any
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
D\subset{\Omega}
, we denote
L^{p}(D)
norm by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(D)}
. We adopt the standard notations
W^{m,p}(\Omega)
and
W_{0}^{m,p}(\Omega)
for Sobolev spaces for
p\in[1,\infty]
and
m\geq 0
equipped with norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{m,p}(\Omega)}
and seminorm
|\cdot|_{W^{m,p}(\Omega)}
. When
p=2
, we denote
W^{m,p}(\Omega)
by
H^{m}(\Omega)
and
W_{0}^{m,p}(\Omega)
by
H^{m}_{0}(\Omega)
and corresponding norm and seminorm are denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}
and
|\cdot|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}
, respectively.
We consider the following state and control constrained optimal control problem: to find
(\tilde{u},\tilde{y})\in L^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle J(\tilde{u},\tilde{y})=\min_{(u,y)\in L^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\,J(u,y)

subject to

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\int_{\Omega}\nabla y\cdot\nabla w\,dx=\int_{\Omega}uw\,dx\;\;\forall w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\;\;\\
\int_{\Omega}u\,dx\geq\delta_{1},\;\;\;\;\;\\
\int_{\Omega}y\,dx\geq\delta_{2},\end{cases}

where
J(u,y)=\|y-y_{d}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
with
y_{d}
as the given desired state,
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta>0
is a given constant.

The rest of the article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we obtain the characterization of the solution of the optimization problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) by the solution of a fourth order variational inequality and discuss the optimality conditions of the underlying OCP with integral state as well as integral control constraints. In Section
3
, we introduce notations and preliminary results required in the subsequent sections. Therein, we also discuss the finite element discretization of the continuous problem by a bubble enriched Morley finite element method and present the optimality conditions associated to the discrete problem. A posteriori error estimator of the underlying finite element method in introduced in Section
4
, followed by that reliability and efficiency estimates are established. In Section
5
, we discuss a posteriori error bounds for an OCP with integral state and pointwise control constraints using the proposed finite element method. Finally, in Section
6
, we present numerical results to illustrate the performance of derived a posteriori error estimators.

2.
Continuous Variational Inequality and Optimality Conditions

This section is devoted to characterize the solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) by the solution of a variational inequality and discuss the associated optimality conditions.

For
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, Lax-Milgram lemma
[
21
]
ensures the existence of a unique solution
y\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying the variational formulation

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla y\cdot\nabla w\,dx=\int_{\Omega}uw\,dx~~\qquad\forall w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Moreover, from elliptic regularity theory (cf.
[
1
,
28
]
) we obtain
y\in H^{2}(\Omega)
. Set
W=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Using
u=-\Delta y
, we can rewrite the optimization problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) as follows: to find
{y}^{*}\in\mathcal{K}
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle{y}^{*}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle argmin_{y\in\mathcal{K}}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(y-y_{d})^{2}\,dx+\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta y)^{2}\,dx\Big]

where
\mathcal{K}
is defined by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=\{w\in W:\,\int_{\Omega}w\,dx\geq\delta_{2}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta w)~dx\geq\delta_{1}\;\}.

The minimizer of (
2.2
) can further be characterized by the minimizer of the following optimization problem: find
{y}^{*}\in\mathcal{K}
such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle{y}^{*}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle argmin_{y\in\mathcal{K}}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}(y,y)-(y_{d},y)\Big]

where

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(v,w)=\beta\int_{\Omega}D^{2}v:D^{2}w\,dx+\int_{\Omega}vw\,dx,\;\;v,w\in W,

with
D^{2}v:D^{2}w=\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\Big)\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\Big)
where
D^{2}
denotes the Hessian matrix.

We assume the following Slater condition holds
[
55
]
: there exists
y\in W
satisfying
\int_{\Omega}y\,dx>\delta_{2}
and
\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta y)\,dx\geq\delta_{1}
. This ensures that the set
\mathcal{K}
is nonempty, together with closed and convex. Since the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
is bounded, coercive and symmetric on
W
, by the standard theory (cf.
[
26
,
35
]
) there exists a unique solution
y^{*}\in\mathcal{K}
of (
2.4
) satisfying
the following variational inequality

(2.6)

\mathcal{A}({y}^{*},w-{y}^{*})\geq\int_{\Omega}y_{d}(w-{y}^{*})\,dx\;\;\;\;\forall w\in\mathcal{K}.

Using the Lagrange multiplier approach, we obtain the following Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions (cf.
[
34
,
47
]
) together with complementarity conditions (
2.8
)-(
2.11
): there exist
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(y^{*},w)=\int_{\Omega}y_{d}w\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\lambda(\Delta w)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mu w\,dx\;\;\;\;\forall w\in W,

with

(2.8)

\displaystyle\lambda\geq 0,\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\text{if}~~\;\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta{y}^{*})\,dx=\delta_{1},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\lambda=0,\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\text{if}~~\;\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta{y}^{*})\,dx>\delta_{1},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mu\geq 0,\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\text{if}~~\;\int_{\Omega}{y}^{*}\,dx=\delta_{2},

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mu=0,\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\text{if}~~\;\int_{\Omega}{y}^{*}\,dx>\delta_{2}.

Note that, the adjoint state
p\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfy

(2.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla p\cdot\nabla w\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(y^{*}-y_{d})\,w\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\mu w\,dx\;\;\forall w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

3.
Notations and Finite Element Discretization

In this section, we introduce the discrete control problem and present some useful tools required for subsequent analysis.
Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a regular triangulation of the domain
\Omega
. The following notations will be used throughout this article.

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{e}:\text{set of elements in}\;\mathcal{T}_{h}\;\text{that share the common edge}\;e,

\displaystyle h_{T}:\,\text{the diameter of the triangle}\;\;T,\;\;\;\;h=\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{h}:\;\text{set of all vertices of}\,\mathcal{T}_{h},

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{T}:\,\text{set of three vertices of}~\,T,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}:\;\text{the set of the edges of the triangle in}\;\mathcal{T}_{h},\text{where}\,\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i}(\text{resp.,}\,\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b})\;\;\text{is the subset of}\,\mathcal{E}_{h}\,

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\text{consisting of edges interior to}\,\Omega\,(\text{resp., along}\,\partial\Omega),

\displaystyle h_{e}:\,\text{length of an edge}\,e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}:\,\text{piesewise (element-wise) Laplacian operator}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{k}(T):~\text{space of polynomials defined on T of degree less
than or equal to}~k,~k\geq 0~\text{integer},

\displaystyle X\lesssim Y:\text{there exists a positive constant}~C~\text{(independent of mesh parameter) such that}~X\leq CY,

\displaystyle X\approx Y:\text{there exists positive constants}~C_{1}~\text{and}~C_{2}~\text{such that}~C_{1}Y\leq X\leq C_{2}Y.

Throughout this article, the constant
C
will denote a positive generic constant.

We denote by
H^{k}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})
the broken Sobolev space

\displaystyle H^{k}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\{w\in L^{2}(\Omega):\,w_{T}=w|_{T}\in H^{k}(T)\;\;\;\;\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

Let
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i}
be the common side shared by elements
T_{+}
and
T_{-}
.
Further, suppose
n_{+}
is the unit normal of
e
pointing from
T_{+}
to
T_{-}
, and
n_{-}=-n_{+}
. For any scalar valued function
w\in H^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})
, we define the jumps
\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\cdot\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]
, and averages
\left\{\hskip-5.0pt\left\{\cdot\right\}\hskip-5.0pt\right\}
across the edge
e
as follows:

\displaystyle\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial w}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]=\frac{\partial w_{+}}{\partial n}\Big|_{e}-\frac{\partial w_{-}}{\partial n}\Big|_{e}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\left\{\hskip-5.0pt\left\{\frac{\partial w}{\partial n}\right\}\hskip-5.0pt\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{\partial w_{+}}{\partial n}\Big|_{e}+\frac{\partial w_{-}}{\partial n}\Big|_{e}\Big).

For any
w\in H^{3}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h}),
we define

\displaystyle\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]=\frac{\partial^{2}w_{+}}{\partial n^{2}}\Big|_{e}-\frac{\partial^{2}w_{-}}{\partial n^{2}}\Big|_{e}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\left\{\hskip-5.0pt\left\{\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}\right\}\hskip-5.0pt\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}w_{+}}{\partial n^{2}}\Big|_{e}+\frac{\partial^{2}w_{-}}{\partial n^{2}}\Big|_{e}\Big),

where
w_{\pm}=w|_{T_{\pm}}
. For
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}
, we choose
n
be the unit outward normal of
e
and let
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
be such that
e=\partial T\cap\partial\Omega
. Set

\displaystyle\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial w}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]=\frac{\partial w|_{T}}{\partial n}\Big|_{e}\;\text{for any}\;w\in H^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h}).

Before introducing the finite element spaces, we define for each triangle
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
a cubic bubble function
b_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{3}(T)
by

\displaystyle b_{T}=60\lambda_{1}^{T}\lambda_{1}^{T}\lambda_{3}^{T},

where
\lambda_{i}^{T},~i=1,2,3
are the barycentric coordinates of
T
associated with the vertices
p_{i}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{T}
.

Discrete Spaces
:
Let
V_{M}
denote the Morley finite element space
[
50
]
defined by

\displaystyle V_{M}=

\displaystyle\{w_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega):\,w_{h}|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(T)~\forall~T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\;w_{h}\,\text{is continuous at the vertices of}~\mathcal{T}_{h},~

\displaystyle\quad\text{and the normal derivative of}\,w_{h}\;\text{is continuous at the midpoint of the edges of}~\mathcal{T}_{h},~

\displaystyle\quad\text{and}\,w_{h}\,\text{vanish on}\,\partial\Omega\},

and define the space
V_{h}
as

\displaystyle V_{h}=\{w_{h}\in L^{2}({\Omega}):w_{h}|_{T}\in~\text{span}({b_{T}})~\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}.

The finite element space
W_{h}
is defined as

W_{h}=V_{M}\oplus V_{h}.

The discrete norm
\|\cdot\|_{h}
on
W_{h}
is defined by

\displaystyle\|w_{h}\|_{h}^{2}=\beta\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}|w_{h}|_{H^{2}(T)}^{2}+\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\quad\mbox{for}~~w_{h}\in W_{h}.

The discrete approximation of the convex set
\mathcal{K}
is then given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{h}=\{w_{h}\in W_{h}:\;\;\int_{\Omega}w_{h}\,dx\geq\delta_{2}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta_{h}w_{h})\,dx\geq\delta_{1}\}.

Next, we define the projection, interpolation and enriching operators and tabulate their approximation properties required in further analysis.

Discrete Operators
:
For any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
w\in L^{1}(T)
, define

(3.2)

\displaystyle Q_{T}(w)=\frac{1}{|T|}\int_{T}w\,dx.

Let
W_{pc,h}:=\{w\in L^{1}(\Omega):\,w|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(T)\;\forall~T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
. Then,
Q_{h}:L^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow W_{pc,h}
is defined by setting
Q_{h}(w)|_{T}=Q_{T}(w)\;\;\text{for all}\;w\in L^{1}(\Omega)
,
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
.

Define interpolation operator
I_{h}:W\rightarrow W_{h}
as: for
\xi\in W
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle(I_{h}\xi)(p)

\displaystyle=\xi(p)\;\;\;\;\forall p\in\mathcal{V}_{h},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{e}\frac{\partial(I_{h}\xi)}{\partial n}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{e}\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial n}\,ds,\;\;\;\;\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h},

(3.5)

\displaystyle Q_{T}(I_{h}\xi)

\displaystyle=Q_{T}(\xi),\;\;\;\;\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

The interpolation operator is well-defined and
(I_{h}w)|_{T}=w
for any
w\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(T)
.

For any
\xi\in W
, using (
3.5
) we find

(3.6)

\int_{\Omega}I_{h}\xi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\xi\,dx.

Further, a use of integration by parts and (
3.4
) yields

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{T}\Delta(I_{h}\xi)\,dx=\sum_{e\in\partial T}\int_{e}\frac{\partial(I_{h}\xi)}{\partial n}\,ds=\int_{T}(\Delta\xi)\,dx\;\;\;\;\;\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

which implies that

(3.8)

\displaystyle Q_{h}(\Delta_{h}(I_{h}\xi))=Q_{h}(\Delta\xi),\;\;\;\;\;\forall\xi\in W.

In view of (
2.3
), (
3.1
), (
3.6
) and (
3.8
), we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle I_{h}\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{K}_{h}.

This relation also depicts that the discrete set
\mathcal{K}_{h}
is non-empty. We would like to remark here that enriching the Morley finite element space
V_{M}
by the bubble function space
V_{h}
plays a crucial role in obtaining (
3.9
).

Below, we state the stability and approximation properties of
I_{h}
, whose proof follows by using Bramble Hilbert lemma and scaling arguments; see
[
8
,
21
]
for details.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
s
be an integer such that
0\leq s\leq 2
and
\psi\in H^{s}(T)
. Then,

(3.10)

\displaystyle|I_{h}\psi|_{H^{s}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\psi|_{H^{s}(T)},\qquad 0\leq s\leq 2

(3.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{s}h_{T}^{k-s}|\psi-I_{h}\psi|_{H^{k}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\psi|_{H^{s}(T)},\qquad 0\leq s\leq 2.

Now we define an important tool for the analysis, the enriching operator
E_{h}:W_{h}\rightarrow(M_{h}\oplus V_{h})\cap W
, where
M_{h}
is the Hsieh-Clough-Tocher macro element space
[
21
]
associated with
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. The operator
E_{h}
can be constructed by averaging techniques (cf.
[
13
,
12
,
52
]
) satisfying

(3.12)

\displaystyle\int_{e}\frac{\partial(E_{h}w_{h})}{\partial n}\,ds=\int_{e}\frac{\partial w_{h}}{\partial n}\,ds\;\;\;\;\;\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\text{and}~~~\int_{T}E_{h}w_{h}\,dx=\int_{T}w_{h}\,dx\;\;\;\;\;\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

An application of integration by parts and (
3.12
) leads to

(3.14)

\displaystyle Q_{h}(\Delta E_{h}w_{h})=Q_{h}(\Delta_{h}w_{h}).

Moreover, the enriching operator satisfies the following approximation properties (cf.
[
12
]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
w_{h}\in W_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big(h_{T}^{-4}\|w_{h}-E_{h}w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}+h_{T}^{-2}|w_{h}-E_{h}w_{h}|_{H^{1}(T)}^{2}+|w_{h}-E_{h}w_{h}|^{2}_{H^{2}(T)}\Big)\lesssim\|w_{h}\|_{h}^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}|w_{h}-E_{h}w_{h}|_{H^{2}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}}\Big\|\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial w_{h}}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\Big\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\;\;\;\;\forall w_{h}\in W_{h}.

We recall the following inverse and trace inequalities which will be useful in later analysis
[
21
]
.

Inverse Inequalities:
For any
w_{h}\in W_{h}
and
1\leq p\,,q<\infty
,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\|w_{h}\|_{W^{m,p}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{T}^{\ell-m}h_{T}^{2\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|w_{h}\|_{W^{\ell,q}(T)}\quad

\displaystyle\forall\,T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h},

(3.16)

\displaystyle\|\nabla w_{h}\|_{L^{p}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{T}^{-1}\|w_{h}\|_{L^{p}(T)}\quad

\displaystyle\forall\,T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}.

Discrete trace inequality:
Let
\psi\in W^{1,p}(T),~T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}

and let
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
be an edge of
T
. Then for any
1\leq p<\infty
, it holds that

(3.17)

\|\psi\|_{L^{p}(e)}^{p}\lesssim h_{e}^{-1}\big(\|\psi\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p}+h_{e}^{p}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p}\big).

Discrete Problem
: The discrete form of the minimization problem (
2.4
) is defined as follows: Find
y_{h}^{*}\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
such that

(3.18)

\displaystyle y_{h}^{*}=argmin_{y_{h}\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\Bigg(\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}_{h}(y_{h},y_{h})-(y_{d},y_{h})\Bigg),

where

(3.19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}(w_{h},v_{h})=\beta\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}D^{2}w_{h}:D^{2}v_{h}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}w_{h}v_{h}\,dx,~~\;\;\;\;w_{h},v_{h}\in W_{h}.

Since
\mathcal{K}_{h}
is non-empty, closed, convex and the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric and positive definite on
W_{h}
, the discrete problem (
3.18
) is well-posed and it’s solution is characterized by the solution of the discrete variational inequality

(3.20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}(y_{h}^{*},w_{h}-y_{h}^{*})\geq(y_{d},w_{h}-y_{h}^{*})\;\;\;\;\forall w_{h}\in\mathcal{K}_{h}.

As in the case of the continuous problem, we have the following optimality conditions associated with the discrete problem
[
55
]
:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{y}_{h}^{*}\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
be the optimal solution of the discrete problem, then there exists Lagrange multipliers
\lambda_{h}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu_{h}\in\mathbb{R}
such that the following conditions hold:

(3.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*},w_{h})-\int_{\Omega}y_{d}w_{h}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\mu_{h}w_{h}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{h}(\Delta_{h}w_{h})\,dx,\quad\forall w_{h}\in W_{h},

together with

(3.22)

\displaystyle\mu_{h}\geq 0,\;\;\;\;\lambda_{h}\geq 0,

(3.23)

\displaystyle\mu_{h}\Big(\delta_{2}-\int_{\Omega}{y}_{h}^{*}\,dx\Big)=0,

(3.24)

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}\Big(\delta_{1}+\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}\,dx\Big)=0.

4.
A Posteriori Error Analysis

In this section we introduce a posteriori error estimator and present the first main result of the paper, namely, the reliability analysis of the error estimator. Followed by that, we also discuss the efficiency estimates of a posteriori error estimator.
The contributions of error estimator are defined by

\displaystyle\eta_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\beta^{-1}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{4}\|y_{d}+\mu_{h}-{y}_{h}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2},

\displaystyle\eta_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\beta\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}\frac{1}{h_{e}}\Big\|\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial{y}^{*}_{h}}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\Big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e)},

\displaystyle\eta_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle=\beta\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}h_{e}\Bigg\|\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial^{2}{y}^{*}_{h}}{\partial n^{2}}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\Bigg\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2},

\displaystyle\eta_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle=\beta\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}h_{e}^{3}\Bigg\|\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial(\Delta{y}_{h}^{*})}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\Bigg\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e)},

\displaystyle\eta_{5}^{2}

\displaystyle=\beta^{-1}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}|\lambda_{h}|^{2}.

The full error estimator
\eta_{h}
is given by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\eta_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=\eta_{1}^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2}+\eta_{3}^{2}+\eta_{4}^{2}+\eta_{5}^{2}.

4.1.
Reliability of Error Estimator

Below, we establish the reliability estimates of a posteriori error estimator
\eta_{h}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{y}^{*}
and
{y}^{*}_{h}
be solutions of variational inequalities (
2.6
) and (
3.20
), respectively. Then, it holds that,

\displaystyle\|{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}\lesssim\eta_{h}.

Proof.

We set
\phi={y}^{*}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}\in W
and let
\phi_{h}\in W_{h}
. Using the coercive property of the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
, (
2.7
) and (
3.21
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|{y}^{*}-E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{A}({y}^{*}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*},\phi)=\mathcal{A}({y}^{*},\phi)-\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*},\phi)+\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*},\phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega}y_{d}\phi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\lambda(\Delta\phi)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mu\phi\,dx-\mathcal{A}_{h}(\bar{y}_{h},\phi)+\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*},\phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega}y_{d}(\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\lambda(\Delta\phi)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mu\phi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{h}(\Delta_{h}\phi_{h})\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\mu_{h}\phi_{h}\,dx

\displaystyle~~~~-\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*},\phi-\phi_{h})+\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*},\phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega}y_{d}(\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\mu_{h}(\phi_{h}-\phi)\,dx-\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*},\phi-\phi_{h})-\int_{\Omega}(\lambda-\lambda_{h})(\Delta\phi)\,dx

(4.2)

\displaystyle~~~~+\int_{\Omega}(\mu-\mu_{h})\phi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{h}\Delta_{h}(\phi_{h}-\phi)\,dx+\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*},\phi).

Now, we bound the terms of the right hand side of the last estimate. The estimation of first three terms is discussed towards the end. We first handle the rest terms other than the first three terms.
For the fourth term in (
4.1
), using the discrete and continuous complementarity conditions we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\lambda-\lambda_{h})(-\Delta\phi)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\lambda-\lambda_{h})\big(-\Delta({y}^{*}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*})\big)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\lambda(-\Delta{y}^{*}+\Delta E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*})\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{h}(-\Delta{y}^{*}+\Delta E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*})\,dx

\displaystyle=\lambda\Big(\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta{y}^{*})~dx-\delta_{1}\Big)+\lambda\Big(\delta_{1}+\int_{\Omega}\Delta E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}~dx\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt-\lambda_{h}\Big(\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta{y}^{*})~dx-\delta_{1}\Big)-\lambda_{h}\Big(\delta_{1}+\int_{\Omega}\Delta E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}~dx\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\Big(\delta_{1}+\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}~dx\Big)-\lambda_{h}\Big(\delta_{1}+\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}~dx\Big)

(4.3)

\displaystyle\leq 0,

where in obtaining second last estimate we have used that
\int_{\Omega}\Delta E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}~dx
,
\lambda_{h}\Big(\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta{y}^{*})~dx-\delta_{1}\Big)\geq 0
and then
\lambda\Big(\delta_{1}+\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}~dx\Big)\leq 0
.

Next, we handle the fifth term of right hand side of (
4.1
). A use of (
2.10
), (
2.11
), (
3.13
) together with
\mu_{h}\geq 0
,
\int_{\Omega}{y}^{*}~dx\geq\delta_{2}
and
\mu\Big(\delta_{2}-\int_{\Omega}{y}_{h}^{*}\,dx\Big)\leq 0
, yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mu-\mu_{h})\phi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mu({y}^{*}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*})\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\mu_{h}({y}^{*}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*})\,dx

\displaystyle=\mu\Big(\int_{\Omega}{y}^{*}\,dx-\delta_{2}\Big)+\mu\Big(\delta_{2}-\int_{\Omega}E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}\,dx\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt-\mu_{h}\Big(\int_{\Omega}{y}^{*}~dx-\delta_{2}\Big)-\mu_{h}\Big(\delta_{2}-\int_{\Omega}E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}~dx\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mu\Big(\delta_{2}-\int_{\Omega}E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}\,dx\Big)-\mu_{h}\Big(\delta_{2}-\int_{\Omega}E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}~dx\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mu\Big(\delta_{2}-\int_{\Omega}{y}_{h}^{*}\,dx\Big)-\mu_{h}\Big(\delta_{2}-\int_{\Omega}{y}_{h}^{*}~dx\Big)

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq 0.

The estimate on the last two terms of (
4.1
) can be realized with an application of Lemmas
3.2
and
3.1
as,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*},\phi)

\displaystyle\leq\|{y}_{h}^{*}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}\|_{h}\|\phi\|_{h}\leq\eta_{2}\|\phi\|_{h},

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{h}\Delta_{h}(\phi_{h}-\phi)\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|\lambda_{h}|\int_{T}|\Delta(\phi_{h}-\phi)|~dx\leq\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}|\lambda_{h}|\|\Delta(\phi_{h}-\phi)\|_{L^{2}(T)}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}|\lambda_{h}||\phi|_{H^{2}(T)}\leq\eta_{5}\|\phi\|_{h}.

We now proceed to handle the first three terms of (
4.1
). Performing integration by parts twice yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*},\phi-\phi_{h})

\displaystyle=\beta\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}D^{2}{y}_{h}^{*}:D^{2}(\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{y}_{h}^{*}(\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx

\displaystyle=\beta\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}\Delta^{2}{y}_{h}^{*}(\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx+\beta\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}{y}^{*}_{h}}{\partial n^{2}}\Big)\Big(\frac{\partial(\phi-\phi_{h})}{\partial n}\Big)\,ds

\displaystyle~~~~+\beta\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}{y}_{h}^{*}}{\partial n\partial t}\Big)\Big(\frac{\partial(\phi-\phi_{h})}{\partial t}\Big)\,ds-\beta\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}\Big(\frac{\partial\Delta{y}_{h}^{*}}{\partial n}\Big)(\phi-\phi_{h})\,ds

\displaystyle~~~~~+\int_{\Omega}{y}_{h}^{*}(\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx

\displaystyle=\beta\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}\int_{e}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial\Delta{y}_{h}^{*}}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right](\phi-\phi_{h})\,ds-\beta\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}\int_{e}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial^{2}{y}_{h}^{*}}{\partial n^{2}}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\left\{\hskip-5.0pt\left\{\frac{\partial(\phi-\phi_{h})}{\partial n}\right\}\hskip-5.0pt\right\}~ds

\displaystyle~~~~-\beta\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}\int_{e}\left\{\hskip-5.0pt\left\{\frac{\partial^{2}{y}_{h}^{*}}{\partial n^{2}}\right\}\hskip-5.0pt\right\}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial(\phi-\phi_{h})}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\,ds-\beta\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}\int_{e}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial^{2}{y}_{h}^{*}}{\partial n\partial t}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]{\frac{\partial(\phi-\phi_{h})}{\partial t}}~ds

(4.7)

\displaystyle~~~~~+\int_{\Omega}{y}_{h}^{*}(\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}y_{d}(\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\mu_{h}(\phi_{h}-\phi)\,dx-\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*},\phi-\phi_{h})=\int_{\Omega}(y_{d}+\mu_{h}-{y}_{h}^{*})(\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx

\displaystyle~~~~~-\beta\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}\int_{e}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial\Delta{y}_{h}^{*}}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right](\phi-\phi_{h})\,ds+\beta\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}\int_{e}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial^{2}{y}_{h}^{*}}{\partial n^{2}}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\left\{\hskip-5.0pt\left\{\frac{\partial(\phi-\phi_{h})}{\partial n}\right\}\hskip-5.0pt\right\}~ds

\displaystyle~~~~~+\beta\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}\int_{e}\left\{\hskip-5.0pt\left\{\frac{\partial^{2}{y}_{h}^{*}}{\partial n^{2}}\right\}\hskip-5.0pt\right\}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial(\phi-\phi_{h})}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\,ds+\beta\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}\int_{e}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial^{2}{y}_{h}^{*}}{\partial n\partial t}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]{\frac{\partial(\phi-\phi_{h})}{\partial t}}~ds.

Now, we estimate the terms of right hand ride as follows: a use of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Lemma
3.1
yields

(4.8)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}(y_{d}+\mu_{h}-{y}_{h}^{*})(\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}\|y_{d}+\mu_{h}-{y}_{h}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(T)}h_{T}^{-2}\|\phi-\phi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}\|y_{d}+\mu_{h}-{y}_{h}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(T)}|\phi|_{H^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\eta_{1}\|\phi\|_{h}.

Using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, discrete trace inequality and Lemma
3.1
, we find

\displaystyle\Big|\beta\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}\int_{e}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial^{2}{y}_{h}^{*}}{\partial n^{2}}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]

\displaystyle\left\{\hskip-5.0pt\left\{\frac{\partial(\phi-\phi_{h})}{\partial n}\right\}\hskip-5.0pt\right\}~ds\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\beta\Bigg(\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}h_{e}\Big\|\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial^{2}{y}_{h}^{*}}{\partial n^{2}}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\Big\|_{L_{2}(e)}^{2}\Bigg)^{1/2}\Bigg(\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}h_{e}^{-1}\Bigg\|\left\{\hskip-5.0pt\left\{\frac{\partial(\phi-\phi_{h})}{\partial n}\right\}\hskip-5.0pt\right\}\Bigg\|_{L_{2}(e)}^{2}\Bigg)^{1/2}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim\eta_{3}\|\phi\|_{h}.

Invoking Lemma
3.1
together with inverse inequality and discrete trace inequality (
3.17
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\beta\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}\int_{e}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial^{2}{y}_{h}^{*}}{\partial n\partial t}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(\phi-\phi_{h})}{\partial t}\,ds\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\beta\Bigg(\displaystyle\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}h_{e}\Big\|\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial^{2}{y}_{h}^{*}}{\partial n\partial t}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\Big\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg(\displaystyle\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}h_{e}^{-1}\Bigg\|\frac{\partial(\phi-\phi_{h})}{\partial t}\Bigg\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim\eta_{2}\|\phi\|_{h}

and,

\displaystyle\Big|\beta\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}\int_{e}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial(\Delta{y}_{h}^{*})}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]

\displaystyle(\phi-\phi_{h})\,ds\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\beta\Bigg(\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}h_{e}^{3}\Bigg\|\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial(\Delta{y}_{h}^{*})}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\Bigg\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{i}}h_{e}^{-3}\|\phi-\phi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim\eta_{4}\|\phi\|_{h}.

Finally, combining the estimates (
4.1
)-(
4.1
) together with (
4.1
), we get the desired result.
∎

In order to obtain the reliability estimates for Lagrange multiplier errors
|\mu-\mu_{h}|\;\;\text{and}\;\;|\lambda-\lambda_{h}|
, we introduce the auxiliary variables
z_{h}\in W
and
\hat{p}_{h}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying the following equations.

(4.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(z_{h},w)=\int_{\Omega}y_{d}w\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{h}\,(-\Delta w)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mu_{h}\,w\,dx\;\;\;\;\forall w\in W.

and

(4.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\hat{p}_{h}\cdot\nabla w\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(z_{h}-y_{d})w\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\mu_{h}w\,dx\;\;\;\;\forall w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

The well-posedness of these auxiliary problems
4.12
and
4.13
follows from Lax-Milgram lemma
[
21
]
. These auxiliary problems help in estimating the errors in Lagrange multipliers. In the next lemma, we estimate the error
\|z_{h}-{y}^{*}\|_{h}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, such that

\displaystyle\|z_{h}-{y}^{*}\|_{h}\leq C\eta_{h}.

Proof.

We have,

(4.14)

\displaystyle\|z_{h}-{y}^{*}\|_{h}\lesssim\Big(\|z_{h}-{y}_{h}^{*}\|_{h}+\|{y}_{h}^{*}-{y}^{*}\|_{h}\Big).

The estimation of
\|z_{h}-{y}_{h}^{*}\|_{h}
follows in similar steps as in Theorem
4.1
. For completeness, we briefly discuss the proof.
A use of triangle inequality gives

(4.15)

\displaystyle\|z_{h}-{y}_{h}^{*}\|_{h}\leq\|z_{h}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}\|_{h}+\|E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}-{y}_{h}^{*}\|_{h}.

For
\phi=z_{h}-E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}\in W
and
\phi_{h}=I_{h}\phi\in W_{h}
, a use of coercive property of
\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
, (
3.21
) and (
4.12
) leads to

(4.16)

\displaystyle\|z_{h}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*}\|^{2}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\phi\|^{2}_{h}\lesssim\mathcal{A}(z_{h}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*},\phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(z_{h},\phi)-\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*},\phi)+\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*},\phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}y_{d}\phi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{h}(-\Delta\phi)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mu_{h}\phi\,dx-\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*},\phi)+\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*},\phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}y_{d}(\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{h}(-\Delta(\phi-\phi_{h}))\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mu_{h}(\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx

\displaystyle-\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*},\phi-\phi_{h})+\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}_{h}^{*}-E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*},\phi).

Each term of right hand side of the last equation are estimated as in Theorem
4.1
, so we omit the details.
The estimate of
\|z_{h}-{y}_{h}^{*}\|_{h}
by
\eta_{h}
is then realized by a use of (
4.15
), (
4.16
) and Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Next, we show that the error in Lagrange multipliers can be estimated in terms of
\|z_{h}-{y}^{*}\|_{h}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

There exists a positive constant
C
depending only on the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, such that

(4.17)

\displaystyle|\mu-\mu_{h}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|y^{*}-z_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(4.18)

\displaystyle|\lambda-\lambda_{h}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|y^{*}-z_{h}\|_{h}.

Proof.

Upon subtracting (
2.12
) and (
4.13
), we find

(4.19)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla(p-\hat{p}_{h})\cdot\nabla w\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(y^{*}-z_{h})w\,dx-\int_{\Omega}(\mu-\mu_{h})w\,dx\;\;\forall w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

We choose the cut-off function
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\psi\,dx=1
and
\|\psi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C
. Let
\hat{C}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}(p-\hat{p}_{h})\,dx
, we observe that
\hat{C}\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Take
w=p-\hat{p}_{h}-\hat{C}\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
in (
4.19
) to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla(p-\hat{p}_{h})\cdot\nabla(p-\hat{p}_{h}-\hat{C}\psi)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(y^{*}-z_{h})(p-\hat{p}_{h}-\hat{C}\psi)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}(\mu-\mu_{h})(p-\hat{p}_{h}-\hat{C}\psi)\,dx.

Using the fact

and
\int_{\Omega}(p-\hat{p}_{h}-\hat{C}\psi)\,dx=0
, we obtain

(4.20)

\displaystyle\|\nabla(p-\hat{p}_{h})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla(p-\hat{p}_{h})\hat{C}\psi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}(y^{*}-z_{h})(p-\hat{p}_{h}-\hat{C}\psi)\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla(p-\hat{p}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\hat{C}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|y^{*}-z_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|p-\hat{p}_{h}-\hat{C}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\hat{C}|\|\nabla(p-\hat{p}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|y^{*}-z_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\{\|p-\hat{p}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+|\hat{C}|\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|p-\hat{p}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla(p-\hat{p}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|y^{*}-z_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\{\|p-\hat{p}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle~~+\|p-\hat{p}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\},

where in obtaining the last estimate, we have used that

. An application of Poincaré inequality keeping in view the construction of

and the standard kick back argument leads to

(4.21)

\displaystyle\|\nabla(p-\hat{p}_{h})\|\lesssim\|y^{*}-z_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

In view of (
4.19
) and (
4.21
), we get the desired estimate (
4.17
). Upon subtracting (
4.12
) from (
2.7
), we get

(4.22)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\lambda-\lambda_{h})(\Delta w)\,dx=\mathcal{A}(z_{h}-{y}^{*},w)+\int_{\Omega}(\mu-\mu_{h})w\,dx\;\;\;\forall w\in W.

Then, the estimate (
4.18
) can be realized from (
4.22
) and (
4.17
).
∎

Finally, in view of lemma
4.2
and

, we have the estimation of error in Lagrange multipliers by the error estimator

.

4.2.
Local Efficiency Estimates

In this section, we derive the local efficiency estimates of a posteriori error estimator
\eta_{h}
obtained in last subsection. Therein, we use the standard bubble function techniques
[
56
]
. We have discussed the main ideas involved in proving these efficiency estimates and skipped the standard details.
For
z\in H^{4}(\Omega,\mathcal{T}_{h})
and

, we have the following integration by parts formula.

(4.23)

\displaystyle\int_{T}(\Delta^{2}z)v\,dx=\int_{T}D^{2}z:D^{2}v\,dx+\int_{\partial T}\frac{\partial\Delta z}{\partial n}v\,ds-\int_{\partial T}\frac{\partial^{2}z}{\partial n\partial t}\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}\,ds-\int_{\partial T}\frac{\partial^{2}z}{\partial n^{2}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}\,ds.

Upon summing up for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\Delta^{2}zv\,dx

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}D^{2}z:D^{2}v\,dx+\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\int_{e}\left\{\hskip-5.0pt\left\{\frac{\partial\Delta z}{\partial n}\right\}\hskip-5.0pt\right\}[[v]]\,ds

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i}}\int_{e}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial(\Delta z)}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\{\{v\}\}\,ds+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i}}\int_{e}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial^{2}z}{\partial n^{2}}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\left\{\hskip-5.0pt\left\{\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}\right\}\hskip-5.0pt\right\}\,ds

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\left\{\hskip-5.0pt\left\{\frac{\partial^{2}z}{\partial n^{2}}\right\}\hskip-5.0pt\right\}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\,ds+\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i}}\int_{e}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial^{2}z}{\partial n\partial t}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\left\{\hskip-5.0pt\left\{\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}\right\}\hskip-5.0pt\right\}\,ds

(4.24)

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\left\{\hskip-5.0pt\left\{\frac{\partial^{2}z}{\partial n\partial t}\right\}\hskip-5.0pt\right\}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\,ds.

Theorem 4.4
.

It holds that,

(4.25)

\displaystyle h_{T}^{2}\|y_{d}+\mu_{h}-{y}_{h}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{2,T}+h_{T}^{2}\|\mu-\mu_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}+\|\lambda-\lambda_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}+Osc(y_{d};T)\;\;\;\;\;\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

\displaystyle{\beta}{h_{e}^{-1/2}}\Big\|\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial{y}^{*}_{h}}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\Big\|_{L^{2}(e)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{e}}\Big(\|{y}^{*}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{2,T}+h_{T}^{2}\|\mu-\mu_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}+\|\lambda-\lambda_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\Big)

(4.26)

5.
Adaptive FEM for OCPs with integral state constraint and poinwise control constraints

This section is devoted to the a posteriori error analysis of OCPs with integral state constraint and pointwise control constraints.
We consider the following minimization problem: find
({y}^{*},{u}^{*})\in K
, such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle({y}^{*},{u}^{*})=\displaystyle argmin_{(y,u)\in{K}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|y-y_{d}\|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\|u\|^{2}\Big)

subject to the constraints

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\int_{\Omega}\nabla y\cdot\nabla w~dx=\int_{\Omega}uw~dx,\;\;\;\;\forall w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\int_{\Omega}y\,dx\geq\delta_{3},\;\;\;\;\;\\
u_{a}\leq u\leq u_{b}~~\text{a.e. in}~~\Omega,\end{cases}

where
{{K}}=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\delta_{3}
is a constant.
The functions
u_{a}
,
u_{b}
are assumed to satisfy
(i)\,u_{a},\;u_{b}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
,
(ii)\,u_{a}<u_{b}
on
\bar{\Omega}
.

As discussed in Section
1
, we then rewrite this optimization problem into a reduced minimization problem involving only the state variable. Analogously to (
2.4
), the reduced optimal control problem is to find
{y}^{*}\in{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}}
such that

(5.3)

\displaystyle{y}^{*}=argmin_{y\in{{{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}}}}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}(y,y)-(y_{d},y)\Big),

where the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}(,\cdot,)
is same as in (
2.5
) and set
{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}}
is defined as

(5.4)

\displaystyle{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}}=\{w\in W:\,\int_{\Omega}w\,dx\geq\delta_{3}\;\;\text{and}\;\;u_{a}\leq-\Delta w\leq u_{b}\;\;\text{a.e. in}\;\Omega\}.

We assume the following Slater condition:
there exists
y\in W
such that
\int_{\Omega}y\;dx>\delta_{3}
and
u_{a}\leq-\Delta y\leq u_{b}
.
Thus, the closed convex set
{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}}
is nonempty. The minimizer of (
5.3
) have the characterization in terms of the solution of the following fourth order variational inequality: find
{y}^{*}\in{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}}
satisfying

(5.5)

\mathcal{A}({y}^{*},w-{y}^{*})\geq\int_{\Omega}y_{d}(w-{y}^{*})\,dx\;\;\;\;\forall w\in{{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}}}.

The following (generalized) Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions hold (see
[
34
,
47
]
): there exist
\lambda\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\beta\int_{\Omega}(\Delta{y}^{*})(\Delta w)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{y}^{*}w\,dx=\int_{\Omega}y_{d}w\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\lambda(\Delta w)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mu w\,dx

for all
w\in W
together with the complementarity conditions

(5.7)

\displaystyle\lambda\geq 0\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\text{if}\;\;-\Delta{y}^{*}=u_{a},

(5.8)

\displaystyle\lambda\leq 0\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\text{if}\;\;-\Delta{y}^{*}=u_{b},

(5.9)

\displaystyle\lambda=0\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\text{otherwise},

(5.10)

\displaystyle\mu\geq 0\;\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\text{if}\;\;\int_{\Omega}{y}^{*}\,dx=\delta_{3},

(5.11)

\displaystyle\mu=0\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\text{if}\;\;\int_{\Omega}{y}^{*}\,dx>\delta_{3}.

The adjoint state
p\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
associated to the problem (
5.1
) -(
5.2
) is given by

(5.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla p\cdot\nabla w\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(y^{*}-y_{d})w\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\mu w\,dx\quad\forall~w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

5.1.
Discrete Problem

The discretization of (
5.3
) is to find
{y}^{*}_{h}\in{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}}_{h}
such that

(5.13)

\displaystyle{y}^{*}_{h}=argmin_{y_{h}\in{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}}_{h}}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}_{h}(y_{h},y_{h})-(y_{d},y_{h})\Big],

where

(5.14)

{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}}_{h}=\{w_{h}\in W_{h}:\int_{\Omega}w_{h}\,dx\geq\delta_{3}\;\;\text{and}\;\;Q_{h}u_{a}\leq Q_{h}(-\Delta_{h}w_{h})\leq Q_{h}u_{b}\}.

.

Owing to the property (
3.6
) and (
3.4
) of

, we have

As in the continuous case, the minimizer of (

{y}^{*}_{h}\in{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}}_{h}
such that

(5.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}^{*}_{h},w_{h}-{y}^{*}_{h})\geq(y_{d},w_{h}-{y}^{*}_{h})\;\;\;\;\;\forall w_{h}\in{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}}_{h}.

where the bilinear form

is defined in (
3.20
). It can be easily checked that the discrete problem (

The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions for the discrete problem
[

,
47
]
is given as follows: there exist

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}^{*}_{h},w_{h})=\int_{\Omega}y_{d}w_{h}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{h}(\Delta_{h}w_{h})\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mu_{h}w_{h}\,dx,\quad\forall w_{h}\in W_{h}

together with the complementary conditions

(5.17)

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}\geq 0\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\text{on}~T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}~\text{such that}\;\;Q_{T}(-\Delta{y}^{*}_{h})=Q_{T}(u_{a}),

(5.18)

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}\leq 0\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\text{on}~T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}~\text{such that}\;\;Q_{T}(-\Delta{y}^{*}_{h})=Q_{T}(u_{b}),

(5.19)

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}=0\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\text{otherwise},

(5.20)

\displaystyle\mu_{h}\geq 0\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\text{if}\;\;\int_{\Omega}{y}^{*}_{h}\,dx=\delta_{3},

(5.21)

\displaystyle\mu_{h}=0\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\;\;\text{if}\;\;\int_{\Omega}{y}^{*}_{h}\,dx>\delta_{3}.

In the following, we derive the reliability estimates of the estimator
\eta_{h}
for the error
\|{y}^{*}-{y}_{h}^{*}\|_{h}
. For this we introduce the following auxiliary problem: let
\tilde{z}_{h}\in W
be the solution of

(5.22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\tilde{z}_{h},w)=\int_{\Omega}y_{d}w\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{h}(-\Delta_{h}w)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mu_{h}w\,dx,\;\;\;\;\forall w\in W.

The well-posedness of (
5.22
) is ensured by Lax-Milgram lemma
[
21
]
.
Now, we proceed to establish the reliability of the error estimator for the error in solution
{y}^{*}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
{y}^{*}_{h}
and
\tilde{z}_{h}
be the solutions of (
5.16
) and (
5.22
), respectively. Then,

(5.23)

\displaystyle\|{y}^{*}_{h}-\tilde{z}_{h}\|_{h}\lesssim{\eta}_{h},

where
\eta_{h}
is defined in (
4.1
).

Proof.

Let
\phi=\tilde{z}_{h}-E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}\in W
and
\phi_{h}=I_{h}\phi\in W_{h}
, a use of coercive property of the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
, (
5.22
) and (
5.16
) leads to

(5.24)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\tilde{z}_{h},\phi)-\mathcal{A}_{h}(E_{h}{y}_{h}^{*},\phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}y_{d}\phi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mu_{h}\phi\,dx-\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}^{*}_{h},\phi)+\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{h}(-\Delta_{h}\phi)\,dx

\displaystyle+\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}^{*}_{h}-E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h},\phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}y_{d}(\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx-\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}^{*}_{h},\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mu_{h}(\phi-\phi_{h})\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{h}(\Delta_{h}(\phi-\phi_{h}))+\mathcal{A}_{h}({y}^{*}_{h}-E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h},\phi)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+I_{5}.

Note that,
I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}
and
I_{5}
can be estimated following same arguments as in the Theorem
4.1
.
Now, it remains to estimate
I_{4}
.
An application of the Cauchy Schwarz inequality and Lemma
3.1
, yields

\displaystyle|I_{4}|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|\int_{T}\lambda_{h}\Delta(\phi_{h}-\phi)~dx|\lesssim\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}|\lambda_{h}\|\|\Delta(\phi_{h}-\phi)\|_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}|\lambda_{h}\||\phi|_{H^{2}(T)}\lesssim{\eta}_{5}\|\phi\|_{h}.

Combining all the estimates together with (
5.24
), we get

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{h}=\|\tilde{z}_{h}-E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}\lesssim{\eta}_{h}.

An use of the triangle inequality
\|\tilde{z}_{h}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}\leq\|\tilde{z}_{h}-E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}+\|E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-{y}^{*}_{h}\|_{h}
, in view of Lemma
3.2
leads to the desired estimate.
∎

Theorem 5.3

Let
{y}^{*}
and

\displaystyle\|{y}^{*}-{y}_{h}^{*}\|_{h}

Proof.

Set
\phi={y}^{*}-E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}\in W

\displaystyle\|{y}^{*}-E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}\|^{2}_{h}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\tilde{z}_{h}-E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h},\phi)+\mathcal{A}({y}^{*},\phi)-\mathcal{A}(\tilde{z}_{h},\phi).

5.6
) and (
5.16

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}({y}^{*},\phi)-\mathcal{A}(\tilde{z}_{h},\phi)=\int_{\Omega}(\lambda-\lambda_{h})(-\Delta\phi)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}(\mu-\mu_{h})\phi\,dx.

Following the same arguments as in Theorem

Now, to estimate the term

,
we split it as

\displaystyle=\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}:Q_{T}(-\Delta y^{*}_{h})=Q_{T}(u_{b})\}.

The first term of the right hand side of equation (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{1}}(\lambda-\lambda_{h})(-\Delta\phi)\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda_{h}(\Delta\phi)\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda(\Delta\phi)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda_{h}(\Delta{y}^{*}-\Delta E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda(\Delta{y}^{*}-\Delta E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,dx.

Using

,

, relation (

) and
\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\Omega_{1}}\lambda_{h}\int_{T}(u_{a}+\Delta_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,dx=0

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda_{h}(\Delta{y}^{*}-\Delta E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda_{h}(\Delta{y}^{*}+u_{a})\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda_{h}(u_{a}+\Delta E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda_{h}(\Delta{y}^{*}+u_{a})\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda_{h}(u_{a}+\Delta_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda_{h}(\Delta_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-\Delta E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,dx

(5.30)

\displaystyle\leq 0.

In view of (

) and Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda(\Delta{y}^{*}-\Delta E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,dx=

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda(\Delta E_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}-\Delta_{h}{y}^{*}_{h})\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda(\Delta_{h}{y}^{*}_{h}+u_{a})\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda(\Delta_{h}{y}^{*}+u_{a})\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\Omega_{1}}\|\lambda\|_{L^{2}(T)}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\frac{1}{h_{e}}\Big\|\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\frac{\partial y_{h}^{*}}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\Big\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lambda(\Delta{y}^{*}_{h}+u_{a})\,dx.

),(
5.30

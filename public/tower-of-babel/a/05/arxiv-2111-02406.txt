Orbit determination with the Keplerian integrals
Giovanni Federico Gronchi

Abstract
We review two initial orbit determination methods for too short arcs (TSAs) of optical observations of a solar system body. These methods employ the conservation laws of Kepler’s problem, and allow to attempt the linkage of TSAs referring to quite far epochs, differing by even more than one orbital period of the observed object. The first method ( Link2 ) concerns the linkage of 2 TSAs, and leads to a univariate polynomial equation of degree 9. An optimal property of this polynomial is proved using Gröbner bases theory. The second method ( Link3 ) is thought for the linkage of 3 TSAs, and leads to a univariate polynomial equation of degree 8. A numerical test is shown for both algorithms.

Introduction

Modern telescopes collect a very large number of optical observations
of solar system bodies, that can be usually grouped in
very short
arcs
(VSAs), see
[
10
]
. A VSA is a set

\{(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}),\quad i=1\ldots m\},\qquad m\geq 2

of pairs of values of
right ascension
and
declination
of
the same celestial body, referring to epochs
t_{i}
, and covering a
very short path in the sky. Usually the data contained in a VSA do
not allow to compute a least squares orbit: in this case we speak of a
too short arc (TSA). Given a TSA, we can compute an
attributable

{\cal A}=(\alpha,\delta,\dot{\alpha},\dot{\delta})

at the mean epoch
\bar{t}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}t_{i}
of the observations
by a linear or quadratic fit, see
[
10
]
.
Given an
attributable, the radial distance
\rho
and the radial velocity

\dot{\rho}
of the observed body remain completely unknown. However,
given two attributables referring to the same celestial body, we can
try to put them together with the aim of computing an orbit that fits
all the data. This operation is called
linkage
in the
orbit determination literature, and it is often challenging:
an orbit produced by
linking together two TSAs usually needs a confirmation with additional
data to be considered reliable. Moreover, we cannot know
a
priori
that two TSAs refer to the same observed body, and to
perform an efficient selection of pairs of TSAs to be passed to a
linkage algorithm is a critical issue.

In this paper we review two recent initial orbit determination methods,
introduced in
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
, for the linkage of two or
three TSAs. These are called
Link2
and
Link3
, respectively. Some interesting algebraic aspects of these algorithms are
also discussed, and a numerical test is shown for both.

2.1
Kepler’s problem and its first integrals

The equation of motion of Kepler’s problem is

\ddot{{\bf r}}=-\mu\displaystyle\frac{{\bf r}}{|{\bf r}|^{3}},

(1)

where
{\bf r}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the unknown position vector and
\mu
is a
positive constant.
The dynamics defined by (
1
) has the following conserved quantities:

\displaystyle{{\bf c}={\bf r}\times\dot{{\bf r}}},\hskip 36.98866pt\mbox{\it angular momentum}

\displaystyle{{\cal E}=\frac{1}{2}|\dot{\bf r}|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{|{\bf r}|}},\hskip 17.07182pt\mbox{\it energy}

\displaystyle{{\bf L}=\frac{1}{\mu}\dot{\bf r}\times{\bf c}-\frac{{\bf r}}{|{\bf r}|}},\hskip 8.5359pt\mbox{\it Laplace-Lenz vector}.

We call these quantities the
Keplerian integrals
.
Since
{\bf c}
and
{\bf L}
have 3 components we get 7 scalar
conserved quantities: among them only 5 are independent, in fact

{\bf c}\cdot{\bf L}=0,\qquad 2|{\bf c}|^{2}{\cal E}+{\mu}^{2}(1-|{\bf L}|^{2})=0.

Given an attributable
{\cal A}
at the epoch
\bar{t}
, we write below the
Keplerian integrals as functions of the unknown radial distance and
velocity
\rho,\dot{\rho}
.
We start by writing

\begin{split}&{\bf r}={\bf q}+\rho\,{\bf e}^{\rho},\cr&\dot{\bf r}=\dot{\bf q}+\dot{\rho}\,{\bf e}^{\rho}+\rho(\dot{\alpha}\cos\delta{\bf e}^{\alpha}+\dot{\delta}{\bf e}^{\delta}),\end{split}

where
{\bf q}
,
\dot{\bf q}
are the position and velocity of the observer at
time
\bar{t}
,

{\bf e}^{\rho}=(\cos\delta\cos\alpha,\cos\delta\sin\alpha,\sin\delta)

gives the line of sight, and

{\bf e}^{\alpha}=(\cos\delta)^{-1}\frac{\partial{\bf e}^{\rho}}{\partial\alpha},\qquad{\bf e}^{\delta}=\frac{\partial{\bf e}^{\rho}}{\partial\delta}.

The angular momentum vector can be expressed as

{\bf c}(\rho,\dot{\rho})={\bf r}\times\dot{{\bf r}}={\bf D}\dot{\rho}+{\bf E}\rho^{2}+{\bf F}\rho+{\bf G}.

The vectors
{\bf D},{\bf E},{\bf F},{\bf G}
depend only on the attributable
{\cal A}

and on
{\bf q},\dot{\bf q}
:

\begin{array}[]{l}{\bf D}={\bf q}\times{\bf e}^{\rho},\cr{\bf E}=\dot{\alpha}\cos\delta{\bf e}^{\rho}\times{\bf e}^{\alpha}+\dot{\delta}{\bf e}^{\rho}\times{\bf e}^{\delta}=\dot{\alpha}\cos\delta{\bf e}^{\delta}-\dot{\delta}{\bf e}^{\alpha},\cr{\bf F}=\dot{\alpha}\cos\delta{\bf q}\times{\bf e}^{\alpha}+\dot{\delta}{\bf q}\times{\bf e}^{\delta}+{\bf e}^{\rho}\times\dot{\bf q},\cr{\bf G}={\bf q}\times\dot{\bf q}.\cr\end{array}

(2)

The energy can be written as

{\cal E}=\frac{1}{2}|\dot{\bf r}|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{|{\bf r}|},

where

|{\bf r}|=\sqrt{\rho^{2}+2({\bf q}\cdot{\bf e}^{\rho})\rho+|{\bf q}|^{2}},

and

|\dot{\bf r}|^{2}=\dot{\rho}^{2}+(\dot{\alpha}^{2}\cos^{2}\delta+\dot{\delta}^{2})\rho^{2}+2\dot{\bf q}\cdot{\bf e}^{\rho}\dot{\rho}+2\dot{\bf q}\cdot(\dot{\alpha}\cos\delta{\bf e}^{\alpha}+\dot{\delta}{\bf e}^{\delta})\rho+|\dot{\bf q}|^{2}.

Finally, the Laplace-Lenz vector
{\bf L}
is given by

\mu{\bf L}(\rho,\dot{\rho})=\Bigl(|\dot{\bf r}|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{|{\bf r}|}\Bigr){\bf r}-(\dot{\bf r}\cdot{\bf r})\dot{\bf r},

where

\dot{\bf r}\cdot{\bf r}=\rho\dot{\rho}+{\bf q}\cdot{\bf e}^{\rho}\dot{\rho}+(\dot{\bf q}\cdot{\bf e}^{\rho}+{\bf q}\cdot{\bf e}^{\alpha}\dot{\alpha}\cos\delta+{\bf q}\cdot{\bf e}^{\delta}\dot{\delta})\rho+\dot{\bf q}\cdot{\bf q}.

Remark 2
.

The expressions of
{\cal E}
and
{\bf L}
are algebraic but not
polynomial, due to the presence of the term
\mu/|{\bf r}|
. If we
consider the auxiliary variable
z
defined by relation

{z|{\bf r}|=\mu,}

(3)

then the Keplerian integrals can be viewed as polynomials in the
variables
\rho,\dot{\rho},z
by writing
z
in place of
\mu/|{\bf r}|
.
In this way, we obtain

\tilde{\cal E}=\frac{1}{2}|\dot{\bf r}|^{2}-z,\hskip 28.45274pt\mu\tilde{\bf L}=(|\dot{\bf r}|^{2}-z){\bf r}-(\dot{\bf r}\cdot{\bf r})\dot{\bf r}.

The relation between
\rho
and
z
can be taken into account through
the polynomial equation

|{\bf r}|^{2}z^{2}=\mu^{2}.

(4)

Moreover, the following relations hold:

{\bf c}\cdot\tilde{{\bf L}}=0,\hskip 28.45274pt2|{\bf c}|^{2}\tilde{{\cal E}}+\mu^{2}(1-|\tilde{{\bf L}}|^{2})=0.

3.2
Selecting the solutions

Given
{\bf A}=({\cal A}_{1},{\cal A}_{2})
with
covariance matrix

{\Gamma_{\bf A}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\Gamma_{{\cal A}_{1}}&0\cr 0&\Gamma_{{\cal A}_{2}}\cr\end{array}\right],}

let

{{\bf R}={\bf R}({\bf A})=(\mathcal{R}_{1}({\bf A}),\mathcal{R}_{2}({\bf A})),\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{R}_{i}=(\rho_{i},\dot{\rho}_{i}),\ \ i=1,2}

be a solution of

{\bm{\Phi}({\bf R};{\bf A})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\bf c}_{1}-{\bf c}_{2}\cr\bm{\xi}\cdot{\bf e}^{\rho}_{1}\cr\end{array}\right)={\bf 0},}

(42)

where
\bm{\xi}
is defined in (
13
), and can also be written as

\bm{\xi}=[\mu({\bf L}_{1}-{\bf L}_{2})-({\cal E}_{1}-{\cal E}_{2}){\bf r}_{1}]\times({\bf r}_{1}-{\bf r}_{2}).

(43)

If both
({\cal A}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{1}({\bf A}))
,

({\cal A}_{2},\mathcal{R}_{2}({\bf A}))
give bounded orbits at epochs

\tilde{t}_{i}=\tilde{t}_{i}({\bf A})=\bar{t}_{i}-\frac{\rho_{i}({\bf A})}{c},\qquad i=1,2,

(44)

where aberration of light with velocity
c
is taken into account, then we can compute the corresponding Keplerian elements.
We introduce the vector

{\bm{\Delta}_{a,\ell}=(\Delta a,\Delta\ell),}

representing the difference in semimajor axis and mean anomaly
of the two orbits, comparing the anomalies at the same time
\tilde{t}_{1}
:

\Delta a=a_{1}-a_{2},\hskip 14.22636pt\Delta\ell=\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-n(a_{2})(\tilde{t}_{1}-\tilde{t}_{2}),

where

n(a)=\sqrt{\mu}a^{-3/2}
is the mean motion.
We consider the map

{({\cal A}_{1},{\cal A}_{2})={\bf A}\mapsto\bm{\Psi}({\bf A})=\left({\cal A}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{1},\bm{\Delta}_{a,\ell}\right),}

giving the orbit
({\cal A}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{1}({\bf A}))
in attributable
coordinates at epoch
\tilde{t}_{1}
, together with the vector

\bm{\Delta}_{a,\ell}({\bf A})
.

We map the covariance matrix
\Gamma_{{\bf A}}
of
{\bf A}
into the covariance matrix of

\bm{\Psi}({\bf A})
by

{\Gamma_{\bm{\Psi}({\bf A})}=\frac{\partial\bm{\Psi}}{\partial{\bf A}}\;\Gamma_{\bf A}\;\left[\frac{\partial\bm{\Psi}}{\partial{\bf A}}\right]^{T}.}

We can consider different ways to select the solutions. Two of them are the following.

3.2.1
Compatibility conditions.

We check whether the considered solution of (
42
) fulfills
the relation

{\bm{\Delta}_{a,\ell}={\bf 0}}

within a thresold defined by
\Gamma_{\bf A}
.
More precisely, consider the marginal covariance matrix

\Gamma_{\bm{\Delta}_{a,\ell}}=\frac{\partial\bm{\Delta}_{a,\ell}}{\partial{\bf A}}\Gamma_{{\bf A}}\left[\frac{\partial\bm{\Delta}_{a,\ell}}{\partial{\bf A}}\right]^{T}

of the vector
\bm{\Delta}_{a,\ell}
.
The inverse matrix

{C^{\bm{\Delta}_{a,\ell}}=\Gamma^{-1}_{\bm{\Delta}_{a,\ell}}}

defines a norm
\|\cdot\|_{\star}

allowing to test the identification of
{\cal A}_{1},{\cal A}_{2}
:

{\|\bm{\Delta}_{a,\ell}\|_{\star}^{2}=\bm{\Delta}_{a,\ell}C^{\bm{\Delta}_{a,\ell}}\bm{\Delta}_{a,\ell}^{T}\leq\chi_{max}^{2},}

where
\chi_{max}
is a control parameter,
that needs to be seleceted on the basis of
simulations and practical tests with real data.

The orbits computed with the method of Section
2
are
such that

I_{1}=I_{2},\qquad\Omega_{1}=\Omega_{2},\qquad a_{1}(1-e_{1}^{2})=a_{2}(1-e_{2}^{2})

(45)

because they fulfill
{\bf c}_{1}={\bf c}_{2}
.
Assuming
a_{1}=a_{2}
we get
e_{1}=e_{2}
from the third relation in (
45
).
Since
a_{1}=a_{2}
corresponds to
{\cal E}_{1}={\cal E}_{2}
, from
\bm{\xi}={\bf 0}
we also obtain

\mu({\bf L}_{1}-{\bf L}_{2})\times({\bf r}_{1}-{\bf r}_{2})={\bf 0}.

(46)

The vectors
{\bf L}_{1},{\bf L}_{2}
have the same size because
e_{1}=e_{2}
. Since it is
quite unlikely that these vector differences are parallel,
generically relation (
46
) implies

\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}.

3.2.2
Attribution.

We can try to attribute the data of
{\cal A}_{2}
to each considered
solution
\mathbf{x}_{1}=({\cal A}_{1},{\cal R}_{1}({\cal A}))
of (
42
), which has

\Gamma_{{\cal A}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{1}}=\Gamma_{{\cal A}_{1}}\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}_{1}}{\partial{\cal A}_{1}}\right]^{T},\hskip 14.22636pt\Gamma_{\mathcal{R}_{1},{\cal A}_{1}}=\Gamma_{{\cal A}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{1}}^{T}.

We recall here the attribution algorithm.
Assume that we have

Assume that

define the prediction function

where
\Phi_{t_{0}}^{t}(\mathbf{x})
is the integral flow of the Kepler problem.
The covariance and normal matrices of
{\cal A}

\Gamma_{\cal A}=\left[\frac{\partial F}{\partial\mathbf{x}}\right]\Gamma_{\mathbf{x}}\left[\frac{\partial F}{\partial\mathbf{x}}\right]^{T},\qquad C_{\cal A}=\Gamma_{\cal A}^{-1},

\begin{split}C_{0}&=C_{2}+C_{p},\qquad\Gamma_{0}=C_{0}^{-1},\\

The values of the attribution penalty
K_{4}/m

, is
used to filter out the pairs orbit-attributable which cannot belong to

Joining three TSAs

Given three TSAs with attributables
{\cal A}_{1},{\cal A}_{2},{\cal A}_{3}
at mean epochs
\bar{t}_{1},\bar{t}_{2},\bar{t}_{3}
, setting the
conservation of angular momentum is enough to obtain a finite number
of orbits. We review the following result, presented in

[
5
]
. Here the subscripts in
{\bf c}_{i},\rho_{i},\dot{\rho}_{i},{\bf D}_{i},{\bf E}_{i},{\bf F}_{i},{\bf G}_{i}
refer to the three epochs.

Proposition 1
.

Assume

{\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2}\cdot{\bf D}_{3}\neq 0.

(47)

Then the polynomial system

\displaystyle({\bf c}_{1}-{\bf c}_{2})\cdot{\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(48a)

\displaystyle({\bf c}_{1}-{\bf c}_{2})\cdot{\bf D}_{1}\times({\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(48b)

\displaystyle({\bf c}_{2}-{\bf c}_{3})\cdot{\bf D}_{2}\times{\bf D}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(48c)

\displaystyle({\bf c}_{2}-{\bf c}_{3})\cdot{\bf D}_{2}\times({\bf D}_{2}\times{\bf D}_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(48d)

\displaystyle({\bf c}_{3}-{\bf c}_{1})\cdot{\bf D}_{3}\times{\bf D}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(48e)

\displaystyle({\bf c}_{3}-{\bf c}_{1})\cdot{\bf D}_{3}\times({\bf D}_{3}\times{\bf D}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

(48f)

in the 6 unknowns

\rho_{1},\dot{\rho}_{1},\rho_{2},\dot{\rho}_{2},\rho_{3},\dot{\rho}_{3}

is equivalent to the redundant system

{\bf c}_{1}={\bf c}_{2},\qquad{\bf c}_{2}={\bf c}_{3},\qquad{\bf c}_{3}={\bf c}_{1}.

(49)

Proof.

System (
49
) trivially implies
(
48
). Assume now that system (
48
)
holds. Using relations (
48e
), (
48f
), to prove
that
{\bf c}_{3}={\bf c}_{1}
we only need to show that

({\bf c}_{3}-{\bf c}_{1})\cdot{\bf v}=0

(50)

for some vector
{\bf v}
that does not belong to the linear space generated by

{\bf D}_{3}\times{\bf D}_{1}
and
{\bf D}_{3}\times({\bf D}_{3}\times{\bf D}_{1})
.
Indeed we show that we can choose

{\bf v}={\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2}.

Note that

({\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2})\cdot({\bf D}_{2}\times{\bf D}_{3})\times\bigl({\bf D}_{2}\times({\bf D}_{2}\times{\bf D}_{3})\bigr)=0,

that is, the vector
{\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2}
belongs to the
linear space generated by
{\bf D}_{2}\times{\bf D}_{3}
and

{\bf D}_{2}\times({\bf D}_{2}\times{\bf D}_{3})

Moreover,
{\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2}
is not generated by
{\bf D}_{3}\times{\bf D}_{1}
and

{\bf D}_{3}\times({\bf D}_{3}\times{\bf D}_{1})
, in fact by (

({\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2})\cdot({\bf D}_{3}\times{\bf D}_{1})\times\bigl({\bf D}_{3}\times({\bf D}_{3}\times{\bf D}_{1})\bigr)=|{\bf D}_{3}\times{\bf D}_{1}|^{2}{\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2}\cdot{\bf D}_{3}\neq 0.

Setting

{\bf v}={\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2},

from (

), (

), (

,
which yield (

) and therefore we obtain

{\bf c}_{3}={\bf c}_{1}
. In a similar way we can prove that

,
{\bf c}_{2}={\bf c}_{3}
, provided that system
(

Equations (

can be written as

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{1}\dot{\rho}_{1}-{\bf D}_{2}\dot{\rho}_{2}={\bf J}_{12}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2}),\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{2}\dot{\rho}_{2}-{\bf D}_{3}\dot{\rho}_{3}={\bf J}_{23}(\rho_{2},\rho_{3}),\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{3}\dot{\rho}_{3}-{\bf D}_{1}\dot{\rho}_{1}={\bf J}_{31}(\rho_{3},\rho_{1}),

where

\displaystyle{\bf J}_{12}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf E}_{2}\rho_{2}^{2}-{\bf E}_{1}\rho_{1}^{2}+{\bf F}_{2}\rho_{2}-{\bf F}_{1}\rho_{1}+{\bf G}_{2}-{\bf G}_{1},

\displaystyle{\bf J}_{23}(\rho_{2},\rho_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf E}_{3}\rho_{3}^{2}-{\bf E}_{2}\rho_{2}^{2}+{\bf F}_{3}\rho_{3}-{\bf F}_{2}\rho_{2}+{\bf G}_{3}-{\bf G}_{2},

\displaystyle{\bf J}_{31}(\rho_{3},\rho_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf E}_{1}\rho_{1}^{2}-{\bf E}_{3}\rho_{3}^{2}+{\bf F}_{1}\rho_{1}-{\bf F}_{3}\rho_{3}+{\bf G}_{1}-{\bf G}_{3}.

), (

depend only on the radial distances. In fact, they correspond to the system

{{\bf J}_{12}\cdot{\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2}=0,\quad{\bf J}_{23}\cdot{\bf D}_{2}\times{\bf D}_{3}=0,\quad{\bf J}_{31}\cdot{\bf D}_{3}\times{\bf D}_{1}=0,}

(51)

which can be written as

\displaystyle q_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{3}\rho_{2}^{2}+b_{3}\rho_{1}^{2}+c_{3}\rho_{2}+d_{3}\rho_{1}+e_{3}=0,

(52)

\displaystyle q_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{1}\rho_{3}^{2}+b_{1}\rho_{2}^{2}+c_{1}\rho_{3}+d_{1}\rho_{2}+e_{1}=0,

(53)

\displaystyle q_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{2}\rho_{1}^{2}+b_{2}\rho_{3}^{2}+c_{2}\rho_{1}+d_{2}\rho_{3}+e_{2}=0,

(54)

where

\displaystyle a_{3}={\bf E}_{2}\cdot{\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2},\qquad b_{3}=-{\bf E}_{1}\cdot{\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2},

\displaystyle c_{3}={\bf F}_{2}\cdot{\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2},\qquad d_{3}=-{\bf F}_{1}\cdot{\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2},

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pte_{3}=({\bf G}_{2}-{\bf G}_{1})\cdot{\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2},

and the other coefficients

of
\rho_{3}

\rho_{1}

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{2}

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{3}

4.1
Straight line solutions

A particular solution of system (
49
) can be obtained by
searching for values of
\rho_{j},\dot{\rho}_{j}
such that

{{\bf c}_{j}(\rho_{j},\dot{\rho}_{j})={\bf 0},\hskip 28.45274ptj=1,2,3.}

Let us drop the index
j
.
Relation
{\bf r}\times\dot{\bf r}={\bf 0}
implies that there exists

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

{\dot{\rho}{\bf e}^{\rho}+\rho\bm{\eta}+\dot{\bf q}=\lambda(\rho{\bf e}^{\rho}+{\bf q}),}

(55)

with
\bm{\eta}=\dot{\alpha}\cos\delta{\bf e}^{\alpha}+\dot{\delta}{\bf e}^{\delta}
.
Setting
\sigma=\dot{\rho}-\lambda\rho
we can write (
55
) as

{\sigma{\bf e}^{\rho}+\rho\bm{\eta}-\lambda{\bf q}=-\dot{\bf q}.}

(56)

We introduce the vector

{{\bf u}={\bf q}-({\bf q}\cdot{\bf e}^{\rho}){\bf e}^{\rho}-\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}({\bf q}\cdot\bm{\eta})\bm{\eta},}

which is orthogonal to both
{\bf e}^{\rho}
and
\bm{\eta}
, where
\eta=|\bm{\eta}|
.

Thus, we can write (
56
) as

{[\sigma-\lambda({\bf q}\cdot{\bf e}^{\rho})]{\bf e}^{\rho}+\Bigl[\rho-\frac{\lambda}{\eta^{2}}({\bf q}\cdot\bm{\eta})\Bigr]\bm{\eta}-\lambda{\bf u}=-\dot{\bf q}.}

Since
\{{\bf e}^{\rho},\bm{\eta},{\bf u}\}
is generically an orthogonal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we
find

{\lambda=\frac{1}{|{\bf u}|^{2}}(\dot{\bf q}\cdot{\bf u}),\qquad\rho=\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}(\lambda{\bf q}-\dot{\bf q})\cdot\bm{\eta},\qquad\dot{\rho}=\lambda\rho+(\lambda{\bf q}-\dot{\bf q})\cdot{\bf e}^{\rho}.}

In particular, we obtain the value

{\rho=\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{|{\bf u}|^{2}}(\dot{\bf q}\cdot{\bf u})({\bf q}\cdot\bm{\eta})-\dot{\bf q}\cdot\bm{\eta}\Bigr)}

for the radial distance, corresponding to a solution with zero angular
momentum.

4.2
Selecting the solutions

Given
{\bf A}=({\cal A}_{1},{\cal A}_{2},{\cal A}_{3})
with
covariance matrices
\Gamma_{{\cal A}_{1}},\Gamma_{{\cal A}_{2}},\Gamma_{{\cal A}_{3}}
, let

{\bf R}={\bf R}({\bf A})=\bigl({\cal R}_{1}({\bf A}),{\cal R}_{2}({\bf A}),{\cal R}_{3}({\bf A})\bigr),\qquad{\cal R}_{i}=(\rho_{i},\dot{\rho}_{i}),\quad i=1,2,3

be a solution of

\bm{\Phi}({\bf R};{\bf A})={\bf 0},

(57)

with

\bm{\Phi}({\bf R};{\bf A})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}({\bf c}_{1}-{\bf c}_{2})\cdot{\bf D}_{1}\times({\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2})\cr({\bf c}_{1}-{\bf c}_{2})\cdot{\bf D}_{1}\times{\bf D}_{2}\cr({\bf c}_{2}-{\bf c}_{3})\cdot{\bf D}_{2}\times({\bf D}_{2}\times{\bf D}_{3})\cr({\bf c}_{2}-{\bf c}_{3})\cdot{\bf D}_{2}\times{\bf D}_{3}\cr({\bf c}_{3}-{\bf c}_{1})\cdot{\bf D}_{3}\times({\bf D}_{3}\times{\bf D}_{1})\cr({\bf c}_{3}-{\bf c}_{1})\cdot{\bf D}_{3}\times{\bf D}_{1}\cr\end{array}\right).

If
({\cal A}_{1},{\cal R}_{1}({\cal A}))
,
({\cal A}_{2},{\cal R}_{2}({\cal A}))
, and

({\cal A}_{3},{\cal R}_{3}({\cal A}))
give bounded orbits at epochs

\tilde{t}_{i}=\bar{t}_{i}-\frac{\rho_{i}({\cal A})}{c},\qquad i=1,2,3,

then we compute the corresponding Keplerian elements.
We introduce the difference vectors

\displaystyle\bm{\Delta}_{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigl(a_{1}-a_{2},\omega_{1}-\omega_{2},\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-n(a_{2})(\tilde{t}_{1}-\tilde{t}_{2})\bigr),

\displaystyle\bm{\Delta}_{32}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigl(a_{3}-a_{2},\omega_{3}-\omega_{2},\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}-n(a_{2})(\tilde{t}_{3}-\tilde{t}_{2})\bigr),

where

n(a)=\sqrt{\mu}a^{-3/2}
is the mean motion.
We consider map

({\cal A}_{1},{\cal A}_{2},{\cal A}_{3})={\bf A}\mapsto\bm{\Psi}({\bf A})=\left({\cal A}_{2},\mathcal{R}_{2},\bm{\Delta}_{12},\bm{\Delta}_{32}\right),

giving the orbit
({\cal A}_{2},\mathcal{R}_{2})
in attributable coordinates at epoch

\tilde{t}_{2}
together with the vectors
\bm{\Delta}_{12}
,

\bm{\Delta}_{32}
, which are not constrained by the angular momentum
integrals.

We map the covariance matrix

\Gamma_{{\bf A}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Gamma_{{\cal A}_{1}}&0&0\cr 0&\Gamma_{{\cal A}_{1}}&0\cr 0&0&\Gamma_{{\cal A}_{1}}\cr\end{array}\right]

of
{\bf A}
into the covariance matrix of

\bm{\Psi}({\bf A})
by the covariance propagation rule:

\Gamma_{\bm{\Psi}({\bf A})}=\frac{\partial\bm{\Psi}}{\partial{\bf A}}\;\Gamma_{\bf A}\;\left[\frac{\partial\bm{\Psi}}{\partial{\bf A}}\right]^{T},

We can check whether the considered solution of (
57
)
fulfills the
compatibility conditions

\bm{\Delta}_{12}=\bm{\Delta}_{32}={\bf 0}

within a threshold defined by
\Gamma_{\cal A}
.
More precisely, consider the marginal covariance matrix

\Gamma_{\bm{\Delta}}

of the vector

\bm{\Delta}=(\bm{\Delta}_{12},\bm{\Delta}_{32}).

The inverse matrix
C^{\bm{\Delta}}=\Gamma^{-1}_{\bm{\Delta}}
defines a norm

\|\cdot\|_{\star}

allowing us to test an
identification between the attributables
{\cal A}_{1},{\cal A}_{2},{\cal A}_{3}
: we check
whether

\|\bm{\Delta}\|_{\star}^{2}=\bm{\Delta}C^{\bm{\Delta}}\bm{\Delta}^{T}\leq\chi_{max}^{2},

(58)

where
\chi_{max}
is a control parameter.

5
Conclusions and future work

We reviewed two initial orbit determination methods for TSAs of
optical observations employing the conservation laws of Kepler’s
problem. Some algebraic properties of these algorithms have also been
discussed and a simple test case has been presented for both.
Being based on conservation laws, these methods are suitable to link
TSAs quite far apart in time, even differing by more than one orbital
period of the observed body. Moreover, these algorithms are very
fast, because they are based on a polynomial formulation with low
degree (9 for
Link2
, 8 for
Link3
). The sensitivity of
these algorithms to astrometric errors is an important feature to be
investigated: in fact it seems that some orbital elements are more
sensitive to these errors. Moreover, it would be important to find
efficient filters to discard
a priori
pairs of TSAs that are not
likely to belong to the same observed object. Indeed, even if some
filters have been proposed in
[
7
]
,
[
4
]
, a
satisfactory solution to this problem is still missing. The mentioned
problems are currently under investigation.

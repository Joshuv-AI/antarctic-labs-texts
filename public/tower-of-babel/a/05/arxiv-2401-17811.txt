Introduction

Stefan problem aims to describe the evolution of the boundary between two phases of a material undergoing a phase change, for example the melting of a solid, such as ice to water. There has been a long history since the problems were posed. Readers can refer to the monographs
[
35
,
53
]
for furhter physical backgrounds and histories of the early mathematical studies. In this paper we consider the classical three dimensional one-phase Stefan problem on an external domain which describes the melting of a ball of ice surrounded by water. The exterior region is denoted by
\Omega(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
u(t,x)
denotes the temperture of the water, which is assumed to be zero at the ice phase
\partial\Omega(t)
. The temperature of the ice is supposed to be maintained at zero. Thus the temperature function
u:\Omega(t)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
evolves as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u-\Delta{u}=0\quad in\ \Omega(t),\\
&\frac{\partial{u}}{\partial\vec{n}}=V_{\partial\Omega(t)}\quad on\ \partial\Omega(t),\\
&u=0\quad on\ \partial\Omega(t),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
V_{\partial\Omega(t)}
stands for the normal velocity of the moving boundary
\partial\Omega(t)

From now on, we restrict our discussion to the radially symmetric case since in this case the Cauchy theory is simpler. The domain
\Omega(t)
is then given by

\Omega(t)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}|\ |x|\geq\lambda(t)\},

and equation (

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u-\partial_{rr}u-\frac{2}{r}\partial_{r}u=0\quad in\ \Omega(t),\\
&\partial_{r}u(t,\lambda(t))=-\dot{\lambda}(t),\\
&u(t,\lambda(t))=0,\\
&u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},\quad\lambda(0)=\lambda_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

As was established in

(u_{0},\lambda_{0})\in\ H^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}

•

Scaling invariance: If

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(|x|\geq\lambda(t))}+\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(|x|\geq\lambda(t))}=0,

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(|x|\geq\lambda(t))}+\lVert\Delta u\rVert^{2}_{(L^{2}(|x|\geq\lambda(t)))}=2\pi(\lambda(t))^{2}(\dot{\lambda}(t))^{3},

As for the long time behavior for the solutions to the Stefan problem (

&(T-t)^{\frac{k+1}{2}},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.\end{aligned}\right.

In this paper, we are devoted to the description of finite time melting for the classical Stefan problem in the radial case in 3D. We prove the existence of stable and unstable type II finite time melting solutions and then study the singularity formation process.

Theorem 1.1

(Melting dynamics)
.

There exists a set of initial data
(u_{0},\lambda_{0})
in
H^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
, such that the corresponding solution
(u,\lambda)\in C(\left[0,T\right);H^{2})\times C^{1}([0,T);\mathbb{R}_{+})
to the exterior Stefan problem (
1.2
) melts in finite time
0<T(u_{0},\lambda_{0})<+\infty
with the following dynamics:

•

Stable regime: the fundamental melting rate is given by

\lambda(t)=4\sqrt{\pi}\frac{\sqrt{T-t}}{|\log(T-t)|}(1+o_{t\rightarrow T}(1)),

(1.3)

and is stable by small radial perturbations in
H^{2}
.

•

Excited regimes: the excited melting rates are given by

\lambda(t)=c(u_{0},k)(1+o_{t\rightarrow T}(1))(T-t)^{\frac{k+1}{2}},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}

(1.4)

for some fixed constant
c(u_{0},k)>0
and it corresponds to the initial data lying on a locally Lipschitz
H^{2}
manifold of codimension
k
.

•

Non-concentration of energy: In all cases, there exists
u^{*}\in\dot{H}^{1}
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\lVert\nabla u\chi_{\{|x|\geq\lambda(t)\}}-\nabla u^{*}\rVert_{{L^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=0.

(1.5)

In this section we compute the first

\forall u\in{H}^{1}_{\rho,0}
with

\langle u,P_{j}\rangle_{0}=0,\quad 0\leq{j}\leq{k},

we have

\Arrowvert\partial_{z}u\Arrowvert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,0}}\geq(2k+2)\Arrowvert{u}\Arrowvert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,0}}.

(2.7)

2.2.
Almost invertibility of the renormalised operator

We consider in this section the renormalised operator

H_{b}=-\Delta+\Lambda\quad{\mathrm{with\ the\ boundary\ condition\ }}u(\sqrt{b})=0

in the radial case where
0<b<b^{*}
small enough. Thanks to the boundary condition
u(\sqrt{b})=0
and (
2.1
),
H_{b}
is self-adjoint on the Hilbert space
L^{2}_{\rho,b}
given by (
1.7
). We will show the invertiliblity property of
H_{b}
simliar to that has been done in
[
17
]
which serves as the starting point of the Lyapunov-Schmidt argument.

Fix a frequency size
K\in\mathbb{N}
and a sufficiently small parameter
b^{*}(K)
such that

0<b<b^{*}(K)\ll{1}.

Define the Gram matrix

M_{b,k}=(\langle P_{i},P_{j}\rangle_{b})_{0\leq{i,j}\leq{k}},\quad{0}\leq k\leq{K}.

(2.8)

Since (
2.4
) implies

\langle P_{i},P_{j}\rangle_{b}=\langle P_{i},P_{j}\rangle_{0}-\int_{0}^{\sqrt{b}}P_{i}P_{j}y^{2}e^{-y^{2}/2}dy=\delta_{ij}+O(b^{\frac{3}{2}}),

one has

M_{b,k}=Id+O(b^{\frac{3}{2}}),

(2.9)

which is invertible for
0\leq{b}<b^{*}
small enough. Then for the vector
\vec{P_{k}}(z)=(P_{j}(z))_{0\leq{j}\leq{k}}
, we introduce the function

m_{k}(b,z)=\langle M_{b,k}^{-1}\vec{P_{k}}(\sqrt{b}),\vec{P_{k}}(z)\rangle.

(2.10)

As a consequence of (
2.2
), (
2.5
), (
2.9
),

m_{k}(b,z)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(C_{j}+O(b))P_{j}(z).

Lemma 2.1

(Near inversion of
H_{b}-2k
)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<b<b^{*}(k)
small enough. Then for all
f\in{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}
with

\langle f,P_{j}\rangle_{b}=0,\quad 0\leq{j}\leq{k},

there exists a unique solution
u\in{H^{1}_{\rho,b}}
to the equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tilde{H}_{b,k}u=f,\\
&\langle u,P_{j}\rangle_{b}=0,\quad\forall\ 0\leq{j}\leq{k},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.11)

where

\quad\tilde{H}_{b,k}u=(H_{b}-2k)u-be^{-\frac{b}{2}}m_{k}(b,z)\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b}).

Moreover, the following estimates hold

\displaystyle\Arrowvert{u}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}

\displaystyle+\Arrowvert\partial_{z}u\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\Arrowvert\Delta{u}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\Arrowvert\Lambda{u}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}

(2.12)

\displaystyle+\Arrowvert zu\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\Arrowvert z^{2}u\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\lvert\sqrt{b}\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b})\rvert\lesssim\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}.

Before giving the proof of lemma
2.1
, we firstly recall a weighted Poincaré-type inequality which will be used frequently in the following proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
u
be a radial function on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
u\in{H^{1}_{\rho,0}}
, then we have

\int_{0}^{+\infty}(zu)^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz\lesssim\int_{0}^{+\infty}u^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz+\int_{0}^{+\infty}(\partial_{z}u)^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz.

Proof.

Integrating by parts and note that
\partial_{z}\rho=-z\rho
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}(zu)^{2}\rho z^{2}dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}z^{3}u^{2}d(-\rho)=3\int_{0}^{+\infty}u^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz+2\int_{0}^{+\infty}z^{3}\partial_{z}u{u}\rho dz

\displaystyle\leq 3\int_{0}^{+\infty}u^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(zu)^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz+2\int_{0}^{+\infty}(\partial_{z}u)^{2}\rho z^{2}dz.

Hence,

\int_{0}^{+\infty}(zu)^{2}\rho z^{2}dz\leq 6\int_{0}^{+\infty}u^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz+4\int_{0}^{+\infty}(\partial_{z}u)^{2}\rho z^{2}dz.

∎

Now we come back to the proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1. The existence and uniqueness.
We will show the existence and uniqueness of (
2.11
) in
H^{1}_{\rho,b}
via a standard Lax-Milgram argument. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the energy functional
\mathcal{F}:\ H^{1}_{\rho,b}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
where

\mathcal{F}(u)=\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}|\partial_{z}u|^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz-2k\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}u^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz-\langle f,u\rangle_{b},

over the constraint set

\mathcal{C}=\{u\in H^{1}_{\rho,b}|\ \langle u,P_{j}\rangle_{b}=0,\quad\forall\ 0\leq{j}\leq{k}\}.

Let

I_{b}=\inf\limits_{u\in{\mathcal{C}}}\mathcal{F}(u).

Note that the spectral gap estimate (
2.7
) and Lemma
2.2
imply that for all
{v}\in{H}^{1}_{\rho,0}
with
\langle v,P_{j}\rangle={0},\ \forall\ 0\leq{j}\leq{k}
, we have

\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\partial_{z}v|^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz-2k\int_{0}^{+\infty}{v}^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz\gtrsim\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1+|z|^{2})|v|^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz.

(2.13)

Applying this to
v(z)=u(z)\textbf{1}_{z\geq\sqrt{b}}\in H^{1}_{\rho,0}
, we conclude that
I_{b}>-\infty
by Cauchy inequality, and any minimizing sequence
u_{n}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}_{\rho,b}
. Therefore, up to a subsequence and due to the compactness of the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}_{\rho,b}\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow\ L^{2}_{\rho,b}
, there exists
u\in{H}^{1}_{\rho,b}
, such that

u_{n}\rightharpoonup{u}\quad in\ H^{1}_{\rho,b},\ \ \mathrm{and}\ u_{n}\rightarrow{u}\quad in\ L^{2}_{\rho,b}.

In particular, since
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\subset{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
, we obtain

u(\sqrt{b})=0,\quad\langle u,P_{j}\rangle_{b}=0\quad\forall\ 0\leq{j}\leq{k}.

(2.14)

Thus
u
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
over
\mathcal{C}
. By Lagrange multiplier theorem, there exists
\lambda_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(H_{b}-2k)u=f-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\lambda_{j}P_{j}.

(2.15)

Standard regularity theory of elliptic equation implies that
u\in H_{loc}^{2}(z\geq\sqrt{b})
. Then taking inner product with
P_{j}
on both sides of (
2.15
), we have

\langle H_{b},P_{j}\rangle_{b}=\langle f,P_{j}\rangle_{b}-\sum_{i=0}^{k}\lambda_{i}\langle P_{i},P_{j}\rangle_{b}.

(2.16)

Direct computation gives

\displaystyle\langle H_{b}u,P_{j}\rangle_{b}=

\displaystyle\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}\left(-\frac{1}{\rho{z}^{2}}\partial_{z}(\rho{z}^{2}\partial_{z}{u})P_{j}\rho{z}^{2}\right)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\rho{z}^{2}\partial_{z}uP_{j}\bigg|^{+\infty}_{\sqrt{b}}+\int^{+\infty}_{\sqrt{b}}\rho{z}^{2}\partial_{z}u\partial_{z}P_{j}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\sqrt{b})b\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b})P_{j}(\sqrt{b})+\langle u,H_{b}P_{j}\rangle_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{b}{2}}b\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b})P_{j}(\sqrt{b})+2j\langle u,P_{j}\rangle_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{b}{2}}b\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b})P_{j}(\sqrt{b}),

which together with (
2.22
), gives

e^{-\frac{b}{2}}b\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b})P_{j}(\sqrt{b})=-\sum_{i=0}^{k}\lambda_{i}\langle P_{i},P_{j}\rangle_{b}.

Combining with (
2.8
), we obtain

(\lambda_{i})_{0\leq{i}\leq{k}}=-(M^{-1}_{b,k}\vec{{P}_{k}}(\sqrt{b}))e^{-\frac{b}{2}}b\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b}).

Hence

(H_{b}-2k)u=f+m_{k}(b,z)be^{-\frac{b}{2}}\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b}),

(2.17)

which is exactly the equation (
2.11
).
Now observe that by the definition of
m_{k}(b,z)
and (
2.14
), we have

\langle m_{k}(b,z),u\rangle_{b}=0.

(2.18)

Then taking inner product with
u
on both sides of (
2.17
), combining with (
2.18
) and the identity
\langle H_{b}u,u\rangle_{b}=\Arrowvert\partial_{z}u\Arrowvert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}
, we get

\Arrowvert\partial_{z}u\Arrowvert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}-2k\Arrowvert{u}\Arrowvert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}=\langle f,u\rangle_{b}.

(2.19)

Applying (
2.13
) to
v(z)=u(z)\textbf{1}_{z\geq\sqrt{b}}\in H^{1}_{\rho,0}
and together with (
2.19
) yields

\Arrowvert{u}\Arrowvert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim\Arrowvert\partial_{z}u\Arrowvert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}-2k\Arrowvert{u}\Arrowvert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}=\langle f,u\rangle_{b}\leq\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\Arrowvert{u}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}.

Hence

\Arrowvert{u}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}},

(2.20)

which implies the uniqueness of the solution to the equation (
2.11
).

Step 2. Proof of (
2.12
).
Firstly, from (
2.19
) and (
2.20
),

\Arrowvert\partial_{z}u\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}.

(2.21)

Then by Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\Arrowvert zu\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim\Arrowvert f\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}.

(2.22)

We now show the estimate of
|\sqrt{b}\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b})|
. Integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\bigg\langle H_{b}u,\frac{1}{z}\bigg\rangle_{b}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{z}d(\rho{z}^{2}\partial_{z}u)

(2.23)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{b}{2}}\sqrt{b}\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b})-\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}\rho\partial_{z}udz

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{b}{2}}\sqrt{b}\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b})-\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}\rho{z}udz.

Hence

\displaystyle|\sqrt{b}\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg|\bigg\langle H_{b}u,\frac{1}{z}\bigg\rangle_{b}\bigg|+\bigg|\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}\rho{z}udz\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Arrowvert{H_{b}}u\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\Arrowvert{u}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Arrowvert{f}+2ku+be^{-\frac{b}{2}}m_{k}(b,z)\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b})\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim b|\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b})|+\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}.

which implies

|\sqrt{b}\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b})|\lesssim\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}

(2.24)

for
0<b<b^{*}
small enough and thus

\Arrowvert{H_{b}u}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}.

(2.25)

We then estimate
\Arrowvert\Lambda{u}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}
.
Firstly, integrating by parts again yields

\displaystyle\langle z\partial_{z}u,H_{b}u\rangle_{b}

\displaystyle=\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}z\partial_{z}u\big(-\frac{1}{\rho{z}^{2}}\partial_{z}(\rho{z}^{2}\partial_{z}u)\big)\rho{z}^{2}dz

\displaystyle=-z\partial_{z}u\rho{z}^{2}\partial_{z}u\bigg|^{+\infty}_{\sqrt{b}}+\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}(z\partial_{zz}u+\partial_{z}u)\rho{z}^{2}\partial_{z}udz

\displaystyle=b^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{-\frac{b}{2}}(\partial_{z}u)^{2}(\sqrt{b})+\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}(\partial_{z}u)^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz+\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}\rho{z}^{3}\partial_{z}u\partial_{zz}udz

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}b^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{-\frac{b}{2}}(\partial_{z}u)^{2}(\sqrt{b})-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}(\partial_{z}u)^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}(z\partial_{z}u)^{2}\rho{z}^{2}dz

Hence by (
2.21
), (
2.24
), (
2.25
) and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\Arrowvert\Lambda{u}\Arrowvert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\Arrowvert{\Lambda{u}}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\Arrowvert{H_{b}u}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\Arrowvert{\Lambda{u}}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}},

from which we deduce that

\Arrowvert\Lambda{u}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}.

(2.26)

Then applying Lemma
2.2
to
zu
and combining with (
2.20
), (
2.22
), (
2.26
), we get

\lVert z^{2}u\rVert_{L^{2}_{{\rho},b}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}.

Finally, from (
2.25
) and (
2.26
), we also have

\Arrowvert\Delta{u}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}.

Then we finished the proof of (
2.12
).
∎

2.3.
Diagonalisation of
H_{b}

We are now in a position to diagonalise
H_{b}
for frequencies
0\leq{k}\leq{K}
under the assumption that
0<b<b^{*}(K)
small enough. The proof relies on a Lyapunov-Schmidt type argument which has already been used to deal with the similar problem in two dimension in
[
17
]
.

Proposition 2.3

(Diagonalisation of
H_{b}
)
.

Let
K\in\mathbb{N}
, then for any
0<b<b^{*}{(K)}
small enough,
H_{b}
admits a sequence of eigenvalues and eigenfunctions which satisfy

H_{b}\psi_{b,k}=\lambda_{b,k}\psi_{b,k},\quad\psi_{b,k}\in{H^{1}_{\rho,b}},\quad\forall\ 0\leq{k}\leq{K}.

(2.27)

•

Expansion of eigenvalues:

\lambda_{b,k}=2k+C^{2}_{k}\sqrt{b}+O(b),\quad|\partial_{b}\lambda_{b,k}|\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}.

(2.28)

•

Expansion of eigenfunctions:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\psi_{b,k}(z)=T_{b,k}(z)+\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}(z),\\
&T_{b,k}(z)=P_{k}(z)\bigg(\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\bigg)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mu_{b,jk}P_{j}(z)\bigg(\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\bigg).\end{aligned}\right.

(2.29)

with

\mu_{b,jk}=\frac{\langle Q_{k},P_{j}\rangle_{0}}{2(j-k)}\sqrt{b}+O(b),\quad|\partial_{b}\mu_{b,jk}|\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}},

(2.30)

here
Q_{k}=-\frac{1}{z}P_{k}+\frac{2}{z^{2}}\partial_{z}P_{k}
.

Moreover, the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\Arrowvert\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}\Arrowvert_{H^{1}_{\rho,b}}+\Arrowvert\Delta\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}

\displaystyle+\Arrowvert\Lambda\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\Arrowvert z\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}

\displaystyle+\Arrowvert z^{2}\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\sqrt{b}|\partial_{z}\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}(\sqrt{b})|\lesssim 1,

(2.31)

and

\Arrowvert\partial_{z}\partial_{b}\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\Arrowvert\partial_{b}\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\sqrt{b}|\partial_{z}\partial_{b}\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}(\sqrt{b})|\lesssim\frac{1}{b}.

(2.32)

•

Spectral gap estimate: if
u\in H^{1}_{\rho,b}
with

\langle u,\psi_{b,j}\rangle_{b}=0,\quad\forall\ 0\leq{j}\leq{k},

then

\Arrowvert\partial_{z}u\Arrowvert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\geq(2k+2+O(\sqrt{b}))\Arrowvert u\Arrowvert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}.

(2.33)

•

Some further estimates: for any
0\leq{j}\leq{k}
,

\langle\psi_{b,k},\psi_{b,i}\rangle_{b}=\frac{1}{b}(\delta_{ik}+O(\sqrt{b})),

(2.34)

and

\langle b\partial_{b}\psi_{b,k},\psi_{b,i}\rangle_{b}=-\frac{1}{2b}(\delta_{ik}+O(\sqrt{b})).

(2.35)

Moreover,

\Arrowvert 2b\partial_{b}\psi_{b,k}+\psi_{b,k}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\Arrowvert H_{b}(2b\partial_{b}\psi_{b,k}+\psi_{b,k})\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim{1},

(2.36)

\Arrowvert\Lambda\psi_{b,0}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim{1},\quad\Arrowvert\Lambda\psi_{b,k}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}},\quad\forall\ k\geq{1},

(2.37)

\Arrowvert z\psi_{b,k}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}},\quad\Arrowvert z^{2}\psi_{b,k}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}},

(2.38)

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\Lambda\psi_{b,k}

\displaystyle-2k\bigg(\psi_{b,k}-\frac{2k+1}{2k}\frac{A_{k-1}}{A_{k}}\psi_{b,k-1}\bigg)\bigg\rVert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}

\displaystyle+\bigg\lVert H_{b}\bigg(\Lambda\psi_{b,k}-2k\bigg(\psi_{b,k}-\frac{2k+1}{2k}\frac{A_{k-1}}{A_{k}}\psi_{b,k-1}\ \bigg)\bigg)\bigg\rVert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim{1}.

(2.39)

Proof.

We divide the proof into five steps.

Step 1. Lyapunov-Schmidt argument.
For simplity, we firstly introduce the unverisal profile

\mathcal{T}_{b}(z):=\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}

and consider
T_{b,k}(z)
which is given by (
2.29
). Direct computation gives

\displaystyle H_{b}T_{b,k}

\displaystyle=H_{b}(P_{k}\mathcal{T}_{b})+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mu_{b,jk}H_{b}(P_{j}\mathcal{T}_{b})

\displaystyle=(H_{b}P_{k})\mathcal{T}_{b}+2\frac{\partial_{z}P_{k}}{z^{2}}-\frac{P_{k}}{z}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mu_{b,jk}\bigg((H_{b}P_{j})\mathcal{T}_{b}+2\frac{\partial_{z}P_{j}}{z^{2}}-\frac{P_{j}}{z}\bigg).

Set

Q_{k}(z)=2\frac{\partial_{z}P_{k}}{z^{2}}-\frac{P_{k}}{z},

which combines with (
2.3
) yields

(H_{b}-2k)T_{b,k}(z)=Q_{k}(z)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mu_{b,jk}[2(j-k)\mathcal{T}_{b}(z)P_{j}(z)+Q_{j}(z)].

(2.40)

Now we study the eigenvalue problem

(H_{b}-2k)\psi_{b,k}=\mu_{k}\psi_{b,k}.

(2.41)

If the solution is of the form (
2.29
), then in view of (
2.41
) and (
2.40
),
\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}
satisfies

\displaystyle(H_{b}-2k)\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}

\displaystyle=\mu_{k}(T_{b,k}+\tilde{\psi}_{b,k})-(H_{b}-2k)T_{b,k}

(2.42)

\displaystyle=-Q_{k}(z)-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mu_{b,jk}[2(j-k)\mathcal{T}_{b}(z)P_{j}(z)+Q_{j}(z)]+\mu_{k}(T_{b,k}+\tilde{\psi}_{b,k})

\displaystyle:=f(\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}),

where the coefficients
\mu_{k}
,
\mu_{b,jk}

(0\leq{j}\leq{k-1})
can be determined by the relation

(M^{-1}_{b,k}(\langle f(\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}),\vec{P_{k}}\rangle_{b}))_{i}=be^{-\frac{b}{2}}\partial_{z}\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}(\sqrt{b})(M_{b,k}^{-1}\vec{P_{k}}(\sqrt{b}))_{i}\quad\ \forall\ 0\leq{i}\leq{k},

which is equivalent to

\langle f(\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}),P_{j}\rangle_{b}=be^{-\frac{b}{2}}\partial_{z}\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}(\sqrt{b})P_{j}(\sqrt{b}),\quad\forall\ 0\leq{j}\leq{k}

(2.43)

Next, (
2.42
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{b,k}\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}=

\displaystyle(H_{b}-2k)\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}-m_{k}(b,z)be^{-\frac{b}{2}}\partial_{z}\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}(\sqrt{b})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f-\sum_{j=0}^{k}(M^{-1}_{b,k}(\langle f(\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}),\vec{P_{k}}\rangle_{b}))_{j}P_{j}:=F(\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}),

where
m_{k}(b,z)
is defined as in (
2.10
).

Thus in order to find
\tilde{\psi}_{b,k}
, it suffices to solve the fixed point equation

\tilde{\psi}=\tilde{H}^{-1}_{b,k}\circ{F}(\tilde{\psi}).

We then consider the closed ball

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}=\Big\{\tilde{\psi}\in{H}^{1}_{\rho,b}|

\displaystyle\Arrowvert\Delta\tilde{\psi}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\sqrt{b}|\partial_{z}\tilde{\psi}(\sqrt{b})|+\Arrowvert\tilde{\psi}\Arrowvert_{H^{1}_{\rho,b}}+\Arrowvert\Lambda\tilde{\psi}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\Arrowvert z\tilde{\psi}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}

\displaystyle+\Arrowvert z^{2}\tilde{\psi}\Arrowvert_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\leq{\alpha},\ \tilde{\psi}(\sqrt{b})=0,\ \langle\tilde{\psi},P_{j}\rangle_{b}=0,\ \forall\ 0\leq{j}\leq{k}\Big\},

where
\alpha
is a universal large number to be determined later.

In what follows, we claim that
\tilde{H}^{-1}_{b,k}\circ{F}
is a contraction from
B_{\alpha}
to itself.

In order to prove the above claim, we firstly need to determine
\mu_{k}
and
\ \mu_{jk}
for
0\leq{j}\leq{k-1}
.
For any
\tilde{\psi}\in{B_{\alpha}}
and
0\leq{i}\leq{k-1}
,

{f}(\tilde{\psi})=-Q_{k}(z)-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mu_{b,jk}(2(j-k)\mathcal{T}_{b}(z)P_{j}(z)+Q_{j}(z))+\mu_{k}(T_{b,k}+\tilde{\psi}).

Therefore, for any
0\leq{i}\leq{k-1}
,

\displaystyle\langle f(\tilde{\psi}),P_{i}\rangle_{b}=

\displaystyle-\langle Q_{k},P_{i}\rangle_{b}-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mu_{b,jk}(\langle 2(j-k)\mathcal{T}_{b}P_{j},P_{i}\rangle_{b}

From the proof of Step 1 and Step 2, one sees that the solution remains in the bootstrap regime as long as it exists in

For the positivity of

The global

-bound follows from the energy identity of (

which together with

implies that

(6.10)

\dot{H}^{2}
-bound, we take a cut-off function
\chi\geq 0
with

and the exponetial weight is uniformly bounded from below and above in

where

(6.25)

Since
0<b\ll 1
, we have
B(t)\gg 1
. Then from (
6.20
),

\left|\frac{d}{d\tau}\int\chi_{R(t)}|\nabla u(\tau)|^{2}dx\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{2}(t)}\int|\nabla u(\tau)|^{2}dx+\int\chi_{R(t)}|\Delta u(\tau)|^{2}dx.

Integrating the above inequality over
[t,T)
and combining with (
6.10
), (
6.22
) gives

\left|\int\chi_{R(t)}|\nabla u(t)|^{2}dx-\int\chi_{R(t)}|\nabla u^{*}|^{2}dx\right|\lesssim\frac{T-t}{R^{2}(t)}+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{|x|\geq\lambda(t)}|\Delta u(\tau)|^{2}dxd\tau.

(6.26)

Next, we claim that

\frac{T-t}{R^{2}(t)}\rightarrow 0\quad{\rm{as}}\ t\rightarrow T.

For
k=0
, from (
6.14
) and (
6.17
), we get

b\sim\frac{1}{|\log\lambda|^{2}}.

Then combining with (
1.3
), (
6.24
) and (
6.25
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{T-t}{R^{2}(t)}=\frac{T-t}{\lambda^{2}(t)}\frac{2b}{|\log b|}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{|\log(T-t)|^{2}}{\log|\log\lambda|}\frac{2}{|\log\lambda|^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{|\log(T-t)|^{2}}{\log|\log\lambda|}\frac{1}{\left|\log\frac{T-t}{|\log(T-t)|^{2}}\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\log|\log\lambda|}\rightarrow 0\quad{\rm{as}}\ t\rightarrow T.

For
k\geq 1
, from (
1.4
), (
3.6
), (
6.18
), (
6.19
), (
6.24
) and (
6.25
), one has

\displaystyle\frac{T-t}{R^{2}(t)}=\frac{T-t}{\lambda^{2}(t)}\frac{2b}{|\log b|}\sim\frac{(T-t)(T-t)^{k}}{(T-t)^{k+1}|\log b|}\lesssim\frac{1}{|\log b|}\rightarrow 0\quad{\rm{as}}\ t\rightarrow T.

In the above argument, one can also infer that

R(t)\rightarrow 0\quad{\rm{as}}\ t\rightarrow T.

Hence, setting
t\rightarrow T
on both sides of (
6.26
), we conclude that

\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\int\chi_{R(t)}|\nabla u(t)|^{2}dx=\int|\nabla u^{*}|^{2}.

(6.27)

Finally, we claim that

\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\int_{|x|\geq\lambda(t)}(1-\chi_{R(t)})|\nabla u(t)|^{2}dx=0,

(6.28)

which together with (
6.27
) and (
6.23
) yields (
1.5
). For
k=0
, from (
2.81
), (
2.82
), (
3.3
), (
3.17
), (
A.10
), (
6.24
) and (
6.25
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq\lambda(t)}(1-\chi_{R(t)})|\nabla u(t)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\lambda(t)\leq|x|\leq 2R(t)}|\nabla u(t)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\lambda(t)\int_{1\leq|y|\leq 2B(t)}|\nabla v(t,y)|^{2}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda(t)\int_{1\leq y\leq 2B(t)}(b^{3}|\partial_{y}\eta_{b,0}|^{2}y^{2}+|\partial_{y}\varepsilon|^{2}y^{2})dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda(t)e^{2bB^{2}}\left(b^{3}\lVert\partial_{y}\eta_{b,0}\rVert_{b}^{2}+\lVert\partial_{y}\varepsilon\rVert_{b}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda(t)\frac{1}{b(t)}\left(b(t)^{3}\frac{1}{b(t)}+b^{3}(t)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda(t)b(t)\rightarrow 0\quad{\rm{as}}\ t\rightarrow T,

and for
k\geq 1
, similar computation gives

\int_{|x|\geq\lambda(t)}(1-\chi_{R(t)})|\nabla u(t)|^{2}dx\lesssim b^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\lambda(t)\rightarrow 0\quad{\rm{as}}\ t\rightarrow T,

then (
6.28
) is proved. This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Appendix A
Coercivity estimates

We now state the main coercivity property at the heart of the energy estimate. The proof is a perturbation consequence of the harmonic oscillator estimates (
2.7
) and (
2.33
) which is similar to that in (
[
17
]

Lemma A.1

(Coercivity of
H_{b}
)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<b<b^{*}(k)
small enough. Suppose
u\in H^{3}_{\rho,b}
satisfy

u(\sqrt{b})=0,\quad\langle u,\psi_{b,j}\rangle_{b}=0,\quad\forall\ 0\leq{j}\leq{k}

Then

\lVert H_{b}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\gtrsim\lVert\Delta u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\lVert(1+z)\partial_{z}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\lVert(1+z)u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+b|\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b})|^{2}.

(A.1)

Moreover, there exists a constant
c_{k}>0
such that

\lVert\partial_{z}H_{b}u(z)\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\geq(2k+2+O(\sqrt{b}))\lVert H_{b}u(z)\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}-c_{k}b^{\frac{5}{2}}|H_{b}u(\sqrt{b})|^{2},

(A.2)

and

\lVert zH_{b}u(z)\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim\lVert\partial_{z}H_{b}u(z)\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+b^{\frac{3}{2}}|H_{b}u(\sqrt{b})|^{2}.

(A.3)

Proof.

Step 1:

Proof of (
A.1
).

Pick a small constant
\delta>0
to be determined later, we compute

\lVert(H_{b}-\delta)u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}=\langle(H_{b}-\delta)u,(H_{b}-\delta)u\rangle_{b}=\lVert H_{b}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}-2\delta\langle H_{b}u,u\rangle_{b}+\delta^{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}},

hence from
u(\sqrt{b})=0
and integrating by parts,

\displaystyle\lVert H_{b}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}

\displaystyle=\lVert(H_{b}-\delta)u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+2\delta\langle H_{b}u,u\rangle_{b}-\delta^{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}

(A.4)

\displaystyle=\lVert(H_{b}-\delta)u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+2\delta\lVert\partial_{z}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}-\delta^{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}.

By spectral gap estimate (
2.33

\lVert\partial_{z}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\geq(2k+2+O(\sqrt{b}))\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}.

Moreover, from Lemma
2.2

\lVert H_{b}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\gtrsim\lVert(H_{b}-\delta)u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\lVert\partial_{z}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\lVert(1+z)u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}.

(A.5)

By direct computation,

\lVert H_{b}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}=\langle(-\Delta+\Lambda)u,(-\Delta+\Lambda)u\rangle_{b}=\lVert\Delta u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\lVert\Lambda u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}-2\langle\Lambda u,\Delta u\rangle_{b},

(A.6)

and note that

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda u,\Delta u\rangle_{b}

\displaystyle=\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}\Lambda u\Delta u\rho z^{2}dz=\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}z\partial_{z}u\bigg(\partial_{zz}u+\frac{2}{z}\partial_{z}u\bigg)\rho z^{2}dz

\displaystyle=2\lVert\partial_{z}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2}\partial_{z}((\partial_{z}u)^{2})\rho z^{3}dz

\displaystyle=2\lVert\partial_{z}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{z}u)^{2}\rho z^{3}\bigg|^{+\infty}_{\sqrt{b}}-\int_{\sqrt{b}}^{+\infty}\bigg(\frac{3}{2}\rho z^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\rho z^{4}\bigg)(\partial_{z}u)^{2}dz

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\partial_{z}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\Lambda u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{b}{2}}b^{\frac{3}{2}}(\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b}))^{2}.

Then inserting into (

(A.7)

As a result, from (

and from (

\lVert\Lambda u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}\lesssim\lVert H_{b}u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho,b}}.

To estimate the term

\displaystyle\sqrt{b}|\partial_{z}u(\sqrt{b})|

where (
A.5

Step 2:

Proof of (

and note that

Appendix B
Cauchy theory for the radial Stefan problem

In this section, we establish the Cauchy theory for the radial Stefan problem in 3D. Since the similar results have already been proved in (
[
17
]
) in 2D and the methods there used can also been applied here, we only briefly recall the main steps and will not give so many details in the following argument.

Taking into account that the problem is a free boundary problem, we firstly convert it to a fixed boundary problem. Hence we pull back the problem (
1.2
) onto the fixed domain
\Omega:=\{\vec{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}|\ y=|\vec{y}|\geq 1\}

Inserting into (

A role of potential on L 2 L^{2} -estimates for some evolution equations
Ryo Ikehata

Abstract
In this papwe we consider an effective role of the potential of the wave equations with/without damping on the L 2 L^{2} -estimate of the solution itself. In the free wave equation case it is known ( [ 8 ] ) that the L 2 L^{2} -norm of the solution itself generally grows to infinity (as t → ∞ t\to\infty ) in the one and two dimensional cases, however, by adding the potential with quite generous condition one can controle the growth property to get the L 2 L^{2} -bounds. This idea can be also applied to the damped wave equations with potential in order to get fast energy and L 2 L^{2} decay results in the low dimensional case, which are open for a long period. Applications to heat and plate equations with a potential can be also studied. In this paper the low dimensional case is a main target.

In this paper, first we are concerned with the following Cauchy problem with a potential:

u_{tt}-\Delta u+V(x)u=0,\quad t>0,\quad x\in{\bf R}^{n},

(1.1)

u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\hskip 14.22636ptu_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x)\quad x\in{\bf R}^{n}.

(1.2)

Throughout the paper one assumes that (for simplicity) the potential function
V\in BC({\bf R}^{n})
satisfies
V(x)\geq 0
(
x\in{\bf R}^{n}
).

Note that functions and solutions treated in this paper are all real-valued.

One defines the total energy for the equation (
1.1
) by

E(t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}\left(|u_{t}(t,x)|^{2}+|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+V(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\right)dx.

(1.3)

Then, it is known that for each initial data
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})

1.1
)-(
1.2

u\in C([0,\infty);H^{1}({\bf R}^{n}))\cap C^{1}([0,\infty);L^{2}({\bf R}^{n}))
satisfying the energy identity

E(t)=E(0).

w_{tt}-\Delta w=0,\quad t>0,\quad x\in{\bf R}^{n},

(1.5)

w(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\hskip 14.22636ptw_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x)\quad x\in{\bf R}^{n}.

(1.6)

Theorem 1.1

Let
n\geq 1
, and
V\in BC({\bf R}^{n})
satifies
V(x)>0

(
\forall x\in{\bf R}^{n}
)
. Suppose that the initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
further satisfies

I_{0}^{2}:=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}\frac{|u_{1}(x)|^{2}}{V(x)}dx<+\infty.

Then the unique solution
u\in C([0,\infty);H^{1}({\bf R}^{n}))\cap C^{1}([0,\infty);L^{2}({\bf R}^{n}))
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfies

\|u(t,\cdot)\|\leq C(\|u_{0}\|+I_{0}),\quad t\geq 0,

with some generous constant
C>0
.

Example 1.
The constant coefficient case
V(x)=m^{2}
(
m>0
) can be included as a trivial example since
I_{0}<+\infty
is automatically satisfied. This is the so-called Klein-Gordon equation case.

Example 2.
One can also choose
V(x):=e^{-|x|^{2}}
as the 2nd example. In this case one has to assume
I_{0}^{2}=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}e^{|x|^{2}}|u_{1}(x)|^{2}dx<+\infty

V(x):=e^{-|x|^{2}}
can be viewed as
V(x)\approx 0

Example 3.
As the 3rd example, one can choose
V(x):=(1+|x|^{2})^{-\alpha/2}
(
\alpha>0
). In this case one has to assume
I_{0}^{2}=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}(1+|x|^{2})^{\alpha/2}|u_{1}(x)|^{2}dx<+\infty

-boundedness.

So, one can ask a question: can one localize the effective region of
V(x)
to the smaller region to still have

-boundedness. A next result gives an answer for this question.

Let

to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfies

\|u(t,\cdot)\|\leq C(\|u_{0}\|+J_{0}),

with some generous constant
C>0
.

If

v_{tt}(t,x)-\Delta v(t,x)+V(x)v(t,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad t>0,\quad x\in{\bf R}^{n},

(2.1)

v(0,x)=0,\hskip 14.22636ptv_{t}(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\quad x\in{\bf R}^{n}.

(2.2)

|(u_{1},v(t,\cdot))|\leq\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|u_{1}(x)||v(t,x)|dx=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}\frac{|u_{1}(x)|}{\sqrt{V(x)}}\left(\sqrt{V(x)}||v(t,x)|\right)dx

(2.5)

because of
v_{t}=u
. It should be repeated that the agument above holds true for all

3
Damped waves

In this section, we shall prove Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
by relying on the powerful method coming from
[
11
]
.

In this case it is sufficient to suppose
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R}^{n})\times C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R}^{n})
. Then, the corresponding solution becomes sufficiently smooth and vanishs outside of
B_{L+t}
with some
L>0
.

To begin with, note that the following energy identity holds:

E(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{t}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds=E(0).

(3.1)

Multiplying both sides of (
1.7
) by
u
and integrating it on
[0,t]\times{\bf R}^{n}
one has the identity:

\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\right)ds+\frac{1}{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

=\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{t}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+(u_{1},u_{0})-(u_{t}(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot))+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}\quad(t\geq 0).

(3.2)

Since

-(u_{t}(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot))\leq\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

from (
3.2
) one has

\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\right)ds+\frac{1}{4}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{t}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+(u_{1},u_{0})+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\quad(t\geq 0).

(3.3)

Thus, from (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) it follows that

\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\right)ds+\frac{1}{4}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\leq 3E(0)+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}+(u_{1},u_{0})=:I_{0}^{2},

\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{t}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\leq E(0),

so that one has arrived at the following result.

Lemma 3.1

Let
n\geq 1
. Then, it holds that

\|u(t,\cdot)\|\leq 2I_{0},\quad\int_{0}^{t}E(s)ds\leq I_{0}^{2}+E(0)\quad(t\geq 0),

where

I_{0}:=\sqrt{3E(0)+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}+(u_{1},u_{0})}.

Next, multiplying both sides of (
1.7
) by
(1+t)u_{t}
, and integrating it over
[0,t]\times{\bf R}^{n}
one has the identity:

(1+t)E(t)+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|u_{t}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds=E(0)+\int_{0}^{t}E(s)ds.

(3.4)

Furthermore, multiplying both sides of (
1.7
) by
(1+t)u
and integrating it on
[0,t]\times{\bf R}^{n}
one has the identity:

\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\left(\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\right)ds+\frac{(1+t)}{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

=\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|u_{t}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\frac{1}{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+(u_{1},u_{0})-(1+t)(u_{t}(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot))+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\quad(t\geq 0).

(3.5)

While, as in the former part one has

-(1+t)(u_{t}(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot))\leq(1+t)\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{(1+t)}{4}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}.

Therefore, together with (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) one can get

\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\left(\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\right)ds+\frac{(1+t)}{4}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

\leq\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|u_{t}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds+\frac{1}{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+(u_{1},u_{0})+(1+t)\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

\leq(E(0)+\int_{0}^{t}E(s)ds)+\frac{1}{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+(u_{1},u_{0})+2(E(0)+\int_{0}^{t}E(s)ds)+\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds

\leq 3E(0)+3\int_{0}^{t}E(s)ds+(u_{1},u_{0})+\frac{1}{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\quad(t\geq 0).

(3.6)

(
3.4
) and (
3.6
) implies the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2

Let
n\geq 1
. Then, it holds that

\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\left(\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}\right)ds+\frac{(1+t)}{4}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

\leq 3E(0)+3\int_{0}^{t}E(s)ds+(u_{1},u_{0})+\frac{1}{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\quad(t\geq 0),

and

\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|u_{t}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\leq E(0)+\int_{0}^{t}E(s)ds\quad(t\geq 0).

In order to get the fast decay estimate one has to note the following inequality:

\frac{d}{dt}\left((1+t)^{2}E(t)\right)\leq 2(1+t)E(t),

so that from Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
one has

(1+t)^{2}E(t)\leq 5E(0)+4\int_{0}^{t}E(s)ds+(u_{1},u_{0})+\frac{1}{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds,

so the lemma below holds.

Lemma 3.3

Let
n\geq 1
. Then, it holds that

(1+t)^{2}E(t)\leq 9E(0)+6I_{0}^{2}+(u_{1},u_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\quad(t\geq 0),

\frac{(1+t)}{4}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\leq 6E(0)+5I_{0}^{2}+(u_{1},u_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds\quad(t\geq 0).

From the observation on Lemma
3.3
one can notice that to get the fast energy and

-decay it suffices to obtain the a priori bound for the quantity

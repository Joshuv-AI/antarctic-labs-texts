Full theta lifting for type II reductive dual pairs
Huajian Xue

Abstract
. In this article, we study the full theta lifting for two cases of type II reductive dual pairs over a nonarchimedean local field. Firstly, we determine the structure of the full theta lifts of all irreducible representations for dual pair ( GL ⁡ ( 2 ) , GL ⁡ ( 2 ) ) ({\mathrm{GL}}(2),{\mathrm{GL}}(2)) . Secondly, we prove that the full theta lift of an irreducible tempered representation is irreducible and tempered for dual pair ( GL ⁡ ( n ) , GL ⁡ ( n + 1 ) ) ({\mathrm{GL}}(n),{\mathrm{GL}}(n+1)) , where n n is a positive integer.

1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a local field of characteristic 0. Let
W
be a finite dimensional symplectic space over
F
with symplectic form
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{W}
. Denote by
{\mathrm{H}}(W):=W\times F
the Heisenberg group attached to
W
.
The group multiplication is defined by

(w_{1},t_{1})(w_{2},t_{2})=(w_{1}+w_{2},t_{1}+t_{2}+\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle_{W}),\quad w_{1},w_{2}\in W,t_{1},t_{2}\in F.

The symplectic group
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
has a natural action on
{\mathrm{H}}(W)
:

g.(w,t):=(g.w,t),\quad g\in{\mathrm{Sp}}(W),(w,t)\in{\mathrm{H}}(W).

Let

(1)

1\to\{\pm 1\}\to\widetilde{{\mathrm{Sp}}}(W)\to{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)\to 1

be the metaplectic cover of
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
. The above exact sequence induces an action of
\widetilde{{\mathrm{Sp}}}(W)
on
{\mathrm{H}}(W)
. We write
\widetilde{{\mathrm{J}}}(W):=\widetilde{{\mathrm{Sp}}}(W)\ltimes{\mathrm{H}}(W)
.

Fix a nontrivial unitary character
\psi
of
F
. Then there is a unique (up to isomorphism) smooth representation
\omega
of
\widetilde{{\mathrm{J}}}(W)
such that
\omega|_{{\mathrm{H}}(W)}
is irreducible and has central character
\psi
.
The representation
\omega
is called the Weil representation.

If
H
is a closed subgroup of
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
, we will write
\widetilde{H}
for the preimage of
H
in
\widetilde{{\mathrm{Sp}}}(W)
. Consider a reductive dual pair
(G,G^{\prime})
in
{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)
. Note that
\widetilde{G}
and
\widetilde{G^{\prime}}
commute with each other, and the representation
\omega

induces a representation of
\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{G^{\prime}}
, which we still denote by
\omega
for simplicity if no confusion can arise. In the theory of local theta correspondence, one is interested in how
\omega
decomposes into irreducible representations of
\widetilde{G}\times\widetilde{G^{\prime}}
.

For a reductive group
G
, write
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
for the set of isomorphism
classes of irreducible admissible representations of
G
. Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(\widetilde{G})
. One may consider the maximal
\pi
-isotypic quotient

\omega/\mathcal{N}(\pi)

of
\omega
, where

\mathcal{N}(\pi):=\bigcap_{\varphi\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\widetilde{G}}(\omega,\pi)}\ker(\varphi).

There is a smooth admissible representation
\Theta(\pi)
of
\widetilde{G^{\prime}}
, such that

(2)

\omega/\mathcal{N}(\pi)\cong\pi\widehat{\boxtimes}\Theta(\pi),

where “
\widehat{\boxtimes}
” stands for the completed tensor product (resp. algebraic tensor product) if
F
is archimedean (resp. non-archimedean). We call
\Theta(\pi)
the full theta lift of
\pi
. Note that
\Theta(\pi)
may be zero, and
\Theta(\pi)=0
if and only if
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\widetilde{G}}(\omega,\pi)=0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
\Theta(\pi)\neq 0
, then there is a unique
\widetilde{G^{\prime}}
submodule
\Theta^{0}(\pi)
of
\Theta(\pi)
, such that

\theta(\pi):=\Theta(\pi)/\Theta^{0}(\pi)

is irreducible.

The representation
\theta(\pi)
is called the (small) theta lift of
\pi
. The above theorem is known as Howe duality principle (see
[
Ho
]

[
Wal
]

[
Mi
]
[
GT
]
[
GSu
]
for proofs of this theorem for various cases).

One basic problem is determining
\theta(\pi)
and
\Theta(\pi)
precisely for given
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(\widetilde{G})
. Although there are many fruitful results concerning the (small) theta lift
\theta(\pi)
(see
[
AB
]
[
LPTZ
]
[
Mo
]
[
Pa
]
etc.), few has been known about the full theta lift
\Theta(\pi)
. For applications to representation theory and automorphic forms, it is desirable to know whether or not the full theta lift itself is irreducible. If the local field
F
is nonarchimedean, Kudla
[
Ku
]
proves that
\Theta(\pi)
has finite length. Furthermore, if
\pi
is a supercuspidal representation, then
\Theta(\pi)
(in the first occurrence) is irreducible and is also supercuspidal. In
[
Mu
]
, Muić shows that the lifts of discrete series behave very much like the lifts of supercuspidal representations. Explicitly, let
(G,G^{\prime})
be a type I reductive dual pair, and let
\pi
be an irreducible discrete series representations of
G
. If the rank of
G^{\prime}
is in a suitable range (which we call the tempered range), then
\Theta(\pi)
is irreducible and tempered. While outside this range,
\Theta(\pi)
is either
0
or non-tempered. In
[
LM
]
, Loke and Ma prove that for type I dual pair in stable range over
\mathbb{R}
, the full theta lift of a unitary representation is always irreducible except for one special case. In our previous work (see
[
Xue
]
[
FSX
]
), we determined the full theta lifting explicitly for dual pair
({\mathrm{GL}}(1),{\mathrm{GL}}(n))
, and proved that
\Theta(\pi)
is irreducible if
\pi
is generic for
({\mathrm{GL}}(n),{\mathrm{GL}}(n))
.

In this article we will focus on theta lifting over nonarchimedean local field. So from now on, let
F
be a nonarchimedean field of characteristic
0
. Denote by
\mathfrak{O}_{F}
the ring of integers of
F
, and let
\varpi_{F}
be a uniformizing element of it.
Write
q:=p^{r}
for the cardinality of the residue field
\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\varpi_{F}\mathfrak{O}_{F}
, where
p
is the characteristic of
\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\varpi_{F}\mathfrak{O}_{F}
.
Denote by
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{F}
the normalized absolute value on
F
. Here and henceforth, the subscript “
F
” in
\mathfrak{O}_{F}
,
\varpi_{F}
and
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{F}
may be omitted
when no confusion can arise.

Let
n,m
be two positive integers(
n\leq m
). Then

(G,G^{\prime})=({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F),{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(F))

is a type II reductive dual pair. Denote by
M_{n\times m}(F)
the space of
n\times m
matrices over
F
.
Let
{\mathcal{S}}(M_{n\times m}(F))
be the space of Schwartz-Bruhat functions on
M_{n\times m}(F)
,
namely, the space of compactly supported, locally constant
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions on
M_{n\times m}(F)
.
The Weil representation can be realized on
{\mathcal{S}}(M_{n\times m}(F))
. More precisely, note that
{\mathcal{S}}(M_{n\times m}(F))
carries a natural action of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(F)
which is given
by

(g_{1},g_{2})f(M):=f(g_{1}^{-1}Mg_{2}),\quad(g_{1},g_{2})\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(F),M\in M_{n\times m}(F).

The Weil representation
\omega_{n,m}
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(F)
is defined to be

\omega_{n,m}:={\mathcal{S}}(M_{n\times m}(F))\otimes(\lvert\det\rvert^{-\frac{m}{2}}\boxtimes\lvert\det\rvert^{\frac{n}{2}}).

As usual, we do not distinguish a representation with its underlying space.

If
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)=\mathrm{Irr}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F))
, then the full theta lift of
\pi
is given by

\Theta(\pi)=\left(\omega_{n,m}\otimes\pi^{\vee}\right)_{G},

where
V_{G}
means taking the maximal quotient of
V
on which
G
(viewed naturally as a subgroup of
G\times G^{\prime}
) acts trivially.

In this paper we will study the structure of
\Theta(\pi)
for two cases of type II dual pairs. We give an outline of this paper. Section
2
is devoted to the full theta lifting for
({\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F),{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F))
. By checking the poles of the L-function of
\pi
, we show that
\Theta(\pi)
is irreducible if
\pi
is non-trivial. The case of trivial representation is much more involved. In fact, we need to consider the generalized semi-invariant distribution on
M_{2\times 2}(F)
. We make use of some results about generalized semi-invariant distribution given in
[
HS
]
, and show that
\Theta(\pi)
is isomorphic to an induce representation of length 2 if
\pi
is trivial. In Section
3
, we study the dual pair
({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F),{\mathrm{GL}}_{n+1}(F))
. We firstly introduce two main tools, Kudla’s filtration and Casselman’s square-integrability criterion, which will be frequently used in our proofs. Then we prove that the full theta lift of a discrete series representation is irreducible and tempered. Based on the discrete series case and an induction technique, we are able to show that the full theta lift of a tempered representation is also irreducible and tempered.

2.
Full theta lifting for
({\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F),{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F))

2.1.
Irreducible representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)

In this subsection we put
G:={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
, and denote by
B
the standard Borel subgroup of
G
which consists of upper triangular matrices. Let
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
be two characters of
F^{\times}
.
The pair
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
determines a character
\chi
of
B
given by

\chi\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&\\
&b\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\right):=\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(b).

We define the normalized smooth induction of
\chi
to be

I(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}):={\mathrm{Ind}}_{B}^{G}\chi=\left\{f\in C^{\infty}(G)\middle|f\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&\\
&b\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
&1\end{pmatrix}g\right)=\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(b)\left|\frac{a}{b}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}f(g)\right\},

on which the group
G
acts by right translation.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
JL
, Theorem 3.3]
)
Let
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
be two characters of
F^{\times}
, then
I(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is admissible. Moreover,

(i) if
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}\neq\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert^{\pm 1}
, then
I(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is irreducible.

(ii) if
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}=\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert
, then
I(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
contains an irreducible subrepresentation of codimension
1
.

(iii) if
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}=\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert^{-1}
, then
I(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
contains a
1
-dimensional subrepresentation whose quotient is irreducible.

A representation
I(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
in case (i) of Lemma
2.1
is called an irreducible principal series representation, while the irreducible subrepresentation of
I(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
in case (ii) and the irreducible quotient of
I(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
in case (iii) of Lemma
2.1
are called special representations.

If
I(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is irreducible, we write
\pi(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=I(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
. Otherwise, we define
\pi(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
to be the corresponding special representation.

For two pairs of characters
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
and
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
, the representations
\pi(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
and
\pi(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
are isomorphic if and only if
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
or
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=(\tau_{2},\tau_{1})
. In particular, for any special representation
\pi
in case (ii) of Lemma
2.1
, there is exactly one special representation
\pi^{\prime}
in case (iii) such that

\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}
.

The representation
\pi(\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert^{1/2},\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert^{-1/2})
is called the Steinberg representation.

Denote by
\pi(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})^{\vee}
the contragredient representation of
\pi(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
. We have

\pi(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})^{\vee}\cong\pi(\chi_{1}^{-1},\chi_{2}^{-1}).

Also, it is not hard to show that

\pi(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\otimes(\chi\circ\det)\cong\pi(\chi\chi_{1},\chi\chi_{2})

for any character
\chi
of
F^{\times}
.

The classification of irreducible representation of
G
is given in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
GH
, Theorem 6.13.4]
)
Let
\pi
be an irreducible admissible representation of
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
. Then
\pi
is isomorphic to one of the following disjoint types, where
\chi
,
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
are characters of
F^{\times}
:

(i) an irreducible principal series representation
\pi(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
, where
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}\neq\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert^{\pm 1}
;

(ii) a special representation, which we may write in the form
\pi(\chi\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert^{1/2},\chi\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert^{-1/2})
;

(iii) a supercuspidal representation;

(iv) a one-dimensional representation, of the form
\chi\circ\det
.

2.2.
Theta lifting of nontrivial representations

The Godement-Jacquet L-functions for all irreducible representation
\pi
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
are listed as follows (see
[
JL
, Chapter I]
).

(i)

For an irreducible principal series
\pi=\pi(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
(where
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}\neq\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert^{\pm 1}
),

L(s,\pi)=\frac{1}{(1-\alpha_{1}q^{-s})(1-\alpha_{2}q^{-s})},

where
\alpha_{i}=\chi_{i}(\varpi)
if
\chi_{i}
is unramified and
\alpha_{i}=0
if
\chi_{i}
is ramified.

(ii)

For a special representation
\pi=\pi(\chi\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert^{1/2},\chi\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert^{-1/2})
,

L(s,\pi)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha q^{-s}},

where
\alpha=\chi(\varpi)\lvert\varpi\rvert^{1/2}=q^{-1/2}\chi(\varpi)
if
\chi
is unramified and
\alpha=0
if
\chi
is ramified.

(iii)

If
\pi
is supercuspidal, then

L(s,\pi)=1.

(iv)

For a one-dimensional representation
\pi=\chi\circ\det
,

\begin{split}L(s,\pi)&=\frac{1}{(1-\chi(\varpi)\lvert\varpi\rvert^{-1/2}q^{-s})(1-\chi(\varpi)\lvert\varpi\rvert^{1/2}q^{-s})}\\
&=\frac{1}{(1-\chi(\varpi)q^{-s+1/2})(1-\chi(\varpi)q^{-s-1/2})}\end{split}

if
\chi
is unramified, and

L(s,\pi)=1,

if
\chi
is ramified.

A direct calculation shows that if
\pi
is not the trivial representation, then
L(s,\pi)
has no pole at
s=1/2
, or
L(s,\pi^{\vee})
has no pole at
s=1/2
. Therefore, by
[
FSX
, Corollary 1.5]
, we have

Theorem 2.3
.

If
\pi
is not the trivial representation, then
\Theta(\pi)
is irreducible.

2.3.
Theta lifting of trivial representation

It remains to study the case that
\pi
is a trivial representation.

Put
G=G^{\prime}={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)

\chi

For a

.

Let
\chi
be a character of a group

. A vector

-invariant (or simply generalized

We also have the following exact sequences

(9)

Lemma 2.6
.

The space
{\mathcal{S}}^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{1})^{\chi}

Proof.

Decompose
{\mathcal{O}}_{1}

Lemma 2.7
.

We have the following equality

{\mathcal{S}}^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{1})^{\chi}={\mathcal{S}}^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{1})^{\chi,\infty}.

Proof.

Note that

(11)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{O}}_{1}&=\bigcup_{t\in F}G\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\bigcup\bigcup_{t\in F}G\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\

t&1\end{pmatrix}\\
&\simeq G/G_{1}\times F\bigcup G/G_{2}\times F,\end{split}

where

G_{1}:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
0&y\end{pmatrix}\middle|x\in F,y\in F^{\times}\right\},

and

G_{2}:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}y&0\\
x&1\end{pmatrix}\middle|x\in F,y\in F^{\times}\right\}.

It suffices to prove that on both open subsets
G/G_{1}\times F
and
G/G_{2}\times F
, every generalized
\chi
-invariant distribution is
\chi
-invariant.
Let
D_{0}
be a nonzero element in

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G,\infty}(({\mathcal{S}}(G/G_{1})\boxtimes{\mathcal{S}}(F))\otimes\left(\lvert\det\rvert^{-1}\boxtimes\lvert\det\rvert\right),\chi^{-1}):={\mathcal{S}}^{*}(G/G_{1}\times F)^{\chi,\infty}.

Take a nonzero test vector

, and let

Proof.

. By the duality (
6

2.9

(12)

\Theta(\pi)\cong{\mathcal{S}}_{\chi}(M_{2})\cong{\mathcal{S}}_{\chi}({\mathcal{O}}_{1}).

{\mathcal{O}}_{1}=G\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}H\simeq({F^{\times}(P_{0}\times Q_{0})})\backslash({G\times H}),

where

Remark 2.11
.

In fact, Theorem
2.10
gives a negative answer to Question 6.3 posed in
[
APS
]
.
Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
. Recall the Euler-Poincaré characteristic
[
APS
, Equation 3.4]

\mathrm{EP}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)}(\omega,\pi):=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\mathrm{Ext}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)}^{i}(\omega,\pi),

which is a well-defined element of the Grothendieck group of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
-modules.

By
[
APS
, Theorem 5.13]
, one has that

(16)

\mathrm{EP}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)}(\omega,\pi)_{\infty}=\pi,

where the subscript “
\infty
” stands for taking smooth vectors. Assume that
\omega
is a projective
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
-module, as is expected in
[
APS
, Question 6.3]
, then (

) implies that

On the other hand, Proposition 4.1 of
[
APS
]
shows that

It follows that

which contradicts to Theorem

.

3.
The almost equal rank case

In this section we study the full theta lifts of discrete series and tempered representations for dual pair
({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F),{\mathrm{GL}}_{n+1}(F))
, where
F
is a non-archimedean field with characteristic
0
. The idea is due to Muić
[
Mu
]
, who has achieved fruitful results on the full theta lifting for type I dual pairs. See also
[
GSa
]
for an exposition on the almost equal rank type I dual pairs.

3.1.
Kudla’s filtration and Casselman’s square-integrability criterion

For a reductive group
G
, denote by
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
the category of smooth representations of
G
. Let
P=MN
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
, where
M
and
N
are the Levi and unipotent part of
P
respectively. We have the normalized (smooth) induction functor

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}:\mathrm{Rep}(M)\to\mathrm{Rep}(G).

Moreover, we have the normalized Jacquet functor

R_{P}:\mathrm{Rep}(G)\to\mathrm{Rep}(M).

Let
\overline{P}=M\overline{N}
be the opposite parabolic subgroup to
P
. We get a Jacquet functor
R_{\overline{P}}
similarly. Then for
\pi\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
,
\sigma\in\mathrm{Rep}(M)
, one has the standard Frobenius reciprocity

(17)

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G}(\pi,{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}(\sigma))\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{M}(R_{P}(\pi),\sigma),

and Bernstein’s Frobenius reciprocity (see
[
Ber
, Theorem 20]
)

(18)

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G}({\mathrm{Ind}}_{P}^{G}(\sigma),\pi)\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{M}(\sigma,R_{\overline{P}}(\pi)).

When we apply the Jacquet functor to the Weil representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{n+1}(F)
, we should look into the Weil representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{l}(F)\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(F)
for various
l
and
m
. To avoid confusion, we write
G_{l}
for the first member of dual pair
({\mathrm{GL}}_{l}(F),{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(F))
, and write
G^{\prime}_{m}
for the second member. Also,
P^{\prime}
,
M^{\prime}
(and so on) stand for subgroups of
G^{\prime}_{m}
.
Denote by
\omega_{l,m}
the Weil representation of
G_{l}\times G^{\prime}_{m}
. Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
G_{l}
. Denote by
\Theta_{l,m}(\pi)
the full theta lift of
\pi
to
G^{\prime}_{m}
. The unique irreducible quotient of
\Theta_{l,m}(\pi)
, i.e. the (small) theta lift of
\pi
, is denoted by
\theta_{l,m}(\pi)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(
[
APS
, Lemma 2.4]
)
Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G_{n})
. We have the following isomorphism

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G_{n}}({\mathcal{S}}(G_{n}),\pi)_{\infty}\cong\pi

as representation of
G^{\prime}_{n}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

(
[
APS
, Proposition 4.1]
)
Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G_{n})
. We have the following isomorphism

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G_{n}}(\omega_{n,m},\pi)_{\infty}\cong\Theta_{n,m}(\pi)^{\vee}

as representations of
G^{\prime}_{m}
.

Let
n=n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{k}
be a partition of
n
. We write
P_{n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{k}}
for the block-wise upper triangular parabolic subgroup of
G_{n}
which has
M_{n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{k}}:=G_{n_{1}}\times G_{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times G_{n_{k}}
as a Levi factor.

The following lemma, known as the Kudla’s filtration (for type II dual pairs), is due to Mínguez
[
Mi
, Proposition 3.3]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The normalized Jacquet module
R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\omega_{n,n+1})
of
\omega_{n,n+1}
has a filtration of
G_{n}\times M^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}
modules

0=R^{t+1}\subset R^{t}\subset\cdots\subset R^{1}\subset R^{0}=R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\omega_{n,n+1})

with successive quotients

\begin{split}J^{i}:&=R^{i}/R^{i+1}\\
&\cong{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P_{i,n-i}\times P^{\prime}_{t-i,i}\times G^{\prime}_{n+1-t}}^{G_{n}\times M^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\chi_{t,i}\otimes{\mathcal{S}}(G_{i})\otimes\omega_{n-i,n+1-t}).\end{split}

Here,
\chi_{t,i}
is a character given by

\chi_{t,i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lvert\det\rvert^{\frac{-1+2t-i}{2}}&\textrm{ on }G_{i}\\
\lvert\det\rvert^{\frac{t-i}{2}}&\textrm{ on }G_{n-i}\\
\lvert\det\rvert^{\frac{1-i+t}{2}}&\textrm{ on }G^{\prime}_{t-i}\\
\lvert\det\rvert^{\frac{1-2t+i}{2}}&\textrm{ on }G^{\prime}_{i}\\
\lvert\det\rvert^{\frac{-t+i}{2}}&\textrm{ on }G^{\prime}_{n+1-t}.\end{array}\right.

While
G_{i}
and
G_{i}^{\prime}
act on
{\mathcal{S}}(G_{i})
via left and right translation. If
i>t
, then
J^{i}
is interpreted to be
0
.

In particular, the bottom piece

J^{t}=R^{t}\cong{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P_{t,n-t}\times G^{\prime}_{t}\times G^{\prime}_{n+1-t}}^{G_{n}\times M^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\chi_{t,t}\otimes{\mathcal{S}}(G_{t})\otimes\omega_{n-t,n+1-t}),

where

\chi_{t,t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lvert\det\rvert^{\frac{-1+t}{2}}&\textrm{ on }G_{t}\\
1&\textrm{ on }G_{n-t}\\
\lvert\det\rvert^{\frac{1-t}{2}}&\textrm{ on }G^{\prime}_{t}\\
1&\textrm{ on }G^{\prime}_{n+1-t},\end{array}\right.

is a subrepresentation of

. This subrepresentation plays an important role in our induction procedure.

The following lemma is a direct consequence of Casselman square-integrability criterion (see
[

, Theorem 6.5.1]

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\pi
be a discrete series representation (resp. tempered representation) of

, the center of
G_{t}
acts by a character of the form
\chi\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert^{\alpha}
, where
\chi
is unitary and
\alpha>0
(resp.
\alpha\geq 0
).

3.2.
The discrete series representations

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible discrete series representation of
G_{n}
. Then every irreducible subquotient of
\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi)
is a tempered representation of
G^{\prime}_{n+1}
.

Proof.

Fix a
G^{\prime}_{n+1}
-invariant filtration of
\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi)
:

0=\Sigma_{\ell+1}\subset\Sigma_{\ell}\subset\cdots\subset\Sigma_{1}\subset\Sigma_{0}=\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi)

such that

\sigma_{i}:=\Sigma_{i}/\Sigma_{i+1}

is irreducible for all

. We argue by contradiction that all
\sigma_{i}
are tempered representations. Assume that
k
is the smallest integer such that
\sigma_{k}
is a non-tempered representation. Then there is parabolic subgroup
P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}
of

such that

\sigma_{k}\hookrightarrow{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}^{G^{\prime}_{n+1}}(\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0}),

where
\tau
is an irreducible discrete series representation of
G_{t}^{\prime}
,
s_{0}>0
, and
\rho_{0}
is an irreducible representation of
G^{\prime}_{n+1-t}
. By Frobenius reciprocity (

), we have

R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\sigma_{k})\twoheadrightarrow\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0}.

By the exactness of the Jacquet functor, we have

R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\Sigma_{\ell+1})\subset R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\Sigma_{\ell})\subset\cdots\subset R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\Sigma_{0})

with

R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\sigma_{i})=R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\Sigma_{i})/R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\Sigma_{i+1}).

Suppose that

\begin{split}\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0}&=(R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\Sigma_{k})/R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\Sigma_{k+1}))/(T/R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\Sigma_{k+1}))\\
&=R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\Sigma_{k})/T,\end{split}

then we obtain the following exact sequence of
M^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}
-modules:

(19)

0\to\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0}\to R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi))/T\to A\to 0,

where

is a finite length representation which has a filtration with successive quotients
R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\sigma_{i})
for
i<k
. A key observation is that the above exact sequence splits. Indeed, since
\sigma_{i}
is a tempered representation for

, Lemma

asserts that on each irreducible constituent of

, the center of

acts by a character of the form

with
\chi
unitary and
\alpha\geq 0
. On the other hand, the center of
G^{\prime}_{t}
acts on

by
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert^{-s_{0}t}
up to a unitary character. It follows that the sequence (

Thus one has a nonzero
M^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}
-equivariant map

R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi))\to\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0}.

By Frobenius reciprocity, one has a nonzero

-equivariant map

\omega_{n,n+1}\twoheadrightarrow\pi\boxtimes\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi)\to\pi\boxtimes{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}^{G^{\prime}_{n+1}}(\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0}).

Hence we have

\begin{split}\pi^{\vee}&\hookrightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G^{\prime}_{n+1}}(\omega_{n,n+1},{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}^{G^{\prime}_{n+1}}(\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0}))_{\infty}\\
&={\mathrm{Hom}}_{M^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\omega_{n,n+1}),\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0})_{\infty}.\end{split}

Recall that the subscript
``\infty^{\prime\prime}
stands for taking smooth vectors. By Lemma
3.3
, we have a natural restriction map

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{M^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(R_{P^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\omega_{n,n+1}),\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0})_{\infty}\to{\mathrm{Hom}}_{M^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(J^{t},\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0})_{\infty}.

We claim that the composition of the above two maps gives a nonzero
G_{n}
-equivariant map

\pi^{\vee}\hookrightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}_{M^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(J^{t},\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0})_{\infty}.

We use Casselman’s square-integrability criterion Lemma
3.4
to argue for this claim. Assume that it is not true, then there would exist a nonzero
G_{n}
-equivariant map

\pi^{\vee}\hookrightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}_{M^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(J^{i},\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0})

for some
i<t
. In particular, the latter space would be nonzero for some
i<t
. By Lemma
3.3
and (
18
), the space

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{M^{\prime}_{t-i,i}\times G^{\prime}_{n+1-t}}(\chi_{t,i}\otimes{\mathcal{S}}(G_{i})\otimes\omega_{n-i,n+1-t},R_{\overline{P^{\prime}_{t-i,i}}}(\tau)\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0})

would be nonzero. Since
\tau
is an irreducible discrete series representation, Casselman’s square-integrability criterion implies that

R_{\overline{P^{\prime}_{t-i,i}}}(\tau)=\tau_{1}\lvert\det\rvert^{t_{1}}\boxtimes\tau_{2}\lvert\det\rvert^{t_{2}}

for some irreducible discrete series representations
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
of
G^{\prime}_{t-i}
and
G^{\prime}_{i}
respectively, and

t_{1}<t_{2}\quad\textrm{and}\quad t_{1}\,\cdot\,(t-i)+t_{2}\,\cdot\,i=0.

In particular, we have
t_{1}\leq 0
. Hence, the center of
G^{\prime}_{t-i}
acts on
R_{\overline{P^{\prime}_{t-i,i}}}(\tau)\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}
by
\lvert\det\rvert^{t_{1}-s_{0}}

acts on
\chi_{t,i}\otimes{\mathcal{S}}(G_{i})\otimes\omega_{n-i,n+1-t}

\lvert\det\rvert^{\frac{1-i+t}{2}}
(see Lemma
3.3

t_{1}-s_{0}<0\quad\textrm{and}\quad 1-i+t>0,

which is a contradiction. The claim is proved.

Note that

\begin{split}&{\mathrm{Hom}}_{M^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(J^{t},\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0})_{\infty}\\
=&{\mathrm{Hom}}_{M^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}({\mathrm{Ind}}_{P_{t,n-t}\times G^{\prime}_{t}\times G^{\prime}_{n+1-t}}^{G_{n}\times M^{\prime}_{t,n+1-t}}(\chi_{t,t}\otimes{\mathcal{S}}(G_{t})\otimes\omega_{n-t,n+1-t}),\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\rho_{0})_{\infty}\\

Here we apply Lemma
3.1
in the last isomorphism.
Thus we obtain a nonzero
G_{n}
-equivariant map

\pi^{\vee}\hookrightarrow{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P_{t,n-t}}^{G_{n}}(\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G^{\prime}_{n+1-t}}(\omega_{n-t,n+1-t},\rho_{0})_{\infty}).

Again by Frobenius reciprocity, there is a nonzero
M_{t,n-t}
-equivariant map

R_{M_{t,n-t}}(\pi^{\vee})\to\tau\lvert\det\rvert^{-s_{0}}\boxtimes\pi_{0}

for some irreducible representation
\pi_{0}
of
G_{n-t}
. Since
\pi^{\vee}
is a discrete series representation, Lemma
3.4
implies that
s_{0}<0
, which is a contradiction. Therefore we complete the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible discrete series representation of
G_{n}
. If
\sigma
is an irreducible subquotient of
\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi)
, then we have
\sigma\subset{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P^{\prime}_{1,n}}^{G^{\prime}_{n+1}}(1\boxtimes\sigma_{0})
for some irreducible constituent
\sigma_{0}
of
\Theta_{n,n}(\pi)
. Here
``1^{\prime\prime}
stands for the trivial character of
G^{\prime}_{1}
.

Proof.

By the explicit theta correspondence for type II dual pair (see
[
Mi
, Theorem 1]
), we have a nonzero
G^{\prime}_{n+1}
-equivariant map

\theta_{n,n+1}(\pi)\to{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P^{\prime}_{1,n}}^{G^{\prime}_{n+1}}(1\boxtimes\theta_{n,n}(\pi)).

Let
\sigma
be an irreducible subquotient of
\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi)
. Since all irreducible subquotients of
\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi)
have the same supercuspidal support (see
[
Mu
, Lemma 4.2]
), we have
\sigma\subset{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P^{\prime}_{1,n}}^{G^{\prime}_{n+1}}(1\boxtimes\sigma_{0})
for some irreducible representation
\sigma_{0}
of

G^{\prime}_{n}
. Denote by
R_{P^{\prime}_{1,n}}(\sigma)_{(G^{\prime}_{1},1)}
the maximal
(G^{\prime}_{1},1)
-isotypic quotient of

1\boxtimes\sigma_{0}

\begin{split}R_{P^{\prime}_{1,n}}(\omega_{n,n+1})&\twoheadrightarrow\pi\boxtimes R_{P^{\prime}_{1,n}}(\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi))\\
&\twoheadrightarrow\pi\boxtimes R_{P^{\prime}_{1,n}}(\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi))_{(G^{\prime}_{1},1)}\end{split}

and the latter has
\pi\boxtimes 1\boxtimes\sigma_{0}
as a subquotient. We claim that the above map vanishes when restrict to
J^{1}
.

Suppose that

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G_{n}\times M^{\prime}_{1,n}}(J^{1},\pi\boxtimes R_{P^{\prime}_{1,n}}(\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi))_{(G^{\prime}_{1},1)})

is nonzero. Then there exists a nonzero
G_{n}\times G^{\prime}_{1}
-equivariant map

J^{1}\to\pi\boxtimes 1.

We set
t=i=1
in Lemma
3.3
to obtain

J^{1}={\mathrm{Ind}}_{P_{1,n-1}\times G_{1}^{\prime}\times G_{n}^{\prime}}^{G_{n}\times M_{1,n}^{\prime}}(\chi_{1,1}\otimes{\mathcal{S}}(G_{1})\otimes\omega_{n-1,n}),

\begin{split}{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G_{n}\times G^{\prime}_{1}}(J^{1},\pi\boxtimes 1)&={\mathrm{Hom}}_{G_{n}\times G^{\prime}_{1}}({\mathrm{Ind}}_{P_{1,n-1}\times G_{1}^{\prime}\times G_{n}^{\prime}}^{G_{n}\times M_{1,n}^{\prime}}(\chi_{1,1}\otimes{\mathcal{S}}(G_{1})\otimes\omega_{n-1,n}),\pi\boxtimes 1)\\

Here we apply Lemma
3.1
in the last isomorphism.
However, Casselman’s square-integrability criterion implies that

acts on

by
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert^{s}

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{M_{1,n-1}}(1\boxtimes\omega_{n-1,n},R_{\overline{P_{1,n-1}}}(\pi))=0,

and we get a contradiction. Hence the claim holds. On the other hand,
by Lemma
3.3
, there is an exact sequence

0\to J^{1}\to R_{P^{\prime}_{1,n}}(\omega_{n,n+1})\to 1\boxtimes\omega_{n,n}\to 0.

It follows by the claim that there is an
G^{\prime}_{n}
-equivariant surjective map

(20)

\Theta_{n,n}(\pi)\twoheadrightarrow R_{P^{\prime}_{1,n}}(\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi))_{(G^{\prime}_{1},1)}.

Thus
\sigma_{0}
is a subquotient of

Theorem 3.7
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible tempered representation of
G_{n}

\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi)
is irreducible and tempered.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
n

\pi
is a discrete series representation, then the assertion has been proved in Theorem
3.7
.

Suppose that
\pi
is tempered but not a discrete series. Then there exists an irreducible discrete representation
\tau
of
G_{k}
, and an irreducible tempered representation
\pi_{0}
of
G_{n-k}
, such that

\pi\hookrightarrow{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P_{k,n-k}}^{G_{n}}(\tau\boxtimes\pi_{0}).

It follows from Lemma

\Theta_{n,n+1}(\pi)^{\vee}\hookrightarrow{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P^{\prime}_{k,n+1-k}}^{G^{\prime}_{n+1}}(\tau\boxtimes\Theta_{n-k,n+1-k}(\pi_{0})^{\vee}).

By induction, we see that

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{P^{\prime}_{k,n+1-k}}^{G^{\prime}_{n+1}}(\tau\boxtimes\Theta_{n-k,n+1-k}(\pi_{0})^{\vee})

∎

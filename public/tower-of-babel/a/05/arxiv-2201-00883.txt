Finite-Element Domain Approximation for Maxwell Variational Problems on Curved Domains
Rubén Aylwin, Carlos Jerez-Hanckes

Abstract




1
Introduction

We consider the problem of domain approximation in finite element methods for
Maxwell’s equations on curved domains, i.e., when affine or polynomial meshes fail to cover
the domain of interest exactly, forcing to approximate the domain by a sequence
of (potentially curved) polyhedra arising from inexact meshes. In particular, we aim at finding conditions
on the quality of these approximations that ensure convergence rates of the
discrete solutions—in the approximate domains—to the continuous one in the original domain. This analysis is classical in the context of the Laplace equation
[
15
]
but has not been studied in the Maxwell case. In
[
4
]
, we showed the effects of numerical
integration on the convergence of the curl-conforming finite element method (FEM)
for Maxwell variational problems and found necessary conditions
on quadrature rules to ensure error convergence rates both in affine and curved meshes. However, we discarded the
error terms associated with domain mesh approximation.

When approximating solutions to variational problems on a given
original domain

by solutions to analogous problems on

approximate
(computational) domains, the choice of error measure is not straightforward as approximate
and exact solutions do not share the same domain. Indeed,
several choices for error measures can be considered: comparisons between extensions of continuous or discrete solutions to a
hold-all
domain
[
9
,
10
,
15
,
41
]
; mismatch measured at the intersection between the original and computational
domains
[
27
,
38
,
23
,
40
,
42
]
; mapping of solutions from computational to
the original domains or vice-versa
[
3
,
19
,
28
]
; and finally, in
[
14
,
17
]

the error is measured in a Hilbert space common to solutions on
the approximate and original domains.

In the present note, our main results are condensed in
theorems

4.18
,
4.20
,
5.8
, and
5.9

in which we estimate the convergence of Maxwell solutions in a series of approximate domains
\{\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}

to the continuous solution in a given original domain—denoted
\mathrm{D}
, and approximated by the sequence
\{\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
—in two different ways. Following
[
28
]
,
theorem

4.18
estimates the error through curl-conforming
pull-backs
mapping fields in approximate domains
\{\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}

to fields in the original one
\mathrm{D}
(
cf.

[
25
, Sec. 2.5]
). Alternatively, and in the spirit of
[
15
]
,
theorem

4.20
bounds the error of approximate
solutions to an extension of the solution in the original domain
\mathrm{D}
, allowing for its evaluation in each approximate domain
in the sequence even though one can not ensure that
\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{i}\subseteq\mathrm{D}
for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
without additional assumptions.
Then,
theorems

5.8
and
5.9
correspond to discrete analogues to
theorems

4.18
and
4.20
, respectively.
Moreover, our findings allow for a straightforward combination with our earlier results
in
[
4
]
, and so
theorems

5.12
and
5.13

correspond to fully discrete versions of
theorems

5.8
and
5.9
, respectively,
by incorporating the effects of numerical integration on error convergence rates.

The structure of the manuscript is as follows. In

section

2

considered throughout, as well as basic parameter and overarching assumptions.
In
section

3

[

displays

7
. Appendices provide proofs of various technical lemmas and results.

2.2
Functional spaces

Let
\Omega
be an open and bounded Lipschitz domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. We introduce the following functional spaces of vector-valued
functions:

\displaystyle\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega):=\left\{\mathbf{U}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\ :\ \operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{U}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\right\},

\displaystyle\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega):=\left\{\mathbf{U}\in\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega)\ :\ \operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{U}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\right\},

together with the inner product on
\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega)
:

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}\right)_{\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega)}:=\left(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}\right)_{{0},{\Omega}}+\left(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{U},\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{V}\right)_{{0},{\Omega}},

so that
\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega)
is Hilbert
[
30
, Sec. 3.5.3]
.
For
s>0
, let us define the scale of
smooth spaces
[
30
, Sec. 3.5.3]
useful to
characterize the regularity of Maxwell solutions:

\displaystyle\bm{H}^{s}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega):=\left\{\mathbf{U}\in\bm{H}^{s}(\Omega)\ :\ \operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{U}\in\bm{H}^{s}(\Omega)\right\},

with norm and semi-norm given by

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{U}\rVert_{\boldsymbol{H}^{s}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega)}:=\left(\lVert\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{U}\rVert_{s,\Omega}^{2}+\lVert\mathbf{U}\rVert_{s,\Omega}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\left|\mathbf{U}\right|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{s}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega)}:=\left(\left|\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{U}\right|_{s,\Omega}^{2}+\left|\mathbf{U}\right|_{s,\Omega}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We also require appropriate trace spaces

[
11
,
13
,
30
]
.
As in
[
11
]
, we introduce two Hilbert
spaces of tangential vector fields on
\partial\Omega
and their
duals:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\parallel}(\partial\Omega):=\{\mathbf{n}\times(\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{n})\ :\ \mathbf{U}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)\},\quad\boldsymbol{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\perp}(\partial\Omega):=\{\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{n}\ :\ \mathbf{U}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)\},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\parallel}(\partial\Omega):=\left(\boldsymbol{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\parallel}(\partial\Omega)\right)^{\prime}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\boldsymbol{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\perp}(\partial\Omega):=\left(\boldsymbol{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\perp}(\partial\Omega)\right)^{\prime}.

where
\mathbf{n}
is the outward unit normal vector on

\partial\Omega
. Trace spaces on
\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega)
are then defined through
first order differential operators on
\partial\Omega
:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\operatorname{div}}(\partial\Omega):=\{\mathbf{U}\in\boldsymbol{H}_{\parallel}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega):\;\operatorname{div}_{\partial\Omega}\mathbf{U}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)\},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\operatorname{curl}}(\partial\Omega):=\{\mathbf{U}\in\boldsymbol{H}_{\perp}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega):\;\operatorname{curl}_{\partial\Omega}\mathbf{U}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)\},

where
\operatorname{div}_{\partial\Omega}
and
\operatorname{curl}_{\partial\Omega}

are the divergence and scalar curl surface operators,
respectively (
cf.

[
12
]
and
[
33
, Sec. 2.5.6]
for detailed definitions).
Moreover, it holds that (
cf.

[
12
, Thm. 2]
)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\operatorname{curl}}(\partial\Omega)=\left(\boldsymbol{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\operatorname{div}}(\partial\Omega)\right)^{\prime}.

We define the following trace operators

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathrm{D}}:\boldsymbol{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega)\!\to\!\boldsymbol{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\partial\Omega}),\quad\gamma_{\mathrm{D}}^{\times}:\boldsymbol{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega)\!\to\!\boldsymbol{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\partial\Omega}),

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathrm{N}}:\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega)\!\to\!\boldsymbol{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\operatorname{div}}(\partial\Omega),

as the unique continuous extensions of their actions on

\mathbf{U}\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\mathbf{U}:=\mathbf{n}\times(\left.\mathbf{U}\right|_{{\partial\Omega}}\times\mathbf{n}),\quad\gamma_{\mathrm{D}}^{\times}\mathbf{U}:=\mathbf{n}\times\left.\mathbf{U}\right|_{{\partial\Omega}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\gamma_{\mathrm{N}}\mathbf{U}:=\mathbf{n}\times\left.\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{U}\right|_{{\partial\Omega}},

dubbed the Dirichlet, flipped Dirichlet trace
and Neumann traces, respectively. Range spaces are characterized as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}(\gamma_{\mathrm{D}})=\boldsymbol{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\operatorname{curl}}({\partial\Omega}),\qquad\mathrm{Im}(\gamma_{\mathrm{D}}^{\times})=\boldsymbol{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\operatorname{div}}(\partial\Omega).

Moreover, for
\mathbf{U}
and

\mathbf{V}\in\boldsymbol{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}},\Omega)
, the following Green identity holds

(\mathbf{U},\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{V})_{\Omega}-(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V})_{\Omega}=-\langle\gamma_{\mathrm{D}}^{\times}\mathbf{U},\gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\mathbf{V}\rangle_{\partial\Omega},\;

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\partial\Omega}
denotes the
duality between
\boldsymbol{H}_{\mathrm{div}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)

and
\boldsymbol{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\operatorname{curl}}(\partial\Omega)
(

[
30

and
[
11
]

The subset of
\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega)
-elements satisfying zero boundary conditions
is defined through the flipped Dirichlet trace as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega):=\{\mathbf{U}\in\boldsymbol{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega)\ :\ \gamma_{\mathrm{D}}^{\times}\mathbf{U}={\boldsymbol{0}}\;\mbox{on}\;\partial\Omega\}.

By continuity of the flipped Dirichlet trace,
\bm{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega)

is a closed subspace of
\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\Omega)
.

2.3
Maxwell variational problems

Let
\mathrm{D}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be an open, bounded domain
with boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\mathrm{D}
of class
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{M}}
for some

\mathfrak{M}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For a circular frequency
\omega>0
and time
dependency
e^{\imath\omega t}
, the time-harmonic Maxwell equations on
\mathrm{D}
read

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{E}+\imath\omega\mu\mathbf{H}&={\boldsymbol{0}},\\
\imath\omega{\varepsilon}\mathbf{E}-\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{H}&=-\mathbf{J},\end{aligned}

(2.1)

where
\mathbf{E}
and
\mathbf{H}
belong to
\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\mathrm{D})
and represent the electric and magnetic fields, respectively. The magnetic permeability
\mu
and electric permittivity
{\varepsilon}

are assumed to be symmetric matrix-valued functions with coefficients in

{L}^{\infty}(\mathrm{D})
, and
\mathbf{J}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{D})

is an imposed current in
\mathrm{D}
.

The system
eq.

2.1
is converted into a second order system for

\mathbf{E}
or
\mathbf{H}
by eliminating the remaining field, requiring pointwise invertibility assumptions on either
{\varepsilon}
or
\mu
depending on the specific
choice. Without loss of generality, we follow
[
4
]
and consider the system for
the electric field only, assuming the existence of a pointwise inverse of
\mu
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}=\imath\frac{1}{\omega}\mu^{-1}\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{E},

from where

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mu^{-1}\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{E}-\omega^{2}{\varepsilon}\mathbf{E}=-\imath\omega\mathbf{J}.

(2.2)

The system is completed by imposing boundary
conditions on traces of
\mathbf{E}
, e.g.,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathrm{D}}^{\times}\mathbf{E}=\bm{g}_{D},\quad\mbox{or}\quad\mathbf{n}\times(\mu^{-1}\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{E})=\bm{g}_{N},

for
\bm{g}_{D}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\operatorname{div}}(\partial\Omega)
or

\bm{g}_{N}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}}(\partial\Omega)
.

We proceed by considering the system
eq.

2.2
with
perfect electric conductor (PEC) boundary conditions, i.e., homogeneous (flipped) Dirichlet boundary conditions given by
\gamma_{\mathrm{D}}^{\times}\mathbf{E}={\boldsymbol{0}}
.
The associated sesquilinear and antilinear forms on
\bm{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\mathrm{D})

for the Maxwell PEC cavity problem, respectively, are

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathrm{D}}\mu^{-1}\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{U}\cdot\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\overline{\mathbf{V}}-\omega^{2}\epsilon\mathbf{U}\cdot\overline{\mathbf{V}}\,{\rm d}\mathbf{x}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{V}):=-\imath\omega\int_{\mathrm{D}}\mathbf{J}\cdot\overline{\mathbf{V}}\,{\rm d}\mathbf{x},

(2.3)

which are continuous on
\boldsymbol{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\mathrm{D})
if we assume

\mu^{-1}
has coefficients in
{L}^{\infty}(\mathrm{D})
. Then, the problem under consideration reads:

Problem 2.1

(Continuous variational problem)
.

Find
\mathbf{E}\in\boldsymbol{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\mathrm{D})
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{V})=\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{V}),

for all
\mathbf{V}\in\boldsymbol{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\mathrm{D})
.

In this work, we are concerned with the approximation of

\mathrm{D}
, the
original domain
, by computational domains
\{\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}

and its consequences on the FEM error convergence rates.
Hence, we take for granted the necessary conditions for
the unique solvability of
2.1
.

Assumption 2.2

(Wellposedness)
.

We assume the sesquilinear form
\Phi
in (
2.3
)
satisfies the following conditions:

\displaystyle\left|{\Phi}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V})\right|<C_{1}\lVert\mathbf{U}\rVert_{\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\mathrm{D})}\lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert_{\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\mathrm{D})}

\displaystyle\qquad\forall\;\mathbf{U},\;\mathbf{V}\in\bm{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};{\mathrm{D}}),

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{U}\in\bm{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\mathrm{D})\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{0}}\}}\left|\Phi(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V})\right|>0

\displaystyle\qquad\forall\;\mathbf{V}\in\bm{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\mathrm{D})\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{0}}\},

and

\inf_{\mathbf{U}\in\bm{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\mathrm{D})\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{0}}\}}\left(\sup_{\mathbf{V}\in\bm{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\mathrm{D})\setminus\{{\boldsymbol{0}}\}}\frac{\left|\Phi(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V})\right|}{\lVert\mathbf{U}\rVert_{\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\mathrm{D})}\lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert_{\bm{H}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\mathrm{D})}}\right)\geq C_{2},

for positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
.

We denote the unique solution of
2.1
as

\mathbf{E}\in\bm{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};\mathrm{D})
. Lastly, for examples of problems satisfying

2.2
we refer to
[
5
,
21
,
29
,
30
]
.

7
Conclusions

For the family of Maxwell variational problems here considered,
theorems

4.18
and
4.20

provide sufficient conditions on the family of approximate domains
\{\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}

to ensure convergence rates of: (i) pull-backs of continuous solutions in approximate domains to those in the original one; and, (ii)
continuous solutions in approximate domains to smooth extensions of the exact solution.

theorems

5.8
and
5.8
extend these results to their
discrete counterparts to then include the effects of numerical integration for a fully discrete analysis in
theorems

5.12
and
5.12
, based on our previous work
[
4
]
.

Our results on curved meshes established various properties of (local) interpolation on curved meshes
and pull-backs
\psi^{c}
in (
3.5
). These correspond to
lemmas

A.2
and
A.3
and
propositions

3.6
and
3.7
, which are of
independent interest. Also the simple numerical examples in
section

6
confirm our findings. Observe the failure of second-order polynomials to achieve second-order convergence rates to the solution on straight meshes in
fig.

4
.

We left out the issue of demonstrating the regularities of the electric field that are required in
order to ensure different rates of convergence (
cf.

[
1
]
for globally smooth boundaries). However, results ensuring
arbitrary degrees of regularity in domains with corners and allowing for an application of the results
as in
[
8
]
, are, to the best of our knowledge,
unavailable for Maxwell’s equations.

Finally, we were able to consider integer degrees of regularity only in

theorems

5.9
and
5.13
(
s\in\{1:k\}
).
This stems from the same deficiency in
lemma

A.2
and further
improvements are left as future work as well as extensions to more specific and
varied Maxwell variational problems such as problems in periodic media,
FEM/BEM couplings and applications in uncertainty quantification
[
6
,
5
,
36
]
.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
3.3
hold and let
\mathbf{U}\in\bm{H}^{s}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};{\breve{K}})
for
s\in\{1:k\}
. Then, for any
l\in\{1:s\}
it holds that,

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{U}-\breve{\bm{r}}(\mathbf{U})\rVert_{l,{\breve{K}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left|\mathbf{U}\right|_{s,{\breve{K}}}+\left|\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{U}\right|_{s,{\breve{K}}}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lVert\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{U}-\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\breve{\bm{r}}(\mathbf{U})\rVert_{l,{\breve{K}}}\leq C\left|\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{U}\right|_{s,{\breve{K}}},

for a positive constant

independent of
\mathbf{U}\in\bm{H}^{s}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};{\breve{K}})
.

Proof.

Take
s\in\{1:k\}
and
l\in\{1:s\}
. We will prove only the estimate

since the remaining estimate follows by analogous arguments.
Let
\mathbf{U}\in\bm{H}^{s}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};{\breve{K}})
and take any
\bm{\phi}\in\mathbb{P}_{k-1}({\breve{K}};{\mathbb{C}}^{3})

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{U}-\breve{\bm{r}}(\mathbf{U})\rVert_{l,{\breve{K}}}=\lVert(\operatorname{\mathsf{I}}-\breve{\bm{r}})(\mathbf{U}+\bm{\phi})\rVert_{l,{\breve{K}}}\leq\lVert\mathbf{U}+\bm{\phi}\rVert_{l,{\breve{K}}}+\lVert\breve{\bm{r}}(\mathbf{U}+\bm{\phi})\rVert_{l,{\breve{K}}}.

(A.6)

Lemma 5.38 in
[
30
]
then allows us to bound the degrees of freedom of
(U+\bm{\phi})
through the
\bm{H}^{s}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};{\breve{K}})
-norm (since

\displaystyle\lVert\breve{\bm{r}}(\mathbf{U}+\bm{\phi})\rVert_{l,{\breve{K}}}\leq C\left(\lVert\mathbf{U}+\bm{\phi}\rVert_{s,{\breve{K}}}+\lVert\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}(\mathbf{U}+\bm{\phi})\rVert_{s,{\breve{K}}}\right),

(A.7)

where
C
may depend on
l\in\{1:s\}
and
s\in\{1:k\}
, but is independent of
\mathbf{U}
and
\bm{\phi}
.
A combination of
eqs.

A.6

A.7

, yields

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{U}-\breve{\bm{r}}(\mathbf{U})\rVert_{l,{\breve{K}}}\leq C\left(\lVert\mathbf{U}+\bm{\phi}\rVert_{s,{\breve{K}}}+\lVert\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}(\mathbf{U}+\bm{\phi})\rVert_{s,{\breve{K}}}\right),

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{U}-\breve{\bm{r}}(\mathbf{U})\rVert_{l,{\breve{K}}}\leq C\left(\left|\mathbf{U}\right|_{s,{\breve{K}}}+\left|\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\mathbf{U}\right|_{s,{\breve{K}}}\right),

[

.

Proof of

.

For

3.3

Proof of

.

\mathbf{U}\in\bm{H}^{s}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}};K)

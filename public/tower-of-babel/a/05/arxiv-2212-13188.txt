An Axiomatic Viewpoint on the Rogers–Veraart and Suzuki–Elsinger Models of Systemic Risk
Yuri Kabanov, Arthur Sidorenko

Abstract
We study a model of clearing in an interbank network with cross-holdings and default charges. Following the Eisenberg–Noe approach, we define the model via a set of natural financial regulations including those related with eventual default charges and derive a finite family of fixpoint problems. These problems are parameterized by vectors of binary variables. Our model combines features of the Ararat–Meimanjanov, Rogers–Veraart, and Suzuki–Elsinger networks. We develop methods of computing the maximal and minimal clearing pairs using the mixed integer-linear programming and a Gaussian elimination algorithm.

Introduction

In a financial system with strongly interconnected institutions, a shock touching even a small number of entities can propagate through the network and lead to substantial losses. Clearing is a procedure that diminishes the total amount of interbank liabilities and, as such, decreases the risk of a system-wide breakdown. In the seminal paper
eisenberg2001systemic
by Eisenberg–Noe, the notion of clearing vectors was introduced in an axiomatic way, via limited liability and absolute priority rules. It was shown that the set of clearing vectors is the set of fixed points of a simple nonlinear mapping and this set contains the maximal and minimal elements. The further development led to more sophisticated models including cross-holdings, seniority of debts, and default charges, see, e.g.

suzuki2002valuing
,
elsinger2009financial
,
RV2013
, and a survey paper
kabanov2018clearing
. Dynamic versions of the Eisenberg–Noe network are suggested, for instance, in
feinstein2019dynamic
and
djete2021mean
.

The adding of default fees, introduced in the paper by Rogers and Veraart
RV2013
, is of particular interest because it allows to study the problem of a rescue consortium to aid insolvent institutions.
In general, default charges have a crisis amplifying effect: external pay-offs may increase the number
of failures.
In the Rogers–Veraart model the clearing vectors were defined directly as solutions of a fixpoint equation. As was observed by Ararat and Meimanjanov in
Ararat2019
, such a formulation does not coincide with a formulation defined in terms of financial regulations.

In this note we consider a model with default charges and cross-holdings using the Ararat–Meimanjanov
“axiomatic” approach. Our aim is to derive the fixpoint equations and suggest algorithms to find their minimal and maximal solutions. In particular, we consider a method using the mixed integer-linear programming
and a Gaussian-type dimensionality reduction algorithm.

. Then

(otherwise

).

. Since

.

Let

Lemma 3.5

The vector

. In particular,

Proof.

4
Gaussian method

In this section we present a Gaussian-type algorithm to find the maximal clearing vector in a model with crossholding and
default charges assuming that
\alpha=\beta=\gamma
and
||\Theta||_{\infty}<1
,
i.e.
\sum_{j}\theta^{ij}<1
for every
i
.

In such a case the mappings
\Phi_{\bf 1}:[0,l]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}\to[0,l]
and
G_{+}:[0,l]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}
are given by the formulae

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bf 1}(p,V)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\alpha(e+\Pi^{\prime}p+\Theta^{\prime}V)\circ{\bf 1}_{D}+l\circ{\bf 1}_{\bar{D}},

\displaystyle G_{+}(p,V)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(e+\Pi^{\prime}p+\Theta^{\prime}V-l)^{+}\circ{\bf 1}_{\bar{D}},

where
D:=\{e+\Pi^{\prime}p+\Theta^{\prime}V<l\}
.

Our aim is to find the maximal solution of the system

\displaystyle p=\Phi_{\bf 1}(p,V),

(4.14)

\displaystyle V=G_{+}(p,V).

(4.15)

existing in virtue of the Knaster–Tarski theorem.

In the case where

(e+\Pi^{\prime}l+\Theta^{\prime}H(l))^{i}\geq l^{i}
for all
i
, the maximal clearing pair is

(l,H(l))
. Of course, to check the condition one needs to compute the value
H(l)
which is the unique solution of the system
v=(c(l)+\tilde{\Theta}v)^{+}
with
c(l):=e+P^{\prime}l-l
. It is known that the latter system
can be solved in a finite number of steps, e.g., by a Gaussian-type algorithms,
kabanov2018clearing
.

In the opposite case where at least one inequality fails to be true, we assume, without loss of generality, that
e^{1}+(\Pi^{\prime}l)^{1}+(\Theta^{\prime}V(l))^{1}<l^{1}
and
V^{1}=0
. The first equation becomes linear:

\alpha e^{1}+\alpha(\Pi^{\prime}p)^{1}+\alpha(\Theta^{\prime}V)^{1}=p^{1}.

(4.16)

The vector equation
p=\Phi_{\bf 1}(p,V)
can be written as the system

\displaystyle p^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha e^{1}+\alpha\pi^{11}p^{1}+\alpha T^{\prime}\tilde{p}+\alpha M^{\prime}\tilde{V},

\displaystyle\tilde{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(\tilde{e}+p^{1}R^{\prime}+\tilde{\Pi}^{\prime}\tilde{p}+\tilde{\Theta}^{\prime}\tilde{V})\circ\tilde{1}_{D}+\tilde{l}\circ\tilde{1}_{\bar{D}},

where
\tilde{\Pi}:=(\pi^{ij})_{i,j=2}^{N}
,
R:=(\pi^{1j})_{j=2}^{N}
,
T:=(\pi^{i1})_{i=2}^{N}
,
\tilde{\Theta}:=(\theta^{ij})_{i,j=2}^{N}
,
M:=(\theta^{i1})_{i=2}^{N}
, and the tilde indicates vectors with the removed first component.

If
\alpha\pi^{11}\neq 1
, then

p^{1}=\alpha(1-\alpha\pi^{11})^{-1}(e^{1}+T^{\prime}\tilde{p}+M^{\prime}\tilde{V}),

(4.17)

Substituting this expression, we get that

\tilde{e}+p^{1}R^{\prime}+\tilde{\Pi}^{\prime}\tilde{p}+\tilde{\Theta}^{\prime}\tilde{V}=e_{1}+\Pi^{\prime}_{1}\tilde{p}+\Theta^{\prime}_{1}\tilde{V},

where

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}+\alpha(1-\alpha\pi^{11})^{-1}TR,

\displaystyle\Theta_{1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\tilde{\Theta}+\alpha(1-\alpha\pi^{11})^{-1}MR,

\displaystyle e_{1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\tilde{e}+\alpha(1-\alpha\pi^{11})^{-1}e^{1}R^{\prime}.

It follows that
(\tilde{p},\tilde{V})
is the solution of the system

\displaystyle\tilde{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(e_{1}+\Pi^{\prime}_{1}\tilde{p}+\Theta^{\prime}_{1}\tilde{V})\circ\tilde{1}_{D}+\tilde{l}\circ\tilde{1}_{\bar{D}},

\displaystyle\tilde{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(e_{1}+\Pi^{\prime}_{1}\tilde{p}+\Theta^{\prime}_{1}\tilde{V}-\tilde{l})^{+}\circ\tilde{1}_{\bar{D}}.

Note that
TR\tilde{\bf 1}=T(R\tilde{\bf 1})=((\Pi{\bf 1})^{1}-\pi^{11})T
and, therefore,

\Pi_{1}\tilde{\bf 1}=\tilde{\Pi}\tilde{\bf 1}+(\alpha(\Pi\tilde{\bf 1})^{1}-\alpha\pi^{11})(1-\alpha\pi^{11})^{-1}T\leq\tilde{\Pi}\tilde{\bf 1}+T=\widetilde{\Pi{\bf 1}}\leq\tilde{\bf 1}.

Thus, the matrix
\Pi_{1}
is substochastic. By the same arguments we get that
||\Theta_{1}||_{\infty}<1
.
Moreover,
\tilde{\bf 1}_{D}={\bf 1}_{D_{1}}
, where
D_{1}:=\{e_{1}+\Pi^{\prime}_{1}\tilde{p}+\Theta^{\prime}_{1}\tilde{V}<l\}
.

If
\alpha=1
and
\pi^{11}=1
, then we can take
p^{1}=l^{1}
and continue with the system

\displaystyle\tilde{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(e_{1}+\Pi^{\prime}_{1}\tilde{p}+\Theta^{\prime}_{1}\tilde{V})\circ\tilde{1}_{D}+\tilde{l}\circ\tilde{1}_{\bar{D}},

\displaystyle\tilde{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(e_{1}+\Pi^{\prime}_{1}\tilde{p}+\Theta^{\prime}_{1}\tilde{V}-\tilde{l})^{+}\circ\tilde{1}_{\bar{D}},

where
e_{1}:=\tilde{e}+e^{1}R^{\prime}
,
\Pi_{1}:=\tilde{P}
,
\Theta_{1}:=\tilde{\Theta}
.

In both cases we reduced the
2N
-dimensional problem to find the maximal solution to
2(N-1)
-dimensional
one of the same type.

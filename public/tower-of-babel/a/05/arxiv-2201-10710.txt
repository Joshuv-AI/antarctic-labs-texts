Invariants from the Sweedler power maps on integrals
Zhihua Wang, Gongxiang Liu, Libin Li

Abstract
. For a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra A A with a nonzero left integral Λ \Lambda , we investigate a relationship between P n ​ ( Λ ) P_{n}(\Lambda) and P n J ​ ( Λ ) P_{n}^{J}(\Lambda) , where P n P_{n} and P n J P_{n}^{J} are respectively the n n -th Sweedler power maps of A A and the twisted Hopf algebra A J A^{J} . We use this relation to give several invariants of the representation category Rep ( A ) (A) considered as a tensor category. As applications, we distinguish the representation categories of 12-dimensional pointed nonsemisimple Hopf algebras. Also, these invariants are sufficient to distinguish the representation categories Rep ( K 8 ) (K_{8}) , Rep ( 𝕜 ​ Q 8 ) (\mathbbm{k}Q_{8}) and Rep ( 𝕜 ​ D 4 ) (\mathbbm{k}D_{4}) , although they have been completely distinguished by their Frobenius-Schur indicators. We further reveal a relationship between the right integrals λ \lambda in A ∗ A^{*} and λ J \lambda^{J} in ( A J ) ∗ (A^{J})^{*} . This can be used to give a uniform proof of the remarkable result which says that the n n -th indicator ν n ​ ( A ) \nu_{n}(A) is a gauge invariant of A A for any n ∈ ℤ n\in\mathbb{Z} . We also use the expression for λ J \lambda^{J} to give an alternative proof of the known result that the Killing form of the Hopf algebra A A is invariant under twisting. As a result, the dimension of the Killing radical of A A is a gauge invariant of A A .

1.
Introduction

It is very interesting to know when two finite-dimensional Hopf algebras are gauge equivalent, in the sense that the categories of their finite-dimensional representations are equivalent as tensor categories. It can be seen from
[
12
, Theorem 2.2]
that two finite-dimensional Hopf algebras
A
and
A^{\prime}
are gauge equivalent if and only if there exists a twist
J
on
A
such that
A^{\prime}
and the twisted Hopf algebra
A^{J}
are isomorphic as bialgebras. However, to determine the existence or nonexistence of such twists and isomorphisms is still highly non-trivial.

A quantity
\kappa(A)
defined for any finite-dimensional
Hopf algebra
A
is called a gauge invariant of
A
if
\kappa(A^{\prime})=\kappa(A)

for any Hopf algebra
A^{\prime}
gauge equivalent to
A
. For instance, the dimension, the quasi-exponent (see
[
2
]
) and the
n
-th indicator (see
[
14
]
) of any finite-dimensional Hopf algebra are gauge invariants. We refer to
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
11
,
19
]
for more details about gauge invariants of Hopf algebras. These invariants are used as powerful tools to distinguish representation categories of Hopf algebras. However, more general but computable invariants are still needed to be discovered.

The present paper is specifically concerned with those invariants raised from the images of the Sweedler power maps on integrals.
For any finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
A
with a nonzero left integral
\Lambda
and a normalized twist
J
, the present paper is dedicated to a study of the relationship between
P_{n}(\Lambda)
and
P_{n}^{J}(\Lambda)
, where
P_{n}
and
P_{n}^{J}
are respectively the
n
-th Sweedler power maps of the Hopf algebra
A
and the twisted Hopf algebra
A^{J}
. We show that

P^{J}_{n}(\Lambda)=TP_{n}(\Lambda)\ \text{for}\ n\in\mathbb{Z},

where
T
is an invertible element of
A
associated to the twist
J
(see Theorem
3.4
).
There are some invariants that can be derived from this equality. For instance, we show that the homogeneous polynomials vanishing on some
P_{n}(\Lambda)
are invariants of the representation category Rep
(A)
(see Theorem
4.1
). As an application, we show that the representation categories of 12-dimensional pointed nonsemisimple Hopf algebras classified in
[
10
]
are mutually inequivalent as tensor categories.

If, moreover,
A
is unimodular, then
T=1
and hence
P^{J}_{n}(\Lambda)=P_{n}(\Lambda)
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, namely,
P_{n}(\Lambda)
is invariant under twisting. Even in this case, the (not necessarily homogeneous) polynomials vanishing on some
P_{n}(\Lambda)
are in general not invariants of Rep
(A)
. However, this kind of polynomials can also be used to distinguish the representation categories of Hopf algebras in a certain sense (see Proposition
4.2
). If
A
is semisimple and
\Lambda
is idempotent, then the polynomials vanishing on some
P_{n}(\Lambda)
are indeed invariants of the representation category Rep
(A)
. We use this approach to distinguish the representation categories Rep
(K_{8})
, Rep
(\mathbbm{k}Q_{8})
and Rep
(\mathbbm{k}D_{4})
whereas they have the same fusion rules, we refer to
[
15
]
and
[
12
]
for other approaches to distinguish them.

Note that these
P_{n}(\Lambda)
are dependent on the choice of
\Lambda
. The values that characters of finite-dimensional
A
-modules taking on them are not gauge invariants. In view of this, we turn to consider the ratio

\chi_{V}(P_{n}(\Lambda)):\chi_{W}(P_{m}(\Lambda))

for finite-dimensional
A
-modules
V
and
W
.
When
A
is unimodular and
\chi_{W}(P_{m}(\Lambda))\neq 0
, we show that this ratio is a gauge invariant of
A
. This invariant can be regarded as a slightly generalization of the
n
-th FS-indicator defined for any semisimple Hopf algebra. Indeed, if
A
is semisimple and
W
is chosen to be the trivial
A
-module
\mathbbm{k}
, then the above ratio becomes
\chi_{V}(P_{n}(\Lambda/\varepsilon(\Lambda)))
, which is the
n
-th FS-indicator of
V
(see
[
5
,
8
]
).

We further describe a relationship between the right integrals
\lambda
in
A^{*}
and
\lambda^{J}
in
(A^{J})^{*}
. We show that

\lambda^{J}=\lambda\leftharpoonup S^{2}(R^{-1})S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J},

where
R
and
Q
are invertible elements of
A
associated to the twist
J
. This generalizes the result of
[
1
, Theorem 3.4]
for a unimodular Hopf algebra.
We use this formula to give a uniform proof of the remarkable result that the
n
-th indicator
\nu_{n}(A)
of
A
is a gauge invariant for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We also use this formula to give an alternative proof of the known result that the Killing form of the Hopf algebra
A
is invariant under twisting. This gives rise to a result that the dimension of the Killing radical of
A
is a gauge invariant of
A
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some basic facts about Hopf algebras and twisted Hopf algebras. In Section 3, we mainly investigate a relationship between
P^{J}_{n}(\Lambda)
and
P_{n}(\Lambda)
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The importance of the relationship is illustrated in Section 4: Some invariants of representation categories of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras can be deduced from the relationship and then they are used to distinguish the representation categories of some Hopf algebras. In Section 5, we show that a right integral
\lambda^{J}
in
(A^{J})^{*}
can be expressed in terms of a right integral
\lambda
in
A^{*}
. This enables us to give a uniform proof of the result that the
n
-th indicator
\nu_{n}(A)
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is a gauge invariant of
A
. This also enables us to give an alternative proof of the known result that the Killing form of the Hopf algebra
A
is invariant under twisting.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper we work over a fixed base field
\mathbbm{k}
. Hopf algebras, vector spaces,
and tensor products are understood to be over
\mathbbm{k}
. For a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
A
over the field
\mathbbm{k}
, we use Sweedler notation in the form

\Delta(a)=a_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}
for comultiplication
\Delta
of
A
and write
S
for the antipode of
A
. The inverse of
S
under composition is denoted by
S^{-1}
.
We fix a left integral
\Lambda
in
A
and a right integral
\lambda
in
A^{*}
such that
\lambda(\Lambda)=1
. We denote
\alpha
to be the distinguished group-like element in
A^{*}
defined by
\Lambda a=\alpha(a)\Lambda
for
a\in A
. The category of finite-dimensional left
A
-modules is denoted by Rep
(A)
, which is a rigid tensor category.

The following equalities are of fundamental importance in this paper. The first equality can be found in
[
7
, Lemma 1.2]
:

(2.1)

S(a)\Lambda_{(1)}\otimes\Lambda_{(2)}=\Lambda_{(1)}\otimes a\Lambda_{(2)}\ \text{for}\ a\in A.

The second can be found in
[
4
]
:

(2.2)

\Lambda_{(1)}a\otimes\Lambda_{(2)}=\Lambda_{(1)}\otimes\Lambda_{(2)}\alpha(a_{(1)})S(a_{(2)})\ \text{for}\ a\in A.

For
a,b\in A
, the integral
\lambda
satisfies the following properties:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\lambda(ab)=\lambda(\alpha(b_{(1)})S^{2}(b_{(2)})a),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lambda(ab_{(1)})b_{(2)}=\lambda(a_{(1)}b)S^{-1}(a_{(2)}),

where the former follows from
[
13
, Theorem 3(a)]
and the latter follows from
[
1
, Remark 3.2]
.
For any
\mathbbm{k}
-linear map
f:A\rightarrow A
, the trace of
f
can be described by Radford’s trace formula (see
[
13
, Theorem 2]
):

(2.5)

\text{tr}(f)=\lambda(S(\Lambda_{(2)})f(\Lambda_{(1)})).

Recall from
[
6
]
that the
n
-th Sweedler power map
P_{n}:A\rightarrow A
is the
n
-th convolution power of the identity map
id_{A}
of
A
. Namely,

P_{n}(a)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{(1)}\cdots a_{(n)},&n\geq 1;\\
\varepsilon(a),&n=0;\\
S(a_{(-n)}\cdots a_{(1)}),&n\leq-1.\end{array}\right.

Note that
P_{1}(a)=a
and
P_{-1}(a)=S(a)
for
a\in A
.

Recall that a normalized twist for a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
A
is an invertible element
J\in A\otimes A
which satisfies
(\varepsilon\otimes id)(J)=(id\otimes\varepsilon)(J)=1
and

(2.6)

(\Delta\otimes id)(J)(J\otimes 1)=(id\otimes\Delta)(J)(1\otimes J).

We write
J=J^{(1)}\otimes J^{(2)}
and
J^{-1}=J^{-(1)}\otimes J^{-(2)}
, where the summation is understood. We also write
J_{21}=J^{(2)}\otimes J^{(1)}
.

Given a normalized twist
J
for
A
one can define a new Hopf algebra
A^{J}
with the same algebra structure and counit as
A
, for which the comultiplication
\Delta^{J}
and antipode
S^{J}
are given respectively by

\Delta^{J}(a)=J^{-1}\Delta(a)J,\ \ \ \ S^{J}(a)=Q^{-1}_{J}S(a)Q_{J}\ \text{for}\ a\in A,

where
Q_{J}=S(J^{(1)})J^{(2)}
, which is an invertible element of
A
with the inverse
Q_{J}^{-1}=J^{-(1)}S(J^{-(2)})
.

The element
Q_{J}
satisfies the following identity (see
[
1
, Eq (5)]
):

(2.7)

\Delta(Q_{J})=(S\otimes S)(J^{-1}_{21})(Q_{J}\otimes Q_{J})J^{-1}.

This implies that

(2.8)

\Delta(Q_{J}^{-1})=J(Q_{J}^{-1}\otimes Q_{J}^{-1})(S\otimes S)(J_{21}).

The following three equalities follow from
[
1
, Lemma 2.4]
:

(2.9)

\displaystyle(1\otimes Q_{J}^{-1})(S\otimes S)(J_{21})=(1\otimes J^{-(1)})\Delta(S(J^{-(2)})),

(2.10)

\displaystyle J_{(1)}^{(1)}\otimes S(J^{(1)}_{(2)})J^{(2)}=J^{-(1)}\otimes S(J^{-(2)})Q_{J},

(2.11)

\displaystyle J^{-(1)}_{(1)}\otimes J^{-(1)}_{(2)}S(J^{-(2)})=J^{(1)}\otimes J^{(2)}Q_{J}^{-1}.

We define
\Delta^{(1)}=id_{A}
and
\Delta^{(n+1)}=(id_{A}\otimes\Delta^{(n)})\circ\Delta
for all
n\geq 1
. Note that a nonzero left integral
\Lambda
of
A
is also a left integral of
A^{J}
. We denote

\Delta^{(n)}(\Lambda)=\Lambda_{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Lambda_{(n)}\ \text{and}\ (\Delta^{J})^{(n)}(\Lambda)=\Lambda_{\langle 1\rangle}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Lambda_{\langle n\rangle}

to distinguish between
\Delta^{(n)}(\Lambda)
and
(\Delta^{J})^{(n)}(\Lambda).
The
n
-th Sweedler power map of
A^{J}
is denoted by
P_{n}^{J}
. Then
P_{n}^{J}(a)
can be written as

P^{J}_{n}(a)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{\langle 1\rangle}\cdots a_{\langle n\rangle},&n\geq 1;\\
\varepsilon(a),&n=0;\\
S^{J}(a_{\langle-n\rangle}\cdots a_{\langle 1\rangle}),&n\leq-1.\end{array}\right.

3.
The Sweedler power maps on integrals

In this section, we first give a property of
P_{n}(\Lambda)
for a left integral
\Lambda
in
A
. We then use this property to describe a relationship between
P_{n}^{J}(\Lambda)
and
P_{n}(\Lambda)
for a normalized twist
J
of
A
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over the field
\mathbbm{k}
with a nonzero left integral
\Lambda
. For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a\in A
, we have
aP_{n}(\Lambda)=P_{n}(\Lambda)a^{{\dagger}}
, where
a^{{\dagger}}=a_{(1)}(\alpha\circ S^{-1})(a_{(2)}).

Moreover, if
A
is unimodular, then
P_{n}(\Lambda)
is a central element of
A
.

Proof.

Obviously, the desired result holds for the cases
n=0,\pm 1
. For the case
n>1
, we have

\displaystyle aP_{n}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=a\Lambda_{(1)}P_{n-1}(\Lambda_{(2)})

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{(1)}P_{n-1}(S^{-1}(a)\Lambda_{(2)})\ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ004})

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{(1)}(S^{-1}(a))_{(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}(S^{-1}(a))_{(2)}\cdots\Lambda_{(n-1)}(S^{-1}(a))_{(n-1)}\Lambda_{(n)}

\displaystyle=P_{n-1}(\Lambda_{(1)}S^{-1}(a))\Lambda_{(2)}

\displaystyle=P_{n-1}(\Lambda_{(1)})\Lambda_{(2)}a_{(1)}\alpha(S^{-1}(a_{(2)}))\ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ005})

\displaystyle=P_{n}(\Lambda)a^{{\dagger}}.

For the case
n<-1
, we have

\displaystyle aP_{n}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=S(\Lambda_{(1)}S^{-1}(a))P_{n+1}(\Lambda_{(2)})

\displaystyle=S(\Lambda_{(1)})P_{n+1}(\Lambda_{(2)}a^{{\dagger}})\ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ005})

\displaystyle=S(\Lambda_{(1)})S(\Lambda_{(-n)}a^{{\dagger}}_{(-n-1)}\cdots\Lambda_{(2)}a^{{\dagger}}_{(1)})

\displaystyle=S(a^{{\dagger}}_{(-n-1)}\Lambda_{(-n-1)}\cdots a^{{\dagger}}_{(1)}\Lambda_{(1)})S(\Lambda_{(-n)})

\displaystyle=P_{n+1}(a^{{\dagger}}\Lambda_{(1)})S(\Lambda_{(2)})

\displaystyle=P_{n+1}(\Lambda_{(1)})S(S^{-1}(a^{{\dagger}})\Lambda_{(2)})\ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ004})

\displaystyle=S(\Lambda_{(-n-1)}\cdots\Lambda_{(1)})S(\Lambda_{(-n)})a^{{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=P_{n}(\Lambda)a^{{\dagger}}.

If
A
is unimodular, then
\alpha=\varepsilon
and hence
a^{{\dagger}}=a
. In this case,
P_{n}(\Lambda)
is a central element of
A
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

For any
a\in A
, we define a family of elements
a_{k}\in A
recursively by

(3.1)

a_{1}=a_{(1)}(\alpha\circ S^{-1})(a_{(2)})\ \text{and}\ a_{k+1}=(a_{k})_{(1)}(\alpha\circ S^{-1})((a_{k})_{(2)})\ \text{for}\ k\geq 1.

By induction on
k
, we may see that
a(P_{n}(\Lambda))^{k}=(P_{n}(\Lambda))^{k}a_{k}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}.

This further implies that

(aP_{n}(\Lambda))^{k}=(P_{n}(\Lambda))^{k}a_{k}\cdots a_{2}a_{1}.

Moreover, for positive integers
k_{1},\cdots,k_{s}
and integers
n_{1},\cdots,n_{s},
we have

(3.2)

(aP_{n_{1}}(\Lambda))^{k_{1}}\cdots(aP_{n_{s}}(\Lambda))^{k_{s}}=(P_{n_{1}}(\Lambda))^{k_{1}}\cdots(P_{n_{s}}(\Lambda))^{k_{s}}a_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{s}}\cdots a_{2}a_{1}.

To describe a relationship between
P_{n}^{J}(\Lambda)
and
P_{n}(\Lambda)
, we need the following lemma:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over the field
\mathbbm{k}
with a normalized twist
J
and a nonzero left integral
\Lambda
. Denote by
R=\alpha(J^{-(1)})J^{-(2)}
and
T=J^{-(1)}\alpha(J^{-(2)}).
For any integer number
n\geq 2
, we have

(1)

\Lambda_{\langle 1\rangle}\otimes\Lambda_{\langle 2\rangle}\Lambda_{\langle 3\rangle}\cdots\Lambda_{\langle n\rangle}

=Q_{J}^{-1}\Lambda_{(1)}J^{(1)}\otimes J^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}P_{n-2}\bigg(J^{(2)}J^{-(2)}\Lambda_{(3)}\bigg)S(R)Q_{J}.

(2)

\Lambda_{\langle n\rangle}\otimes\Lambda_{\langle n-1\rangle}\Lambda_{\langle n-2\rangle}\cdots\Lambda_{\langle 1\rangle}

=S^{-1}(Q_{J}^{-1})\Lambda_{(3)}J^{(2)}\otimes J^{-(2)}\Lambda_{(2)}\bigg(S^{-1}\circ P_{2-n}\bigg)\bigg(J^{(1)}J^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(1)}\bigg)S^{-1}(T)S^{-1}(Q_{J}).

Proof.

We only give a proof of Part (1) and the proof of Part (2) is similar to that of Part (1).
We proceed by induction on
n
. For the case
n=2
,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\langle 1\rangle}\otimes\Lambda_{\langle 2\rangle}

\displaystyle=\Delta^{J}(\Lambda)=J^{-1}\Delta(\Lambda)J=J^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(1)}J^{(1)}\otimes J^{-(2)}\Lambda_{(2)}J^{(2)}

\displaystyle=J^{-(1)}S(J^{-(2)})\Lambda_{(1)}J^{(1)}\otimes\Lambda_{(2)}J^{(2)}\ \ \ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ004})

\displaystyle=J^{-(1)}S(J^{-(2)})\Lambda_{(1)}\otimes\Lambda_{(2)}\alpha(J^{(1)}_{(1)})S(J^{(1)}_{(2)})J^{(2)}\ \ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ005})

\displaystyle=Q_{J}^{-1}\Lambda_{(1)}\otimes\Lambda_{(2)}S(R)Q_{J}\ \ \ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ003})

So the equality holds for the case
n=2
.
Suppose the identity in question holds for the case
n
. Namely,

(3.3)

\Lambda_{\langle 1\rangle}\otimes\Lambda_{\langle 2\rangle}\Lambda_{\langle 3\rangle}\cdots\Lambda_{\langle n\rangle}=Q_{J}^{-1}\Lambda_{(1)}J^{(1)}\otimes J^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}P_{n-2}\bigg(J^{(2)}J^{-(2)}\Lambda_{(3)}\bigg)S(R)Q_{J}.

To prove the case
n+1
, we set
\widetilde{J}^{(1)}\otimes\widetilde{J}^{(2)}=J^{(1)}\otimes J^{(2)}
,
\widetilde{J}^{-(1)}\otimes\widetilde{J}^{-(2)}=J^{-(1)}\otimes J^{-(2)}
and apply
\Delta^{J}\otimes id
to both sides of (
3.3
). It follows that

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \Lambda_{\langle 1\rangle}\otimes\Lambda_{\langle 2\rangle}\otimes\Lambda_{\langle 3\rangle}\cdots\Lambda_{\langle n+1\rangle}

\displaystyle=J^{-1}\Delta(Q_{J}^{-1})\Delta(\Lambda_{(1)})\Delta(J^{(1)})J\otimes J^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}P_{n-2}(J^{(2)}J^{-(2)}\Lambda_{(3)})S(R)Q_{J}

\displaystyle=(Q_{J}^{-1}\otimes 1)\bigg((1\otimes Q_{J}^{-1})(S\otimes S)(J_{21})\bigg)\Delta(\Lambda_{(1)})\Delta(J^{(1)})J\otimes J^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}P_{n-2}(J^{(2)}J^{-(2)}\Lambda_{(3)})S(R)Q_{J}\ \ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equu00003})

\displaystyle=(Q_{J}^{-1}\otimes 1)(1\otimes\widetilde{J}^{-(1)})\Delta(S({\widetilde{J}}^{-(2)}))\Delta(\Lambda_{(1)})\Delta(J^{(1)})J\otimes J^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}P_{n-2}(J^{(2)}J^{-(2)}\Lambda_{(3)})S(R)Q_{J}\ \ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equu0003})

\displaystyle=(Q_{J}^{-1}\otimes\widetilde{J}^{-(1)})\Delta(S({\widetilde{J}}^{-(2)})\Lambda_{(1)})\Delta(J^{(1)})J\otimes J^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}P_{n-2}(J^{(2)}J^{-(2)}\Lambda_{(3)})S(R)Q_{J}

\displaystyle=(Q_{J}^{-1}\otimes\widetilde{J}^{-(1)})\Delta(\Lambda_{(1)})\Delta(J^{(1)})J\otimes J^{-(1)}{\widetilde{J}}^{-(2)}_{(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}P_{n-2}(J^{(2)}J^{-(2)}{\widetilde{J}}^{-(2)}_{(2)}\Lambda_{(3)})S(R)Q_{J}\ \ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ004})

\displaystyle=(Q_{J}^{-1}\otimes\widetilde{J}^{-(1)})\Delta(\Lambda_{(1)})(\widetilde{J}^{(1)}\otimes\widetilde{J}^{(2)}_{(1)}J^{(1)})\otimes J^{-(1)}{\widetilde{J}}^{-(2)}_{(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}P_{n-2}(\widetilde{J}^{(2)}_{(2)}J^{(2)}J^{-(2)}{\widetilde{J}}^{-(2)}_{(2)}\Lambda_{(3)})S(R)Q_{J}\ \ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{qq1})

\displaystyle=Q_{J}^{-1}\Lambda_{(1)}\widetilde{J}^{(1)}\otimes\widetilde{J}^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}\widetilde{J}^{(2)}_{(1)}J^{(1)}\otimes J^{-(1)}\widetilde{J}^{-(2)}_{(1)}\Lambda_{(3)}P_{n-2}(\widetilde{J}^{(2)}_{(2)}J^{(2)}J^{-(2)}{\widetilde{J}}^{-(2)}_{(2)}\Lambda_{(4)})S(R)Q_{J}

It follows that

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \Lambda_{\langle 1\rangle}\otimes\Lambda_{\langle 2\rangle}\Lambda_{\langle 3\rangle}\cdots\Lambda_{\langle n+1\rangle}

\displaystyle=Q_{J}^{-1}\Lambda_{(1)}\widetilde{J}^{(1)}\otimes\widetilde{J}^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}\widetilde{J}^{(2)}_{(1)}J^{(1)}J^{-(1)}\widetilde{J}^{-(2)}_{(1)}\Lambda_{(3)}P_{n-2}(\widetilde{J}^{(2)}_{(2)}J^{(2)}J^{-(2)}{\widetilde{J}}^{-(2)}_{(2)}\Lambda_{(4)})S(R)Q_{J}

\displaystyle=Q_{J}^{-1}\Lambda_{(1)}\widetilde{J}^{(1)}\otimes\widetilde{J}^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}\widetilde{J}^{(2)}_{(1)}\widetilde{J}^{-(2)}_{(1)}\Lambda_{(3)}P_{n-2}(\widetilde{J}^{(2)}_{(2)}{\widetilde{J}}^{-(2)}_{(2)}\Lambda_{(4)})S(R)Q_{J}

\displaystyle=Q_{J}^{-1}\Lambda_{(1)}\widetilde{J}^{(1)}\otimes\widetilde{J}^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}P_{n-1}(\widetilde{J}^{(2)}{\widetilde{J}}^{-(2)}\Lambda_{(3)})S(R)Q_{J}

\displaystyle=Q_{J}^{-1}\Lambda_{(1)}J^{(1)}\otimes J^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}P_{n-1}(J^{(2)}J^{-(2)}\Lambda_{(3)})S(R)Q_{J}.

The equality of Part (1) is now proved by induction on
n
.
∎

We have the following relationship between
P_{n}^{J}(\Lambda)
and
P_{n}(\Lambda)
. This is the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over the field
\mathbbm{k}
with a nonzero left integral
\Lambda
and a normalized twist
J
. For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(1)

P_{n}^{J}(\Lambda)=TP_{n}(\Lambda)
, where
T=J^{-(1)}\alpha(J^{-(2)})
.

(2)

P_{n}^{J}(\Lambda)=Q_{J}^{-1}P_{n}(\Lambda)S(R)Q_{J}
, where
R=\alpha(J^{-(1)})J^{-(2)}
.

If, moreover,
A
is unimodular, then
P_{n}^{J}(\Lambda)=P_{n}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

(1)
It is direct to check that the identity in question holds for the case
n=0,\pm 1
. For the case
n\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle P_{n}^{J}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\langle 1\rangle}\Lambda_{\langle 2\rangle}\Lambda_{\langle 3\rangle}\cdots\Lambda_{\langle n\rangle}

\displaystyle=Q_{J}^{-1}\Lambda_{(1)}J^{(1)}J^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}P_{n-2}\bigg(J^{(2)}J^{-(2)}\Lambda_{(3)}\bigg)S(R)Q_{J}\ \ \ \text{by\ Lemma}\ \ref{prop1}\ (1)

\displaystyle=Q_{J}^{-1}\Lambda_{(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}P_{n-2}(\Lambda_{(3)})S(R)Q_{J}

\displaystyle=Q_{J}^{-1}P_{n}(\Lambda)S(R)Q_{J}

\displaystyle=P_{n}(\Lambda)J^{(1)}(\alpha\circ S^{-1})(J^{(2)}Q_{J}^{-1})\ \ \ \text{by\ Proposition}\ \ref{lem1}

\displaystyle=P_{n}(\Lambda)J^{-(1)}_{(1)}(\alpha\circ S^{-1})(J^{-(1)}_{(2)})\alpha(J^{-(2)})\ \ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ0033})

\displaystyle=TP_{n}(\Lambda)\ \ \ \text{by\ Proposition}\ \ref{lem1}

For the case
n\leq-2
, we have

\displaystyle P_{n}^{J}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=S^{J}(\Lambda_{\langle-n\rangle}\cdots\Lambda_{\langle 1\rangle})

\displaystyle=Q_{J}^{-1}S\bigg(\Lambda_{\langle-n\rangle}\cdots\Lambda_{\langle 1\rangle}\bigg)Q_{J}

\displaystyle=Q_{J}^{-1}S\bigg(S^{-1}(Q_{J}^{-1})\Lambda_{(3)}J^{(2)}J^{-(2)}\Lambda_{(2)}(S^{-1}\circ P_{n+2})(J^{(1)}J^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(1)})S^{-1}(T)S^{-1}(Q_{J})\bigg)Q_{J}

\displaystyle=Q_{J}^{-1}S\bigg(S^{-1}(Q_{J}^{-1})\Lambda_{(3)}\Lambda_{(2)}(S^{-1}\circ P_{n+2})(\Lambda_{(1)})S^{-1}(T)S^{-1}(Q_{J})\bigg)Q_{J}

\displaystyle=S\bigg(\Lambda_{(3)}\Lambda_{(2)}(S^{-1}\circ P_{n+2})(\Lambda_{(1)})S^{-1}(T)\bigg)

\displaystyle=TP_{n}(\Lambda),

where the third equality follows from Lemma

(2).

(2) On the one hand,

\displaystyle P_{n}^{J}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=TP_{n}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=P_{n}(\Lambda)J^{-(1)}_{(1)}(\alpha\circ S^{-1})(J^{-(1)}_{(2)})\alpha(J^{-(2)})\ \ \ \text{by\ Proposition}\ \ref{lem1}

\displaystyle=P_{n}(\Lambda)J^{(1)}(\alpha\circ S^{-1})(J^{(2)}Q_{J}^{-1})\ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ0033})

\displaystyle Q_{J}^{-1}P_{n}(\Lambda)S(R)Q_{J}

\displaystyle=P_{n}(\Lambda)(Q_{J}^{-1})_{(1)}(\alpha\circ S^{-1})((Q_{J}^{-1})_{(2)})S(R)Q_{J}\ \ \text{by\ Proposition}\ \ref{lem1}

We conclude that

If

∎

Remark 3.5
.

Theorem

shows that the sequence

of a unimodular Hopf algebra is invariant under twisting.

4.
Invariants of representation categories of Hopf algebras

In this section, we will use Theorem
3.4
to give several invariants of representation categories of Hopf algebras. As applications, we show that the representation categories of 12-dimensional pointed nonsemisimple Hopf algebras classified in
[
10
]
are mutually inequivalent as tensor categories. We also show that those
8
-dimensional semisimple Hopf algebras, including Kac algebra
K_{8}
, the dihedral group algebra
\mathbbm{k}D_{4}
and the quaternion group algebra
\mathbbm{k}Q_{8}
are mutually not gauge equivalent.

We begin with the following preparations. Let
A
and
A^{\prime}
be finite-dimensional Hopf algebras over the field
\mathbbm{k}
with nonzero left integrals
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
respectively. If the functor
\mathcal{F}:\text{Rep}(A)\rightarrow\text{Rep}(A^{\prime})
is an equivalence of tensor categories, it follows from
[
12
, Theorem 2.2]
that there exist
a normalized twist
J
of
A
such that
A^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
A^{J}
as bialgebras. Let
\sigma:A^{\prime}\rightarrow A^{J}
be such an isomorphism. Then
\sigma
is
automatically a Hopf algebra isomorphism. Therefore, for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\sigma\circ P^{\prime}_{n}=P_{n}^{J}\circ\sigma,

where
P^{\prime}_{n}
and
P_{n}^{J}
are the
n
-th Sweedler power maps of
A^{\prime}
and
A^{J}
respectively.
Suppose
\sigma(\Lambda^{\prime})=\mu\Lambda
for a nonzero scalar
\mu\in\mathbbm{k}
. Then

(4.1)

\sigma(P^{\prime}_{n}(\Lambda^{\prime}))=P^{J}_{n}(\sigma(\Lambda^{\prime}))=\mu P^{J}_{n}(\Lambda)=\mu TP_{n}(\Lambda),

where
T=J^{-(1)}\alpha(J^{-(2)})
.
The isomorphism
\sigma
induces a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear equivalence
(-)^{\sigma}:\text{Rep}(A)\rightarrow\text{Rep}(A^{\prime})
as follows: for any finite-dimensional
A
-module
V
,
V^{\sigma}=V
as
\mathbbm{k}
-linear space with the
A^{\prime}
-module structure given by
a^{\prime}v=\sigma(a^{\prime})v
for
a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}
,
v\in V
, and
f^{\sigma}=f
for any morphism
f
in Rep
(A)
.
Thus,

\chi_{V^{\sigma}}(a^{\prime})=\chi_{V}(\sigma(a^{\prime}))\ \text{for}\ a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}.

Moreover, the equivalence
\mathcal{F}
is naturally isomorphic to the
\mathbbm{k}
-linear equivalence
(-)^{\sigma}
(see
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
).
Therefore,

\chi_{\mathcal{F}(V)}(a^{\prime})=\chi_{V^{\sigma}}(a^{\prime})\ \text{for}\ a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}.

By taking
a^{\prime}
to be
P^{\prime}_{n}(\Lambda^{\prime})
, we have

(4.2)

\chi_{\mathcal{F}(V)}(P^{\prime}_{n}(\Lambda^{\prime}))=\chi_{V^{\sigma}}(P^{\prime}_{n}(\Lambda^{\prime}))=\chi_{V}(\sigma(P^{\prime}_{n}(\Lambda^{\prime})))=\mu\chi_{V}(TP_{n}(\Lambda)),

where the scalar
\mu
is independent on the choice of
V
.

The first result of this section states that any homogeneous polynomial vanishing on some
P_{n}(\Lambda)
is an invariant of the tensor category Rep
(A)
. We shall see that this invariant can be used to distinguish the representation categories of some Hopf algebras.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A
and
A^{\prime}
be gauge equivalent finite-dimensional Hopf
algebras over the field
\mathbbm{k}
with nonzero left integrals
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
respectively. For any homogeneous polynomial
\psi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{s})\in\mathbbm{k}[X_{1},\cdots,X_{s}]
, we have
\psi(P_{n_{1}}(\Lambda),\cdots,P_{n_{s}}(\Lambda))=0
for some
n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}
if and only if
\psi(P^{\prime}_{n_{1}}(\Lambda^{\prime}),\cdots,P^{\prime}_{n_{s}}(\Lambda^{\prime}))=0
.

Proof.

Since Rep
(A)
and Rep
(A^{\prime})
are equivalent as tensor categories, there exists
a normalized twist
J
of
A
such that
A^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
A^{J}
as Hopf algebras. Let
\sigma:A^{\prime}\rightarrow A^{J}
be such an isomorphism. It follows from (
4.1
) that
\sigma(P^{\prime}_{n}(\Lambda^{\prime}))=\mu TP_{n}(\Lambda)
, where
\mu
is a nonzero scalar and
T=J^{-(1)}\alpha(J^{-(2)})
which is an invertible element of
A
. Let
\psi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{s})
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
m
. Then

\displaystyle\sigma(\psi(P^{\prime}_{n_{1}}(\Lambda^{\prime}),\cdots,P^{\prime}_{n_{s}}(\Lambda^{\prime})))

\displaystyle=\psi(\sigma(P^{\prime}_{n_{1}}(\Lambda^{\prime})),\cdots,\sigma(P^{\prime}_{n_{s}}(\Lambda^{\prime})))

\displaystyle=\psi(\mu TP_{n_{1}}(\Lambda),\cdots,\mu TP_{n_{s}}(\Lambda))

\displaystyle=\psi(P_{n_{1}}(\Lambda),\cdots,P_{n_{s}}(\Lambda))\mu^{m}T_{m}\cdots T_{2}T_{1}\ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{eer})

where these elements
T_{1},\cdots,T_{m}\in A
are as defined recursively in (
3.1
). Note that these elements
T_{1},\cdots,T_{m}
are invertible in
A
.
Therefore,
\psi(P_{n_{1}}(\Lambda),\cdots,P_{n_{s}}(\Lambda))=0
if and only if
\psi(P^{\prime}_{n_{1}}(\Lambda^{\prime}),\cdots,P^{\prime}_{n_{s}}(\Lambda^{\prime}))=0
. The proof is completed.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
A
and
A^{\prime}
be gauge equivalent finite-dimensional unimodular Hopf

\mathbbm{k}
. Let
\Lambda
be a nonzero left integral in

.
For any polynomial
\psi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{s})\in\mathbbm{k}[X_{1},\cdots,X_{s}]

\psi(P^{\prime}_{n_{1}}(\Lambda^{\prime}),\cdots,P^{\prime}_{n_{s}}(\Lambda^{\prime}))=0

Proof.

Since Rep
(A)
and Rep
(A^{\prime})
are equivalent as tensor categories, there exists
a normalized twist
J
of
A
such that
A^{\prime}
is isomorphic via a map
\sigma
to
A^{J}
as Hopf algebras. We may choose a left integral
\Lambda^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}

. In this case, the scalar

. The proof is completed.

Proposition 4.3

Let

\Lambda

\psi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{s})\in\mathbbm{k}[X_{1},\cdots,X_{s}]

\psi(P_{n_{1}}(\Lambda),\cdots,P_{n_{s}}(\Lambda))=0

\psi(P^{\prime}_{n_{1}}(\Lambda^{\prime}),\cdots,P^{\prime}_{n_{s}}(\Lambda^{\prime}))=0
.

Proof.

Since Rep
(A)
and Rep
(A^{\prime})
are equivalent as tensor categories, there exists
a normalized twist
J
of
A
such that
A^{\prime}
is isomorphic via a map
\sigma
to

Proposition 4.5

At the end of this section, we pay a little attention to the Hopf order of a nonzero left integral of

If

. That is, the Hopf order of a nonzero left integral

.
It can be seen from above that the Hopf orders of
\Lambda\in\mathbbm{k}D_{4}
and
\Lambda\in\mathbbm{k}Q_{8}
are both equal to 4, while the Hopf order of
\Lambda\in K_{8}
is equal to 8.

5.
Integrals in the dual of twisted Hopf algebras

In this section, we investigate the relationship between a right integral
\lambda\in A^{*}
and a right integral
\lambda^{J}\in(A^{J})^{*}
. Based on this investigation, we provide a unifying proof of the well-known result which says that the indicator
\nu_{n}(A)
of a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
A
is a gauge invariant of
A
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We also use the expression of
\lambda^{J}
to give a different proof of the known result that the Killing form of a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
A
is invariant under twisting.

Note that the expression of a right integral
\lambda^{J}\in(A^{J})^{*}
has been described in
[
1
, Theorem 3.4]
when
A
is a finite-dimensional unimodular Hopf algebra. For a general Hopf algebra
A
, a right integral
\lambda^{J}\in(A^{J})^{*}
can be described as follows:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over the field
\mathbbm{k}
with a normalized twist
J
. Let
R=\alpha(J^{-(1)})J^{-(2)}
. If
\lambda
is a nonzero right integral in
A^{*}
, then

\lambda^{J}:=\lambda\leftharpoonup S^{2}(R^{-1})S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J}

is a nonzero right integral in
(A^{J})^{*}
.

Proof.

We need to show that
\lambda^{J}(b_{\langle 1\rangle})b_{\langle 2\rangle}=\lambda^{J}(b)
for all
b\in A
. We set
\widetilde{J}^{(1)}\otimes\widetilde{J}^{(2)}=J^{(1)}\otimes J^{(2)}
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda^{J}(b_{\langle 1\rangle})b_{\langle 2\rangle}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{J}\bigg(J^{-(1)}b_{(1)}J^{(1)}\bigg)J^{-(2)}b_{(2)}J^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\lambda\bigg(\alpha(\widetilde{J}^{(1)})S^{2}(\widetilde{J}^{(2)})S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J}J^{-(1)}b_{(1)}J^{(1)}\bigg)J^{-(2)}b_{(2)}J^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\lambda\bigg(\alpha(J^{(1)}_{(1)})S^{2}(J^{(1)}_{(2)})\alpha(\widetilde{J}^{(1)})S^{2}(\widetilde{J}^{(2)})S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J}J^{-(1)}b_{(1)}\bigg)J^{-(2)}b_{(2)}J^{(2)}\ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ4004})

\displaystyle=\lambda\bigg(\alpha(J^{(1)}_{(1)}\widetilde{J}^{(1)})S^{2}(J^{(1)}_{(2)}\widetilde{J}^{(2)})S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J}J^{-(1)}b_{(1)}\bigg)J^{-(2)}b_{(2)}J^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\lambda\bigg(\alpha(J^{(1)})S^{2}(J^{(2)}_{(1)}\widetilde{J}^{(1)})S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J}J^{-(1)}b_{(1)}\bigg)J^{-(2)}b_{(2)}J^{(2)}_{(2)}\widetilde{J}^{(2)}\ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{qq1})

We denote
t:=S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J}.
It follows from (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) that

\Delta(t)=t_{(1)}\otimes t_{(2)}=(S^{2}\otimes S^{2})(J)(S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J}\otimes S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J})J^{-1}.

Applying
id\otimes S^{-1}
to both sides of this equality, we obtain that

(5.1)

t_{(1)}\otimes S^{-1}(t_{(2)})=S^{2}(\widetilde{J}^{(1)})S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J}J^{-(1)}\otimes S^{-1}(J^{-(2)})S^{-1}(Q_{J})Q_{J}^{-1}S(\widetilde{J}^{(2)}).

Now we have

\displaystyle\ \lambda^{J}(b_{\langle 1\rangle})b_{\langle 2\rangle}

\displaystyle=\alpha(J^{(1)})J^{-(2)}\lambda\bigg(S^{2}(J^{(2)}_{(1)}\widetilde{J}^{(1)})tJ^{-(1)}b_{(1)}\bigg)b_{(2)}J^{(2)}_{(2)}\widetilde{J}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\alpha(J^{(1)})J^{-(2)}\lambda\bigg(S^{2}(J^{(2)}_{(1)}\widetilde{J}^{(1)}_{(1)})t_{(1)}J^{-(1)}_{(1)}b\bigg)S^{-1}\bigg(S^{2}(J^{(2)}_{(2)}\widetilde{J}^{(1)}_{(2)})t_{(2)}J^{-(1)}_{(2)}\bigg)J^{(2)}_{(3)}\widetilde{J}^{(2)}\ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ4004444})

\displaystyle=\alpha(J^{(1)})J^{-(2)}\lambda\bigg(S^{2}(J^{(2)}_{(1)}\widetilde{J}^{(1)}_{(1)})t_{(1)}J^{-(1)}_{(1)}b\bigg)S^{-1}(J^{-(1)}_{(2)})S^{-1}(t_{(2)})S(\widetilde{J}^{(1)}_{(2)})S(J^{(2)}_{(2)})J^{(2)}_{(3)}\widetilde{J}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\alpha(J^{(1)})J^{-(2)}\lambda\bigg(S^{2}(J^{(2)}\widetilde{J}^{(1)}_{(1)})t_{(1)}J^{-(1)}_{(1)}b\bigg)S^{-1}(J^{-(1)}_{(2)})S^{-1}(t_{(2)})S(\widetilde{J}^{(1)}_{(2)})\widetilde{J}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\lambda\bigg(S^{2}(R^{-1})S^{2}(\widetilde{J}^{(1)}_{(1)})t_{(1)}J^{-(1)}_{(1)}b\bigg)J^{-(2)}S^{-1}(J^{-(1)}_{(2)})S^{-1}(t_{(2)})S(\widetilde{J}^{(1)}_{(2)})\widetilde{J}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\lambda\bigg(S^{2}(R^{-1})S^{2}(\widetilde{J}^{(1)}_{(1)})t_{(1)}J^{(1)}b\bigg)S^{-1}(Q_{J}^{-1})S^{-1}(J^{(2)})S^{-1}(t_{(2)})S(\widetilde{J}^{(1)}_{(2)})\widetilde{J}^{(2)}\ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ0033})

\displaystyle=\lambda\bigg(S^{2}(R^{-1})S^{2}(\widetilde{J}^{-(1)})t_{(1)}J^{(1)}b\bigg)S^{-1}(Q_{J}^{-1})S^{-1}(J^{(2)})S^{-1}(t_{(2)})S(\widetilde{J}^{-(2)})Q_{J}\ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ003})

\displaystyle=\lambda\bigg(S^{2}(R^{-1})S^{2}(\widetilde{J}^{-(1)})S^{2}(\widetilde{J}^{(1)})S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J}J^{-(1)}J^{(1)}b\bigg)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \cdot S^{-1}(Q_{J}^{-1})S^{-1}(J^{(2)})S^{-1}(J^{-(2)})S^{-1}(Q_{J})Q_{J}^{-1}S(\widetilde{J}^{(2)})S(\widetilde{J}^{-(2)})Q_{J}\ \ \text{by}\ ({\ref{r}})

\displaystyle=\lambda\bigg(S^{2}(R^{-1})S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J}b\bigg)S^{-1}(Q_{J}^{-1})S^{-1}(Q_{J})Q_{J}^{-1}Q_{J}

\displaystyle=\lambda\bigg(S^{2}(R^{-1})S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J}b\bigg)=\lambda^{J}(b).

Thus,
\lambda^{J}
is a right integral in
(A^{J})^{*}
. The proof is completed.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

If
A
is a finite-dimensional unimodular Hopf algebra over the field
\mathbbm{k}
with a normalized twist
J
, then
R=\alpha(J^{-(1)})J^{-(2)}=\varepsilon(J^{-(1)})J^{-(2)}=1
. In this case,
\lambda^{J}=\lambda\leftharpoonup S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J}
, which is exactly the result of
[
1
, Theorem 3.4]
. It is interesting to know whether or not
\lambda(1)\neq 0
implying
\lambda^{J}(1)\neq 0
for a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
A
(see
[
1
, Remark 3.9]
). This problem has only been solved in the unimodular case (see
[
1
, Corollary 3.6]
).

For any finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
A
, recall from
[
4
]
and
[
14
]
that the
n
-th indicator of
A
is defined by

\nu_{n}(A):=\text{tr}(S\circ P_{n-1})\ \ \text{for}\ n\in\mathbb{Z}.

By Radford’s trace formula (
2.5
), we have

(5.2)

\nu_{n}(A)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\lambda\circ S)(\Lambda_{(1)}\Lambda_{(2)}\cdots\Lambda_{(n)}),&n\geq 1;\\
\lambda(1)\varepsilon(\Lambda),&n=0;\\
(\lambda\circ S^{2})(\Lambda_{(-n)}\Lambda_{(-n-1)}\cdots\Lambda_{(1)}),&n\leq-1.\end{array}\right.

The remarkable result due to Y. Kashina, S. Montgomery, S.-H. Ng
[
4
, Theorem 2.2]
states that the indicator
\nu_{n}(A)
of a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
A
is a gauge invariant of
A
for any
n\geq 1
. Later this result has been extended to the case
n\leq 0
by K. Shimizu
[
14
, Theorem 3.10]
, where the proof relies on the case of
n\geq 1
and the linear recurrence relation between the Sweedler power maps. In the sequel, we will use Theorem
5.1
to give a uniform proof of the two cases.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over the field
\mathbbm{k}
. The
n
-th indicator
\nu_{n}(A)
is a gauge invariant of
A
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\lambda\in A^{*}
is a right integral and
\Lambda\in A
is a left integral such that
\lambda(\Lambda)=1
. It follows from (
5.2
) that

\nu_{n}(A)=(\lambda\circ S)(P_{n}(\Lambda))\ \ \text{for}\ n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Let
A^{J}
be the Hopf algebra twisted by a normalized twist
J
on
A
. The
n
-th indicator of
A^{J}
is
\nu_{n}(A^{J})=(\lambda^{J}\circ S^{J})(P^{J}_{n}(\Lambda))
. We claim that
\nu_{n}(A^{J})=\nu_{n}(A)
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\nu_{n}(A^{J})

\displaystyle=(\lambda^{J}\circ S^{J})(P^{J}_{n}(\Lambda))

\displaystyle=\lambda^{J}(Q_{J}^{-1}S(P_{n}^{J}(\Lambda))Q_{J})

\displaystyle=\lambda(\alpha(J^{(1)})S^{2}(J^{(2)})S(Q_{J}^{-1})Q_{J}Q_{J}^{-1}S(P_{n}^{J}(\Lambda))Q_{J})

\displaystyle=\lambda(\alpha(J^{(1)})S^{2}(J^{(2)})S(Q_{J}^{-1})S(Q_{J}^{-1}P_{n}(\Lambda)\alpha(J^{(-1)})S(J^{(-2)})Q_{J})Q_{J})\ \ \text{by\ Theorem}\ \ref{cor03}(2)

\displaystyle=\lambda(S(P_{n}(\Lambda))S(Q^{-1}_{J})Q_{J})

\displaystyle=\lambda(S(P_{n}(\Lambda))S(Q^{-1}_{J})S(J^{(1)})J^{(2)})

\displaystyle=\lambda(\alpha(J^{(2)}_{(1)})S^{2}(J^{(2)}_{(2)})S(P_{n}(\Lambda))S(Q^{-1}_{J})S(J^{(1)}))\ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ4004})

\displaystyle=(\lambda\circ S)(J^{(1)}Q^{-1}_{J}P_{n}(\Lambda)\alpha(J^{(2)}_{(1)})S(J^{(2)}_{(2)}))

\displaystyle=(\lambda\circ S)(J^{(1)}Q^{-1}_{J}P_{n-1}(\Lambda_{(1)})\Lambda_{(2)}\alpha(J^{(2)}_{(1)})S(J^{(2)}_{(2)}))

\displaystyle=(\lambda\circ S)(J^{(1)}Q^{-1}_{J}P_{n-1}(\Lambda_{(1)}J^{(2)})\Lambda_{(2)})\ \ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ005})

\displaystyle=(\lambda\circ S)(J^{(1)}J^{-(1)}S(J^{-(2)})\Lambda_{(1)}P_{n-1}(J^{(2)}\Lambda_{(2)}))

\displaystyle=(\lambda\circ S)(J^{(1)}J^{-(1)}\Lambda_{(1)}P_{n-1}(J^{(2)}J^{-(2)}\Lambda_{(2)}))\ \ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ004})

\displaystyle=(\lambda\circ S)(\Lambda_{(1)}P_{n-1}(\Lambda_{(2)}))

\displaystyle=(\lambda\circ S)(P_{n}(\Lambda))

\displaystyle=\nu_{n}(A).

If
A^{\prime}
is a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over the field
\mathbbm{k}
which is gauge equivalent to
A
, then there exists a normalized twist
J
of
A
such that
A^{\prime}
is isomorphic via a map
\sigma
to
A^{J}
as Hopf algebras. Then

\sigma\circ S^{\prime}\circ P^{\prime}_{n}=S^{J}\circ P_{n}^{J}\circ\sigma,

where
S^{\prime}
and
P^{\prime}_{n}
are the antipode and the
n
-th Sweedler power map of
A^{\prime}
respectively. For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle\nu_{n}(A^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\text{tr}(S^{\prime}\circ P^{\prime}_{n-1})=\text{tr}(\sigma^{-1}\circ S^{J}\circ P_{n-1}^{J}\circ\sigma)

\displaystyle=\text{tr}(S^{J}\circ P_{n-1}^{J})=\nu_{n}(A^{J})=\nu_{n}(A).

The proof is completed.
∎

We need to point out that Theorem
5.1
can be used to prove the equality

\text{tr}(S^{n})=\text{tr}((S^{J})^{n})\ \text{for}\ n=\pm 1,\pm 2.

However, we are not sure Theorem
5.1
can be used to prove the equality for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. It is known that if
A
has the Chevalley property, the equality holds for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
(see
[
11
, Theorem 4.3]
).

Finally, we will use Theorem
5.1
to show that the Killing
form of a Hopf algebra is invariant under twisting.
Let
A
be a finite-dimensional Hopf
algebra with antipode
S
over the field
\mathbbm{k}
. The (left) adjoint representation of
A
is the map

\text{ad}:A\rightarrow\text{End}_{\mathbbm{k}}(A),\ a\mapsto\text{ad}a,

where the
\mathbbm{k}
-linear map
\text{ad}a:A\longrightarrow A
is given by

(\text{ad}a)(b)=a_{(1)}bS(a_{(2)})\ \text{for}\ b\in A.

The Killing form of the Hopf algebra
A
is defined by

(a,b)=\text{tr}(\text{ad}a\circ\text{ad}b)=\text{tr}(\text{ad}ab)\ \text{for}\ a,b\in A.

The subspace

A^{\perp}=\{a\in A\mid(a,b)=0\ \text{for\ all}\ b\in A\}

is called the Killing
radical of
A.
This radical is a two-sided ideal of
A
but not a Hopf ideal in general.

We denote the adjoint representation of the twisted Hopf algebra
A^{J}
by
\text{ad}^{J}
and the Killing form of
A^{J}
by
(-,-)^{J}
. Namely,
(a,b)^{J}=\text{tr}(\text{ad}^{J}(ab)).

The following result has been given in
[
16
, Corollary 4.3]
, we present it here with an alternative proof.

Proposition 5.4
.

The Killing form of a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
A
is invariant under twisting. Namely,
if
A^{J}
is the Hopf algebra twisted by a normalized twist
J
on a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
A
, then
(a,b)=(a,b)^{J}
for all
a,b\in A
. In particular,
A^{\perp}=(A^{J})^{\perp}
.

Proof.

For
a,b\in A,
we only need to prove that
(a,1)^{J}=(a,1)
since
(a,b)^{J}=(ab,1)^{J}
and
(a,b)=(ab,1).
Suppose that
\lambda\in A^{*}
is a right integral and
\Lambda\in A
is a left integral such that
\lambda(\Lambda)=1
. Recall from Lemma
3.3
that

\Delta^{J}(\Lambda)=\Lambda_{\langle 1\rangle}\otimes\Lambda_{\langle 2\rangle}=Q_{J}^{-1}\Lambda_{(1)}\otimes\Lambda_{(2)}S(R)Q_{J}.

By Radford’s trace formula (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle(a,1)^{J}

\displaystyle=\text{tr}(\text{ad}^{J}(a))

\displaystyle=\lambda^{J}(S^{J}(\Lambda_{\langle 2\rangle})\text{ad}^{J}(a)(\Lambda_{\langle 1\rangle}))

\displaystyle=\lambda^{J}(S^{J}(\Lambda_{(2)}S(R)Q_{J})\text{ad}^{J}(a)(Q^{-1}_{J}\Lambda_{(1)}))

\displaystyle=\lambda^{J}(Q^{-1}_{J}S(\Lambda_{(2)}S(R)Q_{J})Q_{J}a_{\langle 1\rangle}Q^{-1}_{J}\Lambda_{(1)}S^{J}(a_{\langle 2\rangle}))

\displaystyle=\lambda^{J}(Q^{-1}_{J}S(\Lambda_{(2)}S(R)Q_{J})Q_{J}J^{-(1)}a_{(1)}J^{(1)}Q^{-1}_{J}\Lambda_{(1)}Q^{-1}_{J}S(J^{-(2)}a_{(2)}J^{(2)})Q_{J})

\displaystyle=\lambda(S^{2}(R^{-1})S(Q^{-1}_{J})Q_{J}Q^{-1}_{J}S(\Lambda_{(2)}S(R)Q_{J})Q_{J}J^{-(1)}a_{(1)}J^{(1)}Q^{-1}_{J}\Lambda_{(1)}Q^{-1}_{J}S(J^{-(2)}a_{(2)}J^{(2)})Q_{J})

\displaystyle=\lambda(S(\Lambda_{(2)})Q_{J}J^{-(1)}a_{(1)}J^{(1)}Q^{-1}_{J}\Lambda_{(1)}Q^{-1}_{J}S(J^{-(2)}a_{(2)}J^{(2)})Q_{J})

\displaystyle=\lambda(S(\Lambda_{(2)})J^{(1)}Q^{-1}_{J}Q_{J}J^{-(1)}a_{(1)}\Lambda_{(1)}Q^{-1}_{J}S(J^{(2)})S(a_{(2)})S(J^{-(2)})Q_{J})\ \ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ004})

\displaystyle=\lambda(S^{2}(S(J^{-(2)})Q_{J}\leftharpoonup\alpha)S(\Lambda_{(2)})J^{(1)}J^{-(1)}a_{(1)}\Lambda_{(1)}Q^{-1}_{J}S(J^{(2)})S(a_{(2)}))\ \ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ4004})

\displaystyle=\lambda(S(\Lambda_{(2)})J^{(1)}J^{-(1)}a_{(1)}\Lambda_{(1)}S(J^{-(2)})Q_{J}Q^{-1}_{J}S(J^{(2)})S(a_{(2)}))\ \ \ \text{by}\ (\ref{equ005})

\displaystyle=\lambda(S(\Lambda_{(2)})a_{(1)}\Lambda_{(1)}S(a_{(2)}))

\displaystyle=\text{tr}(\text{ad}(a))

\displaystyle=(a,1)

Now
A^{\perp}=(A^{J})^{\perp}
follows from
(a,b)=(a,b)^{J}
for all
a,b\in A
. ∎

As a consequence, we obtain a gauge invariant of
A
as follows:

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional Hopf
algebra over the field
\mathbbm{k}
. Then
\text{dim}_{\mathbbm{k}}(A^{\perp})
is a gauge invariant of
A
.

Example 5.6
.

Consider the dihedral group
D_{n}=\{1,a,\cdots,a^{n-1},b,ba,\cdots,ba^{n-1}\}
with
a^{n}=1,\ b^{2}=1
and
aba=b
. Suppose
2n\neq 0
in
\mathbbm{k}
. If
n
is odd, then
(\mathbbm{k}D_{n})^{\perp}=0
. If
n
is even, then
(\mathbbm{k}D_{n})^{\perp}
is the ideal of
\mathbbm{k}D_{n}
generated by
1-a^{\frac{n}{2}}
. This is a Hopf ideal of
\mathbbm{k}D_{n}
,
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}((\mathbbm{k}D_{n})^{\perp})=n
and the quotient Hopf algebra is
\mathbbm{k}D_{n}/(\mathbbm{k}D_{n})^{\perp}\cong\mathbbm{k}D_{\frac{n}{2}}
(see
[
16
, Example 3.7]
).

Example 5.7
.

Let
H_{n,d}
denote a generalized Taft algebra over the field
\mathbbm{k}
with
nd\neq 0
in
\mathbbm{k}
. As an algebra,
H_{n,d}
is generated by
g
and
h
subject to the following relations:

g^{n}=1,\ h^{d}=0,\ hg=qgh,

where
d
divides
n
and
q
is a primitive
d
-th root of unity. As a Hopf algebra, the comultiplication
\Delta
, counit
\varepsilon
and the antipode
S
of

H_{n,d}
are given respectively by

\Delta(g)=g\otimes g,\ \Delta(h)=1\otimes h+h\otimes g,\ \varepsilon(g)=1,\ \varepsilon(h)=0,

S(g)=g^{-1},\ S(h)=-q^{-1}g^{n-1}h.

The Hopf algebra
H_{n,d}
has a
\mathbbm{k}
-basis
\{g^{i}h^{j}\mid 0\leq i\leq n-1,0\leq j\leq d-1\}
and
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}(H_{n,d})=nd
. If
d=n
, then
H_{n,n}
is the Taft algebra.
By a straightforward computation, the Killing radical of
H_{n,d}
is
the ideal of
H_{n,d}
generated by
g^{d}-1
and
h
. The dimension of the Killing radical
H_{n,d}^{\perp}
is
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}(H_{n,d}^{\perp})=(n-1)d
, which is a gauge invariant of
H_{n,d}
.

Abstract




A note on Apery’s constant is transcendental

Abstract:
Beuker’s [2] considers the following integral

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{-\log xy}{1-xy}P_{n}(x)P_{n}(y)\ dxdy

If
d_{n}=\text{LCM}(1,2,...,n)
, then

0<\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|}{d_{n}^{3}}<2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}\zeta(3)

for some
A_{n},B_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
. We establish that if Apery’s constant is algebraic then the above inequality fails to be true. This proves that
\zeta(3)
is transcendental.

Keywords and Phrases:
Riemann zeta function, analytic continuation, Apery’s constant.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
11M06

1. Introduction and Definitions
The Riemann zeta function,
\zeta(s)
is defined as the analytic continuation of the Dirichlet series

\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{{\infty}}\frac{1}{n^{s}}

which converges in the half plane
\Re(s)>1
. The Riemann zeta function is a meromorphic function on the whole complex plane, which is holomorphic everywhere except for a simple pole at s = 1 with residue 1. The Riemann Hypothesis states that all the non trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function lie on the critical line
\Re(s)=\frac{1}{2}
. The Riemann xi function is defined as

\xi(s)=\frac{1}{2}s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s)

\xi(s)
is an entire function whose zeros are the non trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
[1]. Further
\xi(s)
satisfies the functional equation [1]

\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)

It is known that the Apery’s constant,
\zeta(3)
is irrational [2].

2. Main Theorems

The goal of this article is to prove the following result.

Theorem :
Apery’s constant,
\zeta(3)
is transcendental.

Proof
: We prove the claim by the method of contradiction. Let us assume that
\zeta(3)
is algebraic. Then it satisfies

c_{0}+c_{1}\zeta(3)+c_{2}\zeta^{2}(3)+...+c_{m}\zeta^{m}(3)=0

(1)

where
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
c_{0}>0
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

It is well known that [2]

2\zeta(3)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{-\log xy}{1-xy}\ dxdy

(2)

Beuker’s [2] considers the following integral

I_{n}:=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{-\log xy}{1-xy}P_{n}(x)P_{n}(y)\ dxdy

(3)

By Beuker’s [2] proof, we have if
d_{n}=\text{LCM}(1,2,...,n)
, then

0<\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|}{d_{n}^{3}}<2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}\zeta(3)

(4)

for some
A_{n},B_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Equation (2) can be rewritten as

0<\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|}{2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3}}<\zeta(3)

(5)

So we get on squaring equation (3)

0<\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|^{2}}{(2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3})^{2}}<\zeta^{2}(3)

(6)

Similarly we get

0<\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|^{3}}{(2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3})^{3}}<\zeta^{3}(3)

(7)

and so on

0<\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|^{m+1}}{(2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3})^{m+1}}<\zeta^{m+1}(3)

(
m
+5)

Now multiplying (3) by
c_{0}
, (4) by
c_{1}
, (5) by
c_{2}
,…, (
m
+3) by
c_{m}
, we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<c_{0}\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|}{2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3}}+c_{1}\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|^{2}}{(2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3})^{2}}+c_{2}\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|^{3}}{(2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3})^{3}}

\displaystyle+...+c_{m}\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|^{m+1}}{(2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3})^{m+1}}<c_{0}\zeta(3)+c_{1}\zeta^{2}(3)+c_{2}\zeta^{3}(3)+...+c_{m}\zeta^{m+1}(3)

(
m
+6)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<c_{0}\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|}{2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3}}+c_{1}\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|^{2}}{(2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3})^{2}}+c_{2}\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|^{3}}{(2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3})^{3}}

\displaystyle+...+c_{m}\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|^{m+1}}{(2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3})^{m+1}}<\zeta(3)(c_{0}+c_{1}\zeta(3)+c_{2}\zeta^{2}(3)+...+c_{m}\zeta^{m}(3))

(
m
+7)

Now using equation (1) we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<c_{0}\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|}{2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3}}+c_{1}\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|^{2}}{(2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3})^{2}}+c_{2}\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|^{3}}{(2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3})^{3}}

\displaystyle+...+c_{m}\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|^{m+1}}{(2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3})^{m+1}}<0

(
m
+8)

So we get

0<\sum_{k=1}^{m+1}c_{k-1}\frac{|A_{n}+B_{n}\zeta(3)|^{k}}{(2(\sqrt{2}-1)^{4n}d_{n}^{3})^{k}}<0

(
m
+9)

which is a contradiction. So our assumption that
\zeta(3)
is algebraic is not possible. Hence
\zeta(3)
is transcendental.
This settles the proof of the Theorem.

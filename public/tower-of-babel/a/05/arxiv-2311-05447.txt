A Blue-Detuned Magneto-Optical Trap of CaF Molecules
Samuel J. Li, Connor M. Holland, Yukai Lu, Lawrence W. Cheuk

Abstract
A key method to produce trapped and laser-cooled molecules is the magneto-optical trap (MOT), which is conventionally created using light red-detuned from an optical transition. In this work, we report a MOT for CaF molecules created using blue-detuned light. The blue-detuned MOT (BDM) achieves temperatures well below the Doppler limit, and provides the highest densities and phase-space densities reported to date in CaF MOTs. We observe short BDM lifetimes at high magnetic field gradients, preventing magnetic compression as a means to increase densities. By directly measuring the BDM restoring force, we find that the short lifetimes are explained by low effective trap depths. Notably, we find sub-mK depths at typical magnetic gradients, in contrast to ∼ 50 ​ mK \sim 50\,\text{mK} depths in red molecular MOTs and ∼ 0.5 ​ K \sim 0.5\,\text{K} depths in red atomic MOTs.

Conventionally, molecular MOTs are created using light red-detuned from an optical transition. They primarily rely on Doppler cooling and achieve temperatures near the Doppler limit
T_{D}=\hbar\Gamma/(2k_{B})
, where
\Gamma
is the excited state linewidth. To achieve the optical cycling needed for laser-cooling, the cooling light addresses a rotational-lowering transition
Stuhl et al. 2008
(e.g.
N=1\rightarrow N^{\prime}=0
transition, where
N
denotes the rotational quantum number). In these so-called type-II systems, the number of excited states is less than or equal to the number of ground states. As pointed out in
Devlin and Tarbutt 2016
, with red-detuned light, type-II systems experience Doppler cooling at high velocities but sub-Doppler heating at low velocities. With blue-detuned light, Doppler heating and sub-Doppler cooling occur instead
Devlin and Tarbutt 2016
;
Truppe et al. 2017
;
Cheuk et al. 2018
;
Ding et al. 2020
;
Langin et al. 2021
. It was subsequently realized that blue-detuned MOTs (BDMs), which offer spatial confinement in addition to sub-Doppler cooling, are possible
Jarvis et al. 2018
;
Langin and DeMille 2023
;
Xu et al. 2022
. The first BDM was realized with Rb atoms
Jarvis et al. 2018
; recently, molecular BDMs of YO
Burau et al. 2023
and SrF
Jorapur et al. 2023
have been created.

In this work, we demonstrate a BDM of CaF molecules, producing the coldest and densest CaF MOT reported to date. Our work starts with a conventional red-detuned MOT of CaF loaded from a cryogenic buffer gas beam. The red MOT utilizes a DC quadrupolar magnetic field (symmetry axis along

1

B_{z}^{\prime}=26\,\text{G/cm}
. Subsequently, the MOT is compressed by ramping the gradient to
B_{z}^{\prime}=104\,\text{G/cm}

;
Lu et al. 2022

\sigma=680(30)\,\mu\text{m}
and a temperature of
T=3.9(4)\,\text{mK}

Subsequently, molecules are captured into the BDM, which uses light blue-detuned to the

We next explore the dependences of

We next explore the dependence of the
\Lambda
-BDM on magnetic gradient
B_{z}^{\prime}
. This investigation is motivated by the practical objective of increasing molecular densities. A viable strategy in red-detuned MOTs is to compress molecular samples by increasing
B_{z}^{\prime}

Williams et al. 2017
. At a fixed temperature, the MOT size decreases with gradient as
1/\sqrt{B_{z}^{\prime}}
, and the density consequently rises as
B_{z}^{\prime}\,{}^{3/2}

Williams et al. 2017
;
Sup
. In detail, the MOT can be modeled with an equation of motion that captures a restoring force and velocity damping:
\ddot{x}=-\alpha(x)\dot{x}+F(x)/m

Barry et al. 2014
;
Anderegg et al. 2017
;
Williams et al. 2017
;
Burau et al. 2023
. Near the MOT center and at low velocities, the damping coefficient
\alpha
is approximately constant, and the restoring force is approximated by Hooke’s law
F(x)=-kx
, where
k
is the spring constant. The restoring force arises from magnetic-field dependent light scattering. Therefore, one can express the restoring force
F
as a function of the local magnetic field
B
. At the center of a quadrupole field used in a MOT,
F(x)=F(B(x))\approx B^{\prime}x\,\left(dF/dB\right)
. Hence, near the center of the MOT where Hooke’s law is valid,
k\propto B_{z}^{\prime}
. Using a generalized Virial theorem that equates potential and kinetic energy
Sup
, one finds that
\sigma\propto 1/\sqrt{B_{z}^{\prime}}
and the density grows as
(B_{z}^{\prime})^{3/2}
at a fixed temperature
T
.

To investigate the origin of the short lifetimes at high gradients, we first examine the dependence of the restoring force on
B_{z}^{\prime}
, since an insufficient restoring force could lead to loss. Assuming Hooke’s law and constant velocity damping, the spring constant

The acceleration curve
a(B)
provides an explanation of the short lifetimes observed at high gradients. In short, we find that the effective BDM trap depth becomes comparable to the molecular temperatures at high gradients, leading to loss. In detail, using
a(B)
, we define an effective conservative trapping potential
U(x)=-m\int a(B(x))dx
along the radial and axial directions
Con
. It follows that both the spatial scale and the magnitude of
U(x)
scale inversely with
B_{z}^{\prime}
(Fig.
4
(c)). In other words, both the size and depth of the effective trap decrease with
B_{z}^{\prime}
. To extract an effective depth, we assume that
U(x)
is of a Gaussian form given by
U(x)=-U_{\text{0}}\exp{[-x^{2}/(2d^{2})]}
, where
U_{\text{0}}
is the maximum trap depth and
d
the Gaussian spatial width. Fitting
a(B)
, we obtain a trap depth of
U_{\text{0}}(B_{z}^{\prime})=k_{B}\times 4.6(10)\,\text{mK}\cdot\text{(G/cm)}/B_{z}^{\prime}
, and a trap size of
d(B_{z}^{\prime})=0.62(4)\,\text{G}/B_{z}^{\prime}
. Notably, at a typical gradient of
20\,\text{G/cm}
,
U_{\text{0}}=k_{B}\times 230(50)\,\mu\text{K}
, which is only four times higher than the observed temperature. These trap depths are much smaller than the
\sim 50\,\text{mK}
depths reported for red-detuned molecular MOTs

;
Williams et al. 2017
;
Langin and DeMille 2023
and
\sim 0.5\,\text{K}
depths reported for red-detuned atomic MOTs
Raab et al. 1987
.

To further support our hypothesis that the short lifetimes arise from insufficient trap depths, we quantitatively examine both the damping
\alpha
and the local temperature-to-trap-depth ratio
\eta(x)=(k_{B}T)/U(x)

suggests that a shallow trap depth (insufficient restoring force), rather than the lack of velocity damping, is the primary cause of loss at high gradients. Semi-quantitatively, the loss rate due to finite trap depth can be estimated as
C\sum_{i}\langle\alpha_{i}\exp(-1/\eta_{i})\rangle

Sup

B_{z}^{\prime}

In summary, we have demonstrated a blue-detuned MOT of CaF molecules that reaches temperatures significantly below the Doppler limit. Specifically, the
\Lambda
-BDM provides the smallest sizes, lowest temperatures, and highest phase space densities reported to date for magneto-optically trapped CaF molecules. We have also uncovered a mechanism that hinders further density enhancement via magnetic compression. We find low effective
\Lambda
-BDM trap depths that decrease with the strength of the magnetic gradient, leading to short lifetimes at high gradients. Looking ahead, BDMs could significantly aid the loading of molecules into optical tweezer arrays, which are a promising platform for quantum simulation and quantum information processing
DeMille 2002
;
Carr et al. 2009
;
Bohn et al. 2017
;
Blackmore et al. 2018
;
Holland et al. 2022
;
Bao et al. 2022
. In addition, the enhanced densities could also aid the exploration of ultracold molecular collisions
Cheuk et al. 2020
;
Jorapur et al. 2023
or provide better starting conditions for evaporative cooling.

We conclude by contextualizing our BDM with those recently reported for YO
Burau et al. 2023
and SrF
Jorapur et al. 2023
. Despite different spin-rotation and hyperfine structure, we find that a CaF BDM is also possible. Specifically, the
\Lambda
-BDM robustly achieves sub-Doppler temperatures and substantially higher densities compared to a red MOT. This suggests that BDMs could be a powerful technique widely applicable to many laser-coolable molecules, including polyatomic ones such as CaOH and SrOH

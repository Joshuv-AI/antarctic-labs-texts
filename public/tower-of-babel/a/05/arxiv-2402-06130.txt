A Study on Auslander Bounds
Andrew J. Soto Levins

Abstract
The Auslander bound of a module can be thought of as a generalization of projective dimension. We say that the Auslander bound of M M is finite if for all finitely generated modules N N such that whenever Ext R n ⁡ ( M , N ) = 0 \ext_{R}^{n}(M,N)=0 for n ≫ 0 n\gg 0 , there exists an integer b b that only depends on M M so that Ext R n ⁡ ( M , N ) = 0 \ext_{R}^{n}(M,N)=0 for n > b n>b . In this paper we give new results on the Auslander bound by weakening the AC condition, generalizing many theorems in the the literature. We then define an Auslander bound for complexes and extend the module results to complexes.



1
Introduction

Throughout this paper
(R,m,k)
is a commutative Noetherian local ring unless otherwise specified and
M
and
N
are finitely generated modules (Section
6
is the only section where
R
might not be Noetherian and where
R
might not be local). The goal of this paper is to study the Auslander bound of a module. To motivate the definition of Auslander bound, if
M
has finite projective dimension, then for all nonzero modules
N
we have
\ext_{R}^{n}(M,N)=0
for
n>\pdim_{R}M
and
\ext_{R}^{\pdim_{R}M}(M,N)\neq 0
by Nakayamas Lemma. We also have
\pdim_{R}M=\depth{R}-\depth{M}
by the Auslander Buchsbaum formula, so the vanishing of Ext is related to depth when
M
has finite projective dimension.

What can be said about the vanishing of Ext when
M
does not necessarily have finite projective dimension?

Definition 1.1
.

For finitely generated modules
M
and
N
, set

1.

P_{R}(M,N)=\sup\{n|\ext_{R}^{n}(M,N)\neq 0\}

2.

b_{M}^{R}=\sup\{P_{R}(M,N)|P_{R}(M,N)<\infty\text{ and }N\text{ is finitely generated}\}
.

The number
b_{M}^{R}
is called the
Auslander bound
of
M
.

If finite, the number
P_{R}(M,N)
is the index of the last nonzero Ext of the pair of modules
M
and
N
. If the projective dimension of
M
is finite, then the discussion above shows
P_{R}(M,N)=\pdim_{R}M
for all
N
, so
b_{M}^{R}=\pdim_{R}M
. Auslander conjectured that for a finitely generated module
M
, if
P_{R}(M,R)=0
and
P_{R}(M,M)=0
, then
M
is projective. To study this conjecture, Auslander introduced the bound
b_{M}^{R}
and studied rings that have AC:

Definition 1.2
.

We say
R
has AC if
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
for all finitely generated modules
M
.

In
[
7
, Theorem 2.3]
Christensen and Holm show that if
R
has AC and
P_{R}(M,R)
and
P_{R}(M,M)
are both finite, then
\pdim_{R}M<\infty
. Christensen and Holm also show in
[
7
, Theorem 4.4]
that if
R
has AC and
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
, then
\gdim_{R}M<\infty
. In
[
17
]
Jorgensen and Sega give an example of a zero dimensional Gorenstein local ring that does not have AC, and so the Auslander bound of a module by itself cannot be used to check if a module has finite Gorenstein dimension (recall that every module over a Gorenstein local ring has finite Gorenstein dimension). Something to notice is that having AC is a condition on the ring and on all modules, whereas the theorems of Christensen and Holm are results about individual modules over such rings. So, a question that one can ask is what can be said about a module with a finite Auslander bound.

In
[
24
, Theorem 2.11.1]
Wei proves if
P_{R}(M,R)
and
P_{R}(M,M)
are both finite, then
b_{M}^{R}=\pdim_{R}M
. This result tells us that
M
has finite projective dimension if
b_{M}^{R}
,
P_{R}(M,R)
, and
P_{R}(M,M)
are all finite, generalizing
[
7
, Theorem 2.3]
. The first main theorem of this paper is the following, generalizing
[
7
, Theorem 4.4]
, as well as
[
14
, Theorem 3.4.3]
. Recall that a module
M
is CM (
Cohen Macaulay
) if
\depth{M}=\dim{M}
, and
M
is MCM (
Maximal Cohen Macaulay
) if
\depth{M}=\dim{R}
.

Theorem A
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring and let
M
be a finitely generated module with
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
.

1.

(Corollary
2.7
below) If
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
, then
\gdim_{R}M<\infty
.

2.

(Corollary
3.13
below) If
R
is Ext bounded (see Definition
3.2

b_{M}^{R}<\infty
and
\gdim_{R}M<\infty
.

Both parts of Theorem

are first proved in the Artinian case, where most of the work is done.

Definition 1.3
.

We say
R
is an AB ring if
R
is Gorenstein and
\sup\{b_{M}^{R}\}<\infty
, where
M
ranges over all finitely generated modules.

The second part of Theorem

generalizes
[
14
, Theorem 3.4.3]
, where Huneke and Jorgensen prove that if
R
is a local Gorenstein ring that is Ext bounded, then it is an AB ring. To see this, note that if
R
is Gorenstein, then
P_{R}(M,R)
is finite for all finitely generated modules
M
. So,
b_{M}^{R}
is finite for all finitely generated modules
M
, telling us that
R
has AC. By
[
7
, Proposition 4.2]

is AB, and so
[
14
, Theorem 3.4.3]
follows.

In Section
4
, we introduce an invariant that is dual to the Auslander bound and prove analogous results. In Section
5

, but in

Huneke and Jorgensen prove if

(Corollary

Let

2
Finite Gorenstein Dimension

The goal of this section is to prove Corollary
2.7
. Most of this section is spent proving Theorem
2.4
, the Artinian case of Corollary
2.7
, and reducing to the Artinian case.

Definition 2.1
.

A module
M
over a ring
R
has Gorenstein dimension zero if
M
is reflexive and if

\ext_{R}^{i}(M,R)=\ext_{R}^{i}(M^{*},R)=0

for all
i\geq 1
, where
M^{*}=\Hom_{R}(M,R)
. We say
M
has Gorenstein dimension
n
, denoted by
\gdim_{R}M=n
, if there exists an exact sequence

0\rightarrow G_{n}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow G_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

where
G_{i}
has Gorenstein dimension zero for all
i
, and no shorter exact sequence of this form exists.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
R
be an Artinian local ring, let
M
be a finitely generated module, let
E
be the injective hull of the residue field, and let
G
be a minimal free resolution of
E
. If
P_{R}(M,R)=0
and
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
, then the canonical map
\Hom_{R}(M,G)\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M,E)
is a quasiisomorphism.

Proof.

We prove the lemma in two steps.

Step 1:

\ext_{R}^{i}(M,\Omega^{n}(E))=0
for all
i\geq 1
and for all
n\geq 0
.

Since
\ext_{R}^{i}(M,R)=0
for
i\geq 1
and since
\ext_{R}^{i}(M,E)
for
i\geq 1
,
\ext_{R}^{i}(M,\Omega^{1}(E))=0
for
i\geq 2
. Suppose
\ext_{R}^{1}(M,\Omega^{1}(E))\neq 0
. Then

\ext_{R}^{i+j}(M,\Omega^{j}(E))\cong\ext_{R}^{i+1}(M,\Omega^{1}(E)).

for all
j\geq 1
and all
i\geq 0
. Thus
P_{R}(M,\Omega^{n}(E))=n
, so
\lim P_{R}(M,\Omega^{n}(E))=\infty
, contradicting the fact that
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
, giving us
P_{R}(M,\Omega^{1}(E))=0
.

A similar argument shows
P_{R}(M,\Omega^{n}(E))=0
for all
n\geq 0
.

Step 2:

\Hom_{R}(M,G^{\prime})
is exact where

G^{\prime}=\cdots\rightarrow G_{1}\rightarrow G_{0}\rightarrow E\rightarrow 0.

To see this, note that

0\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(E)\rightarrow G_{0}\rightarrow E\rightarrow 0

and

0\rightarrow\Omega^{2}(E)\rightarrow G_{1}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(E)\rightarrow 0

are exact, so by step 1

0\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M,\Omega^{1}(E))\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M,G_{0})\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M,E)\rightarrow 0

and

0\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M,\Omega^{2}(E))\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M,G_{1})\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M,M,\Omega^{1}(E))\rightarrow 0

are exact. Putting these two sequences together, we see that

0\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M,\Omega^{2}(E))\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M,G_{1})\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M,G_{0})\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M,E)\rightarrow 0

is exact. Continuing in this way, we get
\Hom_{R}(M,G^{\prime})
is exact.

Finally, since
\Hom_{R}(M,G^{\prime})
is exact,
\Hom_{R}(M,G)
is quasiisomorphic to
\Hom_{R}(M,E)
,
proving the lemma.
∎

Theorem 2.3

(Ext Tor Duality)
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian Local ring, let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules, and let
(\_)^{\vee}=\Hom_{R}(\_,E)
where
E=E_{R}(k)
is the injective hull of the residue field. Then for all
i

1.

\tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)^{\vee}\cong\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N^{\vee})

2.

\tor_{i}^{R}(M,N^{\vee})\cong\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)^{\vee}

The proof of the following Theorem is almost the same as the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 4.4]
. We write
\simeq
for quasiisomorphism.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
R
be an Artinian local ring and let
M
be a finitely generated module. If
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
and
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
, then
\gdim_{R}M=0
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega^{i}(M)
be the
ith
syzygy of
M
. Then
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
if and only if
b_{\Omega^{i}(M)}^{R}<\infty
and
\gdim_{R}M<\infty
if and only if
\gdim_{R}\Omega^{i}(M)<\infty
. Also, if
H=\Omega^{P_{R}(M,R)}(M)
, then
P_{R}(H,R)=0
. Thus, it suffices to prove that
P_{R}(M,R)=0
and
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
implies
\gdim_{R}M=0
. Let
F\rightarrow M
and
G\rightarrow E
be minimal free resolutions where
E
is the injective hull of the residue field. We claim the canonical map

\Hom_{R}(F,R)\otimes_{R}G\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(F,G)

is a quasiisomorphism. First note the following:

1.

Since
P_{R}(M,R)=0
, we have

\Hom_{R}(F,R)\simeq\Hom_{R}(M,R),

so

\Hom_{R}(F,R)\otimes_{R}G\simeq\Hom_{R}(M,R)\otimes_{R}G.

2.

We have

\Hom_{R}(M,R)\otimes_{R}G\cong\Hom_{R}(M,G)

since
G
is degreewise free of finite rank.

Therefore

\displaystyle\Hom_{R}(F,R)\otimes_{R}G\simeq\Hom_{R}(M,R)\otimes_{R}G\simeq\Hom_{R}(M,G)

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{R}(M,E)

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{R}(F,E)

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{R}(F,G),

where the first quasiisomorphism is by 1, the second is by 2, and the third is by the Lemma
2.2
. This proves the claim.

Now notice that
\Hom_{R}(F,G)
has homology only in degree zero since

H^{i}(\Hom_{R}(F,G))\cong\ext_{R}^{i}(M,E)

and
E
is injective, so
\Hom_{R}(F,R)\otimes_{R}G
has homology only in degree zero since

H^{i}(\Hom_{R}(F,R)\otimes_{R}G)\cong H^{i}(\Hom_{R}(F,G))

by the claim. Since

H^{i}(\Hom_{R}(F,R)\otimes_{R}G)\cong\tor_{i}^{R}(M^{*},E)

by 1,
\tor_{i}^{R}(M^{*},E)=0
for all
i\geq 1
, so

\ext_{R}^{i}(M^{*},R)\cong(\tor_{i}^{R}(M^{*},E))^{\vee}=0

for all
i\geq 1
by Ext Tor Duality. Thus
P_{R}(M^{*},R)=0
. Also,

M\cong(\ext_{R}^{0}(M,E))^{\vee}\cong(\tor_{0}^{R}(M^{*},E))^{\vee}\cong M^{**}

where the second isomorphism is by Ext Tor Duality and the claim. Since
M\cong M^{**}
,
M
is reflexive by
[
5
, Proposition 1.1.9.b]
.

Therefore, since
P_{R}(M,R)=0
,
P_{R}(M^{*},R)=0
, and
M
is reflexive,
M
has Gorenstein dimension zero.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring and let
X
and
Y
be finitely generated modules. If
x
is a nonzerodivisor on
R
,
X
, and
Y
, then

P_{R}(X,Y)=P_{R/x}(X/xX,Y/xY).

Proof.

The long exact sequence in Ext associated to

0\rightarrow Y\xrightarrow{x}Y\rightarrow Y/xY\rightarrow 0

and Nakayama’s lemma gives
P_{R}(X,Y)=P_{R}(X,Y/xY)
. Now by
[
19
, Lemma 2 page 140]
we have

\ext_{R}^{i}(X,Y/xY)\cong\ext_{R/x}^{i}(X/xX,Y/xY)

for all
i
, so
P_{R}(X,Y/xY)=P_{R/x}(X/xX,Y/xY)
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring, let
M
be a finitely generated module, and let
x
be a nonzerodivisor on
R
and
M
. If
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
, then
b_{M/xM}^{R/x}<\infty

Proof.

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R/x
module. Then

\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)\cong\ext_{R/x}^{i}(M/xM,N)

for all
i
by
[
19
, Lemma 2 page 140]
, so

P_{R}(M,N)=P_{R/x}(M/xM,N).

Therefore

\displaystyle b_{M/xM}^{R/x}

\displaystyle=\sup\{P_{R/x}(M/xM,N)|P_{R/x}(M/xM,N)<\infty\text{ and }N\text{ is a finitely generated }R/x\text{ module}\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{P_{R}(M,N)|P_{R}(M,N)<\infty\text{ and }N\text{ is a finitely generated }R/x\text{ module}\}

\displaystyle\leq b_{M}^{R}

\displaystyle<\infty.

∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring and let
M
be a finitely generated module. If
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
and
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
, then
\gdim_{R}M<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
d=\depth{R}-\depth{M}
and let
X
be the
dth
syzygy of
M
over
R
. Then
X
is MCM and

\ext_{R}^{i}(X,R)\cong\ext_{R}^{i+d}(M,R)

for all
i>0
, and so
P_{R}(X,R)
and
b_{X}^{R}
are finite (
X
is MCM by the Depth Lemma
[
4
, Proposition 1.2.9]
). Let
\underline{x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{\dim{R}}
be a maximal
R
sequence. Since
P_{R}(X,R)
is finite and since
P_{R}(X,R)=P_{R/\underline{x}}(X/\underline{x}X,R/\underline{x}R)
by Lemma
2.5
,
P_{R/\underline{x}}(X/\underline{x}X,R/\underline{x}R)
is finite. Also, Lemma
2.6
tells us that
b_{X/\underline{x}X}^{R/\underline{x}}
is finite since
b_{X}^{R}
is finite. Let
H
be the
Lth
syzygy of
X/\underline{x}X
over
R/\underline{x}
where
L=P_{R/\underline{x}}(X/\underline{x}X,R/\underline{x}R)
. Then
P_{R/\underline{x}}(H,R/\underline{x})=0
. Since
H
is a syzygy of
X/\underline{x}X
,
H
has a finite Auslander bound over
R/\underline{x}
, so
\gdim_{R/\underline{x}}H=0
by Theorem
2.4
.

Therefore
X/\underline{x}X
has finite Gorenstein dimension over
R/\underline{x}
, and so
X
has finite Gorenstein dimension over
R
by
[
5
, Corollary 1.4.6]
, implying that
M
has finite Gorenstein dimension over
R
.
∎

.

Let

be a CM local ring and let

3
Ext Bounded Rings

The goal of this section is to generalize a theorem of Huneke and Jorgensen. We do this in Corollary
3.13
. We prove the Artinian case in Theorem
3.5
, where most of the work is done. The following is an extension to the definition of Extgap in
[
14
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Set

\displaystyle d_{R}(M,N)=\sup\{r|

\displaystyle\text{there exists }n\text{ so that }\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)=0\text{ for }n+1\leq i\leq n+r

\displaystyle\text{ but }\ext_{R}^{n}(M,N)\neq 0\text{ and }\ext_{R}^{n+r+1}(M,N)\neq 0\}

and

\extgap_{R}(M)=\sup\{d_{R}(M,N)|d_{R}(M,N)<\infty\text{ and }N\text{ is a finitely generated module}\}.

The number
\extgap_{R}(M)
is called the
Ext gap of
M
.

Definition 3.2
.

Define the
Ext Bound of
R
to be

\sup\{\extgap_{R}(M)|M\text{ is a finitely generated module}\}

and denote it by
\extbound(R)
. We say
R
is Ext bounded if
\extbound(R)<\infty
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
M
be a module and let

P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

be the beginning of a projective resolution of
M
. Then the Auslander Transpose of
M
, denoted by
\tr(M)
, is the cokernel of the map

P_{0}^{*}\rightarrow P_{1}^{*}.

Note that the Auslander transpose is unique up to projective equivalence (see
[
18
, Proposition 4]
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring, let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules, and let
n\geq 2
be an integer. If
P_{R}(M,R)=0
, then

\tor_{i}^{R}(\tr(\Omega^{n+1}(M)),N)\cong\ext_{R}^{n+2-i}(M,N)

for
1\leq i\leq n+1
.

Proof.

The lemma follows from the fact that if
F
is a minimal free resolution of
M
, then

F_{0}^{*}\rightarrow F_{1}^{*}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow F_{n+1}^{*}\rightarrow F_{n+2}^{*}\rightarrow\tr(\Omega^{n+1}(M))\rightarrow 0

is the beginning of a minimal free resolution for
\tr(\Omega^{n+1}(M))
since
P_{R}(M,R)=0
.
∎

The proof of the following is very similar to
[
14
, Theorem 3.4.3]
, but we do not assume that
R
is Gorenstein.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
R
be an Artinian local ring that is Ext bounded and let
M
be a finitely generated module. If
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
, then
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
and
\gdim_{R}M=0
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega^{i}(M)
be the
ith
syzygy of
M
. Then
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
if and only if
b_{\Omega^{i}(M)}^{R}<\infty
and
\gdim_{R}M=0
if and only if
\gdim_{R}\Omega^{i}(M)=0
. Also, if
H=\Omega^{P_{R}(M,R)}(M)
, then
P_{R}(H,R)=0
. Thus it suffices to show
b_{H}^{R}<\infty
and
\gdim_{R}H=0
. Let
e=\extbound(R)
.

We first show
b_{H}^{R}<\infty
. Suppose
b_{H}^{R}>0
. Then there exists a module
N_{n}
with
P_{R}(H,N_{n})\geq 1
. Let
D
be an integer so that
D\geq P_{R}(H,N_{n})+e+2
. Then

\tor_{0}^{R}(\tr(\Omega^{D+1}(H)),N_{n})\cong\tr(\Omega^{D+1}(H))\otimes_{R}N_{n}\neq 0

and

\tor_{i}^{R}(\tr(\Omega^{D+1}(H)),N_{n})\cong\ext_{R}^{D+2-i}(H,N_{n})

for
1\leq i\leq D+1
by Lemma
3.4
, and so

\tor_{D+2-P_{R}(H,N_{n})}^{R}(\tr(\Omega^{D+1}(H)),N_{n})\cong\ext_{R}^{P_{R}(H,N_{n})}(H,N_{n})\neq 0

and

\tor_{i}^{R}(\tr(\Omega^{D+1}(H)),N_{n})\cong\ext_{R}^{D+2-i}(H,N_{n})=0

for
1\leq i\leq D+2-P_{R}(H,N_{n})-1
. Since

\ext_{R}^{i}(\tr(\Omega^{D+1}(H)),N_{n}^{\vee})\cong\tor_{i}^{R}(\tr(\Omega^{D+1}(H)),N_{n})^{\vee}

for all
i
by Ext Tor duality, we have

\ext_{R}^{i}(\tr(\Omega^{D+1}(H)),N_{n}^{\vee})=0

for
1\leq i\leq D+2-P_{R}(H,N_{n})-1
and

\ext_{R}^{0}(\tr(\Omega^{D+1}(H)),N_{n}^{\vee})\neq 0\neq\ext_{D+2-P_{R}(H,N_{n})}^{R}(\tr(\Omega^{D+1}(H)),N_{n}^{\vee}).

Since
D+2-P_{R}(H,N_{n})-1>e
, this tells us that
\extgap_{R}(\tr(\Omega^{D+1}(H)))>e
, a contradiction, so
b_{H}^{R}<\infty
.

Now since
P_{R}(H,R)<\infty
and
b_{H}^{R}<\infty
,
\gdim_{R}H=0
by Theorem
2.4
.
∎

The proof of the following is from
[

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring and let
x
be a nonzerodivisor on

. If

is Ext bounded, then
R/x
is Ext bounded.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that if

and

are finitely generated

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\rightarrow\ext_{R/x}^{1}(M,N)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\ext_{R}^{1}(M,N)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\ext_{R/x}^{0}(M,N)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\ext_{R/x}^{2}(M,N)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\ext_{R}^{2}(M,N)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\ext_{R/x}^{1}(M,N)\rightarrow\cdots

(this long exact sequence can be found in

The idea for the proof of the following came from
[
8
, Observation 1.4]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring with canonical module, let
M
be a finitely generated module, and let
x
be a nonzerodivisor on
R
and
M
. Then

b_{M}^{R}\leq\max\{\depth{R}-\depth{M},b_{M/xM}^{R/x}\}.

In particular, if
b_{M/xM}^{R/x}<\infty
, then
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
N
be a finitely generated module and assume
P_{R}(M,N)
is finite. Since
R
is CM with a canonical module,
[
2
, Theorem A]
gives an exact sequence of finitely generated modules

0\rightarrow I\rightarrow Y\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0

where
Y
is MCM and
\idim_{R}I<\infty
. Since
\idim_{R}I<\infty
,

P_{R}(M,I)=\depth{R}-\depth{M}

by a result of Ischebek
[
15
]
(also see
[
4
, Exercise 3.1.24]
), so

\displaystyle P_{R}(M,N)

\displaystyle\leq\max\{P_{R}(M,I),P_{R}(M,Y)\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\depth{R}-\depth{M},P_{R}(M,Y)\}.

Also, since
R
is CM and since
Y
is MCM,
x
is a nonzerodivisor on
Y
, so

P_{R}(M,Y)=P_{R/x}(M/xM,Y/xY)

by Lemma
2.5
. Therefore

\displaystyle P_{R}(M,N)

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\depth{R}-\depth{M},P_{R/x}(M/xM,Y/xY)\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\depth{R}-\depth{M},b_{M/xM}^{R/x}\}.

Now if
b_{M/xM}^{R/x}
is finite, then the work above shows

\sup\{P_{R}(M,N)|P_{R}(M,N)<\infty\}

is finite, so
b_{M}^{R}
is finite.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring that is Ext bounded and which has a canonical module, and let
M
be a finitely generated module. If
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
, then
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
and
\gdim_{R}M<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
X
be an MCM syzygy of
M
(such a syzygy exists by the Depth Lemma
[
4
, Proposition 1.2.9]
). Note the following:

1.

P_{R}(M,R)
is finite if and only if
P_{R}(X,R)
is finite

2.

b_{M}^{R}
is finite if and only if
b_{X}^{R}
is finite

3.

\gdim_{R}M
is finite if and only if
\gdim_{R}X
is finite.

We show that
P_{R}(X,R)<\infty
implies
b_{X}^{R}<\infty
and
\gdim_{R}X<\infty
. Let
\underline{x}
be a maximal regular sequence on
R
. Then

R/\underline{x}
is Ext bounded by Lemma
3.6
and

P_{R}(X,R)=P_{R/\underline{x}}(X/\underline{x}X,R/\underline{x})

by Lemma
2.5
, so
b_{X/\underline{x}X}^{R/\underline{x}}
and
\gdim_{R/\underline{x}}X/\underline{x}X
are both finite by Theorem
3.5
. Since
b_{X/\underline{x}X}^{R/\underline{x}}
is finite,
b_{X}^{R}
is finite by Lemma
3.7
. Also,
\gdim_{R}X
is finite since
\gdim_{R/\underline{x}}X/\underline{x}X
is finite.
∎

Proving Lemmas
3.9
,
3.10
,
3.11

allows us to reduce to the complete case. We can do this because every complete CM local ring has a canonical module. The ideas for these lemmas came from

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring and let
M
be a finitely generated module. If
b_{\hat{M}}^{\hat{R}}
is finite, then
b_{M}^{R}
is finite.

Proof.

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R
module. Then
\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)=0
if and only if

\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)\otimes_{R}\hat{R}\cong\ext_{\hat{R}}^{i}(\hat{M},\hat{N})=0

since
\hat{R}
is a faithfully flat
R
module, and so
P_{R}(M,N)=P_{\hat{R}}(\hat{M},\hat{N})
. Therefore

\displaystyle b_{M}^{R}

\displaystyle=\sup\{P_{R}(M,N)|P_{R}(M,N)<\infty\text{ and }N\text{ is a finitely generated }R\text{ module}\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{P_{\hat{R}}(\hat{M},\hat{N})|P_{\hat{R}}(\hat{M},\hat{N})<\infty\text{ and }N\text{ is a finitely generated }R\text{ module}\}

\displaystyle\leq b_{\hat{M}}^{\hat{R}}<\infty.

∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring and let
M
be a finitely generated module. Then
\gdim_{R}M
is finite if and only if
\gdim_{\hat{R}}\hat{M}
is finite.

Proof.

Let
F
be a minimal free resolution of
M
, let
d=\dim{R}
, and let
H=\Omega^{d}(M)
. Then
F\otimes_{R}\hat{R}\cong\hat{F}
is a minimal free resolution of
\hat{M}
and
\hat{H}=\Omega^{d}(\hat{M})
. Since
\gdim_{R}M<\infty
if and only if
\gdim_{R}H<\infty
and since
\gdim_{\hat{R}}\hat{M}<\infty
if and only if
\gdim_{\hat{R}}\hat{H}<\infty
, it suffices to show that
\gdim_{R}H<\infty
is finite if and only if
\gdim_{\hat{R}}\hat{H}<\infty
. Note that both
H
and
\hat{H}
are MCM since
R
is CM.

Let
\underline{x}
be a maximal
R
sequence. Then
\underline{x}
is a maximal sequence on
H
,
\hat{H}
, and
\hat{R}
. Since both
H/\underline{x}H
and
R/\underline{x}
have finite length, we have
H/\underline{x}H\cong\hat{H}/\underline{x}\hat{H}
and
R/\underline{x}\cong\hat{R}/\underline{x}
. Therefore

\gdim_{R}H=\gdim_{R/\underline{x}}H/\underline{x}H=\gdim_{\hat{R}/\underline{x}}\hat{H}/\underline{x}\hat{H}=\gdim_{\hat{R}}\hat{H}

where the first and third equality are by
[
5
, Proposition 1.4.5]
.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring with canonical module and let
x
be a nonzerodivisor. If
R/x
is Ext bounded, then
R
is Ext bounded.

Proof.

Let
M
and
N
be finitely generated
R
modules, let
d=\dim{R}
, and let
H=\Omega^{d}(M)
. Since
\ext_{R}^{i+d}(M,N)\cong\ext_{R}^{i}(H,N)
for all
i\geq 1
,
d_{R}(M,N)\leq d_{R}(H,N)+d+1
. Also, Since
R
is CM with canonical module,
[
2
, Theorem A]
gives an exact sequence of finitely generated modules

0\rightarrow I\rightarrow Y\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0

where
Y
is MCM and
\idim_{R}I<\infty
. Since
\idim_{R}I<\infty
,

P_{R}(H,I)=\depth{R}-\depth{H}

b a result of Ischebek
[
15
]
(also see
[
4
, Exercise 3.1.24]
), so
\ext_{R}^{i}(H,I)=0
for
i\geq 1
, telling us that

\ext_{R}^{i}(H,Y)\cong\ext_{R}^{i}(H,N)

for
i\geq 1
. We now have
d_{R}(H,N)=d_{R}(H,Y)
.

Now let
x
be a nonzerodivisor and let

e=\sup\{\extgap_{R/x}(W)|\text{W is an }R/x\text{ module}\},

the Ext bound for
R/x
. Then the long exact sequence in Ext associated to

0\rightarrow Y\xrightarrow{x}Y\rightarrow Y/xY\rightarrow 0

gives
d_{R}(H,Y)\leq d_{R}(H,Y/xY)+1
. By
[
19
, Lemma 2 page 140]
we have

\ext_{R}^{i}(H,Y/xY)\cong\ext_{R/x}^{i}(H/xH,Y/xY)

for all
i
, so
d_{R}(H,Y/xY)=d_{R/x}(H/xH,Y/xY)
, telling us that

d_{R}(H,Y)\leq d_{R/x}(H/xH,Y/xY)+1.

Therefore

d_{R}(M,N)\leq d_{R/x}(H/xH,Y/xY)+1+d+1\leq e+d+1+1.

Since
d_{R}(M,N)
is bounded above by
e+d+1+1
for all pairs of modules
(M,N)
,
R
is Ext bounded.
∎

Lemma 3.12
.

If
R
is a CM local ring that is Ext bounded, then
\hat{R}
is Ext bounded.

Proof.

Let
\underline{x}
be a maximal
R
sequence so that
R/\underline{x}\cong\hat{R}/\underline{x}
. If
R
is Ext bounded, then
R/\underline{x}
is Ext bounded by Lemma
3.6
, and so
\hat{R}/\underline{x}
is Ext bounded. Since
\hat{R}
is a CM local ring with canonical module and since
\hat{R}/\underline{x}
is Ext bounded,
\hat{R}
is Ext bounded by Lemma
3.11
.
∎

4
co-Auslander Bounds

In this section we study co-Auslander bounds and prove analogs of results from previous sections. Note that if
M
has finite injective dimension, then for all nonzero modules
N
we have
\ext_{R}^{n}(N,M)=0
for
n>\depth{R}-\depth{N}
and
\ext_{R}^{\depth{R}-\depth{N}}(N,M)\neq 0
by a result of Ischebek
[
15
]
(also see
[
4
, Exercise 3.1.24]
), so the vanishing of Ext is also related to depth when
M
has finite injective dimension.

What can be said about the vanishing of Ext when
M
does not necessarily have finite injective dimension?

Definition 4.1
.

For a finitely generated module
M
, set

cob_{M}^{R}=\sup\{P_{R}(N,M)|P_{R}(N,M)<\infty\text{ and }N\text{ is finitely generated}\}

The number
cob_{M}^{R}
is called the co-Auslander bound of
M
.

It seems like
cob_{M}^{R}
was first defined in
[
24
]
where it is called the small right Auslander bound of
M
and is denoted by
rAbM
. Since we always have
\ext_{R}^{n}(N,M)=0
for
n>\idim_{R}M
, modules with finite injective dimension have finite co-Auslander bound.

4.1
Finite Injective Dimensions

The goal of this subsection is to prove Corollary
4.8
, Corollary
4.11
, and Corollary
4.13
. We start by studying the co-Auslander bound modulo nonzerodivisors and the co-Auslander bound for MCM modules.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring, let
M
be a finitely generated module, and let
x
be a nonzerodivisor on
R
and
M
. Then
cob_{M/xM}^{R/x}\leq cob_{M}^{R}
. In particular, if
cob_{M}^{R}<\infty
, then
cob_{M/xM}^{R/x}<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R/x
module. Then

\ext_{R}^{n}(N,M)\cong\ext_{R/x}^{n-1}(N,M/xM)

for all
n
by
[
19
, Lemma 2 page 140]
, so

P_{R}(N,M)-1=P_{R/x}(N,M/xM).

Therefore

\displaystyle cob_{M/xM}^{R/x}

\displaystyle=\sup\{P_{R/x}(N,M/xM)|P_{R/x}(N,M/xM)<\infty\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\{P_{R}(N,M)|P_{R}(N,M)<\infty\}-1

\displaystyle\leq cob_{M}^{R}

\displaystyle<\infty.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring, let
M
be a finitely generated module, and let
x
be a nonzerodivisor on
R
and
M
. Then

cob_{M}^{R}\leq cob_{M/xM}^{R/x}+\depth{R}.

In particular, if
cob_{M/xM}^{R/x}<\infty
, then
cob_{M}^{R}<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
N
be a finitely generated module with
P_{R}(N,M)<\infty
. Let
d=\depth{R}-\depth{N}
and let
L=\Omega^{d}(N)
so that
L
is MCM (such a syzygy exists by the Depth Lemma
[
4
, Proposition 1.2.9]
). Then

P_{R}(N,M)\leq P_{R}(L,M)+d=P_{R/x}(L/xL,M/xM)+d\leq cob_{M/xM}^{R/x}+\depth{R}<\infty,

where the equality is by Lemma
2.5
, telling us that
cob_{M}^{R}
is finite.
∎

Definition 4.4
.

Assume
R
is a CM local ring with canonical module
\omega_{R}
. Let
M^{+}=\Hom_{R}(M,\omega_{R})
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring with canonical module
\omega_{R}
and let
M
be a finitely generated MCM module. Then

1.

If
\underline{x}
is a maximal
R
sequence, then
M^{+}/\underline{x}M^{+}
is the Matlis dual of
M/\underline{x}M
over
R/\underline{x}
. Also,
cob_{M}^{R}<\infty
if and only if
b_{M^{+}}^{R}<\infty
. In particular,
cob_{M}^{R}
is finite if
R
has AC.

2.

If
N
is a finitely generated MCM module, then
P_{R}(N,M)=P_{R}(M^{+},N^{+})
. In particular,
P_{R}(N,M)<\infty
if and only if
P_{R}(M^{+},N^{+})<\infty
.

Proof.

1. Let
\underline{x}
be a maximal
R
sequence. Then
cob_{M}^{R}<\infty
if and only if
cob_{M/\underline{x}M}^{R/\underline{x}}<\infty
by Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
. Also,
b_{M^{+}}^{R}<\infty
if and only if
b_{M^{+}/\underline{x}M^{+}}^{R/\underline{x}}<\infty
by Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
3.7
, so it suffices to show
cob_{M/\underline{x}M}^{R/\underline{x}}<\infty
if and only if
b_{M^{+}/\underline{x}M^{+}}^{R/\underline{x}}<\infty
. Note that
M^{+}
is MCM by
[
4
, Proposition 3.3.3.a]
.

Since
M
is MCM,
\ext_{R}^{n}(M,\omega_{R})=0
for
n>0
by
[
4
, Theorem 3.3.10.d]
, so
[
4
, Proposition 3.3.3.a]
gives us the second isomorphism below

\displaystyle M^{+}/\underline{x}M^{+}\cong\Hom_{R}(M,\omega_{R})\otimes_{R}R/\underline{x}

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{R/\underline{x}}(M/\underline{x}M,\omega_{R}/\underline{x}\omega_{R})

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{R/\underline{x}}(M/\underline{x}M,\omega_{R/\underline{x}}).

This tells us that
M^{+}/\underline{x}M^{+}
is the Matlis dual of
M/\underline{x}M
. Therefore, if
N
is a finitely generated
R/\underline{x}
module, then Ext Tor duality gives the second and fourth isomorphisms below

\displaystyle\ext_{R/\underline{x}}^{n}(N,M/\underline{x}M)\cong\ext_{R/\underline{x}}^{n}(N,M/\underline{x}M)^{\vee\vee}

\displaystyle\cong\tor_{n}^{R/\underline{x}}(N,(M/\underline{x}M)^{\vee})^{\vee}

\displaystyle\cong\tor_{n}^{R/\underline{x}}((M/\underline{x}M)^{\vee},N)^{\vee}

\displaystyle\cong\ext_{R/\underline{x}}^{n}((M/\underline{x}M)^{\vee},N^{\vee})

\displaystyle\cong\ext_{R/\underline{x}}^{n}(M^{+}/\underline{x}M^{+},N^{\vee}),

for all
n
where the last isomorphism is because
M^{+}/\underline{x}M^{+}
is the Matlis dual of
M/\underline{x}M
, so

P_{R/\underline{x}}(N,M/\underline{x}M)=P_{R/\underline{x}}(M^{+}/\underline{x}M^{+},N^{\vee}).

This last equality tells us that
cob_{M/\underline{x}M}^{R/\underline{x}}<\infty
if and only if
b_{M^{+}/\underline{x}M^{+}}^{R/\underline{x}}<\infty
.

2. Let
\underline{x}
be a maximal
R
sequence and let
N
be a finitely generated MCM module. By Lemma
2.5
we have

P_{R}(N,M)=P_{R/\underline{x}}(N/\underline{x}N,M/\underline{x}M)

and

P_{R}(M^{+},N^{+})=P_{R/\underline{x}}(M^{+}/\underline{x}M^{+},N^{+}/\underline{x}N^{+}),

so it suffices to show
P_{R/\underline{x}}(N/\underline{x}N,M/\underline{x}M)=P_{R/\underline{x}}(M^{+}/\underline{x}M^{+},N^{+}/\underline{x}N^{+})
. The work above shows
M^{+}/\underline{x}M^{+}
is the Matlis dual of
M/\underline{x}M
,
N^{+}/\underline{x}N^{+}
is the Matlis dual of
N/\underline{x}N
, and

\ext_{R/\underline{x}}^{n}(N/\underline{x}N,M/\underline{x}M)\cong\ext_{R/\underline{x}}^{n}((M/\underline{x}M)^{\vee},N/\underline{x}N)^{\vee}),

finishing the proof.
∎

Note that MCM modules of finite injective dimension are known as
Gorenstein modules
and were first introduced in
[

]
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring with canonical module
\omega_{R}
and let
M
be a finitely generated MCM module. If
cob_{M}^{R}
,
P_{R}(\omega_{R},M)
, and
P_{R}(M,M)
are all finite, then
M
has finite injective dimension and
M
is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of copies of
\omega_{R}
.

Proof.

Notice that
b_{M^{+}}^{R}
,
P_{R}(M^{+},\omega_{R}^{+})=P_{R}(M^{+},R)
, and
P_{R}(M^{+},M^{+})
are all finite by Lemma
4.5
, so
M^{+}
has finite projective dimension by
[
24
, Theorem 2.11.1]
. Since
M^{+}
is MCM by
[
4
, Proposition 3.3.3.a]
,
M^{+}
is free by the Auslander Buchsbaum formula, so
M^{+}=\oplus R
is a finite direct sum of copies of
R
. Therefore

M\cong M^{++}\cong\Hom_{R}(M^{+},\omega_{R})\cong\oplus\omega_{R}

where the first isomorphism is by
[
4
, Theorem 3.3.10]
. Thus
M
has finite injective dimension.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring and let

0\rightarrow I\rightarrow Y\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

be an exact sequence of finitely generated modules with
\idim_{R}I<\infty
. Then

1.

cob_{E}^{R}\leq\depth{R}
for all finitely generated modules
E
with finite injective dimension. In particular,
cob_{I}^{R}\leq\depth{R}
.

2.

If
N
is a finitely generated module with
P_{R}(N,M)<\infty
, then
P_{R}(N,Y)<\infty
.

3.

If
cob_{M}^{R}<\infty
, then
cob_{Y}^{R}<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
N
be a finitely generated module. Since
P_{R}(N,E)=\depth{R}-\depth{N}
by a result of Ischebek
[
15
]
(also see
[
4
, Exercise 3.1.24]
), we have

cob_{E}^{R}\leq\depth{R}<\infty.

Also, if
P_{R}(N,Y)
is finite, then
P_{R}(N,M)
is finite since
P_{R}(N,I)
is finite and

0\rightarrow I\rightarrow Y\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

is exact, so

P_{R}(N,Y)\leq\max\{P_{R}(N,I),P_{R}(N,M)\}\leq\max\{\depth{R},cob_{M}^{R}\}.

Therefore
cob_{Y}^{R}<\infty
if
cob_{M}^{R}<\infty
.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring with canonical module
\omega_{R}
and let

be a finitely generated module. If
cob_{M}^{R}
,
P_{R}(\omega_{R},M)
, and
P_{R}(M,M)
are all finite, then
M
has finite injective dimension.

Proof.

Since
R
is CM with canonical module,
[
2
, Theorem A]
gives an exact sequence of finitely generated modules

(1)

0\rightarrow I\rightarrow Y\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

where
Y
is MCM and
\idim_{R}I<\infty
. We claim that

is finite.

To see this, first take an exact sequence

Such an exact sequence exists by work of Auslander and Buchweitz

since

Since

and

are finite, so

is finite. By induction

is finite. Since the exact sequence (

is finite. Again using the fact that

is exact, we see that

is finite since both

and

are finite, proving the claim.

Since

and

,

and
P_{R}(\omega_{R},Y)<\infty
by Lemma
4.7
. Since

,
P_{R}(\omega_{R},Y)
, and

are all finite,

has finite injective dimension by Theorem

. Since

is exact and since

has finite injective dimension.
∎

4.2
Bounds for the co-Auslander Bound

The goal of this subsection is to prove Proposition

. The following was proved in

, Proposition 3.2]

is Gorenstein and every module has a finite Auslander bound. The proof is the same, but we include a proof for completeness.

Lemma 4.18
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring and let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules with
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
and
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
. If
P_{R}(M,N)<\infty
, then
\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Note that
M
has finite Gorenstein dimension by Corollary
2.7
. Let
d=\depth{R}-\depth{M}
, let
L=\Omega^{d}(M)
, and let

C=\cdots\rightarrow C_{1}\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}C_{0}\xrightarrow{\partial_{0}}C_{-1}\rightarrow\cdots

be a complete resolution of
M
with
L=\coker{\partial_{1}}
and
C_{i}
a finitely generated free module for all
i
(see
[
3
, 4.4.4]
). Note that
\gdim_{R}M<\infty
by Corollary
2.7
. Since
P_{R}(L,N)<\infty
,
P_{R}(\coker{\partial_{i}},N)<\infty
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. By Corollary
2.8

0\leq P_{R}(\coker{\partial_{i}},N)\leq\depth{R}-\depth{\coker{\partial_{i}}}=0

for all
i
, so
H^{n}(\Hom_{R}(C,N))=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore

\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{n}(M,N)=H^{n}(\Hom_{R}(C,N))=0

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Lemma 4.19
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring with canonical module
\omega_{R}
and let
M
be a finitely generated MCM module with
P_{R}(\omega_{R},M)<\infty
and
cob_{M}^{R}<\infty
. Then

1.

If
N
is a finitely generated module with
P_{R}(N,M)<\infty
, then

P_{R}(N,M)\leq\depth{R}-\depth{N}.

2.

cob_{M}^{R}\leq\depth{R}=\gidim_{R}M
.

Proof.

Let
d=\depth{R}-\depth{N}
and let
L=\Omega^{d}(N)
be the
dth
syzygy of
N
. Then
L
is MCM (
L
is MCM by the Depth Lemma
[
4
, Proposition 1.2.9]
) and
P_{R}(N,M)\leq P_{R}(L,M)+d
(this can be seen by induction by starting with applying
\Hom_{R}(\_,M)
to

0\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(N)\rightarrow G_{0}\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0,

where
G_{0}
is a free). Also, by Lemma
4.5
we have
P_{R}(M^{+},R)=P_{R}(\omega_{R},M)<\infty
,
P_{R}(M^{+},L^{+})=P_{R}(L,M)<\infty
, and
b_{M^{+}}^{R}<\infty
, so

b_{M^{+}}^{R}=\depth{R}-\depth{M^{+}}=0

by Corollary
2.8
. Therefore

P_{R}(N,M)\leq P_{R}(L,M)+d=P_{R}(M^{+},L^{+})+d\leq b_{M^{+}}^{R}+d=d=\depth{R}-\depth{N}.

Since

P_{R}(N,M)\leq\depth{R}-\depth{N}

for all modules
N
with
P_{R}(N,M)<\infty
, we have
cob_{M}^{R}\leq\depth{R}
. Note that
\gidim_{R}M<\infty
by Corollary
4.11
and
\depth{R}=\gidim_{R}M
by
[
5
, Theorem 6.2.15]
.
∎

Proposition 4.20
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring with canonical module
\omega_{R}
and let
M
be a finitely generated MCM module with
P_{R}(\omega,M)<\infty
and
cob_{M}^{R}<\infty
. If
\underline{x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{\dim{R}}
is a maximal
R
sequence, then
P_{R}(\omega_{R/\underline{x}},M)=\depth{R}
and
cob_{M}^{R}=\depth{R}
.

Proof.

First note that
P_{R}(\omega_{R/\underline{x}},M)
is finite (this can be seen by applying
\Hom_{R}(\_,M)
to

0\rightarrow\omega_{R}\xrightarrow{x_{1}}\omega_{R}\rightarrow\omega_{R/x_{1}}\rightarrow 0

and using induction), so

P_{R}(\omega_{R/\underline{x}},M)\leq\depth{R}-\depth{\omega_{R/\underline{x}}}=\depth{R}

by Lemma
4.19
. Also,

\ext_{R}^{\depth{R}}(\omega_{R/\underline{x}},M)\cong\ext_{R/\underline{x}}^{0}(\omega_{R/\underline{x}},M/\underline{x}M)=\Hom_{R/\underline{x}}(\omega_{R/\underline{x}},M/\underline{x}M)\neq 0

where the isomorphism is by
[
19
, Lemma 2 page 140]
, so
P_{R}(\omega_{R/\underline{x}},M)=\depth{R}
. Therefore

\depth{R}\leq cob_{M}^{R}\leq\depth{R}

where the second inequality is by Lemma
4.19
, so
cob_{M}^{R}=\depth{R}
.

Note that
\Hom_{R/\underline{x}}(\omega_{R/\underline{x}},M/\underline{x}M)\neq 0
since
\omega_{R/\underline{x}}
maps onto the residue field of
R/\underline{x}
and since the residue field of
R/\underline{x}
injects into
M/\underline{x}M
.
∎

5
Symmetry of Ext

The goal of this section is to generalize another theorem of Hueneke and Jorgensen. We do this in Theorem
5.4
.

Definition 5.1
.

For modules
M
and
N
let
q_{R}(M,N)
be

q_{R}(M,N)=\sup\{n|\tor_{n}^{R}(M,N)\neq 0\}.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring and let
L,Q,N
be finitely generated modules with
L^{*}\otimes_{R}N\cong\Hom_{R}(L,N)
,
q_{R}(N,L^{*})=0
, and
P_{R}(L^{*},R)=0
. If
\ext_{R}^{n}(\Hom_{R}(L,N),R)=0
for
n\gg 0
, then
\ext_{R}^{n}(N,L)=0
for
n\gg 0
.

Proof.

If
F\simeq N
and
G\simeq L^{*}
are free resolutions, then

\displaystyle 0=\ext_{R}^{n}(\Hom_{R}(L,N),R)\cong\ext_{R}^{n}(N\otimes_{R}L^{*},R)

\displaystyle\cong H^{n}(\Hom_{R}(F\otimes_{R}G,R))

\displaystyle\cong H^{n}(\Hom_{R}(F,\Hom_{R}(G,R)))

\displaystyle\cong H^{n}(\Hom_{R}(F,L))

\displaystyle\cong\ext_{R}^{n}(N,L)

for
n\gg 0
. Since
q_{R}(N,L^{*})=0
,
F\otimes_{R}G
is a resolution of
N\otimes_{R}L^{*}
, giving us the second isomorphism. Also,
P_{R}(L^{*},R)=0
implies
\Hom_{R}(G,R))\simeq L
, giving us the fourth isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring and let
L
and
N
be finitely generated modules with
\gdim_{R}L=0
and
\ext_{R}^{n}(\Hom_{R}(L,N),R)=0
for
n\gg 0
. If
\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{n}(L,N)=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
P_{R}(N,L)<\infty
.

Proof.

Since

\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{n}(L,N)\cong\widehat{\tor}_{-n-1}^{R}(L^{*},N)

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\widehat{\tor}_{-n-1}^{R}(L^{*},N)=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, so
q_{R}(L^{*},N)=0
since
\gdim_{R}L^{*}=0
and
\tor_{n}^{R}(L^{*},N)\cong\widehat{\tor}_{n}^{R}(L^{*},N)
for
n\geq 1
. Also,
\widehat{\tor}_{n}^{R}(L^{*},N)=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
implies
L^{*}\otimes_{R}N\cong\Hom_{R}(L,N)
by
[
9
, Proposition 6.3]
. We now have
L^{*}\otimes_{R}N\cong\Hom_{R}(L,N)
,
q_{R}(L^{*},N)=0
,
P_{R}(L^{*},R)=0
, and
\ext_{R}^{n}(\Hom_{R}(L,N),R)=0
for
n\gg 0
, so
\ext_{R}^{n}(N,L)=0
for
n\gg 0
by Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Using Lemma
4.18
and Lemma
4.22
we can generalize a theorem of Huneke and Jorgensen. The following was proved in
[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
but assumes
R
is Gorenstein and every module has a finite Auslander bound. The proof is similar to
[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
. Also, recall that
cob_{M}^{R}
is finite over CM local rings that have AC by Proposition
4.15
. Note that
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
,
P_{R}(N,R)<\infty
,
\gdim_{R}N<\infty
,
\ext_{R}^{n}(\Hom_{R}(L,N),R)=0
for
n\gg 0
, and
\ext_{R}^{n}(\Hom_{R}(Q,M),R)=0
for

are automatic if

is Gorenstein. Finitely generated modules over CM rings have MCM syzygies by the Depth Lemma
[

.

and

Assume

,

, and

,

,

, then
P_{R}(N,M)<\infty

2.

If

and

P_{R}(M,N)<\infty

In particular, If

The following is a converse to Corollary

Proposition 5.8
.

Let

be a Gorenstein local ring and let

be a finitely generated module. If

if and only if

if and only if
cob_{M}^{R}<\infty

Proof.

Let

. Then

is MCM. Note that

if and only if

. Also,
R

cob_{M}^{R}<\infty
if and only if
cob_{L}^{R}<\infty
. Let

. Then

\ext_{R}^{n}(M,N)\cong\ext_{R}^{n-d}(L,N)

for

and

\ext_{R}^{n}(L,N)\cong\ext_{R}^{n+d}(L,Q)

for

, so

\ext_{R}^{n}(M,N)\cong\ext_{R}^{n}(L,Q)

for

if and only if
P_{R}(L,Q)
. Similarly

if and only if
P_{R}(Q,L)<\infty
, so
P_{R}(L,Q)<\infty
if and only if
P_{R}(Q,L)<\infty
. Thus is suffices to show
b_{L}^{R}<\infty
if and only if
cob_{L}^{R}<\infty
. Let

be a maximal

is a finitely generated module over an Artinian Gorenstein local ring.

First assume

and

\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{n}(L,Q)=0

Now assume

4.22

\widehat{\tor}_{n}^{R}(Q^{*},L)=0
for all

P_{R}(L,Q)=q_{R}(L,Q^{\vee})=q_{R}(L,Q^{*})=0

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring with
cob_{R}^{R}<\infty
and let

be a finitely generated MCM module with
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
and

. If
P_{R}(N,M)<\infty

P_{R}(M,N)<\infty
for all finitely generated modules

, then

Proof.

Suppose
P_{R}(N,M)<\infty
. Then
P_{R}(M,N)<\infty
, so
\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
by Lemma
4.18

\widehat{\tor}_{n}^{R}(M^{*},N)=0
for all

6
An Auslander Bound for Complexes

In this section
R
is a commutative ring (not necessarily Noetherian and not necessarily local). All complexes are indexed homologically, so if
M
is a complex, then

M=\dots\rightarrow M_{i+1}\rightarrow M_{i}\rightarrow M_{i-1}\rightarrow\dots.

Let
D(R)
be the derived category. Let
D^{b}(R)
be the full subcategory of
D(R)
consisting of all complexes isomorphic to a bounded complex in
D(R)
to a bounded complex and let
D_{f}^{b}(R)
be the subcategory of
D^{b}(R)
whose complexes have degreewise finitely generated homology. Since every homologically bounded complex is quasiisomorphic to a bounded complex,
D^{b}(R)
is the same as the full subcategory of
D(R)
consisting of all complexes with bounded homology.

6.1
Defining
B_{f}(M)

Definition 6.1
.

If
M
is a complex, then

\sup{M}=\sup\{i|M_{i}\neq 0\}

and

\inf{M}=\inf\{i|M_{i}\neq 0\}.

Note that in
[
5
]
the notation
\inf{M}
is used to denote
\inf{H(M)}
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
M
and
N
be a complexes with
\inf{H(N)}
an integer and define

P_{R}(M,N)=\sup\{n\in\mathbb{Z}|\ext_{R}^{n-\inf{H(N)}}(M,N)\neq 0\}.

For
M\in D_{f}^{b}(R)
define

B_{f}(M)=\sup\{P_{R}(M,N)|0\not\simeq N\in D_{f}^{b}(R)\text{ and }P_{R}(M,N)<\infty\}.

Note that
P_{R}(M,N)
is invariant under shifting
N
. The paper
[
23
]
is where we learned about projective dimension and Gorenstein projective dimension of complexes.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
M
and
P
be complexes. We say that
P
is
semiprojective
if
P_{i}
is projective for all
i
and
\Hom_{R}(P,\_)
preserves quasiisomorphisms. The complex
P
is a
semiprojective resolution
of
M
if there exists a quasiisomorphism
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}P\rightarrow M
with
P
semiprojective. The
projective dimension
of
M
is defined to be the infimum of
p\in\mathbb{Z}
for which there exists a semiprojective resolution
P\rightarrow M
with
P_{i}=0
for all
i>p
.

The following motivates our definition of
B_{f}(M)
.

Example 6.4
.

One would expect
B_{f}(M)=\pdim_{R}M
when
\pdim_{R}M<\infty
, and this is the case. Assume
\pdim_{R}M<\infty
where
M\in D_{f}^{b}(R)
. Let

D_{1}=\sup\{\inf{H(N)}-\inf{H(\mathbb{R}\Hom_{R}(M,N))}|0\not\simeq N\in D^{b}(R)\}

and

D_{2}=\sup\{-\inf{H(\mathbb{R}\Hom_{R}(M,N))}|N\text{ is a finitely generated module}\}.

Then
\pdim_{R}M=D_{1}=D_{2}
by
[
5
, Corollary A.5.4]
. Note that

-\inf{H(\mathbb{R}\Hom_{R}(M,N))}=\sup\{n\in\mathbb{Z}|\ext_{R}^{n}(M,N)\neq 0\},

so

\displaystyle\inf{H(N)}-\inf{H(\mathbb{R}\Hom_{R}(M,N))}

\displaystyle=\sup\{n\in\mathbb{Z}|\ext_{R}^{n-\inf{H(N)}}(M,N)\neq 0\}

\displaystyle=P_{R}(M,N).

Therefore
P_{R}(M,N)<\infty
for all
N\in D^{b}(R)
with
N\not\simeq 0
,

D_{1}=\sup\{P_{R}(M,N)|0\not\simeq N\in D^{b}(R)\},

and

D_{2}=\sup\{P_{R}(M,N)|N\text{ is a finitely generated module}\},

which gives

\pdim_{R}M=D_{2}\leq B_{f}(M)\leq D_{1}=\pdim_{R}M.

Definition 6.5
.

Let

L=\dots\rightarrow L_{i+1}\xrightarrow{\partial_{i+1}^{L}}L_{i}\xrightarrow{\partial_{i}^{L}}L_{i-1}\rightarrow\dots

be a complex and define

1.

C_{i}(L)=\text{coker}{\partial_{i+1}^{L}}

2.

\Sigma^{n}L
is the complex with
(\Sigma^{n}L)_{i}=L_{i-n}
and
\partial_{i}^{\Sigma^{n}L}=(-1)^{n}\partial_{i-n}^{L}

3.

L_{\subset n}=0\rightarrow C_{n}(L)\rightarrow L_{n-1}\rightarrow L_{n-2}\rightarrow\dots

4.

L_{\leq n}=0\rightarrow L_{n}\rightarrow L_{n-1}\rightarrow L_{n-2}\rightarrow\dots

5.

L_{\geq n}=\dots\rightarrow L_{n+2}\rightarrow L_{n+1}\rightarrow L_{n}\rightarrow 0

The proof of the following is almost the same as the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 2.10]
.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
R
be a ring and let

and

be complexes with
\gpd_{R}M<\infty
and
N
bounded. If
L\rightarrow N
is a semiprojective resolution, then for each

\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)\cong\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{i+n}(M,C_{n}(L))

for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let
T\rightarrow P\rightarrow M
be a complete projective resolution of
M
and let
L\rightarrow N
be a semiprojective resolution of
N
with
\inf{L}=\inf{H(N)}
so that

is bounded below. Such a resolution exists by
[

so that there is a quasiisomorphism
\widetilde{\pi}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}L_{\subset n}\rightarrow N

\widetilde{\pi}

\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)\cong\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{i}(M,L_{\subset n})

for all

[

Now consider the exact sequence

\star\quad\quad\quad 0\rightarrow L_{\leq n-1}\rightarrow L_{\subset n}\rightarrow\Sigma^{n}C_{n}(L)\rightarrow 0.

Since

.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring and let

be a complex with

and
\gdim_{R}M<\infty
. Then
B_{f}(M)\leq\gdim_{R}M
.

Proof.

Let
N\in D_{f}^{b}(R)
be a complex with
P_{R}(M,N)<\infty
. Since
P_{R}(M,N)=P_{R}(M,N^{\prime})
if
N
is quasiisomorphic to
N^{\prime}
and since
N
is quasiisomorphic to a bounded complex, we can assume
N
is bounded. Since
P_{R}(M,N)
is invariant under taking shifts of
N
, we can assume
\inf{N}=0
. Suppose
P_{R}(M,N)>\gdim_{R}M
. By Lemma
6.6
, if

, then

\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)\cong\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{i+n}(M,C_{n}(L))

for all

. Also,

\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{i}(M,X)\cong\ext_{R}^{i}(M,X)

. Therefore

\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)\cong\ext_{R}^{i+n}(M,C_{n}(L))

∎

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring and let
M
and
0\not\simeq N
be complexes in
D_{f}^{b}(R)
with
B_{f}(M)<\infty
,
\gdim_{R}M<\infty
, and

, then
\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)=0
for all

.

Proof.

Since
P_{R}(M,N)
is invariant under taking shifts of
N
, we can assume
\inf{N}=0
. Let
T\rightarrow P\rightarrow M
be a complete projective resolution. Then for all

we have

\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)\cong\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{i-n}(C_{n}(T),N)

for all

by
[

, Lemma 4.3]

[

, 4.1.1]

\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)\cong\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)

for

. Also,

\widehat{\ext}_{R}^{i}(C_{n}(T),N)\cong\ext_{R}^{i}(C_{n}(T),N)

\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)\cong\ext_{R}^{i-n}(C_{n}(T),N)

.

and since

\mathbb{R}\Hom_{R}(M,N)

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem

Such a resolution in Lemma

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring, let
M\in D_{f}^{b}(R)
be a complex, and let
P\rightarrow M
be a semiprojective resolution with
\inf{P}=\inf{H(M)}
and
P_{i}
finitely generated for all

is a complex and

In particular,
B_{f}(M)<\infty

Let

\ext_{R}^{i}(P_{\leq n-1},N)=0

Lemma 6.13
.

Let
R

N
be bounded complexes. Assume
P_{R}(M_{i},N_{j})=0
for all
i
and all
j
. If
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}N\rightarrow I
is a quasiisomorphism where
I
is a complex of injectives that is bounded to the left, then

\Hom_{R}(M,\pi)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Hom(M,N)\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M,I)

is a quasiisomorphism.

Proof.

By
[
6
, Proposition 2.6]
it is enough to show

\Hom_{R}(M_{i},\pi)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Hom_{R}(M_{i},N)\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M_{i},I)

is a quasiisomorphism for each
i
since
M
is bounded to the right. The proof would be complete if we can show

\cone{\Hom_{R}(M_{i},\pi)}

is exact. Since

\cone{\Hom_{R}(M_{i},\pi)}\cong\Hom_{R}(M_{i},\cone{\pi}),

it is enough to show
\Hom_{R}(M_{i},\cone{\pi})
is exact.

Note that
\cone{\pi}
is exact since
\pi
is a quasiisomorphism and is a bounded to the left complex

0\rightarrow(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}}\xrightarrow{\partial_{0}}(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}-1}\xrightarrow{\partial_{-1}}(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}-2}\rightarrow\dots

with
(\cone{\pi})_{j}=I_{j}\oplus N_{j-1}
and
P_{R}(M_{i},(\cone{\pi})_{j})=0
since
I_{j}
is injective and
P_{R}(M_{i},N_{j})=0
for all
i
and all
j
. Since

0\rightarrow(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}}\rightarrow(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}-1}\rightarrow\text{im}{\partial_{-1}}\rightarrow 0

is exact and since
P_{R}(M_{i},(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}})=P_{R}(M_{i},(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}-1})=0
, we have
P_{R}(M_{i},\text{im}{\partial_{-1}})=0
, so

0\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M_{i},(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}})\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M_{i},(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}-1})\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M_{i},\text{im}{\partial_{-1}})\rightarrow 0

is exact. Also,

0\rightarrow\text{im}{\partial_{-1}}\rightarrow(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}-2}\rightarrow\text{im}{\partial_{-2}}\rightarrow 0

is exact and
P_{R}(M_{i},(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}-2})=P_{R}(M_{i},\text{im}{\partial_{-1}})=0
, so

0\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M_{i},\text{im}{\partial_{-1}})\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M_{i},(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}-2})\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M_{i},\text{im}{\partial_{-2}})\rightarrow 0

is exact and
P_{R}(M_{i},\text{im}{\partial_{-2}})=0
. Therefore

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M_{i},(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M_{i},(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}-1})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M_{i},(\cone{\pi})_{\inf{\cone{\pi}}-2})\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M_{i},\text{im}{\partial_{-2}})\rightarrow 0

is exact. Continuing in this way finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.14
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring and let
M
be a finitely generated module with
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
. Then we have the following.

1.

b_{M}^{R}<\infty
if and only if
B_{f}(M)<\infty
. In particular, if
\gdim_{R}M<\infty
, then
b_{M}^{R}=B_{f}(M)
.

2.

Let
N\in D_{f}^{b}(R)
be a complex and assume
Q\rightarrow N
is a semiprojective resolution with
\inf{Q}=\inf{H(N)}
and
Q_{i}
is finitely generated for all
i
. If
n>\sup{H(N)}
, then

(a)

P_{R}(M,N)\leq\max\{P_{R}(M,C_{n}(Q))-n,n\}+P_{R}(M,R)

(b)

P_{R}(M,C_{n}(Q))\leq\max\{P_{R}(M,N)+n,n\}+P_{R}(M,R)
.

Proof.

Since
b_{M}^{R}\leq B_{f}(M)
is always true, we just need to show
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
implies
B_{f}(M)<\infty
. Let
L=\Omega^{P_{R}(M,R)}(M)
so that
P_{R}(L,R)=0
. Since
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
if and only if
b_{L}^{R}<\infty
and since
B_{f}(M)<\infty
if and only if
B_{f}(L)<\infty
, it is enough to show
b_{L}^{R}<\infty
implies
B_{f}(L)<\infty
.

Let
N\in D_{f}^{b}(R)
be a complex with semiprojective resolution
Q\rightarrow N
where
\inf{Q}=\inf{H(N)}
and
Q_{i}
is finitely generated for all
i
. Let
n>\sup{H(N)}
. Since
P_{R}(L,N)
is invariant under shifting
N
, we can assume
\inf{H(N)}=0
. Now consider the exact sequence

\star\quad\quad 0\rightarrow Q_{\leq n-1}\rightarrow Q\rightarrow Q_{\geq n}\rightarrow 0

and let
Q_{\leq n-1}\rightarrow I
be a semiinjective resolution with
I
bounded to the left. By Lemma
6.13

\Hom_{R}(L,Q_{\leq n-1})
and
\Hom_{R}(L,I)
are quasiisomorphic (Lemma
6.13
applies since projective modules are free over local rings), so

\ext_{R}^{i}(L,Q_{\leq n-1})\cong H_{-i}(\Hom_{R}(L,I))\cong H_{-i}(\Hom_{R}(L,Q_{\leq n-1}))=0

for
i>n-1
. Since
\star
is exact and since
Q_{\geq n}\simeq\Sigma^{n}C_{n}(Q)
,

0\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\Hom_{R}(L,Q_{\leq n-1})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\Hom_{R}(L,N)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\Hom_{R}(L,\Sigma^{n}C_{n}(Q))\rightarrow 0

is exact, so

\star\star\quad\quad\ext_{R}^{i}(L,N)\cong\ext_{R}^{i}(L,\Sigma^{n}C_{n}(Q))\cong\ext_{R}^{i+n}(L,C_{n}(Q))

for
i>n
, proving
b_{L}^{R}<\infty
implies
B_{f}(L)<\infty
(note that
\star\star
also proves 2).

Finally note that if
\gdim_{R}M<\infty
and one of
b_{M}^{R}
or
B_{f}(M)
is finite, then

b_{M}^{R}=\gdim_{R}M=B_{f}(M)

where the first equality is by Corollary
2.8
and the second equality is by Theorem
6.11
.
∎

Lemma 6.15
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring, let
M\in D_{f}^{b}(R)
be a complex with
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
, and let
P\rightarrow M
be a semi projective resolution with
\inf{P}=\inf{H(M)}
and
P_{i}
finitely generated for all
i
. Assume
n>\sup{H(M)}
and consider the following.

1.

B_{f}(M)<\infty

2.

b_{C}^{R}<\infty

3.

b_{\hat{C}}^{\hat{R}}<\infty

4.

B_{f}(\hat{M})<\infty
,

where
C=C_{n}(P)
. Then we have the the implications
1\iff 2\Longleftarrow 3\iff 4
. In particular,
B_{f}(M)<\infty
if
R
has AC. If
R
is also CM, then 2 and 3 are equivalent.

Proof.

First note that
P_{R}(C,R)<\infty
by Lemma
6.12
.

1\iff 2
The is an immediate consequence of Lemma
6.12
and Lemma
6.14

2\Longleftarrow 3
This follows from Lemma
3.9
.

2\Longrightarrow 3
This follows from Proposition
3.14
.

3\iff 4
Since
\hat{P}\rightarrow\hat{M}
is a semiprojective resolution of
\hat{M}
, we have
\hat{C}\cong C_{n}(\hat{P})
, so this follows from Lemma
6.12
and Lemma
6.14
.
∎

Theorem 6.16
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring and let
M\in D_{f}^{b}(R)
be a complex. If
B_{f}(M)<\infty
and
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
, then
\gdim_{R}M<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
P\rightarrow M
be a semi projective resolution with
\inf{P}=\inf{H(M)}
and
P_{i}
finitely generated for all
i
and let
C=C_{n}(P)
where
n>\sup{H(M)}+1
. Then
b_{C}^{R}<\infty
by Lemma
6.15
. Also,
P_{R}(C,R)<\infty
by Lemma
6.12
. If
P_{R}(C,R)
does not equal zero, then we can take syzygies of
C
to assume
P_{R}(C,R)=0
, so
\gdim_{R}C<\infty
by Corollary
2.7
and
\gdim_{R}C=0
since
P_{R}(C,R)=0
. Therefore

0\rightarrow C\rightarrow P_{n-1}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow P_{\inf{P}}\rightarrow 0

is a finite G resolution of
M
, so
\gdim_{R}M<\infty
.
∎

Theorem 6.17
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring and let
M\in D_{f}^{b}(R)
be a complex. If
B_{f}(M)<\infty
,
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
, and
P_{R}(M,M)<\infty
, then
\pdim_{R}M<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
P\rightarrow M
be a semi projective resolution with
\inf{P}=\inf{H(M)}
and
P_{i}
finitely generated for all
i
and let
C=C_{n}(P)
where
n>\sup{H(M)}+1
. Then
b_{C}^{R}<\infty
by Lemma
6.15
. Also,
P_{R}(C,R)<\infty
by Lemma
6.12
. If
P_{R}(C,R)
does not equal zero, then we can take syzygies of

6.3
Derived Depth Formula

Definition 6.18
.

Let
M
and
N
be a complexes and define

q_{R}(M,N)=\sup\{n\in\mathbb{Z}|\tor_{n+\sup{H(N)}}^{R}(M,N)\neq 0\}.

Note that
q_{R}(M,N)
is invariant under shifting
N
. Also, our definition of
q_{R}(M,N)
is motivated by
[
5
, Corollary A.5.6]
.

Lemma 6.19
.

Let
R
a CM local ring, let
M
be a finitely generated MCM module with
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
and
b_{M}^{R}<\infty
, and let
N
be a bounded complex. If
q_{R}(M,N)<\infty
, then
\widehat{\tor}_{n}^{R}(M,N)=0
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Since
q_{R}(M,N)
is invariant under shifting
N
, we can assume
\sup{N}=0
. Let

\dots\rightarrow T_{1}\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}T_{0}\xrightarrow{\partial_{0}}T_{-1}\rightarrow\dots

be a complete resolution of
M
with
M=\coker{\partial_{1}}
and
T_{n}
a finitely generated free module for all
n
and let
F\rightarrow N
be a semiprojective resolution with
\inf{F}=\inf{H(N)}
and
F_{i}
finitely generated for all
i
. Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
C_{n}=\coker{\partial_{n}}
is MCM,
P_{R}(C_{n},R)<\infty
,
b_{C_{n}}^{R}<\infty
,
q_{R}(C_{n},N)<\infty
, and

\widehat{\tor}_{n}^{R}(M,N)\cong\widehat{\tor}_{1}^{R}(C_{n},N)\cong\tor_{1}^{R}(C_{n},N)

(the second isomorphism is by
[
9
, 2.4.1]
). Since

0\rightarrow F_{\leq-1}\rightarrow F\rightarrow F_{\geq 0}\rightarrow 0

is exact and since
F_{\geq 0}\simeq C_{0}(F)
,

\tor_{r}^{R}(C_{n},N)\cong\tor_{r}^{R}(C_{n},C_{0}(F))

for all
r>0
. Since
q_{R}(C_{n},N)<\infty
,
q_{R}(C_{n},C_{0}(F))<\infty
, so
q_{R}(C_{n},C_{0}(F))=0
by
[
21
, Proposition 7.5.5]
(this proposition applies since
P_{R}(C_{n},R)<\infty
and
b_{C_{n}}^{R}<\infty
implies
\gdim_{R}C_{n}<\infty
). In particular,
\widehat{\tor}_{n}^{R}(M,N)=0
.
∎

Definition 6.20
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring with residue field
k
and let
M
be a complex. Then the
depth
of
M
is

\depth{M}=-\sup{H(\mathbb{R}\Hom_{R}(k,M))}.

In
[
10
, Theorem 2.3 and Remark 3.3]
Christensen and Jorgensen show that if
R
is AB and
M
and
N
are finitely generated modules with
q_{R}(M,N)<\infty
, then

\depth{M\otimes_{R}^{\mathbb{L}}N}=\depth{M}+\depth{N}-\depth{R}.

In particular, if
q_{R}(M,N)=0
, then

\depth{M\otimes_{R}N}=\depth{M}+\depth{N}-\depth{R}.

Theorem 6.21
.

Let
R
be a CM local ring and let
M
and
N
be complexes in
D_{f}^{b}(R)
with
P_{R}(M,R)<\infty
and
B_{f}(M)<\infty
. If
q_{R}(M,N)<\infty
, then

\depth{M\otimes_{R}^{\mathbb{L}}N}=\depth{M}+\depth{N}-\depth{R}.

Proof.

Let
P\rightarrow M
be a semiprojective resolution with
\inf{P}=\inf{H(M)}
and
P_{i}
finitely generated for all
i
. Let
L=C_{i}(P)
where
i>\max\{\sup{H(M)},P_{R}(M,R)\}
. If
N
is quasiisomorphic to
N^{\prime}
, then
q_{R}(M,N)=q_{R}(M,N^{\prime})
and
\depth{N}=\depth{N^{\prime}}
. Since
N
is quasiisomorphic to a bounded complex, we can assume
N
is bounded. By Lemma
6.15

b_{L}^{R}<\infty
. Since
\gdim_{R}L=0
and since
R
is CM,
L
is MCM. Also, we can see that
q_{R}(L,N)<\infty
by applying
\_\otimes_{R}N
to the exact sequence

\star\quad\quad 0\rightarrow P_{\leq i-1}\rightarrow P\rightarrow P_{\geq i}\rightarrow 0,

so
\widehat{\tor}_{n}^{R}(L,N)=0
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
by Lemma
6.19
. Therefore

\widehat{\tor}_{n}^{R}(M,N)=\widehat{\tor}_{n-i}^{R}(L,N)=0

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
by
[
9
, Lemma 2.10]
, so

\depth{M\otimes_{R}^{\mathbb{L}}N}=\depth{M}+\depth{N}-\depth{R}

by
[
10
, Theorem 2.3]
.
∎

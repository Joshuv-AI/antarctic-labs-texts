Multidimensional polynomial patterns over finite fields: bounds, counting estimates and Gowers norm control Thanks: The author was supported by the Hellenic Foundation for Research and Innovation, Project No: 1684.
Borys Kuca

Abstract
. We examine multidimensional polynomial progressions involving linearly independent polynomials over finite fields, proving power saving bounds for sets lacking such configurations. This jointly generalises earlier results of Peluse (for the single dimensional case) and the author (for distinct degree polynomials). In contrast to the cases studied in the aforementioned two papers, a usual PET induction argument does not give Gowers norm control over multidimensional progressions that involve polynomials of the same degrees. The main challenge is therefore to obtain Gowers norm control, and we accomplish this for all multidimensional polynomial progressions with pairwise independent polynomials. The key inputs are: (1) a quantitative version of a PET induction scheme developed in ergodic theory by Donoso, Koutsogiannis, Ferré-Moragues and Sun, (2) a quantitative concatenation result for Gowers box norms in arbitrary finite abelian groups, motivated by (but different from) earlier results of Tao, Ziegler, Peluse and Prendiville; (3) an adaptation to combinatorics of the box norm smoothing technique, recently developed in the ergodic setting by the author and Frantzikinakis; and (4) a new version of the multidimensional degree lowering argument.

1.
Introduction

The last decade has witnessed considerable interest in quantifying the polynomial Szemerédi theorem of Bergelson and Leibman
[
3
]
, which asserts that all dense subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
contain polynomial patterns of a fairly general form. In a series of papers, Peluse and Prendiville gave bounds for subsets of natural numbers lacking polynomial progressions

(1)

\displaystyle x,\;x+p_{1}(n),\;\ldots,\;x+p_{\ell}(n)

for fixed
p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[n]
which either have distinct degrees
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
or are monomials of the same degree
[
29
]
. Even more bounds have been obtained in the finite field setting, where one wants to bound the size of subsets of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
(for a large prime
p
) lacking the patterns (
1
)
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
17
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
24
,
25
]
; some of these have recently been extended to the setting of finite commutative rings
[
2
]
. Much less is known in the multidimensional version of the aforementioned problems, in which one looks at configurations such as

(2)

\displaystyle(x_{1},\;x_{2}),\;(x_{1}+n^{2},\;x_{2}),\;(x_{1},\;x_{2}+n^{2}+n);

the necessity of dealing with several different directions at once introduces a number of technical issues that the existing methods found hard to deal with. For the particular progression (
2
), good bounds in the finite field setting have been obtained by Han, Lacey and Yang
[
17
]
; but when
n^{2}
and
n^{2}+n
are replaced by higher degree polynomials of the same degree, no bounds are currently known.
Motivated by recent progress in ergodic theory
[
6
,
7
,
11
,
12
]
, we develop new techniques that enable us to deal with many of the obstructions posed by multidimensional progressions. As a consequence, we give power-saving bounds in the multidimensional polynomial Szemerédi theorem over finite fields for progressions along linearly independent polynomials.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d,D,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
be nonzero vectors, and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[n]
be linearly independent polynomials of degrees at most
d
with zero constant terms. There exist absolute constants
c=c(d,\ell)
,
C=C(d,\ell)>0

such that all subsets of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}
with cardinality at least
Cp^{D-c}
contain

(3)

\displaystyle{\textbf{x}},\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}p_{1}(n),\;\ldots,\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{\ell}p_{\ell}(n)

for some
{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}
,
n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\setminus{\{0\}}
.

For instance, Theorem
1.1
gives the first known bounds for subsets of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}
lacking the progression

\displaystyle(x_{1},\;x_{2}),\;(x_{1}+n^{3},\;x_{2}),\;(x_{1},\;x_{2}+n^{3}+n)

for some
x_{1},x_{2},n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with
n\neq 0
.

Similarly to earlier works on the subject, Theorem
1.1
follows from a counting estimate for the progression (
3
). In what follows, we let
\E_{x\in X}=\frac{1}{X}\sum_{x\in X}
for a finite set
X
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d,D,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
be nonzero vectors, and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[n]
be linearly independent polynomials of degrees at most
d
with zero constant terms. There exist absolute constants
c=c(d,\ell)
,
C=C(d,\ell)>0

such that for all 1-bounded functions
f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we have

(4)

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\E_{{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}}\E_{n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}f_{j}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{j}p_{j}(n))-\E_{{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\E_{n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}f_{j}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{j}n)\mathclose{}\right|\leq Cp^{-c}.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are joint generalisations of the results of Peluse in the single dimensional case
D=1

[
25
]
and the results of the author for distinct degree polynomials
[
20
]
.
Yet Theorem
1.2
, from which Theorem
1.1
follows in a straightforward way, is significantly more difficult to prove than the analogous results in
[
20
,
25
]
. The arguments in
[
20
,
25
]
essentially consist of two steps. First, a PET induction argument establishes control of the relevant counting operator

(5)

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell})=\E_{{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}}\E_{n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}f_{j}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{j}p_{j}(n))

by a Gowers norm of some degree
s
(depending only on
\ell
and the maximum degree of the polynomials) of the function
f_{\ell}
corresponding to the polynomial
p_{\ell}
of highest degree. Second, a degree lowering argument allows us to pass from degree
s
control to degree 1 control. Iterating this argument for each function, we arrive at the identity (
4
).

The strategy outlined above breaks out quickly in the multidimensional case
D>1
whenever some of the polynomials have the same degree. The main issue in this case is that the PET induction scheme that we use, developed in the ergodic setting by Donoso, Ferré-Moragues, Koutsogiannis and Sun
[
6
]
, only allows us to control
\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell})
by a rather complicated average of Gowers box norms of the functions involved. For instance, a PET induction argument for the configuration (
2
) gives the bound

(6)

\mathopen{}\left|\E_{x_{1},x_{2},n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}f_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})f_{1}(x_{1}+n^{2},x_{2})f_{2}(x_{1},x_{2}+n^{2}+n)\mathclose{}\right|^{8}\\
\leq\E_{h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}2(h_{2}+h_{3})({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})+2h_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{2},\ 2h_{2}({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})+2h_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{2},\ 2h_{3}({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})+2h_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{2},\\
2h_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{2},\ 2(h_{2}+h_{3})({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1}),\ 2h_{2}({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1}),\ 2h_{3}({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})\end{subarray}}

for all 1-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, where
{\mathbf{e}}_{1}=(1,0)
,
{\mathbf{e}}_{2}=(0,1)
, and the norm above is a certain box norm whose direction vectors have coordinates polynomial in
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}
.
In order to control
\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell})
by a genuine Gowers norm, we therefore require two more ingredients compared to the arguments in
[
20
,
25
]
.

First, we establish a quantitative concatenation result for averages of box norms over finite abelian groups, which can be seen as a quantitative improvement on some of the results of Tao and Ziegler from
[
32
]
. Its proof uses rather elementary facts about box norms such as the Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and inductive formula for box norms. Although inspired by quantitative concatenation arguments of Peluse and Prendiville from
[
26
,
27
]
, the proof is somewhat simpler than the arguments in these papers as it does not involve any inverse theory for box norms.
Despite the elementary nature of the tools involved, the result is rather general, and it will likely find applications beyond the arguments in this paper. In our setting, it allows us to pass from a control of
\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell})
by an average of complicated box norms to a control by a single box norm, and one of a relatively simple form. This transition has previously been accomplished in a fully qualitative way in the ergodic setting by Donoso, Koutsogiannis, Ferré-Moragues and Sun
[
6
]
; by contrast, our finite-field argument is quantitative. In the example above, it allows us to replace the complicated average on the right hand side of (
6
) by a single box norm
\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})^{\times s},{\mathbf{e}}_{2}^{\times s}}
of degree
2s
for some
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, yielding a bound

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\E_{x_{1},x_{2},n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}f_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})f_{1}(x_{1}+n^{2},x_{2})f_{2}(x_{1},x_{2}+n^{2}+n)\mathclose{}\right|^{O(1)}\ll\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})^{\times s},{\mathbf{e}}_{2}^{\times s}}+p^{-1}

for 1-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
.

Second, we adapt to the combinatorial setting a box norm smoothing technique recently developed by Frantzikinakis and the author in ergodic theory
[
11
,
12
]
. With its help, we replace the control of
\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell})
by a box norm with control by a Gowers norm. For instance, we show that

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\E_{x_{1},x_{2},n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}f_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})f_{1}(x_{1}+n^{2},x_{2})f_{2}(x_{1},x_{2}+n^{2}+n)\mathclose{}\right|^{O(1)}\ll\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{U^{s^{\prime}}({\mathbf{e}}_{2})}+p^{-1},

where
\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{U^{s^{\prime}}({\mathbf{e}}_{2})}
is the Gowers norm of
f_{2}
in the direction
{\mathbf{e}}_{2}=(0,1)
of some degree
s^{\prime}
.

The arguments above, combining the PET inductive scheme, quantitative concatenation and box norm smoothing, yield Gowers norm control of progressions involving not just linearly independent polynomials, but all pairwise independent polynomials, giving the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d,D,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
be nonzero vectors, and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[n]
be pairwise independent polynomials of degrees at most
d
with zero constant terms. There exist
c=c(d,\ell)
,
C=C(d,\ell)>0
and
s=s(d,\ell)\in{\mathbb{N}}

such that for all 1-bounded functions
f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\E_{{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}}\E_{n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}f_{j}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{j}p_{j}(n))\mathclose{}\right|\leq C\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{U^{s}({\textbf{v}}_{\ell})}^{c}+Cp^{-c}.

We remark that in Theorems
1.1
-
1.3
, it is not necessary for the polynomials to have zero constant terms; if they do not, then the same results hold whenever the polynomials
p_{j}-p_{j}(0)
are linearly independent (resp. pairwise independent). However, the assumption of zero constant terms makes the proofs more convenient to write down, which is why we impose it.

Theorem
1.2
can be derived from Theorem
1.3
for linearly independent polynomials by the same degree lowering argument that was used in
[
20
]
to derive the special case of Theorem
1.2
for distinct degree polynomials; the assumption of distinct degrees in
[
20
]
was only needed to obtain Gowers norm control over the relevant counting operators while the degree lowering part worked for any progression with linearly independent polynomials whose counting operator was controlled by Gowers norms. We will however give an alternative (and, arguably, cleaner) version of this degree lowering argument which gives a better idea of what is going on.

1.2.
Notation and conventions

Throughout the paper, the letter
p
always denotes a prime number, and
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
is the finite field of characteristic
p
. We always assume that
p
is larger than the degree of the polynomials currently studied. This is necessary because we do not want the derivative
dx^{d-1}
of the monomial
x^{d}
to vanish over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}
for trivial reasons.

The labels
{\mathbb{N}},{\mathbb{N}}_{0},{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}
denote the sets of positive integers, nonnegative integers, integers, reals and complex numbers. For integers
a<b
, we set
[a,b]=\{a,a+1,\ldots,b\}
, abbreviating
[1,N]
as
[N]
. We also let
{\mathbb{Z}}[n]
denote the set of single variable polynomials with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We write elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}
(for some fixed
D\in{\mathbb{N}}
) as
{\textbf{x}}=(x_{1},...,x_{D})
and elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
as
x
. We usually denote tuples of length
s
as
{\underline{h}}=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{s})
. Given
{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s}
and
{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}
, we also set
{\underline{h}}^{\underline{\epsilon}}=(h_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}},\ldots,h_{s}^{\epsilon_{s}})
, where
h_{i}^{\epsilon_{i}}=h_{i}
if
\epsilon_{i}=0
and
h_{i}^{\epsilon_{i}}=h_{i}^{\prime}
otherwise. Also, for
{\underline{u}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{s}
, we define
\textrm{supp}({\underline{u}})=\{j\in[s]:\ u_{j}>0\}
.

For a finite set
X
, we let
\E_{x\in X}=\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{x\in X}
denote the average over
X
. If
X={\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}
or
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, then we suppress the set from the notation and simply let
\E_{\textbf{x}}=\E_{{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}}
and
\E_{n}=\E_{n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
.

For a finite abelian group
G
with additive notation and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}\in G
, we let

\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}\rangle=\{n_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}v_{s}:\ n_{1},\ldots,n_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}

be the subgroup generated by
v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}
. Given a function
f:G\to{\mathbb{C}}
and a subgroup
H\subseteq G
, we denote
\E(f|H)(x)=\E_{h\in H}f(x+h)
to be any of the three equivalent things: the orthogonal projection on the quotient group
G/H
, the conditional expectation with respect to the factor
\{x+H:\ x\in G\}
, or the average of
f
along the coset
x+H
. If
H=\langle v\rangle
, then we also set
\E(f|H)=\E(f|v)
.

We call a function
f:G\to{\mathbb{C}}

1-bounded
if
\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}:=\max_{x\in G}|f(x)|\leq 1
. We similarly define
\|f\|_{s}=\left(\E_{x\in G}|f({\textbf{x}})|^{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}
for
1\leq s<\infty
.

For
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
, we let
{\mathcal{C}}z=\overline{z}
be the conjugation operator.

We use the asymptotic notation in the standard way. If
f,g:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, with
g
taking positive real values, we denote
f=O(g)
,
f\ll g
,
g\gg f
or
g=\Omega(f)
if there exists
C>0
such that
|f(n)|\leq Cg(n)
for sufficiently large
n
. If the constant
C
depends on a parameter, we record this dependence with a subscript.

We let
{\textbf{v}}_{0}={\mathbf{e}}_{0}=\mathbf{0}
be the zero vector in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}
and
p_{0}(n)=0
be the zero polynomial.

2.
Gowers norms for finite abelian groups

2.1.
Basic definitions and properties

Let
G
be a finite abelian group. For a function
f:G\to{\mathbb{C}}
and
h\in G
, we define
\Delta_{h}f(x):=f(x)\overline{f(x+h)}
, and for
h_{1},\ldots,h_{s}\in G
, we set

\displaystyle\Delta_{h_{1},\ldots,h_{s}}f=\Delta_{h_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{s}}f(x)=\prod_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|}f(x+{\underline{\epsilon}}\cdot{\underline{h}}).

Given subgroups
H_{1},\ldots,H_{s}\subset G
, we define the
(Gowers) box norm of
f
along
H_{1},\ldots,H_{s}
to be

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1},\ldots,H_{s}}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\left(\E_{x\in G}\E_{h_{1}\in H_{1}}\cdots\E_{h_{s}\in H_{s}}\Delta_{h_{1},\ldots,h_{s}}f(x)\mathclose{}\right)^{1/2^{s}}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\left(\E_{x\in G}\E_{h_{1}\in H_{1}}\cdots\E_{h_{s}\in H_{s}}\prod_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|}f(x+{\underline{\epsilon}}\cdot{\underline{h}})\mathclose{}\right)^{1/2^{s}},

letting
s
be its
degree
. For instance, if
G={\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}
,
H_{1}=\langle(1,0)\rangle
and
H_{2}=\langle(0,1)\rangle
, then

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1},H_{2}}=\mathopen{}\left(\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},x_{2},h_{1},h_{2}\end{subarray}}f(x_{1},x_{2})\overline{f(x_{1}+h_{1},x_{2})f(x_{1},x_{2}+h_{2})}f(x_{1}+h_{1},x_{2}+h_{2})\mathclose{}\right)^{1/4}.

If some groups repeat, we also denote
H^{\times s}
to indicate that
H
appears
s
times, e.g.

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}^{\times 2},H_{2}^{\times 3},H_{3}}=\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1},H_{1},H_{2},H_{2},H_{2},H_{3}}.

Whenever
H_{1}=\langle v_{1}\rangle,\ldots,H_{s}=\langle v_{s}\rangle
, we also set

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1},\ldots,H_{s}}=\left\|f\right\|_{v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}}

Lastly, we denote

(7)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\times s}}=\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s}(H)},

or
\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s}(v)}
if
H=\langle v\rangle
, calling (
7
) the
Gowers norm of
f
along
H
of degree
s
.

Box norms are seminorms, and norms for
s\geq 2
. They satisfy a number of other well-known properties, some of whose proofs can be found e.g. in
[
13
,
31
]
. These include the following:

(i)

(Monotonicity)

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1},H_{2}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}}\leq\cdots;

(ii)

(Box norms along subgroups) for subgroups
H_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq H_{1},\ldots,H_{s}^{\prime}\subseteq H_{s}
, we have

(8)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1},\ldots,H_{s}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,H^{\prime}_{s}};

(iii)

(Inductive formula)

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1},\ldots,H_{s}}^{2^{s}}=\E_{h_{1}\in H_{1}}\cdots\E_{h_{s^{\prime}}\in H_{s^{\prime}}}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{s^{\prime}+1},\ldots,H_{s}}^{2^{s-s^{\prime}}}

for
1\leq s^{\prime}\leq s
;

(iv)

(Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality)

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\E_{x\in G}\E_{h_{1}\in H_{1}}\cdots\E_{h_{s}\in H_{s}}\prod_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|}f_{\underline{\epsilon}}(x+{\underline{\epsilon}}\cdot{\underline{h}})\mathclose{}\right|\leq\prod_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|}\left\|f_{\underline{\epsilon}}\right\|_{H_{1},\ldots,H_{s}}.

We will frequently cite the aforementioned properties throughout the paper.

2.2.
Concatenation of box norms for general groups

One of the key components of our argument is a quantitative concatenation result for box norms. On various occasions in additive combinatorics, we have to deal with expressions of the form

(9)

\displaystyle\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1i},\ldots,H_{si}},

i.e. averages of box norms of
f
along some subgroups
H_{1i},\ldots,H_{si}
indexed by a finite set
I
. The main idea behind concatenation results is to bound (
9
) from above by an average of box norms along larger subgroups
H_{ji}+H_{ji^{\prime}}
, or even
H_{ji_{1}}+\cdots+H_{ji_{k}}
. The rationale behind this move is that the larger subgroups, concatenated from the smaller ones, may admit a more explicit form that makes them easier to work with.

Example 1
.

A model example of an average amenable to our concatenation procedure is

\displaystyle\E_{h_{1},h_{2}}\left\|f\right\|_{(h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}){\textbf{v}}_{1}+h_{2}^{2}{\textbf{v}}_{2}}

for some vectors
{\textbf{v}}_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}
(we remind the reader our convention that
\E_{h}=\E_{h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
unless stated otherwise). The subgroups
\langle(h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}){\textbf{v}}_{1}+h_{2}^{2}{\textbf{v}}_{2}\rangle
indexed by
(h_{1},h_{2})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}
are neither particularly intuitive to understand nor easy to work with; however, it turns out that this average can be controlled by a single box norm
\left\|f\right\|_{\langle{\textbf{v}}_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}\rangle}
as follows. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the definition of box norms, we have

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left(\E_{h_{1},h_{2}}\left\|f\right\|_{(h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}){\textbf{v}}_{1}+h_{2}^{2}{\textbf{v}}_{2}}\mathclose{}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\E_{h_{1},h_{2}}\left\|f\right\|_{(h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}){\textbf{v}}_{1}+h_{2}^{2}{\textbf{v}}_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\E_{\textbf{x}}f({\textbf{x}})\E_{h_{1},h_{2},m}\overline{f({\textbf{x}}+((h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}){\textbf{v}}_{1}+h_{2}^{2}{\textbf{v}}_{2})m)}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
x
, we double the variables
h_{1},h_{2},m
, so that the square of the expression above is bounded by

\displaystyle\E_{{\textbf{x}}}\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1},h_{1}^{\prime},\\
h_{2},h_{2}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\E_{m,m^{\prime}}f({\textbf{x}})\overline{f({\textbf{x}}+((h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}){\textbf{v}}_{1}+h_{2}^{2}{\textbf{v}}_{2})m-(({h_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}+h_{1}^{\prime}){\textbf{v}}_{1}+{h_{2}^{\prime}}^{2}{\textbf{v}}_{2})m^{\prime})},

and this is precisely

\displaystyle\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1},h_{1}^{\prime},\\
h_{2},h_{2}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\left\|f\right\|_{\langle(h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}){\textbf{v}}_{1}+h_{2}^{2}{\textbf{v}}_{2},({h_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}+h_{1}^{\prime}){\textbf{v}}_{1}+{h_{2}^{\prime}}^{2}{\textbf{v}}_{2}\rangle}^{2}.

A simple computation shows that as long as

(10)

\displaystyle(h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}){h_{2}^{\prime}}^{2}\neq({h_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}+h_{1}^{\prime})h_{2}^{2},

the larger concatenated subgroup

(11)

\displaystyle\langle(h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}){\textbf{v}}_{1}+h_{2}^{2}{\textbf{v}}_{2},({h_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}+h_{1}^{\prime}){\textbf{v}}_{1}+{h_{2}^{\prime}}^{2}{\textbf{v}}_{2}\rangle

equals the full subgroup
\langle{\textbf{v}}_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}\rangle
. The condition (
10
) holds for all but
O(p^{3})
“bad” tuples
(h_{1},h_{1}^{\prime},h_{2},h_{2}^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{4}
, which gives us the desired bound

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left(\E_{h_{1},h_{2}}\left\|f\right\|_{(h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}){\textbf{v}}_{1}+h_{2}^{2}{\textbf{v}}_{2}}\mathclose{}\right)^{4}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}}^{2}+O(p^{-1}).

Thus has the original messy average of box norms been bounded by a single box norm that involves only the “principal” directions
{\textbf{v}}_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}
rather than linear combinations of them.

The argument above can be divided into two parts:

(i)

first, we have replaced the original subgroups
\langle(h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}){\textbf{v}}_{1}+h_{2}^{2}{\textbf{v}}_{2}\rangle
by larger subgroups (
11
), and in doing so we only used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in a clever way;

(ii)

second, we have shown that the larger subgroups (
11
) almost always equal the full subgroup
\langle{\textbf{v}}_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}\rangle
, and in doing so we have only used basic facts about zero sets of systems of polynomial equations.

In this section, we will only perform the first part, which holds in the very general setting of box norms over arbitrary finite abelian groups (and this condition can further be relaxed if necessary). The second part, exploiting specific properties of polynomials, will be carried out in Section
4
once we have a better understanding of systems of polynomial equations that need to be harnessed.

The first concatenation results have been developed by Tao and Ziegler
[
32
]
with the aim of proving the existence of polynomial progressions in primes
[
33
]
. These results are purely qualitative, though, hence not applicable to our context. In
[
6
]
, Donoso, Ferré-Moragues, Koutsogiannis and Sun used them to obtain a (qualitative) box seminorm control on certain multiple ergodic averages along polynomials; the arguments in Section
4
, in which we obtain quantitative box norm control over our counting operators, can be perceived as a quantitative, finite field version of the arguments from
[
6
]
.

Certain quantitative concatenation results have recently been developed by Peluse and Prendiville
[
26
,
27
,
29
]
in their works on polynomial progressions in subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
. Our proofs are only partly inspired by their techniques; specifically, while proving Lemma
2.2
below, we use a trick observed in
[
26
, Lemma 5.1]
. However, our arguments are more straightforward than those of Peluse and Prendiville in that we do not use at any point an inverse theorem for degree 2 box norms, which is a starting point in their arguments. Instead, we rely entirely on Lemma
2.2
below, Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and simple manipulations of the formulas for box norms based on the inductive formula therefor.

The main objective of this section is to show that the average (
9
) can be controlled by an average of box norms along larger subgroups
H_{ji_{1}}+\cdots+H_{ji_{k}}
. The starting point is the following simple result for degree 1 box norms which utilises the trick used in Example
1
in a more general setting.

Lemma 2.1

(Concatenation of degree 1 norms)
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group,
I
be a finite indexing set and
(H_{i})_{i\in I}
be subgroups of
G
. Then for every 1-bounded function
f:G\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left(\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{i}}^{2}\mathclose{}\right)^{2}\leq\E_{i,i^{\prime}\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{i}+H_{i^{\prime}}}^{2}.

Proof.

Expanding the definition of Gowers norms, we get

\displaystyle\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{i}}^{2}=\E_{i\in I}\E_{x\in G}\E_{h_{i}\in H_{i}}f(x)\overline{f(x+h_{i})}=\E_{x\in G}f(x)\E_{i\in I}\E_{h_{i}\in H_{i}}\overline{f(x+h_{i})}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
x
and using the 1-boundedness of
f
, we infer that

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left(\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{i}}^{2}\mathclose{}\right)^{2}\leq\E_{i,i^{\prime}\in I}\E_{x\in G}\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{i}\in H_{i},\\
h^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}\in H_{i^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}f(x+h_{i})\overline{f(x+h^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}})}.

A change of variables gives

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left(\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{i}}^{2}\mathclose{}\right)^{2}\leq\E_{i,i^{\prime}\in I}\E_{x\in G}\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in H_{i}+H_{i^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}f(x)\overline{f(x+h)},

and the result follows from the definition of Gowers norms.
∎

The argument becomes more complicated when we deal with averages of box norms of degree greater than 1, i.e. when
s>1
in (
9
). An example of such an average is

\displaystyle\E_{h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}}\left\|f\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}2(h_{2}+h_{3})({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})+2h_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{2},\ 2h_{2}({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})+2h_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{2},\ 2h_{3}({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})+2h_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{2},\\
2h_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{2},\ 2(h_{2}+h_{3})({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1}),\ 2h_{2}({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1}),\ 2h_{3}({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})\end{subarray}}

(with
G={\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}
) that has been mentioned in (
6
) as the average that appears after performing the PET induction argument to the progression (
2
). In handling this general case, we will iteratively use the lemma below.
Its proof is based on a trick from the proof of
[
26
, Lemma 5.1]
and relies on rather elementary maneuvers that involve the Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, multiple applications of the inductive formula for box norms and a simple change of variables.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
G
be a finite abelian group,
I
be a finite indexing set and
H_{i},K_{1i},\ldots,K_{si}
be subgroups of
G
for each
i\in I
. For each 1-bounded function
f:G\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left(\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{i},K_{1i},\ldots,K_{si}}^{2^{s+1}}\mathclose{}\right)^{2^{2s+1}}\leq\E_{i,i^{\prime}\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{K_{1i},\ldots,K_{si},K_{1i^{\prime}},\ldots,K_{si^{\prime}},H_{i}+H_{i^{\prime}}}^{2^{2s+1}}

Lemma
2.1
is thus a special case of Lemma
2.2
for
s=0
.

Proof.

Let
\delta=\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{i},K_{1i},\ldots,K_{si}}^{2^{s+1}}
and
K_{i}=K_{1i}\times\cdots\times K_{si}
. The inductive formula for box norms yields

\displaystyle\delta=\E_{i\in I}\E_{x\in G}\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\underline{k}}\in K_{i}\end{subarray}}\E_{h\in H_{i}}\Delta_{{\underline{k}},h}f(x).

We expand

\displaystyle\Delta_{{\underline{h}},k}f(x)=f(x)\overline{f(x+h)}\Delta_{{\underline{k}}}^{*}\Delta_{h}f(x),

where
\Delta_{{\underline{k}}}^{*}f(x)=\prod_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}\setminus\{0\}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|}f(x+{\underline{\epsilon}}\cdot{\underline{k}})
,
and change
the order of summation, so that

\displaystyle\delta=\E_{x\in G}f(x)\E_{i\in I}\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\underline{k}}\in K_{i}\end{subarray}}\E_{h\in H_{i}}\overline{f(x+h)}\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{k}}}\Delta_{h}f(x).

An application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
x
gives

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\leq\E_{x\in G}\E_{i,i^{\prime}\in I}\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\underline{k}}\in K_{i},\\
{\underline{k}}^{\prime}\in K_{i^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in H_{i},\\
h^{\prime}\in H_{i^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\overline{f(x+h)}f(x+h^{\prime})\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{k}}}\Delta_{h}f(x)\overline{\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{k}}^{\prime}}\Delta_{h^{\prime}}f(x)}.

We crucially observe that for each fixed
i,i^{\prime}\in I
and
h\in H_{i},h^{\prime}\in H_{i^{\prime}}
, the average

\displaystyle\E_{x\in G}\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\underline{k}}\in K_{i},\\
{\underline{k}}^{\prime}\in K_{i^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\overline{f(x+h)}f(x+h^{\prime})\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{k}}}\Delta_{h}f(x)\overline{\Delta^{*}_{{\underline{k}}^{\prime}}\Delta_{h^{\prime}}f(x)}

is a box inner product along
K_{1i},\ldots,K_{si},K_{1i^{\prime}},\ldots,K_{si^{\prime}}
, and an application of the Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle\delta^{2^{2s+1}}\leq\E_{i,i^{\prime}\in I}\E_{x\in G}\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\underline{k}}\in K_{i},\\
{\underline{k}}^{\prime}\in K_{i^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in H_{i},\\
h^{\prime}\in H_{i^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\Delta_{{\underline{k}},{\underline{k}}^{\prime}}\mathopen{}\left(f(x+h)\overline{f(x+h^{\prime})}\mathclose{}\right),

Lastly, the inductive formula for box norms combined with a simple change of variables implies that

\displaystyle\delta^{2^{2s+1}}\leq\E_{i,i^{\prime}\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{K_{1i},\ldots,K_{si},K_{1i^{\prime}},\ldots,K_{si^{\prime}},H_{i}+H_{i^{\prime}}}^{2^{2s+1}},

as claimed.
∎

By repeatedly applying Lemma
2.2
to (
9
) for
s>1
, we derive the following preliminary concatenation result. It allows us to bound an average of box norms along some subgroups by an average of box norms along (double) sums of these subgroups. Being the most involved technical result in this section, it will merely serve as an intermediate step in obtaining cleaner and stronger concatenation results afterwards.

Proposition 2.3

(Concatenation of box norms, version I)
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
G
be a finite abelian group,
I
be a finite indexing set and
H_{1i},\ldots,H_{si}
be subgroups of
G
for each
i\in I
. For all 1-bounded functions
f:G\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left(\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1i},\ldots,H_{si}}^{2^{s}}\mathclose{}\right)^{O_{s}(1)}\leq\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{\underline{\epsilon}}\in I,\\
{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}\end{subarray}}\left\|f\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{H_{ji_{\underline{\epsilon}}}+H_{ji_{{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}}}:\ j\in[s],\ {\underline{\epsilon}},{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{s}\\
\mathrm{with}\ (\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{s-j})=(\epsilon^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\epsilon^{\prime}_{s-j}),\ \epsilon_{s+1-j}<\epsilon^{\prime}_{s+1-j}\}\end{subarray}}.

Importantly, the indices
{\underline{\epsilon}},{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}
are always distinct, so that
H_{ji_{\underline{\epsilon}}}+H_{ji_{{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}}}
is strictly larger than
H_{ji_{\underline{\epsilon}}},H_{ji_{{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}}}
unless one of the two subgroups is contained in the other.

Proof.

The proof of Proposition
2.3
relies on a gradual concatenation of the “unconcatenated” subgroups
H_{1i},\ldots,H_{si}
in the average using Lemma
2.2
. We repeatedly use the inductive formula for box norms in order to reinterpret the average in such a way that successive applications of Lemma
2.2
concatenate the subgroups
H_{1i},\ldots,H_{si}
one by one.

Throughout, we shall assume that
h_{j}^{{\underline{\epsilon}}}
is always an element of
H_{ji_{{\underline{\epsilon}}}}
, and an average
\E_{h_{j}^{{\underline{\epsilon}}}}
always runs over the subgroup
H_{ji_{{\underline{\epsilon}}}}
.

The case
s=2
:

For illustrative purposes, we first prove Proposition
2.3
for degree 2 box norms. Let
\delta=\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|^{4}_{H_{1i},H_{2i}}
. We first want to concatenate the group
H_{2i}
. By Lemma
2.2
, we have

(12)

\displaystyle\delta^{8}\leq\E_{i_{0},i_{1}\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1i_{0}},H_{1i_{1}},H_{2i_{0}}+H_{2i_{1}}}^{8},

and so the group
H_{2i}
indeed got concatenated. The price we paid for this is that while applying Lemma
2.2
, the unconcatenated group
H_{1i}
doubled into
H_{1i_{0}}
and
H_{1i_{1}}
. The next goal is therefore to concatenate these groups one by one. Using the inductive formula for box norms, we can rephrase (
12
) as

\displaystyle\delta^{8}\leq\E_{i_{1}\in I}\E_{h^{1}_{1}}\E_{i_{0}\in I}\left\|\Delta_{h^{1}_{1}}f\right\|_{H_{1i_{0}},H_{2i_{0}}+H_{2i_{1}}}^{4}.

Applying Lemma
2.2
to each
\E_{i_{0}\in I}\left\|\Delta_{h^{1}_{1}}f\right\|_{H_{1i_{0}},H_{2i_{0}}+H_{2i_{1}}}^{4}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\delta^{64}\leq\E_{i_{00},i_{01},i_{1}\in I}\E_{h^{1}_{1}}\left\|\Delta_{h^{1}_{1}}f\right\|_{H_{1i_{00}}+H_{1i_{01}},H_{2i_{00}}+H_{2i_{1}},H_{2i_{01}}+H_{2i_{1}}}^{8}.

Rephrasing the inequality above once more using the inductive formula for box norms, we get

\displaystyle\delta^{64}\leq\E_{i_{00},i_{01}\in I}\E_{h^{00}_{1},h^{01}_{1}}\E_{i_{1}\in I}\left\|\Delta_{h^{00}_{1}+h^{01}_{1}}f\right\|_{H_{1i_{1}},H_{2i_{00}}+H_{2i_{1}},H_{2i_{01}}+H_{2i_{1}}}^{8}.

We then apply Lemma
2.2
for the last time, this time to each

\E_{i_{1}\in I}\left\|\Delta_{h^{00}_{1}+h^{01}_{1}}f\right\|_{H_{1i_{1}},H_{2i_{00}}+H_{2i_{1}},H_{2i_{01}}+H_{2i_{1}}}^{8},

obtaining

\displaystyle\delta^{2048}\leq\E_{i_{00},i_{01},i_{10},i_{11}\in I}\E_{h^{00}_{1},h^{01}_{1}}\left\|\Delta_{h^{00}_{1}+h^{01}_{1}}f\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{1i_{10}}+H_{1i_{11}},H_{2i_{00}}+H_{2i_{10}},H_{2i_{00}}+H_{2i_{11}},\\
H_{2i_{01}}+H_{2i_{10}},H_{2i_{01}}+H_{2i_{11}}\end{subarray}}^{32}.

The inductive formula for box norms then implies that

\displaystyle\delta^{2048}\leq\E_{i_{00},i_{01},i_{10},i_{11}\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{1i_{00}}+H_{1i_{01}},H_{1i_{10}}+H_{1i_{11}},H_{2i_{00}}+H_{2i_{10}},\\
H_{2i_{00}}+H_{2i_{11}},H_{2i_{01}}+H_{2i_{10}},H_{2i_{01}}+H_{2i_{11}}\end{subarray}}^{64},

and the exponent 64 can be dropped since
f
is 1-bounded.

We note that the proof of Proposition
2.3
for
s=2
relies on 3 applications of Lemma
2.2
. More generally, the proof for an arbitrary
s\geq 2
will require
1+2+\cdots+2^{s-1}=2^{s}-1
applications of Lemma
2.2
.

The general case:

We move on to prove the general case. Starting with
\delta=\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1i},\ldots,H_{si}}^{2^{s}}
, we apply Lemma
2.2
to bound

(13)

\displaystyle\delta^{O_{s}(1)}\leq\E_{i_{0},i_{1}\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1i_{0}},\ldots,H_{(s-1)i_{0}},H_{1i_{1}},\ldots,H_{(s-1)i_{1}},H_{si_{0}}+H_{si_{1}}}^{2^{2s-1}}.

We note that we passed from having
s
unconcatenated groups indexed by
i
to
s-1
unconcatenated groups indexed by
i_{0}
and another
s-1
unconcatenated groups indexed by
i_{1}
(in addition to the concatenated group
H_{si_{0}}+H_{si_{1}}
). Thus, the total number of groups almost doubled, but what matters is that for each index
i_{0},i_{1}
, the number of unconcatenated groups with this index went down by 1.
At the next stage of the argument, we will apply Lemma
2.2
twice to concatenate
H_{(s-1)i_{0}}
first and then
H_{(s-1)i_{1}}
. As a consequence, the two indices
i_{0},i_{1}
will be replaced by four indices
i_{00},i_{01},i_{10},i_{11}
, and for each of them we will have exactly
s-2
unconcatenated groups.
We will continue in this manner: at each stage, the number of indices
i_{\underline{\epsilon}}
will double, but the number of unconcatenated groups with each index
i_{\underline{\epsilon}}
will decrease by 1.
Eventually, on the
s
-th step, we will be left with
2^{s-1}
unconcatenated groups
H_{1i_{\underline{\epsilon}}}
, and
2^{s-1}
applications of Lemma
2.2
will allow us to concatenate them all without producing any new unconcatenated groups. This will finish the argument.

This is the general strategy; let us see in detail what happens at the second stage, i.e. after obtaining the bound (
13
). Using the induction formula for box norms, we can rephrase (
13
) as

\displaystyle\delta^{O_{s}(1)}\leq\E_{i_{1}\in I}\E_{h^{1}_{1},\ldots,h^{1}_{s-1}}\E_{i_{0}\in I}\left\|\Delta_{h^{1}_{1},\ldots,h^{1}_{s-1}}f\right\|_{H_{1i_{0}},\ldots,H_{(s-1)i_{0}},H_{si_{0}}+H_{si_{1}}}^{2^{s}}.

For each fixed
i_{1},h^{1}_{1},\ldots,h^{1}_{s-1}
, we apply Lemma
2.2
separately to each average over
i_{0}
, obtaining

\displaystyle\delta^{O_{s}(1)}\leq\E_{i_{1}\in I}\E_{h^{1}_{1},\ldots,h^{1}_{s-1}}\E_{i_{00},i_{01}\in I}\left\|\Delta_{h^{1}_{1},\ldots,h^{1}_{s-1}}f\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{1i_{00}},\ldots,H_{(s-2)i_{00}},H_{1i_{01}},\ldots,H_{(s-2)i_{01}},\\
H_{(s-1)i_{00}}+H_{(s-1)i_{01}},H_{si_{00}}+H_{si_{1}},H_{si_{01}}+H_{si_{1}}\end{subarray}}^{2^{2s-1}}.

We rearrange the inequality above using the inductive formula for box norms as

\displaystyle\delta^{O_{s}(1)}\leq\E_{i_{00},i_{01}\in I}\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}h^{00}_{1},\ldots,h^{00}_{s-2},\\
h^{01}_{1},\ldots,h^{01}_{s-2},\\
h^{00}_{s-1},h^{01}_{s-1}\end{subarray}}\E_{i_{1}\in I}\left\|\Delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}h^{00}_{1},\ldots,h^{00}_{s-2},\\
h^{01}_{1},\ldots,h^{01}_{s-2},\\
h^{00}_{s-1}+h^{01}_{s-1}\end{subarray}}f\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{1i_{1}},\ldots,H_{(s-1)i_{1}},\\
H_{si_{00}}+H_{si_{1}},H_{si_{01}}+H_{si_{1}}\end{subarray}}^{2^{s+1}}.

in order to concatenate
H_{(s-1)i_{1}}
. By Lemma
2.2
applied separately to each average over
i_{1}\in I
, we have

\displaystyle\delta^{O_{s}(1)}\leq\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{00},i_{01},\\
i_{10},i_{11}\in I\end{subarray}}\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}h^{00}_{1},\ldots,h^{00}_{s-2},\\
h^{01}_{1},\ldots,h^{01}_{s-2},\\
h^{00}_{s-1},h^{01}_{s-1}\end{subarray}}\left\|\Delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}h^{00}_{1},\ldots,h^{00}_{s-2},\\
h^{01}_{1},\ldots,h^{01}_{s-2},\\
h^{00}_{s-1}+h^{01}_{s-1}\end{subarray}}f\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{1i_{10}},\ldots,H_{(s-2)i_{10}},H_{1i_{11}},\ldots,H_{(s-2)i_{11}},\\
H_{(s-1)i_{10}}+H_{(s-1)i_{11}},H_{si_{00}}+H_{si_{10}},H_{si_{00}}+H_{si_{11}},\\
H_{si_{01}}+H_{si_{10}},H_{si_{01}}+H_{si_{11}}\end{subarray}}^{2^{2s+1}}.

An application of the inductive formula for box norms then gives

\displaystyle\delta^{O_{s}(1)}\leq\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{00},i_{01},\\
i_{10},i_{11}\in I\end{subarray}}\left\|f\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{1i_{00}},\ldots,H_{(s-2)i_{00}},H_{1i_{01}},\ldots,H_{(s-2)i_{01}},\\
H_{1i_{10}},\ldots,H_{(s-2)i_{10}},H_{1i_{11}},\ldots,H_{(s-2)i_{11}},\\
H_{(s-1)i_{00}}+H_{(s-1)i_{01}},H_{(s-1)i_{10}}+H_{(s-1)i_{11}},\\
H_{si_{00}}+H_{si_{10}},H_{si_{00}}+H_{si_{11}},\\
H_{si_{01}}+H_{si_{10}},H_{si_{01}}+H_{si_{11}}\end{subarray}}^{2^{4s-2}},

which can be written more compactly as

\displaystyle\delta^{O_{s}(1)}\leq\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{00},i_{01},\\
i_{10},i_{11}\in I\end{subarray}}\left\|f\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{H_{ji_{\underline{\epsilon}}}:\ {\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{2},\ j\in[s-2]\},\\
\{H_{(s-1)i_{\underline{\epsilon}}}+H_{(s-1)i_{{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}}}:\ {\underline{\epsilon}},{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{2}\ \mathrm{with}\ \epsilon_{1}=\epsilon^{\prime}_{1},\ \epsilon_{2}<\epsilon^{\prime}_{2}\},\\
\{H_{si_{\underline{\epsilon}}}+H_{si_{{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}}}:\ {\underline{\epsilon}},{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{2}\ \mathrm{with}\ \epsilon_{1}<\epsilon^{\prime}_{1}\}\end{subarray}}^{2^{4s-2}}.

We have thus successfully concatenated all the groups
H_{(s-1)i_{\underline{\epsilon}}}
and
H_{si_{\underline{\epsilon}}}
.

At this point, we stop keeping track of the ever more complicated powers of the box norm on the right-hand, and instead use the 1-boundedness of
f
to replace the exponent by 1, so that

\displaystyle\delta^{O_{s}(1)}\leq\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{00},i_{01},\\
i_{10},i_{11}\in I\end{subarray}}\left\|f\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{H_{ji_{\underline{\epsilon}}}:\ {\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{2},\ j\in[s-2]\},\\
\{H_{(s-1)i_{\underline{\epsilon}}}+H_{(s-1)i_{{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}}}:\ {\underline{\epsilon}},{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{2}\ \mathrm{with}\ \epsilon_{1}=\epsilon^{\prime}_{1},\ \epsilon_{2}<\epsilon^{\prime}_{2}\},\\
\{H_{si_{\underline{\epsilon}}}+H_{si_{{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}}}:\ {\underline{\epsilon}},{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{2}\ \mathrm{with}\ \epsilon_{1}<\epsilon^{\prime}_{1}\}\end{subarray}}.

At the next stage, we concatenate the groups
H_{(s-2)i_{\underline{\epsilon}}}
. Applying Lemma
2.2
and the induction formula for box norms four times like before, each time to an average over
i_{00},i_{01},i_{10},i_{11}
respectively, we arrive at the inequality

\displaystyle\delta^{O_{s}(1)}\leq\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{{\underline{\epsilon}}}\in I,\\
{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{3}\end{subarray}}\left\|f\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{H_{ji_{\underline{\epsilon}}}:\ {\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{3},\ j\in[s-3]\},\\
\{H_{ji_{\underline{\epsilon}}}+H_{ji_{{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}}}:\ j=s-2,s-1,s,\ {\underline{\epsilon}},{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{3}\\
\mathrm{with}\ (\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{s-j})=(\epsilon^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\epsilon^{\prime}_{s-j}),\ \epsilon_{s+1-j}<\epsilon^{\prime}_{s+1-j}\}\end{subarray}}

This time, we have successfully concatenated the groups
H_{(s-2)i_{\underline{\epsilon}}}
. At the next step, 8 applications of Lemma
2.2
and the induction formula for box norms allow us to concatenate groups
H_{(s-3)i_{\underline{\epsilon}}}
. Continuing the argument like this, we arrive, after a total of

\displaystyle 1+2+2^{2}+\cdots+2^{s-1}=2^{s}-1

applications of Lemma
2.2
and the induction formula for box norms, at the claimed inequality.
∎

For applications, the following weaker but notationally lighter corollary of Proposition
2.3
is sufficient. While the ordering
(i_{\underline{\epsilon}})_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}
figuring in Proposition
2.3
reflects the nature of the proof of that result more naturally, the ordering
(i_{l})_{l\in 2^{s}}
present in the corollary below is more useful in applications.

Corollary 2.4

(Concatenation of box norms, version II)
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
G
be a finite abelian group,
I
be a finite indexing set and
H_{1i},\ldots,H_{si}
be subgroups of

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left(\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1i},\ldots,H_{si}}^{2^{s}}\mathclose{}\right)^{O_{s}(1)}\leq\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{2^{s}}\in I\end{subarray}}\left\|f\right\|_{\{H_{ji_{l_{1}}}+H_{ji_{l_{2}}}:\ j\in[s],\ 1\leq l_{1}<l_{2}\leq 2^{s}\}}.

Proof.

First, Proposition
2.3
and the monotonicity property for box norms immediately gives

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left(\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1i},\ldots,H_{si}}^{2^{s}}\mathclose{}\right)^{O_{s}(1)}\leq\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{\underline{\epsilon}}\in I,\\
{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}\end{subarray}}\left\|f\right\|_{\{H_{ji_{\underline{\epsilon}}}+H_{ji_{{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}}}:\ j\in[s],\ {\underline{\epsilon}},{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{s},\ {\underline{\epsilon}}<{\underline{\epsilon}}^{\prime}\}},

The result then follows upon enumerating

in an appropriate way.
∎

It is instructive to compare Corollary
2.4
with the relevant results from
[
32
]
. One difference is in the setup itself: our argument is presented for finite groups while the argument from
[
32
]
concerns ergodic seminorms for countable group actions. But with a bit of extra work, our argument could be extended to ergodic seminorms for
{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
actions since it only relies on elementary maneuvers such as the Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality; we however do not need this extension for the purposes of this article. Apart from this, the most important difference is that we quantitatively compare two averages of box norms while an analogous comparison in
[
32
]
is fully qualitative. Moreover, in the norms on the right hand side of (

), we only sum up subgroups with the same index

whereas results from
[
32
]
involve subgroups of the form
H_{ji}+H_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}
for various indices

. Finally, our argument necessitates the introduction of

while the arguments in

allow to average on the right hand side over only two indices

.

We shall use the following iterative consequence of Corollary
2.4
, obtained from an iterated application of Corollary
2.4
. Its advantage is that it allows us to take the directions in the concatenated box norms to be arbitrarily long sums of the original directions rather than just double sums, as is the case in Corollary
2.4
.

Corollary 2.5

(Iterated concatenation of box norms)
.

Let
s,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
G
be a finite abelian group,
I
be a finite indexing set and
H_{1i},\ldots,H_{si}
be subgroups of
G
for each
i\in I
. There exists a natural number
w=O_{s,k}(1)
such that for all 1-bounded functions
f:G\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we have

(15)

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left(\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1i},\ldots,H_{si}}^{2^{s}}\mathclose{}\right)^{O_{s,k}(1)}\leq\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{w}\in I\end{subarray}}\left\|f\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{H_{ji_{l_{1}}}+\cdots+H_{ji_{l_{2^{k}}}}:\ j\in[s],\ 1\leq l_{1}<\cdots<l_{2^{k}}\leq w\}\end{subarray}}.

It will be important for our applications later on that the values
l_{1},\ldots,l_{2^{k}}
indexing the subgroup
H_{ji_{l_{1}}}+\cdots+H_{ji_{l_{2^{k}}}}
are all distinct so that this subgroup is genuinely larger than its constituents.

It is instructive to see what Corollary
2.5
gives when
s=1
. In this case, an iterated application of Lemma
2.1
in place of Corollary
2.4
gives simply

(16)

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left(\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{i}}^{2}\mathclose{}\right)^{2^{k}}\leq\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{2^{k}}\in I\end{subarray}}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{i_{1}}+\cdots+H_{i_{2^{k}}}}^{2},

corresponding to taking
w=2^{k}
in Corollary
2.5
and

for
1\leq l\leq 2^{k}

Proof.

We induct on
k
. For
k=1
, the result holds with
w=2^{s}
by Corollary
2.4
. Suppose that it holds for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
w=w_{k}
(which is allowed to depend on
s
). Let

\displaystyle\tilde{I}=\{(i_{l_{1}},\ldots,i_{l_{2^{k}}})_{1\leq l_{1}<\cdots<l_{2^{k}}\leq w_{k}}:\;i_{1},\ldots,i_{w_{k}}\in I\}

and observe that

\E_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{w}\in I}A((i_{l_{1}},\ldots,i_{l_{2^{k}}})_{1\leq l_{1}<\cdots<l_{2^{k}}\leq w_{k}})=\E_{\tilde{i}\in\tilde{I}}A(\tilde{i})

This gives us a bound

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left(\E_{i\in I}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1i},\ldots,H_{si}}^{2^{s}}\mathclose{}\right)^{O_{s,k}(1)}\leq\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{w_{k+1}}\in I\end{subarray}}\left\|f\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{H_{ji_{l_{1}m_{1}}}+\cdots+H_{ji_{l_{2^{k}}m_{1}}}+H_{ji_{l_{1}m_{2}}}+\cdots+H_{ji_{l_{2^{k}}m_{2}}}:\\

2.3.
Weak inverse theorems for Gowers norms

We now specialise to the case
G={\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}

in order to discuss various notational conventions and inverse theorems for Gowers norms that we shall use throughout the paper. For multiplicative derivatives, we set

\displaystyle\Delta_{{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s};{\underline{h}}}f=\Delta_{{\textbf{v}}_{1}h_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}h_{s}}f\quad\textrm{and}\quad\Delta_{s,{\textbf{v}};{\underline{h}}}f=\Delta_{{\textbf{v}}h_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}h_{s}}f,

so that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}}^{2^{s}}=\E_{{\textbf{x}}}\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s}}\Delta_{{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s};{\underline{h}}}f({\textbf{x}})\quad\textrm{and}\quad\left\|f\right\|^{2^{s}}_{U^{s}({\textbf{v}})}=\E_{{\textbf{x}}}\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s}}\Delta_{s,{\textbf{v}};{\underline{h}}}f({\textbf{x}}).

We move on to discuss inverse theorems.
For
s=1
, we can rephrase the Gowers norm as

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{U^{1}({\textbf{v}})}^{2}=\E_{{\textbf{x}}}f({\textbf{x}})\E(\overline{f}|{\textbf{v}})({\textbf{x}}),

and so
\left\|f\right\|_{U^{1}({\textbf{v}})}^{2}
is a correlation of
f
with a
v
-invariant function. For
s>1
, a similar statement can be obtained by considering degree
s
dual functions in place of the conditional expectation. Letting
\Delta_{s,{\textbf{v}};{\underline{h}}}^{*}f(x)=\prod_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}\setminus\{\underline{0}\}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|}f({\textbf{x}}+({\underline{\epsilon}}\cdot{\underline{h}}){\textbf{v}})
, we define the
degree
s
dual function of
f
along

v
to be

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{s,{\textbf{v}}}f({\textbf{x}})=\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s}}\Delta_{s,{\textbf{v}};{\underline{h}}}^{*}f(x)=\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s}}\prod_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}\setminus\{\underline{0}\}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|}f({\textbf{x}}+({\underline{\epsilon}}\cdot{\underline{h}}){\textbf{v}}).

This gives us a
weak inverse theorem
for the norm
U^{s}({\textbf{v}})
:

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s}({\textbf{v}})}^{2^{s}}=\E_{\textbf{x}}f({\textbf{x}}){\mathcal{D}}_{s,{\textbf{v}}}f({\textbf{x}}).

These weak inverse theorems can be easily deduced from the definitions of Gowers norms in contrast to
strong
inverse theorems developed by Green, Tao, Ziegler and Manners
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
23
]
, which establish deep connections between Gowers norms and nilsequences. Luckily for the quantitative content, we will only need the former; any use of strong inverse theorems for Gowers norms of degree at least 3 would necessarily lead to much worse bounds in our main results. An exception is a strong inverse theorem for
U^{2}({\textbf{v}})
, which gives good bounds, and which we will prove and use in later parts of the paper.

To simplify notation later on, we define
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}({\textbf{v}})
to be the collection of dual functions along
v
of degree at most
s
, and
{\mathfrak{D}}({\textbf{v}})
to be the collection of dual functions along
v
of all degrees.

3.
The PET induction scheme

We move on to describe a PET induction scheme that allows us to control the counting operator (
5
) by an average of box norms. We start with an elementary lemma that addresses the case of linear averages, originally proved in the ergodic setting by Host.

Lemma 3.1

(Box norm control for linear averages,
[
18

)
.

Let

,

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\E_{x}\E_{n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n)\cdots f_{\ell}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{\ell}n)\mathclose{}\right|\leq\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{\ell},{\textbf{v}}_{\ell}-{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{\ell}-{\textbf{v}}_{\ell-1}}.

The key PET bound in this direction is the following. In what follows, we call two polynomials essentially distinct if their difference is nonconstant, and we let
\textrm{supp}({\underline{u}})=\{j:\ u_{j}>0\}

Proposition 3.2

(PET bound)
.

Let
d,D,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
. There exist natural numbers
s,s^{\prime}=O_{d,D,\ell}(1)

{\textbf{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{p}}_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[n]^{D}

and coefficients

(18)

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\E_{{\textbf{x}}}\E_{n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{p}}_{1}(n))\cdots f_{\ell}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{p}}_{\ell}(n))\mathclose{}\right|^{2^{s^{\prime}}}\leq\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s^{\prime}}}\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{{{\textbf{c}}_{1}({\underline{h}})},\ldots,{{\textbf{c}}_{s}({\underline{h}})}}^{2^{s}}

are independent of the functions, and they take the form

\displaystyle{\textbf{c}}_{j}({\underline{h}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\underline{u}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{s^{\prime}},\\

proportion of “bad” values

\displaystyle s^{\prime\prime}=|\{(l_{1},\ldots,l_{2^{k}}):\;1\leq l_{1}<\cdots<l_{2^{k}}\leq w\}|=O_{d,s,s^{\prime}}(1),

we then have

(28)

\mathopen{}\left(\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\underline{h}}_{1},\ldots,{\underline{h}}_{w}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\left\|f\right\|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{H_{j{\underline{h}}_{l_{1}}}+\cdots+H_{j{\underline{h}}_{l_{2^{k}}}}:\ j\in[s],\ 1\leq l_{1}<\cdots<l_{2^{k}}\leq w\}\end{subarray}}\mathclose{}\right)^{O_{d,s,s^{\prime}}(1)}\\

where the error term corresponds to the “bad” values

Propositions

Proposition 4.3

(Control by a single box norm)
.

Let
d,D,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
. There exists a natural number
s=O_{d,D,\ell}(1)
with the following property: for all essentially distinct polynomials
{\textbf{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{p}}_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[n]^{D}
with degrees at most
d

and coefficients
{\textbf{p}}_{j}(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}{\textbf{a}}_{ji}n^{i}
, and for all 1-bounded functions
f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we have the bound

\mathopen{}\left|\E_{{\textbf{x}}}\E_{n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{p}}_{1}(n))\cdots f_{\ell}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{p}}_{\ell}(n))\mathclose{}\right|^{O_{d,D,\ell}(1)}\\
\leq\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{{\textbf{a}}_{\ell d_{\ell 0}}^{\times s},({\textbf{a}}_{\ell d_{\ell 1}}-{\textbf{a}}_{1d_{\ell 1}})^{\times s},\ldots,({\textbf{a}}_{\ell d_{\ell(\ell-1)}}-{\textbf{a}}_{(\ell-1)d_{\ell(\ell-1)}})^{\times s}}+O_{d,D,\ell}(p^{-1}),

where
d_{\ell j}=\deg({\textbf{p}}_{\ell}-{\textbf{p}}_{j})
and
{\textbf{p}}_{0}=\mathbf{0}
.

The main point is that directions in the box norm above depend only on the leading coefficients of the polynomial
{\textbf{p}}_{\ell},{\textbf{p}}_{\ell}-{\textbf{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{p}}_{\ell}-{\textbf{p}}_{\ell-1}
. Swapping the role of the index
\ell
with other indices, we can get analogous bounds for other functions as well.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\E_{{\textbf{x}}}\E_{n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{p}}_{1}(n))\cdots f_{\ell}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{p}}_{\ell}(n))\mathclose{}\right|^{2^{s^{\prime}}}\leq\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s^{\prime}}}\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{{{\textbf{c}}_{1}({\underline{h}})},\ldots,{{\textbf{c}}_{s}({\underline{h}})}}^{2^{s}}

for some
s^{\prime}=O_{d,D,\ell}(1)
and nonzero polynomials
{\textbf{c}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{c}}_{s}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{s^{\prime}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
that take the form

\displaystyle{\textbf{c}}_{j}({\underline{h}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\underline{u}}\in A_{d-1,s^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}c_{{\underline{u}}}({\textbf{a}}_{\ell(|{\underline{u}}|+1)}-{\textbf{a}}_{w_{j{\underline{u}}}(|{\underline{u}}|+1)}){\underline{h}}^{\underline{u}},

where:

(i)

the indices
w_{j{\underline{u}}}
take value in the set
\{0,\ldots,\ell\}
(with
{\textbf{a}}_{0(|{\underline{u}}|+1)}:=\mathbf{0}
) and have the property
w_{j{\underline{u}}}=w_{j{\underline{u}}^{\prime}}
whenever
\textrm{supp}({\underline{u}})=\textrm{supp}({\underline{u}}^{\prime})
;

(ii)

c_{{\underline{u}}}
is the coefficient of
n{\underline{h}}^{\underline{u}}
in the multinomial expansion of
(n+h_{1}+\cdots+h_{s^{\prime}})^{|{\underline{u}}|+1}
; in particular,
c_{\underline{u}}
is nonzero.

Let

\displaystyle G_{j}=\langle{\textbf{a}}_{\ell(|{\underline{u}}|+1)}-{\textbf{a}}_{w_{j{\underline{u}}}(|{\underline{u}}|+1)}:\ {\underline{u}}\in A_{d-1,s^{\prime}}\rangle.

Proposition
4.2
then gives

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\E_{{\textbf{x}}}\E_{n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{p}}_{1}(n))\cdots f_{\ell}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{p}}_{\ell}(n))\mathclose{}\right|^{O_{d,D,\ell}(1)}\leq\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{G_{1}^{\times s^{\prime\prime}},\ldots,G_{s}^{\times s^{\prime\prime}}}+O_{d,D,\ell}(p^{-1})

for some
s^{\prime\prime}=O_{d,D,\ell}(1)
.

For each
j\in[s]
, the polynomials
{\textbf{c}}_{j}
are nonzero, and so there exists
{\underline{u}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{s^{\prime}}
for which
0\leq w_{j{\underline{u}}}<\ell
. Since
w_{j{\underline{u}}}=w_{j{\underline{u}}^{\prime}}
for all
{\underline{u}}^{\prime}
with the same support as
{\underline{u}}
, we can assume that
{\underline{u}}
satisfies
|{\underline{u}}|=d_{\ell w_{j{\underline{u}}}}-1.
Letting
w_{j}:=w_{j{\underline{u}}}
, we deduce that
{\textbf{a}}_{\ell d_{\ell w_{j}}}-{\textbf{a}}_{w_{j}d_{\ell w_{j}}}\in G_{j}
.
Since
{\textbf{a}}_{\ell d_{\ell w_{j}}}-{\textbf{a}}_{w_{j}d_{\ell w_{j}}}\in G_{j}
for each
j\in[s]
, we deduce from the property (
8
) that

\mathopen{}\left|\E_{{\textbf{x}}}\E_{n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{p}}_{1}(n))\cdots f_{\ell}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{p}}_{\ell}(n))\mathclose{}\right|^{O_{d,D,\ell}(1)}\\
\leq\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{({\textbf{a}}_{\ell d_{\ell w_{1}}}-{\textbf{a}}_{w_{1}d_{\ell w_{1}}})^{\times s^{\prime\prime}},\ldots,({\textbf{a}}_{\ell d_{\ell w_{s}}}-{\textbf{a}}_{w_{s}d_{\ell w_{s}}})^{\times s^{\prime\prime}}}+O_{d,D,\ell}(p^{-1}).

Given that
w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}\in\{0,\ldots,\ell-1\}
, each vector

{\textbf{a}}_{\ell d_{\ell 0}},{\textbf{a}}_{\ell d_{\ell 1}}-{\textbf{a}}_{1d_{\ell 1}},\ldots,{\textbf{a}}_{\ell d_{\ell(\ell-1)}}-{\textbf{a}}_{(\ell-1)d_{\ell(\ell-1)}}
appears at most

times in the norm above, and so
we can bound

\mathopen{}\left|\E_{{\textbf{x}}}\E_{n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{p}}_{1}(n))\cdots f_{\ell}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{p}}_{\ell}(n))\mathclose{}\right|^{O_{d,D,\ell}(1)}\\
\leq\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{{\textbf{a}}_{\ell d_{\ell 0}}^{\times s^{\prime\prime\prime}},({\textbf{a}}_{\ell d_{\ell 1}}-{\textbf{a}}_{1d_{\ell 1}})^{\times s^{\prime\prime\prime}},\ldots,({\textbf{a}}_{\ell d_{\ell(\ell-1)}}-{\textbf{a}}_{(\ell-1)d_{\ell(\ell-1)}})^{\times s^{\prime\prime\prime}}}+O_{d,D,\ell}(p^{-1}).

The result follows upon relabelling

as
s
.
∎

Proposition 4.4

Proposition 4.5

Proof.

Applying Proposition

5.
Passing to a Gowers norm control

So far, we have shown in Proposition
3.2
via a PET argument that a counting operator for a multidimensional progression is controlled by an average of box norms, and then we used concatenation results from Section
2.2
to show in Propositions
4.3
and
4.5
that we can in fact control the counting operator by a single box norm. While aesthetically pleasing, this result alone does not give us any immediate advantage for proving Theorem
1.2
due to the lack of a usable inverse theorem for general box norms. Its utility, however, lies in the fact that it can be used as an intermediate step in establishing control by a proper Gowers norm in Theorem
1.3
as long as the polynomials are pairwise independent. Passing from a box norm control to a Gowers norm control can be accomplished via a “box norm smoothing” argument, developed by Frantzikinakis and the author in the ergodic setting
[
11
,
12
]
. This is the first appearance of this argument in the combinatorial setting, and before we present it in the full generality, we illustrate the underlying idea with two examples.

5.1.
Norm smoothing for a linearly independent progression

In this section, let

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})=\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n^{2})f_{2}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}(n^{2}+n))

be the counting operator for the progression

(35)

\displaystyle{\textbf{x}},\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n^{2},\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}(n^{2}+n).

Our goal is to sketch a proof, modulo some simplifying assumptions, that for some absolute
c>0
, we obtain the degree 1 control

(36)

\displaystyle|\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})|\ll\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{2}}^{c}+p^{-c}.

This is a special case of Theorem
1.3
. The argument for this progression is strictly simpler than the argument in the general case, as we use the fact that it is a 3-point pattern involving linearly independent polynomials. However, the argument showcases the key idea that allows us to prove Theorem
1.3
for arbitrary progressions along pairwise independent polynomials.

First, Proposition
4.3
gives
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for any 1-bounded
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we have the bound

(37)

\displaystyle|\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})|^{O(1)}\leq\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{2}^{\times s},({\textbf{v}}_{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{1})^{\times s}}+O(p^{-1}).

For the purpose of this example only, suppose that we can replace the complicated box norm
\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{2}^{\times s},({\textbf{v}}_{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{1})^{\times s}}
by a simpler box norm
\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{1}}
, so that the following bound

(38)

\displaystyle|\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})|^{O(1)}\leq\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{1}}+O(p^{-1})

holds instead. Fix
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}
. We aim to illustrate how we can pass from controlling the counting operator by the norm
\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{1}}
as in (
38
) to a control by the norm
\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{2}}
as in (
36
). Our argument follows a two-step ping-pong strategy. First, we show that the counting operator is controlled by the norm
\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1}}
.
Then, we use this auxiliary control to deduce that the operator is controlled by the norm
\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{2}}
.
Thus, we pass information first from
f_{2}
to
f_{1}
and then back from
f_{1}
to
f_{2}
, like a tennis ball from one end of a table to another; therefore we call these steps
ping
and
pong
respectively.

Let

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{2}({\textbf{x}})=\E_{n}\overline{f_{0}}({\textbf{x}}-{\textbf{v}}_{2}(n^{2}+n))\overline{f_{1}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n^{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{2}(n^{2}+n)),

so that
\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})=\E_{\textbf{x}}f_{2}({\textbf{x}})\overline{\tilde{f_{2}}({\textbf{x}})}
.
Letting
\delta=|\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})|
, we deduce from
Lemma
A.1
that

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},\tilde{f}_{2})\geq\delta^{2}.

We then use the control (
38
) to deduce that, upon assuming that
\delta\gg p^{-c}
for a sufficiently small
c>0
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{f}_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{1}}\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

From Lemma
A.1
we obtain

\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},\E(\tilde{f}_{2}|({\textbf{v}}_{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{1})))\\
=\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n^{2})\E(\tilde{f}_{2}|({\textbf{v}}_{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{1}))({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}(n^{2}+n))\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

At this point, we crucially observe that the function
\E(\tilde{f}_{2}|({\textbf{v}}_{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{1}))
is invariant under shifts by
{\textbf{v}}_{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{1}
, implying that

\displaystyle\E(\tilde{f}_{2}|({\textbf{v}}_{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{1}))({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}(n^{2}+n))=\E(\tilde{f}_{2}|({\textbf{v}}_{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{1}))({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}(n^{2}+n)).

Using this identity, we rewrite the inequality above as

\displaystyle\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n^{2})\E(\tilde{f}_{2}|({\textbf{v}}_{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{1}))({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}(n^{2}+n))\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

Importantly, this inequality involves the counting operator for the progression

(39)

\displaystyle{\textbf{x}},\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n^{2},\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}(n^{2}+n),

which is essentially single dimensional because both polynomials run along the same direction
{\textbf{v}}_{1}
.
It follows from
[
25
, Theorem 2.1]
that the counting operator for (
39
) is controlled by
\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1}}
with power-saving bounds. We therefore deduce, upon assuming once more that the constant
c>0
is sufficiently small, that
\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1}}\gg\delta^{O(1)}
. As promised, we have passed from a control of the original counting operator by the norm
\left\|\tilde{f}_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{1}}
to the control by a norm
\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1}}
of the form

(40)

\displaystyle|\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})|\ll\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1}}^{c^{\prime}}+p^{-c^{\prime}}

for some absolute
c^{\prime}>0
.
This completes the
ping
step.

At this point, we could decompose
f_{1}=\E(f_{1}|{\textbf{v}}_{1})+(f_{1}-\E(f_{1}|{\textbf{v}}_{1}))
, deduce from the newly established norm control (
40
) that the second term contributes at most
O(p^{-c^{\prime}})
to
\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})
, and use the
{\textbf{v}}_{1}
-invariance of
\E(f_{1}|{\textbf{v}}_{1})
to conclude that

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})=\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}(f_{0}\E(f_{1}|{\textbf{v}}_{1}))({\textbf{x}})f_{2}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}(n^{2}+n))+O(p^{-c^{\prime}}).

The claim (
36
) would then follow directly from
[
25
, Theorem 2.1]
. However, this argument does not work for more general progressions, so we now present an alternative method that is more readily generalisable.

We argue similarly as in the
ping
step, but this time we will pass from a control by
\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1}}
to a control by
\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{2}}
; this is the essence of the
pong
step. Letting

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{1}({\textbf{x}})=\E_{n}\overline{f_{0}}({\textbf{x}}-{\textbf{v}}_{1}n^{2})\overline{f_{2}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}(n+n^{2})-{\textbf{v}}_{1}n^{2})

we deduce from Lemma
A.1
that

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},\tilde{f}_{1},{f}_{2})\geq\delta^{2}.

Using this fact and the auxiliary control (
40
) for
\tilde{f}_{1}
in place of
f_{1}
, we infer that
\left\|\tilde{f}_{1}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1}}\gg\delta^{O(1)}
(again, under the assumption that
\delta\gg p^{-c}
for sufficiently small
c>0
). An application of Lemma
A.1
gives

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},\E(\tilde{f}_{1}|{\textbf{v}}_{1}),f_{2})=\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})\E(\tilde{f}_{1}|{\textbf{v}}_{1})({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n^{2})f_{2}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}(n^{2}+n))\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

Crucially, the function
\E(\tilde{f}_{1}|{\textbf{v}}_{1})
is
{\textbf{v}}_{1}
-invariant, implying that

\displaystyle\E(\tilde{f}_{1}|{\textbf{v}}_{1})({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n^{2})=\E(\tilde{f}_{1}|{\textbf{v}}_{1})({\textbf{x}}).

Letting
g=f_{0}\cdot\E(\tilde{f}_{1}|{\textbf{v}}_{1})
, we have thus showed that

\displaystyle\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}g({\textbf{x}})f_{2}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}(n^{2}+n))\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

From this and
[
25
, Theorem 2.1]
, we deduce that
\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{2}}\gg\delta^{O(1)}
. The claim follows.

The argument becomes more complicated when the progression has length greater than 3, the polynomials are pairwise independent rather than linearly independent, and we do not start with the simplifying assumption (
38
) instead of the much weaker but more accurate control (
37
). The next example will address the aforementioned technicalities and give a sense of changes that have to be made to tackle the more general case.

5.2.
Norm smoothing for a linearly dependent progression

In this section, let

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})=\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n)f_{2}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2})f_{3}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n))

be the counting operator for the progression

(41)

\displaystyle{\textbf{x}},\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n,\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2},\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n).

Our goal is to sketch the proof of the following special case of Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 5.1
.

There exists
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
c>0
with the following property: for every
D\in{\mathbb{N}}
and 1-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we have the bound

(42)

\displaystyle|\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})|\ll\left\|f_{3}\right\|_{U^{s}({\textbf{v}}_{3})}^{c}+p^{-c}.

In Proposition
5.3
, we upgrade Proposition
5.1
to one in which
\Lambda
is controlled by a Gowers norm of any of
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
.

The progression (
41
) is no longer linearly independent, and so while proving Proposition
5.1
, we cannot for instance invoke the results of Peluse
[
25
]
after completing the
ping
step. For that reason, the 4-point pattern (
41
) captures better the difficulties present in the general case than the 3-point progression (
35
) while still offering the benefits of concreteness.

Our starting point is Proposition
4.3
which gives
s_{0},s_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
c>0

with the property that for any 1-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we have

(43)

\displaystyle|\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})|\leq\left\|f_{3}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{3}^{\times s_{0}},({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})^{\times s_{1}}}^{c}+O(p^{-c}).

In fact, in the statement of Proposition
4.3
, we have
s_{0}=s_{1}
(which we can always assume by taking
s=\max(s_{0},s_{1})
and using the monotonicity of box norms), but it will be more illustrative to describe these two numbers using separate labels. Proposition
5.1
will follow by an iterated application of the following result, which enables us to replace the vectors
{\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2}
one by one with (possibly many copies of)
{\textbf{v}}_{3}
. In doing so, we “smooth out” or “uniformise” the original norm until we reach one that only involves a large number of
{\textbf{v}}_{3}
.

Proposition 5.2

(Norm smoothing for (
41
))
.

Let
D,s_{0},s_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
c>0
be such that (
43
) holds for all 1-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. Then there exist
s^{\prime}=O_{s_{0},s_{1}}(1)
and
c^{\prime}\gg_{c,s_{0},s_{1}}1
such that the bound

(44)

\displaystyle|\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})|\ll_{c,s_{0},s_{1}}\left\|f_{3}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{3}^{\times s^{\prime}},({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})^{\times s_{1}-1}}^{c^{\prime}}+p^{-c^{\prime}}

holds for all 1-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
.

It is crucial that the norm in (
43
) involves the direction
{\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2}
rather than
{\textbf{v}}_{2}
, otherwise the argument would not work.

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Let
\delta=|\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})|
, and assume without loss of generality that
\delta\gg_{c,s_{0},s_{1}}p^{-c^{\prime}}
for a sufficiently small
0<c^{\prime}\leq c/2
. Like in Section
5.1
, our argument follows a two-step ping-pong strategy. Starting with the assumption that we can control the counting operator by the norm
\left\|f_{3}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{3}^{\times s_{0}},({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})^{\times s_{1}}}
of
f_{3}
, we first show that the counting operator is also controlled by the norm
\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{3}^{\times s_{0}},({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})^{\times s_{1}-1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}^{\times s_{2}}}
of
f_{2}
for some
s_{2}=O_{s_{0},s_{1}}(1)
. Then, we use this auxiliary control to deduce that the operator is controlled by the norm
\left\|f_{3}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{3}^{\times s^{\prime}},({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})^{\times s_{1}-1}}
for some
s^{\prime}=O_{s_{0},s_{1}}(1)
.
As before, we call these two steps
ping
and
pong
respectively. In what follows, we allow all the quantities to depend on
c,s_{0},s_{1}
.

Step 1 (
ping
): Obtaining control by a norm of
f_{2}
.

Set

\tilde{f}_{3}({\textbf{x}})=\E_{n}\overline{f_{0}}({\textbf{x}}-{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n))\overline{f_{1}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n-{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n))\overline{f_{2}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n)),

so that

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\tilde{f}_{3})\geq\delta^{2}.

by Lemma
A.1
.
Thus, at the expense of losing an exponent, we have replaced an arbitrary function
f_{3}
in the counting operator by the structured term
\tilde{f}_{3}
. We deduce from the inequality (
43
) and the assumption
\delta\gg p^{-c/2}
that
\left\|\tilde{f}_{3}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{3}^{\times s_{0}},({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})^{\times s_{1}}}\gg\delta^{O(1)}
.

Our goal is to get to the point where we can apply the inverse theorem for degree 1 seminorm so as to correlate
\tilde{f}_{3}
, or some derivate of it, with a
({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})
-invariant function.
This is accomplished by Corollary
A.3
(i), which gives that

\E_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s_{0}+s_{1}-1}}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})\cdot f_{1,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n)\cdot f_{2,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2})\cdot u_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n))\mathclose{}\right|

is bounded from below by
\Omega(\delta^{O(1)})
for

\displaystyle f_{j,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})=\Delta_{{\textbf{v}}_{3}^{\times s_{0}},({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})^{\times s_{1}-1};{\underline{h}}-{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}f_{j}({\textbf{x}})\quad\mathrm{for}\quad j=0,1,2

and 1-bounded,
({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})
-invariant functions
u_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
.
This invariance property yields the identity

(45)

u_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n))=u_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}(n^{2}+n))

which allows us to rewrite the average over
{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}
as

\E_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s_{0}+s_{1}-1}}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})\cdot f_{1,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n)\cdot f_{2,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2})\cdot u_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}(n^{2}+n))\mathclose{}\right|.

Thus, we have replaced
{\textbf{v}}_{3}
in the counting operator by
{\textbf{v}}_{2}
. Importantly, in the new counting operator, both quadratic polynomials
n^{2}
and
n^{2}+n
lie along the same direction
{\textbf{v}}_{2}
. By the pigeonhole principle, we can find a set
B\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2(s_{0}+s_{1}-1)}
of size
|B|\gg\delta^{O(1)}p^{2(s_{0}+s_{1}-1)}
such that

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})\cdot f_{1,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n)\cdot f_{2,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2})\cdot u_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}(n^{2}+n))\mathclose{}\right|\gg\delta^{O(1)}

for each
({\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime})\in B
. It follows from Proposition
4.3
that there exists
s_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s_{0}+s_{1}-1}}1_{B}({\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime})\cdot\left\|f_{2,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}\right\|_{U^{s_{2}}({\textbf{v}}_{2})}\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

Extending by nonnegativity to all
({\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime})
, invoking the definition of
f_{2,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
, changing variables to replace
{\underline{h}}-{\underline{h}}^{\prime}
by
{\underline{h}}
and using the induction formula for box norms together with the Hölder inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{3}^{\times s_{0}},({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})^{\times s_{1}-1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}^{\times s_{2}}}\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

Hence, the norm
\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{3}^{\times s_{0}},({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})^{\times s_{1}-1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}^{\times s_{2}}}
controls the counting operator
\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
in the sense that

(46)

\displaystyle|\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})|^{O(1)}\ll\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{3}^{\times s_{0}},({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})^{\times s_{1}-1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}^{\times s_{2}}}+p^{-1}

for all 1-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
.

Starting with a control of
\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
by a norm of
f_{3}
, we have arrived at a control by a norm of
f_{2}
; in this sense we passed information from
f_{3}
to
f_{2}
. The bound (
46
) is not particularly useful as an independent result because of the
s_{0}
vectors
{\textbf{v}}_{3}
appearing as directions. However, this bound turns out to be a key intermediate step for obtaining our claimed control of
\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
by a norm
\left\|f_{3}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{3}^{\times s^{\prime}},{\mathbf{e}}_{3}^{\times s_{1}-1}}
for some
s^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Step 2 (
pong
): Obtaining control by a norm of
f_{3}
.

In this step, our starting point is the newly obtained inequality (
46
). Letting

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{2}=\E_{n}\overline{f_{0}}({\textbf{x}}-{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2})\overline{f_{1}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n-{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2})\overline{f_{3}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n)-{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2}),

we employ Lemma
A.1
to obtain

\displaystyle|\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},\tilde{f}_{2},f_{3})|\geq\delta^{2}.

We then apply the inequality (
46
) with
\tilde{f}_{2}
in place of
f_{2}
to conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{f}_{2}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{3}^{\times s_{0}},({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})^{\times s_{1}-1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}^{\times s_{2}}}\gg\delta^{O(1)},

assuming like we have done so far that
\delta\gg p^{-c^{\prime}}
for
c^{\prime}>0
sufficiently small. In the
ping
step, we applied Corollary
A.3
in order to get rid of just one
vector
{\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2}
; this was necessary for us to be able to apply the inverse theorem for degree 1 norm. This time, we aim at ridding ourselves of all the vectors
{\textbf{v}}_{2}^{\times s_{2}}
at once since we do not want any such vector to reappear in the norm of
f_{3}
ultimately controlling our average.
Applying Corollary
A.3
(ii) this time, we get that

\E_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s_{0}+s_{1}-1}}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})\cdot f_{1,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n)\cdot{\mathcal{D}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2})\cdot f_{3,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n))\mathclose{}\right|

is at least
\Omega(\delta^{O(1)})
,
where

\displaystyle f_{j,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})=\Delta_{{\textbf{v}}_{3}^{\times s_{0}},({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})^{\times s_{1}-1};{\underline{h}}-{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}f_{j}({\textbf{x}})\quad\mathrm{for}\quad j=0,1,3

and

{\mathcal{D}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
is a product of
2^{s_{0}+s_{1}-1}
elements of
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s_{2}}({\textbf{v}}_{2})
. Let
B
be the set of
({\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s_{0}+s_{1}-1}
for which

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})\cdot f_{1,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n)\cdot{\mathcal{D}}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2})\cdot f_{3,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n))\mathclose{}\right|\gg\delta^{O(1)};

we note that
|B|\gg\delta^{O(1)}p^{s_{0}+s_{1}-1}
from the pigeonhole principle. Proposition
4.5
tells us that in getting a box norm control of counting operators twisted by dual functions, we can ignore the contribution of dual functions. From Proposition
4.5
and the fact that the only quadratic polynomial outside dual functions is in
f_{3,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
and lies along
{\textbf{v}}_{3}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s_{0}+s_{1}-1}}1_{B}({\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime})\cdot\left\|f_{3,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}\right\|_{U^{s_{3}}}\gg\delta^{O(1)}

for some
s_{3}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Extending by nonnegativity to all
({\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime})
, invoking the definition of
f_{3,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
and using the induction formula for box norms, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|f_{3}\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{3}^{\times s^{\prime}},({\textbf{v}}_{3}-{\textbf{v}}_{2})^{\times s_{1}-1}}\gg\delta^{O(1)}

for
s^{\prime}=s_{0}+s_{3}
, which gives the claim.
∎

Proposition
5.1
can be used to control the other terms of the configuration by Gowers norms as follows.

Proposition 5.3
.

There exists
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
c>0
with the following property: for every
D\in{\mathbb{N}}
and 1-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we have the bound

(47)

\displaystyle|\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})|\ll\min_{j=1,2,3}\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{U^{s}({\textbf{v}}_{j})}^{c}+p^{-c}.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.1
, there exist
s_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
c_{1}>0
such that

(48)

\displaystyle|\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})|\ll\left\|f_{3}\right\|_{U^{s_{1}}({\textbf{v}}_{3})}^{c_{1}}+p^{-c_{1}}

for all 1-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. Suppose that
\delta=|\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})|
for some
\delta\gg p^{-c}
with
0<c<c_{1}/2
.
Letting

\tilde{f}_{3}=\E_{n}\overline{f_{0}}({\textbf{x}}-{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n))\overline{f_{1}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n-{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n))\overline{f_{2}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2}-{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n)),

we obtain
\left\|\tilde{f}_{3}\right\|_{U^{s_{1}}({\textbf{v}}_{3})}\gg\delta^{O(1)}
from Lemma
A.1
.
Another application of Lemma
A.1
, this time part (
iii
), gives

\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\overline{{\mathcal{D}}_{s_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{3}}\tilde{f}_{3}})\\
=\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n)f_{2}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2})\overline{{\mathcal{D}}_{s_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{3}}\tilde{f}_{3}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n))\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

We note that the expression above is the counting operator for the progression
{\textbf{x}},\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n,\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2}
twisted by the term
\overline{{\mathcal{D}}_{s_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{3}}\tilde{f}_{3}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n))
which can be removed using Proposition
4.5
. It follows from this proposition that there exists some
s_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that - assuming
c>0
is small enough - we have
\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{U^{s_{2}}({\textbf{v}}_{2})}\gg\delta^{O(1)}
.

We have thus shown that the counting operator is controlled by a Gowers norm of
f_{2}
and
f_{3}
, and it remains to prove a similar statement for
f_{1}
. Letting

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{2}=\E_{n}\overline{f_{0}}({\textbf{x}}-{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2})\overline{f_{1}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n-{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2}){\mathcal{D}}_{s_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{3}}\tilde{f}_{3}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n)-{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2})

and applying Lemma
A.1
as before, we deduce that

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},\tilde{f}_{2},\overline{{\mathcal{D}}_{s_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{3}}\tilde{f}_{3}})\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

Once again, Proposition
4.5
implies that
\left\|\tilde{f}_{2}\right\|_{U^{s_{2}}({\textbf{v}}_{2})}\gg\delta^{O(1)}

5.3.
The formalism for longer progressions

To prove Theorem
1.2
in full generality, we need a robust formalism, imported from our earlier ergodic work with Frantzikinakis
[
11
]
.
We shall handle longer families by reducing an arbitrary counting operator to a counting operator of a smaller “type” much the same as we did in the proof of Proposition
5.2
. In what follows, we will be dealing with counting operators of the form

(49)

\displaystyle\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})\cdot\prod_{j\in[\ell]}f_{j}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{\eta_{j}}p_{j}(n))\cdot\prod_{j\in[L]}{\mathcal{D}}_{j}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{u}}_{\pi_{j}}q_{j}(n))

for various choices of
\eta=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{\ell})\in[\ell]^{\ell}
and
\pi=(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{L})\in[L]^{L}

We encourage the reader to think of (
49
) as the counting operator for

\displaystyle{\textbf{x}},\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{\eta_{1}}p_{1}(n),\;\ldots,\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{\eta_{\ell}}p_{\ell}(n)

along 1-bounded functions
f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, twisted by the product
\prod_{j\in[L]}{\mathcal{D}}_{j}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{u}}_{\pi_{j}}q_{j}(n))
, where
{\mathcal{D}}_{j}\in{\mathfrak{D}}({\textbf{u}}_{\pi_{j}})
. In other words, we do not think of
\prod_{j\in[L]}{\mathcal{D}}_{j}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{u}}_{\pi_{j}}q_{j}(n))
as an important part of the count (
49
), but rather as an annoying term that can be removed using Proposition
4.4
and which therefore does not play a major role in our analysis.
We let

•

\ell
be the

of (
49
) (noting that a counting operator of length
\ell
corresponds to a polynomial pattern of length
\ell+1
),

•

be its

,

•

\eta:=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{\ell})\in[\ell]^{\ell}
be the
indexing tuple
of (
49
).

Furthermore, we define

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{L}}:=\{j\in[\ell]\colon\ \deg p_{j}=d\}

to be the set of indices corresponding to polynomials

of maximum degree.

The relative complexity of various counting operators is measured by the following notion of type.
Letting
K=|{\mathfrak{L}}|
be the number of maximum degree polynomials among

we set the
type
of (
49

, where each entry

is defined by

\displaystyle w_{t}:=|\{j\in{\mathfrak{L}}\colon\ \eta_{j}=t\}|=|\{j\in[\ell]\colon\ \eta_{j}=t,\ \deg p_{j}=d\}|;

thus, it represents the number of polynomials
p_{j}
of maximum degree appearing along the vector
{\textbf{v}}_{t}
. We note that
|w|:=w_{1}+\cdots+w_{\ell}=K
.
For instance, the counting operator

\displaystyle\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}

\displaystyle f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n^{2})f_{2}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n)f_{3}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n))

\displaystyle f_{4}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}(n^{2}+2n))f_{5}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(2n^{2}+n)){\mathcal{D}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{u}}n^{3})

for some
{\mathcal{D}}\in{\mathfrak{D}}({\textbf{u}})
has length 5, degree 2,
K=4
and indexing tuple
(1,2,3,1,3)
- we stress that we ignore the term
{\mathcal{D}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{u}}n^{3})
while discussing the aforementioned parameters, hence the degree three polynomial

and its direction vector

(2,0,2,0,0)
since two of the quadratic polynomials lie along
{\textbf{v}}_{1}
and two others lie along
{\textbf{v}}_{3}
.

To organise the induction scheme, we need to define a partial ordering on types
w\in[0,K]^{\ell}
. Let
\textrm{supp}(w)=\{t\in[0,K]:\ w_{t}>0\}
. For distinct integers
m,i\in[\ell]
with
m\in\textrm{supp}(w)
, we define the type operation

\displaystyle(\sigma_{mi}w)_{t}:=\begin{cases}w_{t},\;&t\neq m,i\\
w_{m}-1,\;&t=m\\
w_{i}+1,\;&t=i.\end{cases}.

For instance,
\sigma_{12}(2,3,7)=(1,4,7)
.
Letting
w^{\prime}:=\sigma_{mi}w
, we set
w^{\prime}<w
if
w_{m}\leq w_{i}
. In particular,
(1,4,7)<(2,3,7)
in the example above. We note that the tuple
(2,3,7)
of higher type has smaller variance than the tuple
(1,4,7)
of smaller type; this is a consequence of the fact that while passing from
(2,3,7)
to
(1,4,7)
, we decrease by 1 the smallest nonzero value 2.
This observation carries forward more generally: if the condition

is satisfied, then an easy computation shows that
w^{\prime}
has strictly higher variance than
w
, or equivalently
{w^{\prime}_{1}}^{2}+\cdots+{w^{\prime}_{\ell}}^{2}>w_{1}^{2}+\cdots+w_{\ell}^{2}
. Thanks to this fact, we can extend the partial ordering

to all tuples
[0,K]^{\ell}
of length
K
by transitivity, and so for two type tuples

, we let

if there exist types
w_{0},\ldots,w_{r}
with
w_{0}=w
,
w_{r}=w^{\prime}
, such that for every

, we have

for distinct

with

. For instance, this ordering induces the following chains of types:

\displaystyle(4,0,0)<(3,1,0)<(2,2,0)<(2,1,1)\quad\textrm{and}\quad(0,4,0)<(1,3,0)<(2,2,0)<(2,1,1).

We note here that the highest order type

of length
K
is one whose entries only take values 0 and 1, corresponding to the operators (
49
) in which each highest degree polynomial
p_{j}
lies along a different vector. By contrast, the lowest order type is one in which one entry is
K
while the other ones are all 0; this corresponds to the counting operator in which all highest degree polynomials

lie along the same direction. Theorem
1.3
for such operators is a direct consequence of Proposition
4.5
. We call the types of the latter form

. Thus, the ordering on types expresses the intuition that counting operators with plenty of different direction vectors are more complex than those with few.

Our induction scheme works as follows. We want to obtain Gowers norm control on general counting operators of length
\ell
and type

of the form (
49

are pairwise independent. For instance, the counting operator

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})=\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n)f_{2}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2})f_{3}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n))

that we examined in Proposition
5.2
has length

and type

. In the

but lower type

for some
m,i
with
w_{m}<w_{i}
; this will correspond to replacing one instance of the vector
{\textbf{v}}_{m}
by the vector
{\textbf{v}}_{i}
. Indeed, in the
ping

5.2
, we reduced to the counting operator

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})=\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n)f_{2}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2})f_{3}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}(n^{2}+n))

of the same length
\ell=3
but lower type

. The type is basic, and we indeed obtained the control over

by directly invoking Proposition
4.5
(in that case, it sufficed to invoke Proposition
4.3
). In the

, we will reduce the counting operator to one of smaller length

; this will correspond to replacing one of the terms by a product of dual functions. In Proposition
5.2
, the relevant operator of shorter length 2 took the form

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},{\mathcal{D}},f_{3})=\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n){\mathcal{D}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2})f_{3}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n)).

Lastly, once we obtain Gowers norm control on one of the terms with highest-degree polynomials, we will use the weak inverse theorem for Gowers norms to replace this term in the counting operator by a dual function, once again reducing the counting operator to one of length

, to which we will apply the induction hypothesis in order to get Gowers norm control for other terms. In Proposition

, we did this by passing to the counting operator

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},{\mathcal{D}})=\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}n)f_{2}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{2}){\mathcal{D}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{3}(n^{2}+n)).

of length

Thus, in the

for counting operators of the same length

. Similarly, reducing the counting operators in the

.

6.
Degree lowering

Last but not least, we derive Theorem
1.2
from Theorem
1.3
. As stated in the introduction, Theorem
1.2
can be derived from Theorem
1.3
with the exact same degree lowering argument that was used to derive
[
20
, Theorem 1.2]
, the special case of Theorem
1.2
for distinct degree polynomials, from
[
20
, Proposition 4.1]
. Here, we give an alternative version of the degree lowering argument, which we believe gives a better conceptual understanding of what really is going on.

A key concept that we will need is that of an eigenfunction. For
{\textbf{v}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, we say that
\chi:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is an
eigenfunction
of
v
with
eigenvalue

\phi:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
if:

(i)

(Modulus)
|\chi({\textbf{x}})|\in\{0,1\}
for every
{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}
;

(ii)

(Eigenfunction property)
\chi({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}})=e_{p}(\phi({\textbf{x}}))\chi({\textbf{x}})
;

(iii)

(Invariance)
\phi
is
v
-invariant.

More explicitly, if
H=\langle{\textbf{v}}\rangle^{\perp}
and
{\textbf{x}}={\textbf{x}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}n
is the unique decomposition of
x
into two parts
{\textbf{x}}^{\prime}\in H
and
{\textbf{v}}n\in\langle{\textbf{v}}\rangle
, then
\chi({\textbf{x}})=\chi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}n)=1_{E}({\textbf{x}}^{\prime})\lambda e_{p}(\phi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime})n+\psi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}))
for some
\phi,\psi:H\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
,
|\lambda|=1
and
E\subset H
.

We let
{\mathcal{E}}({\textbf{v}})
denote the group of eigenfunctions of
v
; we note that it is closed under complex conjugation. These eigenfunctions are a relatively simple example of the notion of
nonergodic eigenfunctions
originally defined in ergodic setting by Frantzikinakis and Host
[
10
]
. The name comes from the fact that on each coset of
\langle{\textbf{v}}\rangle
, our eigenfunctions become “classical eigenfunctions” with respect to the transformation
T{\textbf{x}}={\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}
.

For instance, if
D=2
and
{\textbf{v}}=(1,0)
, each eigenfunction
\chi\in{\mathcal{E}}({\textbf{v}})
takes the form

\displaystyle\chi(x_{1},x_{2})=\lambda\cdot 1_{E}(x_{2})\cdot e_{p}(\phi(x_{2})x_{1}+\psi(x_{2}))\quad\textrm{for}\quad|\lambda|=1\quad\textrm{and}\quad E\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{p}.

If
\chi\in{\mathcal{E}}({\textbf{v}})
is an eigenfunction with eigenvalue
\phi
, then

(58)

\displaystyle\E(\chi|{\textbf{v}})({\textbf{x}})=\E_{n}\chi({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}n)=\chi({\textbf{x}})\E_{n}e_{p}(\phi({\textbf{x}})n)=\chi({\textbf{x}})1_{\phi({\textbf{x}})=0}.

We will need this fact later.

With these definitions, the
strong inverse theorem
for
U^{2}({\textbf{v}})
takes the following form.

Lemma 6.1

(Strong inverse theorem for
U^{2}({\textbf{v}})
)
.

Let
D\in{\mathbb{N}}
and suppose that
f:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is 1-bounded. Then

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{U^{2}({\textbf{v}})}^{4}\leq\E_{{\textbf{x}}}f({\textbf{x}})\chi({\textbf{x}})

for some
\chi\in{\mathcal{E}}({\textbf{v}})
which additionally satisfies
\E(f\cdot\chi|{\textbf{v}})\geq 0
and
|\chi|=1
.

Proof.

Let
H=\langle{\textbf{v}}\rangle^{\perp}
, so that each
{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}
can be uniquely decomposed as
{\textbf{x}}={\textbf{x}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}n
for
{\textbf{x}}^{\prime}\in H
and
n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. For each
{\textbf{x}}^{\prime}\in H
and
n\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, we then define
f_{{\textbf{x}}^{\prime}}(n)=f({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}n)
. Then the usual
U^{2}
inverse theorem gives
\phi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{U^{2}({\textbf{v}})}^{4}=\E_{{\textbf{x}}^{\prime}\in H}\left\|f_{{\textbf{x}}^{\prime}}\right\|^{4}_{U^{2}}\leq\E_{{\textbf{x}}^{\prime}\in H}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n}f({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}n)e_{p}(\phi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime})n)\mathclose{}\right|.

Picking a phase function
\psi:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
and
|\lambda|=1
satisfying

\displaystyle\E_{n}f({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}n)\cdot\lambda e_{p}(\phi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime})n+\psi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}))=\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n}f({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}n)e_{p}(\phi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime})n)\mathclose{}\right|,

we get

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{U^{2}({\textbf{v}})}^{4}\leq\E_{{\textbf{x}}^{\prime}\in H}\E_{n}f({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}n)\cdot\lambda e_{p}(\phi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime})n+\psi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime})).

The first statement follows upon setting
\chi({\textbf{x}})=\chi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}n)=\lambda e_{p}(\phi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime})n+\psi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}))
, and the second follows from the observation that

\displaystyle\E(f\cdot\chi|{\textbf{v}})({\textbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\E_{m}f({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}m)\chi({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}m)=\E_{m}f({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}(n+m))\chi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}(n+m))

\displaystyle=\E_{m}f({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}m)\chi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}m)=\mathopen{}\left|\E_{m}f({\textbf{x}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}m)e_{p}(\phi({\textbf{x}}^{\prime})m)\mathclose{}\right|\geq 0.

∎

\displaystyle\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})\prod_{j\in[\ell]}f_{j}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{j}p_{j}(n))=\E_{{\textbf{x}}}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})\cdot\prod_{j\in[\ell]}f_{j}({\textbf{x}})\cdot\E_{n}e_{p}\mathopen{}\left(\sum_{j\in[\ell]}\phi_{j}({\textbf{x}})p_{j}(n)\mathclose{}\right),

where
\phi_{j}
is the eigenvalue of
f_{j}
. Since the polynomials
p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}
are linearly independent, we get from the Weil estimates (see e.g.
[
19
, Theorem 3.2]
) that

\displaystyle\E_{n}e_{p}\mathopen{}\left(\sum_{j\in[\ell]}\phi_{j}({\textbf{x}})p_{j}(n)\mathclose{}\right)=1_{\phi_{1}({\textbf{x}})=\cdots=\phi_{\ell}({\textbf{x}})=0}+O_{d}(p^{-1/2}).

Hence

\displaystyle\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})\prod_{j\in[\ell]}f_{j}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{j}p_{j}(n))=\E_{{\textbf{x}}}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})\cdot\prod_{j\in[\ell]}(f_{j}({\textbf{x}})\cdot 1_{\phi_{j}({\textbf{x}})=0})+O_{d}(p^{-1/2}),

and the result follows from (
58
).

The case
m>0

We move on to the case
m>0
, i.e. when
f_{m+1},\ldots,f_{\ell}
are all eigenfunctions of the respective vectors. Our first goal is to show that under this assumption, we have

(60)

\displaystyle|\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell})|^{O(1)}\ll\min_{j\in[\ell]\setminus\{m\}}\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{U^{1}({\textbf{v}}_{j})}+p^{-1}.

Here and for the rest of the proof, we let all quantities depend on
d
and
\ell
. Let
\delta=|\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell})|
, and suppose that
\delta\gg p^{c}
for a sufficiently small
c>0
- otherwise the result follows trivially.
By Lemma
A.1
, we have

\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\tilde{f}_{m},f_{m+1},\ldots,f_{\ell})\geq\delta^{2},

and hence by Theorem
1.3
, there exists
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\left\|\tilde{f}_{m}\right\|_{U^{s}({\textbf{v}}_{m})}\gg\delta^{O(1)}
. By an iterated application of Proposition
6.3
, we have
\left\|\tilde{f}_{m}\right\|_{U^{1}({\textbf{v}}_{m})}\gg\delta^{O(1)}
, and hence Lemma
A.1
(
ii
) gives

\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\E(\tilde{f}_{m}|{\textbf{v}}_{m}),f_{m+1},\ldots,f_{\ell})\\
=\E_{\textbf{x}}(f_{0}\E(\tilde{f}_{m}|{\textbf{v}}_{m}))({\textbf{x}})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[\ell],\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}f_{j}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{j}p_{j}(n))\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

We have reduced to the case
(m-1,\ell-1)
(or
(m-1,\ell)
if we think of 1 as the function evaluated at
{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{m}p_{m}(n)
), and so invoking Proposition
5.4
in this case, we deduce that

\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{U^{1}({\textbf{v}}_{j})}\gg\delta^{O(1)}
for
j\in[\ell]\setminus\{m\}
. Hence (
60
) follows.

It remains to show that the
U^{1}({\textbf{v}}_{m})
norm of
f_{m}
also controls
\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell})
. Splitting
f_{j}=\E(f_{j}|{\textbf{v}}_{j})+(f_{j}-\E(f_{j}|{\textbf{v}}_{j}))
for
j\in[\ell]\setminus\{m\}
and using (
60
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell})=\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}F({\textbf{x}})f_{m}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{m}p_{m}(n))+O(p^{-\Omega(1)}),

where
F=f_{0}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[\ell]\setminus\{m\}\end{subarray}}\E(f_{j}|{\textbf{v}}_{j})
. Since we are left with a single dimensional progression, the claim then follows from
[
25
, Theorem 2.1]
.
∎

It remains to prove Proposition
6.3
for
(m,\ell)
assuming Proposition
5.4
for
(m-1,\ell)
.

Proof of Proposition
6.3
.

We allow all the quantities in the proof to depend on
d,\ell,s
.

Suppose that
\left\|\tilde{f}_{m}\right\|_{U^{s}({\textbf{v}}_{m})}\geq\delta
for some
s\geq 2
and
\delta>0
.
The induction formula for Gowers norms gives

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s-2}}\left\|\Delta_{s-2,{\textbf{v}}_{m};{\underline{h}}}\tilde{f}_{m}\right\|^{4}_{U^{2}({\textbf{v}}_{m})}\geq\delta^{2^{s}}.

By Lemma
6.1
, there exist eigenfunctions
\chi_{\underline{h}}\in{\mathcal{E}}({\textbf{v}}_{m})
with eigenvalues
\phi_{\underline{h}}
satisfying

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s-2}}\E_{\textbf{x}}\Delta_{s-2,{\textbf{v}}_{m};{\underline{h}}}\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})\chi_{\underline{h}}({\textbf{x}})\geq\delta^{2^{s}};

our goal is to show that for many
{\underline{h}}
, the eigenfunctions
\chi_{\underline{h}}
can be expressed as a product of low-complexity functions on many cosets of
{\textbf{v}}_{m}
.
Lemma
6.1
also gives

\E(\Delta_{s-2,{\textbf{v}}_{m};{\underline{h}}}\tilde{f}_{m}\cdot\chi_{\underline{h}}|{\textbf{v}}_{m})\geq 0;

hence the set

\displaystyle B=\{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s-2}:\ \E_{\textbf{x}}\Delta_{s-2,{\textbf{v}}_{m};{\underline{h}}}\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})\chi_{\underline{h}}({\textbf{x}})\geq\delta^{2^{s}}/2\}

has size
|B|\gg\delta^{2^{s}}p^{2^{s-2}}
, and the set

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}_{\underline{h}}=\{{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}:\ \E(\Delta_{s-2,{\textbf{v}}_{m};{\underline{h}}}\tilde{f}_{m}\cdot\chi_{\underline{h}}|{\textbf{v}}_{m})({\textbf{x}})\geq\delta^{2^{s}}/4\}

has cardinality
|{\mathcal{U}}_{\underline{h}}|\gg\delta^{2^{s}}p^{D}
for each
{\underline{h}}\in B
. The sets
{\mathcal{U}}_{\underline{h}}
are
{\textbf{v}}_{m}
-invariant, which gives us the lower bound

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s-2}}1_{B}({\underline{h}})\E_{\textbf{x}}1_{{\mathcal{U}}_{{\underline{h}}}}({\textbf{x}})\Delta_{s-2,{\textbf{v}}_{m};{\underline{h}}}\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})\chi_{\underline{h}}({\textbf{x}})\gg\delta^{2^{s}}.

The next step is to pass the multiplicative derivative from
\tilde{f}_{m}
to the functions
f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell}
. This is accomplished using Proposition
A.2
applied to
u_{\underline{h}}({\textbf{x}})=1_{B}({\underline{h}})1_{{\mathcal{U}}_{{\underline{h}}}}({\textbf{x}})\chi_{\underline{h}}({\textbf{x}})
, which gives

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s-2}}1_{B^{\prime}}({\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime})\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[0,\ell],\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}f_{j,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{j}p_{j}(n))\cdot\mathopen{}\left(1_{{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}}\chi_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}\mathclose{}\right)({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{m}p_{m}(n))\gg\delta^{O(1)}

with

\displaystyle B^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\{({\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2(s-2)}:\;{\underline{h}}^{\underline{\epsilon}}\in B\;\textrm{for\; all}\;{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s-2}\}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s-2}}{\mathcal{U}}_{{\underline{h}}^{\underline{\epsilon}}}

\displaystyle f_{j,{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{s-2,{\textbf{v}}_{m};{\underline{h}}-{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}f_{j}({\textbf{x}})

\displaystyle\chi_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\prod_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s-2}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|}\chi_{{\underline{h}}^{\underline{\epsilon}}}({\textbf{x}}-(\underline{1}\cdot{\underline{h}}^{\prime}){\textbf{v}}_{m}).

We recall that
{\underline{h}}^{\underline{\epsilon}}=(h_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}},\ldots,h_{s}^{\epsilon_{s}})
with
h_{i}^{\epsilon_{i}}=h_{i}
if
\epsilon_{i}=0
and
h_{i}^{\epsilon_{i}}=h_{i}^{\prime}
otherwise.

Crucially, each counting operator indexed by
{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}
corresponds to the case
(m-1,\ell)
of Proposition
5.4
, as the function
1_{{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}}\chi_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
evaluated at
{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{m}p_{m}(n)
is an eigenfunction of
{\textbf{v}}_{m}
. Applying the case
(m-1,\ell)
of Proposition
5.4
,
we therefore get

(61)

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s-2}}1_{B^{\prime}}({\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime})\left\|1_{{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}}\chi_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}\right\|_{U^{1}({\textbf{v}}_{m})}^{2}\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

We define

\displaystyle\phi_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})=\sum_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}(-1)^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|}\phi_{{\underline{h}}^{\underline{\epsilon}}}({\textbf{x}}),

so that

(62)

\displaystyle\chi_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{m}n)=e_{p}(\phi_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})n)\chi_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}).

Since
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
is
{\textbf{v}}_{m}
-invariant, we deduce from (
58
), (
61
) and (
62
) that

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s-2}}1_{B^{\prime}}({\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime})\E_{\textbf{x}}1_{{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}}({\textbf{x}})1_{\phi_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})=0}\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

We have thus shown that for many differences
{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}
and many points
x
, the value
\phi_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})
is 0. We will use this observation to detect some low-complexity structure on the phases
\phi_{\underline{h}}.
First, we apply the pigeonhole principle to find
{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in B
for which

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s-2}}1_{B^{\prime}}({\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime})\E_{\textbf{x}}1_{{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}}({\textbf{x}})1_{\phi_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})=0}\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

Letting

\displaystyle B^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s-2}:\ ({\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime})\in B^{\prime}\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime\prime}_{{\underline{h}}}

\displaystyle=\{{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}:\ {\textbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}\;\textrm{and}\;\phi_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})=0\},

we deduce that

(63)

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s-2}}1_{B^{\prime\prime}}({\underline{h}})\E_{\textbf{x}}1_{{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime\prime}_{{\underline{h}}}}({\textbf{x}})\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

Unsurprisingly, the sets
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime\prime}_{\underline{h}}
are
{\textbf{v}}_{m}
-invariant.

Next, we note that for each
{\underline{h}}\in B^{\prime\prime}
and
{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime\prime}_{{\underline{h}}}
, we can write
\phi_{{\underline{h}}}({\textbf{x}})=\sum_{j=1}^{s-2}\phi_{j{\underline{h}}}({\textbf{x}})
, where
\phi_{j{\underline{h}}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\phi_{j{\underline{h}}}({\textbf{x}})=\begin{cases}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s-2},\epsilon_{j}=1,\\
\epsilon_{1}=\ldots=\epsilon_{j-1}=0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|+1}\phi_{{\underline{h}}^{\underline{\epsilon}}}({\textbf{x}}),\;&{\underline{h}}\in B^{\prime\prime},{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime\prime}_{{\underline{h}}}\\
0,\;&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It follows that for
{\underline{h}}\in B^{\prime\prime}
and
{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime\prime}_{{\underline{h}}}
, we have the identity

(64)

\displaystyle\chi_{\underline{h}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{m}n)=\prod_{j=1}^{s-2}e_{p}(\phi_{j{\underline{h}}}({\textbf{x}})n)\cdot\chi_{\underline{h}}({\textbf{x}}).

Crucially, the sequence
{\underline{h}}\mapsto\phi_{j{\underline{h}}}
does not depend on
h_{j}
.

Bringing together the inclusions
B^{\prime\prime}\subset B
and
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime\prime}_{\underline{h}}\subset{\mathcal{U}}_{\underline{h}}
for
{\underline{h}}\in B^{\prime\prime}
, the inequality (
63
) and the lower bound
\E(\Delta_{s-2,{\textbf{v}}_{m};{\underline{h}}}\tilde{f}_{m}\cdot\chi_{\underline{h}}|{\textbf{v}}_{m})({\textbf{x}})\gg\delta^{O(1)}
for
{\underline{h}}\in B
and
{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{U}}_{\underline{h}}
, we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s-2}}1_{B^{\prime\prime}}({\underline{h}})\E_{\textbf{x}}1_{{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime\prime}_{{\underline{h}}}}({\textbf{x}})\E(\Delta_{s-2,{\textbf{v}}_{m};{\underline{h}}}\tilde{f}_{m}\cdot\chi_{\underline{h}}|{\textbf{v}}_{m})({\textbf{x}})\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

Combined with the identity (
64
), it implies that

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s-2}}1_{B^{\prime\prime}}({\underline{h}})\E_{{\textbf{x}}}1_{{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime\prime}_{{\underline{h}}}}({\textbf{x}})\chi_{\underline{h}}({\textbf{x}})\E_{n}\Delta_{s-2,{\textbf{v}}_{m};{\underline{h}}}\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{m}n)e_{p}\mathopen{}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s-2}\phi_{j{\underline{h}}}({\textbf{x}})n\mathclose{}\right)\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

An application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the
{\textbf{v}}_{m}
-invariance of
\phi_{j{\underline{h}}}
give

\displaystyle\E_{({\underline{h}},h_{s-1})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s-1}}\E_{{\textbf{x}}}\Delta_{s-1,{\textbf{v}}_{m};({\underline{h}},h_{s-1})}\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})e_{p}\mathopen{}\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{s-2}\phi_{j{\underline{h}}}({\textbf{x}})h_{s-1}\mathclose{}\right)\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

The result follows from Lemma
A.4
.

∎

Appendix A
Standard technical lemmas

We gather here standard technical lemmas that we need for various arguments. Throughout this section, we set

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\E_{{\textbf{x}},n}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})\prod_{j\in[\ell]}f_{j}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{j}p_{j}(n))

\displaystyle\textrm{and}\quad\quad\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\E_{n}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[0,\ell],\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}f_{0}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{j}p_{j}(n)-{\textbf{v}}_{m}p_{m}(n)).

for some vectors
{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, polynomials
p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[n]
and 1-bounded functions
f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
.

The following trick has become pretty standard in degree lowering and norm smoothing arguments.

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose that
\E_{\textbf{x}}\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})g({\textbf{x}})\geq\delta
for a 1-bounded function
g:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. Then

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\overline{g},f_{m+1},\ldots,f_{\ell})\geq\delta.

In particular,

(i)

if
|\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell})|\geq\delta
, then
\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\tilde{f}_{m},f_{m+1},\ldots,f_{\ell})\geq\delta^{2}
;

(ii)

if
\left\|\tilde{f}_{m}\right\|_{U^{1}({\textbf{v}}_{m})}\geq\delta
, then
\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\E(\tilde{f}_{m}|{\textbf{v}}_{m}),f_{m+1},\ldots,f_{\ell})\geq\delta^{2}
;

(iii)

if
\left\|\tilde{f}_{m}\right\|_{U^{s}({\textbf{v}}_{m})}\geq\delta
, then
\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\overline{{\mathcal{D}}_{s,{\textbf{v}}_{m}}\tilde{f}_{m}},f_{m+1},\ldots,f_{\ell})\geq\delta^{2^{s}}
;

Proof.

Since
\E_{\textbf{x}}\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})g({\textbf{x}})
is real by assumption, it equals its complex conjugate
\E_{\textbf{x}}\overline{\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})g({\textbf{x}})}
.
The first claim is then a direct consequence of the identity

\displaystyle\E_{\textbf{x}}\overline{\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})g({\textbf{x}})}=\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1},\overline{g},f_{m+1},\ldots,f_{\ell}),

which follows from expanding the definition of
\tilde{f}_{m}
and making the change of variables
{\textbf{x}}\mapsto{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{m}p_{m}(n)
. The claim (
i
) follows from

\displaystyle|\Lambda(f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell})|^{2}=|\E_{\textbf{x}}f_{m}({\textbf{x}})\overline{\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})}|^{2}\leq\left\|\tilde{f}_{m}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\E_{\textbf{x}}\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})\overline{\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})}

upon taking
g=\overline{\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})}
.

The claims (
ii
) and (
iii
) follow from the weak inverse theorems for
U^{1}({\textbf{v}}_{m})
and
U^{s}({\textbf{v}}_{m})
upon taking
g
to be
\overline{\E(\tilde{f}_{m}|{\textbf{v}}_{m})}
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{s,{\textbf{v}}_{m}}\tilde{f}_{m}
respectively.
∎

The next result allows us to pass differences from the structured function
\tilde{f}_{m}
to its component functions. Its proof, based on
s
applications of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality followed by the change of variables
{\textbf{x}}\mapsto{\textbf{x}}-(h_{1}^{\prime}{\textbf{b}}_{1}+\cdots+h^{\prime}_{s}{\textbf{b}}_{s})
, is pretty standard and follows the same strategy as the proofs of
[
8
, Proposition 4.3]
or
[
30
, Lemma 6.3]
.

Lemma A.2

(Dual-difference interchange)
.

Let
s,D\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\textbf{b}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{b}}_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
and for each
{\underline{h}}\in F_{p}^{s}
, let
u_{\underline{h}}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be 1-bounded.
If

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s}}\E_{\textbf{x}}\Delta_{{\textbf{b}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{b}}_{s};{\underline{h}}}\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})u_{\underline{h}}({\textbf{x}})\geq\delta

for some
\delta>0
, then

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s}}\E_{\textbf{x}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[0,\ell],\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}\Delta_{{\textbf{b}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{b}}_{s};{\underline{h}}-{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}f_{j}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{j}p_{j}(n))\cdot u_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{m}p_{m}(n))\geq\delta^{2^{s}},

where

\displaystyle u_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}})=\prod_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|}u_{{\underline{h}}^{{\underline{\epsilon}}}}({\textbf{x}}-(h_{1}^{\prime}{\textbf{b}}_{1}+\cdots+h^{\prime}_{s}{\textbf{b}}_{s})).

In applications, we will mostly need the following corollary of Lemma
A.2
, which combines Lemma
A.2
with the inductive formula for box norms and weak inverse theorem for Gowers norms.

Corollary A.3
.

Let
s,s^{\prime},D\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\textbf{b}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{b}}_{s},{\textbf{b}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
and suppose that

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{f}_{m}\right\|_{{\textbf{b}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{b}}_{s},{\textbf{b}}^{\times s^{\prime}}}\geq\delta

for some
\delta>0
. Then

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s}}\E_{\textbf{x}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[0,\ell],\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}\Delta_{{\textbf{b}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{b}}_{s};{\underline{h}}-{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}f_{j}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{j}p_{j}(n))\cdot u_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{m}p_{m}(n))\geq\delta^{2^{2s+s^{\prime}}},

where

(i)

if
s^{\prime}=1
, then
u_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
is
b
-invariant;

(ii)

if
s^{\prime}\geq 1
, then
u_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
is a product of
2^{s}
elements of
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s^{\prime}}({\textbf{b}})
.

Proof.

From the inductive formula for box norms and the weak inverse theorem for
U^{s^{\prime}}({\textbf{b}})
, we have

\displaystyle\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s}}\E_{\textbf{x}}\Delta_{{\textbf{b}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{b}}_{s};{\underline{h}}}\tilde{f}_{m}({\textbf{x}})u_{\underline{h}}({\textbf{x}})\geq\delta^{2^{s+s^{\prime}}},

where
u_{\underline{h}}\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{s^{\prime}}({\textbf{b}})
. If
s^{\prime}=1
, then
u_{\underline{h}}
is additionally
b
-invariant.
The claim then follows from Lemma
A.2
and the observation that
u_{{\underline{h}},{\underline{h}}^{\prime}}
is a product of
2^{s}
elements of
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s^{\prime}}({\textbf{b}})
(and
b
-invariant for
s^{\prime}=1
).
∎

The last lemma is a standard variant of the Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that allows us to remove low complexity functions, and it can be proved the same way as
[
8
, Lemma 3.4]
or
[
30
, Lemma 6.4]
.

Lemma A.4

(Removing low-complexity functions)
.

Let
D,s\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\textbf{v}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
be a vector
f:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a 1-bounded function.
For
j\in[s]
and
{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s}
, let
g_{j{\underline{h}}}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be 1-bounded functions such that the sequence of functions
{\underline{h}}\mapsto g_{j{\underline{h}}}
does not depend on the variable
h_{j}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\E_{{\underline{h}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{s}}\E_{{\textbf{x}}}\Delta_{s,{\textbf{v}};{\underline{h}}}f({\textbf{x}})\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{s}g_{j{\underline{h}}}({\textbf{x}})\mathclose{}\right|\leq\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s}({\textbf{v}})}.

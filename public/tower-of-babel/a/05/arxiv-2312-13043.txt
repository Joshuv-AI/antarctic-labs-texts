Abstract
We search for the e + ​ e − → η b ​ ( 1 ​ S ) ​ ω e^{+}e^{-}\to\eta_{b}(1S)\omega and e + ​ e − → χ b ​ 0 ​ ( 1 ​ P ) ​ ω e^{+}e^{-}\to\chi_{b0}(1P)\omega processes at a center-of-mass energy of 10.745 GeV, which is close to the peak of the Υ ⁡ ( 10753 ) \Upsilon(10753) state. We use data collected by the Belle II experiment during a special run, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 9.8 ​ fb − 1 9.8\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1} . We reconstruct ω → π + ​ π − ​ π 0 \omega\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0} decays and use the ω \omega meson’s recoil mass to search for the signals. We do not find evidence for either process, and set upper limits on the corresponding Born-level cross sections of 2.5 pb and 7.8 pb, respectively, at the 90% confidence level. The χ b ​ 0 ​ ( 1 ​ P ) ​ ω \chi_{b0}(1P)\omega limit is the result of a combination of this analysis and a previous search using full reconstruction.



I
Introduction

Recently, the Belle experiment observed a new state, the

\Upsilon(10753)
, as a narrow enhancement in the

e^{+}e^{-}\to\Upsilon(1S,2S,3S)\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
cross
sections
Belle:2019cbt
. Subsequently, the Belle II experiment
observed a similar structure in the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\chi_{b{1,2}}(1P)\omega

cross sections, which confirms the existence of the
\Upsilon(10753)

in additional decay channels
Belle-II:2022xdi
.
This state has been interpreted in several ways: as an
\Upsilon(3D)

bottomonium level mixed with nearby
S
-wave states via hadron
loops
Badalian:2009bu
;
Li:2019qsg
;
Liang:2019geg
;
Giron:2020qpb
, a
hadronic molecule with a small admixture of
bottomonium
Bicudo:2020qhp
, a hybrid
meson
TarrusCastella:2019lyq
;
Chen:2019uzm
;
Brambilla:2019esw
,
or a compact tetraquark with a diquark-antidiquark
structure
Wang:2019veq
;
Ali:2019okl
.
Further studies of hadronic transitions from the
\Upsilon(10753)
to
lower bottomonia will help to understand its
structure
Giron:2020qpb
;
Brambilla:2019esw
;
Wang:2019veq
;
Ali:2019okl
;
TarrusCastella:2021pld
;
Li:2021jjt
;
Bai:2022cfz
;
Li:2022leg
;
Liu:2023rhh
.

In this paper, we report on a search for the processes
e^{+}e^{-}\to\eta_{b}(1S)\omega

and
e^{+}e^{-}\to\chi_{b0}(1P)\omega
at a center-of-mass (c.m.) energy of

10.745\,\mathrm{GeV}
, which is close to the peak of the
\Upsilon(10753)

state. We use Belle II data corresponding to an integrated luminosity
of
9.8\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1}

Belle-II:2019usr
collected during a special run
of the SuperKEKB collider at energies above the
\Upsilon(4S)
resonance.

The
\eta_{b}(1S)
and
\chi_{b0}(1P)
mesons do not have exclusive decay channels with
a large product of efficiency and branching fraction. Thus, we
reconstruct only an
\omega
meson in the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
decay and use recoil
mass,

M_{\mathrm{recoil}}(\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})=\sqrt{\left(\frac{\sqrt{s}-E_{\omega}}{c^{2}}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{p_{\omega}}{c}\right)^{2}},

(1)

as the signal-extraction variable, where
E_{\omega}
and
p_{\omega}

are the energy and momentum of the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
combination in the
c.m. frame.

In a previous study
Belle-II:2022xdi
, we searched for the
process
e^{+}e^{-}\to\chi_{b0}(1P)\omega
fully reconstructing the
\chi_{b0}(1P)\to\Upsilon(1S)\gamma

decay and found no significant signal. The probability of the decay

\chi_{b0}(1P)\to\Upsilon(1S)\gamma
is small, thus, the sensitivity of partial
reconstruction, applied in this analysis, might be higher than that of
full reconstruction.

We follow a blind approach, i.e., the analysis procedure is
established before examining the signal-variable distribution in data.

II
Belle II detector and simulation

The analysis is based on a data sample collected with the Belle II
detector
Belle-II:2010dht
at the asymmetric-energy
e^{+}e^{-}

SuperKEKB collider
Akai:2018mbz
.

The detector has a cylindrical geometry with
z
axis approximately
coincident with the electron beam direction, which defines the forward
direction. Belle II includes a two-layer silicon-pixel detector (PXD)
surrounded by a four-layer double-sided silicon-strip detector and a
56-layer central drift chamber (CDC). These detectors reconstruct
tracks (trajectories of charged particles). Only one sixth of the
second layer of the PXD was installed for the data analyzed here.
Surrounding the CDC is a time-of-propagation counter (TOP) in the
central region, and an aerogel-based ring-imaging Cherenkov counter
(ARICH) in the forward region. These detectors provide
charged-particle identification. Surrounding the TOP and ARICH is an
electromagnetic calorimeter (ECL) made with CsI(Tl) crystals that
provides energy and timing measurements for photons and
electrons.
These sub-systems are surrounded by a superconducting solenoid,
providing an axial magnetic field of 1.5 T. An iron flux return
located outside the coil is instrumented with resistive plate chambers
and plastic scintillators to detect
K^{0}_{L}
mesons and to identify
muons (KLM).

We generate signal events using EvtGen
Lange:2001uf
. Events are
generated with a uniform distribution over angular variables and then
are weighted according to the theoretical expectations described in
Appendix
A
.
Initial-state radiation (ISR) is simulated using the PHOKHARA
generator
Rodrigo:2001kf
where processes at next-to-leading
order in the electromagnetic coupling are taken into account. We
assume that the signal processes proceed via the
\Upsilon(10753)
state
with parameters from Ref.
Belle:2019cbt
and weight events
accordingly.
The GEANT4 package is used to simulate the passage of the particles
inside the detector and its response
GEANT4:2002zbu
. All the
data and simulated events are reconstructed and analyzed using the
Belle II analysis software
Kuhr:2018lps
;
basf2-zenodo
.

III
Event selection

We reconstruct the
\omega
meson using the
\omega\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
decay.
Online event selection is based on the number of charged particles and
observed energy in an event, and is fully efficient for signal.
For charged pions, we require the distance from the beam-spot to be
within 0.5 cm along the
z
axis and 0.3 cm in the transverse plane.
Particle identification uses the
dE/dx
measurements in the CDC and
information from TOP, ARICH, ECL, and KLM. The corresponding
likelihoods are calculated for each particle hypothesis, and loose
selections are applied to separate charged pions from kaons, protons,
and electrons.

Candidate
\pi^{0}
mesons are reconstructed from pairs of photons,
which are energy deposits (clusters) in the ECL not matched to a track
in the CDC.
The photon energy is required to exceed
50\,\mathrm{MeV}
in the forward
endcap (
12.4^{\circ}<\theta<31.4^{\circ}
) and barrel
(
32.2^{\circ}<\theta<128.7^{\circ}
) regions of the ECL, and
75\,\mathrm{MeV}

in the backward endcap (
130.7^{\circ}<\theta<155.1^{\circ}
), because the
latter region has higher beam-induced background.
To suppress beam-induced background, we require the difference between
the cluster time and the collision time to satisfy

|\Delta{t}|<50\,\mathrm{ns}
. This requirement corresponds to
approximately two standard deviations in time resolution.
To suppress background from hadronic clusters, which are broader than
electromagnetic ones, we require the ratio of the energy deposit in a

3\times 3
matrix of crystals to that in the enclosing
5\times 5

matrix in which the four corner crystals are excluded to be greater
than 0.8.
The invariant mass of the selected photon pairs
M(\gamma\gamma)
is
required to satisfy
|M(\gamma\gamma)-m_{\pi^{0}}|<12\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
for the

\eta_{b}(1S)\omega
channel and
|M(\gamma\gamma)-m_{\pi^{0}}|<13\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
for the

\chi_{b0}(1P)\omega
channel, where
m_{\pi^{0}}
is the
\pi^{0}

mass
Workman:2022ynf
. These requirements correspond to
approximately two standard deviations in
\pi^{0}
mass resolution. To
suppress combinatorial background from low-energy photons, we require
the momentum of the
\pi^{0}
candidate in the c.m. frame to exceed

260\,\mathrm{MeV}/c
for the
\eta_{b}(1S)\omega
channel and
130\,\mathrm{MeV}/c
for the

\chi_{b0}(1P)\omega
channel. We perform a mass-constrained kinematic fit for
the
\pi^{0}
candidates to improve the
\pi^{0}
momentum resolution.

The
\omega
candidates are selected by combining
\pi^{+}
,
\pi^{-}
,
and
\pi^{0}
, and requiring the invariant mass to satisfy

|M(\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})-m_{\omega}|<13\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
, where
m_{\omega}
is the

\omega
mass
Workman:2022ynf
.
In the Dalitz plot (DP) of the
\omega\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
decay, the
density of the signal decreases towards the boundaries, while the
combinatorial background is concentrated near the boundaries. To
suppress the combinatorial background, we use a normalized distance

r
to the center of the symmetrized DP
Belle:2015fvz
in our
candidate selection criteria. The variable
r
takes the value
r=0

at the DP center and
r=1
at its boundary. We require
r<0.84
for
the
\eta_{b}(1S)
and
r<0.82
for the
\chi_{b0}(1P)
.

To suppress background from continuum events
e^{+}e^{-}\to q\bar{q}

(
q=u,d,s,c
), which have a jet-like shape, we use the ratio
R_{2}
of
the second to the zeroth order Fox Wolfram
moments
Fox:1978vu
. For
b\bar{b}
events, this variable peaks
at approximately 0.1, while for continuum events it is distributed
almost uniformly between 0.0 and 1.0. The selection criteria are

R_{2}<0.21
for the
\eta_{b}(1S)
and
R_{2}<0.28
for the
\chi_{b0}(1P)
.

The above selection criteria are obtained by maximizing the figure of
merit (FoM), defined as
\epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}}/\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{bkg}}}
,
where
\epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}}
is the signal efficiency determined
using simulation, and
N_{\mathrm{bkg}}
is the background yield
estimated using data. This definition of the FoM takes into account
the small signal-to-background ratio for partial reconstruction. To
find the global maximum of the FoM, we scan each selection variable in
an iterative way.

The
M_{\mathrm{recoil}}(\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})
fit intervals are
(9200,9600)\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
and

(9780,9950)\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
for the
\eta_{b}(1S)
and
\chi_{b0}(1P)
candidates,
respectively. For reference, the
\eta_{b}(1S)
mass is

(9398.7\pm 2.0)\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
, and the
\chi_{bJ}(1P)

(J=0,1,2)
masses
are
(9859.4\pm 0.5)\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
,
(9892.8\pm 0.4)\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
, and

(9912.2\pm 0.4)\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
, respectively
Workman:2022ynf
. The

M_{\mathrm{recoil}}(\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})
resolutions are
15.0\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
for the
\eta_{b}(1S)
and
8.4\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}

for the
\chi_{b0}(1P)
.

There are on average 1.6 and 4.0 candidates per event for the

e^{+}e^{-}\to\eta_{b}(1S)\omega
and
e^{+}e^{-}\to\chi_{b0}(1P)\omega
processes, respectively. We
verify that the additional candidates do not peak in the
M_{\mathrm{recoil}}(\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})

signal regions, and therefore, we retain all candidates. The
reconstruction efficiencies are 7.6% and 7.8% for the
\eta_{b}(1S)\omega
and

\chi_{b0}(1P)\omega
channels, respectively. These include corrections for the
discrepancies between data and simulation, which are discussed along
with the systematic uncertainties in Section
V

M(\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})
distributions for the

We perform a least-squares fit to these distributions, in which the

\eta_{b}(1S)\omega

The parameters of the signal fit function are determined from

and
1.07\pm 0.03
for the
\eta_{b}(1S)

(0.17\pm 0.18)\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
and

for the
\chi_{b0}(1P)

The purity of the
\omega

and 5.3% for the
\eta_{b}(1S)\omega
and
\chi_{b0}(1P)\omega

WorkingGrouponRadiativeCorrections:2010bjp

\mathcal{B}(\omega\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})

\mathcal{B}(\pi^{0}\to\gamma\gamma)
branching fractions,

(1+\delta_{\mathrm{ISR}})=0.63

\Upsilon(10753)
.

VI
Conclusions

We report a search for the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\eta_{b}(1S)\omega
and

e^{+}e^{-}\to\chi_{b}(1S)\omega
processes at
\sqrt{s}=10.745\,\mathrm{GeV}
. No
significant signals are observed, and we set the following 90% CL
upper limits on Born-level cross sections:

\begin{split}\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}(e^{+}e^{-}\to\eta_{b}(1S)\omega)&<2.5\,\mathrm{pb},\\
\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}(e^{+}e^{-}\to\chi_{b0}(1P)\omega)&<8.7\,\mathrm{pb}.\\
\end{split}

(4)

The upper limit on the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\chi_{b0}(1P)\omega
cross section is comparable to
the upper limit obtained using full reconstruction of
11.3 pb
Belle-II:2022xdi
. We combine the two results, taking
into account correlations, to obtain

\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}(e^{+}e^{-}\to\chi_{b0}(1P)\omega)<7.8\,\mathrm{pb}.

(5)

The tetraquark model of Ref.
Wang:2019veq
predicts that the
decay rate of
\Upsilon(10753)\to\eta_{b}(1S)\omega
is strongly enhanced
compared to the decay rates of
\Upsilon(10753)\to\Upsilon(nS)\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
. The obtained
upper limit on
\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}(\eta_{b}(1S)\omega)
is close to the measured
values of
\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}(\Upsilon(nS)\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
, which are in the range

(1-3)\,\mathrm{pb}

Belle:2019cbt
. Thus, our results do not
support the tetraquark-model prediction that the
\Upsilon(10753)\to\eta_{b}(1S)\omega

decay is enhanced
Wang:2019veq
.
In the
4S-3D
mixing model, the decay rate of

\Upsilon(10753)\to\eta_{b}(1S)\omega
is smaller than the decay rate of

\Upsilon(10753)\to\Upsilon(nS)\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
by a factor
0.2-0.4

Liu:2023rhh
;
our upper limit is consistent with this expectation.

The upper limit on the
\chi_{b0}(1P)\omega
cross section is higher than the
measured
\chi_{b1}(1P)\omega
and
\chi_{b2}(1P)\omega
cross
sections of
(3.6\pm 0.9)\,\textrm{pb}
and
(2.8\pm 1.3)\,\textrm{pb}
,
respectively
Belle-II:2022xdi
.
For a
4S-3D
mixed state, the decay rate to
\chi_{b0}(1P)\omega
is expected to
be comparable to the decay rates to
\chi_{b1}(1P)\omega
and

\chi_{b2}(1P)\omega

Li:2021jjt
; our upper limit is consistent with this
expectation.
In the charmonium sector, the decay of the
Y(4230)
state to

\chi_{c0}\omega
is enhanced compared to the decays to

\chi_{c1}\omega
and
\chi_{c2}\omega

BESIII:2019gjc
. We do
not find an analogous enhancement in the decay pattern of
\Upsilon(10753)
,
which may indicate that
Y(4230)
and
\Upsilon(10753)
have different
structures.

The backreaction effect of sound speed resonance in DBI inflation.
Bichu Li, Chao Chen, Bo Wang

Abstract
We examine the backreaction effect of the enhanced small-scale scalar perturbations from the sound speed resonance (SSR) mechanism for primordial black hole formation in Dirac-Born-Infeld (DBI) inflation Within the perturbative regime, the backreaction effect of perturbations on the background dynamics can be described by an effective action after integrating out the perturbation sector. Starting with the effective field theory of a specific DBI inflation model that incorporates SSR, we obtain the one-loop effective action by integrating out the scalar perturbations at the quadratic level. Using the effective Friedmann equations derived from this one-loop effective action, we solve the Hubble parameter with backreaction and the effective perturbation dynamics on this background as well. Our numerical findings reveal that, for a viable parameter space, the backreaction effect results in a relative correction to the Hubble parameter of approximately 10 − 7 10^{-7} , whereas the relative correction to the slow-roll parameter can vary between − 0.3 -0.3 and 0.1 0.1 , before gradually converging to 10 − 7 10^{-7} . Furthermore, our results show that the backreaction effect on SSR sound speed causes a slight reduction in the resonant peak of the curvature power spectrum, and the subsequent PBH formation predicted by the SSR mechanism remains almost unchanged.



I
Introduction

The conventional perturbative approach to the classical Einstein’s field equation relies on the balance of the perturbative orders between geometry and matter sectors, and implicitly assumes the negligible interference among different orders.
However, the intrinsic nonlinearity of Einstein’s gravity inevitably allows linear cosmological perturbations around a homogeneous and isotropic Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) Universe to affect on the background dynamics, and this backreaction effect of linear perturbations arise as second and higher orders in the perturbation expansion. It is well known that gravitational waves (GWs) could affect the dynamics of the background on which they are propagating, and thus one can define the effective energy-momentum tensor of GWs by expanding Einstein’s field equation up to the second order and taking a spatial average
Brill and Hartle 1964
;
Isaacson 1968a
;
Isaacson 1968b
;
Abramo et al. 1997
;
Giovannini 2020
. In addition, considerable attention has been given to the backreaction effect of scalar perturbations in relation to whether small-scale inhomogeneities have an impact on the global FLRW evolution,
which may give a new solution to cosmological coincidence problem, i.e., the acceleration epoch begins roughly at the same moment that non-linear structures form
Adamek et al. 2019
;
Schander and Thiemann 2021
. Besides the above-mentioned classical approach to the cosmological backreaction problem, semi-classical and quantum approaches are also investigated
Schander and Thiemann 2021
;
Hu and Verdaguer 2020
.

Intuitively, the backreaction effect is expected to be proportional to the amplitude of perturbations. In the context of primordial black hole (PBH) formation within the inflationary Universe, the small-scale curvature perturbations are required to be amplified around seven orders of magnitude larger than the large-scale perturbations constrained by cosmic microwave background (CMB) radiation experiments.
The amplification mechanism requires a deviation from the standard slow-roll inflation, at least for a period with a few e-folds that accounts for PBH formation
Sasaki et al. 2018

II
Realization of SSR in DBI inflation

In this section, we will review the realization of SSR within DBI inflation
Chen et al. 2020
.
In the original SSR mechanism
Cai et al. 2018
, the sound speed of inflaton is assumed to be time-oscillating during a few e-folds,

c_{s}^{2}(\tau)=1-2\xi\left[1-\cos(2k_{*}\tau)\right]~,~\tau>\tau_{s}~,

(1)

where
\tau
is the conformal time,
\xi
and
k_{*}
are the small amplitude and frequency of the sound speed oscillation, respectively.
\xi<1/4
is required such that
c_{s}^{2}
is positively defined, and the oscillation begins at
\tau_{s}
, where
k_{*}
is chosen to be deep inside the Hubble horizon with
|k_{*}\tau_{s}|\gg 1
. Consequently, the resonance of the curvature perturbations happens inside the Hubble horizon and leads to PBH formation during the later radiation-dominated epoch.

The DBI model contains a non-canonical kinetic term, which can be used to realize a non-trivial sound speed. The DBI action is written as

S_{\text{DBI}}=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{f(\phi)}\left(1-\sqrt{1-2f(\phi)X}\right)-V(\phi)\right]~,

(2)

where
X\equiv-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi
, and
f(\phi)
is the so-called warp factor. The DBI action (
2
) is inspired by string theory with the inflaton field regarded as the radial position of branes moving inside a warped throat
Born and Infeld 1934
;
Silverstein and Tong 2004
;
Alishahiha et al. 2004
, and it has rich cosmological phenomenology
Cai and Wang 2010
;
Cai et al. 2011
;
Cai and Xue 2009
;
Cai and Wang 2010
. The sound speed in the DBI model can be expressed as

c_{s}^{2}=1-f(\phi)\dot{\phi}^{2}~,

(3)

where the dot denotes the derivative with respect to the cosmic time.

To realize a time-oscillating sound speed in the form of Eq. (
1
), Ref.
Chen et al. 2020
introduces an oscillating term into the well-studied anti-de Sitter warp factor
f(\phi)=\lambda/\phi^{4}
with
\lambda
a constant,

f(\bar{\phi})={\lambda\over\bar{\phi}^{4}}\big[1-\Theta\left(\bar{\phi}-\bar{\phi}_{s}\right)C(\bar{\phi})\big]~,

(4)

where
\bar{\phi}(\tau)
is the homogeneous background of inflaton field
\phi
, and
\bar{\phi}_{s}\equiv\bar{\phi}(\tau_{s})
is evaluated at the beginning of sound speed oscillation. The oscillation function
C(\phi)
is defined as

C(\bar{\phi})\equiv\cos\left\{2k_{*}\tau_{s}\exp\left[{H(1-\epsilon)\sqrt{\lambda}\over\sqrt{2\xi}}\left({1\over\bar{\phi}}-{1\over\bar{\phi}_{s}}\right)\right]\right\}~.

(5)

where
H
is the Hubble parameter.
\Theta\left(\bar{\phi}-\bar{\phi}_{s}\right)
is the Heaviside step function, which divides the inflationary epoch into the non-oscillating (
c_{s}^{2}=1-2\xi
for
\tau\leq\tau_{s}
) and oscillating (i.e., Eq. (
1
)) phases.
The functional form (
5
) is derived from the background evolution of inflaton
\bar{\phi}(\tau)
,

\bar{\phi}(\tau)\simeq\left({1\over\bar{\phi}_{i}}+{\sqrt{2\xi}\over H(1-\epsilon)\sqrt{\lambda}}\ln\frac{\tau}{\tau_{i}}\right)^{-1}~,

(6)

in a quasi de-Sitter spacetime
a(\tau)\simeq 1/(\epsilon-1)H\tau
where
\epsilon\equiv-\dot{H}/H^{2}
is the slow-roll parameter.
\bar{\phi}_{i}
is the value taken at the beginning moment of inflation
\tau_{i}
. Although this solution is solved by non-oscillating sound speed, it is considered applicable to both oscillating and non-oscillating phases
Chen et al. 2020
.
The theoretical viability of this DBI model to realize SSR is also investigated in Ref.
Chen et al. 2020
.

III
Backreaction in SSR-DBI inflation

III.1
Backreaction effect on the background

In order to obtain an effective background Lagrangian with backreaction, a partition functional consisting of a DBI inflaton and an external source
J(x)
is introduced as

Z[J]=\int\mathcal{D}\phi\exp\left[iS_{\text{DBI}}+i\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g(x)}J(x)\phi(x)\right]~,

(7)

where
\int\mathcal{D}[\phi]
is the functional integral over all possible inflaton field configurations. Using the semiclassical approach within the path integral formalism
Burgess 2020
, one can perturbatively expand
Z[J]
in Eq. (
7
) with Eq. (
2
) around the classical background
\bar{\phi}
, up to the quadratic order in terms of the field perturbation
\phi_{\rm per}
(i.e.,
\phi=\bar{\phi}+\phi_{\rm per}
. The first-order term of
Z[J]
vanishes due to the classical equation of motion for
\phi_{\rm per}
).
Within the framework of the effective field theory (EFT) of inflation
Cheung et al. 2008
, the inflation field perturbation
\phi_{\rm per}
is “eaten” by graviton (namely the unitary gauge
\phi_{\rm per}=0
), one can write down the most general inflation action merely based on metric which respects the unbroken spatial diffeomorphism. After using the Stückelberg trick, the time diffeomorphism can be restored and the quadratic action for the scalar degree of freedom of gravity is given by
Cheung et al. 2008

S^{(2)}=\int\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}^{3}xa^{3}(t)\left[\alpha(t)\dot{\pi}^{2}-\beta(t){(\nabla\pi)^{2}\over a^{2}(t)}\right]~,

(8)

where the dot refers the derivative with respect to the cosmic time.
\pi
corresponds to the broken time translation symmetry.
The explicit forms of the coefficients
\alpha(t),\beta(t)
in the above action can be obtained by matching the SSR-DBI model with EFT action (see Appendix
A
for details).
After integrating out the quadratic action (
8
) in the partition functional (
7
), one can obtain the effective Lagrangian for background evolution as,

\mathcal{L}_{\text{tot}}\equiv\mathcal{L}_{\text{bg}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{br}}~,

(9)

which describes the backreaction effect and is calculated as follows (see Appendix
B
for details),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{bg}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}R_{\text{tot}}+f(\bar{\phi}_{\text{tot}})^{-1}\left(1-\sqrt{1-f(\bar{\phi}_{\text{tot}})\dot{\bar{\phi}}_{\text{tot}}^{2}}\right)-V(\bar{\phi}_{\text{tot}})~,

(10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{br}}

\displaystyle={c_{s,\mathrm{bg}}^{-3}\over 128\pi^{2}}\Bigg[\Lambda_{k}^{2}\left(\Lambda_{k}^{2}-2\mathcal{M}^{2}\right)+2\mathcal{M}^{4}\ln\left(1+{\Lambda_{k}^{2}\over\mathcal{M}^{2}}\right)-2\Lambda_{k}^{4}\ln\left({\Lambda_{k}^{2}+\mathcal{M}^{2}\over\Lambda_{M}^{2}}\right)\Bigg]~,

(11)

where we have used the subscript “tot/bg” to represent the background with/without backreaction, and the subscript “br” refers to the backreaction term arising from the integrated perturbation sector.
For example, the total background inflaton field is written as
\bar{\phi}_{\rm tot}=\bar{\phi}_{\rm bg}+\bar{\phi}_{\rm br}
, where
\bar{\phi}_{\rm bg}
follows the evolution (
6
).
In Eq. (
11
),
\Lambda_{k}\equiv c_{s,\mathrm{bg}}^{-1}H_{\text{tot}}
is a physical cutoff at the sound horizon which is introduced to prevent the divergence of
Z[J]
as Appendix
B
shows, and
H_{\text{tot}}\equiv H_{\text{bg}}+H_{\text{br}}
.

\mathcal{M}^{2}\equiv\frac{1}{4}\left(3-2s\right)^{2}H_{\text{tot}}^{2}-H_{\text{tot}}\dot{s}
acts as an effective mass implicated by Eq. (
44
), and
s\equiv\dot{c}_{s,\mathrm{bg}}/(H_{\text{tot}}c_{s,\mathrm{bg}})
is a dimensionless parameter representing the rate of change of the sound speed during inflation in presence of the backreaction.

\Lambda_{M}^{2}
is a parameter with the unit of the mass square
Gong and Mylova 2022
, whose value is taken such that
\mathcal{L}_{\text{br}}
vanishes at the initial time of inflation
\tau_{i}
when SSR and its backreaction have not occurred. Here we assume the backreaction correction on background is homogeneous
Mukhanov et al. 1997
, thus above parameters do not depend on the spatial coordinate.

From the total Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{\text{tot}}
in Eq. (
9
), we obtain the effective Friedman equations as follows,

\displaystyle\dot{H}_{\text{bg}}=

\displaystyle-{1\over 2M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}c_{s,\mathrm{bg}}^{-1}\dot{\bar{\phi}}_{\text{bg}}^{2}~,

(12)

\displaystyle\dot{H}_{\text{br}}=

\displaystyle{1\over 2M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{\text{br}}}{\delta\dot{\bar{\phi}}_{\text{bg}}}\dot{\bar{\phi}}_{\text{bg}}~,

(13)

where
\delta/\delta\dot{\bar{\phi}}_{\text{bg}}
refers to the variation with respective to
\dot{\bar{\phi}}_{\text{bg}}
.

In principle, one can integrate the above equations to give the Hubble parameter with/without backreaction. However, since it involves the complicated integration of Eqs. (
5
) and (
6
), it is hard to investigate the effect of backreaction analytically. In the remaining part of this paper, we shall adopt the numerical method and analyze the modification of the SSR-DBI inflation background by the backreaction effect.
As an illustration, we shall show the evolution of the Hubble parameter, slow-roll parameter, sound speed and power spectrum of curvature perturbations with and without backreaction for comparison.

For simplicity, we use
\bar{\phi}_{\rm bg}
instead of
\bar{\phi}_{\rm tot}
as the temporal parameter, using the relation
\dot{\bar{\phi}}_{\rm bg}=\sqrt{\frac{2\xi}{\lambda}}\bar{\phi}_{\rm bg}^{2}
indicated by Eq. (
6
). According to Eqs. (
12

13
), we obtain the approximation,

\frac{\mathrm{d}H_{\text{tot}}}{\mathrm{d}\bar{\phi}_{\text{bg}}}\simeq\frac{\mathrm{d}H_{\text{bg}}}{\mathrm{d}\bar{\phi}_{\text{bg}}}+\frac{1}{2M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}}{\delta\mathcal{L}_{\text{br}}\over\delta\dot{\bar{\phi}}_{\text{bg}}}~.

(14)

On the right-hand side, the background term is given by
Chen et al. 2020

{\mathrm{d}H_{\text{bg}}\over\mathrm{d}\bar{\phi}_{\rm bg}}=-\sqrt{{\xi\over 2\lambda\left[1-2\xi\left(1-\Theta(\bar{\phi}_{\rm bg}-\bar{\phi}_{s})C(\bar{\phi}_{\rm bg})\right)\right]}}{\bar{\phi}_{\rm bg}^{2}\over M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}~,

(15)

while the backreaction term is calculated as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\text{br}}}{\partial\dot{\bar{\phi}}_{\mathrm{bg}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{s,\text{bg}}^{-3}}{256\pi^{2}M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}\dot{\bar{\phi}}_{\mathrm{bg}}}\left[-4\Lambda_{k}^{2}+4\mathcal{M}^{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda_{k}^{2}}{\mathcal{M}^{2}}\right)\right]\left(8s^{2}H_{\text{tot}}^{2}-6sH_{\text{tot}}^{2}-2H_{\text{tot}}\dot{s}\right)c_{s,\text{bg}}^{-2}

(16)

\displaystyle+\frac{3\left(c_{s,\text{bg}}^{-2}-1\right)c_{s,\text{bg}}^{-3}}{256\pi^{2}M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}\dot{\bar{\phi}}_{\rm bg}}\left[\Lambda_{k}^{2}\left(\Lambda_{k}^{2}-2\mathcal{M}^{2}\right)+2\mathcal{M}^{4}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda_{k}^{2}}{\mathcal{M}^{2}}\right)-2\Lambda_{k}^{4}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda_{k}^{2}+\mathcal{M}^{2}}{\Lambda_{M}^{2}}\right)\right]~,

where
c_{s,\text{bg}}^{2}=1-f(\bar{\phi}_{\rm bg})\dot{\bar{\phi}}_{\text{bg}}^{2}
based on Eq. (
3

\bar{\phi}_{\text{tot}}(\tau)

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}_{\text{tot}}(\tau)\equiv

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\bar{\phi}_{i}}+\frac{\sqrt{2\xi}}{H_{\text{tot}}(1-\epsilon_{\text{tot}})\sqrt{\lambda}}\ln\frac{\tau}{\tau_{i}}\right)^{-1}~,

(24)

The derivative of

IV
Conclusions

In this paper, we investigate the backreaction effect of the enhanced small-scale curvature perturbations in the SSR-DBI inflation.
We find that the backreaction effects on both the background and perturbations can be neglected and do not spoil the previous results in the SSR-DBI inflation model in the absence of backreaction.

As a starting point, we review the SSR-DBI inflation model
Chen et al. 2020
and adopt the methods presented in Ref.
Gong and Mylova 2022
to calculate the backreaction effects of perturbations on the background.
After integrating out the quadratic curvature perturbation action, we obtain the effective Lagrangian (
11
) which describes the backreaction effect on the inflationary background dynamics.

Based on this effective Lagrangian, we numerically solve the evolution of the Hubble parameter
H
, the slow-roll parameter
\epsilon
, and the sound speed
c_{s}^{2}
with backreaction.
We find that the backreaction effect causes a tiny correction to the Hubble parameter in an order of
10^{-7}
compared to the original results in Ref.
Chen et al. 2020
, as shown in Fig.
1
.
For the slow-roll parameter, as displayed in Fig.
2
, the correction resulting from the backreaction effect exhibits an oscillation that begins with an amplitude between
-0.3
and
0.1
, but gradually decreases to a value as small as
-2\Delta_{H}\sim 10^{-7}
.
Additionally, the results presented in Fig.
4
tell us that the sound speed is slightly altered by curvature perturbations.
Applying the sound speed with/without backreaction to the Mukhanov-Sasaki equation (
28
), we numerically solve the curvature perturbation
\zeta
and plot the corresponding power spectra
\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}
in Fig.
5
.
We conclude that the backreaction effect on the amplification efficiency of curvature perturbations and the PBH formation predicted by the SSR mechanism is fairly small.

Appendix A
EFT description of SSR-DBI inflation

In this appendix, we shall give an EFT description for SSR-DBI inflation. EFT provides a generic description of metric perturbations around a quasi-de Sitter background
Cheung et al. 2008
. Even without the knowledge of the full UV theory, EFT can remove the unnecessary high-energy degrees of freedom, while keeping track of their influence on the low-energy physics
Weinberg 1979
;
Polchinski 1984
;
Polchinski 1992
;
Georgi 1993
;
Weinberg 2021
;
Burgess 2020
;
Green 2022
.
As the inflation vacuum is time-evolving, the general EFT action for inflation can be built by operators with broken-time translation symmetry
Cheung et al. 2008
;
Weinberg 2008
.
For the single field inflation model
\phi(t,\vec{x})=\phi_{0}(t)+\delta\phi(t,\vec{x})
, one can work in the unitary gauge
\delta\phi(t,\vec{x})=0

Cheung et al. 2008
.
Besides the Riemann tensor
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
that is already invariant under diffeomorphism transformation, operators satisfying this reduced symmetry in unitary gauge include tensors with upper index 0:
g^{00}
and
R^{00}
, extrinsic curvature tensor
K_{\mu\nu}\equiv h_{\mu}{}^{\sigma}\nabla_{\sigma}n_{\nu}
(where
n_{\mu}=\frac{\partial_{\mu}t}{\sqrt{-g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}t\partial_{\nu}t}}
and
h_{\mu\nu}\equiv g_{\mu\nu}+n_{\mu}n_{\nu}
are the normal vector and the induced metric of the hypersurface, respectively), arbitrary time-dependent function
f(t)
and the covariant derivatives of the aforementioned operators. As
R^{00}
can be written in terms of
g^{00}
and
h^{\mu\nu}
, while
n^{\mu}
can be written in terms of
g^{00}
and
K_{\mu\nu}
, the most generic action is given by the following form
Cheung et al. 2008
,

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}F\left(R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},g^{00},K_{\mu\nu},\nabla_{\mu},t\right)~,

(29)

which can be expanded perturbatively as

\displaystyle S_{\text{EFT}}=

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}^{3}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}}{2}R-c(t)g^{00}-\Lambda(t)+{M_{2}(t)^{4}\over 2!}(g^{00}+1)^{2}+{M_{3}(t)^{4}\over 3!}(g^{00}+1)^{3}+\cdots\right.

(30)

\displaystyle-{\bar{M}_{1}(t)^{3}\over 2}(g^{00}+1)\delta K_{\mu}{}^{\mu}-{\bar{M}_{2}(t)^{2}\over 2}(\delta K_{\mu}{}^{\mu})^{2}-{\bar{M}_{3}(t)^{2}\over 2}\delta K_{\nu}{}^{\mu}\delta K_{\mu}{}^{\nu}+\cdots

\displaystyle\left.+m_{1}^{2}(t)h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}g^{00}\partial_{\nu}g^{00}+\lambda_{1}(t)\delta R^{2}+\lambda_{2}(t)\delta R_{\mu\nu}\delta R^{\mu\nu}+\mu_{1}^{2}(t)\left(1+g^{00}\right)\delta R+\cdots\right]~,

where
M_{\text{Pl}}
is the reduced Planck mass,
R
is the Ricci scalar,
c(t)
,
\Lambda(t)
,
M_{2,3}(t)
,
\bar{M}_{1,2,3}(t)
,
\lambda_{1,2}(t)
,
\mu_{1}(t)
,
m_{1}(t)
are arbitrary time-dependent functions. The broken time diffeomorphism is restored by performing an infinitesimal transformation
t\rightarrow t+\pi(x)
, which will induce transformations for time-dependent functions in the action as

f(t)\rightarrow f\left(t+\pi\left(t,x^{i}\right)\right)=f(t)+\dot{f}(t)\pi\left(t,x^{i}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\ddot{f}(t)\pi\left(t,x^{i}\right)^{2}+\ldots~,

(31)

and requiring
\pi
transform as
\pi(x)\rightarrow\widetilde{\pi}(\widetilde{x}(x))=\pi(x)-\xi^{0}(x)
under the time translation
t\rightarrow\tilde{t}=t+\xi^{0}(x)
.
This procedure is called the Stükelberg trick. The
\pi
field that arises from the broken time diffeomorphism is the Goldstone boson, and is related to the curvature perturbation via relation
\zeta=-H\pi

Cheung et al. 2008
. By invoking Arnowitt-Deser-Misner decomposition
ds^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=-N^{2}dt^{2}+h_{ij}\left(dx^{i}+N^{i}dt\right)\left(dx^{j}+N^{j}dt\right)
and solving the constraints for Lagrangian multipliers: the lapse function
N
and the shift functions
N^{i}
, one obtains a quadratic action describing the dynamics of
\pi

Cheung et al. 2008
;
Gleyzes et al. 2013
,

S_{\pi}^{(2)}=\int\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}^{3}xa^{3}(t)\left[\alpha(t)\dot{\pi}^{2}-\beta(t){(\nabla\pi)^{2}\over a^{2}(t)}\right]~,

(32)

where the coefficients are defined as

\displaystyle\alpha(t)

\displaystyle=c(t)+2M_{2}(t)^{4}+\frac{3}{4}{\bar{M}_{1}(t)^{6}\over M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}}~,

(33)

\displaystyle\beta(t)

\displaystyle=c(t)-\frac{1}{4}{\bar{M}_{1}(t)^{6}\over M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\dot{M}_{1}(t)^{3}+H\bar{M}_{1}(t)^{3}\right)~.

(34)

We refer readers to
Cabass et al. 2023
for a recent review on EFT in cosmology.
The sound speed
c_{s}
can be read directly from the action (
32
),

c_{s}^{2}(t)={\beta(t)\over\alpha(t)}~.

(35)

By matching with the DBI action, one can directly obtain the EFT coefficients as

\displaystyle c(t)

\displaystyle=\Lambda(t)=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\bar{\phi}}^{2}\left(1-f(\bar{\phi})\dot{\bar{\phi}}^{2}\right)^{-1/2}~,

(36)

\displaystyle M_{n}(t)^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}f(\bar{\phi})\dot{\bar{\phi}}^{4}\left(1-f(\bar{\phi})\dot{\bar{\phi}}^{2}\right)^{-3/2}~,

\displaystyle\bar{M}_{1}(t)^{3}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1,2}(t)=\mu_{1}^{2}(t)=m_{1}^{2}(t)=0~,

\displaystyle\bar{M}_{2}(t)^{2}

\displaystyle=-\bar{M}_{3}(t)^{2}=-M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}~,

and coefficients
\alpha(t)
and
\beta(t)
in Eq. (
32
) become

\displaystyle\beta_{\text{DBI}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\bar{\phi}}^{2}\left(1-f(\bar{\phi})\dot{\bar{\phi}}^{2}\right)^{-1/2}~,

(37)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\text{DBI}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\bar{\phi}}^{2}\left(1-f(\bar{\phi})\dot{\bar{\phi}}^{2}\right)^{-3/2}~.

with the above coefficients, Eq. (
35
) also gives the sound speed in DBI inflation
c_{s}^{2}=1-f(\bar{\phi})\dot{\bar{\phi}}^{2}
.
Notice that the above EFT description works when
H
is below the energy scale

E^{4}\sim 16\pi^{2}M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}|\dot{H}|\frac{c_{s}^{5}}{1-c_{s}^{2}}\sim 8\pi^{2}H^{2}M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}\frac{\epsilon}{\xi}~,

(38)

namely, the non-renormalizable interactions become insignificant
Cheung et al. 2008
.
In Eq. (
38
), an approximation of time-averaged sound speed
c_{s}^{2}=1-2\xi
is adopted. For typical parameter choices in the SSR-DBI inflation,
H/M_{\rm Pl}\sim 10^{-5}
and
\epsilon=0.01
, the condition (
38
) is satisfied.

Appendix B
One-Loop effective action

Here we derive the Lagrangian with backreaction effect from the one-loop effective action in the SSR-DBI model following the method presented by Ref.
Gong and Mylova 2022
, in which the backreaction is obtained by integrating out the high-order perturbations.
To get this effective action, we start from the partition function
Z[J]
in Eq. (
7
) with the perturbed inflation field as
\phi=\bar{\phi}+\phi_{\rm per}
, which can be perturbatively expanded around the classical background up to the second order as

Z[J]=\int\mathcal{D}\phi\exp\left\{i\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\mathcal{L}[\bar{\phi}]+J\bar{\phi}\right)+{1\over 2}\int\sqrt{-g}\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\mathrm{d}^{4}y{\delta^{2}\mathcal{L}[g,\phi]\over\delta\phi(x)\delta\phi(y)}\Big|_{\bar{\phi}}\phi_{\rm per}(x)\phi_{\rm per}(y)\right\}~.

(39)

The last term in the above formula represents the contribution from the perturbation
\phi_{\text{per}}
,
where the integral over
\phi_{\text{per}}
can be carried out by converting the quadratic term to a Gaussian integral
Peskin and Schroeder 1995
,

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}\eta\exp\left({i\over 2}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\mathrm{d}^{4}y\eta(x)\mathcal{A}(x,y)\eta(y)\right)=\text{const}\times\left(\det\left[-i\mathcal{A}(x,y)\right]\right)^{-1/2}~,

(40)

where
\mathcal{A}(x,y)
is an operator, and the constant can be dropped by the normalization of vacuum state
Peskin and Schroeder 1995
.
The effective action for
\bar{\phi}
is
Gong and Mylova 2022

\displaystyle\Gamma[\bar{\phi}]=

\displaystyle-i\ln Z[J]-\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}J\bar{\phi}~.

(41)

Substituting Eqs. (
39
) and (
40
) into the above, and using the identity
\ln\left[\det(-i\mathcal{A})\right]=\text{Tr}\left[\ln(-i\mathcal{A})\right]

Peskin and Schroeder 1995
, Eq. (
41
) becomes

\displaystyle\Gamma[\bar{\phi}]=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}[\bar{\phi}]-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\ln\left(-i\mathcal{A}\right)\right]\equiv\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\text{bg}}[\bar{\phi}]+\mathcal{L}_{\text{br}}[\bar{\phi}]\right)~,

(42)

for the SSR-DBI model, the background and the one-loop correction are

\mathcal{L}_{\text{bg}}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{DBI}}[\bar{\phi}]~,\quad\mathcal{L}_{\text{br}}=-\frac{i}{2}\int{\mathrm{d}^{4}k\over(2\pi)^{4}}\ln\left(-i\mathcal{A}\right)~,

(43)

where
k
denotes the physical momentum and we have taken the continuous limit of summation.
The explicit form of
\mathcal{A}
can be obtained from Eq. (
32

S^{(2)}=\int\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}^{3}x\eta(\tau,\mathbf{x})\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}}-c_{s}(\tau)^{2}{\nabla^{2}\over a^{2}(\tau)}+\mathcal{M}^{2}\right]\eta(\tau,\mathbf{x})~,

(44)

where
\eta\equiv\pi\left(a^{2}\alpha\right)^{-1/2}
is a normalized variable, the effective mass term is
\mathcal{M}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\left(3-2s\right)^{2}H_{\text{tot}}^{2}-H_{\text{tot}}\dot{s}
.
The operator
\mathcal{A}
can be read by comparing Eq. (
44
) with Eq. (
40
),

\mathcal{A}=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}}-c_{s}(\tau)^{2}{\nabla^{2}\over a^{2}(\tau)}+\mathcal{M}^{2}~,

(45)

Substituting Eq. (
45
) to Eq. (
43
), one gets

\mathcal{L}_{\text{br}}=-\frac{1}{2}c_{s}^{-3}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}\left[\int{\mathrm{d}^{4}\tilde{k}^{\prime}\over(2\pi)^{4}}{1\over\left(\tilde{k}^{\prime}{}^{2}+\mathcal{M}^{2}\right)^{\gamma}}\right]\right|_{\gamma=0}~,

(46)

where

\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}\tilde{k}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\ln\left(\tilde{k}^{2}+\mathcal{M}^{2}\right)=\left.-\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}\left[\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}\tilde{k}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{1}{\left(\tilde{k}^{2}+\mathcal{M}^{2}\right)^{\gamma}}\right]\right|_{\gamma=0}

Gong and Mylova 2022
.
To prevent the divergence of the above integration, we introduce a cutoff at the sound horizon

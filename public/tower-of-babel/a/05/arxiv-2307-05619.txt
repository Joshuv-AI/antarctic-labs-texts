The Riemannian curvature identities of a G 2 G_{2} connection with skew-symmetric torsion and generalized Ricci solitons


Abstract
Curvature properties of the characteristic connection on an integrable G 2 G_{2} manifold are investigated. We consider integrable G 2 G_{2} manifold of constant type, i.e. the scalar product of the exterior derivative of the G 2 G_{2} form with its Hodge dual is constant. We show that on an integrable G 2 G_{2} manifold of constant type with G 2 G_{2} -instanton characteristic curvature and vanishing Ricci tensor the torsion 3-form is harmonic. Consequently, we prove that the characteristic curvature is symmetric in exchange the first and the second pair and Ricci flat if and only if the three-form torsion is parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita and to the characteristic connection simultaneously and this is equivalent to the condition that the characteristic curvature satisfies the Riemannian first Bianchi identity. We find that the Hull connection is a G 2 G_{2} -instanton exactly when the torsion is closed. We observe that any compact integrable G 2 G_{2} manifold with closed torsion is a generalized gradient Ricci soliton and this is equivalent to a certain vector field to be parallel with respect to the characteristic connection. In particular, this vector field is an infinitesimal automorphism of the G 2 G_{2} structure. AMS MSC2010: 53C55, 53C21, 53D18, 53Z05

For a Gauduchon

Theorem 1.3

Let

If the characteristic Ricci tensor

A compact integrable

We show in Theorem

We remark that, in general, metric connections with skew-symmetric torsion

are closely connected with the generalized Ricci flow. Namely, the fixed points of the generalized Ricci flow are Ricci flat metric connections with harmonic torsion 3-form,

, we refer to the recent book

2.1
Metric connection with skew-symmetric torsion and its curvature

On a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of dimension

any metric connection
{\nabla}
with totally skew-symmetric torsion

is connected with the Levi-Civita connection
{\nabla}^{g}
of the metric

{\nabla}^{g}={\nabla}-\frac{1}{2}T\quad leading\quad to\quad{\nabla^{g}}T={\nabla}T+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{T},

(2.1)

where the 4-form

\sigma^{T}(X,Y,Z,V)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{j}\lrcorner T)\wedge(e_{j}\lrcorner T)(X,Y,Z,V),

where

The properties of the 4-form

The exterior derivative

\begin{split}dT(X,Y,Z,V)=d^{{\nabla}}T(X,Y,Z,V)+2\sigma^{T}(X,Y,Z,V),\quad where\\

(2.3)

For the curvature of
{\nabla}
we use the convention
R(X,Y)Z=[\nabla_{X},\nabla_{Y}]Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z
and
R(X,Y,Z,V)=g(R(X,Y)Z,V)
. It has the well known properties

R(X,Y,Z,V)=-R(Y,X,Z,V)=-R(X,Y,V,Z).

(2.4)

The first Bianchi identity for

\begin{split}R(X,Y,Z,V)+R(Y,Z,X,V)+R(Z,X,Y,V)\\
=dT(X,Y,Z,V)-\sigma^{T}(X,Y,Z,V)+(\nabla_{V}T)(X,Y,Z).\end{split}

(2.5)

It is proved in

that the curvature of a metric connection
{\nabla}
with totally skew-symmetric torsion

\begin{split}R(X,Y,Z,V)+R(Y,Z,X,V)+R(Z,X,Y,V)-R(V,X,Y,Z)-R(V,Y,Z,X)-R(V,Z,X,Y)\\
=\frac{3}{2}dT(X,Y,Z,V)-\sigma^{T}(X,Y,Z,V).\end{split}

(2.6)

One gets from (

\begin{split}R(V,X,Y,Z)+R(V,Y,Z,X)+R(V,Z,X,Y)=-\frac{1}{2}dT(X,Y,Z,V)+(\nabla_{V}T)(X,Y,Z)\end{split}

(2.7)

Definition 2.1

We say that the curvature

R(X,Y,Z,V)+R(Y,Z,X,V)+R(Z,X,Y,V)=0.

It is well known algebraic fact that (

(2.9)

Note that, in general, (

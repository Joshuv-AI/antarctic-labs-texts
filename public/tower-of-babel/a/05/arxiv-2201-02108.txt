Conditional mean values of long Dirichlet polynomials
Winston Heap

Abstract
. Conditionally on the Riemann hypothesis we prove asymptotic formulae for mean values of various long Dirichlet polynomials involving the von Mangoldt function. Our results avoid the use of correlation sum estimates although in addition to the Riemann hypothesis we must assume that our Dirichlet polynomials have weights from a specific class whose transforms are sufficiently concentrated near the origin. We also give large deviation estimates for these long Dirichlet polynomials.



1.
Introduction

Mean values of Dirichlet polynomials play a central role in analytic number theory. A fundamental formula, in a sharp form due to Montgomery–Vaughan
[
27
]
, states that for general coefficients
a(n)\in\mathbb{C}
,

\int_{T}^{2T}\Big|\sum_{n\leqslant X}a(n)n^{-it}\Big|^{2}dt=(T+O(X))\sum_{n\leqslant X}|a(n)|^{2}.

Thus, when
X=o(T)
we acquire an asymptotic formula which can be viewed as an approximate form of Parseval’s identity. However, when
X\gg T
the resultant upper bound may be far from the truth. Sums of length
X\gg T
are typically referred to as long Dirichlet polynomials and the computation of their mean values feature throughout analytic number theory. In order to understand these it is typically required to have a good knowledge of the correlation sums

\sum_{n\leqslant x}a(n)\overline{a(n+h)}

for a given
h
, which can be especially challenging if
a(n)
has some multiplicative structure.

Of particular interest to problems in prime number theory and the value distribution of the Riemann zeta function are the Dirichlet polynomials with coefficients given by the von Mangoldt function
\Lambda(n)
. Here, the above correlation sums are the subject of a specific case of the famous Hardy–Littlewood
k
-tuples conjecture stating that

(1)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h)\sim\mathfrak{G}(h)x

for an explicitly given constant
\mathfrak{G}(h)
. Aside from being a deep result in it’s own right, it can be shown
[
4
,
15
]
that in a strong form this would imply Montgomery’s strong pair correlation conjecture (formula (
6
) below) which would have further profound consequences for the distribution of primes and zeros of the Riemann zeta function
[
11
,
14
,
26
]
.
This seems currently out of reach, however there are some interesting results regarding (
1
) on average
[
9
,
24
,
25
]
.

With strong error terms, formula (
1
) and its generalisations to the
k
-fold convolution of the von Mangoldt function
\Lambda^{(k)}=\Lambda*\cdots*\Lambda
would also allow the computation of the moments

(2)

\int_{T}^{2T}\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant X}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{1/2+it}\log n}\bigg|^{2k}dt

beyond the “short” range of
X\leqslant T^{1/k}
. From the work of Selberg on his central limit theorem
[
32
,
37
]
we know that this polynomial provides a good approximation to
\log\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)
on average and plays a key role in its value distribution. This was further exemplified in the work of Soundararajan
[
33
]
who gave Gaussian bounds for the large deviations of
\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|
and as a result proved the near sharp bounds

\int_{T}^{2T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2k}dt\ll T(\log T)^{k^{2}+\epsilon}

which was later refined by Harper
[
16
]
. These last two papers, along with the work of Radziwiłł–Soundararajan
[
30
]
, expanded on this idea and introduced new techniques. A great deal of applications ensued including a new understanding of short interval maxima of the Riemann zeta function
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, value distribution of
L
-functions
[
5
,
7
,
21
,
29
]
and unconditional bounds for moments of zeta and
L
-functions
[
12
,
18
,
19
]
amongst others
[
8
,
22
,
23
]
. Thus, through these techniques it seems a great deal can be extracted from just short Dirichlet polynomials although one barrier to further progress is naturally the issue of longer sums.

In this paper we are interested in this problem and, in particular, computing the mean values (
2
) for
X
beyond the range of
X\leqslant T^{1/k}
. Our arguments do not make use of correlation sum estimates but instead assume the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) and crucially require the Dirichlet polynomial to be weighted. The weights can be taken from a reasonably wide class of functions, although they must have fairly specific properties and, in particular, have transforms that are sufficiently concentrated near the origin. The prototypical example we keep in mind is the pair

(3)

V_{X}(x)=\mathds{1}_{|x|\leqslant\log X}\Big(1-\tfrac{|x|}{\log X}\Big),\qquad\widehat{V}_{X}(z)=\frac{\sin^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}z\log X)}{(\tfrac{1}{2}z)^{2}\log X}

where here and throughout

\widehat{V}_{X}(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{ixz}V_{X}(x)dx

denotes the Fourier transform. Before stating our results we describe this class of weights.

We consider functions with the following properties:

(i)

V_{X}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded, even function with support in
[-\log X,\log X]
.

(ii)

There exists an
m\geqslant 2
such that for fixed
\Re(z)
we have

\widehat{V}_{X}(z/i)\ll\frac{X^{\Re(z)}}{|\Im(z)|^{m}}.

as
\Im(z)\to\infty
.

(iii)

For all
y\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\frac{1}{\log X}\widehat{V}(\tfrac{y}{\log X})\ll\frac{1}{1+y^{2}}.

The condition (
ii
) allows for absolutely convergent contour integrals, and is perhaps not so crucial, whereas condition (
iii
) means that these integrals are sufficiently concentrated near the origin and seems harder to dispense with. For long Dirichlet polynomials with weights from this class we prove the following.

Theorem 1
.

Assume RH and suppose
V_{X}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies conditions (
i
)–(
iii
). Then for
X\leqslant T^{2m}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and fixed
0<\theta<1/100
we have

\int_{T}^{2T}\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant X}\frac{\Lambda(n)V_{X}(\log n)}{n^{1/2+it}\log n}\bigg|^{2k}dt=k!\,T\bigg(\sum_{p\leqslant T^{\theta/k}}\frac{V_{X}(\log p)^{2}}{p}\bigg)^{k}+O(T(Ck)^{4k}(\log\log T)^{k-1/2})

for some positive constant
C
.
For
k\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
we have

\int_{T}^{2T}\bigg(\mathfrak{F}\sum_{n\leqslant X}\frac{\Lambda(n)V_{X}(\log n)}{n^{1/2+it}\log n}\bigg)^{2k}dt=c_{k}\,T\bigg(\sum_{p\leqslant T^{\theta/k}}\frac{V_{X}(\log p)^{2}}{p}\bigg)^{k}+O(T(C^{\prime}k)^{4k}(\log\log T)^{k-1/2})

for some positive constant
C^{\prime}
where
\mathfrak{F}
denotes either the real or imaginary part and

c_{k}=\begin{cases}\frac{(2k)!}{2^{2k}k!}&2k\text{ is even, }\\
0&2k\text{ is odd. }\end{cases}

Here, we see that the Gaussian behaviour persists for quite long sums and thus the well known heuristic that the
(p^{-it})_{p\text{ prime}}
act as independent random variables seems fairly robust in this context.
We also note that the main terms are in the form of purely diagonal contributions. Thus, if one computes these mean values in the usual way using correlation sum estimates such as (
1
), the above result shows that on RH the off-diagonal terms cannot contribute to the leading order in this case. Furthermore, it suggests that the error terms in formula (
1
) and its generalisations to
\Lambda^{(k)}
must exhibit some additional cancellation when averaged over
h
since their best-possible pointwise bound is likely squareroot cancellation and this would not be sufficient for sums of length
X^{k}\geqslant T^{2}
.

Our methods extend to other Dirichlet polynomials and, in particular, a weighted version of the Dirichlet polynomial associated to the logarithmic derivative
(\zeta^{\prime}/\zeta)(s)
:

\sum_{n\leqslant X}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}.

In this case we are restricted to the mean square for reasons we shall explain below. Contrary to the above case, our main term contains an off-diagonal type contribution involving a well-known function from Montgomery’s work on the pair correlation of zeta zeros
[
26
]
, namely,

(4)

F(u)=F(u,T)=\frac{2\pi}{T\log T}\sum_{0<\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\leqslant T}T^{-iu(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})}w(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})

where the sum is over the ordinates
\gamma
of the non-trivial zeros
\rho=1/2+i\gamma
of
\zeta(s)
and
w(u)=4/(4+u^{2})
. Our result, which we state for the specific weight in (
3
), is then as follows.

Theorem 2
.

Assume RH and suppose
X\leqslant T^{4}
. Then

\int_{T}^{2T}\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant X}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}\Big(1-\tfrac{\log n}{\log X}\Big)\bigg|^{2}dt=T\sum_{p\leqslant\min(T,\,X)}\frac{\log^{2}p}{p}\Big(1-\tfrac{\log p}{\log X}\Big)^{2}\\
+\mathds{1}_{X\geqslant t}\cdot{t\log^{2}t}\int_{1}^{\tfrac{\log X}{\log t}}F(u,t)\big(1-\tfrac{u\log t}{\log X}\big)^{2}du\,\bigg|_{t=T}^{2T}+o(T(\log T)^{2})

where
\mathds{1}
denotes the characteristic function.

Regarding the function
F(u)
, Montgomery
[
26
]
showed that

(5)

F(u)=u+o(1)+(1+o(1))T^{-2u}\log T

uniformly for
0\leqslant u\leqslant 1
as
T\to\infty
(see the comment after formula (4.6) in
[
13
]
). We also have
F(u)\geqslant 0
and
F(-u)=F(u)
(see
[
20
]
). Montgomery conjectured that for
u\geqslant 1
,

(6)

F(u)=1+o(1)

as
T\to\infty
, and this implies the famous pair correlation conjecture for the zeros of
\zeta(s)
. Note that for us, it would imply an asymptotic of the form
\sim cT(\log T)^{2}

in Theorem
2
.
However, Montgomery derived the conjecture in (
6
) by assuming (
1
) in a strong form and this would likely allow for a direct proof of an asymptotic in Theorem
2
anyway. In lieu of (
6
) we shall have use for the following type of result which states that on RH,

(7)

c_{1}<\int_{b}^{b+1}F(u)du<c_{2}

uniformly in
b
(possibly dependent on
T
) for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
. See Lemma A of
[
13
]
or the Lemma of
[
14
]
for example. From this we can at least acquire the order.

Corollary 1
.

Assume RH. Then for
T\leqslant X\leqslant T^{4}
we have

\int_{T}^{2T}\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant X}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}\Big(1-\tfrac{\log n}{\log X}\Big)\bigg|^{2}dt\asymp T(\log T)^{2}.

Returning to Theorem
1
, we note the dependency on
k
in the error terms is rather weak, and in fact, is too weak to get good large deviation bounds via Chebyshev’s inequality. However, using similar tools and ideas from the proof of Theorem
1
we can find more efficient methods to compute the large deviations of these Dirichlet polynomials. Owing to large negative values of
\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|
we can only prove Gaussian bounds for large
positive
values of the real part of our sum. The imaginary part is not limited in this way.

Theorem 3
.

Assume RH. Suppose that
\widehat{V}_{X}(y)
satisfies properties (
i
)–(
iii
) and that
\widehat{V}_{X}(y)
is positive.
Then for
X\leqslant T^{2m}
,
\epsilon>0
and
W\geqslant\sqrt{\log\log T}

\frac{1}{T}\mu\bigg(t\in[T,2T]:\bigg|\Im\sum_{n\leqslant X}\frac{\Lambda(n)V_{X}(\log n)}{n^{1/2+it}\log n}\bigg|\geqslant W\cdot V(0)\bigg)\\
\ll\exp(-(1-\epsilon)W^{2}/\log\log T)+\exp(-C_{\epsilon}W\log W)

and

\frac{1}{T}\mu\bigg(t\in[T,2T]:\Re\sum_{n\leqslant X}\frac{\Lambda(n)V_{X}(\log n)}{n^{1/2+it}\log n}\geqslant W\cdot V(0)\bigg)\\
\ll\exp(-(1-\epsilon)W^{2}/\log\log T)+\exp(-D_{\epsilon}W\log W)

for some constants
C_{\epsilon},D_{\epsilon}>0
where
\mu
denotes Lebesgue measure.

Remark
.

For the imaginary part it is not strictly necessary to have
\widehat{V}_{X}(y)
positive. If exception to this occurs then the same bound holds provided we replace
V(0)
on the left hand side with
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}|\widehat{V}_{X}(y)|dy
.

We note that these results show some similarity with Soundararajan’s
[
33
]
large deviation bounds for
\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|
and this is no coincidence since we make key use of his arguments. Note that the Gaussian term dominates for
W\ll\log_{2}T\log_{3}T
. Previously, results of this quality in such a range would only be accessible for
X\leqslant T^{1/(\log_{2}T)(\log_{3}T)^{2}}
. Also, the bound
\ll e^{-CW\log W}
holds in the full range of
W
with such results previously requiring
X\ll(\log T)^{\theta}
for some
\theta\leqslant 2
(see Lemma 3 of
[
17
]
for example).
It is possible that one could use these bounds to help compute the characteristic/moment generating functions of these polynomials for reasonably large
X
.

Turning to the proofs, we first note the following observation which provides a loose basis for our arguments. For large
t\in[T,2T]
the explicit formula roughly states that

\log\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\approx\sum_{p\leqslant X}\frac{1}{p^{1/2+it}}+\sum_{\rho}\int_{1/2}^{\infty}\frac{X^{\rho-\sigma-it}}{\rho-\sigma-it}d\sigma

for a given parameter
X\geqslant 2
. Although the sum over zeros can get large pointwise, on average it is of the order
\log T/\log X
since this is the expected number of zeros in the window
|\gamma-t|<1/\log X
and the exponential integral localises the sum to such
\gamma
. Morally, this fact is independent of whether
X\leqslant T
or
X>T
.
Thus, on comparing the explicit formula with two different parameters
X,Y
with
Y=T^{\epsilon}
, say, we can expect that on average

(8)

\sum_{p\leqslant X}\frac{1}{p^{1/2+it}}\approx\sum_{p\leqslant Y}\frac{1}{p^{1/2+it}}+O(1),

that is, our long Dirichlet polynomial can be replaced by a short one in the mean.

In practice our proofs run slightly differently. We avoid dealing directly with zeros by expressing our sum as a contour integral and shifting close to the half-line, but not past it. Truncating the integral at a low height and raising it to the
2k
th power we see that we need to compute the shifted
2k
th moment of
\log\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+z_{j})
for small shifts
z_{j}
. Here, we can express things in terms of short sums by approximating the logarithm in the mean with

\sum_{p\leqslant T^{1/k}}\frac{1}{p^{1/2+it+z_{j}}}+O(1).

Then once the shifted
2k
th moment has been computed it is a fairly simple matter to perform the
z_{j}
integrals and we acquire our asymptotic formula. The properties of our weights allow us to carry out this procedure without any real losses, which would not be the case for the usual Perron’s formula.

For the Dirichlet polynomial associated to the logarithmic derivative we can integrate by parts to get a similar contour integral involving
\log\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+z_{j})
, although we gain a factor of
\log X
in the process. This is the expected average order of magnitude which means we must be more precise in our shifted moment formulae. In particular, we are required to give asymptotics for the shifted second moment

\int_{T}^{2T}\log\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+z_{1})\log\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+z_{2})dt

down to the level
o(T)
. By using a further contour integration argument we can reduce this to the study of

\int_{T}^{2T}S(t+y_{1})S(t+y_{2})dt

where
S(t)=\tfrac{1}{\pi}\Im\log\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)
and
|y_{j}|\ll\log T
. For this we apply the methods of Goldston
[
13
]
which give lower order terms in the second moment of
S(t)
. Our precise result is stated in Proposition
9
below. The process of specialising to
S(t)
entails some extra mild conditions on the weights and so in an effort to keep the exposition simple we have restricted ourselves to (
3
), although Theorem
2
should hold for more general weights.

It would of course be interesting to remove the assumptions of the Riemann hypothesis and the weights in our results, although it seems difficult to give good mean bounds for

\log\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)-\sum_{p\leqslant X}\frac{V_{X}(\log p)}{p^{1/2+it}}

when
X\geqslant T
without such assumptions. Also, a relaxing of condition (
iii
) would be welcome since, as it stands, common weights such as smooth approximations to the characteristic function of an interval are excluded.

2.
Convolution formulae

Lemma 2
.

Assume RH and suppose
V_{X}
satisfies property (
ii
). Then for
1/2\leqslant\sigma\leqslant 1
and
t\geqslant 1
we have

(9)

\sum_{n\geqslant 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)V_{X}(\log n)}{n^{\sigma+it}\log n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\zeta(\sigma+i(y+t))\widehat{V}_{X}(y)dy+O\big(\tfrac{X^{1-\sigma}}{t^{m}}\big).

Proof.

This is essentially Lemma 5 of Tsang
[
36
]
. We shall give details for completeness. First note that by Fourier inversion

V_{X}(\log n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{V}_{X}(x)n^{-ix}dx=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\widehat{V}_{X}(\tfrac{z}{i})n^{-z}dz

for any
c>0
after shifting contours. Here and throughout
\int_{(c)}
denotes the integral
\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}
. Choosing
c=1-\sigma+1/\log X
and interchanging the order of sum and integral we get

\sum_{n\geqslant 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)V_{X}(\log n)}{n^{\sigma+it}\log n}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\log\zeta(\sigma+it+z)\widehat{V}_{X}(\tfrac{z}{i})dz.

Truncating the integral at height
\Im(z)=\pm Z
incurs an error of size
\ll X^{1-\sigma}\log_{2}X/Z^{m-1}
by property (
ii
) and the bound
\zeta(1+1/\log X+iy)\ll\log X

\forall y\in\mathbb{R}
. We then consider the resultant integral as part of the rectangular contour with vertices at
\pm iZ
,
1-\sigma+1/\log X\pm iZ
whose left edge has small semicircular indentations excluding the zeros at
z=i(\gamma-t)
if
\sigma=1/2
and which has a line from
1-\sigma-it
to
1-it
, a small circular contour enclosing the singularity at
z=1-it
, and then a line back to the left edge of the contour.

The upper and lowermost horizontal components of the contour contribute
\ll X^{1-\sigma}Z^{-m}

\log(Z+t)
since
\int_{\sigma}^{2}|\log\zeta(\alpha+iy)|d\alpha\ll\log y
for all large
y
and
\sigma\geqslant 1/2
(see formula (
11
) below for example). Thus we can let
Z\to\infty
.

By property (
ii
), the Hankel contour enclosing
z=1-it
contributes
\ll X^{1-\sigma}t^{-m}
. On the remaining left edge of the contour, if
\sigma=1/2
, we can let the radii of the semicircular indentations tend to zero (indeed, the singularities are logarithmic and hence integrable). The result then follows.
∎

Our next lemma truncates the integral in (
9
) to a suitable height when
X
is sufficiently large in terms of
t
.

Lemma 3
.

Assume RH and suppose
V
satisfies (
iii
). Then for
1/2\leqslant\sigma\leqslant 1
, large
t\in[T,2T]
and
X
satisfying
\log X\asymp\log T
we have

(10)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\zeta(\sigma+i(y+t))\widehat{V}_{X}(y)dy=\int_{-1}^{1}\log\zeta(\sigma+i(y+t))\widehat{V}_{X}(y)dy+O(1)

Proof.

By property (
iii
) of
\widehat{V}_{X}
we have

\int_{1}^{\infty}\log\zeta(\sigma+i(y+t))\widehat{V}_{X}(y)dy\ll\frac{1}{\log X}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\int_{n}^{n+1}|\log\zeta(\sigma+i(y+t))|dy.

Formula 9.6 (B) of
[
35
]
states that

(11)

\log\zeta(\sigma+iu)=\sum_{|\gamma-u|\leqslant 1}\log(\sigma-\tfrac{1}{2}+i(u-\gamma))+O(\log(2+|u|))

for
-1\leqslant\sigma\leqslant 2
and large
u
. The above integral is thus

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\int_{n}^{n+1}\sum_{|\gamma-(y+t)|\leqslant 1}|\log(\sigma-\tfrac{1}{2}+i(y+t-\gamma))|dy+\log(t+n)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma-(n+t)|\leqslant 2}\int_{-1}^{1}|\log(\sigma-\tfrac{1}{2}+iy)|dy+\log(t+n)\ll\log(t+n).

Therefore, our tail integral is
\ll 1
and of course the same bound holds for the other tail integral, thus giving the result.
∎

We combine Lemmas
2
and
3
into the following key proposition.

Proposition 4
.

Assume RH and suppose
V_{X}
satisfies (
ii
) and (
iii
). Then for large
t\in[T,2T]
and
T^{\epsilon}\leqslant X\leqslant T^{2m}
,

(12)

\sum_{n\geqslant 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)V_{X}(\log n)}{n^{\sigma+it}\log n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\log\zeta(\sigma+i(y+t))\widehat{V}_{X}(y)dy+O(1).

For the Dirichlet polynomial associated with the logarithmic derivative we have the following result.

Proposition 5
.

Assume RH. Let
t\in[T,2T]
and
T^{\epsilon}\leqslant X\leqslant T^{4}
. Then

\sum_{n\leqslant X}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}(1-\tfrac{\log n}{\log X})=\int_{-\log T/\log_{2}T}^{\log T/\log_{2}T}S(t+y)f_{X}(y)dy+O(1)

where

f_{X}(y)=\frac{X^{iy}(2-iy\log X)-2-iy\log X}{y^{3}\log X}

Proof.

By Mellin inversion we have

\sum_{n\leqslant X}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}(1-\tfrac{\log n}{\log X})=\frac{1}{2\pi i\log X}\int_{(c)}-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s+\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\frac{X^{s}}{s^{2}}ds

for any
c>1/2
. For brevity let
\log X=\mathcal{L}
. Shifting to the line with real part
1/\mathcal{L}
we encounter a pole at
s=1/2-it
and the above is

(13)

\frac{1}{2\pi i\mathcal{L}}\int_{(1/\mathcal{L})}-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s+\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\frac{X^{s}}{s^{2}}ds+O\Big(\frac{X^{1/2}}{t^{2}\mathcal{L}}\Big).

By Theorem 9.6(A) of
[
35
]
we have

-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(z)=\sum_{|\gamma-\Im(z)|\leqslant 1}\frac{1}{z-\tfrac{1}{2}-i\gamma}+O(\log(2+|\Im(z)|))

and consequently
(\zeta^{\prime}/\zeta)(s+\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\ll\mathcal{L}\cdot\log(|s|+t)
for
\Re(s)=1/\mathcal{L}
. Thus, the expression in (
13
) is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i\mathcal{L}}\int_{1/\mathcal{L}-iT/2}^{1/\mathcal{L}+iT/2}-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s+\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\frac{X^{s}}{s^{2}}ds+O\Big(\frac{(\log T)^{2}}{T}\Big)+O\Big(\frac{X^{1/2}}{t^{2}\mathcal{L}}\Big).

From Theorem 14.14 (B) of
[
35
]
we have

(14)

\log\zeta(\sigma+i\tau)\ll\frac{\log\tau}{\log_{2}\tau}\log\Big(\frac{2}{(\sigma-1/2)\log_{2}\tau}\Big),\qquad\tfrac{1}{2}<\sigma\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}+C/\log_{2}\tau

for large
\tau
.
Thus on integrating by parts the above integral is given by

(15)

\frac{1}{2\pi i\mathcal{L}}\int_{1/\mathcal{L}-iT/2}^{1/\mathcal{L}+iT/2}\log{\zeta}(s+\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\frac{d}{ds}\bigg[\frac{X^{s}}{s^{2}}\bigg]ds+O\Big(\frac{(\log T)^{2}}{T}\Big).

Now, formula (14.10.5) of
[
35
]
with
\alpha\to 1/2^{+}
gives

\log\zeta(s+\tfrac{1}{2}+it)=i\int_{Y}^{Y^{\prime}}\frac{S(y)}{s+i(t-y)}dy+O\Big(\frac{\log Y}{\Im(s)+t-Y}\Big)+O\Big(\frac{\log Y^{\prime}}{Y^{\prime}-(t+\Im(s))}\Big).

for
Y<\Im(s)+t<Y^{\prime}
. Choosing
Y=T/3
and
Y^{\prime}=3T
the above error terms are
\ll\log T/T
and so (
15
) is

i\int_{T/3}^{3T}S(y)I(t-y)dy+O\Big(\frac{(\log T)^{2}}{T}\Big).

where

I(t-y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i\mathcal{L}}\int_{1/\mathcal{L}-iT/2}^{1/\mathcal{L}+iT/2}\frac{1}{s+i(t-y)}\frac{d}{ds}\bigg[\frac{X^{s}}{s^{2}}\bigg]ds.

Extending the integrals to
\pm i\infty
incurs an error
\ll T^{-2}\log T
. Then, shifting the contour to the left we pick up poles at
s=-i(t-y)
and
s=0
to find

\displaystyle I(t-y)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}}(\mathrm{Res}_{s=-i(t-y)}+\mathrm{Res}_{s=0})\bigg[\frac{X^{s}(s\mathcal{L}-2)}{s^{3}(s+i(t-y))}\bigg]+O\Big(\frac{\log T}{T^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}}\bigg(\frac{X^{-i(t-y)}(-i(t-y)\mathcal{L}-2)}{(-i(t-y))^{3}}+\frac{1}{2!}\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\frac{X^{s}(s\mathcal{L}-2)}{s+i(t-y)}\bigg|_{s=0}\bigg)+O\Big(\frac{\log T}{T^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}}\bigg(\frac{X^{-i(t-y)}(-i(t-y)\mathcal{L}-2)}{(-i(t-y))^{3}}+\frac{\mathcal{L}}{(i(t-y))^{2}}-\frac{2}{(i(t-y))^{3}}\bigg)+O\Big(\frac{\log T}{T^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i(t-y)\mathcal{L}-2+X^{-i(t-y)}(i(t-y)\mathcal{L}+2)}{\mathcal{L}(i(t-y))^{3}}+O\Big(\frac{\log T}{T^{2}}\Big).

Substituting
y\mapsto y+t
and noting that
iI(-y)
is given by
f_{X}(y)
plus a negligible error term we find

\sum_{n\leqslant X}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}(1-\tfrac{\log n}{\log X})=\int_{T/3-t}^{3T-t}S(t+y)f_{X}(y)dy+O\Big(\frac{(\log T)^{2}}{T}\Big).

Truncating the integral at
\pm\log T/\log_{2}T
incurs an error
\ll 1
since
S(t+y)\ll\log T/\log_{2}T
in the range of integration and
f_{X}(y)\ll y^{-2}
for
y\gg 1
. The result then follows.
∎

3.
2k
th Moment formulae: Proof of Theorem
1

3.1.
The
2k
th moment of the real and imaginary parts

We consider the real part since the case of the imaginary part is analogous. Suppose
t\in[T,2T]
and
T^{\epsilon}\leqslant X\leqslant T^{2m}
. Since
V_{X}
is an even function,
\widehat{V}_{X}(y)
is real and hence

(16)

\Re\sum_{n\geqslant 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)V_{X}(\log n)}{n^{1/2+it}\log n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(y+t))|\widehat{V}_{X}(y)dy+O(1)

by Proposition
4
.
Then for even
2k
, by Hölder’s inequality we have

(17)

\int_{T}^{2T}\bigg(\mathfrak{R}\sum_{n\leqslant X}\frac{\Lambda(n)V_{X}(\log n)}{n^{1/2+it}\log n}\bigg)^{2k}dt=J_{k}(T)+O\Big(C^{2k}J_{k}(T)^{1-\tfrac{1}{2k}}T^{1/2k}\Big)

where

J_{k}(T)=\int_{T}^{2T}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+y))|\widehat{V}_{X}(y)dy\bigg)^{2k}dt.

The result for the real part with
2k
even will then follow from the subsequent proposition.

Proposition 6
.

For
k\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
and fixed
0<\theta<1/100
we have

J_{k}(T)=c_{k}T\bigg(\sum_{p\leqslant T^{\theta/k}}\frac{V_{X}(\log p)^{2}}{p}\bigg)^{k}+O(T(Ck)^{4k}(\log\log T)^{k-1/2})

where

c_{k}=\begin{cases}\frac{(2k)!}{2^{2k}k!}&2k\text{ is even, }\\
0&2k\text{ is odd. }\end{cases}

For odd
2k
we simply compute the left hand side of (
17
) as
=J_{k}(T)+O(C^{2k}|J_{k-1/2}|)
. The result then also follows in this case since
J_{k-1/2}(T)\ll T(ck)^{4k}(\log\log T)^{k-1/2}
by the above proposition and the fact that
V_{X}(\log p)\ll 1
.

Proof of Proposition
6
.

We have

J_{k}(T)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2k}}\int_{[-1,1]^{2k}}\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+y_{j}))|dt\prod_{j=1}^{2k}\widehat{V}_{X}(y_{j})dy_{j}.

Write each term in the product of the inner integral as

\bigg(\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+y_{j}))|-\Re\sum_{p\leqslant Y}\frac{1}{p^{1/2+i(t+y_{j})}}\bigg)+\Re\sum_{p\leqslant Y}\frac{1}{p^{1/2+i(t+y_{j})}}

with
Y=T^{\theta/k}
for some
0<\theta<1/100
. After accounting for the small shift
y_{j}\ll 1
, Theorem 5.1 of Tsang’s thesis
[
37
]
gives

(18)

\int_{T}^{2T}\bigg|\mathfrak{F}\log\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+y_{j}))-\mathfrak{F}\sum_{p\leqslant Y}\frac{1}{p^{1/2+i(t+y_{j})}}\bigg|^{2k}dt\ll T(ck)^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{F}}k}

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
where
\alpha_{\Re}=4
and
\alpha_{\Im}=2
. By Hölder’s inequality this extends to real
k>0
. Thus after expanding the product and applying Hölder’s inequality in the form
\int\prod_{j=1}^{2k}f_{j}\ll\prod_{j=1}^{2k}(\int|f_{j}|^{2k})^{1/2k}
we have

J_{k}(T)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2k}}\int_{[-1,1]^{2k}}\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}\Re\sum_{p\leqslant Y}\frac{1}{p^{1/2+i(t+y_{j})}}dt\prod_{j=1}^{2k}\widehat{V}_{X}(y_{j})dy_{j}\\
+O(T(ck)^{4k}(\log\log T)^{k-1/2})

since
\int_{-1}^{1}|\widehat{V}_{X}(y)|dy\ll 1
and

(19)

\int_{T}^{2T}\bigg|\sum_{p\leqslant Y}\frac{1}{p^{1/2+i(t+y)}}\bigg|^{2k}dt\ll T(ck\log\log T)^{k}

for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
and real
k>0
; the integer case following by Lemma
8
below, for example, and the non-integer case by Hölder’s inequality again.

The main term is given by

\sum_{p_{1},\cdots,p_{2k}\leqslant Y}\frac{1}{(p_{1}\cdots p_{2k})^{1/2}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2k}}\int_{[-1,1]^{2k}}\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}\cos((t+y_{j})\log p_{j})dt\prod_{j=1}^{2k}\widehat{V}_{X}(y_{j})dy_{j}.

Applying the identity

\cos((t+y_{j})\log p_{j})=\cos(t\log p_{j})\cos(y_{j}\log p_{j})-\sin(t\log p_{j})\sin(y_{j}\log p_{j})

for each factor and then expanding the product into a sum, we see that any term involving a factor of
\sin(y_{j}\log p_{j})
vanishes since
\widehat{V}_{X}(y)
is an even function. Thus the above is

\sum_{p_{1},\cdots,p_{2k}\leqslant Y}\frac{1}{(p_{1}\cdots p_{2k})^{1/2}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2k}}\int_{[-1,1]^{2k}}\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}\cos(t\log p_{j})dt\prod_{j=1}^{2k}\cos(y_{j}\log p_{j})\widehat{V}_{X}(y_{j})dy_{j}.

Now, by Lemma 4 of
[
28
]
we have

\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}\cos(t\log p_{j})dt=Tf(p_{1}\cdots p_{2k})+O((2Y)^{2k})

where
f
is the multiplicative function defined by
f(p^{\alpha})=\frac{1}{2^{\alpha}}\binom{\alpha}{\alpha/2}
with the convention that the binomial coefficient is zero if
\alpha/2
is not an integer. After summing over the
p_{j}
, the error term here contributes
\ll(cY)^{3k}\ll c^{3k}T^{3\theta}
which is negligible. From the properties of
\widehat{V}_{X}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\cos(y\log p)\widehat{V}_{X}(y)dy=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cos(y\log p)\widehat{V}_{X}(y)dy+O(1/\log X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{X}(\log p)+O(1/\log X)

by Fourier inversion since
V_{X}
is real. Aside from a negligible error term, our main term is then

T\sum_{p_{1},\cdots,p_{2k}\leqslant Y}\frac{f(p_{1}\cdots p_{2k})\prod_{j=1}^{2k}V_{X}(\log p)}{(p_{1}\cdots p_{2k})^{1/2}}.

Since
f(n)
is supported on squares, if
2k
is odd there is no main term so we may assume
2k
is even. In this case, any pairing which results in a fourth power of a prime or higher gives a lower order term since
\sum_{p\leqslant Y}V_{X}(\log p)^{4}/p^{4\sigma}\ll 1
and note that there are at most
(2k)!
such pairings. The main term arises from pairing the
2k
primes into prime squares, which there are
(2k-1)!!=(2k!)/2^{k}k!
ways to do, and since
f(p^{2})=1/2
we see that the above is

\frac{(2k)!}{2^{2k}k!}\bigg(\sum_{p\leqslant Y}\frac{V_{X}(\log p)^{2}}{p}\bigg)^{k}+O((2k)^{2k}(\log\log T)^{k-1}).

∎

3.2.
The absolute
2k
th moment

The case of the absolute moments follows similar lines. By Proposition
4
and Hölder’s inequality we have

(20)

\int_{T}^{2T}\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant X}\frac{\Lambda(n)V_{X}(\log n)}{n^{1/2+it}\log n}\bigg|^{2k}dt=\mathcal{J}_{k}(T)+O\Big(C^{2k}\mathcal{J}_{k}(T)^{1-\tfrac{1}{2k}}T^{1/2k}\Big)

where

\mathcal{J}_{k}(T)=\int_{T}^{2T}\bigg|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\log\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+y))\widehat{V}_{X}(y)dy\bigg|^{2k}dt.

The result then follows from the following proposition.

Proposition 7
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and fixed
0<\theta<1/100
we have

\mathcal{J}_{k}(T)=k!T\bigg(\sum_{p\leqslant T^{\theta/k}}\frac{V_{X}(\log p)^{2}}{p}\bigg)^{k}+O(T(ck)^{4k}(\log\log T)^{k-1/2}).

Proof.

We shall only give a sketch since this follows similarly to Proposition
6
. We first expand the
2k
th power and push the integral over
t
through. We then write

\log\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+y_{j}))=\sum_{p\leqslant Y}\frac{1}{p^{1/2+i(t+y_{j})}}+\Re D_{j}(t)+\Im D_{j}(t).

where
D_{j}(t)
is the difference between the logarithm and the prime sum. Applying this for each term in the integrand and expanding the product into a sum gives
3^{2k}-1
error terms involving either the real or imaginary part of
D_{j}(t)
. These can be dealt as before by using Hölder’s inequality, (
18
) and (
19
) to give an error
\ll T(ck)^{4k}(\log\log T)^{k-1/2}.

In this way we find

\mathcal{J}_{k}(T)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2k}}\int_{[-1,1]^{2k}}\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{p\leqslant Y}\frac{1}{p^{1/2+i(t+y_{j})}}\prod_{j={k+1}}^{2k}\sum_{p\leqslant Y}\frac{1}{p^{1/2-i(t+y_{j})}}dt\prod_{j=1}^{2k}\widehat{V}_{X}(y_{j})dy_{j}\\
+O(T(ck)^{4k}(\log\log T)^{k-1/2}).

By distinguishing diagonal and off-diagonal terms the inner integral is

T\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{j}\leqslant Y\\
p_{1}\cdots p_{k}=p_{k+1}\cdots p_{2k}\end{subarray}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{-iy_{j}}p_{j+k}^{iy_{j+k}}}{(p_{1}\cdots p_{2k})^{1/2}}+O(Y^{3k})

since
\sum_{p\leqslant Y}p^{-1/2}\ll Y^{1/2}
and
\int_{T}^{2T}(p_{1}\cdots p_{k}/p_{k+1}\cdots p_{2k})^{-it}dt\ll Y^{2k}
if
p_{1}\cdots p_{k}\neq p_{k+1}\cdots p_{2k}
.
Extending the integrals over
y_{j}
to
\pm\infty
introduces a negligible error and then computing these integrals as before we find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{k}(T)=

\displaystyle T\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{j}\leqslant Y\\
p_{1}\cdots p_{k}=p_{k+1}\cdots p_{2k}\end{subarray}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}V_{X}(\log p_{j})}{(p_{1}\cdots p_{2k})^{1/2}}+O(T(ck)^{4k}(\log\log T)^{k-1/2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tk!\bigg(\sum_{p\leqslant Y}\frac{V_{X}(\log p)^{2}}{p}\bigg)^{k}+O(T(ck)^{4k}(\log\log T)^{k-1/2})

and so the result follows.
∎

4.
Large deviations: proof of Theorem
3

Before proving Theorem
2
we give the large deviation estimates since the arguments are relatively short. Throughout we make use of the following standard lemma.

Lemma 8
.

Let
T
be large and
2\leqslant x\leqslant T
. Then for
k
such that
x^{k}\leqslant T/\log T
and any complex numbers
a(p)
we have

\int_{T}^{2T}\bigg|\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{a(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}\bigg|^{2k}dt\ll Tk!\bigg(\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{|a(p)|^{2}}{p}\bigg)^{k}.

Proof.

See Lemma 3 of
[
33
]
for example.
∎

4.1.
The real part

Let us consider the real part first and to simplify things we normalise so that
V(0)=1
. Taking real parts in Proposition
4
we have

\Re\sum_{n\geqslant 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)V_{X}(\log n)}{n^{1/2+it}\log n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(y+t))|\widehat{V}_{X}(y)dy+O(1).

since
V_{X}(x)
is even and hence
\widehat{V}_{X}(y)
is real. If
\widehat{V}_{X}(y)
is positive then from the bound
\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|\leqslant(\tfrac{\log 2}{2}+o(1))\tfrac{\log t}{\log\log t}
of
[
6
]
, the right hand side is

\leqslant(1+o(1))\frac{\log 2}{2}\frac{\log t}{\log\log t}

for large
t
by Fourier inversion since
V(0)=1
. Thus, throughout we may assume that

10\sqrt{\log\log T}\leqslant W\leqslant\frac{\log 2}{2}\frac{\log T}{\log\log T}.

We now apply the method of Soundararajan
[
33
]
to find the frequency of large values of
\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(y+t))|
and hence of our Dirichlet polynomial.
Define the parameter

(21)

A=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{2}\log_{3}T&\text{ if }W\leqslant\log\log T\\
\frac{\log\log T}{2W}\log_{3}T&\text{ if }\log\log T\leqslant W\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\log\log T\log_{3}T\\
1&\text{ if }W\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\log\log T\log_{3}T.\end{cases}

and set
Y=T^{A/W}
and
Z=Y^{1/\log\log T}
. Then from Section 3 of
[
33
]
we have for large
t\in[T,2T]
,

\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(y+t))|\leqslant f_{1}(t+y)+f_{2}(t+y)+(\tfrac{3}{4}-\epsilon)\tfrac{W}{A}+O(\log_{3}T)

where

f_{1}(t)=\bigg|\sum_{p\leqslant Z}\frac{1}{p^{1/2+1/2\log Y+it}}\frac{\log(Y/p)}{\log Y}\bigg|

and

f_{2}(t)=\bigg|\sum_{Z<p\leqslant Y}\frac{1}{p^{1/2+1/2\log Y+it}}\frac{\log(Y/p)}{\log Y}\bigg|.

Consequently, by positivity of
\widehat{V}_{X}(y)
,

\Re\sum_{n\geqslant 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)V_{X}(\log n)}{n^{1/2+it}\log n}\leqslant g_{1}(t)+g_{2}(t)+(\tfrac{3}{4}-\epsilon)\tfrac{W}{A}+O(\log_{3}T)

for large
t\in[T,2T]
where

g_{j}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}f_{j}(t+y)\widehat{V}_{X}(y)dy.

Thus, if our sum is greater than
W
we must have either

g_{1}(t)\geqslant W\Big(1-\frac{7}{8A}\Big)=:W_{1}\qquad\text{ or }\qquad g_{2}(t)\geqslant\frac{W}{8A}

We determine the frequency of these conditions by using Chebyshev’s inequality along with high moment bounds. The only difference with
[
33
]
is that we have to deal with the extra integral over
y
. This is easily dealt with however using Hölder’s inequality in the form

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}g_{j}(t)^{2k}dt=

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}f_{j}(t+y)\widehat{V}_{X}(y)\bigg)^{2k}dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\Big(\int_{T}^{2T}f_{j}(t+y)^{2k}dt\Big)\widehat{V}_{X}(y)dy

using the fact that
V(0)=1
. By Lemma
8
we find that for all
y\in[-1,1]
,

\int_{T}^{2T}f_{1}(t+y)^{2k_{1}}dt\ll Tk_{1}!\bigg(\sum_{p\leqslant Z}\frac{1}{p}\bigg)^{k_{1}}\ll T\sqrt{k_{1}}\bigg(\frac{k_{1}\log\log T}{e}\bigg)^{k_{1}}

provided
k_{1}\leqslant\log(T/\log T)/\log Z
, and likewise

\int_{T}^{2T}f_{2}(t+y)^{2k_{2}}dt\ll Tk_{2}!\bigg(\sum_{Z<p\leqslant Y}\frac{1}{p}\bigg)^{k_{2}}\ll T(k_{2}\log_{3}T)^{k_{2}}

provided
k_{2}\leqslant\log(T/\log T)/\log Y
.

Thus, choosing
k_{2}=W/A-1
we see that the measure of the set of
t\in[T,2T]
for which
g_{2}(t)\geqslant W/8A
is

\ll T(W/8A)^{-2k_{2}}(k_{2}\log_{3}T)^{k_{2}}\ll T\exp(-\tfrac{W}{2A}\log W).

Choosing
k_{1}=\lfloor W_{1}^{2}/\log\log T\rfloor
if
W<(\log\log T)^{2}
and
k_{1}=\lfloor 10W\rfloor
when
W>(\log\log T)^{2}
we find that the measure of the set of
t\in[T,2T]
for which
g_{1}(t)\geqslant W_{1}
is

\ll T\frac{W}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{W_{1}^{2}}{\log\log T}\bigg)+T\exp(-4W\log W)

after a short computation. This completes the proof regarding large positive values of the real part.

4.2.
The imaginary part

The imaginary part follows similar lines with some minor modifications which we now describe. We utilise the following formula of Selberg (Theorem 1,
[
31
]
) valid for
t\geqslant 2
and
2\leqslant x\leqslant t^{2}
:

S(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\Im\sum_{n\leqslant x^{2}}\frac{\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{1/2+1/\log x+it}\log n}+O\bigg(\frac{1}{\log x}\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant x^{2}}\frac{\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{1/2+1/\log x+it}}\bigg|\bigg)+O\bigg(\frac{\log t}{\log x}\bigg).

where
\Lambda_{x}(n)=\Lambda(n)
for
n\leqslant x
and equals
\Lambda(n)\log(x^{2}/n)/\log x
for
x<n\leqslant x^{2}
. From this we find that for
t\in[T/2,4T]
,

|S(t)|\leqslant h_{1}(t)+h_{2}(t)+h_{3}(t)+h_{4}(t)+C_{0}\tfrac{\log T}{\log x}

where

\displaystyle h_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\bigg|\sum_{p\leqslant z}\frac{\Lambda_{x}(p)}{p^{1/2+1/\log x+it}\log p}\bigg|,\qquad h_{2}(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\bigg|\sum_{z<p\leqslant x^{2}}\frac{\Lambda_{x}(p)}{p^{1/2+1/\log x+it}\log p}\bigg|,

with
z=x^{2/\log_{2}T}
, and

\displaystyle h_{3}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigg|\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{\Lambda_{x}(p^{2})}{p^{1+2/\log x+2it}\log p}\bigg|,\qquad h_{4}(t)=\frac{B}{\log x}\bigg|\sum_{p\leqslant x^{2}}\frac{\Lambda_{x}(p)}{p^{1/2+1/\log x+it}}\bigg|

for some positive constants
B,C_{0}
. Then taking imaginary parts in Proposition
4
and applying the trivial bound we find

\bigg|\Im\sum_{n\geqslant 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)V_{X}(\log n)}{n^{1/2+it}\log n}\bigg|\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{4}\ell_{j}(t)+C_{0}\tfrac{\log T}{\log x}

where

\ell_{j}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}h_{j}(t+y)\widehat{V}_{X}(y)dy

again by positivity of
\widehat{V}_{X}(y)
and the normalisation
V(0)=1
.

Similarly to before we set
x^{2}=T^{A^{\prime}/W}
where now

(22)

A^{\prime}=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{2}\log_{3}T&\text{ if }W\leqslant\log\log T\\
\frac{\log\log T}{2W}\log_{3}T&\text{ if }\log\log T\leqslant W\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2(5+2C_{0})}\log\log T\log_{3}T\\
5+2C_{0}&\text{ if }W\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2(5+2C_{0})}\log\log T\log_{3}T\end{cases}

and ask when either

\ell_{1}(t)\geqslant W\bigg(1-\frac{4+2C_{0}}{A^{\prime}}\bigg)\qquad\text{ or }\qquad\ell_{j}(t)\geqslant\frac{W}{A^{\prime}}

for
j=2,3
or
4
. We may now proceed as before to give the theorem in the case of the imaginary part. Here we note that the terms
\ell_{3}(t)
,
\ell_{4}(t)
can be dealt with using Lemma
8

along with the bounds

\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\ll 1,\qquad\sum_{p\leqslant x^{2}}\frac{\log^{2}p}{p}\ll\log^{2}x

and a similar choice of parameters to
g_{2}(t)
of the previous section.

5.
Correlations of
S(t)

In order to prove Theorem
2
we require precise correlations of
S(t)
. As mentioned in the introduction, for these purposes we adapt a result of Goldston
[
13
]
which gives lower order terms in the mean square of
S(t)
. Our result is as follows.

Proposition 9
.

Assume RH. Then uniformly in
y_{j}
satisfying
|y_{j}|\leqslant\log T
, we have

(23)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}S(t+y_{1})S(t+y_{2})dt=&\frac{T}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{{\log 2}}^{\log T}\frac{\cos(u(y_{1}-y_{2}))}{u}du\\
&+\frac{T}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{F(u)\cos(u(y_{1}-y_{2})\log T)}{u^{2}}du+\frac{T}{2\pi^{2}}c(y_{1},y_{2})\\
&+O\big(T\tfrac{(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2}}{1+(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2}}\big)+O(T|y_{1}-y_{2}|\log_{2}T)+o(T)\end{split}

where
F(u)
is given by (
4
) and

(24)

c(y_{1},y_{2})=\cos((y_{1}-y_{2})\log 2)\Big(\log\log 2+\gamma+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\sum_{p}\frac{1}{mp^{m}}\Big)\\
+\sum_{p}\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(m(y_{2}-y_{1})\log p)}{m^{2}p^{m}}.

We remark that the second error term in (
23
) can be made explicit and is given by the integral in formula (
35
) below. Since our arguments closely follow those of Goldston, at certain times we may only describe the main points, referring to
[
13
]
for details.

As usual, we start with an approximate formula. Assuming RH, Lemma 1 and (2.12) of
[
13
]
give for
t\geqslant 1
,
t\neq\gamma
and
x\geqslant 4
,

(25)

S(t)=P_{x}(t)+Z_{x}(t)+O(1/t(\log x)^{2})+O(x^{1/2}/(t\log x)^{2})

where

(26)

P_{x}(t)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{\Lambda(n)\sin(t\log n)}{n^{1/2}\log n}f\big(\tfrac{\log n}{\log x}\big),\qquad f(u)=\frac{\pi}{2}u\cot\big(\tfrac{\pi u}{2}\big)

and

(27)

\,\,\,\,\,\,Z_{x}(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{\gamma}h((t-\gamma)\log x),\qquad h(v)=\sin(v)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u}{u^{2}+v^{2}}\frac{du}{\sinh u}.

Throughout we shall assume

\qquad\qquad x=T^{\beta},

for some fixed
0<\beta<1/2
, say. Noting that
S(t+y_{j})
is bounded by
\ll\log T
for
|t|\leqslant 1
we may restrict to the integral over

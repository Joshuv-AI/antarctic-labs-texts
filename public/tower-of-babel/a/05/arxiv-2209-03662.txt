On holomorphic mappings with relatively p p -compact range
A. Jiménez-Vargas

Abstract
. Related to the concept of p p -compact operator with p ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ] p\in[1,\infty] introduced by Sinha and Karn [ 20 ] , this paper deals with the space ℋ 𝒦 p ∞ ​ ( U , F ) \mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F) of all Banach-valued holomorphic mappings on an open subset U U of a complex Banach space E E whose ranges are relatively p p -compact subsets of F F . We characterize such holomorphic mappings as those whose Mujica’s linearisations on the canonical predual of ℋ ∞ ​ ( U ) \mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U) are p p -compact operators. This fact allows us to make a complete study of them. We show that ℋ 𝒦 p ∞ \mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}} is a surjective Banach ideal of bounded holomorphic mappings which is generated by composition with the ideal of p p -compact operators and contains the Banach ideal of all right p p -nuclear holomorphic mappings. We also characterize holomorphic mappings with relatively p p -compact ranges as those bounded holomorphic mappings which factorize through a quotient space of ℓ p ∗ \ell_{p^{*}} or as those whose transposes are quasi p p -nuclear operators (respectively, factor through a closed subspace of ℓ p \ell_{p} ).

Introduction

Inspired by classical Grothendieck’s characterization of a relatively compact subset of a Banach space as a subset of the convex hull of a norm null sequence of vectors
[
12
]
, Sinha and Karn
[
20
]
introduced and studied
p
-compact sets and
p
-compact operators with
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Let
E
be a complex Banach space with closed unit ball
B_{E}
. Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
p^{*}
denote the
conjugate index of
p
given by
p^{*}=p/(p-1)
. Given
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\ell_{p}(E)
denotes the Banach space of all absolutely
p
-summable sequences
(x_{n})
in
E
, equipped with the norm

\left\|(x_{n})\right\|_{p}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|^{p}\right)^{1/p},

and
c_{0}(E)
stands for the Banach space of all norm null sequences
(x_{n})
in
E
, endowed with the norm

\left\|(x_{n})\right\|_{\infty}=\sup\left\{\left\|x_{n}\right\|\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

In the case
E=\mathbb{C}
, we will simply write
\ell_{p}
and
c_{0}
.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
, the
p
-convex hull
of a sequence
(x_{n})\in\ell_{p}(E)
is defined by

p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}x_{n}\colon(a_{n})\in B_{\ell_{p^{*}}}\right\}.

Similarly, we set

\displaystyle 1\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}x_{n}\colon(a_{n})\in B_{c_{0}}\right\},\qquad(x_{n})\in\ell_{1}(E),

\displaystyle\infty\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}x_{n}\colon(a_{n})\in B_{\ell_{1}}\right\},\qquad(x_{n})\in c_{0}(E).

Note that
\infty\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})
coincides with
\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(\{x_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\})
, the norm-closed absolutely convex hull of the set
\{x_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
in
E
.

Following
[
20
]
, given
p\in[1,\infty]
, a set
K\subseteq E
is said to be
relatively
p
-compact
if there is a sequence
(x_{n})\in\ell_{p}(E)
(
(x_{n})\in c_{0}(E)
if
p=\infty
) such that
K\subseteq p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})
. Such a sequence is not unique but a
measure of the size of the
p
-compact set
K
is introduced in
[
10
, p. 297]
(see also
[
14
]
) defining

m_{p}(K,E)=\begin{cases}\inf\{\left\|(x_{n})\right\|_{p}\colon(x_{n})\in\ell_{p}(E),\,K\subseteq p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})\}&\text{if }1\leq p<\infty,\\
\inf\{\left\|(x_{n})\right\|_{p}\colon(x_{n})\in c_{0}(E),\,K\subseteq p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})\}&\text{if }p=\infty,\end{cases}

and
m_{p}(K,E)=\infty
if
K
is not
p
-compact. We frequently will write
m_{p}(K)
instead of
m_{p}(K,E)
.

A linear operator between Banach spaces
T\colon E\to F
is said to be
p
-compact
if
T(B_{E})
is a relatively
p
-compact subset of
F
. The space of all
p
-compact linear operators from
E
to
F
is denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{p}(E,F)
, and
\mathcal{K}_{p}
is a Banach operator ideal endowed with the norm
k_{p}(T)=m_{p}(T(B_{E}))
(see
[
20
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
10
, Proposition 3.15]
).

From the cited Grothendieck’s result
[
12
]
, a set
K\subseteq E
is relatively compact if and only if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a sequence
(x_{n})\in c_{0}(E)
with
\left\|(x_{n})\right\|_{\infty}\leq\sup_{x\in K}\left\|x\right\|+\varepsilon
such that
K\subseteq\infty\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})
. Hence we can consider compact sets as
\infty
-compact sets and, in this way, compact operators can be viewed as
\infty
-compact operators with
k_{\infty}
being the usual operator norm.

The work of Sinha and Karn
[
20
]
motivated many papers on
p
-compactness in operator spaces (see
[
3
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
14
,
19
]
, among others) and also in Lipschitz spaces
[
1
,
2
]
.

The extension of this theory to the polynomial and holomorphic settings was addressed in
[
5
,
6
]
. In these environments, the property of
p
-compactness was studied from the following local point of view: a mapping
f\colon U\to F
is said to be
locally
p
-compact
if every point
x\in U
has a neighborhood
U_{x}\subseteq U
such that
f(U_{x})
is relatively
p
-compact in
F
.

The aim of this note is to study a subclass of locally
p
-compact holomorphic mappings, namely,
holomorphic mappings with relatively
p
-compact range
. Notice that every such mapping is locally
p
-compact but the converse is not true. For instance, if
\mathbb{D}
denotes the open complex unit disc, the holomorphic mapping
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to c_{0}
defined by
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n}e_{n}
, where
(e_{n})
is the canonical basis of
\ell_{1}
, is locally
1
-compact but it has not relatively compact range (see
[
15
, Example 3.2]
) and, consequently, neither relatively
p
-compact range for any
p\geq 1
.

Our motivation to deal with this class of mappings also arises from the study (initiated in
[
15
]
and continued in
[
7
,
13
]
) on the Banach space
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}}(U,F)
formed by all holomorphic mappings from
U
to
F
with relatively compact range, equipped with the supremum norm.

We now briefly describe the content of this paper. Let
E
and
F
be complex Banach spaces,
U
an open subset of
E
and
p\in[1,\infty]
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
denote the Banach space of all bounded holomorphic mappings from
U
into
F
, endowed with the supremum norm. In particular,
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
stands for
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,\mathbb{C})
.

In
[
15
]
, Mujica provided a linearisation method of the members of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
, which will be an essential tool in our analysis of the subject. If
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)
is the canonical predual of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
obtained by Mujica
[
15
]
via an identification denoted
g_{U}
, we will establish that a bounded holomorphic mapping
f\colon U\to F
has relatively
p
-compact range if and only if Mujica’s linearisation
T_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\to F
is a
p
-compact operator. This fact has some interesting applications.

If
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
denotes the space of all holomorphic mappings with relatively
p
-compact range
f\colon U\to F
with the natural norm
k^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)=m_{p}(f(U))
, we will prove that
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}
is a surjective Banach ideal of bounded holomorphic mappings which is generated by composition with the ideal
\mathcal{K}_{p}
of
p
-compact operators. This means that each mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
admits a factorization
f=T\circ g
, where
G
is a complex Banach space,
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,G)
and
T\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(G,F)
. Moreover,
k^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(f)
coincides with
\inf\{k_{p}(T)\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\}
, where the infimum is extended over all such factorizations of
f
and, curiously, this infimum is attained at the factorization
f=T_{f}\circ g_{U}
due to Mujica
[
15
]
.

In parallelism with the linear case, we introduce the notion of right
p
-nuclear holomorphic mapping from
U
to
F
, study its linearisation on
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)
, analyse its ideal property and show that every right
p
-nuclear holomorphic mapping has relatively
p
-compact range.

Moreover, we characterize the members of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
as those bounded holomorphic mappings from
U
to
F
which factorize through a quotient space of
\ell_{p^{*}}
, and also as those whose transposes are quasi
p
-nuclear operators (respectively, factor through a closed subspace of
\ell_{p}
).

1.
The results

From now on, unless otherwise stated,
E
and
F
will denote complex Banach spaces,
U
will be an open subset of
E
and
p\in[1,\infty]
.

As usual,
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
denotes the Banach space of all bounded linear operators from
E
to
F
endowed with the operator canonical norm, and
E^{*}
stands for the dual space of
E
. The subspaces of
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
consisting of all compact operators and finite-rank bounded operators from
E
to
F
will be denoted by
\mathcal{K}(E,F)
and
\mathcal{F}(E,F)
, respectively.

In this section, we will study holomorphic mappings
f\colon U\to F
so that
f(U)
is a relatively
p
-compact subset of
F
. We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
the set formed by such mappings and, for each
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
, we define
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)=m_{p}(f(U))
.

The space of all holomorphic mappings with relatively compact range from
U
to
F
, denoted
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}}(U,F)
, is a Banach space equipped with the supremum norm (see
[
13
, Corollary 2.11]
). On account of the following result, we will only study in this paper the case
1\leq p<\infty
.

Proposition 1.1
.

\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}(U,F)=\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}}(U,F)
and
k_{\infty}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)=\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}
for all
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}(U,F)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}(U,F)
and let
(y_{n})\in c_{0}(F)
be such that
f(U)\subseteq\infty\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(y_{n})
. Since
\infty\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(y_{n})
is relatively compact in
F
, it follows that
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}}(U,F)

with
\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|(y_{n})\right\|_{\infty}
, and taking infimum over all such sequences
(y_{n})
, we have
\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\leq k_{\infty}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)
.

Conversely, let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}}(U,F)
. By classical Grothendieck’s result, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
(y_{n})\in c_{0}(F)
with
\left\|(y_{n})\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon
such that
f(U)\subseteq\infty\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(y_{n})
. Hence
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}(U,F)
and
k_{\infty}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}
.
∎

1.1.
Linearisation

Our first aim is to characterize holomorphic mappings with relatively
p
-compact range in terms of the
p
-compactness of their linearisations on the canonical predual of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
.

Towards this end, we first recall the following result due to Mujica
[
15
]
concerning linearisation of holomorphic mappings on Banach spaces.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
15
]

Let
E
be a complex Banach space and
U
be an open set in
E
. Let
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)
denote the norm-closed linear subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)^{*}
generated by the functionals
\delta(x)\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)^{*}
with
x\in U
, defined by
\delta(x)(f)=f(x)
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
.

(i)

The mapping
g_{U}\colon U\to\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)
defined by
g_{U}(x)=\delta(x)
is holomorphic with
\left\|\delta(x)\right\|=1
for all
x\in U
.

(ii)

For every complex Banach space
F
and every mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
, there exists a unique operator
T_{f}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
such that
T_{f}\circ g_{U}=f
. Furthermore,
\left\|T_{f}\right\|=\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}
.

(iii)

For every complex Banach space
F
, the mapping
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
onto
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
.

(iv)

\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
is isometrically isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)^{*}
, via the mapping
J_{U}\colon\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)\to\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)^{*}
given by
J_{U}(f)(g_{U}(x))=f(x)
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
and
x\in U
.

(v)

B_{\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)}
coincides with
\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(g_{U}(U))
.
\hfill\Box

We are now ready to state the aforementioned characterization.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f
has relatively
p
-compact range.

(ii)

T_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\to F
is a
p
-compact linear operator.

In this case,
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)=k_{p}(T_{f})
. Furthermore, the mapping
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F),k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
onto
(\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F),k_{p})
.

Proof.

Using Theorem
1.2
, we have the following inclusions:

\displaystyle f(U)=T_{f}\circ g_{U}(U)

\displaystyle\subseteq T_{f}(\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(g_{U}(U)))=T_{f}(B_{\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)})

\displaystyle\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(T_{f}\circ g_{U}(U))=\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(f(U)).

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: If
T_{f}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
, then
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
with

k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)=m_{p}(f(U))\leq m_{p}(T_{f}(B_{\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)}))=k_{p}(T_{f})

by the first inclusion above.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: If
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
, then
T_{f}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
with

k_{p}(T_{f})=m_{p}(T_{f}(B_{\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)}))\leq m_{p}(\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(f(U)))=m_{p}(f(U))=k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f),

by the second inclusion above and the known fact that a set
K\subseteq F
is
p
-compact if and only if
\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(K)
is
p
-compact, in whose case
m_{p}(K)=m_{p}(\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(K))
.

The last assertion of the statement follows immediately from part (iii) of Theorem
1.2
and from the above proof.
∎

1.2.
Banach ideal property

Our next goal is to study the Banach ideal structure of
(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}},k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
. Inspired by the notion of Banach operator ideal
[
18
]
, the following type of ideals was considered in
[
7
]
.

An
ideal of bounded holomorphic mappings
(or simply, a
bounded-holomorphic ideal
) is a subclass
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
of the class of bounded holomorphic mappings
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
such that for each complex Banach space
E
, each open subset
U
of
E
and each complex Banach space
F
, the components

\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F):=\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}\cap\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)

satisfy the following three conditions:

(I1)

\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
,

(I2)

For any
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
and
y\in F
, the mapping
g\cdot y\colon x\mapsto g(x)y
from
U
to
F
is in
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
,

(I3)

The ideal property
: if
H,G
are complex Banach spaces,
V
is an open subset of
H
,
h\in\mathcal{H}(V,U)
,
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
and
S\in\mathcal{L}(F,G)
, then
S\circ f\circ h\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(V,G)
.

Suppose that a function
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}\colon\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}\to\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
satisfies the following three properties:

(N1)

(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}})
is a normed (Banach) space with
\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
,

(N2)

\left\|g\cdot y\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}=\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\left\|y\right\|
for all
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
and
y\in F
,

(N3)

If
H,G
are complex Banach spaces,
V
is an open subset of
H
,
h\in\mathcal{H}(V,U)
,
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
and
S\in\mathcal{L}(F,G)
, then
\left\|S\circ f\circ h\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}\leq\left\|S\right\|\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}
.

Then
(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}})
is called a
normed (Banach) bounded-holomorphic ideal
.

A normed bounded-holomorphic ideal
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
is said to be:

(R)

regular
if for any
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
, we have that
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
with
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}=\left\|\kappa_{F}\circ f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}
whenever
\kappa_{F}\circ f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F^{**})
, where
\kappa_{F}
denotes the isometric linear embedding from
F
into
F^{**}
.

(S)

surjective
if for any mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
, any open subset
V
of a complex Banach space
G
and any surjective mapping
\pi\in\mathcal{H}(V,U)
, we have that
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(U,F)
with
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}=\left\|f\circ\pi\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}
whenever
f\circ\pi\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(V,F)
.

Bearing in mind Theorem
1.3
, Theorem 3.2 in
[
4
]
(see also Theorem 2.4 in
[
7
]
) shows that
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}
is generated by composition with the operator ideal
\mathcal{K}_{p}
(see
[
7
, Definition 2.3]
).

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f\colon U\to F
has relatively
p
-compact range.

(ii)

f=T\circ g
for some complex Banach space
G
,
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,G)
and
T\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(G,F)
.

In this case, we have

k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)=\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}:=\inf\{k_{p}(T)\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\},

where the infimum runs over all factorizations of
f
as in
(ii)
, and this infimum is attained at
T_{f}\circ g_{U}
(Mujica’s factorization of
f

[
15
]
).

Furthermore, the mapping
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
(\mathcal{K}_{p}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
onto
(\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F),k_{p})
.
\hfill\qed

The following result gathers some Banach ideal properties of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For each
p\in[1,\infty)
,
(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}},k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
is a surjective Banach bounded-holomorphic ideal. Furthermore, the ideal
(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F),k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
is regular whenever
F
is reflexive.

Proof.

In view of Corollary
1.4
, Corollary 2.5 in
[
7
]
yields that
(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}},k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
is a Banach bounded-holomorphic ideal. Then we only have to study its surjectivity and its regularity.

(S) Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
and assume that
f\circ\pi\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(V,F)
, where
V
is an open subset of a complex Banach space
G
and
\pi\in\mathcal{H}(V,U)
is surjective. Since
f(U)=(f\circ\pi)(V)
, it is immediate that
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
with
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)=k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f\circ\pi)
. Hence
(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}},k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
is surjective.

(R) Suppose now that
F
is reflexive. Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
and assume that
\kappa_{F}\circ f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F^{**})
. We can take a sequence
(y_{n})
in
\ell_{p}(F)
(in
c_{0}(F)
if
p=1
) such that
(\kappa_{F}\circ f)(U)\subseteq p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(\kappa_{F}(y_{n}))
, that is,
\kappa_{F}(f(U))\subseteq\kappa_{F}(p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(y_{n}))
which yields
f(U)\subseteq p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(y_{n})
by the injectivity of
\kappa_{F}
. Hence
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
with
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)\leq\left\|(y_{n})\right\|_{p}=\left\|(\kappa_{F}(y_{n}))\right\|_{p}
, and so
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)\leq k^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}_{p}(\kappa_{F}\circ f)
by extending this infimum over all such sequences
(\kappa_{F}(y_{n}))
. The converse inequality follows from the condition (N3) satisfied by
(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}},k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
, and this completes the proof.
∎

1.3.
Factorization

We now present a factorization result for holomorphic mappings with relatively
p
-compact range which should be compared with
[
11
, Proposition 2.9]
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f\colon U\to F
has relatively
p
-compact range.

(ii)

There exist a closed subspace
M
in
\ell_{p^{*}}
(
c_{0}
instead of
\ell_{p^{*}}
if
p=1
), a separable Banach space
G
, an operator
T
in
\mathcal{K}_{p}(\ell_{p^{*}}/M,G)
, a mapping
g
in
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}}(U,\ell_{p^{*}}/M)
and an operator
S
in
\mathcal{K}(G,F)
such that
f=S\circ T\circ g
.

In this case,
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)=\inf\{\left\|S\right\|k_{p}(T)\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\}
, where the infimum is extended over all factorizations of
f
as in
(ii)
.

Proof.

We will only prove it for
p\in(1,\infty)
. The case
p=1
is similarly obtained.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
. By Theorem
1.3
,
T_{f}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
with
k_{p}(T_{f})=k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)
. Applying
[
11
, Proposition 2.9]
, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a closed subspace
M\subseteq\ell_{p^{*}}
,
a separable Banach space
G
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\ell_{p^{*}}/M,G)
, an operator
S\in\mathcal{K}(G,F)
and an operator
R\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),\ell_{p^{*}}/M)
such that
T_{f}=S\circ T\circ R
with
\left\|S\right\|k_{p}(T)\left\|R\right\|\leq k_{p}(T_{f})+\varepsilon
. Moreover,
R=T_{g}
with
\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|R\right\|
for some
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}}(U,\ell_{p^{*}}/M)
by
[
13
, Corollary 2.11]
. Thus we obtain

f=T_{f}\circ g_{U}=S\circ T\circ R\circ g_{U}=S\circ T\circ T_{g}\circ g_{U}=S\circ T\circ g,

with

\left\|S\right\|k_{p}(T)\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|S\right\|k_{p}(T)\left\|R\right\|\leq k_{p}(T_{f})+\varepsilon=k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, we deduce that
\left\|S\right\|k_{p}(T)\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\leq k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Assume that
f=S\circ T\circ g
is a factorization as in
(ii)
. Since
S\circ T\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\ell_{p^{*}}/M,F)
by the ideal property of
\mathcal{K}_{p}
, Corollary
1.4
yields that
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
with

k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)\leq k_{p}(S\circ T)\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|S\right\|k_{p}(T)\left\|g\right\|_{\infty},

and taking infimum over all such factorizations of
f
, we have
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)\leq\inf\{\left\|S\right\|k_{p}(T)\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\}
.
∎

1.4.
Transposition

Let us recall that the
transpose
of a mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
is the bounded linear operator
f^{t}\colon F^{*}\to\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
defined by

f^{t}(y^{*})=y^{*}\circ f\qquad(y^{*}\in F^{*}).

Moreover,
||f^{t}||=\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}
and
f^{t}=J_{U}^{-1}\circ(T_{f})^{*}
, where
J_{U}\colon\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)\to\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)^{*}
is the isometric isomorphism defined in Theorem
1.2
.

The Banach ideal
\mathcal{K}_{p}
is associated by duality with the ideal of quasi-
p
-nuclear operators. According to
[
17
]
, for every
p\in[1,\infty)
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(E,F)
is said to be
quasi
p
-nuclear
if there is a sequence
(x^{*}_{n})\in\ell_{p}(E^{*})
such that

\left\|T(x)\right\|\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|x^{*}_{n}(x)\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\qquad(x\in E).

If
\mathcal{QN}_{p}(E,F)
denotes the set formed by such operators, then
\mathcal{QN}_{p}
is a Banach operator ideal equipped with the norm

\nu^{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(T)=\inf\left\{\left\|(x^{*}_{n})\right\|_{p}\colon\left\|T(x)\right\|\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|x^{*}_{n}(x)\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p},\;\forall x\in E\right\}.

By
[
10
, Proposition 3.8]
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(E,F)
if and only if its adjoint
T^{*}\in\mathcal{QN}_{p}(F^{*},E^{*})
. Moreover,
k_{p}(T)=\nu^{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(T^{*})
by
[
11
, Corollary 2.7]
. A holomorphic version of this result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f\colon U\to F
has relatively
p
-compact range.

(ii)

f^{t}\colon F^{*}\to\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
is a quasi
p
-nuclear operator.

In this case,
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)=\nu_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}}(f^{t})
.

Proof.

Applying Theorem
1.3
,
[
11
, Corollary 2.7]
and
[
17
, p. 32]
, respectively, we have

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow T_{f}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(T_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{QN}_{p}(F^{*},\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)^{*})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow f^{t}=J_{U}^{-1}\circ(T_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{QN}_{p}(F^{*},\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)).

In this case,
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)=k_{p}(T_{f})=\nu^{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}((T_{f})^{*})=\nu^{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}(f^{t})
.
∎

Given
p\in[1,\infty)
, let us recall (see
[
18
]
) that an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(E,F)
is
p
-summing
if there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that, regardless of the natural number
n
and regardless of the choice of vectors
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
in
E
, we have

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|T(x_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\sup_{x^{*}\in B_{E^{*}}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|x^{*}(x_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The infimum of such constants
C
is denoted by
\pi_{p}(T)
and the linear space of all
p
-summing operators from
E
into
F
by
\Pi_{p}(E,F)
.

The following result could be compared with
[
10
, Proposition 3.13]
stated in the linear setting.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
and
g\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\infty}(U,F^{*})
. Assume that
T_{f}\in\Pi_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
with
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then
f^{t}\circ g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))
with
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f^{t}\circ g)\leq\pi_{p}(T_{f})\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.3
and Proposition
1.1
,
T_{g}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F^{*})
with
||T_{g}||=\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}
. Consequently, by
[
10
, Proposition 3.13]
, the linear operator
(T_{f})^{*}\circ T_{g}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)^{*})
with
k_{p}((T_{f})^{*}\circ T_{g})\leq\pi_{p}(T_{f})||T_{g}||
. From the equality
f^{t}\circ T_{g}=J_{U}^{-1}\circ(T_{f})^{*}\circ T_{g}
, we infer that
f^{t}\circ T_{g}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))
with
k_{p}(f^{t}\circ T_{g})=k_{p}((T_{f})^{*}\circ T_{g})
by the ideal property of
\mathcal{K}_{p}
. Applying Theorem
1.3
, there exists
h\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))
with
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(h)=k_{p}(T_{h})
such that
f^{t}\circ T_{g}=T_{h}
. Hence
f^{t}\circ g=h
and thus
f^{t}\circ g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))
with

k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f^{t}\circ g)=k_{p}(T_{h})=k_{p}((T_{f})^{*}\circ T_{g})\leq\pi_{p}(T_{f})\left\|T_{g}\right\|=\pi_{p}(T_{f})\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}.

∎

p
-Compact operators were characterized as those operators whose adjoints factor through a subspace of
\ell_{p}

[
20
, Theorem 3.2]
. We now obtain a similar factorization for the transpose of a holomorphic mapping with relatively
p
-compact range (compare also to
[
10
, Proposition 3.10]
).

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f\colon U\to F
has relatively
p
-compact range.

(ii)

There exist a closed subspace
M\subseteq\ell_{p}
and
R\in\mathcal{QN}_{p}(F^{*},M)
,
S\in\mathcal{L}(M,\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))
such that
f^{t}=S\circ R
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: If
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
, then
T_{f}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
by Theorem
1.3
. By
[
10
, Proposition 3.10]
, there exist a closed subspace
M\subseteq\ell_{p}
and operators
R\in\mathcal{QN}_{p}(F^{*},M)
and
S_{0}\in\mathcal{L}(M,\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)^{*})
such that
(T_{f})^{*}=S_{0}\circ R
. Taking
S=J_{U}^{-1}\circ S_{0}\in\mathcal{L}(M,\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))
, we have
f^{t}=S\circ R
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Assume that
f^{t}=S\circ R
being
S
and
R
as in the statement. It follows that
(T_{f})^{*}=J_{U}\circ f^{t}=J_{U}\circ S\circ R
, and thus
T_{f}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
by
[
10
, Proposition 3.10]
. Hence
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
by Theorem
1.3

∎

1.5.
Inclusions

We will study the inclusion relations of holomorphic mappings with relatively
p
-compact range between them and with other classes of bounded holomorphic mappings.

Our first result follows immediately by applying Theorem
1.3
and the fact stated in
[
20
, Proposition 4.3]
that
\mathcal{K}_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{q}
whenever
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
.

Corollary 1.10
.

If
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
, then
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{q}}(U,F)
and
k_{q}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)\leq k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)
.
\hfill\qed

Let us recall that a mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
has
finite dimensional rank
if the linear hull of its range is a finite dimensional subspace of
F
. We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(U,F)
the set of all finite-rank bounded holomorphic mappings from
U
to
F
. In the light of Theorem
1.5
, it is clear that
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(U,F)
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
.

In similarity with the linear case, it seems natural to introduce the following class of holomorphic mappings.

Definition 1.11
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. A mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
is said to be
p
-approximable
if there exists a sequence
(f_{n})
in
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(U,F)
such that
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f_{n}-f)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{p}}(U,F)
the space of all
p
-approximable holomorphic mappings from
U
to
F
.

Proposition 1.12
.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, every
p
-approximable holomorphic mapping from
U
to
F
has relatively
p
-compact range.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{p}}(U,F)
. Hence there is a sequence
(f_{n})
in
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(U,F)
such that
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f_{n}-f)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Since
T_{f_{n}}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
by
[
15
, Proposition 3.1]
,
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
by
[
20
, Theorem 4.2]
and
k_{p}(T_{f_{n}}-T_{f})=k_{p}(T_{f_{n}-f})=k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f_{n}-f)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
by Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, we deduce that
T_{f}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
by
[
20
, Theorem 4.2]
, and so
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
by Theorem
1.3
.
∎

Given
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\ell^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p}(E)
denotes the Banach space of all weakly
p
-summable sequences
(x_{n})
in
E
, endowed with the norm

\left\|(x_{n})\right\|^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p}=\sup\left\{\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|f(x_{n})\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\colon f\in B_{E^{*}}\right\}.

Let us recall (see
[
16
]
) that an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(E,F)
is said to be
right
p
-nuclear
if there are sequences
(x^{*}_{n})\in\ell^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p^{*}}(E^{*})
and
(y_{n})\in\ell_{p}(F)
such that

T(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x^{*}_{n}(x)y_{n}\qquad(x\in E),

and the series converges in
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
. The set of such operators, denoted
\mathcal{N}^{p}(E,F)
, is a Banach space with the norm

\nu^{p}(T)=\inf\left\{\left\|(x^{*}_{n})\right\|^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p^{*}}\left\|(y_{n})\right\|_{p}\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all representations of
T
as above.

A holomorphic variant of this class of operators can be introduced as follows.

Definition 1.13
.

Given
p\in[1,\infty)
, a holomorphic mapping
f\colon U\to F
is said to be
right
p
-nuclear
if there exist sequences
(g_{n})
in
\ell^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p^{*}}(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))
and
(y_{n})
in
\ell_{p}(F)
such that
f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}\cdot y_{n}
in
(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty})
. We set

\nu^{p{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}(f)=\inf\left\{\left\|(g_{n})\right\|^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p^{*}}\left\|(y_{n})\right\|_{p}\right\},

with the infimum taken over all right
p
-nuclear holomorphic representations of
f
as above. Let
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{N}^{p}}(U,F)
denote the set of all right
p
-nuclear holomorphic mappings from
U
into
F
.

We now establish the relationships of a right
p
-nuclear holomorphic mapping
f\colon U\to F
with its linearisation
T_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\to F
.

Theorem 1.14
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f\colon U\to F
is right
p
-nuclear.

(ii)

T_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)\to F
is a right
p
-nuclear operator.

In this case,
\nu^{p\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)=\nu^{p}(T_{f})
. Furthermore, the mapping
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{N}^{p}}(U,F),\nu^{p\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
onto
(\mathcal{N}^{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F),\nu^{p})
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: Assume that
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{N}^{p}}(U,F)
and let
\sum_{n\geq 1}g_{n}\cdot y_{n}
be a right
p
-nuclear holomorphic representation of
f
. By Theorem
1.2
, there is a unique operator
T_{f}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U,F)
such that
T_{f}\circ g_{U}=f
. Similarly, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a functional
T_{g_{n}}\in\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)^{*}
with
||T_{g_{n}}||=\left\|g_{n}\right\|_{\infty}
such that
T_{g_{n}}\circ g_{U}=g_{n}
. Notice that
\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}T_{g_{n}}\cdot y_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
since

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|T_{g_{k}}\cdot y_{k}\right\|=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|T_{g_{k}}\right\|\left\|y_{k}\right\|=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|y_{k}\right\|\leq\left\|(g_{n})\right\|^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p^{*}}\left\|(y_{n})\right\|_{p}

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We can write

f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}\cdot y_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(T_{g_{n}}\circ g_{U})\cdot y_{n}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}T_{g_{n}}\cdot y_{n}\right)\circ g_{U}

in
(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty})
. Hence
T_{f}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}T_{g_{n}}\cdot y_{n}
by Theorem
1.2
, where
(T_{g_{n}})\in\ell^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p^{*}}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U)^{*})
with
\left\|(T_{g_{n}})\right\|^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p^{*}}\leq\left\|(g_{n})\right\|^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p^{*}}
. Therefore
T_{f}\in\mathcal{N}^{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
with
\nu^{p}(T_{f})\leq\left\|(g_{n})\right\|^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p^{*}}\left\|(y_{n})\right\|_{p}
. Taking infimum over all right
p
-nuclear holomorphic representation of
f
, we deduce that
\nu^{p}(T_{f})\leq\nu^{p\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Suppose that
T_{f}\in\mathcal{N}^{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
and let
\sum_{n\geq 1}\phi_{n}\cdot y_{n}
be a right
p
-nuclear representation of
T_{f}
. By Theorem
1.2
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a
g_{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)
such that
J_{U}(g_{n})=\phi_{n}
with
\left\|g_{n}\right\|_{\infty}=||\phi_{n}||
. We have

\displaystyle\left\|\left(f-\sum_{k=1}^{n}g_{k}\cdot y_{k}\right)(x)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|f(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}g_{k}(x)y_{k}\right\|=\left\|T_{f}(g_{U}(x))-\sum_{k=1}^{n}J_{U}(g_{k})(g_{U}(x))y_{k}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(T_{f}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{k}\cdot y_{k}\right)(g_{U}(x))\right\|\leq\left\|T_{f}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{k}\cdot y_{k}\right\|\left\|g_{U}(x)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|T_{f}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{k}\cdot y_{k}\right\|

for all
x\in U
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking supremum over all
x\in U
, we obtain

\left\|f-\sum_{k=1}^{n}g_{k}\cdot y_{k}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|T_{f}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{k}\cdot y_{k}\right\|

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}\cdot y_{n}
in
(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty})
, where
(g_{n})\in\ell^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p^{*}}(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))
with
\left\|(g_{n})\right\|^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p^{*}}\leq\left\|(\phi_{n})\right\|^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p^{*}}
. So
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{N}^{p}}(U,F)
with
\nu^{p\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)\leq\left\|(\phi_{n})\right\|^{\mathrm{weak}}_{p^{*}}\left\|(y_{n})\right\|_{p}
, and this implies that
\nu^{p\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)\leq\nu^{p}(T_{f})
.

The last assertion in the statement follows easily from what was proved above and from Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Combining Theorem
1.14
, firstly with
[
4
, Theorem 3.2]
, and secondly with
[
7
, Corollary 2.5]
, we derive the following two results.

Corollary 1.15
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f\colon U\to F
is right
p
-nuclear.

(ii)

f=T\circ g
for some complex Banach space
G
,
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,G)
and
T\in\mathcal{N}^{p}(G,F)
.

In this case, we have

\nu^{p\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)=\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{N}^{p}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}:=\inf\{\nu^{p}(T)\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\},

where the infimum is taken over all factorizations of
f
as in
(ii)
, and this infimum is attained at
T_{f}\circ g_{U}
.

Furthermore, the mapping
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
(\mathcal{N}^{p}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{N}^{p}\circ\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
onto
(\mathcal{N}^{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F),\nu^{p})
.
\hfill\qed

Corollary 1.16
.

For each
p\in[1,\infty)
,
(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{N}^{p}},\nu^{p\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})
is a Banach bounded-holomorphic ideal.
\hfill\qed

The following relation is readily obtained.

Corollary 1.17
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{N}^{p}}(U,F)
. Then
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
with
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)\leq\nu^{p\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
1.14
, we have
T_{f}\in\mathcal{N}^{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
with
\nu^{p}(T_{f})=\nu^{p\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)
. It follows that
T_{f}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}^{\infty}(U),F)
with
k_{p}(T_{f})\leq\nu^{p}(T_{f})
(see
[
10
, p. 295]
). Hence
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
with
k_{p}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)\leq\nu^{p\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(f)
by Theorem
1.3
.
∎

Given Banach spaces
E,F,G
, let us recall that a normed operator ideal
\mathcal{I}
is
surjective
if for every surjection
Q\in\mathcal{L}(G,E)
and every
T\in\mathcal{L}(E,F)
, it follows from
T\circ Q\in\mathcal{I}(G,F)
that
T\in\mathcal{I}(E,F)
with
\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}=\left\|T\circ Q\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}
. The smallest surjective ideal which contains
\mathcal{I}
, denoted by
\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{sur}}
, is called the
surjective hull of
\mathcal{I}
.

We now introduce the analogue concept in the holomorphic setting.

Definition 1.18
.

The
surjective hull
of a bounded-holomorphic ideal
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
is the smallest surjective ideal which contains
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
, and it is denoted by
(\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}})^{\mathrm{sur}}
.

We have seen that
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}
is a surjective bounded-holomorphic ideal which contains
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{N}^{p}}
, and therefore
(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{N}^{p}})^{\mathrm{sur}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}
, but we do not know if both sets are equal as it happens in the linear case (see
[
10
, Proposition 3.11]
) .

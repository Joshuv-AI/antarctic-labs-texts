Homogeneous mixed Herz-Morrey spaces and its Applications
Mingwei Shi, Jiang Zhou

Abstract




Abstract:

In this paper, we introduce homogeneous mixed Herz-Morrey spaces
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and show it’s some properties. Firstly, the boundedness of sublinear operators, fractional type operators in homogeneous mixed Herz-Morrey spaces is investigated. In particular, the above results are still valid for Calder
\acute{o}
n-Zygmund operators and fractional maximal operators. Lastly, the boundedness of their commutators in homogeneous mixed Herz-Morrey spaces is obtained.

Key Words:
homogeneous mixed Herz-Morrey spaces; Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators; sublinear operators; commutators

Mathematics Subject Classification(2010):
42B20, 42B25, 42B35

1
Introduction

In 1938, Morrey
[
1
]
introduced Morrey spaces
\mathcal{M}_{p,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Due to the importance of Morrey spaces
\mathcal{M}_{p,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in partial differential equation, many authors defined the generalization of Morrey spaces and combined it with other spaces. In 2005, Lu and Xu
[
2
]
introduced homogeneous Herz-Morrey spaces
M\dot{K}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which are generalization of Morrey spaces
\mathcal{M}_{p,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and homogeneous Herz spaces
\dot{K}_{q}^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. And defined as follows (
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<p\leqslant\infty
,
0\leqslant\lambda<\infty
,
0<q<\infty
),

M\dot{K}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{f\in L_{loc}^{q}:\|f\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left\{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\alpha p}\|f\chi_{k}\|_{L^{q}}^{p}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

it is obvious that
M\dot{K}_{p,q}^{\alpha,0}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)=\dot{K}_{q}^{\alpha,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and
M_{q}^{\lambda}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\subset M\dot{K}_{q,q}^{0,\lambda}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

In recent years, researchers have done more research in homogeneous Herz-Morrey spaces
M\dot{K}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Specifically speaking in 2005, Lu and Xu
[
2
]
proved the boundedness of rough Singular integral operators. In 2009, Tao, Shi and Zhang
[
3
]
discussed the boundedness of multilinear Riesz potential operators on Herz-Morrey spaces
M\dot{K}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In 2010, Izuki
[
4
]
researched fractional integral with variable exponent on Herz-Morrey spaces
M\dot{K}_{q,p(\cdot)}^{\alpha,\lambda}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)(\alpha\in\mathbb{R},0<q<\infty,p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),0\leqslant\lambda<\infty)
; Gao and Jiang
[
6
]
proved the boundedness of a class of linear commutators; Shi, Tao and Zhang
[
5
]
considered multilinear Riesz potential operators on Herz-Morrey Spaces with non-doubling measures. In 2012, Zhou and Zhu
[
7
]
testified the boundedness of multilinear singular integrals and their commutators on Herz-Morrey spaces with non-doubling measures. In 2015, Mizuta and Ohno
[
8
]
obtained Herz-Morrey spaces
M\dot{K}_{q,p(\cdot)}^{\alpha,\lambda}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
of variable exponent, Riesz potential operators and duality.

In 1961, Benedek and Panzone
[
9
]
introduced mixed Lebesgue spaces
L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})(0<\vec{p}\leqslant\infty)
. Recently, due to the wider usefulness of mixed function spaces on partial differential equations. Mixed spaces have more applications and complex structures, so the study of mixed norm is more difficult in harmonic analysis. Results of previous studies, mixed Morrey spaces
M_{\vec{q}}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})(0\leqslant\lambda<\infty,0<\vec{q}\leqslant\infty)
were first introduced by Nogayama
[
12
]
in 2019; homogeneous mixed Herz spaces
\dot{K}_{\vec{q}}^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})(\alpha\in\mathbb{R},0<p\leqslant\infty,0<\vec{q}\leqslant\infty)
were first introduced by Wei

[
13
]
in 2021.

In this paper, mixed Herz-Morrey spaces are defined, and some properties and boundedness of operators are testified.

Below is the structure of this paper: in Section 2, we introduce homogeneous mixed Herz-Morrey spaces
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and study some of their properties. In Sections 3-4, the boundedness of a wide class of sublinear operators, fractional type operators and their commutators on homogeneous mixed Herz-Morrey spaces
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are proved.

2
Definitions and Preliminaries

In this section, some definitions and properties about homogeneous mixed Herz-Morrey spaces
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are introduced. There are some symbols as follows. Let
B_{k}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|\leqslant 2^{k}\}
and
A_{k}=B_{k}\backslash{B_{k-1}}
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Denote
\chi_{{}_{k}}=\chi_{{}_{A_{k}}}
, for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\chi_{{}_{E}}
is the characteristic function of set
E
. And throughout this paper, we use following notations. The letter
\vec{q}
will denote n-tuples of the numbers in
(0,\infty)^{n}

(n\geqslant 1)
,
\vec{q}=\left(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{n}\right)
. By definition, the inequality
0<\vec{q}<\infty
means that
0<q_{i}<\infty
for all
i
. For
\vec{q}=\left(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{n}\right)
and
k\in\mathbb{R}
, let

\frac{1}{\vec{q}}=\left(\frac{1}{q_{1}},\frac{1}{q_{2}},\dots,\frac{1}{q_{n}}\right),\quad\frac{\vec{q}}{k}=\left(\frac{q_{1}}{k},\frac{q_{2}}{k},\dots,\frac{q_{n}}{k}\right),\quad\vec{q^{\prime}}=\left(q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime},\dots,q_{n}^{\prime}\right),

where
q_{j}^{\prime}=\frac{q_{j}}{q_{j}-1}
is the conjugate exponent of
q_{j}
.
|B|
denotes the volume of the ball
B
.

Definition 2.1
.

(Mixed Lebesgue spaces)
[
9
]

Let
\vec{p}=\left(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{n}\right)\in(0,\infty]^{n}.
Then mixed Lebesgue spaces
L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined to be the set of all measurable functions
f
such that their quasi-norms

\left\|f\right\|_{\vec{p}}\equiv\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dots\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mid f(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\mid^{p_{1}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}dx_{2}\right)^{\frac{p_{3}}{p_{2}}}\dots dx_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}<\infty,

if
p_{j}=\infty,
then we have to make appropriate modifications.

Definition 2.2
.

(Mixed Morrey spaces)
[
12
]

Let
0\leqslant\lambda<\infty
,
0<\vec{q}\leqslant\infty
, where
\vec{q}=(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{n})
, then define mixed morrey spaces
M_{\vec{q}}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

M_{\vec{q}}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{f\in L_{loc}^{\vec{q}}:\|f\|_{M_{\vec{q}}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{M_{\vec{q}}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=sup~r^{-\lambda}\left\|f\chi_{k}\right\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}.

Definition 2.3
.

(Homogeneous Mixed Herz spaces)
[
13
]

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<p\leqslant\infty
,
0<\vec{q}\leqslant\infty
, where
\vec{q}=(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{n})
, then define homogeneous mixed Herz spaces
\dot{K}_{\vec{q}}^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

\dot{K}_{\vec{q}}^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{f\in L_{loc}^{\vec{q}}:\|f\|_{\dot{K}_{\vec{q}}^{\alpha,p}}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{\dot{K}_{\vec{q}}^{\alpha,p}}=\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{k\alpha p}\|f\chi_{k}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Generalize the above
M_{\vec{q}}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\dot{K}_{\vec{q}}^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to a new class of function spaces called homogeneous mixed Herz-Morrey spaces
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is defined as follows.

Definition 2.4
.

(Homogeneous Mixed Herz-Morrey spaces) Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<p<\infty
,
0\leqslant\lambda<\infty
,
1\leqslant\vec{q}<\infty
, where
\vec{q}=(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{n})
, then define homogeneous mixed Herz-Morrey spaces
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{f\in L_{loc}^{\vec{q}}:\|f\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}=\|f\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left\{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\alpha p}\|f\chi_{k}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{p}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Remark 2.1
.

As well known that
L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
\vec{p}=\left(p,p,\dots,p\right)
, so it is obvious that
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=M\dot{K}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
\vec{q}=\left(q,q,\dots,q\right)
and
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\dot{K}_{\vec{q}}^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proposition 2.1
.

Homogeneous mixed Herz-Morrey spaces
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are quasi-Banach spaces if
0<p,\vec{q}<1
,
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are Banach spaces if
1\leqslant p,\vec{q}\leqslant\infty
.

Proof.

First, it is proved that
\|\cdot\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
in homogeneous mixed Herz-Morrey spaces
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The positivity and the homogeneity are clear. Therefore, the most important thing is to check the quasi-triangle inequality. Without losing the generality, it is assumed that
1\leqslant p
.

\displaystyle\|f+g\|_{M\dot{K}^{\alpha,\lambda}_{p,\vec{q}}}

\displaystyle=\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left\{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\alpha p}\|(f+g)\chi_{k}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{p}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left\{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\alpha p}\|f\chi_{k}+g\chi_{k}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{p}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\alpha p}\left(max(1,2^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-q_{i}}{qi}})\left(\|f\chi_{k}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}+\|g\chi_{k}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\right)\right)^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant max(1,2^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-q_{i}}{qi}})\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\alpha p}2^{p-1}\left(\|f\chi_{k}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{p}+\|g\chi_{k}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{p}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant max(1,2^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-q_{i}}{qi}})2^{1-p}2^{p-1}\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left[\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\alpha p}\|f\chi_{k}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\alpha p}\|g\chi_{k}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right]

\quad~\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\leqslant max(1,2^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-q_{i}}{qi}})(\|f\|_{M\dot{K}^{\alpha,\lambda}_{p,\vec{q}}}+\|g\|_{M\dot{K}^{\alpha,\lambda}_{p,\vec{q}}}).

Then, when
1\leqslant q_{i}\leqslant\infty
for any
i=1,2,\dots,n
,
\|\cdot\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is obtained. When
0<q_{i}<1
for exists
i=1,2,\dots,n
, it is a quasi-norm. Next, we will display the completeness of space
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).~

Let a Cauchy sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
satisfied that

\|f_{j+1}-f_{j}\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}\leqslant 2^{-j}.

Let
f(x)=f_{1}(x)+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(f_{j+1}(x)-f_{j}(x)\right)=\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}f_{j}(x)
, then
f\in M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\left\|f_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(f_{j+1}-f_{j}\right)\right\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|f_{1}\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}+\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(f_{j+1}-f_{j}\right)\right\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|f_{1}\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|f_{j+1}-f_{j}\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left(\|f_{1}\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j}\right)\leqslant C.

Furthermore,
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
converge to
f\in M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\displaystyle\|f-f_{J}\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\left\|f_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(f_{j+1}-f_{j}\right)-f_{J}\right\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(f_{j+1}-f_{j}\right)-f_{J}-\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}\left(f_{j+1}-f_{j}\right)-f_{J}\right\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\left(f_{j+1}-f_{j}\right)\right\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{1-J}.

Then

\lim\limits_{J\rightarrow\infty}\|f-f_{J}\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}=0.

Thus,
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are quasi-Banach spaces.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\alpha,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\dots+\alpha_{n}
,
0<p<\infty
,
0\leqslant\lambda<\infty
,
1\leqslant\vec{q}<\infty
, where
\vec{q}=(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{n})
, then

M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}=M^{\lambda}_{\vec{q}~(\mathbb{R}^{n},|x_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}q_{1}}\times|x_{2}|^{\alpha_{2}q_{2}}\dots\times|x_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}q_{n}})}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\alpha q_{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dots\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f\chi_{k}(x)|^{q_{1}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}}dx_{2}\right)^{\frac{q_{3}}{q_{2}}}\dots dx_{n}\right)^{\frac{q_{n}}{q_{n}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{n}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\alpha q_{n}}\left(\int_{2^{k-1}\leqslant|x_{n}|<2^{k}}\dots\left(\int_{2^{k-1}\leqslant|x_{1}|<2^{k}}|f(x)|^{q_{1}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}}\dots dx_{n}\right)^{\frac{q_{n}}{q_{n}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{n}}}

\displaystyle=\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}\left(\int_{2^{k-1}\leqslant|x_{n}|<2^{k}}\dots\left(\int_{2^{k-1}\leqslant|x_{1}|<2^{k}}|f(x)|^{q_{1}}2^{k\alpha_{1}q_{1}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}}\dots 2^{k\alpha_{n}q_{n}}dx_{n}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{n}}}

\displaystyle\sim\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}\left(\int_{2^{k-1}\leqslant|x_{n}|<2^{k}}\dots\left(\int_{2^{k-1}\leqslant|x_{1}|<2^{k}}|f(x)|^{q_{1}}|x_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}q_{1}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}}\dots|x_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}q_{n}}dx_{n}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{n}}}

\displaystyle=\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dots\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{q_{1}}|x_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}q_{1}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}}\dots|x_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}q_{n}}dx_{n}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{n}}}

\displaystyle=M^{\lambda}_{\vec{q}~(\mathbb{R}^{n},|x_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}q_{1}}\times|x_{2}|^{\alpha_{2}q_{2}}\dots\times|x_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}q_{n}})}.

∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<p<\infty
,
0\leqslant\lambda<\infty
,
1\leqslant\vec{q}<\infty
, where
\vec{q}=(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{n})
. The following inclusions are valid.

(1)
If
p_{1}\leqslant p_{2}
, then
M\dot{K}_{p_{1},\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset M\dot{K}_{p_{2},\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(2)
If
\alpha_{2}\leqslant\alpha_{1}
, then
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha_{1},\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha_{2},\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(3)
If
\vec{q_{{}_{1}}}\leqslant\vec{q_{{}_{2}}}
, then
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}_{2}}^{\alpha+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\frac{1}{q_{1i}}-\frac{1}{q_{2i}}),\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}_{1}}^{\alpha+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\frac{1}{q_{1i}}-\frac{1}{q_{2i}}),\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(1) First, note the obvious inequality,

\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|\right)^{r}\leqslant\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{r}~(0<r\leqslant 1,a_{k}\in\mathbb{R}).

The following result can be concluded through this inequality

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M\dot{K}_{p_{2},\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}\left(2^{k\alpha}\|f\chi_{k}\|_{\vec{q}}\right)^{p_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}\left(2^{k\alpha}\|f\chi_{k}\|_{\vec{q}}\right)^{p_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\leqslant\|f\|_{M\dot{K}_{p_{1},\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}.

(2)

\displaystyle||f||_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha_{2},\lambda}}

\displaystyle=2^{k(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})}\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\alpha_{1}p}||f\widetilde{\chi}_{k}||_{\vec{q}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant C||f||_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha_{1},\lambda}}.

(3) Using the H
\ddot{o}
lder inequality and condition (2),

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}_{1}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\alpha p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dots\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f\chi_{k}(x)|^{q_{11}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{q_{12}}{q_{11}}}dx_{2}\right)^{\frac{q_{13}}{q_{12}}}\dots dx_{n}\right)^{\frac{p}{q_{1n}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\alpha p}\left(\int_{2^{k-1}\leqslant|x_{n}|<2^{k}}\dots\left(\int_{2^{k-1}\leqslant|x_{1}|<2^{k}}|f(x)|^{q_{11}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{q_{12}}{q_{11}}}\dots dx_{n}\right)^{\frac{p}{q_{1n}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathop{sup}\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k(\alpha+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\frac{1}{q_{1i}}-\frac{1}{q_{2i}})p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dots\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f\chi_{k}(x)|^{q_{21}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{q_{22}}{q_{21}}}dx_{2}\right)^{\frac{q_{23}}{q_{22}}}\dots dx_{n}\right)^{\frac{p}{q_{2n}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|f\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}_{2}}^{\alpha+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\frac{1}{q_{1i}}-\frac{1}{q_{2i}}),\lambda}}.

∎

3
Boundedness of Sublinear Operators and Fractional Type Operators in homogeneous mixed Herz-Morrey spaces

In this section, the boundedness of the operators is obtained by decomposing the function.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
0\leqslant\lambda<\infty
,
0<p<\infty
,
1<\vec{q}<\infty
and
-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}+\lambda<\alpha<n\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}\right),
suppose sublinear operators
T
satisfied that

(1)

T
is bounded on
L^{\vec{q}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right);

(2)
for suitable functions
f
with
suppf\subseteq A_{k}
and
|x|\geqslant 2^{k+1}
with
k\in\mathbb{Z},

\mid Tf(x)\mid\leqslant C\frac{\|f\|_{L^{1}}}{|x|^{n}};

(1)

(3)
for suitable functions
f
with
suppf\subseteq A_{k}
and
|x|\leqslant 2^{k-2}
with
k\in\mathbb{Z},

\mid Tf(x)\mid\leqslant C\frac{\|f\|_{L^{1}}}{2^{nk}}.

(2)

Then
T
is also bounded on
M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proof.

Assuming
f\in M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

f(x)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\chi_{j}(x)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{j}(x).

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sup_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left\{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}\sum_{j=k_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{-(j-k)\left(\alpha+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}+\lambda\right)p/2}\sum_{l=-\infty}^{j}2^{l\alpha p}\left\|f_{l}\right\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{p}\right\}^{1/p}

\displaystyle=C\sup_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left\{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}\sum_{j=k_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{-(j-k)\left(\alpha+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}+\lambda\right)p/2}2^{j\lambda p}\left[2^{-j\lambda}\left(\sum_{l=-\infty}^{j}2^{l\alpha p}\left\|f_{l}\right\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\right]^{p}\right\}^{1/p}

\displaystyle=C\leqslant C\|f\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}\sup_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left\{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\lambda p}\sum_{j=k_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{-(j-k)\left(\alpha+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\lambda\right)p/2}\right\}^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|f\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}\mathop{sup}\limits_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k_{0}\lambda}\left\{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{k\lambda p}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

~~~~\leqslant C\|f\|_{M\dot{K}_{p,\vec{q}}^{\alpha,\lambda}}.

That is why

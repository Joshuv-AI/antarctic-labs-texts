Weak Freiman isomorphisms and sequencings of small sets
Simone Costa, Stefano Della Fiore

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a weakening of the Freiman isomorphisms between subsets of non necessarily abelian groups. Inspired by the breakthrough result of Kravitz, [ 14 ] , on cyclic groups, as a first application, we prove that any subset of size k k of the dihedral group D 2 ​ m D_{2m} (and, more in general, of a class of semidirect products) is sequenceable, provided that the prime factors of m m are larger than k ! k! . Also, a refined bound of k ! / 2 k!/2 for the size of the prime factors of m m can be obtained for cyclic groups ℤ m \mathbb{Z}_{m} , slightly improving the result of [ 14 ] . Then, applying again the concept of weak Freiman isomorphism, we show that any subset of size k k of the dicyclic group Dic m \mathrm{Dic}_{m} is sequenceable, provided that the prime factors of m m are larger than k k k^{k} .

Introduction

A subset of a group is
sequenceable
if there is an ordering
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
of its elements such that the partial sums
(s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{k})
, given by
s_{0}=0
and
s_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
, are distinct, with the possible exception that we may have
s_{k}=s_{0}=0
; such orderings will be called
sequencings
of the subset. Note that, even if we are using the additive notation, we are not assuming that the group is abelian.

Starting from Graham and Erdős (see
[
12
]
and
[
11
]
), several conjectures and questions concerning the sequenceability of subsets of abelian groups appear in the literature, which have been combined and summarized (see
[
2
]
and
[
8
]
) into the conjecture that if a subset of an abelian group does not contain 0 then it is sequenceable. For related conjectures and applications of these results, we also refer to the papers
[
1
]
and
[
3
]
, and to the surveys
[
18
]
and
[
16
]
.
Even though these papers are more focused on abelian groups, also the case of dihedral ones (and, more in general, semidirect products) have been studied by Ollis in
[
17
]
and by Costa, Della Fiore, and Ollis in
[
7
]
.

In
[
14
]
, Kravitz, prove the breakthrough result that any subset
S
of size
k
of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}\setminus\{0\}
is sequenceable provided that the prime factors of
m
are larger than
(k-1)^{k+1}
. Before his paper, the literature was concentrated on cases where either the group or the set is smaller than a given constant (see, for instance,
[
13
]
,
[
8
]
or
[
9
]
) or where the partial sums were compared only locally (see
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
). Kravitz, in his paper, applies the so-called rectification method: he embeds the elements of
S
in
\mathbb{Z}
through a Freiman isomorphism and then he proves its sequenceability result on
\mathbb{Z}
.

Here we show that by considering a weakening of the Freiman isomorphism method it is possible to generalize this procedure to obtain the same result for the dihedral group
D_{2m}
(and, more in general, for a class of semidirect products) provided that the prime factors of
m
are larger than
k!
.
Also, a refined bound of
k!/2
for the size of the prime factors of
m
can be obtained for cyclic groups
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, slightly improving the result of
[
14
]
.

Then, following the line of the proof of Theorem 3.1 of
[
4
]
we show that any subset of size

of the dicyclic group
\mathrm{Dic}_{m}
is weak Freiman isomorphic to a subset of the semidirect product
\mathbb{Z}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{Z}_{4}
(where
\varphi(1)=-id
) provided that the prime factors of

are larger than
k^{k}
. It will follow that, under the same assumption, any subset of size
k
of the dicyclic group
\mathrm{Dic}_{m}
is sequenceable.

2
Weak Freiman isomorphisms

The rectification method used by Kravitz in
[
14
]
relies on the concept of Freiman isomorphism. Following
[
19
]
here, and in all the paper, we will use the additive notation even if the groups are not assumed to be abelian (if not specified).
We recall the following definition (see Tao and Vu,
[
19
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\ell\geq 1
, and let
S
,
S^{\prime}
be sets with ambient groups
(V,+_{V})
and
(W,+_{W})
respectively. A Freiman homomorphism of order

\ell
, say
\phi
, from
(S,V)
to
(S^{\prime},W)
(or more succinctly from
S
to
S^{\prime}
) is a map
\phi:S\rightarrow S^{\prime}
with the property that:

a_{1}+_{V}a_{2}+_{V}\dots+_{V}a_{\ell}=a_{1}^{\prime}+_{V}a_{2}^{\prime}+_{V}\dots+_{V}a_{\ell}^{\prime}

implies

\phi(a_{1})+_{W}\phi(a_{2})+_{W}\dots+_{W}\phi(a_{\ell})=\phi(a_{1}^{\prime})+_{W}\phi(a_{2}^{\prime})+_{W}\dots+_{W}\phi(a_{\ell}^{\prime}).

If in addition there is an inverse map
\phi^{-1}:S^{\prime}\rightarrow S
which is also a Freiman homomorphism of order
\ell
from
(S^{\prime},W)
to
(S,V)
, then we say that
\phi
is a Freiman isomorphism of order
\ell
and that
(S,V)
and
(S^{\prime},W)
are Freiman isomorphic of order
\ell
.

In case
V=\mathbb{Z}_{m}
and
W=\mathbb{Z}
, it is knowns that (see
[
15
]
) such isomorphism exists provided that the prime factors of
m
are large enough (i.e.
\ell^{k}
).

Inspired by this concept, and to adapt the rectification procedure to the case of semidirect products, we introduce the following weakening.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
k\geq 1
, and let
S
,
S^{\prime}
be sets with ambient groups
(V,+_{V})
and
(W,+_{W})
respectively. A weak Freiman homomorphism of order

k
, say
\phi
, from
(S,V)
to
(S^{\prime},W)
(or more succinctly from
S
to
S^{\prime}
) is a map
\phi:S\rightarrow S^{\prime}
with the property that, for any ordered subset
(a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell})
of cardinality
\ell\leq k
of
A
:

a_{1}+_{V}a_{2}+_{V}\dots+_{V}a_{\ell}=0\rightarrow\phi(a_{1})+_{W}\phi(a_{2})+_{W}\dots+_{W}\phi(a_{\ell})=0.

If in addition there is an inverse map
\phi^{-1}:S^{\prime}\rightarrow S
which is also a weak Freiman homomorphism of order
k
from
(S^{\prime},W)
to
(S,V)
, then we say that
\phi
is a weak Freiman isomorphism of order
k
and that
(S,V)
and
(S^{\prime},W)
are Freiman weakly isomorphic of order
k
.

In case
k=|S|
, we will simply say that
\phi
is a weak Freiman homomorphism (resp. isomorphism).

Roughly speaking a weak Freiman homomorphism only preserves the sets which sum to zero in a given order. A Freiman homomorphism instead preserves all the equality between sequences sums and in particular it allows to have repeated elements in the sum. Note that, if we assume
0\in S
, a Freiman homomorphism also preserves the sequences that sum to zero.

In the following, we consider groups of the form
(\mathbb{Z}_{m}\rtimes_{\varphi}H,+)
where
H
is finite,
(H,+_{H})
is not necessarily abelian and where
\varphi
is a group homomorphism
\varphi:H\rightarrow Aut(\mathbb{Z}_{m})
. We will also assume that the homomorphism
\varphi
satisfies the following property:

(*)

given
h\in H,\varphi(h)
is either the identity or
\varphi(h)(x)=-x
for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
.

Note that the latter is an automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
.

Remark 2.3
.

Two classes of semidirect product that satisfy property
(*)
are the dihedral groups and the standard direct product.
Indeed:

1)

The dihedral group
D_{2m}
(called also
Dih(\mathbb{Z}_{m})
) can be defined as
\mathbb{Z}_{m}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
where

\varphi(0)=id\mbox{ and }\varphi(1)(x)=-x.

This group can be defined also on
\mathbb{Z}
. In this case, we will denote it by
Dih(\mathbb{Z})
.

2)

Any direct product
\mathbb{Z}_{m}\times H
can be defined as
\mathbb{Z}_{m}\rtimes_{\varphi}H
where

\varphi(h)=id\mbox{ for any }h\in H.

Here, following the proof of Theorem 22 of
[
8
]
(see also Theorem 3.14 of
[
7
]
, and Theorem 3.1 of
[
10
]
where an explicit bound was not provided), we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
H
be a finite group, let
\varphi
satisfy property
(*)
. Then any subset
S
of size
k
of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}\rtimes_{\varphi}H\setminus\{0\}
is Freiman weakly isomorphic to a subset
S^{\prime}
of size
k
of
\mathbb{Z}\rtimes_{\varphi}H\setminus\{0\}
provided that the prime factors of

are all greater than
k!
.

First, we need to recall some elementary facts about the solutions of a linear system over fields and commutative rings.

Theorem 2.5

(Corollary of Cramer’s Theorem)
.

Let

\begin{cases}m_{1,1}x_{1}+\dots+m_{1,k}x_{k}=b_{1}\\
\dots\\
m_{l,1}x_{1}+\dots+m_{l,k}x_{k}=b_{l}\end{cases}

(1)

be a linear system over a commutative ring
R
whose associated matrix
M=(m_{i,j})
is an
l\times k
matrix over
R
. Let
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
be a solution of the system and let
M^{\prime}
be a
k^{\prime}\times k^{\prime}
square, nonsingular (i.e.
\det(M^{\prime})
is invertible in
R
), submatrix of
M
. We assume, without loss of generality, that
M^{\prime}
is obtained by considering the first
k^{\prime}
rows and the first
k^{\prime}
columns of
M

\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{k^{\prime}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}m_{1,1}&m_{1,2}&m_{1,3}&\dots&m_{1,k^{\prime}}\\
m_{2,1}&m_{2,2}&m_{2,3}&\dots&m_{2,k^{\prime}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
m_{k^{\prime},1}&m_{k^{\prime},2}&m_{k^{\prime},3}&\dots&m_{k^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}b_{1}-(m_{1,k^{\prime}+1}x_{k^{\prime}+1}+\dots+m_{1,k}x_{k})\\
b_{2}-(m_{2,k^{\prime}+1}x_{k^{\prime}+1}+\dots++m_{2,k}x_{k})\\
\vdots\\
b_{k^{\prime}}-(m_{k^{\prime},k^{\prime}+1}x_{k^{\prime}+1}+\dots+m_{k^{\prime},k}x_{k})\end{bmatrix}.

Equivalently, set

M^{\prime}_{j}:=\begin{bmatrix}m_{1,1}&m_{1,2}&\dots&m_{1,j-1}&b_{1}-(m_{1,k^{\prime}+1}x_{k^{\prime}+1}+\dots+m_{1,k}x_{k})&\dots&m_{1,k^{\prime}}\\
m_{2,1}&m_{2,2}&\dots&m_{2,j-1}&b_{2}-(m_{2,k^{\prime}+1}x_{k^{\prime}+1}+\dots++m_{2,k}x_{k})&\dots&m_{2,k^{\prime}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
m_{k^{\prime},1}&m_{k^{\prime},2}&\dots&m_{k^{\prime},j-1}&b_{k^{\prime}}-(m_{k^{\prime},k^{\prime}+1}x_{k^{\prime}+1}+\dots+m_{k^{\prime},k}x_{k})&\dots&m_{k^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\end{bmatrix}

x_{j}=\frac{\det(M^{\prime}_{j})}{\det(M^{\prime})}.

(2)

Now assume that
R
is a field and that the system (

of

, and fixed

satisfy the relation (

).

Now we are ready to prove our Theorem

Let

Given a subset

x_{\omega(1)}+x_{\omega(2)}\dots x_{\omega(t-1)}+x_{\omega(t)}=0.

(3)

Here
S
is a solution

to the system of equations derived from (

) by varing the subset

In the following, for simplicity, we will denote

3
Sequencing small sets

3.1
Semidirect products

The idea of this subsection is to adapt the rectification procedure of
[
14
]
by using weak Freiman isomorphism to obtain sequenceability in semidirect products (and, in particular in dihedral groups) and to improve the bounds of
[
14
]
in the case of cyclic groups. Indeed Kravitz proved that

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
S
be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Then
S
is sequenceable.

As a consequence, he proved that any subset of size
k
of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}\setminus\{0\}
is sequenceable provided that the prime factors of
m
are larger than
(k-1)^{k+1}
. The first application of the weak Freiman isomorphism method we present is that we can slightly improve this result as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
S
be a set of size
k
of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}\setminus\{0\}
. Then
S
is sequenceable provided that the prime factors of
m
are larger than
k!/2
.

Proof.

Under this hypothesis,
S=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
is Freiman weakly isomorphic to a subset
S^{\prime}=\{\phi(x_{1}),\dots,\phi(x_{k})\}
of
\mathbb{Z}
due to Theorem
2.6
.
Due to Theorem
3.1
,
S^{\prime}
is sequenceable and hence there exists
\omega
so that
(\phi(x_{\omega(1)}),\dots,\phi(x_{\omega(k)}))
is a sequencing.

Here we prove that
(x_{\omega(1)},\dots,x_{\omega(k)})
is also a sequencing.
Indeed,
s_{i}=s_{j}
means
x_{\omega(i+1)}+x_{\omega(i+2)}+\dots+x_{\omega(j)}=0
, it would imply that
\phi(x_{\omega(i+1)})+\phi(x_{\omega(i+2)})+\dots+\phi(x_{\omega(j)})=0
. This is not possible because the partial sums
s_{i}
and
s_{j}
in the sequencing of
S^{\prime}
are distinct. It follows that also
(x_{\omega(1)},\dots,x_{\omega(k)})
is a sequencing.
∎

Finally, we apply this procedure to the case of semidirect products. Following
[
8
]
, we will call
strongly sequenceable
a group whose subsets that do not contain zero are all sequenceable.
First of all we prove the following result for
\mathbb{Z}\rtimes_{\varphi}H
:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
H
be an abelian strongly sequenceable group and let
S
be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}\rtimes_{\varphi}H\setminus\{0\}
. Then
S
is sequenceable.

Proof.

We recall that, for any
h\in H
we have that either
\varphi(h)=id\mbox{ or }\varphi(h)(x)=-x
and that
\varphi(0)=id
. Here we divide
S
into the following four sets:

•

Z:=\{(0,g)\in S:\varphi(g)=id\}
;

•

P:=\{(x,h)\in S:\varphi(h)=id\mbox{ and }x>0\}
;

•

N:=\{(x,h)\in S:\varphi(h)=id\mbox{ and }x<0\}
;

•

F:=\{(y,f)\in S;\varphi(f)=-id\}
.

Also, we may assume that the size of
F
is
\ell\geq 1
otherwise
S
is morally a subset of
\mathbb{Z}\times H
. More precisely,
S
is Freiman isomorphic to a subset of
\mathbb{Z}\times H
and is then sequenceable due to the result of
[
14
]
and because
H
is abelian.

Now we consider three cases:

CASE
1
:
P=N=Z=\emptyset
.
First, we assume that the projection of
S
over
\mathbb{Z}
is constant. In this case, the projection of
S
over
H
is a set. Here if
\pi_{H}(S)
does not contain
0
,
S
is sequenceable since
\pi_{H}(S)
is sequenceable. On the other hand, if we have an element of type
(y,0)\in S
, we can consider a sequencing

((y_{2},f_{2}),(y_{3},f_{3}),\dots,(y_{\ell},f_{\ell}))

of
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{(y,0)\}
and place
(y,0)
at the beginning of this ordering. It is easy to check that

((y,0),(y_{2},f_{2}),(y_{3},f_{3}),\dots,(y_{\ell},f_{\ell}))

is a sequencing of
S
.

Therefore, we may assume that the projection over
\mathbb{Z}
of
F
contains at least two different elements (i.e. not all the
y_{i}
are equal).
In this case, we order the elements of
F
as follows

((y_{2},f_{2}),(y_{3},f_{3}),\dots,(y_{\ell-1},f_{\ell-1}),(y_{\ell},f_{\ell}),(y_{1},f_{1}))

where

\dots\leq y_{7}\leq y_{5}\leq y_{3}\leq y_{1}\leq y_{2}\leq y_{4}\leq y_{6}\leq\dots

and set

F_{1}:=\{(y_{i},f_{i})\in F:i>1,\ y_{i}=y_{i-1}=y_{1}\}

we have that
F_{1}
is either empty or, denoted by
\ell_{1}=|F_{1}\cup\{(y_{1},f_{1})\}|
,
(y_{2},f_{2}),\dots,(y_{\ell_{1}},f_{\ell_{1}})
is a sequencing of
F_{1}
.

We note that

\sum_{x\in F_{1}}x=\sum_{i=2}^{\ell_{1}}(y_{i},f_{i})=(y_{2}-y_{3}+y_{4}-y_{5}+\dots,\sum_{i=2}^{\ell_{1}}f_{i})=

(y_{1}-y_{1}+y_{1}-y_{1}+\dots,\sum_{i=2}^{\ell_{1}}f_{i})=\left(\frac{y_{1}+(-1)^{\ell_{1}}y_{1}}{2},\sum_{i=2}^{\ell_{1}}f_{i}\right)

(5)

does not depend on the ordering of the elements of
F_{1}
(the second component is invariant since

is abelian).
It follows that, up to interchange
(y_{1},f_{1})
with any elements in
F_{1}
, we may assume that:

\sum_{x\in F_{1}}x\not=0.

(6)

Here we have that
(y_{\ell},f_{\ell})\not\in F_{1}
since otherwise any elements of
F
would have
y_{1}
as a first component. On the other hand, note that

could equal
y_{1}
.

If
|F_{1}|\geq 2
, we have at least two choices for
(y_{1},f_{1})
so that Equation (
6
) holds true. At least one of this choice is such that
(y_{1},f_{1})+(y_{\ell},f_{\ell})\not=0
.

If we have that
|F_{1}|=0
then we must have
y_{1}\not=y_{\ell}
and hence also in this case we have that
(y_{1},f_{1})+(y_{\ell},f_{\ell})\not=0
.
Finally, if
|F_{1}|=1
(i.e.
F_{1}=\{(y_{2},f_{2})\}
), Equation (
6

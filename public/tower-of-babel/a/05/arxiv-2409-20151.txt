Holographic RG flows and Janus interfaces from ISO(3) × \times U(1) F ⁡ ( 4 ) F(4) gauged supergravity


Abstract
We study six-dimensional F ⁡ ( 4 ) F(4) gauged supergravity coupled to four vector multiplets with a non-semisimple I ​ S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) × U ⁡ ( 1 ) ISO(3)\times U(1) gauge group. This gauged supergravity arises from a consistent truncation of type IIB string theory on S 2 × Σ S^{2}\times\Sigma with Σ \Sigma being a Riemann surface. We find that the gauged supergravity admits two S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) symmetric A ​ d ​ S 6 AdS_{6} vacua, one with N = ( 1 , 1 ) N=(1,1) supersymmetry and the other one non-supersymmetric. We compute all scalar masses at these critical points and find that only the supersymmetric A ​ d ​ S 6 AdS_{6} vacuum is stable. By truncating to S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) × U ⁡ ( 1 ) SO(3)\times U(1) and S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) × U ⁡ ( 1 ) SO(2)\times U(1) invariant sectors, we study holographic RG flow solutions from this critical point to a number of non-conformal phases of the dual N = 2 N=2 SCFT in five dimensions. Finally, we give some examples of supersymmetric Janus solutions describing conformal interfaces within the five-dimensional SCFT. All of these solutions provide first holographic results from matter-coupled F ⁡ ( 4 ) F(4) gauged supergravity with non-semisimple gauge groups.

In the present paper, we will consider another matter-coupled

We will first study supersymmetric

F(4)

2
Matter-coupled
F(4)
gauged supergravity in six dimensions

In this section, we give a review of matter-coupled
F(4)
gauged supergravity in six dimensions constructed in
[
20
]
and
[
21
]
. Although the construction given in
[
20
]
and
[
21
]
mainly focuses on compact gauge groups of the form
SU(2)\times G_{c}
, the formulation is valid for other types of gauge groups with appropriate modifications in some of the formulae, see also
[
34
]
.

The matter-coupled
F(4)
gauged supergravity consists of the supergravity and vector multiplets with the following field contents

\left(e^{\hat{\mu}}_{\mu},\psi^{A}_{\mu},A^{\alpha}_{\mu},B_{\mu\nu},\chi^{A},\sigma\right)\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad(A_{\mu},\lambda_{A},\phi^{\alpha})^{I},\qquad I=1,2,\ldots,n

(1)

with
n
being the number of vector multiplets. We use the metric signature
(-+++++)
with space-time and flat indices denoted respectively by
\mu,\nu=0,\ldots,5
and
\hat{\mu},\hat{\nu}=0,\ldots,5
. Indices
A,B,\ldots=1,2
correspond to the fundamental representation of
SU(2)_{R}\sim USp(2)_{R}\sim SO(3)_{R}
R-symmetry while indices
\alpha,\beta=(0,r)
correspond to singlet,
0
, and adjoint indices,
r,s,\ldots=1,2,3
, of
SU(2)_{R}
.

The bosonic fields are given by the graviton
e^{\hat{\mu}}_{\mu}
, a two-form field
B_{\mu\nu}
,
n+4
vector fields
A^{\Lambda}=(A^{\alpha}_{\mu},A^{I}_{\mu})
,
\Lambda=(\alpha,I)
, and
1+4n
scalars parametrizing the scalar manifold

\mathbb{R}^{+}\times SO(4,n)/SO(4)\times SO(n)\,.

(2)

The
\mathbb{R}^{+}
factor corresponds to the dilaton
\sigma
while the
4n
scalars from the vector multiplets
\phi^{\alpha I}
are encoded in the
SO(4,n)/SO(4)\times SO(n)
coset representative

{L^{\Lambda}}_{\underline{\Sigma}}=({L^{\Lambda}}_{\alpha},{L^{\Lambda}}_{I}).

(3)

The inverse of
{L^{\Lambda}}_{\underline{\Sigma}}
will be denoted by
{(L^{-1})^{\underline{\Lambda}}}_{\Sigma}=({(L^{-1})^{\alpha}}_{\Sigma},{(L^{-1})^{I}}_{\Sigma})
. Finally, the fermionic fields are given by two gravitini
\psi^{A}_{\mu}
, two spin-
\frac{1}{2}
fields
\chi^{A}
, and
2n
gaugini
\lambda^{I}_{A}
.

As shown in
[
20
]
and
[
21
]
, there are two types of supersymmetric deformations namely the usual gauging of a subgroup
G_{0}
of the global symmetry
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times SO(4,n)
and a massive deformation leading to a mass term for the two-from field. Any consistent gauging is implemented by the gauge structure constant
{f_{\Lambda\Sigma}}^{\Delta}
appearing in the
G_{0}
algebra

\left[T_{\Lambda},T_{\Sigma}\right]={f_{\Lambda\Sigma}}^{\Delta}T_{\Delta}

(4)

with
T_{\Lambda}
being gauge generators. In particular, the gauge covariant derivative of the coset representative is given by

\nabla_{\mu}{L^{\Sigma}}_{\underline{\Delta}}=\partial_{\mu}{L^{\Sigma}}_{\underline{\Delta}}-f^{\phantom{\Gamma}\Sigma}_{\Gamma\phantom{\Sigma}\Lambda}A^{\Gamma}_{\mu}{L^{\Lambda}}_{\underline{\Delta}}

(5)

with
f^{\phantom{\Gamma}\Sigma}_{\Gamma\phantom{\Sigma}\Lambda}
related to
{f_{\Gamma\Sigma}}^{\Lambda}
via
f^{\phantom{\Gamma}\Sigma}_{\Gamma\phantom{\Sigma}\Lambda}={f_{\Gamma\Delta}}^{\Pi}\eta_{\Pi\Lambda}\eta^{\Sigma\Delta}
.
SO(4,n)
indices
\Lambda,\Sigma,\ldots
are raised and lowered by
SO(4,n)
invariant tensor

\eta^{\Lambda\Sigma}=\eta_{\Lambda\Sigma}=(\delta_{\alpha\beta},-\delta_{IJ}).

(6)

We also emphasize that supersymmetry requires
{f_{\Lambda\Sigma}}^{\Gamma}
to satisfy
f_{\Lambda\Gamma\Sigma}={f_{\Lambda\Gamma}}^{\Delta}\eta_{\Delta\Sigma}=f_{[\Lambda\Gamma\Sigma]}
.

In this paper, we will consider only vacua, holographic RG flows and supersymmetric Janus solutions which involve only the metric and scalars. Accordingly, we will set all the other fields to zero in order to further simplify various subsequent formulae. With only scalars and the metric non-vanishing, the bosonic Lagrangian of the matter-coupled
F(4)
gauged supergravity can be written as

\displaystyle e^{-1}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}R-\partial_{\mu}\sigma\partial^{\mu}\sigma-\frac{1}{4}P^{I\alpha}_{\mu}P^{\mu}_{I\alpha}-V

(7)

with
e=\sqrt{-g}
. The kinetic term for scalars
\phi^{\alpha I}
is written in terms of the vielbein
P^{I\alpha}_{\mu}
on
SO(4,n)/SO(4)\times SO(n)
coset. This can be obtained from the left-invariant 1-form

{\Omega^{\underline{\Lambda}}}_{\underline{\Sigma}}={(L^{-1})^{\underline{\Lambda}}}_{\Pi}\nabla{L^{\Pi}}_{\underline{\Sigma}}

(8)

by the following identification

P^{I}_{\phantom{s}\alpha}=(P^{I}_{\phantom{a}0},P^{I}_{\phantom{a}r})=(\Omega^{I}_{\phantom{a}0},\Omega^{I}_{\phantom{a}r}).

(9)

The other components
(\Omega^{rs},\Omega^{r0},\Omega^{IJ})
lead to the
SO(4)\times SO(n)
composite connections.

The scalar potential is given by

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e^{2\sigma}\left[\frac{1}{36}A^{2}+\frac{1}{4}B^{i}B_{i}+\frac{1}{4}\left(C^{I}_{\phantom{s}t}C_{It}+4D^{I}_{\phantom{s}t}D_{It}\right)\right]+m^{2}e^{-6\sigma}\mathcal{N}_{00}

(10)

\displaystyle-me^{-2\sigma}\left[\frac{2}{3}AL_{00}-2B^{i}L_{0i}\right]

with
\mathcal{N}_{00}
being the
00

\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda\Sigma}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda\Sigma}=L_{\Lambda\alpha}{(L^{-1})^{\alpha}}_{\Sigma}-L_{\Lambda I}{(L^{-1})^{I}}_{\Sigma}=(\eta LL^{T}\eta)_{\Lambda\Sigma}\,.

(11)

It should be noted that this implies
\mathcal{N}_{\Lambda\Sigma}=\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma\Lambda}
. The fermion-shift matrices are defined as follows

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{rst}K_{rst},\qquad B^{i}=\epsilon^{ijk}K_{jk0},

(12)

\displaystyle C^{\phantom{ts}t}_{I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{trs}K_{rIs},\qquad D_{It}=K_{0It}

(13)

where the “dressed” or “boosted” structure constants are given by

\displaystyle K_{rs\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{\phantom{\Lambda}\Gamma}_{\Lambda\phantom{\Gamma}\Sigma}{L^{\Lambda}}_{r}(L^{-1})_{s\Gamma}{L^{\Sigma}}_{\alpha},

\displaystyle K_{\alpha It}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{\phantom{\Lambda}\Gamma}_{\Lambda\phantom{\Gamma}\Sigma}{L^{\Lambda}}_{\alpha}(L^{-1})_{I\Gamma}{L^{\Sigma}}_{t}

(14)

with
\alpha=(0,r)

f^{\phantom{\Lambda}\Gamma}_{\Lambda\phantom{\Gamma}\Sigma}
. The constant
m
corresponds to the aforementioned massive deformation.

Finally, supersymmetry transformations for fermionic fields are given by

\displaystyle\delta\psi_{\mu A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\epsilon_{A}-\frac{1}{24}\left(Ae^{\sigma}+6me^{-3\sigma}(L^{-1})_{00}\right)\epsilon_{AB}\gamma_{\mu}\epsilon^{B}

(15)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}\left(B_{t}e^{\sigma}-2me^{-3\sigma}(L^{-1})_{t0}\right)\gamma^{7}\sigma^{t}_{AB}\gamma_{\mu}\epsilon^{B},

\displaystyle\delta\chi_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\sigma\epsilon_{AB}\epsilon^{B}+\frac{1}{24}\left[Ae^{\sigma}-18me^{-3\sigma}(L^{-1})_{00}\right]\epsilon_{AB}\epsilon^{B}

(16)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}\left[B_{t}e^{\sigma}+6me^{-3\sigma}(L^{-1})_{t0}\right]\gamma^{7}\sigma^{t}_{AB}\epsilon^{B},

\displaystyle\delta\lambda^{I}_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{I}_{ri}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{i}\sigma^{r}_{\phantom{s}AB}\epsilon^{B}+P^{I}_{0i}\gamma^{7}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{i}\epsilon_{AB}\epsilon^{B}-\left(2i\gamma^{7}D^{I}_{\phantom{s}t}+C^{I}_{\phantom{s}t}\right)e^{\sigma}\sigma^{t}_{AB}\epsilon^{B}

(17)

\displaystyle+2me^{-3\sigma}(L^{-1})^{I}_{\phantom{ss}0}\gamma^{7}\epsilon_{AB}\epsilon^{B}

with
\sigma^{rA}_{\phantom{sd}B}
being Pauli matrices and
\epsilon_{AB}=-\epsilon_{BA}
. The
SU(2)
fundamental indices
A,B,\ldots
are raised and lowered by
\epsilon^{AB}
and
\epsilon_{AB}
with the convention
T^{A}=\epsilon^{AB}T_{B}

.

The covariant derivative of
\epsilon_{A}
is defined by

D_{\mu}\epsilon_{A}=\partial_{\mu}\epsilon_{A}+\frac{1}{4}\omega_{\mu}^{ab}\gamma_{ab}\epsilon_{A}+\frac{i}{2}\sigma^{r}_{AB}\left[\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{rst}\Omega_{\mu st}-i\gamma_{7}\Omega_{\mu r0}\right]\epsilon^{B}\,.

(18)

Gamma matrices
\gamma^{a}
satisfy the Clifford algebra

\{\gamma^{a},\gamma^{b}\}=2\eta^{ab},\qquad\eta^{ab}=\textrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1,1,1).

(19)

The chirality matrix is defined by
\gamma_{7}=i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}\gamma^{4}\gamma^{5}

\gamma_{7}^{2}=-\mathbf{1}

3
ISO(3)\times U(1)
gauge group and vacua

We now consider
F(4)
gauged supergravity coupled to
n=4
vector multiplets with
ISO(3)\times U(1)
gauge group. This gauged supergravity has been shown to arise from a consistent truncation of type IIB theory on
S^{2}\times\Sigma
in
[
35
]
. In this case, the global symmetry is given by
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times SO(4,4)
. As previously mentioned, the
ISO(3)\sim SO(3)\ltimes\mathbf{R}^{3}
factor is embedded in
SO(4,3)\subset SO(4,4)
while the
U(1)
factor is the abelian gauge symmetry associated with the fourth vector multiplet. We also point out that the other fields are not charged under this
U(1)
factor as in the ungauged supergravity.

The embedding of
ISO(3)
is given by the following structure constants

\displaystyle f_{rst}=g\epsilon_{rst},\qquad f_{r\bar{s}\bar{t}}=-g\epsilon_{r\bar{s}\bar{t}},\qquad f_{\bar{r}\bar{s}\bar{t}}=-2g\epsilon_{\bar{r}\bar{s}\bar{t}}

(20)

in which we have split the indices
I,J=1,2,3,4
as
I=(\bar{r},4)
with
\bar{r},\bar{s}=1,2,3
. The gauge generators are defined by
X_{\Lambda}=f_{\Lambda\Sigma\Gamma}T^{\Sigma\Gamma}
with
T^{\Sigma\Gamma}=-T^{\Gamma\Sigma}
being
SO(4,4)
generators given by
(T^{\Lambda\Sigma})_{\Gamma}^{\phantom{\Gamma}\Delta}=2\delta^{[\Lambda}_{\Gamma}\eta^{\Sigma]\Delta}
. From the structure constants, we can straightforwardly verify that
X_{0}=0
and
X_{r}
generate the compact
SO(3)
subgroup while
X_{r}-X_{\bar{r}}
form a set of three commuting generators in
\mathbf{R}^{3}
and transform as a vector or adjoint representation of
SO(3)
. It should also be noted that in general, the gauge coupling constant
g
and the mass parameter
m
of the massive deformation are two independent parameters. However, the consistent truncations found in
[
35
]
require the relation
g=3m
. This is very similar to the embedding of pure
F(4)
gauged supergravity
[
19
]
in massive type IIA theory using the truncation given in
[
33
]
. In that case, only the gauged supergravity with
g=3m
(called

in
[
19
]
) can be uplifted to ten dimensions.

We are now in a position to compute the scalar potential and look for possible vacua. We follow the usual approach to simplify the computation by considering a truncation invariant under a certain subgroup of the gauge group. In particular, we will consider
SO(3)\subset ISO(3)

U(1)

(21)

The non-compact generators can be identified with

\displaystyle Y_{\alpha I}=e^{\alpha,I+3}+e^{I+3,\alpha}\,.

(22)

16

(\mathbf{4},\mathbf{4})

(\mathbf{4},\mathbf{4})\rightarrow(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})+(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{3})+(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{1})+(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{3})

(\mathbf{4},\mathbf{4})\rightarrow\mathbf{1}^{\prime}+\mathbf{3}_{\textrm{v}}+\mathbf{3}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{v}}+\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{3}_{\textrm{Adj}}+\mathbf{5}\,.

4
Holographic RG flows

In this section, we consider supersymmetric solutions describing holographic RG flows in the
N=2
five-dimensional SCFT dual to the superstmmetric
AdS_{6}
vacuum identified in the previous section. The metric ansatz is given by the standard domain wall

ds^{2}=e^{2A(r)}dx^{2}_{1,4}+dr^{2}

(32)

with
dx^{2}_{1,4}
being the metric on five-dimensional Minkowski space. The metric warp factor
A(r)
and all non-vanishing scalar fields are only functions of the radial coordinate
r
.

To find supersymmetric solutions, we solve the corresponding BPS equations arising from the supersymmetry transformations of fermionic fields. For non-constant scalars, we will also need to impose a projection

\gamma_{r}\epsilon_{A}=-\epsilon_{A}\,.

(33)

The sign choice is chosen to identify the supersymmetric
AdS_{6}
vacuum with the limit
r\rightarrow\infty
at which
A\sim\frac{r}{L}
for the
AdS_{6}
radius
L
. Since there is only one supersymmetric
AdS_{6}
vacuum, all the supersymmetric holographic RG flows are essentially RG flows to non-conformal phases.

4.1
RG flows from
SO(3)
invariant sector

We first consider RG flow solutions with
SO(3)
singlet scalars given by the coset representative (
26
). After imposing the projection condition (
33
), we find the following BPS equations

\displaystyle A^{\prime}=W=\frac{1}{4}\left[ge^{\sigma}(3e^{\phi}-e^{3\phi})+me^{-3\sigma}(e^{-\varphi}+e^{\varphi})\right],

(34)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}=-\frac{4}{3}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi}=2ge^{\sigma+2\phi}\sinh\phi,

(35)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}=-4\frac{\partial W}{\partial\varphi}=-2me^{-3\sigma}\sinh\varphi,

(36)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}=-\frac{\partial W}{\partial\sigma}=-\frac{1}{4}\left[ge^{\sigma}(3e^{\phi}-e^{3\phi})-3me^{-3\sigma}(e^{\varphi}+e^{-\varphi})\right].

(37)

Throughout the paper, we use
′
to denote
r
-derivatives. In these equations, we have also introduced the superpotential
W
in terms of which the scalar potential (
27

V=\left(\frac{\partial W}{\partial\sigma}\right)^{2}+\frac{4}{3}\left(\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi}\right)^{2}+4\left(\frac{\partial W}{\partial\varphi}\right)^{2}-5W^{2}\,.

(38)

It can be readily verified that the BPS equations are compatible with the corresponding field equations. We also note that the BPS equations admit a fixed point solution at which all scalars are constant,
\phi^{\prime}=\varphi^{\prime}=\sigma^{\prime}=0
, and
A=\frac{r}{L}
with
L=\frac{1}{2m}
for
g=3m
. This is the supersymmetric
AdS_{6}
vacuum denoted by critical point I in the previous section.

4.1.1
An RG flow driven by relevant operators

We first consider a simpler solution with
\phi=0
which is a consistent truncation of the above BPS equations. According to the scalar masses given in table
1
,
\phi
is dual to an irrelevant operator of dimension
\Delta=8
. Therefore, solutions with
\phi=0
holographically describe RG flows driven solely by relevant operators dual to
\varphi
and
\sigma

By considering

and
\sigma

\varphi

\displaystyle\sigma=-\frac{1}{4}\ln\left[\frac{g}{6m}(3\cosh\varphi-\cosh 3\varphi+2\sigma_{0}\sinh^{3}\varphi)\right],

for any value of
\sigma_{0}

We now consider asymptotic behaviors of the solution. For simplicity, we will also set

\displaystyle\varphi\sim e^{2mr}\sim e^{-\frac{r}{L}},

\displaystyle\sigma\sim\frac{3}{8}e^{-4mr}-\frac{\sigma_{0}}{4}e^{-6mr}\sim\frac{3}{8}e^{-\frac{2r}{L}}-\frac{\sigma_{0}}{4}e^{-\frac{3r}{L}}

in accordance with the fact that
\varphi
and
\sigma
are dual to operators of dimensions
\Delta=4
and
\Delta=3

\sigma

\sigma_{0}

For
\rho\rightarrow\rho_{0}
, the solution becomes singular with

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle-\ln\left[2m(\rho-\rho_{0})\right],

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\ln\left[\frac{(\sigma_{0}-2)}{32m^{3}}(\rho-\rho_{0})^{-3}+\frac{3(2-\sigma_{0})}{8m}(\rho-\rho_{0})^{-1}+\frac{3}{2}m(2+\sigma_{0})(\rho-\rho_{0})\right],\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\ln\left[\frac{\sigma_{0}-2}{16m^{3}}(\rho-\rho_{0})^{-3}-\frac{3(2-\sigma_{0})(\rho-\rho_{0})^{-1}}{4m}+\frac{3}{2}m(2+\sigma_{0})(\rho-\rho_{0})\right]

(43)

The scalar potential near this singularity is bounded from above with

[

SCFT in five dimensions dual to the supersymmetric

On the other hand, for

In this case, the singularity is unphysical. It would be useful to have a complete truncation ansatz to uplift the solution to type IIB theory and verify whether the singularity is resolved in ten dimensions.

4.1.2
An RG flow driven by relevant and irrelevant operators

We now consider solutions with all
SO(3)
singlet scalars non-vanishing. To solve the BPS equations, we first take the following combinations

Using the solution for

4.1.3
A truncation to three vector multiplets

We finally consider another subtruncation with
\varphi=0

Setting

By the same procedure, we find the following solution

\displaystyle\sigma=\frac{1}{4}\ln\left[\frac{e^{-\phi}(2\sigma_{0}e^{4\phi}-3m)}{2g(e^{2\phi}-1)}\right],

\displaystyle\phi\sim-\frac{1}{3}\ln[3m(\rho-\rho_{0})],\qquad\sigma\sim-\frac{1}{12}\ln[3m(\rho-\rho_{0})],

\displaystyle A\sim\frac{1}{12}\ln[3m(\rho-\rho_{0})].

In this case, the scalar potential becomes unbounded from above

AdS_{6}

6
Conclusions

We have constructed
F(4)
gauged supergravity in six dimensions with
ISO(3)\times U(1)
gauge group by coupling four vector multiplets to the supergravity multiplet. This gauged supergravity can be embedded in type IIB string theory by a consistent truncation on
S^{2}\times\Sigma
as shown in
[
35
]
. It turns out that there is only one supersymmetric
AdS_{6}
vacuum with
SO(3)
symmetry which is expected to be dual to an
N=2
SCFT in five dimensions. We have also given the complete scalar mass spectrum at this
AdS_{6}
critical point which encodes information about conformal dimensions of the corresponding operators dual to the scalars. By truncating to
SO(3)\times U(1)\subset ISO(3)\times U(1)
and
SO(2)\times U(1)\subset SO(3)\times U(1)
invariant sectors, we have studied a number of holographic RG flows from the conformal fixed point dual to the
AdS_{6}
vacuum to different non-conformal phases of the
N=2
SCFT. Many solutions can be found analytically, but some of them can only be found numerically. Solutions within a subtruncation to three vector multiplets leading to
ISO(3)
gauge group have also been considered. As usual in this type of solutions, there are singularities at a finite value of the radial coordinate. We have shown that some of these singularities are physically acceptable using the criterions given in
[
40
]
and
[
39
]
.

In addition, we have also studied a number of supersymmetric Janus solutions by using a curved domain wall ansatz with
AdS_{5}
slices. We have considered the solutions within the
SO(2)\times U(1)
truncation and possible consistent subtruncations. A number of regular Janus solutions interpolating between
AdS_{6}
vacua is given numerically. All of these solutions should holographically describe conformal interfaces within the dual five-dimensional
N=2
SCFT in the presence of sources and position-dependent expectation values for relevant and irrelevant operators dual to different scalar fieds in the bulk matter-coupled
F(4)
gauged supergravity.

It would be interesting to identify the dual
N=2
SCFT in five dimensions and find field theory descriptions of the holographic RG flows given in this paper. Moreover, constructing conformal interfaces dual to the Janus solutions presented here in the framework of the dual five-dimensional
N=2
SCFT is also worth considering. Working out the complete truncation ansatz of type IIB theory to both
ISO(3)
and
ISO(3)\times U(1)
matter-coupled
F(4)
gauged supergravities is not only interesting in its own right but also useful in uplifting all the solutions found in this paper to ten dimensions. This could lead to possible brane configurations described at low energy by six-dimensional solutions and could also give rise to the precise identification of the dual five-dimensional
N=2
SCFT. However, as pointed out in
[
35
]
, it is not clear whether there are globally regular solutions in type IIB theory to which these solutions uplift. Finding other types of holographic solutions within the
ISO(3)\times U(1)

F(4)
gauged supergravity is also of particular interest
[
41
]
and
[
42
]
. Finally, constructing a Euclidean version of Janus solutions given here and using it to holographically study mass deformations of the five-dimensional SCFT along the line of
[
43
]
are also interesting.

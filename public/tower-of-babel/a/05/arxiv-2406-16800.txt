From snapping out Brownian motions to Walsh’s spider processes on star-like graphs Version of August 7, 2026
Adam Bobrowski, Elżbieta Ratajczyk

Abstract
. By analyzing matrices involved, we prove that a snapping-out Brownian motion with large permeability coefficients is a good approximation of Walsh’s spider process on the star-like graph K 1 , k K_{1,k} . Thus, the latter process can be seen as a Brownian motion perturbed by a trace of semi-permeable membrane at the graph’s center.

Introduction

1.1.
Sticky snapping-out Brownian motion

Given a natural number
k\geq 2
and non-negative parameters

a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},\qquad i\in\mathcal{K}\coloneqq\{1,\dots,k\}

with
b_{i}>0
, we think of the following Markov process on the compact space

S_{k}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{K}}(\{i\}\times[0,\infty]).

While on the
i
th copy of
[0,\infty]
, our process is initially indistinguishable from the
sticky Brownian motion
with stickiness coefficient
a_{i}/b_{i}
, as described, for example, in
[
29
]
(see also
[
16
]
); in the particular case of
a_{i}=0
, the sticky Brownian motion is the reflected Brownian motion. However, when the time spent at the
i
th copy of
0
exceeds an exponential time (independent of the Brownian motion) with parameter
c_{i}
, the process jumps to one of the points
(j,0),j\not=i
, all choices being equally likely. At the moment of jump the process forgets its past and starts to behave like a sticky Brownian motion on the
j
th copy of
[0,\infty]
, and so on (see Figure
1
).

Figure 1.
Sticky snapping out Brownian motion is a Feller process on
k

[0,\infty]

i

a_{i}/b_{i}
. After spending enough time at
(0,i)
the process jumps to one of the points
(0,j),j\not=i
to continue its motion on the corresponding copy of
[0,\infty]
, and so on. Times between jumps are governed by parameters
c_{i}
.

The so-defined process has Feller property and thus can be characterized by means of a Feller generator in
C(S_{k})
, the space of continuous functions on
S_{k}
. To describe this generator, say,
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}p
(subscript
\mathpzc p
is a shorthand for the ordered set of parameters

described above), in detail, we introduce first the space

of real-valued, continuous functions

[0,\infty)
such that the limits
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)
exist and are finite, and note that
C(S_{k})
is isometrically isomorphic to

{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}\coloneqq(C[0,\infty])^{k},

the Cartesian product of
k

C[0,\infty]
; we identify a sequence
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
with the function
f:S_{k}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
f(i,x)=f_{i}(x),i\in\mathcal{K},x\in[0,\infty)

of

as composed of
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}
such that

((a))

each

is twice continuously differentiable with

,

((b))

we have

a_{i}f_{i}^{\prime\prime}(0)-b_{i}f_{i}^{\prime}(0)=c_{i}\Big[{{\textstyle\frac{1}{k-1}}\sum\limits_{j\neq i}}f_{j}(0)-f_{i}(0)\Big],\qquad i\in\mathcal{K}.

(1.1)

Moreover, for such

, we agree that

1.3.
The main result

Let
\mathpzc p
be a fixed set of parameters for
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}p
, and for
\varepsilon>0
let
\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)
be the same set with with
c_{i}
s replaced by
\varepsilon^{-1}c_{i},i\in\mathcal{K}
. Our first limit theorem (Theorem
3.2
) says that the semigroups generated by
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)}
converge, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, to the semigroup of sticky Walsh’s process with parameters

\alpha_{i}\coloneqq db_{i}c_{i}^{-1},i\in\mathcal{K}\quad\text{ and }\quad\beta\coloneqq d\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}a_{j}c_{j}^{-1}

(1.2)

where
d\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}(a_{j}+b_{j})c_{j}^{-1}}
is a normalizing constant;
this set of parameters will be denoted
\mathpzc{q(p)}
.
In the case of
k=2
and
a_{1}=a_{2}=0
the result described above has been proved in
[
13
]
, and later reproduced in
[
14
]
; see also
[
6
]
for a recent continuation.

To explain the meaning of the theorem we note first that the state-space of the snapping-out Brownian motion can also be thought of as the
K_{1,k}
graph, provided that we imagine that at the graph’s center there is a multi-faceted, semipermeable membrane, and thus we distinguish between positions ‘virtually at the graph’s center’ but on the
i
th edge,
i\in\mathcal{K}
(we are thus doing a reverse process to that of lumping points). In this interpretation,
c_{i}
is the permeability coefficient, telling us how quickly a particle diffusing on the
i
th edge can filter through the membrane to continue its chaotic motion on the other side. By replacing
c_{i}
by
\varepsilon^{-1}c_{i}
for all
i\in\mathcal{K}
and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
we make the membrane completely permeable, and thus the limit process’s state-space reduces to
K_{1,k}
(see Figure
3
). Formulae (
1.2
) show that despite the apparent absence of the membrane, in the limit there remains some kind of asymmetry in the way particles approaching the graph’s center from different edges pass through this center. Thus, Walsh’s sticky process can be seen as a process with a trace of semipermeable membrane at the graph’s center.

On the more technical side, Theorem
3.2
says that the semigroups
\{\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)}},t\geq 0\}
, which are defined on

C(S_{k})
, converge only on
C(K_{1,k})\subset C(S_{k})
. In Sections
4
and
5
, devoted to the case of
a_{i}=0,i\in\mathcal{K}
, we complement this theorem with information on convergence outside of
C(K_{1,k})
, and on convergence of the related cosine families — see Theorems
4.1
and
5.1
, and Corollary
5.2
. An estimate of the growth of stochastic matrices involved constitutes a key element in this analysis.

The main results are preceded with Section
2
where, as a preparation, we prove two generation theorems, and
Section
3.1
, where they are put into the perspective of the general theory of convergence of semigroups and cosine families.

2.
Two generation theorems

In this section we show that operators defined in Introduction are indeed Feller generators; in particular, we compute their resolvents which will constitute a key to our main limit theorem. We start with a linear algebra lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A_{i},B_{i},C_{i},i\in\mathcal{K}
be given constants such that
A_{i}>0
. Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
there is precisely one solution
(D_{i}(\varepsilon))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
to the system

\varepsilon A_{i}D_{i}(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon B_{i}+\textstyle{\frac{1}{k-1}}{\textstyle\sum\limits_{j\neq i}}(C_{j}+D_{j}(\varepsilon))-C_{i}-D_{i}(\varepsilon),\qquad i\in\mathcal{K}.

(2.1)

Moreover, the limits
\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}D_{i}(\varepsilon),i\in\mathcal{K}
exist and are finite.

Proof.

The idea is to rewrite (
2.1
) so that uniqueness and convergence of
D_{i}(\varepsilon)
becomes evident. To this end, we let

D(\varepsilon)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}}A_{i}D_{i}(\varepsilon)

(2.2)

and note that, by (
2.1
),

D(\varepsilon)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}}B_{i}.

It follows that
D(\varepsilon)
in fact does not depend on
\varepsilon
and we will write simply
D
instead.

Without loss of generality, we assume from now on that

A_{k}=\max_{i\in\mathcal{K}}A_{i}.

Then, by substituting
\frac{D}{A_{k}}-\sum_{i\not=k}\frac{A_{i}}{A_{k}}D_{i}(\varepsilon)
for
D_{k}(\varepsilon)
into (
2.1
), we obtain the following system of relations

\displaystyle\Big(1+\varepsilon A_{i}+\tfrac{A_{i}}{(k-1)A_{k}}\Big)D_{i}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon B_{i}+\textstyle{\frac{1}{k-1}}{\displaystyle\sum_{j\not=i}}C_{j}-C_{i}+\tfrac{D}{(k-1)A_{k}}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\textstyle{\frac{1}{k-1}}{\displaystyle\sum_{j\not=i,k}}\Big(1-{\textstyle\frac{A_{j}}{A_{k}}}\Big)D_{j}(\varepsilon),

involving variables with the first
k-1
indexes

i\in\mathcal{L}\coloneqq\{1,\dots,k-1\}.

This system is easier to handle. To see this, we equip
\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
with the norm
\|(\xi_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{L}}\|\coloneqq\max_{i\in\mathcal{L}}|\xi_{i}|.
Then, for any
\varepsilon\geq 0
, the norm of the linear operator
O_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{k-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
given by

O_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{i})_{i\in L}=\Big({\textstyle\frac{1}{k-1}\frac{1}{m_{i}(\varepsilon)}{\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\{i\}}}}\Big(1-{\textstyle\frac{A_{j}}{A_{k}}}\Big)\xi_{j}\Big)_{i\in L}

where

m_{i}(\varepsilon)\coloneqq 1+\varepsilon A_{i}+{\textstyle\frac{A_{i}}{(k-1)A_{k}}}>1,

is smaller than
1
. For,
\|O_{\varepsilon}\|
does not exceed

\displaystyle\max_{i\in\mathcal{L}}{\textstyle\frac{1}{k-1}\frac{1}{m_{i}(\varepsilon)}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\{i\}}\Big(1-{\textstyle\frac{A_{j}}{A_{k}}}\Big)<\max_{i\in\mathcal{L}}{\textstyle\frac{1}{k-1}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\{i\}}\Big(1-{\textstyle\frac{A_{j}}{A_{k}}}\Big)<{\textstyle\frac{k-2}{k-1}}<1.

Hence,
I-O_{\varepsilon}
is invertible and so (
2.1
) has the unique solution

\left(D_{i}(\varepsilon)\right)_{i\in L}=(I-O_{\varepsilon})^{-1}\left(E_{i}(\varepsilon)\right)_{i\in L}

(2.4)

where

E_{i}(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon B_{i}+\textstyle{\frac{1}{k-1}}{\sum_{j\not=i}}C_{j}-C_{i}+\tfrac{D}{(k-1)A_{k}},i\in\mathcal{L}
. Moreover, we have
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}O_{\varepsilon}=O_{0}
and

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}E_{i}(\varepsilon)=-C_{i}+\tfrac{1}{k-1}{\sum_{j\not=i}}C_{j}+\tfrac{1}{k-1}\tfrac{D}{A_{k}},i\in\mathcal{K}
. Hence,
(
2.4
) establishes convergence of
\left(D_{i}(\varepsilon)\right)_{i\in L}
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Since the sum in (
2.2
) does not depend on
\varepsilon
and
A_{i}
s are nonzero, this implies convergence of all
D_{i}(\varepsilon)
s and thus completes the proof.
∎

3.
The main convergence theorem

3.1.
A sketch of the theory of convergence of semigroups and bird’s-eye view of our results

The main idea of the Trotter–Kato–Neveu convergence theorem
[
1
,
19
,
22
,
32
]
, a cornerstone of the theory of convergence of semigroups
[
14
,
8
]
, is that convergence of resolvents of
equibounded
semigroups in a Banach space
\mathsf{F}
, gives an insight into convergence of the semigroups themselves. Hence, in studying the limit of equibounded semigroups, say,
\left\{\mathrm{e}^{t{B_{\varepsilon}}},{t\geq 0}\right\}
, generated by the operators
B_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon>0
we should first establish existence of
the strong limit

R_{\lambda}\coloneqq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\lambda-B_{\varepsilon}\right)^{-1}.

The general theory of convergence (see
[
14
]
*Chapter 8) covers also the case in which, unlike in the classical version of the Trotter–Kato–Neveu theorem, the (common) range of the operators
R_{\lambda},\lambda>0
so-obtained is
not
dense in
\mathsf{F}
, and stresses the role of the so-called
regularity space
, defined as the closure of the range of
R_{\lambda}
:

\mathsf{F}_{\textnormal{reg}}\coloneqq cl(RangeR_{\lambda})\subset\mathsf{F}.

Namely,
\mathsf{F}_{\textnormal{reg}}
turns out to coincide with the set of
f\in\mathsf{F}
such the limit
T(t)f\coloneqq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathrm{e}^{tB_{\varepsilon}}f
exists and is uniform with respect to
t
in compact subintervals of
[0,\infty)
; then
\{T(t),\,t\geq 0\}
, termed the
regular limit
of
\mbox{$\left\{\mathrm{e}^{t{B_{\varepsilon}}},{t\geq 0}\right\}$},\varepsilon>0
, is a strongly continuous semigroup in
\mathsf{F}_{\textnormal{reg}}
.

It should be stressed, though, that this statement does not exclude the possibility of the existence of
f\not\in\mathsf{F}_{\textnormal{reg}}
such that the strong limit
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathrm{e}^{tB_{\varepsilon}}f
exists for all
t\geq 0
. Such
irregular
convergence of semigroups, which is known to be always uniform with respect to
t
in compact subsets of
(0,\infty)
— see
[
10
]
or
[
14
]
*Thm 28.4 — is not so uncommon, especially in the context of singular perturbations
[
31
,
2
,
14
]
, but needs to be established by different means.

In our first limit theorem (Theorem
3.2
) we fix the set
\mathpzc p
of parameters
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i}
of Section
1.1
and consider operators
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)},\varepsilon>0
defined as
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}p
with
\mathpzc p=\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)
obtained from
\mathpzc p
by replacing all
c_{i}
s by
\varepsilon^{-1}c_{i}
and leaving the remaining parameters intact. We prove that the regularity space for this family of semigroups of operators is
C(K_{1,k})\subset C(S_{k})
, and that the sticky Walsh’s process is their regular limit. In Section
4
, under additional assumption that all
a_{i}
are zero, we will show
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)}
generate also equibounded cosine families, and therefore the limit
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)}}f,t\geq 0
exists also for
f\in C(S_{k})\setminus C(K_{1,k})
. In Section
5
we show that convergence of semigroups (and cosine families) on the regularity space is in fact uniform with respect to
t
, and that outside of this subspace the cosine families do not converge at all.

3.2.
The main theorem

Let
\mathpzc p
and
\mathpzc p(\varepsilon),\varepsilon>0
be as in the preceding section. In other words, elements of the domain of
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)}
satisfy
the transmission conditions

\varepsilon a_{i}f_{i}^{\prime\prime}(0)-\varepsilon b_{i}f_{i}^{\prime}(0)=c_{i}\Big[{\textstyle\frac{1}{k-1}}{\sum\limits_{j\neq i}}f_{j}(0)-f_{i}(0)\Big],\qquad i\in\mathcal{K},

(3.1)

and we are interested in the strong limit of the semigroups
\{\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)}},t\geq 0\}
generated by
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)},
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
As explained above, our first task is to prove existence of the limit of resolvents of
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)}
. This is achieved in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

The limit
R_{\lambda}g\coloneqq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}(\lambda-A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)})^{-1}g
exists for all
g\in C(S_{k})
and
\lambda>0
, and the regularity space for
\{\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)}},t\geq 0\},\varepsilon>0
coincides with
C(K_{1,k})
. Moreover, for
g\in C(K_{1,k})
,
R_{\lambda}g=(\lambda-A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\mathpzc{q(p)}})^{-1}g
where
\mathpzc{q(p)}
is the set of parameters given in (
1.2
).

Proof.

Fix
g\in C(S_{k})
. Function
f_{\varepsilon}=(\lambda-A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)})^{-1}g
solves the resolvent equation for
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)}
and hence, as we know from the proof of Proposition
2.2
, is of the form (
2.5
)–(
2.6
) with certain
D_{i}=D_{i}(\varepsilon)
. More specifically, the vector of these coefficients is a unique solution to the system

\varepsilon\gamma_{i}^{+}D_{i}(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon\gamma_{i}^{-}C_{i}+\varepsilon a_{i}c_{i}^{-1}g_{i}(0)+\tfrac{1}{k-1}{\textstyle\sum\limits_{j\neq i}}(C_{j}+D_{j}(\varepsilon))-C_{i}-D_{i}(\varepsilon),

(3.2)

where, as in Proposition
2.2
,

\gamma_{i}^{\pm}\coloneqq b_{i}c_{i}^{-1}\sqrt{\lambda}\pm\lambda a_{i}c_{i}^{-1},i\in\mathcal{K}.
We are thus dealing again with a special case of (
2.1
), and Lemma
2.1
tells us that the limits
D_{i}^{0}\coloneqq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}D_{i}(\varepsilon),i\in\mathcal{K}
exist and are finite. Since neither the first nor the third term in (
2.5
) depend on
\varepsilon
, this establishes existence of the limit
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}(\lambda-A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)})^{-1}g
.

To prove the second sentence in the proposition, we first let
\varepsilon\to 0
in (
3.2
) to obtain

C_{i}+D_{i}^{0}=\tfrac{1}{k-1}\sum_{j\not=i}(C_{j}+D_{j}^{0}),\qquad i\in\mathcal{K}.

(3.3)

This shows that
f_{i}(0)=C_{i}+D_{i}^{0}
does not depend on
i\in\mathcal{K}
, that is, that
R_{\lambda}g
belongs to
C(K_{1,k})
. Next, we sum both sides of (
3.2
) over
i\in\mathcal{K}
and divide by
\varepsilon
. This renders

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\big(b_{i}c_{i}^{-1}\sqrt{\lambda}+\lambda a_{i}c_{i}^{-1}\big)D_{i}^{0}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\big(b_{i}c_{i}^{-1}\sqrt{\lambda}-\lambda a_{i}c_{i}^{-1}\big)C_{i}+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}}a_{i}c_{i}^{-1}g_{i}(0).

If
g\in C(K_{1,k})
, that is, if
g_{i}(0)
does not depend on
i
, this relation can be rearranged as

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}}(a_{i}c_{i}^{-1})(\lambda(C_{i}+D_{i}^{0})-g(0))=\sqrt{\lambda}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}b_{j}c_{j}^{-1}(C_{j}-D_{j}^{0}).

This means, however, that condition (
2.9
) is satisfied with
\alpha_{i}
s and
\beta
specified in (
1.2
), because we know that
C_{i}+D_{i}^{0}
does not depend on
i
. It follows that for the stated choice of parameters,
D_{i}
s of (
2.8
)–(
2.9
), coincide with
D_{i}^{0}
s. This establishes
R_{\lambda}g=(\lambda-A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\mathpzc{q(p)}})^{-1}g
for
g\in C(K_{1,k})
.

As a by-product, the range of
R_{\lambda}
contains
D(A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\mathpzc{q(p)}})
, and since the latter is dense in
C(K_{1,k})
the closure of the range of
R_{\lambda}
contains
C(K_{1,k})
. But we have already established that this range is contained in
C(K_{1,k})
. Hence, the closure of the range of
R_{\lambda}
coincides with
C(K_{1,k}),
as claimed. ∎

Proposition
3.1
says in fact more than it is visible on its surface. To wit, the fact that on the regularity space
C(K_{1,k})
,
R_{\lambda}
coincides with
(\lambda-A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\mathpzc{q(p)}})^{-1}
implies that
A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\mathpzc{q(p)}}
is the generator of the regular limit of
\{\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)}},t\geq 0\}
— see e.g.
[
14
]
*Thm. 8.1 and Corollary 8.3, compare
[
11
]
*Sections 8.4.3 and 8.4.4. As an immediate corollary we obtain thus the following main theorem of this section.

Theorem 3.2
.

We have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)}}f=\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\mathpzc{q(p)}}}f,\qquad f\in C(K_{1,k})

with the limit uniform with respect to
t
in compact subsets of
[0,\infty)
.

In view of the Trotter–Sova–Kurtz–Mackevic̆ius theorem
[
25
]
, this result can be seen as expressing a convergence of the random processes involved. We note that A. Gregosiewicz in
[
23
]
has found a different proof of our theorem based on a decomposition of resolvents involved.

4.
Convergence outside of
C(K_{1,k})

4.1.
Introductory remarks

As already mentioned in Section
3.1
, Theorem
3.2
need not tell the entire story: it may happen that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc p(\varepsilon)}}f
exists also for
f\in C(S_{k})\setminus C(K_{1,k})
, except that this limit cannot be uniform in compact subintervals containing
0
. Such convergence of semigroups outside of regularity space can be deduced from the convergence of their resolvents provided that the semigroups enjoy additional regularity properties, such as being uniformly holomorphic — see e.g.
[
14
]
*Chapter 31. In
[
7
]
it has been proved that, in the case of
k=2
and
a_{1}=a_{2}=0
, each
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}p
is a cosine operator family generator, and all these families are formed by operators of norm not exceeding
5
. As a corollary, in
[
6
]
we show that in this case convergence spoken of in Theorem
3.2
extends beyond
f\in C(K_{1,k})
.

It is the purpose of this section to prove a similar result for general
k\geq 3
. Hence, in what follows we assume that
a_{i}=0
,
i\in\mathcal{K}
and, without loss of generality, take
b_{i}=1,i\in\mathcal{K},
so that the entire generator

A^{\textnormal{s-o}}p
is characterized by the vector
\mathpzc c=(c_{1},\dots,c_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
of (positive) permeability coefficients; to stress this in what follows we write
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c
instead of
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}p
. As a result, transmission conditions (
1.1
) take the form

f_{i}^{\prime}(0)=c_{i}\Big[f_{i}(0)-{{\textstyle\frac{1}{k-1}}\sum\limits_{j\neq i}}f_{j}(0)\Big],\qquad i\in\mathcal{K}.

(4.1)

We will show (see Theorem
4.1
) that each
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c
generates a strongly continuous cosine family
\{\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}
such that

\|\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c}(t)\|\leq M=M(\mathpzc c),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},

where
M(\mathpzc c)
has the following property: for any
r>0
,
M(r\mathpzc c)=M(\mathpzc c)
. In particular, even though we cannot claim that (as in the case of
k=2
) there is a universal bound for all cosine families generated by
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c
s, we know that for fixed
\mathpzc c
the operators
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc c(\varepsilon)}
, defined as
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c
with
\mathpzc c
replaced by
\varepsilon^{-1}\mathpzc c
, generate cosine families that are bounded by a universal constant:

\|\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc c(\varepsilon)}}(t)\|\leq M(\mathpzc c),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},\varepsilon>0.

(4.2)

Theorem
3.2
says that the semigroups generated by operators
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc c(\varepsilon)}
converge, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, to the semigroup describing the Walsh’s process with parameters

\alpha_{i}\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{c_{i}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}c_{j}^{-1}},\;i\in\mathcal{K}\quad\text{ and }\quad\beta\coloneqq 0.

(4.3)

To denote this simpler set of parameters we write
\alpha(\mathpzc c)
instead of
\mathpzc{q(p)}
.

As explained in detail in
[
6
]
, estimate (
4.2
), when combined with Proposition
3.1
, implies
the following second main result of our paper.

Theorem 4.1
.

(a)

For
f\in C(K_{1,k})
,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc c(\varepsilon)}}(t)f=\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}}(t)f
uniformly in
t\in[0,t_{0}]
for
t_{0}>0
.

(b)

For
f\in C(S_{k})\setminus C(K_{1,k})
,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc c(\varepsilon)}}f=\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}}f
uniformly in
t\in(t_{0}^{-1},t_{0})
for
t_{0}>1
.

To elaborate on these succinct statements: first of all,
A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}
is also the generator of a cosine family in
C(K_{1,k})
(see our Section
5
for more details). As such, despite not being densely defined in
C(S_{k})
, it is also the generator of a semigroup
\{\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}},t\geq 0\}
of operators in
C(S_{k})
— see e.g.
[
9
]
*Corollary 5.1. These operators are extensions of those defined in
C(K_{1,k})
, and the semigroup generated by
A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}
is not strongly continuous in
t\in[0,\infty)
but merely in
t\in(0,\infty)
(of course, for
f\in C(K_{1,k}),\lim_{t\to 0+}\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}}f=f
). This clarifies statement (b).

Condition (a) also requires a comment. Since we were able to extend convergence of semigroups from
C(K_{1,k})
to the entire
C(S_{k})
, it may seem natural to ask weather the same can be done with convergence of the related cosine families. However, as proved in
[
4
]
(see also
[
14
]
*Chapter 61), cosine families by nature cannot converge outside of the regularity space. As applied to our case, this theorem tells us that (a) is the best result possible in the sense that the limit cosine family cannot be extended beyond
C(K_{1,k})
— see also our Section
5
.

Finally, we remark that the fact that each
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c
is the generator of a cosine family can also be proved using decomposition of resolvent techniques (see
[
23
]
); however, this approach does not give a universal bound for the norm of
\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc c(\varepsilon)}}(t),t\in\mathbb{R},\varepsilon>0
(found in (
4.2
)).

4.2.
Definition of
M(\mathpzc c)

Given
\mathpzc c=(c_{1},\dots,c_{k})
with all
c_{i}>0
we think of the
k\times k
intensity matrix
Q=(q_{i,j})_{i,j\in\mathcal{K}}
given by

q_{i,j}=\begin{cases}-c_{i},&i=j,\\
\frac{c_{i}}{k-1},&i\neq j.\end{cases}

(4.4)

The Markov chain generated by
Q
is irreducible and its invariant measure is

\alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
, where
\alpha_{i}
are defined in (
4.3
).

Let
c\coloneqq\max_{i\in\mathcal{K}}c_{i}
and
Q_{0}\coloneqq c^{-1}Q
. We denote the entries of the matrix
\mathrm{e}^{tQ_{0}}
by
p_{i,j}^{0}(t)
.
Since
Q_{0}+I_{k}
, where
I_{k}
is the
k\times k
identity matrix, is a transition matrix of an irreducible and reversible discrete time Markov chain with the invariant measure
\alpha
, Corollary 2.1.5 in
[
33
]
implies that

\displaystyle|p_{i,j}^{0}(t)-\alpha_{j}|\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}{\textstyle\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_{j}}{\alpha_{i}}}}=\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}{\textstyle\sqrt{\frac{c_{i}}{c_{j}}}},\qquad t\geq 0,\;i,j\in\mathcal{K},

(4.5)

where the
spectral gap

\omega
is the smallest non-zero eigenvalue of
-Q_{0}
. We note that
Q_{0}
does not change if
\mathpzc c
replaced by
r\mathpzc c
where
r>0
, and thus neither does change the
\omega
. It follows that
the same applies to the constant

\displaystyle M=M(\mathpzc c)\coloneqq\Big(1+2\max_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\frac{c_{i}}{c\omega}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}\Big({\textstyle\sqrt{\frac{c_{i}}{c_{j}}}}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{k-1}}\sum_{\ell\neq i}{\textstyle\sqrt{\frac{c_{\ell}}{c_{j}}}}\Big)\Big).

(4.6)

4.3.
Cosine families

A strongly continuous family
\{C(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}
of operators in a Banach space
\mathsf{F}
is said to be a cosine family iff
C(0)
is the identity operator and

2C(t)C(s)=C(s+t)+C(t-s),\qquad t,s\in\mathbb{R}.

The generator of such a family is defined by

Af=\lim_{t\to 0}2t^{-2}{(C(t)f-f)}

for all
f\in\mathsf{F}
such the limit on the right-hand side exists.
For example, in
C[-\infty,\infty]
, the space of continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
that have finite limits at
\pm\infty
, there is the
basic cosine family
given by

C(t)f(x)={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}[f(x+t)+f(x-t)],\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},t\in\mathbb{R}.

Its generator is the one-dimensional Laplace operator
f\mapsto f^{\prime\prime}
with domain composed of twice continuously differentiable functions on
\mathbb{R}
such that
f^{\prime\prime}\in C[-\infty,\infty]
.

Each cosine family generator is automatically the generator of a strongly continuous semigroup (but not vice versa). The semigroup such operator generates is given by the
Weierstrass formula
(see e.g.
[
1
]
*p. 219)

T(0)f=f\quad\text{and}\quad T(t)f={\textstyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}C(s)f\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t>0,f\in\mathsf{F}.

This formula expresses the fact that the cosine family is thus (in this case) a more fundamental object than the semigroup, and properties of the semigroup can be hidden in those of the cosine family (see e.g.
[
16
]
). Moreover, semigroups that are generated by generators of cosine families are much more regular than other semigroups. In particular, such semigroups are
holomorphic, but even among holomorphic semigroups there are those that are not generated by cosine family generators (see
[
1
]
again).

4.4.
Lord Kelvin’s method of images and the generation theorem

For a number of Laplace operators in
C[0,\infty]
with domains characterized by Feller–Wentzel boundary conditions at
x=0
the cosine families they generate can be constructed semi-explicitly as (isomorphic images of) subspace cosine families for the basic cosine families in
C[-\infty,\infty]
— see
[
12
,
7
]
and references given there. The trick, known as the
Lord Kelvin method of images

[
12
,
21
]
, comes down to noticing that each boundary condition unequivocally shapes extensions of elements of
C[0,\infty]
to elements of
C[-\infty,\infty]
; for example, the Neumann and Dirichlet boundary conditions lead to even and odd extensions, respectively. These extensions form an invariant subspace for the basic cosine family, and the cosine family we are searching for turns out to be an isomorphic image of the basic cosine family as restricted to this subspace.

In this section we use the same idea to show that the operator
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c
with transmission conditions (
4.1
) generates a cosine family
\{\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c}(t),\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
of operators in
{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}
: we will construct
\{\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c}(t),\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
as an isomorphic image of a subspace cosine family of the
Cartesian product cosine family

\{C_{\textnormal{D}}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}
(‘D’ for ‘Descartes’). The latter is defined in the Cartesian product space

\mathfrak{G}_{k}\coloneqq(C[-\infty,\infty])^{k},

(equipped with the maximum norm) by the formula

C_{\textnormal{D}}(t)(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}=(C(t)f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}},\qquad(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in\mathfrak{G}_{k},t\in\mathbb{R}.

This is to say that
\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c}(t)
will be found to be of the form

\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c}(t)(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}=RC_{\textnormal{D}}(t)(\widetilde{f}_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}},\qquad(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathfrak{F}_{k}},t\in\mathbb{R},

(4.7)

where
\widetilde{f_{i}}\in C[-\infty,\infty],i\in\mathcal{K}
, is a suitable extension of
f_{i}\in C[0,\infty]

and
R\colon\mathfrak{G}_{k}\to{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}

is the restriction operator
assigning to a
(g_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in\mathfrak{G}_{k}
the member
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
of
{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}
given by
f_{i}=g_{i|\mathbb{R}_{+}},i\in\mathcal{K}
. Our first lemma says that extensions
\widetilde{f_{i}},i\in\mathcal{K}
are determined uniquely by the fact that a cosine family leaves the domain of its generator invariant.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in D(A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c)
there exists its unique extension
(\widetilde{f}_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in\mathfrak{G}_{k}
such that

RC_{\textnormal{D}}(t)(\widetilde{f}_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in D(A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c)

for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, each
\widetilde{f_{i}},i\in\mathcal{K}
belongs to the domain of the generator of the basic cosine family and

\|(\widetilde{f_{i}})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\|_{\mathfrak{G}_{k}}\leq M\|(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\|_{{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}},

(4.8)

where
M
is defined in (
4.6
).

Proof.

Step I, existence and uniqueness of
\widetilde{f_{i}},i\in\mathcal{K}
.
Our task is to find

\displaystyle g_{i}(x)\coloneqq\widetilde{f_{i}}(-x),\qquad x\geq 0,i\in\mathcal{K},

(4.9)

satisfying compatibility conditions
f_{i}(0)=g_{i}(0)
. Since
C_{\textnormal{D}}(t)=C_{\textnormal{D}}(-t),t\geq 0
, we must also have

\displaystyle\frac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}[\widetilde{f_{i}}(x-t)+\widetilde{f_{i}}(x+t)]_{|x=0}=c_{i}\Big[\widetilde{f_{i}}(t)+\widetilde{f_{i}}(-t)-{{\textstyle\frac{1}{k-1}}\sum\limits_{j\neq i}}\big(\widetilde{f_{j}}(t)+\widetilde{f_{j}}(-t)\big)\Big],

that is

\displaystyle f_{i}^{\prime}(t)-g_{i}^{\prime}(t)=c_{i}\Big[{f_{i}}(t)+g_{i}(t)-{{\textstyle\frac{1}{k-1}}\sum\limits_{j\neq i}}\big({f_{j}}(t)+g_{j}(t)\big)\Big],

(4.10)

for
t\geq 0,i\in\mathcal{K}
.
This system can be rewritten as

\displaystyle(g_{i}-f_{i})^{\prime}_{i\in\mathcal{K}}=Q(g_{i}-f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}+2Q(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}},

where
Q=(q_{i,j})_{i,j\in\mathcal{K}}
is the intensity matrix defined in (
4.4
).
Hence,
g_{i}
are uniquely determined and (because of the compatibility condition) given by

\displaystyle(g_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle=(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}+2\int_{0}^{t}\big[\mathrm{e}^{(t-s)Q}Q\big](f_{i}(s))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\textrm{d}s,\qquad t\geq 0.

(4.11)

From now on, we treat (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) as the definition of
(\widetilde{f_{i}})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
.

Step II, an estimate for
p_{i,j}^{\prime}(t)
.

We note that
\mathrm{e}^{tQ},t\geq 0
is the matrix of transition probabilities, say,
p_{i,j}(t)
, for the Markov chain with intensity matrix
Q
, whereas
Q\mathrm{e}^{tQ}=\mathrm{e}^{tQ}Q=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathrm{e}^{tQ}=(p_{i,j}^{\prime}(t))_{i,j\in\mathcal{K}}
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle|p_{i,j}^{\prime}(t)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{K}}q_{i,\ell}p_{\ell,j}(t)\Big|=\Big|\sum_{\ell\neq i}\textstyle{\frac{c_{i}}{k-1}}p_{\ell,j}(t)-{c_{i}}p_{i,j}(t)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq{\tfrac{c_{i}}{k-1}}\sum_{\ell\neq i}|p_{\ell,j}(t)-p_{i,j}(t)|\leq{\textstyle\frac{c_{i}}{k-1}}\sum_{\ell\neq i}(|p_{\ell,j}(t)-\alpha_{j}|+|p_{i,j}(t)-\alpha_{j}|)

for
t\geq 0
,
i,j\in\mathcal{K}
. Next, (
4.5
) and
p_{i,j}(t)=p_{i,j}^{0}(ct),i,j\in\mathcal{K}
imply that

\displaystyle|p_{i,j}(t)-\alpha_{j}|\leq\mathrm{e}^{-c\omega t}{\textstyle\sqrt{\frac{c_{i}}{c_{j}}}},\qquad t\geq 0,\;i,j\in\mathcal{K}.

(4.12)

This in turn renders

\displaystyle|p_{i,j}^{\prime}(t)|

\displaystyle=c_{i}\mathrm{e}^{-c\omega t}\Big({\textstyle\sqrt{\frac{c_{i}}{c_{j}}}}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{k-1}}\sum_{\ell\neq i}{\textstyle\sqrt{\frac{c_{\ell}}{c_{j}}}}\Big),\qquad t\geq 0,\;i,j\in\mathcal{K}.

(4.13)

The last two estimates will be of key importance in what follows.

Step III, each
\widetilde{f_{i}}
belongs to
C[-\infty,\infty]
, and (
4.8
) holds.
For our first claim in this step it suffices to show that
g_{i}\in C[0,\infty]
, and since continuity of
g_{i}
is clear, it is enough to prove that the integral in (
4.11
) converges, as
t\to\infty,
to
\Pi m-m
where
m\coloneqq(m_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}},m_{i}\coloneqq\lim_{t\to\infty}f_{i}(t)
and
\Pi m=(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}\alpha_{j}m_{j})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
. Now, the last statement is true if
f_{i}(t)=m_{i}
for
t\geq 0
and
i\in\mathcal{K}
because then the integral equals
\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(t-s)Q}Qm\,\mathrm{d}s=\mathrm{e}^{tQ}m-m
and (
4.12
) implies that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathrm{e}^{tQ}m=\Pi m
. Hence, we are left with showing that the integral in question converges to
0
provided that
m=0
.

To this end, we let
\mathbb{R}^{k}
be equipped with the maximum norm. Then, (
4.13
) shows that the norm of
\mathrm{e}^{tQ}Q
as the operator in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
can be estimated as follows:

\|\mathrm{e}^{tQ}Q\|=\max_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}|p_{i,j}^{\prime}(t)|\leq cM_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-c\omega t}\qquad t\geq 0

(4.14)

where
M_{0}\coloneqq\max_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}\Big({\textstyle\sqrt{\frac{c_{i}}{c_{j}}}}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{k-1}}\sum_{\ell\neq i}{\textstyle\sqrt{\frac{c_{\ell}}{c_{j}}}}\Big)
.

Next, if
m=0
, given
\epsilon>0
we can find a
t_{0}>0
such that
|f_{i}(s)|<\epsilon,i\in\mathcal{K}
as long as
s\geq t_{0}
. Hence, for
t>t_{0}
the integral in question does not exceed
\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\|\mathrm{e}^{(t-s)Q}Q\|\,\|f\|_{{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}}\,\mathrm{d}s+\epsilon\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\mathrm{e}^{(t-s)Q}Q\|\,\mathrm{d}s
. Since, by (
4.14
) the first summand converges to
0
, as
t\to\infty
, and the second is bounded by
\frac{\epsilon M_{0}}{c\omega}
, our first claim follows.

As to the other claim, (
4.13
) implies also
\int_{0}^{\infty}|p_{i,j}^{\prime}(t)|\textrm{d}t\leq{\frac{c_{i}}{c\omega}}\Big({\textstyle\sqrt{\frac{c_{i}}{c_{j}}}}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{k-1}}\sum_{\ell\neq i}{\textstyle\sqrt{\frac{c_{\ell}}{c_{j}}}}\Big)
. Thus, (
4.11
) combined with

\int_{0}^{t}\big[\mathrm{e}^{(t-s)Q}Q\big](f_{i}(s))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\textrm{d}s=\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}\int_{0}^{t}p_{i,j}^{\prime}(t-s)f_{j}(s)\textrm{d}s\Big)_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
shows that

\displaystyle\|(g_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\|\leq\Big(1+2\max_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|p_{i,j}^{\prime}(t)|\textrm{d}t\Big)\leq M\|(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\|.

(4.15)

Hence, (
4.8
) is established.

Step IV,
\widetilde{f_{i}}
is in the domain of the generator of the basic cosine family.

Since
f_{i}
are twice continuously differentiable on
[0,\infty)
, (
4.11
) shows that so are
g_{i}
,
i\in\mathcal{K}
. Also, by differentiating (
4.11
) we recover (
4.10
), which in turn, when evaluated at
t=0
yields

g_{i}^{\prime}(0)=f_{i}^{\prime}(0)-2c_{i}\Big[f_{i}(0)-{{\textstyle\frac{1}{k-1}}\sum_{j\neq i}}\big({f_{j}}(t)\big)\Big]=-f_{i}^{\prime}(0),i\in\mathcal{K}.

This proves that the left-hand and right-hand derivatives of
\widetilde{f_{i}}
agree at
t=0
, and we see from (
4.10
) that
\widetilde{f_{i}}
is continuously differentiable on the entire
\mathbb{R}
. The same relation reveals, furthermore, that
g_{i}^{\prime}
is continuously differentiable on
[0,\infty)
, since so is
f_{i}^{\prime}
, and a little calculation using the already established
f^{\prime}_{i}(0)=-g^{\prime}_{i}(0)
yields
f^{\prime\prime}_{i}(0)=g^{\prime\prime}_{i}(0)
completing the proof that
\widetilde{f_{i}}
is twice continuously differentiable.

We are left with proving that the limits
\lim_{t\to\infty}g_{i}^{\prime\prime}(t)
exist and are finite. To this end, we note first that existence of finite limits
\lim_{t\to\infty}f_{i}^{\prime\prime}(t)
implies that
\lim_{t\to\infty}f_{i}^{\prime}(t)=0,i\in\mathcal{K}
. Furthermore, since, as we have established in Step III,
\lim_{t\to\infty}(g_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}=m+2(\Pi m-m)
, and (
4.10
) can be rewritten as
(g_{i}^{\prime}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}=(f_{i}^{\prime}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}-Q[(g_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}+(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}]
, we obtain
\lim_{t\to\infty}(g_{i}^{\prime}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}=-2Q\Pi m=0
. Hence, differentiating (
4.10
) once again shows that
\lim_{t\to\infty}g_{i}^{\prime\prime}(t)
exists and equals
\lim_{t\to\infty}f_{i}^{\prime\prime}(t)
. ∎

Lemma
4.2
tells us in particular that if (
4.7
) is to define a cosine family generated by
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c
, there is but one choice for extensions
\widetilde{f_{i}},i\in\mathcal{K}
. Hence, we introduce the extension operator

E\colon{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}\ni(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\mapsto(\widetilde{f}_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in\mathfrak{G}_{k},

where, for all
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}
(not just for
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in D(A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c))
,
\widetilde{f_{i}},i\in\mathcal{K}
are given by (
4.9
) and (
4.11
). In terms of
E
, (
4.7
) can be written as

\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c}(t)=RC_{D}(t)E,\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},

(4.16)

and, since, by the lemma,
\|E\|\leq M
, and clearly
\|R\|\leq 1
, we conclude that
\|\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c}(t)\|\leq M,t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Formula (
4.16
) defines a strongly continuous cosine family in
{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}
. Moreover, this cosine family is generated by
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c
.

Proof.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in D(A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c)
. By Lemma
4.2
,
RC_{D}(t)E(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in D(A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and the cosine equation for
\{C_{D}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}
implies that

R{C}_{D}(t){C}_{D}(s)E(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}RC_{D}(t+s)E(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}RC_{D}(t-s)E(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}

belongs to
D(A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
By uniqueness of extensions for elements of
D(A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c)
, established in Lemma
4.2
, it follows that
C_{D}(s)E(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
coincides with
ERC_{D}(s)E(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
. Hence, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle 2\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c}(t)\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c}(s)(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle=2R{C}_{D}(t)[ER{C}_{D}(s)E](f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt=2R{C}_{D}(t){C}_{D}(s)E(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt=R{C}_{D}(t+s)E(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}+R{C}_{D}(t-s)E(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt=\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c}(t+s)(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}+\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c}(t-s)(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}.

Since
D(A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c)
is dense in
{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}
, this proves that
\{\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a cosine family. The family is strongly continuous since so is the Cartesian product cosine family
\{C_{D}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}
.

Turning to the claim concerning the generator: Let
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in D(A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c)
. By Lemma
4.2
each
\widetilde{f_{i}}
belongs to the domain of the basic cosine family and thus we have

\lim_{t\to 0}\tfrac{2}{t^{2}}[C(t)\widetilde{f_{i}}-\widetilde{f_{i}}]=\widetilde{f_{i}}^{\prime\prime},i\in\mathcal{K}

in the sense of the supremum norm of
C[-\infty,\infty]
.
It follows that
\lim_{t\to 0}\tfrac{2}{t^{2}}[C_{D}(t)(\widetilde{f}_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}-(\widetilde{f}_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}]=(\widetilde{f_{i}}^{\prime\prime})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
in the norm of
\mathfrak{G}_{k}
.
Hence,

(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
belongs to the domain of the generator, say,
G
, of the cosine family
\{\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, and

G(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}=R(\widetilde{f_{i}}^{\prime\prime})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}=(f_{i}^{\prime\prime})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}=A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
. On the other hand,
D(A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c)
cannot be a proper subset of the domain of
G
since
A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c
is a Feller generator and, in particular, for
\lambda>0
,
\lambda-A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c
is injective and its range is
{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}
(see e.g.
[
11
]
p. 267).∎

Remark 1
.

A close inspection of the proof of Theorem
4.1
reveals that the space of extensions, that is, the range of
E
, is an invariant subspace for
\{C_{D}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}.
As restricted to this range,
\{C_{D}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a strongly continuous cosine family, and (

) says that
\{\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}c},t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is an isomorphic image of this cosine family.

5.
Convergence of cosine families

It may be thought unclear how do we know, in Theorem
4.1
, that
A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}
is a cosine family generator. The generation theorem for
A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}
can be proved directly, but can also be obtained as a by-product of Proposition
3.1
, Theorem
3.2
and estimate (
4.2
). Indeed, as proved in
[
14
]
*Chapter 60, if (
4.2
) holds,
Proposition
3.1
implies existence of a strongly continuous cosine family in
C(K_{1,k})
given by
\Cos(t)f=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc c(\varepsilon)}}(t)f,t\in\mathbb{R},f\in C(K_{1,k})
. Denoting by
G
the generator of this family we see also, by the Weierstrass formula, that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc c(\varepsilon)}}f=\mathrm{e}^{tG}f,t\geq 0,f\in C(K_{1,k})
. Hence, by Theorem
3.2
, we need to have
G=A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}
, showing that
A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}
is a cosine family generator.

In this section, we want to argue that the abstract Kelvin formula (
4.16
) provides an additional insight into both the convergence theorem (Theorem
4.1
) and the generation theorem for
A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}
. Details are given in Theorem
5.1
, below. Its notations involve the projection operator
\Pi:{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}\to\mathfrak{F}_{k}^{0}
given by

\Pi(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}=\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}\alpha_{j}f_{j}\Bigr)_{i\in\mathcal{K}}

where
\alpha_{i},i\in\mathcal{K}
are defined in (
4.3
). We recall that in Step III of the proof of Lemma
4.2
we used
\Pi
to denote the operator
\Pi:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
which formally acts in the same way as the one introduced here; below, notationally we will not distinguish between these two operators either.

Theorem 5.1
.

(a)

For
f\in C(K_{1,k})
,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc c(\varepsilon)}}(t)f=\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}}(t)f
uniformly in
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(b)

For
f\in C(K_{1,k})
,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc c(\varepsilon)}}f=\mathrm{e}^{tA^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}}
uniformly in
t\geq 0
.

(c)

The cosine family generated by
A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}
is given by the abstract Kelvin formula

\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}}(t)=RC_{D}(t)E,\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},

(5.1)

where
E:C(K_{1,k})(\overset{\text{iz}}{=}\mathfrak{F}_{k}^{0})\to\mathfrak{G}_{k}
maps a vector
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in\mathfrak{F}_{k}^{0}
to the vector
(\widetilde{f}_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\in\mathfrak{G}_{k}
of extensions of
f_{i}
s given by
\widetilde{f_{i}}(-x)=g_{i}(x),x\geq 0,i\in\mathcal{K}
and

(g_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}=2\Pi(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}-(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}.

(5.2)

Proof.

Condition (b) is a direct consequence of (a) by the Weierstrass formula (see
[
6
]
for details, if necessary). Also, by Theorem
4.3
,
\Cos_{A^{\textnormal{s-o}}_{\mathpzc c(\varepsilon)}}(t)
is given by (
4.16
) with
E=E(\varepsilon)
defined in (
4.9
) and (
4.11
) with
Q
replaced by
\varepsilon^{-1}Q
.
Therefore, since we already know that the limit cosine family is generated by
A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\alpha(\mathpzc c)}
, to show (a) and (c) simultaneously it suffices to prove that, for any
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
,

I_{\varepsilon}(t)\coloneqq\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\big[\mathrm{e}^{s\varepsilon^{-1}Q}Q\big](f_{i}(t-s))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\textrm{d}s

converges uniformly in
t\geq 0
to
\Pi(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}-(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
. In the special case of
f_{i}(x)=const.,x\geq 0,i\in\mathcal{K}
, this results is immediate, since then the integral is zero, and so is
\Pi(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}-(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
. Therefore, we are left with proving this convergence for
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
such that
f_{i}(0)=0,i\in\mathcal{K}
.

Under this assumption, given
\epsilon>0
one can find a
\delta>0
such that
|f_{i}(s)|<\epsilon,i\in\mathcal{K}
as long as
s<\delta
. Therefore, by (
4.14
), for
t\leq\delta
,
\|I_{\varepsilon}(t)\|\leq\frac{\epsilon cM_{0}}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\varepsilon^{-1}c\omega s}\,\mathrm{d}s<\frac{\epsilon M_{0}}{\omega}
, and so

\|I_{\varepsilon}(t)-\Pi(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}+(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\|\leq(2+\tfrac{M_{0}}{\omega})\epsilon,\qquad t\in[0,\delta],\varepsilon>0.

(5.3)

Also, there is a
\delta_{1}
, and without loss of generality we can assume that
\delta_{1}<\delta
, such that
|s|<\delta_{1}
implies
|f_{i}(t-s)-f_{i}(t)|<\epsilon,i\in\mathcal{K},t>\delta
, because
f_{i},i\in\mathcal{K}
, being members of
C[0,\infty]
, are uniformly continuous. Hence, introducing
J_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{1},t)\coloneqq\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{0}^{\delta_{1}}\big[\mathrm{e}^{s\varepsilon^{-1}Q}Q\big](f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\,\mathrm{d}s
, and arguing as above we obtain

\|I_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{1})-J_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{1},t)\|\leq\frac{\epsilon M_{0}}{\omega}.

At the same time, using estimate (
4.14
) again,

\|I_{\varepsilon}(t)-I_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{1})\|\leq M_{0}\|(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\|_{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\varepsilon^{-1}\delta_{1}}.
Hence, for
M_{1}\coloneqq\max(2+\tfrac{M_{0}}{\omega},M_{0}\|(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\|_{\mathfrak{F}_{k}}),

\|I_{\varepsilon}(t)-J_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{1},t)\|\leq M_{1}(\epsilon+\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\varepsilon^{-1}\delta_{1}}),\qquad t\geq\delta,\varepsilon>0.

(5.4)

Finally, we have
J_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{1},t)=\mathrm{e}^{\varepsilon^{-1}\delta_{1}Q}(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}-(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
and we know that (a) operators
\mathrm{e}^{\varepsilon^{-1}\delta_{1}Q},\varepsilon>0
are contractions in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
(and thus are in particular equibounded) and
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathrm{e}^{\varepsilon^{-1}\delta_{1}Q}v=\Pi v
for any
v\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and (b) the set
K\coloneqq\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{k};v=(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\text{ for some }t\geq\delta\}
is compact in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. It follows that for sufficiently small
\varepsilon

\sup_{t\geq\delta}\|J_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{1},t)-\Pi(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}+(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\|\leq\epsilon.

This, when combined with (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), shows that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{t\geq 0}\|I_{\varepsilon}(t)-\Pi(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}+(f_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\|\leq(M_{1}+1)\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, the proof is complete.
∎

We note that point (c) in the theorem just proved implies that each
A^{\textnormal{sp}}_{\mathpzc q}
is a cosine family generator, as long as
\beta=0
. Indeed, given
\alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}

It is also interesting to note that the proof of convergence of the integral

to
\Pi(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}-(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{K}}

Corollary 5.2

Exel-Pardo algebras of self-similar k k -graphs
Hossein Larki

Abstract
. We introduce the Exel-Pardo ∗ * -algebra EP R ​ ( G , Λ ) \mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda) associated to a self-similar k k -graph ( G , Λ , φ ) (G,\Lambda,\varphi) . We also prove the ℤ k \mathbb{Z}^{k} -graded and Cuntz-Krieger uniqueness theorems for such algebras and investigate their ideal structure. In particular, we modify the graded uniqueness theorem for self-similar 1-graphs, and then apply it to present EP R ​ ( G , Λ ) \mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda) as a Steinberg algebra and to study the ideal structure.

1.1.
Notation and terminology

Let
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. For
k\geq 1
, we regard
\mathbb{N}^{k}
as an additive semigroup with the generators
e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}
. We use
\leq
for the partial order on
\mathbb{N}^{k}
given by
m\leq n
if and only if
m_{i}\leq n_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
. We also write
m\vee n
and
m\wedge n
for the coordinate-wise maximum and minimum, respectively.

A
k
-graph
is a countable small category
\Lambda=(\Lambda^{0},\Lambda,r,s)
equipped with a
degree functor

d:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{k}
satisfying the unique factorization property: for
\mu\in\Lambda
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
with
d(\mu)=m+n
, then there exist unique
\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda
such that
d(\alpha)=m
,
d(\beta)=n
, and
\mu=\alpha\beta
. We usually denote
\mu(0,m):=\alpha
and
\mu(m,d(\mu)):=\beta
. We refer to
\Lambda^{0}
as the vertex set and define
\Lambda^{n}:=\{\mu\in\Lambda:d(\mu)=n\}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
. For
A,B\subseteq\Lambda
, define
AB=\{\mu\nu:\mu\in A,\nu\in B,~\mathrm{and}~s(\mu)=r(\nu)\}
. Also, for
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda
, define
\Lambda^{min}(\mu,\nu)=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\Lambda\times\Lambda:\mu\alpha=\nu\beta,d(\mu\alpha)=d(\mu)\vee d(\nu)\}
.

We say that
\Lambda
is
row-finite
if
v\Lambda^{n}
is finite for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
and
v\in\Lambda^{0}
. A
source
in
\Lambda
is a vertex
v\in\Lambda^{0}
such that
v\Lambda^{e_{i}}=\emptyset
for some
1\leq i\leq k
.

Standing assumption.
Throughout the article, we work only with row-finite
k
-graphs without sources.

Let
\Omega_{k}:=\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\times\mathbb{N}^{k}:m\leq n\}
. By defining
(m,n).(n,l):=(m,l)
,
r(m,n):=(m,m)
, and
s(m,n):=(n,n)
, then
\Omega_{k}
is a row-finite
k
-graph without sources. A graph homomorphism
x:\Omega_{k}\rightarrow\Lambda
is called an
infinite path of
\Lambda
with the range
r(x)=x(0,0)
, and we write
\Lambda^{\infty}
for the set of all infinite paths of
\Lambda
.

Exel-Pardo algebras of self-similar

In this section, we associate a

Following
[
9
]
, we consider
*
-algebras over
*

R
be a unital commutative
*
-ring. Recall that a

-algebra over

is an algebra

(a^{*})^{*}=a
,
(ab)^{*}=b^{*}a^{*}

(ra+b)^{*}=r^{*}a^{*}+b^{*}

if

, and

.

Definition 2.1

(
[
2
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph without sources. A
Kumjian-Pask
\Lambda
-family
is a collection
\{s_{\mu}:\mu\in\Lambda\}
of partial isometries in a
*
-algebra

such that

(KP1)

\{s_{v}:v\in\Lambda^{0}\}
is a family of pairwise orthogonal projections;

(KP2)

s_{\mu\nu}=s_{\mu}s_{\nu}
for all

;

(KP3)

s_{\mu}^{*}s_{\mu}=s_{s(\mu)}
for all

; and

for all

.

2.1.
Self-similar
k
-graphs and their algebras

Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph without sources. An
automorphism of
\Lambda
is a bijection
\psi:\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda
such that
\psi(\Lambda^{n})\subseteq\Lambda^{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
with the properties
s\circ\psi=\psi\circ s
and
r\circ\psi=\psi\circ r
. We denote by
\mathrm{Aut}(\Lambda)
the group of automorphisms on
\Lambda
. Furthermore, if
G
is a countable discrete group, an
action
of
G
on
\Lambda
is a group homomorphism
g\mapsto\psi_{g}
from
G
into
\mathrm{Aut}(\Lambda)
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph without sources and
G
a discrete group with identity
e_{G}
. We say that a triple
(G,\Lambda,\varphi)
is a
self-similar
k
-graph
whenever the following properties hold:

(1)

G
acts on
\Lambda
by a group homomorphism
g\mapsto\psi_{g}
. We prefer to write
g\cdot\mu
for
\psi_{g}(\mu)
to ease the notation.

(2)

\varphi:G\times\Lambda\rightarrow G
is a 1-cocycle for the action
G\curvearrowright\Lambda
such that for every
g\in G
,
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda
and
v\in\Lambda^{0}
we have

(a)

\varphi(gh,\mu)=\varphi(g,h\cdot\mu)\varphi(h,\mu)\hskip 14.22636pt
(the 1-cocycle property),

(b)

g\cdot(\mu\nu)=(g\cdot\mu)(\varphi(g,\mu)\cdot\nu)\hskip 14.22636pt
(the self-similar equation),

(c)

\varphi(g,\mu\nu)=\varphi(\varphi(g,\mu),\nu)
, and

(d)

\varphi(g,v)=g
.

For convenience, we usually write
(G,\Lambda)
instead of
(G,\Lambda,\varphi)
.

Remark 2.3
.

In
[
15
]
, the authors used the notation

for
\varphi(g,\mu)
. However, we would prefer to follow
[
7
,
8
,
9

for writing

.

Remark 2.4
.

If in equation (2)(a) of Definition
2.2
, we set

, then we get
\varphi(e_{G},\mu)=e_{G}
for every
\mu\in\Lambda
. Moreover,
[
15
, Lemma 3.5(ii)]
shows that
\varphi(g,\mu)\cdot v=g\cdot v
for all
g\in G
,
v\in\Lambda^{0}
, and
\mu\in\Lambda
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(G,\Lambda)
be a self-similar
k
-graph. An
Exel-Pardo
(G,\Lambda)
-family
(or briefly
(G,\Lambda)
-family
) is a set

\{s_{\mu}:\mu\in\Lambda\}\cup\{u_{v,g}:v\in\Lambda^{0},g\in G\}

in a

(1)

\{s_{\mu}:\mu\in\Lambda\}
is a Kumjian-Pask
\Lambda

(2)

u_{v,e_{G}}=s_{v}
for all
v\in\Lambda^{0}
,

(3)

u_{v,g}^{*}=u_{g^{-1}\cdot v,g^{-1}}
for all
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and
g\in G
,

(4)

u_{v,g}s_{\mu}=\delta_{v,g\cdot r(\mu)}s_{g\cdot\mu}u_{g\cdot s(\mu),\varphi(g,\mu)}
for all
v\in\Lambda^{0}
,
\mu\in\Lambda
, and
g\in G
,

(5)

u_{v,g}u_{w,h}=\delta_{v,g\cdot w}u_{v,gh}
for all
v,w\in\Lambda^{0}
and
g,h\in G
.

Then the
Exel-Pardo algebra

\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
is the universal
*
-algebra over
R
generated by a
(G,\Lambda)
-family
\{s_{\mu},u_{v,g}\}
.

Recall that the universality of
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
means that for every
(G,\Lambda)
-family
\{S_{\mu},U_{v,g}\}
in a
*
-algebra
A
, there exists a
*
-homomorphism
\phi:\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)\rightarrow A
such that
\phi(s_{\mu})=S_{\mu}
and
\phi(u_{v,g})=U_{v,g}
for all
v\in\Lambda^{0}
,
\mu\in\Lambda
, and
g\in G
. (See Subsection
2.2
for the construction of
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
.) Throughout the paper, we will denote by
\{s_{\mu},u_{v,g}\}
the
(G,\Lambda)
-family generating
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
.

2.2.
The construction of
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)

Let
(G,\Lambda)
be a self-similar
k
-graph as in Definition
2.2
. The following is a standard construction of a universal algebra
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
subject to desired relations. Consider the set of formal symbols

S=\left\{S_{\mu},S_{\mu}^{*},U_{v,g},U_{v,g}^{*}:\mu\in\Lambda,v\in\Lambda^{0},g\in G\right\}.

Let
X=w(S)
be the collection of finite words in
S
. We equip the free
R
-module

\mathbb{F}_{R}(X):=\big\{\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}x_{i}:l\geq 1,r_{i}\in R,x_{i}\in X\big\}

with the multiplication

\big(\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}x_{i}\big)\big(\sum_{j=1}^{l^{\prime}}s_{j}y_{j}\big):=\sum_{i,j}r_{i}s_{j}x_{i}y_{j},

and the involution

\big(\sum r_{i}x_{i}\big)^{*}:=\big(\sum r_{i}^{*}x_{i}^{*}\big)

where
x^{*}=s_{l}^{*}\ldots s_{1}^{*}
for each
x=s_{1}\ldots s_{l}
. Then
\mathbb{F}_{R}(X)
is a
*
-algebra over
R
. If
I
is the (two-sided and self-adjoint) ideal of
\mathbb{F}_{R}(X)
containing the roots of relations (1)-(5) in Definition
2.5
, then the quotient
\mathbb{F}_{R}(X)/I
is the Exel-Pardo algebra
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
with the desired universal property. Let us define
s_{\mu}:=S_{\mu}+I
and
u_{v,g}:=U_{v,g}+I
for every
\mu\in\Lambda
,
v\in\Lambda^{0}
, and
g\in G
. In case
(G,\Lambda)
is pseudo free (Definition
5.1
), Theorem
4.2
insures that all generators
\{s_{\mu},u_{v,g}\}
of
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
are nonzero.

Proposition
2.7
below describes the elements of
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
. First, see a simple lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(G,\Lambda)
be a self-similar graph (as in Definition
2.2
) and
\{S,U\}
a
(G,\Lambda)
-family. If
S_{\mu}U_{v,g}S_{\nu}^{*}\neq 0
where
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda
,
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and
g\in G
, then
s(\mu)=v=g\cdot s(\nu)
.

Proof.

If
a=S_{\mu}U_{v,g}S_{\nu}^{*}
is nonzero, then by Definition
2.5
we can write

\displaystyle S_{\mu}U_{v,g}S_{\nu}^{*}

\displaystyle=S_{\mu}(S_{s(\mu)}U_{v,g})S_{\nu}^{*}

\displaystyle=S_{\mu}(U_{s(\mu),e_{G}}U_{v,g})S_{\nu}^{*}

\displaystyle=S_{\mu}(\delta_{s(\mu),e_{G}\cdot v}U_{s(\mu),g})S_{\nu}^{*}.

Now, the hypothesis
a\neq 0
forces
s(\mu)=v
. On the other hand, a similar computation gives

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=S_{\mu}(U_{v,g}S_{s(\nu)})S_{\nu}^{*}

\displaystyle=S_{\mu}(U_{v,g}U_{s(\nu),e_{G}})S_{\nu}^{*}

\displaystyle=S_{\mu}(\delta_{v,g\cdot s(\nu)}U_{v,g})S_{\nu}^{*},

and thus
v=g\cdot s(\nu)
.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(G,\Lambda)
be a self-similar graph. Then

(2.1)

\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)=\mathrm{span}_{R}\{s_{\mu}u_{s(\mu),g}s_{\nu}^{*}:g\in G,~\mu,\nu\in\Lambda,~\mathrm{and}~s(\mu)=g\cdot s(\nu)\}.

Proof.

Define
M:=\mathrm{span}_{R}\{s_{\mu}u_{s(\mu),g}s_{\nu}^{*}:g\in G,~\mu,\nu\in\Lambda\}
. For every
g,h\in G
and
\mu,\nu,\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda
with
\alpha=\nu\alpha^{\prime}
for some
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Lambda
, the relations of Definition
2.5
imply that

\displaystyle\big(s_{\mu}u_{s(\mu),g}s_{\nu}^{*}\big)\big(s_{\alpha}u_{s(\alpha),h}s_{\beta}^{*}\big)

\displaystyle=s_{\mu}u_{s(\mu),g}(s_{\nu}^{*}s_{\alpha})u_{s(\alpha),h}s_{\beta}^{*}

\displaystyle=s_{\mu}u_{s(\mu),g}(s_{\alpha^{\prime}})u_{s(\alpha),h}s_{\beta}^{*}

\displaystyle=s_{\mu}\big(\delta_{s(\mu),g\cdot r(\alpha^{\prime})}s_{g\cdot\alpha^{\prime}}u_{g\cdot s(\alpha^{\prime}),\varphi(g,\alpha^{\prime})}u_{s(\alpha),h}\big)s_{\beta}^{*}

\displaystyle=\delta_{s(\mu),g\cdot s(\nu)}s_{\mu(g\cdot\alpha^{\prime})}\big(\delta_{g\cdot s(\alpha^{\prime}),\varphi(g,\alpha^{\prime})\cdot s(\alpha)}u_{g\cdot s(\alpha^{\prime}),\varphi(g,\alpha^{\prime})h}\big)s_{\beta}^{*}\hskip 14.22636pt(\mathrm{as}~~r(\alpha^{\prime})=s(\nu)).

In the case
\nu=\alpha\nu^{\prime}
for some
\nu^{\prime}\in\Lambda
, the above multiplication may be computed similarly, and otherwise is zero. Hence,
M
is closed under multiplication. Also, we have

\big(s_{\mu}u_{s(\mu),g}s_{\nu}^{*}\big)^{*}=s_{\nu}u_{g^{-1}\cdot s(\mu),g^{-1}}s_{\mu}^{*},

so
M^{*}\subseteq M
. Since

s_{\mu}=s_{\mu}u_{s(\mu),e_{G}}s_{s(\mu)}^{*}\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 14.22636ptu_{v,g}=s_{v}u_{v,g}s_{g\cdot v}^{*}

for all
g\in G
,
v\in\Lambda^{0}
, and
\mu\in\Lambda
, it follows that
M
is a
*
-subalgebra of
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
containing the generators of
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
. In light of Lemma
2.6
, this concludes the identification (
2.1
).
∎

2.3.
The unital case

In case

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(G,\Lambda)
be a self-similar

be nonzero in
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)

\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)

Proof.

If
\Lambda^{0}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{l}\}
is finite, then using identification (

is the unit of

\Lambda^{0}
is infinite, then the set
\{s_{v}:v\in\Lambda^{0}\}\subseteq\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)

), there is no element of
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
which acts as an identity on each element of
\{s_{v}:v\in\Lambda^{0}\}
.
∎

Note that if

is a

-family in a

for all

(relations (2) and (5) of Definition

). (See

]
for the definition of multiplier algebras.) Thus relations (3) and (5) of Definition

imply that

, defined by

, is a unitary
*
-representation of
G
on

. In particular, in case

is finite,

’s lie all in

, and we may describe Definition

as the following:

.

Let
(G,\Lambda)

4.
A graded Uniqueness Theorem

In this section, we prove a graded uniqueness theorem for
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
which generalizes and modifies
[
9
, Theorem A]
for self-similar
k
-graphs. This modification, in particular, helps us to prove Theorems
5.5
and
6.8
.

Let us first recall some definitions. Let
\Gamma
be a group and
A
be an algebra over a ring
R
.
A
is called
\Gamma
-graded
(or briefly,
graded
whenever the group is clear) if there is a family of
R
-submodules
\{A_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
of
A
such that
A=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}A_{\gamma}
and
A_{\gamma}A_{\gamma^{\prime}}\subseteq A_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}
for all
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
. Then each set
A_{\gamma}
is called a
\gamma
-homogeneous component of
A
. In this case, we say an ideal
I
of
A
is
\Gamma
-graded
if
I=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}(I\cap A_{\gamma})
. Note that an ideal
I
of
A
is
\Gamma
-graded if and only if it is generated by a subset of
\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}A_{\gamma}
, the homogeneous elements of
A
.

Furthermore, if
A
and
B
are two
\Gamma
-graded algebras over
R
, a homomorphism
\phi:A\rightarrow B
is said to be a
graded homomorphism
if
\phi(A_{\gamma})\subseteq B_{\gamma}
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Hence the kernel of a graded homomorphism is always a graded ideal. Also, if
I
is a graded ideal of
A
, then there is a natural
\Gamma
-grading
(A_{\gamma}+I)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
on the quotient algebra
A/I
, and thus the quotient map
A\rightarrow A/I
is a graded homomorphism.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(G,\Lambda)
be a self-similar
k
-graph. If for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, we define

\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)_{n}:=\mathrm{span}_{R}\big\{s_{\mu}u_{s(\mu),g}s_{\nu}^{*}:g\in G,~\mu,\nu\in\Lambda,~\mathrm{and}~d(\mu)-d(\nu)=n\big\},

then
(\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-grading on
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Consider the free
*
-algebra
\mathbb{F}_{R}(X)
and its ideal
I
as in Subsection
2.2
. If we define

\theta(S_{\mu}):=d(\mu),~\theta(S_{\mu}^{*}):=-d(\mu),~\mathrm{and}~\theta(U_{v,g}):=0

for all
g\in G
,
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and
\mu\in\Lambda
, then
\theta
induces a
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-grading on
\mathbb{F}_{R}(X)
. Also, since the generators of
I
are all homogenous,
I
is a graded ideal. Therefore,
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)\cong\mathbb{F}_{R}(X)/I
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-graded algebra, and Proposition
2.7
concludes the result.
∎

Theorem 4.2

(Graded Uniqueness)
.

Let
(G,\Lambda)
be a self-similar
k
-graph. Let
\phi:\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)\rightarrow B
be a
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-graded
R
-algebra
*
-homomorphism into a
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-graded
*
-algebra

. If
\phi(a)\neq 0
for every nonzero element of the form
a=\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}u_{v,g_{i}}
with
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and
g_{i}^{-1}\cdot v=g_{j}^{-1}\cdot v
for

, then

is injective.

Proof.

For convenience we write
A=\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
. Since
A=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}A_{n}
and
\phi
preserves the grading, it suffices to show that
\phi
is injective on each

. So, fix some
b\in A_{n}
, and assume
\phi(b)=0
. By equation (
2.1
), we can write

(4.1)

b=\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}s_{\mu_{i}}u_{w_{i},g_{i}}s_{\nu_{i}}^{*}

where
w_{i}=s(\mu_{i})=g_{i}\cdot s(\nu_{i})
and

for

\displaystyle s_{\mu_{i}}u_{w_{i},g_{i}}s_{\nu_{i}}^{*}

\displaystyle=s_{\mu_{i}}(s_{s(\mu_{i})})u_{w_{i},g_{i}}s_{\nu_{i}}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum s_{\mu_{i}}(s_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}^{*})u_{w_{i},g_{i}}s_{\nu_{i}}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum s_{\mu_{i}\lambda}\big(s_{\nu_{i}}u_{w_{i},g_{i}}^{*}s_{\lambda}\big)^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum s_{\mu_{i}\lambda}\big(s_{\nu_{i}}u_{g_{i}^{-1}\cdot w_{i},g_{i}^{-1}}s_{\lambda}\big)^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum s_{\mu_{i}\lambda}\big(s_{\nu_{i}}s_{g_{i}^{-1}\cdot\lambda}u_{g_{i}^{-1}\cdot s(\lambda),\varphi(g_{i}^{-1},\lambda)}\big)^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum s_{\mu_{i}\lambda}u_{\varphi(g_{i}^{-1},\lambda)^{-1}g_{i}^{-1}\cdot s(\lambda),\varphi(g_{i}^{-1},\lambda)^{-1}}s_{\nu_{i}(g_{i}^{-1}\cdot\lambda)}^{*}

where the above summations are on

d(\mu_{i})=n^{\prime}
and
d(\nu_{i})=n^{\prime}-n
. Now, for any

and hypothesis forces

. Therefore,

\sum_{i\in[j]}r_{i}s_{\mu_{i}}u_{w_{i},g_{i}}s_{\nu_{i}}^{*}=s_{\mu_{j}}\big(\sum_{i\in[j]}r_{i}u_{w_{i},g_{i}}\big)s_{\nu_{j}}^{*}=0.

Since the index set

is a disjoint union of

5.
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
as a Steinberg algebra

In this section, we want to prove an Steinberg algebra model for
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
. Although our result will be the
k
-graph generalization of
[
9
, Theorem B]
, note that our proof relies on the graded uniqueness theorem, Theorem
4.2
, and is completely deferent from that of
[
9
, Theorem B]
. This gives us a much easier and shorter proof.

Let us first review some terminology about groupoids; see
[
19
]
for more details. A
groupoid
is a small category
\mathcal{G}
with inverses. For each
\alpha\in\mathcal{G}
, we may define the range
r(\alpha):=\alpha\alpha^{-1}
and the source
s(\alpha):=\alpha^{-1}\alpha
satisfying
r(\alpha)\alpha=\alpha=\alpha s(\alpha)
. It follows that for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{G}
, the composition
\alpha\beta
is well-defined if and only if
s(\alpha)=r(\beta)
. The
unit space of
\mathcal{G}
is
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}:=\{\alpha^{-1}\alpha:\alpha\in\mathcal{G}\}
. Throughout the paper we work with
topological groupoids
, which are ones equipped with a topology such that the maps
r
and
s
are continuous. Then a
bisection
is a subset
B\subseteq\mathcal{G}
such that both restrictions
r|_{B}
and
s|_{B}
are homeomorphisms. In case
\mathcal{G}
has a basis of compact open bisections,
\mathcal{G}
is called an
ample groupoid
.

Let
(G,\Lambda)
be a self-similar
k
-graph. We also recall the groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{G,\Lambda}
introduced in
[
15
]
. Let
C(\mathbb{N}^{k},G)
be the group of all maps form
\mathbb{N}^{k}
to
G
with the pointwise multiplication. For
f,g\in C(\mathbb{N}^{k},G)
, define the equivalence relation
f\sim g
in case there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
such that
f(n)=g(n)
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Write
Q(\mathbb{N}^{k},G):=C(\mathbb{N}^{k},G)/\sim
. Also, for each
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, let
\mathcal{T}_{z}:C(\mathbb{N}^{k},G)\rightarrow C(\mathbb{N}^{k},G)
be the automorphism defined by

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(G,\Lambda)

\phi(s_{\mu})=1_{Z(\mu,e_{G},s(\mu))}\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\phi(u_{v,g})=1_{Z(v,g,g^{-1}\cdot v)}

For each

Since

[

is ample with compact open base
\mathcal{B}_{G,\Lambda}
. Since each element

\phi

We will show the injectivity of

, we assume

∎

Theorem 5.10

(The Cuntz-Krieger uniqueness)
.

Let
(G,\Lambda)
be a pseudo free self-similar

(G,\Lambda)
be also

-aperiodic. Suppose that
\phi:\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)\rightarrow A

-algebra homomorphism from
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)

-algebra

is injective.

Proof.

First note that
\mathcal{G}_{G,\Lambda}
is a Hausdorff ample groupoid by
[
16
, Proposition 3.11]
, and that

is a basis for
\mathcal{G}_{G,\Lambda}
consisting compact open bisections. Also,
[
16
, Lemma 3.12]

Denote by

By an

Let

I_{H}
is diagonal-invariant,

r_{j}s_{v}=r_{j}u_{v,e_{G}}=\mathcal{E}(\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}u_{v,g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}})\in I_{H}.

As
v\notin H
, Lemma
6.6
forces
r_{j}=0
for each
1\leq j\leq l
, hence
a=0
. Now Theorem
4.2
implies that
\psi
is an isomorphism.
∎

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
(G,\Lambda)
be pseudo free self-similar
k
-graph. Then
H\mapsto I_{H}
is a one-to-one correspondence between
G
-saturated
G
-hereditary subsets of
\Lambda^{0}
and basic,
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-graded and diagonal-invariant ideals of
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
, with inverse
I\mapsto H_{I}
.

Proof.

The injectivity of
H\mapsto I_{H}
follows from Lemma
6.6
. Indeed, if
I_{H}=I_{K}
for
G
-saturated
G
-hereditary subsets
H,K\subseteq\Lambda^{0}
, then Lemma
6.6
yields that
H=H_{I_{H}}=H_{I_{K}}=K
.

To see the surjectivity, we take a basic,
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-graded and diagonal-invariant ideal
I
of
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
, and then prove
I=I_{H_{I}}
. Write
J:=I_{H_{I}}
for convenience. By Proposition
6.7
we may consider
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda\setminus\Lambda H_{I})\cong\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)/J
as a
*
-
R
-algebra. Let
\{s_{\mu},u_{v,g}\}
and
\{t_{\mu},w_{v,g}\}
be the generators of
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
and
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)/J
, respectively. Since
J\subseteq I
, we may define the quotient map
q:\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)/J\rightarrow\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)/I
such that

q(t_{\mu})=s_{\mu}+I\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 14.22636ptq(w_{v,g})=u_{v,g}+I

for all
\mu\in\Lambda
,
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and
g\in G
. Notice that
q
preserves the grading because
I
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-graded ideal. So, we can apply Theorem
4.2
to show that
q
is an isomorphism. To do this, fix an element of the form
a=\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}w_{v,g_{i}}
with
v\in\Lambda^{0}\setminus H_{I}
such that
g_{i}^{-1}\cdot v=g_{j}^{-1}\cdot v
for
1\leq i,j\leq l
and
q(a)=0
. Then
\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}u_{v,g_{i}}\in I
. As before, we may also assume that the
g_{i}
’s are distinct. Thus, for each
1\leq j\leq l
, we have

b_{j}:=\big(\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}u_{v,g_{i}}\big)\big(u_{g_{j}^{-1}\cdot v,g_{j}^{-1}}\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}u_{v,g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}}\in I.

Since
I
is diagonal-invariant and basic, the case
r_{j}\neq 0
yields
r_{j}s_{v}=r_{j}u_{v,e_{G}}=\mathcal{E}(b_{j})\in I
, and thus
s_{v}\in I
and
v\in H_{I}
, which contradicts the choice of
v
. It follows that
r_{j}
’s are all zero, and hence
a=0
. Now Theorem
4.2
implies that
q
is injective, or equivalently
I=J=I_{H_{I}}
as desired.
∎

In the end, we remark the following about
Q(\mathbb{N}^{k},G)\rtimes_{\mathcal{T}}\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-graded ideals of
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
.

Remark 6.9
.

Let
(G,\Lambda)
be a pseudo free self-similar
k
-graph and
\mathcal{G}_{G,\Lambda}
be the associated groupoid. Denote by
\Gamma:=Q(\mathbb{N}^{k},G)\rtimes_{\mathcal{T}}\mathbb{Z}^{k}
the group introduced in Section
5
. If we define
c:\mathcal{G}_{G,\Lambda}\rightarrow\Gamma
by
c(x;\gamma;y):=\gamma
, then
c
is a cocycle on
\mathcal{G}_{G,\Lambda}
because
c(\alpha\beta)=c(\alpha)*_{\Gamma}c(\beta)
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{G}_{G,\Lambda}
with
s(\alpha)=r(\beta)
. Hence, it induces a
\Gamma
-grading on
A_{R}(\mathcal{G}_{G,\Lambda})=\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
with the homogenous components

A_{\gamma}:=\mathrm{span}_{R}\{1_{V}:V\subseteq c^{-1}(\gamma)\mathrm{~is~a~compact~open~bisection~}\}

(see
[
6
, Proposition 5.1]
for example). By a similar argument as in
[
6
, §6.5]
and combining Theorem
5.5
and
[
6
, Theorem 5.3]
, we may obtain that the ideals of the form
I_{H}
, described in Theorem
6.8
above, are precisely the basic,
\Gamma
-graded ideals of
\mathrm{EP}_{R}(G,\Lambda)
.

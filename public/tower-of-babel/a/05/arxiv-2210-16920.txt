Locally standard measure algebras
Oksana Bezushchak, Bogdana Oliynyk

Abstract
We parameterize countable locally standard measure algebras by pairs of a Steinitz number and a real number greater or equal to 1 . 1. This is an analog of the theorems of J.Dixmier and A.A.Baranov.

Introduction

Let
\mathbf{F}_{2}
be the field of order
2.
By a
Boolean algebra
we mean an associative commutative algebra over the field
\mathbf{F}_{2}
satisfying the identity
x^{2}=x.

Let
[0,\infty)
denote the set of nonnegative real numbers. Let
H
be a Boolean algebra. We call a function
\mu:H\rightarrow[0,\infty)
a
measure
if

(1)

\mu(a)=0
if and only if
a=0,

a\in H;

(2)

if
a,b\in H
and
ab=0,
then
\mu(a+b)=\mu(a)+\mu(b).

Following A.Horn and A.Tarski
[
11
]
, we call a Boolean algebra
H
with a measure
\mu:H\rightarrow[0,\infty)
a
measure algebra
. For more information on measure algebras, see
[
11
,
12
,
15
]
.

If
(H,\mu)
is a measure algebra, then the distance
d_{H}(a,b)=\mu(a-b)
makes it a metric space.

Example 1
.

The Boolean algebra
\mathbf{St}_{n}=\mathbf{F}_{2}^{n},

\mathbf{F}_{2}=\{0,1\},
with the function

\mu_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\big(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}\big)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all}\hskip 9.24994ptx_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\{0,1\}

is a measure algebra. We call the measure algebra
(\mathbf{St}_{n},\mu_{n})

standard
. For all elements
a,b\in\mathbf{St}_{n}
the distance
d_{\mathbf{St}_{n}}(a,b)
equals the number of coordinates where
a
and
b
differ, divided by
n.

Example 2
.

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of positive integers. For a sequence
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots)\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
, define the pseudomeasure function

\widetilde{\mu}(\mathbf{a})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup\frac{1}{n}\big(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\big).

Then
I=\{\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\,\big|\,\widetilde{\mu}(\mathbf{a})=0\}
is an ideal of the Boolean algebra
\mathbf{F}_{2}^{\mathbb{N}}.

Consider the Boolean algebra
B=\mathbf{F}_{2}^{\mathbb{N}}/I
and the measure

\mu(\mathbf{a}+I)=\widetilde{\mu}(\mathbf{a}),\hskip 9.24994pt\mathbf{a}\in\mathbf{F}_{2}^{\mathbb{N}}.

The measure algebra
(B,\mu)
is called
Besicovich measure algebra
(see
[
19
]
).

Example 3
.

Let
X
be an infinite set and let
H
be the Boolean algebra of finite subsets of
X
, including the empty one. The measure
\mu(a)=\#a,

a\in H
, makes
(H,\mu)
a measure algebra. If the set
X
is countable, then we denote the measure algebra
(H,\mu)
as
H(\infty)
.

In order to introduce the next series of examples we need to start with the concept of a Steinitz number.

A
Steinitz number
(
[
18
]
) is an infinite formal
product of the form

\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}}p^{r_{p}},

where
\mathbb{P}
is the set of all primes,
r_{p}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0,\infty\}
for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
.
We can define the product of two Steinitz numbers by the rule:

\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}}p^{r_{p}}\cdot\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}}p^{k_{p}}=\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}}p^{r_{p}+k_{p}},\ r_{p},k_{p}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0,\infty\},

where we assume, that

r_{p}+k_{p}=\begin{cases}r_{p}+k_{p},&\text{if $r_{p}<\infty$ and $k_{p}<\infty$, }\\
\infty,&\text{in other cases}\end{cases}.

By symbol
\mathbb{SN}
we denote the set of all Steinitz numbers. Obviously, the set of all positive integers
\mathbb{N}
is the subset of
\mathbb{SN}
. The elements of the set
\mathbb{SN}\setminus\mathbb{N}
are called
infinite Steinitz numbers
.

Example 4
.

An infinite sequence
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots)\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}

is said to be
periodic
if there exists a positive integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that the equality
a_{i}=a_{i+k}
holds for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. In this case the number
k
is called a
period
of the sequence

\mathbf{a}
.

Let
u
be a Steinitz number. A periodic sequence
\mathbf{a}
is called
u
-periodic
if its minimal
period is a divisor of
u
.

Let
\mathcal{H}(u)
be the set of all
u
-periodic sequences. Clearly,
\mathcal{H}(u)
is a Boolean subalgebra of
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
. The function

\mu_{\mathcal{H}(u)}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots)=\frac{1}{k}\big(a_{1}+\ldots+a_{k}\big),

where
k
is a period of the sequence
(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots),
makes
(\mathcal{H}(u),\mu_{\mathcal{H}(u)})
a measure algebra.

A measure algebra
(H,\mu)
is called
unital
if the Boolean algebra
H
contains
1
and
\mu(1)=1.
In this case it is easy to see that
\mu(H)\subseteq[0,1]
and
1
is the only element of measure
1.

The measure algebras of examples
1
,
2
,
4
are unital. The measure algebras of example
3
are not unital.

If
(H,\mu)
is a measure algebra and
h\in H
is a nonzero element, then
hH
is a unital Boolean algebra. The function

\mu_{h}:hH\rightarrow[0,1],\hskip 9.24994pt\mu_{h}(a)=\frac{\mu(a)}{\mu(h)},\hskip 9.24994pta\in hH,

makes
H_{h}=(hH,\mu_{h})
a unital measure algebra.

We say that two measure algebra
(H_{1},\mu_{1})
and
(H_{2},\mu_{2})
are
scalar equivalent
if there exists a positive number
\alpha>0
and an isomorphism
\varphi:H_{1}\rightarrow H_{2}
of Boolean algebras such that
\mu_{2}(\varphi(a))=\alpha\mu_{1}(a)
for an arbitrary element
a\in H_{1}.
If measure algebras are scalar equivalent and unital, then they are isomorphic.

We call a measure algebra
(H,\mu)

locally standard
if every finite subset of
H
is contained in a measure subalgebra of
(H,\mu)
that is scalar equivalent to
\mathbf{St}_{n}
for some
n\geq 1
.

If the measure algebra
(H,\mu)
is unital and locally standard, then every finite subset of
H
is contained in a measure subalgebra that is isomorphic to
\mathbf{St}_{n}
for some
n\geq 1
.

The measure algebras of Examples
1
,
3
,
4
are locally standard. The Besicovich measure algebra is not locally standard because it contains elements of irrational measure.

In
[
4
]
, we showed that an arbitrary countable unital locally standard measure algebra is isomorphic to a measure algebra
H(s),
where
s
is a Steinitz number (in
[
7
,
4
,
16
,
17
]
, measure algebras are called Hamming systems).

There is a parallelism between locally standard measure algebras and locally matrix algebras (
[
1
,
6
,
4
,
5
,
2
,
10
,
14
]
). Moreover, locally standard measure algebras appear as algebras of idempotents of Cartan subalgebras of locally matrix algebras (see
[
4
]
). From this point of view, the classification of unital countable locally standard measure algebras in
[
4
]
is an analog of the theorem of J.G.Glimm
[
10
]
.

In this paper, we prove an analog of the theorems of J.Dixmier
[
9
]
and A.A.Baranov
[
1
]
:

we parameterize countable locally standard measure algebras by pairs
(s,r),

where
s
is a Steinitz number and
r
is a real number greater or equal to

1.

1
Locally matrix algebras and their spectra

In this Section, we summarize the results about locally matrix algebras and their spectra of Steinitz numbers that we will use later in the paper.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field. An associative
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
A
is called a
locally matrix algebra
if an arbitrary finite collection of elements
a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}\in A
is contained in a subalgebra
A^{\prime}\subset A
that is isomorphic to a matrix algebra
M_{n}(\mathbb{F})
for some
n\geq 1
. If
A\ni 1,
then we say that
A
is a
unital locally matrix algebra
.

For a unital locally matrix algebra
A
, let
D(A)
be the set of all positive integers
n
such that there exists a subalgebra
A^{\prime},

1\in A^{\prime}\subset A,

A^{\prime}\cong M_{n}(\mathbb{F}).
The least common multiple of the set
D(A)
is called the
Steinitz number

\mathbf{st}(A)
of the algebra
A
; see
[
6
]
.

J.G.Glimm
[
10
]
showed that if
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}A\leq\aleph_{0}
and

\mathbf{st}(A)=\prod_{p_{i}\in\mathbb{P}}p_{i},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{then}\hskip 9.24994ptA\cong\bigotimes_{p_{i}\in\mathbb{P}}M_{p_{i}}(\mathbb{F}).

In particular, every countable-dimensional unital locally matrix algebra is uniquely determined by its Steinitz number.

For an element
a
of a unital locally matrix algebra
A
choose a subalgebra
A^{\prime}\subset A
such that
1,a\in A^{\prime},

A^{\prime}\cong M_{n}(\mathbb{F}).
Let
r_{A^{\prime}}(a)
be the range of the matrix
a
in
M_{n}(\mathbb{F}).
As shown by V.M.Kurochkin
[
14
]
, the ratio

r(a)=\frac{1}{n}\ r_{A^{\prime}}(a)

does not depend on the choice of the subalgebra
A^{\prime}.
We call
r(a)
the
relative range
of the element
a.
If
a,b\in A
are orthogonal idempotents, then
r(a+b)=r(a)+r(b).

A subalgebra
H
of the matrix algebra
M_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is called a
Cartan subalgebra
if
H\cong\mathbb{F}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{F}
(
n
summands), in other words,
H
is spanned by
n
pairwise orthogonal idempotents. It is well known that every Cartan subalgebra is conjugate of the diagonal subalgebra of
M_{n}(\mathbb{F})
.

Let
1\in A_{1}\subset A_{2}\subset\cdots
be an ascending chain of matrix subalgebras such that
A=\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}A_{i}
. A
general Cartan subalgebra
of
A
is the union
H=\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}H_{i}
, in which every
H_{i}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
A_{i}
and
{1\in H_{1}\subset H_{2}\subset\cdots}
.

A
Cartan subalgebra
of
A
is a subalgebra
H\subset A
with decompositions

A=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{\infty}A_{i}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994ptH=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{\infty}H_{i},

in which all
A_{i}
are finite-dimensional matrix algebras and
H_{i}
are Cartan subalgebras of
A_{i}
.

Every Cartan subalgebra is a general Cartan subalgebra. In
[
4
]
, it is shown that in an arbitrary countable-dimensional unital locally matrix algebra
A
:

(1)

any two Cartan subalgebras are conjugate via an automorphism of
A
,

(2)

there exists a general Cartan subalgebra of
A
that is not a Cartan subalgebra.

In particular, not all general Cartan subalgebras are conjugate.

Let
C
be a commutative subalgebra of a locally matrix algebra
A
and
1\in C
. Let
E(C)
be the set of all idempotents from
C
(including
0
and
1
). For
e,f\in E(C)
, let
ef
and
e+f-2ef
be their Boolean product and Boolean sum, respectively. Hence, for an arbitrary general Cartan subalgebra
C
of
A
the Boolean algebra
E(C)
with the relative range function
r
is a locally standard measure algebra.

Let
H
be a unital locally standard measure algebra. Let

D(H)=\{n\geq 1\,\big|\,1\in H^{\prime}\subset H,\ H^{\prime}\cong\mathbf{St}_{n}\}.

The least common multiple of the set
D(H)
is called the
Steinitz number of
H
and denoted as
\mathbf{st}(H).

In
[
4
]
, we showed that if
H
is a countable unital locally matrix algebra, then
H\cong\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{st}(H));
see Example
4
above. In particular, every countable unital locally standard measure algebra is uniquely determined by its Steinitz number.

If
A
is a countable-dimensional unital locally matrix algebra with a Cartan subalgebra
C,
then
\mathbf{st}((C,r))=\mathbf{st}(A);
see
[
4
]
.

Now, let
A
be a (not necessarily unital) locally matrix algebra. For an arbitrary idempotent
0\neq e\in A
the subalgebra
eAe
is a unital locally matrix algebra. The subset

\text{Spec}(A)=\{\mathbf{st}(eAe)\,|\,e\in A,\ e\neq 0,\ e^{2}=e\},

where
e
runs through all nonzero idempotents of the algebra
A,
is called the
spectrum
of the algebra
A.
In
[
3
]
, we showed that if
A,B
are countable-dimensional locally matrix algebras, then
A\cong B
if and only if
\text{Spec}(A)=\text{Spec}(B).

Let us give necessary definitions.

For a Steinitz number
s
, let
\Omega(s)
denote the set of all natural numbers
n\in\mathbb{N}
that divide
s;
and for Steinitz numbers
s_{1},

s_{2}
, we say that
s_{1}

finitely divides

s_{2}
if there exists
b\in\Omega(s_{2})
such that
s_{1}=s_{2}/b
(we denote:
s_{1}\big|_{fin}s_{2}
).

Steinitz numbers
s_{1},

s_{2}
are
rationally connected
if
s_{2}=q\cdot s_{1},
where

q
is some rational number.

We call a subset
S\subset\mathbb{SN}

saturated
if

1)

any two Steinitz numbers from
S
are rationally connected;

2)

if
s_{2}\in S
and
s_{1}\big|_{fin}s_{2},
then
s_{1}\in S;

3)

if
s,ns\in S,
where
n\in\mathbb{N},
then
ks\in S
for any
k,

1\leq k\leq n.

Consider some examples of saturated sets.

Example 5
.

For an arbitrary natural number
n
the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
is saturated.

Example 6
.

Let
s
be a Steinitz number. The set

S(\infty,s)=\Big\{\,\frac{a}{b}\cdot s\ \Big|\ a\in\mathbb{N},\ b\in\Omega(s)\,\Big\}

is saturated. For an arbitrary Steinitz number
s^{\prime}\in S(\infty,s)
we have
S(\infty,s)=S(\infty,s^{\prime}).
If
s\in\mathbb{N},
then
S(\infty,s)=\mathbb{N}.

Example 7
.

Let
r
be a real number,
1\leq r<\infty.
Let
s
be an infinite Steinitz number. The set

S(r,s)=\Big\{\,\frac{a}{b}\cdot s\ \Big|\ a,b\in\mathbb{N},\ b\in\Omega(s),\ a\leq rb\,\Big\}

is saturated.

Example 8
.

Let
s
be an infinite Steinitz number and let
r=u/v
be a rational number;
u,v\in\mathbb{N},

v\in\Omega(s).
Then the set

S^{+}(r,s)=\Big\{\,\frac{a}{b}\cdot s\ \Big|\ a,b\in\mathbb{N},\ b\in\Omega(s),\ a<rb\,\Big\}

is saturated.

Which spectra above correspond to unital algebras? A locally matrix algebra
A
is unital if and only if
\text{Spec}(A)=\{1,2,\cdots,n\},
where
n\in\mathbb{N},
or
\text{Spec}(A)=S(r,s),
where
s\in\mathbb{SN}\setminus\mathbb{N},

r=u/v,

u,v\in\mathbb{N},

v\in\Omega(s).

Now following
[
3
]
, we will construct countable-dimensional locally matrix algebras with the above spectra. These algebras include all countable-dimensional locally matrix algebras.

Example 9
.

Let
M_{\infty}(\mathbb{F})
be the algebra of
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
matrices over
\mathbb{F}
having finitely many nonzero entries. Then
\text{Spec}(M_{\infty}(\mathbb{F}))=\mathbb{N}.

Example 10
.

Let
s
be an infinite Steinitz number,

s=\prod_{p_{i}\in\mathbb{P}}p_{i},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and let}\hskip 9.24994ptA(s)=\bigotimes_{p_{i}\in\mathbb{P}}M_{p_{i}}(\mathbb{F})

be the countable-dimensional unital locally matrix algebra with the Steinitz number
s.
The algebra

A(\infty,s)=M_{\infty}(\mathbb{F})\bigotimes{}_{\mathbb{F}}A(s)\cong M_{\infty}(A(s))

is a countable-dimensional nonunital locally matrix algebra,
\text{Spec}(A(\infty,s))=S(\infty,s).

Example 11
.

Let
s
be an infinite Steinitz number and let
1\leq r<\infty
be a real number. Let
S=S(r,s)
or
S=S^{+}(r,s).
Choose a sequence
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots\in\Omega(s)
such that
b_{i}
divides
b_{i+1},

i\geq 1,
and
s
is the least common multiple of
b_{i},

i\geq 1.

Let
m_{i}=[rb_{i}]
if
S=S(r,s).
Let

m_{i}=\begin{cases}\ [rb_{i}],&\text{if}\hskip 9.24994ptrb_{i}\not\in\mathbb{N},\\
rb_{i}-1,&\text{if}\hskip 9.24994ptrb_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\end{cases}

if
S=S^{+}(r,s).

For each
i\geq 1
consider the countable-dimensional unital locally matrix algebra
A(s/b_{i})
and let

A_{i}=M_{m_{i}}\big(A(s/b_{i})\big),\hskip 9.24994pti\geq 1.

In
[
3
]
, it is shown that the algebra
A_{i}
embeds on
A_{i+1}
as a corner. Let

A(S)=\bigcup_{i\geq 1}A_{i}.

Then
\text{Spec}(A(S))=S.

2
Classification of locally standard measure algebras

In
[
4
]
, we showed that given two measure algebras
(H_{1},\mu_{1})
and
(H_{2},\mu_{2})
there exists a unique measure
\mu
on the Boolean algebra
H_{1}\otimes{}_{\mathbf{F}_{2}}H_{2}
such that
\mu(a\otimes b)=\mu_{1}(a)\mu_{2}(b)
for arbitrary elements
a\in H_{1},

b\in H_{2}.

Remark 1
.

In
[
4
]
, we assumed unitality of the Boolean algebra
H_{1},

H_{2}.
However, this unitality has never been used in the definition of tensor product.

For a measure algebra
(H,\mu)
, consider the following set of Steinitz numbers

\text{Spec}(H)=\{\ \mathbf{st}(H_{n})\ |\ 0\neq h\in H\ \}\subseteq\mathbb{SN}.

If the Boolean algebra
H
is unital and
\mathbf{st}(H)=s,
then

\text{Spec}(H)=\Big\{\,\frac{a}{b}\cdot s\ \Big|\ b\in\Omega(s),\ 1\leq a\leq b\,\Big\}.

For nonzero elements
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
we say that
h_{1}\geq h_{2}
if
h_{1}H\supseteq h_{2}H.

Lemma 1
.

Let
(H,\mu)
be a locally standard measure algebra. Then

1)

for arbitrary nonzero elements
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
there exists an element
0\neq h_{3}\in H
such that
h_{1}\leq h_{3},

h_{2}\leq h_{3};

2)

if
h_{2}\leq h_{1},
then
\text{\emph{Spec}}(H_{h_{2}})\subseteq\text{\emph{Spec}}(H_{h_{1}}).

Proof.

Since the measure algebra
(H,\mu)
is locally standard there exists a subalgebra
H^{\prime}\subset H,
such that
h_{1},h_{2}\in H^{\prime}
and
(H^{\prime},\mu)
is scalar equivalent to a standard measure algebra
(\mathbf{St}_{k},\mu_{k}),

k\in\mathbb{N}.
Let
\alpha
be a positive real number and let
\varphi:\mathbf{St}_{k}\rightarrow H^{\prime}
be an isomorphism such that
\mu(\varphi(a))=\alpha\,\mu_{k}(a)
for an arbitrary element
a\in\mathbf{St}_{k}.
Let
e
be the identity of the Boolean algebra
\mathbf{St}_{k},

h_{3}=\varphi(e).
Then
h_{1},h_{2}\leq h_{3}.

Now, suppose that
h_{2}\leq h_{1}.
We will show that

\text{Spec}(H_{h_{2}})\subseteq\text{Spec}(H_{h_{1}}).

Let
s\in\text{Spec}(H_{h_{2}})
which means that there exists an element
h\in h_{2}H
such that

\mathbf{st}\big((H_{h_{2}})_{h}\big)=s.

Clearly,
hh_{2}H=hh_{1}H=hH.
The measure function on
(H_{h_{2}})_{h}
coincides with the measure function on
(H_{h_{1}})_{h}.
Hence,
s\in\text{Spec}(H_{h_{1}}).
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
A
be a countable-dimensional unital locally matrix algebra. Let
C
be a Cartan subalgebra of
A.
Let
r:A\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be the relative range function. Then the spectrum of the measure algebra
(E(C),r)
coincides with the spectrum of
A.

Proof.

In
[
4
]
, it was shown that
\mathbf{st}\big((E(C),r)\big)=\mathbf{st}(A)=s.
Hence,

\text{Spec}\big((E(C),r)\big)=\text{Spec}(A)=\Big\{\,\frac{a}{b}\cdot s\ \Big|\ b\in\Omega(s)\ 1\leq a\leq b\,\Big\}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
A
be a countable-dimensional unital locally matrix algebra, and let
0\neq e\in A
be an idempotent. Let
C
be a Cartan subalgebra of the algebra
eAe.
Then there exists a Cartan subalgebra
\widetilde{C}
of the algebra
A
containing
C.

Proof.

By Koethe‘s Theorem
[
13
]
, we will assume that

A\cong\bigotimes_{i=1}^{\infty}M_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{F}).

There exists an integer
r\geq 1
such that

e\in M_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{F})\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{n_{r}}(\mathbb{F})\cong M_{n_{1}\cdots n_{r}}(\mathbb{F}).

Let
C\,^{\prime}
be a Cartan subalgebra of

e\,M_{n_{1}\cdots n_{r}}(\mathbb{F})\,e.

It is easy to see that
C\,^{\prime}
is embeddable in some Cartan subalgebra
\widetilde{C}\,^{\prime}
of the algebra
M_{n_{1}\cdots n_{r}}(\mathbb{F}).
Let
C\,^{\prime\prime}
be a Cartan subalgebra of the algebra

\bigotimes_{i=r+1}^{\infty}M_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{F}).

Then
C\,^{\prime}\otimes C\,^{\prime\prime}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
eAe,
whereas
\widetilde{C}\,^{\prime}\otimes C\,^{\prime\prime}
is a Cartan subalgebra of the algebra
A.
We have

C\,^{\prime}\otimes C\,^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\widetilde{C}\,^{\prime}\otimes C\,^{\prime\prime}.

Since all Cartan subalgebras in a countable-dimensional unital locally matrix algebra are conjugate (see
[
4
]
), there exists an automorphism
\varphi
of the algebra
eAe
that maps
C\,^{\prime}\otimes C\,^{\prime\prime}
into
C.
By
[
4
, Lemma 12]
, the automorphism
\varphi
extends to an automorphism
\widetilde{\varphi}
of the algebra
A.
Now,
\widetilde{\varphi}\big(\widetilde{C}\,^{\prime}\otimes C\,^{\prime\prime}\big)
is a Cartan subalgebra of the algebra
A
that contains
C.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Let
A
be a nonunital locally matrix algebra that is the union of the strictly ascending chain of subalgebras

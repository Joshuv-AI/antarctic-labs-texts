1.
Introduction

For positive integer
k
and complex number
z
, Srinivasa Ramanujan introduced the sum

\displaystyle c_{k}(z):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}^{k}e^{\frac{2\pi imz}{k}}.

in
[
21
]
. He obtained Fourier series like representations for many well-known arithmetical functions in terms of these sums. These sums were called as the
Ramanujan sums
later. Some orthogonality properties of the Ramanujan sums were derived by Carmichael in
[
7
]
. He showed that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}c_{k_{1}}(j)c_{k_{2}}(j)=0

whenever
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
and
k_{1},k_{2}
both divide
N
, and

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}c_{k}(j)^{2}=\phi(k)N

whenever
k|N
. Here
\phi
denotes the Euler totient function.
Using these orthogonality properties, Carmichael established the existence of Fourier series like representations for certain arithmetical functions in
[
7
]
.

In
[
4
]
, E. Alkan considered a family of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
vectors (where
\mathbb{R}
is the set of all real numbers and
k
a positive integer) with components involving the Ramanujan sums. In this family, he proved that though orthogonality could not be achieved, they can found to be very close to being orthogonal. Precisely, in terms of the usual inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, Alkan proved that for many values of
k
and
r
, the inner product

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{r+1}}\langle 1^{r},2^{r}\ldots,k^{r}\rangle.\langle c_{k}(1),c_{k}(2),\ldots,c_{k}(k)\rangle=\frac{1}{k^{r+1}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}j^{r}c_{k}(j)

is positive and very close to zero. Alkan also proved that

\displaystyle\max\limits_{N}\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}c_{k}(j)\right|\geq\frac{J_{2}(k)}{4k}+\frac{\phi(k)}{2}

where
J_{s}(k)
denotes the Jordan totient function. In addition, he discussed various features of the sum
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}j^{r}c_{k}(j)
including that this sum is always positive irrespective of the values of
k
and
r
. We would like to note that this weighted power sum appeared in many of the problems discussed by Alkan in
[
1
]

[
3
]
, and
[
4
]
. This sum itself became a major point of discussion in many other papers. For example, Alkan himself derived a formula for
S_{r}(k)=\frac{1}{k^{r+1}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}j^{r}c_{k}(j)
in
[
2
]
and L. Tóth derived another formula for
S_{r}(k)
in
[
23
]
.

In
[
8
]
, E. Cohen gave a generalization of the Ramanujan sum (called hereafter as the
generalized Ramanujan sum
or the
Cohen-Ramanujan sum
) defining

\displaystyle c_{k}^{(s)}(j):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
(m,k^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{k^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ijm}{k^{s}}}

where
(a,b)_{s}
is the generalized GCD of
a
and
b
(see the definition in the next section).

A generalization of the sum
S_{r}(k)
involving this generalization of the Ramanujan sums was discussed by K V Namboothiri in
[
19
]
and its asymptotic properties were studied by I. Kiuchi in
[
15
]
.

We would also like to mention another work in which the near orthogonality was discussed. Asymptotic orthogonality of the Möbius function to a particular type of nilsequences was established by Green and Tao in
[
11
]
. This near orthogonality is closely related to
generalized the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture
. Please see
[
11
]
for a detailed discussion on this.

The main goal of this paper is to study some of the problems discussed by Alkan in
[
4
]
on the near orthogonality concepts of vectors involving generalized Ramanujan sums. We here prove that certain vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
formed using generalized Ramanujan sums are also nearly orthogonal. We also prove that the inner product

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\langle 1^{r},2^{r}\ldots,(k^{s})^{r}\rangle.\langle c_{k}^{(s)}(1),c_{k}^{(s)}(2),\ldots,c_{k}^{(s)}(k^{s})\rangle=\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)

is always positive irrespective of the values of
k,r
, and
s
. Further, we establish that

\displaystyle\max\limits_{N}\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N^{s}}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|\geq\frac{J_{2s}(k)}{4k^{s}}+\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2}.

E. Cohen has proved in a series of papers
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
that many of the results involving Ramanujan sums have a natural generalization to problems involving the generalized Ramanujan sums. Analogously, our discussions in this paper show that the results of Alkan in
[
2
]
and
[
4
]
involving Ramanujan sums have such natural generalizations to analogous results involving generalized Ramanujan sums. For more problems related to the concepts discussed above, please see the papers
[
5
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
16
]
, and
[
22
]
.

Notations and basic results

In this section, we will introduce the most commonly used definitions and results required in the following sections. If any of the terms appearing in this paper are not defined in this section, they will be introduced just before their first use, or otherwise, their definitions can be found in

]

For any positive integer
k
with
k>1
, we have

(1)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{d|k}\mu(d)=0\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{tom1976introduction}{}{}, Theorem 2.1]}}.

where
\mu
denotes the usual Möbius function.
Analogous to the Euler totient function
\phi
,
the
Jordan totient function

J_{k}(n)
is defined by

(2)

\displaystyle J_{k}(n):=n^{k}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{k}}\right).

From
[
6
, Chapter 2]
, we have

(3)

\displaystyle J_{s}(n)=\sum\limits_{d|n}\mu(d)\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{s}.

Now we recall the definition of the Bernoulli polynomials and Bernoulli numbers.
For any
x\in\mathbb{C}
, the
Bernoulli polynomials

B_{n}(x)
is defined by the equation

\displaystyle\frac{ze^{xz}}{e^{z}-1}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{n}(x)}{n!}z^{n}\text{ where }|z|<2\pi.

The numbers
B_{n}(0)
are called
Bernoulli numbers
and are denoted by
B_{n}
. Thus

\displaystyle\frac{z}{e^{z}-1}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{n}}{n!}z^{n}\text{ where }|z|<2\pi.

Also by
[
6
, Theorem 12.14]
, we have
B_{n}=B_{n}(0)=B_{n}(1)
. Further we have
B_{n}=0
when
n
is odd and
n\geq 3
. Now from
[
6
, Theorem 12.15]
we get a basic recursion of Bernoulli numbers
as

(4)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n+1}{j}B_{j}=0\text{ for }n\geq 1.

Also from
[
6
, Theorem 12.12]
we have
B_{n}(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}B_{k}x^{n-k}
.
See
[
6
, Chapter 12]
for other properties of Bernoulli numbers and Bernoulli polynomials.

As introduced in the first section, the
Ramanujan sum

c_{k}(j)
is defined as follows:

(5)

\displaystyle c_{k}(j):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}^{k}e^{\frac{2\pi ijm}{k}}.

For
a,b
positive integers,
(a,b)_{s}
will denote the
generalized GCD
of
a
and
b
defined to be the largest
d^{s}\in\mathbb{N}
(where
d\in\mathbb{N}
) such that
d^{s}|a
and
d^{s}|b
. For
s
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
generalized Ramanujan sum
(
[
8
]
) is defined as

(6)

\displaystyle c_{k}^{(s)}(j):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
(m,k^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{k^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ijm}{k^{s}}}.

From

[
10
, Theorem 1]
we have

(7)

\displaystyle c_{k}^{(s)}(j)=\frac{J_{s}(k)\mu(d)}{J_{s}(d)}

where
d^{s}=\frac{k^{s}}{(j,k^{s})_{s}}
.

Let
A
be a set of integers. The
counting function

A(x)
of the set
A
counts the number of positive elements of
A
not exceeding
x
. That is,

\displaystyle A(x):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A\\
1\leq a\leq x\end{subarray}}1.

The
lower asymptotic density
of
A
is defined by

\displaystyle d_{L}(A):=\liminf\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A(x)}{x}.

Similarily the
upper asymptotic density
of
A
is defined by

\displaystyle d_{U}(A):=\limsup\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A(x)}{x}.

The set
A
has
asymptotic density

d(A)=\alpha
if
d_{L}(A)=d_{U}(A)=\alpha
or equivalently,
\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A(x)}{x}=\alpha
. To know more about concepts about density, please see
[
20
]
.

If
\mathbb{P}
is the set of all prime numbers and if
P
is any subset of
\mathbb{P}
, then the
set of integers of Beurling type corresponding to
P
is the semigroup
\left<P\right>
generated by all the primes in
P
, namely

\displaystyle\left<P\right>:=\left\{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{a_{j}}:k\geq 1,a_{j}\geq 0,p_{j}\in P\right\}

with the convention that
\left<P\right>=\{1\}
when
P
is empty.

Define
\omega(k)
to be the function giving the number of distinct prime divisors of
k
.

3.
Main Results and proofs

Our first result is a generalization of
[
2
, Theorem 2]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
B=\left<P\right>
be a set of integers of Beurling type corresponding to a subset
P
of all primes with

\displaystyle N_{P}(x)=cx+O\left(\frac{x}{(logx)^{\lambda}}\right)

where
c>0
and
\lambda>0
are constants and
N_{P}(x)
is the counting function of
B
. Let
k
and
r
be positive integers such that
k\geq 2
and
r\geq 2
. Then, for any
\epsilon
with
0<\epsilon<1
, there exists a subset
B_{\epsilon}\subseteq B
depending only on
\epsilon
and having positive density such that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)-\left(\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2k^{s}}-\frac{1}{r+1}\right)\right|<\epsilon

for any
k\in B_{\epsilon}
.

Proof.

From
[
19
, Proposition 11]
, we have

(8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)=\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2k^{s}}+\frac{1}{r+1}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r+1}{2m}B_{2m}\frac{J_{2ms}(k)}{k^{2ms}}

for any natural number
r
and
k
.
Using equation (
2
), we may rewrite the above as

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2k^{s}}+\frac{1}{r+1}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r+1}{2m}B_{2m}\sum\limits_{d|k}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{2ms}}

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2k^{s}}+\frac{1}{r+1}\sum\limits_{d|k}\mu(d)\sum\limits_{m=1}^{[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r+1}{2m}\frac{B_{2m}}{d^{2ms}}.

Let
r
be an even positive integer.
By
[
2
, Equation 3.3]
, we have

\displaystyle B_{r+1}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{r+1}-\frac{(r+1)}{2}x^{r}+x^{r+1}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\frac{r}{2}}\binom{r+1}{2m}\frac{B_{2m}}{x^{2m}}

so that

\displaystyle B_{r+1}(d^{s})=d^{s(r+1)}-\frac{(r+1)}{2}d^{sr}+d^{s(r+1)}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\frac{r}{2}}\binom{r+1}{2m}\frac{B_{2m}}{d^{2ms}}.

and so

(10)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\frac{r}{2}}\binom{r+1}{2m}\frac{B_{2m}}{d^{2ms}}=\frac{B_{r+1}(d^{s})}{d^{s(r+1)}}-1+\frac{r+1}{2d^{s}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{d|k}\mu(d)\sum\limits_{m=1}^{[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r+1}{2m}\frac{B_{2m}}{d^{2ms}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{d|k}\mu(d)\left(\frac{B_{r+1}(d^{s})}{d^{s(r+1)}}-1+\frac{r+1}{2d^{s}}\right)\quad(\text{from }(\ref{B_2m/d^2ms}))

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{d|k}\mu(d)\frac{B_{r+1}(d^{s})}{d^{s(r+1)}}-\sum\limits_{d|k}\mu(d)+\frac{r+1}{2}\sum\limits_{d|k}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{s}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{d|k}\mu(d)\frac{B_{r+1}(d^{s})}{d^{s(r+1)}}+(r+1)\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2k^{s}}\quad(\text{from (\ref{mu}) and (\ref{J_s})}).

Therefore (
9
) becomes

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2k^{s}}+\frac{1}{r+1}\sum\limits_{d|k}\mu(d)\frac{B_{r+1}(d^{s})}{d^{s(r+1)}}+\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2k^{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{s}(k)}{k^{s}}+\frac{1}{r+1}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\frac{B_{r+1}(d^{s})}{d^{s(r+1)}}\quad\text{ (since }B_{r+1}(1)=0).

For
n>1
, let
S_{r}(n):=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}j^{r}
denote the sum of consecutive
r
th
powers.

We have
(r+1)S_{r}(n)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r+1}{j}B_{j}n^{r+1-j}=B_{r+1}(n)-B_{r+1}

[
14
, Theorem 1, Chapter 15]
. Since
r
is even and
r\geq 2
, we have
B_{r+1}=0
and hence
S_{r}(n)=\frac{B_{r+1}(n)}{r+1}
. Therefore

(12)

\displaystyle S_{r}(d^{s})=\frac{B_{r+1}(d^{s})}{r+1}

for any
d>1
.
Combining (
11
) and (
12
), we get

(13)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)=\frac{J_{s}(k)}{k^{s}}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\frac{S_{r}(d^{s})}{d^{s(r+1)}}

for any even
r
where
r\geq 2
.
Now let
r
be an odd positive integer.
From
[
2
, Equation 3.9]
, we have

\displaystyle B_{r+1}(x)=x^{r+1}-\frac{(r+1)}{2}x^{r}+x^{r+1}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\binom{r+1}{2m}\frac{B_{2m}}{x^{2m}}+B_{r+1}.

Therefore

\displaystyle B_{r+1}(d^{s})=d^{s(r+1)}-\frac{(r+1)}{2}d^{sr}+d^{s(r+1)}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\binom{r+1}{2m}\frac{B_{2m}}{d^{2ms}}+B_{r+1}

so that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\left[\frac{r}{2}\right]}\binom{r+1}{2m}\frac{B_{2m}}{d^{2ms}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\binom{r+1}{2m}\frac{B_{2m}}{d^{2ms}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{s(r+1)}}\left(B_{r+1}(d^{s})-d^{s(r+1)}+\frac{(r+1)}{2}d^{sr}-B_{r+1}\right).

Hence

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{d|k}\mu(d)\sum\limits_{m=1}^{[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r+1}{2m}\frac{B_{2m}}{d^{2ms}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{d|k}\mu(d)\left(\frac{B_{r+1}(d^{s})}{d^{s(r+1)}}-1+\frac{(r+1)}{2d^{s}}-\frac{B_{r+1}}{d^{s(r+1)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{d|k}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{s(r+1)}}\left(B_{r+1}(d^{s})-B_{r+1}\right)+(r+1)\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2k^{s}}.

Therefore from equation (
9
), we have

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2k^{s}}+\frac{1}{r+1}\sum\limits_{d|k}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{s(r+1)}}\left(B_{r+1}(d^{s})-B_{r+1}\right)+\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2k^{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{s}(k)}{k^{s}}+\frac{1}{r+1}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{s(r+1)}}\left(B_{r+1}(d^{s})-B_{r+1}\right)(\text{ since }B_{r+1}(1)=B_{r+1}).

As noted previously,
S_{r}(d^{s})=\frac{1}{r+1}\left(B_{r+1}(d^{s})-B_{r+1}\right)
for any positive integer
d
with
d>1
and so

(15)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)=\frac{J_{s}(k)}{k^{s}}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\frac{S_{r}(d^{s})}{d^{s(r+1)}}

holds for odd
r
as well. Now for
d>1
,

(16)

\displaystyle L_{d,r}=\frac{S_{r}(d^{s})}{d^{s(r+1)}}=\frac{1}{d^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d^{s}-1}j^{r}=\frac{1}{d^{s}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d^{s}-1}\left(\frac{j}{d^{s}}\right)^{r}

is a lower Riemann sum using the equally spaced partitioning points

corresponding to the integral
\int\limits_{0}^{1}x^{r}=\frac{1}{r+1}
. For any

and thus we may define the numbers

\displaystyle\delta_{d^{s},r}:=\frac{1}{r+1}-\frac{S_{r}(d^{s})}{d^{s(r+1)}}>0

as an error in the approximation of this integral by the lower Riemann integral. If we consider the upper Riemann sum for the same integral defined as

(18)

\displaystyle U_{d,r}:=\frac{1}{d^{s}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d^{s}}\left(\frac{j}{d^{s}}\right)^{r},

we can see that

. It follows from (

(19)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{s}(k)}{k^{s}}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\left(\frac{1}{r+1}-\delta_{d^{s},r}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{s}(k)}{k^{s}}-\frac{1}{r+1}-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}(\text{ since }\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)=-1)

follows for any

. Let

. Hence, from (

25
) we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)-\left(\frac{J_{s}(k)}{k^{s}}-\frac{1}{r+1}\right)\right|<\epsilon

for any
k\in B_{\epsilon}
with
k\geq 2
and
r\geq 2
.
∎

Next we prove that for large
n
, the weighted average
\frac{1}{k_{n}^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k_{n}^{s}}j^{r}c_{k_{n}}^{(s)}(j)
is very small for some special sequence
\left\{k_{n}\right\}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that
\left\{k_{n}\right\}
is a sequence of positive integers such that
\omega(k_{n})
is uniformly bounded and all primes dividing
k_{n}
tend to infinity as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Then, for any
r\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k_{n}^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k_{n}^{s}}j^{r}c_{k_{n}}^{(s)}(j)\right)=1-\frac{1}{r+1}.

For every
\lambda>1
, there exists a sequence
\left\{k_{n}\right\}
with
\omega(k_{n})\sim(\lambda-1)\frac{n}{\log(n)}
such that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k_{n}^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k_{n}^{s}}j^{r}c_{k_{n}}^{(s)}(j)\right)=1-\frac{1}{r+1}

again holds for any
r\geq 1
. Finally, for every
\lambda>1
, there exists a sequence
\left\{k_{n}\right\}
with
\omega(k_{n})\sim(\lambda-1)\frac{n}{\log(n)}
such that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k_{n}^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k_{n}^{s}}j^{r}c_{k_{n}}^{(s)}(j)\right)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{S_{r}(2^{s})}{2^{s(r+1)}}

holds for any
r\geq 1
.

Proof.

Asume that
\left\{k_{n}\right\}
is a sequence of positive integers such that
\omega(k_{n})
is bounded and all primes dividing
k_{n}
tend to infinity as
n\rightarrow\infty
. From (
8
), we have

(26)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k_{n}^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k_{n}^{s}}j^{r}c_{k_{n}}^{(s)}(j)=\frac{J_{s}(k_{n})}{2k_{n}^{s}}+\frac{1}{r+1}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r+1}{2m}B_{2m}\prod\limits_{p|k_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2ms}}\right)

for
r\geq 1
and
k_{n}\geq 2
, where the sum over
m
is taken to be zero when
r=1
. By our assumptions, we see that

(27)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{J_{s}(k_{n})}{2k_{n}^{s}}=\frac{1}{2}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\prod\limits_{p|k_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)\right)=\frac{1}{2}

and

(28)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\prod\limits_{p|k_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2ms}}\right)\right)=1

for any
m\geq 1
. By the recursion (
4
) satisfied by Bernoulli numbers we have

\sum\limits_{j=2}^{r}\binom{r+1}{j}B_{j}=\frac{r+1}{2}-1
.
Since
B_{j}=0
when
j
is odd and
j\geq 3
, we may rewrite this as

(29)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r+1}{2m}B_{2m}=\frac{r+1}{2}-1.

From (
26
)-(
29
) we get

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k_{n}^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k_{n}^{s}}j^{r}c_{k_{n}}^{(s)}(j)\right)=1-\frac{1}{r+1}.

Write

(30)

\displaystyle k_{n}=\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n<p\leq\lambda n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}p

where
n\geq 1
and
\lambda>1
, where empty products are equal to one. Let
\pi(x)
be the counting function on the set of all primes. Then by the prime number theorem,

\displaystyle\omega(k_{n})=\pi(\lambda n)-\pi(n)\sim\frac{\lambda n}{\log\lambda n}-\frac{n}{\log n}

which is asymptotically equal to
(\lambda-1)\frac{n}{\log n}
.
Now by
[
2
, Equation 5.5]
we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\prod\limits_{n<p\leq\lambda n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)=1

from which it follows that

(31)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{J_{s}(k_{n})}{k_{n}^{s}}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\prod\limits_{n<p\leq\lambda n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)=1

and

(32)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\prod\limits_{n<p\leq\lambda n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2ms}}\right)=1

for any
m\geq 1
. Hence, from (
26
), (
31
), (
32
) and the recursion of Bernoulli numbers, we get

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k_{n}^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k_{n}^{s}}j^{r}c_{k_{n}}^{(s)}(j)\right)=1-\frac{1}{r+1}.

Next we define

(33)

\displaystyle k_{n}=2\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n<p\leq\lambda n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}p

for
n\geq 1
and
\lambda>1
. From (
19
), we have

(34)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k_{n}^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k_{n}^{s}}j^{r}c_{k_{n}}^{(s)}(j)=\frac{J_{s}(k_{n})}{k_{n}^{s}}-\frac{1}{r+1}-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k_{n}\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}

for
r\geq 2
and
k_{n}\geq 2
.
Now we recall the Merten’s theorem (
[
12
, Theorem 429]
) which states that
\prod\limits_{p\leq x}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\sim\frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log x}
, where
\gamma
is
Euler’s constant
(also known as
Euler-Mascheroni constant
).

From (
33
) and Mertens’ theorem, we have

(35)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{J_{s}(k_{n})}{k_{n}^{s}}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right)\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n<p\leq\lambda n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)\right]=\frac{2^{s}-1}{2^{s}}.

Note that

(36)

\displaystyle\delta_{2^{s},r}=\frac{1}{r+1}-\frac{S_{r}(2^{s})}{2^{s(r+1)}}

and

(37)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k_{n}\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}=-\delta_{2^{s},r}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k_{n}\\
d>2\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}.

Now

(38)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k_{n}\\
d>2\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k_{n}\\
d>2\\
d\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k_{n}\\
d>2\\
d\text{ even}\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}.

From
[
2
, Equation 5.13]
, we get

(39)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|k_{n}\\
p\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{s}}\leq\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|k_{n}\\
p\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\right)^{s}=\left(O(1)\right)^{s}=O(1)

as
n
tends to infinity. Using a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, it follows from (
39
) that

(40)

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k_{n}\\
d>2\\
d\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}\right|=O(1)

as
n
tends to infinity. Also, we have

(41)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k_{n}\\
d>2\\
d\text{ even}\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m|\frac{k_{n}}{2}\\
m>1\end{subarray}}\mu(2m)\delta_{(2m)^{s},r}=-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m|\frac{k_{n}}{2}\\
m>1\end{subarray}}\mu(m)\delta_{(2m)^{s},r}.

Since
0<\delta_{(2m)^{s},r}<\frac{1}{(2m)^{s}}
, from (
41
) and similar arguments as above, we get

(42)

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k_{n}\\
d>2\\
d\text{ even}\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\delta_{d,r}\right|=O(1)

as
n
tends to infinity. Hence, from (
38
), (
40
), (
42
), we get

(43)

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k_{n}\\
d>2\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}\right|=O(1)

as
n
tends to infinity. Now combining (
34
)-(
37
) and (
43
), it follows that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k_{n}^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k_{n}^{s}}j^{r}c_{k_{n}}^{(s)}(j)\right)=\frac{2^{s}-1}{2^{s}}-\frac{S_{r}(2^{s})}{2^{s(r+1)}}.

for
r\geq 2
. When
r=1
, from Equation(
26
) we get the left-hand side of the idenity as
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k_{n}^{2s}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k_{n}^{s}}jc_{k_{n}}^{(s)}(j)\right)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{J_{s}(k_{n})}{2k_{n}^{s}}=\frac{2^{s}-1}{2^{s+1}}
and the right-hand side of the identity as
\frac{2^{s}-1}{2^{s}}-\frac{S_{1}(2^{s})}{2^{2s}}=\frac{2^{s}-1}{2^{s}}-\frac{1}{2^{2s}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2^{s}-1}j\right)=\frac{2^{s}-1}{2^{s}}-\frac{2^{s}(2^{s}-1)}{2^{2s+1}}=\frac{2^{s}-1}{2^{s+1}}
. This completes the proof in all the cases.
∎

Now we prove that the weighted average remains positive.

Theorem 3.3
.

The average value over k of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)

is positive for all
r\geq 1
.

Proof.

Proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
,
we use (
19
) to get

(44)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\leq x}\left(\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right)

\displaystyle=1+\sum\limits_{2\leq k\leq x}\left(\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k\leq x}\frac{J_{s}(k)}{k^{s}}-\frac{1}{r+1}\sum\limits_{2\leq k\leq x}1-\sum\limits_{k\leq x}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}.

From
[
17
, Theorem 6.10]
, we have
\sum\limits_{k\leq x}\frac{J_{s}(k)}{k^{s}}=\frac{x}{\zeta(s+1)}+O(1)
. Therefore

(45)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\leq x}\left(\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right)=\left(\frac{1}{\zeta(s+1)}-\frac{1}{r+1}\right)x-\sum\limits_{k\leq x}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}+O(1).

Now consider

(46)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\leq x}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{1<d\leq x}\sum\limits_{q\leq\frac{x}{d}}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{1<d\leq x}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}\left[\frac{x}{d}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{1<d\leq x}\mu(d)\delta_{d^{s},r}\left(\frac{x}{d}+O(1)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{1<d\leq x}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}x\delta_{d^{s},r}+O\left(\sum\limits_{1<d\leq x}\frac{1}{d^{s}}\right)\text{ since }0<\delta_{d^{s},r}<\frac{1}{d^{s}}

\displaystyle=x\sum\limits_{d=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\delta_{d^{s},r}-x\sum\limits_{d>x}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\delta_{d^{s},r}+O\left(\frac{x^{1-s}}{1-s}+\zeta(s)+O(x^{-s})\right)\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{tom1976introduction}{}{}, Theorem 3.2]}}

\displaystyle=x\sum\limits_{d=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\delta_{d^{s},r}-xO(x^{-s})+O\left(\frac{x^{1-s}}{1-s}+\zeta(s)+O(x^{-s})\right)

\displaystyle=x\sum\limits_{d=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\delta_{d^{s},r}+O(1).

From (
45
) and (
46
), the desired average value over
k
is

(47)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta(s+1)}-\frac{1}{r+1}-\sum\limits_{d=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\delta_{d^{s},r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta(s+1)}-\frac{1}{r+1}+\frac{\delta_{2,r}}{2}+\frac{\delta_{3,r}}{3}+\frac{\delta_{5,r}}{5}+\ldots+\frac{\delta_{n,r}}{n}-\sum\limits_{d=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\delta_{d^{s},r}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{\zeta(s+1)}-\frac{1}{r+1}-\sum\limits_{d=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\delta_{d^{s},r}.

Now

(48)

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{d=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\delta_{d^{s},r}\right|\leq\sum\limits_{d=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{d^{s+1}}\leq\int\limits_{n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{s+1}}\,dt=\frac{1}{n^{s}}.

Combining (
47
) and (
48
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta(s+1)}-\frac{1}{r+1}-\sum\limits_{d=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\delta_{d^{s},r}\geq\frac{1}{\zeta(s+1)}-\frac{1}{r+1}-\frac{1}{n^{s}}>0,

for some positive integer
n
.
Thus the average value over
k
is positive for all
r\geq 1
. Finally when
k\geq 2
is fixed, using (
19
) and the fact that for any fixed
d>1
,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\delta_{d,r}=\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{r+1}-\frac{S_{r}(d^{s})}{d^{s(r+1)}}\right)=0,

we can see that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k^{s(r+1)}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right)=\frac{J_{s}(k)}{k^{s}}>0

which is also true when
k=1
.
∎

Now we give a lower bound for the maximum value of sum of generalized Ramanujan sums.

Theorem 3.4
.

For any
k\geq 1
, the estimate

\displaystyle\max\limits_{N}\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N^{s}}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|\geq\frac{J_{2s}(k)}{4k^{s}}+\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2}

holds.

Proof.

We have
c_{1}^{(s)}(j)=1
.
Hence at
k=1
, the required maximum is infinite and the inequality trivially holds.
Now assume that
k\geq 2
. We have

(49)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)=0\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{cohen1955extension}{}{}, corollary 2.1]}}.

From Equation (
8
) with
r=3
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{4s}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{3}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)=\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2k^{s}}+\frac{J_{2s}(k)}{4k^{2s}}\text{ since }B_{2}=\frac{1}{6}.

Therefore

(50)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{3}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)=\left(\frac{J_{2s}(k)}{4k^{s}}+\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2}\right)k^{3s}.

Using Abel’s identity
[
6
, Theorem 4.2]
and (
49
), we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{3}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)

\displaystyle=-c_{k}^{(s)}(1)-\int\limits_{1}^{k^{s}}3t^{2}\left(\sum\limits_{1\leq j\leq t}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=-\mu(k)-\int\limits_{1}^{k^{s}}3t^{2}\left(\sum\limits_{1\leq j\leq t}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right)\,dt.

Thus

(51)

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{3}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{1}^{k^{s}}\max\limits_{N}\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N^{s}}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|3t^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\max\limits_{N}\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N^{s}}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|\int\limits_{1}^{k^{s}}3t^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\max\limits_{N}\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N^{s}}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|(k^{3s}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\max\limits_{N}\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N^{s}}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|\right)k^{3s}.

From (
50
) and (
51
), we get

\displaystyle\left(\max\limits_{N}\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N^{s}}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|\right)k^{3s}\geq\left(\frac{J_{2s}(k)}{4k^{s}}+\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2}\right)k^{3s}.

and so

\displaystyle\max\limits_{N}\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N^{s}}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|\geq\frac{J_{2s}(k)}{4k^{s}}+\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2}.

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

For any integer
r\geq 1
,
k\geq 1
and
s
, the inequality

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{2}+\left(\frac{1}{r+1}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r+1}{2m}B_{2m}\frac{J_{2ms}(k)}{k^{2ms}}\right)\frac{k^{s}}{J_{s}(k)}\right|\leq k^{s(s-1)}2^{\omega(k)}

holds.

Proof.

Using (
29
), we get

\frac{1}{r+1}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r+1}{2m}B_{2m}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r+1}
.
Therefore
\left|\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{r+1}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r+1}{2m}B_{2m}\right|=\left|1-\frac{1}{r+1}\right|\leq 1
since
r\geq 1
. Thus the inequality holds when
k=1
. Now assume that
k\geq 2
.
From (
8
), we have

(52)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{J_{s}(k)}{2k^{s}}+\frac{1}{r+1}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r+1}{2m}B_{2m}\frac{J_{2ms}(k)}{k^{2ms}}\right)k^{s(r+1)}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{2}+\left(\frac{1}{r+1}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r+1}{2m}B_{2m}\frac{J_{2ms}(k)}{k^{2ms}}\right)\frac{k^{s}}{J_{s}(k)}\right]J_{s}(k)k^{sr}.

Using Abel’s identity, as in the proof of Theorem
3.4
we get

(53)

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}j^{r}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|\leq\max\limits_{N}\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N^{s}}c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|k^{sr}\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}\left|c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|k^{sr}.

Now we consider
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}\left|c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|
. Let
k=\prod\limits_{v=1}^{q}p_{v}^{a_{v}}
be the prime factorization of
k
into distinct primes. Recall that we have
c_{k}^{(s)}(j)=\frac{J_{s}(k)\mu(d)}{J_{s}(d)}
, where
d^{s}=\frac{k^{s}}{(j,k^{s})_{s}}
. If we write
j=\delta^{s}\gamma
, where
(\gamma,k^{s})_{s}=1
, then
d^{s}=\frac{k^{s}}{\delta^{s}}

so that
d=\frac{k}{\delta}
. Therefore,
c_{k}^{(s)}(j)=0
unless
\frac{k}{\delta}
is a square free number. Then
\delta=\prod\limits_{v=1}^{q}p_{v}^{b_{v}}
with
a_{v}-1\leq b_{v}\leq a_{v}
for each
v
and there are exactly
2^{\omega(k)}
values of
d
such that
c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\neq 0
.
Thus
|c_{k}^{(s)}(j)|\leq\frac{J_{s}(k)}{J_{s}(\frac{k}{\delta})}
and so

(54)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}\left|c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq k^{s}\\
c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left|c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq k^{s}\\
c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{J_{s}(k)}{J_{s}(d)}.

The condition
1\leq j\leq k^{s}
and
c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\neq 0
is equivalent to
1\leq j\leq\frac{k^{s}}{\delta^{s}}
and
(j,\frac{k^{s}}{\delta^{s}})_{s}=1
. The number of such integers is
J_{s}\left(\frac{k^{s}}{\delta^{s}}\right)
. From (
54
) we get

(55)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k^{s}}\left|c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq k^{s}\\
c_{k}^{(s)}(j)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{J_{s}(k)}{J_{s}(d)}

\displaystyle=J_{s}(k)\frac{J_{s}\left(\frac{k^{s}}{\delta_{1}^{s}}\right)}{J_{s}\left(\frac{k}{\delta_{1}}\right)}+J_{s}(k)\frac{J_{s}\left(\frac{k^{s}}{\delta_{2}^{s}}\right)}{J_{s}\left(\frac{k}{\delta_{2}}\right)}+\cdots+J_{s}(k)\frac{J_{s}\left(\frac{k^{s}}{\delta_{2^{\omega(k)}}^{s}}\right)}{J_{s}\left(\frac{k}{\delta_{2^{\omega(k)}}}\right)}

\displaystyle=J_{s}(k)\left[\frac{\frac{k^{s^{2}}}{\delta_{1}^{s^{2}}}\prod\limits_{p|\frac{k^{s}}{\delta_{1}^{s}}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)}{\frac{k^{s}}{\delta_{1}^{s}}\prod\limits_{p|\frac{k}{\delta_{1}}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)}+\cdots+\frac{\frac{k^{s^{2}}}{\delta_{2^{\omega(k)}}^{s^{2}}}\prod\limits_{p|\frac{k^{s}}{\delta_{2^{\omega(k)}}^{s}}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)}{\frac{k^{s}}{\delta_{2^{\omega(k)}}^{s}}\prod\limits_{p|\frac{k}{\delta_{2^{\omega(k)}}}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)}\right]

\displaystyle=J_{s}(k)k^{s^{2}-s}\left[\frac{1}{\delta_{1}^{s^{2}-s}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{\delta_{2^{\omega(k)}}^{s^{2}-s}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq J_{s}(k)k^{s^{2}-s}(1+\cdots+1)

\displaystyle\leq J_{s}(k)k^{s^{2}-s}\,2^{\omega(k)}.

From (
52
), (
53
) and (

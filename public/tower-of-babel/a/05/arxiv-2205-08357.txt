Minimum Tournaments with the Strong S k S_{k} -Property and Implications for Teaching
Hans Ulrich Simon

Abstract
A tournament is said to have the S k S_{k} -property if, for any set of k k players, there is another player who beats them all. Minimum tournaments having this property have been explored very well in the 1960’s and the early 1970’s. In this paper, we define a strengthening of the S k S_{k} -property that we name “strong S k S_{k} -property”. We show, first, that several basic results on the weaker notion remain valid for the stronger notion (and the corresponding modification of the proofs requires only little extra-effort). Second, it is demonstrated that the stronger notion has applications in the area of Teaching. Specifically, we present an infinite family of concept classes all of which can be taught with a single example in the No-Clash model of teaching while, in order to teach a class 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} of this family in the recursive model of teaching, order of log ⁡ | 𝒞 | \log|{\mathcal{C}}| many examples are required. This is the first paper that presents a concrete and easily constructible family of concept classes which separates the No-Clash from the recursive model of teaching by more than a constant factor. The separation by a logarithmic factor is remarkable because the recursive teaching dimension is known to be bounded by log ⁡ | 𝒞 | \log|{\mathcal{C}}| for any concept class 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} .

be a prime that is congruent to
3
modulo
4
.
Let
G_{p}
be the quadratic-residue tournament of order
p
.
Then

\mathrm{TD}_{min}(G_{p})>\frac{1}{2}\log(p)-\log\log(p)-1\kern 5.0pt.

Proof.

We make again use of the
implication “
\mbox{strong }S_{k+1}\Rightarrow\mbox{strong }S_{k,2}
”.
According to Theorem
4.3
, the following holds:
if

(k+1)^{2}2^{2k}<p\kern 5.0pt,

(7)

then
G_{p}
has the strong
S_{k+1}
- and therefore also the strong
S_{k,2}
-property.
In this case, we may conclude that
\mathrm{TD}_{min}(G_{p})>k
.
A straightforward calculation shows that (
7
) holds
whenever
k\leq\frac{1}{2}\log(p)-\log\log(p)-1
.
From this discussion, the assertion of the theorem is immediate.
∎

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the static solution
(u,A_{0},A_{1},A_{2})
of the Chern-Simons-Schrödinger (CSS) system, which satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u+V(x)u+(A_{0}+A_{1}^{2}+A_{2}^{2})u=|u|^{p-2}u,\,\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
&\partial_{1}A_{0}=A_{2}u^{2},\ \partial_{2}A_{0}=-A_{1}u^{2},\\
&\partial_{1}A_{2}-\partial_{2}A_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2},\ \partial_{1}A_{1}+\partial_{2}A_{2}=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\varepsilon>0
is a parameter and
V:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a bounded continuous function.

Problem (
1.1
) arises in the study of the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation with the Chern-Simons gauge fields

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-ihD_{0}\phi-h^{2}(D_{1}D_{1}+D_{2}D_{2})\phi+V\phi=|\phi|^{p-2}\phi,\\
&\partial_{0}A_{1}-\partial_{1}A_{0}=-h\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}D_{2}\phi),\\
&\partial_{0}A_{2}-\partial_{2}A_{0}=h\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\phi}D_{1}\phi),\\
&\partial_{1}A_{2}-\partial_{2}A_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}|\phi|^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

where
i
denotes the imaginary unit,
V(x)
is the external potential,
h
is the Plank constant,
\partial_{0}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\ \partial_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ \partial_{2}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}
for
(t,x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
,
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{1+2}\to\mathbb{C}
is a complex scalar field,
A_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}^{1+2}\to\mathbb{R}
is a gauge field and
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+\frac{i}{h}A_{\mu}
is a covariant derivative for
\mu=0,1,2
. It was proposed by Jackiw and Pi
[
20
,
22
,
21
]
to describe the dynamics of a nonrelativistic solitary wave with three dimensional Chern-Simons gauge fields, which is important for explaining electromagnetic phenomena of anyon physics. For the case
p=4
, finite time blow-up solutions were considered in
[
2
,
17
]
, local and global well-posedness were studied in
[
24
,
23
]
and scattering for small solutions was proved in
[
26
]
.

The system (
1.2
), so-called Chern-Simons-Schrödinger (CSS) system, is invariant under the gauge transformation

\phi\to e^{i\chi}\phi,\quad A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}-h\partial_{\mu}\chi,\quad\mu=0,1,2,

for any arbitrary
C^{\infty}
function
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{1+2}\to\mathbb{R}
. If a solution
(\phi,A_{0},A_{1},A_{2})
to (
1.2
) is of the form

\phi(t,x)=u(x)e^{i\omega t},\quad A_{\mu}(t,x)=A_{\mu}(x),\quad\mu=0,1,2,

for some function
u:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
, we call it a standing wave solution of frequency
\omega
. A standing wave with
\omega=0
is referred to be static or time independent.

The results of standing wave solutions to (
1.2
) by variational methods have been investigated extensively in the literature.
Byeon, Huh and Seok in
[
3
]
found standing wave solutions to (
1.2
) of a particular form

\displaystyle\phi(t,x)=u(|x|)e^{i\omega t},\quad A_{0}(t,x)=h_{1}(|x|),

\displaystyle A_{1}(t,x)=\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}h_{2}(|x|),\quad A_{2}(t,x)=-\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}h_{2}(|x|),

where
V(x)
is a constant,
\omega>0
and
u,h_{1},h_{2}
are real valued functions on
[0,\infty)
such that
h_{2}(0)=0
. Since the Palais-Smale condition might not hold for all
p>2
, they devised different minimization arguments for the case
p>4
,
p=4
and
p\in(2,4)
. To be specific, static solutions were found only in the case
p=4
. In the case of
p>4
, they considered a minimization argument on the Nehari-Pohozaev manifold, while in the case of
p\in(2,4)
, they deduced solutions as minimizers on a
L^{2}
-sphere. They also proved the existence of a standing wave with a vortex point of order
N
in
[
4
]
. Based on this type of standing wave solutions, many efforts have been done to study existence
[
18
,
32
,
34
]
, concentration
[
33
]
and multiplicity
[
8
,
35
]
.
The nonexistence of standing wave solutions was discussed in
[
19
]
by applying the Derrick-Pohozaev type identities. By studying the global behavior of energy functional for
p\in(2,4)
, Pomponio and Ruiz in
[
29
]
proved that whether the functional is bounded from below or not depends on frequency
\omega
, which leads to the existence and non-existence of positive solutions. Furthermore, there have been some new achievements to (
1.2
) recently. For instance, Deng and Li
[
11
]
obtained the ground state solution for (CSS) system. Shen, Squassina and Yang
[
31
]
considered the existence results for a class of gauged Schrödinger equations with critical exponential growth and vanishing potentials.

It seems that there are very few results of standing wave solutions to (
1.2
) by singular perturbation arguments. To the best of our knowledge, the first result in this respect seems to be given by Pomponio and Ruiz
[
30
]
. They considered (
1.2
) in a ball under homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, and proved that there existed solutions for large values of the radius and those solutions were located close to the boundary. Moreover, Azzollini and Pomponio recently in
[
1
]
established positive energy static solutions for the (CSS) systems by considering perturbed functional. Later, under the assumption that
V(x)
is non-radial, Deng, Long and Yang in
[
12
]
constructed a clustering solution of the singularly perturbed problem (
1.1
) by Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction method, which is valid for all
p>2
.

When the Chern-Simons terms vanish, (
1.1
) reduces to the perturbed Schrödinger equation

-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u+V(x)u=|u|^{p-2}u.

(1.3)

Existence and local uniqueness of (

,

,

However, there seems to be no results on the existence and the local uniqueness of the multi-peak solutions concentrating at distinct points
to problem (

Suppose that the external potential
V(x)
satisfies:

(V_{1})

V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
0<\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V(x)\leq\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V(x)<\infty;

(V_{2})
There exist
k\ (k\geq 2)

\{a^{1},\cdots,a^{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that for every
1\leq i\leq k
,
V\in C^{\theta}(\overline{B}_{R_{0}}(a^{i}))
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
, and

V(a^{i})<V(x)\ \text{for}\ 0<|x-a^{i}|<r

holds for some
r
with
0<r<R_{0}=1/2\min_{1\leq i,j\leq k,i\neq j}|a^{i}-a^{j}|.

(V_{3})
There exist
m>1
and
\eta>0
such that for each
j=1,2,
and
i=1,\cdots,k
,
V\in C^{1}(B_{\eta}(a^{i}))
and

\left\{\begin{aligned} V(x)&=V(a^{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{2}b_{j,i}|x_{j}-a_{j}^{i}|^{m}+O(|x-a^{i}|^{m+1}),&\quad x\in B_{\eta}(a^{i}),\\
\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{j}}&=mb_{j,i}|x_{j}-a^{i}_{j}|^{m-2}(x_{j}-a^{i}_{j})+O(|x-a^{i}|^{m}),&\quad x\in B_{\eta}(a^{i}),\end{aligned}\right.

where
a^{i}=(a^{i}_{1},a^{i}_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
b_{j,i}\in\mathbb{R}
with
b_{j,i}\neq 0
.

To be precise, we give the definition of multi-peak solutions of equation (
1.1
) as usual.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
k\in\{1,2,\cdots\}
. We say that
u_{\varepsilon}
is a k-peak solution of (
1.1
) if
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

(i)

u_{\varepsilon}
has k local maximum points
y_{\varepsilon}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\ i=1,\cdots,k
, satisfying
y_{\varepsilon}^{i}\rightarrow a^{i}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and
a^{i}\neq a^{j}
for
i\neq j
;

(ii)
For any given small
\tau>0
, there exists
R\gg 1
such that

|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq\tau\ \text{for}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{k}B_{R\varepsilon}(y_{\varepsilon}^{i});

(iii)
There exists
C>0
such that

||u_{\varepsilon}||_{\varepsilon}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+V(x)|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

We denote by
U^{i}
the unique positive radial solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+V(a^{i})u=u^{p-1},\ u>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
&u(0)=\max_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u(x),\ u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{2}}).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

It is well-known that
U^{i}(x)=U^{i}(|x|)
is non-degenerate and satisfies

(U^{i}(r))^{\prime}<0,\ \lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{r}e^{r}U^{i}(r)=C>0,\ \lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(U^{i}(r))^{\prime}}{U^{i}(r)}=-1.

Denote

\langle u,v\rangle_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\varepsilon^{2}\nabla u\nabla v+V(x)uv)\,dx\ \ \text{and}\ \ ||u||_{\varepsilon}^{2}=\langle u,u\rangle_{\varepsilon},

and let

H_{\varepsilon}=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):||u||_{\varepsilon}<\infty\}.

Recall that we assume
a^{i}\neq a^{j}
for
i\neq j
. Let
0<\delta<\min\{|a^{i}-a^{j}|/4:i\neq j\}
and denote

D_{\delta}=\bar{B}_{\delta}(a^{1})\times\cdots\times\bar{B}_{\delta}(a^{k}).

If
(y^{1},\cdots,y^{k})\in D_{\delta}
, then
|y^{i}-y^{j}|\geq|a^{i}-a^{j}|/2\geq 2\delta
for
i\neq j
.

Our first result is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that

and

satisfies
(V_{1})
and
(V_{2})
. Then for

sufficiently small, problem (

) has a k-peak solution

defined as in the Definition

concentrating around

for

Precisely,

is of the form

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}U^{i}(\frac{x-y_{\varepsilon}^{i}}{\varepsilon})+\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)

(1.5)

with

and
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in H_{\varepsilon}
satisfying, for
i=1,\cdots,k
, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

y_{\varepsilon}^{i}\rightarrow a^{i}\quad\text{and}\quad||\varphi_{\varepsilon}||_{\varepsilon}=o(\varepsilon).

(1.6)

Local uniqueness is an important topic in the study of elliptic partial differential equations.
For the local uniqueness of the k-peak solutions obtained in Theorem

Suppose that

2.
The existence of k-peak solutions

2.1.
The finite dimensional reduction

In this subsection, we mainly do the finite dimensional reduction process.

The main result in this subsection is as follows.

Proposition 2.1

There exist

The estimate of the energy functional

In this section, we mainly estimate
I_{\varepsilon}(W_{\varepsilon,Y}).

Proposition A.1
.

Suppose that
p>2
and
V(x)
satisfies
(V_{1})
and
(V_{2})
. Then for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small and
Y\in D_{\delta}
, there holds that

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}(W_{\varepsilon,Y})=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\Big)\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(U^{i}(x))^{p}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(V(y^{i})-V(a^{i}))(U^{i}(x))^{2}\,dx+O(\varepsilon^{2+\theta}).

(A.1)

Proof.

Since the integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{|x-y|}(U^{i}(y))^{2}\,dy
is uniformly bounded for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{|x-y|}(U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}(y))^{2}\,dy=\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{|z-\frac{x-y^{i}}{\varepsilon}|}(U^{i}(z))^{2}\,dz\leq C\varepsilon.

(A.2)

This implies

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{x_{2}-y_{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}W_{\varepsilon,Y}^{2}(y)\,dy\Big)^{2}W_{\varepsilon,Y}^{2}(x)\,dx\Big|

(A.3)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{|x-y|}(U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}(y))^{2}\,dy\Big)^{2}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}(x)\Big)^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}(x)\Big)^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{4}.

Analogously, we can estimate

\Big|\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{x_{1}-y_{1}}{|x-y|^{2}}W_{\varepsilon,Y}^{2}(y)\,dy\Big)^{2}W_{\varepsilon,Y}^{2}(x)\,dx\Big|\leq C\varepsilon^{4}.

(A.4)

Noting that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\varepsilon^{2}\nabla U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}\nabla U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j}+V(a^{i})U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j})\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i})^{p-1}U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j}\,dx

for each
i,j=1,\cdots,k
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla W_{\varepsilon,Y}|^{2}+V(x)|W_{\varepsilon,Y}|^{2})\,dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|W_{\varepsilon,Y}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\varepsilon^{2}\nabla U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}\nabla U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j}+V(x)U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j})\,dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}\Big)^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}((V(x)-V(a^{i}))U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j}\,dx

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i})^{p}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i})^{p-1}U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j}\,dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}\Big)^{p}\,dx\Big)

\displaystyle=:{}

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

To estimate
I_{1}
, note that

\displaystyle I_{1}={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}((V(x)-V(y^{i}))(U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i})^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}((V(y^{i})-V(a^{i}))(U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}((V(x)-V(y^{i}))U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}((V(y^{i})-V(a^{i}))U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j}\,dx.

Since
U^{i}
decays exponentially at infinity and
V(x)
satisfies
(V_{1})
and
(V_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}((V(x)-V(y^{i}))(U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}(y^{i})}((V(x)-V(y^{i}))(U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i})^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{\delta}(y^{i})}((V(x)-V(y^{i}))(U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{B_{\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}}(0)}|\epsilon x|^{\theta}(U^{i}(x))^{2}\,dx+C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}}(0)}(U^{i}(x))^{2}\,dx\leq C\varepsilon^{2+\theta},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}((V(x)-V(y^{i}))U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j}\,dx

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}(y^{i})}((V(x)-V(y^{i}))U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{\delta}(y^{i})}((V(x)-V(y^{i}))U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{B_{\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}}(0)}|\epsilon x|^{\theta}U^{i}(x)U^{j}(x+\frac{y^{i}-y^{j}}{\varepsilon})\,dx+C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}}(0)}U^{i}(x)U^{j}(x+\frac{y^{i}-y^{j}}{\varepsilon})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2+\theta}

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}((V(y^{i})-V(a^{i}))U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j}\,dx\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}U^{i}(x)U^{j}(x+\frac{y^{i}-y^{j}}{\varepsilon})\,dx\leq C\varepsilon^{2+\theta}.

Therefore we can conclude

I_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(V(y^{i})-V(a^{i}))(U^{i}(x))^{2}\,dx+O(\varepsilon^{2+\theta}).

(A.5)

To estimate
I_{2}
, note that for
a,b>0
and
p>2
,

(a+b)^{p}-a^{p}-b^{p}=O(a^{p-1}b+ab^{p-1})

and

(a+b)^{p}-a^{p}-b^{p}-pa^{p-1}b-pab^{p-1}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &O(a^{\frac{p}{2}}b^{\frac{p}{2}}),&\text{if}&\ p\leq 3,\\
&O(a^{p-2}b^{2}+a^{2}b^{p-2}),&\text{if}&\ p>3.\end{aligned}\right.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i}\Big)^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i})^{p}\,dx+p\sum_{i\neq j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i})^{p-1}U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j}\,dx+O(e^{-\frac{\tau}{\varepsilon}})

for some
\tau>0
, which implies that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\Big)\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i})^{p}\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(U_{\varepsilon,y^{i}}^{i})^{p-1}U_{\varepsilon,y^{j}}^{j}\,dx+O(e^{-\frac{\tau}{\varepsilon}})

(A.6)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\Big)\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(U^{i}(x))^{p}\,dx+O(e^{-\frac{\tau}{\varepsilon}}).

Hence (
A.1
) follows from (
A.3
)-(
A.6
).
∎

B
Local Pohozaev identities

In this section, we present the following crucial local Pohozaev identities which are used before
and interesting independently.

Proposition B.1
.

Suppose that
(u,A_{0},A_{1},A_{2})
is a weak solution of (
1.1
). Then we have the following local Pohozaev identity:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{k}}u^{2}\,dx={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}(\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2})\nu_{k}\,d\sigma-\int_{\partial\Omega}\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{k}}\,d\sigma-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{p}\nu_{k}\,d\sigma

(A.1)

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\Omega}(A_{1}\nu_{1}+A_{2}\nu_{2})A_{k}u^{2}\,d\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}(A_{0}+A_{1}^{2}+A_{2}^{2})u^{2}\nu_{k}\,d\sigma,

where
\Omega
is a bounded open domain of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
k=1,2,
and
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
is the outward unit normal of
\partial\Omega
.

Proof.

To prove them, we will take full advantage of the relationship between
A_{0}
,
A_{1}
and
A_{2}.

Assume that
(u,A_{0},A_{1},A_{2})
is a weak solution of (
1.1
). Multiplying the first equation of (
1.1
) by
\overline{\partial_{k}u+iA_{k}u}
and integrating on
\Omega
, we have

\int_{\Omega}\Big(-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u+(V(x)+A_{0}+A_{1}^{2}+A_{2}^{2})u\Big)\overline{\partial_{k}u+iA_{k}u}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\overline{\partial_{k}u+iA_{k}u}\,dx.

Observing that

\operatorname{Re}\Big\{\int_{\Omega}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u\overline{\partial_{k}u+iA_{k}u}\,dx\Big\}=-\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega}\Delta u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{k}}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\nu_{k}\,d\sigma-\int_{\partial\Omega}\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{k}}\,d\sigma,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\Big\{\int_{\Omega}(V(x)+A_{0}+A_{1}^{2}+A_{2}^{2})u\overline{\partial_{k}u+iA_{k}u}\,dx\Big\}=\int_{\Omega}(V(x)+A_{0}+A_{1}^{2}+A_{2}^{2})u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{k}}\,dx

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}(V(x)+A_{0}+A_{1}^{2}+A_{2}^{2})u^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}(V(x)+A_{0}+A_{1}^{2}+A_{2}^{2})u^{2}\nu_{k}\,d\sigma,

and

\operatorname{Re}\Big\{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\overline{\partial_{k}u+iA_{k}u}\,dx\Big\}=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{k}}\,dx=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{p}\nu_{k}\,d\sigma,

we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\nu_{k}\,d\sigma-\int_{\partial\Omega}\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{k}}\,d\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}(V(x)+A_{0}+A_{1}^{2}+A_{2}^{2})u^{2}\,dx

(A.2)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}(V(x)+A_{0}+A_{1}^{2}+A_{2}^{2})u^{2}\nu_{k}\,d\sigma-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{p}\nu_{k}\,d\sigma=0.

Using the Coulomb condition and (
1.1
), we find that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{2}\partial_{1}A_{0}-\partial_{1}\partial_{2}A_{0}=\partial_{2}(A_{2}u^{2})+\partial_{1}(A_{1}u^{2})

\displaystyle=2u(A_{1}\partial_{1}u+A_{2}\partial_{2}u)+u^{2}(\partial_{1}A_{1}+\partial_{2}A_{2})

\displaystyle=2u(A_{1}\partial_{1}u+A_{2}\partial_{2}u).

This shows that
A_{1}\partial_{1}u+A_{2}\partial_{2}u=0
. Then we can verify that for
k=1,2,

\displaystyle 0={}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\Big\{i\int_{\Omega}\overline{\partial_{k}u+iA_{k}u}(A_{1}\partial_{1}u+A_{2}\partial_{2}u)\,dx\Big\}=\int_{\Omega}(A_{k}A_{1}u\partial_{1}u+A_{k}A_{2}u\partial_{2}u)\,dx

(A.3)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{1}(A_{k}A_{1}u)u\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}A_{k}A_{1}u^{2}\nu_{1}\,d\sigma-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{2}(A_{k}A_{2}u)u\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}A_{k}A_{2}u^{2}\nu_{2}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{1}(A_{k}A_{1})u^{2}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}A_{k}A_{1}u\partial_{1}u\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}A_{k}A_{1}u^{2}\nu_{1}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{2}(A_{k}A_{2})u^{2}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}A_{k}A_{2}u\partial_{2}u\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}A_{k}A_{2}u^{2}\nu_{2}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{1}(A_{k}A_{1})u^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{2}(A_{k}A_{2})u^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}A_{k}A_{1}u^{2}\nu_{1}\,d\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}A_{k}A_{2}u^{2}\nu_{2}\,d\sigma.

Applying (
A.3
) with
k=1
and the equation (
1.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}(A_{0}+A_{1}^{2}+A_{2}^{2})u^{2}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{1}A_{0}u^{2}+\partial_{1}(A_{1}^{2})u^{2}+\partial_{1}(A_{2}^{2})u^{2})\,dx

(A.4)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{1}A_{0}u^{2}-\partial_{2}(A_{1}A_{2})u^{2}+\partial_{1}(A_{2}^{2})u^{2})\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}(A_{1}^{2}u^{2}\nu_{1}+A_{1}A_{2}u^{2}\nu_{2})\,d\sigma

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{1}A_{0}u^{2}+(A_{2}\partial_{1}A_{2}-A_{1}\partial_{2}A_{2})u^{2}+A_{2}(\partial_{1}A_{2}-\partial_{2}A_{1})u^{2})\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}(A_{1}^{2}u^{2}\nu_{1}+A_{1}A_{2}u^{2}\nu_{2})\,d\sigma

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{1}A_{0}u^{2}+(A_{2}\partial_{1}A_{2}+A_{1}\partial_{1}A_{1})u^{2}-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\partial_{1}A_{0})\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}(A_{1}^{2}u^{2}\nu_{1}+A_{1}A_{2}u^{2}\nu_{2})\,d\sigma

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{1}A_{0}u^{2}+\partial_{1}(A_{1}^{2})u^{2}+\partial_{1}(A_{2}^{2})u^{2})\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}(A_{1}^{2}u^{2}\nu_{1}+A_{1}A_{2}u^{2}\nu_{2})\,d\sigma

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2\int_{\partial\Omega}(A_{1}^{2}u^{2}\nu_{1}+A_{1}A_{2}u^{2}\nu_{2})\,d\sigma.

Similarly, we have

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}(A_{0}+A_{1}^{2}+A_{2}^{2})u^{2}\,dx=2\int_{\partial\Omega}(A_{1}A_{2}u^{2}\nu_{1}+A_{2}^{2}u^{2}\nu_{2})\,d\sigma.

(A.5)

By substituting (
A.4
) and (
A.5
) into (
A.2
), we finish the proof.
∎

A new family of binary sequences with a low correlation via elliptic curves
Lingfei Jin, Liming Ma, Chaoping Xing, Runtian Zhu

Abstract
In the realm of modern digital communication, cryptography, and signal processing, binary sequences with a low correlation properties play a pivotal role. In the literature, considerable efforts have been dedicated to constructing good binary sequences of various lengths. As a consequence, numerous constructions of good binary sequences have been put forward. However, the majority of known constructions leverage the multiplicative cyclic group structure of finite fields 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} , where p p is a prime and n n is a positive integer. Recently, the authors made use of the cyclic group structure of all rational places of the rational function field over the finite field 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} , and firstly constructed good binary sequences of length p n + 1 p^{n}+1 via cyclotomic function fields over 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} for any prime p p [ 8 , 10 ] . This approach has paved a new way for constructing good binary sequences. Motivated by the above constructions, we exploit the cyclic group structure on rational points of elliptic curves to design a family of binary sequences of length 2 n + 1 + t 2^{n}+1+t with a low correlation for many given integers | t | ⩽ 2 ( n + 2 ) / 2 |t|\leqslant 2^{(n+2)/2} . Specifically, for any positive integer d d with gcd ⁡ ( d , 2 n + 1 + t ) = 1 \gcd(d,2^{n}+1+t)=1 , we introduce a novel family of binary sequences of length 2 n + 1 + t 2^{n}+1+t , size q d − 1 − 1 q^{d-1}-1 , correlation bounded by ( 2 ​ d + 1 ) ⋅ 2 ( n + 2 ) / 2 + | t | (2d+1)\cdot 2^{(n+2)/2}+|t| , and a large linear complexity via elliptic curves.



Index Terms—Binary sequences, low correlation, elliptic curves, automorphisms, rational places.

I-A

Our main result and techniques

In this paper, we provide an explicit construction of binary sequences of length
2^{n}+1+t
with a low correlation via cyclic elliptic function fields over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
.
Let
d\geqslant 2
be a positive integer with
\gcd(d,2^{n}+1+t)=1
. We establish a family of binary sequences of length
2^{n}+1+t
, size
q^{d-1}-1
and correlation upper bounded by
2(2d+1)\sqrt{q}+1
.
For the purpose of comparison, we provide a summary of various families of binary sequences with a low correlation in Table
I
.

In order to understand the main idea of this article better, we give a high-level description of our techniques.
We use language of curves and function fields interchangeably.
To produce a sequence of length
q+1+t
, we use the additive cyclic group structures under the group law
\oplus
of rational places of cyclic elliptic curves over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}

[
23
]
.
The automorphism group of an elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is in the form
T_{E}\rtimes\Aut(E,O)
, where
\Aut(E,O)
is the automorphisms of
E
fixing the infinity place
O
, and
T_{E}
corresponds to translation group of
E

[
16
]
.
In particular,
T_{E}
is a cyclic group which is generated by a rational place
P
in this case, and there exists a generator
\sigma=\tau_{P}\in T_{E}
that makes the set
\{\sigma^{j}(P)\}_{j=0}^{q+t}
being exactly the set of rational places of
E
.
Our sequences are constructed via the absolute trace of evaluations of rational functions on such cyclically ordered rational places
\{\sigma^{j}(P)\}_{j=0}^{q+t}
.
To obtain a lower correlation, we need to choose rational functions
z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{\ell}
for some positive integer
\ell
carefully.
The cyclic group structure of
T_{E}
allows us to convert the process of determining the correlation of this family of binary sequences to the estimation of number of rational places on the Artin-Schreier extensions
E_{j}=E(y)
where
y^{2}+y=z_{j}+\sigma^{-u}(z_{j})
with
1\leqslant j\leqslant\ell
,
1\leqslant u\leqslant q+t
and Artin-Schreier extensions
E_{j,k}=E(y)
where
y^{2}+y=z_{j}+\sigma^{-u}(z_{k})
with
1\leqslant j\neq k\leqslant\ell
,
0\leqslant u\leqslant q+t
.
In order to obtain Artin-Schreier curves with small genus, first we need to choose function
z_{i}
such that
z_{i}
and
\sigma^{-t}(z_{j})
have less poles. Hence, we choose
z_{i}
from a Riemann-Roch space associated to a divisor which is just a place
Q
with degree
d\geqslant 2
of
E
.
In order to obtain Artin-Schreier curves with less rational places,
z_{j}+\sigma^{-t}(z_{k})
cannot be constant, since constant field extensions may produce more rational points.
Thus,
z_{j}
can be chosen as nonzero elements from the complementary dual space of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in the Riemann-Roch space
\mathcal{L}(Q)
.

II
Preliminaries

In this section, we present some preliminaries on binary sequences, extension theory of function fields, elliptic function fields and Artin-Schreier extensions.

II-A

Binary sequences

For a positive integer
N
, a binary sequence of length
N
is a vector
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is the finite field of two elements.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be a family of binary sequences in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
. The number of binary sequences in
\mathcal{S}
is called the family size of
\mathcal{S}
.
For a binary sequence
\mathbf{s}=(s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{N-1})
, the autocorrelation
A_{t}(\mathbf{s})
of
\mathbf{s}
at delay
t
for
1\leqslant t\leqslant N-1
is defined as

A_{t}(\mathbf{s}):=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(-1)^{s_{j}+s_{j+t}},

where
j+t
is identified with the least non-negative integer after taking modulo
N
.
For two distinct binary sequences
\mathbf{u}=(u_{0},u_{1}
,
\dots,u_{N-1})
and
\mathbf{v}=(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{N-1})
,
their cross-correlation at delay
t
with
0\leqslant t\leqslant N-1
is defined as

C_{t}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}):=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(-1)^{u_{j}+v_{j+t}}.

For the family of binary sequences
\mathcal{S}
, its correlation is defined by

\operatorname{Cor}(\mathcal{S}):=\max\left\{\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{S}\\
1\leqslant t\leqslant N-1\end{subarray}}\{|A_{t}(\mathbf{s})|\},\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{u}\neq\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{S}\\
0\leqslant t\leqslant N-1\end{subarray}}\{|C_{t}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})|\}\right\}.

In addition, we can define the linear complexity of binary sequences as follows. For any sequence
\mathbf{0}\neq\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{S}
, the linear complexity of
\mathbf{s}
, denoted by
\ell(\mathbf{s})
, is the smallest positive integer
\ell
such that there exists
\ell+1
elements
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
with
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{\ell}=1
satisfying

\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\lambda_{i}s_{i+u}=0

for all
0\leqslant u\leqslant N-1
. Define the linear complexity of the family of sequences
\mathcal{S}
by

\operatorname{LC}(\mathcal{S}):=\min\{\ell(\bf{s}):\bf{s}\in\mathcal{S}\}.

II-B

Extension theory of function fields

Let
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be an algebraic function field with genus
g
over the full constant field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{F}
denote the set of places of
F
. Any place with degree one of
F
is called rational, and all rational places of
F
form a set denoted by
\mathbb{P}_{F}^{1}
. From the Serre bound
[
26
, Theorem 5.3.1]
, the number
N(F)
of rational places of
F
is bounded by

|N(F)-q-1|\leqslant g\cdot\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor.

Here
\lfloor x\rfloor
stands for the integer part of
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\nu_{P}
be the normalized discrete valuation of
F
with respect to the place
P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
. The principal divisor of a nonzero element
z\in F
is given by
(z):=\sum_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}}\nu_{P}(z)P
.
For a divisor
G
of
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the Riemann-Roch space associated to
G
is defined by

\mathcal{L}(G):=\{z\in F^{*}:\;(z)+G\geqslant 0\}\cup\{0\}.

If
\deg(G)\geqslant 2g-1
, then
\mathcal{L}(G)
is a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of dimension
\deg(G)-g+1
from the Riemann-Roch theorem
[
26
, Theorem 1.5.17]
.

Let
\Aut(F/\mathbb{F}_{q})
denote the automorphism group of
F
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, i.e.,

\Aut(F/\mathbb{F}_{q})=\{\sigma:F\rightarrow F\;|\;\sigma\mbox{ is an }\mbox{automorphism of }F\text{ and }\sigma|_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}=\mathrm{id}\}.

Let us consider the group action of automorphism group
\Aut(F/\mathbb{F}_{q})
on the set of places of
F
.
For any place
P
of
F
,
\sigma(P)
is a place of
F
for any automorphism
\sigma\in\Aut(F/\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
From
[
18
, Lemma 1]
, we have the following results.

Lemma II.1
.

For any automorphism
\sigma\in\Aut(F/\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
and
f\in F
, we have

(1)

\deg(\sigma(P))=\deg(P)
;

(2)

\nu_{\sigma(P)}(\sigma(f))=\nu_{P}(f)
;

(3)

\sigma(f)(\sigma(P))=f(P)
, provided that
\nu_{P}(f)\geqslant 0
.

Let
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite extension of
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The Hurwitz genus formula
[
26
, Theorem 3.4.13]
yields

2{g(E)}-2=[E:F]\cdot(2g(F)-2)+\deg\operatorname{Diff}(E/F),

where
\operatorname{Diff}(E/F)
stands for the different of
E/F
.
For any
P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
and
Q\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
with
Q|P
, let
d(Q|P),e(Q|P)
and
f(Q|P)
be the different exponent, ramification index and relative degree of
Q|P
, respectively. The different of
E/F
is defined as

\text{Diff}(E/F)=\sum_{Q\in\mathbb{P}_{F}}d(Q|P)Q
and different exponents can be determined by the Dedekind different theorem and Hilbert’s different theorem
[
26
, Theorem 3.5.1 and Theorem 3.8.7]
.
In particular, the decomposition group of
Q
over
P
is defined as
G_{Z}(Q|P)=\{\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(E/F)\colon\sigma(Q)=Q\}
and its order is
e(Q|P)f(Q|P)
.

II-C

Elliptic function fields

An elliptic function field
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is an algebraic function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of genus one and a rational place
O
which is called the infinite place of
E
.
The divisor group Div
(E)
of
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is defined as the free abelian group generated by
\mathbb{P}_{E}
. The set of divisors of degree zero forms a subgroup of Div
(E)
, denoted by
\text{Div}^{0}(E)
.
Two divisors
A
and
B
in
\text{Div}(E)
are called equivalent if there exist
z\in E^{*}
such that
A=B+(z)
, which is denote by
A\sim B
.
The set of principal divisors
\text{Princ}(E)=\{(x)=\sum_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{E}}\nu_{P}(x)P:x\in E^{*}\}
is called the group of principal divisors of
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
The factor group
\text{Cl}^{0}(E)=\text{Div}^{0}(E)/\text{Princ}(E)
is called the group of divisor classes of degree zero of
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
There is a bijective map

\Phi\colon\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{P}_{E}^{1}&\longrightarrow&\operatorname{Cl}^{0}(E)\\
P&\longmapsto&[P-O]\end{array}\right.

from the set of rational places of
E
and the group of divisor classes of degree zero,
where
[\cdot]
denotes the equivalence class of
\cdot
in the quotient group
\operatorname{Cl}^{0}(E)
.
Hence, the set of rational places
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{E}
can be endowed with a group operation
\oplus
as

P\oplus Q=\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(P)+\Phi(Q)).

It is easy to see that
P\oplus Q=R
if and only if
P+Q\sim R+O
. Under this operation,
O
is the identity of the additive group
\mathbb{P}_{E}^{1}
. For convenience, we can write
[2]P=P\oplus P
and inductively define
[m+1]P=[m]P\oplus P
for any integer
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma II.2
.

Let
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be an elliptic function field and let
P,Q
be two rational places of
E
. Then

P-Q=(z)
for some element
z\in E^{*}
if and only if
P=Q
.

For elliptic curves, we can consider the isogenies between them, which are non-constant smooth morphisms that preserve the infinity point. It is well known that elliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are isogenous if and only if they have the same number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points
[
32
]
.

Lemma II.3
.

The isogeny classes of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
q=p^{n}
are in one-to-one correspondence with the rational integers
t
whose absolute value not exceed
2\sqrt{q}
and satisfying some one of the following conditions:

(i)

\gcd(t,p)=1
;

(ii)

t=\pm 2\sqrt{q}
if
n
is even;

(iii)

t=\pm\sqrt{q}
if
n
is even and
p\not\equiv 1\pmod{3}
;

(iv)

t=\pm p^{(n+1)/2}
if
n
is odd and
p=2
or
3
;

(v)

t=0
if either (i)
n
is odd or (ii)
n
is even and
p\not\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.

Furthermore, an elliptic curve in the isogeny class corresponding to
t
has
q+1+t
rational points.

The additive group structure of rational places
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{E}
of any elliptic function field
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be found from
[
23
, Theorem 3]
.

Lemma II.4
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q=p^{n}
elements. Let
h=\prod_{\ell}\ell^{h_{\ell}}
be a possible number of rational places of an elliptic function field
E
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then all the possible groups
\mathbb{P}_{E}^{1}
are the following

\mathbb{Z}/p^{h_{p}}\mathbb{Z}\times\prod_{\ell\neq p}\left(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{a_{\ell}}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{h_{\ell}-a_{\ell}}\mathbb{Z}\right)

with

(a)

in case (ii) of Lemma
II.3
: each
a_{\ell}
is equal to
h_{\ell}/2
, i.e,
\mathbb{P}_{E}^{1}\cong\mathbb{Z}/(\sqrt{q}\pm 1)\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/(\sqrt{q}\pm 1)\mathbb{Z}.

(b)

in other cases of Lemma
II.3
:
a_{\ell}
is an arbitrary integer satisfying
0\leqslant a_{\ell}\leqslant\min\{\nu_{\ell}(q-1),[h_{\ell}/2]\}
.
In cases (iii) and (iv) of Lemma
II.3
:
\mathbb{P}_{E}^{1}\cong\mathbb{Z}/h\mathbb{Z}.

In cases (v) of Lemma
II.3
: if
q\not\equiv-1(\text{mod }4)
, then
\mathbb{P}_{E}^{1}\cong\mathbb{Z}/(q+1)\mathbb{Z}
; otherwise,
\mathbb{P}_{E}^{1}\cong\mathbb{Z}/(q+1)\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{P}_{E}^{1}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/\frac{q+1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
.

If all rational places of an elliptic function field
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
form a cyclic group under the addition
\oplus
, then
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is called a cyclic elliptic function field.
In particular, we will employ this cyclic structure of the rational places of elliptic function fields with even characteristic to construct binary sequences as
[
33
, Lemma 5.3]
.

Lemma II.5
.

Let
q=2^{n}
. Let
t
be an integer satisfying one of the following conditions:

(1)

t
is an odd integer between
-2\sqrt{q}
and
2\sqrt{q}
;

(2)

t=0
;

(3)

t=\pm\sqrt{q}
if
n
is even, and
t=\pm\sqrt{2q}
if
n
is odd.

Then there exists a cyclic elliptic function field
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
has
q+1+t
rational places.

II-D

Artin-Schreier extensions

In this subsection, we summarize the main properties of Artin-Schreier extensions from
[
17
, Theorem 3.1.9]
.

Proposition II.6
.

Let
q=2^{n}
be a prime power.
Let
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with genus
g
. Let
f
be an element in
F
such that
f\neq z^{2}+z
for all
z\in F
.
Let

F^{\prime}=F(y)\text{ with }y^{2}+y=f.

For any place
P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
, we define the integer
m_{P}
by

m_{P}=\begin{cases}\ell&\text{ if }\exists z\in F\text{ satisfying }\nu_{P}(u-(z^{2}+z))=-\ell\\
&\text{ with }\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\text{ and }\ell\not\equiv 0(\text{mod }2),\\
-1&\text{ if }\nu_{P}(u-(z^{2}+z))\geqslant 0\text{ for some }z\in F.\end{cases}

Then we have the following results.

(a)

F^{\prime}/F
is a cyclic Galois extension of degree two. The automorphisms of
F^{\prime}/F
are given by
\sigma(y)=y+u
with
u=0,1.

(b)

P
is unramified in
F^{\prime}/F
if and only if
m_{P}=-1
.

(c)

P
is totally ramified in
F^{\prime}/F
if and only if
m_{P}>0
. Denote by
P^{\prime}
the unique place of
F^{\prime}
lying over
P
. Then the different exponent
d(P^{\prime}|P)
is given by

d(P^{\prime}|P)=m_{P}+1.

(d)

Assume that there is at least one place
Q
satisfying
m_{Q}>0
, then the full constant field of
F^{\prime}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the genus of
F^{\prime}
is

g(F^{\prime})=2g-1+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}}(m_{P}+1)\cdot\deg(P).

III
Binary sequences with a low correlation via elliptic curves

Motivated by the construction of families of binary sequences with a low correlation via automorphism groups of cyclotomic function fields in
[
8
,
10
]
,
we provide a new construction of binary sequences via elliptic curves.
Compared with the result in
[
33
]
, our construction achieves the same low correlation and high linear complexity but offers a larger family size.

III-A

A new construction of binary sequences via elliptic curves

Let
n
be a positive integer and
q=2^{n}
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements.
In this subsection, we shall employ the cyclic group structure of rational points and automorphism groups of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
to construct a new family of binary sequences with a low correlation and a large linear complexity.

Let
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be an elliptic function field defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q+1+t
rational places.
Let
\tau_{P}
be the automorphism of
E
induced by
\tau_{P}(Q)=P\oplus Q
for any rational place
P
and
Q
of
E
.
Let
T_{E}=\{\tau_{P}:P\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{E}\}
be the translation group of
E
. Let
O
be the infinity place of
O
and
\Aut(E,O)
be the automorphism group of
E
fixing the infinity place
O
.
From
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
, the automorphism group of
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is
\Aut(E/\mathbb{F}_{q})\cong T_{E}\rtimes\Aut(E,O)
.
Let
F
be the fixed subfield of
E
with respect to the translation group
T_{E}
, i.e.,

F=E^{T_{E}}=\{z\in E:\sigma(z)=z\text{ for any }\sigma\in T_{E}\}.

From Galois theory,
E/F
is a Galois extension with Galois group
{\rm Gal}(E/F)\cong T_{E}
.
Since the number of rational places of
E
is
q+1+t
, there exists a rational place of
F
which splits completely into
q+1+t
rational places of
E
from
[
26
, Theorem 3.1.12 and Theorem 3.7.2]
.

In particular, we assume that
t
satisfies one of the three conditions given in Lemma
II.5
.
Hence, there is a cyclic elliptic function field
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q+1+t
rational places, i.e., there exists a rational place
P
such that the least positive integer
k
satisfying
[k]P=O
is
q+1+t
.
Hence, the translation group
T_{E}
of
E
is a cyclic group of order
q+1+t
with generator
\tau_{P}
.
All rational places of
E
inherits the cyclic group structure of
T_{E}=\langle\tau_{P}\rangle
as follows.
Let
P_{j}=\tau_{P}^{j}(O)=[j]P
be the rational place of
E
for each
0\leqslant j\leqslant q+t
.
By
[
26
, Theorem 3.7.1]
, the set
\{P_{0},P_{1},\cdots,P_{q+t}\}
consists of all rational places of
E
.

Proposition III.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a cyclic elliptic function field defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q+1+t
rational places. Let
T_{E}=\langle\tau_{P}\rangle
be the translation group of
E
and
F=E^{T_{E}}
.
Let
d
be a positive integer and
Q
be a place of
E
with degree
d
. If
\gcd(d,q+1+t)=1
, then
\tau_{P}^{j}(Q)
are pairwise distinct places with degree
d
of
E
for all
0\leqslant j\leqslant q+t
.

Proof.

From
[
33
, Lemma 5.1]
or
[
4
]
, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a unique automorphism
\tau_{P}^{j}=\tau_{[j]P}
of
\Aut(E/\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that

\tau_{[j]P}(Q)+O-Q-[d][j]P

is a principal divisor of
E
for any place
Q
of
E
with degree
d
.
If
\tau_{[j]P}(Q)=Q
, then we have
[dj]P-O
is a principal divisor. By Lemma
II.2
, we have
(q+1+t)|dj
for some
j
.
Since
\gcd(d,q+1+t)=1
, we have
(q+1+t)|j
. Hence, the decomposition group of
Q|Q\cap F
is the identity automorphism of
E
.
Thus,
\tau_{P}^{j}(Q)
are pairwise distinct places with degree
d
of
E
for all
0\leqslant j\leqslant q+t

by
[
26
, Corollary 3.7.2]
.
∎

Lemma III.2
.

Let
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be an elliptic function field defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q+1+t
rational places. Let
B_{d}
be the number of places with degree
d
of
E
. Let
\mu(\cdot)
denote the M
\ddot{o}
bius function.
Then one has

B_{d}=\frac{1}{d}\cdot\sum_{r|d}\mu\left(\frac{d}{r}\right)\cdot\left(q^{r}+1-\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}(-1)^{r-i}\frac{(r-i-1)!\cdot r}{(r-2i)!\cdot i!}t^{r-2i}q^{i}\right).

In particular, we have

B_{2}=\frac{q^{2}+q-t^{2}-t}{2}\text{ and }B_{3}=\frac{q^{3}-q+t^{3}-3qt-t}{3}.

Proof.

By
[
26
, Theorem 5.1.15]
, the
L
-polynomial of
E
is given by

L_{E}(X)=1+[N(E)-(q+1)]X+qX^{2}=1+tX+qX^{2}.

Assume that
L_{E}(X)
factorizes in
\mathbb{C}[X]
as

L_{E}(X)=(1-\alpha_{1}X)(1-\alpha_{2}X).

Thus, we obtain
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=-t
and
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}=q
.
Let
S_{r}
be the sum
\alpha_{1}^{r}+\alpha_{2}^{r}
.
By
[
26
, Proposition 5.2.9]
, for all
d\geqslant 2
, we have

B_{d}=\frac{1}{d}\cdot\sum_{r|d}\mu\left(\frac{d}{r}\right)(q^{r}+1-S_{r}).

From Waring’s Formula
[
15
, Theorem 1.76]
, we have

S_{r}=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}(-1)^{r-i}\frac{(r-i-1)!\cdot r}{(r-2i)!\cdot i!}t^{r-2i}q^{i}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Let
E
be a cyclic elliptic function field with
q+1+t
rational places.
Let
Q
be a place of
E
with
\deg(Q)=d\geqslant 2
such that
\gcd(d,q+1+t)=1
.
The existence of such a place
Q
can be guaranteed by Lemma
III.2
.
Since
\deg(Q)=d\geqslant 2g(E)-1=1
, the dimension of the Riemann-Roch space
\mathcal{L}(Q)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is
\dim\mathcal{L}(Q)=\deg(Q)+1-g(E)=\deg(Q)=d
.
It is clear that
\mathbb{F}_{q}=\mathcal{L}(0)\subseteq\mathcal{L}(Q)
. Hence, there exists a vector space
V
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of dimension
d-1
such that
\mathcal{L}(Q)=\mathbb{F}_{q}\oplus V
, which is the complementary dual space of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in
\mathcal{L}(Q)
.

Let
\Tr
be the absolute trace map from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}
which is defined by
\text{Tr}(\alpha)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\alpha^{2^{i}}=\alpha+\alpha^{2}+\cdots+\alpha^{2^{n-1}}
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)=d-1
, the set
V\setminus\{0\}
can be listed as
\{z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{q^{d-1}-1}\}.

For any nonzero element
z_{i}\in V\setminus\{0\}
with
1\leqslant i\leqslant q^{d-1}-1
, a binary sequence
{\mathbf{s}}_{i}
associated to
z_{i}
can be defined as follows:

{\mathbf{s}}_{i}=(s_{i,0},s_{i,1},\cdots,s_{i,q+t})\text{ with }s_{i,j}=\text{Tr}(z_{i}(P_{j}))\text{ for }0\leqslant j\leqslant q+t.

It is clear that each sequence
{\mathbf{s}}_{i}
has length
q+1+t
and the family of binary sequences
{\mathcal{S}}=\{{\mathbf{s}}_{i}:1\leqslant i\leqslant q^{d-1}-1\}
has size
q^{d-1}-1
.
We are going to show that this family of binary sequences
{\mathcal{S}}
has a low correlation and a large linear complexity.

III-B

Binary sequences with a low correlation via elliptic curves

In this subsection, we shall show that the family of binary sequences
{\mathcal{S}}=\{{\mathbf{s}}_{i}:1\leqslant i\leqslant q^{d-1}-1\}
constructed as above has a low correlation.

Proposition III.3
.

For any elements
z_{1},z_{2}\in V\setminus\{0\}
, one has

(1)

(z_{1}+\tau(z_{2}))_{\infty}=Q+\tau(Q)
for any automorphism
\tau\in{\rm Gal}(E/F)\setminus\{\rm id\}
;

(2)

(z_{1}+z_{2})_{\infty}=Q
, provided that
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
.

Proof.

For any elements
z_{1},z_{2}\in V\setminus\{0\}
, the pole divisors of
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
are given by
(z_{1})_{\infty}=(z_{2})_{\infty}=Q.

By Lemma
II.1
, we have
\nu_{\tau(Q)}(\tau(z_{2}))=\nu_{Q}(z_{2})=-1
and
(\tau(z_{2}))_{\infty}=\tau(Q).

It follows that
z_{1}+\tau(z_{2})\in\mathcal{L}(Q+\tau(Q)).

For any automorphism
\tau\in{\rm Gal}(E/F)\setminus\{\rm id\}
, we have
\tau(Q)\neq Q
by Proposition
III.1
.
Hence, we have

(z_{1}+\tau(z_{2}))_{\infty}=Q+\tau(Q)

from the Strict Triangle Inequality
[
26
, Lemma 1.1.11]
.

Suppose that
z_{1}+z_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. It follows that
z_{1}+z_{2}\in V\cap\mathbb{F}_{q}=\{0\}
, i.e.,
z_{1}=z_{2}
which is a contradiction. Thus, we obtain
(z_{1}+z_{2})_{\infty}=Q
.
∎

Theorem III.4
.

Let
q=2^{n}
be a prime power of
2
for a positive integer
n
. Let
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a cyclic elliptic function field defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q+1+t
rational places.
Let
F
be the fixed subfield of
E
with respect to its translation group
T_{E}
generated by
\tau_{P}
.
Let
Q
be a place of
E
with
\deg(Q)=d\geqslant 2
such that
\gcd(d,q+1+t)=1
.
Then there exists a family of binary sequences
{\mathcal{S}}=\{{\mathbf{s}}_{i}:1\leqslant i\leqslant q^{d-1}-1\}
with length
q+1+t
, size
q^{d-1}-1
and correlation upper bounded by

\operatorname{Cor}({\mathcal{S}})\leqslant(2d+1)\cdot\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor+|t|.

Proof.

Let
\sigma=\tau_{P}
be the generator of the translation group
T_{E}
.
We begin by computing the autocorrelation of the family of binary sequences
{\mathcal{S}}=\{{\mathbf{s}}_{i}:1\leqslant i\leqslant q^{d-1}-1\}
.
For each
1\leqslant i\leqslant q^{d-1}-1
, the autocorrelation of
{\mathbf{s}}_{i}
at delay
u
with
1\leqslant u\leqslant q+t
is given by

\displaystyle A_{u}({\mathbf{s}}_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{q+t}(-1)^{s_{i,j}+s_{i,j+u}}=\sum_{j=0}^{q+t}(-1)^{\Tr(z_{i}(P_{j}))+\Tr(z_{i}(P_{j+u}))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{q+t}(-1)^{\Tr(z_{i}(P_{j}))+\Tr((\sigma^{-u}(z_{i}))(P_{j}))}=\sum_{j=0}^{q+t}(-1)^{\Tr((z_{i}+\sigma^{-u}(z_{i}))(P_{j}))}.

The third equality follows from Lemma
II.1
.
Since
z_{i}\in V\setminus\{0\}
and
1\leqslant u\leqslant q+t
, we have
(z_{i}+\sigma^{-u}(z_{i}))_{\infty}=Q+\sigma^{-u}(Q)
by Proposition
III.3
.
Consider the Artin-Schreier curve
E_{i}=E(y)
defined by

y^{2}+y=z_{i}+\sigma^{-u}(z_{i}).

By Proposition
II.6
, the place
Q
and
\sigma^{-u}(Q)
are the only ramified places in
E_{i}/E
and their different exponents are two.
From the Hurwitz genus formula, we have

2g(E_{i})-2=2[2g(E)-2]+2\deg(Q)+2\deg(\sigma^{-u}(Q)).

Hence, the genus of
E_{i}
is
g(E_{i})=2\deg(Q)+1=2d+1
for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant q+t
.
Let
N_{0}
denote the cardinality of the set
S_{0}=\{0\leqslant j\leqslant q+t:\Tr((z_{i}+\sigma^{-t}(z_{i}))(P_{j}))=0\}
.
Let
N_{1}
denote the cardinality of the set
S_{1}=\{0\leqslant j\leqslant q+t:\Tr((z_{i}+\sigma^{-t}(z_{i}))(P_{j}))=1\}
.
It is clear that

N_{0}+N_{1}=q+1+t.

If
j\in S_{0}
, then
P_{j}
splits into two rational places in
E_{i}
by
[
15
, Theorem 2.25]
and
[
26
, Theorem 3.3.7]
.
If
j\in S_{1}
, then
P_{j}
is inert in
E_{i}
and any place of
E_{i}
lying over
P_{j}
has degree two.
Let
N(E_{i})
be the number of rational points of the Artin-Schreier curve
E_{i}
. Then we have

N(E_{i})=2N_{0}.

From the Serre bound, we have
|N(E_{i})-q-1|\leqslant g(E_{i})\cdot\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor=(2d+1)\cdot\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor.

Hence, we obtain

|A_{u}({\mathbf{s}}_{i})|=|N_{0}-N_{1}|=|2N_{0}-q-1-t|=|N(E_{i})-q-1-t|\leqslant(2d+1)\cdot\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor+|t|.

Now let us consider the cross-correlation of the family of sequences.
For two distinct sequences
{\mathbf{s}}_{i}
and
{\mathbf{s}}_{j}
in
{\mathcal{S}}
for
1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant q^{d-1}-1
, the cross-correlation of
{\mathbf{s}}_{i}
and
{\mathbf{s}}_{j}
at delay
u
with
0\leqslant u\leqslant q+t
is given by

C_{u}({\mathbf{s}}_{i},{\mathbf{s}}_{j})=\sum_{k=0}^{q+t}(-1)^{\Tr(z_{i}(P_{k}))+\Tr(z_{j}(P_{k+u}))}=\sum_{k=0}^{q+t}(-1)^{\Tr((z_{i}+\sigma^{-u}(z_{j}))(P_{k}))}.

Consider the Artin-Schreier curve
E_{i,j}=E(y)
defined by

y^{2}+y=z_{i}+\sigma^{-u}(z_{j}).

If
1\leqslant u\leqslant q+t
, then we have
(z_{i}+\sigma^{-u}(z_{j}))_{\infty}=Q+\sigma^{-u}(Q)
by Proposition
III.3
.
From the Hurwitz genus formula and Proposition
II.6
, we have

2g(E_{i,j})-2=2[2g(E)-2]+2\deg(Q)+2\deg(\sigma^{-u}(Q)).

Hence, the genus of
E_{i,j}
is
g(E_{i,j})=2\deg(Q)+1=2d+1
for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant q-1
.
If
u=0
, then we have
(z_{i}+\sigma^{-u}(z_{j}))_{\infty}=Q
by Proposition
III.3
and the genus of
E_{i,j}
is
g(E_{i,j})=\deg(Q)+1=d+1
by Proposition
II.6
.
Let
N_{0}^{\prime}
denote the cardinality of the set
\{0\leqslant k\leqslant q+t:\Tr((z_{i}+\sigma^{-u}(z_{j}))(P_{k}))=0\}
.
Let
N_{1}^{\prime}
denote the cardinality of the set
\{0\leqslant k\leqslant q+t:\Tr((z_{i}+\sigma^{-u}(z_{j}))(P_{k}))=1\}
.
It is clear that

N_{0}^{\prime}+N_{1}^{\prime}=q+1+t.

Let
N(E_{i,j})
be the number of rational points on the Artin-Schreier curve
E_{i,j}
. Similarly, we have

N(E_{i,j})=2N_{0}^{\prime}.

From the Serre bound, we have

|C_{u}({\mathbf{s}}_{i},{\mathbf{s}}_{j})|=|N_{0}^{\prime}-N_{1}^{\prime}|=|2N_{0}^{\prime}-q-1-t|=|N(E_{i,j})-q-1-t|\leqslant(2d+1)\cdot\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor+|t|.

The proof is completed by taking the maximum of
|A_{u}(\mathbf{s}_{j})|
and
|C_{u}(\mathbf{s}_{j},\mathbf{s}_{k})|
among all possible
j,k
.
∎

Corollary III.5
.

Let
q=2^{n}
be a prime power for a positive integer
n
.
Let
t
be
0
, or
\pm\sqrt{q}
if
n
is even, or
\pm\sqrt{2q}
if
n
is odd.
Then there exists a family of binary sequences with length
q+1+t
, size
q-1
and correlation upper bounded by

\operatorname{Cor}({\mathcal{S}})\leqslant 5\cdot\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor+|t|.

Proof.

Let
E
be a cyclic elliptic function field with
q+1+t
rational places.
By Lemma
III.2
, there exists a place
Q
of
E
of degree
\deg(Q)=2
. Note that
\gcd(2,q+1+t)=1
. Thus,
this corollary follows from Proposition
III.1
, Proposition
III.3
and Theorem
III.4
.
∎

Let

Let

there exists a place

Then this corollary follows from Proposition

∎

III-C

Binary sequences with a high linear complexity

In this subsection, we shall show that the family of binary sequences constructed above has a high linear complexity similarly as
[
33
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Theorem III.7
.

The linear complexity of the family of binary sequence
{\mathcal{S}}=\{{\mathbf{s}}_{i}:1\leqslant i\leqslant q^{d-1}-1\}
satisfies

LC({\mathcal{S}})\geqslant\frac{q+1+2t-2(d+1)\sqrt{q}}{2d\sqrt{q}}.

Proof.

It remains to prove that, for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant q^{d-1}-1
, the linear complexity
\ell(\mathbf{s}_{i})
of binary sequence
\mathbf{s}_{i}
in
\mathcal{S}
satisfies

\ell(\mathbf{s}_{i})\geqslant\frac{q+1+2t-2(d+1)\sqrt{q}}{2d\sqrt{q}}.

Let
\ell=\ell(\mathbf{s}_{i})
. Then there exist
\ell+1
binary elements
\lambda_{0}=1,,\cdots,\lambda_{\ell}=1\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\lambda_{j}\cdot\Tr(z_{i}(P_{j+u}))=0

for all integers
u\geqslant 0
. Let
\sigma=\tau_{P}
be the generator of translation group
T_{E}
.
It is clear that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\lambda_{j}\cdot\Tr(z_{i}(P_{j+u}))

\displaystyle=\Tr\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\lambda_{j}z_{i}(P_{j+u})\right)

\displaystyle=\Tr\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\lambda_{j}z_{i}(\sigma^{j}(P_{u}))\right)

\displaystyle=\Tr\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\lambda_{j}\sigma^{-j}(z_{i})(P_{u})\right)

\displaystyle=\Tr(w_{i}(P_{u})),

provided that
w_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\lambda_{j}\sigma^{-j}(z_{i})
. Hence, we obtain

(\Tr(w_{i}(P_{1})),\Tr(w_{i}(P_{2})),\cdots,\Tr(w_{i}(P_{q+1+t})))={\mathbf{0}}.

Let
L_{i}=E(y)
be the Artin-Schreier extension over
E
generated by

y^{2}+y=w_{i}
. From the Strict Triangle Inequality
[
26
, Lemma 1.1.11]
, the principal divisor of
w_{i}
is given by
(w_{i})_{\infty}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\lambda_{j}\sigma^{-j}(Q)
.
By Proposition
II.6
, we have

2g(L_{i})-2=2\cdot(2g(E)-2)+\deg(\operatorname{Diff}(L_{i}/E))\leqslant 2(\ell+1)\deg(Q).

Hence, the genus of
L_{i}
is upper bounded by

g(L_{i})\leqslant d(\ell+1)+1.

It follows that

2(q+1+t)\leqslant N(L_{i})\leqslant q+1+[d(\ell+1)+1]\cdot\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor.

Hence, we obtain

\ell\geqslant\frac{q+1+2t-(d+1)\cdot\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor}{d\cdot\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor}.\qed

III-D

Comparison

In
[
33
]
, Xing
et al.
provided a construction of binary sequences with a low correlation and a large linear complexity via elliptic curves.
The family of binary sequences given in
[

IV
An explicit construction of such a family of binary sequences

The previous section provides a theoretical framework for constructing a new family of binary sequences with a low correlation and a high linear complexity using elliptic function fields.
In this section, we will make this construction explicitly.
We achieve this by determining the automorphisms and rational places explicitly. Additionally, we present some numerical results generated by using the software Sage.
It turns out that the actual correlation of our family of binary sequences is significantly lower than the estimated upper bounds provided in Theorem
III.4
.

IV-A

Algorithm

Now we provide an algorithm to generate the family of binary sequences with a low correlation and a high linear complexity constructed in Section
III
.
Such an explicit construction of sequences with a low correlation can be given as follows.

Algorithm: Explicit construction of sequences with a low correlation

•

Step 1:
Input a prime power
q=2^{n}
and a fixed integer
t
given in Lemma
II.5
.

•

Step 2:
Find a cyclic elliptic function field
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
with exactly
q+1+t
rational places.

•

Step 3:
Find a rational place
P=(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})
of
E
with order
q+1+t
and calculate
P_{j}=[j]P=(\alpha_{j},\beta_{j})
for
0\leqslant j\leqslant q+t
from
[
25
, Group Law Algorithm 2.3]
.

•

Step 4:
Let
\lambda=\frac{y-\beta_{1}}{x-\alpha_{1}}
and
v=\frac{\beta_{1}x-\alpha_{1}y}{x-\alpha_{1}}
.
The automorphism
\tau_{P}
of
E
can be determined explicitly as

\tau_{P}(x)=\lambda^{2}+a_{1}\lambda-a_{2}-x-\alpha_{1},\quad\tau_{P}(y)=-(\lambda+a_{1})\tau_{P}(x)-v-a_{3}.

Let
T_{E}
be the translation group
E
which is generated by
\tau_{P}
. Let
F
be the fixed subfield of
E
with respect to
T_{E}
.

•

Step 5:
Find the Riemann-Roch space of
\mathcal{L}(Q)
for any place
Q
with degree
d\geqslant 2
which is relatively prime to
q+1+t
and determine the vector space
V
such that
\mathcal{L}(Q)=\mathbb{F}_{q}\oplus V
.

•

Step 6:
Label all elements of
V\setminus\{0\}
with
\{z_{i}:1\leqslant i\leqslant q^{d-1}-1\}
.

•

Step 7:
Output a family of sequences
{\mathcal{S}}=\{{\mathbf{s}}_{i}:1\leqslant i\leqslant q^{d-1}-1\}
defined by

{\mathbf{s}}_{i}=(s_{i,0},s_{i,1},\cdots,s_{i,q+t})\text{ with }s_{i,j}={\Tr(z_{i}(P_{j}))}\text{ for }0\leqslant j\leqslant q+t.

•

Step 8:
Output the correlation
\operatorname{Cor}({\mathcal{S}})=\max\{\{|\sum_{k=0}^{q+t}(-1)^{s_{i,k}}(-1)^{s_{i,k+u}}|:1\leqslant i\leqslant q^{d-1}-1,1\leqslant u\leqslant q+t\}\cup\{|\sum_{k=0}^{q+t}(-1)^{s_{i,k}}(-1)^{s_{j,k+u}}|:1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant q^{d-1}-1,0\leqslant u\leqslant q+t\}\}.

Remark IV.1
.

Now we estimate the computational complexity on generating the family of binary sequences from our construction. We consider the case when
d=2
.
In Step 2, to find an elliptic curve with a specific number of points, one would typically generate random curves and count their points. One of the most effective algorithms for counting points on elliptic curves over finite fields is the Schoof-Elkies-Atkin (SEA) algorithm which takes
O((\log q)^{6})
time
[
31
]
. Thus the worst case complexity for Step 1 is

O(q^{5}(\log q)^{6})
.
In Step 3, for each element, we need to check if it is a generator which takes at most
O(q)
, thus the total time is
O(q^{2}(\log q)^{2})
.
In Step 7, we do the evaluation which takes
O(q^{2}(\log q)^{3})
.
In Step 8, we need to compute the correlation between any two sequences as well as the cyclic shift, thus the computational complexity is
O(q^{4})
.
The complexity of other steps are small, so they can be neglected. Thus, the overall complexity of generating the family of binary sequences is
O(q^{5}(\log q)^{6})
.

V
Conclusion

While significant progress has been made in constructing binary sequences with good parameters, there remains a shortage of options for sequence lengths in existing results. Therefore, it is imperative to develop sequences with alternative lengths to accommodate diverse scenarios.
Most existing families of binary sequences use the cyclic structures inherent in the multiplicative cyclic group of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Notably, recent work by
[
8
,
10
]
capitalized on the cyclic structure of
q+1
rational points on projective lines over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to design a family of binary sequences with a length of
q+1
.

In this paper, we exploit the cyclic group structure of rational points of elliptic curves to design a family of binary sequences of length
2^{n}+1+t
with a low correlation and a high linear complexity.
Our family size surpasses that of
[
33
]
. Furthermore, our construction offers the potential to derive additional sequences with a low correlation and other desirable parameters.

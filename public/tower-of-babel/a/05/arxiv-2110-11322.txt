Global Quenches after Localised Perturbations
Maurizio Fagotti

Abstract
We investigate the effect of a single spin flip preceding a global quench between translationally invariant local Hamiltonians in spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} chains. The effect of the localised perturbation does not fade away however large the distance from the perturbation is. In particular, translational invariance is not restored and the infinite time limit depends on whether the spin was flipped or not. We argue that this phenomenon is more general than the particular example considered and we conjecture that it is triggered by topological properties, specifically, the existence of “semilocal charges”.



Introduction.

In its simplest acceptation, “quantum quench” is a modern term indicating the time evolution of the ground state of some physically meaningful Hamiltonian of a quantum many-body system after a sudden change of a Hamiltonian parameter. The quench was dubbed “global” (presumably around the appearance of Refs
Calabrese and Cardy 2005
;
Eisler and Peschel 2007
) when the parameter is coupled to an extensive operator in such a way that the initial state has a significant overlap with an exponentially large number (with respect to the system size) of excited states. Probably the first analytical study of such dynamics in a spin chain dates back to
1970
, with Ref.
Barouch et al. 1970
. Global quenches have attracted a lot of attention in the last two decades for being a bridge between nonequilibrium and equilibrium physics
Polkovnikov et al. 2011
;
Gogolin and Eisert 2016
;
Eisert et al. 2015
;
Essler and Fagotti 2016
;
Vidmar and Rigol 2016
;
Calabrese and Cardy 2016
. A global quench results in complex dynamics, but at long enough time most of the details become negligible and the local properties of the state fit in the domain of statistical theories originally conceived to describe systems at equilibrium
Srednicki 1994
;
Rigol and Srednicki 2012
.
Arguably, most of our intuition about global quenches comes from studies of translationally invariant systems. Translational invariance significantly reduces complexity, and analytical studies in particularly simple models become feasible.
In this respect, integrable models have been playing a key role
Korepin et al. 1993
;
Caux and Essler 2013
;
Bonnes et al. 2014
;
Piroli et al. 2017a
;
Borsi et al. 2020

;

Integrable systems do not thermalise: they approach generalised Gibbs ensembles (GGE)

Cramer et al. 2008

Calabrese et al. 2011
;
Fagotti and Essler 2013a
;

In this Letter we report another piece of the puzzle that has been overlooked. We consider a global quench from a very simple initial state that is translationally invariant everywhere except for a finite region. Time evolution is generated by a translationally invariant local spin-chain Hamiltonian. Normally, we would expect the weak translational-symmetry breaking in the initial state to be irrelevant at large times and distances. We show that this is not the case.

The example.

We consider a spin chain described by the Hamiltonian

\boldsymbol{\bf H}=\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{L}\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{j-1}^{x}\Bigl(J_{x}\boldsymbol{\bf 1}-J_{y}\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{j}^{z}\Bigr)\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{j+1}^{x}+J_{z}\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{j}^{z}\,,

(1)

where periodic boundary conditions are understood.
This model is dual to the Heisenberg XYZ one, so it is integrable.
The duality transformation is manifest in terms of the auxiliary Pauli matrices

\boldsymbol{\bf\tau}_{j}^{z}=-\prod_{n=j}^{L-1}\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{n}^{z}\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{L}^{y}\ ,\quad\boldsymbol{\bf\tau}_{j}^{x}=\begin{cases}-\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{1}^{y}\prod_{n=2}^{L-1}\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{n}^{z}\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{L}^{y},&j=1\\
\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{j-1}^{x}\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{j}^{x},&j>1\,.\end{cases}

(2)

The Hamiltonian can indeed be written as follows

\boldsymbol{\bf H}=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{j=1}^{L}(J_{x}\boldsymbol{\bf\tau}_{j}^{x}\boldsymbol{\bf\tau}_{j+1}^{x}+J_{y}\boldsymbol{\bf\tau}_{j}^{y}\boldsymbol{\bf\tau}_{j+1}^{y}+J_{z}\boldsymbol{\bf\tau}_{j}^{z}\boldsymbol{\bf\tau}_{j+1}^{z})

(3)

where we introduced the notation

\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{j=1}^{L}\boldsymbol{\bf O}_{j}:=\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\bf 1}+\boldsymbol{\bf\Pi}_{\tau}^{x}}{2}\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}^{L}_{j=1}\!\!\boldsymbol{\bf O}_{j}\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\bf 1}+\boldsymbol{\bf\Pi}_{\tau}^{x}}{2}+\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\bf 1}-\boldsymbol{\bf\Pi}_{\tau}^{x}}{2}\boldsymbol{\bf\tau}_{1}^{z}\sideset{}{{}^{-}}{\sum}_{j=1}^{L}\!\!\boldsymbol{\bf O}_{j}\boldsymbol{\bf\tau}_{1}^{z}\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\bf 1}-\boldsymbol{\bf\Pi}_{\tau}^{x}}{2},

(4)

Note that the projectors into the distinct sectors can be easily written in terms of the

Semilocal charges.

We have provided a counterexample to the belief that localised perturbations in the initial state do not survive the limit of infinite time after a global quench; we have not yet clarified why generally this is not the case. What is so special in the model described by
\boldsymbol{\bf H}
?
In this respect, the duality transformation is again helpful. We have indeed an almost perfect knowledge of
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\bf H}}
with
J_{z}=0
. In particular we know that the model is non-abelian integrable
Fagotti 2014
;
Fagotti 2016b
and the local conservation laws are linear combinations of
\boldsymbol{\bf\tau}_{j}^{z}
and operators of the form

\boldsymbol{\bf\tau}_{j}^{\alpha}\prod_{m=j+1}^{j+n-1}\boldsymbol{\bf\tau}_{m}^{z}\boldsymbol{\bf\tau}_{j+n}^{\beta}
,
where
\alpha,\beta\in\{x,y\}

Grady 1982
;
Prosen 1998
. In order to have a local
\sigma
-representation, they should commute with
\boldsymbol{\bf\Pi}^{x}_{\tau}
, but this happens only if they consist exclusively of operators for which
(\alpha=\beta\wedge n\text{\emph{ is odd}})\vee(\alpha\neq\beta\wedge n\text{\emph{ is even}})
. This is realised only in a subset of the local conservation laws. In fact, such charges are completely irrelevant in the description of the long time limit: they have the same expectation value on both sides of the junction and do not affect the emerging locally quasi-stationary state.
We are therefore left with charges that are local only in the
\tau
-representation. In the
\sigma
-representation they have instead the following form (in the thermodynamic limit):

\boldsymbol{\bf Q}=\sum\nolimits_{j}\boldsymbol{\bf q}_{j}\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{j+1}^{z}\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{j+2}^{z}\cdots\,,

(15)

where
\boldsymbol{\bf q}_{j}
acts like the identity everywhere except for a finite region ending at site
j
. By borrowing a terminology used in field theory, we call them ‘semilocal’; note, in particular, that their density
\boldsymbol{\bf q}_{j}\prod_{n=j+1}^{\infty}\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{n}^{z}
commutes with any local operator with support to the left of the support of
\boldsymbol{\bf q}_{j}
but anticommutes with any local operator to the right if the latter anticommutes with
\boldsymbol{\bf\Pi}^{z}_{\sigma}
.
We can also define the corresponding semilocal currents, which, due to the locality of the Hamiltonian, have the same semilocal form

\boldsymbol{\bf J}_{j}[\boldsymbol{\bf q}]=\boldsymbol{\bf j}_{j}\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{j+1}^{z}\boldsymbol{\bf\sigma}_{j+2}^{z}\cdots
.
We believe that one could capture the long-time dynamics directly in the
\sigma
-representation by including the semilocal charges in the generalised hydrodynamic theory, but this question will be addressed in a separate investigation.

Interaction.

In the following we provide an argument that a similar phenomenology should be expected also in the presence of interactions. To that aim we consider the case
J_{x}=J_{y}\neq J_{z}
, which corresponds to the XXZ Heisenberg model. We point out that this is not a genuine global quench because the initial state is close to an eigenstate of the Hamiltonian.

but we leave it as an open question, which we can also extend to generic systems with a single semilocal charge. There are instead other choices of the coupling constants that are compatible with a ballistic spreading. Besides the

Topological properties.

The typical properties of nonequilibrium states that are locally stationary are generally fragile under global transformations of the initial state
3
3

3

Discussion.

We have shown that, even after global quenches, localised perturbations to the initial state can have macroscopic effects.
We have proved it in a model that can be mapped to noninteracting fermions and provided evidence that analogous behaviours can be seen in the presence of interactions preserving integrability. We traced the phenomenon back to the existence of semilocal charges, which should be incorporated in the generalised Gibbs ensemble, as well as in the generalised hydrodynamic theory. How to do that remains an open question.
All the examples discussed in which we expect relevant localised perturbations are described by Hamiltonians with a non-abelian set of (quasi)local conservation laws. We did not use this hidden structure in the case we solved analytically, but we still wonder whether a duality transformation mapping non-abelian integrable systems into systems with (infinitely many) semilocal charges should be generally expected.

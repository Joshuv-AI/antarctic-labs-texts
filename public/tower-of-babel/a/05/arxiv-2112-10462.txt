1.
Introduction

This paper is devoted to classify the structure of standing waves for the following system of Schrödinger-Poisson type,

(1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &2i\dot{v}_{+}-\Delta v_{+}+(\mu_{11}\phi_{v_{+}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{v_{-}})v_{+}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(v_{+}+e^{i\theta}\overline{v}_{-})d\theta=0,\\
&2i\dot{v}_{-}-\Delta v_{-}+(\mu_{22}\phi_{v_{-}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{v_{+}})v_{-}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(v_{-}+e^{i\theta}\overline{v}_{+})d\theta=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where
v_{\pm}(t,x):\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
\phi_{u}=(4\pi|\cdot|)^{-1}\ast|u|^{2}
,
\mu_{ij}>0
for
i,j=1,2
and
g(v):\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying
g(e^{is}v)=e^{is}g(v)
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
This system arises as a limit system of the nonlinear Maxwell-Klein-Gordon equations when we consider the nonrelativistic limit, taking the speed of light to infinity.
The nonlinear Maxwell-Klein-Gordon equations are written as follows:

(NMKG)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &D_{\alpha}D^{\alpha}\psi=(mc)^{2}\psi-g(\psi),\\
&\partial^{\beta}F_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{c}\text{Im}(\psi\overline{D_{\alpha}\psi}),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\psi(t,x):\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a wave function,
g:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a nonlinear potential satisfying
g(e^{is}v)=e^{is}g(v)
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

D_{\alpha}=\partial_{\alpha}+\frac{i}{c}A_{\alpha},\alpha=0,1,2,3
is the covariant derivative and

F_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_{\alpha}A_{\beta}-\partial_{\beta}A_{\alpha}
is the curvature tensor.
Here, we write
\partial_{0}=\frac{\partial}{c\partial t}
,
\partial_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}},j=1,2,3
.
Indices are raised under the Minkowski metric
g_{\alpha\beta}=\text{diag}(-1,1,1,1)
, i.e.,
X^{\alpha}=g_{\alpha\beta}X_{\beta}
.
The positron part
v_{+}
and electron part
v_{-}
of a solution
\psi
to (NMKG) are given by

v_{+}\coloneqq e^{-ic^{2}t}\frac{1}{2}\left(\psi-i\langle\nabla\rangle_{c}^{-1}D^{+}_{0}\psi\right),\quad v_{-}\coloneqq e^{-ic^{2}t}\frac{1}{2}\left(\bar{\psi}-i\langle\nabla\rangle_{c}^{-1}D^{-}_{0}\bar{\psi}\right),

where
\langle\nabla\rangle_{c}
denotes the Fourier multiplier with the symbol
\sqrt{|\cdot|^{2}+c^{2}}
and

D_{\alpha}^{\pm}=\partial_{\alpha}\pm\frac{i}{c}A_{\alpha}
.
Some formal computation (for example, see
[
21
]
) shows that by taking the nonrelativistic limit
c\to\infty
,

(v_{+},v_{-})
approaches to a solution of the system (
1
) with
\mu_{ij}=2
.
In other words, a solution of (NMKG) can be approximated as the two different modes of oscillations, namely
\psi=e^{ic^{2}t}v_{+}+e^{-ic^{2}t}\overline{v_{-}}+o_{c}(1)
as
c\rightarrow\infty
, where
(v_{+},v_{-})
is a solution to the system (
1
). The integration
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(v_{\pm}+e^{i\theta}\overline{v}_{\mp})d\theta
in (
1
) describes the resonances and if we take the cubic nonlinearity
g(v)=|v|^{2}v
, then it simply becomes
(|v_{\pm}|^{2}+2|v_{\mp}|^{2})v_{\pm}
.

In
[
21
,
22
]
, Masmoudi-Nakanishi rigorously justified the convergence of nonrelativistic limit in the space
C[0,T],H^{1})
either the Maxwell gauge terms are not involved, that is,
A_{\alpha}=0
for
\alpha=0,1,2,3
or
the nonlinear potential term
g
is absent.
In these cases, the system (
1
) reduces to respectively the systems

(2)

2i\dot{v}_{\pm}-\Delta v_{\pm}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(v_{\pm}+e^{i\theta}\overline{v_{\mp}})d\theta=0

or

(3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &2i\dot{v}_{+}-\Delta v_{+}+(\mu_{11}\phi_{v_{+}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{v_{-}})v_{+}=0,\\
&2i\dot{v}_{-}-\Delta v_{-}+(\mu_{22}\phi_{v_{-}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{v_{+}})v_{-}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

We note that the system (
1
) serves as a physically meaningful generalization of several well-studied semilinear PDEs.
For example, when either
v_{+}
or
v_{-}
vanishes, the system (
1
) is reduced to the so-called Schrödinger-Poisson equation

2i\dot{v}-\Delta v+\mu\phi_{v}v-g(v)=0,

which is extensively studied during past two decades. We refer to the literatures
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
23
,
29
]

for the existence and qualitative properties of positive standing waves.
The system (
2
) with
g(u)=|u|^{2}u
is called the coupled system of cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equations and appeared in theory of Bose–Einstein condensates and nonlinear optics (see
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
20
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
30
,
32
,
35
]
and references therein).
Moreover, if we take
v_{+}=v_{-}
and
\mu_{11}=\mu_{22}
in the system (
3
), we obtain the nonlinear Hartree equation

2i\dot{v}-\Delta v+\mu\phi_{v}v=0,

which arises in the self gravitational collapse of quantum mechanical system and physics of laser beams.
We refer to
[
16
,
18
,
19
,
24
]
and references therein for the study of ground states and standing waves.
In relating with the system (
3
), the existence of positive standing waves in
[
34
]
is studied when the parameters
\mu_{11}
and
\mu_{22}
are negative.

The main novelty of this paper therefore is to investigate the existence of standing waves having nontrivial components
v_{+},\,v_{-}
to the full system (
1
) with positive coupling parameters
\mu_{ij}>0
.
We will see that the nontrivial standing waves of the aforementioned reduced systems play a significant role in analyzing structures of standing waves to (
1
).

Now, we insert the standing wave ansatz

v_{+}(x,t)=u_{1}(x)e^{i\frac{\lambda}{2}t}\mbox{ and }v_{-}(x,t)=u_{2}(x)e^{i\frac{\lambda}{2}t}

into the system (
1
) to obtain

(4)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}+\lambda u_{1}+(\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{2}})u_{1}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(u_{1}+e^{i\theta}u_{2})d\theta\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta u_{2}+\lambda u_{2}+(\mu_{22}\phi_{u_{2}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}})u_{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(u_{2}+e^{i\theta}u_{1})d\theta\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \mathbb{R}^{3},\end{cases}

where
u_{1},u_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda,\mu_{ij}>0
for
i,j=1,2
.
We denote by
\vec{u}
a pair of functions
(u_{1},\,u_{2})
.
Here we define some concepts of triviality and positiveness of a vector function
\vec{u}
.

Definition
.

A vector function
\vec{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
is said to be

*

nontrivial if either
u_{1}\neq 0
or
u_{2}\neq 0
;

*

semi-trivial if it is nontrivial but either
u_{1}=0
or
u_{2}=0
;

*

vectorial if both of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are not zero;

*

nonnegative if
u_{1}\geq 0
and
u_{2}\geq 0
;

*

positive if
u_{1}>0
and
u_{2}>0
.

We denote by
H^{1}=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the space of real valued smooth function with compact support, with respect to the standard Sobolev norm

\|u\|_{\lambda}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\lambda u^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The notation
H_{r}^{1}
denotes the space of radially symmetric functions in
H^{1}
.
We also define
{\bf H}\coloneqq H_{r}^{1}\times H_{r}^{1}
equipped with the norm

\|\vec{u}\|_{\bf{H}}^{2}=\|u_{1}\|_{\lambda}^{2}+\|u_{2}\|_{\lambda}^{2}.

We now are ready to state the main results. Our first result deals with the case
g=0
, in which the system (
4
) is reduced to

(5)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}+\lambda u_{1}+(\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{2}})u_{1}=0\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta u_{2}+\lambda u_{2}+(\mu_{22}\phi_{u_{2}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}})u_{2}=0\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{cases}

In this case we can provide a satisfactory picture for the solution structure of (
5
).
Interestingly, it turns out that the sign of determinant of the matrix
(\mu_{ij})
completely determines the existence of nontrivial solutions to (
5
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\lambda,\mu_{ij}>0
for
i,j=1,2
. If
\mu_{11}\mu_{22}\geq\mu_{12}^{2}
, then (
5
) has only the trivial solution in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
If
\mu_{11}\mu_{22}<\mu_{12}^{2}
, The system (
5
) admits a positive vector solution
\vec{u}\in\mathbf{H}
, which is unique in the class of nontrivial nonnegative functions in
H
.
Actually, it is determined as
\vec{u}=(V,a_{0}V)
, where
a_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{11}+\mu_{12}}{\mu_{22}+\mu_{12}}}
and

V
is a unique positive radial solution of the equation

(6)

-\Delta u+\lambda u+\frac{\mu_{11}\mu_{22}-\mu_{12}^{2}}{\mu_{22}+\mu_{12}}\phi_{u}u=0.

Remark 1
.

It seems interesting to compare the system (
5
) with the corresponding single equation, so-called nonlinear Hartree equation

-\Delta u+\lambda u+\mu\phi_{u}u=0.

It is proved in
[
16
,
19
]
that it admit a unique positive radial solution when
\mu<0
. (Note that this makes the statement of Theorem
1.1
valid.) By multiplying by
u
and integrating, it is also easy to see it has only trivial solution if
\mu\geq 0
.
Here, we point out that the sign of
\text{det}(\mu_{ij})
of the system (
5
) plays the same role with the sign of
\mu
.

Remark 2
.

It is immediate to show that the system (
5
) does not have any semi-trivial solution. Suppose, for example,
u_{2}=0
for a solution
\vec{u}

so that (
5
) reduces to
-\Delta u_{1}+\lambda u_{1}+\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}=0
.
Then as mentioned in the previous remark, we see
u_{1}=0
.

We next turn our attention to the case
g\neq 0
. In this paper, we only focus on the standard power function
g(v)=|v|^{p-1}v
.
Then we can write the system (
4
) as

(7)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}+\lambda u_{1}+(\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{2}})u_{1}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|u_{1}+e^{i\theta}u_{2}|^{p-1}(u_{1}+e^{i\theta}u_{2})d\theta\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta u_{2}+\lambda u_{2}+(\mu_{22}\phi_{u_{2}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}})u_{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|u_{2}+e^{i\theta}u_{1}|^{p-1}(u_{2}+e^{i\theta}u_{1})d\theta\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{cases}

In this case, one can compare the system (
7
) with

(8)

-\Delta u+\lambda u+\mu\phi_{u}u=|u|^{p-1}u\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},

which is called the nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson equation.
It is proved in
[
10
]
that when
p\in(0,1]\cup[5,\infty)
, there exists no nontrivial solution to (
8
).
In the interesting paper
[
29
]
, Ruiz investigated the existence of positive radial solutions to (
8
) with the subcritical range of
p\in(1,5)
.
He shows that while a positive radial solution exists when
p\in(2,5)
for each
\lambda,\mu>0
, the situation is changed when we consider the case
p\in(1,2]
, where
there exists no nontrivial solution for large
\mu>0
and there exist two positive radial solutions for small
\mu>0
.

It is therefore naturally expected that the system (
7
) admits only trivial solution if the range of the exponent
p>0
is either sub-linear or
H^{1}
super-critical. The second result of this paper confirms this whenever
\det{(\mu_{ij})}\geq 0
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\lambda,\mu_{ij}>0
for
i,j=1,2
satisfying
\mu_{11}\mu_{22}\geq\mu_{12}^{2}
. If
p\in(0,1]\cup[5,\infty)
, then (
7
) has only the trivial solution in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Remark 3
.

The condition
\mu_{11}\mu_{22}\geq\mu_{12}^{2}
is not a technical matter. Indeed, we note that, when
p=1
, (
7
) becomes the following systems

(9)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}+(\lambda-1)u_{1}+(\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{2}})u_{1}=0\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta u_{2}+(\lambda-1)u_{2}+(\mu_{22}\phi_{u_{2}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}})u_{2}=0\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{cases}

Then, by Theorem
1.1
, we deduce that if
\lambda>1
and
\mu_{ij}>0
with
\mu_{11}\mu_{22}<\mu_{12}^{2}
, then (
9
) has a positive solution.

In the subsequent theorems, we investigate the structure of ground states to (
7
) in the super-linear and sub-critical range of
p\in(1,5)
to search for positive vector solutions.
Observe that the system (
7
) is the Euler-Lagrange system of the action functional

\displaystyle I(\vec{u})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}+\lambda u_{2}^{2}dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{11}u_{1}^{2}\phi_{u_{1}}+\mu_{22}u_{2}^{2}\phi_{u_{2}}-2\mu_{12}u_{1}^{2}\phi_{u_{2}}dx

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{p+1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|u_{1}+e^{i\theta}u_{2}|^{p+1}d\theta dx.

We say that a solution
\vec{u}\in\mathbf{H}
to (
7
) is a (radial) ground state if
\vec{u}
is nontrivial and minimizes the value of
I
among all nontrivial solutions to (
7
) in
\mathbf{H}
.

Similarly with the results on the nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson equations, it turns out that the solution structure of (
7
) changes at the borderline
p=2
.
The next theorem is concerned with the range of
p\in(2,5)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
2<p<5
and
\lambda,\mu_{ij}>0
for
i,j=1,2
.
Then there exists a non-negative ground state solution
\vec{w}
of (
7
).
If either
2<p<5
and
\mu_{11}=\mu_{22}
; or
\frac{1}{3}(-2+\sqrt{73})\leq p<5
, then
\vec{w}
is positive.
Moreover, if
2<p<5
and
\mu_{11}=\mu_{22}
, then
\vec{w}
must be of the form
\vec{w}=(U,U)
, where
U
is a positive ground state solution of

(10)

-\Delta u+\lambda u+({\mu_{11}-\mu_{12}})\phi_{u}u=\frac{c_{p}}{2}|u|^{p-1}u\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}

with
c_{p}=\frac{2^{\frac{p-1}{2}}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+\cos\theta)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}d\theta
.

Remark 4
.

We note that the number
\frac{1}{3}(-2+\sqrt{73})\cong 2.18133
is slightly larger than 2.
We believe that this number appears in just a technical matter and any ground state is actually positive in the whole range of
2<p<5
without the assumption
\mu_{11}=\mu_{22}
.

Now, we turn to the remaining case
p\in(1,2]
.
It turns out that the solution structure varies when we change the sign of
\det{(\mu_{ij})}
.
We first construct a positive solution of (
7
) when
\mu_{12}>0
is taken to be large, i.e.,
\det(\mu_{ij})<0
and
|\det(\mu_{ij})|
is large.
The solution is obtained as a perturbation of
(V,V)
, where
V
is the solution of a nonlinear Hartree equation

-\Delta V+\lambda V-\phi_{V}V=0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
1<p\leq 2
and
\lambda,\mu_{11},\,\mu_{22}>0
be fixed. Then there exists a constant
\mu_{0}>0
such that if
\mu_{12}>\mu_{0}
, the system (
7
) admits a positive solution.

The next two theorems cover the case
\det{(\mu_{ij})}>0
.
We will see that, unlike the case
\det{(\mu_{ij})<0}
, the system (
7
) does not admit any nontrivial solution when
\det(\mu_{ij})
is taken to be large.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
1<p\leq 2
and
\mu_{ij}>0
for
i,j=1,2
. Assume that
\lambda\geq 2
,
\mu_{11}>4
and
(\mu_{11}-4)(\mu_{22}-4)>\mu_{12}^{2}.

Then
(
7
) has only the trivial solution in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Our final result deals with the case that
\det{(\mu_{j})}>0
and
\mu_{ij}
is small.
We construct two positive solutions to (
7
) such that one has positive energy and the other one is an energy minimizer which has negative energy.
This corresponds to the work by Ruiz
[
29
]
in which the same kind of positive solutions for (
10
) are found.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
1<p<2
and
\lambda,\mu_{ij}>0
for
i,j=1,2
. If
\mu_{11}\mu_{22}-\mu_{12}^{2}>0
and
\mu_{ij}
is small enough, where
i,j=1,2
, then there are at least two different positive radial solutions
\vec{u}_{1}
,
\vec{u}_{2}
of (
7
), where
\vec{u}_{1}
is a positive minimizer of (
7
) with negative energy, and
\vec{u}_{2}
is a positive solution of (
7
) with positive energy. Moreover, if we assume
\mu_{11}\mu_{22}-\mu_{12}^{2}>0
,
\mu_{11}=\mu_{22}
and
\mu_{ij}>0
is small enough, where
i,j=1,2
, then
\vec{u}_{1}
and
\vec{u}_{2}
have the form
\vec{u}_{1}=(U_{1},U_{1})
and
\vec{u}_{2}=(U_{2},U_{2})
, where
U_{1}
is a positive minimizer of (
10
) with negative energy, and
U_{2}
is a positive solution of (
10
) with positive energy.

Remark 5
.

Theorem
1.4
and
1.6
show that the value of
\det(\mu_{ij})
affects the solution structure of (
7
) in the case
p\in(1,2]
.
If
\det(\mu_{ij})
is largely negative, then there exists a positive solution bifurcating from the ground state of the nonlinear Hartree equation.
If
\det(\mu_{ij})
is positive and the coefficient are small, then there exists two positive solution bifurcating from two positive solution of the nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson equations.

2.
Classification of solutions in zero potential case

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
, which gives a complete classification of nonnegative solutions for (
5
).
Throughout this section, we assume
\lambda,\mu_{ij}>0
for
i,j=1,2
and
\vec{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
always denotes an arbitrary solution of (
5
).

2.1.
Triviality of
\vec{u}
when
\det(\mu_{ij})\geq 0

We first deal with the case
\det(\mu_{ij})\geq 0
, i.e.,
\mu_{11}\mu_{22}\geq\mu_{12}^{2}
.
Our claim is that
\vec{u}
is trivial.
Multiplying the first equation of (
5
) by
u_{1}
and the second equation by
u_{2}
respectively, and integrating, we see that

(11)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(-\Delta u_{1}+\lambda u_{1}+(\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{2}})u_{1}\Big)u_{1}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}+\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}^{2}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{2}}u_{1}^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}+\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}^{2}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{2}^{2}dx

and

(12)

0=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(-\Delta u_{2}+\lambda u_{2}+(\mu_{22}\phi_{u_{2}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}})u_{2}\Big)u_{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}+\lambda u_{2}^{2}+\mu_{22}\phi_{u_{2}}u_{2}^{2}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{2}^{2}dx.

Note that, since
\mu_{ij}>0,\mu_{11}\mu_{22}\geq\mu_{12}^{2}
and

\nabla\phi_{u_{1}}\cdot\nabla\phi_{u_{2}}\leq\frac{\mu_{11}}{2\mu_{12}}|\nabla\phi_{u_{1}}|^{2}+\frac{\mu_{12}}{2\mu_{11}}|\nabla\phi_{u_{2}}|^{2},

where
i,j=1,2
, we have

(13)

\mu_{11}|\nabla\phi_{u_{1}}|^{2}+\mu_{22}|\nabla\phi_{u_{2}}|^{2}-2\mu_{12}\nabla\phi_{u_{1}}\cdot\nabla\phi_{u_{2}}\geq\frac{\mu_{11}\mu_{22}-\mu_{12}^{2}}{\mu_{11}}|\nabla\phi_{u_{2}}|^{2}\geq 0.

Then, by (
13
), adding (
11
) and (
12
), we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}+\lambda u_{2}^{2}+\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}^{2}+\mu_{22}\phi_{u_{2}}u_{2}^{2}-2\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{2}^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}+\lambda u_{2}^{2}+\mu_{11}|\nabla\phi_{u_{1}}|^{2}+\mu_{22}|\nabla\phi_{u_{2}}|^{2}-2\mu_{12}\nabla\phi_{u_{1}}\cdot\nabla\phi_{u_{2}}dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}+\lambda u_{2}^{2}dx,

which implies that
\vec{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is trivial.

2.2.
Explicit form of
\vec{u}
when
\det(\mu_{ij})<0

We next assume
\mu_{11}\mu_{22}<\mu_{12}^{2}
. Suppose that
\vec{u}
is nontrivial, nonnegative and radial.
We have already seen from Remark
2
that
\vec{u}
is vectorial.
Then the strong maximum principle applies to see that
\vec{u}
is positive.
We now state a lemma which clarifies a structure of
\vec{u}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
\lambda,\mu_{12}>0
,
\mu_{ii}\geq 0
for
i=1,2
.
Then for any positive radial solutions
\vec{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
of (
5
), we have
u_{2}\equiv a_{0}u_{1}
, where
a_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{11}+\mu_{12}}{\mu_{22}+\mu_{12}}}
.

Proof.

Let
\vec{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
be a positive radial solution of (
5
).
By Newton’s Theorem and Holder’s inequality, we see that

(14)

\phi_{u_{i}}(x)=\frac{1}{|x|}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}(s)s^{2}\min\{|x|s^{-1},1\}ds\leq C\|u_{i}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\min\{1,|x|^{-1}\}

for
i=1,2
. Thus, by the comparison principle, for any
\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda<\lambda^{\prime\prime}
, there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}\exp(-\lambda^{\prime\prime}|x|)\leq u_{i}(x)\leq C_{2}\exp(-\lambda^{\prime}|x|)\mbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

and thus
u_{i}^{2}/u_{j}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, where
i,j=1,2
. Denote
v_{2}\equiv a_{0}^{-1}u_{2}
, where
a_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{11}+\mu_{12}}{\mu_{22}+\mu_{12}}}
.
Then, since
\phi_{u_{2}}=a_{0}^{2}\phi_{v_{2}}
, we have

(15)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}+\lambda u_{1}+(\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}-a_{0}^{2}\mu_{12}\phi_{v_{2}})u_{1}=0,\\
-\Delta v_{2}+\lambda v_{2}+(a_{0}^{2}\mu_{22}\phi_{v_{2}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}})v_{2}=0.\end{cases}

By dividing the first and second equations of (
15
) by
u_{1}
and
v_{2}
, respectively, and subtracting the second equation from
the first equation, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-(\Delta u_{1})u_{1}^{-1}+(\Delta v_{2})v_{2}^{-1}+\Big((\mu_{11}+\mu_{12})\phi_{u_{1}}-a_{0}^{2}(\mu_{22}+\mu_{12})\phi_{v_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=-(\Delta u_{1})u_{1}^{-1}+(\Delta v_{2})v_{2}^{-1}+(\mu_{11}+\mu_{12})(\phi_{u_{1}}-\phi_{v_{2}}).

Multiplying the above equation by
u_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2}
, then integration by parts yields

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla\Big(\frac{u_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2}}{u_{1}}\Big)-\nabla v_{2}\cdot\Big(\frac{u_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2}}{v_{2}}\Big)+(\mu_{11}+\mu_{12})(\phi_{u_{1}}-\phi_{v_{2}})(u_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2})\Big|\frac{\nabla u_{1}}{u_{1}}-\frac{\nabla v_{2}}{v_{2}}\Big|^{2}+(\mu_{11}+\mu_{12})|\nabla(\phi_{u_{1}}-\phi_{v_{2}})|^{2}dx,

which implies that
u_{1}\equiv v_{2}
.
∎

By applying Lemma
2.1
, we can immediately see that
\vec{u}
has the form
\vec{u}=(V,a_{0}V)
, where
a_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{11}+\mu_{12}}{\mu_{22}+\mu_{12}}}
and
V
is a positive solution of the Hartree equation

(16)

-\Delta u+\lambda u+\frac{\mu_{11}\mu_{22}-\mu_{12}^{2}}{\mu_{22}+\mu_{12}}\phi_{u}u=0.

We finally refer to
[
16
, Theorem 10]
or
[
19
, Lemma 9]
to conclude that if any positive radial solution of (
16
) is unique when
\mu_{11}\mu_{22}<\mu_{12}^{2}
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3.
Structure theorems for nonnegative solutions in power potential case

The rest of the paper is devoted to the study of the system (
7
), that is, the power potential case.
We begin with defining the energy functional for (
7
):

\displaystyle I(\vec{u})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}+\lambda u_{2}^{2}dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{11}u_{1}^{2}\phi_{u_{1}}+\mu_{22}u_{2}^{2}\phi_{u_{2}}-2\mu_{12}u_{1}^{2}\phi_{u_{2}}dx

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{p+1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|u_{1}+e^{i\theta}u_{2}|^{p+1}d\theta dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}+\lambda u_{2}^{2}dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{11}u_{1}^{2}\phi_{u_{1}}+\mu_{22}u_{2}^{2}\phi_{u_{2}}-2\mu_{12}u_{1}^{2}\phi_{u_{2}}dx

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{p+1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}d\theta dx.

We prove in Section
B
that
I
is twice continuously differentiable on
\mathbf{H}
and its critical points are solutions to (
7
).
By finding nonzero critical points of
I
, one therefore can provide several nontrivial solutions to (
7
). This shall be done throughout subsequent sections.

One fundamental task of this paper is to distinguish these solutions from the semi-trivial ones.
We will see that this can be accomplished by obtaining some information on mathematical structures of the semi-trivial solutions such as the energy levels or Morse indices of them.
The main purpose of this section is to investigate such structures of semi-trivial solutions.
We also prove a rigidity theorem for positive solutions, which are required to give a classification on positive solutions.

3.1.
Morse indices of semi-trivial solutions

In this subsection, we compute a lower bound of Morse indices of semi-trivial critical points of
I
.
We say
n
is the Morse index of a critical point
\vec{u}
of
I
if
n
is the maximal dimension of subspaces
V\subset\mathbf{H}
satisfying

I^{\prime\prime}(\vec{u})[h,h]<0\quad\text{for all }h\in V.

The following identity is straightforward from (
79
) in Appendix
B
but frequently invoked throughout this section.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
1<p<5
. For
i=1,2
, let
\lambda>0,\mu_{ij}\geq 0,(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
(u_{1},u_{2})\in{\bf H}
. Then we have

(17)

\displaystyle I^{\prime\prime}(u_{1},0)\Big[(t_{1}u_{1},t_{2}u_{1}),(t_{1}u_{1},t_{2}u_{1})\Big]

\displaystyle=t_{1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}+3\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}^{2}-p|u_{1}|^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle+t_{2}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}^{2}-\frac{p+1}{2}|u_{1}|^{p+1}dx

and

\displaystyle I^{\prime\prime}(0,u_{2})\Big[(t_{1}u_{2},t_{2}u_{2}),(t_{1}u_{2},t_{2}u_{2})\Big]

\displaystyle=t_{1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}+\lambda u_{2}^{2}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{2}}u_{2}^{2}-\frac{p+1}{2}|u_{2}|^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle\quad+t_{2}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}+\lambda u_{2}^{2}+3\mu_{22}\phi_{u_{2}}u_{2}^{2}-p|u_{2}|^{p+1}dx.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that
1<p<5
. Let
\lambda
and
\mu_{ij}
be positive constants for
i=1,2
.
Then every semi-trivial critical point of
I
has Morse index larger than or equal to
1
.

Proof.

We may only deal with the semi-trivial critical point
(u_{1},0)
due to the symmetry of the system (
7
).
Since
u_{2}=0
,
u_{1}
solves

-\Delta u_{1}+\lambda u_{1}+\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}=|u_{1}|^{p-1}u_{1}

so that one has

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}+\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}^{2}-|u_{1}|^{p+1}dx=0.

Then by (
17
), we see that for
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle I^{\prime\prime}(u_{1},0)\Big[(0,tu_{1}),(0,tu_{1})\Big]

\displaystyle=t^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}^{2}-\frac{p+1}{2}|u_{1}|^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle=-t^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mu_{11}+\mu_{12})\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}^{2}+\frac{p-1}{2}|u_{1}|^{p+1}dx<0.

This shows that
1
is a lower bound of Morse index of
(u_{1},0)
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu_{ij}
be positive constants for
i=1,2
. Assume that
\frac{1}{3}(-2+\sqrt{73})\leq p<5
.
Then every semi-trivial critical point of
I
has Morse index larger than or equal to
2
.

Proof.

Let
(u_{1},0)
be a semi-trivial critical point of
I
so that
u_{1}
solves

-\Delta u_{1}+\lambda u_{1}+\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}=|u_{1}|^{p-1}u_{1}.

Define

a=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}dx,\ \ b=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\lambda u_{1}^{2},\ \ c=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}^{2},\ \ d=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{1}|^{p+1}dx.

Then we have

(18)

\begin{cases}a+b+c-d=0,\\
\frac{1}{2}a+\frac{3}{2}b+\frac{5}{4}c-\frac{3}{p+1}d=0.\end{cases}

Here, the second one is so-called the Pohozaev identity. We refer to
[
29
]
or Proposition
A.1
applied to the solution
(u_{1},0)
in Appendix.

By (
17
), we have

(19)

\displaystyle I^{\prime\prime}(u_{1},0)\Big[(t_{1}u_{1},t_{2}u_{1}),(t_{1}u_{1},t_{2}u_{1})\Big]

\displaystyle=t_{1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}+3\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}^{2}-p|u_{1}|^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle+t_{2}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}^{2}-\frac{p+1}{2}|u_{1}|^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle=t_{1}^{2}(I)+t_{2}^{2}(II).

Since
a+b+c-d=0
, we have

(20)

\displaystyle(II)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mu_{11}+\mu_{12})\phi_{u_{1}}u_{1}^{2}+\frac{p-1}{2}|u_{1}|^{p+1}dx<0.

On the other hand, by (
18
),
we have

a=\frac{1}{3}b+\frac{5p-7}{3(p+1)}d,\ \ c=-\frac{4}{3}b+\frac{2(5-p)}{3(p+1)}d

and

(I)=a+b+3c-pd=\frac{1}{3}\Big(-8b-\frac{3p^{2}+4p-23}{p+1}d\Big).

Thus,
\frac{3p^{2}+4p-23}{p+1}>0
if
p>\frac{1}{3}(-2+\sqrt{73})\cong 2.18133
, which implies that
(I)<0
if
p\geq\frac{1}{3}(-2+\sqrt{73})

and consequently the Morse index of
(u_{1},0)
is larger than or equal to 2.
By the symmetry, Morse index of the semi-trivial solution
(0,u_{2})
for (
7
) is also larger than or equal to two.
∎

3.2.
Energy comparison of semi-trivial solutions

In this subsection, we prove that a semi-trivial solution to (
7
) is not a ground state when
\mu_{11}=\mu_{22}
and
2<p<5
.
We accomplish this by comparing the energy of semi-trivial solutions with the energy of the solution of the form
(W,W)
, whose existence is guaranteed for the case
\mu_{11}=\mu_{22}
.
In fact, if
\mu_{11}=\mu_{22}
then
(W,W)
gives a solution of (
7
) for any solution
W
of the equation

-\Delta u+\lambda u+({\mu_{11}-\mu_{12}})\phi_{u}u=\frac{c_{p}}{2}|u|^{p-1}u,

where we define

(21)

\displaystyle c_{p}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|1+e^{i\theta}|^{p+1}d\theta=\frac{2^{\frac{p-1}{2}}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+\cos\theta)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}d\theta.

We shall show that the energy level of
(W,W)
is lower than that of semi-trivial solutions.

We first prepare some auxiliary lemmas.

Lemma 3.4
.

[
29
, Lemma 3.3]

Let
2<p<5
,
a,c>0
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
. Define
h:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

h(t)=at+bt^{3}-ct^{2p-1}.

Then
h
has a unique critical point, which corresponds to its maximum.

Let us denote

I_{\gamma}(u)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}\Big(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}\Big)+\frac{\gamma}{4}u^{2}\phi_{u}-\frac{1}{p+1}|u|^{p+1}dx.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
v
and
w
be positive ground state solutions of

-\Delta v+v+\gamma\phi_{v}v=|v|^{p-1}v\ \mbox{ and }\ -\Delta w+w+\mu\phi_{w}w=|w|^{p-1}w,

respectively, where
2<p<5
and
\gamma,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
with
\gamma>\mu.

Then we have

I_{\gamma}(v)>I_{\mu}(w).

Remark 6
.

The existence of a positive radial ground states for
I_{\gamma}
with
2<p<5
was proved in
[
29
]
when
\gamma>0
and in
[
33
]
when
\gamma<0
.

Proof.

In fact, we recall

I_{\gamma}(v)=\inf_{u\not\neq 0}\max_{t\geq 0}I_{\gamma}(u_{t})\mbox{ and }I_{\mu}(w)=\inf_{u\not\neq 0}\max_{t\geq 0}I_{\mu}(u_{t}),

where
u_{t}(x)\equiv t^{2}u(tx)
(see
[
4
, Lemma 2.4]
).
Combining the Nehari’s identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla v|^{2}+v^{2}+\gamma v^{2}\phi_{v}-|v|^{p+1}dx=0

and the Pohozaev’s identity (see
[
29
]
or Proposition
A.1
)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{3}{2}v^{2}+\frac{5\gamma}{4}v^{2}\phi_{v}-\frac{3}{p+1}v^{p+1}dx=0,

we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{3}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}+\frac{3\gamma}{4}v^{2}\phi_{v}-\frac{2p-1}{p+1}v^{p+1}dx=0.

From this and Lemma
3.4
, we see that
I_{\gamma}(v)=\max_{t\geq 0}I_{\gamma}(v_{t})
, and thus

\displaystyle I_{\gamma}(v)

\displaystyle=\max_{t\geq 0}I_{\gamma}(v_{t})=\max_{t\geq 0}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}\Big(t^{3}|\nabla v|^{2}+tv^{2}\Big)+\frac{\gamma}{4}t^{3}v^{2}\phi_{v}-\frac{t^{2p-1}}{p+1}|v|^{p+1}dx\bigg]

\displaystyle>\max_{t\geq 0}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}\Big(t^{3}|\nabla v|^{2}+tv^{2}\Big)+\frac{\mu}{4}t^{3}v^{2}\phi_{v}-\frac{t^{2p-1}}{p+1}|v|^{p+1}dx\bigg]\geq I_{\mu}(w).

∎

A direct computation shows that there holds the following scaling properties.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
v
be a solution of

-\Delta v+av+b\phi_{v}v=c|v|^{p-1}v,

where
1<p<5
and
a,b,c>0
,
which is a Euler-Lagrange equation of

I_{a,b,c}(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{a}{2}v^{2}+\frac{b}{4}\phi_{v}v^{2}-\frac{c}{p+1}|v|^{p+1}dx.

Then a rescaled function
\tilde{v}(x)=\Big(\frac{c}{a}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}v\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{a}}\Big)
solves

-\Delta\tilde{v}+\tilde{v}+\gamma(a,b,c)\phi_{\tilde{v}}\tilde{v}=|\tilde{v}|^{p-1}\tilde{v},

where
\gamma(a,b,c)=\frac{b}{a^{2}}\left(\frac{a}{c}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}

and satisfies

I_{\gamma(a,b,c)}(\tilde{v})=I_{1,\gamma(a,b,c),1}(\tilde{v})=\Big(\frac{c}{a}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}a^{\frac{1}{2}}I_{a,b,c}(v).

Now, we are ready to prove the main result of this subsection.

Proposition 3.7
.

Assume
2<p<5
,
\lambda>0,\mu_{ij}>0
,
\mu_{11}=\mu_{22}
, where
i,j=1,2
. Then we have

(22)

I(V,0)=I(0,V)>I(W,W),

where
W
and
V
are positive ground state solutions of

-\Delta u+\lambda u+({\mu_{11}-\mu_{12}})\phi_{u}u=\frac{c_{p}}{2}|u|^{p-1}u\ \mbox{ and }\ -\Delta u+\lambda u+\mu_{11}\phi_{u}u=|u|^{p-1}u,

respectively.

Proof.

Denote
\tilde{W}(x)=\Big(\frac{c_{p}}{2\lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}W\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\Big)
and
\tilde{V}(x)=\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}V\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\Big)
. Then, by Lemma
3.6
, we see that
\tilde{W}
and
\tilde{V}
are positive ground state solutions of

-\Delta u+u+\frac{2^{\frac{2}{p-1}}(\mu_{11}-\mu_{12})}{\lambda^{\frac{2p-4}{p-1}}c_{p}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}\phi_{u}u=|u|^{p-1}u\ \mbox{ and }\ -\Delta u+u+\frac{\mu_{11}}{\lambda^{\frac{2p-4}{p-1}}}\phi_{u}u=|u|^{p-1}u,

respectively. By Jensen’s inequality, we see that for
1<p<5
,

1=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+\cos\theta)d\theta\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}<\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+\cos\theta)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}d\theta.

which implies that
2^{\frac{p+1}{2}}<c_{p}
for
1<p<5
.
Note that this means
\Big(\frac{c_{p}}{2}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}>1
for
2<p<5
and thus we have
\mu_{11}>\frac{2^{\frac{2}{p-1}}(\mu_{11}-\mu_{12})}{c_{p}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}
.
Then, by Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}\Big(|\nabla\tilde{V}|^{2}+\tilde{V}^{2}\Big)+\frac{\mu_{11}}{4\lambda^{\frac{2p-4}{p-1}}}\tilde{V}^{2}\phi_{\tilde{V}}-\frac{1}{p+1}|\tilde{V}|^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-\frac{5-p}{2(p-1)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}\Big(|\nabla V|^{2}+\lambda V^{2}\Big)+\frac{\mu_{11}}{4}V^{2}\phi_{V}-\frac{1}{p+1}|V|^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-\frac{5-p}{2(p-1)}}I(V,0)

\displaystyle>\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}\Big(|\nabla\tilde{W}|^{2}+\tilde{W}^{2}\Big)+\frac{2^{\frac{2}{p-1}}(\mu_{11}-\mu_{12})}{4\lambda^{\frac{2p-4}{p-1}}c_{p}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}\phi_{\tilde{W}}\tilde{W}^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}|\tilde{W}|^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{p}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}{2^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\lambda^{\frac{5-p}{2(p-1)}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}\Big(|\nabla W|^{2}+\lambda W^{2}\Big)+\frac{\mu_{11}-\mu_{12}}{4}\phi_{W}W^{2}-\frac{c_{p}}{2(p+1)}|W|^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{p}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}{2^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\lambda^{\frac{5-p}{2(p-1)}}}\frac{1}{2}I(W,W).

Note that, since

2^{\frac{p+1}{2}}<c_{p}
for
2<p<5
, we get
\lambda^{-\frac{5-p}{2(p-1)}}<\frac{c_{p}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}{2^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\lambda^{\frac{5-p}{2(p-1)}}}.

From this,
we see that for
2<p<5
,

I(V,0)=I(0,V)>I(W,W).

∎

3.3.
Rigidity results for positive solutions

Analogously with Lemma
2.1
, we can show the coincidence of components for positive solutions to (
7
) with
\mu_{11}=\mu_{22}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Assume
1<p<5
,
\lambda>0
,
\mu_{ij}\geq 0
and
\mu_{11}=\mu_{22}
, where
i,j=1,2
. For any positive radial solutions
\vec{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
of (
7
), we have
u_{1}\equiv u_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\vec{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})\in{\bf H}
be a positive solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}+\lambda u_{1}+(\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{2}})u_{1}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)\Big(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}d\theta,\\
-\Delta u_{2}+\lambda u_{2}+(\mu_{22}\phi_{u_{2}}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}})u_{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(u_{2}+u_{1}\cos\theta)\Big(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}d\theta.\end{cases}

By (

h(t)\geq 0\ \ \mbox{ for }t\geq 0\ \ \mbox{ if }\ \ \lambda\geq 2(p-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2-p}}-2(p-1)^{\frac{1}{2-p}}=2(p-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2-p}}(2-p),

and for

(p-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2-p}}(2-p)<(p-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2-p}}\leq 1
.
From these, we can prove the claim, and thus,

, we see that
(u_{1},u_{2})=(0,0)
if
p\geq 5
.

On the other hand, we multiply the second equation (
70
) by
\frac{1}{3}
and subtract the calculated one from the first equation (
69
), we have

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{11}u_{1}^{2}\phi_{u_{1}}+\mu_{22}u_{2}^{2}\phi_{u_{2}}-2\mu_{12}u_{1}^{2}\phi_{u_{2}}dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\frac{2}{p+1}-1\Big)\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}d\theta dx.

From this, (
13
) and the facts that
\mu_{11}>0,\mu_{11}\mu_{22}-\mu_{12}^{2}\geq 0
, we see that
(u_{1},u_{2})=(0,0)
if
p\leq 1
.
\Box

Appendix B
Regularity of the action functional
I(\vec{u})

In this section, we study regularities for the action function
I
for (
7
). We first show (
7
) is the Euler-Lagrange equation of
I
.

Proposition B.1
.

The functional
I
is well-defined and continuously differentiable on
{\bf H}
.
In addition, any critical point of
I
is a weak solution of (
7
).

Proof.

Standard computation shows that
I
is
C^{1}
on
\mathbf{H}
and satisfies

(71)

\displaystyle I^{\prime}(\vec{u})\vec{\psi}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla\psi_{1}+\nabla u_{2}\cdot\nabla\psi_{2}+\lambda u_{1}\psi_{1}+\lambda u_{2}\psi_{2}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{11}u_{1}\phi_{u_{1}}\psi_{1}+\mu_{22}u_{2}\phi_{u_{2}}\psi_{2}-\mu_{12}u_{1}\phi_{u_{2}}\psi_{1}-\mu_{12}u_{2}\phi_{u_{1}}\psi_{2}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big((u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)\psi_{1}+(u_{2}+u_{1}\cos\theta)\psi_{2}\Big)\Big(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}d\theta dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla\psi_{1}+\nabla u_{2}\cdot\nabla\psi_{2}+\lambda u_{1}\psi_{1}+\lambda u_{2}\psi_{2}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{11}u_{1}\phi_{u_{1}}\psi_{1}+\mu_{22}u_{2}\phi_{u_{2}}\psi_{2}-\mu_{12}u_{1}\phi_{u_{2}}\psi_{1}-\mu_{12}u_{2}\phi_{u_{1}}\psi_{2}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|u_{1}+e^{i\theta}u_{2}|^{p-1}(u_{1}+e^{i\theta}u_{2})\psi_{1}+|u_{2}+e^{i\theta}u_{1}|^{p-1}(u_{2}+e^{i\theta}u_{1})\psi_{2}d\theta dx

which implies that any critical point of
I
is a weak solution of (
7
).
Here we used the fact that for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

(72)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}(a+be^{i\theta})|a+be^{i\theta}|^{p-1}d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}(a+b\cos\theta+ib\sin\theta)\Big((a+b\cos\theta)^{2}+(b\sin\theta)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}(a+b\cos\theta)\Big(a^{2}+2ab\cos\theta+b^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}d\theta.

∎

We note that a nontrivial critical point of
I
needs not to be nonnegative. Thus, in order to find a nonnegative solution of (
7
), we consider a truncated functional
I_{+}:{\bf H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

(73)

\displaystyle I_{+}(\vec{u})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}+\lambda u_{1}^{2}+\lambda u_{2}^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{11}(u_{1})_{+}^{2}\phi_{(u_{1})_{+}}+\mu_{22}(u_{2})_{+}^{2}\phi_{(u_{2})_{+}}-2\mu_{12}(u_{1})_{+}^{2}\phi_{(u_{2})_{+}}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p+1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big((u_{1})_{+}^{2}+2(u_{1})_{+}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta+(u_{2})_{+}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}d\theta dx,

where
u_{+}=\max\{u,0\}
.

Proposition B.2
.

The functional
I_{+}
is continuously differentiable on
\mathbf{H}
.
In addition, any critical point of
I_{+}
is a nonnegative weak solution of (
7
).

Proof.

Let us denote

A_{+}(\vec{u})=\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big((u_{1})_{+}^{2}+2(u_{1})_{+}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta+(u_{2})_{+}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}d\theta dx.

To prove that
A_{+}
is
C^{1}
, with derivative given by

(74)

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{+}(\vec{u})[\psi_{1},\psi_{2}]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big((u_{1})_{+}^{2}+2(u_{1})_{+}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta+(u_{2})_{+}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\Big((u_{1})_{+}+1_{\{u_{1}>0\}}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta\Big)\psi_{1}+\Big((u_{2})_{+}+1_{\{u_{2}>0\}}(u_{1})_{+}\cos\theta\Big)\psi_{2}\right)d\theta dx,

we will show that the map
\vec{u}\mapsto A^{\prime}_{+}(\vec{u})
is continuous.
We define

\alpha(t)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+t\cos\theta)(1+2t\cos\theta+t^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}d\theta.

Then since for large
t>0
,

\displaystyle\alpha(t)/t^{p-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+t\cos\theta)\Big(1+2t^{-1}\cos\theta+t^{-2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+t\cos\theta)\Big(1+\frac{p-1}{2}(2t^{-1}\cos\theta+t^{-2})+O(t^{-2})\Big)d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}1+(p-1)(\cos\theta)^{2}d\theta+O(t^{-1})=\pi(p+1)+O(t^{-1}),

we see that
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\alpha(t)=2\pi
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\alpha(t)/t^{p-1}=\pi(p+1)
.
Since
\alpha
is continuous, we see that

\alpha(t)\leq C(1+t^{p-1})\mbox{ for all }t\geq 0.

From this, we get

(75)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big((u_{1})_{+}^{2}+2(u_{1})_{+}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta+(u_{2})_{+}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left((u_{1})_{+}+1_{\{u_{1}>0\}}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=(u_{1})_{+}^{p}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big((1+2(u_{1})_{+}^{-1}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta+(u_{1})_{+}^{-2}(u_{2})_{+}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(1+(u_{1})_{+}^{-1}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta\right)d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C(u_{1})_{+}\left((u_{1})_{+}^{p-1}+(u_{2})_{+}^{p-1}\right).

From this, we see that

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big((u_{1})_{+}^{2}+2(u_{1})_{+}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta+(u_{2})_{+}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left((u_{1})_{+}+1_{\{u_{1}>0\}}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta\right)\bigg\|_{L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|(u_{1})_{+}\left((u_{1})_{+}^{p-1}+(u_{2})_{+}^{p-1}\right)\right\|_{L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}\leq C(\|u_{1}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p}+\|u_{2}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p}),

and hence if
(u_{1},u_{2})\rightarrow(v_{1},v_{2})
in
{\bf H}
, then for
\psi_{1}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big((u_{1})_{+}^{2}+2(u_{1})_{+}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta+(u_{2})_{+}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left((u_{1})_{+}+1_{\{u_{1}>0\}}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\Big((v_{1})_{+}^{2}+2(v_{1})_{+}(v_{2})_{+}\cos\theta+(v_{2})_{+}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left((v_{1})_{+}+1_{\{v_{1}>0\}}(v_{2})_{+}\cos\theta\right)d\theta\psi_{1}dx

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{1}\|_{L^{p+1}}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big((u_{1})_{+}^{2}+2(u_{1})_{+}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta+(u_{2})_{+}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left((u_{1})_{+}+1_{\{u_{1}>0\}}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\Big((v_{1})_{+}^{2}+2(v_{1})_{+}(v_{2})_{+}\cos\theta+(v_{2})_{+}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left((v_{1})_{+}+1_{\{v_{1}>0\}}(v_{2})_{+}\cos\theta\right)d\theta\bigg\|_{L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}\rightarrow 0.

This implies that the map
\vec{u}\mapsto A^{\prime}_{+}(\vec{u})
is continuous.

Then we see that
I_{+}
is
C^{1}
and

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{+}(\vec{u})[\psi_{1},\psi_{2}]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla\psi_{1}+\nabla u_{2}\cdot\nabla\psi_{2}+\lambda u_{1}\psi_{1}+\lambda u_{2}\psi_{2}dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{11}(u_{1})_{+}\phi_{(u_{1})_{+}}\psi_{1}+\mu_{22}(u_{2})_{+}\phi_{(u_{2})_{+}}\psi_{2}-\mu_{12}(u_{1})_{+}\phi_{(u_{2})_{+}}\psi_{1}-\mu_{12}(u_{2})_{+}\phi_{(u_{1})_{+}}\psi_{2}dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big((u_{1})_{+}^{2}+2(u_{1})_{+}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta+(u_{2})_{+}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\Big((u_{1})_{+}+1_{\{u_{1}>0\}}(u_{2})_{+}\cos\theta\Big)\psi_{1}+\Big((u_{2})_{+}+1_{\{u_{2}>0\}}(u_{1})_{+}\cos\theta\Big)\psi_{2}\right)d\theta dx.

Moreover, from a maximum principle, we see that any critical point of
I_{+}
is a non-negative solution of (
7
).
∎

We next show that
I
is actually
C^{2}
. This regularity is required to compute the Morse indices of critical points of
I
.

Proposition B.3
.

The functional
I
is twice continuously differentiable on
\mathbf{H}
.

Proof.

Let
\vec{\psi}=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
,

T(\vec{u})=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{11}u_{1}^{2}\phi_{u_{1}}+\mu_{22}u_{2}^{2}\phi_{u_{2}}-2\mu_{12}u_{1}^{2}\phi_{u_{2}}dx.

and

A(\vec{u})=\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}d\theta dx.

Note that
T
is
C^{2}
functional, and hence we have

T^{\prime}(\vec{u})\vec{\psi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{11}u_{1}\phi_{u_{1}}\psi_{1}+\mu_{22}u_{2}\phi_{u_{2}}\psi_{2}-\mu_{12}u_{1}\phi_{u_{2}}\psi_{1}-\mu_{12}u_{2}\phi_{u_{1}}\psi_{2}dx

and

(76)

\displaystyle T^{\prime\prime}(\vec{u})[\vec{\psi},\vec{\psi}]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}\psi_{1}^{2}+\mu_{11}u_{1}(\phi_{u_{1}}^{\prime}\psi_{1})\psi_{1}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{2}}\psi_{1}^{2}-\mu_{12}u_{2}(\phi_{u_{1}}^{\prime}\psi_{1})\psi_{2}

\displaystyle+\mu_{22}\phi_{u_{2}}\psi_{2}^{2}+\mu_{22}u_{2}(\phi_{u_{2}}^{\prime}\psi_{2})\psi_{2}-\mu_{12}u_{1}(\phi_{u_{2}}^{\prime}\psi_{2})\psi_{1}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}}\psi_{2}^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}\psi_{1}^{2}+\mu_{11}u_{1}(\phi_{u_{1}}^{\prime}\psi_{1})\psi_{1}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{2}}\psi_{1}^{2}-2\mu_{12}u_{2}(\phi_{u_{1}}^{\prime}\psi_{1})\psi_{2}

\displaystyle+\mu_{22}\phi_{u_{2}}\psi_{2}^{2}+\mu_{22}u_{2}(\phi_{u_{2}}^{\prime}\psi_{2})\psi_{2}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}}\psi_{2}^{2}dx.

Moreover, note that since

u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2}=(u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)^{2}+u_{2}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta=(u_{2}+u_{1}\cos\theta)^{2}+u_{1}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta,

we have

(77)

(u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)^{2}\Big(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2}\Big)^{-1}\leq 1

and

(u_{2}+u_{1}\cos\theta)(u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)\Big(u_{2}^{2}+2u_{2}u_{1}\cos\theta+u_{1}^{2}\Big)^{-1}\leq 1,

and hence we get

\left\|\int_{0}^{2\pi}(u_{2}+u_{1}\cos\theta)(u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)\Big(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-3}{2}}d\theta\right\|_{L^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}}\leq C(\|u_{1}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p-1}+\|u_{2}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p-1})

From this, we see that if
(u_{1},u_{2})\rightarrow(v_{1},v_{2})
in
{\bf H}
, then for
\psi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(u_{2}+u_{1}\cos\theta)(u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)\Big(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-3}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-(v_{2}+v_{1}\cos\theta)(v_{1}+v_{2}\cos\theta)\Big(v_{1}^{2}+2v_{1}v_{2}\cos\theta+v_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-3}{2}}d\theta\psi_{1}\varphi_{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{1}\|_{L^{p+1}}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{p+1}}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{2\pi}(u_{2}+u_{1}\cos\theta)(u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)\Big(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-3}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-(v_{2}+v_{1}\cos\theta)(v_{1}+v_{2}\cos\theta)\Big(v_{1}^{2}+2v_{1}v_{2}\cos\theta+v_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-3}{2}}d\theta\bigg\|_{L^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}}\rightarrow 0.

Thus we deduce that
A
is
C^{2}
, with derivatives given by

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(\vec{u})\vec{\psi}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\Big((u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)\psi_{1}+(u_{2}+u_{1}\cos\theta)\psi_{2}\Big)d\theta dx

and

(78)

\displaystyle A^{\prime\prime}(\vec{u})[\vec{\psi},\vec{\psi}]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bigg[\Big(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+(p-1)(u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)^{2}\Big(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-3}{2}}\bigg]\psi_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\bigg[\cos\theta\Big(u_{2}^{2}+2u_{2}u_{1}\cos\theta+u_{1}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

\displaystyle+(p-1)(u_{2}+u_{1}\cos\theta)(u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)\Big(u_{2}^{2}+2u_{2}u_{1}\cos\theta+u_{1}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-3}{2}}\bigg]\psi_{1}\psi_{2}

\displaystyle+\bigg[\Big(u_{2}^{2}+2u_{2}u_{1}\cos\theta+u_{1}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+(p-1)(u_{2}+u_{1}\cos\theta)^{2}\Big(u_{2}^{2}+2u_{2}u_{1}\cos\theta+u_{1}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-3}{2}}\bigg]\psi_{2}^{2}d\theta dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bigg\{\Big[1+(p-1)(u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)^{2}(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2})^{-1}\Big]\psi_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\Big[\cos\theta+(p-1)(u_{2}+u_{1}\cos\theta)(u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)(u_{2}^{2}+2u_{2}u_{1}\cos\theta+u_{1}^{2})^{-1}\Big]\psi_{1}\psi_{2}

\displaystyle+\Big[1+(p-1)(u_{2}+u_{1}\cos\theta)^{2}(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2})^{-1}\Big]\psi_{2}^{2}\bigg\}\Big(u_{2}^{2}+2u_{2}u_{1}\cos\theta+u_{1}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}d\theta dx.

Thus, from (
76
) and (
78
), we have

(79)

\displaystyle I^{\prime\prime}(\vec{u})[\vec{\psi},\vec{\psi}]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\psi_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla\psi_{2}|^{2}+\lambda\psi_{1}^{2}+\lambda\psi_{2}^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{11}\phi_{u_{1}}\psi_{1}^{2}+\mu_{11}u_{1}(\phi_{u_{1}}^{\prime}\psi_{1})\psi_{1}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{2}}\psi_{1}^{2}-2\mu_{12}u_{2}(\phi_{u_{1}}^{\prime}\psi_{1})\psi_{2}

\displaystyle+\mu_{22}\phi_{u_{2}}\psi_{2}^{2}+\mu_{22}u_{2}(\phi_{u_{2}}^{\prime}\psi_{2})\psi_{2}-\mu_{12}\phi_{u_{1}}\psi_{2}^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bigg\{\Big[1+(p-1)(u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)^{2}(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2})^{-1}\Big]\psi_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\Big[\cos\theta+(p-1)(u_{2}+u_{1}\cos\theta)(u_{1}+u_{2}\cos\theta)(u_{2}^{2}+2u_{2}u_{1}\cos\theta+u_{1}^{2})^{-1}\Big]\psi_{1}\psi_{2}

\displaystyle+\Big[1+(p-1)(u_{2}+u_{1}\cos\theta)^{2}(u_{1}^{2}+2u_{1}u_{2}\cos\theta+u_{2}^{2})^{-1}\Big]\psi_{2}^{2}\bigg\}\Big(u_{2}^{2}+2u_{2}u_{1}\cos\theta+u_{1}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}d\theta dx.

∎

Grid method for divergence of averages
Sovanlal Mondal Department of Mathematical Sciences, University of Memphis Memphis, TN 38152, USA email smondal@memphis.edu

Abstract
In this paper, we will introduce the ‘grid method’ to prove that the extreme case of oscillation occurs for the averages obtained by sampling a flow along the sequence of times of the form { n α : n ∈ ℕ } \{n^{\alpha}:n\in\mathds{N}\} , where α \alpha is a positive non-integer rational number . Such behavior of a sequence is known as the ‘strong sweeping out’ property. By using the same method, we will give an example of a general class of sequences which satisfy the strong sweeping out property. This class of sequences may be useful to solve the longstanding open problem: for a given irrational α \alpha , whether the sequence ( n α ) (n^{\alpha}) is bad for pointwise ergodic theorem in L 2 L^{2} or not. In the process of proving this result, we will also prove a continuous version of the Conze principle.



Key words: aperiodic flow, universally bad sequences, strong sweeping out property, pointwise ergodic theorem, Conze’s principle.

Introduction and main results

Let
(T^{t})_{t\in\mathds{R}}
be a measure preserving flow on a Lebesgue probability space

(X,\Sigma,\mu)
. Birkhoff’s pointwise ergodic theorem asserts that for any function
f\in L^{1}(X)
, the Cesàro averages
\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\in[N]}f(T^{n}x)
converge for almost every
x
, where
[N]=\{1,2,\dots,N\}
. This classical result motivated others to study the ergodic averages along a sequence of positive real numbers
(s_{n})
i.e.
\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\in[N]}f(T^{s_{n}}x)
.

Definition 1

(label=defn:1)
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. A sequence
(s_{n})
of positive real numbers is said to be

pointwise good
for
L^{p}
if for
every system

(X,\Sigma,\mu,T^{t})
and every
f\in L^{p}(X)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\in[N]}f(T^{s_{n}}x)
exists for almost every
x\in X.

Definition 2
.

A sequence
(s_{n})
of positive real numbers is said to be

pointwise bad
in
L^{p}
if for every aperiodic system

(X,\Sigma,\mu,T^{t})
, there is an element
f\in L^{p}(X)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{n\in[N]}f(T^{s_{n}}x)
does not exist for almost every
x\in X.

The behavior of ergodic averages along a subsequence has a rich history. First breakthrough result in this direction was due to Krengel
[
Kre71
]
who showed that there exists a sequence of positive integers which is pointwise bad for
L^{\infty}
. A few years later, Bellow
[
Bel83
]
proved that any lacunary sequence, for example
(s_{n})=(2^{n})
, is pointwise bad for
L^{p}
when
p\in[1,\infty)
. On the other extreme, if a sequence grows slower than any positive power of
n
, for example
(s_{n})=\big((\log n)^{c}\big)
,
c>0
, then it is also pointwise bad for
L^{\infty}

[
JW94
, Theorem 2.16]
. In
[
Bel89
]
and
[
Rei94
]
, it was proved that whether a sequence will be pointwise good for
L^{p}
or not also depends on the value of
p
. More precisely, they showed that for any given
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
, there are sequences
(s_{n})
which are pointwise good for
L^{q}
but pointwise bad for
L^{p}
(see also
[
Par11
]
for finer results in terms of Orlicz spaces). There are many instances where the behavior of the averages cannot be determined by either the growth rate of the sequence
(s_{n})
or the value of
p
; instead one has to analyze the intrinsic arithmetic properties of the sequence
(s_{n})
. One such curious example is
(n^{\alpha})
.
A celebrated result of Bourgain
[
Bou88
, Theorem 2]
says that the sequence
(n^{\alpha})
is pointwise good for
L^{2}
when
\alpha
is a positive integer, on the other hand
\lfloor(n^{\alpha}+\log n)\rfloor
is known to be pointwise bad for
L^{2}
when
\alpha
is a
positive integer

[
Bos+05
, Theorem C]
. Later, in a series of papers, it was established that for any polynomial
P(x)
, the sequence
P(n)
, and the sequence of primes are pointwise good for
L^{p}
, when
p>1

[
Bou89
]
,
[
Wie88
]
. However, they are pointwise bad for
L^{1}

[
LaV11
]
,
[
BM10
]
. Thus, the
L^{1}
case turned out to be more subtle than the others. It was largely believed that there cannot be any sequence
(s_{n})
which is pointwise good for
L^{1}
and satisfies
(s_{n+1}-s_{n})\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Buczolich
[
Buc07
]
constructed inductively a sophisticated example to disprove this conjecture. Later, it was shown in
[
UZ07
]
that
\lfloor n^{c}\rfloor,c\in(1,1.001)
is pointwise good for
L^{1}
. The current best result is due to Mirek who showed that
\lfloor n^{c}\rfloor,c\in(1,\frac{30}{29})
is pointwise good for
L^{1}

[
Mir15
]
, see also
[
Tro21
]
. It would be interesting to know if the above result can be extended to all
positive non integer
c
. For further exposition in this area, the reader is referred to the survey article of
[
RW95
]
.

In this paper we will be concerned with the behavior of ergodic averages along
(n^{\alpha})
where
\alpha
is a
positive non-integer rational number
. In a strong contrast with
[
Bou88
, Theorem 2]
, Bergelson, Boshernitzan and Bourgain proved in
[
BBB94
]
that
for any
non integer rational
\alpha
,
(n^{\alpha})
is pointwise bad for
L^{\infty}
. Their proof is based on Bourgain’s entropy method. Later, the result was
improved and the proof was simplified in
[
JW94
, Example 2.8]
: it was proved
that if the averages are taken along
(n^{\frac{a}{b}})
, where

a,b\in\mathds{N}
and
b\geq 2
, then for any given
\epsilon>0
, there
exists a set
E\in\Sigma
such that
\mu(E)<\epsilon
and for almost every

x
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E}(T^{s_{n}}x)\geq\delta
. The constant
\delta

depends on
b
and is explicitly given by
\frac{1}{\zeta(b)}
, where

\zeta(.)
is the Riemann zeta function. For all
b\geq 2
,
\delta
lies
in
(0,1).
In this paper, we
will prove a general result, Proposition
, as a consequence of which
we will prove the following theorems:

Theorem 3

(label=thm:5)
.

Let
\alpha
be a fixed
non-integer rational number. Then for every aperiodic dynamical
system
(X,\Sigma,\mu,T^{t})
and every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
set
E\in\Sigma
such that
\mu(E)<\epsilon
, and

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E}(T^{n^{\alpha}}x)=1
a.e. and

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E}(T^{n^{\alpha}}x)=0
a.e.

This result tells us that an extreme case of oscillation occurs for the averages along the sequence
(n^{\alpha})
, when

\alpha
is a non-integer rational
.

It is interesting to compare this result with
[
Bos+05
, Theorem B]
which says that
\lfloor{n^{\frac{3}{2}}\rfloor}
is pointwise good for
L^{2}
. In the same paper, one can find various interesting results about the behavior of averages along sequences which are modeled on functions from the Hardy field.

In our next theorem, we will give an example of a more general class of
sequences which exhibit similar behavior.

Theorem 4

(label=thm:4)
.

Fix a positive integer
l
. Let
\alpha_{i}=\frac{a_{i}}{b_{i}}
, for

i\in[l]
, be non-integer rational numbers with
gcd
(b_{i},b_{j})=1
for
i\neq j
. Let
S=(s_{n})
be the sequence obtained
by rearranging the elements of the set

\{n_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}n_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\dots n_{l}^{\alpha_{l}}:n_{i}\in\mathds{N}\text{ for all }i\in[l]\}
in an increasing order. Then for every aperiodic dynamical
system
(X,\Sigma,\mu,T^{t})
and every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
set
E\in\Sigma
such that
\mu(E)<\epsilon
and

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E}(T^{s_{n}}x)=1
a.e. and

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E}(T^{s_{n}}x)=0
a.e.

Theorem

is a special case of Theorem
. The sequences considered in Theorem
indeed form a much larger class than the ones considered in
Theorem
. For example, Theorem
does not apply to the sequence

(m^{\frac{1}{3}}n^{\frac{2}{5}}:m,n\in\mathds{N})
, but
Theorem
does.

In Theorem
we will prove the Conze principle for flows.

2
Preliminaries

Let
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
be a
Lebesgue probability space. That means
\mu
is a countably additive
complete positive measure on
\Sigma
with
\mu(X)=1
, with the
further property that the measure space is measure theoretically
isomorphic to a measurable subset of the unit interval with Lebesgue
measure. By a flow
\{T^{t}:t\in\mathds{R}\}
we mean a group of
measurable transformations
T^{t}:X\rightarrow X
with
T^{0}(x)=x
,

T^{t+s}=T^{t}\circ T^{s}
,
s,t\in\mathds{R}
. The flow is called
measurable if the map
(x,t)\rightarrow T^{t}(x)
from
X\times\mathds{R}

into
X
is measurable with respect to the completion of the product
of
\mu
, the measure on
X
, and the Lebesgue measure on
\mathds{R}
. The
flow will be called
measure preserving
if it is measurable and each

T^{t}
satisfies
\mu({T^{t}}^{-1}A)=\mu(A)
for all

A\in\Sigma
. The
quadruple
(X,\Sigma,\mu,T^{t})
will be called a dynamical system.
A measurable flow will be called
aperiodic
(free) if

\mu\{x|T^{t}(x)=T^{s}(x)\text{ for some }t\neq s\}=0.
If
the flow is aperiodic, then the quadruple will be called an aperiodic
system.

We will use the following notation to denote the averages.

\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}f(T^{s_{n}}x)=\frac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{n\in[N]}f(T^{s_{n}}x).

More generally, for a subset
J\subset\mathds{N}
,

\mathds{A}_{n\in J}f(T^{s_{n}}x):=\frac{1}{\#J}\displaystyle\sum_{n\in J}f(T^{s_{n}}x).

Before we go to the proof of our main results, let us give the following definitions.

Definition 5

(label=defn:2)
.

Let
0<\delta\leq 1.
We say that a sequence
(s_{n})
is

\delta
-sweeping out
if in every aperiodic system
(X,\Sigma,\mu,T^{t})
, for a given
\epsilon>0
, there is a set
E\in\Sigma

with
\mu(E)<\epsilon
such that
\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E}(T^{s_{n}}x)\geq\delta
for almost every
x\in X
. If
\delta=1
, then
(s_{n})
is
said to be
strong sweeping out
.

•

The
relative upper density
of a subsequence
B=(b_{n})
in

A=(a_{n})
is defined by

\overline{{d}}_{A}(B):=\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{\#\{a_{n}\in B:n\in[N]\}}{N}
.

•

Similarly,
the
relative lower density
of a subsequence
B=(b_{n})
in

A=(a_{n})
is defined by

\underline{{d}}_{A}(B):=\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{\#\{a_{n}\in B:n\in[N]\}}{N}
.

If both
the limits are equal, we just say
relative density
and denote
it by
d_{A}(B).

Remark
.

[(a)]

1.

If a sequence
(s_{n})
is
\delta
-sweeping out, then by
Fatou’s lemma, it is pointwise bad for
L^{p}
,

p\in[1,\infty].
If a sequence
(s_{n})
contains a subsequence

(b_{n})
of relative density
\delta>0
which is strong sweeping out, then
(s_{n})
is
\delta
-sweeping out, and hence pointwise bad for
L^{p}
,

p\in[1,\infty].

2.

If
(s_{n})
is strong sweeping out, then by

[
JR79
, Theorem 1.3]
, there exists a residual subset
\Sigma_{1}
of
\Sigma
in the symmetric pseudo-metric with the property that for all
E\in\Sigma_{1}
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E}(T^{s_{n}}x)=1\text{ a.e.
and }\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E}(T^{s_{n}}x)=0\text{ a.e.}

(2.1)

This, in particular, implies that for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a set
E\in\Sigma
such that
\mu(E)<\epsilon
and
E
satisfies (
2.1
).

3
Proof of the main results

The proof of Theorem

and Theorem
has two main ideas, one is what we call here the grid method, and which has its origins in
[
Jon04
]
, the other one is partitioning a given sequence of real numbers into linearly independent pieces.
Our plan is to prove the theorems as an application of Proposition
. One of the main
ingredients for the proof of Proposition
is the following theorem:

Theorem 6

(label=thm:conze)
.

Let
(s_{n})
be a sequence of
positive real numbers. Suppose that for any given
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

P_{0}>0
and a finite constant
C
, there exist
P>P_{0}
and a dynamical
system
(\tilde{X},\beta,\m,U^{t})
with a set
\tilde{E}\in\beta
such that

\m\Big(x|\max_{P_{0}\leq N\leq P}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{\tilde{E}}(U^{s_{n}}x)\geq 1-\epsilon\Big)>C\m(\tilde{E}).

(3.1)

Then
(s_{n})
is strong sweeping out.

Proof 3.1
.

This will follow from
[
Akc+96
, Theorem 2.2]

and Proposition
below.

Let
\lambda
denote the Lebesgue measure on
\mathds{R}
.

•

For a Lebesgue measurable subset
B
of
\mathds{R}
, we define the

upper density
of
B
by

\overline{d}(B)=\displaystyle\limsup_{J\to\infty}\frac{1}{J}\lambda\big(B\cap[0,J]\big).

(3.2)

•

Similarly, for a locally integrable function
\phi
on
\mathds{R}
, we define the
upper
density
of the function as follows:

\overline{d}(\phi)=\displaystyle\limsup_{J\to\infty}\frac{1}{J}\int_{[0,J]}\phi(t)dt.

(3.3)

Proposition 7

(label=prop:Calderon1)
.

Let
(s_{n})
be a sequence of positive real
numbers. Suppose there exists
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,
P_{0}>0
and a finite
constant
C
such that for all
P
and Lebesgue measurable set
E\subset\mathds{R}

\overline{d}\Big(t\in\mathds{R}|\max_{P_{0}\leq N\leq P}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E}(s_{n}+t)\geq 1-\epsilon\Big)\leq C\overline{d}(E).

(3.4)

Then for any dynamical system

(\tilde{X},{\beta},\m,U^{t})
, and for any
\tilde{E}\in\beta
we
have

\mathfrak{m}\Big(x|\max_{P_{0}\leq N\leq P}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{\tilde{E}}(U^{s_{n}}x)\geq 1-\epsilon\Big)\leq C\m(\tilde{E}).

(3.5)

We will use here a similar argument as Calderon’s transference principle
[
Cal68
]
.

Proof 3.2
.

Let
P>P_{0}
be an arbitrary integer. Choose
\eta>0
small. It will be sufficient to show that for any
\tilde{E}\in\beta
we
have

\mathfrak{m}\Big(x|\max_{P_{0}\leq N\leq P}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{\tilde{E}}(U^{s_{n}}x)\geq 1-\epsilon\Big)\leq(C+\eta)\m(\tilde{E}).

(3.6)

To prove (
3.6
), we need the following lemma:

Lemma 8
.

Under the hypothesis of Proposition
, there exists
J_{0}=J_{0}(\eta)\in\mathds{N}
such that for every Lebesgue measurable set
E\subset\mathds{R}
the following holds:

\lambda\Big(t\leq J_{0}|\max_{P_{0}\leq N\leq P}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E}(s_{n}+t)\geq 1-\epsilon\Big)\leq(C+\eta)\lambda(E\cap[0,J_{0}]).

(3.7)

Proof 3.3
.

On the contrary, let us assume that the conclusion is not true. Then for every integer
J_{k}
, there exists a Lebesgue measurable set
E_{k}\subset\mathds{R}
such that

\lambda\Big(t\leq J_{k}|\max_{P_{0}\leq N\leq P}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E_{k}}(s_{n}+t)\geq 1-\epsilon\Big)>(C+\eta)\lambda(E_{k}\cap[0,J_{k}]).

(3.8)

Fix such a
J_{k}
and
E_{k}.
Observe that the set
E_{k}^{\prime}:=E_{k}\cap[0,J_{k}+S_{p}]
also satisfies
3.8
. Then for any
J>J_{k}+S_{p}
, we have

\lambda\Big(t\leq J|\max_{P_{0}\leq N\leq P}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E_{k}^{\prime}}(s_{n}+t)\geq 1-\epsilon\Big)>(C+\eta)\lambda(E_{k}^{\prime}\cap[0,J]).

(3.9)

Letting
J\to\infty
, we get

\overline{d}\Big(t\in\mathds{R}:\max_{P_{0}\leq N\leq P}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E_{k}^{\prime}}(s_{n}+t)\geq 1-\epsilon\Big)>(C+\eta)\overline{d}(E_{k}^{\prime}).

(3.10)

But this is a contradiction to our hypothesis (
3.4
). This finishes the proof of our lemma.

Now we will establish (
3.6
).
Let
\tilde{E}
be an arbitrary element of
{\beta}
.
For a fixed element
x\in\tilde{X}
, define
\mathds{1}_{{E_{x}}}
by

\mathds{1}_{{E_{x}}}(t):=\mathds{1}_{\tilde{E}}{(U^{t}x)}

(3.11)

Applying (
3.7
)
on the set
E_{x}
we get

\lambda\Big(t\leq J_{0}|\max_{P_{0}\leq N\leq P}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E_{x}}(s_{n}+t)\geq 1-\epsilon\Big)\leq(C+\eta)\lambda(E_{x}\cap[0,J_{0}]).

(3.12)

By definition of the sets
E_{x}
and
\tilde{E}
, we get

\max_{P_{0}\leq N\leq P}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{\tilde{E}}(U^{s_{n}+t}x)=\max_{P_{0}\leq N\leq P}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{E_{x}}({s_{n}}+t),\text{ for all }t.

Substituting the
above equality in (
3.12
), we get

\lambda\Big(t|\ t\leq J_{0},\ \max_{P_{0}\leq N\leq P}\displaystyle\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{\tilde{E}}(U^{s_{n}+t}x)\geq 1-\epsilon\Big)\leq(C+\eta)\lambda(E_{x}\cap[0,J_{0}]).

(3.13)

Introducing the set

B:=\Big\{y:\max_{P_{0}\leq N\leq P}\displaystyle\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\mathds{1}_{\tilde{E}}(U^{s_{n}}y)\geq 1-\epsilon\Big\}

(3.14)

we can rewrite equation (
3.13
) as

\displaystyle\lambda\big(t:t\leq J_{0}\text{ and }\mathds{1}_{B}(U^{t}x)=1\big)\leq(C+\eta)\lambda(E_{x}\cap[0,J_{0}])

which implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}}\mathds{1}_{\{t\leq J_{0}\}}(t)\mathds{1}_{B}(U^{t}x)dt\leq(C+\eta)\int_{[0,J_{0}]}\mathds{1}_{E_{x}}(t)dt

Integrating the above equation with respect to the
x
variable, using
(
3.11
) in the right hand side and applying Fubini’s
theorem we get

\int_{\mathds{R}}\int_{\tilde{X}}\mathds{1}_{B}(U^{t}x)d\m(x)\mathds{1}_{\{t\leq J_{0}\}}(t)dt\leq(C+\eta)\int_{[0,J_{0}]}\int_{\tilde{X}}\mathds{1}_{\tilde{E}}(U^{t}x)d\m(x)dt

(3.15)

By using the fact that
U^{t}
is measure preserving we
get

\m(B)J_{0}\leq(C+\eta)\m(\tilde{E})J_{0}.

(3.16)

This gives us that

\m(B)\leq(C+\eta)\m(\tilde{E}),

(3.17)

finishing the proof of the desired maximal inequality
(
3.6
). This completes the proof.

One can generalize the above proposition as follows:

Proposition 9

(label=prop:Calderon2)
.

Let
(s_{n})
be a sequence of
positive real numbers. Suppose there exists a finite constant
C
such
that for all locally integrable functions
\phi
and
\gamma>0
we have

\overline{d}\Big(t|\sup_{N}\displaystyle\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\phi(s_{n}+t)\geq\gamma\Big)\leq\frac{C}{\gamma}\overline{d}(|\phi|).

(3.18)

Then for any dynamical system

(\tilde{X},{\beta},\m,U^{t})
, and for any
\tilde{f}\in L^{1}
we have

\m\Big(x|\sup_{N}\displaystyle\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}{\tilde{f}}(U^{s_{n}}x)\geq\gamma\Big)\leq\frac{C}{\gamma}\int_{\tilde{X}}|\tilde{f}|d\m.

(3.19)

The proof of this proposition is very similar to the proof
of Proposition
, and hence we will skip the proof.

The underlying principle of Theorem
is the Conze principle
which has been widely used to prove many results related to the Birkhoff’s pointwise ergodic theorem. Originally, the theorem was proved for a single
transformation system by Conze
[
Con73
]
. Here we will prove a version
of the theorem for flows. The theorem is not required for proving
Proposition
, but it
is of independent interest.

Theorem 10

(label=thm:conze1)
.

Conze principle for flows Let
S=(s_{n})
be a sequence of positive real numbers and

(X,\Sigma,\mu,T^{t})
be an aperiodic flow which satisfies the following maximal
inequality:

There exist a finite constant
C
such that for all
{f}\in L^{1}(X)

and
\gamma>0
we have

\mu\Big(x|\sup_{N}\displaystyle\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}{{f}}(T^{s_{n}}x)\geq\gamma\Big)\leq\frac{C}{\gamma}\int_{X}|f|d\mu.

(3.20)

Then the above maximal inequality holds in every dynamical system with the same constant
C
.

For a Lebesgue measurable subset
A
of
\mathds{R}
, and
F\in\Sigma
, we
will use the notation
T^{A}(F)
to denote the set
\cup_{t\in A}T^{t}(F)
. We will say that
T^{A}(F)
is disjoint if we have

T^{t}(F)\cap T^{s}(F)=\emptyset
for all
t\neq s.

Proof 3.4
.

We want to show that the maximal inequality
(
3.20
) transfers to
\mathds{R}
. If we can show that, then
Proposition
will finish the proof.

Let
\phi
be a
measurable function. After a standard reduction, we can assume that

\gamma=1
and
\phi
is positive. It will be sufficient to show that
for any fixed
P
we have

\overline{d}\Big(t|\max_{N\leq P}\displaystyle\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\phi(s_{n}+t)\geq 1\Big)\leq C\overline{d}(\phi).

(3.21)

Define

G:=\Big\{t:\max_{N\leq P}\mathds{A}_{n\in[N]}\phi({s_{n}}+t)>1\Big\}

(3.22)

Let
(L_{n})
be a sequence such that

\overline{d}(G)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{L_{n}-s_{P}}\lambda\big([0,L_{n}-s_{P}]\cap G\big)

(3.23)

Fix
L_{n}
large enough so that it is bigger than
s_{P}
.
Then by applying the
\mathds{R}
-action version of Rohlin tower
[
Lin75
, Theorem 1]
, we can find a set

Tracer particles coupled to an interacting boson gas
Esteban Cárdenas

Abstract
. In this work, we investigate the mean-field limit of a model consisting of m ⩾ 1 m\geqslant 1 tracer particles, coupled to an interacting boson field. We assume the mass of the tracer particles and the expected number of bosons to be of the same order of magnitude N ⩾ 1 N\geqslant 1 and we investigate the N → ∞ N\rightarrow\infty limit. In particular, we show that the limiting system can be effectively described by a pair of variables ( X t , φ t ) ∈ ℝ 3 ​ m × H 1 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) (\textbf{X}_{t},\varphi_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{3m}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) that solve a mean-field equation. Our methods are based on proving estimates for the number of bosonic particles in a suitable fluctuation state Ω N , t \Omega_{N,t} . The main diffculty of the problem comes from the fact that the interaction with the tracer particles can create or destroy bosons for states close to the vacuum.



1.
Introduction

In this article, we consider
m\geqslant 1
heavy quantum particles (
tracer particles
) that
are coupled to a weakly interacting gas of non-relativistic scalar bosons. We are interested in the regime for which the average number of bosons and the mass of the tracer particles are, in appropiate physical units, large and of the same order of magnitude
N
. The microscopic states of this system are described by wave functions
that belong to the Hilbert space

\mathscr{H}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=L^{2}_{\bm{X}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{b}

(1.1)

where
L^{2}_{\bm{X}}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3m},\mathrm{d}{\bm{X}})
is the state space
for the tracer particles, with positions labeled by
{\bm{X}}=(X^{(1)},\ldots,X^{(m)})\in\mathbb{R}^{3m}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{b}
is the spinless bosonic Fock space

\textstyle\mathcal{F}_{b}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 1}\mathcal{F}_{n}\qquad\textnormal{where}\qquad\mathcal{F}_{n}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\,L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathrm{d}x)^{\otimes_{\mathrm{sym}}n}\,.

(1.2)

We will denote by
\Omega\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=(1,0,\ldots)
the
vacuum
vector in
\mathcal{F}_{b}
and make extensive use of the operator-valued distributions
a_{x}
and
a_{x}^{*}
satisfying
the canonical commutation relations (CCR)

[a_{x},a_{y}^{*}]=\delta(x-y)\,,\qquad[a_{x},a_{y}]=[a_{x}^{*},a_{y}^{*}]=0\,,\,\,\qquad\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,.

(1.3)

The reader is refered to
[
33
, Section 2]
for a nice account on creation and annihilation operators, and to
[
32
, Section X.7]
for a thorough mathematical treatment.

If
\Psi_{N,t}\in\mathscr{H}
denotes the wave function of the above system at time
t\geqslant 0
, the main goal of the present article is to derive an effective description of this object,
in the large
N
limit. From a heuristic point of view, one expects that as the mass of the tracer particles gets larger,
classical behaviour
should be dominant. On the other hand, for low enough temperatures, one expects a boson gas to experience
condensation
–most of the particles will be in the same quantum state. This leads us to introduce a pair of interacting mean-field variables

t\in\mathbb{R}\longmapsto({\bm{X}}_{t},\varphi_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{3m}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

(1.4)

where
{\bm{X}}_{t}
describes the classical trajectories followed by the tracer particles and
\varphi_{t}
is a self-interacting wave function describing the spatial probability distribution of the condensate.

1.1.
The Hamiltonian

Throughout this article, we assume that the boson-tracer and boson-boson interactions
are
mediated
by even, real-valued functions

w:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and

v:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
We will always assume enough regularity so that the

(n+m)
-particle Hamiltonian

H_{N,n}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=-\frac{\Delta_{{\bm{X}}}}{2N}\otimes\mathds{1}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathds{1}\otimes{\Delta_{x_{i}}}+\mathds{1}\otimes\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}^{n}v(x_{i}-x_{j})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}w(x_{i}-X^{(\ell)})\,,\quad n\geqslant 1

(1.5)

is self-adjoint in its natural domain, with the obvious modification for
n=0
.
The dynamics of our combined system will be then described by the second-quantized Hamiltonian in
\mathscr{H}

\mathcal{H}_{N}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=-\frac{\Delta_{{\bm{X}}}}{2N}\otimes\mathds{1}+\mathds{1}\otimes (1.6)

equivalently defined as the direct sum
\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}H_{N,n}
. Here and in the sequel,

denotes the kinetic energy of the bosons

\textstyle T_{b}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\,\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}a_{x}^{*}(-\Delta_{x})a_{x}\mathrm{d}x=(0)\oplus\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(-\Delta_{x_{i}}\big)

(1.7)

and we will be using the following short-hand notation for the total boson-tracer interaction

\textstyle\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}})\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}w(x-X^{(\ell)})\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,,\ {\bm{X}}=(X^{(1)},\ldots,X^{(m)})\in\mathbb{R}^{3m}\,.

The Spectral Theorem then gives meaning to the evolution associated to the Schrödinger equation–from now on refered to as the
microscopic dynamics
of the system–

\begin{cases}&\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}\Psi_{N,t}=\mathcal{H}_{N}\Psi_{N,t}\\
&\Psi_{N,t}\big|_{t=0}=\Psi_{N,0}\end{cases}

(1.9)

by means of exponentiation
\Psi_{N,t}=\exp(-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{N})\Psi_{N,0}
. A few comments are in order.

Remark 1.1
.

Since the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{N}
is diagonal with respect to Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{b}
, it commutes with the particle number operator

\textstyle N_{b}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}a_{x}^{*}a_{x}\mathrm{d}x=\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}n\,.

(1.10)

Therefore, if
\Psi_{N,0}
contains in quantum-mechanical average
N
bosons, so will
\Psi_{N,t}
for all later times. In other words, it holds that

\left\langle\Psi_{N,t},\mathds{1}\otimes N_{b}\Psi_{N,t}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}=\left\langle\Psi_{N,0},\mathds{1}\otimes N_{b}\Psi_{N,0}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}=N\,,\qquad\forall t\geqslant 0\,.

(1.11)

The initial data that we work with will always satisfy this condition.

Remark 1.2
.

Equation (
1.9
) is equivalent to an
infinite
system of equations: one for each component of
\Psi_{N,t}
in its direct sum decomposition. Thus, an effective description of the microscopic dynamics in terms of the
(3m+1)
mean-field variables drastically reduces the number of unknowns one should solve for. Describing such an approximation is the content of the present paper.

Remark 1.3
.

On a formal level, each term present in the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{N}
is of order
N
. Indeed, for the tracer particles, we expect
position
and
velocities
to be of order
1
, so that their kinetic energy is of order
N
due to their heavy mass. For the boson field, we expect its kinetic term to be of order
N
since there should be in average
N
of them, each of which has order
1
kinetic energy. Similary, the interaction terms are scaled so that they remain of the same order of magnitude in
N
; one can directly count the powers on creation and annhilation operators present in each term. This is the so-called weak coupling scaling, or mean-field regime.

Remark 1.4
.

If the potentials
v
and
w
satisfy Condition
2
–stated below–it is well-known that the finite particle Hamiltonian
H_{N,n}
is self-adjoint in

H^{2}_{\bm{X}}\otimes H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{\otimes_{sym}n}
.
Further, for smooth
\Phi\in\mathcal{F}_{b}
with finitely many non-zero entries, one may verify that the boson-boson interaction satisfies the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\big\|\,\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}}$}}v(x-y)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{x}a_{y}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\Phi\big\|_{\mathcal{F}_{b}}\,\lesssim\,\|T_{b}\Phi\|_{\mathcal{F}_{b}}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\|(\mathds{1}+N_{b})^{3}\Phi\|_{\mathcal{F}_{b}}\,.

(1.12)

Similarly, the boson-tracer interaction is controlled by
\mathds{1}\otimes N_{b}
.
Consequently, we have the following characterization for the domain of
\mathcal{H}_{N}
:

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{N})\cap\mathcal{D}(\mathds{1}\otimes N_{b}^{3})=H_{\bm{X}}^{2}\otimes\mathcal{D}(T_{b})\cap\mathcal{D}(\mathds{1}\otimes N_{b}^{3})\,,\qquad\forall N\geqslant 1\,.

(1.13)

1.2.
The Mean-Field Equations

Let us now introduce the equations satisfied by the mean-field variables.
Heuristically, the classical trajectory of a tracer particle should obey Newton’s equation, with a time-dependent force depending on the location of the bosons. Similarly, the quantum boson condensate should feel a time-dependent potential, related to the positions of the tracer particles, plus its usual self-interactions as is well-known from the realm of Hartree’s equation. Thus, we introduce the following system of equations, from now on refered to as the
mean-field
equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\ddot{\bm{X}}_{t}=-\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\,\nabla_{\bm{X}}\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}}_{t})\,|\varphi_{t}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}=-\Delta\varphi_{t}+\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}}_{t})\varphi_{t}+(v*|\varphi_{t}|^{2}\,)\,\varphi_{t}\\
&({\bm{X}}_{t},\dot{\bm{X}}_{t},\varphi_{t})\big|_{t=0}=({\bm{X}}_{0},{\bm{V}}_{0},\varphi_{0})\end{cases}

(1.14)

provided some initial data
({\bm{X}}_{0},{\bm{V}}_{0},\varphi_{0})
has been specified at time zero. Global well-posedness in
\mathbb{R}^{6m}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, for the class of potentials we are going to be working with, is proven in Section
6
. In particular, the
L^{2}
norm of the bosons and the energy of the system are conserved quantities.

Condition 1

(Initial Data)
.

We assume that the initial state of the combined system is a tensor product of the form

where

(1)

({\bm{X}}_{0},{\bm{V}}_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{3m}\times\mathbb{R}^{3m}

\displaystyle\|\,|{\bm{X}}-{\bm{X}}_{0}|^{p}u_{N,0}\,\|_{L^{2}_{\bm{X}}}\,+\,\|\,|N^{-1}{\bm{P}}-{\bm{V}}_{0}|^{p}u_{N,0}\,\|_{L^{2}_{\bm{X}}}\ \leqslant\ \frac{C_{0}}{N^{p/2}}\ .

Remark 2.1
.

Given
u\in\mathcal{D}({\bm{P}}^{3})\cap\mathcal{D}({\bm{X}}^{3})
, we may construct an initial condition that satisfies Condition
1
by appropiate re-scaling, that is, by setting

u_{N}({\bm{X}})=N^{3m/4}\exp(\mathrm{i}N\,{\bm{X}}\cdot{\bm{V}}_{0})\,u(N^{1/2}({\bm{X}}-{\bm{X}}_{0}))\,,\qquad{\bm{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3m}\,,\ N\geqslant 1\,.

In particular,
\|u_{N}\|_{L^{2}}=\|u\|_{L^{2}}
for each
N
.

In
(1)
–besides regularity requirements–we ask the tracer particle’s wave function to be such that
its position and velocity distribution converge to a
Dirac delta, centered at the
phase space point
({\bm{X}}_{0},{\bm{V}}_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{3m}\times\mathbb{R}^{3m}
. In particular, we choose the fastest rate of convergence that is in agreement with Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle:
\Delta{\bm{X}}\Delta{\bm{V}}\geqslant N^{-1}
.
Note that convergence is rather strong, since it includes up to six moments in the position and momentum observables.
Our aim is to understand how these two conditions are propagated in time, provided one replaces
({\bm{X}}_{0},{\bm{V}}_{0})
with
({\bm{X}}_{t},\dot{\bm{X}}_{t})
.

As for the bosons, in (2)
we ask the initial datum to be a
coherent state
; each
n
-particle entry may be described as a symmetrization of the boson field
\varphi_{0}
, in the sense that

\mathcal{W}(\sqrt{N}\varphi_{0})\Omega=\exp(-N/2)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{N^{n/2}}{\sqrt{n!}}\varphi_{0}^{\otimes n}\,.

(2.4)

The time evolution of coherent states has been widely studied in the literature, and the following approach is now standard.
We introduce the one-particle density

\Gamma_{N,t}(x,y)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{1}{N}\left\langle\Psi_{N,t},\mathds{1}\otimes a_{x}^{*}a_{y}\Psi_{N,t}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\,,\qquad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\,

(2.5)

and we let
\Gamma_{N,t}
be the linear operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
whose integral kernel is
\Gamma_{N,t}(x,y)
. In particular, this operator is positive, self-adjoint and trace-class. Similarly, for the solution of the mean-field equations
\varphi_{t}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
we let

\ket{\varphi_{t}}\bra{\varphi_{t}}
be the orthogonal projection onto the subspace spanned by
\varphi_{t}
.
One is then interested in the convergence of
\Gamma_{N,t}
towards

\ket{\varphi_{t}}\bra{\varphi_{t}}
.

The class of potentials that we work with is described in the following Condition.
Most notably, we are able to include Coulomb potentials in the boson-boson interaction. On the other hand, requiring
three derivatives in the boson-tracer interaction is, almost certainly, not optimal.

Condition 2

(Potentials)
.

We assume
w:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and

(1)

(2)

where

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

and

.

2.1.1.
Idea of the proof

The proof of our main results is heavily inspired
by
[
8
]
(to control expectations of tracer particle’s observables)
and by
[
33
]
(to control vacuum fluctuations) and the idea is as follows. First, we introduce
fluctuation states

\Omega_{N,t}\in\mathscr{H}

that show up naturally when trying to replace
a_{x}
with
\sqrt{N}\varphi_{t}(x)
. However, a major difficulty arises when one writes down its infinitesimal generator. Namely, there is a (non-diagonal) term of the form

\sqrt{N}\,\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\big(\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}})-\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}}_{t})\big)\otimes\big(a_{x}\overline{\varphi_{t}}(x)+a_{x}^{*}\varphi_{t}(x)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

which has an apparent growth of

. In other words, the presence of tracer particles
can potentially create or destroy a lot of bosons for states close to the vacuum.
In order to control this term, we study

and apply a Taylor estimate on this interaction

thanks to Ehrenfest’s Theorem. Subsequently, once that control of

has been established, important remainder terms can be
estimated and the proof of our main results follows in a few pages.

We believe that the consequences outweight the benefits and shall not take this approach.

Our main result concerning particle number estimates for fluctuation states

Let

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ Ce^{Ct}N^{1/2}\,,

\displaystyle\|N_{b}\,\Omega_{N,t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ Ce^{Ct}N^{3/2}\,.

(3.4)

Remark 3.1

Even though we are not able to show that the fluctuation state

has particle number moments that are uniformly bounded in

3.1.
Regularization of the Problem

The purpose of regularizing the problem is twofold:
(1) all of the upcoming calculations are rigorously justified, and
(2) the proof of well-posedness for the (auxiliary) truncated dynamics is short.
In this subsection,

Let us be more precise.

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\Big(\nabla_{\bm{X}}\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}})-\nabla_{\bm{X}}\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}}_{t})\Big)|\varphi_{t}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\,\Psi_{N,t}\Big\|

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \|w\|_{C_{b}^{2}}\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta{\bm{X}}(t)\Psi_{N,t}\|\,.

(4.8)

We put the last four estimates together to find that for some
C>0
it holds that

\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta{\bm{V}}(t)\Psi_{N,t}\|^{2}\lesssim\|\Delta{\bm{V}}(t)\Psi_{N,t}\|^{2}+\|\Delta{\bm{X}}(t)\Psi_{N,t}\|^{2}+\frac{\exp{(Ct)}}{N^{1/2}}\,,\qquad t\geqslant 0\,,N\geqslant 1\,,

(4.9)

where we have used the number estimates (
4.1
).
We combine (
4.4
) and (
4.9
) to find that, thanks to the Grönwall inequality,
the following estimate holds for some
C>0

\|\Delta{\bm{X}}(t)\Psi_{N,t}\|^{2}+\|\Delta{\bm{V}}(t)\Psi_{N,t}\|^{2}\lesssim e^{Ct}\Big(\|\Delta{\bm{X}}(0)\,u_{N,0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta{\bm{V}}(0)\,u_{N,0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}\Big)\,.

(4.10)

Since
u_{N,0}
satisfies Condition
1
, the right hand side is of order
\exp(Ct)N^{-1/2}
. Thus,
the proof of the lemma is complete under our time differentiability assumption. To remove it, one may re-do all the calculations for
\Psi_{N,t}^{\varepsilon}
and
{\bm{X}}_{t}^{\varepsilon}
, introduced in Section
3
.
Moreover, thanks to Lemma
3.1
and
3.2
, we may adapt the argument presented in Lemma
3.3
to show that

\textstyle\|\Delta{\bm{X}}(t)\Psi_{N,t}\|^{2}+\|\Delta{\bm{V}}(t)\Psi_{N,t}\|^{2}\leqslant\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\Big(\|\Delta{\bm{X}}^{\varepsilon}(t)\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{N,t}\|^{2}+\|\Delta{\bm{V}}^{\varepsilon}(t)\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{N,t}\|^{2}\Big)\,.

(4.11)

Further, all we used in the proof of (

) were standard bounds, and the particle number estimates (
4.1
). Thus, our claim follows in view of (
3.39
).
∎

The proof of our first main theorem now becomes a straightforward corollary of the last result.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let us fix
t\geqslant 0
and

. Since

has unit norm, one easily finds thanks to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

|\left\langle\Psi_{N,t},{\bm{X}}\Psi_{N,t}\right\rangle-{\bm{X}}_{t}|\leqslant\left\langle\Psi_{N,t},|{\bm{X}}-{\bm{X}}_{t}|\Psi_{N,t}\right\rangle\leqslant\|\Delta{\bm{X}}(t)\Psi_{N,t}\|\,.

(4.12)

Thus, it suffices to apply Lemma
4.1
. The argument for the difference in velocity remains unchanged.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let

be the boson field solving the system (

) satisfied by the Weyl operator implies that

(4.13)

In particular, we have the following expression for the difference of the integral kernels:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{N,t}(x,y)-\overline{\varphi_{t}}(x)\varphi_{t}(y)=\frac{1}{N}\left\langle a_{x}\Omega_{N,t},a_{y}\Omega_{N,t}\right\rangle+\frac{\varphi_{t}(y)}{\sqrt{N}}\left\langle a_{x}\Omega_{N,t},\Omega_{N,t}\right\rangle+\frac{\overline{\varphi_{t}}(x)}{\sqrt{N}}\left\langle\Omega_{N,t},a_{y}\Omega_{N,t}\right\rangle\,.

(4.14)

Next, let

be the integral kernel of the Hilbert-Schmidt operator
\mathcal{K}
. We integrate both sides of the last identity against

and use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to find

\displaystyle\big|\mathrm{Tr}\,\,\mathcal{K}\big(\Gamma_{N,t}-

\displaystyle\ket{\varphi_{t}}\bra{\varphi_{t}}\big)\,\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\|K\|_{L^{2}}}{N}\left(\,\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}$}}|\left\langle a_{x}\Omega_{N,t},a_{y}\Omega_{N,t}\right\rangle|^{2}\,\right)^{1/2}+\frac{2\|K\|_{L^{2}}}{\sqrt{N}}\left(\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}$}}|\varphi_{t}(y)|^{2}\|a_{x}\Omega_{N,t}\|^{2}\,\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\mathcal{K}\|_{HS}}{N}\|N_{b}^{1/2}\Omega_{N,t}\|^{2}+\frac{2\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{HS}}{\sqrt{N}}\|N_{b}^{1/2}\Omega_{N,t}\|\,,

(4.15)

where we have also used the fact that
\Omega_{N,t}
and
\varphi_{t}
have unit norms. It follows now from the number estimates
(
4.1
) that there is

such that for all

\displaystyle\big|\mathrm{Tr}\,\,\mathcal{K}\big(\Gamma_{N,t}-\ket{\varphi_{t}}\bra{\varphi_{t}}\big)\,\big|

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ C\|\mathcal{K}\|_{HS}\Big(\frac{e^{Ct}}{N^{1/2}}+\frac{e^{Ct}}{N^{1/4}}\Big)\,.

(4.16)

Therefore, from the previous estimates we conclude that

\|\Gamma_{N,t}-\ket{\varphi_{t}}\bra{\varphi_{t}}\|_{HS}\ =\sup_{\|\mathcal{K}\|_{HS}\leqslant 1}\big|\mathrm{Tr}\,\mathcal{K}\big(\Gamma_{N,t}-\ket{\varphi_{t}}\bra{\varphi_{t}}\big)\big|\ \leqslant\ \frac{C\,e^{Ct}}{N^{1/4}}\,,

(4.17)

for all

and

. In view of (

), this completes the proof.

5.1.
Dynamical Estimates

We will be working with the following space of

\mathscr{D}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mathscr{D}_{\infty}\cap\mathcal{D}(T_{b})\,,\quad\textnormal{where}\quad\mathscr{D}_{\infty}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}(|{\bm{X}}|^{k})\cap\mathcal{D}(|{\bm{P}}|^{k})\cap\mathcal{D}(N_{b}^{k})\,.

(5.1)

In particular,
it follows from our results in Section
7

that the regularized, truncated dynamics propagates smoothness in the sense that

\mathcal{U}_{N}^{(M,\varepsilon)}(t,s)\mathscr{D}\subset\mathscr{D}\,,\qquad\forall t,s\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(5.2)

All of the upcoming calculations are justified in this space.

We will be mostly concerned in proving estimates for

\|N_{b}^{3/2}\,\mathcal{U}_{N}^{(M,\varepsilon)}(t,0)\,\Phi\|
,
when
\Phi\in\mathscr{D}
and
t\geqslant 0
. Let us note that thanks to the non-diagonal term

\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(\underline{w}^{\varepsilon}(x,{\bm{X}})-\underline{w}^{\varepsilon}(x,{\bm{X}}^{\varepsilon}_{t})\big)\big(a_{x}\overline{\varphi_{t}}^{\varepsilon}(x)+a_{x}^{*}\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x)\big)\mathrm{d}x

(5.3)

present in the generator of the (regularized) truncated dynamics, there is a link between the production of particles and the interaction between bosons and tracer particles. In order to account for this phenomenon, we introduce the time-dependent operators

\displaystyle\Delta{\bm{X}}(t,\varepsilon)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}={\bm{X}}-{\bm{X}}^{\varepsilon}_{t}

(5.5)

where

and

are the tracer particle’s position and momentum observables. It will be convenient to work with their Euclidean components.
Namely, if

labels the different tracer particles and

\displaystyle\Delta{\bm{X}}(t,\varepsilon)=\big(\Delta X_{k}^{(i)}(t,\varepsilon)\big)_{i=1,k=1}^{m,3}\quad\textnormal{ and }\quad\Delta{\bm{V}}(t,\varepsilon)=\big(\Delta V_{k}^{(i)}(t,\varepsilon)\big)_{i=1,k=1}^{m,3}\ .

(5.6)

We will be using the following quadratic form to quantify the production of bosonic particles associated to the interaction with the tracer particles. Here and in the sequel, we let

\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M,\varepsilon)}\equiv\mathcal{U}_{N}^{(M,\varepsilon)}(t,0)
unless stated otherwise.

Definition 5.1
.

For
\Phi\in\mathscr{D}
,
t\geqslant 0
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
we introduce the quantity

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t,\varepsilon)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\bigg(\|\,\Delta X_{k}^{(i)}(t,\varepsilon)^{3}\ \mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M,\varepsilon)}\Phi\|^{2}+\|\,\Delta V_{k}^{(i)}(t,\varepsilon)^{3}\ \mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M,\varepsilon)}\Phi\|^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\bigg(\|\,\Delta X_{k}^{(i)}(t,\varepsilon)\ \mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M,\varepsilon)}\Phi\|^{2}+\|\,\Delta V_{k}^{(i)}(t,\varepsilon)\ \mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M,\varepsilon)}\Phi\|^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N^{3}}\bigg(\|(N_{b}+1)^{3/2}\ \mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M,\varepsilon)}\Phi\|^{2}+\|(N_{b}+1)^{1/2}\ \mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M,\varepsilon)}\Phi\|^{2}\bigg)\,.

(5.7)

When we evaluate at our (regularized) initial condition

The proof of (

\mathcal{L}_{2}(t)
and
\mathcal{L}_{3}(t)
have already been analyzed in the literature; we will take some commutator estimates from

[
33
]
. More precisely, it is known that for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}

there is
C(j)>0
such that for all
smooth
\Psi\in\mathscr{D}
and
t\geqslant 0
it holds that

\big|\left\langle\Psi,[\mathcal{L}_{2}(t)+\mathcal{L}_{3}(t),(N_{b}+1)^{j}]\Psi\right\rangle\big|\leqslant C(j)\big(1+\sqrt{M/N}\big)\left\langle\Psi,(N_{b}+1)^{j}\Psi\right\rangle\,.

(5.17)

The constant
C(j)
depends on the potentials only through
|v|
, as the above estimates are a consequence of
Particle Number Estimates
and the
Potential Estimates
. Therefore, it suffices to focus on deriving similar commutator estimates for
\mathcal{L}_{1}(t)
for the cases
j=1,2,3\,.

Proof of
(a_{1})
.
Let
\Psi\in\mathscr{D}
be smooth but arbitrary. Starting from the definition of
\mathcal{L}_{1}(t)
one easily finds that

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Psi,[\mathcal{L}_{1}(t),(N_{b}+

\displaystyle 1)^{3}]\Psi\rangle\big|

(5.18)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{N}\,\big|\langle\Psi,\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\big(\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}})-\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}}_{t})\big)[a_{x}\overline{\varphi_{t}}(x)+a_{x}^{*}\varphi_{t}(x),(N_{b}+1)^{3}]\Psi\,\mathrm{d}x\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{N}\,\big|\langle\Psi,\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\big(\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}})-\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}}_{t})\big)\,\overline{\varphi_{t}}(x)\,[a_{x},(N_{b}+1)^{3}]\Psi\,\mathrm{d}x\rangle\big|\,.

The remaining commutator can be controlled as follows: by means of the relation
a_{x}N_{b}=(N_{b}+1)a_{x}
one finds that for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that

[a_{x},(N_{b}+1)^{\ell}]=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}\binom{\ell}{k}(N_{b}+1)^{k}a_{x}\,.

(5.19)

We plug this relationship back in (
5.18
) and use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to find that

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Psi,[\mathcal{L}_{1}(t),(N_{b}

\displaystyle+1)^{3}]\Psi\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{N}\,\sum_{k=0}^{2}\,\binom{3}{k}\big|\big\langle\Psi,\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\big(\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}})-\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}}_{t})\big)\,\overline{\varphi_{t}}(x)\,(N_{b}+1)^{k}a_{x}\Psi\,\mathrm{d}x\big\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{N}\,\|(N_{b}+1)^{3/2}\Psi\|\,\big\|\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\big(\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}})-\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}}_{t})\big)\,\overline{\varphi_{t}}(x)\,(N_{b}+1)^{1/2}a_{x}\mathrm{d}x\,\Psi\big\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{N}\,\|(N_{b}+1)^{3/2}\Psi\|\,\|\Delta{\bm{X}}(t)(N_{b}+1)\Psi\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(N_{b}+1)^{3/2}\Psi\|^{2}\ +\ N\,\|\Delta{\bm{X}}(t)(N_{b}+1)\Psi\|^{2}

(5.20)

where, in the third line, we have used a Taylor estimate
together with the
Particle Number Estimates
.
In order to control the second term that appears in (
5.20
) we use the Interpolation Lemma for
A=|\Delta{\bm{X}}(t)|
,
B=N^{-1/2}(N_{b}+1)^{1/2}
and
\lambda=0
to find that

N\,\|\Delta{\bm{X}}(t)\,(N_{b}+1)\Psi\|^{2}\ \lesssim\ \|(N_{b}+1)^{3/2}\Psi\|^{2}+N^{3}\||\Delta{\bm{X}}(t)|^{3}\Psi\|^{2}\,.

(5.21)

By putting together estimates (
5.17
), (
5.20
) and (
5.21
) for

\Psi=\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M)}\Phi

we find

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|(N_{b}+1)^{3/2}\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M)}\Phi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \big|\left\langle\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M)}\Phi,[\mathcal{L}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{L}_{2}(t)+\mathcal{L}_{3}(t),(N_{b}+1)^{3}]\ \mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M)}\Phi\right\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\ \lesssim\ \big(1+\sqrt{M/N}\big)N^{3}\,\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)\,.

(5.22)

Proof of
(a_{2})
.
We repeat the argument presented above; the proof is actually shorter. In particular, the estimate

\displaystyle\big|\left\langle\Psi,[\mathcal{L}_{1}(t),N_{b}+1]\Psi\right\rangle\big|\lesssim\|(N_{b}+1)^{1/2}\Psi\|^{2}\ +\ N\|\,\Delta{\bm{X}}(t)\,\Psi\|^{2}

(5.23)

is the analogous of (
5.20
) but the operators
N_{b}
and
\Delta X(t)
are already decoupled.
For

\Psi=\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M)}\Phi

the right hand side may be immediately bounded above by

N^{3}\,\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)
; an application of (
5.17
) for
j=1
then finishes the proof.

Proof of
(a_{3})
.
Similarly as in the proof of
(a_{1})
, we estimate the following commutator as

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Psi,[\mathcal{L}_{1}(t),(N_{b}+1)^{4}]

\displaystyle\Psi\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{N}\|(N_{b}+1)^{2}\Psi\|\Big\|\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\big(\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}})-\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}}_{t})\big)\overline{\varphi}_{t}(x)(N_{b}+1)a_{x}\Psi\mathrm{d}x\Big\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{N}\|(N_{b}+1)^{2}\Psi\|\|(N_{b}+1)^{3/2}\Psi\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(N_{b}+1)^{2}\Psi\|^{2}+N\|(N_{b}+1)^{3/2}\Psi\|^{2}\,,

(5.24)

where we have used boundedness of
w
instead of a Taylor estimate to control the
{\bm{X}}
-dependent term. We put together estimates (
5.24
) and (
5.17
) for
j=4
, to find that for
\Psi=\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M)}\Phi
we have

\frac{d}{dt}\|(N_{b}+1)^{2}\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M)}\Phi\|^{2}\lesssim(1+\sqrt{M/N})\|(N_{b}+1)^{2}\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M)}\Phi\|^{2}+N\|(N_{b}+1)^{3/2}\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M)}\Phi\|^{2}\,.

(5.25)

The proof of the lemma is finished after we apply the Grönwall inequality.
∎

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Let us introduce simplifications in the notation:

(i)

Since the following proof is conceptually independent of the number of tracer particles
m
, we shall assume for simplicity that
m=1
.
In particular, we drop the boldface notation for
{\bm{X}}
and
{\bm{V}}
; we write instead

{\bm{X}}=X=(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,,\quad\textnormal{and }\quad w(x-X)=\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}})

(5.26)

and similarly for velocity.

(ii)

In order
to avoid double subscripts, we write

{\bm{X}}_{t}=X_{t}=\big(X_{1}(t),X_{2}(t),X_{3}(t)\big)

(5.27)

to denote time dependence of the components of the tracer’s particle position, and similarly for velocity.

(iii)

Whenever there is no risk of confusion, we write

\Phi(t)\equiv\Phi_{t}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M)}\Phi\,.

(5.28)

Let us then fix
k\in\{1,2,3\}
, together with
N,M\geqslant 1
and
\Phi\in\mathscr{D}.

Proof of
(b_{1})
.
Let us first calculate that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta X_{k}(t)\ \Phi(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\Phi(t),\Big(\mathrm{i}[-\frac{\Delta_{X}}{2N},\Delta X_{k}(t)^{2}]-2\,\Delta X_{k}(t)\dot{X}_{k}(t)\Big)\,\Phi(t)\Big\rangle\,.

(5.29)

Moreover, the commutation relations
\mathrm{i}[P_{\ell},X_{k}]=\delta_{k,\ell}
imply that the following identity holds

\mathrm{i}[P^{2},\,\Delta X_{k}(t)^{2}]=P_{k}\Delta X_{k}(t)+\Delta X_{k}(t)P_{k}\,.

(5.30)

Thus, since
\Delta V_{k}(t)=N^{-1}P_{k}-\dot{X}_{k}(t)
, we find thanks to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta X_{k}(t)\Phi(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ =\ 2\mathrm{Re}\left\langle\Phi(t),\Delta V_{k}(t)\Delta X_{k}(t)\Phi(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \|\Delta V_{k}(t)\ \Phi(t)\|^{2}+\|\Delta X_{k}(t)\ \Phi(t)\|^{2}

(5.31)

from which our claim follows easily.

Proof of
(b_{2})
.
First, let us identify the
term cointained in the generator
\mathcal{L}_{M}(t)
that does not commute with
P_{k}=-\mathrm{i}\partial_{k}
; it is given by

\mathcal{F}_{M}(t)=\chi(N_{b}\leqslant M)\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}w(x-X)\,\big(N|\varphi_{t}(x)|^{2}+\sqrt{N}(a_{x}\overline{\varphi_{t}}(x)+h.c)-a_{x}^{*}a_{x}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

(5.32)

Moreover, we recall that the trajectory
X(t)
satisfies the mean-field equations
(
1.14
). Therefore, similarly as we calculated for
(b_{1}),

we find

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta V_{k}(t)\Phi(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ =\ 2\,\mathrm{Re}\Big\langle\Phi(t),\Big(\mathrm{i}\,[\mathcal{F}_{M}(t),N^{-1}\!P_{k}]-\ddot{X}_{k}(t)\Big)\Delta V_{k}(t)\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{(M)}\Phi\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \|\Delta V_{k}(t)\Phi(t)\|^{2}+\big\|\big(\mathrm{i}\,[\mathcal{F}_{M}(t),N^{-1}P_{k}]\,-\ddot{X}_{k}(t)\big)\Phi(t)\big\|^{2}\,.

(5.33)

The first term that shows up in (
5.33
) is clearly bounded above by

N^{2}\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)
. Therefore, it remains to estimate the first one in terms of
N^{2}\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)
. To this end, we write

\Delta F_{k}(t)\ \mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\ \mathrm{i}\,[\mathcal{F}_{M}(t),N^{-1}P_{k}]\,-\ddot{X}_{k}(t)

(5.34)

to denote the error in the force term exherted on the tracer particle. Further, we decompose it as

\Delta F_{k}(t)=\Delta F_{k}^{(1)}(t)\,+\,\Delta F_{k}^{(2)}(t)
, where

\displaystyle\Delta F_{k}^{(1)}(t)\ \mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\ \chi(N_{b}\leqslant M)\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\big(\partial_{k}w(x-X)-\partial_{k}w(x-X_{t})\big)|\varphi_{t}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(5.35)

\displaystyle\Delta F_{k}^{(2)}(t)\ \mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\ \chi(N_{b}\leqslant M)\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\partial_{k}w(x-X)\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}(a_{x}\overline{\varphi_{t}}(x)+h.c)-\frac{1}{N}a_{x}^{*}a_{x}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

(5.36)

Since
w\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R})
, we may use a
Taylor Estimate
and the
Particle Number Estimates
to find that the following inequalities

\displaystyle\|\Delta F_{k}^{(1)}(t)\Psi\|\leqslant\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{L^{\infty}}\||\Delta X(t)|\Psi\|

(5.37)

\displaystyle\|\Delta F_{k}^{(2)}(t)\Psi\|\leqslant\frac{\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\sqrt{N}}\big(1+\sqrt{M/N}\big)\|(N_{b}+1)^{1/2}\Psi\|\,.

(5.38)

hold, for all
\Psi\in\mathscr{D}.
By plugging these estimates back in (
5.33
) for
\Psi=\Phi(t)
one easily finds that

\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta V_{k}(t)\Phi(t)\|^{2}\ \lesssim\ (1+M/N)N^{2}\,\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)\,.

This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proof of Lemma
5.3
.

We will keep on using the same simplifications and notation introduced at the beginning of the proof of lemma
5.2
.
Let us fix
k\in\{1,2,3\}
, together with
N,M\geqslant 1
and
\Phi\in\mathscr{D}.

The following general general calculation will turn out to be useful. Let
A(t)
be a time-dependent, self-adjoint operator. As long as everything is well-defined, one formally has that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|A(t)^{3}\Phi(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ =\ \left\langle\Phi(t),\Big(\mathrm{i}[\mathcal{L}_{M}(t),A(t)^{6}]-6A(t)^{5}\dot{A}(t)\Big)\Phi(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ \lesssim\ \|A(t)^{3}\Phi(t)\|^{2}+\|A(t)^{2}\Big(\mathrm{i}[\mathcal{L}_{M}(t),A]-\dot{A}(t)\Big)\Phi(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \quad+\|A(t)\Big(\mathrm{i}[\mathcal{L}_{M}(t),A]-\dot{A}(t)\Big)A(t)\Phi(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \quad+\|\Big(\mathrm{i}[\mathcal{L}_{M}(t),A]-\dot{A}(t)\Big)A(t)^{2}\Phi(t)\|^{2}\,.

(5.39)

Proof of
(c_{1})
.
First, we specialize to
A(t)=\Delta X_{k}(t)
for which the above estimate turns into

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta X_{k}(t)^{3}\Phi_{t}\|^{2}\ \lesssim\

\displaystyle\|\Delta X_{k}(t)^{3}\Phi_{t}\|^{2}+\|\Delta X_{k}(t)^{2}\Delta V_{k}(t)\Phi_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\Delta X_{k}(t)\Delta V_{k}(t)\Delta X_{k}(t)\Phi_{t}\|^{2}+\|\Delta V_{k}(t)\Delta X_{k}(t)^{2}\Phi_{t}\|^{2}\,.

(5.40)

It suffices to show that every term contained in the right hand side above may be bounded in terms of
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t).
The first one is trivial and follows from the definition, so we omit it. The other three may be controlled using the Interpolation Lemma with
A=\Delta X_{k}(t)
,
B=V_{k}(t)
and
\lambda=-1
: we find that for all
t\geqslant 0

\|\Delta X_{k}(t)^{2}\Delta V_{k}(t)\Phi_{t}\|^{2}+\|\Delta X_{k}(t)\Delta V_{k}(t)\Delta X_{k}(t)\Phi_{t}\|^{2}+\|\Delta V_{k}(t)\Delta X_{k}(t)^{2}\Phi_{t}\|^{2}\,\,\lesssim\,\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)\,.

(5.41)

Proof of
(c_{2})
.
Let us now specialize our general calculation to

A(t)=\Delta V_{k}(t)
. We find that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta V_{k}(t)^{3}\Phi_{t}\|^{2}\ \lesssim\

\displaystyle\|\Delta V_{k}(t)^{3}\Phi_{t}\|^{2}+\|\Delta V_{k}(t)^{2}\Delta F_{k}(t)\Phi_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\Delta V_{k}(t)\Delta F_{k}(t)\Delta V_{k}(t)\Phi_{t}\|^{2}\,+\|\Delta F_{k}(t)\Delta V_{k}(t)^{2}\Phi_{t}\|^{2}\,,

(5.42)

where we recall that
\Delta F_{k}(t)
was defined in (
5.34
). Let us introduce some notation for the different terms that we found above:

\displaystyle T_{1}\ \mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\ \|\Delta F_{k}(t)\Delta V_{k}(t)^{2}\Phi(t)\|^{2}\,

(5.43)

\displaystyle T_{2}\ \mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\ \|\Delta V_{k}(t)\Delta F_{k}(t)\Delta V_{k}(t)\Phi(t)\|^{2}\,

(5.44)

\displaystyle T_{3}\ :=\ \|\Delta V_{k}(t)^{2}\Delta F_{k}(t)\Phi(t)\|^{2}\,.

(5.45)

Our goal is to show that
T_{i}\lesssim(1+M/N)^{2}\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)
for
i\in\{1,2,3\}
.

Bounds on
T_{1}:

Thanks to the force estimates (
5.37
), (
5.38
) one easily finds that

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle\ \lesssim\ \||\Delta X|\,\Delta V_{k}(t)^{2}\Phi(t)\|^{2}+(1+M/N)\,\|N^{-1/2}(N_{b}+1)^{1/2}\Delta V_{k}(t)^{2}\Phi(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \lesssim\ \sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\|\Delta X_{\ell}(t)\,\Delta V_{k}(t)^{2}\Phi(t)\|^{2}+(1+M/N)\,\|N^{-1/2}(N_{b}+1)^{1/2}\Delta V_{k}(t)^{2}\Phi(t)\|^{2}

(5.46)

Both the first and the second term that appear in the right hand side above can be controlled by
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)
using the Interpolation Lemma:

(i)
For the
the first term choose:

A_{\ell}=\Delta X_{\ell}(t)
,
B=\Delta V_{k}(t)
with
\ell\in\{1,2,3\}
and
\lambda_{\ell}\in\{-1,0\}
.

(ii)
For the second term choose:

A=N^{-1/2}(N_{b}+1)^{1/2}
,
B=\Delta V_{k}(t)
and
\lambda=0
.

We conclude that
T_{1}\lesssim(1+M/N)\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)
.

Bounds on

T_{2}:

The idea will be to give an upper bound for
T_{2}
in terms on
T_{1}
, plus a commutator term arising from

[\Delta F_{k}(t),\Delta V_{k}(t)]=\frac{1}{N}[\Delta F_{k}(t),P_{k}]
. For the sake of the upcoming calculations, let us briefly denote by

\mathcal{A}_{N,t}(x)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\big(a_{x}\overline{\varphi_{t}}(x)+h.c\big)+\frac{1}{N}a_{x}^{*}a_{x}\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

(5.47)

the operator-valued distribution, found in the definition of the force term
\Delta F_{k}(t)
.
We find that

\displaystyle T_{2}\ \lesssim\ T_{1}\,+\,\frac{1}{N^{2}}\big\|\,\chi(N_{b}\leqslant M)\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\partial_{k}^{2}w(x-X)\Big(\,|\varphi_{t}(x)|^{2}+\mathcal{A}_{N,t}(x)\,\Big)\mathrm{d}x\Delta V_{k}(t)\Phi(t)\big\|^{2}\,.

(5.48)

Since
w\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\|\varphi_{t}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
we find, thanks to the cut-off term
\chi(N_{b}\leqslant M)
and the
Particle Number Estimates
, that

\displaystyle T_{2}\ \lesssim\ T_{1}\,+\,\frac{1}{N^{2}}\big(1+\sqrt{{M}/{N}}+{M}/{N}\big)^{2}\|\Delta V_{k}(t)\Phi(t)\|^{2}\,\ \lesssim\ T_{1}+\big(1+{M}/{N}\big)^{2}\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)\,.

(5.49)

Thanks to the bound
T_{1}\lesssim(1+M/N)\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)
we conclude that

T_{2}\lesssim(1+M/N)^{2}\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)
.

Bounds on
T_{3}:

Similarly as before, we give an upper bound for
T_{3}
in terms of
T_{2}
, plus commutator terms. In particular, the following estimate is the only one in this article that requires three derivatives of the boson-tracer interaction
w
. We find

\displaystyle T_{3}\ \lesssim\

\displaystyle T_{2}\,+\,\frac{1}{N^{2}}\big\|\Delta V_{k}(t)\,\chi(N_{b}\leqslant M)\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\partial_{k}^{2}w(x-X)\big(|\varphi_{t}(x)|^{2}+\mathcal{A}_{N,t}(x)\big)\mathrm{d}x\,\Phi(t)\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \lesssim\

\displaystyle T_{2}\,+\,\frac{1}{N^{2}}\big\|\,\chi(N_{b}\leqslant M)\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\partial_{k}^{2}w(x-X)\big(|\varphi_{t}(x)|^{2}+\mathcal{A}_{N,t}(x)\big)\mathrm{d}x\ \Delta V_{k}(t)\,\Phi(t)\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N^{4}}\big\|\,\chi(N_{b}\leqslant M)\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\partial_{k}^{3}w(x-X)\big(|\varphi_{t}(x)|^{2}+\mathcal{A}_{N,t}(x)\big)\mathrm{d}x\,\Phi(t)\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \lesssim\

\displaystyle T_{2}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\big(1\,+\,\sqrt{{M}/{N}}+{M}/{N}\big)^{2}\|\Delta V_{k}(t)\,\Phi(t)\|^{2}\,

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N^{4}}\big(1+\sqrt{{M}/{N}}+{M}/{N}\big)^{2}\|\Phi(t)\|^{2}\,

\displaystyle\ \lesssim\

\displaystyle T_{2}\,+\,(1+M/N)^{2}\,\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)+N^{-4}(1+M/N)^{2}\,.

(5.50)

Thanks to the upper bound
T_{2}\lesssim(1+M/N)^{2}\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)
and
N^{-4}\leqslant\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)
we conclude that

T_{3}\lesssim(1+M/N)^{2}\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(t)
. This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

6.
Well-Posedness of the Mean-Field Equations

In this section, we establish well-posedness of the mean-field equations, introduced in (
1.14
). The energy of the system will be described by the functional

E:\mathbb{R}^{6m}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined as

E({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{1}{2}{\bm{V}}^{2}+\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\,\frac{1}{2}\,\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}}$}}|\varphi(x)|^{2}v(x-y)|\varphi(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+\,\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}})|\varphi(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\,.

(6.1)

The fundamental estimate satisfied by the energy functional
E
is the lower bound

{\bm{V}}^{2}+\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\,\leqslant\,\,\mu\,\big(E({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{6}\big)\,,\qquad({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)\in\mathbb{R}^{6m}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

(6.2)

where
\mu=\mu(v,w)>0
is a constant depending only on the potentials. Its proof follows from the operator inequality (
2.19
) for
v
, and the boundedness of
w
.

For notational simplicity, given a solution

({\bm{X}}_{t},\varphi_{t})
of the mean-field equations, we shall simply write

E(t)=E({\bm{X}}_{t},\dot{\bm{X}}_{t},\varphi_{t})
. In addition, we denote by
{\bm{V}}_{t}=\dot{\bm{X}}_{t}
the velocity of the tracer particles. The main result of this section is

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume that
v
and
w
satisfy Condition
2
. Then,
for any initial condition
({\bm{X}}_{0},{\bm{V}}_{0},\varphi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{6m}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, there is a unique global solution

({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)\in C\big(\mathbb{R};\,\mathbb{R}^{6m}\!\times\!H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)

to the mean-field equations (
1.14
) in mild form; it preserves the
L^{2}
-norm of the boson field and the total energy of the system. In particular,
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
it holds that

{\bm{V}}_{t}^{2}\,+\,\|\varphi_{t}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\,\leqslant\,\mu\,\big(E(0)+\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{6}\big)\,.

(6.3)

Further, if
\varphi_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, it holds that

\varphi\in C^{1}\big(\mathbb{R};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)\cap C\big(\mathbb{R};H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
and (
1.14
) holds in the strong sense.

Its proof is divided into three steps: local well-posedness, propagation of
H^{2}
regularity, and conservation laws. These steps are given as the following three lemmas.

Lemma 6.1

(Local Well-Posedness)
.

Let
({\bm{X}}_{0},{\bm{V}}_{0},\varphi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{6m}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be initial conditions for which the system has energy
E_{0}.

Then, there exists
T=T(v,w,E_{0},\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}})>0
such that the system of equations

\displaystyle{\bm{X}}_{t}={\bm{X}}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}{\bm{V}}_{s}\mathrm{d}s

(6.4)

\displaystyle{\bm{V}}_{t}={\bm{V}}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla_{\bm{X}}\underline{w}\big(x,{\bm{X}}_{s}\big)\,|\varphi_{s}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

(6.5)

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}=e^{-\mathrm{i}t\Delta}\varphi_{0}-\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mathrm{i}(t-s)\Delta}\Big((v*|\varphi_{s}|^{2})\,\varphi_{s}+\underline{w}\big(x,{\bm{X}}_{s}\big)\varphi_{s}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\,

(6.6)

has a unique solution

({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)\in C\big([0,T];\,\mathbb{R}^{6m}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
. In addition, the solution depends continuously on the initial data.

Remark 6.1
.

The map
({\bm{X}}_{0},{\bm{V}}_{0},\varphi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{6m}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\mapsto T\in(0,\infty)
is continuous; see (
6.7
) and (
6.20
).

Proof.

Let us fix some initial conditions
({\bm{X}}_{0},{\bm{V}}_{0},\varphi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{6m}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. In particular, it follows from (
6.2
) that the inequality

M^{2}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mu(E_{0}+\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{6})\geqslant\|\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+|{\bm{V}}_{0}|^{2}\geqslant 0\,,

(6.7)

holds. For
T=T(M,v,w)>0
, we will set up a fixed-point argument in the Banach space

Y_{T}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\Big\{({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)\in C\big([0,T];\,\mathbb{R}^{6m}\!\times\!H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)\,\big|\,\sup_{|t|\leqslant T}\Big(|{\bm{V}}(t)-{\bm{V}}_{0}|+\|\varphi(t)-e^{-\mathrm{i}t\Delta}\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\Big)\leqslant M\Big\}\,,

(6.8)

endowed with the norm

\|({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)\|\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\big(|{\bm{X}}(t)|+|{\bm{V}}(t)|+\|\varphi(t)\|_{H^{1}}\big)
.
We consider the map
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2},\mathcal{M}_{3})
on
C\big([0,T];\,\mathbb{R}^{3m}\times\mathbb{R}^{3m}\!\times\!H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)(t)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}={\bm{X}}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}{\bm{V}}_{s}\mathrm{d}s

(6.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)(t)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}={\bm{V}}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla_{\bm{X}}\underline{w}\big(x,{\bm{X}}_{s}\big)\,|\varphi_{s}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

(6.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{3}({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)(t)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=e^{-\mathrm{i}t\Delta}\varphi_{0}-\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mathrm{i}(t-s)\Delta}\Big((v*|\varphi_{s}|^{2})\,\varphi_{s}+\underline{w}\big(x,{\bm{X}}_{s}\big)\varphi_{s}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

(6.11)

Thanks to Banach’s fixed point theorem, existence and uniqueness of solutions is established once we show that
\mathcal{M}
is a contraction from
Y_{T}
into itself, for
T
small enough. We will show that such
T
can be chosen to depend only on
M
,
v
and
w
. The details of the proof of continuity with respect to initial data are left as an exercise to the reader.

Proof of
\mathcal{M}(Y_{T})\subset Y_{T}
. Let

({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)\in Y_{T}
and

t\in[0,T]
. Then, we have the estimates

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}_{2}({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)(t)-{\bm{V}}_{0}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\,T\,\|w\|_{C_{b}^{1}}\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}\|\varphi_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(6.12)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{3}({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)(t)-e^{-\mathrm{i}t\Delta}\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant T\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}\big(\|(v*|\varphi_{\tau}|^{2})\varphi_{\tau}\|_{H^{1}}+\|w(x,{\bm{X}}_{s})\varphi_{s}\|_{H^{1}}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\,T\,(L(v)+2\|w\|_{C_{b}^{1}})\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}\big(\|\varphi_{\tau}\|_{H^{1}}^{3}+\|\varphi_{\tau}\|_{H^{1}}\big)

(6.13)

where, in deriving the last inequality, we have used the Lipschitz property (
2.23
) for
\psi=0
. Thanks to the triangle inequality and the definition of
M
and
Y_{T}
, it follows that

\|\varphi_{\tau}\|_{H_{1}}+|{\bm{V}}_{\tau}|\leqslant\|e^{-i\tau\Delta}\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+|{\bm{V}}_{0}|+\|\varphi_{\tau}-e^{-\mathrm{i}\tau\Delta}\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+|{\bm{V}}_{\tau}-{\bm{V}}_{0}|\leqslant 3M\,.

(6.14)

Therefore, we may combine the last three estimates to conclude that there is

C_{v,w}^{(1)}>0
such that
for all
t\in[0,T]

|\mathcal{M}_{2}({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)(t)-{\bm{V}}_{0}|+\|\mathcal{M}_{3}({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)(t)-e^{-\mathrm{i}t\Delta}\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leqslant C_{v,w}^{(1)}\,T\,(M+M^{3}).

(6.15)

Thus,
\mathcal{M}
maps
Y_{T}
into itself provided

T\leqslant(C_{v,w}^{(1)}(1+M^{2}))^{-1}
.

Proof of Contractivity
.
Consider
({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)
and
(\tilde{\bm{X}},\tilde{\bm{V}},\tilde{\varphi})
in
Y_{T}
, and let
t\in[0,T]
. For the position variables, one simply has that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}_{1}({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)(t)-\mathcal{M}_{1}(\tilde{\bm{X}},\tilde{\bm{V}},\tilde{\varphi})(t)|

\displaystyle\leqslant T\textstyle\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}|{\bm{V}}_{\tau}-\tilde{\bm{V}}_{\tau}|\,.

(6.16)

For the velocity variables, we use a Taylor estimate and the triangle inequality to find that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}_{2}({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi

\displaystyle)(t)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(\tilde{\bm{X}},\tilde{\bm{V}},\tilde{\varphi})(t)|

(6.17)

\displaystyle\leqslant\textstyle T\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}\Big(\|w\|_{C_{b}^{1}}(\|\varphi_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\tilde{\varphi}_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}})\|\varphi_{\tau}-\tilde{\varphi}_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}+\|w\|_{C_{b}^{2}}\|\varphi_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}|{\bm{X}}_{\tau}-\tilde{\bm{X}}_{\tau}|\Big).

For the boson fields, we use the Lipschitz property (
2.23
), a Taylor estimate and the triangle inequality to find that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{3}({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)(t)\,-\,\mathcal{M}_{3}

\displaystyle(\tilde{\bm{X}},\tilde{\bm{V}},\tilde{\varphi})(t)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \textstyle T\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}L(v)(\|\varphi_{\tau}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\tilde{\varphi}_{\tau}\|_{H^{1}}^{2})\|\varphi_{\tau}-\tilde{\varphi}_{\tau}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\quad\ +\ T\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}3\|w\|_{C_{b}^{2}}(\|\varphi_{\tau}\|_{H^{1}}|{\bm{X}}_{\tau}-\tilde{\bm{X}}_{\tau}|+\|\varphi_{\tau}-\tilde{\varphi}_{\tau}\|_{H^{1}})\,.

(6.18)

Since the last three estimates are uniform in
t\in[0,T]
, we put them together to find that there is
C_{v,w}^{(2)}>0
such that

\|\mathcal{M}({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)-\mathcal{M}(\tilde{\bm{X}},\tilde{\bm{V}},\tilde{\varphi})\|\leqslant T\,C_{v,w}^{(2)}(M+M^{2})\|({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)-(\tilde{\bm{X}},\tilde{\bm{V}},\tilde{\varphi})\|

(6.19)

where we have used
(
6.14
)
as well. Since
M+M^{2}\leqslant 2(1+M^{2})
, it follows that by choosing

T(M,v,w)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\min\Big\{\frac{1}{C_{v,w}^{(1)}},\frac{1}{4C_{v,w}^{(2)}}\Big\}\frac{1}{1+M^{2}}

(6.20)

the map
\mathcal{M}
becomes Lipschitz continuous, with Lipschitz constant
1/2
. In particular, it becomes a contraction and the proof is complete.
∎

The following proof of propagation of regularity is heavily inspired by
[
1
, Chapter 4]
.

Lemma 6.2

(Propagation of
H^{2}
Regularity)
.

Let

({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)\in C\big([0,T];\,\mathbb{R}^{6m}\!\times\!H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
be as in Lemma
6.1
with initial data
\varphi_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then, we have that

\varphi\in C^{1}\big([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)\cap C\big([0,T];H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)

and the mean-field equation (
1.14
) holds in the strong sense.

Proof.

In terms of
{\bm{X}}\in C^{1}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{3m})
, as given by Lemma
6.1
, we define the map

F(t,\phi)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=(v*|\phi|^{2})\phi+w\big(x,{\bm{X}}_{t}\big)\phi\,,\qquad(t,\phi)\in[0,T]\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,.

(6.21)

In particular, a Taylor estimate combined with the
Potential Estimate

(
2.21
), imply that the following Lipschitz estimates are satisfied

\displaystyle\|F(t,\phi)-F(s,\phi)\|_{L^{2}}\ \lesssim\ \sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}\!|\dot{\bm{X}}_{\tau}|\ \|\phi\|_{L^{2}}\ |t-s|

(6.22)

\displaystyle\|F(t,\phi)-F(t,\psi)\|_{L^{2}}\ \lesssim\ (\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2})\|\phi-\psi\|_{L^{2}}\,.

(6.23)

Let
\varphi\in C([0,T],H^{1})
be the solution constructed in Lemma
6.1
.
Our goal will be to show that
t\in[0,T]\mapsto\varphi_{t}\in L^{2}
is a Lipschitz map. Indeed,
after a change of variables, we are able to write

\varphi_{t}=e^{-\mathrm{i}t\Delta}\varphi_{0}-\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mathrm{i}s\Delta}F(t-s,\varphi_{t-s})\mathrm{d}t

for all
t\in(0,T)
.
Thus, for all
h
small enough we find thanks to (
6.22
) and (
6.23
)

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{t+h}-\varphi_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\ \lesssim\

\displaystyle|h|\,\|\Delta\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{t}^{t+h}\|F(t+h-s,\varphi_{t+h-s})\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|F(t+h-s,\varphi_{t+h-s})-F(t-s,\varphi_{t-s})\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}s\,,

\displaystyle\ \lesssim\

\displaystyle\,|h|\,\big(\|\Delta\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\nu^{3}\big)+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\nu^{2}|h|+\nu^{2}\|\varphi_{t+h-s}-\varphi_{t-s}\|_{L^{2}}\big)\mathrm{d}s\,,

(6.24)

where
\nu\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}(\,\|\varphi_{t}\|_{H^{1}}+|\dot{\bm{X}}_{t}|\,)<\infty
. A second change of variables in the last integral let us apply the Grönwall inequality and conclude that for some
C>0
, independent of
t
and
h
, it holds that

\|\varphi_{t+h}-\varphi_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant Ce^{C\nu^{2}t}\big(\|\Delta\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\nu^{3}+\nu^{2}t\big)\,|h|\,.

(6.25)

Consequently, the map
t\in[0,T]\mapsto f(t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=F(t,\varphi_{t})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
is Lipschitz continuous and, in particular,
f\in W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
.
One may then view the mean-field equation for
\varphi_{t}
as a semi-linear Schrödinger equation with source term
f
. In particular
W^{1,1}
regularity of the source term implies that
H^{2}
regularity of the solution is propagated in time; see
[
1
, Proposition 4.1.16]
.
∎

In order to obtain global well-posedness, we prove that both the energy of the system and the
L^{2}
mass of the boson field are constant in time. Consequently, the time
T>0
–introduced in Lemma
6.1
–may be used to patch local solutions and cover the real line.

Lemma 6.3

(Conservation Laws)
.

Let
({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)\in C\big([0,T];\,\mathbb{R}^{6m}\!\times\!H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
be as in Lemma
6.1
.
Then, it holds that for all
t\in[0,T]

\|\varphi_{t}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad E(t)=E(0)\,.

(6.26)

where
E(t)
is the energy of the system defined in (
6.1
).

Proof.

Let us first consider the case
\varphi_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

and let
({\bm{X}},{\bm{V}},\varphi)

be the local solution of the mean-field equations, as given by Lemma
6.1
.
Thanks to Lemma
6.2
, the boson field remains in
H^{2}

and satisfies
\dot{\varphi}_{t}=-\mathrm{i}\,h(t)\varphi_{t}
, where we introduce the self-adjoint operator

h(t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=-\Delta+v*|\varphi_{t}|^{2}+\underline{w}\big(x,{\bm{X}}_{t}\big)\,,\qquad t\in[0,T]\,.

(6.27)

Conservation of
L^{2}
-norm
.
We differentiate at
t\in(0,T)
to find

\frac{d}{dt}\|\varphi_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=2\mathrm{Re}\left\langle\varphi_{t},\mathrm{i}\,h(t)\varphi_{t}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=0

(6.28)

thanks to self-adjointness of
h(t)
. It suffices to apply the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.

Conservation of energy
.
Let
DE_{Z}:\mathbb{R}^{6m}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\mapsto\mathbb{R}
be the Fréchet derivative of the energy functional
E
, evaluated at
Z
. Then, the chain rule gives at
t\in(0,T)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E(t)

\displaystyle\ =\ DE_{({\bm{X}}_{t},{\bm{V}}_{t},\varphi_{t})}(\dot{\bm{X}}_{t},\dot{\bm{V}}_{t},\dot{\varphi}_{t})\,,

\displaystyle\ =\ \mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\nabla_{\bm{X}}\underline{w}\big(x,{\bm{X}}_{t}\big)|\varphi_{t}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\cdot\dot{\bm{X}}_{t}\,+\,{\bm{V}}_{t}\cdot\dot{\bm{V}}_{t}\,+\,2\mathrm{Re}\left\langle h(t)\varphi_{t},\dot{\varphi}_{t}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\,.

(6.29)

Thanks to the mean-field equations, the first and second term cancel out. The third term vanishes, thanks to self-adjointess of
h(t)
.

Finally, for the case
\varphi_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
we take a sequence
(\varphi_{0,n})_{n\geqslant 1}\subset H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
that converges to
\varphi_{0}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. In particular, thanks to continuity with respect to intial data, it holds that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\varphi_{t,n}-\varphi_{t}\|_{H^{1}}+|\dot{\bm{X}}_{t,n}-\dot{\bm{X}}_{t}|+|{\bm{X}}_{t,n}-{\bm{X}}_{t}|=0\,,\qquad\forall t\in[0,T]

(6.30)

where
({\bm{X}}_{t},\varphi_{t})
solves (
1.14
) with initial data
({\bm{X}}_{0},{\bm{V}}_{0},\varphi_{0})
and
({\bm{X}}_{t,n},\varphi_{t,n})
solves (
1.14
) with initial data

({\bm{X}}_{0},{\bm{V}}_{0},\varphi_{n,0})
. Thus, it easily follows that for all
t\in[0,T]

E(t)=E({\bm{X}}_{t},\dot{\bm{X}}_{t},\varphi_{t})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E({\bm{X}}_{t,n},\dot{\bm{X}}_{t,n},\varphi_{t,n})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E({\bm{X}}_{0},{\bm{V}}_{0},\varphi_{0,n})=E_{0}\,,

(6.31)

which concludes the proof.
∎

7.
Well-posedness of the Regularized, Truncated Dynamics

Let
N,M\geqslant 1
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Throughout this section, we shall drop the regularization paremeter
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and assume instead that the potentials and the initial data that we work with satisfy the stronger assumptions:

v\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,,\quad w\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,,\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\varphi_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,.

(7.1)

We will then only apply well-posedness results presented in this section, to the truncated dynamics.
Indeed, we now
turn our attention
to the study of the Cauchy problem for solutions
of the equation

\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}\mathcal{U}_{N}^{(M)}(t,s)=\mathcal{L}_{M}(t)\mathcal{U}_{N}^{(M)}(t,s)\,,\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\mathcal{U}_{N}^{(M)}(t,t)=\mathds{1}\,,\qquad t,s\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(7.2)

Here,
\mathcal{L}_{M}(t)
is the regularized, truncated generator introduced in (
3.26
).
We will decompose it in the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{M}(t)\ =\ \mathbb{H}\ +\ \mathcal{I}_{M}(t)\,,\quad\textnormal{where}\quad\mathbb{H}\ \mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\ -\frac{1}{2N}\Delta_{{\bm{X}}}\,+\,T_{b}\,+\,\mathcal{I}_{4}\,.

(7.3)

Remark 7.1
.

\mathbb{H}
is a self-adjoint operator
with a domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H})
satisfying the anlogous characterization (
1.13
), but with
N_{b}^{2}
instead of
N_{b}^{3}
; this thanks to boundedness of
v
. In particular, its domain becomes a Banach space when endowed with the norm

\|\Psi\|_{\mathbb{H}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\|\Psi\|+\|\mathbb{H}\Psi\|\,,\qquad\Psi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H})\,.

(7.4)

Further, note that
\mathbb{H}
commutes with both the particle number operator
N_{b}
and the momentum operator
{\bm{P}}=-\mathrm{i}\nabla_{{\bm{X}}}
.

Thanks to the particle number cut-off, the potentials being bounded,
and the boson field being in

C^{1}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}_{x})
, the interaction term
\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)
enjoys the following two regularity properties.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
T\geqslant 0
and
N,M\geqslant 1
. Then, the following holds:

(1)
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H})}<\infty.

(2) There exists
C=C(v,w,T,N,M)
such that

\|\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)-\mathcal{I}_{M}(s)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H})}\leqslant C\,|t-s|\,,\qquad\forall t,s\in[0,T]\,.

(7.5)

Proof.

(1) Using the
Particle Number Estimates
for
v,w\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
one finds, thanks to the particle number cut-off, that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

\|\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H})}\,\lesssim\,C(N,M)\big(\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)(1+\|\varphi_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

(7.6)

from which our claims follows easily.

(2) Let
t,s\in[0,T]
and write the difference as

\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)-\mathcal{I}_{M}(s)=\chi(N_{b}\leqslant M)\big(N\Delta\mathcal{I}_{0}(t,s)+N^{1/2}\Delta\mathcal{I}_{1}(t,s)+\Delta\mathcal{I}_{2}(t,s)+N^{-1/2}\Delta\mathcal{I}_{3}(t,s)\big)

(7.7)

where each term
\Delta\mathcal{I}_{i}(t)
that has beed introduced above, depends on a
i
-th power on creation and annihilation operator. Since the estimate for each one of these is very similar, we shall only show one in detail. Indeed, for the cubic term we have that

\displaystyle\|\chi(N_{b}\leqslant M)\Delta\mathcal{I}_{3}(t,s)\|

\displaystyle\,\lesssim\,\|\chi(N_{b}\leqslant M)\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{6}$}}}$}}v(x-t)a_{x}^{*}\big(\varphi_{t}(y)-\varphi_{s}(y)\big)a_{y}^{*}a_{x}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\|

\displaystyle\,\lesssim\,M^{3/2}\big(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}v(x-y)^{2}|(\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{s})(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\,\lesssim\,\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}M^{3/2}\|\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{s}\|_{L^{2}}\,\lesssim\,\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}M^{3/2}\sup_{\tau\leqslant T}\|\partial_{\tau}\varphi_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}|t-s|\,,

(7.8)

where, in the last line, we have used
\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{s}=\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{\tau}\varphi_{\tau}\mathrm{d}\tau
. Since

(t\mapsto\varphi_{t})\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}_{x})
, the Lipschitz constant of the right hand side of (
7.8
) is finite.
∎

We combine Lemma
7.1
, together with
[
32
, Theorem X.69]
and
[
1
, Proposition 4.1.16]
to prove existence, uniqueness and basic propagation of regularity for the propagator of the dynamics
defined by (
7.2
). Note that a similar result could have been obtained using the general theory of Kato
[
24
]
.

Proposition 7.1
.

There exists a unique
unitary propagator

\big(\mathcal{U}_{N}^{(M)}(t,s)\big)_{t,s\in\mathbb{R}}
such that
for all
\Psi_{0}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H})
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
the map

t\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto\Psi(t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mathcal{U}_{N}^{(M)}(t,s)\Psi_{0}\in\mathscr{H}
satisfies:

(1)
\Psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathscr{H})\cap C(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H}))

(2)
\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}\Psi(t)=\mathcal{L}_{M}(t)\Psi(t)
holds in
\mathscr{H}
.

Remark 7.2
.

This result can be extended to the following abstract setting. Consider time-dependent operators of the form

\mathbb{H}(t)=\mathbb{H}_{0}+\mathcal{I}(t)\,,\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(7.9)

where
\mathbb{H}_{0}
is self-adjoint with domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H}_{0})
, and

\mathcal{I}(t)
is bounded, self-adjoint and satisfies assumptions (1) and (2) of Lemma
7.1
. Then, there is a unique unitary propagator
U(t,s)
associated to the evolution of
\mathbb{H}(t)
, in the sense of Proposition
7.1
.
Note that we do not require
\mathbb{H}_{0}
to be bounded from below, nor we require
additional assumptions on the derivative of
\mathcal{I}(t)
or on the commutator
[\mathbb{H}_{0},\mathcal{I}(t)]
.

Proof of Proposition
7.1
.

Let us first pass to the interaction picture. Namely, we define

\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{M}(t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=e^{\mathrm{i}t\mathbb{H}}\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathbb{H}}\,,\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(7.10)

Then, Lemma
7.1
and
[
32
, Theorem X.69]
imply the existence of a unitary propagator
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{N}^{M}(t,s)
–explicitly given by the absolutely convergent Dyson series–that satisfies the integral equation

\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{N}^{(M)}(t,s)=\mathds{1}-\mathrm{i}\int_{s}^{t}\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{M}(r)\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{N}^{(M)}(r,s)\mathrm{d}r\,,\qquad t,s\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(7.11)

Consequently, for
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
we let our original propagator be

\mathcal{U}_{N}^{(M)}(t,s)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathbb{H}}\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{N}^{(M)}(t,s)e^{\mathrm{i}s\mathbb{H}}
.
Let now
\Psi(t)
be as in the statement of the proposition. Then, it is straightforward to verify that for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
it holds that

\Psi(t)=e^{-\mathrm{i}(t-s)\mathbb{H}}\Psi_{0}-\mathrm{i}\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\mathrm{i}(t-r)\mathbb{H}}\mathcal{I}_{M}(r)\Psi(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,.

(7.12)

Since the interaction term
\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)
is locally Lipschitz continuous, we may adapt the argument presented in the proof of Lemma
6.2
to show that the map

t\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto\Psi(t)\in\mathscr{H}
is locally Lipschitz continuous, as well. In particular, the source term

t\mapsto F(t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)\Psi(t)\in\mathscr{H}
becomes locally Lipschitz continuous and, therefore, belongs to
W^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R},\mathscr{H})
. We may then apply

[
1
, Proposition 4.1.6]
to conclude propagation of regularity, in the sense that (1) and (2) hold true. Uniqueness follows from standard arguments using the symmetry of
\mathcal{L}_{M}(t)
and denseness of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H})\subset\mathscr{H}
.
∎

Next, we show that the regularized truncated dynamics propagates smoothness with respect to the tracer particle variables, together with boundedness with respect to the particle number operator and its powers. We remind the reader that the
space
\mathscr{D}_{\infty}
has been introduced in (
5.1
)

Proposition 7.2
.

In the notation of Proposition
7.1
, if

\Psi_{0}\in\mathscr{D}_{\infty}
, then
\Psi(t)\in\mathscr{D}_{\infty}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Let
N,M\geqslant 1
be fixed and assume
s=0
for simplicity. We will be using a fixed-point argument. Indeed,
let
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and consider the following Banach space

\mathscr{H}_{k}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\{\Psi\in\mathscr{H}\,|\,\|\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}_{k}}<\infty\}

(7.13)

which we endow with the norm

\|\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}_{k}}\textstyle\ \mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\ \|\,|{\bm{X}}|^{k}\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}}+\|\,|{\bm{P}}|^{k}\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}}+\|N_{b}^{k}\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}}+\|\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}}\,.

(7.14)

Step 1.
First, we show that the interaction term
\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)
is continuous with respect to
\mathscr{H}_{k}
, uniformly in
t\in\mathbb{R}
. To this end, let

\alpha
be a multi-index with
|\alpha|\leqslant k
. Then, the terms in
\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)
that depend on
{\bm{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3m}
satisfy the following estimate

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\bm{X}}^{\alpha}\Big(\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.4}{$\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\textstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}{\scalebox{1.4}{$\scriptscriptstyle\textstyle\int_{\mathchoice{\scalebox{.85}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\textstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}{\scalebox{.85}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}^{3}$}}}$}}\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}})\,\chi(N_{b}\leqslant M)(N|\varphi_{t}|^{2}+

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}a_{x}\varphi_{t}(x)+a_{x}^{*}a_{x})\mathrm{d}x\ \Psi\Big)\|_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C(k)\|w\|_{C_{b}^{k}}(N+\sqrt{NM}+M)\|\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}_{k}}

(7.15)

where
C(k)>0
depends only on
k
. The other terms that show up in
\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)
do not depend on the tracer particle variables
{\bm{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3m}
and can be controlled analogously, thanks to the particle number cut-off.
We put all of the terms together and find that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
it holds that

\||{\bm{P}}|^{k}\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}}\ \lesssim\ (N+\sqrt{NM}+M+\sqrt{M^{3}/N})\,\|\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}_{k}}\,,\qquad\Psi\in\mathscr{H}_{k}\,.

(7.16)

Next, for the particle number operator we can use the particle number cut-off to easily find that the following (rough) estimate holds

\|N_{b}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}}\lesssim M^{k}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H})}\|\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}}\lesssim M^{k}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H})}\|\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}_{k}}\,.

(7.17)

For the position variables, simply note that
{\bm{X}}
commutes with
\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)
. Thus, we put these estimates together to find that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

\|\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}_{k}}\lesssim\big(N+M+\sqrt{M^{3}/N}+M^{k}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\mathcal{I}_{M}(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H})}\big)\|\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}_{k}}\,.

(7.18)

Step 2
. Secondly, we show that the evolution group
(e^{\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{N}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is continuous
with respect to
\mathscr{H}_{k}
, locally uniformly in time. To this end, we note that in one dimension the following estimate holds for the free Schrödinger propagator

\|X^{k}e^{-\mathrm{i}tP^{2}}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|(X+tP)^{k}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant C(k)(\|X^{k}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+t^{k}\|P^{k}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})})

(7.19)

as it can be verified by using the commutation relation
XP=PX+\mathrm{i}
to control mixed powers.
This in turn easily implies that

\displaystyle\|e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathbb{H}}\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\||{\bm{X}}|^{k}e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathbb{H}}\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}}+\||{\bm{P}}|^{k}\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}}+\|N_{b}^{k}\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}}+\|\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}}\leqslant C(1+t^{k})\|\Psi\|_{\mathscr{H}_{k}}\,

(7.20)

for some constant
C=C(k)>0
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Step 3.
Finally,
we use the continuity estimates (
7.18
) and (
7.20
)
to set up a fixed-point argument in
C([0,T],\mathscr{H}_{k})
to show that the equation

\widetilde{\Psi}(t)=e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathbb{H}}\Psi_{0}-\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mathrm{i}(t-r)\mathbb{H}}\mathcal{I}_{M}(r)\widetilde{\Psi}(r)\ \mathrm{d}r.

has a unique solution.
In particular, if
\Psi(t)
is the solution constructed
in Proposition
7.1
, it holds that
\Psi(t)=\widetilde{\Psi}(t)\in\mathscr{H}_{k}
for all
t\in[0,T]
, thanks to uniquess of solutions in
C([0,T],\mathscr{H})
(i.e. the
k=0
case).
Further,
T<1
can be chosen to depend only on the constants that show up in the continuity estimates. Thus, we can iterate the argument to cover
\mathbb{R}
.
Since
k\in\mathbb{N}
was arbitrary,
this concludes the proof.
∎

The Regularization Lemmata

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Let us fix
N\geqslant 1
and
t\geqslant 0
. Then, the triangle inequality
and the unitarity of the evolution group
(e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\varepsilon}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{N,t}-\Psi_{N,t}^{\varepsilon}\|\leqslant\|\Psi_{N,0}-\Psi_{N,0}^{\varepsilon}\|+\|(e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\varepsilon}}-e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{N}})\Psi_{N,0}\|\,,\quad\forall\varepsilon\in(0,1)\,.

The First Term

\|\Psi_{N,0}-\Psi_{N,0}^{\varepsilon}\|\,\lesssim\,\|\mathcal{W}(\sqrt{N}\varphi_{0})\Omega-\mathcal{W}(\sqrt{N}\varphi_{0}^{\varepsilon})\Omega\|_{\mathcal{F}_{b}}+\|u_{N,0}-u_{N,0}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{\bm{X}}^{2}}

(A.2)

where we used the fact that the norms of

u_{N,0}^{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{W}(\sqrt{N}\varphi_{0})\Omega
are uniformly bounded in
N
and
\varepsilon
.
It is known
[
16
, Lemma 3.1]
that the map

f\in L^{2}\mapsto\mathcal{W}(f)\Phi\in\mathcal{F}_{b}
is continuous for every
\Phi\in\mathcal{F}_{b}
.
Thus, we apply the definitions of the regularized quantities
u_{N,0}^{\varepsilon}

and
\varphi_{0}^{\varepsilon}

to conclude that the right hand side of (
A.2
) vanishes as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.

The Second Term
.
Since the Hamiltonians preserve particle number, for any
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
we may write

\|(e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\varepsilon}}-e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{N}})\Psi_{N,0}\|\lesssim\|\chi(N_{b}\leqslant n_{0})(e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\varepsilon}}-e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{N}})\Psi_{N,0}\|+\|\chi(N_{b}>n_{0})\Psi_{N,0}\|\,.

(A.3)

Let us decompose

e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{N}}\Psi_{N,0}=(\psi_{n}(t))_{n\geqslant 0}

and

e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\varepsilon}}\Psi_{N,0}=(\psi_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t))_{n\geqslant 0}
according to their direct sum representation in
\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}L^{2}_{{\bm{X}}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{n}
.
Then,
we obtain the following standard estimates

\displaystyle\|\psi_{n}(t)-\psi_{n}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\bm{X}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{n}}

(A.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}|\left\langle\psi_{n}(s),(H_{N,n}-H_{N,n}^{\varepsilon})\psi_{n}^{\varepsilon}(s)\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\bm{X}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{n}}|\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\textnormal{(in the sense of quad. forms)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}|\big\langle\psi_{n}(s),\Big(\,\frac{n^{2}}{N}(v-v^{\varepsilon})(x_{1}-x_{2})+n(\underline{w}-\underline{w}^{\varepsilon})(x_{1},{\bm{X}})\,\Big)\psi_{n}^{\varepsilon}(s)\big\rangle_{L^{2}_{\bm{X}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{n}}|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\frac{n}{N}\|v-v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3/2}}+n\|w-w^{\varepsilon}\|_{C_{b}}\Big)\|\psi_{n}(s)\|_{L_{\bm{X}}^{2}\otimes H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3n})}\|\psi_{n}^{\varepsilon}(s)\|_{L_{\bm{X}}^{2}\otimes H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3n})}\mathrm{d}s\,.

Here, we have used

|\left\langle\varphi_{1},v\varphi_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}|\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{3/2}}\|\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{6}}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{6}}\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{3/2}}\|\varphi_{1}\|_{H^{1}}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{H^{1}},

which is a consequence of Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev’s Embedding Theorem for
d=3
.
It is also a standard exercise to check that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
H^{1}
norms of
\psi_{n}(t)
and
\psi_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t)
are uniformly bounded in
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Therefore, for each fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\|\psi_{n}(t)-\psi_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{\bm{X}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{n}}=0\,,\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(A.5)

Thus, it easily follows now from (
A.3
) that for all
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}

\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\|(e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\varepsilon}}-e^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{H}_{N}})\Psi\|\,\lesssim\,\|\chi(N_{b}>n_{0})\Psi_{N,0}\|\,.

(A.6)

The proof is complete after we take the limit
n_{0}\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Throughout the proof, we will use the fact that the
H^{1}
norms of both
\varphi_{t}
and
\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}
are uniformly bounded in
t
and
\varepsilon
.
They will be absorbed into universal constants.

The Boson Field
. First, we note that difference between the boson fields may be written as

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=e^{-\mathrm{i}t\Delta}(\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{0}^{\varepsilon})

(A.7)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mathrm{i}(t-s)\Delta}\Big((v*|\varphi_{s}|^{2})\varphi_{s}-(v^{\varepsilon}*|\varphi_{s}^{\varepsilon}|^{2})\varphi_{s}^{\varepsilon}+\underline{w}(x,{\bm{X}}_{s})\varphi_{s}-\underline{w}^{\varepsilon}(x,{\bm{X}}_{s}^{\varepsilon})\varphi_{s}^{\varepsilon}\Big)\mathrm{d}s.

Under our condition for the potential
v
, it is known that the map

\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\mapsto(v*|\varphi|^{2})\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is locally Lipschitz continuous, i.e. it satisfies (
2.23
).
Therefore, we apply the triangle inequality, a first order Taylor estimate and
Lipschitz continuity to find that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|(v-v^{\varepsilon})*|\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}\mathrm{d}s\,.

(A.8)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}(\|w\|_{C_{b}^{1}}+\|\varphi_{s}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\varphi_{s}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}^{2})(\|\varphi_{s}-\varphi_{s}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}+|{\bm{X}}_{s}-{\bm{X}}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|+\|w-w^{\varepsilon}\|_{C_{b}^{1}})\ \mathrm{d}s

For the term containing the difference
\delta v=v-v^{\varepsilon}
, we use the Leibniz rule to find that

\displaystyle\big\|(\delta v*|\varphi|^{2})\varphi\big\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\big\|(\delta v*|\varphi|^{2})\varphi\big\|_{L^{2}}+\big\|\big(\delta v*\bar{\varphi}\nabla\varphi\big)\varphi\big\|_{L^{2}}+\big\|(\delta v*|\varphi|^{2})\nabla\varphi\big\|_{L^{2}}\,.

(A.9)

In addition, we note than an application of Hölder’s and Young’s inequality gives

\big\|(\delta v*\varphi\phi)\,\psi\big\|_{L^{2}}\,\lesssim\,\|\delta v\|_{L^{3/2}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|\phi\|_{L^{p_{2}}}\|\psi\|_{L^{p_{3}}}\,,\qquad\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{p_{3}}=\frac{5}{6}\,.

(A.10)

We apply the last estimate for
(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})=(6,2,6)
and
(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})=(6,6,2)
, combined with the embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, to find that

\displaystyle\big\|(\delta v*|\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{2})\,\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}\big\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\|\delta v\|_{L^{3/2}}\|\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}^{3}\lesssim\varepsilon\,.

(A.11)

Tracer Particle Variables
.
Similarly as before, we may use a Taylor estimate and the triangle inequality to find that

\displaystyle|{\bm{X}}_{t}-{\bm{X}}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}|\dot{\bm{X}}_{s}-\dot{\bm{X}}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|\mathrm{d}s

(A.12)

\displaystyle|\dot{\bm{X}}_{t}-\dot{\bm{X}}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\ \lesssim\ \int_{0}^{t}\big(\|w\|_{C_{b}^{2}}+\|\varphi_{s}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\varphi_{s}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)(\|\varphi_{s}-\varphi_{s}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}+|{\bm{X}}_{s}-{\bm{X}}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|+\|w-w^{\varepsilon}\|_{C_{b}^{1}})\mathrm{d}s\,.

We combine the estimates
for the boson field and the tracer particle position
and apply the Grönwall inequality to the quantity

\|\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{t}\|_{H^{1}}+|{\bm{X}}_{t}-{\bm{X}}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|+|\dot{\bm{X}}_{t}-\dot{\bm{X}}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|
to obtain

\|\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}+|{\bm{X}}_{t}-{\bm{X}}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|+|\dot{\bm{X}}_{t}-\dot{\bm{X}}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|\lesssim\,e^{Ct}\,(1+t)\Big(\varepsilon+\|\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}+\|w-w^{\varepsilon}\|_{C_{b}^{1}}\Big)\,,

(A.13)

for some
C>0
. The proof of the lemma is finished once we apply the definitions of the regularized objects and take the limit
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.
∎

Appendix B
The Interpolation Lemma

In this appendix we give a proof of the Interpolation Lemma, stated as Lemma
5.4
.

Proof.

Let
\Psi
be arbitrary and assume
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
for simplicity. Then, using the commutation relations we find that

\|ABA\Psi\|^{2}=\left\langle\Psi,AB(A^{2}B)A\Psi\right\rangle=\left\langle\Psi,AB^{2}A^{3}\Psi\right\rangle-2\mathrm{i}\,\lambda\left\langle\Psi,ABA^{2}\Psi\right\rangle\,.

(B.1)

Then, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Young’s inequality, we find that for all
\varepsilon>0
there is
C_{\varepsilon}
(independent of
\Psi
) such that

\displaystyle|\left\langle\Psi,AB^{2}A^{3}\Psi\right\rangle|

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon\|AB^{2}\Psi\|^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|A^{3}\Psi\|^{2}

\displaystyle|\left\langle\Psi,ABA^{2}\Psi\right\rangle|

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon\|ABA\Psi\|^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{2}\|A\Psi\|^{2}.

If we let
M(A,B)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\|A^{3}\Psi\|^{2}+\|B^{3}\Psi\|^{2}+\lambda^{2}\|A\Psi\|^{2}+\lambda^{2}\|B\Psi\|^{2}
, we have found that

\|ABA\Psi\|^{2}\leqslant 2\varepsilon\,(\|AB^{2}\Psi\|^{2}+\|ABA\Psi\|^{2})+2\,C_{\varepsilon}\,M(A_{,}B)\,.

(B.2)

Using
\|A^{2}B\Psi\|^{2}\leqslant 2\|ABA\Psi\|^{2}+2\lambda^{2}\|A\Psi\|^{2}

and

\|BA^{2}\Psi\|^{2}\leqslant 2\|ABA\Psi\|^{2}+2\lambda^{2}\|A\Psi\|^{2}
we find (after re-updating
C_{\varepsilon}
)

\|ABA\Psi\|^{2}+\|A^{2}B\Psi\|^{2}+\|BA^{2}\Psi\|^{2}\leqslant 10\varepsilon\,(\|AB^{2}\Psi\|^{2}+\|ABA\Psi\|^{2})+C_{\varepsilon}\,M(A_{,}B)\,.

(B.3)

Similarly, we can interchange the roles of
A
and
B
to find that

\|BAB\Psi\|^{2}+\|B^{2}A\Psi\|^{2}+\|AB^{2}\Psi\|^{2}\leqslant 10\varepsilon\,(\|BA^{2}\Psi\|^{2}+\|BAB\Psi\|^{2})+C_{\varepsilon}\,M(A_{,}B)\,.

(B.4)

A straightforward combination of the last two inequalities, for
\varepsilon<1/20
, finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

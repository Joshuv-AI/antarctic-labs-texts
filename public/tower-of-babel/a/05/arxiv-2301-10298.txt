Classification of saddle-focus singularities
I. K. Kozlov, and, A. A. Oshemkov

Abstract
The paper presents an algorithm for topological classification of nondegenerate saddle-focus singularities of integrable Hamiltonian systems with three degrees of freedom up to semi-local equivalence. In particular, we prove that any singularity of saddle-focus type can be represented as an almost direct product in which the acting group is cyclic. Based on the constructed algorithm, a complete list of singularities of saddle-focus type of complexity 1 1 , 2 2 , and 3 3 , i.e., singularities whose leaf contains one, two, or three singular points of rank 0 0 , is obtained. Earlier both singularities of saddle-focus type of complexity 1 1 were also described by L. M. Lerman.



Keywords and phrases:
integrable system, Liouville foliation, saddle-focus singularity.

Introduction

We study topological properties of singularities of integrable Hamiltonian systems in a neighborhood of a singular leaf.
More precisely, we consider saddle-focus singularities for systems with three degrees of freedom up to semi-local Liouville equivalence (i. e., up to a leafwise homeomorphism of Liouville foliations in a neighborhood of a singular leaf). In particular, the paper presents an algorithm for constructing a complete list of saddle-focus singularities of given complexity
k
, i.e. with
k
singular points of rank
0
on the leaf (see Section
3
).
Based on this algorithm, we obtain a classification of singularities of the saddle-focus type of complexity
\leq 3

(see Theorems
4
,
5
,
6
).

All necessary information about integrable systems and nondegenerate singularities can be found in
[
3
]
or
[
4
]
. We only briefly give the necessary definitions.

An
integrable Hamiltonian system
with
n
degrees of freedom is a
2n
-dimensional symplectic manifold
(M^{2n},\omega)
with
n
functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
defined on it such that the Hamiltonian vector fields
\sgrad f_{i}
are complete, pairwise commute and are linearly independent almost everywhere on
M^{2n}
. The mapping
\Phi=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n}):M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called the
momentum mapping
of an integrable Hamiltonian system
(M^{2n},\omega,f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
.

Note that the definition of a Hamiltonian system usually includes a
Hamiltonian
— some distinguished function
H

from the set
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
(or a function of
f_{i}
), whose Hamiltonian flow defines a dynamical system on the phase space
M^{2n}
. For us the choice of a Hamiltonian is not important, since we are not interested in the dynamics, but only in the topology of the foliation generated by the first integrals

f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
, which is defined as follows.

Definition 1
.

The decomposition of the phase space of an integrable Hamiltonian system into connected components of
\Phi^{-1}(y)
(i.e. the connected components of the common level surfaces of first integrals
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
) is called the Liouville foliation corresponding to this system.

Definition 2
.

Two integrable Hamiltonian systems on
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are called topologically equivalent (or Liouville equivalent), if there exists a homeomorphism
\Psi\colon U_{1}\to U_{2}
that maps each leaf of the Liouville foliation on
U_{1}
to a leaf of the Liouville foliation on
U_{2}
.

Usually the Liouville foliation is not locally trivial and has singularities. In this paper, we consider Liouville foliations such that all their singularities are nondegenerate. These singularities are generic singularities for integrable systems. In fact, they are a multidimensional symplectic analogue of ‘‘Morse singularities’’. We present only a characteristic property
for nondegenerate singularities (Theorem
1
), which can be taken as a definition
(See
[
3
]
or
[
4
]
for a precise definition).

There are three types of simplest ‘‘basic’’ nondegenerate singularities, they are given by the following Liouville foliations:

•

E
is the Liouville foliation defined in a neighborhood of zero in
(\mathbb{R}^{2},dp\wedge dq)
by the function
p^{2}+q^{2}
(elliptic type);

•

H
is the Liouville foliation defined in a neighborhood of zero in
(\mathbb{R}^{2},dp\wedge dq)
by the function
pq
(hyperbolic type);

•

F
is a Liouville foliation defined in a neighborhood of zero in
(\mathbb{R}^{4},dp\wedge dq)
by commuting functions
p_{1}q_{1}+p_{2}q_{2}
and
p_{1}q_{2}-q_{1}p_{2}

(focus-focus type).

If a Liouville foliation is given by functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
, then its singular points are those where the subspace generated by
\sgrad f_{i}

has dimension less than
n
. The
rank
of a singular point is the dimension of this subspace. In particular, the singular points of these foliations
Liouville
E
,
H
and
F
have rank
0
.

Theorem 1

(Eliasson; see
[
5
,
3
]
)
.

In a neighbourhood of a nondegenerate singular point of rank
r
, the Liouville foliation is locally leafwise symplectomorphic to the direct product of
k_{e}
copies of the foliation
E
,
k_{h}
copies of the foliation
H
, and
k_{f}

copies of the foliation
F
, as well as the trivial foliation
\mathbb{R}^{r}\times\mathbb{R}^{r}
.

The triple
(k_{e},k_{h},k_{f})
is called the
type
of a nondegenerate singular point. It is easy to see that the type of a singular point is uniquely defined.

A
singularity
of a Liouville foliation is the germ of the momentum map on a singular leaf, i.e. a leaf containing singular points. A singularity is called
nondegenerate
(in particular, elliptic, hyperbolic, focus) if all its singular points are nondegenerate (and their type is elliptical, hyperbolic or focus, respectively).

The
rank of a singularity
is the minimum rank of points on it. For the singularities of almost direct product type considered in this paper (see Definition
3
) the types of all its minimum rank points are the same. This type
(k_{e},k_{h},k_{f})
is called the
type of a singularity
.

Let
W_{1},\dots,W_{l}
each be either a foliation without singularities or the simplest singularity: elliptic, hyperbolic, or focus-focus. Their product
W_{1}\times\dots\times W_{l}
has a natural Liouville foliation with a singularity. This singularity is called a
direct product
singularity, and the singularities that are Liouville equivalent to it are singularities
of direct product type
.

Definition 3
.

An almost direct product of singularities is a quotient of a direct product singularity
W_{1}\times\dots\times W_{l}

by an action
\psi
of a finite group
G
that satisfies the following conditions:

1.

the action
\psi
on
W_{1}\times\dots\times W_{l}
is component-wise, i.e.

\psi(g)(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})=(\psi_{1}(g)(x_{1}),\dots,\psi_{l}(g)(x_{l})),

where
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{l}
are actions of the group
G
on singularities
W_{1},\dots,W_{l}
,

2.

the action on each factor
\psi_{i}(g):W_{i}\to W_{i}
is a symplectomorphism that preserves the functions defining the Liouville foliation (and, in particular, the foliation itself),

3.

the action
\psi
on
W_{1}\times\dots\times W_{l}
is free.

A singularity that is Liouville equivalent to an almost direct product
(W_{1}\times\dots\times W_{l})/G

is called a singularity of almost direct product type.

Remark 1
.

Below we will consider only singularities of almost direct product type, since N. T. Zung proved in
[
17
]
that
that any nondegenerate singularity that satisfies the non-splitability condition (which means that for each singular point
of minimum rank on a singular leaf its local bifurcation diagram coincides with the bifurcation diagram of the entire singularity),
is a singularity of almost direct product type. Most of the singularities that occur in integrable systems
in mechanics and physics are non-splittable (see
[
3
]
). However, singularities not
satisfying the non-splitability condition arise in systems that are invariant with respect to rotation (see, for example,
[
19
]
as well as the references cited therein). Also one can construct ‘‘artificial’’ examples of ‘‘splitable’’ singularities (see
[
4
]
).

Note that Zung’s theorem is topological in nature, i.e., almost direct products of singularities that are Liouville equivalent need not be leafwise symplectomorphic. Since in this paper we consider singularities only up to Liouville equivalence, the singularities of almost direct product type will sometimes be called simply almost direct products.

There are many works in which the singularities of integrable Hamiltonian systems (or Liouville foliations) were studied from different points of view: a study of specific integrable systems, invariants of singularities (topological, smooth, symplectic), classification of singularities of some fixed type. Since the local classification of nondegenerate singularities is described by the Eliasson theorem (Theorem
1
), here, when we speak about the classification of singularities, we usually mean their
semi-local classification
, i.e., up to leafwise homeomorphism
of Liouville foliations in a neighborhood of a singular leaf.

Note also that there is a well-developed theory describing the ‘‘global’’ properties of Liouville foliations (and in particular their singularities) on isoenergetic manifolds of integrable Hamiltonian systems with two degrees of freedom. This theory was developed in the works of A. T. Fomenko (see
[
6

7

[

]

In this paper, we will consider singularities of saddle-focus type (i.e., of type

A simple minimal model of a saddle-focus singularity

2.1
Existence of a simple minimal model

Definition 4
.

We will call an almost direct product
(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/G
a simple minimal model if any (non-trivial)
element
g\in G

1.

acts nontrivially on
\mathcal{V}_{m}
and
\mathcal{F}_{n}
,

2.

has no fixed points of rank
0
on
\mathcal{F}_{n}
.

This definition of a simple minimal model differs from the definition of a minimal model in N. T. Zung’s
[
17
]

by the addion of condition (
2
).

Theorem 2
.

Consider a singularity of saddle-focus type that is an almost direct product
(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/G
.
Let
N\subset G
be the subgroup generated by elements that act trivially on the saddle component or
have fixed points on the focus component. Then

1.

the subgroup
N
is normal and
G/N\cong\mathbb{Z}_{k}
,

2.

(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/N
is leafwise symplectomorphic to the direct product
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}\times\mathcal{F}_{n^{\prime}}
, where
V^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}=V_{m}/N
,
and
n^{\prime}
is the number of orbits of the action of the subgroup
N
on the set of points of rank
0
of the focus singularity
\mathcal{F}_{n}
,

3.

(\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}\times\mathcal{F}_{n^{\prime}})/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
if a simple minimal model for the singularity
(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/G
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

It is obvious that
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
. Let us show that the almost direct product
(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/G
can be ‘‘factorized’’ by
N
,
i.e. we can replace
\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n}
with
(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/N
and
G
with
G/N
.

It is clear that if in an almost direct product
(W_{1}\times W_{2})/G
the group
G
acts trivially on the component
W_{2}
, then
(W_{1}\times W_{2})/G
is leafwise symplectomorphic to
(W_{1}/G)\times W_{2}
. Therefore, without loss of generality, we may assume that the
the almost direct product
(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/G
is a minimal model (i.e. the condition (
1
) of Definitions
4
is satisfied
).

By assumption,
G
is a subgroup of the automorphism group of a focus singularity
\Aut(\mathcal{F}_{n})
, i.e., the group of leafwise symplectomorphisms of singularity
\mathcal{F}_{n}
into itself that are identical on the base of the foliation. This group is described in
[
8
]
.
Let us recall some assertions proved there. Let
H_{f}
be a subgroup of Hamiltonian automorphisms of the focus singularity
in the group
\Aut(\mathcal{F}_{n})
, and let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be singular points of the focus singularity numbered in order.

Assertion 4

(see
[
8
, Assertion 6]
)
.

The group
\Aut(\mathcal{F}_{n})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{k}\times H_{f}
, where
k
is a divisor of
n
,
\mathbb{Z}_{k}=\langle a\rangle
,

a(x_{q})=x_{q+k\pmod{n}}
for any singular point
x_{q}
.

Corollary 1

(see
[
8
, Assertion 7]
)
.

All elements of finite order in the group
\Aut(\mathcal{F}_{n})
lie in its ‘‘compact part’’
\Aut_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{n})=\mathbb{Z}_{k}\times S^{1}
.

Corollary 2
.

For any minimal model of a saddle-focus singularity, the group
G
is either cyclic or isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{k}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{l}
.
More precisely,
G\cap H_{f}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{l}
and
G/(G\cap H_{f})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, where
k,l\geq 1
(here we formally set
\mathbb{Z}_{1}=\{0\}
).

To prove Theorem
2
, it remains to take the quotient of the singularity by the action of the group
G\cap H_{f}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{l}
, i.e. to prove the following statement.

Lemma 1
.

Any saddle-focus singularity of the form
(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/G
, where the group
G
acts on
\mathcal{F}_{n}
by Hamiltonian automorphisms
(i.e., preserves all singular points of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
) is leafwise symplectomorphic to the singularity
(\mathcal{V}_{m}/G)\times\mathcal{F}_{n}
.

Proof of Lemma
1
.

Let us represent a
2
-atom
\mathcal{V}_{m}
in a form convenient for us. We want to cut

\mathcal{V}_{m}
so that
\mathcal{V}_{m}/G
can be glued together from its parts. To do this, we use the following obvious assertion.

Assertion 5
.

If a group
G
acts freely on a
2
-atom
\mathcal{V}_{m}
, then it can be cut into ‘‘crosses’’
K_{i}
and ‘‘ribbons’’
L_{j}
so that the group
G

acted freely on sets of ‘‘crosses’’ and ‘‘ribbons’’.

In the case under consideration, the group
G
acts freely on
\mathcal{V}_{m}
, since it freely acts on
\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n}

and preserves all singular points of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
. As an example, Fig.
3
shows a similar representation for the
2
-atom
D_{1}
,
on which acts the group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(as a central symmetry), and the fundamental domain for this action is highlighted.

:
Fundamental region for the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
on

Let
\mathcal{V}_{m}/G
be obtained as a result of gluing along the boundary of some
‘‘crosses’’
K_{1},\dots,K_{p}
and ‘‘ribbons’’
L_{1},\dots,L_{q}
.
Consider an arbitrary boundary point
x\in\partial\bigl(\bigl(\bigcup K_{i}\bigr)\cup\bigl(\bigcup L_{j}\bigr)\bigr)
.
Denote by
\sigma(x)
the point with which it is identified. Then
(\mathcal{V}_{m}/G)\times\mathcal{F}_{n}
is homeomorphic to the quotient space

\Bigl(\bigcup K_{i}\times\mathcal{F}_{n}\Bigr)\cup\Bigl(\bigcup L_{j}\times\mathcal{F}_{n}\Bigr)\Big/\sim_{1},

where
(x,y)\sim_{1}(\sigma(x),y)
for any point
y\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
. In turn,
(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/G
is homeomorphic to the quotient space

\Bigl(\bigcup K_{i}\times\mathcal{F}_{n}\Bigr)\cup\Bigl(\bigcup L_{j}\times\mathcal{F}_{n}\Bigr)\Big/\sim_{2},

where
(x,y)\sim_{2}(\sigma(x),h_{x}(y))
for any point
y\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
, where
h_{x}
is some Hamiltonian automorphism of the singularity
\mathcal{F}_{n}
.

Any Hamiltonian automorphism
h
is homotopic to the identity in the class of Hamiltonian automorphisms (if
h
is a shift by unit time along
Hamiltonian vector field
v
, then the homotopy is a shift by time
t
along
v
). Therefore
(\mathcal{V}_{m}/G)\times\mathcal{F}_{n}
and
(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/G
are homeomorphic,
since we can ‘‘unwind’’ each mapping
h_{x}
on a ‘‘rectangle’’ — a small neighborhood of the boundary. For this you can use
the following assertion, which is easy to prove using the tubular neighborhood theorem.

Assertion 6
.

If we cut the manifold
M^{n}
along a submaifold
Q^{n-1}
of codimension
1
, and then glue the resulting boundary components
by a mapping homotopic to the original one, then we get a manifold that is homeomorphic to
M^{n}
.

This implies that
(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/G
and
(\mathcal{V}_{m}/G)\times\mathcal{F}_{n}
are Liouville equivalent. It remains to show that the Liouville equivalence
between them can be made symplectic. To do this, it suffices to note that the constructed homotopy automorphisms
h_{x}
preserve the symplectic
structure. The fact that the homotopy of all Hamiltonian automorphisms
h_{x}
can be made smoothly dependent on the point
x
is guaranteed by the following simple
assertion.

Assertion 7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{n}
be a focus singularity,
f_{1},f_{2}
be its integrals such that the trajectories of the Hamiltonian vector field

\sgrad f_{2}
are closed with period
2\pi
. Then the functions
H_{1}=H_{1}(f_{1},f_{2})
and
H_{2}=H_{2}(f_{1},f_{2})
define the same Hamiltonian
automorphisms of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
, i.e. in other words, the
1
-shifts along the Hamiltonian vector fields
\sgrad H_{1}
and
\sgrad H_{2}
are equal,
if and only if

H_{2}-H_{1}=\frac{c_{1}}{2\pi}f_{2}+c_{2},

where
c_{1}\in\mathbb{Z},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma
1
is proved.
∎

After factorization of the minimal model by the action of the group
G\cap H_{f}
, we obtain a simple minimal model with the action of the group
G/(G\cap H_{f})
.
Thus, item
3
of Theorem

is fulfilled. Item

of

is a consequence of Lemma

, Item

from Corollary

. Theorem

2.2
Uniqueness of a simple minimal model

Theorem 3
.

For any simple minimal model

(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/\mathbb{Z}_{k}

of a saddle-focus singularity
its
2
-atom
\mathcal{V}_{m}
, focus singularity
\mathcal{F}_{n}
and group
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
are uniquely determined
by the structure of the singularity itself.

We list some properties of critical points of a singularity of saddle-focus type in the form of the following statement,
which can be easily proved since we know how the group
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
acts on a simple minimal model
(see Theorem
2
).

Assertion 8
.

Consider a simple minimal model
(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
of a saddle-focus singularity. Denote by
K_{i}
the set of its critical
points of rank
i
.
Then the following holds for a sufficiently small neighborhood of the singular leaf.

1.

The bifurcation diagram, that is, the image of all critical points of the singularity under the momentum mapping is the union of a line
l
and a plane
\Pi
,
transversely intersecting at a point
P
.

2.

The set
K_{0}
consists of
\frac{mn}{k}
points lying in the inverse image of the point
P
.

3.

The set
K_{0}\cup K_{1}
forms a
2
-dimensional symplectic submanifold
M_{1}^{2}
contained in the inverse image of the line
l
.
The induced Liouville foliation on
M_{1}^{2}
is Liouville equivalent to
\frac{n}{k}
instances of the
2
-atom
\mathcal{V}_{m}
.

4.

The set
K_{0}\cup K_{2}
forms a
4
-dimensional symplectic submanifold
M_{2}^{4}
contained in the inverse image of the plane
\Pi
.

(a)

If
k
is odd, then the induced Liouville foliation on
M_{2}^{4}
is Liouville equivalent to the union of
\frac{m}{k}
focus singularities
\mathcal{F}_{n}
.

(b)

If
k=2k_{1}
and the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{k}=\langle a\rangle
on
\mathcal{V}_{m}
has exactly
s
points of rank
0
with stabilizer
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

(i.e., points fixed under the action of the element
a^{k_{1}}
), where

3.2
Classification algorithm for saddle-focus singularities

By Theorem
2
, to classify saddle-focus singularities, it suffices to consider their simple minimal models. They all have the form
(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. Notice, that

•

the complexity of the singularity
(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n})/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
is equal to
md
, where
n=kd
,

•

the generating element of the group
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
acts on
\mathcal{V}_{m}
as an element of order
k
in the symmetry group
\Sym(\mathcal{V}_{m})
of the atom
\mathcal{V}_{m}
,

•

the order of the symmetry group
\Sym(\mathcal{V}_{m})
does not exceed
2m
.

Based on these considerations, we get the following algorithm to obtain a complete list of saddle-focus singularities of complexity
p
.

1.

We enumerate all possible divisors
m
of the number
p
.

2.

For each saddle
2
-atom
\mathcal{V}_{m}
of complexity
m
, we consider all possible
k
for which the group
\Sym(\mathcal{V}_{m})
contains a subgroup
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
.

3.

We consider all possible simple minimal models of the form

(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{\frac{kp}{m}})/\mathbb{Z}_{k},

(1)

if they exist.

4.

If there are several simple minimal models of the same type (
1
), then it is checked whether
they are Liouville equivalent.

Let us show that for a singularity of any fixed complexity
p
, the algorithm will finish in a finite number of steps. It is obvious that there is a finite number of variants for numbers
m
,
k
and
2
-atoms
\mathcal{V}_{m}
(up to Liouville equivalence). Therefore, it suffices to show that
at the step
3
it is enough to consider a finite number of singularities. If the actions of the groups
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
from (
1
) on any of the factors are conjugate
(by an automorphism of Liouville foliations), then the resulting fibrations are Liouville equivalent.

Note that for any simple minimal model
\left(\mathcal{V}_{m}\times\mathcal{F}_{n}/\right)\mathbb{Z}_{k}
the factor
\mathcal{F}_{n}/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
is a nondegenerate focus singularity
(i.e. of type
(0,0,1)
). For fixed
k
and
n
all such singularities
\mathcal{F}_{n}/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
are Liouville equivalent (see, for example,

[
8
]
). Therefore, any two actions of the group
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
on
\mathcal{F}_{n}
in simple minimal models
can be translated into each other by a Liouville equivalence of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
(for fixed
k
and
n
).

It remains to prove the following assertion.

Assertion 11
.

Up to a Liouville automorphism of the
2
-atom
\mathcal{V}_{m}
, there are only finitely many actions of the group
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
on the
2
-atom
\mathcal{V}_{m}
.

The Assertion
11
follows from the following simple statement.

Assertion 12
.

Let
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
be two actions of a finite group
G
on a
2
-atom
cV_{m}^{2}
that preserve the symplectic structure and the function defining the Liouville foliation (and hence the Liouville foliation itself).
Then, if the actions act in the same way on the set of one-dimensional edges of the singular leaf of the
2
-atom
\mathcal{V}_{m}^{2}
, then they coincide up to
an automorphism of the
2
-atom
\mathcal{V}_{m}^{2}
. In other words, there exists a Liouville equivalence
f:\mathcal{V}_{m}^{2}\to\mathcal{V}_{m}^{2}
such that for any
g\in G

\rho_{2}(g)=f^{-1}\circ\rho_{1}(g)\circ f
.

Essentially the proof of Assertion
12
is similar to the proof of Assertion 8 in
[
8
]
.

Proof of Assertion
12
.

By cutting the
2
-atom into ‘‘crosses’’ and then into smaller pieces, it is easy to construct homeomorphic
fundamental areas for
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
actions. The automorphism
f
identifies these domains and then extends to an equivariant mapping. Assertion
12
is proved.
∎

Remark 3
.

Not all mappings acting in the same way on the set of edges are conjugate. For example, shifts along Hamiltonian vector fields
act identically on the set of edges, but are not conjugate to the identity mapping. We used that the group
G
is finite, thus there does not exist an automorphism of
\mathcal{V}_{m}^{2}
of finite order, acting identically on the edges.

Remark 4
.

In Assertion
12
one cannot replace the leafwise homeomorphism
f
with a symplectomorphism.
2
-atoms have symplectic invariants, and the group
G
must preserve them.

Remark 5
.

The number of edge permutations is finite, so Assertion
11
can be generalized. Namely, similarly to Assertion
12
it turns out that actions are conjugate if they generate identical subgroups in
\Sym(\mathcal{V}_{m})
. In particular, one can consider
G\subset\Sym(\mathcal{V}_{m})
.

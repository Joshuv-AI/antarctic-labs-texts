C*-algebras of higher-rank graphs from groups acting on buildings, and explicit computation of their K-theory
Sam A. Mutter, Aura-Cristiana Radu, Alina Vdovina

Abstract
We unite elements of category theory, K-theory, and geometric group theory, by defining a class of groups called k k -cube groups, which act freely and transitively on the product of k k trees, for arbitrary k k . The quotient of this action on the product of trees defines a k k -dimensional cube complex, which induces a higher-rank graph. We make deductions about the K-theory of the corresponding rank- k k graph C ⋆ C^{\star} -algebras, and give examples of k k -cube groups and their K-theory. These are among the first explicit computations of K-theory for an infinite family of rank- k k graphs for k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 , which is not a direct consequence of the Künneth Theorem for tensor products.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a category such that
\Ob(\Lambda)
and
\Hom(\Lambda)
are countable sets (that is, a
countable small category
), and identify
\Ob(\Lambda)
with the identity morphisms in
\Hom(\Lambda)
. For a morphism
\lambda\in\Hom_{\Lambda}(u,v)
, we define range and source maps
r(\lambda):=v
and

respectively.

Let

be a functor, called the

, and let
\lambda\in\Hom(\Lambda)

(\Lambda,d)
a

graph
(or simply a

for some
\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, we can find
unique
elements
\mu,\nu\in\Hom(\Lambda)
such that
\lambda=\nu\mu
, and
d(\mu)=\mathbf{m}
,

. Note that for
\mu
,

to be composable, we must have
r(\mu)=s(\nu)
.

For
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, we write
\Lambda^{\mathbf{n}}:=d^{-1}(\mathbf{n})
; by the above property, we have that
\Lambda^{\mathbf{0}}=\Ob(\Lambda)
, and we call the elements of
\Lambda^{\mathbf{0}}
the

of
(\Lambda,d)

[
18
]
.

Let
(\Lambda,d)
be a rank-
k
graph, let
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, and let

. Write
v\Lambda^{\mathbf{n}}
for the set of morphisms in

which map onto the vertex

v\Lambda^{\mathbf{n}}:=\{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\mathbf{n}}\mid r(\lambda)=v\}

(\Lambda,d)
is

if each set

is finite, and that

Remark 3.2
.

If

with

to

otherwise.

If

are directed graphs with the same vertex set, and such that their associated incidence matrices

commute and satisfy the Unique Common Extension Property, then we can construct a rank-

, as in

.

Let

Definition 3.6
.

Let

We make extensive use of Theorem 3.15 from
[

, displayed here as Theorem
4.2

k=3
or

. For

[

, and we derive analogous results for

and

The proofs make use of so-called
spectral sequences
, generalisations of chain complexes; we direct the unfamiliar reader to
[
22
]
for more detailed background information, but offer an overview here.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an Abelian category. A
spectral sequence (of homological type)
consists of a family
\{(E^{r},d^{r})\}
of bigraded objects

E^{r}:=\bigoplus_{p,q\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{p,q}^{r}

in
\mathcal{C}
, and maps

d^{r}:E_{p,q}^{r}\longrightarrow E_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r},\quad\text{and}\quad d^{r}:E_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}\longrightarrow E_{p,q}^{r},

called
differentials
, which are of bidegree
(-r,r-1)
, and which satisfy
d^{r}\circ d^{r}=0
. We insist that

E_{p,q}^{r+1}\cong H(E_{p,q}^{r}):=\frac{\ker\big(d^{r}:E_{p,q}^{r}\longrightarrow E_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}\big)}{\im\big(d^{r}:E_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}\longrightarrow E_{p,q}^{r}\big)}.

The collections
(E_{p,q}^{r})
for fixed
r
are known as the
sheets
of the spectral sequence. We move to the next sheet by taking the homology
H
, defined above. We call a spectral sequence
bounded
if the sequence of objects
E_{p,q}^{r}
stabilises as
r\rightarrow\infty
; we denote this limit by
E_{p,q}^{\infty}
, and call it the
stable value
.

We say that a bounded spectral sequence
converges
to a family of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules
\{\mathcal{K}_{*}\}
if there exists a finite ascending filtration of modules

0=F_{s}(\mathcal{K}_{*})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{p-1}(\mathcal{K}_{*})\subseteq F_{p}(\mathcal{K}_{*})\subseteq F_{p+1}(\mathcal{K}_{*})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{t}(\mathcal{K}_{*})=\mathcal{K}_{*},

(3)

and an isomorphism

E_{p,q}^{\infty}\cong F_{p}(\mathcal{K}_{p+q})/F_{p-1}(\mathcal{K}_{p+q}),

(4)

for every pair
(p,q)
.

Given a general chain complex
A:=\cdots\rightarrow A_{i+1}\overset{\partial_{i+1}}{\longrightarrow}A_{i}\overset{\partial_{i}}{\longrightarrow}A_{i-1}\rightarrow\cdots
, we frequently write
H_{i}(A)
to denote the
i
-th homology
\ker(\partial_{i})/\im(\partial_{i+1})
.

Theorem 4.2

(Evans 2008)
.

Define the sets

N_{l}:=\begin{cases}\big\{\boldsymbol{\mu}:=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{l})\in\{1,\ldots,k\}^{l}\mid\mu_{1}<\cdots<\mu_{l}\big\}&\text{if }l\in\{1,\ldots,k\},\\
\{*\}&\text{if }l=0,\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and for
l\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}\in N_{l}
, define

\boldsymbol{\mu}^{i}:=\begin{cases}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\hat{\mu}_{i},\ldots\mu_{l})\in N_{l-1}&\text{if }l>1,\\
*&\text{if }l=1.\end{cases}

Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph with no sources. Then there exists a spectral sequence
\{(E^{r},d^{r})\}
converging to
\mathcal{K}_{*}(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda))
with
E_{p,q}^{\infty}\cong E_{p,q}^{k+1}
, and

E_{p,q}^{2}\cong\begin{cases}H_{p}(\mathcal{D}_{k})&\text{if }p\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}\text{ and }q\text{ is even,}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{D}_{k}
is the chain complex with

(\mathcal{D}_{k})_{p}:=\begin{cases}\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in N_{p}}\mathbb{Z}\Lambda^{\mathbf{0}}&\text{if }p\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\},\\

and whose differentials

\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in N_{p}}m_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\longmapsto\bigoplus_{\lambda\in N_{p-1}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in N_{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{i+1}\delta_{\lambda,\boldsymbol{\mu}^{i}}(I-M_{\mu_{i}}^{T})m_{\boldsymbol{\mu}},

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
k
-cube group, and let
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)
be its induced rank-
k

\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)
is strongly connected in the sense that, for any two vertices

]

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
be a
k
-cube group with adjacency structure
E_{1},\ldots,E_{k}
, and consider a pointed
k
-cube
S
in the cube complex
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
. Let

be the
k
-cube obtained by reflecting

through the edges labelled by elements of

, leaving the basepoint and orientation the same as in

(Figure
2
).

Firstly, we aim to show that there is a sequence of
k
-cubes

such that

for all

-cube is

-adjacent to the next.

Each

-cube

in

contains two
(k-1)
-faces (that is,
(k-1)
-sub-cubes) labelled by elements of

. Since the

-cubes have a predetermined orientation, we may label these faces

and

. We may therefore assign to each

Observe that each
(k-1)
-cube appears as

(resp.

) for some

precisely
|E_{1}|
times, and that, by assumption,

. Consider

be a pointed, oriented

Assume then that

, and let

In the same manner, we may show that there is a sequence of adjacent

-cubes connecting each

Now, we construct the set

|E_{2}|

We may proceed inductively to find that

6
Higher-rank graphs arising as double covers of cube complexes

In this section, we deduce information about the K-theory of a certain class of rank-
k
graphs with two vertices, which arise as double covers of the cube complexes discussed above. Whereas the rank-
k
graphs
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)
had vertices labelled by the
k
-cubes of
\Gamma
, these rank-
k
graphs
\Lambda
have vertices labelled by the vertices
(v,0),(v,1)
of the double cover of the complex
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
, where
v
is the single vertex of
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
. For further detail on how these graphs arise, we direct the reader to
[
20
, §8]
, but we outline the process here.

Recall the cube complex
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
, which has one vertex
v
, and has as cover the product of
k
trees by Proposition
2.10
and Example
2.12
. Let
\ell:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2
be a labelling of the elements of
\Gamma
. Then we obtain a cover
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}^{2}
of
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
with vertex set
\{v\}\times(\mathbb{Z}/2)
, and edge set
\Gamma\times(\mathbb{Z}/2)
. In
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}^{2}
, for a given element
a\in\Gamma
, either
(a,0)
and
(a,1)
are loops based at
(v,0)
,
(v,1)
respectively, or
(a,0)
goes from
(v,1)
to
(v,0)
and
(a,1)
from
(v,0)
to
(v,1)
.

We therefore have the following construction. For some non-negative integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}
and
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}
, not necessarily distinct, consider the matrices

D_{i}:=\begin{bmatrix}2m_{i}&0\\
0&2m_{i}\end{bmatrix},\qquad T_{i}:=\begin{bmatrix}0&2n_{i}\\
2n_{i}&0\end{bmatrix}.

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, we let
M_{i}
equal one of
D_{i}
or
T_{i}
. Provided that we have at least one
T_{i}
, we can construct a rank-
k
graph
\Lambda
with our choices of
M_{i}
as incidence matrices (Figure
8
). Such rank-
k
graphs are clearly cofinal and satisfy the Aperiodicity Condition, so their graph

-algebras are uniquely determined by their K-theory.

Our aim is to apply Evans’ result (Proposition
4.3
above) to compute the K-theory of the corresponding rank-
3
graph algebras
\Lambda
in each case, for all
m_{i},n_{i}\geq 2
. To do this, we must investigate the Smith normal forms of the boundary maps
\partial_{i}
. We do so firstly for

, in order to illustrate the method for arbitrary
k
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
k=3
, and let
\Lambda
be one of the three possible rank-
3
graphs constructed as above, namely one with incidence matrices

(a)

M_{1}=T_{1}
,
M_{2}=D_{2}
,
M_{3}=D_{3}
,

(b)

M_{1}=T_{1}
,
M_{2}=T_{2}
,
M_{3}=D_{3}
,

(c)

M_{1}=T_{1}
,
M_{2}=T_{2}
,
M_{3}=T_{3}
,

up to reordering. Write

a_{i}:=\begin{cases}1-2m_{i}&\text{ whenever }M_{i}=D_{i},\\
1-4n_{i}^{2}&\text{ whenever }M_{i}=T_{i}.\end{cases}

Recall the matrices
\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3}
from Proposition
4.3
, and write
I_{2},0_{2}
to denote the
2\times 2
identity and zero matrix, respectively. Then the Smith normal forms of the
\partial_{i}
are given by

S(\partial_{1})=S(\partial_{3})^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&g&0&0&0&0\end{bmatrix},\quad\text{and}\quad S(\partial_{2})=\begin{bmatrix}I_{2}&0_{2}&0_{2}\\
0_{2}&gI_{2}&0_{2}\\
0_{2}&0_{2}&0_{2}\end{bmatrix},

where
g:=\gcd(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
.

Figure 8:
The (
1
-skeleton of the)
3
-graph with two vertices and incidence matrices
\big[\begin{smallmatrix}4&0\\
0&4\end{smallmatrix}\big]
,

\big[\begin{smallmatrix}0&2\\
2&0\end{smallmatrix}\big]
, and

\big[\begin{smallmatrix}0&2\\
2&0\end{smallmatrix}\big]
, respectively represented by blue, magenta, and yellow arrows.

The proof relies on an argument based on the
Cauchy–Binet Theorem
: for a matrix
\partial
of rank
r
, the product of the invariant factors of the Smith normal form
S(\partial)
is equal to the greatest common divisor of all of the determinants of the
r\times r
minors of
\partial
.

Proof.

Consider the matrices
\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3}
in each of the three cases.
In case (a), we have

\partial_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&-2n_{1}&1-2m_{2}&0&1-2m_{3}&0\\
-2n_{1}&1&0&1-2m_{2}&0&1-2m_{3}\end{bmatrix}.

It is known that
\partial_{1}
and
\partial_{3}^{T}
have the same Smith normal form, so it suffices to check
S(\partial_{1})
. The matrix
\partial_{1}
has rank
2
, and the non-zero determinants of its
2\times 2
minors are given by

\begin{gathered}1-4n_{1}^{2},\,(1-2m_{2})^{2},\,(1-2m_{3})^{2},\,1-2m_{2},\,\\
1-2m_{3},\,2n_{1}(1-2m_{2}),\,2n_{1}(1-2m_{3}),\,(1-2m_{2})(1-2m_{3}).\end{gathered}

Let
g
be the greatest common divisor of these determinants. Then, after some relatively harmless algebra, we find that
g=\gcd(1-4n_{1}^{2},1-2m_{2},m_{2}-m_{3})
.

For
\partial_{2}
, which has rank
4
, we compute the determinants of all
4\times 4
minors:

\begin{gathered}(1-4n_{1}^{2})^{2},\,(1-2n_{2})^{4},\,(1-2m_{3})^{4},\,\\
(1-4n_{1}^{2})(1-2n_{2})^{2},\,(1-4n_{1}^{2})(1-2m_{3})^{2},\,(1-2n_{2})^{2}(1-2m_{3})^{2},\end{gathered}

and find that the greatest common divisor of these is equal to

. Hence

S(\partial_{1})=S(\partial_{3})^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&g&0&0&0&0\end{bmatrix},\quad\text{and}\quad S(\partial_{2})=\begin{bmatrix}I_{2}&0_{2}&0_{2}\\
0_{2}&gI_{2}&0_{2}\\
0_{2}&0_{2}&0_{2}\end{bmatrix},

where
I_{2},0_{2}
are the
2\times 2
identity matrix and zero matrix, respectively. Cases (b) and (c) are shown in a similar manner.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
k=3
, let
\Lambda
be one of the three possible rank-
3
graphs constructed as above, and let
g
be the corresponding value as defined in Lemma
6.1
. Then

(i)

If
g=1
, then
K_{0}(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda))\cong K_{1}(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda))\cong 0
,

(ii)

If
g\geq 2
, then
K_{1}(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda))\cong(\mathbb{Z}/g)\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/g)
, and
K_{0}(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda))
is isomorphic to a group of order
g^{2}
.

Proof.

If
g=1
, then
\coker(\partial_{1})\cong\ker(\partial_{3})\cong 0
, and we may apply Corollary
4.4
(i). Using the Smith normal form to calculate the homologies, we find that
K_{0}(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda))\cong K_{1}(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda))\cong 0
.

If
g\geq 2
, then
\coker(\partial_{1})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/g)
, and
\ker(\partial_{3})\cong 0
. Once again, we apply Proposition
4.3
to obtain the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/g)\longrightarrow K_{0}(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda))\longrightarrow\ker(\partial_{2})/\im(\partial_{1})\longrightarrow 0,

and the isomorphism
K_{1}(\mathcal{A}(\Lambda))\cong\ker(\partial_{1})/\im(\partial_{2})
. It is well known (consult, for example,
[
8
]
) that, when the differentials are finitely-generated, the homologies of a chain complex can be computed via the formula

\ker(\partial_{i})/\im(\partial_{i+1})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{c-r-s}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}(\mathbb{Z}/a_{j}),

where
c
is the number of columns of
\partial_{i}
,
r:=\rk(\partial_{i+1})
,
s:=\rk(\partial_{i})
, and
a_{j}
are the non-zero entries of
S(\partial_{i+1})
. In our example, this gives

\ker(\partial_{1})/\im(\partial_{2})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/g)\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/g),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ker(\partial_{2})/\im(\partial_{3})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/g),

and the result follows.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\Lambda
be one of the
k
possible rank-
k
graphs constructed as above, with incidence matrices
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}
. Write

a_{i}:=\begin{cases}1-2m_{i}&\text{ whenever }M_{i}=D_{i},\\
1-4n_{i}^{2}&\text{ whenever }M_{i}=T_{i},\end{cases}

and
g:=\gcd(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
. Recall the matrices
\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{k}
from Theorem
4.2
, and write
I_{s}
to denote the
s\times s
identity matrix. Then the Smith normal forms of the matrices
\partial_{i}
are given by

S(\partial_{1})=S(\partial_{k})^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\mathbf{0}\\
0&g&\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}_{2\times 2k},

and for
2\leq i\leq k-1
, the rank of
\partial_{i}
is equal to
2R:=2{k-1\choose i-1}
, and

S(\partial_{i})=\begin{bmatrix}I_{R}&0&\mathbf{0}\\
0&gI_{R}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}_{2{k\choose i-1}\times 2{k\choose i}}.

Proof.

The main argument of the proof is that, knowing the non-trivial invariant factor of
S(\partial_{1})=S(\partial_{k})^{T}
, along with the rank of each
\partial_{i}
, suffices to completely determine the Smith normal form of these matrices.

Firstly, we compute
S(\partial_{1})=S(\partial_{k})^{T}
in the same manner as Lemma
6.1
. Secondly, we determine the shape (and hence the rank) of the matrices
\partial_{2},\ldots,\partial_{k-1}
. Recall from Theorem
4.2
the sets

N_{l}:=\begin{cases}\big\{\boldsymbol{\mu}:=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{l})\in\{1,\ldots,k\}^{l}\mid\mu_{1}<\cdots<\mu_{l}\big\}&\text{if }l\in\{1,\ldots,k\},\\
\{*\}&\text{if }l=0,\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

From now on, we treat
\partial_{i}
exclusively as block matrices, with shape
|N_{i-1}|\times|N_{i}|
, and which comprise
2\times 2
blocks which are either zero or
\pm(I_{2}-M_{j}^{T})
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Notice that
N_{i}
has
(\begin{smallmatrix}k\\
i\end{smallmatrix})
elements, each a strictly-increasing tuple of length
i
whose entries belong to
\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Thus we consider
N_{i}
and
N_{i-1}
as ordered sets, with
\boldsymbol{\mu}>\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\prime}
if there is some
r
such that
\mu_{r}>\mu_{r}^{\prime}
and
\mu_{t}=\mu_{t}^{\prime}
for
t<r
. We label the (blocks of the) rows and columns of each matrix
\partial_{i}
with the elements, in order, of
N_{i-1}
and
N_{i}
respectively, writing
\boldsymbol{\mu}(p)\in N_{i-1}
for the label of row
p
, and
\boldsymbol{\nu}(q)\in N_{i}
for the label of column
q
.

Denote by
\partial_{i}(p,q)
a block of
\partial_{i}
in position
(p,q)
. Then
\partial_{i}(p,q)
is either
\pm(I_{2}-M_{j}^{T})
for some
j
, or the
2\times 2
zero matrix. It is non-zero if and only if
\boldsymbol{\mu}(p)
can be obtained by deleting one element, say
\nu(q)_{t}
, of
\boldsymbol{\nu}(q)
. Furthermore, the value of
\nu(q)_{t}
determines the index of the matrix
M_{j}
, and the position
t
determines the sign of
\partial_{i}(p,q)
as follows:

\partial_{i}(p,q)=\begin{cases}I_{2}-M_{j}^{T}&\text{if $t$ is odd,}\\
M_{j}^{T}-I_{2}&\text{if $t$ is even.}\end{cases}

Using this interpretation of the blocks of
\partial_{i}
, we conclude that the number of non-zero blocks is equal to
|N_{i-1}|\cdot(k-(i-1))=|N_{i}|\cdot(k-i)
.

It remains to deduce the rank of each matrix
\partial_{i}
. Firstly, observe that

\rk(\partial_{i})\leq\min 2\bigg\{{k\choose i-1},{k\choose i}\bigg\}.

By construction of
\partial_{i}
, there exists a
2(\begin{smallmatrix}k-1\\
i-1\end{smallmatrix})\times 2(\begin{smallmatrix}k-1\\
i-1\end{smallmatrix})
minor of
\partial_{i}
, with block-elements
I_{2}-M_{1}^{T}
on the diagonal, obtained by considering those column-blocks labelled by elements
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in N_{i}
with
\nu_{1}=1
, and row-blocks labelled by elements
\boldsymbol{\mu}\in N_{i-1}
with
\mu_{1}\neq 1
. There are

{k\choose i}-{k-1\choose i}={k-1\choose i-1},\quad\text{and}\quad{k\choose i-1}-{k-1\choose i-2}={k-1\choose i-1}

such column- and row-blocks, respectively, and so

\rk(\partial_{i})\geq 2{k-1\choose i-1}.

We claim that this is in fact an equality, and we display here an outline of the proof. Suppose that there exists a non-vanishing minor
A
which is larger than the one above. Given the number of non-zero blocks, and the fact that minors are square matrices, the diagonal (or when relevant, the anti-diagonal) of
A
must not contain any zero blocks, else
A
vanishes. Using elementary row operations, we take the last
(\begin{smallmatrix}k-1\\
i-1\end{smallmatrix})
row-blocks of
\partial_{i}
and move them to the top, such that the elements
\boldsymbol{\mu}\in N_{i-1}
with
\mu_{1}=1
now label the rows at the bottom of
\partial_{i}
. Hence we must be able to find a column labelled by an element
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in N_{i}
such that
\nu_{1}\neq 1
, and from which we can find the
\boldsymbol{\mu}
, but this is a contradiction.

Indeed, by some careful considerations, it turns out that regardless of the row operations performed, we will arrive at such a contradiction. Thus
\rk(\partial_{i})=2{k-1\choose i-1}
, and from this the result is readily verified.
∎

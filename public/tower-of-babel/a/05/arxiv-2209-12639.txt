Equilibrium pricing of securities in the co-presence of cooperative and non-cooperative populations Forthcoming in ESAIM: Control, Optimisation and Calculus of Variations .
Masaaki Fujii

Abstract
In this work, we develop an equilibrium model for price formation of securities in a market composed of two populations of different types: the first one consists of cooperative agents, while the other one consists of non-cooperative agents. The trading of every cooperative member is assumed to be coordinated by a central planner. In the large population limit, the problem for the central planner is shown to be a conditional extended mean-field control. In addition to the convexity assumptions, if the relative size of the cooperative population is small enough, then we are able to show the existence of a unique equilibrium for both the finite-agent and the mean-field models. The strong convergence to the mean-field model is also proved under the same conditions.



Keywords :

market clearing, mean-field games, extended mean-field control, controlled-FBSDEs

Introduction

In this paper, we study the problem of equilibrium price formation by considering, as an example, a simple model of
a securities market. The prices of securities
have always been the center of interest of many people for centuries.
They inspire people to consider their properties not only for speculative reasons but also
for true academic interests, such as how they are determined, how they behave in a given environment,
how they are influenced by preferences and the level of risk-averseness of investors, and so on.
In fact, since the appearance of modern securities markets, the huge amount of price data they provide
has induced the significant developments of related academic areas,
such as financial mathematics, financial economics, statistical inference etc.
However, despite its importance, we are still quite far from the complete understanding of a price process
even when we restrict our attention to a standard liquid stock.
The main reason behind this difficulty is that any price process is inevitably affected by complicated interactions among
a large number of market participants, which includes various financial firms, individual investors,
governments and even supernational institutions.

Along side of its theoretical developments, MFGs have successfully found vast applications in various
areas. In particular, energy and financial markets have been popular targets for analysis.
As is well known, any price in these free competitive markets is
determined so that it balances the supply and demand of the asset.
Unfortunately, this
market clearing condition
does not fit well to the concept of Nash equilibrium.
In fact, if we shift a control of one agent away from her equilibrium strategy
while keeping the others unchanged, then the market clearing condition would be broken down.
Since the MFG theory has been developed primarily for the analysis of Nash equilibrium,
a standard approach in the literature assumes a specific structure of the price process without
imposing the market clearing condition. A popular way is to hypothesize
that the price process is decomposed into two parts; one is a so-called fundamental price
whose dynamics is exogenously given
and the other is a price-impact term whose size is affected by actions of agents in a specific manner.
Another way is to suppose that a price is given by a certain function

3
Price formation in a finite-agent market

In this section, we consider the price formation problem in a finite-agent market composed of two populations:
the first one consists of
N_{1}
cooperative agents, and the second one consists of
N_{2}
non-cooperative agents.
We often use
i
(respectively,
j
) to index an agent in the first (respectively, second) population.
Each agent represents a registered financial firm trading at a common securities exchange.
She is supposed to have many individual clients who cannot directly access to the exchange, and she provides
financial services by using her own inventory to answer the sale and purchase requests from those clients. This is the so-called
over-the-counter (OTC) market. Therefore, every agent faces the stochastic order flows from her OTC clients
in addition to idiosyncratic as well as common market shocks. Under such an environment,
every agent carries out the optimal continuous trading at the exchange to minimize her cost functional.
For the members of the first population, we assume that every trading is coordinated by a central planner.

We suppose that there are
n
tradable securities in the market, whose prices are denoted by
(\varpi_{t}^{k})_{k=1}^{n}
.
The goal of the paper is to determine the price process
\varpi:=(\varpi_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
endogenously so that it clears the market, i.e.
matches the supply and demand of securities at the exchange.
Unless otherwise stated, we will work on the filtered probability space
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
defined in the last section.
By construction,
(\xi^{i})_{i=1}^{N_{1}}
and also
(\eta^{j})_{j=1}^{N_{2}}
are independently and identically distributed (i.i.d.).
Let us introduce the following processes to describe the various types of information:

•

c^{0}:=(c_{t}^{0})_{t\in[0,T]}
belongs to
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{0};\mathbb{R}^{m})
with
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|c_{t}^{0}|^{2}]<\infty
,
which is used to represent common market information including the cash flows from the securities.

•

For
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
,
c^{i}:=(c_{t}^{i})_{t\in[0,T]}
belongs to
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}^{1,i};\mathbb{R}^{m})
with

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|c_{t}^{i}|^{2}]<\infty
. They are used to represent idiosyncratic shocks for the first population, and

(c^{i})_{i=1}^{N_{1}}
are i.i.d.

•

For
1\leq j\leq N_{2}
,
\mathfrak{c}^{j}:=(\mathfrak{c}_{t}^{j})_{t\in[0,T]}
belongs to
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}^{2,j};\mathbb{R}^{m})

with
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|\mathfrak{c}_{t}^{j}|^{2}]<\infty
. They are used to represent idiosyncratic shocks for the second population,
and
(\mathfrak{c}^{j})_{j=1}^{N_{2}}
are i.i.d.

Here, the dimension
m\in\mathbb{N}
is commonly chosen just for notational simplicity.

Before going to study the details of the optimization problem faced by each population separately,
let us summarize the important features of each problem here.

•

The optimization problem for the second population
1\leq j\leq N_{2}
:

–

Each agent
j
tries to minimize her cost functional by continuous trading at the exchange (see
(\ref {non-c-problem})
).

–

Her cost functional (see
(\ref {2nd-cost-functional})
) depends on her trading speed, her inventory size, the price of securities
as well as the shocks induced by
(c^{0},\mathfrak{c}^{j})
.

–

She is just a price taker (see Definition
3.1
) and hence treats the price of securities
\varpi
as an externally given stochastic process.

–

Each agent
1\leq j\leq N_{2}
solves her own problem independently. In particular, she does not care about the actions of the other agents.

•

The optimization problem for the first population
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
:

–

The cost functional (see
(\ref {1st-cost-functional})
) of each agent
i
depends on her trading speed, her inventory size,
the empirical distribution of the inventories for the first population, the price of securities
as wall as the shocks induced by
(c^{0},c^{i})
.

–

They try to minimize the
sum
of the cost functionals of the whole population
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
by continuous trading
at the exchange, which is fully coordinated by a central planner (see
(\ref {central-problem})
).

–

The first population as a whole has non-negligible market share and hence
the actions of the central planner can influence the price of securities
\varpi
(see
(\ref {vp-expression-1})
),
which in turn affects the actions of the second population.

–

Assuming the agents in the second population rationally react to the price change,
the central planner solves the cost minimization problem with those reactions taken into account.

It is immediate to see that the problem of each agent in the second population is a usual stand-alone optimization.
Hence it makes perfect sense to start our investigation from solving this problem.
The optimization problem for the central planner of the first population becomes much more complicated.
We will see that the market clearing condition (see Definition
3.2
) connects her trading actions
and the price impacts. Through the reactions to the price change, the central planner must control
not only the actions of the first population but also those of the second population indirectly.
This results in stochastic control of a large system of FBSDEs instead of an SDE.

Remark 3.5
.

It is interesting to observe an economic role played by the term
\rho_{2}(t)\varpi_{t}
.
Since
\rho_{2}
is positive, it increases the inflow of securities to the agents’ inventory
when the price
\varpi
rises. This is equivalent to the increase of the sales from the OTC clients.
Since agents (i.e. financial firms) are trying to maintain the optimal inventory level, this
reduces the demand of the securities among the financial firms. Assumption
3.2
(v) guarantees
that this is stronger than the opposite effect caused by the mark-to-market term
-b_{t}\langle x,\varpi\rangle
.
By making the demand for securities react in an opposite way to the price level, the term
\rho_{2}(t)\varpi_{t}

seems to prevent market bubbles/crashes from happening and allow the equilibrium to exist for general duration
T
.

(\rho_{1}(t)\varpi_{t}(y_{t},\beta_{t})+l_{1}^{i}(t))dt

\small\begin{split}J_{1}^{i}(\beta):=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\nu_{t}^{N_{1}},\varpi_{t}(y_{t},\beta_{t}),\beta_{t}^{i})dt+g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{i},\nu_{T}^{N_{1}})\Bigr],\end{split}

We suppose that all the trading actions in the first population are coordinated by the central planner,

Before giving the set of main assumptions, let us mention about the concept of differentiability of functions defined on the
space of probability measures. In this work, we adopt the notion of
L
-differentiability used in
[
14
,
15
]
,
which was first introduced by P.L.Lions in his lecture at College de France.
Here, the differentiation is based on the lifting of function
{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\ni\mu\mapsto u(\mu)\in\mathbb{R}
to
a function
\widetilde{u}
defined on the Hilbert space
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})
by
\widetilde{u}(X):=u({\cal L}(X))

with
X\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Here, the probability space
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
is chosen
arbitrarily with a Polish set
\Omega
and an atomless probability measure
\mathbb{P}
to support the random variable

X
with
{\cal L}(X)=\mu
. The
L
-derivative of
u(\mu)
is defined as the Frechet derivative of the lifted function
\widetilde{u}(X)
.

Definition 3.4

(Definition 5.22 in
[
14
]
)
.

A function
u
on
{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be
L
-differentiable at
\mu_{0}\in{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if there exists
a random variable
X
with law
\mu_{0}
such that the lifted function
\widetilde{u}
is Frechet differentiable at
X
.

We distinguish the Frechet derivative by using the symbol
D
.
By
[
14
, Proposition 5.24]
, if
u
is
L
-differentiable at
\mu_{0}
, the
\widetilde{u}
is
Frechet differentiable at any
\acute{X}
with
{\cal L}(\acute{X})=\mu_{0}
and the law of the pair
(\acute{X},D\widetilde{u}(\acute{X}))

is independent of the choice of the random variable
\acute{X}
. Thus, the
L
-derivative may be denoted by

\partial_{\mu}u(\mu_{0})(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni x\mapsto\partial_{\mu}u(\mu_{0})(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is uniquely defined
\mu_{0}
-a.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
By its definition, the
L
-derivative satisfies

\small\begin{split}u(\mu)=u(\mu_{0})+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle X-X_{0},\partial_{\mu}u(\mu_{0})(X_{0})\rangle\bigr]+o(\|X-X_{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}),\end{split}

for any random variables
X
and
X_{0}
with
{\cal L}(X)=\mu
and
{\cal L}(X_{0})=\mu_{0}
.
For example, if the function
u
is of the form
u(\mu):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)\mu(dx)
for some function

h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we have
\widetilde{u}(X)=\mathbb{E}[h(X)]
with
{\cal L}(X)=\mu
.
If the function
h
is differentiable, then the definition of
L
-derivative implies that
\partial_{\mu}u(\mu)(\cdot)=\partial_{x}h(\cdot)
.
See
[
14
,
15
]
for details and many more examples.

In our analysis, in particular, functions on empirical distributions play a special role.
Suppose that a function
u:{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given.
By considering the map
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}\ni(x^{i})_{i=1}^{N}\mapsto u\Bigr(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x^{i}}\Bigr)\in\mathbb{R}
,

u
can also be seen as a function on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}
.
If
u:{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
L
-differentiable,

[
14
, Proposition 5.35]
tells that the function

, when seen as a function on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N}
, is differentiable for each

and satisfies

\small\begin{split}\partial_{x^{i}}u(x^{1},\ldots,x^{N})=\frac{1}{N}\partial_{\mu}u\Bigl(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x^{i}}\Bigr)(x^{i}).\end{split}

(
3.11)

Here, with slight abuse of notations, we used the same symbol
u
to denote the two maps.

\small\begin{split}&H(t,\acute{u},\acute{\beta})-H(t,u,\beta)\\

\small\begin{split}&\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\langle\Delta F_{P^{i}}(t),\Delta X^{i}\rangle=-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\langle\partial_{x}f_{1}^{i}(t,u,\widehat{\beta}^{i})-\partial_{x}f_{1}^{i}(t,\acute{u},\widehat{\beta}^{\prime,i}),\Delta X^{i}\rangle\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\mathbb{E}^{\theta}\bigl[\langle\partial_{\mu}f_{1}^{i}(t,u,\widehat{\beta}^{i})(X^{\theta})-\partial_{\mu}f_{1}^{i}(t,\acute{u},\widehat{\beta}^{\prime,i})(\acute{X}^{\theta}),\Delta X^{\theta}\rangle\bigr].\end{split}

Finally, as a direct result from Assumption
3.2
(iv), we have

\small\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\langle\Delta F_{y^{j}}(t),\Delta x^{j}\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\langle(-1)\Delta F_{p^{j}}(t),\Delta r^{j}\rangle\leq-\gamma_{2}^{f}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta x^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta r^{j}|^{2})+N_{2}b_{t}\langle\Delta\varpi,\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta x)\rangle.\end{split}

Summing all the terms and using Assumption
3.3
(iv), we get

\small\begin{split}{\cal D}(t)\leq-\gamma_{1}^{f}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta X^{i}|^{2}-\gamma_{2}^{f}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta x^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta r^{j}|^{2})-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}(\lambda_{\beta}|\Delta\widehat{\beta}^{i}|^{2}+\lambda_{\varpi}|\Delta\varpi|^{2})+{\cal R}(t),\end{split}

(
3.17)

where the residual term
{\cal R}(t)
is defined by

\small\begin{split}{\cal R}(t)&:=-N_{2}\rho_{2}(t)(|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y)|^{2}+|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta p)|^{2})-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\langle\partial_{\varpi}f_{1}^{i}(t,u,\widehat{\beta}^{i})-\partial_{\varpi}f_{1}^{i}(t,\acute{u},\widehat{\beta}^{\prime,i}),\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y+\Delta p)\rangle\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+N_{1}\langle(1-\rho_{2}(t)\Lambda_{t})\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta p-\Delta y)-b_{t}\Lambda_{t}\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta r),\mathfrak{m}_{1}(\Delta\widehat{\beta})\rangle-N_{1}\rho_{1}(t)\langle\mathfrak{m}_{1}(\Delta P),\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y+\Delta p)\rangle\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt-N_{2}b_{t}\langle\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta r),\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta p)\rangle+N_{2}b_{t}\langle\Delta\varpi,\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta x)\rangle.\end{split}

We get an estimate on
{\cal R}(t)
from the Lipschitz continuity of
\partial_{\varpi}f_{1}^{i}
and Young’s inequality. In particular,

\small\begin{split}&N_{1}|\rho_{1}(t)\langle\mathfrak{m}_{1}(\Delta P),\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y+\Delta p)\rangle|\leq N_{2}\frac{\rho_{2}(t)}{2}(|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y)|^{2}+|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta p)|^{2})+\frac{N_{1}^{2}\rho_{1}(t)^{2}}{N_{2}\rho_{2}(t)}\Bigl(\frac{1}{N_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta P^{i}|^{2}\Bigr)\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt\leq N_{2}\frac{\rho_{2}(t)}{2}(|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y)|^{2}+|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta p)|^{2})+C\frac{N_{1}}{N_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta P^{i}|^{2},\\
&N_{2}b_{t}|\langle\Delta\varpi,\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta x)\rangle|\leq\frac{\gamma_{2}^{f}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}|\Delta x^{j}|^{2}+\frac{N_{2}b_{t}^{2}}{\gamma_{2}^{f}}|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y)|^{2}+C\frac{N_{1}}{N_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta\widehat{\beta}^{i}|^{2}\end{split}

with some constant
C
. Similar calculations yield

\small\begin{split}{\cal R}(t)\leq&\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\bigl[\frac{\gamma_{1}^{f}}{2}|\Delta X^{i}|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\varpi}}{2}|\Delta\varpi|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\beta}}{2}|\Delta\widehat{\beta}^{i}|^{2}\bigr]+\frac{\gamma_{2}^{f}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta x^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta r^{j}|^{2})\\
&+CN_{1}(|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y)|^{2}+|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta p)|^{2}+|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta r)|^{2})+C\frac{N_{1}}{N_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}(|\Delta\widehat{\beta}^{i}|^{2}+|\Delta P^{i}|^{2})\\
&-N_{2}\frac{\rho_{2}(t)}{2}(|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y)|^{2}+|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta p)|^{2})+\frac{N_{2}b_{t}^{2}}{2\gamma_{2}^{f}}|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta p)|^{2}+\frac{N_{2}b_{t}^{2}}{\gamma_{2}^{f}}|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y)|^{2}\\
\leq&\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\bigl[\frac{\gamma_{1}^{f}}{2}|\Delta X^{i}|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\varpi}}{2}|\Delta\varpi|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\beta}}{2}|\Delta\widehat{\beta}^{i}|^{2}\bigr]+\frac{\gamma_{2}^{f}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta x^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta r^{j}|^{2})\\
&+CN_{1}(|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y)|^{2}+|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta p)|^{2}+|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta r)|^{2})+C\frac{N_{1}}{N_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}(|\Delta\widehat{\beta}^{i}|^{2}+|\Delta P^{i}|^{2}),\end{split}

(
3.18)

where we have used Assumption
3.3
(vi) in the second inequality.
Note that, the constant
C
depends on
L
and convexity parameters but not on
(N_{1},N_{2})
.
Now we choose
\delta_{0}^{F}:=\min\bigl(\frac{\lambda_{\beta}}{2C},\frac{\gamma_{2}^{f}}{6C}\bigr)
.
Then, for any
\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}=N_{1}/N_{2}
satisfying
\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\leq\delta_{0}^{F}
, we have

\small\begin{split}{\cal R}(t)\leq&\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\bigl[\frac{\gamma_{1}^{f}}{2}|\Delta X^{i}|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\varpi}}{2}|\Delta\varpi|^{2}+\lambda_{\beta}|\Delta\widehat{\beta}^{i}|^{2}\bigr]+\gamma_{2}^{f}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\bigl[\frac{1}{2}|\Delta x^{j}|^{2}+\frac{2}{3}|\Delta r^{j}|^{2}\bigr]\\
&+C\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta P^{i}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta y^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta p^{j}|^{2})\bigr].\end{split}

By inserting into
(\ref {eq-cald})
, we obtain the desired estimate on
{\cal D}(t)
.
∎

We now provide the first main result.
Since Peng-Wu’s monotonicity does not hold, we need in general to make all the Lipschitz constants small enough
to guarantee the well-posedness of FBSDEs for a given duration.
However, the result of Lemma
3.3
, which shows the violation of the monotonicity is proportional to
\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}
,
suggests that the well-posedness of the system
(\ref {fbsde-full-finite})
may be available as long as the ratio
\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}=N_{1}/N_{2}
is small enough.
We shall show that this is actually the case by the next theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let Assumptions
3.2
-
3.3
hold. Then, there exists some positive constant
\delta_{*}^{F}\leq\delta_{0}^{F}

such that, for any
(N_{1},N_{2})
satisfying
\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\leq\delta_{*}^{F}
, there exists a
unique square integrable solution to the system of FBSDEs
(\ref {fbsde-full-finite})
with

u:=(X,x,y,P,p,r)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N_{2}}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N_{2}}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N_{2}}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N_{2}}
.

Proof.

First, we put
\gamma:=\min\bigl(\frac{\gamma_{1}^{f}}{2},\frac{\gamma_{2}^{f}}{3},\gamma_{1}^{g},\gamma_{2}^{g}\bigr)
.
Let
I_{X^{i}},I_{x^{j}},I_{r^{j}},I_{P^{i}},I_{y^{j}},I_{p^{j}}
be in
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\theta_{P^{i}},\theta_{y^{j}},\theta_{p^{j}}
be in
\mathbb{L}^{2}({\cal F}_{T},\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
1\leq i\leq N_{1},1\leq j\leq N_{2}
.
We hypothesize that, for any choice of
(I_{X^{i}},I_{x^{j}},I_{y^{j}},I_{P^{i}},I_{p^{j}},I_{r^{j}})
and
(\theta_{P^{i}},\theta_{y^{j}},\theta_{p^{j}})
,
there exists some
\varrho\in[0,1)
such that
there exists a unique square integrable solution with
\overline{u}=(\overline{X},\overline{x},\overline{y},\overline{P},\overline{p},\overline{r})\in\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{2N_{1}+4N_{2}}

to the following system of FBSDEs,
1\leq i\leq N_{1},1\leq j\leq N_{2}
:

\small\begin{split}&d\overline{X}_{t}^{i}=(\varrho B_{X^{i}}(t,\overline{u}_{t})+I_{X^{i}}(t))dt+\sigma_{1}^{i,0}(t)dW_{t}^{0}+\sigma_{1}^{i}(t)dW_{t}^{i},\\
&d\overline{x}_{t}^{j}=(\varrho B_{x^{j}}(t,\overline{u}_{t})+I_{x^{j}}(t))dt+\sigma_{2}^{j,0}(t)dW_{t}^{0}+\sigma_{2}^{j}(t)dB_{t}^{j},\\
&d\overline{r}_{t}^{j}=(\varrho B_{r^{j}}(t,\overline{u}_{t})+I_{r^{j}}(t))dt,\\
&d\overline{P}_{t}^{i}=-\bigl[(1-\varrho)\gamma\overline{X}_{t}^{i}-\varrho F_{P^{i}}(t,\overline{u}_{t})+I_{P^{i}}(t)\bigr]dt+\overline{Q}_{t}^{i,0}dW_{t}^{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\overline{Q}_{t}^{i,k}dW_{t}^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{2}}\overline{\mathfrak{Q}}_{t}^{i,k}dB_{t}^{k},\\
&d\overline{y}_{t}^{j}=-\bigl[(1-\varrho)\gamma\overline{x}_{t}^{j}-\varrho F_{y^{j}}(t,\overline{u}_{t})+I_{y^{j}}(t)\bigr]dt+\overline{z}_{t}^{j,0}dW_{t}^{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\overline{\mathfrak{z}}_{t}^{j,k}dW_{t}^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{2}}\overline{z}_{t}^{j,k}dB_{t}^{k},\\
&d\overline{p}_{t}^{j}=-\bigl[-(1-\varrho)\gamma\overline{r}_{t}^{j}-\varrho F_{p^{j}}(t,\overline{u}_{t})+I_{p^{j}}(t)\bigr]dt+\overline{q}_{t}^{j,0}dW_{t}^{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\overline{\mathfrak{q}}_{t}^{j,k}dW_{t}^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{2}}\overline{q}_{t}^{j,k}dB_{t}^{k},\end{split}

with
\overline{X}_{0}^{i}=\xi^{i},~\overline{x}_{0}^{j}=\eta^{j},~\overline{r}_{0}^{j}=0
and

\overline{P}_{T}^{i}=\varrho G_{P^{i}}(\overline{u}_{T})+(1-\varrho)\overline{X}_{T}^{i}+\theta_{P^{i}},~\overline{y}_{T}^{j}=\varrho G_{y^{j}}(\overline{u}_{T})+(1-\varrho)\overline{x}_{T}^{j}+\theta_{y^{j}},~\overline{p}_{T}^{j}=\varrho G_{p^{j}}(\overline{u}_{T})-(1-\varrho)\overline{r}_{T}^{j}+\theta_{p^{j}}.

The hypothesis clearly holds when
\varrho=0
since then the system becomes the
decoupled
Lipschitz FBSDEs.

Now, with a given input process
\overline{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{2N_{1}+4N_{2}}
, we consider the
perturbed FBSDEs on
u=(X,x,y,P,p,r)
with
\zeta\in(0,1)
,
1\leq i\leq N_{1},1\leq j\leq N_{2}
:

\small\begin{split}&dX_{t}^{i}=\bigl[\varrho B_{X^{i}}(t,u_{t})+\zeta B_{X^{i}}(t,\overline{u}_{t})+I_{X^{i}}(t)\bigr]dt+\sigma_{1}^{i,0}(t)dW_{t}^{0}+\sigma_{1}^{i}(t)dW_{t}^{i},\\
&dx_{t}^{j}=\bigl[\varrho B_{x^{j}}(t,u_{t})+\zeta B_{x^{j}}(t,\overline{u}_{t})+I_{x^{j}}(t)\bigr]dt+\sigma_{2}^{j,0}(t)dW_{t}^{0}+\sigma_{2}^{j}(t)dB_{t}^{j},\\
&dr_{t}^{j}=\bigl[\varrho B_{r^{j}}(t,u_{t})+\zeta B_{r^{j}}(t,\overline{u}_{t})+I_{r^{j}}(t)\bigr]dt,\\
&dP_{t}^{i}=-\bigl[(1-\varrho)\gamma X_{t}^{i}-\varrho F_{P^{i}}(t,u_{t})+\zeta(-\gamma\overline{X}_{t}^{i}-F_{P^{i}}(t,\overline{u}_{t}))+I_{P^{i}}(t)\bigr]dt\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 9.24994pt+Q_{t}^{i,0}dW_{t}^{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}Q_{t}^{i,k}dW_{t}^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{2}}\mathfrak{Q}_{t}^{i,k}dB_{t}^{k},\\
&dy_{t}^{j}=-\bigl[(1-\varrho)\gamma x_{t}^{j}-\varrho F_{y^{j}}(t,u_{t})+\zeta(-\gamma\overline{x}_{t}^{j}-F_{y^{j}}(t,\overline{u}_{t}))+I_{y^{j}}(t)\bigr]dt\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 9.24994pt+z_{t}^{j,0}dW_{t}^{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\mathfrak{z}_{t}^{j,k}dW_{t}^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{2}}z_{t}^{j,k}dB_{t}^{k},\\
&dp_{t}^{j}=-\bigl[-(1-\varrho)\gamma r_{t}^{j}-\varrho F_{p^{j}}(t,u_{t})+\zeta(\gamma\overline{r}_{t}^{j}-F_{p^{j}}(t,\overline{u}_{t}))+I_{p^{j}}(t)\bigr]dt\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 9.24994pt+q_{t}^{j,0}dW_{t}^{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\mathfrak{q}_{t}^{j,k}dW_{t}^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{2}}q_{t}^{j,k}dB_{t}^{k},\end{split}

(
3.19)

with the boundary conditions
X_{0}^{i}=\xi^{i},~x^{j}_{0}=\eta^{j},~r_{0}^{j}=0
and

P_{T}^{i}=\varrho G_{P^{i}}(u_{T})+(1-\varrho)X_{T}^{i}+\zeta(G_{P^{i}}(\overline{u}_{T})-\overline{X}_{T}^{i})+\theta_{P^{i}}
,

y_{T}^{j}=\varrho G_{y^{j}}(u_{T})+(1-\varrho)x_{T}^{j}+\zeta(G_{y^{j}}(\overline{u}_{T})-\overline{x}_{T}^{j})+\theta_{y^{j}}

and
p_{T}^{j}=\varrho G_{p^{j}}(u_{T})-(1-\varrho)r_{T}^{j}+\zeta(G_{p^{j}}(\overline{u}_{T})+\overline{r}_{T}^{j})+\theta_{p^{j}}
.
Note that, by the initial hypothesis, there exists a unique solution
u=(X,x,y,P,p,r)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{2N_{1}+4N_{2}}

to the FBSDEs
(\ref {fbsde-shifted})
. This defines a map
\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{2N_{1}+4N_{2}}\ni\overline{u}\mapsto u\in\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{2N_{1}+4N_{2}}
. If we show that thie map is a contraction for some
\zeta>0
independent of
\varrho
,
then we can extend the hypothesis from
\varrho
to
(\varrho+\zeta)
. Then we are done, because
\varrho=1
and
I,\theta=0

give the interested FBSDEs
(\ref {fbsde-full-finite})
. In the following, we shall show that if
\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\leq\delta_{*}^{F}

with some
\delta_{*}^{F}>0
determined independently from
\varrho
, there exists a sufficiently small
\zeta>0
independent of
\varrho

such that the above map
\overline{u}\mapsto u
becomes a contraction as desired.

First step
:
Let us denote by
u,\acute{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{2N_{1}+4N_{2}}
the solutions to
(\ref {fbsde-shifted})

with two input processes
\overline{u},\acute{\overline{u}}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{2N_{1}+4N_{2}}
, respectively.
As in Lemmas
3.2
-
3.3
, we use the notations:

\Delta u:=u-\acute{u}
,
\Delta\overline{u}:=\overline{u}-\acute{\overline{u}}
,
\Delta B_{X^{i}}(t):=B_{X^{i}}(t,u_{t})-B_{X^{i}}(t,\acute{u}_{t})
,

\Delta\overline{B}_{X^{i}}(t):=B_{X^{i}}(t,\overline{u}_{t})-B_{X^{i}}(t,\acute{\overline{u}}_{t})
,
\Delta F_{P^{i}}(t):=F_{P^{i}}(t,u_{t})-F_{P^{i}}(t,\acute{u}_{t})
,

\Delta\overline{F}_{P^{i}}(t):=F_{P^{i}}(t,\overline{u}_{t})-F_{P^{i}}(t,\acute{\overline{u}}_{t})
,
\Delta G_{P^{i}}:=G_{P^{i}}(u_{T})-G_{P^{i}}(\acute{u}_{T})
,

\Delta\overline{G}_{P^{i}}:=G_{P^{i}}(\overline{u}_{T})-G_{P^{i}}(\acute{\overline{u}}_{T})
, etc.
An application of Itô formula with the result of Lemma
3.3
gives

\small\begin{split}&\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta X_{T}^{i},\Delta P_{T}^{i}\rangle\bigr]+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta x_{T}^{j},\Delta y_{T}^{j}\rangle+\langle(-1)\Delta r_{T}^{j},\Delta p_{T}^{j}\rangle\bigr]\\
&\leq-\gamma\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta X_{t}^{i}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta x_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta r_{t}^{j}|^{2})\bigr]dt\\
&+\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta P_{t}^{i}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta y_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta p_{t}^{j}|^{2})\bigr]dt+\zeta\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\bigl[\langle\Delta\overline{B}_{X^{i}}(t),\Delta P_{t}^{i}\rangle+\langle\gamma\Delta\overline{X}_{t}^{i}+\Delta\overline{F}_{P^{i}}(t),\Delta X_{t}^{i}\rangle\bigr]dt\\
&+\zeta\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\bigl[\langle\Delta\overline{B}_{x^{j}}(t),\Delta y_{t}^{j}\rangle+\langle\gamma\Delta\overline{x}_{t}^{j}+\Delta\overline{F}_{y^{j}}(t),\Delta x_{t}^{j}\rangle+\langle(-1)\Delta\overline{B}_{r^{j}}(t),\Delta p_{t}^{j}\rangle+\langle\gamma\Delta\overline{r}_{t}^{j}-\Delta\overline{F}_{p^{j}}(t),\Delta r_{t}^{j}\rangle\bigr]dt.\end{split}

We also have, from Lemma
3.2
,

\small\begin{split}&\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta X_{T}^{i},\Delta P_{T}^{i}\rangle\bigr]+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta x_{T}^{j},\Delta y_{T}^{j}\rangle+\langle(-1)\Delta r_{T}^{j},\Delta p_{T}^{j}\rangle\bigr]\\
&\geq(\varrho\gamma+(1-\varrho))\mathbb{E}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta X_{T}^{i}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta x_{T}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta r_{T}^{j}|^{2})\bigr]\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\zeta\mathbb{E}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\langle\Delta\overline{G}_{P^{i}}-\Delta\overline{X}_{T}^{i},\Delta X_{T}^{i}\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(\langle\Delta\overline{G}_{y^{j}}-\Delta\overline{x}_{T}^{j},\Delta x_{T}^{j}\rangle+\langle\Delta\overline{G}_{p^{j}}+\Delta\overline{r}_{T}^{j},(-1)\Delta r_{T}^{j}\rangle\bigr].\end{split}

Put
\gamma_{c}:=\min(1,\gamma)
, which satisfies
0<\gamma_{c}\leq\varrho\gamma+(1-\varrho)
, then the above two inequalities yield

\small\begin{split}&\gamma_{c}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta X_{T}^{i}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta x_{T}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta r_{T}^{j}|^{2})\Bigr]+\gamma_{c}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta X_{t}^{i}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta x_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta r_{t}^{j}|^{2})\bigr]dt\\
&\leq\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta P_{t}^{i}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta y_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta p_{t}^{j}|^{2})\bigr]dt+\zeta\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\bigl[\langle\Delta\overline{B}_{X^{i}}(t),\Delta P_{t}^{i}\rangle+\langle\gamma\Delta\overline{X}_{t}^{i}+\Delta\overline{F}_{P^{i}}(t),\Delta X_{t}^{i}\rangle\bigr]dt\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\zeta\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\bigl[\langle\Delta\overline{B}_{x^{j}}(t),\Delta y_{t}^{j}\rangle+\langle\gamma\Delta\overline{x}_{t}^{j}+\Delta\overline{F}_{y^{j}}(t),\Delta x_{t}^{j}\rangle+\langle(-1)\Delta\overline{B}_{r^{j}}(t),\Delta p_{t}^{j}\rangle+\langle\gamma\Delta\overline{r}_{t}^{j}-\Delta\overline{F}_{p^{j}}(t),\Delta r_{t}^{j}\rangle\bigr]dt.\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt-\zeta\mathbb{E}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\langle\Delta\overline{G}_{P^{i}}-\Delta\overline{X}_{T}^{i},\Delta X_{T}^{i}\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(\langle\Delta\overline{G}_{y^{j}}-\Delta\overline{x}_{T}^{j},\Delta x_{T}^{j}\rangle+\langle\Delta\overline{G}_{p^{j}}+\Delta\overline{r}_{T}^{j},(-1)\Delta r_{T}^{j}\rangle\bigr].\end{split}

We now expand the right hand side by using Lipschitz continuity of functions, Lemma
3.1
and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
By choosing
\zeta
small enough to absorb the terms
(|\Delta X|^{2},|\Delta x|^{2},|\Delta r|^{2})
into the left hand side,
we obtain

\small\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta X_{T}^{i}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta x_{T}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta r_{T}^{j}|^{2})\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta X_{t}^{i}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta x_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta r_{t}^{j}|^{2})\bigr]dt\\
&\leq(\zeta+\delta_{\mathfrak{n}})C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta P_{t}^{i}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta y_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta p_{t}^{j}|^{2})\bigr]dt\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\zeta C\mathbb{E}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta\overline{X}_{T}^{i}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta\overline{x}_{T}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta\overline{r}_{T}^{j}|^{2})\bigr]\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\zeta C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}(|\Delta\overline{X}_{t}^{i}|^{2}+|\Delta\overline{P}_{t}^{i}|^{2})+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta\overline{x}_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta\overline{r}_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta\overline{y}_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta\overline{p}_{t}^{j}|^{2})\bigr]dt.\end{split}

(
3.20)

Here, the constant
C
depends only on
L
and the convexity parameters such as
\gamma,\lambda
. In particular,
C

and the
\zeta
used in the above transformation are independent of
\varrho
and the population sizes.

Second step
: We need to get estimates on the backward components
(|\Delta P|^{2},|\Delta y|^{2},|\Delta p|^{2})

in the right hand side of
(\ref {first-step-finite})
.
In addition to
(\overline{u},\acute{\overline{u}})
, let us now treat

(X,x,r)
as well as
(\acute{X},\acute{x},\acute{r})
as exogenous input processes.
We consider
(P,y,p)
as the solution to the BSDEs in
(\ref {fbsde-shifted})
with the input processes
(\overline{u},X,x,r)
,
and
(\acute{P},\acute{y},\acute{p})
with
(\acute{\overline{u}},\acute{X},\acute{x},\acute{r})
, respectively.
We can then apply the standard stability result for Lipschitz BSDEs (see, for example,
[
57
, Theorem 4.4.4]
or

[
50
, Theorem 5.21]
)
to obtain

\small\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Delta P_{t}^{i}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}(|\Delta y_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta p_{t}^{j}|^{2})\bigr]dt\\

As one can see from the form of

4.2

Firstly, let us provide a formal derivation of the corresponding mean field problem.

Theorem 4.1

(

]

Suppose that

If we take

It is also natural to suppose

5
Convergence to the mean-field limit

Finally, in this section, we are going to prove that the finite-agent equilibrium given in Section

converges to the one in Section

when we take a large population limit
N_{1},N_{2}\rightarrow\infty

while keeping the ratio
\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}=N_{1}/N_{2}

To handle the large population limit, we make the probability space
(\overline{\Omega},\overline{{\cal F}},\overline{\mathbb{P}})
big enough

Throughout this section, we work under Assumptions
3.2
-
3.3

so that Theorems
3.4
and

Obviously, the analysis for the mean-field model done in Section
4
is independent from the choice of the representative agents

Thanks to the existence of unique strong solution

-conditionally i.i.d. within each group, and also that the two groups are mutually

in

Recall that, under the finite-agent setup in Section

,
we only have the (

-conditional) exchangeability.

In the remainder of this section, for each

with

Proposition 5.1
.

Let Assumptions
3.2
-
3.3
be in force and the ratio
\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\leq\delta_{*}
be kept fixed.
Then there exists some positive constant
C
independent of the population sizes that satisfies, for any
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
,

\small\begin{split}J_{1}^{i}(\widehat{\beta}^{\mathfrak{n}})-{\cal J}_{1}^{i}(\widehat{\beta}^{i})&\geq\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl[\frac{\gamma_{1}^{f}}{2}|X_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i}-X_{t}^{i}|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\beta}}{2}|\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i}-\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\varpi}}{2}|\varpi_{t}(y^{\mathfrak{n}}_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}})-\pi_{t}(y_{t}^{1},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{1})|^{2}\Bigr]dt\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\frac{\gamma_{1}^{g}}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|X_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},i}-X_{T}^{i}|^{2}\bigr]-C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

Proof.

We have
J_{1}^{i}(\widehat{\beta}^{\mathfrak{n}})-{\cal J}_{1}^{i}(\widehat{\beta}^{i})=V_{1}^{i}+V_{2}^{i}
with

\small\begin{split}&V_{1}^{i}:=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\langle X_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},i}-X_{T}^{i},P_{T}^{i}\rangle+\int_{0}^{T}[f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\nu_{t}^{N_{1}},\varpi_{t}(y^{\mathfrak{n}}_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}}),\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i})-f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t}(y_{t}^{j},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}),\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})]dt\Bigr],\\
&V_{2}^{i}:=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\nu_{T}^{N_{1}})-g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{i},\mu_{T})-\langle X_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},i}-X_{T}^{i},\partial_{x}g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{i},\mu_{T})+\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}^{0}[\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{g}_{1}^{i}(\widetilde{X}_{T}^{i},\mu_{T})(X_{T}^{i})]\rangle\Bigr].\end{split}

By exchangeability, the values of
V_{1}^{i}
and
V_{2}^{i}
are independent from the choice of
i
.
For notional ease, let us put

\Delta X_{t}^{i}:=X_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i}-X_{t}^{i}
,
\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}:=\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i}-\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}
,

\varpi_{t}:=\varpi_{t}(y_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}})
,
\pi_{t}:=\pi_{t}(y_{t}^{1},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{1})=\pi_{t}(y_{t}^{j},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})
and

\Delta\varpi_{t}:=\varpi_{t}-\pi_{t}
, etc.

First step: Estimate on
V_{2}^{i}

Let us separate the terms in
V_{2}^{i}
as follows:
V_{2}^{i}=V_{2,1}^{i}-V_{2,2}^{i}-V_{2,3}^{i}
where

\begin{split}&V_{2,1}^{i}:=\mathbb{E}\bigl[g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\nu_{T}^{N_{1}})-g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{i},\mu_{T})\bigr],\\
&V_{2,2}^{i}:=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta X_{T}^{i},\partial_{x}g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{i},\mu_{T})\rangle\bigr],\quad V_{2,3}^{i}:=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta X_{T}^{i},\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}^{0}[\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{g}_{1}^{i}(\widetilde{X}_{T}^{i},\mu_{T})(X_{T}^{i})]\rangle\bigr].\end{split}

By local Lipschitz continuity in Assumption
3.3
(ii),
(\ref {uniform-moment})
and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\small\begin{split}V_{2,1}^{i}&\geq\mathbb{E}\bigl[g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\nu_{T}^{N_{1}})-g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{i},\mu_{T}^{N_{1}})\bigr]-C\mathbb{E}\bigl[(1+|X_{T}^{i}|+M_{2}(\mu_{T})+M_{2}(\mu_{T}^{N_{1}})+|c_{T}^{0}|+|c_{T}^{i}|)W_{2}(\mu_{T},\mu_{T}^{N_{1}})\bigr]\\
&\geq\mathbb{E}\bigl[g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\nu_{T}^{N_{1}})-g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{i},\mu_{T}^{N_{1}})\bigr]-C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

From Assumption
3.3
(iii), similar calculations give

\small\begin{split}V_{2,2}^{i}\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta X_{T}^{i},\partial_{x}g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{i},\mu_{T}^{N_{1}})\rangle\bigr]+C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

Using Lemma
5.2
and Assumption
3.3
(iii), we get

\small\begin{split}V_{2,3}^{i}&\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\langle\Delta X_{T}^{i},\frac{1}{N_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\partial_{\mu}g_{1}^{k}(X_{T}^{k},\mu^{N_{1}}_{T})(X_{T}^{i})\rangle\Bigr]+C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

Note that the value of
V_{2,3}^{i}
is independent from
i
. From the same technique used in
(\ref {theta-technique})
, we have

\small\begin{split}V_{2,3}^{i}&=\frac{1}{N_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}V_{2,3}^{i}\leq\frac{1}{N_{1}^{2}}\sum_{i,k=1}^{N_{1}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl\langle\Delta X_{T}^{i},\partial_{\mu}g_{1}^{k}(X_{T}^{k},\mu^{N_{1}}_{T})(X_{T}^{i})\bigr\rangle\bigr]+C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}\\
&=\frac{1}{N_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}^{\theta}\bigl[\langle\Delta X_{T}^{\theta},\partial_{\mu}g_{1}^{k}(X_{T}^{k},\mu_{T}^{N_{1}})(X_{T}^{\theta})\rangle\bigr]+C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}^{\theta}\bigl[\langle\Delta X_{T}^{\theta},\partial_{\mu}g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{i},\mu_{T}^{N_{1}})(X_{T}^{\theta})\rangle\bigr]+C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

Combined the above estimates, we obtain, from Assumption
3.3
(v),

\small\begin{split}V_{2}^{i}&\geq\mathbb{E}\bigl[g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\nu_{T}^{N_{1}})-g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{i},\mu_{T}^{N_{1}})\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt-\langle\Delta X_{T}^{i},\partial_{x}g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{i},\mu_{T}^{N_{1}})\rangle-\mathbb{E}^{\theta}[\langle\Delta X_{T}^{\theta},\partial_{\mu}g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{i},\mu_{T}^{N_{1}})(X_{T}^{\theta})\rangle]\bigr]-C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}\\
&\geq\frac{\gamma_{1}^{g}}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\Delta X_{T}^{i}|^{2}\bigr]-C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

(
5.3)

Second step: Estimate on
V_{1}^{i}

Since
\langle\Delta x_{T}^{j},p_{T}^{j}\rangle+\langle\Delta y_{T}^{j},r_{T}^{j}\rangle=0
for any
j
, we have

\small\begin{split}V_{1}^{i}&=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\langle\Delta X_{T}^{i},P_{T}^{i}\rangle+\overline{\delta}_{\mathfrak{n}}(\langle\Delta x_{T}^{j},p_{T}^{j}\rangle+\langle\Delta y_{T}^{j},r_{T}^{j}\rangle)+\int_{0}^{T}[f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\nu_{t}^{N_{1}},\varpi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i})-f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})]dt\Bigr]\\
&\geq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\langle\Delta X_{T}^{i},P_{T}^{i}\rangle+\overline{\delta}_{\mathfrak{n}}(\langle\Delta x_{T}^{j},p_{T}^{j}\rangle+\langle\Delta y_{T}^{j},r_{T}^{j}\rangle)\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{0}^{T}[f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\nu_{t}^{N_{1}},\varpi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i})-f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})-\langle\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i},D_{\beta^{i}}{\cal H}(t,u_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})\rangle]dt\Bigr].\end{split}

Here, in the second line, we have set
u_{t}:=(X_{t}^{i},x_{t}^{j},y_{t}^{j},P_{t}^{i},p_{t}^{j},r_{t}^{j})
and used the optimality condition on
\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}=\widehat{\beta}^{i}(t,u_{t})

for
{\cal H}(t,u_{t},\cdot)
. Direct calculation of the right hand side using the expression of
D_{\beta^{i}}{\cal H}(\cdot)
in
(\ref {H-Frechet-D})
shows that

V_{1}^{i}\geq V_{1,1}^{i}+V_{1,2}^{i}
with

\small\begin{split}V_{1,1}^{i}&:=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl[f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\nu_{t}^{N_{1}},\varpi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i})-f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})-\langle\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i},\partial_{\beta}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})\rangle\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt-\langle\Delta X_{t}^{i},\partial_{x}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})+\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}^{0}[\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{f}_{1}^{i}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widetilde{\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}})(X_{t}^{i})]\rangle\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt-\langle\mathbb{E}^{0}[\partial_{\varpi}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})],-\Delta y_{t}^{j}+\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}\rangle\Bigr]dt,\\
V_{1,2}^{i}&:=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl[\langle\rho_{1}(t)P_{t}^{i},\Delta\varpi_{t}\rangle+\langle\rho_{1}(t)\mathbb{E}^{0}[P_{t}^{i}],\Delta y_{t}^{j}-\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}\rangle\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt+\overline{\delta}_{\mathfrak{n}}\bigl(\langle(-\overline{\Lambda}_{t}+\rho_{2}(t))p_{t}^{j},\Delta\varpi_{t}\rangle+\langle(-\overline{\Lambda}_{t}+\rho_{2}(t))\mathbb{E}^{0}[p_{t}^{j}],\Delta y_{t}^{j}-\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}\rangle\bigr)\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt+\overline{\delta}_{\mathfrak{n}}\bigl(\langle b_{t}r_{t}^{j},\Delta\varpi_{t}\rangle+\langle b_{t}\mathbb{E}^{0}[r_{t}^{j}],\Delta y_{t}^{j}-\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}\rangle\bigr)\Bigr]dt.\end{split}

\bullet~
Analysis for
V_{1,2}^{i}

For the first line, from the (
{\cal F}^{0}
-conditional) exchangeability of
(P^{i})
,
(\Delta y^{j})
and
(\Delta\widehat{\beta}^{i})
, we have

\small\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\rho_{1}(t)P_{t}^{i},\Delta\varpi_{t}\rangle+\langle\rho_{1}(t)\mathbb{E}^{0}[P_{t}^{i}],\Delta y_{t}^{j}-\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}\rangle\bigr]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\rho_{1}(t)(P_{t}^{i}-\mathbb{E}^{0}[P_{t}^{i}]),\Delta\varpi_{t}\rangle+\langle\rho_{1}(t)\mathbb{E}^{0}[P_{t}^{i}],\Delta\varpi_{t}+\Delta y_{t}^{j}-\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}\rangle\bigr]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\rho_{1}(t)(\mathfrak{m}_{1}(P_{t})-\mathbb{E}^{0}[P_{t}^{1}]),\Delta\varpi_{t}\rangle+\langle\rho_{1}(t)\mathbb{E}^{0}[P_{t}^{1}],\Delta\varpi_{t}+\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})-\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\mathfrak{m}_{1}(\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t})\rangle\bigr].\end{split}

Here, the second term is zero since

\small\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}^{0}[\Delta\varpi_{t}+\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})-\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\mathfrak{m}_{1}(\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t})]\\
&=\mathbb{E}^{0}[-\mathfrak{m}_{2}(y_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}})+\mathbb{E}^{0}[y_{t}^{1}]+\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}(\mathfrak{m}_{1}(\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}})-\mathbb{E}^{0}[\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{1}])+\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})-\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\mathfrak{m}_{1}(\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t})]=0.\end{split}

(
5.4)

Applying Lemma
5.1
and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the first term, we obtain,

\small\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\rho_{1}(t)P_{t}^{i},\Delta\varpi_{t}\rangle+\langle\rho_{1}(t)\mathbb{E}^{0}[P_{t}^{i}],\Delta y_{t}^{j}-\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}\rangle\bigr]\geq-C/\sqrt{N_{1}}\geq-C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

By the same technique and
(\ref {delvp-zero})
, the second and third lines can be estimated as

\small\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle(-\overline{\Lambda}_{t}+\rho_{2}(t))p_{t}^{j},\Delta\varpi_{t}\rangle+\langle(-\overline{\Lambda}_{t}+\rho_{2}(t))\mathbb{E}^{0}[p_{t}^{j}],\Delta y_{t}^{j}-\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}\rangle\bigr]\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle b_{t}r_{t}^{j},\Delta\varpi_{t}\rangle+\langle b_{t}\mathbb{E}^{0}[r_{t}^{j}],\Delta y_{t}^{j}-\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}\rangle\bigr]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle(-\overline{\Lambda}_{t}+\rho_{2}(t))(\mathfrak{m}_{2}(p_{t})-\mathbb{E}^{0}[p_{t}^{j}]),\Delta\varpi_{t}\rangle+\langle(-\overline{\Lambda}_{t}+\rho_{2}(t))\mathbb{E}^{0}[p_{t}^{j}],\Delta\varpi_{t}+\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})-\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\mathfrak{m}_{1}(\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t})\rangle\bigr]\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle b_{t}(\mathfrak{m}_{2}(r_{t})-\mathbb{E}^{0}[r_{t}^{j}]),\Delta\varpi_{t}\rangle+\langle b_{t}\mathbb{E}^{0}[r_{t}^{j}],\Delta\varpi_{t}+\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})-\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\mathfrak{m}_{1}(\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t})\rangle\bigr]\\
&\geq-C/\sqrt{N_{2}}\geq-C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

Since every estimate is uniform in
t\in[0,T]
by
(\ref {uniform-moment})
, we get

\small V_{1,2}^{i}\geq-C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.

(
5.5)

\bullet~
Analysis for
V_{1,1}^{i}

From
(\ref {uniform-moment})
and the (local) Lipschitz continuity, it is easy to see, for the first line,

\small\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\bigl[f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\nu_{t}^{N_{1}},\varpi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i})-f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})-\langle\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i},\partial_{\beta}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})\rangle\bigr]\\
&\geq\mathbb{E}\bigl[f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\nu_{t}^{N_{1}},\varpi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i})-f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t}^{N_{1}},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})-\langle\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i},\partial_{\beta}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t}^{N_{1}},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})\rangle\bigr]-C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

Using Lemma
5.2
, exchangeability and the technique in
(\ref {theta-technique})
, we have

\small\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta X_{t}^{i},\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}^{0}[\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{f}_{1}^{i}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widetilde{\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}})(X_{t}^{i})]\rangle\bigr]\leq\frac{1}{N_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\langle\Delta X_{t}^{i},\frac{1}{N_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\partial_{\mu}f_{1}^{k}(t,X_{t}^{k},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{k})(X_{t}^{i})\bigr\rangle\Bigr]+C/\sqrt{N_{1}}\\
&=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}^{\theta}\bigl[\langle\Delta X_{t}^{\theta},\partial_{\mu}f_{1}^{i}(X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})(X_{t}^{\theta})\rangle\bigr]+C/\sqrt{N_{1}}.\end{split}

Thus, from the Lipschitz continuity of
(\partial_{x}f_{1},\partial_{\mu}f_{1})
, we see that the second line of
V_{1,1}^{i}
satisfies

\small\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta X_{t}^{i},\partial_{x}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})+\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}^{0}[\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{f}_{1}^{i}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widetilde{\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}})(X_{t}^{i})]\rangle\bigr]\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta X_{t}^{i},\partial_{x}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t}^{N_{1}},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})\rangle+\mathbb{E}^{\theta}[\langle\Delta X_{t}^{\theta},\partial_{\mu}f_{1}^{i}(X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t}^{N_{1}},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})(X_{t}^{\theta})\rangle]\bigr]+C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

Lastly, the third line of
V_{1,1}^{i}
can be estimated as

\small\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\mathbb{E}^{0}[\partial_{\varpi}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})],-\Delta y_{t}^{j}+\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}\rangle\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\mathbb{E}^{0}[\partial_{\varpi}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})],\Delta\varpi_{t}\rangle\bigr]\\
&\leq\frac{1}{N_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\partial_{\varpi}f_{1}^{k}(t,X_{t}^{k},\mu_{t},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{k}),\Delta\varpi_{t}\rangle\bigr]+C/\sqrt{N_{1}}\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\partial_{\varpi}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t}^{N_{1}},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}),\Delta\varpi_{t}\rangle\bigr]+C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

Here, we have used
(\ref {delvp-zero})
in the first equality, Lemma
5.1
in the second line,
and finally the Lipschitz continuity of

in the last line.
Combined with
(\ref {result-V12})
, the joint convexity of
f_{1}
in Assumption
3.3
(iv) gives

\small\begin{split}V_{1}^{i}&\geq\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl[f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\nu_{t}^{N_{1}},\varpi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i})-f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t}^{N_{1}},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})-\langle\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i},\partial_{\beta}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t}^{N_{1}},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})\rangle\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt-\langle\Delta X_{t}^{i},\partial_{x}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t}^{N_{1}},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})\rangle-\mathbb{E}^{\theta}[\langle\Delta X_{t}^{\theta},\partial_{\mu}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t}^{N_{1}},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})(X_{t}^{\theta})\rangle]\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt-\langle\Delta\varpi_{t},\partial_{\varpi}f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t}^{N_{1}},\pi_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})\rangle\Bigr]dt-C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}\\
&\geq\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl[\frac{\gamma_{1}^{f}}{2}|\Delta X_{t}^{i}|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\varpi}}{2}|\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\varpi}}{2}|\Delta\varpi_{t}|^{2}\Bigr]dt-C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

(
5.6)

The conclusion follows from
(\ref {conv-first-step})
and

.

We now consider the state process of the finite-agent market

\underline{u}^{\mathfrak{n}}:=(\underline{X}^{\mathfrak{n}},\underline{x}^{\mathfrak{n}},\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n}})\in\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{N_{1}}\times(\mathbb{S}^{2}(\mathbb{F};\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{2N_{2}}

with
\underline{X}^{\mathfrak{n}}:=(\underline{X}^{\mathfrak{n},i})_{i=1}^{N_{1}}
,

\underline{x}^{\mathfrak{n}}:=(\underline{x}^{\mathfrak{n},j})_{j=1}^{N_{2}}
, and
\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n}}:=(\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n},j})_{j=1}^{N_{2}}
defined as the unique solution to
the system of FBSDEs:
1\leq i\leq N_{1},1\leq j\leq N_{2}
,

\small\begin{split}&d\underline{X}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i}=(\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}+\rho_{1}(t)\varpi_{t}(\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n}},\widehat{\beta}_{t})+l_{1}^{i}(t))dt+\sigma_{1}^{i,0}(t)dW_{t}^{0}+\sigma_{1}^{i}(t)dW_{t}^{i},\\
&d\underline{x}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},j}=(-\overline{\Lambda}_{t}(\underline{y}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},j}+\varpi_{t}(\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n}}_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}))+\rho_{2}(t)\varpi_{t}(\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n}}_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t})+l_{2}^{j}(t))dt+\sigma_{2}^{j,0}(t)dW_{t}^{0}+\sigma_{2}^{j}(t)dB_{t}^{j},\\

(
5.7)

with the boundary conditions;
\underline{X}_{0}^{\mathfrak{n},i}=\xi^{i},\underline{x}_{0}^{\mathfrak{n},j}=\eta^{j}

and
\underline{y}_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},j}=\partial_{x}g_{2}^{j}(\underline{x}_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},j})
. As in

, we have

\varpi_{t}(\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n}}_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t})=-\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n}})+\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}\mathfrak{m}_{1}(\widehat{\beta}_{t})
.
Recall that
\widehat{\beta}^{i}
denotes the optimal solution to the mean-field setup for the representative agents
i
in the first population
and (arbitrary)
j
in the second population, which is specified by the solution of
(\ref {fbsde-mfg})
.
In other words, the system of equations
(\ref {fbsde-approx})
describes the situation
where each agent
i
in the first population behaves as if she is a representative agent
and adopts
\widehat{\beta}^{i}\in\mathbb{A}_{1}^{i}=\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{1,i};A_{1})
as an approximation for the true optimal control
\widehat{\beta}^{\mathfrak{n},i}\in\mathbb{A}_{1}:=\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{F};A_{1})
. Moreover,

describes the market clearing
state process of the second population under the given order flow
\widehat{\beta}~(:=(\widehat{\beta}^{i})_{i=1}^{N_{1}})
. The unique existence
of such a process
(\underline{x}^{\mathfrak{n}},\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n}})
is guaranteed
by Theorem
3.2
as discussed in Subsection

.
Note that the (
{\cal F}^{0}
-conditional) exchangeability among
(\underline{X}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\widehat{\beta}^{i},\xi^{i},c^{i},W^{i})_{i=1}^{N_{1}}

and
(\underline{x}^{\mathfrak{n},j},\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n},j},\eta^{j},\mathfrak{c}^{j},B^{j})_{j=1}^{N_{2}}
holds in this case, too.
The cost functional for the member
i
in the first population under this setup is given by

\small\begin{split}J_{1}^{i}(\widehat{\beta})=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}f_{1}^{i}(t,\underline{X}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\underline{\nu}_{t}^{N_{1}},\varpi_{t}(\underline{y}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}},\widehat{\beta}_{t}),\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})dt+g_{1}^{i}(\underline{X}_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\underline{\nu}_{T}^{N_{1}})\Bigr],\end{split}

where
\underline{\nu}_{t}^{N_{1}}:=\frac{1}{N_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\delta_{\underline{X}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i}}
.

The following proof is inspired by the monotonicity technique used in
[

Proposition 5.2
.

Let Assumptions
3.2
-
3.3
be in force and the ratio
\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\leq\delta_{*}
be kept fixed.
Then there exists some positive constant
C
independent of the population sizes that satisfies, for any
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
,

\small\begin{split}\bigl|J_{1}^{i}(\widehat{\beta})-{\cal J}_{1}^{i}(\widehat{\beta}^{i})\bigr|\leq C\varepsilon_{N_{1}},\end{split}

where
\varepsilon_{N_{1}}
is defined by
(\ref {vepN1-def})
.

Proof.

Let us denote by
\underline{u}^{\mathfrak{n}}:=(\underline{X}^{\mathfrak{n}},\underline{x}^{\mathfrak{n}},\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n}})
and
u:=(X^{i},x^{j},y^{j})

the unique solution to
(\ref {fbsde-approx})
and
(\ref {fbsde-mfg})
, respectively.
For given processes
\underline{u}^{\mathfrak{n}},u
and
\widehat{\beta}
, we just put

\small\begin{split}&B_{\underline{X}^{i}}(t,\underline{u}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}}):=\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}+\rho_{1}(t)\varpi_{t}(\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n}}_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t})+l_{1}^{i}(t),\\
&B_{\underline{x}^{j}}(t,\underline{u}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}}):=-\overline{\Lambda}_{t}(\underline{y}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},j}+\varpi_{t}(\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n}}_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}))+\rho_{2}(t)\varpi_{t}(\underline{y}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}},\widehat{\beta}_{t})+l_{2}^{j}(t),\\
&F_{\underline{y}^{j}}(t,\underline{u}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}}):=-\partial_{x}\overline{f}_{2}^{j}(t,\underline{x}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},j})+b_{t}\varpi_{t}(\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n}}_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t}),\hskip 9.24994ptG_{\underline{y}^{j}}(\underline{u}_{T}^{\mathfrak{n}}):=\partial_{x}g_{2}^{j}(\underline{x}_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},j}),\end{split}

and also
(B_{X^{i}}(t,u_{t}),B_{x^{j}}(t,u_{t}),F_{y^{j}}(t,u_{t}),G_{y^{j}}(u_{T}))
as in
(\ref {BFG-mfg})
.
For notational ease, we shall use

\Delta X_{t}^{i}:=\underline{X}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i}-X_{t}^{i},\Delta x_{t}^{j}:=\underline{x}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},j}-x_{t}^{j},\Delta y_{t}^{j}:=\underline{y}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},j}-y_{t}^{j}
,

\Delta B_{x^{j}}(t):=B_{\underline{x}^{j}}(t,\underline{u}^{\mathfrak{n}}_{t})-B_{x^{j}}(t,u_{t})
,
\Delta F_{y^{j}}(t):=F_{\underline{y}^{j}}(t,\underline{u}^{\mathfrak{n}}_{t})-F_{y^{j}}(t,u_{t})
,

\Delta G_{y^{j}}:=G_{\underline{y}^{j}}(\underline{u}_{T}^{\mathfrak{n}})-G_{y^{j}}(u_{T})
,
\Delta\varpi_{t}:=\varpi_{t}(\underline{y}^{\mathfrak{n}},\widehat{\beta}_{t})-\pi_{t}(y^{j}_{t},\widehat{\beta}^{i}_{t})
.
We have the following expansion for
\Delta\varpi_{t}
:

\small\begin{split}\Delta\varpi_{t}&=-\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})-(\mathfrak{m}_{2}(y_{t})-\mathbb{E}^{0}[y_{t}^{1}])+\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}(\mathfrak{m}_{1}(\widehat{\beta}_{t})-\mathbb{E}^{0}[\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{1}]).\end{split}

(
5.8)

Using the exchangeability,
(\ref {delvp-expansion})
and the fact that
\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\overline{\Lambda}_{t}(\Delta y_{t}^{j}-\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})),\Delta y_{t}^{j}\rangle\bigr]\geq 0
, we have

\small\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta B_{x^{j}}(t),\Delta y_{t}^{j}\rangle\bigr]=\frac{1}{N_{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle-\overline{\Lambda}_{t}(\Delta y^{j}_{t}+\Delta\varpi_{t})+\rho_{2}(t)\Delta\varpi_{t},\Delta y_{t}^{j}\rangle\bigr]\\
&\leq-\mathbb{E}\big[\rho_{2}(t)|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})|^{2}\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle-(\mathfrak{m}_{2}(y_{t})-\mathbb{E}^{0}[y_{t}^{1}])+\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}(\mathfrak{m}_{1}(\widehat{\beta}_{t})-\mathbb{E}^{0}[\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{1}]),(\rho_{2}(t)-\overline{\Lambda}_{t})\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})\rangle\bigr].\end{split}

Lemma
5.1
and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality give

\small\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta B_{x^{j}}(t),\Delta y_{t}^{j}\rangle\bigr]\leq-\mathbb{E}[\rho_{2}(t)|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})|^{2}]+C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})|^{2}\bigr]^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

From Assumption
3.2
(iv),
(\ref {delvp-expansion})
and Young’s inequality, we also have

\small\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta F_{y^{j}}(t),\Delta x_{t}^{j}\rangle\bigr]&\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[-\gamma_{2}^{f}|\Delta x_{t}^{j}|^{2}+b_{t}\langle-\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})-(\mathfrak{m}_{2}(y_{t})-\mathbb{E}^{0}[y_{t}^{1}])+\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}\Lambda_{t}(\mathfrak{m}_{1}(\widehat{\beta}_{t})-\mathbb{E}^{0}[\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{1}]),\Delta x_{t}^{j}\rangle\bigr]\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[-\frac{\gamma_{2}^{f}}{2}|\Delta x_{t}^{j}|^{2}+\frac{b_{t}^{2}}{\gamma_{2}^{f}}|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})|^{2}|\Bigr]+C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}^{2}.\end{split}

Since
\frac{b_{t}^{2}}{\gamma_{2}^{f}}\leq\frac{\rho_{2}(t)}{2}
by Assumption
3.3
(vi), we obtain

\small\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta B_{x^{j}}(t),\Delta y_{t}^{j}\rangle+\langle\Delta F_{y^{j}}(t),\Delta x_{t}^{j}\rangle\bigr]\leq-\frac{\gamma_{2}^{f}}{2}\mathbb{E}[|\Delta x_{t}^{j}|^{2}]+C\bigl(\varepsilon_{N_{1}}^{2}+\varepsilon_{N_{1}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})|^{2}\bigr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr).

(
5.9)

We also have, as a direct result of Assumption
3.2
(iv),

\small\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta G_{y^{j}},\Delta x_{T}^{j}\rangle\bigr]\geq\gamma_{2}^{g}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\Delta x_{T}^{j}|^{2}].\end{split}

(
5.10)

On the other hand, Itô formula applied to
\langle\Delta y^{j}_{t},\Delta x_{t}^{j}\rangle
gives an equality

\small\begin{split}\bigl[\langle\Delta G_{y^{j}},\Delta x_{T}^{j}\rangle\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl[\langle\Delta B_{x^{j}}(t),\Delta y_{t}^{j}\rangle+\langle\Delta F_{y^{j}}(t),\Delta x_{t}^{j}\rangle\bigr]dt.\end{split}

Applying
(\ref {drift-conv})
and
(\ref {terminal-conv})
to the above equality, we obtain, with some constant
C
,

\small\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[|\Delta x_{T}^{j}|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|\Delta x_{t}^{j}|^{2}dt\Bigr]\leq C\Bigl(\varepsilon_{N_{1}}^{2}+\varepsilon_{N_{1}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\mathfrak{m}_{2}(\Delta y_{t})|^{2}\bigr]^{\frac{1}{2}}dt\Bigr).\end{split}

(
5.11)

Next, a simple application of Itô formula to
|\Delta y_{t}^{j}|^{2}
gives

\small\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\Delta y_{t}^{j}|^{2}\bigr]&\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[|\Delta G_{y^{j}}|^{2}-2\int_{t}^{T}\langle\Delta F_{y^{j}}(s),\Delta y_{s}^{j}\rangle ds\Bigr]\leq C\mathbb{E}\Bigl[|\Delta x_{T}^{j}|^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}(|\Delta x_{s}^{j}|+|\Delta\varpi_{s}|)|\Delta y_{s}^{j}|ds\Bigr]\\
&\leq C\mathbb{E}\Bigl[|\Delta x_{T}^{j}|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|\Delta x_{t}^{j}|^{2}dt\Bigr]+C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}^{2}+C\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\Delta y_{s}^{j}|^{2}\bigr]ds,\end{split}

where, in the last line, we used exchangeability,
(\ref {delvp-expansion})
and Lemma
5.1
.
Hence, from the backward Gronwall’s inequality, exchangeability and
(\ref {del-second-pop})
, we get

\small\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\Delta y_{t}^{j}|^{2}\bigr]&\leq C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}^{2}+C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[|\Delta y_{t}^{j}|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}dt,\end{split}

which gives
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\Delta y_{t}^{j}|^{2}\bigr]\leq C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}^{2}
by Young’s inequality.
This in turn implies, by
(\ref {del-second-pop})
,

\small\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\Delta x_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta y_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta\varpi_{t}|^{2}\bigr]\leq C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}^{2}.\end{split}

Finally, since
\Delta X_{t}^{i}=\int_{0}^{t}\rho_{1}(t)\Delta\varpi_{t}dt
, we conclude, for any
1\leq i\leq N_{1},1\leq j\leq N_{2}
,

\small\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\Delta X_{t}^{i}|^{2}+|\Delta x_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta y_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta\varpi_{t}|^{2}\bigr]\leq C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}^{2}.\end{split}

(
5.12)

Form
(\ref {conv-prop2-stability})
and the local Lipschitz continuity of
g_{1}
, we have

\small\begin{split}&\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[g_{1}^{i}(\underline{X}_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\underline{\nu}_{T}^{N_{1}})-g_{1}^{i}(X_{T}^{i},\mu_{T})\bigr]\bigr|\\
&\leq C\mathbb{E}\bigl[(1+|\underline{X}_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},i}|+|X_{T}^{i}|+M_{2}(\underline{\nu}_{T}^{N_{1}})+M_{2}(\mu_{T})+|c_{T}^{0}|+|c_{T}^{i}|)(|\Delta X_{T}^{i}|+W_{2}(\underline{\nu}_{T}^{N_{1}},\mu_{T}))\bigr]\\
&\leq C\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\Delta X_{T}^{i}|^{2}+W_{2}(\mu_{T}^{N_{1}},\mu_{T})^{2}\bigr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

(
5.13)

Here, the triangle inequality
W_{2}(\underline{\nu}_{T}^{N_{1}},\mu_{T})\leq W_{2}(\underline{\nu}_{T}^{N_{1}},\mu_{T}^{N_{1}})+W_{2}(\mu_{T}^{N_{1}},\mu_{T})

and the fact

\small\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigl[W_{2}(\underline{\nu}_{T}^{N_{1}},\mu_{T}^{N_{1}})^{2}\bigr]\leq\frac{1}{N_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\underline{X}_{T}^{\mathfrak{n},k}-X_{T}^{k}|^{2}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}[|\Delta X_{T}^{i}|^{2}]\end{split}

were used. Similar calculation using the local Lipschitz continuity also yields

\small\begin{split}&\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl[f_{1}^{i}(t,\underline{X}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i},\underline{\nu}_{t}^{N_{1}},\varpi_{t}(\underline{y}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}},\widehat{\beta}_{t}),\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})-f_{1}^{i}(t,X_{t}^{i},\mu_{t},\pi_{t}(y_{t}^{j},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}),\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})\Bigr]dt\Bigr|\\
&\leq C\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\Delta X_{t}^{i}|^{2}+|\Delta\varpi_{t}|^{2}+W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{N_{1}},\mu_{t})^{2}\bigr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

(
5.14)

The conclusion follows from inequalities
(\ref {conv-prop2-terminal})
and
(\ref {conv-prop2-running})
.
∎

Theorem 5.1
.

Let Assumptions
3.2
-
3.3
be in force and the ratio
\delta_{n}\leq\delta_{*}
be kept fixed.
Then the equilibrium of the finite-agent market strongly converges to the mean-field equilibrium in the large population limit of
N_{1}
(and hence
N_{2}
)

\rightarrow\infty
in the sense that, for any
N_{1}
, there exists some positive constant
C
independent of population sizes that satisfies,
with
\varepsilon_{N_{1}}
defined by
(\ref {vepN1-def})
,

\small\begin{split}{\rm(i):}~&|J_{1}^{i}(\widehat{\beta}^{\mathfrak{n}})-{\cal J}^{i}(\widehat{\beta}^{i})|\leq C\varepsilon_{N_{1}},\\
{\rm(ii):}~&\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl[|\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i}-\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}|^{2}+|\varpi_{t}(y^{\mathfrak{n}}_{t},\widehat{\beta}^{\mathfrak{n}}_{t})-\pi_{t}(y_{t}^{1},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{1})|^{2}\Bigr]dt\leq C\varepsilon_{N_{1}},\\
{\rm(iii):}~&\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bigl(|X_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i}-X_{t}^{i}|^{2}+|x_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},j}-x_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|y_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},j}-y_{t}^{j}|^{2}\bigr)\Bigr]\leq C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}\end{split}

for any
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
and
1\leq j\leq N_{2}
.

Proof.

Let us use the same conventions as in Proposition
5.1
, i.e.,

\Delta X_{t}^{i}:=X_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i}-X_{t}^{i}
,
\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}:=\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i}-\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}
,

\varpi_{t}:=\varpi_{t}(y_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}})
,
\pi_{t}:=\pi_{t}(y_{t}^{1},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{1})=\pi_{t}(y_{t}^{j},\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i})
and

\Delta X_{t}^{i}:=X_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i}-X_{t}^{i}
,
\Delta y_{t}^{j}:=y_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},j}-y_{t}^{j}
,
\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}:=\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},i}-\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}
,

\Delta\varpi_{t}:=\varpi_{t}-\pi_{t}
, etc.
Note that, from the results of Propositions
5.1
and
5.2
, we have

\small\begin{split}&{\cal J}_{1}^{i}(\widehat{\beta}^{i})+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl[\frac{\gamma_{1}^{f}}{2}|\Delta X_{t}^{i}|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\beta}}{2}|\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\varpi}}{2}|\Delta\varpi_{t}|^{2}\Bigr]dt+\frac{\gamma_{1}^{g}}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\Delta X_{T}^{i}|^{2}\bigr]-C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}\\
&\leq J_{1}^{i}(\widehat{\beta}^{\mathfrak{n}})\leq J_{1}^{i}(\widehat{\beta})\leq{\cal J}_{1}^{i}(\widehat{\beta}^{i})+C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

This gives (i) and (ii).
Since

\Delta X_{t}^{i}=\int_{0}^{t}\bigl[\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}+\rho_{1}(t)\Delta\varpi_{t}\bigr]dt,

it is easy to obtain

\small\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Delta X_{t}^{i}|^{2}\bigr]\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl[|\Delta\widehat{\beta}_{t}^{i}|^{2}+|\Delta\varpi_{t}|^{2}\bigr]dt\leq C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

Moreover, applying the convexity of
g_{2},\overline{f}_{2}
to the equality

\small\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\Delta y_{T}^{j},\Delta x_{T}^{j}\rangle\bigr]&=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl[\langle-\overline{\Lambda}_{t}\Delta y_{t}^{j}+(\rho_{2}(t)-\overline{\Lambda}_{t})\Delta\varpi_{t},\Delta y_{t}^{j}\rangle+\langle-(\partial_{x}\overline{f}_{2}^{j}(t,x_{t}^{\mathfrak{n},j})-\partial_{x}\overline{f}_{2}^{j}(t,x_{t}^{j}))+b_{t}\Delta\varpi_{t},\Delta x_{t}^{j}\rangle\Bigr]dt,\end{split}

we obtain, with some constant
C
,

\small\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[|\Delta x_{T}^{j}|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|\Delta x_{t}^{j}|^{2}dt\Bigr]\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl[|\Delta\varpi_{t}|^{2}+|\Delta\varpi_{t}||\Delta y_{t}^{j}|\bigr]dt.\end{split}

Following the same procedures used in Proposition
5.2
, it is simple to obtain

\small\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\Delta x_{t}^{j}|^{2}+|\Delta y_{t}^{j}|^{2}\bigr]\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\Delta\varpi_{t}|^{2}dt\leq C\varepsilon_{N_{1}}.\end{split}

BDG inequality then provides the desired estimate.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

If the law-
\mu
dependence in
(f_{1},g_{1})
is just given by its first moment
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x\mu(dx)
,
then we can replace
\varepsilon_{N_{1}}
by
1/\sqrt{N_{1}}
since the first part of Lemma
5.1
becomes unnecessary.
Therefore, in this case, we have an explicit convergence speed
\propto 1/\sqrt{N_{1}}
.

Theorem
5.1
tells that the market clearing price
\varpi_{t}(y_{t}^{\mathfrak{n}},\widehat{\beta}^{\mathfrak{n}}_{t})

converges to an
\mathbb{F}^{0}
-adapted process
\pi_{t}(y_{t},\widehat{\beta}_{t})
.
This means that the securities prices only respond to the market-wide news and shocks,
which is consistent with our intuition. This also allows each agent, when the population is large enough, to focus on the market-wide
and her own idiosyncratic informations without taking care of the idiosyncratic informations of the others.
Recall the discussion given in Remark
4.2
.
Therefore, we can see that an awkward setup with the perfect information with
\mathbb{F}
-adapted controls
is effectively resolved as the population size grows.

6
Conclusion and discussion

In this work, we developed an equilibrium model for price formation in a market composed of
cooperative and non-cooperative populations.
In the large population limit, we have seen that the problem for the central planner
becomes an extended mean-field control over the FBSDEs of
McKean-Vlasov type with common noise.
In addition to the convexity assumptions, if the relative size of the cooperative population is small enough,
then we were able to show the existence of a unique equilibrium for both the finite-agent and the mean-field models.
The strong convergence to the mean-field model was also proved under the same conditions.

The development of feasible numerical calculation techniques is an important remaining problem.
If we adopt the linear-quadratic setting as a special example, which has a semi-analytic solution,
then we may study how the equilibrium is destabilized as the relative population size
\delta_{\mathfrak{n}}
of the cooperative agents grows.
Since, in our model, the control domain
A_{1}
can be an arbitrary closed convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
we can fix the direction of trade if necessary. This will be useful when we consider a model of cooperative producers (or buyers)
in the background of a large number of competitive agents.
Extensions of the current model for these economic applications deserve further research.

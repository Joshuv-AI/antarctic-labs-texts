Unconventional Metallic Ferromagnetism: Non-Analyticity and Sign-Changing Behavior of Orbital Magnetization in Rhombohedral Trilayer Graphene
Mainak Das and Chunli Huang

Abstract
We study an unique form of metallic ferromagnetism in which orbital moments surpasses the role of spin moments in shaping the overall magnetization. This system emerges naturally upon doping a topologically non-trivial Chern band in the recently identified quarter metal phase of rhombohedral trilayer graphene. Our comprehensive scan of the density-interlayer potential parameter space reveals an unexpected landscape of orbital magnetization marked by two sign changes and a line of singularities. The sign change originates from an intense Berry curvature concentrated close to the band-edge, and the singularity arises from a topological Lifshitz transition that transform a simply connected Fermi sea into an annular Fermi sea. Importantly, these variations occur while the groundstate order-parameter (i.e. valley and spin polarization) remains unchanged. This unconventional relationship between the order parameter and magnetization markedly contrasts traditional spin ferromagnets, where spin magnetization is simply proportional to the groundstate spin polarization via the gyromagnetic ratio. We compute energy and magnetization curves as functions of collective valley rotation to shed light on magnetization dynamics and to expand the Stoner-Wohlfarth magnetization reversal model. We provide predictions on the magnetic coercive field that can be readily tested in experiments. Our results challenge established perceptions of magnetism, emphasising the important role of orbital moments in two-dimensional materials such as graphene and transition metal dichalcogenides, and in turn, expand our understanding and potential manipulation of magnetic behaviors in these systems.



Introduction:
Ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic behaviors of most materials are principally guided by the Pauli exclusion principle, which influences the momentum and spatial distributions of electrons based on their relative spin orientations
Herring 1966
. Traditionally, magnetization in these materials has been attributed to the accumulation of spin moments, while the contribution of orbital moments induced by spin-orbit coupling has often been regarded as secondary or negligible. However, this conventional understanding changes in two-dimensional materials like graphene, where electrons in the low-energy regime possess an additional degree of freedom – known as valley – that is intrinsically associated with orbital moments.
The interplay between the Pauli exclusion principle and the expanded four-dimensional spin-valley space in graphene gives rise to an unconventional form of magnetism, wherein orbital moments can compete or even surpass the role of spin moments in determining the overall magnetization.

Discussion and outlook:

The recently discovered systems of multilayer graphene, perturbed by either the moiré potential or/and dielectric displacement field, demonstrate a unique form of ferromagnetism

Zhou et al. 2021a
;
Zhou et al. 2021b
;
Seiler et al. 2022
;
Han et al. 2023
;
He et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2021
;
Tschirhart et al. 2021
;
Sinha et al. 2022
;
Pantaleón et al. 2022
. In these systems, orbital moments can order through exchange-induced valley polarization, thereby playing a crucial role in shaping the overall magnetization. Notably, we found that the orbital magnetization
M
exhibits sign changes and non-analytic behavior, even while the valley polarization remains unchanged. This magnetic behavior starkly deviates from the conventional relationship between spin-magnetization
M_{s}
and spin-polarization
S
:
M_{s}=\gamma S
, where
\gamma
is the gyromagnetic ratio.
At its core, this difference represents two distinct mechanisms by which a ferromagnetic ground state adjusts its energy in response to an external magnetic field, as per the thermodynamic relation:
dE=-(M+M_{s})dB
. The orbital magnetization is associated with the leading order variations in the quasiparticle wavefunctions,
\ket{\delta\psi_{n\bm{k}}}
, induced by the (orbital) magnetic field. Conversely, spin magnetization corresponds directly to the ground state’s spin-polarization, which is not associated with changes in the quasiparticle wavefunction or quasiparticle energy. Unlike spin-polarization is a property of the occupied states only,
\ket{\delta\psi_{n\bm{k}}}
depends on properties of the entire eigenspectrum, including both occupied and unoccupied states. As such, as the valley-Ising quarter metal traverses the
n_{e}-U
parameter space, adjustments to the quasiparticle wavefunctions — such as those required to increase layer polarization
sup
— without modifying the spin-polarization can prompt a significant change in orbital magnetization, all while leaving the spin magnetization undisturbed.

Our prediction regarding the sign-changing behavior of
M
can be verified by observing the divergence of the coercive field, while the divergent characteristic of
\partial M/\partial n_{e}
at the ALT can be corroborated by measuring
\partial\mu/\partial B
and Shubnikov-de Haas oscillations.

The softness of the collective valley rotation, coupled with the interesting landscape of
M
in the
n_{e}-U
space,indicates that the microscopic dynamics of metallic orbital magnetization warrants a deeper investigation. Our work paves the way for applications in novel electrical control of metallic orbital magnetization, offering a compelling outlook for the creation of future (valleytronic) devices.

-times smaller than the Brillouin zone of
H_{0}
. However, our interest lies primarily in the weak-field response limit, and so we may use first-order (many-body) perturbation theory to estimate the variation in total energy
\delta K
to a linear order in
V_{B}
:

\displaystyle\delta K=\sum_{n,\bm{k}}\left[n_{F}(\delta\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\langle\psi_{n,\bm{k}}|\hat{K}_{0}|\psi_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle+n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\langle\psi_{n,\bm{k}}|\hat{V}_{B}|\psi_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle+n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\left(\langle\delta\psi_{n,\bm{k}}|\hat{K}_{0}|\psi_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle+\langle\psi_{n,\bm{k}}|\hat{K}_{0}|\delta\psi_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle\right)\right],

(S7)

\delta\epsilon_{n\bm{k}}=\langle\psi_{n,\bm{k}}|\hat{V}_{B}|\psi_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle,

(S8)

\displaystyle|\delta\psi_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle=\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n,\bm{k^{\prime}}}\frac{\langle\psi_{n^{\prime},\bm{k^{\prime}}}|\hat{V}_{B}|\psi_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle}{\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k^{\prime}}}\right)}|\psi_{n^{\prime},\bm{k^{\prime}}}\rangle\;+\quad\sum_{n,\bm{k^{\prime}}\neq\bm{k}}\frac{\langle\psi_{n,\bm{k^{\prime}}}|\hat{V}_{B}|\psi_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle}{\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{n,\bm{k^{\prime}}}\right)}|\psi_{n,\bm{k^{\prime}}}\rangle.

(S9)

Following Ref.
Shi et al. 2007
, we opt for a field configuration where
V_{B}
remains small in all places, as opposed to the more commonly used Landau gauge:

\displaystyle\bm{B}(\bm{r})=B\cos(qy)\hat{e}_{z},\;\;,\;\;\bm{A}(\bm{r})=-B\frac{\sin(qy)}{q}\hat{e}_{x}.

(S10)

Here the wavevector
q
is small and the magnitude of the magnetic field
B
is a constant. Next, we substitute Eq. (
S10
) into Eq. (
S7
) and take the limit of
q\rightarrow 0
and arrive at the following equation:

\displaystyle\delta K(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=-\tilde{M}_{z}\,B\cos(qy)

(S11)

where
\tilde{M}_{z}
is independent of
q
. Thus

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{z}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{B\mathcal{A}}\lim_{q\rightarrow 0}\int\delta K(\bm{r})\cos(qy)d\bm{r}.

(S12)

This equation enables us to express the orbital magnetization in terms of the microscopic spectrum of
H_{0}
by employing first-order perturbation theory. In the following discussion, we provide details into the calculation of
\delta K(r)
.

It’s essential to highlight that the first two contributions in Eq. (
S7
) vanishes. This is because the expectation value of the current operator vanishes for the ground state and on the Fermi surface. Consequently, the orbital magnetization originates exclusively from the variation in the Bloch-state wavefunction. Let us know separate the third term in n Eq. (
S7
) into the inter-band and intra-band contributions:

\displaystyle\delta K^{(inter)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,\bm{k}}n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n,\bm{k^{\prime}}}\left[\frac{\langle\psi_{n,\bm{k}}|\hat{K}_{0}|\psi_{n^{\prime},\bm{k^{\prime}}}\rangle\langle\psi_{n^{\prime},\bm{k^{\prime}}}|\hat{V}_{B}|\psi_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle}{\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k^{\prime}}}\right)}+c.c\right]

(S13)

\displaystyle\delta K^{(intra)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,\bm{k}}n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\sum_{\bm{k^{\prime}}\neq\bm{k}}\left[\frac{\langle\psi_{n,\bm{k}}|\hat{K}_{0}|\psi_{n,\bm{k^{\prime}}}\rangle\langle\psi_{n,\bm{k^{\prime}}}|\hat{V}_{B}|\psi_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle}{\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{n,\bm{k^{\prime}}}\right)}+c.c\right].

(S14)

The matrix element of velocity operator
\hat{\bm{v}}
is given by the following:

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{n^{\prime},\bm{k^{\prime}}}|\hat{\bm{v}}|\psi_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\hbar}\left[\nabla_{\bm{k}}\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}\delta_{\bm{k},\bm{k^{\prime}}}+\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k^{\prime}}}\right)\langle u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k^{\prime}}}|\nabla_{\bm{k}}u_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle\right].

(S15)

The matrix elements of the perturbation
V_{B}
is given by the following:

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{n^{\prime},\bm{k^{\prime}}}|\hat{V}_{B}|\psi_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{2\mathcal{A}}\int d\bm{r}\psi^{*}_{n^{\prime},\bm{k^{\prime}}}(\bm{r})\left[\bm{A}(\bm{r})\cdot\hat{\bm{v}}+\hat{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{r})\right]\psi_{n,\bm{k}}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=-\frac{eB}{4iq\mathcal{A}}\bm{\hat{e}_{x}}\cdot\left[\int d\bm{r}e^{i(\bm{k}+\bm{q}-\bm{k^{\prime}})\cdot\bm{r}}u^{*}_{n^{\prime},\bm{k^{\prime}}}(\bm{r})\left(\bm{\hat{v}_{k}}+\bm{\hat{v}_{k^{\prime}}}\right)u_{n,\bm{k}}(\bm{r})-\left(\bm{q}\rightarrow-\bm{q}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{eB}{4iq}\bm{\hat{e}_{x}}\cdot\Big[\delta_{\bm{k^{\prime}},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\langle u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}|\left(\bm{\hat{v}_{k}}+\bm{\hat{v}_{k+q}}\right)|u_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle-\left(\bm{q}\rightarrow-\bm{q}\right)\Big]

(S16)

Here
\langle u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}|\left(\bm{\hat{v}_{k}}+\bm{\hat{v}_{k+q}}\right)|u_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{UC}}\int_{UC}d\bm{x}\penalty\ u^{*}_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(\bm{x})\left(\bm{\hat{v}_{k}}+\bm{\hat{v}_{k+q}}\right)\ u_{n,\bm{k}}(\bm{x})
.
Using these expression, we arrive at the following equation for
\delta K^{(inter)}\equiv\int\delta K^{(inter)}(\bm{r})d\bm{r}
:

\displaystyle\delta K^{(inter)}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=-\frac{eB}{4iq\mathcal{A}}\bm{\hat{e}_{x}}\cdot\sum_{n,\bm{k}}n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n}\left[\psi^{*}_{n,\bm{k}}(\bm{r})\hat{K}_{0}\psi_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(\bm{r})\frac{\langle u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}|\left(\bm{\hat{v}_{k}}+\bm{\hat{v}_{k+q}}\right)|u_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle}{\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\right)}-\left(\bm{q}\rightarrow-\bm{q}\right)\right]+c.c

\displaystyle=\Bigg[-\frac{eB}{8iq\mathcal{A}}\bm{\hat{e}_{x}}\cdot\sum_{n,\bm{k}}n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n}\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}+\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}-2\mu\right)e^{i\bm{q}\bm{r}}u^{*}_{n,\bm{k}}(\bm{r})u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\langle u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}|\left(\bm{\hat{v}_{k}}+\bm{\hat{v}_{k+q}}\right)|u_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle}{\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\right)}-\left(\bm{q}\rightarrow-\bm{q}\right)\Bigg]+c.c

(S17)

where we have used the following equation in the second line

\displaystyle\psi^{*}_{n,\bm{k}}(\bm{r})\hat{K}_{0}\psi_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}+\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}-2\mu\right)e^{i\bm{q}\bm{r}}u^{*}_{n,\bm{k}}(\bm{r})u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(\bm{r})

(S18)

Next we substitute
K^{(inter)}(\bm{r})
into Eq.(
S12
), and performed the integration

\int d\bm{r}\cos(\vec{q}\cdot\vec{r})e^{i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}u^{*}_{n,\bm{k}}(\bm{r})u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(\bm{r})
. This lead to the following expression for the inter-band contribution to the zero-temperature magnetization,
\tilde{M}_{z}^{(inter)}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{M}^{(inter)}_{z}

\displaystyle=\lim_{q\rightarrow 0}\Bigg[\frac{e}{8iq\mathcal{A}}\bm{\hat{e}_{x}}\cdot\sum_{n,\bm{k}}n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n}\quad\langle u_{n,\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\rangle\frac{\langle u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}|\left(\bm{\hat{v}_{k}}+\bm{\hat{v}_{k+q}}\right)|u_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle}{\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\right)}\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}+\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}+\bm{q}}-2\mu\right)-\left(\bm{q}\rightarrow-\bm{q}\right)\Bigg]+c.c.

(S19)

Following the same steps, we can derive the intraband contribution which combine to give us the following total magnetization:

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{z}

\displaystyle=\lim_{q\rightarrow 0}\Big[\frac{e}{8iq\mathcal{A}}\bm{\hat{e}_{x}}\cdot\sum_{n,n^{\prime},\bm{k}}n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\langle u_{n,\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\rangle\frac{\langle u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}|\left(\bm{\hat{v}_{k}}+\bm{\hat{v}_{k+q}}\right)|u_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle}{\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\right)}\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}+\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}+\bm{q}}-2\mu\right)-\left(\bm{q}\rightarrow-\bm{q}\right)\Big]+c.c

\displaystyle=\lim_{q\rightarrow 0}\frac{e}{4q\mathcal{A}}\bm{\hat{e}_{x}}\cdot\sum_{n,n^{\prime},\bm{k}}\operatorname{Im}\Big[\frac{\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}+\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}-2\mu\right)\langle u_{n,\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\rangle\langle u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}|\left(\bm{\hat{v}_{k}}+\bm{\hat{v}_{k+q}}\right)|u_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle}{\left(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\right)}\left(n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})-n_{F}(\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}})\right)\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{2\mathcal{A}\hbar}\bm{\hat{e}_{x}}\cdot\sum_{n,\bm{k}}\operatorname{Im}\Big[n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\langle\frac{\partial u_{n,\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}|(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}+\hat{H}_{0}(\bm{k})-2\mu)\times|\frac{\partial u_{n,\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}\rangle-(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\mu)n_{F}^{\prime}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\langle\frac{\partial u_{n,\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}|(\hat{H}_{0}(\bm{k})-\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\times|\frac{\partial u_{n,\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}\rangle\Big]

(S20)

Here
n_{F}^{\prime}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})
represents the derivative of the Fermi-Dirac distribution function
n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})
with respect to
\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}
. At zero temperature,
(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\mu)n_{F}^{\prime}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})=0
, and the intra-band contribution becomes zero.

In the thermodynamic limit, the momentum spacing becomes continuous and we arrive at the following zero-temperature expression for the orbital magnetization used in the maintext:

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{2\hbar}\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\operatorname{Im}\sum_{n}n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\langle\frac{\partial u_{n,\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}|(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}+\hat{H}_{0}(\bm{k})-2\mu)\times|\frac{\partial u_{n,\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}\rangle

(S21)

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{2\hbar}\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\operatorname{Im}\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n}\frac{\langle u_{n,\bm{k}}|\frac{\partial\hat{H}_{0}}{\partial\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}}\rangle\times\langle u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}}|\frac{\partial\hat{H}_{0}}{\partial\bm{k}}|u_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle}{(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}})^{2}}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}+\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}}-2\mu)

(S22)

Note
M
is invariant under any gauge transformation of the Bloch-waves:
u_{n\bm{k}}\rightarrow e^{i\theta(\bm{k})}u_{n\bm{k}}
. It is convenient to separate
M
into a bulk contribution (
M_{B}
) and edge contribution (
M_{E}
)
M=M_{B}+M_{E}
:

\displaystyle M_{B}

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{2\hbar}\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\operatorname{Im}\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n}\frac{\langle u_{n,\bm{k}}|\frac{\partial\hat{H}_{0}}{\partial\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}}\rangle\times\langle u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}}|\frac{\partial\hat{H}_{0}}{\partial\bm{k}}|u_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle}{(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}})^{2}}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}+\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}}),

(S23)

\displaystyle M_{E}

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{2\hbar}\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\operatorname{Im}\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n}\frac{\langle u_{n,\bm{k}}|\frac{\partial\hat{H}_{0}}{\partial\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}}\rangle\times\langle u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}}|\frac{\partial\hat{H}_{0}}{\partial\bm{k}}|u_{n,\bm{k}}\rangle}{(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}})^{2}}(-2\mu)=\frac{\mu e}{\hbar}\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{n}n_{F}(\epsilon_{n,\bm{k}})\bm{\Omega}_{n,\bm{k}}.

(S24)

Here
\bm{\Omega}_{n,\bm{k}}
denotes the Berry curvature.
They can be determined separately because
M_{B}
is independent of the chemical potential
\mu
when
\mu
is inside the gap of an insulator while

M_{e}=e\mu C_{occ}/h
is simply proportional to total Chern number
C_{occ}
of occupied bands. Thus, the orbital magnetization jumps by a factor

\Delta M=\frac{e\penalty\ C_{occ}}{h}E_{gap}
across an energy gap
E_{gap}
.

II
B: Bandstructure, Density of states, Layer polarization and Valley-projected Orbital magnetization of the Paramagnetic state

In this section, we discuss the properties of the band Hamiltonian
\hat{T}_{\bm{k}}
of rhombohedral trilayer graphene (RTG) in the maintext and used the eigenspectrum of
\hat{T}_{\bm{k}}
to discuss valley-projected orbital magnetization
M^{\tau}
v.s. the chemical potential
\mu
. The total magnetization
M^{\tau=+1}+M^{\tau=-1}=0
because the groundstate of
\hat{T}_{\bm{k}}
has time-reversal symmetry.

RTG contains 3 layers of carbon atoms. The interlayer distance is
3.4
Å and each layer has two sublattices label by
\sigma=A,B
which is separated by
a=2.46
Å. The layers of RTG are in a ABC stacking configuration.
Following Refs.
Zhang et al. 2010
;
Koshino and McCann 2009
, we consider a single electronic orbital (
\pi
) per atom, with spin
s=\pm 1/2
, and use the continuum model that can be derived from a tight-binding Hamiltonian by expanding the low-energy dispersion around each valley
\tau
with
\tau=\pm 1
at the corners of the Brillouin zone. We label the four flavor combinations of spin
s
and valley
\tau
with the flavor index
\alpha\equiv(\tau,s)
. In the basis
\ket{\psi_{\alpha}(\bm{k})}=(\psi_{\alpha A1}(\bm{k}),\psi_{\alpha B1}(\bm{k}),\psi_{\alpha A2}(\bm{k}),\psi_{\alpha B2}(\bm{k}),\psi_{\alpha A3}(\bm{k}),\psi_{\alpha B3}(\bm{k}))
, the band Hamiltonian is given by

\hat{T}_{\bm{k}}=\sum_{s=\pm 1,\tau=\pm 1}\ket{\psi_{\tau,s}(\bm{k})}h_{\tau}(\bm{k})\bra{\psi_{\tau,s}(\bm{k})}.

(S25)

The continuum Hamiltonian acting on the orbital space is different in opposite valley but spin-degenerate

\displaystyle h_{\tau}(\bm{k})=\begin{bmatrix}t(\bm{k})+U_{1}&t_{12}(\bm{k})&t_{13}\\
t_{12}^{\dagger}(\bm{k})&t(\bm{k})+U_{2}&t_{12}(\bm{k})\\
t_{13}^{\dagger}&t_{12}^{\dagger}(\bm{k})&t(\bm{k})+U_{3}\end{bmatrix}_{6\times 6}.

(S26)

Here
\bm{k}=(k_{x},k_{y})
is the Bloch momentum measured with respect to the center of valley
\tau
. This Hamiltonian contains matrices with intralayer hopping amplitudes (
t
), nearest-layer hopping amplitudes (
t_{12}
), and next-nearest-layer hopping amplitudes (
t_{13}
), as well as possible potential differences between different layers and/or sublattices due to external gates and/or broken symmetries (terms
U_{i}
). Explicitly, the various hopping amplitudes are given by

\displaystyle t(\bm{k})=\begin{bmatrix}0&v_{0}\pi^{\dagger}\\
v_{0}\pi&0\end{bmatrix},\quad t_{12}(\bm{k})=\begin{bmatrix}-v_{4}\pi^{\dagger}&v_{3}\pi\\
\gamma_{1}&-v_{4}\pi^{\dagger}\end{bmatrix},\quad t_{13}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\gamma_{2}/2\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}

(S27)

where
\pi=\tau k_{x}+ik_{y}
is a linear momentum, and
\gamma_{i}
(

) are hopping amplitudes of the tight-binding model with corresponding velocity parameters
v_{i}=(\sqrt{3}/2)a\gamma_{i}/\hbar
. The
{U_{i}}

\displaystyle U_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\Delta+\delta+U}{2}&0\\
0&\frac{\Delta}{2}\end{bmatrix},\quad U_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}-\Delta&0\\
0&-\Delta\end{bmatrix},\quad U_{3}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\Delta}{2}&0\\
0&\frac{\Delta+\delta-U}{2}\end{bmatrix}

(S28)

We use the model parameters listed in Table I, which are chosen to match quantum oscillation frequency signatures in Ref.
Zhou et al. 2021a

C: Layer polarization and different contributions of orbital magnetization in the quarter metal phase

In this section, we expand the discussion of orbital magnetization in the quarter metal phase. For the valley-Ising quarter metal,

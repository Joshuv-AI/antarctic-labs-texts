Effects of a background scalar field induced by the Lorentz symmetry violation on Non Relativistic Quantum Mechanics
J. D. García-Aguilar

Abstract
The extension of Standard Model of the Fundamental Particles which consider the Lorentz Symmetry Violation governed by a background tensor field presented by Colladay and Kostelecky consider the hypothesis of having a privileged direction in the space-time. Here we investigated a extension of the Schrödinger Equation inspired on that model taking into account a background scalar field. We analyze the behavior of this modified Schrödinger equation on a particle trapped in a well and quantum harmonic oscillator.

Introduction

The Standard Model Extension which consider the CPT symmetry violation (SME)

Lorentz symmetry violation leads to some modification on particle properties such as energy-dependent dispersion relations and field equations (see, e.g., Refs.

;
Coleman:1997xq
;
Coleman:1998ti
;

Currently, from the model building view, a large effort has been done in different branches of physics to find possibles signal on Lorentz symmetry violation, and therefore, provide experimental bounds on SME parameters. Some of these studios utilizing neutrinos

In the present work, we seek to address this gap in the literature by extending the existing work of SME in non relativistic quantum mechanic sector to study its implications over Schrödinger equation, thereby opening the path for additional searching for Lorentz violation. To achieve this goal, we have investigated the effects of a background constant scalar field induced by Lorentz symmetry breaking. Then, we search a bound state solutions considering a time independent modified Schrödinger equation and shown that the Lorentz violation terms can be eliminated through a redefinition on wavefunctions.

II
The modified Schrödinger equation

In order to obtain the modified Schrödinger equation, we perform the study considering the scalar CPT-odd sector of the Lorentz Violation Standard Model Extension
Colladay:1996iz
whose Lagrangian is given by

\mathcal{L}=\left(D_{\mu}\phi\right)^{\dagger}D^{\mu}\phi+\mu^{2}\phi^{\dagger}\phi-\frac{\lambda}{3!}\left(\phi^{\dagger}\phi\right)^{2}+ik^{\mu}\phi^{\dagger}D_{\mu}\phi+h.c.,

(1)

where
\phi
denote the Higgs doublet,
D_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative and
k^{\mu}
is the coefficient which quantifies the Lorentz violation which has dimensions of mass and obeys the relation
k_{\mu}k^{\mu}\ll 1
.

For our purposes we are going to consider a massless scalar field without interactions. Therefore, the equation
1
is reduced to

\mathcal{L}=\partial_{\mu}\phi^{\dagger}\partial^{\mu}\phi+ik_{\mu}\phi^{\dagger}\partial^{\mu}\phi-ik^{\mu}\phi\partial_{\mu}\phi^{\dagger},

(2)

which can be rewritten as

\mathcal{L}=\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}\phi^{\dagger}\partial^{\prime\mu}\phi,

(3)

considering

\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+ik_{\mu},

(4)

so, the redefinition for the derivative operator allows to eliminate the CPT violating term.

Hence, the redefinition
4
establish the possibility to investigate the effects of the momentum operator redefinition given by

\hat{p}=-i\hbar\left(\frac{d}{dx}+i\alpha\right)

(5)

where
\alpha
is a background constant scalar field whose value quantifies the effects of Lorentz symmetry violation.

The equation
5
leads to rewrite the Schrödinger equation for the free particle in the form
\left(\hbar=1\right)

-\frac{1}{2m}\left(\frac{d^{2}\psi}{dx^{2}}+2i\alpha\frac{d\psi}{dx}-\alpha^{2}\psi\right)=E\psi,

(6)

where, it is straightforward to see how the equation
6
is not invariant under
x\rightarrow-x
parity transformation.

We can rewrite the equation
6
in the form

\frac{d^{2}\psi}{dx^{2}}+2i\alpha\frac{d\psi}{dx}=-\left(k^{2}-\alpha^{2}\right)\psi,

(7)

where we define
k=\sqrt{2mE}
.

By imposing that
k^{2}>\alpha^{2}
, we obtain the solution

\psi\left(x\right)=Ae^{i\left(k-\alpha\right)x}+Be^{-i\left(k+\alpha\right)x},

(8)

where, we can see how the effects of Lorentz symmetry violation is present on wavefunctions whereas energy eigenvalues is independent of background scalar field.

Observe that in the limit
\alpha\rightarrow 0
the Lorentz symmetry is restored, so the equations
5
,
6
and
8
are analogous to the usual equations to describe the quantum free particle
Landau:1991wop
;
Griffiths:1987tj
. This is to be expected because if we make a wavefunction redefinition

\psi\left(x\right)\rightarrow e^{-i\alpha x}\psi\left(x\right),

(9)

then the equation
6
becomes

-\frac{1}{2m}\frac{d^{2}\psi}{dx^{2}}=E\psi,

(10)

which is the canonical Schrödinger equation for the free particle.

III
Particle on a box

In this section, we investigate the effects of the background constant constant field over a particle trapped in a well with potential given by

V\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}0&0\leq x\leq L\\
\infty&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

(11)

and hence a particle is completely free, except at the two ends and therefore the wavefunction is only different zero inside the well.

We know from section
II
that the solution for the free particle considering the operator
5
(in order to consider the Lorentz symmetry violation) and subject to the condition

\psi\left(0\right)=\psi\left(L\right)=0,

(12)

is given by

\psi_{n}\left(x\right)=Ae^{-i\alpha x}\sin\left(k_{n}x\right),

(13)

where

k_{n}=\frac{n\pi}{L}

(14)

with
n=1,2,3,\ldots
.

From the expression
13
we obtain the eigenvalues for the Energy in the form

E_{n}=\frac{n^{2}\pi^{2}}{2mL^{2}}

(15)

and the normalization constant sets

A=\sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}.

(16)

Assuming that
\alpha=1/10
and
L=10
for purposes of demonstration we obtain the plot of the first four graphs of the real part to the equation
13
shown in figure

\alpha
value. As we expected in the limit
\alpha\rightarrow 0
, the solution have the same features when is taking into account the usual definition for the momentum operator on the system

;

.

We have know an important result: the effects of Lorentz symmetry violation appears only over wave functions for this system, the observables are independent of background scalar field. Therefore, the Lorentz Violation does not lead to alterations on the Heisenberg uncertainty principle

IV
The Harmonic Oscillator case

In this section, our aim is to solve the Schrödinger equation taking into account the operator
5
and the potential given by

V=\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}x^{2}.

(17)

Thus, the time independent Schrödinger equation to solve is given in the form

-\frac{1}{2m}\left(\frac{d^{2}\psi}{dx^{2}}+2i\alpha\frac{d\psi}{dx}-\alpha^{2}\psi\right)+\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}x^{2}\psi=E\psi.

(18)

which we can solve considering the following ladder operators

a_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m\omega}}\left(\mp\left(\frac{d}{dx}+i\alpha\right)+m\omega x\right),

(19)

where direct calculation reveals that

\left[a_{-},a_{+}\right]=1.

(20)

With this, equation
18
becomes

\omega\left(a_{+}a_{-}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\psi=E\psi,

(21)

and through the annihilation operator we can determine the ground state (
\psi_{0}
) of the system

a_{-}\psi_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m\omega}}\left(\left(\frac{d}{dx}+i\alpha\right)+m\omega x\right)\psi_{0}=0.

(22)

The solution for this differential equation is straightforward to obtain and is given by

\psi_{0}\left(x\right)=Ae^{-\frac{m\omega}{2}x^{2}}e^{-i\alpha x}

(23)

where the normalization constant take the value

A=\left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi}\right)^{1/4},

(24)

and to determine the energy we substitute the equation
23
into right side of the equation
18
, then

E_{0}=\frac{\omega}{2},

(25)

we can see how the last two results does not depend on the
\alpha
parameter.

To generate the excited states we can use the creation operator
a_{+}
over ground state repeatedly

\psi_{n}\left(x\right)=A_{n}a_{+}\psi_{0}\left(x\right),

(26)

where
A_{n}
is the normalization constant and the energy is incremented by
\omega
with each step

E_{n}=\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\omega.

(27)

26

V
Conclusions

This papper deals with time independent Schrödinger equation under effect of the CPT-odd Lorentz violation. We have shown that momemtum operator is modified and, consequently provides a modified Schrödinger equation to be investigated. In this sense, we present the wavefunctions for two different systems.

In our first analysis, we considered a particle trapped in a well, where we have shown that the effects of the Lorentz violation appears only on wavefunctions whereas the energy levels and normalization constant are similar to the case where Lorentz symmetry is invariant. In the second analysis the harmonic oscilator is solved through a redefinition on the ladder operators and we find that similarly to the first analysis, the Lorentz violation is present only on the wavefunctions.

As we seen the presence of Lorentz symmetry violation in the Schrödinger equation for a particle in a well and harmonic oscillator can not induce modifications over energy levels of the system and therefore, it is not possible to expect signal of SME in this energy scale. It is worth mentioning that our analysis can be considered beyond one dimensión and it is in our interest as future perspective to analize the two and three dimensional case.

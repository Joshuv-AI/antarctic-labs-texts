Variational Eigenvalues of the fractional g g -Laplacian
Sabri Bahrouni, Hichem Ounaies, Ariel Salort

Abstract
. In the present work we study existence of sequences of variational eigenvalues to non-local non-standard growth problems ruled by the fractional g − g- Laplacian operator with different boundary conditions (Dirichlet, Neumann and Robin). Due to the non-homogeneous nature of the operator several drawbacks must be overcome, leading to some results that contrast with the case of power functions.



1.
Introduction

Given two functionals
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
defined on a suitable space
\mathcal{X}
and a prescribed number
c
, the task of analyzing the existence of numbers
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and elements
u\in\mathcal{X}
satisfying (in some appropriated sense) equations of the type

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u)=\lambda\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(u),\qquad\mathcal{A}(u)=c,

has been a challenging labor whose beginning dates back to the mid-20th century (here
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
denote the Fréchet derivatives of the functionals). The study on Hilbert spaces was addressed by Krasnoselskij in
[
37
]
; for Banach spaces, it can be found in the literature the works of Citlanadze
[
20
]
and Browder
[
15
,
18
,
19
]
, where the notion of
category of sets
in the sense of Ljusternik and Schnirelman is used. See also
[
30
,
31
]
for some applications to partial differential equations. The amount of research on these topics is nowadays huge. For practical reasons, for an introduction to this theory and a comprehensive list of references we refer to the books
[
16
,
17
,
47
,
56
]
.

After the introduction of the so-called monotonicity methods by Browder
[
13
,
14
]
, Minty
[
44
]
and Vaǐnberg and Kačurovskiǐ
[
54
]
, the study of quasilinear operators experimented an explosive growth, and both variational and nonvariational techniques were introduced by Browder, Fučík, Ladyzhenskaya, Leray, Lions, Morrey, Nečas, Rabinowicz, Schauder, Serrin, and Trudinger, among several other mathematicians.

The prototypical
p-
Laplace operator (
p>1
) then became a focus of study, and in particular, to understand its spectral structure: given an open and bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, to determine the existence couples
(\lambda,u)
satisfying the equation

(1.1)

-\mathop{\text{div}}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}u)=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega

in a suitable sense. In the seminal work of García Azorero and Peral Alonso
[
35
]
, it was proved the existence of a variational sequence of eigenvalues tending to
+\infty
, which however, it is not known to exhaust the spectrum unless
p=2
or
n=1
. Several properties on eigenvalues (and their corresponding eigenfunctions) were addressed by Anane et al
[
3
,
4
]
and Lindqvist
[
41
]
, among others, and also for more general boundary conditions than Dirichlet. See also
[
28
,
42
]
.

At this point, two possible generalizations of the eigenvalue problem (
1.1
) could be considered. First, its non-local counterpart governed by the well-known
fractional
p-
Laplace
operator takes the form

(1.2)

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega

where
s\in(0,1)
is a fractional parameter,
p>1
, and

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u(x):=\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy.

The main difference here arises in the fact that this operator takes into account interactions coming from the whole space. The same occurs with the
boundary
condition. Problem (
1.2
) was introduced in
[
40
]
. Existence of a sequence of (variational) eigenvalues to (
1.2
) and its behavior as
s\uparrow 1
was dealt in
[
11
]
. Several properties on eigenvalues and eigenfunctions were obtained in
[
12
,
22
,
33
,
40
]
.

A second possible generalization of (
1.1
) can be obtained keeping the local structure of the operator but allowing a growth behavior more general than a power. These considerations lead to the well-known
g-
Laplace
operator and the problem

-\Delta_{g}u=\lambda g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

where
\Delta_{g}u:=\mathop{\text{div}}\left(g(|\nabla u|)\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\right)
. The function
g=G^{\prime}
is given in terms of a so-called Young function
G
. Here the structure of the spectrum radically changes due to the non-homogeneity of the operator, fact that is key in the arguments corresponding to results related to problems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). Here several important differences appear: the spectrum may not be discrete, then it is not clear the notion of a first eigenvalue nor of a sequence of them. However, when restricting the
energy level
of functions, some properties of operators with
p-
structure are recovered: in
[
34
,
36
,
45
,
46
]
existence of eigenvalues was studied, and in
[
25
,
53
]
the existence of a discrete sequence of eigenvalues was obtained. It is worth of mention that under these settings, eigenvalues are not in general variational, so less information can be retrieved when using standard techniques.

The object of study of this manuscript is the eigenvalue problem ruled by the
fractional
g-
Laplacian
, which can be seen as the non-local non-standard growth counterpart of (
1.1
). Given an open and bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a fractional parameter
s\in(0,1)
, we are concerned with the following eigenvalue problems:

•

Dirichlet problem:

(
D(\Omega)
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u=\lambda g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}&\text{ in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

•

Neumann problem:

(
N(\Omega)
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u=\lambda g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}&\text{ in }\Omega\\
\mathcal{N}_{g}u=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

•

Robin problem: given
\beta\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega)

(
R(\Omega)
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u=\lambda g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}&\text{ in }\Omega\\
\mathcal{N}_{g}u+\beta g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

The precise notion of
eigenvalues
and
eigenfunctions
to these problems is defined in Section
3
.

Here, the fractional
g-
Laplacian is defined as

(1.3)

(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u:=\,\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(|D_{s}u|\right)\frac{D_{s}u}{|D_{s}u|}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}},

where
G
is a Young function (see Section
2
for the precise definition) such that
g=G^{\prime}
and
D_{s}u:=\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}
. Clearly,
(-\Delta_{g})^{s}
boils down to the fractional
p-
Laplacian when
G
is a power function and to the
p-
Laplacian when
s\uparrow 1
. See
[
26
]
. The boundary conditions in the previous problems reflect the non-local nature of the operator
(-\Delta_{g})^{s}
: the Dirichlet case corresponds to functions vanishing outside
\Omega
and not only on
\partial\Omega
, whereas the Neumann and Robin equations make use of the nonlocal normal derivative
\mathcal{N}_{g}
introduced in
[
6
]
.

Throughout this article we assume the Young function
G=\int_{0}^{t}g(t)\,dt
to satisfy the following structural conditions:

(
G_{1}
)

1<p^{-}\leq\frac{tg(t)}{G(t)}\leq p^{+}<\infty\quad\forall t>0,

(
G_{2}
)

t\mapsto G(\sqrt{t}),\ t\in[0,\infty)\text{ is convex},

(
G_{3}
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{G^{-1}(\tau)}{\tau^{\frac{n+s}{n}}}d\tau<\infty\ \quad\text{and}\quad\displaystyle\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{G^{-1}(\tau)}{\tau^{\frac{n+s}{n}}}d\tau=\infty.

In the case of powers (i.e.
G(t)=t^{p}
) these conditions mean that
2\leq p\leq\frac{np}{n-sp}
.

The following functionals have an essential role in the study of eigenvalue problems for the fractional
g-
Laplacian

(1.4)

\mathcal{I}(u):=\int_{\Omega}G(|u|)\,dx,\qquad\mathcal{J}(u):=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D_{s}u|)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n}}.

and

(1.5)

\mathcal{J}_{\beta}(u):=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus(\Omega^{c})^{2}}G(|D_{s}u|)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\beta G(|u|)\,dx

(see Section
3
for details).

Problem

D

(
Ω
)

was recently studied in
[
49
,
50
]
, where, once established existence of a minimizer of the associated problem under energy constraint

\Lambda_{\alpha}^{D}:=\min\left\{\mathcal{J}(u)\colon\mathcal{I}(u)=\alpha\right\}

for a given value
\alpha>0
, then by means of a version of the Lagrange multipliers rule it is deduced existence of an eigenvalue
\lambda_{\alpha}^{D}
of

D

(
Ω
)

in a suitable sense, and in general
\Lambda_{\alpha}^{D}\neq\lambda_{\alpha}^{D}
. A further extension of this result for problems

N

(
Ω
)

and

R

(
Ω
)

is provided in
[
6
]
. Other very recent results involving eigenvalues of the fractional
g-
Laplacian can be found in
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
23
,
27
,
29
,
49
,
51
]
.

In the wake of the non-homogeneity of the operator, many of the properties that eigenvalues of the fractional
p-
Laplacian fulfill are possibly not inherited in the non-homogeneous case: for instance, isolation, simplicity and a variational characterization of the first eigenvalue, or a variational formula for the second one (see
[
22
,
33
,
40
]
). Moreover, the spectrum of

D

(
Ω
)

could be continuous when
G
is a general Young function, and in principle, it is not clear the meaning of a first or second eigenvalue. Due to these drawbacks, the main aim of this manuscript is to understand under which conditions it is possible to build a sequence of eigenvalues to problems

D

(
Ω
)

,

N

(
Ω
)

and

R

(
Ω
)

.

Our arguments are based in the fact that existence of sequences of variational eigenvalues can be established when prescribing some energy level. First, when the quantities
\mathcal{J}(u)
or
\mathcal{J}_{\beta}(u)
involving the
s-
Hölder quotient are prescribed, by means of the Ljusternik-Schnirelman theory we can infer existence of a discrete sequence of critical points of
\mathcal{I}(u)
which allow us to build a sequence of non-negative eigenvalues. With the notation introduced in Section
2
, our first result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
\alpha>0
there exist sequences of non-negative numbers
\{\lambda^{D}_{k,\alpha}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}},\{\lambda^{N}_{k,\alpha}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\lambda^{R}_{k,\alpha}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
which are eigenvalues of problems

D

(
Ω
)

,

N

(
Ω
)

and

R

(
Ω
)

, respectively. Moreover, these sequences diverge as
k\to\infty
.

The corresponding eigenfunctions

\{u_{k,\alpha}^{D}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega),\quad\{u_{k,\alpha}^{N}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{s,G}_{*}(\Omega)\quad\text{ and }\quad\{u_{k,\alpha}^{R}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{X}_{\beta}(\Omega)

satisfy the constraints

\mathcal{J}(u_{k,\alpha}^{D})=\mathcal{J}_{0}(u_{k,\alpha}^{N})=\mathcal{J}_{\beta}(u_{k,\alpha}^{R})=\alpha

and

\mathcal{I}(u_{k,\alpha}^{D})=c_{k,\alpha}^{D},\qquad\mathcal{I}(u_{k,\alpha}^{N})=c_{k,\alpha}^{N},\qquad\mathcal{I}(u_{k,\alpha}^{R})=c_{k,\alpha}^{R}

where the critical values are obtained as

c_{k,\alpha}^{D}=\sup_{K\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{D}}\inf_{u\in K}\mathcal{I}(u),\qquad c_{k,\alpha}^{N}=\sup_{K\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{N}}\inf_{u\in K}\mathcal{I}(u),\qquad c_{k,\alpha}^{R}=\sup_{K\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{R}}\inf_{u\in K}\mathcal{I}(u)

where, denoting by
\gamma(K)
the Krasnoselskii genus of
K
, we define

\mathcal{C}_{k}^{D}:=\{K\subset M_{\alpha}^{D}\text{ compact, symmetric with }\mathcal{I}(u)>0\text{ on }K\text{ and }\gamma(K)\geq k\},

and

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}^{D}

\displaystyle:=\{u\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\colon\mathcal{J}(u)=\alpha\},

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}^{N}

\displaystyle:=\{u\in W^{s,G}_{*}(\Omega)\colon\mathcal{J}_{0}(u)=\alpha\},

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}^{R}

\displaystyle:=\{u\in\mathcal{X}_{\beta}(\Omega)\colon\mathcal{J}_{\beta}(u)=\alpha\}.

The sets
\mathcal{C}_{k}^{N}
and
\mathcal{C}_{k}^{R}
are defined analogously by changing the superscript
D
by
N
and
R
, respectively.

Secondly, when the quantity
\mathcal{I}(u)
is prescribed, by using the so-called minimax theory we obtain a sequence of critical points of
\mathcal{J}(u)
which provides for a (different) sequence of eigenvalues:

Theorem 1.2
.

For any
\alpha>0
there exist sequences of non-negative eigenvalues
\{\Lambda^{D}_{k,\alpha}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\{\Lambda^{N}_{k,\alpha}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\Lambda^{R}_{k,\alpha}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of

D

(
Ω
)

,

N

(
Ω
)

and

R

(
Ω
)

, respectively.

The corresponding eigenfunctions

\{u_{k,\alpha}^{D}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega),\quad\{u_{k,\alpha}^{N}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{s,G}_{*}(\Omega)\quad\text{ and }\quad\{u_{k,\alpha}^{R}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{X}_{\beta}(\Omega)

satisfy the constraints

\mathcal{I}(u_{k,\alpha}^{D})=\mathcal{I}(u_{k,\alpha}^{N})=\mathcal{I}(u_{k,\alpha}^{R})=\alpha

and

\mathcal{J}(u_{k,\alpha}^{D})=C_{k,\alpha}^{D},\qquad\mathcal{J}_{0}(u_{k,\alpha}^{N})=C_{k,\alpha}^{N},\qquad\mathcal{J}_{\beta}(u_{k,\alpha}^{R})=C_{k,\alpha}^{R}

where the critical values are obtained as

C_{k,\alpha}^{D}=\inf_{h\in\Gamma(S^{k-1},M_{\alpha}^{D})}\sup_{w\in S^{k-1}}\mathcal{J}(h(w))

and
\Gamma(S^{k},M_{\alpha}^{D})=\{h\in C(S^{k},M_{\alpha}^{D})\colon h\text{ is odd}\}
, being
S^{k}
the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
.

The numbers
C_{k,\alpha}^{N}
and
C_{k,\alpha}^{R}
are defined analogously by changing the superscript
D
by
N
and
R
, respectively, and the functional
\mathcal{J}
by
\mathcal{J}_{0}
and
\mathcal{J}_{\beta}
, respectively.
M_{\alpha}^{D}
,
M_{\alpha}^{N}
and
M_{\alpha}^{R}
denote the sets

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}^{D}

\displaystyle:=\{u\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\colon\mathcal{I}(u)=\alpha\},

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}^{N}

\displaystyle:=\{u\in W^{s,G}_{*}(\Omega)\colon\mathcal{I}(u)=\alpha\},

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}^{R}

\displaystyle:=\{u\in\mathcal{X}_{\beta}(\Omega)\colon\mathcal{I}(u)=\alpha\}.

In contrast to what happens in the case of powers, the Ljusternik-Schnirelman and minimax eigenvalues obtained in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, in general, are neither the same nor easily comparable each other. As a consequence, in principle we cannot ensure that the minimax sequences built in Theorems
1.2
diverge as
k\to\infty
. For a discussion of this situation in the case
G(t)=t^{p}
see
[
24
,
43
]
. However, in the Dirichlet case we are able to compare miminax eigenvalues of the
g-
Laplacian with eigenvalues of the
p^{-}-
Laplacian (where
p^{-}
is given in (

G
1

)), giving as a consequence the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

With the notation of Theorem
1.2
,

\Lambda_{k,\alpha}^{D}\to\infty\text{ as }k\to\infty,\qquad C_{k,\alpha}^{D}\to\infty\text{ as }k\to\infty.

In the case of powers, eigenvalues and critical points obtained in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
coincide, i.e.,
\lambda_{k}=c_{k}
and
\Lambda_{k}=C_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. It does not occur for a general Young function, although the following comparison result holds.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\{\lambda_{k,\alpha}^{D}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{c_{k,\alpha}^{D}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be as in Theorem
1.1
, and let
\{\Lambda_{k,\alpha}^{D}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{C_{k,\alpha}^{D}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be as in Theorem
1.2
. Then

\frac{\alpha p^{-}}{p^{+}}\leq c_{k,\alpha}^{D}\cdot\lambda_{k,\alpha}^{D}\leq\frac{\alpha p^{+}}{p^{-}},\qquad\frac{p^{-}}{\alpha p^{+}}\leq\frac{\Lambda_{k,\alpha}^{D}}{C_{k,\alpha}^{D}}\leq\frac{p^{+}}{\alpha p^{-}},

where
p^{+}
and
p^{-}
are the numbers defined in (

G
1

).

Finally, our last result establishes the closedness of the spectrum of the fractional
g-
Laplacian in the following sense:

Theorem 1.5
.

Fixed
\alpha_{0}>0
, let
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\alpha_{0})
. Let
\{\lambda_{\alpha_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of eigenvalues of

(
Ω
)

with eigenfunctions
\{u_{\alpha_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)

\Phi_{G,\Omega}(u_{\alpha_{k}})=\alpha_{k}

Then, if

D

(
Ω
)

with eigenfunction

A similar assertion holds for sequences of eigenvalues of

(
Ω
)

and

(

.

Fixed
\alpha_{0}>0

\hat{\lambda}_{k}^{D}:=\inf_{\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})}\lambda_{k,\alpha}^{D},\quad\hat{\lambda}_{k}^{N}:=\inf_{\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})}\lambda_{k,\alpha}^{N},\quad\hat{\lambda}_{k}^{R}:=\inf_{\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})}\lambda_{k,\alpha}^{R}

(

,

2.
Definitions and preliminary results

In this section we introduce the classes of Young function and fractional Orlicz-Sobolev functions, the
suitable class where the fractional
g
-Laplacian is well defined.

2.1.
Young functions

An application
G\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is said to be a
Young function
if it admits the integral formulation
G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,d\tau
, where the right continuous function
g
defined on
[0,\infty)
has the following properties:

(
g_{1}
)

\displaystyle g(0)=0,\quad g(t)>0\text{ for }t>0,

(
g_{2}
)

\displaystyle g\text{ is non-decreasing on }(0,\infty),

(
g_{3}
)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)=\infty.

From these properties it is easy to see that a Young function
G
is continuous, nonnegative, strictly increasing and convex on
[0,\infty)
.

The following properties on Young functions are well-known. See for instance
[
38
]
for the proof of these results.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a Young function satisfying (

G
1

) and
a,b\geq 0
. Then

(
L_{1}
)

\displaystyle\min\{a^{p^{-}},a^{p^{+}}\}G(b)\leq G(ab)\leq\max\{a^{p^{-}},a^{p^{+}}\}G(b),

(
L_{2}
)

\displaystyle G(a+b)\leq\mathbf{C}(G(a)+G(b))\quad\text{with }\mathbf{C}:=2^{p^{+}},

(
L_{3}
)

\displaystyle G\text{ is Lipschitz continuous}.

Condition (

G
1

) is known as the
\Delta_{2}
condition
or
doubling condition
and, as it is showed in
[
38
, Theorem 3.4.4]
, it is equivalent to the right hand side inequality in (

G
1

).

The
complementary Young function

\tilde{G}
of a Young function
G
is defined as

\tilde{G}(t):=\sup\{tw-G(w):w>0\}.

From this definition the following Young-type inequality holds

(2.1)

ab\leq G(a)+\tilde{G}(b)\qquad\text{for all }a,b\geq 0,

and the following Hölder’s type inequality

\int_{\Omega}|uv|\,dx\leq\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{\tilde{G}}(\Omega)}

for all
u\in L^{G}(\Omega)
and
v\in L^{\tilde{G}}(\Omega)
. Moreover, it is not hard to see that
\tilde{G}
can be written in terms of the inverse of
g
as

(2.2)

\tilde{G}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g^{-1}(\tau)\,d\tau,

see
[
48
, Theorem 2.6.8]
.

Since
g^{-1}
is increasing, from (
2.2
) and (

G
1

) it is immediate the following relation.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be an Young function satisfying (

G
1

) such that
g=G^{\prime}
and denote by
\tilde{G}
its complementary function. Then

\tilde{G}(g(t))\leq p^{+}G(t)

holds for any
t\geq 0
.

The following convexity property proved in
[
39
]
[Lemma 2.1] will be useful.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a Young function satisfying (

G
1

) and (

G
2

). Then for every
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

\frac{G(|a|)+G(|b|)}{2}\geq G\left(\left|\frac{a+b}{2}\right|\right)+G\left(\left|\frac{a-b}{2}\right|\right).

2.2.
Fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

Given a Young function
G
such that
G^{\prime}=g
, a parameter
s\in(0,1)
and an open and bounded set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
we consider the spaces

\displaystyle L^{G}(\Omega):=\left\{u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\text{ measurable such that }\Phi_{G,\Omega}(u)<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u\in L^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ such that }\Phi_{s,G,\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u)<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\ u=0\ a.e.\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\right\}

where the modulars
\Phi_{G,\Omega}
and
\Phi_{s,G,\mathbb{R}^{n}}
are defined as

\displaystyle\Phi_{G,\Omega}(u):=\int_{\Omega}G(|u(x)|)\,dx

\displaystyle\Phi_{s,G,\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u):=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D_{s}u(x,y)|)\,d\mu,

where the
s-
Hölder quotient
is defined as

D_{s}u(x,y):=\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}},

being
d\mu(x,y):=\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n}}
.
These spaces are endowed with the so-called
Luxemburg norms

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\Phi_{G,\Omega}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}+[u]_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where the
(s,G)
-Gagliardo semi-norm
is defined as

\displaystyle[u]_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\Phi_{s,G,\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

Since we assume (

G
1

), the space
W^{s,G}(\Omega)
is a reflexive Banach space. Moreover
C_{c}^{\infty}
is dense in
W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. See
[
26
, Proposition 2.11]
and
[

The space of fractional Orlicz-Sobolev functions is the appropriated one where to define the

As proved in

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus(\Omega)^{2}}G(|D_{s}u(x,y)|)\,d\mu

Hence, it is natural to defined the space

which will be the appropriated one when dealing with the Neumann boundary condition.

The suitable space in which to define a Robin boundary condition of the type

See
[
6
]
for more details.

3.
Lagrange multipliers and the eigenvalue problem

In this section we define the notion of Dirichlet, Neumann and Robin eigenvalue problems in the context of fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. We recall some existence results already proved in
[
49
]
and
[
50
]
for the Dirichlet case, and state further extension to more general boundary conditions.

We say that

\lambda
is an
eigenvalue
of

D

(
Ω
)

with
eigenfunction

u\in W_{0}^{s,G}(\Omega)
if

(3.1)

\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u,v\rangle=\lambda\int_{\Omega}g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}v\,dx\qquad\forall v\in W_{0}^{s,G}(\Omega).

\lambda
is an
eigenvalue
of

N

(
Ω
)

with
eigenfunction

u\in W^{s,G}_{*}(\Omega)
if

\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u,v\rangle_{*}=\lambda\int_{\Omega}g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}v\,dx\qquad\forall v\in W^{s,G}_{*}(\Omega).

\lambda
is an
eigenvalue
of

R

(
Ω
)

with with
eigenfunction

if

\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u,v\rangle_{*}=\lambda\int_{\Omega}g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}v\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\beta g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}v\,dx\qquad\forall v\in\mathcal{X}_{\beta}(\Omega).

As mentioned, eigenvalues for non-homogeneous eigenproblems strongly depend on the energy level
\alpha>0
. It is possible to prove existence of an eigenvalue by a multiplier argument once it is proved existence of the following associated minimization problems

\displaystyle\Lambda^{D}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\min\{\mathcal{J}(u)\colon u\in M^{D}_{\alpha}\},\quad\text{ where }M^{D}_{\alpha}=\{u\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\colon\mathcal{I}(u)=\alpha\}

\displaystyle\Lambda^{N}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\min\{\mathcal{J}_{0}(u)\colon u\in M^{N}_{\alpha}\},\quad\text{ where }M^{N}_{\alpha}=\{u\in W^{s,G}_{*}(\Omega)\colon\mathcal{I}(u)=\alpha\}

\displaystyle\Lambda^{R}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\min\{\mathcal{J}_{\beta}(u)\colon u\in M^{R}_{\alpha}\},\quad\text{ where }M^{R}_{\alpha}=\{u\in\mathcal{X}_{\beta}(\Omega)\colon\mathcal{I}(u)=\alpha\}.

As pointed out in
[
6
]
, by standard computations of the calculus of variations, for each
\alpha>0
the quantities
\Lambda_{\alpha}^{D}
,
\Lambda_{\alpha}^{N}
and
\Lambda_{\alpha}^{R}
are attained by suitable functions
u_{\alpha}^{D}
,
u_{\alpha}^{N}
and
u_{\alpha}^{R}
, respectively. Since condition (

G
1

) is assumed on
G
, the functionals
\mathcal{J}
,
\mathcal{J_{\beta}}
and
\mathcal{I}
are class
C^{1}
with Frechét derivatives given by

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle(\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle&=\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u,v\rangle\qquad\forall v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega),\\
\langle(\mathcal{J}_{0}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle&=\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u,v\rangle_{*}\qquad\forall v\in W^{s,G}_{*}(\Omega),\\
\langle(\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle&=\int_{\Omega}g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}v\,dx\qquad\forall v\in L^{G}(\Omega),\\
\langle(\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{\beta}(u),v\rangle&=\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u,v\rangle_{*}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\beta g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}v\,dx\qquad\forall v\in W^{s,G}_{*}(\Omega),\end{split}

and therefore, by an application of the Lagrange multipliers rule, there exist numbers
\lambda^{D}_{\alpha}
,
\lambda^{N}_{\alpha}
and
\lambda^{R}_{\alpha}
which are eigenvalues of

D

(
Ω
)

,

N

(
Ω
)

and

(
Ω
)

with eigenfunctions

Proceeding as in

(see Theorem

it holds that

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}^{D}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\lambda^{D}_{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})\},

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}^{N}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\lambda^{N}_{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})\},

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}^{R}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\lambda^{R}_{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})\}

are eigenvalues of

D

(
Ω
)

,

N

(
Ω
)

and

R

(
Ω
)

In contrast with

Laplacian type problems, when dealing with non-homogeneous eigenproblems, eigenvalues are not variational in general. One could consider the variational quantity

\bar{\lambda}^{D}=\displaystyle\inf_{u\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(|D_{s}u|)|D_{s}u|\,d\mu}{\int_{\Omega}g(|u|)|u|\,dx},

but in general this number cannot be probed to be an eigenvalue. In an analogous way, we can define
\bar{\lambda}^{R}
and

Theorem 3.1
.

It holds that
\bar{\lambda}^{D}\leq\hat{\lambda}^{D}
and there is no eigenvalue
\lambda
of

D

(
Ω
)

such that
\lambda<\bar{\lambda}^{D}

The same holds by changing the superscript

Proof.

First observe that, since
\hat{\lambda}^{D}
is an eigenvalue of

(
Ω
)

If we suppose that there exists

which is an eigenvalue of problem

D

(
Ω
)

with eigenfunction

We finish this section by proving Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.5

Let
\{\lambda_{\alpha_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

(
Ω
)

(3.3)

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(|D_{s}u_{\alpha_{k}}|)\frac{D_{s}u_{\alpha_{k}}}{|D_{s}u_{\alpha_{k}}|}D_{s}v\,d\mu=\lambda_{\alpha_{k}}\int_{\Omega}g(|u_{\alpha_{k}}|)\frac{u_{\alpha_{k}}}{|u_{\alpha_{k}}|}v\qquad\forall v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega).

Observe that

since by (

\displaystyle\Phi_{s,G,\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{\alpha_{k}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{\alpha_{k}}}{p^{-}}\int_{\Omega}g(|u_{\alpha_{k}}|)|u_{\alpha_{k}}|\leq\frac{p^{+}}{p^{-}}\lambda_{\alpha_{k}}\alpha_{k}

From the continuity of

4.
Ljusternik-Schnirelman eigenvalues

In order to build a sequence of eigenvalues for problems

D

(
Ω
)

,

N

(
Ω
)

and

R

(
Ω
)

, the idea is to apply an abstract theorem of the so-called Ljusternik-Schnirelman theory. See for instance
[
17
,
47
,
56
]
. We will particularly use the result stated in
[
47
, Theorem 9.27]
.

Given
\alpha>0
, assume that
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
are two functionals defined in a reflexive Banach space
\mathcal{X}
, such that

(
h_{1}
)

\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
are
C^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
even functionals with
\mathcal{I}(0)=\mathcal{B}(0)=0
and the level set

M_{\alpha}:=\{u\in\mathcal{X}\colon\mathcal{B}(u)=\alpha\}

is bounded.

(
h_{2}
)

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is strongly continuous, i.e.,

u_{k}\rightharpoonup u\text{ in }\mathcal{X}\implies\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(u_{k})\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(u).

Moreover, for any
u
in the closure of the convex hull of
M_{\alpha}
,

\langle\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=0\iff\mathcal{A}(u)=0\iff u=0.

(
h_{3}
)

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is continuous, bounded and, as
k\to\infty
, it holds that

u_{k}\rightharpoonup u,\quad\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u_{k})\rightharpoonup v,\quad\langle\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u_{k}),u_{k}\rangle\to\langle v,u\rangle\implies u_{k}\to u\text{ in }\mathcal{X}.

(
h_{4}
)

For every
u\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}
it holds that

\langle\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u),u\rangle>0,\qquad\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathcal{B}(tu)=+\infty,\qquad\displaystyle\inf_{u\in M_{\alpha}}\langle\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u),u\rangle>0.

Define max-min values

c_{k,\alpha}=\begin{cases}\sup_{K\in\mathcal{C}_{k}}\inf_{u\in K}\mathcal{A}(u),&\mathcal{C}_{k}\neq\emptyset,\\
0,&\mathcal{C}_{k}=\emptyset,\end{cases}

where, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathcal{C}_{k}:=\{K\subset M_{\alpha}\text{ compact, symmetric with }\mathcal{A}(u)>0\text{ on }K\text{ and }\gamma(K)\geq k\},

and the Krasnoselskii genus of
K
is defined as

\gamma(K):=\inf\{p\in\mathbb{N}\colon\exists h\colon K\to\mathbb{R}^{p}\setminus\{0\}\ \text{such that}\ h\text{ is continuous and odd}\}

see
[
37
]
for details.

Thus,
\{c_{k,\alpha}\}_{k\geq 1}
forms a nonincreasing sequence

+\infty>c_{1,\alpha}\geq c_{2,\alpha}\geq\ldots\geq c_{k,\alpha}\geq\ldots\geq 0.

Under these considerations, the Ljusternik–Schnirelmann principle stated in
[
47
, Theorem 9.27]
establishes that there exists a sequence
\{(\mu_{k,\alpha},u_{k,\alpha})\}_{k\geq 1}
such that

\langle\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(u_{k,\alpha}),v\rangle=\mu_{k,\alpha}\langle\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle\qquad\forall v\in\mathcal{X}^{*}

such that
u_{k,\alpha}\in M_{\alpha}
,
\mathcal{A}(u_{k,\alpha})=c_{k,\alpha}
,
\mu_{k,\alpha}\neq 0
,
\mu_{k,\alpha}\to 0
, and
u_{k,\alpha}\rightharpoonup 0
in
\mathcal{X}
.

4.1.
The Dirichlet case

With these preliminaries, we are in position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

We consider the space
\mathcal{X}:=W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
and the functionals
\mathcal{B}(u):=\mathcal{J}(u)
and
\mathcal{A}(u):=\mathcal{I}(u)
defined in (
2.3

and their Frechét derivatives are given in (

Lemma 4.1
.

The functionals
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{I}
defined above fulfill hypotheses
(h_{1})
–
(h_{4})

Proof.

(i)
Clearly, the maps
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}
are even and
\mathcal{I}(0)=\mathcal{J}(0)=0
.

(ii)
We notice that

p^{-}\xi^{-}(\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)})\leq p^{-}\mathcal{I}(u)\leq\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),u\rangle\leq p^{+}\mathcal{I}(u)\leq p^{+}\xi^{+}(\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}).

Then immediately we obtain

\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=0\ \Leftrightarrow\ \mathcal{I}(u)=0\ \Leftrightarrow\ u=0.

Thus, it remains to check that
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
is strongly continuous. Let
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
, then
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. We need to show that
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{k})\to\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)
in
W^{-s,\tilde{G}}(\Omega)
.

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{k})-\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega}\left(g(|u_{k}|)\frac{u_{k}}{|u_{k}|}-g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}\right)v\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\Omega}g(|u_{k}|)\left(\frac{u_{k}}{|u_{k}|}-\frac{u}{|u|}\right)v\,dx\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega}(g(|u_{k}|)-g(|u|))\frac{u}{|u|}v\,dx\right|

\displaystyle:=I_{1,k}+I_{2,k}.

By using Lemma
2.2
and Hölder’s inequality, the first term can be bounded as

I_{1,k}\leq\|g(|u_{k}|)\|_{\tilde{G}}\|2v\|_{G}\rightarrow 0,\ k\rightarrow+\infty.

Similarly, by using the

and Hölder’s inequality we get

I_{2,k}\leq\|g(|u_{k}|)-g(|u|)\|_{\tilde{G}}\|v\|_{G}.

Since
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
in

u_{k}\to u
strongly in

and a.e. in

, moreover, from Lemma

, therefore, by dominated convergence theorem,
\int_{\Omega}\tilde{G}(|g(|u_{k}|)-g(|u|)|)\,dx\to 0
and hence
\|g(|u_{k}|)-g(|u|)\|_{L^{\tilde{G}}(\Omega)}\to 0
as

From the last relations it follows that
\|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{k})-\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)\|_{W^{-s,\tilde{G}}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
as required.

(iii)
One can easily see that
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}

from there
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
is bounded.

It remains to be showed that if
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

(4.1)

u_{k}\rightharpoonup u,\quad\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u_{k})\rightharpoonup v,\quad\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u_{k}),u_{k}\rangle\to\langle v,u\rangle\

then

in
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Since
G
is convex, we have

G(|D_{s}u|)\leq G\bigg(\bigg|\frac{D_{s}u+D_{s}u_{k}}{2}\bigg|\bigg)+g(|D_{s}u|)\frac{D_{s}u}{|D_{s}u|}.\frac{D_{s}u-D_{s}u_{k}}{2}

and

G(|D_{s}u_{k}|)\leq G\bigg(\bigg|\frac{D_{s}u+D_{s}u_{k}}{2}\bigg|\bigg)+g(|D_{s}u_{k}|)\frac{D_{s}u_{k}}{|D_{s}u_{k}|}.\frac{D_{s}u_{k}-D_{s}u}{2}

for every
u,v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. Adding the above two relations and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we find that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(g(|D_{s}u|)\frac{D_{s}u}{|D_{s}u|}

\displaystyle-g(|D_{s}u_{k}|)\frac{D_{s}u_{k}}{|D_{s}u_{k}|}\Big)(D_{s}u-D_{s}u_{k})\ d\mu

(4.2)

\displaystyle\geq\Phi_{s,G,\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u)+\Phi_{s,G,\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{k})-2\Phi_{s,G,\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{u+u_{k}}{2}\right)

for every
u,v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. By applying Lemma
2.3
, the right part of the inequality above can be bounded by below by
2\Phi_{s,G,\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{u-u_{k}}{2}\right)
, and hence we get

\langle(-\Delta_{g})(u)-(-\Delta_{g})(u_{k}),u-u_{k}\rangle\geq 4\Phi_{s,G,\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u-u_{k}).

This, together with Lemma
2.6
yields

(4.3)

\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u)-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u_{k}),u-u_{k}\rangle\geq\xi^{-}([u-u_{k}]_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}).

On the other hand, Proposition
2.5
gives that
u_{k}\to u
in
L^{G}(\Omega)
and a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which, mixed up with the assumptions (
4.1
) allows us to deduce that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u_{k})-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u),u_{k}-u\rangle=\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u_{k}),u_{k}\rangle-\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u_{k}),u\rangle-\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u),u_{k}-u\rangle\right)=0.

Hence, from (
4.3
),
[u_{k}-u]_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
as required.

(iv)
It is clear that, for any
u\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
,

\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u),u\rangle>0,\qquad\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathcal{J}(tu)=+\infty,\qquad\displaystyle\inf_{u\in M_{\alpha}}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u),u\rangle>0.

This concludes the proof.
∎

(Dirichlet case).

D

(

Remark 4.2

We mention some observations regarding the Ljusternik-Schnirelman sequence of eigenvalues obtained in Theorem

(a)

(

, for any

fixed.

5.
Minimax eigenvalues

This section is devoted to prove Theorem
1.2
. We start with the Dirichlet case.

Given
\alpha>0
we recall that

M_{\alpha}^{D}:=\{u\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\colon\mathcal{I}(u)=\alpha\}

defines a
C^{1}
manifold. We denote
\tau_{\alpha}
the restriction of
(-\Delta_{g})^{s}
to
M_{\alpha}^{D}
, i.e., for each
u\in M_{\alpha}^{D}
,

\langle\tau_{\alpha}(u),v\rangle:=\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u,v\rangle\quad\forall v\in T_{u}M_{\alpha}^{D}

where the tangent space of
M_{\alpha}^{D}
at
u
is defined as

T_{u}M_{\alpha}^{D}:=\left\{v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\colon\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=0\right\}.

Thus, we define

\|\tau_{\alpha}u\|:=\|\tau_{\alpha}u\|_{(T_{u}M_{\alpha}^{D})^{*}}

where
(T_{u}M_{\alpha}^{D})^{*}
is the dual space of
T_{u}M_{\alpha}^{D}
.

We recall that the
duality mapping

J\colon W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\to(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}
(where we have denoted the dual space of the space
\mathcal{X}
as
\mathcal{X}^{*}
) is defined as a bijective isometry such that

(5.1)

\|Ju\|_{(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}}=[u]_{W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)},\qquad\langle Ju,u\rangle=[u]_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\qquad\forall u\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega).

See
[
55
, Chapter 18.11c]
and
[
56
, Proposition 47.18]
for details.

Given
\alpha>0
, the idea is to apply the minimax theorem stated in
[
21
]
to the functional
\mathcal{J}(u)
under the constraint
\mathcal{I}(u)=\alpha
to obtain a sequence of critical points of the form

C_{k,\alpha}^{D}=\inf_{h\in\Gamma(S^{k-1},M_{\alpha}^{D})}\sup_{w\in S^{k-1}}\Phi_{s,G,\mathbb{R}^{n}}(h(w)).

being
\Gamma(S^{k-1}k,M_{\alpha}^{D})=\{h\in C(S^{k-1},M_{\alpha}^{D})\colon h\text{ is odd}\}
.

Recall that the derivatives of
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
are given by
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u)=(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u
and
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)=\frac{g(|u|)u}{|u|}
for any
u\in L^{G}(\Omega)
.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
\mathcal{J}
satisfies the
Palais-Smale condition on
M_{\alpha}^{D}
at level
c
if any sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset M_{\alpha}^{D}
such that
\mathcal{J}(u_{n})\to c
and
\|\tau_{\alpha}u_{n}\|\to 0
, possesses a convergent subsequence.

The key ingredient is to analyze the validity of the Palais-Smale condition.

Lemma 5.2
.

Given
\alpha>0
the functional
\mathcal{J}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition on
M_{\alpha}^{D}
at level
C_{k,\alpha}^{D}
.

Proof.

We follow closely the construction of
[
52
, Theorem 5.3]
. Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
M_{\alpha}^{D}
such that

(5.2)

\mathcal{I}(u_{n})\to C_{k,\alpha}^{D}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\tau_{\alpha}(u_{n})\|\to 0.

For each
u\in M_{\alpha}^{D}
define the projection
P_{u}\colon W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\to T_{u}M_{\alpha}^{D}
such that

P_{u}v=v-\frac{\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle}{\|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)\|^{2}_{(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}}}J^{-1}\left(\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)\right).

Observe first that
P_{u}
is well defined: given
v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u\in M_{\alpha}^{D}
we have

\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),P_{u}v\rangle=\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle-\frac{\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle}{\|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)\|^{2}_{(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}}}\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),J^{-1}\left(\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)\right)\rangle=0

since due to (
5.2
),
\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),J^{-1}\left(\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)\right)\rangle=\|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)\|^{2}_{(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}}
.

Moreover, for every
v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
we get the inequality

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle\leq\|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)\|_{(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}}[v]_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

and by (
5.1
) it follows that

\left[J^{-1}\left(\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)\right)\right]_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)\|_{(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}}.

Therefore we get that
[P_{u}v]_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2[v]_{{}_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}
, which implies that, for any
v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)

\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u,P_{u}v\rangle=\langle\tau_{\alpha}(u),P_{u}v\rangle\leq 2\|\tau_{\alpha}(u)\|[v]_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Consequently, by (
5.2
)

\sup_{[v]_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1}|\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u_{n},P_{u_{n}}v\rangle|\leq\sup_{[v]_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1}2\|\tau_{\alpha}(u_{n})\|[v]_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Hence we get that

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u_{n},P_{u_{n}}v\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u_{n},v\rangle-\left\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u_{n},\frac{\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}u_{n},v\rangle}{\|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)\|^{2}_{(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}}}J^{-1}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n}))\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u_{n},v\rangle-\left\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u_{n},J^{-1}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n}))\right\rangle\frac{\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n}),v\rangle}{\|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)\|^{2}_{(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}}}

\displaystyle=\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u_{n},v\rangle-\left\langle\frac{\left\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u_{n},J^{-1}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n}))\right\rangle}{\|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)\|^{2}_{(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}}}\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n}),v\right\rangle\to 0,

that is

(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u_{n}-\frac{\left\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u_{n},J^{-1}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n}))\right\rangle}{\|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)\|^{2}_{(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}}}\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n})\rightharpoonup 0\quad\text{ weakly in }W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega).

From (
5.2
), up to a subsequence
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
and strongly in
L^{G}(\Omega)
to some
u\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
due to Proposition
2.5
. Observe that in Lemma
4.1
we have proved that
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
satisfies property
(h_{2})
of Section
4
, i.e.,
\mathcal{I}
is strongly continuous, which implies that
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n})\to\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)
.

Moreover, (
5.2
) also gives that
(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u_{n}
is bounded and
u_{n}
is bounded away from zero. Therefore, up to a subsequence

(5.3)

(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u_{n}\rightharpoonup v\quad\text{ weakly in }(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}

In Lemma
4.1
we have proved that
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}=(-\Delta_{g})^{s}
satisfies property
(h_{3})
of Section
4
, from where, in view of (
5.3
) we have that
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. This proves that
\mathcal{J}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition on
M_{\alpha}^{D}
at level
C_{k,\alpha}^{D}
which concludes the proof.
∎

.

Due to Lemma

,
\mathcal{J}
(resp.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
u_{k,\alpha}^{D}
be the eigenfunctions corresponding to

and property (

In a similar way, if we consider the sequence

The lower bounds follow analogously.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Step 1.

In
[
27
, Corollary 2.10]
it is proved that
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\subset W^{s,p^{-}}_{0}(\Omega)
, therefore

[u]_{W^{s,p^{-}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C[u]_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Given
u\in M_{\alpha}^{D}
we get

\mathcal{J}(u)=\Phi_{s,G,\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u)\geq\xi^{-}([u]_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})\geq C\xi^{-}([u]_{W^{s,p^{-}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})

where we have used Lemma
2.6
. Then

(5.4)

C[u]_{W^{s,p^{-}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\beta}\leq\mathcal{J}(u)

for some
\beta=\beta(p^{\pm})
.

Step 2.
Define

\mathcal{M}_{\delta}^{D}:=\{u\in W^{s,p^{-}}_{0}(\Omega)\colon[u]^{\beta}_{L^{p^{-}}(\Omega)}\leq\delta\}

where
\beta
is the same of step 1.

As in
[
27
, Lemma 2.7]
it can be seen that
L^{G}(\Omega)\subset L^{p^{-}}(\Omega)
. Then
\|u\|_{L^{p^{-}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}
.

Given
u\in M_{\alpha}^{D}
,

\alpha=\mathcal{I}(u)\geq\xi^{-}(\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)})\geq C\xi^{-}(\|u\|_{L^{p^{-}}(\Omega)}).

Therefore, there exists some
\delta=\delta(\alpha,p^{\pm})
such that
\|u\|^{\beta}_{L^{p^{-}}(\Omega)}\leq\delta
and then

(5.5)

M_{\alpha}^{D}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\delta}^{D}.

Step 3.

By (
5.4
) and (
5.5
)

(5.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{k,\alpha}^{D}&=\inf_{h\in\Gamma(S^{k-1},M_{\alpha}^{D})}\sup_{w\in S^{k-1}}\mathcal{J}(h(w))\\
&\geq C\inf_{h\in\Gamma(S^{k-1},\mathcal{M}_{\delta}^{D})}\sup_{w\in S^{k-1}}[h(w)]_{W^{s,p^{-}}}^{\beta}:=C(\mu_{k}^{D})^{\beta}\end{split}

where

From Theorem

Since

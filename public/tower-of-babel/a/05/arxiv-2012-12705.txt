2
Preliminaries

In this section, we list a few notation, definitions, and preliminary results.

(P1)

All vectors are considered as column vectors unless stated otherwise.

(P2)

The transpose of a matrix
A
is denoted by
A^{\prime}
. We use
\mathop{\rm adj}(A)
and
{\rm det}(A)
to represent the adjoint and determinant of
A
, respectively.

(P3)

The notation
n
will stand for an even integer which is atleast four. We reserve the notation
m
to denote the integer
2n-1
.

(P4)

The wheel graph on
n
vertices is denoted by
W_{n}
. Let
V:=\{1,\dotsc,n\}
be the vertex set of
W_{n}
and
E
be the edge set of
W_{n}
. We label
W_{n}
as follows: The center of
W_{n}
is labeled
1
and the
n-1
vertices in the outer cycle of
W_{n}
are labeled
2,3,\dotsc,n
, anticlockwise. Let
\widetilde{V}:=V\cup\{n+1,n+2,\dotsc,m\}
and
\widetilde{E}:=E\cup\{(j,n+j-1):2\leq j\leq n\}
. Now the helm graph
H_{n}
is the graph
(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{E})
. It is clear that
H_{n}
has
m
vertices and
3(n-1)
edges. For an example, see Figure
1
.

(P5)

We use
\mathbf{1}
to denote the column vector of all ones in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. The
({n-1})\times({n-1})
matrix of all ones is denoted by
J
. The identity matrix of order
{n-1}
is denoted by
I
. If
\mu\neq{n-1}
, then
\mathbf{1}_{\mu}
denote the column vector of all ones in
\mathbb{R}^{\mu}
and
I_{\mu}
,
J_{\mu}
will denote the
\mu\times\mu
identity and all ones matrix, respectively.

(P6)

Let
s=(s_{1},s_{2},\dotsc,s_{\mu})^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mu}
. We use the notation
{\rm Circ}(s^{\prime})
to denote the circulant matrix

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}s_{1}&s_{2}&s_{3}&\ldots&s_{\mu}\\
s_{\mu}&s_{1}&s_{2}&\ldots&s_{\mu-1}\\
s_{\mu-1}&s_{\mu}&s_{1}&\ldots&s_{\mu-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ldots&\vdots\\
s_{2}&s_{3}&s_{4}&\ldots&s_{1}\end{array}\right].

It is easy to note that if
C
is a circulant matrix, then
{\rm Circ}(s^{\prime})C={\rm Circ}(s^{\prime}C)
.

(P7)

Let
\widetilde{D}={\rm Circ}(v^{\prime})
, where
v=(0,1,2,\dotsc,2,1)^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. The distance matrix
D
of the helm graph
H_{n}
is now

D=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\mathbf{1}^{\prime}&2\mathbf{1}^{\prime}\\
\mathbf{1}&\widetilde{D}&\widetilde{D}+J\\
2\mathbf{1}&\widetilde{D}+J&\widetilde{D}+2(J-I)\end{array}\right].

(1)

We note that
\widetilde{D}\mathbf{1}=2(n-3)\mathbf{1}
.

(P8)

The following identity will be useful in the paper. If
n
is even, then

\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}(-1)^{k}{(n-1-2k)}=\frac{2-n}{2}.

(P9)

Let
A
be a
\mu\times\mu
matrix partitioned

A=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}\end{array}\right],

where
A_{11}
and
A_{22}
are square matrices. If
A_{11}
is nonsingular then the Schur complement of
A_{11}
in
A
is
A_{22}-A_{21}A_{11}^{-1}A_{12}
and

{\rm det}(A)={\rm det}(A_{11}){\rm det}(A_{22}-A_{21}A_{11}^{-1}A_{12}).

(See section 6.3 in
[
9
]
).

(P10)

Let
A
be an
\mu\times\mu
matrix. If
u
and
v
belong to
\mathbb{R}^{\mu}
, then

{\rm det}(A+uv^{\prime})=\begin{cases}(1+v^{\prime}A^{-1}u){\rm det}(A)&\mbox{if}~A~\mbox{ is invertible}\\
{\rm det}(A)+v^{\prime}\mathop{\rm adj}(A)u&\mbox{if}~A~\mbox{is not invertible}.\end{cases}

This result is well known as matrix determinant lemma.

(P11)

An
n\times n
non-negative symmetric matrix
A
is called a Euclidean distance matrix, if all the diagonal entries are equal to zero and
x^{\prime}Ax\leq 0
for all
x\in\{\mathbf{1}\}^{\perp}.
A Euclidean distance matrix has exactly one positive eigenvalue. For details, see chapter 3 in
[
10
]
.

3.3
Properties of the special Laplacian

In this section, we show that
\mathcal{L}
is a positive semidefinite matrix, has rank
m-1
and all row sums equal to zero. We also show that all the cofactors of
\mathcal{L}
are equal to
2^{n-3}
. The proofs of all the results in this section follows from the same technique used in
[
4
]
. We include the proofs here for completion.

Theorem 4
.

Row and column sums of
\mathcal{L}
are zero and
\mathop{\rm rank}(\mathcal{L})=m-1
.

Proof.

From equation
(\ref{ld2})
, we have

\mathcal{L}D+2I_{m}=2u\mathbf{1}_{m}^{\prime}.

(15)

Since
u=\frac{1}{4}(5-n,-1,\dotsc,-1,2,\dotsc,2)^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we have

\mathbf{1}_{m}^{\prime}u=\frac{5-n}{4}-\frac{n-1}{4}+\frac{2(n-1)}{4}=1.

Thus, equation (
15
) gives
\mathbf{1}^{\prime}\mathcal{L}D=0
.
Let
p\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a non-zero vector such that
p^{\prime}\mathcal{L}D=0
.
In view of (
15
), we have

p^{\prime}(-2I_{m}+2u\mathbf{1}_{m}^{\prime})=0.

This gives

p^{\prime}=(p^{\prime}u)\mathbf{1}_{m}^{\prime}.

So,
p
is a multiple of
\mathbf{1}_{m}
. Thus,
nullity of
\mathcal{L}D
is one and hence
\mathop{\rm rank}(\mathcal{L}D)=m-1
.
As
D
is non-singular,
\mathop{\rm rank}(\mathcal{L})=m-1
.
Since
\mathcal{L}
is symmetric and
\mathbf{1}_{m}^{\prime}\mathcal{L}D=0
if and only if
\mathbf{1}_{m}^{\prime}\mathcal{L}=0
, all the row and column sums of
\mathcal{L}
are zero. The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 5
.

\mathcal{L}
is positive semidefinite.

Proof.

Since
\mathop{\rm rank}(\mathcal{L})=m-1
and
\mathcal{L}\mathbf{1}_{m}=0
,
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}}
is a symmetric idempotent matrix with null space equal to
\mbox{span}\{\mathbf{1}_{m}\}
.
Thus,
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}}=I_{m}-\frac{J_{m}}{n}
. Define
P:=I_{m}-\frac{J_{m}}{n}
. By the identity

\mathcal{L}D=-2I_{m}+2u\mathbf{1}_{m}^{\prime},

we get

PDP=-2\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}}
. Let
D:=[d_{ij}]
and
\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}}:=[a_{ij}]
. It is now easy to get the relation

d_{ij}=a_{ii}+a_{jj}-2a_{ij}.

From the above equation,

D=\mathop{\rm diag}(\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}})J_{m}+J_{m}\mathop{\rm diag}(\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}})-2\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}}.

(16)

By Theorem 14 in
[
8
]
,
x^{\prime}Dx\leq 0
for all
x\in\{\mathbf{1}_{m}\}^{\perp}
.
Now,
(\ref{ldj})
implies that
x^{\prime}\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}}x\geq 0
for all
x\in\{\mathbf{1}_{m}\}^{\perp}
. We know that
\mathop{\rm rank}(\mathcal{L})=m-1
and
\mathcal{L}\mathbf{1}_{m}=0
. By the properties of Moore-Penrose inverse, we deduce that
x^{\prime}\mathcal{L}x\geq 0
for all
x\in\{\mathbf{1}_{m}\}^{\perp}
. Furthermore, since
\mathcal{L}\mathbf{1}_{m}=0
, it follows that
\mathcal{L}
is positive semidefinite. The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 6
.

All cofactors of
\mathcal{L}
are equal to
2^{n-3}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{L}
is symmetric and
\mathcal{L}\mathbf{1}=0
, all cofactors of
\mathcal{L}
are equal. Let the common cofactor of
\mathcal{L}
be
\delta
.
By the inverse formula,

D^{-1}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}+\frac{4}{3(n-1)}uu^{\prime}.

By using (P
10
) and
\mathbf{1}^{\prime}_{m}u=1
, we get

\displaystyle{\rm det}(D^{-1})

\displaystyle={\rm det}(-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L})+\frac{4}{3(n-1)}u^{\prime}\mbox{adj}(-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L})u

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3(n-1)}(-1)^{m-1}\frac{1}{2^{m-1}}\delta.

Since
m=2n-1
and
n
is even, we have

\displaystyle{\rm det}(D^{-1})=\frac{4}{3(n-1)}\frac{1}{2^{2n-2}}\delta.

(17)

By Theorem
2
,

{\rm det}(D^{-1})=\frac{1}{3(n-1)}\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}.

(18)

Comparing (
17
) and (
18
), we get

\delta=2^{n-3}.

The proof is complete.
∎

By Theorem 14 in

Theorem 7
.

Suppose the eigenvalues of
D
and
\mathcal{L}
are arranged as

\mu_{1}>0>\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{m},

and

\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{m-1}>\lambda_{m}=0,

respectively. Then

0>-\frac{2}{\lambda_{1}}\geq\mu_{2}\geq-\frac{2}{\lambda_{2}}\geq\cdots\geq-\frac{2}{\lambda_{m-1}}\geq\mu_{m}.

Proof.

The eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}}
are

0<\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\leq\cdots\leq\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{m-1}}.

Let
Q
be an orthogonal matrix such that

Q^{\prime}\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}}Q=\mathop{\rm diag}\bigg(0,\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{1}},\dotsc,\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{m-1}}\bigg).

By an easy computation, we see that

Q^{\prime}\mathop{\rm diag}(\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}})JQ
has first column non-zero and remaining columns equal to zero.
Since

D=\mathop{\rm diag}(\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}})J_{m}+J_{m}\mathop{\rm diag}(\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}})-2\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}},

it follows that

\mathop{\rm diag}\big(-\frac{2}{\lambda_{1}},\dotsc,-\frac{2}{\lambda_{m-1}}\big)
is a principal submatrix of
Q^{\prime}DQ
.
By interlacing theorem, we deduce

\mu_{1}\geq 0>-\dfrac{2}{\lambda_{1}}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq-\dfrac{2}{\lambda_{m-1}}\geq\mu_{m}.

The proof is complete.
∎

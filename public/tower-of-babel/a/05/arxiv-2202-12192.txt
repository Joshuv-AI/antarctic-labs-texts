A decreasing upper bound of energy for time-fractional phase-field equations
Chaoyu Quan, Tao Tang, Boyi Wang, Jiang Yang

Abstract
In this article, we study the energy dissipation property of time-fractional Allen–Cahn equation. We propose a decreasing upper bound of energy that decreases with respect to time and coincides with the original energy at t = 0 t=0 and as t t tends to ∞ \infty . This upper bound can also be viewed as a nonlocal-in-time modified energy, the summation of the original energy and an accumulation term due to the memory effect of time fractional derivative. In particular, this indicates that the original energy indeed decays w.r.t. time in a small neighborhood at t = 0 t=0 . We illustrate the theory mainly with the time-fractional Allen–Cahn equation, but it could be applied to other time-fractional phase-field models such as the Cahn–Hilliard equation. On the discrete level, the first-order L1 and second-order L2 schemes for time-fractional Allen–Cahn equation have similar decreasing modified energies, so that the stability can be established. Some numerical results are provided to illustrate the behavior of this modified energy and to verify our theoretical results.



Keywords
:
time-fractional Allen–Cahn equation, energy dissipation, L1 approximation, L2 approximation

Introduction

Phase-field models have various applications in diverse regions such as hydrodynamics, material sciences, image processing and biology simulation, etc.
Most phase-fields models are derived as gradient flows associating with some specific energy functional, such as the Ginzburg–Landau energy for Allen–Cahn equations and Cahn–Hilliard equations, Swift–Hohenberg energy for phase-field crystal models.
Seeking numerical solutions of phase-field equations has attracted a lot of attentions in the passed decade, which could be a delicate task: intrinsic properties of the solution shall be recovered on the discrete level (energy dissipation, maximum principle)
and the presence of small parameter
\varepsilon>0
can generate practical difficulties.
There have been plenty of numerical schemes for phase-field equations, including the convex-splitting schemes
[
7
,
8
,
35
,
4
]
, the stabilization schemes
[
37
,
36
,
30
]
, the implicit-explicit (IMEX) schemes
[
32
,
14
,
15
]
, the operator splitting methods
[
16
,
17
]
, the scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) schemes
[
28
,
29
]
, and the exponential time differencing (ETD) schemes
[
5
,
10
]
.

Recently much interest has arisen in the study of the time-fractional phase-field (TFPF) equations.
For instance, phase-field framework has been successfully employed to describe the evolution of structural damage and fatigue
[
2
]
, in which the damage is described by a variable order time fractional
derivative.
In
[
21
]
, the TFPF models account for the anomalously subdiffusive transport behavior in heterogeneous porous materials.
Liu et al. study the coarsening dynamics for the time-fractional Cahn-Hilliard (TFCH) model based on numerical observations in
[
21
]

, while Chen et al. consider the time-fractional molecular beam epitaxy model in
[
3
]
.These problems are challenging due to the existence of both nonlocality and nonlinearity.
It is natural to extend the relevant discrete level intrinsic properties,
i.e., the maximum principle and energy stability to handle the
TFPF equations, e.g.,
[
6
,
22
,
18
]
.

The Allen-Cahn (AC) model is a popular phase-field model with the governing equation

\partial_{t}u=\gamma(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-F^{\prime}(u)),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega,

(1.1)

where
\varepsilon>0
is the interface width,
\gamma>0
is the diffusion mobility constant, and

\displaystyle F(u)=\frac{1}{4}(u^{2}-1)^{2}

(1.2)

is the double well potential.
The energy functional of the AC equation (
1.5
) is

E(u)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+F(u)\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

(1.3)

With homogeneous Dirichlet/Neumann or periodic boundary condition, this energy decreases with respect to time:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(u)=-\gamma^{-1}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq 0,

(1.4)

i.e., the so-called energy dissipation law.

In this work we are concerned with the time-fractional Allen–Cahn (TFAC) equation:

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\gamma\left(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-F^{\prime}(u)\right),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega,

(1.5)

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
is the Caputo fractional derivative defined by

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\coloneqq\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial_{\tau}u(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau,\quad t>0.

It is still an open question if the solution of TFAC equation still preserves the energy dissipation law.
As for ordinary fractional integral equation, Volterra considered the energy law and proposed an energy equation
[
34
, p.193]
.
In
[
33
]
, Tang, Yu, and Zhou proved the energy boundedness for different TFPF equations that the energy is bounded by initial energy:
\forall t\geq 0,

E(t)\leq E(0).

(1.6)

Later in
[
6
]
, Du, Yang, and Zhou studied the well-posedness, regularity, and maximal principle of the TFAC equation, and observed numerically the fractional energy law

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}E\leq 0,

(1.7)

which is proved theoretically by some of us in
[
24
]
. Fritz, Khristenko, and Wohlmuth proposed the equivalence between the time-fractional gradient flow and an integer-order gradient flow in the augmented Hilbert space
[
9
]
, where a dissipation-preserving augmented energy
E^{\mathrm{aug}}
is constructed.
For example for the TFAC equation,

E^{\mathrm{aug}}(t)=E(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}c_{0}^{2}(\theta)\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c_{1}(\theta)\cdot(t-s)}\mu\,{\mathrm{d}}s\right|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,w_{\alpha,1}(\theta)\,{\mathrm{d}}\theta,

(1.8)

where
c_{0}(\theta)=(1-\theta)^{-1},~c_{1}(\theta)=\theta(1-\theta)^{-1}
and
w_{\alpha,1}=c_{1}^{1-\alpha}(\theta)/\left(\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)\right)
.
A variational energy law is proposed by Liao, Tang, and Zhou for the TFAC equation in
[
19
]
as follows

E_{\alpha}(t)=E(t)+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\alpha}\|\delta_{u}E\|^{2},

(1.9)

where
\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\alpha}
denotes the Riemann-Liouville fractional integration operator of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
However it is not obvious to generalize the variational energy properly to the TFCH model.

On the discrete level, the discrete fractional energy law
\overline{\partial}_{t}^{\alpha}E\leq 0
also holds for L1 schemes of TFPF equations
[
25
]
.
It is further shown that the energy boundedness by initial energy can be ensured for arbitrary nonuniform time meshes.
In recent interesting works
[
11
,
12
]
, Hou and Xu split the nonlocal time-fractional derivative to local and nonlocal terms for the TFAC equation, and treat the derived nonlocal term with the SAV technique, so that the modified discrete energy of L1 and L2 schemes decreases w.r.t. time.

In this article, we show the following identity on the original energy of TFAC equation

\displaystyle\boxed{\gamma\Gamma(1-\alpha)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(t)=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{D_{\alpha}(t)}-\alpha D_{\alpha+1}(t),}

(1.10)

where the nonlocal term
D_{\alpha}(t)\geq 0
is well defined in (
3.3
).
Then a decreasing upper bound
\tilde{E}(t)
of the original energy is deduced

\boxed{\tilde{E}(t)=E(t)+\frac{1}{\gamma\Gamma(1-\alpha)}D_{\alpha}(t).}

(1.11)

This energy bound functional is the sum of the original energy and a nonnegative term arisen from the time-fractional Caputo derivative (see Figure
1
for graphical illustration of
\tilde{E}
and
E
).
Particularly
\tilde{E}
has the following features:

•

\tilde{E}
decreases w.r.t. time;

•

\tilde{E}=E
at
t=0
and
\tilde{E}\rightarrow E
as
t\to\infty
;

•

for any fixed
t\in(0,\infty)
,
\tilde{E}(t)\rightarrow E(t)
as
\alpha\to 1
.

Clearly, such result can be viewed as a generalization of the boundedness by initial energy proposed in
[
33
]
.
It also indicates that the original energy indeed decays w.r.t. time in some small neighborhood at
t=0
.
However, it is still unknown if this holds true in longer time.
The construction of upper bound for TFAC equation can be generalized to the time-fractional Cahn–Hilliard (TFCH) equation.
Furthermore, on the discrete level, similar decreasing discrete upper bounds can be obtained for the L1-type and L2-type schemes of the TFAC equation.

\tilde{E}

We introduce some definitions and theoretical results that are useful in later analysis.

For abbreviation, let
u(t)=u(t,\cdot)
denote
u(t,x)
and
\|\cdot\|
denote the standard spatial
L^{2}
norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
We consider an equivalent definition of the Caputo derivative obtained from integrating by part as follows (see
[
1
]
)

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial_{\tau}(u(\tau)-u(t))}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau

(2.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\left(\frac{u(\tau)-u(t)}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha}}\Bigg|_{\tau=0}^{\tau=t-\delta}-\alpha\int_{0}^{t-\delta}\frac{u(\tau)-u(t)}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\Bigg(\frac{u(t)-u(0)}{t^{\alpha}}+\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\frac{u(t)-u(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\Bigg).

We recall the definition of Bochner-Sobolev space
H^{s}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
, see for example
[

]

For any

, one can define Sobolev-Slobodeckii seminorm
|\cdot|_{H^{s}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}

|v|_{H^{s}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\|(v(t)-v(\tau))\|^{2}}{|t-\tau|^{1+2s}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau{\mathrm{d}}t,

(2.2)

and the full norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{s}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}
by

\|v\|_{H^{s}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}=\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+|v|^{2}_{H^{s}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}.

(2.3)

We recall the following regularity result for TFAC equation with Dirichlet boundary condition obtained by Du et al.

]

(

3
Decreasing energy bound

In this part, we consider the TFAC equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\gamma\left(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-f(u)\right),\quad x\in\Omega,\quad t>0

(3.1a)

\displaystyle u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\Omega

(3.1b)

with
f(u)=u^{3}-u
. For the simplicity, we consider the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition so that Proposition
2.1
holds. The cases of other boundary conditions can be done in the same way.

3.1
A property of Caputo derivative

Before proposing the decreasing upper bound of the original energy, we first propose a property of Caputo derivative.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assuming that
u_{0}(x)\in H^{2}(\Omega)\bigcap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
For any
t\in(0,T]
, it holds

\displaystyle\Gamma(1-\alpha)\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u,\partial_{t}u\rangle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{dt}}D_{\alpha}(t)+\alpha D_{\alpha+1}(t),

(3.2)

where

D_{\alpha}(t)=\frac{\|u(t)-u(0)\|^{2}}{2t^{\alpha}}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|u(t)-u(\tau)\|^{2}}{{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau

(3.3)

is wellposed.

Proof.

According to the definition (
2.1
) of Caputo derivative, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma(1-\alpha)\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u,\partial_{t}u\rangle=\left\langle\frac{u(t)-u(0)}{t^{\alpha}}+\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\frac{u(t)-u(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau,\partial_{t}u\right\rangle.

(3.4)

Replacing
\partial_{t}u(t)
in (
3.4
) by
\partial_{t}\left(u(t)-u(\tau)\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma(1-\alpha)\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u,\partial_{t}u\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle u(t)-u(0),\partial_{t}(u(t)-u(0))\rangle}{t^{\alpha}}+\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\langle u(t)-u(\tau),\partial_{t}(u(t)-u(\tau))\rangle}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle={I}_{1}+\alpha{I}_{2},

(3.5)

where

{I}_{1}=\frac{\langle u(t)-u(0),\partial_{t}(u(t)-u(0))\rangle}{t^{\alpha}}

and

{I}_{2}=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\langle u(t)-u(\tau),\partial_{t}(u(t)-u(\tau)\rangle}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau.

As for
I_{1}
, by writing the numerator as a derivative of the
L^{2}(\Omega)
norm, direct calculation leads to

\displaystyle{I}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2t^{\alpha}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|u(t)-u(0)\|^{2}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Bigg(\frac{\|u(t)-u(0)\|^{2}}{2t^{\alpha}}\Bigg)+\alpha\frac{\|u(t)-u(0)\|^{2}}{2t^{\alpha+1}}.

(3.6)

As for
I_{2}
, we have, for
t>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left\|u(t)-u(\tau)\right\|^{2}}{{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t-\delta}\frac{\|u(t)-u(\tau)\|^{2}}{{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{\|u(t)-u(t-\delta)\|^{2}}{\delta^{\alpha+1}}+2\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\langle u(t)-u(\tau),\partial_{t}(u(t)-u(\tau)\rangle}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau-(\alpha+1)\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|u(t)-u(\tau)\|^{2}}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{I}_{2}-(\alpha+1)\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|u(t)-u(\tau)\|^{2}}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau,

which gives

\displaystyle{I}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|u(t)-u(\tau)\|^{2}}{{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\Bigg)+\frac{(\alpha+1)}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|u(t)-u(\tau)\|^{2}}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau.

(3.7)

Thus,

\displaystyle\Gamma(1-\alpha)\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u,\partial_{t}u\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Bigg(\frac{\|u(t)-u(0)\|^{2}}{2t^{\alpha}}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|u(t)-u(\tau)\|^{2}}{{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\Bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha\|u(t)-u(0)\|^{2}}{2t^{\alpha+1}}+\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|u(t)-u(\tau)\|^{2}}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau.

(3.8)

We then obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma(1-\alpha)\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u,\partial_{t}u\rangle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}D_{\alpha}(t)+\alpha D_{\alpha+1}(t),

(3.9)

where
D_{\alpha}(t)
is defined in (
3.3
).

The remaining task is to show the wellposedness of the terms on the right-hand side of (
3.1
).
According to Proposition
2.1
, we have
\|\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\|\leq Ct^{\alpha-1}
for a fixed
t\in(0,T]
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\|u(t)-u(0)\|^{2}}{{t^{\alpha+1}}}=\frac{\|\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{\xi}u(\xi,\cdot)\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi\|^{2}}{{t^{\alpha+1}}}\leq\frac{(\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{\xi}u(\xi,\cdot)\|\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi)^{2}}{{t^{\alpha+1}}}\leq Ct^{\alpha-1}

(3.10)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|u(t)-u(\tau)\|^{2}}{{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|\int_{\tau}^{t}\partial_{\xi}u(\xi)\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi\|^{2}}{{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\leq\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(\int_{\tau}^{t}\|\partial_{\xi}u(\xi)\|\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi)^{2}}{{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(\int_{\tau}^{t}\xi^{\alpha-1}\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi)^{2}}{{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau=\frac{C}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t^{\alpha}-\tau^{\alpha})^{2}}{{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{Ct^{\alpha-1}}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-s^{\alpha})^{2}}{{(1-s)^{\alpha+2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\leq Ct^{\alpha-1}.

(3.11)

Similarly we have

\displaystyle\frac{\|u(t)-u(0)\|^{2}}{{t^{\alpha}}}\leq Ct^{\alpha},\quad\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|u(t)-u(\tau)\|^{2}}{{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\leq Ct^{\alpha}.

(3.12)

Therefore the well-posedness of
D_{\alpha}
and
D_{\alpha+1}
in the above proof is established.
∎

3.2
Upper bound of energy

We propose a decreasing upper bound functional of the original energy for the TFAC equation as follows

\displaystyle\tilde{E}(t)

\displaystyle=E(t)+\frac{1}{\gamma\Gamma(1-\alpha)}D_{\alpha}(t),

(3.13)

where
E(t)
is the original Ginzburg–Landau energy given by (
1.3
) and
D_{\alpha}(t)
is given by (
3.3
).
We show that this “modified energy” decreases w.r.t. time

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\tilde{E}(t)\leq 0,\quad t\geq 0.

(3.14)

We are ready to state prove our main result on the dissipation of the modified energy.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume the initial data
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\bigcap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
The solution
u
to the TFAC equation (
3.1a
) satisfies

E(t)\leq\tilde{E}(t)\leq\tilde{E}(s)\leq E(0),\quad\forall 0\leq s\leq t\leq T.

(3.15)

where
E(t)
is the original Ginzburg–Landau energy (
1.3
) and
\tilde{E}(t)
is the upper bound (
3.13
) (or modified energy).

Proof.

Multiplying the equation (
3.1a
) with
\partial_{t}u
, integrating the resulting one over
\Omega
and using
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to denote the
L^{2}(\Omega)
inner product, we have

\left\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u,\partial_{t}u\right\rangle=\gamma\left\langle\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-f(u),\partial_{t}u\right\rangle.

(3.16)

The right-hand side of this equation is

\text{(RHS)}:=\gamma\langle\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-f(u),\partial_{t}u\rangle=-\gamma\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(t),

(3.17)

while according to Lemma
3.1
, the left-hand side can be written as

\displaystyle\text{(LHS)}:=\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u,u_{t}\rangle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{D_{\alpha}(t)}+\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}{D_{\alpha+1}(t)},

(3.18)

where
D_{\alpha}(t)
is given by (
3.3
).
We then have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(E(t)+\frac{1}{\gamma\Gamma(1-\alpha)}{D_{\alpha}(t)}\right)+\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\Gamma(1-\alpha)}D_{\alpha+1}(t)=0.

(3.19)

Note that
D_{\alpha+1}(t)
is positive..
The desired result (
3.15
) in Theorem
3.1
follows by integrating (
3.19
) over the interval
(s,t)
for
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
.
∎

3.3
Relation between
\tilde{E}
and
E

We show the relation between
\tilde{E}(t)
and
E(t)
.
Precisely speaking, the energy functional
\tilde{E}(t)
coincides the original Ginzburg-Landau energy
E(t)
at
t=0
and as
t\to\infty
, if the solution
u(t)
of the TFAC equation (
3.1a
) converges strongly to some steady state
u_{\infty}(x)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
t\to\infty
.
Before proving this result, we first propose two useful lemmas.

Lemma 3.2

(maximum principle & Hölder continuity)
.

Let
u(t,x)
be the unique solution of the system (
3.1a
). Assume that
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
-1\leq u_{0}\leq 1
on
\bar{\Omega}
. Then we have

(1)

-1\leq u(t,x)\leq 1
for any
x\in\bar{\Omega}
and
t\geq 0
;

(2)

\exists C>0
, s.t.
\|u(t,\cdot)-u(\tau,\cdot)\|\leq C|t-\tau|^{\alpha}
for any
t,\tau\geq 0
.

Proof.

See Appendix
A
for details.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
v\in C^{\alpha}([0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))
.
If

•

\exists M>0
, s.t.
\|v(t,\cdot)\|\leq M
;

•

\exists C>0
, s.t.
\|v(t,\cdot)-v(\tau,\cdot)\|\leq C|t-\tau|^{\alpha}
for any
t,\tau\geq 0
;

•

\|v(t,\cdot)-v_{\infty}\|\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow+\infty}0
for some
v_{\infty}(x)
,

we then have

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|v(t)-v(\tau)\|^{2}}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\rightarrow 0,\quad\mbox{as }t\to\infty.

(3.20)

Proof.

Given an arbitrary small value
\delta>0
, it is sufficient to show that there exists some
t_{*}>0
s.t.

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|v(t)-v(\tau)\|^{2}}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|v(t)-v(t-s)\|^{2}}{s^{\alpha+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s<\delta,\quad\mbox{when }t>t_{*}.

(3.21)

Firstly, there exists sufficiently small
t_{1}>0
such that

\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\frac{\|v(t)-v(t-s)\|^{2}}{s^{\alpha+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\leq C^{2}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}s^{\alpha-1}\,{\mathrm{d}}s=\frac{C^{2}}{\alpha}t_{1}^{\alpha}<\frac{\delta}{3}.

(3.22)

Secondly, there exists sufficiently large
t_{2}>0
such that

\int_{t_{2}}^{t}\frac{\|v(t)-v(t-s)\|^{2}}{s^{\alpha+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\leq M^{2}\int_{t_{2}}^{\infty}s^{-1-\alpha}\,{\mathrm{d}}s=\frac{M^{2}}{\alpha}t_{2}^{-\alpha}<\frac{\delta}{3}.

(3.23)

Note that
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
are fixed now.
Since
v(t)\rightarrow v_{*}
, there exists some large
t_{3}>0
s.t.

\|v(t,\cdot)-v_{\infty}\|<\delta_{1}\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{\alpha\delta}{12(t_{2}^{-\alpha}-t_{1}^{-\alpha})}},\quad\forall t>t_{3}.

(3.24)

As a consequence, when
t>t_{*}=t_{2}+t_{3}
, we then have

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{\|v(t)-v(t-s)\|^{2}}{s^{\alpha+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s<\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{(2\delta_{1})^{2}}{s^{\alpha+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s=(2\delta_{1})^{2}\alpha^{-1}\left(t_{2}^{-\alpha}-t_{1}^{-\alpha}\right)=\frac{\delta}{3}.

(3.25)

Summing the above inequalities, we conclude that when
t>t_{*}=t_{2}+t_{3}
,

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|v(t)-v(t-s)\|^{2}}{s^{\alpha+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s<\delta.

(3.26)

The proof is completed.
∎

Proposition 3.1

(asymptotic analysis)
.

Let
\tilde{E}(t)
and
E(t)
be defined by (
3.13
) and (
1.3
).
Assume that
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\bigcap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
u(t)
is a global bounded solution of (
3.1a
). Then, for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{E}(0)=E(0).

(3.27)

Moreover, assuming that
u(t)
converges to some steady state
u_{\infty}
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
then we have

\displaystyle\tilde{E}(t)\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow+\infty}E(t).

(3.28)

In addition, for any
t\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\tilde{E}(t)\xrightarrow{\alpha\rightarrow 1}E(t).

(3.29)

Proof.

Firstly, we denote

T_{1}(t)=\frac{\|u(t,\cdot)-u(0,\cdot)\|^{2}}{2\gamma t^{\alpha}},\quad T_{2}(t)=\frac{\alpha}{2\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|u(t,\cdot)-u(\tau,\cdot)\|^{2}}{{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}}d\tau,

so that
\tilde{E}=E+\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}(T_{1}+T_{2})
.
According to Lemma
3.2
,
u\in C^{\alpha}([0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))
, i.e.,

\sup_{t,\tau\in[0,\infty)}\frac{\|u(t,\cdot)-u(\tau,\cdot)\|}{|t-\tau|^{\alpha}}<\infty.

(3.30)

Thus
T_{1}(t)
and
T_{2}(t)
are continuous at
t=0
.
The result (
3.27
) then follows.

Secondly, since
u(t)
is a global bounded solution of (
3.1a
), we have
T_{1}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.
According to Lemma
3.3
, we have
T_{2}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.
Thus, the result (
3.28
) is proved.

Finally (
3.29
) holds true, because for any fixed
t\in(0,T)
,
T_{1}(t)
and
T_{2}(t)
are bounded, and
\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\rightarrow 0
as
\alpha\rightarrow 1
.
∎

4.1
L1-IMEX scheme with stabilization

We adopt the L1 approximation
[
31
,
20
]
of Caputo derivative that appears on the left-hand side in (

).
For simplicity, we consider uniform time mesh here.
Let
\triangle t=\frac{T}{N}
be the time step size and
t_{n}=n\triangle t,~0\leq n\leq N

The L1 approximation is written as

\overline{\partial}_{t}^{\alpha}u^{n}\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{n-k}\left(u^{k}-u^{k-1}\right),

(4.1)

where
\overline{\partial}_{t}^{\alpha}
is the discrete fractional derivative with coefficients

b_{k}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)\triangle t^{\alpha}}\left[(k+1)^{1-\alpha}-k^{1-\alpha}\right],\quad 0\leq k\leq n-1.

(4.2)

(
4.1
) can be recast as

\overline{\partial}_{t}^{\alpha}u^{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}{(b_{n-k-1}-b_{n-k})(u^{n}-u^{k})}+b_{n-1}(u^{n}-u^{0}),.

(4.3)

We propose the following lemma on the L1 operator
\overline{\partial}_{t}^{\alpha}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For the L1 approximation (
4.1
), the following inequality hold:

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\partial}_{t}^{\alpha}u^{n}

\displaystyle,u^{n}-u^{n-1}\rangle\geq D^{n}-D^{n-1},

(4.4)

where

D^{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,&&n=0,\\
&\frac{1}{2}b_{0}\|u^{1}-u^{0}\|^{2},&&n=1,\\
&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(b_{n-k-1}-b_{n-k})\|u^{n}-u^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}b_{n-1}\|u^{n}-u^{0}\|^{2},&&n\geq 2.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.5)

Proof.

In the case of
n=1
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\partial}_{t}^{\alpha}u^{1},u^{1}-u^{0}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}b_{0}\|u^{1}-u^{0}\|^{2}-0=D^{1}-D^{0}.

(4.6)

In the case of
n=2
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\partial}_{t}^{\alpha}u^{2},u^{2}-u^{1}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}(b_{0}-b_{1})\|u^{2}-u^{1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}b_{1}\|u^{2}-u^{1}\|-\frac{1}{2}b_{1}\|u^{1}-u^{0}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}(b_{0}-b_{1})\|u^{2}-u^{1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}b_{1}\|u^{2}-u^{1}\|-\frac{1}{2}b_{0}\|u^{1}-u^{0}\|^{2}=D^{2}-D^{1}.

(4.7)

In the case of
n\geq 3
, multiplying (
4.3
) by
u^{n}-u^{n-1}
and integrating over
\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\partial}_{t}^{\alpha}u^{n},u^{n}-u^{n-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(b_{n-k-1}-b_{n-k})\langle u^{n}-u^{k},u^{n}-u^{k}-(u^{n-1}-u^{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+b_{n-1}\langle u^{n}-u^{0},u^{n}-u^{0}-(u^{n-1}-u^{0})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(b_{n-k-1}-b_{n-k})\|u^{n}-u^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}(b_{n-k-1}-b_{n-k})\|u^{n-1}-u^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}b_{n-1}\|u^{n}-u^{0}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}b_{n-1}\|u^{n-1}-u^{0}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(b_{n-k-1}-b_{n-k})\|u^{n}-u^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}(b_{n-k-2}-b_{n-k-1})\|u^{n-1}-u^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}b_{n-1}\|u^{n}-u^{0}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}b_{n-2}\|u^{n-1}-u^{0}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=D^{n}-D^{n-1}.

(4.8)

∎

We adopt the L1 approximation for the Caputo derivative and use the stabilization technique for the bulk force term.
Consider the following semidiscrete L1 implicit-explicit scheme for equation (

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}_{t}^{\alpha}u(t_{n})=\gamma\left(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u^{n}-f(u^{n-1})-S(u^{n}-u^{n-1})\right),

(4.9)

where
S\geq 0

Theorem 4.1

(modified energy dissipation)
.

If

and
\|u^{0}\|_{\infty}\leq 1

\displaystyle\tilde{E}(u^{n})\leq\tilde{E}(u^{n-1})\quad\forall n\geq 1,

(4.10)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{E}(u^{n})

\displaystyle=E(u^{n})+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(b_{n-k-1}-b_{n-k})\|u^{n}-u^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\gamma}b_{n-1}\|u^{n}-u^{0}\|^{2}.

(4.11)

Proof.

It is already known (cf.
[
33
]
) that in the case of

\|u^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.
Multiplying (

(u^{n}-u^{n-1})
and integrating the equation over

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}\langle\overline{\partial}_{t}^{\alpha}u(t_{n}),u^{n}-u^{n-1}\rangle=\langle\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u^{n}-f(u^{n-1})-S(u^{n}-u^{n-1}),u^{n}-u^{n-1}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u^{n}-f(u^{n-1})-S(u^{n}-u^{n-1}),u^{n}-u^{n-1}\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla u^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla u^{n-1}\|^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\nabla u^{n}-\nabla u^{n-1}\|^{2}

L2-IMEX scheme with stabilization

We study the decreasing energy bound of higher-order schemes for the TFAC equation.
Consider the L2 approximation
[
23
]
of time fractional derivative:

\begin{array}[]{r@{}l}\begin{aligned} L_{1}^{\alpha}u&=&&\frac{1}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)\Delta t^{\alpha}}\left(u^{1}-u^{0}\right),\\
L_{n}^{\alpha}u&=&&\frac{1}{\Gamma(3-\alpha)\Delta t^{\alpha}}{\Bigg\{}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(a_{j}u^{n-j-1}+b_{j}u^{n-j}+c_{j}u^{n-j+1}\right)\\

Here, we reformulate (

\begin{array}[]{r@{}l}\begin{aligned} L_{1}^{\alpha}u&=\frac{1}{\Gamma(3-\alpha)\Delta t^{\alpha}}(r_{1}+d_{1})\delta u^{1},\\

(4.18)

(4.19)

(4.20)

and

&c_{j}-a_{j-1},&&j=2,\ldots,n.\\
\end{aligned}\end{array}\right.

(4.21)

See
[
26
]
for the detailed calculations.

Lemma 4.2
.

For the L2 approximation (

\displaystyle\langle L_{n}^{\alpha}u^{n}

\displaystyle,\delta u^{n}\rangle\geq\tilde{D}^{n}-\tilde{D}^{n-1}+\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(3-\alpha)\Delta t^{\alpha}}\|\delta u^{n}\|^{2},\quad n\geq 2,

(4.22)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{D}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(3-\alpha)\Delta t^{\alpha}}\bigg[\frac{\alpha}{4}\|u^{n}-u^{n-1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(d_{n-j}-d_{n-j+1})\|u^{n}-u^{j}\|^{2}

(4.23)

\displaystyle+\frac{c_{n}}{2}\|u^{n}-u^{1}\|^{2}-\frac{a_{n}}{2}\|u^{n}-u^{0}\|^{2}\bigg].

Proof.

According to (
4.18

\displaystyle{\Gamma(3-\alpha)\Delta t^{\alpha}}\langle L_{n}^{\alpha}u^{n},\delta u^{n}\rangle=Q_{1}+Q_{2}+Q_{3},

(4.24)

where

\displaystyle Q_{1}=\left\langle\frac{3\alpha}{2}\delta u^{n}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\delta u^{n-1},\delta u^{n}\right\rangle,

Clearly, we have

Q_{1}\geq\alpha\|\delta u^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}(\|\delta u^{n}\|^{2}-\|\delta u^{n-1}\|^{2}).

(4.26)

Further,

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{n-j+1}\left\langle\delta u^{j},\delta u^{n}\right\rangle

(4.27)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(d_{n-j}-d_{n-j+1})\langle u^{n}-u^{j},u^{n}-u^{j}-(u^{n-1}-u^{j})\rangle

\displaystyle+d_{n-1}\langle u^{n}-u^{0},u^{n}-u^{0}-(u^{n-1}-u^{0})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(d_{n-j}-d_{n-j+1})\|u^{n}-u^{j}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}(d_{n-j}-d_{n-j+1})\|u^{n-1}-u^{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}d_{n}\|u^{n}-u^{0}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}d_{n}\|u^{n-1}-u^{0}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(d_{n-j}-d_{n-j+1})\|u^{n}-u^{j}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}(d_{n-j-1}-d_{n-j})\|u^{n-1}-u^{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}d_{n}\|u^{n}-u^{0}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}d_{n-1}\|u^{n-1}-u^{0}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(d_{n-1}-d_{n})\|u^{n-1}-u^{0}\|^{2},

where we use the fact

(see
[
26
]
for this property).
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle Q_{3}=\left\langle-c_{n}\delta u^{1},\delta u^{n}\right\rangle

(4.28)

\displaystyle=c_{n}\left\langle u^{n}-u^{1},\delta u^{n}\right\rangle-c_{n}\left\langle u^{n}-u^{0},\delta u^{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{n}}{2}(\|u^{n}-u^{1}\|^{2}-\|u^{n}-u^{0}\|^{2})-\frac{c_{n-1}}{2}(\|u^{n-1}-u^{1}\|^{2}-\|u^{n-1}-u^{0}\|^{2})

(see

]

{d_{n-1}-d_{n}\geq c_{n-1}-c_{n},}

5
Extension to TFCH equation

In this section, we extend the construction of energy upper bound to the TFCH equation

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\gamma\Delta\left(-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u+F^{\prime}(u)\right),\quad x\in\Omega,\quad 0<t<T,

(5.1)

with homogeneous Dirichlet/Neumann or periodic boundary condition.
The analysis is similar, excepted for a minor difference and careful computations, where the energy bound of CH equations diverges from that of AC equations obtained in the former subsection.
For simplicity, we consider the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition and present some results briefly.

Let
\Psi(t,x)
be the solution of

-\Delta\Psi(t,x)=u(t,x)-u(0,x),\quad x\in\Omega,t\geq 0

(5.2)

with the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition, zero mean
\int_{\Omega}\Psi(t,x)dx=0
, and zero initial condition
\Psi(0,x)=0
.
We define the modified energy (upper bound)

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}(t)=

\displaystyle E(t)+\frac{1}{\gamma\Gamma(1-\alpha)}D_{\Delta,\alpha}(t),

\displaystyle D_{\Delta,\alpha}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{\|\nabla\Psi(t,\cdot)\|^{2}}{2t^{\alpha}}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|\nabla(\Psi(t,\cdot)-\Psi(\tau,\cdot))\|^{2}}{{(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

(5.3)

where
E(t)
is the original Ginzburg–Landau energy (
1.3
).
Now we state our result on the decreasing dissipation-preserving energy functional as follows.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
u
be the solution to the problem (
5.1
).
Assume that
D_{\Delta,\alpha}(t)
and
D_{\Delta,\alpha+1}(t)
are well-defined for
0<t\leq T
. Then we have the following decreasing energy dissipation law

E(t)\leq\widetilde{E}(t)\leq\widetilde{E}(s)\leq E(0),\quad\forall 0\leq s\leq t\leq T.

(5.4)

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of Theorem
3.1
,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(E(t)+\frac{\|\nabla\Psi(t,\cdot)\|^{2}}{2\gamma\Gamma(1-\alpha)t^{\alpha}}+\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|\nabla(\Psi(t,\cdot)-\Psi(\tau,\cdot))\|^{2}}{{2\gamma\Gamma(1-\alpha)(t-\tau)^{\alpha+1}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\alpha\|\nabla\Psi(t,\cdot)\|^{2}}{2\gamma\Gamma(1-\alpha)t^{\alpha+1}}+\alpha(\alpha+1)\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|\nabla(\Psi(t,\cdot)-\Psi(\tau,\cdot))\|^{2}}{2\gamma\Gamma(1-\alpha)(t-\tau)^{\alpha+2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\right)\leq 0,

(5.5)

that is,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(E(t)+\frac{1}{\gamma\Gamma(1-\alpha)}D_{\Delta,\alpha}(t)\right)=-\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\Gamma(1-\alpha)}D_{\Delta,\alpha+1}(t)\leq 0,

(5.6)

Integrating (

) over

leads to the desired dissipation-preserving law.
∎

7
Conclusions and discussions

An upper bound of energy is constructed that decreases w.r.t. time, coincides with the original energy at
t=0
and as
t
tends to
\infty
, and converges to the original energy when the fractional order
\alpha\rightarrow 1
.
This upper bound can also be seen as a modified energy, the summation of the original energy and a nonnegative modification term
\frac{1}{\gamma}D_{\alpha}
.
Accordingly, we prove on the discrete level that L1 and L2 schemes have similar modified energy decreasing w.r.t. time.
Several numerical experiments are presented to verify the theoretical results.

Our result is stronger than that the energy of time-fractional phase-field equation is bounded by the initial energy.
A direct deduction is that the original energy preserves the dissipation property in a small neighbourhood at
t=0
.
However, it is still an open problem if the dissipation of original energy holds for all time.

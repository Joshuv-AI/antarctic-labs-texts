Intersection formulas for parities on virtual knots
Igor Nikonov

Abstract
We prove that parities on virtual knots come from invariant 1-cycles on the arcs of knot diagrams. In turn, the invariant cycles are determined by quasi-indices on the crossings of the diagrams. The found connection between the parities and the (quasi)-indices allows to define a new series of parities on virtual knots.

1.1
Virtual knots

We start with the definitions of virtual knots.

A
4
-graph
is any union of four-valent graphs and
trivial components

A
virtual diagram
is an embedding of a
4

classical
of
virtual
vertex.
At a classical vertex one a pair of opposite edges (called
overcrossing
) is chosen. The other pair of opposite edges at the vertex is called
undercrossing
.

Virtual crossings of a virtual diagram are usually drawn circled. The undercrossing of a classical vertex is drawn with a broken line whereas the overcrossing is drawn with a solid line (see Fig.
1

Figure 1:
A virtual trefoil diagram with two classical and one virtual crossings

Moves of virtual diagrams
include classical Reidemeister moves (
R1
,
R2
,
R3

DM
)
that replace any diagram arc, which has only virtual crossings, with a new arc, which has the same ends and contains only virtual crossings (see Fig.
2

virtual link

[
23
]
.

Figure 2:

A
unicursal component
is a minimal set of diagram edges which is closed under passing from an edge to its opposite (at some end of the edge) edge. A
virtual knot
is an equivalence class of diagrams with one unicursal component.

On the other hand, virtual knots can be defined by means Gauss diagrams
[
13

1.2
Parity on virtual knots

Let
\mathcal{K}
be an oriented virtual knot. Consider the set
\mathfrak{K}
of the diagrams of the knot
K
. The set
\mathfrak{K}
can be considered as the objects of a diagram category whose morphisms are compositions of isotopies and Reidemeister moves.

For any diagrams
D,D^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{K}
and a morphism
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
between them, there is a correspondence
f_{*}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to{\mathcal{V}}(D^{\prime})
between the crossings of the diagrams. The map
f_{*}
is a partial bijection between the crossing set because some crossing can disappear within the transformation
f
and other crossing can appear by a first or second Reidemeister move.

Let
A
be an abelian group. Let us recall the definition of oriented parity
[
38
,
39
]
.

Definition 1
.

An
oriented parity

p
is a family of maps
p_{D}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to G
defined for each diagram
D
of the knot
\mathcal{K}
, that possesses the following properties:

(P0)

for any morphism
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
and any crossing
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
for which there exists the corresponding crossing
f_{*}(v)\in{\mathcal{V}}(D^{\prime})
, one has
p_{D}(v)=p_{D^{\prime}}(f_{*}(v))
;

(P1)

if
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
is a decreasing first Reidemeister move and
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
is the disappearing crossing then
p_{D}(v)=0
;

(P2)

p_{D}(v_{1})+p_{D}(v_{2})=0
for any crossings
v_{1},\,v_{2}
to which a decreasing second Reidemeister move can be applied;

(P3+)

if
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
is a third Reidemeister move then

\epsilon_{\Delta}(v_{1})\cdot p_{D}(v_{1})+\epsilon_{\Delta}(v_{2})\cdot p_{D}(v_{2})+\epsilon_{\Delta}(v_{3})\cdot p_{D}(v_{3})=0

where
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
are the crossings involved in the move and
\epsilon_{\Delta}(v_{i})
is the incidence index of the crossing
v_{i}
to the disappearing triangle
\Delta
, see Fig.
9
.

Figure 9:
Incidence indices

Remark 1
.

1. The properties (P1),(P2) and (P3+) can be unified in the following property:
\sum_{i}\epsilon_{\Delta}(v_{i})\cdot p_{D}(v_{i})=0
if
f
is a decreasing first or second Reidemeister move or a third Reidemeister move,
v_{i}
are the crossings which take part in the move and

\epsilon_{\Delta}(v_{i})
is the incidence index of the crossing to the disappearing region
\Delta
.

2. Oriented parities include parities with coefficients defined in
[
17
]
because those parities posses the property
2p(v)=0
for any crossing
v
so the incidence indices
\epsilon_{\Delta}(v)
do not matter.

Among examples of parities are the Gaussian parity, the link parity (with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
) of virtual knots, the index parity with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}
and the homological parity of knots in a given surface (with coefficients in the first homology group of the surface)
[
18
,
39
]
.

(Gaussian parity)
.

Let

\mathcal{K}

Let

Let

Example 4

(Homological parity)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a knot in a fixed oriented surface

Let us remind basic properties of oriented parity
[

Proposition 1
.

Let
p
be an oriented parity on diagrams of a knot
\mathcal{K}
and
D
be a diagram of
\mathcal{K}
.

1. Assume that crossings
v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
form a bigon (Fig.
13

p_{D}(v_{1})+p_{D}(v_{2})=0
;

2. Assume that crossings
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
form a triangle
\Delta
(Fig.
13

\epsilon_{\Delta}(v_{1})\cdot p_{D}(v_{1})+\epsilon_{\Delta}(v_{2})\cdot p_{D}(v_{2})+\epsilon_{\Delta}(v_{3})\cdot p_{D}(v_{3})=0
.

Figure 13

Proof.

1. We need only to prove the statement for alternating bigons. Apply a first Reidemeister move
f
as shown in Fig.
14
. By the properties (P1) and (P3+) we have

\epsilon(v_{1})p_{D^{\prime}}(v_{1})+\epsilon(v_{2})p_{D^{\prime}}(v_{2})+\epsilon(w)p_{D^{\prime}}=0

and
p_{D^{\prime}}(w)=0
. Hence,
\epsilon(v_{1})p_{D^{\prime}}(v_{1})+\epsilon(v_{2})p_{D^{\prime}}(v_{2})=0
. Since
\epsilon(v_{1})=\epsilon(v_{2})

p_{D^{\prime}}(v_{1})+p_{D^{\prime}}(v_{2})=0
and
p_{D}(v_{1})+p_{D}(v_{2})=0
.

Figure 14:

2. Let
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
be the crossings of a triangle in the diagram
D
. If a third Reidemeister move can be applied to the triangle, the equality follows from the property (P3+).

Figure 15:

If

form an alternating triangle, apply a second Reidemeister move

as shown in Fig.

\epsilon(v_{1})=\epsilon(w)

Thus,
\epsilon(v_{1})p_{D^{\prime}}(v_{1})+\epsilon(v_{2})p_{D^{\prime}}(v_{2})+\epsilon(v_{3})p_{D^{\prime}}(v_{3})=0

\epsilon(v_{1})p_{D}(v_{1})+\epsilon(v_{2})p_{D}(v_{2})+\epsilon(v_{3})p_{D}(v_{3})=0.

2.2
Parity cycle

Consider the cell complex
C_{*}(\tilde{D},A)
of the covering graph
\tilde{D}
of the diagram
D
. The 0-chain space
C_{0}(\tilde{D},A)
can be identified with the group
A{\mathcal{V}}(D)
which consists of linear combinations of crossings of the diagram
D
with coefficients in the group
A
. The group of 1-chains
C_{1}(\tilde{D},A)
is identified with the group
A\mathcal{A}(D)
of linear combinations of arcs of
D
with coefficients in
A
. Having in mind these identifications, we shall write below
C_{i}(D,A)
instead of
C_{i}(\tilde{D},A)
,
i=0,1
.

Note that
H_{1}(D,A)=H_{1}(\tilde{D},A)
is the set of 1-cycles in the diagram
D
with values in the group
A
.

Definition 3
.

Let
\gamma_{D}\in C_{1}(D,A)
,
D\in\mathfrak{K}
, be a family of 1-chains assigned to all diagrams
D
of the virtual knot
\mathcal{K}
. The family of chains
\gamma_{D}
is
invariant
if for any morphism (an isotopy or a Reidemeister move)
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
and any correspondent arcs
a\in\mathcal{A}(D)
and
a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}(D^{\prime})
their coefficients coincide:
\gamma_{D}(a)=\gamma_{D^{\prime}}(a^{\prime})
.

A cycle
\delta_{D}\in H_{1}(D,A)
is
normalized
if the intersection number
D\cdot\delta_{D}=0
.

The next theorem shows that any parity determines an invariant normalized cycle.

Definition 4
.

Let
D
be a diagram of the oriented virtual knot
\mathcal{K}
, and
\tilde{D}
be the covering graph of the diagram
D
. For an oriented parity

with coefficients in an abelian group

we define a 1-chain

as follows

\delta_{D}^{p}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}(D)}\delta_{a}\cdot a\in C_{1}(\tilde{D},A).

(1)

The 1-chain
\delta_{D}^{p}
is called the
parity cycle
of the oriented parity
p
in the diagram

.

Theorem 1
.

Let
p
be an oriented parity with coefficients in an abelian group
A
on the diagrams of an oriented virtual knot
\mathcal{K}
,
D
be a diagram of
\mathcal{K}
, and
\delta_{D}^{p}
be the parity cycle. Then

1.

\delta_{D}^{p}
is a genuine 1-cycle in the cell complex
C_{*}(\tilde{D},A)
, i.e.

;

2.

\delta^{p}_{D}
is an invariant cycle;

3.

\delta^{p}_{D}
is normalized;

4.

(the intersection formula) for any crossing

we have

p_{D}(v)=D^{l}_{v}\cdot\delta_{D}^{p}.

(2)

Proof.

1) The first statement of the theorem is a reformulation of Corollary
2

Definition 12

A
regular virtual knot

Let
{\mathcal{K}}

15
) defines an invariant 1-cycle

and the formula (

)
defines an oriented parity

,
D\in\mathfrak{K}_{ac}

{\mathcal{K}}
with coefficients in the group
\bar{A}=A/\left<\sigma(\pi)\right>

\sigma(\pi)\in A
is the signature of the index
\pi

6
Intersection formula for parity functors

Let
A
and
B
be two abelian groups. A
partial isomorphism
between
A
and
B
is a group isomorphism
f\colon A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
where
A^{\prime}\subset A
and
B^{\prime}\subset B
are some subgroups.

Recall the definition of an oriented parity functor
[
39
]
.

Definition 16
.

An
oriented parity functor
P
on the diagrams of a virtual knot
\mathcal{K}
is a family of pairs
(P_{D},A(D))
,
D\in\mathfrak{K}
, where
A(D)
is an abelian group and
P_{D}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to\mathcal{A}(D)
is a map from the set of crossings of the diagram
D
, such that for any isotopy or a Reidemeister move
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
a partial isomorphism
A(f)
between
A(D)
and
A(D^{\prime})
is fixed, and

(P0)

for any corresponding crossings
v\subset{\mathcal{V}}(D)
and
v^{\prime}=f_{*}(v)\in{\mathcal{V}}(D^{\prime})
we have
P_{D^{\prime}}(v^{\prime})=A(f)(P_{D}(v))
;

(P1)

P_{D}(v)=0
if
f
is a decreasing first Reidemeister move and
v
is the disappearing crossing;

(P2)

P_{D}(v_{1})+P_{D}(v_{2})=0
if
f
is a decreasing second Reidemeister move and
v_{1},\,v_{2}
are the disappearing crossings;

(P3+)

if
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
is a third Reidemeister move then

\epsilon(v_{1})P_{D}(v_{1})+\epsilon(v_{2})P_{D}(v_{2})+\epsilon(v_{3})P_{D}(v_{3})=0

where
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
are the crossings involved in the move and
\epsilon(v_{i})
is the incidence index of the crossing
v_{i}
in relation with the disappearing triangle.

Given an oriented parity functor
(P_{D},A(D))
,
D\in\mathfrak{K}
, denote by
\bar{A}(D)
the subgroup generated by the elements
P_{D}(v)
,
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
, in the coefficient group
A(D)
. The parity functor is called
reduced
if
A(D)=\bar{A}(D)
for all diagrams
D
of the knot
{\mathcal{K}}
.

Remark 12
.

1. Each parity
p
with coefficients in a group
A
is a parity functor with
A(D)=A
,
A(f)=id_{A}
and
P_{D}=p_{D}
.

2. A parity functor assigns to any diagram its own coefficient group where the parity values lie, and these groups are identified by partial isomorphisms for diagrams connected by a Reidemeister move. This makes parity functors more flexible comparing to parities. For example, parity functors can assimilate monodromy of quasi-indices.

Theorem 11
.

Let
\pi
be a quasi-index with coefficients in an abelian group
A
on diagrams of a virtual knot
\mathcal{K}
. Denote
\bar{A}=A/\left<\sigma(\pi)\right>
where
\sigma(\pi)
is the signature of the quasi-index
\pi
. Then the family of maps
P_{D}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to\bar{A}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}
given by the formula

P_{D}(v)=\left(\sum_{v^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)}\pi_{D}(v^{\prime})\cdot(D^{l}_{v}\cdot D^{r}_{v^{\prime}}),\ ip_{D}(v)\right)

(25)

defines an oriented parity functor on diagrams of the knot
\mathcal{K}
with coefficients in the groups
A(D)\equiv\bar{A}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}
. For a Reidemeister move
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
the isomorphism
A(f)
is defined by the formula

A(f)(x,y)=(x+\Delta_{\pi}(f)\cdot y,y)

where
\Delta_{\pi}(f)=\rho(D^{\prime})-\rho(D)
is determined by Proposition
8
.

Proof.

The properties (P1)–(P3+) hold because the right-hand side of the formula (
25
) is the intersection of the half
D^{l}_{v}
with a normalized 1-cycle.

Let us check the property (P0). Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a Reidemeister move,
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
and
v^{\prime}=f_{*}(v)\in{\mathcal{V}}(D^{\prime})
be corresponding crossings. Denote
P_{D}=(p_{D},ip_{d})
. By Proposition
8
we have

p_{D^{\prime}}(v^{\prime})=p_{D}(v)-\Delta_{\pi}(f)\cdot(D^{l}_{v}\cdot D)=p_{D}(v)+\Delta_{\pi}(f)\cdot ip_{D}(v).

Then

A(f)(p_{D}(v),ip_{D}(v))=\left(p_{D}(v)+\Delta_{\pi}(f)\cdot ip_{D}(v),ip_{D}(v)\right)=(p_{D^{\prime}}(v^{\prime}),ip_{D^{\prime}}(v^{\prime})),

i.e.
P_{D^{\prime}}(v^{\prime})=A(f)(P_{D}(v))
.
∎

\bar{\sigma}(\pi)=D\cdot\bar{\delta}^{\pi}_{D}=-\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)\colon\pi_{D}(v)\neq\pi^{\circ}}\pi_{D}(v)\cdot ip_{D}(v)

and
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
if the floor function.

Proof.

9
Derived parities of virtual knots

In this section we look at connection between parities and indices from another (and more direct) side. This will allow us to loop the intersection formula and define derived parities.

9.1
Signed index

Note that an (oriented) parity
p
is not an index in general. Let us introduce an intermediate notion between parities and indices. Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a virtual knot and
\mathfrak{K}
be the category of its diagrams. Let
A
be an abelian group.

Definition 18
.

A
signed index
\tau
on the diagrams of the knot
{\mathcal{K}}
with coefficients in
A
is a family of maps
\tau_{D}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to A
,
D\in\mathfrak{K}
, which obeys the properties

(I0)

for any Reidemeister move
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
and any crossings
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
and
v^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D^{\prime})
such that
v^{\prime}=f_{*}(v)
, one has
\tau_{D}(v)=\tau_{D^{\prime}}(v^{\prime})
;

(I2+)

\tau_{D}(v_{1})=-\tau_{D}(v_{2})
for any crossings
v_{1},\,v_{2}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
to which a decreasing second Reidemeister move can be applied.

Let us list some simple properties of signed indices.

Proposition 13
.

1.

Any oriented parity
p
is a signed index;

2.

The sign function
sgn
is a signed index with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}
;

3.

If
\tau_{1}
is a signed index with coefficients in a group
A
and
\tau_{1}
is a signed index with coefficients in a group
B
then their product
\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}
where

(\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})_{D}(v)=(\tau_{1})_{D}(v)\otimes(\tau_{2})_{D}(v)\in A\otimes B,\ v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D),

is an index with coefficients in
A\otimes B
;

4.

If
\tau
is a signed index with coefficients in a group
A
and
\pi
is an index with coefficients in a group
B
then their product
\tau\otimes\pi
is a signed index with coefficients in
A\otimes B
.

Note that if
\pi_{1}
is an index with coefficients in
A
and
\pi_{1}
is an index with coefficients in
B
then
\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}
is an index with coefficients in
A\otimes B
.

Definition 19
.

Let
A
and
B
be groups. A map
f\colon A\to B
is called
odd
if for any
x\in A

f(-x)=-f(x)
in
B
, and the map
f
is called
even
if for any
x\in A

f(-x)=f(x)
.

By definition, if
f\colon A\to B
is a group homomorphism then

is odd.

Proposition 14
.

Let
\tau
be a signed index with coefficients in a group
A
and
f\colon A\to B
be a map to another group
B
. Then

•

if
f
is odd then
f\circ\tau
is a signed index with coefficients in
B
;

•

if
f
is even then
f\circ\tau
is an index with coefficients in
B
.

If
\pi
be an index with coefficients in
A
and
f\colon A\to B
be an arbitrary map then
f\circ\pi
is an index with coefficients in
B
.

Example 12
.

Let
p
be an oriented parity with coefficients in an abelian group
A
. Then its product with the sign function
sgn\cdot p
is an index. If
\pi
is an index with coefficients in
A
then
sgn\cdot\pi
is a signed index. For a signed index
\tau
, the product

is an index.

If
A={\mathbb{Z}}
(for example,
p=ip
is the index parity) then the modulus
|p|
(
|p|_{D}(v)=|p_{D}(v)|
,
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
) of the parity
p
is an index. The sign
sgn(p)
of the parity
p
where
sgn(p)_{D}(v)=sgn(p_{D}(v))
, is a signed index with values
-1,0,1
. The signed index
sgn(p)
can be used as a substitution for the crossing sign in the case of flat knots.

9.2
Linking invariant

Let
\tau
be a signed index on diagrams of a virtual knot
{\mathcal{K}}
with coefficients in an abelian group
A
. Denote the value of
\tau
on the loop crossings of type
l_{+}
(see Fig.
35
) by
\tau^{\circ}
, and the value of crossings of type
r_{+}
by
\tau^{\bullet}
. Then the crossings of type
l_{-}
have the signed index
-\tau^{\bullet}
and the crossings of type
r_{-}
have the signed index
-\tau^{\circ}
.

Definition 20
.

The signed index
\tau
is called
R1-reduced
if
\tau^{\circ}=\tau^{\bullet}=0
.

Note that any parity
p
is R1-reduced.

Proposition 15
.

Let
\tau
be an R1-reduced signed index on diagrams of a virtual knot
{\mathcal{K}}
with coefficients in an abelian group
A
. Then the value

lk(\tau)_{D}=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)}\tau_{D}(v),\quad D\in\mathfrak{K},

(36)

is invariant under Reidemeister moves

Proof.

Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be an increasing first Reidemeister move and
v^{\prime}_{0}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D^{\prime})
be the new crossing. Then

lk(\tau)_{D^{\prime}}=\sum_{v^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D^{\prime})}\tau_{D^{\prime}}(v)=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)}\tau_{D^{\prime}}(f_{*}(v))+\tau_{D^{\prime}}(v^{\prime}_{0})=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)}\tau_{D}(v)=lk(\tau)_{D}

where
\tau_{D^{\prime}}(v^{\prime}_{0})=0
because
\tau
is R1-reduced.

Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be an increasing second Reidemeister move and
v^{\prime}_{1},v^{\prime}_{2}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D^{\prime})
be the new crossings. Then

lk(\tau)_{D^{\prime}}=\sum_{v^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D^{\prime})}\tau_{D^{\prime}}(v)=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)}\tau_{D^{\prime}}(f_{*}(v))+\tau_{D^{\prime}}(v^{\prime}_{1})+\tau_{D^{\prime}}(v^{\prime}_{2})=\\
\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)}\tau_{D}(v)=lk(\tau)_{D}

because
\tau_{D^{\prime}}(v^{\prime}_{1})=-\tau_{D^{\prime}}(v^{\prime}_{2})
.

For a third Reidemeister move
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
we have

lk(\tau)_{D^{\prime}}=\sum_{v^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D^{\prime})}\tau_{D^{\prime}}(v)=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)}\tau_{D^{\prime}}(f_{*}(v))=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)}\tau_{D}(v)=lk(\tau)_{D}.

∎

Definition 21
.

For an R1-reduced signed index
\tau
, the invariant
lk(\tau)=lk(\tau)_{D}\in A
is called the
linking invariant
of the signed index
\tau
.

It
\pi
is an R1-reduced index on diagrams the knot
{\mathcal{K}}
with coefficients in
A
, we define its
linking invariant
as the linking invariant of its product with the sign function:
lk(\pi)=lk(sgn\cdot\pi)
. Then

lk(\pi)=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)}sgn(v)\cdot\pi_{D}(v)

(37)

for any diagram
D
of the knot
{\mathcal{K}}
.

Example 13
.

Let
D=D_{1}\cup D_{2}
be a diagram of a two-component link
\mathcal{L}
. Consider the index
\pi
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}

\pi_{D}(v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1,&v\mbox{ is a mixed crossing},\\

Then

lk(\pi)=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)}sgn(v)\cdot\pi(v)=\sum_{v\mbox{\scriptsize\ is a mixed crossing}}sgn(v)=lk(\mathcal{L})

is the linking number of the link
\mathcal{L}

9.3
Inner product of parities

The linking invariant
lk
allows to define an invariant inner product on the space of parities.

Definition 22
.

Let
p_{1}
be an oriented parity on diagrams of a virtual knot
{\mathcal{K}}
with coefficients in an abelian group
A
, and
p_{2}
be an oriented parity on diagrams of
{\mathcal{K}}
with coefficients in an abelian group
B
. Define the
inner product
of the parities
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
as the linking invariant of the index
p_{1}\otimes p_{2}
:

\left<p_{1},p_{2}\right>=lk(p_{1}\otimes p_{2})=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)}sgn(v)\cdot(p_{1})_{D}(v)\otimes(p_{2})_{D}(v)\in A\otimes B.

(39)

Analogously, one can define the inner product of any R1-reduced (signed) index with any (signed) index.

Remark 18
.

1. The formula (
39
) defines a bilinear map

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K},A)\times\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K},B)\to A\otimes B

where
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K},A)
(
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K},B)
) is the abelian group of oriented parities on diagrams of
{\mathcal{K}}
with coefficients in
A
(

).

If

is a ring (for example,

) then

becomes an

.

2. The signature

of an index

is equal to the inner product with the index parity

\sigma(\pi)=-\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)}\pi_{D}(v)\cdot ip_{D}(v)=-\left<\pi,ip\right>.

Definition 23
.

Let
p
be oriented parity on diagrams of a virtual knot
{\mathcal{K}}

Remark 19
.

Fix a diagram

{\mathcal{K}}

For the higher derivation we have the formula
\mathbf{p}^{(n)}=M^{n}\mathbf{p}\in(A^{(n)})^{{\mathcal{V}}(D)}

The coefficients of the intersection matrix can be calculated with the formulas from

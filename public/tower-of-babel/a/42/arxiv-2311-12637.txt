1.
Introduction

The Novikov Higher Signature conjecture states that the higher signatures of closed oriented
manifolds are invariant under orientation-preserving homotopy equivalences. For aspherical manifolds it is equivalent to say that
the rational Pontryagin classes are homotopy invariant. The conjecture has a long and eventful history (see the survey
[
Yu
]
) and it is still open.

One of the approaches to the Novikov conjecture was introduced more than 30 years ago by Connes, Gromov, and Moscovici by defining
Lipschitz cohomology classes of a discrete group and proving the Novikov conjecture for such classes
[
CGM
]
.
Their approach recovered the Novikov conjecture for all classes of groups where it was proven by that time. In particular, it gave another proof of
the Novikov conjecture for hyperbolic groups. More than 20 years ago T. Kato used Lipschitz cohomology to prove the Novikov conjecture for
the combable groups
[
K1
]
,
[
K2
]
. In
[
Dr
]
Lipschitz cohomology were used to recover Carlson-Pedersen result
[
CP
]
on the Novikov conjecture
for groups
\Gamma
admitting certain equivariant compactification of the classifying space
E\Gamma
.

Since then the Lipschitz cohomology approach to the Novikov conjecture seems to be
completely abandoned. This paper is an attempt to revive it.

Lipschitz cohomology classes of a discrete group were defined in [CGM] as the collection of images under a family of certain slant product homomorphisms
denoted
\alpha_{\cap}
and defined by maps
\alpha:\Gamma\times P\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
where
P
is a parameter space. Connes, Gromov, and Moscovici
first defined a light version of such classes called
proper Lipschitz cohomology
.
It turns out that in all cases where all cohomology classes of an aspherical manifold are known to be proper Lipshitz, like in the case of manifolds
with non-positive curvature
K(M)\leq 0
,
they all
belong to the image of one homomorphism
\alpha_{\cap}
. In such situation we say that
the group
\Gamma
has
canonically Lipschitz cohomology
. Unfortunately the canonically Lipschitz cohomology cannot lead to a proof of the Novikov conjecture, since
it was shown in
[
Dr
]
that there are aspherical manifolds
which do not have all its rational cohomology classes canonically Lipschitz.

It was noticed in
[
CGM
]
that to prove that the 0-dimensional cohomology class
1\in H^{0}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{R}})={\mathbb{R}}
is proper Lipschitz could be a difficult problem.
In the second half of the paper
[
CGM
]
the authors
introduced a heavier version of Lipschitz cohomology classes for which the Novikov conjecture still holds true and for which is quite easy to show that
the class
1\in H^{0}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{R}})
is Lipschitz for any group.

In this paper we explore the possibility of proving the Novikov conjecture for a group
\Gamma
by proving that
its Berstein-Schwarz class
\beta_{\Gamma}\in H^{1}(\Gamma,I(\Gamma))
is Lipschitz. Here
I(\Gamma)
is the augmentation ideal.
Due to universality of
\beta_{\Gamma}

[
DR
]
,
[
Sc
]
every cohomology class of
\Gamma
is the image of some power of

\beta_{\Gamma}
under a coefficient homomorphism.
In order to use this property of
\beta_{\Gamma}
with the aim the Novikov conjecture we extend the notion of Lipschitz
cohomology classes to cohomology with local coefficients. Like in
[
CGM
]
we do it in two steps: First we define proper Lipschitz cohomology
and then general Lipschitz cohomology.

We note that, since the authors in
[
CGM
]
worked with differential forms, their definition of Lipschitz cohomology classes makes sense only for real coefficients.
In this paper we use a combinatorial approach. For that, first we give an integral version of Connes-Gromov-Moscovici definition of proper
Lipschitx cohomology with constant coefficients
and then
we transform it to a definition of proper
Lipschitz cohomology with coefficients in a
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
-module. We prove the product theorem, the coefficient homomorphism theorem,
and the connecting homomorphism theorem
for proper Lipschitz cohomology with local coefficients. These results allow us to reduce the Novikov conjecture to the question about proper Lipschitz property of
the 0-dimensional class
1\in H^{0}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Z}}
. As it was already noted above, this is far from being easy.

In
[
CGM
]
the definition of proper Lipschitz cohomology classes of a group
\Gamma
was extended by dropping at some the properness of

\Gamma
-action on the paramiter space
P
.
We extend our definition to the non-proper case similarly. The bad news about general Lipschitz cohomology with local coefficients is that the connecting
homomorpphism theorem does not hold anymore.
It means that we cannot reduce the Novikov conjecture to the above question about 0-dimensional cohomology. The good news is that
he product theorem and the coefficient homomorphism theorem still hold true.
In view of universality of
\beta_{\Gamma}
we can reduce the Novikov conjecture to
the question about 1-dimensional cohomology. Namely, we reduce it to the question whether the Berstein-Schwarz class
\beta_{\Gamma}
is Lipschitz.

Also in the paper we computed the cohomology group
H^{k}(\Gamma,I(\Gamma)^{\otimes k})={\mathbb{Z}}
in the case when
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of a closed aspherical orientable manifold.
Thus, since the Novikov conjecture is rational, to prove it for a group
\Gamma
it suffices to find any nonzero Lipschitz cohomology class in
H^{1}(\Gamma,I(\Gamma))
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we give a combinatorial definition of proper Lipschitz cohomology with constant coefficients and present examples of aspherical manifolds with
canonically proper Lipschitz integral cohomology.
In Section 3 we define the Berstein-Schwarz cohomology class, state the Universality Theorem, and carry out computations of cohomology
of aspherical manifolds with coefficients in
I(\Gamma)^{\otimes k}
. In Section 4 first we give definition of proper and then general Lipschitz cohomology with local coefficients.
There we prove the coefficient homomorphism, the product, and the connecting homomorphism theorems.

2.1.
Equivariant slant product

We recall the slant product. Let

[

/:Hom(C\otimes D,{\mathbb{Z}})\times D\to Hom(C,{\mathbb{Z}})

/:H^{n}(C\otimes D)\times H_{k}(D)\to H^{n-k}(C).

Now we assume that the group
\Gamma
acts on the chain complexes

\Gamma

/:Hom_{\Gamma}(C\otimes D,{\mathbb{Z}})\times D\to Hom_{\Gamma}(C,{\mathbb{Z}})

where

denotes the group of
\Gamma
-invariant homomorphisms.

Suppose that the discrete group
\Gamma
acts freely on a finite dimensional locally finite simplicial complex
P
by simplicial transformations.
Let

be a free resolution of
{\mathbb{Z}}
over
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
and
let

to be the chain complex that consist of
\Gamma
-invariant infinite simplicial chains on

.
Thus,

is isomorphic to the chain complex of infinite simplicial chains

on the orbit sapce.

We consider the equivariant slant product

where

. Note that if

is compact, then

.

The space of probability measures on

which is supported on a tubular neighborhood

of the diagonal. We may assume that the neighborhood

under the natural projection

is the image under

of the Thom class

of normal bundle of the diagonal in

.

We recall that the Thom class

of the normal bundle of the diagonal

and
the slant product with

is the Poincare Duality isomorphism

This isomorphism can be stated in terms of

Thus, the slant product
(\bar{p}p^{\prime})^{*}(u^{\prime})/:H^{\Gamma}_{k}(\widetilde{M})\to H_{n-k}^{\Gamma}(\widetilde{M})
is an isomorphism. Since
(p^{\prime})^{*}(u^{\prime})=\hat{\alpha}^{*}([\omega])
,
the slant product

is an isomorphism.

Therefore, all cohomology of
\Gamma
are proper Lipschitz
[

]
.

If

is not parallelizable the same result can be achieved by adding to

the complementary vector bundle and make the above argument with a large parameter space

Then one should use the Poincare Duality for the infinite chain homology (sometimes called the Borel-Moore homology).

3.
Berstein-Schwarz class of aspherical manifolds

3.1.
Berstein-Schwarz class

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group,
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
denote the group ring, and let
\epsilon:{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be the augmentation homomorphism. Then the
augmentation ideal
I(\Gamma)
is the kernel of
\epsilon
.
The Berstein-Schwarz class
\beta_{\Gamma}\in H^{1}(\Gamma,I(\Gamma))
is the image
\beta_{\Gamma}=\delta(1)

of the generator
1\in H^{0}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}})
under connecting homomorphism
\delta:H^{0}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{1}(\Gamma,I(\Gamma))
in the coefficient long exact sequence
generated by the short exact sequence

0\to I(\Gamma)\to{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\epsilon}}{{\to}}{\mathbb{Z}}\to 0.

We recall
[
Br
]
that the cap product
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}
of two cohomology classes
\alpha_{i}\in H^{k_{i}}(\Gamma,L_{i})
,
i=1,2
, with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
-modules
L_{i}
belongs to
H^{k_{1}+k_{2}}(\Gamma,L_{1}\otimes L_{2})
where the tensor product is taken over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

3.1 Theorem

(Universality)
.

[
DR
]
,
[
Sc
]

For any
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
-module
L
and any cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{k}(\Gamma,L)
there is a homomorphism
\phi:I(\Gamma)^{\otimes k}\to L

such that
\alpha
is the image of
(\beta_{\Gamma})^{k}
under the induced homomorphism

\phi^{*}:H^{k}(\Gamma,I(\Gamma)^{\otimes k})\to H^{k}(\Gamma,L).

In the case when
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of a closed orientable aspherical manifold the group
H^{k}(\Gamma,I(\Gamma)^{\otimes k})
can be computed.

3.2 Theorem
.

For a closed orientable aspherical
n
-manifold
M
with the fundamental group
\Gamma
for
k<n
the cohomology group

H^{k}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes k})
is an infinite cyclic group generated by
(\beta_{\Gamma})^{k}
.

The cohomology group
H^{n}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n})
is the group of coinvariants
(I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n})_{\Gamma}=I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n}\otimes_{\Gamma}{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

For any
\ell
the short exact sequences

0\to I(\Gamma)\to{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma\to{\mathbb{Z}}\to 0

defines by tensor product with
I(\Gamma)^{\otimes\ell-1}
a short exact sequence

0\to I(\Gamma)^{\otimes\ell}\to I(\Gamma)^{\otimes\ell-1}\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma\to I(\Gamma)^{\otimes\ell-1}\to 0.

We note that the
\Gamma
-module
I(\Gamma)^{\otimes\ell-1}\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
is projective
[
DR
]
.
Then the coefficient long exact sequence for homology and the fact that homology of a group with coefficients in projective module
are trivial in dimensions
>0

[
Br
]
produce the isomorphisms for
i>0

H_{i+1}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes\ell-1})\to H_{i}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes\ell}).

In particular,

{\mathbb{Z}}=H_{n}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})=H_{n-1}(M;I(\Gamma))=H_{n-2}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes 2})=\cdots=H_{1}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n-1}).

By the Poincare duality with local coefficients
[
Br
]

H^{k}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes k})=H_{n-k}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes k})={\mathbb{Z}}

for
k<n
and

H^{n}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n})=H_{0}(\Gamma,I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n})=(I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n})_{\Gamma}.

∎

3.3 Theorem
.

For any group
\pi
there is an isomorphism
H_{1}(\pi,{\mathbb{Z}})=I(\pi)_{\pi}
. For
\ell>1
there is a short exact sequence

0\to H_{\ell}(\pi;{\mathbb{Z}})\to(I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell})_{\pi}\to I(\pi)I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell-1}\to 0.

Proof.

The equality
H_{1}(\pi,{\mathbb{Z}})=I(\pi)_{\pi}
follows from the coefficients long exact sequence

0=H_{1}(\pi,{\mathbb{Z}}\pi)\to H_{1}(\pi,{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{0}(\pi,I(\pi))\to H_{0}(\pi,{\mathbb{Z}}\pi)\to H_{0}(\pi,{\mathbb{Z}})\to 0

and the fact that
H_{0}(\pi,{\mathbb{Z}}\pi)=({\mathbb{Z}}\pi)_{\pi}\to{\mathbb{Z}}=H_{0}(\pi,{\mathbb{Z}})
is an isomorphism. For
\ell>1
we have the following exact sequence

0\to H_{1}(\pi,I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell-1})\to H_{0}(\pi,I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell})\to H_{0}(\pi,I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell-1}\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}\pi)\to H_{0}(\pi,I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell-1})\to 0

which defines the short exact sequence

0\to H_{1}(\pi,I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell-1})\to H_{0}(\pi,I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell})\to K_{\ell-1}\to 0

where

K_{\ell}=\operatorname{Ker}\{(I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell}\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}\pi)_{\pi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1\otimes\epsilon}}{{\longrightarrow}}(I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell}\otimes{\mathbb{Z}})_{\pi}\}.

In view of the equality
(M\otimes N)_{\pi}=M\otimes_{\pi}N
we obtain

K_{\ell}=Ker\{I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell}\to(I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell})_{\pi}\}=I(\pi)I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell}.

The chain of isomorphisms

H_{i+1}(\pi,I(\pi)^{\otimes k-1})\to H_{i}(\pi;I(\pi)^{\otimes k})

for
i\geq 1
defines the equalities

H_{1}(\pi,I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell-1})=H_{2}(\pi,I(\pi)^{\otimes\ell-2})=\cdots=H_{l-1}(\pi,I(\pi))=H_{\ell}(\pi,{\mathbb{Z}}).

∎

3.4 Proposition
.

For a closed orientable aspherical manifold
M
with the fundamental group
\Gamma
,

H^{n}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n})=I(\Gamma)I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n-1}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.3
there is the short exact sequence

0\to{\mathbb{Z}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\to}}H^{n}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\to}}I(\Gamma)I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n-1}\to 0.

As it follows from the commutative diagram

\begin{CD}H_{n}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})@>{\cap\beta_{\Gamma}}>{}>H_{n-1}(M.I(\Gamma))@>{\cap\beta_{\Gamma}}>{}>\dots @>{\cap\beta_{\Gamma}}>{}>H_{0}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n})\\
@A{[M]\cap}A{}A@A{[M]\cap}A{}A@A{[M]\cap}A{}A\\
H^{0}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})@>{\cup\beta_{\Gamma}}>{}>H^{1}(M;I(\Gamma))@>{\cup\beta_{\Gamma}}>{}>\dots @>{\cup\beta_{\Gamma}}>{}>H^{n}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n})\\
\end{CD}

the homomorphism
i
being the Poincare dual to the iterated cup product with
\beta_{\Gamma}
is an iterated
\cap
-product with
\beta_{\Gamma}
. Hence
i:H_{n}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{0}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n})

takes the fundamental class
[M]
to
[M]\cap(\beta_{\Gamma})^{n}
, the Poincare dual to
(\beta_{\Gamma})^{n}
.
By the Universality of the Berstein-Schwarz class
there is a homomorphism of
\Gamma
-modules
\phi:I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
the induced homomorphism

\phi^{*}:H^{n}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n})\to H^{n}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Z}}

takes
(\beta_{\Gamma})^{n}

to the generator
1\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Let
H^{n}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Z}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\to}}{\mathbb{Z}}=H_{n}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
be the isomorphism that takes 1 to
[M]
. We obtain the commutative diagram

\begin{CD}H^{n}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})@<{\phi^{*}}<{}<H^{n}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n})\\
@V{\psi}V{}V@V{[M]\cap}V{}V\\
H_{n}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})@>{-\cap(\beta_{\Gamma})^{n}}>{}>H_{0}(M;I(\Gamma)^{\otimes n})\\
\end{CD}

that defines the splitting.
∎

4.
Lipschitz cohomology with local coefficients

4.1.
Proper Lipschitz cohomology with local coefficients

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group with finite classifying complex
B\Gamma
.
We recall that he proper Lipschitz cohomology classes of
\Gamma
were by
the following data
[
CGM
]
:
Suppose that there is a finite-dimensional connected complex
P
with a proper
\Gamma
-action
and a map
\alpha:\Gamma\times P\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfying the conditions

•

(1)
\alpha
is invariant with respect to the diagonal action of
\Gamma
on

\Gamma\times P
;

•

(2) the restriction
\alpha\mid_{\gamma\times P}
is proper for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
;

•

(3) the restriction
\alpha\mid_{\Gamma\times p}
is 1-Lipschitz for all
p\in P
for the word metric on
\Gamma
.

Connes-Gromov-Moscovici used constant real coefficients
{\mathbb{R}}
. Here we present the definition which extends to local coefficients
L
with base ring
{\mathbb{Z}}
as well as
{\mathbb{R}}
.

We assume that the action of
\Gamma
on
P
is a free simplicial action.
Let
D=C_{*}(P)
be the simplicial chain complex of
P
and let
S_{m}
denote the set of all
m
-simplices in
P
.
The group of
\Gamma
-invariant

m
-chains
D_{m}^{\Gamma}(L)=(D_{m}\otimes L)^{\Gamma}
with coefficients in
L
consists of
formal sums
\sum_{\sigma\in S_{m}}\lambda_{\sigma}\sigma

where coefficients
\lambda_{\sigma}\in L
are satisfying the equalities
\lambda_{\gamma\sigma}=\gamma\lambda_{\sigma}
.

4.1 Proposition
.

If
D
is a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
-module, then

D^{\Gamma}(L)\cong L\otimes_{\Gamma}D
.

Proof.

Since
D
is finitely generated, every its element
\sum_{\sigma\in S_{m}}\lambda_{\sigma}\sigma
can be splitted in a finite sum
\sum_{i}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\lambda_{\gamma\sigma_{i}}(\gamma\sigma_{i})
.
The map
\Phi:D^{\Gamma}(L)\to L\otimes_{\Gamma}D
defined on generators as

\Phi(\sum_{\gamma}\lambda_{\gamma\sigma}(\gamma\sigma))=\lambda_{\sigma}\otimes_{\Gamma}\sigma

is well-defined homomorphism, since

\lambda_{\sigma}\otimes_{\Gamma}\sigma=\gamma\lambda_{\sigma}\otimes_{\Gamma}\gamma\sigma=\lambda_{\gamma\sigma}\otimes_{\Gamma}\gamma\sigma.

It
is an isomorphism with the inverse
\Phi^{-1}
that takes
\lambda\otimes_{\Gamma}\sigma
to
\sum_{\gamma}\gamma\lambda(\gamma\sigma)
.
∎

Let
C_{*}
be the normal resolution of
\Gamma
, i.e. the simplicial chain complex of the simplex
\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma)
spanned by
\Gamma

[
Br
]
.
Suppose that
\phi:(C\otimes D)_{n}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is a
\Gamma
-invariant cocycle such that the sum

\sum_{\sigma}\phi(c\otimes\sigma)

is finite for any
\Gamma
-invariant infinite chain
z=\sum_{\sigma}n_{\sigma}\sigma
in
D^{\Gamma}=D^{\Gamma}({\mathbb{Z}})
where
\sigma
runs over all
(n-k)
-dimensional simplices
in
P
. Thus, this sum defines a
\Gamma
-invariant cocycle
\bar{\phi}:(C\otimes D^{\Gamma})_{n}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We define a
\Gamma
-invariant slant product

\bar{\phi}/:D^{\Gamma}_{n-k}(L)\to Hom_{\Gamma}(C_{k},L)

by the formula

(\bar{\phi}/z)(\Delta)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-k}}\phi(\Delta\otimes\sigma)\lambda_{\sigma}

where
z=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-k}}\lambda_{\sigma}\sigma
is a
\Gamma
-invariant
(n-k)
-chain with coefficients in
L
and
\Delta=[\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{k}]
is a
k
-simplex in
\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma)
.

Next, we show that the cochain
\bar{\phi}/z:C_{k}\to L

is equivariant:

(\bar{\phi}/z)(g\Delta)=\sum_{\sigma}\phi(g\Delta\otimes\sigma)\lambda_{\sigma}=\sum_{\sigma}\phi(\Delta\otimes g^{-1}\sigma)gg^{-1}\lambda_{\sigma}=

\sum_{\sigma}\phi(\Delta\otimes g^{-1}\sigma)g\lambda_{g^{-1}\sigma}=g(\bar{\phi}/z)(\Delta).

Here we use the fact that
g^{-1}:S_{n-k}\to S_{n-k}
is a bijection.
The cochain
\bar{\phi}/z
is a
cocycle when
z=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\lambda_{\gamma\sigma}\gamma\sigma
is a
\Gamma
-invariant cycle.
Indeed,
it is a cocycle since
\bar{\phi}
is a cocycle and applying the Leibnitz formula
\partial(c\otimes a)=\partial c\otimes a\pm c\otimes\partial a

we obtain
\delta(\bar{\phi}/z)=(\delta\bar{\phi})/z\pm\phi/\partial z=0
.

Then we show that slant product with
\bar{\phi}
defines the homomorphism

H_{n-k}(D_{*}^{\Gamma}(L))\to H^{k}(C_{*};L)

which we denote as
[\bar{\phi}]/
.
Let
z
be a
\Gamma
-invariant cycle as above and let
z^{\prime}=\sum_{\sigma}\mu_{\sigma}\sigma^{\prime}
be an invariant
(n-k+1)
-dimensional chain.
Then
\bar{\phi}/(z+\partial z^{\prime})=\bar{\phi}/z+\bar{\phi}/\partial z^{\prime}
. Note that
\bar{\phi}/\partial z^{\prime}=\delta(\bar{\phi}/z^{\prime})
. Therefore,
\bar{\phi}/(z+\partial z^{\prime})
defines the same cohomology class as

\bar{\phi}/z
.

Assume that we have a map
\alpha:\Gamma\times P\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfying the conditions 1-3 and let
\bar{\alpha}:\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma)\times\Gamma\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the extension defined in Section 2.
Let
\omega
be a cocycle on

H^{n}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Z}}
.
We define
\phi=\bar{\alpha}^{*}(\omega)

\phi(\Delta\otimes\sigma)=\omega(\bar{\alpha}|\Delta\times\sigma)

where we treat
\bar{\alpha}:\Delta^{k}\times\sigma^{n-k}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
as a singular chain with the staircase triangulation of the product of two oriented simplices.
The cocycle
\phi
is
\Gamma

We denote by

the homology group defined by infinite invariant chains with coefficients in a
\Gamma
-module

Note that if

As in the case of constant coefficients

Thus, the cohomology class

All classes in the image

.

In the case of constant coefficients

4.4 Remark
.

In the above construction we can use cellular chain complexes

4.5 Theorem
.

The image of a proper Lipschitz class under coefficients homomorphism is proper Lipschitz.

Proof.

Let

be a homomorphism of
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
-modules and let

∎

Given a short exact sequence of

4.6 Theorem
.

Let
0\to K\to L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\to}}M\to 0
be a short exact sequence of
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
-modules,
C_{*}
and
D_{*}
be chain complexes of free
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
-modules,
and let
a^{*}\in H^{n}(C\otimes D^{\Gamma};{\mathbb{Z}})

\begin{CD}H_{n-k}(D^{\Gamma}(M))@>{a^{*}/}>{}>H^{k}(C,M)\\
@V{\bar{\partial}}V{}V@V{\bar{\delta}}V{}V\\
H_{n-k-1}(D^{\Gamma}(K))@>{a^{*}/}>{}>H^{k+1}(C,K)\\
\end{CD}

commutes up to the sign,
where
\bar{\partial}
and

are connecting homomorphisms in the coefficient homology and cohomology long exact sequences.

Proof.

Let

denote a cocycle representing

and let

be a cycle in

,
m_{\sigma}\in M
and

. Then
by the Snake Lemma for homology
\bar{\partial}z=\sum\ell_{\sigma}\partial\sigma
with

and

. Then

(a/\bar{\partial}z)(c)=\sum_{\sigma}a(c\otimes\partial\sigma)\ell_{\sigma}=\pm\sum_{\sigma}a(\partial c\otimes\sigma)\ell_{\sigma}

For a cochain

a lift
\bar{\psi}:C_{k}\to L

by the formula

with

chosen above.

By the Snake Lemma for cohomology we obtain

∎

4.7 Corollary
.

Suppose that the integral 0-dimensional cohomology class
1\in H^{0}(\Gamma)
is proper Lipschitz.
Then the Berstein-Schwarz class
\beta_{\Gamma}\in H^{1}(\Gamma,I(\Gamma)
is proper Lipschitz.

Proof.

We consider the short exact sequence

0\to I(\Gamma)\to{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\epsilon}}{{\to}}{\mathbb{Z}}\to 0

defined by the augmentation.
Let
\alpha:\Gamma\times P\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a map satisfying
conditions 1-3 required for proper Lipschitz property of
1\in H^{0}(\Gamma)

and let
\bar{\phi}
be the cocycle from the definition of
\alpha_{\cap}
. Since the action of
\Gamma
on
P
is proper, we have
n>0
.
Thus,
H^{0}(\Gamma)\ni 1=\alpha_{\cap}([z])
for some
[z]\in H_{n}(P:\Gamma)
.
By Theorem
4.6
we obtain
\alpha_{\cap}(\bar{\partial}[z])=\bar{\delta}(1)=\beta_{\Gamma}
.
∎

4.8 Proposition

is proper Lipschitz via a map

. Then
b
is proper Lipschitz via the map
\alpha\times 1:\Gamma\times P\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}={\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}

Proof.

Here we consider the pul-back action of
\Gamma
on
P\times R
and we take
\bar{\alpha}=\alpha\times 1
.
Let

be the infinite 1-chain for the natural simplicial complex structure on

. Note that

is a cycle.
Let
\omega_{0}
be a generator of

supported in
1/4
-neighborhood of

Suppose that

for some cycle

(b/z)(\Delta)=\sum_{\sigma}(\omega(\bar{\alpha}|\Delta\times\sigma)\lambda_{\sigma}=\sum_{\sigma}(\omega\otimes\omega_{0})((\bar{\alpha}\times 1)|\Delta\times\sigma\times[0,1])\lambda_{\sigma}=

4.10 Theorem
.

The cup product of proper Lipschitz cohomology classes is proper Lipschitz.

Proof.

Let
\bar{\alpha}_{1}:\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma)\times P_{1}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}}
and
\bar{\alpha}_{2}:\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma)\times P_{1}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}}
be the maps that realize Lipschitz classes
(\alpha_{1})_{\cap}([z_{1}])\in H^{k_{1}}(\Gamma,L)

and
(\alpha_{2})_{\cap}([z^{\prime}])\in H^{k_{2}}(\Gamma,M)
with
z=\sum\lambda_{\sigma}\sigma
and
z^{\prime}=\sum\mu_{\sigma^{\prime}}\sigma^{\prime}
. We take the product
\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma)\times\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma)

with diagonal action of
\Gamma
for the classifying space
E\Gamma
and cosider a CW-complex
P=P_{1}\times P_{2}
with the diagonal
\Gamma
-action. We
define

\bar{\alpha}:(\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma)\times\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma))\times(P_{1}\times P_{2})\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}}={\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}

as
\bar{\alpha}(x_{1}\times x_{2},y_{1}\times y_{2})=\bar{\alpha}_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})\times\bar{\alpha}_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})
. We show that

\alpha_{\cap}([z\otimes z^{\prime}])=(\alpha_{1})_{\cap}([z])(\alpha_{2})_{\cap}([z^{\prime}]).

Let
k=k_{1}+k_{2}
and
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
. Let
\Delta^{i}\times\Delta^{k-i}
be a
k
-cell in
\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma)\times\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma)
. The cocycle
\phi
from the definition of
\alpha_{\cap}
is given by the formula

\phi((\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2})\otimes(\sigma\times\sigma^{\prime}))=(\omega_{1}\otimes\omega_{2})(\bar{\alpha}|(\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2})\times(\sigma\times\sigma^{\prime}))=

(\omega_{1}\otimes\omega_{2})(\bar{\alpha}_{1}|(\Delta_{1}\times\sigma)\times\bar{\alpha}_{2}|(\Delta_{2}\times\sigma^{\prime}))=\omega_{1}(\bar{\alpha}_{1}|(\Delta_{1}\times\sigma_{1})\otimes\omega_{2}(\bar{\alpha}_{2}|(\Delta_{2}\times\sigma_{2})

=\phi_{1}(\Delta_{1}\otimes\sigma)\phi_{2}(\Delta_{2}\otimes\sigma^{\prime}).

We compare the cocycles
\bar{\phi}/(z_{1}\otimes z_{2})
and
(\bar{\phi}_{1}/z_{1})\cup(\bar{\phi}_{2}/z_{2})
which are both elements of

Hom_{\Gamma}(C(\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma))\otimes C(\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma)),L\otimes M).

Since
z_{1}\otimes z_{2}=\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}(\lambda_{\sigma}\otimes\mu_{\sigma^{\prime}})(\sigma\times\sigma^{\prime})
, we obtain

(\bar{\phi}/z\otimes z^{\prime})(\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2})=\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\phi((\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2})\otimes(\sigma\times\sigma^{\prime}))\lambda_{\sigma}\otimes\mu_{\sigma^{\prime}}.

Then we evaluate the cup product cocycle using the fact that when we do it with respect to the product resolution
C_{*}(\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma))\otimes C_{*}(\Delta^{\infty}(\Gamma))
of
\Gamma

the cup product cocycle coincides with the cross product cocycle treated as a
\Gamma
-equivariant homomorphism instead of
(\Gamma\times\Gamma)
-equivariant (see
[
Br
]
). Thus,

((\bar{\phi}_{1}/z_{1})\cup(\bar{\phi}_{2}/z_{2}))(\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2})=\bar{\phi}_{1}/(\sum_{\sigma}\lambda_{\sigma}\sigma)(\Delta_{1})\otimes\bar{\phi}_{2}/(\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\mu_{\sigma^{\prime}}\sigma^{\prime})(\Delta_{2})

=\sum_{\sigma}\phi_{1}(\Delta_{1}\times\sigma)\lambda_{\sigma}\otimes\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\phi_{2}(\Delta_{2}\times\sigma^{\prime})\mu_{\sigma^{\prime}}=\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\phi((\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2})\otimes(\sigma\times\sigma^{\prime}))\lambda_{\sigma}\otimes\mu_{\sigma^{\prime}}.

Therefore, the cocycles
\bar{\phi}/(z_{1}\otimes z_{2})
and
(\bar{\phi}_{1}/z_{1})\cup(\bar{\phi}_{2}/z_{2})
coincide.
∎

4.2.
General Lipschitz cohomology

Now in the definition of Lipschitz cohomology we drop the assumption of properness of the action of
\Gamma
on
P
.
We assume that
P
is a simplicial complex and
\Gamma
acts by simplicial homeomorphisms.
Still the equivariant infinite chains
D^{\Gamma}
for
D=C_{*}(P)
are defined and the homomorphism
\alpha_{\cap}
can be defined by the same formula.
Thus,

(\bar{\phi}/z)(\Delta)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-k}}\phi(\Delta\otimes\sigma)\lambda_{\sigma}

for
\phi(\Delta\otimes\sigma)=\omega(\bar{\alpha}|\Delta\times\sigma)

and
\alpha_{\cap}=[\bar{\phi}]
.

The difference is in formulas for
\bar{\phi}/z
in the case when the chain
z=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\lambda_{\gamma\sigma}(\gamma\sigma)

is defined by a singhle orbit. In the proper cohomology case it is

\bar{\phi}/z(\Delta)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\omega(\bar{\alpha}|\Delta\times\gamma\sigma)\lambda_{\gamma\sigma}

whereas in the general case

\bar{\phi}/z(\Delta)=\sum_{\gamma\Gamma_{\sigma}\in\Gamma/\Gamma_{\sigma}}\omega(\bar{\alpha}|\Delta\times\gamma\sigma)\lambda_{\gamma\sigma}

where
\Gamma_{\sigma}
is the isotropy group of
\sigma
and the sum is indexed by the set
\Gamma/\Gamma_{\sigma}
of cosets of
\Gamma_{\sigma}
.

The following two examples are modifications of examples from
[
CGM
]

4.11 Example
.

The integral 0-dimensional cohomology class of
\Gamma
is Lipschitz for any group
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Consider the case when
P
is a point with a trivial action of
\Gamma
and with the constant map
\alpha:\Gamma\times P\to{\mathbb{R}}^{0}
.
∎

4.12 Example
.

All integral 1-dimensional cohomology classes of any finitely generated group
\Gamma
are Lipschitz.

Proof.

Let
f:\Gamma\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a 1-cocycle. We take
P={\mathbb{R}}
with the action of
\Gamma
defined as
\gamma(x)=x+f(x)
. Define
\alpha:\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
as
\alpha(\gamma,x)=-\gamma^{-1}(x)
. The axioms 1-2 are obviously satisfied. Let
S
be a finite generating for
\Gamma
and let
\lambda=\max\{|f(s)\mid s\in S
.
Then

|\alpha(\gamma,x)-\alpha(\gamma s,x)=|f(\gamma^{-1})-f((\gamma s)^{-1})|=|f(\gamma s)-f(\gamma)|=|f(s)|\leq\lambda d_{S}(\gamma s,\gamma).

This implies that
\alpha
is
\lambda
-Lipschitz.
We resale
{\mathbb{R}}
accordingly to get
\alpha
1-Lipschitz.

Suppose that the generator
\omega\in H_{c}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
has support concentrated in a small neighborhood around
1/2\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Le
z=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}m
be an infinite simplicial 0-chain with respect to the triangulation of
{\mathbb{R}}
where
{\mathbb{Z}}
is the 0-skeleton. Then
\alpha_{\cap}([z])
is defined by the cocycle

\bar{\phi}/z
which takes value

(\bar{\phi}/z)([e,\gamma])=\sum_{m}\omega(\bar{\alpha}|[1,\gamma]\times m)=\sum_{m}\omega([\alpha(e,m),\alpha(\gamma,m)])=\sum_{m}\omega([-m,f(\gamma)-m]).

Since for
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}
only
r
different integer translates of the interval
[0,r]
can hit 1/2, we obtain that
(\bar{\phi}/z)([e,\gamma])=f(\gamma)
.
Therefore,
\alpha_{\cap}([z])=[f]
.
∎

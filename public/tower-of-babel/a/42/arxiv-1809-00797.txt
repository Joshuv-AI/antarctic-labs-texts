The properties of D ​ 1 D1 -branes from lattice super Yang–Mills theory using gauge/gravity duality
Raghav Govind Jha

Abstract
The two-dimensional supersymmetric Yang-Mills (SYM) theory with sixteen supercharges at large N N and strong ’t Hooft coupling is conjectured to be dual to certain supergravity solutions in the decoupling limit. We discretize the gauge theory preserving a subset of supersymmetries on the lattice. Based on the choice of a point in the moduli space for the expansion of the gauge links to target the correct continuum theory, one ends up with different lattice geometries. In our previous work, we explored the free energy and the phase structure on a skewed torus corresponding to A 2 ∗ A_{2}^{*} lattice geometry. Here, we will consider square lattice and calculate the free energy, equation of state and speed of sound in this strongly coupled supersymmetric plasma. Since there is no shear viscosity in two dimensions, we comment on the expectations for the bulk viscosity from the calculations on the dual supergravity side, which unlike the conformal 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYM case does not vanish and is proportional to the trace of energy-momentum tensor.

Introduction

It is conjectured that the strong coupling, large

The general form of the gauge/gravity duality is valid in lower dimensions as well, however, the
four-dimensional case is special because
\mathcal{N}=4

Unlike SYM, the theory of strong interactions (QCD) has no known gravity dual, but QCD at high temperatures (about

\eta/s

viscosity coefficient is
\eta

\zeta

\mathcal{N}=4

Recently, we explored the two-dimensional supersymmetric gauge theory on a skewed torus and

In this proceedings, we
propose

\emph{i.e}

c_{s}

2.1
Supergravity and its predictions

The IIB supergravity is dual to the ‘decoupling’ limit of
N
coincident
D1
-branes
[
1

finite-energy excitations

D1

a perturbative description. In the intermediate regime, there is supergravity (SUGRA) description in terms of
D0/D1
brane solutions and at
sufficiently low temperatures, one flows to a free orbifold CFT description. See Figure (
1

different regimes.
The region in which the strongly coupled Yang Mills theory (denoting
p

dual to the Type IIA/IIB supergravity is given by,

1\ll\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}\ll N^{\frac{10-2p}{7-p}}

(1)

where,
\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}=\lambda_{p+1}\beta^{3-p}=t^{-(3-p)}

\lambda_{p+1}
is the coupling in

(p+1)

1\ll\lambda_{4}\ll N
in four dimensions.
We will refer to
\lambda_{2}
and

\displaystyle T=\frac{(7-p)U_{0}^{\frac{5-p}{2}}}{4\pi\sqrt{d_{p}\lambda_{p+1}}}

\frac{E}{N^{2}}\Big|_{\mathrm{Dp-brane}}=\frac{(9-p)U_{0}^{7-p}L^{p}}{2^{11-2p}\pi^{\frac{13-3p}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{9-p}{2}\right)\lambda_{p+1}^{2}}

2.2
Finite temperature supersymmetric gauge theory

We consider the maximally supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory on two-torus (
S^{1}_{\beta}\times S^{1}_{L}
) with anti-periodic
boundary conditions for the fermions along the time cycle (
\beta=1/T
) and denote the trace of the energy-momentum
tensor (also known as ‘trace anomaly’ or ‘interaction measure’) by
\Delta=E-P
, where
E
and
P
are defined as,

E=T^{2}~\frac{\partial\text{ln}~Z}{\partial T}\bigg\rvert_{V}~~~~~;\ ~~~~~~\\
P=VT~\frac{\partial\text{ln}~Z}{\partial V}\bigg\rvert_{T}

(8)

Using the approximation for Eq. (
8
) for homogeneous systems as,

P\approx T~\text{ln}~Z,

(9)

we can deduce an expression that relates the pressure to
\Delta
given by,

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta}{T^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{E}{T^{2}}-\frac{P}{T^{2}}

\displaystyle=T\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\Big(\frac{P}{T^{2}}\Big)

(10)

Integrating Eq.(
10
) gives,

\frac{P(T)}{T^{2}}-\frac{P(T_{0})}{T_{0}^{2}}=\int_{T_{0}}^{T}dT^{\prime}\frac{1}{T^{\prime~3}}\Delta(T^{\prime})

(11)

In principle, this relation will help us determine the EoS and the speed of sound. The range of temperatures (
t=T/\sqrt{\lambda}
and we set
\lambda=1
)
considered for the numerical integration would have to satisfy,
1/N<t<\alpha^{2}/c_{\text{gravity}}
with
t\ll 1
.

3
Lattice action

The lattice
\mathcal{N}=4
super Yang-Mills action based on topological twisting formulation can be written as a sum of
\mathcal{Q}
-exact and

\mathcal{Q}
-closed terms. The details (and, devils) can be found elsewhere
[
20
,
21
]
. For a
recent review, see
[
22
]
. The action is given by,

\displaystyle S_{\rm exact}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{4\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}}\sum_{\textbf{n}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigg[-\mathcal{\overline{F}}_{ab}(\textbf{n})\mathcal{F}_{ab}(\textbf{n})-\chi_{ab}(\textbf{n})\mathcal{D}_{[a}^{(+)}\psi_{b]}^{\ }(\textbf{n})-\eta(\textbf{n})\mathcal{\overline{D}}_{a}^{(-)}\psi_{a}(\textbf{n})

\displaystyle\hskip 230.46732pt+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{\overline{D}}_{a}^{(-)}\mathcal{U}_{a}(\textbf{n})\right)^{2}\bigg],

(12)

\displaystyle S_{\rm closed}

\displaystyle=-\frac{N}{16\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}}\sum_{\textbf{n}}{\rm Tr}\bigg[{\epsilon_{abcde}\ \chi_{de}(\textbf{n}+\hat{\mu}_{a}+\hat{\mu}_{b}+\hat{\mu}_{c})\mathcal{\overline{D}}_{c}^{(-)}\chi_{ab}(\textbf{n})}\bigg],

(13)

where
\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}
is the dimensionless ’t Hooft coupling and we sum over repeated indices.
Supersymmetric theories have flat directions which are
a problem for numerical simulations and we control this by adding a
\mathcal{Q}
breaking term to the lattice action (which we extrapolate to zero) as,

S_{\text{flat}}=\frac{N}{4\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}}\mu^{2}\sum_{\textbf{n},\ a\neq 3}{\rm Tr}{\bigg(\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a}(\textbf{n})\mathcal{U}_{a}(\textbf{n})-\mathbb{I}_{N}\bigg)^{2}}

(14)

Since we are interested in the dimensionally reduced
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM to two dimensions with the same number of supercharges
(known as
\mathcal{N}=(8,8)
SYM), we dimensionally
reduce along the two spatial directions. To have a meaningful dimensional reduction, we have observed that an extra term has to be added to the action given by,

S_{\text{center}}=\frac{N}{4\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}}\mu^{2}\sum_{\textbf{n},\ i=x,y}\text{ReTr}\bigg[\bigg(\varphi_{i}(\textbf{n})-\mathbb{I}_{N}\bigg)^{\dagger}\bigg(\varphi_{i}(\textbf{n})-\mathbb{I}_{N}\bigg)\bigg].

(15)

On the right we have
A_{2}^{*}
lattice where the three links are treated equally and expanded symmetrically to
target the continuum theory. On the left, by modifying the
third link and requiring that it is expanded around zero, we get square lattice. See
[
23
,
24
]
for details.

The lattice supersymmetric theories based on
\mathcal{Q}
-exact formulation are naturally adapted to non-orthogonal lattices.
For example,
\mathcal{N}=4
in four dimensions is formulated on
A_{4}^{*}
lattice
which has a bigger point group symmetry than the hypercubic lattice. However, we want to study the two-dimensional
theory on a square lattice. In
[
23
,
24
]
, it was argued that one can get different lattice geometries by the choice of the expansion point for the
fields in the moduli space (the trajectory one follows to the infinity) . We add an additional term,
S_{A_{2}^{*}\to\text{hyp.}}
given by,

S_{A_{2}^{*}\to\text{hyp.}}=\frac{N}{4\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}}\sigma^{2}\sum_{\textbf{n}}{\rm Tr}{\bigg(\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{3}(\textbf{n})\mathcal{U}_{3}(\textbf{n})\bigg)^{2}}

(16)

to the action which consists of the gauge links in the extra direction of the skewed geometry. The resulting lattice
is square [see Figure (
2
)] and we keep
\sigma=O(1)
fixed for all couplings/temperatures.
The complete lattice action reads,

S=S_{\mathrm{exact}}+S_{\mathrm{closed}}+S_{\text{flat}}+S_{\text{center}}+S_{A_{2}^{*}\to\text{hyp.}}

(17)

We are carrying out the numerical simulations using
17
on the parallel software
SUSY LATTICE

developed in
[
25
]
and will report the results in the future.

1.
Introduction

Let
H^{2}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
be the Hardy space on the open unit disk, and let
C_{\phi}f=f\circ\phi
be the bounded composition operator induced by an analytic self-map
\phi
of
\mathbb{D}
. For an inner function
\Theta
, the Beurling subspace
\Theta H^{2}
and the model space

K_{\Theta}=H^{2}\ominus\Theta H^{2}

are invariant under the unilateral shift
(Sf)(z)=zf(z)
for
f\in H^{2}
and its adjoint, respectively. We ask which of these spaces are also invariant under composition operators with symbols from a family of affine self-maps of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
defined by

(1.1)

\phi_{a}(z)=az+1-a,\qquad 0<a<1.

These maps satisfy
\phi_{a}\circ\phi_{b}=\phi_{ab}
and have a common attractive fixed point at
1
. Carmo and Noor
[
3
]
proved that
C_{\phi_{a}}
has universal translates in the sense of Rota
[
12
]
. Subtracting a scalar multiple of the identity does not change
the invariant subspaces. Consequently, for any fixed
a\in(0,1)
, the Invariant Subspace Problem (ISP) for complex separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces is equivalent to the assertion that every minimal nonzero invariant subspace of
C_{\phi_{a}}
is one-dimensional. Further related results were obtained by Carmo, Eidt and Noor
[
2
]
.

The quotient criterion of Bose, Muthukumar and Sarkar [1]
and the affine model-space criterion of Muthukumar and
Sarkar [11] provide the starting point for our classifications.
For every affine self-map
\varphi(z)=\alpha z+\beta
of

\mathbb{D}
with
0<|\alpha|<1
, we prove that the only
nonzero model spaces invariant under
C_{\varphi}
are

K_{z^{n}}=\operatorname{span}\{1,z,\ldots,z^{n-1}\},\qquad n\geq 1.

For
\varphi_{a}(z)=az+1-a
, with
0<a<1
, we show that the
singular measure of an inner function
\Theta
defining a

C_{\varphi_{a}}
-invariant Beurling subspace
\Theta H^{2}

must be concentrated at
1
, even when
\Theta
has zeros.
Together with the Blaschke multiplicity
criterion, this gives a complete classification.

The corresponding Cesàro classifications follow from
the semigroup correspondence of Gallardo-Gutiérrez and
Partington [7]; the model-space case was already established
by Gallardo-Gutiérrez, Partington and Ross [8].
Finally, Section 6 shows that a cyclic
C_{\varphi_{a}}
-invariant
subspace contains a nonzero function analytic across
1

precisely when it intersects a model space whose boundary
spectrum omits
1
nontrivially. We also construct an
infinite-dimensional cyclic subspace containing an
eigenvector but having trivial intersection with every
such model space.

.

Let
\phi(z)=\alpha z+\beta
be a self-map of

with

. For an inner function

(1)

;

(2)

\Theta(z)=\lambda z^{n}
for some

.

The atomic singular inner function with a single atom at

is

(1.2)

S_{c}(z)=\exp\left(-c\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right),\qquad c\geq 0,

with associated singular measure

For a nonzero analytic function
F
, let
m_{F}(w)
denote its zero multiplicity at
w
, with
m_{F}(w)=0
when
F(w)\neq 0
. Then our Beurling space classification is

Theorem B
.

Fix
a\in(0,1)
. For an inner function
\Theta
, the space
\Theta H^{2}
is
C_{\phi_{a}}
-invariant if and only if

(1.3)

\Theta=\lambda BS_{c},\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{T},\quad c\geq 0,

where

The multiplicity criterion for Blaschke products is due to Bose, Muthukumar and Sarkar
[
1
]
, building on earlier work of Cowen and Wahl

]
. The invariance of

for maps with boundary Denjoy–Wolff point

]

We then apply Theorems A and B to classify invariant model and Beurling subspaces for the

(\mathcal{C}f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}f_{k}\right)z^{n},\qquad f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}z^{k}

using a correspondence result established by Gallardo-Gutiérrez and Partington

: for any closed subspace

we have

.

Let
\Theta
be an inner function. Then

(1)

\Theta H^{2}
is
\mathcal{C}
-invariant if and only if
\Theta=\lambda z^{n}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
and

.

2.
Preliminaries

We use
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
and write
m
for normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
. An inner function is a bounded analytic function in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
with unimodular radial limits almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
. The canonical factorization of an inner function is

\Theta=\lambda BS_{\mu},\qquad S_{\mu}(z)=\exp\left(-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\xi+z}{\xi-z}\,d\mu(\xi)\right),

where
\mu
is a finite positive measure singular with respect to
m
, and the zeros of
B
, counted with multiplicity, satisfy the Blaschke condition. We allow
B=1
and
\mu=0
. For background on Hardy spaces and the Beurling and model subspaces we use
[
6
,
10
]
. The
boundary spectrum
of an inner function
\Theta
is defined by

(2.1)

\Sigma(\Theta)=\bigl(\overline{Z(\Theta)}\cap\mathbb{T}\bigr)\cup\supp\mu,

where
Z(\Theta)
denotes the zero-set of
\Theta
and its closure is taken in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. This is the set of boundary points across which
\Theta
does not extend analytically. Every function in
K_{\Theta}
extends analytically across
\mathbb{T}\setminus\Sigma(\Theta)
; see
[
6
, Lemma 14.27, Theorem 14.28]
.

Let
\psi
be a nonconstant analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
. The quotient criterion of Bose, Muthukumar and Sarkar
[
1
]
, together with the equivalent
H^{\infty}
formulation in
[
11
]
, is

(2.2)

C_{\psi}(\Theta H^{2})\subseteq\Theta H^{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{\Theta\circ\psi}{\Theta}\in H^{\infty}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{\Theta\circ\psi}{\Theta}\in\mathcal{S},

where
\mathcal{S}=\{q\in H^{\infty}:\|q\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}
is the
Schur
class. In particular, for a Blaschke product,

(2.3)

C_{\psi}(BH^{2})\subseteq BH^{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m_{B}(w)\leq m_{B\circ\psi}(w)\quad(w\in\mathbb{D}).

For affine
\phi(z)=\alpha z+\beta
,
\alpha\neq 0
, Muthukumar and Sarkar
[
11
, Theorem 4.3]
prove

(2.4)

C_{\phi}K_{\Theta}\subseteq K_{\Theta}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{\Theta\circ\sigma}{\Theta}\in H^{\infty},\qquad\sigma(z)=\frac{\overline{\alpha}z}{1-\overline{\beta}z}.

These established criteria will be used throughout this work.

3.
Invariant model spaces

Proof of Theorem
A
.

Suppose
K_{z^{n}}=\spanop\{1,z,\ldots,z^{n-1}\}
for
n\geq 1
. Composition with an affine map cannot increase polynomial degree, hence these spaces are trivially invariant under
C_{\alpha z+\beta}
. Conversely suppose
C_{\phi}K_{\Theta}\subseteq K_{\Theta}
. By the criterion of Muthukumar and Sarkar, there is a function
q\in H^{\infty}
such that

(3.1)

\Theta\circ\sigma=\Theta q,\qquad\sigma(z)=\frac{\overline{\alpha}z}{1-\overline{\beta}z}.

We first locate the zeros of
\Theta
. Since
\phi(z)=\alpha z+\beta
maps
\mathbb{D}
into itself,
|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 1
. For
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

|\sigma(z)|\leq\frac{|\alpha||z|}{1-|\beta||z|}\leq|z|\frac{|\alpha|}{1-|\beta|}\leq|z|.

Thus
\sigma
is a self-map of
\mathbb{D}
fixing
0
; it is injective because
\alpha\neq 0
. It satisfies
|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|=|\alpha|<1
. The strict form of Schwarz’s lemma gives

|\sigma(z)|<|z|\quad(0<|z|<1),\qquad\sigma^{\circ j}(z)\longrightarrow 0.

Indeed, the moduli of the iterates decrease; if their limit were positive, continuity would contradict the strict inequality on a compact circle about
0
. If
\Theta(w)=0
, identity (
3.1
) says that
\Theta(\sigma(w))=0
. Thus every forward iterate of
w
is a zero. When
w\neq 0
, these iterates remain nonzero because
\sigma
is injective and
\sigma(0)=0
. Their moduli strictly decrease, so they are distinct; they tend to the interior point
0
. The identity theorem would force
\Theta\equiv 0
, which an inner function cannot be. Hence
0
is the only possible zero. Factoring it with its multiplicity gives

\Theta=\lambda z^{n}S,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where
S
is a zero-free inner function. We now show that
S
is constant. Substitution into (
3.1
) gives

q(z)=\left(\frac{\overline{\alpha}}{1-\overline{\beta}z}\right)^{n}g(z),\qquad g(z):=\frac{S(\sigma(z))}{S(z)}.

The first factor and its reciprocal belong to
H^{\infty}
, so
g\in H^{\infty}
. At almost every boundary point, the radial limit of
S
has modulus one, while
|S(\sigma(z))|\leq 1
inside the disk. Taking radial limits in
S\circ\sigma=Sg
gives
|g^{*}|\leq 1
almost everywhere. The boundary norm theorem for
H^{\infty}
implies
\|g\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. Because
S
has no zeros and
\sigma(0)=0
, we also have
g(0)=S(0)/S(0)=1
. The maximum modulus principle forces
g\equiv 1
. It follows that
S\circ\sigma=S
. Iterating this identity and using
\sigma^{\circ j}(z)\to 0
gives
S(z)=S(0)
for every
z
. Since a constant inner function is unimodular, we absorb
S
into
\lambda
and obtain
\Theta=\lambda z^{n}
.

∎

The last part of this proof is the fixed-point argument used for zero-free inner functions in
[
1
]
. We note that the hypothesis
|\alpha|<1
is essential since the identity preserves every model space, and rotations can preserve additional model spaces.

4.
Invariant Beurling subspaces

Before proving Theorem B, we record a basic result about analytic continuation of an
H^{2}
function across an arc in
\mathbb{T}
and the vanishing of its singular measure there. This follows from Garnett’s analytic-continuation result

[
9
, Chapter II, Theorem 6.3]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
F=\Theta q\in H^{2}
be nonzero, where
\Theta
is inner
and
q\in H^{2}
. Let
\mu
be the singular measure determining
the singular inner factor of
\Theta
.
If
F
extends analytically across an open arc

I\subseteq\mathbb{T}
, then
\mu(I)=0
.

Proof.

Let
\nu_{F}
and
\nu_{q}
denote the positive singular measures
determining the singular inner factors of
F
and
q
,
respectively. Since
F=\Theta q\neq 0
, uniqueness of canonical
factorization gives

\nu_{F}=\mu+\nu_{q}.

Since
F
extends analytically across
I
,
by Garnett’s theorem [9, Chapter II,
Theorem 6.3] and its proof,
\nu_{F}(I)=0
. Positivity of the measures yields
\mu(I)=0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
B
.

We prove necessity first. Suppose
\Theta H^{2}
is invariant under
C_{\phi_{a}}
. The quotient criterion (
2.2
) provides a Schur function
q
with

(4.1)

F:=\Theta\circ\phi_{a}=\Theta q.

The function
F
is nonzero because
\phi_{a}
is non-constant and
\Theta
is nonzero. The image
\phi_{a}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
is the closed disk with center
1-a
and radius
a
, meeting
\mathbb{T}
only at
1
. Thus for each
\xi\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\{1\}
, the point
\phi_{a}(\xi)
lies in
\mathbb{D}
. In a sufficiently small neighborhood of
\xi
,
\phi_{a}
still takes values in
\mathbb{D}
. So the formula
F=\Theta\circ\phi_{a}
gives an analytic continuation of
F
across the boundary arc
I=\mathbb{T}\setminus\{1\}
. If
\mu
is the singular measure determining
\Theta
, then
\mu(I)=0
by Lemma
4.1
and hence
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subseteq\{1\}
. It follows that
\mu=c\delta_{1}
, for some constant
c\geq 0
. This proves
\Theta=\lambda BS_{c}
, with
B
its (possibly constant) Blaschke factor. It remains to prove the multiplicity condition on
B
. At any
w\in\mathbb{D}
, the identity
F=\Theta q
implies
m_{\Theta}(w)\leq m_{F}(w)
. The factors
S_{c}
and
\lambda
have no zeros. Moreover,
\phi_{a}^{\prime}(w)=a\neq 0
, so composition with
\phi_{a}
preserves the order of a zero at its image:

m_{B}(w)=m_{\Theta}(w)\leq m_{F}(w)=m_{\Theta}(\phi_{a}(w))=m_{B}(\phi_{a}(w)).

This proves (
1.4
), including when
B
has infinitely many zeros. For sufficiency, assume
\Theta=\lambda BS_{c}
and (
1.4
). Since
\phi_{a}^{\prime}
never vanishes, (
1.4
) is equivalent to
m_{B}(w)\leq m_{B\circ\phi_{a}}(w)
for every
w
. The Blaschke criterion (
2.3
), followed by (
2.2
), gives a Schur function

q_{B}=\frac{B\circ\phi_{a}}{B}.

The singular factor can be treated explicitly. Set
h(z)=(1+z)/(1-z)
, which has positive real part on
\mathbb{D}
. Since
1-\phi_{a}(z)=a(1-z)
, a calculation yields

h(\phi_{a}(z))=\frac{h(z)}{a}+\frac{1-a}{a}.

Therefore

(4.2)

q_{c}(z):=\frac{S_{c}(\phi_{a}(z))}{S_{c}(z)}=\exp\left(-\frac{c(1-a)}{a}\bigl(h(z)+1\bigr)\right).

The exponent has nonpositive real part, so
|q_{c}(z)|\leq 1
. Thus
q_{c}
is Schur, also when
c=0
. Multiplying the two factor identities gives
\Theta\circ\phi_{a}=\Theta(q_{B}q_{c}).

Since
q_{B}q_{c}
is a Schur function, (
2.2
) shows that
\Theta H^{2}
is invariant.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

If
\Theta
is nonconstant and
\Theta H^{2}
is
C_{\phi_{a}}
-invariant, then

\Sigma(\Theta)=\{1\}.

In particular, either
\Theta=\lambda S_{c}
with
c>0
, or its zero set is infinite and has
1
as its only boundary accumulation point. In the latter case, a factor
S_{c}
with
c\geq 0
is still allowed.

Proof.

Theorem
B
already places the singular measure at
1
. If distinct zeros
w_{j}
converged to
\xi\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\{1\}
, the distinct zeros
\phi_{a}(w_{j})
would converge to
\phi_{a}(\xi)\in\mathbb{D}
, contradicting the identity theorem. If a zero
w
exists, all points

(4.3)

\phi_{a}^{\circ n}(w)=1-a^{n}(1-w),\qquad n\geq 0,

are distinct zeros tending to
1
. If there are no zeros, nonconstancy forces
c>0
. Formula (
2.1
) now proves the assertion.
∎

5.
Common invariant subspaces and the Cesàro operator

Before we prove Theorem C, we establish a result that is of independent interest regarding the common Beurling invariant subspaces of
C_{\phi_{a}}
for
0<a<1
. For an inner function
\Theta
, define

A_{\Theta}=\{a\in(0,1):C_{\phi_{a}}(\Theta H^{2})\subseteq\Theta H^{2}\}.

This set is multiplicatively closed since
\phi_{a}\circ\phi_{b}=\phi_{ab}
.
The next result shows that either
A_{\Theta}=(0,1)
, or it has no accumulation points in
(0,1]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The set
A_{\Theta}
has an accumulation point in
(0,1]
if and only if
\Theta=\lambda S_{c}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
and
c\geq 0
. In this case
A_{\Theta}=(0,1)
. If
\Theta
has a zero, then
A_{\Theta}\cap[\varepsilon,1)
is finite
\forall

\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

Suppose distinct
a_{j}\in A_{\Theta}
tend to
a_{*}\in(0,1]
. If
\Theta(w)=0
for some
w\in\mathbb{D}
, the quotient criterion (
2.2
) makes

\phi_{a_{j}}(w)=1-a_{j}+a_{j}w

a zero of
\Theta
for every
j
. These points are distinct because
w\neq 1
, and they converge to
1-a_{*}+a_{*}w\in\mathbb{D}
. The identity theorem gives a contradiction. Thus
\Theta
has no zeros. Applying Theorem
B
to any one of the parameters shows that
\Theta=\lambda S_{c}
. Conversely, (
4.2
) is Schur for every
a\in(0,1)
, so such a
\Theta
has
A_{\Theta}=(0,1)
. Finally, if
\Theta
has a zero and
A_{\Theta}\cap[\varepsilon,1)
were infinite, compactness of
[\varepsilon,1]
would give an accumulation point in
(0,1]
, which we have just ruled out.
∎

Proof of Theorem
C
.

For part (1), set
M=\Theta H^{2}
in (
1.5
). Its orthogonal complement is
K_{\Theta}
, so
M
is
\mathcal{C}
-invariant exactly when
K_{\Theta}
is invariant under every
C_{\phi_{a}}
. By Theorem
A
, invariance under even one of these operators forces
\Theta=\lambda z^{n}
. Conversely, Theorem
A
also says that
K_{z^{n}}
is invariant under every
C_{\phi_{a}}
. The correspondence then gives
\mathcal{C}
-invariance of
z^{n}H^{2}
. For part (2), set
M=K_{\Theta}
in (
1.5
) and so
M^{\perp}=\Theta H^{2}
. Thus
K_{\Theta}
is
\mathcal{C}
-invariant exactly when
A_{\Theta}=(0,1)
and so Lemma

implies
\Theta=\lambda S_{c}
. Conversely, (
4.2
) and the quotient criterion make

invariant under every

, and (
1.5
) gives invariance of
K_{S_{c}}
. We next address the assertions about minimality and maximality. Given
0<d<c
, the identity
S_{c}=S_{d}S_{c-d}
yields a strict inclusion

\{0\}\subsetneq K_{S_{d}}\subsetneq K_{S_{c}}.

Both model spaces are
\mathcal{C}
-invariant by part (2), so
K_{S_{c}}
is not minimal. For any

, the same factorization gives
K_{S_{c}}\subsetneq K_{S_{e}}
; hence it is not maximal. Finally, part (1) shows that every invariant Beurling subspace

contains
z^{n+1}H^{2}
and hence none is minimal.
∎

6.
Cyclic subspaces intersecting
\Theta H^{2}
and
K_{\Theta}

Fix
0<a<1
. For
0\neq f\in H^{2}
, write

\mathcal{M}_{f}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{C_{\phi_{a}}^{\,j}f:j\geq 0\}

for its cyclic
C_{\phi_{a}}
-invariant subspace. It was shown by Carmo and Noor
[
3
]
that the ISP for complex separable Hilbert spaces has an
affirmative solution if and only if every minimal
\mathcal{M}_{f}
is
one-dimensional (equivalently
f
is a
C_{\phi_{a}}
-eigenvector). In this section we consider what happens when a Beurling or model subspace intersects some
\mathcal{M}_{f}
non-trivially.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\Theta
be a non-constant zero-free inner function such that

\Theta H^{2}
is
C_{\phi_{a}}
-invariant. If
\mathcal{M}_{f}\cap\Theta H^{2}\neq\{0\}
, then
\mathcal{M}_{f}
is not a minimal invariant subspace.

Proof.

By Theorem B,
\Theta=\lambda S_{c}
for some
c>0
. Since

\mathcal{M}_{f}\cap\Theta H^{2}\neq\{0\}
and both spaces are

C_{\phi_{a}}
-invariant, their intersection is a non-zero invariant
subspace of
\mathcal{M}_{f}
. Suppose on the contrary that
\mathcal{M}_{f}
is minimal. This gives

\mathcal{M}_{f}\subseteq S_{c}H^{2}
, and in particular
f\in S_{c}H^{2}
.
This contradicts a result of Carmo and Noor
[

, Theorem 5.5]
that no

generates a minimal

-invariant cyclic subspace.
∎

If

contains a nonzero function analytic at

, then it is minimal if and only if dim(

. This follows from

, since minimality implies

for every nonzero

Theorem 6.2
.

Fix
0<a<1
and

(1)

\mathcal{M}_{f}
contains a nonzero function analytic in a

;

(2)

the

-eigenvector

for some

;

(3)

\mathcal{M}_{f}\cap K_{\Theta}\neq\{0\}

\Theta
such that

.

Proof.

Suppose (1) holds. Choose a nonzero

. Let

be the order of its zero at

, and write

near

, where

. Since

\phi_{a}^{\circ k}(z)=1-a^{k}(1-z),

is defined on

and converges
uniformly there to
\gamma
. For such
k
,

a^{-kn}C_{\phi_{a}}^{\,k}g=(1-z)^{n}h\circ\phi_{a}^{\circ k}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left\|a^{-kn}C_{\phi_{a}}^{\,k}g-\gamma(1-z)^{n}\right\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1-z)^{n}\|_{H^{2}}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left|h\bigl(\phi_{a}^{\circ k}(z)\bigr)-\gamma\right|\longrightarrow 0.

Because
\mathcal{M}_{f}
is closed and
C_{\phi_{a}}
-invariant,

(1-z)^{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{f}
. This proves (2). If (2) holds, choose
\Theta(z)=z^{n+1}
. Then

(1-z)^{n}\in K_{\Theta}
, and
\Sigma(\Theta)=\varnothing
.
Hence (3) holds. Finally, suppose (3) holds and choose

0\neq g\in\mathcal{M}_{f}\cap K_{\Theta}
. Every function in
K_{\Theta}

extends analytically across boundary points outside

\Sigma(\Theta)
. Since
1\notin\Sigma(\Theta)
, the function
g
is analytic
near
1
. This proves (1).
∎

The next example demonstrates the existence of an infinite-dimensional
\mathcal{M}_{f}
which contains an eigenvector and yet
\mathcal{M}_{f}\cap K_{\Theta}=\{0\}
for all inner

\Theta
with
1\notin\Sigma(\Theta)
.

Example 6.3
.

Fix
0<a<1
and
c>0
, and set
g(z)=(1-z)^{2\pi i/\log a}
and
f=g+S_{c}
which belong to
H^{2}
. Moreover,

C_{\phi_{a}}g=g
and a direct calculation using (1.2) gives

C_{\phi_{a}}^{\,n}S_{c}=e^{-c(a^{-n}-1)}S_{c/a^{n}},\qquad\bigl\|C_{\phi_{a}}^{\,n}S_{c}\bigr\|_{H^{2}}=e^{-c(a^{-n}-1)}\longrightarrow 0.

Consequently
C_{\phi_{a}}^{\,n}f\to g
in
H^{2}
, so the eigenvector

g\in\mathcal{M}_{f}
and
S_{c}=f-g\in\mathcal{M}_{f}
. The space
\mathcal{M}_{f}
is infinite-dimensional since the iterates of
S_{c}
are linearly
independent. Indeed, along the radius these iterates are nonzero scalar multiples of
e^{-ca^{-n}t}
, where
t=(1+r)/(1-r)
, and exponential functions with distinct rates are linearly independent. We show that
\mathcal{M}_{f}
contains no nonzero function analytic
at
1
. To see this, since
S_{c}H^{2}
is
C_{\phi_{a}}
-invariant, every

F\in\mathcal{M}_{f}\subset\mathbb{C}g+S_{c}H^{2}
can be written as
F=\lambda g+S_{c}h
for
h\in H^{2}
(notice that the inclusion holds because the sum of a finite dimensional subspace with a closed subspace remains closed). From the
H^{2}
point-evaluation estimate we obtain

|S_{c}(r)h(r)|\leq\frac{\|h\|_{H^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\,\exp\!\left(-c\,\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right),\qquad 0<r<1

and in particular that

(6.1)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\to 1^{-}}|S_{c}(r)h(r)|=0.

On the other hand

g(1-a^{n})=1,\qquad g(1-a^{n+1/2})=-1,

which implies that
\lim\limits_{r\to 1^{-}}g(r)
does not exist. Suppose some
0\neq F\in\mathcal{M}_{f}
extends analytically
across
1
. Since
S_{c}h(r)\to 0
, the function

\lambda g(r)=F(r)-S_{c}h(r)
has a finite radial limit.
The two displayed sequences force
\lambda=0
.
Thus
F=S_{c}h\neq 0
extends analytically across
1
,
contradicting Lemma 4.1 because the singular measure
of
S_{c}
assigns mass
c>0
to
1
. This implies that
F=S_{c}h
is analytic at
1
which contradicts Lemma
4.1
. Therefore
\mathcal{M}_{f}
contains no nonzero function analytic at
1
and hence
\mathcal{M}_{f}\cap K_{\Theta}=\{0\}
for all inner
\Theta
with
1\notin\Sigma(\Theta)
by Theorem
6.2
.

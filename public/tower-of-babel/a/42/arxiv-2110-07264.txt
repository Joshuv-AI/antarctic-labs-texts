An upper bound on the dimension of the Rauzy gasket
Mark Pollicott, Benedict Sewell

Abstract




1
Introduction

The Rauzy Gasket
\mathcal{G}
is a compact subset of the standard 2-simplex,

\Delta=\{(x,y,z)\;:\;x,y,z\geq 0,\ x+y+z=1\}.

It plays the role of an exceptional set in the theory of interval exchange transformations and other settings, and is the limit set of the iterated function scheme for the three weak projectivised linear maps
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}:\Delta\to\Delta
, defined by

\displaystyle T_{1}(x,y,z)=\left(\frac{1}{2-x},\frac{y}{2-x},\frac{z}{2-x}\right),

\displaystyle T_{2}(x,y,z)=\left(\frac{x}{2-y},\frac{1}{2-y},\frac{z}{2-y}\right),

\displaystyle T_{3}(x,y,z)=\left(\frac{x}{2-z},\frac{y}{2-z},\frac{1}{2-z}\right);

i.e.,
\mathcal{G}
is the smallest non-trivial closed set such that
\mathcal{G}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{3}T_{j}(\mathcal{G})
.

Figure 1:
The Rauzy gasket

The gasket has an interesting history, appearing for the first time in 1991 in the work of Arnoux and Rauzy
[
1
]
, in the context of interval exchange transformations, where it was conjectured that
\Leb(\mathcal{G})=0
.
The gasket was rediscovered by Levitt in 1993
[
13
]
, in a paper which also included a proof (due to Yoccoz) that
\Leb(\mathcal{G})=0
.
The gasket
\mathcal{G}
emerged for a third time in the work of De Leo and Dynnikov
[
5
]
, this time in the context of Novikov’s theory of magnetic induction on monocrystals (see
[
6
]
for the dichotomy between this and
[
1
]
). They gave an alternative proof that
\Leb(\mathcal{G})=0
and proposed the stronger result
\dim_{H}(\mathcal{G})<2
.
Novikov and Maltsev
[
14
]
also conjectured the stronger bound
\dim_{H}(\mathcal{G})<2
, which was rigorously established by Avila, Hubert and Skripchenko
[
4
]
.
Empirical estimates in
[
5
]
suggest
\dim_{H}(\mathcal{G})\approx 1.72
, and a lower bound was shown in
[
11
]
. Lastly, Fougeron used semiflows and thermodynamic techniques to show
\dim_{H}(\mathcal{G})<1.825

[
9
]
. Using completely elementary methods, we show the following improved upper bound.

Theorem 1.1
.

\dim_{H}(\mathcal{G})\leq 1.7415
.

The Rauzy Gasket has a number of interesting recent applications. Gamburd, Magee and Ronan
[
10
]
showed asymptotic estimates for integer solutions of the Markov-Hurwitz equations featuring
\dim_{H}(\mathcal{G})
.
Hubert and Paris-Romaskevich in
[
12
]
considered triangular tiling billiards, modelling refraction in crystals. The gasket
\mathcal{G}
parameterises triangles admitting trajectories which escape non-linearly to infinity and closed orbits which approximate fractal-like sets.

In section 2 we give the technical result which leads to the bound in Theorem
1.1
. This is formulated in terms of certain infinite matrices.
In section 3 we give elementary preliminary bounds on the area and diameter of small triangles given as the images of
\Delta
under compositions of the maps
T_{1}
,
T_{2}
and
T_{3}
.
In section 4 we use these to obtain a bound for the dimension, provided an associated sequence of real numbers
X_{n}
converges to zero.
In sections 5 and 6 we present the core of the proof. In section 5, we use the estimates from section 3 to bound
X_{n}
in terms of expressions satisfying an iterative relation.
In section 6 we use the renewal theorem to deduce that
X_{n}\to 0
under the hypotheses of Theorem
2.8
. Finally, in section 7 we apply Theorem
2.8
empirically to deduce the bound in Theorem
1.1
.

A fuller account appears in
[
15
]
.

A formal statement

The bound in Theorem
1.1
is a special case of a decreasing sequence of upper bounds, indexed by a parameter
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}

(An index set)

3
Triangle estimates

In this section we collect together elementary but useful estimates for the triangles
\Delta_{\underline{i}}
.

Lemma 3.1

(Area estimate)
.

If
\underline{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in\{1,2,3\}^{n}
and
j\in\{1,2,3\}

then, writing
j\underline{i}=(j,i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
, we have that

\frac{\area(\Delta_{j\underline{i}})}{\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}})}\leq\max_{x\in\Delta_{\underline{i}}}(2-x_{j})^{-3}.

Proof.

By a change of variables,

\area(\Delta_{j\underline{i}})=\area(T_{j}\Delta_{\underline{i}})=\int_{\Delta_{\underline{i}}}\Jac T_{j}(x)\;\mathrm{d}x\leq\max_{x\in\Delta_{\underline{i}}}\big(\Jac{T}_{j}(x)\big)\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}}).

To complete the proof, we now show that
\Jac T_{j}(x)=(2-x_{j})^{-3}
. If
j=1
, with respect to the orthogonal basis

(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}),\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(2\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}))
,
the derivative map
DT_{j}(x)
takes the following form:

DT_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{(2-x_{1})^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(x_{2}-x_{3})&2-x_{1}\\
\end{array}\right).

(1)

Thus,
\Jac T_{1}(x)=(2-x_{1})^{-3}
. The other cases (
j\neq 1
) follow by symmetry.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In fact, one can deduce a simple formula for
\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}})
from the precise form of the above jacobean, using matrix products (see
[
15
]
).

We can apply a similar reasoning to the associated diameters.

Lemma 3.3

(Diameter estimate)
.

If
\underline{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in\{1,2,3\}^{n}
and
j\in\{1,2,3\}
then

\frac{\diam(\Delta_{j\underline{i}})}{\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})}\leq\max_{x\in\Delta_{\underline{i}}}(2-x_{j})^{-\lambda}.

Remark 3.4
.

The constant
\lambda
used as the
exponent in Lemma
3.3
was suggested by noticing empirically that the contours of the operator norm
\|DT_{1}(x)\|
of
DT_{1}(x)
were very similar to those of its main eigenvalue,
(2-x_{1})^{-1}
. Hence it was natural to consider the ratio

\frac{\log\|DT_{1}(x)\|}{\log(2-x_{1})},

whose supremal value is obtained in the limit as
x\to(1,0,0)
, and may be computed using L’Hôpital’s rule.

Proof.

Consider the operator norm,
\|DT_{j}(x)\|
, of the derivatives
T_{j}(x)
(i.e., with respect to the ambient metric in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
). Considering
j=1
, the matrix in (
1
) satisfies

\big\|DT_{1}(x)\big\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2b^{2}}\left({1+a+b+\sqrt{(1+a+b)^{2}-4b}}\right),

where
a=\frac{1}{3}(x_{2}-x_{3})^{2}
and
b=(2-x_{1})^{2}
. Using the simple bound
a\leq\frac{1}{3}(1-x_{1})^{2}
, one has that, for all
x\in\Delta
,

\big\|DT_{1}(x)\big\|^{2}\leq f(x_{1}):=\frac{2x_{1}^{2}-7x_{1}+8+2(1-x_{1})\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-5x_{1}+7}}{3(2-x_{1})^{4}}.

We claim that
g:t\mapsto f(t)(2-t)^{2\lambda}
is increasing on
[0,1]
, and hence has maximum
g(1)=1
(we defer the proof of this statement to the appendix), i.e.,
\|DT_{1}(x)\|\leq\sqrt{f(x_{1})}\leq(2-x_{1})^{-\lambda}
. The result for
j=1
follows simply from the mean value theorem and for
j\neq 1
by symmetry.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

In the context of higher-dimensional Rauzy gaskets (see
[
2
]
for the definition), an effective version of Lemma
3.3
certainly applies, since the derivative maps there have a very similar structure to that observed above.

In view of the previous lemmas, it is useful to have more explicit bounds involving
(2-x_{j})^{-1}
depending on where the point
x\in\Delta
approximately lies. We henceforth denote

A_{n,k}=\{\underline{i}\in\{1,2,3\}^{n}\;:\;i_{1}=\cdots=i_{k}\neq i_{k+1}\}\qquad(1\leq k<n)

and work with the following simple bounds.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
\underline{i}\in A_{n,k}
and
i_{1}=i,j,l\in\{1,2,3\}
are distinct
then, for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\max_{x\in\Delta_{\underline{i}}}(2-x_{i})^{-1}\leq\frac{k+2}{k+3},

\displaystyle\phantom{\qquad\hbox{and}}\max_{x\in\Delta_{\underline{i}}}(2-x_{j})^{-1}\leq\frac{k+1}{2k+1},\qquad\hbox{and}

\displaystyle\max_{x\in\Delta_{\underline{i}}}\left\{(2-x_{j})^{-3\delta}+(2-x_{l})^{-3\delta}\right\}\leq\left(\frac{k+1}{2k+1}\right)^{3\delta}+\frac{1}{8^{\delta}}.

Proof.

By symmetry, it suffices to consider
\underline{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
with
i_{1}=\cdots=i_{k}=1
and
i_{k+1}\neq 1
. Then
\Delta_{\underline{i}}
is contained in

\text{cl}\big(T_{1}^{k}(\Delta)\setminus T_{1}^{k+1}(\Delta)\big)=\bigg\{x\in\Delta\;:\;\frac{k}{k+1}\leq x_{1}\leq\frac{k+1}{k+2}\bigg\}

(2)

(cl denoting the topological closure), where we have used that
T_{1}^{k}(\Delta)=\{x\in\Delta\,:\,x_{1}\geq\frac{k}{k+1}\}
. The first two bounds follow directly:

(2-x_{1})^{-1}\leq\left(2-\frac{k+1}{k+2}\right)^{-1}=\frac{k+2}{k+3}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(2-x_{j})^{-1}\leq\left(2-\frac{1}{k+1}\right)^{-1}=\frac{k+1}{2k+1}.

For the third bound, we observe that the function
f(x):=(2-x_{2})^{-3\delta}+(2-x_{3})^{-3\delta}
is convex on
\Delta
and thus takes its maximum on
T_{1}^{k}(\Delta)
at one of its vertices:

\max_{\Delta_{\underline{i}}}f\leq\max_{T_{1}^{k}(\Delta)}f=\max\left\{f\circ T_{1}^{k}(1,0,0),f\circ T_{1}^{k}(0,1,0),f\circ T_{1}^{k}(0,0,1)\right\}.

More explicitly, noting that
f\circ T_{1}^{k}(1,0,0)=f(1,0,0)\leq f\circ T_{1}^{k}(0,1,0)=f\left(\frac{k}{k+1},\frac{1}{k+1},0\right)=f\left(\frac{k}{k+1},0,\frac{1}{k+1}\right)=f\circ T_{1}^{k}(0,0,1)
, one has

\max_{\Delta_{\underline{i}}}f\leq\left(2-\frac{1}{k+1}\right)^{-3\delta}+2^{-3\delta}=\left(\frac{k+1}{2k+1}\right)^{3\delta}+\frac{1}{8^{\delta}},

as required.
∎

4
Cover estimates

The upper bound on the dimension in Theorem
2.8
is based on finding a value
\delta\in(0,1)
so that Lemma 4.1 below applies.

Its proof is simple and based on a simple sequence of open covers of
\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{U}_{n}

\{\Delta_{\underline{i}}\hbox{ : }|\underline{i}|=n\}

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
\delta>0

X_{n}:=\sum_{|\underline{i}|=n}\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{\delta}\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{1-\delta}\to 0\qquad\hbox{as}\qquad n\to\infty.

Then
\dim_{H}(\mathcal{G})\leq 1+\delta.

\displaystyle\frac{2\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}})}{{\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})}}
\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})
a
b
\Delta_{\underline{i}}

Figure 2:
Left: covering

by a rectangle. Right: covering a rectangle by open disks.

Proof.

As illustrated in Figure 2, each triangle

is contained in a rectangle with side lengths

and
a=2\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}})/\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})
, which can in turn be covered by

\Delta_{\underline{i}}
can be covered by
\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{2}/\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}})

Denoting by

the union of the disks covering all of the triangles

\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}_{n}}\diam(U)^{1+\delta}\leq 4^{1+\delta}\sum_{|\underline{i}|=n}\left(\frac{\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{2}}{\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}})}\right)\left(\frac{\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}})}{\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})}\right)^{1+\delta}=4^{1+\delta}X_{n}.

The result follows from the standard definition of the Hausdorff dimension (see, e.g.,
[

]
).

5
Inductive bounds on
X_{n,\underline{v}}
and
X_{n,k}

We now turn to the problem of showing that
X_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, which will enable us to apply Lemma
4.1
.
Our method, broadly speaking, is based on obtaining inductive bounds bounding

1.

X_{n+1,k+1}
in terms of
X_{n,k}
, and

2.

X_{n+1,\underline{v}}
in terms of
X_{n,\underline{w}}
and
X_{n,k}
.

These will prove useful in applying the renewal theorem in the next section.

5.1
Bounds of terms indexed by numbers

The next simple lemma is independent of
m
, and features the constants
b_{k}
.

Lemma 5.1

(Number Lemma)
.

Suppose
\delta\in(0,1)
. Then, for all
n>k
,

\displaystyle X_{n+1,k+1}\leq b_{k}X_{n,k}.

Proof.

Since
j\underline{i}\in A_{n+1,k+1}
if and only if
\underline{i}\in A_{n,k}
and
j=i_{1}
, we may write

\displaystyle X_{n+1,k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underline{i}\in A_{n,k}}\area(\Delta_{i_{1}\underline{i}})^{\delta}\diam(\Delta_{i_{1}\underline{i}})^{1-\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\underline{i}\in A_{n,k}}\left(\frac{k+2}{k+3}\right)^{3\delta}\left(\frac{k+2}{k+3}\right)^{\lambda(1-\delta)}\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{\delta}\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{1-\delta}

\displaystyle=b_{k}X_{n,k},

where we have applied Lemmas
3.1
and
3.3
.
∎

5.2
Bounds of terms indexed by words

We can bound
X_{n+1,\underline{v}}
,
n>m,
with
\underline{v}\in\mathcal{V}\cup\{m\}
, using the matrix
B
and the constants
a_{k}
, as formulated in the next lemma.

Lemma 5.2

(Word Lemma)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
,
n>m
and
\underline{v}\in\mathcal{V}\cup\{m\}
. Then, recalling
\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}=(1,{2^{m}})
, there exists a positive sequence
c_{n}
such that
\sum_{n}c_{n}<\infty
and

\displaystyle X_{n+1,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}+\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}a_{k}X_{n,k}\qquad\hbox{and}

\displaystyle X_{n+1,\underline{v}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\underline{w}\in\mathcal{V}}B_{\underline{v},\underline{w}}X_{n,\underline{w}}\qquad\hbox{if}\ \underline{v}\neq\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}.

Proof.

For the first inequality, note that
j\underline{i}\in A_{n+1,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}
if and only if
j\neq i_{1}
and
\underline{i}\in\bigcup_{k=m}^{n-1}A_{n,k}\cup\{(1^{n}),(2^{n}),(3^{n})\}
. Therefore, applying Lemmas
3.3
and
3.1
in turn and using symmetry, one has that

\displaystyle X_{n+1,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}

\displaystyle=6\area(\Delta_{(2,1^{n})})^{\delta}\diam(\Delta_{(2,1^{n})})^{1-\delta}+\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}\sum_{\underline{i}\in A_{n,k}}\sum_{j\neq i_{1}}\area(\Delta_{j\underline{i}})^{\delta}\diam(\Delta_{j\underline{i}})^{1-\delta}

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}+\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}\sum_{\underline{i}\in A_{n,k}}\left(\frac{k+1}{2k+1}\right)^{\lambda(1-\delta)}\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{1-\delta}\sum_{j\neq i_{1}}\area(\Delta_{j\underline{i}})^{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}+\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}\sum_{\underline{i}\in A_{n,k}}\left(\frac{k+1}{2k+1}\right)^{\lambda(1-\delta)}\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{1-\delta}\left(\left(\frac{k+1}{2k+1}\right)^{3\delta}+\frac{1}{8^{\delta}}\right)\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{\delta}

\displaystyle=c_{n}+\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}a_{k}X_{n,k},

where
c_{n}:=6\area(\Delta_{(2,1^{n})})^{\delta}\diam(\Delta_{(2,1^{n})})^{1-\delta}
, as required.

Regarding the second inequality: The combinatorics above and in the previous proof show that
\underline{i}\in\bigcup_{k=m}^{n-1}A_{n,k}\cup\{(1^{n}),(2^{n}),(3^{n})\}
implies
j\underline{i}\not\in A_{n+1,\underline{v}}
for every
\underline{v}\in\mathcal{V}\cup\{m\}\setminus\{\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}\}
. Fixing such a
\underline{v}
and considering the contrapositive, one sees that
j\underline{i}\in A_{n+1,\underline{v}}
implies that
\underline{i}\in A_{n,\underline{w}}
and
\underline{w}\mapsto_{j^{\prime}}\underline{v}
for some
\underline{w},j^{\prime}
. Assuming
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m+1})=\underline{w}
for simplicity, we have that
j^{\prime}=j
and
\Delta_{\underline{i}}\subset\Delta_{\underline{w}}
. Hence, by Lemmas
3.1
and
3.3
,

\frac{\area(\Delta_{j\underline{i}})^{\delta}\diam(\Delta_{j\underline{i}})^{1-\delta}}{\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{\delta}\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{1-\delta}}\leq\max_{\underline{x}\in\Delta_{\underline{w}}}(2-x_{j})^{-3\delta-\lambda(1-\delta)}=:B_{\underline{v},\underline{w}}.

Thus, we may write the following.

\displaystyle X_{n+1,\underline{v}}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{w}\in\mathcal{V}:\\[2.0pt]
\underline{w}\mapsto_{j}\underline{v}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\underline{i}\in A_{n,\underline{w}}}\area(\Delta_{j\underline{i}})^{\delta}\diam(\Delta_{j\underline{i}})^{1-\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{w}\in\mathcal{V}:\\
B_{\underline{v},\underline{w}}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\underline{i}\in A_{n,\underline{w}}}B_{\underline{v},\underline{w}}\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{\delta}\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{1-\delta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underline{w}\in\mathcal{V}}B_{\underline{v},\underline{w}}X_{n,\underline{w}},

as required.

Finally, a direct computation of the vertices of
\Delta_{(2,1^{n})}=T_{2}T_{1}^{n}(\Delta)
shows that its diameter and area are proportional to
n^{-1}
and
n^{-2}
respectively, as
n\to\infty
. Hence
c_{n}
is proportional to
n^{-1-\delta}
as
n\to\infty
, and is thus summable for every
\delta>0
, completing the proof.
∎

6
Renewal Theorem

We now use the iterative bounds of the last section to show that
X_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, under the hypotheses of Theorem
2.8
. This uses the following mild adaptation of the classical renewal theorem of Feller
[
8
, p.330]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose that the sequences
(Y_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
,

(\mu_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
(\nu_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
are non-negative and satisfy
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu_{n}<1
,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\nu_{n}<\infty
and

Y_{n}\leq\nu_{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu_{k}Y_{n-k}

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
Y_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

We apply this theorem with
Y_{n}=X_{n+m+1,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}
to give Theorem
2.8
, which can be rewritten as follows.

Theorem 6.2
.

If
\delta>\frac{1-\lambda}{3-\lambda}=0.037\ldots
satisfies

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(B^{k})_{m,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\cdot\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}a_{k}\prod_{i=m}^{k-1}b_{i}\leq 1,

(3)

then
\dim_{H}(\mathcal{G})\leq 1+\delta
.

Proof.

Assume that (
3
) holds with a strict inequality (the case of equality follows in the limit, since the LHS is decreasing in
\delta
), and that
\delta<1
(the conclusion being otherwise trivial). Applying Lemma
5.1
repeatedly in the first estimate of Lemma
5.2
gives

X_{n+1,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}\leq c_{n}+\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}a_{k}\prod_{i=m}^{k-1}b_{i}X_{n+m-k,m}.

Moreover, the second estimate of Lemma
5.2
extends inductively to give, for any
k\in\mathbb{N},

X_{n+m+k,m}\leq\sum^{n+m-1}_{j=1}(B^{j})_{m,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}X_{n+m,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{v}\in\mathcal{V}:\\
\underline{v}\neq\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}\end{subarray}}(B^{n+k-1})_{m,\underline{v}}X_{m+1,\underline{v}}.

Putting these two together gives the renewal-style inequality

X_{n+1,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}\leq\nu_{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-m}\mu_{k}X_{n+1-k,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}},

with coefficients

\mu_{k}=\sum_{i+j=k}(B^{i})_{m,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}a_{m+j}\prod_{l=m}^{m+j-1}b_{l}

and remainder term

\nu_{n}=c_{n}+\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}a_{k}\prod_{i=m}^{k-1}b_{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\cdot\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sum_{\underline{v}\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\{\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}\}}(B^{n-k-1})_{m,\underline{v}}X_{m+1,\underline{v}}.

Consider the other hypotheses of the renewal theorem. The hypothesis on the coefficients is precisely our assumption that strengthens (
3
):

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(B^{k})_{m,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\cdot\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}a_{k}\prod_{i=m}^{k-1}b_{i}<1.

Regarding the summability of the remainder terms
\nu_{n}
: By elementary combinatorics,
B^{m+1}
has only one zero row and zero column, so this inequality also ensures that the spectral radius
\rho=\rho(B)<1
. In any case, by the Perron-Frobenius theorem there exists
C>0
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\nu_{n}-c_{n}\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}a_{k}\prod_{i=m}^{k-1}b_{i}\rho^{n-k}=C\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}a_{k}\prod_{i=m}^{k-1}b_{i}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho^{n}.

Thus, since
\rho\in(0,1)
and

a_{k}\prod_{i=m}^{k-1}b_{i}=\mathcal{O}\big(k^{-3\delta-\lambda(1-\delta)}\big)\qquad(k\to\infty),

we see that the right hand side is summable if
\delta>\frac{1-\lambda}{3-\lambda}
. That is,
\sum_{n}\nu_{n}<\infty
, and the renewal theorem gives
X_{n,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Finally, we use this to bound
X_{n}
via the following lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

Given
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exists
C>0
such that
X_{n}\leq CX_{n+m+1,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}
.

Proof.

For each
\underline{i}\in\{1,2,3\}^{n}
, the word
\underline{i}^{\prime}=(1,2^{m},i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in A_{n+m+1,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}
. This word satisfies

\frac{\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}^{\prime}})^{\delta}\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}^{\prime}})^{1-\delta}}{\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{\delta}\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{1-\delta}}=\frac{\area(T_{1}T_{2}^{m}\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{\delta}\diam(T_{1}T_{2}^{m}\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{1-\delta}}{\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{\delta}\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}})^{1-\delta}}\geq K^{(1+\delta)(m+1)},

where
K
is obtained by bounding the
minimum
singular value of
DT_{j}(x)
uniformly over
x\in\Delta
: namely, applying the estimates of section 3,

\big\|D(T_{j}^{-1})\big(T_{j}(x)\big)\big\|=\frac{\Jac T_{j}(x)}{\|DT_{j}(x)\|}\geq(2-x_{j})^{\lambda-3}\geq 2^{\lambda-3}=:K.

In particular, with
C=K^{(1+\delta)(m+1)}

X_{n+m+1,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}\geq\sum_{|\underline{i}|=n}\area(\Delta_{\underline{i}^{\prime}})^{\delta}\diam(\Delta_{\underline{i}^{\prime}})^{1-\delta}\geq CX_{n},

as required.
∎

Thus,
X_{n,\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\displaystyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\displaystyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\textstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\textstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptstyle\star$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.77778}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\star$\cr}}}}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
implies that
X_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, and applying Lemma
4.1
completes the proof of Theorem
2.8
.
∎

as a ratio of two determinants (via Cramer’s Rule); and the second factor,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}a_{k}\prod_{i=m}^{k-1}b_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\left[\left(\frac{k+1}{2k+1}\right)^{3\delta+\lambda(1-\delta)}+\frac{1}{8^{\delta}}\left(\frac{k+1}{2k+1}\right)^{\lambda(1-\delta)}\right]\prod_{i=m}^{k-1}\left(\frac{i+2}{i+3}\right)^{3\delta+\lambda(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\left(\frac{(k+1)(m+2)}{(2k+1)(k+2)}\right)^{3\delta+\lambda(1-\delta)}+\frac{1}{8^{\delta}}\left(\frac{(k+1)(m+2)}{(2k+1)(k+2)}\right)^{\lambda(1-\delta)}(k+2)^{-3\delta},

using the inbuilt
NSum
function. For the precise code, explanation and a breakdown of the confirmation, see
[
16
]
and the documentation therein. In particular, we rely on the closed-source Wolfram Mathematica programme (with its inbuilt error bounds) to accurately

•

calculate the determinant of large matrices, and

•

estimate the polynomially decaying sum.

Both calculations can be rigorised (see
[
16
]
for details) and we encourage all efforts to do so. However, any changes we observed were too insignificant to affect the final values given here: accuracy to three decimal places in each factor appears to suffice.

That
\delta_{m}
is computed only up to
m=9
owes to the exponential size of
B
, which places a commensurable computing burden on the hardware. We note that slightly larger choices of
m
should be possible with more efficient compiled programming languages.

Remark 7.1
.

The elementary nature of this method suggests a similar approach might be applied to other related examples of sets to give bounds on their dimension; though likely with different limiting returns.

Appendix A
Proof that
g
is increasing.

Here, we give an elementary but delicate proof of the following technical fact, used in the proof of Lemma
3.3
:

Lemma A.1
.

The function
g:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
,

g(t):=f(t)(2-t)^{2\lambda}=\frac{1}{3}(2-t)^{-1-2/\sqrt{3}}\left(2t^{2}-7t+8+2(1-t)\sqrt{t^{2}-5t+7}\right)

is increasing. In particular,
g(t)\leq g(1)=1
for all
t
.

Proof.

We simply show that
g^{\prime}>0
on
(0,1)
. Writing
\alpha(t)=\sqrt{t^{2}-5t+7}>0
, we have

g^{\prime}(t)=\frac{h(t)}{\alpha(t)}\cdot\frac{1}{9}\cdot(2-t)^{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-2-2/\sqrt{3}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}},

where
h(t)\in\mathbb{Q}\left[t,\alpha(t),\sqrt{3}\right]
is given by

h(t):=\left(6-4\sqrt{3}\right)t^{3}+\left(\left(4\sqrt{3}-6\right)\alpha(t)+24\sqrt{3}-39\right)t^{2}\\
+\left(\left(24-14\sqrt{3}\right)\alpha(t)-48\sqrt{3}+87\right)t+\left(16\sqrt{3}-18\right)\alpha(t)+28\sqrt{3}-72.

It remains to show
h
is positive, using Taylor approximants for
\alpha
based at 1. Namely, since

\alpha^{(3)}(t)=\frac{9}{8}\frac{5-2t}{\alpha(t)^{5}},\qquad\alpha^{(4)}(t)=\frac{9}{16}\frac{16t^{2}-80t+97}{\alpha(t)^{7}}

are positive for all
t\in(0,1)
, the remainder term given by Taylor’s Theorem for the degree two and degree three Taylor polynomials are negative and positive, respectively, hence these polynomials are upper and lower bounds, respectively, for
\alpha
. More explicitly, for
t\in[0,1]
,

\frac{t^{3}-t^{2}-25t+73}{16\sqrt{3}}\leq\alpha(t)\leq\frac{t^{2}-14t+37}{8\sqrt{3}}.

Applying these bounds everywhere in the definition of
h
(according to the signs of the coefficients) reduces to the concrete polynomial bound

\displaystyle h(t)

\displaystyle\geq\left(5\sqrt{3}+8+\left(2-\sqrt{3}\right)t^{2}(t+1)-3\left(\sqrt{3}+2\right)t\right)(1-t)^{2}.

It is easy to see that the right hand side is everywhere positive, hence so too are
h
and
g^{\prime}
.
∎

The equidistribution of Elliptic Dedekind sums and generalized Selberg-Kloosterman sums
Kim Klinger-Logan, Tian An Wong

Abstract
. We show that the values of elliptic Dedekind sums, after normalization, are equidistributed mod 1. The key ingredient is a non-trivial bound on generalized Selberg-Kloosterman sums for discrete subgroups of PSL 2 ​ ( ℂ ) \mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}) using Poincaré series.

11F20 (primary), 11M36 (secondary).

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The distribution of elliptic Dedekind sums

Let
c,d
be relatively prime integers. The classical Dedekind sum is given by

s(c,d)=\sum_{n=1}^{c}((n/c))((nd/c))

where
((x)):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{x\}-\frac{1}{2}&\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}-\mathbb{Z},\\
0&\text{for }x\in\mathbb{Z}\end{array}\right.
and
\{x\}
is the fractional part of
x
. Following conjectures of Grosswald and Rademacher
[
9
]
, Hickerson showed that the image of
s(c,d)
is dense in
\mathbb{R}

[
6
]
, and Vardi later showed that classical Dedekind sums are furthermore equidistributed mod 1
[
12
]
. Viewing
s(c,d)
as a function on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, a generalization of this result to non-cocompact lattices in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
was also recently proven by Burrin
[
2
]
.

Elliptic Dedekind sums are analogues of classical Dedekind sums for imaginary quadratic fields. The goal of this paper is to study the value distribution of elliptic Dedekind sums. To further describe our results and motivation, we introduce some basic notation. Let
L=\omega_{1}\mathbb{Z}+\omega_{2}\mathbb{Z}
be a lattice in
\mathbb{C}
with
\text{Im}(\omega_{1}/\omega_{2})>0
and let
\mathcal{O}_{L}:=\{m\in\mathbb{C}\penalty\ |\penalty\ mL\subset L\}
be its ring of multipliers.
For
x\in\mathbb{C}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, define the Eisenstein-Kronecker series

E_{k}(x):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in L\\
w+x\neq 0\end{subarray}}(w+x)^{-k}|w+x|^{-s}\Big|_{s=0},

where the value at
s=0
is understood in the sense of analytic continuation.
Using these, Sczech
[
11
]
defines the elliptic Dedekind sum, for
c,d\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
and
c\neq 0
,

{D}(c,d):=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{r\in L/cL}E_{1}\left(\frac{rd}{c}\right)E_{1}\left(\frac{r}{c}\right),

and an additive homomorphism
\Phi:\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})\to\mathbb{C}
given by

(1.1)

\Phi\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}E_{2}(0)I\left(\frac{a+d}{c}\right)-{D}(c,d)&c\neq 0\\
E_{2}(0)I\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)&c=0\end{array}\right.

where
I(z):=z-\bar{z}=2i\text{Im}(z)
and
i=\sqrt{-1}
.
Note that
E_{2}(0)
vanishes if only if
\mathcal{O}_{L}
is equal to
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{Z}[i]
, or
\mathbb{Z}[\rho]
where
\rho=(-1+\sqrt{-3})/2
.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic number field with discriminant
d_{K}
. We take
L
to be the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, so
\mathcal{O}_{L}=\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Define also the normalizations, for
c\neq 0
,

\widetilde{D}(c,d)=D(c,d)/(2E_{2}(0)\sqrt{-1})\text{ and }\widetilde{\Phi}=\Phi/(2E_{2}(0)\sqrt{-1}).

In this paper weassume that
K
is a fixed imaginary quadratic field different from
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
(in which case
D
is identically zero). In
[
1
]
, Berkopec, Branch, Heikkinen, Nunn, and Wong showed that the values of
\widetilde{D}
are real and dense in
\mathbb{R}
, extending work of Ito
[
7
]
from Euclidean
K
to the general case. Following Vardi’s work in the classical case, it is then natural to ask how the values of
\widetilde{D}
are distributed.

In this paper, we show that the values of the normalized elliptic Dedekind sum are equidistributed mod
1
.

Theorem 1
.

For all positive
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
the sequence

\{r\widetilde{D}(c,d)\,:\,c,d\in\mathcal{O}_{K},0<|d|<|c|,(c,d)=1\}

is equidistributed on
[0,1)
as
|c|\to\infty
.

To prove Theorem
1
, by the Weyl criterion for equidistribution, it suffices to obtain a nontrivial bound on the following sum for any positive integer
n
and real
X>0
,

\sum_{0<|c|<X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\bmod c\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}e(rn\widetilde{D}(c,d)),

where
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
(the trivial bound being
O(X^{4})
). If we let
\Gamma=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
and
\Gamma_{\infty}
be the stabilizer of the cusp at infinity, then the indexing set corresponds to a complete set of representatives of the quotient
\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma
. Using the homomorphism (
1.1
), the inner sum transforms into a generalized Selberg-Kloosterman sum associated to
\Gamma
and
\chi
at the cusp
\infty
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\bmod c\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}e(r\widetilde{D}(c,d))=S_{\infty}(\lfloor-r\rfloor,\lfloor-r\rfloor,c,{\chi_{\alpha}})

for
c\neq 0
, where we set
\alpha=-r+\lfloor-r\rfloor
with
\lfloor x\rfloor
the greatest integer less than or equal to
x
and

(1.3)

{\chi_{\alpha}(M)}=e(\alpha\widetilde{\Phi}(M)),\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

A more general definition is given in (
1.7
). Note that
\widetilde{\Phi}
is integral by
[
11
, Satz 4]
and moreover real-valued. The function
S_{\infty}
in defined in (
1.7
) and can also be viewed as a Kloosterman sum twisted by a unitary character related to the ‘phase’ of the elliptic Dedekind sum.

In the classical case, the required bounds for Selberg-Kloosterman sums were proved by Goldfeld and Sarnak
[
5
]
. The equidistribution of classical Dedekind sums was proved by Vardi using a similar relation between Dedekind sums and Selberg-Kloosterman sums on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

[
12
, Theorem 1.4]
, which take the form

e\left(\frac{r}{4}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<d<c\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}e(rs(c,d))=S\left(\Big\lfloor\frac{-r}{12}\Big\rfloor,\Big\lfloor\frac{-r}{12}\Big\rfloor,c,\chi_{r},\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\right),

for
c\neq 0
and where
S
is defined as on
[
12
, p.191]
.
We will use a similar strategy to prove Theorem
1
in a more general setting.

1.2.
Sums of generalized Selberg-Kloosterman sums

Let
\Gamma
be a cofinite and non-cocompact discrete subgroup of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
with an associated cusp
\zeta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
and
A\in\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
so that
A\zeta=\infty
.
Let
\Gamma_{\zeta}
be the stabilizer of
\zeta
in
\Gamma
, and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta}
its maximal unipotent subgroup. Let
\mathcal{R}
be a complete set of representatives of double cosets in the quotient

(1.4)

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}*&*\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in A\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta}A^{-1}\backslash A\Gamma A^{-1}/A\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta}A^{-1},\quad c\neq 0,

which we also write as
(c,d)
for short. Given
\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta}
, there is an associated complex lattice
\Lambda
described in (
2.1
), with dual lattice
\Lambda^{\prime}
. Note that
\Lambda^{\prime}
is non-standard and is defined with respect to the inner product in (
1.5
). For this reason, the Selberg-Kloosterman sums we define are also non-standard.

Define a bilinear form on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
as

(1.5)

\langle u,v\rangle=\frac{u\bar{v}-\bar{u}v}{2i}=\text{Im}(u\bar{v}).

We note that this choice of bilinear form generally differs from convention, such as in
[
3
]
; this difference is due to the fact that (
1.1
) involves the function
I(z)
.
For any fixed
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, let
\chi_{\alpha}:\Gamma\to S^{1}
be a homomorphism such that
for each lattice
\Lambda
associated to a cusp
\zeta
of
\Gamma
,

(1.6)

\chi_{\alpha}:\begin{bmatrix}1&\omega\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\mapsto e\left(-\text{Im}(\alpha\bar{\omega})\right)=e(-\langle\alpha,\omega\rangle),\qquad\omega\in\Lambda.

This includes the special case of
\Gamma=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
with
\chi_{\alpha}
as in (
1.3
), which follows from the fact that
for any
a\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
, we have
\widetilde{\Phi}(\begin{smallmatrix}1&a\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})=\text{Im}(a)
and also
\overline{\Phi(M)}=-\Phi(M)
by
[
11
]
.

For any
m,n\in\Lambda^{\prime},c\in\Lambda
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, we define the generalized Selberg-Kloosterman sum on
\Gamma
associated to
\zeta
and
\chi_{\alpha}
to be

(1.7)

S_{\zeta}(m,n,c,\chi_{\alpha}):=\sum_{(c,d)\in\mathcal{R}}\overline{\chi_{\alpha}(\gamma)}\,\widetilde{e}\left(\frac{\overline{(m-\alpha)}a+\overline{(n-\alpha)}d}{c}\right),

where
\widetilde{e}(z)=e^{2\pi i\text{Im}(z)}
.
Note that in the original Selberg-Kloosterman sums over
\mathbb{Q}
, the character
\chi
is obtained from the multiplier system associated to a given factor of automorphy. For this reason, the estimate given in Theorem
2
is of independent interest.

The proof of Theorem
1
follows from bounds on both the generalized Selberg-Kloosterman sums and the Kloosterman zeta function given by

(1.8)

\displaystyle Z_{\zeta}(m,n,s,\chi_{\alpha}):=\sum_{c\neq 0}\frac{S_{\zeta}(m,n,c,\chi_{\alpha})}{|c|^{2s}}.

The main ingredients in the proof of Theorem
1
are the associated Eisenstein and Poincaré series. These Poincaré series give bounds for our generalized Selberg-Kloosterman sums and hence generalize a classical result of Goldfeld and Sarnak
[
5
]
to the imaginary quadratic case. For simplicity, we state the theorem for the case
\zeta=\infty
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
m,n\in\Lambda^{\prime}
,
c\in\Lambda
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
\chi_{\alpha}
be a unitary character of
\Gamma
satisfying (
1.6
). Then for any
\epsilon>0
, we have

\sum_{|c|\leq x}\frac{S_{\infty}(m,n,c,\chi_{\alpha})}{|c|^{2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\tau_{j}x^{\beta_{j}}+O(x^{1+\epsilon}),

for certain constants
\tau_{j}
and real
0<\beta_{j}<2
. The implied constant depends only on
\Gamma,\chi_{\alpha},k,m
and
n
.

Theorem
2
can be adapted to hold for other
\Gamma
and
\zeta
by applying the argument in Section
3
to the Poincaré series defined in (
2.3
).

In Section
4.2
, together with a routine application of the analytic theory of Eisenstein series on
\Gamma
, we deduce the main result by specializing to
\Gamma=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
and
K\neq\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1}),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
.
The general nature of these results draws, in part, on the work of Elstrodt, Grunewald, and Mennicke
[
3
]
. In particular, similar to the work of Burrin
[
2
]
in the classical case, our methods should apply to future generalizations of elliptic Dedekind sums to more general subgroups of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

2.1.
Upper Half-Space

The upper half-space in Euclidean 3-space is a model for 3-dimensional hyperbolic space

\mathbb{H}^{3}=\{(z,r)\penalty\ |\penalty\ z\in\mathbb{C},r>0\}=\{(x,y,r)\penalty\ |\penalty\ x,y\in\mathbb{R},r>0\}.

We can view
\mathbb{H}^{3}
as a subset of Hamilton’s quaternions, and a point
P\in\mathbb{H}^{3}
is written

P=(z,r)=(x,y,r)=z+rj,

where
z=x+iy
and
j=(0,0,1)
.
The action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on
P\in\mathbb{H}^{3}
is given by the isometry

P\mapsto MP:=M(P)=(aP+b)(cP+d)^{-1},

where the inverse is taken in the skew field of the quaternions. More explicitly, we write

M(z+rj)=z_{M}+r_{M}j
where

z_{M}=\frac{(az+b)(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})+a\bar{c}r^{2}}{|cz+d|^{2}+|c|^{2}r^{2}}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ r_{M}=\frac{r}{|cz+d|^{2}+|c|^{2}r^{2}}.

We are interested in discrete subgroups
\Gamma
of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and functions on the quotient
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{3}
. Define
D_{\Gamma}
to be a fundamental domain of
\Gamma
. Define also the Laplace-Beltrami operator

\Delta=r^{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial^{2}x}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial^{2}y}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial^{2}r}\right)-r\frac{\partial}{\partial r},

and the associated volume form
dv=dx\,dy\,dr/r^{3}
and length form

ds^{2}=(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dr^{2})/r^{2}
.

We write
\Gamma_{\infty}
for the stabilizer of the cusp at infinity in
\Gamma
. In the case that
\Gamma=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
, we have that

\Gamma_{\infty}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}:a\in\mathcal{O}_{K}\right\},

and a coset representation of
\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma
is given by

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}*&*\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma:(c,d)=1\right\}.

More generally, given a cusp
\zeta
of a general
\Gamma
such that
A\zeta=\infty
, the set

(2.1)

\Lambda:=\left\{\omega\in\mathbb{C}:\begin{pmatrix}1&\omega\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in A\Gamma A^{-1}\right\}

forms a lattice in
\mathbb{C}
. We shall denote by
\Gamma_{\zeta}
the stabilizer of
\zeta
in
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta}
the maximal unipotent subgroup of
\Gamma_{\zeta}
.
We have then that

A\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta}A^{-1}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&\omega\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}:\omega\in\Lambda\right\}.

Denote by
|\Lambda|
the area of a fundamental parallelogram associated to the lattice
\Lambda
, and
\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma)
the covolume of
\Gamma
obtained by integrating
ds
over a fundamental domain of
\Gamma
. For example, if
\Gamma=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K}),
a well-known result of Humbert shows that

\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma)={|d_{K}|^{3/2}}\zeta_{K}(2)/(4\pi^{2}),

where
d_{K}
is the discriminant of
K
and
\zeta_{K}(s)
is the Dedekind zeta function of
K

[
3
, Chapter 7, Theorem 1.1]
.

2.2.
Eisenstein series

The Eisenstein series on
\Gamma
associated to the cusp
\zeta
is defined by

E_{A}(P,s):=\sum_{M\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta}\setminus\Gamma}(r_{AM})^{s},

which converges absolutely for Re
(s)>2
. The map

z+rj\mapsto r^{s}

satisfies the differential equation
\Delta r^{s}=s(s-2)r^{s},
so that
E_{A}(P,s)
is a
\Gamma
-invariant eigenfunction of
\Delta
with

\Delta E_{A}(P,s)=s(s-2)E_{A}(P,s),

but is not square-integrable on
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{3}
. By Theorem 6.1.2 of
[
3
]
,
E_{A}(P,s)
has meromorphic continuation to the whole
s
-plane. Furthermore, Theorem 6.1.11 of
[
3
]
shows
E_{A}(P,s)
is holomorphic for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>0
, except for a finite number of simple poles on the real line
(0,2]
, and the simple pole of
E_{A}(P,s)
at
s=2
has residue equal to
|\Lambda|/\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma)
. Note also that the series

E_{A}^{*}(P,s)=\sum_{M\in(A\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta}A^{-1})\setminus A\Gamma A^{-1}}(r_{M})^{s},

agrees with
E_{A}(P,s)
term by term by the change of variable
M\mapsto AMA^{-1}
whenever the series converges absolutely.

The analytic properties of
E_{A}(P,s)
are controlled by the behaviour of the zeroth coefficient of its Fourier expansion at the cusp. The Fourier expansion is given by

\sum_{\omega^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}a_{\omega^{\prime}}(r,s)q^{\omega^{\prime}},

where
q^{\omega^{\prime}}=e(\omega^{\prime}\cdot z),
with
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
and the operation
\cdot
denotes the Euclidean scalar product in
\mathbb{C}
, and
\Lambda^{\prime}
is the dual lattice of
\Lambda
with respect to the latter inner product. The Fourier coefficients can be expressed as

(2.2)

a_{0}(r,s)=[\Gamma_{\zeta}:\Gamma_{\zeta}^{\prime}]|d_{0}|^{-2s}r^{s}+\frac{\pi}{(s-1)|\Lambda|}\phi_{0}(s)r^{2-s}

and

a_{\omega^{\prime}}(r,s)=\frac{2\pi^{s}}{|\Lambda|\Gamma(s)}|\omega^{\prime}|^{s-1}\phi_{\omega^{\prime}}(s)rK_{s-1}(2\pi|\omega^{\prime}|r),\qquad\omega^{\prime}\neq 0,

where
d_{0}
is a constant given in
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
,
K_{s}
denotes the modified Bessel function of the second kind, and
\phi_{\omega^{\prime}}(s)
is the Dirichlet series

\phi_{\omega^{\prime}}(s)=\sum_{(c,d)\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{e(\omega^{\prime}\cdot\frac{d}{c})}{|c|^{2s}},

with the summation running over
\mathcal{R}
as defined in (
1.4
).

Remark 3
.

For example, if
\Gamma=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[i])
, then
\phi_{0}(s)=\zeta_{K}(s-1)/\zeta_{K}(s)
and for
\Gamma=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
, it can be written as

\sum^{h}_{j=1}\frac{L_{j}(s-1)}{L_{j}(s)},

where

is the class number of
K
and

L_{j}(s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\psi_{j}(\mathfrak{a})N(\mathfrak{a})^{-s},

the sum running over nonzero ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
\psi_{j}
an ideal class character
[
10
, p.210]
. For general
\Gamma

follows from the functional equation of the associated Eisenstein series (see
[
3
, p. 232-233]
).

2.3.
Poincaré series

For
s\in\mathbb{C}
and

\Gamma
associated to

\displaystyle U_{A,m}(P,s,\chi_{\alpha},\Gamma,\zeta):=\sum_{M\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta}\backslash\Gamma}\overline{\chi_{\alpha}(AM)}\,r_{AM}^{s}e(i|m-\alpha|r_{AM}-\langle m-\alpha,z_{AM}\rangle)

For the purpose of clarity, we will set

Lemma 4
.

The function

is

-invariant, and

U_{m}(MP,s,\chi_{\alpha})=\chi_{\alpha}(M)\,U_{m}(P,s,\chi_{\alpha}).

Proof.

For any

, we have that
M^{\prime}
acts trivially on

and by translation on

. We then have, up to conjugaton,

\displaystyle\overline{\chi_{\alpha}(M^{\prime}M)}r_{M^{\prime}M}^{s}e(i|m-\alpha|r_{M^{\prime}M}-\langle m-\alpha,z_{M^{\prime}M}\rangle),\qquad M^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\omega\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=e(\langle\alpha,\omega\rangle)\overline{\chi_{\alpha}(M)}r_{M}^{s}e(i|m-\alpha|r_{M}-\langle m-\alpha,z_{M}+\omega\rangle)

\displaystyle=\overline{\chi_{\alpha}(M)}r_{M}^{s}e(i|m-\alpha|r_{M}-\langle m-\alpha,z_{M}\rangle),

where we note that

Secondly, by direct computation we have

\displaystyle U_{m}(M_{1}P,s,\chi_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\overline{\chi_{\alpha}(M_{1}^{-1})}U_{m}(P,s,\chi_{\alpha}),

and by the homomorphism property of

∎

Let

In this section, we take
\Gamma

\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
,
\zeta=\infty
a cusp of
\Gamma

\chi_{\alpha}

\Gamma

3.1.
Preliminary estimates

We shall first prove an estimate for the Kloosterman zeta function in (
1.8
). We continue to assume
m,n
are in the dual lattice of
\Lambda
.

Theorem 5
.

The function
Z_{\infty}(m,n,s,\chi_{\alpha})
is meromorphic in
\text{Re}(s)>1
with at most a finite number of simple poles in
(1,2)
and satisfies the growth condition

|Z_{\infty}(m,n,s,\chi_{\alpha})|=O\left(\frac{|n-\alpha|^{2}|m-\alpha|^{2}|s|}{(\sigma-1)^{2}}\right),

for
s=\sigma+it
,
\sigma>1
as

. The implied constant depends only on
\Gamma,\chi_{\alpha},m,n
.

For the proof of Theorem
5
, we rely on two lemmas and Stirling’s formula. The first result we need is an upper bound for the
L^{2}
-norm of our Poincaré series. The proof and formulation of the following lemma is similar to that of
[
5
, Theorem 1]

Lemma 6

Define the counting function

Proposition 9
.

For

as in (

Proof.

The proof follows the same method as that of Theorem
2
(see also
[
2

We first write

\phi_{0}(s)=\sum_{(c,d)\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{1}{|c|^{2s}}=\sum_{c\neq 0}\frac{1}{|c|^{2s}}\left|\left\{d:\begin{pmatrix}*&*\\

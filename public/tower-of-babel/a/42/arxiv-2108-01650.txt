Application of Hardy inequalities for some singular parabolic equations
Nikolai Kutev, Tsviatko Rangelov

Abstract
Boundary value problems for non-linear parabolic equations with singular potentials are considered. Existence and non-existence results as an application of different Hardy inequalities are proved. Blow-up conditions are investigated too.

1
Introduction

We consider boundary value problem for the singular p-heat equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&u_{t}-\Delta_{p}u=\mu W(x)|u|^{p-2}u,\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,t>0.\\
&u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,\\
&u(x,t)=0\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\partial\Omega,t>0.\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

Here
\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u=\hbox{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
is the p-Laplacian,
\displaystyle\mu=const\in\mathbb{R}
,
\displaystyle p>1
,
\displaystyle\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\displaystyle n\geq 2
,
\displaystyle 0\in\Omega
and function
\displaystyle W(x)
is singular on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
or/and at
\displaystyle 0
.

More precisely we consider the following functions
\displaystyle W(x)

\begin{array}[]{ll}i)&W(x)=d^{-p}(x),d(x)=\hbox{dist}(x,\partial\Omega),\ p>1,p\neq n;\\
ii)&W(x)=d^{-p}(x)+\frac{p}{2(p-1)}d^{-p}\log^{-2}\frac{d(x)}{D},d(x)=\hbox{dist}(x,\partial\Omega),p>n,\\
&D=\sup_{x\in\Omega}d(x);\\
iii)&W(x)=\left(|x|\log\frac{R}{|x|}\right)^{-n}\hbox{ in }\Omega\subset B_{R}=\{|x|<R\},R>e\sup_{x\in\Omega}|x|;\\
iv)&W(x)=|x|^{m-n}\left|\varphi^{m}(x)-|x|^{m}\right|^{-p}\hbox{ in }\Omega=\{|x|<\varphi(\theta)\},p>n,m=\frac{p-n}{p-1},\\
&\theta=\frac{x}{|x|}\hbox{ is the angular variable of }x,\hbox{ and }\Omega\ \ \hbox{ is star-shaped with respect }\\
&\hbox{ to an interior ball centered at the origin },\varphi\in C^{0,1}.\end{array}

(1.2)

The interest to parabolic problems with singular potentials is due to the applications, for example in molecular physics, see
Lévy-Leblond 1967
, quantum cosmology, see
Berestycki and Esteban 1997
, quantum mechanics, see
Baras and Goldstein 1984
, electron capture problems, see
Giri et al. 2008
, porous medium of fluids, see
Ansini and Giacomelli 2004
and the combustion models, see
Gel’fand 1959
. Also in some reaction-diffusion problems involving the heat equation with critical reaction term, the regularized equation is of the type (
1.1
).

In the remarkable pionering paper
Baras and Goldstein 1984
is proved that problem (
1.1
) with Hardy potential
\displaystyle W(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}
and
\displaystyle p=2
has a global solution for
\displaystyle\mu\leq\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}
. Moreover if
\displaystyle\mu>\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}
then the solution of (
1.1
) blows-up for a finite time.

The results of Baras and Goldstein are extended in different directions. In
Vázquez and Zuazua 2000
the authors present a complete description of the functional framework in which it is possible to obtain well-posedness for the singular heat equation.

The singular parabolic equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\Delta u+\frac{\nabla\rho}{\rho}.\nabla u+\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}u,\ \ \hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

with Kolmogorov operator is studied in
Canale et al. 2019
, where
\displaystyle\rho
is probability density on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The quasilinear case

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}-\hbox{div}\left(a(x,t,\nabla u)\right)=\frac{\mu u}{|x|^{2}}+f(x,t),\ \ \hbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,\\
u(x,t)=0,\ \ \hbox{ for }(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

is considered in
Porzio 2019
. In the problem (
1.3
) function
\displaystyle W(x)=|x|^{-2}
is Hardy potential and the asymptotic behavior of the solutions of (
1.3
) when
\displaystyle f\neq 0
is investigated.

Another generalization of
Baras and Goldstein 1984
is given in
Azorero and Alonso 1998
for problem (
1.1
) with general Hardy potential
\displaystyle W(x)=|x|^{-p}
,
\displaystyle 1<p<n
,
\displaystyle n\geq 3
and
\displaystyle 0\in\Omega
. The authors prove existence and nonexistence results when
\displaystyle\mu\leq\left(\frac{n-p}{p}\right)^{p}
or
\displaystyle\mu>\left(\frac{n-p}{p}\right)^{p}
respectively. Here
\displaystyle\left(\frac{n-p}{p}\right)^{p}
is the optimal Hardy constant in the corresponding Hardy inequality.

In
Junqiang et al. 2012
the more general problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}-\Delta_{p}u=\frac{\mu}{|x|^{s}}|u|^{q-2}u,\ \ \hbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,\\
u(x,t)=0,\ \ \hbox{ for }(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

with
\displaystyle 1<p<n
,
\displaystyle 0<s\leq p\leq q\leq\frac{n-s}{n-p}p
,
\displaystyle\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\displaystyle 0\in\Omega
is investigated. By means of Sobolev–Hardy inequality

\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\right)^{q/p}\geq C_{n,p,q,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{q}}{|x|^{s}}dx,\ \ u\in W_{0}^{1.p}(\Omega),

existence of weak, very weak and entropy solutions to (
1.4
) are proved for
\displaystyle\mu\leq C_{n,p,q,s}
and finite blow-up for
\displaystyle\mu>C_{n,p,q,s}
.

In
Abdellaoui et al. 2014
where singular reaction–diffusion equation for p-Laplace operator with diffusion and reaction term

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(u)^{\theta}_{t}-\Delta_{p}u=\frac{\mu|u|^{p-1}}{|x|^{p}}+u^{q}+f,\ \ \hbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),\ \ u\geq 0\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,\\
u(x,t)=0,\ \ \hbox{ for }(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

is investigated. Here
\displaystyle\theta=1
or
\displaystyle\theta=p-1
,
\displaystyle f(x)\geq 0
,
\displaystyle 1<p<n
,
\displaystyle q>0
. The authors prove existence of a critical exponent
\displaystyle q_{+}(p,\lambda)
such that for
\displaystyle q<q_{+}(p,\lambda)
there are global solutions to (
1.5
) and for
\displaystyle q>q_{+}(p,\lambda)
there is no solution. In the semilinear case of (
1.5
) existence of a critical exponent
\displaystyle q_{+}(\lambda)
of Fujita type, see
Fujita 1966
is
stated and existence of a solution is proved if and only if
\displaystyle q<q_{+}(\lambda)
.

Without a presence of a reaction term, for
\displaystyle\theta=1
and
\displaystyle p<\frac{2n}{n+2}
an existence of a global solution for all
\displaystyle\mu>0
in (
1.5
) is given in
Aguilar and Peral 2000
under suitable conditions on
\displaystyle f
and
\displaystyle u_{0}
. The above results are extended to quasilinear parabolic equations without reaction term and with Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg weights in
Dall’aglio et al. 2004
. When
\displaystyle\theta\neq 1
,
\displaystyle\mu=0
, the one dimensional case of (
1.5
) without reaction term is studied in
Esteban and Vázquez 1988
.

Finally, let us mention the results in
Biccari and Zuazua 2016
for the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}-\Delta u=\frac{\mu}{d^{2}(x)}u+f,\ \ \hbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,\\
u(x,t)=0,\ \ \hbox{ for }(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\end{array}\right.

which is singular on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
. For
\displaystyle\mu\leq\frac{1}{4}
the authors prove existence of a unique weak solution. If
\displaystyle\mu>\frac{1}{4}
then there is no control, which means that the blow-up phenomena cannot be prevented. Here
\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}
is the optimal constant in the corresponding Hardy inequality.

In the present paper we extend the result for (
1.1
) with
\displaystyle p=2
in
Baras and Goldstein 1984
and for
\displaystyle 2<p<n
in
Azorero and Alonso 1998
when the Hardy potential
\displaystyle W(x)=|x|^{-p}
is singular at the origin, to more general singular parabolic equations with
\displaystyle W(x)
given in i) - iv) of (
1.2
) above. The new Hardy potentials are singular on the boundary of the domain or both at the origin and on the boundary. Let us recall that for the corresponding Hardy inequalities, the Hardy constants are optimal, see (
2.1
) - (
2.4
). We prove that for
\displaystyle\mu
less than the optimal Hardy constant problem (
1.1
) has a global weak solution. When
\displaystyle\mu
is greater than the optimal Hardy constant than the solution blows-up for a finite time. To our best knowledge these type of singular parabolic equations are not consider in the literature.

In Sect.
2
we recall some Hardy inequalities with weights
\displaystyle W(x)
and prove global existence of weak solutions of (
1.1
) while Sect.
3
deals with finite time blow-up of solutions to (
1.1
).

2
Existence of global solution

In this section we prove existence of a global, generalized solution of (
1.1
) when
\displaystyle W(x)
has one of the forms (
1.2
)i) - (
1.2
)iv) and
\displaystyle\mu<C_{p}
or
\displaystyle\mu<C_{p,n}
respectively.

For this purpose we recall some well-known Hardy‘s inequalities which are important in the proof of the main results.

Inequality with kernel (
1.2
)i)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\geq C_{p}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{p}}{d^{p}(x)}dx,

(2.1)

for
\displaystyle u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, where
\displaystyle C_{p}=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}
,
\displaystyle p>1,n\neq p,n\geq 2
,
\displaystyle d(x)=\hbox{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
. Here
\displaystyle\Omega
is a bounded
\displaystyle C^{2}
smooth domain in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
with nonnegative mean curvature
\displaystyle H(x)\geq 0
. The constant
\displaystyle C_{p}=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}
is optimal. Inequality (
2.1
) is proved in
Lewis et al. 2012
, see Theorem 1.2.

Inequality with kernel (
1.2
)ii).

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\geq C\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{p}}{d^{p}(x)}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)\left(\log^{-2}\frac{d(x)}{D}\right)\right)dx,

(2.2)

for
\displaystyle u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, where
\displaystyle D\geq\sup_{x\in\Omega}d(x)
,
\displaystyle p>n\geq 2
and
\displaystyle\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is bounded domain with
\displaystyle C^{2}
smooth boundary, satisfying some geometric conditions like the mean convexity of
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
. The constant
\displaystyle C_{p}=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}
is optimal. Inequality (
2.2
) is proved in
Barbatis et al. 2003
, see Theorem A.

Inequality with kernel (
1.2
)iii).

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{n}dx\geq C_{n}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{n}}{\left(|x|\log\frac{R}{|x|}\right)^{n}}dx,

(2.3)

for
\displaystyle u\in W^{1,n}_{0}(\Omega)
, where
\displaystyle R\geq e\sup_{x\in\Omega}|x|
,
\displaystyle\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2
,
\displaystyle 0\in\Omega
,
\displaystyle\Omega
is a bounded domain, see Theorem 1.1 in
Adimurthi and Esteban 2005
. Inequality (
2.3
) for
\displaystyle\Omega=B_{1}=\{|x|<1\}
is proved in
Ioku and Ishiwata 2015
. The constant
\displaystyle C_{n}=\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^{n}
is optimal;

Inequality with kernel (
1.2
)iv).

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\geq C_{p,n}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{n-m}|\varphi^{m}(x)-|x|^{m}|^{p}}dx,

(2.4)

for
\displaystyle u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, where
\displaystyle p>n
,
\displaystyle n\geq 2
,
\displaystyle\Omega=\{|x|<\varphi(\theta)\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\displaystyle\theta=\frac{x}{|x|}
is angular variable of
\displaystyle x
and
\displaystyle\Omega
is a star shape domain with respect to a small ball centered at the origin,
\displaystyle\varphi\in C^{0,1}
. The constant
\displaystyle C_{p,n}=\left(\frac{p-n}{p}\right)^{p}
is optimal, see
Fabricant et al. 2017
and
Kutev and Rangelov 2020
, Theorem 5.1.

By means of Hardy inequalities (
2.1
) - (
2.4
) we have the following results.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
\displaystyle\Omega
is a bounded
\displaystyle C^{2}
smooth domain in
\displaystyle R^{n}
,
\displaystyle n\geq 2
, with nonnegative mean curvature
\displaystyle H(x)\geq 0
and
\displaystyle p>1,p\neq n
. Then if
\displaystyle\mu<\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}
,
\displaystyle u_{0}(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
problem (
1.1
) with
\displaystyle W(x)
in (
1.2
)i) has a global solution

u(x,t)\in L^{\infty}\left((0,\tau),L^{2}(\Omega)\right)\cup L^{p}\left((0,\tau),W^{1,p}(\Omega)\right),\ \ \hbox{ for all }\tau>0.

(2.5)

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose
\displaystyle\Omega
is a bounded convex
\displaystyle C^{2}
smooth domain in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\displaystyle n\geq 2
. Then if
\displaystyle\mu<\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}
,
\displaystyle p>1,p\neq n
,
\displaystyle u_{0}(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, problem (
1.1
) with
\displaystyle W(x)
defined in (
1.2
)ii) has a global solution satisfying (
2.5
).

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose
\displaystyle\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\displaystyle n\geq 2
,
\displaystyle 0\in\Omega
. Then if
\displaystyle\mu<\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^{n}
,
\displaystyle u_{0}(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, problem (
1.1
) with
\displaystyle W(x)
defined in (
1.2
)iii) has a global solution satisfying (
2.5
).

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose
\displaystyle\Omega=\{|x|<\varphi(x)\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\displaystyle n\geq 2
, is a star-shaped domain with respect to a small ball centered at the origin. Then if
\displaystyle\mu<\left(\frac{p-n}{p}\right)^{p}
,
\displaystyle p>n
,
\displaystyle u_{0}(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, problem (
1.1
) with
\displaystyle W(x)
given by (
1.2
)iv) has a global solution, satisfying (
2.5
).

Proof of Theorems
2.1
-
2.4
.

In order to prove the Theorems
2.1
-
2.4
we consider the truncated function

W_{N}(x)=\min\{N,W(x)\},N=1,2,\ldots

(2.6)

where
\displaystyle W(x)
is one of the functions (
1.2
)i) - (
1.2
)iv). Let
\displaystyle u_{N}(x,t)
be the solution of the truncated problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&u_{N,t}-\Delta_{p}u_{N}=\mu W_{N}(x)|u_{N}|^{p-2}u_{N},\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,t>0.\\
&u_{N}(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,\\
&u_{N}(x,t)=0\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\partial\Omega,t>0.\end{array}\right.

(2.7)

Multiplying (
2.7
) with
\displaystyle u_{N}
and integrating by parts we get from the Hardy‘s inequalities (
2.1
) - (
2.4
) the following estimates for every
\displaystyle T>0

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\int_{\Omega}|u_{N}(x,T)|^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{N}(x,t)|^{p}dxdt\\
&=\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx+\mu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}W_{N}(x)|u_{N}(x,t)|^{p}dxdt\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx+\mu C^{-1}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{N}(x,t)|^{p}dxdt,\end{array}

(2.8)

where

C=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&C_{p}=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)\hbox{ in Theorems \ref{th1} and \ref{th2}},\\
&C_{n}=\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)\hbox{ in Theorem \ref{th3}},\\
&C_{n,p}=\left(\frac{p-n}{p}\right)\hbox{ in Theorem \ref{th4}}.\end{array}\right.

(2.9)

Since
\displaystyle\mu C^{-1}<1
we get from (
2.8
) the energy estimate

\int_{\Omega}|u_{N}(x,T)|^{2}dx+(1-\mu C^{-1})\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{N}(x,t)|^{p}dxdt\leq\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx.

From the comparison principle
\displaystyle u_{N}(x,t)
is a nondecreasing sequence of functions because
\displaystyle W_{N}(x,t)\geq W_{M}(x,t)
for every
\displaystyle x\in\Omega
,
\displaystyle t>0
and
\displaystyle N\geq M
. We can pass to the limit
\displaystyle N\rightarrow\infty
by using Theorem 4.1 in
Boccardo and Murat 1992
. Thus the global solution
\displaystyle u(x,t)
of (
1.1
) is defined as a limit of the solution
\displaystyle u_{N}(x,t)
of the truncated problem (
2.7
) and
\displaystyle u(x,t)
has the regularity properties given in (
2.5
).
∎

3
Finite time blow-up

In this section we prove finite time blow-up
of the solutions to the problem (
1.1
) with
\displaystyle u_{0}(x)>0
for
\displaystyle x\in\bar{\Omega}
, i.e.,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&u_{t}-\Delta_{p}u=\mu W(x)|u|^{p-2}u,\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,t>0.\\
&u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)>0\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\bar{\Omega},\\
&u(x,t)=0\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\partial\Omega,t>0,\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

for
\displaystyle W(x)
defined in (
1.2
)i) - (
1.2
)iv).

If
\displaystyle\lambda_{1N}
,
\displaystyle\phi_{1N}(x)
are the first eigenvalue and the first eigenfunction of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&-\Delta_{p}\phi_{1N}=\lambda_{1N}W_{N}(x)|\phi_{1N}|^{p-2}\phi_{1N},\ \ x\in\Omega,\\
&\phi_{1N}=0,\ \ x\in\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

where
\displaystyle W_{N}(x)
is defined in(
2.6
), then the following result holds.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
\displaystyle\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\displaystyle n\geq 2
and
\displaystyle\lambda>C
, where
\displaystyle C
is given in (
2.9
). Then we have

\lambda_{1N}\geq C,\ \ N=1,2,\ldots

(3.2)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{1N}=C

(3.3)

Proof.

Inequality (
3.2
) follows from the Rayleigh quotient and Hardy inequalities (
2.1
) - (
2.4
). Indeed

\lambda_{1N}=\inf_{v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}dx}{\int_{\Omega}W_{N}(x)|v|^{p}dx}\geq\inf_{v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}dx}{\int_{\Omega}W(x)|v|^{p}dx}\geq C

In order to proof (
3.3
) we suppose by contradiction that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{1N}=C+2\delta,\hbox{ for some }\delta>0.

(3.4)

From the optimality of the constant
\displaystyle C
in Hardy inequalities (
2.1
) - (
2.4
) there exists
\displaystyle v_{N}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that

\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{N}|^{p}dx}{\int_{\Omega}W_{N}(x)|v_{N}|^{p}dx}<C+\delta

Thus
\displaystyle\lambda_{1N}\leq C+\delta
for every
\displaystyle N=1,2,\ldots
which contradicts (
3.4
).
∎

We will construct a positive sub-solution to (
3.1
) by the method of separation of variables, i.e., a sub-solution
\displaystyle v(x,t)=X(x)T(t)
where
\displaystyle X(x)
and
\displaystyle T(t)
satisfy the problems.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&-\Delta_{p}X(x)-\mu W_{N}(x)X^{p-1}(x)=-X(x),\ \ x\in\Omega,\\
&X(x)=0,\ \ x\in\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(3.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&T^{\prime}(t)=T^{p-1}(t),\ \ t>0,\\
&T(0)=T_{0}.\end{array}\right.

(3.6)

Since
\displaystyle T(t)=T_{0}\left[1-(p-2)T_{0}^{p-2}t\right]^{-\frac{1}{p-2}}
for
\displaystyle p>2
, then
\displaystyle T(t)
blows-up for
\displaystyle t=\left[(p-2)T^{p-2}_{0}\right]^{-1}
.

For
\displaystyle a^{p-2}=\mu
, after the change of the functions
\displaystyle aX=Y
, problem (
3.5
) becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&-\Delta_{p}Y(x)=\mu\left(W_{N}(x)Y^{p-1}(x)-Y(x)\right),\ \ x\in\Omega,\\
&Y(x)=0,\ \ x\in\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(3.7)

We need the following lemma (see Lemma 5.1 in
Azorero and Alonso 1998
)

Lemma 3.2
.

(1) There exists a constant
\displaystyle A>0
such that if (
3.7
) has a positive solution
\displaystyle Y(x)
then
\displaystyle\|Y\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}>A
;

(2) If
\displaystyle\lambda_{1N}
is the first eigenvalue of
\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}
with weight
\displaystyle W_{N}(x)
then
\displaystyle\lambda_{1N}
is the unique bifurcation point from infinity for the problem (
3.7
)

As it is mention in
Azorero and Alonso 1998
the unbounded bifurcation branch of positive solutions given in Lemma
3.2
(2) cannot cross the level
\displaystyle\|Y\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=A
from Lemma
3.2
(1), neither, obviously, the hyperplane
\displaystyle\mu=0
for
\displaystyle\mu>\lambda_{1N}
. Hence, problem (
3.7
) has at least one positive solution for
\displaystyle\mu>\lambda_{1N}
, i.e., from (
3.3
) for
\displaystyle N>N_{0}
,
\displaystyle N_{0}
sufficiently large.

Theorem 3.1

(Comparison principle)
.

Suppose
\displaystyle u(x,t)
, defined in (
2.5
) is solution of (
2.7
) and
\displaystyle v(x,t)
is a positive sub-solution to (
2.7
), i.e.,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&v_{t}-\Delta_{p}v\leq\lambda W_{N}(x)v^{p-1},\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,t\in[0,\tau),\\
&v(x,0)\leq u_{0}(x),\ \ u_{0}(x)>0\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\bar{\Omega},\\
&v(x,t)\leq 0\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\partial\Omega,t\in[0,\tau),\end{array}\right.

(3.8)

for a fixed
\displaystyle\tau
.
Then

v(x,t)\leq u(x,t),\ \ \hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,t\in[0,\tau).

(3.9)

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that (
3.9
) fails, i.e., the set
\displaystyle Q=\Omega_{+}(s)\times[0,\tau)
,
\displaystyle\Omega_{+}(s)=\{x\in\Omega,s\in[0,\tau);v(x,s)>u(x,s)\}
is not empty. Multiplying (
3.8
) and (
2.7
) with
\displaystyle(v-u)_{+}
where

(v-u)_{+}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&v-u,\ \ \hbox{for }v>u,\\
&0,\ \ \hbox{for }v\leq u\end{array}\right.,\ \ (v-u)_{+}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega\times[0,\tau),

and integrating in
\displaystyle\Omega\times[0,t]
,
\displaystyle t<\tau
we get for their difference

\begin{array}[]{ll}0\geq&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{+}(s)}(v-u)_{t}(v-u)_{+}dxdt\\
&-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{+}(s)}\hbox{div}\left[|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right](v-u)_{+}dxdt\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{+}(s)}(v(x,t)-u(x,t)^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{+}(0)}(v(x,0)-u(x,0)^{2}dx\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{+}(s)}\left[|\nabla v|^{p-2}v_{x_{i}}(v_{x_{i}}-u_{x_{i}})-|\nabla u|^{p-2}u_{x_{i}}(v_{x_{i}}-u_{x_{i}})\right]dxdt\end{array}

(3.10)

Simple computation give us

\begin{array}[]{ll}&|\nabla v|^{p-2}v_{x_{i}}(v_{x_{i}}-u_{x_{i}})-|\nabla u|^{p-2}u_{x_{i}}(v_{x_{i}}-u_{x_{i}})\\
&=|\nabla v|^{p}-|\nabla v|^{p-2}v_{x_{i}}u_{x_{i}}+|\nabla u|^{p}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}v_{x_{i}}u_{x_{i}}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{p}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{p-2}|\nabla(v-u)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{p}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{p-2}|\nabla(v-u)|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{p-2}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left[|\nabla v|^{p-2}+|\nabla u|^{p-2}\right]|\nabla(u-v)|^{2}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\left[|\nabla v|^{p-2}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\right]\left[|\nabla v|^{2}-|\nabla u|^{2}\right]\geq 0.\end{array}

(3.11)

Since

\int_{\Omega_{+}(0)}\left[v(x,0)-u(x,0)\right]^{2}dx=0,

from (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) we get for every
\displaystyle t\in[0,\tau)

\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}\left[v(x,t)-u(x,t)\right]^{2}dx=0.

Thus
\displaystyle v(x,t)=u(x,t)
for every
\displaystyle x\in\Omega_{+}(t)
and every
\displaystyle t\in[0,\tau)
, i.e., for
\displaystyle(x,t)\in Q
and
\displaystyle Q=\emptyset
which contradicts our assumption.
∎

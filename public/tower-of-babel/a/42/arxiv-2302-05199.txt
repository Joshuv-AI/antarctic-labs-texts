1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a locally compact group with the left Haar measure
m_{G}
(in
the case when
G
is compact,
m_{G}
will denote normalized Haar measure on

G
) and let
M\left(G\right)
be the convolution measure algebra of
G.

As usual,
C_{0}\left(G\right)
will denote the space of all complex
valued continuous functions on
G
vanishing at infinity. Since
C_{0}\left(G\right)^{\ast}=M\left(G\right),
the space
M\left(G\right)
carries
the weak
∗
topology
\sigma\left(M\left(G\right),C_{0}\left(G\right)\right).
In the following, the w
∗
-topology on
M\left(G\right)
always means this topology. For a subset
S
of
G,
by
\left[S\right]
we will denote the closed subgroup of
G
generated by
S.
A
probability measure
\mu
on
G
is said to be
adapted
if
\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right]=G.
Also, a probability measure
\mu
on
G
is
said to be
strictly aperiodic
if the support of
\mu
is not
contained in a proper closed left coset of
G.
For
n\in\mathbb{N},
by
\mu^{n}
we will denote
n
-th convolution power of
\mu\in M\left(G\right)
, where
\mu^{0}:=\delta_{e}
is the Dirac measure concentrated
at the unit element
e
of
G.

A classical Kawada-Itô theorem
[
14
, Theorem 7]
asserts that if
\mu
is an adapted measure on a compact metrizable group
G,
then the sequence
of probability measures
\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
weak
∗
converges to the Haar measure on
G
(see also
[
11
, Theorem 3.2.4]
). If
\mu
is an adapted and strictly aperiodic measure
on a compact metrizable group
G,
then w
∗
-
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu^{n}=m_{G}

[
14
, Theorem 8]
. If
\mu
is an adapted
measure on a second countable non-compact group
G
, then w
∗
-
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu^{n}=0

[
18
, Theorem 2]
. In
[
3
, Théorème 8]
, it was proved that if
\mu
is a strictly aperiodic
measure on a non-compact locally compact group

then w
∗
-
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu^{n}=0
. For related results see also,
[
1
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
.

In this paper, we present some results concerning convergence of probability
measures on locally compact groups.

2.
Weak
∗
convergence

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and let
B\left(X\right)
be the algebra
of all bounded linear operators on
X
. An operator
T\in B\left(X\right)

is said to be
mean ergodic
if the limit

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}T^{i}x\text{ \ exists in
norm }\forall x\in X.

If
T
is mean ergodic, then

P_{T}x:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}T^{i}x\text{ \ }\left(x\in X\right)

is the projection onto
\ker\left(T-I\right)
. The projection
P_{T}

will be called
mean ergodic projection associated with
T.

An operator
T\in B\left(X\right)
is said to be
power bounded
if

C_{T}:=\sup_{n\geq 0}\left\|T^{n}\right\|<\infty.

A power bounded operator
T
on a Banach space
X
is mean ergodic if and
only if

(2.1)

X=\overline{\text{ran}\left(T-I\right)}\oplus\ker\left(T-I\right).

It is well known that a power bounded operator on a reflexive Banach space
is mean ergodic (for instance see,
[
15
, Chapter 2]
).

The following result is an immediate consequence of the identity (2.1).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
T
be a power bounded operator on a Banach space
X
and assume that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T^{n+1}x-T^{n}x\right\|=0
for all

x\in X.
If
T
is mean ergodic
(
so if
X
is reflexive
)
, then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T^{n}x-P_{T}x\right\|=0
for all
x\in X,
where
P_{T}
is the mean ergodic projection associated
with
T
.

The open unit disc and the unit circle in the complex plane will be denoted
by
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{T}
, respectively. If
T\in B\left(X\right)

is power bounded then clearly,
\sigma\left(T\right)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, where
\sigma\left(T\right)
is the spectrum of
T.
The
classical Katznelson-Tzafriri theorem
[
13
]
states that if
T\in B\left(X\right)
is power bounded, then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T^{n+1}-T^{n}\right\|=0
if and only if
\sigma\left(T\right)\cap\mathbb{T}\subseteq\left\{1\right\}.

If
T\in B\left(X\right)
is mean ergodic, then
T
is Cesàro bounded,
that is,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}T^{i}\right\|<\infty.

It follows from the spectral mapping theorem that
r\left(T\right)\leq 1,

where
r\left(T\right)
is the spectral radius of
T.

Let
N
be a normal operator on a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with
the spectral measure
E

(
recall that
\sigma\left(N\right)=\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}E
). If
N
is mean ergodic, then
\left\|N\right\|=r\left(N\right)\leq 1
( notice also that a normal operator is power bounded if
and only it is a contraction). It easily follows from the spectral theorem
that if
N
is a normal contraction operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=i}^{n}N^{i}x=E\left\{1\right\}x\text{\ \ in norm }\forall x\in\mathcal{H}.

On the other hand, a few lines of computation show that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|N^{n+1}x-N^{n}x\right\|^{2}=\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\limits_{\sigma\left(N\right)\cap\mathbb{T\diagdown}\left\{1\right\}}\left|z-1\right|^{2}d\langle E\left(z\right)x,x\rangle\text{, \ }\forall x\in\mathcal{H}\text{.}

From this identity and from Proposition 2.1 it follows that the sequence
\left\{N^{n}x\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in norm for every
x\in\mathcal{H}
if and only if
\left\|N\right\|\leq 1
and
E\left[\sigma\left(N\right)\cap\mathbb{T\diagdown}\left\{1\right\}\right]=0.
Under these conditions we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|N^{n}x-E\left\{1\right\}x\right\|=0\text{, \ }\forall x\in\mathcal{H}.

Recall from
[
8
, Definition 21.1]
that a sequence
\left\{a_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{C}
is called
good weight

for the mean ergodic theorem

(briefly
good weight
) if for every Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
every contraction
T
on
\mathcal{H}
the limit

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}T^{i}x\text{ \ exists }\forall x\in\mathcal{H}\text{.}

By Theorem 21.2 of
[
8
]
, a bounded sequence
\left\{a_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a good weight if and only if the limit

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\xi^{i}=:a\left(\xi\right)\text{ \ exists }\forall\xi\in\mathbb{T}\text{.}

Let
\left(\Omega,\Sigma,m\right)
be a probability space and let
\varphi:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega
be a measure-preserving transformation.
It follows from the Wiener-Wintner theorem
[
8
, Corollary 21.6]
that the
sequence
\left(f\left(\varphi^{n}\left(\omega\right)\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded good weight for any
f\in L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\right)

and for all almost every
\omega\in\Omega
.

If
\left\{a_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded good weight, then for every contraction
T
on a Hilbert
space
\mathcal{H}
one has

(2.2)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}T^{i}x=\sum_{\xi\in\sigma_{p}\left(T\right)\cap\mathbb{T}}a\left(\xi\right)P_{\xi}x\text{\ \ in norm }\forall x\in\mathcal{H},

where
P_{\xi}
are the orthogonal projections onto the mutually orthogonal
eigenspaces
\ker\left(T-\xi I\right)
for
\xi\in\sigma_{p}\left(T\right)\cap\mathbb{T}
, where
\sigma_{p}\left(T\right)
is the point
spectrum of
T

[
8
, Theorem 21.2]
(it follows that
a\left(\xi\right)\neq 0
for at most countably many
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
).

Let
N
be a normal contraction operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}

with the spectral measure
E.
Assume that
E\left\{\xi\right\}=0
for
all
\xi\in\mathbb{T}\diagdown\left\{1\right\}.
It follows from the
identity (2.2) that if
\left\{a_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded good weight, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}N^{i}x=\left(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\right)E\left\{1\right\}x\text{\ \ in norm }\forall x\in\mathcal{H}\text{.}

Let
G
be a locally compact group with the left Haar measure
m_{G}
. If
H

is a closed subgroup of
G
, then the measure
m_{H}
may be regarded as a
measure on
G
by putting
\overline{m}_{H}\left(E\right)=m_{H}\left(E\cap H\right)
for every Borel subset
E
of
G.
Note that
\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\overline{m}_{H}=H.

One of the main results of the paper is the following.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mu
be a strictly aperiodic measure on a locally compact group
G

and let
\left\{a_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded good weight. The following assertions hold:

\left(a\right)
If
G
is compact, then

w^{\ast}-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mu^{i}=\left(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\right)\overline{m}_{\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right]}.

\left(b\right)
If
\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right]
is not compact, then

w^{\ast}-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mu^{i}=0.

Below, we collect some preliminary results that will needed later on.

Let
G
be a locally compact group. A
representation

\pi
of
G

on a Banach space
X_{\pi}
(the representation space of
\pi)
is a
homomorphism from
G
to the group of invertible isometries on
X_{\pi}.

We assume that
\pi
is strongly continuous. Then, for an arbitrary
\mu\in M\left(G\right)
we can define a bounded linear operator
\widehat{\mu}\left(\pi\right)
on
X_{\pi}
by

\widehat{\mu}\left(\pi\right)x=\int_{G}\pi\left(g\right)xd\mu\left(g\right)\text{, \ }x\in X_{\pi}.

The map
\mu\rightarrow\widehat{\mu}\left(\pi\right)
is linear,
multiplicative, and contractive;
\left\|\widehat{\mu}\left(\pi\right)\right\|\leq\left\|\mu\right\|_{1},
where
\left\|\mu\right\|_{1}
is the total variation norm of
\mu\in M\left(G\right).

By
\widehat{G}
we will denote unitary dual of
G
, the set of all
equivalence classes of irreducible continuous unitary representations of
G

with the Fell topology. Recall that
\pi_{0}\in\widehat{G}
is a limit
point of
M\subset\widehat{G}
in the Fell topology, if the matrix function

g\rightarrow\langle\pi_{0}\left(g\right)x_{0},x_{0}\rangle

\left(x_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{0}}\right)
can be uniformly approximated on
every compact subset of
G
by the matrix functions
g\rightarrow\langle\pi\left(g\right)x,x\rangle

\left(\pi\in M,\text{ }x\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\right)
(in the case when
G
is abelian, Fell topology coincides
with the usual topology of
\widehat{G},
the dual group of
G
). The
function
\pi\rightarrow\widehat{\mu}\left(\pi\right)

\left(\pi\in\widehat{G}\right)
is called
Fourier-Stieltjes transform
of
\mu\in M\left(G\right)
(if
G
is abelian, the Fourier-Stieltjes transform
of
\mu\in M\left(G\right)
will be denoted simply by
\widehat{\mu}
).
If
\widehat{\mu}\left(\pi\right)=0
for all
\pi\in\widehat{G},
then

\mu=0
(for instance see,
[
5
, §18]
). It is well known that if
G

is compact, then every
\pi\in\widehat{G}
is finite dimensional. Also, we
know that if
G
is compact (resp. compact and metrizable) then
\widehat{G}

is discrete (resp. countable). These facts are consequences of the
Peter-Weyl theory
[
17
, Chapter 4]
.

By
B_{X}
and
S_{X}
respectively, we denote the closed unit ball and the
unit sphere of a Banach space
X.
Notice that ext
B_{X}\subseteq S_{X},

where ext
B_{X}
is the set of all extreme points of
B_{X}.
For the sake
of convenience,
X
will be called
A
-
space
if ext
B_{X}=S_{X}
.
For example, uniformly convex Banach spaces, in particular, Hilbert spaces
and
L^{p}

\left(1<p<\infty\right)
spaces are
A
-spaces.

The proof of the following result is similar to the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on a locally compact group
G
and let
\pi
be a continuous Banach representation of
G
. If the representation
space
X_{\pi}
is a Banach
A
-space, then for an arbitrary
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
we have

\ker\left[\widehat{\mu}\left(\pi\right)-\xi I_{\pi}\right]=\left\{x\in X_{\pi}:\pi\left(g\right)x=\xi x,\text{ }\forall g\in\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right\}.

Proof.

Assume that
\widehat{\mu}\left(\pi\right)x=\xi x
for some
x\in X_{\pi}
with
\left\|x\right\|=1
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}.
Let
s\in
\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu
and let
\left\{U_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
be a directed (by
reverse inclusion) basis neighborhood system of
s.
Then
\mu\left(U_{i}\right)>0
for all
i\in I.
There is no restriction to assume that
\mu\left(U_{i}\right)<1
for all
i\in I.
If

x_{i}:=\frac{1}{\mu\left(U_{i}\right)}\int_{U_{i}}\pi\left(g\right)xd\mu\left(g\right)\text{ \ and \ \ }y_{i}:=\frac{1}{1-\mu\left(U_{i}\right)}\int_{G\diagdown U_{i}}\pi\left(g\right)xd\mu\left(g\right),

then
\left\|x_{i}\right\|\leq 1
and
\left\|y_{i}\right\|\leq 1
. The identity

\xi x=\mu\left(U_{i}\right)x_{i}+\left[1-\mu\left(U_{i}\right)\right]y_{i}

shows that
\xi x
is a convex combination of
x_{i}
and
y_{i}.
Since
\left\|\xi x\right\|=1,
we have
\xi x=x_{i}
for all
i\in I
and
therefore,

\frac{1}{\mu\left(U_{i}\right)}\int_{U_{i}}\left[\pi\left(g\right)x-\xi x\right]d\mu\left(g\right)=0\text{, \ }\forall i\in I.

As
U_{i}\rightarrow\left\{s\right\}
we obtain
\pi\left(s\right)x=\xi x.

Let

have the following.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\mu
be a strictly aperiodic measure on a locally compact group
G

and let
\pi
be a continuous unitary representation of
G
on a Hilbert
space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
. If
\left\{a_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded good weight, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\widehat{\mu}\left(\pi\right)^{i}x=\left(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\right)P_{\mu}^{\pi}x\text{ \ in norm }\forall x\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\text{,}

where
P_{\mu}^{\pi}
is the orthogonal projection onto

\left\{x\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}:\pi\left(g\right)x=x:\forall g\in\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right]\right\}.

In addition if
\mu
is adapted and
\pi\in\widehat{G}\diagdown id,
then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\widehat{\mu}\left(\pi\right)^{i}x=0\text{ \ in norm }\forall x\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}.

Let
G
be a locally compact group. For an arbitrary
f\in L^{p}\left(G\right)

\left(1<p<\infty\right),
we put
f^{\vee}\left(g\right):=f\left(g^{-1}\right)
and
\widetilde{f}:=\overline{f\left(g^{-1}\right)}
. Notice that for every
u,\upsilon\in L^{2}\left(G\right),
the function
u\ast\widetilde{\upsilon}
is in
C_{0}\left(G\right)

and

\langle\mu,u\ast\widetilde{\upsilon}\rangle=\langle\mu\ast\overline{\upsilon},\overline{u}\rangle\text{, \ }\forall\mu\in M\left(G\right).

It follows that
\left\{u\ast\widetilde{\upsilon}:u,\upsilon\in L^{2}\left(G\right)\right\}
is linearly dense in
C_{0}\left(G\right).

Notice also that if
f\in L^{p}\left(G\right)

\left(p\neq 2\right)

and
h\in L^{q}\left(G\right)
(
1/p+1/q=1
), then
h\ast f^{\vee}\in C_{0}\left(G\right)
and

\langle\mu,h\ast f^{\vee}\rangle=\langle\mu\ast f,h\rangle,\text{ \ }\forall\mu\in M\left(G\right).

It follows that
\left\{h\ast f^{\vee}:h\in L^{q}\left(G\right),\text{ }f\in L^{p}\left(G\right)\right\}
is linearly dense in
C_{0}\left(G\right).

Let
\pi
be the left regular representation of
G
on
L^{p}\left(G\right)

\left(1\leq p<\infty\right)
, where

\pi\left(g\right)f\left(s\right)=f\left(g^{-1}s\right):=f_{g}\left(s\right).

Then,
\pi
is continuous and
\widehat{\mu}\left(\pi\right)f=\mu\ast f
for every
\mu\in M\left(G\right).
We will denote this operator by
\lambda_{p}\left(\mu\right).
It is well known that the left convolution
operator
\lambda_{p}\left(\mu\right)f:=\mu\ast f
is a bounded linear
operator on
L^{p}\left(G\right),
that is,

\left\|\lambda_{p}\left(\mu\right)f\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|\mu\right\|_{1}\left\|f\right\|_{p}\text{ \ and \ }\left\|\lambda_{1}\left(\mu\right)\right\|_{1}=\left\|\mu\right\|_{1}.

From the identity (2.2) and Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4, we obtain the following.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\mu
be a strictly aperiodic measure on a locally compact group
G
.
If
\left\{a_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mu^{i}\ast f=\left(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\right)P_{\mu}f\text{ \ in }L^{2}\text{-norm }\forall f\in L^{2}\left(G\right),

\left\{f\in L^{2}\left(G\right):f_{g}=f:\forall g\in\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right]\right\}.

If
\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right]

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mu^{i}\ast f=0\text{ \ in }L^{2}\text{-norm for every }f\in L^{2}\left(G\right).

It follows from Lemma 2.3 that if
\mu\in M\left(G\right)
is a
probability measure and

then for every

,

\ker\left[\lambda_{p}\left(\mu\right)-\xi I\right]=\left\{f\in L^{p}\left(G\right):f_{g}=\xi f,\text{ }\forall g\in\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right\}.

\left(1<p<\infty\right)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\displaystyle\sum}\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left|\langle T^{n}x,\varphi\rangle\right|=0\text{ \ for all }x\in X\text{
and }\varphi\in X^{\ast}.

Hence we have the following.

Corollary 2.7
.

If
\mu\in M\left(G\right)
is a probability measure with non-compact
support, then for every
f\in L^{p}\left(G\right)

\left(1<p<\infty\right)
and

\left(1/p+1/q=1\right),
we

It follows from the spectral mapping theorem that
\sigma\left(\lambda_{1}\left(\nu\right)\right)\cap\mathbb{T\subseteq}\left\{1\right\}.
By Proposition 3.1, there exists an idempotent measure
\theta\in M\left(G\right)
such that w
∗
-
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu^{n}=\theta.

As in the proof of Proposition 2.12 we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\nu^{n}\ast f\right)=\theta\ast f\text{
\ weakly for every }f\in L^{p}\left(G\right).

On the other hand, by the Katznelson-Tzafriri theorem,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\lambda_{1}\left(\nu\right)^{n+1}-\lambda_{1}\left(\nu\right)^{n}\right\|=0.

Now, it follows from Proposition 2.1 that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\nu^{n}\ast f\right)=\theta\ast f\text{\ \ in }L^{p}\text{-norm.}

As we have noted above, if
\mu
is a strictly aperiodic measure on a
non-compact locally compact group
G,
then w
∗
-
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu^{n}=0

[
3
, Théorème 8]
. In
[
18
, Theorem 2]
it
was proved that if
\mu
is an adapted measure on a second countable
locally compact non-compact group
G
, then w
∗
-
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu^{n}=0
.

As is well known, equipped with the involution given by
d\widetilde{\mu}\left(g\right)=\overline{d\mu\left(g^{-1}\right)}
, the algebra
M\left(G\right)
becomes a Banach
\ast
-algebra. If
\mu
is a
probability measure on a locally compact group
G,
then as
\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\widetilde{\mu}=\left(\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right)^{-1}
, we have

\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu\right)=\overline{\left\{\left(\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right)^{-1}\cdot\left(\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right)\right\}}

(for instance see,
[
11
, Theorem 2.2.2]
).

The following result is certainly known, but we have not been able to find a
precise reference.

Proposition 3.3
.

A measure
\mu\in M\left(G\right)
is strictly aperiodic if and only if
the measure
\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu
is adapted.

Proof.

Assume that the measure
\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu
is not adapted. This
means that

\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu\right)\right]:=H\neq G.

It follows that
g^{-1}\cdot\left(\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right)\subseteq H
or
\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\subseteq gH
for all
g\in\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu.
This shows that

\mu
is not strictly aperiodic. Now, assume that
\mu
is not strictly
aperiodic. Then
\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\subseteq gH
for some closed proper
subgroup
H
of
G
and
g\in G
. It follows that
\left(\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right)^{-1}\subseteq Hg^{-1}
and therefore
\left(\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right)^{-1}\cdot\left(\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\mu\right)\subseteq H.
Thus we have

\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu\right)\right]\subseteq H\neq G.

This shows that the measure
\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu
is not adapted.

Next, we have the following.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on a locally compact group
G.
If one
of the subgroups
\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu\right)\right]
and
\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\mu\ast\widetilde{\mu}\right)\right]
is not compact, then

w^{\ast}-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu^{n}=0.

Proof.

Recall that a contraction
T
on a Hilbert space is said to be
completely non-unitary
if it has no proper reducing subspace on which it
acts as a unitary operator. By the Nagy-Foiaş theorem
[
7
, Chapter II,
Theorem 3.9]
if
T
is a completely non-unitary contraction, then
T^{n}\rightarrow 0
in the weak operator topology. Now, assume that
\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu\right)\right]
is not compact.
Let us show that
\lambda_{2}\left(\mu\right)
is a completely
non-unitary contraction. Since
\lambda_{2}\left(\mu\right)^{\ast}=\lambda_{2}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\right),
it suffices to show that
\lambda_{2}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\right)\lambda_{2}\left(\mu\right)f=f
, where
f\in L^{2}\left(G\right)
implies
f=0.
By Lemma 2.3,

\displaystyle\left\{f\in L^{2}\left(G\right):\lambda_{2}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\right)\lambda_{2}\left(\mu\right)f=f\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{f\in L^{2}\left(G\right):\lambda_{2}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu\right)f=f\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{f\in L^{2}\left(G\right):f_{g}=f\text{, }\forall g\in\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu\right)\right]\right\}.

Since
\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu\right)\right]

is not compact, from the identity
f_{g}=f
for all
g\in\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu\right)\right]
, we have
f=0
(a.e.).
Hence
\lambda_{2}\left(\mu\right)
is a completely non-unitary
contraction. Consequently,
\lambda_{2}\left(\mu\right)^{n}\rightarrow 0

in the weak operator topology. Then, for an arbitrary
u,\upsilon\in L^{2}\left(G\right)
we can write

\langle\mu^{n},u\ast\widetilde{\upsilon}\rangle=\langle\mu^{n}\ast\overline{\upsilon},\overline{u}\rangle=\langle\lambda_{2}\left(\mu\right)^{n}\overline{\upsilon},\overline{u}\rangle\rightarrow 0.

Since the set
\left\{u\ast\widetilde{\upsilon}:u,\upsilon\in L^{2}\left(G\right)\right\}
is linearly dense in
C_{0}\left(G\right),

we have
\langle\mu^{n},f\rangle\rightarrow 0
for all
f\in C_{0}\left(G\right).
Hence w
∗
-
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu^{n}=0.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on a locally compact group
G.
If one
of the subgroups
\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu\right)\right]
and
\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\mu\ast\widetilde{\mu}\right)\right]
is not compact, then

\mu^{n}\ast f\rightarrow 0\text{ \ weakly for all }f\in L^{p}\left(G\right)\text{ }\left(1<p<\infty\right).

Proof.

If
f\in L^{p}\left(G\right)
and
h\in L^{q}\left(G\right)

(1/p+1/q=1),
then
h\ast f^{\vee}\in C_{0}\left(G\right)
. By Theorem
3.4, w
∗
-
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu^{n}=0
and therefore we
have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle\mu^{n}\ast f,h\rangle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle\mu^{n},h\ast f^{\vee}\rangle=0.

However, we have the following.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on a locally compact group
G
and
assume that one of the subgroups
\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu\right)\right]
and
\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\mu\ast\widetilde{\mu}\right)\right]
is not compact. If the Lebesgue measure of
\sigma\left(\lambda_{2}\left(\mu\right)\right)\cap\mathbb{T}
is zero, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\mu^{n}\ast f\right\|_{p}=0,\text{
\ }\forall f\in L^{p}\left(G\right)\text{ }\left(1<p<\infty\right).

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem 3.4 we can see that if one of the subgroups
\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu\right)\right]
and
\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\mu\ast\widetilde{\mu}\right)\right]
is not
compact, then
\lambda_{2}\left(\mu\right)
is a completely non-unitary
contraction. On the other hand, Nagy-Foias theorem
[
7
, Chapter II,
Proposition 6.7]
states that if
T
is a completely non-unitary
contraction on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and if
\sigma\left(T\right)\cap\mathbb{T}
is of the Lebesgue measure zero, then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T^{n}x\right\|=0
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}.

Therefore we have
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\mu^{n}\ast f\right\|_{2}=0
for all
f\in L^{2}\left(G\right).
Now, assume that
p\neq 2.
Let
C_{c}\left(G\right)
be the space of all complex valued
continuous functions on
G
with compact support and let
f\in C_{c}\left(G\right).
Then,
\nu\ast f\in L^{p}\left(G\right)
for any
\nu\in M\left(G\right)
and for all
1\leq p\leq\infty.
By the Riesz-Thorin
Convexity Theorem
[
6
, Chapter VI, Lemma 10.9]
,
\alpha\mapsto\log\left\|\nu\ast f\right\|_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
is a convex function
on
\left[0,1\right]
. Let
q
be chosen such that
q>p
for
p>2
and
1<q<p
for
1<p<2.
If
\lambda:=\frac{2q-2p}{pq-2p},
then
0<\lambda<1

and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\lambda}{q}+\frac{\lambda}{2}.
Consequently, we
can write

\left\|\nu\ast f\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|\nu\ast f\right\|_{q}^{1-\lambda}\left\|\nu\ast f\right\|_{2}^{\lambda},\text{ \ }\forall\nu\in M\left(G\right).

Hence we have

\left\|\mu^{n}\ast f\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|\mu^{n}\ast f\right\|_{q}^{1-\lambda}\left\|\mu^{n}\ast f\right\|_{2}^{\lambda}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{q}^{1-\lambda}\left\|\mu^{n}\ast f\right\|_{2}^{\lambda},\text{ \ }\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

It follows that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\mu^{n}\ast f\right\|_{p}=0
for all
f\in C_{c}\left(G\right).
Since
C_{c}\left(G\right)
is dense in
L^{p}\left(G\right),
we have
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\mu^{n}\ast f\right\|_{p}=0
for
all
f\in L^{p}\left(G\right).

Remark 3.7
.

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group and
\mu\in M\left(G\right).

The Fourier-Plancherel transform estabilishes a unitary equivalence between
the convolution operator
\lambda_{2}\left(\mu\right)
on
L^{2}\left(G\right)
and the multiplication operator
M_{\widehat{\mu}}
on
L^{2}\left(\widehat{G}\right)
. Therefore, we have
\sigma\left(\lambda_{2}\left(\mu\right)\right)=\overline{\widehat{\mu}\left(\widehat{G}\right)}.
If
\mathcal{E}_{\mu}:=\left\{\chi\in\widehat{G}:\left|\widehat{\mu}\left(\chi\right)\right|=1\right\}
, then
\mathcal{E}_{\mu}=\mathcal{F}_{\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu}
. Notice also that
\widehat{\mu}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mu}\right)\subseteq\sigma\left(\lambda_{2}\left(\mu\right)\right)\cap\mathbb{T}
. If
\mu
is a
probability measure, then
\mathcal{E}_{\mu}
is a closed subgroup of
\widehat{G},
that is,
\mathcal{E}_{\mu}=\left[\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\widetilde{\mu}\ast\mu\right)\right]^{\bot}

(
see, Remark 2.11
).

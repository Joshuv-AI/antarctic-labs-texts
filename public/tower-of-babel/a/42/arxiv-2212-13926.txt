Abstract
For a positive integer r r , George Andrews proved that the set of partitions of n n in which odd multiplicities are at least 2 ​ r + 1 2r+1 is equinumerous with the set of partitions of n n in which odd parts are congruent to 2 ​ r + 1 2r+1 modulo 4 ​ r + 2 4r+2 . This was given as an extension of MacMahon’s theorem ( r = 1 r=1 ). Andrews, Ericksson, Petrov and Romik gave a bijective proof of MacMahon’s theorem. Despite several bijections being given, until recently, none of them was in the spirit of Andrews-Ericksson-Petrov-Romik bijection. Andrews’ theorem has also been extended recently. Our goal is to give a generalized bijective mapping of this further extension in the spirit of Andrews-Ericksson-Petrov-Romik bijection.



A note on Andrews-MacMahon theorem

1
Introduction

A partition of
n
is a representation
\lambda=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{s}
in which the summands
\lambda_{i}
’s are integers such that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}=n
and
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{s}
. The summands are called
parts
of
\lambda
and the total sum
n
is called the
weight
of
\lambda
, denoted by
|\lambda|
. A more convinient way of presenting
\lambda
is to write

\lambda=(\mu_{1}^{m_{1}},\mu_{2}^{m_{2}},\ldots\mu_{\ell}^{m_{\ell}})

where
m_{i}
is the multiplicity of the part
\mu_{i}
(the number of times
\mu_{i}
appears) and
\mu_{1}>\mu_{2}>\cdots>\mu_{\ell}
. Instead of considering any partition of fixed weight, sometimes, restrictions are imposed on the multiplicities as well as the parts themselves. When this happens, one may discover interesting identities. A classical example of this is the following theorem due to P. A. MacMahon.

Theorem 1.1

(MacMahon,
[
4
]
)
.

The number of partitions of
n
in which odd multiplicities are greater than 1 is equal to the number of partitions of
n
in which odd parts are congruent to
3\pmod{6}
.

There are a couple of bijections for MacMahon’s theorem and one of them was given by Andrews, Ericksson, Petrov, and Romik
[
2
]
. We shall call it the
Andrews-Ericksson-Petrov-Romik bijection
and describe it in the sequel.

Andrews-Ericksson-Petrov-Romik Bijection

Let
A(n)
denote the set of partitions of
n
in which odd multiplicities are greater than 1 and let
B(n)
denote the set of partitions of
n
in which odd parts are congruent to
3\pmod{6}
.

Let
\lambda=(l^{h_{l}},(l-1)^{h_{l-1}},\ldots,3^{h_{3}},2^{h_{2}},1^{h_{1}})\in A(n)
where
h_{i}
is the multiplicity of
i
. Since no part appears exactly once, it follows that
h_{i}\in\{0,2,3,4,\ldots\}
. Uniquely decompose
h_{i}
as

h_{i}=k_{i}+g_{i},\,\,\text{where}\,\,\,k_{i}\in\{0,3\},\,\,g_{i}\in\{0,2,4,6,8,\ldots\}.

For
j\geq 1
, define
d_{j}
as follows:

\displaystyle d_{6t+1}

\displaystyle=d_{6t+5}=0,

\displaystyle d_{6t+2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}g_{3t+1},

\displaystyle d_{6t+4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}g_{3t+2},

\displaystyle d_{6t+3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}k_{2t+1}+g_{6t+3},

\displaystyle d_{6t+6}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}k_{2t+2}+g_{6t+6},

where
t=0,1,2,\ldots

The partition
(f^{d_{f}},(f-1)^{d_{f-1}},\ldots,2^{d_{2}},1^{d_{1}})
is in
B(n)
and the transformation is invertible.

Andrews gave the following generalisation of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2

(Andrews,
[
1
]
)
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. The set of partitions of
n
in which parts with odd multiplicity appear at least
2r+1
times is equinumerous with the set of partitions of
n
in which parts are even or congruent to
2r+1\pmod{4r+2}
.

Bijective proofs for Theorem
1.2
have been given (see
[
3
]
and the references therein). However, a bijection for this theorem that extends Andrews-Ericksson-Petrov-Romik bijection has been given recently
[
5
]
.
In
[
6
]
, Theorem
1.2
was extended as follows.

Theorem 1.3

(Nyirenda, Mugwangwavari,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
a,p\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\gcd(a,p)=1
and
a<p
. The number of partitions of
n
in which multiplicities congruent to
ja\pmod{p}
are at least
j(pr+a)
for
j=0,1,2\ldots,p-1
is equal to the number of partitions of
n
wherein parts not divisible by
p
are congruent to
-s(pr+a)\pmod{p^{2}r+pa}
where
s=1,2,\ldots,p-1
.

The authors, not only provided this extention of Andrews’ theorem but also gave a bijection which naturally extends a bijection of Fu and Sellers
[
6
]
. However, finding a bijection for this extension that is in the spirit of the Andrews-Ericksson-Petrov-Romik Bijection is still an open question.

In this paper we completely settle this question by providing a bijective proof of Theorem
1.3
that is reminiscent of Andrews-Ericksson-Petrov-Romik bijection. The mapping we give also generalizes the bijection in
[
5
]
.

2
The generalized bijection

Let
\tilde{A}(n)
be the set of partitions of
n
in which multiplicities congruent to
ja\pmod{p}
are at least
j(pr+a)
for
j=0,1,2\ldots,p-1
and
\tilde{B}(n)
denote the set of partitions of
n
wherein parts not divisible by
p
are congruent to
-s(pr+a)\pmod{p^{2}r+pa}
where
s=1,2,\ldots,p-1
.

Suppose that
\lambda=(l^{h_{l}},(l-1)^{h_{l-1}},\ldots,3^{h_{3}},2^{h_{2}},1^{h_{1}})\in\tilde{A}(n)
, Here,
h_{i}
is the multiplicity of
i
.

We can decompose
h_{i}
uniquely as
h_{i}=k_{i}+g_{i}
where

k_{i}=(pr+a)v_{i}\,\,\text{and}\,\,g_{i}=h_{i}-(pr+a)v_{i}.

The integer
v_{i}
is the least positive residue of
a^{-1}h_{i}
modulo
p
. Here
a^{-1}
is the inverse of
a
modulo
p
(it exists because
\gcd(a,p)=1
).
Since
0\leq v_{i}\leq p-1
, we must have

k_{i}\in\{0,pr+a,2(pr+a),\ldots,(p-1)(pr+a)\}

and

g_{i}\in\{0,p,2p,3p,4p,5p,\ldots\}.

Define
d_{j}
as follows:

\displaystyle d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-a}

\displaystyle=0,j\in\{1,2,3,\ldots,pr+a\}\setminus\{(p-1)r+a\}

\displaystyle d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-2a}

\displaystyle=0,j\in\{1,2,3,\ldots,pr+a\}\setminus\{(p-2)r+a\}

\displaystyle d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-3a}

\displaystyle=0,j\in\{1,2,3,\ldots,pr+a\}\setminus\{(p-3)r+a\}

\displaystyle\quad\vdots

\displaystyle\quad\vdots

\displaystyle d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-(p-1)a}

\displaystyle=0,j\in\{1,2,3,\ldots,pr+a\}\setminus\{r+a\},

d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj}=\frac{1}{p}g_{(pr+a)t+j},j=1,2,3,\ldots,pr+a-1

and

d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(p-j)(pr+a)}=\frac{1}{pr+a}k_{pt+p-j}+g_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)},j=1,2,3,\ldots,p

with
t=0,1,2\ldots
.

Let
\mu=(\ldots,i^{d_{i}},(i-1)^{d_{i-1}},(i-2)^{d_{i-2}},\ldots 2^{d_{2}},1^{d_{1}})
. We claim that
\mu\in\tilde{B}(n)
. To see this, observe that, if
f
is a positive integer such that
f=(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-ia
for some
t\geq 0
, and
1\leq i\leq p-1
with
j\in\{0,1,2,3,\ldots,pr+a\}\setminus\{(p-i)r+a\}
, then

f\equiv-ia\pmod{p}
so that
f\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Also, observe that

\displaystyle(p^{2}r+pa)t+p((p-i)r+a)-ia

\displaystyle=(p^{2}r+pa)t+p^{2}r-ipr+pa-ia

\displaystyle=(p^{2}r+pa)(t+1)-i(pr+a)

\displaystyle\equiv-i(pr+a)\pmod{p^{2}r+pa}.

Since
j\neq(p-i)r+a
, we conclude that
f
is not congruent to
-s(pr+a)\pmod{p^{2}r+pa}
with
1\leq s\leq p-1
. Thus,
d_{f}=0
implies that such integers cannot appear as parts, which is part of the description of partitions in
\tilde{B}(n)
.

Note that integers congruent to
0\pmod{p}
are allowed to appear, and these integers are precisely
those of the form

(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj\,\,\,\text{where}\,\,\,j=0,1,2,\ldots,pr+a-1\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,t\geq 0.

By definition of
d_{j}
’s, one can check that

d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{p}g_{pr+a)t+j},&\text{if $j=1,2,\ldots,pr+a-1$;}\\
\\
\frac{1}{pr+a}k_{pt}+g_{(p^{2}r+pa)t},&\text{if $j=0$,}\end{cases}

which is not necessarily 0, i.e. the multiplicity of a positive integer congruent to 0 modulo
p
may be 0 or not.
Indeed,
\mu\in\tilde{B}(n)
.

Define a mapping
\phi:\tilde{A}(n)\rightarrow\tilde{B}(n)
by
\lambda\mapsto\mu
. In the next section, we prove that
\phi
is a bijection from
\tilde{A}(n)
onto
\tilde{B}(n)
.

3
Proof of the bijection

We first show that
\phi
preserves weight, i.e.
|\mu|=n
. Now

\displaystyle|\mu|

\displaystyle=|(\ldots,i^{d_{i}},(i-1)^{d_{i-1}},(i-2)^{d_{i-2}},\ldots 2^{d_{2}},1^{d_{1}})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}id_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{(p-1)r+a-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-a\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-a}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=(p-1)r+a+1}^{pr+a}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-a\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-a}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=1}^{(p-2)r+a-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-2a\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-2a}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=(p-2)r+a+1}^{pr+a}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-2a\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-2a}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=1}^{(p-3)r+a-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-3a\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-3a}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=(p-3)r+a+1}^{pr+a}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-3a\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-3a}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=1}^{(p-4)r+a-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-4a\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-4a}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=(p-4)r+a+1}^{pr+a}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-4a\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-4a}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\qquad\vdots

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=1}^{r+a-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-(p-1)a\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-(p-1)a}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=r+a+1}^{pr+a}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-(p-1)a\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-(p-1)a}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=1}^{pr+a-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+(p-j)(pr+a)\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(p-j)(pr+a)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\sum_{j=1}^{(p-i)r+a-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-ia\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-ia}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\sum_{j=(p-i)r+a+1}^{pr+a}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-ia\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj-ia}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=1}^{pr+a-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+(p-j)(pr+a)\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(p-j)(pr+a)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{pr+a-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+pj}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+(p-j)(pr+a)\right)d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(p-j)(pr+a)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{pr+a-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+pj\right)\frac{1}{p}g_{pr+a)t+j}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+(p-j)(pr+a)\right)\left(\frac{1}{pr+a}k_{pt+p-j}+g_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{pr+a-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((pr+a)t+j\right)g_{(pr+a)t+j}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left(pt+p-j\right)k_{pt+p-j}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+(p-j)(pr+a)\right)g_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}.

For simplification purposes, define summation operators
G
and
H
as follows:

G(x,y)=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left(xt+y\right)g_{xt+y}

and

K(x,y)=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left(xt+y\right)k_{xt+y}.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((pr+a)t+j\right)g_{pr+a)t+j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+j+(pr+a)\ell\right)g_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+j+(pr+a)\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}G(p^{2}r+pa,j+(pr+a)\ell),

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left(pt+p-j\right)k_{pt+p-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{pr+a-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+p-j+p\ell\right)k_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+p-j+p\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{pr+a-1}K(p^{2}r+pa,p-j+p\ell)

and

\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+(p-j)(pr+a)\right)g_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}=G(p^{2}r+pa,(pr+a)(p-j)).

Hence,

\displaystyle|\mu|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{pr+a-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}G(p^{2}r+pa,j+(pr+a)\ell)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{\ell=0}^{pr+a-1}K(p^{2}r+pa,p-j+p\ell)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{j=1}^{p}G(p^{2}r+pa,(pr+a)(p-j))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{pr+a-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}G(p^{2}r+pa,j+(pr+a)\ell)+\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{pr+a-1}K(p^{2}r+pa,j+p\ell)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\,\,\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}G(p^{2}r+pa,(pr+a)\ell)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{pr+a-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}G(p^{2}r+pa,j+(pr+a)\ell)+\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{pr+a-1}K(p^{2}r+pa,j+p\ell)

Since modulo
pr+a
, the residues are
0,1,2,\ldots pr+a-1
, we have

\displaystyle\{j+(pr+a)\ell:0\leq j\leq pr+a-1,0\leq\ell\leq p-1\}

\displaystyle=\{0,1,2,\ldots,pr+a-1+(pr+a)(p-1)\}

\displaystyle=\{0,1,2,3,\ldots,p^{2}r+pa-1\}.

Similarly, modulo
p
, the residues are
0,1,2,\ldots,p-1
and so we have

\displaystyle\{j+p\ell:0\leq j\leq p-1,0\leq\ell\leq pr+a-1\}

\displaystyle=\{0,1,2,\ldots,p-1+p(pr+a-1)\}

\displaystyle=\{0,1,2,3,\ldots,p^{2}r+pa-1\}.

Thus,

\{j+(pr+a)\ell:0\leq j\leq pr+a-1,0\leq\ell\leq p-1\}=\{j+p\ell:0\leq j\leq p-1,0\leq\ell\leq pr+a-1\}

which implies that

\displaystyle|\mu|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p^{2}r+pa-1}G(p^{2}r+pa,j)+\sum_{j=0}^{p^{2}r+pa-1}K(p^{2}r+pa,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p^{2}r+pa-1}\left(G(p^{2}r+pa,j)+K(p^{2}r+pa,j)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p^{2}r+pa-1}\left(\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(p^{2}r+pa)t+j)g_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+j}+\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}((p^{2}r+pa)t+j)k_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p^{2}r+pa-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left((p^{2}r+pa)t+j)g_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+j}+((p^{2}r+pa)t+j)k_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p^{2}r+pa-1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(p^{2}r+pa)t+j)h_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+j)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}ih_{i}

\displaystyle=|\lambda|.

Finally, the transformation
\phi
is reversible and its inverse is given as follows:

Let
\mu=(\ldots,i^{d_{i}},(i-1)^{d_{i-1}},(i-2)^{d_{i-2}},\ldots 2^{d_{2}},1^{d_{1}})
. For
j=1,2,\ldots,pr+a-1
, we define

g_{(pr+a)t+j}=pd_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+j},t\geq 0

and for
j=0
, i.e.
g_{(pr+a)t}
, we use:

For
j=1,2,3,\ldots,p
,

g_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}=\begin{cases}d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)},&\text{if $d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}\equiv 0\pmod{p}$};\\
\\
d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}-1,&\text{if $d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}\equiv 1\pmod{p}$};\\
\\
d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}-2,&\text{if $d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}\equiv 2\pmod{p}$};\\
\\
d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}-3,&\text{if $d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}\equiv 3\pmod{p}$};\\
\\
\quad\qquad\qquad\vdots&\quad\qquad\qquad\vdots\\
d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}-(p-1),&\text{if $d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}\equiv-1\pmod{p}$}\end{cases}

and

k_{pt+p-j}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}\equiv 0\pmod{p}$};\\
\\
pr+a,&\text{if $d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}\equiv 1\pmod{p}$};\\
\\
2(pr+a),&\text{if $d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}\equiv 2\pmod{p}$};\\
\\
3(pr+a),&\text{if $d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}\equiv 3\pmod{p}$};\\
\\
\quad\qquad\vdots&\quad\qquad\qquad\vdots\\
(p-1)(pr+a),&\text{if $d_{(p^{2}r+pa)t+(pr+a)(p-j)}\equiv p-1\pmod{p}$}.\end{cases}

Then the partition

\lambda=(\ldots,v^{k_{v}+g_{v}},(v-1)^{k_{v-1}+g_{v-1}},(v-2)^{k_{v-2}+g_{v-2}},\ldots,2^{k_{2}+g_{2}},1^{k_{1}+g_{1}})

is in
\tilde{A}(n)
.

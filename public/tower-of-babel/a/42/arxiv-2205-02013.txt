A Simple Nonconforming Tetrahedral Element for the Stokes Equations
Peter Hansbo, Mats G. Larson

Abstract
In this paper we apply a nonconforming rotated bilinear tetrahedral element to the Stokes problem in ℝ 3 {\mathbb{R}}^{3} . We show that the element is stable in combination with a piecewise linear, continuous, approximation of the pressure. This gives an approximation similar to the well known continuous P 2 P^{2} – P 1 P^{1} Taylor–Hood element, but with fewer degrees of freedom. The element is a stable non–conforming low order element which fulfils Korn’s inequality, leading to stability also in the case where the Stokes equations are written on stress form for use in the case of free surface flow.

INTRODUCTION

The nonconforming rotated
Q_{1}
tetrahedron is derived from the nonconforming
hexahedral element proposed by Rannacher and Turek
[
6
]
and was applied to linear and nonlinear elasticity problems in
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
.
It has properties similar to the hexahedral element, with improved bending behaviour in elasticity, compared to the
P^{1}
tetrahedron, and it allows for diagonal mass lumping for explicit time–stepping in dynamic problems.

In this paper, we investigate its properties as a Stokes element, and show that in combination with linear, continuous pressures, it
is
inf–sup
stable. In a sense it is thus a reduced Taylor–Hood
[
7
]
element with fewer degrees of freedom. It is one of the lowest order elements that is stable for Stokes, and, unlike some other low order non–conforming elements
[
2
,
6
]
, it fulfills Korn’s inequality and can thus handle the strain form of Stokes, cf.
[
3
]
.

An outline of the paper is as follows: in Section
2
we recall the rotated
Q_{1}
element; in Section 3 we apply it to the Stokes equations and prove stability and convergence;
in Section
4
we give some numerical examples to show the properties of the approximation.

2
THE ROTATED
Q_{1}
APPROXIMATION

In order to define a low order approximation which is stable for the Stokes problem, Rannacher and Turek
[
6
]
constructed
a hexahedral element with nodes on the faces. Two different kinds of continuity can now be imposed: point-wise continuity and
average continuity and we will in this paper consider point-wise continuity. The Rannacher-Turek element may be viewed as an
extension of the classical Crouzeix-Raviart nonconforming tetrahedral element
[
2
]
, for which point-wise and average
continuity is identical.

The difficulty in the construction of the Rannacher-Turek element compared to the Crouzeix-Raviart element
is that we have six degrees of freedom while the dimension of the space of linears is four, trilinears eight, and quadratics ten. Therefore
we shall start with linears and add two suitable quadratic functions in such a way that the nodal mapping is invertible. To that end let

\{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
be an orthonormal coordinate system
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and let
\hat{K}=[-1,1]^{3}
be the reference cube. The midpoints of the faces of
\hat{K}
takes the form
\{{\boldsymbol{x}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{6}=\{\pm{\boldsymbol{e}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
, and therefore has one coordinate equal to
\pm 1
and the other two equal to
0
. See Fig.
1

for the enumeration of the midpoints. We now seek a space
{V}(\hat{K})
of shape functions on
\hat{K}
such that
{P}_{1}(\hat{K})\subset{V}(\hat{K})\subset P_{2}(\hat{K})
and that has the values at the six midpoints
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{6}
of the faces as degrees of freedom. The linear functions
{P}_{1}(\hat{K})=\text{span}(1,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
is a four dimensional vector space and therefore we need to add two quadratic polynomials to obtain a six dimensional space. To find these quadratic polynomials let
N:V(\hat{K})\ni v\mapsto[v({\boldsymbol{x}}_{i})]_{i=1}^{6}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{6}
be the nodal mapping and note that
N(x_{i}x_{j})={\boldsymbol{0}}
, for
i\neq j
,
and therefore we are restricted to adding polynomials in
\text{span}(x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2},x_{3}^{2})
. Now
N(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2})=N(1)
and
it is therefore natural to consider the two dimensional space

R_{2}(\hat{K})=\{v\in\text{span}(x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2},x_{3}^{2}):N(v)\cdot N(1)=0\}=\text{span}(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2},x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}^{2})

(1)

where the two (non unique) basis functions on the right hand side is easily chosen by observing that

N(b_{1}x_{1}^{2}+b_{2}x_{2}^{2}+b_{3}x_{3}^{2})\cdot N(1)=2(b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3})
. We define

\boxed{V(\hat{K})=P_{1}(\hat{K})+R_{2}(\hat{K})=\text{span}(1,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2},x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}^{2})}

(2)

and verify by explicit calculation that the coordinate mapping
N:V(\hat{K})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{6}
is indeed invertible. Solving for Lagrange basis functions
\varphi_{i}
such that
N(\varphi_{i})=[\delta_{ij}]_{j=1}^{6}
, gives

V(\hat{K})=\text{span}(\varphi_{i})_{i=1}^{6}

(3)

where

\begin{array}[]{>{\displaystyle}l}\varphi_{1}=\varphi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\\[8.53581pt]
\varphi_{2}=\varphi(-x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\\[8.53581pt]
\varphi_{3}=\varphi(x_{2},x_{1},x_{3})\\[8.53581pt]
\varphi_{4}=\varphi(-x_{2},x_{1},x_{3})\\[8.53581pt]
\varphi_{5}=\varphi(x_{3},x_{1},x_{2})\\[8.53581pt]
\varphi_{6}=\varphi(-x_{3},x_{1},x_{2})\end{array}

(4)

and

\varphi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\frac{1}{6}(1+3x_{1}+2x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}^{2})

(5)

The terminology rotated
Q_{1}
elements is motivated by the fact that
x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}=(x_{1}-x_{2})(x_{1}+x_{2})=\xi_{1}\xi_{2}
where
\xi_{1}=x_{1}-x_{2}
and
\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}
are the degrees of freedom in a coordinate system rotated
\pi/2
around the
{\boldsymbol{e}}_{3}
axis.

In
[
3
]
, we made the observation that there is a reference tetrahedron
\hat{T}

inscribed in the reference hexahedron
\hat{K}
, with edges that are diagonals of the faces of
\hat{K}
, and thus
the midpoints of the faces of
\hat{K}
are precisely the midpoints of the edges of
\hat{T}
, see Fig.
1
.
We note that for the reference element all edges have the same length and the centre of gravity is the origin. Using an
affine map
F:\hat{T}\rightarrow T
, we map mid side nodes in the reference configuration to mid edge nodes in the
physical configuration.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}:=\{T\}
be a conforming, shape regular tetrahedrization of
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with mesh parameter

h\in(0,h_{0}]
. We also let
\mathcal{F}_{h}
be the set of faces and
\mathcal{E}_{h}
the set of edges. We make the standard assumption
for the Taylor–Hood approximation
[
1
]
, that

every
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
has at least three internal edges

(6)

We define the non-conforming finite element space

\boxed{V_{h}:=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):v|_{T}\circ F\in V(\hat{T})\;\;T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\text{$v$ is continuous in the midpoints of $E\in\mathcal{E}_{h,I}$}\}}

(7)

with midpoint continuity for all interior edges. We note that
\text{dim}(V_{h})=|\mathcal{E}_{h}|
, the number of edges in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

3
APPLICATION TO THE STOKES EQUATIONS

3.1
Problem Formulation and Finite Element Approximation

We consider the Stokes equations in a domain

\Omega
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
: find the velocity

{\boldsymbol{u}}=\left[u_{i}\right]_{i=1}^{3}
and the pressure
p
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\Delta{\boldsymbol{u}}+\nabla p&=&{\boldsymbol{f}}\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$}\\
\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}&=&\textbf{0}\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$}\\
{\boldsymbol{u}}&=&\textbf{0}\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$}\end{array}\right.

(8)

Let us define the spaces

W:=\{{\boldsymbol{v}}:{\boldsymbol{v}}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{3},\;{\boldsymbol{v}}\;\text{is zero on $\partial\Omega$}\}

(9)

and

Q:=\{q:q\in L^{2}(\Omega),\;(q,1)_{\Omega}=0\}

(10)

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Omega}
is the standard
L^{2}
scalar product. Then we have the weak form of (
8
):
find
({\boldsymbol{u}},p)\in W\times Q
such that

\boxed{a({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{v}})-b({\boldsymbol{v}},p)+b({\boldsymbol{u}},q)=({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}})\quad\forall({\boldsymbol{v}},q)\in W\times Q}

(11)

where the forms are given by

a({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{w}}):=(\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}})_{\Omega},\quad b({\boldsymbol{v}},q):=(\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}},q)_{\Omega}

(12)

To define the finite element method, we introduce the non-conforming finite element space constructed from the space

V_{h}
in (
7
) by defining

W_{h}:=\{{\boldsymbol{v}}:{\boldsymbol{v}}\in[V_{h}]^{3},\;{\boldsymbol{v}}\;\text{is zero in the midpoints of $E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}$ on $\partial\Omega$}\}

(13)

and the space of continuous piecewise linear polynomials

Q_{h}:=\{q:q\in C^{0}(\Omega)\cap Q,\;q\;\text{is linear on $T$, $\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}$}\}

(14)

The finite element method is to find
({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{h})\in W_{h}\times Q_{h}
such that

\displaystyle A_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{h}),({\boldsymbol{v}},q))=({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}})_{\Omega}\qquad\forall({\boldsymbol{v}},q)\in W_{h}\times Q_{h}

(15)

Here the form is defined by

A_{h}(({\boldsymbol{v}},q),({\boldsymbol{w}},r))=a_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{w}})-b({\boldsymbol{v}},r)+b({\boldsymbol{w}},p)

(16)

with

a_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{w}}):=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}})_{T},\quad\quad b({\boldsymbol{v}},q):=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}},q)_{T}-({\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}},q)_{\partial T}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla q)_{T}

(17)

and
\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}={\boldsymbol{v}}\otimes\nabla
is the tensor with elements
({\boldsymbol{v}}\otimes\nabla)_{ij}=\partial_{j}v_{i}
.

Remark 3.1

We will also consider the alternative form

\displaystyle\tilde{b}({\boldsymbol{v}},q)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}},q)_{T}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}-({\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla q)_{T}+({\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}},q)_{\partial T}

(18)

This form is preferable in the presence of natural boundary conditions, and requires less numerical computations, but we will see that the proof of the inf-sup condition
is more complicated due to the presence of the trace term on
\partial T
in the right hand side of (
18
). Throughout the
paper we will focus our presentation on the form
b
and we will for each result add a remark on the modifications necessary to obtain
the corresponding result for
\tilde{b}
. Finally, we will present an inf-sup result for
\tilde{b}
in
A
.

Remark 3.2

Unlike some nonconforming approximations, the rotated
Q_{1}
approximation fulfills Korn’s inequality
[
3
]
, which
means that we may also use the strain form of Stokes: find the velocity

{\boldsymbol{u}}
and the pressure
p
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-2{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\boldsymbol{u}})+\nabla p&=&{\boldsymbol{f}}\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$}\\
\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}&=&\text{\bf 0}\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$}\\
{\boldsymbol{u}}&=&\text{\bf 0}\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}\end{array}\right.

(19)

where

({\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}})_{ij}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)

(20)

is the strain tensor and
{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\cdot
denotes matrix divergence. This is of interest in free surface flows where we need zero stress as a natural boundary condition, cf. Section
4.2
.

3.2
Norms and Continuity of the Forms

Define the norms

\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{a_{h}}:=a_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{v}})^{1/2},\quad\|q\|_{\Omega}:=\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(21)

and

\displaystyle|||({\boldsymbol{v}},q)|||_{h}^{2}=\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|^{2}_{a_{h}}+\|q\|^{2}_{\Omega}+h^{2}\|\nabla q\|^{2}_{\Omega}

(22)

We let
a\lesssim b
denote
a\leq Cb
with
C
a positive constant independent of the mesh parameter. Then we have
the following continuities of the forms
b
and
A_{h}
.

Lemma 3.1

There are constants such that for all functions in
(W+W_{h})\times Q
,

\displaystyle\boxed{b({\boldsymbol{v}},q)\lesssim\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}\Big(\|q\|_{\Omega}+h\|\nabla q\|_{\Omega}\Big)}

(23)

and

\displaystyle\boxed{A_{h}(({\boldsymbol{v}},q),({\boldsymbol{w}},r))\lesssim|||({\boldsymbol{v}},q)|||_{h}|||({\boldsymbol{w}},r)|||_{h}}

(24)

PROOF
. Define
[{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}]={\boldsymbol{n}}_{1}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}_{1}+{\boldsymbol{n}}_{2}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}_{2}
for an interior face shared by elements
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
and
[{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}]={\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}
for a face at the boundary belonging to element
T
. Noting that for a face
F
we then have

[{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{E})]=0
for the midpoint
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{E}
of the edge
E
. Therefore
([{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}],1)_{F}=0
, since the quadrature
formula based on the midpoints of the edges on a triangle is exact for quadratic polynomials. We therefore have

([{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}],q)_{F}=([{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}],(I-P_{0,F})q)_{F}
, where
P_{0,F}
is the
L^{2}
projection on constants on the face
F
. With these
preparations at hand we obtain the following bound

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}},q)_{T}-({\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}},q)_{\partial T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}},q)_{T}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}((I-P_{0,F})[{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}],(I-P_{0,F})q)_{F}

(25)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{T}\|q\|_{T}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\|(I-P_{0,F})[{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}]\|_{F}\|(I-P_{0,F})q\|_{F}

(26)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{T}\|q\|_{T}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h\|\nabla_{F}[{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}]\|_{F}\|\nabla_{F}q\|_{F}

(27)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{T}\|q\|_{T}+\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{T}h\|\nabla q\|_{T}

(28)

which proves (
23
). Finally, (
24
) follows directly from (
23
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Remark 3.3

For
\tilde{b}
defined in (
18
) we directly have

\displaystyle\tilde{b}({\boldsymbol{v}},q)\leq\|\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}\|q\|_{\Omega}

(29)

since there are no trace terms on the boundary of the elements.

3.3
Interpolation

We shall now define interpolants for the finite element space. Starting with the pressure space we let

\pi_{h,p}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow Q_{h}
be a Clement interpolant. We then have the standard estimate

\|q-\pi_{h,p}q\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}\lesssim h^{k-m}\|q\|_{H^{k}(\Omega)},\qquad 0\leq m\leq k\leq 2

(30)

To construct an interpolant for the velocity space we use component-wise Scott-Zhang interpolation to
satisfy the Dirichlet boundary conditions (in the nodes),

\pi_{h,u}{\boldsymbol{v}}=[\pi_{h,SZ}v_{i}]_{i=1}^{3}

(31)

Here we also have the interpolation estimate

\|{\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi_{h,u}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}\lesssim h^{k-m}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{H^{k}(\Omega)},\qquad 0\leq m\leq k\leq 2

(32)

From here on we simplify the notation and write
\pi_{h,p}=\pi_{h}
and
\pi_{h,u}=\pi_{h}
and interpret the operator
in the correct way depending on in which space the argument reside. Combining the estimates we get

\displaystyle|||({\boldsymbol{v}},q)-(\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}},\pi_{h}q)|||_{h}\lesssim h(\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|q\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)})

(33)

3.4
Stability Analysis

We first recall the following standard result from
[
1
]
.

Theorem 3.1

If there is a constant such that

\boxed{\|q\|_{\Omega}+h\|\nabla q\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\sup_{{\boldsymbol{v}}\in W_{h}}\frac{b(q,{\boldsymbol{v}})}{\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{a_{h}}}}

(34)

Then there is a constant such that

\boxed{|||({\boldsymbol{v}},q)|||_{h}\lesssim\sup_{({\boldsymbol{v}},q)\in W_{h}\times Q_{h}}\frac{A_{h}(({\boldsymbol{v}},q),({\boldsymbol{w}},r))}{|||({\boldsymbol{w}},r)|||_{h}}}

(35)

We shall now prove that (
34
) holds for the nonconforming space
W_{h}\times Q_{h}
using an approach
called Verfürth’s trick
[
8
]
, which proceeds in two steps.

Lemma 3.2
(Step 1)

There are constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

\boxed{\sup_{{\boldsymbol{v}}\in W_{h}}\frac{b({\boldsymbol{v}},q)}{\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{a_{h}}}\geq c_{1}\|q\|-c_{2}h\|\nabla q\|}

(36)

PROOF
. For each
q\in Q_{h}
there exists a
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in W
such that

\displaystyle c_{1}\|q\|_{\Omega}\leq\frac{(\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}},q)_{\Omega}}{\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}}

(37)

We shall now replace
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in W
by the interpolant
\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}
and estimate the remainder term as follows

\displaystyle-\frac{(\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla q)_{\Omega}}{\|\nabla\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{({\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla q)_{\Omega}}{\|\nabla\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}}+\frac{({\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla q)_{\Omega}}{\|\nabla\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}}

(38)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla q)_{\Omega}}{\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}}\frac{\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}}{\|\nabla\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}}+\frac{({\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla q)_{\Omega}}{\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}}\frac{\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}}{\|\nabla\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}}

(39)

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\|q\|_{\Omega}-\frac{\|{\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}\|\nabla q\|_{\Omega}}{\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}}\frac{\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}}{\|\nabla\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}}

(40)

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\|q\|_{\Omega}-c_{2}h\|\nabla q\|_{\Omega}

(41)

Here we used partial integration together with the continuity of
q
and the boundary condition
{\boldsymbol{v}}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, the interpolation estimate (
32
) and the boundedness
\|\nabla\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Omega}
of the interpolation operator.

Lemma 3.3
(Step 2)

Under the assumption (
6
) there exists
c_{3}>0
such that

\boxed{\sup_{{\boldsymbol{v}}\in W_{h}}\frac{b({\boldsymbol{v}},q)}{\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{a_{h}}}\geq c_{3}h\,\|\nabla q\|}

(42)

PROOF
. Using partial integration we have the identity

\displaystyle b({\boldsymbol{v}},q)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}-({\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla q)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}

(43)

Observing that
\nabla p
is element-wise constant we may apply the quadrature formula

\int_{T}w({\boldsymbol{x}})dT\approx\int_{T}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}(T)}w({\boldsymbol{x}}_{E})\varphi_{T,E}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{E})dT=\frac{|T|}{6}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}(T)}w({\boldsymbol{x}}_{E})

(44)

which is exact for
w\in V_{h}
, to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}-({\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla q)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|T|}{6}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}(T)}-{\boldsymbol{v}}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{E})\cdot\nabla p({\boldsymbol{x}}_{E})

(45)

where
\{\varphi_{E}\}_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}
is the global basis in
V_{h}
,
\mathcal{E}_{h}(T)
is the set of edges belong to element
T
, and
\varphi_{T,E}=\varphi_{E}|_{T}
.

Since
q
is continuous it follows that the tangent derivative
{\boldsymbol{t}}_{E}\cdot\nabla q
along each edge
E
, with unit tangent vector
{\boldsymbol{t}}_{E}
,
is also continuous and thus taking

{\boldsymbol{v}}_{*}=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}-h^{2}({\boldsymbol{t}}_{E}\cdot\nabla q){\boldsymbol{t}}_{E}\varphi_{E}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}^{I}_{h}(T)}-h^{2}({\boldsymbol{t}}_{E}\cdot\nabla q){\boldsymbol{t}}_{E}\varphi_{T,E}

(46)

where
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}
is the set of interior edges, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}-(\nabla q,{\boldsymbol{v}})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{|T|}{6}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}(T)}h^{2}({\boldsymbol{t}}_{E}\cdot\nabla q({\boldsymbol{x}}_{E}))^{2}\gtrsim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h^{2}\|\nabla q\|^{2}_{T}

(47)

since
\nabla q
is element-wise constant and there are, by assumption, three linearly independent tangent vectors in the set

\{{\boldsymbol{t}}_{E}\}_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}(T)}
for each element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Finally, noting that

\displaystyle\|\nabla v_{*}\|_{T}^{2}\lesssim h^{4}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}(T)}({\boldsymbol{t}}_{E}\cdot\nabla q({\boldsymbol{x}}_{E}))^{2}\|\nabla\varphi_{T,E}\|_{T}^{2}\lesssim h^{4}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}^{I}_{h}(T)}|\nabla q({\boldsymbol{x}}_{E})|^{2}h^{-2}h^{3}\lesssim h^{2}\|\nabla q\|_{T}^{2}

(48)

where we used an inverse bound to conclude that
\|\nabla\varphi_{E}\|_{T}^{2}\lesssim h^{-2}\|\varphi_{E}\|_{T}^{2}\lesssim h^{-2}h^{3}
, and
summing over
T
gives

\displaystyle\|v_{*}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim h\|\nabla q\|_{\Omega}

(49)

Combining the estimates we get the desired result since

\displaystyle\frac{b(v_{*},q)}{\|\nabla v_{*}\|_{\Omega}}\gtrsim\frac{h^{2}\|\nabla q\|^{2}_{\Omega}}{\|\nabla v_{*}\|_{\Omega}}\gtrsim h\|\nabla q\|_{\Omega}\frac{h\|\nabla q\|_{\Omega}}{\|\nabla v_{*}\|_{\Omega}}\gtrsim h\|\nabla q\|_{\Omega}

(50)

Remark 3.4

For the alternative form
\tilde{b}
we get the more complicated expression

\displaystyle\tilde{b}({\boldsymbol{v}},q)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}-({\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla q)_{T}+({\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}},q)_{\partial T}

(51)

where the trace term on the boundary of
T
does not vanish. We will however show in
A
that with the same
choice of
{\boldsymbol{v}}_{*}
the trace term can be shown to be dominated by the bulk term on each element. The proof is based on mapping to the
reference element and explicit computation of the two integrals. Thus Lemma
3.3
also holds for the form
\tilde{b}
.

Lemma 3.4

There is a constant such that the inf-sup condition (
34
) holds.

PROOF
. Multiply (
36
) by
c_{3}
and (
42
) by
2c_{2}
and add up to find

\displaystyle(c_{3}+2c_{2})\sup_{{\boldsymbol{v}}\in W_{h}}\frac{b(q,{\boldsymbol{v}})}{\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{a_{h}}}\geq c_{3}c_{1}\|q\|_{\Omega}-c_{3}c_{2}h\|\nabla q\|_{\Omega}+2c_{3}c_{2}h\,\|\nabla q\|_{\Omega}=c_{3}c_{1}\|q\|_{\Omega}+c_{3}c_{2}h\,\|\nabla q\|_{\Omega}

(52)

Thus (
34
) holds with the hidden constant
c_{3}=\min(c_{1},c_{2})/(c_{3}+2c_{2})
.

3.5
Error Analysis

Theorem 3.2

There is a constant such that

\displaystyle\boxed{|||({\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p-p_{h})|||_{h}\lesssim h\Big(\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+\|p\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big)}

(53)

PROOF
. We first split the error in an interpolation error part and a discrete part

\displaystyle|||({\boldsymbol{u}},p)-({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{h})|||_{h}\leq|||({\boldsymbol{u}},p)-(\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{u}},\pi_{h}p)|||_{h}+|||(\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{u}},\pi_{h}p)-({\boldsymbol{u}}_{,}p_{h})|||_{h}

(54)

The first term can be estimated using the interpolation error estimate (
33
). To estimate the discrete part
of the error we employ the inf-sup condition (
35
) to obtain

\displaystyle|||(\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\pi_{h}p-p_{h})|||_{h}\lesssim\sup_{({\boldsymbol{v}},q)\in W_{h}\times Q_{h}}\frac{A_{h}((\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\pi_{h}p-p_{h},({\boldsymbol{v}},q))}{|||({\boldsymbol{v}},q)|||_{h}}

(55)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{({\boldsymbol{v}},q)\in W_{h}\times Q_{h}}\frac{A_{h}((\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}},\pi_{h}p-p),({\boldsymbol{v}},q))}{|||({\boldsymbol{v}},q)|||_{h}}+\sup_{({\boldsymbol{v}},q)\in W_{h}\times Q_{h}}\frac{A_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}},p),({\boldsymbol{v}},q))-({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}})_{\Omega}}{|||({\boldsymbol{v}},q)|||_{h}}

(56)

\displaystyle\lesssim|||(\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}},\pi_{h}p-p)|||_{h}+\sup_{({\boldsymbol{v}},q)\in W_{h}\times Q_{h}}\frac{A_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}},p),({\boldsymbol{v}},q))-({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}})_{\Omega}}{|||({\boldsymbol{v}},q)|||_{h}}

(57)

Here we used continuity (
24
) of the form
A_{h}
for the first term which can now be estimated using the
interpolation error estimate (
33
). The second term accounts for the consistency error and using partial
integration we find that

\displaystyle A_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}},p),({\boldsymbol{v}},q))-({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}})_{\Omega}=a_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{v}})-b({\boldsymbol{v}},p)+b({\boldsymbol{u}},q)-({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}})_{\Omega}

(58)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}},\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}})_{T}+({\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla p)_{T}-({\boldsymbol{u}},\nabla q)_{T}-({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}})_{T}

(59)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}-(\Delta{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{v}})_{T}+(\nabla_{n}{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{v}})_{\partial T}+({\boldsymbol{v}},\nabla p)_{T}+(\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}},q)_{T}-({\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}},q)_{\partial T}-({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}})_{T}

(60)

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{u}}\otimes\nabla,{\boldsymbol{v}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}})_{\partial T}

(61)

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{u}}\otimes\nabla,[{\boldsymbol{v}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}}])_{F}

(62)

where
[{\boldsymbol{v}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}}]={\boldsymbol{v}}_{1}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}}_{1}+{\boldsymbol{v}}_{2}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}}_{2}
for a face
F
shared by elements

T_{1}
and
T_{2}
with
{\boldsymbol{v}}|_{T_{i}}={\boldsymbol{v}}_{i}
,
i=1,2.
and
[{\boldsymbol{v}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}}]={\boldsymbol{v}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}}
for a face at the boundary.
Then using the fact that
[{\boldsymbol{v}}]={\boldsymbol{0}}
in the midpoints of the edges and that midpoint quadrature is exact for quadratic polynomials
on a triangle it follows that
(1,([{\boldsymbol{v}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}}])_{ij})_{F}=0
and therefore we may subtract
the
L^{2}
-projection on constant functions on the faces and then estimate the contributions using the following standard bounds

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{u}}\otimes\nabla,[{\boldsymbol{v}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}}])_{F}

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}((I-P_{0,F}){\boldsymbol{v}}\otimes\nabla,(I-P_{0,F})[{\boldsymbol{v}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}}])_{F}

(63)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\|(I-P_{0,F}){\boldsymbol{u}}\otimes\nabla\|_{F}\|(I-P_{0,F})[{\boldsymbol{v}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}}]\|_{F}

(64)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h\|\nabla_{F}({\boldsymbol{u}}\otimes\nabla)\|_{F}h\|\nabla_{F}[{\boldsymbol{v}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}}]\|_{F}

(65)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{H^{3}(T)}\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{T}

(66)

where
\nabla_{F}=(I-{\boldsymbol{n}}_{F}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}})\nabla
, with
{\boldsymbol{n}}_{F}
a unit normal to the
F
, is the tangential gradient to the face
F
.
Combing the bounds gives the desired estimate.

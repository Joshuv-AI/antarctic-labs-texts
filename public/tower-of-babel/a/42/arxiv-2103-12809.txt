Multipath-based SLAM using Belief Propagation with Interacting Multiple Dynamic Models
Erik Leitinger 1 , Stefan Grebien 1 2 , and Klaus Witrisal 1 2

Abstract
In this paper, we present a Bayesian multipath-based simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) algorithm that continuously adapts interacting multiple models (IMM) parameters to describe the mobile agent state dynamics. The time-evolution of the IMM parameters is described by a Markov chain and the parameters are incorporated into the factor graph structure that represents the statistical structure of the SLAM problem. The proposed belief propagation (BP)-based algorithm adapts, in an online manner, to time-varying system models by jointly inferring the model parameters along with the agent and map feature states. The performance of the proposed algorithm is finally evaluating with a simulated scenario. Our numerical simulation results show that the proposed multipath-based SLAM algorithm is able to cope with strongly changing agent state dynamics.

II
Signal Model

The agent is equipped with an
H
-element antenna array. At every discrete time step
n
, the element locations are denoted by
\bm{p}_{n}^{(h)}
,
h\in\{1,\dots,H\}
, the agent position
\bm{p}_{n}
refers to the center of gravity of the array. We also define

and
\psi^{(h)}=\angle{(\bm{p}_{n}^{(h)}-\bm{p}_{n})}-\psi_{n}

\bm{p}_{n}
and the orientation, respectively, of the
h

\psi_{n}
is unknown.

At every time
n
, anchor
j\in\{1,\dots,J\}
transmits a baseband signal

centered at carrier frequency
f_{\text{c}}
. The signal at antenna element

is then given as

s_{\textrm{RX},n}^{(j,h)}(t)=\sum_{l=1}^{L_{n}^{(j)}}\alpha_{l,n}^{(j,h)}\hskip 0.85358pts\big(t\hskip-0.85358pt-\hskip-0.85358pt\tau_{l,n}^{(j,h)}\big)+\hskip 0.85358pt\nu_{n}^{(j,h)}(t)\hskip 0.85358pt+\hskip 0.85358ptw_{n}^{(j,h)}(t)\hskip 0.85358pt.

(1)

The first term on the right-hand side (RHS) describes
L_{n}^{(j)}
specular MPCs with complex amplitudes
\alpha_{l,n}^{(j,h)}=\alpha_{l,n}^{(j)}\exp\big(i2\pi f_{\mathrm{c}}\frac{d^{(h)}}{c}\cos\big(\varphi_{l,n}^{(j)}-\psi_{n}-\psi^{(h)}\big)\big)
and delays
\tau_{l,n}^{(j,h)}=\tau_{l,n}^{(j)}-\frac{d^{(h)}}{c}\cos(\varphi_{l,n}^{(j)}-\psi_{n}-\psi^{(h)})
with
\tau_{l,n}^{(j)}\hskip-0.85358pt=\hskip-0.85358pt\big\|\bm{p}_{n}\!-\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{p}_{l}^{(j)}\big\|/c
with

related to the distance and angle between the agent’s position

and the PA/VA positions
\bm{p}_{l}^{(j)}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, respectively. The second term on the RHS of (
1

1
),

N_{0}
.

Channel estimation
: A sparse Bayesian multipath channel estimator
[
20
,
21
]
is used to estimate at each time
n
and for each PA a set of
M^{(j)}_{n}
MPC distances
\hat{d}^{(j)}_{m,n}=c\hat{\tau}^{(j)}_{m,n}
and AoAs
\hat{\varphi}^{(j)}_{m,n}
and the corresponding complex amplitudes
\hat{\alpha}^{(j)}_{m,n}
, where
m\hskip-0.85358pt\in\hskip-0.85358pt{\cal{M}}_{n}^{(j)}=\{1,\dots,M_{n}^{(j)}\}
. To infer the variances
\hat{\sigma}^{(j)\hskip 0.85358pt2}_{\alpha,m,n}
of the amplitudes, we use the antenna array at the agent. The measured normalized amplitude is given as
\hat{u}^{(j)}_{m,n}=|\hat{\alpha}^{(j)}_{m,n}|/\hat{\sigma}^{(j)}_{\alpha,m,n}

\bm{z}^{(j)}_{n}=[\bm{z}^{(j)\hskip 0.85358pt\mathrm{T}}_{1,n},\dots,\bm{z}^{(j)\hskip 0.85358pt\mathrm{T}}_{M^{(j)}_{n},n}]^{\mathrm{T}}

III-B

State Evolution

Following the interacting multiple model (IMM) approach

The agent state
\bm{x}_{n}
and the augmented states of the legacy PVAs,

\displaystyle f\big(\bm{x}_{n},\underline{\bm{y}}_{n},q_{n}|\bm{x}_{n-1},\bm{y}_{n-1},q_{n-1}\big)

\displaystyle=f_{q_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{x}_{n-1})f(q_{n}|,q_{n-1})\prod_{j=1}^{J}\prod_{k=1}^{K_{n-1}^{(j)}}\hskip-0.85358pt\hskip-0.85358ptf\big(\underline{\bm{y}}_{k,n}^{(j)}\big|\bm{y}_{k,n-1}^{(j)}\big)

(2)

where

is the state-transition PDFs of legacy PVA

and

is distributed according to the state-transition PDF

(for details cf.
[

]

,

III-D

Minimum Mean-Square Error (MMSE) Estimation

Our goal is to estimate the agent state
\bm{x}_{n}
and the positions
\bm{p}_{k}
and the amplitudes
u_{k,n}
of the PVAs from past and present measurements, i.e., from the total measurement vector
\bm{z}_{1:n}
. In the Bayesian framework, estimation of the states are based on their respective posterior PDFs. We develop an approximate calculation of the minimum mean-square error (MMSE) estimates of the agent state
\bm{x}_{n}
, the positions
\bm{p}_{k}
and the amplitudes
u_{k,n}
of the PVAs based on the marginal posterior PDFs:

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{x}}^{\text{MMSE}}_{n}\,

\displaystyle\triangleq\hskip-0.85358pt\int\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}_{n}\hskip 0.85358ptf(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{z}_{1:n})\hskip 0.85358pt\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{n}

(8)

\displaystyle\hat{q}^{\text{MMSE}}_{n}\,

\displaystyle\triangleq\hskip-0.85358pt\sum_{i\in\mathcal{Q}}\hskip 0.85358pti\hskip 0.85358ptf(q_{n}=i|\bm{z}_{1:n})

(9)

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{x}}^{(j)\hskip 0.85358pt\text{MMSE}}_{k,n}\,

\displaystyle\triangleq\hskip-0.85358pt\int\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}^{(j)}_{k,n}\,f\big(\bm{x}^{(j)}_{k,n}\big|r^{(j)}_{k,n}=1,\bm{z}_{1:n}\big)\hskip 0.85358pt\mathrm{d}\bm{x}^{(j)}_{k,n}

(10)

where
f(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{z}_{1:n})=\sum_{q_{n}\in\mathcal{Q}}f(\bm{x}_{n},q_{n}|\bm{z}_{1:n})
with the joint marginal PDF of the agent state and the dynamic model state
f(\bm{x}_{n},q_{n}|\bm{z}_{1:n})

[
17
]
,

f\big(\bm{x}^{(j)}_{k,n}\big|r^{(j)}_{k,n}=1,\bm{z}_{1:n}\big)=\frac{f\big(\bm{x}^{(j)}_{k,n},r^{(j)}_{k,n}=1\big|\bm{z}_{1:n}\big)}{p\big(r^{(j)}_{k,n}=1\big|\bm{z}_{1:n}\big)}\hskip 0.85358pt,\\

(11)

and
p\big(r^{(j)}_{k,n}=1\big|\bm{z}_{1:n}\big)=\int\hskip-0.85358ptf\big(\bm{x}^{(j)}_{k,n},r^{(j)}_{k,n}=1\big|\bm{z}_{1:n}\big)\hskip 0.85358pt\mathrm{d}\bm{x}^{(j)}_{k,n}
. The state of the
k
-th PVA is only estimated if it is considered as detected at time

, i.e.,
p\big(r^{(j)}_{k,n}=1\big|\bm{z}_{1:n}\big)>P_{\mathrm{det}}
with detection probability threshold
P_{\mathrm{det}}

By performing sequential particle-based message passing by means of the SPA rules

V
Conclusions

We have extended a multipath-based SLAM algorithm that continuously adapts IMM parameters to describe the mobile agent state dynamics. The time-evolution of the IMM parameters is described by a Markov chain and the parameters are incorporated into the factor graph. Our numerical simulations have shown that the proposed multipath-based algorithm is able to adapt the agent state space dynamic in an online manner enabling small agent errors even for rapid changes in the agent movement.

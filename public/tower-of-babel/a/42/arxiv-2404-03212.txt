Holographic Global Vortices with Novel Boundary Conditions
Markus A.G. Amano, Minoru Eto

Abstract
The AdS/CFT correspondence has significantly impacted the study of strongly coupled systems, providing insights into various condensed matter phenomena through its holographic duality. This paper introduces an alternative approach to the breaking of the global U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) symmetry in the bulk in two asymptotically AdS spacetimes: AdS plus hard wall and AdS Blackbrane. We explore a ( 3 + 1 ) (3+1) -dimensional bulk U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) symmetry-breaking phase vacuum within a global U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) ϕ 4 \phi^{4} field theory, without the gauge field present in prior models. We find the symmetry-breaking vacuum requires that the mass squared is proportional to the quartic coupling. We also investigate numerical solutions of topologically stable vortex strings extending into the bulk. We find evidence that the full UV expansion is dual to a point-like boundary excitation.

\displaystyle\sim\phi_{(0)}e^{i\Theta_{12}({R(0)})}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}iz^{2}R^{(2)}(0)\Theta_{12}^{\prime}({R(0)})+\frac{1}{6}i{R^{(3)}(0)}z^{3}\Theta_{12}^{\prime}({R(0)})\right)+O\left(z^{4}\right)

(28)

\displaystyle\sim\phi_{(0)}e^{i\Theta_{12}({R(0)})}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}iz^{2}R^{(2)}(0)\Theta_{12}^{\prime}({R(0)})+\mathrm{C}_{1}\left\langle\mathcal{O}_{\phi}\right\rangle z^{3}\Theta_{12}^{\prime}({R(0)})\right)+O\left(z^{4}\right)

where
\Theta_{12}^{\prime}({R(0)})\equiv(d\Theta_{12}/d{R(0)})=sy/\left(y^{2}+\left(x+{R(0)}\right)^{2}\right)-y/\left(y^{2}+\left(x-{R(0)}\right)^{2}\right)
.
The normalization is necessary because, in the legitimate boundary action, we need to be able to vary it with respect to a sourcing term, which could be the leading order term of
\phi
.
This leading order term is of constant order and can be identified with
\phi
evaluated at
z=0
, i.e.,
\phi(0)=\phi_{(0)}e^{i\Theta_{12}}
.

For a free bulk massless scalar field, the boundary action takes the form
S_{\text{boundary}}\propto\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\left({\phi}^{(0)}\right)^{2}{\phi}^{(3)}
, where
\phi\sim{\phi}^{(0)}(1+{\phi}^{(3)}z^{3})
.
Via the AdS/CFT correspondence, we can identify the source term as
{\phi}^{(0)}
and the VEV as
\left\langle\mathcal{O}_{\phi}\right\rangle={\delta S_{\text{boundary}}}/{\delta{\phi}^{(0)}}
.
Thus, the third leading order coefficient is identified as the VEV, with
\left\langle\mathcal{O}_{\phi}\right\rangle
being the VEV of the associated boundary scalar operator and

For a pair of vortex strings, the VEV can be written as

\left\langle\mathcal{O}_{\phi}\right\rangle\propto{R^{(3)}(0)}\Theta_{12}^{\prime}({R(0)})={R^{(3)}(0)}\left(\frac{sy}{\left(y^{2}+\left(x+{R(0)}\right)^{2}\right)}-\frac{y}{\left(y^{2}+\left(x-{R(0)}\right)^{2}\right)}\right)\,.

(29)

While

might seem related, it is fundamentally different from the vacuum expectation value (VEV) of a vortex.
Unlike a vortex VEV,

Moreover,
\left\langle\mathcal{O}_{\phi}\right\rangle
vanishes for large

\left\langle\mathcal{O}_{\phi}\right\rangle
are localized.

In the full UV expansion with the UV cutoff,
z_{\mathrm{cutoff}}

This, coupled with the fact that
\left\langle\mathcal{O}_{\phi}\right\rangle
is localized, provides evidence that such holographic global vortices can induce a phase transition, with the vortices themselves potentially acting as nucleation sites.
This phase transition is similar to a cosmological phase bubbles
[

30
]

4
Conclusion

We analyzed a bulk global
\phi^{4}

U(1)
scalar plus Einstein gravity theory in two
(3+1)
-dimensional asymptotically Anti-de Sitter (AdS) spacetimes: AdS plus hard wall and AdS black brane.
The bulk scalar field
\phi
is governed by the equation of motion in (
2
) and a quartic potential (
3
).
Near the conformal boundary, we found a constant
\phi\sim z^{0}
solution that preserves the AdS asymptotics and exhibits
U(1)
symmetry breaking in the bulk, as discussed in Section
2.1
.
This vacuum is linearly stable against scalar perturbations that do not source boundary operators.
This novel boundary condition required the scalar mass to be related to the quartic coupling as
m^{2}=-\lambda/L^{2}
.

We investigated vortex solutions in the bulk
U(1)
breaking vacuum, Section
2.2
.
We numerically solved for the magnitude profile,
\mathit{h}
, of a single vortex, centered at
x=y=0
, parallel to the
z
-axis.

\mathit{h}
obeyed a 2nd order non-linear ODE (
14
).
The presence of the the conformal factor
z^{2}
prohibited a separation by parts solution.
Nevertheless, some interesting conclusions can be made if the solution is modeled approximated to be separable.
We found its width increases with the bulk coordinate
z
, corresponding to a decrease in the effective coupling
\lambda
.
Roughly, the conformal factor
z^{2}
makes the effect coupling at a cross section of
z
to be
\sim\lambda/z^{2}
, so the radius of the vortex should go like
\sim z/\sqrt{\lambda}
.
This behavior can be seen in Figure
2
.
We found that at the boundary, the vortex became singular up to numerical error.
This necessitated the use of a UV cut off around the singular point on the boundary.
We believe that this is evidence that the bulk vortex is dual to a nucleation sites for a change of phase on the boundary.

We also studied vortex string approximation as pairs of interacting vortex-vortex or vortex-antivortex configurations, Section
2.3
.
We found that the radial profiles must satisfy Neumann boundary conditions at their termination points, and their unique solution requires specifying the initial location
{R(0)}
and the third derivative
{R^{(3)}(0)}
, Section
3.2
.
The global nature of the vortices introduces a log divergence in the energy integral, necessitating UV and IR cutoffs around the boundary (
z\to\infty
) and
r\to\infty
.
In the limit of large vortex separation, analytical expressions for the radial profiles were found, Section
3.1
.
Neumann boundary conditions restricted these profiles to only flat profiles.
This also implied that for pure AdS (without the HW) the radial profile is constant.
By analogy to a massless scalar field, we found evidence that
{R^{(3)}(0)}
proportional to a scalar VEV,
\left\langle\mathcal{O}_{\phi}\right\rangle
, Section
3.1
.

We found vortex string pairs for a given
{R(0)}
and
z_{\mathrm{h}}
and output
{R^{(3)}(0)}
in Section
3.3
.
Results confirmed the repulsive and attractive behaviors of vortex-vortex and vortex-antivortex pairs, respectively.
Further, we found that for large distances the induced candidate
\left\langle\mathcal{O}_{\phi}\right\rangle
decreases with inter-vortex string distance.
We also found vortex string pairs with a given
{R(0)}
and
{R^{(3)}(0)}
where
z_{\mathrm{h}}
is the output.
For large enough
\left|{R^{(3)}(0)}\right|
we found that the string profile diverge from each other for
{R^{(3)}(0)}>0
and converge to the center (
x=y=0
) for
{R^{(3)}(0)}<0
.
We also found a maximum
z_{\mathrm{h}}
for small
\left|{R^{(3)}(0)}\right|
corresponding to a minimum temperature
T_{\mathrm{min}}\propto 1/{R(0)}
in the black brane case.

This study found that the UV cutoff limited what could be said about the boundary theory.
Nevertheless, in order to integrate to the boundary with vortices their diverging self-energies must be accounted for as back-reaction to the geometry.
This new boundary geometry would likely differ from a conformally flat geometry.
One possible connection is that vortices are conformal defects, studied in
[
17
]
.
It would interesting to see if the vortex string solutions found in this work could be nucleation sites in a phase transition.
Furthermore, the vortex string approximation breaks down for small enough
{R(0)}
, requiring future work where the full vortex solution needs to be found.
The recent study by Chaffey et al.
[
32
]
explored a similar setup.
Their method of breaking
U(1)
symmetry is with a UV brane that has a quartic potential.

With

The steps of the shooting method can be summarized as follows:

1.

Develop a near-boundary expansion around
z=0
, parameterizing it with variables
{R(0)}
and
{R^{(3)}(0)}
.

2.

With the near-boundary expansion, evaluate
R(\epsilon)
and
R^{(1)}(\epsilon)
.

3.

Using
R(\epsilon)
and
R^{(1)}(\epsilon)
, solve the equation of motion from
z=\epsilon
to the other end of the domain at
{z=z_{\mathrm{h}}>0}
.

4.

Employ a numerical solver to adjust the initial values
{R(0)}
and
{R^{(3)}(0)}
such that
R^{(1)}(z_{h})
is zero.

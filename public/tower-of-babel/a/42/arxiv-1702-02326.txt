Knapp–Stein Type Intertwining Operators for Symmetric Pairs II. – The Translation Principle and Intertwining Operators for Spinors
Jan FRAHM and Bent ØRSTED

Abstract
For a symmetric pair ( G , H ) (G,H) of reductive groups we extend to a large class of generalized principal series representations our previous construction of meromorphic families of symmetry breaking operators. These operators intertwine between a possibly vector-valued principal series of G G and one for H H and are given explicitly in terms of their integral kernels. As an application we give a complete classification of symmetry breaking operators from spinors on a Euclidean space to spinors on a hyperplane, intertwining for a double cover of the conformal group of the hyperplane.



keywords
Knapp–Stein intertwiners; intertwining operators; symmetry breaking operators; symmetric pairs; principal series; translation principle

Introduction

In the study of representations of real reductive Lie groups, intertwining operators play a decisive role. The most prominent family of such operators is given by the standard Knapp–Stein operators which intertwine between two principal series representations of a group
G
. More recently, intertwining operators have been studied and used in the framework of branching problems, i.e., the restriction of a representation to a subgroup
H\subseteq G
and its decomposition. Here one is interested in operators from a representation of
G
to a representation of
H
, intertwining for the subgroup. Such operators are also called
symmetry breaking operators
, a term coined by T. Kobayashi in his program for branching problems (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
).

Such symmetry breaking operators have been studied in great detail in the special case of the conformal groups corresponding to a Euclidean space and a hyperplane by Kobayashi–Speh
[
10
]
. In this case some of the operators turn out to be differential operators, namely exactly the operators found by A. Juhl
[
5
]
in connection with his study of
Q
-curvature and holography in conformal geometry. Further connections to elliptic boundary value problems
[
15
]
and automorphic forms
[
12
]
indicate the broad spectrum of applications that these operators provide.

In our recent work with Y. Oshima
[
14
]
we generalized the construction of symmetry breaking operators by Kobayashi–Speh to a large class of symmetric pairs
(G,H)
and spherical principal series representations, proving meromorphic dependence on the parameters in general and generic uniqueness in some special cases. In this paper we shall further extend our construction to the case of vector-valued principal series representations; we establish many of the properties, now for arbitrary vector bundles, in particular the meromorphic dependence on the parameters. The main argument is a version of a well-known translation principle, namely by tensoring with a finite-dimensional representation of the group. As an application and illustration we give all details for the case of spinors on a Euclidean space carrying a representation of the spin cover of the conformal group; here we invoke a variation of the method of finding the eigenvalues on
K
-types of Knapp–Stein operators. The result is a complete classification of symmetry breaking operators from spinors on the Euclidean space to spinors on a hyperplane.

Let us now explain our results in more detail.

1.1
The translation principle

Let
G
be a real reductive group and
H\subseteq G
a reductive subgroup. For parabolic subgroups
P=MAN\subseteq G
and
P_{H}=M_{H}A_{H}N_{H}\subseteq H
we form the generalized principal series representations (smooth normalized parabolic induction)

\displaystyle\textup{Ind}_{P}^{G}\big(\xi\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\textup{Ind}_{P_{H}}^{H}\big(\eta\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big),

where
\xi
and
\eta
are finite-dimensional representations of
M
and
M_{H}
, and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
,
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}_{H,\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, the complexified duals of the Lie algebras of
A
and
A_{H}
. Consider the space

\displaystyle\textup{Hom}_{H}\big(\textup{Ind}_{P}^{G}\big(\xi\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big),\textup{Ind}_{P_{H}}^{H}\big(\eta\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\big)

of continuous
H
-intertwining operators. Realizing
\textup{Ind}_{P}^{G}\big(\xi\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)
and
\textup{Ind}_{P_{H}}^{H}\big(\eta\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)
on the spaces of smooth sections of the vector bundles

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}=G\times_{P}\big(\xi\otimes e^{\lambda+\rho}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\to G/P\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathcal{W}_{\nu}=H\times_{P_{H}}\big(\eta\otimes e^{\nu+\rho_{H}}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\to H/P_{H},

where
\rho\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
and
\rho_{H}\in\mathfrak{a}_{H}^{*}
are the half sums of positive roots, one can identify continuous
H
-intertwining operators with their distribution kernels. More precisely, Kobayashi–Speh
[
10
]
showed that taking distribution kernels is a linear isomorphism

\displaystyle\textup{Hom}_{H}\big(\textup{Ind}_{P}^{G}\big(\xi\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big),\textup{Ind}_{P_{H}}^{H}\big(\eta\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\big(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G/P,\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{*})\otimes W_{\nu}\big)^{\Delta(P_{H})},\qquad\!A\mapsto K^{A},

where
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{*}
is the dual bundle of
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
and
W_{\nu}
the
P_{H}
-representation defining
\mathcal{W}_{\nu}
(see Section
2.2
for the precise definition).

Now suppose
(\tau,E)
is an irreducible finite-dimensional representation of
G
. Then the restriction
\tau|_{P}
of
\tau
to
P
contains a unique irreducible subrepresentation
i\colon E^{\prime}\hookrightarrow E
(see Lemma
3.1
). With respect to the Langlands decomposition
P=MAN
this subrepresentation is of the form
\tau^{\prime}\otimes e^{\mu^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbf{1}
for some irreducible finite-dimensional representation
\tau
of
M
and
\mu^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
. Let
(E^{\prime})^{\vee}=\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(E^{\prime},\mathbb{C})
denote the dual of
E^{\prime}
.

Theorem A

(see Theorem
3.3
and Proposition
3.4
)
.

For every
P_{H}
-equivariant quotient
p\colon E|_{P_{H}}\allowbreak\twoheadrightarrow E^{\prime\prime}
with
E^{\prime\prime}\simeq\tau^{\prime\prime}\otimes e^{\mu^{\prime\prime}}\otimes\mathbf{1}
as
P_{H}
-representations, there is a unique linear map

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\ \textup{Hom}_{H}\big(\textup{Ind}_{P}^{G}\big(\xi\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big),\textup{Ind}_{P_{H}}^{H}\big(\eta\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}\to\textup{Hom}_{H}\big(\textup{Ind}_{P}^{G}\big((\xi\otimes\tau^{\prime})\otimes e^{\lambda+\mu^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big),\textup{Ind}_{P_{H}}^{H}\big((\eta\otimes\tau^{\prime\prime})\otimes e^{\nu+\mu^{\prime\prime}}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\big)

with the property that for every intertwining operator
A
with distribution kernel
K^{A}
, the distribution kernel
K^{\Phi(A)}
of
\Phi(A)
is given by
K^{\Phi(A)}=\varphi\otimes K^{A}
, the multiplication of
K^{A}
with the smooth section
\varphi\in C^{\infty}\big(G/P,G\times_{P}(E^{\prime})^{\vee}\big)\otimes E^{\prime\prime}
defined by

\displaystyle\varphi(g)=p\circ\tau(g)\circ i\in\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(E^{\prime},E^{\prime\prime})\simeq(E^{\prime})^{\vee}\otimes E^{\prime\prime},\qquad g\in G.

The translation principle allows to construct new intertwining operators from existing ones. But one can also reverse the roles and in some cases use the translation principle to classify intertwining operators (see, e.g., Theorem
4.10
).

We note that although
\varphi
is a non-trivial analytic section, the map
\Phi
might be trivial for certain parameters. However, being a multiplication operator,
\Phi
behaves nicely when applied to holomorphic/meromorphic families of intertwining operators (see Remark
3.5
for details).

1.2
Knapp–Stein type intertwining operators

Now assume that
(G,H)
is a symmetric pair, i.e.,
H
is an open subgroup of the fixed points
G^{\sigma}
of an involution
\sigma
of
G
. For simplicity we further assume that
G
is in the Harish-Chandra class. Let
P=MAN\subseteq G
be a
\sigma
-stable parabolic subgroup, then
P_{H}=P\cap H
is a parabolic subgroup of
H
and we write
P_{H}=M_{H}A_{H}N_{H}
for its Langlands decomposition.

In our previous paper
[
14
]
with Y. Oshima we constructed meromorphic families of intertwining operators between the spherical principal series representations
\textup{Ind}_{P}^{G}\big(\mathbf{1}\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)
and
\textup{Ind}_{P_{H}}^{H}\big(\mathbf{1}\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)
. We now generalize this construction and obtain intertwining operators between vector-valued principal series representations using the translation principle.

Assume that
P
and its opposite parabolic
\overline{P}
are conjugate via the Weyl group, i.e.,
P=\tilde{w}_{0}\overline{P}\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}
, where
\tilde{w}_{0}
is a representative of the longest Weyl group element
w_{0}
. Then for any finite-dimensional representation
\xi
of
M
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
we consider the function

\displaystyle K_{\xi,\alpha,\beta}(g)=\xi\big(m\big(\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}g^{-1}\big)\big)^{-1}a\big(\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}g^{-1}\big)^{\alpha}a\big(\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}g^{-1}\sigma(g)\big)^{\beta},\qquad g\in G,

where
m(g)
and
a(g)
are the densely defined projections of
g\in\overline{N}MAN
onto the
M
- and
A
-component. For
\xi=\mathbf{1}
the trivial representation, these are the kernel functions constructed in
[
14
]
. Since
a(g)
is only defined on an open dense subset, it may happen that the factor
a\big(\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}g^{-1}\sigma(g)\big)
is not defined for any
g\in G
, whence we additionally assume that the domain of definition for
a\big(\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}g^{-1}\sigma(g)\big)
is not empty. In
[
14
]
we showed that in this case
K_{\xi,\alpha,\beta}(g)
is defined on an open dense subset in
G
, and also gave a criterion to check this.

Under the above assumptions, the translation principle combined with our previous results from
[
14
]
yields:

Corollary B

(see Corollary
3.9
)
.

Assume that the finite-dimensional representation
\xi
of
M
is extendible to
G

(
see Section
3.4
for the precise definition
)
. Then the functions
K_{\xi,\alpha,\beta}(g)
extend to a meromorphic family of distributions

\displaystyle K_{\xi,\alpha,\beta}\in(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G/P,\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{*})\otimes W_{\nu})^{\Delta(P_{H})},\qquad\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*},

where

\displaystyle\lambda=-w_{0}\alpha+\sigma\beta-w_{0}\beta+\rho,\qquad\nu=-\alpha|_{\mathfrak{a}_{H,\mathbb{C}}}-\rho_{H}.

(1.1)

Therefore, they give rise to a meromorphic family of intertwining operators

\displaystyle A(\xi,\alpha,\beta)\in\textup{Hom}_{H}\big(\textup{Ind}_{P}^{G}\big(\tilde{w}_{0}\xi\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big),\textup{Ind}_{P_{H}}^{H}(\xi|_{M_{H}}\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1})\big).

As in
[
14
, Corollary B]
this construction gives in particular lower bounds on multiplicities:

\displaystyle\dim\textup{Hom}_{H}\big(\textup{Ind}_{P}^{G}\big(\tilde{w}_{0}\xi\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big),\textup{Ind}_{P_{H}}^{H}\big(\xi|_{M_{H}}\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\big)\geq 1

for all parameters
(\lambda,\nu)
of the form (
1.1
).

1.3
Symmetry breaking operators for rank one orthogonal groups

We illustrate the translation principle in two examples, the first one being scalar-valued principal series representations for the symmetric pair
(G,H)=(\textup{O}(n+1,1),\textup{O}(n,1))
. The parabolic subgroups
P
and
P_{H}
satisfy

\displaystyle M_{H}\simeq\textup{O}(n-1)\times\textup{O}(1)\subseteq\textup{O}(n)\times\textup{O}(1)\simeq M,

\displaystyle A_{H}=A\simeq\mathbb{R}_{+},\qquad N_{H}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\simeq N.

We identify
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\simeq\mathbb{C}
such that
\rho=\frac{n}{2}
, and denote by
sgn
the non-trivial character of
O(1)\subseteq M_{H}\subseteq M
. Then for
\delta,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
\lambda,\nu\in\mathbb{C}
we consider intertwining operators between

\displaystyle\pi_{\lambda,\delta}=\textup{Ind}_{P}^{G}\big(\textup{sgn}^{\delta}\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\tau_{\nu,\varepsilon}=\textup{Ind}_{P_{H}}^{H}\big(\textup{sgn}^{\varepsilon}\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big).

Tensoring with characters of
G
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\textup{Hom}_{H}(\pi_{\lambda,\delta}|_{H},\tau_{\nu,\varepsilon})\simeq\textup{Hom}_{H}(\pi_{\lambda,1-\delta}|_{H},\tau_{\nu,1-\varepsilon}).

For the pair
(G,H)
, the restriction of a distribution kernel
K\in(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G/P,\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{*})\otimes W_{\nu})^{\Delta(P_{H})}
to the open dense Bruhat cell in
G/P
, which is isomorphic to
\overline{N}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, defines an isomorphism onto a subspace of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
(see Kobayashi–Speh
[
10
, Theorem 3.16]
or Theorem
2.2
for details). Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}
, resp.
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}
, denote the space of distribution kernels defining intertwining operators in
\textup{Hom}_{H}(\pi_{\lambda,\delta}|_{H},\tau_{\nu,\varepsilon})
with
\delta+\varepsilon\equiv 0(2)
, resp.
\delta+\varepsilon\equiv 1(2)
.

The space
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}
was classified by Kobayashi–Speh
[
10
]
, and we briefly describe this classification, borrowing their notation. First, they construct a meromorphic family of distributions
K^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}
,
(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, given by

\displaystyle K^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x^{\prime},x_{n})=|x_{n}|^{\lambda+\nu-\frac{1}{2}}\big(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\big)^{-\nu-\frac{n-1}{2}},\qquad(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then they show that every distribution in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}
is given by
K_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},+}
or a regularization of it. By a detailed analysis of the meromorphic nature, the poles and all possible residues of the family
K^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}
they obtain a complete description of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}
for all
(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. More precisely, let

\displaystyle L_{\textup{even}}=\big\{(-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j)\colon i,j\in\mathbb{N},i-j\in 2\mathbb{N}\big\},

then the corresponding statement for symmetry breaking operators is: For
\delta+\varepsilon\equiv 0(2)
we have

\displaystyle\dim\textup{Hom}_{H}(\pi_{\lambda,\delta}|_{H},\tau_{\nu,\varepsilon})=\dim\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}=\begin{cases}2,&\mbox{for $(\lambda,\nu)\in L_{\textup{even}}$,}\\
1,&\mbox{for $(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}-L_{\textup{even}}$,}\end{cases}

and every intertwining operator is given by the distribution kernel
K^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}
or a regularization of it.

We apply the translation principle to the kernels
K^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}
to obtain a meromorphic family
K_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},-}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}
given by

\displaystyle K^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=x_{n}\cdot K^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda-1,\nu}(x)=\textup{sgn}(x_{n})|x_{n}|^{\lambda+\nu-\frac{1}{2}}\big(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\big)^{-\nu-\frac{n-1}{2}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

In Theorem
4.7
we derive from the poles and residues of
K^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}
all poles and all possible residues of the family
K^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}
and use them to classify intertwining operators. For the statement let

\displaystyle L_{\textup{odd}}=\big\{(-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j)\colon i,j\in\mathbb{N},i-j\in 2\mathbb{N}+1\big\}.

Theorem C

(see Theorems
4.7
and
4.10
)
.

For
\delta+\varepsilon\equiv 1(2)
we have

\displaystyle\dim\textup{Hom}_{H}(\pi_{\lambda,\delta}|_{H},\tau_{\nu,\varepsilon})=\begin{cases}2,&\text{for $(\lambda,\nu)\in L_{\textup{odd}}$,}\\
1,&\text{for $(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}-L_{\textup{odd}}$,}\end{cases}

and every intertwining operator is given by the distribution kernel
K^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}
or a regularization of it.

We remark that for
\lambda-\nu=-\frac{3}{2}-2\ell
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a residue of
K^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}
which is supported at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, inducing a differential intertwining operator. In this setting
G/P\simeq S^{n}
and
H/P_{H}\simeq S^{n-1}
, and the differential intertwining operators form the family of odd order conformally invariant differential operators
C^{\infty}\big(S^{n},\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}\big)\to C^{\infty}\big(S^{n-1},\mathcal{W}_{\nu}\big)
studied previously by Juhl
[
5
]
(see also
[
9
]
). The even order Juhl operators were already obtained as residues of the family
K^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}
by Kobayashi–Speh
[
10
]
.

1.4
Symmetry breaking operators for rank one pin groups

The second (and more involved) illustration of the translation principle is for the symmetric pair
\big(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{H}\big)=(\textup{Pin}(n+1,1),\textup{Pin}(n,1))
. Here
\textup{Pin}(p,q)
denotes a certain double cover of the group
\textup{O}(p,q)
(see Appendix
A
for details) so that we have compatible double covers
\widetilde{G}\to G
and
\widetilde{H}\to H
with
G
and
H
as in the previous section. The preimages of the parabolic subgroups
P
and
P_{H}
under the covering maps are
\widetilde{P}\simeq\widetilde{M}AN
and
\widetilde{P}_{H}\simeq\widetilde{M}_{H}A_{H}N_{H}
with
A
,
N
,
A_{H}
and
N_{H}
as in the previous section and

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{H}\simeq\textup{Pin}(n-1)\times\textup{O}(1)\subseteq\textup{Pin}(n)\times\textup{O}(1)\simeq\widetilde{M}.

The fundamental representations of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(n)
of
\widetilde{M}
are the exterior power representations
\wedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the spin representations. Symmetry breaking operators for the exterior power representations have been investigated in detail by Kobayashi–Speh
[
11
]
(see also Fischmann–Juhl–Somberg
[
4
]
and Kobayashi–Kubo–Pevzner
[
8
]
for the case of differential symmetry breaking operators). Here we focus on the fundamental spin representations.

The group
\textup{Pin}(n)
can be realized inside the Clifford algebra
\Cl(n)
with
n
generators (see Appendix
A
for details). The fundamental spin representations of
\textup{Pin}(n)
are the restrictions of irreducible representations of the complex Clifford algebra
\Cl(n;\mathbb{C})=\Cl(n)\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
to
\textup{Pin}(n)
, and therefore we do not distinguish between the representations of
\Cl(n;\mathbb{C})
and their restrictions to
\textup{Pin}(n)
. For even
n
the Clifford algebra
\Cl(n;\mathbb{C})
has a unique irreducible representation, and for odd
n
it has two inequivalent irreducible representations. Let
(\zeta_{n},\mathbb{S}_{n})
be an irreducible representation of
\Cl(n;\mathbb{C})
and
(\zeta_{n-1},\mathbb{S}_{n-1})
an irreducible representation of
\Cl(n-1;\mathbb{C})
. If
sgn
denotes the non-trivial representation of
\textup{O}(1)
, we have for all
\lambda,\nu\in\mathbb{C}
and
\delta,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
principal series representations

\displaystyle\not{\pi}_{\lambda,\delta}=\textup{Ind}_{\widetilde{P}}^{\widetilde{G}}\big(\big(\zeta_{n}\otimes\textup{sgn}^{\delta}\big)\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\not{\tau}_{\nu,\varepsilon}=\textup{Ind}_{\widetilde{P}_{H}}^{\widetilde{H}}\big(\big(\zeta_{n-1}\otimes\textup{sgn}^{\varepsilon}\big)\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big),

and we study intertwining operators in the space

\displaystyle\textup{Hom}_{\widetilde{H}}\big(\not{\pi}_{\lambda,\delta}|_{\widetilde{H}},\not{\tau}_{\nu,\varepsilon}\big).

As above, taking distribution kernels and restricting them to the open dense Bruhat cell in
G/P
identifies the space of intertwining operators with a subspace

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{S}_{n},\mathbb{S}_{n-1})\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{\pm}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{S}_{n},\mathbb{S}_{n-1})\big)\simeq\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)\otimes\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{S}_{n},\mathbb{S}_{n-1}),

where the sign
+
represents
\delta+\varepsilon\equiv 0(2)
and the sign
-
represents
\delta+\varepsilon\equiv 1(2)
.

The translation principle applied to the distribution kernels
K_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},\pm}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{\pm}
yields meromorphic families of distribution kernels
P\not{K}_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},\pm}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{S}_{n},\mathbb{S}_{n-1})\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{\pm}
given by

\displaystyle P\not{K}_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},\pm}(x)=(P\zeta_{n}(x))\cdot K_{\lambda-\frac{1}{2},\nu+\frac{1}{2}}^{\mathbb{A},\mp}(x),

where
\zeta_{n}(x)\in\textup{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{S}_{n})
is the value of the representation
\zeta_{n}
of the Clifford algebra
\Cl(n;\mathbb{C})
at the vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\subseteq\Cl(n)\subseteq\Cl(n,\mathbb{C})
and
0\neq P\in\textup{Hom}_{\textup{Pin}(n-1)}([\zeta_{n}\otimes\det]|_{\textup{Pin}(n-1)},\zeta_{n-1})
. (Note that the spin representation
\zeta_{n-1}
of
\textup{Pin}(n-1)
occurs in the restriction
[\zeta_{n}\otimes\det]|_{\textup{Pin}(n-1)}
with multiplicity one, where
\det\colon\textup{Pin}(n)\to\textup{O}(n)\to\{\pm 1\}
denotes the determinant character.)

To state our result on the classification of intertwining operators, let

\displaystyle\not{L}_{\textup{even}}=\big\{\big({-}\rho-\tfrac{1}{2}-i,-\rho_{H}-\tfrac{1}{2}-j\big)\colon i,j\in\mathbb{N},i-j\in 2\mathbb{N}\big\},

\displaystyle\not{L}_{\textup{odd}}=\big\{\big({-}\rho-\tfrac{1}{2}-i,-\rho_{H}-\tfrac{1}{2}-j\big)\colon i,j\in\mathbb{N},i-j\in 2\mathbb{N}+1\big\}.

Theorem D

(see Theorems
5.3
,
5.4
and
6.5
)
.

1.

For
\delta+\varepsilon\equiv 0(2)
we have

\displaystyle\dim\textup{Hom}_{\widetilde{H}}\big(\not{\pi}_{\lambda,\delta}|_{\widetilde{H}},\not{\tau}_{\nu,\varepsilon}\big)=\begin{cases}2,&\mbox{for $(\lambda,\nu)\in\not{L}_{\textup{even}}$,}\\
1,&\mbox{for $(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}-\not{L}_{\textup{even}}$,}\end{cases}

and every intertwining operator is given by the distribution kernel
P\not{K}_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},+}
or a regularization of it.

2.

For
\delta+\varepsilon\equiv 1(2)
we have

\displaystyle\dim\textup{Hom}_{\widetilde{H}}\big(\not{\pi}_{\lambda,\delta}|_{\widetilde{H}},\not{\tau}_{\nu,\varepsilon}\big)=\begin{cases}2,&\mbox{for $(\lambda,\nu)\in\not{L}_{\textup{odd}}$,}\\
1,&\mbox{for $(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}-\not{L}_{\textup{odd}}$,}\end{cases}

and every intertwining operator is given by the distribution kernel
P\not{K}_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},-}
or a regularization of it.

In Theorems
5.3
and
5.4
we determine all poles and residues of the meromorphic families
P\not{K}_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},\pm}
, and hence give an explicit description of the distribution kernels of all intertwining operators between spinor-valued principal series representations.

We remark that for
\lambda-\nu=-\frac{1}{2}-2\ell
, resp.
\lambda-\nu=-\frac{3}{2}-2\ell
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a residue of

1.5
Relation to conformal geometry

Let
(X,g)
be a connected oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
with a spin structure. Let
G
denote the conformal group of
X
, i.e., the group of diffeomorphisms
h\colon X\to X
such that there exists a conformal factor
\Omega(h,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}(X)
with
(h^{*}g)_{gx}=\Omega(h,x)^{2}g_{x}
for all
h\in G
and
x\in X
. Write
{\rm or}\colon G\to\{\pm 1\}
for the character of
G
which takes the value
+1
on orientation preserving diffeomorphisms and
-1
on orientation reversing diffeomorphisms. Let
\Sigma X\to X
denote the spin bundle and
C^{\infty}(\Sigma X)
its smooth sections, also called spinors. For a spinor
\sigma\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma X)
and a diffeomorphism
h\in G
the pullback
h^{*}\sigma
can in general not be defined unambiguously, but there exists a double covering
\widetilde{G}\to G
and a smooth action of
\widetilde{G}
on
C^{\infty}(\Sigma X)
which resolves this ambiguity. Abusing notation, we lift the orientation character
{\rm or}
and the conformal factor
\Omega
to the double cover
\widetilde{G}
. This gives rise to a family
\varpi_{\lambda,\delta}
of representations of
\widetilde{G}
on
C^{\infty}(\Sigma X)
depending on two parameters
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
given by

\displaystyle\not{\pi}_{\lambda,\delta}(h)\sigma(x)={\rm or}(h)^{\delta}\Omega\big(h^{-1},x\big)^{\lambda+\frac{n}{2}}(h^{*}\sigma)(x),\qquad x\in X,h\in G,\sigma\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma X).

For
X=S^{n}
with the Euclidean metric the group
\widetilde{G}
is essentially
{\rm Pin}(n+1,1)
and the definition of
\not{\pi}_{\lambda,\delta}
agrees with previous definition since
G/P\simeq S^{n}
.

For an oriented submanifold
Y\subseteq X
we may consider the group
H=\{h\in G\colon hY=Y\}
which acts conformally on
(Y,g|_{Y})
. Fixing a spin structure on
Y
we denote by
C^{\infty}(\Sigma Y)
the space of spinors and by
\not{\tau}_{\nu,\varepsilon}
(
\nu\in\mathbb{C}
,
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
) the corresponding representations of
H
on
C^{\infty}(\Sigma Y)
.

In this context it is natural to ask for a construction and classification of (differential) operators
A\colon C^{\infty}(\Sigma X)\to C^{\infty}(\Sigma Y)
such that
A\circ\not{\pi}_{\lambda,\delta}(h)=\not{\tau}_{\nu,\varepsilon}(h)\circ A
for all
h\in H
. The analogous question for differential forms was previously discussed by Kobayashi–Kubo–Pevzner
[
8
]
and Kobayashi–Speh
[
11
]
. In the model case
(X,Y)=\big(S^{n},S^{n-1}\big)
a complete classification for differential forms was obtained in
[
4
,
8
,
11
]
, and our results in Theorem
D
can be viewed as the analogous classification for spinors. We also refer to
[
2
]
for the case
X=Y=S^{n}
.

2.1

The multiplication map

We consider representations of

be a finite-dimensional representation of

and denote by
\mathbf{1}
the trivial representation of

as the left-regular representation on the space

\displaystyle C^{\infty}(G,V_{\lambda})^{P}=\big\{f\in C^{\infty}(G,V)\colon f(gman)=a^{-\lambda-\rho}\xi(m)^{-1}f(g)\,\forall\,g\in G,\,man\in MAN\big\}.

If we write

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}=G\times_{P}V_{\lambda}\to G/P

for the homogeneous vector bundle associated to the representation

of

, then

of smooth sections of

.

2.2
Distribution sections of vector bundles

Let
V_{\lambda}^{*}=\xi^{\vee}\otimes e^{-\lambda+\rho}\otimes\mathbf{1}
, where
\xi^{\vee}
is the contragredient representation of
\xi
, and write
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{*}=G\times_{P}V_{\lambda}^{*}
for the dual bundle of
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
. We define the space
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G/P,\mathcal{V}_{\lambda})
of distribution sections of the bundle

as the (topological) dual of

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G/P,\mathcal{V}_{\lambda})=C^{\infty}(G/P,\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{*})^{\prime}.

Note that since

embeds

\displaystyle\langle T_{f},\varphi\rangle=\int_{K}\langle f(k),\varphi(k)\rangle\,{\rm d}k\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}(G,V_{\lambda}^{*})^{P},

Let

be another finite-dimensional representation of

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G/P,\mathcal{V}_{\lambda})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G/P,\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}),\qquad u\mapsto f\otimes u,

which is dual to the composition

\displaystyle C^{\infty}(G,E^{\vee}\otimes V_{\lambda}^{*})^{P}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f\otimes}}{{\to}}C^{\infty}(G,E\otimes E^{\vee}\otimes V_{\lambda}^{*})^{P}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c_{*}}}{{\to}}C^{\infty}(G,V_{\lambda}^{*})^{P}

of the pointwise tensor product

and the push-forward by the contraction map

3
The translation principle

We describe a technique, called the

3.1
General technique

Fix a principal series representation
\textup{Ind}_{P}^{G}\big(\xi\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)
of
G

(\tau,E)
be a finite-dimensional representation of
G

\displaystyle\iota_{\tau}\colon\ \textup{Ind}^{G}_{P}\big(\big(\xi\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\otimes E|_{P}\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\textup{Ind}^{G}_{P}\big(\xi\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\otimes E.

(3.1)

When we view both sides as
(V\otimes E)
-valued functions on

\displaystyle(\iota_{\tau}f)(g)=(\textup{id}\otimes\tau(g))f(g),\qquad g\in G.

Now, for any

-stable subspace

\displaystyle\textup{Ind}^{G}_{P}\big(\big(\xi\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\otimes E^{\prime}\big)\hookrightarrow\textup{Ind}^{G}_{P}\big(\big(\xi\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\otimes E|_{P}\big).

Suppose that

and

\displaystyle\textup{Ind}^{G}_{P}\big((\xi\otimes\tau^{\prime})\otimes e^{\lambda+\mu^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\hookrightarrow\textup{Ind}^{G}_{P}\big(\big(\xi\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\otimes E|_{P}\big).

(3.2)

Assuming irreducibility of
\tau

Lemma 3.1

It suffices to treat the case of

\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbb{C}}

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}

\mathfrak{n}

\mathfrak{m}

\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}}

Now let

be any irreducible subrepresentation for

is the

Similarly, for a fixed principal series representation

\displaystyle\textup{Ind}_{P_{H}}^{H}\big(\eta\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\otimes E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\textup{Ind}^{H}_{P_{H}}\big(\big(\eta\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\otimes E|_{P_{H}}\big).

(3.3)

We also take a

3.2
Integral kernels

Recall that
H
-intertwining operators are given by distribution kernels (see Proposition
2.1
). Let us see how the integral kernel behaves under the translation principle. Suppose that

\displaystyle A\colon\ \textup{Ind}^{G}_{P}\big(\xi\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\to\textup{Ind}^{H}_{P_{H}}\big(\eta\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)

is given by a distribution kernel
K^{A}\in(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G/P,\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{*})\otimes W_{\nu})^{\Delta(P_{H})}
in the sense that

\displaystyle Af(h)=\int_{G/P}c\big(f(x)\otimes K^{A}\big(h^{-1}x)\big)\,{\rm d}x,\qquad f\in C^{\infty}(G/P,\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}),

where
c
denotes the contraction map
V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda}^{*}\otimes W_{\nu}\to W_{\nu}
and the integral is meant in the distribution sense (see Section
2.3
for details). Write
i\colon E^{\prime}\to E
for the inclusion map and
p\colon E\to E^{\prime\prime}
for the quotient map, and let
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=G\times_{P}E^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}=H\times_{P_{H}}E^{\prime\prime}
denote the corresponding homogeneous vector bundles over
G/P
and
H/P_{H}
. Let
u\in C^{\infty}(G/P,\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{\lambda})
, then
\Phi(A)u\in C^{\infty}(H/P_{H},\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{\nu})
is given by

\displaystyle\Phi(A)f(h)

\displaystyle=\big(\big(p\circ\tau\big(h^{-1}\big)\big)\otimes\textup{id}_{W_{\nu}}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{=}{}\times\int_{G/P}(\textup{id}_{E}\otimes c)\big(\big((\tau(x)\circ i)\otimes\textup{id}_{V_{\lambda}}\big)(f(x))\otimes K^{A}\big(h^{-1}x\big)\big)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{G/P}c\big(\big(p\circ\tau\big(h^{-1}x\big)\circ i\big)f(x)\otimes K(h^{-1}x)\big)\,{\rm d}x.

This implies:

Proposition 3.4
.

The integral kernel
\Phi\big(K^{A}\big)=K^{\Phi(A)}
of
\Phi(A)
is given by

\displaystyle\Phi\big(K^{A}\big)=\varphi\otimes K^{A},

where
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(G/P,(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})^{\vee})\otimes E^{\prime\prime}\simeq C^{\infty}(G,(E^{\prime})^{\vee}\otimes E^{\prime\prime})^{P}
is given by

\displaystyle\varphi(g)=p\circ\tau(g)\circ i\in\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(E^{\prime},E^{\prime\prime})\simeq(E^{\prime})^{\vee}\otimes E^{\prime\prime}.

Remark 3.5
.

Since the operator
\Phi
is given by multiplication with the fixed smooth function
\varphi
, and the multiplication map

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G/P,\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{*})\otimes W_{\nu}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(G/P,\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{*}\otimes(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})^{\vee}\big)\otimes(W_{\nu}\otimes E^{\prime\prime}),\qquad K\mapsto\varphi\otimes K

is continuous, the operator
\Phi
maps holomorphic families of distributions to holomorphic families. More precisely, if
K_{z}\in(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G/P,\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{*})\otimes W_{\nu})^{\Delta(P_{H})}
depends holomorphically on
z\in\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then
\Phi(K_{z})
depends holomorphically on
z\in\Omega
. More generally, if
K_{z}
depends meromorphically on
z\in\Omega
with poles in the set
\Sigma\subseteq\Omega
, then
\Phi(K_{z})
depends meromorphically on
z\in\Omega
and its poles are contained in
\Sigma
. However, it may of course happen that
K_{z}
has a pole at
z=z_{0}
whereas
\Phi(K_{z})
is regular at
z=z_{0}
since the multiplication map
K\mapsto\varphi\otimes K
can have a non-trivial kernel (see, e.g., Remark
4.9
). This also implies that a holomorphic/meromorphic family
K_{z}
, which does not vanish identically, might be mapped to
\Phi(K_{z})=0
for all
z\in\Omega
(see, e.g., Remark
4.5
). If, however, the family
K_{z}
has generically full support, i.e.,
\supp K_{z}=G/P
for generic
z\in\Omega
, then
\Phi(K_{z})=\varphi\otimes K_{z}
cannot be identically zero for all
z\in\Omega
. In fact,
\varphi
is an analytic function which is non-zero due to the irreducibility of
(\tau,E)
, so it has full support
\supp\varphi=G/P
and hence

\displaystyle\supp K_{z}=G/P\quad\Rightarrow\quad\supp(\varphi\otimes K_{z})=G/P.

Remark 3.6
.

Since
\varphi(g)
is a matrix coefficient of a finite-dimensional repesentation, it is obviously smooth in
g\in G
. In view of Theorem
2.2
one can in some cases study the distribution kernels by their restriction to
\overline{\mathfrak{n}}\simeq\overline{N}\hookrightarrow G/P
. On
\overline{\mathfrak{n}}
the function
\varphi(g)
is actually a polynomial. In fact, the nilpotency of
\overline{\mathfrak{n}}
implies that there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\tau(X_{1})\cdots\tau(X_{n})=0
for all
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in\overline{\mathfrak{n}}
and
n\geq N
. This shows that
\varphi|_{\overline{\mathfrak{n}}}
is a polynomial of degree at most
N
.

4.1
Parabolic subgroups and the symmetric pair

Let

\textup{GL}(n+2,\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+2}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad x\mapsto x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n+1}^{2}-x_{n+2}^{2}.

We choose the (minimal) parabolic subgroup

\displaystyle H_{0}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&&1\\
&\mathbf{0}_{n}&\\

and

for

\displaystyle M=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}\varepsilon&&\\
&m&\\
&&\varepsilon\end{matrix}\right)\colon m\in\textup{O}(n),\varepsilon=\pm 1\right\}\simeq\textup{O}(n)\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

Let

and use this identification to parametrize

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathfrak{n}}\xrightarrow{\exp}\overline{N},\qquad x\mapsto\overline{n}_{x}=\left(\begin{matrix}1+|x|^{2}/2&x^{\top}&|x|^{2}/2\\
x&\mathbf{1}_{n}&x\\
-|x|^{2}/2&-x^{\top}&1-|x|^{2}/2\end{matrix}\right).

\displaystyle\textup{Ad}(m)x=mx,\quad m\in\textup{O}(n),\qquad\textup{Ad}(m_{0})x=-x.

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{0}=\textup{diag}(1,\ldots,1,-1).

Lemma 4.1

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}\overline{n}_{x}\in\overline{N}MAN\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}-\{0\},

\displaystyle a\big(\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}\overline{n}_{x}\big)^{\lambda}=|x|^{2\lambda},\qquad m\big(\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}\overline{n}_{x}\big)=\left(\begin{matrix}1&&\\
&\mathbf{1}_{n}-2\frac{xx^{\top}}{|x|^{2}}&\\
&&1\end{matrix}\right).

be the involution of

\displaystyle M_{H}=M\cap H=\textup{O}(n-1)\cup m_{0}\textup{O}(n-1)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathfrak{a}_{H}=\mathfrak{a}=\mathbb{R}H_{0}.

\displaystyle\sigma(x)=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},-x_{n}),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

4.2
Principal series representations and symmetry breaking operators

Denote by
\textup{sgn}\colon M\to\{\pm 1\}
the character of
M=\textup{O}(n)\cup m_{0}\textup{O}(n)
given by
\textup{sgn}(m)=1
for
m\in\textup{O}(n)
and
\textup{sgn}(m_{0})=-1
. Abusing notation we also write
sgn
for the corresponding character of
M_{H}
. For
\delta,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
\lambda,\nu\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\simeq\mathbb{C}
we define the scalar principal series representations (smooth normalized parabolic induction)

\displaystyle\pi_{\lambda,\delta}=\textup{Ind}_{P}^{G}\big(\textup{sgn}^{\delta}\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\tau_{\nu,\varepsilon}=\textup{Ind}_{P_{H}}^{H}\big(\textup{sgn}^{\varepsilon}\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big).

We consider the space
\textup{Hom}_{H}(\pi_{\lambda,\delta}|_{H},\tau_{\nu,\varepsilon})
of symmetry breaking operators between
\pi_{\lambda,\delta}
and
\tau_{\nu,\varepsilon}
. By Theorem
2.2
every such operator is given by a distribution kernel
K\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}})^{M_{H}A_{H},\mathfrak{n}_{H}}
satisfying certain invariance conditions for the action of
M_{H}A_{H}
and
\mathfrak{n}_{H}
. Since
m_{0}\in M_{H}
acts by
K(x)\mapsto(-1)^{\delta+\varepsilon}K(-x)
, it is clear that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}})^{M_{H}A_{H},\mathfrak{n}_{H}}
only depends on the parity of
\delta+\varepsilon
. Identifying
\overline{\mathfrak{n}}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}
as above we write
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}
, resp.
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}
, for the space
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)^{M_{H}A_{H},\mathfrak{n}_{H}}
with
\delta+\varepsilon\equiv 0(2)
, resp.
\delta+\varepsilon\equiv 1(2)
. Then, taking distribution kernels is a linear isomorphism

\displaystyle\textup{Hom}_{H}(\pi_{\lambda,\delta}|_{H},\tau_{\nu,\varepsilon})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\begin{cases}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}&\mbox{for $\delta+\varepsilon\equiv 0(2)$,}\\
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}&\mbox{for $\delta+\varepsilon\equiv 1(2)$.}\end{cases}

4.3
Construction of symmetry breaking operators

In this section we describe all intertwining operators in
\textup{Hom}_{H}(\pi_{\lambda,\delta},\tau_{\nu,\varepsilon})
for all
\lambda,\nu\in\mathbb{C}
,
\delta,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. We note that the notation is essentially due to Kobayashi–Speh
[
10
]
.

4.3.1
Spherical principal series

For
\delta=\varepsilon=0
the representations
\pi_{\lambda,\delta}
and
\tau_{\nu,\varepsilon}
are spherical, i.e. possess a vector invariant under a maximal compact subgroup. In this setting, Section
2.4
provides a meromorphic family of intertwining operators
\mathbb{A}_{\lambda,\nu}\in\textup{Hom}_{H}(\pi_{\lambda,0}|_{H},\tau_{\nu,0})
given by the distribution kernels

\displaystyle K_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},+}(x)=|x_{n}|^{\lambda+\nu-\frac{1}{2}}\big(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\big)^{-\nu-\frac{n-1}{2}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By analyzing the poles and residues of
K^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}
explicitly, Kobayashi–Speh
[
10
]
completely determine the space
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}
for all
\lambda,\nu\in\mathbb{C}
. (Note that our parameters
(\lambda,\nu)
are normalized such that
\pi_{\lambda,\delta}
and
\tau_{\nu,\varepsilon}
are unitary for
\lambda,\nu\in i\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, in our notation one has to replace
(\lambda,\nu)
by
(\lambda-\rho,\nu-\rho_{H})
to obtain Kobayashi–Speh’s notation, see
[
10
]
.) To summarize their results let

\displaystyle\,\,\backslash\kern-8.00003pt{\backslash}\;^{+}=\big\{(\lambda,\nu)\colon\lambda+\nu=-\tfrac{1}{2}-2k,k\in\mathbb{N}\big\},

\displaystyle\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{+}=\big\{(\lambda,\nu)\colon\lambda-\nu=-\tfrac{1}{2}-2\ell,\ell\in\mathbb{N}\big\},

\displaystyle L_{\textup{even}}=\big\{(\lambda,\nu)=(-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j)\colon i,j\in\mathbb{N},i-j\in 2\mathbb{N}\big\}\subseteq\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{+}.

For
(\lambda,\nu)\in\,\,\backslash\kern-8.00003pt{\backslash}\;^{+}
with
\lambda+\nu=-\frac{1}{2}-2k
we further define

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{B},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu+\frac{1}{2}\big)\big)}\big(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\big)^{-\nu-\frac{n-1}{2}}\delta^{(2k)}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{i}(2k)!\big(\nu+\frac{n-1}{2}\big)_{i}}{i!(2k-2i)!\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu+\frac{1}{2}\big)\big)}|x^{\prime}|^{1-n-2\nu-2i}\delta^{(2k-2i)}(x_{n}),

and for
(\lambda,\nu)\in\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{+}
with
\lambda-\nu=-\frac{1}{2}-2\ell
we put

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\frac{2^{2\ell-2j}(\nu-\ell)_{\ell-j}}{j!(2\ell-2j)!}\big(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}^{j}\partial_{n}^{2\ell-2j}\delta\big)(x).

Note that both
\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{B},+}_{\lambda,\nu}
and
\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},+}_{\lambda,\nu}
depend holomorphically on
\nu\in\mathbb{C}
(or
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
).

Theorem 4.2
.

The renormalized distribution

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\frac{K^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda+\nu+\frac{1}{2}\big)\big)\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu+\frac{1}{2}\big)\big)}

depends holomorphically on
(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
and vanishes only for
(\lambda,\nu)\in L_{\textup{even}}
. More precisely,

1.

For
(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}-(\,\,\backslash\kern-8.00003pt{\backslash}\;^{+}\cup\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{+})
we have
\supp\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.

For
(\lambda,\nu)\in\,\,\backslash\kern-8.00003pt{\backslash}\;^{+}-\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{+}
with
\lambda+\nu=-\frac{1}{2}-2k
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{k}k!}{(2k)!}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{B},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x).

In particular,
\supp\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.

3.

For
(\lambda,\nu)\in\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{+}-L_{\textup{even}}
with
\lambda-\nu=-\frac{1}{2}-2\ell
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{\ell}\ell!\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{2^{2\ell}\Gamma\big(\nu+\frac{n-1}{2}\big)}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x).

In particular,
\supp\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}=\{0\}
.

4.

For
(-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j)\in L_{\textup{even}}
,
2\ell=i-j
, restricting the function
(\lambda,\nu)\mapsto\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}
to two different complex hyperplanes in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
through
(-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j)
and renormalizing gives two holomorphic families of distributions:

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{\ell}2^{2\ell}}{\ell!\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\Gamma(\nu+\rho_{H})\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x),\qquad\lambda-\nu=-\tfrac{1}{2}-2\ell,

\displaystyle{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\Gamma\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu+\tfrac{1}{2}\big)\big)\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x),\qquad\nu=-\rho_{H}-j.

Their special values at
(-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j)
satisfy

\displaystyle\supp\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},+}_{-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j}=\{0\},

\displaystyle\supp{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}^{n},&\mbox{for $n$ even,}\\
\mathbb{R}^{n-1},&\mbox{for $n$ odd,}\end{cases}

more precisely, for
n
odd and
2k=i+j+n-1

\displaystyle{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j}=\frac{(-1)^{k}k!}{(2k)!}\big(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\big)^{j}\delta^{(2k)}(x_{n}).

Proof.

This is a summary of the results of
[
10
]
.
∎

These results can be used to describe the space
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}
:

Theorem 4.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu},&\mbox{for $(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}-L_{\textup{even}}$,}\\
\mathbb{C}{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}\oplus\mathbb{C}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},+}_{\lambda,\nu},&\mbox{for $(\lambda,\nu)\in L_{\textup{even}}$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

See
[
10
, Theorem 1.9]
and also
[
13
, Theorem 4.9]
.
∎

4.3.2
Scalar principal series

We use the translation principle to describe
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}
in terms of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}
.

Let
(\tau,E)
be the defining representation of
G=\textup{O}(n+1,1)
on
E=\mathbb{C}^{n+2}
. Then

\displaystyle i\colon\ E^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}\hookrightarrow E,\qquad z\mapsto(z,0,\ldots,0,z)^{\top}

is the maximal subspace on which
N
acts trivially, and
E^{\prime}|_{P}=\textup{sgn}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes\mathbf{1}
. For the
P_{H}
-quotient space we choose

\displaystyle p\colon\ E\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n+2})\mapsto z_{n+1},

then
E^{\prime\prime}|_{P_{H}}=\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}
. Now let us compute the
(E^{\prime})^{*}\otimes E^{\prime\prime}
-valued function
p\circ\tau(g)\circ i
on
\overline{N}
. For
g=\overline{n}_{x}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle p\circ\tau(g)\circ i(1)^{\top}=p\big(1+|x|^{2},2x,1-|x|^{2}\big)^{\top}=2x_{n}.

Hence, by Theorem
3.3
, we get a linear map

\displaystyle x_{n}\colon\ \mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{\pm}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda+1,\nu}^{\mp},\qquad K(x)\mapsto x_{n}K(x).

Applying this map to the meromorphic family of distributions
K^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)
we obtain another meromorphic family

\displaystyle K^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=x_{n}\cdot K^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda-1,\nu}=\textup{sgn}(x_{n})|x_{n}|^{\lambda+\nu-\frac{1}{2}}\big(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\big)^{-\nu-\frac{n-1}{2}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

To normalize
K^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)
so that it becomes holomorphic in
(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
we first consider the kernel of the map

\displaystyle x_{n}\colon\ \mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda+1,\nu}^{-}.

(4.1)

Lemma 4.4
.

We have

\displaystyle\big\{K\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}\colon x_{n}K=0\big\}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{B},+}_{\lambda,\nu},&\mbox{for $\lambda+\nu=-\frac{1}{2}$,}\\
\mathbb{C}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},+}_{\lambda,\nu},&\mbox{for $\lambda-\nu=-\frac{1}{2}$,}\\
\{0\},&\mbox{else.}\end{cases}

Note that for
(\lambda,\nu)=\big({-}\frac{1}{2},0\big)
we have
\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{B},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\delta(x)
.

Proof.

If
K\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}
such that
x_{n}K=0
then clearly
\supp(K)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Hence,

\displaystyle K=\sum_{m=0}^{M}u_{m}(x^{\prime})\delta^{(m)}(x_{n})

for some distributions
u_{m}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\big)
. Since
x_{n}\delta^{(m)}(x_{n})=-m\delta^{(m-1)}(x_{n})
we must have
u_{m}=0
for
m>0
, so that
K(x)=u_{0}(x^{\prime})\delta(x_{n})
. By the classification in Theorems
4.2
and
4.3
the only possibilities for such
K
in the space
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{+}
are

\displaystyle K(x)=\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{B},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\frac{|x^{\prime}|^{1-n-2\nu}\delta(x_{n})}{\Gamma(-\nu)}

with
\lambda+\nu=-\frac{1}{2}
(i.e.,
k=0
) and

\displaystyle K(x)=\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\delta(x)

with
\lambda-\nu=-\frac{1}{2}
(i.e.,
\ell=0
).
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Lemma
4.4
implies that the families

\displaystyle K^{\mathbb{B}}_{\nu}=\widetilde{K}_{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu,\nu}^{\mathbb{B},+}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad K^{\mathbb{C}}_{\nu}=\widetilde{K}_{-\frac{1}{2}+\nu,\nu}^{\mathbb{C},+},

which depend holomorphically on
\nu\in\mathbb{C}
and are not identically zero, are mapped to identically zero families by (
4.1
), i.e.,
x_{n}K^{\mathbb{B}}_{\nu}=x_{n}K^{\mathbb{C}}_{\nu}=0
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{C}
. However, the family
\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}
which depends holomorphically on
(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
has generically full support, i.e.,
\supp\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
for generic
(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, so it follows from Remark
3.5
that also
\supp x_{n}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
for generic
(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. In particular, the holomorphic family
x_{n}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}
is not identically zero.

For the full classification of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}
we also need to understand the kernel of the map

\displaystyle x_{n}\colon\ \mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda+1,\nu}^{+}.

Lemma 4.6
.

We have

\displaystyle\big\{K\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}\colon x_{n}K=0\big\}=\{0\}.

Proof.

As in the previous proof any
K
with
x_{n}K=0
must be of the form
K(x)=u_{0}(x^{\prime})\delta(x_{n})
. Now, if
K\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}
then by the invariance under
m_{0}\in M_{H}
we have
u(-x)=-u(x)
and since
\delta(-x_{n})=\delta(x_{n})
this implies

\displaystyle u_{0}(-x^{\prime})=-u_{0}(x^{\prime}).

On the other hand, invariance of
K
under
\textup{O}(n-1)\subseteq M_{H}
implies
u_{0}(mx^{\prime})=u_{0}(x^{\prime})
for all
m\in\textup{O}(n-1)
, in particular for
m=-\mathbf{1}
, hence

\displaystyle u_{0}(-x^{\prime})=u_{0}(x^{\prime}).

This shows that
u_{0}=0
and the proof is complete.
∎

To state the analogue of Theorem
4.2
for
K^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)
let

\displaystyle\,\,\backslash\kern-8.00003pt{\backslash}\;^{-}=\big\{(\lambda,\nu)\colon\lambda+\nu=-\tfrac{3}{2}-2k,k\in\mathbb{N}\big\},

\displaystyle\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{-}=\big\{(\lambda,\nu)\colon\lambda-\nu=-\tfrac{3}{2}-2\ell,\ell\in\mathbb{N}\big\},

\displaystyle L_{\textup{odd}}=\big\{(\lambda,\nu)=(-\rho-i-1,-\rho_{H}-j)\colon i,j\in\mathbb{N},i-j\in 2\mathbb{N}\big\}\subseteq\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{-}.

For

with

we further define

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{B},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu+\frac{3}{2}\big)\big)}\big(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\big)^{-\nu-\frac{n-1}{2}}\delta^{(2k+1)}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{i}(2k+1)!\big(\nu+\frac{n-1}{2}\big)_{i}}{i!(2k-2i+1)!\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu+\frac{3}{2}\big)\big)}|x^{\prime}|^{1-n-2\nu-2i}\delta^{(2k-2i+1)}(x_{n}),

Theorem 4.7
.

The renormalized distribution

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\frac{K^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda+\nu+\frac{3}{2}\big)\big)\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu+\frac{3}{2}\big)\big)}

and vanishes only for

. More precisely,

1.

For
(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}-(\,\,\backslash\kern-8.00003pt{\backslash}\;^{-}\cup\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{-})
we have
\supp\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.

For
(\lambda,\nu)\in\,\,\backslash\kern-8.00003pt{\backslash}\;^{-}-\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{-}
with

,

, we have

In particular,

.

For

with

,

, we have

In particular,

.

4.

For
(-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j)\in L_{\textup{odd}}
,
2\ell=i-j-1
, restricting the function
(\lambda,\nu)\mapsto\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}
to two different complex hyperplanes in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
through
(-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j)

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{\ell}2^{2\ell}}{\ell!\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\Gamma(\nu+\rho_{H})\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x),\qquad\lambda-\nu=-\tfrac{3}{2}-2\ell,

\displaystyle{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\Gamma\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu+\tfrac{3}{2}\big)\big)\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x),\qquad\nu=-\rho_{H}-j.

Their special values at
(-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j)
satisfy

\displaystyle\supp\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},-}_{-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j}=\{0\},

\displaystyle\supp{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}^{n},&\mbox{for $n$ even,}\\
\mathbb{R}^{n-1},&\mbox{for $n$ odd,}\end{cases}

more precisely, for
n
odd and
2k=n+i+j-2

\displaystyle{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}k!}{(2k+1)!}\big(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\big)^{j}\delta^{(2k+1)}(x_{n}).

Proof.

We first apply the map

\displaystyle x_{n}\colon\ \mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda-1,\nu}^{+}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}

to the holomorphic family
\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda-1,\nu}
and obtain a holomorphic family of distributions

\displaystyle x_{n}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda-1,\nu}(x)=\frac{K^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda+\nu-\frac{1}{2}\big)\big)\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu-\frac{1}{2}\big)\big)}.

By Theorem
4.2
and Lemma
4.4
we have
x_{n}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda-1,\nu}=0
if
\lambda+\nu=\frac{1}{2}
or
\lambda-\nu=\frac{1}{2}
. We can therefore renormalize the kernels

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\frac{x_{n}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda-1,\nu}(x)}{\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda+\nu-\frac{1}{2}\big)\cdot\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu-\frac{1}{2}\big)}=\frac{K^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda+\nu+\frac{3}{2}\big)\big)\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu+\frac{3}{2}\big)\big)}.

This shows that
\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)
depends holomorphically on
(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Next, we apply the map

\displaystyle x_{n}\colon\ \mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda+1,\nu}^{+}

(4.2)

to
\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)
and find

\displaystyle x_{n}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}=\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda+1,\nu}.

By Lemma
4.6
the map (
4.2
) is injective, hence
\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}=0
if and only if
\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda+1,\nu}=0
, which is only the case for
(\lambda+1,\nu)\in L_{\textup{even}}
, i.e.,
(\lambda,\nu)\in L_{\textup{odd}}
.

1.

Let
(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}-(\,\,\backslash\kern-8.00003pt{\backslash}\;^{-}\cup\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{-})
. Then
(\lambda+1,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}-(\,\,\backslash\kern-8.00003pt{\backslash}\;^{+}\cup\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{+})
and hence
\supp\big(x_{n}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}\big)=\supp\big(\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda+1,\nu}\big)=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This implies
\supp\big(\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}\big)=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.

Let
(\lambda,\nu)\in\,\,\backslash\kern-8.00003pt{\backslash}\;^{-}-\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{-}
with
\lambda+\nu=-\frac{3}{2}-2k
, then it is easy to see, using
x_{n}\delta^{(m)}(x_{n})=-m\delta^{(m-1)}(x_{n})
, that

\displaystyle x_{n}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{B},+}_{\lambda-1,\nu}=2(k+1)(\nu+k+1)\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{B},-}_{\lambda,\nu}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda+\nu-\frac{1}{2}\big)\cdot\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu-\frac{1}{2}\big)}x_{n}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda-1,\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k+1)(\nu+k+1)}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}(k+1)!}{(2k+2)!}x_{n}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{B},+}_{\lambda-1,\nu}=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}k!}{(2k+1)!}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{B},-}_{\lambda,\nu}.

3.

For
(\lambda,\nu)\in\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{-}-L_{\textup{odd}}
the same method as in (2) applies to show (3). Here we use that
x_{n}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},+}_{\lambda-1,\nu}=-4(\nu-\ell-1)\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},-}_{\lambda,\nu}
for
\lambda-\nu=-\frac{3}{2}-2\ell
.

4.

Now let
(-\rho-i,-\rho_{H}-j)\in L_{\textup{odd}}
. The first statement about
\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)
follows immediately from (3). For the second statement note that
(-\rho-(i+1),-\rho_{H}-j)\in L_{\textup{even}}
and we can use Theorem
4.2
(4). Restricting to
\nu=-\rho_{H}-j
we have

\displaystyle{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\Gamma\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu+\tfrac{3}{2}\big)\big)\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu+\tfrac{3}{2}\big)\big)x_{n}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda-1,\nu}(x)}{\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda+\nu-\frac{1}{2}\big)\cdot\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda-\nu-\frac{1}{2}\big)}=\frac{x_{n}{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda-1,\nu}(x)}{\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda+\nu-\frac{1}{2}\big)}.

The denominator is equal to
\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\lambda+\nu-\tfrac{1}{2}\big)=-\frac{1}{2}(n+i+j)=-(k+1)\neq 0
, whence
{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}
depends holomorphically on
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Moreover, for even
n
we have
\supp{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda-1,\nu}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
so that
\supp\big(x_{n}{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda-1,\nu}\big)=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and hence
\supp{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For odd
n
it follows from
x_{n}\delta^{(m)}(x_{n})=-m\delta^{(m-1)}(x_{n})
that

\displaystyle{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{k+1}\cdot\frac{(-1)^{k+1}(k+1)!}{(2k+2)!}x_{n}\big(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\big)^{j}\delta^{(2k+2)}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}k!}{(2k+1)!}\big(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\big)^{j}\delta^{(2k+1)}(x_{n}).

∎

Remark 4.8
.

The differential intertwining operators belonging to the distribution kernels
\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},+}_{\lambda,\nu}
are the even order conformally covariant differential operators found by Juhl
[
5
]
, sometimes also referred to as
Juhl operators
. Kobayashi–Speh showed that they are obtained as residue families of the non-local intertwining operators with kernels
\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},+}_{\lambda,\nu}
. Theorem
4.7
proves that also the odd order Juhl operators with integral kernels
\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},-}_{\lambda,\nu}
can be obtained in this way. Further, the translation principle explains the following relation between even and odd order Juhl operators:

\displaystyle x_{n}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},+}_{\lambda,\nu}=-2\big(\lambda+\nu+\tfrac{1}{2}\big)\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},-}_{\lambda+1,\nu}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad x_{n}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},-}_{\lambda,\nu}=-\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},+}_{\lambda+1,\nu}.

Remark 4.9
.

Theorem
4.2
implies that the unnormalized meromorphic family
K_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},+}
has poles for
(\lambda,\nu)\in\,\,\backslash\kern-8.00003pt{\backslash}\;^{+}\cup\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{+}
. Multiplication with
x_{n}
gives the meromorphic family
x_{n}K_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},+}=K_{\lambda+1,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},-}
which, by Theorem
4.7
, has poles for
(\lambda+1,\nu)\in\,\,\backslash\kern-8.00003pt{\backslash}\;^{-}\cup\,\,/\kern-8.00003pt{/}\;^{-}
. This shows that for
\lambda+\nu=-\frac{1}{2}
and
\lambda+\nu=-\frac{1}{2}
the family
K_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},+}
has a pole, while
x_{n}K_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},+}
does not.

Using the translation principle we can finally determine the space
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}
completely:

Theorem 4.10
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu},&\mbox{for $(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}-L_{\textup{odd}}$,}\\
\mathbb{C}{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}\oplus\mathbb{C}\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},-}_{\lambda,\nu},&\mbox{for $(\lambda,\nu)\in L_{\textup{odd}}$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

By Lemma
4.6
the map

\displaystyle x_{n}\colon\ \mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda+1,\nu}^{+}

is injective and hence

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}\leq\dim\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda+1,\nu}^{+}.

(4.3)

For
(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}-L_{\textup{odd}}
we have
0\neq\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}
, and hence the left hand side of (
4.3
) is
\geq 1
. For
(\lambda,\nu)\in L_{\textup{odd}}
we have
{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu},\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},-}_{\lambda,\nu}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{-}
and these distributions are linearly independent since

\displaystyle\supp\widetilde{K}^{\mathbb{C},-}_{\lambda,\nu}=\{0\}\subsetneq\supp{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\hbox{$\displaystyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\textstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}{\widetilde{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widetilde{K}$}}}}^{\mathbb{A},-}_{\lambda,\nu}.

This shows that the left hand side of (
4.3
) is
\geq 2
. Now note that
(\lambda,\nu)\in L_{\textup{odd}}
if and only if
(\lambda+1,\nu)\in L_{\textup{even}}
, and therefore (
4.3
) is actually an equality for all
(\lambda,\nu)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, thanks to Theorem
4.3
. This finishes the proof.
∎

5
Example:
\boldsymbol{(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{H})=(\textup{Pin}(n+1,1),\textup{Pin}(n,1))}

We explicitly construct intertwining operators between principal series representations of
\widetilde{G}=\textup{Pin}(n+1,1)
and
\widetilde{H}=\textup{Pin}(n,1)
induced from fundamental spin representations of
\widetilde{M}\simeq\textup{Pin}(n)\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
\widetilde{M}_{H}\simeq\textup{Pin}(n-1)\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
using the translation principle.

5.1
Parabolic subgroups and the symmetric pair

We consider the two-fold covering

\displaystyle q\colon\ \widetilde{G}=\textup{Pin}(n+1,1)\to\textup{O}(n+1,1)=G,

realized inside the Clifford algebra
\Cl(n+1,1)
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
. For details on the definition and properties of the pin groups and Clifford algebras we refer the reader to Appendix
A.1
. In what follows we will write
\widetilde{S}=q^{-1}(S)
for any subgroup
S\subseteq G
.

We choose the same parabolic subgroup
P=MAN\subseteq G
as in Section
4.1
, then
\widetilde{M}=\textup{Pin}(n)\cdot\{1,m_{0}=e_{1}e_{n+2}\}
, where the embedding of
\textup{Pin}(n)
into
\textup{Pin}(n+1,1)
is the restriction of the embedding of Clifford algebras
\Cl(n)\hookrightarrow\Cl(n+1,1)
induced by

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1},\qquad e_{i}\mapsto e_{i+1},\qquad 1\leq i\leq n.

We note that
m_{0}=e_{1}e_{n+2}
commutes with
\textup{Pin}(n)
and hence
\widetilde{M}\simeq\textup{Pin}(n)\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Both
A
and
N
split in the cover, so that
\widetilde{A}\simeq A\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
\widetilde{N}\simeq N\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, and we identify
A
and
N
with
\widetilde{A}_{0},\widetilde{N}_{0}\subseteq\widetilde{G}
. Then
\widetilde{P}=\widetilde{M}AN
is the Langlands decomposition of the parabolic subgroup
\widetilde{P}
of
\widetilde{G}
.

For the Weyl group element
w_{0}
we choose the representative

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{0}=\pm e_{n+2}

(5.1)

with the sign yet to be determined. Since
q(\tilde{w}_{0})=\textup{diag}(1,\ldots,1,-1)
, the element
\tilde{w}_{0}
corresponds to the representative chosen in Section
4.1
for the group
G
. Hence, by Lemma
4.1
we find

\displaystyle q\big(m\big(\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}\overline{n}_{x}^{-1}\big)^{-1}\big)=\left(\begin{matrix}1&&\\
&\mathbf{1}_{n}-2\frac{xx^{\top}}{|x|^{2}}&\\
&&1\end{matrix}\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}-\{0\}.

The matrix
\mathbf{1}_{n}-2\frac{xx^{\top}}{|x|^{2}}\in\textup{O}(n)
is the orthogonal reflection in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
at the hyperplane orthogonal to

, and the corresponding elements in the cover
\textup{Pin}(n)\subseteq\Cl(n)
are
\pm\frac{x}{|x|}
. Therefore, we may choose the sign in (
5.1
) so that under the identification
\widetilde{M}\simeq\textup{Pin}(n)\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
we have

\displaystyle m\big(\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}\overline{n}_{x}^{-1}\big)^{-1}=-\tfrac{x}{|x|}\in\textup{Pin}(n)\subseteq\widetilde{M}.

Further we note that

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{0}m\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}=\alpha(m)=\det(m)m,\qquad m\in\textup{Pin}(n),\qquad\tilde{w}_{0}m_{0}\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}=-m_{0},

where
\alpha
denotes the canonical automorphism of the Clifford algebra
\Cl(n)
and
\det\colon\textup{Pin}(n)\to\textup{O}(n)\to\{\pm 1\}
the determinant character of
\textup{Pin}(n)
.

The symmetric pair
(G,H)=(\textup{O}(n+1,1),\textup{O}(n,1))
as introduced in Section
4.1
takes in the cover the form
\big(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{H}\big)=(\textup{Pin}(n+1,1),\textup{Pin}(n,1))
. We have

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}=\widetilde{G}\cap\Cl(n,1),

where the embedding of
\Cl(n,1)
into
\Cl(n+1,1)
is induced by the embedding

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n,1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1},\qquad x\mapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},0,x_{n+1}).

Further,
\widetilde{P}_{H}=\widetilde{P}\cap\widetilde{H}=\widetilde{M}_{H}AN_{H}
is a parabolic subgroup of
\widetilde{H}
with
\widetilde{M}_{H}=\textup{Pin}(n-1)\cdot\{1,m_{0}\}
.

5.2
Principal series representations and symmetry breaking operators

For the fundamental spin representations of
\textup{Pin}(n)
we use the notation introduced in Appendix
A.2
. The group
\textup{Pin}(n)
has for even
n
one fundamental spin representation, and for odd
n
two fundamental spin representations. For
n
even we have
\zeta_{n}\otimes\det\simeq\zeta_{n}
for the fundamental spin representation
\zeta_{n}
, and for
n
odd and
\zeta_{n}
any fundamental spin representation of
\textup{Pin}(n)
the representations
\zeta_{n}
and
\zeta_{n}\otimes\det
are non-equivalent.

Denote by
\textup{sgn}\colon\{\mathbf{1},m_{0}\}\to\{\pm 1\}
the non-trivial character of the two-element group

. Then for
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and a fundamental spin representation
\zeta_{n}
of
\textup{Pin}(n)
we consider the representation
\zeta_{n}\otimes\textup{sgn}^{\delta}
of
\widetilde{M}=\textup{Pin}(n)\times\{\mathbf{1},m_{0}\}
. Note that

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{0}\big(\zeta_{n}\otimes\textup{sgn}^{\delta}\big)=[\zeta_{n}\otimes\det]\otimes\textup{sgn}^{1-\delta}.

(5.2)

For
(\zeta_{n},\mathbb{S}_{n})
a fundamental spin representation of
\textup{Pin}(n)
,

and

Similarly, for
(\zeta_{n-1},\mathbb{S}_{n-1})
a fundamental spin representation of
\textup{Pin}(n-1)
,

and

The main result of this section is the construction of symmetry breaking operators

\displaystyle A\in\textup{Hom}_{\widetilde{H}}\big(\not{\pi}_{\lambda,\delta}|_{\widetilde{H}},\not{\tau}_{\nu,\varepsilon}\big).

(5.3)

In Section
6

By Theorem

every intertwining operator in (

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{S}_{n},\mathbb{S}_{n-1})\big)_{\lambda,\nu}^{\pm}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{S}_{n},\mathbb{S}_{n-1})\big)

5.3
Construction of symmetry breaking operators

We extend the representation
(\zeta_{n},\mathbb{S}_{n})

\displaystyle w_{0}\big(\tau^{\prime}\otimes e^{\mu^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\simeq(\zeta\otimes\textup{sgn})\otimes e^{-\mu^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbf{1}.

Further, using Lemma

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}\big(m\big(\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}\overline{n}_{x}^{-1}\big)^{-1}\big)\cdot a\big(\tilde{w}_{0}^{-1}\overline{n}_{x}^{-1}\big)^{-\mu^{\prime}}=[\zeta\otimes\det]\big({-}\tfrac{x}{|x|}\big)\cdot|x|^{-2\mu^{\prime}}=\zeta(x).

Then Corollary

\displaystyle\textup{Hom}_{H}\big(\pi_{\lambda,\delta}|_{H},\tau_{\nu,\varepsilon}\big)\to\textup{Hom}_{\widetilde{H}}\big(\not{\pi}_{\lambda+\frac{1}{2},1-\delta},\textup{Ind}_{\widetilde{P}_{H}}^{\widetilde{H}}\big(\big([\zeta\otimes\det]\otimes\textup{sgn}^{\varepsilon}\big)\otimes e^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big)\big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\textup{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{S})\big),\qquad K(x)\mapsto\zeta(x)\cdot K(x).

(5.4)

To obtain intertwining operators into
\not{\tau}_{\nu,\varepsilon}
note that
\zeta_{n{-}1}
occurs in the restriction

with multiplicity one, and fix a projection

\displaystyle\textup{Hom}_{H}\big(\pi_{\lambda,\delta}|_{H},\tau_{\nu,\varepsilon}\big)\to\textup{Hom}_{\widetilde{H}}\big(\not{\pi}_{\lambda+\frac{1}{2},1-\delta},\not{\tau}_{\nu-\frac{1}{2},\varepsilon}\big),

By Theorems

and

-valued kernels

\displaystyle P\not{K}^{\mathbb{A},\pm}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=(P\zeta(x))\cdot K^{\mathbb{A},\mp}_{\lambda-\frac{1}{2},\nu+\frac{1}{2}}(x).

For
n

-representation

to
\textup{Pin}(n-1)
is isomorphic to
\zeta_{n-1}
, so the map

is an isomorphism. Therefore, we can as well study the

-valued distributions

\displaystyle\not{K}_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},\pm}(x)=\zeta(x)\cdot K_{\lambda-\frac{1}{2},\nu+\frac{1}{2}}^{\mathbb{A},\mp}(x).

For
n

\textup{Pin}(n)
-representation

to
\textup{Pin}(n-1)

and
\zeta_{n-1}\otimes\det
. The next result shows that the poles of
P\not{K}_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},\pm}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\textup{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{S}_{n},\mathbb{S}_{n-1})\big)
and
\not{K}_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},\pm}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\textup{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{S})\big)
agree and that the residues of
P\not{K}_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},\pm}
agree with the composition of the residues of
\not{K}_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},\pm}
with
P
. Note that

\displaystyle\not{K}_{\lambda,\nu}^{\mathbb{A},\pm}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(x_{i}\cdot K_{\lambda-\frac{1}{2},\nu+\frac{1}{2}}^{\mathbb{A},\mp}(x)\big)\underline{e_{i}},

where
\underline{e_{i}}=\zeta_{n}(e_{i})\in\textup{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{S}_{n})
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume
n
is even and let
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\textup{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{S}_{n})\big)
be a distribution of the form

\displaystyle u(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}(x)\underline{e_{i}}

with
u_{i}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)
. Then
\supp u=\supp Pu
, in particular
Pu=0
if and only if
u=0
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle(Pu)(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}(x)\cdot(P\circ\underline{e_{i}}).

We claim that the operators
P\circ\underline{e_{1}},\ldots,P\circ\underline{e_{n}}
are linearly independent, then the statement follows.

Decompose
\zeta_{n}\otimes\det
into irreducible
\textup{Pin}(n-1)
-representations
\mathbb{S}_{n}=\mathbb{S}_{n}^{1}\oplus\mathbb{S}_{n}^{2}
, where
P\colon\mathbb{S}_{n}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}_{n-1}
is an isomorphism and
\mathbb{S}_{n}^{2}=\ker P
. Now note that for
1\leq i\leq n-1
we have

\displaystyle P\circ\underline{e_{i}}=P\circ\zeta_{n}(e_{i})=-P\circ[\zeta_{n}\otimes\det](e_{i})=-\zeta_{n-1}(e_{i})\circ P

and therefore

\lambda_{1}=\dots=\lambda_{n-1}=0
since

are linearly independent. Then also

Remark 5.5
.

The identity

which is spanned by

Remark 5.6
.

The intertwining differential operators
C^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}\big)\to C^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\mathbb{S}\big)
with integral kernel

6
The compact picture of symmetry breaking operators

between spinors

We use the method developed in
[
13
]
to show that the symmetry breaking operators found in Section
5
between spinor-valued principal series span the space of all symmetry breaking operators. The notation will be as in the previous section.

6.1
Reduction to the pair
\boldsymbol{\big(\widetilde{G}_{1},\widetilde{H}_{1}\big)}

To simplify computations we first reduce the study of symmetry breaking operators for the pair
\big(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{H}\big)=(\textup{Pin}(n+1,1),\textup{Pin}(n,1))
to the pair
\big(\widetilde{G}_{1},\widetilde{H}_{1}\big)=(\textup{Pin}(n+1,1)_{1},\textup{Pin}(n,1)_{1})
, where

\displaystyle\textup{Pin}(p,q)_{1}=\textup{Pin}(p,q)_{++}\cup\textup{Pin}(p,q)_{-+}.

We refer the reader to Appendix
A.1
for the definition of
\textup{Pin}(p,q)_{\pm\pm}
. Note that
\widetilde{P}_{1}=\widetilde{P}\cap\widetilde{G}_{1}=\widetilde{M}_{1}AN
is a parabolic subgroup of
\widetilde{G}_{1}
with
\widetilde{M}_{1}=\textup{Pin}(n)
. Similarly,
\widetilde{P}_{H,1}=\widetilde{P}_{H}\cap\widetilde{H}_{1}=\widetilde{M}_{H,1}AN_{H}
is a parabolic subgroup of
\widetilde{H}_{1}
with
\widetilde{M}_{H,1}=\textup{Pin}(n-1)
. Further,
\widetilde{G}_{1}/\widetilde{P}_{1}\simeq\widetilde{G}/\widetilde{P}
and
\widetilde{H}_{1}/\widetilde{P}_{H,1}\simeq\widetilde{H}/\widetilde{P}_{H}
, and hence

\displaystyle\not{\pi}_{\lambda,\delta}|_{\widetilde{G}_{1}}\simeq\not{\pi}_{\lambda}=\textup{Ind}_{\widetilde{P}_{1}}^{\widetilde{G}_{1}}\big(\zeta_{n}\otimes e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big),

\displaystyle\not{\tau}_{\nu,\varepsilon}|_{\widetilde{H}_{1}}\simeq\not{\tau}_{\nu}=\textup{Ind}_{\widetilde{P}_{H,1}}^{\widetilde{H}_{1}}\big(\zeta_{n-1}\otimes e^{\nu}\otimes\mathbf{1}\big).

Note that the restrictions are independent of
\delta
and
\varepsilon
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For
\lambda,\nu\in\mathbb{C}
and
\delta,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
we have

\displaystyle\dim\textup{Hom}_{\widetilde{H}}(\not{\pi}_{\lambda,\delta},\not{\tau}_{\nu,\varepsilon})+\dim\textup{Hom}_{\widetilde{H}}(\not{\pi}_{\lambda,1-\delta},\not{\tau}_{\nu,\varepsilon})=\dim\textup{Hom}_{\widetilde{H}_{1}}(\not{\pi}_{\lambda},\not{\tau}_{\nu}).

Proof.

The proof of the two identities is analogous to
[
8
, Theorem 2.10]
and uses
\widetilde{G}/\widetilde{G}_{1}\simeq\widetilde{M}/\widetilde{M}_{1}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
\widetilde{H}/\widetilde{H}_{1}\simeq\widetilde{M}_{H}/\widetilde{M}_{H,1}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

In the remaining part of this section we determine
\dim\textup{Hom}_{\widetilde{H}_{1}}(\not{\pi}_{\lambda},\not{\tau}_{\nu})
using the technique developed in
[
13
]
.

6.2
Harish-Chandra modules

Let
\theta
denote the Cartan involution of
G=\textup{O}(n+1,1)
given by conjugation with the matrix
\textup{diag}(1,\ldots,1,-1)
. Then
\theta
lifts to a Cartan involution of
\widetilde{G}
which restricts to Cartan involutions of
\widetilde{G}_{1}
,
\widetilde{H}
and
\widetilde{H}_{1}
. This gives the following maximal compact subgroups:

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}_{1}=\widetilde{G}_{1}^{\theta}=\textup{Pin}(n+1),\qquad\widetilde{K}_{H,1}=\widetilde{H}_{1}^{\theta}=\textup{Pin}(n).

The embedding
\widetilde{K}_{H,1}\subseteq\widetilde{K}_{1}
is given by the embedding
\Cl(n)\subseteq\Cl(n+1)
induced from the standard embedding
\mathbb{R}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\,x^{\prime}\mapsto(x^{\prime},0)
.

The method in
[
13
]
actually constructs and classifies intertwining operators between the underlying Harish-Chandra modules of
\not{\pi}_{\lambda}
and
\not{\tau}_{\nu}
which we denote by
(\not{\pi}_{\lambda})_{\textup{HC}}
and
(\not{\tau}_{\nu})_{\textup{HC}}
. As vector space
(\not{\pi}_{\lambda})_{\textup{HC}}
is the space of all
\widetilde{K}_{1}
-finite vectors in
\not{\pi}_{\lambda}
, or equivalently the sum of all irreducible
\widetilde{K}_{1}
-subrepresentations of
\not{\pi}_{\lambda}
. Hence,
(\not{\pi}_{\lambda})_{\textup{HC}}
clearly carries a
\widetilde{K}_{1}
-action. It is further invariant under the action of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
\widetilde{G}_{1}
and hence carries the structure of a
\big(\mathfrak{g},\widetilde{K}_{1}\big)
-module.

Since
(\not{\pi}_{\lambda})_{\textup{HC}}
is dense in
\not{\pi}_{\lambda}
, we obtain an injective map

\displaystyle\textup{Hom}_{\widetilde{H}_{1}}(\not{\pi}_{\lambda}|_{\widetilde{H}_{1}},\not{\tau}_{\nu})\hookrightarrow\textup{Hom}_{(\mathfrak{h},\widetilde{K}_{H,1})}((\not{\pi}_{\lambda})_{\textup{HC}}|_{(\mathfrak{h},\widetilde{K}_{H,1})},(\not{\tau}_{\nu})_{\textup{HC}}).

Using the method in
[
13
]
we determine in this section the dimension of the space of intertwining operators between the Harish-Chandra modules. This gives an upper bound for the dimension of the space of intertwining operators between the representations
\not{\pi}_{\lambda}
and
\not{\tau}_{\nu}
. From the explicit construction of intertwining operators in Section
5
we also have a lower bound, and it turns out that these bounds agree, so that the injective map in fact is a bijection.

6.3
\boldsymbol{K}
-types

We study the representations
\not{\pi}_{\lambda}
and
\not{\tau}_{\nu}
in the compact picture, i.e. on sections of spin bundles over
\widetilde{K}_{1}/\widetilde{M}_{1}\simeq S^{n}
and
\widetilde{K}_{H,1}/\widetilde{M}_{H,1}\simeq S^{n-1}
. More precisely,

\displaystyle\not{\pi}_{\lambda}|_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}=C^{\infty}\big(\widetilde{K}_{1}\times_{\widetilde{M}_{1}}\zeta_{n}\big).

By Frobenius Reciprocity and using the classical branching rules for spin groups we easily obtain

\displaystyle(\not{\pi}_{\lambda})_{\textup{HC}}|_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}

\displaystyle\simeq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\big(\zeta_{n+1,i}^{+}\oplus\zeta_{n+1,i}^{-}\big),&\mbox{for $n$ even,}\\
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{n+1,i},&\mbox{for $n$ odd,}\end{cases}

where
\zeta_{n+1,i}^{(\pm)}
denote the higher spin representations of
\textup{Pin}(n+1)
(see Appendix
A.3
). By Appendix
A.4

\displaystyle\zeta_{n+1,i}^{\pm}|_{\widetilde{K}_{H,1}}\simeq\bigoplus_{j=0}^{i}\zeta_{n,j}\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{($n$ even)},

\displaystyle\zeta_{n+1,i}|_{\widetilde{K}_{H,1}}\simeq\bigoplus_{j=0}^{i}\big(\zeta_{n,j}^{+}\oplus\zeta_{n,j}^{-}\big)\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{($n$ odd)}.

We use the notation
\alpha=(i,\pm)
and
\alpha^{\prime}=j
for even
n
, and
\alpha=i
,
\alpha^{\prime}=(j,\pm)
for odd
n
, and write
\alpha^{\prime}\subset\alpha
if
j\leq i
. Denote by
\mathcal{E}(\alpha)
the subspace of
C^{\infty}\big(\widetilde{K}_{1}\times_{\widetilde{M}_{1}}\zeta_{n}\big)
which is isomorphic to the
\widetilde{K}_{1}
-representation
\zeta_{n+1,i}^{\pm}
if
\alpha=(i,\pm)
and
\zeta_{n+1,i}
if
\alpha=i
, and similar for the corresponding subspace
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})
of

. In this notation

\displaystyle(\not{\pi}_{\lambda})_{\textup{HC}}|_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(\alpha),\qquad(\not{\tau}_{\nu})_{\textup{HC}}|_{\widetilde{K}_{H,1}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha^{\prime}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime}).

Further,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha)|_{\widetilde{K}_{H,1}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha^{\prime}\subset\alpha}\mathcal{E}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}),

where
\mathcal{E}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})\simeq\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})
. We fix a non-zero
\widetilde{K}_{H,1}
-intertwining operator

\displaystyle R_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\colon\ \mathcal{E}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})

for each pair
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
with

6.4
Scalar identities for symmetry breaking operators

Write
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{s}
for the eigenspace decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
under
\theta
and
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{k}_{H}\oplus\mathfrak{s}_{H}
for the one of
\mathfrak{h}
. We identify
\mathfrak{s}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
via

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{s},\qquad Y\mapsto\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{0}_{n+1}&Y\\
Y^{\top}&0\end{matrix}\right).

Consider the map
\omega\colon\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbb{C}}\to C^{\infty}\big(\widetilde{K}_{1}/\widetilde{M}_{1}\big)=C^{\infty}(S^{n})
given by

\displaystyle\omega(Y)(x)=Y^{\top}x,\qquad x\in S^{n},Y\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}.

Multiplication by
\omega(Y)
defines an operator
M(\omega(Y))\colon C^{\infty}\big(\widetilde{K}_{1}\times_{\widetilde{M}_{1}}\zeta_{n}\big)\to C^{\infty}\big(\widetilde{K}_{1}\times_{\widetilde{M}_{1}}\zeta_{n}\big)
. From the weights of
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbb{C}}
it is easy to see that
M(\omega(Y))
maps
\mathcal{E}(\alpha)
to
\mathcal{E}(\beta)
if and only if

\displaystyle\alpha=(i,\pm)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta\in\{(i+1,\pm),(i,\mp),(i-1,\pm)\}\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{($n$ even)},

\displaystyle\alpha=i\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta\in\{i+1,i,i-1\}\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{($n$ odd)}.

Write
\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta
if this is the case.

We use the same notation for
\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}
and write
M(\omega^{\prime}(Y))
for multiplication by the function
\omega^{\prime}(Y)(x)=Y^{\top}x
,
x\in S^{n-1}
,
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Further write
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})\leftrightarrow(\beta,\beta^{\prime})
if
\alpha^{\prime}\subset\alpha
,
\beta^{\prime}\subset\beta
and
\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta
,
\alpha^{\prime}\leftrightarrow\beta^{\prime}
.

For
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})\leftrightarrow(\beta,\beta^{\prime})
define

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}(Y)=\textup{proj}_{\mathcal{E}(\beta,\beta^{\prime})}\circ M(\omega(Y))|_{\mathcal{E}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})},

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta^{\prime}}(Y)=\textup{proj}_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\beta^{\prime})}\circ M(\omega^{\prime}(Y))|_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})}.

Then both
R_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}\circ\omega_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}
and
\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta^{\prime}}\circ R_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}
are
\widetilde{K}_{H,1}
-intertwining operators
\mathfrak{s}_{H,\mathbb{C}}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\beta^{\prime})
. Since
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\beta^{\prime})
occurs in
\mathfrak{s}_{H,\mathbb{C}}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
with multiplicity at most one, we have

\displaystyle R_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}\circ\omega_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}(Y)=\lambda_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}\cdot\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta^{\prime}}(Y)\circ R_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}

(6.1)

for some constant
\lambda_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Now, every
\widetilde{K}_{H,1}
-intertwining operator
T\colon(\not{\pi}_{\lambda})_{\textup{HC}}\to(\not{\tau}_{\nu})_{\textup{HC}}
is uniquely determined by its restriction to the subspaces
\mathcal{E}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
on which it is given by

\displaystyle T|_{\mathcal{E}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})}=t_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot R_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}

(6.2)

for some scalars
t_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{C}
. In
[
13
]
it is proven that
T
is
(\mathfrak{h},\widetilde{K}_{H,1})
-intertwining if and only if for all
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
and
\alpha^{\prime}\leftrightarrow\beta^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle(2\nu+\sigma_{\beta^{\prime}}^{\prime}-\sigma_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\prime})t_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\\
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})\leftrightarrow(\beta,\beta^{\prime})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}(2\lambda+\sigma_{\beta}-\sigma_{\alpha})t_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}.

(6.3)

Here the constants
\sigma_{\alpha}
and
\sigma_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\prime}
are essentially the eigenvalues of the Casimir element on the
K
-types
\mathcal{E}(\alpha)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})
. From
[
1
, Section 3.a]
it follows that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\beta}-\sigma_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}2i+n+1,&\mbox{for $(\alpha,\beta)=((i,\pm),(i+1,\pm))$ resp.~$(i,i+1)$,}\\
0,&\mbox{for $(\alpha,\beta)=((i,\pm),(i,\mp))$ resp.~$(i,i)$,}\\
-2i-n+1,&\mbox{for $(\alpha,\beta)=((i,\pm),(i-1,\pm))$ resp.~$(i,i-1)$,}\end{cases}

and similarly for
\sigma_{\beta^{\prime}}^{\prime}-\sigma_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\prime}
. Hence, the only missing constants in (
6.3
) are the numbers
\lambda_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}
which we now compute after choosing explicit operators
R_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}
.

6.5
Explicit embeddings of
\boldsymbol{K}
-types

We realize the
K

\zeta_{n+1,i}^{(\pm)}

\not{\pi}_{\lambda}

\widetilde{M}_{1}
-representation

Lemma 6.2
.

Let

For

Appendix A
Clifford algebras, pin groups and their representations

We recall the basic definitions for Clifford algebras, pin groups and their representations. Most of the results are well-known or follow easily from the standard literature.

A.1
Clifford algebras and pin groups

For
p,q\geq 0
we let
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}=\big(\mathbb{R}^{p+q},Q_{p,q}\big)
, where
Q=Q_{p,q}
is the following quadratic form on
\mathbb{R}^{p+q}
:

\displaystyle Q(x)=-x_{1}^{2}-\cdots-x_{p}^{2}+x_{p+1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{p+q}^{2}.

Abusing notation, we also write
Q(x,y)
for the associated symmetric bilinear form on
\mathbb{R}^{p+q}
.

We define the Clifford algebra
\Cl(p,q)
to be the unital
\mathbb{R}
-algebra generated by
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
subject to the relation

\displaystyle xy+yx=2Q(x,y)\mathbf{1}.

For the standard basis vectors
e_{1},\ldots,e_{p+q}\in\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
this implies

\displaystyle e_{i}^{2}=Q(e_{i})\mathbf{1},\qquad e_{i}e_{j}+e_{j}e_{i}=0,\qquad i\neq j.

For
(p,q)=(n,0)
we also write
\Cl(n)=\Cl(n,0)
for short. The Clifford algebra
\Cl(p,q)
has a natural grading into even and odd elements

\displaystyle\Cl(p,q)=\Cl(p,q)_{\textup{even}}\oplus\Cl(p,q)_{\textup{odd}}.

The map
\alpha
of
\Cl(p,q)
which acts by
+1
on
\Cl(p,q)_{\textup{even}}
and by
-1
on
\Cl(p,q)_{\textup{odd}}
is an algebra involution called the
canonical automorphism
.

Denote by
\Cl(p,q;\mathbb{C})=\Cl(p,q)\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
and
\Cl(n;\mathbb{C})=\Cl(n)\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
the complexifications of
\Cl(p,q)
and
\Cl(n)
. Abusing notation, we will also use
Q
for the extension of the symmetric
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear form
Q_{p,q}
on
\mathbb{R}^{p+q}
to a symmetric
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear form on
\mathbb{C}^{p+q}
. Note that
\Cl(p,q;\mathbb{C})\simeq\Cl(p+q;\mathbb{C})
as
\mathbb{C}
-algebras.

We define the groups
\textup{Pin}(p,q)
and
\textup{Spin}(p,q)
by

\displaystyle\textup{Pin}(p,q)=\big\{v_{1}\cdots v_{k}\colon k\geq 0,v_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{p,q},Q(v_{i})=\pm 1\big\}\subseteq\Cl(p,q),

\displaystyle\textup{Spin}(p,q)=\textup{Pin}(p,q)\cap\Cl(p,q)_{\textup{even}}.

Further, put
\textup{Pin}(n)=\textup{Pin}(n,0)
and
\textup{Spin}(n)=\textup{Spin}(n,0)
. Then
\textup{Spin}(n)
is connected and
\textup{Pin}(n)=\textup{Spin}(n)\cup\textup{Pin}(n)_{-}
, where
\textup{Pin}(n)_{-}=\textup{Spin}(n)\cdot e_{1}
. For
p,q>0
the group
\textup{Pin}(p,q)
has four connected components

\displaystyle\textup{Pin}(p,q)_{++},\quad\textup{Pin}(p,q)_{+-},\quad\textup{Pin}(p,q)_{-+}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\textup{Pin}(p,q)_{--},

where the first
+
(resp.
-
) means that the number of
v_{i}
’s with
Q(v_{i})=1
in the product of an element
x=v_{1}\cdots v_{k}\in\textup{Pin}(p,q)
is even (resp. odd), and the second
+
(resp.
-
) the same for the number of
v_{i}
’s with
Q(v_{i})=-1
. Then clearly
\textup{Spin}(p,q)=\textup{Pin}(p,q)_{++}\cup\textup{Pin}(p,q)_{--}
, so
\textup{Spin}(p,q)
has two connected components.

For all
p,q\geq 0
the group
\textup{Pin}(p,q)
is a double cover of the indefinite orthogonal group
\textup{O}(p,q)
, the covering map being

\displaystyle q\colon\ \textup{Pin}(p,q)\to\textup{O}(p,q),\qquad q(x)y=\alpha(x)yx^{-1}.

The restriction of
q
to
\textup{Spin}(p,q)
induces a double covering

\displaystyle q\colon\ \textup{Spin}(p,q)\to\textup{SO}(p,q).

A.2
Clifford modules and fundamental spin representations

We now describe the irreducible representations of
\Cl(n;\mathbb{C})
. For
n=2m
even there is only one irreducible representation, and for
n=2m+1
odd there are two. To construct these we choose the maximal isotropic subspaces

\displaystyle W=\mathbb{C}w_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{C}w_{m}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad W^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}w_{1}^{\prime}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{C}w_{m}^{\prime}

of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, where
w_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\big(e_{2i-1}+\sqrt{-1}e_{2i}\big)
,
w_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\big(e_{2i-1}-\sqrt{-1}e_{2i}\big)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{n}=\begin{cases}W\oplus W^{\prime},&\mbox{for $n$ even},\\
W\oplus W^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{2m+1},&\mbox{for $n$ odd.}\end{cases}

We define an irreducible representation
\zeta_{n}
of
\Cl(n;\mathbb{C})
on
\mathbb{S}_{n}=\bigwedge^{\bullet}W
by

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(w)\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{k}=w\wedge\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{k},

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(w^{\prime})\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{k}=2\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i-1}Q(w^{\prime},\xi_{i})\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{\xi_{i}}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{k},

for
w\in W
,
w^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}
, and in case
n
is odd additionally

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(e_{2m+1})\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{k}=(-1)^{k}\sqrt{-1}\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{k}.

Then for
n
even
(\zeta_{n},\mathbb{S}_{n})
is the unique irreducible representation of
\Cl(n;\mathbb{C})
and
\zeta_{n}\circ\alpha\simeq\zeta_{n}
, where
\alpha
is the canonical automorphism of
\Cl(n;\mathbb{C})
. More precisely, the map

\displaystyle\gamma\colon\ \mathbb{S}_{n}\to\mathbb{S}_{n},\qquad\gamma(\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{k})=(-1)^{k}\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{k}

(A.1)

intertwines
\zeta_{n}\circ\alpha
and
\zeta_{n}
. For
n
odd,
\zeta_{n}^{+}=\zeta_{n}
and
\zeta_{n}^{-}=\zeta_{n}\circ\alpha
are inequivalent and we obtain two irreducible inequivalent representations
(\zeta_{n}^{\pm},\mathbb{S}_{n})
of
\Cl(n;\mathbb{C})
. For
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
we write

\displaystyle\underline{x}=\zeta_{n}(x)

whenever the representation
\zeta_{n}
is clear from the context.

The restriction of any irreducible complex representation of the Clifford algebra
\Cl(n;\mathbb{C})
to
\textup{Pin}(n)\subseteq\Cl(n)\subseteq\Cl(n;\mathbb{C})
defines an irreducible representation of
\textup{Pin}(n)
. These representations are called
fundamental spin representations
and will also be denoted by
(\zeta_{n},\mathbb{S}_{n})
for
n
even and
(\zeta_{n}^{\pm},\mathbb{S}_{n})
for
n
odd. Note that for
n
even
\zeta_{n}\otimes\det\simeq\zeta_{n}
and for
n
odd
\zeta_{n}^{\pm}\otimes\det\simeq\zeta_{n}^{\mp}
, where
\det
is the one-dimensional representation of
\textup{Pin}(n)
given by the determinant character
\det\colon\textup{Pin}(n)\to\textup{O}(n)\to\{\pm 1\}
.

For
n
even the restriction of
\zeta_{n}
to
\textup{Spin}(n)\subseteq\textup{Pin}(n)
decomposes into the direct sum of two irreducible representations of
\textup{Spin}(n)
according to the decomposition

\displaystyle\textstyle\bigwedge^{\bullet}W=\bigwedge^{\textup{even}}W\oplus\bigwedge^{\textup{odd}}W.

They have highest weights
\big(\frac{1}{2},\ldots,\frac{1}{2},\pm\frac{1}{2}\big)
in the standard notation. For
n
odd the restrictions of
\zeta_{n}^{+}
and
\zeta_{n}^{-}
to
\textup{Spin}(n)
define equivalent irreducible representations of
\textup{Spin}(n)
whose highest weight is
\big(\frac{1}{2},\ldots,\frac{1}{2}\big)
.

A.3
Higher spin representations on monogenic polynomials

Let
i\geq 0
. For
n

\textup{Spin}(n)

\textup{Pin}(n)

n

We realize the representations
\zeta_{n,i}^{(\pm)}
as monogenic polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

the group
\textup{Pin}(n)
acts on the space
\Pol_{i}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big)
of
\mathbb{S}_{n}

of degree

\displaystyle(g\cdot\phi)(x)=\zeta_{n}(g)\phi\big(q(g)^{-1}x\big),\qquad g\in\textup{Pin}(n),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We consider the Dirac operator
\dirac

Then the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{i}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big)=\big\{\phi\in\Pol_{i}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big)\colon\dirac\phi=0\big\}

of

and defines an irreducible representation
\zeta_{n,i}
of
\textup{Pin}(n)
. For
n

, the isomorphism being

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{i}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big)\to\mathcal{M}_{i}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big),\qquad\phi\mapsto\gamma\circ\phi.

For
n

\zeta_{n,i}^{+}=\zeta_{n,i}
and
\zeta_{n,i}^{-}=\zeta_{n,i}\otimes\det

\big(\zeta_{n,i}^{\pm},\mathcal{M}_{i}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big)\big)
of
\textup{Pin}(n)

I_{j\to i}\phi
is monogenic, and for this we abbreviate

\displaystyle p=(n+i+j-1)C_{i-j}^{\frac{n-1}{2}+j}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad q=(n+2j-1)C_{i-j-1}^{\frac{n+1}{2}+j}.

Then

\displaystyle I_{j\to i}\phi(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})=|x|^{i-j}\phi(x^{\prime})p\big(\tfrac{x_{n+1}}{|x|}\big)+|x|^{i-j-1}\underline{x^{\prime}e_{n+1}}\phi(x^{\prime})q\big(\tfrac{x_{n+1}}{|x|}\big).

Applying the Dirac operator to
I_{j\to i}\phi
yields, after a short computation:

\displaystyle\dirac(I_{j\to i}\phi)(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\underline{e_{k}}\,\frac{\partial(I_{j\to i}\phi)}{\partial x_{i}}(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})+\underline{e_{n+1}\,}\frac{\partial(I_{j\to i}\phi)}{\partial x_{n+1}}(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=|x|^{i-j-2}\underline{x^{\prime}}\phi(x^{\prime})\big((i-j)p(z)-zp^{\prime}(z)+(i-j-1)zq(z)+\big(1-z^{2}\big)q^{\prime}(z)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+|x|^{i-j-1}\underline{e_{n+1}}\phi(x^{\prime})\big((i-j)zp(z)+\big(1-z^{2}\big)p^{\prime}(z)-(n+i+j-1)q(z)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+(i-j-1)z^{2}q(z)+\big(1-z^{2}\big)zq^{\prime}(z)\big),

where
z=\frac{x_{n+1}}{|x|}
. This vanishes if and only if

\displaystyle(i-j)p-zp^{\prime}=-(i-j-1)zq-(1-z^{2})q^{\prime},

\displaystyle(i-j)zp+\big(1-z^{2}\big)p^{\prime}=(n+i+j-1)q-(k-j-1)z^{2}q-\big(1-z^{2}\big)zq^{\prime}.

Multiplying the first equation with
z
and subtracting it from the second one yields

\displaystyle p^{\prime}=(n+i+j-1)q.

If we now insert this into the first equation we obtain

\displaystyle\big(1-z^{2}\big)p^{\prime\prime}-(n+2j)zp^{\prime}+(i-j)(n+i+j-1)p=0,

which is the Gegenbauer differential equation with solution
p(z)=C_{i-j}^{\frac{n-1}{2}+j}(z)
, the classical Gegenbauer polynomial. Finally, using (see
[
3
, Chapter 10.9, equation (23)]
)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}C_{n}^{\lambda}(z)=2\lambda C_{n-1}^{\lambda+1}(z)

and renormalizing
p
and
q
shows that indeed
\dirac(I_{j\to i}\phi)=0
and the proof is complete.
∎

A.5
Multiplication with coordinates

By the Fischer decomposition we have

\displaystyle\Pol\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big)=\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\underline{x}^{j}\mathcal{M}_{i}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big).

(A.2)

Note that
\underline{x}^{2}=-|x|^{2}
. We now study how the product of a monogenic polynomial with a coordinate function
x_{k}
decomposes according to this decomposition.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{i}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big)
and
1\leq k\leq n
. Then

\displaystyle x_{k}\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{i+1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big)\oplus\underline{x}\mathcal{M}_{i}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big)\oplus|x|^{2}\mathcal{M}_{i-1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big).

More precisely,
x_{k}\phi=\phi_{k}^{+}-\underline{x}\phi_{k}^{0}+|x|^{2}\phi_{k}^{-}
with

\displaystyle\phi_{k}^{+}=x_{k}\phi+\frac{1}{n+2i}\left(\underline{xe_{k}}\phi-|x|^{2}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{i+1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big),

\displaystyle\phi_{k}^{0}=\frac{1}{n+2i}\left(\underline{e_{k}}\phi-\frac{2}{n+2i-2}\underline{x}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{i}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big),

\displaystyle\phi_{k}^{-}=\frac{1}{n+2i-2}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}\in\mathcal{M}_{i-1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big).

Proof.

This follows from the following identities which are easily verified:

\displaystyle\dirac(x_{k}\phi)=\underline{e_{k}}\phi,\qquad

\displaystyle\dirac(\underline{xe_{k}}\phi)=-(n+2i)\underline{e_{k}}\phi+2\underline{x}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}},

\displaystyle\dirac(\underline{e_{k}}\phi)=-2\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}},\qquad

\displaystyle\dirac\left(\underline{x}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}\right)=-(n+2i-2)\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}},

\displaystyle\dirac\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}=0,\qquad

\displaystyle\dirac\left(|x|^{2}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}\right)=2\underline{x}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}.

∎

Now, recall the intertwining map
I_{j\to i}\colon\mathcal{M}_{j}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}_{n}\big)\to\mathcal{M}_{i}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\mathbb{S}_{n+1}\big)
from Lemma
A.3

and
x_{k}\phi(x^{\prime})
for
1\leq k\leq n
.

Lemma A.5
.

For
0\leq j\leq i
and

\displaystyle(I_{j\to i}\phi)_{k}^{+}=\frac{n+2j-1}{n+2i+1}I_{j+1\to i+1}\big(\phi_{k}^{+}\big)+\frac{i-j+1}{n+2i+1}I_{j\to i+1}\big(\underline{e_{n+1}}\phi_{k}^{0}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{(I_{j\to i}\phi)_k^+ =}{}-\frac{(i-j+1)(i-j+2)}{(n+2i+1)(n+2j-3)}I_{j-1\to i+1}\big(\phi_{k}^{-}\big),

\displaystyle\hphantom{(I_{j\to i}\phi)_k^0 =}{}+\frac{(n+2i)(n+2j-1)}{(n+2i-1)(n+2i+1)}I_{j\to i}\big(\phi_{k}^{0}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{(I_{j\to i}\phi)_k^0 =}{}-\frac{2(i-j+1)(n+i+j-1)}{(n+2i-1)(n+2i+1)(n+2j-3)}I_{j-1\to i}\big(\underline{e_{n+1}}\phi_{k}^{-}\big),

\displaystyle(I_{j\to i}\phi)_{k}^{-}=-\frac{n+2j-1}{n+2i-1}I_{j+1\to i-1}\big(\phi_{k}^{+}\big)-\frac{n+i+j-1}{n+2i-1}I_{j\to i-1}\big(\underline{e_{n+1}}\phi_{k}^{0}\big)

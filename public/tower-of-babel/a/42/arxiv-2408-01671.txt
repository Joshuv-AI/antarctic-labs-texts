Phonon thermal Hall effect in Mott insulators via skew-scattering by the scalar spin chirality
Taekoo Oh, Naoto Nagaosa

Abstract
Thermal transport is a crucial probe for studying excitations in insulators. In Mott insulators, the primary candidates for heat carriers are spins and phonons, and which dominates the thermal conductivity is a persistent issue. Typically, phonons dominate the longitudinal thermal conductivity while the thermal Hall effect (THE) is primarily associated with spins, which requires time-reversal symmetry breaking. The coupling between phonons and spins usually depends on spin-orbit interaction and is relatively weak. Here, we propose a new mechanism for this coupling and the associated THE: the skew scattering of phonons via spin fluctuations by the scalar spin chirality. This coupling does not require spin-orbit interaction and is ubiquitous in Mott insulators, leading to a thermal Hall angle on the order of 10 − 3 10^{-3} to 10 − 2 10^{-2} . Based on this mechanism, we investigate the THE in YMnO 3 with a trimerized triangular lattice where the THE beyond spins was recognized, and predict the THE in the Kagome and square lattices.

In Mott insulators, spins play crucial roles in low-energy phenomena, including magnetic, optical, and thermodynamic properties.
Regarding transport properties, the charge current is blocked by the Mott gap while the spin current can be carried by spin excitations.
The spin system also carries heat current, which can be detected by thermal conductivity and thermal Hall conductivity measurements.
Additionally, phonons are also active in Mott insulators, contributing to the heat current simultaneously.

The Born-Oppenheimer Approximation

The complete Hamiltonian for the solids are given by

\displaystyle H=H_{nu}+H_{el}+V

(1)

where
H_{nu}=-\sum_{a}\nabla_{R_{a}}^{2}/2M_{a}
is the kinetic Hamiltonian for nuclei,

Here,

is the position vectors for electrons,
\{\vec{R}_{a}\}
is the position vectors for nuclei,
M_{a}
is the mass of
a
th nucleus,
m_{e}
is the electron mass,
Z_{a}
is the charge of nucleus, and the unit charge
e
and
\hbar
are unit.
The conventional Born-Oppenheimer approximation
[
47
]
estimates the eigenfunction of the Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle\approx|\psi_{el}(\{\vec{r}_{i}\},\{\vec{R}_{a}\})\rangle|\psi_{nu}(\{\vec{R}_{a}\})\rangle,

(2)

where

(H_{el}+V)|\psi_{el}\rangle=E_{el}(\{\vec{R}_{a}\})|\psi_{el}\rangle,

and

(H_{nu}+E_{el}(\{\vec{R}_{a}\}))|\psi_{nu}\rangle=E_{nu}|\psi_{nu}\rangle.

However, this is not a complete theory. When the magnetic field is applied, the nuclei Hamiltonian
H_{BO}^{0}=H_{nu}+E_{el}
changes to

\sum_{a}\frac{(\vec{P}_{a}-Z_{a}\vec{A}_{a})^{2}}{2M_{a}}+E_{el}(\{\vec{R}_{a}\}).

Here,
\vec{A}_{a}
is the vector potential applied to
a
-th nucleus due to the external magnetic field.
This does not reflect the charge neutrality of phonons.
To consider the charge neutrality, we should include the correction term.
Since the length scales
l_{el}
and
l_{nu}
for
|\psi_{el}\rangle
and
|\psi_{nu}\rangle
is estimated as
l_{el}/l_{nu}=(M_{a}/m_{e})^{1/4}
, the derivative
\gradient_{R_{a}}
of
|\psi_{el}\rangle
cannot be neglected.
Accordingly, the Born-Oppenheimer approximation becomes the following
[
48
,
49
,
16
,
18
,
19
]
:

\displaystyle H_{BO}=\sum_{a}\frac{(\vec{P}_{a}-Z_{a}\vec{A}_{a}-\vec{a}_{a})^{2}}{2M_{a}}+E_{el}(\{\vec{R}_{a}\}).

(3)

Here,
\vec{a}_{a}=-i\langle\psi_{el}|\nabla_{R_{a}}|\psi_{el}\rangle
is the Berry connection coming from the electronic many-body wavefunction
|\psi_{el}\rangle

In a single hydrogen-like atom, the Berry connection exactly cancels out the external vector potential, explaining the screening of magnetic field by electrons
[
49
]
. In the lattice, although the field is screened, the cancellation of Berry connection is not perfect, resulting in the finite Raman interaction, phonon Berry curvature, and associated intrinsic THE
[
19

In the following sections, we show that the SSC also causes the emergent Raman interaction from the electronic many-body wavefunction.

\displaystyle a_{2x}=\beta\chi U_{2y},~~a_{2y}=-\beta\chi U_{2x}.

(8)

If we transform
a_{2x}
and
a_{2y}
into the atomic basis,

\displaystyle\vec{a}_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\vec{B}_{\text{eff}}\times(2\vec{u}_{1}-\vec{u}_{2}-\vec{u}_{3}),

\displaystyle\vec{a}_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\vec{B}_{\text{eff}}\times(2\vec{u}_{2}-\vec{u}_{3}-\vec{u}_{1}),

\displaystyle\vec{a}_{3}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\vec{B}_{\text{eff}}\times(2\vec{u}_{3}-\vec{u}_{1}-\vec{u}_{2}),

(9)

up to the gauge transform.
Here,
\vec{B}_{\text{eff}}=\beta\chi\hat{z}/3\sim B_{e}\hat{z}
. Thus, now we have the Hamiltonian for the nuclei motion in 2D of a single triangle with harmonic approximation.

\displaystyle H_{nu}=\sum_{a}\frac{(\vec{P}_{a}-\vec{a}_{a})^{2}}{2M}+\sum_{ab,mn}u_{a}^{m}D_{ab}^{mn}u_{b}^{n},

(10)

with
a,b=1,2,3
and
m,n=x,y
.

To consider the phonon, we expand our attention from a single triangle to the lattice system. Accordingly, the emergent Raman interaction at the
i
-th unit cell is:

\displaystyle H_{R}(r)=

\displaystyle-\sum_{a}\frac{\vec{P}_{a}\cdot\vec{a}_{a}}{M}\delta(r-\vec{R}_{i,a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{B_{e}}{2M}\sum_{ab}\Lambda_{ab}L_{ba}^{z}\delta(\vec{r}-\vec{R}_{i,a}).

(11)

where
\Lambda_{ab}=3\delta_{ab}-1
,
\vec{L}_{ba}=\vec{u}_{b}\times\vec{P}_{a}
is the generalized phonon angular momentum,
\vec{R}_{i,a}=\vec{R}_{i}+\vec{d}_{a}
,
\vec{R}_{i}
is the unit cell position, and
\vec{d}_{a}
is the sublattice position in the unit cell.

Closing this section, we note several points.
First, the conventional phonon angular momentum is defined as
\vec{L}=\sum_{a}\vec{L}_{aa}

[
65
]
, so we call
\vec{L}_{ba}
the generalized phonon angular momentum.
Second, the total field applied to
a
-th sublattice is
\sum_{b}\Lambda_{ab}B_{e}\hat{z}=0
, which means the absence of external magnetic field
[
19
]
.
However, although the field is canceled, the Berry connection remains, similar to the Aharonov-Bohm Effect. This is responsible for the phonon skew-scattering in Sec.

5.2
Boltzmann theory

In this section, we are going to obtain the skew-scattering by means of the Boltzmann theory.
We explain how to compute the skew-scattering contribution of THE by the Boltzmann theory
[
67
,
68
,
69
,
70
,
43
,
71
,
45
]
.

Let us consider the wave packet made by the eigenfunctions of band
n
, whose position center is at
\vec{r}
and canonical momentum center is
\vec{k}
.
The Boltzmann equation is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{l}}{\partial t}+\dot{\vec{r}}\cdot\gradient_{r}f_{l}+\dot{\vec{p}}\cdot\gradient_{p}f_{l}=(\frac{df_{l}}{dt})_{coll}.

(15)

f_{l}
is the nonequilibrium distribution of quasiparticle, and
l=(n,\vec{k})
is the index for the band and momentum center of the wave packet.
In general, the collision integral is given by

(\frac{df_{l}}{dt})_{coll}=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}[\omega_{l^{\prime}l}f_{l}(1-f_{l^{\prime}})-w_{ll^{\prime}}f_{l^{\prime}}(1-f_{l})].

Here
\omega_{ll^{\prime}}
means the scattering rate from
l^{\prime}
to
l
. In the skew-scattering, we only consider the elastic scattering. This reduces the collision integral to

(\frac{df_{l}}{dt})_{coll}=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}\{\omega_{l^{\prime}l}f_{l}-\omega_{ll^{\prime}}f_{l^{\prime}}\}.

Thus, the Boltzmann equation for phonons only with temperature gradient is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\epsilon_{l}}{\partial\vec{k}}\cdot\nabla_{r}T\frac{\partial f_{l}}{\partial T}=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}\{\omega_{l^{\prime}l}f_{l}-\omega_{ll^{\prime}}f_{l^{\prime}}\}.

(16)

Here,
\epsilon_{l}
is the energy dispersion of the phonon.

The scattering rate is decomposed into symmetric and antisymmetric parts, i.e.,
\omega_{ll^{\prime}}=\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{s}+\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}
, where
\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{s}=(\omega_{ll^{\prime}}+\omega_{l^{\prime}l})/2,\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}=(\omega_{ll^{\prime}}-\omega_{l^{\prime}l})/2
.
Then,

\frac{\partial\epsilon_{l}}{\partial\vec{k}}\cdot\nabla_{r}T\frac{\partial f_{l}}{\partial T}=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}[\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{s}\{f_{l}-f_{l^{\prime}}\}-\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}\{f_{l}+f_{l}^{\prime}\}].

We here adopt the relaxation time approximation for the symmetric parts, i.e.
\sum_{l^{\prime}}\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{s}\{f_{l}-f_{l}^{\prime}\}=(f_{l}-f_{l}^{0})/\tau=g_{l}/\tau
. Here
f_{l}^{0}
is the equilibrium distribution of the quasiparticle at
l
and
g_{l}
is the deviation. Also,
\sum_{l^{\prime}}\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}f_{l}
is absorbed into the relaxation time approximation. Then, the equation becomes:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\epsilon_{l}}{\partial\vec{k}}\cdot\nabla_{r}T\frac{\partial f_{l}^{0}}{\partial T}=-\frac{g_{l}}{\tau}-\sum_{l^{\prime}}\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}g_{l}^{\prime}.

(17)

Here,
\frac{1}{\tau}\gg|\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}|
since
\frac{1}{\tau}
comes from the leading order and
\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}
comes from the subleading order of impurity strength. We discuss how to acquire
\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}
in the next section.

Then, we divide
g_{l}=h_{l}+\delta h_{l}
. Also, the temperature gradient is applied along
x
direction:
\gradient_{r}T=\Delta T\hat{x}
. Then,

\displaystyle h_{l}=

\displaystyle-\tau\frac{\partial\epsilon_{l}}{\partial k_{x}}\Delta T\frac{\partial f_{l}^{0}}{\partial T},

\displaystyle\delta h_{l}=

\displaystyle-\tau\sum_{l^{\prime}}\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}h_{l^{\prime}}=\tau^{2}\sum_{l^{\prime}}\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}\frac{\partial\epsilon_{l^{\prime}}}{\partial k_{x}}\Delta T\frac{\partial f_{l^{\prime}}^{0}}{\partial T}.

(18)

The thermal conductivities are

\displaystyle\kappa_{xx}=-\sum_{l}\epsilon_{l}\frac{\partial\epsilon_{l}}{\partial k_{x}}\frac{h_{l}}{\Delta T},~\kappa_{yx}=-\sum_{l}\epsilon_{l}\frac{\partial\epsilon_{l}}{\partial k_{y}}\frac{\delta h_{l}}{\Delta T}.

(19)

The trimerized triangular and Kagome lattices are two-dimensional, so we consider the two acoustic bands only.
Also, we ignore the Umklapp process since we consider
T\ll\Theta_{D}
. Here,
\Theta_{D}
is the Debye temperature.
For YMnO
3
, the THE appears near
T_{N}\approx 72~K
while
\Theta_{D}\approx 425~K

[
32
,
72
]
.
We set
\tau\sim 10^{-12}~s
in the calculation.

Now, we explain how to acquire the scattering rate. We denote the original Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}
(the phonon) and the perturbative Hamiltonian
\hat{V}=H_{R}
in Eq.
11
. The Fermi golden rule approximates the scattering rate as

\displaystyle\omega_{l^{\prime}l}=2\pi\langle|T_{l^{\prime}l}|^{2}\rangle_{d}\delta(\epsilon_{l^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{l}),

(20)

where
T_{l^{\prime}l}=\langle l^{\prime}|\hat{V}|\psi_{l}\rangle
is the T-matrix, and
\langle\rangle_{d}
is the averaged value for random spin configurations.
Here,
|l^{\prime}\rangle
is the incident wavefunction which is an eigenfunction of
\hat{H}_{0}
while
|\psi_{l}\rangle
is the scattered wavefunction.

\psi_{l}
satisfies Lippmann-Schwinger equation.

|\psi_{l}\rangle=|l\rangle+\hat{G}_{0}^{l}\hat{V}|\psi_{l}\rangle.

where
\hat{G}_{0}^{l}=(\epsilon_{l}-\hat{H}_{0}+i\eta)^{-1}
is the Green function. The Born approximation gives

|\psi_{l}\rangle\approx|l\rangle+\hat{G}_{0}^{l}\hat{V}|l\rangle.

Thus, the T-matrix elements are

T_{l^{\prime}l}\approx\langle l^{\prime}|\hat{V}|l\rangle+\langle l^{\prime}|\hat{V}\hat{G}_{0}^{l}\hat{V}|l\rangle.

We denote
V_{l^{\prime}l}=\langle l^{\prime}|\hat{V}|l\rangle
. Then,

T_{l^{\prime}l}=V_{l^{\prime}l}+\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}}\frac{V_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l}}{\epsilon_{l}-\epsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta}.

Its absolute square is approximated as

\displaystyle|T_{l^{\prime}l}|^{2}\approx|V_{l^{\prime}l}|^{2}+\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}}\frac{V_{ll^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l}}{\epsilon_{l}-\epsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta}+c.c.+O(\hat{V}^{4}).

(21)

The antisymmetric part is given by

\displaystyle\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}=

\displaystyle 2\pi(\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}}[(\frac{\langle V_{l^{\prime}l}V_{ll^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l^{\prime}}\rangle_{d}}{\epsilon_{l^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta}+c.c.)

\displaystyle-(\frac{\langle V_{ll^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l}\rangle_{d}}{\epsilon_{l}-\epsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta}+c.c.)]\delta(\epsilon_{l}-\epsilon_{l^{\prime}}).

(22)

The leading order is symmetric while the subleading order has both symmetric and antisymmetric parts.
That is,
1/\tau\gg|\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}|
, since the leading order gives
1/\tau
and the subleading order gives
\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}
in Eq.
17
. In our numerical computation, we find that the average of
|V_{ll^{\prime}}|^{2}
is 2-order larger than that of
|\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}|
for
B_{e}=5~meV

\theta_{H}
can be around
10^{-2}
.

In numerical computation, we replace the delta function with

\delta(\epsilon_{l}-\epsilon_{l^{\prime}})\approx-\frac{1}{\pi}\imaginary(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{l}-\epsilon_{l^{\prime}}+i\eta}).

Thus, we get

\displaystyle\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}=

\displaystyle-2\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}}[(\frac{\langle V_{l^{\prime}l}V_{ll^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l^{\prime}}\rangle_{d}}{\epsilon_{l^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta}+c.c.)

\displaystyle-(\frac{\langle V_{ll^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l}\rangle_{d}}{\epsilon_{l}-\epsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta}+c.c.)]\imaginary(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{l}-\epsilon_{l^{\prime}}+i\eta}).

(23)

We let
\eta=0.01

B_{e}\approx 5~meV
, which is the emergent field strength at the polar angle of spins

with

The matrix elements

can be acquired as follows.
If the triangle with the SSC is in the unit cell at

\displaystyle V_{ll^{\prime}}=\int d^{2}r\langle\nu_{l}(r)|\hat{V}(r)|\nu_{l^{\prime}}(r)\rangle.

(24)

Here, for
l=(n,k)
,
|\nu_{l}(r)\rangle=e^{ik\cdot r}|\chi_{l}(r)\rangle
is the Bloch wavefunction of phonons,
|\chi_{l}\rangle=[u_{l},-i\epsilon_{l}u_{l}]^{T}/N_{l}
is the eigenvector of the phonon energy band, and

is the polarization vector such that
D(k)u_{l}=\epsilon_{l}^{2}u_{l}
with the dynamical matrix

The rest of algebra is straightforward:

\displaystyle V_{ll^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{B_{e}}{2M}\sum_{ab}\Lambda_{ab}(L_{ba}^{z})_{ll^{\prime}}e^{i(\vec{k^{\prime}}-\vec{k})\cdot\vec{d}_{a}}.

Here, we call
(L_{ba}^{z})_{ll^{\prime}}=\langle\chi_{l}|L_{ba}^{z}|\chi_{l^{\prime}}\rangle
the generalized angular momentum. For

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{T^{2}}\sum_{n}\int\frac{kdkd\theta}{(2\pi)^{2}}v_{n}^{2}k^{2}(v_{n}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta)\frac{\exp(v_nk/T)}{(\exp(v_nk/T)-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tau T^{2}}{4\pi}\sum_{n}\int dX_{n}~X_{n}^{3}\frac{\exp(X_n)}{(\exp(X_n)-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tau T^{2}}{4\pi}\sum_{n}6\zeta(3)=\frac{3\tau T^{2}}{\pi}\zeta(3).

(26)

Here,
X_{n}=v_{n}k/T
. Thus,
\kappa_{xx}\propto T^{2}
.
Also, the thermal Hall conductivity is

\displaystyle\kappa_{yx}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\tau^{2}}{T^{2}}\sum_{nn^{\prime}}\int\frac{dkd\theta}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{dk^{\prime}d\theta^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}v_{n}^{2}k^{2}\sin\theta\omega_{nkn^{\prime}k^{\prime}}^{a}\cos\theta^{\prime}

\displaystyle\times\frac{v_{n^{\prime}}^{2}k^{\prime 2}\exp(v_{n'}k'/T)}{(\exp(v_{n'}k'/T)-1)^{2}}.

When we numerically observe that the maximum value of
|\omega_{nk,n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}^{a}|
, it is nearly independent of
k
only near the
\Gamma
point.
So, we can assume
\omega_{nk,n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}^{a}\approx N_{nn^{\prime}}B_{e}^{3}\sin(\ta-\ta')\delta(v_{n}k-v_{n^{\prime}}k^{\prime})
with the coefficient
N_{nn^{\prime}}
, where the sine function comes from the antisymmetric condition. Then,

\displaystyle\kappa_{yx}\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\tau^{2}}{T^{2}}B_{e}^{3}\sum_{nn^{\prime}}\frac{N_{nn^{\prime}}}{v_{n^{\prime}}}\int\frac{dkd\theta}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{dk^{\prime}d\theta^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}v_{n}^{4}k^{4}\sin\theta

\displaystyle\times\sin(\ta-\ta')\delta(k^{\prime}-\frac{v_{n}k}{v_{n}^{\prime}})\cos\theta^{\prime}\frac{\exp(v_{n}k/T)}{(\exp(v_{n}k/T)-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tau^{2}T^{3}B_{e}^{3}\frac{\pi^{2}}{60}\sum_{nn^{\prime}}\frac{N_{nn^{\prime}}}{v_{n}^{\prime}v_{n}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle|\kappa_{yx}|\propto\tau^{2}T^{3}.

(27)

Accordingly, the Hall angle should be

\displaystyle\theta_{H}=

\displaystyle\frac{|\kappa_{yx}|}{\kappa_{xx}}\propto\tau T.

(28)

At the higher temperature, the acoustic phonons at nonlinear dispersion participate in the transport,
\kappa_{xx},\kappa_{yx}
, and
\theta_{H}
are deviated from the temperature dependence.

Also, in the Kagome lattice, we numerically observe that the sign change in
\omega_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}
occurs during the increment of energy while in the trimerized triangular lattice, this is not the case.
This causes the sign change of
\kappa_{yx}
only in the Kagome lattice.
We believe that the sign change of
\kappa_{yx}
is not universal and depends on the system’s microscopic detail.

Physically, the skew-scattering arises as follows. [See Fig.
1
.]
With the time-reversal and inversion symmetry, the left and right circularly polarized chiral phonons are degenerate in the
k
-space.
The atoms rotating in the clockwise (counterclockwise) direction form the left (right) circularly polarized chiral phonons.
In general, left and right circularly polarized chiral phonons have opposite generalized angular momenta
L_{ba}
.
When the emergent field from the local SSC is there, the time-reversal symmetry is broken, and this induces the THE due to phonon skew-scattering.
This is analogous to the skew-scattering induced anomalous Hall Effect by magnetic impurities
[
43
]
.

So far, we have discussed that the THE can be induced by the phonon skew-scattering by the SSC.
The electronic many-body wavefunction is deformed by the SSC, giving rise to the emergent Raman interaction analogous to the Aharanov-Bohm Effect.
The emergent Raman interaction skew-scatter the chiral phonons with the opposite angular momenta, inducing the THE that can be measured in experiments.
By revealing the brand-new phonon skew-scattering mechanism, we believe that our work expands the window of noncoplanar spin structures and fluctuations with their correlations with the phonons.

Hasse principle violation for algebraic families of del Pezzo surfaces of degree 4 4 and hyperelliptic curves of genus congruent to 1 1 modulo 4 4 Thanks: MSC 2020 : Primary 14G12, 14G05; Secondary 11G35, 14H25, 14G25
Kai Huang, Yongqi Liang

Abstract
. Let g g be a positive integer congruent to 1 1 modulo 4 4 and K K be an arbitrary number field. We construct infinitely many explicit one-parameter algebraic families of degree 4 4 del Pezzo surfaces and genus g g hyperelliptic curves such that each K K -member of the families violates the Hasse principle. In particular, we obtain algebraic families of non-trivial 2 2 -torsion elements in the Tate–Shafarevich group of algebraic families of elliptic curves over K K . These Hasse principle violations are explained by the Brauer–Manin obstruction.



1.
Introduction

We consider the Hasse principle for existence of rational points on algebraic varieties defined over number fields. Among various classes of algebraic varieties, it is conjectured that violation of the Hasse principle is explained by the Brauer–Manin obstruction for

•

del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4
by Colliot-Thélène–Sansuc
[
6
]
,

•

smooth projective curves by Scharaschkin
[
25
]
and Skorobogatov
[
27
, §6.2]
.

Examples in these two classes of varieties which violate the Hasse principle have been constructed by so many authors that we are not able to exhaust. We would like to mention Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer
[
2
, Theorem 3]
for del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4
and Lind
[
20
]
and Reichardt
[
24
]
for the first examples of curves.

Though lots of single examples are known, but extending them to algebraic families seems very difficult, even using the powerful tool of the Brauer–Manin obstruction, which has been widely considered during the last fifty years. Compared to the related question of weak approximation properties, it is observed that a nontrivial Brauer group usually does not obstruct the Hasse principle. Roughly speaking, examples of violation of the Hasse principle within families are rare. The result of Bright
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
gave a possible explanation of this phenomenon for algebraic families whose parameter spaces are projective spaces. To be precise, by an
algebraic family
over a number field
K
, we mean a morphism of
K
-varieties
\textbf{V}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
, whose general fibers belong to a certain class of varieties, and we will consider arithmetic properties simultaneously for all (but finitely many) fibers over
K
-rational points.

In the present paper, we mainly discuss algebraic families of varieties violating the Hasse principle, and we focus on the two classes of varieties mentioned above. It is a challenge to prove the existence or produce explicit such algebraic families, especially in the class of geometrically rational varieties, which are most likely to satisfy the assumptions of Bright’s result. To the knowledge of the authors, in the literature no algebraic families of degree
4
del Pezzo surfaces are known to violate the Hasse principle. However, in the other direction, Jahnel and Schindler
[
18
]
showed that the degree
4
del Pezzo surfaces that violate the Hasse principle are Zariski dense in the moduli scheme. This indicates that the task of producing an algebraic family of degree
4
del Pezzo surfaces violating the Hasse principle still appears feasible.

For curves, the situation is better (at least over
\mathbb{Q}
) but still far from satisfactory. In
[
8
]
, Colliot-Thélène and Poonen proved the existence of nonisotrivial algebraic families of genus
1
curves over
\mathbb{Q}
violating the Hasse principle. Soon after that, in
[
23
]
Poonen produced an explicit family. For an integer
g>5
not divisible by
4
, in
[
12
]
Dong Quan constructed explicit algebraic families of genus
g
curves over
\mathbb{Q}
violating the Hasse principle. All these families are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

It is worth adding that, though not directly related to results in this paper, Dong Quan constructed algebraic families of K3 surfaces over
\mathbb{Q}
violating the Hasse principle in
[
10
]
.

Now we state our main results.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
K
be a number field and
g
be a positive integer such that
g\equiv 1~\mathrm{mod}~4
.
Then there exist infinitely many explicit algebraic families
\textbf{S}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
4
del Pezzo surfaces and
\textbf{X}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
of genus
g
hyperelliptic curves, such that for all rational points
\theta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
the fibers
\textbf{S}_{\theta}
and
\textbf{X}_{\theta}
violate the Hasse principle.

In order to compare, we briefly recall Bright’s result
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
. It roughly states that, under certain cohomological assumptions, the specializations of classes in the algebraic Brauer group of the generic fiber do not obstruct the Hasse principle on 100% of the fibers.
While our result goes to a totally opposite conclusion: for the families
\textbf{S}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
of del Pezzo surfaces, violations of the Hasse principle on fibers
\textbf{S}_{\theta}
over any rational points
\theta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
are all explained by an obstruction coming from quaternion classes
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}\in\textup{Br}(\textbf{S}_{\theta})
defined in the formula (
5.8
).
These classes are the specialization of a single uniform class of the same form
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}\in\textup{Br}(k(\eta)(\textbf{S}_{\eta}))
(with
\theta
viewed as an indeterminate) which actually belongs to the algebraic Brauer group
\textup{Br}_{1}(\textbf{S}_{\eta})
of the generic fiber.

Our method differs from those for curves mentioned above by Colliot-Thélène, Poonen, and Dong Quan. Our argument is a rather straightforward application of the Brauer–Manin obstruction. We check fiber by fiber the local solvability and the nonexistence of global rational points by computing the relevant Brauer–Manin set.
One of the advantages of our argument is that it works explicitly in general over arbitrary number fields. It is hard to imagine that such a direct method works, since there is great difficulty in the choice of
arithmetic
parameters and the choice of the form of the defining equations in the construction. We need to make sure that the parameters give correct values for the Brauer–Manin pairing.

Existence of arithmetic parameters

In this section, we prove the following key proposition to obtain suitable arithmetic parameters

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

Then there exist algebraic integers

not corresponding to a place

;

(iii)

Remark 2.2
.

When
\pi
is an odd non-archimedean place, for an invertible element
\alpha
of
\mathcal{O}_{K_{\pi}}
, Hensel’s lemma ensures that
\alpha
is a square in
K_{\pi}^{*}
if and only if the reduction is a square in
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{\pi}
.
If
\beta
is another invertible element of
\mathcal{O}_{K_{\pi}}
, then it follows that
\alpha,\beta\notin K_{\pi}^{*2}
implies that
\alpha\beta\in K_{\pi}^{*2}
since it is the case in
\mathbb{F}_{\pi}^{*}
.

These apply to the
a,b,c,d
(and their products) obtained in the proposition and we frequently make use of it in the forthcoming part of this paper without further mention.

The existence of such arithmetic parameters is essentially a consequence of Chebotarev’s density theorem. For a precise proof, we recall the setup of global class field theory, please refer to
[
22
, Chapter VI §7]
for more details.

For a modulus
\mathfrak{m}
of a number field
K
, let
K_{\mathfrak{m}}
be the corresponding ray class field. The Artin reciprocity law says that the Artin map

\begin{array}[]{l@{}c@{}l}\gamma:&I_{\mathfrak{m}}&\rightarrow\textup{Gal}(K_{\mathfrak{m}}/K)\\
&\mathfrak{p}&\mapsto\textup{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}\end{array}

fits into a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow P_{\mathfrak{m}}\rightarrow I_{\mathfrak{m}}\xrightarrow{\gamma}\textup{Gal}(K_{\mathfrak{m}}/K)\rightarrow 0,

where
I_{\mathfrak{m}}
is the group of fractional ideals that are coprime to the modulus
\mathfrak{m}
and
P_{\mathfrak{m}}
is its subgroup of principal fractional ideals
(\lambda)\in I_{\mathfrak{m}}
such that
\textup{val}_{\pi}(\lambda-1)\geq\textup{val}_{\pi}(\mathfrak{m})
for all non-archimedean places
\pi\mid\mathfrak{m}
and
\lambda_{\pi}>0
for all real places
\pi\mid\mathfrak{m_{\infty}}
.

Lemma 4.1

Lemma 4.2
.

Consider the
F

5.1.

Every

Theorem 5.1

Let

be an odd integer. Let

be the finite set of places given by

(5.1)

\Omega^{0}=\{\pi\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\infty};\quad\pi\nmid 2\mbox{ and }\textup{char}(\mathbb{F}_{\pi})\leq 4g^{2}\}.

Let arithmetic parameters

be chosen as in Proposition

.
Consider the algebraic families

of genus

{}^{h,g}\negthinspace\textbf{Y}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
of genus
1

{}^{h,g}\negthinspace\textbf{S}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
4

.

(1)

Assume that

, then the maps
{}^{h,g}\negthinspace\textbf{X}(L)\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(L)
,
{}^{h,g}\negthinspace\textbf{Y}(L)\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(L)
, and

are surjective for all local fields

.

In particular, for every closed point
\theta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
the fibers

,
{}^{h,g}\negthinspace\textbf{Y}_{\theta}

(2)

For any

, the varieties
{}^{h,g}\negthinspace\textbf{X}_{\theta}
,

In particular, for any

, the varieties
{}^{h,g}\negthinspace\textbf{X}_{\theta}

Summary of proof of Theorem
5.1
.

The proof is not difficult but rather lengthy. For (1), we mainly apply Hensel’s lemma to lift a smooth rational point over a finite field of the reduction of a certain equation. For (2), we take an element of the Brauer group and verify that it gives an obstruction to the existence of a global rational point. Since we have a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-morphism

Remark 5.2

Though we will not present the details, with some more effort we are able to say something on smooth fibers over closed points

Remark 5.3
.

The theorem can be stated a bit more generally in the following sense.

•

The surjectivity of

and

The two statements still hold for

Remark 5.4
.

The condition that

for odd integer

implies that

. Conversely, if

we can take

for any integer

.

When

, this condition is superfluous. We have the following immediate consequence.

Corollary 5.5

For each integer

, there exists an explicit algebraic family of elliptic curves

contains a nonzero element given by the class of the algebraic family of torsors

•

is a nonconstant function on

be the Zariski open subset defined by

by Lemma

to be a smooth compactification of the composition of the family of Jacobian varieties

5.2.
Local solvability

The aim of this subsection is to prove Theorem
5.1
(1). We first establish several preparatory results.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
g\geq 0
be an integer and
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field of characteristic
p>4g^{2}
.
Let
\mathfrak{X}\subset\mathbb{A}^{2}
be an affine curve defined over
\mathbb{F}
by the equation in coordinates
(S,T)

\mathfrak{a}S^{2}=\mathfrak{b}(1-\mathfrak{r}T^{g+1})

with
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{r}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
. Then
\mathfrak{X}
possesses at least one smooth
\mathbb{F}
-point.

Proof.

We may suppose that
g\geq 1
, otherwise the statement is trivially true.
We observe that
p
is odd and
p\nmid g+1
by assumption.
The Jacobian matrix of the curve
\mathfrak{X}
is

J=\begin{pmatrix}2\mathfrak{a}S&(g+1)\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{r}T^{g}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
p\nmid g+1
, we need to find a solution of the equation with either
S\neq 0
or
T\neq 0
.
Therefore, we are done when
1-\mathfrak{r}T^{g+1}=0
has a solution, which is automatically nonzero. From now on, we assume in addition that
1-\mathfrak{r}\mathfrak{e}^{g+1}\neq 0
for all
\mathfrak{e}\in\mathbb{F}
.

If
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{F}^{*2}
, it suffices to take
(S,T)=(\sqrt{\frac{\mathfrak{b}}{\mathfrak{a}}},0)
.

If
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\notin\mathbb{F}^{*2}
, we claim that there exists an
\mathfrak{e}\in\mathbb{F}
such that
1-\mathfrak{r}\mathfrak{e}^{g+1}
is not a square in
\mathbb{F}
. Then we find immediately that
\frac{\mathfrak{b}}{\mathfrak{a}}(1-\mathfrak{r}\mathfrak{e}^{g+1})
is a nonzero square in
\mathbb{F}
, and thus
(S,T)=(\sqrt{\frac{\mathfrak{b}}{\mathfrak{a}}(1-\mathfrak{r}\mathfrak{e}^{g+1})},\mathfrak{e})
is a smooth
\mathbb{F}
-point. To prove the claim, we consider an auxiliary affine curve
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime o}
defined by

S^{2}=1-\mathfrak{r}T^{g+1}.

It can be compactified to a smooth (as
p\nmid g+1
) projective hyperelliptic curve
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
, i.e. the quadratic twist
\mathfrak{X}^{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}}
of
\mathfrak{X}
. The curve
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
has genus
\lceil\frac{g-1}{2}\rceil
, where
\lceil x\rceil
is the smallest integer no less than a real number
x
. The morphism
\lambda:\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
given by the projection to the coordinate
T
is a double cover. The additional assumption that
1-\mathfrak{r}\mathfrak{e}^{g+1}
is never
0
for
\mathfrak{e}\in\mathbb{F}
implies that
|\lambda(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}))|=|\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}(\mathbb{F})|/2
. Then the Hasse–Weil bound
[
28
, Corollaire 3]
together with
|\mathbb{F}|\geq p>4g^{2}
implies that

|\lambda(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}))|\leq(1+|\mathbb{F}|+2\lceil\frac{g-1}{2}\rceil\sqrt{|\mathbb{F}|})/2\leq|\mathbb{F}|-1=|\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F})|-2.

In other words
\lambda:\mathfrak{X}^{\prime o}(\mathbb{F})\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}(\mathbb{F})
cannot be surjective, which proves the claim.
∎

Recall that

5.3.

The aim of this subsection is to prove Theorem

(2). We first establish several preparatory results.
Recall that
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
is a surface defined over

by

(5.5)

\left\{\begin{split}x^{2}-az^{2}&=-b(u-Av)(u-Bv)\\
x^{2}-ay^{2}&=-aC^{2}uv\end{split}\right.

where

, or equivalently

\left\{\begin{split}x^{2}-az^{2}&=-b\varphi\psi\\
x^{2}-ay^{2}&=-aC^{2}uv\end{split}\right.

where
\varphi=u-Av
and

. We suppose that
\mathcal{S}
is smooth, which is the case for

When the constants

and

are all nonzero, we consider the class of the following quaternion algebra defining an element of
\textup{Br}(K(\mathcal{S}))
of order dividing
2

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\left(a,\frac{b(u-Av)}{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(a,\frac{b\varphi}{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(a,\frac{-(u-Bv)}{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(a,\frac{-\psi}{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(a,\frac{b(u-Av)}{-au}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(a,\frac{b\varphi}{-au}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(a,\frac{-(u-Bv)}{-au}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(a,\frac{-\psi}{-au}\right)\in\textup{Br}(K(\mathcal{S})).

In the equation above, the equalities of the left column follow from the defining equations of
\mathcal{S}
and the fact that
\left(a,x^{2}-ay^{2}\right)=0
and
\left(a,x^{2}-az^{2}\right)=0

Note that
\mathcal{S}\cap V(u,v)
is of codimension
2
in
\mathcal{S}
with complement
\mathcal{S}^{0}=\mathcal{S}\cap(D_{+}(u)\cup D_{+}(v))

, hence the Azumaya algebra
\mathcal{A}
extends to
\mathcal{S}^{0}
. Furthermore, by the purity theorem for Brauer groups, the element
\mathcal{A}
lies in the subgroup
\textup{Br}(\mathcal{S})\subset\textup{Br}(K(\mathcal{S}))
. We will compute the local invariants

for places
\pi
of

and local rational points

and the forthcoming calculation of

Since

and

Lemma 5.12

g+1\mid 4h+2
.
There exists a Brauer–Manin obstruction to the Hasse principle on any smooth proper models of
{}^{h,g}\negthinspace\textbf{X}
,
{}^{h,g}\negthinspace\textbf{Y}
, and
{}^{h,g}\negthinspace\textbf{S}
.

Proof.

By looking at the generic fibers of the regular locus
\textbf{X}^{\textup{reg}}
,
\textbf{Y}^{\textup{reg}}
, and
\textbf{S}^{\textup{reg}}
viewed as
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-schemes, we see that they are geometrically integral over
K
.
According to
[
7
, Proposition 6.1(i)]
together with Chow’s lemma, the existence of Brauer–Manin obstruction to the Hasse principle is birational invariant among smooth proper geometrically integral varieties. Hence it suffices to prove the statement for one of the smooth proper models of each variety.

Combining Nagata’s compactification
[
21
]
and Hironaka’s resolution of singularities
[
17
]
, we know that given a morphism between smooth varieties
V\longrightarrow W
, then
V
and
W
admit smooth compactifications, and for any smooth compactification
\widetilde{W}
of
W
there exists a compactification
\widetilde{V}
of
V
such that the morphism extends to
\widetilde{V}\longrightarrow\widetilde{W}
. Now we take smooth open dense subvarieties
\textbf{X}^{o}\subset\textbf{X}
,
\textbf{Y}^{o}\subset\textbf{Y}
, and
\textbf{S}^{o}\subset\textbf{S}
such that the morphism
\delta:\textbf{X}\longrightarrow\textbf{Y}\subset\textbf{S}
restricts to
\delta^{o}:\textbf{X}^{o}\longrightarrow\textbf{Y}^{o}\subset\textbf{S}^{o}
.
We can extend
\delta^{o}
to morphisms between certain smooth compactifications
\widetilde{\textbf{X}}\longrightarrow\widetilde{\textbf{Y}}\longrightarrow\widetilde{\textbf{S}}
. By the functoriality of the Brauer–Manin set, it remains to show that there exists a Brauer–Manin obstruction to the Hasse principle on a certain smooth compactification
\widetilde{\textbf{S}}
of
S
.

As the generic fiber of
\widetilde{\textbf{S}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a geometrically rationally connected variety, it has a section over
\bar{K}
by the Graber–Harris–Starr theorem
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
. In
[
14
, Thérème 4.2.1]
and
[
15
, Proposition 3.1.1]
, D. Harari proved that for such a fibration, the existence of a family of local rational points surviving the Brauer–Manin obstruction implies the existence of a family of local rational points on a smooth fiber over a certain rational point surviving the Brauer–Manin obstruction, provided that all fibers over closed points are split. But this last statement contradicts to Theorem
5.1
. To conclude, it remains to take
\textbf{S}^{o}=\textbf{S}^{\textup{reg}}
and check the splitness assumption, which is the task of the forthcoming Proposition
6.8
.
∎

Proposition 6.8
.

Assume that
g
is an odd integer. Let
\tilde{\tau}:\widetilde{\textbf{S}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a smooth compactification of the morphism
\tau^{\textup{reg}}:\textbf{S}^{\textup{reg}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
which is the restriction of
\tau:\textbf{S}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
to the regular locus
\textbf{S}^{\textup{reg}}=\textbf{S}\setminus\textbf{S}^{\textup{sing}}
of
S
.

Then for any closed point
\theta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the fiber
\widetilde{\textbf{S}}_{\theta}
is split, i.e. it contains an open geometrically integral
K(\theta)
-subscheme
[
26
, Definition 0.1]
.

Proof.

According to Proposition
6.1
,
\textbf{S}^{\textup{sing}}
has codimension
2
in
S
, hence for each closed point
\theta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
the fiber
\textbf{S}^{\textup{reg}}_{\theta}
is an open
K(\theta)
-subscheme of both
\textbf{S}_{\theta}
and
\widetilde{\textbf{S}}_{\theta}
. It suffices to show that
\textbf{S}^{\textup{reg}}_{\theta}
is split.

According to Proposition
6.1
, the singular locus
\textbf{S}^{\textup{sing}}
is the union of
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{F}_{C}
and finitely many closed points whose projections to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
do not intersect
\mathcal{F}_{C}
.
When
\theta\notin\mathcal{F}_{C}
, we denote by

\begin{split}\Phi_{1}&=x^{2}-az^{2}+b(u-A_{\theta}v)(u-B_{\theta}v),\\
\Phi_{2}&=x^{2}-ay^{2}+aC_{\theta}^{2}uv,\end{split}

the two quadratic forms defining
\textbf{S}_{\theta}
. It is clear that
\textup{rank}(\Phi_{1})\geq 3
and
\textup{rank}(\Phi_{2})=4
since
C_{\theta}\neq 0
. Thanks to
[
9
, Lemma 1.11]
, where sufficient conditions are given to deduce that
\textbf{S}_{\theta}
is geometrically integral, we thus find that
\textbf{S}^{\textup{reg}}_{\theta}
is geometrically integral.
We check the required conditions of the relevant lemma as follows.

•

The polynomials
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
have no common factor since both are irreducible, which is a consequence of their ranks and the diagonalization.

•

For
\lambda=1
and
\mu\in K(\theta)^{*}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
not a root of the quadratic equation
(aC_{\theta}\mu-bA_{\theta}-bB_{\theta})^{2}=4b^{2}A_{\theta}B_{\theta}
, the form
\lambda\Phi_{1}+\mu\Phi_{2}
is of rank
5
.

•

For all
(\lambda,\mu)
, no nonzero form
\lambda\Phi_{1}+\mu\Phi_{2}
is of rank less than
3
. Indeed, we may assume that
\lambda\neq 0
and
\mu\neq 0
since both
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
have rank no less than
3
. Seen by taking
x=v=0
, the form
\lambda\Phi_{1}+\mu\Phi_{2}
has rank at least
3
.

When
\theta\in\mathcal{F}_{C}
, we deduce that
a
is a square in the residue field
K(\theta)
from the equality
C_{\theta}=a^{2h+1}\theta^{g+1}-1=0
since
g
is odd. In this case, the second defining equation of
\textbf{S}_{\theta}
degenerates to
(x+\sqrt{a}y)(x-\sqrt{a}y)=0
. It defines a union of two hyperplanes
\mathcal{H}^{+}
and
\mathcal{H}^{-}
in
\mathbb{P}^{4}_{K(\theta)}
. As we have seen in the proof of Proposition
6.1
, the polynomials
A_{\theta}
and
B_{\theta}
takes nonzero constant values
A
and
B
with
A\neq B
once
C_{\theta}=0
. The fiber
\textbf{S}_{\theta}
is a union of the intersections
\mathcal{Q}^{\pm}
of the
3
-dimensional quadric defined over
K(\theta)
by

x^{2}-az^{2}=-b(u-Av)(u-Bv)

and the hyperplanes
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}
. Each of
\mathcal{Q}^{\pm}
is a
2
-dimensional geometrically integral quadric in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{K(\theta)}
since it is given by a quadratic form of rank
4
. As
\mathcal{H}^{+}\cap\mathcal{H}^{-}
is given by
x=y=0
, the intersection
\mathcal{Q}^{+}\cap\mathcal{Q}^{-}
is exactly the smooth conic
\mathcal{C}_{K(\theta)}=\textbf{S}^{\textup{sing}}\cap\textbf{S}_{\theta}
. The two irreducible components
\mathcal{Q}^{+}\setminus\mathcal{Q}^{-}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{-}\setminus\mathcal{Q}^{+}
of
\textbf{S}^{\textup{reg}}_{\theta}
are both geometrically integral.
∎

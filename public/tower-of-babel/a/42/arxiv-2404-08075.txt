The algebra of the monoid of order-preserving functions on an n n -set and other reduced E E -Fountain semigroups
Itamar Stein

Abstract
With every reduced E E -Fountain semigroup S S which satisfies the generalized right ample condition we associate a category with zero morphisms 𝒞 ⁡ ( S ) \mathcal{C}(S) . Under some assumptions we prove an isomorphism of 𝕜 \Bbbk -algebras 𝕜 ​ S ≃ 𝕜 0 ​ 𝒞 ​ ( S ) \Bbbk S\simeq\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(S) between the semigroup algebra and the contracted category algebra where 𝕜 \Bbbk is any commutative unital ring. This is a simultaneous generalization of a former result of the author on reduced E-Fountain semigroups which satisfy the congruence condition, a result of Junying Guo and Xiaojiang Guo on strict right ample semigroups and a result of Benjamin Steinberg on idempotent semigroups with central idempotents. The applicability of the new isomorphism is demonstrated with two well-known monoids which are not members of the above classes. The monoid of order-preserving functions on an n n -set and the monoid of binary relations with demonic composition.

1
Introduction

Given a semigroup
S
and a commutative ring
\Bbbk
, it is of interest
to study the semigroup algebra
\Bbbk S
. One approach is to associate
with
S
a category
\mathcal{C}(S)
such that the semigroup algebra

\Bbbk S
is isomorphic to the category algebra
\Bbbk\mathcal{C}(S)
.
It is usually easier to study the category algebra because many of
the natural basis elements are orthogonal. A pioneering work in this
direction was done by Solomon
[
20
]
who proved that
the semigroup algebra of a finite semilattice is isomorphic to the
algebra of a discrete category. This was generalized to finite inverse
semigroups by Steinberg
[
28
]
. From the ESN theorem

[
16
, Theorem 4.1.8]
we know that every inverse semigroup

S
is associated with an inductive groupoid
G(S)
. Steinberg proved
that if
S
is finite then
\Bbbk S\simeq\Bbbk G(S)
and this result
is fundamental in the representation theory of finite inverse semigroups

[
29
, Chapter 9]
. Guo and Chen
[
11
]
obtained
a similar result for finite ample semigroups. Motivated by questions
on self-injective and Frobenius semigroup algebras, Guo and Guo
[
10
]

generalized this result to a class of semigroup they call strict right
ample semigroups. Taking a different direction, the author extended
the result of Guo and Chen on finite ample semigroups to a class of
right restriction
E
-Ehresmann semigroups
[
22
,
23
]

(see also
[
35
]
) and later to a wider class of reduced

E
-Fountain semigroups
[
26
]
. Let
S
be a semigroup
and let
E
be a distinguished subset of idempotents
E\subseteq S

with the property that
ef=e\iff fe=e
for
e,f\in E
. The semigroup

S
is called a reduced
E
-Fountain semigroup if for every element

a\in S
the sets of its right and left identities from
E
have
minimum elements (with respect to the natural partial order on idempotents).
The minimal right identity of
a
from
E
is denoted
a^{\ast}

and the minimal left identity of
a
from
E
is denoted
a^{+}
.
Reduced
E
-Fountain semigroups form a wide class that contains:
inverse semigroups,
\Jc
-trivial semigroups and many other types
of semigroups. If
S
is reduced
E
-Fountain then we can define
a graph
\mathcal{C_{\bullet}}(S)
whose objects are the idempotents
of
E
and every
a\in S
corresponds to a morphism
C(a)
from

a^{\ast}
to
a^{+}
. If
S
satisfies the congruence condition
(the identities
(ab)^{\ast}=(a^{\ast}b)^{\ast}
and
(ab)^{+}=(ab^{+})^{+}
)
then
\mathcal{\mathcal{C_{\bullet}}}(S)
becomes a category when
we define
C(b)C(a)=C(ba)
if
b^{\ast}=a^{+}
. If, in addition,

S
satisfies some further conditions that will be described below
then it is proved in
[
26
, Theorem 4.4]
that
\Bbbk S\simeq\Bbbk\mathcal{C_{\bullet}}(S)

for any commutative unital ring
\Bbbk
. This result has led to several
applications regarding quivers
[
21
,
25
]
, global-dimension

[
24
]
and projective modules
[
18
]
for
algebras of semigroups of partial functions. A special case of this
result is the neat proof that the algebra of the Catalan monoid is
an incidence algebra
[
19
]
and there are also applications
to the study of certain partition monoids
[
4
]
.

Recently, as part of a detailed study of semigroup determinants
[
30
]
,
Steinberg proved a similar result for the class of finite idempotent
semigroups with central idempotents. These are finite semigroups that
satisfy
S^{2}=S
and
ea=ae
for every element
a\in S
and idempotent

e\in S
. Such a semigroup
S
is reduced
E
-Fountain if we take

E
to be the set of all idempotents of
S
. In this case the associated
graph
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}(S)
contains only endomorphisms and
despite not being a category it is possible to add zero morphisms
and turn it into a category with zero morphisms. Then, the semigroup
algebra is isomorphic to the contracted algebra of the category with
zero morphisms
[
30
, Theorem 6.5]
.

The goal of this paper is to achieve a simultaneous generalization
for our result on reduced
E
-Fountain semigroups that satisfy the
congruence condition, Guo and Guo’s result on strict right ample semigroups
and Steinberg’s result on idempotent semigroups with central idempotents.
In Section
2
we give some required preliminaries. In
Section
3
we consider the generalized right ample condition,
discussed in
[
26
,
27
]
and show that it is equivalent
to the condition
(ba)^{\ast}=a^{\ast}\implies(ba^{+})^{\ast}=a^{+}

for every
a,b\in S
. If a reduced
E
-Fountain semigroup
S
satisfies
the generalized right ample condition, then we can add zero morphisms
to
\mathcal{C_{\bullet}}(S)
and obtain a new graph
\mathcal{C}(S)
.
We define composition
C(b)\cdot C(a)
of two non-zero morphisms

C(b),C(a)
with
b^{\ast}=a^{+}
to be equal to
C(ba)
if
(ba)^{\ast}=b^{\ast},\quad(ba)^{+}=a^{+}

and
0
otherwise. We prove that this endows
\mathcal{C}(S)
with
a structure of a category with zero morphisms. We define a relation

\trianglelefteq_{l}
on
S
by the rule that
a\trianglelefteq_{l}b

if
a=be
for an
e\in E
. The main theorem of Section
3

shows that if a reduced
E
-Fountain semigroup
S
satisfies the
generalized right ample condition and
\trianglelefteq_{l}
is contained
in a principally finite partial order then
\Bbbk S\simeq\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(S)

for every commutative unital ring
\Bbbk
(where
\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(S)

is the contracted algebra of
\mathcal{C}(S)
). In Section
4

we explain why this is a generalization of the three above-mentioned
results of the author, Guo and Guo, and Steinberg.

In Section
5
we show that the above isomorphism applies to
two additional examples which are not included in any of the three
older results: The monoid
\Op_{n}
of all order-preserving functions
on an
n
-element set (see
[
5
, Chapter 14]
and
references therein) and the monoid of binary relations with demonic
composition, whose origins are in computer science
[
1
]
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Semigroups and categories

Let
S
be a semigroup and let
S^{1}=S\cup\{1\}
be the monoid
formed by adjoining a formal unit element. Recall that Green’s preorders

\leq_{\Rc}
,
\leq_{\Lc}
and
\leq_{\Jc}
are defined by:

\displaystyle a\,\leq_{\Rc}\,b\iff

\displaystyle aS^{1}\subseteq bS^{1}

\displaystyle a\,\leq_{\Lc}\,b\iff

\displaystyle S^{1}a\subseteq S^{1}b

\displaystyle a\,\leq_{\Jc}\,b\iff

\displaystyle S^{1}aS^{1}\subseteq S^{1}bS^{1}.

The associated Green’s equivalence relations on
S
are denoted by

\Rc
,
\Lc
and
\Jc
. Additional basic notions and proofs of
semigroup theory can be found in standard textbooks such as
[
12
]
.

We denote by
E(S)
the set of idempotents of the semigroup
S
.
The natural partial order on
E(S)
is defined by
e\leq f\iff(ef=e=fe)

and note that
\leq\,=\,\leq_{\Rc}\cap\leq_{\Lc}
. For every subset
of idempotents
E\subseteq E(S)
and every
a\in S
we denote by

a_{E}
the set of right identities of
a
from
E
:

a_{E}=\{e\in E\mid ae=a\}

Dually
\prescript{}{E}{a}
is the set of left identities from
E
.
We define two equivalence relations
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}
on
S
by

a\,\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}\,b\iff(a_{E}=b_{E})

a\,\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}\,b\iff(\prescript{}{E}{a}=\prescript{}{E}{b}).

These relations are one type of “generalized” Green’s relations
(see
[
7
]
). Note that
\Lc\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
and
\Rc\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The semigroup
S
is called
E
-Fountain
if every
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
-class
contains an idempotent from
E
and every
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}
-class contains an
idempotent from
E
.

We remark that this property is also called “
E
-semiabundant”
in the literature.

Definition 2.2
.

An
E
-Fountain semigroup
S
is called
reduced
if

ef=e\iff fe=e

for every
e,f\in E
. Equivalently,
S
is reduced if
\leq_{\Lc}=\leq_{\Rc}=\leq

when restricted to the set
E
.

A reduced
E
-
Fountain

semigroup is called a “DR
semigroup” in
[
31
]
.

Let
S
be a semigroup and let
E\subseteq S
be a subset of idempotents
which satisfies
ef=e\iff fe=e
for every
e,f\in E
.
Then every
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
-class (
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}
-class) contains at most one idempotent
from
E
. The
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
-class (
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}
-class) of
a\in S
contains an
element of
E
if and only if the set
a_{E}
(respectively,
\prescript{}{E}{a}
)
contains a minimum element.

If
S
is a reduced
E
-Fountain semigroup, the unique idempotent
from
E
in the
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
-class (
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}
-class) of
a\in S
is denoted

a^{\ast}
(respectively,
a^{+}
). We emphasize that if
e\in E

satisfies
ae=a
then
ea^{\ast}=a^{\ast}e=a^{\ast}
and dually
if
e\in E
satisfies
ea=a
then
ea^{+}=a^{+}e=a^{+}
. See
[
31
]

for proofs and additional details.

The following simple observation will be very useful.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
S
be a reduced
E
-Fountain semigroup
and let
a,b\in S
. Then
(ba)^{\ast}\leq a^{\ast}
and
(ba)^{+}\leq b^{+}
.

Proof.

The equality
baa^{\ast}=ba
implies
(ba)^{\ast}a^{\ast}=(ba)^{\ast}

so
(ba)^{\ast}\leq a^{\ast}
. Proving
(ba)^{+}\leq b^{+}
is dual.
∎

Green’s relation
\Lc
(
\Rc
) is always a right congruence (respectively,
left congruence), but this is not the case with the generalized Green’s
relations
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
S
be a reduced
E
-Fountain semigroup. We say that
S
satisfies
the
right

congruence condition
if
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
is a right
congruence. It is known that this is equivalent to the identity
(ab)^{\ast}=(a^{\ast}b)^{\ast}

for every
a,b\in S

[
7
, Lemma 4.1]
. Dually,
S
satisfies
the
left

congruence condition
if
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}
is a left congruence
which is equivalent to the identity
(ab)^{+}=(ab^{+})^{+}
.

Denote by
\PT_{X}
the monoid of all partial functions on a set

X
- composing functions from right to left. Then
\PT_{X}^{\op}

is the monoid with the same underlying set but composing functions
from left to right. Let
\psi:S\to\PT_{X}
be a semigroup homomorphism.
We follow
[
9
, Definition 3.2]
and call
\psi
an
incomplete
left action
of
S
on
X
. Equivalently, we say that
X
is an

incomplete left
S
-action
(note that
partial
action
usually has a different meaning - for instance, see
[
14
, Definition 2.2]
).
Another convention is to write
s\bullet x
instead of
\psi(s)(x)

for
s\in S
and
x\in X
. Note that
s\bullet x
might be undefined.
Of course, we could have added a symbol
0
and talk about actions
of
S
on
X\cup\{0\}
such that
s\bullet 0=s
for every
s\in S
.
However, we prefer not to add
0
at this point.

It is known
[
27
, Lemma 3.4]
that every
\Lc
-class
L

is an incomplete left
S
-action according to

s\bullet x=\begin{cases}sx&sx\in L\\
\text{undefined}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

for every
x\in L
and
s\in S
. The same is true if we replace

\Lc
-class by
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
-class
[
18
, Lemma 5.1]
. Assume
that
X,Y
are incomplete left
S
-actions. A partial function
f:X\to Y

is a
partial

homomorphism of incomplete left
S
-actions

if for every
x\in X
and
s\in S
,
f(s\bullet x)
is defined
if and only if
s\bullet f(x)
is defined and in this case

f(s\bullet x)=s\bullet f(x).

There is a dual notion of course. An
incomplete right action

of
S
on a set
X
is a homomorphism
\psi:S\to\PT_{X}^{\op}
.
Equivalently, we say that
X
is an
incomplete right
S
-action
.
Partial homomorphisms of incomplete right
S
-actions are defined
in the obvious dual way.

We can regard any graph
\mathcal{C}
as set of objects, denoted

\mathcal{C}^{0}
, and a set of morphisms, denoted
\mathcal{C}^{1}
.
It is equipped with two functions
\db,\rb:\mathcal{C}^{1}\to\mathcal{C}^{0}

associating every morphism
m\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
with its
domain

\db(m)

and its
range

\rb(m)
. The set of morphisms with
domain
e
and range
f
(also called an
hom-set
) is denoted

\mathcal{C}(e,f)
. Since any category has an underlying graph, the
above notations hold also for categories. We compose morphisms in
a category from right to left so
m^{\prime}m
is defined in a category

\mathcal{C}
if
\db(m^{\prime})=\rb(m)
.

A category
C
is called a
category with zero morphisms
if
for every
x,y\in\mathcal{C}^{0}
there exists a zero morphism
0_{y,x}\in\mathcal{C}(x,y)

and for every three objects
e,f,g\in\mathcal{C}^{0}
and morphisms

m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}(g,f)
,
m\in\mathcal{C}(e,g)
we have

m^{\prime}0_{g,e}=0_{f,e}=0_{f,g}m.

Equivalently, a category with zero morphisms is a category enriched
over the category of pointed sets.

2.2
Algebras

Let
\Bbbk
be a unital commutative ring and let
S
be a semigroup.
The
semigroup algebra

\mathbb{\Bbbk}S
is defined in the
following way. It is a free
\mathbb{\Bbbk}
-module with basis the
elements of
S
, that is, it consists of all formal linear combinations

\{k_{1}s_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}s_{n}\mid k_{i}\in\mathbb{\Bbbk},\,s_{i}\in S\}.

The multiplication in
\mathbb{\Bbbk}S
is the linear extension of
the semigroup multiplication. Note that in general
\Bbbk S
might
not possess a unit element.

The
category algebra

\mathbb{\Bbbk}\mathcal{C}
of a (small)
category
\mathcal{C}
is defined in the following way. It is a free

\mathbb{\Bbbk}
-module with basis the morphisms of
\mathcal{C}
,
that is, it consists of all formal linear combinations

\{k_{1}m_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}m_{n}\mid k_{i}\in\mathbb{\Bbbk},\,m_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}\}.

The multiplication in
\mathbb{\Bbbk}\mathcal{C}
is the linear extension
of the following:

m^{\prime}\cdot m=\begin{cases}m^{\prime}m&\text{if $m^{\prime}m$ is defined}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

If
\mathcal{C}
has a finite number of objects then the unit element
of
\Bbbk\mathcal{C}
is
{\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{C}^{0}}1_{e}}

where
1_{e}
is the identity morphism of the object
e
of
\mathcal{C}
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category with zero morphisms and denote by

Z
the ideal of
\Bbbk\mathcal{C}
generated by the zero morphisms.
The
contracted category algebra
of
\mathcal{C}
is defined
by
\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}=\Bbbk\mathcal{C}/Z
. In a contracted category
algebra we identify all the zero morphisms with the zero element of
the algebra.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional and unital
\Bbbk
-algebra. Let

E=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be a set of idempotents from
A
. Recall
that
E
is a complete set of orthogonal idempotents if
{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}=1_{A}}

and
e_{i}e_{j}=0
if
i\neq j
. If, in addition, the idempotents
of
E
are
central
(i.e.
ea=ae
for every
e\in E
and

a\in A
) then
A\simeq{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}Ae_{i}}

is an isomorphism of
\Bbbk
-algebras.

Two
\Bbbk
-algebras
A
and
B
are
Morita equivalent
if
the category of
A
-modules is equivalent to the category of
B
-modules.

Assume that
\Bbbk
is a field and recall that
a
\Bbbk
-algebra
A
is called
semisimple

if any
A
-module is semisimple (= a direct sum
of simple modules). We denote by
\Rad A
the

radical
of
A
,
which is the minimal ideal such that
A/\Rad A

is a semisimple algebra. The radical
\Rad A

is also the only nilpotent ideal
I
of
A

with the property that
A/I
is a semisimple
\Bbbk
-algebra.

3
An isomorphism theorem and the generalized
right ample condition

Definition 3.1
.

Let
S
be a reduced
E
-Fountain
semigroup. We say that
S
satisfies the
generalized right
ample condition
(or the
generalized right ample identity
)
if

(b(a(ba)^{\ast})^{+})^{\ast}=(a(ba)^{\ast})^{+}

for every
a,b\in S
.

Remark 3.2
.

This identity is given in
[
27
]
in the form
(b^{\ast}(a(b^{\ast}a)^{\ast})^{+})^{\ast}=(a(b^{\ast}a)^{\ast})^{+}

for
a,b\in S
(or
(e(a(ea)^{\ast})^{+})^{\ast}=(a(ea)^{\ast})^{+}

for
e\in E
and
a\in S
). This is equivalent to the identity in

3.1
if we assume the right congruence
condition. In this paper we do not assume the right congruence condition
so we are forced to take a slightly different form of the identity.

Let
S
be a reduced
E
-Fountain semigroup. We denote the
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
-class
of
e\in E
by
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}(e)
. We have already mentioned in the preliminaries
that for every
e\in E
, the set
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}(e)
is an incomplete
S
-action
according to

s\bullet x=\begin{cases}sx&sx\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}(e)\\
\text{undefined}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

for
s\in S
and
x\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}(e)
.

For every
a\in S
we can define a partial function
r_{a}:\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}(a^{+})\to\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}(a^{\ast})

by

r_{a}(b)=\begin{cases}ba&(ba)^{\ast}=a^{\ast}\\
\text{undefined}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where
b\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}(a^{+})
.

The following proposition strengthens
[
27
, Theorem 3.6]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
S
be a reduced

E
-Fountain semigroup. The following conditions are equivalent.

1.

The semigroup
S
satisfies the generalized right ample condition.

2.

The semigroup
S
satisfies
(ba)^{\ast}=a^{\ast}\implies(ba^{+})^{\ast}=a^{+}

for every
a,b\in S
.

3.

The partial function
r_{a}:\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}(a^{+})\to\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}(a^{\ast})
is a partial
homomorphism of incomplete left
S
-actions for every
a\in S
.

Proof.

For
(1\Rightarrow 2)
substitute
(ba)^{\ast}=a^{\ast}
in the generalized
right ample identity to obtain

(b(aa^{\ast})^{+})^{\ast}=(aa^{\ast})^{+}

so we get
(ba^{+})^{\ast}=a^{+}
as required. For
(2\Rightarrow 1)
,
assume that
(yx)^{\ast}=x^{\ast}
implies
(yx^{+})^{\ast}=x^{+}
.
Note that by
2.3

(ba)^{\ast}=(ba(ba)^{\ast})^{\ast}\leq(a(ba)^{\ast})^{\ast}\leq(ba)^{\ast}

so
(a(ba)^{\ast})^{\ast}=(ba)^{\ast}
and therefore,

(b(a(ba)^{\ast}))^{\ast}=(ba)^{\ast}=(a(ba)^{\ast})^{\ast}.

Setting
y=b
and
x=a(ba)^{\ast}
implies that

(b(a(ba)^{\ast})^{+})^{\ast}=(a(ba)^{\ast})^{+}

as required. For
(3\Rightarrow 2)
we assume that
(ba)^{\ast}=a^{\ast}
.
This means that
b\bullet r_{a}(a^{+})=ba\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}(a^{\ast})
. Since

r_{a}
is a partial homomorphism of incomplete left
S
-actions
we know that
r_{a}(b\bullet a^{+})
is also defined so in particular

ba^{+}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}(a^{+})
hence
(ba^{+})^{\ast}=a^{+}
. It is left
to prove
(2\Rightarrow 3)
. Assume that
(ba)^{\ast}=a^{\ast}
implies

(ba^{+})^{\ast}=a^{+}
and let
s,a\in S
and
x\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}(a^{+})
.
If
s\bullet r_{a}(x)
is defined then
(xa)^{\ast}=a^{\ast}
and

(sxa)^{\ast}=a^{\ast}
. According to the assumption (with
b=sx
)
we find that
(sxa^{+})^{\ast}=a^{+}
but
a^{+}=x^{\ast}
so
(sx)^{\ast}=x^{\ast}
.
This shows that
sx\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}(a^{+})
and therefore
s\bullet x
is
defined. Moreover
(sxa)^{\ast}=a^{\ast}
so
r_{a}(s\bullet x)

is defined. In the other direction if
r_{a}(s\bullet x)
is defined
then
(sx)^{\ast}=x^{\ast}
and
(sxa)^{\ast}=a^{\ast}
. Since
a^{\ast}=(sxa)^{\ast}\leq(xa)^{\ast}\leq a^{\ast}

we see that

Definition 3.4

Lemma 3.5

Let

First observe that

Corollary 3.6

Lemma 3.8

Let

be a reduced

-Fountain semigroup which satisfies the generalized right ample

Proof.

Let

Theorem 3.10

Let

-Fountain semigroup and assume that

Generalization of older results

In this section we will explain why

literature.

The congruence condition

is proved in
[
26
, Theorem 4.4]

under the additional assumption that

satisfies the congruence
condition. To be precise,
[
26
, Theorem 4.4]
is the simplified
case given in
3.13
because in this case

is a category by

.
This case includes inverse semigroups
[

]
, semigroups
of partial functions
[

]
and the Catalan monoid
[

]
.

Generalized matrix representations and strict right ample semigroups

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
be algebras over a commutative unital ring

\Bbbk
. For every
1\leq i,j\leq n
let
A_{i,j}
be an
(A_{i},A_{j})
-bimodule
where
A_{i,i}=A_{i}
. Assume that for every triple
(i,j,k)
there
exists an
(A_{i},A_{j})
-bimodule homomorphism
\Phi_{i,k}^{j}:A_{i,j}\otimes A_{j,k}\to A_{i,k}

such that the associativity condition

\Phi_{i,l}^{k}\circ(\Phi_{i,k}^{j}\otimes\id_{k,l})=\Phi_{i,l}^{j}\circ(\id_{i,j}\otimes\Phi_{j,l}^{k})

holds (here
\id_{i,j}:A_{i,j}\to A_{i,j}
is the identity function).
The
\Bbbk
-module
M[A_{i,j}]
of all matrices

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1}&a_{1,2}&\cdots&a_{1,n}\\
a_{2,1}&a_{2}\\
\vdots&&\ddots\\
a_{n,1}&&&a_{n}\end{array}\right)

with
a_{i}\in A_{i}
,
a_{i,j}\in A_{i,j}
is a
\Bbbk
-algebra
with the obvious multiplication

[M\cdot N]_{i,j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Phi_{i,j}^{k}(M_{i,k}\otimes N_{k,j}).

If
A
is a
\Bbbk
-algebra, an isomorphism of
\Bbbk
-algebras

\varphi:A\to M[A_{i,j}]
is called a
generalized matrix representation

of
A
with degree
n
.

We can restate
3.12
in this language, at
least when
E
is a finite set. Let
S
be a reduced
E
-Fountain
semigroup which satisfies the generalized right ample condition and
assume that
E=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is a finite set. Define
A_{i,j}=\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(S)(e_{j},e_{i})

where
\Bbbk
is any commutative unital ring. Note that
A_{i}=\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(S)(e_{i},e_{i})

is indeed a
\Bbbk
-algebra. Define
\Phi_{i,k}^{j}:A_{i,j}\otimes A_{j,k}\to A_{i,k}

on basis elements by

\displaystyle\Phi_{i,k}^{j}(C(b)\otimes C(a))

\displaystyle=C(b)\cdot C(a)

The associativity of
\mathcal{C}(S)
implies the associativity condition
on
M[A_{i,j}]
.

We can define
\varphi:\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(S)\to M[A_{i,j}]

by setting

[\varphi(C(s))]_{i,j}=\begin{cases}C(s)&e_{i}=s^{+},\quad e_{j}=s^{\ast}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is clear that
\varphi
is a generalized matrix representation
for
\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(S)
of degree
|E|
.
3.12

now implies:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
S
be a
reduced
E
-Fountain semigroup and let
\Bbbk
be a commutative
unital ring. Assume that
E
is finite,

satisfies the generalized
right ample condition and
\trianglelefteq_{l}
is contained in a
partial order. Then
\Bbbk S
has a generalized matrix representation
of degree
|E|
.

This generalizes
[
10
, Theorem 3.2]
which was proved for
the class of strict right ample semigroups. A strict right ample semigroup

is a reduced
E
-Fountain semigroup (with

) that satisfies
stronger assumptions than the assumptions of
4.1
.
For instance, in a strict right ample semigroup the set
E(S)
is
a commutative subsemigroup so

clearly holds and
\trianglelefteq_{l}

is a partial order. Moreover, the right ample condition

[

Finite idempotent semigroups with central idempotents

Let
S
be a finite idempotent semigroup with central idempotents.
This means that
S^{2}=S
and
ea=ae
for every
a\in S
and

.
Setting

, it is shown in
[

that every

has a left (right) identity from

. If

are right identities of

is also an idempotent and

we deduce that every

and

.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
S
be a reduced
E
-Fountain semigroup where the set
E
is
central then

satisfies the generalized right ample condition.

Proof.

Assume

. We need to prove

which is equivalent to
(ba^{\ast})^{\ast}=a^{\ast}
. Since

(ba^{\ast})^{\ast}\leq a^{\ast}

Therefore

Since the set

so
\Bbbk S\simeq\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(S)

. In this special case,

.

Let

be a finite semigroup
with central idempotents such that

.

5.1
The monoid of order-preserving functions

Let
\T_{n}
be the monoid of all functions
f:[n]\to[n]
(where

[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
). A function

is called

for every

. We denote
by
\Op_{n}
the submonoid of
\T_{n}
of all order-preserving functions.
In this section we will see that
\Op_{n}
is a reduced
E
-Fountain
semigroup that satisfies the generalized
left
ample condition
so by the dual of
3.12
there is an isomorphism

\Bbbk\Op_{n}\simeq\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})
of
\Bbbk
-algebras
for any commutative unital ring
\Bbbk
. We will also describe more
explicitly the composition in
\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})
.

Elementary observations

Recall that the kernel of of a function
f\in\Op_{n}
-
\ker(f)

- is the equivalence relation on its domain defined by
(i_{1},i_{2})\in\ker(f)

if and only if
f(i_{1})=f(i_{2})
. Since
f
is order-preserving,
the kernel classes of
f
are
intervals
- if
i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq i_{3}

and
(i_{1},i_{3})\in\ker(f)
then
(i_{1},i_{2})\in\ker f
also.

Let
K_{1},\ldots,K_{l}
be the kernel classes of
f
. Let
y_{i}=f(K_{i}
)
and assume that the indices are arranged such that
y_{1}<y_{2}<\cdots<y_{l}
.
Choose
x_{i}
to be the maximal element of
K_{i}
and note that
it must be the case that
x_{l}=n
. Now, set

X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{l}\},\quad Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{l}\}

so from every

we can extract two sets

such that
|X|=|Y|
and
n\in X
. In the other direction, from two
such sets
X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{l}\}
where
x_{l}=n
and
Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{l}\}

we can retrieve an

by setting

f(x)=\begin{cases}y_{1}&x\leq x_{1}\\
y_{i}&x_{i-1}<x\leq x_{i}\quad(2\leq i\leq l).\end{cases}

As a conclusion, we observe that there is a one-to-one correspondence
between functions
f\in\Op_{n}
and pairs of sets
X,Y\subseteq[n]

such that
|X|=|Y|
and
n\in X
. From now on we denote by
f_{Y,X}

the function associated with
X,Y\subseteq[n]
(in contrary to
[
27
, Section 4.2]

where this function was denoted
f_{X,Y})
. Note that
\im(f_{Y,X})=Y

(where
\im(f)
is the image of
f
) and
X
contains the maximal
elements of the kernel classes of
f_{Y,X}
. Another easy observation
is that
f_{Z,Y}f_{Y,X}=f_{Z,X}
.

The monoid
\Op_{n}
is a regular submonoid of
\T_{n}
and its
Green’s equivalence relations are well-known (see
[
6
, Section 2]
).
In particular:

\displaystyle f_{Y_{1},X_{1}}\,\Lc\,f_{Y_{2},X_{2}}

\displaystyle\iff\ker(f_{Y_{1},X_{1}})=\ker(f_{Y_{2},X_{2}})\iff X_{1}=X_{2}

\displaystyle f_{Y_{1},X_{1}}\,\Rc\,f_{Y_{2},X_{2}}

\displaystyle\iff\im(f_{Y_{1},X_{1}})=\im(f_{Y_{2},X_{2}})\iff Y_{1}=Y_{2}

We want to define our distinguished set of idempotents
E
. Note
that for every
X\subseteq[n]
with
n\in X
the function
f_{X,X}

is an idempotent that can be described by
f_{X,X}(i)=\min\{x\in X\mid i\leq x\}
.
It will be convenient to define
e_{X}=f_{X,X}
where
X\subseteq[n]

with
n\in X
and we set
E=\{e_{X}\mid n\in X\}
(this is precisely
the set of idempotents of the Catalan monoid - see
[
29
, Section 17.5.2]
).
We have just seen that
f_{Y,X}\Lc e_{X}
and since
\Lc\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}

we have
f_{Y,X}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}\,e_{X}
. On the other hand, it is not always
true that
f_{Y,X}\,\Rc\,e_{Y}
because
e_{Y}
is defined only
if
n\in Y
. Denote by
\OF_{n}
the submonoid of
\Op_{n}
of
all order-preserving functions such that
f(n)=n
and note that
\OF_{n}=\{f_{Y,X}\in\Op_{n}\mid n\in Y\}
.
This monoid was discussed in detail in
[
27
, Section 4.2]
.
We have
f_{Y,X}\,\Rc\,e_{Y}
if only if
f_{Y,X}\in\OF_{n}
. However,
it is easy to see that

e_{Z}f_{Y,X}=f_{Y,X}

if and only if
Y\subseteq Z
. Therefore, the minimal left identity
of
f_{Y,X}
from
E
is
e_{Y\cup\{n\}}
hence
f_{Y,X}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}\,e_{Y\cup\{n\}}
.
It will be convenient to set
\widetilde{Y}=Y\cup\{n\}
for every

Y\subseteq[n]
so
f_{Y,X}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}\,e_{\widetilde{Y}}
for every
f_{Y,X}\in\Op_{n}
.
The first conclusion is that there exists an idempotent of
E
in
every
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
-class and
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}
-class of
\Op_{n}
. It is also easy
to check that
e_{Y}e_{X}=e_{X}\iff e_{X}e_{Y}=e_{X}\iff X\subseteq Y

(see also
[
26
, Section 6]
where we derived this fact
from
[
2
, Lemma 3.6]
) so
\Op_{n}
is a reduced
E
-Fountain
semigroup. Since
\Lc\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
and there is a unique element
of
E
in every
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
-class we deduce that
\Lc=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
and therefore

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}
is a right congruence so
\Op_{n}
satisfies the right congruence
condition. We will see later that
(f_{W,Z})^{\ast}=(f_{Y,X})^{+}

does not always imply that
(f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X})^{+}=(f_{W,Z})^{+}
so
in view of
3.6
we deduce that
\Op_{n}

does not satisfy the generalized right ample condition. However, it
turns out that it satisfies the generalized left ample condition.

Generalized left ample condition and isomorphism of algebras

First, we state the dual of the relevant results from Section
3
.
Every
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}
-class of a reduced
E
-Fountain semigroup
S
is an
incomplete right
S
-action and a partial function

l_{a}:\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}(a^{\ast})\to\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}(a^{+})

is defined by

l_{a}(b)=\begin{cases}ab&(ab)^{+}=a^{+}\\
\text{undefined}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The dual of
3.3
is:

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
S
be
a reduced
E
-Fountain semigroup. The following conditions are equivalent.

1.

The semigroup
S
satisfies
(((ab)^{+}a)^{\ast}b)^{+}=((ab)^{+}a)^{\ast}

for every
a,b\in S
.

2.

The semigroup
S
satisfies
(ab)^{+}=a^{+}\implies(a^{\ast}b)^{+}=a^{\ast}

for every
a,b\in S
.

3.

The partial function
l_{a}:\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}(a^{\ast})\to\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{E}(a^{+})
is a partial
homomorphism of incomplete right
S
-actions for every
a\in S
.

Define a relation
\trianglelefteq_{r}
on
S
by the rule that

a\trianglelefteq_{r}b
if and only if
a=eb
for some
e\in E
.
If we assume that
\mathcal{C}(S)
is a category and the relation

\trianglelefteq_{r}
is principally finite we can define a function

\varphi:\Bbbk S\to\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(S)
on basis elements by

\varphi(a)=\sum_{c\trianglelefteq_{r}a}C(c).

The dual of
3.12
is:

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
S
be a reduced
E
-Fountain
semigroup and assume that
\trianglelefteq_{r}
is contained in a
principally finite partial order. Then, the generalized left ample
condition holds if and only if
\mathcal{C}(S)
is a category and

\varphi
is an isomorphism of
\Bbbk
-algebras.

Next, we want to prove that
\Op_{n}
satisfies the generalized left
ample condition.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
f,g\in\Op_{n}
. If
\im(fg)=\im(f)

then
(f^{\ast}g)^{+}=f^{\ast}
.

Proof.

First note that
\im(fg)=\im(f)
if and only if
\im(g)
intersects
every kernel class of
f
. Now,
\ker(f)=\ker(f^{\ast})
so
\im(fg)=\im(f)

implies that
g
intersects every kernel class of
f^{\ast}
and
therefore
\im(f^{\ast}g)=\im(f^{\ast})
. This implies

(f^{\ast}g)^{+}=f^{\ast}.

∎

Proposition 5.4
.

The monoid
\Op_{n}
satisfies the generalized
left ample condition.

Proof.

Let
f,g\in\Op_{n}
and assume
(fg)^{+}=f^{+}
. We need to prove

(f^{\ast}g)^{+}=f^{\ast}
. The fact that
(fg)^{+}=f^{+}
implies
that
\im(fg)\cup\{n\}=\im(f)\cup\{n\}
. Since
\im(fg)\subseteq\im(f)

we have two options. If
\im(fg)=\im(f)
then
(f^{\ast}g)^{+}=f^{\ast}

by
5.3
. The only other option is

\im(fg)=\im(f)\backslash\{n\}.

In this case
\im(g)
intersects all the kernel classes of
f
except
the kernel class of
n
. Since
\ker(f)=\ker(f^{\ast})
, the image

\im(g)
intersects all the kernel classes of
f^{\ast}
except
the kernel class of
n
. Therefore

\im(f^{\ast}g)=\im(f^{\ast})\backslash\{n\}

which also implies
(f^{\ast}g)^{+}=f^{\ast}
as required.
∎

We want to prove that
\trianglelefteq_{r}
on
\Op_{n}
is contained
in a partial order. The following lemma will be useful.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
f_{Y,X},f_{W,Z}\in\Op_{n}
.
If
f_{Y,X}\trianglelefteq_{r}f_{W,Z}
then
X\subseteq Z
.

Proof.

The assumption that
f_{Y,X}\trianglelefteq_{r}f_{W,Z}
implies that

f_{Y,X}=ef_{W,Z}

for some idempotent
e\in E
. Recall that
X
is the set of all
maximal elements of kernel classes of
f_{Y,X}
and note that
x\neq n

is a maximal element of a kernel class of
f\in\Op_{n}
if and only
if
f(x)\neq f(x+1)
. If
f_{W,Z}(x)=f_{W,Z}(x+1)
then

f_{Y,X}(x)=ef_{W,Z}(x)=ef_{W,Z}(x+1)=f_{Y,X}(x+1).

Therefore if
x\neq n
is a maximal element of a kernel class of

f_{Y,X}
then it is a maximal element of a kernel class of
f_{W,Z}
.
With the fact that
n\in X,Z
we deduce that
X\subseteq Z
.
∎

Let
Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}\}
,
W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\}
(arranged
in increasing order) be two subsets of
[n]
of size
k
. We write

Y\geq W
if
y_{i}\geq w_{i}
for every
i
.

Lemma 5.6
.

The relation
\trianglelefteq_{r}

on
\Op_{n}
is contained in a partial order.

Proof.

Define a relation
\preceq
on
\Op_{n}
by
f_{Y,X}\preceq f_{W,Z}

if
X\subsetneqq Z
or (
X=Z
and
Y\geq W
) . It is easy to verify
that
\preceq
is indeed a partial order on
\Op_{n}
(it is the
lexicographic order on the Cartesian product of
\subseteq
and
\leq
).
We prove that
\trianglelefteq_{r}
is contained in
\preceq
. If

f_{Y,X},f_{W,Z}\in\Op_{n}
such that
f_{Y,X}\trianglelefteq_{r}f_{W,Z}

then
X\subseteq Z
by
5.5
. If
X\subsetneqq Z

then we are done. If
X=Z
then
|Y|=|X|=|Z|=|W|
, say

Z=X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\},\quad Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}\},\quad W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\}

Since
f_{Y,X}\trianglelefteq_{r}f_{W,Z}
then
f_{Y,X}=ef_{W,X}

for some idempotent
e\in E
. Now

y_{i}=f_{Y,X}(x_{i})=ef_{W,X}(x_{i})\geq f_{W,X}(x_{i})=w_{i}

so indeed
Y\geq W
and this finishes the proof.
∎

According to
5.2
,
5.4

and
5.6
we obtain:

Theorem 5.7
.

The graph
\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})

with composition as defined in
3.7

is a category (with zero morphisms) and for every commutative unital
ring
\Bbbk
there is an isomorphism of algebras
\Bbbk\Op_{n}\simeq\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})
.

Remark 5.8
.

The fact that
\Bbbk\Op_{n}
and
\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})

are Morita equivalent (i.e., their module categories are equivalent)
follows from the well-known Dold-Kan correspondence
[
3
,
13
]
.
It is possible to show that
\Bbbk\Op_{n}
is Morita equivalent to
the algebra of the category of sets
\{1,\ldots,k\}
for
1\leq k\leq n

and all the order-preserving functions between them - this is the
simplex category
[
17
, Section VII 5]
truncated at

n
. According to the Dold-Kan theorem, this algebra is Morita equivalent
to the contracted algebra of the skeleton of
\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})

which is Morita equivalent to
\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})
itself
(see
[
36
, Proposition 2.2]
). The contracted algebra of
the skeleton is the algebra of a straight line quiver with
n
vertices
and the relations are that each composition of successive arrows equals

0
. See also the Lack-Street equivalence
[
15
]
and
[
34
, Example 4.9]
.

Associated category

We want to show now that we can give a simple description of the composition
in
\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})
. We start with some useful lemmas.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
f_{W,Z},f_{Y,X}\in\Op_{n}
.
If
(f_{W,Z})^{\ast}=(f_{Y,X})^{+}
and
f_{Y,X}\in\OF_{n}
then

f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X}=f_{W,X}
.

Proof.

If
(f_{W,Z})^{\ast}=(f_{Y,X})^{+}
then
Z=\widetilde{Y}
(recall
that
\widetilde{Y}=Y\cup\{n\}
) but
f_{Y,X}\in\OF_{n}
implies

n\in Y
so
\widetilde{Y}=Y
. Therefore
Z=Y
and
f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X}=f_{W,X}
.
∎

For every non-empty
W\subseteq[n]
we set
\max(W)
to be the maximal
element of
W
and
W^{\prime}=W\backslash\{\max(W)\}
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
f_{W,Z},f_{Y,X}\in\Op_{n}
.
If
(f_{W,Z})^{\ast}=(f_{Y,X})^{+}
and
f_{Y,X}\notin\OF_{n}
then

f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X}=f_{W^{\prime},X}
.

Proof.

If
(f_{W,Z})^{\ast}=(f_{Y,X})^{+}
then
Z=\widetilde{Y}
but
f_{Y,X}\notin\OF_{n}

implies
n\notin Y
so
f_{W,Z}(n)=\max(W)
is not in the image
of
f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X}
. Every
w\in W^{\prime}
is the image of some

z\in Z\backslash\{n\}=Y
and therefore
w
is in the image of
f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X}
.
It is now clear that
f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X}=f_{W^{\prime},X}
.
∎

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
f_{W,Z},f_{Y,X}\in\Op_{n}

such that
(f_{W,Z})^{\ast}=(f_{Y,X})^{+}
. If
f_{W,Z}\in\OF_{n}

and
f_{Y,X}\notin\OF_{n}
or vice versa then
f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X}\notin\OF_{n}
.

Proof.

If
f_{W,Z}\in\OF_{n}
and
f_{Y,X}\notin\OF_{n}
then
\max(W)=n

and
W^{\prime}=W\backslash\{n\}
. By
5.10

we deduce
f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X}=f_{W\backslash\{n\},X}\notin\OF_{n}
. If

f_{W,Z}\notin\OF_{n}
then the image of
f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X}
which
is contained in the image of
f_{W,Z}
does not contain
n
so
f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X}\notin\OF_{n}.

∎

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
f_{W,Z},f_{Y,X}\in\Op_{n}
.
If
(f_{W,Z})^{\ast}=(f_{Y,X})^{+}
and at least one of
f_{W,Z},f_{Y,X}

is in
\OF_{n}
then
(f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X})^{+}=(f_{W,Z})^{+}
. If
f_{W,Z},f_{Y,X}\notin\OF_{n}

then
(f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X})^{+}\neq(f_{W,Z})^{+}
.

Proof.

If
f_{Y,X}\in\OF_{n}
then
5.9
implies

f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X}=f_{W,X}
so
(f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X})^{+}=e_{\widetilde{W}}=(f_{W,Z})^{+}
.
If
f_{Y,X}\notin\OF_{n}
then
f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X}=f_{W^{\prime},X}

by
5.10
so
(f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X})^{+}=e_{\widetilde{W^{\prime}}}

where
(f_{W,Z})^{+}=e_{\widetilde{W}}
. If
f_{W,Z}\in\OF_{n}

then
\max(W)=n
and
W^{\prime}=W\backslash\{n\}
so
\widetilde{W^{\prime}}=W=\widetilde{W}

and we obtain
(f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X})^{+}=(f_{W,Z})^{+}
. If
f_{W,Z},f_{Y,X}\notin\OF_{n}

then
\max(W)\neq n
so
\max(W)\notin\widetilde{W^{\prime}}
hence

\widetilde{W^{\prime}}\neq\widetilde{W}
and
(f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X})^{+}\neq(f_{W,Z})^{+}
.
∎

The graph
\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})
associated with
\Op_{n}
can be
described as follows. The objects are in one-to-one correspondence
with the set of idempotents
E=\{e_{X}\}
(where
X\subseteq[n]

such that
n\in X
). For every
f_{Y,X}\in\Op_{n}
we associate
a non-zero morphism
C(f_{Y,X})
from
e_{X}
to
e_{\widetilde{Y}}
.
Before we turn to describe the composition we emphasize an important
observation. Let
X,Y\subseteq[n]
such that
n\in X,Y
. If
|Y|=|X|

then
C(f_{Y,X})
is the unique non-zero morphism from
e_{X}
to

e_{Y}
(note that
f_{Y,X}\in\OF_{n}
in this case). If
|Y|=|X|+1

then
C(f_{Y^{\prime},X})
is the unique non-zero morphism from
e_{X}

to
e_{Y}
(note that
Y^{\prime}=Y\backslash\{n\}
and
f_{Y^{\prime},X}\notin\OF_{n}

in this case). If
|Y|\notin\{|X|,|X|+1\}
then there are no non-zero
morphisms from
e_{X}
to
e_{Y}
.

Example 5.13
.

The graph
\mathcal{C}(\Op_{3})
has the four objects
e_{\{3\}},e_{\{1,3\}},e_{\{2,3\}},e_{\{1,2,3\}}

and
10
non-zero morphisms. If we naturally draw
e_{Y}
above

e_{X}
if
|Y|>|X|
and ignore the identity and zero morphisms
then the graph
\mathcal{C}(\Op_{3})
is the following graph:

e_{\{1,2,3\}}
e_{\{1,3\}}
e_{\{2,3\}}
e_{\{3\}}
f_{\{2,3\},\{1,3\}}
f_{\{1,3\},\{2,3\}}
f_{\{1,2\},\{1,3\}}
f_{\{1,2\},\{2,3\}}
f_{\{1\},\{3\}}
f_{\{2\},\{3\}}

Recall that composition in
\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})
of two non-zero
morphisms
C(g),C(f)
with
g^{\ast}=f^{+}
is defined by:

C(g)\cdot C(f)=\begin{cases}C(gf)&(gf)^{\ast}=f^{\ast},\quad(gf)^{+}=g^{+}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We can simplify this description. Since
\Op_{n}
satisfies the right
congruence condition,
g^{\ast}=f^{+}\implies(gf)^{\ast}=f^{\ast}

so we can drop the requirement for
(gf)^{\ast}=f^{\ast}
. From
5.12

we deduce that
g^{\ast}=f^{+}
implies
(gf)^{+}=g^{+}
if at least
one of
g,f
is in
\OF_{n}
and
(gf)^{+}\neq g^{+}
otherwise.
Therefore, we obtain:

Lemma 5.14
.

Composition in
\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})

of two non-zero morphisms
C(g),C(f)
with
g^{\ast}=f^{+}
can
be described by

C(g)\cdot C(f)=\begin{cases}C(gf)&f\in\OF_{n}\text{ or }g\in\OF_{n}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Maximal semisimple image

We finish this subsection with an observation on the maximal semisimple
image of the algebra
\Bbbk\Op_{n}
where
\Bbbk
is a field. Let

D_{n}
be the subcategory of
\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})
with the same
set of objects but the morphisms are only morphisms of the form
C(f_{Y,X})

where
f_{Y,X}\in\OF_{n}
. Note that if
f_{W,Z},f_{Y,X}\in\OF_{n}

then
\widetilde{Y}=Y
(since
n\in Y
) and if
Z=Y
then
C(f_{W,Z})\cdot C(f_{Y,X})=C(f_{W,X})

where
f_{W,X}\in\OF_{n}
. Therefore,
D_{n}
is indeed a subcategory
(without zero morphisms) of
\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})
. Moreover, every
morphism
C(f_{Y,X})
in
D_{n}
has an inverse
C(f_{X,Y})
so

D_{n}
is a groupoid. Note also that the identity morphisms
C(e_{X})

(for
X\subseteq[n]
with
n\in X
) are the only endomorphisms in

D_{n}
.

Lemma 5.15
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field. The Jacobson radical of
\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})

is the subvector space spanned by all the morphisms of the form
C(f_{Y,X})

where
f_{Y,X}\notin\OF_{n}
(or equivalently,
n\notin Y)
and

\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})/\Rad(\Bbbk\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n}))\simeq\Bbbk D_{n}.

Proof.

Denote by
R
the subvector space of
\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})

spanned by all the morphisms of the form
C(f_{Y,X})
where
f_{Y,X}\notin\OF_{n}
.
First note that according to
5.14

if
f_{W,Z},f_{Y,X}\notin\OF_{n}
then

C(f_{W,Z})\cdot C(f_{Y,X})=0.

Moreover, if
f_{Y,X}\notin\OF_{n}
and
f_{W,Z}\in\OF_{n}
are
such that
C(f_{W,Z})\cdot C(f_{Y,X})\neq 0
then
f_{W,Z}f_{Y,X}\notin\OF_{n}

according to
5.11
(and the dual holds
if
C(f_{Y,X})\cdot C(f_{W,Z})\neq 0
). Both facts together imply
that
R
is a nilpotent ideal (of nilpotency degree
2
). Since
every non-zero morphism of
\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})
is either in
R

or in
D_{n}
it is clear that
\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})/R\simeq\Bbbk D_{n}
.
It is well-known that the algebra
\Bbbk\mathcal{G}
of a finite
groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is semisimple if and only if the order of
every endomorphism group is invertible in the field
\Bbbk
(
[
29
, Theorem 8.15]
).
In our case, the endomorphism groups of
D_{n}
are trivial so
\Bbbk D_{n}

is semisimple for every field
\Bbbk
. In conclusion,
R
is a nilpotent
ideal such that
\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})/R
is semisimple so

R=\Rad\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})
and the statement follows.
∎

Corollary 5.16
.

For any field
\Bbbk
. The maximal semisimple image of
\Bbbk\Op_{n}

is isomorphic to
\Bbbk\OF_{n}
.

Proof.

According to
5.7
,
\Bbbk\Op_{n}\simeq\Bbbk_{0}\mathcal{C}(\Op_{n})
.
We have just proved that the maximal semisimple image of the last
is
\Bbbk D_{n}
. Finally, it is shown in
[
27
, Section 4.2]

that
\Bbbk D_{n}\simeq\Bbbk\OF_{n}
which finishes the proof.
∎

5.2
Binary relations with demonic composition

Let
\B_{n}
be the set of binary relations on the set
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
It is a monoid under the standard “angelic” composition:

\beta\cdot\alpha=\{(x,y)\mid\exists z\quad(x,z)\in\alpha,\quad(z,y)\in\beta\}

-note that we are composing right to left here. Set
E=\{\id_{A}\mid A\subseteq[n]\}

where
\id_{A}=\{(a,a)\mid a\in A\}
. This is a commutative subsemigroup
of
\B_{n}
. The monoid
\B_{n}
is a reduced
E
-Fountain semigroup
which satisfies both the right and left congruence conditions and

\alpha^{\ast}=\id_{\dom(\alpha)},\quad\alpha^{+}=\id_{\im(\alpha)}

(see
[
4
, Section 3.2]
). We can also define “demonic”
composition:

\beta\ast\alpha=\{(x,y)\in\beta\cdot\alpha\mid(x,z)\in\alpha\implies z\in\dom(\beta)\}.

Denote by
\B_{n}^{d}
the set
\B_{n}
with demonic composition
as operation. It is known that
\B_{n}^{d}
is indeed a monoid. Moreover,
it is a reduced
E
-Fountain semigroup which satisfies the right
congruence condition and the right ample condition (see
[
32
]
).
The right ample condition is a stronger condition than our generalized
right ample condition (
[
26
, Proposition 3.14]
). Since

E
is a commutative subsemigroup we know that
\trianglelefteq_{l}

is a partial order. Set
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}(\B_{n}^{d})

and note that here
\mathcal{C}
is a category by
3.6
.
Therefore, we can apply
3.13
. The objects
of
\mathcal{C}
can be identified with subsets of
[n]
. For every

\alpha\in\B_{n}
there is an associated morphism
C(\alpha)
from

\dom(\alpha)
to
\im(\alpha)
. If
\beta^{\ast}=\alpha^{+}
then

\dom(\beta)=\im(\alpha)
hence
\beta\ast\alpha=\beta\cdot\alpha
.
This shows that for composition of morphisms in this category, the
angelic and demonic definitions coincide.
3.13

implies:

Theorem 5.17
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a commutative unital ring. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the
category whose objects are subsets of
[n]
and the hom-set
\mathcal{C}(X,Y)

(for
X,Y\subseteq[n]
) contains all total onto relations from
X

to
Y
. Then

\Bbbk\B_{n}^{d}\simeq\Bbbk\mathcal{C}

is an isomorphism of
\Bbbk
-algebras.

Remark 5.18
.

Similar examples can be derived from any domain semiring as explained
in
[
32
, Section 6.1]
.

For every relation
\alpha\in\B_{n}
denote by
\alpha^{-1}
the
converse relation
\alpha^{-1}=\{(y,x)\mid(x,y)\in\alpha\}
. It is
clear that we can define
(-)^{-1}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
a contravariant
isomorphism which is identity on objects and
(C(\alpha))^{-1}=C(\alpha^{-1})

on morphisms. Therefore,
\mathcal{C\simeq\mathcal{C^{\op}}}
and
we can deduce that:

Proposition 5.19
.

The algebra
\Bbbk\B_{n}^{d}
is isomorphic to its opposite.

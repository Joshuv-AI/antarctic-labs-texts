Convergence analysis of the stochastic reflected forward-backward splitting algorithm
Nguyen Van Dung, B ă ` \grave{\text{\u{a}}} ng Công Vũ[5mm]

Abstract
We propose and analyze the convergence of a novel stochastic algorithm for solving monotone inclusions that are the sum of a maximal monotone operator and a monotone, Lipschitzian operator. The propose algorithm requires only unbiased estimations of the Lipschitzian operator. We obtain the rate 𝒪 ⁡ ( l ​ o ​ g ​ ( n ) / n ) \mathcal{O}(log(n)/n) in expectation for the strongly monotone case, as well as almost sure convergence for the general case. Furthermore, in the context of application to convex-concave saddle point problems, we derive the rate of the primal-dual gap. In particular, we also obtain 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / n ) \mathcal{O}(1/n) rate convergence of the primal-dual gap in the deterministic setting.

Introduction

A wide class of problems in monotone operator theory, variational inequalities, convex optimization, image processing, machine learning, reduces to the problem of solving monotone inclusions
involving Lipschitzian operators; see
[
2
,
4
,
3
,
5
,
8
,
11
,
22
,
32
,
23
,
33
,
35
]
and the references therein. In this paper, we revisit the generic monotone inclusions of
finding a zero point of the sum of a maximally monotone operator
A
and a monotone,
\mu
-Lipschitzian operator
B
, acting on a real separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, i.e.,

\text{Find $\overline{x}\in\mathcal{H}$ such that}\;0\in(A+B)\overline{x}.

(1.1)

The first splitting method proposed for solving problem was in
[
33
]
which is now known as the forward-backward-forward splitting method (FBFSM). Further investigations of this method lead to a new primal-dual splitting method in
[
4
]
where
B
is a linear monotone skew operator in suitable product spaces.
A main limitation of the FBFSM is their two calls of
B
per iteration. This issue was recently resolved in
[
22
]
in which the forward reflected backward splitting method (FRBSM) was proposed, namely,

\gamma\in\left]0,+\infty\right[,\quad x_{n+1}=(\operatorname{Id}+\gamma A)^{-1}(x_{n}-2\gamma Bx_{n}+\gamma Bx_{n-1}).

(1.2)

An alternative approach to overcome this issue was in
[
11
]
where the reflected forward backward splitting method (RFBSM) was proposed:

\gamma\in\left]0,+\infty\right[,\quad x_{n+1}=(\operatorname{Id}+\gamma A)^{-1}(x_{n}-\gamma B(2x_{n}-x_{n-1})).

(1.3)

It is important to stress that the methods FBFSM, RFBSM and RFBSM are limited to the deterministic setting. The stochastic version of FBFSM was investigated in
[
35
]

and recently in
[
14
]
. Both works
[
35
]
and
[
14
]
requires two
stochastic approximation of
B
. While, a stochastic version of FRBSM was also considered in
[
22
]
for the case when
B
is a finite sum. However, it remains require to evaluate the operator
B
.

The objective of this paper is to avoid these above limitations of
[
35
,
14
,
22
]
by considering the stochastic counterpart of (
1.3
).
At each iteration, we use only one unbiased estimation of
B(2x_{n}-x_{n-1})
and hence the resulting algorithm shares the same structure as the standard stochastic forward-backward splitting
[
7
,
9
,
28
]
. However, it allows to solve a larger class of problems involving non-cocoercive operators.

In Section
2
, we recall the basic notions in convex analysis and monotone operator theory as well as the probability theory,
and establish the results which will be used in the proof of the convergence of the proposed method. We present the proposed method and derive the almost sure convergence, convergence in expectation in Section
3
. In the last section, we will further apply the proposed algorithm to the convex-concave saddle problem involving the infimal convolutions, and establish the rate of the ergodic convergence of the primal-dual gap.

Definition 2.2

We have the following definitions:

(i)

We say that

\big(\forall(x,u)\in\operatorname{gra}A\big)\big(\forall(y,v)\in\operatorname{gra}A\big)\quad\left\langle{x-y}\mid{u-v}\right\rangle\geq 0.

(2.1)

(ii)

(iii)

\big(\forall u\in Ax\big)\big(\forall(y,v)\in\operatorname{gra}A\big)\quad\left\langle{x-y}\mid{u-v}\right\rangle\geq\phi_{A}(\|y-x\|).

(2.2)

If

is
\nu_{A}
-strongly monotone.

(iv)

The resolvent of

(2.3)

where

\mathcal{H}
.

(v)

A single-valued operator

(\forall(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}^{2})\;\left\langle{x-y}\mid{Bx-By}\right\rangle\geq\|Bx-By\|^{2}.

Let

3
Algorithm and convergences

We propose the following algorithm, for solving (
1.1
),
that requires only the unbiased estimations of the monotone,
\mu-
Lipschitzian operators
B
.

Algorithm 3.1

Let
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\left]0,+\infty\right[
. Let

x_{0},x_{-1}
be
\mathcal{H}
-valued, squared integrable random variables. Iterates

(\forall n\in\mathbb{N})\quad\begin{array}[]{l}\left\lfloor\begin{array}[]{l}y_{n}=2x_{n}-x_{n-1}\\
\text{Finding $r_{n}$}:\mathsf{E}[r_{n}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]=By_{n}\\
x_{n+1}=J_{\gamma_{n}A}(x_{n}-\gamma_{n}r_{n}),\end{array}\right.\\[5.69054pt]
\end{array}\\

(3.1)

where
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sigma(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.

Using

\displaystyle\left\langle{r_{n}-Bx}\mid{x_{n+1}-x}\right\rangle

A

\displaystyle\big(G(x_{n+1},v)-G(x,v_{n+1})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\big(\left\langle{v-v_{n+1}}\mid{v_{n+1}-v_{n}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{x_{n}-x_{n+1}}\mid{x_{n+1}-x}\right\rangle\big)+\dfrac{\mu_{h}\gamma_{n}}{2}\|x_{n+1}-y_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ +\gamma_{n}\big(\left\langle{K(x_{n+1}-y_{n})}\mid{v-v_{n+1}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{K(x_{n+1}-x)}\mid{v_{n+1}-u_{n}}\right\rangle\big)+\frac{\mu_{\ell}\gamma_{n}}{2}\|v_{n+1}-u_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ +\gamma_{n}^{2}\big(\|\nabla H(y_{n},\xi_{n})-\nabla h(y_{n})\|^{2}+\|\nabla L(u_{n},\zeta_{n})-\nabla\ell(u_{n})\|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\ \ +\gamma_{n}\big(\left\langle{\nabla h(y_{n})-\nabla H(y_{n},\xi_{n})}\mid{\bar{x}_{n+1}-x}\right\rangle+\left\langle{\nabla\ell(u_{n})-\nabla L(u_{n},\zeta_{n})}\mid{\bar{v}_{n+1}-v}\right\rangle\big).

(4.25)

For simple, set
\mu_{0}=\max\{\mu_{h},\mu_{\ell}\}
and let us define some notations in the space
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{G}
where the scalar product and the associated norm are defined in the normal manner,

\begin{cases}\mathsf{x}&=(x,v),\quad\;\mathsf{x}_{n}=(x_{n},v_{n}),\quad\;\mathsf{y}_{n}=(y_{n},u_{n}),\;\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{n}=(\overline{x}_{n},\overline{v}_{n}),\\
\mathsf{r}_{n}&=(\nabla H(y_{n},\xi_{n}),\nabla L(u_{n},\zeta_{n})),\\
\mathsf{R}_{n}&=(\nabla h(y_{n}),\nabla\ell(u_{n})),\\
\end{cases}

(4.26)

and

S\colon\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{G}\colon(x,v)\mapsto(K^{*}v,-Kx).

(4.27)

Then, one has
\|S\|=\|K\|
and

\displaystyle\left\langle{K(x_{n+1}-y_{n})}\mid{v-v_{n+1}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{K(x_{n+1}-x)}\mid{v_{n+1}-u_{n}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\left\langle{S(\mathsf{x}_{n+1}-\mathsf{x}_{n})}\mid{\mathsf{x}_{n+1}-\mathsf{x}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{S(\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}_{n-1})}\mid{\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{S(\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}_{n-1})}\mid{\mathsf{x}_{n+1}-\mathsf{x}_{n}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\leq d_{n+1}-d_{n}+\frac{\|K\|}{2}\big(\|\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}_{n-1})\|^{2}+\|\mathsf{x}_{n+1}-\mathsf{x}_{n}\|^{2}\big),

(4.28)

where we set
d_{n}=\left\langle{S(\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}_{n-1})}\mid{\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}}\right\rangle
.
Moreover, we also have

\displaystyle\left\langle{v-v_{n+1}}\mid{v_{n+1}-v_{n}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle{x_{n}-x_{n+1}}\mid{x_{n+1}-x}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\mathsf{x}_{n+1}-\mathsf{x}_{n}}\mid{\mathsf{x}-\mathsf{x}_{n+1}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}_{n+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathsf{x}_{n+1}-\mathsf{x}\|^{2}.

(4.29)

Furthermore, using the triangle inequality, we obtain

\dfrac{\mu_{h}\gamma_{n}}{2}\|x_{n+1}-y_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{\mu_{\ell}\gamma_{n}}{2}\|v_{n+1}-u_{n}\|^{2}\leq\gamma_{n}\mu_{0}\big(\|\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}_{n+1}\|^{2}+\|\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}_{n-1}\|^{2}\big).

(4.30)

Finally, we can rewrite the two last terms in (
4.25
) as

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}^{2}\big(\|\nabla H(y_{n},\xi_{n})-\nabla h(y_{n})\|^{2}+\|\nabla L(u_{n},\zeta_{n})-\nabla\ell(u_{n})\|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\ \ +\gamma_{n}\big(\left\langle{\nabla h(y_{n})-\nabla H(y_{n},\xi_{n})}\mid{\bar{x}_{n+1}-x}\right\rangle+\left\langle{\nabla\ell(u_{n})-\nabla L(u_{n},\zeta_{n})}\mid{\bar{v}_{n+1}-v}\right\rangle\big)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n}^{2}\|\mathsf{r}_{n}-\mathsf{R}_{n}\|^{2}+\gamma_{n}\left\langle{\mathsf{r}_{n}-\mathsf{R}_{n}}\mid{\mathsf{x}-\bar{\mathsf{x}}_{n+1}}\right\rangle

(4.31)

Therefore, inserting (
4.28
), (
4.29
), (
4.30
) and (
4.31
) into
(
4.25
) and rearranging, we get

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}\big(G(x_{n+1},v)-

\displaystyle G(x,v_{n+1})\big)\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathsf{x}_{n+1}-\mathsf{x}\|^{2}+\gamma_{n}d_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}d_{n}

\displaystyle-(\frac{1}{2}-\gamma_{n}\mu_{0}-\frac{\gamma_{n}\|K\|}{2})\|\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}_{n+1}\|^{2}+(\gamma_{n}\mu_{0}+\frac{\gamma_{n}\|K\|}{2})\|\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}_{n-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\gamma_{n}^{2}\|\mathsf{r}_{n}-\mathsf{R}_{n}\|^{2}+\gamma_{n}\left\langle{\mathsf{r}_{n}-\mathsf{R}_{n}}\mid{\mathsf{x}-\bar{\mathsf{x}}_{n+1}}\right\rangle.

(4.32)

Let us set

b_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}\|^{2}+(\gamma_{n}\mu_{0}+\frac{\gamma_{n}\|K\|}{2})\|\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}_{n-1}\|^{2}-\gamma_{n}d_{n}.

(4.33)

We have

\displaystyle|\gamma_{n}d_{n}|\leq\gamma_{n}\|K\|\|\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}\|\|\mathsf{x_{n}}-\mathsf{x}_{n-1}\|\leq\frac{\gamma_{n}\|K\|}{2}\big(\|\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}\|^{2}+\|\mathsf{x_{n}}-\mathsf{x}_{n-1}\|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow b_{n}\geq 0\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}

Then, we can rewrite (
4.32
) as

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}\big(G(x_{n+1},v)-

\displaystyle G(x,v_{n+1})\big)\leq b_{n}-b_{n+1}-(\frac{1}{2}-2\gamma_{n}\mu_{0}-\frac{2\gamma_{n}\|K\|}{2})\|\mathsf{x}_{n}-\mathsf{x}_{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+(\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{n+1})d_{n+1}+\gamma_{n}^{2}\|\mathsf{r}_{n}-\mathsf{R}_{n}\|^{2}+\gamma_{n}\left\langle{\mathsf{r}_{n}-\mathsf{R}_{n}}\mid{\mathsf{x}-\bar{\mathsf{x}}_{n+1}}\right\rangle.

(4.34)

Now, using our assumption, since
\overline{\mathsf{x}}_{n+1}
is independent of
(\xi_{n},\zeta_{n})
, we have

\mathsf{E}\left[\left\langle{\mathsf{r}_{n}-\mathsf{R}_{n}}\mid{\mathsf{x}-\bar{\mathsf{x}}_{n+1}}\right\rangle|(\xi_{0},\zeta_{0}),\ldots(\xi_{n-1},\zeta_{n-1})\right]=0.

(4.35)

Moreover, the condition on the learning rate gives

\frac{1}{2}-2\gamma_{n}\mu_{0}-\gamma_{n}\|K\|\geq 0\ \text{and}\;\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{n+1}\geq 0.

(4.36)

Therefore, taking expectation both sides of (
4.34
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}[\gamma_{n}\big(G(x_{n+1},v)-

\displaystyle G(x,v_{n+1})\big)]\leq\mathsf{E}\left[b_{n}\right]-\mathsf{E}\left[b_{n+1}\right]+(\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{n+1})c(x,v)+\gamma_{n}^{2}\mathsf{E}\left[\|\mathsf{r}_{n}-\mathsf{R}_{n}\|^{2}\right].

(4.37)

Now, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
summing (
4.34
) from
n=0
to
n=N
and invoking the convexity-concavity of
G
, we arrive at the desired result.

Remark 4.4

Here are some remarks.

(i)

To the best of our knowledge, this is first work establishing the rate convergence of the primal-dual gap for structure convex optimization involving infimal convolutions.

(ii)

The results presented in this Section are new even in the deterministic setting. In this case, by setting

, our results share the same rate convergence
\mathcal{O}(1/N)
of the primal-dual gap as in

17
]

[
30
]
under the same conditions on

and variances as in (
4.7
). However, the work in
[
30
]
are limited to the case

is a constant function.

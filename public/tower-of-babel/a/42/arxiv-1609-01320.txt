Introduction

Itô formula for the square of the norm is an essential tool
in the study of stochastic evolution equations
of the type

dv(t)=\mathbb{A}(t,v(t))\,dt+\sum_{k}\mathbb{B}_{k}(t,v(t))\,dW^{k}(t),

(1.1)

where
(W^{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of independent
Wiener processes, and
\mathbb{A}(t,\cdot)
and
\mathbb{B}_{k}(t,\cdot)

are (possibly random nonlinear) operators on a separable
real Banach space
V
, with values
in a Banach space
V^{\prime}
and a Hilbert space
H
respectively,
such that
V\hookrightarrow H\hookrightarrow V^{\prime}
with continuous and
dense embeddings.
We assume there is a constant
K

such that
(v,h)\leq K\|v\|_{V}\|h\|_{V^{\prime}}
for all
v\in V

and
h\in H
. This means that for the linear mapping
\Psi:H\to H^{\ast}
, which identifies

H
with its dual
H^{\ast}
via the inner product in
H
,
we have
\|\Psi(h)\|_{V^{\ast}}\leq K\|h\|_{V^{\prime}}
.
Therefore, since
H
is dense in
V^{\prime}
,
\Psi

can be extended to a continuous mapping from
V^{\prime}
into
V^{\ast}
,
the dual of
V
.
It is assumed that this extension
is one-to-one from
V^{\prime}
into
V^{\ast}
.
Thus an initial value problem for
equation (
1.1
)
can be viewed
as

v(t)=\int_{0}^{t}v^{\ast}(s)\,ds+h(t)=:y(t)

(1.2)

with the
V^{\ast}
-valued process
v^{\ast}(t):=\mathbb{A}(t,v(t))
and

H\equiv H^{\ast}
-valued process

h(t):=h_{0}+\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{B}^{k}(s,v(s))\,dW^{k}(s),

where
h_{0}
is a given initial value and the equality (
1.2
)
in
V^{\ast}

is required

almost everywhere.
In the special case
B_{k}=0
for every

, and nonrandom

and
A
, i.e.,
in the case

v(t)=h_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}v^{\ast}(s)\,ds,\quad dt\text{-a.e.},

it is well-known that when

,

for
T>0
and conjugate exponents

and

, then there is

such that
u=v
for

-almost
all

and the “energy equality”

|u(t)|_{H}^{2}=|h_{0}|_{H}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle v^{\ast}(s),v(s)\rangle\,ds

holds for all

, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the

and

.
This formula is used in proofs of existence and uniqueness theorems for PDEs,
see e.g.,
Evans
and
Lions
. A generalisation of it,
a “stochastic energy equality”, i.e.,
an Itô formula for the square of the
H
-norm of

, was first presented in Pardoux

,
and was used to obtain existence and uniqueness theorems for SPDEs.
The proof of it in

was not separated
from the theory of SPDEs developed there. A proof, not bound
to the theory of SPDEs,
was given in Krylov and Rozovskii

,
and then this stochastic energy equality

to
V^{\ast}
-valued semimartingales

of the form

(1.3)

where

in Gyöngy

to extend the theory of SPDEs developed in

and

In the present paper we are interested in stochastic energy equalities
which can be applied to SPDEs (

In the special case when

We note that in the context of stochastic evolution
equations it is possible to prove Itô formulae for more general functions
(satisfying appropriate differentiability assumptions),
see again Pardoux
pardoux:thesis
,
Krylov
krylov:ito_fla
,
K2010
,

The Itô formula for the square of the norm is

The more general Itô formula can then be used to study finer properties

Main Results

For

let
(V_{i},\|\cdot\|_{V_{i}})
be

(V_{i}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{V_{i}^{*}})

Let

denote the vector space
V_{1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{m}

with the norm
\|\cdot\|:=\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}+\cdots+\|\cdot\|_{V_{m}}
.
Then clearly,
V
is a Banach space. Assume that it is separable and
is continuously and densely
embedded in a Hilbert space
(H,|\cdot|)
, which is identified with its dual
H^{\ast}

by the help of the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
in
H

V\hookrightarrow H\equiv H^{\ast}\hookrightarrow V^{\ast},

where
H^{\ast}\hookrightarrow V^{\ast}
is the adjoint of the embedding

V\hookrightarrow H
.
We use the notation

for the duality pairing between

and
V^{\ast}
.
Note that if

for some

belongs
to
V^{\ast}
and
|\langle v^{\ast},v\rangle|\leq\|v^{\ast}\|_{V^{\ast}_{i}}\|v\|_{V_{i}}

for all
v\in V
. Note also that
\langle v^{\ast},v\rangle=(h,v)
for
for all
v\in V
when
v^{\ast}=h\in H
.

A complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})

together with an increasing family of
\sigma
-algebras

(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
,
\mathcal{F}_{t}\subset\mathcal{F}
will be used throughout the paper.
Moreover it is assumed that the usual conditions are satisfied:

\bigcap_{s>t}\mathcal{F}_{s}=\mathcal{F}_{t}
and
\mathcal{F}_{0}
contains all subsets of
\mathbb{P}
-null sets of
\mathcal{F}
.
We use the notation
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for the
\sigma
-algebra of Borel subsets of

, and for a real-valued increasing
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\otimes\mathcal{F}
-measurable

the notation

stands for the measure

by

(dA\times\mathbb{P})(F)=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\bf 1}_{F}\,dA(t),\quad F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\otimes\mathcal{F}.

Let
h=(h(t))_{t\geq 0}
be an

.
Its quadratic variation process is denoted by

Furthermore let

Finally let

-valued progressively measurable process
and for

let

be

-valued processes
such that
\langle\varphi,v_{i}^{\ast}\rangle
are progressively measurable for any

\varphi\in V
. Notice that

is also progressively measurable as a process
with values in
\bar{V}_{i}
, the closure in
V_{i}
-norm of the linear hull of

\{v(t):t\geq 0,\omega\in\Omega\}.

Let there be
p_{i}\in[1,\infty)
and
q_{i}=p_{i}/(p_{i}-1)\in(1,\infty]
,
where, as usual,

.
Assume that for each

and

\int_{0}^{T}\|v(t)\|_{V_{i}}^{p_{i}}\,dA(t)<\infty,\quad\left(\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{i}^{q_{i}}(t)\,dA(t)\right)^{1/q_{i}}<\infty,

for some progressively measurable process

Theorem 2.1

Let
\tau
be a stopping time.
Suppose that for all
\varphi\in V
and for
dA\times\mathbb{P}

almost all
(\omega,t)
such that
t\in(0,\tau(\omega))
we have

(v(t),\varphi)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{(0,t]}\langle v^{\ast}_{i}(s),\varphi\rangle\,dA(s)+(h(t),\varphi).

(2.2)

Then there is
\tilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega

with
\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\Omega})=1
and
an
H

\tilde{v}
such that
the following statements hold.

(i)

For

almost all

satisfying

we have
\tilde{v}=v
.

(ii)

For all
\omega\in\tilde{\Omega}
and

we have

(\tilde{v}(t),\varphi)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{(0,t]}\langle v^{\ast}_{i}(s),\varphi\rangle\,dA(s)+h(t)\varphi\quad\textrm{for all }\,\varphi\in V.

(2.3)

(iii)

For all
\omega\in\tilde{\Omega}
and
t\in[0,\tau(\omega))

\begin{split}|\tilde{v}(t)|^{2}=&|h(0)|^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{(0,t]}\langle v^{\ast}_{i}(s),v(s)\rangle\,dA(s)+2\int_{(0,t]}(\tilde{v}(s-)\,dh(s))\\
&-\int_{(0,t]}|v^{\ast}(s)|^{2}\Delta A(s)dA(s)+[h]_{t},\end{split}

(2.4)

where
v^{\ast}(t):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}v^{\ast}_{i}(t)\in H

for
\Delta A(t)>0
.

Consider now a situation where the assumptions on

h
and
A
are as above but

and regarding

and
v^{\ast}:=v^{\ast}_{1}

we know that
\|v(t)\|
,

.
Let

\bar{v}^{\ast}(t):=\frac{v^{\ast}(t)}{1+\|v^{\ast}(t)\|_{V^{*}}}\,\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,\,\bar{A}(t):=\int_{(0,t]}(1+\|v^{\ast}(t)\|_{V^{*}})dA(t).

Then

and so

\bar{v}^{\ast}

and
\bar{A}
satisfy the conditions
on

,

and

and

.
If (

) holds

and for

Applying Theorem

then means that we have all of its

&-\int_{(0,t]}\left|\bar{v}^{\ast}(s)\right|^{2}\Delta\bar{A}(s)d\bar{A}(s)+[h]_{t}\\
=&|h(0)|^{2}+2\int_{(0,t]}\langle v^{\ast}(s),v(s)\rangle\,dA(s)+2\int_{(0,t]}(\tilde{v}(s-),dh(s))\\

Remark 1
.

One might think that Theorem
2.1

follows from the main theorem
in
gyongy:krylov:on:stochastic:II
by considering the process

v^{\ast}=\sum_{i}v^{\ast}_{i}
as a process with values in
V^{\ast}
.
However, taking into account
that for any
w^{\ast}\in V^{\ast}

\|w^{\ast}\|_{V^{\ast}}=\inf\Big\{\max_{i=1,\ldots,m}\|w_{i}^{\ast}\|_{V^{\ast}_{i}}:w^{\ast}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}^{\ast},w_{i}^{\ast}\in V_{i}^{\ast}\Big\}

(see for example Gajewski,
Gröger and Zacharias
(
ggz
, Chapter 1, Theorem 5.13)
),
one can show that the local integrability condition in
gyongy:krylov:on:stochastic:II

for

\|v\|_{V}\|v^{\ast}\|_{V^{\ast}}=(\|v\|_{1}+\cdots+\|v\|_{m})\|v^{\ast}\|_{V^{\ast}}

is not implied by our assumption (
2.1
).
Thus the main theorem in
gyongy:krylov:on:stochastic:II

is not applicable in our situation.

We consider the following motivating example.

Example 1

Consider the stochastic partial differential equation

\begin{split}du=&\left[\nabla(|\nabla u|^{p_{1}-2}\nabla u)+|u|^{p_{2}-2}u\right]dt\\
&+f(u,\nabla u)\,dW+\int_{Z}g(u)\,q(dt,dz)\,\,\textrm{in}\,\,\mathscr{D}\times(0,T).\end{split}

Here
W
is a Wiener process (finite or infinite dimensional depending
on the choice of
f
),

(Z,\Sigma)
is a measurable space and
q(ds,dz)

a stochastic martingale measure on
[0,\infty)\times Z
.
See, for example, Gyöngy and Krylov
gyongy:krylov:on:stochastic:I

for detailed definition.
We take
\mathscr{D}
to be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\R^{d}
.

It is natural to assume that a solution
u
should be such that

\|u\|_{W^{1}_{p_{1}}(\mathscr{D})}^{p_{1}}

and

\|u\|_{L_{p_{2}}(\mathscr{D})}^{p_{2}}
are almost surely
locally integrable.
To apply the result in
Gyöngy and Krylov
gyongy:krylov:on:stochastic:II

one could try to take

V:=W^{1}_{p_{1}}(\mathscr{D})\cap L_{p_{2}}(\mathscr{D})

with the norm

\|\cdot\|_{V}=\|\cdot\|_{W^{1}_{p_{1}}(\mathscr{D})}+\|\cdot\|_{L_{p_{2}}(\mathscr{D})}
.
The dual of
V
can be identified with the linear space

V^{*}=\{f=f_{1}+f_{2}:f_{1}\in W^{1}_{p_{1}}(\mathscr{D})^{*},f_{2}\in L_{p_{2}}(\mathscr{D})^{*}\}

equipped with the norm

\begin{split}\|f\|_{V*}=\inf\{\max(&\|f_{1}\|_{W^{1}_{p_{1}}(\mathscr{D})^{*}},\|f_{2}\|_{L_{p_{2}}(\mathscr{D})^{*}}):\\
&f=f_{1}+f_{2},f_{1}\in W^{1}_{p_{1}}(\mathscr{D})^{*},\,\,f_{2}\in L_{p_{2}}(\mathscr{D})^{*}\}.\end{split}

One would then need to show that

\|u\|_{V}\,\|\nabla(|\nabla u|^{p_{1}-2}\nabla u)+|u|^{p_{2}-2}u\|_{V^{*}}

is locally integrable.
To ensure this in general we need, in particular, that

\|u\|_{W^{1}_{p_{1}}(\mathscr{D})}\,\||u|^{p_{2}-2}u\|_{L_{p_{2}}(\mathscr{D})^{*}}=\|u\|_{W^{1}_{p_{1}}(\mathscr{D})}\,\|u\|^{p_{2}-1}_{L_{p_{2}}(\mathscr{D})}

is locally integrable, which we may not have
if
p_{1}<p_{2}
.
Thus one cannot apply the Itô formula from Gyöngy and Krylov.
On the other hand it is easy to check that the assumptions of
Theorem
2.1
are satisfied.

An application of the above Itô’s formula to SPDEs driven by
Wiener processes is given in

(Chapter 2,
Example 5.1) and in

(

Proof.

Note that by the shift invariance of the Lebesgue measure

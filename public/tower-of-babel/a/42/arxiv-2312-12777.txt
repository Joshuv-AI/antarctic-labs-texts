Octonionic Dirac operators in bounded lattices: Convergence and Plemelj projections Thanks: This work was supported by the NNSF of China (12171448).
Guangbin Ren, Xin Zhao

Abstract
. This article explores octonionic analysis on the lattice ℤ 8 \mathbb{Z}^{8} , emphasizing the octonionic discrete Cauchy integral within a bounded domain, the Sokhotski-Plemelj jump formulas, and the convergence of discrete regular functions. We address the challenge of articulating the discrete Cauchy-Pompeiu integral formula, particularly the complexities tied to the associator of octonions. Adopting an innovative approach, we assimilate complicating terms into integral kernels, leading to distinct surface and volume kernels. A new ’star product’ arises in handling octonionic function multiplications. Our study connects closely with the Teodorescu operator and singular kernel management, utilizing a Fourier variant with 24 unique singular points. Drawing from the foundational work of Shivakumar and Wong about the asymptotic expansion of the Fourier transform, we bridge the relationship between the Cauchy and Teodorescu integrals. This allows us to provide quantitative estimates for these kernels, which prove to be pivotal in the theory of the discrete regular extensions. The research culminates in the revelation that a continuous octonionic function is regular precisely when it is a scale limit of discrete regular functions.

2.1.
Octonion Algebra

The octonion algebra, denoted as
\mathbb{O}
, stands as the largest composition algebra, as confirmed by Hurwitz’s Theorem
[
2
]
. Representing an eight-dimensional
\mathbb{R}
-space, it is constructed on the standard basis:

\bm{e}_{0}=1,\bm{e}_{1},\cdots,\bm{e}_{7}

and adheres to the relations:

\bm{e}_{i}\bm{e}_{j}=\begin{cases}\bm{e}_{j},\quad&i=0,\\
\bm{e}_{i},\quad&j=0,\\
-\delta_{i,j}+\varepsilon_{ijk}\bm{e}_{k},&otherwise,\end{cases}

where
\delta_{i,j}
symbolizes the Kronecker delta, and
\varepsilon_{ijk}
is an entirely antisymmetric tensor that equals 1 when the sequence
ijk
is one of:

123,145,176,246,257,347,365.

The octonions, by nature, are non-commutative and non-associative, as evidenced by the relation:

(\bm{e}_{1}\bm{e}_{2})\bm{e}_{4}=-\bm{e}_{1}(\bm{e}_{2}\bm{e}_{4}).

Further, the relationship can be generalized as:

(2.1)

(\bm{e}_{i}\bm{e}_{j})\bm{e}_{k}=\begin{cases}\bm{e}_{i}(\bm{e}_{j}\bm{e}_{k}),&k=0,i,j,i\oplus j,\\
\\
-\bm{e}_{i}(\bm{e}_{j}\bm{e}_{k}),&otherwise.\end{cases}

Here
i\oplus j
is the standard basis in
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
; see
[
28
]
.

Definition 1
.

The associator for any elements
\bm{a,b,c}\in\mathbb{O}
is defined as

\bm{[a,b,c]:=(ab)c-a(bc).}

Lemma 1

(
[

,
2
]
)

The octonion algebra
\mathbb{O}
exhibits both alternativity and flexibility, implying that

\bm{[a,a,b]=[b,a,a]}=0

and

\bm{[a,b,a]}=0

for any elements

Additionally, the octonions satisfy the Moufang identities:

\bm{(aba)c=a(b(ac)),\hskip 11.49994ptc(aba)=((ca)b)a,\hskip 11.49994pta(bc)a=(ab)(ca).}

Definition 2
.

Given a subset

Here, the forward and backward difference operators,

\displaystyle\partial_{l}^{+,h}\bm{f}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}[\bm{f}(x+he_{l})-\bm{f}(x)],

\displaystyle\partial_{l}^{-,h}\bm{f}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}[\bm{f}(x)-\bm{f}(x-he_{l})]

for any function
\bm{f}
in

We also consider different types of discrete boundaries, opting for a two-layered boundary in this context.

Definition 3

For a subset

\partial B=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}|N(x)\cap B\neq\emptyset,N(x)\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\setminus B)\neq\emptyset\}

We can also define the discrete closure

\overline{B}=B\cup\partial B,\quad B^{\circ}=B\setminus\partial B.

In this study, we represent the set of functions

A significant challenge in the discrete Stokes theorem is the complex nature of the discrete derivative formula under multiplication, especially when contrasted against the continuous scenario. To navigate this complexity, we introduce specific shift operators to assimilate the extra terms.

Lemma 3

(Leibniz rule I)
.

Let
B

\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}.
Then for any

\displaystyle\partial^{+,h}_{l}(\bm{f}\bm{g})

\displaystyle=\partial_{l}^{+,h}\bm{f}\bm{g}+\tau_{-he_{l}}\bm{f}\partial_{l}^{+,h}\bm{g},

\displaystyle\partial^{+,h}_{l}(\bm{f}\bm{g})

\displaystyle=\partial^{+,h}_{l}\bm{f}\tau_{-he_{l}}\bm{g}+\bm{f}\partial_{l}^{+,h}\bm{g},

\displaystyle\partial^{-,h}_{l}(\bm{f}\bm{g})

\displaystyle=\partial_{l}^{-,h}\bm{f}\bm{g}+\tau_{he_{l}}\bm{f}\partial_{l}^{-,h}\bm{g},

\displaystyle\partial^{-,h}_{l}(\bm{f}\bm{g})

\displaystyle=\partial_{l}^{-,h}\bm{f}\tau_{he_{l}}\bm{g}+\bm{f}\partial_{i}^{-,h}\bm{g}.

Proof.

For the first two equalities,
by definition we have

\displaystyle\partial_{l}^{+,h}(\bm{f}\bm{g})(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}(\bm{f}(x+he_{l})\bm{g}(x+he_{l})-\bm{f}(x)\bm{g}(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}(\bm{f}(x+he_{l})\bm{g}(x+he_{l})-\bm{f}(x+he_{l})\bm{g}(x)+\bm{f}(x+he_{l})\bm{g}(x)-\bm{f}(x)\bm{g}(x))

\displaystyle=\tau_{-he_{l}}\bm{f}(x)\partial^{+,h}_{l}\bm{g}(x)+\partial_{l}^{+,h}\bm{f}(x)\bm{g}(x),

and

\displaystyle\partial_{l}^{+,h}(\bm{f}\bm{g})(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}(\bm{f}(x+he_{l})\bm{g}(x+he_{l})-\bm{f}(x)\bm{g}(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}(\bm{f}(x+he_{l})\bm{g}(x+he_{l})-\bm{f}(x)\bm{g}(x+he_{l})+\bm{f}(x)\bm{g}(x+he_{l})-\bm{f}(x)\bm{g}(x))

\displaystyle=\partial_{l}^{+,h}\bm{f}(x)\bm{g}(x+he_{l})+\bm{f}(x)\partial_{l}^{+,h}\bm{g}(x).

The proofs for the remaining two equalities follow a similar approach.
∎

To unify the variables of functions on the right side of (

(3.2)

\displaystyle\partial^{+,h}_{l}(\bm{f}\bm{g})

3.2.
Discrete Stokes theorem

In this subsection, we will employ the discrete derivative formula in the context of multiplication to formulate a discrete Stokes theorem without the supplementary term. This is in alignment with the methodology delineated in
[
29
,
30
]
. As highlighted in
[
29

30
]

(3.4)

\begin{cases}n_{l}dS=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}\chi_{B}dV,\\
\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}n_{l}^{2}=\chi_{B}.\end{cases}

Here,

Subsequently, we derive a discrete counterpart of this theorem. Throughout this paper,

\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}

\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}}\bm{f}dV^{h}=\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}}\bm{f}(x)h^{8}.

Given a set

.

Let
B

\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}

\begin{cases}n_{l}^{\pm}dS=-\partial_{l}^{\pm,h}\chi_{B}dV^{h},\\

Proof.

We commence by proving the first equation in (

Suppose

\displaystyle n_{l}^{\pm}(x)dS(x)

\displaystyle=n_{l}^{\pm}(x)s(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\partial_{l}^{\pm,h}\chi_{B}(x)}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=0}^{7}\left((\partial_{l}^{+,h}\chi_{B})^{2}+(\partial_{l}^{-,h}\chi_{B})^{2}\right)}}\cdot\frac{h^{8}}{2}\sqrt{\sum_{i=0}^{7}\left((\partial_{l}^{+,h}\chi_{B})^{2}+(\partial_{l}^{-,h}\chi_{B})^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\partial_{l}^{-,h}\bm{f}\tau_{he_{l}}\bm{g}+\bm{f}\partial_{i}^{-,h}\bm{g}dV^{h},

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B}\bm{f}n_{l}^{-}\bm{g}dS

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\partial_{l}^{-,h}\bm{f}\bm{g}+\tau_{he_{l}}\bm{f}\partial_{l}^{-,h}\bm{g}dV^{h},

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B}\bm{f}n_{l}^{-}\bm{g}dS

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\partial^{+,h}l\bm{f}\tau{-he_{l}}\bm{g}+\bm{f}\partial_{l}^{+,h}\bm{g}dV^{h}.

Proof.

By Theorem
1
, we have

(3.12)

\int_{\partial B}\bm{f}n_{l}^{+}\bm{g}dS=\int_{B}\partial^{-,h}l(\bm{f}\bm{g})dV^{h}.

From relation (
3.1
), it is evident that:

(3.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{l}^{-,h}(\bm{f}\bm{g})

\displaystyle=\partial_{l}^{-}\bm{f}\bm{g}+\tau{he_{l}}\bm{f}\partial_{i,h}^{-}\bm{g},

\displaystyle\partial_{l}^{-,h}(\bm{f}\bm{g})

\displaystyle=\partial_{l}^{-,h}\bm{f}\tau_{he_{l}}\bm{g}+\bm{f}\partial_{i}^{-,h}\bm{g}.

Incorporating results from (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we derive:

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B}\bm{f}n_{l}^{+}\bm{g}dS

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\partial_{l}^{-,h}\bm{f}\bm{g}+\tau_{he_{l}}\bm{f}\partial_{l}^{-,h}\bm{g}dV^{h},

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B}\bm{f}n_{l}^{+}\bm{g}dS

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\partial_{l}^{-,h}\bm{f}\tau_{he_{l}}\bm{g}+\bm{f}\partial_{i}^{-,h}\bm{g}dV^{h}.

The proof of the remaining relationships in (
3.11
) follows a similar methodology.
∎

4.
The Cauchy-Pompeiu Formula and Its Inverse on
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}

In this section, our focus lies on understanding the Cauchy-Pompeiu formula and its inverse in the realm of discrete octonionic analysis.

For this purpose, we utilize the following relation:

(\bm{e}_{i}\bm{e}_{j})\bm{e}_{k}=\begin{cases}\bm{e}_{i}(\bm{e}_{j}\bm{e}_{k}),&\text{when }k=0,i,j,i\oplus j,\\
\\
-\bm{e}_{i}(\bm{e}_{j}\bm{e}_{k}),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

This equation stems from (
2.1
). Note that
\oplus
represents the standard addition in
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
.
It is noteworthy that, in general,

(\bm{e}_{i}\bm{e}_{j})\bm{e}_{k}\neq-\bm{e}_{i}(\bm{e}_{j}\bm{e}_{k}).

Definition 8
.

The discrete Laplacian
\Delta^{h}:M(\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h},\mathbb{O})\longrightarrow M(\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h},\mathbb{O})
is defined by

\Delta^{h}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{7}\partial_{l}^{h}\partial_{l}^{h}.

For
h=1
, we have

\Delta^{1}\bm{f}(x)=\frac{1}{4}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{7}(\bm{f}(x+2e_{i})+\bm{f}(x-2e_{i})-2\bm{f}(x))

for any
\bm{f}\in M(\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h},\mathbb{O}).

The fundamental solution of
\Delta^{1}
is given by

(4.1)

F^{1}(x)=-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{8}}\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^{8}}{\left(\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\sin^{2}u_{l}\right)^{-1}}e^{i\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}u_{l}x_{l}}du,

such that

(4.2)

\Delta^{1}F^{1}(x)=\delta^{1}_{0}(x)

where

\delta_{0}^{1}(x)
is the discrete Dirac delta function defined by

\delta_{0}^{1}(x)=\begin{cases}1,\quad x=0,\\
\\
0,\quad x\neq 0.\end{cases}

Indeed, it can be shown that

\Delta^{1}\left(e^{i\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}u_{l}x_{l}}\right)=\left(-\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\sin^{2}u_{l}\right)e^{i\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}u_{l}x_{l}},

which leads to

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Delta^{1}F^{1}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{8}}\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^{8}}\left(\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\sin^{2}u_{l}\right)^{-1}\left(-\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\sin^{2}u_{l}\right)e^{i\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}u_{l}x_{l}}du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{8}}\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^{8}}e^{i\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}u_{l}x_{l}}du

\displaystyle=\delta_{0}^{1}(x).

Definition 9
.

A function
\bm{E}^{h}\in M(\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h},\mathbb{O})
is called the fundamental solution of
\bm{D}^{h}
if

(4.4)

\bm{D}^{h}\bm{E}^{h}=\delta_{0}^{h}(x)

in a point-wise sense,
where
\delta_{0}^{h}(x)
is the discrete Dirac delta function defined by

\delta_{0}^{h}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{h^{8}},\quad x=0,\\
\\
0,\quad x\neq 0\end{cases}.

Notably, (
4.4
) holds in a point-wise sense, leading to

(4.5)

\delta^{h}_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{h^{8}}\chi_{\{0\}}(x).

This contrasts with the continuous case.

Lemma 6
.

The fundamental solution
\bm{E}^{h}(x)
of
\bm{D}^{h}
satisfies

\bm{E}^{h}(x)=\frac{1}{h^{7}}\bm{E}^{1}(\frac{x}{h}),

where

(4.6)

\bm{E}^{1}(x):=-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{8}}\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^{8}}i\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\bm{\overline{e}}_{l}\sin(u_{l}){\left(\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\sin^{2}(u_{l})\right)^{-1}}e^{i\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}u_{l}x_{l}}du.

Proof.

We may assume
h=1.
Define the discrete octonionic Cauchy-Fueter operator
\bm{D}^{1}
as

\bm{D}^{1}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{7}\bm{e}_{i}\partial_{l},

and
\bm{\overline{D}}^{1}
and its conjugate as

\bm{\overline{D}}^{1}=\bm{e}_{0}\partial_{0}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{7}\bm{e}_{i}\partial_{l}.

A direct calculation yields

(4.7)

\Delta^{1}=\bm{D}^{1}\bm{\overline{D}^{1}}.

With the definitions of
E^{1}
,
F
, and noting that

\bm{\overline{D}}^{1}\Big(e^{i\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}u_{l}x_{l}}\Big)=e^{i\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}u_{l}x_{l}}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}i\bm{\overline{e}_{l}}\sin(u_{l}),

we deduce

(4.8)

\bm{\overline{D}^{1}}F^{1}=\bm{E}^{1}.

From (
4.2
),(
4.7
), it follows that

\bm{D}^{1}\bm{E}^{1}=\bm{D}^{1}\bm{\overline{D}^{1}}F^{1}=\Delta^{1}F^{1}=\delta_{0}^{1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 7
.

The fundamental solution
\bm{E}^{1}
is an element of
L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{8}).

Proof.

Recall

\bm{E}^{1}(x):=-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{8}}\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^{8}}i\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\bm{\overline{e}}_{l}\sin(u_{l}){\left(\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\sin^{2}(u_{l})\right)^{-1}}e^{i\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}u_{l}x_{l}}du.

It implies
\bm{E}^{1}
is the Fourier transform of

i\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\bm{\overline{e}}_{l}\sin(u_{l}){\left(\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\sin^{2}(u_{l})\right)^{-1}}.

We claim that this function is dominated by

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}(u_{l}-s_{l})^{2}}}

near any of its singular points
(s_{0},s_{1},\cdots,s_{7}).

Indeed, by periodicity, we only need to consider the singular point
(0,0,0,0)
, where

\left\|i\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\bm{\overline{e}}_{l}\sin(u_{l}){\left(\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\sin^{2}(u_{l})\right)^{-1}}\right\|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\sin^{2}u_{l}}}\thicksim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}u_{l}^{2}}}.

Thus it belongs to
L^{2}([-\pi,\pi]^{4})
and by Parseval’s identity we have
\bm{E}^{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}).

∎

Remark 3
.

The integration expression of
\bm{E}^{1}
from (
4.6
) shows that
x=0
is not a singular point of
\bm{E}^{1}
, contrasting the continuous case where

\bm{E}(x)=\frac{1}{\omega_{7}}\frac{\bm{\bar{x}}}{|\bm{x}|^{8}}.

Here
\omega_{7}=\frac{2\pi^{4}}{\Gamma(4)}
is the surface area of the unit sphere
S^{7}
.

Here’s a mathematically formatted version:

In the discrete context, the boundary integral kernel of
\bm{D}^{h}

differs from its continuous counterpart. Specifically, while the continuous version is characterized by the fundamental solution
\bm{E}^{1}

the discrete form is described by

(4.9)

\bm{K}^{h}(x,y):=-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{7}\left(\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{-}(x)+\bm{E}^{h}(-he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{+}(x)\right)\bm{e}_{l}.

Given the octonionic nature of the terms within this kernel, multiplying
\bm{K}^{h}(x,y)
by an octonionic function, say

\bm{f}(y)
, presents challenges due to the non-associativity of the octonions. For our purposes, we adopt a specific multiplication method that respects this non-associativity:

(4.10)

\bm{K}^{h}(x,y)*\bm{f}(x):=-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\left(\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{-}(x)+\bm{E}^{h}(-he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{+}(x)\right)(\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x))

By using this approach, we can develop a Cauchy-Pompeiu formula for

\bm{D}^{h}

that mirrors its continuous counterpart in form, but compensates with unique boundary and volume integral kernels.

Theorem 3

(Cauchy-Pompeiu formula)
.

Let
B
be a bounded set in
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}.
Then for any function
\bm{f}:\overline{B}\longrightarrow\mathbb{O},
we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle\chi_{B}(y)\bm{f}(y)=\int_{\partial B}\bm{K}^{h}(x,y)*\bm{f}(x)dS(x)+\int_{B}\bm{E}^{h}(y-x)(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f})(x)dV^{h}(x).

Proof.

Employing the discrete divergence theorem on the boundary integral, we get

(4.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B}\bm{K}^{h}(x,y)*\bm{f}(x)dS(x)=\int_{B}G^{h}(x,y)dV^{h}(x),

where the function
G^{h}(x,y)
is defined as

G^{h}(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\Bigg(\partial_{l}^{+,h}\Big(\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-\cdot+y)(\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(\cdot))\Big)(x)+\partial_{l}^{-,h}\Big(\bm{E}^{h}(-he_{l}-\cdot+y)(\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(\cdot))\Big)(x)\Bigg).

Applying the Leibniz rule, we can evaluate the components of the sum as

\displaystyle\partial_{l}^{+,h}\Big(\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-\cdot+y)(\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(\cdot))\Big)(x)

\displaystyle=\bm{E}^{h}(-x+y)(\bm{e}_{l}\partial_{l}^{+,h}\bm{f}(x))-(\partial_{l}^{-,h}\bm{E}^{h})(-x+y)(\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x)),

\displaystyle\partial_{l}^{-,h}\Big(\bm{E}^{h}(-he_{l}-\cdot+y)(\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(\cdot))\Big)(x)

\displaystyle=-(\partial_{l}^{+,h}\bm{E}^{h})(-x+y)(\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x))+\bm{E}^{h}(-x+y)(\bm{e}_{l}\partial_{l}^{-,h}\bm{f}(x)).

By direct computation, this gives

\displaystyle G^{h}(x,y)

\displaystyle=(\bm{E}^{h}\bm{D}^{h})(-x+y)\bm{f}(x)-\bm{E}^{h}(-x+y)(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f}(x))-\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}[(\partial_{l}^{h}\bm{E}^{h})(-x+y),\bm{e}_{l},\bm{f}(x)]

\displaystyle=\delta^{h}_{0}(-x+y)\bm{f}(x)-\bm{E}^{h}(-x+y)(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f}(x))-\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}[(\partial_{l}^{h}\bm{E}^{h})(-x+y),\bm{e}_{l},\bm{f}(x)].

We claim that the last term vanishes, i.e.,

\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}[(\partial_{l}^{h}\bm{E}^{h})(-x+y),\bm{e}_{l},\bm{f}]=0.

Given this claim, integrating over

B
yields

\displaystyle\int_{B}G^{h}(x,y)dV^{h}(x)=\bm{f}(y)\chi_{B}(y)-\int_{B}\bm{E}^{h}(-x+y)(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f}(x))dV^{h}(x).

Combining this with (
4.12
), we arrive at our conclusion.

To verify our claim, consider that

\bm{E}^{h}(y-x)=\bm{\overline{D}}^{h}F^{h}(y-x).

Here
F^{h}(y-x)
is the fundamental solution of the discrete Laplacian
\Delta^{h}
from (
4.1
). It satisfies the relation

F^{h}(-x+y)=\frac{1}{h^{7}}F^{1}(\frac{-x+y}{h})

and it is real-valued. Thus

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}[(\partial_{l}^{h}\bm{E}^{h})(-x+y),\bm{e}_{l},\bm{f}]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\sum\limits_{t=0}^{7}[\partial_{l}\bm{\overline{e}}_{t}(\partial_{t}F^{h})(-x+y),\bm{e}_{l},\bm{f}]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\sum\limits_{t=0}^{7}(\partial_{l}\partial_{t}F^{h})(-x+y)[\bm{\overline{e}}_{t},\bm{e}_{l},\bm{f}]

\displaystyle=0.

This holds true as

\partial_{l}^{h}\partial_{t}^{h}F=\partial_{t}^{h}\partial_{l}^{h}F

and for each
t,l=0,\cdots,7
,

(4.13)

[\bm{\overline{e}}_{l},\bm{e}_{t},\bm{f}]+[\bm{\overline{e}}_{t},\bm{e}_{l},\bm{f}]=0.

∎

Remark 4
.

Theorem
3

can be expanded to encompass general alternative algebras. However, this is not applicable to Cayley-Dickson algebras, due to the need for the alternative property of octonions as showcased in (
4.13
).

Theorem 4

(Cauchy integral theorem)
.

Let
B
be a bounded set in
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}.
Then for any function
\bm{f}\in M(\overline{B},\mathbb{O})
such that
\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f}=0
in
B
, we have

\chi_{B}(y)\bm{f}(y)=\int_{\partial B}\bm{K}^{h}(x,y)*\bm{f}(x)dS(x).

Proof.

This follows directly from the discrete Cauchy-Pompeiu integral formula. ∎

The discrete Cauchy-Pompeiu integral formula prompts further exploration, particularly the introduction of the discrete Cauchy-Bitsadze operator and the discrete Teodorescu operator.

Definition 10
.

Let
B
be a bounded set in the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}
.

1. The
discrete Cauchy-Bitsadze operator

\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}:M(\partial B,\mathbb{O})\longrightarrow M(\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h},\mathbb{O})
is defined by

\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y):=\int_{\partial B}\bm{K}^{h}(x,y)*\bm{f}(x)dS(x).

2. The
discrete Teodorescu operator

\bm{T}^{h}_{B}:M(B,\mathbb{O})\longrightarrow M(\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h},\mathbb{O})
is defined by

\bm{T}^{h}_{B}\bm{f}(y):=\int_{B}\bm{E}^{h}(y-x)\bm{f}(x)dV^{h}(x).

Theorem 5

(Inverse of Cauchy integral theorem)
.

Let
B
be a bounded set in
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h},
and
\bm{f}\in M(\overline{B},\mathbb{O}).
The following assertions hold:

•

The Cauchy type integral
\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}

is regular both in
B^{\circ}
and in
(\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\setminus B)^{\circ}.

•

If there holds

\bm{f}(y)=\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)
for any
y\in B,

then
\bm{f}
is regular in
B^{\circ}
.

Proof.

Since
B
is a bounded set in
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}
, it is a finite subset so that the integral by the
Cauchy type integral
is
a finite sum. This means we can calculate differential under the integral sign. Hence it suffices to show that for any
x\in\partial B

and any
y\in B^{\circ}\cup(\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\setminus B)^{\circ}
we have

\bm{D}^{h}\left(\bm{K}^{h}(x,\cdot)*\bm{f}(x)\right)(y)=0.

Indeed, by the definition of the star product from (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle\bm{D}^{h}\left(\bm{K}^{h}(x,\cdot)*\bm{f}(x)\right)(y)

\displaystyle=\bm{D}^{h}_{y}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\left(\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{-}(x)+\bm{E}^{h}(-he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{+}(x)\right)(\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x))\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{D}^{h}_{y}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{-}(x)+\bm{E}^{h}(-he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{+}(x)\right)(\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x))

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}[\bm{D}^{h}_{y},\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{-}(x)+\bm{E}^{h}(-he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{+}(x),\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[\bm{D}^{h}_{y},\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{-}(x)+\bm{E}^{h}(-he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{+}(x),\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x)].

In the last equality, we used
Lemma

6
, which means for any
x\in\partial B
and
y\in(\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\setminus\partial B)^{\circ},

\displaystyle\bm{D}^{h}_{y}\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-x+y)=\delta^{h}_{0}(he_{l}-x+y)=0,

\displaystyle\bm{D}^{h}_{y}\bm{E}^{h}(-he_{l}-x+y)=\delta^{h}_{0}(-he_{l}-x+y)=0.

Thus it remains to show

[\bm{D}^{h}_{y},\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{-}(x)+\bm{E}^{h}(-he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{+}(x),\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x)]=0.

We claim that for each
l=0,1\cdots,7,

\left[\bm{D}^{h}_{y},\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{-}(x),\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x)\right]=\left[\bm{D}^{h}_{y},\bm{E}^{h}(-he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{+}(x),\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x)\right]=0.

By (
4.8
),

\bm{\overline{D}}^{h}F=\bm{E}^{h},

where
F^{h}
is scale, the fundamental solution of the discrete Laplacian
\Delta^{h}
given by

F^{h}(x)=\frac{1}{h^{7}}F^{1}(\frac{x}{h}),

see (
4.1
). We have

\displaystyle\left[\bm{D}^{h}_{y},\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{-}(x),\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x)\right]

\displaystyle=[\bm{D}^{h}_{y},(\bm{D}^{h}_{y}F^{h})(he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{-}(x),\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x)]

\displaystyle=[\sum\limits_{i=0}^{7}\bm{e}_{i}\partial^{h}_{i},\sum\limits_{j=0}^{7}\bm{e}_{j}(\partial^{h}_{j}F^{h})(he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{-}(x),\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x)]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i,j=0}^{7}(\partial^{h}_{i}\partial^{h}_{j}F)(he_{l}-x+y)[\bm{e}_{i},\bm{e}_{j}n^{-}_{l}(x),\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x)].

Since
\partial^{h}_{i}\partial^{h}_{j}F=\partial^{h}_{j}\partial^{h}_{i}F
and

[\bm{e}_{i},\bm{e}_{j}n^{-}_{l}(x),\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x)]+[\bm{e}_{j},\bm{e}_{i}n^{-}_{l}(x),\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x)]=0,

we get

\left[\bm{D}^{h}_{y},\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{-}(x),\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x)\right]=0.

Similarly,

\left[\bm{D}^{h}_{y},\bm{E}^{h}(-he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{+}(x),\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x)\right]=0.

This finishes the proof.
∎

As in the continuous setting, the Teodorescu operator
\bm{T}^{h}_{B}
is a right inverse of the Cauchy-Fueter operator
\bm{D}^{h}
:

Theorem 6
.

Let B be a bounded set in
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}.
Then for any
\bm{f}\in M(B,\mathbb{O}),
there holds

\bm{D}^{h}(\bm{T}^{h}_{B}\bm{f})(y)=\chi_{B}(y)\bm{f}(y).

Proof.

Since the discrete integral

\bm{T}^{h}_{B}\bm{f}(y):=\int_{B}\bm{E}^{h}(y-x)\bm{f}(x)dV^{h}(x)

over a bounded
B
is actually a finite sum, we can calculate differential under the integral sign. That is,

(4.14)

\displaystyle\bm{D}^{h}(\bm{T}^{h}_{B}\bm{f})(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\bm{D}^{h}_{y}(\bm{E}^{h}(y-x)\bm{f}(x))dV^{h}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}(\bm{D}^{h}_{y}\bm{E}^{h}(y-x))\bm{f}(x)+[\bm{D}^{h}_{y},\bm{E}(y-x),\bm{f}(x)]dV^{h}(x)

\displaystyle=\chi_{B}(y)\bm{f}(y)+\int_{B}[\bm{D}^{h}_{y},\bm{E}(y-x),\bm{f}(x)]dV^{h}(x).

So it suffices to show

[\bm{D}^{h}_{y},\bm{E}(y-x),\bm{f}(x)]=0.

By (
4.8
),

\bm{\overline{D}}^{h}F=\bm{E}^{h},

where
F^{h}
is scale, the fundamental solution of the discrete Laplacian
\Delta^{h}
given by

F^{h}(x)=\frac{1}{h^{7}}F^{1}(\frac{x}{h}),

see (
4.1
). We have that

\displaystyle=[\bm{D}^{h}_{y},\bm{\overline{D}}^{h}_{y}F(y-x),\bm{f}(x)]

\displaystyle=[\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\bm{e}_{l}\partial^{h}_{y_{l}},\sum\limits_{m=0}^{7}\overline{\bm{e}}_{m}\partial^{h}_{y_{m}}F(y-x),\bm{f}(x)]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{l,m=0}^{7}\partial^{h}_{y_{l}}\partial^{h}_{y_{m}}F(y-x)[\bm{e}_{l},\overline{\bm{e}}_{m},\bm{f}(x)]

\displaystyle=0.

In the last equality, we use the fact that

\partial^{h}_{y_{l}}\partial^{h}_{y_{m}}F(y-x)=\partial^{h}_{y_{m}}\partial^{h}_{y_{l}}F(y-x)

and

[\bm{e}_{l},\overline{\bm{e}}_{m},\bm{f}(x)]+[\bm{e}_{m},\overline{\bm{e}}_{l},\bm{f}(x)]=0

for any
l,m=0,1,\cdots,7.

∎

5.
Estimation of the Discrete Cauchy Kernel

Let us consider the discrete Cauchy kernel in the lattice structure. We aim to provide a quantitative estimate for this kernel, which proves to be pivotal in the context of the discrete regular extension theory. Specifically, the relevance of this estimate can be observed in Theorems
11
and
12
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}
,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{8}}
be the Fourier transforms on
\mathbb{R}^{8}
and on
\mathbb{T}:=[-\pi,\pi]^{8}
respectively, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}g(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{8}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}g(u)e^{iu\cdot v}du,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{8}}g(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{8}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{8}}g(u)e^{iu\cdot v}du.

It is known that the fundamental solution of the Dirac operator in octonions

D=\sum_{l=0}^{7}e_{l}\partial_{l}

is given by

\bm{E}(x)=\dfrac{3}{\pi^{8}}\dfrac{\bm{\overline{x}}}{|x|^{8}}.

For its discrete version, we know that the discrete Dirac operator

D^{h}=\sum_{l=0}^{7}e_{l}\partial_{l}^{h}

has the the fundamental solution
E^{h}
, given in (
4.6
). We call it the discrete Cauchy kernel of octonions.
Without loss of generality, we may assume
h=1
.

Within the Fourier domain, we consider the discrete and continuous fundamental solutions above, which take the form

(5.1)

\displaystyle\bm{S_{c}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}^{-1}\bm{E},

\displaystyle\bm{S_{d}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{8}}^{-1}\bm{E}^{1}.

By direct calculation, they can be expressed explicitly as

\begin{split}\bm{S_{d}}(u)&=\dfrac{\sum_{l=0}^{7}\overline{\bm{e}_{l}}\,\sin u_{l}}{i\sum_{l=0}^{7}\sin^{2}u_{l}},\\
\bm{S_{c}}(u)&=\dfrac{\sum_{l=0}^{7}\overline{\bm{e}_{l}}\,u_{l}}{i|u|^{2}}.\end{split}

This two functions exhibit distinct phenomena for their singularities. In contrast to the classical case that

\bm{S_{c}}
has its sole singular point at
u=0
,
\bm{S_{d}}
exhibits 24 singular points characterized by
u=(u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{7})
where
u_{i}\in\{0,\pm\pi\}
. An alternative representation is

u=\pi\sum_{l=0}^{7}\alpha_{l}e_{l},\quad\alpha_{l}\in\{0,\pm 1\}.

We will provide an expression of the discrete Cauchy kernel in terms of the continuous Cauchy kernel as well as some auxiliary functions introduced below.

1.
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{8}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth, real-valued cutoff function, defined as

\phi(u)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }u\in\left[-\frac{\pi}{8},\frac{\pi}{8}\right]^{8},\\
0&\text{if }u\notin\left[\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{\pi}{4}\right]^{8}.\end{cases}

Utilizing
\phi
, we can discern the singular points from the aforementioned symbols, leading to auxiliary functions (2) and (3).

2.
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{8}\to\mathbb{R}
is a modified discrete fundamental solution, defined by

\Phi(u)=(\phi(u)-1)\bm{S_{c}}(u).

3. We consider an error function

\Theta(u)=\bm{S_{d}}(u)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{l}\in\{0,\pm 1\},\\
0\leq l\leq 7\end{subarray}}\phi\left(u+\pi\sum_{l=0}^{7}\alpha_{l}e_{l}\right)\bm{S_{c}}\left(u+\pi\sum_{l=0}^{7}\alpha_{l}e_{l}\right)

4. Introducing a bounded weight function:

\omega(x)=\prod_{l=0}^{7}\left(1+2\cos(\pi x_{l})\right)

Now we can provide close relation between the discrete and continuous Cauchy kernels.

Theorem 7
.

Te fundamental solution
\bm{E}^{1}
admits the following expansion:

(5.2)

\bm{E}^{1}(x)=\omega(x)\bm{E}(x)+\frac{1}{|x|^{8}}\omega(x)\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}(\Delta^{4}\Phi)(x)+\frac{1}{|x|^{8}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{8}}(\Delta^{4}\Theta)(x)

for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}\setminus\{0\},
In particular,

\bm{E}^{1}(x)=\omega(x)\bm{E}(x)+O(|x|^{-8})

as
|x|\rightarrow+\infty
.

The proof of
Theorem

7
relies heavily on the remarkable work by Shivakumar and Wong

[
32
]

concerning the asymptotic
expansion of Fourier transforms.
We denote

L_{q}=2^{q+n/2}\Gamma\left(\dfrac{q+n}{2}\right)\left(\Gamma\left(\dfrac{-q}{2}\right)\right)^{-1}.

Theorem 8

(
[
32
]
)
.

Let function f
\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
admit an expansion of a finite sum

f(u)=\sum\limits_{p,q}c_{p,q}u^{p}|u|^{q}+\Phi(u),

where

q+n>0

for all the real numbers q under the summation,
p=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n})
is a multi-index of non-negative integers,

u^{p}=u_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots u_{n}^{p_{n}}
,
and
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Then there holds

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(v)=\sum\limits_{p,q}c_{p,q}L_{q}\left(\frac{1}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{1}}\right)^{p_{1}}\cdots\left(\frac{1}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{n}}\right)^{p_{n}}(|v|^{-q-n})+\dfrac{(-1)^{m}}{|v|^{2m}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta^{m}\Phi)(v)

provided the following assumptions holds true:

(1) We assume

2m-1\leqslant Q+n\leqslant 2m+1

where

Q=\mathop{\max}\limits_{p,q}(q+|p|)

with the maximum taken over all the multi-indices
p
and the real number
q
under the summation.

(2) For any
j=0,1,...,m,
assume that, as
u\rightarrow 0,

\Delta^{j}\Phi(u)=\begin{cases}O(|u|^{Q-2j+2}),&Q+n=2m-1,\\
\\
O(|u|^{Q-2j+1}),&Q+n\neq 2m-1.\end{cases}

(3) There exists a constant
\rho>0
such that, for any
j=0,1,...,m,
the limit

\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\rho<|u|<R}\Delta^{j}f(u)e^{iu\cdot v}du

converges uniformly with respect to
v
for sufficiently large
|v|
.

Proof of
Theorem

7
.

The expansion (
5.2
) can be derived by the following two claims:

claim 1
:

\bm{E}^{1}(x)=\omega(x)\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}(\phi\bm{S}_{c})(x)+\dfrac{1}{|x|^{8}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{8}}(\Delta^{4}\Theta)(x),

claim 2
:

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}(\phi\bm{S}_{c})(x)=\bm{E}(x)+\dfrac{1}{|x|^{8}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}(\Delta^{4}\Theta)(x).

We prove claim 1 first. By (
5.1
),
\bm{E}^{1}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}}^{8}\bm{S}_{d},
which means

\bm{E}^{1}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}}^{8}\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{l}=0,\pm 1,\\
0\leqslant l\leqslant 7\end{subarray}}\phi(u+\pi\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\alpha_{l}e_{l})\bm{S}_{c}(u+\pi\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\alpha_{l}e_{l})\right)+\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}}^{8}\Theta.

A direct calculation shows that the first summand on the right side equals
\omega\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}(\phi\bm{S}_{c}).
On the other hand, since all the derivatives of
\Theta
up to the eighth order belong to
L^{1}([-\pi,\pi]^{8})
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{8}}\Theta=\frac{1}{|x|^{8}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{8}}(\Delta^{4}\Theta).

Combining the results above yields
claim 1
.

Now we prove
claim 2
. In
Theorem

8
, we take

\bm{\Phi}=(\phi-1)\bm{S}_{c},

and

\bm{f}(u)=(\phi\bm{S}_{c})(u)=\dfrac{\sum_{l=0}^{7}\overline{\bm{e}}_{l}\,u_{l}}{i|u|^{2}}+\bm{\Phi}(u),

also take the parameters

n=8,\quad m=4,\quad Q=-1,\quad|p|=1,\quad q=-2.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}(\phi\bm{S}_{c})(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi^{4}i}\left(\dfrac{1}{i}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\overline{\bm{e}}_{l}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}\right)|x|^{-6}+\frac{1}{|x|^{8}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}(\Delta^{4}\bm{\Phi})(x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{3}{\pi^{4}}\dfrac{\bm{\overline{x}}}{|x|^{8}}+\frac{1}{|x|^{8}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}(\Delta^{4}\bm{\Phi})(x)

\displaystyle=\bm{E}(x)+\frac{1}{|x|^{8}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}(\Delta^{4}\bm{\Phi})(x).

This finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem

7
gives an estimate for the discrete fundamental solution
\bm{E}^{1}
. For a much more common estimate for this function, we stipulate that
\bm{E}(0):=0
to get the following result.

Theorem 9
.

For any
h>0
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}
, we have

(5.3)

|\bm{E}^{h}(x)-\omega(h^{-1}x)\bm{E}(x)|\leqslant\dfrac{Ch}{|x|^{8}+h^{8}}.

Proof.

First, we take
h=1.
Then by
Theorem

7
, we have

|\bm{E}^{1}(x)-\omega(x)\bm{E}(x)|\leqslant\dfrac{C_{0}}{|x|^{8}},\quad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}\setminus\{0\}

where

C_{0}=\frac{1}{\pi^{8}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}|\Delta^{4}\Phi(u)|du+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{8}}\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^{8}}|\Delta^{4}\Theta(u)|du.

Furthermore,

|\bm{E}^{1}(0)-\omega(0)\bm{E}(0)|=|\bm{E}^{1}(0)|\neq+\infty.

Thus

(5.4)

|\bm{E}^{1}(x)-\omega(x)\bm{E}(x)|\leqslant\dfrac{C}{|x|^{8}+1}

with
C=\max\{|\bm{E}^{1}(0),2C_{0}\}.

By the definitions of the discrete and continuous fundamental solutions, we have

\bm{E}^{h}(x)=h^{-7}\bm{E}^{1}(h^{-1}x),\quad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h},

and

\bm{E}(x)=h^{-7}\bm{E}(h^{-1}x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{8}.

Therefore,

|\bm{E}^{h}(x)-\omega(h^{-1}\bm{E}(x))|=h^{-7}|\bm{E}^{1}(x^{\prime})-\omega(x^{\prime})\bm{E}(x^{\prime})|

with
x^{\prime}=h^{-1}x.
Hence (
5.4
) yields that

|\bm{E}^{h}(x)-\omega(h^{-1}x)\bm{E}(x)|\leqslant\dfrac{Ch^{-7}}{|x^{\prime}|^{8}+1}=\dfrac{Ch}{|x|^{8}+h^{8}}.

∎

6.
Sokhotski-Plemelj formula

This section delves into the Sokhotski-Plemelj formula, spotlighting operators like the discrete Cauchy-Bitsadze and Teodorescu. These operators, tied to the lattice’s boundaries, interlink functions on boundaries with those spanning the lattice. Key properties, like the discrete Cauchy-Pompeiu formula, enable function decomposition. Furthermore, the Sokhotski-Plemelj theorem and the discrete Plemelj projections underscore the significance of boundary values in discerning function behaviors, solidifying our understanding of functions on lattices.

Definition 11
.

For a bounded set
B
in the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}
,
we define the operators:

•

The
discrete Cauchy-Bitsadze operator

\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}:M(\partial B,\mathbb{O})\to M(\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h},\mathbb{O})
, given by

\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y):=\int_{\partial B}\bm{K}^{h}(x,y)\ast\bm{f}(x)\,dS(x).

•

The
discrete Teodorescu operator

\bm{T}^{h}_{B}:M(B,\mathbb{O})\to M(\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h},\mathbb{O})
, defined as

\bm{T}^{h}_{B}\bm{f}(y):=\int_{B}\bm{E}^{h}(y-x)\bm{f}(x)\,dV^{h}(x).

In parallel with the continuous context, the Teodorescu operator
\bm{T}^{h}_{B}
serves as a right inverse to the Cauchy-Fueter operator
\bm{D}^{h}
. Formally, for any
\bm{f}\in M(B,\mathbb{O})
,

\bm{D}^{h}\bm{T}^{h}_{B}\bm{f}=\bm{f}.

Leveraging these notations, the discrete Cauchy-Pompeiu formula (refer to
Theorem

3
) can be articulated as follows:

(6.1)

\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}+\bm{T}^{h}_{B}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f})=\chi_{B}\bm{f}.

Definition 12
.

Given a bounded domain
B
in the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}
, we define an integral operator
\bm{S}^{h}_{\partial B}:M(\partial B,\mathbb{O})\to M(\partial B,\mathbb{O})
by

\bm{S}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=2\int_{\partial B}\bm{K}^{h}(x,y)\ast(\bm{f}(x)-\bm{f}(y))\,dS(x)+\bm{f}(y).

Notation 1
.

We represent the inner and outer boundaries of
B
as
\partial^{+}B
and
\partial^{-}B
respectively, where

\partial^{+}B=\partial B\cap B\quad\text{and}\quad\partial^{-}B=\partial B\setminus B.

Theorem 10

(Discrete Sokhotski-Plemelj formula)
.

Given a bounded set
B
in
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}
and any function
\bm{f}\in M(\partial B,\mathbb{O})
, the following relation holds:

\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\pm\bm{f}(y)+\bm{S}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)\right)\quad\forall y\in\partial^{\pm}B.

Proof.

Using the definition of
\bm{S}^{h}_{\partial B}
for any
y\in\partial B
:

\displaystyle\bm{S}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)

\displaystyle=2\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}(\bm{f}-\bm{f}(y))(y)+\bm{f}(y)

\displaystyle=2\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)-2\bm{f}(y)\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}(1)(y)+\bm{f}(y).

Rearranging gives:

(6.2)

\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)-\frac{1}{2}\bm{S}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=\bm{f}(y)\left(\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}(1)(y)-\frac{1}{2}\right).

With the choice
\bm{f}\equiv 1
in (
6.1
), we determine:

\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}(1)(y)=\chi_{B}(y)\quad\forall y\in\partial B.

Further, for
y\in\partial B
, by definition:

\chi_{B}(y)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }y\in\partial^{+}B,\\
0,&\text{if }y\in\partial^{-}B.\end{cases}

Incorporating this into (
6.2
) completes the proof.
∎

Definition 13
.

We define the discrete Plemelj projections by

\displaystyle\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(I+\bm{S}^{h}_{\partial B})\bm{f},

\displaystyle\bm{Q}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(I-\bm{S}^{h}_{\partial B})\bm{f}.

Using
Theorem

10
, the following representation holds:

(6.3)

\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=\begin{cases}\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y),&\text{if }y\in\partial^{+}B,\\
\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)-\bm{f}(y),&\text{if }y\in\partial^{-}B.\end{cases}

This relation reflects the discontinuity between the inner and outer boundaries.

Theorem 11

(Discrete regular extensions)
.

For a bounded subset
B
of
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}
and any function
\bm{f}:\partial B\rightarrow\mathbb{O}
, we have

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

The projection
\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}
extends into
B
as a discrete regular function.

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

The projection
\bm{Q}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}
extends into
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\setminus B
as a discrete regular function vanishing at infinity.

Proof.

(i) Let the zero-extension of
\bm{f}
over the space
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}
also be denoted by
\bm{f}
. From (
6.1
), we deduce

\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=\begin{cases}\bm{f}(y)-\bm{T}^{h}_{B}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f})(y),&y\in\partial^{+}B,\\
-\bm{T}^{h}_{B}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f})(y),&y\in\partial^{-}B.\end{cases}

Combining with (
6.3
), we find

\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=\bm{f}(y)-\bm{T}^{h}_{B}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f})(y),\qquad\forall\ y\in\partial B.

Invoking
Theorem

6
, for any
\bm{g}\in M(B,\mathbb{O})
, we have:

\bm{D}^{h}(\bm{T}^{h}_{B}\bm{g})(y)=\chi_{B}(y)\bm{g}(y).

This implies that the function
\bm{f}(y)-\bm{T}^{h}_{B}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f})(y)
mapping
\overline{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{O}
is discrete regular. Hence,
\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)
is extendable into
\overline{B}
as a discrete regular function.

(ii) Since

\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}+\bm{Q}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}=\bm{f},

we obtain

\bm{Q}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=\bm{T}^{h}_{B}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f})(y),\quad\forall\ y\in\partial B.

The projection
\bm{Q}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)
then corresponds to the boundary values of the function

\bm{T}^{h}_{B}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f}):\overline{\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\setminus B}\rightarrow\mathbb{O}.

Using
Theorem

6
again, this function is discrete regular.

To show that
\bm{T}^{h}_{B}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f})(y)
vanishes at infinity, consider its definition:

\bm{T}^{h}_{B}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f})(y)=\int_{B}\bm{E}^{h}(y-x)(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f})(y)dV^{h}(x).

From
Theorem

9
, we can bound

\displaystyle|\bm{E}^{h}(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left|\prod_{l=0}^{7}(1+2\mbox{cos}(h^{-1}\pi x_{l}))\right||E(x)|+\frac{Ch}{|x|^{8}+h^{8}}

so that

(6.4)

\displaystyle|\bm{E}^{h}(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C}{|x|^{7}}+\frac{Ch}{|x|^{8}+h^{8}}.

Here
C
is a constant independent of
x
and
h
.

Consequently,

\begin{split}|\bm{T}^{h}_{B}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f})(y)|&\leqslant V^{h}(B)\max_{B}|\bm{D}^{h}f|\max_{x\in B}|\bm{E}^{h}(y-x)|\\
&\leqslant V^{h}(B)\max_{B}|\bm{D}^{h}f|\left(\frac{C}{(|y|-\rho)^{7}}+\frac{Ch}{(|y|-\rho)^{8}+h^{8}}\right),\end{split}

where

\rho=\max_{x\in B}|x|.

This verifies that
\bm{T}^{h}_{B}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f})(y)
vanishes at infinity, completing the proof.
∎

We now consider the Cauchy formula for discrete regular functions that vanish at infinity, pivotal for characterizing their boundary values.

Lemma 8

(Cauchy formula for discrete regular functions)
.

Given a bounded subset
B
of
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}
and a discrete regular function
\bm{f}:\overline{\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\setminus B}\rightarrow\mathbb{O}
that vanishes at infinity, the following relation holds:

(6.5)

\displaystyle-\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=\chi_{\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\setminus B}(y)\bm{f}(y).

Proof.

Let us extend
\bm{f}
to the entirety of
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}
with zero outside its original domain, and still denote it by
\bm{f}
. Define

\Omega_{n}:=\left\{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}:|y_{k}|\leqslant nh,\text{ for }k=0,\dots,7\right\}.

Invoking the discrete Cauchy-Pompeiu formula from (
6.1
), we can write

\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial\Omega_{n}}\bm{f}=\chi_{\Omega_{n}}\bm{f}-\bm{T}^{h}_{\Omega_{n}}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f})\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}=\chi_{B}\bm{f}-\bm{T}^{h}_{B}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f}).

Subtracting these expressions leads to

(6.6)

\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial\Omega_{n}}\bm{f}-\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}=\chi_{\Omega_{n}\setminus B}\bm{f}-\bm{T}^{h}_{\Omega_{n}\setminus B}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f}),

valid for sufficiently large values of
n
.

Since
\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f}
vanishes on
\Omega_{n}\setminus B
, it follows that

\bm{T}^{h}_{\Omega_{n}\setminus B}(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f})=0,

which simplifies (
6.6
) to

(6.7)

\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial\Omega_{n}}\bm{f}-\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}=\chi_{\Omega_{n}\setminus B}\bm{f}.

By letting
n
go to infinity in (
6.7
) and recognizing that
\chi_{\Omega_{n}\setminus B}
converges to
\chi_{\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\setminus B}
, our task is now reduced to proving that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial\Omega_{n}}\bm{f}(y)=0

for every fixed
y
.

To approach this, note that the boundary
\partial\Omega_{n}
is defined by

\partial\Omega_{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}:x_{k}\in\{nh,(n+1)h\},\text{ and }|x_{j}|\leqslant nh\text{ for }j\neq k\}.

Hence, there exists a constant
C
, independent of
n
and
h
, such that the discrete boundary measure
S
on
\partial\Omega_{n}
satisfies

S(\partial\Omega_{n})\leqslant Ch^{7}(n+1)^{7}.

By definition, the Cauchy operator is given by

\displaystyle\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial\Omega_{n}}\bm{f}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega_{n}}\bm{K}^{h}(x-y)\bm{f}(x)dS(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega_{n}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=0}^{7}\left(\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{-}(x)+\bm{E}^{h}(-he_{l}-x+y)n_{l}^{+}(x)\right)(\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}(x))dS(x).

We define the maximum absolute value of the integrand as
\bm{I}
.
We thus have

(6.8)

\displaystyle|\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial\Omega_{n}}\bm{f}(y)|\leqslant S(\partial\Omega_{n})\max_{x\in\partial\Omega_{n}}|\bm{f}(x)|\bm{I}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ch^{7}(n+1)^{7}\max_{x\in\partial\Omega_{n}}|\bm{f}(x)|\bm{I}.

Since

\sum_{l=0}^{7}(n_{l}^{-}+n_{l}^{+})=4,

it follows that

\bm{I}\leqslant\max_{x\in\partial\Omega_{n}}\sum_{l=0}^{7}|\bm{E}^{h}(he_{l}-x+y)|+|\bm{E}^{h}(-he_{l}-x+y)|.

For a fixed
y
and using (
6.4
), we deduce that

\bm{I}\leqslant\left(\frac{16C}{(nh-|y|-h)^{7}}+\frac{16Ch}{(nh-|y|-h)^{8}+h^{8}}\right),

which implies

(6.9)

\bm{I}=O(n^{-7}).

Since
\bm{f}(y)
vanishes at infinity, we have

(6.10)

\max_{x\in\partial\Omega_{n}}|\bm{f}(x)|=o(1).

Inserting (
6.9
) and (
6.10
) into (
6.8
), it becomes clear that

|\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial\Omega_{n}}|=o(1)

as
n\to\infty
, completing the proof. ∎

Theorem 12
.

Let
B
be a bounded subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}
and let
\bm{f}:\partial B\to\mathbb{O}
be any function. The following holds:

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

\bm{f}
is the boundary value of a discrete regular function on
B
if and only if

\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=\bm{f}(y),\qquad\forall\ y\in\partial B.

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

\bm{f}
is the boundary value of a discrete regular function on
\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\setminus B
that vanishes at infinity if and only if

\bm{Q}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=\bm{f}(y),\qquad\forall\ y\in\partial B.

Proof.

The sufficiency is directly derived from
Theorem

11
, so we focus on establishing the necessity.

(i) Suppose
\bm{f}:\partial B\to\mathbb{O}
is the boundary value of a discrete regular function
\bm{u}:B\to\mathbb{O}
. By equations (
6.1
) and (
6.3
), we get

\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{u}(y)=\bm{u}(y)\chi_{B}(y)

and

\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=\begin{cases}\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y),&y\in\partial^{+}B,\\
\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)+\bm{f}(y),&y\in\partial^{-}B.\end{cases}

Combining the above, we deduce

\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=\bm{f}(y),\qquad\forall\ y\in\partial B.

(ii) If
\bm{f}:\partial B\to\mathbb{O}
represents the boundary value of a discrete regular function

\bm{u}:\overline{\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\setminus B}\to\mathbb{O}
vanishing at infinity, then
Lemma

8
states

-\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=\begin{cases}\bm{u}(y),&y\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\setminus B,\\
0,&y\in B.\end{cases}

Furthermore, from
Theorem

10
, we have

\bm{Q}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=\begin{cases}-\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y),&y\in\partial^{-}B,\\
\bm{f}(y)-\bm{C}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y),&y\in\partial^{+}B.\end{cases}

This results in
\bm{Q}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}(y)=\bm{f}(y)
for any
y\in\partial B
, completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 2
.

The following algebraic relations hold:

\displaystyle(\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B})^{2}

\displaystyle=\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B},

\displaystyle(\bm{Q}^{h}_{\partial B})^{2}

\displaystyle=\bm{Q}^{h}_{\partial B},

\displaystyle\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{Q}^{h}_{\partial B}

\displaystyle=\bm{Q}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}=0.

Proof.

Considering
\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}+\bm{Q}^{h}_{\partial B}=I
, it is sufficient to establish the first relation. For any
\bm{f}\in M(\partial B,\mathbb{O})
, it follows from
Theorem

11
that
\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}
represents the boundary values of a discrete regular function on
B
. Therefore, from
Theorem

12
,

(\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B})^{2}\bm{f}=\bm{P}^{h}_{\partial B}\bm{f}.

∎

7.
Convergence of Discrete Regular Octonionic Functions

In this section, we confirm that a continuous octonionic function is regular precisely when it is a scale limit of discrete regular functions. In the context of the complex case, as referenced in
[
11
]
, this phenomenon is underscored through the uniform stability of Dirichlet problems related to discrete Laplace equations. Our approach is based on the methodology introduced for the quaternionic scenario in
[
29
]
.

7.1.
Discrete Approximations of Domains in
\mathbb{R}^{8}

Definition 14
.

For a bounded domain
B
within
\mathbb{R}^{8}
, a collection of sets
B^{h}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{8}
with
h>0
is called to converge to
B
, expressed as

\lim_{h\mapsto 0}B^{h}=B,

when the ensuing conditions are fulfilled:

(7.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\max_{\alpha\in\partial B}\min_{\beta\in\partial B^{h}}\|\alpha-\beta\|=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\max_{\alpha\in\partial B^{h}}\min_{\beta\in\partial B}\|\alpha-\beta\|=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\max_{\alpha\in\overline{B}}\min_{\beta\in B^{h}}\|\alpha-\beta\|=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\max_{\alpha\in B^{h}}\min_{\beta\in\overline{B}}\|\alpha-\beta\|=0.

Furthermore, if
B^{h}
converge to
B
and

V^{h}(B^{h}\setminus B)=O(h^{2}),

then
the collection of sets
B^{h}
is said to converge to
B
at a rate of
O(h^{2})
,
symbolized as

B=B^{h}+O(h^{2}).

We can demonstrate that any bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{8}
can be approximated by discrete sets at a rate of
O(h^{2})
.

Theorem 13
.

For a bounded domain
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{8}
, the sets

B^{h}\equiv(B\cap h\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{\circ}

converge to
B
at a rate of
O(h^{2})
.

Proof.

Let us confirm the four convergence identities from
Definition

14
.

(i) First we verify the fourth identity. Since
B^{h}
is contained in
B
, we have

\min_{\beta\in\overline{B}}\|\alpha-\beta\|=0,\quad\forall\,\alpha\in B^{h}

so that

\lim_{h\mapsto 0}\max_{\alpha\in B^{h}}\min_{\beta\in\overline{B}}\|\alpha-\beta\|=0.

(ii) To verify the third identity, we aim to show that given any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
ensuring

\overline{B}\subset B^{h}+B(0,2\varepsilon)

for any
h\leqslant\delta
.

For any given
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in\overline{B}
one can find
\delta>0
and
x^{\prime}\in B
such that

B(x^{\prime},\delta)\subset\subset B\cap B(x,\varepsilon).

Consequently,

B(x^{\prime},\delta)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\subset(B\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h})^{\circ}=B^{h}

for sufficiently small

h
, implying

B(x^{\prime},\delta)\cap B^{h}=B(x^{\prime},\delta)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{h}\neq\emptyset

for sufficiently small

h
. From the fact

B(x,\varepsilon)\cap B^{h}\supset B(x^{\prime},\delta)\cap B^{h},

it follows that

(7.2)

B(x,\varepsilon)\cap B^{h}\neq\emptyset,\quad\forall h\ll 1.

Due to the compactness of
B
, we can find a sequence
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
such that

\overline{B}\subset\bigcup\limits_{l=1}^{n}B(x_{l},\varepsilon).

As we have proved for each
x_{l}
there exists
\eta_{l}>0
such that

B(x_{l},\varepsilon)\cap B^{h}\neq\emptyset

for any
h<\eta_{l}.
Hence for any
h<\min\limits_{1\leqslant l\leqslant n}\{\eta_{l}\},
we have

\overline{B}\subset B^{h}+B(0,2\varepsilon).

(iii) Now we come to prove the second identity. It suffices to prove that

\min_{y\in\partial B}\|x-y\|\leqslant 2h

for any
x\in\partial B^{h}
.

Case 1:

x\in\partial^{+}B^{h}:=\partial B^{h}\cap B^{h}.

For any
y\in\partial^{+}(B\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}),
according to the definition of
\partial^{+}(B\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}),
we have
y\in B
and there is a point
y^{\prime}\in Z^{d}_{h}\setminus B
such that
\|y^{\prime}-y\|=h.
The fact that
y\in B
and
y^{\prime}\notin B
leads to the conclusion that

\mathop{\min}\limits_{\beta\in\partial B}\|y-\beta\|\leqslant h

for all
y\in\partial^{+}(B\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h})
.

For any
x\in\partial^{+}B^{h},
there exists
y\in N(x)
such that
y\notin B^{h}
. Since
B^{h}
is the discrete interior of
B\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h},
we have
y\in\partial^{+}(B\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}),
which implies

\min_{\beta\in\partial B}\|x-\beta\|\leqslant\|x-y\|+\min_{\beta\in\partial B}\|y-\beta\|\leqslant 2h.

Case 2:

x\in\partial^{-}B^{h}:=\partial B^{h}\setminus B^{h}.

From the fact that
B^{h}=(B\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h})^{\circ},
it follows that

x\in\partial^{+}(B\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}).

As shown above in case 1, we find

\min_{\beta\in\partial B}\|x-\beta\|\leqslant h.

(iv) At the final step, we come to verify the first identity

\lim_{h\mapsto 0}\max_{\alpha\in\partial B}\min_{\beta\in\partial B^{h}}\|\alpha-\beta\|=0.

Since
\partial B
is compact, it is sufficient to prove that for any
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in\partial B
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\min_{\beta\in\partial B^{h}}\|x-\beta\|<\varepsilon

for any
h<\delta.

Indeed, for any given
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in\partial B
, as shown in (
7.2
), there exists
\eta>0
such that

B(x,\varepsilon)\cap B^{h}\neq\emptyset

for any
h<\eta.

On the other hand, because
B(x,\varepsilon)\setminus B
is open, there exists
\eta^{\prime}>0
such that

B(x,\varepsilon)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\setminus\overline{B}\neq\emptyset

for all
h<\eta^{\prime}
.

When
h<\min\{\eta,\eta^{\prime}\},
we can thus find a sequence

\{x_{l}\}_{l=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\cap B(x,\varepsilon)

such that

x_{1}\in B^{h},\quad x_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\setminus B^{h}

and

\|x_{l}-x_{l+1}\|=h

for all
1\leqslant l\leqslant n-1
.

This completes the proof.

∎

7.2.
Convergence of Scaling Limits

In this subsection, we shall provide our main result about the convergence of scaling limits. To this end we need two auxiliary lemmas below.

Lemma 9
.

Let
B
is a bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{8}
and assume
\bm{f}\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{8})
is regular on
B
. Then we have

\max_{B}\|\bm{D^{h}f}\|=O(h^{2}).

Proof.

Since
\bm{D}\bm{f}=0,
we have

\displaystyle\bm{D^{h}f}(x)

\displaystyle=\bm{D}^{h}\bm{f}(x)-\bm{D}\bm{f}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\bm{e}_{l}\frac{\bm{f}(x+he_{l})-\bm{f}(x-he_{l})-\partial_{x_{l}}\bm{f}(x)2h}{2h}.

To deal with the last fractions, we need the following observation. For any
g\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{8},\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{g(x+h)-g(x-h)-\partial_{x}g(x)2h}{2h}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{x}g(x+h^{\prime})+\partial_{x}g(x-h^{\prime})-\partial_{x}g(x)2}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\partial_{x}^{2}g(x+h^{\prime\prime})-\partial_{x}^{2}g(x-h^{\prime\prime}))h^{\prime}}{2}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}^{3}(x+\lambda)h^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}

with
0<h^{\prime\prime}<h^{\prime}<h
and
\lambda\in(-h^{\prime\prime},+h^{\prime\prime}).

Given the previous analysis, the conclusion is evident.
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
B
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{8}
. If
B^{h}\subset B
for any
h>0
, then

\int_{B^{h}}1dV^{h}=O(1).

Proof.

Since
B
is bounded we may assume
B\subset\subset B(0,R).
Therefore, we have
B^{h}\subset B(0,R)
so that

\int_{B^{h}}1dV^{h}\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}}\chi_{B(0,R)}dV^{h}.

The last integral is identical to a Riemann sum of the integral
\int_{B(0,R)}1dV
where
dV
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{8}
, so that

\lim_{h\mapsto 0+}\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}}\chi_{B(0,R)}dV^{h}=\pi R^{2}.

Thus we come to the conclusion that

\int_{B^{h}}1dV^{h}=O(1).

∎

Now we come to the first main theorem in this section.

Theorem 14
.

Suppose that
B
is a bounded domain with
C^{3}
-boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{8}
and

B=B^{h}+O(h^{2}).

Then for any regular function
\bm{f}\in C^{3}(\overline{B},\mathbb{O}),
there exists a sequence of discrete regular functions
\bm{f}^{h}:B^{h}\longrightarrow\mathbb{O}
that converges to
f
in the sense that

\lim_{h\mapsto}\max_{B^{h}\cap B}\|\bm{f}-\bm{f}^{h}\|=0.

Proof.

Consider the discrete function
\bm{f}^{h}
given by

(7.3)

\bm{f}^{h}(x):=\int_{\partial B}\bm{K}^{h}(y,x)*\bm{\tilde{f}}(y)dS^{h}(y),

where
\bm{\tilde{f}}
is a
C^{3}_{0}
-extension of
\bm{f}
. By
Theorem

5
, we know that
\bm{f}^{h}
is discrete regular in
(B^{h})^{\circ}
. It suffices to prove that the scaling limit of
\bm{f}^{h}
is exactly
\bm{f}
.

Applying the discrete Cauchy-Pompeiu formula in
Theorem

3
to the function
\bm{\tilde{f}},
we obtain

\bm{f}(x)-\bm{f}^{h}(x)=\int_{B^{h}}\bm{E}^{h}(x-y)\left(\bm{D}^{h}\bm{\tilde{f}}(y)\right)dV^{h}(y)

for any
x\in B^{h}\cap B.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\|\bm{f}(x)-\bm{\tilde{f}}(x)\|\leqslant

\displaystyle\max_{B^{h}\cap B}\|\bm{D}^{h}\bm{\tilde{f}}\|\int_{B^{h}\cap B}\|\bm{E}^{h}(x-y)\|dV^{h}(y)

\displaystyle+\max_{B^{h}\setminus B}\|\bm{D}^{h}\bm{\tilde{f}}\|\int_{B^{h}\cap B}\|\bm{E}^{h}(x-y)\|dV^{h}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

Observing the first term
I_{1}
, we have

I_{1}\leqslant\max_{B^{h}\cap B}\|\bm{D}^{h}\|\left(\int_{B^{h}\cap B}1dV^{h}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{B^{h}\cap B}\|\bm{E}^{h}(x-y)\|^{2}dV^{h}(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

From the fact that
dV^{h}
is the Haar measure of
Z^{8}_{h}
, we find

\displaystyle\int_{B^{h}}\|\bm{E}(x-y)\|^{2}dV^{h}(y)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}}\|\bm{E}(y)\|^{2}dV^{h}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}}\|\frac{1}{h^{3}}\bm{E}(\frac{y}{h})\|^{2}h^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h^{2}}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}}\|\bm{E}(x)\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{8}}\|\bm{E}(x)\|dV

\displaystyle=O(h^{-2}).

Utilizing
Lemma

7
, we note that
\bm{E}
is an element of
L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{8})
.

By Lemmas
9

and
10
, we have

\displaystyle\max_{B^{h}\cap B}\|\bm{D}^{h}\bm{\tilde{f}}\|

\displaystyle=O(h^{2}),

\displaystyle\int_{B^{h}\cap B}1dV^{h}

\displaystyle=O(1).

From the preceding three evaluations, we derive

I_{1}\leqslant O(h^{2})O(1)O(h^{-1})=O(h).

According to the assumption that
B=B^{h}+O(h^{2})
, we have

V^{h}(B^{h}\setminus B)=O(h^{2}).

In addition, by the similar method used in the proof of Lemma 6.1, we can prove that

\max_{B^{h}\setminus B}\|\bm{D}^{h}\bm{\tilde{f}}\|=o(1).

Indeed, as a direct result of the mean value theorem, we have

\max_{B^{h}\cap B}\|\bm{D}^{h}\bm{\tilde{f}}-\bm{D}\bm{\tilde{f}}\|=O(h^{2}),

and from the facts that
B^{h}
converges to
B
and
\bm{D}\bm{\tilde{f}}=0
on B, we can deduce that

\max_{B^{h}}\|\bm{D}\bm{\tilde{f}}\|=o(1).

Therefore,

\max_{B^{h}\setminus B}\|\bm{D}^{h}\bm{\tilde{f}}\|=o(1).

In the same way as we deal with
I_{1}
, we can also obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\max_{B^{h}\setminus B}\|\bm{D}^{h}\bm{\tilde{f}}\|(V^{h}(B^{h}\setminus B))^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{B^{h}\setminus B}\|\bm{E}^{h}(x-y)\|^{2}dV^{h}(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=o(1)O(h)O(h^{-1})=o(1).

This completes the proof.

∎

Next, we demonstrate that when the scaling limit of discrete regular functions is present, it is indeed regular.

Theorem 15
.

Suppose
B
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{8}
and
B^{h}\subset Z^{8}_{h}
converges to
B
. If a function
\bm{f}\in C^{1}(B)
is the scaling limit of discrete regular functions
\bm{f}^{h}:B^{h}\longrightarrow\mathbb{O},
i.e.,

\lim_{h\mapsto 0+}\max_{B^{h}\cap B}\|\bm{f}-\bm{f}^{h}\|=0,

then
\bm{f}
is regular on
B
.

The proof of
Theorem

15
relies on the next two lemmas.

Lemma 11
.

Let
B
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{8}
and assume
B^{h}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}
convergent to
B
. Then for any
U\subset\subset B,
there exists
\delta>0
such that

U\cap Z^{8}_{h}\subset B^{h}

whenever
h<\delta.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
U=B(x_{0},R)\subset\subset B.

From the assumption that
B^{h}
converges to
B
,we have

\lim_{h\mapsto 0}\max_{\alpha\in\overline{B}}\min_{\beta\in B^{h}}\|x-y\|=0,

which means that, for any given
\varepsilon>0,

x_{0}\in\overline{B}\subset B^{h}+B(0,\varepsilon),

when
h
is sufficiently small. In particular, by taking
\varepsilon=R,
we have

x_{0}\in B^{h}+B(0,R),

i.e.,
(x_{0}-B(0,R))\cap B^{h}\neq\emptyset,
which means
U\cap B^{h}\neq\emptyset.

On the other hand, we denote

d=\inf_{\alpha\in U,\beta\in\partial B}\|\alpha-\beta\|.

From the assumption that
B^{h}
converges to
B
, it follows that

\lim_{h\mapsto 0+}\max_{\alpha\in\partial B^{h}}\min_{\beta\in\partial B}\|\alpha-\beta\|=0.

For any
h
sufficiently small, we thus have

\partial B^{h}\subset\partial B+B(0,d)

so that

\partial B^{h}\cap U\subset(\partial B+B(0,d))\cap U.

Since
(\partial B+B(0,d))\cap U=\emptyset,
we obtain

\partial B^{h}\cap U=\emptyset.

We have now established that for sufficiently small
h

both conditions

U\cap B^{h}\neq\emptyset,\qquad U\cap\partial B^{h}=\emptyset

hold true.

Now we have proved that, when
h
is sufficiently small,

U\cap B^{h}\neq\emptyset

and

U\cap\partial B^{h}=\emptyset

are both valid.

Based on these facts, we come to show that

U\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\subset B^{h}

for all sufficiently small
h
.

Assume this is not valid, then there exists
h^{*}
such that

U\cap B^{h^{*}}\neq\emptyset,\quad U\cap\partial B^{h^{*}}=\emptyset,\quad U\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\nsubseteq B^{h^{*}}.

From the fact that

\alpha,\beta\in U\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h^{*}}=B(0,R)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}

and that
B(x_{0},R)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}
is discrete connected in the grid
Z^{8}_{h^{*}}
, we can derive that there exists a sequence

\{x_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}\subset U\cap Z^{8}_{h^{*}}

such that

x_{1}=\alpha,\quad x_{m}=\beta,\quad\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|=h

for each
k=1,2,\cdots,m-1.

Notice that

\alpha\in B^{h^{*}},\qquad\beta\notin B^{h^{*}},

one can find
k^{*}
with
1\leqslant k^{*}\leqslant m-1
such that

x_{k^{*}}\in B^{h^{*}},\quad x_{k^{*}+1}\notin B^{h^{*}}.

According to the definition of discrete boundaries,
x_{k^{*}}
and
x_{k^{*}+1}
are both in
\partial B^{h^{*}}
. It violates the assumption that
\partial B^{h^{*}}\cap U=\emptyset
. The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
B
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{8}
and
B^{h}\subset Z^{8}_{h}
convergent to
B
. Then we have

\lim_{h\mapsto 0+}\int_{B^{h}\cap B}\bm{f}dV^{h}=\int_{B}\bm{f}dV

for any
\bm{f}\in C_{0}(B,\mathbb{O}).

Proof.

Let’s consider any function

\bm{f}

from the set

C_{0}(B)
.
We can pinpoint a set

U
, which is the finite union of balls, fulfilling the condition:

(7.4)

\mbox{supp}\bm{f}\subset U\subset\subset B.

From the evidence provided in
Lemma

11
, when

h
is sufficiently small, it is clear that

U\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\subset B^{h}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{8},

which implies the equality
B^{h}\cap U=U\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}.

Utilizing both the above relation and (
7.4
), we can deduce

\int_{B^{h}\cap B}\bm{f}dV^{h}=\int_{B^{h}\cap U}\bm{f}dV^{h}=\int_{U\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}}\bm{f}dV^{h}.

It is worth noting that the latter integral serves as a Riemann sum for the Riemann integral

\int_{U}\bm{f}dV
. Hence we obtain

\lim_{h\mapsto 0+}\int_{B^{h}\cap B}\bm{f}dV^{h}=\lim_{h\mapsto 0+}\int_{U\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}}\bm{f}dV^{h}=\int_{U}\bm{f}dV=\int_{B}\bm{f}dV.

This concludes our proof.
∎

We now turn our attention to proving
Theorem

15
.

Proof of
Theorem

15
.

Assume that a sequence of discrete regular functions
\bm{f}^{h}:B^{h}\longrightarrow\mathbb{O}
converges to a function
\bm{f}\in C^{1}(B,\mathbb{O}).
We come to prove that
\bm{f}
is regular on
B
.

We only need to verify that
\bm{Df}=0
holds true in the sense of distribution, i.e.,

(7.5)

\int_{B}(\phi\bm{D})\bm{f}dV=0

for any
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B,\mathbb{R}).

We emphasize that it is sufficient where test functions
\phi
can be chosen to be

\mathbb{R}
-valued rather than
\mathbb{O}
-valued.
Moreover, associators can be avoided in this manner.

Actually, if (
7.5
) holds for any
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B,\mathbb{R})
. Then we have

(7.6)

0=\int_{B}(\phi\bm{D})\bm{f}dV=-\int_{B}\phi(\bm{Df})dV.

Denote

\bm{Df}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{7}g_{i}\bm{e}_{i},

where
g_{i}\in C_{0}(B,\mathbb{R}).
Then
(
7.6
) implies that
for any
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B,\mathbb{R})
,

(7.7)

\int_{B}\phi g_{i}=0,\quad i=0,\cdots,7.

Thus
g_{i}=0
if we let
\phi=g_{i}
in (
7.7
) for each
i=0,\cdots,7.

Now we prove (
7.5
). As shown in
Lemma

12
, we have

\lim_{h\mapsto 0}\int_{B^{h}\cap B}(\phi\bm{D})\bm{f}dV^{h}=\int_{B}(\psi\bm{D})\bm{f}dV.

Hence it suffices to prove

\lim_{h\mapsto 0+}\int_{B^{h}\cap B}(\phi\bm{D})\bm{f}dV^{h}=0.

We now split the last integral into three parts

\int_{B^{h}\cap B}(\phi\bm{D})\bm{f}dV^{h}=I^{h}_{1}+I^{h}_{2}+I^{h}_{3},

where

(7.8)

\displaystyle\bm{I}^{h}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{h}\cap B}(\phi\bm{D})\bm{f}dV^{h},

\displaystyle\bm{I}^{h}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{h}\cap B}(\phi\bm{D})(\bm{f}-\bm{f}^{h})dV^{h},

\displaystyle\bm{I}^{h}_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{h}\cap B}(\phi\bm{D}-\phi\bm{D}^{h})\bm{f}dV^{h}.

Consider the first term
\bm{I}^{h}_{1}
. Since
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B)
we have

\mbox{supp}(\phi\bm{D}^{h})\subset B

for any sufficiently small
h
. Hence

\bm{I}^{h}_{1}=\int_{B^{h}}(\phi\bm{D})\bm{f}dV^{h}.

Applying the discrete divergence theorem (
Theorem

1
), we get

\displaystyle\bm{I}^{h}_{1}=\int_{\partial B^{h}}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{7}(\phi(x+he_{l})n_{l}^{+}(x)+\phi(x-he_{l})n_{l}^{-}(x))\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}^{h}(x)dS^{h}(x)-\int_{B^{h}}\phi(\bm{D}^{h}f^{h})dV^{h}.

Take
U=\mbox{supp}\phi+B(0,\frac{d}{2})
with

d=\min_{x\in\mbox{supp}\phi}\min_{y\in\partial B}\|x-y\|.

Since
B^{h}
converges to
B
,
Lemma

11
indicates that

(\mbox{supp}\phi+B(0,4h))\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\subset U\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\subset B^{h}

for sufficient ly small
h
.
Consequently, for any

\alpha\in(\mbox{supp}\phi+B(0,2h))\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h},

we have

N(\alpha)\subset B(\alpha,2h)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\subset B^{h}

so that

(\mbox{supp}\phi+B(0,2h))\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}\subset(B^{h})^{\circ}.

This implies

(\mbox{supp}\phi+B(0,2h))\cap\partial B^{h}=\emptyset

and yields the fact that

\int_{\partial B^{h}}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{7}\left(\phi(\cdot+he_{l})n_{l}^{+}+\phi(\cdot-he_{l})n_{l}^{-}\right)\bm{e}_{l}\bm{f}^{h}dS^{h}=0

so that

\bm{I}^{h}_{1}=-\int_{B^{h}}\phi(\bm{D^{h}f^{h}})dV^{h}=\int_{(B^{h})^{\circ}}\phi(\bm{D^{h}f^{h}})dV^{h}.

Since
\bm{D^{h}f^{h}}=0
on
(B^{h})^{\circ},

\bm{I^{h}_{1}}
thus vanishes for any sufficiently small
h
.

Consider the second item
\bm{I^{h}_{2}}
in (
7.8
). By definition,

\displaystyle\|\bm{I}^{h}_{2}\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{B^{h}\cap B}\|(\phi\bm{D}^{h})(\bm{f}-\bm{f}^{h})\|dV^{h}

\displaystyle\leqslant\max_{B^{h}\cap B}\|\bm{f}-\bm{f}^{h}\|\max_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}\|\phi\bm{D}^{h}\|\int_{B^{h}\cap B}1dV^{h}.

Pick
R>0
such that
B\subset B(0,R).
We thus have

B^{h}\cap B\subset B(0,R)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}.

It is clear that

\lim_{h\mapsto 0+}\int_{B(0,R)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{8}_{h}}1dV^{h}=\int_{B(0,R)}1dV,

which yields

\int_{B^{h}\cap B}1dV^{h}=O(1).

Since
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B),
we have

\max_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}\|\phi\bm{D}^{h}\|=O(1).

Hence from the assumption

\max_{B^{h}\cap B}\|\bm{f}-\bm{f}^{h}\|=o(1),

we derive that

\|\bm{I}^{h}_{2}\|\leqslant o(1)O(1)O(1)=o(1).

Now it remains to show that
\bm{I}^{h}_{3}=o(1)
.
Similar to
\bm{I}^{h}_{2},
we have

\displaystyle\|\bm{I}^{h}_{3}\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{B^{h}\cap B}\|(\phi\bm{D}-\phi\bm{D}^{h})\bm{f}\|dV^{h}

\displaystyle\leqslant\max_{\mbox{supp}\phi+\overline{B(0,h)}}\|\bm{f}\|\max_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}\|\phi\bm{D}-\phi\bm{D}^{h}\|\int_{B^{h}\cap B}1dV^{h}.

Since
\bm{f}\in C^{1}(B)
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B),
we have

(7.9)

\max_{\mbox{supp}\phi+\overline{B(0,h)}}\|\bm{f}\|=O(1),\quad\max_{\mathbb{R}^{8}}\|\phi\bm{D}-\phi\bm{D}^{h}\|=o(1).

As we have observed,

\int_{B^{h}\cap B}1dV^{h}=O(1),

we thus obtain

\bm{I}^{h}_{3}=o(1).

As a result,

\bm{I^{h}_{1}+I^{h}_{2}+I^{h}_{3}}=o(1).

This completes the proof.

∎

In conclusion, a function is deemed regular precisely when it stands as the scaling limit of discrete regular functions.

Theorem 16
.

Suppose
B
is a bounded domain with
C^{3}
-boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{8}

\bm{f}\in C^{3}(B)

\bm{f}^{h}:B^{h}\longrightarrow\mathbb{O}
such that
\bm{f}
is the scaling limit of
\bm{f}^{h}
.

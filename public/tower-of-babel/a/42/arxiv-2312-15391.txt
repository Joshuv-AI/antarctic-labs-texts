Abstract
In this paper, we study the connection between the tribimaximal and bitrimaximal mixing patterns. In doing so, we are forced to work in a non-diagonal charged lepton basis. This leads to several relations that must hold between the lepton mixing angles. After a short discussion, we analyze the underlying flavor symmetry responsible for this prediction. Finally, we add CP violation to bitrimaximal mixing and study its effect on the flavor symmetry group.

Framework and Motivation

2.1
The Neutrino Sector and
\boldsymbol{Z_{2}\times Z_{2}}

We begin this discussion with an overview of the
Z_{2}\times Z_{2}
symmetry in neutrino mixing. By assuming that neutrinos are Majorana fermions (see Refs.
[
12
]
and
[
14
]
for reviews), the neutrino mass matrix
M_{\nu}
is a complex symmetric matrix, i.e.,
M_{\nu}=M_{\nu}^{T}
. Thus, it can be diagonalized by a unitary matrix,
U_{\nu}
, such that

U_{\nu}^{T}M_{\nu}U_{\nu}=M_{\nu}^{\text{Diag}}=\text{Diag}(|m_{1}|e^{i\alpha_{1}},|m_{2}|e^{i\alpha_{2}},|m_{3}|e^{i\alpha_{3}})=\text{Diag}(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}),

(1)

where
\alpha_{1,2,3}
contribute to the two physical Majorana phases
[
31
,
32
]
. The utility of keeping the neutrino masses complex has been demonstrated in Refs.
[
33
,
34
]
. Notice that the neutrino masses can be made real and positive with the replacement of
U_{\nu}\rightarrow U_{\nu}P_{\text{Maj}}
, where
P_{\text{Maj}}=\text{Diag}(e^{i\alpha_{1}/2},e^{i\alpha_{2}/2},e^{i\alpha_{3}/2})

In contrast to the neutrinos, the charged leptons cannot be assumed to be Majorana fermions. Thus, the diagonalization of the charged lepton mass matrix
m_{e}
can be achieved by defining
M_{e}=m_{e}m_{e}^{\dagger}
and then diagonalizing this Hermitian matrix by a unitary matrix
U_{e}
such that

U_{e}^{\dagger}M_{e}U_{e}=M_{e}^{\text{Diag}}=\text{Diag}(|m_{e}|^{2},|m_{\mu}|^{2},|m_{\tau}|^{2}).

(2)

In what follows,
U_{\nu}
will be referred to as the neutrino mixing matrix and
U_{e}
as the charged lepton mixing matrix. However, recall that it is only possible to measure the overlap of these two mixing matrices, i.e., the MNSP lepton mixing matrix
U_{\text{MNSP}}=U_{e}^{\dagger}U_{\nu}

[
10
,
11
]
. Expressing this in the standard PDG parameterization
[
11
]
, but rephasing it by the charged lepton phase matrix
P_{e}

[
33
]
yields

\displaystyle U_{\text{MNSP}}=

\displaystyle P_{e}U_{\text{MNSP}}^{\text{PDG}}=P_{e}R_{x}(\theta_{23})R_{y}(\theta_{13},\delta)R_{z}(\theta_{12})

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}c_{12}c_{13}&c_{13}s_{12}&e^{-i\delta}s_{13}\\
-c_{23}s_{12}-c_{12}e^{i\delta}s_{13}s_{23}&c_{12}c_{23}-e^{i\delta}s_{12}s_{13}s_{23}&c_{13}s_{23}\\
-s_{12}s_{23}+c_{12}c_{23}e^{i\delta}s_{13}&c_{12}s_{23}+c_{23}e^{i\delta}s_{12}s_{13}&-c_{13}c_{23}\\
\end{array}\right),

where

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}R_{x}(\theta_{23})=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&c_{23}&s_{23}\\
0&-s_{23}&c_{23}\end{array}\right),&R_{y}(\theta_{13},\delta)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}c_{13}&0&s_{13}e^{-i\delta}\\
0&1&0\\
-s_{13}e^{i\delta}&0&c_{13}\end{array}\right),\\
&\\
R_{z}(\theta_{12})=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}c_{12}&s_{12}&0\\

2.3
The
\boldsymbol{U^{\text{TBM}}}
and
\boldsymbol{U^{\text{BTM}}}
Connection

The previous discussion hinted at a possible connection between the tribimaximal and bitrimaximal mixing angle values when considering the conditions on the mixing angles that are needed to preserve a trimaximal middle column, see Eq. (
33
). For the remainder of this section, we will further elaborate on their relationship. To begin this discussion, assume that tribimaximal mixing solely originates in the neutrino sector such that
U_{\nu}=U^{\text{TBM}}
, cf. Eq. (
2.2
). Furthermore, assume that the MNSP lepton mixing matrix has the bitrimaximal form given in Eq. (
2.2
). Then, it is possible to relate the bitrimaximal MNSP matrix to the tribimaximal neutrino matrix through the charged lepton mixing matrix,
U_{e}
, such that

U_{\text{MNSP}}=U_{e}^{\dagger}U_{\nu}=U_{e}^{\dagger}U^{\text{TBM}}\equiv U^{\text{BTM}}.

(34)

A straightforward calculation reveals that

\displaystyle U_{e}=U^{\text{TBM}}(U^{\text{BTM}})^{{\dagger}}=\frac{1}{6}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}2+\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{6}&2-2\sqrt{2}&2+\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{6}\\
2+\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{6}&2+\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{6}&2-2\sqrt{2}\\
2-2\sqrt{2}&2+\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{6}&2+\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{6}\\
\end{array}\right).

Notice that this charged lepton mixing matrix has the rather peculiar property that in addition to being unitary, i.e.,
U_{e}U_{e}^{\dagger}=U_{e}^{\dagger}U_{e}=1
, the sum of all of the entries of each row as well as the sum of all of the entries of each column is non-trivially equal to unity, i.e.,

\sum_{i=1}^{3}(U_{e})_{ik}=1,\penalty\ k=1,2,3\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \sum_{j=1}^{3}(U_{e})_{kj}=1,\penalty\ k=1,2,3.

(38)

Now that the matrix which diagonalizes the charged lepton mass matrix
M_{e}
has been deduced, we next look at the implications it has on said matrix.

2.4

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{6}(2+\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{6})=0.977\approx\lambda^{0},

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{6}(2-2\sqrt{2})=-0.138\approx-\lambda,

2.5
Implications for Lepton Flavor Symmetries

The neutrino mixing matrix being of TBM form implies that the non-trivial diagonal
Z_{2}\times Z_{2}

\displaystyle T_{1}^{\text{Diag}}=\text{Diag}(1,\omega,\omega^{2}),\penalty\ T_{2}^{\text{Diag}}=\text{Diag}(\omega^{2},1,\omega),\penalty\ T_{3}^{\text{Diag}}=\text{Diag}(\omega,\omega^{2},1),

\displaystyle(T_{1}^{\text{Diag}})^{2}=\text{Diag}(1,\omega^{2},\omega),\penalty\ (T_{2}^{\text{Diag}})^{2}=\text{Diag}(\omega,1,\omega^{2}),\penalty\ (T_{3}^{\text{Diag}})^{2}=\text{Diag}(\omega^{2},\omega,1),

Now that the forms of

Noticeably, in addition to satisfying

4
Conclusions

A trimaximal middle column is a feature common to both bitrimaximal and tribimaximal mixing patterns. Usually, these patterns are derived by considering a diagonal charged lepton sector and obtaining all of the mixing from the neutrino sector. In the current work, this assumption was relaxed in order to explore the implications of starting with tribimaximal mixing in the neutrino sector and arriving at the bitrimaximal mixing MNSP form through the non-trivial charged lepton mixing matrix given in Eq. To delve deeper into the connection between both mixing patterns, we started with the group elements for tribimaximal mixing in the neutrino sector, see Eq. (

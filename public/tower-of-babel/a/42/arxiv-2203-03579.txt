L ⁡ ( 2 , 1 ) L(2,1) -labeling of some zero-divisor graphs associated with commutative rings
Rameez Raja Annayat Ali

Abstract
Let 𝒢 = ( 𝒱 , ℰ ) \mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}) be a simple graph, an L ⁡ ( 2 , 1 ) L(2,1) -labeling of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} is an assignment of labels from non-negative integers to vertices of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} such that adjacent vertices get labels which differ by at least by two, and vertices which are at distance two from each other get different labels. The λ \lambda -number of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} , denoted by λ ⁡ ( 𝒢 ) \lambda(\mathcal{G}) , is the smallest positive integer ℓ \ell such that 𝒢 \mathcal{G} has an L ⁡ ( 2 , 1 ) L(2,1) -labeling with all labels as members of the set { 0 , 1 , … , ℓ } \{0,1,\dots,\ell\} . The zero-divisor graph of a finite commutative ring R R with unity, denoted by Γ ⁡ ( R ) \Gamma(R) , is the simple graph whose vertices are all zero divisors of R R in which two vertices u u and v v are adjacent if and only if u ​ v = 0 uv=0 in R R . In this paper, we investigate L ⁡ ( 2 , 1 ) L(2,1) -labeling of some zero-divisor graphs. We study the partite truncation , a graph operation that allows us to obtain a reduced graph of relatively small order from a graph of significantly larger order. We establish the relation between λ \lambda -numbers of the graph and its partite truncated one. We make use of the operation partite truncation to contract the zero-divisor graph of a reduced ring to the zero-divisor graph of a Boolean ring.

Given a simple undirected finite graph

of

-labeling with minimum value

-labeling number, denoted by,

-labeling

. The

-labeling problem has been studied extensively for the case

Partite truncation of a graph

In this section, we study a graph operation called as
partite truncation
of a graph. This operation allows us to obtain a graph of relatively smaller order from a graph of significantly larger order. We prove an interesting result in which we determine the
\lambda
-number and a corresponding minimum labeling of Beck’s zero-divisor graph
\Gamma^{\prime}(R)
from the graph
\Gamma(R)
of a ring
R

diam(\Gamma(R))<3
.

Definition 9
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an
n
-partite graph with
\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{n}

\hat{\mathcal{G}}
of

\hat{v_{i}}

\hat{v_{i}}

\hat{v_{j}}

adjacent to some vertex in
\mathcal{V}_{j}

Thus, in a partite truncation of an
n
-partite graph, each partite set is truncated to one vertex, and edges (if any) connecting two partite sets are truncated to a single edge.
It is clear from the definition that if

\hat{\mathcal{G}}
.
If

is equal to one, then

-partite graphs

with

in which there is at least one partite set

Let

denote the

-partite graph with the property that the bipartite subgraph induced by any two distinct partite sets

\mathcal{G}

Let

In the following result, we demonstrate that by applying the operation
“partite truncation”
to the zero-divisor graph of a reduced ring we obtain the zero-divisor graph associated with a Boolean ring.

Proposition 16
.

For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}}
be finite fields of order
q_{i}
and let
\mathcal{G}\cong\Gamma(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
. Then
\mathcal{G}
is a
(2^{n}-2)
-partite graph with

\hat{\mathcal{G}}\cong\Gamma(\prod\limits^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{2}).

Proof.

We prove the result in two parts parts. First, we show that
\mathcal{G}
is
(2^{n}-2)
-partite graph, and then we show its partite truncation is isomorphic to
\Gamma(\prod\limits^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.

Part-I

Note that each non-trivial zero divisor of
\Gamma(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
, and hence every vertex of
\mathcal{G}
is an n-tuple which has at least one zero and one non-zero entry in it. For
1\leq t<n
, we partition the vertices of
\mathcal{G}
as follows,

\mathcal{U}_{t}=\{v_{t}:v_{t}\mbox{ has exactly }t\mbox{ non-zero entries in it}\}.

For each such
t
, the number of ways to choose
t
entries out of
n
entries is
\binom{n}{t}
. Furthermore, for each
t
, we partition the set
\mathcal{U}_{t}
into
\binom{n}{t}
sets as,
\mathcal{U}_{t}=\big\{\mathcal{V}_{l,t}:1\leq l\leq\binom{n}{t}\big\},

where
\mathcal{V}_{l,t}
consists of those vertices of
\mathcal{U}_{t}
that have
t
non-zero entries in the same position. For each
l
and
t
, no two vertices in
\mathcal{V}_{l,t}
are adjacent to each other. On the other hand, if a vertex of one such set say
\mathcal{V}_{l,r}
is adjacent to some vertex of another set say
\mathcal{V}_{l^{\prime},r^{\prime}}
, where
1\leq r\leq r^{\prime}<n
, then each vertex of
\mathcal{V}_{l,r}
is adjacent to every vertex of
\mathcal{V}_{l^{\prime},r^{\prime}}
. The total number of all such sets in
V(\mathcal{G})
is given as,

\sum\limits_{1\leq t<n}\binom{n}{t}=2^{n}-2.
This implies,
\mathcal{G}
is a
(2^{n}-2)
-partite graph.

Part-II

As in Part-I, we partition vertex set of the graph
\Gamma(\prod\limits^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{2})
into
\big\{\mathcal{V}_{l,t}:1\leq l\leq\binom{n}{t}\big\}
. Since there is only one non-zero term in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, therefore, for each
l
and
t
,
|\mathcal{V}_{l,t}|=1
. It follows that the order of
\Gamma(\prod\limits^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is equal to the number of partite sets in
\Gamma(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
, which by Part-I is equal to
2^{n}-2
. The map
\hat{v_{l,t}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}_{l,t}
defines an isomorphism between
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
and
\Gamma(\prod\limits^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{2}).

∎

Conclusion

In this research article, we studied partite truncation, a graph operation that aids to obtain a smaller order graph from relatively larger order
n
-partite graph. This contraction of the order allow us to determine the
\lambda
-number and corresponding minimal labeling of the
n
-partite graph from the contracted graph. We exhibited that the zero-divisor graph associated with a Boolean ring is a partite truncated graph of the zero-divisor graph realized by a reduced ring. We also proved an interesting result that illustrates the deduction of
\lambda
-number of Beck’s zero-divisor graph
\Gamma^{\prime}(R)
from
\lambda
-number of the zero-divisor graph
\Gamma(R)
. We concluded this paper with an open problem for future research work.

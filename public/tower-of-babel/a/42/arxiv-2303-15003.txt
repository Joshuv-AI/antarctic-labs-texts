Toni Ikonen

Abstract
. We prove that a Sobolev map from a Riemannian manifold into a complete metric space pushes forward almost every compactly supported integral current to an Ambrosio–Kirchheim integral current in the metric target, where "almost every" is understood in a modulus sense. As an application, we prove that when the target supports an isoperimetric inequality of Euclidean type for integral currents, an isoperimetric inequality for Sobolev mappings relative to bounded, closed and additive cochains follows. Using the results above, we answer positively to an open question by Onninen and Pankka on sharp Hölder continuity for quasiregular curves. A key tool in the continuity proof is Almgren’s isoperimetric inequality for integral currents.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Pushforward and Sobolev maps

Given Riemannian manifolds
M
and
N
, Federer–Fleming integral currents
\mathbf{I}_{k,c}(M)

[
FF60
]
, for every
k=0,1,\dots
, can be pushed forward using smooth or Lipschitz mappings
f\colon M\to N
. We prove a Sobolev analog of the statement. We use a notion of an exceptional collection of integral currents in the spirit of Fuglede
[
Fug57
]
. To this end, given a Borel function
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{+}(M)
, we say that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\rho_{0})\coloneqq\bigcup_{k\in\left\{1,\dots,\lfloor p\rfloor\right\}}

\displaystyle\left\{T\in\mathbf{I}_{k,c}(M)\colon\int_{M}\rho_{0}^{k}\,d\|T\|+\int_{M}\rho_{0}^{k-1}\,d\|\partial T\|=\infty\right\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\left\{T\in\mathbf{I}_{0,c}(M)\colon\int_{M}\rho_{0}^{0}\,d\|T\|=\infty\right\}

is
p
-exceptional
, where the conventions
0^{0}=0
and
\infty^{0}=\infty
are used.

We consider the
reduced chain complex
(\mathbf{I}_{\star,c}(\rho_{0}),\partial)
consisting of the direct sum of the
\mathbb{Z}
-modules

\mathbf{I}_{k,c}(\rho_{0})=\mathbf{I}_{k,c}(M)\setminus\mathcal{F}(\rho_{0}),\quad k\in\left\{0,1,\dots,\lfloor p\rfloor\right\}

and the usual boundary operation of currents, denoted
\partial
.

Our main result,
Theorem

1.1
below, states the existence of a chain morphism

f_{\star}\colon(\mathbf{I}_{\star,c}(\rho_{0}),\partial)\to(\mathbf{I}_{\star}(N),\partial),

induced by a Sobolev map
f\in W^{1,p}(M,N)
from
(\mathbf{I}_{\star,c}(\rho_{0}),\partial)
into the chain complex
(\mathbf{I}_{\star}(N),\partial)
of integral currents in the sense of Ambrosio–Kirchheim
[
AK00a
]
.

Proving the existence of the pushforward involves approximating Sobolev mappings by Lipschitz mappings and for this reason we first need to isometrically embed
N
into an injective Banach space, by using a Kuratowski embedding, for example. The embedding is used since there are topological obstructions for
N
-valued Lipschitz mappings being dense in the Sobolev spaces of interest, cf.
[
Bet91
,
HL03
,
Haj09
,
HS14
,
BPVS17
]
. The core of our argument involves Sobolev theory tailored for metric spaces and for mappings taking values in injective Banach spaces. In fact, replacing
N
by a complete Lipschitz manifold, or any complete metric space for that matter, poses no further challenge.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
M
is a Riemannian manifold, with or without boundary, and
N
is a complete metric space and
f\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(M,N)
for
1\leq p<\infty
.

Then there exist a Borel representative
(\widehat{f},\widehat{\rho})
of
(f,|df|)
and Borel
\widehat{\rho}_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{+}(M)
together with a chain morphism

(1.1)

\widehat{f}_{\star}\colon(\mathbf{I}_{\star,c}(\widehat{\rho}_{0}),\partial)\to(\mathbf{I}_{\star}(N),\partial)

for which

(1.2)

\|\widehat{f}_{\star}T\|(E)\leq\int_{\widehat{f}^{-1}(E)}\widehat{\rho}^{k}\,d\|T\|<\infty\quad\text{for Borel $E\subset N$},

whenever
T\in\mathbf{I}_{k,c}(\widehat{\rho}_{0})
and
k\in\left\{0,1,\dots,\lfloor p\rfloor\right\}
.

The chain morphism has the further property that for every isometric embedding
\iota\colon N\to\mathbb{V}
to
an injective Banach space
\mathbb{V}
, there is a Borel decomposition sets
(E_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
of
M
, with negligible
E_{0}\supset\left\{\rho_{0}=\infty\right\}
, and Lipschitz mappings
f_{i}\colon M\to\mathbb{V}
extending
(\iota\circ f)|_{E_{i}}
for
i\geq 1
, for which

(1.3)

\iota_{\sharp}\left(\widehat{f}_{\star}T\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(f_{i})_{\sharp}(T\llcorner E_{i})\quad\text{for every $T\in\mathbf{I}_{\star,c}(\widehat{\rho}_{0})$.}

When applying
Theorem

1.1
, we identify
\widehat{f}
with
f
and
\widehat{\rho}
with the
p
-minimal
p
-weak upper gradient
|df|
, with the understanding that
Theorem

1.1
is applied using the representatives
(\widehat{f},\widehat{\rho})
as above.

The notation
h_{\sharp}
for Lipschitz
h
refers to the usual pushforward along Lipschitz mappings, cf.
Section

2.7
, and equality (
1.3
) is intended as a pointwise convergence of the sequence of currents
\sum_{i=1}^{l}(f_{i})_{\sharp}(T\llcorner E_{i})
,
l\in\mathbb{N}
, to
\iota_{\sharp}\left(\widehat{f}_{\star}T\right)
. This technical condition, and standard locality properties of currents, guarantees that for any pair of chain maps satisfying the conclusions of
Theorem

1.1
, there is
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{+}(M)
such that the pair of chain maps are well-defined and equal in
(\mathbf{I}_{\star,c}(\rho_{0}),\partial)
. Observe that the set
E_{0}
plays no role in (
1.3
).

The statement above uses the integral currents of Ambrosio and Kirchheim, while the Sobolev theory follows the upper gradient approach due to Heinonen and Koskela
[
HK98
,
Sha00
,
HKST01
]
. We recall that in Riemannian manifolds, the classes of integral currents in the sense of Federer–Fleming and compactly supported integral currents in the sense of Ambrosio–Kirchheim coincide, cf.
[
AK00a
, Theorem 11.1]
, as do the upper gradient and Reshetnyak approaches to metric-valued Sobolev theory, cf.
[
Res97
,
Sha00
,
HKST01
,
HKST15
]
.

1.2.
Pullback of cochains

It is well-understood that Sobolev maps between Riemannian manifolds induce a
pullback

f^{\star}
of bounded differential forms, defined using the weak differential of Sobolev mappings.

The pullback
f^{\star}
induces a chain morphism from the (restricted) de Rham complex of bounded and smooth differential forms to the de Rham complex of Sobolev differential forms; various authors have considered cohomology theories based on the Sobolev complexes, see, for example,
[
DS89
,
IM93
,
Pan08
,
HIMO08
,
GT10
,
KP19
]
.
Theorem

1.1
leads to a similar theory on the homology level.

We establish a pullback version of
Theorem

1.1
in the metric setting, partially motivated by the Riemannian results above. We formulate the pullback using the metric cochains due to Rajala and Wenger
[
RW13
]
.

Given a metric space
N
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider a
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbf{I}_{k}(N)
. We say that

(1.4)

\Omega\colon\mathcal{C}\to[-\infty,\infty]

is a
subadditive cochain
if
\Omega(0)=0
and

|\Omega(T)|\leq|\Omega(T+S)|+|\Omega(S)|\quad\text{for every $T,S\in\mathcal{C}$.}

If also
\Omega(T+S)=\Omega(T)+\Omega(S)
whenever each term is finite, we say that
\Omega
is
additive
. In case
\Omega(T)
is always finite, we say that
\Omega
is
finite
.

A subadditive
\Omega
has an
upper norm

\rho\colon N\to[0,\infty]
if
\rho
is Borel and

\quad|\Omega(T)|\leq\int_{N}\rho\,d\|T\|\quad\text{for all $T\in\mathcal{C}$.}

It is crucial for us later, see
Theorem

1.5
below, that the mass measure
\|T\|
is defined in the Ambrosio–Kirchheim sense. Following
[
RW13
]
, we denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\mathcal{C})
the collection of all
subadditive
cochains with a bounded upper norm.

Using the notation from
Theorem

1.1
, we set

(1.5)

f^{\star}\Omega(T)\coloneqq\Omega(f_{\star}T),\quad(T,\Omega)\in\mathbf{I}_{k,c}(\widehat{\rho}_{0})\times\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\mathbf{I}_{k}(N)),\quad k\in\left\{0,\dots,\lfloor p\rfloor\right\}.

By
Theorem

1.1
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 1.2

(Pullback)
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold, with or without boundary,
N
a complete metric space and
f\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(M,N)
,
1\leq p<\infty
. If
k\in\left\{0,1,\dots,\lfloor p\rfloor\right\}
and
\Omega\in\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\mathbf{I}_{k}(N))
has a bounded upper norm
\rho
, the finite subadditive cochain

f^{\star}\Omega\colon\mathbf{I}_{k,c}(\widehat{\rho}_{0})\to\mathbb{R}

has an upper norm
(\rho\circ f)|df|^{k}\in\mathcal{L}^{p/k}_{loc}(M)
. In case
\Omega
is additive, so is the pullback.

We use the convention
p/0=\infty
and recall the notation

|df|^{0}=\left\{\begin{split}&0,\quad&&\text{when $|df|=0$,}\\
&\infty,\quad&&\text{when $|df|=\infty$,}\\
&1,\quad&&\text{otherwise}.\end{split}\right.

We refine the conclusion of
Corollary

1.2
in the case that the
coboundary
of
\Omega\in\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\mathbf{I}_{k}(N))
is bounded. To this end, given a
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{I}_{k+1}(N)
with
\partial\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{C}
, we call the map

\delta\Omega\colon\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R},\quad S\mapsto\Omega(\partial S),

the
coboundary
of
\Omega
(relative to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
). We denote
\Omega\in\mathcal{W}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
in case
\Omega
and its coboundary have a bounded upper norm. We say that
\Omega
is
closed
(relative to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
) if the coboundary is identically zero.

When
N
is a Riemannian manifold, we denote
\Omega\in\mathcal{W}_{\alpha,\beta,loc}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
in case
\Omega
has a locally
\alpha
-integrable upper norm,
\partial\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{C}
, and
\delta\Omega
has a locally
\beta
-integrable upper norm.
Corollary

1.2
implies the following.

Corollary 1.3

(Sobolev cochains)
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold, with or without boundary,
N
a complete metric space and
f\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(M,N)
,
1\leq p<\infty
. If
k\in\left\{0,1,\dots,\lfloor p\rfloor\right\}
and
k\leq\mathrm{dim}(M)
, there is an
\mathbb{R}
-linear pullback

f^{\star}\colon\mathcal{W}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbf{I}_{k}(N),\mathbf{I}_{k+1}(N))\to\mathcal{W}_{p/k,p/(k+1),loc}(\mathbf{I}_{k,c}(\widehat{\rho}_{0}),\mathbf{I}_{k+1,c}(\widehat{\rho}_{0})).

In case
k=\mathrm{dim}(M)
, the pullback is always closed.

Given any
k
as in
Corollary

1.3
, any element
\Omega
in the codomain of
f^{\star}
admits a subadditive extension to

(1.6)

\mathcal{W}_{p/k,p/(k+1),loc}(\mathbf{I}_{k,c}(M),\mathbf{I}_{k+1,c}(M)),

simply by extending
\Omega
and its coboundary as infinity to the set where they are not yet defined. The spaces in (
1.6
) for
k\in\left\{0,1\dots,\lfloor p\rfloor\right\}
are the collection of (local) Sobolev cochains in the sense of
[
RW13
, Section 3.2]
. When
p=\mathrm{dim}(M)
, the direct sum of the spaces over
k\in\left\{0,1,\dots,\mathrm{dim}(M)\right\}
, endowed with the coboundary operation, provides a reduced cochain complex that is a
quasiconformal invariant
of
M
. This is readily seen from
Theorem

1.1
and
Corollary

1.2
.

When
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
p\in[n,\infty)
,
k\in\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}
, and
\Omega
is an additive element from the space in (
1.6
), the action of
\Omega
on polyhedral chains is uniquely determined by a Sobolev differential form
\tau\in\mathcal{W}_{d,loc}^{p/k,p/(k+1)}(\Lambda^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
; this follows from the Wolfe-type representation result
[
PRW15
, Theorem 1.1]
. This provides a concrete representation of the pullback in this special case. In fact, if
f
is Lipschitz, the form can be chosen to be a flat Whitney form, see
[
PRW15
, Corollary 1.2]
.

1.3.
Closed cochains and an isoperimetric inequality for Sobolev maps

Theorem

1.1
immediately implies the following isoperimetric inequality for Sobolev mappings.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Suppose that
M
is an oriented
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, with or without boundary,
f\in W^{1,n}_{loc}(M,N)
, and
\Omega\in\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\mathbf{I}_{n}(N))
is a closed and additive cochain with a bounded upper norm
\rho(x)=C
,
x\in N
, for some
C>0
.

If
N
supports isoperimetric inequalities of Euclidean type with dimension
n
and constant
A^{\prime}
, and dimension
n-1
and constant
A
in the sense of (
6.1
), respectively, then

(1.7)

\displaystyle|\Omega(f_{\star}[\widetilde{M}])|

\displaystyle\leq C\inf_{S\in\mathbf{I}_{n+1}(N)}M(f_{\star}[\widetilde{M}]+\partial S)\leq AC\left(\int_{\partial\widetilde{M}}|df|^{n-1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}

whenever
[\widetilde{M}]\in\mathbf{I}_{n,c}(\widehat{\rho}_{0})
.

We consider top-dimensional compact Lipschitz submanifolds
\widetilde{M}
of
M
as integral currents
[\widetilde{M}]
, with orientation induced from
M
.

When deriving
Corollary

1.4
, the
n
-dimensional isoperimetric inequality is only applied to deduce that every integral
n
-cycle is the boundary of some
\mathbf{I}_{n+1}(N)
, which, together with the closedness of
\Omega
, allows us to bound the left-hand side of (
1.7
) by the minimal mass in the homology class of
f_{\star}[\widetilde{M}]
. The
(n-1)
-dimensional inequality gives the quantitative upper bound in (
1.7
).

Corollary

1.4
can be considered a generalization of the classical isoperimetric inequality for Sobolev mappings proved by Reshetnyak, cf.
[
Res66b
]
or
[
Res89
, Lemma 1.2, p. 80]
. Indeed,
[
Res89
, Lemma 1.2]
follows from (
1.7
) and Almgren’s sharp isoperimetric inequality
[
Alm86
, Theorem 10]
when
N=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Omega
is the cochain induced by the Riemannian volume form of
N
.
Corollary

1.4
plays a key role in our main application,
Theorem

1.5
below.

The isoperimetric inequality for integral currents on Euclidean spaces was initially proved by Federer and Fleming
[
FF60
]
, the sharp isoperimetry constant obtained by Almgren
[
Alm86
]
. These results were later extended to the Banach and metric settings by Gromov and Wenger
[
Gro83
,
Wen07
]
.

1.4.
Quasiregular curves

We consider a complete Riemannian manifold
N
and a closed and bounded differential form
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
for some
n\leq\mathrm{dim}(N)
.

Given an oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold
M
, we say that
f\colon M\to N
is a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve
if
f\in W^{1,n}_{loc}(M,N)
and

(1.8)

(\|\omega\|\circ f)|df|^{n}\leq K\,{\star}f^{\star}\omega\quad\text{$\mathcal{H}^{n}$-almost everywhere,}

where

(1.9)

\|\omega\|(x)=\sup\left\{\omega(v)\colon\text{$v$ is a unit simple $n$-vector on $T_{x}N$}\right\}

is the
comass
of
\omega
, and
{\star}f^{\star}\omega
is the Hodge star of the pullback
f^{\star}\omega
, defined pointwise using the weak differential of
f
.

In contrast to the definition of quasiregular curves in
[
Pan20
]
, we do not assume
a priori
that
f
is
continuous
or that
\omega
is
strongly nonvanishing
(i.e.
\|\omega\|(x)\neq 0
for every
x\in N
). In fact, in our applications, we only assume that
\|\omega\|(x)
is bounded from above by some
C>0
everywhere and from below by
c>0
in the image of
f
. These assumptions are motivated by the calibrated geometries of Harvey and Lawson
[
HL82
]
.

There are various contexts at which quasiregular curves naturally occur. Standard applications include pseudoholomorphic curves on Kähler manifolds, cf.
[
Gro85
,
Don96
,
Gro07
]
. Further examples can be obtained from the classical theory of quasiregular mappings
[
Res89
,
Ric93
,
IM01
,
AIM09
]
. See
[
Smi11
,
CKM20
,
Hei23
,
HPP23
]
for related work.

Reshetnyak proved that every
K
-quasiregular mapping from
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
into
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is locally
(1/K)
-Hölder continuous, cf.
[
Res66a
,
Res89
]
. This was later extended to
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curves for nonzero constant-coefficient
n
-forms
\omega
by Onninen and Pankka in
[
OP21
, Theorem 1.1]
with a smaller Hölder exponent. We prove the following sharp version of
[
OP21
, Theorem 1.1]
, answering positively to a question posed by Onninen and Pankka.

Theorem 1.5
.

Every
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve
f\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
for open
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and nonzero constant-coefficient
n
-form
\omega
has a locally
(1/K)
-Hölder continuous representative.

We outline the proof of
Theorem

1.5
. First, the constant-coefficient form
\omega
in
Theorem

1.5
induces a closed and additive cochain
\Omega\in\mathcal{W}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbf{I}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),\mathbf{I}_{n+1}(\mathbb{R}^{m}))
. In fact, the definitions of mass due to Ambrosio and Kirchheim and of upper norms for cochains allow us to identify the comass
x\mapsto\rho(x)=\|\omega\|(x)
from (
1.9
) as an upper norm of
\Omega
after which we may apply (
1.7
) and (
1.8
); the results are applicable because the pullback
f^{\star}\Omega
coincides with the cochain induced by the pullback
f^{\star}\omega
, cf.
Lemma

5.3
. These observations and the sharp isoperimetric inequality
[
Alm86
, Theorem 10]
reduce
Theorem

1.5
to a standard argument due to Morrey. We also prove the following.

Corollary 1.6
.

Every
1
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve
f\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
for open
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and nonzero constant-coefficient n-form
\omega
has a representative that is locally Lipschitz and
n
-harmonic.

A simple argument, using the closedness of the cochain induced by
\omega
, shows that if
f
is as in
Corollary

1.6
, the pushforward integral currents
f_{\star}[\widetilde{M}]
, for compact and oriented Lipschitz submanifolds
\widetilde{M}
, are integral currents that are calibrated in the sense of Harvey and Lawson, cf.
[
HL82
, Section 4]
. In particular, such pushforward integral currents are mass-minimizing in their homology class. A similar idea is used to prove the
n
-harmonicity of the continuous representative of
f
.

1.5.
Further observations

The proof of
Theorem

1.5
, based on
Theorem

1.1
and Corollaries
1.2
and
1.4
, allows us to generalize the modulus of continuity estimate to various settings. Indeed, the constant-coefficient
n
-forms in
Theorem

1.5
can be replaced by closed flat Whitney forms satisfying
\|\omega\|(x)\geq c
pointwise on the image of
f
, in which case
K
in the modulus of continuity estimate ought to be replaced by
Q=K\|\omega\|_{\infty}/c
, where
\|\omega\|_{\infty}
is the
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-essential supremum of the comass of
\omega
. We later formulate a similar extension to the class of closed and bounded additive cochains.

A quantitative local Hölder continuity as in
Theorem

1.5
can be obtained in various contexts, e.g. on any compact Lipschitz manifold
N
, or more generally, complete metric targets supporting isoperimetric inequalities of Euclidean type for the dimensions
n-1
and
n
up to some mass scale; see
Section

6
for the precise definition. Targets satisfying these assumptions include complete Riemannian manifolds with an upper bound on sectional curvature. Subtler examples are obtained by considering CAT(
\kappa
)-spaces (see e.g.
[
Wen07
]
) or Heisenberg group of high enough dimension, endowed with any standard left-invariant subRiemannian distance, e.g. the Korányi distance, cf.
[
BWY21
]
.

As far as the author is aware, the (local) Hölder continuity is new already for quasiregular mappings taking values in complete Riemannian manifolds with a sectional curvature upper bound, see
[
GHP19
]
and the recent monograph
[
Kan21
]
for further discussion.

We elaborate on these topics in
Section

6
.

2.
Preliminaries

We introduce the notation and basic definitions we need during the manuscript.

2.1.
Lipschitz mappings

Given metric spaces
N
and
Y
,
\LIP(N,Y)
refers to the Lipschitz mappings
h\colon N\rightarrow Y
. We say that
h
is Lipschitz whenever

\LIP(h)\coloneqq\inf\left\{L>0\colon d(h(x),h(y))\leq Ld(x,y)\,\text{for every $(x,y)\in N\times N$}\right\}<\infty.

When
\LIP(h)\leq L
, we say that
h
is
L
-Lipschitz.

The class
\LIP_{b}(N,Y)
refers to the collection of all
h\in\LIP(N,Y)
whose image is a bounded subset of the image. When
Y
is a Banach space and
f\in\LIP(N,Y)
, the set
\overline{\left\{f\neq 0\right\}}
is the
support
of
f
. The class
\LIP_{bs}(N,Y)
consists of all Lipschitz mappings with
bounded support
, i.e. mappings whose support has finite diameter. In particular,
\LIP_{bs}(N,Y)\subset\LIP_{b}(N,Y)
. When
Y=\mathbb{R}
we omit
Y
from the notations above.

Given any metric spaces
N
and
Y
,
f\in\LIP(N,Y)
and
x\in N
, we denote

\lip(f)(x)\coloneqq\limsup_{x\neq y\rightarrow x}\frac{d(f(x),f(y))}{d(x,y)},

and call
\lip(f)(x)
the
pointwise Lipschitz constant
of
f
at
x
. The corresponding function is called the
pointwise Lipschitz function
of
f
.

2.2.
Injective Banach spaces

Given a nonempty index set
N
, we consider the space

L^{\infty}(N)\coloneqq\left\{a\colon N\to\mathbb{R}\colon\sup_{x\in N}|a(x)|<\infty\right\}

endowed with componentwise addition and summation together with the supremum norm
\|a\|=\sup_{x\in N}|a(x)|
. Then
L^{\infty}(N)
becomes a Banach space. We typically denote
(a_{x})_{x\in N}
instead of
a\colon N\to\mathbb{R}
.

When
N
is a metric space, we use the
Kuratowski embedding

\iota\colon N\to L^{\infty}(N)
to isometrically embed a metric space
N
into
L^{\infty}(N)
: fix
x_{0}\in N
, consider
f_{y}(x)\coloneqq d(x,y)-d(y,x_{0})
, and set
\iota(x)=(f_{y}(x))_{y\in N}
. We often identify
N
with its image
\iota(N)
. Observe that if
N^{\prime}\subset N
is a dense subset, we may define an isometric embedding of
N
into
L^{\infty}(N^{\prime})
using the formula
\iota^{\prime}(x)=(f_{y}(x))_{y\in N^{\prime}}
as above. In particular, when
N
is separable, this idea yields an isometric embedding into
\ell^{\infty}\coloneqq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{N})
.

A Banach space
\mathbb{V}
is
injective
if whenever
\mathbb{W}
and
\mathbb{H}
are Banach spaces,
\iota\colon\mathbb{W}\to\mathbb{H}
is a linear isometric embedding, and there is a bounded linear map
L\colon\mathbb{W}\to\mathbb{V}
, then there is a linear map
\widetilde{L}\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{V}
with
\widetilde{L}\circ\iota=L
, so that the operator norms of
L
and
\widetilde{L}
coincide.

Lemma 2.1
.

Given any nonempty index set
N
, the space
L^{\infty}(N)
is injective. Furthermore, if
\mathbb{V}
is injective and
N
is the closed unit ball of
\mathbb{V}^{*}
, there is a linear isometric embedding
\iota\colon\mathbb{V}\to L^{\infty}(N)
and a linear
P\colon L^{\infty}(N)\to\mathbb{V}
so that
P
has unit operator norm and
P\circ\iota
is the identity.

Proof.

Given an index set
N
, we endow it with the topology making every set open. Then
L^{\infty}(N)
can be linearly isometrically isomorphically identified with the space of continuous functions
C(\beta N)
for the Stone–Čech compactification
\beta N
of
N
. Now Hahn–Banach theorem implies the claim for
C(\beta N)
, see
[
Lac74
, Chapter 3, Section 11, Theorem 3, p. 87]
. Thus
L^{\infty}(N)
is injective.

In case
\mathbb{V}
is injective, we denote by
N
the closed unit ball of the dual
\mathbb{V}^{*}
and consider the linear isometric embedding
\iota(v)=(z(v))_{z\in N}
. The existence of
P
follows by extending
L=\iota^{-1}
from
\mathbb{W}=\iota(\mathbb{V})
using the definition of injectivity of
\mathbb{V}
.
∎

On the one hand,
Lemma

2.1
shows that
L^{\infty}(N)
for arbitrary index sets
N\neq\emptyset
are injective. On the other hand,
Lemma

2.1
implies the following key lemma.

Lemma 2.2

(McShane for
L
-Lipschitz mappings)
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be an injective Banach space,
K\subset M
a set in a metric space
M
, and
L\geq 0
. Let
\LIP_{b,L}(K,\mathbb{V})\subset\LIP_{b}(K,\mathbb{V})
denote the collection of
L
-Lipschitz mappings. Then there is an extension operator

\mathcal{E}\colon\LIP_{b,L}(K,\mathbb{V})\to\LIP_{b}(M,\mathbb{V})

satisfying the following:

•

for each
F\in\LIP_{b,L}(K,\mathbb{V})
, the extension
\mathcal{E}(F)
is
L
-Lipschitz;

•

If
F,G\in\LIP_{b,L}(K,\mathbb{V})
, then

\sup_{x\in K}|F(x)-G(x)|=\sup_{x\in M}|\mathcal{E}(F)(x)-\mathcal{E}(G)(x)|.

Proof.

Lemma

2.1
implies that it suffices to prove the claim when
\mathbb{V}=L^{\infty}(N)
, for arbitrary
N\neq\emptyset
. To verify the special case, consider an
L
-Lipschitz
F\in\LIP_{b}(K,\mathbb{V})
and identify
F
with
(f_{z})_{z\in N}
for
f_{z}\colon K\to\mathbb{R}
.

Every
f_{z}
is
L
-Lipschitz and
|f_{z}(x)|\leq|F(x)|
for all
(x,z)\in K\times N
, so we may consider the McShane extension

\mathcal{E}(f_{z})(x)\coloneqq\inf_{y\in K}\left\{f_{z}(y)+Ld(y,x)\right\},\quad x\in M.

Then
\mathcal{E}(f_{z})
extends
f_{z}
, is
L
-Lipschitz, and if
g\in\LIP_{b,L}(K)
, we have

(2.1)

\sup_{x\in M}|\mathcal{E}(f_{z})(x)-\mathcal{E}(g)(x)|=\sup_{y\in K}|f_{z}(y)-g(y)|.

We define
\mathcal{E}(F)(x)\coloneqq\left(\mathcal{E}(f_{z})(x)\right)_{z\in N}
for every
x\in M
,
observing that
\mathcal{E}(F)
is an
L
-Lipschitz extension of
F
. Then (
2.1
) implies the claim.
∎

2.3.
Measure theory

Given a metric space
M
endowed with a(n outer) measure
\mu
, a set
E\subset M
is
\mu
-negligible
if
\mu(E)=0
. We say that a property holds
almost everywhere
if the property is true for every point
x\in M\setminus E
for a
\mu
-negligible set
E
. The
\mu
-measurable sets are defined using the Carathéodory criterion.

In case
N
is a metric space, we say that the image of
f\colon M\to N
is
essentially separable
if there exists a
\mu
-negligible set
E\subset M
so that
f(M\setminus E)
is separable. We say that
f\colon M\to N
is
\mu
-measurable
if the image of
f
is essentially separable and
f^{-1}(U)
is
\mu
-measurable for every open
U\subset N
.

A mapping is
Borel
if its image is essentially separable and the preimage of every open set is Borel. We say that
\mu
is a
Borel measure
if every Borel set is
\mu
-measurable and for every
\mu
-measurable set
E\subset M
, there is Borel
B\supset E
with
\mu(B)=\mu(E)
. The measures we consider are Borel measures.

We say that
\mu
is
locally finite
if
M
can be covered by open sets at which
\mu
is finite. We say that
\mu
is
finite
if
\mu(M)<\infty
.

Given two outer measures,
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
, we say that
\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}

in the sense of measures
if
\mu_{1}(E)\leq\mu_{2}(E)
for every
E\subset M
.

We often use the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{n}
for
n\in\left\{0,1,2,\dots\right\}
. The measure is normalized in such a way that
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-measure of the
n
-dimensional Euclidean unit ball is equal to the corresponding
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
\omega_{n}
.

Given a Borel map
f\colon M\to N
and a finite Borel measure
\mu
, the
pushforward

f_{\sharp}\mu
is the Borel measure defined by
f_{\sharp}\mu(E)\coloneqq\mu(f^{-1}(E))
for
E\subset N
.

2.4.
Lebesgue spaces

Given a Banach space
\mathbb{W}
, a
\mu
-measurable
f\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{W}
is an element of the space
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)
if there is
C>0
such that
|f(x)|\leq C
outside a
\mu
-negligible set. The infimum over such
C
is denoted by
\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(M;\mu)}
.

Given a locally finite
\mu
on
M
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we say that a
\mu
-measurable
f\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{W}
is an element of
\mathcal{L}^{p}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)
if

\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)}\coloneqq\left(\int_{M}|f|^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

For all
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we omit
\mathbb{W}
from the notation when
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{R}
, and the notation
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{+}(M;\mu)
refers to
everywhere
nonnegative elements of
\mathcal{L}^{p}(M;\mu)
.

We let
L^{p}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)
denote the Banach space obtained under the equivalence relation
f_{1}\sim f_{2}
defined by
\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)}=0
. The induced norm on the quotient space is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)}
.

We write
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{loc}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)
in case
M
can be covered by open sets
(U_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
for which
\chi_{U_{i}}f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)
. Here
\chi_{E}
refers to the
characteristic function
of
E
: the function being one on
E
and zero otherwise. The space
L^{p}_{loc}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)
is obtained from
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{loc}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)
by identifying elements whose difference is zero
\mu
-almost everywhere.

The function
\infty\cdot\chi_{E}
on
M
is defined by

\infty\cdot\chi_{E}(x)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{split}&\infty,\quad&&\text{when $x\in E$},\\
&0,\quad&&\text{otherwise}.\end{split}\right.

2.5.
Sobolev spaces

In this section we consider a Riemannian
n
-manifold
M
endowed with the Riemannian length distance
d
and a locally finite Borel measure
\mu
. We also consider a Banach space
\mathbb{W}
.

When defining
W^{1,p}_{loc}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)
, we follow the approach due to Heinonen and Koskela
[
HK98
]
, see also
[
Sha00
,
HKST01
,
Wil12
,
HKST15
]
. To this end, given
f\colon M\to\mathbb{W}
, we say that a Borel function
\rho\colon M\to[0,\infty]
is an
upper gradient
of
f
if

(2.2)

|f(\gamma(1))-f(\gamma(0))|\leq\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds,

for every rectifiable
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to N
, where the right-hand side is the
path integral
of
\rho
over the length measure of
\gamma
. Given that (
2.2
) is parametrization-independent, we typically assume that
\gamma
has constant speed.

We say that
\rho
is a
locally
p
-integrable
p
-weak upper gradient
of
f
if
\rho\colon M\to[0,\infty]
is Borel,
\rho\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{loc}(M)
, and there is a nonnegative Borel
\widehat{\rho}_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{loc}(M;\mu)
so that (
2.2
) holds for the triple
(f,\gamma,\rho)
for every rectifiable
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to M
satisfying
\int_{\gamma}\widehat{\rho}_{0}\,ds<\infty
. The class of locally
p
-integrable
p
-weak upper gradients defined as above coincides with the one in
[
HK98
,
HKST01
,
Wil12
,
HKST15
]
.

Whenever
f\colon M\to\mathbb{W}
has a locally
p
-integrable
p
-weak upper gradient it has a
p
-minimal one
: there is a unique element in
L^{p}_{loc}(M;\mu)
, denoted by
|df|
, such that every nonnegative Borel representative
\rho
of
|df|
is a
p
-weak upper gradient of
f
and
\rho\leq\rho^{\prime}

\mu
-almost everywhere for every locally
p
-integrable
p
-weak upper gradient
\rho^{\prime}
of
f
. Moreover, if a mapping
f_{1}\colon M\to\mathbb{W}
has a locally
p
-integrable
p
-weak upper gradient
\rho_{1}
and
f=f_{1}

\mu
-almost everywhere, then
\rho\leq\rho_{1}

\mu
-almost everywhere.

With the above observation at hand, we define the local Sobolev space

W^{1,p}_{loc}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)\subset L^{p}_{loc}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)

as the subset of equivalence classes which have a representative with a locally
p
-integrable
p
-weak upper gradient. We may also unambigously refer to the
p
-minimal
p
-weak upper gradient

|df|
of
f\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)
as an element of
L^{p}_{loc}(M;\mu)
.

For a complete metric space
N
and an isometric embedding
\iota\colon N\to\mathbb{W}
into an injective Banach space
\mathbb{V}
, we define the
local Sobolev space

W^{1,p}_{loc}(M,N;\mu)\coloneqq W^{1,p}_{loc}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)\cap\left\{f\colon f(x)\in N\,\mu\text{-almost everywhere}\right\}.

The space is independent of the embedding and
\mathbb{W}
, cf.
[
HKST15
, Section 7.1]
.

We obtain the space
W^{1,p}(M,N;\mu)\subset W^{1,p}_{loc}(M,N;\mu)
by requiring that
f\in L^{p}(M,\mathbb{W};\mu)
and
|df|\in L^{p}(M;\mu)
. Notice that the norm on the former space depends on the embedding, see
[
Haj07
,
Haj09
]
and
[
HKST15
, Sections 7.1 and 7.6]
for related discussion.

We omit
\mu
from the notation when
M
is a Riemannian
n
-manifold endowed with the Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{n}
.

We prove a key lemma used during the construction of the pushforward of currents. The interested reader can easily modify the following proof to
p
-PI spaces, i.e. complete metric spaces
M
endowed with a doubling Borel measure
\mu
and supporting a weak
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality in the sense of Heinonen and Koskela, see
[
HK98
]
for the definitions. The result also generalizes to bounded uniform domains on
p
-PI spaces due to
[
GIZ22
, Theorem 1.8, Proposition 1.9 and Proposition 1.10]
. However, we specialize the lemma to our needs.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian
n
-manifold, with or without boundary,
\mathbb{V}
be an injective Banach space, and
f\in W^{1,p}(M,\mathbb{V})
for
1\leq p<\infty
. Then there exists a sequence
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
from
\LIP_{bs}(M,\mathbb{V})
satisfying

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\|f-f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(M,\mathbb{V})}+\||df|-\lip(f_{j})\|_{L^{p}(M)}\right)<\infty.

In fact, we may assume the existence of finite-dimensional subspaces
\mathbb{W}_{j}\subset\mathbb{W}_{j+1}
such that
f_{j}
takes values in
\mathbb{W}_{j}
.

During the proof, we use the following definition and the subsequent lemmas. We say that a Banach space
\mathbb{H}
has the
metric approximation property
if for every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{H}
and every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a finite rank linear map
T\colon\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}
with operator norm at most one for which

\sup_{z\in K}|z-T(z)|\leq\epsilon.

Lemma 2.4
.

Every injective Banach space
\mathbb{V}
has the metric approximation property.

Proof of
Lemma

2.4
.

Lemma

2.1
reduces the proof to the special case
L^{\infty}(Z)
for
Z\neq\emptyset
. The claim in that case follows e.g. from
[
Kö79
, p. 258, (9)]
.
∎

We also need the following result.

Proposition 2.5

(Proposition 4.4,
[
GIZ22
]
)
.

Let
M
be a complete and separable metric space endowed with a Borel measure
\mu
that is finite on bounded sets,
\mathbb{V}
is a Banach space with the metric approximation property, and
1\leq p<\infty
.

If
f\in W^{1,p}(M,\mathbb{V};\mu)
and
\epsilon>0
, there is
h\in W^{1,p}(M,\mathbb{V})
so that

\|f-h\|_{L^{p}(M,\mathbb{V};\mu)}+\||df|-|dh|\|_{L^{p}(M;\mu)}<\epsilon

and there is a finite-dimensional subspace
\mathbb{W}\subset\mathbb{V}
so that the image of each representative of
h
satisfies
h(M\setminus E)\subset\mathbb{W}
for some
\mu
-negligible set
E\subset M
.

Proof of
Lemma

2.3
.

We apply
Lemma

2.4
and
Proposition

2.5
for
\epsilon_{j}=2^{-j-1}
. The conclusion is as follows. There is a sequence of finite-dimensional subspaces
\mathbb{W}_{j}\subset\mathbb{W}_{j+1}\subset\mathbb{V}
and
h_{j}\in W^{1,p}(M,\mathbb{V})
, taking values in
\mathbb{W}_{j}
up to a negligible set, such that

\|f-h_{j}\|_{L^{p}(M,\mathbb{V})}+\||df|-|dh_{j}|\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq 2^{-j-1}.

It follows, e.g. from
[
EBS21
, Theorem 1.9]
that
\LIP_{bs}(M)
is norm-dense in
W^{1,p}(M)
(although the conclusion in our case is classical). Given that
\mathbb{W}_{j}
have finite dimension, the norm-density of
\LIP_{bs}(M,\mathbb{W}_{j})
in
W^{1,p}(M,\mathbb{W}_{j})
follows from the corresponding real-valued case by John’s theorem and componentwise approximation; see e.g.
[
GIZ22
, Lemma 4.5]
. Now
[
EBS21
, Theorem 1.8]
implies that for
g\in\LIP_{bs}(M,\mathbb{W}_{j})
, the pointwise Lipschitz constant
\lip(g)
and
p
-minimal
p
-weak upper gradient
|dg|
coincide. Combining this with the stated norm-density shows the existence of
f_{j}\in\LIP_{bs}(M,\mathbb{W}_{j})\subset\LIP_{bs}(M,\mathbb{V})
for which

\|h_{j}-f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(M,\mathbb{V})}+\||dh_{j}|-\lip(f_{j})\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq 2^{-j-1}.

The claim follows from triangle inequality and from summing over
j
.
∎

2.6.
Support and a measure-theoretic axiom

Given a subset
E
of a metric space
N
, we say that a Borel measure
\mu
on
N
is
concentrated on
E
if
\mu(N\setminus E)=0
.

The
support
of
\mu
is the set of all
x\in N
which satisfy
\mu(B(x,r))>0
for every
r>0
. We say that
\mu
is
compactly supported
if
\mu
is
concentrated
on a compact set. In particular, the support is compact and
\mu
is concentrated on its support.

We recall that for any finite Borel measure
\mu
on
N
, the support is separable, cf.
[
Fed69
, 2.2.16]
. If
\mu
is concentrated on its support, we may consider
\mu
as a finite measure on a complete and
separable
space. Then
[
Bog07
, Theorem 7.1.7]
implies the following standard fact.

Lemma 2.6
.

If
N
is a complete metric space and
\mu
a finite Borel measure on
N
that is concentrated on its support, then

\mu(B)=\sup\left\{\mu(K)\colon B\supset K\text{ is compact}\right\}\quad\text{for every Borel set $B\subset N$.}

In other words,
\mu
is Radon.

It is clear that if a finite Borel measure
\mu
on a complete metric space is Radon, then
\mu
is concentrated on a countable union of compact sets, i.e. on a
\sigma
-compact set. Observe that every finite Borel measure concentrated on a
\sigma
-compact set is concentrated on its support. Hence the following corollary of
Lemma

2.6
holds.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
N
is a complete metric space and
\mu
a finite Borel measure on
N
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mu
is concentrated on its support;

(2)

\mu
is concentrated on a
\sigma
-compact set;

(3)

\mu
is Radon;

(4)

\mu
is concentrated on a closed and separable subset of
N
.

We recall the following related fact.

Lemma 2.8

(2.2.16,
[
Fed69
]
)
.

If
N
is a complete metric space which contains a dense subset whose cardinality is an Ulam number, then every finite Borel measure on
N
is concentrated on its support.

We refer the reader to
[
Fed69
, 2.1.6]
for the definition of Ulam numbers but recall that e.g. all accessible cardinals are Ulam numbers. In particular,
\ell^{\infty}
satisfies the assumptions of
Lemma

2.8
.

As Ambrosio and Kirchheim remark, see
[
AK00a
, p. 12]
, when developing their theory of metric currents in
every
complete metric space, they make use of the simplifying axiom, consistent with standard ZFC set theory, that the cardinality of
every set
is an Ulam number. We use the same axiom. They remark that even without the axiom, an equivalent theory is obtained in complete metric spaces whose finite Borel measures are concentrated on
\sigma
-compact sets; recall also
Lemma

2.7
. To see the relevance of the axiom for our main theorem, see
Remark

3.4
.

2.7.
Ambrosio-Kirchheim currents

We recall the basic definitions and properties of Ambrosio–Kirchheim currents for any complete metric space
N
.

2.7.1.
Currents

A
0
-current
is any linear function
T\colon\LIP_{b}(N)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
for which there is a finite Borel measure
\mu
with

(2.3)

|T(g)|\leq\int_{N}|g|\,d\mu\quad\text{for every $g\in\LIP_{b}(N)$.}

If
k\geq 1
, a
k
-current
is a function
T\colon\LIP_{b}(N)\times\LIP(N,\mathbb{R}^{k})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following axioms:

(1)

(multilinearity):
(g,h)\mapsto T(g,h)
is multilinear with respect to
g
and the components of
h
;

(2)

(
\text{weak}^{\star}
-continuity): for every
g\in\LIP_{b}(N)
and every
h_{j}\in\LIP(N,\mathbb{R}^{k})
converging to
h\in\LIP(N,\mathbb{R}^{k})
pointwise and
\sup_{j}\LIP(h_{j})<\infty
, we have

T(g,h)=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}T(g,h_{j});

(3)

(locality):
T(g,h)=0
if some component of
h
is constant in an open neighbourhood of
\left\{g\neq 0\right\}
;

(4)

(finite mass): there is a finite Borel measure
\mu
on
N
for which

(2.4)

|T(g,h)|\leq\int_{N}|g|\,d\mu

whenever
g\in\LIP_{b}(N)
and
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{k})\in\LIP(N;\mathbb{R}^{k})
has
1
-Lipschitz components.

2.7.2.
Mass

When
k=0
or
k\geq 1
and
T
is a
k
-current, the
mass measure

\|T\|
is the smallest measure satisfying (
2.3
) or (
2.4
), respectively. We denote

T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}(N)\quad\text{and}\quad M(T)\coloneqq\|T\|(N),

where
M(T)
is the
mass
of
T
. We also define
\mathbf{M}_{-1}(N)=\left\{0\right\}
to ease later notations.

The space
\mathbf{M}_{k}(N)
becomes a vector space when endowed with addition and scalar product defined pointwise. It becomes a Banach space when endowed with the norm
(T,S)\mapsto M(T-S)
.

2.7.3.
Boundary

We define the
boundary

\partial T
of a
k
-current
T
: When
k=0
or
k=1
, respectively, we set
\partial T\equiv 0
and

\partial T(g)\coloneqq T(1,g)\quad\text{for every $g\in\LIP_{b}(N)$.}

Finally, when
k\geq 2
,

\partial T(g,h)\coloneqq T(1,(g,h))\quad\text{for every $(g,h)\in\LIP_{b}(N)\times\LIP(N,\mathbb{R}^{k-1})$.}

2.7.4.
Basic properties

Any
T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}(N)
can be uniquely extended to
\mathcal{L}^{1}(N;\|T\|)\times\LIP(N,\mathbb{R}^{k})
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

(continuity):
\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}T(g_{i},h_{i})=T(g,h)
whenever
g_{i}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(N;\|T\|)
converge to
g
in
\mathcal{L}^{1}(N;\|T\|)
and
h_{i}\rightarrow h
pointwise with
\sup\LIP(h_{i})<\infty
.

(2)

(strong locality):
T(g,h)=0
if
\left\{g\neq 0\right\}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}B_{i}
with Borel
B_{i}
such that at least one of the components of
h
vanishes at each
B_{i}
.

See
[
AK00a
, Theorem 3.5]
for the proof.

Given a Borel set
E\subset N
, the
restriction

T\llcorner{E}
of a current
T
is defined as follows:

T\llcorner{E}(g,h)=T(\chi_{E}g,h)\quad\text{for every $(g,h)\in\LIP_{b}(N)\times\LIP(N,\mathbb{R}^{k})$}.

Observe that the value
T\llcorner{E}(g,h)
depends only on the values of
g
and
h
in the Borel set
E
, by the strong locality of
T
. That is, if
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime}),(g,h)\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(N;\|T\|)\times\LIP(N,\mathbb{R}^{k})
coincide on
E
, we have
T\llcorner{E}(g,h)=T\llcorner{E}(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
.

2.7.5.
Pushforward

Given a Lipschitz mapping
f\colon N\rightarrow Y
and
T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}(N)
, the
pushforward

f_{\sharp}T
is defined as follows:

f_{\sharp}T(g,h)\coloneqq T(g\circ f,h\circ f)\quad\text{for every $(g,h)\in\LIP_{b}(Y)\times\LIP(Y,\mathbb{R}^{k})$}.

We observe the following: When
f\colon N\to Y
is Lipschitz for a complete metric space
Y
, we have

(2.5)

\|f_{\sharp}T\|\leq f_{\sharp}(\lip(f)^{k}\|T\|)\quad\text{for every $T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}(N)$ and $k\geq 0$,}

in the sense of measures.

For
k=0
, (
2.5
) is clear, so we assume
k\geq 1
. Inequality (
2.5
) follows by observing that given
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{k})\in\LIP(N,\mathbb{R}^{k})
, we have

\displaystyle|(f_{\sharp}T)(g,h)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{N}(|g|\circ f)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\lip(h_{i}\circ f)\right)\,d\|T\|\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\lip(h_{i}\circ f)

\displaystyle\leq\LIP(h_{i})\lip(f)\quad\text{for each $i=1,2,\dots k$}.

See
[
RW13
, Lemma 3.10, Eq. (3.5)]
for a proof of the first inequality; the proof applies the fact that the mass measure of
T
is concentrated on its support. Notice the slight difference in notation between the cited Lemma and the first inequality above. The second inequality is elementary, so (
2.5
) is immediate from the definition of mass.

2.7.6.
Subclasses

When
k\geq 1
and
(T,\partial T)\in\mathbf{M}_{k}(N)\times\mathbf{M}_{k-1}(N)
, we say that
T
is a
normal
k
-current
and denote
T\in\mathbf{N}_{k}(N)
. Observe also that
\partial(\partial T)\equiv 0
for every
T\in\mathbf{N}_{k}(N)
by the locality axiom of currents. When
\partial T=0
, we say that
T
is a
k
-cycle
. Also,
\mathbf{N}_{0}(N)=\mathbf{M}_{0}(N)
, i.e. every
0
-current is a
0
-cycle and
\mathbf{N}_{-1}(N)=\left\{0\right\}
.

We say that
T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}(N)
is
k
-rectifiable
if
\|T\|
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{k}
and there is a sequence of Borel sets
B_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
and bi-Lipschitz maps
f_{i}\colon B_{i}\rightarrow f(B_{i})\subset N
for which

\|T\|(N\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}(B_{i}))=0.

As an example, if
\theta\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k};\mathcal{H}^{k})
and
k\geq 1
, then

\left[\theta\right](g,h)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}g(x)\mathrm{det}(dh)(x)\theta(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{k},\quad\text{$(g,h)\in\LIP_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\times\LIP(\mathbb{R}^{k},\mathbb{R}^{k})$,}

defines a
k
-rectifiable current. Here
\mathrm{det}(dh)
is the usual
determinant
of the differential
dh
.

A
k
-rectifiable
current is
integer rectifiable
if for every Lipschitz
f\colon N\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
and for every open set
A\subset N
, there exists an integer-valued
\theta\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k};\mathcal{H}^{k})
for which
f_{\sharp}(T\llcorner{A})=\left[\theta\right]
. We denote such currents by
\mathcal{I}_{k}(N)
, including
\mathcal{I}_{-1}(N)=\left\{0\right\}
. We recall a representation result of integer-rectifiable currents in
Section

5
.

An
integral
k
-current
refers to any
T\in\mathcal{I}_{k}(N)\cap\mathbf{N}_{k}(N)
with
\partial T\in\mathcal{I}_{k-1}(N)
, the collection of which we denote by
\mathbf{I}_{k}(N)
.

The
support
of
T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}(N)
is the support of
\|T\|
as a measure. We say that
T
is
compactly supported
, and denote
T\in\mathbf{M}_{k,c}(N)
, when
\|T\|
is compactly supported, i.e. concentrated on a compact set. We also denote
\mathbf{N}_{k,c}(N)\coloneqq\mathbf{M}_{k,c}(N)\cap\mathbf{N}_{k}(N)
and
\mathbf{I}_{k,c}(N)\coloneqq\mathbf{M}_{k,c}(N)\cap\mathbf{I}_{k}(N)
.

2.7.7.
Extension to locally complete spaces

We say that a metric space
N
is
locally complete
if every point has an open neighbourhood whose closure is complete. For such an
N
and
k\geq 1
, we define
\mathbf{M}_{k,c}(N)
as the collection of all
T\colon\LIP_{b}(N)\times\LIP(N;\mathbb{R}^{k})\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying axioms (1) to (4) as above, with the further assumption that some finite Borel measure
\mu
satisfying (
2.4
) is concentrated on a compact set. When
k=0
, the original definition of a
k
-current is modified by requiring that some measure satisfying (
2.3
) is concentrated on a compact set. The results and notation above extend to the subclasses of
\mathbf{M}_{k,c}(N)
.

2.7.8.
Exceptional collections

We define a notion of exceptional collections of normal currents. The following notion is in parts motivated by the modulus of surface families considered by Fuglede
[
Fug57
]
; see also
[
RW13
,
KP22
]
.

Consider a locally complete metric space
M
endowed with a locally finite Borel measure
\mu
. Given
p\in[1,\infty)
and a Borel
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{+}(M;\mu)
, we say that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\rho_{0})\coloneqq\bigcup_{k\in\left\{1,\dots,\lfloor p\rfloor\right\}}

\displaystyle\left\{T\in\mathbf{N}_{k,c}(M)\colon\int_{N}\rho_{0}^{k}\,d\|T\|+\int_{N}\rho_{0}^{k-1}\,d\|\partial T\|=\infty\right\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\left\{T\in\mathbf{N}_{0,c}(M)\colon\int_{N}\rho_{0}^{0}\,d\|T\|=\infty\right\}

is
p
-exceptional. A countable union of
p
-exceptional collections is contained in a
p
-exceptional collection and since every element of
\mathcal{F}
is compactly supported, an equivalent definition of
p
-exceptionality is obtained if we only require that
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{loc}(M;\mu)
and
\rho_{0}
is a nonnegative Borel function. We consider the direct sum

\mathbf{N}_{\star,c}(\rho_{0})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\mathbf{N}_{k,c}(M)\setminus\mathcal{F}(\rho_{0})

of vector spaces over
\mathbb{R}
, endowed with the usual boundary operation.

We say that
\mathbf{N}_{\star,c}(\rho_{0})
is a
common refinement
of
\mathbf{N}_{\star,c}(\rho_{1})
and
\mathbf{N}_{\star,c}(\rho_{2})
, for Borel
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{+}(M)
, if
\mathbf{N}_{\star,c}(\rho_{0})\subset\mathbf{N}_{\star,c}(\rho_{1})\cap\mathbf{N}_{\star,c}(\rho_{2})
.

When we consider integral currents, we simply replace
\mathbf{N}
by
\mathbf{I}
in the definitions of this subsection. Notice, however, that
\mathbf{I}_{\star,c}(\rho_{0})
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-module instead of a vector space over
\mathbb{R}
.

3.
Currents and Sobolev maps

For the duration of this section, we consider a compact Riemannian
n
-manifold
M
, with or without boundary,
1\leq p<\infty
and an injective Banach space
\mathbb{V}
. We endow
M
with the Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{n}
. We recall that
M
is a
p
-PI space, see
[
HK98
, Section 6]
.

The goal of this section is to simplify the proof of
Theorem

4.1
in
Section

4
. In particular, we establish some terminology and notation in this section and establish the existence of a
precise
representative of any given Sobolev equivalence class taking values in an injective Banach space.

We recall the following consequence of the
p
-PI property.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold, with or without boundary. Then, for every injective Banach space
\mathbb{W}
and every
H\in W^{1,p}(M,\mathbb{W})
for
1\leq p<\infty
, there is a decomposition
(B_{i},H_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
defined as follows: the collection
\left\{B_{i}\right\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
is a Borel decomposition of
M
,
B_{0}
has negligible measure, and, for every
i\geq 1
,
H_{i}
is Lipschitz and an extension of
H|_{B_{i}}
, with
B_{i}
having positive measure.

We refer to such a decomposition
(B_{i},H_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
as a
Lusin–Lipschitz decomposition
of
H
. There are no constraints on
H_{0}
. The proof is based on the existence of Hajłasz gradients
[
Haj96
]
for Banach-valued Sobolev mappings.

Proof.

It suffices to observe that every
H\in W^{1,p}(M,\mathbb{W})
has a
Hajłasz
gradient in weak-
\mathcal{L}^{p}(M)
; see, for example,
[
HKST15
, Theorem 8.1.49]
. That is, there exist a nonnegative Borel
g
in weak-
\mathcal{L}^{p}(M)
and a negligible Borel set
N_{0}\subset M
such that

(3.1)

|H(x)-H(y)|\leq d(x,y)(g(x)+g(y))\quad\text{for every $x,y\in M\setminus N_{0}$.}

We find a Borel decomposition of
M\setminus N_{0}
into a negligible set
C_{0}
and a countable number of Borel sets
C_{i}\subset\left\{g\leq\lambda_{i}\right\}
for some
\lambda_{i}\in(0,\infty)
, with positive measure, for
i\in\left\{1,2,\dots\right\}
. We define the set
B_{0}=C_{0}\cup N_{0}
and
B_{i}=C_{i}
for
i\geq 1
.

Now
H|_{B_{i}}
is Lipschitz for every
i\geq 1
by (
3.1
), so the claim follows by extending
H|_{B_{i}}
for
i\geq 1
using
Lemma

2.2
for
L_{i}=\LIP(H|_{B_{i}})
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The construction of the following subsection,
Section

3.1
, can be carried out e.g. on bounded Lipschitz domains on Euclidean spaces or even bounded uniform domains in
p
-PI spaces. The key point is that the

in question satisfies the conclusions of Lemmas
2.3
and
3.1
. These conclusions and the reference measure of

being Radon are the only assumptions we need in
Section

3.1
.

Section

3.2
only uses the construction from
Section

3.1
and

being locally complete. We prefer to state the proof for compact Riemannian

so that we may focus on the key ideas.

Nevertheless, the aforementioned setting is of interest to us in light of a recent exhaustion result of bounded domains in
p
-PI spaces by bounded uniform domains, see
[
Raj21
]
, and the exhaustion method used in the proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

3.1.
Precise representatives

We apply
Lemma

2.3
for
f\in W^{1,p}(M,N)
for a closed set
N\subset\mathbb{V}
. We are given a sequence
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
from
\LIP_{bs}(M,\mathbb{V})
satisfying

(3.2)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\|f-f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(M,\mathbb{V})}+\||df|-\lip(f_{j})\|_{L^{p}(M)}\right)<\infty.

This subsection consists of three steps and two properties of interest which we apply in the subsequent subsection.

Step (1)
: Constructing a representative
\widehat{f}
of
f
using
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
.

To this end, we consider the Borel function

\rho_{2}\coloneqq|f_{1}|+\lip(f_{1})+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|f_{j+1}-f_{j}|+|\lip(f_{j+1})-\lip(f_{j})|\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{+}(M).

Notice that the claimed integrability of
\rho_{2}
follows from (
3.2
). When
\rho_{2}(x)<\infty
, the sequences
(f_{j}(x))_{j=1}^{\infty}
and
(\lip(f_{j})(x))_{j=1}^{\infty}
are Cauchy.

Let
E
denote the collection of all
x\in M
for which
(f_{j}(x))_{j=1}^{\infty}
is not a Cauchy sequence. We denote
\widehat{f}(x)=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}f_{j}(x)
in
x\in M\setminus E
and
\widehat{f}(x)=x_{0}
in
E
for arbitrary
x_{0}\in f_{1}(M)
. The function
\widehat{f}
is a Borel representative of
f
since
\|f_{j}-f\|_{L^{p}(M,\mathbb{V})}
converges to zero as
j\rightarrow\infty
. The following is immediate.

Property (1)
: The images of
f_{j}
,
j\geq 1
, and
\widehat{f}
lie in some closed and separable subspace
\mathbb{V}_{0}\subset\mathbb{V}
.

We use
Property (1)
in the following subsection when applying Prokhorov’s theorem.

Step (2)
: We define
\widehat{\rho}(x)=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\lip(f_{j})(x)
whenever the limit exists and is finite and
\widehat{\rho}(x)=\infty
otherwise. Then
\widehat{\rho}\leq\rho_{2}
everywhere and
\widehat{\rho}
is a Borel representative of
|df|
and a
p
-integrable
p
-weak upper gradient of
\widehat{f}
.

Step (2)
is clear by Fuglede’s lemma, cf.
[
HKST15
, Fuglede’s Lemma]
, as
\widehat{\rho}
is the pointwise almost everywhere limit of the upper gradients
\lip(f_{j})
of
f_{j}
. To this end, we start with the Borel function

(3.3)

\rho_{1}=\infty\cdot\chi_{E}+\rho_{2}.

Observe that
\rho_{1}\geq\widehat{\rho}
everywhere. If
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to M
is nonconstant, has constant metric speed and satisfies

(3.4)

\int_{\gamma}\rho_{1}\,ds<\infty,

the absolutely continuous paths
f_{i}\circ\gamma
form an equicontinuous sequence of functions converging pointwise to
f\circ\gamma
outside the negligible set
\gamma^{-1}(E)
. Indeed, the equicontinuity follows from the fact that
\rho_{1}
is an upper gradient of every
f_{i}
for
i\geq 1
, while (
3.4
) implies that
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\gamma^{-1}(E))=0
. The equicontinuity of
f_{i}\circ\gamma
and the density of
[0,1]\setminus\gamma^{-1}(E)
in
[0,1]
imply that
(f_{i}\circ\gamma(s))_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence for every
s\in[0,1]
. Thus
\gamma^{-1}(E)=\emptyset
. Having verified this, the fact that
\lip(f_{m})+\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}|\lip(f_{m+1})-\lip(f_{m})|
is an upper gradient of every
f_{j}
for
j\geq m
and is bounded from above by
\rho_{1}
implies

\displaystyle|\widehat{f}(\gamma(1))-\widehat{f}(\gamma(0))|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left(\int_{\gamma}\lip(f_{m})+\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}|\lip(f_{j+1})-\lip(f_{j})|\,ds\right)=\int_{\gamma}\widehat{\rho}\,ds.

Consequently,
\widehat{\rho}
is a
p
-weak upper gradient of
\widehat{f}
. The remaining properties of
\widehat{\rho}
are immediate from the defining property of
(f_{j},\lip(f_{j}))_{j=1}^{\infty}
.

Step (3)
: Constructing a Borel function
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{+}(M)
defining the
p
-exceptional collection
\mathcal{F}(\rho_{0})
.

We show in the following subsection that the pushforward is well-defined in
\mathbf{N}_{\star,c}(\rho_{0})
.

The construction of
\rho_{0}
is done in two pieces using
\rho_{1}
from (
3.3
) and an auxiliary Banach space
\mathbb{W}
. We denote
f_{0}\coloneqq\widehat{f}
for convenience.

Let
\mathbb{W}
denote the collection of all sequences
V\coloneqq(v_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
such that

and
\|V\|\coloneqq\sup_{i\geq 0}|v_{i}|<\infty
. When we endow
\mathbb{W}
with componentwise multiplication and summation,

becomes a Banach space. Since

is injective, so is

.

We consider the mapping
H=(f_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}\colon M\to\mathbb{W}
defined by

H(x)=\left\{\begin{split}&(f_{0}(x))_{i=0}^{\infty}\quad&&\text{when $x\in E$ and}\\
&(f_{0}(x))_{i=0}^{\infty}+\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}(f_{i}(x)-f_{j}(x))_{i=0}^{m}\quad&&\text{when $x\in M\setminus E$.}\end{split}\right.

Observe that

is Borel measurable as a pointwise limit of Borel measurable mappings, see e.g.
[

, Pettis measurability theorem and Corollary 3.1.5]
. Furthermore,
H(x)=(f_{i}(x))_{i=0}^{\infty}
for every

By arguing as in

for a nonconstant

is a

. Since also

.

Property (2)
: The mapping

has a Lusin–Lipschitz decomposition
(B_{i},H_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
with the following properties:

(3.5)

\widehat{f}^{-1}(\mathbb{V}\setminus N)\cup\left\{\rho_{1}=\infty\right\}\subset B_{0}\quad

and for

,

is compact and the Lipschitz

uniformly on

.

Towards establishing

, we fix an initial Lusin–Lipschitz decomposition

of

, obtained from

. We first modify the decomposition

.

The Borel set on the left-hand side of (

) is

-negligible. Then (

, for

. Applying

converge uniformly to

Indeed, the pointwise convergence of the

.
Property (2)
holds after replacing the original

.

Having fixed

(3.6)

\rho_{0}=\infty\cdot\chi_{B_{0}}+\rho_{1}.

Remark 3.3
.

The construction above shows the following fact we find curious: Whenever

3.2.
Construction of the pushforward

We use the assumptions and notation from
Section

3.1
.

Definition:
When
k\in\left\{1,\dots,\lfloor p\rfloor\right\}
and
T\in\mathbf{N}_{k,c}(\rho_{0})
, the pushforward
\widehat{f}_{\star}T
is defined by the formula

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}T(\chi_{B_{i}}g\circ\widehat{f},h\circ h_{i,0}),\quad\text{$(g,h)\in\LIP_{b}(\mathbb{V})\times\LIP(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{R}^{k})$,}

and not defined in
\mathbf{N}_{k}(M)\cap\mathcal{F}(\rho_{0})
. For
k=0
, we denote

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}T(\chi_{B_{i}}g\circ\widehat{f}),\quad\text{$g\in\LIP_{b}(\mathbb{V})$,}

in
\mathbf{N}_{0,c}(\rho_{0})
and do not define the pushforward otherwise. Recall the notations
\mathbb{V}_{0}
and
H_{i}=(h_{i,j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
from
Properties (1)
and
(2)
, respectively.

Property (3)
: Whenever
T\in\mathbf{N}_{\star,c}(\rho_{0})
, we have
\widehat{f}_{\star}T\in\mathbf{N}_{\star}(\mathbb{V})
and the pushforward is a pointwise limit of
(f_{j})_{\sharp}T
. We have
\widehat{f}_{\star}T\in\mathbf{I}_{\star}(\mathbb{V})
if

.

We proceed with the verification of
Property (3)
when

, the argument requiring some care.

First, by

\int_{M}\rho_{0}^{k}\,d\|T\|<\infty,

the measure
\|T\|
is concentrated on

, so the pushforward of the measure
\|T\|
under
\widehat{f}
or

, respectively, is concentrated on
\mathbb{V}_{0}
. Hence the pushforward measures are Radon, recall
Lemma

2.7
. The same argument holds when
(k,T)
is replaced by
(k-1,\partial T)
.

We denote
\mu\coloneqq\widehat{f}_{\sharp}(\rho_{0}^{k}\|T\|)
and
\nu\coloneqq\widehat{f}_{\sharp}(\rho_{0}^{k-1}\|\partial T\|)
. By dominated convergence in
\mathcal{L}^{1}(M;\|T\|)
and
Step (1)
,
\mu
is a weak limit of the finite Radon measures
(f_{j})_{\sharp}(\rho_{0}^{k}\|T\|)
on
\mathbb{V}_{0}
, the respective convergence holding for the boundary mass measures and
\nu
. Thus we may apply Prokhorov’s theorem (see e.g.
[
Bog07
, Theorem 8.6.4]
): there exists a sequence of nested compact sets
(K_{r})_{r=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathbb{V}_{0}
for which

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{j}\left((f_{j})_{\sharp}\left(\rho^{k}_{0}\|T\|\right)\right)(\mathbb{V}\setminus K_{r})+\mu(\mathbb{V}\setminus K_{r})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-r}\quad\text{and}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{j}\left((f_{j})_{\sharp}\left(\rho^{k-1}_{0}\|\partial T\|\right)\right)(\mathbb{V}\setminus K_{r})+\nu(\mathbb{V}\setminus K_{r})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-r}.

By applying (
3.7
), we observe from (
2.5
) that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|(f_{j})_{\sharp}T\|(\mathbb{V}\setminus K_{r})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{f_{j}^{-1}(\mathbb{V}\setminus K_{r})}\lip(f_{j})^{k}\,d\|T\|\leq\int_{f_{j}^{-1}(\mathbb{V}\setminus K_{r})}\rho^{k}_{0}\,d\|T\|

\displaystyle=\left((f_{j})_{\sharp}\left(\rho^{k}_{0}\|T\|\right)\right)(\mathbb{V}\setminus K_{r})\leq 2^{-r}.

Similarly, (
3.8
) yields

(3.10)

\|(f_{j})_{\sharp}\partial T\|(\mathbb{V}\setminus K_{r})\leq\left((f_{j})_{\sharp}\left(\rho_{0}^{k-1}\|\partial T\|\right)\right)(\mathbb{V}\setminus K_{r})\leq 2^{-r}.

Inequalities (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) imply that the sequence of currents
((f_{j})_{\sharp}T)_{j=1}^{\infty}
is compact, by
[
AK00a
, Theorem 5.2]
, and as such, its accumulation points are normal
k
-currents supported on
\mathbb{V}_{0}
. In fact, we show that the sequence has a unique accumulation point given by
f_{\star}T
.

For each
j
and Borel set
F\subset M
, we have

(3.11)

\left|T(\chi_{F}(g\circ f_{j}),(h\circ f_{j}))\right|\leq\|g\|_{\infty}\LIP^{k}(h)\int_{F}\rho^{k}_{0}\,d\|T\|\quad\text{by \eqref{eq:masspushforward}.}

Inequality (
3.11
) and dominated convergence in
\mathcal{L}^{1}(M;\|T\|)
and
\ell_{1}
, respectively, show

(3.12)

\displaystyle T(g\circ f_{j},h\circ f_{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}T(\chi_{B_{i}}(g\circ f_{j}),(h\circ f_{j})).

By the strong locality of
T
, we may replace
h\circ f_{j}
by
h\circ h_{i,j}
in the series above, where
h_{i,j}
is the
j
’th component of
H_{i}
from
Property (2)
. By
Steps (1)
to
(3)
and, in particular,
Property (2)
,
h\circ h_{i,j}
are uniformly Lipschitz and converge uniformly to
h\circ h_{i,0}
for every
i\geq 1
. Furthermore,
\chi_{B_{i}}(g\circ f_{j})
converge to
\chi_{B_{i}}(g\circ\widehat{f})
in
\mathcal{L}^{1}(M;\|T\|)
by dominated convergence. Consequently, by the continuity of currents,

\displaystyle T(\chi_{B_{i}}(g\circ f_{j}),(h\circ f_{j}))=T(\chi_{B_{i}}(g\circ f_{j}),(h\circ h_{i,j}))\rightarrow T(\chi_{B_{i}}(g\circ\widehat{f}),(h\circ h_{i,0})).

This pointwise convergence, together with (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), and dominated convergence in
\ell_{1}
imply

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}T(g\circ f_{j},h\circ f_{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}T(\chi_{B_{i}}g\circ\widehat{f},h\circ h_{i,0}),\quad\text{$(g,h)\in\LIP_{b}(\mathbb{V})\times\LIP(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{R}^{k})$.}

Observe that if also
T\in\mathbf{I}_{k}(M)
, then
(f_{n})_{\sharp}T\in\mathbf{I}_{k}(\mathbb{V})
(readily verified using
[
AK00a
, Theorem 8.8 (ii)]
). On the other hand, integral currents are closed under pointwise convergence, under the additional assumption of equiboundedness of mass of
(f_{n})_{\sharp}T
and
\partial(f_{n})_{\sharp}T
, cf.
[
AK00a
, Theorem 8.5]
. Thus
\widehat{f}_{\star}T\in\mathbf{I}_{k}(\mathbb{V})
whenever
T\in\mathbf{I}_{k,c}(\rho_{0})
holds. The remaining case
k=0
is similar as above but simpler.

Property (4)
(mass-energy inequality): For every Borel set
F\subset\mathbb{V}
, we have

(3.13)

\|\widehat{f}_{\star}T\|(F)\leq\widehat{f}_{\star}\left(\widehat{\rho}^{k}\|T\|\right)(F)\quad\text{and}\quad\|\widehat{f}_{\star}\partial T\|(F)\leq\widehat{f}_{\star}\left(\widehat{\rho}^{k-1}\|\partial T\|\right)(F).

Furthermore, the mass measure
\widehat{f}_{\star}T
(resp.
\widehat{f}_{\star}\partial T
) is concentrated on the closed subset
N\cap\mathbb{V}_{0}
of the closed and separable subspace
\mathbb{V}_{0}
of
\mathbb{V}
.

We only prove the claim for
T
since the claim for the boundary follows by minor modifications. The case
k=0
is also simpler so we assume
k\geq 1
.

Whenever
g\in\LIP_{b}(N)
, dominated convergence shows

(|g|\circ f_{j})\lip(f_{j})^{k}\rightarrow(|g|\circ\widehat{f})\widehat{\rho}^{k}\quad\text{in $\mathcal{L}^{1}(M;\|T\|)$};

here we apply
Steps (1)
and
(2)
. Observe that for any
g\in\LIP_{b}(\mathbb{V})
and every
h\in\LIP(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{R}^{k})
whose components are
1
-Lipschitz,
Property (3)
implies

\displaystyle|\widehat{f}_{\star}T(g,h)|\leq\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{M}|g\circ f_{j}|\lip(f_{j})^{k}\,d\|T\|=\int_{M}(|g|\circ\widehat{f})\widehat{\rho}^{k}\,d\|T\|.

The definition of mass therefore shows

\|\widehat{f}_{\star}T\|\leq\widehat{f}_{\star}(\widehat{\rho}^{k}\|T\|)\quad\text{in the sense of measures,}

as claimed. The observation that
\|\widehat{f}_{\star}T\|
is concentrated on
N\cap\mathbb{V}_{0}
is immediate from (
3.13
).

Property (5)
: If
(E_{i},f_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
is a Lusin–Lipschitz decomposition of
\widehat{f}
, then

\widehat{f}_{\star}T(g,h)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}T(\chi_{E_{i}}(g\circ f_{i}),(h\circ f_{i}))

outside a
p
-exceptional collection.

We verify the claim outside the collection
\mathcal{F}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}(\max\left\{\rho_{0},\infty\cdot\chi_{E_{0}}\right\})
. In case
T\in\mathbf{N}_{k,c}(\max\left\{\rho_{0},\infty\cdot\chi_{E_{0}}\right\})
, then
\|T\|(E_{0})+\|\partial T\|(E_{0})=0
and for any
(g,h)\in\LIP_{b}(N)\times\LIP(N,\mathbb{R}^{k})
, we have

\displaystyle T(\chi_{B_{j}}(g\circ\widehat{f}),(h\circ h_{0,j}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}T(\chi_{E_{i}}\chi_{B_{j}}(g\circ\widehat{f}),(h\circ h_{0,j}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}T(\chi_{E_{i}}\chi_{B_{j}}(g\circ\widehat{f}),h\circ f_{i}),

where the first equality applies dominated convergence in
\mathcal{L}^{1}(M;\|T\|)
and finite additivity of
T
, together with dominated convergence in
\ell^{1}
, while the second equality follows from the strong locality of
T
. We may sum over
j\geq 1
and exchange the order of summations over
j
and
i
due to Fubini’s theorem and absorb the sum over
j
inside the last term. The steps are justified by (
3.11
).
Property (5)
follows.

Property (6)
: We claim that the pushforward is well-defined for every representative
\widehat{f}_{1}
of
f
with a
p
-minimal
p
-weak upper gradient
\widehat{\rho}_{1}
outside a
p
-exceptional collection
\mathcal{F}_{1}
.

To this end, let
F
denote any
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-negligible Borel set containing the points for which
\widehat{f}_{1}\neq\widehat{f}
or
\widehat{\rho}_{1}\neq\widehat{\rho}
. We define
\mathcal{F}_{1}=\mathcal{F}(\max\left\{\rho_{0},\infty\cdot\chi_{F}\right\})
, and deduce for every
T\in\mathbf{N}_{k,c}(\max\left\{\rho_{0},\infty\cdot\chi_{F}\right\})
that
\widehat{f}=\widehat{f}_{1}
and
\widehat{\rho}=\widehat{\rho}_{1}

(\|T\|+\|\partial T\|)
-almost everywhere. This guarantees

\widehat{f}_{\sharp}(\widehat{\rho}^{k}\|T\|)=(\widehat{f}_{1})_{\sharp}(\widehat{\rho}^{k}_{1}\|T\|)\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{f}_{\sharp}(\widehat{\rho}^{k-1}\|\partial T\|)=(\widehat{f}_{1})_{\sharp}(\widehat{\rho}^{k-1}_{1}\|\partial T\|).

With these equalities at hand,
Property (4)
justifies the definition
(\widehat{f}_{1})_{\star}\coloneqq\widehat{f}_{\star}
for such
T
. In case we already had a definition for
(\widehat{f}_{1})_{\star}
satisfying
Properties (4)
and
(5)
above, similar reasoning as in the proof of

shows that the two definitions coincide outside a
p
-exceptional collection. In this sense, the obtained pushforward is independent of the approximating sequence of Lipschitz mappings we started from. In particular,
Property (6)
follows.

4.
Pushforward and pullback

In this section we prove
Theorem

1.1
. In fact, we prove the following stronger statement.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
M
is a Riemannian manifold, with or without boundary,
N
is a closed subset of an injective Banach space
\mathbb{V}
, and
f\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(M,N)
for
1\leq p<\infty
.

Then there exist a Borel representative
(\widehat{f},\widehat{\rho})
of
(f,|df|)
and Borel
\widehat{\rho}_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{+}(M)
together with a chain morphism

(4.1)

\widehat{f}_{\star}\colon(\mathbf{N}_{\star,c}(\widehat{\rho}_{0}),\partial)\to(\mathbf{N}_{\star}(N),\partial)

with the following properties: there is a Lusin–Lipschitz decomposition of
(\widehat{B}_{i},\widehat{f}_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
of
\widehat{f}
with

(4.2)

\widehat{f}^{-1}(\mathbb{V}\setminus N)\subset B_{0}\subset\left\{\widehat{\rho}_{0}=\infty\right\},\quad\widehat{f}_{\star}T=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(\widehat{f}_{i})_{\sharp}\left(T\llcorner{B_{i}}\right),

and

(4.3)

\|\widehat{f}_{\star}T\|(E)\leq\int_{f^{-1}(E)}\widehat{\rho}^{k}\,d\|T\|<\infty\quad\text{for Borel $E\subset N$},

whenever
T\in\mathbf{N}_{k,c}(\widehat{\rho}_{0})
and
k\in\left\{0,1,\dots,\lfloor p\rfloor\right\}
. Furthermore,
\widehat{f}_{\star}
restricts to a chain morphism

\widehat{f}_{\star}\colon(\mathbf{I}_{\star,c}(\widehat{\rho}_{0}),\partial)\to(\mathbf{I}_{\star}(N),\partial).

If another tuple
((\widetilde{f},\widetilde{\rho}),\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{f}_{\star},(\widetilde{B}_{i},\widetilde{f}_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty})
satisfies the conclusions (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) in
\mathbf{N}_{\star,c}(\widetilde{\rho}_{0})
, the chain morphisms
\widehat{f}_{\star}
and
\widetilde{f}_{\star}
coincide on a common refinement of
(\mathbf{N}_{\star,c}(\widehat{\rho}_{0}),\partial)
and
(\mathbf{N}_{\star,c}(\widetilde{\rho}_{0}),\partial)
.

Theorem

1.1
is an immediate consequence of
Theorem

4.1
above. The series representation in (
4.2
) should be understood as follows:

\widehat{f}_{\star}T(g,h)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\widehat{f}_{i})_{\sharp}(T\llcorner{B_{i}})(g,h),\quad\text{$(g,h)\in\LIP_{b}(\mathbb{V})\times\LIP(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{R}^{k})$,}

and that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|(\widehat{f}_{i})_{\sharp}(T\llcorner{B_{i}})(g,h)|<\infty
. Notice that the summation starts from
1
instead of
0
. That is, the map
\widehat{f}_{0}
plays no role in the claim.

Proof of

4.1
.

The proof has two parts.

Case (1)
:

is compact.

We may apply the results from

3
somewhat directly. Given the technicality of the statement, let us provide the details.

First,

2.3
shows that
f
satisfies the assumptions of

3.1
. Let
\widehat{f}
and
\widehat{\rho}
denote the precise representatives of

and its
p
-minimal

Given

The boundary property also holds when

We recall from

We apply the compact case, namely

, to each of

and

\widehat{f}_{m}

We apply the locality of the

\bigcup_{m=3}^{\infty}\bigcup_{j=m-1}^{\infty}\left\{x\in M_{m-2}\colon(\widehat{f}_{m},\widehat{\rho}_{m})(x)\neq(\widehat{f}_{j},\widehat{\rho}_{j})(x)\right\}

has

.

, we identify

with its zero extension to

5.
Representing integer-rectifiable currents

5.1.
Vectors and covectors

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a Banach space. For each
k=0,1\dots
, we let
\bigwedge_{n}\mathbb{V}
denote the collection of all
n
-vectors. We say that
\tau\in\bigwedge_{k}\mathbb{V}
is
simple
if
\tau=v_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge v_{n}
for some
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{V}
. When
\tau
is simple,
\mathrm{Span}(\tau)
is the linear subspace span by the vectors
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
\bigwedge^{n}\mathrm{Span}(\tau)
consists of all
n
-covectors on the subspace
\mathrm{Span}(\tau)
.

We say that
\tau=v_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge v_{n}
has
unit norm
if
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{V}
can be chosen in such a way that the linear map

L\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{V},\quad\text{where }L(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}v_{i}

has unit Jacobian. That is, we have

\mathcal{H}^{n}(L(E))=\mathcal{H}^{n}(E)\quad\text{for every Borel set $E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$;}

we refer the reader to
[
AK00a
, Section 9]
for well-posedness.

Given a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{V}
, a map
\tau\colon E\rightarrow\bigwedge_{n}\mathbb{V}
is
measurable
if
\tau(x)=\tau_{1}(x)\wedge\dots\wedge\tau_{n}(x)
for some Borel maps
\tau_{i}\colon E\rightarrow\mathbb{V}
. In this case, we say that
\tau
is a
measurable section
of
\bigwedge_{n}\mathbb{V}
.

5.2.
Integer-rectifiable currents

We consider a closed subset
N
of an injective Banach space
\mathbb{V}
and
T\in\mathbf{I}_{n}(N)
. Since
T
is concentrated on its (separable) support, we might as well assume that
N
is separable. Hence we may isometrically embed
N
into
\ell^{\infty}
leading to the simplification
\mathbb{V}=\ell^{\infty}
. The assumption
\mathbb{V}=\ell^{\infty}
allows us to apply the results from
[
AK00a
, Section 9]
.

We recall from
[
AK00a
, Theorem 9.1]
that every

Cochains from differential forms

Using (

n

\Omega_{\omega}(T)\coloneqq\int_{E_{T}}\langle\omega,\tau_{T}\rangle(x)\theta_{T}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x)\quad\text{for $T\in\mathcal{I}_{k}(N)$,}

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

In particular,

We use the following lemma during the proof.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

-Lipschitz

Proof of
Lemma

.

When

-Lipschitz function

When

Proof of
Lemma

5.1
.

Let

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, with or without boundary, and

. If

is a bounded and smooth differential

f^{\star}\Omega_{\omega}(T)=\int_{E_{T}}\langle f^{\star}\omega,v_{T}\rangle\theta_{T}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\quad\text{for every $T\in\mathbf{I}_{n,c}(\rho_{0})$.}

Proof.

We isometrically embed

into
\ell^{\infty}
for the duration of the following proof. The embedding is only applied implicitly when using the results from
[
AK00b
]
.

The notation

has several equivalent definitions based on various definitions of weak differentials of

(1)

We Nash embed

into an Euclidean space and use the usual Euclidean-valued weak differential of Sobolev mappings;

(2)

We isometrically embed

into
\ell^{\infty}
and consider a Lusin–Lipschitz decomposition
(B_{i},f_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
of

. Then every

is
\text{weak}^{*}
-differentiable at

-almost every

in the sense
[

; the differential denoted by

. We define

so that it coincides with

in

We observe that both of these definitions satisfy the axioms of the intrinsic colocal weak derivative
[
CVS16
, Definition 1.2]
, so they coincide

[

, Proposition 1.5]
. Definition (2) is directly linked to (

Consider a basis representation of

\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\psi_{j}\sum_{I}\omega_{j,I}(\varphi^{j,I})^{\star}(\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})

of a bounded differential
n

is the Riemannian volume form on

corresponding to the increasing multi-index

are chosen in such a way that

contains the support of

and that

is a smooth partition of unity of

. We assume that the partition of unity has infinite number of terms since the finite case is simpler and follows with minor modifications.

We consider componentwise McShane extensions of
\psi_{j}
,
\omega_{j,I}
and
\varphi^{j}
to
\ell^{\infty}
, and use the same notation for the extensions. We define

f^{\star}(\chi_{E}\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{I}\left(\left(\chi_{E}\psi_{j}\omega_{j,I}\right)\circ f\right)f^{\star}\left((\varphi^{j,I})^{\star}(\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})\right),\quad\text{for every Borel $E\subset N$,}

by pointwise evaluation using the usual linear algebra pullback, see e.g.
[
Fed69
, Chapter 1]
, and the weak differential of

We fix

\displaystyle|\Omega_{\omega}(f_{\star}T)-\int_{E_{T}}\langle f^{\star}\omega,\tau_{T}\rangle\theta_{T}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}|<\epsilon+\left|\Omega_{\omega}((f_{\star}T)\llcorner E)-\int_{E_{T}\cap f^{-1}(E)}\langle f^{\star}\omega,\tau_{T}\rangle\theta_{T}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right|.

The claim follows by setting

6.
Quasiregular curves on singular spaces

In this section, we consider a complete metric space
N
and an
n
-dimensional oriented Riemannian manifold
M
, with or without boundary, for
n\geq 2
. Top-dimensional compact Lipschitz submanifolds
\widetilde{M}
of
M
are endowed with the orientation induced from
M
, considered as integral currents
[\widetilde{M}]
.

6.1.
Isoperimetric inequalities of Euclidean type

In this section we consider a complete metric space
N
satisfying various isoperimetric inequalities. For this purpose, given
j=1,2,\dots
and
T\in\mathbf{I}_{j}(N)
, we denote

\mathrm{Fillvol}(T)\coloneqq\inf\left\{M(S)\colon S\in\mathbf{I}_{j+1}(X)\,\,\text{and}\,\,\partial S=T\right\}.

We say that
N
supports an
isoperimetric inequality of Euclidean type with dimension
j
, mass bound
M
and constant
A
if

(6.1)

\text{$T\in\mathbf{I}_{j}(N)$, $M(T)\leq M$, and $\partial T=0$}\quad\text{imply}\quad\mathrm{Fillvol}(T)\leq A(M(T))^{\frac{j+1}{j}}.

When
M=\infty
, we say that
N
supports an
isoperimetric inequality of Euclidean type with dimension
j
and constant
A
.

We refer the reader to
[
Wen07
,
BWY21
]
and references therein for further information about spaces satisfying (
6.1
). Our main applications of interest are metric spaces
N
for which there is
\delta,\gamma>0
such that subsets of diameter at most
\delta
are
\gamma
-Lipschitz contractible (see
[
Wen07
, Section 3.2]
for the precise definition). These include the classes of compact Lipschitz manifolds, Banach spaces, and CAT(
\kappa
)-spaces.

Almgren proved, cf.
[
Alm86
, Theorem 10]
, that when
N=\mathbb{R}^{m}
for any
m\geq 1
and any
j\geq 1
, the optimal Euclidean isoperimetry constants are

(6.2)

M=\infty\quad\text{and}\quad A_{j}\coloneqq\omega_{j+1}^{\frac{1}{j}}(j+1)^{\frac{j+1}{j}},

where
\omega_{j+1}
is the volume (Lebesgue measure) of the
(j+1)
-dimensional unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{j+1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{j+1}
.

We prove
Corollary

1.4
after the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

When
N
supports an isoperimetric inequality of Euclidean type with dimension
n
, mass bound
M^{\prime}
and constant
A^{\prime}
, and
P\in\mathbf{I}_{n}(N)
satisfies
M(P)\leq M^{\prime}/2
, then

\inf_{S\in\mathbf{I}_{n+1}(N)}M(P+\partial S)=\mathrm{Fillvol}(\partial P).

In case
N
supports an isoperimetric inequality of Euclidean type with dimension
n-1
, mass bound
M
and constant
A
, then

P\in\mathbf{I}_{n}(N)\quad\text{and}\quad M(P)\leq AM^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\quad\text{imply}\quad\mathrm{Fillvol}(\partial P)\leq A\left(M(\partial P)\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}.

Proof.

Given the isoperimetric inequality of dimension
n
, the equality
\partial T=\partial P
and
M(T)\leq M(P)\leq M^{\prime}/2
imply
P-T=\partial S
for some
S\in\mathbf{I}_{n+1}(N)
. Hence

\mathrm{Fillvol}(\partial P)=\inf_{S\in\mathbf{I}_{n+1}(N)}M(P+\partial S)\quad\text{whenever $M(P)\leq M^{\prime}/2$.}

Next, suppose that
N
supports an isoperimetric inequality of Euclidean type with dimension
n-1
, mass bound
M
and constant
A
. If
M(P)\leq AM^{\frac{n}{n-1}}
, then

\mathrm{Fillvol}(\partial P)\leq A\left(M(\partial P)\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}.

Indeed, otherwise

\mathrm{Fillvol}(\partial P)>A\left(M(\partial P)\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}},

so
M(\partial P)<M
. These two inequalities then contradict the isoperimetric inequality.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Suppose that
N
supports an isoperimetric inequality of Euclidean type with dimension
n
, mass bound
M^{\prime}
and constant

and with dimension

Suppose that

6.2.
Calibrations and quasiregular curves

We consider an additive cochain

\Omega\colon\mathbf{I}_{n}(N)\to\mathbb{R},

for
n\in\left\{0,1,\dots\right\}
. Before giving the definition of quasiregular curves, we prove show that cochains with bounded upper norms have
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-essentially unique
minimal upper norms
. To this end, we observe the following.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose that a cochain
\Omega\colon\mathbf{I}_{n}(N)\to\mathbb{R}
has
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-essentially bounded upper norms
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
. Then
g=\min\left\{g_{1},g_{2}\right\}
is an upper norm of
\Omega
.

Lemma

6.3
yields the following lattice property.

Corollary 6.4
.

Suppose that a cochain
\Omega\colon\mathbf{I}_{n}(N)\to\mathbb{R}
has an
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-essentially bounded norm. Then the
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-essentially bounded upper norms of
\Omega
is closed with respect to taking Borel
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(N;\mathcal{H}^{n})
-convex combinations and taking pointwise
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-essential infima.

Given
Corollary

6.4
, we deduce that the collection of
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-essentially bounded upper norms of
\Omega
is empty or there is a
minimal upper norm
, unique up to
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-negligible sets. The minimal upper norm is denoted by
\|\Omega\|
. The minimality should be understood as follows: every
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-essentially bounded upper norm of
\Omega
is bounded from below by
\|\Omega\|

\mathcal{H}^{n}
-almost everywhere.

Proof of
Lemma

6.3
.

Let
E=\left\{g_{1}\leq g_{2}\right\}
and
T\in\mathbf{I}_{n}(N)
. Given
\epsilon>0
, the
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-essential boundedness of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
yield the existence of a compact set
K\subset E
and open set
V\supset E
for which

\int_{V\setminus K}g_{1}+g_{2}\,d\|T\|<\frac{\epsilon}{2};

here we apply the fact that
\|T\|
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{n}
and the fact that
\|T\|
is Radon. Consider the Lipschitz function
h(z)=d(K,z)
,
z\in N
. Then, by the localization lemma
[
AK00a
, Lemma 5.3]
,
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-almost every
t\in\mathbb{R}
is such that

T_{-}\coloneqq T\llcorner{\left\{h\leq t\right\}}\in\mathbf{N}_{n}(N),\quad T_{+}\coloneqq T\llcorner{\left\{h>t\right\}}\in\mathbf{N}_{n}(N),\quad\text{and}\quad T=T_{-}+T_{+}.

In fact, here
T_{\pm}\in\mathbf{I}_{n}(N)
by the closure theorem
[
AK00a
, Theorem 8.5]
and the construction of
T_{+}
from
[
AK00a
, Lemma 5.3]
. This can alternatively be deduced from the intrinsic representation theorem of integer-rectifiable currents, cf.
[
AK00a
, Theorem 9.1]
.

We consider only those
0<t
for which
\left\{h\leq t\right\}\subset V
. For such
t
, the upper norm inequality gives

\displaystyle|\Omega(T_{-})|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{N}g_{1}\,d\|T_{-}\|=\int_{\left\{h\leq t\right\}}g_{1}\,d\|T\|\leq\int_{E}g_{1}\,d\|T\|+\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

By symmetry in the argument and the subadditivity of
\Omega
, we deduce that

\displaystyle|\Omega(T)|\leq|\Omega(T_{-})|+|\Omega(T_{+})|\leq\int_{E}g_{1}\,d\|T\|+\int_{N\setminus E}g_{2}\,d\|T\|+\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrarily small and
T\in\mathbf{I}_{n}(N)
arbitrary, the claim follows.
∎

Definition 6.5
.

Consider a complete metric space

and a closed

Remark 6.6

(Consistency)
.

When

f
is continuous and
\|\omega\|(x)>0
everywhere in
N
, the definition above coincides with the one by Pankka
[
Pan20
]
.

The lower bound (
6.4
) is motivated by the calibrated geometries of Harvey and Lawson in
[
HL82
]
. The connection is highlighted by the following proposition, the proof of which is immediate from the definition above.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, with or without boundary,
N
a complete metric space and
f\colon M\to N
a
K
-quasiregular
\Omega
-curve with
(\Omega,f)
being
(C,c)
-calibrated. Then

(6.5)

cM(f_{\star}[\widetilde{M}])\leq c\int_{\widetilde{M}}|df|^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq K\Omega(f_{\star}[\widetilde{M}]),\quad\text{$[\widetilde{M}]\in\mathbf{I}_{n,c}(\rho_{0})$}.

Observe that the smallest and largest terms in
Proposition

6.7
imply that
f_{\star}[\widetilde{M}]
is a
quasiminimizer
of mass in its homology class of integral currents. That is,

M(f_{\star}[\widetilde{M}])\leq Q\inf_{S\in\mathbf{I}_{n+1}(N)}M(f_{\star}[\widetilde{M}]+\partial S),\quad\text{for $Q=KC/c$.}

This observation is connected to the quasiminimality of
f
in the sense of Gromov
[
Gro07
, Definition 6.36]
. The latter inequality in (
6.5
), combined with the mass–energy inequality (
4.3
), implies quasiminimality of
f
in
energy
for Sobolev mappings in the same homology class, cf.
[
Pan20
, Section 1.1]
. See
Theorem

below for a related result.

The observations above relate quasiregular curves to the area- and energy-minimization problems considered by Lytchak and Wenger in
[
LW16
,
LW17
,
LW18
,
LW20
]
. See also
[

,

]
and references therein.

6.3.
Regularity of quasiregular curves

The following theorem is a simple consequence of the tools developed so far, formulated for quasiregular curves defined on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

Theorem 6.8
.

Suppose that
N
supports an isoperimetric inequality of Euclidean type with dimension
n
, mass bound
M^{\prime}
and constant
A^{\prime}
and with dimension
(n-1)
, mass bound
M
, and constant
A
, respectively, for some
n\geq 2
.

If
f\colon\mathbb{B}^{n}\to N
is a
K
-quasiregular
\Omega
-curve,
(\Omega,f)
is
(C,c)
-calibrated, and

(6.6)

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|df|^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq\min\left\{M^{\frac{n}{n-1}}A,\frac{M^{\prime}}{2}\right\},

then
f
has a continuous representative
\widehat{f}
such that

d(\widehat{f}(x),\widehat{f}(y))\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|df|^{n}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{1/n}|x-y|^{\beta},\quad x,y\in B\left(0,1/2\right),

for
Q=KC/c
,
\beta=\left(QA/A_{n-1}\right)^{-1}
and
C=C(n,\beta)>0
.

By conformal invariance of the left-hand side of (
6.6
),
Theorem

6.8
readily implies a local version of the statement and can be applied e.g. on Riemannian manifolds using normal coordinates. Observe, however, that passing to charts typically increases the constant
K
. The conformal invariance also implies
Theorem

1.5
as an immediate corollary.

Proof of
Theorem

6.8
.

We fix any Borel
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{n}_{+}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
for which
f
satisfies the conclusions of
Theorem

4.1
and
Proposition

6.7
whenever
[\widetilde{M}]\in\mathbf{I}_{n,c}(\rho_{0})
.

Observe that
f
satisfies the assumptions of
Corollary

6.2
. Next, we apply (
6.5
) together with the conclusion of
Corollary

6.2
. We deduce that

(6.7)

\int_{\widetilde{M}}|df|^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq AQ\left(\int_{\partial\widetilde{M}}|df|^{n-1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\quad\text{whenever $[\widetilde{M}]\in\mathbf{I}_{n,c}(\rho_{0})$.}

Fix any
x\in B(0,1/2)
. For
0<r\leq 1/2
, consider the functions

\displaystyle H(r)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{B(x,r)}|df|^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n},\quad

\displaystyle h(r)\coloneqq\int_{\partial B(x,r)}|df|^{n-1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle c(r)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\partial B(x,r)}|df|^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\quad\text{and}\quad

\displaystyle s(r)\coloneqq\left(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B(x,r))\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}.

By Fubini’s theorem, (
6.7
) holds for
B(x,r)
for almost every
0<r\leq 1/2
. In other words,

(6.8)

H(r)\leq QA\left(h(r)\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\quad\text{for almost every $0<r\leq 1/2$.}

Moreover, by Hölder’s inequality,

(6.9)

\displaystyle h(r)\leq\left(c(r)s(r)\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\quad\text{for every $0<r\leq 1/2$.}

Notice that

(6.10)

s(r)=\left(n\omega_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}r=\frac{1}{nA_{n-1}}r.

We combine (
6.8
), (
6.9
), and (
6.10
) to deduce that

H(r)\leq QAc(r)s(r)=\frac{1}{n\beta}rc(r)\quad\text{for almost every $0<r\leq 1/2$}.

Observe that
r\mapsto H(r)
is absolutely continuous and
H^{\prime}(r)=c(r)
for almost every
0<r\leq 1/2
by Fubini’s theorem. Consequently,

(6.11)

H(r)\leq\frac{1}{n\beta}rH^{\prime}(r)\quad\text{for almost every $0<r\leq 1/2$.}

Grönwall’s lemma and (
6.11
) imply

(6.12)

H(s)\leq H(S)\left(\frac{s}{S}\right)^{n\beta}\quad\text{for every $0<s<S\leq 1/2$}.

Next, we denote

K(x,S)\coloneqq\left(\omega_{n}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(H(S)\right)^{1/n}S^{-\beta}.

Hölder inequality and (
6.12
) show

(6.13)

\displaystyle\||df|\|_{L^{1}(B(x,s))}\leq\left(\omega_{n}s^{n}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(H(S)\left(\frac{s}{S}\right)^{n\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}=K(x,S)s^{(n-1)+\beta}.

We set
S=1/2
and notice that

C\left(0,1/2\right)\coloneqq(\omega_{n})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|df|^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(1/2\right)^{-\beta}\geq K\left(x,1/2\right),\quad x\in B\left(0,1/2\right).

It is a classical result due to Morrey, see, e.g.
[
GT01
, Theorem 7.19, p 165]
, that (
6.13
) and the uniform upper bound on
K(x,1/2)
imply

\mathrm{esssup}_{y,z\in B(0,1/2),y\neq z}\frac{d(\widehat{f}(y),\widehat{f}(z))}{|y-z|^{\beta}}\leq C\left(n,\beta\right)C\left(0,1/2\right).

In fact, the quoted result is formulated only for real-valued functions but considering
N
as a subset of
L^{\infty}(N)
and recalling that the image of
f
is essentially separable, the result readily generalizes. The claim about the existence of a continuous representative of
f
follows by covering
\mathbb{B}^{n}
by balls such that the ball with double the radius is contained in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
and by doing the obvious modifications in the argument above.
∎

Next, we show that quasiregular curves are (local) quasiminimizers of energy in the following sense.

Theorem 6.9
.

Suppose that
N
supports an isoperimetric inequality of Euclidean type with dimension
n
, mass bound
M^{\prime}
and constant
A^{\prime}
, for some
n\geq 1
.

If
f\colon\mathbb{B}^{n}\to N
is a
K
-quasiregular
\Omega
-curve,
(\Omega,f)
is
(C,c)
-calibrated, and

(6.14)

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|df|^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq\frac{M^{\prime}}{2},

then whenever
g\in W^{1,n}_{loc}(\mathbb{B}^{n},N)
is such that
f=g

\mathcal{H}^{n}
-almost everywhere outside a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}
, the inequality

\int_{E}|df|^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq Q\int_{E}|dg|^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}

holds for
Q=KC/c
.

Corollary

1.6
is an immediate consequence of Theorems
6.8
and
6.9
. Indeed, the modulus of continuity follows from
Theorem

6.8
, while
f
being
n
-harmonic follows from
Theorem

6.9
.

Proof of
Theorem

6.9
.

There is a Borel
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{n}_{+}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
so that the pushforwards
f_{\star}
and
g_{\star}
satisfy the conclusions of
Theorem

4.1
,
f
the conclusions of
Proposition

6.7
on
\mathbf{I}_{n,c}(\rho_{0})
, and that

(6.15)

\left\{f\neq g\right\}\subset\left\{\rho_{0}=\infty\right\}.

Given any compact Lipschitz
n
-dimensional manifold
\widetilde{M}\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}
with
\partial\widetilde{M}\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}\setminus E
and
[\widetilde{M}]\in\mathbf{I}_{n,c}(\rho_{0})
, we deduce from (
4.2
) and (
6.15
) that
f_{\star}[\partial\widetilde{M}]=g_{\star}[\partial\widetilde{M}]
.

Observe that the energy upper bound on
f
implies that
f_{\star}[\widetilde{M}]
satisfies the first upper bound appearing in
Lemma

6.1
. Therefore
f_{\star}[\partial\widetilde{M}]=g_{\star}[\partial\widetilde{M}]
implies

\inf_{S\in\mathbf{I}_{n+1}(M)}M(f_{\star}[\partial\widetilde{M}]+\partial S)=\mathrm{Fillvol}(f_{\star}[\partial\widetilde{M}])\leq M(g_{\star}[\widetilde{M}])\leq\int_{\widetilde{M}}|dg|^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

Combining these inequalities with (
6.5
) yields

(6.16)

\int_{\widetilde{M}}|df|^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq Q\int_{\widetilde{M}}|dg|^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

We recall that
\mathbb{B}^{n}\setminus E
can be exhausted by open sets
U_{j}\subset U_{j+1}
so that
\partial U_{j}\cap\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}=\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
\partial U_{j}\setminus\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}
is a smooth
(n-1)
-dimensional submanifold of

contained in

E_{j}\coloneqq\left\{x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}\colon d(E,x)<2^{-j}\right\},\quad\text{cf. \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Dan:08}{}{}, Proposition 8.2.1]}.}

Up to a perturbation of

in

, we may assume that
\widetilde{M}_{j}\coloneqq\mathbb{B}^{n}\setminus U_{j}
induces an integral current in
\mathbf{I}_{n,c}(\rho_{0})
. Then applying (
6.16
) for each
j
and passing to the limit
j\rightarrow\infty
shows the claim.
∎

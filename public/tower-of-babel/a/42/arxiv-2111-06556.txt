On the Complexity of Separating Cutting Planes for the Knapsack Polytope
Alberto Del Pia, Jeff Linderoth, Haoran Zhu

Abstract
We close three open problems on the separation complexity of valid inequalities for the knapsack polytope. Specifically, we establish that the separation problems for extended cover inequalities, ( 1 , k ) (1,k) -configuration inequalities, and weight inequalities are all 𝒩 ​ 𝒫 \mathcal{NP} -complete. We also show that, when the number of constraints of the LP relaxation is constant and its optimal solution is an extreme point, then the separation problems of both extended cover inequalities and weight inequalities can be solved in polynomial time. Moreover, we provide a natural generalization of ( 1 , k ) (1,k) -configuration inequality which is easier to separate and contains the original ( 1 , k ) (1,k) -configuration inequality as a strict sub-family.

1
Introduction

The
multi-dimensional knapsack problem
is the integer programming (IP) problem

\max\{c^{\mathsf{T}}x:Ax\leq d,\ x\in\{0,1\}^{n}\},

(1)

where
A\in\mathbb{Z}^{m\times n}_{+}
,
c\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
, and
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+}
.
When the constraint matrix
A
only has one row
a
and the right-hand side vector is a positive integer
b
, problem (
1
) is referred to as
knapsack problem
, and the convex hull of the associated feasible region,
\operatorname{conv}(\{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}:a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b\})
, is referred to as the
knapsack polytope
.

The multi-dimensional knapsack problem is a fundamental problem in discrete optimization, and valid inequalities for the feasible region have been widely studied, see, e.g.,
kellerer2004multidimensional
;
puchinger2010multidimensional
;
10.1007/978-3-030-73879-2_14
and the modern survey
hojny2019knapsack
.
In this paper, we study the complexity of the separation problem for well-known families of valid inequalities for (
1
).

A standard and computationally useful way for generating cuts for (
1
) is to construct cuts for the knapsack polytope defined by its individual constraints.
Suppose
a
is a row of the constraint matrix
A
, and let
b
be the corresponding coordinate of the right-hand side
d
. We denote the associated knapsack polytope by
K:=\operatorname{conv}(\{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}:a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b\})
.

Many families of valid inequalities for
K
are based on the notion of a
cover
, which is a subset
C
of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
such that
\sum_{i\in C}a_{i}>b
.
Given a cover
C
, the inequality

\sum_{i\in C}x_{i}\leq|C|-1

is valid for
K
, and is called a
cover inequality
(CI). Cover inequalities can often be strengthened through a process called lifting, and the resulting inequalities are called
lifted cover inequalities
(LCIs)
balas1978facets
;
MR1656936
;
letchford2019lifted
;
wolsey1975faces
;
padberg1975note
.
Balas
balas1975facets
gave one family of LCIs known as
extended cover inequality
(ECI), which have the form

\sum_{j\notin C:a_{j}\geq\max_{i\in C}a_{i}}x_{j}+\sum_{i\in C}x_{i}\leq|C|-1.

A
minimal cover
is a cover
C
such that
\sum_{i\in C\setminus\{j\}}a_{i}\leq b
for any
j\in C
.
A set
N\cup\{t\}
with
N\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
t\notin N
is called a
(1,k)
-configuration
for
k\in\{2,\ldots,|N|\}
if
\sum_{i\in N}a_{i}\leq b
and
Q\cup\{t\}
is a minimal cover for every
Q\subseteq N
with
|Q|=k
. Padberg
padberg19801
showed that for any
(1,k)
-configuration
N\cup\{t\}
, the inequality

(|S|-k+1)x_{t}+\sum_{i\in S}x_{i}\leq|S|

is valid for
K
for every
S\subseteq N
with
|S|\geq k
. This inequality is called a
(1,k)
-configuration inequality
.

Other valid inequalities for the knapsack polytope
K
arise from the concept of a pack.
For the knapsack polytope
K
, a set
P\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
is a
pack
if
\sum_{i\in P}a_{i}\leq b
.
Given a pack
P
, the corresponding
pack inequality

\sum_{i\in P}a_{i}x_{i}\leq\sum_{i\in P}a_{i}
is trivially valid for
K
, as it is implied by the upper bound constraints
x_{i}\leq 1
.
However, pack inequalities can be lifted in several different ways to obtain more interesting
lifted pack inequalities (LPIs)

atamturk2005cover
.
Weismantel
weismantel19970
derived the weight-inequalities, which are LPIs. To define the weight inequalities, let
r(P):=b-\sum_{i\in P}a_{i}
be the
residual capacity
of the pack
P
.
The indices
j\notin P
with
a_{j}>r(P)
are lifted to obtain the
weight inequality (WI)
:

\sum_{i\in P}a_{i}x_{i}+\sum_{j\notin P}\max\{a_{j}-r(P),0\}x_{j}\leq\sum_{i\in P}a_{i}.

Consider the
linear programming (LP) relaxation
of (
1
):

\max\{c^{\mathsf{T}}x:Ax\leq d,\ x\in[0,1]^{n}\}.

(2)

For a given family
\mathscr{F}
of valid inequalities for (
1
), the associated
separation problem
is defined as follows: “Let
x^{*}
be a feasible solution to (
2
), does there exist an inequality in
\mathscr{F}
that is violated by
x^{*}
?
If so, return one such inequality from
\mathscr{F}
.”
In this paper, we are mainly interested in the weaker decision version of the separation problem where we do not have to return a separating inequality, even if it does exist, and we assume that
x^{*}
is an optimal solution to (
2
).
In fact, the separation of an optimal solution is not harder than the separation of a general feasible solution, and from a computational point of view,
x^{*}
almost always corresponds to an optimal solution to some linear relaxation.

The separation problem for several families of valid inequalities for the knapsack polytope has been shown to be
\mathcal{NP}
-complete, including
CIs
klabjan1998complexity
,
and LCIs
MR1688131
.
On the other hand, the complexity of the separation problem for extended cover inequalities,
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities, and weight inequalities are, to the best of our knowledge, unknown.
Kaparis and Letchford stated that the separation problem seems likely to be
\mathcal{NP}
-hard for ECIs in
kaparis2010separation
.
It was conjectured explicitly in
ferreira1996solving
that the separation problem for
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities is
\mathcal{NP}
-hard.
Moreover, the complexity of the separation problem for WIs is also open, as mentioned in
hojny2019knapsack
.
In this paper, we provide positive answers to all these conjectures.
Namely, we show that the separation problems for ECIs, for
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities, and for WIs are all
\mathcal{NP}
-complete.
The first two results are proven via a reduction from the separation problem for CIs, and the separation complexity for WIs is given via a reduction from the Subset Sum Problem (SSP).

Along with these
\mathcal{NP}
-hardness results, we also present some positive results for the separation problems of those cutting-planes.
Specifically, we show that when the number of constraints of the LP relaxation (
2
) is constant, and the optimal solution
x^{*}
is an extreme point, then the separation problems for ECIs,
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities, and WIs, are all polynomial-time solvable. See Corollary
1
, Corollary
2
, and Corollary
4
.

We remark that several heuristics and exact separation algorithms are present in the literature for these families of cuts. Both Gabrel and Minoux
gabrel2002scheme
and Kaparis and Letchford
kaparis2010separation
provide an exact separation algorithm for ECIs that runs in pseudo-polynomial time. Ferreira et al.
ferreira1996solving
presented simple heuristics for the separation problem of
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities.
For the separation problem for WIs, Weismantel
weismantel19970
proposed an exact algorithm that runs in pseudo-polynomial time.
Helmberg and Weismantel
MR1607353
presented a fast separation heuristic for WIs that simply inserts items into the pack
P
in non-increasing order of
x^{*}
value.
Kaparis and Letchford
kaparis2010separation
gave two exact algorithms and a heuristic for separating WIs and show how to convert these methods into heuristics for separating LPIs.

Next, we formally define the separation problems considered in this paper.

Problem CI-SP

Input:
(A,d,c)\in(\mathbb{Z}^{m\times n}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+})
and an optimal solution
x^{*}
to the LP relaxation (
2
).

Question:
Is there a cover
C
with respect to some row constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
of (
2
), such that
\sum_{i\in C}x^{*}_{i}>|C|-1
?

Problem ECI-SP

Input:

(A,d,c)\in(\mathbb{Z}^{m\times n}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+})
and an optimal solution
x^{*}
to the LP relaxation (
2
).

Question:
Is there a cover
C
with respect to some row constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
of (
2
), such that
\sum_{j\notin C:a_{j}\geq\max_{i\in C}a_{i}}x_{j}+\sum_{i\in C}x_{i}>|C|-1
?

Problem CONFIG-SP

Input:

(A,d,c)\in(\mathbb{Z}^{m\times n}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+})
and an optimal solution
x^{*}
to the LP relaxation (
2
).

Question:
Is there a
(1,k)
-configuration
N\cup\{t\}
and a subset
S\subseteq N
with
|S|\geq k
with respect to some row constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
of (
2
), such that
(|S|-k+1)x^{*}_{t}+\sum_{i\in S}x^{*}_{i}>|S|
?

Problem WI-SP

Input:

(A,d,c)\in(\mathbb{Z}^{m\times n}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+})
and an optimal solution
x^{*}
to the LP relaxation (
2
).

Question:
Is there a pack
P
with respect to some row constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
of (
2
), such that
\sum_{i\in P}a_{i}x^{*}_{i}+\sum_{j\notin P}\max\{a_{j}-r(P),0\}x^{*}_{j}>\sum_{i\in P}a_{i}
?

For CI-SP, we have the following classic results.

Theorem 1.1
(
klabjan1998complexity
)

CI-SP is
\mathcal{NP}
-complete, even if
m=1
, or if
x^{*}
is an extreme point.

We will show that the other three problems, ECI-SP, CONFIG-SP, and WI-SP are all
\mathcal{NP}
-complete.

Clearly, the
\mathcal{NP}
-hardness of the above problems imply the
\mathcal{NP}
-hardness of the more general separation problem where
x^{*}
is a feasible, and not necessarily optimal, solution to (
2
). We should also remark that, since verifying if a given point violates a given inequality can be obviously done in polynomial time with respect to the input size of such point and inequality, the separation problems for these families of cuts are clearly in the class
\mathcal{NP}
.
Therefore, when we discuss the separation complexity for those families of cuts, the concepts of
\mathcal{NP}
-hardness and
\mathcal{NP}
-completeness coincide.

2
Extended Cover Inequalities

In this section, we establish the complexity of the separation problem for extended cover inequalities, with a simple reduction from the separation problem for cover inequalities.
In the case where the point to be separated has a small number of fractional components, then extended cover inequality separation can be accomplished in polynomial time.

Theorem 2.1

Problem ECI-SP is
\mathcal{NP}
-complete, even if
m=1
, or if
x^{*}
is an extreme point solution to the LP relaxation (
2
).

Proof

We transform CI-SP to ECI-SP.
Let
(A,d,c,x^{*})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{m\times n}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+},[0,1]^{n})
be the input to CI-SP.
We construct the input to ECI-SP with the property that there is a yes-certificate to CI-SP with input
(A,d,c,x^{*})
if and only if there is a yes-certificate to ECI-SP with input
(A^{\prime},d^{\prime},c^{\prime},y^{*})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{m\times(n+1)}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}_{+},[0,1]^{n+1})
.

The data for the ECI-SP instance are constructed as follows:

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{ij}=A_{ij}\ \forall i\in[m],\forall j\in[n],

\displaystyle\qquad A^{\prime}_{i,n+1}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}\ \forall i\in[m],

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{j}=c_{j}\ \forall j\in[n],

\displaystyle\qquad c^{\prime}_{n+1}=M,

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{i}=d_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}\ \forall i\in[m].

The constant
M
is chosen to be large enough so that if
x^{*}
is an optimal solution to the linear program (
2
), then
y^{*}=(x^{*},1)
is an optimal solution to the linear program

\max\{(c^{\prime})^{\mathsf{T}}y:A^{\prime}y\leq d^{\prime},y\in[0,1]^{n+1}\}.

(3)

It is a consequence of linear programming duality that selecting
M\geq(\pi^{*})^{\mathsf{T}}Ae
, where
\pi^{*}
are optimal dual multipliers for the inequality constraints in (
2
), will ensure the optimality of
y^{*}
. Since there is an optimal solution
\pi^{*}
whose encoding length is of polynomial size
Schrijver86
, the encoding size of
M
is a polynomial function of the input size of CI-SP.

Let
C\subseteq[n]
be a cover with respect to a row constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
of
Ax\leq d

such that the associated CI does not hold at
x^{*}
, so
x^{*}(C)>|C|-1
.
Then
C^{\prime}:=C\cup\{n+1\}
is a cover with respect to the constraint
(a^{\mathsf{T}},a^{\mathsf{T}}e)\cdot y\leq b+a^{\mathsf{T}}e
within
A^{\prime}y\leq d^{\prime}
, and the associated ECI cuts off
y^{*}
, since
y^{*}(C^{\prime})=1+x^{*}(C)>|C|=|C^{\prime}|-1
.

On the other hand, assume that
C^{\prime}
is a cover with respect to some row constraint

a^{\prime\mathsf{T}}y=(a^{\mathsf{T}},a^{\mathsf{T}}e)\cdot y\leq b+a^{\mathsf{T}}e=b^{\prime}
within
A^{\prime}y\leq d^{\prime}
such that the associated ECI cuts off
y^{*}
.
Note that if
n+1\notin C^{\prime}
, then
\sum_{j\in C^{\prime}}a^{\prime}_{j}\leq a^{\prime}([n])=a^{\mathsf{T}}e<b+a^{\mathsf{T}}e
, and
C^{\prime}
cannot be a cover with respect to that row constraint. Thus,
n+1\in C^{\prime}
, and the ECI of
C^{\prime}
is just its cover inequality
y(C^{\prime})\leq|C^{\prime}|-1
.
By construction, the set
C:=C^{\prime}\setminus\{n+1\}
is a cover with respect to the constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
within
Ax\leq d
.
The ECI of
C^{\prime}
cuts off
y^{*}
,
y^{*}(C^{\prime})=1+x^{*}(C)>|C^{\prime}|-1=|C|
, so
x^{*}(C)>|C|-1
, and the CI from
C
cuts off
x^{*}
.

We have shown that there is a yes-certificate to CI-SP with input
(A,d,c,x^{*})
if and only if there is a yes-certificate to ECI-SP with input
(A^{\prime},d^{\prime},c^{\prime},y^{*})
.
Together with Theorem
1.1
, this establishes that ECI-SP is
\mathcal{NP}
-complete, even if
m=1
, or if
x^{*}
is an extreme point to the LP relaxation (
2
).
To conclude the proof, it suffices to realize that the input
y^{*}=(x^{*},1)
for ECI-SP will be an extreme point of
\{y\in[0,1]^{n+1}:A^{\prime}y\leq d^{\prime}\}
if
x^{*}
is an extreme point of
\{x\in[0,1]^{n}:Ax\leq d\}
.
∎

In the next theorem, we show that, if the fractional support of the input vector
x^{*}
is “sparse”, then we can separate ECIs in polynomial time.
Here, the fractional support of
x^{*}
denotes the set of index
i
with
x^{*}_{i}\in(0,1)
.

Theorem 2.2

Let
x^{*}
be the input solution to ECI-SP. If
|\{i\in[n]:x^{*}_{i}\in(0,1)\}|\leq\alpha
, then a separating ECI can be obtained in
O(n^{2}\cdot 2^{\alpha}\cdot\log n\cdot m)
time, if one exists.

Proof

For a given point
x^{*}
and constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
of
Ax\leq d
, there exists a separating ECI from the constraint if and only if for some
t\in[n]
, there exists a cover
C
with
\max_{i\in C}a_{i}=a_{t}
, such that

\sum_{i\in[n]:a_{i}\geq a_{t}}x^{*}_{i}+\sum_{i\in C:a_{i}<a_{t}}x^{*}_{i}>|C|-1.

(4)

We partition
C
into four sets,
C=T_{1}\cup T_{f}\cup T_{0}\cup T
, with
T_{1}=\{i\in C:a_{i}<a_{t},x^{*}_{i}=1\}
,
T_{f}=\{i\in C:a_{i}<a_{t},x^{*}_{i}\in(0,1)\}
,
T_{0}=\{i\in C:a_{i}<a_{t},x^{*}_{i}=0\},
and
T=\{i\in C:a_{i}=a_{t}\}
.
With this definition, (
4
) can be equivalently stated as

\sum_{i\in[n]:a_{i}\geq a_{t}}x^{*}_{i}>\sum_{i\in T_{f}}(1-x^{*}_{i})+|T_{0}|+|T|-1.

(5)

The algorithm loops over all
t\in[n]
and enumerates all
T_{f}\subseteq\{i\in[n]:a_{i}<a_{t},x^{*}_{i}\in(0,1)\}
.
By our assumption on the cardinality of fractional support of
x^{*}
, there are
O(n\cdot 2^{\alpha})
iterations.
For a fixed
t\in[n]
and
T_{f}\subseteq C
, the separation problem then amounts to completing the cover
C
so that

|T_{0}|+|T|<\sum_{i\in[n]:a_{i}\geq a_{t}}x_{i}^{*}-\sum_{i\in T_{f}}(1-x_{i}^{*})+1.

(6)

The right-hand side of (
6
) is a constant, so separation for a fixed index
t
and subset
T_{f}
amounts to solving the knapsack problem

\min_{z\in\{0,1\}^{|S_{t}|}}\left\{\sum_{i\in S_{t}}z_{i}:\sum_{i\in S_{t}}a_{i}z_{i}\geq b_{t,T_{f}}\right\},

(7)

where
S_{t}=\{i\in[n]:a_{i}=a_{t}\text{ or }a_{i}<a_{t},x^{*}_{i}=0\}
, and
b_{t,T_{f}}=b+1-\sum_{i\in T_{f}}a_{i}-\sum_{i:a_{i}<a_{t},x^{*}_{i}=1}a_{i}.

As the non-zero objective coefficients of the knapsack problem (
7
) are all the same, the problem can be solved by a simple greedy procedure, after sorting
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in S_{t}}
in
O(n\log n)
time. Therefore, overall this algorithm runs in
O(n^{2}\cdot 2^{\alpha}\cdot\log n)
time, since there are
O(n\cdot 2^{\alpha})
iterations. The theorem then follows by arguing for all
m
constraints.
∎

Theorem
2.2
immediately implies the following corollary.

Corollary 1

If
m
is polylogarithmic in
n
and
x^{*}
is an extreme point solution to (
2
), then a separating ECI can be obtained in polynomial time, if one exists.

Proof

Since
x^{*}
is an extreme point, we know that at most
m
components of
x^{*}
are fractional. Then Theorem
2.2
implies that ECI-SP can be solved in
O(n^{2}\cdot 2^{m}\cdot\log n\cdot m)
time, which is polynomial in
n
since
m
is polylogarithmic in
n
.
∎

3
(1,k)
-Configuration Inequalities

In this section, we establish that the separation problem for
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities is
\mathcal{NP}
-complete using a reduction similar to the one in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 3.1

Problem CONFIG-SP is
\mathcal{NP}
-complete, even if
m=1
, or if
x^{*}
is an extreme point solution to the LP relaxation (
2
).

Proof

We transform CI-SP to CONFIG-SP.
Given an input
(A,d,c,x^{*})
to CI-SP which is in
(\mathbb{Z}^{m\times n}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+},[0,1]^{n})
, we will construct a corresponding input
(A^{\prime},d^{\prime},c^{\prime},y^{*})
to CONFIG-SP such that there is a yes-certificate to CI-SP with input
(A,d,c,x^{*})
if and only if there is a yes-certificate to CONFIG-SP with input
(A^{\prime},d^{\prime},c^{\prime},y^{*})
.

In the construction, the first
n
columns of
A^{\prime}
are the columns of
A
, while the last two columns of
A^{\prime}
are the sum of all other columns; the right-hand side vector in the construction is
d^{\prime}=d+2Ae
; and the first
n
components of
c^{\prime}
are the same as those of
c
, while the last two components are a large positive constant:

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{ij}=A_{ij}\ \forall i\in[m],\forall j\in[n],

\displaystyle\qquad A^{\prime}_{ik}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}\ \forall i\in[m],\forall k\in\{n+1,n+2\},

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{j}=c_{j}\ \forall j\in[n],

\displaystyle\qquad c^{\prime}_{n+1}=c^{\prime}_{n+2}=M,

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{i}=d_{i}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}\ \forall i\in[m].

The constant
M
is chosen to be large enough so that if
x^{*}
is an optimal solution to the linear program (
2
), then
y^{*}=(x^{*},1,1)
is an optimal solution to the linear program

\max\{(c^{\prime})^{\mathsf{T}}y:A^{\prime}y\leq d^{\prime},y\in[0,1]^{n+2}\}.

(8)

It is a consequence of linear programming duality that selecting
M\geq(\pi^{*})^{\mathsf{T}}Ae
, where
\pi^{*}
are optimal dual multipliers for the inequality constraints in (
2
), will ensure the optimality of
y^{*}
.
As it is know that there is an optimal solution
\pi^{*}
whose encoding length is of polynomial size
Schrijver86
, we see that
M
exists and its encoding size is a polynomial function of the input size of CI-SP.

Let
C\subseteq[n]
be a cover with respect to the row constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
of
Ax\leq d
such that the associated CI cuts off
x^{*}:x^{*}(C)>|C|-1
. Let
C^{\prime}:=C\cup\{n+1,n+2\}
. By definition of the input
(A^{\prime},d^{\prime})
,
C^{\prime}
is a cover with respect to the row constraint
a^{\prime\mathsf{T}}y=(a^{\mathsf{T}},a^{\mathsf{T}}e,a^{\mathsf{T}}e)y\leq b+2a^{\mathsf{T}}e=b^{\prime}

within
A^{\prime}y\leq d^{\prime}
, and the associated CI cuts off
y^{*}:y^{*}(C^{\prime})=2+x^{*}(C)>|C|+1=|C^{\prime}|-1
. As every cover inequality is dominated by a minimal cover inequality, there is a a minimal cover contained in
C^{\prime}
whose associated minimal CI cuts off
y^{*}
.
Every minimal CI is a special case of
(1,k)
-configuration inequality, so we have obtained a
(1,k)
-configuration inequality with respect to a row constraint within
A^{\prime}y\leq d^{\prime}
that cuts off
y^{*}
.

To complete the proof, we must show that if
N\cup\{t\}
is a
(1,k)
-configuration with respect to some row constraint
a^{\prime\mathsf{T}}y\leq b^{\prime}
within
A^{\prime}y\leq d^{\prime}
, and

.

By construction of

is a row constraint in

.
First, observe that in the constraint
(a^{\mathsf{T}},a^{\mathsf{T}}e,a^{\mathsf{T}}e)y\leq b+2a^{\mathsf{T}}e

and
n+2

By definition of a
(1,k)
-configuration, for any subset

is a minimal cover. Specifically,
N\cup\{t\}
is a cover.

must contain

or

if and only if there is a yes-certificate to CONFIG-SP with input

Together with Theorem

To conclude the proof, it suffices to realize that the input

∎

We have settled the complexity of the separation problem for
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities, for an input solution
x^{*}
that is an extreme point to the LP-relaxation (
2
).
In our preliminary IPCO version
10.1007/978-3-031-06901-7_13
, we conjectured the separation problem to be
\mathcal{NP}
-complete, even for points

with a small number of fractional components.
However, we are able to refute this conjecture.
We show that, in this special case, the separation problem of
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities is in fact polynomially solvable.
The following easy lemma gives an equivalent condition for a set
N\cup\{t\}
to be a
(1,k)
-configuration.

Lemma 1

For knapsack constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
with
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\ldots\leq a_{n}
, set
N\cup\{t\}\subseteq[n]
is a
(1,k)
-configuration with respect to the knapsack constraint if and only if
a(N)\leq b
,
a_{t}+a(N^{[k-1]})\leq b
, and
a_{t}+a(N_{[k]})>b
.

Recall that, in the statement of Lemma
1
,
N^{[k-1]}
denotes the set of the
k-1
largest elements in
N
and
N_{[k]}
denotes the set of the
k
smallest elements in
N
.

Proof

By definition of
(1,k)
-configuration, it suffices to check that, when
a(N)\leq b
,
Q\cup\{t\}
is a minimal cover for every
Q\subseteq N
with
|Q|=k
if and only if
a_{t}+a(N^{[k-1]})\leq b
and
a_{t}+a(N_{[k]})>b
.

Picking
Q=N_{[k]}
,
Q\cup\{t\}
being a cover implies that
a_{t}+a(N_{[k]})>b
. Picking
Q=N^{[k]}
,
Q\cup\{t\}
being a minimal cover implies that
a_{t}+a(N^{[k-1]})\leq b
.
Next we want to show that, for any
Q\subseteq N
with
|Q|=k
, if
a(N)\leq b
,
a_{t}+a(N^{[k-1]})\leq b
, and
a_{t}+a(N_{[k]})>b
, then
Q\cup\{t\}
is a minimal cover.
By assumption
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\ldots\leq a_{n}
, we know that
a(Q)\geq a(N_{[k]})
.
So
a(Q)+a_{t}\geq a(N_{[k]})+a_{t}>b
implies that
Q\cup\{t\}
is a cover. Now arbitrarily pick
i^{\prime}\in Q\cup\{t\}
.
If
i^{\prime}=t
, then
a(Q)\leq a(N)\leq b
; If
i^{\prime}\in Q
, then
a(Q\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})\leq a(N^{[k-1]})
, which gives
a(Q\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})+a_{t}\leq a_{t}+a(N^{[k-1]})\leq b
.
Hence,
Q\cup\{t\}
is a minimal cover.
∎

\underline{S},\bar{S},T_{f},T_{1}^{c}\subseteq[n]
with
|\underline{S}|=|\bar{S}|+1\leq 2\alpha
,
T_{f}\subseteq\{i\in[n]:x^{*}_{i}\in(0,1)\}
, and
|T_{1}^{c}|\leq 2\alpha-1

can be upper bounded by

n\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{2\alpha}\binom{n}{k}\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{2\alpha-1}\binom{n}{k}\cdot 2^{\alpha}\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{2\alpha-1}\binom{n}{k}=O(n^{6\alpha-1}\cdot 2^{\alpha}).

Hence, the above procedure can be implemented in polynomial time.

From the discussion provided in the above two cases, the proof is complete.
∎

Similarly to how Corollary
1
directly follows from Theorem
2.2
, Theorem
3.2
implies the following corollary.

Corollary 2

If
m
is a constant and
x^{*}
is an extreme point solution to (
2
), then a separating
(1,k)
-configuration inequality can be obtained in polynomial time, if one exists.

4
Generalized
(1,k)
-Configuration Inequalities

In this section, we study a simple generalization of the
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities, which we call
generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities
.

Definition 1

For knapsack constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
, a set
N\cup\{t\}
with
N\subsetneq[n]
and
t\notin N
is called a
generalized
(1,k)
-configuration
for
k\leq|N|
, if
Q\cup\{t\}
is a cover for every
Q\subseteq N
with
|Q|=k
. For any generalized
(1,k)
-configuration
N\cup\{t\}
, the inequality

(n^{\prime}-k+1)x_{t}+\sum_{i\in N}x_{i}\leq n^{\prime}

(19)

is called a
generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequality
. Here,
n^{\prime}
is the smallest number such that inequality
\sum_{i\in N}x_{i}\leq n^{\prime}
is valid for
K
.

It is easy to see that any
(1,k)
-configuration inequality is a generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequality.
In fact, when
a(N)\leq b
and
Q\cup\{t\}
is a
minimal
cover for every
Q\subseteq N
with
|Q|=k
, then the inequality (
19
) becomes a
(1,k)
-configuration inequality.
The next simple theorem states that any generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequality is also valid for the knapsack polytope
K
.

Theorem 4.1

Let
N\cup\{t\}
be a generalized
(1,k)
-configuration with respect to knapsack constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
. Then inequality (
19
) is valid for
K
.

Proof

Arbitrarily pick
x^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
with
a^{\mathsf{T}}x^{\prime}\leq b
.
We consider the component
x^{\prime}_{t}
.
If
x^{\prime}_{t}=0
, then (
19
) reduces to
\sum_{i\in N}x^{\prime}_{i}\leq n^{\prime}
, which is true because of the assumption on
n^{\prime}
.
If
x^{\prime}_{t}=1
, then (
19
) reduces to
\sum_{i\in N}x^{\prime}_{i}\leq k-1
. If
\sum_{i\in N}x^{\prime}_{i}\geq k
, then arbitrarily pick
Q\subseteq\{i\in N:x^{\prime}_{i}=1\}
with
|Q|=k
. By definition of generalized
(1,k)
-configuration, we know that
Q\cup\{t\}
is a cover. However,
a(Q\cup\{t\})\leq a^{\mathsf{T}}x^{\prime}\leq b
, which gives the contradiction.
∎

The next two examples show that the class of generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities is strictly broader than the class of
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities.
In particular, by relaxing either the “minimal cover” assumption, or the assumption
a(N)\leq b
, in the definition of
(1,k)
-configuration inequality, one is able to obtain different facet-defining inequalities.

Example 1

Consider the knapsack constraint
(2,4,5,6,7,20)^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq 30
.
For
N=\{1,2,3,4,5\}
and
k=3
,
N\cup\{6\}
is a generalized
(1,k)
-configuration, but it is not a
(1,k)
-configuration.
This is because
\{4,5,6\}
is a cover, so
\{3,4,5\}\cup\{t\}
cannot be a minimal cover.
However, for any
Q\subseteq N
with
|Q|=3
, we have
a_{6}+a(Q)\geq a_{6}+a(N_{[3]})=a_{6}+a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}=31>30
.
Furthermore, the corresponding generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequality

\sum_{i=1}^{5}x_{i}+3x_{6}\leq 5
is facet-defining.
Similarly, another facet-defining inequality
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{4}+x_{5}+2x_{6}\leq 4
is a generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequality from
N=\{1,2,4,5\}
,
t=6
,
k=3
, while it is not a
(1,k)
-configuration inequality.
Notice that in both cases, the
(1,k)
-configuration
N\cup\{t\}
satisfies
a(N)\leq b
, so the only assumption violated in the definition of
(1,k)
-configuration inequality is the “minimal cover” assumption.

\hfill\diamond

Example 2

Consider the knapsack constraint
(2,4,7,10,10,20)^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq 30
.
Consider
N=\{2,3,4,5\}
,
t=6
,
k=2
.
Then
a(N)=31>30
, and
\sum_{i\in N}x_{i}\leq n^{\prime}:=3
is valid for
K
, and for any
Q\subseteq N
with
|Q|=2
,
Q\cup\{t\}
is a minimal cover.
So
N\cup\{t\}
is a generalized
(1,k)
-configuration and not a
(1,k)
-configuration.
The only assumption violated from the definition of
(1,k)
-configuration is
a(N)\leq b
.
Furthermore, the corresponding generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequality
\sum_{i=2}^{5}x_{i}+2x_{6}\leq 3
is facet-defining.

\hfill\diamond

We denote the separation problem associated with generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities by
G-CONFIG-SP
.
The proof of the following
\mathcal{NP}
-hardness result about G-CONFIG-SP uses the same reduction technique as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 4.2

Problem G-CONFIG-SP is
\mathcal{NP}
-complete, even if
m=1
, or if
x^{*}
is an extreme point solution to the LP relaxation (
2
).

Proof

Using the same reduction as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we want to show that:
given an input
(A,d,c,x^{*})
to CI-SP which is in
(\mathbb{Z}^{m\times n}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+},\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+},[0,1]^{n})
, we can find a corresponding input
(A^{\prime},d^{\prime},c^{\prime},y^{*})
to G-CONFIG-SP such that there is a yes-certificate to CI-SP with input
(A,d,c,x^{*})
if and only if there is a yes-certificate to G-CONFIG-SP with input
(A^{\prime},d^{\prime},c^{\prime},y^{*})
.

Recall the construction in the proof of Theorem
3.1
:

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{ij}=A_{ij}\ \forall i\in[m],\forall j\in[n],

\displaystyle\qquad A^{\prime}_{ik}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}\ \forall i\in[m],\forall k\in\{n+1,n+2\},

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{j}=c_{j}\ \forall j\in[n],

\displaystyle\qquad c^{\prime}_{n+1}=c^{\prime}_{n+2}=M,

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{i}=d_{i}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}\ \forall i\in[m].

As shown previously, when
M
is chosen to be large enough, then
y^{*}:=(x^{*},1,1)
is an optimal solution to LP

\max\{(c^{\prime})^{\mathsf{T}}y:A^{\prime}y\leq d^{\prime},y\in[0,1]^{n+2}\}

(20)

if
x^{*}
is an optimal solution to the linear program (
2
).

Let
C\subseteq[n]
be a cover with respect to the row constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
of
Ax\leq d
such that the associated CI cuts off
x^{*}:x^{*}(C)>|C|-1
. Let
C^{\prime}:=C\cup\{n+1,n+2\}
. By definition of the input
(A^{\prime},d^{\prime})
,
C^{\prime}
is a cover with respect to the row constraint
a^{\prime\mathsf{T}}y=(a^{\mathsf{T}},a^{\mathsf{T}}e,a^{\mathsf{T}}e)y\leq b+2a^{\mathsf{T}}e=b^{\prime}
within
A^{\prime}y\leq d^{\prime}
, and the associated CI cuts off
y^{*}:y^{*}(C^{\prime})=2+x^{*}(C)>|C|+1=|C^{\prime}|-1
.
As every cover inequality is also a generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequality, we have obtained a generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequality with respect to a row constraint within
A^{\prime}y\leq d^{\prime}
that cuts off
y^{*}
.

To complete the proof, we must show that if
N\cup\{t\}
is a generalized
(1,k)
-configuration with respect to some row constraint
a^{\prime\mathsf{T}}y\leq b^{\prime}
within
A^{\prime}y\leq d^{\prime}
, and

(n^{\prime}-k+1)y^{*}_{t}+\sum_{i\in N}y^{*}_{i}>n^{\prime}
, for some
n^{\prime}
such that
\sum_{i\in N}x_{i}\leq n^{\prime}
is valid for
\{x\in\{0,1\}^{n+2}:a^{\prime\mathsf{T}}y\leq b^{\prime}\}
, then we can construct a cover
C
with respect to the associated row constraint in
Ax\leq b
such that the associated CI cuts off
x^{*}
.

By construction of
(A^{\prime},d^{\prime})
, we know that the row constraint
a^{\prime\mathsf{T}}y\leq b^{\prime}
takes the form
(a^{\mathsf{T}},a^{\mathsf{T}}e,a^{\mathsf{T}}e)y\leq b+2a^{\mathsf{T}}e
, where
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
is a row constraint in
Ax\leq b
.
Observe that in the constraint
(a^{\mathsf{T}},a^{\mathsf{T}}e,a^{\mathsf{T}}e)y\leq b+2a^{\mathsf{T}}e
, any cover must contain both
n+1
and
n+2
. By definition of generalized
(1,k)
-configuration, we know
\{n+1,n+2\}\subseteq N\cup\{t\}
.
Next, we continue our discussion by considering separately two cases.

Case 1:

t\notin\{n+1,n+2\}
. Then
\{n+1,n+2\}\subseteq N
. By definition of
(1,k)
-configuration, we know that, for any
Q\subseteq N
with
|Q|=k
,
Q\cup\{t\}
is a cover.
This means
\{n+1,n+2\}\subseteq Q
for any
Q\subseteq N
with
|Q|=k
. Hence
k=|N|
, and we have

(n^{\prime}-|N|+1)y^{*}_{t}+\sum_{i\in N}y^{*}_{i}>n^{\prime}.

(21)

Here,
n^{\prime}
is such that
\sum_{i\in N}x_{i}\leq n^{\prime}
is valid for
\{x\in\{0,1\}^{n+2}:a^{\prime\mathsf{T}}y\leq b^{\prime}\}
.
Notice that
N\setminus\{n+2\}
is not a cover to
a^{\prime\mathsf{T}}y\leq b^{\prime}
, so
n^{\prime}\geq|N|-1
.
If
n^{\prime}=|N|-1
, then
N
is a cover to
a^{\prime\mathsf{T}}y\leq b^{\prime}
, and (
21
) is simply
y^{*}(N)>|N|-1
.
Note that
\{n+1,n+2\}\subseteq N
, and
a^{\prime\mathsf{T}}=(a^{\mathsf{T}},a^{\mathsf{T}}e,a^{\mathsf{T}}e)
,
b^{\prime}=b+2a^{\mathsf{T}}e
,
y^{*}_{n+1}=y^{*}_{n+2}=1
, hence
C:=N\setminus\{n+1,n+2\}
is a cover to
a^{\mathsf{T}}y\leq b
, and
y^{*}(C)=y^{*}(N)-2>|N|-3=|C|-1
.
If
n^{\prime}=|N|
, then (
21
) gives
y^{*}_{t}+\sum_{i\in N}y^{*}_{i}>|N|
.
Hence
C:=\{t\}\cup N\setminus\{n+1,n+2\}
is a cover to
a^{\mathsf{T}}y\leq b
, and
y^{*}(C)=y^{*}_{t}+\sum_{i\in N}y^{*}_{i}-y^{*}_{n+1}-y^{*}_{n+2}>|N|-2=|C|-1
.

Case 2:

t\in\{n+1,n+2\}
.
Since
a^{\prime}_{n+1}=a^{\prime}_{n+2}=a^{\mathsf{T}}e
, without loss of generality, we assume
t=n+2
.
Because for any
Q\subseteq N
with
|Q|=k
,
Q\cup\{t\}
is a cover, and any cover to
a^{\prime\mathsf{T}}x\leq b^{\prime}
should contain both
n+1
and
n+2
, we know that
k=|N|
and
n+1\in N
. Moreover, since
t=n+2\notin N
, then
N
is not a cover to
a^{\prime\mathsf{T}}x\leq b^{\prime}
. Hence
n^{\prime}=|N|
. Therefore, the separating generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequality gives:

y^{*}_{n+2}+\sum_{i\in N}y^{*}_{i}>|N|.

(22)

Here
N\cup\{n+2\}
is a cover to
a^{\prime\mathsf{T}}\leq b^{\prime}
.
By definition of
a^{\prime},b^{\prime},
and
y^{*}
, we know that
C:=N\setminus\{n+1\}
is a cover to
a^{\mathsf{T}}\leq b
, whose associated CI separates
x^{*}
.

We have thereby shown that there is a yes-certificate to CI-SP with input
(A,d,c,x^{*})
if and only if there is a yes-certificate to G-CONFIG-SP with input
(A^{\prime},d^{\prime},c^{\prime},y^{*})
.
Together with Theorem
1.1
, our proof establishes that G-CONFIG-SP is
\mathcal{NP}
-complete, even if
m=1
, or if
x^{*}
is an extreme point to the LP relaxation (
2
).
To conclude the proof, it suffices to realize that the input
y^{*}=(x^{*},1,1)
for G-CONFIG-SP will be an extreme point of
\{y\in[0,1]^{n+2}:A^{\prime}y\leq d^{\prime}\}
if
x^{*}
is an extreme point of
\{x\in[0,1]^{n}:Ax\leq d\}
.
∎

Since the definition of generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities is simpler than the definition of
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities, from Theorem
3.2
, one can expect that G-CONFIG-SP is polynomially solvable when the fractional support of
x^{*}
has relatively small size.

Theorem 4.3

Let
x^{*}
be the input solution to G-CONFIG-SP. If
|\{i\in[n]:x^{*}_{i}\in(0,1)\}|\leq\alpha
, then a separating generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequality can be obtained in
O(n^{2}\cdot 2^{\alpha}\cdot\log n\cdot m)
time, if one exists.

Proof

Let
N\cup\{t\}
be a generalized
(1,k)
-configuration with respect to some row
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
, whose corresponding inequality (
19
) separates
x^{*}
:

(n^{\prime}-k+1)x^{*}_{t}+x^{*}(N)>n^{\prime}.

(23)

We assume that
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\ldots\leq a_{n}
.

Claim

There exists
N^{*}\subseteq[n]\setminus\{t\}
and
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
N^{*}\cup\{t\}
is a generalized
(1,k^{\prime})
-configuration whose corresponding inequality (
19
) separates
x^{*}
, and
\{i\in[n]\setminus\{t\}:x^{*}_{i}=1\}\subseteq N^{*}
,
\{i\in N^{*}:x^{*}_{i}=0\}=\emptyset
.

Proof of claim

If there exists
i^{\prime}\in[n]\setminus\{t\}
with
x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}=1
and
i^{\prime}\notin N
, then for
N^{\prime}:=N\cup\{i^{\prime}\}
,
N^{\prime}\cup\{t\}
is a generalized
(1,k+1)
-configuration. Since
x(N)\leq n^{\prime}
is valid for
\{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}:a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b\}
, then
x(N^{\prime})=x(N)+x_{i^{\prime}}\leq n^{\prime}+1
is also valid for
\{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}:a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b\}
. Therefore, we obtain a generalized
(1,k+1)
-configuration inequality
(n^{\prime}-k+1)x_{t}+x(N^{\prime})\leq n^{\prime}+1
, which cuts off
x^{*}
because of (
23
) and
x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}=1
.

If there exists
i^{\prime\prime}\in N
such that
x^{*}_{i^{\prime\prime}}=0
, then for
N^{\prime\prime}:=N\setminus\{i^{\prime\prime}\},
we have
|N^{\prime\prime}|\geq k
.
This is because, otherwise,
|N|=k
, and (
23
) gives
(n^{\prime}-|N|+1)x^{*}_{t}+x^{*}(N^{\prime\prime})>n^{\prime}.

Note that
x^{*}_{t}\leq 1
and
x^{*}(N^{\prime\prime})\leq|N^{\prime\prime}|=|N|-1
, thus we have
n^{\prime}<(n^{\prime}-|N|+1)x^{*}_{t}+x^{*}(N^{\prime\prime})\leq n^{\prime}-|N|+1+|N|-1=n^{\prime}
, which gives a contradiction.
Therefore,
N^{\prime\prime}\cup\{t\}
is a generalized
(1,k)
-configuration, whose corresponding inequality (
19
) is
(n^{\prime}-k+1)x_{t}+x(N^{\prime\prime})\leq n^{\prime}
, and which cuts off
x^{*}
because of (
23
) and
x^{*}_{i^{\prime\prime}}=0
.

Applying the above arguments recursively, eventually we will end up with a set
N^{*}\subseteq[n]\setminus\{t\}
and
k^{\prime}\geq k
, such that
N^{*}\cup\{t\}
is a generalized
(1,k^{\prime})
-configuration whose corresponding inequality (
19
) separates
x^{*}
.

\hfill\diamond
From the above claim, without loss of generality, we can assume that
\{i\in[n]\setminus\{t\}:x^{*}_{i}=1\}\subseteq N
and
\{i\in N:x^{*}_{i}=0\}=\emptyset
.
Therefore, G-CONFIG-SP can be solved by the following procedure:

1.

Arbitrarily pick
t\in[n]
and set
N_{f}\subseteq\{i\in[n]\setminus\{t\}:x^{*}_{i}\in(0,1)\}
.

2.

Let
N:=\{i\in[n]\setminus\{t\}:x^{*}_{i}=1\}\cup N_{f}
. If
a(N)+a_{t}\leq b
, then stop.

3.

Let
k
be the smallest integer number such that
a_{t}+a(N_{[k]})>b
, and let
n^{\prime}
be the smallest integer number such that
x(N)\leq n^{\prime}
is valid for
\{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}:a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b\}
. Check if (
23
) holds.

4.

If the answer to the previous check is yes for some
t
and
N_{f}
, then the corresponding
N\cup\{t\}
provides a yes-certificate to G-CONFIG-SP, since the associated generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequality separates
x^{*}
;
If the answer is no for all
t
and
N_{f}
, then
x^{*}
cannot be separated by any generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequality from the knapsack constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
.

Here step 2 runs in
O(n)
time, step 3 runs in
O(n\log n)
time.
Since
|\{i\in[n]:x^{*}_{i}\in(0,1)\}|\leq\alpha
, we have

|\left\{N_{f}:N_{f}\subseteq\{i\in[n]\setminus\{t\}:x^{*}_{i}\in(0,1)\}\right\}|\leq 2^{\alpha}.

So the above procedure can be implemented in
O(n^{2}\cdot 2^{\alpha}\cdot\log n)
time,
and we complete the proof by applying the above argument over all knapsack constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
.
∎

Compared with the separating scheme in Theorem
3.2
, the above theorem suggests that it is in fact easier to separate the generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequality, compared with the standard
(1,k)
-configuration inequality, even though the later one is a special case.
For generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequalities, we also directly obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3

If
m
is polylogarithmic in
n
and
x^{*}
is an extreme point solution to (
2
), then a separating generalized
(1,k)
-configuration inequality can be obtained in polynomial time, if one exists.

5
Weight Inequalities

In this section, we show that WI-SP is
\mathcal{NP}
-hard, and we present special cases where it can be solved in polynomial time.
For a pack
P
of a given knapsack constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
, we denote by
C(P):=\{i\in[n]\setminus P:a_{i}>r(P)\}
.
With this notation, the WI associated with
P
takes the form

\sum_{i\in P}a_{i}x_{i}+\sum_{j\in C(P)}(a_{j}-r(P))x_{j}\leq a(P),

where we remind the reader that
r(P):=b-a(P)
.
We will need the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 2

Let
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}_{+}
with
a([n])/b\notin\mathbb{Z}
, and let
x^{*}_{1}=\ldots=x^{*}_{n}=b/a([n])
.
Then there exists a pack
P
of
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
whose associated WI separates
x^{*}
if and only if there exists a pack
P^{\prime}
of
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
such that
r(P^{\prime})>0
,
P^{\prime}\cup C(P^{\prime})=[n]
, and
|C(P^{\prime})|=\lfloor a([n])/b\rfloor
.

Proof

First, assume that there exists a pack
P^{\prime}
of
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
such that
r(P^{\prime})>0
,
P^{\prime}\cup C(P^{\prime})=[n]
, and
|C(P^{\prime})|=\lfloor a([n])/b\rfloor
.
We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in P^{\prime}}a_{i}x_{i}^{*}+\sum_{j\in C(P^{\prime})}(a_{j}-r(P^{\prime}))x^{*}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in P^{\prime}\cup C(P^{\prime})}a_{i}x^{*}_{i}-r(P^{\prime})\sum_{j\in C(P^{\prime})}x^{*}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[n]}a_{i}x^{*}_{i}-r(P^{\prime})\sum_{j\in C(P^{\prime})}x^{*}_{j}

\displaystyle=b-r(P^{\prime})\cdot|C(P^{\prime})|\cdot\frac{b}{a([n])}

\displaystyle>b-r(P^{\prime})

\displaystyle=a(P^{\prime}),

where the inequality holds because
r(P^{\prime})>0
,
a([n])/b\notin\mathbb{Z}
, and
|C(P^{\prime})|=\lfloor a([n])/b\rfloor
.
Therefore, we know that the WI associated with pack
P^{\prime}
separates
x^{*}
.

Next, assume that there exists a pack
P
of
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
whose associated WI separates
x^{*}
.
Namely:

f(P):=\sum_{i\in P}a_{i}x^{*}_{i}+\sum_{j\in C(P)}(a_{j}-r(P))x^{*}_{j}-a(P)>0.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
P
has the largest value
f(P)
among all packs of the knapsack constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
.
By re-arranging the terms and using some basic algebra, we have that
f(P)>0
implies
r(P)>0
and

\sum_{j\in C(P)}x^{*}_{j}<\frac{\sum_{i\in P\cup C(P)}a_{i}x^{*}_{i}-a(P)}{r(P)}.

Since
\sum_{i\in P\cup C(P)}a_{i}x^{*}_{i}\leq b
,
we obtain

\sum_{j\in C(P)}x^{*}_{j}<\frac{b-a(P)}{r(P)}=1.

Replacing
\sum_{j\in C(P)}x^{*}_{j}=|C(P)|\cdot b/a([n])
, we have shown

|C(P)|<\frac{a([n])}{b}.

(24)

Now let
i^{\prime}\in P
.
Note that we have
r(P\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})=r(P)+a_{i^{\prime}}
, so we obtain

\displaystyle f(P)-f(P\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})

\displaystyle=a_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j\in C(P\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}-1\right)+\sum_{j\in C(P)\setminus C(P\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})}\left(a_{j}-r(P)\right)x^{*}_{j}

\displaystyle\in\left[a_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j\in C(P\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}-1\right),\ a_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j\in C(P)}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}-1\right)\right],

where the last relation holds because
a_{j}>r(P)
for every
j\in C(P)
and
a_{j}-r(P)=a_{j}-r(P\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})+a_{i^{\prime}}\leq a_{i^{\prime}}
for every
j\notin C(P\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})
.
Since
P\setminus\{i^{\prime}\}
is clearly also a pack, our maximality assumption on
f(P)
implies that
f(P)\geq f(P\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})
.
Hence we have
a_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j\in C(P)}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}-1\right)\geq 0
.
This implies that
\sum_{j\in C(P)}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}\geq 1
for any
i^{\prime}\in P
.
Since
x^{*}_{1}=\ldots=x^{*}_{n}=b/a([n])
, we obtain

|C(P)|\geq\frac{a([n])}{b}-1.

Combined with (
24
) and the assumption that
a([n])/b\notin\mathbb{Z}
, we have:

|C(P)|=\left\lfloor\frac{a([n])}{b}\right\rfloor.

To complete the proof, we only need to show that
P\cup C(P)=[n]
.
If not, there exists
i^{\prime}\in[n]\setminus(P\cup C(P))
, such that
P\cup\{i^{\prime}\}
remains a pack.
Hence

\displaystyle f(P\cup\{i^{\prime}\})-f(P)

\displaystyle=a_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j\in C(P)}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}-1\right)+\sum_{j\in C(P\cup\{i^{\prime}\})\setminus C(P)}\left(a_{j}-r(P\cup\{i^{\prime}\})\right)x^{*}_{j}

\displaystyle\in\left[a_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j\in C(P)}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}-1\right),\ a_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j\in C(P\cup\{i^{\prime}\})}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}-1\right)\right].

Since
|C(P)|=\lfloor a([n])/b\rfloor
and
a([n])/b\notin\mathbb{Z}
, we know that
\sum_{j\in C(P)}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}>1
, which implies that

f(P\cup\{i^{\prime}\})>f(P)
.
This contradicts the maximality assumption on
f(P)
and the fact that
P\cup\{i^{\prime}\}
is a pack.
We have thereby shown
P\cup C(P)=[n]
.
∎

\mathcal{NP}
-hard, we establish a reduction from the

to WI-SP.

Problem SSP

Input:

and

.

Question:

such that

The SSP is among Karp’s 21
\mathcal{NP}
-complete problems
karp1972reducibility
.
It is simple to check that SSP is
\mathcal{NP}
-complete even if

.
We are now ready to prove that WI-SP is
\mathcal{NP}

Theorem 5.1

Problem WI-SP is
\mathcal{NP}

, or if

is an extreme point solution to the LP relaxation (

Proof

First, we prove the first part of the statement.
We show that WI-SP is
\mathcal{NP}
-hard even in case of a single knapsack constraint.
Given an instance
(\alpha,w)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}_{+}
of SSP with
w>\max(\alpha)
, we construct a knapsack problem
\max\{c^{\mathsf{T}}x:a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b,x\in\{0,1\}^{2n+2}\}
and give an optimal solution
x^{*}
to the associated LP relaxation.
The data
a,b,c
of the constructed knapsack problem is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&a_{i}:=\alpha_{i}+2,\qquad\forall i=1,\ldots,n,\\
&a_{n+1}:=w\cdot(n+1)+2(n+1)^{2}-3n-\alpha([n]),\\
&a_{n+1+j}:=2,\qquad\forall j=1,\ldots,n+1,\\
&b:=w+2n+3,\\
&c:=a,\\
&x^{*}_{1}:=\ldots:=x^{*}_{2n+2}:=\frac{w+2n+3}{w\cdot(n+1)+2n^{2}+5n+4}.\end{split}

(25)

It is simple to check that
a,b,c
are all integral, that
(a,b,c,x^{*})
has polynomial encoding size with respect to that of
(\alpha,w)
, and that
a^{\mathsf{T}}x^{*}=b
.

is an optimal solution to the knapsack problem described by (

is a feasible input to WI-SP where

.
Note that
(w\cdot(n+1)+2n^{2}+5n+4)/(w+2n+3)=n+1+1/(w+2n+3)\notin\mathbb{Z}
.
Hence, we can apply Lemma

and obtain that there exists a separating WI for

if and only if there exists a pack

\begin{split}&r(P)>0,\qquad P\cup C(P)=[2n+2],\\
&|C(P)|=\left\lfloor\frac{w\cdot(n+1)+2n^{2}+5n+4}{w+2n+3}\right\rfloor=n+1.\end{split}

(26)

Claim

There exists a WI from constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
that separates

if and only if there exists a subset

such that
\alpha(S)=w

Proof of claim

It suffices to show that there exists pack
P
such that (

) holds if and only if there exists a subset

such that
\alpha(S)=w
.

First, we assume that
P
is a pack such that (

) holds.
The two equations in (

) imply
|P|=2n+2-|C(P)|=n+1
.
If
\{n+2,n+3,\ldots,2n+2\}\cap C(P)=\emptyset
, then
P\cup C(P)=[2n+2]
implies that
\{n+2,n+3,\ldots,2n+2\}\subseteq P
, which means
P=\{n+2,n+3,\ldots,2n+2\}
since
|P|=n+1
.
However, since
w>\max(\alpha)
, we know that

is a pack for any

, and this contradicts the assumption
C(P)=[2n+2]\setminus P
of (

Therefore, there must exist some

.
Hence
r(P)=b-a(P)<a_{i^{\prime}}=2
.
Moreover, because

, we have
r(P)=1
, which implies
a(P)=b-1=w+2n+2
.
Since
a_{n+1}=w\cdot(n+1)+2(n+1)^{2}-3n-\alpha([n])\geq w+2(n+1)^{2}-3n+(w\cdot n-\alpha([n]))>w+2n+2
, we know

Let
S:=P\cap[n]
.
We then obtain

and
a(P\setminus S)=2(|P|-|S|)=2(n+1-|S|)
.
Therefore,
w+2n+2=a(P)=a(S)+a(P\setminus S)=\alpha(S)+2n+2
, which gives us
\alpha(S)=w.

Next, we assume that
S
is a subset of
[n]
with
\alpha(S)=w
.
Clearly,

.
Then, we define the set
\tilde{S}
containing

arbitrary indices from

.
Then

is a pack such that (

) holds.
In fact, we have

\displaystyle r(P)

\displaystyle=b-a(P)

\displaystyle=w+2n+3-a(S)-a(\tilde{S})

\displaystyle=w+2n+3-(2|S|+\alpha(S))-2(n+1-|S|)

\displaystyle=1.

This further implies

and
|C(P)|=2n+2-|P|=n+1
, since
a_{i}>1
for all
i\in[2n+2]
. Hence (
26
) is satisfied by pack
P
.

\hfill\diamond

Claim
Claim
completes the proof of the first part of the statement, since SSP itself is
\mathcal{NP}
-hard.

Next, we prove the second part of the statement.
We show that WI-SP is
\mathcal{NP}
-hard, even if

is an extreme point solution to the LP relaxation (

Given an instance

of SSP with

&y_{i}+2z_{1}+2z_{2}+2z_{3}\leq 3+\epsilon,\qquad\forall i\in[N].\end{split}

(27)

Here

Even though the problem WI-SP is
\mathcal{NP}
-hard in general, in the next theorem we provide a special case where it can be solved in polynomial time, and a separating WI can be obtained in polynomial time, if one exists.

Theorem 5.2

Let
x^{*}
be the input solution to WI-SP. If
\max\{|S|:x^{*}_{i}\in(0,1)\ \forall i\in S,x^{*}(S)<1\}
is bounded by a constant, then a separating WI can be obtained in polynomial time, if one exists.

Proof

We assume without loss of generality that
Ax\leq d
has only a single constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
, since we can always solve WI-SP with input
(A,d,c,x^{*})
by solving the corresponding WI-SP problems for each single constraint individually.
For any
P\subseteq[n]
, let
f(P):=\sum_{i\in P}a_{i}x^{*}_{i}+\sum_{j\in C(P)}(a_{j}-r(P))x^{*}_{j}-a(P).
Then
f(P)>0
implies

\sum_{j\in C(P)}x^{*}_{j}<\frac{\sum_{i\in P\cup C(P)}a_{i}x^{*}_{i}-a(P)}{r(P)}\leq\frac{b-a(P)}{r(P)}=1.

Among all the packs with the largest
f(P)
value, let
P^{\prime}
be one that is inclusion-wise maximal.
In other words,
f(P^{\prime})\geq f(P)
for any pack
P
, and
f(P)=f(P^{\prime})
implies that
P^{\prime}
is not contained in
P
.
Let
C:=C(P^{\prime})
.
Note that for any
i^{\prime}\in P^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle f(P^{\prime})-f(P^{\prime}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})

\displaystyle=a_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j\in C(P^{\prime}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}-1\right)+\sum_{j\in C\setminus C(P^{\prime}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})}\left(a_{j}-r(P^{\prime})\right)x^{*}_{j}

\displaystyle\in\left[a_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j\in C(P^{\prime}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}-1\right),a_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j\in C}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}-1\right)\right].

Here, the last inequality
f(P^{\prime})-f(P^{\prime}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})\leq a_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j\in C}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}-1\right)
holds because
a_{j}\leq a_{i^{\prime}}+r(P^{\prime})
for any
j\in C\setminus C(P^{\prime}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})
.
Since
P^{\prime}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\}
is also a pack and
f(P^{\prime})\geq f(P^{\prime}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\})
, we have

\sum_{j\in C}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}\geq 1,\quad\forall i^{\prime}\in P^{\prime}.

(28)

On the other hand, for any
i^{\prime}\in[n]\setminus(C\cup P^{\prime})
:

\displaystyle f(P^{\prime}\cup\{i^{\prime}\})-f(P^{\prime})

\displaystyle=a_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j\in C}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}-1\right)+\sum_{j\in C(P^{\prime}\cup\{i^{\prime}\})\setminus C}\left(a_{j}-r(P^{\prime}\cup\{i^{\prime}\})\right)x^{*}_{j}

\displaystyle\in\left[a_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j\in C}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}-1\right),a_{i^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j\in C(P^{\prime}\cup\{i^{\prime}\})}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}-1\right)\right].

Since
i^{\prime}\in[n]\setminus(C\cup P^{\prime})
, the set
P^{\prime}\cup\{i^{\prime}\}
is still a pack, hence
f(P^{\prime}\cup\{i^{\prime}\})-f(P^{\prime})\leq 0
.
Furthermore, since
P^{\prime}
is an inclusion-wise maximal pack with the largest
f(P^{\prime})
value, we have
f(P^{\prime}\cup\{i^{\prime}\})-f(P^{\prime})<0
.
Therefore,

\sum_{j\in C}x^{*}_{j}+x^{*}_{i^{\prime}}<1,\quad\forall i^{\prime}\in[n]\setminus(C\cup P^{\prime}).

(29)

From (
28
) and (
29
), we obtain

P^{\prime}=\{i\in[n]\setminus C:x^{*}(C)+x^{*}_{i}\geq 1\}.

(30)

We have thereby shown that there exists a WI from knapsack constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
which separates
x^{*}
, if and only if there exists
C\subseteq[n]
, such that the corresponding
P^{\prime}
, as defined in (
30
), is a pack satisfying
f(P^{\prime})>0
.
Therefore, WI-SP can be solved by checking whether the set
P^{\prime}=\{i\in[n]\setminus C:x^{*}(C)+x^{*}_{i}\geq 1\}
is a pack with
f(P^{\prime})>0
, for any possible
C\subseteq[n]
with
x^{*}(C)<1
.

Let
I_{0}:=\{i\in[n]:x^{*}_{i}=0\}
and
I_{f}:=\{i\in[n]:x^{*}_{i}\in(0,1)\}
.
From the assumptions of this theorem, we know that
\alpha:=\max\{|S|:x^{*}(S)<1,S\subseteq I_{f}\}
is a constant.
For any
T\subseteq I_{0}
and
S\subseteq I_{f}
with
x^{*}(S)<1
, it is easy to see that

\{i\in[n]\setminus S:x^{*}(S)+x^{*}_{i}\geq 1\}=\{i\in[n]\setminus(S\cup T):x^{*}(S\cup T)+x^{*}_{i}\geq 1\}.

Hence,
\{i\in[n]\setminus C:x^{*}(C)+x^{*}_{i}\geq 1\}
is a pack with positive
f
value for some
C\subseteq[n]
with
x^{*}(C)<1
, if and only if
\{i\in[n]\setminus(C\setminus I_{0}):x^{*}(C\setminus I_{0})+x^{*}_{i}\geq 1\}
is a pack with positive value, where
C\setminus I_{0}\subseteq I_{f}
and
x^{*}(C\setminus I_{0})=x^{*}(C)<1
.
Therefore, WI-SP can be solved by the following procedure:

1.

For any
S\subseteq I_{f}
with
x^{*}(S)<1
, construct the corresponding
P^{\prime}=\{i\in[n]\setminus S:x^{*}(S)+x^{*}_{i}\geq 1\}
.

2.

Check if
P^{\prime}
is a pack with
f(P^{\prime})>0
.

3.

If the answer to the previous check is yes for some
S\subseteq I_{f}
with
x^{*}(S)<1
, then the corresponding
P^{\prime}
provides a yes-certificate to WI-SP, and its corresponding WI separates
x^{*}
; If the answer is no for all
S\subseteq I_{f}
with
x^{*}(S)<1
, then
x^{*}
cannot be separated by any WI from the knapsack constraint
a^{\mathsf{T}}x\leq b
.

Since
\alpha=\max\{|S|:x^{*}(S)<1,S\subseteq I_{f}\}
, we have

|\{S:x^{*}(S)<1,S\subseteq I_{f}\}|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}\binom{n}{k}=O(n^{\alpha}).

So this above procedure can be implemented in polynomial time,
and we complete the proof.
∎

Corollary 4

If

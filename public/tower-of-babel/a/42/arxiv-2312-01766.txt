Introduction

1.1
Background and motivations

The classical Sobolev inequality with exponent
1<p<n
states that, for any
n\geq 2
and
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it holds

S_{p}\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}},

(1.1)

where
p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-p}
and
S_{p}
is the sharp constant. It is well known that the extremal functions of (
1.1
) (see
[
2
,
59
]
) form an
(n+2)
-dimensional manifold:

\mathcal{M}_{p}\coloneqq\Big\{w_{a,b,x_{0}}\coloneqq\frac{a}{(1+b|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}})^{\frac{n-p}{p}}}:a\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\ b>0,\ x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\Big\}.

(1.2)

When
p=2
,the Euler-Lagrange equation associated to the inequality (
1.1
) is, up to suitable normalization, given by

\Delta u+u|u|^{2^{*}-2}=0.

(1.3)

It was shown in
[
40
]
that all the positive solutions of (
1.3
) are Talenti bubbles:

T[z,\lambda](x)\coloneqq(n(n-2))^{\frac{n-2}{4}}\frac{\lambda^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{(1+\lambda^{2}{\lvert x-z\rvert}^{2})^{\frac{n-2}{2}}},\quad\lambda>0,\ z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.4)

Once the rigidity results for (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) are established, it is natural to consider stability versions. In the functional setting, a fundamental question arises — does the deviation of a function from attaining equality in (
1.1
) control its distance from the extremal manifold
\mathcal{M}_{p}
? Similarly, in the critical point setting, does almost being a positive solution of (
1.3
) imply almost being a Talenti bubble?

The question on the stability of functional inequalities was first raised by Brézis and Lieb in
[
10
]
. In their work, they improved the Sobolev embedding on bounded domains by introducing a remainder term, thereby raising an open problem about whether the homogeneous inequality (
1.1
) can be reinforced. A comprehensive answer was provided by Bianchi and Egnell in
[
7
]
for the case
p=2
, presenting a complete result as follows:

C_{\mathrm{BE}}\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda>0,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}\|\nabla(u-\alpha U[z,\lambda])\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-S_{2}^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}}}.

(1.5)

The result is considered complete because both the exponent
2
and the norm in the left hand side are sharp. For the case
p\neq 2
, due to the absence of Hilbertian structure, the stability of (
1.1
) remained an open problem for a long time and was recently solved by Figalli and Zhang in
[
35
]
(see
[
35
,
50
,
34
]
and the references therein for earlier works).

Besides the classical Sobolev inequality, there are many other important geometric and functional inequalities with well studied stability. Examples include the fractional Sobolev inequality
[
5
,
15
]
, the Sobolev trace inequality
[
52
,
14
]
, the isoperimetric inequality
[
39
,
33
,
46
,
49
,
17
,
49
,
28
]
, the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
[
13
,
12
]
, the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality
[
13
,
23
,
54
,
8
,
9
]
and the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality
[
61
,
22
,
38
]
.

Once a complete stability result is established, a natural and interesting question is to quantify the stability. For example, what can be said about the constant
C_{\mathrm{BE}}
in (
1.5
)? Traditional stability results of Sobolev-type inequalities rely heavily on local spectrum analysis and global concentration-compactness principle, which offer limited information about the constants involved. Recent advancements by Dolbeault, Esteban, Figalli, Frank and Loss
[
24
]
stated shedding light on explicit lower bound of
C_{\mathrm{BE}}
using competing symmetries and continuous Steiner symmetrization. The subsequent work by König in
[
41
,
42
]
gave an upper bound of
C_{\mathrm{BE}}
for general fractional Sobolev inequality, and showed that
C_{\mathrm{BE}}
can be attained by certain functions. In a parallel vein, Chen, Lu and Tang
[
14
]
gave a lower bound for stability constants of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, the fractional Sobolev inequality and some trace inequalities. Wei, Wu
[
62
]
and Deng, Tian
[
21
]
established analogous results for the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality.

The question on the stability of critical points traces back to the celebrated global compactness principle of Struwe
[
57
]
. This principle states that a bounded nonnegative sequence
\{u_{n}\}
satisfying
\left\|\Delta u+u|u|^{2^{*}-2}\right\|_{H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\rightarrow 0
can be approximated by several Talenti bubbles up to a subsequence. Ciraolo, Figalli and Maggi
[
18
]
provided the first quantitative version of this principle, yielding an optimal linear estimate when dealing with a single bubble:

C\dis(u,\mathcal{M}_{T})\leq\left\|\Delta u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\right\|_{H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
\mathcal{M}_{T}
is the manifold of Talenti bubbles. When there are more bubbles, it becomes complicated due to the interaction of distinct bubbles. Figalli and Glaudo
[
29
]
tackled such case and found an interesting phenomenon: linear estimates were possible for dimension
3\leq n\leq 5
, but for
n>5
, counter-examples were constructed. The remaining case
n>5
was ultimately solved by Deng, Sun and Wei in
[
20
]
using finite dimensional reduction method, highlighting that logarithmic (
n=6
) or sublinear (
n>6
) estimates are sharp in high dimension.

In addition to the classical Yamabe equation (
1.3
), the stability of several inequality-related critical equations have been extensively studied, such as the fractional Sobolev inequality
[
1
,
19
]
, the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality
[
61
]
and the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
[
45
,
53
]
.

As in the functional setting, a natural question is to study the optimal stability constant for critical equations. However, this question seems much harder, for the possible reason that in general, dual norms do not behave as well as Sobolev norms. Standard tools such as expansions and Brézis-Lieb type arguments, which are useful in functional settings, are difficult to handle dual norms. To our best knowledge, the first in this direction was given by De Nitti and König in
[
19
]
, which found an explicit upper bound for the stability constant of the fractional Yamabe equation in one bubble case by treating the dual norm carefully and using a third-order expansion.

1.2
Problem setup and main results

In this paper, we focus on the following sharp fractional Sobolev trace inequality given by Einav and Loss in
[
25
]
:

S(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{\frac{2(n-m)}{n-2\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\leq{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},

(1.6)

where
0\leq m<n
,
\frac{m}{2}<\alpha<\frac{n}{2}
,
f\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\tau_{m}f
is the restriction of
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n-m}
. The space
D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the standard fractional Sobolev space in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under the norm:

{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\lvert\hat{f}(k)\rvert}{\lvert 2\pi k\rvert}^{2\alpha}\,dk\right)^{1/2}.

The sharp constant
S(n,m,\alpha)
and the extremal manifold
\mathcal{M}_{n,m,\alpha}
are explicit and given by

S(n,m,\alpha)=2^{2\alpha}\pi^{\alpha}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(n/2+\alpha-m)}{\Gamma(n/2-\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha-m/2)}\left(\frac{\Gamma(n-m)}{\Gamma((n-m)/2)}\right)^{\frac{m-2\alpha}{n-m}},

\mathcal{M}_{n,m,\alpha}=\left\{\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-m}}\frac{1}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+|x^{\prime\prime}-y^{\prime\prime}|^{2})^{(n-2\alpha)/2}}\frac{1}{(\gamma^{2}+|y^{\prime\prime}-a|^{2})^{(n+2\alpha-2m)/2}}\,dy^{\prime\prime}\right\},

Where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\gamma>0,a\in\mathbb{R}^{n-m},x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},x^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-m}
.
When
m=0
, (
1.6
) reduces to the fractional Sobolev inequality. When
m=\alpha=1
, (
1.6
) is equivalent to the Escobar trace inequality in
[
27
]
:

S_{\mathrm{E}}(n){\lVert f\rVert}_{L^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\leq{\lVert f\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2},

where
S_{\mathrm{E}}(n)
is the sharp constant. The stability of these two inequalities are well studied (see
[
5
,
15
,
52
,
18
,
29
,
20
,
41
,
19
,
42
,
14
]
for examples).

In this paper, we consider the stability of (
1.6
) in both functional and critical point settings.

In the functional setting, we prove the following sharp estimate:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0\leq m<n
and
\frac{m}{2}<\alpha<\frac{n}{2}
. There exists a constant
C=C(n,m,\alpha)
such that for any
f\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

C{\lVert f-v\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}-S(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{\frac{2(n-m)}{n-2\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})}^{2}

(1.7)

for some
v\in\mathcal{M}_{n,m,\alpha}
. We denote
C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha)
to be the best stability constant
C
above.

The inequality (
1.7
) is sharp in the sense that the exponent
2
is optimal and the norm
D_{\alpha}
is the strongest. A crucial method employed in proving (
1.7
) is a reduction principle (see Theorem
3.3
) proved in
[
25
]
. This reduction allows us to connect (
1.6
) with standard fractional Sobolev inequality.
Precisely, we show that the stability of (
1.6
) is equivalent to that of the fractional Sobolev inequality.

With the establishment of (
1.7
), we are interested in the following minimization problem:

C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha)=\inf_{f\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{n,m,\alpha}}S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(f),

(1.8)

where

S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(f)\coloneqq\frac{{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}-S(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{\frac{2(n-m)}{n-2\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})}^{2}}{\dis^{2}(f,\mathcal{M}_{n,m,\alpha})}.

Here we prove, to our surprise, the optimal stability constant
C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha)
is identical to the optimal constant
C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n-m,0,\alpha-\frac{m}{2})
for the fractional Sobolev inequality (see Remark
3.5
), which is well studied. Consequently, both the upper bound and the lower bound of
C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha)
come directly from
[
41
,
42
]
and
[
14
]
, respectively. Furthermore, following ideas from
[
41
]
, we derive the existence of minimizers which attain the infimum in (
1.8
)(see Theorem
3.6
).

Based on the stability result (
1.7
), we derive enhanced Sobolev embedding results for general domains. Specifically, we can add a remainder term equipped with some weak
L^{q}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{L_{w}^{q}}
(see Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.4
). Such type results were initially established by Brézis and Lieb in
[
10
]
. Later, Bianchi and Egnell
[
7
]
presented an alternative proof using gradient stability. Chen, Frank and Weth
[
15
]
generalized their results to the fractional Sobolev inequality. We follow similar ideas in our proofs.

Another corollary of (
1.7
) is a stability result of a sharp a priori estimate for the Neumann problem:

\begin{cases}\Delta\mathcal{P}[u]=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}[u]}{\partial t}=-u&\text{on }\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\end{cases}

(1.9)

where
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}=\left\{(x,t)|x\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},t>0\right\}
. Given any
u\in L^{\frac{2n-2}{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
, we have

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2n-2}{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}-\frac{2}{S(n,1,1)}\left\|\nabla\mathcal{P}[u]\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2}\geq C\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Neu}}}\|u-v\|_{L^{\frac{2n-2}{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}.

(1.10)

Here
\frac{2}{S(n,1,1)}
is the sharp constant and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Neu}}
is the extremal manifold (see Theorem
4.6
in this paper). The proof of (
1.10
) relies on a standard dual scheme developed by Carlen in
[
12
]
. Precisely, we show that this a priori estimate is the dual of the Escobar trace inequality,
i.e.
inequality (
1.6
)
with
m=\alpha=1
.

In the critical point setting, we consider the Euler-Lagrange equation associated with the Escobar trace inequality:

\begin{cases}\Delta u=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=-|u|^{\frac{2}{n-2}}u&\text{on }\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\end{cases}

(1.11)

It was shown by Ou in
[
51
]
that, up to a scaling, any nontrivial nonnegative solution of (
1.11
) is an Escobar bubble:

U[z,\lambda](x,t)=(n-2)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{(1+\lambda t)^{2}+\lambda^{2}|x-z|^{2})}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}

for some
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\lambda>0
. Motivated by Struwe’s work, we initially establish a Struwe-type profile decomposition for (
1.11
) (see Theorem
5.1
). Specifically, we demonstrate that any almost solution of (
1.11
) can be approximated by multiple exact solutions. Furthermore, we present a nonnegative counterpart of this result (see Theorem
5.3
): every nonnegative almost solution of (
1.11
) can be approximated by several Escobar bubbles. The transition from the general version to the nonnegative version requires a Brézis-Lieb type lemma (see Lemma
5.2
), which was previously used in
[
47
]
. Following ideas of Figalli and Glaudo in
[
29
]
, we derive the following optimal quantitative profile decomposition

Theorem 1.2
.

For
n=3,\nu\in\mathbb{N}
or
n\geq 3,\nu=1
, there exist a small constant
\delta=\delta(n,\nu)>0
and
a large constant
C=C(n,\nu)>0
such that the following statement holds.
Let
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
be a function such that

{\left\lVert\nabla u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\nabla\tilde{U}_{i}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}\leq\delta,

(1.12)

where
(\tilde{U}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\nu}
is a
\delta
-interacting family of Escobar bubbles (see Definition
1.3
).
Then there exist
\nu
Escobar bubbles
U_{1},U_{2},\dots,U_{\nu}
such that

{\left\lVert\nabla u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\nabla U_{i}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}\leq C{\lVert\Delta u+{\lvert u\rvert}^{p-1}u\rVert}_{H^{-1}}.

where
p=\frac{n}{n-2}
. Furthermore, for any
i\not=j
, the interaction between the bubbles can be estimated as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}U_{i}^{p}U_{j}\leq C{\lVert\Delta u+{\lvert u\rvert}^{p-1}u\rVert}_{H^{-1}}.

(1.13)

The term in the right-hand side of (
1.13
) is defined by

{\lVert\Delta u+{\lvert u\rvert}^{\frac{2}{n-2}}u\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}=\sup_{v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}|u|^{\frac{2}{n-2}}uv}{{\lVert v\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}}

(1.14)

and the weak-interaction is defined by

Definition 1.3

(interaction of Escobar bubbles)
.

Let
U_{1}=U[z_{1},\lambda_{1}]
,…,
U_{\nu}=U[z_{\nu},\lambda_{\nu}]
be a family of Escobar bubbles. We say that the family is
\delta
-interacting for some
\delta>0
if

\mu_{ij}\coloneqq\min\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}},\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}},\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{2}}\right)\leq\delta,\quad\forall\,1\leq i\neq j\leq\nu.

(1.15)

Moreover, we say the family with some positive coefficients
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\nu}
is
\delta
-interacting, if (
1.15
) holds and

\max_{1\leq i\leq\nu}{\lvert\alpha_{i}-1\rvert}\leq\delta.

Thanks to qualitative results, the weak-interacting assumption in Theorem
1.2
can be substituted with nonnegativity, as demonstrated in Theorem
6.7
. Similar to (
1.8
), we study the following minimization problem in the critical point setting:

C_{\mathrm{CP}}(n,\nu)=\inf_{u}\frac{{\lVert\Delta u+{\lvert u\rvert}^{\frac{2}{n-2}}u\rVert}_{H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}}{\dis(u,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{E}}^{\nu})},\quad\text{for }u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{E}}^{\nu}.

(1.16)

We are able to give the following explicit upper bound (see Theorem
7.2
and
7.4
) for
C_{\mathrm{CP}}(n,1)
:

C_{\mathrm{CP}}(n,1)<\frac{2}{n+2},

using arguments inspired by De Nitti and König’s work in
[
19
]
. Our primary methods involve a precise spectral gap inequality, a well-defined expression for the
H^{-1}
norm and a third-order expansion. To derive the spectral gap inequality, we employ a conformal transformation, linking nondegeneracy results on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
to the classical Steklov eigenvalue problem on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. We will use the operator
\mathcal{P}
in (
1.9
) to express the
H^{-1}
norm, which makes it clear to do the third-order expansion.

1.3
Structure of the paper

After a section of notations and preliminaries, in Section
3
we presents the proof of the functional stability result (
1.7
). A discussion about the optimal stability constant and the existence of minimizers will also be given. Based on results in Section
3
, we derive some corollaries in Section
4
, including some refined Sobolev embeddings and a stability result of a priori estimate. Section
5
and Section
6
are devoted to qualitative and quantitative profile decomposition respectively. We focus on both the general case and the nonnegative case. Finally, in Section
7
, we provide some improved estimates of the stability constant for critical point in one bubble case. We begin with the derivation of an explicit spectral gap inequality. Based on it, we can proceed to give an upper bound of
C_{\mathrm{CP}}(n,1)
in terms of specturm constants.

2
Preliminaries

We begin with setting the notations and definitions we need in our paper.

For
n\geq 1
and
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{2}
, we define the space
D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as the space of tempered distributions
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
whose Fourier transform belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},|k|^{2\alpha}\,dk)
. That is to say, for any element
T\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of it, there exists a function
\hat{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},|k|^{2\alpha}\,dk)
such that for all
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\widehat{T}(\phi)=:T(\hat{\phi})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{f}(k)\phi(k)\,dk.

Here the Fourier transform is defined by

\hat{f}(k)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot k}\,dx.

D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
equipped with the following norm is a Hilbert space, in which
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense:

{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\lvert\hat{f}(k)\rvert}{\lvert 2\pi k\rvert}^{2\alpha}\,dk\right)^{1/2}.

We should claim that, when
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, the norm above is equal to classical Sobolev norm:

{\lVert\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f\rVert}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}.

For
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define the restriction of
f
to the
(n-m)
-dimensional hyperplane given by
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n-m+1}=\cdots=x_{n}=0\}
as

(\tau_{m}f)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-m}):=f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-m},0,\cdots,0).

For general
f\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\tau_{m}f
can be uniquely defined through a density argument.

For
n\geq 3
, in modern terminology,
2^{*}
always denotes the critical Sobolev exponent
\frac{2n}{n-2}
. In our settings, to avoid confusions, we will always use
2^{\dagger}
to denote the trace critical exponent
\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}
. We also define
p=2^{\dagger}-1=\frac{n}{n-2}
for simplicity.

In the whole paper, we denote by
S(n,m,\alpha)
the sharp constant of the fractional Sobolev trace inequality with respect to parameters
0\leq m<n
and
\frac{m}{2}<\alpha<\frac{n}{2}
, i.e.

S(n,m,\alpha):=\inf\left\{\frac{{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}}{{\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})}^{2}}:f\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\backslash\{0\},\,s=\frac{2(n-m)}{n-2\alpha}\right\}.

Note that, under this notation,
S(n,0,\alpha)
is the sharp constants of fractional Sobolev inequality.

A useful remark is that, when
m=\alpha=1
, the trace inequality above is equivalent to the original Escobar trace inequality in
[
27
]
via a simple reflection:

S_{\mathrm{E}}(n){\lVert f\rVert}_{L^{2^{\dagger}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\leq{\lVert f\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2},

where
S_{\mathrm{E}}(n)=\frac{S(n,1,1)}{2}
is the sharp constant, and
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}
with respect to the norm

{\lVert f\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The extremal functions, up to normalization, are precisely the so-called Escobar bubbles below.

For any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and
\lambda>0
, the Escobar bubble
U[z,\lambda]
is defined as

U[z,\lambda](x,t)=(n-2)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{(1+\lambda t)^{2}+\lambda^{2}|x-z|^{2})}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\,t>0.

(2.1)

The corresponding manifold is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{E}}
. We state some important properties of Escobar bubbles
U=U[z,\lambda]
.

•

They are extremal functions of the Escobar trace inequality:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}{\lvert\nabla U\rvert}^{2}=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}U^{2^{\dagger}}=S_{\mathrm{E}}(n)^{n-1}.

•

They and their derivatives satisfy

\begin{cases}\Delta U=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}=-U^{p}&\text{on }\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\end{cases}\quad\begin{cases}\Delta(\partial_{s}U)=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
\frac{\partial(\partial_{s}U)}{\partial t}=-pU^{p-1}\partial_{s}U&\text{on }\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\end{cases}

for
s=\lambda,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n-1}
.

•

We have the following expression for
\partial_{\lambda}U
:

\partial_{\lambda}U(x,t)=\frac{n-2}{2\lambda}U(x,t)\left(\frac{1-\lambda^{2}t^{2}-\lambda^{2}{\lvert x-z\rvert}^{2}}{(1+\lambda t)^{2}+\lambda^{2}{\lvert x-z\rvert}^{2}}\right),

which yields the estimate of
{\lvert\partial_{\lambda}U\rvert}
:

{\lvert\partial_{\lambda}U\rvert}\leq\frac{n-2}{2\lambda}{\lvert U\rvert}.

•

Symmetries: for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and exponents set
(\gamma_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
with
\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{k}=2^{\dagger}
,
k
Escobar bubbles
U[z_{1},\lambda_{1}],\dots,U[z_{k},\lambda_{k}]
satisfy

\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}U[z_{1},\lambda_{1}]^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots U[z_{k},\lambda_{k}]^{\gamma_{k}}=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}U[0,1]^{\gamma_{1}}\prod_{j=2}^{k}U[\lambda_{1}(z_{j}-z_{1}),\lambda_{j}/\lambda_{1}]^{\gamma_{j}}.

In addition, when
k=2
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\nabla U[z_{1},\lambda_{1}]\cdot\nabla U[z_{2},\lambda_{2}]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\nabla U[0,1]\cdot\nabla U[\lambda_{1}(z_{2}-z_{1}),\lambda_{2}/\lambda_{1}].

With these symmetries, for the stability problem, it is enough to consider
U[0,1]
instead of a generic Escobar bubble. When dealing with multiple bubbles, we need to evaluate their interaction (recall Definition
1.3
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Given
n\geq 1
, let us fix
\alpha+\beta=2n
with
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
,
\lambda\in(0,1]
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, set
D\coloneqq|z|
. If
|\alpha-\beta|\geq\epsilon
for some
\epsilon>0
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|x|^{2}}\right)^{\alpha/2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}|x-z|^{2}}\right)^{\beta/2}\approx_{n,\epsilon}\mu^{\frac{\min(\alpha,\beta)}{2}},

where
\mu=\min\left\{\lambda,\frac{1}{\lambda D^{2}}\right\}
, If instead
\alpha=\beta=n
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|x|^{2}}\right)^{\alpha/2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}|x-z|^{2}}\right)^{\beta/2}\approx_{n}\ln(\mu)\mu^{n/2}.

Here and in the following, the notation
A\approx_{n,\epsilon}B
means
0<c\leq\frac{A}{B}\leq C
for some constants
c,C
depending only on
n
and
\epsilon
, and the notation
A\approx_{n}B
is similar. We also use
A\lesssim B
to mean that there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
n,m,\alpha
such that
A\leq CB
.

Remark 2.2
.

It is a general version of
[
29
, Lemma B.1]
and the proof is similar. For general
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{1}^{2}|x-z_{1}|^{2}}\right)^{\alpha/2}\left(\frac{\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{2}^{2}|x-z_{2}|^{2}}\right)^{\beta/2}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|x|^{2}}\right)^{\alpha/2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}|x-z|^{2}}\right)^{\beta/2}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\ \mu^{\frac{\min(\alpha,\beta)}{2}}

with
\lambda=\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}\in(0,1]
and
z=\lambda_{1}(z_{2}-z_{1})
. So
\lambda D^{2}=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}
and
\mu=\min\left\{\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}},\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}\right\}
. If instead
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
, the result is valid with
\mu=\min\left\{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}},\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}\right\}
.

Thus, in order to identify whether the interactions of bubbles are weak, it is natural to introduce the definition of weak-interaction (See Definition
1.15
).

It is remarkable that the interaction of bubbles concentrates on some area depending on the bubble:

Lemma 2.3
.

Given
n\geq 3
and two Escobar bubbles
U_{1}=U[z_{1},\lambda_{1}]
and
U_{2}=U[z_{2},\lambda_{2}]
with
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}
, it holds

\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}U_{1}^{p}U_{2}\approx_{n}\int_{B^{n-1}(z_{1},\lambda_{1}^{-1})\cap\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}U_{1}^{p}U_{2}.

This lemma can be obtained by
[
29
, Corollary B.4]
.

For convenience, we will omit the domain of norms in the following sections when no confusion arises.

3
Stability of fractional Sobolev trace inequality

In this section, we are devoted to analyzing the stability of the following sharp fractional Sobolev trace inequality given by Einav and Loss in
[
25
]
:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
0\leq m<n
and
\frac{m}{2}<\alpha<\frac{n}{2}
. For any
f\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

S(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{\frac{2(n-m)}{n-2\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})}^{2}\leq{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},

(3.1)

where

S(n,m,\alpha)=2^{2\alpha}\pi^{\alpha}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(n/2+\alpha-m)}{\Gamma(n/2-\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha-m/2)}\left(\frac{\Gamma(n-m)}{\Gamma((n-m)/2)}\right)^{\frac{m-2\alpha}{n-m}}

and the equality holds if and only if
f(x)=f(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime})
,
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\,x^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-m}
is proportional to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-m}}\frac{1}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+|x^{\prime\prime}-y^{\prime\prime}|^{2})^{(n-2\alpha)/2}}\frac{1}{(\gamma^{2}+|y^{\prime\prime}-a|^{2})^{(n+2\alpha-2m)/2}}\,dy^{\prime\prime}

(3.2)

for some
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n-m}
and
\gamma\neq 0
. We denote the set of minimizers by
\mathcal{M}_{n,m,\alpha}
.

It was obtained by showing the two following inequalities and combining them together:

Theorem 3.2

(fractional Sobolev inequality)
.

Let
0<\alpha<n/2
. For any
f\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

S(n,0,\alpha){\lVert f\rVert}_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},

(3.3)

where
s=\frac{2n}{n-2\alpha}
and the equality holds if and only if

f(x)=A(\gamma^{2}+|x-a|^{2})^{-\frac{n-2\alpha}{2}}

for some
A\in\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma\neq 0
and
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The set of minimizers is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{n,0,\alpha}
.

Theorem 3.3

(reduction principle)
.

Assume
0\leq m<n
and
\frac{m}{2}<\alpha<\frac{n}{2}
. Then the trace
\tau_{m}
has a unique extension to a bounded operator
\tau_{m}:D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to D_{\alpha-m/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})
. Moreover, for any
f\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

R(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})}^{2}\leq{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},

(3.4)

where

R(n,m,\alpha)=2^{m}\pi^{m/2}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{\Gamma((2\alpha-m)/2)},

and the equality holds if and only if

\hat{f}(k_{1},k_{2})=\frac{\hat{g}(k_{1})}{(|k_{1}|^{2}+|k_{2}|^{2})^{\alpha}}

(3.5)

with

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-m}}\frac{{\lvert\hat{g}(k_{1})\rvert}^{2}}{{\lvert k_{1}\rvert}^{2\alpha-m}}\,dk_{1}<\infty.

(3.6)

Here
k_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-m}
and
k_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

To get the stability result (Theorem
1.1
), we also need a stability result of the fractional Sobolev inequality proved by Chen, Frank and Weth in
[
15
]
:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
0<\beta<N
,
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta}
, then
C_{\mathrm{BE}}(N,0,\beta)\in(0,1)
and we have

\dis^{2}(u,\mathcal{M})\geq{\lVert u\rVert}_{D_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}-S(N,0,\beta){\lVert u\rVert}_{L^{\frac{2N}{N-2\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\geq C_{\mathrm{BE}}(N,0,\beta)\dis^{2}(u,\mathcal{M})

(3.7)

for all
u\in D_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
\dis(u,\mathcal{M})=\min\{{\lVert u-\varphi\rVert}_{D_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:\varphi\in\mathcal{M}\}
.

The main idea of deriving Theorem
1.1
is using Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
to construct
v
directly. Precisely, any function
f\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be orthogonally decomposed into two parts
g
and
f-g
, where
g
attains the equality of (
3.4
) with the same trace as
f
, and
{\lVert f-g\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}
can be calculated directly. Then, estimating
\dis(\tau_{m}g,\mathcal{M}_{n-m,0,\alpha-m/2})
by (
3.7
) leads to the result. It is remarkable that the exponent 2 in (
1.7
), coming from that of (
3.7
), is sharp due to the spectrum analysis in
[
15
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For a given
f\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we claim that there exists a function
g\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
g
takes equality of (
3.4
) and
\tau_{m}g=\tau_{m}f
. Set

\hat{g}(k_{1},k_{2})=C_{1}(m,\alpha)^{-1}\frac{\widehat{\tau_{m}f}(k_{1})|k_{1}|^{2\alpha-m}}{(|k_{1}|^{2}+|k_{2}|^{2})^{\alpha}},\quad k_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-m},k_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

where
C_{1}(m,\alpha)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(1+|x|^{2})^{-\alpha}\,dx
is a constant. Since
\tau_{m}f\in D_{\alpha-m/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})
, if we take
\hat{h}(k_{1})\coloneqq C^{-1}(m,\alpha)\widehat{\tau_{m}f}(k_{1})|k_{1}|^{2\alpha-m}
, then
h\in D_{\alpha-m/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})
, and
g
satisfies (
3.5
). On the other hand,

\displaystyle\widehat{\tau_{m}g}(k_{1})=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\hat{g}(k_{1},k_{2})\,dk_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\frac{\hat{h}(k_{1})}{(|k_{1}|^{2}+|k_{2}|^{2})^{\alpha}}\,dk_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1}(m,\alpha)\hat{h(k_{1})}|k_{1}|^{-2\alpha+m}=\widehat{\tau_{m}f}(k_{1}),

thus
\tau_{m}g=\tau_{m}f
. This proves the claim. Next, we compute the distance from
f
to
g
:

\begin{split}{\lVert f-g\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}={}&{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}+{\lVert g\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\overline{\hat{f}(k_{1},k_{2})}\hat{g}(k_{1},k_{2})(|2\pi k_{1}|^{2}+|2\pi k_{2}|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dk_{1}dk_{2}\\
={}&{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}+R(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}\\
&-2C_{1}(m,\alpha)^{-1}(2\pi)^{m}\int_{R^{n-m}}\overline{\widehat{\tau_{m}f}(k_{1})}\widehat{\tau_{m}f}(k_{1})|2\pi k_{1}|^{2\alpha-m}\,dk_{1}\\
={}&{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}-R(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.8)

Since
\tau_{m}f\in D_{\alpha-m/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})
, picking
N=n-m
and
\beta=\alpha-\frac{m}{2}
in Theorem
3.4
, we get some
\tilde{v}\in\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta}
such that

\begin{split}{\lVert\tau_{m}f-\tilde{v}\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}\leq{}&C_{\mathrm{BE}}(N,0,\beta)^{-1}\left({\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}-S(N,0,\beta){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{s}}^{2}\right),\end{split}

where
s=\frac{2(n-m)}{n-2\alpha}
. Finally, taking
v\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that attains equality of (
3.4
) and
\tau_{m}v=\tilde{v}
, we know that
g-v
also attains the equality by checking (
3.5
). Hence,

\begin{split}{\lVert g-v\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}={}&R(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}g-\tau_{m}v\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}\\
={}&R(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f-\tilde{v}\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}\\
\leq{}&C_{\mathrm{BE}}(N,0,\beta)^{-1}R(n,m,\alpha)\left({\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}-S(N,0,\beta){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{s}}^{2}\right).\end{split}

(3.9)

By similar computation as in (
3.8
), we know by
\tau_{m}f=\tau_{m}g
that

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\overline{(\hat{f}-\hat{g})(k_{1},k_{2})}(\hat{g}-\hat{v})(k_{1},k_{2})({\lvert 2\pi k_{1}\rvert}^{2}+{\lvert 2\pi k_{2}\rvert}^{2})^{\alpha}\,dk_{1}dk_{2}\\
={}&C_{1}(m,\alpha)^{-1}(2\pi)^{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-m}}\overline{(\widehat{\tau_{m}f}-\widehat{\tau_{m}g})(k_{1})}(\widehat{\tau_{m}g}-\widehat{\tau_{m}v})(k_{1}){\lvert 2\pi k_{1}\rvert}^{2\alpha-m}dk_{1}=0.\end{split}

Thus, combining (
3.8
), (
3.9
), (
3.4
), and noticing that
C_{\mathrm{BE}}(N,0,\beta)\in(0,1)
, we get the desired estimate (
1.7
):

\displaystyle{\lVert f-v\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}={}

\displaystyle{\lVert f-g\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}+{\lVert g-v\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}-R(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C_{\mathrm{BE}}(N,0,\beta)^{-1}\,R(n,m,\alpha)\left({\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}-S(N,0,\beta){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{s}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}+\left(C_{\mathrm{BE}}(N,0,\beta)^{-1}-1\right)R(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle-C_{\mathrm{BE}}(N,0,\beta)^{-1}\,S(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{BE}}(N,0,\beta)^{-1}\left({\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}-S(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{s}}^{2}\right).\qed

Remark 3.5

(estimate of stability constant)
.

We can go further to discuss the best constant
C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha)
of (
1.7
). Define

S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(f)\coloneqq\frac{{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}-S(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{s}}^{2}}{\dis^{2}(f,\mathcal{M}_{n,m,\alpha})},\quad\text{for }f\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{n,m,\alpha},

(3.10)

\mathcal{E}(f)\coloneqq\frac{{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}-S(N,0,\beta){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{s}}^{2}}{\dis^{2}(f,\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta})},\quad\text{for }f\in D_{\alpha-m/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta},

with
N=n-m,\,\beta=\alpha-m/2,\,s=\frac{2(n-m)}{n-2\alpha}
. The above proof shows that for any
f\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\begin{split}\dis^{2}(f,\mathcal{M}_{n,m,\alpha})={}&\inf_{h\in\mathcal{M}_{n,m,\alpha}}{\lVert f-g\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}+{\lVert g-h\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}\\
={}&{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}-R(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}+\inf_{h\in\mathcal{M}_{n,m,\alpha}}{\lVert g-h\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}\\
={}&{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}-R(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}+R(n,m,\alpha)\inf_{h\in\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta}}{\lVert\tau_{m}f-h\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}\\
\coloneqq{}&\delta_{1}(f)+R(n,m,\alpha)\dis^{2}(\tau_{m}f,\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta})\end{split}

with
g
defined as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Hence, if
\tau_{m}f\notin\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta}
, by

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{R(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}-S(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{s}}^{2}}{R(n,m,\alpha)\dis^{2}(\tau_{m}f,\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta})}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}-S(N,0,\beta){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{s}}^{2}}{\dis^{2}(\tau_{m}f,\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta})}=\mathcal{E}(\tau_{m}f)\leq 1,

we get

S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(f)=\frac{\delta_{1}(f)+R(n,m,\alpha)\left({\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}-S(N,0,\beta){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{s}}^{2}\right)}{\delta_{1}(f)+R(n,m,\alpha)\dis^{2}(\tau_{m}f,\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta})}\geq\mathcal{E}(\tau_{m}f),

(3.11)

and equality holds if and only if
f=g
. If
\tau_{m}f\in\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta}
, then
\dis^{2}(f,\mathcal{M}_{n,m,\alpha})=\delta_{1}(f)
,
{\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}=S(N,0,\beta){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{s}}^{2}
, and so

S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(f)=\frac{{\lVert f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}-R(n,m,\alpha)S(N,0,\beta){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{s}}^{2}}{\delta_{1}(f)}=1.

As a result,

C_{\text{BE}}(n,m,\alpha)=\inf_{f\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{n,m,\alpha}}S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(f)=\inf_{g\in D_{\alpha-m/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta}}\mathcal{E}(g)=C_{\mathrm{BE}}(N,0,\beta),

and the best constant of Theorem
1.1
is exactly that of Theorem
3.4
.

Now based on the work in
[
41
,
42
]
and
[
14
]
, we have the bound:

\frac{\min\{K_{n-m,\alpha-m/2},1,2^{\frac{n+2\alpha-2m}{n-m}}-2\}}{4}\leq C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha),

and

C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha)\begin{cases}<\min\left\{\frac{4\alpha-2m}{n+2\alpha+2-2m},2-2^{\frac{n-2\alpha}{n-m}}\right\},&n-m\geq 2,\\
\leq\frac{4\alpha-2m}{4+2\alpha},&n-m=1,\end{cases}

where

At the end of this section, we consider the existence of minimizers for the stability constant
C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha)
in the spirit of König’s work in
[
42
]
. We show the following compactness result.

Theorem 3.6

(compactness of minimizing sequence)
.

Let
0\leq m<n-1
and
\frac{m}{2}<\alpha<\frac{n}{2}
. Let
(u_{n})
be a minimizing sequence for (
3.10
) with
{\lVert u_{n}\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
. Then there exist a function
u\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{n,m,\alpha}
such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}
converge to
u
strongly in
D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,
S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(u)=C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha)
,
i.e.

u
is a minimizer for
C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha)
.

Proof.

We still set
N=n-m
and
\beta=\alpha-m/2
. By Remark
3.5
, we may assume each
S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(u_{n})<1
to avoid the case
\tau_{m}u_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta}
. Denote by
\tilde{u}_{n}
the minimizer of (
3.4
) with
\tau_{m}\tilde{u}_{n}=\tau_{m}u_{n}\in D_{\alpha-m/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta}
, then
\mathcal{E}(\tau_{m}u_{n})=S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(\tilde{u}_{n})\leq S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(u_{n})
and so
\lim_{n}\mathcal{E}(\tau_{m}u_{n})=\lim_{n}S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(u_{n})=C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha)
, and
\left(\frac{\tau_{m}u_{n}}{{\lVert\tau_{m}u_{n}\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}}\right)
is a minimizing sequence for
\mathcal{E}
with norm 1. Using
[
42
, Theorem 1.2]
, there exists a function
v\in D_{\alpha-m/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{N,0,\beta}
such that, up to a subsequence,
\frac{\tau_{m}u_{n}}{{\lVert\tau_{m}u_{n}\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}}
converge to
v
strongly in
D_{\alpha-m/2}
, and in addition
\mathcal{E}(v)=C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha)
.

We now claim
\lim_{n}{\lVert\tau_{m}u_{n}\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}=R(n,m,\alpha)^{-1}
. By (
3.11
),

\displaystyle\left(1-S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(u_{n})\right)\delta_{1}(u_{n})={}

\displaystyle R(n,m,\alpha)S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(u_{n})\dis^{2}(\tau_{n}u_{n},\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle-R(n,m,\alpha)\left({\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}-S(N,0,\beta){\lVert\tau_{m}f\rVert}_{L^{s}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle R(n,m,\alpha)\dis^{2}(\tau_{n}u_{n},\mathcal{M})\big(S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(u_{n})-\mathcal{E}(\tau_{m}u_{n})\big).

Since
\lim_{n}S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(u_{n})=\lim_{n}\mathcal{E}(\tau_{m}u_{n})=C_{\mathrm{BE}}<1
, the right side tends to 0 as
n\to\infty
. On the other hand, the left side is larger than
\frac{1-C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha)}{2}>0
if
n
is large enough, and so

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{1}(u_{n})=0\implies\lim_{n}{\lVert\tau_{m}u_{n}\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\lim_{n}R(n,m,\alpha)^{-1}\big({\lVert u_{n}\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}-\delta_{1}(u_{n})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(n,m,\alpha)^{-1}.

Hence, up to a subsequence,
\tau_{m}u_{n}\to R(n,m,\alpha)^{-1}v
strongly in
D_{\alpha-m/2}
. As a result, if we set
u\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to be the minimizer of (
3.4
) with
\tau_{m}u=R(n,m,\alpha)^{-1}v
, then

\displaystyle{\lVert u_{n}-u\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}={}

\displaystyle{\lVert u_{n}-\tilde{u}_{n}\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}+{\lVert\tilde{u}_{n}-\tilde{v}\rVert}_{D_{\alpha}}^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\delta_{1}(u_{n})+R(n,m,\alpha){\lVert\tau_{m}u_{n}-R(n,m,\alpha)^{-1}v\rVert}_{D_{\alpha-m/2}}^{2}\to 0,\quad n\to\infty,

yielding that
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
D_{\alpha}
up to a subsequence. The only thing left is to show
S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(u)=C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha)
. By the definition of
u
, we have

S_{\mathrm{Tr}}(u)=\mathcal{E}(\tau_{m}u)=\mathcal{E}(v)=C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,m,\alpha).\qed

4
Some consequences

In this section we give some corollaries of the gradient stability result (
1.7
).

We first prove the following theorem concerning refined Sobolev embedding in domains with finite measure.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
0\leq m<n,\frac{m}{2}<\alpha<\frac{n}{2}
and
s=\frac{2(n-m)}{n-2\alpha},q_{1}=\frac{n-m}{n-2\alpha},q_{2}=\frac{n-m}{n-\alpha-\frac{m}{2}}
. Then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
n,\alpha
such that, for any domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathcal{H}^{m}(\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}^{m})<\infty
, and any
u\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\tau_{m}{u}=0
in
\Omega^{c}
, we have

\|u\|_{D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}-S(n,m,\alpha)\|\tau_{m}{u}\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})}^{2}\geq C\mathcal{H}^{m}(\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}^{m})^{-\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\|\tau_{m}{u}\|_{L_{w}^{q_{1}}(\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}^{m})}^{2}.

(4.1)

Here
\mathcal{H}
denotes the Hausdorff measure. If
\alpha-\frac{m}{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, then we also have

\|u\|_{D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}-S(n,m,\alpha)\|\tau_{m}{u}\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n-m})}^{2}\geq C\mathcal{H}^{m}(\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}^{m})^{-\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\|\Delta^{\frac{2\alpha-m}{4}}(\tau_{m}{u})\|_{L_{w}^{q_{2}}(\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}^{m})}^{2}.

(4.2)

Moreover, the weak norm on the right-hand side cannot be replaced by the strong norm.

The proof of (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) can be reduced to the case
m=0
using Theorem
3.3
. Based on Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show the following lemma:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{2},q_{1}=\frac{n}{n-2\alpha},q_{2}=\frac{n}{n-\alpha}
. Then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
\alpha,n
such that

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q_{1}}_{w}(\Omega)}\leq C|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2q_{1}}}d(u,\mathcal{M}_{n,0,\alpha})

(4.3)

for all subdomains
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|\Omega|<\infty
and all
u\in D_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
u=0
in
\Omega^{c}
.

If in addition
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, then the following estimate holds:

\left\|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u\right\|_{L^{q_{2}}_{w}(\Omega)}\leq C|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2q_{1}}}d(u,\mathcal{M}_{n,0,\alpha}).

(4.4)

Remark 4.3
.

The inequality (
4.3
) has been proved by Chen, Frank and Weth in
[
15
]
. When
\alpha=1
, (
4.4
) was proved by Brézis and Lieb in
[
10
]
. Although the proof of (
4.4
) for general
\alpha
are similar to that applied in
[
15
]
, for completeness, we give a detailed proof below.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Assume (
4.4
) fails and
|\Omega|=1
. Then there exists a sequence
(u_{n})\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{d(u_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n,0,\alpha})}{\left\|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{n}\right\|_{L^{q_{2}}_{w}(\Omega)}}\rightarrow 0.

(4.5)

By homogeneity, we can assume
\left\|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}=1
. By the Hölder inequality,
\left\|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{n}\right\|_{L^{q_{2}}_{w}(\Omega)}
is bounded and hence
d(u_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n,0,\alpha})\to 0
. This shows that there exist
c_{n}\rightarrow 1,z_{n}\in\Omega
and
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\infty
such that

\displaystyle d(u_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n,0,\alpha})=\left\|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(u_{n}-c_{n}U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}])\right\|_{L^{2}},

with
U[z,\lambda]=\left(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}{\lvert x-z\rvert}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2\alpha}{2}}
. A direct computation yields

\displaystyle d(u_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n,0,\alpha})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}c_{n}U[0,\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{c})}^{2}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left\|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}U[0,\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{2}(0)^{c})}^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{B_{2}(0)^{c}}\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2\alpha-n}}{|x|^{2(n-\alpha)}}\gtrsim\lambda_{n}^{2\alpha-n}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{n}\right\|_{L^{q_{2}}_{w}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(u_{n}-c_{n}U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}])\right\|_{L^{q_{2}}_{w}(\Omega)}+\left\|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}c_{n}U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{L^{q_{2}}_{w}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(u_{n}-c_{n}U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}])\right\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda_{n}^{\frac{2\alpha-n}{2}}\left\|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}U[0,1]\right\|_{L^{q_{2}}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim d(u_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n,0,\alpha}).

This contradicts (
4.5
).
∎

By further exploiting the proof above, we can establish similar results for strips.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume
m,n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
satisfy
\frac{n}{4}\leq m<\frac{n}{2}
. Let
q=\frac{n}{n-2m}
. Then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
m,n
such that

\|u\|_{D_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}-S(n,0,m)\|u\|_{L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\geq C\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{w}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}

(4.7)

for all
u\in D_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
whose support belongs to
I\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
; here
I=[-1,1]
is an interval in
\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 4.5
.

When
m=1
, (
4.7
) was proved by Wang and Willem in
[
60
]
. They in fact considered the refined Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality in more general domains. Their methods relied on a maximum principle. In our cases, due to the absence of maximum principle for the polyharmonic operator, we restrict our analysis to domains that are bounded in one direction.

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

Assume that (
4.7
) fails, then there exists a sequence
(u_{n})\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\frac{d(u_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n,0,m})}{\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{q}_{w}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}}\rightarrow 0.

Assume
\|u_{n}\|_{D_{m}}=1
. Note that

\int_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}|\Delta^{\frac{m}{2}}u_{n}|^{2}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{I}\left|\frac{\partial^{m}u_{n}}{\partial x_{1}^{m}}\right|^{2}\gtrsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{I}|u_{n}|^{2}.

The assumption
\frac{n}{4}\leq m<\frac{n}{2}
, the interpolation inequality and the fractional Sobolev inequality yield

\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{q}_{w}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\leq\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\lambda}\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2q}}^{1-\lambda}\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{D_{m}}

(4.8)

for some
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. Hence
\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{q}_{w}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}
is bounded and
d(u,\mathcal{M}_{n,0,m})\rightarrow 0
. This indicates the existence of
c_{n}\rightarrow 1,z_{n}\in I\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\infty
such that

\displaystyle d(u_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n,0,m})=\left\|\Delta^{\frac{m}{2}}(u_{n}-c_{n}U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}])\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Similarly to the computation in (
4.6
), we derive that
d(u_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n,0,m})\gtrsim\lambda_{n}^{\frac{2m-n}{2}}
. Now fix a cut-off function
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta=1
in
2I
,
\eta=0
outside
3I
. Using (
4.8
), we can estimate:

\displaystyle\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{q}_{w}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|u_{n}-c_{n}\eta U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{L^{q}_{w}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}+\left\|c_{n}\eta U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{L^{q}_{w}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|u_{n}-c_{n}\eta U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{D_{m}}+\left\|c_{n}U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{L^{q}_{w}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|u_{n}-c_{n}U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{D_{m}}+\left\|c_{n}(1-\eta)U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{D_{m}}+\lambda_{n}^{\frac{2m-n}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim d(u_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n,0,m})+\left\|(1-\eta)U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{D_{m}}.

It will lead to a contradiction if there exists a uniform constant
C
such that

\left\|(1-\eta)U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{D_{m}}\leq C\lambda_{n}^{\frac{2m-n}{2}},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.9)

Based on our choice of
z_{n},\lambda_{n}
and
\eta
, (
4.9
) can be derived from the following three simple observations:

\displaystyle\left\|(1-\eta)U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{D^{m}}\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\left\|\nabla^{i}U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{L^{2}((3I-2I)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}+\left\|\nabla^{m}U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{L^{2}((2I)^{c}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})},

\displaystyle\left\||\nabla^{i}U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}]|\right\|_{L^{2}((3I-2I)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{\frac{2m-n}{2}},\quad 0\leq i\leq m-1,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{m}U[z_{n},\lambda_{n}]\right\|_{L^{2}((2I)^{c}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{\frac{2m-n}{2}}.\qed

Next we establish the dual stability results for the Escobar trace inequality:

S_{\mathrm{E}}(n){\lVert f\rVert}_{L^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\leq{\lVert f\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2}.

The extremal functions are given by (
2.1
) up to normalizations. Consider the pairing
(X,Y,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
:

X=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}),\;Y=L^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),\;\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}fg,

and two functionals
\Phi
and
\Psi
on
X
:

\Phi(f)={\lVert f\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2},\quad\Psi(f)=S_{\mathrm{E}}(n){\lVert f\rVert}_{L^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}.

It is easy to compute the Legendre transform
\Phi^{*}
and
\Psi^{*}
on
Y
:

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}(g)

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in X}\left\{\langle f,g\rangle-\Phi(f)\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in X}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}fg-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla f|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla\mathcal{P}[g]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\Psi^{*}(g)

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in X}\left\{\langle f,g\rangle-\Phi(f)\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in X}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}fg-S_{\mathrm{E}}(n){\lVert f\rVert}_{L^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4S_{\mathrm{E}}(n)}{\lVert g\rVert}_{L^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n}}}^{2}.

The function
\mathcal{P}[g]
in the above computation is the unique solution of

\begin{cases}\Delta\mathcal{P}[g]=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}[g]}{\partial t}=-g&\text{on }\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}.\end{cases}

(4.10)

Based on standard dual scheme developed by Carlen in
[
12
]
, we can immediately obtain the following stability result:

Theorem 4.6
.

For any
n\geq 3
and
u\in L^{\frac{2n-2}{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
, it holds

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2n-2}{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\geq S_{\mathrm{E}}(n)^{-1}\|\nabla\mathcal{P}[u]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2},

(4.11)

where
\mathcal{P}[u]
is the unique solution of (
4.10
). The equality holds precisely when
u(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}}
after suitable translation, scaling and normalization.

Moreover, if denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Neu}}
the extremal manifold, the following sharp stability result holds:

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2n-2}{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}-S_{\mathrm{E}}(n)^{-1}\|\nabla\mathcal{P}[u]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2}\geq C\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Neu}}}\|u-v\|_{L^{\frac{2n-2}{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}

(4.12)

for a positive constant
C
which can be writen explicitly in terms of
C_{\mathrm{BE}}(n,1,1)
.

Remark 4.7
.

By more calculations, the a priori estimate above is equivalent to the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
(see for example
[
6
]
). However, this estimate has its own interest, and the operator
\mathcal{P}
will be used in Section
7
to handle the dual Sobolev norm.

5
Qualitative profile decomposition

In this section we establish qualitative profile decomposition for the following nonlinear critical Neumann boundary problem in the same spirit of pioneer works of Struwe in
[
57
]
:

\begin{cases}\Delta u=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=-|u|^{p-1}u&\text{on }\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}.\end{cases}

(5.1)

Although we deal with the trace version here, our main methods are parallel to those in
[
58
]
, where (
1.3
) is treated. We first consider the global compactness for general functions.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
be a sequence of functions with
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}
uniformly bounded. Assume that (recall the dual norm is defined in (
1.14
))

\left\|\Delta u_{k}+|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}\right\|_{H^{-1}}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\;k\rightarrow\infty.

(5.2)

Then there exist a number
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, a subsequence of
(u_{k})_{k}
, which we still denote by
(u_{k})_{k}
, a sequence
(z_{1}^{(k)},...,z_{\nu}^{(k)})
of
\nu
-tuples of points in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, a sequence
(\lambda_{1}^{(k)},...,\lambda_{\nu}^{k})
of
\nu
-tuples of positive real numbers and a sequence of functions
(w_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\nu}
, which are nontrivial solutions of (
5.1
), such that

\left\|\nabla\left(u_{k}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}w_{i}[z_{i}^{(k)},\lambda_{i}^{(k)}]\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\;k\rightarrow\infty,

(5.3)

\left\|\nabla u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\left\|\nabla w_{i}[z_{i}^{(k)},\lambda_{i}^{(k)}]\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\;k\rightarrow\infty.

(5.4)

Here
w_{i}[z,\lambda](x,t):=\lambda^{\frac{n-2}{2}}w_{i}(\lambda(x-z),\lambda t)
for every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\lambda>0
.

Proof.

In the following proof, we will use
B_{r}(x,t)
to denote the ball centered at
(x,t)
with radius
r
and omit the subscript
r
when
r=1
. We also define
B_{r}^{+}(x,t)=B_{r}(x,t)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
,
\partial_{b}B_{r}^{+}(x,t)=\partial B_{r}^{+}(x,t)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.

Since
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}
is uniformly bounded, we may assume
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\rightarrow L\in[0,\infty)
. If
L=0
, then
u_{k}\rightarrow 0
and the results hold clearly. In the following we assume
L>0
and
\frac{L}{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\leq\frac{3L}{2}
for any
k
. Due to translation and dilation invariance, we can take a sequence
(z_{k})_{k}
of points in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, a sequence
(\lambda_{k})_{k}
of positive real numbers and a sequence
(t_{k})_{k}
of nonnegative numbers such that

\int_{B^{+}(0,t_{k})}\left|\nabla u_{k}\left[z_{k},\lambda_{k}\right]\right|^{2}=\sup_{(z,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\int_{B^{+}(z,t)}\left|\nabla u_{k}\left[z_{k},\lambda_{k}\right]\right|^{2}=\epsilon

(5.5)

for any
k
and some
\epsilon
sufficiently small that will be determined later.

If
t_{k}\rightarrow\infty
as
k\to\infty
, we may assume
t_{k}\nearrow\infty
and set
v_{k}(x,t)=u_{k}\left[z_{k},\lambda_{k}\right](x,t+t_{k})
and
\Omega_{k}=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(-t_{k},\infty)
. Then
v_{k}\subset H^{1}(\Omega_{k})
and
\Omega_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
u_{k}\left[z_{k},\lambda_{k}\right]
is uniformly bounded, there exist a subsequence and a function
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

v_{k}\rightharpoonup v\quad\text{in}\;H^{1}(\Omega_{m})\quad\text{for any fixed }m,

and

v_{k}\rightarrow v\quad\text{in}\;L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

From (
5.2
) we also know that

\left\|\Delta v_{k}+|v_{k}|^{p-1}v_{k}\right\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega_{k})}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\;k\rightarrow\infty.

(5.6)

Hence
v
is a global harmonic function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. But
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and so
v
must be zero function. Now take a cut-off function
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta=1
in
B(0,0)
and
\eta=0
outside
B_{2}(0,0)
. Using (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) and Hölder inequality we can obtain

\displaystyle o(1)

\displaystyle=\left\langle v_{k}\eta,\Delta v_{k}+|v_{k}|^{p-1}v_{k}\right\rangle

(5.7)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{2}(0,0)}\nabla(v_{k}\eta)\cdot\nabla v_{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{2}(0,0)}\eta|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}+\int_{B_{2}(0,0)}v_{k}\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla v_{k}

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon+o(1),

where
o(1)\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. The computation above yields a contradiction!

So
t_{k}
must be uniformly bounded. Without loss of generality, we assume
t_{k}\rightarrow\bar{t}
for some
\bar{t}\in[0,\infty)
and
|t_{k}-\bar{t}|\leq\frac{1}{8}
. Choose cut-off functions
\eta_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
i=1,2
,
\eta_{1}=1
in
B_{2}(0,\bar{t})
,
\eta_{1}=0
outside
B_{3}(0,\bar{t})
, while
\eta_{2}=1
in
B_{3}(0,\bar{t})
,
\eta_{2}=0
outside
B_{4}(0,\bar{t})
. Set
v_{k}(x,t)=u_{k}\left[z_{k},\lambda_{k}\right](x,t)
. There exists a function
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
such that

v_{k}\rightharpoonup v\quad\text{in}\;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}),

v_{k}\rightarrow v\quad\text{in}\;L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}),

and

v_{k}\rightarrow v\quad\text{in}\;L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\;\text{for any }1\leq q<p+1.

From (
5.2
) we know that

\left\|\Delta v_{k}+|v_{k}|^{p-1}v_{k}\right\|_{H^{-1}}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\;k\rightarrow\infty.

(5.8)

With these two lemmas, we can now prove (

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists a constant
\delta=\delta(n,\nu)>0
such that if
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]
is a linear combination of Escobar bubbles and
\rho\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
satisfies (
6.3
) with
U_{i}=U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]
, then

\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\sigma^{p-1}\rho^{2}\leq\frac{c}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla\rho|^{2},

where
p=\frac{n}{n-2}
and
c=c(n,\nu)<1
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{\nu}
be the cut-off functions built in Lemma
6.4
for some
\epsilon=\epsilon(\delta)
. Thanks to Lemma
6.4
-(2), it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\sigma^{p-1}\rho^{2}\leq{}

\displaystyle\int_{\{\sum\Phi^{2}_{i}\geq 1\}\cap\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\Phi_{i}^{2}\right)\sigma^{p-1}\rho^{2}+\int_{\{\sum\Phi^{2}_{i}<1\}\cap\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\sigma^{p-1}\rho^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle(1+C\epsilon^{p-1})\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\Phi_{i}^{2}\rho^{2}U_{i}^{p-1}+\int_{\{\sum\Phi^{2}_{i}<1\}\cap\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\sigma^{p-1}\rho^{2}.

By Lemma
6.4
-(1), using the Hölder and Escobar trace inequality we obtain

\int_{\{\sum\Phi^{2}_{i}<1\}\cap\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\sigma^{p-1}\rho^{2}\leq\left(\int_{\{\sum\Phi^{2}_{i}<1\}\cap\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\sigma^{p+1}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}{\lVert\rho\rVert}_{L^{p+1}(\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2}\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{n-1}}{\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2}.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be, up to scaling, one of the functions
U_{i},\,\partial_{\lambda}U_{i},\,\partial_{z_{j}}U_{i}
, with
\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\psi^{2}U_{i}^{p-1}=1
. Then by the orthogonal conditions (
6.3
) one gets

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}(\rho\Phi_{i})\psi U_{i}^{p-1}\right|={}

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\rho\psi U_{i}^{p-1}(1-\Phi_{i})\right|\leq\left|\int_{\{\Phi_{i}<1\}\cap\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\rho\psi U_{i}^{p-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle{\lVert\rho\rVert}_{L^{p+1}}\left(\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\psi^{2}U_{i}^{p-1}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\{\Phi_{i}<1\}\cap\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}U_{i}^{p+1}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{n-1}}{\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})},

Which means
\rho\Phi_{i}
is almost orthogonal to
\psi
. Hence, by Lemma
6.2
,

\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}(\rho\Phi_{i})^{2}U_{i}^{p-1}\leq\frac{1}{\Lambda_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla(\rho\Phi_{i})|^{2}+C\epsilon^{\frac{2}{n-1}}{\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2}.

Note that

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla(\rho\Phi_{i})|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\Phi_{i}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\rho^{2}|\nabla\Phi_{i}|^{2}+2\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\rho\Phi_{i}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi_{i}.

Using the Hölder and Sobolev inequality (to use Sobolev inequality in the whole space, we need to extend
\rho\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
to
H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by
\rho(x,t)\coloneqq\rho(x,|t|)
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\rho^{2}|\nabla\Phi_{i}|^{2}\leq{}

\displaystyle{\lVert\rho\rVert}_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2}{\lVert\nabla\Phi_{i}\rVert}_{L^{n}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}{\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\rho\Phi_{i}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi_{i}\leq{}

\displaystyle{\lVert\rho\rVert}_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}{\lVert\Phi_{i}\rVert}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}{\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}{\lVert\nabla\Phi_{i}\rVert}_{L^{n}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}\lesssim\epsilon{\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2}.

As a consequence of Lemma
6.4
-(2),
\{\Phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\nu}
have disjoint supports, and so

\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\Phi_{i}^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}.

Combining all these estimates, we finally get

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{n}^{n}}\sigma^{p-1}\rho^{2}\leq\frac{1+o(1)}{\Lambda_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}+o(1){\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2}=\left(\frac{1}{\Lambda_{3}}+o(1)\right){\lVert\nabla\rho\rVert}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2},

where
o(1)
denotes some small quantities going to zero as
\epsilon\to 0
. Since
\Lambda_{3}>p
, we get the desired result.
∎

Theorem 6.8

For any

and
0<\delta<1
, there exist a nonnegative function
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
, a constant
C
depending only on
n,\nu
and a
\delta
-interacting family
U_{1},...,U_{\nu}
of Escobar bubbles such that

\left\|\nabla\left(u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{i}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\inf_{W_{1},...,W_{\nu}}\left\|\nabla\left(u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}W_{i}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\delta

(6.23)

and

\left\|\Delta u+u^{p}\right\|_{H^{-1}}\leq C\left\|\nabla\left(u-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{i}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}.

(6.24)

Proof.

Fix
\nu
distinct directions
\theta_{1},...,\theta_{\nu}
in
\mathbb{S}^{n-2}
. For any
0<\epsilon\ll 1\ll R<\infty
, we set
u_{\epsilon,R}(x,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{R,i}(x,t)+\rho_{\epsilon}(x,t)
, here
U_{R,i}=U[R\theta_{i},1],\rho_{\epsilon}(x,t)=\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|t-1|^{2}}}{\epsilon}\right)_{+}
. It is easy to see that
\|\nabla\rho_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}=C_{n}\epsilon^{\frac{n-2}{2}}
and
|\nabla\rho|=\frac{1}{\epsilon}
in
B^{n}_{\epsilon}(0,1)
. When
R
is sufficiently large,
\{U_{R,i}\}_{1\leq i\leq v}
is clearly a
\delta
-interacting family and
\|\nabla U_{R,i}\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{n}_{\epsilon})}
tend to
0
uniformly as
R\rightarrow\infty
.

Now fix
\epsilon=\left(\frac{\delta}{4C_{n}}\right)^{\frac{2}{n-2}}
, then there exists
R_{\epsilon}\gg 1
such that, when
R\geq R_{\epsilon}
, we have

\frac{\delta}{8}\leq\inf_{W_{1},...,W_{\nu}}\left\|\nabla\left(u_{\epsilon,R}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}W_{i}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|\nabla\left(u_{\epsilon,R}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U_{i}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}

and

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta u_{\epsilon,R}+u_{\epsilon,R}^{p}\right\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\left(\Delta U_{i}+U_{i}^{p}\right)+\Delta\rho_{\epsilon}+\rho_{\epsilon}^{p}\right\|_{H^{-1}}+\delta

\displaystyle=\left\|\Delta\rho_{\epsilon}\right\|_{H^{-1}}+\delta=\frac{5}{4}\delta.

Thus
u_{\epsilon,R}
is a function satisfying the requirements.
∎

7
Refined estimates for one bubble case

In this section, we aim to give an explicit upper bound for the stability constant
C_{\mathrm{CP}}(n,1)
. To achieve it, let us study the nondegeneracy of (
5.1
) first.

Consider the following conformal map from the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
(
n\geq 3
):

F(y^{\prime},y_{n})=\left(\frac{2y^{\prime}}{(1+y_{n})^{2}+|y^{\prime}|^{2}},\frac{1-|y|^{2}}{(1+y_{n})^{2}+|y^{\prime}|^{2}}\right),\quad y^{\prime}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.

(7.1)

The inverse map is given by

F^{-1}(x,t)=\left(\frac{2x}{(1+t)^{2}+|x|^{2}},\frac{1-t^{2}-|x|^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}+|x|^{2}}\right),\quad x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.

(7.2)

For any function
\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
, we can define
\mathcal{F}[\varphi]
in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
by

\mathcal{F}[\varphi](y)=(U[0,1]^{-1}\varphi)\circ F(y)\quad\text{for }y\in\mathbb{B}^{n}.

(7.3)

It is not hard to verify following identities (for details, we refer to
[
27
]
and
[
52
]
):

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}|\varphi|^{2^{\dagger}}\,dx=\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{n-1}\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}}|\mathcal{F}[\varphi]|^{2^{\dagger}}d\sigma,

(7.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dxdt=\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{n-2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|\nabla\mathcal{F}[\varphi]|^{2}\,dy+\frac{n-2}{2}\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}}\mathcal{F}[\varphi]^{2}\,d\sigma\right),

(7.5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U[0,1]^{p-1}\varphi^{2}\,dx=\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{n-1}\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}}\mathcal{F}[\varphi]^{2}\,d\sigma,

(7.6)

\Delta\varphi=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\iff\Delta\mathcal{F}[\varphi]=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{B}^{n},

(7.7)

\mathcal{F}[U[0,1]]=1,\quad\mathcal{F}[\partial_{\lambda}U[0,1]]=-\frac{n-2}{2}y_{n},\quad\mathcal{F}[\partial_{z_{i}}U[0,1]]=-\frac{n-2}{2}y_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1.

(7.8)

In the following we always equip
H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
with the norm

{\lVert u\rVert}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})}^{2}\coloneqq\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{n-2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dy+\frac{n-2}{2}\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}}u^{2}\,d\sigma\right).

(7.9)

From Poincaré inequality, this norm is equivalent to the standard Sobolev norm on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. Under this norm,
H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
is isometric to
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
via map
\mathcal{F}
.

It is well known (see
[
3
]
for example) that the quotient

\frac{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dy}{\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}}u^{2}\,d\sigma}

is related to the Steklov eigenvalue problem on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
:

\begin{cases}\Delta u=0&\text{in }\mathbb{B}^{n}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\vec{n}}=\mu u&\text{on }\partial\mathbb{B}^{n},\end{cases}

(7.10)

where
\vec{n}
denotes the outward unit normal vector. The Steklov eigenspaces
E_{k}
are the restrictions of the spaces
H_{k}^{n}
of homogeneous harmonic polynomials of degree k in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The corresponding eigenvalue
\mu_{k}=k
has multiplicity

\dim\,E_{k}=C_{n+k-1}^{n-1}-C_{n+k-3}^{n-1}.

Note that
E_{0}
is spanned by constant function
1=\mathcal{F}[U[0,1]]
and
E_{1}
is spanned by
n
coordinate functions:

\left\{y_{i}=-\frac{2}{n-2}\mathcal{F}[\partial_{z_{i}}U[0,1]],\ 1\leq i\leq n-1;\ y_{n}=-\frac{2}{n-2}\mathcal{F}[\partial_{\lambda}U[0,1]]\right\}.

The eigenvalue problem provides an orthogonal decomposition of
H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
:

H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})=\bigoplus_{i=-1}^{\infty}E_{i},

(7.11)

where
E_{-1}\coloneqq\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})|\;u=0\ \text{on }\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}\right\}=H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
.

Using the isometry
\mathcal{F}
and identities above, we can transform the equation (
7.10
) to

\begin{cases}\Delta u=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=-\left(1+\frac{2\mu}{n-2}\right)U[0,1]^{p-1}u&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.\end{cases}

(7.12)

Set

\kappa_{k}=1+\frac{2\mu_{k}}{n-2}=1+\frac{2k}{n-2}

(7.13)

and
R_{i}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(E_{i})
, then the decomposition (
7.11
) yields

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})=\bigoplus_{i=-1}^{\infty}R_{i},

(7.14)

where
R_{-1}=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})|\;u=0\ \text{on }\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\right\}
and
R_{k}
is the eigenspace of (
7.12
) with eigenvalue
\kappa_{k}
for
k\geq 0
. It is easy to see that

R_{0}=\text{Span}\left\{U[0,1]\right\},\quad R_{1}=\text{Span}\left\{\partial_{z_{i}}U[0,1],1\leq i\leq n-1;\partial_{\lambda}U[0,1]\right\}.

Let
T_{U}=R_{0}\oplus R_{1}
be the tangent space at
U[0,1]
in the manifold
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{E}}
of Escobar bubbles. Thanks to arguments above, we can establish the following nondegeneracy result:

Theorem 7.1

(spectral gap)
.

Let
\rho\in T_{U}^{\perp}
. Then

{\lVert\rho\rVert}^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}\geq\frac{n+2}{n-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U[0,1]^{p-1}\rho^{2}.

(7.15)

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
\rho\in R_{2}
, i.e.
\mathcal{F}[\rho]
is a homogeneous harmonic polynomial of degree
2
.

Now we can state and proof our main results.

Theorem 7.2

(asymptotic behavior near
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{E}}
)
.

Let
n\geq 3
. Assume
(u_{k})_{k}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
is a sequence of functions such that

\frac{1}{2}S_{\mathrm{E}}(n)^{n-1}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}S_{\mathrm{E}}(n)^{n-1},

and

\left\|\Delta u_{k}+|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}\right\|_{H^{-1}}\rightarrow 0\quad as\;k\rightarrow\infty.

Then

\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left\|\Delta u_{k}+|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}\right\|_{H^{-1}}}{d(u_{k},\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{E}})}\geq\frac{2}{n+2}.

(7.16)

Moreover, equality holds if and only if the following holds: Assume after suitable normalizations, translations and dilations,
d(u_{k},\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{E}})={\lVert u_{k}-U[0,1]\rVert}_{H^{1}}
. Let
u_{k}=\sum_{i=-1}^{\infty}\rho_{k,i}
be the unique decomposition with respect to (
7.14
). Then (up to some subsequence)

{\lVert u_{k}-U[0,1]-\rho_{k,2}\rVert}_{H^{1}}=o({\lVert\rho_{k,2}\rVert}_{H^{1}}).

(7.17)

Remark 7.3
.

A natural corollary of above theorem is that
C_{\mathrm{CP}}(n,1)\leq\frac{2}{n+2}
. It seems that the proof of Theorem
1.2
may only give the inequality (
7.16
). The proof there cannot give such upper bound because the identification of equality case is not available there. One possible reason is that (
6.4
) causes a loss.

Theorem 7.4

(strict upper bound)
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
C_{\mathrm{CP}}(n,1)
denotes the optimal constant in (
1.16
). Then

C_{\mathrm{CP}}(n,1)<\frac{2}{n+2}.

(7.18)

Remark 7.5
.

The combination of above two theorem indicates that: Any minimizing sequence must be away from
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{E}}
. However, whether minimizers exist is still unknown.

Before the proof, let us make two observations:

\left\|\Delta u_{k}+|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}\right\|_{H^{-1}}={\lVert u_{k}-\mathcal{P}[|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}]\rVert}_{H^{1}},

Recall that the operator
\mathcal{P}
is defined in (
4.10
).

For simplicity, in the proofs below, we will write
U
to denote
U[0,1]
.

Proof of Theorem
7.2
.

From Remark
5.5
, we can assume
d(u_{k},\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{E}})={\lVert u_{k}-U\rVert}_{H^{1}}
without loss of generality. Take the decomposition
u_{k}=\sum_{i=-1}^{\infty}\rho_{k,i}
with respect to (
7.14
). It is easy to see that
\rho_{k,1}=0
and
\rho_{k,0}=\beta_{k}U
, where
\beta_{k}\rightarrow 1
. Set
v_{k}=\rho_{k,-1}+\sum_{i=3}^{\infty}\rho_{k,i}
and
w_{k}=\rho_{k,-1}+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\rho_{k,i}
. Then
u_{k}=\beta_{k}U+w_{k}
. Let us expand the dual norm:

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta u_{k}+|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}\right\|_{H^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle={\lVert u_{k}-\mathcal{P}[|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}]\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle={\lVert u_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+{\lVert\mathcal{P}[|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}]\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+2\left\langle u_{k},\mathcal{P}[|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}]\right\rangle_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle={\lVert u_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}|u_{k}|^{p+1}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}\mathcal{P}[|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}]\,.

Using the orthogonal decomposition, continuity of
\mathcal{P}
, observations above and estimate (
6.5
), we have:

\displaystyle{\lVert u_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}=\beta_{k}^{2}{\lVert U\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+{\lVert w_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}|u_{k}|^{p+1}=\beta_{k}^{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U^{p+1}+\frac{p(p+1)}{2}\beta_{k}^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U^{p-1}w_{k}^{2}+o({\lVert w_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}\mathcal{P}[|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}]

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\left(\beta_{k}^{p}U^{p}+p\beta_{k}^{p-1}U^{p-1}w_{k}+A\right)\mathcal{P}\left[\beta_{k}^{p}U^{p}+p\beta_{k}^{p-1}U^{p-1}w_{k}+A\right]

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\beta_{k}^{p}U^{p}\mathcal{P}[\beta_{k}^{p}U^{p}]+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}p\beta_{k}^{p-1}U^{p-1}w_{k}\mathcal{P}[p\beta_{k}^{p-1}U^{p-1}w_{k}]+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}A\mathcal{P}[A]

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\beta_{k}^{p}U^{p}\mathcal{P}[p\beta_{k}^{p-1}U^{p-1}w_{k}]+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}p\beta_{k}^{p-1}U^{p-1}w_{k}\mathcal{P}[A]+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\beta_{k}^{p}U^{p}\mathcal{P}[A]

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\beta_{k}^{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U^{p+1}+p^{2}\beta_{k}^{2p-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U^{p-1}w_{k}\mathcal{P}[U^{p-1}w_{k}]+2\beta_{k}^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}UA+o({\lVert w_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\beta_{k}^{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U^{p+1}+p^{2}\beta_{k}^{2p-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U^{p-1}w_{k}\mathcal{P}[U^{p-1}w_{k}]

\displaystyle+p(p-1)\beta_{k}^{2p-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U^{p-1}w_{k}^{2}+o({\lVert w_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}),

where
A=|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}-\beta_{k}^{p}U^{p}-p\beta_{k}^{p-1}U^{p-1}w_{k}
. Combining all estimates above and using
\beta_{k}=1+o(1)
, we finally get

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta u_{k}+|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}\right\|_{H^{-1}}^{2}={}

\displaystyle(\beta_{k}-\beta_{k}^{p})^{2}{\lVert U\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+{\lVert w_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}-2p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U^{p-1}w_{k}^{2}

Note that

{\lVert w_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}={\lVert\rho_{k,-1}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}{\lVert\rho_{k,i}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2},

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U^{p-1}w_{k}^{2}=\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\kappa_{i}^{-1}{\lVert\rho_{k,i}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2},

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U^{p-1}w_{k}\mathcal{P}[U^{p-1}w_{k}]=\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\kappa_{i}^{-2}{\lVert\rho_{k,i}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2},

where
\kappa_{k}
is defined in (
7.13
). Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta u_{k}+|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}\right\|_{H^{-1}}^{2}={}

\displaystyle(\beta_{k}-\beta_{k}^{p})^{2}{\lVert U\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+{\lVert\rho_{k,-1}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}

(7.19)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}(1-p\kappa_{i}^{-1})^{2}{\lVert\rho_{k,i}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+o({\lVert w_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\beta_{k}-\beta_{k}^{p})^{2}{\lVert U\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+{\lVert\rho_{k,-1}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle+(1-p\kappa_{2}^{-1})^{2}\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}{\lVert\rho_{k,i}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+o({\lVert w_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\beta_{k}-\beta_{k}^{p})^{2}{\lVert U\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+(1-p\kappa_{2}^{-1})^{2}{\lVert w_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+o({\lVert w_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}).

The denominator of the quotient can be evaluated by

d(u_{k},\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{E}})^{2}={\lVert u_{k}-U\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}=(1-\beta_{k})^{2}{\lVert U\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+{\lVert w_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}.

(7.20)

Now the full quotient holds

\frac{\left\|\Delta u_{k}+|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}\right\|_{H^{-1}}^{2}}{d(u_{k},\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{E}})^{2}}\geq\frac{(\beta_{k}-\beta_{k}^{p})^{2}{\lVert U\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+(1-p\kappa_{2}^{-1})^{2}{\lVert w_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+o({\lVert w_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2})}{(1-\beta_{k})^{2}{\lVert U\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+{\lVert w_{k}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}}.

and

7.16
) follows directly from (

).

) indicates

{\lVert\rho_{k,-1}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}+\sum_{i=3}^{\infty}(1-p\kappa_{i}^{-1})^{2}{\lVert\rho_{k,i}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}=o((1-p\kappa_{2}^{-1})^{2}{\lVert\rho_{k,2}\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2})

and (

These are equivalent to (

). The proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}BU+o(\varepsilon^{3})

where

. Combining terms above, we can compute

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u\right\|_{H^{-1}}^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}(1-p\kappa_{2}^{-1})^{2}+\varepsilon^{3}\frac{p(p-1)}{3}(\kappa_{2}^{-1}-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U^{p-2}\rho^{3}+o(\varepsilon^{3}).

Note that

d(u,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{E}})^{2}={\lVert u-U\rVert}_{H^{1}}^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}.

The full quotient is

\frac{\left\|\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u\right\|_{H^{-1}}^{2}}{d(u,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{E}})^{2}}=(1-p\kappa_{2}^{-1})^{2}+\varepsilon\frac{p(p-1)}{3}(\kappa_{2}^{-1}-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U^{p-2}\rho^{3}+o(\varepsilon).

Since
\kappa_{2}=\frac{n+2}{n-2}
, it suffices to take suitable
\rho
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U^{p-2}\rho^{3}<0
. Let

\rho=-\mathcal{F}^{-1}[y_{1}y_{2}+y_{2}y_{3}+y_{3}y_{1}],

then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}U^{p-2}\rho^{3}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(y_{1}y_{2}+y_{2}y_{3}+y_{3}y_{1})^{3}\,dy

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}6y_{1}^{2}y_{2}^{2}y_{3}^{2}\,dy<0.

The proof is complete.
∎

Bounds on the Möbius-signed partition numbers
Taylor Daniels

Abstract
. For n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} let Π ⁡ [ n ] \Pi[n] denote the set of partitions of n n , i.e., the set of positive integer tuples ( x 1 , x 2 , … , x k ) (x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k}) such that x 1 ≥ x 2 ≥ ⋯ ≥ x k x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x_{k} and x 1 + x 2 + ⋯ + x k = n x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{k}=n . Fixing f : ℕ → { 0 , ± 1 } f:\mathbb{N}\to\{0,\pm 1\} , for π = ( x 1 , x 2 , … , x k ) ∈ Π ⁡ [ n ] \pi=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})\in\Pi[n] let f ( π ) := f ( x 1 ) f ( x 2 ) ⋯ f ( x k ) f(\pi):=f(x_{1})f(x_{2})\cdots f(x_{k}) . In this way we define the signed partition numbers p ⁡ ( n , f ) = ∑ π ∈ Π ⁡ [ n ] f ⁡ ( π ) . p(n,f)=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi[n]}f(\pi). Following work of Vaughan and Gafni on partitions into primes and prime powers, we derive asymptotic formulae for p ⁡ ( n , μ ) p(n,\mu) and p ⁡ ( n , λ ) p(n,\lambda) , where μ \mu and λ \lambda denote the Möbius and Liouville functions from prime number theory, respectively. In addition we discuss how quantities p ⁡ ( n , f ) p(n,f) generalize the classical notion of restricted partitions.

F_{0}(z)
instead of
F_{2}(z)
because the sums
\sum_{n}f^{2}(n)z^{n}
provide the main asymptotic terms in our analyses of
p(n,\mu)
and
p(n,\lambda)
.

For
j\in\{0,1\}
we split the functions
\Psi_{j}(z)
into leading sums and tail sums, namely

(3.3)

\Psi_{j}(z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq K\\
k\equiv j\,(2)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k}F_{j}(z^{k})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k>K\\
k\equiv j\,(2)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k}F_{j}(z^{k}),

where
K>1
is a real parameter of our choosing. The tail sums in equation (
3.3
) are always part of our error terms, so we record the following lemma bounding these tail sums independently of
j
, and even independently of
f
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\{0,\pm 1\}
. For real
\alpha
and large
X
and
K
one has that

\sum_{k>K}\frac{1}{k}F_{k}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))\ll\frac{X}{K}.

Proof.

For fixed
k
we see that

F_{k}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f^{k}(n)\rho^{nk}e(nk\alpha)\ll\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho^{nk}=\frac{\rho^{k}}{1-\rho^{k}}.

Using the relation
\rho=e^{-1/X}
, it follows that

\sum_{k>K}\frac{1}{k}F_{k}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))\ll\sum_{k>K}\frac{1}{k}\,\frac{e^{-k/X}}{1-e^{-k/X}}=\sum_{k>K}\frac{1/k}{e^{k/X}-1}\ll\sum_{k>K}\frac{X}{k^{2}},

and the result follows.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

It is clear that Lemma
3.2
extends to all bounded
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
.

The analyses of
\Psi_{0}(z)
and
\Psi_{1}(z)
are thus reduced to analyses of
F_{0}(z^{k})
and
F_{1}(z^{k})
for
k\leq K
. In the following discussion let
j\in\{0,1\}
and let
X
be sufficiently large.
Suppose that for
z
in some arbitrary
\Omega\subset\{|z|<1\}
there exist functions
\mathcal{X}_{0}(z)
and
\mathcal{X}_{1}(z)
such that: For
z\in\Omega
and
k\leq K
one has

(3.4)

F_{j}(z^{k})=\mathcal{X}_{j}(z)/k.

Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq K\\
k\equiv j\,(2)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathcal{X}_{j}(z)}{k^{2}}=c_{j}\mathcal{X}_{j}(z)+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\mathcal{X}_{j}(z)}{K}}}\right)\qquad(z\in\Omega),

where
c_{0}=\tfrac{1}{4}\zeta(2)
and
c_{1}=\tfrac{3}{4}\zeta(2)
, and it follows from Lemma
3.2
that

(3.5)

\Psi_{j}(z)=c_{j}\mathcal{X}_{j}(z)+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\mathcal{X}_{j}(z)}{K}}}\right)+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{X}{K}}}\right)\qquad(z\in\Omega).

If instead of (
3.4
) one has
F_{j}(z^{k})\ll\mathcal{X}_{j}(z)/k
for
z\in\Omega
, then one concludes that

\Psi_{j}(z)\ll\mathcal{X}_{j}(z)+X/K\qquad(z\in\Omega)

in place of (
3.5
). The above discussions are summarized in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\{0,\pm 1\}
, and for
j\in\{0,1\}
let
F_{j}(z)
and
\Psi_{j}(z)
be defined as in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), so that

\Psi(z)=\sum_{k\equiv 0\,(2)}\frac{1}{k}F_{0}(z^{k})+\sum_{k\equiv 1\,(2)}\frac{1}{k}F_{1}(z^{k})=\Psi_{0}(z)+\Psi_{1}(z)\qquad(|z|<1).

Fixing
j\in\{0,1\}
, if for
z\in\Omega
and
1\leq k\leq K
one has
F_{j}(z^{k})=\mathcal{X}_{j}(z)/k
, then

(3.6)

\Psi_{j}(z)=c_{j}\mathcal{X}_{j}(z)+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\mathcal{X}_{j}(z)}{K}}}\right)+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{X}{K}}}\right)\qquad(z\in\Omega),

where
c_{0}=\tfrac{1}{4}\zeta(2)
and
c_{1}=\tfrac{3}{4}\zeta(2)
. If instead of the relation
F_{j}(z^{k})=\mathcal{X}_{j}(z)/k
one has that
F_{j}(z^{k})\ll\mathcal{X}_{j}(z)/k
, then in place of equation (
3.6
) one concludes that

\Psi_{j}(z)\ll\mathcal{X}_{j}(z)+X/K\qquad(z\in\Omega).

4.
The minor arcs

We now begin our specific analyses of
\Psi(z,\mu)
, so
\Psi(z)
always indicates
\Psi(z,\mu)
unless otherwise stated. As
\Psi_{1}(z)
is easier to estimate than
\Psi_{0}(z)
, we analyze
\Psi_{1}(z)
first.

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix
A>0
. For
X>X_{0}(A)
and
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
one has

\Psi_{1}(\rho e(\alpha))\ll X(\log X)^{-A}.

Proof.

With
f=\mu
, the function
F_{1}(z)
of (
3.1
) is
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(n)z^{n}
, whereby

\Psi_{1}(\rho e(\alpha))=\sum_{k\equiv 1\,(2)}\frac{1}{k}F_{1}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))=\sum_{k\equiv 1\,(2)}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(n)e^{-nk/X}e(nk\alpha).

Let
k\leq X^{1/2}
. Integrating by parts and recalling that
S_{f}(t,\theta)=\sum_{n\leq t}f(n)e(n\theta)
, we find that

(4.1)

F_{1}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))=\frac{k}{X}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tk/X}S_{\mu}(t,k\alpha)\,dt.

When
t\geq X^{3/4}/k
, we bound
S_{\mu}(t,k\alpha)
using Davenport’s inequality
[
7
]
:

(4.2)

S_{\mu}(t,\theta)\ll t(\log t)^{-A}\qquad\text{for $t\geq 3$, uniformly in $\theta$.}

Trivially bounding
S_{\mu}(t,k\alpha)\ll t
when
t<X^{3/4}/k
, it follows that integral (
4.1
) is

\displaystyle\ll\frac{k}{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\int_{0}^{X^{3/4}k^{-1}}e^{-tk/X}t\,dt+\int_{X^{3/4}k^{-1}}^{\infty}e^{-tk/X}t(\log t)^{-A}\,dt}}\right]

(4.3)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{X}{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\int_{0}^{X^{-1/4}}e^{-u}u\,du+\log^{-A}(X^{3/4}k^{-1})\int_{X^{-1/4}}^{\infty}e^{-u}u\,du}}\right],

and we note that the assumption
k\leq X^{1/2}
ensures that our use of inequality (
4.2
) remains valid as
X\to\infty
. We also note the change of
k/X
to
X/k
in expression (
4.3
).
As
\int_{0}^{a}e^{-u}u\,du\ll a
and
\int_{a}^{\infty}e^{-u}u\,du\ll 1
when
0<a<1
, we deduce that

F_{k}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))\ll\frac{X}{k}\Big[X^{-\frac{1}{4}}+\log^{-A}(X^{\frac{3}{4}}k^{-1})\Big]\ll\frac{X/k}{(\log X)^{A}}\qquad(k\leq X^{\frac{1}{2}}).

Applying Lemma
3.4
with
K=X^{1/2}
we find that

\Psi_{1}(\rho e(\alpha))\ll X(\log X)^{-A}+X/X^{1/2},

and the result follows.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The reader may notice that the proof of Lemma
4.1
did not utilize the assumption that
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
. Indeed, because Davenport’s inequality (
4.2
) is uniform in the variable
\theta
(which became
k\alpha
above), we immediately deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 4.3
.

For fixed
A>0
, real
\alpha
, and all
X>X_{0}(A)
, one has that

\Psi_{1}(\rho e(\alpha))\ll X(\log X)^{-A}.

The analysis of
\Psi_{0}(\rho e(\alpha))
is much more involved than that of
\Psi_{1}(\rho e(\alpha))
, as we require a suitable replacement for Davenport’s inequality (
4.2
) for the sums
S_{\mu^{2}}(t,\theta)
. Our starting point is a theorem of Brüdern and Perelli that is built on previous work of Brüdern, Granville, Perelli, Vaughan, and Wooley
[
4
]
.

Lemma 4.4

(
[
5
]
, Thm. 4)
.

If
1\leq Q\leq X^{3/7}
, then

(4.4)

\sup_{\theta\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|S_{\mu^{2}}(X,\theta)}}\right|\ll X^{1+\varepsilon}Q^{-1}.

To motivate how Lemma
4.4
is used to establish our substitute for inequality (
4.2
) (and ultimately our bound on
\Psi_{0}
), we discuss a technical item to be addressed. Namely, to apply Lemma
3.4
we need that
F_{0}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))\ll\mathcal{X}_{0}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))/k
for
k\leq K
, with
\mathcal{X}_{0}
not depending on
k
. However, the multiples
k\alpha
may not all be contained in
\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
as is necessary to apply the bound (
4.4
). In fact, it may be that some
k\alpha>1
; however, as
S_{f}(t,\theta)=S_{f}(t,\{\theta\})
for any
f
, it suffices to consider the fractional parts
\{k\alpha\}
of the
k\alpha
.

Suppose for the moment that
K
,
\mathcal{X}
, and
\mathcal{Q}
are quantities such that
\{k\alpha\}\in\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Q})
for all
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
and all
k\leq K
. In this case we have (for a general
f
) that

(4.5)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)\\
k\leq K\end{subarray}}|S_{f}(t,k\alpha)|=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)\\
k\leq K\end{subarray}}|S_{f}(t,\{k\alpha\})|\leq\sup_{\theta\in\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Q})}|S_{f}(t,\theta)|.

Next, considering the definitions of the sets
\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Q})
and
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Q})
, one sees that if
t\geq\mathcal{X}
, then
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Q})\subset\mathfrak{m}(t,\mathcal{Q})
. From (
4.5
) then, for all
t\geq\mathcal{X}
we have that

(4.6)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)\\
k\leq K\end{subarray}}|S_{f}(t,k\alpha)|\leq\sup_{\theta\in\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Q})}|S_{f}(t,\theta)|\leq\sup_{\theta\in\mathfrak{m}(t,\mathcal{Q})}|S_{f}(t,\theta)|.

Thus we need only ensure that we may apply Lemma
4.4
to the final expression in (
4.6
) with
f=\mu^{2}
. These ideas are summarized in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that
1\leq Q\leq X
, that
1\leq\mathcal{Q}\leq\mathcal{X}^{3/7}
, that
K>0
, and that: For all
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
and
k\leq K
one has
\{k\alpha\}\in\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Q})
. Then for
t\geq\mathcal{X}
one has

(4.7)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)\\
k\leq K\end{subarray}}|S_{\mu^{2}}(t,k\alpha)|\leq\sup_{\theta\in\mathfrak{m}(t,\mathcal{Q})}|S_{\mu^{2}}(t,\theta)|\ll t^{1+\varepsilon}\mathcal{Q}^{-1}.

Proof.

Immediate by inequality (
4.6
) and Lemma
4.4
.
∎

We now consider how to select
K
,
\mathcal{X}
, and
\mathcal{Q}
. The simple choices
\mathcal{X}=X
and
\mathcal{Q}=Q
yield the estimate
\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))\ll X^{1+\varepsilon}
, which is insufficient since
\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))
is comparable to
X
when
\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}(X,Q)
.
Examining Lemma
4.4
and the proof of Lemma
4.1
, one may deduce that
decreasing

\mathcal{X}
has a far greater effect on the resulting estimates of
\Psi_{0}(\rho e(\alpha))
than does
increasing

\mathcal{Q}
, so we prioritize decreasing
\mathcal{X}
. The quantities
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are linked through the set
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Q})
however, so we must consider an “exchange” between them. The mechanism of this exchange is shown in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

Fix
\kappa\in(0,1)
, let
1\leq Q\leq X
, let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
, and let
1\leq k\leq Q^{\kappa}
. If
(a_{k},q_{k})=1
and

(4.8)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|q_{k}k\alpha-a_{k}}}\right|\leq Q^{1-\kappa}/X,

then
q_{k}>Q^{1-\kappa}
. In particular one has
\{k\alpha\}\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q^{1-\kappa})
. Finally, if
0<\nu<1-\kappa
then

(4.9)

\mathfrak{m}(X,Q^{1-\kappa})\subset\mathfrak{m}(XQ^{-\nu},Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}).

Proof.

We begin by recalling that
\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
consists of those
\beta\in[0,1)
such that: if
0\leq b<r
,
(b,r)=1
, and
|r\beta-b|\leq Q/X
, then it holds that
r>Q
.

By Dirichlet’s approximation theorem there exist
q_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
q_{k}\leq X/Q
,
(a_{k},q_{k})=1
, and
|q_{k}k\alpha-a_{k}|\leq Q/X
. By writing
a_{k}/(kq_{k})=a_{k}^{\prime}/(k^{\prime}q_{k})
with
(a_{k}^{\prime},k^{\prime}q_{k})=1
and
k^{\prime}\leq k
, we deduce that

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\alpha-\frac{a_{k}^{\prime}}{k^{\prime}q_{k}}}}\right|=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\alpha-\frac{a_{k}}{kq_{k}}}}\right|\leq\frac{Q}{kq_{k}X}\leq\frac{Q}{k^{\prime}q_{k}X}.

As
(a_{k}^{\prime},k^{\prime}q_{k})=1
and
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
, we thus have
k^{\prime}q_{k}>Q
and

q_{k}>Q/k^{\prime}\geq Q^{1-\kappa}
.
Again using Dirichlet’s approximation theorem, there exist
0\leq b_{k}<r_{k}
with
(b_{k},r_{k})=1
and
|r_{k}\{k\alpha\}-b_{k}|\leq Q/X
. Setting
a_{k}:=b_{k}+r_{k}\lfloor k\alpha\rfloor
, we have that
(a_{k},r_{k})=1
and
|r_{k}k\alpha-a_{k}|\leq Q/X
, whereby
r_{k}>Q^{1-\kappa}
and
\{k\alpha\}\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q^{1-\kappa})
.

Now let
0<\nu<1-\kappa
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q^{1-\kappa})
, and suppose that
(b,r)=1
and

(4.10)

|r\beta-b|\leq Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}/(XQ^{-\nu}).

Of course
|r\beta-b|\leq Q^{1-\kappa}/X
then, whereby
r>Q^{1-\kappa}\geq Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}
, yielding the result.
∎

We now establish the desired substitute for Davenport’s inequality (
4.2
).

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
X
be sufficiently large, and let
\delta,\kappa,\nu\in(0,1)
be such that
\nu<1-\kappa
and
\delta(1-\kappa-\nu)\leq\tfrac{3}{7}(1-\delta\nu)
. Let
Q=X^{\delta}
. For all
x\geq XQ^{-\nu}
one has

(4.11)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)\\
k\leq Q^{\kappa}\end{subarray}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|S_{\mu^{2}}(x,k\alpha)}}\right|\ll x^{1+\varepsilon}Q^{\kappa+\nu-1}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.6
, we have
\{k\alpha\}\in\mathfrak{m}(XQ^{-\nu},Q^{1-\kappa-\nu})
for all
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
and
k\leq Q^{\kappa}
. As
Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}\leq(XQ^{-\nu})^{3/7}
by our assumptions, we apply Lemma
4.5
with
\mathcal{X}=XQ^{-\nu}
,
\mathcal{Q}=Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}
, and
K=Q^{\kappa}
to conclude that

(4.12)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)\\
k\leq Q^{\kappa}\end{subarray}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|S_{\mu^{2}}(t,k\alpha)}}\right|\ll t^{1+\varepsilon}(Q^{1-\kappa-\nu})^{-1}\qquad(t\geq XQ^{-\nu}).

∎

At last we estimate
\Psi_{0}(\rho e(\alpha))
, and thereby
\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))
, for all
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
A>0
be fixed and let
Q=X^{2/5}
. For all
X>X_{0}(A)
and all
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
one has

\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))\ll X(\log X)^{-A}.

Proof.

Since
\Psi_{1}(\rho e(\alpha))\ll X(\log X)^{-A}
by Lemma
4.1
, it is more than sufficient to show that
\Psi_{0}(\rho e(\alpha))\ll X^{9/10}
. Recalling from (
3.1
) that
F_{0}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu^{2}(n)z^{n}
, we sum by parts to find that

F_{0}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu^{2}(n)\rho^{nk}e(nk\alpha)=\frac{k}{X}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tk/X}S_{\mu^{2}}(t,k\alpha)\,dt.

For the sake of exposition we let
\delta,\kappa,\nu\in(0,1)
be unknowns such that
\nu<1-\kappa
and
\delta(1-\kappa-\nu)\leq\tfrac{3}{7}(1-\delta\nu)
, let
Q=X^{\delta}
, and let
k\leq Q^{\kappa}
. Bounding
S_{\mu^{2}}(t,k\alpha)\ll t
when
t\leq XQ^{-\nu}
and using (
4.12
) when
t>XQ^{-\nu}
, we have

\displaystyle F_{0}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))

\displaystyle\ll\frac{k}{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\int_{0}^{XQ^{-\nu}}e^{-tk/X}t\,dt+Q^{\kappa+\nu-1}\int_{XQ^{-\nu}}^{\infty}e^{-tk/X}t^{1+\varepsilon}\,dt}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{X}{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\int_{0}^{kQ^{-\nu}}e^{-u}u\,du+Q^{\kappa+\nu-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{X}{k}}}\right)^{\varepsilon}\int_{kQ^{-\nu}}^{\infty}e^{-u}u\,du}}\right]

\displaystyle\ll\frac{X}{k}\Big[1-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+kQ^{-\nu}}}\right)\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-kQ^{-\nu}}}\right)+Q^{\kappa+\nu-1}X^{\varepsilon}\Big].

As
k\leq Q^{\kappa}
and the function
1-(1+t)e^{-t}
is increasing in
t
, this is

(4.13)

\ll\frac{X}{k}\Big[1-(1+Q^{\kappa-\nu})\exp(-Q^{\kappa-\nu})+Q^{\kappa+\nu-1}X^{\varepsilon}\Big].

If, in addition to our previous assumptions, we suppose that
\kappa-\nu<0
, then the inequality
1-(1+t)e^{-t}\ll t^{2}
(for small
t
), and the fact that
Q
may be assumed to be large (since
Q=X^{\delta}
and
X
is large), together imply that (
4.13
) is

(4.14)

\ll\frac{X}{k}\Big[Q^{2(\kappa-\nu)}+Q^{\kappa+\nu-1}X^{\varepsilon}\Big]=\frac{X}{k}\Big[X^{-2\delta(\nu-\kappa)}+X^{-\delta(1-\kappa-\nu)+\varepsilon}\Big].

Taking
\varepsilon<\tfrac{1}{5}\delta(1-\kappa-\nu)
, we deduce that

(4.15)

F_{0}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))\ll\frac{1}{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X^{1-2\delta(\nu-\kappa)}+X^{1-\frac{4}{5}\delta(1-\kappa-\nu)}}}\right],

and applying Lemma
3.4
using (
4.15
) and
K=Q^{\kappa}=X^{\delta\kappa}
, it follows that

\Psi_{0}(\rho e(\alpha))\ll X^{1-2\delta(\nu-\kappa)}+X^{1-\frac{4}{5}\delta(1-\kappa-\nu)}+X^{1-\delta\kappa}.

Setting
\delta=\tfrac{2}{5}
,
\kappa=\tfrac{1}{4}
, and
\nu=\tfrac{2}{5}
, we see that these satisfy the conditions

\nu<1-\kappa,\quad\kappa<\nu,\quad\text{and}\quad\delta(1-\kappa-\nu)\leq\tfrac{3}{7}(1-\delta\nu)

and yield the bound

\Psi_{0}(\rho e(\alpha))\ll X^{\frac{17}{25}}+X^{\frac{111}{125}}+X^{\frac{9}{10}}\ll X^{\frac{9}{10}},

and the proposition follows.
∎

5.
The major arcs

We now analyze
\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))
for
\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}(X,Q)
, which includes when
\alpha
is in
\mathfrak{P}
or
\mathfrak{P}_{*}
. Although the choice
Q=X^{2/5}
mentioned in the proof of Proposition
4.8
is ultimately used, we again treat
Q
as a free parameter until this is choice is necessary.
In truth, we need not further analyze
\Psi_{1}(\rho e(\alpha))
since Corollary
4.3
provides a satisfactory estimate already. While our analysis of
\Psi_{0}(\rho e(\alpha))
on the minor arcs requires estimates of the exponential sums
S_{\mu^{2}}(t,\theta)
, our analysis on the major arcs requires results on sums of
\mu^{2}(n)
as
n
runs over arithmetic progressions.

We recall that
\mu(n)\neq 0
if and only if
p^{2}\nmid n
for all prime
p
, and that such
n
are said to be squarefree or
quadratfrei
. For positive
q
and
r
, not necessarily coprime, we define

\mathcal{Q}(t;q,r)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq t\\
n\equiv r\,(q)\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(n),

which is simply the number of squarefree
n\leq t
that are congruent to
r
modulo
q
.
The following function determines the asymptotic behavior of
\mathcal{Q}(t;q,r)
, so it is convenient to define it now.

Definition 5.1
.

If
(q,r)
is squarefree set

(5.1)

g(q,r):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
(n^{2},q)\hskip 0.32771pt|\hskip 0.32771ptr\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)(n^{2},q)}{n^{2}q},

and set
g(q,r):=0
otherwise.

We note that (
5.1
) immediately implies that
g(q,r)=g(q,(q,r))
. Although the following result is well known, we include a proof here for completeness.

Lemma 5.2
.

For all positive integers
q
and
r
and all sufficiently large
t
, one has

\mathcal{Q}(t;q,r)=g(q,r)t+O(t^{1/2})

with implicit constant independent of
q
and
r
.

Proof.

Writing
n=ab^{2}
with
a
squarefree, and using the well known identity

\mu^{2}(n)=\sum_{d^{2}\mid n}\mu(d),

we rearrange terms in the sum
\mathcal{Q}(t;q,r)
to find that

\mathcal{Q}(t;q,r)=\sum_{b\leq\sqrt{t}}\mu(b)\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq t/b^{2}\\
ab^{2}\equiv r\,(q)\end{subarray}}1\bigg)=\sum_{d\hskip 0.32771pt|\hskip 0.32771ptr}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\leq\sqrt{t}\\
(b^{2},q)=d\end{subarray}}\mu(b)\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq t/b^{2}\\
ab^{2}\equiv r\,(q)\end{subarray}}1\bigg).

For any
b
let
d=(b^{2},q)
, let
b^{2}=\beta d
, let
q=\theta d
, and let
r=\rho d
. Then

, i.e., that

As

otherwise, it follows that

(12.4)

\displaystyle F_{0}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))\ll\frac{X}{k}\int_{0}^{kQ^{\kappa-1}}e^{-u}u\,du+\|k\alpha\|^{-1}\int_{kQ^{\kappa-1}}^{\infty}e^{-u}\,du.

Integrating by parts and applying (
12.3
), the right side of (

) is seen to be

\ll\frac{X}{k}\big(1-(1+kQ^{\kappa-1})e^{-kQ^{\kappa-1}}\big)+XQ^{\kappa-1}e^{-kQ^{\kappa-1}}=\frac{X}{k}(1-e^{-kQ^{\kappa-1}}).

As

and
2\kappa-1<0
, we bound

for

to deduce that

F_{0}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))\ll\frac{X}{k}(1-e^{-X^{\delta(2\kappa-1)}})\leq\frac{X}{k}X^{\delta(2\kappa-1)}.

Using

we apply Lemma
3.4
to conclude that

\Psi_{0}(\rho e(\alpha))\ll X^{1+\delta(2\kappa-1)}+X^{1-\delta\kappa},

and letting

yields the bound

∎

Short-time estimates for a real moment problem with Two-Term Weyl Spectral Law Thanks: Université de Lorraine, CNRS, IECL, F-54000 Nancy, France.
Rémi Buffe, Alessandro Duca

Abstract
. In this work, we first study the solvability of moment problems involving real exponentials e λ k ​ t \displaystyle e^{\lambda_{k}t} and provide explicit estimates of the associated control cost. The result holds when the increasing sequence of distinct real numbers ( λ k ) k ∈ ℕ ∗ \displaystyle(\lambda_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}} satisfies a suitable two-term Weyl asymptotic law, without imposing any uniform spacing condition on blocks of its elements. We then deduce a corresponding controllability result for a linear control problem. Next, we present an exponential family fitting our hypotheses that cannot be treated by existing results of this type. Finally, we show how to deduce new exact controllability results for suitable fractional bilinear heat equations in higher-dimensional domains.



1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Moment problems

Different problems arising in applied mathematics can be formulated in terms of the solvability of a so-called moment problem. Let
\displaystyle\mathsf{H}
be a Hilbert space defined over a scalar field
\displaystyle\mathsf{F}
, and let
\displaystyle(\xi_{k})_{k\in I}
be a family of elements of
\displaystyle\mathsf{H}
, with
\displaystyle I\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}
. The solvability of the associated moment problem consists in determining whether, for some specific given sequence of scalars
\displaystyle(x_{k})_{k\in I}\subset\mathsf{F}
, there exists
\displaystyle h\in\mathsf{H}
such that

\langle\xi_{k},h\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}=x_{k},\quad\forall k\in I.

For instance, let
\displaystyle\mathsf{H}=L^{2}(0,2\pi;{\mathbb{C}})
and
\displaystyle\xi_{k}=e^{ikt}
with
\displaystyle k\in I={\mathbb{Z}}
, the moment problem reads

\int^{2\pi}_{0}e^{-ikt}h(t)dt=x_{k},\quad\forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}},

for
\displaystyle(x_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{C}})
. In this case, the solution
\displaystyle h
is uniquely reconstructed from the coefficients
\displaystyle(x_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
by considering them as the Fourier coefficients of
\displaystyle h
(up to a normalization factor). More generally, this type of construction applies whenever
\displaystyle(\xi_{k})_{k\in I}
is an orthogonal basis of
\displaystyle\mathsf{H}
.

In this work, we consider the Hilbert space
\displaystyle\mathsf{H}=L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}})
, with
\displaystyle T>0
, together with a family of real exponentials

\xi_{k}(t)=e^{\lambda_{k}t},\quad k\in I={\mathbb{N}}^{*},\;t\in(0,T),

generated by an increasing sequence of distinct positive numbers
\displaystyle(\lambda_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
. We investigate the solvability of the following moment problem: given
\displaystyle x=(x_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
in a suitable normed sequence space
\displaystyle\mathsf{X}
, we ask whether it is possible to find
\displaystyle h
such that

(1.1)

\int^{T}_{0}e^{\lambda_{k}t}h(t)dt=x_{k},\quad\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}.

In addition, we estimate the cost associated with (
1.1
), defined as the optimal constant
\displaystyle C_{T}>0
such that

\|h\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leqslant C_{T}\|x\|_{\mathsf{X}},

given
\displaystyle h
solving (
1.1
). In this setting, the functions
\displaystyle\xi_{k}
are not guaranteed to form either a basis or an orthonormal system, and the solvability of the moment problem requires techniques that differ from the standard orthogonal expansion framework.

For a real sequence of non-decreasing positive numbers
\displaystyle\lambda=(\lambda_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}

\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}(\Gamma)=\#\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*},\;\lambda_{k}\leqslant\Gamma\}.

Let

1.1.3.
Literature overview

The solvability of moment problems involving real exponentials to establish controllability properties for parabolic equations goes back to the seminal work of Fattorini and Russel
[
22
,
23
]
. There, spectral assumptions are made,
e.g

(1.13)

\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}<+\infty,

and

(1.14)

\inf_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}|\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{k}|\neq 0.

In higher dimensions, Fursikov and Imanuvilov
[
25
]
and Lebeau and Robbiano
[
28
]
proved the null-controllability of the heat equation using Carleman estimates, with localized controls depending on both space and time. These results show that no minimal time is required, that is, the solution can be driven to rest in an arbitrarily small time. However, Dolecki
[
21
]
identified a specific control operator for which a positive minimal time is necessary. This is now a very active field of research
[
4
,
6
,
5
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
26
]
, where authors have explored how spectral conditions and control operator properties lead to the existence or non-existence of a minimal time in general abstract settings.

In
[
5
]
, under the hypothesis that the eigenvectors forms a Riesz basis, the minimal time needed to have a null-control depends on the condensation index of the spectral sequence
\displaystyle\mu
and the spectral properties of the control operator. In fact, they proved that if the control operator has sufficiently good properties (namely, if it allows to reach all frequencies with small control cost), then the minimal time is equal to the condensation index of the spectral sequence (see
[
5
,
14
,
32
]
or Section
1.1.4
below for a definition). In this setting, the authors do not provide an explicit dependence of the control cost with respect to small times.

In
[
13
,
15
]
, the authors succeeded to solve in arbitrarily short time a block moment problem where the spectral sequence admits some condensation, along with condensation of the associated (generalized) eigenvectors. There, and in the recent article
[
26
]
, small time estimates on the control cost are provided, under the assumption of (
1.13
), and where (
1.14
) is replaced by the existence of uniformly bounded block ensuring the spectral gap, of the form

(1.15)

\exists p\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*},\rho>0\text{ s.t. }\#(\mu\cap[\lambda,\lambda+p])<\rho,\quad\forall\lambda>0.

Finally, recent progress
[
6
]
has been made toward the solvability of the moment problem arising from controlled equations in greater dimension than one, that is when (
1.13
) is not satisfied.

Here, we are concerned with small time null-controls costs. Our approach addresses cases where (
1.13
) holds but (
1.15
) may fail (see Section
1.1.5
),
i.e.
,

\{M\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*},\;\inf_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}|\mu_{k+M}-\mu_{k}|\neq 0\}=\emptyset.

We initially follow the framework of
[
26
]
(see Proposition
2.1
below). In this Proposition, we observe that the constants in the exponential estimates can be improved in the following manner: by allowing
\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{2}
and
\displaystyle\mathfrak{gp_{1}}^{-1}
to depend slightly on the frequency, we can weaken the assumptions on the Weyl asymptotics and spectral gap. This is achieved through a Lebeau-Robbiano strategy: we first control the frequencies below a cut-off
\displaystyle\Lambda>0
(where
\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{2}
and
\displaystyle\mathfrak{gp_{1}}^{-1}
may grow slowly with
\displaystyle\Lambda
), and then leverage the dissipation of the heat equation to complete the construction of the final null-control.

1.2.2.
Literature overview

In the well-known result
[
7
]
, Ball, Marsden, and Slemrod established that multiplicative bilinear controls can introduce obstructions to the exact controllability of a system dynamics. Roughly speaking, when the control operator
\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}
is bounded on
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}
, the authors showed that the reachable set is contained in a countable union of compact sets. This phenomenon is very general: it occurs not only to the bilinear heat equation, but also to the bilinear Schrödinger and wave equations. The negative result established in
[
7
]
is the primary motivation for studying reachable targets in evolution equations with bilinear controls. One can cite the important works
[
11
,
8
]
on the exact controllability of bilinear wave or Schrödinger equations, in the case where the control operator is not bounded.

Small-time local exact controllability to eigensolutions properties for the heat-like equation (
1.17
) was firstly derived by Alabau-Boussouira, Cannarsa and Urbani in
[
3
,
2
]
assuming the gap condition

(1.18)

\exists\gamma>0,\;\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*},\quad|\sqrt{\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{j}}-\sqrt{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}}|\geqslant\gamma>0

and the spreading assumption

(1.19)

\langle\mathcal{B}\phi_{j},\phi_{j}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\neq 0,\quad|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}|^{q}|\langle\mathcal{B}\phi_{j},\phi_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|\geqslant b,\quad\forall\,k\neq j,

with
\displaystyle p,b>0
. A direct application of their work is the controllability of the heat equation (
1.17
) in the one-dimensional case
\displaystyle\Omega=(0,1)
. For related problems and further developments on this topic, see also
[
1
,
19
]
.

These results have been extended in
[
16
]
by the second author with Cannarsa and Urbani, where they proved the same local controllability to the eigensolutions for the equation (
1.17
) on network-type domains. In this framework, the spectrum of the Laplacian
\displaystyle-\Delta
has different properties with respect to the unit interval as we only know the existence of
\displaystyle N\in N^{*}
such that

\sqrt{\lambda_{k+N}}-\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\geqslant\gamma>0.

In this work, as an application of Theorem

) is not verified (see the example in Section

(see also

The result is a direct consequence of
[

, Theorem 1.2]
. Indeed, (H2), (H3), and (H4) in
[
26
, Definition 1.1]
are fulfilled. Next, conditions (
2.2
) imply (H1). Let us show that (H5) is satisfied. First, we prove by induction that for any
\displaystyle j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{j})\qquad v_{k+j\mathfrak{n}}-v_{k}\geqslant\mathfrak{g}((k+j\mathfrak{n})^{2}-k^{2}),\quad\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}.

Indeed,
\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{1})
holds from assumption (
2.2
). Moreover, assume that
\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{j})
holds for some
\displaystyle j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
. Then, using
\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{1})
and
\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{j})
, for any
\displaystyle k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle v_{k+(j+1)\mathfrak{n}}-v_{k}=v_{(k+j\mathfrak{n})+\mathfrak{n}}-v_{k+j\mathfrak{n}}+v_{k+j\mathfrak{n}}-v_{k}\geqslant\mathfrak{g}((k+(j+1)\mathfrak{n})^{2}-k^{2}),

and then the assertion holds for
\displaystyle j+1
, ensuring
\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{j+1})
. Hence
\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{j})
holds for any
\displaystyle j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}.
Now, let
\displaystyle j,n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
be such that
\displaystyle j\mathfrak{n}<n<(j+1)\mathfrak{n}
. Then, for any
\displaystyle k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu_{k+n}-\mu_{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geqslant\mu_{k+j\mathfrak{n}}-\mu_{k}\geqslant\mathfrak{g}(2kj\mathfrak{n}+j^{2}\mathfrak{n}^{2})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{4}(2k(j+1)\mathfrak{n}+(j+1)^{2}\mathfrak{n}^{2})\geqslant\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{4}(2kn+n^{2}).

which is (H5). Then, the sequence
\displaystyle v
belongs to the class
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(0,\tfrac{\mathfrak{g}}{4},\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{p}_{0},\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2},\alpha)
, defined in
[
26
, Definition 1.1]
. Hence,
[
26
, Theorem 1.2]
ensures the existence of

, a bi-orthogonal family to the exponentials
\displaystyle(e^{-\nu_{k}t})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
in

The bi-orthogonal family to the exponentials

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(t)=\widetilde{\sigma}_{k}(T-t)e^{-\nu_{k}T}

[

, adapted to our setting.

\displaystyle T\in(0,1)
, there exists
\displaystyle(\sigma_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}\subset L^{2}(0,T)
and a constant
\displaystyle C>0
depending only on
\displaystyle\mu_{1}
,
\displaystyle\min(c_{1},c_{2})
, such that for any
\displaystyle k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,

\|\sigma_{k}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leqslant Ca_{k}^{n_{\Lambda}}\left(\frac{16+4\gamma_{\Lambda}c_{2}\Lambda^{2a-1}}{\gamma_{\Lambda}^{2}c_{1}^{4}}\right)^{2n_{\Lambda}-2}\\
\times\exp\left(C\big(-v_{k}T+v_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1+n_{\Lambda}+16(c_{1}\gamma_{\Lambda})^{-2}+c_{2}\Lambda^{\frac{2a-1}{2}}\right)+\frac{(1+c_{2}\Lambda^{\frac{2a-1}{2}})^{2}}{T}\big)\right).

From (

), we deduce the existence of
\displaystyle C_{0}>0
(independent on
\displaystyle\Lambda

\|\sigma_{k}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leqslant C_{0}\exp\left(C_{0}\left(\Lambda^{1-\varepsilon}+\nu_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}+\frac{\Lambda^{1-2\varepsilon}}{T}\right)\right),\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*},\;\Lambda\geqslant\Lambda_{0}.

3.
The Lebeau-Robbiano method

Let
\displaystyle H
be a real separable Hilbert space endowed with a scalar product
\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
associated to the norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{H}
. Let
\displaystyle A:D(A)\subset H\to H
be a densely defined linear operator such that

•

\displaystyle A
is self-adjoint;

•

\displaystyle\langle Ax,x\rangle_{H}\geqslant 0
for every
\displaystyle x\in D(A)
;

•

there exists
\displaystyle\mu>0
such that the operator
\displaystyle(\mu I+A)^{-1}:H\to H
is compact.

In this framework, the spectrum of
\displaystyle A
is purely discrete and consists of a sequence of ordered non-negative real numbers
\displaystyle(\mu_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
. We denote by
\displaystyle(\varphi_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
the associated eigenfunctions, which form a Hilbert basis of
\displaystyle H
. The operator
\displaystyle-A
generates a strongly continuous semigroup denoted by
\displaystyle e^{-tA}
.

We now introduce the linear controlled problem

(3.1)

\begin{cases}\xi^{\prime}(t)+A\xi(t)+Bv(t)=0,&t\in(0,T),\\
\xi(0)=\xi_{0}\in H,\end{cases}

where
\displaystyle v\in L^{2}(0,T)
and
\displaystyle B\in H
.
We denote by
\displaystyle\xi
the (mild) solution to the linear control problem (
3.1
) corresponding to the initial condition
\displaystyle\xi_{0}
and control
\displaystyle v
given by

\xi(t)=e^{-tA}\xi_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)A}Bv(s)ds.

We now introduce the partial null-controllability definition we shall use in what follows.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle E
be a closed subspace of
\displaystyle H
. We say that the problem (
3.1
) is null-controllable in
\displaystyle E
and in a time
\displaystyle T>0
, if there exists a constant
\displaystyle K_{E}(T)>0
such that, for any
\displaystyle\xi_{0}\in H
, there exists a control
\displaystyle v\in L^{2}(0,T)
such that

\Pi_{E}\xi(T)=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leqslant K_{E}(T)\|\xi_{0}\|_{H},

where
\displaystyle\Pi_{E}
denotes the orthogonal projection on
\displaystyle E
in
\displaystyle H
. The smallest constant
\displaystyle K_{E}(T)
is called the control cost, and it is defined as

K_{E}(T):=\sup_{\|\xi_{0}\|_{H}=1}\inf_{v\in L^{2}(0,T)}\left\{\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\,:\,\Pi_{E}\xi(T)=0\right\}.

When
\displaystyle E=H
, we simply say that the problem (
3.1
) is null-controllable and we write
\displaystyle K_{E}(T)=K(T)
.

3.1.
Null-controllability in filtered spaces using the moment method

Let
\displaystyle T>0
and
\displaystyle\Lambda>0
. We denote by

J_{\Lambda}:=\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*},\;\mu_{k}\leqslant\Lambda\},\quad\quad\quad E_{\Lambda}:=\Span\{\varphi_{k},\;k\in J_{\Lambda}\}.

We introduce a classical result of null-controllability on finite-dimensional spaces.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\Lambda>0
and
\displaystyle j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
. Assume that
\displaystyle B\in H
satisfies the following identities:

\langle B,\varphi_{k}\rangle_{H}\neq 0,\ \ \ \forall k\in J_{\Lambda}.

If there exists
\displaystyle(\sigma_{\Lambda,j})_{j\in J_{\Lambda}}\subset L^{2}(0,T)
such that

\int_{0}^{T}e^{\mu_{k}t}\sigma_{\Lambda,j}(t)dt=\delta_{j,k},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall j,k\in J_{\Lambda},

where
\displaystyle\delta_{j,k}
denotes the Kronecker symbol, then the problem (
3.1
) is null-controllable in
\displaystyle E_{\Lambda}
in any time
\displaystyle T>0
with control cost

K_{E_{\Lambda}}(T)^{2}\leqslant(\#J_{\Lambda})\frac{\sup_{k\in J_{\Lambda}}\|\sigma_{k}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}}{\inf_{k\in J_{\Lambda}}|\langle B,\varphi_{k}\rangle_{H}|^{2}}

Proof.

By the Duhamel formula, the problem (
3.1
) is null-controllable in
\displaystyle E_{\Lambda}
and in a time
\displaystyle T>0
, if there exists a function
\displaystyle v_{\Lambda}\in L^{2}(0,T)
such that, for any
\displaystyle k\in J_{\Lambda}
, the following identities are verified:

\int_{0}^{T}e^{t\mu_{k}}v_{\Lambda}(t)dt=d_{k},\quad\quad\quad\quad\forall k\in J_{\Lambda},

where
\displaystyle d_{k}=-\tfrac{\langle\xi_{0},\varphi_{k}\rangle_{H}}{\langle B,\varphi_{k}\rangle_{H}}
. For every
\displaystyle T>0
, we can define
\displaystyle v_{\Lambda}
as the linear combination

v_{\Lambda}=\sum_{k\in J_{\Lambda}}d_{k}\sigma_{\Lambda,k}.

The estimate of
\displaystyle v_{\Lambda}
follows by noticing

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|v_{\Lambda}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leqslant(\#J_{\Lambda})\sum_{k\in J_{\Lambda}}|d_{k}|^{2}\|\sigma_{\Lambda,k}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\leqslant(\#J_{\Lambda})\frac{\sup_{k\in J_{\Lambda}}\|\sigma_{\Lambda,k}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}}{\inf_{k\in J_{\Lambda}}|\langle B,\varphi_{k}\rangle_{H}|^{2}}\|\xi_{0}\|^{2}_{H}.

∎

3.2.
Null-controllability in the full space

In this section, we present the adaptation due to Miller
[
30
]
(see also
[
10
]
) of the well-known Lebeau-Robbiano method to prove the observability of the problem (
3.1
). First, we need to introduce the definition of partial observability, which is an adaptation of
[
27
, Proposition 7.7]
to our case.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\displaystyle E
be a closed subspace of
\displaystyle H
. The null-controllability of the problem (
3.1
) in
\displaystyle E
at time
\displaystyle T>0
with control cost
\displaystyle K_{E}(T)
is equivalent to the following observability estimate :

\|e^{-TA}\Pi_{E}\zeta\|_{H}\leqslant K_{E}(T)\|\langle B,e^{-tA}\Pi_{E}\zeta\rangle_{H}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)},\quad\forall\zeta\in H.

Proof.

Consider the bounded control operator, with
\displaystyle U={\mathbb{R}}
,

\begin{array}[]{rccl}M:&U&\rightarrow&H\\
&v&\mapsto&Bv,\end{array}

whose adjoint is given by

\begin{array}[]{rccl}M^{*}:&H&\rightarrow&U\\
&z&\mapsto&\langle B,z\rangle_{H}.\end{array}

The proof follows by a direct application of
[
27
, Proposition 7.7]
.
∎

We are finally ready to show how to use the Lebeau-Robbiano-Miller method to prove the null-controllability of the problem (
3.1
) when partial observability inequalities are verified in suitable finite-dimensional subspaces of
\displaystyle H
and the control cost satisfies specific inequalities.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that there exist
\displaystyle C_{0}>0
and
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for any
\displaystyle T\in(0,1)
and for any
\displaystyle\Lambda>0
, the problem (
3.1
) is null-controllable in
\displaystyle E_{\Lambda}
at time
\displaystyle T>0
with control cost

K_{E_{\Lambda}}(T)\leqslant C_{0}\exp\left(C_{0}\big(\Lambda^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{T^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}}\big)\right).

Then there exists
\displaystyle C_{1}>0
such that for any
\displaystyle T\in(0,1)
, the problem (
3.1
) is null-controllable with control cost

K(T)\leqslant C_{1}\exp\left(\frac{C_{1}}{T^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}}\right).

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\tau>0
and
\displaystyle 0<T_{1}<T_{2}<1
be such that
\displaystyle\tau=T_{2}-T_{1}
. Let
\displaystyle\delta\in(0,1/2)
to be fixed below. We have, for any
\displaystyle\zeta\in H
, and any
\displaystyle\Lambda>0
,

\|e^{-T_{2}A}\zeta\|_{H}^{2}=\|e^{-T_{2}A}\Pi_{E_{\Lambda}}\zeta\|_{H}^{2}+\|e^{-T_{2}H}(I-\Pi_{E_{\Lambda}})\zeta\|_{H}^{2}.

From Proposition
3.3
, it follows

\|e^{-T_{2}A}\zeta\|_{H}^{2}\leqslant K_{E_{\Lambda}}(\delta\tau)^{2}\|\langle B,e^{-2tA}\Pi_{E_{\Lambda}}\zeta\rangle_{H}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(T_{2}-\delta\tau,T_{2})}+e^{-\Lambda\tau}\|e^{-2T_{1}A}(I-\Pi_{E_{\Lambda}})\zeta\|_{H}^{2}.

Yet, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle B,e^{-tA}\Pi_{E_{\Lambda}}\zeta\rangle_{H}\|_{L^{2}(T_{2}-\delta\tau,T_{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\;2\|\langle B,e^{-tA}\zeta\rangle_{H}\|_{L^{2}(T_{1},T_{2})}^{2}+2\|\langle B,e^{-tA}(I-\Pi_{E_{\Lambda}})\zeta\rangle_{H}\|_{L^{2}(T_{2}-\delta\tau,T_{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\;2\|\langle B,e^{-tA}\zeta\rangle_{H}\|_{L^{2}(T_{1},T_{2})}^{2}+2e^{-2(1-\delta)\Lambda\tau}\|B\|^{2}_{H}\|e^{-T_{1}A}\zeta\|_{H}^{2}.

The above estimates yield the existence of
\displaystyle C>0
such that, for any
\displaystyle 0<\tau
,
\displaystyle 0<T_{1}<T_{2}<1
such that
\displaystyle\tau=T_{2}-T_{1}
,
\displaystyle\delta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
,
\displaystyle\Lambda>0
, and any
\displaystyle\zeta\in H
,

\|e^{-T_{2}A}\zeta\|_{H}^{2}\leqslant C\exp\left(C(\Lambda^{\alpha}+(\delta\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}})\right)\|\langle B,e^{-tA}\zeta\rangle_{H}\|_{L^{2}(T_{1},T_{2})}^{2}\\
+\Big(C\exp\left(C(\Lambda^{\alpha}+(\delta\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}})-2\Lambda(1-\delta)\tau\right)+\exp(-2\Lambda\tau)\Big)\|e^{-T_{1}A}\zeta\|_{H}^{2}.

Now, we choose
\displaystyle\Lambda=(\delta\tau)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}
and there exists
\displaystyle\tilde{C}>0
independent on
\displaystyle\delta
,
\displaystyle\tau
, and
\displaystyle\zeta
such that

\|e^{-T_{2}A}\zeta\|_{H}^{2}\leqslant\tilde{C}\exp\left(\tilde{C}(\delta\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\right)\|\langle B,e^{-tA}\zeta\rangle_{H}\|_{L^{2}(T_{1},T_{2})}^{2}\\
+\Big(\tilde{C}\exp\left(\tilde{C}(\delta\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}-2(1-\delta)\delta^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}\tau^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\right)+\exp\left(-2\delta^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}\tau^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\right)\Big)\|e^{-T_{1}A}\zeta\|_{H}^{2}.

Let
\displaystyle f_{\delta}(\tau)=\tilde{C}^{-1}\exp(-\tilde{C}(\delta\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}})
. We fix
\displaystyle\delta\in(0,1)
sufficiently small (independently on
\displaystyle\tau,T_{1},T_{2}
) so that the following approximate observability holds : there exists
\displaystyle\tilde{C}>0
such that for any
\displaystyle 0<T_{1}<T_{2}<1
, we have

f_{\delta}(\tau)\|e^{-T_{2}A}\zeta\|_{H}^{2}\leqslant\|\langle B,e^{-tA}\zeta\rangle_{H}\|_{L^{2}(T_{1},T_{2})}^{2}+f_{\delta}\Big(\frac{\tau}{2}\Big)\|e^{-T_{1}A}\zeta\|_{H}^{2}.

For any
\displaystyle T\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we choose
\displaystyle T_{2}=\tfrac{T}{2^{k}}
and
\displaystyle T_{1}=\tfrac{T}{2^{k+1}}
with
\displaystyle k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle f_{\delta}(\tfrac{T}{2^{k}})\|e^{-T/2^{k}A}\zeta\|_{H}^{2}\leqslant\|\langle B,e^{-tA}\zeta\rangle_{H}\|_{L^{2}\big(\tfrac{T}{2^{k+1}},\tfrac{T}{2^{k}}\big)}^{2}+f_{\delta}(\tfrac{T}{2^{k+1}})\|e^{-\tfrac{T}{2^{k+1}}A}\zeta\|_{H}^{2}.

Summing over
\displaystyle k\in{\mathbb{N}}
yields the following observability inequality

\|e^{-TA}\zeta\|_{H}^{2}\leqslant f_{\delta}^{-1}(T)\|\langle B,e^{-tA}\zeta\rangle_{H}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}.

Finally, it is sufficient to apply Proposition
3.3
to end the proof.
∎

The aim of this section is to prove the exact controllability to eigensolutions of the bilinear parabolic equation (

Proposition 5.1

Let
\displaystyle T>0

\displaystyle\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\psi\in C^{0}([0,T],\mathcal{H})

We introduce the following linearized problem in

(5.1)

\begin{cases}\xi^{\prime}(t)+\mathcal{A}\xi(t)+v(t)\mathfrak{B}\phi_{j}=0,&t\in(0,T),\\

The proof of Theorem

Proposition 5.2

If the problem (
5.1

\displaystyle T>0

such that

(5.2)

K(\tau)\leqslant e^{\nu/\tau^{\gamma}},\quad\forall\,0<\tau\leqslant T_{0}.

Proposition

is a standard result that follows from the arguments of
[

. The derivation in
[

handles the case

, but the proof extends naturally to any

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Let us consider (

).

5.2.
Bilinear controllability on a rectangle

Let us now discuss the specific case
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\Omega)
, where

\Omega=(0,a)\times(0,b),

with
\displaystyle a,b>0
such that
\displaystyle a^{2}/b^{2}
is an algebraic irrational number. In this case, we consider
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
to be the Bi-Laplace operator
\displaystyle\Delta^{2}
with hinged boundary conditions, with domain

D(\Delta^{2}):=\{\psi\in H^{4}(\Omega):\;\psi,\Delta\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\}.

Let
\displaystyle Q^{1}\in L^{\infty}(0,a)
,
\displaystyle Q^{2}\in L^{\infty}(0,b)
such that, for every
\displaystyle\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, there exist
\displaystyle C>0
and
\displaystyle p_{1},p_{2}>0
such that for any
\displaystyle k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}

(5.3)

\begin{split}\big|\int_{0}^{a}\sin\big(\tfrac{k}{a}\pi x\big)Q_{1}(x)\sin\big(\tfrac{\ell}{a}\pi x\big)dx\big|\geqslant\frac{C}{k^{p_{1}}},\\
\big|\int_{0}^{b}\sin\big(\tfrac{k}{b}\pi y\big)Q^{2}(y)\sin\big(\tfrac{\ell}{b}\pi y\big)dy\big|\geqslant\frac{C}{k^{p_{2}}}.\end{split}

We define
\displaystyle Q(x,y)=Q^{1}(x)Q^{2}(y)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and
\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}
as the multiplication operator

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathfrak{B}:L^{2}(\Omega)&\longrightarrow&L^{2}(\Omega)\\
\psi&\longmapsto&Q\psi,\end{array}

In this case, System (
1.17
) reads

(5.4)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\psi+\Delta^{2}\psi+vQ\psi=0,&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\psi_{|_{\partial\Omega}}=\Delta\psi_{|_{\partial\Omega}}=0,&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega\\
\psi_{|_{t=0}}=\psi_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega),&\text{ in }\Omega,\end{cases}

with
\displaystyle v\in L^{2}(0,T)
. In this framework, the ordered eigenvalues
\displaystyle\big(\mu_{k}\big)_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
are defined by two sequences of numbers
\displaystyle(\ell_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}},(m_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
such that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu_{k}=\pi^{4}\Big(\frac{\ell_{k}^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{m_{k}^{2}}{b^{2}}\Big)^{2},

with corresponding eigenfunctions
\displaystyle\big(\phi_{k}\big)_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
given by

\phi_{k}(x,y)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{a\,b}}\sin\Big(\frac{\ell_{k}}{a}\pi x\Big)\sin\Big(\frac{m_{k}}{b}\pi y\Big).

The suitable choice of
\displaystyle a
and
\displaystyle b
so that
\displaystyle a^{2}/b^{2}
is an algebraic irrational number allows to ensure the exact controllability of Theorem
1.8
for (
5.4
).

Remark 5.3
.

In the case where
\displaystyle\Omega=(0,1)\times\big(0,\sqrt[3]{2}\big)
, and
\displaystyle Q^{1}(x)=x^{2}
,
\displaystyle Q^{2}(y)=y^{2},
our hypotheses are verified. Indeed, the number
\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{2}{3}}
is algebraic and irrational. In addition, for every
\displaystyle\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
and
\displaystyle k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}\setminus\{\ell\}
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big|\int_{0}^{1}{t^{2}}\sin({k}\pi t)\sin({\ell}\pi t)dt\big|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big|\int_{0}^{2^{\frac{1}{3}}}t^{2}\sin\Big(2^{-\frac{1}{3}}k\pi t\Big)\sin\Big(2^{-\frac{1}{3}}\ell\pi t\Big)dt\big|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{4k\,\ell}{(k^{2}-m^{2})^{2}\pi^{2}}\geqslant\frac{C_{\ell}}{k^{3}}.

When
\displaystyle k=\ell
, we compute

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big|{t^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\sin^{2}({\ell}\pi)dt\big|=\frac{1}{2}\big|\int_{0}^{2^{\frac{1}{3}}}t^{2}\sin^{2}\Big(\frac{\ell}{2^{\frac{1}{3}}}\pi t\Big)dt\big|=\frac{\left|2\ell^{2}\pi^{2}-3\right|}{12\ell^{2}\pi^{2}}>0.

Similar results can be obtained for other polynomial controls
\displaystyle Q^{1}
and
\displaystyle Q^{2}
of different orders.

Theorem 5.4
.

System (
5.4
) is small-time locally exactly controllable to eigensolutions.

Proof.

The result is a direct application of Theorem
1.8
to this setting. Indeed, the eigenvalues of the Dirichlet Bi-Laplace operator on a rectangle satisfy (
1.2
), (
1.3
), and (
1.4
) with
\displaystyle a=\tfrac{1}{2}
and
\displaystyle b=\tfrac{1}{4}
(similarly to the square case described in Section
1.1.5
).
The validity of (
1.5
), is ensured as follows. First, Roth’s Theorem (see
[
31
]
) implies that, when
\displaystyle z
is an algebraic irrational number, we have that, for every
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
, there exists
\displaystyle C_{\epsilon}>0
small enough such that

\Big|z-\frac{n}{m}\Big|\geqslant\frac{C_{\epsilon}}{m^{2+\epsilon}},\quad\forall m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}.

Recall (
5.5
). Now,
\displaystyle a^{2}/b^{2}
is an algebraic irrational number and for any
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
, there exists
\displaystyle C_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\begin{split}|\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{k}|&=\Big|\Big(\frac{\ell_{k+1}^{2}\pi^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{m_{k+1}^{2}\pi^{2}}{b^{2}}\Big)^{2}-\Big(\frac{\ell_{k}^{2}\pi^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{m_{k}^{2}\pi^{2}}{b^{2}}\Big)^{2}\Big|\\
&=\pi^{2}\Big|\Big(\frac{\ell_{k+1}^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{m_{k+1}^{2}}{b^{2}}\Big)-\Big(\frac{\ell_{k}^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{m_{k}^{2}}{b^{2}}\Big)\Big|\times\big|\sqrt{\mu_{k+1}}+\sqrt{\mu_{k}}\big|\\
&=\frac{\pi^{2}|m_{k}^{2}-m_{k+1}^{2}|}{a^{2}}\Big|\frac{\ell_{k+1}^{2}-\ell_{k}^{2}}{m_{k}^{2}-m_{k+1}^{2}}-\frac{a^{2}}{b^{2}}\Big|\times\big|\sqrt{\mu_{k+1}}+\sqrt{\mu_{k}}\big|\\
&\geqslant 2\sqrt{\mu_{k+1}}\frac{\pi^{2}|m_{k}^{2}-m_{k+1}^{2}|C_{\epsilon}}{a^{2}|{m_{k}^{2}-m_{k+1}^{2}}|^{2+\epsilon}}\geqslant 2\sqrt{\mu_{k+1}}\frac{\pi^{2}C_{\epsilon}}{a^{2}\max(m_{k},m_{k+1})^{2+2\epsilon}}\\
&\geqslant 2\mu_{k+1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi^{4+2\epsilon}C_{\epsilon}}{a^{2}b^{2+2\epsilon}\mu_{k+1}^{\frac{1+\epsilon}{2}}}=\frac{2\pi^{4+2\epsilon}C_{\epsilon}}{a^{2}b^{2+2\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\mu_{k+1}^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}}.\end{split}

The Weyl asymptotics ensures the validity of (
1.5
). Finally, we can apply Theorem
1.8
, since (
1.16
) is ensured by the identities (
5.3
).

∎

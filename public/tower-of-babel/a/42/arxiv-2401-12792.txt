The Alekseev-Meinrenken diffeomorphism arising from the Stokes phenomenon
Xiaomeng Xu

Abstract
The Alekseev-Meinrenken diffeomorphism is a distinguished diffeomorphism from the space of n × n n\times n Hermitian matrices to the space of n × n n\times n positive definite Hermitian matrices. This paper derives the explicit expression of the diffeomorphism, via the Stokes phenomenon of meromorphic linear systems of ordinary differential equations with Poncaré rank 1 1 .

Introduction and the main result

Let us consider the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{u}}(n)
of the unitary group
{\rm U}(n)
. The Ginzburg-Weinstein linearization theorem
[
13
]
states that the dual Poisson Lie group
{\rm U}(n)^{*}
, of the standard Poisson Lie group
{\rm U}(n)
(see e.g.
[
19

), is Poisson isomorphic to the dual of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}(n)^{*}
.
There are many proofs of Ginzburg-Weinstein linearization theorem, from the different perspectives of the cohomology calculation, Moser’s trick in symplectic geometry, Stokes phenomenon, the quantum algebras and so on, see e.g.,
[

It is natural to ask if these seemingly rather different methods are related to each other in some ways. We have been working on this problem with the belief that the connection between the different methods will bring new insights into both subjects.

In this paper, we make one step further to build a precise connection between the Alekseev-Meinrenken construction and the Boalch construction.

First, let us recall the Alekseev-Meinrenken construction.
Let us identify the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{u}}(n)
, consisting of skew-Hermitian matrices, with the space
{\rm Herm}(n)
of
n\times n
Hermitian matrices via the pairing
\langle A,\xi\rangle=2{\rm Im}({\rm tr}A\xi)
. Thus
{\rm Herm}(n)
inherits a Poisson structure from the canonical linear (Kostant-Kirillov-Souriau) Poisson structure on
{\mathfrak{u}}(n)^{*}
. Furthermore, the dual Poisson Lie group
{\rm U}(n)^{*}
, which is the group of complex
upper triangular matrices with strictly positive diagonal entries,
is identified with the space
{\rm Herm}^{+}(n)
of positive definite Hermitian
n\times n
-matrices, by
taking the upper triangular matrix
X\in{\rm U}(n)^{*}
to the positive Hermitian matrix
(X^{*}X)^{1/2}\in{\rm Herm}^{+}(n)
. The Poisson manifolds
{\mathfrak{u}}(n)^{*}\cong{\rm Herm}(n)
and
{\rm U}(n)^{*}
carry extra structures: Guillemin-Sternberg
[
14
]
introduced the Gelfand–Tsetlin integrable system on
\mathfrak{u}(n)^{*}
; Flaschka-Ratiu
[
12
]
described a multiplicative Gelfand-Tsetlin system for the dual Poisson Lie group
{\rm U}(n)^{*}
.
Later on in
[
2
]
, Alekseev and Meinrenken used these integrable systems to construct a distinguished Ginzburg-Weinstein linearization
\Gamma_{AM}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
2
]

There exists a unique diffeomorphism

\Gamma_{AM}\colon{\rm Herm}(n)\cong\mathfrak{u}(n)^{*}\rightarrow{\rm Herm}^{+}(n)\cong{\rm U}(n)^{*},

which intertwines the Gelfand-Tsetlin systems on both sides (and has one extra property, see Section
3.3
). In particular, the map
\Gamma_{AM}
is a Poisson isomorphism.

Second, let us recall the Boalch construction.
Let
\mathfrak{h}(\mathbb{R})
(resp.
\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}(\mathbb{R})
) denote the set of diagonal matrices with (resp. distinct) real eigenvalues. Let us consider the meromorphic linear system for a function
F(z)\in{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,

\displaystyle\frac{dF}{dz}=\left({\mathrm{i}}u-\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{A}{z}\right)\cdot F,

(1)

where
{\mathrm{i}}=\sqrt{-1}
,
u\in\mathfrak{h}(\mathbb{R})
and
A\in{{\rm Herm}}(n)
. The system has an order two pole at
\infty
and (if
A\neq 0
) a first order pole at
0
. It has a unique formal solution
\hat{F}(z)
around

z=\infty
. Then the standard theory of resummation states that there exist certain sectorial regions around
z=\infty
, such that on each of these sectors there is a unique (therefore canonical) holomorphic solution with the prescribed asymptotics
\hat{F}(z)
. These solutions are in general different (that reflects the Stokes phenomenon), and the transition between them can be measured by a pair of Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}(u,A)\in{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. The Stokes matrices
S_{+}
and
S_{-}
are upper and lower triangular ones, and, due to the real condition
u\in\mathfrak{h}(\mathbb{R})
and
A\in{\rm Herm}(n)
, are complex conjugate to each other. See Section
2
for more details. Then the remarkable theorem of Boalch states that

Theorem 1.2
.

[
7
]

For any fixed
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}(\mathbb{R})
, the Riemann-Hilbert map

\displaystyle\nu(u)\colon{\rm Herm}(n)\cong\mathfrak{u}(n)^{*}\rightarrow{\rm Herm}^{+}(n)\cong{\rm U}(n)^{*};\ A\mapsto\nu(u,A):=S_{-}(u,A)S_{+}(u,A),

(2)

is a Poisson isomorphism (here since the Stokes matrices satisfy
S_{-}(u,A)=S_{+}(u,A)^{\dagger}
, the product
S_{-}S_{+}
is a positive definite Hermitian matrix).

In order to understand the relation between the Poisson diffeomorphisms
\Gamma_{AM}
and
\nu(u)
, first note that
\Gamma_{AM}
is a diffeomorphism compatible with the Gelfand-Tsetlin chain of subalebras

\mathfrak{u}(1)\subset\mathfrak{u}(2)\subset\cdots\subset\mathfrak{u}(n),

(3)

where
\mathfrak{u}(k)\subset\mathfrak{u}(k+1)
is the upper left corner, see Section
3
for more details. While
\nu(u)
is a family of diffeomorphisms depending on extra parameter space
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore, to relate
\nu(u)
to
\Gamma_{AM}
, we need to break the symmetry of
\nu(u)
, by choosing specific
u
such that the particular chain structure (
3
) can come out. The naive observation is that if
u_{n}\gg u_{n-1}\gg\cdots\gg u_{1}
in
u={\rm diag}(u_{1},...,u_{n})
, the
n\times n
linear system (
1
) seems to be decoupled into
n
systems of rank
n,n-1,n-2,...,1
, according to the chain (
3
). In
[
29
]
, we made this naive observation a precise statement about the regularized limit of the Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}(u,A)
as
u_{n}\gg u_{n-1}\gg\cdots\gg u_{1}
. The statement relies on the study of the nonlinear isomonodromy deformation equations of the linear system (
1
).

In this paper, motivated by the analysis results in
[
29
]
, we construct a family of explicit Poisson diffeomorphisms
\psi(u):{\rm Herm}(n)\rightarrow{\rm Herm}(n)
parameterized by
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}(\mathbb{R})
, via the Thimm action in the Gelfand-Tsetlin system. See Section
3.4
for the explicit expression of
\psi(u)
. Then we show that the diffeomorphisms are such that the composed map
\Gamma_{AM}\circ\psi(u):{\rm Herm}(n)\rightarrow{\rm Herm}^{+}(n)
is the first order approximation of the Riemann-Hilbert map
\nu(u)
as
u_{n}\gg u_{n-1}\gg\cdots\gg u_{1}
. That is

Theorem 1.3
.

For any fixed
A\in{\rm Herm}(n)
, the Hermitian matrix function

\Gamma_{AM}\left(\psi(u,A)\right)=\nu(u,A)+\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{u_{k}-u_{k-1}}{u_{k+1}-u_{k}}\right),

(4)

as
\frac{u_{k+1}-u_{k}}{u_{k}-u_{k-1}}\rightarrow+\infty
for all
k=2,...,n-1
. Here we use the big
\mathcal{O}
notation: each
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{u_{k}-u_{k-1}}{u_{k+1}-u_{k}}\right)
stands for a remainder whose norm is less than
M\times\left(\frac{u_{k+1}-u_{k}}{u_{k}-u_{k-1}}\right)^{-1}
for a positive real number
M
as
\frac{u_{k+1}-u_{k}}{u_{k}-u_{k-1}}
big enough.

As an application of Theorem
1.3
, we find the explicit formula of
\Gamma_{AM}
. See Section
3.3
for more details. We denote by
\lambda^{(k)}_{1}\geq\lambda^{(k)}_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{(k)}_{k}
the ordered eigenvalues of the left-top
k\times k
submatrix of
A
, and denote by
A_{n}={\rm diag}(\lambda^{(n)}_{1},...,\lambda^{(n)}_{n})
.

Theorem 1.4
.

The Alekseev-Meinrenken diffeomorphism is given by

\Gamma_{AM}\colon{\rm Herm}(n)\rightarrow{\rm Herm}^{+}(n)~;~A\mapsto\psi(A)e^{A_{n}}\psi(A)^{-1}

where
\psi
is the pointwise multiplication
\psi(A)=\psi^{(1)}(A)\cdot\cdot\cdot\psi^{(n-1)}(A)\psi^{(n)}(A)
of unitary matrices
\psi^{(k)}(A)
, and each map
\psi^{(k)}:{\rm Herm}(n)\rightarrow{\rm U}(n)
is given by

\displaystyle\psi^{(k)}(A)_{ij}=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\lambda^{(k-1)}_{i}-\lambda^{(k)}_{j}}{4}}\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{v=1,v\neq i}^{k-1}{\rm sinh}\Big(\frac{\lambda^{(k-1)}_{v}-\lambda^{(k)}_{j}}{2}\Big)\prod_{v=1,v\neq j}^{k}{\rm sinh}\Big(\frac{\lambda^{(k-1)}_{i}-\lambda^{(k)}_{v}}{2}\Big)}{\prod_{v=1,v\neq j}^{k}{\rm sinh}\Big(\frac{\lambda^{(k)}_{v}-\lambda^{(k)}_{j}}{2}\Big)\prod_{v=1,v\neq i}^{k-1}{\rm sinh}\Big(\frac{\lambda^{(k-1)}_{i}-\lambda^{(k-1)}_{v}}{2}\Big)}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{(-1)^{k-1+i}\Delta^{1,...,k-1}_{1,...,k-2,k}\left(A-\lambda^{(k-1)}_{i}\right)}{\sqrt{-{\prod_{l=1}^{k}(\lambda^{(k-1)}_{i}-\lambda^{(k)}_{l})\prod_{l=1}^{k-2}(\lambda^{(k-1)}_{i}-\lambda^{(k-2)}_{l})}}},

for
1\leq i\leq k-1,1\leq j\leq k
, and

\displaystyle\psi^{(k)}(A)_{kj}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\lambda^{(k)}_{j}-\lambda^{(k-1)}_{k}}{4}}\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{v=1}^{k-1}{\rm sinh}\Big(\frac{\lambda^{(k-1)}_{v}-\lambda^{(k)}_{j}}{2}\Big)}{\prod_{v=1,v\neq j}^{k}{\rm sinh}\Big(\frac{\lambda^{(k)}_{v}-\lambda^{(k)}_{j}}{2}\Big)}},\ \ \ \text{ for }\ 1\leq j\leq k,

\displaystyle\psi^{(k)}(A)_{ii}

\displaystyle=1,\ \ \ \ \ \ \text{for}\ k<i\leq n,\ \ \ \ \text{ and }\psi^{(k)}(A)_{ij}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \text{otherwise}.

Here
\Delta^{1,...,k-2,k-1}_{1,...,k-2,k}(A-{\lambda^{(k-1)}_{i}})
is the
(k-1)\times(k-1)
minor of the matrix
A-{\lambda^{(k-1)}_{i}}\cdot{\rm Id}_{n}
formed by the first
k-1
rows and
1,...,k-2,k
columns (
{\rm Id}_{n}
is the rank
n
identity matrix).

The left hand side of the identity (
4
) is characterized by the algebraic integrable system, while the right hand side is highly transcendental. Therefore, Theorem
1.3
provides us the possibility to study the Stokes phenomenon, as well as the associated isomonodromy deformation equation, via algebraic methods. For example, in the above discussion, we have chosen a particular Gelfand-Tsetlin chain of subalgebras. Instead, we can take another chain, the chain of subalgebras
\mathfrak{u}(1)\subset\mathfrak{u}(2)\subset\cdots\subset\mathfrak{u}(n),

where
\mathfrak{u}(k)\subset\mathfrak{u}(k+1)
is the lower right (instead of upper left) corner. Accordingly, we can define Alekseev-Meinrenken diffeomorphism from
{\rm Herm}(n)
to
{\rm Herm}^{+}(n)
compatible with the corresponding Gelfand-Tsetlin systems. Then such diffeomorphism is related to the Riemann-Hilbert map
\nu(u)
as
u_{1}\gg u_{2}\gg\cdots\gg u_{1}
in the same way as Theorem
1.3
. The comparison of the results, associated to the two different choices of Gelfand-Tsetlin chains, can be used to characterize the connection formula of the nonlinear isomonodromy deformation equations between the two different asymptotic zones
\frac{u_{k+1}-u_{k}}{u_{k}-u_{k-1}}\rightarrow\infty
for
k=2,...,n-1
and
\frac{u_{k+1}-u_{k}}{u_{k}-u_{k-1}}\rightarrow 0
for
k=2,...,n-1
. See
[
28
]
for more details.

Another application of Theorem
1.4
is that it enables us to write down an explicit Moser flow of the Hamiltonian vector fields on the coadjoint orbit of
\mathfrak{u}(n)^{*}
constructed by Alekseev in
[
1
]
. Therefore, it will relate Alekseev’s construction using Moser’s trick
[
1
]
to the other constructions of Ginzburg-Weinstein linearization, and thus bring more insights into different subjects.
We also remark that it is interesting to study the behaviour of the expression in Theorem
1.4
under the toric degeneration of Gelfand-Tsetlin systems, see e.g.,
[
22
]
. We leave the study of the relations between the Stokes phenomenon, Moser flow and toric degeneration to a future work.

Canonical solutions

Definition 2.1

The

{\rm Sect}_{+}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}~|~-\pi<{\rm arg}(z)<\pi\}

Theorem 2.2

For any

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}F_{+}(z;u)\cdot e^{-{\mathrm{i}}uz}\cdot z^{\frac{[A]}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}}

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}F_{-}(z;u)\cdot e^{-{\mathrm{i}}uz}\cdot z^{\frac{[A]}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}}

{\rm Id}_{n}

2.2
Stokes matrices

For any
\sigma\in S_{n}
, let us denote by
U_{\sigma}
the component
\{(u_{1},...,u_{n})~|~u_{\sigma(1)}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<u_{\sigma(n)}\}

\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})(\mathbb{R})
, and denote by
P_{\sigma}\in{\rm GL}_{n}
the corresponding permutation matrix.

Definition 2.3
.

For any
u\in U_{\sigma}
, the
Stokes matrices
of the system (
1
) (with respect
to
{\rm Sect}_{+}
and the chosen branch of
{\rm log}(z)
) are the elements
S_{\pm}(u,A)\in{\rm GL}(n)
determined by

F_{+}(z)=F_{-}(z)\cdot e^{-\frac{[A]}{2}}P_{\sigma}S_{+}(u,A)P_{\sigma}^{-1},\ \ \ \ \ F_{-}(ze^{-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}})=F_{+}(z)\cdot P_{\sigma}S_{-}(u,A)P_{\sigma}^{-1}e^{\frac{[A]}{2}},

where the first (resp. second) identity is understood to hold in
{\rm Sect}_{-}

(resp.
{\rm Sect}_{+}
) after
F_{+}
(resp.
F_{-}
)
has been analytically continued anticlockwise around
z=\infty
.

The prescribed asymptotics of
F_{\pm}(z)
at
z=\infty

2.3
, ensures that the Stokes matrices
S_{+}(u,A)
and
S_{-}(u,A)
are upper and lower triangular matrices respectively. see e.g.,
[
25

F(z)
is a solution, so is

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
S_{+}(u,A)^{\dagger}
denote the conjugation transpose of
S_{+}(u,A)
, then
S_{-}(u,A)=S_{+}(u,A)^{\dagger}
.

Since the system (
1

\frac{A}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}
for

[

Lemma 2.5
.

There is a unique holomorphic fundamental solution

F_{0}(z;u,A)\in{\rm GL}(n)

, such that
F_{0}\cdot z^{\frac{A}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}}\rightarrow{\rm Id}_{n}

.

The

C(u,A)\in{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})

{\rm Sect}_{+}

F_{0}(z;u,A)=F_{+}(z;u,A)\cdot C(u,A),

(5)

2.3
Isomonodromy deformation

In this subsection, we recall some facts about the theory of isomonodromy deformation. In general, the Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}(u,A)
of the system (
1
) will depend on the irregular term
u
.
The isomonodromy deformation (also known as monodromy preserving) problem is to find the matrix valued function
\Phi(u)
such that the Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}(u,\Phi(u))
are (locally) constant. In particular, the following definition and proposition are known. See more detailed discussions in e.g.,
[
16
,
17
,
10
,
8
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

The isomonodromy equation is the differential equation for a matrix valued function
\Phi(u):\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow{\rm Herm}(n)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial u_{k}}=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}[\Phi,{\rm ad}^{-1}_{u}{\rm ad}_{E_{k}}\Phi],\ \text{for all}\ k=1,...,n.

(7)

Here
E_{k}
is the
n\times n
diagonal matrix whose
(k,k)
-entry is
1
and other entries are
0
.
Note that
{\rm ad}_{E_{k}}\Phi
takes values in the space
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}^{od}
of off diagonal matrices and that
{\rm ad}_{u}

is invertible when restricted to
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}^{od}
.

Set
\Phi(u)=(\phi_{ij}(u))
, then in terms of the components, the equation (
7
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}}\phi_{ij}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\left(\frac{1}{u_{k}-u_{j}}-\frac{1}{u_{k}-u_{i}}\right)\phi_{ik}(u)\phi_{kj}(u),\quad i,j\neq k,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}}\phi_{ik}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\sum_{j\neq k}\frac{\phi_{ij}(u)\phi_{jk}(u)-\delta_{ij}\phi_{kk}(u)\phi_{jk}(u)}{u_{k}-u_{j}},\quad i\neq k,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}}\phi_{kj}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\sum_{i\neq k}\frac{\delta_{ij}\phi_{kk}(u)\phi_{ki}(u)-\phi_{ki}(u)\phi_{ij}(u)}{u_{k}-u_{i}},\quad j\neq k,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}}\phi_{kk}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Proposition 2.8
.

For any solution
\Phi(u)
of the isomonodromy equation, the Stokes matrices
S_{\pm}(u,\Phi(u))

are locally constants (independent of
u
).

Remark 2.9
.

Following Miwa
[
21
]
, the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-valued solutions
\Phi(u)
of the equation (
7
) with
u_{1},...,u_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
have the strong Painlevé property: they are multi-valued meromorphic functions of
u_{1},...,u_{n}
and the branching occurs when
u
moves along a loop around the fat diagonal

\Delta=\{(u_{1},...,u_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}~|~u_{i}=u_{j},\text{for some }i\neq j\}.

Then, according to Boalch
[
7
]
, when restricts to the real case, the
{\rm Herm}(n)
-valued solutions
\Phi(u)
of (
7
) are real analytic on each connected component of
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}(\mathbb{R})
.

2.4
The boundary value and explicit Stokes matrices

Now let us consider the
n\times n
system of partial differential equations for a function
F(z,u)\in GL(n)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\mathrm{i}}u-\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{\Phi(u)}{z}\right)\cdot F,

(8)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\mathrm{i}}E_{k}z-\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}{\rm ad}^{-1}_{u}{\rm ad}_{E_{k}}\Phi(u)\right)\cdot F,\ \text{for all}\ k=1,...,n.

(9)

where the residue
\Phi(u)\in{\rm Herm}(n)
is a solution of the isomonodromy equation (
7
). One checks that (
7
) is the compatibility condition of the above PDE system. The boundary value and monodromy problem of the system were studied in
[
28
]
.

Theorem 2.10
.

[
28
, Theorem 1.1]

For any
{\rm Herm}(n)
-valued solution
\Phi(u)
of the isomonodromy equation (
7
) on the connected component
U_{\rm id}:=\{u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}(\mathbb{R})~|~u_{1}<\cdots<u_{n}\}
, there exists a unique constant
\Phi_{0}\in{\rm Herm}(n)
such that as the real numbers
\frac{u_{k+1}-u_{k}}{u_{k}-u_{k-1}}\rightarrow+\infty
for all
k=2,...,n-1
,

\displaystyle\Phi(u)=

\displaystyle{\rm Ad}{\left((u_{2}-u_{1})^{\frac{\delta_{1}(\Phi_{0})}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}}\cdot\overrightarrow{\underset{k=2,...,n-1}{\prod}}\left(\frac{u_{k+1}-u_{k}}{u_{k}-u_{k-1}}\right)^{\frac{\delta_{k}(\Phi_{0})}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}}\right)}\Phi_{0}+O\left(\left(\frac{u_{k+1}-u_{k}}{u_{k}-u_{k-1}}\right)^{-1};k=2,...,n-1\right),

(10)

where
{\rm Ad}(g)X=gXg^{-1}

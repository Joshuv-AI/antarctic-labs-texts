An unbounded approach to the theory of kernel functions
Michio SETO

Abstract
In this paper, we give a new approach to the theory of kernel functions. Our method is based on the structure of Fock spaces. As its applications, various examples of strictly positive kernel functions are given. Moreover, we give a new proof of the universal approximation theorem for the Gaussian kernel.

1
Introduction

Let
X
be a set, and
let
k
be a complex-valued function on
X\times X
.
Then,
k
is called a kernel function if
k
is self-adjoint, that is,
k(y,x)=\overline{k(x,y)}
, and

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}c_{i}\overline{c_{j}}k(x_{i},x_{j})\geq 0

for any
n\geq 1
, any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
and any
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
In this paper, kernel functions will be called kernels for short.
Moreover, a kernel
k
is said to be strictly positive if

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}c_{i}\overline{c_{j}}k(x_{i},x_{j})>0

for any
n\geq 1
, any
n
distinct points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
and any
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
.
Strictly positive kernels have received attention not only in functional analysis (see Subsection 3.2 in Paulsen-Raghupathi
[
7
]
)
but also in numerical analysis (see Micchelli
[
5
]
)
and machine learning (see Guella
[
1
]
).

Now, the purpose of this paper is to introduce a new method for dealing with strictly positive kernels.
Our method is based on the structure of Fock spaces induced by kernels.
We will apply the tensor algebra structure of the full Fock space
to the theory of kernels. This is the main idea of this paper.
As consequences, we obtain not only various examples of strictly positive kernels (Theorem
3.5
and examples in Section 4),
but also a new proof of the universal approximation theorem for the Gaussian kernel
(Theorem
5.1
and Corollary
5.2
).

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2,
we introduce huge reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces from power series whose coefficients are strictly positive.
Although all materials of Section 2 are well known to specialists in Hilbert space operator theory,
we will give the details for the sake of general readers.
In Section 3, we investigate relations between
the strict positivity of kernels and reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces constructed in Section 2.
In Section 4, various examples of strictly positive kernels are given as applications of results obtained in Section 3.
In Section 5, we prove the universal approximation theorem for the Gaussian kernel with our method.

2
Preliminaries

Let
k
be a kernel, and
let
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
be the reproducing kernel Hilbert space generated by
k
.
We will use the notation
k_{x}(y)=k(y,x)
throughout the whole of this paper.
We fix a sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
such that
a_{n}>0
for any
n\geq 0
and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\|k_{x}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}^{2n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}k(x,x)^{n}<\infty

for any
x
in
X
.
Moreover, we set
\varphi(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
.

In this section, we construct a huge reproducing kernel Hilbert space from
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
and
\varphi
.
The contents of this section are well known to specialists.
For example, see Exercise (k) in p. 320 of Nikolski
[
6
]
,
Chapters 5 and 7 in Paulsen-Raghupathi
[
7
]
and Theorem 8.2 in Saitoh-Sawano
[
8
]
.
However, we give the details for the sake of general readers.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}
denote the reproducing kernel Hilbert space obtained
by the pull-back construction with the
n
-fold tensor product Hilbert space

{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\otimes n}={\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{k}

and the
n
-dimensional diagonal map

\Delta_{n}:X\to X^{n},\ x\to(x,\ldots,x).

More precisely,
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}
is equal to
\{F\circ\Delta_{n}:F\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\otimes n}\}
as vector spaces and
its inner product is defined by

\langle f,g\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}}=\langle P_{(\ker\Delta_{n})^{\perp}}F,P_{(\ker\Delta_{n})^{\perp}}G\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes n}}\quad(f=F\circ\Delta_{n},\ g=G\circ\Delta_{n}),

where we define
\ker\Delta_{n}=\{F\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes n}:F\circ\Delta_{n}=0\}
and

P_{(\ker\Delta_{n})^{\perp}}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto the orthogonal complement of
\ker\Delta_{n}
.
Thus, we identify
\Delta_{n}
with the linear map
F\mapsto F\circ\Delta_{n}
.
Then, it is easy to see that
k_{x}^{\otimes n}
belongs to
(\ker\Delta_{n})^{\perp}
.
Hence, for any function
f=F\circ\Delta_{n}
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle f,k_{x}^{n}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\langle P_{(\ker\Delta_{n})^{\perp}}F,P_{(\ker\Delta_{n})^{\perp}}k_{x}^{\otimes n}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes n}}

\displaystyle=\langle F,k_{x}^{\otimes}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes n}}

\displaystyle=F(x,\ldots,x)

\displaystyle=f(x).

This concludes that
k_{x}^{n}
is the reproducing kernel of
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}

(for the further details of this construction, see Theorems 5.7 and 5.16 in
[
7
]
).

Next,
let
{\mathcal{F}}
denote the Hilbert space with the inner product

\langle(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots)^{\top},(g_{0},g_{1},\ldots)^{\top}\rangle_{{\mathcal{F}}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\langle f_{n},g_{n}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}},

where

f_{n}
and
g_{n}
are functions in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}
, and
we set
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{0}={\mathbb{C}}
.
We consider the map
\Gamma
defined as follows:

\Gamma:\begin{pmatrix}f_{0}\\
f_{1}\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}f_{n}\quad\left(\begin{pmatrix}f_{0}\\
f_{1}\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathcal{F}}\right).

Proposition 2.1
.

\Gamma
is a linear map from
{\mathcal{F}}

to the vector space consisting of functions on
X
.
Moreover,
\ker\Gamma
is closed.

Proof.

For any
F=(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots)^{\top}
in
{\mathcal{F}}
,
we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{m}a_{\ell}f_{\ell}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{m}\left|a_{\ell}f_{\ell}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{m}a_{\ell}\|f_{\ell}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\ell}}\|k_{x}^{\ell}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{m}a_{\ell}\|f_{\ell}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\ell}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{m}a_{\ell}\|k_{x}^{\ell}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\ell}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{m}a_{\ell}\|f_{\ell}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\ell}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{m}a_{\ell}\|k_{x}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}^{2\ell}\right)^{1/2}.

(2.1)

Hence,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}f_{n}(x)
converges. This concludes that

\Gamma
is a linear map from
{\mathcal{F}}

to the vector space consisting of functions on
X
.
Moreover, by (
2
), we have

|(\Gamma F)(x)|\leq\|F\|_{{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}k(x,x)^{n}\right)^{1/2}.

(2.2)

This inequality concludes that
\ker\Gamma
is closed.
∎

By Proposition
2.1
, the pull-back construction can be applied to
\Gamma
.

Definition 2.2
.

We define
\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
as the reproducing kernel Hilbert space obtained by the pull-back construction
with the linear map
\Gamma
, that is,

\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
is equal to the range of
\Gamma
as vector spaces and
its inner product is defined by

\langle f,g\rangle_{\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})}=\langle P_{(\ker\Gamma)^{\perp}}F,P_{(\ker\Gamma)^{\perp}}G\rangle_{{\mathcal{F}}}\quad(f=\Gamma F,\ g=\Gamma G,\ F,G\in{\mathcal{F}}).

We summarize basic properties of
\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
.

Proposition 2.3
.

\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space consisting of functions on
X
.
More precisely, for any
f
in
\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
,
there exists a vector
(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,)^{\top}
in
{\mathcal{F}}

such that

f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}f_{n}.

Moreover,
the reproducing kernel of
\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
is

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}k_{x}^{n}=\varphi(k_{x}),

that is,

f(x)=\langle f,\varphi(k_{x})\rangle_{\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})}

for any
f
in
\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
and any
x
in
X
.

Proof.

We shall show that
\varphi(k_{x})
is the reproducing kernel of
\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
.
If
(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},\ldots)^{\top}
is a vector in
\ker\Gamma
, then

\langle(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},\ldots)^{\top},(1,k_{x},k_{x}^{2},\ldots)^{\top}\rangle_{{\mathcal{F}}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\langle h_{n},k_{x}^{n}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}h_{n}(x)=0.

Hence,
(1,k_{x},k_{x}^{2},\ldots)^{\top}
belongs to
(\ker\Gamma)^{\perp}
.
Next, for any function
f=\Gamma(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\ldots)^{\top}
in
\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
, we have

\displaystyle\langle f,\varphi(k_{x})\rangle_{\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})}

\displaystyle=\langle P_{(\ker\Gamma)^{\perp}}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\ldots)^{\top},P_{(\ker\Gamma)^{\perp}}(1,k_{x},k_{x}^{2},\ldots)^{\top}\rangle_{{\mathcal{F}}}

\displaystyle=\langle(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\ldots)^{\top},(1,k_{x},k_{x}^{2},\ldots)^{\top}\rangle_{{\mathcal{F}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\langle f_{n},k_{x}^{n}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}f_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=f(x).

This concludes the proof.
∎

3
Strictly positive kernels

In this section, we will investigate relations between the strict positivity of
\varphi(k)
and the structure of
\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
.
Some of results obtained in Kuwahara-S
[
4
]
are generalized into the setting of this paper.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\psi
be a function in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
. Then,
the multiplication operator
M_{\psi}
with symbol
\psi

is a densely defined closable linear operator in
\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
.
In particular,
the adjoint operator
M_{\psi}^{\ast}
is a densely defined closed linear operator in
\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
, and
every
\varphi(k_{x})
is an eigenfunction of
M_{\psi}^{\ast}
.
More precisely,

M_{\psi}^{\ast}\varphi(k_{x})=\overline{\psi(x)}\varphi(k_{x}).

Proof.

We define the bounded linear operator
L_{\psi}
as follows:

L_{\psi}:{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\otimes n}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\otimes n+1},\quad F\mapsto\psi\otimes F.

Then, the following diagram commutes:

\begin{CD}{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\otimes n}@>{L_{\psi}}>{}>{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\otimes n+1}\\
@V{\Delta_{n}}V{}V@V{}V{\Delta_{n+1}}V\\
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}@>{}>{M_{\psi}|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}}}>{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n+1},\end{CD}

where
\Delta_{n}
is identified with the linear map
F\mapsto F\circ\Delta_{n}
.
Hence,
for any function
f_{n}
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}
,

\psi f_{n}
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n+1}
.
Let
F=(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{N},0,\ldots)^{\top}
be a vector with finite support in
{\mathcal{F}}
.
We set
f=\Gamma F
.
Then,

\psi f=\psi\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_{n}f_{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_{n}\psi f_{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_{n+1}\dfrac{a_{n}}{a_{n+1}}\psi f_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{N+1}a_{n}\dfrac{a_{n-1}}{a_{n}}\psi f_{n-1},

where we note that
\psi f_{n-1}
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}
.
Hence, setting

G=\left(0,\dfrac{a_{0}}{a_{1}}\psi f_{0},\dfrac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}\psi f_{1},\ldots,\dfrac{a_{N}}{a_{N+1}}\psi f_{N},0,\ldots\right)^{\top},

G
belongs to
{\mathcal{F}}
and
\Gamma G=\psi f
, that is,
\psi f
belongs to
\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
.
Therefore,
M_{\psi}
is a densely defined linear operator in
\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
.
Moreover,
it is easy to see that
M_{\psi}
is closable and
M_{\psi}^{\ast}\varphi(k_{x})=\overline{\psi(x)}\varphi(k_{x})
.
∎

We extract the next definition and theorem from the proof of the main theorem in
[
4
]

where exponentials of de Branges-Rovnyak kernels were discussed.

Definition 3.2

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space on a set
X
.
Then,
X
is said to be finitely separated by
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
if,
for any positive integer
n
and
for any
n
distinct points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
in
X
,
there exists a function
\psi
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
such that

\psi(x_{i})\neq\psi(x_{j})
whenever
i\neq j
.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space on a set
X
.
If
X
is finitely separated by
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
, then
\varphi(k)
is strictly positive definite.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

\{\varphi(k_{x})\}_{j=1}^{n}
is linearly independent
for any
n
in
\mathbb{N}
and any
n
distinct points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
in
X
.
Suppose that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\varphi(k_{x_{j}})=0

for some
n
in
\mathbb{N}
, some
n
distinct points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
in
X
,
and some
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
in
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Then, for any function
\psi
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
,
by Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1\\
\overline{\psi(x_{1})}&\cdots&\overline{\psi(x_{n})}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\overline{\psi(x_{1})}^{n-1}&\cdots&\overline{\psi(x_{n})}^{n-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}\varphi(k_{x_{1}})\\
c_{2}\varphi(k_{x_{2}})\\
\vdots\\
c_{n}\varphi(k_{x_{n}})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\varphi(k_{x_{j}})\\
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\overline{\psi(x_{j})}c_{j}\varphi(k_{x_{j}})\\
\vdots\\
\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\overline{\psi(x_{j})}^{n-1}\varphi(k_{x_{j}})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\varphi(k_{x_{j}})\\
M_{\psi}^{\ast}\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\varphi(k_{x_{j}})\\
\vdots\\
(M_{\psi}^{\ast})^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\varphi(k_{x_{j}})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0}.

However, by the assumption, there exists a function
\psi
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
such that

\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(\psi(x_{i})-\psi(x_{j}))\neq 0.

Then, the Vandermonde matrix

\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1\\
\overline{\psi(x_{1})}&\cdots&\overline{\psi(x_{n})}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\overline{\psi(x_{1})}^{n-1}&\cdots&\overline{\psi(x_{n})}^{n-1}\end{pmatrix}

is nonsingular.
Therefore, we have that

\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}\varphi(k_{x_{1}})\\
c_{2}\varphi(k_{x_{2}})\\
\vdots\\
c_{n}\varphi(k_{x_{n}})\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf{0}.

This concludes that
c_{1}=\cdots=c_{n}=0
.
Indeed,
if
\varphi(k_{x_{j}})=0
as a function for some
1\leq j\leq n
,
then we would have
f(x_{j})=0
for any
f
in
\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
.
However,
\varphi({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
includes
{\mathbb{C}}
by definition.
Hence,
\varphi(k_{x_{j}})\neq 0
for any
1\leq j\leq n
.
∎

Further, in the case where
\varphi(z)=e^{z}
, we have the following useful results
(cf. Theorem 4.1 in Guella
[
1
]
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space on a set
X
.
If
\exp tk
is strictly positive definite for some
t>0
,
then so is
\exp(-t\|k_{x}-k_{y}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}^{2})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\exp tk
is strictly positive definite.
Then, since

\displaystyle\exp(2t\operatorname{Re}\langle k_{x},k_{y}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}})

\displaystyle=\exp(t\langle k_{x},k_{y}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}})\exp(\overline{t\langle k_{x},k_{y}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}})

\displaystyle=\overline{\exp{tk(x,y)}}\exp tk(x,y),

\exp(2t\operatorname{Re}\langle k_{x},k_{y}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}})
is strictly positive definite
by the Schur product theorem (Theorem 7.5.3 in Horn-Johnson
[
3
]
).
Hence, for any distinct points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
in
X
and any
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
,
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}c_{i}\overline{c_{j}}\exp(-t\|k_{x_{i}}-k_{x_{j}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}c_{i}\overline{c_{j}}e^{-t\|k_{x_{i}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}^{2}}e^{-t\|k_{x_{j}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}^{2}}\exp(2t\operatorname{Re}\langle k_{x_{i}},k_{x_{j}}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}})>0.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\Phi
be any map from a set
X
to a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
.
If
\Phi
is injective, then
\exp t\langle\Phi(y),\Phi(x)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}
is strictly positive definite for any
t>0
.
Moreover, then, so is
\exp(-t\|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2})
.

Proof.

We set
k(x,y)=\langle\Phi(y),\Phi(x)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}
. Then,
k
is a kernel.
First, we shall show that
\exp tk
is strictly positive definite.
By virtue of Theorem
3.3
,
it suffices to show that
X
is finitely separated by
{\mathcal{H}}_{tk}
.
Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
be any distinct points in
X
,
and let
{\mathcal{M}}
be the subspace generated by
\Phi(x_{1}),\ldots,\Phi(x_{n})
.
Since
\Phi
is injective, these are
n
distinct vectors in
{\mathcal{H}}
.
We shall show that there exists some vector
\mathbf{z}
in
{\mathcal{H}}
such that

\langle\Phi(x_{i}),\mathbf{z}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}\neq\langle\Phi(x_{j}),\mathbf{z}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}
whenever
i\neq j
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mathbf{z}
is a vector in
{\mathcal{M}}
.
For any vector
\mathbf{y}
in
{\mathcal{M}}
,
if there existed
1\leq i(\mathbf{y})<j(\mathbf{y})\leq n

such that
\langle\Phi(x_{i(\mathbf{y})}),\mathbf{y}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\langle\Phi(x_{j(\mathbf{y})}),\mathbf{y}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}
,
then we would have

{\mathcal{M}}\subset\bigcup_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\{\Phi(x_{i})-\Phi(x_{j})\}^{\perp},

where the above orthogonal complements are taken in
{\mathcal{M}}
. However,
comparing the volumes of
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\{\Phi(x_{i})-\Phi(x_{j})\}^{\perp}
in
{\mathcal{M}}
, we have a contradiction.
Hence, there exists a vector
\mathbf{z}
in
{\mathcal{M}}
such that

\langle\Phi(x_{i}),\mathbf{z}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}\neq\langle\Phi(x_{j}),\mathbf{z}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}\quad(i\neq j).

Moreover,
setting
\psi(x)=\overline{\langle\Phi(x),\mathbf{z}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}}
and

\mathbf{z}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\Phi(x_{j})
, we have,

\psi(x)=\langle\mathbf{z},\Phi(x)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\langle\Phi(x_{j}),\Phi(x)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}k(x,x_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}k_{x_{j}}(x).

Hence,
\psi
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}_{tk}
.
Thus, we have the first half of the theorem.
Moreover, since

\displaystyle\|k_{x}-k_{y}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle=k(x,x)-2\operatorname{Re}k(x,y)+k(y,y)

\displaystyle=\langle\Phi(x),\Phi(x)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}-2\operatorname{Re}\langle\Phi(x),\Phi(y)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}+\langle\Phi(y),\Phi(y)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle=\|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2},

we have the second half by Lemma
3.4
.
∎

4
Examples

4.1
Gaussian kernel

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be any Hilbert space.
Then, it is well known that

\exp\left(-t\|x-y\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\right)

is strictly positive definite on
{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}
for any
t>0
.
Indeed, this is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.5
.

4.2
Drury-Arveson kernel

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a Hilbert space, and let
{\mathbb{B}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
be the open unit ball in
{\mathcal{H}}
.
Then, it is well known that

\frac{1}{1-\langle x,y\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}}

is strictly positive definite on
{\mathbb{B}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\times{\mathbb{B}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
(see Subsection 7.3.1 in
[
7
]
).
Indeed, consider the case
where
k(x,y)=\langle x,y\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}
and
\varphi(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}
in Theorem
3.3
.

4.3
Pseudo-hyperbolic distance

Let
{\mathbb{D}}
be the open unit disk in the complex plane
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Then, it is well known that
{\mathbb{D}}
is a metric space with the distance function

d(\lambda,\mu)=\left|\frac{\lambda-\mu}{1-\overline{\mu}\lambda}\right|\quad(\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{D}}),

which is called the pseudo-hyperbolic distance.
We shall prove that

K_{t}(\lambda,\mu)=\exp\left(-t\left|\frac{\lambda-\mu}{1-\overline{\mu}\lambda}\right|^{2}\right)

is strictly positive definite on
{\mathbb{D}}^{2}
for any
t>0
.
We set

\psi(\lambda,\mu)=d(\lambda,\mu)^{2}.

Then, trivially,
\psi
is real-valued and symmetric.
Let
H^{2}
denote the Hardy space over
{\mathbb{D}}
,
and let
s_{\lambda}
be the normalized reproducing kernel of
H^{2}
, that is, we set

s_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{\sqrt{1-|\lambda|^{2}}}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}\quad(\lambda\in{\mathbb{D}}).

Then, for any
n
in
{\mathbb{N}}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
in
{\mathbb{D}}
, and
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
in
{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}=0
,
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{c_{i}}c_{j}\psi(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{c_{i}}c_{j}(1-|\langle s_{\lambda_{i}},s_{\lambda_{j}}\rangle_{H^{2}}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{c_{i}}c_{j}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{c_{i}}c_{j}|\langle s_{\lambda_{i}},s_{\lambda_{j}}\rangle_{H^{2}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{c_{i}}c_{j}|\langle s_{\lambda_{i}},s_{\lambda_{j}}\rangle_{H^{2}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{c_{i}}c_{j}\overline{\langle s_{\lambda_{i}},s_{\lambda_{j}}\rangle_{H^{2}}}\langle s_{\lambda_{i}},s_{\lambda_{j}}\rangle_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 0

by the Schur product theorem.
Hence,
\psi
is conditionally negative definite.
In particular,
\exp(-t\psi)
is a kernel for any
t>0
by Schoenberg’s generator theorem (Theorem 9.7 in
[
7
]
).
Moreover, it follows from Proposition 9.3 in
[
7
]
that

\displaystyle k(\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle=-\psi(\lambda,\mu)+\psi(\lambda,0)+\psi(0,\mu)-\psi(0,0)

\displaystyle=-d(\lambda,\mu)^{2}+|\lambda|^{2}+|\mu|^{2}

(4.1)

is a kernel on
{\mathbb{D}}^{2}
.
Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
denote the reproducing kernel Hilbert space generated by this
k
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\|k_{\lambda}-k_{\mu}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(k(\lambda,\lambda)-2k(\lambda,\mu)+k(\mu,\mu))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\{2|\lambda|^{2}-2(-d(\lambda,\mu)^{2}+|\lambda|^{2}+|\mu|^{2})+2|\mu|^{2}\}

\displaystyle=d(\lambda,\mu)^{2}

\displaystyle=\psi(\lambda,\mu).

Hence,
by virtue of Theorem
3.5
,
in order to prove that
K_{t}
is strictly positive definite,
it suffices to show that the map
\Phi:\lambda\mapsto k_{\lambda}
is injective.
However, it is trivial, because

d(\lambda,\mu)^{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\|k_{\lambda}-k_{\mu}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}^{2}.

4.4
Word metric

Let
G
be a free group with the finite generators
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}
.
For any element
g
in
G
,
|g|
will denote the word length of
g
.
Then, it is well known that
d(g,h)=|h^{-1}g|
defines a metric on
G
, which is called the word metric on
G
.
Furthermore, in
[
2
]
, Haagerup showed that

K_{t}(g,h)=\exp(-t|h^{-1}g|)

is positive semi-definite on
G\times G
for any
t>0
.
We shall show that
K_{t}
is strictly positive definite.
Some notations in Lemma 1.2 of
[
2
]
are needed.
We set

\Lambda_{j}=\{(g,h)\in G\times G:g^{-1}h=a_{j}\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Lambda=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}\Lambda_{j}.

Then,
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda}
denotes a Hilbert space
with an orthonormal basis
\{\mathbf{e}_{(g,h)}\}_{(g,h)\in\Lambda}
indexed by
\Lambda
.
Moreover, we set
{\mathbf{e}}_{(g,h)}=-{\mathbf{e}}_{(h,g)}
if
g^{-1}h=a_{j}^{-1}
for some
1\leq j\leq N
.
Let
g
be an element in
G
, and let

g=g_{1}\cdots g_{n}
be the word for
g
.
Then, we set
f_{0}=e
, the unit element of
G
,
and
f_{\ell-1}^{-1}f_{\ell}=g_{\ell}
for
1\leq\ell\leq n
.
Let
\Phi
be the map from
\Lambda
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda}
defined as follows:

\Phi(g)={\mathbf{e}}_{(f_{0},f_{1})}+{\mathbf{e}}_{(f_{1},f_{2})}+\cdots+{\mathbf{e}}_{(f_{n-1},f_{n})}.

Then, it is proved that

\|\Phi(g)-\Phi(h)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda}}^{2}=|h^{-1}g|

(4.2)

(see p. 283 of
[
2
]
).
Hence,
by virtue of Theorem
3.5
,
in order to show that
K_{t}
is strictly positive definite,
it suffices to show that
\Phi:G\to{\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda}
is injective.
However, it is trivial by (
4.2
).

5
Universality

In this section, we deal with
real-valued kernels and real Hilbert spaces.
Let
X
be a locally compact Hausdorff space, and let
k=k(x,y)
be a real-valued continuous kernel on
X\times X
.
Then,
\exp k
is continuous, and so are all functions in
\exp{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
.
Let
K
be a compact subset in
X
,
and let
C(K)
be the Banach algebra consisting of all continuous functions on
K
.
Then it follows from (
2.2
) that

\|f\|_{\infty,K}:=\sup_{x\in K}|f(x)|\leq\|F\|_{{\mathcal{F}}}\sup_{x\in K}\exp\frac{k(x,x)}{2}<\infty.

for every
f
in
\exp{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
denote the subspace of
{\mathcal{F}}
consisting of vectors with finite support.
Then, by Lemma
3.1
,
\Gamma{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
is an algebra.
In fact, the following diagram commutes:

\begin{CD}{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\otimes n}@>{L_{\Psi}}>{}>{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\otimes m+n}\\
@V{\Delta_{n}}V{}V@V{}V{\Delta_{m+n}}V\\
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}@>{}>{M_{\psi}|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{n}}}>{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{m+n}\end{CD}

where
\Psi
is a function in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\otimes m}
, and we set

\psi=\Psi\circ\Delta_{m}
and

L_{\Psi}:{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\otimes n}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{\otimes m+n},\quad F\mapsto\Psi\otimes F.

Hence, the closure of
\{f|_{K}:f\in\Gamma{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\}
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty,K}
is
a Banach subalgebra of
C(K)
.
This observation leads us to a new proof of the universal approximation theorem for the Gaussian kernel.
The following is the main theorem of this section.
(cf. Theorem 9 in Steinwart
[
9
]
and Theorem 4.1 in Guella
[
1
]
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
X
be a locally compact Hausdorff space,
and let
k=k(x,y)
be a real-valued continuous kernel on
X\times X
.
If
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
separates any distinct two points in
X
,
then, for any compact subset
K
in
X
, any
\varepsilon>0
and any function
f
in
C(K)
,
there exist
c_{1},\ldots,c_{N}
in
{\mathbb{R}}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}
in
X
such that

\left\|f-\sum_{j=1}^{N}c_{j}\exp k(x,a_{j})\right\|_{\infty,K}<\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
A
denote the Banach algebra obtained
by taking the closure of
\{f|_{K}:f\in\Gamma{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\}
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty,K}
.
Then, it follows from the Stone-Weierstrass theorem that
A=C(K)
.
Hence, for any
\varepsilon>0
and any function
f
in
C(K)
,
there exists a vector
G
in
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
such that
\|f-\Gamma G\|_{\infty,K}<\varepsilon
.
Moreover, there exists a finite linear combination
h
of reproducing kernels of
\exp{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
such that

\|\Gamma G-h\|_{\exp{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}}<\varepsilon
.
Hence, setting

M_{K}=\sup_{x\in K}\exp\frac{k(x,x)}{2},

M_{K}
is finite and
we have

\displaystyle|f(x)-h(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|f(x)-(\Gamma G)(x)|+|(\Gamma G)(x)-h(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\|f-\Gamma G\|_{\infty,K}+\|\Gamma G-h\|_{\exp{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}\|\exp k_{x}\|_{\exp{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}

\displaystyle<(1+M_{K})\varepsilon

for any
x
in
K
.
Therefore, we have the conclusion.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

For any compact subset
K
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, any
\varepsilon>0
and any function
f
in
C(K)
,
there exist
c_{1},\ldots,c_{N}
in
{\mathbb{R}}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that

\left\|f-\sum_{j=1}^{N}c_{j}e^{-\|x-a_{j}\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}^{2}}\right\|_{\infty,K}<\varepsilon.

Proof.

We set
k(x,y)=2\langle x,y\rangle_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
. Then,
k
is a real-valued continuous kernel on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Let
f
be any function in
C(K)
.
Then, by Theorem
5.1
,
there exist
d_{1},\ldots,d_{N}
in
{\mathbb{R}}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that

\left\|e^{\|x\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}^{2}}f-\sum_{j=1}^{N}d_{j}e^{2\langle x,a_{j}\rangle_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}\right\|_{\infty,K}<\varepsilon.

We set
c_{j}=d_{j}e^{\|a_{j}\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}^{2}}
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\left|f(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}c_{j}e^{-\|x-a_{j}\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|f(x)-e^{-\|x\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}c_{j}e^{-\|a_{j}\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}^{2}}e^{2\langle x,a_{j}\rangle_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}\right|

\displaystyle=e^{-\|x\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}^{2}}\left|e^{\|x\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}^{2}}f(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}d_{j}e^{2\langle x,a_{j}\rangle_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|e^{\|x\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}^{2}}f-\sum_{j=1}^{N}d_{j}e^{2\langle x,a_{j}\rangle_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}\right\|_{\infty,K}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Implications in pseudocomplemented and Stone lattices
Ivan Chajda, Helmut Länger

Abstract
Two kinds of the connective implication are introduced as term operations of a pseudocomplemented lattice. It is shown that they share a lot of properties with the intuitionistic implication based on Heyting algebras. In particular, if the pseudocomplemented lattice in question is a Stone lattice then the considered implications satisfy some kind of quasi-commutativity, of the exchange property, some version of adjointness with the meet-operation and some kind of the derivation rule Modus Ponens and of the contraposition law. Two kinds of deductive systems are defined and their elementary properties are shown. All investigated concepts are illuminated by examples.

Starting with L. E. J. Brouwer

of

The question arises if some kind of implication can be derived by means of pseudocomplementation. This means that if

is a bounded distributive lattice with pseudocomplementation and an implication is introduced as a term of
\mathbf{L}

The aim of the present paper is to show how such an implication can be introduced and which restrictions are necessary in order to obtain a formalization of an implication similar to that considered in intuitionistic logic.

An implication in pseudocomplemented lattices

On a pseudocomplemented lattice
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{*},0,1)
we define a binary term operation
\rightarrow
by

x\rightarrow y:=x^{*}\vee y

for all
x,y\in L
. Obviously,

•

x^{**}\rightarrow y\approx x\rightarrow y\approx x\rightarrow(x\rightarrow y)
,

•

y\leq x\rightarrow y
for all
x,y\in L
,

•

x\rightarrow 0\approx x^{*}
.

Remark 3.2
.

If
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{*},0,1)
is a pseudocomplemented lattice and
a\in L
then one can easily check the following:

\begin{array}[]{l|lllll}\rightarrow&0&a&a^{*}&a^{**}&1\\

We are going to list several elementary properties of this implication including the so-called exchange property.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented lattice and
a,b,c\in L
. Then the following holds:

(i)

If
a\leq b
then
a\rightarrow b\in D(\mathbf{L})
,

(ii)

(a\rightarrow b)\vee c=a\rightarrow(b\vee c)=(a\rightarrow c)\vee b
,

(iii)

a\rightarrow(b\rightarrow c)=(a\rightarrow c)\vee(b\rightarrow c)=b\rightarrow(a\rightarrow c)

(
exchange property
)
,

(iv)

a\rightarrow(b\rightarrow a)\in D(\mathbf{L})
,

(v)

if
a\leq b
then
c\rightarrow a\leq c\rightarrow b
and
b\rightarrow c\leq a\rightarrow c
.

Proof.

(i)

If
a\leq b
then
(a\rightarrow b)^{*}=(a^{*}\vee b)^{*}\leq a^{**},b^{*}
and hence
(a\rightarrow b)^{*}\leq a^{**},a^{*}
which implies
(a\rightarrow b)^{*}\leq a^{**}\wedge a^{*}=0
, i.e.
a\rightarrow b\in D
.

(ii)

We have
(a\rightarrow b)\vee c=a^{*}\vee b\vee c=a\rightarrow(b\vee c)=a\rightarrow(c\vee b)=(a\rightarrow c)\vee b
.

(iii)

We have

\displaystyle a\rightarrow(b\rightarrow c)

\displaystyle=a^{*}\vee(b^{*}\vee c)=(a^{*}\vee c)\vee(b^{*}\vee c)=(a\rightarrow c)\vee(b\rightarrow c)=

\displaystyle=(b\rightarrow c)\vee(a\rightarrow c)=b\rightarrow(a\rightarrow c).

(iv)

Since
a\leq b\rightarrow a
, (iv) follows from (i).

(v)

If
a\leq b
then
c\rightarrow a=c^{*}\vee a\leq c^{*}\vee b=c\rightarrow b
and
b\rightarrow c=b^{*}\vee c\leq a^{*}\vee c=a\rightarrow c
.

∎

If
\mathbf{L}
is, moreover, distributive, we can prove even the following.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{*},0,1)
be a distributive pseudocomplemented lattice and
a,b,c\in L
. Then the following holds:

(i)

a\wedge(a\rightarrow b)\leq b
,

(ii)

a\leq b\rightarrow c
implies
a\wedge b\leq c
,

(iii)

if
a\rightarrow b=1
then
a\leq b
.

Proof.

(i)

a\wedge(a\rightarrow b)=a\wedge(a^{*}\vee b)=(a\wedge a^{*})\vee(a\wedge b)\leq b
.

(ii)

If
a\leq b\rightarrow c
then
a\wedge b\leq(b\rightarrow c)\wedge b=(b^{*}\vee c)\wedge b=(b^{*}\wedge b)\vee(c\wedge b)\leq c
.

(iii)

If
a\rightarrow b=1
then
a^{*}\vee b=1
thus, by distributivity,

a\leq a\vee b=(a\vee b)\wedge 1=(a\vee b)\wedge(a^{*}\vee b)=(a\wedge a^{*})\vee b=b.

∎

Let us note that condition (i) of Lemma
3.4
can be read as follows: The truth value of proposition
y
cannot be less than the truth value of the conjunction of the propositions
x
and
x\rightarrow y
, which is nothing else than the Modus Ponens derivation rule.

Observe that assertion (ii) of Lemma
3.4
does not hold if
\mathbf{L}
is not distributive. For example, consider the lattice
N_{5}
depicted in Fig. 1 (a). Then we have
c\leq 1=c\rightarrow a
, but
c\wedge c=c\not\leq a
.

In a Stone lattice, we can prove a modified version of adjointness between
\wedge
and
\rightarrow
, see the following lemma.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{*},0,1)
be a Stone lattice and
a,b,c\in L
. Then the following holds:

(i)

(a^{*}\rightarrow b^{*})\rightarrow b^{*}=a^{*}\vee b^{*}=(b^{*}\rightarrow a^{*})\rightarrow a^{*}
,

(ii)

a\wedge b^{*}\leq c
if and only if
a\leq b^{*}\rightarrow c
.

Proof.

(i)

According to Lemma
2.1
we have

\displaystyle(a^{*}\rightarrow b^{*})\rightarrow b^{*}

\displaystyle=(a^{**}\vee b^{*})^{*}\vee b^{*}=(a^{*}\wedge b^{**})\vee b^{*}=(a^{*}\vee b^{*})\wedge(b^{**}\vee b^{*})=

\displaystyle=a^{*}\vee b^{*}=b^{*}\vee a^{*}=(b^{*}\rightarrow a^{*})\rightarrow a^{*}.

(ii)

If
a\wedge b^{*}\leq c
then

a\leq(b^{**}\vee a)\wedge(b^{**}\vee b^{*})=b^{**}\vee(a\wedge b^{*})\leq b^{**}\vee c=b^{*}\rightarrow c.

The converse implication follows from Lemma
3.4
.

∎

We can characterize Stone lattices by using properties of the implication
\rightarrow
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathbf{L}_{1}=(L,\vee,\wedge,\rightarrow,0,1)
be an algebra of type
(2,2,2,0,0)
such that
(L,\vee,\wedge,0,1)
is a bounded distributive lattice and put
x^{*}:=x\rightarrow 0
for all
x\in L
. Then
\mathbf{L}_{2}:=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{*},0,1)
is a Stone lattice if and only if
\mathbf{L}_{1}
satisfies the identities

(1)

x\wedge(0\rightarrow 0)\approx x
,

(2)

x\wedge(x\rightarrow 0)\approx 0
,

(3)

x\wedge\big((x\wedge y)\rightarrow 0\big)\approx x\wedge(y\rightarrow 0)
,

(4)

(x\rightarrow 0)\vee\big((x\rightarrow 0)\rightarrow 0\big)\approx 1
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in L
. If
\mathbf{L}_{2}
is a Stone lattice then

(1)

x\wedge(0\rightarrow 0)\approx x\wedge 0^{*}\approx x\wedge 1\approx x
,

(2)

x\wedge(x\rightarrow 0)\approx x\wedge x^{*}\approx 0
,

(3)

Using (ii) of Lemma
2.2
we obtain
x\wedge\big((x\wedge y)\rightarrow 0\big)\approx x\wedge(x\wedge y)^{*}\approx x\wedge(x^{*}\vee y^{*})\approx(x\wedge x^{*})\vee(x\wedge y^{*})\approx x\wedge y^{*}\approx x\wedge(y\rightarrow 0)
,

(4)

(x\rightarrow 0)\vee\big((x\rightarrow 0)\rightarrow 0\big)\approx x^{*}\vee x^{**}\approx 1
,

i.e.
\mathbf{L}_{1}
satisfies identities (1) – (4). Conversely, assume
\mathbf{L}_{1}
to satisfy identities (1) – (4). If
a\wedge b=0
the according to (1) and (3) we have

b=b\wedge(0\rightarrow 0)=b\wedge\big((b\wedge a)\rightarrow 0\big)=b\wedge(a\rightarrow 0)\leq a\rightarrow 0=a^{*}.

If, conversely,
b\leq a^{*}
then
b\leq a\rightarrow 0
and according to (2) we have
a\wedge b\leq a\wedge(a\rightarrow 0)=0
. This shows that
a^{*}
is the pseudocomplement of
a
. Finally, (4) is nothing else than the Stone identity. Altogether, we obtain that
\mathbf{L}_{2}
is a Stone lattice.
∎

The previous theorem shows that the class of Stone lattices can be described by identities using the operation symbols
\vee
,
\wedge
,
\rightarrow
and
0
. Hence, the class of these implication algebras forms a variety similar to that of intuitionistic logic based on Heyting algebras.

Another possible implication in pseudocomplemented lattices

We can introduce a connective implication in a pseudocomplemented lattice
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{*},0,1)
also in another way as follows:

x\Rightarrow y:=x^{*}\vee y^{**}

for all
x,y\in L
. Obviously,

•

x\Rightarrow y\approx x\rightarrow y^{**}
,

•

x\Rightarrow y^{*}\approx x\rightarrow y^{*}

•

x\Rightarrow y\approx x^{**}\Rightarrow y\approx x\Rightarrow y^{**}\approx x^{**}\Rightarrow y^{**}

•

x\rightarrow y\leq x\Rightarrow y
for all
x,y\in L
,

•

x\Rightarrow y\approx y^{*}\Rightarrow x^{*}\approx y^{*}\rightarrow x^{*}
(
contraposition law
),

•

x\Rightarrow 0\approx x^{*}
.

3.4

(iii)

If
a\rightarrow b=a\Rightarrow b
then

\displaystyle a^{**}\wedge b

\displaystyle=(a^{**}\wedge a^{*})\vee(a^{**}\wedge b)=a^{**}\wedge(a^{*}\vee b)=a^{**}\wedge(a\rightarrow b)=a^{**}\wedge(a\Rightarrow b)=

\displaystyle=a^{**}\wedge(a^{*}\vee b^{**})=(a^{**}\wedge a^{*})\vee(a^{**}\wedge b^{**})=a^{**}\wedge b^{**}.

∎

Using the previous results, we can prove some important properties of implications provided the pseudocomplemented lattice is a Stone lattice.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{*},0,1)
be a Stone lattice and
a,b,c\in L
. Then the following holds:

(i)

(a\Rightarrow b)\Rightarrow b=a^{**}\vee b^{**}=(b\Rightarrow a)\Rightarrow a

(
quasi-commutativity
)
,

(ii)

a\wedge b^{*}\leq c^{**}
if and only if
a\leq b^{*}\Rightarrow c
,

(iii)

a\rightarrow b=a\Rightarrow b
if and only if
a^{**}\wedge b=a^{**}\wedge b^{**}
,

(iv)

a\Rightarrow(b\Rightarrow c)=(a\Rightarrow c)\vee(b\Rightarrow c)=b\Rightarrow(a\Rightarrow c)

(
exchange property
)
,

(v)

(a\Rightarrow b^{*})\vee c^{*}=a\Rightarrow(b^{*}\vee c^{*})=(a\Rightarrow c^{*})\vee b^{*}
,

(vi)

a\Rightarrow b=1
if and only if
a\leq b^{**}
.

Proof.

(i)

According to Lemma
2.1
we have

\displaystyle(a\Rightarrow b)\Rightarrow b

\displaystyle=(a^{*}\vee b^{**})^{*}\vee b^{**}=(a^{**}\wedge b^{*})\vee b^{**}=(a^{**}\vee b^{**})\wedge(b^{*}\vee b^{**})=

\displaystyle=a^{**}\vee b^{**}=b^{**}\vee a^{**}=(b\Rightarrow a)\Rightarrow a.

(ii)

This follows from Proposition
3.5
.

(iii)

If
a^{**}\wedge b=a^{**}\wedge b^{**}
then

\displaystyle a\rightarrow b

\displaystyle=a^{*}\vee b=(a^{*}\vee a^{**})\wedge(a^{*}\vee b)=a^{*}\vee(a^{**}\wedge b)=a^{*}\vee(a^{**}\wedge b^{**})=

\displaystyle=(a^{*}\vee a^{**})\wedge(a^{*}\vee b^{**})=a^{*}\vee b^{**}=a\Rightarrow b.

The converse implication follows from Lemma
4.5
.

(iv)

We have

\displaystyle a\Rightarrow(b\Rightarrow c)

\displaystyle=a^{*}\vee(b^{*}\vee c^{**})^{**}=a^{*}\vee(b^{*}\vee c^{**})=(a^{*}\vee c^{**})\vee(b^{*}\vee c^{**})=

\displaystyle=(a\Rightarrow c)\vee(b\Rightarrow c)=(b\Rightarrow c)\vee(a\Rightarrow c)=b\Rightarrow(a\Rightarrow c).

(v)

We have

\displaystyle(a\Rightarrow b^{*})\vee c^{*}

\displaystyle=(a^{*}\vee b^{*})\vee c^{*}=a^{*}\vee(b^{*}\vee c^{*})=a^{*}\vee(b^{*}\vee c^{*})^{**}=a\Rightarrow(b^{*}\vee c^{*})=

\displaystyle=a\Rightarrow(c^{*}\vee b^{*})=(a\Rightarrow c^{*})\vee b^{*}.

(vi)

One implication follows from
a\Rightarrow b=a\rightarrow b^{**}
and Lemma
3.4
and the converse implication from Lemma
4.4
.

∎

The question if the implication
\Rightarrow
is “better” than the implication
\rightarrow
has not a simple answer: Although the implication
\Rightarrow
, contrary to the implication
\rightarrow
, satisfies that
x\leq y
implies
x\Rightarrow y=1
provided the pseudocomplemented lattice satisfies the Stone identity, the implication
\Rightarrow
does not satisfy Modus Ponens in its genuine version. On the other hand,
\Rightarrow
satisfies the contraposition law.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{*},0,1)
be a Stone lattice and
A
a deductive system of the second kind of
\mathbf{L}
. Then the following holds:

(i)

\Theta(A)
is reflexive, symmetric and compatible with all fundamental operations of
\mathbf{L}
and
[1]\big(\Theta(A)\big)=\{x\in L\mid x^{**}\in A\}
,

(ii)

if
A
is closed with respect to
\wedge
then
\Theta(A)\in\Con\mathbf{L}
.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in L
.

(i)

Because of
a\Rightarrow a=a^{*}\vee a^{**}=1\in A
, the relation
\Theta(A)
is reflexive. Clearly,
\Theta(A)
is symmetric. Now assume
(a,b)\in\Theta(A)
. Then
a\Rightarrow b\in A
and
b\Rightarrow a\in A
and we have

\displaystyle(a\vee c)\Rightarrow(b\vee c)

\displaystyle=(a\vee c)^{*}\vee(b\vee c)^{**}=(a^{*}\wedge c^{*})\vee(b^{**}\vee c^{**})=

\displaystyle=(a^{*}\vee b^{**}\vee c^{**})\wedge(c^{*}\vee b^{**}\vee c^{**})=a^{*}\vee b^{**}\vee c^{**}\geq a^{*}\vee b^{**}=

\displaystyle=a\Rightarrow b\in A,

\displaystyle(a\wedge c)\Rightarrow(b\wedge c)

\displaystyle=(a\wedge c)^{*}\vee(b\wedge c)^{**}=(a^{*}\vee c^{*})\vee(b^{**}\wedge c^{**})=

\displaystyle=(a^{*}\vee c^{*}\vee b^{**})\wedge(a^{*}\vee c^{*}\vee c^{**})=a^{*}\vee c^{*}\vee b^{**}\geq a^{*}\vee b^{**}=

\displaystyle=a\Rightarrow b\in A,

\displaystyle a^{*}\Rightarrow b^{*}

\displaystyle=b\Rightarrow a\in A.

According to Lemma
5.7
(i) we obtain
(a\vee c)\Rightarrow(b\vee c),(a\wedge c)\Rightarrow(b\wedge c),a^{*}\Rightarrow b^{*}\in A
. Analogously,
(b\vee c)\Rightarrow(a\vee c),(b\wedge c)\Rightarrow(a\wedge c),b^{*}\Rightarrow a^{*}\in A
can be proved. This shows
(a\vee c,b\vee c),(a\wedge c,b\wedge c),(a^{*},b^{*})\in\Theta(A)
. Finally, the following are equivalent:
a\in[1]\big(\Theta(A)\big)
;
a\Rightarrow 1\in A
and
1\Rightarrow a\in A

a^{*}\vee 1^{**}\in A
and
1^{*}\vee a^{**}\in A

1,a^{**}\in A

a^{**}\in A

(ii)

Assume
(a,b),(b,c)\in\Theta(A)

a\Rightarrow b\in A

b\Rightarrow c\in A
and hence
(a\Rightarrow b)\wedge(b\Rightarrow c)\in A

\displaystyle\big((a\Rightarrow b)\wedge(b\Rightarrow c)\big)\Rightarrow(a\Rightarrow c)

\displaystyle=\big((a^{*}\vee b^{**})\wedge(b^{*}\vee c^{**})\big)^{*}\vee(a^{*}\vee c^{**})^{**}=

\displaystyle=\big((a^{**}\wedge b^{*})\vee(b^{**}\wedge c^{*})\big)\vee a^{*}\vee c^{**}=

\displaystyle=(a^{**}\vee b^{**}\vee a^{*}\vee c^{**})\wedge(a^{**}\vee c^{*}\vee a^{*}\vee c^{**})\wedge

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\wedge(b^{*}\vee b^{**}\vee a^{*}\vee c^{**})\wedge(b^{*}\vee c^{*}\vee a^{*}\vee c^{**})=

\displaystyle=1\wedge 1\wedge 1\wedge 1=1\in A.

6
Concluding remark

The goal of our paper was to introduce a connective implication in pseudocomplemented lattices as a term operation. We succeeded it in two different ways. The question is if such implications satisfy properties required for implication in classical or many-valued logics. It is well-known (see e.g.
[
1
]
) that the implication in the so-called implication algebras derived from Boolean algebras are fully determined by the following three axioms:

(a)

(x\rightarrow y)\rightarrow x\approx x
(contraction property),

(b)

(x\rightarrow y)\rightarrow y\approx(y\rightarrow x)\rightarrow x
(quasi-commutativity),

(c)

x\rightarrow(y\rightarrow z)\approx y\rightarrow(x\rightarrow z)
(exchange property),

see e.g.
[
1
]
. What we get in our study is the following. If the pseudocomplemented lattice in question is a Stone lattice, then the implication
\Rightarrow
satisfies quasi-commutativity, the exchange property and the identity

(x\Rightarrow y)\Rightarrow x\approx x^{**},

see e.g. Theorem
4.6
(i) and (iv) and Lemma
4.3
(iv). Thus our implication
\Rightarrow
differs only in the point (a) where, however,
x^{**}\geq x
.

Moreover, we can compare our implication
\Rightarrow
with that derived in many-valued Lukasiewicz logic, see e.g.
[
5
]
. Here the implication is fully determined by quasi-commutativity, the exchange property and the identities

x\rightarrow 1\approx 1\text{ and }1\rightarrow x\approx x.

As mentioned above,
\Rightarrow
satisfies quasi-commutativity, the exchange property and the identity
x\Rightarrow 1\approx 1
, thus it differs only in the last axiom where we have the identity

1\Rightarrow x\approx x^{**}

with
x^{**}\geq x
. We hope that this justifies our investigation of implications derived by a pseudocomplemented lattices, in particular of Stone ones.

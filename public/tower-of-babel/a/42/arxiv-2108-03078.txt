Dispersionless BKP equation, the Manakov-Santini system and Einstein-Weyl structures
L.V. Bogdanov

Abstract
We construct a map of solutions of the dispersionless BKP (dBKP) equation to solutions of the Manakov-Santini (MS) system. This map defines an Einstein-Weyl structure corresponding to the dBKP equation through the general Lorentzian Einstein-Weyl structure corresponding to the MS system. We give a spectral characterisation of reduction of the MS system which singles out the image of the dBKP equation solutions and also consider more general reductions of this class. We define the BMS system and extend the map defined above to the map (Miura transformation) of solutions of the BMS system to solutions of the MS system, thus obtaining an Einstein-Weyl structure for the BMS system.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}H_{1}-\partial_{y}H_{2}+\{H_{1},H_{2}\}=0,

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{1}{5}u_{t}+u^{2}u_{x}-\frac{1}{3}uu_{y}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{y}-\frac{1}{9}\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{yy}=0.

(3)

In what follows we will rescale the times to simplify the coefficients of equation

In potential form,

\textstyle\partial_{x}\left(f_{t}+\frac{1}{3}f_{x}^{3}-f_{x}f_{y}\right)=f_{yy}.

From the dBKP equation to the MS system

In explicit form vector fields (
6

\displaystyle V_{1}=\partial_{y}-(p^{2}+f_{x})\partial_{x}+f_{xx}p\partial_{p},

\displaystyle V_{2}=\partial_{t}-(p^{4}+3f_{x}p^{2}+v)\partial_{x},+(f_{xx}p^{2}+v_{x})p\partial_{p},

(7)

v=f_{y}+f_{x}^{2}
.
Symmetry of vector fields
V(-p)=V(p)
characterises
dispersionless BKP hierarchy in the framework of dKP hierarchy.

Let us transform the spectral variable

p^{2}=\mu
, commutation relations evidently remain the same,

\displaystyle V_{1}=\partial_{y}-(\mu+f_{x})\partial_{x}+2f_{xx}\mu\partial_{\mu},

\displaystyle V_{2}=\partial_{t}-(\mu^{2}+3f_{x}\mu+v)\partial_{x}+2(f_{xx}\mu+v_{x})\mu\partial_{\mu}

Vector fields are still Hamiltonian (the bracket should
be changed).

Now let us make a change

\lambda=\mu+2f_{x}
(which also preserves commutation relations)

\displaystyle V_{1}=\partial_{y}-(\lambda-f_{x})\partial_{x}+2(f_{xy}-f_{x}f_{xx})\partial_{\lambda},

\displaystyle V_{2}=\partial_{t}-(\lambda^{2}-f_{x}\lambda+f_{y}-f_{x}^{2})\partial_{x}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+2((f_{xy}-f_{x}f_{xx})\lambda+(f_{xt}-f_{y}f_{xx}+f_{x}^{2}f_{xx}-2f_{xy}f_{x}))\partial_{\lambda}.

(8)

Lax pair
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
has the structure of
the Manakov-Santini (MS) system Lax pair

\begin{split}X_{1}&=\partial_{y}-(\lambda-v_{x})\partial_{x}+u_{x}\partial_{\lambda},\\
X_{2}&=\partial_{t}-(\lambda^{2}-v_{x}\lambda+u-v_{y})\partial_{x}+(u_{x}\lambda+u_{y})\partial_{\lambda},\end{split}

(9)

the MS system reads

\begin{split}u_{xt}&=u_{yy}+(uu_{x})_{x}+v_{x}u_{xy}-u_{xx}v_{y},\\
v_{xt}&=v_{yy}+uv_{xx}+v_{x}v_{xy}-v_{xx}v_{y}\end{split}

(10)

A comparison of Lax pairs (
9
),
(
8
) gives a map from solution of
dBKP equation (
5

10
),

\displaystyle v=f,\quad u=2f_{y}-f_{x}^{2},

(11)

corresponding solutions of the MS system
satisfy a reduction

\displaystyle u=2v_{y}-v_{x}^{2}.

(12)

This map defines the Einstein-Weyl structure
corresponding to dBKP equation.

Einstein-Weyl structure for the dBKP equation

Let us remind (see

6

,
[
7
]

{{D}}
consistent with

for every

\displaystyle{{D}}g=\omega\otimes g

(13)

for some differential form
\omega
(the connection

Einstein-Weyl spaces are defined by the condition that
the trace-free part of the symmetrised Ricci tensor of
the connection

which together with relation
(
13

constitute Einstein-Weyl equations system,
in a coordinate form

\displaystyle D_{i}g_{ij}=\omega_{i}g_{ij},\quad R_{(ij)}=\Lambda g_{ij},

(14)

here
\Lambda

Einstein-Weyl equations are correctly
defined for arbitrary manifold dimension not
less than three, but the most interesting case
is three-dimensional when they are integrable

8

The Manakov-Santini system (

\displaystyle\begin{split}g&=-(dy+v_{x}dt)^{2}+4(dx+(u-v_{y})dt)dt,\\
\omega&=v_{xx}dy+(-4u_{x}+2v_{xy}+v_{x}v_{xx})dt,\end{split}

where

satisfy the MS system.
Using the map (

),

\displaystyle\begin{split}g&=-(dy+f_{x}dt)^{2}+4(dx+(f_{y}-f_{x}^{2})dt)dt,\\
\omega&=f_{xx}dy+3(-2f_{xy}+3f_{x}f_{xx})dt,\end{split}

(16)

It could be possible to construct this Einstein-Weyl structure

dBKP equation as a reduction of the MS system

It is possible to get a condition (
12
) starting from the reduction of the MS hierarchy,
which is characterised by the existence of wave function of adjoint linear operators
of the hierarchy with special analytic properties (with respect to the spectral variable).
The technique to construct this type of reductions was developed in
[
9
]
.
Here we will do an elementary derivation on the level of Lax operator for the MS system.

First we introduce formally adjoint linear operators,
defined by the rule
(u\partial)^{*}=-\partial u
(for all partial derivatives),

\displaystyle-X^{*}=X+\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits X,

for the Lax operator of the MS system (
9
) we get

\displaystyle-X_{1}^{*}=\partial_{y}-(\lambda-v_{x})\partial_{x}+u_{x}\partial_{\lambda}+v_{xx}=X_{1}+v_{xx}

(17)

We should emphasize that adjoint vector fields in general are not vector fields and contain an extra term
without derivative, equal to divergence of vector field, for zero divergence vector fields are
(anti) self-adjoint. However, the commutation of adjoint vector fields gives the same
compatibility conditions.

Let us suggest that adjoint Lax operator (
17
) possesses a
wave function of the form

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}=(\lambda-g)^{\alpha},\quad X_{1}^{*}\widetilde{\psi}=0,

(18)

where
g
is a function of times.
This condition is compatible with the dynamics of the hierarchy and defines a reduction,
see
[
9
]
.
The form of this wave function
can be found considering the map
from the dispersionless BKP equation to the MS system, we will skip the details.
For the logarithm of the wave function we have an equation

\displaystyle X_{1}\ln\widetilde{\psi}+v_{xx}=(\partial_{y}-(\lambda-v_{x})\partial_{x}+u_{x}\partial_{\lambda})\ln\widetilde{\psi}+v_{xx}=0,

and, substituting
\widetilde{\psi}=(\lambda-g)^{\alpha}
, we get

\displaystyle u_{x}=g_{y}+g_{x}v_{x}-gg_{x},

\displaystyle v_{x}=-\alpha g,

(19)

implying a condition

\displaystyle u=-\alpha^{-1}v_{y}-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha^{-2}+\alpha^{-1})v_{x}^{2}.

(20)

For
\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}
this condition coincides with condition (
12
)
and MS system (
10
) reduces to potential dispersionless BKP equation (
5
)
(
f=v
).
For
\alpha=0
relations (
19
) imply that
v=0
, and we obtain the dKP equation
for the function
u
,
u_{x}=g_{y}-gg_{x}
.
For general
\alpha
the MS system reduces to the equation

\displaystyle v_{xt}=v_{yy}-\alpha^{-1}(v_{y}+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha^{-1}+1)v_{x}^{2})v_{xx}+v_{x}v_{xy}-v_{xx}v_{y}.

(21)

An interesting special case corresponds to
\alpha=-1
, then condition
(
20
) takes the form
u=v_{y}
and the MS system reduces to the equation

\displaystyle v_{xt}=v_{yy}+v_{x}v_{xy}.

(22)

The Einstein-Weyl structure for equation (
21
)
is obtained by the substitution of expression for
u
(
20
)
to the MS system Einstein-Weyl structure (
15
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}g&=-(dy+v_{x}dt)^{2}+4(dx-(\alpha^{-1}+1)(v_{y}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}v_{x}^{2})dt)dt,\\
\omega&=v_{xx}dy+(2(1+2\alpha^{-1})v_{xy}+(1+4(\alpha^{-2}+\alpha^{-1}))v_{x}v_{xx})dt,\end{split}

for equation (
22
) it reduces to

\displaystyle\begin{split}g&=-(dy+v_{x}dt)^{2}+4(dx+dt)dt,\\
\omega&=v_{xx}dy+(-2v_{xy}+v_{x}v_{xx})dt,\end{split}

It is natural to suggest that, similar to dBKP case, equation
(
21
) for arbitrary
\alpha
could be obtained from some Hamiltonian
Lax pair. And it is indeed so!
Let us consider
dBKP type Lax pair (
1
) with the Hamiltonians

\displaystyle H_{1}=\frac{1}{1+\beta}p^{\beta+1}+f_{x}p,

\displaystyle H_{2}=\frac{1}{1+2\beta}p^{2\beta+1}+f_{x}p^{\beta+1}+vp,

(23)

\beta=2
corresponds to the dBKP equation case.
In terms of Hamiltonian vector fields we have

\displaystyle V_{1}=\partial_{y}-(p^{\beta}+f_{x})\partial_{x}+f_{xx}p\partial_{p},

\displaystyle V_{2}=\partial_{t}-(p^{2\beta}+(\beta+1)f_{x}p^{\beta}+v)\partial_{x},+(f_{xx}p^{\beta}+v_{x})p\partial_{p}.

(24)

Similar to dBKP equation Lax pair, we will transform this Lax pair
to obtain the Lax pair of the MS type.
The first step is to perform a transformation
\mu=p^{\beta}
,

\displaystyle V_{1}=\partial_{y}-(\mu+f_{x})\partial_{x}+\beta f_{xx}\mu\partial_{\mu},

\displaystyle V_{2}=\partial_{t}-(\mu^{2}+(\beta+1)f_{x}\mu+v)\partial_{x},+\beta(f_{xx}\mu+v_{x})\mu\partial_{\mu}.

The second step is a transformation
\lambda=\mu+\beta f_{x}
,

\displaystyle V_{1}=\partial_{y}-(\lambda+(1-\beta)f_{x})\partial_{x}+(\beta f_{xy}-\beta f_{x}f_{xx})\partial_{\mu}.

Comparing with the MS Lax operator (
9
),
we get

\displaystyle u_{x}=\beta f_{xy}-\beta f_{x}f_{xx},

\displaystyle v=(\beta-1)f.

After the identification
g=\beta f_{x}
,
\beta^{-1}=\alpha+1
, this transformation
coincides with expressions (
19
). Thus, equation (
21
) can be obtained from the
Hamiltonian Lax pair (
23
), (
24
), substituting
\beta^{-1}=\alpha+1
,
v=\alpha f
.

BMS system

The symmetry of vector fields
V(-p)=V(p)
(
7
) characterising
dispersionless BKP hierarchy in the framework of dKP hierarchy can be extended to the
Manakov-Santini hierarchy. The Lax pair for the first equation of the hierarchy
(the BMS system) reads

\displaystyle V_{1}=\partial_{y}-(p^{2}-v_{x})\partial_{x}+u_{x}p\partial_{p},

\displaystyle V_{2}=\partial_{t}-(p^{4}+(2u-v_{x})p^{2}+w_{1})\partial_{x}+(u_{x}p^{2}+w_{2})p\partial_{p},

(25)

Compatibility conditions imply that
w_{2}=u_{y}+(u^{2})_{x}
, and the BMS system can be written in the form

\displaystyle v_{xt}=v_{xx}w_{1}-v{w_{1}}_{x}-{w_{1}}_{y},

\displaystyle u_{xt}=u_{yy}+(u^{2})_{xy}+u_{xx}w_{1}+vu_{xy}+v(u^{2})_{xx},

(26)

\displaystyle{w_{1}}_{x}=-2uv_{xx}-v_{xy}.

For
u=0
, corresponding to linearly degenerate case, when vector fields
in the Lax pair
do not include the derivative over spectral parameter,
BMS system (
26
) reduces to the equation

\displaystyle v_{xt}=v_{x}v_{xy}-v_{xx}{v_{y}}+v_{yy},

coinciding with the linearly degenerate reduction of MS system
(
10
). It is not unexpected, because in linearly degenerate case we have
a freedom of arbitrary change of the spectral variable
(independent of times).

Hamiltonian reduction corresponds to
u=-v_{x}
, and system
(
26
) reduces to potential dBKP equation (
5
) for the function
f=-v
.

The condition
v=0
is evidently also a reduction of system (
26
),
it leads to the equation

\displaystyle u_{xt}=u_{yy}+(u^{2})_{xy},

which coincides with equation (
22
) after the identification
v_{x}=2u
.

It is rather suprising that, following the steps of transformation of the
dBKP Lax pair to the MS type Lax pair described above,
we are able to define a map (Miura transformation) from solutions
of the BMS system to solutions of the MS system,
thus defining the Einstein-Weyl structure corresponding to the
BMS system.

Performing a transformation
\lambda=p^{2}+2u

in the Lax pair (
25
), we obtain
a Lax pair of MS type with a Lax operator

\displaystyle V_{1}=\partial_{y}-(\lambda-2u-v_{x})\partial_{x}+2(u_{y}+v_{x}u_{x})\partial_{\lambda}.

Comparing this Lax operator to the MS Lax operator (
9
),
we obtain a transformation of solutions of the BMS system
to the solutions of MS system,

\displaystyle 2u+v_{x}\rightarrow v_{x},

\displaystyle 2(u_{y}+v_{x}u_{x})\rightarrow u_{x}.

Substituting this transformation to Einstein-Weyl structure
(
15
), we obtain the Einstein-Weyl structure
corresponding to BMS system (
26
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}g&=-(dy+(2u+v_{x})dt)^{2}+4(dx+(2\partial_{x}^{-1}(v_{x}u_{x})-v_{y})dt)dt,\\
\omega&=(2u_{x}+v_{xx})dy+(-8v_{x}u_{x}-4u_{y}+2v_{xy}+\frac{1}{2}((2u+v_{x})^{2})_{x})dt,\end{split}

Hydrodynamic type reductions of the MS system

Let us consider a multicomponent generalisation of reduction (
18
)

\widetilde{\psi}=\prod(\lambda-g^{i})^{\alpha_{i}}

and complement it with a standard waterbag ansatz
[
10
]
for
the wave functions of MS linear operators (
9
)

\psi=\lambda+\sum\gamma_{j}\ln(\lambda-f^{j})
.
Some special examples of related reductions were considered
in
[
11
]
.
These reductions
lead to (1+1)-dimensional systems of hydrodynamic type
defining the dynamics with respect to
y
,

\displaystyle g^{i}_{y}-(g^{i}-v_{x})g^{i}_{x}-u_{x}=0,

\displaystyle f^{j}_{y}-(f^{j}-v_{x})f^{j}_{x}-u_{x}=0,

\displaystyle v_{x}=-\sum\alpha_{i}g^{i},\quad u=-\sum\gamma_{j}f^{j}

and with respect to
t
,

\displaystyle g^{i}_{t}-((g^{i})^{2}-v_{x}g^{i}+u-v_{y})g^{i}_{x}-(g^{i}u_{x}+u_{y})=0,

\displaystyle f^{j}_{t}-((g^{i})^{2}-v_{x}g^{i}+u-v_{y})g^{i}_{x}-(g^{i}u_{x}+u_{y})=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}v_{y}=-\partial_{y}\sum\alpha_{i}g^{i}=-\sum\alpha_{i}((g^{i}-v_{x})g^{i}_{x}+u_{x}),

\displaystyle u_{y}=-\sum\gamma_{j}((f^{j}-v_{x})f^{j}_{x}+u_{x}).

These (1+1)-dimensional systems are compatible and their common solution represents a
solution of the MS system.

By definition of

Step 1

|k|>N

\displaystyle|\langle w_{j^{\prime}},w_{j^{\prime\prime}}\rangle-\delta_{j^{\prime}j^{\prime\prime}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{|k|>N}\frac{c^{2}e^{-\beta(|j^{\prime}-k|+|j^{\prime\prime}-k|)}}{\langle j^{\prime}\rangle\langle j^{\prime\prime}\rangle\langle k\rangle^{2}\langle j^{\prime}-k\rangle^{\alpha-1}\langle j^{\prime\prime}-k\rangle^{\alpha-1}}\,\leq\,\varepsilon_{j^{\prime}j^{\prime\prime}},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j^{\prime}j^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}c_{\alpha-1}e^{-\beta|j^{\prime}-j^{\prime\prime}|}}{\langle j^{\prime}\rangle\langle j^{\prime\prime}\rangle(N+1)^{2}\langle j^{\prime}-j^{\prime\prime}\rangle^{\alpha-1}}.

(4.2)

Proposition 4.1

For any sufficiently large

).

We start proof of Proposition
4.1
from the following simple lemma.

Lemma 4.1

If
N
is sufficiently large,

\sum_{l=-N}^{N}\varepsilon_{jl}\varepsilon_{lk}\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{jk}\quad\mbox{for any }j,k\in\{-N,\ldots,N\}.

Proof
. Direct computation with the help of the inequality (
4.2
).

We have to perform an orthogonalization of the “almost Hermitian” basis
w_{-N},\ldots,w_{N}
. The standard Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization procedure gives bad estimates for the differences
U_{jk}-w_{jk}
. We use another version of the orthogonalization process, based on the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{s},a_{s+1}
be linearly independent vectors such that

\langle a_{j},a_{k}\rangle=\delta_{jk}+{\cal E}_{jk},\quad|{\cal E}_{jk}|\leq\sigma_{jk},\quad\sum_{l=1}^{s}\sigma_{jl}\sigma_{lk}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{jk}\quad\mbox{for any }j,k\in\{1,\ldots,s\}.

(4.5)

Then there exists a vector

\displaystyle b=(1+\sigma^{\prime})^{-1}\big(a_{s+1}-\sum_{j=1}^{s}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\big)
such that

\displaystyle|b|=1,\quad\langle a_{j},b\rangle=0,\qquad 1\leq j\leq s,

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\lambda_{j}|\leq 2\sigma_{js+1},\quad|\sigma^{\prime}|\leq\sigma_{s+1s+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}2\sigma_{js+1}(1+\sigma_{jj}).

(4.6)

Proof
. First note that the equation
(|a_{j}|-1)(|a_{j}|+1)=\sigma_{jj}
implies

\big||a_{j}|-1\big|\leq\sigma_{jj},\qquad j=1,\ldots,s+1.

(4.7)

We put
b^{\prime}=a_{s+1}-\sum_{j=1}^{s}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
and assume that

\langle a_{k},b^{\prime}\rangle=0
for any
k=1,\ldots,s
. Then

\langle a_{k},a_{s+1}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\langle a_{k},a_{j}\rangle\lambda_{j}.

This implies
(I+{\cal E})\lambda=\mu
, where

\lambda=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\lambda_{1}\\
\ldots\\
\lambda_{s}\end{array}\right),\quad\mu=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\langle a_{1},a_{s+1}\rangle\\
\ldots\\
\langle a_{s},a_{s+1}\rangle\end{array}\right),\quad{\cal E}=({\cal E}_{jk}),\qquad j,k\in\{1,\ldots,s\}.

The equation

(I+{\cal E})^{-1}=I-{\cal E}+{\cal E}^{2}-{\cal E}^{3}+\ldots
and the inequality (

Since

Putting

\displaystyle\displaystyle|b^{\prime}-a_{s+1}|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{s}2\sigma_{js+1}|a_{j}|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{s}2\sigma_{js+1}(1+\sigma_{jj}),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{|b^{\prime}|}{|b|}=1+\sigma^{\prime},\quad|\sigma^{\prime}|=\big||b^{\prime}|-1\big|\leq\sigma_{s+1s+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}2\sigma_{js+1}(1+\sigma_{jj}).

Lemma
4.2
is proved.

We apply Lemma

We have:

(4.8)

where the coefficients

are

with properly changed indices and

(4.9)

For any

4.9

This proves (

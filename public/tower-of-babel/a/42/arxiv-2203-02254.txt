Berezin density and planar orthogonal polynomials
Haakan Hedenmalm, Aron Wennman

Abstract
. We introduce a nonlinear potential theory problem for the Laplacian, the solution of which characterizes the Berezin density B ⁡ ( z , ⋅ ) B(z,\cdot) for the polynomial Bergman space, where the point z ∈ ℂ z\in\mathbb{C} is fixed. When z = ∞ z=\infty , the Berezin density is expressed in terms of the squared modulus of the corresponding normalized orthogonal polynomial P P . We use an approximate version of this characterization to study the asymptotics of the orthogonal polynomials in the context of exponentially varying weights. This builds on earlier works by Its-Takhtajan and by the first author on a soft Riemann-Hilbert problem for planar orthogonal polynomials, where in place of the Laplacian we have the ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} -operator. We adapt the soft Riemann-Hilbert approach to the nonlinear potential problem, where the nonlinearity is due to the appearance of | P | 2 |P|^{2} in place of P ¯ \overline{P} . Moreover, we suggest how to adapt the potential theory method to the study of the asymptotics of more general Berezin densities B ⁡ ( z , w ) B(z,w) in the off-spectral regime, that is, when z z is fixed outside the droplet. This is a first installment in a program to obtain an explicit global expansion formula for the polynomial Bergman kernel, and, in particular, of the one-point function of the associated random normal matrix ensemble.

1.
Overview

We consider a novel potential theoretic problem involving the Laplacian
and the Berezin density associated with a polynomial Bergman kernel
for exponentially varying weights in the plane. The Berezin density
for the point at infinity has a particularly simple form, which allows us
to use this framework to characterize the planar orthogonal polynomials.
In particular, we supply an independent proof of the main results of

[
20
,
18
]
aimed at obtaining
an asymptotic expansion of the associated
polynomial Bergman density.
The method is reminiscent of the Riemann-Hilbert approach to orthogonal
polynomials on the real line, and is closely related to the

\bar{\partial}
-approach introduced by Its-Takhtajan, and developed
further by the first-named author in
[
18
]
,
where it was called a
soft Riemann-Hilbert problem
.
We present the potential problem for the point at infinity in §
2
.
In §
1
we indicate how to modify the
approach to analyze the Berezin density for more
general (off-spectral) points. At the same time, we also mention an interpretation of
the Ward identity in terms of the potential for the Berezin density, and
look at the zero set of the polynomial Bergman kernel and its motion
under holomorphic variation of the second variable. The proofs that the
potential problems indeed characterize the Berezin densities are carried
out in §
4
.
In §
5
, we consider an approximate potential problem
for the asymptotic analysis of the orthogonal polynomials.
Moreover, in §
6
–
7
we supply an
algorithm which obtains a formal solution in the sense of asymptotic expansions
to the potential theoretic problem. We carry this out in detail in the instance
of the Berezin density for the point at infinity, but any off-spectral point
could be analyzed in an analogous fashion.
To make the formal solution rigorous, we estimate the size of the involved quantities
in the asymptotic expansions, and use a standard
\bar{\partial}
-surgery
technique based on Hörmander estimates. This is carried out
in §
8
–
10
.

For the convenience of the reader, we point out that the expansion formula
for the orthogonal polynomials is obtained in Theorem
5.1
and
Theorem
8.2
, see also Lemma
7.3
for the truncation to
a finite asymptotic expansion.

2.
Orthogonal polynomials through a potential problem

2.1.
The exact potential problem

In this section, we explore the potential problem for
planar orthogonal
polynomials, where the orthogonality is taken
with respect to the exponentially varying measure

\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA}

on the plane. We are interested in the asymptotics as
the degree
n
and the parameter
m
grow to infinity
in a proportional fashion:
n=\tau m
. We use the notation
P=P_{m,n}

for the
L^{2}
-normalized orthogonal polynomial, and
\pi=\pi_{m,n}
for its monic
cousin. Here,
\mathrm{dA}=(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{-1}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}\wedge\mathrm{d}z
is the planar area element,
and
Q
is a
C^{2}
–smooth weight function subject to the growth condition

(2.1)

\liminf_{|z|\to+\infty}\frac{Q(z)}{\log|z|}>1,

at infinity.
We refer to §
2.4
below for detailed additional assumptions and
for basic notation.

As in the classical Riemann-Hilbert approach to orthogonal
polynomials on the real line, and also in
[
18
]
,
the current method is based on the observation that the solvability of
a certain system of PDEs determine the
orthogonal polynomial uniquely. This system may be solved
(at least approximately) in a constructive manner,
and the resulting approximate solution
must be close to the orthogonal polynomial in question, in a precise sense
specified below.

The potential problem, which inspires our approach,
reads as follows. We use the notation

\varDelta=\varDelta_{z}=\partial_{z}\bar{\partial}_{z}=\frac{1}{4}(\partial^{2}_{x}+\partial^{2}_{y})

for the quarter Laplacian, where

\partial=\partial_{z}
and
\bar{\partial}=\bar{\partial}_{z}
are the standard Wirtinger
derivatives, defined below in (
2.8
),
and where
z=x+\mathrm{i}y
.

Problem 2.1
.

Determine a pair
(\mathscr{U}_{0},\pi)
of functions on
\mathbb{C}
such that

\mathscr{U}_{0}
is real-valued, and such that

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\varDelta\mathscr{U}_{0}(z)=|\pi(z)|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(z)},\\
\bar{\partial}\pi\equiv 0,\\
\pi^{-1}\partial\,\mathscr{U}_{0}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{C}),\end{cases}

with asymptotic behavior

(2.3)

\big(\mathscr{U}_{0}(z),\pi(z)\big)=\big(\kappa^{2}\log|z|^{2}+\mathrm{O}(|z|^{-1}),\,z^{n}(1+\mathrm{O}(|z|^{-1})\big)

as
|z|\to+\infty
, for some positive constant
\kappa=\kappa_{m,n}
.

We have the following elementary observation.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
m
is sufficiently large and if
n\leq m
,
Problem
2.1
admits a unique solution
(\mathscr{U}_{0},\pi)
.
The function
\pi=\pi_{m,n}
is then the
n
-th monic orthonormal
polynomial in
L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA})
, and
\mathscr{U}_{0}
is the
associated logarithmic potential

\mathscr{U}_{0}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}g(z,w)\,|\pi_{m,n}(w)|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(w)}\mathrm{dA}(w),

where
g(z,w)=\log|z-w|^{2}
is the fundamental solution for the operator

\varDelta
with respect to the normalized area measure
\mathrm{dA}
.
Moreover, we have that

\kappa_{m,n}^{2}=\lVert\pi_{m,n}\rVert_{2mQ}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\pi_{m,n}(z)|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(z)}\mathrm{dA}(z).

The proof of Proposition
2.2
is rather elementary,
but relies on some computations
which we defer to §
4.1
.
We try to obtain approximate solutions to Problem
2.1
, and
this allows us derive an asymptotic formula for the planar orthogonal polynomials
(see Theorems
5.1
and
8.2
below).
As for the approximate solution,
we will replace the delicate divisibility criterion
\pi^{-1}\partial\,\mathscr{U}_{0}\in C^{2}
,
which implies that the gradient of
\mathscr{U}_{0}
vanishes on the zero set of
\pi
,
by the requirement that the gradient of
\mathscr{U}_{0}
vanishes asymptotically
interiorly of a curve which is characteristic for the problem
(the boundary of the associated droplet).

Introducing the pair
(\mathscr{U}_{1},P)=(\kappa^{-2}\mathscr{U}_{0},\kappa^{-1}\pi)
,
we obtain a solution to the
L^{2}
-normalized problem

(2.4)

\begin{cases}\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}_{1}(z)=|P(z)|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(z)},\\
\bar{\partial}P\equiv 0,\\
P^{-1}\partial\,\mathscr{U}_{1}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{C}),\end{cases}

with asymptotic behavior

(2.5)

\big(\mathscr{U}_{1}(z),P(z)\big)=\big(\log|z|^{2}+\mathrm{O}(|z|^{-1}),\kappa^{-1}z^{n}(1+\mathrm{O}(|z|^{-1})\big)

as
|z|\to+\infty
, where
\kappa=\kappa_{m,n}
is as before.
It then follows
from Proposition
2.2
that
P=\pi/\lVert\pi\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA})}

is the normalized
orthogonal polynomial.

2.2.
Motivation

The study of the asymptotic expansions of planar orthogonal
polynomials for exponentially varying weights
is a rather recent development.
In
[
20
]
, the asymptotic
expansion of the normalized orthogonal polynomial

P_{m,n}
(and hence
\pi_{m,n}
) was found, and this result was used
to establish boundary universality in the random normal matrix model.
Related results on orthogonal polynomials and Bergman kernels were obtained
in
[
22
,
21
]
.
In the work
[
18
]
, the study of planar orthogonal
polynomials was pursued using a
“soft Riemann-Hilbert”-approach, which involves the equation

(2.6)

\bar{\partial}{\Psi}=\overline{\pi}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ},

where we recall that
\pi=\pi_{m,n}
is the
n
-th
monic orthogonal polynomial in the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA})
.

A strong motivation for studying planar orthogonal polynomials
comes from the connection with
random normal matrix theory, see e.g.
[
19
]
,
[
31
]
.
This connection comes from the fact that the
polynomial Bergman kernel

(2.7)

{\rm k}_{m,n}(z,w)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}P_{m,k}(z)\overline{P_{m,k}(w)}

is, up to a weight factor, the correlation kernel for the determinantal
random normal matrix eigenvalue ensemble.
It is natural to analyze the
diagonal restriction

{\rm k}_{m,n}(z,z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|P_{m,k}(z)|^{2},

in view of the connection with the one-point function
(or density of states) in the random normal matrix model,
see, e.g., the introduction of
[
20
]
as well as

[
3
,
4
]
. Studying the diagonal restriction is also motivated by the fact that
it uniquely determines the full kernel

{\rm k}_{m,n}(z,w)
by polarization.
We would like to find a suitable asymptotic expansion for
{\rm k}_{m,n}(z,z)

valid throughout the complex plane. This is well understood in the instance when there is
no polynomial truncation, see, e.g., the works
[
29
,
12
,
30
,
6
,
13
]

of Tian, Catlin, Zelditch, Berman-Berndtsson-Sjöstrand, and Deleporte-Hitrik-Sjöstrand.
In addition, the recent progress in understanding each individual
orthogonal polynomial suggests that such asymptotics may be available also in the truncated case.
To make substantial progress on this issue, we would need to be able to capture each
contribution
|P_{m,k}|^{2}
as well as the effect of summation over the degrees.
This splits the difficulty in two parts: (a) to derive asymptotic expansions of each
contribution
|P_{m,k}|^{2}
, and
(b) to effectively sum these asymptotic expansions
over
k=0,\ldots,n-1
.

Here, we analyze aspect (a) of this program, with an aim to
use this to make progress on (b) later on. In principle, we could just
implement the results from
[
20
]
and
[
18
]
which give
the asymptotics for
P_{m,k}
, take the squared modulus of it for part (a)
and sum the resulting expressions over
k
for (b). This was carried out
in
[
20
]
resulting in near-diagonal asymptotics
which gave rise to a boundary universality result.
A similar approach was recently carried by Ameur-Cronvall
[
1
]

in some other regimes, further away from the diagonal. However, given that we need
to sum the resulting expression
|P_{m,k}|^{2}
over
k
, it is much more
convenient to have a calculus which gives the squared moduli
|P_{m,k}|^{2}
directly.
This is the purpose of the present work.
As for the summation in part (b), given that in many situations integration
is actually easier than summation, one should attempt to find a primitive
with respect to the degree parameter
k
of
|P_{m,k}|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}
. We believe that this is within reach,
and that this primitive function combined with the Euler-MacLaurin summation formula
can be used to approximate the true one-point function.

To characterize the squared modulus
|P_{m,n}|^{2}
,
we appeal to Proposition
2.2
above,
which says that we should study the renormalized version of
Problem
2.1
expressed in (
2.4
) and (
2.5
).
This may be viewed as an extension of the
\bar{\partial}
-approach
to planar orthogonal polynomials, as formulated by
Its and Takhtajan in
[
24
]

and developed in
[
18
]
.
If
\mathscr{V}_{0}=\partial\mathscr{U}_{0}
,
then
\mathscr{V}_{0}
solves

\bar{\partial}\mathscr{V}_{0}=|\pi|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ},

and, given that
\pi^{-1}\mathscr{V}_{0}\in C^{2}
by assumption,
we must have
\mathscr{V}_{0}=\pi\Psi
,
where
\Psi
solves the equation (
2.6
).
Our approach to the study of the
functions
|P_{m,n}|^{2}
is based on
finding approximate solutions of the renormalized version of
Problem
2.1
, see Theorem
8.2

and Corollary
8.3
below
(cf. Proposition
8.1
).
In particular, lifting to the Laplacian allows
us to keep the approach real-valued.

2.4.
Notation

We denote by
\mathrm{dA}
and
\mathrm{ds}
the usual area and length elements
in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, normalized such that
the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
and the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
have unit area and length, respectively.
We recall that we use the “quarter Laplacian”

\varDelta:=\partial\bar{\partial}=\tfrac{1}{4}\big(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}),

where
\partial=\partial_{z}
and

\bar{\partial}=\bar{\partial}_{z}
are the standard Wirtinger
differential operators

(2.8)

\partial=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\partial_{x}-\mathrm{i}\partial_{y}\big),\qquad\bar{\partial}=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\partial_{x}+\mathrm{i}\partial_{y}\big),

where
z=x+\mathrm{i}y
.

Denote by
\mathrm{erf}
the standard error function, given by

\mathrm{erf}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{-t^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}t.

With this normalization, we have the identity
\mathrm{erf}(x)+\mathrm{erf}(-x)=1
,
the derivative is given by
\mathrm{erf}^{\prime}(x)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp(-t^{2}/2)

and we have
\lim_{x\to+\infty}\mathrm{erf}(x)=1
.
We note the bound

(2.9)

0<\mathrm{erf}(x)<(2\pi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}|x|^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/2},

valid for
x<0
, while for
x>0
we have

(2.10)

1-(2\pi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}x^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/2}<\mathrm{erf}(x)<1.

We will denote by
Q
an external “potential”, by which we mean
a
C^{2}
-smooth function
Q:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
, subject to the growth
condition (
2.1
).

We denote by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2mQ}
and

\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle_{2mQ}
the usual norm and sesquilinear
inner product
in the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA})
, respectively.

2.5.
Potential theoretic background

For a parameter
\tau\in(0,1]
, we define the envelope function

(2.11)

\check{Q}_{\tau}(z):=\sup\big\{u(z):u\in\mathrm{SH}_{\tau}(\mathbb{C}),\,u(z)\leq Q(z)\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{C}\big\},

where
\mathrm{SH}_{\tau}(\mathbb{C})
denotes the class of subharmonic
functions which grow at most as
\tau\log|z|+\mathrm{O}(1)
at infinity.
The function
\check{Q}_{\tau}
is
C^{1,1}
-smooth and
subharmonic; see e.g.
[
19
]
.

We let
\mathcal{S}_{\tau}
be the
coincidence set

\mathcal{S}_{\tau}:=\big\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,\check{Q}_{\tau}(z)=Q(z)\big\}.

By a Perron-type argument,
one sees that the function
\check{Q}_{\tau}

is harmonic outside
\mathcal{S}_{\tau}
.
Since
\tau\leq 1
, the set
\mathcal{S}_{\tau}
is compact;
see
[
28
]
.
We will moreover assume that
\mathcal{S}_{\tau}
is a connected set, and that
that the outer boundary, i.e. the boundary
\Gamma_{\tau}

of the unbounded component
\Omega_{\tau}
of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{\tau}
,
is a real-analytically smooth curve.
In addition, we assume that
Q
is strictly subharmonic
(i.e. that
\varDelta Q>0
holds) and real-analytic
in a neighborhood of
\Gamma_{\tau}
, which in particular implies
that other components of the complement

\partial\mathcal{S}_{\tau}\setminus\Gamma_{\tau}
are located at a positive
distance from the curve
\Gamma_{\tau}
.
We denote by
\phi_{\tau}
the standard Riemann mapping of
\Omega_{\tau}

onto the exterior disk
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{e}}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|>1\}
, normalized
so that
\phi_{\tau}(\infty)=\infty
and
\phi_{\tau}^{\prime}(\infty)>0
.

The function
\check{Q}_{\tau}
is harmonic on the complement

\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{\tau}
, so in particular on the unbounded
component
\Omega_{\tau}
.
Under the present assumptions,
the restriction
\check{Q}_{\tau}|_{\Omega_{\tau}}

extends to a harmonic function
\breve{Q}_{\tau}
on a larger domain

\Omega_{\tau,\epsilon}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\rm dist}_{\mathbb{C}}(z,\Omega_{\tau})\leq\epsilon\}

for some small positive
\epsilon
.
Due to well-known properties of the obstacle problem, the function

Q-\breve{Q}_{\tau}
is non-negative on a small neighborhood of
\Omega_{\tau}
,
which we can take to equal
\Omega_{\tau,\epsilon}
without loss of generality.
In fact, we have that

(Q-\breve{Q}_{\tau})(z)\asymp{\rm dist}_{\mathbb{C}}(z,\Gamma_{\tau})^{2}

holds in a neighborhood of
\Gamma_{\tau}
.
There exists a uniquely determined

C^{2}
-smooth square root
V_{\tau}
of
(Q-\breve{Q}_{\tau})(z)

which is positive outside
\Gamma_{\tau}
and negative inside.
The square root is automatically real-analytically smooth
with non-vanishing gradient in a small neighborhood of
\Gamma_{\tau}
,
and
C^{2}
–smooth and strictly positive on
\Omega_{\tau}
.

3.1.
Polynomial Bergman kernels and their zero sets

The polynomial Bergman kernel

\mathrm{k}(z,w)={\rm k}_{m,n}(z,w)
given by (
2.7
)
is a polynomial of degree
\leq n-1
in
z

(and in
\bar{w}
as well, given the symmetry
\mathrm{k}(z,w)=\overline{\mathrm{k}(w,z)}
).
As such, the zero locus

\mathfrak{Z}=\mathfrak{Z}_{m,n}:=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\,\mathrm{k}(z,w)=0\}

is an algebraic variety in the variables
(z,\bar{w})

(and, alternatively, in
(\bar{z},w)
). The slices

\mathfrak{Z}(z)=\mathfrak{Z}_{m,n}(z):=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:\,\mathrm{k}(z,w)=0\}

generically consist of
n-1
points if we count multiplicities, and they move about
holomorphically in
\bar{z}
. When
z
approaches infinity, the zero set

\mathfrak{Z}(z)
converges to

\mathfrak{Z}(\infty)=\mathfrak{Z}_{m,n}(\infty):=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:\,P_{n-1}(w)=0\},

where we write
P_{n-1}=P_{m,n-1}
to simplify the notation. The reason for
this is that we have the convergence

\lim_{|z|\to+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{k}(z,w)}{P_{n-1}(z)}=\overline{P_{n-1}(w)}.

It is an interesting task to study the motion of
\mathfrak{Z}(z)
as the point

z\in\mathbb{C}
varies. The work here as well as in the work
[
22
]
suggests
that for off-spectral points
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathcal{S}
where
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{\tau}
with

\tau=n/m
, the zero set

\mathfrak{Z}(z)
will typically be confined to the droplet, i.e., we should have

\mathfrak{Z}(z)\subset\mathcal{S}
, and then
\mathfrak{Z}(z)
consists of precisely

n-1
points. The only way to have fewer points than
n-1
is to have at least
one point escape to infinity. We could call such points
z
where
\mathfrak{Z}(z)

has fewer than
n-1
points (counting multiplicities)
singular
.
It is easy to see that
z
is singular if and only if the degree of

\mathrm{k}(\cdot,z)
is
<n-1
, and that this is so precisely when

P_{n-1}(z)=0
. So
the singular points form the zero locus of

P_{n-1}
.
Moreover, we know from
[
20
]
and
[
18
]
that under
suitable additional topological and regularity conditions, the zero locus is contained
in the interior of
\mathcal{S}
with some control on the distance to the boundary.
A numerical study of the zero set in the case of the kernel for the elliptic Ginibre ensemble
is illustrated in Figure
1
.

3.2.
The Berezin density at infinity

The
orthogonal polynomial wave function
, given by

|P_{m,n}|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}
, may be interpreted as an instance of the
Berezin density for a polynomial ensemble. To see this, we introduce the
notation
\mathrm{K}(z,w)={\rm K}_{m,n}(z,w)
for the weighted kernel

{\rm K}(z,w)={\rm k}(z,w)\,\mathrm{e}^{-m(Q(z)+Q(w))},

where
\mathrm{k}(z,w)={\rm k}_{m,n}(z,w)
is the polynomial
Bergman kernel (
2.7
),
and define the Berezin (probability) density
B(z,w)=B_{m,n}(z,w)
by
the formula

B(z,w)=\frac{|{\rm K}_{m,n}(z,w)|^{2}}{{\rm K}_{m,n}(z,z)}=\frac{|{\rm k}_{m,n}(z,w)|^{2}}{{\rm k}_{m,n}(z,z)}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(w)}.

A limit case of the Berezin density is

|P_{n-1}(w)|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(w)}=B(\infty,w)\coloneqq\lim_{|z|\to+\infty}B(z,w),

where the expression on the left-hand side is the density which
occurs in our potential problem (
2.4
) with
n
replaced by
n-1
.
The algorithm presented in this work supplies an asymptotic expansion
for the density
B_{m,n}(\infty,\cdot)=|P_{m,n}|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}

and the associated potential
\mathscr{U}_{1}
, where the two are
connected via (
2.4
) and (
2.5
).
It is a natural question to ask whether this asymptotic analysis extends beyond
the instance
z=\infty
. We reflect on this in the subsection below.

3.3.
The Berezin density at a general point

We fix a general point
z\in\mathbb{C}
, and introduce the
Berezin potential

\mathscr{B}(z,\cdot)=\mathscr{B}_{m,n}(z,\cdot)
given by

\mathscr{B}_{m,n}(z,w)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}B_{m,n}(z,\xi)\log|w-\xi|^{2}\mathrm{dA}(\xi).

Then
\mathscr{U}_{1}=\mathscr{B}_{m,n}(\infty,\cdot)
, and the
\partial_{w}
-derivative of the
Berezin potential equals

(3.1)

\partial_{w}\mathscr{B}(z,w)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{B_{m,n}(z,\xi)}{w-\xi}\mathrm{dA}(\xi).

By the reproducing
property of kernels, we find that

(3.2)

\partial_{w}\mathscr{B}(z,w)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{B_{m,n}(z,\xi)}{w-\xi}\mathrm{dA}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{k}(z,z)}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{k}(z,\xi)\,\frac{\mathrm{k}(\xi,z)}{w-\xi}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(\xi)}\mathrm{dA}(\xi)\\
=\frac{1}{\mathrm{k}(z,z)}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{k}(z,\xi)\,\frac{\mathrm{k}(\xi,z)-\mathrm{k}(w,z)}{w-\xi}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(\xi)}\mathrm{dA}(\xi)+\frac{\mathrm{k}(w,z)}{\mathrm{k}(z,z)}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathrm{k}(z,\xi)}{w-\xi}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(\xi)}\mathrm{dA}(\xi)\\
=\frac{1}{\mathrm{k}(z,z)}\,\frac{\mathrm{k}(z,z)-\mathrm{k}(w,z)}{w-z}\,+\frac{\mathrm{k}(w,z)}{\mathrm{k}(z,z)}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathrm{k}(z,\xi)}{w-\xi}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(\xi)}\mathrm{dA}(\xi).

It is our task here to formulate an analogue to Problem 1 which characterizes the Berezin
potential. As a first step, we introduce appropriate notation, and write

\mathfrak{c}(z,w):=(w-z)^{-1}
for the
Cauchy kernel
.
It follows from (
3.2
) that

(3.3)

\frac{\mathrm{k}(z,z)}{\mathrm{k}(w,z)}\big(\partial_{w}\mathscr{B}(z,w)-\mathfrak{c}(z,w)\big)+\mathfrak{c}(z,w)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathrm{k}(z,\xi)}{w-\xi}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(\xi)}\mathrm{dA}(\xi)

which expression defines a smooth function of
w\in\mathbb{C}
. This suggests the following
formulation of the potential problem for the Berezin density:

(3.4)

\begin{cases}\varDelta\mathscr{A}(z,\cdot)=|q|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}-\delta_{z},\\
\bar{\partial}q\equiv 0\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,q(z)>0,\\
q^{-1}\partial\,\mathscr{A}(z,\cdot)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{z\}),\end{cases}

coupled with the asymptotic behavior

(3.5)

\big(\mathscr{A}(z,w),q(w)\big)=\big(\mathrm{O}(|w|^{-1}),\eta_{0}w^{n-1}(1+\mathrm{O}(|w|^{-1})\big)\quad\text{as}\,\,\,|w|\to+\infty,

for some complex parameter
\eta_{0}\neq 0
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The solution to the system (
3.4
)–(
3.5
) exists
uniquely provided that
z
is nonsingular, i.e,
P_{n-1}(z)\neq 0
, and is given by

(\mathscr{A}(z,w),q(w))=\big(\mathscr{B}(z,w)-\log|z-w|^{2},k(z,z)^{-\frac{1}{2}}k(w,z)\big).

The proof of this theorem is deferred to §
4.2
.
The theorem holds for any point
z\in\mathbb{C}
. However, for an off-spectral point

z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathcal{S}
, things are sufficiently similar to the case
z=\infty

worked out here so that we may guess a shape for
(\mathscr{A}(z,\cdot),q)
and
get the algorithm to supply asymptotic expansions.
We do not carry out any of the necessary details here.
We could mention that the analysis should share some features with
the method introduced in
[
22
]
,
where an asymptotic expansion formula for the kernel

\mathrm{k}(w,z)
was found for a fixed off-spectral point
z
.

3.4.
The Berezin density at bulk points

The asymptotic expansion formula for the polynomial Bergman kernel

\mathrm{k}(z,w)=\mathrm{k}_{m,n}(z,w)
for bulk points
z
is more classical.
We recall that in the context,
z\in\mathbb{C}
is a
bulk point
if
z
is in the interior
of the droplet
\mathcal{S}
, and in addition,
\varDelta Q(z)>0
holds at the point.
The Hörmander
\bar{\partial}
-theory allows for localization of the polynomial Bergman
kernel
\mathrm{k}(z,\cdot)
at bulk points, which gives that the kernel is asymptotically
the same as the Bergman kernel without polynomial growth restraint
[
2
]
.
This would not immediately supply the Berezin potential
\mathscr{B}(z,\cdot)
for bulk points
z
,
but it is expected such a theory could be developed.
We hope to be able to pursue this matter elsewhere.

Remark 3.2
.

There are of course points
z
which
are neither off-spectral nor bulk. In principle there are two possible types of such points.
We have the
boundary points

z\in\mathcal{S}
as well as the interior points
z
of the
droplet
\mathcal{S}
where
\varDelta Q(z)=0
. We might refer to the latter as

weak
bulk points. Under some radiality assumptions,
such weak points (or “bulk singularities”) were studied
by Ameur-Seo in
[
5
]
, who found that Mittag-Leffler kernels
appear in the local scaling limit.
There is currently no method available to expand polynomial Bergman
kernels at general weak bulk points, but we expect that
such a theory may be developed, at least in particular instances, depending on the type
of zero of
\varDelta Q
. As for the expansion at regular boundary points, partial insight was
reached in
[
20
]
, but we could ask for asymptotic expansion there as well.

3.5.
The Ward identity in terms of the Berezin potential

We aim to characterize the information content of the Ward identities in terms
of a pointwise identity involving the Berezin potential
\mathscr{B}(z,\cdot)
.
To this end, we let
Q^{\circledast}=Q^{\circledast}_{m,n}

denote the function

Q^{\circledast}(z)=\tau\int_{\mathbb{C}}u_{n}(w)\log|z-w|^{2}\,\mathrm{dA}(w)+c,

where as usual
\tau=n/m
. Here,
u_{n}=u_{m,n}
is the so-called one-point function

(3.6)

u_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{n}\,\mathrm{K}(z,z)=\frac{1}{n}\,\mathrm{k}(z,z)\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(z)},

which is a probability density in
\mathbb{C}
. The constant
c=c_{Q,\tau}
should
only depend on
Q
and
\tau
, and it
is chosen such that
\lim_{m\to+\infty}Q^{\circledast}=\check{Q}
holds.
The Ward identity
\mathbb{E}W_{n}^{+}[v]=0
of Proposition 3.1 in
[
4
]

has an equivalent formulation in terms of the Berezin potential.

Theorem 3.3
.

The Ward identity is equivalent to the near-diagonal behavior

\mathscr{B}(z,w)-\mathscr{B}(z,z)+\Lambda(z)-\Lambda(w)=\mathrm{O}(|z-w|^{2}),

where
\mathscr{B}=\mathscr{B}_{m,n}
and we let
\Lambda=\Lambda_{m,n}
denote the function

\Lambda(z)=2m({Q^{\circledast}}(z)-Q(z))-\log{\rm K}_{m,n}(z,z).

We defer the proof of this statement to later work
[
23
]
,
where we intend to explore
higher order Ward identities in the planar context as well.

Remark 3.4
.

The statements taken from
[
4
]
were formulated for
\tau=1
, i.e.,
m=n
.
To arrive at the more general statement we should replace
Q
by
\tau^{-1}Q

in the statements imported from
[
4
]
.

Remark 3.5
.

An interpretation of Theorem
3.3
is that the Ward identity gives local
perturbation information on the Berezin potential
\mathscr{B}(z,w)
. However, the theorem
does not offer any way of calculating the Berezin potential, so that type of information
should be considered as deeper lying than the Ward identity itself.

4.
Proofs of Proposition
2.2
and Theorem
3.1

4.1.
The proof of Proposition
2.2

We verify that if
(\mathscr{U}_{0},\pi)
is a solution
to Problem
2.1
, then

\pi
is the
n
-th degree monic orthogonal polynomial.
That
\pi
is a monic polynomial of degree
n
follows by a Liouville theorem type
argument. We argue that
\mathscr{U}_{0}
takes the form

(4.1)

\mathscr{U}_{0}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\pi(w)|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(w)}\log|z-w|^{2}\mathrm{dA}(w),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}.

This follows from the fact that

(4.2)

\log|z-w|^{2}=\log|z|^{2}+\log\Big|1-\frac{w}{z}\Big|^{2}=\log|z|^{2}-2\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{w}{z}+\mathrm{O}\bigg(\frac{|w|^{2}}{|z|^{2}}\bigg),

as
|z|\to\infty
while
|w|=\mathrm{o}(|z|)
,
together with the rapid decay of
|\pi|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}
at infinity and a Liouville-type
argument. We observe that the representation (
4.1
) implies the
asymptotics

(4.3)

\mathscr{U}_{0}(z)=\lVert\pi\rVert_{2mQ}^{2}\log|z|^{2}+\mathrm{O}(|z|^{-1})

as
|z|\to+\infty
, which proves that
\kappa^{2}=\lVert\pi\rVert_{2mQ}^{2}
.
We may differentiate the relation (
4.1
) to obtain

(4.4)

\partial\,\mathscr{U}_{0}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{1}{z-w}|\pi(w)|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(w)}\mathrm{dA}(w),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C},

which implies the asymptotic relation

(4.5)

\partial\,\mathscr{U}_{0}(z)=\frac{\kappa^{2}+\mathrm{O}(|z|^{-1})}{z}\qquad\text{as}\quad|z|\to+\infty.

We proceed to verify that the orthogonality relation

(4.6)

\int_{\mathbb{C}}q(z)\overline{\pi(z)}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(z)}\mathrm{dA}(z)=0

holds for any polynomial
q
of degree at most
n-1
.
By assumption,
\partial\mathscr{U}_{0}
has the form

\partial\,\mathscr{U}_{0}=\pi\,\Phi,

where
\Phi
is
C^{2}
-smooth. It follows that

|\pi|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}=\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}_{0}=\bar{\partial}\partial\,\mathscr{U}_{0}=\bar{\partial}\big(\pi\Phi\big)=\pi\bar{\partial}\Phi,

from which we conclude that

\bar{\partial}\Phi=\overline{\pi}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}
.
We may also conclude that

\Phi(z)=\frac{1}{\pi(z)}\partial\,\mathscr{U}_{0}(z)=\frac{\kappa^{2}+\mathrm{O}(|z|^{-1})}{z^{n+1}}\qquad\text{as}\quad|z|\to+\infty.

By a standard argument (see e.g. §1.3 in
[
18
]
), this implies the
desired orthogonality relation (
4.6
).

Conversely, we let
\pi
be the monic orthogonal polynomial of degree
n

in the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA})
, and define
\mathscr{U}_{0}
according to (
4.1
).
By repeating the above argument, it follows that
\mathscr{U}_{0}
has the claimed asymptotic behavior
at infinity, and it only remains to show that

\pi^{-1}\partial\,\mathscr{U}_{0}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{C}).

To this end, we observe that the formula (
4.4
) for
\partial\,\mathscr{U}_{0}

remains valid, and that

\frac{|\pi(w)|^{2}}{z-w}=\overline{\pi(w)}\,\frac{\pi(w)-\pi(z)}{z-w}+\frac{\pi(z)\overline{\pi(w)}}{z-w}.

As a consequence, we obtain

(4.7)

\partial\,\mathscr{U}_{0}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{|\pi(w)|^{2}}{z-w}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(w)}\mathrm{dA}(w)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{\pi(w)}\,\frac{\pi(w)-\pi(z)}{z-w}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(w)}\mathrm{dA}(w)\\
+\pi(z)\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\overline{\pi(w)}}{z-w}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(w)}\mathrm{dA}(w)=\pi(z)\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\overline{\pi(w)}}{z-w}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(w)}\mathrm{dA}(w),

where we use that
(\pi(z)-\pi(w))/(z-w)
is a polynomial of
degree at most
n-1
in
w
.
It follows that

\pi(z)^{-1}\partial\,\mathscr{U}_{0}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\overline{\pi(w)}}{z-w}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(w)}\mathrm{dA}(w),

which expresses a
C^{2}
–smooth function by elliptic regularity.∎

4.2.
The proof of Theorem
3.1

We first check that the proposed solution

(\mathscr{A}(z,\cdot),q)=\big(\mathscr{B}(z,\cdot)-\mathfrak{l}(z,\cdot),k(z,z)^{-\frac{1}{2}}k(\cdot,z)\big)

indeed solves the system (
3.4
)–(
3.5
), where

\mathfrak{l}(z,w):=\log|z-w|^{2}
.
From the definition of the Berezin potential
\mathscr{B}(z,\cdot)
, it is clear that

(4.8)

\varDelta\mathscr{A}(z,\cdot)=\varDelta(\mathscr{B}(z,\cdot)-\mathfrak{l}(z,\cdot))=\varDelta\mathscr{B}(z,\cdot)-\delta_{z}=B_{m,n}(z,\cdot)-\delta_{z}\\
=\frac{|\mathrm{k}(z,\cdot)|^{2}}{\mathrm{k}(z,z)}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}-\delta_{z}=|q|^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}-\delta_{z},

where in the last step, we used the proposed formula for
q
. This settles the first line of
(
3.4
). Next, since
z
is nonsingular, i.e.,
P_{n-1}(z)\neq 0
,
the proposed formula for
q
is a polynomial of
degree precisely
n-1
with point value
q(z)=k(z,z)^{\frac{1}{2}}>0
, which settles the second line of
(
3.4
) as well as the asymptotic growth of
q
in (
3.5
).
By the argument which leads up to (
4.3
), we obtain that

(4.9)

\mathscr{B}(z,w)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{|\mathrm{k}(z,\xi)|^{2}}{\mathrm{k}(z,z)}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(\xi)}\log|w-\xi|^{2}\mathrm{dA}(\xi)\\
=\frac{\|\mathrm{k}(z,\cdot)\|^{2}_{2mQ}}{k(z,z)}\log|w|^{2}+\mathrm{O}(|w|^{-1})=\log|w|^{2}+\mathrm{O}(|w|^{-1})

as
|w|\to+\infty
. Next, since
\log|w|^{2}-\log|z-w|^{2}=\mathrm{O}(|w|^{-1})
, we obtain the required
asymptotic decay of
\mathscr{A}(z,\cdot)
at infinity.
Finally, in view of the identity (
3.3
), the third line of (
3.4
)
follows as well.

It remains to check that the solution to the system (
3.4
)–(
3.5
)
is unique. We observe that according to the second line of (
3.4
),

q
is entire, and according to the growth bound in (
3.5
),
q
must be a
polynomial of degree equal to
n-1
, as the leading coefficient equals
\eta_{0}\neq 0
. Next, we put

(4.10)

\mathscr{A}^{\circledast}(z,w):=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|q(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(\xi)}\log|w-\xi|^{2}\mathrm{dA}(\xi)-\log|z-w|^{2},

and, by the argument which gives (
4.3
), we find that

\mathscr{A}^{\circledast}(z,w):=\|q\|^{2}_{2mQ}\log|w|^{2}-\log|z-w|^{2}=(\|q\|^{2}_{2mQ}-1)\log|w|^{2}+\mathrm{O}(|w|^{-1}),

as
|w|\to+\infty
. A calculation gives that

\varDelta\mathscr{A}^{\circledast}(z,\cdot)=|q|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}-\delta_{z},

which is the same as
\varDelta\mathscr{A}(z,\cdot)
. As a consequence,

H:=\mathscr{A}(z,\cdot)-\mathscr{A}^{\circledast}(z,\cdot)
is harmonic, and given the decay of

\mathscr{A}(z,\cdot)
required by (
3.5
), it follows from the above bound that

H(w)=(1-\|q\|^{2}_{2mQ})\log|w|^{2}+\mathrm{O}(|w|^{-1})

holds as
|w|\to+\infty
. A Liouville-type argument for harmonic functions now applies to
show that such weak growth forces
H
to be constant, and in a second step, that

\|q\|_{2mQ}=1
and
H(w)\equiv 0
. We conclude that
\mathscr{A}(z,w)=\mathscr{A}^{\circledast}(z,w)
,
which allows us to differentiate (
4.10
) to obtain

(4.11)

\partial_{w}\mathscr{A}(z,w)=\partial_{w}\mathscr{A}^{\circledast}(z,w):=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{|q(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(\xi)}}{w-\xi}\mathrm{dA}(\xi)-\frac{1}{w-z}=\mathrm{O}(|w|^{-2}).

Let us write

\mathscr{X}(z,\cdot)=q^{-1}\partial\mathscr{A}(z,\cdot),

which by assumption is
C^{2}
-smooth in the whole punctured plane
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{z\}
.
Away from the zeros of
q
, we have

(4.12)

\bar{\partial}\mathscr{X}(z,\cdot)=\bar{\partial}q^{-1}\partial\mathscr{A}(z,\cdot)=q^{-1}\bar{\partial}\partial\mathscr{A}(z,\cdot)q^{-1}\varDelta\mathscr{A}(z,\cdot)\\
=q^{-1}(|q|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}-\delta_{z})=\bar{q}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}-q(z)^{-1}\delta_{z},

and since
\mathscr{X}(z,\cdot)
is
C^{2}
-smooth in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{z\}
according to
the third line of (
3.4
), and
q(z)>0
holds at the point, this equality
holds in the sense of distribution theory in the whole plane. Next, since
q
has degree
precisely equal to
n-1
, we have the decay

(4.13)

\mathscr{X}(z,w):=\mathrm{O}(|w|^{-n-1})\quad\text{as}\,\,\,|w|\to+\infty.

If we put

(4.14)

\mathscr{X}^{\circledast}(z,w):=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\bar{q}(\xi)\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(\xi)}}{w-\xi}\,\mathrm{dA}(\xi)-\frac{q(z)^{-1}}{w-z},

an elementary estimates gives that
\mathscr{X}^{\circledast}(z,w)=\mathrm{O}(|w|^{-1})

as
|w|\to+\infty
. But then the entire function

\mathscr{X}(z,\cdot)-\mathscr{X}^{\circledast}(z,\cdot)
has to decay as well, which is only
possible if it vanishes. So
\mathscr{X}^{\circledast}(z,\cdot)=\mathscr{X}(z,\cdot)
as functions.
By finite geometric series expansion of the Cauchy kernel
\mathfrak{c}(z,w)=(w-z)^{-1}
,
as in §1.3 in
[
18
]
, it is possible to show that the decay
(
4.13
) entails that

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\xi^{j}q(z)\overline{q(\xi)}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ(\xi)}\mathrm{dA}(\xi)=z^{j},\qquad j=0,\ldots,n-1.

This is the reproducing property in the space of polynomials of degree
\leq n-1
, which
determines the function
q(z)\overline{q(\xi)}
uniquely:

q(z)\overline{q(\xi)}=\mathrm{k}(z,\xi).

Then
q(z)=\mathrm{k}(z,z)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
q(w)=\mathrm{k}(z,z)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{k}(w,z)
,
as claimed. In view of this, the formula giving
\mathscr{A}(z,w)
is immediate from (
4.10
).
∎

5.
An approximate potential problem for orthogonal polynomials

5.1.
Heuristic derivation of an approximate potential problem

To reduce the quantity of indices, we usually drop indication of the
parameters
m,n
and
\tau=m/n
.
Hence, we write in the sequel

\mathcal{S}
,
\Omega
,
\Omega_{\epsilon}
,
\Gamma
,
P
, and
V
.

We return to the potential problem for the orthogonal polynomials.
We will work with an approximate version of the renormalized version of
Problem
2.1
,
which is more robust. In particular,
the delicate divisibility condition
P^{-1}\partial\,\mathscr{U}_{1}\in C^{2}

will be replaced by a decay condition in the interior
direction of the droplet, which allows us to introduce
a cut-off function at little cost and ignore the
interior completely.

We will also work with the
L^{2}
-normalized orthogonal polynomial rather than the monic
polynomial
\pi=\pi_{m,n}
, and we denote it by
P=P_{m,n}
.
By now it is understood that
(see e.g.
[
20
]
,
[
18
]
), to leading order

P_{m,n}\sim c_{m,n}\phi^{n}\mathrm{e}^{m\mathscr{Q}}F,

where
c_{m,n}
is a constant and where
\mathscr{Q}
and
F

are holomorphic and bounded on
\Omega_{\epsilon}
, and where in addition

F
is bounded away from zero. The function

\mathscr{Q}=\mathscr{Q}_{\tau}

satisfies
\mathrm{Re}\,\mathscr{Q}_{\tau}=Q
on

\Gamma=\Gamma_{\tau}
, so that

\tau\log|\phi_{\tau}|+\mathrm{Re}\,\mathscr{Q}_{\tau}=\breve{Q}_{\tau},

and
F
should admit an asymptotic expansion in non-negative powers
of
m^{-1}
. We may consequently conclude that

|P|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\sim c_{m,n}^{2}|F|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mV^{2}}

holds near
\Gamma
,
where we recall that
V^{2}=Q-\breve{Q}
holds there.
Since
F
is zero-free and holomorphic on
\Omega_{\epsilon}
,
it follows that
\log|F|
is harmonic there, which allows us to write

F=\tilde{c}_{m,n}\mathrm{e}^{h+\mathrm{i}\tilde{h}},\qquad\text{where }\;\tilde{c}_{m,n}=c_{m,n}^{-1}m^{\frac{1}{4}},

where
h
is a real-valued bounded harmonic function, while

\tilde{h}
is the harmonic conjugate of
h
, normalized
to vanish at infinity.
In terms of the function
h
, we expect

|P|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\sim m^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{2h-2mV^{2}}

to hold in a suitably approximate sense.
It is also convenient to relax the above renormalized version of
Problem
2.1
somewhat,
by asking that the potential, now denoted
\mathscr{U}
, satisfies the
weaker growth condition

\mathscr{U}(z)=\log|z|^{2}+\mathrm{O}(1)

at infinity. The solution
(\mathscr{U},P)
is no longer unique, but the non-uniqueness
is only up to an additive constant in the potential, i.e. the solution space
is
\{(\mathscr{U}_{1}+a,P):a\in\mathbb{R}\}
, where
(\mathscr{U}_{1},P)
is the unique solution to the
renormalized version of Problem
2.1
expressed in (
2.4
)
and (
2.5
). Since
\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{U}_{1}+a
, we have

(5.1)

\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}=\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}_{1}=|P|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\sim m^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{2h-2mV^{2}}.

The freedom of adding a constant to
\mathscr{U}_{1}
will allow
us to ensure that
\mathscr{U}
decays rapidly in the interior direction to
the droplet
\mathcal{S}

at
\Gamma
, and this effectively replaces the divisibility condition

P^{-1}\partial\mathscr{U}_{1}=P^{-1}\partial\mathscr{U}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{C})
(given that we are
convinced that the zeros are located further into the interior of
\mathcal{S}
).
The decay in the interior direction is encoded by the ansatz

(5.2)

\mathscr{U}\sim H\operatorname{erf}(2\sqrt{m}V)+\frac{G}{\sqrt{2\pi m}}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}

where
H
is harmonic while
G
is
smooth, both real-valued. The function
H
should be defined on the whole of

\Omega_{\epsilon}=\Omega_{\tau,\epsilon}
and have the appropriate growth

(5.3)

H(z)=\log|z|^{2}+\mathrm{O}(1)

at infinity, but
G
only needs to be defined
in a small neighborhood of
\Gamma
. Away from
\Gamma
,
it is heavily suppressed
by the Gaussian
\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}
anyway.
The principal properties of these functions
are that they admit asymptotic expansions

(5.4)

H=\widehat{H}_{0}+m^{-1}\widehat{H}_{1}+\ldots,\qquad G=\widehat{G}_{0}+m^{-1}\widehat{G}_{1}+\ldots

with harmonic and smooth coefficient functions
\widehat{H}_{j}
and
\widehat{G}_{j}
, respectively,
which for fixed
\tau
do not depend on the parameter
m
.
We stress that the function
V
is not yet uniquely determined apart
from in a neighborhood of the curve
\Gamma
, but
V^{2}
is supposed
to be positive throughout

\Omega_{\epsilon}\setminus\Gamma
.

We proceed to solve the problem

(5.5)

\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}\sim m^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{2h-2mV^{2}}

algorithmically, where
\mathscr{U}
takes the form (
5.2
)
while
h
admits an asymptotic expansion

(5.6)

h=\widehat{h}_{0}+m^{-1}\widehat{h}_{1}+m^{-2}\widehat{h}_{2}+\ldots

with bounded harmonic coefficient functions
\{\widehat{h}_{j}\}_{j}
which
do not depend on
m
.
The point is that if a pair
(\mathscr{U},h)
can be found such that
(
5.1
) holds up to a small error, then we obtain a
good approximation of the wave function
|P|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}
.

We do not impose any convergence properties on the asymptotic
expansions (
5.4
) and (
5.6
).
At first, we will focus on algorithmic aspects
and produce formal solutions to (
5.5
) at the
level of asymptotic expansions.
Later on, we will control the error terms that are introduced
by suitably truncating the asymptotic expansions.

5.2.
A preliminary version of the main result

A preliminary version of the main result
reads as follows. We work under the standing assumptions of
§
2.5
.

Theorem 5.1
.

There exist
(i)
harmonic functions
H\sim\widehat{H}_{0}+\frac{1}{m}\widehat{H}_{1}+\ldots
with
growth
H(z)=\log|z|+O(1)
as
|z|\to+\infty
,

(ii)
smooth functions
G\sim\widehat{G}_{0}+\frac{1}{m}\widehat{G}_{1}+\ldots
and
(iii)
bounded harmonic
functions
h\sim\widehat{h}_{0}+\frac{1}{m}\widehat{h}_{1}+\ldots
,
such that if
\mathscr{U}
is given by

\mathscr{U}=H\operatorname{erf}(2\sqrt{m}V)+\frac{G}{\sqrt{2\pi m}}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}},

it holds that

\varDelta\mathscr{U}\sim m^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2h-2mV^{2}}.

As a consequence of this approximate potential equation,
the
L^{2}
-normalized orthogonal polynomial
P=P_{m,n}
satisfies

|P|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\sim m^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{2h-2mV^{2}},

which in turn leads to an asymptotic expansion for
P
.

The coefficient functions in the various asymptotic expansions
get uniquely determined by a nonlinear algorithm which takes the form
of a fixed-point equation. The symbol
\sim
denotes equality of
asymptotic expansions. By suitably truncating the asymptotic expansions,
we obtain an error term of order
\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}
with
c_{0}>0
, valid in a neighborhood
of width
\asymp m^{-\frac{1}{4}}
of
\overline{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{\tau}}
.
A more precise statement of this theorem
is contained in Theorem
8.2

below.

6.
Derivation of the master equation

6.1.
The ansatz and its consequences

We make the ansatz

(6.1)

\mathscr{U}=H\operatorname{erf}(2\sqrt{m}V)+\frac{G}{\sqrt{2\pi m}}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}},

where
G
and
H
are as above, with asymptotic expansions
given by (
5.4
). This means that
\mathscr{U}
is no longer
considered as an exact solution to the above Laplace equation
(
5.1
), but only in an approximate sense.
Taking the
\bar{\partial}
-derivative of the ansatz, we find that

(6.2)

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}\,\mathscr{U}

\displaystyle=\bar{\partial}H\operatorname{erf}(2\sqrt{m}V)+\sqrt{\frac{2m}{\pi}}\bar{\partial}V(H-2VG)\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}+\frac{\bar{\partial}G}{\sqrt{2\pi m}}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}

(6.3)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2m}{\pi}}\bar{\partial}VE\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}+\bar{\partial}H\operatorname{erf}(2\sqrt{m}V)+\frac{\bar{\partial}G}{\sqrt{2\pi m}}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}},

where we have introduced the simplifying notation

(6.4)

E=H-2VG.

Taking the
\partial
-derivative of the above expression,
we arrive at

(6.5)

\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}=\partial\bar{\partial}\,\mathscr{U}=-2^{\frac{5}{2}}m^{\frac{3}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}V|\partial V|^{2}\,E\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}\\
+2^{\frac{1}{2}}m^{\frac{1}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big\{\partial V(\bar{\partial}H-2V\bar{\partial}G)+\partial\big(E\bar{\partial}V\big)\Big\}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}+\frac{\varDelta G}{\sqrt{2\pi m}}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}.

By the product rule, we have the identity

-2V\partial V\bar{\partial}G=-2\partial V\bar{\partial}(GV)+2|\partial V|^{2}G,

which gives that

\partial V(\bar{\partial}H-2V\bar{\partial}G)=\partial V\bar{\partial}E+2|\partial V|^{2}G.

Combining this with (
6.5
) we find that

(6.6)

\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}=-2^{\frac{5}{2}}m^{\frac{3}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}V|\partial V|^{2}\,E\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}\\
+2^{\frac{1}{2}}m^{\frac{1}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big\{\partial V\bar{\partial}E+2|\partial V|^{2}G+\partial E\bar{\partial}V+E\varDelta V\Big\}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}+\frac{\varDelta G}{\sqrt{2\pi m}}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}.

6.2.
The master equation

As we want to solve the equation

(6.7)

\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}=m^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2h-2mV^{2}},

we are led to consider

(6.8)

\bigg\{-2^{\frac{5}{2}}m^{\frac{3}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}V|\partial V|^{2}\,E+2^{\frac{1}{2}}m^{\frac{1}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(2\mathrm{Re}\,\{\partial V\bar{\partial}E\}+2|\partial V|^{2}G+E\varDelta V\Big)\\
+\frac{\varDelta G}{\sqrt{2\pi m}}\bigg\}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}=m^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{2h-2mV^{2}},

where we recall that
h
is supposed to be a bounded harmonic
function with an asymptotic expansion of the form (
5.6
).
This should hold in the sense of equalities of formal
asymptotic expansions in powers of
m^{-1}
,
on a neighborhood
of the outer boundary curve
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\tau}
.
In terms of the functions
E
,
H
,
G
and
h
, the equation
(
6.8
) posits that

(6.9)

-4mV|\partial V|^{2}\,E+2\mathrm{Re}\,\big\{\partial V\bar{\partial}E\big\}+E\varDelta V+2|\partial V|^{2}G+\tfrac{1}{2m}\varDelta G=\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2h}

should hold in the sense of equality of asymptotic expansions.
We recall that we require that
h
is a real-valued bounded harmonic function
on the fattened exterior domain
\Omega_{\epsilon}
.

7.
Solving the master equation

In this section (and in the sequel) we will use the notation

\vartheta=m^{-1}
. While we will specialize to
\vartheta>0
in the end,
it is useful to allow the parameter
\vartheta
to take on complex values. Restated
in terms of
\vartheta
, the master equation (
6.9
) becomes

(7.1)

-4\vartheta^{-1}V|\partial V|^{2}\,E+2\mathrm{Re}\,\big\{\partial V\bar{\partial}E\big\}+E\varDelta V+2|\partial V|^{2}G+\frac{\vartheta}{2}\varDelta G=\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2h}.

7.1.
Reduction of the master equation: Step 1

We assume that the equation (
7.1
) admits a formal
solution tuple
(E,G,h)
, where
E
takes the form
E=\widehat{E}_{0}+\vartheta\widehat{E}_{1}+\ldots
and where

(G,H,h)
are given by (
5.4
)–(
5.6
) with
m
replaced by
\vartheta^{-1}
.
If we multiply (
6.9
) by
\vartheta
and evaluate at
\vartheta=0
, we obtain

4V|\partial V|^{2}\,\widehat{E}_{0}=0.

Given that this relation needs to hold in a neighborhood of the curve

\Gamma
and since
|\partial V|
does not vanish there, we find that
the leading order coefficient

\widehat{E}_{0}
of
E
must
vanish identically in a neighborhood of
\Gamma
:

(7.2)

\widehat{E}_{0}=0.

It is thus convenient to work with the function
\mathcal{E}:=\vartheta^{-1}E
, where
\mathcal{E}

should admit an asymptotic expansion

(7.3)

\mathcal{E}=\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}+\vartheta\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1}+\vartheta^{2}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{2}+\ldots,

rather than with
E
. If we rewrite (
7.1
)
as an equation for
(\mathcal{E},H,G,h)
, we obtain

(7.4)

-4V|\partial V|^{2}\,\mathcal{E}+2\vartheta\mathrm{Re}\,\big\{\partial V\bar{\partial}\mathcal{E}\big\}+\vartheta\mathcal{E}\varDelta V+2|\partial V|^{2}G+\tfrac{1}{2m}\varDelta G=\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2h}.

As
\vartheta\mathcal{E}=H-2VG
and since
V
vanishes on
\Gamma
, we have

(H-\vartheta\mathcal{E})|_{\Gamma}=0
. Given that we know that
H
meets the growth
bound (
5.3
), we obtain the formula

(7.5)

H=\log|\phi|^{2}+\vartheta\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}].

Here, we recall that
\phi
is the
conformal mapping of
\Omega
onto the exterior disk

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{e}}
, and denote by

\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}[f]=\int_{\Gamma}P_{\Omega}(z,w)f(w)\mathrm{ds}(w)

the usual Poisson extension operator for
\Omega
, given
in terms of the Poisson kernel

P_{\Omega}
for the domain
\Omega
.
For sufficiently regular functions
f
, this
means that
\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}[f]
is the bounded harmonic extension of

f
to
\Omega
, and if
f
is real-analytic
\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}[f]
extends
harmonically past
\Gamma
in the interior direction. If
\epsilon

is small enough we may assume that
\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}[E]
extends harmonically
to the whole fattened exterior domain
\Omega_{\epsilon}
.
The relation
\vartheta\mathcal{E}=H-2VG
backwards gives that

(7.6)

G=\frac{H-\vartheta\mathcal{E}}{2V}=\frac{\vartheta(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}-\mathbf{I})[\mathcal{E}]+\log|\phi|^{2}}{2V},

where we observe that the above ratio is smooth in view of Weierstrass’
preparation theorem.
The reason is that the numerator is a real-analytic function which
vanishes along
\Gamma
, while
V
has non-vanishing gradient
along
\Gamma
.
This allows us to get rid of the function
G
in the
equation (
6.9
), so that

(7.7)

-4V|\partial V|^{2}\,\mathcal{E}+2\vartheta\mathrm{Re}\,\big(\partial V\bar{\partial}\mathcal{E}\big)+\vartheta\mathcal{E}\varDelta V+\frac{|\partial V|^{2}}{V}\big(\vartheta(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}-\mathbf{I})\mathcal{E}+\log|\phi|^{2}\big)\\
+\frac{\vartheta}{4}\varDelta\frac{\vartheta(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}-\mathbf{I})\mathcal{E}+\log|\phi|^{2}}{V}=\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2h}.

7.2.
Reduction of the master equation: Step 2

In terms of the linear operator

(7.8)

\mathbf{S}[f]=\mathbf{S}_{\vartheta}[f]:=\partial V\bar{\partial}f+\bar{\partial}V\partial f+f\varDelta V+|\partial V|^{2}\frac{(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}-\mathbf{I})f}{V}+\frac{\vartheta}{4}\varDelta\frac{(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}-\mathbf{I})f}{V},

and the expression

(7.9)

L:=2|\partial V|^{2}\frac{\log|\phi|}{V}+\frac{\vartheta}{2}\varDelta\frac{\log|\phi|}{V},

the equation (
7.7
) reads, given that
\mathcal{E}
is real-valued,

(7.10)

-4V|\partial V|^{2}\mathcal{E}+\vartheta\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]+L=\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2h}.

Hence, since
V
vanishes on
\Gamma
, the equation for
\mathcal{E}
and
h
becomes

L+\vartheta\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]=\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2h}\qquad\text{on }\;\,\Gamma,

which is equivalent to the equation

\log(L+\vartheta\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}])=2h+\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\pi}{2}\qquad\text{on }\;\,\Gamma

so that
h
is determined from
E
by

(7.11)

h=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(L+\vartheta\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}])\big]-\frac{1}{4}\log\frac{\pi}{2},

provided that
L+\vartheta\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]>0
. We recall
\mathcal{E}
should admit a full asymptotic expansion
(
7.3
) with leading order behavior
\mathcal{E}=\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}+\mathrm{O}(\vartheta)
,
and it is clear that
L>0
holds on
\Gamma
when
|\vartheta|

is sufficiently small. Hence,

L+\vartheta\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]>0
will be satisfied on
\Gamma

for sufficiently small
|\vartheta|
.
In view of the relation (
7.11
), we
may rid the master equation of the dependence on
h
as well,
and we obtain the following non-linear equation for
\mathcal{E}
:

(7.12)

-4V|\partial V|^{2}\mathcal{E}+\Big\{L+\vartheta\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]-\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(L+\vartheta\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}])\big]\Big)\Big\}=0.

The equation (
7.12
) may equivalently be stated as

(7.13)

\mathcal{E}=\frac{1}{4V|\partial V|^{2}}\Big\{L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]-\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}])\big]\Big)\Big\}

where we note that the division by
V
does not produce any
singularity, as the expression in brackets vanishes along
\Gamma
.
This is, as before, related with the Weierstrass division theorem
(see, e.g., Section 6.1 in
[
20
]
and Section 5.5 in
[
18
]
).
Moreover, the division by
|\partial V|^{2}
is harmless, since
\partial V\neq 0

in a neighborhood of the curve
\Gamma
.

7.3.
Solution in terms of an asymptotic expansion

We proceed to outline an algorithm which will determine a formal solution
\mathcal{E}

to (
7.13
) in the form of an asymptotic expansion (
7.3
) in
\vartheta
.
In the proofs below, we will take an alternative approach to deriving approximate solutions.
Here, we focus on explaining the ideas, and suppress the technical details for the reader’s convenience.

The term
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}

The main term
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
is obtained by evaluating both sides of (
7.13
)
at
\vartheta=0
. This gives

(7.14)

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}=\mathcal{E}\big|_{\vartheta=0}=\frac{1}{4V|\partial V|^{2}}\Big\{\widehat{L}_{0}-\exp\big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log\widehat{L}_{0}\big]\big)\Big\},

where we use the notation
\widehat{L}_{0}=L|_{\vartheta=0}
.
Since
\widehat{L}_{0}-\exp(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}[\log\widehat{L}_{0}])
vanishes along
\Gamma

and, the
division by
V
does not produce any singularity, and, since
\partial V\neq 0
on
\Gamma
the term

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
becomes real-analytic on some neighborhood of
\Gamma
.

The term
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1}

In order to obtain
the coefficient function
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
, we apply
Taylor expansion in
\vartheta
in equation (
7.13
).
In particular, the expansion of the left-hand side is

(7.15)

\mathcal{E}=\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}+\vartheta\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1}+\mathrm{O}(|\vartheta|^{2}),

where, for formal Taylor series
f
and
g
,
the notation
f=g+\mathrm{O}(\vartheta^{k+1})
means that the Taylor coefficients of
f
and
g

agree up to order
k
.
If we introduce the notation
L=\widehat{L}_{0}+\vartheta\widehat{L}_{1}
and
\mathbf{S}=\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{0}+\vartheta\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{1}

where
\widehat{L}_{j}
and
\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{j}
do not depend on
\vartheta
(cf. (
7.8
) and (
7.9
)),
the expression in brackets on the right-hand side of (
7.13
) becomes

(7.16)

L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]-\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}])\big]\Big)\\
=\widehat{L}_{0}+\vartheta(\widehat{L}_{1}+\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]\big)-\mathrm{e}^{\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log\widehat{L}_{0}}\exp\big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log\big(1+\vartheta\tfrac{\widehat{L}_{1}+\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]}{\widehat{L}_{0}}\big)\big)\\
=\widehat{L}_{0}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log\widehat{L}_{0}}+\vartheta(\widehat{L}_{1}+\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{0}[\mathcal{E}]+\vartheta\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{1}[\mathcal{E}]\big)\\
-\mathrm{e}^{\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log\widehat{L}_{0}}\Big[\exp\big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log\big(1+\vartheta\tfrac{\widehat{L}_{1}+\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{0}[\mathcal{E}]+\vartheta\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{1}[\mathcal{E}]}{\widehat{L}_{0}}\big)\big)-1\Big].

Inserting the expansion (
7.3
) of
\mathcal{E}
and Taylor expanding the logarithm to first order,
we obtain the Taylor expansion

(7.17)

L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]-\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}])\big]\Big)\\
=\widehat{L}_{0}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log\widehat{L}_{0}}+\vartheta\Big(\widehat{L}_{1}+\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{0}[\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}]-\mathrm{e}^{\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log\widehat{L}_{0}}\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\Big[\tfrac{\widehat{L}_{1}+\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{0}[\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}]}{\widehat{L}_{0}}\Big]\Big)+\mathrm{O}(|\vartheta|^{2}).

We hence see that
the the equality (
7.13
) up to terms of order 1 in
\vartheta
amounts to

(7.18)

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1}=\frac{1}{4V|\partial V|^{2}}\Big(\widehat{L}_{1}+\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{0}[\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}]-\mathrm{e}^{\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log\widehat{L}_{0}}\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\Big[\tfrac{\widehat{L}_{1}+\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{0}[\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}]}{\widehat{L}_{0}}\Big]\Big).

On the curve
\Gamma
, we have

\widehat{L}_{1}+\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{0}[\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}]-\mathrm{e}^{\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log\widehat{L}_{0}}\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\Big[\tfrac{\widehat{L}_{1}+\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{0}[\widehat{E}_{0}]}{\widehat{L}_{0}}\Big]=\widehat{L}_{1}+\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{0}[\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}]-\mathrm{e}^{\log\widehat{L}_{0}}\tfrac{\widehat{L}_{1}+\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{0}[\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}]}{\widehat{L}_{0}}=0,

which means that we may divide smoothly by
V|\partial V|^{2}
in (
7.18
),
so
\mathcal{E}_{1}
becomes real-analytic
on a neighborhood of
\Gamma
.

The general term

We now describe how to obtain the coefficient functions
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{j}

for
j\geq 2
. As we enter the general step in the algorithm,
we have determined the coefficient functions
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0},\ldots,\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{k}

for some given positive integer
k
.
In view of (
7.13
), the next coefficient,
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{k+1}
,
must be equal to the
(k+1)
-st Taylor coefficient of

\frac{1}{4V|\partial V|^{2}}\Big\{L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]-\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}])\big]\Big)\Big\}.

We will refrain from writing the explicit formula for
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{k+1}
, but
we should explain that it may be expressed in terms of the known coefficient
functions
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{j}
for
j\leq k
, the operators
\mathbf{S}
and
\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}

and the function
L
.
To see this, we first introduce the notation

\mathcal{E}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\vartheta^{j}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{j}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\vartheta^{j}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{j}=:\mathcal{E}^{\rm I}+\vartheta^{k+1}\mathcal{E}^{\rm II},

where we stress that
\mathcal{E}^{{\rm I}}
is considered
known
at this stage,
and find that

(7.19)

L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]-\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}])\big]\Big)\\
=L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]-\mathrm{e}^{\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log\widehat{L}_{0}}\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(1+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]/\widehat{L}_{0})\big]\Big)\\
=L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]-\mathrm{e}^{\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log\widehat{L}_{0}}+\mathrm{e}^{\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log\widehat{L}_{0}}\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log\big(1+\,\vartheta(\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}^{\rm I}]+\vartheta^{k+2}\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}^{\rm II}])/\widehat{L}_{0}\big)\big]\Big).

Next, we rewrite the expression in the exponent on the right-hand side as

(7.20)

\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(1+\,\big(\vartheta\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}^{\rm I}]+\vartheta^{k+2}\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}^{\rm II}]\big)/\widehat{L}_{0})\big]\\
=\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\Big[\log\big(1+\,\vartheta\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}^{\rm I}]/\widehat{L}_{0}\big)+\log\Big(1+\vartheta^{k+2}\frac{\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}^{\rm II}]}{\widehat{L}_{0}+\vartheta\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}^{\rm I}]}\Big)\Big]\\
=\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\Big[\log\big(1+\,\vartheta\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}^{\rm I}]/\widehat{L}_{0}\big)\Big]+\mathrm{O}(\vartheta^{k+2}),

where the last equality holds since
\widehat{L}_{0}
is non-zero on
\Gamma

for sufficiently small
\vartheta
. As a consequence, we obtain that

(7.21)

L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]-\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}])\big]\Big)\\
=L+\vartheta\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}^{\rm I}]-\mathrm{e}^{\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log\widehat{L}_{0}}\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(1+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}^{\rm I}]/\widehat{L}_{0})\big]\Big)+\mathrm{O}(\vartheta^{k+2}),

which shows that the
(k+1)
-st Taylor coefficient of the right-hand side of the
master equation (
7.13
)
only depends on
\mathcal{E}^{\rm I}
; and not on
the higher order terms contained in
\mathcal{E}^{\rm II}
.
Combining (
7.13
) with (
7.21
), we obtain the structural
formula

(7.22)

\mathcal{E}_{k+1}=\frac{1}{4V|\partial V|^{2}}\mathfrak{F}_{k}(\mathcal{E}_{0},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{k})

where the expression
\mathfrak{F}_{k}(\mathcal{E}_{0},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{k})
can be computed in terms of known functions.
As in the previous steps, the fact that

L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}]-\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}])\big]\Big)=0\quad\text{on }\,\Gamma

ensures
\mathfrak{F}_{k}
vanishes on
\Gamma
, and no singularity is created
when we divide by
V|\partial V|^{2}
in (
7.22
) with
j=k+1
.
Hence,
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{k+1}
is real-analytic in a neighborhood of
\Gamma
.

By induction, the above algorithm produces a sequence
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0},\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1},\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{2},\ldots
of coefficient
functions, so that
\mathcal{E}=\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}+\vartheta\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1}+\vartheta^{2}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{2}+\ldots
supplies a formal
solution to (
7.13
).

7.4.
The asymptotic expansion of the coefficient functions

We recall the equations (
7.5
), (
7.6
)
and (
7.11
) for the remaining coefficient functions involved
in the approximate potential equation (
6.9
),
which express
H
,
G
and
h
in terms of

E=\vartheta\mathcal{E}
. With
\mathcal{E}
denoting the formal asymptotic obtained
expansion in the previous section, we let
\mathcal{E}^{\approx}

denote a suitable approximation. For instance, we may choose
to truncate the series at a level
k=k_{\vartheta}

\mathcal{E}^{\approx}=\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}+\vartheta\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1}+\ldots+\vartheta^{k}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{k}.

It turns out to be convenient for estimates to do things somewhat differently
and only truncate at the end, but the approximation
\mathcal{E}^{\approx}
will
have a growing number of coefficients equal to
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{j}
;
we discuss this more in the following section.
Given
\mathcal{E}^{\approx}
, we put

(7.23)

H^{\approx}=\log|\phi|^{2}+\vartheta\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}[\mathcal{E}^{\approx}],\qquad G^{\approx}=\frac{H^{\approx}-\vartheta\mathcal{E}^{\approx}}{2V}

and finally

(7.24)

h^{\approx}=\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log\big(L+\vartheta\mathbf{S}[\mathcal{E}^{\approx}])-\frac{1}{4}\log\frac{\pi}{2}.

The function
h^{\approx}
admits an asymptotic expansion
h^{\approx}=\widehat{h}_{0}^{\approx}+\vartheta\widehat{h}_{1}^{\approx}+\ldots
,
and, provided that the approximate solution
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}
has correct coefficients up to order
k
,
we have
\widehat{h}^{\approx}_{j}=\widehat{h}_{j}
for
j\leq k+1
. The leading coefficient function becomes

\widehat{h}_{0}=\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log(\widehat{L}_{0})-\frac{1}{4}\log\frac{\pi}{2}

and, by Taylor expanding the logarithm, we see that the subsequent corrections are given by

\widehat{h}_{1}=\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\Big\{\frac{\widehat{L}_{1}+\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{0}[\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}]}{\widehat{L}_{0}}\Big\}

and

\widehat{h}_{2}=\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\Big\{\frac{\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{1}[\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}]+\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{0}[\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1}]}{\widehat{L}_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}\widehat{h}_{1}^{2}\Big\},

where
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
are given by (
7.15
) and (
7.18
), respectively.
Here, we recall that
L=\widehat{L}_{0}+\vartheta\widehat{L}_{0}

and
\mathbf{S}=\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{0}+\vartheta\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{1}
, where the coefficients
\widehat{L}_{j}
and
\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{j}

do not depend on
\vartheta
.

In the end, we will further truncate
h^{\approx}
by replacing it with its Taylor polynomial

\mathfrak{h}_{k}
in
\vartheta
, for a suitable choice of
k
.
With these functions in hand, we will construct
a suitably approximate local potential for
m^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2h^{\approx}-2mV^{2}}
from the ansatz
(
5.2
),
and derive the asymptotics of the orthogonal polynomial
P=P_{m,n}
from this fact; see
Proposition
8.1
and Theorem
8.2
.

7.5.
The key estimates

The equation (
7.13
) may equivalently
be stated as the fixed point equation

(7.25)

\mathbf{T}\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E},

where
\mathbf{T}
denotes the nonlinear operator

(7.26)

\mathbf{T}[f]=\mathbf{T}_{\vartheta}[f]:=\frac{1}{4V|\partial V|^{2}}\Big\{L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[f]-\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[f])\big]\Big)\Big\},

where
\mathbf{S}
and
L
were defined in (
7.8
)
and (
7.9
), respectively. We remark that the expression in brackets
vanishes on
\Gamma
, so the division by
V|\partial V|^{2}
does not create any
singularity.

Rather than truncating the asymptotic expansion of
\mathcal{E}
, we will
obtain the approximate solution
\mathcal{E}^{\approx}
as an iterate
\mathcal{E}^{\approx}=\mathbf{T}^{k+1}[0]

of the nonlinear operator
\mathbf{T}
.
While the operator
\mathbf{S}
preserves real-analyticity in some neighborhood
of
\Gamma
, its iterates can in fact grow quite wildly.
Hence, we need to carefully choose the number
k=k_{\vartheta}
of iterates of
\mathbf{T}

in relation to the parameter
\vartheta
, and only keep iterating as long as the size of

\mathbf{T}^{k+1}[0]
remains suitably controlled.

Below, we will prove estimates which imply the following lemmas.
We recall that while in the end we will apply the results with
\vartheta=\frac{1}{m}
,
it is convenient to allow
\vartheta
to take complex values.

Lemma 7.1
.

There exist positive constants
C_{0}
,
c_{0}
,
\varepsilon
and
\varrho
and a neighborhood

\mathcal{N}
of
\Gamma
, all depending only on
V
and
\phi
, such that
if
k=k_{\vartheta}
is the integer part of
\varepsilon|\vartheta|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,
the function
\mathcal{E}^{\approx}=\mathbf{T}^{k+1}[0]
satisfies

(7.27)

\sup_{z\in\mathcal{N}}\big|\mathcal{E}^{\approx}(z)-\mathbf{T}[\mathcal{E}^{\approx}](z)\big|\leq C_{0}\exp\big(-c_{0}|\vartheta|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)

for
\vartheta\in\mathbb{D}(0,\varrho)
.

Recall the definitions of
H^{\approx}
,
G^{\approx}

and
h^{\approx}
from (
7.23
)–(
7.24
).

Lemma 7.2
.

With
\mathcal{N}
and
\varrho
as in Lemma
7.1
,
the functions
\mathcal{E}^{\approx}
,
H^{\approx}
,
G^{\approx}
are real analytic on
\mathcal{N}
,
while
h^{\approx}
is bounded and harmonic on
\mathcal{N}\cup\Omega
for
\vartheta\in\mathbb{D}(0,\varrho)
.
In addition, the
C^{1}(\mathcal{N})
–norms of the
functions
\mathcal{E}^{\approx}
,
H^{\approx}
,
G^{\approx}
,
h^{\approx}
and the harmonic conjugate

(h^{\approx})^{*}
of
h^{\approx}
in
\Omega

are uniformly bounded for
\vartheta\in\mathbb{D}(0,\varrho)
.

In order to obtain an asymptotic expansions for the orthogonal polynomial in the end
(rather than a formula in terms of iterates of
\mathbf{T}
),
we need to know that
h^{\approx}
is well approximated by its
k
-th Taylor polynomial in
the variable
\vartheta
with
k=k_{\vartheta}
,
which we denote by
\mathfrak{h}^{\approx}=\mathfrak{h}^{\approx}_{k}
.

Lemma 7.3
.

With
\mathcal{N}
and
\varrho
as in Lemma
7.1
and
k=k_{\vartheta}
,
there exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
c_{1}
such that

\sup_{z\in\mathcal{N}\cup\Omega}\big|\mathfrak{h}^{\approx}-h^{\approx}\big|\leq C_{1}\exp(-c_{1}|\vartheta|^{-\frac{1}{2}})

for
\vartheta\in\mathbb{D}(0,\varrho)
.

For the reader’s convenience, we defer the rather technical proofs of these
three lemmas to §
9
-
10
below.
In fact, we will obtain stronger versions which control
the relevant quantities in a scale of Banach spaces which
implies uniform pointwise and
C^{1}
-control
on
\mathcal{N}
; see Theorem
9.9
, Proposition
9.10
and
Lemma
10.2
, respectively. In the upcoming section, we proceed
to investigate the consequence of these results for
the potential equation and for the asymptotics of the orthogonal
polynomials.

8.
Deriving the asymptotic expansion

8.1.
Cut-off functions

We introduce a cut-off function
\chi_{1}

with the following properties. The function
\chi_{1}
is
C^{\infty}
–smooth
and takes values in
[0,1]
. It
vanishes on a neighborhood of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathcal{N}
while
it equals one on a smaller ring domain

\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
containing
\Gamma
. Without loss of generality we assume that

\mathcal{N}=\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{A})
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}=\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
, where
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}

are two centered open annuli containing the unit circle with
\overline{\mathbb{A}^{\prime}}\subset\mathbb{A}
.
A way to construct
\chi_{1}
is to begin with a
C^{\infty}
–smooth radial
cut-off function
\chi_{1}^{\prime}
with values
in
[0,1]
which
equals 1 on the annulus
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
and vanishes
off the larger annulus
\mathbb{A}
,
and put
\chi_{1}:=\chi_{1}^{\prime}\circ\phi
.
Later on, we will also need a
C^{\infty}
–smooth cut-off function
\chi_{2}:\mathbb{C}\to[0,1]

which vanishes on
\mathbb{C}\setminus(\Omega\cup\mathcal{N})

and equals
1
on
\Omega\cup\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. We construct this function in a similar fashion
based on a radial cut-off function.
In addition, we require that
\chi_{2}=\chi_{1}
holds on
the set
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\cup(\mathbb{C}\setminus\Omega)
, so that the support of the
difference
\chi_{2}-\chi_{1}
is contained in
\Omega\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
.

8.2.
The approximate potential equation

We now return to the original problem, and specify
\vartheta
to
\vartheta=m^{-1}
,
where
m
is the large semiclassical weight parameter.
By a slight abuse of notation, we will write
k_{m}
for
k_{\vartheta}
. That is,

k_{m}
is the integer part of
\varepsilon\sqrt{m}
for some fixed
\varepsilon>0
.
We let

(8.1)

\mathscr{U}^{\approx}=\chi_{1}\Big(H^{\approx}\operatorname{erf}(2\sqrt{m}V)+\frac{G^{\approx}}{\sqrt{2m\pi}}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}\Big)+(\chi_{2}-\chi_{1})H^{\approx},

where
\mathcal{E}^{\approx}=\mathbf{T}^{k_{m}+1}[0]
and where the auxiliary coefficient
functions
H^{\approx}
and
G^{\approx}
are given by (
7.23
).
We also recall the harmonic function
h^{\approx}
given by (
7.24
).
The following is an immediate consequence of Lemma
7.1

and these definitions.

Proposition 8.1
.

We have

\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}=\chi_{1}\sqrt{m}\mathrm{e}^{2h^{\approx}-2mV^{2}}\Big(1+\mathrm{O}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}\big)\Big)+\mathrm{O}\big(1_{\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}\big)

as
m\to\infty
, where the implicit
constants are uniform throughout the plane, and where
c_{0}>0

is the positive constant from Lemma
7.1
.

Proof.

We introduce the notation
\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}
for the approximate potential

\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}=H^{\approx}\operatorname{erf}(2\sqrt{m}V)+\frac{G^{\approx}}{\sqrt{2m\pi}}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}},

which may possibly be defined only on
the neighborhood
\mathcal{N}
of
\Gamma
.
Then the potential
\mathscr{U}^{\approx}
satisfies
\mathscr{U}^{\approx}=\chi_{1}\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}+(\chi_{2}-\chi_{1})H^{\approx}
,
and we may calculate the Laplacian
of
\mathscr{U}^{\approx}
as

(8.2)

\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}=\chi_{1}\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}+2\mathrm{Re}\,\big(\bar{\partial}\chi_{1}\;\partial\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}+\bar{\partial}(\chi_{2}-\chi_{1})\;\partial H^{\approx}\big)\\
+\varDelta\,\chi_{1}\;\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}+\varDelta(\chi_{2}-\chi_{1})\;H^{\approx}=:\chi_{1}\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}+W,

where we treat
W=W_{m}
as part of the error term.

We first claim that the term
W
is of order

\mathrm{O}\big(1_{\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}\big)
,
so that it fits within the given error parameters.
Indeed, it vanishes outside
\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
, and on
the set
\mathcal{N}\setminus(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\cup\Omega)
,
we use the fact that
\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}
there, so that

W=2\mathrm{Re}\,\big(\bar{\partial}\chi_{1}\,\partial\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}\big)+\varDelta\,\chi_{1}\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}\qquad\text{on}\quad\mathcal{N}\setminus(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\cup\Omega).

We observe that
\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}

and
\partial\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}
are both of order

\mathrm{O}(\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}})
on
\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
.
This holds in view of the decay estimate (
2.9
)
for the error function together with Lemma
7.2
,
which asserts that the coefficient functions
H^{\approx}
and
G^{\approx}

together with their gradients are uniformly bounded on
\mathcal{N}
.
As a consequence, we obtain

W=\mathrm{O}\big(1_{\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}\big)\qquad\text{on}\quad\mathbb{C}\setminus\Omega.

On the set
(\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime})\cap\Omega
,
we instead have
\nabla\chi_{2}\equiv 0
, and hence

W=2\mathrm{Re}\,\big\{\bar{\partial}\chi_{1}\,\partial(\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}-H^{\approx})\big\}+\varDelta\,\chi_{1}\,(\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}-H^{\approx})\qquad\text{on}\quad\Omega.

But in view of the estimate (
2.10
) and the fact that

\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}-H^{\approx}=H^{\approx}(\operatorname{erf}(2\sqrt{m}V)-1)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi m}}G^{\approx}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}},

we find that both
\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}-H^{\approx}
and
\nabla(\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}-H^{\approx})
are of order

\mathrm{O}(\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}})
on the open set
(\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime})\cap\Omega
, so it follows that

W=\mathrm{O}(1_{\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}})

globally.

It only remains to show that

(8.3)

\chi_{1}\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}=\chi_{1}m^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{2h^{\approx}-2mV^{2}}\Big(1+\mathrm{O}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}\big)\Big)

where the implicit constant is supposed to be uniform on
\mathcal{N}
.
To see this, we should relate (
8.3
) to the nonlinear operator
\mathbf{T}

studied above. In view of the equations (
6.6
),
(
6.8
), and (
6.9
), with
H
and
G
replaced by
H^{\approx}
and
G^{\approx}
,
respectively, we have that

\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}-m^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2h^{\approx}-2mV^{2}}=\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}m^{\frac{1}{2}}A\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mV^{2}},

where

(8.4)

A=-4V|\partial V|^{2}\,\mathcal{E}^{\approx}+\tfrac{2}{m}\mathrm{Re}\,\big\{\partial V\bar{\partial}\mathcal{E}^{\approx}\big\}+\tfrac{1}{m}\mathcal{E}^{\approx}\varDelta V+2|\partial V|^{2}G^{\approx}\\
+\tfrac{1}{2m}\varDelta G^{\approx}-\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2h^{\approx}}.

Following the calculations in §
7
, in particular the equations
(
7.7
), (
7.8
), (
7.10
), (
7.11
)
and (
7.12
) (cf. also the definition of
\mathbf{T}
in (
7.26
)),
we find that
A

may be expressed as

A=-4V|\partial V|^{2}\Big(\mathcal{E}^{\approx}-\mathbf{T}[\mathcal{E}^{\approx}]\Big).

As a consequence, we obtain

(8.5)

\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}_{*}-m^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2h^{\approx}-2mV^{2}}=-4\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}m^{\frac{1}{2}}V|\partial V|^{2}\big(\mathcal{E}^{\approx}-\mathbf{T}[\mathcal{E}^{\approx}]\big)\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}\\
=\mathrm{O}\big(m^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}\big),

where the last step follows from Lemma
7.1
.
But this bound is equivalent to the equation (
8.3
),
and hence the proof is complete.
∎

8.3.
Asymptotics of orthogonal polynomials

In §
8.2
, we proved that high iterates
\mathcal{E}^{\approx}=\mathbf{T}^{k+1}[0]

supply approximate solutions to the fixed-point equation
\mathbf{T}\mathcal{E}\approx\mathcal{E}
. If we form the
functions
H^{\approx}
and
G^{\approx}
as in §
7.4

and the potential
\mathscr{U}
according to the ansatz (
5.2
), then
\varDelta\mathscr{U}

should approximate to the orthogonal polynomial wave function
|P|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}
.
To actually prove that it is correct, we need
go back to
P=P_{m,n}
itself, which amounts to a kind of polarization.
As such, the result is essentially equivalent
to the main result of
[
18
]
, but with a different
proof and a new formula for
P_{m,n}
. Another difference is that
we do not work with truncated asymptotic expansions, but rather the
full iterates
\mathcal{E}^{\approx}=\mathbf{T}^{k+1}[0]
. These iterates admit asymptotic
expansions in negative powers of
m
, but
will in general contain terms of all orders.
In view of Lemma
7.3
(see also §
10
below),
it is also possible to work with finite asymptotic
expansions with an
m
-dependent number of terms.

We let
h^{\approx}
be as above,
and denote by
(h^{\approx})^{*}
the harmonic conjugate of

h^{\approx}
in the domain
\Omega
, normalized to vanish at infinity.
We recall that
\tau
is a fixed parameter with
0<\tau\leq 1

such that the assumptions on
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{\tau}

in §
2.4
hold.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
c_{0}>0
be the positive constant from
Lemma
7.1
. Then, we have

\big\lVert P_{m,n}-\chi_{2}m^{\frac{1}{4}}\phi^{n}\,\mathrm{e}^{h^{\approx}+\mathrm{i}(h^{\approx})^{*}+m\mathcal{Q}}\big\rVert_{2mQ}=\mathrm{O}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}\big),

as
m,n\to+\infty
with
n=\tau m
.

Theorem
8.2
will be shown to follow from Proposition
8.1
.
Several of the necessary steps have appeared previously in the works

[
20
,
21
,
18
]
, and for this reason we keep the
presentation brief.

Proof.

We let
f
be the holomorphic function on
\Omega
defined by

f(z)=m^{\frac{1}{4}}\phi^{n}\mathrm{e}^{h^{\approx}+\mathrm{i}(h^{\approx})^{*}+m\mathcal{Q}}.

Notice that
|f(z)|\asymp|z|^{n}
at infinity, and that
f
extends holomorphically
to
\Omega\cup\mathcal{N}
.
As a first step, we apply a version of Hörmander’s

L^{2}
-estimates for the
\bar{\partial}
-operator to find a polynomial
p

of degree
n

such that

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\big|p-\chi_{2}f\big|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA}(z)=\mathrm{O}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-2dm}\big).

where
d>0
, see e.g. §3.6 in
[
20
]
. It remains to show that
p

is close to the sought orthogonal polynomial
P_{m,n}
.

We will use the approximate potential
equation from Proposition
8.1

together with the structure of
\mathscr{U}^{\approx}
and
Green’s formula to verify that
p
is
appropriately approximately orthogonal to any polynomial
q
of
degree at most
n-1
. If
q
is such a polynomial, we observe that

\int_{\mathbb{C}}q\,\overline{p}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\chi_{2}\,q\,\overline{f}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA}+\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{e}^{-dm}\lVert q\rVert_{2mQ}),

so it remains to estimate the first term on the right-hand side.
But we have

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\chi_{2}q\,\overline{f}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\chi_{2}\,\frac{q}{f}\,|f|^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA}=m^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\chi_{2}\,\frac{q}{f}\,\mathrm{e}^{2h^{\approx}-2mV^{2}}\mathrm{dA}.

We rewrite this expression in the form

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\chi_{2}\,\frac{q}{f}\,\mathrm{e}^{2h^{\approx}-2mV^{2}}\mathrm{dA}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\chi_{1}\,\frac{q}{f}\,\mathrm{e}^{2h^{\approx}-2mV^{2}}\mathrm{dA}+m^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}(\chi_{2}-\chi_{1})q\,\overline{f}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA},

where we should stress that the integrand in the last term on the right-hand side
is non-zero only on
\Omega\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
.
Moreover, since
h^{\approx}
is uniformly bounded on
\Omega
, we have the pointwise estimate

|f|\,\mathrm{e}^{-mQ}\leq Cm^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathrm{e}^{-m(Q-\breve{Q})}\qquad\text{on}\quad\Omega,

and a simple
application of the Bernstein-Walsh lemma (see e.g. Proposition 2.3 in
[
20
]
)
shows that
|q|\mathrm{e}^{-mQ}\leq Cm^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-m(Q-\breve{Q})}\lVert q\rVert_{2mQ}
,
for some positive constant
C=C(Q)
.
Hence, we find that (since
\chi_{2}-\chi_{1}\geq 0
automatically)

(8.6)

\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{C}}(\chi_{2}-\chi_{1})q\,\overline{f}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA}\bigg|\leq C^{2}m^{\frac{3}{4}}\lVert q\rVert_{2mQ}\int_{\mathbb{C}}(\chi_{2}-\chi_{1})\mathrm{e}^{-2m(Q-\breve{Q})}\mathrm{dA}\\
=\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{e}^{-d^{\prime}m}\lVert q\rVert_{2mQ})

for some
d^{\prime}>0
, where the last step follows from the fact that

Q-\breve{Q}
is bounded below by a positive constant on
\mathrm{supp}(\chi_{2}-\chi_{1})

and the known growth of
Q-\breve{Q}
at infinity.

We proceed to show that

(8.7)

m^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\chi_{1}\,\frac{q}{f}\,\mathrm{e}^{2h^{\approx}-2mV^{2}}\mathrm{dA}=\mathrm{O}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}\lVert q\rVert_{2mQ}\big),

and it is here that the potential equation enters. Indeed, in view of
Proposition
8.1
, we have that

m^{\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{1}\mathrm{e}^{2h^{\approx}-2mV^{2}}=\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}+\mathrm{O}\big(\big(\chi_{1}m^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}+1_{\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}\big)\operatorname{e}^{-2mV^{2}}\big),

from which it follows that

(8.8)

m^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\chi_{1}\,\frac{q}{f}\,\mathrm{e}^{2h^{\approx}-2mV^{2}}\mathrm{dA}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{q}{f}\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}\mathrm{dA}\\
+\mathrm{O}\bigg(m^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\chi_{1}\frac{|q|}{|f|}\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}\mathrm{e}^{-2mV^{2}}\mathrm{dA}+\int_{\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}\frac{|q|}{|f|}\mathrm{e}^{-2mV^{2}}\mathrm{dA}\bigg).

In addition, in view of the uniform boundedness of
h^{\approx}

on the support of
\chi_{1}
, we find that

(8.9)

|f|^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-2mV^{2}}=\mathrm{O}\big(|f|^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{2h^{\approx}-2mV^{2}}\big)\\
=\mathrm{O}\big(m^{-\frac{1}{2}}|f|^{-1}\,|f|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\big)=\mathrm{O}\big(m^{-\frac{1}{2}}|f|\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\big),

and hence, an application of the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality
gives that

(8.10)

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\chi_{1}\frac{|q|}{|f|}\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}\mathrm{e}^{-2mV^{2}}\mathrm{dA}=\mathrm{O}\Big(m^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\chi_{1}\,|q|\,|f|\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA}\Big)\\
=\mathrm{O}\big(m^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}\lVert q\rVert_{2mQ}\lVert\chi_{1}f\rVert_{2mQ}\big).

Since
\lVert\chi_{1}f\rVert_{2mQ}
is clearly bounded (this will be verified below),
it follows that the first Ordo expression on the right-hand side of (
8.8
)
is of order
\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}\lVert q\rVert_{2mQ})
.

Turning to the last error term in (
8.8
), we observe that
(
8.9
) implies that

\int_{\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}\frac{|q|}{|f|}\mathrm{e}^{-2mV^{2}}\mathrm{dA}\leq Cm^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}|q|\,|f|\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA},

so an application of the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality together with the observation that

\int_{\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}|f|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\leq Cm^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{-2mV^{2}}\mathrm{dA}=\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{e}^{-d^{\prime\prime}m})

for some
d^{\prime\prime}>0
shows that the second error term in (
8.8
) is
of order
\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{e}^{-d^{\prime\prime}m}\lVert q\rVert_{2mQ})
. Hence, it follows that

m^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\chi_{1}\,\frac{q}{f}\,\mathrm{e}^{2h^{\approx}-2mV^{2}}\mathrm{dA}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{q}{f}\,\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}\,\mathrm{dA}+\mathrm{O}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}\lVert q\rVert_{2mQ}\big)

for
m
sufficiently large.
To complete the proof of (
8.7
), we observe that by Green’s formula,

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{q}{f}\,\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}\,\mathrm{dA}=\lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}(0,R)}\Big(\frac{q}{f}\partial_{\rm n}\mathscr{U}^{\approx}-\mathscr{U}^{\approx}\partial_{\rm n}\frac{q}{f}\Big)\mathrm{ds}=0,

where
\mathbb{T}(0,R)=\{z:|z|=R\}
, and where the last step
relies on the facts that the gradient of
\mathscr{U}^{\approx}

decays like
\mathrm{O}(1/|z|)
at infinity, and that

q/f
decays like
\mathrm{O}(|z|^{-1})
at infinity.
If we put the pieces together, it follows that

(8.11)

\int_{\mathbb{C}}q\,\overline{p}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA}=\mathrm{O}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}\lVert q\rVert_{2mQ}\big),

as
m\to+\infty
.

Up to the point where we applied Green’s formula,
essentially the same computations may be repeated
with
q
replaced by
p
, which is very close to

f
on
\Omega
. Specifically, we have that

(8.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}|p|^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\chi_{1}|f|^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2mQ}\mathrm{dA}+\mathrm{O}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-dm}\big)

(8.13)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\varDelta\,\mathscr{U}^{\approx}\mathrm{dA}+\mathrm{O}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}\big)

(8.14)

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}(0,R)}\partial_{\rm n}\mathscr{U}^{\approx}\mathrm{ds}+\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}})

(8.15)

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}(0,R)}\partial_{\rm n}H^{\approx}\mathrm{ds}+\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}})

(8.16)

\displaystyle=1+\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}),

where the last step relies on the fact that

\partial_{\rm n}H^{\approx}(z)=2|z|^{-1}+\mathrm{O}(|z|^{-2})
at
\mathbb{T}(0,R)

for large

.

As was explained on pp. 349–350 in
[
20
]
,
the bounds (
8.12
) and (
8.11
) together
with a simple duality argument then yield the claim.
∎

We let
\tau
and
c_{0}
be as in Theorem
8.2
.

Corollary 8.3
.

There exists a positive number
\delta>0
, such that
for

Remark 8.4

9.
Estimates for the nonlinear operator
\mathbf{T}

9.1.
Banach scales of quantitatively real-analytic functions

In this section and the next, we return to the notation
\vartheta=m^{-1}

and agree to let
\vartheta
take complex values.
Recall that the equation (
7.13
) may equivalently
be stated as the fixed point equation

(9.1)

\mathbf{T}\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E},

where
\mathbf{T}
is the nonlinear operator

(9.2)

\mathbf{T}[f]=\mathbf{T}_{\vartheta}[f]:=\frac{1}{4V|\partial V|^{2}}\Big\{L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[f]-\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[f])\big]\Big)\Big\},

where
\mathbf{S}
and
L
were defined in (
7.8
)
and (
7.9
), respectively.
We will obtain approximate solutions
E^{\approx}
by iteration of
\mathbf{T}
, i.e.
we put
\mathcal{E}^{\approx}=\mathcal{E}_{k}
for a suitable
k=k_{\vartheta}
, where

\mathcal{E}_{k}=\mathbf{T}^{k+1}[0].

In order to see to what extent this iteration scheme
will supply an approximate solution to (
7.25
)
we will need Lipschitz-type
estimates for the operator
\mathbf{T}
.
More specifically, in view of the calculation

(9.3)

\mathbf{T}[f]-\mathbf{T}[g]\\
=\frac{1}{4V|\partial V|^{2}}\bigg\{\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[f-g]+\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[f])]\Big)-\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(L+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[g])]\Big)\bigg\}\\
=\frac{1}{4V|\partial V|^{2}}\bigg\{\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[f-g]+\mathrm{e}^{\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}[\log L]}\mathbf{R}(f,g)\Big\},

where we have introduced the notation

(9.4)

\mathbf{R}(f,g):=\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(1+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[f]/L)]\Big)-\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log(1+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[g]/L)]\Big),

the non-trivial part is to estimate the nonlinear contribution
\mathbf{R}(f,g)

in terms of the size of
f-g
.
The problem is that the nonlinear operator
\mathbf{T}

fails to be Lipschitz in the naïve sense, e.g.,
when acting on spaces of real-analytic functions
on a given domain containing
\Gamma
. Instead, we will need to work with a decreasing
scale of spaces
\mathscr{H}_{\sigma}^{\infty}(\Gamma)

of suitably
quantitatively real-analytic
functions
defined in a complexified neighborhood of the boundary curve
\Gamma
,
and we will be able to prove Lipschitz estimates for
\mathbf{T}

only if we allow the co-domain to increase along this scale.

We follow the approach touched upon in
[
20
]
and
further developed in
[
18
]
,
and denote by
\mathbb{A}(\rho)
the annulus

\mathbb{A}(\rho)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\rho\leq|z|\leq\rho^{-1}\}

where
0<\rho<1
, and for
0<\sigma<1
we introduce the
2\sigma
-fattened diagonal
annulus in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
by

\widehat{\mathbb{A}}(\rho,\sigma)=\big\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{A}(\rho)\times\mathbb{A}(\rho):|z-w|\leq 2\sigma\big\}.

It turns out to be especially convenient to
restrict attention to parameters
\rho
and
\sigma
such that

\rho=\rho(\sigma)=\frac{1}{\sigma+\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}}}=1-\sigma+\mathrm{O}(\sigma^{2}),

where the indicated error terms refers to the limiting procedure
\sigma\to 0
.
We let
\widehat{\mathbb{A}}(\sigma)
denote the annulus
\widehat{\mathbb{A}}(\sigma)=\widehat{\mathbb{A}}(\rho(\sigma),\sigma)
.

We denote by
\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Gamma)
the space of real-analytic
functions
f
on the neighborhood of
\Gamma
given by
\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{A}(\rho(\sigma)))
,
such that the Hermitian-analytic
polarization
f_{\phi}(z,w)
of
f\circ\phi^{-1}

is bounded on
\widehat{\mathbb{A}}(\sigma)
. Here, we recall
that
\phi
is the exterior conformal mapping

\phi:\Omega\to\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{e}}
, which preserves the point at infinity, and we
will only work with small enough parameters
0<\sigma<1
such
that
\phi^{-1}
extends conformally to
\mathbb{A}(\rho(\sigma))
.
If we endow
\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Gamma)
with the norm

\lVert f\rVert_{\sigma}=\sup_{(z,w)\in\widehat{\mathbb{A}}(\sigma)}|f_{\phi}(z,w)|,

it becomes a commutative Banach algebra.
We will show that
\mathbf{T}
maps
\mathscr{H}_{\sigma}^{\infty}(\Gamma)
into

\mathscr{H}_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\infty}(\Gamma)
whenever
\sigma>\sigma^{\prime}
, and
obtain appropriate Lipschitz bounds of the form

\lVert\mathbf{T}(f)-\mathbf{T}(g)\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})^{2}}+\frac{|\vartheta|}{(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})^{4}}\Big)\,|\vartheta|\,\lVert f-g\rVert_{\sigma},

valid for
f
and
g
e.g. in a ball of fixed radius
r_{0}
in

\mathscr{H}_{\sigma}^{\infty}(\Gamma)
and for
\vartheta
in a fixed disk
\mathbb{D}(0,\varrho_{0})
.
Since for some
\sigma=\sigma^{*}
, the initial iterate

\mathcal{E}_{0}=\mathbf{T}[0]
belongs to a fixed ball in
\mathscr{H}_{\sigma^{*}}^{\infty}(\Gamma)
,
this will allow us to keep good control of iterates
\mathbf{T}^{k}[0]

as long as
k
is not too large. We will quantify these statements shortly.

9.2.
Basic estimates

To get started, we need the following Lemma. It
follows directly from
[
18
, §5.5]
.

Lemma 9.1
.

Assume that
f\in\mathscr{H}_{\sigma}^{\infty}(\Gamma)

vanishes along
\Gamma
.
Then
(1-|\phi|^{2})^{-1}f\in\mathscr{H}_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\infty}(\Gamma)

for any
\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma
, and we have

\big\lVert(1-|\phi|^{2})^{-1}f\big\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\leq 6\frac{\lVert f\rVert_{\sigma}}{\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}}.

As a first step, we show that the two linear operators
\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}
and
\mathbf{S}

act boundedly as operators
\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Gamma)\to\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)

whenever
\sigma>\sigma^{\prime}
. In fact, for
\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}
this holds true for
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}

as well, and we have

(9.5)

\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\rVert_{\sigma\to\sigma}:=\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\rVert_{\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Gamma)\to\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Gamma)}\leq\frac{6}{\sigma},

as follows from
[
18
, §5.5]
.
With the simplifying notation

(9.6)

\lVert\mathbf{S}\rVert_{\sigma\to\sigma^{\prime}}:=\lVert\mathbf{S}\rVert_{\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Gamma)\to\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)}

for the operator norm of
\mathbf{S}
, we have the following result.

Proposition 9.2
.

For
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
with
0<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma<1
, we have that

(9.7)

\lVert\mathbf{S}\rVert_{\sigma\to\sigma^{\prime}}\leq\lVert\varDelta V\rVert_{\sigma}+\frac{12\sigma^{\prime}\lVert\partial V\rVert_{\sigma}\lVert\phi^{\prime}\rVert_{\sigma}+42\lVert(\partial V)^{2}\rVert_{\sigma}\lVert(1-|\phi|^{2})/V\rVert_{\sigma}}{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})\sigma^{\prime}}\\
+|\vartheta|\frac{10206\big\lVert\phi^{\prime}\big\rVert_{\sigma}^{2}\,\big\lVert(1-|\phi|^{2})/V\big\rVert_{\sigma}}{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})^{3}\sigma^{\prime}}.

Ignoring the precise value of the constants, we have

(9.8)

\lVert\mathbf{S}\rVert_{\sigma\to\sigma^{\prime}}\leq M_{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\vartheta):=C_{0}\left(\frac{1}{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})\sigma^{\prime}}+\frac{|\vartheta|}{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})^{3}\sigma^{\prime}}\right)

where the constant
C_{0}
depends only on
V
and
\phi
, provided that
\sigma

and
\sigma^{\prime}
are small enough (depending only on
V
and
\phi
).

Proof.

Before proceeding to estimate
the operator norm of
\mathbf{S}
, we recall the bounds

(9.9)

\big\lVert\bar{\partial}f\big\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\leq\frac{6\lVert f\rVert_{\sigma}\lVert\phi^{\prime}\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}}{\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}}

and

(9.10)

\big\lVert(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\Omega})f\big\rVert_{\sigma}\leq\frac{7\lVert f\rVert_{\sigma}}{\sigma}

which follow from the equation (5.5.2) in

[
18
]
together with a change of variables,
and from (5.5.3) in
[
18
]
, respectively.

The operator
\mathbf{S}
acts by

\mathbf{S}(f)=\partial V\bar{\partial}f+\bar{\partial}V\partial f+f\varDelta V+|\partial V|^{2}\frac{(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}-\mathbf{I})f}{V}+\frac{\vartheta}{2}\varDelta\frac{(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}-\mathbf{I})f}{2V},

so it follows from the triangle inequality and the above bound (
9.9
)
for the
\bar{\partial}
-operator that

(9.11)

\lVert\mathbf{S}\rVert_{\sigma\to\sigma^{\prime}}\leq\frac{12\lVert\partial V\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\lVert\phi^{\prime}\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}}{\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}}+\lVert\varDelta V\rVert_{\sigma}+\big\lVert(\partial V)^{2}\big\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\sup_{\lVert f\rVert_{\sigma}=1}\bigg\lVert\frac{(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\Omega})f}{V}\bigg\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\\
+\frac{|\vartheta|}{4}\sup_{\lVert f\rVert_{\sigma}=1}\bigg\lVert\varDelta\frac{(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}-\mathbf{I})f}{V}\bigg\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}.

It remains to estimate the last two terms. The first term may be estimated
using (
9.10
) and Lemma
9.1
, and we obtain

\sup_{\lVert f\rVert_{\sigma}=1}\bigg\lVert\frac{(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\Omega})f}{V}\bigg\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\leq\frac{42\big\lVert V^{-1}(1-|\phi|^{2})\big\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}}{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})\sigma}.

The last remaining term will be
handled by repeated application of (
9.9
) and (
9.10
).
Namely, we insert two intermediate parameters
\sigma_{0}=\sigma^{\prime}+\frac{1}{3}(\sigma+\sigma^{\prime})
and

\sigma_{1}+\frac{2}{3}(\sigma+\sigma^{\prime})
and apply (
9.9
)
to obtain

\bigg\lVert\Delta\frac{(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\Omega})f}{V}\bigg\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\leq\frac{6\lVert\partial g\rVert_{\sigma_{0}}\lVert\phi^{\prime}\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}}{\sigma_{0}-\sigma^{\prime}},

where we have introduced the auxiliary function

g=\frac{(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\Omega})}{V}f.

But applying (
9.9
) again (formally a conjugated version)
followed by the Banach algebra property and Lemma
9.1
, we find that

\lVert\partial g\rVert_{\sigma_{0}}\leq\frac{6\lVert\phi^{\prime}\rVert_{\sigma_{0}}}{(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{0})}\bigg\lVert\frac{(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\Omega})f}{V}\bigg\rVert_{\sigma_{1}}\leq\frac{36\times 7\lVert\phi^{\prime}\rVert_{\sigma_{0}}\big\lVert(1-|\phi|^{2})/V\big\rVert_{\sigma_{1}}}{(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{0})(\sigma-\sigma_{1})\sigma_{1}}\lVert f\rVert_{\sigma}.

Putting these observations together, we arrive at

(9.12)

\lVert\mathbf{S}\rVert_{\sigma\to\sigma^{\prime}}\leq\frac{12\sigma^{\prime}\lVert\partial V\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\lVert\phi^{\prime}\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}+42\lVert(\partial V)^{2}\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\big\lVert(1-|\phi|^{2})/V\big\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}}{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})\sigma^{\prime}}\\
+\lVert\varDelta V\rVert_{\sigma}+\frac{|\vartheta|}{4}\frac{6^{3}\times 7\lVert\phi^{\prime}\rVert_{\sigma_{0}}\lVert\phi^{\prime}\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\big\lVert(1-|\phi|^{2})/V\big\rVert_{\sigma_{1}}}{(\sigma_{0}-\sigma^{\prime})(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{0})(\sigma-\sigma_{1})\sigma_{1}}

The proof follows by calculating the constants and using the
monotonicity of the norm

\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\sigma}
in
\sigma
.
∎

We record the following simple bound for some quantities related to
V
and
\phi

which will appear repeatedly.

Proposition 9.3
.

There exist constants
0<\sigma^{*}<1
and
\epsilon_{0}>0
, depending only on
V
and
\phi
,
such that all the quantities

(9.13)

\big\lVert(\partial V)^{-2}\big\rVert_{\sigma},\quad\big\lVert V^{-1}(1-|\phi|^{2})\big\rVert_{\sigma},\quad\big\lVert\phi^{\prime}\big\rVert_{\sigma},\quad\text{and}\quad\big\lVert\partial V\big\rVert_{\sigma},

as well as

(9.14)

\sup_{|\vartheta|\leq\epsilon_{0}}\big\lVert 1/L\big\rVert_{\sigma},\quad\text{ and }\quad\sup_{|\vartheta|\leq\epsilon_{0}}\big\lVert\mathbf{T}[0]\big\rVert_{\sigma}

are uniformly bounded for
0<\sigma\leq\sigma^{*}
.

Proof.

We recall the definitions (
7.9
) and
(
9.2
)

of
L
and
\mathbf{T}
, respectively, and write
L=\widehat{L}_{0}+\vartheta\widehat{L}_{1}

where the coefficient functions do not depend on
\vartheta
.
We have

\mathbf{T}[0]=\frac{1}{4V|\partial V|^{2}}\Big\{L-\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log L\big]\Big)\Big\}=\Big\{\widehat{L}_{0}+\vartheta\widehat{L}_{1}-\exp\Big(\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big[\log\widehat{L}_{0}+\vartheta\widehat{L}_{1}\big]\Big)\Big\}.

It is clear that the relevant quantities are bounded for some
\sigma^{*}
and for
\vartheta=0
.
Possibly after shrinking
\sigma^{*}
slightly, the coefficient
\widehat{L}_{1}

is also bounded in
\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\sigma^{*}}(\Gamma)
.
Using the fact that both
1/t
and
\exp(t)
are Lipschitz (the former away from the origin),
we conclude that for
\vartheta
sufficiently small, both the quantities

\sup_{|\vartheta|\leq\epsilon_{0}}\big\lVert 1/L\big\rVert_{\sigma^{*}},\quad\sup_{|\vartheta|\leq\epsilon_{0}}\big\lVert\mathbf{T}[0]\big\rVert_{\sigma^{*}}

are bounded. The claim now follows from the monotonicity
of
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\sigma}
in
\sigma
.
∎

We fix, once and for all, the radius
r_{0}
to be

(9.15)

r_{0}:=2\sup_{|\vartheta|\leq\epsilon_{0}}\big\lVert\mathbf{T}[0]\big\rVert_{\sigma^{*}}=2\sup_{|\vartheta|\leq\epsilon_{0}}\big\lVert\mathcal{E}_{0}\big\rVert_{\sigma^{*}}.

9.3.
A preliminary Lipschitz-type estimate

We proceed to bound the
quantity
\mathbf{R}(f,g)
that appeared in (
9.4
).

Proposition 9.4
.

Fix
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
with
0<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma<1
.
There exists a constant
m_{1}
, depending only on
L
,
\Omega
,
\sigma
,
\sigma^{\prime}
and
r_{0}
,
such that for
|\vartheta|\leq\frac{1}{m_{1}}
and for all
f
and
g
in the
ball of radius
r_{0}
in
\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Gamma)
, we have that

(9.16)

\lVert\mathbf{R}(f,g)\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\leq 6|\vartheta|\,\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}\to\sigma^{\prime}}\lVert\mathbf{S}\rVert_{\sigma\to\sigma^{\prime}}\lVert 1/L\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\lVert f-g\rVert_{\sigma}.

Remark 9.5
.

We mention that the constant
m_{1}
is explicitly given below in (
9.19
).

Proof.

In the proof, we keep in mind that if
F
is holomorphic on

\mathbb{D}(0,\lVert f\rVert_{\sigma})
,
then the polarization of
(F\circ f)_{\phi}

equals
F\circ f_{\phi}
. We also recall that the spaces

\mathscr{H}_{\sigma}^{\infty}(\Gamma)
are Banach algebras.

In view of the bound

\big\lVert\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[f]/L\big\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\leq|\vartheta|\,\lVert 1/L\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\lVert\mathbf{S}\rVert_{\sigma\to\sigma^{\prime}}\lVert f\rVert_{\sigma}.

and the fact that
\big|\log(1+t)\big|\leq 2|t|
holds for complex
t

whenever
|t|\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
we find that for
f
of norm at most
r_{0}
in
\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Gamma)
,
we have that

(9.17)

\big\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log(1+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[f]/L)\big\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\leq\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}\to\sigma^{\prime}}\big\lVert\log(1+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[f]/L)\big\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\\
\leq 2|\vartheta|\;\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}\to\sigma^{\prime}}\lVert\mathbf{S}\rVert_{\sigma\to\sigma^{\prime}}\lVert 1/L\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\lVert f\rVert_{\sigma}

for all
\vartheta
with
|\vartheta|
sufficiently small,
and similarly for
g
. Here, “sufficiently small” means that

(9.18)

|\vartheta|\leq\frac{1}{m_{0}},\quad\text{where }\;m_{0}:=\max\big\{\epsilon_{0}^{-1},4M_{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\vartheta)\sup_{|\vartheta|\leq\epsilon_{0}}\lVert 1/L\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}r_{0}\big\},

where
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(V,\phi)
is the positive
parameter from Proposition
9.3

while
M_{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\vartheta)
is the constant from
(
9.8
).
Since

|\exp(t)-\exp(t^{\prime})|\leq 3|t-t^{\prime}|

whenever
t,t^{\prime}
are complex numbers
with
|t|\leq 1
and
|t^{\prime}|\leq 1
,
we find that for all
f
and
g
in the ball of radius
r_{0}

in
\mathscr{H}_{\sigma}^{\infty}(\Gamma)

and for all
\vartheta
such that

(9.19)

|\vartheta|\leq\frac{1}{m_{1}},\quad\text{where }\;m_{1}:=\max\Big\{m_{0},2\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}\to\sigma^{\prime}}M_{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\vartheta)\sup_{|\vartheta|\leq\epsilon_{0}}\lVert 1/L\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}r_{0}\Big\},

we have that

(9.20)

\lVert\mathbf{R}(f,g)\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\leq 3\big\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log(1+\vartheta\,L\mathbf{S}[f]/L)-\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\log(1+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[g]/L)\big\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\\
\leq 3\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}\to\sigma^{\prime}}\big\lVert\log(1+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[f]/L)-\log(1+\vartheta\,\mathbf{S}[g]/L)\big\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}.

Similarly, the logarithm meets the Lipschitz bound

\big|\log(1+t)-\log(1+t)\big|\leq 2|t-t^{\prime}|

whenever

are complex with
|t|,|t^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
As a consequence, we find that

\lVert\mathbf{R}(f,g)\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\leq 6|\vartheta|\;\big\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\big\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}\to\sigma^{\prime}}\big\lVert\mathbf{S}\big\rVert_{\sigma\to\sigma^{\prime}}\big\lVert 1/L\big\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\big\lVert f-g\big\rVert_{\sigma}.

This holds for
f,g
in the ball of radius
r_{0}
in

\mathscr{H}_{\sigma}^{\infty}(\Gamma)
and for

\vartheta\in\mathbb{D}(0,\frac{1}{m_{1}})
,
where
m_{1}
is given above in (
9.19
).
The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 9.6
.

We remark that the condition (

) looks like it could be

. However, if we initially fix

, then the quantity

will

. Hence the

9.4.
The local Lipschitz estimate for
\mathbf{T}

We proceed to the central statement of this section. We let
m_{1}

be the lower bound from Proposition

and recall the number
r_{0}

from (

).

Theorem 9.7
.

Let
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
be positive real numbers with

0<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma<1.

For any
\vartheta\in\mathbb{D}\big(0,m_{1}^{-1}\big)
and for any two functions

and

in the
ball of radius
r_{0}
in
\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Gamma)
, we have that

(9.21)

\lVert\mathbf{T}(f)-\mathbf{T}(g)\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\leq C(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\,M_{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime\prime},\vartheta)\,|\vartheta|\,\lVert f-g\rVert_{\sigma},

where the constant

is given by

(9.22)

C(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})=\frac{3\big\lVert|\partial V|^{-2}\big\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}\,\big\lVert(1-|\phi|^{2})/V\big\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime}}}{2(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})}\\
\Big(1+6\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\to\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\sup_{|\vartheta|\leq\epsilon_{0}}\lVert 1/L\rVert_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\exp\big(\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\Omega}[\log L]\rVert_{\sigma}\big)\Big),

where

In Theorem

The function

is given by (

is a large parameter of order
\mathrm{O}(m^{\frac{1}{2}})

The function

h^{\approx}\sim\widehat{h}_{0}+m^{-1}\widehat{h}_{1}+m^{-2}\widehat{h}_{2}+\ldots,

where the coefficients
\widehat{h}_{j}
for

k

are stable, meaning they do not change if we increase
m
and iterate further.
The goal of this section is to show that we can truncate the asymptotic expansion
at some appropriate

\mathfrak{h}=\widehat{h}_{0}+m^{-1}\widehat{h}_{1}+m^{-2}\widehat{h}_{2}+\ldots+m^{-k}\widehat{h}_{k}

and still have

P=m^{\frac{1}{4}}(\phi^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi^{n}e^{\mathfrak{h}+\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{h}^{*}+m\mathcal{Q}}\Big(1+\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}\sqrt{m}}\Big),

valid in the same region
\{z:\mathrm{d}_{\mathbb{C}}(z,\mathcal{S}_{\tau}^{c})\leq\delta m^{-\frac{1}{4}}\}
.

We begin with a lemma concerning the error term in the complex version of Taylor’s formula.

Lemma 10.1
.

Let
f
be a bounded analytic function on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{k}f(z)
the
k

Taylor polynomial of
f(z)
. Then

\left|\mathcal{P}_{k}f(z)-f(z)\right|\leq|z|^{k+1}\Big(1+\frac{1}{\pi}\log\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}}\Big)\;\lVert f\rVert_{H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Proof.

We begin with the Cauchy integral formula

f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{f(\eta)}{1-z\bar{\eta}}\mathrm{ds}(\eta),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The Taylor coefficients are similarly given by

\widehat{f}(j)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bar{\eta}^{j}f(\eta)\mathrm{ds}(\eta),

so that

\mathcal{P}_{k}f(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}z^{j}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bar{\eta}^{j}f(\eta)\mathrm{ds}(\eta).

As a consequence, we get

f(z)-\mathcal{P}_{k}f(z)=z^{k+1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\bar{\eta}^{k+1}}{1-z\bar{\eta}}f(\eta)\mathrm{ds}(\eta),

which leads to the estimate

\big|f(z)-\mathcal{P}_{k}f(z)\big|\leq|z|^{k+1}\lVert f\rVert_{H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{\eta}|}\mathrm{ds}(\eta).

We rewrite the right-hand side integral as

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{\eta}|}\mathrm{ds}(\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big|(1-z\bar{\eta})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big|^{2}\mathrm{ds}(\eta)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{((\frac{1}{2})_{j})^{2}}{(j!)^{2}}|z|^{2j}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+j)^{2}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})^{2}\Gamma(j+1)^{2}}|z|^{2j}.

In view of the estimate

\Gamma\big(\tfrac{1}{2}+j\big)\leq\sqrt{j}\,\Gamma(j),\qquad j=1,2,3,\ldots

and the identity
\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})=\sqrt{\pi}
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{|1-z\bar{\eta}|}\mathrm{ds}(\eta)\leq 1+\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{|z|^{2j}}{j}=1+\frac{1}{\pi}\log\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

h_{k}

belongs to the tensor product space

\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\frac{1}{3}\sigma^{*}}(\Gamma)\otimes H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}(0,C_{2}^{-1}k^{-2})),

and has norm at most
C^{\prime}
. Here, the tensor product space should be understood
as the class of functions
f(z,\vartheta)
which are holomorphic in
\vartheta\in\mathbb{D}(0,\epsilon_{1}k^{-2})
,
such that for fixed
\vartheta
, we have
f(\cdot,\vartheta)\in\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Gamma)
.
But then, by Lemma
10.1
and a simple scaling argument,
the Taylor polynomial
\mathcal{P}_{k^{\prime}}h_{k}
of
h_{k}

in the variable
\vartheta
satisfies

\big\lVert\mathcal{P}_{k^{\prime}}h_{k}-h_{k}\big\rVert_{\frac{1}{3}\sigma^{*}}\leq C_{5}\big(C_{2}|\vartheta|k^{2}\big)^{k^{\prime}+1},

for
\vartheta
in the smaller disk
\mathbb{D}(0,\frac{1}{2}C_{2}^{-1}k^{-2})
.
This completes the proof.
∎

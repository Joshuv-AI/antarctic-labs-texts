Abstract
. We present a first-order aggregation model on a group ring, and study its asymptotic dynamics. In a positive coupling strength regime, we show that the flow generated by the proposed model tends to an equilibrium manifold asymptotically. For this, we introduce a Lyapunov functional which is non-increasing along the flow, and using the temporal decay of the nonlinear functional and the LaSalle invariance principle, we show that the flow converges toward an equilibrium manifold asymptotically. We also show that the structure of an equilibrium manifold is strongly dependent on the structure of an underlying group.

Our main goal of this paper is to answer the aforementioned posed question, i.e., we provide a first-order aggregation model and study how the underlying group

\mathbb{K}[G]

x
and

\mathbb{K}[G]

(1.1)

x=\sum_{g\in G}x_{g}g\quad\mbox{and}\quad y=\sum_{g\in G}y_{g}g,

where coefficients
x_{g}
and
y_{g}
are elements of
\mathbb{K}
. Then, addition, scalar multiplication, group ring multiplication and hermitian conjugate are defined as follows: for
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
and

in (
1.1

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &x+y:=\sum_{g\in G}(x_{g}+y_{g})g,\quad\lambda x:=\sum_{g\in G}(\lambda x_{g})g,\\
&xy:=\sum_{g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\in G}x_{g^{\prime}}y_{g^{\prime\prime}}(g^{\prime}*g^{\prime\prime}),\quad x^{\dagger}:=\sum_{g\in G}\overline{x_{g}}g^{-1},\end{aligned}

where
g^{\prime}*g^{\prime\prime}
is a group operation of
g^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime\prime}
in
G
and
g^{-1}
is an inverse of
g
. Here
\overline{x_{g}}
stands for the complex conjugate of
x_{g}
.

The main results of this paper are three-fold. First, we propose a first-order aggregation model on
\mathbb{K}[G]
for an ensemble
{\mathcal{X}}
:

(1.3)

\frac{dx^{i}}{dt}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big[x^{k}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}-x^{i}(x^{k})^{\dagger}x^{i}\Big],\quad i=1,\cdots,N,

where
\kappa
is a nonnegative constant. Then, system (
1.3
) can also be written as a mean-field form:

(1.4)

\frac{dx^{i}}{dt}=\kappa\Big[x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{i}\Big],\quad i=1,\cdots,N,

where
\displaystyle x^{c}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}x^{i}
is the centroid of the ensemble
{\mathcal{X}}
.

Note that the right-hand side of (
1.4
) is well-defined in the group ring
\mathbb{K}[G]
via operations (
1.2
). Other than the elementary operations in (
1.2
), we can also introduce a trace, associated inner product and norm on
\mathbb{K}[G]
as well (see Definition
2.1
and (
2.5
)). Then, by straightforward calculations, we can show that
\|x^{i}\|_{F}
is a constant of motion for (
1.4
) (see Lemma
3.1
):

\|x^{i}(t)\|_{F}=\|x^{i}(0)\|_{F},\quad t\geq 0.

On the other hand, system (
1.4
) can be rewritten in terms of coefficient
x_{g}^{i}\in\mathbb{K}
(see Lemma
2.2
):

\frac{dx_{g}^{i}}{dt}=\sum_{g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\left(x_{g_{1}}^{c}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}-x_{g_{1}}^{i}\overline{x^{c}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\right),\quad\forall g\in G,~~i=1,\cdots,N.

Secondly, we show that the flow
X(t)
converges to the equilibrium manifold
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{K}[G])
:

\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{K}[G]):=\left\{X:=(x^{1},\cdots,x^{N})\in\mathbb{K}[G]^{N}:~x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{i}=0_{G},\quad\forall~i=1,\cdots,N\right\},

where
0_{G}=\sum_{g\in G}0g
.
For the convergence, we introduce a Lyapunov functional which is non-increasing along the flow
X(t)
and its zero set for orbital derivative coincides with the equilibrium manifold
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{K}[G])
. Then, by the LaSalle invariance principle, one can show that for a positive coupling strength

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathrm{dist}(X(t),{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{K}[G]))=0,

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first study basic properties of the group ring
\mathbb{K}[G]
and then present basic properties for the aggregation model (
1.2
) such as a conservation law and invariance by an automorphism group on
G
. For notational simplicity, we also denote
g_{1}*g_{2}
by
g_{1}g_{2}
by deleting
*
. First, we introduce a concept of a trace in following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\displaystyle x=\sum_{g\in G}x_{g}g
be an element in
\mathbb{K}[G]
. Then, the trace of
x
is given by the coefficient of the identity element
e
:

\mathrm{tr}(x)=x_{e}.

Remark 2.1
.

It is easy to see that for
x,y\in\mathbb{K}[G]
,

\mathrm{tr}(xy):=\sum_{g^{\prime}*g^{\prime\prime}=e}x_{g^{\prime}}y_{g^{\prime\prime}}.

Next, we list basic properties for hermitian conjugate and trace in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x
and
y
be elements of the group ring
\mathbb{K}[G]
. Then we have

(xy)^{\dagger}=y^{\dagger}x^{\dagger},\quad\mathrm{tr}(xy)=\mathrm{tr}(yx).

Proof.

We set

x=\sum_{g\in G}x_{g}g\quad\mbox{and}\quad y=\sum_{g\in G}y_{g}g.

\bullet
(First relation): By group ring multiplication and hermitian conjugation defined in (
1.2
), we obtain the first identity:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (xy)^{\dagger}&=\left(\sum_{g_{1},g_{2}\in G}x_{g_{1}}y_{g_{2}}g_{1}g_{2}\right)^{\dagger}=\sum_{g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\overline{x_{g_{1}}}\overline{y_{g_{2}}}(g_{1}g_{2})^{-1}\\
&=\sum_{g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\overline{y_{g_{2}}}\overline{x_{g_{1}}}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{-1}=\Big(\sum_{g_{2}\in G}\overline{y_{g_{2}}}g_{2}^{-1}\Big)\Big(\sum_{g_{1}\in G}\overline{x_{g_{1}}}g_{1}^{-1}\Big)=y^{\dagger}x^{\dagger}.\end{aligned}

\bullet
(Second relation): We use (
1.2
) and the fact that the left and right inverses are equal for all elements in
G
to see

\mathrm{tr}(xy)=\sum_{g_{1}*g_{2}=e}x_{g_{1}}y_{g_{2}}=\sum_{g_{2}*g_{1}=e}y_{g_{2}}x_{g_{1}}=\mathrm{tr}(yx).

∎

In what follows, we study several properties of the aggregation model (
1.4
) on
\mathbb{K}[G]
. First, we provide other alternative representation of system (
1.4
) as a component form.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{x^{i}(t)=\sum_{g\in G}x^{i}_{g}(t)g\}
be a solution to system (
1.4
). Then, for each
g\in G
, the coefficient
x^{i}_{g}
satisfies

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{dx_{g}^{i}}{dt}=\kappa\sum_{g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\left(x_{g_{1}}^{c}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}-x_{g_{1}}^{i}\overline{x^{c}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\right),\quad i=1,\cdots,N.

Proof.

We substitute the ansatz
x^{i}=\sum_{g\in G}x^{i}_{g}g
into (
1.4
), and then compare the coefficients of
g
in the resulting relation to derive the desired system.

\bullet
(L.H.S (
1.4
)): Note that

(2.2)

\frac{dx^{i}}{dt}=\sum_{g\in G}\left(\frac{dx^{i}_{g}}{dt}\right)g.

\bullet
(R.H.S (
1.4
)): By definition of multiplication and hermitian conjugate, one has

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}&=\sum_{g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\in G}x^{c}_{g_{1}}\overline{x_{g_{2}}^{i}}x^{i}_{g_{3}}(g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{3})=\sum_{g\in G}\left(\sum_{g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{3}=g}x^{c}_{g_{1}}\overline{x_{g_{2}}^{i}}x^{i}_{g_{3}}\right)g\\
&=\sum_{g\in G}\left(\sum_{g_{1},g_{2}\in G}x_{g_{1}}^{c}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\right)g,\end{aligned}

where we used the relation
g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{3}=g\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g_{3}=g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g
.

In an exact same manner, we also have

(2.4)

x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{i}=\sum_{g\in G}\Big(\sum_{g_{1},g_{2}\in G}x_{g_{1}}^{i}\overline{x^{c}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\Big)g.

In (
1.4
), we collect all the relations (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) to obtain

\sum_{g\in G}\left(\frac{dx_{g}^{i}}{dt}\right)g=\kappa\sum_{g\in G}\Big[\sum_{g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\left(x_{g_{1}}^{c}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}-x_{g_{1}}^{i}\overline{x^{c}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\right)\Big]g.

Finally, we use the fact that
G
is a basis to derive the desired system (
2.1
).
∎

Now, we are ready to introduce the Frobenius inner product and norm as follows. For
x,y\in\mathbb{K}[G]
,

(2.5)

\langle x,y\rangle_{F}:=\mathrm{tr}(x^{\dagger}y),\quad\|x\|_{F}:=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle_{F}}.

Then, it is easy to check that
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{F}
and
\|\cdot\|_{F}
satisfies all the axioms for inner product and norm. In particular, one has

\langle x,y\rangle_{F}=\sum_{g\in G}\overline{x_{g}}y_{g},\quad\langle x,y\rangle_{F}=\overline{\langle y,x\rangle_{F}}.

Moreover, one can also check

\|x+y\|_{F}\leq\|x\|_{F}+\|y\|_{F}\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\langle x,y\rangle_{F}|\leq\|x\|_{F}\cdot\|y\|_{F}.

Next, we show that the norm
\|x\|_{F}
is a constant of motion for (
1.4
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\{x^{i}\}
be the solution to system (
1.4
) with initial data satisfying

\|x^{i}(0)\|_{F}=1\quad i=1,2,\cdots,N.

Then, one has

\|x^{i}(t)\|_{F}=1,\quad t>0,\quad i=1,\cdots,N.

Proof.

We use (
2.1
) and (
2.5
) to find

(2.6)

\frac{d}{dt}\|x^{i}\|_{F}^{2}=\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{g\in G}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}x_{g}^{i}=\sum_{g\in G}\left(\frac{d{x}_{g}^{i}}{dt}\right)\overline{x_{g}^{i}}+(c.c.),

where
(c.c.)
is the complex conjugate of the first term in (
2.6
).

Note that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{g\in G}\left(\frac{d{x}_{g}^{i}}{dt}\right)\bar{x}_{g}^{i}&=\sum_{g,g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\left(x_{g_{1}}^{c}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}-x_{g_{1}}^{i}\overline{x^{c}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\right)\bar{x}_{g}^{i}\\
&=\sum_{g,g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\left(x_{g_{1}}^{c}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}-x_{g_{1}}^{i}\overline{x^{c}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}\right).\end{aligned}

Then, we use (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) to obtain

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}\|x^{i}\|_{F}^{2}&=\sum_{g,g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\left(x_{g_{1}}^{c}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}-x_{g_{1}}^{i}\overline{x^{c}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}\right)+(c.c.)\\
&=\sum_{g,g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\left(x_{g_{1}}^{c}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}-x_{g_{1}}^{i}\overline{x^{c}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}+\overline{x_{g_{1}}^{c}}x_{g_{2}}^{i}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}g^{-1}_{1}g}}x_{g}^{i}-\overline{x_{g_{1}}^{i}}x_{g_{2}}^{c}\overline{x_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}^{i}}x_{g}^{i}\right).\end{aligned}

Since
g_{1},g_{2}
and
g
are dummy variables, one has

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{g,g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\left(x_{g_{1}}^{c}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}-x_{g_{1}}^{i}\overline{x^{c}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}+\overline{x_{g_{1}}^{c}}x_{g_{2}}^{i}\overline{x_{g_{2}g^{-1}_{1}g}^{i}}x_{g}^{i}-\overline{x_{g_{1}}^{i}}x_{g_{2}}^{c}\overline{x_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}^{i}}x_{g}^{i}\right)\\
&=\sum_{g,g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\left(x_{g_{1}}^{c}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}-x_{g_{1}}^{i}\overline{x^{c}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}+\overline{x_{g_{2}}^{c}}x_{g_{1}}^{i}\overline{x_{g_{1}g^{-1}_{2}g}^{i}}x_{g}^{i}-\overline{x_{g_{2}}^{i}}x_{g_{1}}^{c}\overline{x_{g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g}^{i}}x_{g}^{i}\right)\\
&=\sum_{g,g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\left(x_{g_{1}}^{c}(\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}}}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}-\overline{x_{g_{2}}^{i}}\overline{x_{g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g}^{i}}x_{g}^{i})+\overline{x_{g_{2}}^{c}}(x_{g_{1}}^{i}\overline{x_{g_{1}g^{-1}_{2}g}^{i}}x_{g}^{i}-x_{g_{1}}^{i}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}})\right)\\
&=\sum_{g,g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\left(x_{g_{1}}^{c}\overline{x^{i}}_{g_{2}}(x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}-\overline{x_{g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g}^{i}}x_{g}^{i})+\overline{x_{g_{2}}^{c}}x_{g_{1}}^{i}(\overline{x_{g_{1}g^{-1}_{2}g}^{i}}x_{g}^{i}-x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}})\right)\\
&=\sum_{g,g_{1},g_{2}\in G}x_{g_{1}}^{c}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}}}\left(x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}-\overline{x_{g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g}^{i}}x_{g}^{i}\right)+(c.c.)\\
&=\sum_{g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\left[x_{g_{1}}^{c}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}}}\sum_{g\in G}\Big(x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}-\overline{x_{g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g}^{i}}x_{g}^{i}\Big)\right]+(c.c.),\end{aligned}

where in the second line, we used index exchange transformation
g_{1}\longleftrightarrow g_{2}
. Now, we use the property of the dummy variables:

\sum_{g\in G}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}=\sum_{g\in G}x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}(g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g)}\overline{x_{g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g}^{i}}=\sum_{g\in G}x^{i}_{g}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g}},

i.e., for all

, one has

(2.10)

\sum_{g\in G}(x^{i}_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g}\overline{x_{g}^{i}}-\overline{x_{g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g}^{i}}x_{g}^{i})=\sum_{g\in G}(x^{i}_{g}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g}}-\overline{x_{g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}g}^{i}}x_{g}^{i})=0.

Finally, we use (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) to get the desired estimate.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
G=\{e\}
and
\{x^{i}\}
be a solution to system (
1.4
) with
\|x^{i}(0)\|_{F}=1
, and we set

(2.11)

x^{i}=e^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta^{i}},\quad i=1,\cdots,N.

Then the phase configuration
\{\theta^{i}\}
satisfies the Kuramoto model for identical oscillators:

\frac{d\theta^{i}}{dt}=\frac{2\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin(\theta^{k}-\theta^{i}),\quad i=1,\cdots,N.

Proof.

By Proposition

x^{i}=x^{i}_{e}e,\quad|x^{i}_{e}|=1,\quad i=1,\cdots,N,

and its component

satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{dx^{i}_{e}}{dt}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(x_{e}^{k}-x_{e}^{i}\overline{x_{e}^{k}}x_{e}^{i}\Big),\quad i=1,\cdots,N.\end{aligned}

Next, we substitute the ansatz (
2
) into (

e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{i}}{\mathrm{i}}{\dot{\theta}}^{i}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{k}}-e^{\mathrm{i}(2\theta^{i}-\theta^{k})}\Big).

After further simplifications, one derive the desired Kuramoto model.
∎

Next, we show the invariance of system (
1.4
) under a group automorphism on
G
. More precisely, let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
be a group automorphism. Then, we can define its extension
\tilde{\varphi}:\mathbb{K}[G]\to\mathbb{K}[G]
by

\tilde{\varphi}\left(\sum_{g\in G}x_{g}g\right):=\sum_{g\in G}x_{g}\varphi(g)=\sum_{g\in G}x_{\varphi^{-1}(g)}g.

Then, it is easy to see

\tilde{\varphi}(g)=\tilde{\varphi}(1g)=1\varphi(g)=\varphi(g),\quad\forall~g\in G,\quad\mbox{i.e.,}\quad\tilde{\varphi}\Big|_{G}=\varphi.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
x,y\in\mathbb{K}[G]
and
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
. Then, its extension
\tilde{\varphi}
satisfies

\tilde{\varphi}(xy)=\tilde{\varphi}(x)\tilde{\varphi}(y)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{\varphi}(x^{\dagger})=\tilde{\varphi}(x)^{\dagger}.

Proof.

(i) We use the relation (
1.2
) to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{\varphi}(xy)&=\tilde{\varphi}\Big(\sum_{g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\in G}x_{g^{\prime}}y_{g^{\prime\prime}}(g^{\prime}g^{\prime\prime})\Big)=\sum_{g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\in G}x_{g^{\prime}}y_{g^{\prime\prime}}\varphi(g^{\prime}g^{\prime\prime})=\sum_{g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\in G}x_{g^{\prime}}y_{g^{\prime\prime}}\varphi(g^{\prime})\varphi(g^{\prime\prime})\\
&=\Big(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in G}x_{g^{\prime}}\varphi(g^{\prime})\Big)\Big(\sum_{g^{\prime\prime}\in G}x_{g^{\prime\prime}}\varphi(g^{\prime\prime})\Big)=\tilde{\varphi}(x)\tilde{\varphi}(y).\end{aligned}

(ii) By definition of hermitian conjugation, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{\varphi}(x^{\dagger})&=\tilde{\varphi}\Big(\sum_{g\in G}\bar{x_{g}}g^{-1}\Big)=\sum_{g\in G}\bar{x_{g}}\varphi(g^{-1})=\sum_{g\in G}\overline{x_{g}}\varphi(g)^{-1}=\Big(\tilde{\varphi}\Big(\sum_{g\in G}x_{g}g\Big)\Big)^{\dagger}=\tilde{\varphi}(x)^{\dagger}.\end{aligned}

∎

In the following proposition, we show that the aggregation model (
1.4
) is invariant under the automorphism
\tilde{\varphi}\in\mbox{Aut}(\mathbb{C}[G])
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}:=\{x^{i}\}
be a solution to system (
1.4
), and
\varphi\in\mbox{Aut}(G)
. Then
y^{i}
defined by

y^{i}:=\tilde{\varphi}(x^{i}),\quad i=1,\cdots,N

satisfies the same system (
1.4
):

\frac{dy^{i}}{dt}=\kappa\Big[y^{c}(y^{i})^{\dagger}y^{i}-y^{i}(y^{c})^{\dagger}y^{i}\Big],

where
y^{c}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}y^{i}
.

Proof.

Recall
x^{i}
satisfies

\frac{dx^{i}}{dt}=\kappa\Big[(x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{i}\Big].

Then we use Lemma
2.3
to see

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{dy^{i}}{dt}&=\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{\varphi}(x^{i})=\tilde{\varphi}\left(\frac{dx^{i}}{dt}\right)=\kappa\tilde{\varphi}\left(x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{i}\right)\\
&=\kappa\Big[\tilde{\varphi}(x^{c})\tilde{\varphi}(x^{i})^{\dagger}\tilde{\varphi}(x^{i})-\tilde{\varphi}(x^{i})\tilde{\varphi}(x^{c})^{\dagger}\tilde{\varphi}(x^{i})\Big]=\kappa\Big[y^{c}(y^{i})^{\dagger}y^{i}-y^{i}(y^{c})^{\dagger}y^{i}\Big].\end{aligned}

∎

3.
Large-time behavior of the flow

In this section, we study asymptotic convergence of (
1.4
) toward an equilibrium manifold, and analyze the geometric structure of the equilibrium manifold for the special finite groups
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

Recall the aggregation model:

(3.1)

\frac{dx^{i}}{dt}=\kappa\Big[x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{i}\Big],\quad i=1,\cdots,N.

Then, we define two sets associated with (
3.1
):

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{E}(\mathbb{K}[G])&:=\left\{X\in\mathbb{K}[G]^{N}:~x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{i}=0_{G},\quad\forall~i=1,\cdots,N\right\},\\
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{K}[G])&:=\left\{X\in\mathbb{K}[G]^{N}:~x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}=0_{G},\quad\forall~i=1,\cdots,N\right\}.\end{aligned}

Note that the set
\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{K}[G])
consists of all equilibria, and the completely aggregated state
X_{eq}=(x,\cdots,x)\in\mathbb{K}[G]^{N}
lies in the equilibrium set
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{K}[G])
. In this case, since
x^{c}=x
,

x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{i}=xx^{\dagger}x-xx^{\dagger}x=0_{G}.

This yields

x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{i}=(x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger})x^{i}=0_{G},

we can easily see

{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{K}[G])\subset{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{K}[G]).

The other inclusion is true, thus the above two sets are in fact the same (see Remark
3.1
).

3.1.
Asymptotic convergence to
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{K}[G])

In this subsection, we study the asymptotic convergence of the flow generated by system (
1.4
) using the LaSalle invariance principle
[
31
]
. For this, we define two nonlinear functionals measuring the degree of aggregation for the configuration
\mathcal{X}=\{x^{i}\}
with
\|x^{i}\|_{F}=1
:

(3.3)

R^{2}:=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\langle x^{i},x^{j}\rangle_{F}=\|x^{c}\|_{F}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad V:=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\|x^{i}-x^{j}\|_{F}^{2}.

Then, it is easy to see

(3.4)

0\leq R\leq 1,\quad V=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\Big(2-\langle x^{i},x^{j}\rangle_{F}-\langle x^{j},x^{i}\rangle_{F}\Big)=2-2R^{2}\geq 0.

In next lemma, we show that
V
satisfies a Lyapunov estimate along the flow (
1.4
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that the coupling strength and initial data satisfy

\kappa>0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|x^{i}(0)\|_{F}=1\quad\forall~i=1,2,\cdots,N,

and let
\{x^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a solution to system (
1.4
). Then, one has

\frac{dV}{dt}=-\frac{2\kappa}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}\leq 0.

Proof.

Since

\frac{dV}{dt}=-2\frac{dR^{2}}{dt}=-\frac{2}{N^{2}}\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\langle x^{i},x^{j}\rangle_{F}.

Hence it suffices to check

(3.5)

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\langle x^{i},x^{j}\rangle_{F}=N\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}.

Derivation of (
3.5
)
: We use (
1.4
) and Lemma
2.1
to find

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}\langle x^{i},x^{j}\rangle_{F}&=\kappa\left\langle\frac{dx^{i}}{dt},x^{j}\right\rangle_{F}+\kappa\left\langle x^{i},\frac{dx^{j}}{dt}\right\rangle_{F}\\
&=\kappa\Big\langle x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{i},x^{j}\Big\rangle_{F}+\kappa\Big\langle x^{i},x^{c}(x^{j})^{\dagger}x^{j}-x^{j}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{j}\Big\rangle_{F}\\
&=\kappa\mathrm{tr}\left((x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{j}-(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{j}+(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{c}(x^{j})^{\dagger}x^{j}-(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{j}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{j}\right)\\
&=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathrm{tr}\left((x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}(x^{k})^{\dagger}x^{j}-(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{k}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{j}+(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{k}(x^{j})^{\dagger}x^{j}-(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{j}(x^{k})^{\dagger}x^{j}\right).\end{aligned}

We sum up the above relation over
i,j
to derive (
3.5
):

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\langle x^{i},x^{j}\rangle_{F}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}\mathrm{tr}\left((x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}(x^{k})^{\dagger}x^{j}-(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{k}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{j}+(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{k}(x^{j})^{\dagger}x^{j}-(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{j}(x^{k})^{\dagger}x^{j}\right)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt=N\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{tr}\left((x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{c}-(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{c}+(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}-(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{i}\right)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt=N\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{tr}\left(x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}-x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}+x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}\right)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt=N\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger},x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}\right\rangle_{F}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt=N\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}.\end{aligned}

This and (
3.3
) yield

(3.6)

\frac{dR^{2}}{dt}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}\geq 0.

Again, (
3.6
) and
\eqref{C-3}_{2}
imply the desired estimate.

∎

Remark 3.1
.

As a ramification of Lemma
3.1
, we obtain the following assertions:

(1)

Since
V
is nonnegative and non-increasing over time, there exists an asymptotic limit
V_{\infty}
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}V(t)=V_{\infty}.

(2)

Suppose that
{\mathcal{Y}}=\{y^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{K}[G])
is an equilibrium, i.e., it is stationary. Thus
R
must be constant and
\frac{dR}{dt}=0
. Then, it follows from Lemma
3.1
that

0=\frac{dR^{2}}{dt}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|y^{c}(y^{i})^{\dagger}-y^{i}(y^{c})^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2},\quad\mbox{i.e.,}\quad{\mathcal{Y}}\in{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{K}[G]).

Thus, two sets defined in (
3.2
) are the same:

{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{K}[G])={\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{K}[G]).

Next, we state our first main result on the asymptotic convergence of the flow.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\{x^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a solution to system (
1.4
) with the initial data satisfying

\|x^{i}(0)\|_{F}=1\quad\forall i=1,2,\cdots,N.

Then, the flow approaches to the equilibrium manifold
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{K}[G])
asymptotically in the sense that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathrm{dist}(X(t),{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{K}[G]))=0.

Proof.

Let
X(t)
be a given flow. Then, the bounded Lyapunov functional
V
satisfies Lyapunov estimate (see Lemma
3.1
):

\frac{dV}{dt}=-\frac{2\kappa}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}\leq 0.

Then, the zero set of the orbital derivative
\frac{dV}{dt}
exactly coincides with
{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{K}[G])
which is a subset of equilibrium manifold, (in fact, we will see that they are the same).
Thus, the largest invariant subset of
{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{K}[G])
is itself, and by LaSalle’s invariance principle, we can confirm the asymptotic convergence of the flow
X(t)
to the set
{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{K}[G])={\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{K}[G])
.
∎

In the following two subsections, we consider the group ring
\mathbb{R}[G]
over the real number field, and study the structure of the equilibrium set for the following two special cases for
G
:

G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\quad\mbox{or}\quad\mathbb{Z}_{3}.

3.2.
Geometric structure of
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{2}])

In this subsection, we show that
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{2}])
can be the whole state space itself: every solution is an equilibrium.

Proposition 3.1
.

The equilibrium set
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{2}])
is the whole state space:

{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{2}])=\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{2}]^{N}.

Proof.

We set
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{e,a\}
and recall the equilibrium manifold for (
1.4
):

\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{2}]):=\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{2}]^{N}:~x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}=0_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\right\}.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}=\{x^{i}\}
be a solution to (
1.4
) on
\mathbb{R}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. Then, we claim that
{\mathcal{X}}
is an equilibrium for (
1.4
):

(3.7)

x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}=0_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\quad\mbox{i.e.,}\quad\frac{dx^{i}}{dt}=0,\quad t>0,\quad\forall~i=1,2,\cdots,N.

Proof of (
3.7
)
: Note that

(3.8)

g^{2}=e\quad\forall~g\in\mathbb{Z}_{2},\quad g_{1}g_{2}=g_{2}g_{1},\quad g_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}-x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g_{1},g_{2}}\left(x^{c}_{g_{1}}\overline{x^{i}_{g_{2}}}g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}-x^{i}_{g_{1}}\overline{x^{c}_{g_{2}}}g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}\right)=\sum_{g_{1},g_{2}}\left(x^{c}_{g_{1}}{x}^{i}_{g_{2}}g_{1}g_{2}-x^{i}_{g_{2}}{x}^{c}_{g_{1}}g_{2}g_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g_{1},g_{2}}\left(x^{c}_{g_{1}}{x}^{i}_{g_{2}}-x^{i}_{g_{2}}{x}^{c}_{g_{1}}\right)g_{1}g_{2}=0_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}.

Thus,
X
is an equilibrium.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Note that the same argument employed in the proof can be applied to the following group:

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{2}\simeq\{\underbrace{(\pm 1,\pm 1,\cdots,\pm 1)}_{n\text{ times}}\},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

so that

{\mathcal{E}}\left(\mathbb{R}\left[\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{2}\right]\right)=\mathbb{R}\left[\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{2}\right]^{N}.

3.3.
Geometric structure of
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])

Consider the case
\mathbb{Z}_{3}=\{e,a,a^{2}\}
. Then, any element
x\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}]
with a unit norm has the unique representation for
x
:

x=x_{e}e+x_{a}a+x_{a^{2}}a^{2},\quad x_{e}^{2}+x_{a}^{2}+x_{a^{2}}^{2}=1.

Note that
\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}]
can be embedded into
\mathbb{S}^{2}
: there exists an injective map
\phi
:

\phi:\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}]\to\mathbb{S}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad\mbox{by}~~\phi(x)=(x_{e},x_{a},x_{a^{2}}).

Proposition 3.2
.

The equilibrium manifold
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])
can be a disjoint union of the following three submanifolds:

{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])={\mathcal{E}}_{1}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{3}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}]),

where

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathcal{E}}_{1}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])&:=\{\{x^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}]:~x^{c}=0_{\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\},\\
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])&:=\{\{x^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}]:~\langle x^{i},y\rangle=0\mbox{ for some }y\neq 0_{\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\},\\
{\mathcal{E}}_{3}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])&:=\{\{x^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}]:~\phi(x^{c})\sslash(1,1,1)\}.\end{aligned}

Proof.

Suppose that
\{x^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is an equilibrium solution:

(3.10)

x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}=x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger},\quad i=1,\cdots,N.

Now we set

(3.11)

x^{c}=x_{e}^{c}e+x_{a}^{c}a+x_{a^{2}}^{c}a^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad x^{i}=x_{e}^{i}e+x_{a}^{i}a+x_{a^{2}}^{i}a^{2}.

Then, their hermitian conjugates are

(3.12)

(x^{i})^{\dagger}=x_{e}^{i}e+x_{a^{2}}^{i}a+x_{a}^{i}a^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(x^{c})^{\dagger}=x_{e}^{c}e+x_{a^{2}}^{c}a+x_{a}^{c}a^{2},

where we used

e^{-1}=e,\quad a^{-1}=a^{2},\quad(a^{2})^{-1}=a.

In (
3.10
), we use (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) to find that for each
i=1,\cdots,N
,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} x^{c}(x^{i})^{\dagger}&=x_{e}^{c}x_{e}^{i}e*e+x_{e}^{c}x_{a^{2}}^{i}e*a+x_{e}^{c}x_{a}^{i}e*a^{2}+x_{a}^{c}x_{e}^{i}a*e+x_{a}^{c}x_{a^{2}}^{i}a*a+x_{a}^{c}x_{a}^{i}a*a^{2}\\
&\hskip 5.69046pt+x_{a^{2}}^{c}x_{e}^{i}a^{2}*e+x_{a^{2}}^{c}x_{a^{2}}^{i}a^{2}*a+x_{a^{2}}^{c}x_{a}^{i}a^{2}*a^{2}\\
&=\Big(x_{e}^{c}x_{e}^{i}+x_{a}^{c}x_{a}^{i}+x_{a^{2}}^{c}x_{a^{2}}^{i}\Big)e+\Big(x_{e}^{c}x_{a^{2}}^{i}+x_{a}^{c}x_{e}^{i}+x_{a^{2}}^{c}x_{a}^{i}\Big)a\\
&\hskip 5.69046pt+\Big(x_{e}^{c}x_{a}^{i}+x_{a}^{c}x_{a^{2}}^{i}+x_{a^{2}}^{c}x_{e}^{i}\Big)a^{2}.\end{aligned}

Similarly, one has

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} x^{i}(x^{c})^{\dagger}&=\Big(x_{e}^{i}x_{e}^{c}+x_{a}^{i}x_{a}^{c}+x_{a^{2}}^{i}x_{a^{2}}^{c}\Big)e+\Big(x_{e}^{i}x_{a^{2}}^{c}+x_{a}^{i}x_{e}^{c}+x_{a^{2}}^{i}x_{a}^{c}\Big)a\\
&\hskip 5.69046pt+\Big(x_{e}^{i}x_{a}^{c}+x_{a}^{i}x_{a^{2}}^{c}+x_{a^{2}}^{i}x_{e}^{c}\Big)a^{2}.\end{aligned}

We compare (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) to get

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} e:\quad x_{e}^{c}x_{e}^{i}+x_{a}^{c}x_{a}^{i}+x_{a^{2}}^{c}x_{a^{2}}^{i}=x_{e}^{i}x_{e}^{c}+x_{a}^{i}x_{a}^{c}+x_{a^{2}}^{i}x_{a^{2}}^{c},\\
a:\quad x_{e}^{c}x_{a^{2}}^{i}+x_{a}^{c}x_{e}^{i}+x_{a^{2}}^{c}x_{a}^{i}=x_{e}^{i}x_{a^{2}}^{c}+x_{a}^{i}x_{e}^{c}+x_{a^{2}}^{i}x_{a}^{c},\\
a^{2}:\quad x_{e}^{c}x_{a}^{i}+x_{a}^{c}x_{a^{2}}^{i}+x_{a^{2}}^{c}x_{e}^{i}=x_{e}^{i}x_{a}^{c}+x_{a}^{i}x_{a^{2}}^{c}+x_{a^{2}}^{i}x_{e}^{c}.\end{aligned}

Note that the relation
\eqref{C-9}_{1}
trivially true, and if we set

\phi^{i}:=\phi(x^{i}),\quad i.e.,\quad(x_{e}^{i},x_{a}^{i},x_{a^{2}}^{i})=(\phi_{1}^{i},\phi_{2}^{i},\phi_{3}^{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Then relations
\eqref{C-9}_{2}
and
\eqref{C-9}_{3}
reduce to the following single relation:

\phi^{c}_{1}\phi^{i}_{3}+\phi^{c}_{2}\phi^{i}_{1}+\phi^{c}_{3}\phi^{i}_{2}=\phi^{i}_{1}\phi^{c}_{3}+\phi^{i}_{2}\phi^{c}_{1}+\phi^{i}_{3}\phi^{c}_{2},

which can be rewritten as

(3.16)

(\phi_{2}^{c}\phi_{3}^{i}-\phi_{3}^{c}\phi^{i}_{2})+(\phi_{3}^{c}\phi_{1}^{i}-\phi_{1}^{c}\phi^{i}_{3})+(\phi_{1}^{c}\phi_{2}^{i}-\phi_{2}^{c}\phi^{i}_{1})=0.

We can further simplify the relation (
3.16
) as

(1,1,1)\cdot(\phi^{c}\times\phi^{i})=0.

By the vector identity, this yields

(3.17)

\phi^{i}\cdot((1,1,1)\times\phi^{c})=0.

The analysis for the relation (
3.17
) yields the complete classification of
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])
. Now, we consider all cases satisfying the relation (
3.17
).

\bullet
Case A: Suppose

(1,1,1)\times\phi^{c}=0.

In this case, we have the following two cases:

\mbox{Either}~\phi^{c}=0\quad\mbox{or}\quad 0\not=\phi^{c}\sslash(1,1,1).

\bullet
Case B: Suppose

(1,1,1)\times\phi^{c}\not=0.

In this case, if we set
y:=(1,1,1)\times\phi^{c}\not=0
, then

\langle\phi^{i},y\rangle=\langle x^{i},y\rangle=0,\quad\mbox{for all $i=1,\cdots,N$}.

Hence, it follows from Case A and Case B that the equilibrium set
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])
in (
3.2
) can be decomposed into three pieces:

{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])={\mathcal{E}}_{1}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{3}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}]),

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathcal{E}}_{1}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])&:=\{\{x^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}]:~x^{c}=0_{G}\},\\
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])&:=\{\{x^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}]:~\langle x^{i},y\rangle=0\mbox{ for some }y\neq 0_{G}\},\\
{\mathcal{E}}_{3}(\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}])&:=\{\{x^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}]:~\phi(x^{c})\sslash(1,1,1)\}.\end{aligned}

If we visualize the element of

with embedding

,

lies on the same great circle as following figure.

4.
Geometry of the equilibrium manifold

In this section, we extend the results for
\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}_{3}]
to the equilibrium manifold
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{R}[G])
for
\mathbb{R}[G]
.

Recall the equilibrium set for (
1.4
):

(4.1)

\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{R}[G])=\left\{Y\in\mathbb{R}[G]^{N}:y^{c}(y^{i})^{\dagger}-y^{i}(y^{c})^{\dagger}=0_{G},\quad i=1,\cdots,N~~\right\},

where
y^{c}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}y^{i}
.

4.1.
Geometric structure of an equilibrium manifold

To provide an alternative representation of
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{R}[G])
, we introduce an elementary matrix-like object
A^{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{G\times G}
for each
g\in G
:

(4.2)

\displaystyle[A^{g}]_{g_{1},g_{2}}:=\begin{cases}1,\quad\text{when }g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}=g,\\
0,\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In the following lemma, we study several properties of
A^{g}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
g\in G
, let
A^{g}
be a matrix-like object defined by (
4.2
). Then, it satisfies

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &[A^{g}]_{g_{1},g_{2}}=[A^{g^{-1}}]_{g_{2},g_{1}},\quad(A^{g})^{T}=A^{g^{-1}},\quad A^{g}A^{\tilde{g}}=A^{g\tilde{g}},\\
&A^{g_{1}}\neq A^{g_{2}}\quad\mbox{if $g_{1}\neq g_{2}$}.\end{aligned}

Proof.

\bullet
(First identity): For
g\in G
, we use (
4.2
) to see

[A^{g}]_{g_{1},g_{2}}=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}=g\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}=g^{-1}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad[A^{g^{-1}}]_{g_{2},g_{1}}=1.

\bullet
(Second identity): Now we take a transpose the first relation: for
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
,

[(A^{g})^{T}]_{g_{1},g_{2}}=[A^{g}]_{g_{2},g_{1}}=[A^{g^{-1}}]_{g_{1},g_{2}},

which yields the desired second identity.

\bullet
(Third identity): It suffices to check

\sum_{g_{2}\in G}[A^{g}]_{g_{1},g_{2}}[A^{\tilde{g}}]_{g_{2},g_{3}}=[A^{g\tilde{g}}]_{g_{1},g_{3}}.

Recall Kronecker-delta function defined on a group
G
:

\delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}=\begin{cases}1,\quad\text{when }g_{1}=g_{2}\in G,\\
0,\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For
g_{1},g_{3}\in G

(4.4)

\sum_{g_{2}\in G}[A^{g}]_{g_{1},g_{2}}[A^{\tilde{g}}]_{g_{2},g_{3}}=\sum_{g_{2}\in G}\delta_{g,g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}}\delta_{\tilde{g},g_{2}g_{3}^{-1}}.

On the other hand, we use

\delta_{g,g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}}=\delta_{gg_{2},g_{1}}=\delta_{g_{2},g^{-1}g_{1}}

to find

g_{2}=g^{-1}g_{1}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\delta_{g,g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}}=1.

Thus, one has

(4.5)

\sum_{g_{2}\in G}\delta_{g,g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}}\delta_{\tilde{g},g_{2}g_{3}^{-1}}=\delta_{\tilde{g},g_{2}g_{3}^{-1}}\Big|_{g_{2}=g^{-1}g_{1}}=\delta_{\tilde{g},g^{-1}g_{1}g_{3}^{-1}}=\delta_{g\tilde{g},g_{1}g_{3}^{-1}}=[A^{g\tilde{g}}]_{g_{1},g_{3}}.

Finally, we combine (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) to find the desired estimate.

\bullet
(Forth identity): Suppose
g_{1}\neq g_{2}
. Then, we have

[A^{g_{1}}]_{g_{1},e}=1,\quad[A^{g_{2}}]_{g_{2},e}=0,

which yields
A^{g_{1}}\neq A^{g_{2}}
.
∎

(4.9)

Proof of the second assertion

Our second statement in Theorem

Recall the subgroup

and we claim: for

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(i)~\mathrm{Null}\left(A^{g}-A^{g^{-1}}\right)=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}(G):x_{g_{1}}=x_{g_{2}}\quad\text{for $g_{1}$ and $g_{2}$ such that}~H(g)g_{1}=H(g)g_{2}\Big\}.\\

\bullet
(Derivation of (i)): Let
g\in G
be any, and suppose that

:

\left(A^{g}-A^{g^{-1}}\right)x=0_{G}.

Then,
g_{1}
-th component of above relation is

(4.16)

\sum_{g_{2}\in G}\left[A^{g}-A^{g^{-1}}\right]_{g_{1},g_{2}}~x_{g_{2}}=0.

By definition of

in (
4.2
), one has

(4.17)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{g_{2}\in G}\left[A^{g}-A^{g^{-1}}\right]_{g_{1},g_{2}}~x_{g_{2}}&=\sum_{g_{2}\in G}\left(\delta_{g,g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}}-\delta_{g^{-1},g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}}\right)x_{g_{2}}\\
&=\sum_{g_{2}\in G}\left(\delta_{g_{2},g^{-1}g_{1}}-\delta_{g_{2},gg_{1}}\right)x_{g_{2}}=x_{g^{-1}g_{1}}-x_{gg_{1}}.\end{aligned}

It follows from (
4.16
) and (
4.17
) that

x_{g^{-1}g_{1}}=x_{gg_{1}}\quad\forall g_{1}\in G.

This yields

(4.18)

\displaystyle x_{g_{1}}=x_{g^{2}g_{1}}\quad\forall g_{1}\in G.

Hence, we have shown that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} x\in\mathrm{Null}\left(A^{g}-A^{g^{-1}}\right)&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_{g_{1}}=x_{g^{2}g_{1}}\quad\forall g_{1}\in G\\

On the other hand, note that

(4.20)

H(g)g_{1}=H(g)g_{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g_{2}=(g^{2})^{k}g_{1}\quad\mbox{for some $k\in\mathbb{Z}$}.

which verifies the first estimate (i).

Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied how the aggregation dynamics on a group ring can interplay with the group structure. For this, we have proposed a first-order aggregation model on group ring, and showed that the flow generated by the proposed model approaches to the equilibrium manifold for all initial data in a positive coupling regime. Our convergence result is based on the Lyapunov functional approach and the LaSalle invariance principle. Unfortunately, we cannot guarantee whether the flow will converge to some equilibrium along the full flow, since we do not have enough information on the explicit structure of the equilibrium manifold. In a full generic setting, we can decompose the equilibrium manifold as a disjoint union of three submanifolds. The geometric structure of one of such manifold depends on the group structure. For a cyclic group with prime order, we explicit analyzed the structures of the equilibrium sets. There are several issues that we do not consider here. For example, we did not show whether the flow can converge to some equilibrium along the full flow, i.e., we did not exclude a possibility of limit cycle in current analysis. Moreover, our convergence rate to the equilibrium manifold is also not explicit. Our proposed model is only for the aggregate modeling of identical particles. Thus, how to incorporate non-identical particles in our current modeling is also an interesting open question. We leave these interesting problems for a future work.

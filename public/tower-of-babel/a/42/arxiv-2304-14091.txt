Correlation Functions in T ​ T ¯ {\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}} -deformed Conformal Field Theories


Abstract
We study the correlation functions of local operators in unitary T ​ T ¯ {\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}} -deformed field theories, using their formulation in terms of Jackiw-Teitelboim gravity. The position of the operators is defined using the dynamical coordinates of this formalism. We focus on the two-point correlation function in momentum space, when the undeformed theory is a conformal field theory. In particular, we compute the large momentum behavior of the correlation functions, which manifests the non-locality of the T ​ T ¯ {\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}} -deformed theory. The correlation function has UV-divergences, which are regulated by a point-splitting regulator. Renormalizing the operators requires multiplicative factors depending on the momentum, unlike the behavior in local QFTs. The large momentum limit of the correlator, which is the main result of this paper, is proportional to | q | − q 2 π ​ | Λ | |q|^{-\frac{q^{2}}{\pi|\Lambda|}} , where q q is the momentum and 1 / | Λ | 1/|\Lambda| is the deformation parameter. Interestingly, the exponent here has a different sign from earlier results obtained by resummation of small q q computations. The decay at large momentum implies that the operators behave non-locally at the scale set by the deformation parameter.

In the JT-description, the action is given by:

S_{\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}}(\psi,g_{\alpha\beta},\varphi)=S_{0}(\psi,g_{\alpha\beta})+\int d^{2}\sigma\sqrt{g}\left(\varphi R-\Lambda\right),

&=S_{0}(\psi,e^{a}_{\alpha})+\frac{\Lambda}{2}\int d^{2}\sigma\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{ab}(\partial_{\alpha}X^{a}-e^{a}_{\alpha})(\partial_{\beta}X^{b}-e^{b}_{\beta}).\end{split}

(10)

\mathcal{O}(X_{0})=\int d^{2}\sigma\sqrt{g(\sigma)}O(\sigma)\delta\left(X(\sigma)-X_{0}\right),

\mathcal{O}(q_{0})=\int d^{2}\sigma\sqrt{g(\sigma)}O(\sigma)\exp\left(iq_{0}\cdot X(\sigma)\right).

\begin{split}C(q_{1},q_{2})\equiv&\left<\mathcal{O}(q_{1})\mathcal{O}(q_{2})\right>=\frac{1}{Z_{{\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}}}}\int\frac{\mathcal{D}e\mathcal{D}X\mathcal{D}\psi}{V_{\textrm{diff}}}\mathcal{O}(q_{1})\mathcal{O}(q_{2})e^{-S_{\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}}}\\

(13)

where the partition function is:

Z_{{\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}}}\equiv\int\frac{\mathcal{D}e\mathcal{D}X\mathcal{D}\psi}{V_{\textrm{diff}}}e^{-S_{\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}}}.

(14)

We will calculate the two-point correlator (
13

In particular, we will be interested in the large momentum limit of this correlator, and use it to learn about locality properties of the
{\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}}

Given the non-locality, another natural question is to compare the calculation on an infinite plane to that on a torus. This question will not be answered here, although the calculation on a torus is similar in many ways to the one that will be presented here.

2
General Properties of the Action

In this section some properties of the JT-action (
10
) are discussed. TS indices will be written using Latin letters and WS indices using Greek letters. With respect to the WS and the TS, the field content in the action has the following properties:

1.

\psi
- The fields of the undeformed theory. They have some structure with respect to the WS, and we consider some primary operator
O
which is built using them. They are scalars with respect to the TS.

2.

X^{a}
- Coordinates on the TS. As such, they are scalars of the WS. Because the TS is flat, the coordinates behave as a vector. We are interested in mappings from the WS to the TS which cover it once, so it will be convenient to write
X^{a}(\sigma)
in the form:

X^{a}(\sigma)\equiv I^{a}_{\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}+Y^{a}(\sigma)

(15)

(where
I^{a}_{\alpha}
is a constant matrix whose values are
I^{1}_{1}=I^{2}_{2}=1,\ I^{1}_{2}=I^{2}_{1}=0
).
The first term in (
15
) describes passing over the whole TS, the analogue of “winding one” on a torus, and will be called the winding part. The second term describes local changes from it, which are finite as
|\sigma|\to\infty
.

3.

e^{a}_{\alpha}
- The vielbein. They are vectors of the TS and co-vectors (1-form) of the WS.

We add here three comments. The first concerns the limit
|\Lambda|\to\infty
, where the undeformed correlation function should be recovered. In this limit, one can use a saddle point approximation for
S_{JT}
in (
10
), and the path-integral (PI) over
\mathcal{D}e\mathcal{D}X
is trivial (after an appropriate gauge-fixing). Therefore, one has to replace any appearance of
e^{a}_{\alpha},X^{a}
by their values at the saddle point (“saddle”):

\begin{split}e^{a}_{\alpha}(\sigma)&\rightarrow I^{a}_{\alpha},\\
X^{a}(\sigma)&\rightarrow I^{a}_{\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}.\end{split}

(16)

Then, one gets the following limits:

\begin{split}Z_{{\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}}}&\rightarrow\int\mathcal{D}\psi e^{-S_{0}(\psi,\delta)+0}=Z_{0},\\
\mathcal{O}(q)&\rightarrow\int d^{2}\sigma\cdot 1\cdot O(\sigma)e^{iq\cdot\sigma}=O(q),\end{split}

(17)

and the two-point correlator reduces to the momentum space correlation function of the undeformed theory, as expected.

The second comment is about regularization and renormalization. The theory will not be regularized from the beginning. Instead, progress in formal developments will be made, as far as possible, and only after gathering all the divergent pieces, it will be regularized. In this way, the calculations are much easier, and it will be clear what is the appropriate UV-regularization to use. It will be chosen such that the assumptions made while deriving the formal expression will be justified. As for IR-regularization, one way to do it is to put the theory on a torus. However, in this paper, for simplicity, we will not go into any details of this regularization, but just use its general features.

The third comment concerns the decomposition (
15
). By using this decomposition, we assume specific boundary conditions for the
X^{a}(\sigma)
and for allowed diffeomorphisms
\sigma^{\prime}(\sigma)
at
|\sigma|\to\infty
. In particular, the allowed diffeomorphisms should go to the identity map plus some bounded function, and
Y^{a}(\sigma)
should have a Fourier transform. We could also use boundary conditions for the vielbein
e^{a}_{\alpha}(\sigma)\to I^{a}_{\alpha}
as
|\sigma|\to\infty
. However, we will be more general, and require only that the WS should be flat at the boundary, i.e., constant vielbein. In the infinite plane limit, it will become
I^{a}_{\alpha}
dynamically. The boundary conditions for
\psi(\sigma)
are those used in the undeformed CFT. We refer the reader to appendix
B
for more details.

Path Integral Calculation

The diffeomorphism freedom in the computation (
13
) will be gauge-fixed following the usual FP procedure. We choose the orthogonal gauge (sometimes called the conformal gauge), where the vielbein takes the form:

\bar{e}^{a}_{\alpha}({\sigma})\equiv e^{\Omega(\sigma)}(e^{\epsilon\phi(\sigma)})^{\beta}_{\alpha}I^{a}_{\beta}\ \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}({\sigma})=e^{2\Omega(\sigma)}\delta_{\alpha\beta},

(18)

and we denote:

\epsilon^{\beta}_{\alpha}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\rightarrow\left(e^{\epsilon\phi}\right)^{\beta}_{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\phi)&\sin(\phi)\\
-\sin(\phi)&\cos(\phi)\end{pmatrix}.

(19)

It should be noted that the generally covariant action (
10

e^{a}_{\alpha}(\sigma)\rightarrow e^{\Omega(\sigma)}e^{a}_{\alpha}(\sigma)
cannot be compensated by other manipulations). Thus, after removing the diff-invariance redundancy by gauge-fixing the metric, the metric will remain with one degree of freedom (
3-2=1
). In the orthogonal gauge used here, this is the scaling factor
e^{2\Omega(\sigma)}
. As for the vielbein, except for the scaling factor
e^{\Omega(\sigma)}
, which the vielbein inherits from the metric, one has also their local orientation
\phi(\sigma)
. As usual, the orthogonal gauge choice leaves the freedom of conformal transformations, but most of these are not allowed by the boundary conditions; only the freedom of translations remains. They will be fixed by taking the coordinate of the first operator insertion to
\sigma_{1}^{\alpha}=0

Then, we use the FP identity for the integral over the space of diffeomorphisms
V

1=\int\mathcal{D}V\mathcal{D}\Omega\mathcal{D}\phi J(e,\sigma_{1})\delta\left(e^{(V)}-\bar{e}(\Omega,\phi)\right)\delta\left(\sigma_{1}^{(V)}-0\right).

(20)

The determinant
J(\bar{e},\sigma_{1})
can be calculated, and it is
\sqrt{\det(c)}

c^{\delta}_{\beta}=-\frac{1}{2}{\delta}^{\delta}_{\beta}\partial^{2}+\partial^{\delta}\Omega\partial_{\beta}-\partial_{\beta}\Omega\partial^{\delta}-{\delta}^{\delta}_{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\Omega\partial^{\alpha}+\frac{\delta(\sigma-\sigma_{1})}{\sqrt{\bar{g}(\sigma_{1})}}\delta^{\delta}_{\beta}.

(21)

Using the complex coordinates, the determinant
J(\bar{e},0)
takes the simple form:

J(\bar{e},0)=|\det\left(e^{-2\Omega}\right)|\left|\det(-\pd\bpd-4\pd\Om\bpd+ \de(w))\right|,

(22)

where:

\begin{split}&\partial\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial w},\ \bar{\partial}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{w}},\\
&d^{2}w\equiv 2d\sigma^{1}d\sigma^{2},\ \delta(w)\equiv\frac{\delta\left(\sigma^{1},\sigma^{2}\right)}{2}\Rightarrow\int d^{2}w\delta(w)=1.\end{split}

(23)

The details of the calculation are left for appendix
A
. Inserting (
20
) into (
13
) gives the following expression:

\begin{split}\int&\frac{\mathcal{D}V\mathcal{D}e\mathcal{D}X\mathcal{D}\psi\mathcal{D}\Omega\mathcal{D}\phi dA(\sigma_{1})dA(\sigma_{2})}{V_{\textrm{diff}}}\delta\left(e^{(V)}-\bar{e}(\Omega,\phi)\right)\delta\left(\sigma_{1}^{(V)}\right)\cdot\\
&\qquad\qquad J(e,\sigma_{1})O(\psi(\sigma_{1}))O(\psi(\sigma_{2}))e^{iq_{1}\cdot X(\sigma_{1})+iq_{2}\cdot X(\sigma_{2})-S_{\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}}(\psi,e,X)},\end{split}

(24)

where the WS area element
dA(\sigma)\equiv d^{2}\sigma\sqrt{g(\sigma)}
is used.

If the undeformed theory has a reflection symmetry then taking all diffeomorphisms and all vielbeins will give an overall factor of two. The Vielbein and Diffeomorphism Path Integral

\begin{split}\int&\frac{\mathcal{D}V\mathcal{D}e\mathcal{D}X\mathcal{D}\psi\mathcal{D}\Omega\mathcal{D}\phi dA(\sigma_{1})dA(\sigma_{2})}{V_{\textrm{diff}}}\delta\left(e^{(V)}-\bar{e}(\Omega,\phi)\right)\delta\left(\sigma_{1}^{(V)}\right)\cdot\\
&J\left(e^{(V)},\sigma_{1}^{(V)}\right)O\left(\psi^{(V)}(\sigma_{1}^{(V)})\right)O\left(\psi^{(V)}(\sigma_{2}^{(V)})\right)e^{iq_{1}\cdot X^{(V)}(\sigma_{1}^{(V)})+iq_{2}\cdot X^{(V)}(\sigma_{2}^{(V)})-S_{\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}}\left(\psi^{(V)},e^{(V)},X^{(V)}\right)}.\end{split}

\tilde{X}=X^{(V)}

\sigma_{1}
and

3.2
The Target-Space Coordinates Path Integral

The next PI to be evaluated is the

3.

\frac{1}{2}\int d^{2}\sigma\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{ab}\partial_{\alpha}Y^{a}\partial_{\beta}Y^{b}=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{2}\sigma\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{ab}\partial_{\alpha}(Y^{a}\partial_{\beta}Y^{b})

4.

\int d^{2}\sigma\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{ab}\partial_{\alpha}Y^{a}I^{b}_{\beta}
- this is also a boundary term and will be treated as the former.

5.

-\int d^{2}\sigma\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{ab}\bar{e}^{a}_{\alpha}I^{b}_{\beta}
.

6.

-\int d^{2}\sigma\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{ab}\bar{e}^{a}_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}Y^{b}
- this term alone will contribute to the EOM of
Y^{a}
(or

We change the

Going further with the electrostatic analogy, the energy density of the two electric fields is:

Point-like sources cause UV-divergences, in any dimension

\sigma_{i}

For an electric field created by some source distribution, it can happen only in one- or two-dimensions, and it is solely caused by the monopole moment. The dipole moment and higher moments do not cause any IR-divergences. However, momentum conservation ensures that the monopole moment vanishes. Another IR-divergence may be caused by a constant electric field, which is the general homogeneous solution of (
35

The modulus and phase of the summands relates to the modulus and phase of the result (in analogy with addition of complex numbers):

\begin{split}&f_{\Omega}(\Omega_{0},\bar{\Omega},\phi_{0},\bar{\phi})\equiv\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(e^{2\Omega_{0}}+e^{2\bar{\Omega}}+2e^{\Omega_{0}+\bar{\Omega}}\cos(\phi_0-\bar{\phi})\right),\\

3.5.1
General Case

It remains to find the non-homogeneous solution
\bar{e}_{s}

[\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}]_{w}=e^{2\Omega(\sigma)}\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{2}\\
\frac{1}{2}&0\end{pmatrix},

(55)

[\bar{e}^{a}_{\alpha}]_{w}=e^{\Omega(\sigma)}e^{\epsilon\phi(\sigma)}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}&\frac{1}{2}\\

-ie^{-i\phi}&ie^{i\phi}\end{pmatrix}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\bar{z}&z\\
-i\bar{z}&iz\end{pmatrix},

(56)

where

-\frac{i}{2}\begin{pmatrix}i(\partial z+\bar{\partial}\bar{z})\\
-\partial z+\bar{\partial}\bar{z}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}Q_{1}\\
Q_{2}\end{pmatrix}2\pi(-\delta(w-w_{1})+\delta(w-w_{2})),

(57)

\bar{\partial}\bar{z}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}\bar{Q}\\

(58)

\begin{split}&Q_{a}\equiv\frac{(q_{1})_{a}}{2\pi|\Lambda|}=-\frac{(q_{2})_{a}}{2\pi|\Lambda|},\\
&Q\equiv Q_{1}+iQ_{2},\ \bar{Q}\equiv Q_{1}-iQ_{2}.\end{split}

(59)

The two equations are complex conjugates of each other, as should happen in complex coordinates. (
58

\mathbb{C}\setminus\{w_{1},w_{2}\}

\delta
-function singularities at

(60)

where
\bar{\Omega},\bar{\phi}
are constants.

The divergences in
[\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}]_{\sigma}=|z|^{2}\delta_{\alpha\beta}
at

The second divergence comes through the WS area
\bar{A}

(61)

where

. In our conventions

. Upon integration over

(with any UV-regularization that respects translation and rotation symmetries, in particular point-splitting regularization), the middle term in (

\int\frac{d^{2}w}{2}\frac{|w_{21}|^{2}}{|w-w_{1}|^{2}|w-w_{2}|^{2}}=\int\frac{d^{2}w}{2}\frac{|w_{21}|^{2}}{|w|^{2}|w-w_{21}|^{2}}=4\pi\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\ln\left(\frac{|w_{21}|}{2\epsilon}\right)\rightarrow\infty,

(62)

(

, see (
2

4.3
The Large Momentum Limit

4.3.1
Two-Point Correlator

In the large momentum limit, one expects a saddle point approximation for the integral in (

1.

\frac{C}{|\sigma|^{2\Delta}}
- Does not depend on the momentum.

2.

5
Comparison to Previous Results

Our final result (

[
17
]

\Lambda

\Lambda_{c}
to distinguish it from our deformation parameter
\Lambda

here, and the deformation parameter

) is
t
here. Using (

First, we compare the renormalization of the operators in momentum space. It is given in (1.6) in
[
17
]

e^{\lambda_{c}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda_{c}}{\mu}\right)q^{2}}=\left(\frac{\Lambda_{c}}{\mu}\right)^{\lambda_{c}q^{2}}\rightarrow(\epsilon\mu)^{-\frac{q^{2}}{2\pi|\Lambda|}}

(125)

(where the arrow denotes translation to our notations). This is exactly the first factor in our renormalization in (

|Q|=\frac{|q|}{2\pi|\Lambda|}
. The second and third factors in (

\sqrt{g(\sigma_{i})}
in the operators’ definition in (
12

{\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}}
-formalism, there is no equivalent notion of the WS which appears in the JT-formalism. There, the (bare) operators in momentum space can be simply defined as the Fourier transform of the operators
O(\sigma)

{\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}}
-formalism is not clear (though they agree to leading order in the deformation). In appendix

we suggest an alternative definition of the operators in our formalism (with the same large-momentum behavior for their correlators), which in a specific gauge (the gauge
X^{a}=\sigma^{a}
) looks identical to the perturbative definition, but these operators still live on a space with a curved metric, so it is not clear how to compare them to the original formulation.

Next, we compare the large momentum limit of the renormalized correlation functions. It is given in (4.27) in
[
17

|q|^{2\Delta_{c}}\left(\frac{|q|}{\mu}\right)^{2\lambda_{c}q^{2}}\rightarrow|q|^{2\Delta-2}\left(\frac{|q|}{\mu}\right)^{\frac{q^{2}}{\pi|\Lambda|}}

1.

The most important is in the sign of the exponent, before the common factor of
\frac{q^{2}}{\pi|\Lambda|}

(\epsilon\mu)^{-\frac{q^{2}}{2\pi|\Lambda|}}

2.

The dimensionful quantity
q^{-2}
in Cardy’s formalism made the appearance of
|q|^{2\Delta}
become the undeformed correlator
C^{0}(q)
. In our result, the dimensions were taken care of by a factor of

3.

The basis of the exponent. While in (

is multiplied by the inverse of the renormalization scale
\mu

\frac{1}{2\pi e}\frac{\mu}{|\Lambda|}

4.

An extra factor of

1/\sin\left(\frac{q^{2}}{2|\Lambda|}+\pi\Delta\right)
(from the Fourier transform).

The main reason for these differences is that Cardy’s result was based on computing the leading logs in the correlation function at small momentum and resumming them. We find precisely the same leading logs at small momentum in our result, as shown for the first order expansion in

in appendix

\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}(q)\hat{\mathcal{O}}(-q+k)\rangle\propto\delta(k)\int d^{2}\sigma e^{iq\cdot\sigma}\frac{C}{|\sigma|^{2\Delta}}\left(\frac{\mu|\sigma|}{2}\right)^{-\frac{q^{2}}{\pi|\Lambda|}}.

\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)^{2}}\left(\frac{|q|}{2}\right)^{2\beta-2}\ ,\ \beta\equiv\frac{q^{2}}{2\pi|\Lambda|}+\Delta.

For the large

\frac{1}{Z_{{\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}}}}\int\mathcal{D}e\mathcal{D}\psi\int dA(\sigma_{1})dA(\sigma_{2})O(\sigma_{1})O(\sigma_{2})e^{iq_{1}\cdot\sigma_{1}+iq_{2}\cdot\sigma_{2}-S_{\textrm{T}\bar{\textrm{T}}}}.

(169)

\mathcal{O}(X_{0})=\sqrt{g(X_{0})}O(X_{0}).

(170)

\Lambda^{-1}

(171)

In appendix

\mathcal{O}
.

As

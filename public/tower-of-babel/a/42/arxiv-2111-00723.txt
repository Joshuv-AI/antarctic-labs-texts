Abstract
. For a graph H H , the H H -recolouring problem Recol ⁡ ( H ) \operatorname{Recol}(H) asks, for two given homomorphisms from a given graph G G to H H , if one can get between them by a sequence of homomorphisms of G G to H H in which consecutive homomorphisms differ on only one vertex. We show that, if G G and H H are reflexive and H H is triangle-free, then this problem can be solved in polynomial time. This shows, at the same time, that the closely related H H -reconfiguration problem Recon ⁡ ( H ) \operatorname{Recon}(H) of deciding whether two given homomorphisms from a given graph G G to H H are in the same component of the Hom-graph Hom ⁡ ( G , H ) \operatorname{{Hom}}(G,H) , can be solved in polynomial time for triangle-free reflexive graphs H H .

Introduction

Reconfiguration, in various settings, is the common notion of moving between solutions of a combinatorial problem via small changes; see van den Heuvel
[
19
]
and Nishimura
[
17
]
for detailed surveys. For example, a reconfiguration, or
recolouring

The
k
-recolouring problem
is to decide, for two given
k
-colourings of
G
, whether or not there is a

[

[
1

showed that

-recolouring is

The

[

Analogously, the

Recall that a

The

to

unless there exists an isolated vertex

If

Let

Let

is a YES instance. In one direction, it is clear that if

Now, suppose that

to
\psi
in

in which consecutive

is an edge of

and

We assume, throughout the rest of the paper, that

A more subtle obstruction comes from cycles in

3.2.
The fundamental group of a triangle-free graph

The above construction and definition is agrees with a more general construction in

Definition 6.1

1.
Introduction

Let
f
be a Hecke eigenform for the group
\Gamma_{0}(q)
of even weight
k
with trivial central character. We write its Fourier expansion as

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)\,n^{(k-1)/2}\,e(nz),\,\,z\in\mathbb{H}.

with
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
and
f
has been normalised so that the Deligne’s bound gives
|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq\tau(n)
for all
n\geq 1
, where
\tau(n)
is the divisor function.
Let
d
be a fundamental discriminant coprime to
q
, and let
\chi_{d}(\cdot)=\left(\frac{d}{\cdot}\right)
denote the primitive quadratic character of conductor
|d|
. Then
f\otimes\chi_{d}
is a newform on
\Gamma_{0}(q|d|^{2})
(though
f
need not be a newform) and the twisted
L
-function is given by

L(s,f\otimes\chi_{d})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\,\chi_{d}(n)}{n^{s}},\,\,\Re(s)>1.

It has an analytic continuation to the whole of
\mathbb{C}
and satisfies the following functional equation

\Lambda(s,f\otimes\chi_{d})=i^{k}\eta\chi_{d}(-q)\,\Lambda(1-s,f\otimes\chi_{d}),

where

\Lambda(s,f\otimes\chi_{d})=\left(\frac{|d|\sqrt{q}}{2\pi}\right)^{s}\Gamma\left(s+\frac{k-1}{2}\right)\,L(s,f\otimes\chi_{d}),

and
\eta
is the eigenvalue of the Fricke involution which is always
\pm 1
. We denote the root number by
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{d}):=i^{k}\eta\chi_{d}(-q)
. Note that if
d
is a fundamental discriminant, then
\chi_{d}(-1)=1
if
d
is positive and
\chi_{d}(-1)=-1
if
d
is negative. The derivative of the
L
-function
L(s,f\times\chi_{d})
is given by

L^{\prime}(s,f\otimes\chi_{d})=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\chi_{d}(n)\log n}{n^{s}},\,\,\Re(s)>1,

which also has a
functional equation

\Lambda^{\prime}(s,f\otimes\chi_{d})=-\omega(f\otimes\chi_{d})\,\Lambda^{\prime}(1-s,f\otimes\chi_{d}).

In this paper, we are interested in the asymptotic behaviour of
L^{\prime}(s,f\otimes\chi_{d})
at the central point
1/2
over the family of quadratic twists. The mean value of this family has been explored previously by many authors, particularly by Bump-Friedberg-Hoffstein
[
1
]
, Murty-Murty
[
6
]
, Iwaniec
[
2
]
, Munshi
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
and Petrow
[
8
]
. Petrow, assuming GRH, proved

(1)

\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2q)=1\\
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=-1\end{subarray}}|L^{\prime}\left(1/2,f\times\chi_{8d}\right)|^{2}J\left(\frac{8d}{X}\right)\sim C_{f}^{\prime}X\log^{3}X,

where
C_{f}
is a fixed explicit contant depending on
f
only and
J:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a compactly supported smooth function having support in
[1/2,2]
. This result was motivated by an important work by Soundarajan-Young
[
9
]
, who proved an analog of (
1
) without the derivative, i.e.,

(2)

\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2q)=1\end{subarray}}|L\left(1/2,f\times\chi_{8d}\right)|^{2}J\left(\frac{8d}{X}\right)\sim C_{f}X\log X,

assuming GRH. The above result was proved unconditionally recently in a breakthrough work by Li
[
3
]
. The main aim of this article is prove (
1
) unconditionally.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f
and
J
be as above. Then we have

\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2q)=1\\
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=-1\end{subarray}}|L^{\prime}\left(1/2,f\times\chi_{8d}\right)|^{2}J\left(\frac{8d}{X}\right)\,=\,C_{f}\,\widetilde{J}(1)\,X\log^{3}X+O\left(X\,(\log X)^{\frac{5}{2}+\epsilon}\right),

where
C_{f}
is some explicit constant depending only on
f
and
\widetilde{J}
is the Mellin transform of
J
.

We combine the methods of Li
[
3
]
and Petrow
[
8
]
to prove the above theorem. A similar result holds when
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=1
.

Corollary 1
.

We have

\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2q)=1\end{subarray}}|L^{\prime}\left(1/2,f\times\chi_{8d}\right)|^{2}J\left(\frac{8d}{X}\right)\,=\,\mathcal{C}_{f}\,\widetilde{J}(1)\,X\log^{3}X+O\left(X\,(\log X)^{\frac{5}{2}+\epsilon}\right),

where
\mathcal{C}_{f}
is some constant depending on
f
only.

Proof.

When
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=1
, it follows from the functional equation

\Lambda^{\prime}(s,f\otimes\chi_{8d})=-\,\Lambda^{\prime}(1-s,f\otimes\chi_{8d}),

that
\Lambda^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})=0
. Thus

L^{\prime}({1}/{2},f\otimes\chi_{8d})=-L({1}/{2},f\otimes\chi_{8d})\Big\{\log\left(\frac{8|d|\sqrt{q}}{2\pi}\right)\,+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}\left({k}/{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left({k}/{2}\right)}\Big\}.

On applying the asymptoic expression

\sum_{(d,2q)=1}\left(L\left({1}/{2},f\otimes\chi_{8d}\right)\right)^{2}\,J\left(\frac{8d}{X}\right)=C^{\prime}_{f}\,\widetilde{J}(1)\,X\log X+O\left(X(\log X)^{1/2+\epsilon}\right)

from Li
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2q)=1\\
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=1\end{subarray}}|L^{\prime}\left(1/2,f\times\chi_{8d}\right)|^{2}J\left(\frac{8d}{X}\right)\,

\displaystyle=(\log X)^{2}\sum_{(d,2q)=1}\left(L\left({1}/{2},f\otimes\chi_{8d}\right)\right)^{2}\,J\left(\frac{8d}{X}\right)+O\left(X\log X\right)

\displaystyle=C_{f}^{\prime}\,\widetilde{J}(1)\,X(\log X)^{3}+O\left(X(\log X)^{\frac{5}{2}+\epsilon}\right).

Thus the corollary follows by Theorem
1
and the above expression.
∎

A similar method works for all discriminants
d
as well. As an application, we improve upon the lower bound for

\displaystyle N(X)=\#\{0<d\leq X:d\ \mathrm{is\ a\ fundamental\ discriminant\ with}\ (d,q)=1

\displaystyle\text{and}\ L^{\prime}(1/2,E\otimes\chi_{d}\neq 0)\}.

Here
E
be a modular elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with the
L
-function
L(s,E)=L(s,f)
for some eigenform
f
of weight
2
and level
q
, where
q
is the conductor of
E
. Iwaniec
[
2
]
proved that
N(X)\gg_{\epsilon}X^{2/3-\epsilon}
for any
\epsilon>0
. This bound was later improved to

(3)

\displaystyle N(X)\gg_{\epsilon}X^{1-\epsilon},

by Perelli and Pomykala
[
7
]
. We improve upon (
3
) in the following corollary.

Corollary 2
.

We have

N(X)\gg X/\log X.

Proof.

Main ingredients to prove Corollary
2
are non-trivial lower bound for the first moment of
L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})
and an upper bound for the second moment of
L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})
over
d
(see
[
7
]
). Indeed, by the work of Murty-Murty
[
6
]
and Iwaniec
[
2
]
, we have

(4)

\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{(d,2q)=1}L^{\prime}({1}/{2},f\otimes\chi_{d})\sim CX\,\log X,

for some constant
C\neq 0
, and by Corollary
1
we have

(5)

M^{\prime}(2):=\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{(d,2q)=1}|L^{\prime}({1}/{2},f\otimes\chi_{d})|^{2}\ll_{f}X\log^{3}X.

Thus on applying Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to the left-hand side of (
4
), and then using (
5
), we have the corollary.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1

We are interested in getting an asymptotic formula for the smoothed sum

M^{\prime}_{\text{s}}(2)=\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2q)=1\\
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=-1\end{subarray}}|L^{\prime}\left(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d}\right)|^{2}\,J\left(\frac{8d}{X}\right),

where
J
is a non-negative smooth function suppored in
[1/2,2]
. Following Lemma 3.1 of Petrow
[
8
]
, we express
L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})
as a Dirichlet series using the approximate functional equation

L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})\,=\left(1-i^{k}\eta\chi_{8d}(-q)\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\chi_{d}(n)}{n^{1/2}}\,W\left(\frac{n}{|8d|}\right),

which holds if
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=-1
. Here the cut-off function
W
is given by

W(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(3)}\frac{\Gamma\left(u+{k}/{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left({k}/{2}\right)}\left(\frac{2\pi y}{\sqrt{q}}\right)^{-u}\,\frac{du}{u^{2}}.

Next we split the Dirichlet series into the main part and the tail part. To this end, we set

\mathcal{A}(8d):=\mathcal{A}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})=(1-i^{k}\eta\chi_{8d}(q))\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\chi_{8d}(n)}{n^{1/2}}\,W\left(\frac{n}{\mathcal{M}}\right),

where
\mathcal{M}=X/(\log X)^{1000}
, and

\mathcal{B}(8d):=\mathcal{B}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})=L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})-\mathcal{A}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d}).

Theorem
1
follows from the following propositions.

Proposition 2
.

We have

\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{(d,2q)=1}\Big|\mathcal{B}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})\Big|^{2}\,J\left(\frac{8d}{X}\right)\ll_{f,\epsilon}X(\log X)^{2}(\log\log X)^{4}.

Proposition 3
.

We have

\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{(d,2q)=1}\Big|\mathcal{A}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})\Big|^{2}\,J\left(\frac{8d}{X}\right)=C_{f}\,\check{J}(0)\,X\,(\log X)^{3}+O\left(X\,(\log X)^{2}\right),

where
C_{f}
is a constant depending on
f
.

Indeed, on applying the identity
(a+b)^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}+2ab
to
M^{\prime}_{\text{s}}(2)
and then Cauchy-Schwartz inequality followed by the above propositions, we get Theorem
1
.
In the rest of the paper, we prove Proposition
2
(in Section
4
) and Proposition
3
(in Section
5
).

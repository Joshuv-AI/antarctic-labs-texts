Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a nonlocal model for linear steady Stokes system with physical no-slip boundary condition. We use the idea of volume constraint to enforce the no-slip boundary condition and prove that the nonlocal model is well-posed. We also show that and the solution of the nonlocal system converges to the solution of the original Stokes system as the nonlocality vanishes.

More precisely, for

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Delta{\bm{u}}(\bm{x})-\nabla p(\bm{x})&=&{\bm{f}}(\bm{x}),\quad\bm{x}\in\Omega\\

For the pressure, we impose average zero condition

The no-slip boundary condition is a Dirichlet type boundary condition and it is often used in many
real world applications. However, the theoretical study with no-slip boundary condition is also much more difficult.
The first question is how to enforce no-slip boundary condition in the nonlocal approach.
Recently, Du et.al.

proposed volume constraint to deal with the boundary condition in the nonlocal diffusion problem by enforcing the condition over
a nonlocal region adjacent to the boundary.
Adopting this idea, in the nonlocal Stokes system, we extend the no-slip condition to a small layer as shown in Fig.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})({{\bm{u}}(\bm{y})-{\bm{u}}(\bm{x})})\mathrm{d}\bm{y},

Analysis to the linear steady Stokes equation in this paper could give some new understanding to the theoretical foundation of these methods.

The Stokes system (
1
) is well-known to be a saddle point problem. This remains the case for the nonlocal Stokes system given in
[
13
]
subject to periodic boundary conditions.
Here, different from
[
13
]
, we add a relaxation term,
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\delta}p_{\delta}(\bm{x})
, in the second equation of (
1

It mimics the classical technique of stabilizing the approximation of incompressibility by adding a positive definite block to the original saddle point system. Although this results in a slightly compressible system,
the stabilization term vanishes as
\delta\to 0

so that it does not destroy the approximation of the nonlocal formulation to the local limit. Yet, this additional term is crucial
for the stability and well-posedness in our case where smooth nonlocal kernels are used to define the nonlocal operators.
Indeed, the well-posed study in
[
13
]
showed that, without extra relaxation, it is necessary to use singular kernels. A remedy was provided in
[
24
]
by incorporating non-radial nonlocal interactions. The addition of the relaxation term enables the use of smooth kernels in the definition of the associated nonlocal operators which may allow more flexible practical implementation such as more conventional quadratures for smooth functions.
For the Fourier analysis of a related formulation with periodic boundary conditions, we refer to
[
42
]
.

nonlocal model is established in Section 3. The vanishing nonlocality limit is analyzed in Section 4. In Section 5, we conclude with a summary and a discussion on future research.

2
Nonlocal Stokes system with related assumptions and estimates

Then, the rescaled kernels used in the definitions of the nonlocal operators are defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=C_{\delta}R\left(\frac{\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|^{2}}{4\delta^{2}}\right),\quad\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=C_{\delta}\bar{R}\left(\frac{\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|^{2}}{4\delta^{2}}\right){,}

2.2
Nonlocal Stokes system with volume constraint

By combining the volume constraint boundary condition of
{\bm{u}}
and the average zero condition of
p

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rclc}\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\delta}{\bm{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\delta}p_{\delta}(\bm{x})&=&{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y}){\bm{f}}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}},&\bm{x}\in\Omega_{\delta},\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\delta}{\bm{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\delta}p_{\delta}(\bm{x})&=&0,&\bm{x}\in\Omega,\\[2.84544pt]
{\bm{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{x})&=&0,&\bm{x}\in\mathcal{V}_{\delta},\\
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}(\bm{x})\mathrm{d}\bm{x}&=&0.\end{array}\right.

The integral operators have been defined in (
1.8

Formally, the choices of normalization specified in this paper further imply that the local limits of

\Delta

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{x}-\bm{y})\cdot\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}+\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})p_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}

where
\bar{w}_{\delta}(\bm{x})=\int_{\Omega}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}
and
\bar{\bm{v}}=\bm{v}-{\bm{u}}_{\delta}
.

Then, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{x}-\bm{y})p_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{x}-\bm{y})\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}

(3.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p^{2}_{\delta}(\bm{x})\bar{w}_{\delta}(\bm{x})\mathrm{d}\bm{x}-\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})p_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}.

The first term is positive, thus a good term. The second term becomes

(3.15)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})p_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(p_{\delta}(\bm{x})-p_{\delta}(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}-\int_{\Omega}p^{2}_{\delta}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}.

The second term of (
3.15
) can be controlled by the first term of (
3
). And the first term is bounded by

(3.16)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(p_{\delta}(\bm{x})-p_{\delta}(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(p_{\delta}(\bm{x})-p_{\delta}(\bm{y}))^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}+\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}p_{\delta}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(p_{\delta}(\bm{x})-p_{\delta}(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(p_{\delta}(\bm{x})-p_{\delta}(\bm{y}))^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}+\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(p_{\delta}(\bm{x})-p_{\delta}(\bm{y}))^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle-\left|\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(p_{\delta}(\bm{x})-p_{\delta}(\bm{y}))p_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(p_{\delta}(\bm{x})-p_{\delta}(\bm{y}))^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}p^{2}_{\delta}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}.

Combining (
3
)-(
3.16
), we get

(3.17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{x}-\bm{y})p_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}p^{2}_{\delta}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}

Now, we are ready to get the estimate of
p_{\delta}
.
Multiplying
\bar{\bm{v}}
on both sides of the first equation of (
2.2
) and integrating over
\Omega_{\delta}
, using the fact that

\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{x})=0,\;\;\bm{x}\in\mathcal{V}_{\delta}
, we have

(3.18)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})({\bm{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-{\bm{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{y}))\cdot(\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{x})-\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{x}-\bm{y})p_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y}){\bm{f}}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}.

Using (
3.3
), (
3.9
), (
3.12
), (
3.18
) and (
3.17
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|{\bm{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-{\bm{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{y})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{x})-\bar{\bm{v}}(\bm{y})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+\|\bar{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})}\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|{\bm{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-{\bm{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{y})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)^{1/2}\left(\left(\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|\bm{v}(\bm{x})-\bm{v}(\bm{y})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)^{1/2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|\bm{u}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-\bm{u}_{\delta}(\bm{y})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)^{1/2}\right)+(\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\|\bm{u}_{\delta}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})})\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}_{\delta}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})}\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{u}_{\delta}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{1/2}\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})}+C(\|p_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\|{\bm{f}}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)})\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|p_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\|{\bm{f}}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)})\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}.

(3.19)

Using (
3.3
), (
3.9
), (
3.19
) and Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\|p_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C\|{\bm{u}}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|{\bm{f}}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+C\|p_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|p_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|{\bm{f}}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)})\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

which implies

(3.20)

\displaystyle\|p_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}.

This also gives the
H^{1}
estimate of
{\bm{u}}_{\delta}
using (

),

(3.21)

\displaystyle\|{\bm{u}}_{\delta}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})}\leq C\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}.

and using (

),

(3.22)

\displaystyle\|\bar{p}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|p_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\left|\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}(\bm{x})\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\right|\leq C\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}.

Note that in the above, the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\left|\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}(\bm{x})\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Omega|}}\|p_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

is used.

4
Vanishing nonlocality

Besides the well-posedness, we are also interested in the limiting behavior of the nonlocal Stokes system (
2.2
) as the nonlocality vanishes,
i.e.
\delta\rightarrow 0
.
In this section, under some assumptions, we prove that solutions of the nonlocal Stokes system converge to the solution of the
Stokes system as
\delta\rightarrow 0
. Furthermore, we give an estimate on the convergence rate. The result is summarized in Theorem
8
.

Before stating the main theorem, we give several technical results that are used to prove the main theorem.

We also need the following theorem on the order of the nonlocal approximation
which can be proved via simple Taylor expansion.

Theorem 7
.

Let

\displaystyle r(\bm{x})=-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(u(\bm{x})-u(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{y}-\int_{{\Omega}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\Delta u(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y},\quad\forall\bm{x}\in\Omega_{\delta}.

There exist constants

C,T_{0}
depending only on
{\Omega}
, so that for any
\delta\leq T_{0}
,
for
u\in H^{3}({\Omega})
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left\|r(\bm{x})\right\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C{\delta\|u\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}},

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla r(\bm{x})\right\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}.

We then have the main result of this section regarding the convergence of the nonlocal Stokes system as the nonlocality vanishes.

Theorem 8
.

Let
{\bm{u}}(\bm{x})
,
p(\bm{x})
be solution of Stokes system (
1
) and
{\bm{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{x}),\;p_{\delta}(\bm{x})
be solution of nonlocal Stokes system (
2.2
)
with
{\bm{f}}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
There exists a constant
C>0
that only depends on
{\Omega}
and
R
, such that

\displaystyle\|{\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{\delta}\|_{H^{1}({\Omega}_{\delta})}+\|p-p_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\sqrt{\delta}\|f\|_{H^{1}({\Omega})}

Proof.

Let
\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})={\bm{u}}(\bm{x})-{\bm{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{x})
and
d_{\delta}=p-p_{\delta}-\frac{1}{|\Omega_{\delta}|}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}(p(\bm{x})-p_{\delta}(\bm{x}))\mathrm{d}\bm{x}
, then
\bm{e}_{\delta}
and
d_{\delta}
satisfy

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rclc}\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{y}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{x}-\bm{y})d_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}&=&\displaystyle\bm{r}_{\bm{u}}(\bm{x}),&\bm{x}\in\Omega_{\delta},\\
\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})&=&{\bm{u}}(\bm{x}),&\bm{x}\in\mathcal{V}_{\delta},\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{x}-\bm{y})\cdot\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}-\int_{\Omega}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(d_{\delta}(\bm{x})-d_{\delta}(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{y}&=&\displaystyle r_{p}(\bm{x}),&\bm{x}\in\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}d_{\delta}(\bm{x})\mathrm{d}\bm{x}&=&0,&\end{array}\right.

where

(4.7)

\displaystyle\bm{r}_{\bm{u}}(\bm{x})=

\displaystyle\int_{{\Omega}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\Delta{\bm{u}}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}+\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})({\bm{u}}(\bm{x})-{\bm{u}}(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{y},

\displaystyle\quad\forall\bm{x}\in\Omega_{\delta}

(4.8)

\displaystyle r_{p}(\bm{x})=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(p(\bm{x})-p(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{y},

\displaystyle\quad\forall\bm{x}\in\Omega.

First, we focus on the following estimate

(4.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})\cdot\int_{{\Omega}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})\cdot\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}+\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})\cdot\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}+\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})\cdot\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}.

The second term of the right hand side of (
4.9
) can be calculated as

(4.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})\cdot\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}|\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})|^{2}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})\cdot\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y}){\bm{u}}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}.

Here we use the definition of
\bm{e}_{\delta}
and the volume constraint condition
{\bm{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{x})=0,\;\bm{x}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}
to get that
\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})=u(\bm{x}),\;\bm{x}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}
.

The first term is positive which is good for us. We only need to bound the second term of (
4.10
).
First, the second term can be bounded as following

(4.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\left|\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})\cdot\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y}){\bm{u}}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}|\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})|\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|{\bm{u}}(\bm{y})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)^{1/2}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\left(\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}\frac{1}{2}|\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})|^{2}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}+2\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|{\bm{u}}(\bm{y})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}|\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})|^{2}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}+\frac{2}{\delta^{2}}\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}|{\bm{u}}(\bm{y})|^{2}\left(\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{y}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}|\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})|^{2}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}+\frac{C}{\delta^{2}}\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}|{\bm{u}}(\bm{y})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}|\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})|^{2}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}+C\delta\|{\bm{f}}\|^{2}_{H^{1}({\Omega})}.

Here we use Lemma
9
in Appendix
B
to get the last inequality.

By substituting (
4.11
), (
4.10
) in (
4.9
), we get

(4.12)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})\cdot\int_{{\Omega}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y}))\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{{\Omega}_{\delta}}|\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})|^{2}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}-C\|{\bm{f}}\|_{H^{1}({\Omega})}^{2}\delta.

This is the key estimate to show the convergence.

We also need the following bound

(4.13)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})\cdot\left(\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{x}-\bm{y})d_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}+\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}d_{\delta}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{x}-\bm{y})\cdot\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}d_{\delta}(\bm{x})\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{x}-\bm{y})\cdot\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|d_{\delta}(\bm{x})||{\bm{u}}(\bm{y})|\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta}\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|d_{\delta}(\bm{x})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|{\bm{u}}(\bm{y})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sqrt{\delta}\|{\bm{f}}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|d_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Multiplying
\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})
,
d_{\delta}(\bm{x})
on both sides of the first and third equations in (
4
) and integrating over
\Omega_{\delta}
,
\Omega

respectively and adding them together, using (
4.12
), (
4.13
), we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})-\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}+\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}|\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{x})|^{2}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{V}}_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})|d_{\delta}(\bm{x})-d_{\delta}(\bm{y})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\|\bm{r}_{\bm{u}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})})\|\bm{e}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\|r_{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|d_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\sqrt{\delta}\|{\bm{f}}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|d_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\delta\|{\bm{f}}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}.

To simplify the notation, we denote the right hand side of (

) as
Q^{2}
.

It is well known (e.g. Section 3.3 of
[
17
]
) that with the condition that

there exists at least one function
\bm{\psi}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})
, such that

(4.15)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{\psi}(\bm{x})=d_{\delta}(\bm{x}),\quad\bm{x}\in\Omega_{\delta},\quad\text{and}\quad\|\bm{\psi}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})}\leq c\|d_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

and

is a constant independent on

, the proof can be found in Appendix

.

Then, we extend
\bm{\psi}
to
\Omega
by assigning the value on

to be 0 and denote the new function also by

. Obviously, we have

(4.16)

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\quad\|\bm{\psi}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\|d_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

Using the third equation of (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\bar{w}_{\delta}(\bm{x})d_{\delta}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})d_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}+\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{x}-\bm{y})\cdot\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}-r_{p}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\bar{R}_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\nabla\cdot\bm{\psi}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}+\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}R_{\delta}(\bm{x},\bm{y})(\bm{x}-\bm{y})\cdot\bm{e}_{\delta}(\bm{y})\mathrm{d}\bm{y}

5
Discussion and Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a nonlocal model for linear steady Stokes equation with no-slip boundary condition. The main idea is to use volume constraint to enforce
the no-slip boundary condition and add a relaxation term in the divergence free condition to maintain the well-posedness of the nonlocal system. As the nonlocal
horizon paramter
\delta
approaches 0, the solution of the nonlocal system converges to
the solution of the original Stoke equation, assuming that the solution to the latter is sufficiently smooth.

In terms of future work, one may examine the convergence with minimal regularity assumptions on the local systems. It is also interesting to consider the numerical discretizations.
From the nonlocal system, we can derive a numerical scheme for the original Stokes system on point cloud.

a set of sample points
P
sampling the domain
{\Omega}
and a subset

sampling the boundary of
{\Omega}
.
In addition, assume we are given
one vector
\mathbf{V}=(V_{1},\cdots,V_{n})^{t}
where
V_{i}
is an volume weight of
\bm{x}_{i}
in

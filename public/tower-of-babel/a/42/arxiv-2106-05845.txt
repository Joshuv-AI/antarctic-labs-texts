2.1
The Dunkl Operator and the Dunkl Transform

For sake of simplicity, we set
|E|_{\lambda}=c_{\lambda}\int_{E}|x|^{2\lambda}dx
for a measurable set

, and
\langle f,g\rangle_{\lambda}=c_{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)g(x)|x|^{2\lambda}dx
whenever the integral exists. The Dunkl operator on the real line is given by

Df(x)=f^{\prime}(x)+\frac{\lambda}{x}[f(x)-f(-x)].

A direct computation shows that

(D^{2}f)(x)=f^{\prime\prime}(x)+\frac{2\lambda}{x}f^{\prime}(x)-\frac{\lambda}{x^{2}}[f(x)-f(-x)].

For
f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, an inverse operator of
D
is given by

\displaystyle f(x)=f(0)+\frac{x}{2}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}({\rm sgn}\,s)(Df)(sx)ds+\int_{-1}^{1}(Df)(sx)|s|^{2\lambda}ds\right).

(9)

For

D
can be given by

\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}[\tau_{x}(Df)](-t)({\rm sgn}\,t)dt,

(10)

is invariant in
{\mathscr{D}}({\mathbb{R}})
and
{\mathbb{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
.
If

,

\displaystyle\langle Df,\phi\rangle_{\lambda}=-\langle f,D\phi\rangle_{\lambda}.

Theorem 3.4

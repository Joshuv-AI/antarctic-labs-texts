Abstract
. Let ℳ X \mathcal{M}_{X} denote the ideal of meager subsets of a topological space X X . We prove that if X X is a completely metrizable space without isolated points, then the smallest cardinality of a non-meager subset of X X , denoted non ⁡ ( ℳ X ) \mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X}) , is exactly non ⁡ ( ℳ X ) = cf ​ [ κ ] ω ⋅ non ⁡ ( ℳ ℝ ) \mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})=\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}\cdot\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{R}}) , where κ \kappa is the minimum weight of a nonempty open subset of X X . We also characterize the additivity and covering numbers for ℳ X \mathcal{M}_{X} in terms of simple topological properties of X X . Some bounds are proved and some questions raised concerning the cofinality of ℳ X \mathcal{M}_{X} and the cofinality of the related ideal of nowhere dense subsets of X X . We also show that if X X is a compact Hausdorff space with π \pi -weight κ \kappa , then non ⁡ ( ℳ X ) ≤ cf ​ [ κ ] ω ⋅ non ⁡ ( ℳ ℝ ) \mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\leq\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}\cdot\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{R}}) . This bound for compact Hausdorff spaces is not sharp, in the sense that it is consistent for such a space to have non-meager subsets of even smaller cardinality.

2.
Additivity and covering for a completely metrizable space

Recall that the
weight
of a space
X
, denoted
w(X)
, is the minimum cardinality of a basis for
X
. Let

w_{\downarrow}(X)\,=\,\min\left\{\kappa\colon\text{some nonempty open $U\subseteq X$ has weight $\kappa$}\right\}.

We say that
X
has
uniform weight
\kappa
if
w(X)=w_{\downarrow}(X)=\kappa
, or, equivalently, if every nonempty open subset of
X
has weight
\kappa
.

A
cellular family
in a space
X
is a collection of nonempty pairwise disjoint open subsets of
X
. The
cellularity
of
X
, denoted
c(X)
, is

c(X)\,=\,\sup\left\{|\mathcal{S}|\colon\mathcal{S}\text{ is a cellular family in }X\right\}.

By a theorem of Erdős and Tarski in
[
8
]
, if
X
is any space and
c(X)
is not a regular limit cardinal,
then there is a cellular family in
X
of cardinality
c(X)
: in other words, the supremum in the definition of
c(X)
is attained.
Furthermore, if
X
is metrizable then the supremum in the definition of
c(X)
is attained regardless of
whether
c(X)
is a regular limit cardinal or not.

In what follows, if a cardinal number
\kappa
is referred to as a topological space, assume that
\kappa
carries the discrete topology (not its usual order topology).
So, for example,
\kappa
is the discrete space of size
\kappa
, and
\kappa^{\omega}
is a completely metrizable space with uniform weight
\kappa
. The latter is sometimes called the
Baire space of weight
\kappa
(e.g. in
[
7
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
X
is a completely metrizable space with uniform weight
\kappa
. Then
X
has a co-meager subset homeomorphic to
\kappa^{\omega}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\kappa=w(X)=w_{\downarrow}(X)
.
By the Bing Metrization theorem,
X
has a
\sigma
-discrete basis.
That is, there is a sequence
\mathcal{B}_{0},\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2},\dots
of countable collections of open subsets of
X
such that
\mathcal{B}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\mathcal{B}_{n}
is a basis for
X
and each
\mathcal{B}_{n}
is discrete, which means that every point of
X
has a neighborhood meeting at most one member of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
.
Let

Y\,=\,X\setminus\textstyle\bigcup\left\{\overline{U}\setminus U\colon U\in\mathcal{B}\right\}.

In other words,
Y
is obtained from
X
by removing the boundary of every member of
\mathcal{B}
.
We claim that
Y
is co-meager in
X
and homeomorphic to
\kappa^{\omega}
.

To see that
Y
is co-meager in
X
, note that
\bigcup\left\{\overline{U}\setminus U\colon U\in\mathcal{B}_{n}\right\}
is closed and nowhere dense in
X
for each
n\in\omega
. (This follows easily from the fact that
\mathcal{B}_{n}
is discrete.) Hence
\bigcup\left\{\overline{U}\setminus U\colon U\in\mathcal{B}\right\}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\bigcup\left\{\overline{U}\setminus U\colon U\in\mathcal{B}_{n}\right\}
is a meager
F_{\sigma}
set, and
Y
is a co-meager
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
.

To see that
Y
is homeomorphic to
\kappa^{\omega}
, we appeal to a theorem of Stone from
[
21
]
, which states:

A completely metrizable space is homeomorphic to
\kappa^{\omega}
if and only if it is strongly
0
-dimensional and has uniform weight
\kappa
.

(This generalizes to higher weights the famous characterization of the Baire space
\omega^{\omega}
by Alexandrov and Urysohn; see
[
15
, Exercise 7.2.G]
.)
The fact that
Y
is completely metrizable follows from the fact that it is a
G_{\delta}
subset of the completely metrizable space
X
. (Recall that, by a theorem of Alexandrov, a subspace of a completely metrizable space is completely metrizable if and only if it is
G_{\delta}
.)
The fact that
Y
is strongly
0
-dimensional follows from
[
7
, Theorem 7.3.8]
.
The fact that
Y
has uniform weight
\kappa
follows from the fact that
X
does.
Specifically, for every nonempty open
U\subseteq Y
there is a nonempty open
W\subseteq X
with
U=W\cap Y
. But
U
is dense in
W
(because
Y
is co-meager), and in any
T_{3}
space, the weight of a dense subspace is equal to the weight of the original space. Thus
w(U)=w(W)=\kappa
.
∎

Recall that a
\pi
-base
for
X
is a collection
\mathcal{B}
of nonempty open sets such that every nonempty open subset of
X
contains a member of
\mathcal{B}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
X
is a metric space and
\mathcal{B}
is a
\pi
-base for
X
, then there is a maximal cellular family
\mathcal{S}
in
X
such that
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
and
|\mathcal{S}|=w(X)
.

Proof.

By the aforementioned result of Erdős and Tarski, there is a cellular family
\mathcal{S}_{0}
in
X
with
|\mathcal{S}_{0}|=c(X)
. But
w(X)=c(X)
for metrizable spaces (see
[
7
, Theorem 4.1.15]
), so
|\mathcal{S}_{0}|=w(X)
.
For every
U\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
, a straightforward application of Zorn’s Lemma gives a maximal subset
\mathcal{T}_{V}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
such that
\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{V}\subseteq V
and the members of
\mathcal{T}_{V}
are pairwise disjoint.
Because
\mathcal{B}
is a
\pi
-base, each
\mathcal{T}_{V}
is a maximal cellular family in
V
. But then
\mathcal{S}=\bigcup_{V\in\mathcal{S}_{0}}\mathcal{T}_{V}
is a maximal cellular family in
X
. Furthermore,
c(X)=w(X)=|\mathcal{S}_{0}|\leq|\mathcal{S}|\leq c(X)
, so
|\mathcal{S}|=w(X)
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a completely metrizable space with uniform weight
\kappa\geq\aleph_{1}
. Then
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})=\aleph_{1}
.

Proof.

First, we claim that
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})=\aleph_{1}
for uncountable
\kappa
. To see this, for each
\alpha<\kappa
define

F_{\alpha}\,=\,\left\{x\in\kappa^{\omega}\colon\alpha\notin\mathrm{range}(x)\right\}.

Here, as usual, we are thinking of
x
as a function
\omega\to\kappa
.
Recall that
\kappa^{\omega}
has a basis of sets of the form

[\![s]\!]\,=\,\left\{x\in\kappa^{\omega}\colon x\!\restriction\!(\mathrm{length}(s)=s)\right\}

where
s\in\kappa^{<\omega}
.
Each
F_{\alpha}
is closed in
\kappa^{\omega}
, because

\kappa^{\omega}\setminus F_{\alpha}\,=\,\left\{x\in\kappa^{\omega}\colon\alpha\in\mathrm{range}(x)\right\}\,=\,\textstyle\bigcup\left\{[\![s]\!]\colon\alpha\in\mathrm{range}(s)\right\}.

Furthermore, if
[\![s]\!]
is any element of our basis for
\kappa^{\omega}
, then
[\![s\!\,^{\frown}\alpha]\!]
is another set in the basis with
[\![s\!\,^{\frown}\alpha]\!]\subseteq[\![s]\!]
and
[\![s\!\,^{\frown}\alpha]\!]\cap F_{\alpha}=\emptyset
. Thus each
F_{\alpha}
is a nowhere dense closed subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
.
Now observe that

\textstyle\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}F_{\alpha}\,=\,\left\{x\in\kappa^{\omega}\colon\mathrm{range}(x)\not\supseteq\omega_{1}\right\}\,=\,X,

where the second equality holds because
\mathrm{range}(x)
is countable for every
x\in\kappa^{\omega}
.
Thus we have an
\aleph_{1}
-sized collection of nowhere dense subsets of
\kappa^{\omega}
whose union is all of
\kappa^{\omega}
.

By Lemma
2.1
, there is a meager
F\subseteq X
such that
X\setminus F\approx\kappa^{\omega}
. By the previous paragraph,
X\setminus F
can be covered by
\aleph_{1}
sets meager in
X\setminus F
, and therefore meager in
X
. Adding
F
to this collection, we obtain a collection of
\aleph_{1}
meager sets covering
X
.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

If
X
is a completely metrizable space with no isolated points,

\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\,=\,\begin{cases}\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})&\text{ if $w_{\downarrow}(X)=\aleph_{0}$,}\\
\aleph_{1}&\text{ if $w_{\downarrow}(X)>\aleph_{0}$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For the first case, suppose
w_{\downarrow}(X)=\aleph_{0}
. In other words, there is a nonempty open
U\subseteq X
with countable weight. Because open subsets of completely metrizable spaces are completely metrizable,
U
is Polish. Hence
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})
meager-in-
U
sets are required to cover
U
; equivalently,
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})
nowhere-dense-in-
U
sets are required to cover
U
. But because
U
is open, if
F\subseteq X
is nowhere dense in
X
then
U\cap F
is nowhere dense in
U
. Thus
\geq\!\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})
nowhere dense subsets of
X
are required to cover
U
, and it follows that
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\geq\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.

For the opposite inequality, first observe that

\mathcal{B}\,=\,\left\{U\subseteq X\colon U\text{ is open, }U\neq\emptyset,\text{ and }w_{\downarrow}(U)=w(U)\right\}

is a
\pi
-base for
X
. By Lemma
2.2
, there is a maximal cellular family
\mathcal{S}
in
X
such that
w_{\downarrow}(U)=w(U)
for every
U\in\mathcal{S}
. For each
U\in\mathcal{S}
, either
(1)

U
is Polish, and there is a sequence
\left\langle F^{U}_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\right\rangle
of nowhere dense subsets of
U
covering
U
, or
(2)

U
is not Polish,
w(U)=w_{\downarrow}(U)>\aleph_{0}
, and by Lemma
2.3
there is a sequence
\left\langle F^{U}_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\omega_{1}\right\rangle
of nowhere dense subsets of
U
covering
U
.

For each
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, let
F_{a}=\bigcup\left\{F^{U}_{\alpha}\colon U\in\mathcal{S}\right\}
, and if
\omega_{1}\leq\alpha<\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})
, let
F_{\alpha}=\bigcup\left\{F^{\alpha}_{U}\colon U\in\mathcal{S}\text{ and }w(U)=\aleph_{0}\right\}
. Using the fact that
\mathcal{S}
is a cellular family, it is not difficult to see that each
F_{\alpha}
is nowhere dense in
X
.

Because
\mathcal{S}
is a maximal cellular family,
\bigcup\mathcal{S}
is an open dense subset of
X
; hence
F=X\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{S}
is nowhere dense.

Now
\{F\}\cup\left\{F_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})\right\}
is a collection of
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})
nowhere dense subsets of
X
, and this collection covers
X
. Hence
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\leq\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.

For the second case, suppose
w_{\downarrow}(X)>\aleph_{0}
.
Because
X
is completely metrizable, and therefore satisfies the conclusion of the Baire Category Theorem,
\mathcal{M}_{X}
is a
\sigma
-ideal and
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\geq\aleph_{1}
.

For the reverse inequality, note that, just as in the first case, there is a maximal cellular family
\mathcal{S}
in
X
such that
w_{\downarrow}(U)=w(U)>\aleph_{0}
for every
U\in\mathcal{S}
.
By Lemma
2.3
, for each
U\in\mathcal{S}
there is a sequence
\left\langle F^{U}_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\omega_{1}\right\rangle
of nowhere dense subsets of
U
covering
U
.
As above, for each
\alpha<\omega_{1}
let
F_{a}=\bigcup\left\{F^{U}_{\alpha}\colon U\in\mathcal{S}\right\}
, and let
F=X\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{S}
.
Then
\{F\}\cup\left\{F_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<w_{1}\right\}
is a collection of
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})
nowhere dense subsets of
X
, and this collection covers
X
. Hence
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\leq\aleph_{1}
.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

If
X
is a completely metrizable space with no isolated points,

\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\,=\,\begin{cases}\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{M})&\text{ if }X\text{ has a $\pi$-base consisting of Polish spaces},\\
\aleph_{1}&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For the first case, suppose
X
does not have a
\pi
-base consisting of Polish spaces.
Because

\left\{U\subseteq X\colon U\text{ is open, }U\neq\emptyset,\text{ and }w_{\downarrow}(U)=w(U)\right\}

is a
\pi
-base for
X
, there is some nonempty open
U\subseteq X
with
w_{\downarrow}(U)>\aleph_{1}
.
By the previous theorem,
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{U})=\aleph_{1}
, i.e., there is a set of
\aleph_{1}
meager-in-
U
sets covering
U
. But every set meager in
U
is also meager in
X
, so there is a collection of
\aleph_{1}
meager subsets of
X
whose union covers
U
. Because
U
is non-meager in
X
, this shows
\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\leq\aleph_{1}
.
But
\mathcal{M}_{X}
is a
\sigma
-ideal, so
\aleph_{1}\leq\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{M}_{X})
, and we conclude that
\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{M}_{X})=\aleph_{1}
.

Suppose next that
X
does have a
\pi
-base consisting of Polish spaces.
By Lemma
2.2
, there is a maximal cellular family
\mathcal{S}
in
X
consisting of Polish spaces.
Because each
U\in\mathcal{S}
is open, if
Y\subseteq X
is meager in
X
then
Y\cap U
is meager in
U
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is a family of meager subsets of
X
with
|\mathcal{F}|=\kappa<\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{M})
. For each
U\in\mathcal{S}
,

U\cap\bigcup\mathcal{F}=\bigcup\left\{U\cap M\colon M\in\mathcal{F}\right\}
is a union of
\kappa
meager-in-
U
sets. Because each
U\in\mathcal{S}
Polish and
\kappa<\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{M})
,
U\cap\bigcup\mathcal{F}
is meager in
U
.
Because
\mathcal{S}
is a cellular family, it follows that
\bigcup\left\{U\cap\bigcup\mathcal{F}\colon U\in\mathcal{S}\right\}=\big(\bigcup\mathcal{S}\big)\cap\big(\bigcup\mathcal{F}\big)
is meager in
X
. But
X\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{S}
is also meager in
X
: in fact it is a nowhere dense closed set, because
\bigcup\mathcal{S}
is dense in
X
. Thus

\textstyle\bigcup\mathcal{F}\,\subseteq\,\Big(\big(\bigcup\mathcal{S}\big)\cap\big(\bigcup\mathcal{F}\big)\Big)\cup\big(X\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{S}\big)

is meager in
X
. Thus
\kappa<\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{M}_{X})
, and this shows
\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{M})\leq\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{M}_{X})
.

For the reverse inequality, fix some nonempty open
U\subseteq X
such that
U
is Polish.
There is a set
\mathcal{F}
of meager subsets of
U
such that
|\mathcal{F}|=\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{M})
and
\bigcup\mathcal{F}
is not meager in
U
.
But because
U
is open, a subset of
U
is meager in
U
if and only if it is also meager in
X
.
Hence
\mathcal{F}
is a collection of meager subsets of
X
such that
|\mathcal{F}|=\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{M})
and
\bigcup\mathcal{F}
is not meager in
X
.
Thus
\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\leq\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{M})
.
∎

3.
Uniformity for a completely metrizable space

In this section we prove the main theorem of the paper, a characterization of
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})
in terms of
w_{\downarrow}(X)
when
X
is completely metrizable.

If
\kappa
is an infinite cardinal, then
[\kappa]^{\omega}
denotes the set of all countably infinite subsets of
\kappa
.
A family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq[\kappa]^{\omega}
is
cofinal
if for every
A\in[\kappa]^{\omega}
there is some
B\in\mathcal{F}
such that
B\supseteq A
. In other words,
\mathcal{F}\subseteq[\kappa]^{\omega}
is cofinal if it is (in the usual sense of the word) cofinal in the poset
\big([\kappa]^{\omega},\subseteq\!\big)
. Let

\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}\,=\,\min\left\{|\mathcal{F}|\colon\mathcal{F}\text{ is a cofinal subset of }[\kappa]^{\omega}\right\}.

For example,
\mathrm{cf}[\omega_{1}]^{\omega}=\aleph_{1}
because, identifying an ordinal with the set of its predecessors as usual,
\left\{\alpha\colon\omega\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}\right\}
is a cofinal subset of
[\omega_{1}]^{\omega}
.
In fact, this example with
\omega_{1}
can be extended to show that
\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}=\kappa
for all uncountable
\kappa<\aleph_{\omega}
.

As for cardinals
>\!\aleph_{\omega}
, it is not difficult to see that
\kappa\leq\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
for all uncountable
\kappa
, and a reasonably straightforward diagonalization argument shows also that
\kappa^{+}\leq\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
whenever
\kappa
is a singular cardinal with cofinality
\omega
.

Shelah’s Strong Hypothesis
, abbreviated
\mathsf{SSH}
, is the statement that these straightforward lower bounds are sharp:

\text{\scalebox{.95}{$(\mathsf{SSH})\!:$}}\qquad\qquad\qquad\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}\,=\,\begin{cases}\kappa&\text{ if }\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)>\omega,\\
\kappa^{+}&\text{ if }\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)=\omega.\end{cases}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

The failure of
\mathsf{SSH}
implies the consistency of large cardinal axioms (see
[
14
]
or
[
6
]
). Nonetheless, it is consistent, relative to large cardinals, that
\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
takes on values larger than those listed above (see
[
16
]
).

Following
[
10
]
, a subset
\mathcal{C}
of
[\kappa]^{\omega}
is
closed
if whenever
\left\{A_{n}\colon n\in\omega\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
and
A_{0}\subseteq A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}\subseteq\dots
, then
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}A_{n}\in\mathcal{C}
. If
\mathcal{C}\subseteq[\kappa]^{\omega}
is both closed and cofinal, then
\mathcal{C}
is called a
club
. (In
[
10
]
the word “unbounded” is used rather than “cofinal”.) A subset of
[\kappa]^{\omega}
is
stationary
if it intersects every club subset of
[\kappa]^{\omega}
.

Given a sequence of functions
\vec{g}=\left\langle g_{n}\colon n\in\omega\right\rangle
mapping
\kappa^{<\omega}\to\kappa^{<\omega}
, let

\mathcal{C}(\vec{g}\,)\,=\,\left\{A\in[\kappa]^{\omega}\colon\text{ for every $n\in\omega$, if }s\in A^{<\omega}\text{ then }g_{n}(s)\in A^{<\omega}\right\}.

In other words,
\mathcal{C}(\vec{g}\,)
is the set of all
A\in[\kappa]^{\omega}
that are closed, in the appropriate sense, with respect to all the
g_{n}
.
The following result can be found in
[
11
]
or
[
20
]
, and is proved by a standard closing-off argument:

Lemma 3.1
.

For any sequence
\vec{g}=\left\langle g_{n}\colon n\in\omega\right\rangle
of functions
\kappa^{<\omega}\to\kappa^{<\omega}
,
\mathcal{C}(\vec{g}\,)
is club in
[\kappa]^{\omega}
. In particular, every stationary subset of
[\kappa]^{\omega}
contains a member of
\mathcal{C}(\vec{g}\,)
.

The main tool for proving our characterization of
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})
in this section is the following result of Shelah.

Theorem 3.2

(Shelah)
.

For any infinite cardinal
\kappa
, there is a stationary subset of
[\kappa]^{\omega}
with cardinality
\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.

All stationary subsets of
[\kappa]^{\omega}
are cofinal: this follows from the fact that for any given
A\in[\kappa]^{\omega}
,
\left\{B\in[\kappa]^{\omega}\colon B\supseteq A\right\}
is club, and so any given stationary set must contain some
B\supseteq A
.
Consequently, every stationary set has size
\geq\!\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
. Shelah’s theorem says this lower bound is sharp.

All known proofs of Theorem
3.2
rely on pcf theory.
A proof first appeared in
[
18
]
, and the result also appears in Shelah’s book
[
19
]
. A simplified and corrected version of the proof is presented in
[
20
]
.

It is worth pointing out that if
\kappa=\aleph_{n}<\aleph_{\omega}
then Theorem
3.2
can be proved for
\kappa
without recourse to pcf theory, via a relatively straightforward induction on
n
. Indeed, this special case of the theorem was proved by Baumgartner and Taylor in
[
3
]
before the invention of pcf theory.

In Remark
3.5
below, we provide an example that suggests the use of stationary sets, and Shelah’s Theorem
3.2
, may be necessary for proving our characterization of
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be a completely metrizable space with uniform weight
\kappa
. Then
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})=\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.

Proof.

If
X
has uniform weight
\kappa
, then by Lemma
2.1

X
has a co-meager subspace homeomorphic to
\kappa^{\omega}
. But if
Y
is co-meager in
X
, then
Z
is meager in
X
if and only if
Z\cap Y
is meager in
Y
. Thus, to prove the theorem it suffices to show that
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})=\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.

To see that
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\leq\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
, note that
\kappa^{\omega}\approx(\kappa\times\omega)^{\omega}\approx\kappa^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}
. Let
\pi
denote the natural projection
\kappa^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}\to\omega^{\omega}
. If
Z\subseteq\kappa^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}
and
|Z|<\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})
, then
|\pi[Z]|<\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})
and so
\pi[Z]
is meager in
\omega^{\omega}
. Thus there is a sequence
F_{0},F_{1},F_{2},\dots
of nowhere dense subsets of
\omega^{\omega}
with
\pi[Z]\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}
. But it is clear that if
F
is nowhere dense in
\omega^{\omega}
then
\pi^{-1}(F)
is nowhere dense in
\kappa^{\omega}
.
Therefore
\pi^{-1}(F_{0}),\pi^{-1}(F_{1}),\pi^{-1}(F_{2}),\dots
is a sequence of nowhere dense subsets of
\kappa^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}
with
Z\subseteq\pi^{-1}(\pi[Z])\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\pi^{-1}(F_{n})
. Thus
Z
is meager. Hence

\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\leq\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}})=\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
.

To see that
\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}\leq\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
, fix some
Z\subseteq\kappa^{\omega}
with
|Z|<\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.
Viewing the points of
\kappa^{\omega}
as functions
\omega\to\kappa
, we have
|\left\{\mathrm{range}(z)\colon z\in Z\right\}|\leq|Z|<\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
, and therefore
\left\{\mathrm{range}(z)\colon z\in Z\right\}
is not cofinal in
[\kappa]^{\omega}
.
Thus there is some
A\in[\kappa]^{\omega}
such that
\mathrm{range}(z)\not\supseteq A
for any
z\in Z
.

As in the proof of Lemma
2.3
, let
F_{\alpha}=\left\{x\in\kappa^{\omega}\colon a\notin\mathrm{range}(x)\right\}
for each
\alpha<\kappa
.
In the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we showed that each of these
F_{\alpha}
is closed and nowhere dense in
\kappa^{\omega}
. Consequently

\textstyle\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}F_{\alpha}\,=\,\left\{x\in\kappa^{\omega}\colon\mathrm{range}(x)\not\supseteq A\right\}

is meager in
\kappa^{\omega}
. But this set contains
Z
, by the previous paragraph.
Thus
Z
is meager, and it follows that

\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}\leq\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
.

It remains to prove the reverse inequality:
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})\leq\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
. To do this, we find a non-meager subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
with size
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.

For each
A\in[\kappa]^{\omega}
, observe that
A^{\omega}
is a closed subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
, and is homeomorphic to the Baire space
\omega^{\omega}
. In particular, if
A\in[\kappa]^{\omega}
then there is some
Z_{A}\subseteq A^{\omega}
such that
Z_{A}
is non-meager in
A^{\omega}
and
|Z_{A}|=\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})
.
Applying Theorem
3.2
, let
\mathcal{S}
be a stationary subset of
[\kappa]^{\omega}
with
|\mathcal{S}|=\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.
Let

Z=\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{S}}Z_{A}
. Clearly
|Z|=|\mathcal{S}|\cdot\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})=\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
, and we claim that
Z
is a non-meager subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
.

Aiming for a contradiction, suppose instead that
Z
is meager in
\kappa^{\omega}
.
Fix a sequence
F_{0},F_{1},F_{2},\dots
of closed nowhere dense subsets of
\kappa^{\omega}
with
Z\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}
.
Recall that
\kappa^{\omega}
has a basis of sets of the form

[\![s]\!]\,=\,\left\{x\in\kappa^{\omega}\colon x\!\restriction\!(\mathrm{length}(s)=s)\right\}

where
s\in\kappa^{<\omega}
.
Fix
n\in\omega
.
For each basic (cl)open set
[\![s]\!]
, there is a basic (cl)open set
[\![t]\!]\subseteq[\![s]\!]
such that
[\![t]\!]\cap F_{n}=\emptyset
, because
F_{n}
is nowhere dense.
For each
n\in\omega
, fix a function
g_{n}:\kappa^{<\omega}\to\kappa^{<\omega}
choosing some such
t
for each
s
: i.e., for each
s\in\kappa^{<\omega}
,
[\![g_{n}(s)]\!]\subseteq[\![s]\!]
and
[\![g_{n}(s)]\!]\cap F_{n}=\emptyset
.

By Lemma
3.1
,

\mathcal{C}\,=\,\left\{A\in[\kappa]^{\omega}\colon\text{for any $n\in\omega$, if }x\in A^{<\omega}\text{ then }g_{n}(s)\in A^{<\omega}\right\}

is a club subset of
[\kappa]^{\omega}
. Using the fact that
\mathcal{S}
is stationary, fix
A\in\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{C}
.

For any
s\in\kappa^{<\omega}
, obesrve that
[\![s]\!]\cap A^{\omega}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
s\in A^{<\omega}
. So the basic (cl)open neighborhoods of
A^{\omega}
are the sets of the form
[\![s]\!]\cap A
with
s\in A^{<\omega}
.
Fix
n\in\omega
.
If
s\in A^{<\omega}
, then
g_{n}(s)\in A^{<\omega}
, and therefore
[\![g_{n}(s)]\!]\cap A^{\omega}
is a nonempty (cl)open subset of
A^{\omega}
such that
[\![g_{n}(s)]\!]\subseteq[\![s]\!]
and
[\![g_{n}(s)]\!]\cap F_{n}=\emptyset
. As
s
was arbitrary, this shows that
F_{n}\cap A^{\omega}
is nowhere dense in
A^{\omega}
. Because this is true for all
n
and
Z\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}
, this shows that
Z\cap A^{\omega}
is meager in
A^{\omega}
.
This is a contradiction, because
Z\supseteq Z_{A}
and
Z_{A}
is non-meager in
A^{\omega}
.

Hence
Z
is a non-meager subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
with size
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
, and therefore
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})\leq\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X
be a completely metrizable space, and let
\kappa=w_{\downarrow}(X)
. Then
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})=\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.

Proof.

Fix some nonempty open
U\subseteq X
with
w(U)=w_{\downarrow}(U)=\kappa
. By Theorem
3.3
, there is some
Z\subseteq U
such that
Z
is non-meager in
U
and
|Z|=\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
. But if
Z
is non-meager in
U
and
U
is open in
X
, then
Z
is non-meager in
X
. Thus
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\leq\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.

For the reverse inequality, fix some
Z\subseteq X
with
|Z|<\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
. By Lemma
2.2
, there is a maximal cellular family
\mathcal{S}
in
X
such that
w_{\downarrow}(U)=w(U)
for every
U\in\mathcal{S}
.
For each
U\in\mathcal{S}
, we have
\lambda=w(U)\geq\kappa
, which implies
\mathrm{cf}[\lambda]^{\omega}\geq\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.
Thus
|Z\cap U|<\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}\leq\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot[\lambda]^{\omega}
, and it follows from Theorem
3.3
that
Z\cap U
is meager in
U
.

For each
U\in\mathcal{S}
, fix a sequence
F_{1}^{U},F_{2}^{U},F_{3}^{U},\dots
of nowhere dense sets with
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}\supseteq Z\cap U
. Because
\mathcal{S}
is a cellular family,
F_{n}=\bigcup_{U\in\mathcal{S}}F_{n}^{U}
is nowhere dense in
X
for each
n
. Also,
F_{0}=X\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{S}
is nowhere dense, because
\mathcal{S}
is a maximal cellular family. Thus
F_{0},F_{1},F_{2},\dots
is a sequence of nowhere dense subsets of
X
, and
Z\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}
. Hence
Z
is meager, and
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\geq\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Shelah’s Theorem
3.2
enters this proof as a tool for showing the inequality

\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})\,\leq\,\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega},

i.e., for showing that
\kappa^{\omega}
has a non-meager subset of size
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.
In summary, our idea was: let
\mathcal{F}
be a stationary family in
[\kappa]^{\omega}
, and for each
A\in\mathcal{F}
let
Z_{A}
be a non-meager subset of
A^{\omega}
with size
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})
. (Note that
A^{\omega}
is just a copy of the Baire space, so it does in fact have a non-meager subset of this size.) Then
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{F}}Z_{A}
is non-meager.

This raises the question of whether we really need
\mathcal{F}
to be stationary. Would any cofinal family have sufficed?
The purpose of this remark is to give an example answering this question in the negative: if
\mathcal{F}
is merely cofinal in
[\kappa]^{\omega}
, then
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{F}}Z_{A}
may not be non-meager in
\kappa^{\omega}
.

To see this, fix a cardinal
\kappa>\omega
and let

\mathcal{F}\,=\,\left\{A\in[\kappa]^{\omega}\colon\sup A\in A\right\}.

It is clear that
\mathcal{F}
is cofinal in
[\kappa]^{\omega}
. However, a little thought reveals that

\mathcal{C}=\left\{A\in[\kappa]^{\omega}\colon\sup A\notin A\right\}
is club, and thus
\mathcal{F}
is non-stationary.

For each
A\in\mathcal{F}
, let

U_{A}\,=\,\left\{x\in A^{\omega}\colon\sup A\in\mathrm{range}(x)\right\}\,=\,\textstyle\bigcup\left\{[\![s]\!]\colon\sup A\in\mathrm{range}(s)\right\}.

This set is open in
A^{\omega}
. Furthermore, if
[\![s]\!]
is a basic (cl)open subset of
A^{\omega}
, then
[\![s\!\,^{\frown}(\sup A)]\!]
is a basic (cl)open subset of
A^{\omega}
contained in
U_{A}\cap[\![s]\!]
. Thus
U_{A}
is a dense open subset of
U_{A}
. Because
A^{\omega}
is just a copy of the Baire space, there is some
Z_{A}\subseteq U_{A}
with
|Z_{A}|=\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})
.

We claim that
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{F}}Z_{A}
is meager in
\kappa^{\omega}
. To see this, let

F_{n}\,=\,\left\{x\in\kappa^{\omega}\colon x(n)=\sup\mathrm{range}(x)\right\}.

Each
F_{n}
is a closed subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
, because

\kappa^{\omega}\setminus F_{n}\,=\,\textstyle\bigcup\left\{[\![s]\!]\colon\mathrm{length}(s)>n\text{ and }s(n)\neq\sup\mathrm{range}(s)\right\}.

Furthermore, if
[\![s]\!]
is a basic (cl)open subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
, then we can find some
s^{\prime}\supseteq s
with
\mathrm{length}(s^{\prime})>n
, and then set
t=s^{\prime}\!\,{}^{\frown}\alpha
for some
\alpha>\sup\mathrm{range}(s^{\prime})
. Then
[\![t]\!]
is a basic (cl)open subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
with
[\![t]\!]\subseteq[\![s]\!]
and
[\![t]\!]\cap F_{n}=\emptyset
. Thus each
F_{n}
is nowhere dense in
\kappa^{\omega}
. Consequently
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}
is meager. Furthermore,
U_{A}\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}
for each
A\in\mathcal{F}
, and this means that
Z=\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{F}}Z_{A}\subseteq\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{F}}U_{A}\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}
. Therefore
Z
is meager.

\square

4.
The cofinality of
\mathcal{M}_{X}
and
\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{X}

Given an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on a set
X
, we say
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
is
cofinal
if
\mathcal{F}
is cofinal (in the usual sense of the word) in the poset
(\mathcal{I},\subseteq)
; i.e., for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
there is some
B\in\mathcal{F}
such that
B\supseteq A
.
Recall from the introduction that
\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{\mathcal{I}})
is the minimal size of a cofinal subset of
\mathcal{I}
.

Given a topological space
X
, let

\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{X}\,=\,\left\{N\subseteq X\colon N\text{ is nowhere dense in }X\right\}.

If
X
is a
T_{1}
space with no isolated points then
\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{X}
is an ideal on
X
. It is not generally a
\sigma
-ideal.

It is not difficult to see that
\mathrm{cov}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{X})=\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})
whenever
\mathcal{M}_{X}
is a
\sigma
-ideal, and therefore, by the results in the previous section,

\mathrm{cov}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{X})\,=\,\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\,=\,\begin{cases}\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})&\text{ if }w_{\downarrow}(X)=\aleph_{0}\\
\aleph_{1}&\text{ if }w_{\downarrow}(X)>\aleph_{0}\end{cases}

if
X
is completely metrizable and has no isolated points.
Using the fact that the density of a metric space is equal to its weight (see
[
7
, Theorem 4.1.15]
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{non}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{X})

\displaystyle\,=\,\min\left\{|D|\colon D\text{ is dense in a nonempty open $U\subseteq X$}\right\}

\displaystyle\,=\,\left\{w(U)\colon U\subseteq X\text{ is nonempty and open}\right\}\,=\,w_{\downarrow}(X)

for every metrizable space
X
(whether it has isolated points or not).
If
X
is metrizable and has no isolated points, then taking
F_{n}
to be a maximal
\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}}
-separated subset of
X
for each
n
, we obtain a countable collection of nowhere dense sets whose union is dense. Thus

\mathrm{add}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{X})\,=\,\aleph_{0}.

Hence, as with
\mathcal{M}_{X}
, three of the four cardinal invariants of
\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{X}
are determined, all but the cofinality.

In this section we prove some results and ask some questions concerning
\mathrm{cof}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
and
\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
. The reason for focusing on the generalized Baire spaces
\kappa^{\omega}
is twofold: (1) these spaces played a fundamental part in our analysis of additivity, covering, and uniformity in the preceding two sections, and (2) already the spaces
\kappa^{\omega}
present significant difficulties and raise some interesting questions.
The foremost of these questions is:

Question 4.1
.

Is
\mathrm{cof}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}})=\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
for every
\kappa
?

For the case
\kappa=\omega
(the Baire space) the answer to this question is yes, by a theorem of Fremlin in
[
9
, Section 3]
. For uncountable
\kappa
the question remains open (even for
\kappa=\omega_{1}
). However, we do have the following inequality:

Theorem 4.2
.

\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})\leq\mathrm{cof}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
for every
\kappa
.

Proof.

For each
n\in\omega
, let

B_{n}=\left\{x\in\kappa^{\omega}\colon x(0)=n\right\}
, and let

\phi_{n}:B_{n}\to\kappa^{\omega}
be the natural homeomorphism sending
x\in B_{n}
to the function
n\mapsto x(n+1)
(in other words,
\phi_{n}
deletes the first coordinate of
x
).
Given
F\subseteq\kappa^{\omega}
, define
\tilde{F}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\phi_{n}[F\cap B_{n}]
.
It is clear that the map
F\mapsto\tilde{F}
sends nowhere dense subsets of
\kappa^{\omega}
to meager subsets of
\kappa^{\omega}
. We claim that if
\mathcal{F}
is a cofinal subset of
\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}}
, then
\{\tilde{F}:\,F\in\mathcal{F}\}
is cofinal in
\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}}
.

To see this, let
Z
be a meager subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
, and let
F_{0},F_{1},F_{2},\dots
be a sequence of nowhere dense sets covering
Z
. But then
F=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\phi_{n}^{-1}(F_{n})
is nowhere dense in
\kappa^{\omega}
, and
\tilde{F}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{n}\supseteq Z
. Because
\mathcal{F}
is cofinal, there is some
E\in\mathcal{F}
with
E\supseteq F
, and then
\tilde{E}\supseteq Z
.
Thus
\{\tilde{F}:\,F\in\mathcal{F}\}
is cofinal in
\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}}
, and this shows
\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})\leq\mathrm{cof}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
.
∎

We next proceed to prove three lower bounds for

Theorem 4.4
.

For any infinite cardinal
\kappa
,

\kappa^{+},\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega},\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{\mathcal{M}})\,\leq\,\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})\,\leq\,\mathrm{cof}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}})\,\leq\,2^{\kappa}.

Proof.

To see that
\kappa^{+}\leq\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
, let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of meager subsets of
\kappa^{\omega}
with
|\mathcal{F}|=\kappa
. Enumerate
\mathcal{F}=\left\{F_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\}
, and for each
\alpha<\kappa
let

B_{\alpha}=\left\{x\in\kappa^{\omega}\colon x(0)=\alpha\right\}
.
Because each
B_{\alpha}
is clopen in
\kappa^{\omega}
and
F_{\alpha}
is nowhere dense, for each
\alpha
there is some
x_{\alpha}\in B_{\alpha}\setminus F_{\alpha}
. But then
\left\{x_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\}
is meager (in fact it is a closed discrete set) and is not contained in any member of
\mathcal{F}
. Thus
\mathcal{F}
is not cofinal.

To see that
\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}\leq\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
, simply note that
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})\leq\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
, and
\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}\leq\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
by Theorem
3.3
.

To see that
\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M})\leq\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
, first note, as in the proof of Theorem
3.3
, that
\kappa^{\omega}\approx(\kappa\times\omega)^{\omega}\approx\kappa^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}
. Let
\pi:\kappa^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}\to\omega^{\omega}
denote the natural projection.
Given
A\subseteq\kappa^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}
, define

\tilde{A}=\left\{x\in\omega^{\omega}\colon\pi^{-1}(x)\subseteq A\right\}.

If
A\subseteq\kappa^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}
, then
A\supseteq\pi^{-1}(\tilde{A})
. Using this fact, Lemma
4.3
implies that if
A
is meager in
\kappa^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}
then
\tilde{A}
is meager in
\omega^{\omega}
.

Now suppose
\mathcal{F}
is a cofinal subset of
\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}}
. By the previous paragraph,
\{\tilde{A}:\,A\in\mathcal{F}\}
is a subset of
\mathcal{M}_{\omega^{\omega}}
. We claim that it is also cofinal.
To see this, let
X\subseteq\omega^{\omega}
be meager.
By the easy direction of Lemma
4.3
,
\pi^{-1}(X)
is meager in
\kappa^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}
.
Consequently, there is some
A\in\mathcal{F}
with
A\supseteq\pi^{-1}(X)
, and this implies
\tilde{A}\supseteq X
.
Thus
\{\tilde{A}:\,A\in\mathcal{F}\}
is a cofinal subset of
\mathcal{M}_{\omega^{\omega}}
, and it follows that
\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M})\leq\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}})=\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
.

Finally, we wish to show that
\mathrm{cof}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}})\leq 2^{\kappa}
. This is straightforward:
2^{\kappa}
has a basis of size
\kappa
(the sets of the form
[\![s]\!]
) and thus has
2^{\kappa}
open sets and
2^{\kappa}
closed sets. The inequality follows, because the collection of nowhere dense closed sets is cofinal in
\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{X}
.
∎

Let us note that these three lower bounds are independent of one another, in the sense that any of
\kappa^{+}
,
\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
, or
\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M})
can be strictly larger than the other two, for certain values of
\kappa
in certain models of set theory.

If
\mathsf{GCH}
holds, this theorem implies
\kappa^{+}=2^{\kappa}=\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})=\mathrm{cof}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
for every
\kappa
. This gives a consistent positive answer to Question
4.1
.
Combining this observation with Theorem
4.2
, the open part of Question
4.1
is whether it is consistent to have
\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})<\mathrm{cof}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
for some
\kappa
.

Question 4.5
.

Is it consistent that
\max\{\kappa^{+},\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega},\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M})\}<\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
for some infinite cardinal
\kappa
? What about
\kappa=\omega_{1}
?

Question 4.6
.

Is it consistent to have
\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa^{\omega}})<2^{\kappa}
for some infinite cardinal
\kappa
? What about
\mathrm{cof}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}})<2^{\kappa}
?

We end this section by proving a lower bound for
\mathrm{cof}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
.
Consider the poset
\omega^{\kappa}
of functions
\kappa\to\omega
ordered by

f\leq g\qquad\text{ if and only if }\qquad f(\alpha)\leq g(\alpha)\text{ for all }\alpha<\kappa.

Let
\mathrm{cof}(\omega^{\kappa},\leq)
denote the cofinality of this poset, i.e.,

\mathrm{cof}(\omega^{\kappa},\leq)\,=\,\min\left\{|\mathcal{F}|\colon\forall g\in\omega^{\kappa}\,\exists f\in\mathcal{F}\ g\leq f\right\}.

This poset and its cofinality were studied by Jech and Prikry in
[
12
]
, where they proved that if
2^{\aleph_{0}}
is regular and
2^{\aleph_{0}}<2^{\aleph_{1}}
, then
\mathrm{cof}(\omega^{\omega_{1}},\leq)=2^{\aleph_{0}}
implies there is an inner model with a measurable cardinal.
It is also known that if
\kappa^{\aleph_{0}}=\kappa
then
\mathrm{cof}(\omega^{\kappa},\leq)=2^{\kappa}
.
It is an open problem, and has been since the appearance of Jech and Prikry’s paper in 1984, whether it is consistent to have
\mathrm{cof}(\omega^{\kappa},\leq)<2^{\kappa}
for any uncountable
\kappa
.
The following theorem shows that finding a positive answer to Question
4.6
is at least as hard as solving this problem of Jech and Prikry.

Theorem 4.7
.

\mathrm{cof}(\omega^{\kappa},\leq)\,\leq\,\mathrm{cof}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}})
.

Proof.

To each
F\in\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}}
we associate a function
g_{F}\in\omega^{\kappa}
as follows:

g_{F}(\alpha)\,=\,\min\left\{\mathrm{length}(s)\colon s(0)=\alpha\text{ and }[\![s]\!]\cap F=\emptyset\right\}.

Observe that
g_{F}
is well-defined because
F
is nowhere dense, which means that there must be some
s
with
[\![s]\!]\subseteq[\![\langle\alpha\rangle]\!]\text{ and }[\![s]\!]\cap F=\emptyset
.
Also observe that if
F\subseteq E
then
g_{F}\leq g_{E}
.

If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}}
and
|\mathcal{F}|<\mathrm{cof}(\omega^{\kappa},\leq)
, then
\left\{g_{F}\colon F\in\mathcal{F}\right\}
is not cofinal in
(\omega^{\kappa},\leq)
, and there is some
f\in\omega^{\kappa}
such that
f\not\leq g_{F}
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
. Let

K\,=\,\left\{x\in\kappa^{\omega}\colon x(n)=0\text{ if }n>f(x(0))\right\}.

This set is clearly nowhere dense, because if
[\![s]\!]
is a basic (cl)open subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
then we may take
t=s\!\,^{\frown}1\!\,^{\frown}1\!\,^{\frown}\cdots\text{($n$ times)}\cdots\!\,^{\frown}1
for some
n>f(t(0))
, and then
[\![t]\!]\cap K=\emptyset
.
However, it is also clear that
g_{K}\geq f
(in fact,
g_{K}=f
), because if
s(0)=\alpha
and
\mathrm{length}(s)<f(\alpha)
, then

x(n)\,=\,\begin{cases}s(n)&\text{ if }n\in\mathrm{dom}(s),\\
0&\text{ if not}\end{cases}

is a point of
[\![s]\!]\cap F
. Hence
g_{K}\not\leq g_{F}
for any
F\in\mathcal{F}
. But recall that if
F\supseteq K
then
g_{K}\leq g_{F}
; so
F\not\supseteq K
for any
F\in\mathcal{F}
. In other words,
K
witnesses that
\mathcal{F}
is not cofinal in
\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{\kappa^{\omega}}
.
∎

5.
Compact Hausdorff spaces

In this final section we turn our attention briefly to the class of locally compact Hausdorff spaces.
The main theorem is an upper bound for
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})
reminiscent of Theorem
3.4
.

Let us point out that when
X
is a compact Hausdorff space, the invariant
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})
has been widely investigate already.
If
\mathbb{B}
is the Boolean algebra of regular open subsets of
X
, then
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})=\mathfrak{m}(\mathbb{B})
, the
Martin number
of
\mathbb{B}
. The Martin number of a Boolean algebra is very important in the theory of forcing. For example, Martin’s Axiom
\mathsf{MA}_{\aleph_{1}}
is equivalent to the assertion that
\mathfrak{m}(\mathbb{B})>\aleph_{1}
for every ccc Boolean algebra
\mathbb{B}
, or equivalently, that
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})>\aleph_{1}
for every compact Hausdorff
X
with
c(X)=\aleph_{0}
.
Similarly, the assertion
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{c}
is equivalent to the assertion that
\mathfrak{m}(\mathbb{B})>\aleph_{1}
for every
\sigma
-centered Boolean algebra
\mathbb{B}
, or equivalently, that
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})>\aleph_{1}
for every separable compact Hausdorff space
X
(see
[
4
]
).
Furthermore,
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})=\mathrm{cov}(\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\mathcal{NWD}$}}_{X})
for any compact Hausdorff space
X
, and the latter has also been studied from a more topological point of view. It is sometimes called the
Novák number
of
X
; see, for example,
[
1
]
,
[
13
]
, or
[
22
]
.

Slightly less well known is the
dual Martin number
of a Boolean algebra
\mathbb{B}
, denoted
\mathfrak{n}\hskip-3.1298pt\mathfrak{v}(\mathbb{B})
in
[
2
]
. This is the Stone dual of
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})
, in the same way that
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})
is the Stone dual of
\mathfrak{m}
. That is, if
\mathbb{B}
is the Boolean algebra of regular open subsets of
X
, then
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})=\mathfrak{n}\hskip-3.1298pt\mathfrak{v}(\mathbb{B})
.
A kind of “opposite” to Martin’s axiom was proved by Merrill in
[
17
]
: he showed that in the Bell-Kunen model described in
[
5
]
,
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{X})<\mathfrak{c}
for every separable compact Hausdorff space
X
.
The main result of this section adds to what is known about
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})
when
X
is (locally) compact Hausdorff, or dually, what is known about the invariant
\mathfrak{n}\hskip-3.1298pt\mathfrak{v}(\mathbb{B})
.

Recall that the
\pi
-weight
of a space
X
, denoted
\pi w(X)
, is the minimum cardinality of a
\pi
-base for
X
. Let

\pi w_{\downarrow}(X)\,=\,\min\left\{\kappa\colon\text{some nonempty open $U\subseteq X$ has $\pi$-weight $\kappa$}\right\}.

We say that
X
has
uniform
\pi
-weight
\kappa
if
\pi w(X)=\pi w_{\downarrow}(X)=\kappa
, or, equivalently, if every nonempty open subset of
X
has
\pi
-weight
\kappa
.

Let us note that if
X
is metric space then
w(X)=\pi w(X)
(see
[
7
, Theorem 4.1.15]
). With that in mind, the following lemma is analogous to Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space with uniform
\pi
-weight
\kappa
. Then there is a (not necessarily continuous) function
F:\kappa^{\omega}\to X
such that if
Z
is a non-meager subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
, then
F[Z]
is non-meager in
X
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a
\pi
-base for
X
with
|\mathcal{B}|=\kappa
.

Let us say that a sequence
\langle U_{0},U_{1},\dots,U_{\ell}\rangle
of sets in
\mathcal{B}
is
strongly decreasing
if
U_{j}\supseteq\overline{U_{j+1}}
for all
j<\ell
.
Let
\mathcal{T}
be the collection of all strongly decreasing finite sequences of members of
\mathcal{B}
.
Given
s,t\in\mathcal{T}
, define
s\leq t
to mean
s=t\!\restriction\!(\mathrm{length}(s))
(i.e.,
t
is an end-extension of
s
).
This ordering makes
\mathcal{T}
into a tree.

We claim that each
s=\langle U_{0},U_{1},\dots,U_{\ell}\rangle\in\mathcal{T}
has exactly
\kappa
successors in
\mathcal{T}
. To see this, note that

\mathcal{E}_{s}\,=\,\left\{V\in\mathcal{B}\colon\overline{V}\subseteq U_{\ell}\right\}

is a
\pi
-base for
U_{\ell}
. Because
\pi w_{\downarrow}(X)=\kappa
, this means
|\mathcal{E}_{s}|\geq\kappa
, and clearly
|\mathcal{E}_{s}|\leq|\mathcal{B}|=\kappa
. Thus
|\mathcal{E}_{s}|=\kappa
. But
\mathcal{E}_{s}
is precisely the set of all
V
such that
s\!\,^{\frown}V=\langle U_{0},U_{1},\dots,U_{\ell},V\rangle\in\mathcal{T}
, so
s
has
\kappa
successors in
\mathcal{T}
.

Thus
\mathcal{T}
is a subtree of
\mathcal{B}^{<\omega}
such that every
s\in\mathcal{T}
has exactly
\kappa
successors in
\mathcal{T}
.
This characterizes
\mathcal{T}
up to order-isomorphism: it is isomorphic to
\kappa^{<\omega}
.

A
branch
through
\mathcal{T}
is a function
b:\omega\to\mathcal{B}
such that
b\!\restriction\!n\in\mathcal{T}
for every
n
. Let
B
denote the set of all branches through
\mathcal{T}
. Observe that
B
has a natural topology, with a basis of sets of the form

[\![s]\!]\,=\,\left\{b\colon b\text{ is a branch through $\mathcal{T}$ and }b\!\restriction\!(\mathrm{length}(s))=s\right\}.

Because the tree
\mathcal{T}
is order-isomorphic to the tree
\kappa^{<\omega}
, it is clear that
B
, endowed with this topology, is homeomorphic to
\kappa^{\omega}
. To prove the lemma, it remains find a function
F:B\to X
such that if
Z
is a non-meager subset of
B
, then
F[Z]
is non-meager in
X
.

For each
b\in B
we have an infinite sequence
b(0),b(1),b(2),\dots
of members of
\mathcal{B}
such that
b(n)\supseteq\overline{b(n+1)}
for all
n
. Because
X
is compact, this implies
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}b(n)=\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\overline{b(n+1)}\neq\emptyset
. Let
F:B\to X
be a choice function mapping each
b\in B
to some (any) element of
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}b(n)
.

We claim that if
V
is an open dense subset of
X
, then
F^{-1}(V)
contains an open dense subset of
B
.
To see this,
fix some
s=\langle U_{0},U_{1},\dots,U_{\ell}\rangle\in\mathcal{T}
. Because
V
is open and dense in
X
,
V\cap U_{\ell}
is a nonempty open subset of
X
. Because
\mathcal{B}
is a
\pi
-base for
X
, there is some
W\in\mathcal{B}
such that
\overline{W}\subseteq U_{\ell}\cap V
. Because
\overline{W}\subseteq U_{\ell}
, we have
s\!\,^{\frown}W\in\mathcal{T}
. Because
W\subseteq V
, if
b\in[\![s\!\,^{\frown}W]\!]
then
F(b)\in b(\ell+1)=W\subseteq V
.
Hence
[\![s\!\,^{\frown}W]\!]\subseteq F^{-1}(V)
.
Thus
[\![s\!\,^{\frown}W]\!]
is a basic (cl)open subset of
B
such that
[\![s\!\,^{\frown}W]\!]\subseteq[\![s]\!]\cap F^{-1}(V)
.
As
s
was arbitrary, this shows
F^{-1}(V)
contains an open dense set.

To finish the proof, suppose
Z\subseteq B
and
F[Z]
is meager in
X
.
This means there is a sequence
V_{0},V_{1},V_{2},\dots
of dense open subsets of
X
such that
F[Z]\cap\bigcap_{n\in\omega}V_{n}=\emptyset
. Pulling things back through
F
, this implies
Z\cap\bigcap_{n\in\omega}F^{-1}(V_{n})=\emptyset
. By the previous paragraph,
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}F^{-1}(V_{n})
is a co-meager subset of
B
. Thus
Z
is meager.
Thus, by contraposition, if
Z
is non-meager in
B
then
F[Z]
is non-meager in
X
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
X
be a locally compact Hausdorff space, and let
\kappa=\pi w_{\downarrow}(X)
. Then
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\leq\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}\cdot\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

If
\kappa=\pi w_{\downarrow}(X)
then there is a nonempty open
U\subseteq X
with uniform
\pi
-weight
\kappa
.
Using the fact that
X
is locally compact, and passing to a subspace of
U
if necessary, we may (and do) assume
\overline{U}
is compact.
By the previous lemma, there is a function
F:\kappa^{\omega}\to\overline{U}
such that if
Z
is meager in
\kappa^{\omega}
then
F[Z]
is meager in
\overline{U}
. By Theorem
3.3
, there is a non-meager
Z\subseteq\kappa^{\omega}
with
|Z|=\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.
So
F[Z]
is a non-meager subset of
\overline{U}
with
|Z|=\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.
Because
U
is open in
X
,
F[Z]
is also non-meager in
X
, so this shows
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\leq\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\cdot\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}
.
∎

Because
w(X)=\pi w(X)
and
w_{\downarrow}(X)=\pi w_{\downarrow}(X)
whenever
X
is a metric space (see
[
7
, Theorem 4.1.15]
), this theorem essentially asserts the same thing as Theorem
3.4
, but for locally compact spaces, and with the equality weakened to an inequality. It is worth pointing out that, as opposed to metric spaces, with compact spaces full equality cannot be established.

Theorem 5.3
.

It is consistent with
\mathsf{ZFC}
that there is a compact Hausdorff space
X
with uniform
\pi
-weight
\kappa
such that
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})<\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}\cdot\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

In
[
2
, Theorem 3.1.8]
it is shown that if
\mathbb{B}
is the measure algebra on
\mathbb{R}
then
\mathfrak{n}\hskip-3.1298pt\mathfrak{v}(\mathbb{B})=\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{L})
(the least size of a non-Lebesgue-measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}
).
Equivalently, if
X
is the Stone dual of the measure algebra then
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})=\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{L})
.
Consequently, if
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{L})<\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})
then
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M}_{X})<\mathrm{cf}[\kappa]^{\omega}\cdot\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})
.
The consistency of
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{L})<\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})
is well known (for example, it holds in the random model).
∎

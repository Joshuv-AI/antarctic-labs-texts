Abstract

We study the sums ∑ n ≤ X , ( n , q ) = 1 μ ⁡ ( n ) n s ​ log k ⁡ ( X n ) \sum_{n\leq X,(n,q)=1}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{s}}\log^{k}\left(\frac{X}{n}\right) , where k ∈ { 0 , 1 } k\in\{0,1\} , s ∈ ℂ s\in\mathbb{C} , ℜ ⁡ s > 0 \Re s>0 and give asymptotic estimations in an explicit manner. In this paper, we aim at evaluating explicitely the following two quantities

A wide ranging estimate

Our first result is a simple but efficient estimate.

Theorem 1.1
.

When
k\geq 1
is an integer,
\sigma\geq 1
and
X\geq 1
, we have

0\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\log^{k}\biggl(\frac{X}{n}\biggr)\leq 1.00303\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\bigl(k+(\sigma-1)\log X\bigr)(\log X)^{k-1}.

When
k\neq 1
, we may replace
1.00303
by 1.

The non-negativity is very useful in practice.
The case
k=0
,
\sigma=1

[
8

[
14

[

Its extension to

1.1]

Notation

Throughout the present work the variable
p
denotes a prime
number. We also use the
O^{*}
notation
: we write

f(X)=O^{*}(h(X))
, as
X\to a

in a
neighborhood of

to
\infty

q\mapsto q^{s}\prod_{p|q}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)

Theorem 1.4

(The Identity Factory)
.

Let
f
and
g
be two arithmetic functions.
We define
S_{f}(t)=\sum_{n\leq t}f(n)
and
S_{f\star g}(t)
similarly.
Let
h:(0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be Lebesgue-integrable
over every segment
\subset(0,1]
and let
H
be function over

[1,\infty)
that is absolutely
continuous on every finite interval of
[1,\infty)
. When
X\geq 1
, we have

\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)H\left(\frac{X}{n}\right)-H(1)S_{f}(X)=\int^{X}_{1}S_{f\star g}\Bigl(\frac{X}{t}\Bigr)h\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)\frac{dt}{t}\\
+\int_{1}^{X}S_{f}\Bigl(\frac{X}{t}\Bigr)\biggl(H^{\prime}(t)-\frac{1}{t}\sum_{n\leq t}g(n)h\left(\frac{n}{t}\right)\biggr)dt.

In Section
2
, we prove the above identity and describe several of its consequences.

Furthermore, we show below that Theorem
1.4
enables one to bound

\sum_{n\leq X}\mu(n)/n^{\sigma}
,
\sigma>0
, in terms of

\{\sum_{n\leq t}\mu(n)/n\}_{t}
, which in turn may be expressed in terms of

\{\sum_{n\leq t}\mu(n)\}_{t}
, if required.

As a surprising upshot, the
important question of deriving efficient estimates for

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\log p}{p}
from estimates of

\sum_{p\leq X}\log p
can be settled with the choices

f=\Lambda
,
g=1
,
H=\text{Id}
, since
f\star g=\log
, and still
have several possibilities for choosing
h
(even
h=\mathds{1}
leads
to interesting results). Independently and recently, M. Balazard used a similar approach for
this question, which we include in the Appendix
A

question has also been the subject of
[

]
,

[

]
by R. Vanlalngaia and its extension to primes in
arithmetic progression is treated in

(see also

[

A methodological remark

The way error terms are handled in analytic number theory is of
utmost importance. While the Perron summation formula exhibits the use of
complex analysis and multiplicative characters, the
exponential sum method in essence relies on the Fourier expansion of
the sawtooth function
x\mapsto\{x\}-\frac{1}{2}
, and thus uses complex analysis
in the additive world. Instead, our approach is to use real analysis
and handle the arising error terms directly by absolute value bounds. In the language of Theorem
1.4
, they correspond to the
terms
H^{\prime}(t)-\frac{1}{t}\sum_{n\leq t}g(n)h\left(\frac{n}{t}\right)
, where we are usually selecting either
g(n)=1
or
g(n)=(-1)^{n+1}
.
Precisely, one of the novelties of this article is
the usage of
g(n)=(-1)^{n+1}
rather than solely of
g(n)=1
; this will become clearer in Appendix
A
.

2.
The Identity Factory, proof of Theorem
1.4

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

On the one hand, by the local absolute continuity of
H
,
H
has a derivate almost everywhere, which is Lebesgue-integrable. Thus, by integration by parts, we obtain

\int_{1}^{X}S_{f}\Bigl(\frac{X}{t}\Bigr)H^{\prime}(t)dt=\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)H\left(\frac{X}{n}\right)-H(1)S_{f}(X).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{X}S_{f}\Bigl(\frac{X}{t}\Bigr)\frac{1}{t}\sum_{n\leq t}g(n)h\left(\frac{n}{t}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq X}g(n)\int_{n}^{X}S_{f}\Bigl(\frac{X}{t}\Bigr)h\left(\frac{n}{t}\right)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq X}g(n)\int_{1}^{\frac{X}{n}}S_{f}\left(\frac{\frac{X}{n}}{t}\right)h\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{X}\sum_{n\leq{X}/{t}}g(n)S_{f}\left(\frac{\frac{X}{t}}{n}\right)h\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)\frac{dt}{t},

where we have used summation by parts, a change of variables and
then Fubini’s theorem, respectively. The proof follows on noticing
the identity

\sum_{n\leq{X}/{t}}g(n)\sum_{m\leq\frac{X}{tn}}f(m)=\sum_{\ell\leq{X}/{t}}(f\star g)(\ell)

valid for any real number
X\geq 1
.

\square

The case
H=\text{Id}
,
g=\mathds{1}
and
f=\mu
yields the
following statement, which is the initial result of
[
7
]
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
h:(0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be any Lebesgue-integrable function
over every segment of
(0,1]
. When
X\geq 1
, we have

m(X)-\frac{M(X)}{X}=\frac{1}{X}\int_{\frac{1}{X}}^{1}\frac{h(t)}{t}dt-\frac{1}{X}\int_{1}^{X}M\left(\frac{X}{t}\right)\biggl(1-\frac{1}{t}\sum_{n\leq t}h\left(\frac{n}{t}\right)\biggr)dt.

Although not required, it is better to normalize
h
by imposing the condition
\int_{0}^{1}h(t)dt=1
.

In
[
29

, it is proven that one recovers all the (regular enough) identities linking
m(X)
and

with the result of Corollary

Let us see three examples. Let
X\geq 1
. On selecting

, the Dirac delta function at

\sum_{n\leq X}\mu(n)\left\{\frac{X}{n}\right\}=-1+Xm(X);

on selecting

\int_{1}^{X}\bigg[\frac{X}{t}\bigg]\frac{M(t)}{t}dt=\log X,

which can be also found in
[

and
[

\sum_{n\leq X}\mu(n)\frac{\left\{\frac{X}{n}\right\}^{2}-\left\{\frac{X}{n}\right\}}{\frac{X}{n}}=Xm(X)-M(X)-2+\frac{2}{X};

see
[

The functional transform that, to a function

Corollary 2.2
.

When
X\geq 1
, we have

\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\log\left(\frac{X}{n}\right)+\gamma\biggl(\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}-\frac{M(X)}{X}\biggr)\\
=1-\frac{1}{X}+\frac{1}{X}\int_{1}^{X}M\left(\frac{X}{t}\right)\biggl(\log t+\gamma+\frac{1}{t}-\sum_{n\leq t}\frac{1}{n}\biggr)dt,

where
\gamma
is Euler’s constant.

Proof.

We select
H=\text{Id}\cdot\log-\log+\ \gamma\cdot\text{Id}
,

h=\text{Id}^{-1}
,
f=\mu
and
g=\mathds{1}
in Theorem
1.4

We readily derive

\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\log\left(\frac{X}{n}\right)-\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{\mu(n)}{X}\log\left(\frac{X}{n}\right)+\gamma\biggl(\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}-\frac{M(X)}{X}\biggr)\\
=1-\frac{1}{X}+\frac{1}{X}\int_{1}^{X}M\left(\frac{X}{t}\right)\biggl(\log t+\gamma-\sum_{n\leq t}\frac{1}{n}\biggr)dt.

The result is obtained by observing that, by summation by parts, we have

\sum_{n\leq X}\mu(n)\log\left(\frac{X}{n}\right)=\int_{1}^{X}M\left(\frac{X}{t}\right)\frac{dt}{t}.

∎

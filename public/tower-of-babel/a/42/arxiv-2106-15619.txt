An endpoint estimate of the bilinear paraboloid restriction operator Thanks: This research was supported by Basic Science Research Program through the National Research Foundation of Korea(NRF) funded by the Ministry of Education (NRF-2020R1I1A1A01062209)
Jungjin Lee

Abstract
. In Fourier restriction problems, a cone and a paraboloid are model surfaces. The sharp bilinear cone restriction estimate was first shown by Wolff, and later the endpoint was obtained by Tao. For a paraboloid, the sharp L 2 L^{2} bilinear restriction estimate was obtained by Tao, but the endpoint was remained open. In this paper we prove the endpoint L 2 L^{2} bilinear restriction estimate for a paraboloid.

1.
Introduction

Fix
n\geq 2
, let
\Sigma
be a hypersurface
defined by

\Sigma=\{(\xi,s(\xi)):\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
. Then the (adjoint) Fourier restriction operator

\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma}f
for the hypersurface
\Sigma
can be
defined by

\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma}f(x,t):=\int e^{2\pi i(x\cdot\xi+ts(\xi))}f(\xi)a(\xi)d\xi,

where
a(\xi)
is a smooth cut-off function.

The (adjoint) restriction estimate

\mathds{R}^{*}_{\Sigma}(p,q)
for
1\leq p,q\leq\infty

is of the form

(1.1)

\|\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma}f\|_{q}\leq C_{p,q,\Sigma}\|f\|_{p},

and the restriction problem is to determine
(p,q)
for
which the estimate
\mathds{R}^{*}_{\Sigma}(p,q)
holds.
There are two representative model hypersurfaces. One is
a cone
\Sigma_{cone}=\{(\xi,|\xi|):\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
, and the other is a paraboloid

\Sigma_{parab}=\{(\xi,-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{2}):\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
.
For these two surfaces the restriction operators
\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma_{cone}}f
and
\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma_{parab}}f
are
related to other problems such
as the Bochner-Riesz conjecture, Kakeya conjecture and
Sogge’s local smoothing conjecture,
see
[
20
,
12
,
22
,
17
,
28
,
2
]
.
Moreover, they are also connected to the wave and
Schrödinger equations because
\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma_{cone}}\hat{f}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma_{parab}}\hat{f}
are the solutions to the free wave
equation
u_{tt}-\Delta u=0
and the free Schrödinger
equation
4\pi i\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=0
,
respectively, see
[
22
,
23
,
15
]
.

The bilinear restriction estimate
\mathds{R}^{*}_{\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}}(p\times p,q)
,
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
is of the form

\|\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma_{1}}f\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma_{2}}g\|_{q}\leq C_{p,q,\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}}\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{p},

where
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
are two compact subsets of

\Sigma
such that the set
of unit normal vectors of
\Sigma_{1}
are separated by
a non-zero distance from the set of unit normal vectors of

\Sigma_{2}
.
This bilinear restriction estimate

\mathds{R}^{*}_{\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}}(p\times p,q/2)

was used to improve the linear restriction estimate

\mathds{R}^{*}_{\Sigma}(p,q)
.
(The restriction estimate have been improved further by Bourgain–Guth
[
4
]
, Guth
[
5
,
6
]
, Wang
[
26
]
, Hickman–Rogers
[
8
]
.)
In addition, as the relation between the Stein-Tomas restriction theorem and the Strichartz estimate, the bilinear restriction estimates
\mathds{R}^{*}_{\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}}(2\times 2,q)
lead to the corresponding bilinear estimates applied to null form estimates for the relevant dispersive equations, see
[
16
,
22
,
23
,
7
,
9
,
10
]
.

The
L^{2}
bilinear restriction estimate is based on the
argument of Wolff
[
27
]
for a cone. His
arguments are roughly composed of two steps.
One is to use induction to avoid some critical case of
the Kakeya set. The other is to deal with the remaining relaxed
Kakeya set by utilizing some geometrical observation as follows:

(1.2)

The union
\Lambda(x_{0})
of all lines passing through a
point
x_{0}
and of direction normal to
\Sigma_{cone}
becomes a cone.

We can see that if a line
\ell

passes through
\Lambda(x_{0})
, then
\ell\cap\Lambda(x_{0})
has at most
O(1)
points, which is the key
to obtain the sharp bilinear cone restriction.

However, in a paraboloid the analogous property does not hold. Specifically, the union

\Lambda(x_{0})
of all lines passing through

x_{0}
and of direction normal to
\Sigma_{parab}
does
not contained in a hypersurface. The reason is that
while the cone has one vanishing principle curvature,
the paraboloid has non-vanishing Gaussian curvature.
Thus,
\ell\cap\Lambda(x_{0})
may have infinitely
many points. Because of this difference, Wolff’s argument
cannot be directly applied to the paraboloid case.

This difficulty was resolved by Tao
[
24
]
who used a kind of orthogonality due to the non-vanishing curvature.
Such an orthogonality was first observed in the proof of the 2-dimensional restriction theorem by Fefferman and Córdoba.
By combining Wolff’s arguments with the orthogonality Tao obtained the
sharp bilinear restriction estimate
\mathds{R}^{*}_{\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}}(2\times 2,p)
,

p>\frac{n+3}{n+1}
for a paraboloid.

It is natural to ask whether the endpoint bilinear estimate
\mathds{R}^{*}_{\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}}(2\times 2,\frac{n+3}{n+1})
is valid or not. Since the Kakeya example does not work in the endpoint
L^{2}
bilinear restriction estimate, we can expect it. Tao
[
23
]
obtained the endpoint bilinear cone restriction estimate by exploring energy concentrations and the geometric observation (
1.2
). If one makes an attempt to prove the endpoint bilinear restriction estimate for a paraboloid, it is reasonable, first of all, to apply Tao’s arguments, but the geometric observation (
1.2
) does not hold for the paraboloid restriction operator. However, it still seems to have the
L^{2}
bilinear paraboloid restriction estimate because the Kakeya example does not work.

In this paper we will prove the endpoint estimate
\mathds{R}^{*}_{\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}}(2\times 2,\frac{n+3}{n+1})
for a paraboloid.
To state more explicitly,
let
\mathit{\Sigma}=\mathit{\Sigma}_{parab}
and

\displaystyle\mathit{\Sigma}_{j}

\displaystyle=\{(\xi,\tau)\in\mathit{\Sigma}:1<|\xi|<2,\ \angle(\xi,(-1)^{j-1}e_{1})<\pi/8\},\qquad j=1,2,

where
e_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a standard unit
vector. We define the operator
\mathcal{U}_{j}

by
\mathcal{U}_{j}f=\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma_{j}}\hat{f}
for
j=1,2
;

(1.3)

\mathcal{U}_{j}f(x,t):=\int e^{2\pi i(x\cdot\xi-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{2})}\widehat{f}(\xi)a_{j}(\xi)d\xi,

where
a_{j}
is a smooth function which is equal to
1
on

\Xi_{j}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:(\xi,\tau)\in\mathit{\Sigma}_{j}\}

and supported in

\tilde{\Xi}_{j}=\{\xi:1/2\leq|\xi|\leq 4,\ \angle(\xi,(-1)^{j-1}e_{1})\leq\pi/4\}.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
\frac{n+3}{n+1}\leq p\leq 2
, the estimate

(1.4)

\|\mathcal{U}_{1}f_{1}\mathcal{U}_{2}f_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{p}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Xi_{1})}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Xi_{2})}

holds for all
f_{1}\in L^{2}(\Xi_{1})
and
f_{2}\in L^{2}(\Xi_{2})
.

To obtain the endpoint we basically follow the arguments in
[
23
]
. We first reprove the sharp bilinear restriction estimate for a paraboloid without dyadic pigeonholing. Next we use an energy concentration argument.
But, as mentioned above, we cannot directly apply Tao’s endpoint argument to the paraboloid problem because of the lack of (
1.2
).
To get around this we will devise a new energy concentration argument where the dispersiveness of
\mathcal{U}_{j}f
and the transversality between
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
play a crucial role instead of the geometric observation (
1.2
).

The Fourier restriction operator can be generalized
to some oscillatory integral operator. The cone
restriction operator is generalized to the oscillatory
integrals satisfying the cinematic curvature condition, and
similarly the paraboloid one
generalized to the oscillatory integrals with
the Carleson–Sjölin condition, see
[
18
,
19
,
14
,
13
,
25
]
.
It was shown by S. Lee
[
14
]
that these
two classes of oscillatory integral operators also satisfy
the estimate (
1.4
) for
p>\frac{n+3}{n+1}
,
provided a suitable transversality condition is assumed, see

[
14
,
13
,
11
,
25
]
. For the oscillatory integrals with the cinematic curvature
condition, the endpoint bilinear estimate
was obtained by the author
[
11
]
. It is likely
that the oscillatory integral operators with the Carleson–Sjölin condition have the endpoint bilinear estimate, too.

\overline{\mathcal{K}}(R)\leq C_{0}^{C}2^{CC_{0}}\leq 2^{C^{\prime}C_{0}}

(
2.6
).

3.
Preliminaries for the bilinear restriction estimate

Let
R\geq 2^{C_{0}}
,
0<c\leq 2^{-C_{0}}
and
\kappa>0
an integer such that

(3.1)

r:=2^{-\kappa}R\sim R^{1/2}.

3.1.
A wave packet decomposition

Let
L=c^{-2}r\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
V_{j}
be a maximal
r^{-1}
-separated
subset of
\tilde{\Xi}_{j}
for
j=1,2
.
For each
(x_{0},v_{j})\in L\times V_{j}
, we define a
tube

T_{j}=T_{j}^{(x_{0},v_{j})}
with initial position
x_{0}
and direction
v_{j}

by

and let
\mathbf{T}_{j}
denote the collection of these tubes. We denote by
x(T_{j})=x_{0}
the position of
T_{j}
and
v(T_{j})=v_{j}
the velocity of
T_{j}
.

Now we decompose
\phi_{j}
into wave packets essentially supported on tubes
T_{j}
.
To partition
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into cubes of sidelength
{c^{-2}r}
,
we set

(3.2)

\eta^{x_{0}}(x):=\eta\Big(\frac{x-x_{0}}{c^{-2}r}\Big).

Then,

(3.3)

\sum_{x_{0}\in L}\eta^{x_{0}}(x)=1.

To partition
\tilde{\Xi}_{j}
, let
B_{v_{j}}
be a neighborhood of
v_{j}\in V_{j}
with pairwise disjoint interiors so that

\tilde{\Xi}_{j}=\bigcup_{v_{j}\in V_{j}}B_{v_{j}}.

For each
\omega\in D(0;1/r)
, we define a map

\Omega_{\omega}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\Omega_{\omega}(v)=v+w
. Let
G
be the set of these maps, and
define
d\Omega
by

\int_{G}F(\Omega)d\Omega=\frac{1}{|D(0;1/r)|}\int_{D(0;1/r)}F(\Omega_{w})dw.

For each
\Omega\in G
and
v_{j}\in V_{j}
, we define the
multiplier
P_{\Omega,v_{j}}
by

\widehat{P_{\Omega,v_{j}}f}=\chi_{\Omega(B_{v_{j}})}\widehat{f},

where
\chi
denotes a characteristic function.
For each
T_{j}=T_{j}^{(x_{0},v_{j})}\in\mathbf{T}_{j}
, we define a function
f_{T_{j}}

by

(3.4)

f_{T_{j}}(x):=\eta^{x_{0}}(x)\int_{G}P_{\Omega,v_{j}}f_{j}(x){d\Omega}.

We define a
wave packet

\phi_{T_{j}}
as

(3.5)

\phi_{T_{j}}:=\mathcal{U}_{j}f_{T_{j}}.

Then by the linearity of Fourier transform,

(3.6)

\phi_{j}(x,t)=\sum_{T_{j}\in\mathbf{T}_{j}}\phi_{T_{j}}(x,t).

Lemma 3.1

(Properties of the wave packets)
.

Suppose that
\phi_{j}
has a Fourier support in
\mathit{\Sigma}_{j}
and a margin

(3.7)

\mathrm{marg}(\phi_{j})\geq Cr^{-1}

for
j=1,2
. Let
T_{j}=T_{j}^{(x_{0},v_{j})}
.
We define a constant

h_{T_{j}}
by

(3.8)

h_{T_{j}}:=r^{n/2}\mathcal{M}\Big(\int_{G}P_{\Omega,v_{j}}f_{j}{d\Omega}\Big)(x_{0}),

where
\mathcal{M}
denotes the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator.
Then we have the followings.

•

The margin of
\phi_{T_{j}}
satisfies

(3.9)

\mathrm{marg}(\phi_{T_{j}})\geq\mathrm{marg}(\phi_{j})-Cr^{-1}.

•

For
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[-CR,CR]
,

(3.10)

|\phi_{T_{j}}(x,t)|\leq C_{M}c^{-C}r^{-n/2}h_{T_{j}}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{dist}(T_{j},(x,t))}{r}\right)^{-M},\qquad\forall M>0.

•

(3.11)

\Big(\sum_{T_{j}\in\mathbf{T}_{j}}h_{T_{j}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim c^{-C}\|f_{j}\|_{2}.

Proof.

Consider (
3.9
). From the definition of
f_{T_{j}}
we can see that the Fourier support of
f_{T_{j}}
is contained in a
O(r^{-1})
-neighborhood of
v_{j}
. So, the spacetime Fourier transform of
\phi_{T_{j}}
is supported in a
O(r^{-1})
-neighborhood of the spacetime Fourier support of
\phi_{j}
. From this we have (
3.9
).

Consider (
3.10
). If
\rho
is a smooth bump function supported on a

O(1)
-neighborhood of the origin and
\rho^{v_{j}}(\xi):=\rho\big(4^{-1}r(\xi-v_{j})\big)
then we may replace
\widehat{f}_{T_{j}}
with
\rho^{v_{j}}\widehat{f}_{T_{j}}
.
By interchanging the integrals we may write

\phi_{T_{j}}(x,t)=\int K_{v_{j}}(x-y,t)f_{T_{j}}(y)dy,

where

(3.12)

K_{v_{j}}(x,t)=\int e^{2\pi i(x\cdot\xi-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{2})}\rho^{v_{j}}(\xi)d\xi.

By integration by parts, if
|t|\lesssim R
then

(3.13)

|K_{v_{j}}(x,t)|\leq C_{M}r^{-n}\left(1+\frac{|x-tv_{j}|}{r}\right)^{-M},\qquad\forall M>0.

Indeed, let
\delta=r^{-1}
and

\Psi(x,t,\xi)=2\pi(x\cdot(\delta\xi+v_{j})-\frac{1}{2}t|\delta\xi+v_{j}|^{2})
. We rewrite as

(3.14)

K_{v_{j}}(x,t)=\delta^{n}\int e^{i\psi(x,t,\xi)}\rho(\xi)d\xi.

Suppose that
|x-tv_{j}|\geq C\delta^{-1}
. Then we have

|\nabla_{\xi}\Psi(x,t,\xi)|\geq C\delta|x-tv_{j}|,

since
|\delta^{2}t\xi|\lesssim 1
. By integration by parts,

|K_{v_{j}}(x,t)|\leq C_{M}\delta^{n}(\delta|x-tv_{j}|)^{-M},\qquad\forall M>0.

On the other hand, we have a trivial estimate

|K_{v_{j}}(x,t)|\lesssim\delta^{n}.

By combining these two estimates we have (
3.13
).
Thus,

\displaystyle|\phi_{T_{j}}

\displaystyle(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}r^{-n}\int\left(1+\frac{|(x-y)-tv_{j}|}{r}\right)^{-M}\eta^{x_{0}}(y)\bigg|\int_{G}P_{\Omega,v_{j}}f_{j}(y){d\Omega}\bigg|dy

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}c^{-C}\left(1+\frac{|(x-x_{0})-tv_{j}|}{r}\right)^{-M}\mathcal{M}\Big(\int_{G}P_{\Omega,v_{j}}f_{j}{d\Omega}\Big)(x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}c^{-C}r^{-n/2}h_{T_{j}}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{dist}(T_{j},(x,t))}{r}\right)^{-M}.

Consider (
3.11
). By the uncertainty principle, if
|x-x_{0}|\lesssim c^{-2}r
then

\mathcal{M}\Big(\int P_{\Omega,v_{j}}f_{j}{d\Omega}\Big)(x_{0})\lesssim c^{-C}\mathcal{M}\Big(\int P_{\Omega,v_{j}}f_{j}{d\Omega}\Big)(x).

Thus,

\sum_{T_{j}\in\mathbf{T}_{j}}h_{T_{j}}^{2}\lesssim c^{-C}\sum_{v_{j}\in V_{j}}\int\Big|\mathcal{M}\Big(\int P_{\Omega,v_{j}}f_{j}{d\Omega}\Big)(x)\Big|^{2}dx.

By the Hardy–Littlewood maximal theorem and Minkowski’s inequality,

\displaystyle\big(\sum_{T_{j}\in\mathbf{T}_{j}}h_{T_{j}}^{2}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim c^{-C}\Big(\sum_{v_{j}\in V_{j}}\Big\|\int P_{\Omega,v_{j}}f_{j}{d\Omega}\Big\|_{2}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq c^{-C}\int\big(\sum_{v_{j}\in V_{j}}\|P_{\Omega,v_{j}}f_{j}\|_{2}^{2}\big)^{1/2}{d\Omega}.

By Plancherel’s theorem and orthogonality,

\sum_{v_{j}\in V_{j}}\|P_{\Omega,v_{j}}f_{j}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\|f_{j}\|_{2}^{2}.

Inserting this into the previous integral we have (
3.11
).
∎

Estimates on a light conic set.

Then

is written as

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Lambda_{j,t}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:(x,t)\in\Lambda_{j}(0)\}
where
\Lambda_{j}(0)
is defined as in (

\big|K_{j,t}(x)\big|\leq C_{M}(1+\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{j,t},x))^{-M},\qquad\forall M>0.

If

\big|K_{j,t}(x)\big|\lesssim 1.

Suppose that

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
Q
be a cube of sidelength
R^{C}
. Let
\epsilon>0
and
j,k=1,2
but
j\neq k
. Let
\pi_{k}\subset\Xi_{k}
be a hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that there is
0\leq\varepsilon_{0}<1
such that for any
v\in\Xi_{j}
and any
w,w^{\prime}\in\pi_{k}
with
w\neq w^{\prime}
,

(3.17)

\Big|\Big\langle\frac{v-w}{|v-w|},\frac{w^{\prime}-w}{|w^{\prime}-w|}\Big\rangle\Big|\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

Then for any

,

\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(Q\cap\Lambda_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r))}\lesssim r^{1/2}E(\phi_{j})^{1/2}.

Proof.

It suffices to show

\int\big\|\chi_{\Lambda^{Q}_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(t)[\mathcal{U}_{j}(t)]f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}dt\lesssim r\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},

where
\Lambda_{\pi_{k}}^{Q}=\Lambda_{\pi_{k}}\cap Q
.
By duality this is equivalent to

(3.18)

\left\|\int\mathcal{U}_{j}(t)^{*}\big(\chi_{\Lambda^{Q}_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(t)g(t)\big)dt\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\lesssim r\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}^{2}.

The left side of the above estimate is written as

\iint\left\langle[\mathcal{U}_{j}(s)\mathcal{U}_{j}(t)^{\ast}]\big(\chi_{\Lambda^{Q}_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(t)g(t)\big),~\chi_{\Lambda^{Q}_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(s)g(s)\right\rangle dtds.

Let

G_{k}(x,t):=\chi_{\Lambda^{Q}_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(x,t)g(x,t)

and

\mathcal{I}_{j}f(s,t):=\left\langle[\mathcal{U}_{j}(s)\mathcal{U}_{j}(t)^{\ast}]f(t),f(s)\right\rangle.

The previous integral is divided into two parts

\iint\mathcal{I}_{j}G_{k}(s,t)dtds=\iint_{|s-t|\leq Cr}\mathcal{I}_{j}G_{k}(s,t)dtds+\iint_{|s-t|\geq Cr}\mathcal{I}_{j}G_{k}(s,t)dtds.

To show (
3.18
) it suffices to prove

(3.19)

\displaystyle\bigg|\iint_{|s-t|\geq Cr}\mathcal{I}_{j}G_{k}(s,t)dtds\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-N}\|g\|_{2}^{2}

and

(3.20)

\displaystyle\bigg|\iint_{|s-t|\leq Cr}\mathcal{I}_{j}G_{k}(s,t)dtds\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim r\|g\|_{2}^{2}.

Consider (
3.19
).
If we set

K_{j,t}(x)=\int e^{2\pi i(x\cdot\xi-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{2})}a_{j}^{2}(\xi)d\xi

then

[\mathcal{U}_{j}(s)\mathcal{U}_{j}(t)^{\ast}]f(x)=\int K_{j,s-t}(x-y)f(y)dy.

We rewrite
\mathcal{I}_{j}G_{k}
as

\mathcal{I}_{j}G_{k}=\iint K_{j,s-t}(x-y)\chi_{\Lambda^{Q}_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(y,t)\chi_{\Lambda^{Q}_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(x,s)g(y,t)\overline{g(x,s)}dxdy.

We divide

K_{j,t}=\chi_{\Lambda_{j,t}(0;r)}K_{j,t}+(1-\chi_{\Lambda_{j,t}(0;r)})K_{j,t}.

From Lemma
3.2
it follows that

(1-\chi_{\Lambda_{j,s-t}(0;r)}(x))|K_{j,s-t}(x)|\lesssim r^{-M},\qquad\forall M>0.

Using this we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\iiiint((1-\chi_{\Lambda_{j,s-t}(0;r)})K_{j,s-t})(x-y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\chi_{\Lambda^{Q}_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(y,t)\chi_{\Lambda^{Q}_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(x,s)g(y,t)\overline{g(x,s)}dxdydtds\bigg|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim r^{-M}\|\chi_{\Lambda^{Q}_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}g\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim r^{-M}R^{C}\|g\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim r^{-M}\|g\|_{2}^{2},\qquad\forall M>0,

where the last line follows from
r\gtrsim R^{\epsilon}
.
Now, to show (
3.19
) it suffices to show

\begin{split}&\iiiint_{|s-t|\geq Cr}\chi_{\Lambda_{j,s-t}(0;r)}(x-y)K_{j,s-t}(x-y)\\
&\qquad\qquad\times\chi_{\Lambda^{Q}_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(y,t)\chi_{\Lambda^{Q}_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(x,s)g(y,t)\overline{g(x,s)}dxdydtds=0.\end{split}

It is enough to show that for
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
with
|s-t|\geq Cr
, the equation

(3.21)

\chi_{\Lambda_{j,s-t}(0;r)}(x-y)\chi_{\Lambda^{Q}_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(y,t)\chi_{\Lambda^{Q}_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(x,s)

vanishes. Consider the contrapositive statement that if
(
3.21
)
is nonzero then one has
|s-t|\lesssim r
.
Suppose that
(
3.21
)
is nonzero. Then, from the characteristic function
\chi_{\Lambda_{j,s-t}(0;r)}(x-y)
we can restrict ourselves to the case

(3.22)

x-y=(s-t)v+{O}(r)

for some
v\in\Xi_{j}
.
On the other hands,
from

\chi_{\Lambda_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(y,t)
and

\chi_{\Lambda_{\pi_{k}}(z_{0};r)}(x,s)
we also have

(3.23)

\begin{split}x-x_{0}&=(s-t_{0})w+{O}(r)\\
y-x_{0}&=(t-t_{0})w^{\prime}+{O}(r)\end{split}

for some
w,w^{\prime}\in\pi_{k}
.
By combining (
3.22
) and (
3.23
) it follows that

(3.24)

(s-t)(v-w)+(t-t_{0})(w^{\prime}-w)=O(r).

If
w=w^{\prime}
, then we have
|s-t|\lesssim r
. Otherwise, from
(
3.17
) we can see that there exists a unit vector
u
such
that
(v-w)\cdot u\neq 0
but
(w^{\prime}-w)\cdot u=0
. By taking inner
product with such
u
for (

|s-t|\lesssim r
.

Consider (
3.20
).
By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Plancherel’s theorem it follows that

|\mathcal{I}_{j}f(s,t)|\lesssim\|f(s)\|_{2}\|f(t)\|_{2}.

By this and the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality,

\displaystyle\bigg|\iint_{|s-t|\lesssim r}\mathcal{I}_{j}G_{k}(s,t)dtds\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\iint_{|s-t|\lesssim r}\|g(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}dtds

\displaystyle\lesssim r\|g\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus we have (
3.20
).
∎

3.3.
A basic bilinear restriction estimate

Let
\psi
be a nonnegative Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\int\psi=1
such that
\widehat{\psi}
is supported in the unit ball and

(3.25)

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}}\psi^{4}(z-k)=1

where
z=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
If
q
is a cube of sidelength
r_{q}
with center
z_{q}
, we define

\psi_{q}(z):=\psi(r_{q}^{-1}(z-z_{q})).

For convenience, we use the notations
\int_{\psi}\cdot
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\psi)}
to denote

\int_{\psi}f:=\int f\psi\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|f\|_{L^{2}(\psi)}:=\|f\psi\|_{2}.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
q
be a cube of side-length
R^{1/2}
.
Suppose that
\phi_{T_{1}}
and
\phi_{T_{2}}
are wave packets defined as (
3.5
).
Then

(3.26)

\|\phi_{T_{1}}\phi_{T_{2}}\|_{L^{2}({\psi_{q}^{2}})}\lesssim R^{-\frac{n+1}{4}}\|\phi_{T_{1}}\|_{L^{2}({\psi_{q}})}\|\phi_{T_{2}}\|_{L^{2}({\psi_{q}})}.

Proof.

By Plancherel’s theorem the estimate (
3.26
) is equivalent to

\|\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{1}}}\ast\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{2}}}\|_{2}\lesssim R^{-\frac{n+1}{4}}\|\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{1}}}\|_{2}\|\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{2}}}\|_{2}.

By interpolation it suffices to show the following two estimates:

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{1}}}\ast\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{2}}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{1}}}\|_{1}\|\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{2}}}\|_{1},

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{1}}}\ast\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{2}}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}\|\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{1}}}\|_{\infty}\|\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{2}}}\|_{\infty}.

By Young’s inequality the first one is easily obtained.
Consider the second one. Observe that the Fourier support of
\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{j}}
is contained in a ball
B(v(T_{j}),-{|v(T_{j})|^{2}}/{2};CR^{-1/2})
.
Let
\chi_{j}:=\chi_{B(v(T_{j}),-{|v(T_{j})|^{2}}/{2};CR^{-1/2})}
be a characteristic function. Then,

\|\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{1}}}\ast\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{2}}}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|\chi_{1}\ast\chi_{2}\|_{\infty}\|\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{1}}}\|_{\infty}\|\widehat{\psi_{q}\phi_{T_{2}}}\|_{\infty}.

Simple computation gives

\|\chi_{1}\ast\chi_{2}\|_{\infty}\lesssim R^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}
. Thus we have the second estimate.
∎

We begin with the following averaging lemma.

(Lemma 6.1 in

\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}m_{q,T_{j}}\leq 1

T_{j}\in\mathbf{T}_{j}
.
Then,

(5.3)

\bigg(\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}E\Big(\sum_{T_{j}\in\mathbf{T}_{j}}m_{q,T_{j}}\phi_{T_{j}}\Big)\bigg)^{1/2}\leq(1+Cc)E(\phi_{j})^{1/2}.

Proof.

To get the constant
(1+Cc)
, we need to consider the Fourier support
of
f_{T_{j}}
. Let

Y_{j}=\bigcup_{v_{j}\in V_{j}}\{\xi\in B_{v_{j}}:\mathrm{dist}(\xi,\tilde{\Xi}_{j}\setminus B_{v_{j}})>Cc^{2}R^{-1/2}\}.

We define the operator
P_{\Omega(Y)}
by

\widehat{P_{\Omega(Y)}f}=\chi_{\Omega(Y)}\widehat{f}.

By Minkowski’s inequality,

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}E\big(\sum_{T_{j}\in\mathbf{T}_{j}}m_{q,T_{j}}\phi_{T_{j}}\big)\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\Big\|\sum_{T_{j}\in\mathbf{T}_{j}}m_{q,T_{j}}\phi_{T_{j}}(0)\Big\|_{2}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\Big\|\sum_{T_{j}\in\mathbf{T}_{j}}m_{q,T_{j}}\int_{G}\eta^{x(T_{j})}P_{\Omega,v(T_{j})}\phi_{j}(0)d\Omega\Big\|_{2}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}\bigg(\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\Big\|\sum_{T_{j}\in\mathbf{T}_{j}}m_{q,T_{j}}\eta^{x(T_{j})}P_{\Omega,v(T_{j})}\phi_{j}(0)\Big\|_{2}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}{d\Omega},

which is less than or equal to the sum of

(5.4)

\int\bigg(\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\Big\|\sum_{T_{j}\in\mathbf{T}_{j}}m_{q,T_{j}}\eta^{x(T_{j})}P_{\Omega,v(T_{j})}P_{\Omega(Y_{j})}\phi_{j}(0)\Big\|_{2}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}{d\Omega}

and

(5.5)

\int\bigg(\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\Big\|\sum_{T_{j}\in\mathbf{T}_{j}}m_{q,T_{j}}\eta^{x(T_{j})}P_{\Omega,v(T_{j})}(1-P_{\Omega(Y_{j})})\phi_{j}(0)\Big\|_{2}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}{d\Omega}.

To prove (
5.3
), it suffices to show that

\eqref{B'v}\leq E(\phi_{j})^{1/2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\eqref{Bv}\leq CcE(\phi_{j})^{1/2}.

Consider (
5.4
). From (
3.2
) we see that the Fourier transform of
\eta^{x_{0}}
is supported in
D(0;c^{2}R^{-1/2})
. So, the Fourier support of

\eta^{x_{0}}P_{\Omega,v_{j}}P_{\Omega(Y_{j})}f
is contained in
B_{v_{j}}
.
By orthogonality,
(
5.4
) is bounded by

\int\bigg(\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{v_{j}\in V_{j}}\Big\|\sum_{x_{0}\in L}m_{q,T_{j}^{(x_{0},v_{j})}}\eta^{x_{0}}P_{\Omega,v_{j}}P_{\Omega(Y_{j})}\phi_{j}(0)\Big\|_{2}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}{d\Omega}.

By rearranging, it is equal to

\int\bigg(\sum_{v_{j}\in V_{j}}\int|P_{\Omega,v_{j}}P_{\Omega(Y_{j})}\phi_{j}(x,0)|^{2}\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\bigg|\sum_{x_{0}\in L}{m_{q,T_{j}^{(x_{0},v_{j})}}}\eta^{x_{0}}(x)\bigg|^{2}dx\bigg)^{1/2}{d\Omega},

which is bounded by

\int\bigg(\sum_{v_{j}\in V_{j}}\int|P_{\Omega,v_{j}}P_{\Omega(Y_{j})}\phi_{j}(x,0)|^{2}\bigg|\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{x_{0}\in L}{m_{q,T_{j}^{(x_{0},v_{j})}}}\eta^{x_{0}}(x)\bigg|^{2}dx\bigg)^{1/2}{d\Omega}.

By (
3.3
) and (
5.2
), this is bounded by

\int\bigg(\sum_{v_{j}\in V_{j}}\int|P_{\Omega,v_{j}}P_{\Omega(Y_{j})}\phi_{j}(x,0)|^{2}dx\bigg)^{1/2}{d\Omega}.

By orthogonality, the above is bounded by

\int\|P_{\Omega(Y_{j})}\phi_{j}(0)\|_{2}{d\Omega}.

Since
\|P_{\Omega(Y_{j})}\phi_{j}(0)\|_{2}\leq E(\phi_{j})^{1/2}
, we have that
\eqref{B'v}\leq E(\phi_{j})^{1/2}
.

Consider (
5.5
). Apply the previous arguments but using
almost orthogonality instead of orthogonality. Then we have

\eqref{Bv}\lesssim\int\|(1-P_{\Omega(Y_{j})})\phi_{j}(0)\|_{2}{d\Omega}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality this is bounded by

\Big(\int\|(1-P_{\Omega(Y_{j})})\phi_{j}(0)\|_{2}^{2}{d\Omega}\Big)^{1/2}.

By Plancherel’s theorem and rearranging the integrals, this is equal
to

\Big(\int\|(1-\chi_{\Omega(Y_{j})})\widehat{\phi_{j}(0)}\|_{2}^{2}{d\Omega}\Big)^{1/2}=\Big(\int\Big(\int(1-\chi_{\Omega(Y_{j})})(\xi){d\Omega}\Big)|\widehat{\phi_{j}(0)}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\Big)^{1/2}.

By a direct calculation we have that for any
\xi\in\tilde{\Xi}_{j}
,

\displaystyle\int 1-\chi_{\Omega(Y_{j})}(\xi){d\Omega}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|D(0;CR^{-1/2})|}\int_{D(0;CR^{-1/2})}1-\chi_{Y_{j}}(\xi+w)dw

\displaystyle\lesssim c^{2}.

Inserting this into the previous, we obtain that
\eqref{Bv}\lesssim c^{2}E(\phi_{j})
.

∎

6.
A localization operator

In this section we introduce a localization operator and state some relevant basic estimates. When exploiting energy concentrations, the localization operator is used as a tool.

By (
2.2
) we have

\widehat{\phi_{j}(t)}(\xi)=e^{-\pi i(t-t_{0})|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{\phi_{j}(t_{0})}(\xi),
which is written as

(6.1)

\phi_{j}(t)=\mathcal{U}_{j}[\phi_{j}(t_{0})](t-t_{0}).

Definition 6.1
.

Let
D=D(x_{D},t_{D};r)
be a disc. We define an operator
P_{D}\phi_{j}
by

(6.2)

P_{D}\phi_{j}(t)=\mathcal{U}_{j}[(\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}})\phi_{j}(t_{D})](t-t_{D})

where
\eta_{r}
is defined as (
1.8
).

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
r\geq C_{0}
,
D=D(x_{D},t_{D};r)
and

D^{\pm}:=D(x_{D},t_{D};r(1\pm r^{-1/2N})).

Suppose that
\phi_{j}

satisfies that
\mathrm{marg}(\phi_{j})\geq C_{0}r^{-1+1/N}

for
j=1,2
. Then,

(6.3)

\mathrm{marg}(P_{D}\phi_{j})\geq\mathrm{marg}(\phi_{j})-C_{0}r^{-1+1/N}

and

(6.4)

\displaystyle\|P_{D}\phi_{j}(t_{D})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D^{+})}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-N}E(\phi_{j})^{1/2},

(6.5)

\displaystyle\|(1-P_{D})\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(D^{-})}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-N}E(\phi_{j})^{1/2},

(6.6)

\displaystyle E(P_{D}\phi_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(D^{+})}^{2}+Cr^{-N}E(\phi_{j}),

(6.7)

\displaystyle E((1-P_{D})\phi_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{j}(t_{D})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D^{-})}+Cr^{-N}E(\phi_{j}).

Proof.

Consider (
6.3
). Observe that the size of
\supp\widehat{\phi}_{j}
is comparable to that of
\supp\widehat{\phi_{j}(0)}
. Similarly, the size of
\supp\widehat{P_{D}\phi_{j}}
and
\supp\widehat{P_{D}\phi_{j}(t_{D})}
is comparable.
From (
6.2
) we have

(6.8)

\widehat{P_{D}\phi_{j}(t_{D})}=(\widehat{\chi_{D}}\widehat{\eta_{r^{1-1/N}}})\ast\widehat{\phi_{j}(t_{D})}.

Since
\widehat{\eta_{r^{1-1/N}}}(\xi)=\widehat{\eta}(r^{1-1/N}\xi)
is supported on
D(0;r^{-1+1/N})
, the Fourier support of
P_{D}\phi_{j}(t_{D})
is expanded
O(r^{-1+1/N})
more than that of
\phi_{j}(t_{D})
.
Thus we have (
6.3
).

We have that

\frac{1}{2}D\subset D^{-}\subset D\subset D^{+}\subset 2D.

From this relation it follows that

\displaystyle 0\leq\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}}\leq 1,

(6.9)

\displaystyle\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}}(x)\lesssim r^{-N}\quad\text{ for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D^{+}$},

(6.10)

\displaystyle 1-\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}}(x)\lesssim r^{-N}\quad\text{ for $x\in D^{-}$ }.

Indeed, the first one is trivial. Consider (
6.9
).
We have that

(6.11)

\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}}(x)\lesssim\bigg(1+\frac{\mathrm{dist}(x,D)}{r^{1-1/N}}\bigg)^{-M},\qquad\forall M>0.

If
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D^{+}
then

\mathrm{dist}(x,D)\geq r(1+r^{-1/2N})-r=r^{1-1/2N}.

By inserting this into the previous inequality we can
obtain (
6.9
).

Consider (
6.10
). We have that

(6.12)

1-\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}}(x)\lesssim\bigg(1+\frac{\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D)}{r^{1-1/N}}\bigg)^{-M},\qquad\forall M>0.

If
x\in D^{-}
, then

\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D)\geq r-r(1-r^{-1/2N})=r^{1-1/2N}.

Thus we have (
6.10
).

Now consider from (
6.4
) to (
6.7
).
By (
6.2
) and (
6.9
) it follows that

\|P_{D}\phi_{j}(t_{D})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D^{+})}=\|(\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}})\phi_{j}(t_{D})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D^{+})}\lesssim r^{-N}E(\phi_{j})^{1/2}.

So we have (
6.4
). Similar arguments also give (
6.5
).

From (
6.4
) it follows that

\displaystyle E(P_{D}\phi_{j})

\displaystyle=\|P_{D}\phi_{j}(t_{D})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{D}\phi_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D^{+})}+Cr^{-N}E(\phi_{j})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D^{+})}+Cr^{-N}E(\phi_{j})^{1/2}.

So we have (
6.6
). Analogously we have (
6.7
).
∎

Now we consider some properties of
P_{D}\phi_{j}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
r\geq C_{0}
and
D=D(x_{D},t_{D};r)
. Suppose that

\phi_{j}
satisfies that
\mathrm{marg}(\phi_{j})\geq C_{0}r^{-1+1/N}
. Then,

(6.13)

\displaystyle|P_{D}\phi_{j}(x,t)

\displaystyle|\leq C_{M}r^{n/2}\bigg(1+\frac{\mathrm{dist}\big((x,t),\Lambda_{j}(x_{D},t_{D};r)\big)}{r^{1-1/N}}\bigg)^{-M}E(\phi_{j})^{1/2},\qquad\forall M>0

and

(6.14)

\displaystyle\|(1-P_{D})\phi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q(x_{D},t_{D};r/4))}\lesssim r^{-N}E(\phi_{j})^{1/2}.

Proof.

Consider (
6.13
). By (
6.2
) and
(
3.15
),

P_{D}\phi_{j}(x,t)=\int K_{j,t-t_{D}}(x-y)(\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}})(y)\phi_{j}(y,t_{D})dy.

By Lemma
3.2
and (
6.11
),

|K_{j,t-t_{D}}(x-y)(\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}})^{1/2}(y)|\leq C_{M}\bigg(1+\frac{\mathrm{dist}\big((x,t),\Lambda_{j}(x_{D},t_{D};r)\big)}{r^{1-1/N}}\bigg)^{-M}

for any
M>0
.
Thus,

\displaystyle|P_{D}\phi_{j}(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}\bigg(1+\frac{\mathrm{dist}\big((x,t),\Lambda_{j}(x_{D},t_{D};r)\big)}{r^{1-1/N}}\bigg)^{-M}\int(\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}})^{1/2}(y)|\phi_{j}(y,t_{D})|dy

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}r^{n/2}\bigg(1+\frac{\mathrm{dist}\big((x,t),\Lambda_{j}(x_{D},t_{D};r)\big)}{r^{1-1/N}}\bigg)^{-M}E(\phi_{j})^{1/2},

where the last line follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz
inequality.

Consider (
6.14
). Similarly, by (
6.2
) and
(
3.15
), it is written as

(1-P_{D})\phi_{j}(x,t)=\int K_{j,t-t_{D}}(x-y)(1-\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}})(y)\phi_{j}(y,t_{D})dy.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

(6.15)

|(1-P_{D})\phi_{j}(x,t)|\leq\bigg(\int\big|K_{j,t-t_{D}}(x-y)(1-\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}})(y)\big|^{2}dy\bigg)^{1/2}E(\phi_{j})^{1/2}.

By Lemma
3.2
and (
6.12
) we have that
for
(x,t)\in Q(x_{D},t_{D};r/4)
,

\displaystyle\int\big|K_{j,t-t_{D}}(x-y)(1-\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}})(y)\big|^{2}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-M}\int|K_{j,t-t_{D}}(x-y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-M}

for any
M>0
.
Substituting this in (
6.15
) we can obtain
(
6.14
).

∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
r\geq C_{0}
and
D=D(x_{D},t_{D};r)
.
Then, for each
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
there is a disc
\mathcal{G}_{j,t_{0}}(D)
of radius
A_{w}|t_{D}-t_{0}|/2+4r
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{t_{0}\}
such that

\mathcal{G}_{j,t_{0}}(D)
contains
\Lambda_{j}(x_{D},t_{D};r)\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{t_{0}\})
and

E(P_{D}\phi_{j})\leq\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{j,t_{0}}(D))}+Cr^{-N}E(\phi_{j}).

Proof.

By (
6.1
) and (
6.2
),

\displaystyle P_{D}\phi_{j}(x,t_{D})

\displaystyle=(\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}})(x)\mathcal{U}_{j}[\phi_{j}(t_{0})](x,t_{D}-t_{0})

(6.16)

\displaystyle=\int(\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}})(x)K_{j,t_{D}-t_{0}}(x-y)\phi_{j}(y,t_{0})dy.

If we ignore Schwarz tails, the equation
K_{j,t_{D}-t_{0}}(x-y)
implies that
x-y
is contained in
\Lambda_{j,t_{D}-t_{0}}
by Lemma
3.2
. The
\Lambda_{j,t_{D}-t_{0}}
is contained in a disc of radius
A_{w}|t_{D}-t_{0}|/2+C
, so
x-y
is contained in a disc of radius
A_{w}|t_{D}-t_{0}|/2+C
. Since
x
is contained in
D
, we see that that
y
is contained in a disc
D_{j}^{\star}:=D(x_{j},t_{0};A_{w}|t_{D}-t_{0}|/2+2r)
for some
x_{j}
.
Using the symmetric property of
\Lambda_{j}
about the origin, we also have that
y-x
is contained in
\Lambda_{j,t_{0}-t_{D}}
, which implies
(y,t_{0})\in\Lambda_{j}(x,t_{D})
. So, we can see that
D_{j}^{\star}
contains
\Lambda_{j}(x_{D},t_{D};r)\cap\mathbb{(}R^{n}\times\{t_{0}\})
.
Using this observation we have

|(\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}})^{1/2}(x)K_{j,t_{D}-t_{0}}(x-y)|\leq C_{M}\bigg(1+\frac{\mathrm{dist}(y,D_{j}^{\star})}{r^{1-1/N}}\bigg)^{-M},\quad\forall M>0.

Let
\tilde{D}_{j}^{\star}:=D(x_{j},t_{0};A_{w}|t_{D}-t_{0}|/2+2r(1+r^{-1/2N}))
.
By the above estimate,

|(\chi_{D}\ast\eta_{r^{1-1/N}})^{1/2}(x)K_{j,t_{D}-t_{0}}(x-y)(1-\chi_{\tilde{D}_{j}^{\star}}(y))|\leq C_{M}r^{-M},\quad\forall M>0.

We divide
\phi_{j}(t_{0})=\chi_{\tilde{D}_{j}^{\star}}\phi_{j}(t_{0})+(1-\chi_{\tilde{D}_{j}^{\star}})\phi_{j}(t_{0})
and insert it into (
6.16
). Then,

\displaystyle\|P_{D}\phi_{j}(t_{D})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{U}_{j}[\chi_{\tilde{D}_{j}^{\star}}\phi_{j}(t_{0})](t_{D}-t_{0})\|_{2}+Cr^{-N}E(\phi_{j})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\chi_{\tilde{D}_{j}^{\star}}\phi_{j}(t_{0})\|_{2}+Cr^{-N}E(\phi_{j})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(D(x_{j},t_{0};A_{w}|t_{D}-t_{0}|/2+4r))}+Cr^{-N}E(\phi_{j})^{1/2}.

If we take
\mathcal{G}_{j,t_{0}}(D):=D(x_{j},t_{0};A_{w}|t_{D}-t_{0}|/2+4r)
then we have the desired estimate. Since
D_{j}^{\star}
contains
\Lambda(x_{D},t_{D};r)\cap\mathbb{(}R^{n}\times\{t_{0}\})
, the
\mathcal{G}_{j,t_{0}}(D)
also contains
\Lambda_{j}(x_{D},t_{D};r)\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{t_{0}\})
.
∎

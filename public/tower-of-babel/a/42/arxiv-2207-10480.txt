A micropolar shell model for hard-magnetic soft materials Journal: JOURNAL
Farzam Dadgar-Rad, Mokarram Hossain

Abstract
Hard-magnetic soft materials (HMSMs) are particulate composites that particles with high coercivity are dispersed in a soft matrix. Since applying the magnetic loading induces a body couple in HMSMs, the resulting Cauchy stress is predicted to be asymmetric. Therefore, the micropolar continuum theory can be employed to capture the deformation of these materials. On the other hand, the geometries and structures made of HMSMs often possess small thickness compared to the overall dimensions of the body. Accordingly, in the present contribution, a 10-parameter micropolar shell formulation to model the finite elastic deformation of thin hard-magnetic soft structures under magnetic stimuli is developed. The proposed shell formulation allows for using three-dimensional constitutive laws without any need for modification to apply the plane stress assumption in thin structures. A nonlinear finite element formulation is also presented for the numerical solution of the governing equations. To alleviate the locking phenomenon, the enhanced assumed strain method is employed. Several examples are presented that demonstrate the performance and effectiveness of the proposed formulation.

Introduction

Magneto-active soft materials consist of magnetic particles dispersed into a soft elastomeric matrix and undergo large deformations under magnetic loading.
This class of materials has been used in vibration absorbers, sensors, actuators, soft robots, flexible electronics, and isolators (see, e.g.,

Therefore, developing reliable theoretical models plays an essential role in the optimum and cost-effective design of the aforementioned devices and instruments.

Based on the type of the embedded particles, magneto-active soft materials are divided into two sub-classes, namely
soft-magnetic soft materials
(SMSMs) and
hard-magnetic soft materials
(HMSMs).
The former contains particles with low coercivity, such as iron or iron oxides, and their magnetization vector varies under external magnetic loading.
This sub-class has been the subject of a huge amount of research work in this century (e.g.,
Saxena2013
,
Miehe2016
,
Mehnert2017
,
Mukherjee2020
,
Bustamante2021
,
Hu2022
,

The latter sub-class is composed of particles of high coercivity, such as CoFe
2
O
4
or NdFeB, so that their magnetization vector, or equivalently, their remnant magnetic flux, remains unchanged for a wide range of the applied external magnetic flux (e.g.,
Schumann2020
,
Lee2020
).
One of the main characteristics of HMSMs is that external magnetic induction of relatively small magnitude causes rapid finite deformations in these materials (e.g.,
Lum2016
,
Wu2019
).
Moreover, the 3D printing technologies have enabled the researchers to program the ferromagnetic domains in complex structures, which leads to the desired deformations

,
Alapan2020
,

,
Wang2021

3
Basic relations of HMSMs

The main property of hard-magnetic soft materials is the existence of a remnant magnetic flux density, that remains almost unchanged under a wide range of the applied external magnetic flux
\mathbbm{B}^{\text{ext}}
(e.g.,
Lum2016
,
Wu2019
,
Zhao2019
).
Let
\tilde{\mathbbm{B}}^{\text{rem}}
and
{\mathbbm{B}}^{\text{rem}}
be the remnant magnetic flux in the reference and current configurations, respectively.
The relation between
\tilde{\mathbbm{B}}^{\text{rem}}
and
{\mathbbm{B}}^{\text{rem}}
is as follows

{\mathbbm{B}}^{\text{rem}}=J^{-1}\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathbbm{B}}^{\text{rem}},\quad B^{\text{rem}}_{i}=J^{-1}F_{iJ}\tilde{B}^{\text{rem}}_{J}.

(10)

The action of
\mathbbm{B}^{\text{ext}}
on
{\mathbbm{B}}^{\text{rem}}
leads to a body couple (moment per unit volume) in HMSMs.
The relations for the body couple per unit current volume
\mathbbm{p}
, and the body couple per unit reference volume

may be written as (e.g.,
Zhao2019
,
Dorfmann2014
)

\mathbbm{p}^{\ast}=J\mathbbm{p}=\frac{J}{\mu_{0}}{\mathbbm{B}}^{\text{rem}}\times\mathbbm{B}^{\text{ext}}=\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\big(\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathbbm{B}}^{\text{rem}}\big)\times\mathbbm{B}^{\text{ext}},

(11)

where the constant
\mu_{0}=4\pi\times 10^{-7}\frac{N}{A^{2}}
is the free space magnetic permeability.
For HMSMs, the external magnetic flux density
\mathbbm{B}^{\text{ext}}
is often assumed to remain constant in space (e.g., Refs.
Zhao2019
,
Wang2020
,
Chen2020a
,
Chen2021
,
Rajan2021
).
Using this point, it has been proven that the following Maxwell equations are satisfied in HMSMs (e.g.,
Zhao2019
,
Dorfmann2014
):

\text{Div}\mathbbm{B}=B_{I,I}=0,\quad\text{Curl}\mathbbm{H}=\epsilon_{IJK}H_{J,K}\mathbbm{E}_{I}={\bf 0},

(12)

where
\mathbbm{B}
is the referential magnetic flux density,

\mathbbm{H}
is the referential magnetic field,

\mathbbm{E}_{I}
is the basis vector of the referential coordinate system
\{X_{I}\}
defined in the previous section
, and "
Curl
" is the referential curl operator.

\Delta\boldsymbol{\alpha}\end{Bmatrix}=-\begin{Bmatrix}\mathbbm{F}_{\text{int}}^{v}-\mathbbm{F}_{\text{ext}}^{v}\\
\mathbbm{F}_{\text{int}}^{\alpha}\end{Bmatrix},\end{split}

(48)

where the subscripts "mat", "geo", and "load", represent the material, geometric, and load part of the element stiffness matrix.
In particular, the material sub-matrices
\mathbbm{K}^{vv}_{\text{mat}}
,

\mathbbm{K}^{v\alpha}_{\text{mat}}
, and

\mathbbm{K}^{\alpha\alpha}_{\text{mat}}
in Eq. (

) are as follows:

\left.\begin{split}\mathbbm{K}^{vv}_{\text{mat}}=\int_{V_{0}^{\mathfrak{e}}}[\tilde{\mathbbm{B}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mskip-1.0mu\top\mskip-2.0mu}(\mathbbm{C}^{[1]}\tilde{\mathbbm{B}}+\mathbbm{C}^{[3]}\mathbbm{B}^{[2]})+\mathbbm{B}^{[2]\scriptscriptstyle\mskip-1.0mu\top\mskip-2.0mu}(\mathbbm{C}^{[2]}\mathbbm{B}^{[2]}+\mathbbm{C}^{[4]}\tilde{\mathbbm{B}})]\text{d}\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\mathfrak{e}}\\
\mathbbm{K}^{v\alpha}_{\text{mat}}=\int_{V_{0}^{\mathfrak{e}}}(\tilde{\mathbbm{B}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mskip-1.0mu\top\mskip-2.0mu}\mathbbm{C}^{[1]}+\mathbbm{B}^{[2]\scriptscriptstyle\mskip-1.0mu\top\mskip-2.0mu}\mathbbm{C}^{[4]})\bar{\mathbbm{B}}\text{d}\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\mathfrak{e}},\quad\mathbbm{K}^{\alpha\alpha}_{\text{mat}}=\int_{V_{0}^{\mathfrak{e}}}\bar{\mathbbm{B}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mskip-1.0mu\top\mskip-2.0mu}\mathbbm{C}^{[1]}\bar{\mathbbm{B}}\text{d}\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\mathfrak{e}}\end{split}\right\},

(49)

where
\mathbbm{C}^{[\mathcal{J}]}
are the matrix forms of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}^{[\mathcal{J}]}
.
Moreover, the load sub-matrices
\mathbbm{K}^{vv}_{\text{load}}
,

\mathbbm{K}^{v\alpha}_{\text{load}}
are given by

\left.\begin{split}\mathbbm{K}^{vv}_{\text{load}}=\int_{V_{0}^{\mathfrak{e}}}\epsilon_{ijk}Q^{-1}_{JN}\tilde{B}^{\text{rem}}_{N}B^{\text{ext}}_{j}\mathbbm{y}_{k}(\mathbbm{b}^{[0]}_{iJ}+z\mathbbm{b}^{[1]}_{iJ})\text{d}\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\mathfrak{e}}\\
\mathbbm{K}^{v\alpha}_{\text{load}}=\int_{V_{0}^{\mathfrak{e}}}\epsilon_{ijk}Q^{-1}_{JN}\tilde{B}^{\text{rem}}_{N}B^{\text{ext}}_{j}\mathbbm{y}_{k}\bar{\mathbbm{b}}_{iJ}\text{d}\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\mathfrak{e}}\end{split}\right\},

(50)

where
\mathbbm{y}_{k}=\{{\bf 0}_{1\times 3(n^{u}+n^{w})},{\bf 0}_{1\times(k-1)n^{\theta}},\mathbbm{N}_{\theta},{\bf 0}_{1\times(3-k)n^{\theta}},{\bf 0}_{1\times n^{\phi}}\}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mskip-1.0mu\top\mskip-2.0mu}
, with
k\in\{1,2,3\}
, is a column vector whose nonzero entry is
\mathbbm{N}_{\theta}
.
The expressions for the geometric sub-matrices, resulting from the terms

\mathbb{P}^{[0]}\mskip 1.75mu\raisebox{1.07639pt}{:}\mskip 2.0mu\Delta\delta\mathbb{H}^{[1]}
and
\mathbb{M}^{[0]}\mskip 1.75mu\raisebox{1.07639pt}{:}\mskip 2.0mu\Delta\delta\mathbb{H}^{[2]}
in Eq. (
30
), are too lengthy and are not presented here.
The assembled system of equations is of the form

\tilde{\mathbbm{K}}\Delta\tilde{\mathbbm{V}}=-\tilde{\mathbbm{R}}
, where

\tilde{\mathbbm{K}}
,
\Delta\tilde{\mathbbm{V}}
, and
\tilde{\mathbbm{R}}
are the assembled forms of the stiffness matrix, incremental generalized displacement, and residual vector, respectively.
After finding
\Delta\tilde{\mathbbm{V}}
, the non-rotational quantities are update via the relations
\mathbbm{u}+\Delta\mathbbm{u}\rightarrow\mathbbm{u}
,

\mathbbm{w}+\Delta\mathbbm{w}\rightarrow\mathbbm{w}
,
and
\phi+\Delta\phi\rightarrow\phi
.
However, the update procedure for the rotation pseudo-vector is completely different.
Let
\Delta\boldsymbol{\theta}
be the increment of the rotation pseudo-vector.
The updated rotation pseudo-vector
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\ast}_{\text{updated}}
resulting from the two subsequent rotations
\boldsymbol{\theta}
and
\Delta\boldsymbol{\theta}
is then calculated via the following relations
Argyris1982
:

{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\ast}_{\text{updated}}=\frac{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\ast}+\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\ast}+({\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}^{\ast}){\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\ast}}{1-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\ast}\cdot{\Delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\ast}},

(51)

where
{\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}^{\ast}=-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\ast}
and
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\ast}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}\tan\frac{\theta}{2}
.
Moreover,
\boldsymbol{\alpha}={\boldsymbol{\theta}}/{\theta}
is the unit vector along
\boldsymbol{\theta}
.
The proof of Eq. (
51
) is lengthy and is available in, e.g., Argyris
Argyris1982
.

8
Summary

In this research, a 10-parameter micropolar shell model for simulating the finite elastic deformation of thin hard-magnetic soft structures was formulated.
The idea of employing the micropolar theory comes from the fact that magnetic stimulation induces a body couple on these materials, which in turn leads to asymmetric Cauchy stress tensor.
Since the governing equations at finite strains, including magnetic effects, cannot be solved analytically, a nonlinear finite element formulation for simulating the problems of arbitrary thin geometry, boundary conditions, and loading cases was also presented.
Six different numerical examples were solved to assess the applicability of the present formulation.
It was shown that the results of the proposed formulation are in good agreement with the available experimental and numerical ones.
The viscoelastic and thermal effects will be taken into account in the forthcoming contributions.

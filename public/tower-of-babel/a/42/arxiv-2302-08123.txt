On the limit of the positive ℓ \ell -degree Turán problem
Oleg Pikhurko Mathematics Institute and DIMAP University of Warwick Coventry CV4 7AL, UK

Abstract
The minimum positive ℓ \ell -degree δ ℓ + ​ ( G ) \delta^{+}_{\ell}(G) of a non-empty k k -graph G G is the maximum m m such that every ℓ \ell -subset of V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) is contained in either none or at least m m edges of G G ; let δ ℓ + ​ ( G ) := 0 \delta^{+}_{\ell}(G):=0 if G G has no edges. For a family ℱ {\mathcal{F}} of k k -graphs, let co + ​ ex ℓ ​ ( n , ℱ ) \mathrm{co^{+}ex}_{\ell}(n,{\mathcal{F}}) be the maximum of δ ℓ + ​ ( G ) \delta^{+}_{\ell}(G) over all ℱ {\mathcal{F}} -free k k -graphs G G on n n vertices. We prove that the ratio co + ​ ex ℓ ​ ( n , ℱ ) / ( n − ℓ k − ℓ ) \mathrm{co^{+}ex}_{\ell}(n,{\mathcal{F}})/{n-\ell\choose k-\ell} tends to limit as n → ∞ n\to\infty , answering a question of Halfpap, Lemons and Palmer. Also, we show that the limit can be obtained as the value of a natural optimisation problem for k k -hypergraphons; in fact, we give an alternative description of the set of possible accumulation points of almost extremal k k -graphs.

Introduction

A
k
-graph

G

(V(G),E(G))
, where
V(G)
is the
vertex set
of
G
and

is a collection of
k

, called

. We call

if
E(G)\not=\emptyset
.

Fix an integer
\ell

0\leqslant\ell\leqslant k-1
. The
minimum positive
\ell
-degree
of a non-empty
k
-graph

\delta^{+}_{\ell}(G)

the maximum
m

\ell
-subset
L
of

m

G

G

\delta^{+}_{\ell}(G):=0
.
For a family

of
k
-graphs, the

\ell
-degree Turán problem
is to determine
\mathrm{co^{+}ex}_{\ell}(n,{\mathcal{F}})

\delta^{+}_{\ell}(G)
over all
{\mathcal{F}}
-free
k
-graphs
G
on
n
vertices. Let

\gamma_{\ell}^{+}({\mathcal{F}}):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{co^{+}ex}_{\ell}(n,{\mathcal{F}})}{{n-\ell\choose k-\ell}}.

(1)

If
\ell=0
, then
\mathrm{co^{+}ex}_{0}(n,{\mathcal{F}})
is the usual
Turán function

\mathrm{ex}(n,{\mathcal{F}})
, the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
{\mathcal{F}}
-free
k
-graph and thus
\gamma_{0}^{+}({\mathcal{F}})
is the
Turán density

\pi({\mathcal{F}}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{ex}(n,{\mathcal{F}})/{n\choose k}
, where the existence of the limit was established by Katona, Nemetz and Simonovits
[
8
]
by an easy averaging argument. Also, one can show that
\gamma_{1}^{+}({\mathcal{F}})=\pi({\mathcal{F}})
. (For example, this easily follows from Proposition
1

1

[
16
]
and Keevash
[
10
]
.

Another relative is the
\ell
-degree Turán fuction

\mathrm{co\mbox{-}ex}_{\ell}(n,{\mathcal{F}})

-free

A systematic study of this function for

exists for

[

[
10

[

The problem of determining

Halfpap, Lemons and Palmer

Proposition 1

For every (possibly infinite)
k
-graph family
{\mathcal{F}}
and every integer
\ell

, the ratio
\mathrm{co^{+}ex}_{\ell}(n,{\mathcal{F}})/{n-\ell\choose k-\ell}

2
Proof of Proposition
1

The main proof idea (to take an
n
-vertex sample from a larger nearly optimal
{\mathcal{F}}
-free
k
-graph) is the same as in the proofs of
Mubayi and Zhao
[
14
, Proposition 1.2]
and Lo and Markström
[
12
, Proposition 1.5]
that the limit in (
2
) exists. Here, we face some new (minor) technicalities due to the fact that
\ell
-sets of zero degree have to be treated differently.

We need the following auxiliary lemma which states, roughly speaking, that a
k
-graph with
n\to\infty
vertices and positive
\ell
-degree
\Omega(n^{k-\ell})
must have
\Omega(n^{k})
edges.

Lemma 2

Let
0\leqslant\ell<k
be integers and
\gamma\geqslant 0
be a real number.
If a
k
-graph
G
with
m
vertices and
e>0
edges satisfies
\delta_{\ell}^{+}(G)\geqslant\gamma m^{k-\ell}/(k-\ell)!
then
e\geqslant\gamma^{k/(k-\ell)}m^{k}/k!
.

Proof.
Assume that
\gamma>0
as otherwise there is nothing to prove. Let
\lambda>0
be the number of
\ell
-sets covered by at least one edge of
G
. Let
x
be the real number at least
k
that satisfies
e={x\choose k}
, where we define

{y\choose k}:=\frac{y(y-1)\dots(y-k+1)}{k!},\quad\mbox{for $y\in{\mathbbm{R}}$}.

Note that the function
{y\choose k}
is strictly increasing and continuous for
y\geqslant k
so
x
exists and is unique.
By a version of the Kruskal-Katona Theorem
[
9
,
11
]
that is due to Lovász
[
13
, Exercise 13.31(b)]
, we have that
\lambda\geqslant{x\choose\ell}
.

Thus the number of pairs
(K,L)
, where
K\in E(G)
and
L
is an
\ell
-subset of
K
is at least
\lambda\cdot\gamma m^{k-\ell}/(k-\ell)!
on one hand and is exactly
e\cdot{k\choose\ell}
on the other hand. Putting these two estimates together, we get that

{x\choose k}{k\choose\ell}\geqslant{x\choose\ell}\,\frac{\gamma m^{k-\ell}}{(k-\ell)!}.

By cancelling the same (positive) factors and rearranging, we get that
(x-\ell)\dots(x-k+1)\geqslant\gamma m^{k-\ell}
. Now, the required inequality follows:

k!\,e=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(x-i+1)\geqslant\left(\prod_{i=\ell+1}^{k}(x-i+1)\right)^{k/(k-\ell)}\geqslant\gamma^{k/(k-\ell)}m^{k},

where the first inequality can be proved by observing that, after raising it to power
k-\ell
and cancelling identical terms, we are left with two products of
\ell(k-\ell)
factors, with each factor at least
x-\ell+1
on the left-hand side and at most
x-\ell
on the right-hand side.

Proof of Proposition
1
.
Let
\gamma:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{co^{+}ex}_{\ell}(n,{\mathcal{F}})}{{n-\ell\choose k-\ell}}
be the limit superior in of the stated ratios. If
\gamma=0
, then by the non-negativity of each term, the limit exists and is 0. So suppose that
\gamma>0
. Take any
\varepsilon\in(0,\gamma)
.

Let
n
be sufficiently large. Pick any
{\mathcal{F}}
-free
k
-graph
G
with
N\geqslant n
vertices such that
\delta_{\ell}^{+}(G)\geqslant(\gamma-\varepsilon/2){N-\ell\choose k-\ell}
.

Take a uniformly random
n
-subset of
V(G)
and a uniformly random enumeration
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
of its vertices. Equivalently, for
i=1,\dots,n
, let
v_{i}
be a random element of
V(G)\setminus\{v_{1},\dots,v_{i-1}\}
with all
N-i+1
choices being equally likely.

Let
H
be the
k
-graph on
[n]
where a
k
-set
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}\subseteq[n]
is an edge if and only if
\{v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}\}
is an edge of
G
. Thus, up to relabelling of its vertices,
H
is the
k
-graph induced in
G
by a uniformly random set of
n
vertices. Clearly,
H
is
{\mathcal{F}}
-free.

Claim 3

Let
g:=\big\lfloor(\gamma-\varepsilon){n-\ell\choose k-\ell}\big\rfloor
. Then the probability that there is an
\ell
-set
L\subseteq[n]
with
0<\mathrm{deg}_{L}(H)\leqslant g
is less than
1/2
.

Proof of Claim.
It is enough to show by the Union Bound that, for every
\ell
-set
L\subseteq[n]
, the probability over the random choices of
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
that
\mathrm{deg}_{L}(H)\in[g]
is less than
\frac{1}{2}{n\choose\ell}^{-1}
. Fix any
L\in{[n]\choose k}
. By symmetry between the vertices of
H
, we can assume for notational convenience that
L=[\ell]
. It is enough to prove the stated bound when we condition on
A:=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell}\}
. The conditional distribution can be obtained by picking
v_{\ell+1},\dots,v_{n}
one by one, each being uniform in the remaining subset of
V(G)\setminus A
.
If
\mathrm{deg}_{A}(G)=0
, then
\mathrm{deg}_{L}(H)=0
deterministically and the stated event cannot occur. So suppose that
\mathrm{deg}_{A}(G)>0
and thus it is at least
\delta_{\ell}^{+}(G)\geqslant(\gamma-\varepsilon/2){N-\ell\choose k-\ell}
.
For
i=0,\dots,n-\ell
, let
X_{i}
be the expectation of
\mathrm{deg}_{L}(H)
after we have exposed
v_{\ell+1},\dots,v_{\ell+i}
. In other words,
(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-\ell})
is the vertex-exposure martingale for
\mathrm{deg}_{L}(H)
conditioned on
A
. Note that
X_{0}
is constant and its value is

X_{0}={\mathbbm{E}}[X_{n-\ell}]=\mathrm{deg}_{A}(G){n-\ell\choose k-\ell}\Big/{N-\ell\choose k-\ell}\geqslant(\gamma-\varepsilon/2){n-\ell\choose k-\ell},

(3)

while
X_{n-\ell}=\mathrm{deg}_{L}(H)
.

Let us show that
|X_{i}-X_{i-1}|\leqslant{n-\ell-1\choose k-\ell-1}
for every
i\in[n-\ell]
. It is enough to prove this inequality, conditioned on every choice of
v_{\ell+1},\dots,v_{\ell+i-1}
.
For every two different choices
u
and
u^{\prime}
for the vertex
v_{\ell+i}
there is a natural coupling of the follow-up processes so that
|B\bigtriangleup B^{\prime}|\leqslant 2
always holds for the current unordered sets
B,B^{\prime}\subseteq V(H)
of the selected vertices: namely, run the process for
u
and let the process for
u^{\prime}
choose the same vertex at each step, except if we see a vertex on which the current sets
B
and
B^{\prime}
differ then we make these two sets equal from this step onwards. Thus the final unordered sets
\{v_{\ell+1},\dots,v_{n}\}
differ in at most two places and the respective degrees of
L
differ by at most
{n-\ell-1\choose k-\ell-1}
, giving the claimed inequality.

Thus Azuma’s inequality (see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 7.2.1]
) gives that the probability of
X_{n-\ell}\leqslant X_{0}-(\varepsilon/2){n-\ell\choose k-\ell}
is at most

{\mathrm{e}}^{-\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}{n-\ell\choose k-\ell}\right)^{2}\big/\left(2(n-\ell){n-\ell-1\choose k-\ell-1}^{2}\right)}<{\mathrm{e}}^{-\varepsilon^{2}n/(9k^{2})}<\frac{1}{2}{n\choose\ell}^{-1}.

Recalling that
X_{n-\ell}=\mathrm{deg}_{L}(H)
while the constant
X_{0}
satisfies (
3
), we obtain that the probability of
\mathrm{deg}_{H}(L)\leqslant g
is less than
\frac{1}{2}{n\choose\ell}^{-1}
, giving the claim.

Claim 4

The probability that
H
is empty is less than
1/2
.

Proof of Claim.
Since
n
is large, the edge density of
G
is by Lemma
2
at least, for example,
\beta:=(\gamma-\varepsilon)^{k/(k-\ell)}>0
. Thus the expected size of
H
is at least
\beta{n\choose k}
. Consider the vertex-exposure martingale
(Y_{0},\dots,Y_{n})
for
|E(H)|
. Similarly as before, one can show that
|Y_{i}-Y_{i-1}|\leqslant{n-1\choose k-1}
for each
i\in[n]
. Thus if
H
has no edges then
Y_{n}=|E(H)|=0
is at least
\beta{n\choose k}
away from its mean
Y_{0}
and, again by Azuma’s inequality, the probability of this is at most

{\mathrm{e}}^{-\left(\beta{n\choose k}\right)^{2}\big/\left(2n{n-1\choose k-1}^{2}\right)}<{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta^{2}n/(3k^{2})}<\frac{1}{2},

as desired.

By Claims
3
and
4
the random
{\mathcal{F}}
-free
k
-graph
H
on
[n]
is non-empty and satisfies
\delta_{\ell}^{+}(H)>(\gamma-\varepsilon){n-\ell\choose k-\ell}
with positive probability. So at least one such choice for
H
exists and
\mathrm{co^{+}ex}(n,{\mathcal{F}})>(\gamma-\varepsilon){n-\ell\choose k-\ell}
. Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary and this inequality holds for all sufficiently large
n
, we conclude that the ratio
\mathrm{co^{+}ex}(n,{\mathcal{F}})/{n-\ell\choose k-\ell}
tends to
\gamma
, finishing the proof of the proposition.

3
Positive degree via hypergraph limits

In order to state our main result of this paper (Theorem
5
) we need to give various definitions related to the limit theory of hypergraphs. We generally follow the notation from
[
19
]
.

For a finite set
A
and an integer
m\geqslant 1
, let

r(A,m):=\{X\subseteq A:0<|X|\leqslant m\},

consist of all non-empty subsets of
A
with at most
m
elements. Also,
let

r(A):=r(A,|A|)=\{X:\emptyset\not=X\subseteq A\}

denote the set of all non-empty subsets of
A
and

r_{<}(A):=r(A,|A|-1)=\{X:\emptyset\not=X\subsetneq A\}

denote the set of all
proper
subsets of
A
. If
A
is
[m]=\{1,\dots,m\}
, then we may abbreviate
r([m])
and
r_{<}([m])
to
r[m]
and
r_{<}[m]
respectively.

For a family
{\mathcal{A}}
of sets, let
{\bf x}_{{\mathcal{A}}}\in{\mathbbm{R}}^{{\mathcal{A}}}
denotes the vector of reals
(x_{A})_{A\in{\mathcal{A}}}
indexed by
{\mathcal{A}}
. When
{\bf x}
has already been specified, we use this notation as follows. If every index in
{\bf x}
appears in
{\mathcal{A}}
then by
{\bf x}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
we mean an
extension
of
{\bf x}
, that is, any vector indexed by
{\mathcal{A}}
which coincides with
{\bf x}
on the common set of indices. If every element of
{\mathcal{A}}
appears as an index in
{\bf x}
then
{\bf x}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
means the
restriction
of
{\bf x}
to
{\mathcal{A}}
, that is, the vector indexed by
{\mathcal{A}}
whose
A
-coordinate is the same as the
A
-coordinate of
{\bf x}
for every
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
. We assume that the sets in
{\mathcal{A}}
come in some fixed and consistently used order, which is preserved when we pass to subfamilies. In all concrete examples for
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq r[n]
that we give, we first order the sets increasingly by their size and then use the lexicographic order to break ties on sets of equal size.

The
symmetric group

\mathrm{Sym}_{k}
(consisting of all permutations of
[k]
) acts naturally on
[0,1]^{r_{<}[k]}
. A function
W:[0,1]^{r_{<}[k]}\to{\mathbbm{R}}
is called
symmetric
if its values do not change under the action of
\mathrm{Sym}_{k}
on
[0,1]^{r_{<}[k]}
. For example, for
r=2
, this means that
W(x_{1},x_{2})=W(x_{2},x_{1})
for all
(x_{1},x_{2})\in[0,1]^{2}
, and for
r=3
this means that

W(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{12},x_{13},x_{23})=W({\bf x}^{\sigma}),\quad\mbox{for all ${\bf x}\in[0,1]^{r_{<}[3]}$ and $\sigma\in S_{3}$,}

(4)

where we abbreviate
x_{\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}}
to
x_{a_{1}\dots a_{k}}
and denote

{\bf x}^{\sigma}:=(x_{\sigma(1)},x_{\sigma(2)},x_{\sigma(3)},x_{\sigma(1)\,\sigma(2)},x_{\sigma(1)\,\sigma(3)},x_{\sigma(2)\,\sigma(3)}).

A
k
-hypergraphon
is a symmetric (Lebesgue) measurable function
W:[0,1]^{r_{<}[k]}\to[0,1]
.

Elek and Szegedy
[
4
, Theorem 7]
(see also Zhao
[
19
, Theorem 1.5]
for a different proof) showed that
k
-hypergraphons can serve as limit objects for
k
-graphs as follows. The
(homomorphism) density
of a
k
-graph
F
in
W
is defined as

t(F,W):=\int_{[0,1]^{r(V(F),k-1)}}\prod_{A\in E(F)}W({\bf x}_{r_{<}(A)})\,\mathrm{d}{\bf x}.

(5)

A
k
-graph
G
with vertices enumerated as
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
corresponds to the
k
-hypergraphon
W^{G}
constructed as follows: partition
[0,1]
into
n
intervals
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
of length
1/n
each, and let

W^{G}({\bf x}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\mbox{if $\{v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}\}\in E(G)$, where $i_{j}$ is the index with $I_{i_{j}}\ni x_{j}$ for $j\in[k]$,}\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(6)

Thus
W^{G}
is a
\{0,1\}
-valued function on
[0,1]^{r_{<}[k]}
that depends on the
1
-dimensional coordinates only, naturally encoding the edge set of
G
.
Note that

t(F,W^{G})=t(F,G),\quad\mbox{for every $k$-graph $F$},

(7)

where
t(F,G)
is the usual
(homomorphism) density
of
F
in
G
, which is the probability that a random function
f:V(F)\to V(G)
, with all
|V(G)|^{|V(F)|}
choices being equally likely, sends every edge of
F
to an edge of
G
. Call a sequence
(G_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
k
-graphs
convergent
if, for every
k
-graph
F
, the densities
t(F,G_{n})
converge to a limit as
n\to\infty
. One of the main results of Elek and Szegedy
[
4
, Theorem 7]
is that, for every convergent sequence
(G_{n})_{i=1}^{\infty}
of
k
-graphs, there is a
k
-hypergraphon
W
, called the
limit
of
(G_{n})_{i=1}^{\infty}
, such that

t(F,W)=\lim_{n\to\infty}t(F,G_{n}),\quad\mbox{for every $k$-graph $F$}.

(8)

Note that, even though the right-hand side of (
8
) involves hypergraphons depending on the 1-dimensional coordinates only (when we replace
t(F,G_{n})
by
t(F,W^{G_{n}})
using (
7
)), the resulting limit may in general depend
on the extra (more than 1-dimensional) coordinates. Informally speaking, we may need
to account for the limits of hypergraph constructions where the density of edges of
E(G)
depends not only on the locations of vertices in a regularity partition of
G
but also on the higher-dimensional cylinder structure, that is, the “colours” of subsets of sizes between
2
and
k-1
. One such example is the
directed cycle
3
-graph construction

C(T)
where one takes a quasi-random tournament
T
and defines the edge set of
C(T)
to consist of all triples spanning a directed cycle; this construction is known to asymptotically optimal for some extermal 3-graph problems, see e.g.
[
5
,
6
,
15
]
.
A possible corresponding
3
-hypergraphon can be defined to be
0
except it is 1 on
{\bf x}^{\sigma}
for all
\sigma\in S_{3}
and
{\bf x}
such that
x_{1}<x_{2}<x_{3}
,
x_{12},x_{23}\in[0,1/2]
and
x_{13}\in(1/2,1]
.

Accordingly, when we extend the definition of (positive)
\ell
-degree to a
k
-hypergraphon
W
, we need to take into account not only 1-dimensional coordinates (
\ell
of them) but all higher-dimensional ones (within the corresponding
\ell
-set). Formally, for
0\leqslant\ell<k
, the
degree
of
{\bf x}\in[0,1]^{r[\ell]}
in
W
is defined as

\mathrm{deg}_{W}({\bf x}):=\int_{[0,1]^{r_{<}[k]\setminus r[\ell]}}W({\bf x}_{r_{<}[k]})\,\mathrm{d}{\bf x}_{r_{<}[k]\setminus r[\ell]},

(9)

that is, we take the average of
W
over all extensions
{\bf x}_{r_{<}[k]}\in[0,1]^{r_{<}[k]}
of
{\bf x}\in[0,1]^{r[\ell]}
, where the new coordinates (those indexed by
{r_{<}[k]\setminus r[\ell]}

. The expected number of
\ell
-sets
L\subseteq[n]
such that
{\bf x}_{r(L)}\in X
is
\Omega(n^{\ell})
. By Azuma’s inequality, the probability that no
L
satisfies this is
{\mathrm{e}}^{-\Omega(n)}<\varepsilon/6
. Furthermore, if we take any
L
with
{\bf x}_{r(L)}\in Z
and condition on
{\bf x}_{r(L)}
then Azuma’s equality, when applied to the martingale where we expose for each vertex
i\in[n]\setminus L
all information up to
i
, shows that the probability of
\mathrm{deg}_{G}(L)
being at least
(\varepsilon/2){n-\ell\choose k-\ell}
away from its expected value
\mathrm{deg}_{W}({\bf x}){n-\ell\choose k-\ell}
is
{\mathrm{e}}^{-\Omega(n)}<\varepsilon/6
. Thus with probability at least
1-\varepsilon/3
there is an
\ell
-set
L\subseteq[n]
with
0<\mathrm{deg}_{G}(L)\leqslant(\delta+\varepsilon){n-\ell\choose k-\ell}
, which implies that
\delta_{\ell}^{+}(G)\leqslant(\delta+\varepsilon){n-\ell\choose k-\ell}
.

By putting all together, we conclude that, with probability at least
1-\varepsilon
, the random
n
-sample
G\sim{\mathbbm{G}}(n,W)
has positive
\ell
-degree
(\delta\pm\varepsilon){n-\ell\choose{}^{k-\ell}}
, as desired.

5
Concluding remarks

One can also consider extremal limits for the
\ell
-degree Turán problem. Namely, let

{\mathcal{W}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{F}})
consist of all
k
-hypergraphons
W
such that there is a sequence
(G_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
{\mathcal{F}}
-free
k
-graphs convergent to
W
such that
|V(G_{n})|\to\infty
and the
mininum
\ell
-degree

\delta_{\ell}(G_{n}):=\min\{\mathrm{deg}_{G_{n}}(L):L\subseteq[n],\ |L|=\ell\}

is
(\gamma_{\ell}({\mathcal{F}})+o(1)){|V(G_{n})|-\ell\choose k-\ell}
as
n\to\infty
, where
\gamma_{\ell}({\mathcal{F}})
is defined in (
2
). Also, let the
minimum
\ell
-degree
of a
k

be

\delta_{\ell}(W):=\einf_{{\bf x}\in[0,1]^{r[\ell]}}\mathrm{deg}_{W}({\bf x}).

Our proof of Theorem
5
can be easily adapted to to produce the following result (in fact, the proof is simpler since we do not have to treat
\ell
-sets of zero degree in a special way).

Theorem 8

Let
k>\ell\geqslant 0
be integers and let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a

-hypergraphons

.
